On the Evaluation of the Ray-Singer Torsion Path Integral
Matthias Blau, Mbambu Kakona, George Thompson

Abstract
There are very few explicit evaluations of path integrals for topological gauge theories in more than 3 dimensions. Here we provide such a calculation for the path integral representation of the Ray-Singer Torsion of a flat connection on a vector bundle on base manifolds that are themselves S 1 S^{1} bundles of any dimension. The calculation relies on a suitable algebraic choice of gauge which leads to a convenient factorisation of the path integral into horizontal and vertical parts.



1
Introduction

There are very few explicit evaluations of path integrals for
topological gauge theories in more than 3 dimensions.
Here we
will provide such calculations for a class of topological field
theories giving a path integral realisation of the Ray-Singer Torsion.
The Ray-Singer Torsion
Ray-Singer
;
Ray-Singer-Analytic
is an
invariant that can be defined on any compact closed manifold
M
of
real dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M=m
. The
definition requires a bundle
E
over
M
that admits a flat
connection. Schwarz
Schwarz-Partition
;
Schwarz
;
Schwarz-Tyupkin
gave a deceptively easy
topological field theory
representation for the Torsion. The Schwarz action for the Ray-Singer
Torsion of a flat connection
\mathcal{A}
on a bundle
E\rightarrow M

is given by

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}=\int_{M}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits B\wedge\,d_{\mathcal{A}}\,C

(1)

with
B\in\Omega^{p}(M,E^{*})
,
C\in\Omega^{q}(M,E)

where
q=m-p-1
. The action (
1
) has reducible symmetries
as
d_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}=F_{\mathcal{A}}=0
. Schwarz formally
quantised these reducible systems in terms of
resolvents. The resolvent approach was
shown to be equivalent to the more usual Faddeev-Popov gauge fixing
procedure in
BT-RST
;
BT-BF
;
BT-Metric
. Either way the partition function
Z[\mathcal{A},E]
with
action (
1
) is related to the Ray-Singer torsion

\tau_{M}(\mathcal{A},E)
by

\displaystyle Z[\mathcal{A},E]=\tau_{M}(\mathcal{A},E)^{(-1)^{p+1}}

(2)

In
BKT
we began a study of the exact evaluation of the Schwarz
path integral representation of the Ray-Singer Torsion. Particular
emphasis was paid to the issue of zero modes and we advocated using
the addition of a mass to take these into account. Formally, the
addition of a mass is straightforward, however, computationally we
focused on mapping tori as on these manifolds the mass could be understood as a
part of a connection for an extended gauge group. Using path integral techniques
we were able to reproduce for the Ray-Singer Torsion a result of Fried

Fried
that was established for the Reidemeister Torsion. That
Fried’s result should also hold for Ray-Singer Torsion is a
consequence of the equivalence of the two Torsions conjectured by Ray
and Singer and proven by Cheeger

Cheeger-1
;
Cheeger-2
and by Müller
Muller
.

Here we consider the same path integrals but for the manifold
M
taken to be an
S^{1}
bundle
over
N
and where
E
is a flat
G=SU(r)
vector bundle over
M
. We
provide an explicit evaluation of the path integrals involved for
M

of any dimension and
E
of any rank.

The
natural nowhere vanishing vector field on
M
generating the
S^{1}
-action allows for a
decomposition of any connection
\mathcal{A}
on
E
into a component
\phi

along the fibre and a horizontal component
A
. By the same
logic the fields
B
and
C
and their entire Batalin-Vilkovisky
triangles of ghost and multiplier fields
BV1983
may also be decomposed into
components. This allows one to impose an algebraic gauge condition
which both simplifies the field content and simultaneously allows one
to perform the path integrals.

We find that quite generally
(
2
) factorises as

\displaystyle Z[\mathcal{A},E]=Z_{\parallel}[A,E]\,.\,Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]

(3)

where
Z_{\parallel}[A,E]
contains all the information about
the zero modes associated with the flat connection. Hence in the acyclic
case (and up to trivial normalisation)
Z_{\parallel}[A,E]=1
,
otherwise
Z_{\parallel}[A,E]
is a section of an appropriate
determinant line bundle. Whether a flat connection which is acyclic
exists or not depends on the
SU(r)
bundle
E
. For example if
E
is the fundamental
representation then there are flat connections which are acyclic whereas
for the adjoint bundle case no flat connection is acyclic. As how to deal with zero
modes has been explained previously
BT-BF
;
BKT
we do not spend much time on this
part of the factorisation formula.

The more interesting factor is
Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]
and to evaluate it
we make some choices. We presume that
E
is the pullback of a bundle

V
on
N
. On
N
,
A
is a connection while
\phi
is a section of
the
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits V
bundle. In this case the flatness equations for the combined gauge field
on
E
over
M
imply
that either
\phi
is zero (in which case
A
is a flat connection on

N
) or
\phi
is non-zero and
A
is a reducible connection. If
\phi

is zero there is nothing to do, so we fix our attention on the case
where
\phi
is not zero. In this case the flatness
condition implies that the connection
A

splits as
A^{K}\oplus A^{U(1)}
with
A^{U(1)}
lying in the same
direction as
\phi
in the Lie algebra and
K\times U(1)\subset G
. With this set-up the computations are quite doable and one finds
that

\displaystyle Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]=\left(\tau_{S^{1}}(\phi,E_{\perp}|_{S^{1}})^{\chi(N)}\right)^{(-1)^{p+1}}

(4)

where
E_{\perp}|_{S^{1}}
is the restriction to the fibre of the
bundle
E_{\perp}
over
M
,
\tau_{S^{1}}
is the Ray-Singer
torsion on the circle and
\chi(N)
is the Euler characteristic of

N
. (The definition of
E_{\perp}
is given in
section
6
; here one understands that in (
4
)
it corresponds to having no zero modes.)

Combining (
2
) and (
4
) in the acyclic case
we arrive at

\displaystyle\tau_{M}(\mathcal{A},E)=\tau_{S^{1}}(\phi,E_{\perp}|_{S^{1}})^{\chi(N)}

(5)

which agrees with another result of Fried for Reidemeister Torsion (result
(V) on page 26 of
Fried
) when
M
is an
S^{1}
fibration. Our results
for the non-acyclic case therefore represent a generalisation of that
work.

While this may be of some limited interest in its own right, the ability
to reproduce or generalise known mathematical results serves mainly
as a check on the methods that we develop and use here to evaluate the path integral,
which can then also be applied with more confidence in other cases.

In Section
2
, in order to set the stage, we review the
flatness equations for connections on a vector bundle over an

S^{1}
bundle, while a more general background on the geometry of

Appendix
A
. In particular, we explain how to decompose

Section
3
is devoted to establishing that the
partition function factorises as in (
3
). In the current geometric situation
it is possible to impose algebraic gauge fixing conditions that
simplify the ghost for ghost system of the quantisation of the theory
and we carry this out in detail in
Appendix
B
. With the gauge fixing choices that we make the

the connection explicitly exhibited in
(
3
).

An analysis of the factor
Z_{\parallel}[A,E]
is the subject of
Section
4
. There it is shown that the path integrals
devolve to integrals over the zero modes or, equivalently, to
producing a section of a determinant line bundle of tensor powers of cohomology
groups associated with the twisted de Rham operator. Such determinant
lines appear in the work of Ray and Singer
Ray-Singer-Analytic
and from the quantum
gauge theory point of view
BT-BF
;
BKT
so we are brief about
this here. However, we do spend some time on establishing which harmonic modes
are actually zero modes and which are part of the gauge symmetry.

In Section
5
we turn our attention to the second
factor and begin a first evaluation of
Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]
in
generality, that is before making specific choices of the bundles and
connections on them. We show that
Z_{\perp}[A,E]

is essentially products of powers of the determinant of the covariant
derivative in the fibre direction
D_{\phi}
acting on the spaces
spanned by the forms
B
,
C
and their ghost systems. At this general level one cannot go too far but
we do obtain a formal expression for the partition function that
suggests that the final answer only depends on cohomology
groups with values in certain vector bundles over
N
.

In order to proceed further with the analysis of the partition
function
Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]
we take a more in-depth look at the
flatness equations in Section
6
. The flat bundle
E

over
M
that is considered is a pullback of a bundle
V
over
N
,
so that
E=\pi^{*}V
. In this situation
A
is the pullback of a
connection on
V
that is not flat,
\phi
is the pullback of a section
of the
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits V
bundle while the flat connection on
E
is a
combination of these two. We also assume that
\phi
is not zero so
that, as described above, the structure group
reduces to
K\times U(1)\subset G
and that the connection splits as

A^{K}\oplus A^{U(1)}
. In such a situation
V
splits as the direct
sum of powers of a basic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over
N
and the
curvature 2-form of the connection
A^{K}
is flat on
N
.

Section
7
is where we put all of the pieces together and
determine
Z_{\perp}[\phi,E]
for the choices of the previous
section. The determinants of
D_{\phi}
found in Section
5
are now over
spaces of forms on
N
with values in tensor powers of the line bundle

\mathcal{L}
. The combinations for fixed Fourier mode (on the fibre)
are regularised as a Heat Kernel and the combination is the super-trace
of the eigenmodes of the twisted de Rham operator, which in turn is a
multiple of the Euler characteristic
\chi(N)
of
N
, which explains
its appearance in (
4
). The product over the Fourier modes
is regularised using a
\zeta
function regularisation and provides
the
\tau_{S^{1}}
that appears in (
4
).

Finally, in Section
8
we briefly mention certain
generalisations that can be considered, some of which straightforwardly

There are two appendices, as mentioned above. In the first Appendix

A
we provide a quick summary of the geometric structure
that is available on an
S^{1}
bundle

. This includes

2
Connections on Bundles over
\boldsymbol{S^{1}}
Bundles

Let
M
be the total space of a smooth
S^{1}
-bundle over a smooth manifold
N
.
We will need to make use of some aspects of the geometry of
S^{1}
-bundles.
The results that we need are quite standard, so we do not repeat them here, but
for convenience we have assembled and briefly reviewed them in Appendix
A
.

Thus let
\pi:M\to N
be a smooth
S^{1}
-bundle over the smooth manifold
N
,
and denote by
\xi
the fundamental nowhere vanishing vector field generating the

S^{1}
-action on
M
. A choice of connection on this bundle is equivalent to a
choice of 1-form
\kappa
on
M
satisfying

\displaystyle\iota_{\xi}\kappa=1\quad,\quad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\kappa=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\iota_{\xi}d\kappa=0

(6)

(with
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}=\iota_{\xi}d+d\iota_{\xi}
the Lie derivative),
and we choose one such
\kappa
in the following.

With respect to a choice of
\kappa
, any

p
-form
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M)
on
M
can be decomposed into a
“horizontal” and a “vertical” component,

\displaystyle\alpha=(\iota_{\xi}\kappa)\alpha=\iota_{\xi}(\kappa\wedge\alpha)+\kappa\wedge\iota_{\xi}\alpha\equiv\alpha^{(p)}+\kappa\wedge\alpha_{(p-1)}

(7)

with
\alpha^{(p)}=\iota_{\xi}(\kappa\wedge\alpha)
and

\alpha_{(p-1)}=\iota_{\xi}\alpha

both manifestly horizontal, in the sense that

\displaystyle\iota_{\xi}\alpha^{(p)}=0\quad,\quad\iota_{\xi}\alpha_{(p-1)}=0\;\;.

(8)

The space of horizontal
p
-forms
\alpha
, i.e. which satisfy

\iota_{\xi}\alpha=0
, is denoted by
\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)
.
Likewise, the exterior derivative can be decomposed as (
92
)

\displaystyle d=\iota_{\xi}(\kappa\wedge d)+\kappa\wedge\iota_{\xi}d\equiv d_{H}+\kappa D\;\;.

(9)

Now let
E
be a vector bundle over
M
. A connection

\mathcal{A}
on
E\rightarrow M
may be decomposed, as above, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi,\quad\iota_{\xi}A=0

(10)

so that locally
A
is a Lie algebra valued horizontal 1-form and

\phi
is, locally, a Lie algebra valued 0-form. Gauge transformations
decompose appropriately,

\displaystyle A\rightarrow g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}d_{H}g,\quad\phi\rightarrow g^{-1}\phi g+g^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g

(11)

and covariant derivatives split as in (
9
), i.e. one has

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{A}}=d+\mathcal{A}\;=d_{A}^{H}+\kappa D_{\phi},\quad D_{\phi}=D+\phi,

(12)

(with the connection in the appropriate representation), and
the square of the covariant derivative is

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{A}^{H}\right)^{2}+\left\{d_{A}^{H},\kappa D_{\phi}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{A}^{H}\right)^{2}+d\kappa D_{\phi}-\kappa[d_{A}^{H},D_{\phi}]

(13)

The corresponding decomposition of the curvature 2-form is

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{A}}=F_{A}^{H}+d\kappa\phi+\kappa\left(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A-d_{A}^{H}\phi\right)

(14)

where for simplicity we have adopted the notation

\displaystyle F_{A}^{H}=d_{H}A+A^{2},\quad d_{A}^{H}\phi=d_{H}\phi+[A,\phi]

(15)

Note that
F_{A}^{H}
does not satisfy the ‘horizontal’ Bianchi
identity
d_{A}^{H}F_{A}^{H}=0
in general as
d_{H}^{2}\neq 0
. However,
if
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=0
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=0\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;d_{H}A=dA\quad\Rightarrow\quad F_{A}^{H}=F_{A}

(16)

and thus this condition implies the horizontal Bianchi identity,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=0\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;d_{A}^{H}F_{A}^{H}=d_{A}F_{A}=0\;\;.

(17)

If this condition is satisfied then one can consider
A
to be the pullback of a
connection (which we also denote by
A
) on a bundle on
N
. The pullback
curvature
F_{A}^{H}
now correctly satisfies the appropriate Bianchi
identity.

The equations for a flat connection are, from (
14
),

\displaystyle F_{A}^{H}=-d\kappa\,\phi,\quad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=d_{A}^{H}\phi

(18)

The ‘operator’ version of these equations that follow from (
13
)
are

\displaystyle\left(d_{A}^{H}\right)^{2}=-d\kappa D_{\phi},\quad\kappa\wedge[d_{A}^{H},D_{\phi}]=0

(19)

and will be useful in the next section. In particular, the second
equation implies that the operators
d_{A}^{H}
and
D_{\phi}
commute
on horizontal vector-valued forms
\alpha\in\Omega_{H}^{p}(M,E)
,

\displaystyle\alpha\in\Omega_{H}^{p}(M,E)\quad\Rightarrow\quad[d_{A}^{H},D_{\phi}]\,\alpha=0

(20)

3
Factorisation of the Partition Function

Let
\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi
be a flat connection on a vector bundle

E
. The Schwarz action for the corresponding Ray-Singer torsion is the
action (
1
),

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}=\int_{M}\langle B,\,d_{\mathcal{A}}C\rangle

(21)

with the fields in
\Omega^{*}(M,E)
. Here we use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote a fixed
invariant fibre scalar product on
E
(which in (
1
) was
denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits
). In case
E
is a complex vector
bundle it is to be understood throughout that we have added the
complex conjugates of the fields in (
21
) so that the
action is real. Specific choices
are e.g. the bundle
E=F
for the fundamental representation of some group
G
, or

E=\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P
for the adjoint representation (associated to the principal
G
-bundle

P
). These are the examples that we will occasionally consider explicitly.
However, we can describe both the gauge fixing and the evaluation of the
path integral in a way that is largely independent of the choice of
E
(or
representation).

The gauge fixing is described in detail in Appendix
B

for any
E
equipped with a flat connection. The gauge choices are
a mixture of algebraic conditions (imposed directly on components
of the fields and do not involve differential operators) and those
that do involve differential operators (as in the Landau gauge in
Yang-Mills theory, say).

There is a good reason to use such algebraic gauges in the present context. These
gauge conditions greatly simplify the evaluation of the path integral
and because of that simplification lead directly to the somewhat
startling fact that the partition function factorises into two pieces,
one piece only contains possible zero mode dependence and otherwise is
independent of the
A
part of the background flat connection
\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi
. The second factor, one sees in a very direct
fashion, depends
only on the
\phi
component of the connection. Denoting the complete
partition function by
Z[\mathcal{A},E]
, one thus has the factorisation

\displaystyle Z[\mathcal{A},E]=Z_{\parallel}[A]\,.\,Z_{\perp}[\phi]

(22)

(the notation will become apparent). To exhibit this simple
dependence on
\phi
one does not
need to know the details of the vector bundle nor the details
of the flat connection under consideration.

The key step in order to establish this factorisation (and, as it
turns out, to neatly separate zero modes from non-zero modes) is
to perform a decomposition of horizontal forms according to the
action of
D_{\phi}
on them.
To that end, let

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,E)=\Omega^{p}_{\parallel}(M,E)\oplus\Omega^{p}_{\perp}(M,E)

(23)

where
\Omega^{p}_{\parallel}
is the kernel of the map

\displaystyle D_{\phi}:\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,E)

(24)

and
D_{\phi}
is invertible on
\Omega^{p}_{\perp}(M,E)
. By virtue
of (
20
) one also has that

\displaystyle d_{A}^{H}:\Omega^{p}_{\parallel}(M,E)\rightarrow\Omega^{p}_{\parallel}(M,E)

(25)

For a horizontal form
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,E)
we write

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha_{\parallel}+\alpha_{\perp}

(26)

with the obvious notation. As in (
7
), the fields
and the ghosts etc have the decomposition

\displaystyle\Psi\in\Omega^{q}(M,E)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Psi=\Psi^{(q)}+\kappa\Psi_{(q-1)}

(27)

with
\Psi^{(q)}
and
\Psi_{(q-1)}
horizontal.
As one sees from (
113
) and (
114
), or in more
detail in (
118
), the field

\Psi_{(q-1)}
will transform as

\displaystyle\Psi_{(q-1)}\rightarrow\Psi_{(q-1)}+D_{\phi}\Lambda_{\perp}^{(q-1)}

(28)

where we have chosen the gauge parameter
\Lambda_{\perp}^{(q-1)}\in\Omega^{q-1}_{\perp}(M,E)
. Taking
\Lambda_{\perp}^{(q-1)}=-D_{\phi}^{-1}\Psi^{\perp}_{(q-1)}
allows one to remove
the
\Psi^{\perp}_{(q-1)}
from
\Psi_{(q-1)}
, so that only
the parallel component survives,

\displaystyle\Psi_{(q-1)}\rightarrow\Psi_{(q-1)}^{\parallel}

(29)

Now we can state the algebraic gauge condition: for all fields
\Psi

we impose the condition that

\displaystyle\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\perp}=0

(30)

In particular, for the fields

\displaystyle B=B^{(p)}+\kappa B_{(p-1)},\quad C=C^{(q)}+\kappa C_{(q-1)}

(31)

in the action (
21
), the algebraic conditions are

\displaystyle B_{(p-1)}^{\perp}=0,\quad C_{(q-1)}^{\perp}=0

(32)

and
the classical action (
21
) for the vector bundle
E

then becomes

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\langle B^{(p)}_{\perp},\,\kappa D_{\phi}C^{(q)}_{\perp}\rangle-\langle B^{(p)}_{\parallel},\,\kappa d^{H}_{A}C_{(q-1)}^{\parallel}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad+\kappa\langle B_{(p-1)}^{\parallel},\,d^{H}_{A}C^{(q)}_{\parallel}\rangle+\kappa d\kappa\langle B_{(p-1)}^{\parallel},\,C_{(q-1)}^{\parallel}\rangle\right)

(33)

with the fields being sections of the appropriate bundles. Notice that
‘parallel’ and ‘perpendicular’ fields do not couple.

There are still more symmetries to gauge fix. We have only used up the

\Omega^{*}_{\perp}(M,E)
parts of the symmetry. If
\Psi
satisfies
(
30
) then the transformation

\displaystyle\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}\rightarrow\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}+d_{A}^{H}\Lambda^{(p-1)}_{\parallel}+d\kappa\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel},\quad\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}\rightarrow\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}-d_{A}^{H}\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}

(34)

will preserve the gauge condition. These symmetries only involve the
fields in
\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)
and so gauge fixing these
symmetries does not mix with the gauge fixing of the perpendicular
symmetries. Consequently we conclude that for the complete partition
function the total action including ghosts and other fields will split
precisely into perpendicular and parallel components just as in
(
33
) and this is borne out in some detail in Appendix

B
. This gauge fixing and ghost structure therefore guarantees that (
22
) holds.

4
\boldsymbol{Z_{\parallel}[A]}
and the Zero Mode Measure

One can be somewhat more precise about the structure of

Z_{\parallel}[A]
and at the same time come to a better
understanding of the
d\kappa
component of the gauge transformations.

We gauge fix in two steps. Firstly note that by (
34
) we may
covariantly gauge fix both the ‘upstairs’ parallel fields

\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}
and the ‘downstairs’
\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}
ones as

\displaystyle d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}=0,\quad d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}=0

(35)

There is still a residual symmetry that is available even after we
have used
\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
in order to impose the second
condition in (
35
). Those
\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}

which are harmonic with respect to
\Delta_{A}^{H}
(so those that
satisfy
d_{A}^{H}\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}=0
in the
transformation (
34
) as well as
d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}=0
so as to preserve the condition
on
\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}
in (
35
)) are still
available.

Likewise there are
\Lambda^{(p)}_{\parallel}

harmonic modes. We will discuss the roles of the harmonic modes and
their relationship with zero modes below.

All fields in
\Omega_{\parallel}^{*}(M,E)
are either
d_{A}^{H}

exact or
\delta^{H}_{A}
exact or harmonic with respect to

\Delta_{A}^{H}
. This follows from the fact that
(d_{A}^{H})^{2}=0
on
\Omega_{\parallel}^{*}(M,E)

as follows from
(
19
),

\displaystyle\left(d_{A}^{H}\right)^{2}\alpha_{\parallel}=-d\kappa D_{\phi}\alpha_{\parallel}=0

(36)

and that with the respect to the
inner product (
106
) this Hodge decomposition is an
orthogonal one. With this in mind it is convenient to set

\displaystyle Z_{\parallel}[A]=\mu\,.\,\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]

(37)

where
\mu
is a section of an appropriate determinant line bundle of
cohomology groups and

\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]
is the path integral we had before except
with the harmonic mode sector projected away.
\mu
contains all the
information of the harmonic modes and is there to ensure that the
overall partition function is metric independent. It is important to
note that
Z_{\perp}[\phi]
on the other hand carries no
zero modes as it only involves the perpendicular fields.

We now establish that
\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]=1
. In
Appendix
B
we show that in the gauge transformations
(
34
), in the sector orthogonal to harmonic modes,
the
d\kappa
terms can be ignored. We give a
slightly different argument to this effect here. Start with the
action (
33
), with all fields orthogonal to the
harmonic modes, and send

\displaystyle B^{(p)}_{\parallel}\rightarrow tB^{(p)}_{\parallel},\quad C^{\parallel}_{(q-1)}\rightarrow t^{-1}C^{\parallel}_{(q-1)}

(38)

so that the last term goes over to

\displaystyle t^{-1}\int_{M}\kappa\,d\kappa\,\langle B^{\parallel}_{(p-1)},\,C^{\parallel}_{(q-1)}\rangle

(39)

while the others do not change. Now on taking the
t\rightarrow\infty
limit this last term completely vanishes. For consistency,
however, we also need to scale all the fields in the symmetry
transformations. The overall effect is to send the
d\kappa\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
in (
34
) to
t^{-1}d\kappa\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
so that this too vanishes in the
limit. One must bear in mind that these scalings are done orthogonally
to the harmonic modes, so that each mode under discussion here appears
in the action.

At this point the action for the partition function
\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]
is

\displaystyle S_{A}^{\parallel}=\int_{M}\left(-\langle B^{(p)}_{\parallel},\,\kappa d^{H}_{A}C_{(q-1)}^{\parallel}\rangle+\kappa\langle B_{(p-1)}^{\parallel},\,d^{H}_{A}C^{(q)}_{\parallel}\rangle\right)

(40)

while the symmetries have gone over to

\displaystyle\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}\rightarrow\Psi^{(p)}_{\parallel}+d_{A}^{H}\Lambda^{(p-1)}_{\parallel},\quad\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}\rightarrow\Psi_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}-d_{A}^{H}\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}

(41)

At the outset, the
d\kappa
part of the symmetries were there as

(d_{A}^{H})^{2}\neq 0
. One may therefore wonder how we have managed to
get away with eliminating those pieces. Essentially, this is due to the fact
that on parallel fields, as we have already seen,
(d_{A}^{H})^{2}=0

so that on those fields
d_{A}^{H}
is essentially flat. In this way we understand that
(
40
), which only contains parallel fields,
and the symmetries (
41
) (which exclude the
harmonic mode symmetries)
correspond to two Schwarz actions of Ray-Singer type for the ‘flat’
connection
d_{A}^{H}
. We thus see that the partition function
\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]

itself factorises into two parts, one for the
(B^{(p)}_{\parallel},C_{(q-1)}^{\parallel})
system and the other for the
(B_{(p-1)}^{\parallel},C^{(q)}_{\parallel})
system.

The path integrals in question need regularisation and one
may adopt the original definition in terms of
\zeta
-functions of Ray
and Singer
Ray-Singer
where the Laplacian in question is

\Delta_{A}^{H}=d_{A}^{H}\delta_{A}^{H}+\delta_{A}^{H}d_{A}^{H}
where

\delta_{A}^{H}
is the
*_{H}
dual of
d_{A}^{H}
. Now with this
definition a general
(B^{(p)}_{\parallel},C_{(q-1)}^{\parallel})
system yields
\tau(A)^{(-1)^{p-1}}
where

\tau(A)
is the ‘Ray-Singer torsion’ of the connection
A
. Consequently, the product of
these two partition functions cancel and we are left with unity. Thus

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{\parallel}[A]=1

(42)

as we set out to show. In particular the definition used by Ray and
Singer in
Ray-Singer
was to project out the harmonic modes just
as we have done here.

Clearly if all of the

\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)
are trivial then (up to normalisation)
one may set
Z_{\parallel}[A]=1
. This fact is related to the
acyclicity of the de Rham complex
(d_{\mathcal{A}},E)
. The twisted de Rham
complex is said to be acyclic if all the twisted de
Rham groups
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(M,E)
are trivial. The
representatives of the twisted de Rham groups satisfy

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{A}}v=0,\quad\Rightarrow\quad d_{A}^{H}v^{(p)}+d\kappa\wedge v_{(p-1)}=0,\quad D_{\phi}v^{(p)}-d_{A}^{H}v_{(p-1)}=0

(43)

It is
straightforward to see that if
D_{\phi}
is invertible on

\Omega_{H}^{p}(M,E)\oplus\Omega_{H}^{p-1}(M,E)
(so that

\Omega_{\parallel}^{p}(M,E)\oplus\Omega_{\parallel}^{p-1}(M,E)
is
trivial) then the de Rham group
\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M,E)
is
trivial. Let
u
be a representative of
[u]\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M,E)
then
v=u+d_{\mathcal{A}}\lambda
is also a representative of
[u]
. If

\Omega_{\parallel}^{p-1}(M,E)=0
then one may choose
\lambda
so
that
v_{(p-1)}=0
. The condition that
d_{\mathcal{A}}v=0
(
43
) now
includes the equation
D_{\phi}v^{(p)}=0
, but by assumption there are
no solutions to this so that
v=0
and consequently

\mathrm{H}_{\mathcal{A}}^{p}(M,E)=0
.

More generally, when
D_{\phi}
has a kernel pick a
v
so that

v_{(p-1)}^{\perp}=0
. The last equation in (
43
) now
breaks into two parts

\displaystyle D_{\phi}v^{(p)}_{\perp}=0,\quad d_{A}^{H}v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}=0

(44)

of which the first, by definition, implies that

v^{(p)}_{\perp}=0
. Consequently all the cohomology lives in the
parallel fields so that the zero mode measure is a part of

Z_{\parallel}[A]
. Indeed in the
gauges chosen there are two types of zero modes, namely
either

\displaystyle d_{A}^{H}v^{(p)}_{\parallel}=0\quad,\quad v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}=0

(45)

or
v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle d_{A}^{H}v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}=0\quad,\quad d_{A}^{H}v^{(p)}_{\parallel}=-d\kappa v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}

(46)

The second of these says that any harmonic
v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}

such that
d\kappa\wedge v_{(p-1)}^{\parallel}
is equal to a standard
part of the gauge symmetry is a zero mode. In short, all zero modes
are harmonic but the converse need not be true.

Turning back to the discussion around (
35
) those harmonic

\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
for which
d\kappa\wedge\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
are of the form
d_{A}^{H}\sigma^{(p)}_{\parallel}
are such that one can redefine

\Lambda^{(p)}_{\parallel}
in (
34
) to eliminate the
term proportional to
d\kappa
from the transformations. Such harmonic

\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
are zero modes of the theory. They do not
appear in the action nor in the transformations. The second type of
harmonic
\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
are those for which
d\kappa\wedge\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
is harmonic, hence cannot be expressed
as
d_{A}^{H}\sigma^{(p)}_{\parallel}
and so cannot be eliminated from the gauge
symmetry (
34
) transformations. Such

\Lambda_{(p-2)}^{\parallel}
are part of the gauge group. They are
harmonic but they are not zero modes. These modes are used
to gauge fix some of the harmonic

\Lambda^{(p+1)}_{\parallel}
modes which have not been used to impose the
covariant conditions (
35
). In Example
B.2
we
show how this is done in practice. In summary some of the harmonic
modes are part of the gauge symmetry and are to be gauge fixed in the
standard BRST fashion. Those which are not part of the gauge symmetry
are zero modes and need to be dealt with in a different manner.

How to
deal with zero modes in the path integral representation has been
described in detail in

BT-BF
;
BKT
. In particular, it has been shown in
BKT
how a choice of
gauge matches the definition of Ray and
Singer
Ray-Singer-Analytic
. We will not repeat that analysis
here, but rather take the attitude that the zero modes have been
dealt with according to one of the above prescriptions. Indeed
we take the stronger attitude that all harmonic modes have been dealt
with and in this way
\mu
has been determined.

Before leaving this section, however, we pose a question. Could the
(horizontal and parallel) “torsion”
\tau(A)
by itself be an
interesting invariant of the base space
N
? From the viewpoint of
the path integral, varying the metric on
N
amounts to a BRST exact
term, just as for the standard Ray-Singer torsion, and hence formally

\tau(A)
would be invariant under such deformations.

5
A Formal Expression for

\boldsymbol{Z_{\perp}[\phi]}

Apart from the zero modes, the complete partition function is just

Z_{\perp}[\phi]
. That is the complete flat connection on
E
over

M
is not needed - rather it is just the component
\phi
in the fibre
direction that appears. In a sense
\phi
contains almost all of the gauge
invariant data of the bundle as we will see when we analyse the
flatness equations in more detail.

To perform the path integral in the algebraic gauges, we can rely on
the discussion in Appendix
B
which shows that the ghost
triangle for the perpendicular fields can be disentangled from the
parallel fields (discussed in the previous section). Furthermore
we only need the right hand edge of the BV-triangle thanks to the
algebraic conditions.

The total action for the path integral of interest (including the
ghost terms and taking the algebraic conditions into account) is then

\displaystyle S_{\perp}=i\int_{M}\left(\langle B^{(p)}_{\perp},\,\kappa D_{\phi}C^{(q)}_{\perp}\rangle+\kappa\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}\langle\overline{\omega}^{(r)}_{\perp},\,*_{H}D_{\phi}\omega^{(r)}_{\perp}\rangle+\kappa\sum_{s=0}^{q-1}\langle\overline{\lambda}^{(s)}_{\perp},\,*_{H}D_{\phi}\lambda^{(s)}_{\perp}\rangle\right)

(47)

where the
(\omega_{\perp},\overline{\omega}_{\perp})
are the ghosts for
B_{\perp}
and

(\lambda_{\perp},\overline{\lambda}_{\perp})
are the ghosts for
C_{\perp}
all in the
appropriate representations. By making use
of the Hodge star operator (
105
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\kappa\wedge\langle C^{(q)}_{\perp},\,D_{\phi}B^{(p)}_{\perp}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\kappa\wedge\langle\overline{B}^{(p)}_{\perp},\,*_{H}D_{\phi}B^{(p)}_{\perp}\rangle

(48)

where
\overline{B}^{(p)}_{\perp}=*_{H}C^{(q)}_{\perp}
and
\kappa\wedge*_{H}C^{(q)}_{\perp}=*C^{(q)}_{\perp}
. By virtue of (
107
)
D_{\phi}

commutes with
*_{H}
in the integrals. Now all the terms in (
47
) have
the same form.

At least formally, we then
find that the partition function
takes the form of an infinite-dimensional determinant,

\displaystyle Z_{\perp}[\phi]=\mathop{\mathrm{Det}}\nolimits{D_{\phi}}|_{\Omega_{H}^{\perp}(M,E)}

(49)

where

\Omega_{H}^{\perp}(M,E)
is the direct (signed) sum of infinite-dimensional vector spaces

\displaystyle\Omega_{H}^{\perp}(M,E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{p-i+1}\Omega_{\perp}^{i}(M,E)+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(-1)^{q-j+1}\Omega_{\perp}^{j}(M,E)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{p-i+1}\Omega_{\perp}^{i}(M,E)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-p-1}(-1)^{n-p-j+1}\Omega_{\perp}^{n-j}(M,E)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\Omega_{\perp}^{i}(M,E)

(50)

(here
(-1)^{2r}
means
\oplus
while
(-1)^{2r+1}
stands for
\ominus
).
Note that the second equality holds by Hodge duality
*_{H}
so that
\Omega_{\perp}^{j}(M,E)\simeq\Omega_{\perp}^{n-j}(M,E)
and that
q=n-p
while the third equality comes on
setting
j-n=i
. Of course, in order to define the theory we still need to
specify the regularisation that will be used. All in all the last equality
then suggests that the
sum over infinite dimensional vector spaces may reduce to a sum over some finite
dimensional cohomology spaces. We cannot say more at this level of
generality. However, once we have specified the flat bundles
and the flat connections on them that we are interested in,
the formulae we find are indeed cohomological in nature.

6
Flat Connections on a Pull Back Bundle

\boldsymbol{E}=\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*}\boldsymbol{V}

In previous sections we have developed the path integral for the
partition function as far as possible without specifying either the vector bundle
E
or the
allowed flat connection
\mathcal{A}
on it. In this section we do not
aim for complete generality but, rather, choose a natural class of bundles and
connections.

Recall that the flatness equations
F_{\mathcal{A}}=0
by (
18
) are

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &F_{A}^{H}=-d\kappa\,\phi\\
&\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=d_{A}^{H}\phi\end{aligned}

(51)

As a first example consider a solution with
\phi=0
. In this case the
solutions satisfy
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A=0
and the connection
A
can
be thought of as a connection on a bundle on
N
. The first equation
of (
51
) then tells us that the connection
A
is a flat
connection. So the upshot is that in this case the bundle
E
is the
pullback of a flat vector bundle on
N
with flat connection on
E

being the pullback flat connection
\mathcal{A}=\pi^{*}A
. Of
course in the case at hand, from the results of the previous sections,
the partition function is given completely by
Z_{||}[A]
and only contains
information about the zero modes, the flat connection
A
playing no other
role.

A more general and more interesting situation, and the one that we concentrate on here, is
where we still have a pullback bundle but with
\phi\neq 0
. Let

V\rightarrow N
be a
G
vector bundle over
N
.
V
is taken to
be such that it admits a connection
A
which satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &F_{A}=-d\kappa\,\phi\\
&d_{A}\phi=0\end{aligned}

(52)

where
\phi\in\Omega^{0}(N,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P_{V})
and

P_{V}
is the associated principle bundle for
\phi\neq 0
. By
construction one admits a
\phi
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi=0

from the point of view of
M
.

Now fix the bundle
E=\pi^{*}V
and equip it with the connection

\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi
. This is not the pullback connection

\pi^{*}A\simeq A
which is not flat. However, as one can add to any
connection a Lie algebra valued 1-form and still have a connection,
we have added
\kappa\phi
to
A
and, by construction, we now have

\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi
. This connection is a flat connection on the pullback
bundle as (
51
) is equivalent to (
52
) for these fields.

We fix on
G=SU(r)
. Solutions to the equation
d_{A}v=0
with
v\in\Omega^{0}(N,\,\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P_{V})

span the Lie
algebra of the stabiliser of the connection
A
and a connection with
non-trivial stabiliser is reducible. The
first part of the flatness equation tells us that the curvature of
A

is non-zero only in the Abelian direction generated by
\phi
. In this
way we see that the connections of interest reduce to the form

K\times U(1)
and are flat in the
K
direction. The connection can be expressed as
A^{SU(r)}=A^{K}\oplus A^{U(1)}
with curvature 2-form that satisfies

\displaystyle F_{A^{K}}=0,\quad F_{A^{U(1)}}=-d\kappa\,\phi

(53)

With such a split, one has
[A,\phi]=0
so that the second equation of
(
52
) together with
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\phi=0
implies that

d\phi=0
on
M
, i.e. that
\phi
is constant. Note that not only
the connection
A
is reducible, but also the flat connection
\mathcal{A}=A+\kappa\phi
is, since

\displaystyle d_{A}\phi=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad d_{\mathcal{A}}\phi=0\;\;.

(54)

Note that, with
\phi
constant, the curvature formula

\displaystyle F_{A}=-\phi d\kappa=d(-\phi\kappa)

(55)

shows that all of the Chern-Weil representatives of the characteristic classes
of the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}V
on
M
are trivial. However, the bundle
V

on
N
may well be non-trivial. After all, on
N
one has
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{M})=[d\kappa]
. This will be seen in the examples below.

In the following, in order to illustrate the evaluation of the path
integral, we will not consider the most general reducible connection
but restrict our attention to reducible connections with
A^{K}=0

(though the more general case can also be dealt with), and we write

A^{U(1)}=A
. Moreover,
since
\phi
is constant we may as well take it to be diagonal. From
here on we do not carry around the
U(1)
superscript as it is
clear which component of
A
is non-zero.

Let
d\kappa
correspond to
p_{M}
times an integral class on
N
(for details
see Appendix
A
). Then (
53
) makes sense for

\phi
having any entries (as a matrix) of the form

2\pi ib_{j}/p_{M}
with the
b_{j}
integral and
\sum_{j=1}^{r}b_{j}=0
. The non-trivial
equations for
A
in (
53
) with
G

simply connected and
I(G)
the integral lattice of
G
then imply that,

\displaystyle\phi\in I(G)/p_{M}

(56)

For example, with
G=SU(2)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi=2\pi in/p_{M}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}

(57)

By
F_{A}=-d\kappa\phi
,
a
\phi
as in (
57
) implies that one has a non-trivial
complex line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{n}
on

N
with
U(1)
connection
A
on
\mathcal{L}
having integral real first Chern class
(cf. (
98
)) and (
99
))

\displaystyle c_{1}[\mathcal{L}]=[iF_{A}/2\pi]=1/p_{M}\,[\omega_{M}]\equiv[\omega]

(58)

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{M}=\mathcal{L}^{p_{M}}
where

\mathcal{L}_{M}
is the line bundle associated to the circle bundle
which is
M
. The
SU(2)

vector bundle
V\rightarrow N
defined by (
57
) is split according to

\displaystyle V=\mathcal{L}^{n}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{-n}

(59)

with total Chern class

\displaystyle c(V)=1-n^{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\wedge c_{1}(\mathcal{L})

(60)

Likewise for the complexified adjoint bundle

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathcal{L}^{0}\oplus\left(\mathcal{L}^{2n}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{-2n}\right),\quad c(\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P_{\mathbb{C}})=1-4n^{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\wedge c_{1}(\mathcal{L})

(61)

Consequently both
V
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ad}}\nolimits P_{\mathbb{C}}

these are obviously

N=\mathbb{P}^{m}
for

For
G=SU(r)
, given our choices for the flat connections, the vector
bundle

for a general representation is taken to split as

\displaystyle V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\;\mathcal{L}^{q_{i}},

(62)

and fields are direct sums of sections of
\Omega^{p}(N,\mathcal{L}^{q_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{r}_{M})
for

and

(see Fact

\displaystyle D_{\phi}\,\omega_{r,q}^{(p)}=(2\pi ir+2\pi iq/p_{M})\,\omega_{r,q}^{(p)}

and we see that zero modes occur precisely when

The determinant of the

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Det}}\nolimits{D_{\phi}}|_{\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}^{q})}\simeq\prod_{r\in\mathbf{Z}}(2\pi ir+2\pi iq/p_{M})|_{\Omega^{p}(N,\mathcal{L}^{q}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{r}_{M})}

Therefore, if

,

,
there is a zero mode eigenvalue. In particular, if

and is
to be deleted in the calculation for

\displaystyle q=0\bmod p_{M}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left(\mathop{\mathrm{Det}}\nolimits{D_{\phi}}|_{\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}^{q})}\right)_{\perp}\simeq\prod_{r\in\mathbf{Z}_{\neq 0}}(2\pi ir)|_{\Omega^{p}(N,\mathcal{L}^{r}_{M})}

Though strictly a contribution to

With the above understood, we now decompose the vector bundle

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &V=V_{\perp}\bigoplus V_{\parallel}&\\
&V_{\perp}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\,\mathcal{L}^{q_{i}}\quad&\text{for}\;q_{i}\neq 0\bmod\,p_{M}\\
&V_{\parallel}=\bigoplus_{i=k_{1}+1}^{k}\,\mathcal{L}^{q_{i}}\quad&\text{for}\;q_{i}=0\bmod p_{M}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle E_{\parallel}=\pi^{*}V_{\parallel},\quad E_{\perp}=\pi^{*}V_{\perp}

Evaluation of the Path Integral Representation of

Summarising the previous sections we note that (

\displaystyle\int D\Phi^{(p)}_{q}D\overline{\Phi}^{(p)}_{-q}\,\exp{\left(i\int_{M}\kappa\wedge\langle\overline{\Phi}^{(p)}_{-q},\,*_{H}D_{\phi}\Phi^{(p)}_{q}\rangle\right)}=(\mathop{\mathrm{Det}}\nolimits{D_{\phi}})^{\pm 1}|_{\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,\pi^{*}\mathcal{L}^{q})}

Appendix A
Some Background on the Geometry of
S^{1}
Bundles

Let
\pi:M\to N
be a smooth
S^{1}
-bundle over the smooth manifold
M
,
and denote by
\xi
the fundamental nowhere vanishing vector field generating the

S^{1}
-action on
M
. Writing a connection on this bundle as
(-2\pi i)\kappa

for
\kappa
a globally-defined 1-form on

, the condition that this is
a connection is equivalent to requiring that
\kappa
satisfies

\displaystyle\iota_{\xi}\kappa=1\quad,\quad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\kappa=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\iota_{\xi}d\kappa=0

(85)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}=\iota_{\xi}d+d\iota_{\xi}

(86)

is the Lie derivative. We choose one such
\kappa
in the following.

As always, the extra structure provided by a choice of connection
provides one with a notion of horizontality and, concretely, in the
case at hand allows one to create corresponding projection operators

\displaystyle P_{H}=\iota_{\xi}\,\kappa,\quad P_{V}=\kappa\,\iota_{\xi}

(87)

on differential forms. One easily checks that indeed

\displaystyle P_{H}+P_{V}=1,\quad P_{H}^{2}=P_{H},\quad P_{V}^{2}=P_{V},\quad P_{H}P_{V}=P_{V}P_{H}=0

(88)

as required for projectors.
Consequently, one can decompose differential forms
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M,\bullet)
as

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha^{(p)}+\kappa\,\alpha_{(p-1)}=P_{H}.\alpha+P_{V}.\alpha

(89)

where both “components” are horizontal, i.e.

\displaystyle\alpha^{(p)}=\iota_{\xi}(\kappa\wedge\alpha)\;\Rightarrow\;\iota_{\xi}\alpha^{(p)}=0\quad,\quad\alpha_{(p-1)}=\iota_{\xi}\alpha\;\Rightarrow\;\iota_{\xi}\alpha_{(p-1)}=0

(90)

One denotes the horizontal spaces of forms by
\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)
and,
by the decomposition (
89

\displaystyle\Omega^{p}(M)=P_{H}\Omega^{p}(M)\oplus P_{V}\Omega^{p}(M)\simeq\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)\oplus\Omega_{H}^{p-1}(M)

Likewise, one may project the action of the de Rham operator

\displaystyle d=d_{H}+\kappa\,D,\quad d_{H}=P_{H}\,d=\iota_{\xi}\kappa d,\quad\kappa D=P_{V}\,d,\quad D=\iota_{\xi}d

(92)

where

\displaystyle d_{H}:\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)\rightarrow\Omega_{H}^{p+1}(M),\quad D:\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)\rightarrow\Omega_{H}^{p}(M)

(93)

The horizontal de Rham operator on horizontal forms may be written as

\displaystyle d_{H}=d-\kappa\,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}

(94)

and on horizontal forms the vertical part of the exterior derivative
is the Lie derivative along the
S^{1}
vector field
\xi

\displaystyle D=\mathcal{L}_{\xi},\quad\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\equiv\iota_{\xi}d+d\iota_{\xi}

(95)

Unlike the de Rham operator the horizontal de Rham operator

to the Lie derivative with respect to the
S^{1}
vector field

\displaystyle d_{H}^{2}=-d\kappa\,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}

Therefore, if one further restricts to forms which are not only horizontal but

cohomology called basic cohomology where the horizontal exterior
derivative coincides with the de Rham operator by (

forms which are both horizontal and have vanishing Lie derivative by

. Then one has the following important results:

Fact 1

acting
on
\Omega_{H}^{*}(M,E)
. This split is implicitly understood with

\Psi\in\Omega^{p}_{H}(M,E)

\displaystyle\Psi=\Psi_{\perp}+\Psi_{\parallel},\quad\Psi_{\perp}\in\Omega_{\perp}^{p}(M,E),\quad\Psi_{\parallel}\in\Omega_{\parallel}^{p}(M,E)

(123)

(there is no difference between superscripts and subscripts of
\perp

and
\parallel
and the positions are chosen for legibility).

The form of the symmetries suggests we start with the fields at the
left of (
119
) and work our way to the right. The first
transformation is

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda^{(1)}=d_{A}^{H}\Lambda^{(0)},\quad\delta\Lambda_{(0)}=D_{\phi}\Lambda^{(0)}

(124)

Using
\Lambda^{(0)}_{\perp}
allows us to set
\Lambda_{(0)}^{\perp}=0
.
\Lambda^{(0)}_{\parallel}
is still
available and only enters in the variation of the parallel
components of
\Lambda^{(1)}
(we may then use a Landau type gauge for
these components). We are then left with
\Lambda^{(1)}_{\perp}
,
\Lambda^{(1)}_{\parallel}
and

\Lambda_{(0)}^{\parallel}
. These are the available
symmetries at the next step to the right of (
119
). It is
important to note that thus far the gauge fixing is on
\Lambda_{(0)}
.

On going one step to the right in (
119
) we have

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda^{(2)}=d_{A}^{H}\Lambda^{(1)}+d\kappa\,\Lambda_{(0)},\quad\delta\Lambda_{(1)}=D_{\phi}\Lambda^{(1)}-d_{A}^{H}\Lambda_{(0)}

(125)

Given that
\Lambda_{(0)}\in\Omega^{0}_{\parallel}(M,E)
then, as was the case with
\Lambda^{(0)}
, one may use

\Lambda^{(1)}_{\perp}
to impose the
condition that
\Lambda_{(1)}\in\Omega^{1}_{\parallel}(M,E)
(i.e.
\Lambda_{(1)}^{\perp}=0
). After
this gauge fixing the only free modes for

\Lambda^{(1)}
are its parallel components. The left over
transformations in (
125
) are all in
\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)
and all of the gauge
choices have, thus far, been on
\Lambda_{(1)}
.

This repeats at each stage so that the gauge conditions are on

\Lambda_{(q)}
while the symmetries that are left are in
\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)

and of the form

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda^{(q)}=d^{H}_{A}\Lambda^{(q-1)}+d\kappa\,\Lambda_{(q-2)},\quad\delta\Lambda_{(q-1)}=-d^{H}_{A}\Lambda_{(q-2)}

(126)

with all fields in
\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)
.

These conditions can now be imposed on the fields in
\Omega^{*}_{\perp}(M,E)
without the full might of the Batalin Vilkovisky
procedure as they are fully algebraic.
The typical gauge fixing term takes the form

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}Q\left(\langle\overline{\omega}^{(q)}_{\perp},\,*\omega_{(q)}^{\perp}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\langle\pi^{(q)}_{\perp},\,*\omega_{(q)}^{\perp}\rangle-\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\langle\overline{\omega}^{(q)}_{\perp},\,*\left(D_{\phi}\omega^{(q)}_{\perp}-d_{A}^{H}\omega_{(q-1)}^{\perp}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\langle\pi^{(q)}_{\perp},\,*\omega_{(q)}^{\perp}\rangle-\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\langle\overline{\omega}^{(q)}_{\perp},\,*D_{\phi}\omega^{(q)}_{\perp}\rangle

(127)

where
\omega_{(p-1)}^{\perp}=B_{(p-1)}^{\perp}
,
\omega_{(-1)}^{\perp}=0
and in the second line we
have used the delta function constraints imposed by the multiplier
fields that the
\omega_{(q)}^{\perp}=0
. There are no differential operators that
act on the multiplier fields
and we have chosen
D_{\phi}
to have no zero modes on the perpendicular spaces so that neither

\pi_{\perp}
nor
\overline{\omega}_{\perp}
have any extra symmetry here.
Consequently, no extra ghosts for ghosts (and their associated fields) are required
to compensate such redundant symmetries.

Of course one could have also used the complete Batalin-Vilkovisky
algorithm and the complete set of fields in the Batalin-Vilkovisky
triangle to arrive at the same result in the end. Here is an example
to illustrate this.

Example B.1
.

We show here how the complete Batalin-Vilkovisky
triangle leads to (
127
) for a 2-form
B
for the part
in
\Omega^{*}_{\perp}(M,E)
. The ghost system
is

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llcrr}&&B&&\\
&\overline{\omega}&&\omega&\\
\beta^{\prime}&&\overline{\beta}&&\beta\end{array}\longrightarrow\begin{array}[]{rrcll}&&B^{(2)}+\kappa B_{(1)}&&\\
&\overline{\omega}^{(1)}+\kappa\overline{\omega}_{(0)}&&\omega^{(1)}+\kappa\omega_{(0)}&\\
\omega^{\prime(0)}&&\overline{\omega}^{(0)}&&\omega^{(0)}\end{array}

The nilpotent BRST transformations are then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &QB^{(2)}=d_{A}^{H}\omega^{(1)}+d\kappa\,\omega_{(0)},\quad QB_{(1)}=D_{\phi}\omega^{(1)}-d_{A}^{H}\omega_{(0)}\\
&Q\omega^{(1)}=d_{A}^{H}\,\omega^{(0)},\quad Q\omega_{(0)}=D_{\phi}\,\omega^{(0)},\quad Q\omega^{(0)}=0\\
&Q\overline{\omega}^{(1)}=\pi^{(1)},\quad Q\overline{\omega}_{(0)}=\pi_{(0)},\quad Q\pi^{(1)}=0,\quad Q\pi_{(0)}=0\\
&Q\omega^{\prime(0)}=\pi^{\prime(0)},\quad Q\pi^{\prime(0)}=0,\quad Q\overline{\omega}^{(0)}=\pi^{(0)},\quad Q\pi^{(0)}=0.\end{aligned}\

(135)

The fields that are not present in (
127
) are
(\omega^{\prime(0)},\,\pi^{\prime(0)},\overline{\omega}_{(0)},\pi_{(0)})
so one needs to append to
(
127
) terms involving this set. However, we have already
achieved the gauge fixing that is available to us (in
\Omega^{*}_{\perp}(M,E)
) and none
of the quadruplet transform with derivatives so one may simply add
the ghost number neutral term

\displaystyle Q\langle\omega^{\prime(0)}_{\perp},\,*\overline{\omega}_{(0)}^{\perp}\rangle=\langle\pi^{\prime(0)}_{\perp},\,*\overline{\omega}_{(0)}^{\perp}\rangle-\langle\omega^{\prime(0)}_{\perp},\,*\pi_{(0)}^{\perp}\rangle

(136)

which shows that one is allowed to ignore these fields.

The path integral should not depend on the metric that is chosen for
the gauge fixing conditions, since it always enters in a BRST exact
term, so that one may use this freedom to pick a metric on
M
adapted
to the needs of the problem. The metric of choice here on
M
is

g_{M}
as given in (
102
). In particular between horizontal
fields the Hodge star operator is
*_{H}
(
105
).

There are also symmetries that do not act, corresponding to zero modes
that is to elements of
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,E)
. For
example, the fields
\Lambda_{(q-2)}^{\parallel}
and
\Lambda^{(q-1)}_{\parallel}
with
d^{H}_{A}\Lambda_{(q-2)}^{\parallel}=0

and
d\kappa\Lambda_{(q-2)}^{\parallel}=d_{A}^{H}\Lambda^{(q-1)}_{\parallel}
correspond to
zero modes of the covariant derivative and they do not appear in
(
126
). The treatment of such modes using a BRST analysis can
be found in
BT-BF
;
BKT
and we do not repeat that here.

The symmetries that remain unaccounted for, those that appear in
(
126
), are all in

\Omega^{*}_{\parallel}(M,E)
and we should employ the complete
Batalin-Vilkovisky triangle for these when gauge fixing them.

Example B.2
.

We
continue Example
B.1
to include the parallel sector in order to exhibit the relevant
features. The transformations are (we drop the parallel sub- and super-scripts)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &QB^{(2)}=d_{A}^{H}\omega^{(1)}+d\kappa\,\omega_{(0)},\quad QB_{(1)}=-d_{A}^{H}\omega_{(0)}\\
&Q\omega^{(1)}=d_{A}^{H}\,\omega^{(0)},\quad Q\omega_{(0)}=0,\quad Q\omega^{(0)}=0\\
&Q\overline{\omega}^{(1)}=\pi^{(1)},\quad Q\overline{\omega}_{(0)}=\pi_{(0)},\quad Q\pi^{(1)}=0,\quad Q\pi_{(0)}=0\\
&Q\omega^{\prime(0)}=\pi^{\prime(0)},\quad Q\pi^{\prime(0)}=0,\quad Q\overline{\omega}^{(0)}=\pi^{(0)},\quad Q\pi^{(0)}=0.\end{aligned}

(137)

and in order to gauge fix the non-harmonic modes we form the action

\displaystyle\int_{M}\kappa\;

\displaystyle Q\left(\langle\overline{\omega}^{(1)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}B^{(2)}\rangle+\langle\overline{\omega}_{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}B_{(1)}\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\langle\overline{\omega}^{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\omega^{(1)}\rangle+\langle\omega^{\prime(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\overline{\omega}^{(1)}\rangle\right)

(138)

The gauge fixing terms are

\displaystyle\int_{M}\kappa\;

\displaystyle\left(\langle\pi^{(1)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}B^{(2)}\rangle+\langle\pi_{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}B_{(1)}\rangle+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\langle\pi^{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\omega^{(1)}\rangle+\langle\pi^{\prime(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\overline{\omega}^{(1)}\rangle-\langle\omega^{\prime(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}\pi^{(1)}\rangle\right)

(139)

while the ‘ghost’ terms are

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\kappa\;\left(\langle\overline{\omega}^{(1)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}(d_{A}^{H}\omega^{(1)}+d\kappa\,\omega_{(0)})\rangle+\langle\overline{\omega}_{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}d_{A}^{H}\omega_{(0)}\rangle-\langle\overline{\omega}^{(0)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}d_{A}^{H}\omega^{(0)}\rangle\right)

(140)

There is only one mixing term between the ghosts of the two BV
triangles associated with
B^{(2)}
and
B_{(1)}
which in combined
form appear in (
B.1
), and it appears in the combination

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\kappa\;\langle\overline{\omega}^{(1)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}d\kappa\,\omega_{(0)})\rangle

(141)

Note that as the gauge fixing condition on
\omega^{(1)}
and the
integral over
\overline{\omega}^{(1)}
imply that
\omega^{(1)}
is
harmonic the mixing term (
141
) vanishes
regardless of the properties of
\omega_{(0)}
which
means that one could just as well set the mixing term

\langle\overline{\omega}^{(1)},\,d_{A}^{H}*_{H}d\kappa\,\omega_{(0)}\rangle
to
zero from the outset. All in all this means that we could have set the
d\kappa

term in the gauge symmetries to zero to be left with two independent
and standard sets of gauge symmetries for
B^{(2)}
and
B_{(1)}

respectively at this level. But now we must address the harmonic
modes as every field in this sector potentially
has harmonic modes and in particular we must determine which of those
modes is part of the symmetry group and which is actually a zero
mode. This questions devolves in this case to understanding the
harmonic modes of
\omega_{(0)}
as they are the ones that will
appear in the gauge algebra. Let
\widehat{d\kappa}
be the harmonic, with respect to
\Delta_{H}
,
part of
d\kappa
. If
\omega_{(0)}
is harmonic then so
too is
\widehat{d\kappa}\,\omega_{(0)}
as it is obviously closed with
respect to
d_{A}^{H}
while
d_{A}^{H}*_{H}(\widehat{d\kappa}\,\omega_{(0)})=(d_{H}*\widehat{d\kappa})\,\omega_{(0)}=0
. As
\widehat{d\kappa}\,\omega_{(0)}
is harmonic it is part of the gauge symmetry and we
can use it to partially fix some of the harmonic modes of

B^{(2)}
. To that end add to the action

\displaystyle Q\int_{M}\kappa\,\langle\widehat{d\kappa}\,\overline{\omega}_{(0)},*_{H}B^{(2)}\rangle=\int_{M}\kappa\,\left(\langle\widehat{d\kappa}\,\pi_{(0)},*_{H}B^{(2)}\rangle-\widehat{d\kappa}*_{H}\widehat{d\kappa}\,\langle\overline{\omega}_{(0)},\omega_{(0)}\rangle\right)

(142)

where all the fields in (
142
) are harmonic. Note that this
gauge fixing term is now the only one that mixes the BV triangles. All the other
harmonic modes of
B^{(2)}
and of the other fields are zero modes and
may dealt with as such
Ray-Singer-Analytic
;
BKT
.

Turning to the general situation for the parallel fields,
we can split off the BV triangles
for the field
B^{(p)}
and that of
B_{(p-1)}
, even though they are
not independent due to the mixing terms. All the covariant gauge fixing is, as
above, with

However, integrating out the

sets all the ghost fields to be harmonic- including the ones that would make

triangle. Up to this

terms completely and the
triangles are essentially independent. This is exactly what we found
in Section

to treat the harmonic modes one must first ascertain if they are part of
the gauge symmetry or not. If they are part of the gauge symmetry then a gauge fixing of the
type (

gauge symmetry then they are zero modes and one may follow

Ray-Singer-Analytic
;

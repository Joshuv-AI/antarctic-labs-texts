Abstract
. We give an elementary proof of an efficient version of the Wagner’s theorem on almost invariant subspaces (see [ 5 ] ) and deduce some consequences in the context of Galois extensions.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this note is to present an elementary proof of the following linear algebra result
and to consider its consequences in the context of Galois extensions (see Theorem B). In
[
3
]
this result is applied to the study of the Schmidt rank of quartic polynomials.

Theorem A
.
Let
V
be a vector space over a field
\mathbf{k}
,
X
a collection of subspaces of
V
,
G
a group acting linearly on
V
and preserving
X
setwise.
For
x\in X
we denote by
A_{x}\subset V
the corresponding subspace.
Assume that for some
r\geq 1
the following condition holds: for any
x,y\in X
one has
\dim A_{x}/(A_{x}\cap A_{y})\leq r
.
Then there exists a
G
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
, which is a finite sum of some finite intersections of subspaces from
X
,
such that

\dim W/(W\cap A_{x})\leq r,\ \ \dim A_{x}/(W\cap A_{x})\leq r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1}.

Originally, this theorem was proved by Wagner
[
5
]
, using model theory and without an explicit bound on
\dim A_{x}/(W\cap A_{x})
.
To get an explicit bound we combine Wagner’s proof with the idea of the proof of Neumann’s explicit bound in Bergman-Lenstra’s theorem on almost normal subgroups (see
[
1
]
). We conjecture that it should be possible to improve the bound
r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1}

to a polynomial (possibly linear) function of
r
, at least in the case when
G
is finite.

In section 3 we show that in the special case when
G
is a finite group interchanging vectors of a basis, and subspaces are spanned by subsets of this bases, one can replace
r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1}
by
2r
. This reduces to the known set-theoretic result
(see
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
), for which we give a new short proof assuming that
G
is finite (see Theorem
3.1
).
Our version of this result suggests a formula for the approximating
G
-invariant subspace, for which a better bound (polynomial or even linear in
r
) might hold, see Sec.
3
for a discussion.

As a corollary of Theorem A, we obtain the following result about linear subspaces and linear operators that are almost invariant
under the action of the Galois group. This result will be used in our forthcoming study of the Schmidt rank of quartics over non-closed fields.

Theorem B
.
Let
E/\mathbf{k}
be a finite Galois extension with the Galois group
G
, and let
V_{0}
be a finite dimensional
\mathbf{k}
-vector space.
Let us set
V=V_{0}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}E
. We consider the natural action of
G
on
V
and the induced action of
G
on the set of

E
-linear subspaces of
V
and on
{\operatorname{End}}_{E}(V)
.

(i) Suppose
A\subset V
is an
E
-linear subspace such that for each
\sigma\in G
, one has

\dim_{E}(A/(A\cap\sigma A))\leq r,

for some
r\geq 0
. Then there exists a
\mathbf{k}
-linear subspace
W_{0}\subset V_{0}
such that for

W=W_{0}\otimes E\subset V
one has

\dim_{E}(W/(W\cap A))\leq r,\ \ \dim_{E}(A/(W\cap A))\leq r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1}.

(ii) Suppose
V^{\prime}_{0}
is another finite dimensional
\mathbf{k}
-vector space,
V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{0}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}E
, and

T:V\to V^{\prime}
is an
E
-linear operator such that for any
\sigma\in G
, one has

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(\sigma(T)-T)\leq r,

for some
r\geq 0
.
Then there exists a
\mathbf{k}
-linear operator
T_{0}:V_{0}\to V^{\prime}_{0}
, such that

{\operatorname{rk}}_{E}(T-(T_{0})_{E})\leq 2r+r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1},

where
(T_{0})_{E}:V\to V^{\prime}
is obtained from
T_{0}
by the extension of scalars.

. We are grateful to Ehud Hrushovski for pointing out the references and for useful discussions.

2.
Linear algebra results

2.1.
Proof of Theorem A

Notation: for a subset
S\subset X
we set
A_{S}:=\cap_{x\in S}A_{x}
.

For each
m
,
0\leq m\leq r
, let
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
denote the set of all nonempty subsets
S\subset X
such that

\dim(A_{S}+A_{x})/A_{x}\leq m\ \text{ for any }x\in X.

Note that
S=X
is contained in
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{m-1}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
.
Set

h(m)=\min_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}}|S|

which is either a natural number or
\infty
. Note that by assumption
h(r)=1
. We also have
h(m-1)\geq h(m)
.

We consider separately two cases.

Case 1
. There exists
m
,
1\leq m\leq r
, such that
h(m)
is finite and
h(m-1)>(r+1)h(m)+1
. Let us take the maximal
m
with this property
and set

W:=\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}:|S|=h(m)}A_{S}.

Since for all
m^{\prime}>m
we have
h(m^{\prime}-1)\leq(r+1)h(m^{\prime})+1
, we get

h(m)\leq(r+1)^{r-m}+\ldots+(r+1)^{2}+(r+1)+1\leq(r+1)^{r}.

Note that since
A_{S}\subset W
for some
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
with
|S|=h(m)
, we have for any
x\in X
,

\dim A_{x}/A_{x}\cap W\leq\dim A_{x}/A_{x}\cap A_{S}\leq r\cdot h(m)\leq r\cdot(r+1)^{r}.

Next, we claim that for any
x\in X
one has
\dim W/W\cap A_{x}\leq r
. Indeed, suppose there exists
x\in X
such that

\dim(W+A_{x})/A_{x}\geq r+1
. Then there exist
r+1
subsets
S_{1},\ldots,S_{r+1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
with
|S_{i}|=h(m)
, such that

\dim(\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}A_{S_{i}}+A_{x})/A_{x}\geq r+1.

Let us consider the subset

T:=\{x\}\cup S_{1}\cup\ldots\cup S_{r+1}\subset X.

Then we have
|T|\leq(r+1)h(m)+1<h(m-1)
. Hence,
T\not\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m-1}
, so there exists an element
y\in X
such that

\dim(A_{T}+A_{y})/A_{y}\geq m.

But for any
i=1\ldots,r+1
, we have

(A_{T}+A_{y})/A_{y}\subset(A_{S_{i}}+A_{y})/A_{y},

and the latter space has dimension
\leq m
by the definition of
{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
. Hence, we have

A_{T}+A_{y}=A_{S_{i}}+A_{y}
for any
i
. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}A_{S_{i}}\subset A_{T}+A_{y}\subset A_{x}+A_{y},

so

\dim(\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}A_{S_{i}}+A_{x})/A_{x}\leq\dim(A_{x}+A_{y})/A_{x}\leq r,

which is a condtradiction.

Finally, we note that since
W/(W\cap A_{S})
is finite dimensional, for any
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m}
,
W
can be written as a finite sum of some
intersections
A_{S}
.

Case 2
. For each
m=1,\ldots,r
one has
h(m-1)\leq(r+1)h(m)+1
. This implies that

h(0)\leq(r+1)^{r}+\ldots+(r+1)^{2}+(r+1)+1\leq(r+1)^{r+1}.

Note that
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
consists of
S
such that
A_{S}=\cap_{x\in X}A_{x}
.
In this case we set

W:=\cap_{x\in X}A_{x}.

Then since there exists a subset
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
with
|S|=h(0)
, we get

\dim A_{x}/A_{x}\cap W=\dim A_{x}/A_{x}\cap A_{S}\leq r\cdot h(0)\leq r(r+1)^{r+1}.

This ends the proof.

2.2.
Almost invariant operators

In this section we use Theorem A to approximate almost
G
-invariant operators by
G
-invariant ones by considering their graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be linear representation of a group
G
, such that any
G
-invariant subspace
W\subset V
(resp.,
W^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
) of finite codimension (resp., dimension)
admits a
G
-invariant complement.
Assume that
T:V\to V^{\prime}
is a linear operator such that for any
g\in G
, one has
{\operatorname{rk}}(gTg^{-1}-g)\leq r
, for some
r\geq 0
.
Then there exists a
G
-invariant operator
T_{0}:V\to V^{\prime}
such that

{\operatorname{rk}}(T-T_{0})\leq 2r+r\cdot(r+1)^{r+1}.

Proof.

Let
A=A_{T}\subset V\oplus V^{\prime}
denote the graph of
T
, i.e.,
A=\{(v,Tv)\}
. Note that if
A
and
A^{\prime}
are graphs of
T
and
T^{\prime}
then

T\cap T^{\prime}=\{(v,Tv)\ |\ v\in\ker(T-T^{\prime})\}
, so

\dim T/T\cap T^{\prime}={\operatorname{rk}}(T-T^{\prime}).

Thus, the assumption on
T
implies that
\dim(A/(A\cap gA))\leq r
for all
g\in G
. Applying Theorem A,
we obtain a
G
-invariant subspace
A_{0}\subset V\oplus V^{\prime}
such that

\dim A_{0}/(A_{0}\cap A)\leq r,\ \ \dim A/(A_{0}\cap A)\leq r(r+1)^{r+1}.

Let
p_{1}:V\oplus V^{\prime}\to V
and
p_{2}:V\oplus V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}
be the projections.
Set
K:=\{v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}\ |\ (0,v^{\prime})\in A_{0}\}\subset V^{\prime}
and
I:=p_{1}(A_{0})\subset V
.
Note that the subspaces
K
and
I
are
G
-invariant, and
p_{2}:A_{0}\to V^{\prime}
induces a well defined
G
-invariant linear map

\overline{T}_{0}:I\to V^{\prime}/K,

such that
A_{0}
is the pull-back of the graph
\overline{A}_{0}\subset I\oplus V^{\prime}/K
of
\overline{T}_{0}
under the projection
\pi:I\oplus V^{\prime}\to I\oplus V^{\prime}/K
.
Furthermore, we have

\dim K\leq r,\ \ \dim V/I\leq r(r+1)^{r+1}.

Hence, we can find a
G
-linear projector
p_{I}:V\to I
and a
G
-linear projector
p_{C}:V^{\prime}\to C\subset V^{\prime}
, where
C
is a
G
-invariant
complement to
K
, such that
p_{C}(K)=0
. Now we set

T_{0}=p_{C}\circ\overline{T}_{0}\circ p_{I}.

Note that
\overline{T}_{0}
is obtained as the composition of
T_{0}|_{I}
with the projection
V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}/K
.
Let
\overline{T}:I\to V^{\prime}/K
denote the composition of
T|_{I}
with the projection
V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}/K
, and let
\overline{A}\subset I\oplus V^{\prime}/K
denote the graph of

\overline{T}
. It is easy to see that
\pi
induces a surjection

A_{0}/(A_{0}\cap A)\to\overline{A}_{0}/(\overline{A}_{0}\cap\overline{A}),

so we get

{\operatorname{rk}}(\overline{T}-\overline{T}_{0})=\dim\overline{A}_{0}/(\overline{A}_{0}\cap\overline{A})\leq r.

But
\overline{T}-\overline{T}_{0}
is obtained from
T-T_{0}
by restricting to the subspace
I\subset V
and composing with the projection
V^{\prime}\to V^{\prime}/K
,
so we get

{\operatorname{rk}}(T-T_{0})\leq\dim K+\dim V/I+{\operatorname{rk}}(\overline{T}-\overline{T}_{0})\leq 2r+r(r+1)^{r+1}.

∎

2.3.
Proof of Theorem B

(i) Let us apply Theorem A to the collection
(\sigma A)
of all Galois conjugates of
A
.
Note that the action of
G
is only
\mathbf{k}
-linear, so we should view this is a collection of
\mathbf{k}
-linear subspaces, and use
the dimension function
\dim_{\mathbf{k}}=[E:\mathbf{k}]\cdot\dim_{E}
.
However, the resulting
G
-invariant
\mathbf{k}
-subspace
W
is in the lattice generated by
(\sigma A)
, so it is actually
an
E
-linear subspace. But
G
-invariant
E
-linear subspaces of
V
are precisely subspaces obtained
by extension of scalars from
\mathbf{k}
-linear subspaces of
V_{0}
. This gives the statement.

(ii) We apply the same strategy as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
First, we find a
G
-invariant
E
-subspace
W
approximating the graph
A
of
T
. Then we use the fact that
W
is an extension
of scalars from
W_{0}\subset V_{0}\oplus V^{\prime}_{0}
and construct
\mathbf{k}
-linear projectors
p_{C}:V^{\prime}_{0}\to C\subset V^{\prime}_{0}
and
p_{I}:V_{0}\to I
,
as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, where
I=p_{1}(W_{0})
and
C
is a complement in
V^{\prime}_{0}
to
(0\oplus V^{\prime}_{0})\cap W_{0}
.
This gives the required operator defined over
\mathbf{k}
.

3.
Almost invariant subsets

The following result is a more concrete version of a theorem of Neumann
[
4
]
(see also
[
2
]
for related results),
stating that if a subset
A
in a
G
-set
X
satisfies
|A\setminus gA|\leq r
for every
g\in G
and some
r>0
, then there exists
a
G
-invariant subset
A_{0}\subset X
such that

|A\setminus A_{0}|+|A_{0}\setminus A|<2r.

We make an extra assumption that
G
is finite but as a bonus we get an explicit construction of
A_{0}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a set with action of a finite group
G
. Let
A\subset X
be a subset such that for any
g\in G
one has

|A\setminus gA|\leq r
for some
r>0
. For every subset
S\subset G
we denote
A_{S}:=\cap_{g\in S}gA.

Now consider the
G
-invariant subset

A_{0}:=\cup_{|S|>|G|/2}A_{S}.

Then

|A\setminus A_{0}|+|A_{0}\setminus A|\leq 2r.

Proof.

We have

A\setminus A_{0}=\cup_{|S|>|G|/2}A\setminus A_{S}=\{a\in A\ |\ |\{g\in G\ |\ ga\in A\}|\leq|G|/2\}.

Now let us consider the set

P:=\{(a\in A,g\in G)\ |\ ga\not\in A\}.

Considering the fibers of the projection of
P
to
G
and using the assumption, we see that

|P|\leq r\cdot|G|.

Now, let us consider the projection
p_{A}:P\to A
.
For every
a\in A\setminus A_{0}
, we have

|p_{A}^{-1}(a)|=|\{g\in G\ |\ ga\not\in A\}|\geq\frac{|G|}{2}.

Hence, setting

P_{1}:=p_{A}^{-1}(A\setminus A_{0}),

we get

\frac{|G|}{2}\cdot|A\setminus A_{0}|\leq|P_{1}|.

(1)

Next, setting
B=X\setminus A
, we observe that

A_{0}\setminus A=A_{0}\cap B=\cup_{|S|>|G|/2}A_{S}\cap B=\{b\in B\ |\ |\{g\in G\ |\ gb\in A\}|>|G|/2\}.

Let us consider the projection

p_{B}:P\to B:(a,g)\mapsto ga

and set

P_{2}:=p_{B}^{-1}(A_{0}\cap B).

Since for every
b\in A_{0}\cap B
, we have

|p_{B}^{-1}(b)|=|\{g\in G\ |\ g^{-1}b\in A\}|>\frac{|G|}{2},

we deduce that

\frac{|G|}{2}\cdot|A_{0}\cap B|\leq|P_{2}|.

(2)

Finally, we claim that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
do not intersect. Indeed, suppose

(a,g_{0})\in P_{1}\cap P_{2}
. Then

|\{g\in G\ |\ ga\not\in A\}|\geq\frac{|G|}{2}

and

|\{g\in G\ |\ gg_{0}a\in A\}|>\frac{|G|}{2},

which is impossible since

|\{g\in G\ |\ gg_{0}a\in A\}|=|\{g\in G\ |\ ga\in A\}|=|G|-|\{g\in G\ |\ gg_{0}a\in A\}|.

Thus, combining (
1
) and (
2
), we get

\frac{|G|}{2}\cdot(|A\setminus A_{0}|+|A_{0}\setminus A|)\leq|P_{1}|+|P_{2}|\leq|P|\leq r\cdot|G|,

which gives

|A\setminus A_{0}|+|A_{0}\setminus A|\leq 2r.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

One can see from the proof that the only case when we possibly do not get a strict inequality

|A\setminus A_{0}|+|A_{0}\setminus A|<2r

is when
A_{0}\subset A
and
P_{1}=P
, which is equivalent to
A_{S}=\cap_{g\in G}gA
whenever
|S|>|G|/2
.
In this case we can replace
A_{0}
with

A^{\prime}_{0}:=\cup_{|S|\geq|G|/2}A_{S}.

Since for any
S\subset G\setminus\{1\}
with
|S|\geq|G|/2
, we have
A\cap A_{S}=\cap_{g\in G}gA
,
assuming that
A
is not
G
-invariant, we get
A\setminus A^{\prime}_{0}\neq\emptyset
.
In this case, running the similar argument to the above proof, we get that

|A\setminus A^{\prime}_{0}|+|A^{\prime}_{0}\setminus A|<2r.

Theorem
3.1
suggests that in the linear algebra setup with
A
a linear subspace of a
G
-representation
V
,
such that
\dim(A/A\cap gA)\leq r
, for finite
G
, one can try to define the approximating
G
-invariant subspace as

A_{0}:=\sum_{|S|>|G|/2}A_{S},

(3)

where
A_{S}=\cap_{g\in S}gA
.

Question.

Does there exist a polynomial (or even a linear) function
c(r)

such that for
(V,G,A)
as above and
A_{0}
given by (
3
) we have

\dim A/(A_{0}\cap A)\leq c(r)\ \text{ and }\ \dim A_{0}/(A_{0}\cap A)\leq c(r)?

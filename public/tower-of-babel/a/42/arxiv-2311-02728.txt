Generalized Fourier quasicrystals, almost periodic sets, and zeros of Dirichlet series
Sergii Yu.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
S(z)
be an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with a bounded spectrum and only real zeros
a_{n},\,n=1,2,\dots
, let
\mu
be the sum of unit masses at points
a_{n}
. It is proven that the Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
in the sense of distributions is a purely point measure. Conversely, in terms of the properties of
\hat{\mu}
, a sufficient condition was found when
a_{n}
are zeros of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with bounded spectrum. Also, in terms of the properties of
\hat{\mu}
, a criterion is established that
a_{n}
are zeros of an almost periodic entire function of exponential growth. In all cases, the multiplicity of zeros is taken into account.

Almost periodic sets, introduced by B.Levin and M.Krein in 1948, play important role in our investigations. In particular, we show a new simple representation of such sets.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 42A75, 42A38, 52C23

Keywords: Fourier quasicrystal, Fourier transform in the sense of distributions, pure point measure, almost periodic entire function,
almost periodic sets, zero set of entire functions

1.
Introduction

A crystalline measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a complex measure with discrete locally finite support, which is a temperate distribution and its distributional Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}

is also a measure with locally finite support; if, in addition, the measures
|\mu|
and
|\hat{\mu}|
are temperate distributions, then
\mu
is called the Fourier quasicrystal.

The Fourier quasicrystal may be considered as a mathematical model for atomic arrangement
having a discrete diffraction pattern. There are a lot of papers devoted to study properties of Fourier quasicrystals or, more generally, crystalline measures.
For example, one can mark collections of papers
[
2
]
,
[
17
]
, in particular, the basic paper
[
12
]
.

Measures of the form

(1)

\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda},\qquad c_{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{N}},

are the most important case of Fourier quasicrystal. Recently A. Olevsky and A. Ulanovsky
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
showed that the supports
\Lambda
of these measures are zero sets of exponential polynomials with purely imaginary exponents and only real zeros with multiplicities
c_{\lambda}
at points
\lambda\in\Lambda
and, conversely, the zero sets of such exponential polynomials are supports of some Fourier quasicrystals of the form (
1
).

In our article, using the same methods, we present analogues results for measures (
1
) with the distribution Fourier transform

(2)

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma},

where
\Gamma
is an arbitrary countable set. In this case the corresponding Poisson’s formula

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}\hat{f}(\lambda)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}f(\gamma).

also takes place for every function
f
from Schwarz’s class. In order to describe such measures, we use the concept of almost periodic sets, which was introduced
by M. Krein and B. Levin (
[
10
]
, App. VI). In modern notations (cf.
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
), a locally finite set
\Lambda
with multiplicities
c_{\lambda}
at points
\lambda\in\Lambda

is almost periodic, if the convolution of measure (
1
) with every continuous function with compact support is an almost periodic function.
We will write an almost periodic set like the sequence
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where each point
a_{n}=\lambda
occurs
c_{\lambda}
times. Therefore, almost periodic sets are in fact multisets.

In Section
2
we give the original definition of almost periodic sets given by Krein and Levin, which is equivalent to the one given above.
Also we prove some properties of almost periodic sets, in particular, show that such sets
have the form
\{\alpha n+\phi(n)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
\alpha>0
and an almost periodic mapping
\phi:\,{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

In Section
3
we consider an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with bounded spectrum and a real set of zeros. We prove that the Fourier transform of the measure
\mu_{A}
corresponding to such a set
A
is always a purely point measure. Note that the zero set of any absolutely convergent Dirichlet series (or, more general, any almost periodic holomorphic function and even any holomorphic function with almost periodic modulus) is almost periodic (cf.
[
8
]
).

In section
4
we investigate the inverse problem. We consider a measure
\mu
of form (
1
) with the Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
of form (
2
) and find sufficient conditions
for which
A
is the zero set of an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with bounded spectrum.

Here we assume that the measure
|\hat{\mu}|
is a temperate distribution. By
[
5
]
, Lemma 1, the multiset
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where each point
a_{n}=\lambda
occurs
c_{\lambda}
times, is almost periodic.
In
[
8
]
it was proven that every almost periodic set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is exactly the zero set of some entire almost periodic function. Every almost periodic function is bounded on the real line,
hence, by the Phragmen-Lindelöf principle, any almost periodic entire function cannot grow less than exponentially. We find a criterion for
A
to be the zero set
of an almost periodic entire function of the exponential growth in terms of
\gamma
and
\beta_{\gamma}
from (
2
).

2.
Almost periodic sets

Definition 1

(for example, see
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

A continuous function
g(x)
on the real line
is almost periodic if for any
\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|g(x+\tau)-g(x)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense, i.e.,
E_{\varepsilon}\cap(x,x+L)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and some
L
depending on
\varepsilon
.

For example every sum

Q(x)=\sum q_{n}e^{2\pi ix\omega_{n}},\quad\omega_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad q_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\sum_{n}|q_{n}|<\infty,

is an almost periodic function.

Spectrum of an almost periodic function
g
is the set

\spec g=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}e^{-2\pi i\omega x}g(x)dx\neq 0\right\}.

It is easy to see that
\spec Q(x)=\{\omega_{n}:\,q_{n}\neq 0\}
. Note that spectra of almost periodic functions are at most countable.

Definition 2

(see
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

A continuous function
g(z)
on a strip

S=\{z=x+iy:\,-\infty\leq a<y<b\leq+\infty\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

is almost periodic if for any
\alpha,\,\beta
such that
[\alpha,\beta]\subset(a,b)
and

\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\leq y\leq\beta}|g(x+\tau+iy)-g(x+iy)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense, i.e.,
E_{\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon}\cap(x,x+L)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and some
L
depending on
\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta
.

Just as it was done in
[
10
]
, we could define an almost periodic set in a strip. Here we give
this definition only in the simplified form. The general definition, which takes into account the behavior of
A
near the boundary, was given by H.Tornehave in
[
19
]
.
The connection between almost periodic sets of general form and zeros of holomorphic almost periodic functions in terms of Chern cohomology see
[
3
]
.

Definition 3

(M. Krein and B. Levin
[
10
]
, App. VI)
.

Let
S
be a horizontal strip of a finite width. A discrete locally finite multiset
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset S
is almost periodic if for any
\varepsilon>0
the set of its
\varepsilon
-almost periods

(3)

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\exists\ \text{ a bijection }\sigma:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{such that}\ \sup_{n}|a_{n}+\tau-a_{\sigma(n)}|<\varepsilon\}

has nonempty intersection with every interval
(x,x+L_{\varepsilon})
.

In our article we consider, as a rule, only the case of almost periodic sets on the real line.

Set
\mu_{A}=\sum_{n}\delta_{a_{n}}
. Clearly, the mass of
\mu_{A}
in any point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
is equal to the multiplicity of this point in the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

It was proved in
[
8
]
that almost periodicity of
A
is equivalent to almost periodicity of convolution
\mu_{A}\star\varphi
for every
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi(x),\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
with compact support. But it is easy to replace
C^{\infty}
-functions by continuous functions with compact support.

Indeed, take
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi\geq 0
such that
\varphi(x)\equiv 1
for
0<x<1
. If
\mu_{A}\star\varphi
is almost periodic, then it is uniformly bounded, hence

\mu_{A}[x,x+1]<k_{1}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with some constant
k_{1}
. For any continuous function
\psi
with support in
(0,1)
one can take
\varphi\in C^{\infty}
such that

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|\psi(x)-\varphi(x)|<\varepsilon/k_{1}
. We obtain that every
\varepsilon
-almost period of
\mu_{A}\star\varphi
is
2\varepsilon
-almost period of
\mu_{A}\star\psi
.

By the way, we gave the proof of the following proposition

Proposition 1

(
[
8
]
)
.

For any almost periodic set there is
k_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\#A\cap[x,x+1]\leq k_{1}
. Also,
\#A\cap[x,x+h)\leq k_{1}(h+1)
.

Here and below,
\#H
means the number of points in the multiset
H
taking into account their multiplicities.

Proposition 2
.

For any almost periodic set there is
k_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
h>0
and every half-intervals
[x_{1},x_{1}+h),\,[x_{2},x_{2}+h)
we have

|\#A\cap[x_{1},x_{1}+h)-\#A\cap[x_{2},x_{2}+h)|\leq k_{2}
. Also, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,h>0,\,M\in{\mathbb{N}}

|\#A\cap[x,x+h)-(1/M)\#A\cap[x,x+Mh)|\leq k_{2}.

Proof
. Let
L_{1},\,E_{1}
be defined in (
3
), and
\tau\in E_{1}\cap[x_{1}-x_{2},L_{1}+x_{1}-x_{2})
. Since

[x_{2},x_{2}+h)+\tau\subset[x_{1},x_{1}+L_{1}+h)
, we see that to each
a_{n}\in[x_{2},x_{2}+h)
assign a point
a_{\sigma(n)}\in[x_{1}-1,x_{1}+L_{1}+h+1)
. Therefore,

\#A\cap[x_{2},x_{2}+h)\leq\#A\cap[x_{1},x_{1}+h)+\#A\cap[x_{1}-1,x_{1})+\#A\cap[x_{1}+h,x_{1}+h+L_{1}+1).

By Proposition
1
, the last two terms are bounded by
k_{1}+(L_{1}+2)k_{1}
. The proof of the opposite inequality is the same.

To prove the second assertion, we have to add together all the inequalities

\#A\cap[x,x+h)-k_{2}\leq\#A\cap[x+(m-1)h,x+mh)\leq k_{2}+\#A\cap[x,x+h),\quad m=1,2,\dots,M.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A
be an almost periodic set. There is a strictly positive density
d
such that for any
\eta>0
and any half-interval
I
with length
l(I)>N_{\eta}
we have

\left|\frac{\#A\cap I}{l(I)}-d\right|<\eta.

This result was generalized to multidimensional Euclidean spaces in
[
7
]
.

Proof of Proposition
3
. Let
I_{1}=[x_{1},x_{1}+h_{1}),I_{2}=[x_{2},x_{2}+h_{2})
be two half-intervals such that
h_{1}/h_{2}=p/q,\,p,q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We have

\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap I_{2}}{h_{2}}=\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap qI_{1}}{qh_{1}}+\frac{\#A\cap qI_{1}}{qh_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap pI_{2}}{ph_{2}}+\frac{\#A\cap pI_{2}}{ph_{2}}-\frac{\#A\cap I_{2}}{h_{2}}.

Applying Proposition
2
, we get

(4)

\left|\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap I_{2}}{h_{2}}\right|\leq\frac{k_{2}}{h_{1}}+\frac{k_{2}}{qh_{1}}+\frac{k_{2}}{h_{2}}\leq k_{2}\left(\frac{2}{h_{1}}+\frac{1}{h_{2}}\right).

For arbitrary
h_{1},\,h_{2}
take a half-interval
I^{\prime}=[x_{1},x_{1}+h^{\prime})
such that
h_{1}<h^{\prime}<h_{1}+1
and
h^{\prime}/h_{2}
rational. We have

\left|\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap I^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right|\leq\frac{\#A\cap[x_{1}+h_{1},x_{1}+h^{\prime})}{h^{\prime}}+\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}h^{\prime}}.

By Proposition
1
, we obtain

\left|\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\#A\cap I^{\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right|\leq\frac{k_{1}}{h^{\prime}}+\frac{k_{1}(h_{1}+1)}{h_{1}h^{\prime}}.

Applying (
4
) with
I^{\prime}
instead of
I_{1}
, we obtain for all
I_{1},\,I_{2}

\left|\frac{\#A\cap I_{1}}{l(I_{1})}-\frac{\#A\cap I_{2}}{l(I_{2})}\right|\leq k_{2}\left(\frac{2}{l(I_{1})}+\frac{1}{l(I_{2})}\right)+k_{1}\left(\frac{2}{l(I_{1})}+\frac{1}{l(I_{1})^{2}}\right).

Therefore there is a limit

d=\lim_{l(I)\to\infty}\frac{\#A\cap I}{l(I)}.

It is easy to check that the set
A
has nonempty intersection with every interval of length
2+L_{1}
, hence this limit is strictly positive.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A=\{a_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an almost periodic set of density
d
such that
a_{n}\leq a_{n+1}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then

(5)

a_{n}=n/d+\phi(n)\quad\text{with an almost periodic mapping}\quad\phi:\,Z\to{\mathbb{R}}.

Remark 1
. The incomplete proof of this Theorem was given in
[
6
]
.

Remark 2
. The converse assertion is simple, because for every
\varepsilon
-almost period
\tau\in{\mathbb{Z}}
of the mapping
\phi(n)
the number
\tau/d
is
\varepsilon
-almost period of an almost periodic set
A=\{n/d+\phi(n)\}

with any
d>0
and
\sigma(n)=n+\tau
.

Proof of Theorem
1
. We may suppose that
a_{0}<a_{1}
.
It follows from Proposition
1
that every interval of length
1
contains at least one subinterval of length
1/(2k_{1})
that does not intersect
A
.
Take
\varepsilon<\min\{1/(6k_{1}),(a_{1}-a_{0})/3\}
. Divide
{\mathbb{R}}
into an infinite number of disjoint half-intervals
I_{j}=(t_{j},t_{j+1}],\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
t_{j+1}-t_{j}<2

and
A\cap(t_{j}-2\varepsilon,t_{j}+2\varepsilon)=\emptyset
for all

.

Let
\tau
be any number from
E_{\varepsilon}
in (
3
), and let
\sigma
be the corresponding bijection. Then
\rho(j)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
corresponds to any

such that
\sigma
is the bijection of
A\cap I_{j}
to
A\cap I_{\rho(j)}
. Hence,
\#(A\cap I_{j})=\#(A\cap I_{\rho(j)})
. Let
\sigma_{j}
be the monotone increasing bijection of
A\cap I_{j}
on
A\cap I_{\rho(j)}
. Check that

(6)

|a_{n}+\tau-a_{\sigma_{j}(n)}|<\varepsilon\qquad\forall\,a_{n}\in I_{j}.

Suppose the contrary. Let
n_{0}
be the minimal number such that (
6
) does not satisfy. If
a_{n_{0}}+\tau+\varepsilon\leq a_{\sigma_{j}(n_{0})}
, then

a_{n}+\tau+\varepsilon\leq a_{k}
for all

and
k\geq\sigma_{j}(n_{0})
,
a_{n}\in I_{j},\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}
. Therefore,
k\neq\sigma(n)
for these numbers,
and
\sigma
gives a correspondence
between points from the set
\{n\leq n_{0}:\,a_{n}\in I_{j}\}
and points from the set
\{k<\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}
. But
by definition of
\sigma_{j}
, we have

\#\{n\leq n_{0}:\,a_{n}\in I_{j}\}=\#\{k\leq\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}=1+\#\{k<\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}.

We get a contradiction.

If
a_{n_{0}}+\tau\geq a_{\sigma_{j}(n_{0})}+\varepsilon
, then
a_{n}+\tau\geq a_{k}+\varepsilon
for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
k\leq\sigma_{j}(n_{0})
,
a_{n}\in I_{j},\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}
.
Therefore,
k\neq\sigma(n)
for these numbers, and
\sigma
gives a correspondence
between points from the set
\{n\geq n_{0}:\,a_{n}\in I_{j}\}
and points from the set
\{k>\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}
. But by definition of
\sigma_{j}
, we have

\#\{n\geq n_{0}:\,a_{n}\in I_{j}\}=\#\{k\geq\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}=1+\#\{k>\sigma_{j}(n_{0}):\,a_{k}\in I_{\rho(j)}\}.

We get a contradiction as well.

Since numbers
\#(A\cap I_{j})
and
\#(A\cap I_{\rho(j)})
coincide, we see that the differences between indices of the first elements in these sets coincide for all

.
Hence there is a number
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that inequality (
3
) satisfies for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\sigma(n)=n+h
.

It follows from the definition of
\tau
for all

and

-\varepsilon<a_{kh}-\tau-a_{(k-1)h}<\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad-N\varepsilon<a_{Nh}-N\tau-a_{0}<N\varepsilon.

Let

-almost periods

.

For any almost periodic set

3.
Zeros of infinite exponential sums

Denote by
S({\mathbb{R}})
the Schwartz space of test functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with the finite norms

N_{n,m}(\varphi)=\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}}\max_{k\leq m}|(1+|x|^{n})\varphi^{(k)}(x)|,\quad n,m=0,1,2,\dots

These norms generate the topology on
S({\mathbb{R}})
. Elements of the space
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}})
of continuous linear functionals on
S({\mathbb{R}})
are called temperate distributions.

The Fourier transform of a temperate distribution
f
is defined by the equality

\hat{f}(\varphi)=f(\hat{\varphi})\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}),

where

\hat{\varphi}(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi ixt}dx

is the Fourier transform of the function
\varphi
. By
\check{\varphi}
we denote the inverse Fourier transform of
\varphi
.
The Fourier transform is the bijection of
S({\mathbb{R}})
on itself and the bijection of
S^{*}({\mathbb{R}})
on itself.

Let
\mathfrak{T}
be the class of exponential sums

f(x)=\sum_{n}q_{n}e^{2\pi i\omega_{n}x},\quad q_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}

with finite Wiener’s norm
\|f\|_{W}:=\sum_{n}|q_{n}|
and a bounded spectrum
\Omega:=\{\omega_{n}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

Every function
f(x)\in\mathfrak{T}
expands to an entire almost periodic function
f(z)
of exponential type
\sigma=\max_{n}|\omega_{n}|
; the zero set
A=\{a_{n}\}
of
f(z)

lies in some horizontal strip of a finite width if and only if
\inf\Omega\in\Omega
and
\sup\Omega\in\Omega
(cf.
[
10
]
, Ch.VI, Cor.2). Moreover,
A
is an almost periodic set (cf.
[
10
]
, App.VI, Lemma 1).

If
0\not\in A
and
a_{n}=\alpha n+\phi(n)
with almost periodic
\phi:\,{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that the Fourier series of
\phi
converges absolutely, then the function

(1-z/a_{0})\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(1-z/a_{n})(1-z/a_{-n})

expands in an absolutely convergent exponential series (cf.
[
10
]
, App. VI, Th.9), hence it belongs to
\mathfrak{T}
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathfrak{T}
has the zero set

\log G(x+i)=K_{2}x-g(x+i)-id\pi x+O(1).

Since
G(x+i)
is almost periodic, left-hand side of this equality is also uniformly bound. Then (
26
) implies

K_{2}x-g(x)-id\pi x=O(1).

By (
25
),
g(x)=o(|x|)
, therefore,
K_{2}=i\pi d
and
g(x)
is bounded.

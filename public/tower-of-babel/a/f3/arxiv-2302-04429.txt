On the calculation of the ramified Siegel series
Masahiro Watanabe

Abstract
The ramified Siegel series is an important factor that arises in the Fourier coefficient of the Siegel-Eisenstein series. Many results of the formula of the ramified Siegel series under some conditions are already known. However, the explicit formula of the general condition is not calculated. We calculate the formula of the Siegel series where the dimension n n is arbitrary, and the additive character ψ \psi is primitive. We calculate when the field F F is a non-archimedean non-dyadic local field, including F = ℚ p F=\mathbb{Q}_{p} case. Moreover, we calculate the explicit value of the ramified Siegel series when degree n n is 1, 2 and 3.

Introduction

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
We recall the Fourier expansion of the Eisenstein series.
For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathrm{Im}(z)>0\}
,
let
G_{2k}(\tau)
be the Eisenstein series of weight
2k
, defined by

G_{2k}(\tau):=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}}\frac{1}{(m+n\tau)^{2k}}.

The Fourier expansion of
G_{2k}(\tau)
is well-known as follows.

G_{2k}(\tau)=2\zeta(2k)+\frac{2(2\pi i)^{2k}}{(2k-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^{n}.

Here
\zeta(z)
is Riemann’s zeta function, and
\sigma_{p}(n)
is the divisor sum function. We write
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
.
So the number-theoretic function
\sigma_{p}
arises in the Fourier coefficients.

Now we consider the Siegel-Eisenstein series. Let
k\geq 2,\,\,l,n\geq 1
be integers, and
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
l
.
The Siegel-Eisenstein series
E_{k,l,\psi}^{n}(Z)
is defined as

E_{k,l,\psi}^{n}(Z):=\sum_{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{\infty}^{n}\backslash\Gamma_{0}^{n}(l)}\psi(\det D)\det(CZ+D)^{-k},

where the set
\Gamma_{\infty}^{n}
and
\Gamma_{0}^{n}(l)
are subgroups of
\Gamma^{n}=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\infty}^{n}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}C=0\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}^{n}(l)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma^{n}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}C\equiv 0\mod l\right\},

respectively, and
Z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:=\{Z\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mid{}^{t}Z=Z,\,\,\mathrm{Im}(Z)\,\,\text{is positive definite}\}
.

The Fourier expansion of the Siegel-Eisenstein series can be written as

E_{k,l,\psi}^{n}(Z)=\sum_{A\in S_{n}^{*},A\geq 0}c(A)\exp(2\pi i\,\mathrm{Tr}(AZ)),

where

S_{n}^{*}:=\left\{A=(a_{ij})\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\mid a_{ii}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}/{2}\,\,(i\neq j)\right\},

and
c(A)
is a constant depending only on the matrix
A
. The symbol
A\geq 0
means that the matrix
A
is positive definite.

The ramified Siegel series is an important factor arose in
c(A)
and partially calculated in many papers.
In this article, we calculate the ramified Siegel series under some condition.

More precisely, the Fourier coefficient
c(A)
is calculated as

\displaystyle c(A)=\frac{2^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{{2}}}(-2\pi i)^{nk}}{\pi^{\frac{n(n-1)}{4}}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\Gamma(s-j/2)}(\det A)^{k-\frac{n+1}{2}}\prod_{p:\,\,\text{prime}}b_{n}^{p}(A,s),

when
A\geq 0
and the factor
b_{n}^{p}(A,s)
is called the ramified Siegel series when
p
is divided by
l
.
Here we note
\Gamma(s)
the Gamma function.

Many results of the formula of the Siegel series are already known.
Katsurada
[
12
]
gave the explicit formula of
l=1
, i.e., full-modular case.
When
n=2
, the results are well-known when the additive character
\psi
is primitive, that is,
\psi=\prod\psi_{p}
.
For example, Mizuno
[
13
]
shows
l
: square-free case,
and Takemori
[
18
]
shows
l
: any integer case.
Gunji calculated when
n=2
, in
[
6
]
, and when
n=3
, in
[
7
]
, respectively.
In this article, we calculate the ramified Siegel series when
n
: arbitrary.

In
[
8
]
, Gunji proved the same result of Theorem 1.1.
We extend this result when
F
is non-dyadic local field, while all results above are
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
case.
By calculating the integration of the Siegel series, we divide the domain of integration by the orbits of action of
\Gamma_{0}
and use the method of Sato and Hironaka
[
9
]
.
We define a Weil constant
\alpha_{\psi}(x)
(
x\in F^{\times}
) so that the integral
I(a),I^{*}(a)
in
[
9
]
, which is well-known as Gauss sum when
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, is calculated on the field
F
by using this constant.

The ramified Siegel series is also related to the degenerate Whittaker function
[
14
]
.
When we use the functinoal equation of the Whittaker function calculated in
[
16
]
and
[
10
]
, we can get the functional equation of the Siegel series.
The calculation of the functional equation is in progress, and we will write a new article of this point of view of the ramified Siegel series.

Let us write down our main results.

Let
G=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(F)
be the symplectic group of rank
n
over a non-archimedean, non-dyadic field
F
.
Define
\mathfrak{o}
,
\mathfrak{p}
as the ring of integers of
F
and the maximal ideal of
\mathfrak{o}
, respectively.
We write
K=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})
.
Let
P
be the parabolic subgroup of
G
, and the subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is defined by

\Gamma=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in K\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}C\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{p}\right\}.

We define
\{w_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
by

w_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}1_{n-i}&&&\\
&&&-1_{i}\\
\hline\cr&&1_{n-i}&\\
&1_{i}&&\end{array}\right).

The set
\{w_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
is a complete set of representatives of the double coset
P\backslash G/\Gamma
.

Let
\psi
be an additive character of
F
of order
0
and
\omega
be a character of
F^{\times}
satisfying
\omega^{2}=1
.
We define
I_{n}(\omega,s)=\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\omega\circ|\det|^{s})
the space of smooth functions on
G
satisfying

f\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&*\\
0&{}^{t}\!A^{-1}\end{pmatrix}g\right)=\omega(\det A)|\det A|^{s+\frac{n+1}{2}}f(g).

We also define the space

I_{n}\left(\omega,s-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\Gamma,\omega}=\left\{f\in I_{n}\left(\chi,s-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}f(gk)=\omega(k)f(g)\,\,\text{for any}\,\,k\in\Gamma\right\}.

This space is spanned by the functions
\{f_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
which satisfies
f_{i}(w_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.

A ramified Siegel series associated to

\displaystyle\varphi\in I_{n}\left(\omega,s-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\Gamma,\omega}
is defined by an integral

\int_{\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)}\varphi\left(w_{n}\begin{pmatrix}1&X\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}g\right)\psi(-\mathrm{tr}(BX))dX.

We write
S_{t}(B,s)^{\omega}
for the ramified Siegel series associated to
\varphi=f_{t}
(
0\leq t\leq n
).

We divide the domain of integration
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)
by the orbits of action of

\Gamma_{0}=\{\gamma=(\gamma_{ij})\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})\mid\gamma_{ij}\in\mathfrak{p}\,(i>j)\}

and use a formula of the volume of each orbits due to Sato and Hironaka
[
9
]
.

We need some notations.
We define
\chi
, a ramified non-trivial character of
F^{\times}
, by
\chi(x)=\langle\pi,x\rangle
.
Here we note
\langle\,,\,\rangle
a Hilbert symbol.
Put
I=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and consider the standard action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
I
.
For
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
satisfying
\sigma^{2}=1
and

I=I_{0}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}
a partition of
I
into disjoint
\sigma
-stable subset.
We define the numbers
c_{1}^{(k)}(\sigma),c_{2}(\sigma),n_{k},n^{(k)},n(k)
, associated to the partition
\{I_{i}\}
, by

\displaystyle c_{1}^{(k)}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\#\{i\in I_{k}\mid\sigma(i)=i\},

\displaystyle c_{2}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\#\{i\in I\mid\sigma(i)\neq i\},

and

\displaystyle n_{k}

\displaystyle=\#I_{k},\quad n^{(k)}=\sum_{l=k}^{r}n_{l},\quad n(k)=\frac{n^{(k)}(n^{(k)}+1)}{2}.

We also put

\displaystyle\tau(\{I_{i}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\#\{(i,j)\in I_{l}\times(I_{0}\cup\cdots\cup I_{l-1})\mid j<i\},

\displaystyle t(\sigma,\{I_{i}\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{r}\#\{(i,j)\in I_{l}\times I_{l}\mid i<j<\sigma(i),\,\sigma(j)<\sigma(i)\},

\displaystyle e_{\sigma,i,k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&(k\leq i,\,k\leq\sigma(i))\\
1&(\sigma(i)<k\leq i\,\,\,\text{or}\,\,\,i<k\leq\sigma(i))\\
2&(i<k,\,\sigma(i)<k).\end{cases}

Let
B
a matrix
B=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{1}\pi^{e_{1}},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\pi^{e_{n}})
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}^{\times}
and
0\leq e_{1}\leq\cdots\leq e_{n}
. Set

\displaystyle b_{l}(\sigma,B)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{min}\left\{\{e_{i}\mid i\in I_{l},\sigma(i)>i\}\cup\{e_{i}+1\mid i\in I_{l},\sigma(i)\leq i\}\right\},

\displaystyle B_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{k\mid 1\leq k\leq i-1,\,\,e_{k}+\lambda<0,\,\,e_{k}\not\equiv\lambda\bmod 2\}

\displaystyle\quad\cup\,\{k\mid i+1\leq k\leq n,\,\,e_{k}+\lambda+2<0,\,\,e_{k}\not\equiv\lambda\bmod 2\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{l,\lambda}(\sigma;B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I_{l}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{min}\{e_{k}+e_{\sigma,i,k}+\lambda,\,0\}.

We further define

\displaystyle\xi_{i,\lambda}(B)_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\in B_{i}(\lambda)}\chi(v_{k})\times\begin{cases}0&e_{i}+\lambda\geq 0,\,\,\#B_{i}(\lambda):\text{even}\\
(1-q^{-1})\chi(-1)^{[\#B_{i}(\lambda)/2]+1}&e_{i}+\lambda\geq 0,\,\,\#B_{i}(\lambda):\text{odd}\\
\chi(v_{i})\chi(-1)^{[\#B_{i}(\lambda)/2]+1}&e_{i}+\lambda=-1,\,\,\#B_{i}(\lambda):\text{even}\\
-q^{-1/2}\chi(-1)^{[\#B_{i}(\lambda)/2]+1}&e_{i}+\lambda=-1,\,\,\#B_{i}(\lambda):\text{odd}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the above notations, the ramified Siegel series
S_{0}(B,s)^{\chi}
is given by

\displaystyle S_{0}(B,s)^{\chi}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\psi}(\pi)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\\
\sigma^{2}=1\end{subarray}}(1-q^{-1})^{c_{2}(\sigma)}\sum_{I=I_{0}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}}q^{-c_{2}(\sigma)-\tau(\{I_{i}\})-t(\sigma,\{I_{i}\})}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\{\nu\}}\prod_{l=0}^{r}\chi(-1)^{\nu_{l}n^{(l)}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\nu_{l}n^{(l)}(2s-1-n^{(l)})+\tilde{\rho}_{l,\nu_{0}+\cdots+\nu_{l}}(\sigma;B)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{l}\\
\sigma(i)=i\end{subarray}}\xi_{i,\nu_{0}+\cdots+\nu_{l}}(B)_{\chi}.

Here the summation with respect to
I=I_{0}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}
is taken over all
partitions of
I
into disjoint
\sigma
-stable subsets and the summation with respect
to
\{\nu\}
is taken over the finite set

\{(\nu_{0},\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{r}\mid-b_{l}(\sigma,B)\leq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{l}\leq-1\,\,(0\leq l\leq r)\}.

For
\varphi\in\{f_{0},f_{1}\cdots,f_{n}\}
, we give an explicit formula of the ramified Siegel series in the same way.

Theorem 1.2
.

The ramified Siegel series
S_{t}(B,s)^{\chi}

(0\leq t\leq n)
is given by

\displaystyle S_{t}(B,s)^{\chi}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\psi}(\pi)^{n-t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\\
\sigma^{2}=1\end{subarray}}(1-q^{-1})^{c_{2}(\sigma)}q^{-c_{2}(\sigma)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I=I_{0}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}\\
n^{(k)}=t\end{subarray}}q^{-\tau(\{I_{i}\})-t(\sigma,\{I_{i}\})}\frac{(1-q^{-1})^{\sum_{l=k}^{r}c_{1}^{(l)}(\sigma)}q^{n(k)}}{\prod_{l=k}^{r}(q^{n(l)}-1)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\{\nu\}_{k}^{t}}\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\chi(-1)^{\nu_{l}(n^{(l)}-n^{(k)})}q^{\nu_{l}((sn^{(l)}-n(l))-(sn^{(k)}-n(k)))+\tilde{\rho}_{l,\nu_{0}+\cdots+\nu_{l}}(\sigma;B)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{l}\\
\sigma(i)=i\end{subarray}}\xi_{i,\nu_{0}+\cdots+\nu_{l}}(B)_{\chi}.

Here the summation with respect to
\{\nu\}_{k}^{t}
for
k\geq 1
is taken over the finite set

\left\{(\nu_{0},\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{k-1})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{k-1}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}-b_{l}(\sigma,B)\leq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{l}\leq-1\,\,(0\leq l\leq k-1),\,n^{(k)}=t\right\}.

2
The ramified Siegel series

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic 0.
Let us denote the ring of integers of
F
by
\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{o}_{F}
.
We write
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{F}
,
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{k}_{F}=\mathfrak{o}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}

for the maximal ideal and the reside field of
\mathfrak{o}
, respectively.
We fix a prime element
\pi\in\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
q
denote the cardinality of
\mathfrak{k}
.
Later we assume
q
is odd.

The set of symmetric matrices of degree
n
over
F
is denoted by
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)
,
and the subset of non-degenerate symmetric matrices is denoted by
S_{n}(F)
.

The symplectic group of degree
n
over
F
is defined by

G=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(F):=\{M\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(F)\mid{}^{t}\!Mw_{n}M=w_{n}\}

where we write
{}^{t}\!M
for the transpose of a matrix
M
, and
w_{n}
for

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&-1_{n}\\
1_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Let
K=\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})
denote a maximal compact subgroup of
G
.
We define

P=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Sp}_{n}(F)\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}C=0\right\}

by the Siegel parabolic subgroup of
G
.
We call the decomposition
G=PK
the Iwasawa decomposition.
Set

\Gamma=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in K\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}C\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{p}\right\}.

Let
\omega
be a character of
F^{\times}
satisfying
\omega^{2}=1
.
Later we only consider
\omega=\mathbf{1}
the trivial character or
\omega=\chi
which satisfies
\chi(x)=\langle\pi,x\rangle
; a ramified nontrivial character.
We define a character
\omega^{\Gamma}
on
\Gamma
as

\omega^{\Gamma}\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\right)=\omega(\det D)

and we write
\omega
for
\omega^{\Gamma}
by abuse of notation.

Definition 2.1
.

The Siegel series is an integral

\int_{\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)}\varphi\left(w_{n}\begin{pmatrix}1&X\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\psi(-\mathrm{tr}(BX))dX,

where
\psi
is an additive character of
F
,
B\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)

and
\varphi\in I_{n}\left(\omega,s-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\Gamma,\omega}
.

S_{t}(B,s)^{\omega}
is denoted by the Siegel series when
\varphi=f_{t}
(
0\leq t\leq n
).

Remark
2.1
.

When
\mathrm{Re}\,s>0
, the Siegel series is absolutely integrable.
This integral easily extends to a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
.

Later we only consider when B is non-degenerate, that is,
\det B\neq 0
.

Define
I_{n}(\omega,s)=\mathrm{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\omega\circ|\det|^{s})
as the space of the induced representation,
the space of smooth functions on
G
satisfying

f\left(\begin{pmatrix}A&*\\
0&{}^{t}\!A^{-1}\end{pmatrix}g\right)=\omega(\det A)|\det A|^{s+\frac{n+1}{2}}f(g),

and we also define
I_{n}(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}
as

I_{n}(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}=\{f\in I(\omega,s)\mid f(gk)=\omega(k)f(g)\,\,\text{for all}\,\,k\in\Gamma\}.

The double coset

P\backslash G/\Gamma\simeq(P\cap K)\backslash K/\Gamma=\Gamma\backslash K/\Gamma

is a finite set and one can choose a complete set of representatives
\{w_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
by

w_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}1_{n-i}&&&\\
&&&-1_{i}\\
\hline\cr&&1_{n-i}&\\
&1_{i}&&\end{array}\right).

It is known that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in K
is an element of
\Gamma w_{i}\Gamma
if and only if
the matrix
C
, considered as
\bmod\,\mathfrak{p}
, has rank
i
.

Each
f\in I_{n}(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}
is determined by its value on
K
, so
is determined by
\{f(w_{i})\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
. Now we take the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Define
f_{i}\in I(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}
satisfying
f_{i}(w_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Then the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
I(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}
is spanned by the functions
\{f_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq n}
, in other words,

I_{n}(\omega,s)^{\Gamma,\omega}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{C}f_{i}.

3
Calculation of the Siegel series associated to
\varphi=f_{0}

In this section, we consider the case where
\varphi=f_{0}
.
Let
B\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)
.
Recall that we define the Siegel series as

\displaystyle S_{0}(B,s)^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)}f_{0}\left(w_{n}\begin{pmatrix}1&X\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\psi(-\mathrm{tr}(BX))dX

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)}f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&X\end{pmatrix}\right)\psi(-\mathrm{tr}(BX))dX.

Note that the function
f_{0}
is taken in the space
I_{n}\left(\omega,s-\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\Gamma,\omega}
.
We can certainly assume that
X
is an invertible matrix,
since the measure of
\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(F)\backslash S_{n}(F)
is 0.
We need the Iwasawa decomposition of the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&X\end{pmatrix}
.
The following lemma is well-known and called the Jordan splitting;

Lemma 3.1
.

For
X\in\mathrm{Sym}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})
, there are
U\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})
and
diagonal matrix
Y
such that
X={}^{t}UYU
.
Moreover, when we write
Y=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha_{1}\pi^{v_{1}},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\pi^{v_{n}})

(\alpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{o}^{\times},v_{i}\geq 0)
then
for each
m\geq 0
, the value

\#\{i\mid\nu_{i}=m\}\quad\text{and}\quad\prod_{\nu_{i}=m}\alpha_{i}

are uniquely determined by the matrix
X
.

When we write
X={}^{t}UYU
where
U\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathfrak{o})
we have

\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&{}^{t}UYU\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}U^{-1}&\\
&{}^{t}U\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&Y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{t}U^{-1}&\\
&U\end{pmatrix}

Then

\displaystyle f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&X\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}U^{-1}&\\
&{}^{t}U\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&Y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{}^{t}U^{-1}&\\
&U\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega(\det U^{-1}{}^{t}U^{-1})\left|\det U^{-1}{}^{t}U^{-1}\right|^{-s}f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&Y\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&Y\end{pmatrix}\right)

since
\omega^{2}=1
and
\det U\in\mathfrak{o}^{\times}
.

We write

Y=\begin{pmatrix}Y^{\prime}&\\
&Y^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}

where
Y^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}
are diagonal matrices of degree
r
and
n-r
(
0\leq r\leq n
), respectively.
We assume that
Y^{\prime-1}\in\mathfrak{p}M_{r}(\mathfrak{o})
and
Y^{\prime\prime}\in M_{n-r}(\mathfrak{o})
. Since

\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}0&&-1&\\
&0&&-1\\
\hline\cr 1&&Y^{\prime}&\\
&1&&Y^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}0&&-1&\\
&0&&-1\\
\hline\cr 1&&0&\\
&1&&Y^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}1&&Y^{\prime}&\\
&1&&0\\
\hline\cr 0&&1&\\
&0&&1\end{array}\right)

so we may assume that
Y^{\prime}=0
.
Now we consider an Iwasawa decomposition;

\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}0&&-1&\\
&0&&-1\\
\hline\cr 1&&0&\\
&1&&Y^{\prime\prime}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr|rr}1&&0&\\
&1&&-Y^{\prime\prime}\\
\hline\cr 0&&1&\\

3.3
Weil constant

We recall the definition of the Weil constant as in

10
]

For each Schwartz function

Note that the Haar measure

Definition 3.1

Let

.
The Weil constant

[formula]

for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(F)
.

The following lemmas are fundamental.
Let
\langle*,*\rangle
denote the Hilbert symbol of index 2 on
F
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
a,b\in F^{\times}
,

\frac{\alpha_{\psi}(a)\alpha_{\psi}(b)}{\alpha_{\psi}(ab)\alpha_{\psi}(1)}=\langle a,b\rangle.

Here we note that, for
a,b\in F^{\times}
,
\alpha_{\psi}(ab^{2})=\alpha_{\psi}(a)
.
The next lemma says that the Gauss integral,
\displaystyle I(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{o}}\psi(ax^{2})dx
, can be calculated by using the Weil constant, when
F
is non-archimedean non-dyadic local field.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
a\in F^{\times}
. If
\mathrm{ord}(a)<0
, then
I(a)=\alpha_{\psi}(a)|a|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

We assume
\phi=1_{\mathfrak{o}}
, which is the characteristic function of
\mathfrak{o}
.

\hat{\phi}=1_{\mathfrak{o}}
is well-known, so (1) is

\int_{\mathfrak{o}}\psi(ax^{2})dx=\alpha_{\psi}(a)|2a|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathfrak{o}}\psi\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{4a}\right)dx.

The integral
\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{o}}\psi\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{4a}\right)dx
is 1 because

\mathrm{ord}\left(\displaystyle-\frac{x^{2}}{4a}\right)\geq 0
.
Since
F
is non-dyadic, we have
|2|=1
. Hence the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
a\in F^{\times}
,

\alpha_{\psi}(\delta a)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\psi}(a)&(\mathrm{ord}\,a:\mathrm{even})\\
-\alpha_{\psi}(a)&(\mathrm{ord}\,a:\mathrm{odd}).\end{cases}

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the following equations.

\displaystyle\alpha_{\psi}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\psi}(1)=1.

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\psi}(\delta\pi)

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{\psi}(\pi).

(3)

(2) is trivial. To prove (3), we use Lemma
3.3

\frac{\alpha_{\psi}(\delta)\alpha_{\psi}(\pi)}{\alpha_{\psi}(\delta\pi)\alpha_{\psi}(1)}=\langle\delta,\pi\rangle.

Because the fact that
\alpha_{\psi}(\delta)=\alpha_{\psi}(1)=1
and
\langle\delta,\pi\rangle=-1
, the lemma is proved.
∎

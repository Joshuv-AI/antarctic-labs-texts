Strong Convexity for harmonic functions on compact symmetric spaces
Gabor Lippner 1 1, Dan Mangoubi 2 2, Zachary McGuirk 3 3, Rachel Yovel 4 4

Abstract
. Let h h be a harmonic function defined on a spherical disk. It is shown that Δ k ​ | h | 2 \Delta^{k}|h|^{2} is nonnegative for all k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} where Δ \Delta is the Laplace-Beltrami operator. This fact is generalized to harmonic functions defined on a disk in a normal homogeneous compact Riemannian manifold, and in particular in a symmetric space of the compact type. This complements a similar property for harmonic functions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} discovered by the first two authors and is related to strong convexity of the L 2 L^{2} -growth function of harmonic functions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Delta_{E}
denote the Laplace operator on the Eudlidean
n
-dimensional space,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
h
be a harmonic function defined on a disk in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It was proved in
[
12
]
that
\Delta_{E}^{k}|h|^{2}
is nonnegative for all
k
.
The main motivation to study powers of the Laplace operators comes from the well known classical fact that the Taylor series expansion
of the spherical mean function is expressed in terms of the powers
\Delta^{k}
(see e.g.
[
15
]
), and as such
this positivity statement has given a new argument for the logarithmic convexity of the
L^{2}
-growth function of harmonic functions
[
1
]
or, equivalently, the monotonicity of the frequency function
[
2
]
. Moreover, this series of linear inequalities was important in deducing a
discrete
version of the three circles theorems for harmonic functions.

The main goal of this paper is to show that, maybe surprisingly, an analogous convexity phenomenon also holds on the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and more generally on symmetric spaces of the compact type.
In fact, it is shown to hold on a more general family of spaces, namely, normal homogeneous compact Riemannian manifolds, which will be discussed in §
3
.

To avoid an overly technical introduction, we first state the theorem for spheres.
Let
\Delta_{S}
denote the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the standard unit sphere,
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and denote by
B\left(\rho\right)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
a ball of radius
0<\rho<\pi
. We prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h\colon B\left(\rho\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a harmonic function. Then,

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\Delta_{S}^{k}|h|^{2}\geq 0

More generally, we show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a semisimple, connected, compact Lie group with a fixed bi-invariant Riemannian metric.
Let
K
be a closed subgroup. Consider the homogeneous Riemannian manifold
M=G/K
with the corresponding induced metric. Let
h
be a harmonic function defined on an open subset of
M
.
Then

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\quad\Delta_{M}^{k}|h|^{2}\geq 0

where
\Delta_{M}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
.

On compact Riemannian symmetric spaces of rank one or on two-point compact homogeneous spaces one can interpret Theorem
1.2

as an absolute monotonicity result for the spherical mean function,
since in these cases the spherical mean function can be expressed in terms of the Laplace powers
[
7
]
.

The proof in
[
12
]
is strongly based on the invariance of harmonic functions under translations. On a general Lie group equipped with an invariant Riemannian structure, harmonic functions are invariant under right translations, while, clearly, in the class of Lie groups possessing bi-invariant Riemannian structures we again recover the invariance of harmonic functions under all left and right translations.
The paper deals with harmonic functions on quotient spaces of Lie groups equipped with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric.
The proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is based on the possibility to write the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
as a sum of squares
of vector fields which commute with
\Delta
. While in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
one can represent the Laplace-Beltrami operator as a sum of
n
squares, one needs more than
n=\dim M
vector fields to do it on
M=G/K
.
The number of vector fields needed is the dimension of
G
.

Organization of the paper.

Although Theorem
1.2
generalizes the special case in Theorem
1.1
, we give the proof of that special case first as it is direct and different than the one for the general case. In addition, the proof in the case of the sphere can be made without any reference to notions from Lie Theory.

2.
The case of the sphere: Proof of Theorem
1.1

For the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space, the Laplace operator can be written as a sum of squares of
n
orthogonal constant vector fields. The proposition below shows that for the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{S}
can be written as a sum of squares of
\binom{n+1}{2}
rotation generating vector fields. This fact is well known and important in quantum mechanics of the hydrogen atom (see the two dimensional case in
[
11
]
*ch. 4.9). We include its proof for completeness and as an alternative to the general Lie group theory proof in the next section.

First, we define the vector fields concerned. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n+1}
be the standard coordinates for
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
Let
R_{ij}(t)
be the rotation of angle
t
in the oriented
x_{i}x_{j}
plane. Then, we define
X_{ij}
to be the vector field generating the flow
R_{ij}(t),
namely,
X_{ij}=x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i}.

Proposition 2.1
.

The Laplace-Beltrami operator on the sphere can be written as

\Delta_{S}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}X_{ij}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function, and let
\tilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{\{0\}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be its dilation invariant extension to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus{\{0\}}
. Then,

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}^{2}\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<j}\left(x_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}+x_{j}^{2}\partial_{i}^{2}\tilde{f}-2x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}+x_{i}\partial_{i}\tilde{f}+x_{j}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\left(x_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}-x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}\right)-n\sum_{i}x_{i}\partial_{i}\tilde{f}.

Since
\tilde{f}
is constant along the radial direction,

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}\partial_{i}\tilde{f}=r\partial_{r}\tilde{f}=0.

Fixing
j
and using (
2
), we have

(3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}=x_{j}\partial_{j}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n+1}x_{i}\partial_{i}\tilde{f}\right)=-\left(x_{j}\partial_{j}\right)^{2}\tilde{f}.

Summing (
3
) over
1\leq j\leq n+1
we obtain

(4)

\sum_{i\neq j}x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{f}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}x_{j}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}.

From (
1
), (
2
) and (
4
) we get

\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}^{2}\tilde{f}=\sum_{i\neq j}x_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}+\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}x_{j}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}=r^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\partial_{j}^{2}\tilde{f}=r^{2}\Delta_{E}\tilde{f}.

It only remains to recall the formula
\Delta_{E}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{n-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{S},
to get

\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}^{2}f=\Delta_{S}f.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
h:B\left(\rho\right)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a harmonic function. Using Proposition
2.1
we notice that

\Delta_{S}|h|^{2}=2\Re(\bar{h}\Delta_{S}h)+2\sum_{i<j}\left|X_{ij}h\right|^{2}=2\sum_{i<j}\left|X_{ij}h\right|^{2}.

Since
X_{ij}
commutes with the spherical Laplacian this formula expresses
\Delta_{S}|h|^{2}
as a sum of squares of harmonic functions. Therefore, by induction, we conclude that
\Delta_{S}^{k}|h|^{2}\geq 0
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

3.
The Laplacian on normal homogeneous Riemannian manifolds

3.1.
General Lie group notations

Let
G
be a semisimple, connected, compact Lie group.
Let
K
be a closed subgroup of
G
.
Following Helgason’s notation from
[
9
]
*Ch. II,
for
X\in T_{e}G
we denote by

\tilde{X}
the unique left invariant vector field on
G
such that

\tilde{X}_{e}=X
. Similarly,
\bar{X}
denotes the corresponding right invariant vector field. We consider
T_{e}G
with the Lie algebra structure induced from the Lie brackets
of left invariant vector fields and denote it by
\mathfrak{g}
.
For every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
there exists a unique one parametric subgroup
\gamma_{X}(t)

such that
\dot{\gamma}(0)=X
. This subgroup is denoted by
\exp tX
.

Let
\mathfrak{k}
be the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to a closed subgroup
K
.
Let
L_{g},
and
R_{g}
denote the left and right translations, respectively, in
G
by an element
g
. Then

\mathrm{Ad}:G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})

is the adjoint representation of
G
,
where

\mathrm{Ad}(g)=(dR_{g^{-1}})|_{g}(dL_{g})|_{e}.

The homogeneous manifold
M=G/K
is the space of left
K
-cosets. The canonical projection map is
\pi:G\to M
.
The coset
K
will be called the “origin” of
M
, and will be denoted by
o
.
The left action of
g\in G
on
M
is denoted by
\tau_{g}
. When convenient we write
go=\tau_{g}\cdot o=\pi(g)
.

Given
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
X^{+}

[
9
]
*Ch. II, §3 the vector field on
M
generating the flow
p\mapsto\tau_{\exp tX}\cdot p
, i.e.

X^{+}f(p):=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f(\tau_{\exp tX}\cdot p)\ .

It is useful to note the following identities:

(5)

\displaystyle d\pi|_{g}(\bar{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X^{+}_{go}\ ,

(6)

\displaystyle d\tau_{g}|_{ho}\circ d\pi|_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\pi|_{gh}\circ dL_{g}|_{h}\ ,

(7)

\displaystyle d\tau_{g}(X^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Ad}(g)X)^{+}\ ,

(8)

\displaystyle[X^{+},Y^{+}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[X,Y]^{+}\ .

For (
8
) see
[
9
]
*Ch. II, Theorem 3.4.

3.2.
A bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
and an induced Riemannian metric on
G/K

Fix a positive definite
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
,

B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}

such that:

(9)

\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g},\quad B([Z,X],Y)+B(X,[Z,Y])=0\ ,

where the latter identity is equivalent (as
G
is connected) to

\forall g\in G\quad B(\mathrm{Ad}(g)X,\mathrm{Ad}(g)Y)=B(X,Y)\ .

By our assumptions, positive definite
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant forms on
\mathfrak{g}
exist
(
[
9
]
*Ch. II, Prop 6.6).
The form
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
induces a bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
,

\langle V,W\rangle_{g}:=B\left((dL_{g})^{-1}V,(dL_{g})^{-1}W\right).

To define a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
M
, let
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{k}
in
\mathfrak{g}
with respect
to the quadratic form
B
.
Since
B
is in particular
\mathrm{Ad(K)}
-invariant, the linear decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}

is likewise
\mathrm{Ad}(K)
-invariant.
Observe that
\ker(d\pi)|_{e}=\mathfrak{k}
and thus,
d\pi|_{e}:\mathfrak{m}\to T_{o}M
is an isomorphism.
Every
X\in\mathfrak{g}
can therefore be uniquely decomposed as,

X=X_{\mathfrak{m}}+X_{\mathfrak{k}},

where
X_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathfrak{m}
and
X_{\mathfrak{k}}\in\mathfrak{k}
.

For
V\in T_{o}M
, let
(d\pi)^{-1}(V)
denote (with abuse of notation) the unique
X\in\mathfrak{m}

such that
d\pi|_{e}X=V
. Then, one can define a metric on
M
by

(10)

\langle V,W\rangle_{go}:=B\left((d\pi)^{-1}(d\tau_{g})^{-1}V,(d\pi)^{-1}(d\tau_{g})^{-1}W\right)\ .

Homogeneous Riemannian manifolds constructed in the manner above were studied by Nomizu
[
13
]
and are called
normal
homogeneous Riemannian manifolds following M. Berger’s terminology
[
3
]
.

3.3.
The Levi-Civita connection on a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold

We would like to understand the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
. To that end, we first need
to compute the Levi-Civita connection on
M
. On Lie groups with bi-invariant Riemannian structure this computation (as well as the more involved curvature computation) was first carried out by Cartan
[
5
]
*p. 64. Then, it was extended by Nomizu
[
13
]
to naturally reductive homogeneous Riemannian manifolds
(in particular, normal homogeneous spaces). We give here our own variation on this classical calculation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold as above. Then,
for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
one has

Z^{+}\langle X^{+},Y^{+}\rangle=\langle[Z^{+},X^{+}],Y^{+}\rangle+\langle X^{+},[Z^{+},Y^{+}]\rangle

Remark
. This formula does not make use of the
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariance of
B
nor the positivity of
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
. It is valid
on any homogeneous Riemannian manifold.

Proof.

We confirm the validity of the formula only at the origin as this is all we use below.

\begin{split}Z^{+}_{o}\langle X^{+},Y^{+}\rangle&=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\langle(d\tau_{\exp tZ})^{-1}X^{+}_{(\exp tZ)o},(d\tau_{\exp tZ})^{-1}Y^{+}_{(\exp tZ)o}\rangle_{o}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{identity:tau-Ad-correspondence}}}{{=}}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\langle(\mathrm{Ad}\exp(-tZ)X)^{+},(\mathrm{Ad}\exp(-tZ)Y)^{+}\rangle_{o}\\
&=-\langle[Z,X]^{+},Y^{+}\rangle_{o}-\langle X^{+},[Z,Y]^{+}\rangle_{o}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{form:X+Y+commutator}}}{{=}}\langle[Z^{+},X^{+}],Y^{+}\rangle_{o}+\langle X^{+},[Z^{+},Y^{+}]\rangle_{o}\end{split}

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold as above.
Then, for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
one has

2\langle\nabla_{X^{+}}Y^{+},Z^{+}\rangle=\langle X^{+},[Y^{+},Z^{+}]\rangle-\langle Y^{+},[Z^{+},X^{+}]\rangle+\langle Z^{+},[X^{+},Y^{+}]\rangle\ .

Remark
. This formula is valid on any homogeneous Riemannian manifold.

Proof.

By Koszul’s formula we have

\begin{split}2\langle\nabla_{X^{+}}Y^{+},Z^{+}\rangle&=X^{+}\langle Y^{+},Z^{+}\rangle+Y^{+}\langle Z^{+},X^{+}\rangle-Z^{+}\langle X^{+},Y^{+}\rangle\\
&-\langle X^{+},[Y^{+},Z^{+}]\rangle+\langle Y^{+},[Z^{+},X^{+}]\rangle+\langle Z^{+},[X^{+},Y^{+}]\rangle\ .\end{split}

We now substitute for the first three terms the expressions given by Lemma
3.1

and collect terms to obtain the claimed formula.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold as above. Then, for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}

\langle X^{+},[Y^{+},Z^{+}]\rangle_{o}=-B(X_{\mathfrak{m}},[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{m}})\ .

More generally,

\langle[X^{+},Y^{+}],Z^{+}\rangle_{go}=-B((\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})[X,Y])_{\mathfrak{m}},(\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})Z)_{\mathfrak{m}})\ .

Remark
. This formula is valid on any homogeneous Riemannian manifold.

Proof.

We only explain the formula at the origin, as this is all we use below.
At the origin the formula follows immediately from (
8
) and the definition of the metric on
M
in (
10
).
∎

We are now able to compute the Riemannian connection:

Proposition 3.4

(
[
13
]
*Theorem 10.1)
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold. Then,

\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{m}\quad\nabla_{X^{+}_{o}}Y^{+}=\frac{1}{2}[X^{+},Y^{+}]_{o}\ .

In particular,

\forall X\in\mathfrak{m}\quad\nabla_{X^{+}_{o}}X^{+}=0\ .

Remark
. The lemmas holds true on any naturally reductive homogeneous Riemannian manifold.
The curve
ge^{tX}o
is a geodesic through
go

[
13
]
[
14
]
*ch. 11, prop. 25, but we will not use this fact.

Proof.

From Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
we know

2\langle\nabla_{X^{+}}Y^{+},Z^{+}\rangle_{o}=-B(X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{m}})+B(Y,[Z,X]_{\mathfrak{m}})-B(Z,[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}})

A simple observation shows that condition (
9
) implies the
natural reductivity condition, namely,

(11)

\forall X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{m}\quad B([Z,X]_{\mathfrak{m}},Y)+B(X,[Z,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}})=0\ .

Thus,

2\langle\nabla_{X^{+}}Y^{+},Z^{+}\rangle_{o}=-B([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{m}},Z)=\langle[X^{+},Y^{+}],Z^{+}\rangle_{o}\ .

∎

3.4.
The projected Casimir operator is
G
-invariant

We denote by
U_{\mathfrak{g}}
the universal enveloping algebra of
G
.
One then defines the Casimir element
\Omega\in U_{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to our fixed
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
-invariant positive definite form
B
as

\Omega=\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{*}X_{j}

where
(X_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
is any basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
X_{j}^{*}
is the dual basis with respect
B
. One can verify that the element
\Omega
is independent of the choice of basis
X_{j}
and, very importantly, that the
\mathrm{Ad}
-invariance of
B
implies (see e.g.
[
4
]
*Prop. 10.3 or
[
10
]
*Ch. III, §7)
that

\Omega\in Z(U_{\mathfrak{g}})\ ,

where
Z(U_{\mathfrak{g}})
denotes the center of
U_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Consider the left invariant differential operator on
G
corresponding to the Casimir element given by

\tilde{\Omega}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{X}^{*}_{j}\tilde{X}_{j}

Proposition 3.5
.

The Casimir operator
\tilde{\Omega}
is both left and right invariant (i.e.
it commutes with left and right translations). In other words,

\bar{\Omega}=\tilde{\Omega}\ ,

where
\bar{\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{X}^{*}_{j}\bar{X}_{j}
.

Proof.

A left invariant differential operator on a connected Lie group
G
is right invariant if and only if it commutes with all
left invariant vector fields
\tilde{X}
on
G
(see
[
8
]
*Ch. II, Lemma 4.4).
Since the Casimir element is in the center of
U_{\mathfrak{g}}

it follows that
\tilde{\Omega}
is right invariant.
∎

The next theorem makes use of the full structure of the normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold
M
. In particular, it is not enough to assume that
M
is naturally reductive.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous Riemannian manifold, and let
(X_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
be a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
as above. Then, the projected Casimir operator on
M
,

\Omega^{+}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(X^{*}_{j})^{+}X^{+}_{j}\ ,

is
G
-invariant. Moreover,
X^{+}\Omega^{+}=\Omega^{+}X^{+}
for all
X\in\mathfrak{m}
.

Remark
. In this theorem, we could take the Casimir operator corresponding to any bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
, not necessarily
the one corresponding to our fixed background Riemannian metric on
M
.

Proof.

Observe that

(\Omega^{+}f)\circ\pi=\bar{\Omega}(f\circ\pi)=\tilde{\Omega}(f\circ\pi)\ .

Hence,

\begin{split}(\Omega^{+}f)\circ\tau_{g}\circ\pi&=(\Omega^{+}f)\circ\pi\circ L_{g}=\tilde{\Omega}(f\circ\pi)\circ L_{g}\\
&=\tilde{\Omega}(f\circ\pi\circ L_{g})=\tilde{\Omega}(f\circ\tau_{g}\circ\pi)=\Omega^{+}(f\circ\tau_{g})\circ\pi\ ,\end{split}

implying that
(\Omega^{+}f)\circ\tau_{g}=\Omega^{+}(f\circ\tau_{g})
.
Also, for
X\in\mathfrak{m}

(X^{+}\Omega^{+}f)\circ\pi=\bar{X}\bar{\Omega}(f\circ\pi)=\bar{X}{\tilde{\Omega}(f\circ\pi})=\tilde{\Omega}\bar{X}(f\circ\pi)=\bar{\Omega}\bar{X}(f\circ\pi)=(\Omega^{+}X^{+}f)\circ\pi\ .

∎

3.5.
The Laplace-Beltrami operator vs. the Casimir operator

In this section we identify, roughly speaking, the Laplace-Beltrami operator with the Casimir operator.
In the context of
rank one
symmetric spaces this statement can be found in
[
6
]
*p. 378,
and in the context of Riemannian symmetric spaces of the
noncompact
type it can be found in
[
8
]
*Ch. II, exercise A.4.
The argument for Riemannian symmetric spaces of the
compact
type (or normal homogeneous
compact
Riemannian manifolds) is essentially the same and we include it here for completeness.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
M
be a normal homogeneous compact Riemannian manifold as above. Let
\Delta_{M}
be the Laplace-Beltrami
operator on
M
and
\Omega^{+}
be the projected Casimir operator as in Theorem
3.6
.
Then,

(12)

\Delta_{M}=\Omega^{+}\ .

In particular, the operator
\Delta_{M}
can be written as a sum of squares of vector fields on
M
which commute with
\Delta_{M}
.

Proof.

Let
X_{1},\ldots X_{m},X_{m+1},\ldots X_{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the form
B
which is compatible with
the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
, i.e.
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}
for
1\leq j\leq m

and
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{k}
for
m+1\leq j\leq n
.
Observe that the projected Casimir operator
\Omega^{+}
in Theorem
3.6
takes with this choice of basis the form

\Omega^{+}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(X_{j}^{+})^{2}\ .

Since both
\Delta_{M}
and
\Omega^{+}
are
G
-invariant by Theorem
3.6
, then it
suffices to verify they coincide at the origin.
By definition

\Delta_{M}f=\mathrm{tr}(Y\mapsto\nabla_{Y}\mathrm{grad}f)\ .

Since the basis
(X_{j}^{+})_{j=1}^{m}
is orthonormal at the origin we have that

\begin{split}(\Delta_{M}f)(o)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle\nabla_{X_{j}^{+}}\mathrm{grad}f,X_{j}^{+}\rangle_{o}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(X_{j}^{+})^{2}f(o)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\nabla_{X_{j}^{+}}X_{j}^{+})_{o}f\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Prop.}~\ref{prop:nat-reductive-geodesics}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(X_{j}^{+})^{2}f(o)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(X_{j}^{+})^{2}f(o)=\Omega^{+}_{o}f\ .\end{split}

In applying Proposition
3.4
we are making use of the fact that
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{m}
for
1\leq j\leq m
and then we make use of the fact that
X_{j}^{+}|_{o}=0
for
m+1\leq j\leq n
.
∎

On Function of Evolution of Distribution for Time Homogeneous Markov Processes
Tomasz R. Bielecki, Jacek Jakubowski, Maciej Wiśniewolski

Abstract
A study of time homogeneous, real valued Markov processes with a special property and a non-atomic initial distribution is provided. The new notion of a function of evolution of distribution which determines the dependency between one dimensional distributions of a process is introduced. This, along with the notion of bridge operators which determine the backward structure, as opposed to the forward structure determined by the usual semi-group operators, paves a way to the new approach for dealing with finite-dimensional distributions of Markov processes. This, in particular, produces explicit formulas which effectively simplify the computations of finite-dimensional distributions, giving an alternative to the standard approach based on computations using the chain rule of transition densities. Various examples illustrating the new approach are presented.



Key words
: Markov processes, function of evolution of distribution, Feller processes, bridge operator, conditional dependence, Kolmogorov distance

AMS Subject Classification
: 60J35, 60H30, 91G80.

Introduction

We study a class of Markov processes with a non-atomic initial distribution and provide their special property. We introduce a new notion of a function of evolution of distribution (in short FOED), which determines the dependency between one dimensional distributions of the process. We develop an original analysis based on the so called bridge operators which determine the backward structure which is opposite to the forward structure determined by the usual semi-group operators.

The usual distributional description of a Markov process relies on determining the form of its transition density which by the classical chain rule gives the formula for finite dimensional distributions of the process (see
[
25
]
). If the transition density can not be determined explicitly the usual tool to find its approximate form involves Fokker-Planck equations. Some relatively new studies in this area can be find in
[
27
]
,
[
19
]
and in case of diffusions the older results are in
[
29
]
.

Development of various techniques to determine finite dimensional distributions of Markov processes has been motivated by their role in various applications, in particular in finance. Surprisingly, the list of Markovian models for which such distributions have not yet been described explicitly is still quite vast. These include the so called stochastic volatility models, which are systems of stochastic differential equations of various types (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]

[
22
]
,
[
2
]
). While studying a class of stochastic volatility models Jakubowski and Wiśniewolski (
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
) discovered phenomena which we present and study in this work. Specifically, for Markov process with some non-trivial initial distribution we present a new way to describe the relations between its finite dimensional distributions. This is done by introduction of a function, which we call the function of evolution of distribution, and a related property which we call the FOED property. We derive several important consequences stemming from the FOED property. It turns out that for the class of Feller processes having the FOED property a one-dimensional distribution can be determined explicitly in terms of the initial distribution and the generator. There is no need to look for the transition density.

The FOED property for a stochastic process is strictly connected to the Markov property, and in general to properties of Markov families. The notion of FOED leads to a new backward approach to analysis of a Markov process in terms of so called bridge operators. Instead of looking at the starting point and continue with classical chain rule, the bridge operators pave a way to new iteration-type formulas describing the finite dimensional distributions of Markov processes. It turns out that under some assumptions about the underlying Markov process the computations of finite dimensional distributions of the process can be reduced to dealing with independent copies of the initial distribution of this process. Last but not least, the bridge operators and the FOED property deliver a new description of conditional distributions of Markov processes.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we define the FOED property, we present properties of processes with the FOED property and we give illustrating examples.
In Section 3 we investigate Feller processes with the FOED property. In particular, we prove that a one-dimensional distribution can be determined explicitly in terms of the initial distribution and the infinitesimal generator of the process.
In Section 4 we define a bridge operator and we observe that the finite dimensional distribution of a Markov process
X
, enjoying the FOED property, may be determined in terms of bridge operators and the FOED property. This gives a new algorithmic approach to the computational problems related to finite dimensional distributions of a Markov process. The algorithm uses the initial distribution of
X
and a form of bridge operator. Using our methodology, in Section 5 we compute the expectation

\mathbb{E}\big(\Psi\big(X_{0},\ldots,X_{t_{n}}\big)\big|X_{s+t_{1}+\ldots t_{n}}=z\big)

for a Markov process
X
with the FOED property, a bounded Borel function
\Psi
, the discrete time tenor
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
and
s\geq 0
.
In Section 6 we present an application of the FOED property to finding the Kolmogorov distance
between
B_{t}
and
X_{t}
, where
B
is a Brownian motion with the initial distribution
\gamma
and
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property and the initial distribution
\gamma
.

2
Function of Evolution of Distribution

We consider a complete probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. On this probability space we consider a cádlág
process
X
with values in a Polish space
(E,\mathcal{E})

and with the initial non-atomic distribution
\gamma
. In examples we usually have
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The assumption about non-atomic
\gamma
is not restrictive since for a time-homogeneous Markov process
X
the process defined by
(Y_{t})=(X_{\epsilon+t})
,
\epsilon>0
suits our setup. By
\mathbb{F}^{X}=(\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
we denote the filtration generated by
X
and augmented to satisfy usual conditions. When we say that
X
is a Markov process, we understand that
X
is a Markov process with respect to
\mathbb{F}^{X}
. We assume that the shift operator
\theta
is well defined, so
X_{s}\circ\theta_{t}=X_{t+s}
.We start with the notion of a function of evolution of distribution.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that process
X
admits the FOED property if there exists a Borel measurable function

F:R_{+}\times E\mapsto R
such that for every
y\in E
the function
u\mapsto F(u,y)
is locally integrable and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}\big(f(X_{0})\,e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\big),

(1)

for every bounded Borel function
f

and every
t\geq 0
. We call such
F
the function of evolution of distribution for
X
.

Please note that distribution of the random variable
X_{t}
is determined by values of
\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})
for bounded Borel functions
f
, or by values of
\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})
for positive Borel functions
f
. Thus, we may replace in the above definition the class of bounded Borel functions
f
with the class of positive Borel functions
f
.

Moreover, if such
F
exists then it is uniquely determined on the support of distribution of
X_{0}
.
Also, it should be noted that in general
F
depends on the initial distribution
\gamma
, but for simplicity of notation we do not explicitly exhibit this dependence. Let observe however that the notion of the function of evolution of distribution for
X
is trivial, ergo not useful, in case of some initial distributions of
X
. For example, if
X_{0}=x
and
X
possesses the FOED property, then

X_{t}=x
a.s. for every
t
. Indeed, taking
f\equiv 1
shows that
e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)}=1
for every
t
and (
1
) implies
\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=f(x)
for every bounded Borel function
f
. For a nontrivial example, where the FOED property is not useful, it is enough to consider a
X_{t}=X_{0}+B_{t}
, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and
X_{0}
is a random variable independent of
B
, uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
.

As usual, given a process
X
, then by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
for
x\in E
we denote the probability measure defined on
\mathcal{F}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A|X_{0}=x),\quad A\in\mathcal{F},

where the right hand side should be understood in the sense of the regular conditional probabilities.

Example 2.2
.

Consider a linear stochastic volatility model, where we assume that the price
X_{t}
at time
t

of some underlying financial asset has a stochastic volatility

Y_{t}
, which has the dynamics given by a time dependent
SDE,
so the dynamics of the vector
(X,Y)
is given by

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=Y_{t}X_{t}dW_{t},

(2)

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=\mu(t,Y_{t})\ dt+\sigma(t,Y_{t})dZ_{t},

(3)

with
X_{0}=x
,
Y_{0}=y
which are positive constants. Here the processes
W,Z
are
correlated Brownian motions,
d{\left\langle W,Z\right\rangle}_{t}=\rho dt
with
\rho\in(-1,1)
, and
\mu:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are continuous functions such
that there exists a unique positive strong solution of (
3
). It turns out that for such a process
X
and independent of it gamma random variable
\gamma
with parameters
\alpha=1/2
and
\beta=1
, i.e. random variable with density
g(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi x}}e^{-x}
we have

\displaystyle d\Big(\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)=2\Big(\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)Y_{t}dW_{t}+\Big(\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}\Big)Y_{t}^{2}dt

and

\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}\in dz\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{x^{2}}{4\gamma}\in dz\Big)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}

(
[
17
, Thm. 3.5, Corr. 3.6]
). This means that process
\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}
has the FOED property.
The use of the FOED property also gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(X_{t}e^{-\lambda X_{t}^{2}}\Big)=x\exp\Big(-\lambda x^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}F(u,1/4\lambda)du\Big),\quad\lambda>0,

where
F
is the function of evolution of distribution of
\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{4\gamma}

(
[
17
, Prop.3.7]
). This shows that determining
F
gives the explicit formula for a (slightly transformed) Laplace transform of the random variable
X_{t}^{2}
.

Since we are interested in time homogenous Markov processes we start with a proposition giving a broad class of such processes admitting the FOED property for each initial distribution with strictly positive density.
In particular, the proposition gives a way to construct the FOED function
F
. By
\mathcal{C}^{1}[0,\infty)
we understand the class of functions defined on
[0,\infty)
which belong to
\mathcal{C}^{1}(0,\infty)
and have the right-continuous derivative at
0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Markov process with the transition density
p
with respect to the Lebesgue measure such that
t\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}[0,\infty)
for
x,y\in E
.
Assume that
X
has the initial distribution with the strictly positive density
g
(with respect to the Lebesgue measure).
Then
X
has the FOED property and

e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)du}=\frac{\int_{E}p_{t}(z,x)g(z)dz}{g(x)},\ \textrm{for}\ t\geq 0.

(4)

Proof.

Let
\phi(t,x)=\int_{E}p_{t}(z,x)g(z)dz,\ t>0
,
\phi(0,x)=g(x)
. Put

\displaystyle F(t,x)=\frac{\partial\phi(t,x)}{\partial t},\qquad t>0,

(5)

and
F(0,x)=\frac{1}{g(x)}\frac{\partial\phi(t,x)}{\partial t}|_{t=0^{+}}
. Then for any
t>0
and
x\in E
we have

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)du}=\frac{\phi(t,x)}{g(x)},

(6)

which implies (
1
), so
X
has the FOED property with function
F
satisfying (
4
).
∎

Example 2.4
.

Let
X
be as in Proposition
2.3
and
X_{0}=x\in E
. Let

be such that the distribution of

has the strictly positive density

.
For this

\gamma(dz)=g_{s}(z)dz

It turns out that under the above assumptions the process

on

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X_{s}}f(X_{t})=\int_{E\times E}f(z)p_{t}(v,z)g_{s}(v)dvdz=\int_{E}f(z)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}g_{s}(z)dz,

where, in line with the proof of Proposition

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}=\frac{\int_{E}p_{t}(v,z)g_{s}(v)dv}{g_{s}(z)}.

Other examples of processes with the FOED property will be given later.

In the sequel we will use the following notation

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(dz)=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in dz),\quad t>0.

The next proposition gives two immediate consequences of the FOED property.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
X
has the FOED property. Then,

a) For
0\leq s<t
and for every positive Borel function
f

\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}\big(f(X_{0})\,e^{\int_{s}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}e^{\int_{0}^{s}F(u,X_{0})du}\big)=\mathbb{E}\big(f(X_{s})\,e^{\int_{s}^{t}F(u,X_{s})du}\big).

b) The function
F
determines uniquely the density of
\frac{d\mu_{t}}{d\mu_{s}}
, namely

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(dz)=\mu_{s}(dz)e^{\int_{s}^{t}F(u,z)du},

(7)

in particular

\mu_{t}(dz)=\gamma(dz)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}
,
so

is the support of
\mu_{t}
provided that
E
is the support of
\mu_{0}
. This means the FOED property implies absolute continuity of one dimensional distributions of

Another immediate consequence of the FOED property is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(f(X_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{t})du}\big)=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0}).

(8)

Taking

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{-\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{t})du}=1.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big[f(X_{t-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=2}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})\Big]=\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big(f_{2}(X_{t_{2}-t_{1}})\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big[f(X_{t-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\Big]\Big).

On the other hand, the right-hand side of (

) is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle\Big(h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},X_{t_{2}-t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=2}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big(f_{2}(X_{t_{2}-t_{1}})\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big[h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},X_{t_{2}-t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\Big]\Big).

So from (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle\Big[f(X_{t-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2}\Big]

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{1}}\Big[h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{1}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{1}})|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2}\Big].

Observe that the last equality may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(f(X_{t-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\Big)\circ\theta_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2},X_{0}=x_{1}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},x_{2},X_{t_{3}-t_{2}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\Big)\circ\theta_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2},X_{0}=x_{1}\Big],

so by the second condition in (i) we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(f(X_{t-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\circ\theta_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\Big)\big|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},x_{2},X_{t_{3}-t_{2}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\Big)\circ\theta_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big|X_{t_{2}-t_{1}}=x_{2}\Big].

Using (
11
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle f(X_{t-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x_{2}}\Big[h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},x_{2},X_{t_{3}-t_{2}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}-t_{2}})\Pi_{i=3}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}-t_{2}})\Big].

Repeating the last procedure
(n-3)
times, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x_{n}}\Big[f(X_{t-t_{n}})\Big]=h_{1}(t,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),

which after recalling the definition of
h
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{t_{n}}}\Big[f(X_{t-t_{n}})\Big]=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})|X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}}].

This proves (
9
). The proof of implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is finished.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
The second condition in (i) follows directly from the Markov property, so we only need to show the first one. Using the argument of monotone-class theorem it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}+t})\big)=\mathbb{E}\Big(\big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})\big)\circ\theta_{t}\Big)=\mathbb{E}\big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(X_{0},u)du}\big)

(22)

for arbitrary
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
positive Borel functions on
E
.

The proof goes by induction. For
n=1
the assertion follows directly by the definition of FOED. Indeed, using Markov and FOED properties we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle f_{1}(X_{t_{1}+t})=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}+t})|\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t}\big)=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}_{X_{t}}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(X_{0},u)du}\mathbb{E}_{X_{0}}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\big)=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}\big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})|X_{0}\big)=\mathbb{E}e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}}).

Assume that the assertion holds for
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n-1}
and
(n-1)
positive, Borel functions. We conclude
from the Markov property that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}+t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}\Big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}+t})|\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t_{n-1}+t}\Big)=\mathbb{E}\Big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n-1}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}+t})\mathbb{E}_{X_{t_{n-1}+t}}f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})\Big).

From the induction assumption the last expression is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n-1}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})\Big(\mathbb{E}_{X_{t_{n-1}}}f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})\Big)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\Big),

which from the Markov property is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n-1}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})\mathbb{E}\big(f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})|\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t_{n-1}}\big)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\Big)=\mathbb{E}\Big(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\Big).

This gives (
22
).
The proof is complete.
∎

For an
\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\infty}
measurable positive or bounded random variable
Z
we
have the following representation theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that process
X
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
has the FOED property and the inital distribution with strictly positive density such that
\mathbb{E}|F(t,X_{0})|<\infty
for every
t\geq 0
. If
(X,\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in E}
is a Markov family with respect to
\mathbb{F}^{X}
, then
for an
\mathcal{F}^{X}_{\infty}
-measurable positive or bounded random variable
Z
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z\circ\theta_{t})=\mathbb{E}Z+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}(Z\circ\theta_{r})F(r,X_{r})dr.

Proof.

Using the assumption
\mathbb{E}|F(t,X_{0})|<\infty
we conclude from Theorem
2.6
for
r>0
and

\widetilde{Z}(r)=F(r,X_{0})Z
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\widetilde{Z}(r)\circ\theta_{r}\big)=\mathbb{E}\widetilde{Z}(r)e^{\int_{0}^{r}F(X_{0},u)du}.

Since
F(\cdot,y)
is locally integrable, integrating both sides with respect to
r
and using Fubini’s theorem yield for any
t>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}(r)\circ\theta_{r}dr=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}(r)e^{\int_{0}^{r}F(X_{0},u)du}dr.

Hence, upon noting that
(\widetilde{Z}(r)\circ\theta_{r})(\omega)=\widetilde{Z}(r)(\theta_{r}(\omega))=F(r,X_{0}(\theta_{r}(\omega)))Z(\theta_{r}(\omega))=(Z\circ\theta_{r})(\omega)F(r,X_{r})(\omega)
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle(Z\circ\theta_{r})F(r,X_{r})dr=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}F(r,X_{0})Ze^{\int_{0}^{r}F(u,X_{0})du}dr

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}Z\Big[e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}-1\Big]=\mathbb{E}(Z\circ\theta_{t})-\mathbb{E}Z,

where in the last equality we used again Theorem
2.6
, this time for
Z
.
∎

We end this section with a few examples of processes which are Markov and have the FOED property.

Example 2.8
.

Let
B
be the (real valued) standard Brownian motion process.

a) (A Gauss-Gauss process with a parameter
a>0
)

Consider

. Here we have

\mu_{0}(dz)=\gamma(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2a}}dz\ \textrm{and}\ \mu_{t}(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t+a)}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2(t+a)}}dz.

Thus, using Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
we conclude that
X
has the FOED property and the function of
evolution of distribution, say
F_{a}(u,z)
, satisfies

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F_{a}(u,z)du}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{a+t}}e^{\frac{z^{2}t}{2a(a+t)}}.

(23)

and thus it has the form

\displaystyle F_{a}(t,z)=-\frac{1}{2(a+t)}+\Big(\frac{1}{2a}+\frac{1}{2(a+t)^{2}}\Big)z^{2}.

(24)

b)
Consider a model analogous to Gauss-Gauss, this time built on Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. Namely, let
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
Y

\displaystyle Y_{t}=y+B_{t}-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}ds,\quad t\geq 0.

For a brief characterization of such a process see
[

. It is well known that

Y_{t}=ye^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda s}dB_{s},\quad t\geq 0.

Thus,
Y_{t}\sim N(\nu_{t},\sigma^{2}_{t})
, where

and

X_{t}=Y_{t+a}
for

Then,

\mu_{0}(dz)=\gamma(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}_{a}}}e^{-\frac{(z-\nu_{a})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{a}}}dz\ \textrm{and}\ \mu_{t}(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}_{t+a}}}e^{-\frac{(z-\nu_{t+a})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{t+a}}}dz.

Consequently, using Propositions
2.3
and
2.5
we conclude that
X
has the FOED property and the function of
evolution of distribution, say
F_{a}(u,z)
, satisfies

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F_{a}(u,z)du}=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}_{a}}{\sigma^{2}_{t+a}}}e^{-\frac{(z-\nu_{t+a})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{t+a}}+\frac{(z-\nu_{a})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}_{a}}}.

(25)

Clearly, with
y=\lambda=0
the result (
25
) agrees with (
23
).

In
[
3
, App. I, point 24]
the transition density for
X
is given relative to the speed measure of

. Accordingly, it can be shown that

\mu_{t}(dz)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sinh(\lambda t)}}\exp\Big\{\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big[t+y^{2}+z^{2}-(y^{2}+z^{2})\coth(\lambda t)+\frac{2zy}{\sinh(\lambda t)}\Big]\Big\}m(dz),

where

is the speed measure for
X
. Given that, the ”speed measure” representation for the function of evolution of distribution takes the form

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}=\sqrt{\frac{\sinh(\lambda a)}{\sinh(\lambda(a+t))}}\exp\Big\{

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big(t+(y^{2}+z^{2})(\coth(\lambda a)-\coth(\lambda(a+t)))

\displaystyle+2yz\big(\frac{1}{\sinh(\lambda a)}-\frac{1}{\sinh(\lambda(a+t))}\big)\Big)\Big\}.

The ”speed measure” representation approach will be taken in part c) below as well.

c) Consider a model built on a squared Bessel process. Namely, let
y\geq 0
,
\delta\geq 0
and
Y
be the unique strong solution of

\displaystyle Y_{t}=y+2\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{Y_{s}}dB_{s}+\delta t,\quad t\geq 0.

For a brief characterization of such a process see

. It is well known that

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(dz)=\frac{1}{t}\big({yz}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{\delta}{2})}\exp\Big(-\frac{y+z}{2t}\Big)I_{\frac{\delta}{2}-1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{yz}}{t}\Big)m(dz),

where

denotes the modified Bessel function with index

. Then for fixed

and
X_{t}=Y_{t+a}
, the process

)

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}=\frac{a}{a+t}\exp\Big\{\frac{(y+z)t}{2a(a+t)}\Big\}\frac{I_{\frac{\delta}{2}-1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{yz}}{t+a}\Big)}{I_{\frac{\delta}{2}-1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{yz}}{a}\Big)}.

3
Feller processes with the FOED property

When we deal with Feller processes we will make standard assumption that
E
is a LCCB space. Let
X
is be a cádlág Feller process (in the sense of
[
25
, Ch. III]
) admitting function
F
of evolution of distribution and with the infinite lifetime. By
\mathcal{C}_{0}(E)
we denote the space of bounced and continuous functions on
E

vanishing at infinity, and by
\mathbb{D}_{A}\subset\mathcal{C}_{0}(E)
we denote the domain of infinitesimal generator of
X
.
By
C^{2}_{c}(E)
we denote the space of continuous functions on
E
with compact support and both first and second derivatives continuous.

The next result shows how one-dimensional distributions of
X
depend on
A
and
F.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{D}_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})+Af(X_{0})\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}F(u,X_{0})du}ds\Big),\quad t\geq 0.

(26)

Proof.

Since
f\in\mathbb{D}_{A}
the process
f(X_{t})-f(X_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}Af(X_{s})ds
is a martingale, so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}Af(X_{s})ds\Big).

Because
Af\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(E)
we conclude from Fubini’s theorem and the FOED property that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}Af(X_{s})ds=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}Af(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{s}F(u,X_{0})du}ds

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})+Af(X_{0})\int_{0}^{t}e^{\int_{0}^{s}F(u,X_{0})du}ds\Big).

The proof is complete.
∎

If
t\mapsto F(t,z)\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for each
z\in E
, then we define a sequence of functions
\mathcal{L}^{m}F:[0,\infty)\times E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{0}F\equiv 1,\ \mathcal{L}^{1}F\equiv F,\ \mathcal{L}^{m+1}F=\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}^{m}F+F\mathcal{L}^{m}F,\quad m\leq n.

(27)

Next, define

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{(n)}_{A}=\{f\in\mathbb{D}_{A}:A^{i}f\in\mathbb{D}_{A}\ {\rm for}\ i\leq n\},

and
A^{n+1}f=(A\circ A^{n})f
for
f\in\mathbb{D}^{(n)}_{A}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
A^{1}=A
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that the function
t\mapsto F(t,z)\in\mathcal{C}^{n}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for each
z\in E
and
\mathbb{E}|F(t,X_{t})|<\infty
for each
t\geq 0
. Let
f\in\mathbb{D}^{(n)}_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(A^{n}f(X_{t}))=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{t})\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,X_{t})\Big)

(28)

for every
t>0
.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction.
For
n=1
using Theorem
2.7
for
Z=f(X_{0})
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0})+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}f(X_{s})F(s,X_{s})ds.

On the other hand we have for
f\in C^{2}_{c}(E)
(see
[
25
, Remark p.295]
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f(X_{t})=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0})+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}Af(X_{s})ds.

Comparing both equalities, using Fubini’s theorem and taking derivative with respect to
t
yield the assertion.

Assume now that identity (
28
) is true for any
k\leq n-1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}A^{n}f(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}A(A^{n-1}f(X_{t}))=\mathbb{E}F(t,X_{t})A^{n-1}f(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}F(t,X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}A^{n-1}f(X_{0}),

(29)

where in the last equality we used the assumption that
X
has the FOED property.

Next, by assumption
\mathbb{E}|F(0,X_{0})|<\infty
and
f\in\mathbb{D}^{(n)}_{A}
, so
z\mapsto A^{n-1}f(z)
is bounded and we may differentiate with respect to
t
under expectation to conclude that RHS of (
3
) is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}F(t,X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}A^{n-1}f(X_{0})=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\mathbb{E}e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}A^{n-1}f(X_{0})\Big).

By the FOED property and the induction assumption we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\mathbb{E}e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}A^{n-1}f(X_{0})\Big)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\mathbb{E}A^{n-1}f(X_{t})\Big)=\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{t})\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{t})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})\Big)=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})\frac{d}{dt}\Big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})\Big)\Big),

which is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})\Big(F(t,X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})+e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})\Big)\Big).

Thus, using the above as well as (
27
) and the FOED property, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle A^{n}f(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{0})\Big(F(t,X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})+e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}^{n-1}F(t,X_{0})\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}f(X_{0})\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,X_{0})du}=\mathbb{E}\Big(f(X_{t})\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,X_{t})\Big).

The proof is complete.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Consider the Gauss-Gauss process from Example
2.8
with parameter
a>0
. We find a description of the functions
\mathcal{L}^{m}F
in terms of the density of
X_{t}
.

The assumptions of Proposition
3.2
are satisfied. Let
g_{t+a}
denote the density of
B_{t+a}
. Using formula (
28
) for
f(x)=e^{-\lambda x}
with
\lambda>0
and
X_{t}=B_{t+a}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2n}}{2^{n}}\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda B_{t+a}}=\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda B_{t+a}}\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,B_{t+a}),\qquad t>0.

Let now
f(s)=1_{\{s>0\}}\frac{s^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}
and
h(z)=\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,z)g_{t+a}(z)
. Hence, since
\lambda^{-2n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}\frac{t^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}ds=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}f(s)ds
, then, using the convolution theorem for Laplace transforms, we obtain the following chain of equalities for each
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda B_{t+a}}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\lambda^{-2n}\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda B_{t+a}}\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,B_{t+a})

\displaystyle=2^{n}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda s}f(s)ds\Big)\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda z}g(z)dz\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda v}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(v-z)h(z)dz\right)dv

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda v}\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma(2n)}\int_{-\infty}^{v}(v-z)^{2n-1}g_{t+a}(z)\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,z)dz\right)dv,

so that we obtain an integral equation for
\mathcal{L}^{n}F

\displaystyle g_{t+a}(v)=\frac{2^{n}}{\Gamma(2n)}\int_{-\infty}^{v}(v-z)^{2n-1}g_{t+a}(z)\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,z)dz.

Using the Leibnitz rule iteratively we can compute
\mathcal{L}^{n}F(t,z)
. For example, for
n=1
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{1}F(t,v)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}_{t+a}(v)}{g_{t+a}(v)}.

∎

4
Bridge operators

In order to study the finite dimensional distributions of a Markov process
X
with the initial distribution
\gamma
having
E
as the support and admitting the FOED property, we introduce a bridge operator.

Towards this end we consider a Banach space
L^{1}(\gamma):=L^{1}(E,\mathcal{B}(E),\gamma)
with the norm given by
\left\|h\right\|=\mathbb{E}|h(X_{0})|
.
For
t\geq 0
and for
f\in L^{1}(\gamma)
we denote

\Lambda_{t}f=\mathbb{E}(f(X_{0})\big|X_{t}).

For each
t\geq 0
the operator
f\mapsto\Lambda_{t}f
is a linear contraction on
L^{1}(\gamma)
as well as on
L^{2}(\gamma)
.
We can treat
\Lambda_{t}f
as a function on
E
, namely

\Lambda_{t}f(z)=h(z),z\in E
, where
h
is a Borel function such that
\mathbb{E}\big(f(X_{0})|X_{t}\big)=h(X_{t})
,
so

\Lambda_{t}f(z)=\mathbb{E}(f(X_{0})\big|X_{t}=z).

We will always assume that
(t,z)\mapsto\Lambda_{t}(f)(z)
is a measurable function.
Observe also that for a Markov process
X
with the FOED property, with the initial distribution
\gamma(dz)=g(z)dz
and with the transition density
p_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
we have a nice formula for
\Lambda_{t}f

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}f(z)=\frac{\int_{E}f(v)p_{t}(v,z)\mu_{0}(dv)}{\mu_{t}(dz)/dz}=\frac{\int_{E}f(v)p_{t}(v,z)\gamma(dv)}{g(z)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}},\quad f\in L^{1}(\gamma),

(30)

where for the second equality we used (
7
). So in this case clearly
(t,z)\mapsto\Lambda_{t}f(z)
is measurable.
We will call the operator
\Lambda_{t}
the
bridge operator
.

We will demonstrate below that the finite dimensional distributions of
X
can be described in terms of the bridge operators
(\Lambda_{t})_{t>0}
and the FOED function. This gives a new algorithmic approach to the computational problems related to finite dimensional distributions of a Markov process. The algorithm uses the initial distribution
\gamma
and the form of bridge operator given by (
30
). The first step to achieve this goal is is the theorem below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. Assume that
f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
are Borel and bounded functions on
E
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

(31)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big).

Proof.

Notice that each successive component of
the RHS of (
31
) is well defined since
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are Borel bounded functions on
E
. Let
c
be such that
\max_{i\leq n}\|f_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq c
.

The proof of (
31
) goes by induction. For
n=1
, we see that she function
f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\gamma)
since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|f_{1}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(X_{0})\Big|\leq c^{2}\mathbb{E}e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,X_{0})du}=c^{2},

and then using the definitions of
\Lambda_{t}
and having the FOED property we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})=\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(X_{t_{1}})=\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(X_{0}).

For the induction step suppose that identity (
31
) holds for
n-1
and for bounded Borel functions
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n-1}
. Using the Markov property and the induction assumption we obtain the following chain of equalities

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots\ \mathbb{E}_{X_{t_{n-1}}}f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n-1}(X_{0})\mathbb{E}_{X_{0}}f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n-1}(X_{0})f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n-1}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(X_{0})\Big|X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(\cdot)\Big)\Big(X_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big)\Big).

By the FOED property the last expression is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(\cdot)\Big)\big(X_{0}\big)\Big),

and the assertion follows.
∎

For a fixed
t>0
denote

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}=\{h\in L^{1}(\gamma):\Lambda_{t}h=0\quad\gamma-a.s.\}.

It follows from standard arguments of functional analysis that
\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}
is a closed subspace of
L^{1}(\gamma)
.
Any
f\in L^{1}(\gamma)
decomposes

\displaystyle f=\Lambda_{t}f+(f)^{\bot}_{t},\qquad t>0,

(32)

where
(f)^{\bot}_{t}\in\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}
. We are ready to formulate the result, which gives a simple recurrent formula for determining the distribution of a vector
(X_{0},X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}})
. The formula is strictly connected with the notion of kernel of a bridge operator. We will need the following definition: For a given non-negative integer
n
,
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
and for bounded Borel functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
define

\displaystyle\Xi^{(n)}_{[f_{1},..,f_{n}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(z)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,z)du},\quad n\geq 1,\ z\in E.

(33)

We will also need the following class of functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\{g\in L^{1}(\gamma):\quad\alpha\in\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{s}\ {\rm for}\ {\rm all}\ s>0\Rightarrow\ g\alpha\in\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}\ {\rm for}\ {\rm all}\ t>0\}.

(34)

Actually, for a broad class of diffusions we may expect that
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
.

Example 4.2
.

Consider a Gauss-Gauss process, that is
X_{t}=B_{t+a}
, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and
a>0
is a fixed number, so
\gamma(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2a}}dz
. Then
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
. Indeed, first observe that if
h\in L^{1}(\gamma)
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(h(B_{a})\big|B_{t+a}=z\big)=0,

(35)

for each
z\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(h(B_{a})\big|B_{t+a}\big)=0.

It follows that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\big(h(B_{a})\big|B_{t+a}\big)e^{\lambda B_{t+a}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(h(B_{a})e^{\lambda B_{t+a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(h(B_{a})e^{\lambda(B_{t+a}-B_{a})}e^{\lambda B_{a}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\lambda(B_{t+a}-B_{a})}\right)\mathbb{E}\left(h(B_{a})e^{\lambda B_{a}}\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(h(B_{a})e^{\lambda B_{a}}\right)=0

for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and so
h\equiv 0
. This yields that
\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}=\{0\}
, so
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
.

By a similar arguments we may conclude analogous results for an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, a Bessel process and many other processes. We omit the details.

Using the notion of class
\mathcal{K}
we are able to give a description of
\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})
in terms of distribution of
X_{0}
, which allows to compute the distribution of vector
(X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}})
for
X
having the FOED property in terms of distribution of
X_{0}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. Assume that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
are bounded Borel functions. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})=\mathbb{E}\Xi^{(1)}_{[f_{1}]}(t_{1},X_{0}).

Moreover, if for
n\geq 2
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
it holds

f_{n-1}(\cdot)\cdots f_{j}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in\mathcal{K},

then for
n>1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})=\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big[\Xi^{(n)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X_{0})

(36)

\displaystyle-f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\big(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\cdot)\big)^{\bot}_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(X_{0})\Big].

Proof.

Step 1.
Let
n=1
. By definition, for
t_{1}>0
and
z\in E

\displaystyle\Xi^{(1)}_{[f_{1}]}(t_{1},z)=f_{1}(z)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z)du}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[f_{1}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Big]=\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}}),

where in that last equality we used the definition of FOED. This proves the assertion for
n=1
.

Step 2.

We recall formula (
31
) for
f_{0}\equiv 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})

\displaystyle\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

(37)

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}1\Big)(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big).

Take
n\geq 2
. Set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{t_{1}}1(z);

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{i}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}\Big(f_{i-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}-t_{i-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{i-1}-t_{i-2}}\Big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}1\Big)(\cdot)\Big)(z),\quad i\geq 2,\ z\in E,

so, using (
37
), we may write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})=\mathbb{E}\Big(

\displaystyle f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n-1}(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big),

which is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big(f_{n-2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}-t_{n-3}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n-2}(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big).

We rewrite the last expression using decomposition (
32
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Big(f_{n-2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}-t_{n-3}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n-2}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\quad-\Big(f_{n-2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}-t_{n-3}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n-2}(\cdot)\Big)^{\bot}_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\Big)(X_{0})\Big)=:\mathcal{I}.

By assumption
f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in\mathcal{K}
, so we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}f_{n-2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}-t_{n-3}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{n-2}(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big),

and, after iterating in the analogous way, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}1(\cdot)\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

Since
\Lambda_{t_{1}}1=1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\ldots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}

\displaystyle\quad-\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\big)^{\bot}_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\prod_{i=0}^{n}f_{i}(X_{0})

\displaystyle\quad-f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)f_{n-2}(\cdot)\cdots f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\big)^{\bot}_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Xi^{(n)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X_{0})

\displaystyle\quad-f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\big(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\cdot)\big)^{\bot}_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(X_{0})\Big].

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let us comment on a possible use of formula (
36
). Suppose that we know the distribution
\gamma
of
X_{0}
and the transition density
p_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
of a Markov process
X
. In the classical forward approach, in order to compute
\mathbb{E}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})\big)
we need to compute a multiple integral involving a chain of transition densities. The alternative backward approach is completely different and uses the notion of bridge operator and its kernel. We have the following algorithm:

Step 1. We determine
e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}
as

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}=\frac{\mu_{t}(dz)}{\gamma(dz)},\quad\mu_{t}(dz)=\int_{E}p_{t}(v,dz)\gamma(dv).

This is needed for the computation of the family of objects
(\Xi^{(i)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}]}(t,z))_{t>0,i\leq n}
.

Step 2. Using (
33
) we compute
(\Xi^{(i)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}]}(t_{0},\ldots,t_{i},z))_{0<t_{1}<..<t_{i}\leq t,i\leq n}
in straightforward recursive way.

Step 3. To determine the distribution of
(X_{0},X_{t_{1}},\ldots,X_{t_{n}})
by applying Theorem
4.3
we need to verify that for
n\geq 2
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
it holds

f_{n-1}(\cdot)\cdots f_{j}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in\mathcal{K}.

Once this is verified, we then follow the recurrence (
36
) using formulas (
30
) and (
32
).

In the corollary below we see that under appropriate assumptions the FOED property reduces the complexity of computational problems related to finite-dimensional distributions of a Markov process. The standard approach relies on computations based on chain rule of transition densities. The FOED backward approach offers the new possibility of dealing with such computations.

Corollary 4.5
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
4.3
and under the assumption that
X_{0}
has a density
g>0
, formula (
36
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})=\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{f_{n}(X_{0})}{g(X_{0})}\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},\widehat{X}_{0})p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\widehat{X}_{0},X_{0})\Big],

(38)

where
\widehat{X}_{0}
is an independent copy of
X_{0}
.

Proof.

Using (
32
) we rewrite (
36
) in the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Xi^{(n)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X_{0})-f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},X_{0})-\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\cdot))(X_{0})\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\cdot))(X_{0})\Big).

By (
30
) the last expression is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{f_{n}(X_{0})}{g(X_{0})}\int_{E}(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},v)p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(v,X_{0})g(v)dv)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{f_{n}(X_{0})}{g(X_{0})}\mathbb{E}\left(\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\widehat{X}_{0})p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\widehat{X}_{0},X_{0})|X_{0}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(\frac{f_{n}(X_{0})}{g(X_{0})}\Xi^{(n-1)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1},\widehat{X}_{0})p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\widehat{X}_{0},X_{0})\Big),

where the first equality is a consequence of the independence lemma (see e.g.
[
21
]
, page 72, Rule 7). This finishes the proof.
∎

Using Theorem
4.3
we are able to determine the finite-dimensional distributions of the process
e^{X}
for various processes
X
satisfying the FOED property.

Example 4.6
.

Let
X
be a Gauss-Gauss or an Ornstein-Uhnlenbeck process. We saw in Example
2.8
that
e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}
is a function of order
e^{cz^{2}}
with respect to
z
for a constant
c>0
dependent on
t
. Since, as shown in Example
4.2
,
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
, then to apply Theorem
4.3
we need to verify integrability of the functions

f_{n-1}(\cdot)\cdots f_{j}(\cdot)e^{\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}

for
j\leq n-1
.
This will always hold if we choose
f_{i}(z)=e^{-\lambda_{i}e^{z}}
for
\lambda_{i}>0
,
i=1,..,n
. In this case formula (
38
) enables us to establish a formula for Laplace transform for vector of exponents of
X_{t_{i}}
,
i=1,..,n
, namely

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}e^{X_{t_{i}}}}=\mathbb{E}\Big(e^{-\lambda_{n}e^{X_{0}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}e^{\widetilde{X}_{0}}}e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\widetilde{X}_{0})du}\frac{p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(\widetilde{X}_{0},X_{0})}{g(X_{0})}\Big),

where
\widetilde{X}_{0}
is an independent copy of
X_{0}
. In terms of computation the above equality provides a reduction of a multivariate integration to bivariate integration.

Under additional assumptions we are able to describe the distribution of vector
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
in terms of distribution of
X_{0}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume that
X
is a Markov process with the FOED property,
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
and

e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in L^{1}(\gamma)
for
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. Then for bounded Borel functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X_{t_{i}})=\mathbb{E}\Xi^{(n)}_{[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},X_{0}).

(39)

Proof.

If
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
, then
\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}=\{0\}
for each
t>0
. Indeed, take
\alpha\in\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}
.
Since
\alpha\in L^{1}(\gamma)=\mathcal{K}
, by definition of
\mathcal{K}
it follows that
\alpha^{2}\in\operatorname{Ker}\Lambda_{t}
which easily yields that
\alpha\equiv 0
. The assertion follows from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Then formula (
39
) becomes powerful. Let us see this on the following example.

Example 4.8
.

Consider a Gauss-Gauss process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(B_{t+a})_{t\geq 0}
for a fixed
a>0
. Hence

\displaystyle\gamma(dz)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2a}},\qquad e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{a+t}}e^{\frac{z^{2}t}{2a(a+t)}}.

Let for a given natural
n
,
t_{i}=i\leq n
and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
be bounded Borel functions. Then we easily find that the condition

e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in L^{1}(\gamma)
holds if
n\leq a+1
. Since
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
the formula (
39
) is true in that case.

Example 4.9
.

Let
\gamma(dx)=g(x)dx
with
g>0
. Suppose that
X
is a Markov process with the transition density
p

such that
t\mapsto p_{t}(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}[0,\infty)
for
x,y\in E
.
Then by Proposition
2.3
the process
X
has the FOED property and

\displaystyle e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)du}=\frac{\int_{E}p_{t}(z,x)g(z)dz}{g(x)}\leq\mathbb{E}k(t,X_{0}),

where
k(t,z)=\sup_{x}\frac{p_{t}(z,x)}{g(x)},\ t\geq 0,\ z\in E.
If, moreover,
\mathbb{E}k(t_{i}-t_{i-1},X_{0})<\infty
for each
i
,
then

\displaystyle e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,z)du}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}k(t_{i}-t_{i-1},X_{0}),

which means that
e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\in L^{1}(\gamma)
. If additionally
\mathcal{K}=L^{1}(\gamma)
, assumptions of Corrolary
4.7
are satisfied.

5
Conditional structures and FOED

One of the consequences of the FOED property and of the definition of bridge operators is a convenient computation of conditional structures of Markov processes. By a conditional structure of a Markov process we mean a function of the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\Psi(X_{0},X_{t_{1}})\cdots,X_{t_{n}})\Big|X_{s+t_{1}+\ldots t_{n}}=z\Big),\quad z\in E,

(40)

for an integrable Borel function
\Psi:E^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
and
s\geq 0
. A particular conditional structure takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\Big|X_{s+t_{1}+\ldots t_{n}}=z\Big),\quad z\in E,

(41)

for a sequence
(f_{i})_{i=0,\ldots,n}
of bounded Borel functions,
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
and
s\geq 0
.
Such conditioning is motivated by use of the FOED property in what follows.
The conditional structures and theory of stochastic bridges are strictly connected.
For a Markov process with the FOED property using the bridge operators
(\Lambda_{t})_{t\geq 0}
we can give a full characterization of objects given by (
41
). For
s\geq 0
and
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
let

\displaystyle T^{s}_{k,n}=s+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i},

and let
\mu_{t}
be the distribution of
X_{t}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property and the initial distribution
\gamma
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\geq 0
and
0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
. Assume that
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are bounded Borel functions. Then a.e. with respect to the Lebesgue measure

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{0}(X_{0})

\displaystyle f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\Big|X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}=z\Big)=e^{-\int_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}^{T^{s}_{n,n}}F(u,z)du}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Lambda_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\Big(f_{n}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\Big(\ldots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\Big)(\cdot)\Big)(z).

(42)

Proof.

Let
B\in\mathcal{E}
. By the Markov property and Theorem
4.1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\in B\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{0}(X_{0})f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\mathbb{P}_{X_{t_{n}}}\big(X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in B\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathbb{P}_{X_{0}}\big(X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in B\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\mathbb{E}\big(1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in B\}}\big|\mathcal{F}^{X}_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(X_{0})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big(1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in B\}}f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(X_{0})\Big).

Rewriting the last expression by conditioning with respect to
X_{s+t_{1}+\ldots+t_{n-1}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbb{E}\big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}

\displaystyle\quad\times\ \Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(X_{0})\big|X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\big)\ 1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in B\}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\Lambda_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\Big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(z)\Big)\mathbb{P}\big(X_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\in dz\big).

Denoting

\displaystyle R_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}\big(f_{n}(X_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,X_{0})du}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\cdots\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(z),

we conclude after comparing the first and the last term of the above sequence of equalities that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})

\displaystyle 1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\in B\}}\big)=\int_{B}R_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)\mu_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(dz).

(43)

On the other hand

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})

\displaystyle\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\in B\}}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(\mathbb{E}\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\big|X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\big)1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\in B\}}\big),

(44)

so denoting

\displaystyle S_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)=\mathbb{E}\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})

\displaystyle\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\big|X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}=z\big),\quad z\in E,

we can write (
5
) in the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})

\displaystyle 1_{\{X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}\in B\}}\Big)=\int_{B}S_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)\mu_{T^{s}_{n,n}}(dz).

Compare this and (
43
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B}R_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)\mu_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(dz)=\int_{B}S_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)\mu_{T^{s}_{n,n}}(dz),

which by the FOED property is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{B}R_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)\mu_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(dz)=\int_{B}S_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)e^{\int_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}^{{T^{s}_{n,n}}}F(u,z)du}\mu_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(dz).

The arbitrary choice of
B
yields
z
- a.e.

\displaystyle S_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z)=e^{-\int_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}^{{T^{s}_{n,n}}}F(u,z)du}R_{s,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}(z),

which is exactly our assertion.
∎

In the next theorem we give a computation of the conditional structure (
40
).
We start with an auxiliary result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a Markov process having the FOED property.
For any
s,t>0
and
x\in E
we have

\displaystyle p_{s+t}(x,w)\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\in dw)=p_{s}(x,w)\mathbb{P}(X_{t+s}\in dw).

(45)

Proof.

By the FOED property, and the time homogeneity of
X
, we have for any
s,t>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{s+t}\in dw)=\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\in dw)e^{\int_{s}^{t+s}F(u,w)du},

which actually means that

\displaystyle p_{s+t}(x,w)\gamma(dx)=p_{s}(x,w)e^{\int_{s}^{t+s}F(u,w)du}\gamma(dx).

This yields
\gamma
- a.s.

\displaystyle p_{s+t}(x,w)=p_{s}(x,w)e^{\int_{s}^{t+s}F(u,w)du},

so

\displaystyle p_{s+t}(x,w)\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\in dw)=p_{s}(x,w)e^{\int_{s}^{t+s}F(u,w)du}\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\in dw),

which by the FOED property, and the time homogeneity of
X
, becomes

\displaystyle p_{s+t}(x,w)\mathbb{P}(X_{s}\in dw)=p_{s}(x,w)\mathbb{P}(X_{t+s}\in dw).

The proof is complete.
∎

For a transition density
p_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
and a measure
\gamma
we will use the notation

\displaystyle\gamma^{n+1}(d(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}))=\gamma(dz_{0})\otimes\ldots\otimes\gamma(dz_{n}),\qquad g_{t}(z)=\int_{E}p_{t}(v,z)\gamma(dv).

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property with the initial distribution
\gamma
and the transition density
p_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\geq 0
and

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}

\Psi:E^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Borel function such that
\mathbb{E}|\Psi(X_{0},\ldots,X_{t_{n}})|<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big(\Psi\big(X_{0},\ldots,X_{t_{n}}\big)\big|X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}=w\big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\int_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}^{T^{s}_{n,n}}F(u,w)du}\int_{E^{n+1}}\Psi(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{p_{t_{i}}(z_{i-1},z_{i})}{g_{t_{i}}(z_{i-1})}\frac{p_{t_{n}}(z_{n},w)}{g_{t_{n}}(w)}

\displaystyle\times e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,z_{i})du}\gamma^{n+1}(d(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})).

(46)

Proof.

First we prove that formula (
5.3
) holds for

\Psi(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})=f_{0}(z_{0})\cdots f_{n}(z_{n}),

where
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
are bounded Borel functions.

For a bounded Borel function
f
we will determine the form of
\Lambda_{t}f
.
By (
30
) and Proposition
2.3
we have, for any
t>0
and
w\in E
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}f(w)=\int_{E}f(v)\frac{p_{t}(v,w)\gamma(dv)}{\int_{E}p_{t}(r,w)\gamma(dr)}.

Let us define a function
(t,w)\rightarrow\eta_{t,w}
, where
\eta_{t,w}
is a measure on
[0,\infty)
given by

\displaystyle\eta_{t,w}(dv)=\frac{p_{t}(v,w)\gamma(dv)}{\int_{E}p_{t}(r,w)\gamma(dr)},

(47)

so

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}f(w)=\int_{E}f(v)\eta_{t,w}(dv).

(48)

Now, we compute the succesive inner components of the RHS of (
5.1
). By (
48
) we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big(f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)(w)=\int_{E}f_{1}(v)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,v)}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(v)\eta_{t_{2}-t_{1},w}(dv).

(49)

Next, for
n=3
, using formulas (
49
) and (
48
) we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t_{3}-t_{2}}\big(f_{2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\big(f_{1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}\big)\big)(w)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{t_{3}-t_{2}}\Big(f_{2}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Big(\int_{E}f_{1}(z_{1})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z_{1})du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(z_{1})\eta_{t_{2}-t_{1},\cdot}(dz_{1})\Big)\Big)(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\Big(f_{2}(z_{2})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,z_{2})du}\Big(\int_{E}f_{1}(z_{1})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z_{1})du}\Lambda_{t_{1}}f_{0}(z_{1})\eta_{t_{2}-t_{1},z_{2}}(dz_{1})\Big)\Big)\eta_{t_{3}-t_{2},w}(dz_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\Big(f_{2}(z_{2})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,z_{2})du}\Big(\int_{E}f_{1}(z_{1})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z_{1})du}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\int_{E}f_{0}(z_{0})\eta_{t_{1},z_{1}}(dz_{0})\eta_{t_{2}-t_{1},z_{2}}(dz_{1})\Big)\Big)\eta_{t_{3}-t_{2},w}(dz_{2}),

which, using Fubini theorem, we rewrite as

\displaystyle\int_{E}\int_{E}\int_{E}f_{2}(z_{2})f_{1}(z_{1})f_{0}(z_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,z_{2})du+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z_{1})du}\eta_{t_{1},z_{1}}(dz_{0})\eta_{t_{2}-t_{1},z_{2}}(dz_{1})\eta_{t_{3}-t_{2},w}(dz_{2}).

By formula (

this is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{E}

\displaystyle\int_{E}\int_{E}f_{2}(z_{2})f_{1}(z_{1})f_{0}(z_{0})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{2}-t_{1}}F(u,z_{2})du+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F(u,z_{1})du}

\displaystyle\times\quad\frac{p_{t_{3}-t_{2}}(z_{2},w)p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(z_{1},z_{2})p_{t_{1}}(z_{0},z_{1})}{g_{t_{3}-t_{2}}(z_{2})g_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(z_{1})g_{t_{1}}(z_{0})}\gamma(dz_{2})\gamma(dz_{1})\gamma(dz_{0}).

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\big(f_{n-1}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Lambda_{t_{n-1}-t_{n-2}}\big(\ldots\big)\big)(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\ldots\int_{E}f_{n-1}(z_{n-1})\ldots f_{0}(z_{0})e^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}F(u,z_{i})du}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}p_{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}(z_{i-1},z_{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}g_{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}(z_{i-1})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\frac{p_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(z_{n-1},w)}{g_{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}(z_{n-1})}\gamma(dz_{0})\ldots\gamma(dz_{n-1}).

(50)

Denote the last expression by
\Theta(n)(w)
. Using (

\displaystyle\Lambda_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}

\displaystyle\Big(f_{n}(\cdot)e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,\cdot)du}\Theta(n)(\cdot)\Big)(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}f_{n}(z_{n})e^{\int_{0}^{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}F(u,z_{n})du}\Theta(n)(z_{n})\frac{p_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(z_{n},w)}{g_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(w)}\gamma(dz_{n}).

) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\big(f_{1}(X_{t_{1}})\cdots f_{n}(X_{t_{n}})\big|X_{T^{s}_{n,n}}=w\big)

\displaystyle\frac{p_{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}(z_{i-1},z_{i})}{g_{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}(z_{i-1})}=\frac{p_{t_{i}}(z_{i-1},z_{i})}{g_{t_{i}}(z_{i-1})},\qquad\quad\frac{p_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(z_{n},w)}{g_{T^{s}_{n-1,n}}(w)}=\frac{p_{t_{n}}(z_{n},w)}{g_{t_{n}}(w)},

) holds for

The general case, that is (

) for a Borel functions
\Psi:E^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

follows by using a monotone convergence theorem.

The proof is complete.
∎

6
FOED and Kolmogorov distance: example

In our considerations in this section we use the FOED property to solve the following problem: Consider a Brownian motion
B
with the initial distribution
\gamma
on
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property. Our task is to measure the similarity between
B_{t}
and
X_{t}
, for fixed
t
, if
X_{0}
has a distribution
\gamma
.
In statistics, the Kolmogorov distance is a convenient way of measuring similarity of distributions (see
[
31
]
for an overview). For a couple of nice recent results measuring the Kolmogorov distance between the general Poisson random variables and Gaussian distribution see for example
[
5
]
.
Recall that the Kolmogorov distance between real valued random variables
X
and
Y
is defined as

\displaystyle d_{Kol}(X,Y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}(X\leq x)-\mathbb{P}(Y\leq x)\big|.

We will show that for computation Kolmogorov distance
d_{Kol}(X_{t},B_{t})
it suffices to consider the supremum over a special set that is related to the FOED function and the distribution
\gamma
. We use below the common notation: by
\Phi
we denote the cumulative distribution function of a standard Gaussian random variable
G
and by
\phi
the p.d.f of
G
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a Markov process having the FOED property with the function of evolution of distribution
F
,
X_{0}
has a distribution
\gamma
and a differentiable density
g
. Let
B
be a Brownian motion with the initial distribution
\gamma
. Fix
t>0
. Let
\mathcal{X}_{0}
be the set of solutions of the following equation with respect to variable
x

\displaystyle g(x)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)du}=\mathbb{E}1_{\{G\leq 0\}}\big(g(x-\sqrt{t}G)+g(x+\sqrt{t}G)\big).

(51)

Then

\displaystyle d_{Kol}

\displaystyle(X_{t},B_{t})

(52)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}}\Big|\int_{-\infty}^{x_{0}}\Big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}-\Phi\Big(\frac{x_{0}-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(z)dz-\int_{x_{0}}^{\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{x_{0}-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(z)dz\Big|.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{t}\leq x)=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(z-y)^{2}}{2t}}dy\Big)g(z)dz=\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(z)dz.

Hence and from the fact that
X
has the FOED property we have

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\leq x)-\mathbb{P}(B_{t}\leq x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}g(z)dz-\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(z)dz,

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(1_{(-\infty,\ x]}(z)e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(z)dz

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(z)dz-\int_{x}^{\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(z)dz.

(53)

Substituting in the last integral
w=2x-z
yields

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(z)dz=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Phi\Big(\frac{w-x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(2x-w)dw.

Hence

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big[\Big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(z)-\Phi\Big(\frac{z-x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g(2x-z)\Big]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big[\Big(e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(z)-\Big(1-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big)g(2x-z)\Big]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big[e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,z)du}g(z)-\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big(g(z)-g(2x-z)\Big)-g(2x-z)\Big]dz.

The last representation of
\psi
clearly shows that
\psi
is differentiable.
We have

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}F(u,x)du}g(x)-g(x)+\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big[

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big(g(z)-g(2x-z)\Big)

\displaystyle+2\Phi\Big(\frac{x-z}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)g^{\prime}(2x-z)-2g^{\prime}(2x-z)\Big]dz.

Concurrent Donsker-Varadhan and hydrodynamical large deviations
L. Bertini, D. Gabrielli, C. Landim

Abstract
. We consider the weakly asymmetric exclusion process on the d d -dimensional torus. We prove a large deviations principle for the time averaged empirical density and current in the joint limit in which both the time interval and the number of particles diverge. This result is obtained both by analyzing the variational convergence, as the number of particles diverges, of the Donsker-Varadhan functional for the empirical process and by considering the large time behavior of the hydrodynamical rate function. The large deviations asymptotic of the time averaged current is then deduced by contraction principle. The structure of the minimizers of this variational problem corresponds to the possible occurrence of dynamical phase transitions.

1.
Introduction

Stochastic lattice gases, that describe the evolution of interacting
random particles on a lattice of mesh
1/N
, have been an instrumental
tool in the development of non-equilibrium statistical mechanics

[
4
,
15
,
20
]
. Their macroscopic behavior, usually referred to as
hydrodynamic scaling limit, is described as follows. Given a
microscopic realization of the process, the empirical density

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}
is defined by counting locally the average number of
particles while the empirical current
{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}
is defined by counting
the net flow of particles. By the local conservation of the number of
particles,
{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}
satisfy the continuity
equation. The content of the hydrodynamical limit is the law of
large numbers for the pair
({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})
in the limit

N\to\infty
. For driven-diffusive systems the limiting evolution is
given by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{j}}=0,\\
{\boldsymbol{j}}=-D({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})E,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
E=E(x)
is the applied external field,
D
is the diffusion
matrix, and
\sigma
is the mobility. In particular, the density
profile
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}={\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x)
solves the non-linear driven
diffusive equation

\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\nabla\cdot\big(\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})E\big)=\nabla\cdot\big(D({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\big).

(1.2)

We refer to
[
15
]
for the details on the derivation of
(
1.2
), while the scaling limit of the empirical current leading
to (
1.1
) is discussed in
[
3
]
in the case of the
symmetric exclusion process.

The large deviations with respect to the hydrodynamic limit in the
time window
[0,T]
are characterized by the rate function

A_{T}({\boldsymbol{\rho}},{\boldsymbol{j}})=\int_{0}^{T}\!dt\!\int\!dx\,\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}+D({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})E\big|^{2}}{4\,\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})},

(1.3)

that is at the base of the Macroscopic Fluctuations Theory and it is
widely used in non-equilibrium statistical mechanics
[
4
]
. We
refer to
[
15
,
16
]
for the derivation of this rate function when
the empirical current is disregarded.

A significant problem is the behavior of the average of empirical
current over the time interval
[0,T]
in the limit when
N\to\infty

and then
T\to\infty
. By the hydrodynamical large deviations
principle and contraction principle, this amounts to analyze the
behavior as
T\to\infty
of the minimizers to (
1.3
) with the
constraint
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!dt\,{\boldsymbol{j}}=J
. This problem has been
initially raised in
[
7
]
while in
[
1
]
it has been
pointed out that the minimizers can exhibit a non-trivial time
dependent behavior. In
[
2
,
8
]
it has been then shown that
this is actually the case for the weakly asymmetric exclusion process
and the Kipnis-Marchioro-Presutti model where, for suitable value of
the parameters, traveling waves are more convenient than constant
profiles.

Denote by
I^{(2)}(J)
the limiting value as
T\to\infty
of the
minimum to
T^{-1}\,A_{T}
with the constraint

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!dt\,{\boldsymbol{j}}=J
. Varadhan
[
21
]
proposed the
following representation for
I^{(2)}

I^{(2)}(J)=\inf\bigg\{\!\!\int\!\!dP\!\!\int\!\!dx\,\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t)+D({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t)-\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))E\big|^{2}}{4\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))};\,\int\!\!dP\,{\boldsymbol{j}}(t)=J\!\bigg\}

(1.4)

where the infimum is carried out over the probabilities
P
invariant by
time translations on the set of paths
({\boldsymbol{\rho}},{\boldsymbol{j}})
satisfying
the continuity equation
\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{j}}=0
.
Note that
I^{(2)}
is
convex and that, by the stationarity of
P
, the actual value of
t

on the right hand side of (
1.4
) is irrelevant.

The purpose of the present analysis is to prove the validity of the
representation (
1.4
) in the context of the weakly asymmetric
exclusion process for which
D=1
and
\sigma(\rho)=\rho(1-\rho)
. This will be achieved both when the limit
T\to\infty

is carried out after the hydrodynamic limit
N\to\infty
and when the
limits are carried out in the opposite order. In fact, the
representation (
1.4
) will be deduced by the contraction
principle from a large deviation result at the level of the empirical
processes that we next introduce.

Consider first the case in which the limit
N\to\infty
is taken
after
T\to\infty
. By the Donsker-Varadhan result, see e.g.
[
11
,
22
]
, as
T\to\infty
the empirical process associated to the
weakly asymmetric exclusion process satisfies a large deviation
principle in which the affine rate function is the relative entropy per unit
of time with respect to the stationary process. By projecting this
functional to the stationary probabilities on the empirical density
and current, and analyzing its variational convergence as
N\to\infty

we deduce the desired large deviation principle with affine rate function
given by

{\boldsymbol{I}}(P)=\int\!P(d{\boldsymbol{\rho}},d{\boldsymbol{j}})\,\int\!dx\,\,\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t)+D({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t)-\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))E\big|^{2}}{4\,\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t))}\;\cdot

(1.5)

The main ingredient in this derivation is, as for hydrodynamical large
deviations, the validity of local equilibrium with probability
super-exponentially close to one as
N\to\infty
. Observe that the
rate function in (
1.4
) is obtained from (
1.5
) by
contraction. The proof for the case in which the limit
T\to\infty

is taken after
N\to\infty
is achieved by lifting the hydrodynamical
rate function (
1.3
) to the set of stationary probabilities on
density and current and analyzing its variational convergence as

T\to\infty
.

As a corollary of the analysis here presented, we also deduce the
“level two” large deviations relative to the family of random
probability measures
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!dt\,\delta_{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}(t)}
in
the joint limit
N,T\to\infty
. Letting

\imath_{t}({\boldsymbol{\rho}},{\boldsymbol{j}})={\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t)
, the corresponding rate
function is

{\mathscr{I}}(\wp)=\inf\Big\{{\boldsymbol{I}}(P)\;;\;P\circ\imath_{t}^{-1}\,=\wp\Big\}

(1.6)

Since
{\mathscr{I}}(\wp)=0
if and only if
\wp
is a stationary measure for
the flow associated to the hydrodynamic equation (
1.2
), this
large deviations statement implies the hydrostatic limit for the
weakly asymmetric exclusion process: in the limit
N\to\infty
the
empirical density constructed by sampling the particles according to
the stationary measure converges to the unique stationary solution to
(
1.2
).

2.
Notation and Results

Microscopic dynamics

Denote by
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}=[0,1)^{d}
the
d
-dimensional torus of length

1
and let
dx
be the corresponding Haar measure.
Fix
N\geq 1
, and let
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
the discretization of

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
:
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}={\mathbb{T}}^{d}\cap(N^{-1}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
. The
elements of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
are represented by
x
and

y
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
be the set of ordered,
nearest-neighbor pairs
(x,y)
in
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\Sigma_{N}:=\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}
the space of
configurations. Elements of
\Sigma_{N}
are represented by
\eta
, so
that
\eta_{x}=1
, resp.
0
, if site
x
is occupied, resp. vacant,
for the configuration
\eta
. Fix
E
in

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}C^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the space of continuously
differentiable vector fields defined on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. In some statements
we assume that
E
is
orthogonally decomposable
: there are

U\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
and
\tilde{E}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
vanishing divergence,
\nabla\cdot\tilde{E}=0
, satisfying the
pointwise orthogonality
\nabla U(x)\cdot\tilde{E}(x)=0
,

x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, such that
E=-\nabla U+\tilde{E}
.

The weakly asymmetric exclusion process (WASEP) with external field

E
is the Markov process on
\Sigma_{N}
whose generator
L_{N}
acts on
functions
f\colon\Sigma_{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as

(L_{N}f)(\eta)\;=\;N^{2}\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}\eta_{x}\,[1-\eta_{y}]\,e^{(1/2)\,E_{N}(x,y)}\big[f(\sigma^{x,y}\eta)-f(\eta)\big]\;.

(2.1)

In this formula, the configuration
\sigma^{x,y}\eta
is obtained from

\eta
by exchanging the occupation variables
\eta_{x}
,
\eta_{y}
:

(\sigma^{x,y}\eta)_{z}\;:=\;\begin{cases}\eta_{y}&\textrm{ if \ }z=x\;,\\
\eta_{x}&\textrm{ if \ }z=y\;,\\
\eta_{z}&\textrm{ if \ }z\neq x,y\;,\end{cases}

and
E_{N}(x,y)
represents the line integral of
E
along the oriented
segment from
x
to
y
:

E_{N}(x,y)\;=\;\int_{x}^{y}E\cdot\,d\ell\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}E(x+r\,[y-x])\cdot[y-x]\,dr\;,

(2.2)

where
a\cdot b
is the inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Note that

E_{N}(\cdot,\cdot)
is antisymmetric and that
E_{N}
is of order
1/N
.

Denote by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\Sigma_{N,K}=\{\eta\in\Sigma_{N}:\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}\eta_{x}=K\}
,
K=0,\ldots,N^{d}
, the set of configurations
with
K
particles. The Markov process with generator
L_{N}
is
irreducible in the finite state space
\Sigma_{N,K}
. It has therefore
a unique stationary probability measure, denoted by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\mu_{N,K}
.

Hereafter,
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{R}}
represents either
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
or

{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}D({\mathcal{R}},\Sigma_{N})
,
the set of right-continuous functions with left-limits from
{\mathcal{R}}

to
\Sigma_{N}
, endowed with the Skorohod
topology and the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Elements of

D({\mathcal{R}},\Sigma_{N})
are represented by
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
.

For a probability measure
\nu
on
\Sigma_{N}
, denote by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\nu}
the probability measure on

D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\Sigma_{N})
induced by the Markovian dynamics associated
to the generator
L_{N}
starting from
\nu
. When the measure
\nu
is
concentrated on a configuration
\eta\in\Sigma_{N}
,

\nu=\delta_{\eta}
, we write
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}
instead
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\delta_{\eta}}
. For
K=0,\ldots,N^{d}
, the stationary
processes associated to the WASEP dynamics with
K
particles is
denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu_{N,K}}^{N}
that we regard as a
probability measure on
D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})
invariant with respect
to time-translations. Expectation with respect to

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu_{N,K}}^{N}
is represented by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu_{N,K}}^{N}
.

Empirical density

Let
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
be the set of positive measures
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
with total mass bounded by
1
, endowed with the weak
topology and the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra. Let also

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{M}}_{m}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
,
m\in[0,1]
, be the closed subset
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
given by the measures whose total mass is
equal to
m
.

The
empirical density
is the map

\pi_{N}\colon\Sigma_{N}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
defined by

\pi_{N}(\eta)\;:=\;\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}\eta_{x}\,\delta_{x}\;,

(2.3)

where
\delta_{x}
,
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, is the point mass at
x
. For a
continuous function
f\colon{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and a measure
\nu
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, we represent by
\langle\nu\,,\,f\rangle
the integral of
f

with respect to
\nu
so that

\langle\pi_{N}\,,\,f\rangle\;=\;\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}\eta_{x}\,f(x)\;.

(2.4)

We also call empirical density the map

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}\colon D({\mathcal{R}},\Sigma_{N})\to D({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))
defined by

[\,{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,]\,(t)\;:=\;\pi_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t))\;=\;\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{x}(t)\,\delta_{x}\;,\quad t\in{\mathcal{R}}\;.

(2.5)

Empirical current.

For an oriented bond

(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
and
s<t
, let
{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(s,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
be
the number of jumps from
x
to
y
in the time interval
(s,t]
of
the path
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in D({\mathcal{R}},\Sigma_{N})
:

{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(s,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\;=\;\sum_{s<r\leq t}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{x}(r-)\,[1-{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{y}(r-)]\,{\boldsymbol{1}}\{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(r)=\sigma^{x,y}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(r-)\}\;.

(2.6)

Fix a trajectory
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in D({\mathcal{R}},\Sigma_{N})
, and denote by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}C({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the space of continuous vector
fields on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. If
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is a trajectory compatible with the
WASEP dynamics, i.e. such that for each jump time
t
we have

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t)=\sigma^{x,y}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t-)
for some
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
,
we define the
integrated empirical current

{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
as follows. Let

[\,{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,]\,(0)=0
, and, for
t\,>\,0
, let

[\,{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,]\,(t)
be the linear functional on

C({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
defined by

\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,(t)\,,\,F\rangle\;:=\;\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(0,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\int_{x}^{y}F\cdot d\ell\;,\quad F\,\in\,C({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\;.

(2.7)

For
t<0
, we replace in the previous formula
{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(0,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
by
-\,{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(t,0]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
.
If
F=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})
, then for
t\geq 0

\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,(t)\,,\,F\rangle=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}\frac{1}{N}\,\Big\{{\mathscr{N}}^{x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}}_{(0,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})-{\mathscr{N}}^{x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{j},x}_{(0,t]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\Big\}\int_{0}^{1}F_{j}(x+r{\mathfrak{e}}_{j})\,dr\;,

where,
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathfrak{e}}_{j}=e_{j}/N
and
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d}\}

represents the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Discrete vector fields.

For technical issues, we need to define the empirical current also for
paths
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
not coming from the WASEP dynamics. Consider an
arbitrary path
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in D\big({\mathcal{R}};\Sigma_{N,K}\big)
. If for
some
t\in{\mathcal{R}}
it happens
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t)\neq{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t-)
some of the
particles in the configuration
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t-)
have rearranged
themselves to construct the configuration
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t)
. The
definition of the empirical current requires to decide the actual path
taken by those particles.

A
discrete vector field

W
is an antisymmetric function

W\colon{\mathbb{B}}_{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The
discrete divergence
of the
discrete vector field
W
is the function

\nabla_{N}\cdot W\colon{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(\nabla_{N}\cdot W)\,(x):=\sum_{y\colon(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}W(x,y)\;.

Fix
0\leq K\leq N^{d}
, and consider two configurations
\eta
,

\xi\in\Sigma_{N,K}
. Let
W_{\eta,\xi}
be a discrete vector field
which solves

(\nabla_{N}\cdot W_{\eta,\xi})\,(x)\;=\;\eta_{x}\,-\,\xi_{x}\;,\quad x\,\in\,{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}\;.

(2.8)

Such a discrete vector field
W_{\eta,\xi}
always exists. The
configuration
\zeta=\eta-\xi
belongs to
\{-1,0,1\}^{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}

and
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}\zeta_{x}=0
. To define
W_{\eta,\xi}
, one
just needs to create nearest-neighbor flows from each
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}

such that
\zeta_{x}=1
to each
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
such that

\zeta_{x^{\prime}}=-1
, and add all these flows.

Regarding
\eta
and
\xi
as positive measures on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
, both
of mass
K
, by the analysis of the discrete Beckmann’s problem, see
e.g.
[
19
]
, we deduce that there exists a constant
C_{0}
such
that for all
N\geq 1
,
0\leq K\leq N^{d}
,
\eta
,
\xi\in\Sigma_{N,K}

there exists a discrete vector field
W_{\eta,\xi}
such that

\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}\big|\,W_{\eta,\xi}(x,y)\,\big|\;\leq\;C_{0}\,N^{d+1}\;.

(2.9)

Of course, equation (
2.8
) admits more than one solution and we
do not claim that there is only one satisfying the previous bound. It
turns out, however, that the scaling limit of the empirical current
does not depend on the specific selection among those fulfilling
(
2.9
). Hence, in the sequel and without further mention, we
assume that, for each pair
(\eta,\xi)\in\Sigma^{2}_{N,K}
, a
discrete vector field which match (
2.9
)
W_{\eta,\xi}
has
been chosen.

When
\xi=\sigma^{x,y}\eta
for some
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
and

\eta_{x}=1
,
\eta_{y}=0
, we define
W_{\eta,\xi}
as

W_{\eta,\xi}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\;=\;\begin{cases}1&\text{if }(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x,y)\;,\\
-1&\text{if }(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(y,x)\;,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\;.\end{cases}

(2.10)

This discrete vector field clearly satisfies (
2.8
) and (
2.9
).

Integrated currents for generic paths.

Fix a generic path
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in D({\mathcal{R}};\Sigma_{N,K})
and denote by

\tau_{i}
its jump times. Let
W_{i}
be the discrete vector field given by

W_{i}\;=\;W_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\tau_{i}-),{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(\tau_{i})}\;.

(2.11)

For
t>0
, the integrated empirical current of the path
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}

is then defined by

\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})(t),F\rangle=\frac{1}{2N^{d}}\,\sum_{i\colon\tau_{i}\in(0,t]}\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}W_{i}(x,y)\,F_{N}(x,y)

(2.12)

where
F_{N}
is the discrete vector field constructed from

F\in C({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by (
2.2
). In view of (
2.10
), for
trajectories
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
coming from the WASEP dynamics, this
definition corresponds to the original one, given in (
2.7
).

As before,
{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})(0)=0
and for
t<0

\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})(t),F\rangle=-\frac{1}{2N^{d}}\sum_{i\colon\tau_{i}\in[t,0)}\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}W_{i}(x,y)F_{N}(x,y).

In view of the bound (
2.9
)
and noticing that
|F_{N}|\leq\|F\|_{\infty}/N
, if
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
has a single jump
then
|\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})(t),F\rangle|\leq C\|F\|_{\infty}
for
some constant
C
independent of
N
.

Sobolev spaces.

Let
h_{n}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
,

n=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, be the orthonormal basis of

L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
given by

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}h_{n}(x)=\exp\{2\pi i(n\cdot x)\}
.

Denote by

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
,
and by
{\mathfrak{f}}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
the Fourier coefficients of
the function
f
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
:

{\mathfrak{f}}(n):=\langle f,h_{n}\rangle\;=\;\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}f(x)\,\overline{h_{n}(x)}\,dx\;,\quad n\,\in\,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\;,

where
\overline{z}
represents the complex conjugate of

z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence,

f\;=\;\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\mathfrak{f}}(n)\,h_{n}\;

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
,
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the Hilbert space
obtained by completing the space of smooth complex-valued functions on

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
endowed with the scalar product

\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{p}
defined by

\langle f\,,\,g\rangle_{p}\;=\;\langle(1-\Delta)^{p}f\,,\,g\rangle\;,

(2.13)

where
\Delta
represents the Laplacian. An elementary computation
yields that

\langle f\,,\,g\rangle_{p}\;:=\;\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big(1+4\pi^{2}|n|^{2}\big)^{p}\,{\mathfrak{f}}(n)\,\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}(n)}\;,

(2.14)

where
|n|^{2}=|(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}n^{2}_{j}
. Denote by

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{p}
the norm of
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
:

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\|f\|^{2}_{p}=\langle f\,,\,f\rangle_{p}
. It is well
known that
{\mathcal{H}}_{-p}
, is the dual of
{\mathcal{H}}_{p}
relatively to the
pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\rangle
defined by

\langle j\,,\,g\rangle\;:=\;\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\mathfrak{j}}(n)\,\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}(n)}\;,\quad j\,\in\,{\mathcal{H}}_{-p}\;,\,g\,\in\,{\mathcal{H}}_{p}\;.

(2.15)

It follows from the conservation of mass that for each

3.
Donsker-Varadhan large deviations principle

In this section, we recall the Donsker-Varadhan large deviations
principle in the context of the WASEP dynamics with fixed number of
particles.

Recall from (
2.19
) the definition of the empirical process

R_{T}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
. Referring to
[
22
]
for equivalent
characterizations, we introduce the rate functional for the family

(R_{T}:T>0)
by a variational representation that will be most useful
for our purposes. For
t>0
, let

H_{N,K}(t,\,\cdot\,):{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}\to[0,+\infty]
be
the functional given by

H_{N,K}(t,{\mathbb{Q}})\;=\;\sup_{\Phi}\int\!d{\mathbb{Q}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\Big[\Phi({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,-\,\log{\mathbb{E}}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(0)}^{N}\big(e^{\Phi}\big)\Big]\;,

where the supremum is carried over the bounded and continuous
functions
\Phi
on
D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})
that are measurable with
respect to
\sigma\{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),\>s\in[0,t]\big\}
. Let
H_{N,K}\colon{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}\to[0,+\infty]
be the functional
defined by

H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})\;:=\;\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\,H_{N,K}(t,{\mathbb{Q}})\;=\;\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\,H_{N,K}(t,{\mathbb{Q}})\;,

(3.1)

where the second identity follows from the inequality before

[
22
, Theorem 10.9]
. By
[
22
, Theorems 10.6 and 10.8]
,
the functional
H_{N,K}
is good and affine.

The classical Donsker-Varadhan theorem, see
[
11
]
or Theorems
11.6 and 12.5 in
[
22
]
, states that, uniformly on the initial
configuration
\eta\in\Sigma_{N,K}
, the family of probability
measures
({\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}\circ R_{T}^{-1}:T>0)
satisfies a large
deviations principle with rate function
H_{N,K}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix
N
and
K
. For each closed set
{\mathscr{F}}
and each open set
{\mathscr{G}}
in

{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\sup_{\eta\in\Sigma_{N,K}}\frac{1}{T}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}\,\big[R_{T}\in{\mathscr{F}}\,\big]\;\leq\;-\,\inf_{{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\mathscr{F}}}H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})\;,

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}\inf_{\eta\in\Sigma_{N,K}}\frac{1}{T}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}\,\big[R_{T}\in{\mathscr{G}}\,\big]\;\geq\;-\,\inf_{{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\mathscr{G}}}H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})\;.

The rate function
H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})
can also be understood as the
relative entropy
per unit of time
of the stationary probability

{\mathbb{Q}}
with respect to the stationary process
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}
.
Given
T_{0}<T_{1}
, denote by

i_{T_{0},T_{1}}\colon D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})\to D([T_{0},T_{1}],\Sigma_{N,K})
the canonical projection. Given two probability
measures
{\mathbb{Q}}^{1},{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
on
D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})
, let

{\mathbb{H}}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}
be the relative entropy between the marginal of

{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
relative to the time interval
[T_{0},T_{1}]
and the marginal
of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
on the same interval:

{\mathbb{H}}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}({\mathbb{Q}}^{1}|{\mathbb{Q}}^{2})\;=\;\textrm{Ent}\big({\mathbb{Q}}^{1}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}\big|{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}\big):=\int\log\frac{d\,{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}}{d\,{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}}\;d\,{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}\;,

(3.2)

where
{\mathbb{Q}}^{j}_{[T_{0},T_{1}]}={\mathbb{Q}}^{j}\circ i_{T_{0},T_{1}}^{-1}
,
j=1
,
2
.
We also shorthand
{\mathbb{H}}_{[0,T]}
by
{\mathbb{H}}^{(T)}
.
By
[
13
, Theorem 5.4.27]
, for each
{\mathbb{Q}}
in

{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}

H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})\;=\;\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\,{\mathbb{H}}^{(T)}\big(\,{\mathbb{Q}}\,\big|\,{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}\,\big)\;=\;\sup_{T>0}\frac{1}{T}\,{\mathbb{H}}^{(T)}\big(\,{\mathbb{Q}}\,\big|\,{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}\,\big)\;,

(3.3)

where the second identity follows by a super-additivity argument which
stems from
[
22
, Lemma 10.3]
. Actually, Theorem 5.4.27 in

[
13
]
states that the empirical process
(R_{T}:T>0)
satisfies
a large deviations principle with good rate function given by

H^{\star}_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}}):=\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-1}\,{\mathbb{H}}^{(T)}(\,{\mathbb{Q}}\,\big|\,{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}\,)
. Since, by Theorem

3.1
,
(R_{T}:T>0)
also satisfies a large deviations principle
with good rate function given by
H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})
, a simple argument
using the lower semi-continuity of the functionals yields that

H_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})=H^{\star}_{N,K}({\mathbb{Q}})
for all

{\mathbb{Q}}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}
.

Recall that we denote by
{\mathscr{S}}
the set of trajectories

({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})
which satisfy the continuity equation
(
2.21
). By (
2.17
), the map

({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})\colon D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})\to D({\mathbb{R}},{\mathscr{M}})
takes values in
{\mathscr{S}}
. As already observed,

({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})(\vartheta_{t}{\boldsymbol{\eta}})=[\vartheta_{t}({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})]({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
so that
({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})
induces a
map from the stationary probabilities on
D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})
to
the stationary probabilities on
{\mathscr{S}}
. More precisely, if

{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}^{N,K}
, then

{\mathbb{P}}\circ({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})^{-1}
belongs to

{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}
. Let

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}:{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}\to[0,\infty]
be defined by

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}(P)\;=\;\inf\Big\{H_{N,K}({\mathbb{P}})\colon\;{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat},\;{\mathbb{P}}\circ({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})^{-1}=P\Big\}\;.

(3.4)

Note that the set on the right-hand side is either empty (for example,
if the
P
-measure of the set of piece-wise constant paths is not
equal to
1
) or it is a singleton because the map
({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})\colon D({\mathbb{R}},\Sigma_{N,K})\to D({\mathbb{R}},{\mathscr{M}})
is injective.

Corollary 3.2
.

Fix
N
and
K=0,\ldots,N^{d}
. The functional

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}\to[0,+\infty]
is affine
and good. Moreover, for each closed
{\mathscr{F}}
and each open
{\mathscr{G}}
in

{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\sup_{\eta\in\Sigma_{N,K}}\frac{1}{T}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}\,\big[\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\in{\mathscr{F}}\,\big]\;\leq\;-\,\inf_{P\in{\mathscr{F}}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}(P)\;,

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}\inf_{\eta\in\Sigma_{N,K}}\frac{1}{T}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}\,\big[\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\in{\mathscr{G}}\,\big]\;\geq\;-\,\inf_{P\in{\mathscr{G}}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}(P)\;.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the map

{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}\ni{\mathbb{Q}}\mapsto{\mathbb{Q}}\circ({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N})^{-1}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}
is continuous. The statement then
follows from Theorem
3.1
by the contraction principle.

Since the map

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}\colon D({\mathbb{R}};\Sigma_{N,K})\to D({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))

introduced in (
2.5
) is continuous, we directly deduce the
continuity of the map

{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}\ni{\mathbb{P}}\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}\circ{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N}^{-1}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}\big(D({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}))\big)
. In
contrast, the map
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\mapsto{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
is not
continuous. Indeed, consider the sequence
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}
in which

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}
has a unique jump at time
1/k
from
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(0)
to

\sigma^{x,y}{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(0)
for some
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
. Then,

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)}
converges to the path
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
with a single jump at
time

but
{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}^{(k)})
does not converge to

{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
. In contrast, the map

{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\mapsto{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})
is continuous if
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
does
not have a jump at time

. Moreover, if
{\mathbb{Q}}
is a stationary
probability on

, then the

{\mathbb{Q}}
-probability of the paths
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
which have a jump a time

is necessarily zero. This implies that the map

{\mathscr{P}}^{N,K}_{\rm stat}\ni{\mathbb{Q}}\mapsto{\mathbb{Q}}\circ{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}^{-1}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}\big(D({\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{-p})\big)
is continuous.
∎

4.
Variational convergence of the
Donsker-Varadhan functional.

Referring to
[
9
]
for an overview, we recall the definition of

\Gamma
-convergence. Fix a Polish space
{\mathcal{X}}
and a sequence

(U_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
of functionals on
{\mathcal{X}}
,

U_{n}\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
. The sequence
U_{n}
is

equi-coercive
if for each
\ell\geq 0
there exists a compact
subset
{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
such that

\{x\in{\mathcal{X}}\colon U_{n}(x)\leq\ell\}\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
for any

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The sequence
U_{n}

\Gamma
-converges
to the
functional
U\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
, i.e.
U_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}U
, if and only if the two
following conditions are met:

(i)

\Gamma
-liminf.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-liminf for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
and each
sequence
x_{n}\to x
, we have that
\liminf_{n}U_{n}(x_{n})\geq U(x)
.

(ii)

\Gamma
-limsup.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-limsup for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
there
exists a sequence
x_{n}\to x
such that
\limsup_{n}U_{n}(x_{n})\leq U(x)
.

Recall the definition of the functionals
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
,
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}

introduced in (
2.26
) and (
3.4
), respectively. The main
result of this section reads as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Fix
0<m<1
,
p>(d+2)/2
, and a sequence
K_{N}
so that
K_{N}/N^{d}\to m
.
The sequence
(N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}:N\geq 1)
is equi-coercive. The
functional
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
is a
\Gamma
-liminf for
N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}
.
If
E
is orthogonally decomposable, then the functional
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
is
also a
\Gamma
-limsup for
N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}
. Therefore, under
this hypothesis on
E
,

N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
.

The proof of this theorem is divided in three parts. In Subsection
4.1, we prove that the sequence
N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}
is
equi-coercive. In Subsection 4.2, that
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
is a
\Gamma
-liminf,
and, in Subsection 4.3, that
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
is a
\Gamma
-limsup provided

E
is decomposable. For the rest of this section, fix
m\in(0,1)
,

p>(d+2)/2
, and a sequence
K_{N}
such that
K_{N}/N^{d}\to m
.

4
.1 Equi-coercivity.
Set

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}P_{N}:={\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\circ\big({\boldsymbol{\pi}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}\big)^{-1}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{{\rm stat}}
. We first establish the
exponential tightness of the sequence

(P_{N}:N\geq 1)\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{{\rm stat}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a sequence
({\mathscr{K}}_{\ell}:\ell\geq 1)
of compact subsets
of
{\mathscr{S}}
such that

\lim_{\ell\to+\infty}\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\log P_{N}\big(\,{\mathscr{K}}_{\ell}^{\complement}\,\big)\;=\;-\,\infty\;.

Proof.

In view of Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, the compactness of

{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, and the compact embedding

{\mathcal{H}}_{-p}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{-p^{\prime}}
for
p^{\prime}>p
, the assertion
of this proposition follows from the next three lemmata.
∎

Let

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

We use the notation and statements introduced in
(
2.13
)-(
2.16
), and denote
{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)({H}^{j,n})
by

\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}\rangle
. By (
2.16
),

\big\|\,{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\big\|^{2}_{-p}\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{p}_{n}}\;\big|\,\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;,\qquad\gamma_{n}\,:=\,1+4\pi^{2}|n|^{2}\;.

Let
\beta_{n}=\gamma^{p}_{n}/[c_{p}\,(1+|n|)^{2(p-1)}]
. Here,
c_{p}
is
a constant such that
d\,\sum_{n}(\beta_{n}/\gamma^{p}_{n})=1
, that
is,
c_{p}=d\sum_{n}(1+|n|)^{-2(p-1)}
. Note that this sum is finite
because we assumed
p>1+(d/2)
. Introducing the supremum inside the
sum yields that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{p}_{n}}\,\big|\,\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;>\;A\,\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\;{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\Big\{\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\,\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}\rangle\,\big|^{2}\;>\;A\,\Big\}\,\Big]\;.

Fix
1\leq j\leq d
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and denote by
{H}^{j,n}_{1}
,

{H}^{j,n}_{-1}
the real and the imaginary part of
{H}^{j,n}
,
respectively. The previous expression is then bounded by

\sum_{b=\pm 1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\,\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}_{b}\rangle\,\big|\;>\;\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}\,\Big]\;.

We may remove the absolute value from the previous expression at the
cost of an extra factor
2
in front of the sum and an estimation of

{H}^{j,n}_{b}
and
-\,{H}^{j,n}_{b}
. We next bound the probability of
the event

\{\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}_{b}\rangle\,\,>\,\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}\,\}
, the other one being similar.

Recall the notation introduced in (
2.2
) and let

B_{x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}(\eta)=B_{x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}(\eta,{H}^{j,n}_{b})
,

1\leq k\leq d
,
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}
, be given by

\displaystyle B_{x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}(\eta)\;

\displaystyle=\;N^{2}\,\eta_{x}\,[\,1-\eta_{x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}\,]\,e^{(1/2)\,E_{N}(x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k})}\,\Big[e^{{H}^{j,n}_{b,N}(x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k})}\,-\,1\Big]

\displaystyle+\;N^{2}\,\eta_{x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}\,[\,1-\eta_{x}\,]\,e^{(1/2)\,E_{N}(x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k},x)}\,\Big[e^{{H}^{j,n}_{b,N}(x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k},x)}\,-\,1\Big]\;,

By
[
15
, Proposition A1.2.6]
, for each
\eta\in\Sigma_{N}
the
process

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}}_{N}(t)\;:=\;\exp\Big\{N^{d}\,\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,{H}^{j,n}_{b}\rangle\;-\;\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}}B_{x,x+e_{k}}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\;ds\,\Big\}

is a mean-one
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta}
-martingale.

Since
N\big|{H}^{j,n}_{b,N}(x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k})\big|
is bounded
uniformly in
b,j,k,x,n,N
, a Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle\bigg|\,\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}}B_{x,x+e_{k}}(\eta)\;-\;N^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}{H}^{j,n}_{b,N}(x,x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k})\,(\eta_{x}\,-\,\eta_{x+{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}}\,)\bigg|\;\leq\;C_{1}\,N^{d}\;,

for some constant
C_{1}
independent of
b,j,n,N
. Summing by parts
and using that

\big|\partial_{x_{k}}{H}^{j,n}_{b}(x)\big|\leq C_{2}|n|
for some
constant
C_{2}
independent of
b,j,k,x,n
, we conclude that

\Big|\,\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}_{N}}B_{x,x+e_{k}}(\eta,{H}^{j,n}_{b})\,\Big|\;\leq\;C_{0}N^{d}(1+|n|)

for some constant
C_{0}
independent of
b,j,n,N
.

In view of the previous estimate, adding and subtracting the sum of
the time-integrals of
B_{x,x+e_{k}}
and taking exponentials, we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\,,\,H^{j,n}_{b}\rangle\;>\;\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}\;\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\;\leq\;{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{M}}_{N}(t)\;>\;e^{\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}N^{d}-C_{0}TN^{d}(1+|n|)}\,\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\;\leq\;e^{-\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}N^{d}+C_{0}TN^{d}(1+|n|)}\;,

where we used Doob’s inequality in the last step and the fact that

{\mathbb{M}}_{N}(t)
is a mean-one martingale.

We have thus shown that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\Big[\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big\|{\boldsymbol{J}}_{N}(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{-p}}\;>\;A\,\Big]\;\leq\;4\,d\,\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}e^{-N^{d}\big[\sqrt{A\,\beta_{n}/2}-C_{0}T(1+|n|)\big]}\;.

By definition of
\beta_{n}
, there exists a positive constant

c_{0}
such that
\beta_{n}\geq c_{0}(1+|n|)^{2}
. The statement follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

By a standard inclusion of events and the stationarity of

where we used that

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(P_{N}\colon N\geq 1)
be a sequence of probability measures in

{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}
such that

\liminf_{N}N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}(P_{N})<+\infty

for some

\begin{split}&E_{P}\Big[\,\int_{0}^{T}dt\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}dx\,\frac{|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t,x)|^{2}}{\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x))}\;+\;\int_{0}^{T}dt\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}dx\,\frac{|\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x)|^{2}}{\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x))}\,\Big]\\

{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)\;=\;\int_{0}^{t}{\boldsymbol{j}}(s)\,ds

Proof.

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume
\liminf_{N}N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}(P_{N})=\lim_{N}N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}(P_{N})
. By
(
3.4
) and (

. In particular, for every

\limsup_{N}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\,{\mathbb{H}}^{(T)}\big(\,{\mathbb{Q}}_{N}\,\big|\,{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}\,\big)\;\leq\;T\,\lim_{N}\frac{1}{N^{d}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}}(P_{N})\;.

(4.3)

By this bound, the marginal of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{N}
in the time interval

{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K_{N}}}
in the same interval.
Moreover, for each
continuous function

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}

\Big|\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}dt\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t,dx)\,g(t,x)\,\Big|\;\leq\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}dt\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\big|\,g(t,x)\,\big|\,dx\;.

This implies that
P
-almost surely, for Lebesgue almost all

is absolutely continuous with respect to the

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t,dx)={\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x)\,dx

.

On the other hand, as

.

\rho:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
of
total mass
m
.
For each sequence
(\eta^{N}:N\geq 1)
associated to

\rho
, each closed set
{\mathscr{F}}\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
, and each
open set
{\mathscr{G}}\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta^{N}}\,\big[{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\in{\mathscr{F}}\,\big]\;\leq\;-\,\inf_{P\in{\mathscr{F}}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)\;,

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\,\log{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\eta^{N}}\,\big[{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\in{\mathscr{G}}\,\big]\;\geq\;-\,\inf_{P\in{\mathscr{G}}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)\;.

Moreover,
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}\to[0,+\infty]
is a good rate function.

5.2.
Variational convergence of the hydrodynamical rate
function.

The main result of this section reads as follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

Fix
m\in(0,1)
and a density profile
\rho:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
of
total mass
m
. As
T\to+\infty
, the sequence

T^{-1}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}
is equi-coercive uniformly in
\rho
, and

\Gamma
-converges uniformly in
\rho
to the functional
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}

introduced in (
2.26
). That is:

(i)

For each
\ell>0
there exists a compact

{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
such that for any
T>1
and
any
\rho
,

\big\{P:T^{-1}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)\leq\ell\big\}\subset{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}
;

(ii)

For any
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
, any sequence of
density profiles
\rho_{T}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
of total mass
m
and any
sequence
P_{T}\to P
,

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho_{T}}(P_{T})\geq{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)\;;

(iii)

If the external field
E
is orthogonally decomposable,
then for any
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
and any sequence of
density profiles
\rho_{T}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
of total mass
m
, there
exists a sequence
P_{T}\to P
such that

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho_{T}}(P_{T})\leq{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)\;.

Proof.

The proof is divided in three parts.

Equicoercivity.

In view of the compactness of
{\mathcal{M}}_{m}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, the compact
embedding of
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
into
{\mathcal{H}}_{-p}^{d}
for
p>0
,
Ascoli-Arzelà theorem, and Chebyshev inequality, it is enough to
prove the following bounds. For each
T_{0}>0
and each smooth vector
field
H\colon{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there exists constants

C_{0}=C_{0}(T_{0})
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(T_{0},H)
such that for any
T\geq 1
and

\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle E_{P}\Big[\sup_{t\in[-T_{0},T_{0}]}\big\|{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\Big]\leq C_{0}\,\Big[\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)+1\Big]

(5.4)

\displaystyle E_{P}\bigg[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in[-T_{0},T_{0}]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\big|\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}(t),H\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}(s),H\rangle\big|^{2}\bigg]\leq C_{1}\delta\,\Big[\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)+1\Big].

(5.5)

As already remarked right after the statement of Lemma
4.5
, by
choosing
H=\nabla g
, the bound (
5.5
) provides indeed also a
control on the continuity modulus of the map
t\mapsto{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t)
.

By the stationarity of
P
and the argument below (
2.26
), if

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)<+\infty
, then there exists a constant
C

depending only on
E
such that

\frac{1}{2T}\,E_{P}\bigg[\int_{-T}^{T}dt\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}dx\,\frac{|{\boldsymbol{j}}\,|^{2}}{4\,\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\bigg]\leq C\Big[\frac{1}{T}\,{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)+1\Big]\;,

where
{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}{\boldsymbol{j}}(s)\,ds
.
By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and condition (b’) on the current
{\boldsymbol{J}}

stated at the beginning of this section,

\sup_{t\in[-T_{0},T_{0}]}\big\|{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)\big\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\leq T_{0}\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}\!dt\int\!dx\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t,x)\big|^{2}\leq T_{0}\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}\!dt\int\!dx\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t,x)\big|^{2}}{4\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x))}\;\cdot

Since
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)<+\infty
implies
that
P
is a
T
-holonomic probability measure,

E_{P}\bigg[\int_{-T_{0}}^{T_{0}}\!dt\int\!dx\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t,x)\big|^{2}}{4\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x))}\bigg]=\frac{T_{0}}{T}E_{P}\bigg[\int_{-T}^{T}\!dt\int\!dx\frac{\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(t,x)\big|^{2}}{4\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t,x))}\bigg]

which completes the proof of (
5.4
).

For
s<t
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and condition (b’) yield

\big|\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}(t),H\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{J}}(s),H\rangle\big|^{2}\leq(t-s)\|H\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\!du\int\!dx\,\big|{\boldsymbol{j}}(u,x)\big|^{2}\;,

so that (
5.5
) is obtained by the same argument as before.

\Gamma
-liminf.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}{\mathscr{S}}^{T}_{m,\mathrm{ac}}
the subset of the
paths
({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\in{\mathscr{S}}^{T}
satisfying only conditions (a’),
(b’) and let
A_{m,T}
be the action defined in (
5.1
) with the
constraint
({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\in{\mathscr{S}}^{T}_{m,\mathrm{ac},\rho}
replaced
by
({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\in{\mathscr{S}}^{T}_{m,\mathrm{ac}}
. Accordingly, let

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T}\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}\to[0,+\infty]
be the
functional defined by

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T}(P):=\inf\big\{A_{m,T}({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\,,\;({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\in\chi_{T}^{-1}\big(P\big)\big\}\;.

By the translation invariance of
A_{m,T}
, if
\chi_{T}^{-1}(P)
is not
empty (i.e., if
P
is
T
-holonomic) then

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T}(P)=E_{P}\big[A_{m,T}\big]\;.

Hence, in view of the translation invariance of
P
,

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho}(P)\geq{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T}(P)\;=\;E_{P}\big[A_{m,T}\big]\;=\;TE_{P}\big[A_{m,1}\big]\;.

Let
(\rho_{T}\colon T>0)
be an arbitrary sequence and

\big(P_{T}\colon T>0\big)\subset{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}
be a sequence
converging to
P
. By (
2.26
), the previous displayed bound and the
lower semi-continuity of
A_{m,1}
,

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho_{T}}(P_{T})\geq\liminf_{T\to\infty}E_{P_{T}}\big[A_{m,1}\big]\geq E_{P}\big[A_{m,1}\big]={\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)\;.

\Gamma
-limsup.

By Theorem
4.10
, it suffices to consider the case in which
P

is an
S
-holonomic measure with smooth density. More precisely, we
may assume that

P=\frac{1}{S}\int_{0}^{S}\delta_{\vartheta_{s}({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})}\,ds

(5.6)

for some
S>0
, where the
S
-periodic path
({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})

has smooth densities
({{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{j}}}^{*})
with
{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}^{*}

bounded away from
0
and
1
. Given the sequence

(\rho_{T},\,T>0)\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{m}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
, let

\big(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}(t),\bar{{\boldsymbol{J}}}(t)\big)
,
t\in[0,\bar{T}]
be the
path provided by Lemma
4.12
with
\pi_{0}=\rho_{T}\,dx
and

\pi_{1}={{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*}(0)
. Let also
\big({\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t),{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)\big)
,

t\in[0,+\infty)
be the path defined by

\big({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}(t),{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)\big):=\begin{cases}\big(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}(t)\,,\,\bar{{\boldsymbol{J}}}(t)\big)&\textrm{ if }t\in[0,\bar{T}],\\
\big({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*}(t-\bar{T})\,,\,\bar{{\boldsymbol{J}}}(\bar{T})+{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*}(t-\bar{T})\big)&\textrm{ if }t>\bar{T}.\end{cases}

Note that, although not explicit in the notation, the path

({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}},{{\boldsymbol{J}}})
depends on
T
via the sequence
\rho_{T}
. Denote
finally by
\big({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{T},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{T}\big)
the
T
-periodization, as
defined in (
2.23
), of
\big({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}\big)
and by
P_{T}

the associated
T
-holonomic probability, i.e.,

P_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\delta_{\vartheta_{s}({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{T},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{T})}\,ds\;.

Since
\bar{T}/T\to 0
, as

T\to\infty
the sequence
P_{T}
converges to
P
given by (
5.6
).
Moreover, in view of (
5.3
) and (
4.21
),

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho_{T}}(P_{T})=A_{m,\bar{T},\rho_{T}}\big(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\pi}}},\bar{{\boldsymbol{J}}}\big)+A_{m,T-\bar{T}}({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})\leq C_{0}+A_{m,T-\bar{T}}({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})\;.

Hence, by the
S
-periodicity of
({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})
,

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho_{T}}(P_{T})=\frac{1}{S}A_{m,S}({{\boldsymbol{\pi}}}^{*},{{\boldsymbol{J}}}^{*})=\frac{1}{S}E_{P}\big[A_{m,S}\big]={\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)\;,

which concludes the proof of Theorem
5.3
.
∎

6.
Large deviations and projections

In this section, relying on the variational convergence proven before,
we discuss the large deviations asymptotics in the joint limit

N\to\infty
,
T\to\infty
. In particular, we conclude the proof of
Theorem
2.1
and discuss the corresponding projections.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We start by considering the case in which we first perform the limit
as
T\to\infty
and then take limit as
N\to\infty
. The asymptotic
as
T\to\infty
follows directly from the Donsker-Varadhan large
deviation principle for the empirical process, see
Corollary
3.2
. By
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
or
[
18
, Corollary
4.3]
the limit as
N\to\infty
is accomplished by the

\Gamma
-convergence of the family

\big(N^{-d}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K_{N}},\,N\geq 1\big)
that has been proven in
Theorem
4.1
. Actually, the statements in
[
3
,
18
]

give the upper bound only for compact sets. However, the goodness of
the functional
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{N,K}
together with the equi-coercivity in
Theorem
4.1
allow to deduce the upper bound for closed sets.

The proof of the statement when the limit as
T\to\infty
is carried
out after the limit as
N\to\infty
is accomplished by the similar
argument. Indeed, the asymptotic as
N\to\infty
follows directly
from the hydrodynamical large deviations, see Corollary
5.2
,
while the
\Gamma
-convergence of the family

\big(T^{-1}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m,T,\rho},\,T\geq 1\big)
has been proven,
uniformly with respect to
\rho
, in Theorem
5.3
.
∎

We now discuss the level two projection and the hydrostatic limit.

Proof of Corollary
2.2
.

Recall (
2.28
) and that

\wp_{T,N}={\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\circ\imath_{t}^{-1}
where

\imath_{t}\colon{\mathscr{S}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
is the map

({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})\mapsto{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t)
. Note that
i_{t}
is not
continuous since we are using the Skorohod topology. However, the map

{\mathscr{P}}_{\rm stat}\ni P\mapsto P\circ\imath_{t}^{-1}\in{\mathscr{P}}\big({\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\big)
is continuous since, by stationary, the

P
-probability of a jump at a time

is zero. The large deviations
asymptotic thus follows from Theorem
2.1
by the contraction
principle.

We now show that the zero level set of
{\mathscr{I}}_{m}
is equal to the set
of invariant probability measures for the flow
\Phi^{m}
associated to
the hydrodynamic evolution (
2.18
). By the goodness of
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
,
if
\wp
lies in the zero level set of
{\mathscr{I}}_{m}
then the infimum in
(
2.28
) is achieved, i.e. there exists
P\in{\mathscr{P}}_{\textrm{stat}}

satisfying
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)=0
and
\wp=P\circ\imath_{t}^{-1}
. As follows
from (
2.26
) and (
2.25
)
{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}(P)=0
implies that
P

almost surely
({\boldsymbol{\pi}},{\boldsymbol{J}})
have densities
({\boldsymbol{\rho}},{\boldsymbol{j}})
that
satisfy
{\boldsymbol{j}}=-\nabla{\boldsymbol{\rho}}+\sigma({\boldsymbol{\rho}})E
. Hence, the
marginal of
P
on the first variable is concentrated on the set of

{\boldsymbol{\pi}}
whose density
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
solves (
1.2
) with
D=1
. By
stationarity of
P
, this implies that
P\circ\imath_{t}^{-1}
is an
invariant probability of
\Phi^{m}
as claimed.

It remains to show that if
E
is orthogonally decomposable then

{\mathscr{I}}_{m}(\wp)=0
implies
\wp=\delta_{\bar{\rho}\,dx}
where
\bar{\rho}

is the unique stationary solution to (

. As
already remarked, if

argument in
[

, Thm. 7.7]
then implies that there exists a
unique stationary solution of mass
m
to the hydrodynamic equation
that is globally attractive, hence a unique stationary probability for
the flow
\Phi^{m}
that is concentrated on the stationary solution.
∎

We consider now the level one projection.

Proof of Corollary

.

Let

\psi\colon{\mathscr{P}}_{\mathrm{stat}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{+}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})\times{\mathcal{H}}_{-p}^{d}

is defined only for the probabilities

does not depend on

Recall the definitions of
\pi_{T,N}
,

, and
{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}
,
in (
2.29
), (
2.30
), and (
2.24
). Recall also the relation
(

\psi\big({\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}\big)=\int\!{\mathfrak{R}}_{T,N}(d{\boldsymbol{\pi}},d{\boldsymbol{J}})\,\Big({\boldsymbol{\pi}}(t),\frac{{\boldsymbol{J}}(t)}{t}\Big)=\big(\pi_{T,N},J_{T,N}\big)-\frac{1}{T}\big(0,\mathcal{E}_{T,N}\big)\,.

(6.1)

Since by (

The next result concerns the projection on the density for the level

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

In the case

Finally, we give a sketch of the proof of Theorem

7.
Uniqueness of periodic solutions

Fix
T>0
. Throughout this section,

F:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a smooth,
T
-periodic vector
field. We investigate in this section the asymptotic behavior of
solutions to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}\;=\;\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}\,+\,\nabla\cdot[\,\sigma({\boldsymbol{u}})\,F\,]\\
&{\boldsymbol{u}}(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot)\;,\end{aligned}\right.

(7.1)

where the initial condition
u_{0}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
is such that

0\leq u_{0}(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.

Existence of weak solutions is provided by the hydrodynamic limit of
WASEP. This argument shows that the solution takes value in the
interval
[0,1]
. These bounds can be derived also from the maximum
principle and the observation that
\sigma(1)=\sigma(0)=0
. By
parabolic regularity, a weak solution is smooth in

(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
. Uniqueness is derived as in
[
14
, Lemma
7.2]
. The proof of this lemma yields that the
L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
norm
of the difference of two weak solutions does not increase in time. The
main result of this section strengthen this lemma and asserts that the

L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{d})
distance of two different weak solutions decreases in
time. It reads as follows.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a smooth,
T
-periodic
vector field. For each
m\in[0,1]
, the equation

\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{u}}\;=\;\Delta{\boldsymbol{u}}\,+\,\nabla\cdot[\,\sigma({\boldsymbol{u}})\,F\,]\;.

admits a unique
T
-periodic solution

{\boldsymbol{u}}:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x)\,dx=m
and
0\leq{\boldsymbol{u}}(t,x)\leq 1
for all

t
. This solution is represented by
{\boldsymbol{u}}^{(m)}
. Moreover, for each

u_{0}:{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to[0,1]
such that

There exist constants

for all

Asymptotic behavior of linear parabolic equations.

Let
G:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Proposition 7.4
.

Fix two probability densities

w_{2}
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}

Recall the definition of the diffusions

Since

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{2}(t,x)\,f(x)\,dx\;-\;\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{1}(t,x)\,f(x)\,dx

Therefore, by (

where the supremum is carried over all continuous function

\int\big|\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{2}(t,x)\,-\,{\boldsymbol{w}}_{1}(t,x)\,\big|\,dx\;\leq\;2\,\sup_{x,y\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\,P\big[\,\tau^{Z}_{x,y}\,\geq\,t\,\big]\;,

Appendix A

We present in this section some estimates used in the article. Let

c_{x,y}(\eta)\;=\;\eta_{x}\,[1-\eta_{y}]\,e^{(1/2)\,E_{N}(x,y)}\;,\quad(x,y)\,\in\,{\mathbb{B}}_{N}\;

Lemma A.1
.

Given a set of bounded functions

\phi^{x,y}_{s}:D([0,\infty),\Sigma_{N})\to{\mathbb{R}}

(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}
, progressively measurable, the process

{\mathbb{M}}^{\phi}_{t}\;=\;\exp\sum_{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{N}}\Big\{\int_{0}^{t}\phi^{x,y}_{s}\,{\mathscr{N}}^{x,y}_{(s,s+ds]}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})\,-\,N^{2}\int_{0}^{t}c_{x,y}({\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\{e^{\phi^{x,y}_{s}}-1\}\,ds\Big\}

The proof of this lemma is similar to the one of Proposition A.2.6 in

[
15
]
and left to the reader. This martingale corresponds to the
Radon-Nikodym derivative (restricted to the interval

) of the

with
respect

Recall that the symmetric simple exclusion process is the Markov chain
on
\Sigma_{N}
whose generator is

, introduced in (

\color[rgb]{0.2,0.2,0.8}\nu_{\alpha}

\alpha

Lemma A.2

For all

\log\,{\mathbb{E}}^{0}_{\nu_{1/2}}\Big[\,\Big(\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\nu_{1/2}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\Big)^{p}\,\Big]\;\leq\;C_{p}\,(1\,+\,T)\,N^{d}

,

,

Proof.

The proof reduces to a standard computation of exponential
martingales. To emphasize the dependence of the measure

on the external field

represent the measure
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}_{\mu_{N,K}}

As

On the other hand,

{\mathbb{E}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}\Big[\,\Big(\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{E}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\Big)^{p}\,\Big]\;=\;{\mathbb{E}}^{pE}_{\mu_{N,K}}\Big[\,\Big(\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{E}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\Big)^{p}\;\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{pE}_{\mu_{N,K}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\,\Big]\;.

\Big\|\,\Big(\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{E}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\Big)^{p}\;\frac{d{\mathbb{P}}^{0}_{\mu_{N,K}}}{d{\mathbb{P}}^{pE}_{\mu_{N,K}}}\Big|_{[0,T]}\,\Big\|_{\infty}\;\leq\;e^{C_{p}\,T\,N^{d}}

A geometric approach to Feigin–Loktev fusion product and cluster relations in coherent Satake category
Ilya Dumanski

Abstract
. We propose a geometric realization of the Feigin–Loktev fusion product of graded cyclic modules over the current algebra. This allows us to compute it in several new cases. We also relate the Feigin–Loktev fusion product to the convolution of perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian of the adjoint group. This relation allows us to establish the existence of exact triples, conjecturally corresponding to cluster relations in the Grothendieck ring of coherent Satake category.

0.3.

Another application of the geometric interpretation of Feigin–Loktev fusion product is the following.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is of simply-laced type until the end of the Introduction.

Recall that in
[
CW
]
the authors proved for
G=GL_{n}
and conjectured for other groups that there is a cluster structure on the Grothendieck ring of the category of equivariant perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
(we do not encounter loop-rotation equivariance here for simplicity).

On the other hand, the cluster patterns of the category
\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
of finite-dimensional modules over the quantum loop group, were noticed by several authors (probably most well-known approach is due to Hernandez–Leclerc, see
[
HL
]
; we do not appeal to it in the present paper). In particular, in
[
Ked
,
DK2
]
, the cluster interpretation of Q-systems is suggested. The mutation matrix of the appearing cluster algebra is the same as conjectured in
[
CW
]
.

Despite this obvious similarity between the Grothendieck ring of the category
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
and the Q-systems ring, studied in
[
Ked
,
DK2
]
(related to the Grothendieck ring of the category
\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
), no precise statements about relation between these two categories seem to be present in the literature. We propose such a statement. Moreover, it helps to derive some new results about
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
, using the corresponding well-known results for
\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
.

Q-systems are certain short exact sequences, whose terms are tensor products of Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules, which we denote by
KR_{i,\ell}
(here
i
is a vertex of Dynkin diagram and
\ell
is a nonnegative integer). Thus, one can say that the role of cluster variables in the initial seed in the cluster algebra of
[
Ked
,
DK2
]
is played by
KR_{i,\ell}
. The corresponding objects in
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
are simple real perverse coherent sheaves
\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}
(see Section
3
for definition).

For certain modules
M
over
U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
their graded limit
M_{loc}
is defined, which is a module over
\mathfrak{g}[t]
(see
[
BCKV
]
). Then the following isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules holds, which is a starting point of our connection:

(KR_{i,\ell})_{loc}\simeq D(\ell,\ell\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\simeq R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell})^{*}[-\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}],

where
D(\ell,\ell\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
is an affine Demazure module
(note that
R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell})
is concentrated in the cohomological degree
\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
). We extend this isomorphism to “products” of these objects. Here “product” of perverse coherent sheaves is their convolution
\star
, “product” of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules is their Feigin–Loktev fusion product, and “product” of quantum loop group modules is just their tensor product (note that the relation between fusion product and convolution of perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian for
PGL_{2}
was stated in
[
BFM
, Proposition 8.5]
).

Theorem C
.

(Propositions
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
) Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is of simply-laced type. Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}
be vertices of the Dynkin diagram and
\ell_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\ell_{k}
be nonnegative integers. Then

(KR_{i_{1},\ell_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes KR_{i_{k},\ell_{k}})_{loc}\simeq D(\ell_{1},\ell_{1}\omega_{i_{1}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\ast\ldots\ast D(\ell_{k},\ell_{k}\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\simeq\\
R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{i_{1},\ell_{1}}\star\ldots\star\mathcal{P}_{i_{k},\ell_{k}})^{*}[-\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i_{1}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}-\ldots-\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i_{k}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}]

Further, let us point out how this connection can be exploited. In
[
CW
]
the authors showed that the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
has a structure of cluster algebra for
G=GL_{n}
and conjectured for other groups (
[
CW
, Conjecture 1,10]
). An important part of the proof for
GL_{n}
is showing the existence of certain exact triangles (
[
CW
, Proposition 2.17]
), which correspond to cluster mutations of the initial cluster seed. We propose the analog of these exact triangles for all simply-laced types.

The idea of the proof is the following. We reduce the exactness of triple of sheaves to the exactness of global sections of these sheaves, shifted by large powers of the very ample (determinant) line bundle. The resulting
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules turn out to be graded limits of Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules, which fit into Q-systems. Using
[
CV
]
, this implies required exactness. So in short, we use the existence of Q-systems for modules over the loop quantum group to deduce their analogs for perverse coherent sheaves.

Theorem D
.

(See Theorem
3.5
for precise statement) There are certain exact sequences of perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grasmannian. They are analogs of Q-systems for modules over
U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
and they correspond to cluster relations in the Grothendieck ring of
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
.

Note that in
[
CW
, Subsection 6.4]
it is already stated, that taking global sections reduces these short exact sequences of sheaves to the graded limits of Q-systems. However, the authors only take into account the
\mathfrak{g}
-action on the global sections, which seems to be too weak structure to go in the opposite direction (deduce Theorem D from the existence of Q-systems). Taking into account
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-action resolves this problem (note also that in general the graded limit of
KR_{i,\ell}
is not isomorphic to
V(\ell\omega_{i})
outside of type A).

We expect that a similar proof can be given in non-simply laced case as well.

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section 1 we fix the notation, define all required algebraic and geometric objects and recall their properties to be used.

In Section 2 we present our main result, which gives a geometric interpretation of the Feigin–Loktev fusion product (Theorem
2.1

In Section 3 we prove a relation between the Feigin–Loktev fusion product and convolution of perverse coherent sheaves (Proposition

3.3

1.2.
Feigin–Loktev fusion product

In
[
FeLo
]
the following operation was introduced. Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
be cyclic graded finite-dimensional modules over
\mathfrak{g}[t]
. We assume that each
M_{i}
has a unique up to scaling highest
\mathfrak{g}
-weight vector
m_{i}
, and this vector is cyclic. For
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
we define
M_{i}(c_{i})
to be a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module, which is obtained as a composition of the twist
\mathfrak{g}[t]\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}[t]
,
xt^{s}\mapsto x(t+c_{i})^{s}
(here
x\in\mathfrak{g}
), with the action of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
on
M_{i}
(so
M_{i}(c_{i})
is isomorphic to
M_{i}
as a vector space, but
xt^{s}\in\mathfrak{g}[t]
acts as
x(t+c_{i})^{s}
).
M_{i}(c_{i})
is cyclic (with the same cyclic vector
m_{i}
), but no longer graded. By
[
FeLo
, Proposition 1.4]
, for
c_{i}\neq c_{j}
the tensor product

M_{1}(c_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes M_{k}(c_{k})

is cyclic with the cyclic vector
m_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{k}
.
The grading on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
by
t
-degree induces the filtration on this tensor product:

F_{\leq n}(M_{1}(c_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes M_{k}(c_{k}))=U(\mathfrak{g}[t])_{\leq n}(m_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{k}).

The Feigin–Loktev fusion product is defined as the associated graded module:

M_{1}\ast\ldots\ast M_{k}=\mathrm{gr}_{F}(M_{1}(c_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes M_{k}(c_{k})).

It was conjectured in
[
FeLo
]
that the resulting module does not depend on the choice of parameters
c_{i}
, and that the operation of fusion product is associative.

It is beyond our current possibilities to prove this conjecture for arbitrary modules
M_{i}
. But one suitable family of modules for which
some results can be obtained are the affine Demazure modules.

1.3.
Affine Demazure modules

We refer to
[
BCKV
,
CV
,
Nao2
,
Rav2
]
for details on (generalized) affine
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-invariant Demazure modules.

To an integrable dominant affine weight
\Lambda\in\widehat{P}^{+}
we associate the irreducible highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation
V(\Lambda)
.
Let
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k}\in\widehat{P}^{+}
, and
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\widehat{P}
, such that
\xi_{i}\in\widetilde{W}\Lambda_{i}
for any
i
. Since dimensions of weight spaces of
V(\Lambda)
are invariant under the action of
\widetilde{W}
, for any
i
there is a unique (up to scaling) vector
v_{\xi_{i}}\in V(\Lambda_{i})
of weight
\xi_{i}
. The generalized affine Demazure module is defined as

\mathbb{D}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}):=U(\mathfrak{I}).(v_{\xi_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{\xi_{k}})\subset V(\Lambda_{1})\otimes\ldots V(\Lambda_{k}).

By definition, it is a module over
\mathfrak{I}
, but in fact, if
\langle\xi_{i},{\alpha^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\rangle\leq 0
for any
i
and positive coroot
{\alpha^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
, it is
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-invariant (recall that
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{g}[t]
).

The most well-known case is
k=1
. In this case the module
\mathbb{D}(w\Lambda)
for
w\in\widehat{W},\Lambda\in\widehat{P}^{+}
(sometimes we will denote it by
V_{w}(\Lambda)
) is called the affine Demazure module (note that if
w
is an element of
\widehat{W}
rather than of
\widetilde{W}
, the vector of weight
w\Lambda
may lie not in
V(\Lambda)
but in some other irreducible highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module).

The
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-invariant affine Demazure modules are naturally parametrized by the set
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times P^{+}
.
Namely, if for
w\in\widehat{W},\Lambda\in\widehat{P}^{+},\mu\in P^{+}
one has
w\Lambda=\ell\Lambda_{0}+w_{0}\mu
, then
\mathbb{D}(w\Lambda)
is
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-invariant. We denote such a module by
D(\ell,\mu)
.

D(\ell,\mu)
is naturally a graded cyclic highest weight
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module with cyclic vector of weight
\mu
. Note that in this case
w
can me presented in a form
\tau_{w_{0}\nu^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}v
with
\nu^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in\widehat{P}^{\vee+}
,

.

The modules of the form
D(\ell,\ell\iota(\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))
, where
\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
is a dominant coweight, are particularly well-studied (see, for instance,
[
FoLi1
,
FoLi2
]
). For them the corresponding (extended) affine Weyl group element is
\tau_{w_{0}\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
, and the affine dominant weight is
\ell\Lambda_{0}
, so
D(\ell,\ell\iota(\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))\simeq\mathbb{D}(\tau_{w_{0}\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\ell\Lambda_{0}))
. We omit the symbol
\iota
in their notation and simply write

(note that the authors of
[

]

1.4.
Affine Grassmannian and affine flag variety

Fix the notations
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}((t)),\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{C}[[t]]
.
Let
G^{sc}
be the simply-connected group, corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}
, and

be the adjoint group.

We denote by
{\mathrm{Gr}}
the affine Grassmannian of the group
G=G^{ad}
.
It has the left action by the group
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
. All sheaves we consider are considered as
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant sheaves. Global sections of such sheaves are naturally
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-representations (although we usually consider them as
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-representations). Informally speaking, consideration of
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant sheaves on
{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{ad}}
allows us to get as many
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-representations geometrically as possible. See
[

-equivariant sheaves on
{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{ad}}
(in non-simply-laced types the twisted version is considered there, which we do not need here). Note also that it agrees with
[

There is a very ample
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant line bundle, called the determinant line bundle, and denoted by

. For any
\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in P^{\vee}
, there is a point

. All

-orbits on

are of the form
G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}

, and we denote
{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}=G(\mathcal{O})t^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
. The closures of these orbits are called the spherical affine Schubert varieties. Their dimensions are given by:
\dim\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}=\langle 2\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle
. The Borel–Weil–Bott-type theorem claims that for
\ell\geq 0

-modules (see

(1.1)

\displaystyle H^{0}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\mathcal{L}^{\ell})

\displaystyle\simeq D(\ell,\ell\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{*},

\displaystyle H^{>0}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\mathcal{L}^{\ell})

\displaystyle\simeq 0.

\mathrm{Fl}

1.6.
Global convolution diagrams

An important feature of the convolution diagrams is that they admit “globalization”. The global convolution diagram of the affine Grassmannians is particularly well-known, because it may be used to define the commutativity constraint in the geometric Satake equivalence.

We need a variant of this construction, the global convolution diagram of

2.
Main results

Let
M_{i}
be
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules as in Subsection
1.2
.
We prove the following key theorem, which tells how to compute
M_{1}\ast\ldots\ast M_{k}
if these modules appear in a certain geometric context.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{k}
be a proper morphism of schemes. Suppose the group scheme
G^{sc}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathcal{O})\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts on
X
, in such a way that
f
is
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant (here
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acts by the loop rotation on
G^{sc}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathcal{O})
, and by the standard dilations on
\mathbb{A}^{k}
).

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a
G^{sc}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathcal{O})\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariant coherent sheaf on
X
, flat over
\mathbb{A}^{k}
, and we have:

(i)

For any
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
with pairwise distinct coordinates,
H^{0}(X|_{\bf c},\mathcal{F}|_{\bf c})^{*}
is the
G^{sc}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathcal{O})|_{\bf c}=G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})^{\times k}
-module isomorphic to
M_{1}(c_{1})\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes M_{k}(c_{k})
.

(ii)

The dual zero fiber
M=H^{0}(X|_{0},\mathcal{F}|_{0})^{*}
is a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module with a unique up to scaling highest
\mathfrak{g}
-weight vector, and this vector is cyclic.

(iii)

Higher cohomologies of all fibers vanish:
H^{>0}(X|_{\bf c},\mathcal{F}|_{\bf c})=0
for all
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
.

Then the fusion product of modules
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
is independent of the choice of parameters and is isomorphic to the dual zero fiber of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
:

M_{1}\ast\ldots\ast M_{k}\simeq M.

Moreover, it is associative, which means that for all
m
one has:

(M_{1}\ast\ldots\ast M_{m})\ast M_{m+1}\ast\ldots\ast M_{k}\simeq M.

Proof.

Due to
[
DFF
, Lemma 4.1]
we have a natural inclusion of
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
to the group of global sections of
G^{sc}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathcal{O})
(viewed as a sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{k}
). Hence
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
admits a natural
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-action. It also admits the
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathbb{A}^{k})\simeq\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}]
-action, and these two actions commute. So we can regard
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
as a
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-bimodule.

Moreover, due to
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-equivariance of
f
, there is a nonnegative grading on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
, which is respected both by
t
-degree grading on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
and by the standard grading on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}]\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{k}}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
.

Due to the Euler characteristic invariance in flat families and higher cohomology of fibers of
\mathcal{F}
vanishing,
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
is flat and hence free over
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
. Let us consider the
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-dual
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-bimodule
\Gamma=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})^{\vee}
.

For any (closed) point
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
, let us denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}
the one dimensional
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-module, obtained as a quotient by the maximal ideal, corresponding to
{\bf c}
.

By the assumption (ii),
\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{0}
is
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-generated by a highest weight vector. Let
v
be its homogeneous lift to
\Gamma
. The set
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])v
spans the zero fiber
\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{0}
. Hence, by the graded Nakayama lemma,
\Gamma
is generated by
v
as a
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-bimodule.

It follows that for any
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
the module
\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}
is
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-cyclic, as
v\otimes 1
is the cyclic vector. As in the definition of the fusion product, we obtain a filtration
F
on
\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}
, induced by the
t
-grading on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
.

Let
{\bf c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{A}^{k}
be a point with pairwise distinct coordinates
c_{i}
. We now claim that one has a surjection of cyclic
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
-modules:

(2.1)

\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{0}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{gr}_{F}(\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}).

We need to show that if
u\in U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
annihilates the cyclic vector of the left-hand side module, then it also annihilates the cyclic vector of the right-hand side module. We may assume
u
is homogeneous. The fact that it annihilates the cyclic vector
v\otimes 1\in\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{0}
means that there are
u^{\prime}\in U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
and
P\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
such that
P(0)=0
and

(2.2)

(u-Pu^{\prime})v=0

in
\Gamma
. We may assume that
P,u^{\prime}
and the relation (
2.2
) are homogeneous, and hence
\deg(u^{\prime})<\deg(u)
in
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
. (
2.2
) implies also that one has
(u-P({\bf c})u^{\prime})(v\otimes 1)=0
in
\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}
, and because of the degrees inequality it follows that
u
annihilates the cyclic vector in

\mathrm{gr}(\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c}).

Thus, (
2.1
) is indeed a surjection.

However, since
\Gamma
is free over
\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{A}^{k}})
, modules in (
2.1
) have equal dimensions, and thus it is in fact an isomorphism. Hence, using the assumption (i), we obtain

M\simeq\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{0}\simeq\mathrm{gr}_{F}(\Gamma\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})}\mathbb{C}_{\bf c})\simeq\mathrm{gr}_{F}(M_{1}(c_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes M_{k}(c_{k})).

Note that the filtration here coincides with the one in the definition of fusion product, as both are induced by the
t
-degree grading on
U(\mathfrak{g}[t])
, applied to the unique up to scalar highest weight vector.

Hence, the first part of the Theorem is proved.

The second part about the associativity (in the assumption that
\Gamma
is free, which is true in our case), is proved in
[
DFF
, Propositin A.1(b)]
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In fact one can also prove that the
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A}^{k})
-dualized global sections

-
\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{A}^{k}})

For

For

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
be dominant coweights,
\mu
be a dominant weight and
\ell_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\ell_{k}\geq\ell
be nonnegative integers. Then

D(\ell_{1},\ell_{1}\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\ast\ldots\ast D(\ell_{k},\ell_{k}\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\ast D(\ell,\mu)\simeq\\
\mathbb{D}\Bigl(\tau_{w_{0}\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})\Lambda_{0},\tau_{w_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{3})\Lambda_{0},\ldots,\tau_{w_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})}(\ell_{k}-\ell)\Lambda_{0},\ell\Lambda_{0}+\ell w_{0}(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})+w_{0}\mu\Bigr),

and the fusion product of these modules is associative.

Note that the module on the right-hand side is more natural from the geometric point of view, as it is just the dual space of sections of a line bundle on the convolution of Schubert varieties due to Proposition
1.1
.

An alternative way to describe it algebraically is as the cyclic submodule generated by the tensor product of cyclic vectors inside

D(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2},(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\otimes D(\ell_{2}-\ell_{3},(\ell_{2}-\ell_{3})(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))\otimes\ldots\\
\otimes D(\ell_{k}-\ell_{k+1},(\ell_{k}-\ell_{k+1})(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))\otimes D(\ell,\ell\iota(\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})+\mu).

Proof.

Let
D(\ell,\mu)\simeq\mathbb{D}(w\Lambda)
,
w\in\widehat{W},\Lambda\in\widehat{P}^{+}
.

We apply Theorem
2.1
to the variety
X=\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}{\widetilde{\times}}\ldots{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{\mathrm{Fl}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{w}
and its line bundle
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k},\Lambda)}
. Let us check that all assumptions of the theorem are satisfied.

The flatness assumption is verified in Proposition
1.2
.

The assumption (i) is satisfied, as due to (
1.10
), (
1.11
), (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), for
{\bf c}
with pairwise distinct coordinates we have

H^{0}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}{\widetilde{\times}}\ldots{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{\mathrm{Fl}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{w}|_{{\bf c}},\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k},\Lambda)}|_{\bf c})^{*}\simeq\\
H^{0}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\times\ldots\times\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\times\overline{\mathrm{Fl}}^{w},\mathcal{L}^{\ell_{1}}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}^{\ell_{k}}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}^{\Lambda})^{*}\simeq\\
D(\ell_{1},\ell_{1}\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes D(\ell_{k},\ell_{k}\lambda_{k}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\boxtimes D(\ell,\mu).

The assumption (ii) is satisfied due to (

We check the assumption (iii) for

and for

3.
Coherent Satake category and quantum loop group

Perverse coherent sheaves were introduced in
[
Bez
]
,
[
AB
]
. The category of equivariant perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian was studied in
[
BFM
]
,
[
CW
]
,
[
FF
]
. In this section, we explain how our results are related to the convolution of certain objects in this category, and how it provides a bridge between two categories: perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian and finite-dimensional modules over the quantum loop group, giving a conceptual explanation of the same cluster algebras appearance in
[
CW
]
and
[
DK2
]
.
Using this, we construct short exact sequences of perverse coherent sheaves for all simply-laced types, whose existence was conjectured in
[
CW
]
.

3.1.
Coherent Satake category

Let us recall some notation of
[
CW
]
(see this paper for definitions). Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
the category of
G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant perverse coherent sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. We do not encounter loop-rotation equivarience here for simplicity, though all statements we are going to prove have the analogs for the category
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{{}^{sc}}(\mathcal{O})\rtimes\mathbb{C}^{\times}}({\mathrm{Gr}})
, with proofs being essentially the same (
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-twist of sheaves corresponds to grading shift of their global sections, which we are going to consider).

Simple objects in this category are parametrized by pairs
(\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\mu)\in P^{\vee}\times P
, such that
\mu
is dominant for the Levi factor of the parabolic subgroup of
G
, corresponding to
\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
. Namely, there is a
G(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant vector bundle
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
on
{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
, and one defines
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\mu}=IC({\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\mathcal{V}_{\mu}[\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}])
.

The case of major importance is when
\mathcal{V}_{\mu}
is a power of the determinant line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}
, since the corresponding perverse sheaves are real simple and they are expected to serve as cluster variables in the Grothendieck ring. In this case one has
\mathcal{P}_{{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\ell}:=IC({\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\mathcal{L}^{\ell}[\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle])=\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{\ell}[\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle]
(recall that
\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}=\langle 2\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle
).
We also set
\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}:=\mathcal{P}_{\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell}
for
i\in I
.

Note that
H^{>0}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}},\mathcal{L}^{\ell})=0
, hence
R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}},\ell})
is a
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module concentrated in a single cohomological degree
\langle\rho,{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\rangle
. We consider such modules as objects of the (abelian) category of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules. Then we have the following result, relating the convolution
\star
in
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
and Feigin–Loktev fusion product
\ast
(it appeared for
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}=\omega^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}
in
[
BFM
, Proposition 8.5]
):

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\in P^{\vee+}
,
\ell_{1}\geq\ell_{2}\geq 0
,
R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{2}})
is concentrated in a single cohomological degree
\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{2}\rangle
, and one has an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules

\bigl(R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}})[-\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}\rangle]\bigr)^{*}\ast\bigl(R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{2}})[-\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{2}\rangle]\bigr)^{*}\simeq\\
\bigl(R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{2}})[-\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{2}\rangle]\bigr)^{*}.

A similar isomorphism holds for any number of modules.

Proof.

By the definition of operation
\star
(see
[
BFM
]
,
[
CW
]
),

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{2}}=R\theta_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}[\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{2}\rangle]),

where
\theta:\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\rightarrow\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\lambda_{2}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
is the convolution morphism. Now the Proposition follows from Proposition
1.1
and Corollary
2.3
.

The case of arbitrary number of modules is proved similarly.
∎

3.2.
Quantum loop group

From now on we assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is of simply-laced type. Consider the loop algebra
\bm{L}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}[t,t^{-1}]
, and the corresponding quantum loop group
U_{q}(\bm{L}\mathfrak{g})
. Denote by
\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\bm{L}\mathfrak{g})
the category of its finite-dimensional representations.
We denote Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules by
KR_{i,\ell}
(here
i\in I,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
; we omit an extra spectral parameter in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, as it will not matter in what follows).
Their tensor products fit into short exact sequences, called Q-systems, see
[
Her
, Theorem 6.1]
. In
[
Ked
,
DK2
]
the cluster meaning of Q-systems was noticed. In the cluster algebra described there, the role of cluster variables in the initial cluster seed is played by certain Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules (up to a sign).

For certain modules
M\in\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\bm{L}\mathfrak{g})
their
graded limit

M_{loc}
is defined, which is a finite-dimensional graded
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module; we refer to
[
BCKV
]
for a nice survey on this.
[
Nao4
, Theorem A]
claims that the following holds:

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
i_{1},i_{2}\in I
,
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules:

(KR_{i_{1},\ell_{1}})_{loc}\ast(KR_{i_{2},\ell_{2}})_{loc}\simeq(KR_{i_{1},\ell_{1}}\otimes KR_{i_{2},\ell_{2}})_{loc},

and similar isomorphism holds for any number of modules.

3.3.
Relation between the two via fusion product

Combining the above results, we get the following

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in I
,
\ell_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\ell_{k}\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules:

(KR_{i_{1},\ell_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes KR_{i_{k},\ell_{k}})_{loc}\simeq\bigl(R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{i_{1},\ell_{1}}\star\ldots\star\mathcal{P}_{i_{k},\ell_{k}})[-\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i_{1}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}+\ldots+\omega_{i_{k}}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}]\bigr)^{*}.

Proof.

Using Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, it is sufficient to prove this Proposition for
k=1
. The case
k=1
follows from

(KR_{i,\ell})_{loc}\simeq D(\ell,\ell\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\simeq\bigl(R\Gamma(\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell})[-\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}]\bigr)^{*},

where the first isomorphism is
[
FoLi2
, Theorem 4]
, and the second isomorphism is (
1.1
).
∎

We do not know if this proposition is a shadow of existence of some monoidal functor between subcategories of
\mathcal{P}_{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}({\mathrm{Gr}})
and of
\mathrm{Rep}\ U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
.

3.4.
Cluster relations in coherent Satake category

We now give an application of this connection. In
[
CW
, Proposition 2.17]
, the authors prove that certain perverse coherent sheaves fit into short exact sequences. These correspond to cluster relations in the Grothendieck ring. Their proof works for
G=GL_{n}
only, as it uses the lattice realization of affine Grassmannian, though a similar result is expected for any type, see
[
CW
, Conjecture 1.10]
. We provide a proof in all simply-laced types. Thus, it can be seen as a first step towards the proof of
[
CW
, Conjecture 1.10]
.

First, we need the following

Lemma 3.4
.

For any dominant coweights
\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}
and integers
\ell_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\ell_{k}
the perverse coherent sheaf
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\ldots\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k},\ell_{k}}
on
{\mathrm{Gr}}
is concentrated in a single cohomological degree
\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}\rangle
(with respect to the standard t-structure).

Proof.

By Serre vanishing, it is sufficient to check that for all
N\gg 0
the complex

R\Gamma((\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\ldots\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k},\ell_{k}})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{N})

is concentrated in this cohomological degree. Let
\theta:\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}}{\widetilde{\times}}\ldots{\widetilde{\times}}\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}}\rightarrow\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}}
be the convolution morphism. Then we have

(3.1)

R\Gamma((\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{1}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},\ell_{1}}\star\ldots\star\mathcal{P}_{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k},\ell_{k}})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\simeq}}R\Gamma((R\theta_{*}\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k})}[\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}}])\otimes\mathcal{L}^{N})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\simeq}}\\
R\Gamma(R\theta_{*}(\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k})}[\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}}]\otimes\theta^{*}\mathcal{L}^{N}))\simeq R\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{(\ell_{1}+N,\ldots,\ell_{k}+N)}[\frac{1}{2}\dim{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}}]),

and the last complex is concentrated in the cohomological degree
\langle\rho,\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{k}\rangle
for any
N>-\ell_{k}
by Proposition
1.1
. Here the isomorphism
(i)
holds by definition of the operation
\star
, and the isomorphism
(ii)
is the projection formula.
∎

Before we state our next theorem, we make the following observation, which will be crutial for its proof.

Recall the standard Serre equivalence between the category of coherent sheaves on a projective variety
Z
and the quotient of the category of finitely generated graded modules over the homogeneous coordinate ring of
Z
by the subcategory of finite-dimensional modules. Namely, if
L
is a very ample line bundle on
Z
, the functor
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{F}\otimes L^{n})
induces the equivalence between
\mathrm{coh}(Z)
and
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})
-
\mathrm{grmod}_{f.g.}\left/\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})\right.
-
\mathrm{grmod}_{f.d.}
.

Now, assume an affine group scheme
H
acts on
Z
and
L
is an equivariant very ample line bundle. This induces the action of
H
on the ring
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})
by graded automorphisms (and conversely, such an action on this ring determines the action and an equivariant very ample line bundle on its
\operatorname{Proj}
). Given a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Z
, the data of equivariant structure on it, under the Serre equivalence, induces the structure of
H
-equivariant graded
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})
-module on
\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{F}\otimes L^{n})
(and conversely, given such a module, we get an equvariant coherent sheaf on
Z
).
H
-equivariant morphisms on both sides are
H
-invariants in spaces of all morphisms, hence the equivariant morphisms of sheaves correspond to equivariant morphisms of modules. Thus, there is an equivariant version of the Serre equivalence:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{coh}^{H}(Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})\text{-}\mathrm{grmod}^{H}_{f.g.}\left/\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(L^{n})\right.\text{-}\mathrm{grmod}^{H}_{f.d.}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\mapsto\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{F}\otimes L^{n}).

Let us introduce some notation. We denote by
C=(C_{ij})_{i,j}
the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote by
\alpha_{i}
(
\alpha^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{i}
) simple roots (coroots). For
i,j\in I
write
i\sim j
if
C_{ij}<0
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
i\in I
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
there are exact sequences of perverse coherent sheaves on
{\mathrm{Gr}}
:

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\star}_{i,\ell}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell+1}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}\rightarrow 0;

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell-1}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell+1}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\star}_{i,\ell}\rightarrow 0,

where

\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}^{\star}=\bigstarlim_{j\sim i}\mathcal{P}_{j,\ell}^{\star(-C_{ij})},

and the product is taken in arbitrary order.

Note that we assumed that
\mathfrak{g}
is of simply-laced type, and hence
C_{ij}=-1
whenever
j\sim i
, but we write it in this form just to be consistent with non-simply-laced case.

Proof.

The second exact sequence is obtained from the first one by applying the Grothendieck–Serre duality. We prove the first.

Tensoring by a power of
\mathcal{L}
we may assume
\ell=0
.

Note that

\sum_{j\sim i}-C_{ij}\omega^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{j}=\left(\sum_{j\in I}-C_{ij}\omega^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{j}\right)+2\omega^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{i}=-\alpha^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}_{i}+2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}.

Hence,

(3.3)

\mathcal{P}^{\star}_{i,0}\simeq\bigstarlim_{j\sim i}\mathcal{P}^{\star-(C_{ij})}_{j,0}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}[\langle\rho,2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle].

Also,

(3.4)

\mathcal{P}_{i,0}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,0}\simeq\mathcal{P}_{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee},0}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}[\langle\rho,2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle].

Now, it is sufficient to construct the distinguished triangle of the form

(3.5)

\mathcal{P}_{i,1}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,0}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,0}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\star}_{i,0}[1]

By Lemma
3.4
, all three terms of (
3.5
) lie in a single cohomological degree. Clearly,
\langle\rho,2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle=\langle\rho,{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\rangle+1
, so in fact they lie in the same cohomological degree.

Hence, we are to prove that in the abelian category of coherent sheaves on
{\mathrm{Gr}}
there is an exact triple

0\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{i,1}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,-1}[-2\langle\rho,\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{P}_{i,0}\star\mathcal{P}_{i,0}[-2\langle\rho,\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{P}^{\star}_{i,0}[-2\langle\rho,\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}\rangle+1]\rightarrow 0,

whose terms we denote by
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
(so we need to construct the exact triple
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{C}
).

Using (
3.4
) and (
3.3
), we see the existence of a surjective morphism

(3.6)

\mathcal{B}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}\simeq\mathcal{C}.

To construct
\phi
and prove the exactness, we consider these sheaves as sheaves on a finite-dimensional projective variety
\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
.
In view of the equivalence (
3.2
), the functor

\mathcal{F}\mapsto\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})

from
\mathrm{coh}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}(\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}})
to equivariant modules over the ring
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
is fully faithful, and thus, it is sufficient to construct the short exact triple of
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
-modules.

Due to (
3.1
) together with Corollary
2.3
, for
n>0
we have:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}&\simeq D(n+1,(n+1)\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\ast D(n-1,(n-1)\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee});\\
\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}&\simeq D(n,n\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\ast D(n,n\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee});\\
\Gamma(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}&\simeq\bigastlim_{j\sim i}D(n,n\omega_{j}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{\ast(-C_{ij})}.\end{split}

Note that these modules are nothing else but the graded limits of modules, appearing in Q-systems for
U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
-modules. Using this, in
[
CV
]
the authors deduced that these
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules fit into exact triples (see Theorem 4 and Section 5.9; note that for classical types usage of quantum loop groups may be avoided in the proof).
The map
\Gamma(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}\hookrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}
, constructed in
[
CV
]
is the natural embedding
D(n,n(2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))\hookrightarrow D(n,n2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
; and the map
\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})^{*}
is the surjective map, which maps the natural cyclic vector to the natural cyclic vector.

Hence, there is a short exact sequence of graded vector spaces

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}}}{{\rightarrow}}\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\psi}}}{{\rightarrow}}\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})\rightarrow 0.

It is left to show that these maps are in fact morphisms of
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
-modules.

First, we claim that the morphism
\tilde{\psi}
, constructed in this way, coincides with the one, induced from
\psi
of (
3.6
). Indeed, the map constructed in
[
CV
]
is induced by the embedding
D(n,n(2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}))\hookrightarrow D(n,2n\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})
, whose geometric counterpart, the embedding
\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}-\alpha_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
, was used to construct
\psi
. Since
\psi
is a morphism of sheaves,
\tilde{\psi}
is a morphism of graded modules over the ring
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
, so the claim is proved.

Now we prove that
\tilde{\phi}
is also a map of modules. First, note that

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}D(n,2n\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})^{*},

and the multiplication is dual to the inclusion as a cyclic component:

D(n_{1}+n_{2},(n_{1}+n_{2})2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\hookrightarrow D(n_{1},2n_{1}\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee})\otimes D(n_{2},2n_{2}\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}).

\mathcal{B}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}
, so
\bigoplus_{n>0}\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n})
is a regular
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
-module.

Now, let us note that
\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}
is a cyclic
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-module, and due to (
3.7
),
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}
is a tensor product of cyclic
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules (for all
n_{1},n_{2}>0
). Note that the natural cyclic vector of
\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}
(defined up to scalar) is a linear functional on
\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})
, which sends a section to its value on the fiber
\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}}|_{t^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}}
(
t^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
is a point of
\overline{{\mathrm{Gr}}}^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
). In the same way, the tensor product of natural cyclic vectors of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}
is the functional (again defined up to scalar), sending a section to its value at the same point
t^{2\omega_{i}^{\!\scriptscriptstyle\vee}}
. It follows that the dual
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{*}\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}
to the action map
\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})
maps the tensor product of cyclic vectors to the tensor product of cyclic vectors.

In view of the above, the diagram

(3.8)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\phi}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}\otimes\tilde{\phi}_{n_{2}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}}

of
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-modules is commutative for any
n_{1},n_{2}>0
, since
\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{*}
is
\mathfrak{g}[t]
-cyclic and both
m_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}\circ\tilde{\phi}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}^{*}
and
{\mathop{\rm id}}\otimes\tilde{\phi}_{n_{2}}^{*}\circ m_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}
map the cyclic vector of
\Gamma(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}+n_{2}})^{*}
to the tensor product of cyclic vectors in
\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n_{1}})^{*}\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{n_{2}})^{*}
(note again that by their construction in
[
CV
]
,
\tilde{\phi}^{*}_{n}
map cyclic vector to cyclic).

Dualizing all the modules (and morphisms) in (
3.8
), we obtain that
\tilde{\phi}
is indeed a morphism of
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Gamma(\mathcal{L}^{n})
-modules.
∎

We hope that a similar proof should exist in non-simply laced cases.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that in
[
Ked
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
DK2
, Lemma 2.1]
, the classes of Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules are multiplied by roots of 1, so that a sign changes in the Q-systems relation, and after this change it becomes a cluster relation. In our case, this change of sign naturally appears in the proof from the cohomological shift (
3.5
).

Moreover, it is stated in
[
Ked
]
that the variables
Q_{i,\ell}
have no meaning for
\ell<0
from the point of view of
U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
representation theory, but they are needed in order to get a cluster structure. In our case, the sheaves
\mathcal{P}_{i,\ell}
are well-defined and natural equally for any integer
\ell
.

Both of these observations demonstrate that perhaps the category
\mathcal{P}^{G^{sc}(\mathcal{O})}_{coh}({\mathrm{Gr}})
is more natural for categorical realization of cluster algebra of
[
Ked
,
DK2
]
than some category related to
\mathrm{Rep}U_{q}(\mathbb{L}\mathfrak{g})
, though these realizations are related, as our proof shows.

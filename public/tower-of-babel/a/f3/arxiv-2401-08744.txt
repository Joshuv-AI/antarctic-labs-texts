Gapless Fermionic Systems as Phase-space Topological Insulators: Non-perturbative Results from Anomalies
Taylor L. Hughes, Yuxuan Wang

Abstract
We present a theory unifying the topological responses and anomalies of various gapless fermion systems exhibiting Fermi surfaces, including those with Berry phases, and nodal structures, which applies beyond non-interacting limit. As our key finding, we obtain a general approach to directly relate gapless fermions and topological insulators in phase space, including first- and higher-order insulators. Using this relation we show that the low-energy properties and response theories for gapless fermionic systems can be directly obtained without resorting to microscopic details. Our results provide a unified framework for describing such systems using well-developed theories from the study of topological phases of matter.



Introduction.—

One of the most remarkable properties of topological insulators (TI) is their description in terms of quantized, detail-independent quantum anomalies
Qi et al. 2008
;
Witten 2016
. In contrast, spectral, thermodynamical, and transport properties of gapless systems such as Fermi liquids
Shankar 1994
;
Delacrétaz et al. 2022
typically depend on the details of the dispersion and interactions. However, there are properties of interacting, gapless fermionic systems (GFSs) that are independent of microscopic details. Perhaps the most well-known example is Luttinger’s theorem for a Fermi liquid
Luttinger 1960
;
Luttinger and Ward 1960
;
Oshikawa 2000
;
Else et al. 2021
, which is a non-perturbative result that relates the total electric charge with the volume enclosed by the Fermi surface.

In recent years, it has been increasingly appreciated that Luttinger’s theorem itself has a topological origin
Oshikawa 2000
— most directly, it naturally follows from a topological Chern-Simons term in
phase space
(combining real and momentum space)
Else et al. 2021
;
Lu et al. 2023
. Indeed, phase-space topology has been utilized to classify gapped
Qi et al. 2008
and gapless
Bulmash et al. 2015
;
Lu et al. 2023
fermionic systems having various internal symmetries. Additionally, the topological nature of Fermi surfaces even places important non-perturbative constraints on transport.
Shi et al. 2022
The power of phase space topology relies on an analogy between TIs, which harbor localized gapless excitations in position space, and GFSs which harbor gapless modes localized on submanifolds of momentum space, e.g., Fermi surfaces or nodal lines/points.

While seminal work establishing this analogy was carried out in Refs.
Bulmash et al. 2015
;
Else et al. 2021
;
Lu et al. 2023
, in this work we obtain a general recipe for mapping GFSs to TIs in phase space. As a direct consequence of the phase-space approach, we obtain the low-energy properties, of both the bulk and the boundary, without relying on microscopic details. Importantly, such an approach yields a unified framework to understand a wide variety of GFSs including Fermi surfaces enclosing Berry phases and topological semimetals. From the quantum anomalies of the phase space TI, we unify various response theories for these GFSs and generate results that act as new types of Luttinger theorems that remain valid in the presence of interactions. Our work also sheds light on the classification of a variety of GFSs.
Hořava 2005
;
Lu et al. 2023

S_{4}
-symmetric WSM.—

Recent works have pointed out that more complicated Weyl-point configurations can have quasi-topological responses to electromagnetic and crystalline gauge fields

The phase-space coordinates of the HOTI are

From Ref.

\Theta=\pi
,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{e}{2}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{S}_{i}}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}}d\left(k_{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}^{\alpha}\right)\wedge d\left(k_{\beta}\mathfrak{e}^{\beta}\right)\wedge A

The surface states for this system can also be directly obtained from phase-space topology. The top surface at

is parameterized by
(w,v,x,y)

\displaystyle H_{\rm surf}=

\displaystyle-i\int_{w,v,x,y}\psi^{\dagger}\left[(\partial_{x}-iw)\Gamma_{1}+(\partial_{y}-iv)\Gamma_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\partial_{w}\Gamma_{3}+\partial_{v}\Gamma_{4}+i\mu(v^{2}-w^{2})\Gamma_{5}\right]\psi,

Outlook.—
In this work we presented a unified framework of treating GFS’s as phase-space TIs, leading naturally to non-perturbative results such as generalized Luttinger relations, topological response theories, and surface state properties. As we mentioned, one immediate future direction is the classification of interacting GFS’s from that of PSTIs. It will also be interesting to extend this framework to GFSs with topological orders.

\mathcal{V}_{a}
within which the dispersion around the Weyl points is approximately linear, the Berry curvature is an odd function of the momentum deviation from the Weyl point, and thus

\int_{(w,v,u)\in\mathcal{V}_{a}}(d\mathfrak{a}_{m})\wedge dk_{i}=0,~~~\Delta\mathcal{P}_{i}=0.

(S35)

In other words, to leading order the size of Fermi pockets does not contribute to a correction to the coefficient
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{0,i}
.

Combining all terms, we obtain Eq. (9) of the main text:

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\int_{x,y,z,t}\left[\frac{e\mathcal{V}}{8\pi^{3}}A\wedge\mathfrak{e}^{x}\wedge\mathfrak{e}^{y}\wedge\mathfrak{e}^{z}-\frac{e^{2}\mathcal{P}_{i}}{8\pi^{2}}\mathfrak{e}^{i}\wedge A\wedge dA\right]

(S36)

where
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\rm BZ}-\sum_{a=1,2}\mathcal{V}_{1,2}
is the volume enclosed by the FS’s. As we mentioned in the main text, the first term in (
S36
) leads to the Luttinger theorem, while the second term is responsible for the anomalous Hall effect.

Admissible weak factorization systems on extriangulated categories
Yajun Ma, Hanyang You, Dongdong Zhang

Abstract




Abstract

Extriangulated categories, introduced by Nakaoka and Palu, serve as a simultaneous generalization of exact and triangulated categories. In this paper, we first introduce the concept of admissible weak factorization systems and establish a bijection between cotorsion pairs and admissible weak factorization systems on extriangulated categories. Consequently, we give the equivalences between hereditary cotorsion pairs and compatible cotorsion pairs via admissible weak factorization systems under certain conditions in extriangulated categories, thereby generalizing a result by Di, Li, and Liang.

Keywords:
extriangulated category; admissible weak factorization system; cotorsion pair

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
18G80; 18E10; 18A32

1.
Introduction

The notion of extriangulated categories, defined by
\mathbb{E}
-triangles satisfying specific axioms, was introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in
[
14
]
as a simultaneous generalization of exact and triangulated categories. More precisely, triangulated categories and extension-closed subcategories of triangulated categories are examples of extriangulated categories. Additionally, there exist other examples of extriangulated categories that are neither exact nor triangulated (see
[
14
,
19
,
9
]
).

A model structure on a category
\mathcal{C}
is a triple of three classes of morphisms respectively called
cofibrations
,
fibrations
, and
weak equivalences
, satisfying Retract axiom, Lifting axiom, Factorization axiom and Two out of three axiom. For more details, see
[
8
,
16
]
. Given an additive category
\mathcal{C}
with a model structure, Quillen’s homotopy category (i.e., the localization of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to the weak equivalences) is a pretriangulated category in the sense of
[
1
]
. However, it is not a triangulated category in general.

A triple
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{V})
of classes of objects in an exact category
\mathcal{C}
is called a
Hovey triple
if both
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W}\bigcap\mathcal{V})
and
(\mathcal{U}\bigcap\mathcal{W},\mathcal{V})
are complete cotorsion pairs, and
\mathcal{W}
is
thick
(i.e.,
\mathcal{W}
is closed under direct summands and if two out of three terms in an admissible exact sequence are in
\mathcal{W}
, then so is the third one).

Nakaoka and Palu established a bijective correspondence between admissible model structures and Hovey triples
[
14
]
, unifying Hovey’s work on abelian categories
[
7
]
, Gillespie’s work on weakly idempotent complete exact categories
[
5
]
, and Yang’s work on triangulated categories
[
17
]
. These correspondences offer a method for constructing model structures from Hovey triples, which are simpler to work with. For example, Gillespie provided a convenient way to construct Hovey triples using compatible and complete hereditary cotorsion pairs (see
[
6
]
).

Theorem (Gillespie)

[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
Let
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
be compatible and complete hereditary cotorsion pairs in an abelian category
\mathcal{C}
. Then there is a subcategory
\mathcal{W}
such that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{V})
forms a Hovey triple.

Zhou generalized the above theorem to extriangulated categories, see
[
18
]
.

Theorem (Zhou)

[
18
, Theorem 3.10]
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an extriangulated category satisfying Condition (WIC), and
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
be two hereditary cotorsion pairs satisfying
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
. Then there is a subcategory
\mathcal{W}
such that
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{V})
forms a Hovey triple.

Recently, Di, Li, and Liang generalized the aforementioned theorem to more general categories by introducing weak factorization systems as counterparts to complete cotorsion pairs (see
[
4
]
). Specifically, for two compatible weak factorization systems
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
satisfying certain properties, they provided a method to construct model structures on general categories with pushouts along morphisms in
\mathcal{U}
and pullbacks along morphisms in
\mathcal{V}
(see
[
4
, Theorem C]
). They also showed that, in the case where
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian category, a pair
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of classes of objects in
\mathcal{C}
forms a complete and hereditary cotorsion pair if and only if the pair
(\mathrm{Mon(}\mathcal{U}{)},\mathrm{Epi(}\mathcal{V}{)})
is a weak factorization system under additional assumptions (see
[
4
, Theorem A]
), where

\mathrm{Mon}(\mathcal{U})=\{f\>\mid f~\text{is a monomorphism with~}\mathrm{Coker}(f)\in\mathcal{U}\}

and

\mathrm{Epi}(\mathcal{V})=\{f\>\mid f~\text{is an epimorphism with~}\mathrm{Ker}(f)\in\mathcal{V}\}.

Moreover, two cotorsion pairs are compatible if and only if the corresponding weak factorization systems are compatible, see
[
4
, Theorem B]
.

The results above establish a connection between weak factorization systems and complete cotorsion pairs. The aim of this paper is to generalize these results from abelian categories to weakly idempotent complete extriangulated categories.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition of an extriangulated category and outline some basic properties that will be used later. In Section 3, we introduce the concept of admissible weak factorization systems and establish a bijection between cotorsion pairs and admissible weak factorization systems (see Theorem
3.11
). As a consequence, we provide equivalences of hereditary cotorsion pairs via admissible weak factorization systems under certain conditions (see Theorem
3.15
). Finally, we give the equivalences of compatible cotorsion pairs through compatible admissible weak factorization systems (see Theorem
3.22
).

.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a co-t-structure;

(2)

(\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U},\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V})
is an admissible weak factorization system such that
\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U}
satisfies left cancellation property;

(3)

(\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U},\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V})
is an admissible weak factorization system such that
\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V}
satisfies right cancellation property.

Definition 3.19
.

[
4
, Definition 1.11]

Two weak factorization systems
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})
and

(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{R})
in
\mathcal{C}
are called
compatible
if the following conditions hold:

(\mathrm{CP1})

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
(or equivalent,
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
);

(\mathrm{CP2})
given composable morphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\mathcal{R}
, if two of three morphisms
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\beta\alpha
are in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
, then so is the third one;

(\mathrm{CP3})
given
l\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
and
r\in\mathcal{R}
,
if
rl\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
, then
r\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})
and

(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{R})
be two compatible weak factorization systems in
\mathcal{C}
.
We define

\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}=\{\alpha~|~\alpha~\text{can be decomposed as }\alpha=\widetilde{r}\widetilde{l}~\text{with}~\widetilde{l}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}~\text{and}~\widetilde{r}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}\}.

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})
and

(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{R})
be two compatible admissible weak factorization systems in extriangulated category
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
. If the composition
g=fh
is in
\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
with

f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
, then
h\in\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{L},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}})
is an admissible weak factorization system, we have
h=h_{2}h_{1}
with

and

g=fh_{2}h_{1}
with
fh_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
by Remark
3.3
.

h_{1}
, that is,
h_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
, then
h_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\bigcap\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
. It follows from
[
4
, Lemma 1.12]
that
h_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
, so
h=h_{2}h_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
. So we only deal with the special case that
h\in\mathcal{L}
.
Let

and

.

, by definition, there is a morphism
\widetilde{l}:X\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
and a morphism

such that the following diagram commutes:

Definition 3.21
.

Two cotorsion pairs
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
are
compatible
if
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
(or equivalently
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\mathcal{V})
and
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category, and let
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
be two pairs of classes of objects in
\mathcal{C}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
are compatible cotorsion pairs on
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

(\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U},\mathrm{Defl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V})
are compatible admissible weak factorization systems.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
By Theorem
3.11
,
(\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U},\mathrm{Defl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V})
are two admissible weak factorization systems.
Now we show that they are compatible. By
[
18
, Theorem 3.9]

there is a thick class
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}\bigcap\mathcal{W}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{V}\bigcap\mathcal{W}
.
The (CP1) holds clearly.
Suppose that
f:X\rightarrow Y
and
g:Y\rightarrow Z
are in
\mathrm{Defl}~(\mathcal{V}).

Then
gf
is a deflation and then there exists a conflation

\mathrm{CoCone}(f)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoCone}(gf)\rightarrow\mathrm{CoCone}(g)

in
\mathcal{C}
by
[
10
, Lemma 2.11]
.
Thus the condition (CP2) holds as
\mathcal{W}
is a thick class.

To prove (CP3), let
h:A\rightarrow B
be in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
and
f:B\rightarrow C
be in
\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V}
such that
g=fh
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}.

Then
h
and
g
are inflations with
\mathrm{Cone}(h)
and
\mathrm{Cone}(g)
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
.
Thus by
[
10
, Lemma 2.12]
, there is a conflation

\mathrm{CoCone}(f)\rightarrow\mathrm{Cone}(h)\rightarrow\mathrm{Cone}(g).

Since
\mathcal{W}
is a thick class, it follows that
\mathrm{CoCone}(f)
belongs to
\mathcal{W}
.
Thus
\mathrm{CoCone}(f)
is in
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
By Theorem
3.11
,
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
are two cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}.

If
X\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
, then
0\rightarrow X
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}

and hence in
\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U}
, so
X\in\mathcal{U}
which yields that
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}.

Next we claim that
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}.

Take
M\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
.
Then
0\rightarrow M
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
and
M\rightarrow 0
is in
\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V}.
Since
(\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U},\mathrm{Defl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
and
(\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathrm{Defl}~\mathcal{V})
are compatible weak factorization systems, it follows from the Condition (CP3) that
M\rightarrow 0
is in
\mathrm{Defl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
as
0\rightarrow 0
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}.

Hence
M\in\widetilde{\mathcal{V}},
so
M\in\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}.

We put
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=\mathrm{Defl}~~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
.
To prove
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V},

let
N
be an object in
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}.

Then
0\rightarrow N
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U}
and
N\rightarrow 0
is in
\mathrm{Defl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
.
Since
0\rightarrow 0
is in
\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}
, it follows from Lemma
3.20
that
0\rightarrow N
is in
\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}}\bigcap\mathrm{Infl}~\mathcal{U}
. Thus, by
[
4
, Lemma 1.12]
,
0\rightarrow N
is in
\mathrm{Infl}~\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}
.
So
N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}.

On the other hand,
N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}^{\perp}=\mathcal{V}.

Therefore
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}.

We have proven that
\mathcal{U}\bigcap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{U}}\bigcap\mathcal{V}
, so
(\widetilde{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{V})
and
(\mathcal{U},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})
are compatible cotorsion pairs.
∎

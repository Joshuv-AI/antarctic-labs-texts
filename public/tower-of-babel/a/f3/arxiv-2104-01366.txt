Two mixed finite element formulations for the weak imposition of the Neumann boundary conditions for the Darcy flow Thanks: Erik Burman was partially supported by the EPSRC grants EP/P01576X/1 and EP/T033126/1. Riccardo Puppi was partially supported by ERC AdG project CHANGE n. 694515.
Erik Burman, Riccardo Puppi

Abstract
. We propose two different discrete formulations for the weak imposition of the Neumann boundary conditions of the Darcy flow. The Raviart-Thomas mixed finite element on both triangular and quadrilateral meshes is considered for both methods. One is a consistent discretization depending on a weighting parameter scaling as 𝒪 ⁡ ( h − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(h^{-1}) , while the other is a penalty-type formulation obtained as the discretization of a perturbation of the original problem and relies on a parameter scaling as 𝒪 ⁡ ( h − k − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(h^{-k-1}) , k k being the order of the Raviart-Thomas space. We rigorously prove that both methods are stable and result in optimal convergent numerical schemes with respect to appropriate mesh-dependent norms, although the chosen norms do not scale as the usual L 2 L^{2} -norm. However, we are still able to recover the optimal a priori L 2 L^{2} -error estimates for the velocity field, respectively, for high-order and the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas discretizations, for the first and second numerical schemes. Finally, some numerical examples validating the theory are exhibited.

Introduction

We consider the finite element approximation for the weak imposition of the Neumann boundary conditions for the Poisson problem in its mixed formulation, also known as Darcy’s law in the context of fluid dynamics.

Let us point out that the situation is dual with respect to the standard formulation of the Poisson problem: here the Neumann boundary conditions are essential and to the best of our knowledge it is not clear in the literature how to proceed in order to enforce them by manipulating the weak formulation rather than the functional spaces.

As far as the primary formulation is concerned, a wide variety of techniques have already been proposed and are now well-understood, the most prominent of which are undoubtedly the penalty method introduced in
[
4
]
, the Lagrange multipliers approach of
[
3
]
and, of course, the Nitsche method developed in
[
15
]
and later promoted in
[
17
]
, where the author relates it to the stabilized Lagrange multiplier method of
[
5
]
.

In this work two different discrete formulations of the Darcy problem for the weak imposition of the Neumann boundary conditions are provided.
Both of them are based on the Raviart-Thomas finite element discretization for triangular and quadrilateral meshes. Let us notice that the two schemes do not add any additional degrees of freedoms and moreover, for simplicity, all dimensionless parameters have been set to
1
.

The first formulation is a consistent discretization of the Darcy system, hence it falls into the class of Nitsche-type methods. A weighting parameter scaling as
\mathcal{O}(h^{-1})
needs to be introduced. Let us observe that this formulation had already appeared in the literature in
[
9
]
for the lowest-order Raviart-Thomas element, in the context of incompressible flows in fractured media.

The latter, which is inspired by
[
13
]
and based on a perturbed variational principle, belongs instead to the family of penalty methods. In this case the penalization parameter scales as
\mathcal{O}(h^{-k-1})
,
k
being the order of the Raviart-Thomas discretization, entailing a much more severe ill-conditioning of the resulting stiffness matrix.

We are able to prove that both formulations are stable and give rise to optimal convergent schemes with respect to suitable mesh-dependent norms, which do not scale as the
L^{2}
-norm as it is customary for the Darcy problem. At this point we are able to demonstrate super convergence results that allow us to find an optimal a priori estimate of the velocity error with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm, respectively, for any higher order Raviart-Thomas discretization combined with the first method and for the lowest order element with the second formulation.

Note that this work should be considered as a preliminary step towards the much more involved situation of an underlying mesh which is not fitted with the boundary of the physical domain
[
7
]
.

Let us briefly sketch the outline of the paper. In the next two sections we introduce, respectively, the strong formulation of the Darcy problem and a singularly perturbed formulation of it, parametrized by
\eps\geq 0^{+}
. For the latter we are able to show that its solution is stable independently of
\eps
and that, for
\eps\to 0^{+}
, it converges to the solution of the original problem under some extra regularity assumptions on the data and on the boundary. In the third section the Raviart-Thomas finite element is introduced together with our two discrete formulations, both depending on a mesh-dependent weighting parameter
\gamma
. As already mentioned, for the first one,
\gamma=h^{-1}
, while for the other
\gamma=h^{-\left(k+1\right)}
. In section 4 we prove the desired stability estimates, with respect to different mesh-dependent norms, guaranteeing the well-posedness of the associated problems. Then, in section 5, optimal a priori error estimates, in terms of the chosen norms, are demonstrated for the velocity and pressure fields. We demonstrate some super convergent results that enable us, for the two methods, to recover optimality for the
L^{2}
-error of the velocity field as well. Finally, some two-dimensional numerical examples are provided in order to corroborate the theory.

1.
The Darcy problem and its variational formulation

We introduce some useful notations for the forthcoming analysis. Let
D
be a Lipschitz-regular domain (subset, open, bounded, connected) of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
. Standard Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(D)
for any
s\in\R
and
H^{t}(\gamma)
for
t\in\left[-1,1\right]
are defined on the domain
D
and on a non-empty open subset of its boundary
\gamma\subset\partial D
, see
[
1
]
, with the convention
H^{0}(D):=L^{2}(D)
,
H^{0}(\gamma):=L^{2}(\gamma)
. Moreover, we introduce the following usual notations:

\displaystyle L^{2}_{0}(D):=

\displaystyle L^{2}(D)/\R,

\displaystyle H_{0,\gamma}(D):=

\displaystyle\text{ closure of }C_{0,\gamma}^{\infty}(D)\text{ with respect to }\norm{\cdot}_{H^{s}(D)}=\{v\in H(D):\restr{v}{\gamma}=0\},

\displaystyle H_{f,\gamma}(D):=

\displaystyle\{v\in H(D):\restr{v}{\gamma}=f\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{s}(D):=

\displaystyle\left(H^{s}(D)\right)^{d},\quad\bm{H}_{0,\gamma}^{s}(D):=\left(H_{0,\gamma}^{s}(D)\right)^{d},\quad\bm{H}_{f,\gamma}^{s}(D):=\left(H_{f,\gamma}^{s}(D)\right)^{d},\quad\bm{H}^{s}(\gamma):=\left(H^{t}(\gamma)\right)^{d},

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\dive;D):=

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}^{0}(D):\dive\mathbf{v}\in H^{0}(D)\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{0,\gamma}(\dive;D):=

\displaystyle\text{ closure of }\left(C_{0,\gamma}^{\infty}(D)\right)^{d}\text{ with respect to }\norm{\cdot}_{H(\dive;D)}=\{\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}(\dive;D):\restr{\mathbf{v}\cdot\n}{\gamma}=0\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\sigma,\gamma}(\dive;D):=

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}(\dive;D):\restr{\mathbf{v}\cdot\n}{\gamma}=\sigma\},

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{0}(\dive;D):=

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}(\dive;D):\dive\mathbf{v}=0\},

where the divergence operator and the traces on
\gamma
are defined in the sense of distributions, see
[
14
]
. For the sake of convenience we are going to employ the same notation
\abs{\cdot}
for the volume (Lebesgue) and surface (Hausdorff) measures of
\R^{d}
.

We also denote as
\mathbb{Q}_{r,s,t}
the vector space of polynomials of degree at most
r
in the first variable, at most
s
in the second and at most
t
in the third one (analogously for th case
d=2
),
\mathbb{P}_{u}
the vector space of polynomials of degree at most
u
. For the sake of simplicity of the notation we may write
\mathbb{Q}_{k}
instead of
\mathbb{Q}_{k,k}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{k,k,k}
.

Note that throughout this document
C
will denote generic constants that may change at each occurrence, but that are always independent of the local mesh size.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz-regular domain of
\R^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
. We assume its boundary
\Gamma
to be partitioned into
\Gamma=\Gamma_{N}\cup\Gamma_{D}
with
\Gamma_{N}\cap\Gamma_{D}=\emptyset
.
Let us consider the following problem, often associated to a linearized model for the flow of groundwater through our domain
\Omega
, here representing a saturated porus medium with permeability
\kappa
.
Given
\f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\R^{d})
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
u_{N}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{N})
,
p_{D}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})
, we look for
\left(\u{,}p\right)\in H_{u_{N},\Gamma_{N}}(\dive;\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{cases}\kappa^{-1}\u{-}\nabla p=\f\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\dive\u{=}g\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\u{\cdot}\n=u_{N}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N},\\
p=p_{D}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{D}.\end{cases}

(1)

The unknowns and
p
represent, respectively, the seepage velocity and the pressure of the fluid. The first equation of (
1
) is called
Darcy law
relating the velocity and the pressure gradient of the fluid, the second one expresses
mass conservation
, the third and the fourth equations are, respectively, a
Neumann boundary condition
for the velocity field and a
Dirichlet boundary condition
for the pressure. Moreover,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric positive definite with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
such that
0<\lambda_{\min}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{\max}<+\infty
, for every
i=1,\dots,d
.

Remark 1.1
.

Contrary to the case of the Poisson problem, here Dirichlet boundary conditions for the pressure are
natural
, in the sense that they can be implicitly enforced in the weak formulation of the problem, while Neumann boundary conditions for the velocity are
essential
, i.e., they are imposed on the functional space. Moreover, let us observe that in the case of purely Neumann boundary conditions, in order to have well-posedness, we have to “filter out” the constant pressures, i.e., the trial and test functions for the pressures are required to lie in
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, and to impose a compatibility condition on the data:
\int_{\Gamma}u_{N}=\int_{\Omega}g
.

2.
A perturbed formulation

Find
\left(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}\right)\in H(\dive;\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{cases}\kappa^{-1}\u{^}\eps-\nabla p^{\eps}=\f\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\dive\u{^}\eps=g\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\eps^{-1}\u{^}\eps\cdot\n=\eps^{-1}u_{N}-p^{\eps}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N},\\
p^{\eps}=p_{D}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{D}.\end{cases}

(2)

Note that as
\eps\to 0^{+}
problem (
2
) formally degenerates to (
1
). In this sense (
2
) is a perturbation of problem (
1
).

In the subsequent analysis we are going to consider, for the sake of simplicity,
\kappa=I
the identity matrix.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}),(\u{,}p)
be respectively the solutions to (
2
) and (
1
), then there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\norm{\u{-}\u{^}\eps}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\eps\left(\norm{\dive\f}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{u_{N}}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{N})}+\norm{p_{D}}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})}\right),

provided that
\f\in\bm{H}(\dive;\Omega)
.

Proof 2.2
.

Let us observe that if
\left(\u{,}p\right)
and
\left(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}\right)
solve, respectively, the problems (
1
) and (
2
), then
p
and
p^{\eps}
are the solutions of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta p=-g+\dive\f\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}=u_{N}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N},\\
p=p_{D}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{D},\end{cases}

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta p^{\eps}=-g+\dive\f\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}+\eps p^{\eps}=u_{N}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N},\\
p^{\eps}=p_{D}\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{D}.\end{cases}

(4)

Let
\delta:=p-p^{\eps}
, with
p
and
p^{\eps}
respectively the solutions of (
3
) and (
4
), then
\delta
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\delta=0\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\delta+\eps^{-1}\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial n}=p\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N},\\
\delta=0\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{D}.\end{cases}

(5)

We rewrite (
5
) in variational form. Find
\delta\in H^{1}_{0,\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\left(\nabla\delta,\nabla\varphi\right)_{\Omega}+\eps\langle\delta,\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}=\eps\langle p,\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\qquad\forall\ \varphi\in H^{1}_{0,\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega).

(6)

From standard theory, (
6
) is well-posed and, in particular, the bilinear form inducing its left hand side is coercive, meaning that

\displaystyle\norm{\delta}^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\norm{\nabla\delta}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\eps\norm{\delta}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}.

(7)

By combining (
6
), (
7
), the Cauchy-Schwarz and a standard trace inequality, we get

\displaystyle\norm{\delta}^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eps\norm{p}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\norm{\delta}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(8)

On the other hand,
p
solves (
3
), hence

\displaystyle\norm{\delta}^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eps\left(\norm{\dive\f}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{u_{N}}_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{N})}+\norm{p_{D}}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})}\right)\norm{\delta}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Since
\u{-}\u{^}\eps=\nabla p+\f-\left(\nabla p^{\eps}+\f\right)=\nabla\left(p-p^{\eps}\right)=\nabla\delta
, then we are done.

In order to avoid technicalities, let us assume
\Omega
to be a convex domain with a
C^{2}
boundary and the Neumann data to be homogeneous.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\left(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}\right)
be the solution of (
2
) and suppose that
\Omega
is convex,
\Gamma
is
C^{2}
and
u_{N}=0
. Then there exists
C>0
, independent of
\eps
, such that

\displaystyle\norm{\u{^}\eps}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\norm{\f}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

(9)

provide that
\f\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Proof 2.4
.

As before, let us consider an equivalent formulation for (
2
) and, without loss of generality, put ourselves in the pure Neumann case
\Gamma=\Gamma_{N}
. We observe that
\left(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}\right)
is the solution to (
2
) if and only if
p^{\eps}
solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta p^{\eps}=-g+\dive\f\qquad&\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}+\eps p^{\eps}=0\qquad&\text{on}\;\Gamma.\end{cases}

(10)

Let us recall that given
\w:\Omega\to\R^{d}
and
\varphi:\Omega\to\R
, the following decompositions hold in an open neighborhood of the boundary

\displaystyle\w=\w_{T}+w_{n}\n,\qquad\nabla\varphi=\nabla_{T}\varphi+\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}\n,

\w_{T}
and
w_{n}
being, respectively, the tangent and normal components of
\w
. The operator
\nabla_{T}:H^{1}(\Gamma)\to\bm{L}^{2}_{T}(\Gamma)
is the tangential gradient and can be defined as in
[
14
]
.
Theorem 3.1.1.1 in
[
11
]
states that if
\Omega
is open, bounded with
\Gamma
of class
C^{2}
, then every
\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle\norm{\dive\mathbf{v}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(D_{j}v_{i},D_{i}v_{j}\right)_{\Omega}=-2\langle\mathbf{v}_{T},\nabla_{T}v_{n}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\int_{\Gamma}\{(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{B})v_{n}^{2}+\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{v}_{T},\mathbf{v}_{T})\},

(11)

\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
being the second fundamental quadratic form associated to
\Gamma
and
\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{B}
its trace, see
[
11
]
for the definitions. Let us apply (
11
) to
\mathbf{v}=\nabla p^{\eps}
. We have

\displaystyle\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\abs{D^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}p^{\eps}}^{2}\geq-2\langle\nabla_{T}p^{\eps},\nabla_{T}\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}\rangle_{\Gamma},

(12)

since

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma}\mathcal{B}(\nabla_{T}p^{\eps},\nabla_{T}p^{\eps})\geq 0,\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal{B})\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}^{2}\geq 0,

having used the non-positiveness of
\mathcal{B}
due to the convexity of
\Omega
(see
[
11
]
). Moreover, the boundary conditions of (
2
) imply

\displaystyle-2\langle\nabla_{T}p^{\eps},\nabla_{T}\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}\rangle_{\Gamma}=2\eps\norm{\nabla_{T}p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

hence (
12
) in turn implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\abs{D^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}p^{\eps}}^{2}\leq\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-2\eps\norm{\nabla_{T}p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

and, in particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\abs{D^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}p^{\eps}}^{2}\leq\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let us bound the other terms which are part of
\norm{p^{\eps}}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
. Green formula states

\displaystyle-\left(\Delta p^{\eps},p^{\eps}\right)_{\Omega}=\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}p^{\eps}\rangle_{\Gamma},

so that, using the boundary conditions of (
2
),

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=

\displaystyle-\left(\Delta p^{\eps},p^{\eps}\right)_{\Omega}+\langle\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n},p^{\eps}\rangle_{\Gamma}=-\left(\Delta p^{\eps},p^{\eps}\right)_{\Omega}-\langle\eps p^{\eps},p^{\eps}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\norm{-\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\norm{p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\eps\norm{p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

implying

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\norm{p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(13)

Because of the compatibility condition on the data when
\Gamma=\Gamma_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}p^{\eps}=\eps^{-1}\left(\int_{\Gamma}u_{N}-\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial p^{\eps}}{\partial n}\right)=\eps^{-1}\left(\int_{\Gamma}u_{N}-\int_{\Omega}\dive\u{^}\eps\right)=\eps^{-1}\left(\int_{\Gamma}u_{N}-\int_{\Omega}g\right)=0,

hence, using the Friedrichs inequality,

\displaystyle\norm{p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{\Gamma}p^{\eps}\right)=C\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(14)

Combining (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

so that

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\norm{p^{\eps}}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\norm{p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\abs{D^{2}_{x_{i},x_{j}}p^{\eps}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\norm{\Delta p^{\eps}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\norm{\dive\f}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Finally, using the relation
u^{\eps}=\nabla p^{\eps}+\f
, it holds

\displaystyle\norm{\u{^}\eps}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\norm{\nabla p^{\eps}}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\norm{\f}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\norm{p^{\eps}}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{\f}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\norm{\dive\f}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)+\norm{\f}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\norm{\f}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\norm{g}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Remark 2.5
.

Let us point out that the statement of Proposition
2.3
holds true when
\Omega
is convex with a Lipschitz polygonal boundary
\Gamma
. We refer the interested reader to Remark 3.2.4.6 of
[
11
]
.

3.
The finite element discretization

Let
\left(\mathcal{T}_{h}\right)_{h>0}
denote a family of triangular or quadrilateral meshes of
\Omega
. It will be useful to partition the collection of edges (or faces if
d=3
)
\mathcal{F}_{h}
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
into three collections: the internal ones
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
and the ones lying on
\Gamma_{N}
and on
\Gamma_{D}
, grouped respectively in
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})
and
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})
,
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})\cup\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})
. For every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
h>0
, let
h_{K}:=\operatorname{diam}(K)
and
h:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}
. We assume the mesh to be
shape-regular
, i.e., there exists
\sigma>0
, independent of
h
, such that
\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{K}}{\rho_{K}}\leq\sigma
,
\rho_{K}
being the diameter of the largest ball inscribed in
K
. Moreover,
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is supposed to be
quasi-uniform
in the sense that there exists
\tau>0
, independent of
h
, such that
\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}\geq\tau h
.
Let
\varphi:\Omega\to\R
be smooth enough so that for every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
its restriction
\restr{\varphi}{K}
can be extended up to the boundary
\partial K
. Then, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}
and a.e.
x\in f
, we define the
jump
of
\varphi
as

\displaystyle[\varphi]_{f}(x):=\restr{\varphi}{K_{1}}(x)-\restr{\varphi}{K_{2}}(x),

where
f=\partial K_{1}\cap\partial K_{2}
. We may remove the subscript
f
when it is clear from the context to which
facet
(edge if
d=2
, face if
d=3
) we refer to.

In order to discretize problem (
1
), we need to choose a suitable couple of subspaces
V_{h}\subset H\left(\dive;\Omega\right)
and
Q_{h}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. In the following,
\hat{K}
will be our
reference element
, and, according to the type of mesh employed, it will be either the unit
d
-simplex, i.e., the triangle of vertices
(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)
, or the unit
d
-cube
\left[0,1\right]^{d}
.

For the triangular meshes, the
Raviart-Thomas
finite element on
\hat{K}
is

\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\hat{K}):=\left(\mathbb{P}_{k}(\hat{K})\right)^{d}\oplus\bm{x}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(\hat{K}),

while, in the case of quadrilaterals, it reads as follows (see, for instance,
[
2
]
):

\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\hat{K}):=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}_{k+1,{k}}(\hat{K})\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k+1}(\hat{K})\qquad&\text{if}\;d=2,\\
\mathbb{Q}_{k+1,{k},k}(\hat{K})\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k+1,k}(\hat{K})\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k,k+1}(\hat{K})\qquad&\text{if}\;d=3.\end{cases}

We map the reference element to a general
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
via the
affine map

F_{K}:\hat{K}\to K
,
F_{K}(\hat{x}):=B_{K}\hat{x}+b_{K}
, where
B_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is diagonal and invertible, and
b_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
H(\dive;\Omega)
, the natural way to transform functions from
\hat{K}
to
K
is through the
Piola transform
. Namely, given
\hat{\u{:}}\hat{K}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
\u{=}\mathcal{P}_{K}\hat{\u{:}}K\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\u{(}x)=\mathcal{P}_{K}\hat{\u{(}}x):=\abs{\det\left(B_{K}\right)}^{-1}B_{K}\hat{\u{(}}\hat{x}),\qquad\text{where}\qquad\hat{x}=B^{-1}_{K}\left(x-b_{K}\right).

For functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we just compose them with the affine map, namely
\hat{q}:\hat{K}\to\R
is transformed to
q=\hat{q}\circ F_{K}^{-1}:K\to\R
. In this way, we can define the finite-dimensional subspaces:

\displaystyle V_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in H\left(\dive;\Omega\right):\restr{\mathbf{v}_{h}}{K}\in\mathbb{RT}_{k}(K)\quad\forall\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{q_{h}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right):\restr{q_{h}}{K}\circ F_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(K\right)\quad\forall\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},\qquad\text{for triangles,}

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{q_{h}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right):\restr{q_{h}}{K}\circ F_{K}\in\mathbb{Q}_{k}\left(K\right)\quad\forall\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},\qquad\text{for quadrilaterals,}

where
\mathbb{RT}_{k}(K):=\{\mathcal{P}_{K}\hat{\w}_{h}:\hat{\w}_{h}\in\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\hat{K})\}
. Remember that in the pure Neumann case, i.e.,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{N}
, we have to filter out constant discrete pressures by imposing the zero average constraint to the space
Q_{h}
.
Let us construct the interpolation operator onto the discrete velocities
r_{h}:\prod_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})\to V_{h}
, by gluing together the local interpolation operators
r_{K}:\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})\to\mathbb{RT}_{k}(K)
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
K^{\mathrm{o}}
denoting the interior of
K
, and using the natural degrees of freedom of the Raviart-Thomas finite element. For every
\mathbf{v}\in\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})
,
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
r_{K}
is uniquely defined by:

\begin{cases}\langle r_{K}\mathbf{v}\cdot\n_{e},q_{h}\rangle_{f}=\langle\mathbf{v}\cdot\n_{e},q_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad&\forall\ q_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),\\
\left(r_{K}\mathbf{v},\w_{h}\right)_{K}=\left(\mathbf{v},\w_{h}\right)_{K}\qquad&\forall\ \w_{h}\in\Psi_{k}\left(K\right)\qquad\text{if}\;k>0,\\
\end{cases}

where, for triangles,

\Psi_{k}\left(K\right):=\left(\mathbb{P}_{k-1}(K)\right)^{d},\qquad\Psi_{k}(f):=\mathbb{P}_{k}(f),\\

and, for quadrilaterals,

\Psi_{k}\left(K\right):=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}_{k-1,{k}}(K)\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k-1}(K)\qquad&\text{if}\;d=2,\\
\mathbb{Q}_{k-1,{k},k}(K)\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k-1,k}(K)\times\mathbb{Q}_{k,k,k-1}(K)\qquad&\text{if}\;d=3,\end{cases}\qquad\Psi_{k}\left(f\right):=\begin{cases}\mathbb{P}_{k}(f)\qquad&\text{if}\;d=2,\\
\mathbb{Q}_{k}(f)\qquad&\text{if}\;d=3,\end{cases}

for all facets
f
and triangles or quadrilaterals
K
of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. The natural choice in order to interpolate onto
Q_{h}
is to employ an elementwise
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection, i.e.,
\Pi_{h}:L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\to Q_{h}
such that for every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\restr{\Pi_{h}}{K}:=\Pi_{K}
, where for
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\left(\Pi_{K}\xi,q_{h}\right)_{K}

\displaystyle=\left(\xi,q_{h}\right)_{K}\qquad\forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

for every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is worth mentioning that the following numerical analysis remains valid if we employ another H(div)-conforming discretization instead, such as the so-called Brezzi-Douglas-Marini mixed element
[
6
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The following diagram commutes:

\begin{CD}H(\dive;\Omega)\cap\prod_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})@>{\dive}>{}>L^{2}(\Omega)\\
@V{}V{r_{h}}V@V{}V{\Pi_{h}}V\\
V_{h}@>{\dive}>{}>Q_{h}.\end{CD}

(16)

In particular, it holds

\displaystyle\dive V_{h}=Q_{h}.

Proof 3.3
.

For the commutative diagram note that, for every
\mathbf{v}\in H(\dive;\Omega)\cap\prod_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})
, it holds

\displaystyle\left(\Pi_{h}\dive\mathbf{v},\varphi_{h}\right)_{K}=

\displaystyle\left(\dive\mathbf{v},\varphi_{h}\right)_{K}=-\left(\mathbf{v},\nabla\varphi_{h}\right)_{K}+\langle\varphi_{h},\mathbf{v}\cdot\n\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(r_{h}\mathbf{v},\nabla\varphi_{h}\right)_{K}+\langle\varphi_{h},r_{h}\mathbf{v}\cdot\n\rangle_{\partial K}=\left(\dive r_{h}\mathbf{v},\varphi_{h}\right)_{K}\qquad\forall\ \varphi_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\;(\text{resp.}\;\mathbb{Q}_{k}(K)).

A direct calculation readily shows the inclusion
\dive V_{h}\subseteq Q_{h}
. Let us prove the other one. Let
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, then, by the surjectivity of
\dive:\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\ L^{2}(\Omega)

[
6
]
, there exists
\mathbf{v}\in H(\dive;\Omega)\cap\prod_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bm{H}^{s}(K^{\mathrm{o}})\subseteq\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\dive\mathbf{v}=q_{h}
. Let us define
\mathbf{v}_{h}:=r_{h}\mathbf{v}
. Thanks to the commutativity diagram we have
\dive\mathbf{v}_{h}=q_{h}
.

We are now ready to introduce the discrete formulations we want to analyze.

3.0.1.
First formulation

Find
\left(\u{_}h,p_{h}\right)\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{h}(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h})+b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},p_{h})=\left(\f,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+h^{-1}\langle u_{N},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}+\langle p_{D},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\qquad&\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
b_{m}(\u{_}h,q_{h})=\left(g,q_{h}\right)_{\Omega}-m\langle q_{h},u_{N}\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad&\forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h},\end{cases}

(17)

where
m\in\{0,1\}
. Here,

\displaystyle a_{h}(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}):=\left(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+h^{-1}\langle\u{_}h\cdot\n,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},

(18)

\displaystyle b_{m}(\u{_}h,p_{h}):=\left(p_{h},\dive\u{_}h\right)_{\Omega}-m\langle p_{h},\u{_}h\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \u{_}h\in V_{h},p_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(19)

In what follows just the analysis for the symmetric case
m=1
will be presented, however numerical results will be provided for the case
m=0
as well.

3.0.2.
Second formulation

Find
\left(\u{_}h,p_{h}\right)\in V_{h}\times Q_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{\eps}(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h})+b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},p_{h})=\left(\f,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+\langle\eps^{-1}u_{N},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}+\langle p_{D},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\qquad&\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\\
b_{0}(\u{_}h,q_{h})=\left(g,q_{h}\right)_{\Omega}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad&\forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.\end{cases}

(20)

where

\displaystyle a_{\eps}(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}):=\left(\u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+\eps^{-1}\langle\u{_}h\cdot\n,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \u{_}h,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}.

Remark 3.4
.

Let us observe that the non-symmetric version of problem (
17
), i.e., with
m=0
, and formulation (
20
), thanks to Proposition
3.2
allows for a
weakly divergence-free
numerical solution
\u{_}h
, namely
\dive\u{_}h=0
in the sense of
L^{2}
, provided that the right hand side
g
vanishes.

Lemma 3.5
.

Formulations (
17
) and (
20
) are consistent discretizations of (
1
) and (
2
) respectively.

Proof 3.6
.

It is clear that (
17
) is a consistent discetization of (
1
).
Let
\left(\u{^}\eps,p^{\eps}\right)
be the solution to (
2
). Of course, we have

\displaystyle b_{0}(\u{^}\eps,q_{h})=\left(g,q_{h}\right)\qquad\forall\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

By integrating by parts the first equation of (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\u{^}\eps,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},p^{\eps})-\langle p^{\eps},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}=\left(\f,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+\langle p_{D},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}.

(21)

By performing
static condensation
of the multiplier from the boundary conditions, we obtain

\displaystyle p^{\eps}=\eps^{-1}\left(\u{_}N-\u{^}\eps\cdot\n\right)\qquad\text{on}\;\Gamma_{N}.

(22)

Substituting (
22
) back into (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle a_{\eps}(\u{^}\eps,\mathbf{v}_{h})+b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},p^{\eps})=\left(\f,\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}+\langle\eps^{-1}\u{_}N,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}+\langle p_{D},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}.

For the numerical analysis of (
17
), we endow the discrete spaces with the following mesh-dependent norms

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{0,h}

\displaystyle:=\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}h^{-1}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)},

\displaystyle\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{1,h}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\norm{\nabla q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h^{-1}\norm{\left[q_{h}\right]}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}h^{-1}\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)},

while for (
20
) we are going to employ:

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{0,h,\eps}

\displaystyle:=\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}\eps^{-1}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)},

\displaystyle\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{1,h,\eps}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\norm{\nabla q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h^{-1}\norm{\left[q_{h}\right]}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}}h^{-1}\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)},

for every
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
and
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Informally speaking, the idea of both approaches is to unbalance the norms in order to go back to the elliptic case. Note that the natural functional setting for the mixed formulation of the Poisson problem is
H(\dive;\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, but here we consider norms that induce the same topology as that of
\left[L^{2}(\Omega)\right]^{d}\times H^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we observe that in both formulations (
17
) and (
20
) a superpenalty parameter
\gamma
is imposed in the flux variable. Indeed, the natural weight, mimicking the
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}
-scalar product, would be
h\langle\u{_}h\cdot\n,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}
. However such a weight does not lead to an optimally converging scheme. In addition, this is also what destroys the conditioning (see subsection
6.2
).

4.
Stability estimates

In this section we carry on at the same time the proofs of the well-posedness of the two discrete formulations.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exist
M_{a_{h}},M_{a_{\eps}},M_{b_{m}}>0
,
m=0,1
, such that

\displaystyle\abs{a_{h}(\w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}\leq M_{a_{h}}\norm{\w_{h}}_{0,h}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle\abs{a_{\eps}(\w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}\leq M_{a_{\eps}}\norm{\w_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle\abs{b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}\leq M_{b_{1}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\ q_{h}\in Q_{h},

\displaystyle\abs{b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}\leq M_{b_{0}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h,\eps}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

Proof 4.2
.

Let
\w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
,
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
be arbitrary. It holds

\displaystyle\abs{a_{h}(\w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\norm{\w_{h}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\w_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\leq\norm{\w_{h}}_{0,h}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h},

\displaystyle\abs{a_{\eps}(\w_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\norm{\w_{h}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\eps^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\eps^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\w_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\leq\norm{\w_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}.

By integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})=

\displaystyle\left(q_{h},\dive\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}-\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(q_{h},\dive\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}-\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\langle\left[q_{h}\right],\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\abs{b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\norm{\nabla q_{h}}_{L^{2}(K)}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\left[q_{h}\right]}_{L^{2}(f)}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{q_{h}}_{L^{2}(f)}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}.

We recall some standard inverse inequalities, namely,

\displaystyle h^{\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}\lesssim\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(K)}\qquad f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D}),f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i},f\subset\partial K.

(23)

In this way we obtain

\displaystyle\abs{b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}+\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\partial K}=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\langle\left[q_{h}\right],\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}.

We have

\displaystyle\abs{b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\norm{\nabla q_{h}}_{L^{2}(K)}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\left[q_{h}\right]}_{L^{2}(f)}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\norm{q_{h}}_{L^{2}(f)}h^{\frac{1}{2}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}\lesssim\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h,\eps},

having used again (
23
) and
h^{-\frac{1}{2}}<\eps^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
\eps\ll h
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exist
{\alpha_{a_{h}}},\alpha_{a_{\eps}}>0
such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{a_{h}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{0,h}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle a_{\eps}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{a_{\eps}}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{0,h,\eps}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}.

Proof 4.4
.

Let us take
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
arbitrary and compute

\displaystyle a_{h}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h^{-1}\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}=\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}^{2}_{0,h}.

The other coercivity estimate follows anologously. Hence,
\alpha_{a_{h}}=\alpha_{a_{\eps}}=1
.

Proposition 4.5
.

There exist
\beta_{m}>0
,
m\in\{0,1\}
, such that

\displaystyle\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h}}\geq

\displaystyle\beta_{1},

\displaystyle\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h,\eps}\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h,\eps}}\geq

\displaystyle\beta_{0}.

Proof 4.6
.

We start with
m=1
. Let us fix
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
arbitrary. We construct
\mathbf{v}_{h}
by using the dofs of the Raviart-Thomas space.

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=h^{-1}\langle\left[q_{h}\right],\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i},\ \varphi_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),

(24)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N}),\ \varphi_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),

(25)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=h^{-1}\langle q_{h},\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D}),\ \varphi_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),

(26)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{v}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)_{K}=-\left(\nabla q_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)_{K}\qquad

(27)

By using the definition of
\mathbf{v}_{h}
,

\displaystyle b_{1}(\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})=

\displaystyle\left(q_{h},\dive\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{\Omega}-\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(q_{h},\dive\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}-\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}+\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{\partial K}-\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\nabla q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\right)_{K}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}\langle\left[q_{h}\right],\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}\langle q_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\norm{\nabla q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h^{-1}\norm{\left[q_{h}\right]}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)}+\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{D})}h^{-1}\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)}=\norm{q_{h}}^{2}_{1,h}.

Finally, let us show that
\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{0,h}\leq C\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h}
. Note that for every

, since
\restr{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(f)

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}^{2}_{L^{2}(f)}=\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n\rangle_{f}=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}_{L^{2}(f)}=0.

Then, let us show
\norm{\mathbf{v}_{h}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\norm{q_{h}}_{1,h}

\restr{\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n}{f}=h_{K}^{-1}\restr{\pi_{f,k}\left[q_{h}\right]}{f}
for every

and from (

\restr{\pi_{K,k}\mathbf{v}_{h}}{K}=-\restr{\pi_{K,k}\nabla q_{h}}{K}

\Psi_{k}(K)

\pi_{f,k}

,

being a facet of

[

]

\mathbf{v}_{h}
.

Let us now take
m=0
and
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
. We define
\mathbf{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=h^{-1}\langle\left[q_{h}\right],\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i},\ \varphi_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),

(28)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=h^{-1}\langle q_{h},\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\partial}(\Gamma_{N}),\ \varphi_{h}\in\Psi_{k}(f),

(29)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\n,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}=h^{-1}\langle q_{h},\varphi_{h}\rangle_{f}\qquad

6.2.
A remark about the condition numbers

Proceeding as in
[
10
]
it would be possible to prove that the
\ell^{2}
-condition number of the stiffness matrix arising from the discretizations (
17
), for both
m\in\{0,1\}
, scales as
h^{-2}
, as Figures
13
and
15
confirm. The penalty parameter for the weak imposition of the Neumann boundary conditions is the responsible of the deterioration of the conditioning with respect to the standard mixed finite element discretization of the Poisson problem, for which the condition number scales as
h^{-1}
. An even worse situation occurs when formulation (
20
) is employed. In this case the condition number scales as
h^{-\left(s+2\right)}
,
s=\min\{r,k\}
,
r
being the Sobolev regularity of the exact solution for the pressure field and
k
the polynomial degree of the Raviart-Thomas discretization, as confirmed by Figures
14
and
16
.

In all numerical experiments, we do not detect any particular sensitivity of the convergence of the error of the velocities with respect to
\gamma
in the case of method (
17
). On the other hand, it is a different matter altogether as far as the formulation (
20
) is concerned: this time we can realize the influence of
\gamma
on the approximation power of the method.

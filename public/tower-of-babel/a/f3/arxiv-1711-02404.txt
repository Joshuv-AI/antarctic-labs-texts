Υ ⁡ ( nS ) \Upsilon(\mathrm{nS}) polarizations in p \mathrm{p} p \mathrm{p} collisions at s \sqrt{s} = 7 and 8 Te ​ V \mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV} by the LHCb collaboration
Alexander Artamonov on behalf of the LHCb Collaboration

Abstract
A polarization measurement carried out for the Υ ⁡ ( 1 ​ S ) \Upsilon(1\mathrm{S}) , Υ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \Upsilon(2\mathrm{S}) and Υ ⁡ ( 3 ​ S ) \Upsilon(3\mathrm{S}) mesons produced in pp collisions at s = 7 \sqrt{s}=7 and 8 Te ​ V \mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV} is presented. Data samples used for the polarization measurement were collected by the LHCb experiment during the 2011 and 2012 data taking runs with integrated luminosities of 1 and 2 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} , respectively. The measurement has been performed in three polarization frames, using an angular distribution analysis of the Υ → μ + ​ μ − \Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}} decays in the kinematic region of the Υ \Upsilon transverse momentum p T Υ < 30 ​ Ge ​ V / c p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<30~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c} and rapidity 2.2 < y Υ < 4.5 2.2<y^{\Upsilon}<4.5 . No large polarization is observed.



1
Introduction

It is already forty years since the discovery of the first bottomonium
state, the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
meson
[
1
]
,
but studies of heavy quarkonium production continue
to play an important role in the development of quantum chromodynamics
(QCD)
[
2
]
.
According to the current theoretical framework,
nonrelativistic QCD
(NRQCD)
[
3
,
4
]
,
inclusive production of a heavy quarkonium
is viewed as a two-step process.
In the first step, a heavy quark-antiquark pair,
{\mathrm{Q}}{\overline{{\mathrm{Q}}}}
,
is perturbatively created in a color singlet or color octet state,
and then, in the second step,
the hadronization process non-perturbatively
transforms the
{\mathrm{Q}}{\overline{{\mathrm{Q}}}}
pair into an observable colorless bound state.
The non-perturbative transitions
are described by long-distance matrix elements
which are conjectured to be independent of production processes
in the first step,
and need to be extracted experimentally.
The most distinct signature of the first NRQCD
calculations
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]

was the prediction of
transverse polarization for S-wave quarkonium states
(such as the
{\mathrm{J}\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\uppsi\mskip 2.0mu}
,
\uppsi{(2S)}
and
\Upsilon(\mathrm{nS})
mesons)
directly produced (i.e. not coming from decays)
at large transverse momentum
in high-energy hadron collisions.
NRQCD calculations for promptly produced heavy quarkonia
(if charmonia then those not coming from b-hadron decays)
are complicated by
feed-down (electromagnetic or hadronic transitions)
from higher level states.

Full NLO calculations
[
9
]

performed for the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
,
\Upsilon(2\mathrm{S})
and
\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})
mesons (shortly denoted by
\Upsilon
),
including effects of feed-down contributions
for the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
and
\Upsilon(2\mathrm{S})
but not for the
\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})
,
give very small transverse polarizations for the first two
bottomonium states and a large transverse polarization
for the third state.
These calculations were done before
LHCb
obtained that
the fractions of
\Upsilon
mesons originating from

\upchi_{{\mathrm{b}}}
decays are around

30-40\,\%
for high transverse momenta,

p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}\gtrsim 20{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}

[
10
]
.
The
\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})
was considered as almost
feed-down free state before.
Although taking into account feed-down contributions
improves the description of
\Upsilon
polarization,
there are still problems in describing the

{\mathrm{J}\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\uppsi\mskip 2.0mu}
polarization
[
11
]

(even with considering feed-down)
and the
\uppsi{(2S)}
polarization
[
12
]

(which includes negligible feed-down contributions).

The angular distribution of muons from the
\Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
decay
can be written as
[
13
,
14
,
15
]

\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}\Omega}=\frac{3}{4\pi}~\frac{1}{3+\uplambda_{\theta}}\left(1+\uplambda_{\theta}\cos^{2}\theta+\uplambda_{\theta\phi}\sin 2\theta\cos\phi+\uplambda_{\phi}\sin^{2}\theta\cos 2\phi\right),

(1)

where the angular quantities
\Omega=\left(\cos\theta,\phi\right)

describe a direction of
\upmu^{+}
in the
\Upsilon
rest frame
with respect to some specified axes,

\vec{\uplambda}\equiv(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})

are the angular distribution parameters
directly related to the spin-1 density-matrix
elements
[
13
,
16
,
17
]
.
The parameter
\uplambda_{\theta}
is a measure of spin-alignment,
and can be expressed as

\uplambda_{\theta}=\left(\sigma_{T}-2\sigma_{L}\right)/\left(\sigma_{T}+2\sigma_{L}\right)
, where

\sigma_{T}
(
\sigma_{L}
) is the transverse (longitudinal)
component of the cross section.
If the spin-alignment parameter

\uplambda_{\theta}>0
(
\uplambda_{\theta}<0
),
the
\Upsilon
meson is called to be transversely (longitudinally)
polarized in a specified frame, while the case

\uplambda_{\theta}=\uplambda_{\theta\phi}=\uplambda_{\phi}=0

means that the
\Upsilon
meson is unpolarized.
The parameters
\vec{\uplambda}

depend on a definition of coordinate axes specified in
the
\Upsilon
rest frame.
The following three coordinate systems are widely used
in polarization analyses:
helicity (HX)
[
18
]
,
Collins-Soper (CS)
[
19
]
and
Gottfried-Jackson (GJ)
[
19
]
.
The frames are specified by different directions of the
spin-quantization axis,
z
axis, defined in
the production plane of
\Upsilon
meson
[
15
]
.
In all these frames, the
y
axis is normal to
the production plane
[
15
,
21
]
,
and the remaining
x
axis completes a right-handed coordinate system.

Until recently, quarkonium polarization measurements have been
reduced only to studies of the parameter
\uplambda_{\theta}
,
some times in different polarization frames.
As pointed out in
[
15
]
,
measuring all the three polarization
parameters is important from the theoretical and
experimental points of view. Since from having
the
\vec{\uplambda}
in one frame, it is possible to transform
them into another
[
15
,
22
]
,
and perform some cross checks of results.
In particular, an important cross check is provided by a
polarization parameter

\tilde{\uplambda}=\left(\uplambda_{\theta}+3\uplambda_{\phi}\right)/\left(1-\uplambda_{\phi}\right)

[
23
,
24
]
,
which is invariant for all rotations around the
y
axis,
that is invariant in the HX, CS and GJ frames.
The physical meaning of the parameter
\tilde{\uplambda}

was first recognized in
[
25
]

(see also
[
26
,
27
]
).

The first full angular distribution analysis of muons
from the
\Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
decays was performed by
the
CDF
collaboration
[
28
]

using data of
{\mathrm{p}}{\overline{{\mathrm{p}}}}
collisions
at
\sqrt{s}=1.96

\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
.

CDF
found that the angular distributions of muons from
all the three
\Upsilon
states are nearly isotropic
in the central rapidity region
|y^{\Upsilon}|<0.6

and
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<40~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
This result is consistent with
the previous CDF measurement
[
29
]
,
and inconsistent with the measurement performed by
the
D0
collaboration
[
30
]
.

D0
observed the significant
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}
dependent longitudinal
polarization for the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
mesons produced in
{\mathrm{p}}{\overline{{\mathrm{p}}}}

collisions at
\sqrt{s}=1.96

\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
, for

|y^{\Upsilon}|<1.8
and
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<20~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
The next full angular distribution analysis for
the
\Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
was performed by
the
CMS
collaboration
[
31
]

using
{\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{p}}
collisions data at
\sqrt{s}=7

\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
,
for the rapidity ranges
|y^{\Upsilon}|<0.6

and
0.6<|y^{\Upsilon}|<1.2
,
and for
10<p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<50~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}

[
31
]
.
CMS found no evidence of large transverse or longitudinal
polarization for any of the three
\Upsilon
mesons in
the explored kinematic region.
The experimental situation is complicated by
the result of the fixed-target experiment E866
[
32
]
,
which performed the polarization measurement of
the
\Upsilon
mesons produced in p-Cu collisions at

\sqrt{s}=38.8

\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
in
0.0<x^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{F}}<0.6
and

p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<4~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
The E866 collaboration found that
the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
meson is produced weakly polarized,
while the
\Upsilon(2\mathrm{S})
and
\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})
mesons are produced with a maximal transverse polarization.
Although different production energies may determine
different dominant contributions in the production processes,
all these results underscore the need for further experimental
study of the
\Upsilon
polarization.

The
LHCb
collaboration performed
the full angular distribution analysis
[
21
]

for the
\Upsilon
mesons produced in
{\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{p}}
collisions at

\sqrt{s}=7
and 8
\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
in the LHCb setup during the 2011 and
2012 data taking runs with integrated luminosities of
1 and 2
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, respectively.
The polarization measurement was done
in the HX, CS and GJ frames
in the
\Upsilon
kinematic range defined by

p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<30~{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
and
2.2<y^{\Upsilon}<4.5
.

2
LHCb
detector and selection of
\Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
decays

The
LHCb
detector is a single-arm forward spectrometer
primarily designed to look for indirect evidence of new physics in

C\!P
violation and rare decays of charm and beauty hadrons.
The detector covers a unique pseudorapidity range
2<\eta<5

corresponding to
\sim 4\%
of the solid angle, but

\sim 25\%
of all produced
{\mathrm{b}}{\overline{{\mathrm{b}}}}
pairs fall into this
geometrical acceptance.
A detailed description of the
LHCb
detector
and its performance are given in
[
33
,
34
]
.

The selection of
\Upsilon
candidates is similar to those used in
the previous LHCb analyses
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
.
The
\Upsilon
candidates are formed from pairs of oppositely charged tracks
reconstructed in the tracking system. Each track is required to have
a good reconstruction quality
[
39
]

and to be identified as a muon
[
40
]
.
Each muon is then required to have momentum satisfying

1<p_{\mathrm{T}}<25{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
,
10<\mbox{$p$}<400{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}

and pseudorapidity within the region

2.2<\upeta<4.5
.
The two muons are required to originate from a common vertex
with a good
\chi^{2}
probability of the vertex fit.
In addition, the consistency of the dimuon vertex with a primary
vertex is ensured via the quality requirement of a global
fit, performed for each dimuon candidate using the primary vertex
position as a constraint
[
41
]
.
This global fit requirement also reduces the background
caused by genuine muons coming from decays of long-lived charm
and beauty hadrons.
A large fraction of the combinatorial background is populated
at large values of
\left|\cos\theta_{\text{GJ}}\right|
,
where
\theta_{\text{GJ}}
is the polar angle of
{\upmu^{+}}

in the GJ frame.
To reduce this background, a requirement

\left|\cos\theta_{\text{GJ}}\right|<0.8

is applied.
Finally, the mass of the muon pair is required to be in the range

8.8<m_{{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}}<11.0\,{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

As an example, a dimuon mass distribution of the

\Upsilon\!\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
candidates finally selected in
6<p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}<8{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
and

2.2<y^{\Upsilon}<3.0
for
\sqrt{s}=7\,\mathrm{TeV}

data set is shown in
Fig. 1.
The dimuon mass distribution is parametrized
by the sum of three double-sided Crystal Ball
functions
[
42
,
43
]

for describing the
\Upsilon
mesons and an exponential function for
the combinatorial background.
The parametrization is done by an unbinned extended maximum
likelihood fit.
The dimuon mass fit is performed in each
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bin,
and results of the fit are then used by the

sPlot
analysis
[
44
]
for
obtaining so called signal
sWeights
:

w_{i}^{\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})}
,
w_{i}^{\Upsilon(2\mathrm{S})}
and
w_{i}^{\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})}
.
The
sWeights
are assigned to each,
i^{th}
, dimuon candidate
for extracting the appropriate
\Upsilon
signal component.
The total signal yields
obtained from the dimuon mass fit
in the full explored range of
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}
and
y^{\Upsilon}
are

0.8\times 10^{6}

(1.8\times 10^{6})

\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
candidates,

0.2\times 10^{6}

(0.5\times 10^{6})

\Upsilon(2\mathrm{S})
candidates and

0.1\times 10^{6}

(0.2\times 10^{6})

\Upsilon(3\mathrm{S})
candidates
in
\sqrt{s}=7\,\mathrm{TeV}

(
\sqrt{s}=8\,\mathrm{TeV}
) data set.
The average mass resolution of the
\Upsilon(1\mathrm{S})
peak is
42\,{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

3
Polarization analysis

The polarization measurement is performed using an unbinned
maximum likelihood approach
[
45
]

already applied in the
{\mathrm{J}\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\uppsi\mskip 2.0mu}
and
\uppsi{(2S)}
polarization
analyses
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.
The polarization parameters are determined from fits to the
two-dimensional

\left(\cos\theta,\phi\right)
angular distribution
of
\upmu^{+}
from the
\Upsilon\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
decay,
described by Eq.
1
.
In each
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bin,
the following logarithm of the likelihood function
is constructed for each
\Upsilon
state:

\log\mathcal{L}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})_{\Upsilon}=s_{w}\sum^{N_{\mathrm{tot}}}_{i=1}w_{i}^{\Upsilon}\times\log\left[\frac{\mathcal{P}(\cos\theta_{i},\phi_{i}|\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})}{\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})}\right]\;,

(2)

where

\mathcal{P}(\cos\theta_{i},\phi_{i}|\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})\equiv 1+\uplambda_{\theta}\cos^{2}\theta_{i}+\uplambda_{\theta\phi}\sin 2\theta_{i}\cos\phi_{i}+\uplambda_{\phi}\sin^{2}\theta_{i}\cos 2\phi_{i}
,

\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})

is normalization factor determined by Monte Carlo
for each
\Upsilon
meson,

w_{i}^{\Upsilon}
is one of the
sWeights
for
the
i^{th}

\Upsilon
candidate,
and
N_{\mathrm{tot}}
is the total number of all selected

\Upsilon
candidates in a considered
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bin.
The constant scale factor

s_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tot}}}w_{i}^{\Upsilon}/\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{tot}}}(w_{i}^{\Upsilon})^{2}

is introduced to take into account correctly the effect of the

sWeights
on statistical uncertainties of the
\vec{\uplambda}

parameters obtained after the polarization fit.
The influence of the
s_{w}
was validated by pseudoexperiments.
The normalization factor

\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})

is defined as

\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})\equiv\int\mathrm{d}\Omega~\mathcal{P}(\cos\theta,\phi|\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})\times\varepsilon(\cos\theta,\phi)

(3)

and is calculated using simulated events.
In the simulation, where the
\Upsilon
mesons are generated unpolarized,
the two-dimensional
\left(\cos\theta,\phi\right)

angular distribution of
\upmu^{+}
from decays of selected
\Upsilon
candidates
is proportional to the total efficiency

\epsilon(\cos\theta,\phi)
, so

\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})

is evaluated by summing

P(\cos\theta_{j},\phi_{j}|\lambda_{\theta},\lambda_{\theta\phi},\lambda_{\phi})

over the selected
\Upsilon
candidates in the simulated sample

\mathcal{N}(\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi})\propto\sum_{j}\epsilon^{{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}}\kappa^{\Upsilon}\mathcal{P}(\cos\theta_{j},\phi_{j}|\uplambda_{\theta},\uplambda_{\theta\phi},\uplambda_{\phi}),

(4)

where
\epsilon^{{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}}
is a muon identification efficiency
measured directly from data using a large sample of low-background

{{\mathrm{J}\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\uppsi\mskip 2.0mu}}\to{\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}}
events
(no muon identification requirement was applied when selecting
the
\Upsilon
candidates in the simulated samples);

\kappa^{\Upsilon}
is a correction factor for MC, obtained
using data-driven techniques to account for small differences between
data and simulation in a tracking efficiency of
muons
[
39
,
40
]

and in the
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}
and
y^{\Upsilon}
spectra
[
46
,
47
]
.

4
Results and conclusions

Different sources of systematic uncertainty have been considered
when determining the polarization parameters,
namely systematic uncertainty
related to: a) the
\Upsilon
signal extraction procedure;
b) the muon identification efficiency;
c) the track reconstruction efficiency;
d) a possible small difference in the trigger
efficiency between data and simulation;
e) correction factors for the muon identification efficiency;
f) the finite size of the simulated samples.
All these sources have been studied for the polarization
parameters
\uplambda_{\theta}
,
\uplambda_{\theta\phi}
,

\uplambda_{\phi}
and
\tilde{\uplambda}
in the HX, CS and GJ
frames for each
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bin.
It was found that the most dominant systematic uncertainty
is related to the finite size of MC samples,
varying between
30\%
and
70\%
of the statistical uncertainty.
The total systematic uncertainty for each polarization
parameter is calculated as the quadratic sum of systematic
uncertainties coming from all the considered sources,
assuming no correlation between them.
For some high-
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}
bins the systematic
and statistical uncertainties are comparable.

All the
\Upsilon
polarization results obtained by the
LHCb
collaboration for data collected at
\sqrt{s}=7\,\,{\mathrm{TeV}}

and
8\,{\mathrm{TeV}}
are given in
[
21
]
.
Here we outline the main features of the results.
The values of the parameter
\uplambda_{\theta}

obtained for the
\Upsilon
mesons
do not show any significant transverse or longitudinal
polarization in all frames
over the considered kinematic region.
The values of the parameters
\uplambda_{\theta\phi}
and

\uplambda_{\phi}
are small
in all frames over all
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bins.
All the three polarization parameters do not manifest
a distinct dependence on the
y^{\Upsilon}
.
The values of the frame invariant parameter

\tilde{\uplambda}
measured in the HX, CS and GJ
frames are consistent with each other.
Moreover, all values of the
\tilde{\uplambda}

are close to zero in all phase-space bins.
In the considered phase space domain,
the
\Upsilon
polarization results corresponding to

\sqrt{s}=7
and 8
\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
are in good agreement
with each other.

The
\vec{\uplambda}
parameters have been checked for
positivity constraints imposed on the spin-1
density-matrix
[
15
,
21
,
48
,
26
,
27
]
.
The values of the
\vec{\uplambda}
satisfy all the six positivity
constraints in all frames over all phase-space bins.
In particular,
Fig.
2
shows regions allowed by the positivity
constraints together with the parameters
\uplambda_{\theta}
and

\uplambda_{\phi}
measured in all
\left(p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}},~y^{\Upsilon}\right)
bins,
for data collected at
\sqrt{s}=7
and
8\mathrm{\,Te\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
Further, since the
z
axes of the HX, CS and GJ frames
coincide in the limit

p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}\to 0

[
15
,
22
]
,
we checked this constraint
and found that all values of the
\vec{\uplambda}
are
very similar for low-
p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}
bins in all frames.
It was also found that the parameters
\uplambda_{\theta\phi}

and
\uplambda_{\phi}
are very close to zero in the limit

p^{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{T}}\to 0
, in accordance with kinematic constraints
pointed out in
[
14
]
.

Figs.
3
and
4
show
a comparison of the
LHCb
results
[
21
]

with results obtained
by the
CDF

[
28
]
and
CMS

[
31
]

collaborations
for the HX and CS frames, respectively.
There is good agreement with
CMS
results for both frames,
and with
CDF
for the CS frame.

The
LHCb
collaboration continues to perform measurements
devoted to
\mathrm{b}
-hadron and quarkonium
physics
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
.
In particular,
the polarization studies performed by

LHCb

[
11
,
12
,
21
]

allowed one to shed some new facts on production mechanism of heavy
quarkonium states, and to aggravate old ones.

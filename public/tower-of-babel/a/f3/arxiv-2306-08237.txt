Abstract
. We study porous medium equations with a divergence form of drift terms in a bounded domain with no-flux lateral boundary conditions. We establish L q L^{q} -weak solutions for 1 ≤ q < ∞ 1\leq q<\infty in Wasserstein space under appropriate conditions on the drift, which is an extension of authors’ previous works done in the whole space into the case of bounded domains. Applying existence results to a certain Keller-Segel equation of consumption type, construction of L q L^{q} -weak solutions is also made, in case that the equation of a biological organism is of porous medium type.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the porous medium equations with the divergence form of drifts in the form of

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\rho=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\rho^{m}-V\rho)\qquad\ \text{ in }\ \Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\Omega\Subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, is a bounded domain with smooth boundaries and
0<T<\infty
. Here
V:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given vector field, which will be more specific later. In this paper, we study the initial value problem of (
1.1
) with the boundary conditions

(1.2)

\left(\nabla\rho^{m}-V\rho\right)\cdot\textbf{n}=0\ \text{ on }\ \partial\Omega\times(0,T)\quad\ \text{ and }\quad\ \rho(\cdot,0)=\rho_{0}\ \text{ on }\ \Omega,

where the vector
n
is normal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

For given nonnegative initial data
\rho_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for
q\geq 1
, our main purpose is to establish the existence of nonnegative weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) when the drift belongs to proper function spaces, so-called
sub-scaling classes
, which also allows mass conservation property (see Definition
2.2
).

This work is an extension to the case of bounded domains of previous results done by authors for the case of the whole space, i.e.,
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(we refer
[
6
]
for more details and references therein for related works).

Although not a few minor modifications are required in comparison to the results in
[
6
]
for whole space, it is worth making clear the noticeable differences for the case of bounded domains.
Firstly, moment estimates are required to control entropy and obtain estimates of speed for the whole space, and it is obviously straightforward in bounded domains. As a result, no need for moment estimates allows us to construct
L^{1}
-weak solution newly in Theorem
2.4
(i), Theorem
2.8
, and Theorem
2.8
.

Another good point for a bounded domain is the matter of the embedding of the drift vector field
V
, in particular for spatial variables. To be more precise, if
V
is integrable in
L^{q_{1}}_{x}L^{q_{2}}_{t}(\Omega_{T})
, then it is automatic that
L^{p_{1}}_{x}L^{p_{2}}_{t}(\Omega_{T})
for all
1\leq p_{i}\leq q_{i},\,i=1,2
, and meanwhile it is in general not valid for
1\leq p_{1}\leq q_{1}
for the whole space. In virtue of such advantage, the existence of weak solutions is ensured in a bit larger class of
V
compared to the case of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

On the other hand, since we look for solutions of mass conservation, we need to place a restriction on
V
relevant to the boundary condition (
1.2
). Roughly speaking,
V
is an approximation of smooth vector fields with homogeneous normal flux (see more details in Definition
2.2
and compare to
[
6
, Definition 1.1]
). Another shortcoming in bounded domains is the lack of uniqueness results since the way of proving it does not work out (compare to
[
6
, Section 2.2]
), although we expect that it is the case as in the whole space.

In the meantime, an improvement has been made regarding the compactness arguments so that we can cover the range
1\leq q\leq m+1
(see Proposition
4.9
for more precise statements), which is valid for the case of whole space as well. This new observation allows covering all
q\geq 1
together with the result for the range
q\geq\max\{1,m-1\}
proved in Proposition
4.11
and, as a result, the combination of both propositions enables us to enlarge the subscaling space of
V
. We reiterate that this additional compactness argument is applicable to the existence results in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we give an improved version of statements in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in Appendix
B
.

As an application, we establish
L^{q}
-weak solution for a certain Keller-Segel equation of consumption type given in (
2.30
)-(
2.31
) (see Section
2.2
).
More specifically, with the aid of our existence results for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
L^{q}
-weak solution with
1\leq q<\infty
can be constructed in dimensions three and higher (see Theorem
2.17
).

The notion of Wasserstein space and distance is reviewed in Section
3.2
, and the structures of
V
are categorized as one of three different types: (i)
V\in\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
, or (ii)
\nabla\cdot V\geq 0
, or (iii)
V\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{m,q}^{(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2})}
. The scaling invariant classes of

are described in Definition

, Remark
2.3
, and
[

Main results

)-(

) are categorized according to hypotheses of given initial data and drifts. As an application, we study coupled parabolic type Keller-Segel equations to improve previously known results by taking advantage of developed main results.

Here we introduce the notion of weak solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
and
V
be a measurable vector field.
We say that a nonnegative measurable function
\rho
is
a
L^{q}
-weak solution
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with
\rho_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
if the followings are satisfied:

(i)

It holds that

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)\right)\cap L^{m}(\Omega_{T}),\quad\nabla\rho^{m}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T}),\quad\nabla\rho^{\frac{m+q-1}{2}}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}),\quad\text{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T}).

(ii)

For any function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T))
, it holds that

(2.2)

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left\{\rho\varphi_{t}-\nabla\rho^{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\rho V\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\}\,dxdt=-\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}(\cdot)\varphi(\cdot,0)\,dx.

We remind the property of
mass conservation
for nonnegative solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), i.e.

\|\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
(see e.g.
[
14
, Theorem 11.2]
). Without loss of generality, assuming that
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, that is,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
, the mass conservation property in time implies that
\|\rho(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. If
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=c>0
, then we replace
\rho
by
\tilde{\rho}=\rho/c
that does satisfy the equation with similar structure (see
[
6
, Remark 2.2]
).

Motivated by preserving
L^{q}
-norm of a weak solution of (
1.1
), we derive the corresponding scaling-invariant classes for
V
and
\nabla V
in
L^{q_{1},q_{2}}_{x,t}
spaces (refer
[
6
, Definition 1.1]
). In the following definition, we introduce, the (sub)scaling-invariant classes for
V
and
\nabla V
adapted to the conservation of mass and boundary conditions (
1.2
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m,q\geq 1
and
q_{m,d}:=\frac{d(m-1)}{q}
. For constants
q_{1},q_{2},\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2}>0
, we define following spaces.

(i)

Define

\mathcal{L}_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}:=\overline{\{V:V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T))~~\mbox{and}~~V\cdot\textbf{n}=0~\mbox{on}~\partial\Omega\}}^{L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}},

where
n
is the outward unit normal vector to the boundary of
\Omega
.
That is, the vector field
V\in\mathcal{L}_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}
means that there exists a sequence
V_{n}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T))
such that

V\cdot\textbf{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

\|V_{n}-V\|_{L_{x,t}^{q_{1},q_{2}}}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty.

(ii)

The scaling invariant classes of
V
and
\nabla V
are defined as

(2.3)

\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{L}^{q_{1},q_{2}}_{x,t}:\ \|V\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q_{1},q_{2}}_{x,t}}<\infty\ \text{ where }\ \frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{q_{2}}=1+q_{m,d}\right\},

and, for
\tilde{q}^{\ast}_{1}=\frac{d\tilde{q}_{1}}{d-\tilde{q}_{1}}
where
\tilde{q}_{1}\in(1,d)
,

(2.4)

\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{m,q}^{(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2})}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{L}_{x,t}^{\tilde{q}^{\ast}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2}}:\ \|\nabla V\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2}}_{x,t}}<\infty\ \text{ where }\ \frac{d}{\tilde{q}_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{\tilde{q}_{2}}=2+q_{m,d}\right\}.

Moreover, let us name
\|V\|_{\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}}
and
\|V\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{m,q}^{(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2})}}
, as the scaling invariant norms corresponding to each spaces.

(iii)

The sub-scaling classes are defined as

(2.5)

\mathfrak{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{L}^{q_{1},q_{2}}_{x,t}:\ \|V\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q_{1},q_{2}}_{x,t}}<\infty\ \text{ where }\ \frac{d}{q_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{q_{2}}\leq 1+q_{m,d}\right\},

and, for
\tilde{q}^{\ast}_{1}=\frac{d\tilde{q}_{1}}{d-\tilde{q}_{1}}
where
\tilde{q}_{1}\in(1,d)
,

(2.6)

\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{m,q}^{(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2})}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{L}_{x,t}^{\tilde{q}^{\ast}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2}}:\ \|\nabla V\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2}}_{x,t}}<\infty\text{ where }\frac{d}{\tilde{q}_{1}}+\frac{2+q_{m,d}}{\tilde{q}_{2}}\leq 2+q_{m,d}\right\}.

Let us name
\|V\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}}
and
\|V\|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{m,q}^{(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2})}}
, as the sub-scaling norms corresponding to each spaces.

2.1.
Existence results

In this section, we present all existence results and related remarks composed of three subsections depending on the structure of the drift
V
. Results in each subsection are categorized by the type of weak solutions, the initial data, and the range of
m
and
q
.

2.1.1.
Existence for case:
V
in sub-scaling classes

Here, we assume that
V
belongs to the sub-scaling class
\mathfrak{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
that includes the scaling invariant class
\mathcal{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}
. We begin with
L^{q}
-weak solutions for
1\leq q<\infty
satisfying energy inequality, and later we introduce absolutely continuous
L^{q}
-weak solutions.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
m>1
and
q\geq 1
.

(i)

Let
1<m\leq 2
. Assume that
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
and
\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\log\rho_{0}\,dx<\infty
and

(2.9)

V\in\mathfrak{S}_{m,1}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}\ \text{ for }\ \begin{cases}2\leq q_{2}\leq\frac{m}{m-1},&\text{ if }d>2\\
2\leq q_{2}<\frac{m}{m-1},&\text{ if }d=2.\end{cases}

Then, there exists a nonnegative
L^{1}
-weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in Definition
2.1

that holds

(2.10)

\esssup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho\left|\log\rho\right|(\cdot,t)\,dx+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C,

with
C=C(\|V\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{m,1}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}},\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\log\rho_{0}\,dx)
.

(ii)

Let
q>1
and
q\geq m-1
.
Assume that
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\cap L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

(2.11)

V\in\mathfrak{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}\ \text{ for }\ \begin{cases}2\leq q_{2}\leq\frac{q+m-1}{m-1},&\text{ if }d>2\\
2\leq q_{2}<\frac{q+m-1}{m-1},&\text{ if }d=2.\end{cases}

Then, there exists a nonnegative
L^{q}
-weak solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in Definition
2.1

that holds

(2.12)

\esssup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{q}(\cdot,t)\,dx+\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{q+m-1}{2}}\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C,

with
C=C(\|V\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{m,q}^{(q_{1},q_{2})}},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})
.

2.4
.

Remark 2.5
.

(i)

Compared to the existence results of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
(see
[

2.2.
An application

As an application, we consider the following coupled system, which is a Keller-Segel equations of consumption type.

(2.30)

\rho_{t}-\Delta\rho^{m}=-\chi\nabla(\rho\nabla c),\qquad c_{t}-\Delta c=-\rho c,\qquad\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, is a bounded convex domain with smooth boundaries.
Boundary conditions are of no-flux, namely

(2.31)

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\textbf{n}}=\frac{\partial c}{\partial\textbf{n}}=0,\qquad\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

which satisfy (
1.2
).
We are interested in existence of weak solutions of (
2.30
)-(
2.31
), in case that non-negative initial data of
\rho
and
c
satisfy

(2.32)

\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|c_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty.

It is known in
[
16
]
that if
m>\frac{3d-2}{2d}
,
d\geq 3
, then there exists a bounded weak solution for (
2.30
)-(
2.32
). In the case that
d=3
, such a result was improved in
[
17
]
as
m>\frac{9}{8}
even for the system coupled with fluid equations (see
[
2
]
for Hölder continuous solutions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
).
In the case of
d=2
, it was proved in
[
13
]
that a bounded weak solution exists, provided that
m>1
(see also
[
3
]
for the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
). One consequence of our main results is existence of
L^{q}
-weak solutions for the case
1+\frac{d-2}{3d}\leq m\leq\frac{3d-2}{2d}
,
d\geq 3
.
We first define the notion of
L^{q}
-weak solutions of (
2.30
)-(
2.32
).

Definition 2.16
.

Let
1<m\leq\frac{3d-2}{2d}
,
d\geq 3
and
1<q<\infty
.
We say that
(\rho,c)
is a weak solution of the system (
2.30
)-(
2.32
), if the followings are satisfied:

(i)

\rho
and
c
are non-negative functions defined in
\Omega=\Omega\times(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(L^{1}\cap L^{q})(\Omega)),\quad\nabla\rho^{\frac{m+l-1}{2}}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}),\quad 1\leq l\leq q,

\displaystyle c\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(L^{\infty}\cap H^{1})(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)).

(ii)

(\rho,c)
satisfies (
2.30
) in the sense of distributions, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-\rho\varphi_{t}+\nabla\rho^{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi-\chi\rho\nabla c\cdot\nabla\varphi)dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)dx,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(-c\varphi_{t}+\nabla c\cdot\nabla\varphi+\rho c\varphi)dxdt=\int_{\Omega}c_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)dx

for all test function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega\times[0,T))
.

We call
(\rho,c)
a
bounded weak solution
, if
(\rho,c)
is a
L^{q}
-weak solution for any
q<\infty
and
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T})
. For simplicity, we assume that
\chi=1
.
We remind that a priori estimates (see e.g.
[
17
]
) show that

(2.33)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\ln\rho+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}\right)+\frac{4}{m^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}+N\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla c|^{4}}{c^{3}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|D^{2}c|^{2}}{c}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}\leq 0.

Indeed, this is due to following estimates

(2.34)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho\ln\rho+\frac{4}{m^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}\rho\Delta c

(2.35)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}+\int_{\Omega}c\left|D^{2}\ln c\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{c}\frac{|\nabla c|^{2}}{\partial\nu}=\int_{\Omega}\rho\Delta c

Adding (
2.34
) and (
2.35
), it follows that

(2.36)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\ln\rho+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}\right)+\frac{4}{m^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{m}{2}}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}c\left|D^{2}\ln c\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\rho|\nabla c|^{2}}{c}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{c}\frac{|\nabla c|^{2}}{\partial\nu}=0.

The case of convex domain implies that the boundary term in (
2.36
) becomes positive.
Recalling the inequality

(2.37)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla c|^{4}}{c^{3}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|D^{2}c|^{2}}{c}\leq N\int_{\Omega}c\left|D^{2}\ln c\right|^{2},

we obtain (
2.33
).

This estimate implies that
\nabla c\in L^{4}(\Omega_{T})
. With the aid of main results, we note that
L^{q}
-weak solution for the system (
2.30
) exists, provided that

\frac{d+2+q_{m,d}}{4}\leq 1+q_{m,d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\geq 1+\frac{q(d-2)}{3d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\leq\frac{3d(m-1)}{d-2}.

If
\frac{2(2d-1)}{3d}\leq m\leq\frac{3d-2}{2d}
, it follows that
if
1\leq q\leq\frac{3d(m-1)}{d-2}
, and thus, a
L^{q}
-weak solution exists.
In case that
d=3
, if
15/13\leq m\leq 7/6
, via the bootstrapping argument, bounded weak solutions exist, provided that initial data is sufficiently regular.
In general, since a
L^{q}
-weak solution exists for
q=\frac{3d(m-1)}{d-2}
, we note that

q_{m,d}=\frac{d-2}{3}
and
it follows from parabolic embedding that

(2.38)

\rho\in L^{r_{1},r_{2}}_{x,t}(\Omega_{T}),\qquad\frac{d}{r_{1}}+\frac{2+\frac{d-2}{3}}{r_{2}}=\frac{d-2}{3(m-1)},\quad m-1+\frac{3d(m-1)}{d-2}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty.

In particular, choosing
(r_{1},r_{2})=(d,2)
, we have

2+\frac{d-2}{6}=\frac{d-2}{3(m-1)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad m=1+\frac{2(d-2)}{d+10}.

On the other hand, since
m\leq\frac{3d-2}{2d}
, only the case
d=3
is available to satisfy

Due to the maximal regularity of the equation

and spatial embedding, we obtain

for any

weak solution and, in addition, by bootstrapping argument, it follows that

\rho
. Since it is rather straightforward that

becomes bounded, we skip its details.

In summary, we obtain the following:

3.2.
Wasserstein space

In this subsection, we introduce the Wasserstein space and its properties. For more detail, we refer
[
1
,
10
,
15
]
.
Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
the set of all Borel probability measures on
\Omega
with a finite
p
-th moment. That is,

\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega):=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu(x)<\infty\}
.
We note that
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)=\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
if
\Omega
is a bounded set.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
, we consider

(3.4)

W_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}|x-y|^{p}\,d\gamma(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\Gamma(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of all Borel probability measures on
\Omega\times\Omega
which has
\mu
and

\nu
as marginals;

\gamma(A\times\Omega)=\mu(A)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma(\Omega\times A)=\nu(A)

for every Borel set
A\subset\Omega.

Equation (
3.4
) defines a distance on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
which is called the
Wasserstein distance
and denoted by
W_{p}
.
Equipped with the Wasserstein distance,
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
is called the
Wasserstein space
.
We denote by
\Gamma_{o}(\mu,\nu)
the set of all
\gamma
which minimize the expression.

We say that a sequence of Borel probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
is narrowly convergent to
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
as

n\rightarrow\infty
if

(3.5)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,d\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\,d\mu(x)

for every function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(\Omega)
, the space of continuous and bounded real functions defined on
\Omega
. Then we recall

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}W_{p}(\mu_{n},\mu)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}\mu_{n}~\mbox{narrowly converges to }\mu~\mbox{ in }\mathcal{P}(\Omega)\\
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu_{n}(x)=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{p}\,d\mu(x).\end{cases}

Hence, if
\Omega
is compact then
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
is also a compact metric space.

Now, we introduce the notion of absolutely continuous curve and its relation with the continuity equation.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\sigma:[0,T]\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
be a curve.
We say that
\sigma
is absolutely continuous and denote it by
\sigma\in AC(0,T;\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega))
, if there exists
l\in L^{1}([0,T])
such that

(3.6)

W_{p}(\sigma(s),\sigma(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}l(r)dr,\qquad\forall~0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

If
\sigma\in AC(0,T;\mathcal{P}(\Omega))
, then the limit

|\sigma^{\prime}|(t):=\lim_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{W_{p}(\sigma(s),\sigma(t))}{|s-t|},

exists for
L^{1}
-a.e
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover, the function
|\sigma^{\prime}|
belongs to
L^{1}(0,T)
and satisfies

|\sigma^{\prime}|(t)\leq l(t)\qquad\mbox{for}~L^{1}\mbox{-a.e.}~t\in[0,T],

for any
l
satisfying (
3.6
).
We call
|\sigma^{\prime}|
by the metric derivative of
\sigma
.

Lemma 3.5
.

[
10
, Theorem 5.14]

If a narrowly continuous curve
\sigma:[0,T]\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
satisfies the continuity equation

\partial_{t}\sigma+\nabla\cdot(V\sigma)=0,

for some Borel vector field
V
with
\|V(t)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma(t))}\in L^{1}(0,T)
, then
\sigma:[0,T]\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)

is absolutely continuous and

|\sigma^{\prime}|(t)\leq\|V(t)\|_{L^{p}(\sigma(t))}
for
L^{1}
-a.e
t\in[0,T]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

[
1
, Proposition 3.3.1]

Let
K\subset\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
be a sequentially compact set w.r.t the narrow topology.
Let
\sigma_{n}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
be curves such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(t)\in K,\quad\forall~n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N},~t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle W_{p}(\sigma_{n}(s),\sigma_{n}(t))

\displaystyle\leq\omega(s,t),\qquad\forall~0\leq s\leq t\leq T,~n\in\mathbb{N},

for a continuous function
\omega:[0,T]\times[0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

\omega(t,t)=0,~~\forall~t\in[0,T].

Then there exists a subsequnece
\sigma_{n_{k}}
and a limit curve
\sigma:[0,T]\rightarrow{P}_{p}(\Omega)
such that

\sigma_{n_{k}}(t)\mbox{ narrowly converges to}~~\sigma(t),\qquad\text{for all}~~t\in[0,T].

3.3.
Flows on
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
generated by vector fields

For a given
T>0
, let
V\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}))
be such that
V\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
where
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal to the
boundary of
\Omega
. For any
s,~t\in[0,T]
, let

\psi:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\Omega\mapsto\Omega
be the flow map
of the vector field
V
.
More precisely,
\psi
solves the following ODE

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}\psi(t;s,x)=V(\psi(t;s,x),t),&\text{for }s,t\in[0,T]\\
\psi(s;s,x)=x,&\text{for }x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

Using the flow map
\psi
, we define a flow
\Psi:[0,T]\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
through the push forward operation as follows

(3.8)

\Psi(t;s,\mu):={\psi}(t;s,\cdot)_{\#}\mu,\qquad\forall~\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega).

In this subsection, we remind two basic results on the flow map
\psi
.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
s\in[0,T]
and
\psi
be defined as in (
3.7
). Then, for any
t\in[s,T]
, the map
\psi(t;s,\cdot):\Omega\mapsto\Omega
satisfies

e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{Lip}(V(\tau))\,d\tau}|x-y|\leq|\psi(t;s,x)-\psi(t;s,y)|\leq e^{\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{Lip}(V(\tau))\,d\tau}|x-y|,\qquad\forall~x,y\in\Omega,

where

\mbox{Lip}(V(\tau)):=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,\,x\neq y}\frac{|V(x,\tau)-V(y,\tau)|}{|x-y|}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
s\in[0,T]
and
\psi
be defined as in (
3.7
). For any
t\in[s,T]
, let
J_{s,t}
be the Jacobian corresponding to the map

\psi(t;s,\cdot):\Omega\mapsto\Omega
. That is,

(3.9)

\int_{\Omega}\zeta(y)dy:=\int_{\Omega}\zeta(\psi(t;s,x))J_{s,t}(x)\,dx,\qquad\forall~\zeta\in C(\Omega).

Then, the Jacobian
J_{s,t}
is given as

(3.10)

J_{s,t}(x)=e^{\int_{s}^{t}\nabla\cdot V(\psi(\tau;s,x),\tau)\,d\tau},\qquad\forall~x\in\Omega.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega
be given. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J_{s,t}(x)\big|_{t=\tau}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot V(\psi(\tau;s,x),\tau)J_{s,\tau}(x).

Since
J_{s,s}\equiv 1
, we have

(3.11)

\log J_{s,t}(x)=\int_{s}^{t}\nabla\cdot V(\psi(\tau;s,x),\tau)\,d\tau.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We note that
\psi(t;s,\cdot)\circ\psi(s;t,\cdot)=Id
, that is,

\psi(t;s,\psi(s;t,x))=x,\quad\forall~x\in\Omega.

Exploiting this, we have

J_{s,t}(\psi(s;t,x))=\frac{1}{J_{t,s}(x)}.

Lemma 3.10
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
s\in[0,T]
and
\Psi
be in (
3.8
). For any
t\in[s,T]
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
,
we have

(3.12)

e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{Lip}(V(\tau))\,d\tau}W_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq W_{p}(\Psi(t;s,\mu),\Psi(t;s,\nu))\leq e^{\int_{s}^{t}\mbox{Lip}(V(\tau))\,d\tau}W_{p}(\mu,\nu).

Lemma 3.11
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.4]

Let
\psi
and
\Psi
be defined as in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), respectively. If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ac}(\Omega)
then

\Psi(t;s,\mu)\in\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ac}(\Omega)
. Here,
\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ac}(\Omega)
is the set of all probability measures in
\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)
which are absolutely
continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\Omega
. Furthermore, suppose
\mu=\varrho\,dx
and
\Psi(t;s,\mu)=\rho\,dx
then

(3.13)

\rho(\psi(t;s,x))J_{s,t}(x)=\varrho(x),\qquad a.e\quad x\in\Omega,

where
J_{s,t}
is the Jacobian of the map
\psi(t;s,\cdot)
as in (
3.9
).
We also have

(3.14)

\int_{\Omega}\rho\log\rho\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\varrho\log\varrho\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\varrho\log J_{s,t}\,dx.

Moreover, if
\varrho\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for
q\in[1,\infty]
, then

{\rho}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and we have

(3.15)

\|{\rho}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|\varrho\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}e^{\frac{q-1}{q}\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla\cdot V\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,d\tau},

where
\frac{q-1}{q}=1
if
q=\infty
.

Lemma 3.12
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.18]

Let
\psi
and
\Psi
be defined as in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), respectively. Suppose that
V\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))

(i)

If

and
\varrho\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\Omega)\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
for some

then
\rho:=\Psi(t;s,\varrho)
is also Hölder continuous. More
precisely, we have
\|\rho\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}<C
where

(ii)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\rho^{a}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(a+2)\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla V\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,d\tau}\left\{\|\nabla\varrho^{a}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\varrho^{a}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\times\left(a\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}V\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\,d\tau\right)\right\}.

)-(

Definition 4.1

Let

and

We say that a nonnegative measurable function

of (

(ii)

For any

\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\left\{\rho\varphi_{t}-\nabla\rho^{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\rho V\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)\,dx.

Proposition 4.2

For carrying the splitting method, it requires a priori estimates, propagation of compact support, and Hölder continuity of the following form of homogeneous PME:

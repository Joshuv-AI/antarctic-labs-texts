Divergence of Majorana-Phonon Scattering in Kitaev Quantum Spin Liquid
Haoxiang Li, A. Said, J.Q. Yan, D.M. Mandrus, H. N. Lee, S. Okamoto, Gábor B. Halász, H. Miao

Abstract
Magnetoelastic interaction couples spin and lattice degrees of freedom and plays a key role in thermal transport properties of magnetic insulators. In the Kitaev quantum spin liquid, the low energy excitations are charge neutral Majorana fermions, which transform the magnetoelasctic interaction into Majorana-phonon scattering. Motivated by anomalous thermal properties of the Kitaev quantum spin liquid candidate α \alpha -RuCl 3 , in this letter, we combine meV resolution inelastic x-ray scattering and theoretical calculation to examine the Majorana-phonon scattering. We analytically derive the velocity-dependent Majorana-phonon scattering and find a divergence when the acoustic phonons and the itinerant Majorana fermions have the same velocity. Based on the experimentally determined acoustic phonon velocity in α \alpha -RuCl 3 , we estimate the range in the Kitaev interaction for which divergent Majorana-phonon scattering can happen. Our result opens a new avenue to uncover fractionalized quasiparticles in the Kitaev quantum spin liquid and emphasizes the critical role of lattice excitations in α \alpha -RuCl 3 .

Before analyzing the IXS result, we analytically derive the velocity dependent Majorana-phonon scattering in a gapless Kitaev spin liquid. Focusing on the lowest temperatures, we consider acoustic phonons of velocity
v_{\mathrm{ph}}
and linearly dispersing Majorana fermions of velocity
v_{\mathrm{f}}
; the two Dirac points of Majorana fermions at momenta
\pm\mathbf{K}
(which correspond to the
\mathrm{K}
and
\mathrm{K}^{\prime}
points of the Brillouin zone) are equivalent to a single Dirac point of complex fermions at momentum
\mathbf{K}
. The simplest processes annihilating a phonon of momentum
\mathbf{q}
then involve the scattering of a fermion with relative momentum
\mathbf{k}
(with respect to
\mathbf{K}
) into another fermion with relative momentum
\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}
. Note that processes creating or annihilating two fermions are forbidden due to the nontrivial momentum
\mathbf{K}
of the fermion Dirac point (
2\mathbf{K}
is not a reciprocal lattice vector). We also remark that, while the phonons are generally three dimensional, only those with a purely in-plane (two-dimensional) momentum
\mathbf{q}
can participate in the above scattering processes.

Importantly, due to the conservation of energy and momentum, the Majorana-phonon scattering is strongly velocity dependent. For
v_{\mathrm{ph}}>v_{\mathrm{f}}
[see Fig.
1
(c)], a negative-energy fermion can be scattered into a positive-energy fermion, whereas for
v_{\mathrm{ph}}<v_{\mathrm{f}}
[see Fig.
1
(e)], a positive-energy fermion can scatter into a positive-energy fermion or a negative-energy fermion can scatter into negative-energy fermion
Ye et al. 2020
;
Feng et al. 2021
. Furthermore, as we show in the Supplemental Material
SI
, the real and imaginary parts of the phonon self energy (corresponding to the energy renormalization and the decay rate, respectively) both diverge for
v_{\mathrm{ph}}\to v_{\mathrm{f}}
[see Fig.
1
(d)]. The divergent components of these two quantities at momentum
\mathbf{q}
and temperature
T=1/\beta
are found to be

\mathrm{Re}\Sigma_{\mathbf{q}}\propto\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{c}+|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\left(v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{I}^{(2)}(\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|)\quad(v_{\mathrm{ph}}>v_{\mathrm{f}}),\\
-|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\left(v_{\mathrm{f}}-v_{\mathrm{ph}}\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}(\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|)\quad(v_{\mathrm{ph}}<v_{\mathrm{f}}),\end{array}

(2)

\mathrm{Im}\Sigma_{\mathbf{q}}\propto\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{c}|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\left(v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}(\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|)\quad(v_{\mathrm{ph}}>v_{\mathrm{f}}),\\
|\mathbf{q}|^{2}\left(v_{\mathrm{f}}-v_{\mathrm{ph}}\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{I}^{(2)}(\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|)\quad(v_{\mathrm{ph}}<v_{\mathrm{f}}),\end{array}

(3)

where
\mathcal{I}^{(1,2)}(x)
with
x=\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|
are positive dimensionless functions with asymptotic forms

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}(x)\sim\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{c}x\quad(x\ll 1),\\
1\quad(x\gg 1),\end{array}\quad\mathcal{I}^{(2)}(x)\sim\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{c}x^{-1}\quad\,\,\,\,(x\ll 1),\\
x^{-3/2}\quad(x\gg 1).\end{array}

(4)

Hence, the real and imaginary parts of the phonon self energy have completely different scaling behaviors as a function of
\beta v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|
. In particular, at the smallest phonon momenta (

v_{\mathrm{ph}}<v_{\mathrm{f}}

2
corresponds to hardening (softening) of the phonon energy.

Finally we discuss the implications of our result to the sample dependent thermal transport measurement in
\alpha
-RuCl
3

Kasahara et al. 2018
;
Yamashita et al. 2020
;
Yokoi et al. 2021
;

In summary, we investigated the velocity-dependent Majorana-phonon scattering in the Kitaev quantum spin liquid and found that it shows divergent behavior when the phonon and Majorana velocities match. Our result helps to establish the phonon dynamical structure factor as a promising avenue to uncover fractionalized excitations and sheds light on the novel thermal transport properties of the Kiteav spin liquid candidate

from the first term of Eq. (
6
) is divergent in the
limit of
\alpha=\omega/(qv_{\mathrm{f}})\to 1
due to the
factor
(\omega^{2}-q^{2}v_{\mathrm{f}}^{2})^{-1/2}
in
Eq. (
8
).

Focusing on the second term of Eq. (
6
), the delta
function
\delta[\omega-v_{\mathrm{f}}(|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|-|\mathbf{k}|)]
necessarily vanishes for
\omega>v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|
due to
|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|-|\mathbf{k}|\leq|\mathbf{q}|
. In contrast, for
\omega\leq v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|
, the delta function is nonzero along a hyperbola
defined by
|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|-|\mathbf{k}|=\alpha|\mathbf{q}|
, where
\alpha=\omega/(v_{\mathrm{f}}|\mathbf{q}|)\leq 1
is the inverse eccentricity. Taking

\mathbf{q}=(q,0)
without loss of generality, and introducing the
elliptical coordinates
\mathbf{k}=q(\cosh\mu\cos\nu-1,\sinh\mu\sin\nu)/2
, this hyperbola corresponds to a contour
satisfying
\cos\nu=\alpha
. Given that
|\mathbf{k}|=q(\cosh\mu-\cos\nu)/2
and
|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|=q(\cosh\mu+\cos\nu)/2
, while
d^{2}\mathbf{k}=q^{2}d\mu\,d\nu\,(\cosh^{2}\mu-\cos^{2}\nu)/4
and

\delta\big[\omega-v_{\mathrm{f}}\left(|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|-|\mathbf{k}|\right)\big]=\delta\left[qv_{\mathrm{f}}\left(\alpha-\cos\nu\right)\right]=\frac{\delta\left[\alpha-\cos\nu\right]}{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}=\frac{\delta\left[\nu-\mathrm{arccos}\,\alpha\right]}{qv_{\mathrm{f}}|\sin\nu|}=\frac{\delta\left[\nu-\mathrm{arccos}\,\alpha\right]}{qv_{\mathrm{f}}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}},

(10)

the phonon decay rate from the second term of Eq. (
6
)
then becomes

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\Sigma^{(2)}(\mathbf{q},\omega)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{2v_{\mathrm{f}}v_{\mathrm{ph}}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu\left(\cosh^{2}\mu-\alpha^{2}\right)\sum_{\xi=\pm}\xi\tanh\left[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}(\cosh\mu+\xi\alpha)}{4T}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}q^{3}}{v_{\mathrm{f}}}\left(q^{2}v_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}-\omega^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\left[\frac{\omega}{qv_{\mathrm{f}}},\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\right]\quad(\omega<qv_{\mathrm{f}}),

where the dimensionless function
\mathcal{I}^{(2)}[\alpha,\gamma]
with
\alpha=\omega/(qv_{\mathrm{f}})
and
\gamma=qv_{\mathrm{f}}/T
is given by the integral

\mathcal{I}^{(2)}[\alpha,\gamma]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\alpha}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu\left(\cosh^{2}\mu-\alpha^{2}\right)\sum_{\xi=\pm}\xi\tanh\left[\frac{\gamma(\cosh\mu+\xi\alpha)}{4}\right].

(12)

Since the function
\mathcal{I}^{(2)}[\alpha,\gamma]
is finite
and well behaved for all
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
\gamma>0
, the
phonon decay rate
\mathrm{Im}\Sigma^{(2)}(\mathbf{q},\omega)

from the second term of Eq. (
6
) is divergent in the
limit of
\alpha=\omega/(qv_{\mathrm{f}})\to 1
due to the
factor
(q^{2}v_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}-\omega^{2})^{-1/2}
in
Eq. (
11
).

In the limit of

\mathrm{Im}\Sigma(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\mathrm{Im}\Sigma^{(1)}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+\mathrm{Im}\Sigma^{(2)}(\mathbf{q},\omega)

\alpha=\omega/(qv_{\mathrm{f}})=1
in the first

(13)

where
\theta(\tilde{\omega})
is the Heaviside step function,

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}[\gamma]
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(1)}[\gamma]\equiv\mathcal{I}^{(1)}[1,\gamma]=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\nu\sin^{2}\nu\sum_{\xi=\pm}\tanh\left[\frac{\gamma(1+\xi\cos\nu)}{4}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2)}[\gamma]\equiv\mathcal{I}^{(2)}[1,\gamma]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mu\sinh^{2}\mu\sum_{\xi=\pm}\xi\tanh\left[\frac{\gamma(\cosh\mu+\xi)}{4}\right].

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}[\gamma]\sim\Big\{\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\quad(\gamma\ll 1),\\

\gamma^{-3/2}\quad(\gamma\gg 1).\end{array}

(15)

Using the Kramers-Kronig relations, the divergent component of the
phonon energy renormalization, corresponding to the real part of the
phonon self energy, can then be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q},\tilde{\omega})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\,\mathbb{P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q},\tilde{\omega}^{\prime})}{\tilde{\omega}-\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{q^{5}}{v_{\mathrm{f}}^{3}}}\Bigg\{\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]\,\mathbb{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}}(\tilde{\omega}-\tilde{\omega}^{\prime})}+\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]\,\mathbb{P}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{\omega}^{\prime\prime}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\omega}^{\prime\prime}}(\tilde{\omega}+\tilde{\omega}^{\prime\prime})}\Bigg\}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{q^{5}}{v_{\mathrm{f}}^{3}|\tilde{\omega}|}}\left\{\theta\left(\tilde{\omega}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]-\theta\left(-\tilde{\omega}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]\right\}.

\omega=qv_{\mathrm{ph}}

\tilde{\omega}=q(v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{f}}^{3}|v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}|}}\left\{\theta\left(v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]+\theta\left(v_{\mathrm{f}}-v_{\mathrm{ph}}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]\right\},

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{\sqrt{v_{\mathrm{f}}^{3}|v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}|}}\left\{\theta\left(v_{\mathrm{ph}}-v_{\mathrm{f}}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]-\theta\left(v_{\mathrm{f}}-v_{\mathrm{ph}}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigg[\frac{qv_{\mathrm{f}}}{T}\bigg]\right\}.

(17)

Both the phonon decay rate and the phonon energy renormalization
diverge in the limit of matching phonon and fermion velocities
(
v_{\mathrm{ph}}\to v_{\mathrm{f}}
). Moreover, the divergent

\gamma=qv_{\mathrm{f}}/T\approx qv_{\mathrm{ph}}/T

\mathbf{q}
and/or

Also, the divergent component of the energy renormalization is

(corresponding to phonon softening).

Appendix B
Inelastic X-ray scattering and spectral peak fitting

Inelastic X-ray (IXS) scattering is powerful tool for probing phonon excitations with meV energy resolution
Baron 2020
. In this work, the experimental setup provides an energy resolution of 1.3 meV and a small X-ray beam size of
\mathrm{35}\times\mathrm{15}

\mathrm{\mu m}^{2}
. The energy resolution is calibrated by fitting the elastic peak (Fig.
S5
) to a pseudo-voigt function:

R(\omega)=(1-\alpha)\frac{I}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-\frac{\omega}{2\sigma^{2}}}+\alpha\frac{I}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma}{\omega^{2}+\Gamma^{2}}

(18)

where the energy resolution is the full-width-at-half-maximum (FWHM).

IXS directly probes the phonon dynamical structure factor,
S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)
. The IXS cross-section for solid angle
d\Omega
and bandwidth
d\omega
can be expressed as
Baron 2020
:

\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d\Omega d\omega}=\frac{k_{f}}{k_{i}}r_{0}^{2}|\vec{\epsilon_{i}}\cdot\vec{\epsilon_{f}}|^{2}S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)

(19)

where
k
and
\epsilon
represent the scattering vector and x-ray polarization and
i
and
f
denote initial and final states.
r_{0}
is the classical radius of the electron. In a typical measurement, the energy transfer
\omega
is much smaller than the incident photon energy (23.71 keV in our study). Therefore, the term
\frac{k_{f}}{k_{i}}\sim 1
, and
\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d\Omega d\omega}\propto S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)
.

S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)
is related to the imaginary part of the dynamical susceptibility,
\chi^{\prime\prime}\left(\mathbf{Q},\omega\right)
, through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Baron 2020
:

S\left(\mathbf{Q},\omega\right)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(1-e^{-\omega/k_{B}T})}\chi\prime\prime(\mathbf{Q},\omega)

(20)

Where
\chi\prime\prime(\mathbf{Q},\omega)
can be described by the damped harmonic oscillator form
Baron 2020
, which has antisymmetric Lorentzian lineshape:

\chi^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{Q},\omega)=\sum_{i}I_{i}[\frac{\Gamma_{i}}{(\omega-\omega_{\mathbf{Q},i})^{2}+\Gamma_{i}^{2}}-\frac{\Gamma_{i}}{(\omega+\omega_{\mathbf{Q},i})^{2}+\Gamma_{i}^{2}}]

(21)

here
i
indexes the different phonon peaks.

The phonon peak can be extracted by fitting the IXS spectrum at constant-momentum transfer
Q
, using Eq. (
20
) and (
21
). Due to the finite experimental resolution, the IXS intensity is a convolution of
S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)
and the instrumental resolution function,
R(\omega)
:

I(\mathbf{Q},\omega)=S(\mathbf{Q},\omega)\otimes R(\omega)

(22)

Here
R(\omega)
was determined by fitting elastic peak near a Bragg condition (Fig.
S5
).

The dashed curves in Fig. 2(e)-(g) of the main text represent fits of the experimental data following Eq. (
20
) to (
22
). In all these fittings, the intrinsic phonon linewidth is much smaller than the energy resolution
\Delta E\sim
1.3 meV. Therefore, all peak-widths are essentially resolution-limited. An additional resolution-limited elastic peak is included to account for the elastic diffuse scattering. The extracted peak positions are shown in Fig. 3(a) of the main text.

The extracted energy resolution using the full width half maximum (FWHM) is 1.3meV. The error bars represent one standard deviation assuming Poisson counting statistics.

Local C 0 C^{0} -estimate and existence theorems for some prescribed curvature problems on complete noncompact Riemannian manifolds Thanks: Research supported in part by NSFC grant 11801587.
Rirong Yuan

Abstract
. In this article we study a class of prescribed curvature problems on complete noncompact Riemannian manifolds. To be precise, we derive local C 0 C^{0} -estimate under an asymptotic condition which is in effect optimal, and prove the existence of complete conformal metrics with prescribed curvature functions. A key ingredient of our strategy is Aviles-McOwen’s result or its fully nonlinear version on the existence of complete conformal metrics with prescribed curvature functions on manifolds with boundary.



1.
Introduction

An important problem in conformal geometry is to determine which function could arise as a curvature function of a metric that is complete and conformally equivalent to a given metric.
A special case, known as Yamabe problem, conjectures that any closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
\geq 3
can be conformally deformed to achieve constant scalar curvature. The Yamabe problem on closed manifolds is valid according to the work of Trudinger
[
29
]
, Aubin
[
1
]
and Schoen
[
27
]
, while for general complete noncompact Riemannian manifolds the corresponding Yamabe problem is not always true as shown by Jin
[
17
]
who constructed some counterexamples.
This reveals that there are great differences between the conformal change of Riemannian metrics on closed manifolds and those on complete noncompact manifolds.
Therefore,
it is reasonable to investigate which complete noncompact Riemannian manifold could admit a complete conformal metric
with the scalar curvature being a constant or a more general function.
The problem in this and related topic has attracted enormous interest, see for instance

[
2
,
3
,
8
,
18
,
24
]
and references therein.

This paper is devoted to study a fully nonlinear version and then extend it to higher order curvatures.

Without specific clarification, we assume throughout this article that
(M,g)
is a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with the Levi-Civita connection

\nabla
. For a Riemannian metric
g
, we denote the sectional, Ricci and scalar curvature tensors by
Sec_{g}
,

Ric_{g}
and
R_{g}
, respectively.

In fact we are primarily concerned with a more general problem of finding

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(\lambda(\tilde{g}^{-1}A_{\tilde{g}}^{\tau,\alpha}))=\psi\mbox{ }\mbox{ in }M,\mbox{ }0<\psi\in C^{\infty}(M),

The equation is a fully nonlinear equation of similar type to Hessian equations going back to the work of Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck

In addition,

Moreover, the following three conditions are required:

(1.2)

f_{i}(\lambda):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0\mbox{ in }\Gamma,\ 1\leq i\leq n,

(1.3)

(1.4)

\displaystyle f(t\lambda)=tf(\lambda)\mbox{ for any }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }t>0.

Without loss of generality, we assume throughout the article that

\displaystyle f|_{\partial\Gamma}\equiv 0,\mbox{ }f|_{\Gamma}>0\mbox{ and }f(\vec{\bf 1})=1,

\vec{\bf 1}=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

This implies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\geq nf(\lambda),\,

\displaystyle\lambda\in\Gamma.

2.1.
Notation

Let

On

Under a locally unit orthogonal frame

\displaystyle Ric_{g}(e_{i},e_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{i},e_{j}),\,

\displaystyle R_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Ric_{g}(e_{i},e_{i}),

thus one can check

(2.1)

\displaystyle Ric_{g}(e_{i},e_{i})=\frac{R_{g}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l\neq i}g(R(e_{k},e_{l})e_{k},e_{l}),\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq i\leq n.

2.2.
Some formulas for conformal change

Under the conformal change

,

\displaystyle Ric_{\tilde{g}}=

\displaystyle Ric_{g}-\Delta ug-(n-2)\nabla^{2}u-(n-2)|\nabla u|^{2}g+(n-2)du\otimes du,

\displaystyle e^{2u}R_{\tilde{g}}=

\displaystyle R_{g}-2(n-1)\Delta u-(n-1)(n-2)|\nabla u|^{2},

where and hereafter

\displaystyle A_{\tilde{g}}^{\tau,\alpha}=

\displaystyle A_{g}^{\tau,\alpha}+\frac{\alpha(\tau-1)}{n-2}\Delta ug-\alpha\nabla^{2}u+\frac{\alpha(\tau-2)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g+\alpha du\otimes du.

.
Thus

3.

Throughout this section, we assume that

3.1.1.
Fully uniform ellipticity

The linearized operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v=

\displaystyle F^{ij}g_{ij}\Delta v-\varrho(x)F^{ij}\nabla_{ij}v+2a(x)g(\nabla u,\nabla v)F^{ij}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(F^{pq}g_{pq}g^{ij}-\varrho(x)F^{ij})\nabla_{ij}v+\mbox{lower order terms},

where

The eigenvalues of matrix

\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}-\varrho(x)f_{1},\cdots,\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}-\varrho(x)f_{n}.

Next, we prove that (

There is

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}-\varrho(x)f_{i}\geq\theta\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j},\,

\displaystyle\forall i.

Proof.

If
\varrho<0
, (
3.8
) holds for
\theta=1
.
In what follows we assume
0<\varrho(x)<\frac{1}{1-\kappa_{\Gamma}\vartheta_{\Gamma}}

As a corollary of Lemma

\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}-f_{i}\geq\kappa_{\Gamma}\vartheta_{\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\geq\left(1-\varrho(x)(1-\kappa_{\Gamma}\vartheta_{\Gamma})\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}+\varrho(x)f_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
This proves (
3.8
).

∎

3.2.1.

Lemma 3.5

3.2.2.
Local barriers and boundary estimate for gradient

Let
\rho(x)
be the distance function from

to boundary
\partial M
with respect to
g

\Omega_{\delta}=\{x\in M:\rho(x)<\delta\}.

Notice
\rho
is smooth in

when

, and
|\nabla\rho|=1
on
{\partial M}
.

Locally lower barrier

We use
\rho
to construct local barriers.
Let

w=\beta\log\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta^{2}+\rho}.

Then
e^{2\gamma w}=(\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta^{2}+\rho})^{2\beta\gamma}.

First, a straightforward computation gives

Lemma 3.7

\displaystyle V[w]=

\displaystyle A+\frac{\beta(1+a\beta)}{(\delta^{2}+\rho)^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}g+\frac{\beta(b\beta-\varrho)}{(\delta^{2}+\rho)^{2}}d\rho\otimes d\rho

\displaystyle+cL(\frac{-\beta}{\delta^{2}+\rho}d\rho)-\frac{\beta}{\delta^{2}+\rho}(\Delta\rho g-\varrho\nabla^{2}\rho),

\displaystyle V[w+\varphi]=

\displaystyle V[w]+c(L(d(w+\varphi))-L(dw))+(\Delta\varphi g-\rho\nabla^{2}\varphi)

\displaystyle+a\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{2}-\frac{2\beta}{\delta^{2}+\rho}g(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\rho)\right)g+bd\varphi\otimes d\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta b}{\delta^{2}+\rho}(d\varphi\otimes d\rho+d\rho\otimes d\varphi)

Using this lemma, if (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) hold then there is a positive constant
c_{0}
such that for small

\displaystyle V[w]\geq\frac{c_{0}}{(\delta^{2}+\rho)^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}g,\,

\displaystyle V[w+\varphi]\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2(\delta^{2}+\rho)^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}g\mbox{ on }\Omega_{\delta},

and so

\displaystyle f(\lambda(g^{-1}V[w+\varphi])\geq\psi e^{2\gamma(w+\varphi)}\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\delta}.

One can check
(w+\varphi-u)|_{\partial M}=0

(w+\varphi-u)|_{\rho=\delta}=\beta\log\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}+(\varphi-u)|_{\rho=\delta}<0\mbox{ if }\delta\ll 1.

Here we use Lemma
3.6
.
Now we have a local lower barrier
w+\varphi
near boundary.

Locally upper barrier

Let
v=\beta^{\prime}\log(1+\frac{\rho}{\delta^{2}})+\varphi.

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}V[v])=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{\prime}|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{(\delta^{2}+\rho)^{2}}\left\{-n+\varrho+(na+b)\beta^{\prime}\right\}+\frac{\beta^{\prime}(n-\varrho)}{\delta^{2}+\rho}\Delta\rho

\displaystyle+\frac{2(na+b)\beta^{\prime}}{\delta^{2}+\rho}g(\nabla\rho,\nabla\varphi)+c\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}L(d\varphi+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\delta^{2}+\rho}d\rho))

\displaystyle+(n-\varrho)\Delta\varphi+(na+b)|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\mathrm{tr}(g^{-1}A).

By (

. Note

on
\partial M

\delta

It is easy to see

\{\rho=\delta\}

for
0<\delta\ll 1
.

Therefore, near the boundary we now obtain a locally upper barrier

As a consequence, we obtain the gradient estimate at boundary

By standard continuity method, we can prove Theorem
3.4
.
The conformal metrics with prescribed boundary metric is also obtained.

3.3.
Complete conformal metrics on manifolds on boundary

The following theorem provides a straightforward approach to local bound of solutions from above.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose (

), (

), (

\displaystyle f(\lambda(g^{-1}A_{\tilde{g}_{k}}^{\tau,\alpha}))=\psi e^{2u_{k}},\mbox{ }\tilde{g}_{k}=e^{2u_{k}}g\mbox{ in }M,\,

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\Delta\tilde{u}+(n-1)(n-2)|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}-R_{g}=e^{2\tilde{u}}\mbox{ in }M,\,

\displaystyle u_{k}\leq u_{k+1}\leq\tilde{u}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\alpha(n\tau+2-2n)}{2n(n-1)(n-2)\inf_{M}\psi}\mbox{ in }M\mbox{ for }k\geq 1.

Given any compact subset

By Theorem

Combining with Schauder theory, let

,
there is a smoothly admissible function
u_{\infty}
in
M
to solve

\displaystyle f(\lambda(g^{-1}A_{g_{\infty}}^{\tau,\alpha}))=\psi e^{2{u_{\infty}}},\mbox{ }g_{\infty}=e^{2u_{\infty}}g\mbox{ in }M,\mbox{ }\lim_{x\rightarrow\partial M}u_{\infty}(x)=+\infty.

\displaystyle u_{\infty}\leq\tilde{u}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\alpha(n\tau+2-2n)}{2n(n-1)(n-2)\inf_{M}\psi}.

is complete.

.
The straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(\tau-1)}{n-2}\Delta h_{k}g-\alpha\nabla^{2}h_{k}+\frac{\alpha(\tau-2)}{2}|\nabla h_{k}|^{2}g+\alpha dh_{k}\otimes dh_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(n\tau+2-2n)}{2(n-2)}h_{k}^{\prime 2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}g+\alpha h^{\prime}_{k}(\frac{\tau-1}{n-2}\Delta\rho g-\nabla^{2}\rho).

then according to Theorem

\displaystyle|u_{k}|_{C^{2}(K)}\leq C_{1},\,

\displaystyle\forall k\geq k_{0}

holds for a uniformly positive constant
C_{1}
depending not on
k
.
The Evans-Krylov theorem
[
9
,
19
]
and classical Schauder theory give

\displaystyle|u_{k}|_{C^{l,\alpha}(K)}\leq C_{l}=C_{l}(K,K_{1}),\,

\displaystyle\forall k\geq k_{0},\mbox{ }l\geq 2,\mbox{ for some }0<\alpha<1.

By diagonal process, we obtain a desired solution
u_{\infty}\in C^{\infty}(M)
to equation (
1.1
).

From Theorem
4.3
,

u_{\infty}\geq\underline{u}+\frac{1}{2}\min\{0,\log\Lambda_{1}\}\mbox{ in }M.

Combining with the completeness of
\underline{g}=e^{2\underline{u}}g
, we know
g_{\infty}=e^{2u_{\infty}}g
is complete.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The method also works for prescribed scalar curvature equation.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
R_{g}<0
.
Let
\psi
be a smooth positive function which is bounded from above in terms of
-R_{g}
, i.e. there is a constant
\delta>0
such that

0<\psi\leq-\delta R_{g}
. Then there exists a smooth complete conformal metric
\tilde{g}
with
R_{\tilde{g}}=-\psi.

5.
Complete conformal metrics on Euclidean spaces

There are two fundamental models of complete manifolds: standard hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{H}^{n},g_{-1})
and flat Euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
.

In this section we deal with the latter one, and
the purpose is to prove the existence of complete metric
that is conformal to the standard Euclidean metric.
The main ingredient is to construct certain admissible complete smooth conformal metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which then confirms assumption (
1.11
) of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be the standard coordinate systems, and
r^{2}=|x|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}.

Let
h
be a smooth radial symmetric function:

\displaystyle h=\beta\log(1+r^{2})\mbox{ for }\beta>0.

Since
g_{0}
is flat,
A_{g_{0}}^{\tau,\alpha}\equiv 0
. Let
g=e^{2h}g_{0}
, then

A_{{g}}^{\tau,\alpha}=\frac{\alpha(\tau-1)}{n-2}\Delta hg-\alpha D^{2}h+\frac{\alpha(\tau-2)}{2}|Dh|^{2}g+\alpha dh\otimes dh.

Some simple computations (with respect to the flat metric
g_{0}
) give

\displaystyle|Dr^{2}|^{2}=4r^{2},\,

\displaystyle D^{2}r^{2}=2g_{0},

\displaystyle dh\otimes dh=\frac{\beta^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}dr^{2}\otimes dr^{2},\,

\displaystyle|Dh|^{2}=\frac{4\beta^{2}r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle D^{2}h=\frac{2\beta}{1+r^{2}}g_{0}-\frac{\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}dr^{2}\otimes dr^{2},

\displaystyle\Delta h=\frac{2n\beta}{1+r^{2}}-\frac{4\beta r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}=\frac{2\beta(n-2)r^{2}+2\beta n}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\tau-1}{n-2}\Delta hg_{0}-D^{2}h+\frac{\tau-2}{2}|Dh|^{2}g_{0}+dh\otimes dh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\left((\tau-2)(1+\beta)r^{2}+\frac{n(\tau-2)+2}{n-2}\right)g_{0}+\frac{\beta(1+\beta)}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}dr^{2}\otimes dr^{2},

with the eigenvalues (with respect to
g_{0}
):

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=

\displaystyle\cdots=\lambda_{n-1}=\frac{2\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\left((\tau-2)(1+\beta)r^{2}+\frac{n(\tau-2)+2}{n-2}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\left(\tau(1+\beta)r^{2}+\frac{n(\tau-2)+2}{n-2}\right).

5.1.
Modified Schouten tensors

With replacing (
1.8
) by a stronger condition

(5.2)

\displaystyle\tau<0,\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\alpha=-1;

\displaystyle\tau>\max\left\{2,1+(n-2)(1-\kappa_{\Gamma}\vartheta_{\Gamma})\right\},\,

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\alpha=1,

we prove

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
5.2
) hold. Let
\delta>0
be fixed.
For any smooth function

\psi
with

(5.3)

\displaystyle 0<\psi(x)\leq\Lambda_{2}|x|^{-2-\delta}\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ whenever }|x|\gg 1.

There is a smooth admissible complete metric
\tilde{g}
that is conformal to
g_{0}
such that

\displaystyle f(\lambda(\tilde{g}^{-1}A_{\tilde{g}}^{\tau,\alpha}))=\psi,\mbox{ }\lambda(\tilde{g}^{-1}A_{\tilde{g}}^{\tau,\alpha})\in\Gamma\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

Given (
5.2
) we can check that

\lambda(g_{0}^{-1}A_{\tilde{g}}^{\tau,\alpha})\geq c_{0}r^{-2}g_{0}
for some
c_{0}>0
.
The proof is complete by using Theorem
1.5
.
∎

5.2.
Ricci tensor

In this case,
\tau=0,\alpha=-1
. We divide this case into two subcases.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
f
satisfy (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
), and
we assume that for some
\delta>0
,
\psi
satisfies (
5.3
). Then there exists a
smooth admissible complete conformal metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g_{0}
satisfying with

f(\lambda(-\tilde{g}^{-1}Ric_{\tilde{g}}))=\psi,\mbox{ }\lambda(-\tilde{g}^{-1}Ric_{\tilde{g}})\in\Gamma\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

provided that
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{n}
.

Proof.

The eigenvalues of
-Ric_{\tilde{g}}
with respect to
g_{0}
are as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=

\displaystyle\cdots=\lambda_{n-1}=\frac{2\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}}\left(2(1+\beta)r^{2}+\frac{2n-2}{n-2}\right)\geq\frac{4\beta(1+\beta)r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=

\displaystyle\frac{4(n-1)\beta}{(1+r^{2})^{2}(n-2)}.

Since
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{n}
,
(1,\cdots,1,0)\in\Gamma
and then
c_{0}^{\prime}:=f(1,\cdots,1,0)>0
is well defined. So

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-g_{0}^{-1}Ric_{\tilde{g}}))\geq\frac{4\beta(1+\beta)c_{0}^{\prime}r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2}},

that is

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-\tilde{g}^{-1}Ric_{\tilde{g}}))\geq\frac{4\beta(1+\beta)c_{0}^{\prime}r^{2}}{(1+r^{2})^{2+2\beta}}.

This completes the proof by setting
\beta=\frac{\delta}{4}
.
∎

However, it is much more complicated for the case
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
. While for special cases
f=(\sigma_{n,k})^{1/(n-k)}
,
0\leq k<n
,
where
\sigma_{n,k}=C_{n}^{k}\sigma_{n}/\sigma_{k}
, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

There exists a smooth complete metric
\tilde{g}
, which is conformal to
g_{0}
, with negative Ricci curvature and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k}(-\tilde{g}^{-1}Ric_{\tilde{g}})=\psi\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\,

\displaystyle 0\leq k<n,

provided
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
0<\psi(x)\leq\Lambda_{2}|x|^{-2(n-1-k)-\delta}\mbox{ for }|x|\gg 1,\mbox{ for some }\delta>0
.

When
f
is the
(k,l)
-quotient functions,
f=(C_{n}^{l}\sigma_{k}/C_{n}^{k}\sigma_{l})^{1/(k-l)}
,
the problem for negative Ricci tensor on Euclidean spaces was discussed in
[
28
]
by constructing radial symmetric subsolutions and supersolutions.

6.0.1.
Application to Ricci tensor and certain modified Schouten tensors

Let

\tilde{f}

in the corresponding cone

We can check that
\kappa_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=n-1

\Gamma

Again, a straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(-g^{-1}A_{g})g-\varrho(-A_{g})=\frac{\varrho}{n-2}Ric_{g}-\frac{(n-2+\varrho)R_{g}}{2(n-1)(n-2)}g.

As consequences, we derive many results.

When

6.0.2.
Application to the Einstein tensor

Let
\mu_{i}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\lambda_{j}

Given a homogeneous function of degree one, we let
\tilde{f}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=f(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n})

We can check that when

By a simple computation, one derives

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(-g^{-1}A_{g})g-(-A_{g})=\frac{1}{n-2}(Ric_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{2}g)=\frac{1}{n-2}G_{g}.

As an application, we obtain Theorem 1.1 of

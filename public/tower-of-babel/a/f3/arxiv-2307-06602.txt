Introduction

Recently, the following time-dependent fractional Choquard-type equation

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=(-\Delta)^{s}\Psi-(I_{\mu}*F(\Psi))f(\Psi),\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.1)

has attracts much attention, where
i
denotes the imaginary unit,
s\in(0,1)
,

I_{\mu}(x)=\frac{{1}}{{|x|^{\mu}}}
with
\mu\in(0,N)
,
F
is the primitive function of
f
, and
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional Laplacian operator defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=C({N,s})\ \mbox{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
where P.V. means the Cauchy principal value and
C({N,s})
is some positive normalization constant, we refer to
[
17
]
for more details.
When we searching for stationary waves of
(\ref{1.1})
with the form
\Psi(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then
u
solves the following equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

(1.2)

To get solutions of
(\ref{1.2})
, one way is to fix
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and look for solutions of
(\ref{1.2})

as critical points of the energy functional
\hat{J}:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
14
,
15
,
36
,
48
]
)

\displaystyle\hat{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}-\lambda u^{2})\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x

with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

where
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product and norm respectively

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\mathrm{d}x,

\|u\|=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/2}.

Another important way is to prescribe the
L^{2}
-norm of the unknown
u
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
appears as a Lagrange multiplier, that is to consider the following problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}u=\lambda u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

for any fixed
a>0
. This type of solutions is called normalized solution, and can be obtained by
looking for critical points of the following energy functional

\displaystyle\bar{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x

on the
L^{2}
-sphere

\bar{S}(a):=\Big\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2}\Big\}.

In particular, we are interested in looking for ground state solutions, i.e., solutions minimizing
\bar{J}
on
\bar{S}(a)
among all nontrivial solutions, and the associated energy is called ground state energy.

The study of normalized solutions for the following semilinear elliptic equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u+g(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\end{cases}

(1.4)

goes back to the pioneering work
[

,

by Stuart. In that paper, Stuart dealt with problem (
1.4
) for

and

(
L^{2}
-subcritical case), here
2+\frac{4}{N}
is called the
L^{2}
-critical exponent, which comes from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
34
]
. When

is
L^{2}
-supercritical growth, by using a mountain pass structure for the stretched functional, Jeanjean
[

By using a minimax principle based on the homotopy stable family,

[
7
,
8
]
also presented a new approach that is based on a natural constraint associated to the problem and proved the existence of normalized solutions for problem (
1.4
). Inspired by
[

,
7
,
8
]
, Soave
[

]
studied problem (
1.4
) with combined nonlinearities

,

and

, where

if

and

if

. The Sobolev critical case

and

was considered by Soave
[
38
]
. Furthermore, the above results are later generalized to exponential critical case by Alves et al.
[
1
]
and the fractional Laplace case by Luo and Zhang
[

]
, Zhang and Han
[

]

.
More results of normalized solutions for scalar equations and systems can be found in

,

,

]

If

[

considered problem

In the case

The fractional case of problem

For more results of normalized solutions for the Choquard equation, we refer the readers to

In particular,

Motivated by the results above, considering that there is no results on normalized solutions for the fractional Choquard-type equation with exponential critical growth in dimension one.
In this paper, we are interested in the following problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{1/2}u=\lambda u+(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u),\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where

denotes the 1/2-Laplacian operator,

, and

]

[

]

[

]

More precisely, we assume that

satisfies:

(f_{1})

f\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, and
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{|f(t)|}{|t|^{\kappa}}=0
for some
\kappa>2-\mu
;

(f_{2})

f
has exponential critical growth at infinity, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{|f(t)|}{e^{\alpha t^{2}}}=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\mbox{for}\ \ \alpha>\pi,\\
+\infty,\quad&\mbox{for}\ \ 0<\alpha<\pi;\end{cases}

(f_{3})
There exists a constant

for all

(f_{4})
There exist

and

for any
|t|\geq t_{0}
.

(f_{5})
For any

, define
\widetilde{F}(t):=f(t)t-(2-\mu)F(t)
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{F}(t)}{|t|^{3-\mu}}\ \mbox{is non-increasing in $(-\infty,0)$ and non-decreasing in $(0,+\infty)$.}

(f_{6})
There exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that
\liminf\limits_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(t)}{e^{\pi t^{2}}}\geq{\beta_{0}}
.

Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f
satisfies
(f_{1})
-
(f_{6})
, then problem
(\ref{problem})
has at least one ground state solution.

Remark 1.1
.

A typical example satisfying

-
(f_{6})
is

\displaystyle f(t)=|t|^{p-2}te^{\pi t^{2}},\quad\forall\ p>\max\{3,\kappa+1,\theta\}.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some preliminaries. For the fractional Laplacian operator, the special case when
s=1/2
is called the square of the Laplacian. We recall the definition of the fractional Sobolev space

H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y<\infty\Big\},

endowed with the standard norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1/2}=\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}[u]_{1/2}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/2},

where the term

[u]_{1/2}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{1/2}

denotes the Gagliardo semi-norm of a function
u
. Moreover, by
[
17
, Proposition 3.6]
, we have

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}).

Next, we recall the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
29
, Theorem 4.3]
Let
1<r,t<\infty
and
0<\mu<N
with
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\mu}{N}=2
. If
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then there exists a sharp constant
C(N,\mu,r,t)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{f(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\leq C(N,\mu,r,t)\|f\|_{r}\|h\|_{t}.

(2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

(Cauchy-Schwarz type inequality)
[
32
]

For
g,h\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}\ast|g(x)|)|h(x)|\mathrm{d}x\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}\ast|g(x)|)|g(x)|\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\mu}\ast|h(x)|)|h(x)|\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.2
.

(The fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality)
[
18
]
Let
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
p\in[2,\frac{2N}{N-2s})
, then there exists a sharp constant
C(N,s,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq C(N,s,p)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4s}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}-\frac{N(p-2)}{4s}}.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.3
.

(Full range Adachi-Tanaka-type on
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
)
[
42
]
It holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\{0\},\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}\leq 1}\frac{1}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\alpha|u|^{2}}-1)\mathrm{d}x\begin{cases}<\infty,\quad\alpha<\pi,\\
=\infty,\quad\alpha\geq\pi.\end{cases}

(2.4)

Lemma 2.4
.

[
23
, Lemma 4.8]

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be any open set. For
1<s<\infty
, let
\{u_{n}\}
be bounded in
L^{s}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}(x)\to u(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
. Then
u_{n}(x)\rightharpoonup u(x)
in
L^{s}(\Omega)
.

3
The variational framework

Equation
(\ref{problem})
has a variational structure and its associated energy functional
J:H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x.

By using assumptions
(f_{1})
and
(f_{2})
, it follows that for each
\zeta>0
,
q>1
and
\alpha>\pi
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(t)|\leq\zeta|t|^{\kappa}+C|t|^{q-1}(e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1)\quad\mbox{for all}\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},

and using
(f_{3})
, we have

\displaystyle|F(t)|\leq\zeta|t|^{\kappa+1}+C|t|^{q}(e^{\alpha t^{2}}-1)\quad\mbox{for all}\ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.1)

By (
2.1
) and (
3.1
), we know
J
is well defined in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
and
J\in C^{1}(H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})
with

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{[u(x)-u(y)][v(x)-v(y)]}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)v\mathrm{d}x,

for any
u,v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence, a critical point of
J
on

S(a)=\Big\{u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2}\Big\}.

corresponds to a solution of (
1.5
).

To understand the geometry of
J|_{S(a)}
, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)(x):=e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u(e^{\beta}x),\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}$}.

One can easily check that
\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{1}),\beta_{2})=\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{2}),\beta_{1})
for any
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we find that
J
is unbounded from below on
S(a)
.
It is well known that all critical points of
J|_{S(a)}
belong to the Pohoz̆aev manifold (see
[
15
,
33
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(a)=\big\{u\in S(a):P(u)=0\big\},

where

\displaystyle P(u)=\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}+(2-\mu)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)u\mathrm{d}x.

This enlightens us to consider the minimization of
J
on
\mathcal{P}(a)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle m(a)=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}(a)}J(u).

Our task is to show that
m(a)
is a critical level of
J|_{S(a)}
. As will be shown in Lemma
4.2
,
\mathcal{P}(a)
is nonempty, thus any critical point
u
of
J|_{S(a)}
with
J(u)=m(a)
is a ground state solution of problem (
1.5
).

With a similar argument of
[
6
, Lemma 3.5]
, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\beta_{n}\to\beta
in
\mathbb{R}
as
n\to\infty
, then
\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{n})\to\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)
in

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{4})
hold, let

be a bounded sequence in

H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
,
if

in

and

for some

, then for any

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))f(u_{n})\phi\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)\phi\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mbox{as $n\rightarrow\infty$}.

Proof.

By Fatou Lemma, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)u\mathrm{d}x\leq K_{0}.

Denote
\Omega:=supp\phi
. For any given

, let
M_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{K_{0}\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon}
, then by

, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u_{n}|\geq M_{\varepsilon}\}\cup\{|u|=M_{\varepsilon}\}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))\big|f(u_{n})\phi\big|\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{K_{0}}\int_{|u_{n}|\geq\frac{M_{\varepsilon}}{2}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))f(u_{n})u_{n}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2\varepsilon

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\int_{|u|\geq M_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))\big|f(u)\phi\big|\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{K_{0}}\int_{|u|\geq M_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))f(u)u\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon.

(3.3)

Let

|f(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}}\to|f(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}}|f(u_{n})|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}}|f(u)|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x.

(3.4)

Choosing
L_{\varepsilon}>t_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\infty}(\frac{M_{0}K_{0}}{L_{\varepsilon}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(u)|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}<\varepsilon

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle\int_{|u|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u){\chi_{|u|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}})|f(u)\phi|\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon.

(3.6)

Then from
(f_{4})
, (
2.1
), (

),

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u|\neq M_{\varepsilon}\}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}){\chi_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}})|f(u_{n})\phi|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}){\chi_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}})|f(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\phi\|_{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}){\chi_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}})|F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*|f(u_{n})|\chi_{\{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}})|f(u_{n})|{\chi_{\{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\Big(\int_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))F(u_{n})\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\{\Omega\backslash G_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}}|f(u_{n})|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\Big(\frac{M_{0}}{L_{\varepsilon}}\int_{|u_{n}|\geq L_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))f(u_{n})u_{n}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(u)|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\Big(\frac{K_{0}M_{0}}{L_{\varepsilon}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(u)|^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}<\varepsilon.

(3.7)

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

\displaystyle d_{n}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\quad\mbox{and}\quad d(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y.

According to
(\ref{Ft})
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
R>0
, one has

\displaystyle|d_{n}(x)-d(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|}{|x-y|^{\mu}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{|x-y|\leq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\leq R}|x-y|^{-\frac{1+\mu}{2}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{2-\mu}{2-2\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2-2\mu}{2-\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}|x-y|^{\mu-2}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{4}{1-\mu}R^{\frac{1-\mu}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\leq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{2}{(1-\mu)R^{1-\mu}}\Big)^{\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{2-\mu}{2-2\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2-2\mu}{2-\mu}}.

Similar to
(
3.4
)
, we can get

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\leq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\ n\to\infty.

Moreover, it follows from
(\ref{Ft})
that

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}-|F(u)|{\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{2-\mu}{2-2\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2-2\mu}{2-\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|u_{n}\|_{\frac{(\kappa+1)(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+\|u_{n}\|_{\frac{q(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{q}+\|u\|_{\frac{(\kappa+1)(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+\|u\|_{\frac{q(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{q}\Big)\leq C.

Choosing
R>0
large enough, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain
d_{n}(x)\to d(x)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Using
(\ref{Ft})
again, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we also have

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{n}(x)\leq&\Big(\int_{|x-y|\leq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{1+\mu}{1-\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\leq R}|x-y|^{-\frac{1+\mu}{2}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}}\\
&+\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}\Big||F(u_{n})|{\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}}\Big|^{\frac{2-\mu}{2-2\mu}}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{2-2\mu}{2-\mu}}\Big(\int_{|x-y|\geq R}|x-y|^{\mu-2}\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}}\\
\leq&\Big(\frac{4}{1-\mu}R^{\frac{1-\mu}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2\mu}{1+\mu}}(2R)^{\frac{1-\mu}{1+\mu}}\max_{|t|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}|F(t)|\\
&+C\Big(\frac{2}{(1-\mu)R^{1-\mu}}\Big)^{\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}}\Big(\|u_{n}\|_{\frac{(\kappa+1)(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+\|u_{n}\|_{\frac{q(2-\mu)}{2-2\mu}}^{q}\Big)\leq C.\end{split}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\big|d_{n}(x)f(u_{n}(x)){\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}}\phi(x)\big|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\max_{|t|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}|f(t)|,

for any
x\in\Omega
. This inequality together with
d_{n}(x)\to d(x)
, and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, yields that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\{|u_{n}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\}\cap\{|u|\neq M_{\varepsilon}\}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}){\chi_{|u_{n}|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}})|f(u_{n})\phi|\mathrm{d}x=\int_{|u|\leq M_{\varepsilon}}(I_{\mu}*F(u){\chi_{|u|\leq L_{\varepsilon}}})|f(u)\phi|\mathrm{d}x.

(3.8)

Combining
(\ref{27})
,
(\ref{28})
and
(\ref{26})
-
(\ref{24})
, we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
hold. Let
u\in S(a)
be arbitrary but fixed, then we have

(i)
J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\to 0^{+}
as
\beta\to-\infty
;

(ii)
J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\to-\infty
as
\beta\to+\infty
.

Proof.

(i)
By a straightforward calculation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a^{2},\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x=e^{\frac{(\xi-2)\beta}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{\xi}\mathrm{d}x,\ \ \forall\ \xi>2,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)(x)-\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=e^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Thus there exist
\beta_{1}<<0
such that
\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}^{2}<\frac{2-\mu}{2}
for any
\beta<\beta_{1}
. Fix
\alpha>\pi
close to
\pi
and
\nu>1
close to
1
such that

\frac{2\alpha\nu}{2-\mu}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}^{2}<\pi,\quad\text{for any $\beta<\beta_{1}$}.

Then, for
\frac{1}{\nu}+\frac{1}{\nu^{\prime}}=1
, using (
2.4
),
(\ref{Ft})
, the Hölder and Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|F(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\|_{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\leq\|f(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+C\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(e^{\alpha|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|^{2}}-1)|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|^{q}\big]^{\frac{2}{2-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{2-\mu}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+C\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\frac{2\alpha\nu}{2-\mu}|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|^{2}}-1)\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{2-\mu}{2\nu}}\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+C\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{\frac{2\alpha\nu}{2-\mu}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}^{2}\big(\frac{|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)|}{\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{2}}\big)^{2}}-1)\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{2-\mu}{2\nu}}\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+C\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta e^{\frac{(\kappa+\mu-1)\beta}{2}}\|u\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{\kappa+1}+Ce^{\frac{(q\nu^{\prime}+\mu-2)\beta}{2\nu^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}}^{q}.

(4.1)

Hence, it follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(I_{\mu}*F(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\big)F(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}^{2(\kappa+1)}+C\|\mathcal{H}(u,\beta)\|_{{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}}^{2q}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{(\kappa+\mu-1)\beta}\|u\|_{{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}}^{2(\kappa+1)}+Ce^{\frac{(q\nu^{\prime}+\mu-2)\beta}{\nu^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}}^{2q}.

Since
\kappa>2-\mu
,
q>1
and
\nu^{\prime}
large enough, it follows that

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\geq\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}-Ce^{(\kappa+\mu-1)\beta}\|u\|_{{\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{2-\mu}}}^{2(\kappa+1)}-Ce^{\frac{(q\nu^{\prime}+\mu-2)\beta}{\nu^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{{{\frac{2q\nu^{\prime}}{2-\mu}}}}^{2q}\to 0^{+},\ \ \mbox{as}\ \beta\to-\infty.

(ii)

For any fixed
\beta>>0
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(tu))F(tu)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for $t>0$}.

Using
(f_{3})
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{W}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}}{\mathcal{W}(t)}>\frac{2\theta}{t}\quad\text{for $t>0$}.

Thus, integrating this over
[1,e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}]
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u))F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)\mathrm{d}x\geq e^{\theta\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x.

(4.2)

Hence,

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}e^{(\theta+\mu-2)\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u))F(u)\mathrm{d}x.

Since

, the above inequality yields that
J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))\to-\infty
as
\beta\to+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
and
(f_{5})
hold. Then for any fixed
u\in S(a)
, the function
I_{u}(\beta):=J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))
reaches its unique maximum with positive level at a unique point
\beta_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u})\in\mathcal{P}(a)
. Moreover, the mapping
u\rightarrow\beta_{u}
is continuous in
u\in S(a)
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
, there exists
\beta_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
P(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u}))=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\beta}I_{u}(\beta)\Big|_{\beta=\beta_{u}}=0
and
J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u}))>0
. Next, we prove the uniqueness of
\beta_{u}
.
For
u\in S(a)
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we know

\displaystyle I_{u}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}e^{(\mu-2)\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u))F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)\mathrm{d}x,

and

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta))=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\beta}I_{u}(\beta)=

\displaystyle e^{\beta}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2-\mu}{2}e^{(\mu-3)\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u))F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\frac{1}{2}e^{(\mu-3)\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u))f(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{\beta}\Big(\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u\|_{2}^{2}-\Phi(\beta)\Big),

where

\displaystyle\Phi(\beta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(I_{\mu}*\frac{F(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)}{(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}})^{3-\mu}}\Big)\frac{\widetilde{F}(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u)}{(e^{\frac{\beta}{2}})^{3-\mu}}\mathrm{d}x.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, using
(f_{3})
and
(f_{5})
, we see that

is strictly increasing in

and
\frac{\widetilde{F}(\beta t)}{\beta^{3-\mu}}
is non-decreasing in
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
. This implies that
\Phi(\beta)
is strictly increasing in
\beta\in(0,+\infty)
and there is at most one
\beta_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u})\in\mathcal{P}(a)
.

From the above arguments, the mapping
u\rightarrow\beta_{u}
is well defined. Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S(a)
be a sequence such that
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
as
n\to\infty
. We only need to prove that, up to a subsequence,
\beta_{u_{n}}\to\beta_{u}
in
\mathbb{R}
as
n\to\infty
.

On the one hand, if up to a subsequence,
\beta_{u_{n}}\to+\infty
as
n\to\infty
, then by (
4.2
) and
u_{n}\to u\neq 0
in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
as
n\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{-\beta_{u_{n}}}J(\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u_{n}}))

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\Big[\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}-e^{(\theta+\mu-3)\beta_{u_{n}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(I_{\mu}*F(u_{n}))F(u_{n})\mathrm{d}x\Big]

\displaystyle=-\infty,

which is a contradiction. Hence,
\{\beta_{u_{n}}\}
is bounded from above.

On the other hand, by Proposition
3.1
,
we know
\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u})\to\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u})
in

as

. Then

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u_{n}}))\geq J(\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u}))=J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u}))+o_{n}(1),

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}J(\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u_{n}}))\geq J(\mathcal{H}(u,\beta_{u}))>0.

If up to a subsequence,
\beta_{u_{n}}\to-\infty
as

, we get

\displaystyle J(\mathcal{H}(u_{n},\beta_{u_{n}}))\leq\frac{e^{\beta_{u_{n}}}}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\to 0,\quad\text{as $n\rightarrow\infty$},

which is impossible. So we get
\{\beta_{u_{n}}\}
is bounded from below. Up to a subsequence, we assume that

as

. Since

∎

\displaystyle\omega_{n}(x)=\frac{a}{2}\begin{cases}\log n(1+o(1)),\ \ &\mbox{for}\ \ |x|<\frac{1}{n},\\

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{1/4}\omega_{n}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\big|\frac{a}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\varpi_{n}(x)-\frac{a}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}\varpi_{n}(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

(4.4)

For any

From Lemmas

.

-

(\ref{tain1})

\bf{Case\ 1}

, then
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=0
. By

-

Proof.

For any given

By the definition of

(5.1)

Consider a cut-off function

if

\varrho(x)=0
if

\varepsilon>0

3.1

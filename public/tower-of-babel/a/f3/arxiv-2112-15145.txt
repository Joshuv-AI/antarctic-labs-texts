Weak Approximation for 0 0 -cycles on a product of elliptic curves
Evangelia Gazaki* with an appendix by Angelos Koutsianas**

Abstract
. In the 1980’s Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc, Kato and S. Saito proposed conjectures related to local-to-global principles for 0 0 -cycles on arbitrary smooth projective varieties over a number field. We give some evidence for these conjectures for a product X = E 1 × E 2 X=E_{1}\times E_{2} of two elliptic curves. In the special case when X = E × E X=E\times E is the self-product of an elliptic curve E E over ℚ \mathbb{Q} with potential complex multiplication, we show that the places of good ordinary reduction are often involved in a Brauer-Manin obstruction for 0 0 -cycles over a finite base change. We give many examples when these 0 0 -cycles can be lifted to global ones.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over a number field
F
. We denote by
\Omega
the set of all places
v
of
F
and by
F_{v}
the completion of
F
at a place
v
. The classical local-to-global principles for
X
refer to the image of the diagonal embedding

X(F)\hookrightarrow X(\mathbf{A}_{F}):=\prod_{v\in\Omega}X(F_{v})

to the set
X(\mathbf{A}_{F})
of adelic points of
X
. The cohomological Brauer group
\Br(X):=H^{2}(X_{\text{\'{e}t}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
is known to often obstruct either the existence of an
F
-rational point or the density of
X(F)
in
X(\mathbf{A}_{F})
. Namely, by the foundational work of Y. Manin (
[
Man71
]
), the Brauer group gives rise to an intermediate closed subset
X(F)\subseteq X(\mathbf{A}_{F})^{\Br(X)}\subseteq X(\mathbf{A}_{F})
, which is often empty or properly contained in
X(\mathbf{A}_{F})
.
Unfortunately, the Brauer-Manin obstruction cannot always explain the failure of the Hasse principle and weak approximation for points (cf.
[
Sko99
,
Poo10
]
). However, this happens to be the case for abelian varieties, assuming finiteness of their Tate-Shafarevich groups, and it is conjectured to be the case for geometrically rationally connected varieties (
[
CT03
, p. 174]
). The answer is likely to be yes also for
K3
-surfaces (cf.
[
SZ08
, p. 4]
).

In this article we are interested in an analog of this study for
0
-cycles, namely for the group
\CH_{0}(X)
.
Denote by
\Omega_{f}(F)
(resp.
\Omega_{\infty}(F)
) the set of all finite (resp. infinite) places of
F
. For a place
v\in\Omega
denote by
X_{v}
the base change to
F_{v}
. We have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
CTS81
, Section 4]
,
[
KS86
, Section 7]
, see also
[
CT95
, Conjecture 1.5 (c)]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over a number field
F
.
The following complex is exact,

\hskip 10.0pt\varprojlim_{n}\CH_{0}(X)/n\rightarrow\varprojlim_{n}\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)/n\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

The above formulation is due to van Hamel (
[
vH03
]
), see also
[
Wit12
, Conjecture (
E_{0}
)]
. The adelic Chow group
\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)
is equal to
\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}(F)}\CH_{0}(X_{v})
when
F
is totally imaginary, and it has a small contribution from the infinite real places otherwise.

When
X
is a smooth projective curve,
section 1
has been proved by Colliot-Thélène (
[
CT99
, paragraph 3]
) assuming the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group of its Jacobian.
In higher dimensions, the main evidence for
section 1
is for rationally connected varieties starting with the work of Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer (
[
CTSSD87b
,
CTSSD87a
]
) and continued by the work of Liang (
[
Lia13
, Theorem B]
). The latter proved this conjecture assuming that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to Weak Approximation for points on
X_{L}
where
L
is any finite extension of
F
. There is some recent partial evidence of Ieronymou of similar flavor for
K3
-surfaces (cf.
[
Ier21
, Theorem 1.2]
). Moreover, Harpaz and Wittenberg (
[
HW16
, Theorem 1.3]
) proved that the conjecture is compatible with fibrations over curves.

The purpose of this article is to give some evidence for this conjecture for a product
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
of elliptic curves. We focus on the following weaker question.

Question 1.2
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
\Br(X)\{p\}
be the
p
-primary torsion subgroup of
\Br(X)
. Is the following complex exact

(1.3)

\varprojlim_{n}\CH_{0}(X)/p^{n}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{n}\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(X)\{p\},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})?

Assuming the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group of
X
, and that the action of the absolute Galois group
G_{F}
on the Néron-Severi group
\NS(X\otimes_{F}\overline{F})
is trivial, the exactness of
1.3
can be reduced (cf.
subsection 2.3
,
subsection 2.3
) to the exactness of the complex,

(1.4)

\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}(X)/p^{n}\rightarrow\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}\rightarrow\Hom\left(\frac{\Br(X)\{p\}}{\Br_{1}(X)\{p\}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\right),

where
F^{2}(X)
is the kernel of the Albanese map of
X
and
\Br_{1}(X)
is the algebraic Brauer group of
X
. From now make the above assumptions.
Our first result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

(
subsection 3.1
) Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves over a number field
F
. There is an infinite set
T
of primes
p
for which the middle term in
1.4
vanishes, making the complex exact. If we further assume that at least one of the curves does not have potential complex multiplication, then the complement
S
of
T
is a set of primes of density zero (in the sense of
[
Ser81
]
).

When both
E_{1},E_{2}
have potentially good reduction, this result was already obtained in
[
GH21
, Corollary 1.8]
.
It follows by
[
RS00
, Theorem 3.5]
that the only places that might contribute a nontrivial factor in
\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}
are the places of bad reduction and the places above
p
. The key to prove
section 1
is that under some assumptions on the prime
p
, the group
F^{2}(X_{v})
is
p
-divisible for every unramified place
v
above
p
of good reduction (cf.
[
GH21
, Theorem 1.1]
).
One can think of this result as a weaker analog of the vanishing
F^{2}(X_{v})=0
for
X
a rationally connected variety and
v
a place of good reduction (
[
KS03
, Theorem 5]
).

A natural follow-up question is whether the places of good reduction are ever involved in a Brauer-Manin obstruction for
0
-cycles. Our second theorem gives an affirmative answer for places of good ordinary reduction that are ramified enough.

Theorem 1.6
.

(cf.
subsection 4.1
) Let
X=E\times E
be the self product of an elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose that
E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has complex multiplication by the full ring of integers of a quadratic imaginary field
K
. Let
p\geq 5
be a prime which is coprime to the conductor
\mathfrak{n}
of
E
and suppose that
p
splits completely in
K
,
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
for some prime element
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\overline{E_{p}}
be the reduction of
E
modulo
p
. Suppose there exists a finite Galois extension
F_{0}/K
of degree
n<p-1
such that there is a unique unramified place
w
of
F_{0}
above
\pi
with the property that
p
divides
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{w})|
, where
\mathbb{F}_{w}
is the residue field of
F_{0}
at
w
.
Then there exists a finite Galois extension
L/F_{0}
of degree
p-1
, totally ramified at
w
such that the following are true for the group
\displaystyle\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X_{L})/p^{n}
.

(\mathrm{1})

It is equal to
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})/p^{n}
, and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
.

(\mathrm{2})

It is orthogonal to the transcendental Brauer group
\Br(X_{L})/\Br_{1}(X_{L})
.

The same result holds if the elliptic curve
E
is defined over
K
.

The primes
p
of good reduction that split completely in
K
are precisely the primes of good
ordinary reduction
.
The involvement of the ordinary reduction places in a Brauer-Manin obstruction after suitable base change is not surprising. An analogous result for rational points was recently obtained in
[
BN20
]
, which in particular applies to weak approximation for points on abelian varieties and
K3
surfaces. See also
[
Pag21
]
for an explicit example.

Global Approximation

We next pass to the main question of this article namely, whether in the situation considered in
section 1
, one can lift the group
\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X_{L})/p^{n}
to global elements. An important observation is that
\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X_{L})/p^{n}
coincides with
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})/p
(cf.
subsection 3.2
). Thus, one is looking for genuine
0
-cycles and the question is reduced to showing that the diagonal map

F^{2}(X_{L})/p\xrightarrow{\Delta}\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})/p
is surjective.

We focus mainly on the simplest case considered in
section 1
, namely when
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
; that is, when the reduction of the elliptic curve
E
satisfies
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
.
One can find infinite families with this property. For example, consider the family of elliptic curves with Weierstrass equation
\{y^{2}=x^{3}+c;\;c\in\mathbb{Z},c\neq 0\}
having potential CM by the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
. Let
p
be a prime of the form
4p=1+3v^{2}
. Then, there exist exactly
\frac{p-1}{6}
congruence classes
c\mod p
that give elliptic curves that satisfy
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
.

In
subsection 4.2
we give various sufficient conditions that guarantee the ability to lift. The conditions are supported by explicit examples, giving therefore some evidence for
section 1
. The following
section 1
is indicative of the computations we do in
section 4
.
Before stating the result, we need to refer to some notation.

Suppose the pair
E,p
satisfies the assumptions of
section 1
.
Let
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
. We will denote by
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
the image of
P
under the restriction map
E(\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We abuse notation and denote by
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
also its image modulo
pE(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In
subsubsection 3.2.1
we construct a decomposition
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\oplus\overline{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, with
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E}(p\mathbb{Z}_{p})/[p]
, and
\overline{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})/p
(cf. notation (
3.5
)).
We can now state our next theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime and
E
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the assumptions of
section 1
. Assume further that
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
. Let
L/K
be the extension constructed in
section 1
. Suppose that the Mordell-Weil group
E(\mathbb{Q})
has positive rank and there exists a point
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
of infinite order such that the image
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of
P
under the restriction map
\displaystyle\res_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}}:E(\mathbb{Q})/p\rightarrow E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p
, has the property that
\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\neq 0\in\frac{\widehat{E}(p\mathbb{Z}_{p})}{[p]\widehat{E}(p\mathbb{Z}_{p})}
.
Then we have a surjection

F^{2}(X_{L})/p\to\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})/p\to 0.

In particular, the complex (
1.4
) is exact.

We expect that
section 1
is satisfied by infinite families of elliptic curves. The criterion given in
section 1
can be checked computationally in SAGE for small values of the prime
p
. In the appendix
A
we study the family of elliptic curves
\{y^{2}=x^{3}-2+7n,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and the prime
p=7
. When
n
lies in the interval
[-5000,5000]
, we show that about 86.68% of elliptic curves with rank one over
\mathbb{Q}
have such a “good point”
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
.

The method used to prove
section 1
can be generalized to more situations (cf.
section 4
for when
F_{0}/K
is a quadratic extension). In
section 4
we give criteria of similar flavor for elliptic curves that do not possess a suitable rational point of infinite order. For the purposes of the introduction, we chose to state our theorem in its simplest form.

Remark 1.8
.

In a recent article Liang (
[
Lia20
]
) showed some compatibility of weak approximation for
0
-cycles with certain products using the fibration method. The same method has been used in most known results (
[
Lia13
,
HW16
,
Ier21
]
). Unfortunately, this breakthrough method does not seem to generalize to abelian varieties. For, the key cohomological properties
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
, for
i=1,2
, and finiteness of
\Br(X)/\Br_{0}(X)
(used for rationally connected varieties and
K3
surfaces respectively) are no longer true. Our approach is different in the sense that it does not use the assumption that the weak approximation is the only one for rational points. The downside is that we cannot find a uniform treatment that guarantees lifting, but we instead present many different sufficient conditions. The advantages on the other hand are first that our results are unconditional and second that we construct global
0
-cycles on the nose. In fact, if one only focuses on proving exactness of
1.4
, then the finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group is no longer needed.

2.
Background

2.1.
Elliptic Curves with Complex Multiplication

In this subsection we recall some necessary facts about elliptic curves with complex multiplication. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with potential complex multiplication by the full ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
K
. It follows by
[
Rub99
, Corollary 5.12]
that
K
has class number one.
Note that there exactly
9
such fields
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
. Namely,
D\in\{-1,-2,-3,-7,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163\}
. The same result holds if
E
is defined over
K
.

Notation 2.1
.

For an element
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
we will denote by
E[a]
the kernel of the isogeny
a:E_{K}\rightarrow E_{K}
. Similarly, if
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
\mathfrak{a}=(a)
for some element
a\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
which is uniquely defined up to units. We will also write
E[\mathfrak{a}]
for the kernel of
a:E_{K}\rightarrow E_{K}
(which is independent of the choice of generator).

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a nonzero proper ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
which is prime to the conductor
\mathfrak{n}_{K}
of
E_{K}
. It follows by
[
Rub99
, Cor. 5.20 (ii)]
that there is an isomorphism:

\Gal(K(E[\mathfrak{a}])/K)\xrightarrow{\simeq}(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}.

In particular, if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
not dividing the conductor, then the Galois group
\Gal(K(E[\mathfrak{p}])/K)
is cyclic of order
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})-1
, where
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}:K^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is the norm map.
Next suppose that
p
is a rational prime coprime to
\mathfrak{n}
. We distinguish the following two cases:

•

p
is a prime element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, so
k=K_{(p)}
is a quadratic unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Then
\Gal(K(E[p])/K)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
. In this case the elliptic curve
E_{k}
has good supersingular reduction.

•

p
splits completely in
K
, that is,
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
, where
\pi
is a prime element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Then we have an isomorphism,

\Gal(K(E[p])/K)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},
and hence
K(E[p])/K
is an extension of degree
(p-1)^{2}
. In this case the elliptic curve
E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
has good ordinary reduction.

The case of interest to us in this note is the latter. From now on we will refer to such primes as
ordinary primes
. We fix such an ordinary prime
p
and a factorization
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
into prime elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We will denote by
\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
the prime ideals
(\pi),(\overline{\pi})
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
respectively. Notice that the two completions
K_{\mathfrak{p}},K_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
of
K
are both equal to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
\overline{E_{p}}
be the reduction of
E
modulo
p
and
r:E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\rightarrow\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the reduction map. We will the use same notation for the reduction
E(k)\xrightarrow{r}\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
where
k/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is any finite extension. Moreover, we will denote by
\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
the formal group of
E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.

It follows
by
[
Deu41
]
(see also
[
Lan87
, 13.4, Theorem 12]
) that
we can choose
\pi
so that the endomorphism
\pi:E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
when reduced modulo
p
coincides with the Frobenius endomorphism
\phi_{p}:\overline{E_{p}}\rightarrow\overline{E_{p}}
. In particular, the reduction of
\pi
is an automorphism of
\overline{E_{p}}
, and hence has trivial kernel. This implies that for every
n\geq 1
the subgroup
E[\pi^{n}]
of
E[p^{n}]
coincides with the
p^{n}
-torsion of the formal group,
\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}[p^{n}]
. Moreover, the relation
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
implies that
\overline{\pi}
induces a height zero isogeny
[\overline{\pi}]:\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\to\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
, and hence for every
n\geq 1
the reduction induces an isomorphism of abelian groups
E[\overline{\pi}^{n}]\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{E_{p}}[p^{n}]
.

Let
k/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension. Since
E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
has good ordinary reduction, for every
n\geq 1
there is a short exact sequence of
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
-modules,

(2.2)

0\rightarrow\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}[p^{n}]\rightarrow E[p^{n}]\rightarrow\overline{E_{p}}[p^{n}]\rightarrow 0.

The above discussion shows that
2.2
splits, since the subgroup
E[\overline{\pi}^{n}]
of
E[p^{n}]
maps isomorphically to
\overline{E_{p}}[p^{n}]
(see also
[
Ser89
, A.2.4]
for more general results).

Special Fiber

We next recall formulas for
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
for the various choices of the quadratic imaginary field
K
. We are particularly interested in finding examples of curves that satisfy
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
. That is, curves for which the extension
F_{0}
of
section 1
can be taken to be
\mathbb{Q}
. In all cases we have a formula,

(2.3)

|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p+1-(\pi+\overline{\pi}),

where
\pi\overline{\pi}=p
and
\pi
reduces mod
p
to the Frobenius (cf.
[
JM95
, p. 1]
). For the various choices of quadratic imaginary field
K
, the correct choice of prime element
\pi
can be computed. We list a few examples.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose
D=-3
, that is,
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
, and
E
is given by the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+c
with
c\in\mathbb{Z}
. It follows by
[
Raj69
, Theorem 1]
that

(2.5)

|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p+1-\left(\frac{4c}{\pi_{0}}\right)_{6}\cdot\overline{\pi}_{0}-\left(\frac{4c}{\overline{\pi}_{0}}\right)_{6}\cdot\pi_{0},

where
\pi_{0}
is a prime element of
K
such that
\pi_{0}\overline{\pi}_{0}=p
and
\pi_{0},\overline{\pi}_{0}
are normalized, that is, they are congruent to
1\mod 3
. Here
\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{\pi_{0}}\right)_{6}=a^{\frac{p-1}{6}}(\text{mod}\;\pi_{0})
is the sixth power residue symbol. The symbol
\displaystyle\left(\frac{4c}{\pi_{0}}\right)_{6}
can take any value within the set of units
\{\pm 1,\pm\zeta_{3},\pm\zeta_{3}^{2}\}
of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
. In particular, when
p
is of the form
4p=1+3v^{2}
for some
v\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are exactly
\frac{p-1}{6}
different reductions
\overline{E_{p}}
which satisfy
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
. Primes of this form include
p=7,37,61,\ldots
. For example, when
p=7
, the family of elliptic curves
\{E_{n}:y^{2}=x^{3}-2+7n,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfies the desired equality. Later in this article we will make this family a case study to construct examples that satisfy
section 1
. A second example is given for
p=61
and the family
\{E_{t}:y^{2}=x^{3}+2+61t,t\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Example 2.6
.

Suppose
D=-11
. A sage computation shows that for
p=223
the family
\{E_{s}:y^{2}=x^{3}-1056x+13552+223s,s\in\mathbb{Z}\}
satisfies the desired equality. Another class of examples is given for
D=-19
,
p=43
and
\{E_{l}:y^{2}=x^{3}-152x+722+43l,l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

Example 2.7
.

When
D=-43,-67,-163
, and
E
is given by a CM Weierstrass equation with parameter
c
(cf.
[
JM95
, Tableau 1]

2.2.

-cycles and Somekawa

Let

where

where

is the Somekawa

-group attached to the Jacobian varieties
J_{1},J_{2}
of
C_{1},C_{2}
. The group

, defined by K. Kato and Somekawa in
[

]
, is a quotient of
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{L/k\text{ finite}}J_{1}(L)\otimes J_{2}(L)
by two relations. The first relation is known as
projection formula
and the second as

. In this article we won’t make explicit use of these relations, and hence we omit the precise definition (see
[
RS00
, Definition 2.1.1]
and the footnote on p. 10 for a correction to Somekawa’s original definition).

.

Suppose that

is the self-product of an elliptic curve

over

2.3.
Weak Approximation for
0
-cycles

Let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over a number field
F
. Let
v\in\Omega_{f}(F)
. There is a local pairing,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}:\CH_{0}(X_{v})\times\Br(X_{v})\rightarrow\Br(F_{v})\simeq\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

called the
Brauer-Manin pairing
defined as follows. For a closed point
P\in X_{v}
and a Brauer class
\alpha\in\Br(X_{v})
,
the pull-back of
\alpha
along
P

is denoted by
\alpha(P)\in\Br(F_{v}(P))
, where
F_{v}(P)
is the residue field of
P
.
The pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
is defined on points by
\langle P,\alpha\rangle_{v}:=\mathrm{Cor}_{F_{v}(P)/F_{v}}(\alpha(P))
and it factors through rational equivalence (cf.
[
CT95
, p. 4]
). Here
\Cor_{F_{v}(P)/F_{v}}
is the Corestriction map of Galois cohomology (cf.
[
Ser79
, VIII.2]
).

Definition 2.12
.

Suppose that
F
is totally imaginary. The adelic Chow group
\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)
is defined to be the product
\displaystyle\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X):=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}(F)}\CH_{0}(X_{v})
. Similarly, we define
\displaystyle F^{1}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}(F)}F^{1}(X_{v})
and
\displaystyle F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{f}(F)}F^{2}(X_{v})
.

When
F
is not totally imaginary, it follows by
[
CT95
, Théorème 1.3]
that the group
\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)
has a small (
2
-torsion) contribution from the infinite real places. In this article we will mainly be working over totally imaginary number fields, and hence we omit the more general definition. For more details see
[
GH21
, Def. 5.2]
.

The local pairings induce a global pairing,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)\times\Br(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

defined by
\langle(z_{v})_{v},\alpha\rangle=\sum_{v}\inv_{v}(\langle z_{v},\iota_{v}^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle_{v})
, where
\iota_{v}^{\star}
is the pullback of
\iota_{v}:X_{v}\to X
.
The short exact sequence of global class field theory,

0\rightarrow\Br(F)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in\Omega}\Br(F_{v})\xrightarrow{\sum\inv_{v}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0,

implies that the group
\CH_{0}(X)
lies in the left kernel of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Thus, we obtain a complex,

(2.13)

\CH_{0}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
\Delta
is the diagonal map.
section 1
then predicts that the complex (
2.13
) becomes exact after passing to the completions; namely that
the induced complex

(2.14)

\widehat{\CH_{0}(X)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}}(X)\rightarrow\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

is exact.

Next we consider the filtration
\Br(X)\supset\Br_{1}(X)\supset\Br_{0}(X)
induced by the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, where
\Br_{1}(X):=\ker(\Br(X)\rightarrow\Br(X_{\overline{F}}))
is the
algebraic Brauer group
of
X
, and
\Br_{0}(X)=\img(\Br(F)\to\Br(X))
are the constants. When
X
has a
F
-rational point, the exactness of (
2.14
) is reduced to the exactness of the induced complex,

(2.15)

\widehat{F^{1}(X)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widehat{F^{1}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\Hom(\Br(X)/\Br_{0}(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

When
X
is a product of elliptic curves more can be said.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
X=E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{d}
be a product of elliptic curves over a number field
F
. Let
G_{F}
be the absolute Galois group of
F
. Suppose that the Néron-Severi group
\NS(X_{\overline{F}})
of the base change to the algebraic closure of
F
has trivial
G_{F}
-action. Then the restriction of the map
\varepsilon:F^{1}(X)\to\Hom(\Br(X)/\Br_{0}(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
to the Albanese kernel
F^{2}(X)
factors through the group
\Hom(\Br(X)/\Br_{1}(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

We need to show that for every
z\in F^{2}(X)
and every
\alpha\in\Br_{1}(X)/\Br_{0}(X)
, it follows
\langle z,\alpha\rangle=0
. A direct computation of the Hochshild-Serre spectral sequence gives an isomorphism
\Br_{1}(X)/\Br_{0}(X)\simeq H^{1}(F,\Pic(X_{\overline{F}}))
. The
G_{F}
-module
\Pic(X_{\overline{F}})
fits into a (splitting) short exact sequence

0\to\Pic^{0}(X_{\overline{F}}))\to\Pic(X_{\overline{F}}))\to\NS(X_{\overline{F}}))\to 0.

Since
X
is an abelian variety, the group
\NS(X_{\overline{F}})
is torsion free, and hence a finitely generated free abelian group. Since we assumed that
G_{F}
acts trivially on it, it follows that
H^{1}(F,\NS(X_{\overline{F}}))=0
. Thus, we obtain an isomorphism

H^{1}(F,\Pic(X_{\overline{F}}))\simeq H^{1}(F,X(\overline{F}))\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}H^{1}(k,E_{i}).

For
i=1,\ldots,d
, let
\pr_{i}:X\to E_{i}
be the projection. The map
\pr_{i}
is proper and it induces a pushforward
\pr_{i\star}:\CH_{0}(X)\to\CH_{0}(E_{i})
on Chow groups and a pullback
\pr_{i}^{\star}:\Br(E_{i})\to\Br(X)
on Brauer groups. Since the Brauer-Manin pairing is defined just by evaluation, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\CH_{0,\mathbf{A}}(E_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\Br(E_{i}),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{i\star}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(\pr_{i}^{\star},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})}

That is, for
z\in\CH_{0}(X)
and
\beta\in\Br(E_{i})
we have an equality

\langle z,\pr_{i}^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle=\langle\pr_{i\star}(z),\alpha\rangle.

Moreover, both homomorphisms preserve the two piece filtrations of
\CH_{0}
and
\Br
. The pullbacks
p_{i}^{\star}

induce a homomorphism

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}\Br_{1}(E_{i})/\Br_{0}(E_{i})\xrightarrow{\oplus\pr_{i}^{\star}}\Br_{1}(X)/\Br_{0}(X).

Our previous analysis shows that this is in fact an isomorphism. We compute

\langle z,\pr_{i}^{\star}(\alpha)\rangle=\langle\pr_{i\star}(z),\alpha\rangle=0.

The vanishing follows, because
\pr_{i\star}(z)\in F^{2}(E_{i})=0
, since
E_{i}
is a curve.

∎

3.1.
Proof of

In this subsection
k
will denote a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We will often assume that

be a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over

the maximal divisible subgroup of the Albenese kernel

and by

the quotient
F^{2}(X)/F^{2}(X)_{\dv}

F^{2}(X)=F^{2}(X)_{\dv}\oplus F^{2}(X)_{\nd}.

The subgroup

is expected to be finite (cf.
[
RS00
, Conjecture 3.5.4]
, see also
[
CT95
, 1.4(g)]
). When
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
is the product of two elliptic curves, this conjecture has been established in a large number of cases.
In particular, the following are true.

(\mathrm{1})

When
X
has good reduction, the group
F^{2}(X)
is
m
-divisible for every integer
m
coprime to
p
(
[
RS00
, Theorem 3.5]
).

(\mathrm{2})

Suppose
p\geq 3
,
X
has split semistable reduction and at most one of the curves has good supersingular reduction. Then the group
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}
is finite (
[
RS00
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
GL22
, Theorem 1.2]
).

(\mathrm{3})

Suppose
p\geq 3
,
k
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
X
has good reduction and at most one of the curves has good supersingular reduction. Then
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}=0
(
[
GH21
, Theorem 1.4]
).

The finiteness of
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}
yields an equality
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}=\widehat{F^{2}(X)}
.
In the special case when

has good reduction, it follows that the group
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}
has
p
-power order. Suppose
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}=p^{N}
for some

. Then
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}\simeq F^{2}(X)/p^{N}
.

We are now ready to prove
section 1
, which we restate here.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X=E_{1}\times E_{2}
be a product of elliptic curves over a number field
F
. Suppose that the action of the absolute Galois group
G_{F}
on the Néron-Severi group
\NS(X_{\overline{F}})
is trivial. Then, there is an infinite set
T
of rational primes
p
for which the group
\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}
vanishes. In particular, the complex
1.4
is exact for every
p\in T
. If we further assume that at least one of the curves does not have potential complex multiplication, then the complement
S
of
T
is a thin set of primes.

Proof.

The proof will be along the lines of
[
GH21
, Lemma 5.9]
.
Let
p
be an odd prime and suppose that for every place
v
above
p
the surface
X_{v}
has good reduction.
It follows by
subsection 3.1
(1) that the only factors of
\displaystyle F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)
that might not be
p
-divisible correspond to those places
v
above
p
and to the places of bad reduction. Let
\Lambda=\{v\text{ place of bad reduction}\}
. Then
for every

we have,

F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}=\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X)/p^{n}\times\prod_{v\in\Lambda}F^{2}(X)/p^{n}.

Let
v\in\Lambda
be a place of bad reduction. Then there exists a finite extension
L_{v}/F_{v}
such that the base change

has split semistable reduction. This means that either both elliptic curves

have good reduction, or if any of them has bad reduction, then it has split multiplicative reduction. In the former case, since
v\nmid p
, it follows by
[
RS00
, Theorem 3.5]
that the group
F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})
is
p
-divisible. In the latter case, it follows by
[
GL22
, Theorem 1.2]
) that the group
F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}
is finite. Write
N_{v}
for its order. To unify notation, we set
N_{v}=1
for the case of potentially good reduction. Consider the following positive integer,

M=\prod_{v\in\Lambda}[L_{v}:F_{v}]\prod_{v\in\Lambda}N_{v}.

We claim that for every prime
p
which is coprime to
M
and for every
v\in\Lambda
the group
F^{2}(X_{v})
is
p
-divisible. Consider the projection
X_{L_{v}}\xrightarrow{\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}}}X_{v}
, and let
\CH_{0}(X_{L_{v}})\xrightarrow{\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}\star}}\CH_{0}(X_{v})
, and
\CH_{0}(X_{v})\xrightarrow{\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}}^{\star}}\CH_{0}(X_{L_{v}})
be the induced push-forward and pull back maps respectively. Then we have an equality
\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}\star}\circ\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}}^{\star}=[L_{v}:F_{v}]
. Since

is coprime to

, it follows that the pull back map induces an injective map of

-vector spaces

F^{2}(X_{v})/p\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{L_{v}/F_{v}}^{\star}}}{{\hookrightarrow}}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})/p.

By our choice of

, it follows that the group
F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})
is
p
-divisible, and hence so is
F^{2}(X_{v})
.

As a conclusion, for every prime
p\nmid M
such that for every place
v
above
p
the surface
X_{v}
has good reduction, we have an isomorphism

\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}\simeq\prod_{v|p}\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}(X_{v})/p^{n}.

Let
T_{0}
be the set of odd primes

such that for every place

above
p
the abelian surface

has good reduction, at most one of the curves

. Then it follows by

(2) that for every

and for every place

is finite of

(3.2)

\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X)/p^{n}=\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{v})_{\nd}.

If we further assume that for each place

is unramified, then it follows by
[

that the group

Then for every

and the complex

is exact.

Note that the set

Next suppose that one of the curves does not have potential complex multiplication; without loss of generality, assume

3.2.
Computing the local Albanese kernel over ramified base fields

Throughout this subsection
E
will be an elliptic curve defined over a finite extension
k
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We assume that
E
has
good ordinary reduction
. We will denote by
\widehat{E}
the formal group of
E
and by
\overline{E}
the reduction of
E
, which is an ordinary elliptic curve over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{k}
. Moreover, we will denote by

the absolute ramification index of

. The purpose of this subsection is to obtain some explicit information on the group
F^{2}(X)_{\nd}=\widehat{F^{2}(X)}
.

3.2.1.
Decomposition of local points

We first obtain some information on points
P\in E(k)
, which will be of use in
subsection 4.2
.
Since the elliptic curve
E
has good ordinary reduction, for every integer
n\geq 1
we have a short exact sequence of
\Gal(\overline{k}/k)
-modules.

(3.3)

0\rightarrow\widehat{E}[p^{n}]\rightarrow E[p^{n}]\rightarrow\overline{E}[p^{n}]\rightarrow 0,

where as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules both
\widehat{E}[p^{n}]
and
\overline{E}[p^{n}]
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}
. The sequence
3.3
is also known as the

exact sequence. In general this sequence does not split, but it does when

, and unconditionally on
k
if
E
has complex multiplication (
[
Ser89
, A.2.4]
). In particular it splits when
E
satisfies the assumptions of
subsection 2.1
.
We assume we are in the CM situation. We additionally suppose that
k
is an unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\overline{E}[p]\subset\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
.
We consider the exact sequence

(3.4)

0\rightarrow\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}_{k})\rightarrow E(k)\xrightarrow{r}\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\rightarrow 0,

where
E(k)\xrightarrow{r}\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
is the reduction map. The group
\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
is finite, thus it decomposes as
\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\simeq\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\{p\}\oplus\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\{m\}
, for some large enough integer

coprime to
p
. Because

has good reduction, it follows by the criterion of Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich (
[
Sil09
, Theorem 7.1]
that the coprime-to-

torsion subgroup of

is isomorphic to
\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\{m\}
. Moreover, since
\overline{E}
is an ordinary elliptic curve and we assumed
\overline{E}[p]\subset\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})
, it follows that
\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\{p\}\simeq\overline{E}[p^{N_{0}}]
for some integer
N_{0}\geq 1
. In particular, the group
\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})\{p\}
is cyclic of order

. The splitting of

implies that the map
E[p^{N_{0}}](k)\xrightarrow{r}\overline{E}[p^{N_{0}}](\mathbb{F}_{k})
is surjective. Since we assumed that

is unramified, it follows by
[

, Theorem 6.1]
that
\widehat{E}[p]=0
, and hence this map is an isomorphism. We conclude that the reduction map

induces an isomorphism on torsion subgroups

and hence the short exact sequence (

) has a natural splitting. Moreover, if

is any finite extension, this splitting commutes with the restriction map

.
It also induces a split short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}_{k})/p\rightarrow E(k)/p\xrightarrow{r}\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})/p\rightarrow 0.

This splitting can be made more explicit as follows. We have an isomorphism,

\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})/p\simeq\overline{E}[p^{N_{0}}]/\overline{E}[p^{N_{0}-1}]\simeq\overline{E}[p].

We fix a generator
\overline{P}_{0}
of
\overline{E}[p^{N_{0}}](\mathbb{F}_{k})
and its unique lift

. Let
P\in E(k)
. There exists an integer
c\in\{0,1,\ldots,p^{N_{0}}-1\}
such that
r(P\mod p)-r(cP_{0}\mod p)=0
. This implies that
P
has an expression as
P=\widehat{P}+cP_{0}+pQ
for some points
Q\in E(k),\widehat{P}\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}_{k})
.

Notation 3.5
.

From now we will abuse notation and for a point
P\in E(k)
we will denote also by
P
its image in
E(k)/p
. The above discussion shows that
P
has a unique decomposition as
P=(\widehat{P},\overline{P})
where
\widehat{P}\in\widehat{E}(\mathfrak{m}_{k})/p
and
\overline{P}:=r(cP_{0}\mod p)\in\overline{E}(\mathbb{F}_{k})/p
. It is clear by the construction that this decomposition is compatible with the restriction map
\res_{L/k}
for finite extensions
L/k
. Namely,
\res_{L/k}(P)=(\res_{L/k}(\widehat{P}),\res_{\mathbb{F}_{L}/\mathbb{F}_{k}}(\overline{P}))
.

3.2.2.
Explicit isomorphism

In this subsection we consider a finite extension
L/k
such that

E[p^{n}]\subset E(L)
for some
n\geq 1
. It follows by
[
RS00
, Section 4]
(see also
[
Hir16
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
HH13
, Theorem 3.4]
) that we have an isomorphism

(3.6)

F^{2}(X_{L})_{\nd}/p^{n}=F^{2}(X_{L})/p^{n}\simeq K_{2}(L;E)/p^{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}.

Let
N=\max\{n\geq 1:E[p^{n}]\subset E(L)\}
. If we further assume that the extension
L(E[p^{N+1}])/L
has wild ramification, it follows by
[
GL22
, Theorem 3.14]
that we have an isomorphism

(3.7)

\widehat{F^{2}(X_{L})}=F^{2}(X_{L})_{\nd}=F^{2}(X_{L})/p^{N}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}.

The case of interest to us is when
N=1
. To prove
section 1
, the above information is enough. However, in order to construct global
0
-cycles and prove
section 1
, we need to recall the explicit construction of the isomorphism
K_{2}(L;E_{L})/p\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. We are particularly interested in describing necessary and sufficient conditions for a symbol
\{P,Q\}_{L/L}\in K_{2}(L;E_{L})/p
to be nontrivial.

The group
K_{2}(L;E_{L})
is defined to be the quotient of the Mackey product
(E_{L}\otimes^{M}E_{L})(L)
(cf.
[
GH21
, Section 2.1]
) modulo Weil reciprocity. It follows by
[
RS00
]
(see also
[
Hir16
, Theorem 4.2]
) that when
E[p]\subset E(L)
we have an isomorphism

K_{2}(L;E_{L})\simeq(E_{L}\otimes^{M}E_{L})(L)/p.

The Mackey functor
E_{L}/p
has a direct decomposition

E_{L}/p\simeq\widehat{E_{L}}/p\oplus[E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}]/p,
induced by the splitting short exact sequences

0\to\widehat{E_{L}}(m_{L^{\prime}})/p\to E_{L}(L^{\prime})/p\to E_{L}(\mathbb{F}_{L^{\prime}}/p)\to 0,

where
L^{\prime}/L
is any finite extension. For the definition of the Mackey functor
[E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}]
we refer to
[
GL22
, Proof of Theorem 3.14]
. It follows by
[
RS00
, Lemma 3.4.2]
and
[
GL22
, Proof of Theorem 3.1.4]
respectively that the Mackey products
([E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}]\otimes^{M}[E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}])(k)
,
(\widehat{E_{L}}\otimes^{M}[E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}])(L)/p,
and
([E_{L}/\widehat{E_{L}}]\otimes^{M}E_{L})(k)/p
all vanish. Thus we have an isomorphism

K_{2}(L;E_{L})/p\simeq(\widehat{E_{L}}\otimes^{M}\widehat{E_{L}})(k)/p=(\widehat{E_{L}}/p\otimes^{M}\widehat{E_{L}}/p)(k).

The upshot is the following, which will be used in
subsection 4.2
. Let
k/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a finite unramified extension like in the previous subsection and let
P\in E(k)/p
. Consider the restriction of
P
in
E(L)/p
, which for simplicity we will denote again by
P
. Consider the decomposition
P=(\widehat{P},\overline{P})
as in notation (
3.5
). Let
\widehat{Q}\in\widehat{E_{L}}(\mathfrak{m}_{L})/p
. Then we have,

(3.8)

\{P,\widehat{Q}\}_{L/L}=\{\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}\}_{L/L}\in K_{2}(L;E_{L})/p.

Next we analyze the Mackey functor
\widehat{E_{L}}/p
.

The inclusion
E[p]\subset E(L)
implies
\mu_{p}\subset L^{\times}
, and
\widehat{E}[p]\subset\widehat{E_{L}}(\mathfrak{m}_{L})
. We fix a primitive
p
-th root of unity
\zeta_{p}\in L^{\times}
and a non-canonical isomorphism of
\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
-modules

\widehat{E}[p]\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\simeq\mu_{p}.
This induces an isomorphism

H^{1}(L,\widehat{E_{L}}[p])\simeq H^{1}(L,\mu_{p})=L^{\times}/L^{\times p}.

We consider the connecting homomorphism of the Kummer sequence for
\widehat{E_{L}}
(cf.
[
GH21
, Lemma 3.5]
)

\delta_{L}:\widehat{E_{L}}(L)/p\hookrightarrow H^{1}(L,\widehat{E_{L}}[p]).

Let
U_{L}^{1}
be the group of
1
-units of
L^{\times}
with its natural filtration
\{U_{L}^{i}=1+\mathfrak{m}_{L}^{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and define

\overline{U}_{L}^{i}=\img(U_{L}^{i}\rightarrow L^{\times}/L^{\times p}),\;\;i\geq 1.

It then follows by
[
Kaw02
, Theorem 2.1.6]
, (see also
[
GH21
, Prop. 3.13]
) that the homomorphism
\delta_{L}
induces an isomorphism

(3.9)

\delta_{L}:\widehat{E_{L}}(L)/p\xrightarrow{\simeq}\overline{U}_{L}^{1},

and the same holds over any finite extension
L^{\prime}/L
. In fact we have an isomorphism as Mackey functors
\widehat{E_{L}}/p\simeq\overline{U}_{L}^{1}
.

Additionally,
\delta_{L}
is a filtered isomorphism in the following sense. The formal group
\widehat{E_{L}}(\mathfrak{m}_{L})
has a natural filtration

\widehat{E_{L}}^{i}(L):=\widehat{E_{L}}(\mathfrak{m}_{L}^{i})
, for
i\geq 1
. This induces a filtration on the group
\widehat{E_{L}}(\mathfrak{m}_{L})/[p]=\widehat{E_{L}}^{1}(L)/p
by defining (cf.
[
GH21
, Definition 3.10]
),

\mathcal{D}^{i}:=\frac{\widehat{E_{L}}^{i}(L)}{[p]\widehat{E_{L}}(L)\cap\widehat{E_{L}}^{i}(L)},\;\;i\geq 1.

Then the isomorphism
3.9
has the property that
\delta_{L}(\mathcal{D}^{i})\subset\overline{U}_{L}^{i}
, for all
i\geq 1
. Moreover, it is functorial with respect to norm maps associated with finite extensions
L^{\prime}/L
.

Since
\mu_{p}\subset L^{\times}
, we have isomorphisms
H^{2}(L,\mu_{p}\otimes\mu_{p})\simeq H^{2}(L,\mu_{p})\simeq\Br(L)_{p}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p
. For
a,b\in L^{\times}/L^{\times p}
write
(a,b)_{p}\in\Br(L)_{p}
for the cyclic symbol algebra generated by
a,b
. Then we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle s_{p}:(\widehat{E_{L}}/p\otimes^{M}\widehat{E_{L}}/p)(k)\rightarrow H^{2}(L,\mu_{p})=\Br(L)_{p}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\{\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}\}_{L/L}\mapsto(\delta(\widehat{P}),\delta(\widehat{Q}))_{p},

known as
the generalized Galois symbol
. For a proof of the injectivity of
s_{p}
see for example
[
Hir16
, Theorem 4.2]
.

3.2.3.
Criterion for nontriviality

It follows that a symbol
\{\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}\}_{L/L}
is nonzero in
K_{2}(L;E_{L})/p
if and only if
\delta_{L}(\widehat{P})
is not in the image of the norm map

N:L\left(\delta_{L}(\widehat{Q})^{1/p}\right)^{\times}\rightarrow L^{\times},

or equivalently if and only if
\widehat{P}
is not in the image of the norm map

N:\widehat{E_{L}}\left(L\left(\frac{1}{p}\widehat{Q}\right)\right)/p\rightarrow\widehat{E_{L}}(L)/p.

Here we denoted by
L\left(\frac{1}{p}\widehat{Q}\right)
the smallest Galois extension of
L
over which there exists a point
\widehat{Q}^{\prime}
such that
p\widehat{Q}^{\prime}=\widehat{Q}
. The same equivalence holds with the roles of
\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}
interchanged.

4.
Self-product of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication

In this subsection we are going to prove theorems (
1
) and (
1
). Throughout this section we are making the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

K
will be a quadratic imaginary field of class number one.
E
will be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
or
K
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
p\geq 5
will be an ordinary prime for
E
. Moreover, we fix a factoring
p=\pi\overline{\pi}
into prime elements of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that the endomorphism
\pi:E_{K}\rightarrow E_{K}
reduces to the Frobenius automorphism
\phi_{p}:\overline{E_{p}}\rightarrow\overline{E_{p}}
.

4.1.
Local
0
-cycles orthogonal to the Brauer group

We are now ready to prove
section 1
, which we restate here.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X=E\times E
. Suppose there exists a finite Galois extension
F_{0}/K
of degree
n<p-1
such that there is a unique inert place
w
of
F_{0}
above
\pi
with the property that
p
divides
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{w})|
, where
\mathbb{F}_{w}
is the residue field of
F_{0}
at
w
.
Consider the extension
L:=F_{0}\cdot K(E[\pi])
. Then the group
\displaystyle\varprojlim\limits_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X_{L})/p^{n}
has the following properties.

(\mathrm{1})

It is equal to
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
.

(\mathrm{2})

It is orthogonal to the transcendental Brauer group
\displaystyle\frac{\Br(X_{L})\{p\}}{\Br_{1}(X_{L})\{p\}}
.

Proof.

The proof involves three main steps.

Claim 1:
There is an isomorphism
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
.

It follows by
[
Rub99
, Corollary 5.20 (ii), (iii)]
that
K(E[\pi])/K
is a Galois extension of degree
p-1
and it is totally ramified above
\mathfrak{p}=(\pi)
. Since
F_{0}/K
is unramified above
\pi
, the extensions
F_{0},K(E[\pi])
are totally disjoint and hence
\Gal(L/K)\simeq\Gal(F_{0}/K)\times\Gal(K(E[\pi])/K)
. In particular,
L/K
is a Galois extension of degree
n(p-1)<(p-1)^{2}
, and there exists a unique place
v
of
L
above
\pi
with ramification index
p-1
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{w_{0}}
of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Moreover, the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, and hence there exists a unique place
\overline{v}
above
\overline{\pi}
with the same properties and these are precisely the places of
L
that lie above
p
. The extensions
L_{v}
and
L_{\overline{v}}
are the same. It is enough therefore to prove an isomorphism
\widehat{F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

We will simply denote by
E_{v}
(resp.
E_{\overline{v}}
) the base change
E_{L_{v}}
(resp.
E_{L_{\overline{v}}}
) which is an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction over the
p
-adic field
L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We will denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L_{v}}
,
\widehat{E_{v}}
will be the formal group of
E_{v}
and
\overline{E_{v}}
the reduction.
We claim that
E_{v}[p]\subset E_{v}(L_{v})
. As noted in
subsection 2.1
, the subgroup
E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[\pi]
of
E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[p]
coincides with the
p
-torsion of the formal group
\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}[p]
. It follows that
E_{v}[\pi]
coincides with
\widehat{E_{v}}[p]
, and hence it is
L_{v}
-rational.
Since the residue field of
L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is
\mathbb{F}_{w_{0}}
, the assumption that
p
divides
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{w_{0}})|
implies that
\overline{E_{p}}[p]\subseteq\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{w_{0}})
. The inclusion
E_{v}[p]\subset E_{v}(L_{v})
then follows by the splitting short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\widehat{E_{v}}[p]\rightarrow E_{v}[p]\rightarrow\overline{E_{p}}[p]\rightarrow 0.

The desired isomorphism
\widehat{F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
follows by (
3.7
). More precisely, the above argument shows that we have an inequality
N\geq 1
. We claim that
N=1
. It is enough to show that the extension
L_{v}(E[p^{2}])/L_{v}(E[p])
has wild ramification. But this is clear, since
L_{v}(E[p^{2}])
contains
L_{v}(\mu_{p^{2}})
, which has absolute ramification index dividing
p(p-1)
, while
L_{v}=L_{v}(E[p])
has absolute ramification index
p-1
.

Claim 2:
There is an isomorphism
\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}F^{2}_{\mathbf{A}}(X_{L})/p^{n}\simeq\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}
.

We use the notation introduced in the proof of
subsection 3.1
. Namely, let

M=\prod_{v_{0}\in\Lambda}n_{v_{0}}\prod_{v_{0}\in\Lambda}N_{v_{0}},

where
\Lambda
is the set of all bad reduction places of
E_{L}
,
n_{v_{0}}
is the degree of an extension
L^{\prime}_{v_{0}}/L_{v_{0}}
over which the elliptic curve
E_{L^{\prime}_{v_{0}}}
attains split semistable reduction and
N_{v_{0}}=1
in the potentially good reduction case,
N_{v_{0}}=|F^{2}(X_{L^{\prime}_{v_{0}}})_{\nd}|
otherwise. To prove the claim, it is enough to show that
p
is coprime to
M
, because then the proof of
subsection 3.1
gives us the desired isomorphism (cf.
3.2
).
Since the elliptic curve
E
has complex multiplication, it follows by
[
ST68
, Theorem 7]
that the set
\Lambda
only contains places of potentially good reduction. Thus,
N_{v_{0}}=1
for every
v_{0}\in\Lambda
.
It remains to show that for each
v_{0}\in\Lambda
there exists a finite extension
L^{\prime}_{v_{0}}/L_{v_{0}}
of degree coprime to
p
over which
E
attains good reduction. Let
v_{0}\in\Lambda
and
l
be the rational prime lying below
v_{0}
. It follows by
[
Rub99
, Corollary 5.22]
that there exists an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
E^{\prime}
has good reduction at
l
and
E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\simeq E^{\prime}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
. But any two elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
become isomorphic over a degree
6
extension. Since we assumed that
p\geq 5
, the claim follows.

Claim 3:
The group
\displaystyle\frac{\Br(X_{L})\{p\}}{\Br_{1}(X_{L})\{p\}}
is trivial.

The group
\Br(X_{L})/\Br_{1}(X_{L})
is finite by
[
SZ08
, Theorem 1.1]
. More precisely, if
G_{L}=\Gal(\overline{L}/L)
, then Skorobogatov and Zarhin
(
[
SZ12
, Proposition 3.3]
) showed an isomorphism,

\frac{\Br(X_{L})_{p}}{\Br_{1}(X_{L})_{p}}\simeq\frac{\Hom_{G_{L}}(E_{L}[p],E_{L}[p])}{(\Hom(E_{L},E_{L})/p)^{G_{L}}}.

Since
E_{L}
has CM defined over
L
,
(\Hom(E_{L},E_{L})/p)^{G_{L}}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. We claim that this is equal to the numerator
\Hom_{G_{L}}(E_{L}[p],E_{L}[p])
. Since by assumption
n<p-1
, we have proper field extensions
K\subsetneq L\subsetneq K(E[p])
. It follows that
E_{L}[\pi]
is the unique nonzero submodule of
E_{L}[p]
with a trivial
G_{L}
-action. Hence, any
G_{L}
-equivariant homomorphism
f:E_{L}[p]\rightarrow E_{L}[p]
must send
E_{L}[\pi]
to itself. This is precisely a subgroup of rank
2
of
\Hom(E_{L}[p],E_{L}[p])\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{4}
containing
(\Hom(E_{L},E_{L})/p)^{G_{L}}
, and hence it must be equal to it.

∎

Definition 4.3
.

Let
F_{0}/K
be an extension satisfying the assumptions of

\mathbb{F}_{w_{0}}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}

is

and

When we construct global
0

and apply
subsection 4.1
for
F_{0}\cdot K
. When
p||\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
,
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
. In all other cases a minimal

is not uniquely determined. The following proposition shows that the extension

constructed in

is in some sense minimal over which interesting things happen at the places above the ordinary prime

.

Proposition 4.4

(E,p)

subsection 4.1
. Let

be an extension as in

above

. Moreover, since

4.2.
Constructing global
0
-cycles

In this last subsection we investigate the exactness of the complex (
1.4
) in the situation considered in
subsection 4.1
. That is, we are looking for criteria that allow us to lift the group
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}
to global
0
-cycles in
F^{2}(X)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with
D\in\{-1,-2,-3,-7,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163\}
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
such that
E_{K}
has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a totally real extension and suppose that the Mordell-Weil group
E(F)
has positive rank. Let
P\in E(F)
be a point of infinite order. Write
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\omega_{D}]
. Then the points
P,\omega_{D}(P)\in E(K\cdot F)
are
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly independent.

Proof.

We will simply write
\omega
instead of
\omega_{D}
. Note that
\omega(P)\in E(K\cdot F)\setminus E(F)
. Let
\mu(x)=x^{2}-bx+c\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\omega
. Let
\overline{\omega}
be the conjugate of
\omega
, so the equalities
\omega+\overline{\omega}=b
and
\omega\overline{\omega}=c
hold.
Suppose for contradiction that
P,\omega(P)
are
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly dependent. Then we can find
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
nP+m\omega(P)=0
. We may assume that
n,m
are relatively prime, and hence we can find
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
nx+my=1
. The relation
nP+m\omega(P)=0
implies that
nxP+mx\omega(P)=0=nyP+my\omega(P)
. Applying the endomorphism
\overline{\omega}
to the first equality yields
nx\overline{\omega}(P)+mxcP=0
, and hence
nx\omega(P)-(mxc+nxb)P=0
. Adding the latter to the equation
nyP+my\omega(P)=0
gives

\omega(P)=(nxb-ny+mxc)P\in E(F),

which is a contradiction.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

Suppose
D\in\{-3,-7,-11,-19,-43,-67,-163\}
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\omega_{D}]
, where
\displaystyle\omega_{D}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{-D}}{2}
. The minimal polynomial of
\omega_{D}
is
x^{2}+x+\displaystyle\frac{1+D}{4}
so that in the notation of
subsection 4.2
we have
b=-1
,
c=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\frac{D}{4}
. Let
p
be an odd prime,
p\neq-D
. Then the system of equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}x^{2}=c\\
2x=-1\end{array}\right.
has no solution over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For, if
x
were a solution, then the solution would be
x=-1/2\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, giving
4c\equiv 1\mod p\Leftrightarrow D\equiv 0\mod p
. This simple observation will be used in the proof of the following
section 4
.

Proof of lifting when a “good” rational point exists

In this section we give a first criterion to lift the local
0
-cycles to global. Our criterion relies only on the existence of a “good” rational point
P
. Before proving our main results, we briefly describe our methodology.

From now on we assume we are in the set-up of
subsection 4.1
with the extension
F_{0}
minimal.
The base change
X_{L}
, where
L=F_{0}\cdot K(E[\pi])
, contains a nontrivial
L
-rational
\pi
-torsion point
A\in E[\pi](L)
. Our strategy is to examine when this torsion point can be used to construct global
0
-cycles.

We say that a point
P\in E(F_{0})
is good if the
0
-cycle
z_{1}=[A,P]-[A,O]-[O,P]+[O,O]\in F^{2}(X_{L})
induces a nontrivial element
(z_{1v},z_{1\overline{v}})\in\displaystyle F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\oplus F^{2}(X_{L_{\overline{v}}})_{\nd}
. When this happens, we show that the
0
-cycle
z_{2}=[A,\omega(P)]-[A,O]-[O,\omega(P)]+[O,O]
induces a second
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearly independent element of
\displaystyle F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\oplus F^{2}(X_{L_{\overline{v}}})_{\nd}
. To find an explicit condition that guarantees

(z_{1v},z_{1\overline{v}})\neq(0,0)
, we will use the nonvanishing criterion from
subsubsection 3.2.3
. From now on we identify the
0
-cycle
z_{1}
with the symbol
\{A,P\}_{L/L}\in K_{2}(L;E)
and we denote by
\{A_{v},P_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}
the corresponding element of
K_{2}(L_{v};E_{v})
. We will abuse notation and write
\{A_{v},P_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}
for the class of this symbol modulo
pK_{2}(L_{v};E_{v})
. We recall from (
3.8
) that we have an equality

\{A_{v},P_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}=\{\widehat{A_{v}},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}=\{A_{v},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}},

where
P_{v}=(\widehat{P}_{v},\overline{P_{v}})
is the decomposition of
P
modulo
pE(L_{v})
as in (
3.5
). Here we used the fact that
A_{v}\in\widehat{E_{v}}[p]
.

The following
section 4
develops the above idea when
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
. In this case we are looking for a point
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
inducing the desired symbol
\{A,P\}_{L/L}
. For such a point we will denote by
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=(\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\overline{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})\in\widehat{E}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p\oplus\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
the corresponding local point.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose
p
is a prime and
E
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the assumptions of
subsection 4.1
. Assume further that
|\overline{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
, and consider the extension
L=K(E[\pi])
constructed in
subsection 4.1
. Suppose that the Mordell-Weil group
E(\mathbb{Q})
has positive rank and there exists a global point
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
of infinite order such that the induced local point
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has the property that
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p
is nontrivial.
Then the global
p
-torsion
0
-cycles
z_{1},z_{2}\in F^{2}(X_{L})
induced by
A,P
and
A,\omega(P)
respectively lift the group
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Proof.

The assumption
|\overline{E}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
implies that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with
D\equiv 1\mod 4
. Let
\mu(x)=x^{2}+x+c\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be the minimal polynomial of
\omega=\omega_{D}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Claim 1:
The symbol
\{A_{v},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}\in K_{2}(L_{v};E_{v})/p
is nontrivial.

To prove the claim, we consider the Kummer extension
k_{0}=L_{v}\left(\delta(A_{v})^{1/p}\right)=L_{v}\left(\frac{1}{p}A_{v}\right)
. Recall from
subsubsection 3.2.3
that the symbol
\{A_{v},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}
is nontrivial if and only if
\delta(\widehat{P}_{v})
is not in the image of the norm map

N_{k_{0}/L_{v}}:k_{0}^{\times}\rightarrow L_{v}^{\times}.

We consider the filtration
\{\widehat{E_{v}}^{i}(L_{v})\}_{i\geq 1}
of
\widehat{E_{v}}(L_{v})
and the induced filtration
\{\mathcal{D}^{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
of
\widehat{E_{v}}(L_{v})/p
(cf.
subsubsection 3.2.2
). We claim that
A_{v}\in\widehat{E_{v}}^{1}\setminus\widehat{E_{v}}^{2}
and it therefore induces a nontrivial element of
\mathcal{D}^{1}/\mathcal{D}^{2}
. For, let
v(A_{v})
be the valuation of
A_{v}
. Since
A_{v}
is an element of
\widehat{E}(L_{v})
of exact order
p
, it follows by
[
Sil09
, IV.6, Theorem 6.1]
that

1\leq v(A_{v})\leq\frac{v(p)}{p-1}.

The extension
L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is totally ramified of degree
p-1
, and hence it follows that
\frac{v(p)}{p-1}=1
yielding the desired equality
v(A_{v})=1
.

We note that the integer
\frac{v(p)}{p-1}=1
is precisely the integer
t_{0}(\phi)
defined in
[
GH21
, page 11, Lemma 3.5]
where
\phi=[p]
is the
p
-isogeny on
\widehat{E_{v}}
, which is of height one. Using the filtered isomorphism (
3.9
), we deduce that
\delta(A_{v})\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{1}\setminus\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{2}
. It follows by
[
Kaw02
, Lemma 2.1.5]
(see also
[
GH21
, Lemma 3.5]
) that the extension
k_{0}/L_{v}
is totally ramified of degree
p
with the jump in the ramification filtration of
\Gal(k_{0}/L_{v})
happening at
s=p-1
. It then follows by
[
Ser79
, V.3, Corollary 7]
that there exists a unit
u\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}
such that the symbol algebra
(\delta(A_{v}),u)_{p}
is nontrivial in
\Br(L_{v})_{p}
. In fact, we can conclude that
u\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}\setminus\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}
. For, it follows by
[
Ser79
, V.3, Corollaries 2 & 3]
that
(\delta(A_{v}),u^{\prime})_{p}=0
for every
u^{\prime}\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}
. The next key observation is that

(\delta(A_{v}),u)_{p}\neq 0,\text{ {for every unit} }u\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}\setminus\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}.

This is because the residue field of
L_{v}
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and therefore we have an isomorphism (cf.
[
GH21
, Lemma 3.4]
)
\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Thus, in order to prove the claim, it is enough to show
\delta(\widehat{P}_{v})\in\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}\setminus\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}
, or equivalently that
v(\widehat{P}_{v})=p-1
. Consider the inclusion

\res_{L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}:\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p\hookrightarrow\widehat{E_{v}}^{p-1}(L_{v})/p,

sending
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
to
\widehat{P}_{v}
. This map is injective, because we have an equality
N_{L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\circ\res_{L_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=[L_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=p-1
, which is coprime to
p
.
Our last claim will then follow once we show that
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\setminus\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let us assume otherwise that
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and hence
\widehat{P}_{v}\in\widehat{E_{v}}^{2(p-1)}
. Because
2(p-1)>p
, it follows by the filtered isomorphism
3.9
and
[
GH21
, Lemma 3.4]
that
\widehat{P}_{v}
(and hence also
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
) is a multiple of
p
, which contradicts our assumption that
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/p
is nontrivial. This completes the proof of the Claim. The above computation shows that the global
0
-cycle

z_{1}=[A,P]-[A,O]-[O,P]+[O,O]\in F^{2}(X_{L})
has a nontrivial image
(z_{1v},z_{1\overline{v}})
under the diagonal map
F^{2}(X_{L})/p\xrightarrow{\Delta}\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}
.

A byproduct of the proof of Claim 1 is that the following two maps are isomorphisms,

\displaystyle f:\mathcal{D}^{1}/\mathcal{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p,

\displaystyle g:\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;x\mapsto\{x,\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}},

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;y\mapsto\{A_{v},y\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}.

The extensions
L_{v}
and
L_{\overline{v}}
are isomorphic as abstract fields. Therefore Claim 1 holds true also for the symbol
\{A_{\overline{v}},P_{\overline{v}}\}_{L_{\overline{v}}/L_{\overline{v}}}\in K_{2}(L_{\overline{v}},E_{\overline{v}})/p
corresponding to the
0
-cycle
z_{1\overline{v}}
.
Since
P
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, it follows that
P_{\overline{v}}=P_{v}
.
Moreover, the map
f
being an isomorphism implies that there exists some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
such that

f(A_{\overline{v}})=\{A_{\overline{v}},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}=a\{A_{v},\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{\overline{v}}/L_{\overline{v}}}.

In other words, if we fix an isomorphism

F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}/p
sending

z_{1v}
to
1
,
then the diagonal map
\Delta:F^{2}(X_{L})/p\rightarrow F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\oplus F^{2}(X_{L_{\overline{v}}})_{\nd}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
,
z\mapsto(z_{v},z_{\overline{v}})
maps
z_{1}
to the tuple
(1,a)
.

Claim 2:
The element
\Delta(z_{2})
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearly independent from
\Delta(z_{1})=(1,a)
, where
z_{2}
is the
0
-cycle
z_{2}=[A,\omega(P)]-[A,O]-[O,\omega(P)]+[O,O]\in F^{2}(X_{L})
.

The
0
-cycles
z_{2v},z_{2\overline{v}}
correspond to the symbols
\{A_{v},\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}
,
\{A_{\overline{v}},\widehat{\omega(P)}_{\overline{v}}\}_{L_{\overline{v}}/L_{\overline{v}}}
. Since
P\in E(K)\subset E(L)
, the point
\omega(P)_{\overline{v}}
is the restriction of
\omega(P)_{\overline{\mathfrak{p}}}
, and hence it is none other than the complex conjugate,
\overline{\omega}(P)_{v}
. The points
\omega(P),\overline{\omega}(P)
induce elements
\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v},\widehat{\overline{\omega}(P)}_{v}\in\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}
. Since

\omega(P)+\overline{\omega}(P)=-P
, and
\widehat{P}_{v}\in\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}
is nontrivial, at least one of these points induces a nontrivial element of
\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}
. Without loss of generality, assume there exists
m\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
such that
\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v}\equiv m\widehat{P}_{v}\mod\mathcal{D}^{p}
.
The isomorphism
g
then yields an equality

g(\omega(P)_{v})=\{A_{v},\omega(P)_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}=m\{A_{v},P_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}.

To prove linear independence, it is enough to show

\{A_{\overline{v}},\overline{\omega}(P)_{v}\}_{L_{\overline{v}}/L_{\overline{v}}}\neq ma\{A_{v},P_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}.

Notice that we have

\{A_{\overline{v}},\overline{\omega}(P)_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}=a\{A_{v},\overline{\omega}(P)_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}.
Thus, it suffices to prove that the elements
\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v},\widehat{\overline{\omega}(P)}_{v}
induce distinct elements of
\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}
. Suppose for contradiction that they are the same. That is,
\widehat{\omega(P)_{v}}\equiv\widehat{\overline{\omega}}(P)_{v}\equiv m\widehat{P}_{v}\mod\mathcal{D}^{p}
. We have
\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v}+\widehat{\overline{\omega}}(P)_{v}=-\widehat{P}_{v}
, giving
2m=-1
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Moreover,
\widehat{\overline{\omega}(\omega(P))}_{v}=m^{2}\widehat{P}_{v}=c\widehat{P}_{v}
, where
c=(D+1)/4
. That is,
m\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is a solution to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}x^{2}=c\\
2x=-1\end{array}\right.,

which is a contradiction by
subsection 4.2
.

∎

Remark 4.8
.

We believe that the condition of having a “good” rational point
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
is satisfied by a positive proportion of elliptic curves with positive rank over
\mathbb{Q}
. As we saw in the proof of
section 4
, all we need is a point
P
such that
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in\widehat{E}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\setminus\widehat{E}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The latter can be checked computationally as follows.

(\mathrm{1})

Compute the coordinates of a point
P\in E(\mathbb{Q})
of infinite order.

(\mathrm{2})

Compute the coordinates of the induced local point
\widehat{P}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\in E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

(\mathrm{3})

Compute the coordinates of a local nonzero
p
-torsion point
P_{0}\in E[p](\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

(\mathrm{4})

Find a scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that the
x
-coordinate of
P_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}-\lambda P_{0}
has negative valuation.

(\mathrm{5})

If the above valuation is exactly
-2
, then
P
is a point that satisfies the condition of
section 4
.

When the ordinary prime
p
is small enough, this is a computation that can be carried out in SAGE. As a case study, in the appendix
A
we carry out this computation for the family of elliptic curves
\{y^{2}=x^{3}-2+7n:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, and the prime
p=7
.

Remark 4.9
.

An interesting case to consider next is the following. Start with a pair
(E,p)
that satisfies the assumptions of
subsection 4.1
with
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
and consider extensions
M/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
g<p-1
such that
p
splits completely in
M
. Then we still have a vanishing
\Br(X_{L\cdot M})\{p\}/\Br_{1}(X_{L\cdot M})\{p\}=0
, where
L=K(E[\pi])
. An analog of
subsection 4.1
gives

\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{(LM)_{v}})_{\nd}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2g}.

We see that as
g
gets larger, it becomes increasingly harder to find concrete criteria that guarantee the lifting of this group to global
0
-cycles.
For example, consider the curve
E
given by the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+2
and the prime
p=61
. Then
p
splits completely over the degree six extension
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is a root of the polynomial
x^{6}-x^{4}+x^{2}-3x+3
.

The key ingredient in the proof of
section 4
was that the two homomorphisms

\displaystyle f:\mathcal{D}^{1}/\mathcal{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p,

\displaystyle g:\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;x\mapsto\{x,\widehat{P}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}},

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;y\mapsto\{A_{v},y\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}

are isomorphisms and this was because the residue field of
K(E[\pi])
is only
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. A similar method can be extended to many more situations. For example the following corollary describes a generalization to when
L=F_{0}(E[\pi])
with
F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}
a quadratic extension.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
(E,p)
be a pair that satisfies the assumptions of
subsection 4.1
. Suppose that
p\nmid|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
, but
p||\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})|
. Let
F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic extension having a unique inert place
w_{0}
above
p
. Set
k=F_{0}\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Suppose there exists a point
P\in E(F_{0})\setminus E(\mathbb{Q})
of infinite order such that the induced local point
P_{k}\in E(k)
has the property
\widehat{P}_{k}\in\widehat{E_{k}}(k)/p
is nontrivial. Then the global
p
-torsion
0
-cycles
z_{1},z_{2}\in F^{2}(X_{L})
induced by
A,P
and
A,\omega(P)
respectively lift the group
\displaystyle\prod_{v|p}F^{2}(X_{L_{v}})_{\nd}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as in
section 4
.
In this case we have an isomoprhism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces (cf.
[
Kaw02
, Lemma 2.1.4]
)

\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}\simeq\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p.

It follows by
[
Ser79
, V.3, Corollary 7]
that there exists some
B\in\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p}
such that
\displaystyle(\delta(A_{v}),\delta(B))_{p}\neq 0
. We will show that we can take
B=(P_{k})_{v}
. Consider the extension
F=K(E[\pi])
and denote by
v_{0}
the unique place of
F
below
v
. Since
p\nmid|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|
, we have a proper inclusion
F\subsetneq L
. It follows by
subsection 4.1
that
K_{2}(F\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{p};E_{F\mathbb{Q}_{p}})/p=0
. This implies that for every
C\in\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}_{L_{v}}^{p}
which is the restriction of some element defined over
F_{v_{0}}
, it follows
(\delta(A_{v}),\delta(C))_{p}=0
. Since
\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p-1}/\overline{U}_{L_{v}}^{p}
is two dimensional over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, every element
B
that is not in the image of the restriction map
\res_{L_{v}/F_{v_{0}}}
gives rise to a nontrivial symbol. The assumption
P\in E(F_{0})\setminus E(\mathbb{Q})
implies that we can take
B=(P_{k})_{v}
, which yields the desired nonvanishing
\displaystyle(\delta(A_{v}),(P_{k})_{v})_{p}\neq 0
.

The rest of the proof of
section 4
carries through to show that the
0
-cycles
z_{1}=[A,P_{k}]-[A,O]-[O,P_{k}]+[O,O]
and
z_{2}=[A,\omega(P_{k})]-[A,O]-[O,\omega(P_{k})]+[O,O]
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearly independent. The only difference is that we have to consider also the case when
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
with
D\not\equiv 1\mod 4
(see
2.9
for examples of elliptic curves with potential CM by
\mathbb{Z}[i]
which satisfy the assumptions of the corollary). The computation of linear independence is in fact simpler in this case. The minimal polynomial of
\omega
is
\mu(x)=x^{2}-D
, giving
\omega(P)=-\overline{\omega}(P)
. Since
p>2
, it is clear that the elements
\widehat{\omega(P)}_{v},\widehat{\overline{\omega}(P)}_{v}
induce distinct elements of
\mathcal{D}^{p-1}/\mathcal{D}^{p}
.

∎

Extensions

We close this article by discussing alternative criteria of lifting when a “good” rational point does not exist. We focus on the simplest case considered in
section 4
, namely when
|\overline{E_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p
. In this case the point
A\in E[\pi]
can no longer be used to produce global
0
-cycles. One needs to find instead two “good” points
P,Q\in E(L)
such that the induced symbol
\{\widehat{P}_{v},\widehat{Q}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}\neq 0
. To look for such points we need to start with points of infinite order living in intermediate extensions
\mathbb{Q}\subsetneq F\subseteq L=K(E[\pi])
. In the following paragraphs we develop this idea.

Suppose we have a local point
B_{v}\in\widehat{E}(L_{v})
such that
B_{v}\in\widehat{E}^{l}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{l+1}(L_{v})
for some integer
0<l<p
. Then it follows by
[
Ser79
, V.3, Corollaries 2 & 3]
that the map

\mathcal{D}^{p-l}/\mathcal{D}^{p-l+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p,\;\;\;x\mapsto\{x,B_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}

is an isomorphism. Thus, if we can find intermediate extensions
K\subset M,F\subset L
and points
P\in E(M),Q\in E(F)
of infinite order such that the induced local formal points
\widehat{P}_{v},\widehat{Q}_{v}
lie in
\widehat{E}^{l}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{l+1}(L_{v}),
and
\widehat{E}^{p-l}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{p-l+1}(L_{v})
respectively, then the symbol
\{\widehat{P}_{v},\widehat{Q}_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}
is nonvanishing in
K_{2}(L_{v};E_{v})/p
. Then we can produce a second
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linearly independent symbol using the complex multiplication, as we did in the proof of
section 4
. We next describe a potential algorithm to compute such “good points”
P,Q
in a particular example. A similar method can be used more generally, but the algorithm will require more steps.

An extended example

To illustrate our method, we consider the case when the quadratic imaginary field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
, and
p=7
.
We consider the family of elliptic curves

\{E_{n}:y^{2}=x^{3}-2+7n,\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

so that the pair
(7,E_{n})
satisfies the assumptions of
subsection 4.1
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that for a certain value of
n
the condition of
section 4
is not satisfied. That is, there is no good point
P\in E_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
. For simplicity we will write
E=E_{n}
.

The Galois group
G=\Gal(L/K)
is cyclic of order
6
, and hence there exist unique intermediate subfields
K\subset M,F\subset L
such that
[M:K]=\displaystyle 2
and
\displaystyle[F:K]=3
.
Moreover, the extension
L/K
is totally ramified at
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{\overline{p}}
. This implies that there exist unique places
s,\overline{s}
of
M
lying over
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{\overline{p}}
respectively and the extension
M_{s}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is totally ramified of degree
2
. Similarly there exist unique places
t,\overline{t}
of
F
over
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{\overline{p}}
with
F_{t}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
totally ramified of degree
3
.

Suppose we can find global points
B\in E(M)
,
C\in E(F)
such that
\widehat{B}_{s}\in\widehat{E}^{1}(M_{s})\setminus\widehat{E}^{2}(M_{s})
, and
\widehat{C}_{t}\in\widehat{E}^{1}(F_{t})\setminus\widehat{E}^{2}(F_{t})
. Their restrictions
\widehat{B}_{v},\widehat{C}_{v}
in
\widehat{E}(L_{v})
lie in

\widehat{E}^{3}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{4}(L_{v})
and
\widehat{E}^{2}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{3}(L_{v})
respectively.

Next consider the points
\omega(C),\omega^{2}(C)\in E(F)
. Similarly to the proof of
section 4
, at least one of these points induces a formal point in
\widehat{E}^{2}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{3}(L_{v})
. Call this point
C^{\prime}
. Then there exists some scalar
m\in\mathbb{F}_{7}^{\times}
such that
\widehat{C}^{\prime}_{v}\equiv m\widehat{C}_{v}\mod\mathcal{D}^{3}
, This is equivalent to saying that the
x
-coordinate of the formal point
\widehat{C}^{\prime}_{t}-m\widehat{C}_{t}
has strictly smaller valuation than the one of
\widehat{C}_{t}
. If it happens that
\widehat{C}^{\prime}_{t}-m\widehat{C}_{t}\in\widehat{E}^{2}(F_{t})\setminus\widehat{E}^{3}(F_{t})
, then the point
Q=C^{\prime}-mC
is a “good” point. That is,
\widehat{Q}_{v}\in\widehat{E}^{4}(L_{v})\setminus\widehat{E}^{5}(L_{v})
, and since
3+4=7
, our earlier discussion shows that

\{B_{v},Q_{v}\}_{L_{v}/L_{v}}\neq 0\in K_{2}(L_{v};E)/p
.

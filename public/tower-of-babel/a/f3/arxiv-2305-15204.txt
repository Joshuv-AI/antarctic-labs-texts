Lax monoidality for products of enriched higher categories
Tyler Lawson

Abstract
We prove that a lax E n + 1 \mathbb{E}_{n+1} -monoidal functor from 𝒱 \mathcal{V} to 𝒲 \mathcal{W} induces a lax E n \mathbb{E}_{n} -monoidal functor from 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -enriched ∞ \infty -categories to 𝒲 \mathcal{W} -enriched ∞ \infty -categories in the sense of Gepner–Haugseng. We prove this as part of a general-purpose interaction with the Boardman–Vogt tensor product ⊗ \otimes : given a construction that takes an ℰ \mathcal{E} -monoidal ∞ \infty -category to a category expressible in diagrammatic terms, we give a criterion for it to take ( 𝒪 ⊗ ℰ ) (\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathcal{E}) -monoidal ∞ \infty -categories to 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -monoidal ∞ \infty -categories using a “pointwise” monoidal structure.

Introduction

The cases where
n\leq p
are comparatively easy. […] We will, however, give a unified proof which makes these cases look just as bad as the others.

J. W. Milnor,
Topology from the differentiable viewpoint

Orphan Black

Classical enriched categories are functorial, and we can take products of them. More specifically:

•

If
f\colon\thinspace V\to W
is lax monoidal, then we can base-change along
f
, turning any
V
-enriched category
C
into a W-enriched category
f_{*}C
.

•

If
C
is
V
-enriched and
D
is
W
-enriched, then there is an external product
C\boxtimes D
which is a
(V\times W)
-enriched category: we take the product of the object sets and the Hom-objects.

•

These interact well: if
V
is symmetric monoidal, then the product
m\colon\thinspace V\times V\to V
is a lax monoidal functor, and the product
C\mathop{\boxtimes}^{V}D=m_{*}(C\boxtimes D)
is part of a symmetric monoidal structure on
V
-enriched categories.

•

This symmetric monoidal structure is functorial: for a lax symmetric monoidal functor
f\colon\thinspace V\to W
, the functor
f_{*}\colon\thinspace\Cat^{V}\to\Cat^{W}
is also lax symmetric monoidal.

Except for one detail, all of this has already been generalized to enriched
\infty
-categories. For example, Gepner and Haugseng proved that the external product makes the functor
\mathcal{V}\mapsto\Cat_{\infty}^{\mathcal{V}}
lax symmetric monoidal from monoidal
\infty
-categories to
\infty
-categories
[
GH15
, 5.7.11]
. If
\mathcal{V}
is
\mathbb{E}_{n+1}
-monoidal, then they applied this to give
\Cat_{\infty}^{\mathcal{V}}
the structure of an
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal
\infty
-category, and gave generalizations using the Boardman–Vogt style tensor product of
\infty
-operads
[
Lur17
, 2.2.5]
. Alternative approaches were also developed by Heine
[
Hei20
, 7.17]
and Hinich
[
Hin20
, 3.5.3]
.

Unfortunately, while
\Cat_{\infty}^{\mathcal{V}}
is functorial in
lax
monoidal functors
[
GH15
, 5.7.6]
, in the above references the
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal structures on
\Cat_{\infty}^{\mathcal{V}}
are only functorial in
strong
monoidal functors. For instance, Gepner–Haugseng’s proof produces a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}\colon\thinspace\Alg_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}(\Cat_{\infty})\to\Cat_{\infty}.

We can apply
\Alg_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}
to both sides to get a functor

\Alg_{\mathbb{E}_{n+1}}(\Cat_{\infty})\to\Alg_{\mathbb{E}_{n}}(\Cat_{\infty})

by Dunn additivity. However, for any
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{O}
, maps
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}
in
\Alg_{\mathcal{O}}(\Cat_{\infty})
are strong
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal functors rather than lax ones. (Similarly, Heine’s construction in terms of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in monoidal categories, and Hinich’s in terms of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in planar operads, both unwind to functoriality in strict functors.)

The main application of this note is that we have this lax functoriality. For the majority of readers, the following result is probably as much detail as is needed.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}
denote the functor
\mathcal{V}\mapsto\Cat_{\infty}^{\mathcal{V}}
, sending an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoidal
\infty
-category to the category of
\mathcal{V}
-enriched
\infty
-categories. Then
\Cat^{(-)}
extends to a functor from
\mathbb{E}_{n+1}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories and
lax
functors to
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories and lax functors.

A few words about the approach are in order. Given any
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, expressed as coCartesian fibrations
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
, a lax
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal functor is expediently described as a map
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}
of
\infty
-operads over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
. The proof of this theorem will therefore follow by exhibiting
\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}
as induced by a right adjoint in a Quillen adjunction, between marked simplicial sets over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
and marked simplicial sets over the Boardman–Vogt tensor product
(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathbb{E}_{1})^{\otimes}
.

In an ideal future, the proof in this paper will become obsolete. The natural home for a result of this type should probably be a systematic study of compatibility between the Boardman–Vogt tensor product of
\infty
-operads and lax structures. This paper is the result of losing this particular game of “hot potato”: Theorem
1.1
is important in an application, but the author is not currently equipped to develop an
(\infty,2)
-categorical framework. We look forward to having a good laugh about it someday.

Of necessity, this paper is of a rather technical nature. Our proof that
\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}
is part of a Quillen adjunction uses the formalism of categorical patterns developed in
[
Lur17
, Appendix B]
. Many of the components of the proof are upgraded versions of the construction of the
\infty
-operad
\Fun(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})^{\otimes}
of functors from one
\infty
-operad to another in
[
Lur17
, 2.2.6]
, used there to set up the Day convolution. In the case at hand, our proof uses the following setup for enriched
\infty
-categories.

•

We have a marked simplicial set
\Delta^{op}
with a fixed map
\Delta^{op}\to\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\otimes}
to the associative
\infty
-operad.

•

For any set
X
, we have a marked simplicial set
\Delta^{op}_{X}
with functor
\Delta^{op}_{X}\to\Delta^{op}

[
GH15
, 4.1.1]
; the fiber over
[n]
is
X^{n+1}
.

•

The construction
X\mapsto\Delta^{op}_{X}
is functorial in
X
, and is symmetric monoidal: it takes products of sets to fiber products over
\Delta^{op}
.

•

For a map
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}\to\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\otimes}
of
\infty
-operads, a
\mathcal{V}
-enriched
\infty
-category is defined to be a pair
(X,s)
of a set
X
of “objects” and a functor
s\colon\thinspace\Delta^{op}_{X}\to\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}
giving a commutative diagram of marked simplicial sets:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{op}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{op}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

For any
(a,b)\in X\times X
, the image of
(a,b)
in
\mathcal{V}
represents an enriched
\Hom
-object
\Hom^{\mathcal{V}}(a,b)
of maps from
a
to
b
. Similarly, maps are appropriately compatible pairs of a map of sets and a natural transformation of functors.

For any
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
, we will upgrade this construction to a functor

\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}\colon\thinspace\Op_{\infty}/(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathbb{E}_{1})^{\otimes}\to\Op_{\infty}/\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}

which allows us to conclude that
\Cat_{\infty}^{(-)}
takes lax
(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathbb{E}_{1})
-monoidal functors to lax
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal functors (see Theorem
10.6
).

To help these results find their natural generality, we have given a general-purpose result (Theorem
9.1
) which produces a “pointwise” monoidal structure on certain structures in monoidal
\infty
-categories. This allows us to obtain similar results whenever we have similar identities to those satisfied by the map
\Delta^{op}\to\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\otimes}
and the functor
\Delta^{op}_{(-)}
.

2
Inert maps

Definition 2.1
.

Fix an
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
, and let
\Inert(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes})\subset\Fun(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{A}^{\otimes})
be the full sub-
\infty
-category spanned by the inert morphisms.

Remark 2.2
.

Evaluation at
1\in\Delta^{1}
determines a functor
\ev_{1}\colon\thinspace\Inert(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes})\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
. This is a coCartesian fibration, pulled back from
\Inert({\mathrm{NFin}_{*}})\to{\mathrm{NFin}_{*}}
, and classifies a composite functor

\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\to{\mathrm{NFin}_{*}}\xrightarrow{\mathscr{P}}\Set.

Here the functor
\mathscr{P}
sends a finite pointed set
S_{+}
to the power set
\mathscr{P}(S)
, and sends a pointed map
S_{+}\to T_{+}
to the “non-basepoint image” functor
\mathscr{P}(S)\to\mathscr{P}(T)
. This functor has a right adjoint: the “inverse image” functor
\mathscr{P}(T)\to\mathscr{P}(S)
, and so the functor
\ev_{1}\colon\thinspace\Inert(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes})\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is also a Cartesian fibration.

Evaluation at
0
determines a second functor
\ev_{0}\colon\thinspace\Inert(\mathcal{A}^{\otimes})\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is an
\infty
-operad and
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a map of simplicial sets.
We define the
inert expansion of
\Gamma
to be the simplicial set

\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{K}\times_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}\Inert(\mathcal{A}),

formed as the pullback in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Inert(\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{1}}

(If
\Gamma
is understood, we will denote it simply by
\mathcal{K}
.)
The inert expansion has two maps
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
: the map
\ev_{1}\circ\pi
in the above diagram, and the map
s=\ev_{0}\circ\pi
which we refer to as the
source
map.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration. Then so is the map
s\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
. In particular,
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
is an
\infty
-category.

Under these circumstances, a map
f
in
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
is
s
-coCartesian if and only if
\pi(f)
is
(\ev_{0})
-coCartesian in
\Inert(\mathcal{A})
and
e(f)
is
p
-coCartesian in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

CoCartesian fibrations are stable under pullback, and hence
\pi\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}\to\Inert(A)
is a coCartesian fibration. By
[
Lur09
, 2.4.2.3]
, the composite
s\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}\to\Inert(A)\to A^{\otimes}
is also a coCartesian fibration with the stated coCartesian morphisms.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

If the map
p
is a coCartesian fibration, then the map
s
is a flat categorical fibration.

Proof.

CoCartesian fibrations are flat by
[
Lur17
, B.3.11]
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration and
\gamma\colon\thinspace\Delta^{1}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is an inert morphism. If we define
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}|_{\gamma}=\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}\times_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}\Delta^{1}
, then the restriction

s|_{\gamma}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}|_{\gamma}\to\Delta^{1}

is a Cartesian fibration.

Under these circumstances, a map
f
in
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}|_{\gamma}
is
(s|_{\gamma})
-Cartesian if and only if both
\ev_{1}\pi(f)
and
e(f)
are equivalences.

Suppose that for

we have inert maps

in
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
and

. Then the space of maps

is computed as a natural iterated (homotopy) pullback:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}}((g_{1},Z_{1}),(g_{2},Z_{2}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\Inert(\mathcal{A})}(g_{1},g_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U_{1},U_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\Gamma}(Z_{1},Z_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(\overline{U}_{1},\overline{U}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U_{1},\overline{U}_{2})}

(1)

Now take any inert map

together with a lift of

to
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
along

, in the form of a pair
(h,W)
of an inert morphism

and an object

in the fiber

. The composite

is also inert, and so

is a lift of

to
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
with a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

in
\Inert(\mathcal{A})
. We wish to show that this map

is

-Cartesian.

For any
(g,Z)
in
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
consisting of an inert morphism

in
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
and

, we consider the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\Gamma}(Z,W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U,\overline{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\Gamma}(Z,W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U,\overline{Y})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U,Y)}

The vertical maps in the left-hand square are equivalences, so the left-hand square is a homotopy pullback square. Therefore, applying Equation (

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}}((g,Z),(h\alpha,W))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Map_{\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}(U,X)}

was arbitrary, this shows that the map

is

-Cartesian by
[

, 2.4.1.10]

Given any inert edge
\gamma\colon\thinspace\Delta^{1}\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
, any map

in
\Delta^{1}
has inert image in

. Then
\alpha
has

-Cartesian. Moreover, a general map

-Cartesian if and only if it is equivalent to one of these, which is true if and only if
\ev_{0}(f)
and

are equivalences.

3
Markings

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration. A
compatible marking
is a marking on the simplicial set
\Gamma
such that:

•

the map
p
takes marked edges to inert morphisms,

•

p
-coCartesian lifts of inert morphisms are marked,

•

marked edges are closed under composition, and

•

for any inert
\gamma\colon\thinspace U\to V
, the induced functor
\gamma_{!}\colon\thinspace\Gamma_{U}\to\Gamma_{V}
preserves marked edges.

Remark 3.2
.

In particular, note that equivalences are automatically marked.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration with a compatible marking, and form the inert expansion as the pullback:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Inert(\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{1}}

The
associated marking
on the inert expansion
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
is the set of edges
f
of
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
such that:

•

the image
s(f)=\ev_{0}\pi(f)
is inert in
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
, and

•

the image
e(f)
is marked in
\Gamma
.

This equips
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
with the structure of a marked simplicial set, and the map
s
is a map of marked simplicial sets.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration with a compatible marking. Then, in the associated marking on
\mathcal{K}^{\Gamma}
, marked edges are closed under equivalences and composition.

Proof.

The maps
s
and
e
preserve composition and equivalences. Therefore, it suffices to observe that inert morphisms in the
\infty
-operad
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
are closed under composition and equivalences by
[
Lur09
, 2.4.1.5, 2.4.1.7]
, and that marked edges in
\Gamma
are closed under composition and equivalences by assumption.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\colon\thinspace\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
be a coCartesian fibration with a compatible marking. Suppose we are given a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}

The assumptions imply that

Proposition 3.6
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

4
Spans

Remark 4.1
.

Suppose that we have a span of
\infty
-categories in the form of a diagram

\mathcal{A}\xleftarrow{p}\Gamma\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{C}

such that
p
is a coCartesian fibration.

Given such a span, each object
U\in\mathcal{A}
gives rise to a category
\Gamma_{U}
with a functor
q|_{U}\colon\thinspace\Gamma_{U}\to\mathcal{C}
. Each map

.

Suppose that we have a sum-preserving span

Any coCartesian lift of this sum diagram to

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\rho}_{i}}

7
CoCartesian properties

Definition 7.1
.

Suppose that
r\colon\thinspace\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
is a coCartesian fibration and that
T\colon\thinspace\Delta^{1}\times L\to\mathcal{C}

T_{!}\colon\thinspace\Fun_{f}(L,\mathcal{D})\to\Fun_{g}(L,\mathcal{D})

to be the map on functor categories that, object-by-object, applies the functor associated to the natural transformation:

(T_{!}F)(x)=(Tx)_{!}(F(x)).

The following records some basic properties of this construction.

Proposition 7.2
.

For composable natural transformations
T\colon\thinspace f\Rightarrow g
and
S\colon\thinspace g\Rightarrow h
, we have a natural equivalence

(S\cdot T)_{!}(F)\simeq S_{!}(T_{!}(F)).

For a functor
\alpha\colon\thinspace L\to L^{\prime}
and a natural transformation
T\colon\thinspace f\Rightarrow g
between functors
L^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}
, we have a natural equivalence

(T_{!}F)\circ\alpha\simeq(T\circ\alpha)_{!}(F\circ\alpha).

For functors
F\colon\thinspace L\to\mathcal{D}
over
f
and
G\colon\thinspace L\to\mathcal{D}
over
g
and a natural transformation
T\colon\thinspace f\Rightarrow g
, there is an equivalence between spaces of lifted natural transformations

\Nat^{T}(F,G)\simeq\Nat^{g}(T_{!}F,G).

Proposition 7.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
is a coCartesian fibration, and that

and

are objects of

This is an application of Corollary

.

The Boardman–Vogt tensor

Two
\infty
-operads
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}
have a Boardman–Vogt tensor product
(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}
, whose defining property is that it is universal among
\infty
-operads with a pairing of marked simplicial sets

\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\times\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}\to(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}

over the smash product functor
{\mathrm{NFin}_{*}}\times{\mathrm{NFin}_{*}}\to{\mathrm{NFin}_{*}}

[
Lur17
, 2.2.5]
.

The pairing preserves sum diagrams in each variable separately. As a result, the Boardman–Vogt tensor can be a natural source of sum-preserving spans.

Definition 8.1
.

A diagram
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\xleftarrow{p}\Gamma\xrightarrow{r}\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}
is

if
p
is a coCartesian fibration and

takes
p
-coCartesian maps in
\Gamma
to equivalences.

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose that we have a sum-trivializing diagram
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\xleftarrow{p}\Gamma\xrightarrow{r}\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}
. Then the composite map

q\colon\thinspace\Gamma\xrightarrow{(p,r)}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\times\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}\to(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes},

makes the span
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\leftarrow\Gamma\to(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}
sum-preserving.

Proof.

A
p
-coCartesian lift of a sum diagram is taken by
p
to a sum diagram, and by
r
to an essentially constant diagram. Therefore, the image in
(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}
becomes a sum diagram.
∎

Definition 8.3
.

.

Suppose

Proposition 8.5
.

Suppose that we have a sum-trivializing diagram

of marked simplicial sets, with the marking on

. Then there is a Quillen adjunction

inducing a functor

For any fibration of
\infty
-operads
\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}\to(\mathcal{A}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}
and

\mathcal{A}
, the fiber of

over

\infty
-category

of marked lifts in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}.}

.

Theorem 9.1
.

Suppose that we have a diagram

\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}\leftarrow\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\leftarrow\Gamma\to D\to\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}

\mathcal{P}
-monoidal functor

Then there is a Quillen adjunction

whose right adjoint induces a functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{Y}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

over
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}
.

\Gamma_{{\overline{\phi}}_{!}(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n})}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma_{Y_{1}}\times_{D}\dots\times_{D}\Gamma_{Y_{n}}\xrightarrow{\prodop\displaylimits s_{i}}\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}_{X_{i}}\times_{\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}}\dots\times_{\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}_{X_{i}}\xrightarrow{\phi_{!}}\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}_{Y}.

Proof.

We first define
\tilde{\Gamma}=\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}}\Gamma
, and note that there is an associated natural diagram

\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\xleftarrow{p}\tilde{\Gamma}\xrightarrow{q}\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}

of marked simplicial sets. The map

is a coCartesian fibration; it is the base-change of the map
\Gamma\to\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
, the
p
-coCartesian edges map to coCartesian edges in
\Gamma
, and there is a canonical isomorphism

between fibers. The
p
-coCartesian edges map to equivalences in
D
because all coCartesian edges in
\sSet^{+}/D
map to equivalences in
D
, and so this diagram is sum-trivializing. By Proposition
8.2
, the associated diagram

\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\xleftarrow{p}\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}}\Gamma\to((\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{A})\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}\to(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}.

, we get a Quillen adjunction inducing a functor

\Op_{\infty}/(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}\to\Op_{\infty}/\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{P}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}.

that we denote by
\Fun^{+}_{D}(\Gamma,-)^{\otimes}

\mathcal{V}^{\otimes}\to(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}
, the fiber over
(X,Y)\in\mathcal{O}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{A}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{X}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{(X,Y)\}\times D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{Y\}\times\mathcal{E}^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{O}\bvt\mathcal{E})^{\otimes}.}

is a coCartesian fibration if the map

\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}

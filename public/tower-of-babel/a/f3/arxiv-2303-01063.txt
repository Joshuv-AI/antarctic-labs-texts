Derived Blow-ups and Birational Geometry of Nested Quiver Varieties
Yu Zhao

Abstract
. Given a quiver, Nakajima introduced the quiver variety and the Hecke correspondence, which is a closed subvariety of Cartesian products of quiver varieties. In this paper, we consider two nested quiver varieties as fiber products of Hecke correspondences along natural projections. After blowing up the diagonal, we prove that they are isomorphic to a quadruple moduli space which Neguţ observed for the Jordan quiver. The main ingredient is Jiang’s derived projectivization theory and Hekking’s derived blow-up theory, while we obtain the local model when both the two derived schemes are quasi-smooth.



Dedicated to Professor Hiraku Nakajima on the occasion of his 60th birthday

1.8.1.
Quivers

A quiver

is a directed graph, where
I
is the vertex set and
E

as the source and target map. For any

and

. We denote

as the associated double quiver. For

, we denote
\overline{\bar{e}}:=e
.

An edge

is called a loop if

, and we say that the origin of

is
in(e)
. Given

, we denote the quiver

, where

We denote
g_{k}
as the number of loops in

.

1.8.4.
Derived schemes

A derived scheme is a pair
X=(|X|,\mathcal{O}_{X})
,where
|X|
is a topological space and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a sheaf on
|X|
with values in the
\infty
-category of simplicial commutative rings such that the ringed space
X^{cl}=(|X|,\pi_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))
is a scheme and the sheaf
\pi_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a quasi-coherent
\pi_{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module for each
n>0
. We will also denote
X^{cl}
as
\pi_{0}(X)
, and say it as the underlying scheme of
X
. Here, all derived schemes will be defined over
\mathbb{C}
, hence derived schemes can be modeled
locally by dg-algebras rather than simplicial ones.

For a morphism of derived schemes
f:X\to Y
, we denote
L_{f}
as the cotangent complex
f
. We define the tangent complex
T_{f}
as the derived dual of
L_{f}
and the conormal complex
C_{f}:=L_{f}[-1]
. We say that the morphism
f:X\to Y
is quasi-smooth if
L_{f}
is perfect with Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
and constant rank. We say that the morphism
f:X\to Y
is smooth if
L_{f}
is a locally free sheaf of constant rank over
X
. Given a quasi-smooth morphism
f:X\to Y
, we define the virtual dimension
vdim(f):=rank(L_{f})
and the canonical line bundle
K_{f}:=det(L_{f})
.

Example 1.20
.

When
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of smooth schemes, we have

L_{f}\cong\{f^{*}T^{*}Y\to T^{*}X\},\quad T_{f}\cong\{TX\to f^{*}TY\}[-1]

and thus
f
is quasi-smooth.

Example 1.21
.

The composition of two quasi-smooth morphisms is also quasi-smooth.

We say that a derived scheme
X
is classical if
\pi_{0}(X)\cong X
. In this paper, we always assume that
X
is finite type over
Spec(\mathbb{C})
. Given a derived scheme
X
, we say
X
is smooth or quasi-smooth if the canonical morphism to
Spec(\mathbb{C})
is smooth or quasi-smooth.
Given a derived scheme
X
and a global section of locally free sheaf
f:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to V
, we denote
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}(f)
as the derived zero locus of
f
. The closed embedding
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{Z}(f)\to X
is always quasi-smooth.

1.8.5.

Let
X
be a derived scheme and
F\in QCoh(X)

\mathbb{P}_{X}(F)
as the derived projectivization of Jiang
[

, and
pr_{F}:\mathbb{P}_{X}(F)\to X
as the projection morphisms. Over

Let

Quivers, and Representations of Preprojective Algebras

In this section, we review the cohomology theory of preprojective algebra representations. In this section, we fix a quiver
Q=(I,E)
.

2.1.
The preprojective algebra and representations

We fix a function
\epsilon:E^{\#}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
\epsilon(h)+\epsilon(\bar{h})=0
for any
h\in E
. Given
B\in E(G,H)
, we denote
\epsilon B:=\oplus_{h\in E^{\#}}\epsilon(h)B_{h}
. For any
B\in E(G,H)
and
C\in E(H,K)
, we have
\epsilon BC+B\epsilon C=0
.

Example 2.1
.

Given
k\in I
, we have

E(\mathbb{C}_{k},\mathbb{C}_{k})=\oplus_{l\in E_{k}^{\#}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2g_{k}}

and we denote it as
\mathbf{L}_{k}
. We denote
x=(x_{l})_{l\in E_{k}^{\#}}
as an element of
\mathbf{L}_{k}
. There is a symplectic form on
\mathbf{L}_{k}
:

\omega_{k}(x^{1},x^{2}):=\epsilon(l)x_{l}^{1}x_{l}^{2}.

Let
kQ^{\#}
be the path algebra of
Q^{\#}
, and we denote the preprojective algebra of
Q
as

\Pi_{Q}:=kQ^{\#}/\sum_{h\in E^{\#}}\epsilon(h)h\bar{h}.

Definition 2.2
.

We define an
I
-graded
\Pi_{Q}
-module as a pair
(H,B_{H})
, where
H
is a finite dimensional
I
-graded vector space
H
and
B_{H}\in E(H,H)
such that
\epsilon B_{H}B_{H}=0
.

In this paper, we will omit

and just say

is an
I
-graded
\Pi_{Q}
-module. We define the dimension of

as its dimension as an
I
-graded vector space.
Given two

-graded
\Pi_{Q}
-modules

, we define the

-space

Given

,

and

-graded
\Pi_{Q}

-graded
\Pi_{Q}

Example 2.3
.

Given
k\in I
, for any

and thus

-graded
\Pi_{Q}

2.2.
\mathcal{E}xt

Given two

where

\sigma(G,H)(\zeta)=B_{H}\zeta-\zeta B_{G},\quad\tau(G,H)(J)=\epsilon B_{H}J+\epsilon JB_{G}.

We denote

\displaystyle Ext^{0}_{\Pi_{Q}}(G,H):=ker(\sigma(G,H)),\quad Ext^{2}_{\Pi_{Q}}(G,H):=coker(\tau(G,H)),

\displaystyle Ext^{1}_{\Pi_{Q}}(G,H):=ker(\tau(G,H))/im(\sigma(G,H)).

Then we have

Given

and hence induces natural morphisms

The perfect pairing

between

and

(J^{1},J^{2})\to tr(\epsilon J^{1}J^{2}).

induces an isomorphism

and hence we have the perfect pairing:

We notice that

is an symplectic form on

-graded
\Pi_{Q}
-module

Given two dimension vectors

-modules

Definition 4.1

Link residual closeness of graphs with fixed parameters
Leyou Xu, Chengli Li, Bo Zhou

Abstract
Link residual closeness is a newly proposed measure for network vulnerability. In this model, vertices are perfectly reliable and the links fail independently of each other. It measures the vulnerability even when the removal of links does not disconnect the graph. In this paper, we characterize those graphs that maximize the link residual closeness over the connected graphs with fixed order and one parameters such as connectivity, edge connectivity, bipartiteness, independence number, matching number, chromatic number, number of vertices and number of cut edges. Mathematics Subject Classifications: 68M15, 68R10, 05C12, 05C35 Keywords and Phrases: network vulnerability, link residual closeness, graph parameters

Introduction

The vulnerability of a network is the measurement of the global strength of its underlying graph where the vertices represent the processing elements of the system and the edges (or links) connect pair of vertices
that mutually interact exchanging information
[
12
,
13
]
.
In understanding of computer networks, how to protect a
network from vulnerability or to improve network robustness remains an overarching concern.

It is highly desirable to identify a class of easily
computed measures that characterize network vulnerability.
There are lots of different measures for network vulnerability such as connectivity, toughness, scattering number, binding number, and their link counterparts, see, e.g.
[
11
,
7
,
14
,
18
]
.
These measures may be used if network failure (by the removal of vertices or links) means that the underlying graph has become disconnected or trivial. To measure the vulnerability
even when the removal of vertices/links does not disconnect the graph,
Dangalchev
[
8
]
proposed a new type vulnerability measure that is called
residual closeness (including vertex and link versions).
It was argued in
[
8
]
that vertex (link, respectively) residual closeness is the most appropriate approach for modeling the robustness of network topologies in the face of possible vertex (link, respectively) destruction.
The vertex version has received a lot of attention, see, e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
15
]
for the computational aspects and
[
6
,
17
,
21
]
for the extremal properties.
However, the link version received less attention.
Berberler and Yiǧit established formulae for the link residual closeness of path-type graphs such as regular caterpillars in
[
4
]
, wheel type graphs in
[
20
]
, and
composite graphs such as graph unions and graph joins in
[
19
]
. Some preliminary extremal properties of the link residual closeness were given in
[
21
]
. For example, the trees with minimum and maximum link residual closeness were determined there.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge
E(G)
.
For a graph
G
with
u,v\in V(G)
, the distance between
u
and
v
in
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
, is the length of a shortest path connecting them in
G
, and let
d_{G}(u,v)=\infty
if there is no path connecting
u
and
v
in
G
. In particular,
d_{G}(u,u)=0
for any
u\in V(G)
.
For a vertex
u
of a graph
G
, the closeness of
u
in
G
is defined as

C_{G}(u)=\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}2^{-d_{G}(u,v)}
.
The closeness of a graph
G
is defined as

C(G)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}C_{G}(u)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}2^{-d_{G}(u,v)}.

The link residual closeness of a nonempty graph
G
is defined as
[
8
]

R^{L}(G)=\min\{C(G-e):e\in E(G)\}.

If
G
is empty, then we set
R^{L}(G)=0
. It is evident that
R^{L}(G)=0
if and only if
|E(G)|=0,1
.
For completeness, we mention that vertex residual closeness of a nontrivial graph
G
is defined as
[
8
]

R(G)=\min\{C(G-v):v\in V(G)\}.

To have a fuller understanding of the relationship between the link residual closeness and the structural properties of the graphs, we consider the extremal problems to maximize the link residual closeness in some families of connected graphs. In this paper, we identify those graphs that maximize the link residual closeness in the families of connected graphs of fixed order and one of the parameters such as connectivity, edge connectivity, bipartiteness, independence number, matching number, chromatic number, number of cut vertices and number of cut edges.

. For vertex disjoint graphs

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a nonempty graph in which two vertices
u
and
v
are not adjacent. Then
R^{L}(G)<R^{L}(G+uv)
.

Proof.

Let
H=G+uv
.
Assume that
R(H)=C(H-e)
with
e\in E(H)
.
If
e=uv
, then
R^{L}(H)=C(H-e)=C(G)>C(G-f)\geq R^{L}(G)
for any
f\in E(G)
.
If
e\neq uv
, then
R^{L}(H)=C(H-e)>C(H-e-uv)=C(H-uv-e)=C(G-e)\geq R^{L}(G)
.
∎

If
G=K_{n_{0}}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})
, then we call
K_{n_{i}}
the
i
-th inner copy of
G
, where
i=1,\dots,t
.

Lemma 2
.

For integers
t\geq 2
,
n_{0}\geq 1
and
1\leq n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t}
,
let
G=K_{n_{0}}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})
and
n=\sum_{i=0}^{t}n_{i}
.
If
n_{0}=1
, then

R^{L}(G)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n&\mbox{ if }n_{1}=1,\\
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n+\frac{1}{4}n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }n_{1}\geq 2.\end{cases}

Otherwise,

R^{L}(G)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{n_{0}-1}{2}n-\frac{1}{4}n_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

It can be easily seen that

\displaystyle C(G)

\displaystyle=n_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{t}{n_{i}\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq t}n_{i}n_{j}

\displaystyle=n_{0}(n-n_{0})+\frac{1}{2}(n_{0}^{2}-n_{0})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+(n-n_{0})^{2}-2(n-n_{0})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{n_{0}-1}{2}n-\frac{1}{4}n_{0}^{2}.

Case 1.

n_{0}=1
.

Denote by
u
the unique vertex with degree
n-1
in
G
.
Let
v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}
.

If the degree of
v
is one, then

\displaystyle C(G-uv)

\displaystyle=C(K_{1}\vee(K_{n_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}}))

\displaystyle=C(G)-1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i},

which is minimum for if
v
is in the
1
-st inner copy of
G
because
n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t}
.

If
v
is of degree at least two, then
v
is in the
\ell
-th inner copy of
G
such that
n_{\ell}\geq 2
for some
\ell=1,\dots,k
,

d_{G-uv}(u,v)=2
,
d_{G-uv}(v,z)=3
if
z
is any vertex in the
i
-th inner copy with
i=1,\dots,t
and
i\neq\ell
, and
as we pass from
G
to
G-uv
, the distance between any other vertex pair remains unchanged, so

\displaystyle C(G-uv)

\displaystyle=C(G)-1+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1,i\neq\ell}^{t}n_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1,i\neq\ell}^{t}n_{i}

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1,i\neq\ell}^{t}n_{i},

which is minimum for
\ell=1,\dots,t
if
\ell=1
because
n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t}
.

Now, let
wz
be any edge of
G
with
w,z\neq u
. Then both the degrees of
w
and
z
are at least two. Evidently,
d_{G}(w,z)=1
,
d_{G-wz}(w,z)=2
, and
as we pass from
G
to
G-wz
, the distance between any other vertex pair remains unchanged, so

C(G-wz)=C(G)-1+\frac{1}{2}>C(G-uv)

whether the degree of
v
is one or at least two.

If
n_{1}=1
, then
C(G)-1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i}<C(G)-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1,i\neq\ell}^{t}n_{i}
for any
\ell=1,\dots,t
,
so
R^{L}(G)=C(G-uv)
with
v
in the
1
-st inner copy of
G
. That is,

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(G)-1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n.

If
n_{1}\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=2}^{t}n_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}(n-1-n_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n+\frac{1}{4}n_{1}-\frac{1}{2}.

Case 2.

n_{0}\geq 2
.

For any
wz\in E(G)
, as we pass from
G
to
G-wz
, the distance between
w
and
z
is changed from
1
to
2
, and the distance between any other vertex pair remains unchanged, so
C(G-wz)=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}
.
It follows that

R^{L}(G)=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{n_{0}-1}{2}n-\frac{1}{4}n_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{2},

as desired.
∎

A path
u_{0}\dots u_{\ell}
in a graph
G
is a pendant path of length
\ell
of
G
at
u_{0}
if
\delta_{G}(u_{0})\geq 3
,
\delta_{G}(u_{\ell})=1
, and if
\ell\geq 2
, then
\delta_{G}(u_{i})=2
for each
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
.
Particularly, if
\ell=1
, then

is called a pendant edge of

at

.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a graph with a pendant path
v_{0}v_{1}\dots v_{\ell}
at
v_{0}
, where
\ell\geq 2
.
Then
C(G-v_{i}v_{i+1})>C(G-v_{i-1}v_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
.

Proof.

Let
V_{0}=V(G)\setminus\{v_{i}:i=0,\dots,\ell\}
,
V_{1}=V_{0}\cup\{v_{j}:j=0,\dots,i-1\}
and
V_{2}=\{v_{j}:j=i+1,\dots,\ell\}
.
As we pass from
G-v_{i-1}v_{i}
to
G-v_{i}v_{i+1}
, the distance between any pair of vertices in
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
remains unchanged.
Then

\displaystyle\quad C(G-v_{i}v_{i+1})-C(G-v_{i-1}v_{i})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{u\in V_{1}}2^{-d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{i})}-2\sum_{u\in V_{2}}2^{-d_{G-v_{i-1}v_{i}}(u,v_{i})}.

Note that
V_{0}\subset V_{1}
and for any
u\in V_{0}
,

\displaystyle d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{i})

\displaystyle=d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{0})+d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(v_{0},v_{i})

\displaystyle=d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{0})+i.

Then

\displaystyle\quad C(G-v_{i}v_{i+1})-C(G-v_{i-1}v_{i})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{u\in V_{0}}2^{-d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{0})-i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}2^{-j}-\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-i-1}2^{-j}

\displaystyle=2^{-i+1}\sum_{u\in V_{0}}2^{-d_{G-v_{i}v_{i+1}}(u,v_{0})}+(2-2^{-i+1})

\displaystyle\quad-(2-2^{-\ell+i+1})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-i+1}+(2-2^{-i+1})-(2-2^{-\ell+i+1})

\displaystyle=2^{-\ell+i+1}

\displaystyle>0.

So the result follows.
∎

3
Connectivity and edge connectivity

The (vertex) connectivity
\kappa(G)
of a graph
G
is defined as the minimum
number of vertices whose removal from
G
results in a disconnected graph or
in the trivial graph. If
G
is trivial or disconnected, then
\kappa(G)=0
. For a connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
,
1\leq\kappa(G)\leq n-1
, and

\kappa(G)=n-1
if and only if
G\cong K_{n}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with connectivity
k
, where
1\leq k\leq n-2
. Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2n^{2}-7n+13}{4}&\mbox{if $k=1$ and $n=5,\dots,9$}\\
\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}&\mbox{if $k=1$ and $n=3,4$ or $n\geq 9$}\\
\frac{n^{2}-2n+k}{2}&\mbox{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3})
when
k=1
and
5\leq n\leq 8
,
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2}),K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3})
when
k=1
and
n=9
, and

G\cong K_{k}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})
otherwise.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with connectivity
k
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

By the definition of connectivity, there is a vertex subset

V_{0}
with
|V_{0}|=k
so that

G-V_{0}
is disconnected.
Assume that
G_{1}
is a component of
G-V_{0}
.
Let
V_{1}=V(G_{1})
and
V_{2}=V(G)-V_{0}-V_{1}
.
Let
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
for
i=1,2
. Assume that
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
.
As
n_{1}+n_{2}=n-k
, one has
n_{1}\leq\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
. By Lemma
1
,
G[V_{0}\cup V_{i}]
is complete for
i=1,2
.
That is,
G\cong K_{k}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n-k-n_{1}})
.
By Lemma
2
, one has

R^{L}(G)=\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}&\mbox{if $k=n_{1}=1$},\\
f(n_{1})&\mbox{if $k=1$ and $n_{1}\geq 2$},\\
g_{k}(n_{1})&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(x^{2}+(n-1-x)^{2})+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n+\frac{1}{4}x-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(x^{2}-\left(n-\frac{3}{2}\right)x\right)+\frac{1}{4}(2n^{2}-3n-1)

with
2\leq x\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
,
and

\displaystyle\quad g_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(x^{2}+(n-k-x)^{2})+\frac{1}{4}(n^{2}-k^{2})+\frac{k-1}{2}n-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-(n-k)x)+\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n-1)

with
2\leq x\leq\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
.
It is easy to see that
f(x)
is strictly decreasing for
2\leq x\leq\frac{2n-3}{4}
,
f(\frac{n-1}{2})=f(\frac{n-2}{2})
and

g_{k}(x)
is strictly decreasing for
1\leq x\leq\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
.

Case 1.

k=1
.

If
n_{1}\geq 3
, then
R^{L}(G)=f(n_{1})<f(2)=R^{L}(K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3}))
, a contradiction. So
n_{1}=1,2
.

If
n=3,4
, then
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})
with
R^{L}(G)=\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}
.

Suppose that
n\geq 5
. Note that

f(2)=\frac{2n^{2}-7n+13}{4}<\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}
if and only if
n>9
.
and
f(2)=\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}
if and only if
n=9
.
Thus

n_{1}=\begin{cases}2&\mbox{if $n=5,\dots,8$},\\
1,2&\mbox{if $n=9$},\\
1&\mbox{if $n\geq 10$}.\end{cases}

Correspondingly,

G\cong K_{2}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-3})\mbox{ if }n=5,\dots,8,

G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2}),K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3})\mbox{ if }n=9,

and

G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})\mbox{ if }n\geq 10.

Case 2.

k\geq 2
.

If
n_{1}\geq 2
, then
R^{L}(G)=g_{k}(n_{1})<g_{k}(1)
,
a contradiction.
So
n_{1}=1
,
G\cong K_{k}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})
and
R^{L}(G)=\frac{n^{2}-2n+k}{2}
.
∎

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with connectivity at most
k
that maximizes the link residual closeness, where
1\leq k\leq n-2
and
n\geq 5
. By Lemma
1
,
G
is connected. So we have the following corollary from previous theorem.

.

Let
G
be an
n

, where

G\cong K_{k}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})

The edge connectivity

Lemma 4
.

For positive integers
s
and
n
with
2s\leq n
,
let
G
be a graph obtained from
K_{s}\cup K_{n-s}
by adding an edge.
Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}

with equality if and only if
s=1
.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(K_{s})
and
v\in V(K_{n-s})
so that
uv\in E(G)
.
Let
V_{1}=V(K_{s})\setminus\{u\}
and
V_{2}=V(K_{n-s})\setminus\{v\}
.
If
e=uv
, then

C(G-e)=C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s}).

If
e=uw
with
w\in V_{1}
, then we have either

s=2
and
C(G-e)=C(K_{n-2})+1+\frac{n-2}{2}>C(K_{n-2})+C(K_{2})

or
s\geq 3
and

\displaystyle\quad C(G-e)

\displaystyle=C(K_{s})-\frac{1}{2}+C(K_{n-s})+1+\frac{n-s-1}{2}+\frac{s-2}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{(s-2)(n-s-1)}{4}+\frac{n-s-1}{8}

\displaystyle=C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s})+\frac{s(n-s-1)}{4}+\frac{s-1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{n-s-1}{8}

\displaystyle>C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s}).

Similarly, if
e=vw
with
w\in V_{2}
, then by direct calculation, we have

C(G-e)>C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s}).

If
e=wz
with
w,z\in V_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\quad C(G-e)

\displaystyle=C(K_{s})-\frac{1}{2}+C(K_{n-s})+1+\frac{n-s-1}{2}+\frac{s-1}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(s-1)(n-s-1)}{4}

\displaystyle>C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s}).

Similarly, if
e=wz
with
w,z\in V_{2}
, then

C(G-e)>C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s}).

So

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(G-uv)=C(K_{s})+C(K_{n-s})

\displaystyle=s^{2}-ns+\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}.

Let
f(s)=s^{2}-ns+\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}
.
Since
2s\leq n
,
f(s)
is strictly decreasing and hence
f(s)\leq f(1)
with equality if and only if
s=1
.
The result follows.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with edge connectivity at most
r
, where
1\leq r\leq n-2
.
Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}&\mbox{ if }r=1\\
\frac{n^{2}-2n+r}{2}&\mbox{ if }r\geq 2\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{r}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-r-1})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with edge connectivity at most
r
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

By Lemma
1
,
G
is connected.

If
r=1
, then
G
has a (cut) edge, say
e
, and we have by Lemma
1
that

G-e\cong K_{s}\cup K_{n-s}
for some
s\geq 1
, so it follows from
Lemma
4
that
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})
with
R^{L}(G)=\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}
.

Suppose that
r\geq 2
. Then
n\geq 4
.
Let
\kappa(G)=k
.
Then
k\leq r
. We claim that
k=r
.
Suppose to the contrary that
k<r
.

If
k=1
and
n=4
, then
r=2
, and it is easy to see that there is no such graphs. So
n\geq 5
if
k=1
. Suppose first that
k=1
and
n=5,\dots,8
.
Let
H=K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3})
.
By Theorem
1
,
R^{L}(G)\leq R^{L}(H)
.
Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be the vertices of
K_{2}
(the
1
st inner copy of
H
).
Let
W
be a subset or vertices of
K_{n-3}
(the
2
nd inner copy of
H
) with
|W|=r-2
if
r\geq 3
.
Let
H^{\prime}=H+\{u_{1}w:w\in V(K_{n-3})\}
if
r=2
, and

H^{\prime}=H+\{u_{1}w:w\in V(K_{n-3})\}+\{u_{2}w:w\in W\}
if
r\geq 3
.
Evidently,
u_{2}
is the only vertex of
H^{\prime}
with minimum degree
r
and each other vertex has degree at least
n-2
.
It is easy to see that
\kappa^{\prime}(H^{\prime})=r
.
By Lemma
1
,
R^{L}(H^{\prime})>R^{L}(H)\geq R^{L}(G)
, a contradiction.

Suppose next that
k=1
and
n\geq 9
or
k\geq 2
.
Note that
1\leq r-k\leq n-k-2
.
Let
H=K_{k}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-1})
.
By Theorem
1
,
R^{L}(G)\leq R^{L}(H)
.
Let
u
be the vertex with degree
k
and
W
be a subset of vertices of
K_{n-k-1}
(the
2
nd inner copy of
H
) with
|W|=r-k
.
Let
H^{\prime}=H+\{uw:w\in W\}
. Evidently,
u
is the only vertex of
H^{\prime}
with minimum degree
r
and each other vertex has degree at least
n-2
.
It is easy to see that
\kappa^{\prime}(H^{\prime})=r
.
By Lemma
1
,
R^{L}(H^{\prime})>R^{L}(H)\geq R^{L}(G)
, also a contradiction.

Now we conclude that
k=r
, so by Theorem
1
, we have

G\cong K_{r}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-r-1})
with
R^{L}(G)=\frac{n^{2}-2n+r}{2}
.
∎

Noting that
\kappa^{\prime}(G)\leq\delta(G)
for a connected graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)
and by similar argument as in Theorem
2
, we have the following result.

Corollary 2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at most
\delta
, where
1\leq\delta\leq n-2
.
Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}&\mbox{ if }\delta=1\\
\frac{n^{2}-2n+\delta}{2}&\mbox{ if }\delta\geq 2\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\delta}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-\delta-1})
.

4
Bipartiteness

The bipartiteness of a graph
G
is the minimum number of vertices whose deletion from
G
yields a bipartite graph.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph on
n\geq 3
vertices. Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}-n-3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
.

Proof.

Let
r
and
s
be the partite sizes of
G
with
1\leq r\leq s
.
Note that

\displaystyle R^{L}(K_{1,s})

\displaystyle=C(K_{1,s-1})=\frac{(s-1)(s+2)}{4}=\frac{n^{2}-n-2}{4}

\displaystyle<\frac{n^{2}-n-3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

and if
r\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle R^{L}(K_{r,s})

\displaystyle=C(K_{r,s})-2\times\frac{1}{2}+2\times\frac{1}{8}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{r\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}{s\choose 2}+rs-\frac{3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{r(r-1)}{4}+\frac{s(s-1)}{4}+rs-\frac{3}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}-n-3}{4}+\frac{rs}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{2}-n-3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

with equality if and only if
r=s
. Now the result follows from Lemma
1
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with bipartiteness
k
, where
1\leq k\leq n-2
.
Then

\displaystyle R(G)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{8}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n+\frac{1}{4}nk-\frac{k^{2}}{8}-\frac{k}{4}

\displaystyle\quad-\begin{cases}\frac{5}{8}&\mbox{ if $n-k$ is odd}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{cases}

with equality if and only
G\cong K_{k}\vee K_{\lfloor(n-k)/2\rfloor,\lceil(n-k)/2\rceil}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with bipartiteness
k
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

Let
V_{0}\subset V(G)
with
|V_{0}|=k
such that
G-V_{0}
is bipartite.
Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be the bipartite sets of
G-V_{0}
.
By Lemma
1
,
G-V_{0}
is a complete bipartite graph and
G[V_{0}\cup V_{i}]
is complete for
i=1,2
.
Let
r=|V_{1}|
and
s=|V_{2}|
.
Then
r+s=n-k
and
we may assume that
G=K_{k}\vee K_{r,s}
.
Let
wz\in E(G)
. Then
d_{G}(w,z)=1
,
d_{G-wz}(w,z)=2
, and
if
\{u,v\}\neq\{w,z\}
, then
d_{G-wz}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)
. So

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(G)-2\times\frac{1}{2}+2\times\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle={k\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}{r\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}{s\choose 2}+k(r+s)+rs-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=k(n-k)+\frac{2k(k-1)+(n-k)(n-k-1)}{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{r(n-k)-r^{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

Denote by
g_{k}(r)
the above expression for
R^{L}(G)
.
Assume that
r\leq s
. Then
r\leq\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
.
It is easy to see that
g_{k}(r)
is strictly increasing for
1\leq r\leq\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
.
So
r=\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
. This is because, if
r<\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\rfloor
, then

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=g_{k}(r)<g_{k}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=R^{L}(K_{k}\vee K_{\lfloor(n-k)/2\rfloor,\lceil(n-k)/2\rceil}),

which is a contradiction.
So
G\cong K_{k}\vee K_{\lfloor(n-k)/2\rfloor,\lceil(n-k)/2\rceil}
with
R^{L}(G)=g_{k}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n-k}{2}\right\rfloor\right)
.
∎

5
Independence number and matching number

The independence number of a graph
G
, denoted by
\alpha(G)
, is the maximum cardinality of an independent set of vertices in
G
. Evidently, the complete graph is the unique one with independence number one.

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with independent number
\alpha
, where
2\leq\alpha\leq n-1
. Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-n-2}{4}&\mbox{if $\alpha=n-1$}\\
\frac{n^{2}-n-1}{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}-\alpha}{4}&\mbox{if $\alpha\leq n-2$}\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{n-\alpha}\vee\overline{K_{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with independence number
\alpha
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

Let
S
be an independent set of
G
with
|S|=\alpha
. By Lemma
1
,
G[V(G)\setminus S]\cong K_{n-\alpha}
, so

G\cong K_{n-\alpha}\vee\overline{K_{\alpha}}\cong K_{n-\alpha}\vee(\underbrace{K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{1}}_{\alpha\mbox{ times}})
.
By Lemma
2
,

R^{L}(G)=\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-n-2}{4}&\mbox{if $\alpha=n-1$,}\\
\frac{n^{2}-n-1}{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}-\alpha}{4}&\mbox{if $\alpha\leq n-2$,}\end{cases}

as desired.
∎

The matching number of a graph
G
is the cardinality of a maximum matching of
G
, denoted by
\beta(G)
. Berge
[
5
]
extended
Tutte’s 1-Factor Theorem
[
16
]
to the Berge-Tutte Formula for
the matching number of a graph, which states that

\beta(G)=\frac{1}{2}\min_{S\subset V(G)}(|V(G)|+|S|-o(G-S)),

where, for a graph
H
,

o(H)
denotes the number of odd components (those with an odd number of vertices) of
H
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n\geq 3
vertices with matching number one.
If
n=3
, then
G\cong S_{3},K_{3}
with
R^{L}(S_{3})=1<R^{L}(K_{3})=\frac{5}{2}
.
If
n\geq 4
, then
G\cong S_{n}
with
R^{L}(S_{n})=\frac{(n-2)(n+1)}{4}
.

By
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
,
K_{n}
maximizes the link residual closeness among all
n
-vertex connected graphs and
R^{L}(K_{n})=\frac{n^{2}-n-1}{2}
. Thus, if
G
is a connected graph on
n\geq 2
vertices with matching number
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, then
R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}-n-1}{2}
with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{n}
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with matching number
\beta
, where
2\leq\beta\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
.

If
2\leq\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5}
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{1}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}}
.

If
\frac{2n+3}{5}<\beta<\frac{n}{2}-1
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{3}{4}n+\beta^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\beta+\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)
.

If
\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5}
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{1}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}},K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)
.

If
\beta=\frac{n}{2}-1
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq 2\beta^{2}+\beta-\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{2\beta+1}\cup(n-2\beta-1)K_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with matching number
\beta
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

By the Tutte-Berge formula, there is a vertex set
S\subset V(G)
such that
\beta=\frac{1}{2}(n+|S|-o(G-S))
.
Let
s=|S|
and
t=o(G-S)
.
Then
n-2\beta=t-s
.
Since
\beta\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
, we have
t-s\geq 2
and hence
t\geq 2
.
As
n-s\geq t=n-2\beta+s
, we have
s\leq\beta
.

Claim 1.
All components of
G-S
are odd.

Otherwise, there is an even component in
G-S
.
Let
H
be a graph obtained from
G
by adding all possible edges between vertices in an even component and an odd component of
G-S
.
Then

\beta(H)\geq\beta(G)=\beta
.
Moreover,
o(H-S)=t
and
\beta(H)\leq\frac{1}{2}(n+s-t)=\beta
.
So
\beta(H)=\beta
.
By Lemma
1
,
R^{L}(H)>R^{L}(G)
, a contradiction.
This proves Claim 1.

Denote by
G_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,t
the components of
G-S
with.

n_{i}=|V(G_{i})|
.
By Claim 1 and Lemma
1
,
n_{i}
is odd and
G_{i}\cong K_{n_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,t
.
So
G\cong K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})
, where
K_{0}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})=K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}}
.
Assume that
n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t}
.

Claim 2.

n_{1}=\dots=n_{t-1}=1
and
n_{t}=n-s-t+1
.

Otherwise,
n_{t-1}\geq 3
.
Let

G^{\prime}=K_{1}\vee((t-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-t})
if
s=1
and
n_{1}=\dots=n_{t-1}=3
, and
G^{\prime}=K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t-2}}\cup K_{n_{t-1}-2}\cup K_{n_{t}+2})
otherwise.
As
o(G^{\prime}-S)=t
,
\beta(G^{\prime})\leq\frac{n+s-t}{2}=\beta
.
Note that
\beta(G^{\prime})\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{t}n_{i}-t}{2}+s=\beta
.
So
\beta(G^{\prime})=\beta
.

Suppose first that
s=0
. Note that
n_{i}=1
or
n_{i}\geq 3
, and
n_{t-1}\geq 3
. Then it is easy to see

R^{L}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}C(K_{n_{i}})-\frac{1}{2},

R^{L}(G^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{t-2}C(K_{n_{i}})+C(K_{n_{t-1}-2})+C(K_{n_{t}+2})-\frac{1}{2},

so

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(K_{n_{t-1}-2})+C(K_{n_{t}+2})-C(K_{n_{t-1}})-C(K_{n_{t}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((n_{t}+2)^{2}+(n_{t-1}-2)^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{t}^{2}+n_{t-1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2(n_{t}-n_{t-1}+2)

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction.
Suppose next that
s=1
and
n_{1}=\dots=n_{t-1}=3
. Then
n_{t}=n-3t+2
, so by Lemma
2
, we have

R^{L}(G)=\frac{9}{4}(t-1)+\frac{1}{4}(n-3t+2)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n+\frac{1}{4}

and

R^{L}(G^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}(t-2)+\frac{1}{4}(n-t)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n.

As
n\geq 3t+1
, we have

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(t-2)+\frac{1}{4}(n-t)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{9}{4}(t-1)-\frac{1}{4}(n-3t+2)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle=-2t^{2}+(n+1)t-\frac{5}{4}n+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-2t^{2}+\left(t-\frac{5}{4}\right)(3t+1)+t+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=t^{2}-\frac{7}{4}t-\frac{3}{4}

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction. Now we are left with the following three cases:
(a)
s=1
and
n_{1}=\dots=n_{r}=1
,
n_{r+1}\geq 3
for some
r=1,\dots,t-2
, or
(b)
s=1
,
n_{1}\geq 3
and
n_{t-1}\geq 5
, or
(c)
s\geq 2
.
For any case, we have by Lemma
2
that

\displaystyle R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}((n_{t}+2)^{2}+(n_{t-1}-2)^{2})-\frac{1}{4}(n_{t}^{2}+n_{t-1}^{2})

\displaystyle=n_{t}-n_{t-1}+2

\displaystyle>0,

also a contradiction. This proves Claim 2.

Recall that
t=n-2\beta+s
. By Claim 2,

G\cong K_{s}\vee((n-2\beta+s-1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-2s+1})
.
For fixed
n
, let
f(s)=R^{L}(K_{s}\vee((n-2\beta+s-1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-2s+1}))
. We want to maximize
f(s)
.

Suppose that
s\geq 2
. By Lemma
2
again,

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(2\beta-2s+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(n-2\beta+s-1)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}s^{2}+\frac{1}{2}ns-\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{1}{2}.

As a quadratic function of
s\in[2,\beta]
,
f(s)
has axis of symmetry
s=s^{*}:=\frac{4\beta}{3}-\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{2}
.
Note that
\beta-s^{*}>s^{*}-2
if and only if
\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5}
.
Thus,

f(\beta)>f(s)\mbox{ if $s\in[2,\beta)$ and $\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5}$},

f(2)=f(\beta)>f(s)\mbox{ if $s\in(2,\beta)$ and $\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5}$},

and

f(2)>f(s)\mbox{ if $s\in(2,\beta]$ and $\beta>\frac{2n+3}{5}$}.

That is,
f(s)
is maximized if and only if

s=\begin{cases}\beta&\mbox{ if }\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5},\\
2,\beta&\mbox{ if }\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5},\\
2&\mbox{ if }\beta>\frac{2n+3}{5},\end{cases}

and correspondingly, the maximum of
f(s)
is equal to

f(\beta)=\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{1}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}
if
\beta\leq\frac{2n+3}{5}
, and

f(2)=\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{3}{4}n+\beta^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\beta+1
if
\beta\geq\frac{2n+3}{5}
.

Next, by Lemma
2
, we have

f(1)=\frac{1}{4}(2\beta-1)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(n-2\beta-1)+\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n.

For
\beta\in[2,\frac{2n+3}{5}]
, we have

f(\beta)-f(1)=\phi(\beta):=-\frac{5}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta+\frac{5}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}.

As

\displaystyle\phi(\beta)

\displaystyle\geq\phi\left(\frac{2n+3}{5}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2n+3)^{2}}{20}+\frac{(2n+3)n}{10}+\frac{2n+3}{4}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{5}>0,

we have

f(\beta)>f(1)
.
On the other hand, we have
f(2)>f(1)
as
f(2)-f(1)=n-2\beta+1>0
. Thus
s=1
is impossible.

Now, we have by direct calculation that

f(0)=2\beta^{2}+\beta-\frac{1}{2}.

For
\beta\in[2,\frac{2n+3}{5}]
, we have

f(\beta)-f(0)=\varphi(\beta):=\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{9}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{5}{4}\beta.

and

\varphi(\beta)\geq g\left(\frac{2n+3}{5}\right)=\frac{9n^{2}+63n-6}{100}>0
,
so
f(\beta)>f(0)
, implying that
s=0
is impossible.
On the other hand, for
\beta\in[\frac{2n+3}{5},\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1]
,

f(2)-f(0)=\psi(\beta):=\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{3}{4}n-\beta^{2}-\frac{9}{2}\beta+\frac{3}{2}.

As

\psi(\beta)=0
has a positive root

\beta_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{(n+\frac{3}{2})^{2}+24}}{2}-\frac{9}{4}>\frac{n}{2}-\frac{3}{2},

we have

f(2)>f(0)

if
n
is odd, or if
\beta<\frac{n}{2}-1
and
n
is even. In such cases,
s=0
is also impossible. If

\beta=\frac{n}{2}-1
, then
f(2)-f(0)=-2n+8<0
, so
f(2)<f(0)
, implying that
s=2
is impossible.

Therefore, we conclude that
f(s)
is maximized if and only if

s=\begin{cases}\beta&\mbox{ if }2\leq\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5},\\
2&\mbox{ if }\frac{2n+3}{5}<\beta<\frac{n}{2}-1,\\
2,\beta&\mbox{ if }\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5},\\
0&\mbox{ if }\beta=\frac{n}{2}-1.\end{cases}

Correspondingly,

G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}}

with

R^{L}(G)=f(\beta)

if
2\leq\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5}
,

G\cong K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)

with
R^{L}(G)=f(2)

if
\frac{2n+3}{5}<\beta<\frac{n}{2}-1
,

G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}},K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)

with
R^{L}(G)=f(\beta)=f(0)

if
\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5}
,
and

G\cong K_{2\beta+1}\cup(n-2\beta-1)K_{1}

with
R^{L}(G)=f(0)

if
\beta=\frac{n}{2}-1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Suppose that
G
is connected in the above proof. As
t-s=n-2\beta\geq 2
, we have
s\neq 0
, so one immediately has the following corollary.

Corollary 3
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex connected graph with matching number
\beta
, where
2\leq\beta\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
.

If
2\leq\beta<\frac{2n+3}{5}
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{1}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}}
.

If
\frac{2n+3}{5}<\beta\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}+\frac{3}{4}n+\beta^{2}-\frac{7}{2}\beta+\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)
.

If
\beta=\frac{2n+3}{5}
, then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{1}{4}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\beta-\frac{1}{4}\beta-\frac{1}{2}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{\beta}\vee\overline{K_{n-\beta}},K_{2}\vee\left((n-2\beta+1)K_{1}\cup K_{2\beta-3}\right)
.

6
Chromatic number

The chromatic number of a graph
G
, written
\chi(G)
, is the least
k
such
that the vertex set can be expressed as the union of
k
independent sets.

The chromatic number of a graph
G
is defined to be the number of colors necessary to color the vertices of
G
such that no two adjacent vertices have the same color.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq 3
and
2\leq k\leq n
,
let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with chromatic number
k
.
Suppose that
n=kq+r
with
0\leq r<k
.
Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-n-3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{1}{4}n^{2}\right\rfloor&\mbox{if }k=2\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}-\frac{n+r(q+1)^{2}+(k-r)q^{2}}{4}&\mbox{if }k\geq 3\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G
is a balanced complete
k
-partite graph.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with chromatic number
k
that maximizes the link residual closeness.

Denote by
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
the coloring classes of
G
.
Let
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
for
i=1,\dots,k
.
Assume that
n_{i}\geq n_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots,k-1
.
By Lemma
1
,
G\cong K_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}
.

If
k=2
, then the result follows from Theorem
3
.

Suppose next that
k\geq 3
.
Recall that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n
.
For any
wz\in E(G)
,
d_{G}(w,z)=1
,
d_{G-wz}(w,z)=2
, and if
\{u,v\}\neq\{w,z\}
, then
d_{G-wz}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)
. So

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{4}n_{i}(n_{i}-1)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}n_{i}n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

Suppose that
n_{1}-n_{k}\geq 2
.
Let
m_{1}=n_{1}-1
,
m_{k}=n_{k}+1
and
m_{i}=n_{i}
for
i=2,\dots,k-1
. Let
G^{\prime}=K_{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}}
.
Then

\displaystyle R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}(m_{1}^{2}+m_{k}^{2})+\frac{1}{4}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n_{1}-n_{k}-1)

\displaystyle>0,

a contradiction. So
n_{1}-n_{k}=0,1
.
As
n=kq+r
with
0\leq r<k
, we have
n_{i}=q+1
for
i
with
1\leq i\leq r
and
n_{i}=q
for
i=r+1,\dots,k
.
Then

R^{L}(G)=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{4}n-\frac{1}{4}r(q+1)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}(k-r)q^{2}-\frac{1}{2},

as desired.
∎

7
Number of cut edges

A cut edge (or bridge) is a single edge whose removal disconnects a graph.
For a connected graph
G
on
n
vertices, if
G
is a tree, then
every edge is a cut edge. Otherwise, there are at most
n-3
cut edges.

A path
u_{0}u_{1}\dots u_{s}
in a graph
G
is
an internal path of
G
with length
s
if
\delta_{G}(u_{0}),\delta_{G}(u_{s})\geq 3
and if
s\geq 2
, then
\delta_{G}(u_{i})=2
for each
i=1,\dots,s-1
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with an internal path
u_{0}\dots u_{t}
, where
t\geq 1
.
Let
N_{0}=N_{G}(u_{t})\setminus\{u_{t-1}\}
and

G^{\prime}=G-\{u_{t}w:w\in N_{0}\}+\{u_{0}w:w\in N_{0}\}.

Then
R^{L}(G^{\prime})>R^{L}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
be the component of
G-u_{0}u_{1}
containing
u_{0}
and
G_{2}
the component of

G-u_{t-1}u_{t}
containing
u_{t}
.
Let
V_{i}=V(G_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Let
G_{2}^{\prime}=G^{\prime}[V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}\cup\{u_{0}\}]
. Then
G_{2}^{\prime}\cong G_{2}
.
Assume that
R^{L}(G^{\prime})=C(G^{\prime}-f)
with
f\in E(G^{\prime})
. By Lemma
3
,

f\neq u_{i}u_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq t-1
.
If
f=u_{0}u_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{w\in V_{1}}2^{-d_{G^{\prime}}(z,w)}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}2^{-d_{G}(z,u_{i})}

\displaystyle=2^{-d_{G}(z,u_{t})}\left(\sum_{w\in V_{1}}2^{-d_{G}(u_{0},w)}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}2^{-(t-i)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-d_{G}(z,u_{t})}\left(1+2\cdot\frac{1}{2}-(2-2^{-(t-1)})\right)

\displaystyle>0

for any
z\in V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}
,
so

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle\geq C(G^{\prime}-f)-C(G-f)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{z\in V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}}\left(\sum_{w\in V_{1}}2^{-d_{G^{\prime}}(z,w)}-\sum_{i=1}^{t}2^{-d_{G}(z,u_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle>0.

If
f\in E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2}^{\prime})
, then

\sum_{i=0}^{t}\left(2^{-d_{G^{\prime}-f}(z,u_{i})}-2^{-d_{G-f}(z,u_{i})}\right)=0

for any
z\in V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}
, so

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle\geq C(G^{\prime}-f)-C(G-f)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{z\in V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}}\sum_{w\in V_{1}\setminus\{u_{0}\}}\left(2^{-d_{G^{\prime}-f}(z,w)}-2^{-d_{G-f}(z,w)}\right)

\displaystyle>0,

where the second inequality follows because

d_{G^{\prime}-f}(z,w)=d_{G-f}(z,w)-t<d_{G-f}(z,w)
or

d_{G^{\prime}-f}(z,w)=d_{G-f}(z,w)=\infty

for any
z\in V_{2}\setminus\{u_{t}\}
and
w\in V_{1}
, and there is indeed such vertex pair for which the former inequality holds.
It follows that
R^{L}(G^{\prime})>R^{L}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with a
pendant path
u_{0}u_{1}\dots u_{t}
at
u_{0}
, where
t\geq 2
.
Let

G^{\prime}=G-\{u_{i-1}u_{i}:i=2,\dots,t\}+\{u_{0}u_{i}:i=2,\dots,t\}.

Then
R^{L}(G^{\prime})>R^{L}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
V_{1}=V(G)\setminus\{u_{1},\dots,u_{t}\}
.
Assume that
R^{L}(G^{\prime})=C(G^{\prime}-e)
with
e\in E(G^{\prime})
.

Suppose first that
e\in E(G)\setminus\{u_{0}u_{1}\}
.
As we pass from
G-e
to
G^{\prime}-e
, the distance between any pair of vertices in
V_{1}\cup\{u_{1}\}
remains unchanged. For any
w\in V_{1}
and
i=2,\dots,t
,

d_{G^{\prime}-e}(w,u_{i})=d_{G-e}(w,u_{i})-i+1
if
d_{G-e}(w,u_{0})
is finite, and there is indeed such a vertex
w=w_{0}
, a neighbor of
u_{0}
that is not incident to
e
.
Moreover,
G[\{u_{0},\dots,u_{t}\}]\cong P_{t+1}
and
G^{\prime}[\{u_{0},\dots,u_{t}\}]\cong S_{t+1}
.
Note that
C(S_{t+1})\geq C(P_{t+1})
.
So

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle\geq C(G^{\prime}-e)-C(G-e)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=2}^{t}\sum_{w\in V_{1}}\left(2^{-d_{G^{\prime}-e}(u_{i},w)}-2^{-d_{G-e}(u_{i},w)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C(S_{t+1})-C(P_{t+1})

\displaystyle\geq 2\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(2^{-d_{G^{\prime}-e}(u_{i},w_{0})}-2^{-d_{G-e}(u_{i},w_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=2}^{t}\left(2^{-2}-2^{-(i+1)}\right)

\displaystyle>0.

If
e=u_{0}u_{i}
for some
i=1,\dots,t
, say
i=1
, then

\displaystyle\quad R^{L}(G^{\prime})-R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle\geq C(G^{\prime}-e)-C(G-e)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=2}^{t}\sum_{w\in V_{1}\setminus\{u_{0}\}}2^{-1-d_{G}(u_{0},w)}+C(S_{t})-C(P_{t})

\displaystyle>0.

Thus, in either case, we have
R^{L}(G^{\prime})>R^{L}(G)
.
∎

If
G
is a tree on
n\geq 2
vertices, then
R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{(n-2)(n+1)}{4}
with equality if and only if
G\cong S_{n}
, which is known in
[
21
]
and follows also from Lemma
6
.

For
1\leq k\leq n-3
, by
C_{n,k}
, we denote the
n
-vertex graph obtained from
K_{n-k}
by attaching
k
pendant edges at one common vertex. It is clear that
C_{n,1}\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})
. For
n\geq 5
, let
C_{n,2}^{\prime}
be be graph obtained from
C_{n-1,1}
by attaching a pendant edge at a vertex of degree
n-3
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex connected graph with
k
cut
edges, where
1\leq k\leq n-3
. Then

R^{L}(G)\leq\frac{n^{2}-nk}{2}-n+\frac{k^{2}+3k}{4}

with equality if and only if
G\cong C_{n,k}
for
k\neq 2
and
G\cong C_{n,k},C_{n,k}^{\prime}
for
k=2
.

Proof.

If
k=1
, then the edge connectivity of
G
is
1
, so
the result follows from Theorem
2
.

Suppose that
k\geq 2
. Let
G
be a connected graph with
n
vertices and
k
cut
edges that maximizes the link residual closeness.

By Lemmas
5
and
6
, all cut edges of
G
are pendant edges.
Let
V_{0}
be the set of vertices with degree one in
G
.
By Lemma
1
,
G-V_{0}\cong K_{n-k}
.
Assume that
V(G-V_{0})=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n-k}\}
and that
d_{G}(v_{i})=n-k-1+a_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,n-k
,
with
a_{1}\geq\dots\geq a_{n-k}
. That is,
G
is obtained from
K_{n-k}
by attaching
a_{i}
pendant edges at
v_{i}
if
a_{i}>0
for
i=1,\dots,n-k
.
By direct calculation,

\displaystyle C(G)

\displaystyle={n-k\choose 2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}{a_{i}\choose 2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{n-k-1}{2}+1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}a_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-k}a_{i}a_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n-k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(n-k)+\frac{2(n-k)+1}{4}k

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8}k^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n-k}a_{i}^{2}.

If
a_{i}\geq 1
with
i=1,\dots,n-k
, then for a pendant edge

at
v_{i}

\displaystyle\quad C(G-e_{i})

\displaystyle=C(G)-\left(1+\frac{n-k-1}{2}+\frac{a_{i}-1}{2}+\frac{k-a_{i}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{4}a_{i}.

For
1\leq i<j\leq n-k
whether

or not,

\displaystyle\quad C(G-v_{i}v_{j})

\displaystyle=C(G)-1+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}(a_{i}+a_{j})+\frac{1}{4}(a_{i}+a_{j})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}a_{i}a_{j}+\frac{1}{8}a_{i}a_{j}

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}(a_{i}+a_{j})-\frac{1}{8}a_{i}a_{j}

Recall that
a_{1}\geq\dots\geq a_{n-k}
,
so

R^{L}(G)=\min\{C(G-e_{1}),C(G-v_{1}v_{2})\}
, which is equal to the minimum of

C(G)-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{4}a_{1}
and

C(G)-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}(a_{1}+a_{2})-\frac{1}{8}a_{1}a_{2}
.

If
k=2
, then we have either

,

and so

a_{1}=a_{2}=1
,
a_{3}=\dots=a_{n-k}=0
and so

.
In the former case,

C(G-e_{1})-C(G-v_{1}v_{2})=1-\frac{1}{2}n<0,

and in the latter case,

C(G-e_{1})-C(G-v_{1}v_{2})=\frac{11}{8}-\frac{1}{2}n<0.

Note that
C_{n,2}-e_{1}\cong C_{n,2}^{\prime}-e_{1}
.
So
G\cong C_{n,2},C_{n,2}^{\prime}
, and
R^{L}(G)=C(G-e_{1})=\frac{1}{2}(n-k)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}
in either case.

Suppose that
k\geq 3
. We claim that
a_{2}=0
.
Suppose to the contrary that
a_{2}\geq 1
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from

by attaching

pendant edges at

.
Note that

\displaystyle\quad C(G^{\prime}-e_{1})-C(G-e_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(a_{1}-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n-k}a_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n-k}a_{i}a_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}(a_{1}-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n-k}a_{i}+\frac{1}{4}a_{2}\sum_{i=3}^{n-k}a_{i}

\displaystyle>0

and

\displaystyle\quad C(G^{\prime}-v_{1}v_{2})-C(G-v_{1}v_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n-k}a_{i}a_{j}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=3}^{n-k}a_{i}+\frac{1}{8}a_{1}a_{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{4}(a_{1}-1)\sum_{i=3}^{n-k}a_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

So

\displaystyle R^{L}(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min\{C(G^{\prime}-e_{1}),C(G^{\prime}-v_{1}v_{2})\}

\displaystyle>\min\{C(G-e_{1}),C(G-v_{1}v_{2})\}=R^{L}(G),

a contradiction.
It follows that
a_{2}=0
, so
G\cong C_{n,k}
and

R^{L}(G)=C(G-e_{1})=\frac{1}{2}(n-k)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(n-k)+\frac{2(n-k)+1}{4}k+\frac{1}{4}k^{2}-\frac{n}{2}=\frac{n^{2}-nk}{2}-n+\frac{k^{2}+3k}{4}
,
as desired.
∎

8
Number of cut vertices

A vertex
v
in a connected graph
G
is a cut vertex if
G-v
is disconnected.
For a connected graph
G
on
n\geq 2
vertices, it has at least two vertices that are not cut vertices, so
G
possesses at most
n-2
cut vertices. Moreover, if
G
has
n-2
cut vertices, then it is the
n
-vertex path.
If
G
is a graph of order
n\geq 4
with one cut vertex, then, by Theorem
1
,

R^{L}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{n^{2}-3n+2}{2}&\mbox{if $n=4,n\geq 9$}\\
\frac{2n^{2}-7n+13}{4}&\mbox{if $5\leq n\leq 8$}\end{cases}

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3})
for
5\leq n\leq 8
,
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{2}\cup K_{n-3}),K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})
for
n=4,9
and
G\cong K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup K_{n-2})
for
n\geq 10
.
In the remainder of this section, we consider the
n
-vertex connected graphs with
k
cut vertices, where
2\leq k\leq n-3
.

For integers
r\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\geq 0
, we denote by
K^{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}}
the graph obtained from the complete graph

K_{r}
with vertex set
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
by attaching a pendant path of length
a_{i}
at
v_{i}
if
a_{i}>0
for
i=1,\dots,r
.

Lemma 7
.

For fixed integers
r\geq 3
and
s\geq 2
, let

\mathcal{G}(r,s)=\left\{K^{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}}:a_{1}\geq\dots\geq a_{r}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}=s\right\}.

Then

G=K^{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}}
maximizes the link residual closeness in
\mathcal{G}(r,s)
if and only if
a_{1}-a_{r}=0,1
.

Proof.

Let
G=K^{a_{1},\dots,a_{r}}\in\mathcal{G}(r,s)
. First, we show that

\displaystyle R^{L}(G)

\displaystyle=2s-4+2^{2-a_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{r}2^{-a_{i}}

(1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}(2-2^{-a_{i}})(2-2^{-a_{j}}).

For
i=1,\dots,r
, let
Q_{i}
be the component of
G-E(K_{r})
containing
v_{i}
.
Then
Q_{i}
is a path
P_{a_{i}+1}
with one terminal vertex
v_{i}
, say
Q_{i}=v_{i,0}v_{i,1}\dots v_{i,a_{i}}
with
v_{i,0}=v_{i}
.
Let
V_{i}=V(Q_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r
.

Let
u\in V_{i}
and
v\in V_{j}
with
1\leq i<j\leq r
.
Note that
d_{G-v_{i}v_{j}}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)+1
, and if
\{w,z\}\neq\{u,v\}
, then
d_{G-v_{i}v_{j}}(w,z)=d_{G}(w,z)
. So

\displaystyle\quad C(G-v_{i}v_{j})

\displaystyle=C(G)-2\sum_{u\in V_{i}}\sum_{v\in V_{j}}\left(2^{-d_{G}(u,v)}-2^{-d_{G-v_{i}v_{j}}(u,v)}\right)

\displaystyle=C(G)-\sum_{u\in V_{i}}\sum_{v\in V_{j}}2^{-d_{G}(u,v)}

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u\in V_{i}}2^{-d_{G}(u,v_{i})}\sum_{v\in V_{j}}2^{-d_{G}(v_{j},v)}

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=0}^{a_{i}}2^{-s}\sum_{s=0}^{a_{j}}2^{-s}

\displaystyle=C(G)-\frac{1}{2}(2-2^{-a_{i}})(2-2^{-a_{j}}).

As
a_{1}\geq\dots\geq a_{r}
,
we see that for
1\leq i<j\leq r
,

\displaystyle C(G-v_{i}v_{j})

\displaystyle\geq C(G-v_{1}v_{2})

(2)

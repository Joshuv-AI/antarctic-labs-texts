It is well known that an elliptic pseudo-differential operator

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The Fourier analysis of a compact Lie group

Let
\textnormal{d}x
be the Haar measure on a compact Lie group
G.

The Hilbert space
L^{2}(G):=L^{2}(G,\textnormal{d}x)
will be endowed with
the inner product

(f,g)=\smallint\limits_{G}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\textnormal{d}x.

The Peter-Weyl theorem gives a spectral decomposition of
L^{2}(G)
in terms of the entries of unitary representations of
G
. In order to present such a result we will give some preliminaries.

Definition 2.1

(Unitary representation of
G
)
.

A continuous and unitary representation of
G
on
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
is any continuous mapping
\xi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(\ell)),
where
\textnormal{U}(\ell)
is the Lie group of unitary matrices of order
\ell\times\ell.
The integer number
\ell=d_{\xi}
is called the dimension of the representation
\xi.

Remark 2.2

(Irreducible representations)
.

We recall that:

•

a subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}}
is called
\xi
-invariant if for any
x\in G,

\xi(x)(L)\subseteq L,
where
\xi(x)(L):=\{\xi(x)v:v\in L\}.

•

The representation
\xi
is irreducible if its only invariant subspaces are
L=\emptyset
and
L=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}},
the trivial ones.

•

Any unitary representation
\xi
is a direct sum of unitary irreducible representations. We denote it by
\xi=\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{k},
with
\xi_{i},

1\leq i\leq k,
being irreducible representations on factors
\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi_{i}}}
that decompose the representation space

\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi_{k}}}.

The notion of
equivalent representations
allows us to define an equivalence relation in the family of unitary representations. We recall it in the following definition.

Definition 2.3

(Equivalent representations)
.

Two unitary representations

\xi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(d_{\xi}))\textnormal{ and }\eta\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(d_{\eta}))

are equivalent if there exists a linear mapping
S:\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d_{\eta}}
such that for any
x\in G,

S\xi(x)=\eta(x)S.
The mapping
S
is called an intertwining operator between
\xi
and
\eta.
The set of all the intertwining operators between
\xi
and
\eta
is denoted by
\textnormal{Hom}(\xi,\eta).

Remark 2.4

(Schur Lemma, 1905)
.

If
\xi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(d_{\xi}))
is irreducible, then
\textnormal{Hom}(\xi,\xi)=\mathbb{C}I_{d_{\xi}}
is formed by scalar multiples of the identity matrix
I_{d_{\xi}}
of order
d_{\xi}.

Definition 2.5

(The unitary dual)
.

The relation
\sim
on the set of unitary representations, which we denote by
\textnormal{Rep}(G),
and defined by:
\xi\sim\eta
if and only if
\xi
and
\eta
are equivalent representations,
is an equivalence relation. The quotient set

\widehat{G}:={\textnormal{Rep}(G)}/{\sim}

is called the unitary dual of
G.
Since
G
is a compact Lie group,
\widehat{G}
is a discrete set.

The unitary dual encodes all the Fourier analysis on the group. The Fourier transform is defined as follows.

Definition 2.6

(Group Fourier transform)
.

If
\xi\in\textnormal{Rep}(G),
the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}_{G}
associates to any
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
a matrix-valued function
\mathscr{F}_{G}f
defined on
\textnormal{Rep}(G)
as follows

(\mathscr{F}_{G}f)(\xi)\equiv\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int\limits_{G}f(x)\xi(x)^{*}\textnormal{d}x,\,\,\xi\in\textnormal{Rep}(G).

Remark 2.7

(The Fourier inversion formula on a compact Lie group)
.

The discrete Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G}):=\mathscr{F}_{G}(C^{\infty}(G))
is the image of the Fourier transform on the class of smooth functions. This operator admits a unitary extension from
L^{2}(G)
into
\ell^{2}(\widehat{G}),
with

\ell^{2}(\widehat{G})=\left\{\phi:\forall[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\,\phi(\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{\xi}\times d_{\xi}}\textnormal{ and }\|\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(\widehat{G})}<\infty\right\},

(2.1)

where

\|\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(\widehat{G})}:=\left(\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\phi(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The norm
\|\phi(\xi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}=(\textnormal{Tr}(\phi(\xi)^{*}\phi(\xi)))^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the standard Hilbert-Schmidt norm of matrices. The Fourier inversion formula takes the form

f(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\widehat{f}(\xi)],\forall f\in L^{1}(G),\forall x\in G,

(2.2)

where the summation is understood in the sense that from any equivalence class
[\xi]
we choose one (any) unitary representation.

2.2.
The quantisation formula

Let
A:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
be a continuous linear operator with respect to the standard Fréchet structure on
C^{\infty}(G).
There is a way of associating to the operator
A
a matrix-valued function
\sigma_{A}
defined on the non-commutative phase space
G\times\widehat{G}
to rewrite the operator
A
in terms of the Fourier inversion formula and in terms of the Fourier transform. Such a expression is called the quantisation formula. To introduce it we require the following definition.

Definition 2.8

(Right convolution kernel of an operator)
.

The Schwartz kernel theorem associates to
A
a kernel/distribution
K_{A}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(G\times G)
such that

Af(x)=\int\limits_{G}K_{A}(x,y)f(y)\textnormal{d}y,\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

The distribution defined via
R_{A}(x,xy^{-1}):=K_{A}(x,y)
that provides the convolution identity

Af(x)=\int\limits_{G}R_{A}(x,xy^{-1})f(y)\textnormal{d}y,\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(G),

is called the right-convolution kernel of
A.

Remark 2.9

(The quantisation formula)
.

Now, we will associate a global symbol
\sigma_{A}:G\times\widehat{G}\rightarrow\cup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times\ell}
to
A.
Indeed, in view of the identity
Af(x)=(f\ast R_{A}(x,\cdot))(x),
we get

\widehat{Af}(\xi)=\widehat{R}_{A}(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

Then we have
that

Af(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\widehat{R}_{A}(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)],\,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

(2.3)

In view of the identity (
2.3
), from any equivalence class
[\xi]\in\widehat{G},
we can choose one and only one irreducible unitary representation
\xi_{0}\in[\xi],
such that the matrix-valued function

\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\equiv\sigma_{A}(x,\xi_{0}):=\widehat{R}_{A}(x,\xi_{0}),\,(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},

(2.4)

satisfies that

Af(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi_{0}(x)\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi_{0})],\,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

(2.5)

The representation in (
2.5
) is independent of the choice of the representation
\xi_{0}\in\textnormal{Rep}(G)
from any equivalent class
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}.
This is a consequence of the Fourier inversion formula.
So, we can simply write

Af(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)],\,\forall f\in C^{\infty}(G).

(2.6)

In the following quantisation theorem we observe that the distribution
\sigma_{A}
in (
2.6
) defined on
G\times\widehat{G}
is unique and can be written in terms of the operator
A,
see Theorems 10.4.4 and 10.4.6 of
[
39
]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
A:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
be a continuous linear operator. The following statements are equivalent.

•

The matrix-valued distribution
\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi]):G\times\widehat{G}\rightarrow\cup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}^{\ell\times\ell}
satisfies that

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(G),\,\forall x\in G,\,\,Af(x)=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)].

(2.7)

•

We have that
\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)=\widehat{R}_{A}(x,\xi).

•

The following identity holds:
\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)=\xi(x)^{*}A\xi(x),
where
A\xi(x):=[A\xi_{ij}(x)]_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}.

Remark 2.11
.

In view of the quantisations formulae (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), a symbol
\sigma_{A}
can be considered as a mapping defined on
G\times\widehat{G}
or as a mapping defined on
G\times\textnormal{Rep}(G)
by identifying all the values
\sigma_{A}(x,\xi)=\sigma_{A}(x,\xi^{\prime})=\sigma(x,[\xi])
when
\xi^{\prime},\xi\in[\xi].

Example 2.12

(The symbol of a measurable function of the Laplacian)
.

Let
\mathbb{X}=\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
be an orthonormal basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.
The positive Laplacian on
G
is the second order differential operator

\mathcal{L}_{G}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2}.

(2.8)

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{G}
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\mathbb{X}
of
\mathfrak{g},
see e.g.
[
39
]
. The
L^{2}
-spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
is a discrete set that can be enumerated in terms of the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
as

\textnormal{Spectrum}(\mathcal{L}_{G})=\{\lambda_{[\xi]}:[\xi]\in\widehat{G}\}.

(2.9)

For a Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the right-convolution kernel
R_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}
of the operator
f(\mathcal{L}_{G})
(defined by the spectral calculus) is determined by the identity

f(\mathcal{L}_{G})\phi(x)=\phi\ast R_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}(x),\,x\in G,

(2.10)

where

\forall[\xi]\in\widehat{G},\,\,\widehat{R}_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}([\xi])=f(\lambda_{[\xi]})I_{d_{\xi}}.

(2.11)

Then the matrix-valued symbol of
f(\mathcal{L}_{G})
can be determined e.g. using Theorem
2.10
as follows

\sigma_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}(x,\xi)=\widehat{R}_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}([\xi]).

(2.12)

Since the operator
f(\mathcal{L}_{G})
is left-invariant the symbol
\sigma_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}(\xi)=\sigma_{f(\mathcal{L}_{G})}(x,\xi)
does not depend of
x\in G.
Of particular interest for the definition of the global Hörmander classes on
G,
will be the Japanese bracket function

\langle t\rangle:=(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}},\,t\geq-1.

(2.13)

In particular the symbol of the operator
\langle\mathcal{L}_{G}\rangle=(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is given by

\sigma_{\langle\mathcal{L}_{G}\rangle}([\xi]):=\langle\xi\rangle I_{d_{\xi}},\,\,\,\langle\xi\rangle:=\langle\lambda_{[\xi]}\rangle.

(2.14)

2.3.
Global Hörmander classes on compact Lie groups

In this section we denote for any linear mapping
U
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
\|U\|_{\textnormal{op}}
the standard operator norm

\|U\|_{\textnormal{op}}=\|U\|_{\textnormal{End}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}:=\sup_{l\neq 0}\|Ul\|_{e}/\|l\|_{e},

where
\|l\|_{e}=(l_{1}^{2}+\cdots+l_{n}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the Euclidean norm.

For introducing the Hörmander classes on compact Lie groups we have to measure the growth of derivatives of symbols in the group variable, for this we use vector fields
X\in T(G).
To derivate symbols with respect to the discrete variable
[\xi]\in\widehat{G}
we use difference operators. Before introducing the Hörmander classes on compact Lie groups we have to define these differential/difference operators.

Definition 2.13

(Left-invariant canonical differential operators)
.

If
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
is an arbitrary family of left-invariant vector fields, we will denote by

X_{x}^{\alpha}:=X_{1,x}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n,x}^{\alpha_{n}}

an arbitrary canonical differential operator of order
m=|\alpha|.

Also, we have to take derivatives with respect to the “discrete” frequency variable
\xi\in\textnormal{Rep}(G).
To do this, we will use the notion of difference operators introduced in
[
43
]
. Indeed, the frequency variable in the symbol
\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])
of a continuous and linear operator
A
on
C^{\infty}(G)
is discrete. This is since
\widehat{G}
is a discrete space.

Definition 2.14

(Canonical difference operators
\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}
on the dual
\widehat{G}
)
.

If
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{k},
are fixed irreducible and unitary representations of
G
, which not necessarily belong to the same equivalence class, then each coefficient of the matrix

\xi_{\ell}(g)-I_{d_{\xi_{\ell}}}=[\xi_{\ell}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi_{\ell}}},\,\quad g\in G,\,\,1\leq\ell\leq k,

(2.15)

that is each function

q^{\ell}_{ij}(g):=\xi_{\ell}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij}
,
g\in G,
defines a difference operator

\mathbb{D}_{\xi_{\ell},i,j}:=\mathscr{F}_{G}(\xi_{\ell}(g)_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\mathscr{F}^{-1}_{G}.

(2.16)

We can fix
k\geq\mathrm{dim}(G)
of these representations in such a way that the corresponding family of difference operators is admissible, that is,

\textnormal{rank}\{\nabla q^{\ell}_{i,j}(e):1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k\}=\textnormal{dim}(G).

To define higher order difference operators of this kind, let us fix a unitary irreducible representation
\xi_{\ell}
.
Since the representation is fixed we omit the index
\ell
of the representations
\xi_{\ell}
in the notation that will follow.
Then, for any given multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d_{\xi_{\ell}}^{2}}
, with

|\alpha|=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi_{\ell}}}\alpha_{i,j}
, we write

\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}:=\mathbb{D}_{1,1}^{\alpha_{11}}\cdots\mathbb{D}^{\alpha_{d_{\xi_{\ell}},d_{\xi_{\ell}}}}_{d_{\xi_{\ell}}d_{\xi_{\ell}}}

for a difference operator of order
m=|\alpha|
.

Now, we are ready for introducing the global Hörmander classes on compact Lie groups.

Definition 2.15

(Global
(\rho,\delta)
-Hörmander classes in the whole range
0\leq\delta,\rho\leq 1
)
.

We say that
\sigma\in{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
if the following symbol inequalities

\|{X}^{\beta}_{x}\mathbb{D}^{\alpha}\sigma(x,\xi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\gamma|+\delta|\beta|},

(2.17)

are satisfied for all multi-indices
\beta
and
\gamma,
and for all
(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},
where
\langle\xi\rangle
denotes the Japanese bracket function at
\lambda_{[\xi]}\in\textnormal{Spectrum}[\mathcal{L}_{G}]
defined in
2.14
.

The class
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})\equiv\textnormal{Op}({S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}))
is defined by those continuous and linear operators on
C^{\infty}(G)
such that
\sigma_{A}\in{S}^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

In the next theorem we describe some fundamental properties of the global Hörmander classes of pseudo-differential operators
[
39
]
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\rho,\delta\in[0,1]
be such that
0\leqslant\delta\leqslant\rho\leqslant 1,

\rho\neq 1.
Then

\Psi^{\infty}_{\rho,\delta}(G):=\cup_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G)

is an algebra of operators stable under compositions and adjoints, that is:

1.

the mapping

A\mapsto A^{*}:\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})\rightarrow\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})

is a continuous linear mapping between Fréchet spaces.

2.

The mapping

(A_{1},A_{2})\mapsto A_{1}\circ A_{2}:\Psi^{m_{1}}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})\times\Psi^{m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})\rightarrow\Psi^{m_{1}+m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})

is a continuous bilinear mapping between Fréchet spaces.

Moreover, any operator in the class
\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
admits a bounded extension from
L^{2}(G)
to
L^{2}(G).

Remark 2.17
.

With
0\leqslant\delta<\rho\leqslant 1
such that
\rho\geq 1-\delta,
the condition
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
where
m\in\mathbb{R},
is equivalent to the fact that, when microlocalising the operator
A
into a local coordinate system
U,
the operator
A
takes the form

Af(x)=\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(x-y)\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)f(y)\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}\xi,\,\,\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U),\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the function
a=a_{U},
is such that for every compact subset
K\subseteq U
and for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},
the inequalities

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta,K}(1+|\xi|)^{m-\rho|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|},

(2.18)

hold uniformly in
(x,\xi)\in K\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.
This characterisation of the Hörmander classes on
G
was proved in
[
41
]
. So, for any compact Lie group
G,
the classes
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
agree with the ones introduced by Hörmander
[
36
]
when
0\leqslant\delta<\rho\leqslant 1
and
\rho\geq 1-\delta.

3.
Schatten Properties

In this section we analyse the membership of pseudo-differential operators in the Schatten classes on
L^{2}(G).

3.1.
Schatten properties for operators. Limited regularity symbols

In this section we study the Schatten properties of operators with symbols of limited regularity.
Without assumptions of regularity we start with the following criterion.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that for a symbol
\sigma_{A}
we have
\sigma_{A}\in L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))
for some
1<p<2.
Then, the corresponding pseudo-differential operator
A
satisfies that
A\in\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}(L^{2}(G)),
where
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1).

Proof.

Let us consider the following criterion due to Russo (see
[
38
]
):

\|A\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}\leq(\|K\|_{p,p^{\prime}}\times\|K^{*}\|_{p,p^{\prime}})^{1/2},

(3.1)

where
K
is the kernel of
A
and
K^{*}
is the kernel of the adjoint operator
A^{*},

1<p<2,
and

\|K\|_{p,p^{\prime}}=\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\left(\smallint\limits_{G}|K(x,y)|^{p}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\textnormal{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

(3.2)

For a moment, let us assume that
A
is self-adjoint. Then,
K=K^{*}
and then

\|A\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}\leq\|K\|_{p,p^{\prime}}.

(3.3)

Observe that the Hausdorff-Young inequality (see e.g.
[
32
, Page 69]
) gives

\displaystyle\smallint\limits_{G}|K(x,y)|^{p^{\prime}}\textnormal{d}y

\displaystyle=\|y\mapsto\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}(\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot))(xy^{-1})\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G;\textnormal{d}y)}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|z\mapsto\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}(\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot))(z)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G;\textnormal{d}z)}^{p^{\prime}}\leq\|\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p^{\prime}}.

Since
p^{\prime}>2>p
the Minkowski integral inequality implies that

\displaystyle\|K\|_{p,p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\|K\|_{p^{\prime},p}=\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\left(\smallint\limits_{G}|K(x,y)|^{p^{\prime}}\textnormal{d}y\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{A}\|_{L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))}.

So, we have proved the statement in Theorem
3.1
if
A
is self-adjoint. Now, in the general case consider the decomposition of
A
into its real and imaginary part

A=\textnormal{Re}(A)+i\textnormal{Im}(A).

(3.4)

Note that

\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)}(x,[\xi])+i\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)}(x,[\xi]),

(3.5)

that

\forall x\in G,\,\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})}\asymp\|\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)}(x,[\xi])\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})}+\|\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)}(x,[\xi])\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})},

(3.6)

and using that

\|A\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}\asymp\|\textnormal{Re}(A)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}+\|\textnormal{Im}(A)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}},

(3.7)

and the following inequalities (in view of the self-adjointness of
\textnormal{Re}(A)
and
\textnormal{Im}(A)
) we have that

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\asymp\|\textnormal{Re}(A)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}+\|\textnormal{Im}(A)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sigma_{\textnormal{Re}(A)}(x,[\xi])\|_{L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))}+\|\sigma_{\textnormal{Im}(A)}(x,[\xi])\|_{L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))}

\displaystyle\asymp\|\sigma_{A}\|_{L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In terms of the
\ell^{p}
-Schatten norm on
\widehat{G}
one has the Hausdorff-Young inequality (see e.g.
[
32
, Page 67]
)

\|\widehat{f}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\,\,1\leq p\leq 2,

(3.8)

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p.
However, since the group
G
is compact one has the following refined versions for this inequality

\|\mathscr{F}_{G}^{-1}\sigma\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(G)}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\ell^{p}(\widehat{G})},\,\,\|\widehat{f}\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\,1\leq p\leq 2.

(3.9)

In particular, the second inequality in (
3.9
) is sharper than (
3.8
) because of the embedding
\mathscr{S}_{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})\subset\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\widehat{G})
for
2\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty,
see
[
32
, Page 70]
. We have used the first inequality in (
3.9
), which is the Hausdorff-Young inequality for the inverse Fourier transform to estimate the inequality

\|K\|_{p,p^{\prime}}\leq\|\sigma_{A}\|_{L^{p}(G,\ell^{p}(\widehat{G}))}

in the first part of the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In the next result, we assume more regularity in the spatial variable to deduce a criterion for pseudo-differential operators to belong to the Schatten classes.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group of dimension
n.
Let
A:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
be a continuous linear operator and assume that its matrix-valued symbol satisfies the regularity condition

\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}=\smallint\limits_{G}\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}\textnormal{d}x<\infty

(3.10)

where
N>n.
Then
A\in\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))
for all
1\leq p<\infty.

Proof.

Let us consider the matrix-valued symbol
\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])=(\sigma_{A,ij}(x,[\xi]))_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}.
The Fourier inversion formula allows one to write

\sigma_{A,ij}=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\eta}\eta(x)_{rs}\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,ij,sr}(\eta,\xi),

(3.11)

where
\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,ij,sr}([\eta],[\xi])
denotes the
(s,r)
-Fourier coefficient of the function
\sigma_{A,ij}(\cdot,[\xi])
at
\eta\in[\eta]\in\widehat{G}.
For any
f\in C^{\infty}(G),

the quantisation formula gives the identity

\displaystyle Af(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)]=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{d_{\xi}}d_{\xi}\xi_{ij}(x)\sigma_{A,\,ji}(x,[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)_{i\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i,j,\ell=1}^{d_{\xi}}d_{\xi}\xi_{ij}(x)\eta_{rs}(x)\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,ji,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)_{i\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}\eta_{rs}(x)d_{\xi}\textnormal{Tr}[\xi(x)\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi])\widehat{f}(\xi)],

where

\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi]):=(\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,ji,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi]))_{j,i=1}^{d_{\xi}}.

Let us define the operator
\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))
corresponding to the matrix
\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot)
. Let
M_{\eta_{rs}}
be the multiplication operator associated to the function
\eta_{rs}.
Note that
M_{\eta_{rs}}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(G)
with operator norm

\|M_{\eta_{rs}}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G))}=\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}.

(3.12)

Then, we have that

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(G),\,\,\forall x\in G,\,Af(x)=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}M_{\eta_{rs}}[\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))f](x).

(3.13)

Note that for all
N\in\mathbb{N},
the symbol

(x,[\xi])\mapsto\sigma_{N,rs}(x,[\xi]):=(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A,\,rs}(x,[\xi])

satisfies the Fourier transform identity

\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi])=\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\widehat{\sigma}_{N,rs}([\eta],[\xi]).

(3.14)

This identity allows us to write

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\|M_{\eta_{rs}}\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{A,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\|M_{\eta_{rs}}\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\|M_{\eta_{rs}}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{2})}\|\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\|\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}.

Since

\forall x\in G,\,|\eta_{rs}(x)|\leq\left(\sum_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}=1}^{d_{\eta}}|\eta_{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\textnormal{Tr}[\eta(x)\eta(x)^{*}]}=\sqrt{d}_{\eta},

(3.15)

we also have

\forall x\in G,\,\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}|\eta_{rs}(x)|\leq\left(\sum_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}=1}^{d_{\eta}}|\eta_{r^{\prime}s^{\prime}}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\eta}=\sqrt{\textnormal{Tr}[\eta(x)\eta(x)^{*}]}d_{\eta}=d_{\eta}\sqrt{d}_{\eta},

(3.16)

where the last implies

\,\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq d_{\eta}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}.

(3.17)

On the other hand, using the triangle inequality for the
\mathscr{S}_{p}
-norm we have that

\displaystyle\|\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|^{p}_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\smallint\limits_{G}{\sigma}_{N}(x,[\xi])\eta_{rs}(x)^{*}\textnormal{d}x\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|\eta_{rs}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}^{p}\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{p},

where, in the last line, we have used (
3.15
) to estimate

\|\eta_{rs}^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}^{p}=\|\overline{\eta}_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}^{p}=\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}^{p}\leq\sqrt{d}_{\eta}^{p}.

The Minkowski integral inequality for
p\geq 1
implies that

\displaystyle\|\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}^{p}\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{d}_{\eta}\smallint\limits_{G}\left(\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d}_{\eta}\smallint\limits_{G}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\sqrt{d}_{\eta}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}.

All the analysis above allows the following estimate for the
p
-Schatten norm of
A
as follows:

\displaystyle\|Af\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\|\textnormal{Op}(\widehat{\sigma}_{N,\,sr}([\eta],\cdot))\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}

\displaystyle=\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d_{\eta}}\|\eta_{rs}\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}d_{\eta}\sqrt{d}_{\eta}.

Thus, for
N>n
where
n=\dim(G)
we have that

\|Af\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}\lesssim\|{\sigma}_{N}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\eta}^{2}\langle\eta\rangle^{-N}<\infty,

(3.18)

where we have use that (see Lemma 3.8 of
[
29
]
)

\sum_{[\eta]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\eta}^{2}\langle\eta\rangle^{-s}<\infty\Longleftrightarrow s>n.

The proof of Theorem
3.3
is complete.
∎

The following is a consequence of Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group of dimension
n.
Let
A:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
be a continuous linear operator and assume that its matrix-valued symbol satisfies the regularity condition

\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(G,\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G}))}=\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

(3.19)

where
N>n.
Then
A\in\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G))
provided that
1\leq p<\infty.

Proof.

Using the Hölder inequality and that the Haar measure of
G
is normalised we have that

\displaystyle\smallint\limits_{G}\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}\textnormal{d}x\leq\left(\smallint\limits_{G}\|(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{N}{2}}\sigma_{A}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G})}^{p}\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

(3.20)

for all
N>n.
Then, in view of Theorem
3.3
follows the membership of
A
in the Schatten class
\mathscr{S}_{p}(L^{2}(G)).

∎

3.2.
Schatten properties of elliptic operators. The
(\rho,\delta)
-case

Now we characterise the membership of elliptic pseudo-differential operators to the Schatten classes on
L^{2}(G).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R},

r>0,
and let
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1.
Consider an elliptic pseudo-differential operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

m\in(-\infty,0)
and
A
belongs to the Schatten class of order
r>0:

A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G));

(2)

The Bessel potential of order
m
belongs to the Schatten class of order
r>0:

B_{m}:=(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{m}{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).

(3)

The following summability condition holds

\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\smallint\limits_{G}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\textnormal{d}x<\infty;

(3.21)

(4)

The following summability condition holds

\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{r}\textnormal{d}x<\infty;

(3.22)

(5)

m<-n/r.

Proof.

We start with the first equivalence
(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2):

•

(1)\Longrightarrow(2).
Assume that
A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).
Let
B_{s}:=(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{s}{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
be the Bessel potential of order
s\in\mathbb{R}.
The global functional calculus for elliptic operators on compact Lie groups (see
[
42
]
) implies that

(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{\pm{m}}\in\Psi^{\pm m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

(3.23)

In view of the composition properties of the pseudo-differential calculus we have that

B_{m}(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{-m}\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

The Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem (see Theorem 5.2 in
[
42
]
) implies that
B_{m}(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{-m}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(G).
Since
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
is an ideal of operators on the algebra
\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G)),
we have that

B_{m}=B_{m}(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{-m}(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m},

(3.24)

belongs to the Schatten class
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
provided that

(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).

Now, we are going to prove this fact. Since
m\leq 0,
if
s_{j}(A)
is the
j
-singular value of
A,
then the
j
-singular value
s_{j}((1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m})
of
(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m}
satisfies the inequality

s_{j}((1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m})\leq s_{j}(A),

(3.25)

and then the membership of
(1+|A|^{\frac{1}{|m|}})^{m}
in
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
follows from (1).

•

(2)\Longleftrightarrow(5).
Observe that

\displaystyle B_{m}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))\Longleftrightarrow B_{mr/2}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}(L^{2}(G)).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|B_{mr/2}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{2}(L^{2}(G))}^{2}=\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{mr}<\infty,

if and only if
mr<-n,
(see Lemma 3.8 of
[
29
]
) or equivalently, if
m<-n/r
proving the statement.

•

(2)\Longrightarrow(1).
Let us give a similar proof to the one given for the reverse statement. Because of
(2)
we have that
m<-n/r<0.
On the other hand, by the pseudo-differential calculus, we have that

AB_{-m}\in\Psi^{0}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G}).

(3.26)

Moreover, the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem (see Theorem 5.2 in
[
42
]
) gives the boundedness of
AB_{-m}
on
L^{2}(G).
If
B_{m}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)),
then using that
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
is an ideal in the algebra of the bounded operators on
L^{2}(G),
we have that

A=AB_{-m}B_{m}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).

•

(1)\Longleftrightarrow(3).
Note that

A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))\Longleftrightarrow|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}(L^{2}(G)).

(3.27)

Let
R_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,y)
and
K_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,y)
be the right-convolution kernel and the Schwartz kernel of the operator
|A|^{\frac{r}{2}},
respectively. We have the identity

\forall x,y\in G,\,\,R_{A}(x,xy^{-1})=K_{A}(x,y).

By the Plancherel theorem we have the equivalences

\displaystyle|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}(L^{2}(G))

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow K_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,y)\in L^{2}(G\times G)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\smallint\limits_{G}\smallint\limits_{G}|R_{A}(x,y)|^{2}\textnormal{d}y\textnormal{d}x<\infty

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|^{2}_{\textnormal{HS}}\textnormal{d}x<\infty.

•

(3)\Longleftrightarrow(4).
In order to prove the equivalence of these summability conditions we are going to apply the global functional calculus (see
[
42
]
). We start with the identity

\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])=|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])|^{\frac{r}{2}}+r_{A}(x,[\xi]),

(3.28)

where the lower term
r_{A}\in S^{\frac{mr}{2}-(\rho-\delta)}(G\times\widehat{G})
because of the asymptotic expansions. First, note that

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}

\displaystyle\leq\||\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])|^{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{\textnormal{HS}}+\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{\frac{r}{2}}+\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}.

Similarly, since
|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])|^{\frac{r}{2}}=\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])-r_{A}(x,[\xi]),
we have that

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{\frac{r}{2}}=\||\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])|^{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\leq\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}+\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}.

To estimate the remainder
\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}
let us use that
r_{A}\in S^{\frac{mr}{2}-(\rho-\delta)}(G\times\widehat{G}).
Then,

\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\leq\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\langle\xi\rangle^{-mr/2+(\rho-\delta)}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{mr/2-(\rho-\delta)}I_{d_{\xi}}\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\lesssim d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{mr}{2}-(\rho-\delta)}.

(3.29)

To prove that
(3)\Longrightarrow(4),
observe that the condition
(3)
is equivalent to the fact that
A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)),
from which we deduce that
B_{m}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).
In terms of the order
m
we have that
m<-n/r.
To prove that
(4)
holds, note that

\displaystyle\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{r}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\max\{2\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}},\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\}^{2}

\displaystyle=4\max\left\{\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\textnormal{d}x,\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim 4\max\left\{\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\textnormal{d}x,\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{mr-2(\rho-\delta)}\right\}.

Since
\rho>\delta,
we can compare
\langle\xi\rangle^{mr-2(\rho-\delta)}\leq\langle\xi\rangle^{mr},
and then

\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{mr-2(\rho-\delta)}\leq\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{mr}<\infty

(3.30)

because
mr<-n.
Then,
(3)
together with the convergence of the series in (
3.30
) implies
(4)
. Now, to finish the proof, let us assume
(4)
and from it let us deduce
(3).
To do so we are going to use the inequality

\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\leq\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{\frac{r}{2}}+\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}.

(3.31)

Then, we argue as follows:

\displaystyle\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}(2\max\{\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{\frac{r}{2}},\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}\})^{2}

\displaystyle=4\max\left\{\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\mathscr{S}_{r}}^{r}\textnormal{d}x,\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|r_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim 4\max\left\{\smallint\limits_{G}\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}\|\sigma_{|A|^{\frac{r}{2}}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}\textnormal{d}x,\sum_{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}}d_{\xi}^{2}\langle\xi\rangle^{mr-2(\rho-\delta)}\right\}

from which we deduce the convergence of the series in (3).

In view of the analysis above the proof of Theorem
3.5
is complete.
∎

3.3.
Schatten properties of non-elliptic operators. Classical symbols

We start this subsection, by showing that the order of an operator can be used as a sufficient condition for deducing its Schatten-von-Neumann properties, even if the operator is non-elliptic.

The next corollary is a consequence of Theorem
3.5
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
m<-n/r,

r>0,
and let
0\leq\delta<\rho\leq 1.
Consider a pseudo-differential operator
A\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(G\times\widehat{G})
. Then,
A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).

Proof.

Let us give an algebraic argument. Since
m<-n/r,
then the Bessel potential satisfies
B_{m}=(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{m}{2}}\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).
Using again that the class
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
is an ideal on the algebra of all bounded operators on
L^{2}(G),
and that
AB_{-m}
is bounded on
L^{2}(G)
(in view of the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem), we have that
A=AB_{-m}B_{m}
belongs to the Schatten class
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G)).

∎

Now, using the local Weyl formula (see
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
) for elliptic operators we have the following improvement of the equivalence
\textnormal{(1)}\Longleftrightarrow\textnormal{(5)}
in Theorem
3.5
for trace class operators in the Kohn-Nirenberg algebra. Here, let us consider the norm
||\cdot||_{g}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
induced by the Killing form
B(X,Y)=\textnormal{Tr}[\textnormal{ad}(X)\textnormal{ad}(Y)],

X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},
defined by
||X||_{g}:=\sqrt{-B(X,X)}.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}_{1,0}(G)
be a classical pseudo-differential operator of order
m\leq 0
. Assume that the average of the principal symbol
\sigma_{loc,A}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}G)
of
A
on the co-sphere is non-zero, that is

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)\neq 0,

(3.32)

with
\mu_{L}
denoting the Liouville measure on the spherical vector bundle
T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)=\{(x,\eta)\in T^{*}G:||\eta||_{g}=1\}.
Then,
A\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G))
if and only if
m<-n.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.6
it follows that for
m<-n,
we have
A\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G)).
Now, let us prove the converse statement, that is if
A\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G))
then
m<-n.
Let us prove this by assuming that
m\geq-n,
and let us get a contradiction.

Note that if
A\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G))
then for any orthonormal basis
(\phi_{k})_{k}
of
L^{2}(G),
the series
\sum_{k}(A\phi_{k},\phi_{k})_{L^{2}(G)}
is absolutely convergent and this sum is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
(\phi_{k}).
Then, the trace of
A
is given by

\textnormal{Tr}(A):=\sum_{k}(A\phi_{k},\phi_{k})_{L^{2}(G)}.

(3.33)

For our purposes, we will consider the orthonormal basis

\{d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i,j}:{[\xi]\in\widehat{G}:1\leq i,j\leq d_{\xi}}\},

(3.34)

of
L^{2}(G)
provided by the Peter-Weyl theorem. Consider the spectrum of the positive Laplacian

\textnormal{Spect}(\mathcal{L}_{G})=\{|\xi|:=\lambda_{[\xi]}:[\xi]\in\widehat{G}\}.

For any
\lambda>0,
we will consider the partial sum

S_{\lambda}:=\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}(\tilde{A}(d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}),d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}),

(3.35)

and the discussion above allows us to use the identity

\textnormal{Tr}(A)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}S_{\lambda}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}(\tilde{A}(d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}),d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}).

(3.36)

Next, we will prove that if
m\geq-n,
then the series in (
3.36
) is not absolutely convergent and then, that
A
is not of trace class which would contradict our hypothesis.

To simplify the proof, let us consider the real part
\textnormal{Re}(A)
and the imaginary part
\textnormal{Im}(A)
of
A,
respectively. We have the identities

\textnormal{Re}(A)=(A+A^{*})/2,\,\,\textnormal{Re}(A)=(A-A^{*})/2i.

(3.37)

To simplify the notation we will write
A_{0}=\textnormal{Re}(A),
and
A_{1}=\textnormal{Im}(A).

The following facts hold:

•

A_{i}\in\Psi^{m}(G),

i=0,1,
in view of the pseudo-differential calculus properties for sums operators.

•

A_{i},

i=0,1
, are self-adjoint operators.

Note that any entry
(A_{i}(\xi_{ij}),\xi_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}
is a real number because of the self-adjointness of
A.
Now, let us use a specific property of the Peter-Weyl basis (
3.34
).
In
[
8
]

the following Local Weyl-formula was obtained for any pseudo-differential operator
\tilde{A}\in\Psi^{0}(G):

\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}(\tilde{A}(d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}),d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij})=(2\pi)^{-n}C_{n,\tilde{A}}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}),

(3.38)

for any
\lambda>0.
Moreover, in
[
8
]
the left-hand side of this identity was simplified as follows

\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}d_{\xi}(\tilde{A}\xi_{ij},\xi_{ij})=\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}d_{\xi}\smallint\limits_{G}\textnormal{Tr}[\sigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,\xi)]\textnormal{d}x.

(3.39)

Let

\tilde{A}_{0}:=\textnormal{Re}(\tilde{A})=(\tilde{A}+{\tilde{A}}^{*})/2,\,\,\tilde{A}_{1}:=\textnormal{Im}(\tilde{A})=(\tilde{A}-{\tilde{A}}^{*})/2i.

(3.40)

Because these identities are valid for general operators of order
m>0,
for
k=0,1
we also have that

\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}(\tilde{A}_{k}(d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij}),d_{\xi}^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{ij})=(2\pi)^{-n}C_{n,\tilde{A}_{k}}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}),

(3.41)

for any
\lambda>0,
where the constant
C_{n,\tilde{A}_{k}}
is given by

C_{n,\tilde{A}_{k}}=\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}_{k}}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}_{k}}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta).

(3.42)

As before we can write

\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d_{\xi}}d_{\xi}(\tilde{A}_{k}\xi_{ij},\xi_{ij})=\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}d_{\xi}\smallint\limits_{G}\textnormal{Tr}[\sigma_{\tilde{A}_{k}}(x,\xi)]\textnormal{d}x,

(3.43)

where that the left hand side of (
3.43
) is real valued. Note that in view of (
3.41
) and (
3.43
) we have the identity

\sum_{|\xi|\leq\lambda}d_{\xi}\smallint\limits_{G}\textnormal{Tr}[\sigma_{\tilde{A}_{k}}(x,\xi)]\textnormal{d}x=(2\pi)^{-n}C_{n,\tilde{A}_{k}}\lambda^{n}+O(\lambda^{n-1}).

(3.44)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{\lambda}{2}<|\xi|\leq\lambda}d_{\xi}\smallint\limits_{G}\textnormal{Tr}[\sigma_{\tilde{A}_{k}}(x,\xi)]\textnormal{d}x

As a consequence of the previous corollary we have the following characterisation of the trace class pseudo-differential operators.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

be a classical pseudo-differential operator of order

.
Then,

Proof.

Note that if the average of the principal symbol

of

then the statement follows from Proposition

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)=0.

(3.48)

Note that

(3.49)

Note that if

\tilde{A}

Now, we will use the ideal properties of the Schatten classes to derive the extension of Proposition

to the case

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
A\in\Psi^{m}_{1,0}(G)
be a classical pseudo-differential operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that the average of the principal symbol
\sigma_{loc,A}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}G)
of
A
on the co-sphere is non-zero, that is

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)\neq 0.

(3.50)

For any

if
A\in\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(G))
then
m\leq-n/r.
Moreover, for
r=\infty,

A\in\mathscr{S}_{\infty}(L^{2}(G))=\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G))
if and only if
m\leq 0.

Proof.

Let us consider first, the case where
1<r<\infty.
Let
\varepsilon>0.
Consider the operator
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{s/2}
where
s
satisfies

s:=-\frac{n}{q}-\varepsilon<-\frac{n}{q},

and where
q:=r/(r-1)
is the conjugate exponent of
r.
Then,
q
is given by the identity

1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}.
Note that since
r>1,
one has that
q>0.
Proposition
3.7
implies that
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{s/2}\in\mathscr{S}_{q}(L^{2}(G)).
Since
q
and
r
and conjugate exponents, we have that

\tilde{A}:=A\mathcal{L}_{G}^{s/2}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(G).

Note that the principal symbol
\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}
satisfies the identity

\forall(x,\eta)\in T^{*}G\setminus\{0\},\,\,\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}(x,\eta)=\sigma_{loc,{A}}(x,\eta)\|\eta\|^{s}.

(3.51)

In particular, on the co-sphere we have

\forall(x,\eta)\in T^{*}\mathbb{S},\,\,\,\,\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}(x,\eta)=\sigma_{loc,{A}}(x,\eta).

(3.52)

Then, (
3.50
) allows to deduce the non-vanishing property

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)\neq 0,

allowing the use of the Proposition
3.7

m+s
of
\tilde{A}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}
satisfies the inequality

m+s<-n.
Then, we have that

m<-s-n=\frac{n}{q}+\varepsilon-n=n\left(\frac{1}{q}-1\right)+\varepsilon=-\frac{n}{r}+\varepsilon\,,

where the last hold tru for any
\epsilon>0
.
Taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
we have that
m\leq-n/r.

Now, let us assume that
r=\infty.
For
m\leq 0,
it follows from the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem that
A\in\mathscr{S}_{\infty}(L^{2}(G))=\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G)).
Now, let us assume that
A\in\mathscr{S}_{\infty}(L^{2}(G))=\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(G))
and let us give an argument proving that its order satisfies
m\leq 0.
Proceeding by contradiction suppose that
m>0.
Note that the conjugate exponent to
r=\infty
is
q=1.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,m)
and let
s=-n-\varepsilon.
Consider the operator
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{s/2}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G))
where
s<-n
. Then the operator

\displaystyle\tilde{A}:=A\mathcal{L}_{G}^{s/2}\in\mathscr{S}_{\infty}(L^{2}(G))\circ\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G))\subseteq\mathscr{S}_{1}(L^{2}(G)),

is of trace class. Since the principal symbol is given by (
3.51
) the co-sphere condition in (
3.50
) is satisfied and Proposition
3.7
implies that the order of
\tilde{A}
satisfies the inequality
m+s<-n.
We have that

m+s=m-n-\varepsilon=(m-\varepsilon)-n\geq-n,

which contradicts the analysis above. So, we must have that
m\leq 0.

The proof of Proposition
3.9
is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.10

Assume that

Theorem 3.11

(A)

Theorem 3.12
.

For any
\varkappa>0,
there exists a non-elliptic pseudo-differential operator
A
in the exotic class
\Psi^{-\varkappa}_{0,0}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\setminus\Psi^{-\varkappa-\varepsilon}_{0,0}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),
for all
\varepsilon>0,
that belongs to all the Schatten ideals
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),
where
r>0.

Proof.

Let
\varkappa>0,
and let
(e_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
be the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n},
and consider the set

\mathbb{D}=\{2^{k}e_{1}=(2^{k},0,\cdots,0):k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(3.64)

Define the symbol

a(\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{-\varkappa},\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{D};\,\,a(\xi)=0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{D}.

(3.65)

Observe that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},
we have

\displaystyle|\Delta^{\alpha}a(\xi)|\leq\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}{{\alpha}\choose{\beta}}|a(\xi+\beta)|\leq\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}{{\alpha}\choose{\beta}}\langle\xi+\beta\rangle^{-\varkappa}\lesssim_{\alpha}(1+|\xi|)^{-\varkappa},\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

To see that the right-hand side of this inequality is the best possible, we consider the case where
\alpha=e_{1}
and when
\xi_{k}=2^{k}e_{1}\in\mathbb{D}.
Indeed, note that

\displaystyle|\Delta^{e_{1}}a(\xi_{k})|

\displaystyle=|a(\xi_{k}+e_{1})-a(\xi_{k})|=|\langle 2^{k}e_{1}\rangle^{-\varkappa}|=\langle\xi_{k}\rangle^{-\varkappa}.

This argument proves that the estimate
|\Delta^{e_{1}}a(\xi)|\leq\langle\xi\rangle^{-\varkappa},
is sharp and even that it is an equality when
\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{D}.

To prove that the operator
A=\textnormal{Op}(a)
associated to the symbol
a
belongs to the Schatten class
\mathscr{S}_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
note that the sequence of singular values of
A
is determined by the values of its symbol, that is

s_{\xi}(A)=|a(\xi)|,\,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

(3.66)

are all the singular values of
A.
Consequently

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}s_{\xi}(A)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{D}}s_{\xi}(A)^{r}\asymp\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k\varkappa r}<\infty.

Now, to prove that
A
is not elliptic and that it is of order
m=-\varkappa,
observe that the best estimate from below that
a
satisfies is the following

|a(\xi)|\geq 0,

(3.67)

with equality when
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{D}.
The proof of this atypical case is complete.

Proposition 3.15

and let
A\in\Psi^{\mu}_{1,0}(G)
be a classical pseudo-differential operator such that its global symbols satisfies

\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{t}.

(3.68)

Then,
A\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G),
provided that the average of its principal symbol
\sigma_{loc,A}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}G)
on the co-sphere is non-zero:

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)\neq 0.

(3.69)

Proof.

Let us consider two cases:
t=0
and
t\neq 0.

•

Case 1:
t=0.
Assume that
A\in\Psi^{\mu}_{1,0}(G).
It is clear that if
\mu\leq 0
we have nothing to prove. So, the relevant case is when
\mu>0.
First, let us prove the statement in the case where
\mu\in\mathbb{N}.
To do it, we will proceed using induction. So, let us start by proving that the result is true if
\mu=1.
Then, assuming that
A\in\Psi^{1}_{1,0}(G)
and with the additional condition

\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C,

(3.70)

we have to deduce that
A\in\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G).
To prove this, we will use Corollary 2.2 of
[
40
]
to deduce that
A
is bounded on
L^{2}(G),
and then from Proposition
3.9
we use the
L^{2}(G)
-boundedness of
A
and the average condition in (
3.69
) to deduce that its order
\mu\leq 0.
This proves the case
\mu=1.
Now, let us state our induction hypothesis:

–

Let us assume that
\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
any classical pseudo-differential operator
\tilde{A}\in\Psi^{\mu_{0}}_{1,0}(G)
with a bounded operator norm of its matrix-valued symbol

\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\|\sigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C,

(3.71)

and with its principal symbol satisfying the average condition (
3.69
) belongs to the class
\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G).

Now, let
A\in\Psi^{\mu_{0}+1}_{1,0}(G)
be a classical pseudo-differential operator whose symbol satisfies (
3.69
) and (
3.70
). Define
\tilde{A}=A(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.
Note that
\tilde{A}\in\Psi^{r_{0}}_{1,0}(G)
and that its symbol satisfies (
3.71
) since

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}=\|\sigma_{{A}}(x,[\xi])\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C.

Our induction hypothesis implies that
\tilde{A}
belongs to the class
\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G).
But then, the Calderón-Vaillancourt theorem implies that
\tilde{A}
is bounded on
L^{2}(G).
Since on the co-sphere
T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)
the principal symbols of
\tilde{A}
and of
A
agree,
\tilde{A}
satisfies the average condition (
3.69
). Then, Proposition
3.9
implies that
\mu_{0}\leq 0.
Since

A(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in\Psi^{\mu_{0}}_{1,0}(G)\subseteq\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G),

then

A\in\Psi^{\mu_{0}+1}_{1,0}(G)\subseteq\Psi^{1}_{1,0}(G),

and together with the hypothesis (
3.70
) we can use the base case
\mu=1
in the inductive process to establish that
A\in\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G).
So, by the mathematical induction we have proved the statement in Theorem
3.15
in the case where
\mu\in\mathbb{N}.
Now, in the general case if
[\mu]
is the integer part of every
\mu>0,
and if
A\in\Psi^{\mu}_{1,0}(G)
is a classical pseudo-differential operator satisfying (
3.70
), we have that
A\in\Psi^{[\mu]+1}_{1,0}(G)
and then the better conclusion
A\in\Psi^{0}(G)
follows from the statement of Theorem
3.15
for integer orders. Thus, we have proved Theorem
3.15
in the case
t=0.

•

Case 2.
t\neq 0.
Assume that
A\in\Psi^{\mu}_{1,0}(G)
has a matrix-valued symbol satisfying (
3.72
) and the average condition in (
3.69
). Then,
\tilde{A}=A(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{-\frac{t}{2}}\in\Psi^{r-t}_{1,0}(G)
satisfies (
3.69
) and its matrix-valued symbol satisfies the inequality (
3.70
). From the first part of the poof, we deduce that
\tilde{A}=A(1+\mathcal{L}_{G})^{-\frac{t}{2}}\in\Psi^{0}_{1,0}(G).
Then, the pseudo-differential calculus implies that
A\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G).

The proof of Proposition
3.15
is complete.
∎

Now, we will remove the geometric average condition in Proposition
3.15
to prove a general statement on compact Lie groups.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\mu,t\in\mathbb{R}
and let
A\in\Psi^{\mu}_{1,0}(G)
be a classical pseudo-differential operator such that its global symbol satisfies

\forall(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},\,\|\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq C\langle\xi\rangle^{t}.

(3.72)

Then
A\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G).

Proof.

Note that if the average of the principal symbol
\sigma_{loc,A}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}G)
of
A
on the co-sphere is non-zero:

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)\neq 0,

(3.73)

the statement follows from Proposition
3.15
. On the other hand, if the principal symbol of
A
has average zero on
T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G),
that is,

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,A}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)=0,

(3.74)

we define the operator
\tilde{A}:=A+(\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{t}{2}}.
Is it clear that the principal symbol of
\tilde{A}
is given by

\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}:=\sigma_{loc,A}+\|\xi\|_{g}^{t}.

Note that the matrix-valued symbol of
\tilde{A}
satisfies also

\sigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,[\xi])=\sigma_{A}(x,[\xi])+|\xi|^{t}I_{d_{\xi}},\,(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G}.

(3.75)

From our hypothesis we have the estimate

\|\sigma_{\tilde{A}}(x,[\xi])\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq\tilde{C}\langle\xi\rangle^{t},\,(x,[\xi])\in G\times\widehat{G},

and the average condition

\textnormal{Av}[\sigma_{loc,\tilde{A}}]:=\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}\sigma_{loc,A}(x,\eta)d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)+\smallint\limits_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}(G)}||\eta||_{g}^{t}d\mu_{L}(x,\eta)=\textnormal{Vol}(T^{*}G)\neq 0.

(3.76)

From Proposition
3.15
follows that
\tilde{A}\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G)
and using the property
(\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{t}{2}}\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G),
one obtains that
A=\tilde{A}-(\mathcal{L}_{G})^{\frac{t}{2}}\in\Psi^{t}_{1,0}(G).
The proof is complete.
∎

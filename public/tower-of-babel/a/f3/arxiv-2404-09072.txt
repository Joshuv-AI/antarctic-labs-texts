b_{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}a_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots a_{\gamma_{j}}\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}b_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots b_{\gamma_{j}}

for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
.

Proof.

The case when
g=1
is trivial. Assume that
g
is a non-constant free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin. According to
[
40
]
,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|b_{\alpha}|^{2}\right)^{1/2k}<\infty

and there is
r\in(0,1)
such that

\varphi(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n}):=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}r^{|\alpha|}b_{\alpha}S_{\alpha}
is convergent in the operator norm topology and
\|\varphi(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})\|<1
.
Therefore, the operator
g(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})=I+\varphi(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})
is invertible, different from the identity, and
its inverse

g(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})^{-1}=I-\varphi(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})+\varphi(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})^{2}-\cdots

is in the noncommutative disk algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
. Moreover,
g(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})^{-1}

has a unique “Fourier representation”

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}r^{|\alpha|}S_{\alpha}
for some constants
a_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Consequently,
using the fact that
r^{|\alpha|}a_{\alpha}=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}S_{\alpha}^{*}g(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})^{-1}(1)
, we deduce that

g(rS_{1},\ldots,rS_{n})^{-1}
has the Fourier representation

I+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}b_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots b_{\gamma_{j}}\right)r^{|\alpha|}S_{\alpha}.

Due to the uniqueness of the Fourier representation of the elements
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, we have

a_{g_{0}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}b_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots b_{\gamma_{j}}\quad\text{ if }\ |\alpha|\geq 1.

Moreover, taking
0<r_{0}<r
, we have

g(r_{0}S_{1},\ldots,r_{0}S_{n})^{-1}=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}r_{0}^{|\alpha|}S_{\alpha}

where the convergence is in the operator norm topology.
Due to the noncommutative von Neumann inequality
[
32
]
, we deduce that

f:=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}
is a
free holomorphic function in the noncommutative ball
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{r_{0}}
and, taking into account the relations above, we also have
fg=gf=1
. The converse can be proved in a similar manner.
The proof is complete.

Definition 1.2

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
be a collection of strictly positive real numbers such that

b_{g_{0}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the associated
n
-tuple of weighted left creation operators on the full Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
. If
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
, then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{E}}
and an operator
V:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}
such that

VT_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})V,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

There is a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{E}}
and an operator
\Theta_{0}\in B({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{E}})
,

\Theta_{0}\neq 0
, such that the series

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\Theta_{0}^{*}\Theta_{0}T_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the weak operator topology.

(iii)

There is a positive operator
D(T)\in B({\mathcal{H}})
such that the series

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the weak operator topology.

If condition (iii) holds, then the operator
\widetilde{V}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
defined by setting

\widetilde{V}h:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes D(T)^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}},

where
{\mathcal{D}}:=\overline{D(T){\mathcal{H}}}
, satisfies the relation

\widetilde{V}T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\widetilde{V}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

In addition,
\widetilde{V}
is an isometry if and only if

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

where the convergence is in the weak operator topology.

Proof.

Assume that condition (i) holds. For each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, define the bounded operator

\Theta_{(\alpha)}:=uP_{e_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}}V
, where
u:e_{\alpha}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is the unitary operator given by
u(e_{\alpha}\otimes x):=x
,
x\in{\mathcal{E}}
. It is clear that

Vh=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Theta_{(\alpha)}h
for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
and

(1.1)

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\Theta_{(\alpha)}^{*}\Theta_{(\alpha)}\leq\|V\|^{2}I.

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

VT_{i}^{*}h=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}e_{\beta}\otimes\Theta_{(\beta)}T_{i}^{*}h,\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}},

and, due to the definition of the weighted shift
W_{i}
, we have

\begin{split}(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})Vh&=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})\left(\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}e_{g_{i}\beta}\otimes\Theta_{(g_{i}\beta)}h\right)=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{\frac{b_{\beta}}{b_{g_{i}\beta}}}e_{\beta}\otimes\Theta_{(g_{i}\beta)}h.\end{split}

Since
VT_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}})V
, we deduce that

(1.2)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\beta}}}\Theta_{(\beta)}T_{i}^{*}h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i}\beta}}}\Theta_{(g_{i}\beta)}

for any
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
\gamma=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Using relation (
1.2
) repeatedly, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}}\Theta_{(g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}})}T_{i_{1}}^{*}h&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}}\Theta_{(g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}})}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i_{3}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}}\Theta_{(g_{i_{3}}\cdots g_{i_{k}})}T_{i_{2}}^{*}T_{i_{1}}^{*}h&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}}\Theta_{(g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}})}T_{i_{1}}^{*}h\\
&\ \ \vdots\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{0}}}}\Theta_{(g_{0})}T_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}h&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{k}}}}\Theta_{(g_{k})}T_{i_{k-1}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}h.\end{split}

Using these relations and taking into account that
b_{g_{0}}=1
, we deduce that

\Theta_{(g_{0})}T_{i_{k}}^{*}\cdots T_{i_{1}}^{*}h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}}\Theta_{(g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}})}

for any
\gamma=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Hence, we have

\Theta_{(\gamma)}=\sqrt{b_{\gamma}}\Theta_{0}T_{\gamma}^{*},\ \gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

where
\Theta_{0}:=\Theta_{(g_{0})}
, and relation (
1.1
) shows that
the series

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\Theta_{0}^{*}\Theta_{0}T_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the weak operator topology. Therefore, item (ii) holds.
If item (ii) holds, then relation (iii) follows if we take
D(T):=\Theta_{0}^{*}\Theta_{0}
.

Now, assume the item (iii) holds .
Set
{\mathcal{D}}:=\overline{D{\mathcal{H}}}
and define
the operator
\widetilde{V}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
by relation

\widetilde{V}h:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\qquad h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

Note that
\widetilde{V}
is a bounded operator and

\widetilde{V}T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\widetilde{V},\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Indeed, we have

\left<\widetilde{V}T_{i}^{*}h,e_{\beta}\otimes h^{\prime}\right>=\sqrt{b_{\beta}}\left<DT_{\beta}^{*}T_{i}^{*}h,h^{\prime}\right>,\qquad h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}},\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

and

\begin{split}\left<(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\widetilde{V}h,e_{\beta}\otimes h^{\prime}\right>&=\left<\widetilde{V}h,(W_{i}e_{\beta}\otimes h^{\prime}\right>\\
&=\left<\sqrt{b_{g_{i}\beta}}e_{g_{i}\beta}\otimes DT_{g_{i}\beta}^{*}h,\frac{\sqrt{b_{\beta}}}{\sqrt{b_{g_{i}\beta}}}e_{g_{i}\beta}\otimes h^{\prime}\right>=\sqrt{b_{\beta}}\left<DT_{g_{i}\beta}h,h^{\prime}\right>\end{split}

for any
h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, which proves our assertion.
Consequently, item (i) holds. On the other hand, since

\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*},

it is clear that the last part of the theorem follows.
The proof is completed.

The questions that we would like to answer are the following. Under what conditions on the weights
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
, is the associated
n
-tuple

{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
of weighted left creation operators on the full Fock space

a

universal model
for the
n
-tuples of operators in a noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
which contains a ball
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0

?
What is the maximal noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{H}})
so that each
n
-tuple

{\bf W}
as universal model
?

According to Theorem
1.3
, we need to impose appropriate conditions on
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
so that, for each

such that

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}},

where the convergence is in the weak operator topology.
Note that in the scalar case when
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathbb{C}}
and
T=\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in[{\mathbb{C}}^{n}]_{\epsilon}

(1.3)

\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}\right)D(\lambda)=1.

\lambda\in[{\mathbb{C}}^{n}]_{\epsilon}
and

must be its inverse.
This leads us to impose on the coefficients

be a free holomorphic function on

1.1
, the later condition implies that

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}D(T)T_{\alpha}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}},

On the other hand, since Theorem
1.3
holds when

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be an admissible free holomorphic function with inverse
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}
and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the coresponding weighted left creation operators associated with

.

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the strong operator topology and
\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}
, the orthogonal projection of
F^{2}(H_{n})
onto the constants
{\mathbb{C}}1

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})W_{\alpha}^{*}=I,

where the convergence is in the strong operator topology.

Proof.

First, note that the operators
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
are bounded if and only if the condition (a) holds.
Since

W_{i}e_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{{b_{\alpha}}}{{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}}}e_{g_{i}\alpha}

\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}

we deduce that

(1.7)

0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Using relation (
1.7

0&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

\left(I+\sum\limits_{1\leq|\beta|\leq k}a_{\beta}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right)e_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\left(1+\sum\limits_{{\beta\gamma=\alpha}\atop{1\leq|\beta|\leq k}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\gamma}}{b_{\alpha}}\right)e_{\alpha},&\quad\text{ if }|\alpha|\geq 1\\
1,&\quad\text{ if }\alpha=g_{0}.\end{cases}

Due to relation (
1.5

{\mathbb{C}}e_{\beta}

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})W_{\alpha}^{*}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}W_{\alpha}^{*}=I,

where the convergence is in the strong operator topology.
The proof is complete.

Proposition 1.9
.

The condition (
1.4

\sup_{{N\in{\mathbb{N}}},{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}}\left|\sum_{{0\leq|\beta|\leq N}\atop{\beta\gamma=\alpha}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\gamma}}{b_{\alpha}}\right|<\infty.

Proof.

According to the proof of Proposition
1.8
, for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the operator

\sum\limits_{0\leq|\beta|\leq N}a_{\beta}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}
is diagonal and

\left\|\sum\limits_{0\leq|\beta|\leq N}a_{\beta}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|=\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\left|\sum\limits_{{\beta\gamma=\alpha}\atop{0\leq|\beta|\leq N}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\gamma}}{b_{\alpha}}\right|.

The proof is complete.
∎

Following Theorem
1.3
, we are led to define the

noncommutative Berezin kernel
associated with an arbitrary
n
-tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
to be the operator
K_{g,T}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})({\mathcal{H}})}
given by

K_{g,T}h:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

An important consequence of Theorem

is the following.

Corollary 1.10
.

If
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin, with
b_{\alpha}>0
, if
|\alpha|\geq 1
, and with inverse
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin, with
b_{\alpha}>0
, if
|\alpha|\geq 1
, and with inverse
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}a_{\gamma}Z_{\gamma}
. If
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is such that

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}|a_{\alpha}|X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\leq cI

for some
c\in(0,1)
, then
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a pure element. Moreover, there exists

\epsilon>0
such that
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
and any
n
-tuple in

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}
is pure and radial in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Proof.

First, note that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*}):=I+\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\geq I-\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}|a_{\alpha}|X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\geq 0.

Set
\Phi_{X}(Y):=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}YX_{\alpha}^{*}
for
Y\in B({\mathcal{H}})
and
\hat{\Phi}_{X}(Y):=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}|a_{\alpha}|X_{\alpha}YX_{\alpha}^{*}
. If
Y\geq 0
, then we have

\begin{split}|\left<\Phi_{X}^{k}(Y)h,h\right>|&\leq\left|\left<\hat{\Phi}_{X}^{k}(Y)h,h\right>\right|\leq\|Y\|\left|\left<\hat{\Phi}_{X}^{k}(I)h,h\right>\right|\leq c^{k}\|Y\|\|h\|^{2}\end{split}

for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}

and, hence,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\left<\Phi_{X}^{k}(Y)h,h\right>|<\infty
.
As a consequence, for each
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we can rearrange the convergent series of real numbers

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left<(-1)^{k}\Phi_{X}^{k}(\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*}))h,h\right>
and show that it is equal to

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\beta|=m}\left<\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\beta}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}a_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots a_{\gamma_{j}}\right)X_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}}^{*}h,h\right>.

Now,
using Lemma
1.1
and the fact that

\left<\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})h,h\right>=\|h\|^{2}+\left<\Phi_{X}(I)h,h\right>,

we deduce that

\begin{split}&\left<\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\beta}X_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\beta}^{*}h,h\right>=\left<\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})h,h\right>+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\beta|=m}\left<b_{\beta}X_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\beta}^{*}h,h\right>\\
&=\left<\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})h,h\right>+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\beta|=m}\left<\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\beta}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}(-1)^{j}a_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots a_{\gamma_{j}}\right)X_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}}^{*}h,h\right>\\
&=\left<\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})h,h\right>+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left<(-1)^{k}\Phi_{X}^{k}(\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*}))h,h\right>\\
&=\|h\|^{2}-\lim_{m\to\infty}\left<(-1)^{m}\Phi_{X}^{m}(I)h,h\right>\end{split}

for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently, since
\lim_{m\to\infty}\left<(-1)^{m}\Phi_{X}^{m}(I)h,h\right>=0
, we obtain

K_{g,X}^{*}K_{g,X}=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\beta}X_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(X,X^{*})X_{\beta}^{*}=I.

To prove the last part of the proposition,
let
\rho
be the radius of convergence of the free holomorphic function
g^{-1}
, i.e.

\frac{1}{\rho}:=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}\right)^{1/2k}.

Due to Lemma
1.1
, we have
\rho>0
.
If
0<t<\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{n}}
, then the
n
-tuple
(tI_{\mathcal{H}},\ldots,tI_{\mathcal{H}})
is in the ball
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\rho}
, which implies

M:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|t^{k}<\infty.

Let
0<\omega<1
be such that
\omega M<c<1
and set
\epsilon:=\sqrt{\omega t}
. Note that, if
X\in[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}
, then

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}|a_{\alpha}|\|X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\epsilon^{2k}\leq\omega\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|t^{k}=\omega M<c.

Applying the first part of the proposition, we deduce that

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
and any
n
-tuple in

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}
is pure and radial with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function and let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
. Then there is a Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{D}}
such that

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})|_{{\mathcal{H}}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{H}}
is identified with a coinvariant subspace for the operators
W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}},\ldots,W_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}}
, if and only if
T
is a pure
n
-tuple in the noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
In this case, we have

K_{g,T}T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})K_{g,T},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and the noncommutative Berezin kernel
K_{g,T}
is an isometry.

Proof.

To prove the direct implication, assume that

T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})|_{{\mathcal{H}}}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then we have

\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\sum_{p=0}^{k}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|\leq\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\sum_{p=0}^{k}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|<\infty.

Due to Proposition
1.8
,

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}

converges in the strong operator topology and
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(W,W^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}
, the orthogonal projection of
F^{2}(H_{n})
onto the constants
{\mathbb{C}}1
.
Consequently,

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}):=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}=P_{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=p}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}

converges in the strong operator topology to a positive operator. On the other hand, Proposition
1.8
shows that

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}=P_{\mathcal{H}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})W_{\alpha}^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}=I_{\mathcal{H}},

where the convergence is in the strong operator topology. Hence,
T
is a pure
n
-tuple in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Conversely, assume that
T
is a pure
n
-tuple in the noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
In this case,
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})\geq 0
and
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, where the convergence is in the weak operator topology. Applying Theorem

1.3
, we deduce that the Berezin kernel
K_{g,T}
is an isometry and

K_{g,T}T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})K_{g,T}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
where
{\mathcal{D}}:=\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*}){\mathcal{H}}}
.
Identifying
{\mathcal{H}}
with
K_{g,T}{\mathcal{H}}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 1.13
.

If
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function and

\Psi_{T}:{\mathcal{S}}:=\overline{span}\{W_{\alpha}W_{\beta}^{*}:\ \alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}\to B({\mathcal{H}}),

is a radially pure
n
-tuple with respect to

\Psi_{T}(A)=\lim_{r\to 1}K_{g,rT}^{*}(A\otimes I)K_{g,rT},\qquad A\in{\mathcal{S}},

Proof.

Fix
T\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and let
\{T^{(p)}\}_{p\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})

2.
Noncommutative Hardy algebras and functional calculus

In this section, we introduce the noncommutative Hardy algebras
F^{\infty}(g)
associated with formal power series
g
with strictly positive coefficients. We present some of their basic properties and provide a
w^{*}
-continuous
F^{\infty}(g)
-functional calculus for pure (resp. completely non-coisometric)
n
-tuples of operators in the noncommutative domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
associated with an admissible free holomorphic function
g
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series such that the coefficients
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
are strictly positive real numbers such that

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty
,

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the associated weighted left creation operators.
Let
\varphi({\bf W})=\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}
be a formal series such that,
for each
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\beta}|^{2}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{\beta\gamma}}<\infty

and

\sup_{p\in{\mathcal{P}},\|p\|\leq 1}\left\|\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}(p)\right\|<\infty,

where
{\mathcal{P}}
is the linear span of
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
.
In this case, there is a unique bounded linear operator acting on the Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
, which
we denote by
\varphi({\bf W})
, such that

\varphi({\bf W})p=\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}(p)\quad\text{ for any }\ p\in{\mathcal{P}}.

The set of all operators
\varphi({\bf W})\in B(F^{2}(H_{n}))

satisfying the above-mentioned properties is denoted by

F^{\infty}(g)
.
One can prove that
F^{\infty}(g)
is a Banach algebra, which we
call noncommutative Hardy algebra associated with the formal power series
g
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series such that the coefficients
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
are strictly positive real numbers and

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}<\infty
,

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Define the
weighted right creation operators

\Lambda_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})
by setting

\Lambda_{i}:=R_{i}G_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where
R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}
are
the right creation operators on the full Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
and
each diagonal operator
G_{i}
is defined by

G_{i}e_{\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}}e_{\alpha},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

In this case, we have

(2.1)

\Lambda_{\beta}e_{\gamma}=\frac{\sqrt{b_{\gamma}}}{\sqrt{b_{\gamma\tilde{\beta}}}}e_{\gamma\tilde{\beta}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Lambda_{\beta}^{*}e_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{b_{\gamma}}}{\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}}e_{\gamma}&\text{ if }\alpha=\gamma\tilde{\beta}\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, where
\widetilde{\beta}:=g_{i_{p}}\cdots g_{i_{1}}
is the reverse of
\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. We use the notation
{\bf\Lambda}:=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})
.
If we assume that
\tilde{g}:=1+\sum_{|\gamma|\geq 1}b_{\widetilde{\gamma}}Z_{\gamma}
is an admissible free holomorphic function, one can use relation (
1.6
) to show
that

\Delta_{\widetilde{g}^{-1}}({\bf\Lambda},{\bf\Lambda}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\tilde{\beta}}\Lambda_{\beta}\Delta_{\widetilde{g}^{-1}}({\bf\Lambda},{\bf\Lambda}^{*})\Lambda_{\beta}^{*}=I,

where the convergence is in the strong operator topology. the proof is similar to that of Proposition
1.8
.
As a consequence, one can easily see that

(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{{\widetilde{g}}^{-1}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
and

U^{*}\Lambda_{i}U=W_{i}^{(\widetilde{g})}
,
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
where
(W_{1}^{(\widetilde{g})},\ldots,W_{n}^{(\widetilde{g})})
is the universal model associated with
\widetilde{g}
and

U\in B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
is the unitary operator
defined by
Ue_{\alpha}:=e_{\tilde{\alpha}}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.

Using the weighted right creation
operators
{\bf\Lambda}=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})

associated with the formal power series
g
with the properties mentioned above, we can define the corresponding
noncommutative Hardy algebra
R^{\infty}(g)
.
Indeed, if
g({\bf\Lambda})=\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\tilde{\beta}}\Lambda_{\beta}
is a formal sum such that,
for each
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\beta}|^{2}\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{\gamma\beta}}<\infty

and

\sup_{p\in{\mathcal{P}},\|p\|\leq 1}\left\|\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\tilde{\beta}}\Lambda_{\beta}(p)\right\|<\infty,

then there is a unique bounded operator on
F^{2}(H_{n})
, which we
denote by
g({\bf\Lambda})
, such that

g({\bf\Lambda})p=\sum\limits_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\tilde{\beta}}\Lambda_{\beta}(p)
for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}.

The set of all operators
g({\bf\Lambda})\in B(F^{2}(H_{n}))

satisfying the above-mentioned properties is denoted by
R^{\infty}(g)
. Note that
R^{\infty}(g)
is a Banach algebra.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series such that the coefficients

\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
are strictly positive real numbers and such that

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty,\qquad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}<\infty,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})

(
resp.

{\bf\Lambda}:=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}))
be the associated weighted left
(resp. right) creation operators. Then

(i)

F^{\infty}(g)=\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}\}^{\prime}=R^{\infty}(g)^{\prime}
, where
′
stands for the commutant,

(ii)

F^{\infty}(g)^{\prime\prime}=F^{\infty}(g)
and

R^{\infty}(g)^{\prime\prime}=R^{\infty}(g)
.

Proof.

First, note that the conditions in the hypothesis are necessary and sufficient to ensure that
the operators
W_{i}
and
\Lambda_{i}
are bounded.
Since

W_{i}\Lambda_{j}=\Lambda_{j}W_{i}
for any

i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, it is clear that
F^{\infty}(g)\subset\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}\}^{\prime}
. To prove the reverse inclusion, let
A\in\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}\}^{\prime}
. Since
A(1)\in F^{2}(H_{n})
, we have

A(1)=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\beta}}}e_{\beta}
for some
coefficients
\{c_{\beta}\}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
with
\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\beta}|^{2}\frac{1}{b_{\beta}}<\infty
. On the other hand, since
A\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{i}A
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, relations (
1.7
)
and (
2.1
) imply

\begin{split}Ae_{\alpha}&=\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}A\Lambda_{\tilde{\alpha}}(1)=\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}\Lambda_{\tilde{\alpha}}A(1)=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}\frac{\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{b_{\beta\alpha}}}e_{\beta\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}(e_{\alpha}).\end{split}

Therefore, for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\beta}|^{2}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\beta\alpha}}<\infty

and
A(q)=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}(q)
for any polynomial
q\in{\mathcal{P}}

&=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}r^{|\beta|}\sqrt{b_{\beta}}\left<p({\bf W})^{*}e_{\beta},e_{\sigma}\right>\left<W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\gamma},\Delta_{g^{-1},r{\bf W}}e_{\epsilon}\right>\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}r^{|\beta|}\sqrt{b_{\beta}}\left<\varphi({\bf W})^{*}e_{\beta},e_{\sigma}\right>\left<W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\gamma},\Delta_{g^{-1},r{\bf W}}e_{\epsilon}\right>\\
&=\left<[\varphi({\bf W})^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}]K_{g,r{\bf W}}e_{\gamma},e_{\sigma}\otimes e_{\epsilon}\right>\end{split}

for any
r\in(\delta,1)
and
\gamma,\sigma,\epsilon\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
Consequently, since
\varphi_{r}({\bf W})
and
\varphi({\bf W})
are
bounded operators, we deduce that

K_{g,r{\bf W}}\varphi_{r}({\bf W})^{*}=[\varphi({\bf W})^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}]K_{g,r{\bf W}}.

Since
K_{g,r{\bf W}}
is an isometry, we deduce that
\|\varphi_{r}({\bf W})\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
for
r\in(\delta,1)
.
Now, using that fact that
\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi_{r}({\bf W})e_{\alpha}=\varphi({\bf W})e_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, an approximation argument shows that

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi_{r}({\bf W})=\varphi({\bf W})
.
Since
Y\mapsto Y\otimes I
is SOT-continuous on bounded sets, we can pass to the limit in relation (
2.3
) and deduce that

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi_{r}(T)=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})K_{g,T}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Our next goal is to extend the
w^{*}
-continuous
F^{\infty}(g)
-functional calculus calculus to completely
non-coisometric
n
-tuples in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
. We define the domain
{{\mathcal{D}}}^{cnc}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
of all
n
-tuples
T\in{{\mathcal{D}}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
which are completely non-coisometric and note that
{{\mathcal{D}}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})\subset{{\mathcal{D}}}^{cnc}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Definition 2.8
.

An
n
-tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is said to be completely non-coisometric with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
if
there is no nonzero joint invariant subspace under
T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{n}^{*}
such that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}=0.

We remark that, in the single variable case of one contraction, i.e.
\|T\|\leq 1
, we recover the well-known definition of a completely non-coisometric contraction.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
, then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

There is no
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
h\neq 0
such that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}h=0
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

(ii)

T
is completely non-coisometric with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

(iii)

The noncommutative Berezin kernel
K_{g,T}
is a one-to-one operator.

Proof.

Note that item (ii) is equivalent to the condition

\bigcap_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\ker\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}=\{0\},

which shows that (i) is equivalent to (ii). Recall that the noncommutative Berezin kernel associated with

T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
is the operator
K_{g,T}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes\overline{\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})({\mathcal{H}})}
given by

K_{g,T}h=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\otimes\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h,\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

Consequently, if
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, then

\left<K_{g,T}^{*}K_{g,T}h,h\right>=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\alpha}\left<T_{\alpha}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}h,h\right>=0

if and only if
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}h=0
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, if and only if

h\in\bigcap_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\ker\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*}
.
Since

\bigcap_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\ker\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})T_{\alpha}^{*}=\bigcap_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\ker\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})^{1/2}T_{\alpha}^{*},

it is clear that (iii) is equivalent to (i). The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Any
n
-tuple
T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
which is either pure or has the defect operator

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(T,T^{*})
injective is completely non-coisometric with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Recall that
A\in B({\mathcal{H}})
is a trace class operator if
\|A\|_{1}:=\text{\rm trace}(A^{*}A)^{1/2}<\infty
. We denote by
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
the ideal of trace class operators in
B({\mathcal{H}})
. It is well-known that
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})
is a Banach space with respect to the trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
and the dual space
{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{H}})^{*}
is isometrically isomorphic to
B({\mathcal{H}})
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function with
b_{\alpha}>0
, if
|\alpha|\geq 1
, and
such that

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}<\infty,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
F^{\infty}(g)
be the associated noncommutative Hardy algebra. If
\Phi:F^{\infty}(g)\to B({\mathcal{H}})
is a linear map, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Phi:(F^{\infty}(g),w^{*})\to(B({\mathcal{H}}),w^{*})
is continuous;

(ii)

\Phi:(F^{\infty}(g),w^{*})\to(B({\mathcal{H}}),WOT)
is sequential continuous.

Proof.

First, note that Theorem
2.5
ensures that
F^{\infty}(g)
is a
w^{*}
-closed subspace in
B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.
Since
{\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))^{*}=B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
, the duality theory for Banach spaces shows that

F^{\infty}(g)=\left({\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))/_{{}^{\perp}F^{\infty}(g)}\right)^{*},

where

{}^{\perp}F^{\infty}(g):=\left\{T\in{\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n})):\ \text{\rm trace}(TF)=0,\text{ for any }F\in F^{\infty}(g)\right\}

is the pre-annihilator of
F^{\infty}(g)
in
{\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.
Since
{\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
is separable, so is the closed subspace
{}^{\perp}F^{\infty}(g)
. Now, it is clear that
the quotient
{\mathcal{T}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))/_{{}^{\perp}F^{\infty}(g)}
is a separable Banach space and, consequently, the noncommutative Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}(g)
is the dual of a separable Banach space.
Applying Proposition 20.3 from
[
15
]
in our setting we complete the proof.
∎

In what follows, we provide a
w^{*}
-continuous
F^{\infty}(g)
-functional
calculus for the completely non-coisometric
n
-tuples in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be an admissible free holomorphic function with the property that

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}<\infty
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

and let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the universal model of the noncommutative domain
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
.
Suppose that
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{cnc}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

(i)

If
T\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
, then the completely contractive linear map

\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}(g)\to B({\mathcal{H}}),\qquad\Phi(p({\bf W})):=p(T),

where
p
is any polynomial in
n
noncommutative indeterminates,
has a unique extension to a
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism

\Psi_{T}:F^{\infty}(g)\to B({\mathcal{H}})

which is completely contractive and sequentially WOT-continuous (resp. SOT-continuous).
Moreover, if
\varphi({\bf W})\in F^{\infty}(g)
has the Fourier representation
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
, then

\Psi_{T}(\varphi({\bf W}))=\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{N}},s\leq N}\left(1-\frac{s}{N+1}\right)\sum_{|\alpha|=s}c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}.

(ii)

In particular, if
T
and
{\bf W}
are radially pure with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
and
\varphi({\bf W})\in F^{\infty}(g)
has the Fourier representation
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
, then

\varphi_{r}(T):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}r^{|\alpha|}c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}
converges in the operator norm and

\begin{split}\Psi_{T}(\varphi({\bf W}))&=\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi_{r}(T)=\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{r\to 1}K_{rT}^{*}[\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}]K_{rT}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\varphi({\bf W})\in F^{\infty}(g)
and let
\{p_{k}({\bf W})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
be a sequence of polynomials such that
SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{k}({\bf W})=\varphi({\bf W})
. Since
T\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
, Corollary
1.10
shows that

(2.4)

p_{k}(T)K_{g,T}^{*}x=K_{g,T}^{*}(p_{k}({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})x,\qquad x\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}.

Using the fact that the map
Y\mapsto Y\otimes I
is SOT-continuous on bounded sets, we can define the operator
A:\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}\to{\mathcal{H}}
by setting

(2.5)

A(K_{g,T}^{*}x):=\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{k}(T)K_{g,T}^{*}x=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})x,\qquad x\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}.

Note that
A
does not depend on the choice of the sequence
\{p_{k}({\bf W})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{P}}(W)
.
Consequently, according to Theorem
2.4
, we can take

p_{k}({\bf W}):=\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{N}},s\leq k}\left(1-\frac{s}{N+1}\right)\sum_{|\alpha|=s}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha},

in which case

\|p_{k}({\bf W})\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
.
Suppose that
T\in\overline{{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})}
and let
\{T^{(m)}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a sequence such that
\|T-T^{(m)}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
Due to Theorem
1.13
, we have
\|p_{k}(T^{(m)})\|\leq\|p_{k}({\bf W})\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
and

\begin{split}\|A(K_{g,T}^{*}x)\|&\leq\sup_{k}\|p_{k}(T)\|\|K_{g,T}^{*}x\|\leq\sup_{k,m}\|p_{k}(T^{(m)})\|\|K_{g,T}^{*}x\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|\|K_{g,T}^{*}x\|\end{split}

for any
x\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}.

Since
T
is completely non-coisometric, Proposition
2.9
shows that the noncommutative Berezin kernel
K_{g,T}
is one-to-one, thus
\text{\rm range}\,K_{g,T}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently,
A
can be uniquely be extended to a bounded linear operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
, which is also denoted by
A
, such that

\|Ah\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|\|h\|
for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

In what follows, we show that
Ah=\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{k}(T)h
for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. To show that the later limit exists, let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}
be such that
\|y_{n}-h\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
\|y_{N}-h\|<\frac{\epsilon}{2\|\varphi({\bf W})\|}
.
Using the fact that
\|p_{k}(T^{(m)})\|\leq\|p_{k}({\bf W})\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
for any
m,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|Ah-p_{k}(T^{(m)})h\|&\leq\|A\|\|h-y_{N}\|+\|p_{k}(T^{(m)})\|\|y_{N}-h\|+\|Ay_{N}-p_{k}(T^{(m)})y_{N}\|\\
&\leq 2\|\varphi({\bf W})\|\|y_{N}-h\|+\|Ay_{N}-p_{k}(T^{(m)})y_{N}\|\\
&\leq\epsilon+\|Ay_{N}-p_{k}(T^{(m)})y_{N}\|.\end{split}

Taking
m\to\infty
, we obtain

\|Ah-p_{k}(T)h\|\leq\epsilon+\|Ay_{N}-p_{k}(T)y_{N}\|.

Due to relation (
2.4
) and taking
k\to\infty
, we get

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|Ah-p_{k}(T)h\|\leq\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
. Hence,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|Ah-p_{k}(T)h\|=0
, which proves our assertion.

Now, we define the map
\Psi_{T}:F^{\infty}(g)\to B({\mathcal{H}})
by setting
\psi_{T}(\varphi({\bf W}))=\varphi(T):=A
.
Let
\{\varphi_{\iota}({\bf W})\}_{\iota}\subset F^{\infty}(g)
be a bounded net such that

\varphi_{\iota}({\bf W})\to\varphi({\bf W})\in F^{\infty}(g)
in the strong operator topology.
Then
\varphi_{\iota}({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}\to\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
in the same topology.
According to relation (
2.5
), we have

\varphi_{\iota}(T)K_{g,T}^{*}x=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi_{\iota}({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})x\to K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})x=\varphi(T)K_{g,T}^{*}x

for any
x\in F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since
\|\varphi_{\iota}(T)\|\leq\|\varphi_{\iota}({\bf W})\|
, the net
\{\varphi_{\iota}(T)\}_{\iota}
is also bounded. Since
\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, a standard approximation argument, as above, shows that

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{\iota}\varphi_{\iota}(T)=\varphi(T)
. In a similar manner, one can prove a similar result when SOT is replaced by WOT or
w^{*}
-topology.
Consequently, since the map
\Psi_{T}:F^{\infty}(g)\to B({\mathcal{H}})
is sequentially
w^{*}
-continuous, we can use Lemma
2.11
to conclude that
\Psi_{T}
is
w^{*}
-continuous.

Now, we prove that
\Psi_{T}
is completely contractive homomorphism.
Let
[\varphi_{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\in M_{q\times q}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes F^{\infty}(g)
.
According to Theorem
2.4
, there a sequence of matrices
[p_{k}^{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\in M_{q\times q}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
such that

[p_{k}^{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\to[\varphi_{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}
strongly as
k\to\infty
and

\|[p_{k}^{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\|\leq\|[\varphi^{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\|
.
Let
h=\oplus_{i=1}^{q}h_{i}\in\oplus_{i=1}^{q}F^{2}(H_{n})
and let
\{T^{(m)}\}_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}^{pure}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{H}})
be a sequence such that
\|T-T^{(m)}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
Note that, due to the results above,

[\varphi_{ij}(T)]_{q}h=\lim_{k\to\infty}[p_{k}^{ij}(T)]_{q}h

and

\begin{split}\|[\varphi_{ij}(T)]_{q}h\|&\leq\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|[p_{k}^{ij}(T)]_{q}\|\|h\|\leq\sup_{k,m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|[p_{k}^{ij}(T^{(m)})]_{q}]_{q}\|\|h\|\leq\|[\varphi_{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\|\|h\|.\end{split}

Consequently,
\|[\varphi_{ij}(T)]_{q}\|\leq\|[\varphi_{ij}({\bf W})]_{q}\|
, which proves that
\Psi_{T}
is a completely contractive linear map. On the other hand, since
\Psi_{T}
is a homomorphism on the algebra of polynomials

{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
and
\Psi_{T}
is sequentially WOT-continuous, one can use the WOT-density of
{\mathcal{P}}({\bf W})
in
F^{\infty}(g)
to show that
\Psi_{T}
is a homomorphism on
F^{\infty}(g)
.

To prove item (ii), let
T
and
{\bf W}
be radially pure
n
-tuples with respect to
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
and let
\varphi({\bf W})\in F^{\infty}(g)
have the Fourier representation
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
.
According to the proof of Theorem
2.7
,

\varphi_{r}({\bf W}):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}r^{|\alpha|}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
converges in the operator norm,

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi_{r}({\bf W})=\varphi({\bf W})
and
\|\varphi_{r}(W)\|\leq\|\varphi(W)\|

for any
r\in(\delta,1)
, where
\delta\in(0,1)
is chosen such that both
r{\bf W}
and
rT
are pure elements.
Consequently, using the von Neumann inequality of Corollary
1.14
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}r^{|\alpha|}T_{\alpha}\right\|&\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{k}\left\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}\right\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{k}\left\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-r}\|\varphi({\bf W})\|<\infty.\end{split}

Therefore,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}r^{|\alpha|}T_{\alpha}
converges in the operator norm.
Using the properties of the Berezin kernel, for any
t,r\in(\delta,1)
such that
rt\in(\delta,1)
, we have

(2.6)

K_{g,rT}^{*}(\varphi_{t}({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\varphi_{t}(rT)K_{g,rT}^{*}.

On the other hand, since
\varphi(rT)
and
\varphi_{t}(rT)
are given by series which are convergent in norm, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can find
N_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that

\begin{split}\|\varphi_{t}(rT)-\varphi(rT)\|&\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{0}}\sum_{|\beta|=k}r^{k}(t^{k}-1)c_{\beta}T_{\beta}\right\|+\epsilon\end{split}

Taking
t\to 1
, we deduce that
\lim_{t\to 1}\varphi_{t}(rT)=\varphi(rT)
in the operator norm. Consequently, relation (
2.6
) implies

K_{g,rT}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\varphi(rT)K_{g,rT}^{*}.

Hence, we also deduce that
\|\varphi(rT)\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
for any
r\in(\delta,1)
.
Now, since
T_{i}K_{g,T}^{*}=K_{g,T}^{*}(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and using the convergence in norm of the series defining
\varphi(rT)
and
\varphi(r{\bf W})
, we deduce that

(2.7)

\varphi(rT)K_{g,T}^{*}=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi(r{\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})

for any
r\in(\delta,1)
.
Taking
r\to 1
in this relation, we deduce that the map
\tilde{A}:\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}\to{\mathcal{H}}
given by

\tilde{A}y:=\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi(rT)y
for
y\in\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}
, is well-defined, linear, and

\begin{split}\tilde{A}(K_{g,T}^{*}x)&\leq\limsup_{r\to 1}\|\varphi(rT)\|\|K_{g,T}^{*}x\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|\|K_{g,T}^{*}x\|.\end{split}

Consequently,
\tilde{A}
has a unique continuous extension to
{\mathcal{H}}
and

\|\tilde{A}\|\leq\|\varphi({\bf W})\|
. Now, using a standard approximation argument, we can show that
\tilde{A}h=\lim_{r\to 1}\varphi(rT)h
for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
Due to the definition of
\tilde{A}
and relation (
2.7
), we have

\tilde{A}K_{g,T}^{*}=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})
. On the other hand, due to relation (
2.5
), we have

AK_{g,T}^{*}=K_{g,T}^{*}(\varphi({\bf W})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})
. Consequently, since both operators
A
and
\tilde{A}
are bounded and
\text{\rm range}K_{g,T}^{*}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, we conclude that
A=\tilde{A}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Multipliers and multi-analytic operators

This section is devoted to the multipliers on Hilbert spaces
F^{2}(g)
associated with formal power series
g
with
strictly positive coefficients and to the multi-analytic operators with respect to the universal model associated with
g
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series with
b_{\alpha}>0
and let
F^{2}(g)
be the Hilbert space of formal power series in indeterminates
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
with orthogonal basis
\{Z_{\alpha}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}
such that
\|Z_{\alpha}\|_{g}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}}
. Note that

F^{2}(g)=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{g}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{b_{\alpha}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}

and
\left\{\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
is an orthonormal basis for
F^{2}(g)
.
The
left multiplication
operator
L_{Z_{i}}
on
F^{2}(g)
is defined by
L_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=Z_{i}\zeta
for all
\zeta\in F^{2}(g)
. We also use the notation
L_{Z_{i}}^{(g)}
when needed.
One can easily see that
L_{Z_{i}}
is a bounded operator if and only if

(3.1)

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty.

We remark that the operator
U:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(g)
, defined by
U(e_{\alpha}):=\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, is unitary and

UW_{i}=L_{Z_{i}}U,\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is the universal model associated with
g
.
Similarly, we define the
right multiplication
operator
R_{Z_{i}}:F^{2}(g)\to F^{2}(g)
by setting
R_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=\zeta Z_{i}
for all
\zeta\in F^{2}(g)
. We also use the notation
R_{Z_{i}}^{(g)}
when needed. Note that
R_{Z_{i}}
is a bounded operator if and only if

(3.2)

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}<\infty.

We also have
U\Lambda_{i}=R_{Z_{i}}U
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where

{\bf\Lambda}:=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})
was defined in Section 2.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces and let
f=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}^{\prime}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series where the coefficients
\{b_{\alpha}^{\prime}\}
satisfy similar properties to those satisfied by

\{b_{\alpha}\}
.
A formal power series
\varphi=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}Z_{\alpha}\otimes A_{(\alpha)}
, with operator-valued coefficients
A_{(\alpha)}\in B({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
, is called (
twisted
)
right multiplier
from
F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
to
F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
and we denote
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
if, for any

\zeta=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}Z_{\beta}\otimes h_{(\beta)}
in
F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, we have

\zeta\varphi:=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}Z_{\gamma}\otimes\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\alpha=\gamma}A_{(\alpha)}h_{(\beta)}\in F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}.

Due to the closed graph theorem, the right multiplication operator

R_{\varphi}:F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
defined by
R_{\varphi}\zeta:=\zeta\varphi
is a bounded linear operator. The (right) multiplier norm of
\varphi
is given by

\|\varphi\|^{{\mathcal{M}}^{r}}:=\|R_{\varphi}\|
.
If
g=f
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}={\mathcal{E}}_{2}={\mathcal{E}}
, we use the notation

{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}):={\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}})
. Note that

{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}})
becomes a Banach algebra with respect to the multiplier norm.
Similarly, we introduce the the set of
left multipliers
from
F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
to
F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
and denote it by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
. In this case, the left multiplication operator

L_{\varphi}:F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is defined by
L_{\varphi}\zeta:=\varphi\zeta
and it is a bounded linear operator. The (left) multiplier norm of
\varphi
is given by

\|\varphi\|^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}}:=\|L_{\varphi}\|
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be formal power series satisfying the conditions corresponding to relations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Then the following statements hold.

(i)

A bounded linear operator
X:F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
satisfies the relation

X(L_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}})=(L_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}})X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

if and only if there is
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
such that
X=R_{\varphi}
.

(ii)

A bounded linear operator
X:F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
satisfies the relation

X(R_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}})=(R_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}})X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

if and only if there is
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
such that
X=L_{\varphi}
.

Since the proof of the theorem is similar to the proof of Theorem 4.3 from
[
46
]
, we omit it.

Let
{\bf W}^{(f)}:=(W_{1}^{(f)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(f)})
and
{\bf W}^{(g)}:=(W_{1}^{(g)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(g)})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with the formal power series
f
and
g
, respectively. We say that a bounded linear operator

\widehat{X}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is multi-analytic with respect to
{\bf W}^{(g)}
and
{\bf W}^{(f)}
if

\widehat{X}(W_{i}^{(g)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}})=(W_{i}^{(f)}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}})\widehat{X},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

If
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
, we define the unitary operator

U_{g}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(g)
by

U_{g}(e_{\alpha}):=\sqrt{b_{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

A consequence of Theorem
3.1
is the following

Corollary 3.2
.

A bounded linear operator

\widehat{X}:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is multi-analytic with respect to
{\bf W}^{(g)}
and
{\bf W}^{(f)}
if and only if

\widehat{X}=(U_{f}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{2}})R_{\varphi}(U_{g}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}})

for some right multiplier
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(g)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
.

Combining the results above with Theorem
2.5
and Proposition
2.6
, one can easily deduce that following

Theorem 3.3
.

The right multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
is completely isometrically isomorphic to the noncommutative Hardy algebra
R^{\infty}(f)\bar{\otimes}_{min}B({\mathcal{K}})
. Moreover, the map

\Psi:{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{K}})\to R^{\infty}(f)\bar{\otimes}_{min}B({\mathcal{K}})
defined by

\Psi(\varphi):=(U_{f}^{*}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}})R_{\varphi}(U_{f}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{K}}}),\qquad\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}})

is a completely isometric isomorphism.

It is quite clear that there is a version of Theorem
3.3
for the left multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{K}})
. We remark that, Theorem

3.3
can be used to deduce that

{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{1},F^{2}(f)\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
is completely isometrically isomorphic to
R^{\infty}(f)\bar{\otimes}_{min}B({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})
.

4.
Admissible free holomorphic functions for operator model theory

The goal of this section is to introduce
several classes of admissible free holomorphic functions for operator model theory and the associated noncommutative domains. The examples presented here will be refered to throughout the paper.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that the noncommutative set
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain if
g
is a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that

g^{-1}=1+\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}

for some coefficients
a_{\alpha}\leq 0
and
a_{g_{i}}<0
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that

g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha},

where
a_{\alpha}\leq 0
for
|\alpha|\geq 1
and
a_{g_{i}}<0
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A close look at the results from
[
44
]
reveals that
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function. The regular domains were extensively studied in
[
44
]
. In this case, we have

b_{\alpha}b_{\beta}\leq b_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

We remark that the converse is not true. A weaker converse of this result was considered in
[
3
]
and will be extended in Theorem
4.3
.

The next result provides sufficient conditions on a free holomorphic functions
g
to ensure that
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain. This result extends the corresponding one from
[
3
]
and the proof is similar.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin,

which satisfies the conditions

(i)

b_{g_{0}}=1
,
b_{\alpha}>0
for
|\alpha|\geq 1
;

(ii)

for any
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\frac{b_{\beta g_{i}}}{b_{g_{j}\beta g_{i}}}\leq\frac{b_{\beta}}{b_{g_{j}\beta}}.

Then
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function and
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series with
b_{\alpha}=b_{\beta}
for any
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|=|\beta|
such that

b_{0}=1
and
b_{\alpha}>0
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. In this case, the Hilbert space

F^{2}(g)=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{g}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{b_{\alpha}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}

is called
unitarily invariant Hilbert space
. If, in addition,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}=1,

where
b_{k}:=b_{|\alpha|}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|=k
, we call
F^{2}(g)

normalized unitarily invariant Hilbert space
.

A simple consequence of Theorem
4.3
is the following

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{|\alpha|}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
b_{|\alpha|}>0
and
\left\{\frac{b_{|\alpha|}}{b_{|\alpha|+1}}\right\}
is a decreasing sequence.
Then
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function and

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}
is a regular domain.

Some classes of unitarily invariant Hilbert spaces are given in the following examples.

Example 4.5
.

For each
s\in(0,\infty)
, consider the formal power series

g_{s}:=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})^{k}.

The associated Hilbert space
F^{2}(g_{s})
has orthonormal basis

\left\{\sqrt{\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)}Z_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
. It is easy to see that
\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}=\frac{k+1}{s+k}
and, consequently,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}=1
.
Note that the sequence
\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}
is decreasing if and only if
s\in(0,1]
. In this case,
Theorem
4.3
implies that
{\mathcal{D}}_{g_{s}^{-1}}
is a regular domain.
We remark that if
s=1
, then the corresponding subspace
F^{2}(g_{1})
is the full Fock space on
n
generators.
On the other hand, if
s=n
(resp.
s=n+1
) we obtain the noncommutative Hardy (resp. Bergman) space over the unit ball

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
. In the particular case when
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain the noncommutative weighted Bergman space over
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
, which was extensively studied in
[
41
]
,
[
44
]
, and
[
46
]
.
The scale of generalized Bergman spaces
\{F^{2}(g_{s}):s\in(0,\infty)\}
was never considered and studied in the multivariable noncommutative setting, except the case when
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We remark that when
s\in(0,1]
, the Hilbert spaces
F^{2}(g_{s})
are noncommutative generalizations of the classical Besov-Sobolev spaces on the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with representing kernel
\frac{1}{(1-\left<z,w\right>)^{s}}
,
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}
.

Example 4.6
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let

\xi_{s}:=1+\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1}(|\alpha|+1)^{s}Z_{\alpha}.

The associated Hilbert space
F^{2}(\xi_{s})
has orthonormal basis
\left\{\sqrt{(|\alpha|+1)^{s}}Z_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
. Since

\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}=\frac{(k+1)^{s}}{(k+2)^{s}}\to 1,\quad\text{as }\ k\to\infty,

F^{2}(\xi_{s})
is a normalized unitarily invariant Hilbert space.
On the other hand, it is clear that
the sequence
\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k+1}}
is decreasing if and only if
s\leq 0
. In this case, Theorem
4.3
shows that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\xi_{s}^{-1}}
is a regular domain. We remark that the scale of spaces
\{F^{2}(\xi_{s}):s\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
contains the noncommutative Dirichlet space
F^{2}(\xi_{-1})
over the unit ball

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
. When
s=0
, we recover again the full Fock space with
n
generators.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
the following conditions are satisfied.

(a)

b_{\alpha}>0
and

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(b)

If
g^{-1}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is the inverse of
g
, there is
N_{0}\geq 2
such that
a_{\alpha}\geq 0

(
or
a_{\alpha}\leq 0)
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq N_{0}
.

Then
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function.

Proof.

Assume that
a_{\alpha}\leq 0
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq N_{0}
.
We prove that there is
C>0
such that

(4.1)

\left|\sum_{{0\leq|\beta|\leq N}\atop{\beta\gamma=\alpha}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\gamma}}{b_{\alpha}}\right|<C

for any
N>N_{0}
and any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq N_{0}
.
Due to Lemma
1.1
, we have

a_{g_{0}}=1
and
a_{g_{i}}=-b_{g_{i}}<0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Given
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, we say that
\beta\leq\alpha
if
\alpha=\beta\gamma
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. In this case, we set
\alpha\backslash\beta:=\gamma
.
According to relation (
1.5
), we have

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha}a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}=0

if
|\alpha|\geq 1.

Consequently,

(4.2)

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N}a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|>N}a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}=0.

Since
a_{\beta}\leq 0
for every
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\beta|\geq N>N_{0}
, the second sum in the relation above is negative. Now, relation (
4.2
) implies

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N}a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}\geq 0

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|>N_{0}
and
N>N_{0}
. Using the later relation and the fact that

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|>N_{0}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}\leq 0,

we deduce that

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}\right|&\leq\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N_{0}}\frac{a_{\beta}b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}\\
&\leq\left(\sup_{{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N_{0}}\atop{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}}\frac{b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}\right)\left(\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|\leq N_{0}}|a_{\beta}|\right).\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we obtain

\sup_{{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N_{0}}\atop{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}}\frac{b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}<\infty.

Indeed, let
\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
be such that
k\leq N_{0}
and let
\alpha=\beta\gamma
, where
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Let
M>0
be such that
\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<M
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Note that

\frac{b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}=\frac{b_{\gamma}}{b_{g_{i_{k}}\gamma}}\frac{b_{g_{i_{k}}\gamma}}{b_{g_{i_{k-1}}g_{i_{k}}\gamma}}\cdots\frac{b_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i+k}\gamma}}{b_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i+k}\gamma}}<M^{|\beta|}

for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and any
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\beta|\leq N_{0}
.
Consequently,
\sup_{{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\leq\alpha,|\beta|\leq N_{0}}\atop{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}}\frac{b_{\alpha\backslash\beta}}{b_{\alpha}}<\infty
and relation (
4.1
) holds.
Using Proposition
1.9
, we deduce that
g
is an admissible free holomorphic function.
The case when
a_{\alpha}\geq 0
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq N_{0}
, can be treated in a similar manner. The proof is complete.
∎

Example 4.8
.

For each
s\in(0,\infty)
, consider the formal power series

g_{s}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})^{k}.

and the associated Hilbert space
F^{2}(g_{s})
(see Example
4.5
). In this case, we have

g_{s}^{-1}=\left[1-(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})\right]^{s}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}s\\
k\end{matrix}\right)(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})^{k}.

Therefore

a_{\alpha}=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s\\
|\alpha|\end{matrix}\right)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}\frac{s(s-1)\cdots(s-|\alpha|+1)}{|\alpha|!},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1.

Note that there is
N_{s}\geq 2
such that
a_{\alpha}\geq 0

(
or
a_{\alpha}\leq 0)
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|>N_{s}
.
Applying Theorem
4.7
, we deduce that
g_{s}
is an admissible free holomorphic function.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
\varphi=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}d_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series such that
d_{\alpha}\geq 0
and
d_{g_{i}}>0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Consider the formal power series
\psi_{s}
with inverse

\psi_{s}^{-1}=(1-\varphi)^{s}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(\begin{matrix}s\\
k\end{matrix}\right)\varphi^{k}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}

and let
\psi_{s}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
. Then the coefficients
\{b_{\alpha}\}
satisfy the inequalities

\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{d_{g_{i}}}
and
\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}\leq\frac{1}{d_{g_{i}}}

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Moreover, if
\varphi
is a noncommutative polynomial, then

\psi_{s}
is an admissible free holomorphic function for every
s\geq 1
.

Proof.

Note that

\begin{split}\psi_{s}&=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)\varphi^{k}=1+\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s+j-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{j}}\right)Z_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce that

(4.3)

b_{g_{0}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad b_{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s+j-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{j}}\quad\text{ if }\ |\alpha|\geq 1.

Assume that
s\geq 1
and note that
\left(\begin{matrix}s+j\\
j+1\end{matrix}\right)\geq\left(\begin{matrix}s+j-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)
for any
j\geq 1
. Using relation (
4.3
), for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
, we obtain

\begin{split}b_{g_{i}\alpha}&=\sum_{t=1}^{|\alpha|+1}\left(\begin{matrix}s+t-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{t}=g_{i}\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{t}|\geq 1}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{t}}\geq\sum_{t=2}^{|\alpha|+1}\left(\begin{matrix}s+t-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{g_{i}\gamma_{2}\cdots\gamma_{t}=g_{i}\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{2}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{t}|\geq 1}}d_{g_{i}}d_{\gamma_{2}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{t}}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s+j\\
j+1\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{g_{i}\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=g_{i}\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}d_{g_{i}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{j}}=d_{g_{i}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s+j\\
j+1\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{j}}\right)\\
&=d_{g_{i}}b_{\alpha}.\end{split}

Note also that
b_{g_{i}}=sd_{g_{i}}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Consequently, we have

\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{d_{g_{i}}}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
We remark that, we can show in a similar manner that
\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}\leq\frac{1}{d_{g_{i}}}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.

Now, assume that
\varphi
is a noncommutative polynomial. Note that there is
N_{s}\geq 2
such that
a_{\alpha}\geq 0

(
or
a_{\alpha}\leq 0)
for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|>N_{s}
.
Theorem
4.7
shows that
\psi_{s}
is an admissible free holomorphic function whenever
s\geq 1
. The proof is complete.
∎

5.
C^{*}
-algebras associated with noncommutative domains and classification

We associate with each universal model
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
generated by
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
and identity. The main goal of this section is to obtain Wold decompositions for any unital
*
-representations of
C^{*}({\bf W})
. Under certain conditions on
{\bf W}
, we obtain some classification results and an exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras, generalizing the corresponding results obtained by Coburn and Cuntz. All the results of this section hold, in particular, for the admissible free holomorphic functions provided by Example
5.3
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
g=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that
b_{\alpha}>0
and

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
If

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

be a free holomorphic function in a neighborhood of the origin such that

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}

(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
are the corresponding weighted left creation operators associated with

Proof.

Fix

Since the series

q({\bf W})P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}g({\bf W})^{*}

let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
such
that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
is convergent in the operator norm topology, where
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
.
Let
\pi
be a unital

*
-representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
on a separable Hilbert
space
{\mathcal{K}}
and
set

V_{i}:=\pi(W_{i})
. Then the noncommutative Berezin kernel
{K_{g,V}}
is a partial isometry. Setting

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}:=\text{\rm range}\,K_{g,V}^{*}\ \text{ and }\ {\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}:=\ker K_{g,V},

the orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}\bigoplus{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
has the following properties.

(i)

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
are reducing subspaces for each operator
V_{i}
.

(ii)

V|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}}:=(V_{1}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}},\ldots,V_{n}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}})
is a pure tuple in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}}^{(0)})
.

(iii)

V|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}}:=(V_{1}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}},\ldots,V_{n}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}})
is a Cuntz tuple in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}}^{(1)})
.

Moreover,
the Berezin kernel

{K_{g,V}}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}}
is a unitary operator satisfying relation

V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}}=\left({K_{g,V}}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}}\right)^{*}\left(W_{i}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{D}}}\right)\left({K_{g,V}}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}}\right),\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
{\mathcal{D}}:=\text{\rm range}\,\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})
and

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})

is an orthogonal projection.

In addition, the orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{K}}
is uniquely determined by the properties (i), (ii), and (iii) and we have

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}V_{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}),\qquad\text{where}\quad{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{K}}\ominus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\overline{V_{i}{\mathcal{K}}}\right),

and

{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=s}\overline{V_{\alpha}({\mathcal{K}})}\right).

Proof.

Due to Theorem
5.6
, the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}
admits an orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
such that
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
are reducing subspaces for each operator
V_{i}
and there is a unitary operator
U:{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{G}}
with the property that

V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}=U^{*}(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})U
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
With respect to the orthogonal decomposition
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, we have

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}=0
,

V_{i}=\left(\begin{matrix}V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}&0\\
0&V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{1}}\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right).

Taking into account that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
is convergent in the operator norm topology,

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}

is an orthogonal projection in
C^{*}({\bf W})
. Since

\pi
is a
*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
, we deduce that the operators

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})
and
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}
are orthogonal projections.
Consequently, we have

{\mathcal{D}}:=\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*}){\mathcal{K}}=\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*}){\mathcal{K}}_{0}.

Since
b_{\beta}W_{\beta}P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}W_{\beta}^{*}=P_{{\mathbb{C}}e_{\beta}}
and
\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\in C^{*}({\bf W})
, and using the fact that
\pi
is a
*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
, we deduce that the operators

b_{\beta}^{(m)}V_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\beta}^{*}
, where

\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, are pairwise orthogonal projections and

K_{g,V}^{*}K_{g,V}=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\beta}V_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\beta}^{*}

is an orthogonal projection. This shows, in particular, that
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}})
.
On the other hand, with respect to orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
,
the noncommutative Berezin kernel
K_{g,V}
has the representation

K_{g,V}=\left[K_{g,V|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}}\ 0\right]:{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{D}}.

Since
V|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}
is a pure
n
-tuple in
{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}}_{0})
, the Berezin kernel

{K_{g,V|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}}}
is an isometry satisfying relation

V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}=\left({K_{g,V|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}}}\right)^{*}\left(W_{i}\otimes I_{{\mathcal{D}}}\right)\left({K_{g,V}}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}\right),\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Moreover, since
V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}=U^{*}(W_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}})U
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
K_{g,{\bf W}\otimes I_{\mathcal{G}}}
is a unitary operator, so is
{K_{g,V|_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}}}}
.
Since
K_{g,V}
is a partial isometry, we have

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}:=\text{\rm range}\,K_{g,V}^{*}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\ \text{ and }\ {\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}:=\ker K_{g,V}={\mathcal{K}}_{1}.

The fact that the orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{K}}
is uniquely determined by the properties (i), (ii), and (iii)
is a consequence of Theorem
5.6
.
Using the definition of the Berezin kernel
K_{g,V}
, we obtain

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}=\left\{\xi\in{\mathcal{K}}:\ \sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}b_{\beta}V_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\beta}^{*}\xi=\xi\right\}

and

(5.2)

{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\left\{\xi\in{\mathcal{K}}:\ \Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\alpha}^{*}\xi=0\ \text{ for every }\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}.

Since the operators

b_{\beta}V_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})V_{\beta}^{*}
,

\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, are pairwise orthogonal projections, we deduce that
\sqrt{b_{\beta}}V_{\beta}\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})
is a partial isometry with range equal to
V_{\beta}{\mathcal{D}}
and
V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{D}}\perp V_{\beta}{\mathcal{D}}
if
\alpha\neq\beta
. On the other hand, due to Theorem
5.6
,
we have
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{D}}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}
.
Now, it is clear that

(5.3)

{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}V_{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}).

Due to relation (
5.2
), if
\xi\in{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
then
\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})\xi=0
, which implies

\xi\in\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=1}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
. Since
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
are reducing subspaces for each operator
V_{i}
, we obtain
{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=1}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
.
Note that the later relation implies

{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\Span_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
for any
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and, consequently, we have

(5.4)

{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\Span_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}.

Using the orthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
and that
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
are reducing subspaces for each operator
V_{i}
,
one can use the relation (
5.4
) to prove that

\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}=\left(\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}\right)\bigoplus{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}.

Due to relation (
5.3
), we have
\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}=\{0\}
. Therefore,

\bigcap_{s=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}.

Since
V_{i}^{*}V_{j}=0
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
i\neq j
,
we have

\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=s}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}=\bigoplus_{|\alpha|=s}\overline{V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}}.

Note that the orthogonal decomposition (
5.3
) and the fact that
{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}=\overline{\Span}_{|\alpha|=1}V_{\alpha}{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
can be used to show that

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{K}}\ominus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\overline{V_{i}{\mathcal{K}}}\right)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Let
\pi

*

Definition 5.9
.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function and
let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
such
that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
is convergent in the operator norm topology, where
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
.
The algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(g)
is the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
\pi(W_{1}),\ldots,\pi(W_{n})
and the identity, where

\pi
is a unital
*
-representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
such that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(\pi({\bf W}),\pi({\bf W}^{*}))=0,

where
\pi({\bf W}):=(\pi(W_{1}),\ldots,\pi(W_{n}))
.

Note that if
g^{-1}=1-(Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n})
,

, then

coincides with the Cuntz algebra
{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
(see
[
16
]
).

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\{\pi_{\omega}\}_{\omega}
be the collection of all Cuntz type
*
-representations of

C^{*}({\bf W})
such that
C^{*}(\pi_{\omega}({\bf W}))
is irreducible. The universal algebra
{\mathcal{O}}(g)
is
*
-isomorphic to the
C^{*}
-algebra

C^{*}(\widetilde{\pi}({\bf W}))
, where
\widetilde{\pi}:=\bigoplus_{\omega}\pi_{\omega}
is the direct sum representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
.

Proof.

Note that
\widetilde{\pi}
is a Cuntz type
*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
and set
\widetilde{V}:=(\widetilde{V}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{V}_{n})
, where

\widetilde{V}_{i}:=\widetilde{\pi}(W_{i})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We prove that
C^{*}(\widetilde{V})
is
*
-isomorphic to

{\mathcal{O}}(g)
.
Let
\pi
be an arbitrary Cuntz type
*
-representation of

C^{*}({\bf W})
and set
V:=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
, where

V_{i}:=\pi(W_{i})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We need to prove that there is a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\Omega:C^{*}(\widetilde{V})\to C^{*}(V)
such that
\Omega(\widetilde{V}_{i})=V_{i}
.
Due to the GNS construction, given a polynomial
q({\bf Z},{\bf Y}\})
in noncommutative indeterminates
{\bf Z}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
and
{\bf Y}=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
, there is an irreducible representation
\gamma
of
C^{*}(V)
such that

\|\gamma\left(q(V,V^{*})\right)\|=\|q(V,V^{*})\|.

Note that
\gamma\circ\pi
is an irreducible Cuntz type
*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
. Consequently,

\|q((\gamma\circ\pi)({\bf W}),(\gamma\circ\pi)({\bf W})^{*})\|\leq\|q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})\|.

Combining these relations, we obtain

\|q(V,V^{*})\|\leq\|q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})\|,

for all polynomials
q({\bf Z},{\bf Y}\})
, which proves that there is a surjective
*
-homomorphism
\Omega:C^{*}(\widetilde{V})\to C^{*}(V)
such that
\Omega(\widetilde{V}_{i})=V_{i}
. The proof is complete.
∎

[
14
]
for the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the unilateral shift on the Hardy space
H^{2}
of the disk
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}
and Cuntz’ extension
[
16
]
for the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the left creation operators on the full Fock space with
n
generators.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
g
be an admissible free holomorphic function with
g^{-1}=1+\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
and
let
{\bf W}=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left creation operators associated with
g
.
If
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}
is convergent in the operator norm topology, then the sequence
of
C^{*}
-algebras

0\to\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\to C^{*}({\bf W})\to{\mathcal{O}}(g)\to 0

is exact, where
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
stands for the ideal of all compact
operators in
B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.

Proof.

Due to Theorem
5.5
, the two-sided ideal
{\mathcal{J}}
generated by the orthogonal projection

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W})
in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})
coincides with the ideal
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
of all compact operators in
B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
.
According to Lemma
5.10
, it is enough to prove that
C^{*}(\widetilde{V})
is
*
-isomorphic to the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\bf W})/_{{\mathcal{J}}}
.

Since the Cuntz type
*
-representation
\widetilde{\pi}:C^{*}({\bf W})\to C^{*}(\widetilde{V})
has the property that
\widetilde{\pi}({\mathcal{J}})=0
, it induces a
*
-representation

\sigma:C^{*}({\bf W})/_{{\mathcal{J}}}\to C^{*}(\widetilde{V})

such that

\sigma\left(q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})+{\mathcal{J}}\right)=q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})

for every polynomial
q({\bf Z},{\bf Y})
in noncommutative indeterminates
{\bf Z}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\}
and
{\bf Y}=\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
.
Therefore,
\sigma
is surjective and

(5.5)

\|q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*}))\|\leq\|q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})+{\mathcal{J}}\|.

Let
\rho:C^{*}({\bf W})\to C^{*}({\bf W})/_{{\mathcal{J}}}
be the canonical quotient map and note that, due to the fact that

{\mathcal{J}}
is generated by the orthogonal projection

\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W})
, we have

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(\rho({\bf W}),\rho({\bf W}^{*}))=0,

where
\rho({\bf W}):=(\rho(W_{1}),\ldots,\rho(W_{n}))
. Therefore,
\rho
is a Cuntz type
*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
and

\|q(\rho({\bf W}),\rho({\bf W}^{*}))\|\leq\|q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})\|.

Hence and using relation (
5.5
), we obtain

\|q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})\|=\|q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})+{\mathcal{J}}\|.

Now, the map defined by

\Omega(q(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V}^{*})):=q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})+{\mathcal{J}}

for all noncommutative polynomials
q({\bf Z},{\bf Y})
is a contractive
*
-homomorphism on
C^{*}(\widetilde{V})
which extends by continuity to a
*
-homomorphism of
C^{*}(\widetilde{V})
onto
C^{*}({\bf W})/_{\mathcal{J}}
. It is clear now that
\Omega
is a
*
-isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebras.
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
\pi
be a unital

*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
on a separable Hilbert
space
{\mathcal{K}}
and let
V:=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
, where
V_{i}:=\pi({W}_{i})
.
If
\pi
is not a Cuntz type
*
-representation, then the
C^{*}
-algebras
C^{*}({\bf W})
and
C^{*}({V})
are
*
-isomorphic.

Proof.

According to Theorem
5.7
, we have the Wold decomposition
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}
and

V_{i}=({W}_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}})\bigoplus V_{i}^{\prime}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\},

where
V^{\prime}_{i}:=V_{i}|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}}
and

{\mathcal{D}}
is a Hilbert space with
\dim{\mathcal{D}}\geq 1
.
Moreover,
{V}^{\prime}:=(V_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,V_{k}^{\prime})
is a Cuntz
k
-tuple in

{\mathcal{D}}_{g^{-1}}({\mathcal{K}}^{(1)})
.
As a consequence, we deduce that

q(V,V^{*})=\left(q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}}\right)\bigoplus q(V^{\prime},V^{\prime*})

for any noncommutative polynomial
q({\bf Z},{\bf Y})
. Since
\pi|_{{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)}}
is a
*
-representation, we have

\|q(V^{\prime},V^{\prime*})\|\leq\|q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\|.

On the other hand, since
{\mathcal{D}}\neq\{0\}
, we have

\|q(V,V^{*})\|=\max\left\{\|q({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*}))\|,\|q(V^{\prime},V^{\prime*}))\|\right\}=\|q({\bf W},{\bf W})\|.

Due to the fact that
\pi:C^{*}({\bf W})\to C^{*}({V})
is surjective, we have

\|\pi(g)\|=\|g\|
for all
g\in C^{*}({\bf W})
. Therefore,
\pi
is a
*
-isomorphism of
C^{*}
-algebras.
The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\pi_{c}:C^{*}({\bf W})\to B({\mathcal{K}})
be a Cuntz type

*
-representation of
C^{*}({\bf W})
on a Hilbert
space
{\mathcal{K}}
, set
C_{i}:=\pi_{c}({W}_{i})
and
V_{i}:={W}_{i}\oplus C_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Then there is a unique minimal nontrivial two-sided ideal
{{\mathcal{J}}_{\Delta}}
of
C^{*}({V})
and

{{\mathcal{J}}_{\Delta}}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}\cap C^{*}({V})=\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0,

where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}
is the ideal of all compact operators in
B(F^{2}(H_{n})\oplus{\mathcal{K}})
.
Moreover, if
V:=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
,
then the sequence
of
C^{*}
-algebras

0\to{{\mathcal{J}}_{\Delta}}\to C^{*}({V})\to{\mathcal{O}}(g)\to 0

is exact.

Proof.

Set
{\bf C}:=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{n})
, and note that

\Delta_{g^{-1}}(V,V^{*})=\Delta_{g^{-1}}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\bigoplus\Delta_{g^{-1}}(C,C^{*})=P_{{\mathbb{C}}1}\oplus 0.

This shows that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}\cap C^{*}({V})
is a nontrivial ideal in
C^{*}({V})
. Let
{\mathcal{J}}
be an arbitrary nontrivial ideal of
C^{*}({V})
and let
E\oplus F\in{\mathcal{J}}
with
E\oplus F\neq 0
.
Then there is a sequence
q_{s}\in{\mathbb{C}}\left<{\bf Z},{\bf Y}\right>
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of polynomials such that

q_{s}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\to E
and
q_{s}(C,C^{*})\to F
in the operator norm as

s\to\infty
.
Due to the fact that

\|q_{s}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\bigoplus q_{s}(C,C^{*})\|\leq\|q_{s}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\|,

we deduce that
\|E\oplus F\|\leq\|E\|
. Since
E\oplus F\neq 0
, we deduce that
E\neq 0
. Consequently, there is
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
such that
Ee_{\alpha}\neq 0
.
As in the proof of Theorem
5.5
, part (ii), one can use the fact that
{C}
is a Cuntz tuple
to show that
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0\subset{\mathcal{J}}
.
This shows that
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0
is the minimal nontrivial ideal of
C^{*}({V})
.
Thus
{{\mathcal{J}}_{\Delta}}=\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0
.
Since
\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0\subset\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}\cap C^{*}(V)
, it remains to prove the reverse inclusion.
Indeed, if we fix an operator
E\oplus F\in\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}\cap C^{*}(V)\subset B(F^{2}(H_{n})\oplus{\mathcal{K}})
, then
E\in\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))
and
F
is a compact operator in
C^{*}({C})\subset B({\mathcal{K}})
.
Let
q_{s}\in{\mathbb{C}}\left<{\bf Z},{\bf Y}\right>
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be a sequence of polynomials such that

q_{s}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})\to E
and
q_{s}(C,C^{*})\to F
in the operator norm as

s\to\infty
. According to the proof of Theorem
5.11
, we have

\|q_{s}(C,C^{*})\|\leq\|q_{s}({\bf W},{\bf W}^{*})+\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\|

which implies
\|F\|\leq\|\rho(E)\|
, where

\rho:C^{*}({\bf W})\to C^{*}({\bf W})/_{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))}
is the quotient map. Since
E
is a compact operator, we have
\|\rho(E)\|=0
, which implies
F=0
. Consequently, we have

\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}(F^{2}(H_{n}))\oplus 0=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{K}}}}\cap C^{*}(V).

The proof of the exactness of the sequence is similar to that of Theorem
5.11

Higher-order time domain boundary elements for elastodynamics – graded meshes and hp versions
Alessandra Aimi, Giulia Di Credico, Heiko GimperleinErnst P. Stephan

Abstract
The solution to the elastodynamic equation in the exterior of a polyhedral domain or a screen exhibits singular behavior from the corners and edges. The detailed expansion of the singularities implies quasi-optimal estimates for piecewise polynomial approximations of the Dirichlet trace of the solution and the traction. The results are applied to h ​ p hp and graded versions of the time domain boundary element method for the weakly singular and the hypersingular integral equations. Numerical examples confirm the theoretical results for the Dirichlet and Neumann problems for screens and for polygonal domains in 2d. They exhibit the expected quasi-optimal convergence rates and the singular behavior of the solutions.

Introduction

Solutions to elliptic and parabolic boundary value problems in polyhedral domains exhibit singularities in a neighborhood of the corners and edges. Numerical approximations by finite or boundary element methods take into account the nonsmooth behavior with local mesh refinements or higher polynomial degrees to recover optimal convergence rates. The resulting
h
,
p
and
hp
methods have been studied for several decades, see e.g.
[
54
]
for finite elements and
[
29
]
for boundary elements.

For hyperbolic equations in conical or wedge domains the singular behavior of the solution has been clarified by Plamenevskiǐ and collaborators since the late 1990’s
[
34
,
36
,
41
,
51
]
. The explicit singular expansions were used by Müller and Schwab to prove optimal convergence rates for a finite element method on algebraically graded meshes for the wave and elastodynamic equations in polygonal domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
43
,
44
]
. Corresponding results for the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
were obtained by two of the authors, leading to approximation results for boundary element methods (TDBEM) on graded meshes
[
22
]
,
hp
versions
[
24
]
and the efficiency of a posteriori error estimates for adaptive refinement procedures
[
25
]
.

In this article we initiate the study of
h
,
p
and
hp
time domain boundary element methods for the Dirichlet and Neumann problems of elastodynamics in a polyhedral domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
. Based on the approach by Plamenevskiǐ and singular expansions for the time independent Lamé equation, we obtain a detailed description of the singularities of the solution for the model 3d geometries of a wedge and a cone, as well as 2d polygonal domains. The expansions imply quasi-optimal convergence rates for piecewise polynomial approximations on graded meshes and by
hp
versions.

To be specific, we formulate the set-up and results for exterior problems. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3
, be a screen or closed surface and denote by
\Omega
the connected exterior
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\Gamma

Lamé parameters

\lambda,\mu>0

\rho

(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\textbf{u})+\mu\Delta\textbf{u}-\rho\ddot{\textbf{u}}=0,\quad\textbf{x}\in\Omega,\>t\in(0,T]\ .

(1)

We impose homogeneous initial conditions
\textbf{u}(0,\textbf{x})=\partial_{t}\textbf{u}(t,\textbf{x})=0
and consider either Dirichlet boundary conditions,
\textbf{u}=\textbf{g}
, or Neumann boundary conditions involving the traction,
\textbf{p}(\textbf{u})=\textbf{h}
.

To solve (
1

\Gamma
. For Dirichlet boundary conditions we study

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\textbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{K}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\textbf{g}(\textbf{x},t),\qquad(\textbf{x},t)\in\Gamma\times[0,T]\ ,

(2)

involving the weakly singular integral operator
\mathcal{V}
and the double layer integral operator
\mathcal{K}

\mathcal{V}

are defined from a fundamental solution

\textbf{p}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\textbf{G})

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\textbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\textbf{G}(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\xi};t,\tau)\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\tau)d\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}d\tau\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\textbf{x},t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\textbf{p}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\textbf{G})(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\xi};t,\tau)^{T}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\tau)d\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}d\tau\ .

The Neumann problem is similarly formulated as an equation for the hypersingular integral operator
\mathcal{W}
, see (
13

\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{h,\Delta t}\in\left(V_{\Delta t,q}\otimes X^{-1}_{h,p}\right)^{n}
, based on tensor products of piecewise polynomial functions on a quasi-uniform or graded mesh in space and a uniform mesh in time.

The convergence rate of the error is determined by the singularities of the solution of (
1

\Omega
. Near an edge or a cone point of the boundary
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
we obtain a singular expansion of the solution into a leading part of explicit singular functions plus smoother remainder terms. Expansions in a wedge, respectively a cone, are obtained in (
45

\displaystyle\textbf{u}(t,\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\chi(r)r^{\nu^{*}}\mathbb{a}(t,\phi)+\textbf{u}_{0}(t,r,\phi)\ ,

\displaystyle\textbf{p}(\textbf{u})(t,\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\chi(r)r^{\nu^{*}-1}\mathbb{b}(t,\phi)+\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}(t,r,\phi)\ .

Here,
(r,\phi)
are polar coordinates centered at the vertex, the exponent
\nu^{*}
is determined by the opening angle

\textbf{u}_{0}

are remainder terms of lower order. In particular, for a fixed time

3
Regularity of solutions to the Dirichlet problem

In this section we obtain precise results for the singular behaviour of the solution to the original initial-boundary value problem of elastodynamics with Dirichlet conditions (
4
) - (
6
) for two model geometries, the circular cone and the wedge. The decomposition results for the solution of the differential equation lead (by taking traces) to decompositions also for the solutions of the integral equations in singular terms and more regular remainders. The problem with Neumann conditions can be dealt with by appropriate modifications; therefore this is omitted for brevity. Solving (

To obtain the generic matrix entry of the sub-block
E^{(l)}_{\mathcal{V}}
, where

is the nonnegative difference between two time indexes, we can perform an analytical integration in the time variables

, obtaining

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V},ij}^{(l)}\right)_{\widetilde{m},m}

\displaystyle=\langle V_{ij}w^{(p)}_{m}\partial_{t}v^{(0)}_{n},w^{(p)}_{\widetilde{m}}v^{(0)}_{\widetilde{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=-\langle V_{ij}w^{(p)}_{m}v^{(0)}_{n},w^{(p)}_{\widetilde{m}}\partial_{t}v^{(0)}_{\widetilde{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\xi,\varsigma=0}^{1}(-1)^{\xi+\varsigma}\int_{\Gamma}w^{(p)}_{\widetilde{m}}(\textbf{x})\int_{0}^{t_{\widetilde{n}+\xi}}\int_{\Gamma}G_{ij}(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\xi};t_{\widetilde{n}+\xi},\tau)w^{(p)}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\xi})H[\tau-t_{n+\varsigma}]d\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}d\tau d\Gamma_{\textbf{x}}.

(73)

Further, it is also possible to compute exactly the integration in

(74)

for all
i,j=1,2
;
m,\widetilde{m}=1,...,M^{(p)}_{h}
and
n,\widetilde{n}=0,...,N_{\Delta t}-1
.
Here, the positive time difference

and the integration kernel
\nu^{\mathcal{V}}_{ij}

\displaystyle\nu^{\mathcal{V}}_{ij}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}):=\quad\quad

\displaystyle\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}\left(\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{4}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{P}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{P}}}\varphi_{\mathtt{P}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})-\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{S}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{S}}}\varphi_{\mathtt{S}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{P}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}}\widehat{\varphi}_{\mathtt{P}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})+\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{S}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}\widehat{\varphi}_{\mathtt{S}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})\right].

\displaystyle{\varphi_{\gamma}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}):=\sqrt{c^{2}_{\gamma}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{\gamma}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}):=\log\left(\sqrt{c^{2}_{\gamma}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}+c_{\gamma}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}\right)-\log(r).

(77)

If

the kernel
\nu_{ij}
has a reduced form:

\displaystyle\nu^{\mathcal{V}}_{ij}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}-c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}{c_{\mathtt{P}}c_{\mathtt{S}}}\left(\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}\right)\frac{\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}}{c_{\mathtt{P}}\sqrt{c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}+c_{\mathtt{S}}\sqrt{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}}-\frac{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}+c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}\log(r)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}}\log\left(c_{\mathtt{P}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}+\sqrt{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}\log\left(c_{\mathtt{S}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}+\sqrt{c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}-r^{2}}\right)\right],

(78)

with space singularity of kind
\mathcal{O}\left(\log(r)\right)
for

. This behavior is well-studied for boundary integral operators related to 2D elliptic problems.

The discrete function
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i,h,\Delta t}
in the weak formulation (

similar to the one obtained by the discretization of the single layer operator

for all
i,j=1,2
;
m,\widetilde{m}=1,...,M^{(q)}_{h}
and

.

and the integration kernel

\displaystyle\nu^{\mathcal{W}}_{ij}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{P}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{P}}^{3}}\left[\left(D_{\varphi,c_{\mathtt{P}}}^{ij}+D_{c_{\mathtt{P}}}^{ij}\frac{\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}c_{\mathtt{P}}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}\>\varphi_{\mathtt{P}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})}{r^{2}}+D_{\widehat{\varphi},c_{\mathtt{P}}}^{ij}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{\mathtt{P}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})}{c_{\mathtt{P}}}\right]

\displaystyle\frac{H[c_{\mathtt{S}}\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}-r]}{c_{\mathtt{S}}^{3}}\left[\left(D_{\varphi,c_{\mathtt{S}}}^{ij}+D_{c_{\mathtt{S}}}^{ij}\frac{\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}^{2}c_{\mathtt{S}}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\frac{\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma}\>\varphi_{\mathtt{S}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})}{r^{2}}+D_{\widehat{\varphi},c_{\mathtt{S}}}^{ij}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{\mathtt{S}}(r;\Delta_{\widetilde{n}+\xi,n+\varsigma})}{c_{\mathtt{S}}}\right],

Recalling the notation stated in Section

\Gamma
determines a partition of

made by the bounded interior domain
\Omega^{\prime}
, with
\partial\Omega^{\prime}=\Gamma
, and its complement
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
. From Section

we know that the solution

in the exterior set

and near a corner point of

locally behaves like a power of the distance

to the vertex:

u_{i}\approx C_{i,\omega_{ext}}(t)r^{\nu^{*}(\omega_{ext})},\quad\>r\to 0,

) and the exponent

\sin^{2}(\omega_{ext}\>\nu^{*})=\left(\frac{\nu^{*}}{k}\sin{\omega_{ext}}\right)^{2},

(83)

with positive real part, where
k=3-2\lambda/(\lambda+\mu)
.
The prefactor

is a smooth function in
t
, independent of

, so the leading singular behaviour does not change with time. The solution
\boldsymbol{\Phi}=\textbf{p}(\textbf{u})|_{\Gamma}
of the boundary integral equation (
15
) represents the traction at the obstacle and, from the discussion in Section
3
, its asymptotic behaviour a the vertex can be expressed as

\Phi_{i}\approx\widetilde{C}_{i,\omega_{ext}}(t)r^{\nu^{*}(\omega_{ext})-1},\>r\to 0.

For Lamé parameters
\lambda=2
,
\mu=1
and mass density
\varrho=1

\nu^{*}(\omega_{ext})=\nu^{*}(2\pi-\omega_{int})

. Red crosses indicate the exponents
\nu^{*}
corresponding to the red corners of the polygons depicted on the right of Figure

Conclusions

In this work we initiate the study of higher-order versions of the boundary element method for linear elastodynamics, including
h
,
p
and
hp
versions. The asymptotic expansions for the solution obtained near geometric singularities of the domain give rise to efficient discretizations, with the same approximation rates as known for
h
,
p
and
hp
approximations of time independent
problems.

The quasi-optimal
hp
explicit estimates in this article complement the recent analysis for the wave equation, for both finite and boundary element methods
[
22
,
24
,
43
]
, and for linear elastodynamics in 2d
[
44
]
.
The convergence is determined by the singular behavior of the solution near the non-smooth boundary points of
the domain. Our analysis relies on the classical approximation results for time independent problems
[
16
]
, in combination with the analysis of the leading singular terms in the time dependent problem
[
41
]
.

Extensive numerical experiments for a slit and polygonal domains in 2d illustrate the quasi-optimal convergence rates and confirm the expected leading asymptotic behavior of the solution near a vertex.
On a slit the energy error
O(p^{-1})
of the
p
version converges with the same rate as an
h
version on a
2
-graded mesh. For closed
polygonal domains the solution is less singular near the vertices, depending on the material parameters and the opening angle. Accordingly, higher convergence rates are obtained in both the analysis and in the numerical experiments.

Appendix A

In this appendix we introduce space–time anisotropic Sobolev spaces on the boundary
\Gamma
as a convenient functional analytic setting for the analysis of the time dependent boundary integral operators. A detailed exposition may be found in
[
30
,
20
]
. Furthermore, we collect mapping properties of the integral operators
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W}
in these space-time anisotropic spaces (Theorem
A.2
) and show continuity and coercivity of the associated bilinear forms (Proposition
A.3
). The latter imply the stability of the Galerkin schemes in Section 4. In the case of an open screen or line segment,
\partial\Gamma\neq\emptyset
, we first extend
\Gamma
to a closed, orientable Lipschitz manifold
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.
On
\Gamma
we recall the usual Sobolev spaces of supported distributions:

\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Gamma)=\{u\in H^{s}(\widetilde{\Gamma}):\mathrm{supp}\ u\subset{\overline{\Gamma}}\}\ ,\quad\ s\in\mathbb{R}\ .

The Sobolev space
{H}^{s}(\Gamma)
is the quotient space
H^{s}(\widetilde{\Gamma})/\widetilde{H}^{s}({\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\overline{\Gamma}})
.
To define a family of Sobolev norms,
\alpha_{i}
be a partition of unity subordinate to a covering of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
by open sets
B_{i}
. Given diffeomorphisms
\varphi_{i}
from
B_{i}
to the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Sobolev norms are induced from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
:

||u||_{s,\omega,{\widetilde{\Gamma}}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(|\omega|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2})^{s}|\mathcal{F}\left\{(\alpha_{i}u)\circ\varphi_{i}^{-1}\right\}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ .

Here,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{x}\mapsto\boldsymbol{\xi}}
denotes the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\int e^{-i\mathbb{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}\varphi(\mathbb{x})\ d\mathbb{x}
. Different
\omega\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
induce equivalent norms on
H^{s}(\Gamma)
,
\|u\|_{s,\omega,\Gamma}=\inf_{v\in\widetilde{H}^{s}(\widetilde{\Gamma}\setminus\overline{\Gamma})}\ \|u+v\|_{s,\omega,\widetilde{\Gamma}}
and on
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Gamma)
,
\|u\|_{s,\omega,\Gamma,\ast}=\|e_{+}u\|_{s,\omega,\widetilde{\Gamma}}
.
e_{+}
extends the distribution
u
by
0
from
\Gamma
to
\widetilde{\Gamma}
. When a specific
\omega
is fixed, we write
H^{s}_{\omega}(\Gamma)
for
H^{s}(\Gamma)
, respectively
\widetilde{H}^{s}_{\omega}(\Gamma)
for
\widetilde{H}^{s}(\Gamma)
. The norm
\|u\|_{s,\omega,\Gamma,\ast}
is stronger than
\|u\|_{s,\omega,\Gamma}
.

We may now define a family of space-time anisotropic Sobolev spaces:

Definition A.1
.

For
\sigma>0
and
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
define

\displaystyle H^{r}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},{H}^{s}(\Gamma))

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(H^{s}(\Gamma)):e^{-\sigma t}u\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(H^{s}(\Gamma))\textrm{ and }||u||_{r,s,\Gamma}<\infty\}\ ,

\displaystyle H^{r}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\widetilde{H}^{s}({\Gamma}))

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(\widetilde{H}^{s}({\Gamma})):e^{-\sigma t}u\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(\widetilde{H}^{s}({\Gamma}))\textrm{ and }||u||_{r,s,\Gamma,\ast}<\infty\}\ .

(85)

Here,
\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(E)
denotes the space of all distributions on
\mathbb{R}
with support in
[0,\infty)
, taking values in a Hilbert space
E={H}^{s}({\Gamma})
, respectively
E=\widetilde{H}^{s}({\Gamma})
.
\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(E)\subset\mathcal{D}^{{}^{\prime}}_{+}(E)
denotes the subspace of tempered distributions. The Sobolev spaces are endowed with the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{r,s}:=\|u\|_{r,s,\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-\infty+i\sigma}^{+\infty+i\sigma}|\omega|^{2r}\ \|\hat{u}(\omega)\|^{2}_{s,\omega,\Gamma}\ d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{r,s,\ast}:=\|u\|_{r,s,\Gamma,\ast}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{-\infty+i\sigma}^{+\infty+i\sigma}|\omega|^{2r}\ \|\hat{u}(\omega)\|^{2}_{s,\omega,\Gamma,\ast}\ d\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(86)

They are Hilbert spaces. For
r=s=0
they correspond to the weighted
L^{2}
-space with scalar product
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\sigma t}\int_{\Gamma}u\overline{v}d\Gamma_{\mathbb{x}}\ dt
. Because
\Gamma
is Lipschitz, these spaces are independent of the choice of
\alpha_{i}
and
\varphi_{i}
when
|s|\leq 1
, as for standard Sobolev spaces.

We shall also use the norms
\|u\|_{r,s,(t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Gamma}
and
\|u\|_{r,s,(t_{1},t_{2}]\times\Gamma,\ast}
for restrictions on the time interval
(t_{1},t_{2}]
.

Let now
\widetilde{\Gamma}=\partial\Omega^{\prime}
the boundary of a Lipschitz subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Gamma\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}
open. Denote
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
.

We review the mapping properties for the weakly singular integral operator
\mathcal{V}
and the hypersingular operator
\mathcal{W}
.

Theorem A.2
.

The single layer potential operator and the hypersingular operator are continuous for

and

:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle:H_{\sigma}^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\to H_{\sigma}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\ ,\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:H_{\sigma}^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\to H_{\sigma}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle:H_{\sigma}^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\to H_{\sigma}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle:H_{\sigma}^{r+1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\to H_{\sigma}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\ .

This may be found in Theorem 3.1 in
[
13
]
, see also
[
9
]
for
\mathcal{W}
in 2d, with an analogous proof. See also
[

]

For convenience of the reader we recall basic properties of the bilinear form
for the Dirichlet problem in the infinite space-time cylinder
\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\displaystyle B_{D,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Phi}}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{\Gamma}\mathcal{V}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\Phi}(t,\mathbb{x})\ \boldsymbol{\tilde{\Phi}}(t,\mathbb{x})\ d\Gamma_{\mathbb{x}}\,d_{\sigma}t\ ,

(87)

where
d_{\sigma}t=e^{-2\sigma t}dt
, as well as the corresponding bilinear form for the Neumann problem,

\displaystyle B_{N,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Psi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Psi}}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{\Gamma}\mathcal{W}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\Psi}(t,\mathbb{x})\ \boldsymbol{\tilde{\Psi}}(t,\mathbb{x})\ d\Gamma_{\mathbb{x}}\,d_{\sigma}t\ ,

(88)

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\sigma>0
.

a) For every
\boldsymbol{\Phi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Phi}}\in H^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))^{n}
there holds:

|B_{D,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Phi}})|\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}\|_{1,-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}\|\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Phi}}\|_{1,-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}

(89)

and

\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}\|_{0,-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}^{2}\lesssim B_{D,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Phi},\boldsymbol{\Phi}).

(90)

b) For every
\boldsymbol{\Psi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Psi}}\in H^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))^{n}
there holds:

|B_{N,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Psi},\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Psi}})|\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}\|_{1,-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}\|\boldsymbol{\tilde{\Psi}}\|_{1,\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}

(91)

and

\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}\|_{0,\frac{1}{2},\Gamma,\ast}^{2}\lesssim B_{N,\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\boldsymbol{\Psi},\boldsymbol{\Psi}).

(92)

Proof.

The inequalities (
89
) and (
91
) follow from the mapping properties in Theorem
A.2
. The coercivity (
92
) was shown in
[
8
,
9
]
in 2d, and the proof holds verbatim in any dimension.

To show (
90
), we consider the elastic problem in the frequency domain:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\textbf{u})+\mu\Delta\textbf{u}+\rho\omega^{2}\textbf{u}=\textrm{div}\>\sigma(\textbf{u})+\rho\omega^{2}\textbf{u}=0,\quad\textbf{x}\in\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega\\
\textbf{u}=\textbf{g},\quad\textbf{x}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}.\end{array}\right..

(93)

We assume
\mathrm{Im}\ (\omega)\geq\sigma>0
. The energetic weak formulation for the single layer equation for the traction
[\textbf{p}]=[\sigma(\textbf{u})\textbf{n}]
in frequency domain is given by (using Parseval’s identity):

Find

[\textbf{p}]\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\omega}(\widetilde{\Gamma})^{n}

such that

B_{D,\omega}([\textbf{p}],\overline{\boldsymbol{q}})=\langle-i\omega\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}=\langle-i\omega\textbf{g},\overline{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}

(94)

for all

\boldsymbol{q}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\omega}(\widetilde{\Gamma})^{n}
.

It involves the single layer operator
\mathcal{V}_{\omega}
obtained from
\mathcal{V}
by Fourier transformation. Using Green’s formula as in
[
9
]
, Thm 3.1, we have

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}\left(\overline{\sigma(\textbf{u})}:\varepsilon(\textbf{u})-\rho\omega^{2}|\textbf{u}|^{2}\right)d\textbf{x}=\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\textbf{u}\cdot\overline{[\sigma(\textbf{u})\textbf{n}]}d\widetilde{\Gamma}\equiv\langle\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{[\textbf{p}]}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}.

Now note that
|\langle-i\omega\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{[\textbf{p}]}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|\geqslant\mathrm{Re}\>i\overline{\omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{[\textbf{p}]}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\>i\overline{\omega}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{[\textbf{p}]}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Re}\left(i\overline{\omega}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}\overline{\sigma(\textbf{u})}:\varepsilon(\textbf{u})d\textbf{x}\right)+\mathrm{Re}\left(-i\omega\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}\rho|\omega|^{2}|\textbf{u}|^{2}d\textbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Im}(\omega)E_{\omega}\geqslant 0,

(95)

with

E_{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}\left(\overline{\sigma(\textbf{u})}:\varepsilon(\textbf{u})+\rho|\omega|^{2}|\textbf{u}|^{2}\right)d\textbf{x}\ .

Physically,
E_{\omega}
is the energy of the displacement
u
, and it satisfies

E_{\omega}\geqslant C_{\sigma}\|\textbf{u}\|^{2}_{1,\omega,\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}

(96)

for a positive constant
C_{\sigma}
. From (
95
) and (
96
) we deduce that

|\langle-i\omega\mathcal{V}_{\omega}[\textbf{p}],\overline{[\textbf{p}]}\rangle_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}|\geqslant\widetilde{C}_{\sigma}\|\textbf{u}\|^{2}_{1,\omega,\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega}\ .

From the trace theorem there exists a positive constant
C_{trace}

Domination and packing in graphs
Renzo Gómez 1, Juan Gutiérrez 2

Abstract
Given a graph G G , the domination number, denoted by γ ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G) , is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set in G G . Dual to the notion of domination number is the packing number of a graph. A packing of G G is a set of vertices whose pairwise distance is at least three. The packing number ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) of G G is the maximum cardinality of one such set. Furthermore, the inequality ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ γ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G)\leq\gamma(G) is well-known. Henning et al. conjectured that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) + 1 \gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)+1 if G G is subcubic. In this paper we progress towards this conjecture by showing that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 120 49 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) {\gamma(G)\leq\frac{120}{49}\rho(G)} if G G is a bipartite cubic graph. We also show that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 3 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq 3\rho(G) if G G is a maximal outerplanar graph, and that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G) if G G is a biconvex graph. Moreover, in the last case, we show that this upper bound is tight.

Introduction

For the problem considered here, the input graph is always simple and
connected (even if this is not stated explicitly). Given a graph
G
,
we say that a set of vertices
X
of
G
is a
dominating set

if every vertex in
V(G)\setminus X
is adjacent to a vertex in
X
.
In other words,
X
intersects the closed neighborhood
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}

of each vertex
v\in V(G)
. The
domination number
of
G
,
denoted by
\gamma(G)
, is the minimum size of a dominating set of
G
.

The
distance
between vertices
u
and
v
in a graph
G
,
denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
, is the minimum length of a path between them.
Also, we extend this definition to subsets of vertices
of
G
. That is, if
A,B\subseteq V(G)
,
then
d_{G}(A,B)=\min\{d_{G}(u,v):u\in A,v\in B\}
.
Dual to the notion of domination number
is the packing number of a graph. A
packing
of a graph
G
is a set of
vertices
Y\subseteq V(G)
such that
N_{G}[u]\cap N_{G}[v]=\emptyset
, for every pair
of distinct vertices
u,v\in Y
. That is,
d_{G}(u,v)\geq 3
, for every pair of distinct
vertices
u,v\in Y
. The maximum size of such set is called the
packing number
of
G
,
and it is denoted by
\rho(G)
. Observe that, while
\gamma(G)
is the minimum number of closed
neighborhoods that cover
G
,
\rho(G)
is the maximum number of disjoint closed
neighborhoods in
G
. Since a dominating set intersects each of these disjoint
closed neighborhoods, we have that
\rho(G)\leq\gamma(G)
.

We observe that, in general,
\rho(G)\neq\gamma(G)
. For instance,
if we consider
C_{4}
, the cycle on four vertices, we have
that
1=\rho(C_{4})<\gamma(C_{4})=2
. Thus, a natural question is to
investigate how large the ratio
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)
can be. This is the
aim of this work, to find upper bounds for
\gamma(G)
in terms
of
\rho(G)
. Burger et al.
[
6
, Lemma 4, Theorem 1]

observed that, if we consider
G=K_{n}\square K_{n}
, the Cartesian product
of two complete graphs on
n
vertices, then
\gamma(G)=n
and
\rho(G)=1
.
That is, there exists a graph
G
with
\gamma(G)\geq\sqrt{|V(G)|}\rho(G)
.
A first upper bound for arbitrary graphs was observed by Henning et
al.
[
9
, Observation 1]
. He showed
that
\gamma(G)\leq\Delta(G)\rho(G)
for any graph
G
. If we restrict
the class of graphs, we can obtain constant upper bounds.
Löwenstein
[
12
, Theorem 8]

showed that
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
if
G
is a cactus. Moreover,
this bound is tight (as shown by the cycle
C_{4}
).

On the other hand, Meir & Moon
[
13
, Theorem 7]
showed
that
\gamma(G)=\rho(G)
on trees. Another interesting technique to
show this equality on some classes of graphs is to use linear programming
duality
[
14
, Section 7.4]
. To this end, note that
\gamma(G)
and
\rho(G)

are the optimal values of the following integer programs.

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle}{\mathrm{min}}\quad\sum_{v\in V(G)}{x_{v}}

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{}}{\mathrm{min}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{min}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle x(N[v])\geq 1,

\displaystyle\,\forall v\in V(G),

\displaystyle x_{v}\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle\,\forall v\in V(G).

\displaystyle\underset{\displaystyle}{\mathrm{max}}\quad\sum_{v\in V(G)}{y_{v}}

\displaystyle\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\underset{\displaystyle\phantom{}}{\mathrm{max}}$}][c]{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\mathrm{max}$}][l]{\mathrm{\kern 1.00006pts.t.}}}\quad

\displaystyle y(N[v])\leq 1,

\displaystyle\,\forall v\in V(G),

\displaystyle y_{v}\in\{0,1\},

\displaystyle\,\forall v\in V(G).

Moreover, consider the fractional analogues of the domination number and packing number
that are obtained as the linear relaxations of the above integer programs. Note that, if
the polytope defined by one of these relaxations has integer vertices for a graph
G
,
then
\gamma(G)=\rho(G)
. A first result using this technique was obtained by
Farber
[
8
, Corollary 3.4]
. He
showed that
\gamma(G)=\rho(G)
for strongly chordal graphs. Brandstädt et
al.
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
extended this result to dually
chordal graphs. Furthermore, Lovász
[
11
]
used the fractional
packing number to show a (
\log\Delta
)-approximation for the minimum dominating
set problem, where
\Delta
denotes the maximum degree of the graph.

In this work, we are interested in two problems. First,
we investigate graphs for which
\gamma(G)\leq c\cdot\rho(G)

where
c
is a constant. The second question of our interest is a conjecture
raised by Henning et al.
[
9
]
for graphs with
\Delta(G)\leq 3

(i.e. subcubic graphs). As mentioned above, they showed that
\gamma(G)\leq\Delta(G)\rho(G)
for any graph
G
.
When
\Delta(G)\leq 2
, they improved this bound
to
\rho(G)+1
. For a subcubic graph
G
, they proved
that
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
if
G
is also claw-free; and proposed the
following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Henning et al., 2011)
.

Let
G
be a subcubic graph. Then,
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)+1
,
and equality occurs only on three special graphs.

In this paper we progress towards Conjecture
1

by proving the following result.

•

If
G
is a bipartite cubic (bicubic) graph , then
\gamma(G)\leq\frac{120}{49}\rho(G)
.

As mentioned above, if
G
is a cactus, then
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
.
A superclass of cacti are outerplanar graphs. Regarding this class,
we obtain the following result.

•

If
G
is a maximal outerplanar graph, then
\gamma(G)\leq 3\rho(G)
.

Finally, we consider the class of biconvex graphs and show the following upper bound.

•

If
G
is a biconvex graph, then
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
. Moreover,
this upper bound is tight.

2
Bicubic graphs

In this section, we show that
\gamma(G)\leq\frac{120}{49}\rho(G)
if
G
is a
bipartite cubic graph, also called a
bicubic
graph. For this,
we will bound separately the parameters
\gamma(G)
and
\rho(G)
.
The following result of Kostochka & Stocker
[
10
]
gives us
an upper bound for the domination number of cubic graphs.

Lemma 2

(
[
10
]
)
.

For every connected cubic graph
G
on
n\geq 9
vertices,

\gamma(G)\leq\frac{5}{14}n
.

Now, we show a lower bound for
\rho(G)
on bicubic graphs.

Lemma 3
.

For every bicubic graph on
n\geq 16
vertices,
\rho(G)\geq\frac{7}{48}n
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a bicubic graph with parts
X
and
Y
. As
G
is a
bipartite regular graph, we have that
|X|=|Y|=\frac{n}{2}
. Let
P
be a maximum
packing that consists of only vertices in
X
. Let
Q=N(P)
,
R=N(Q)\setminus P
,
and
S=N(R)\setminus Q
. Let
p=|P|
.

Note that
X=P\cup R
. Indeed, if there exists a vertex
v\in X\setminus(P\cup R)
,
then
d_{G}(\{v\},P)\geq 4
(since
R
contains the vertices at distance two from
P
).
Thus
P\cup\{v\}\subseteq X
is a packing, which contradicts the maximality of
P
.
So,
|R|=|X|-p=\frac{n}{2}-p
. Since
X=P\cup R
, we have that
Y=Q\cup S
.
As the neighborhood of every vertex in
P
is pairwise disjoint, we have that
|Q|=3p
and
|S|=|Y|-3p=\frac{n}{2}-3p
.

Let
T
be a maximum packing that consists of vertices in
S
. Let
t=|T|
and let
W=N(T)
.
Note that
W\subseteq R
, and consider the induced bipartite graph
H
with parts
W
and
S\setminus T
.
We depict this situation in Figure
1
. As
G
is cubic and every vertex
u\in W

has a neighbor in
Q
and a neighbor in
T
, every such
u
has degree at most one in
H
. Observe
that every vertex in
S\setminus T
has at least one neighbor in
W
. Otherwise, there exists
v\in S

such that
N_{G}[v]\cap W=\emptyset
, and the set
T\cup\{v\}
is a packing contained in
S
, a
contradiction to the maximality of
T
. Hence, every vertex in
S\setminus T
has degree at least
one in
H
and, therefore,
|S\setminus T|\leq|W|
.

Figure 1
:
The sets
W
,
T
and the graph
H
in the proof of Lemma
3
.

As the neighborhood of every vertex in
T
is pairwise disjoint, we have that
|W|=3t
.
Thus
|S|\leq 4t
. On the other hand, recall that
|S|=|Y|-3p
which implies that
|Y|\leq 3p+4t

or, equivalently, that
|Y|\leq 4(p+t)-p
. Observe that
P\cup T
is a packing of
G
, since the vertices
in
P\subseteq X
are not adjacent to the vertices in
T\subseteq Y
. Therefore,

|Y|\leq 4\rho(G)-p.

(1)

Now, if instead of starting with a maximum packing with vertices only in
X
, we begin with a maximum packing with vertices only in
Y
,
by symmetric arguments, we obtain that

|X|\leq 4\rho(G)-p^{\prime},

(2)

where
p^{\prime}
is the cardinality of a maximum packing
with vertices only in
Y
. In what follows, we show
a lower bound for
p
(resp.
p^{\prime}
) in terms of the size of
X
(resp.
Y
).

Claim 1
.

Let
G
be a connected bicubic graph of order
n\geq 16
with parts
X
and
Y
.
If
P^{*}
is a maximum packing with vertices only in
X
(resp.
Y
), then
|P^{*}|\geq\frac{|X|}{6}

(resp.
|P^{*}|\geq\frac{|Y|}{6}
).

Proof of Claim.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
P^{*}\subseteq X
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the auxiliary graph obtained
from
G
as follows.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}V(G^{\prime})&=&X,\\
E(G^{\prime})&=&\{xx^{\prime}:x,x^{\prime}\in X,x\neq x^{\prime},N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(x^{\prime})\neq\emptyset\}.\end{array}

In other words,
G^{\prime}
is the subgraph of
G^{2}
(the square of
G
) induced by the vertices of
X
.
Observe that, under this setting, a maximum packing with vertices only in
X
is a maximum independent set of
G^{\prime}
.
Moreover, since
G
is cubic, we have that
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq 6
. To show the claim, we will use the
following result known as
Brook’s Theorem
:

Theorem 4

(
[
3
, Theorem 14.4]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph, and is neither an odd cycle nor a complete graph,
then
\chi(G)\leq\Delta(G)
.

Note that, as
G
is connected, any path in
G
between two vertices in
X
induces
a path in
G^{\prime}
between those vertices. Therefore,
G^{\prime}
is also connected. Furthermore,
observe that
G^{\prime}
is not a complete graph. Otherwise, since
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq 6
, we have that
the order
G^{\prime}
is at most
7
. But, this implies that
G
has at most
14
vertices, a
contradiction.

If
G^{\prime}
is an odd cycle, any maximum independent set has size at
least
\left\lfloor\frac{|X|}{2}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{|X|}{6}
and we are done.
Finally, if
G^{\prime}
is not an odd cycle, then,
by Theorem
4
, we have that
G^{\prime}
admits a
\Delta(G^{\prime})
-coloring.
Thus,
G
has an independent set of size at least
V(G^{\prime})/\Delta(G^{\prime})\geq|X|/6
.
This finishes the proof of Claim
1
.
∎

By Claim
1
, after adding (
1
) and (
2
), we obtain the following.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}n&\leq&8\rho(G)-(p+p^{\prime})\\[5.69054pt]
&\leq&8\rho(G)-\frac{n}{6},\end{array}

or, equivalently,
\rho(G)\geq\frac{7}{48}n
.
This finishes the proof of Lemma
3
.
∎

Using an integer programming solver and the database in
[
7
]
, we have confirmed
that the inequality
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
holds for any bicubic graph of order at most
14
. Therefore,
Lemma
2
and Lemma
3
imply the following result.

Theorem 5
.

If
G
is a bicubic graph, then
\gamma(G)\leq\frac{120}{49}\rho(G)<2.45\rho(G)
.

Maximal outerplanar graphs

A graph is
outerplanar
if it has an embedding in the plane such that
all of its vertices belong to the boundary of its unbounded face. An outerplanar graph
is maximal if the addition of a new edge breaks its outerplanarity. In this section,
we will show that
\gamma(G)\leq 3\rho(G)
if
G
is a maximal
outerplanar graph. In fact, we will show an stronger result.

Theorem 6
.

For every maximal outerplanar
graph
G
,
{\gamma(G)\leq\min\{3\rho(G),\frac{9}{4}\rho(G)+t/4}\}
,
where
t
is the number of vertices of degree at most 3 in
G
.

In what follows, given a maximal outerplanar graph
G
, we will
suppose that
G
is embedded in the plane (even if this is not stated
explicitly). Let
G
be a maximal outerplanar graph. We denote by
G^{*}

the
dual
of
G
; that is, the vertices of
G^{*}
represent the triangles
of
G
, and two vertices in
G^{*}
are adjacent if the corresponding triangles
share an edge. Also, we denote by
G^{\prime}
the
clique graph
of
G
;
that is, the vertices of
G^{\prime}
represent the maximal cliques of
G
, and two vertices
in
G^{\prime}
are adjacent if they share a vertex. Since
G
is maximal outerplanar,
the maximal cliques of
G
are also its triangles and, thus,
G^{*}
is a
subgraph of
G^{\prime}
. To avoid confusion, hereafter, we refer to the elements
of
V(G^{*})
(resp.
V(G^{\prime})
) as the
nodes
of
G^{*}
(resp.
G^{\prime}
).
It is known that
G^{\prime}
is a dually chordal graph
[
5
, Corollary 11]
.
Hence, by Brandstadt et al.
[
4
]
, the following property holds.

Proposition 7

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

\gamma(G^{\prime})=\rho(G^{\prime})
.

Thus, by Proposition
7
,
it suffices to show that
\gamma(G)\leq\min\{3\gamma(G^{\prime}),\frac{9}{4}\gamma(G^{\prime})+t/4\}

(see Lemma
9
) and that
\rho(G)\geq\rho(G^{\prime})

(see Lemma
10
) to prove Theorem
6
.
For the first lemma, we will use the following result obtained by Tokunaga
[
15
]
.

Proposition 8

(
[
15
, Lemma 1]
)
.

A maximal outerplanar graph can be 4-colored such
that every cycle of length 4 has all four colours.

In what follows, if
\{u,v,w\}
induces a triangle in
G
, we
denote it by
uvw
. Sometimes we will
abuse this notation and refer to the node that represents
uvw
in
G^{\prime}

also as
uvw
. In each case, its meaning will be clear from the context.
We now proceed to the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 9
.

For every maximal outerplanar graph
G
,

{\gamma(G)\leq\min\{3\gamma(G^{\prime}),\frac{9}{4}\gamma(G^{\prime})+t/4}\}
,
where
t
is the number of vertices of degree at most 3 in
G
.

Proof.

Let
X^{\prime}
be a dominating set in
G^{\prime}
.
We define a set of vertices
X
in
G

as the union of all the vertices of the corresponding
nodes in
G^{\prime}
. That is,
X=\bigcup_{abc\in X^{\prime}}\{a,b,c\}
.
We will show that
X
is a dominating set of
G
.
Let
u\in V(G)
.
Let
uvw
be a triangle in
G
. As

X^{\prime}
is a dominating set in
G^{\prime}
, either
uvw\in X^{\prime}
or
uvw

is adjacent, in
G^{\prime}
, to a node in
X^{\prime}
. Therefore, there exists
a vertex in
\{u,v,w\}
that belongs to
X
. Thus,
either
u\in X
or
u
has a neighbor in
X
.
Hence
\gamma(G)\leq 3\gamma(G^{\prime})
.

Now, consider a
4
-coloring
\chi=\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}\}
of
G
as in Proposition
8
.
We will show that

C_{123}=(C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup C_{3})\cap X

dominates every vertex of degree at least 4.
Let
u
be a vertex of degree at least 4. Since
the neighborhood of a vertex in an outerplanar
graph induces a path
[
2
, Theorem 1A]
,
let
v_{1}v_{2}\ldots v_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 4
, be a path in
G
such that
every
v_{i}
is a neighbor of
u
.
Suppose by contradiction that
C_{123}
does not dominate
u
.
Then
X\cap\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\subseteq C_{4}
.
Furthermore,
X\cap\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\neq\emptyset

since
X
is a dominating set of
G
.
Let
v_{i}\in X\cap\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
.
As the edge
uv_{i}
is in at most two triangles, there exists
a triangle
uv_{j}v_{k}
that do not contain
v_{i}
and
\{u,v_{i},v_{j},v_{k}\}
form a cycle.
But then
u,v_{j},v_{k}\notin C_{4}
. This implies that
u,v_{j},v_{k}\notin X
and,
therefore, the node
uv_{j}v_{k}
is not dominated by
X^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
Hence,
C_{123}
dominates every vertex of degree at least
4
.

Thus,

C_{123}\cup U_{123}
is a dominating set of
G
,
where
U_{123}
is the set of vertices not dominated by
C_{123}
.
By repeating the same argument, we conclude that

C_{ijk}\cup U_{ijk}
is a dominating set for any
ijk\in\{123,124,134,234\}
,
where
U_{ijk}
is the set of vertices not dominated by

C_{ijk}
.
Now, note that
U_{ijk}\cap U_{ijk^{\prime}}=\emptyset
,
for every
i,j,k,k^{\prime}\in\{1,2,3,4\}
,
k\neq k^{\prime}
.
Indeed, suppose by contradiction that there exists a vertex
u\in U_{ijk}\cap U_{ijk^{\prime}}
.
As
\{i,j,k,k^{\prime}\}=\{1,2,3,4\}
, no neighbor of
u
is in
X
, a contradiction to the fact
that
X
dominates
G
. Finally, if we sum up
|C_{ijk}|
, for each
ijk\in\{123,124,134,234\}
,
we obtain
3|X|
as each color
C_{i}
is repeated
3
times in that summation. Hence, by an averaging
argument, we have that
\gamma(G)\leq\frac{3|X|+t}{4}=\frac{9|X^{\prime}|+t}{4}
.
∎

We now proceed to the proof of our second lemma.

Lemma 10
.

For every maximal outerplanar graph
G
,
we have
\rho(G)\geq\rho(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

First, note that
G^{*}
is a spanning

G^{\prime}
. Let
Z
be a packing in
G^{\prime}
.
We select an arbitrary node in
Z
, say
r
,
and direct the edges of
G^{*}
away from

,
obtaining a rooted directed tree
T
with root
r
.
For any node
u
, we denote by
T_{u}
the subtree

T

u
, and by
G_{u}
, the corresponding

G
, that is,

G_{u}=G[\{v:v\text{ is a vertex of some node in }T_{u}\}]
.
For a node
u\in V(T)
, we denote by
p(u)
the

u
in
T
, and by

, the height
of
u
in
T
(see Figure

G^{\prime}

,

In a similar way,

G
.

.

Observe that, it suffices to show that, for any

,
we have a packing of size
|Z\cap T_{u}|

. Indeed,

, we have

. We will show this property by

. Actually, we will prove a stronger statement.

Claim 2
.

For any
u\in Z
, there exists a packing

in

such that the vertices
in

are not in

.

Proof of Claim.

By induction on

.
If

, then

is a leave in

, which implies that the only neighbor
of

is its parent

.
Hence, if

, then
Y=\{c\}

and the claim follows. Now, suppose that
h(u)>0
. Let
c
be the vertex in
u

that is not in
u\cap p(u)
. As
h(u)>0
,
T_{u}

is not a single node.
Let
Q
be the set of nodes
q\in(Z\cap T_{u})\setminus\{u\}

such that the path in
T
from
u
to
q
does
not contain any other node in
(Z\cap T_{u})\setminus\{u\}
.
By the induction hypothesis, for every
q\in Q
,
there exists a packing
Y_{q}
in
G_{q}
of size

|Z\cap T_{q}|
that does not contain any vertex
in
q\cap p(q)
.

Let
Y=\{c\}\cup\bigcup_{q\in Q}Y_{q}
.
We will show that
Y
is a packing in
G
.
By the definition of
Q
, if
q,q^{\prime}
are distinct nodes of
Q
,
then
G_{q}\cap G_{q^{\prime}}=\emptyset
.
Thus, the vertices in
q\cap p(q)
separate
Y_{q}

from
Y_{q^{\prime}}
in
G
. As
Y_{q}
does not contain any vertex
in
q\cap p(q)
, we have that
d_{G}(Y_{q},Y_{q^{\prime}})\geq 3
.
Also, as
u,q\in Z
, we have
d_{G^{\prime}}(u,q)\geq 3
.
This implies that
d_{G}(u,q)\geq 2

As the vertices in

We conclude that

∎

By Claim

∎

Biconvex graphs

In this section, we show that
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
on
biconvex graphs. Also, we exhibit a family of biconvex graphs
that attain this bound. Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph
with parts
X
and
Y
. We say that
X
has a
convex ordering

if there exists an ordering of its vertices, say
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
, such
that
N_{G}(y)
consists of consecutive vertices in that ordering, for
each
y\in Y
. Furthermore, we say that
G
is
biconvex
if
both
X
and
Y
admit a convex ordering. A subclass of biconvex graphs
that will be of our interest are the bipartite permutation graphs.
Yu & Chen
[
17
]
showed that, given a biconvex graph
G
with parts
X

and
Y
, we can obtain a bipartite permutation graph from
G
after removing
some (maybe none) of the first and last vertices of a convex ordering of
X
.
We will use this result along with a structural result regarding
bipartite permutation graphs to show the desired upper bound on
\gamma(G)
.
In what follows, we present these results and the necessary definitions.

Let
G
be a connected biconvex graph with parts
X
and
Y
.
Also, let
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
and
\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
be convex
orderings of
X
and
Y

x_{L}
(resp.
x_{R}
) be the
vertex in
N(y_{1})
(resp.
N(y_{m})
) such that
N(x_{L})
(resp.
N(x_{R})
) is not
properly contained in any other neighborhood set. In case of ties, we choose
as
x_{L}
(resp.
x_{R}
) the smallest (resp. largest) vertex that satisfies the
previous condition. Without loss of generality, suppose that
x_{L}\leq x_{R}
,
otherwise consider the reverse ordering of
X
. Let
G_{p}=G[X_{p}\cup Y]
where

X_{p}=\{x_{i}:x_{L}\leq x\leq x_{R}\}
. Yu & Chen
[
17
]
showed the
following (see also
[
1
, Lemma 9]
).

Lemma 11

(
[
17
, Lemma 7]
)
.

G_{p}
is a connected bipartite permutation graph.

Consider the convex orderings
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
and
\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
.
These orderings form a
strong
ordering of
V(G)
if
x_{i}y_{a},x_{k}y_{c}\in E(G)

whenever
x_{i}y_{c},x_{k}y_{a}\in E(G)
, for
i\leq k
and
a\leq c
.
Abbas & Stewart
[
1

proved the following properties of
X-X_{p}
.

Lemma 12

(
[
1

)
.

Let
G
be a biconvex graph with parts
X
and
Y
. There exist
convex orderings
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle

and
\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
that satisfy the following properties:

1.

G_{p}
is a connected bipartite permutation graph.

2.

X_{p}=\langle x_{L},x_{L+1},\ldots,x_{R}\rangle
and
Y=\langle y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle

is a strong ordering of
V(G_{p})

3.

For all
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
, where
x_{1}\leq x_{i}<x_{j}\leq x_{L}

or
x_{R}\leq x_{j}<x_{i}\leq x_{n}
, we have that
N(x_{i})\subseteq N(x_{j})
.

Now, we describe a decomposition of a bipartite permutation graph that along
with Lemma
12
give us the necessary tools to show the main
result of this section. Let
H
be a bipartite permutation graph
with convex orderings
A=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle
and
B=\langle b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\rangle

Uehara & Valiente
[
16
]
showed that
H
can be decomposed into
subgraphs
(K_{1}\cup J_{1}),\ldots,(K_{k}\cup J_{k})
, called a
complete bipartite decomposition

of
H
, in the following way. Let
K_{1}
be the graph induced by
N_{H}(a_{1})\cup N_{H}(b_{1})
. Let
J_{1}
be the
set of isolated vertices in
H-K_{1}
(maybe empty). Next, consider the
graph
H^{\prime}=H-(K_{1}\cup J_{1})
and repeat this process until the graph becomes empty.
For
i=1,\ldots,k
, let
\ell_{A}(K_{i})
and
r_{A}(K_{i})
be the vertices
in
A\cap V(K_{i})
such that
\ell_{A}(K_{i})\leq a\leq r_{A}(K_{i})
, for each vertex
a\in A\cap V(K_{i})
.
Analogously, we define
\ell_{B}(K_{i})
and
r_{B}(K_{i})
. We say that subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
of
H

are adjacent if there exists an edge, in
H

H_{1}
and the other end in
H_{2}
.
Uehara & Valiente
[
16
, Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 5]
showed that a complete bipartite decomposition of
H

satisfies the following properties.

1.

K_{i}
is a complete bipartite graph (with at least one edge); and
K_{i}

is adjacent to
K_{j}
if only if
|i-j|=1
.

2.

Either
J_{i}\subseteq A
or
J_{i}\subseteq B
; moreover
N_{H}(J_{i})\subseteq V(K_{i})
,
and if
a,a^{\prime}\in J_{i}
and
a\leq a^{\prime}
, then
N_{H}(a^{\prime})\subseteq N_{H}(a)
.

3.

If
J_{i}\subseteq B
(resp.
J_{i}\subseteq A
), then
r_{A}(K_{i})\in N_{H}(z)

(resp.
r_{Y}(K_{i})\in N_{H}(z)
), for every vertex
z\in J_{i}
.

Let
G
be a connected biconvex graph with parts
X
and
Y
, and let
G_{p}
the bipartite
permutation graph given by Lemma
12
. Furthermore, let
(K_{1}\cup J_{1}),\ldots,(K_{k}\cup J_{k})

be a complete bipartite decomposition of
G_{p}
. In what follows, we call
k

the

, and denote it by
\text{dw}(G)

Lemma 13
.

Let
G
be a connected biconvex graph with parts
X
and
Y

such that
\text{dw}(G)=k
. Then,
\rho(G)\geq k
. Furthermore,
if
\max\{d_{G}(x_{1},a):a\in J_{1}\}\geq 3
or
N_{G}(x_{n})\subseteq J_{k}
,
then
\rho(G)\geq k+1
.

Proof.

Let
(K_{1}\cup J_{1}),\ldots,(K_{k}\cup J_{k})
be a complete bipartite decomposition of
G_{p}
.
First, we will show that
\rho(G)\geq k
. Let

S:=\{\ell_{X}(K_{i}):1\leq i\leq k,\,i\text{ is odd}\}\cup\{\ell_{Y}(K_{i}):1\leq i\leq k,\,i\text{ is even}\}.

Note that
|S|=k
. Now, we show that
S
is a packing of
G
. By definition of
K_{i}
, we have
that
N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{i}))\cap V(K_{i+1})=\emptyset
. Thus
1
implies that the
distance between
\ell_{X}(K_{i})
and
\ell_{X}(K_{j})
is at least 4, if
i\neq j
and
i,j
are odd.
The same arguments applies to
\ell_{Y}(K_{i})
and
\ell_{Y}(K_{j})
when
i\neq j
and
i,j
are even.
Finally, by
1
, the only case left to consider is a pair of vertices
\ell_{X}(K_{i})

and
\ell_{Y}(K_{i+1})
. Since
N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{i}))\cap V(K_{i+1})=\emptyset
, the distance
between
\ell_{X}(K_{i})
and
\ell_{Y}(K_{i+1})
is at least 3. Therefore,
\rho(G)\geq k
.

Now, suppose that
N_{G}(x_{n})\subseteq J_{k}
. By
2
, the set
J_{k}\subseteq Y
,
and by the definition of
K_{k}
, we have that
N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{k}))\cap J_{k}=\emptyset
. This
implies that the distance between
x_{n}
and the set
\{\ell_{X}(K_{k}),\ell_{Y}(K_{k})\}
is at least 3.
Thus, if we consider the set
S
defined before, then
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{x_{n}\}
is a packing of
G
.
Therefore
\rho(G)\geq k+1
.

Finally, suppose that
\max\{d_{G}(x_{1},a):a\in J_{1}\}\geq 3
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

J_{1}\subseteq X
.

Let
x^{\prime}\in J_{1}
such that
d_{G}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\geq 3
, and consider the following set:

S:=\{\ell_{X}(K_{i}):2\leq i\leq k,i\text{ is odd}\}\cup\{\ell_{Y}(K_{i}):2\leq i\leq k,i\text{ is even}\}.

Then,
|S|=k-1
. By similar arguments as before,
S
is a packing of
G
. Now,
consider the set
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{x_{1},x^{\prime}\}
. Since
N_{G}(x_{1})\cup N_{G}(x^{\prime})\subseteq V(K_{1})
, we have
that the distance between
\ell_{Y}(K_{2})
and
\{x_{1},x^{\prime}\}
is at least 3 and, thus
S^{\prime}
is
a packing of
G
.

Case 2:

J_{1}\subseteq Y
.

In this case, consider the set

S:=\{\ell_{X}(K_{i}):2\leq i\leq k,i\text{ is even}\}\cup\{\ell_{Y}(K_{i}):2\leq i\leq k,i\text{ is odd}\}.

As in the previous case
S
is a packing of
G
such that
|S|=k-1
. Consider the set
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{x_{1},y^{\prime}\}

where
d_{G}(x_{1},y^{\prime})\geq 3
,
y^{\prime}\in J_{1}
. As
N_{G}(y^{\prime})\subseteq V(K_{1})
, we have that
d_{G}(y^{\prime},\ell_{X}(K_{2}))\geq 3
.
Finally, we will show that
N_{G}(x_{1})\cap N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{2}))=\emptyset
. Suppose by contradiction that there exists
a vertex
y^{*}\in N_{G}(x_{1})\cap N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{2}))
.
Since
N_{G}(x_{1})\subseteq V(K_{1})
, we have that
y^{*}<y^{\prime}<\ell_{Y}(K_{2})
. As
G
is biconvex, this implies
that
\ell_{X}(K_{2})
is adjacent to
y^{\prime}\in J_{1}
. This contradicts the fact that
J_{1}
is a set of isolated
vertices in
G_{p}-K_{1}
. Thus
S^{\prime}
is a packing of
G
.

Therefore
\rho(G)\geq k+1
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
G
be a connected biconvex graph with parts
X
and
Y
such that
\text{dw}(G)=k\geq 2
.
If
\max\{d_{G}(x_{1},a):a\in J_{1}\}\leq 2
and
N_{G}(x_{n})\cap V(K_{k})\neq\emptyset
,
then
\gamma(G)\leq 2k
; otherwise we have that
\gamma(G)\leq 2k+2
.

Proof.

Let
(K_{1}\cup J_{1}),\ldots,(K_{k}\cup J_{k})
be a complete bipartite decomposition of
G_{p}
.
First, suppose that
\max\{d_{G}(x_{1},a):a\in J_{1}\}\leq 2
and
N_{G}(x_{n})\cap V(K_{k})\neq\emptyset
.
In this case, we claim that
J_{1}\subseteq X
. Otherwise
J_{1}\subseteq Y
, and
since
N_{G}(x_{1})\subseteq N_{G}(x_{L})=N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{1}))
, we have that
d_{G}(x_{1},J_{1})\geq 3
,
a contradiction. Thus
J_{1}\subseteq X
. Let

S=\{r_{X}(K_{i}),r_{Y}(K_{i}):2\leq i\leq k-1\}.

By
3
, the set
S
dominates
K_{i}\cup J_{i}
, for
i=2,\ldots,k-1
.
Since
d_{G}(x_{1},x^{\prime})\leq 2
, for each
x^{\prime}\in J_{1}
,
2
implies
that there exists
y^{*}\in N_{G}(x_{1})\cap N_{G}(x^{\prime})
, for every
x^{\prime}\in J_{1}
.
Thus, the set
\{r_{X}(K_{1}),y^{*}\}
dominates
K_{1}\cup J_{1}\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{L}\}
.
On the other hand, since
N_{G}(x_{n})\cap V(K_{k})\neq\emptyset
, there
exists
y^{\prime}\in N_{G}(x_{n})\cap V(K_{k})
. By similar arguments as before, the set
\{y^{\prime},r_{X}(K_{k})\}

dominates
K_{k}\cup J_{k}\cup\{x_{R},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Observe that, in case
J_{k}\subseteq X
,
the vertex
y^{\prime}
dominates
J_{k}
since
G
is biconvex. Therefore,
S\cup\{r_{X}(K_{1}),y^{*},r_{X}(K_{k},y^{\prime})\}

dominates
G
implying that
\gamma(G)\leq 2k
.

Finally, suppose that
\max\{d_{G}(x_{1},a):a\in J_{1}\}\geq 3
or
N_{G}(x_{n})\cap V(K_{k})=\emptyset
.
First, let
S
be the same set defined above. Let
y^{*}
and
y^{\prime}
be vertices adjacent to
x_{1}
and
x_{n}
,
respectively. By Lemma
12

c)
, the set
\{y^{*},y^{\prime}\}
dominates
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{L},x_{R},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
Therefore, the set

S^{\prime}=S\cup\{r_{X}(K_{1}),r_{Y}(K_{1}),r_{X}(K_{k}),r_{Y}(K_{k}),y^{*},y^{\prime}\}

dominates
G
, which implies that
\gamma(G)\leq 2k+2
.

Theorem 15
.

If
G
is a connected biconvex graph, then
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
.

Proof.

Let
k=\text{dw}(G)
. Lemma
13

k\geq 2
. So suppose that
k=1
, that is
G_{p}=K_{1}\cup J_{1}
.
We distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

d_{G}(x_{1},x_{n})=2
.

In this case, there exists a vertex
y^{*}\in N_{G}(x_{1})\cap N_{G}(x_{n})
. Since

G
is biconvex, the vertex
y^{*}
dominates
X
. Moreover, as
k=1
, we
have that
N_{G}(\ell_{X}(K_{1}))=Y
. Thus, the set
\{\ell_{X}(K_{1}),y^{*}\}

dominates
G
. Therefore
\gamma(G)\leq 2\leq 2\rho(G)
.

Case 2:

d_{G}(x_{1},x_{n})\geq 3
.

In this case,
\{x_{1},x_{n}\}
is a packing of
G
, which implies that
\rho(G)\geq 2
.
Let
y^{*}\in N_{G}(x_{1})

y^{\prime}\in N_{G}(x_{n})

c)
,

y^{*}

y^{\prime}

dominates

\{r_{X}(K_{1}),r_{Y}(K_{1})\}

Final remarks

In this work, we progress towards Conjecture 1 by showing
that
\gamma(G)<2.45\rho(G)
for every bicubic graph
G
.
We believe that this result can be extended to
bipartite and subcubic graphs by using analogous techniques. For this,
a first step could be to extend the result obtained by
Kostochka & Stocker
[
10
]
to subcubic graphs.

Henning et al.
[
9
]
observed that
\gamma(G)\leq\Delta\rho(G)

for any graph
G
without isolated vertices. We propose a stronger version of
Conjecture 1 as follows.

Conjecture 2
.

For any connected graph
G
with at least two vertices,

\gamma(G)\leq(\Delta-1)\rho(G)+1
.

We believe our techniques are still far from settling Conjecture
2
.
However, we can relax this problem by considering a decrease
in the second parameter of the upper bound, that is, is it true
that,
\gamma(G)\leq\max\{(\Delta-1)\rho(G),\Delta(\rho(G)-1)\}+1
,
for every connected graph
G
with at least two vertices?

Figure 5
:
The sun graph satisfies that
\gamma(G)=2\rho(G)
.

On the other hand, we also investigated some classes of graphs, and showed
constant upper bounds for the ratio
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)
in two cases.
As we have seen in Section
1
, Löwenstein
[
12
]

showed that
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)\leq 2
when
G
is a cactus graph.
We took a first step towards extending this result by studying maximal outerplanar
graphs. We show that, in this class, the inequality
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)\leq 3
holds.
We believe that our technique to analyze maximal outerplanar graphs can be improved
to obtain a better upper bound for this class of graphs.

Conjecture 3
.

For any maximal outerplanar graph
G
,
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
.

If Conjecture
3
is true, then this upper bound is tight.
Since the graph in Figure
5
, known as the sun graph, meets this bound.
Further subclasses of planar graphs can be studied,
as planar triangulations and triangulated disks.
Finally, we considered the class of biconvex graphs and obtained a tight upper bound
of
2
for the ratio
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)
. We observe that for many classes of graphs
it is unkwown whether they admit a constant upper bound or not. We depict this information
in Figure
6
for some classes of graphs.

. Moreover,

Quandle cohomology, extensions and automorphisms
Valeriy Bardakov, Mahender Singh

Abstract
. A quandle is an algebraic system with a binary operation satisfying three axioms modelled on the three Reidemeister moves of planar diagrams of links in the 3-space. The paper establishes new relationship between cohomology, extensions and automorphisms of quandles. We derive a four term exact sequence relating quandle 1-cocycles, second quandle cohomology and certain group of automorphisms of an abelian extension of quandles. A non-abelian counterpart of this sequence involving dynamical cohomology classes is also established, and some applications to lifting of quandle automorphisms are given. Viewing the construction of the conjugation, the core and the generalised Alexander quandle of a group as an adjoint functor of some appropriate functor from the category of quandles to the category of groups, we prove that these functors map extensions of groups to extensions of quandles. Finally, we construct some natural group homomorphisms from the second cohomology of a group to the second cohomology of its core and conjugation quandles.

Primary 57M27, 18G60; Secondary 18G50, 20B25

1.
Introduction

A quandle is an algebraic system with a binary operation satisfying three axioms that are algebraic formulations of the three Reidemeister moves of planar diagrams of links in the 3-space. These objects have shown appearance in a wide spectrum of mathematics including knot theory
[
25
,
29
]
, group theory, set-theoretic solutions to the quantum Yang-Baxter equation and Yetter-Drinfeld Modules
[
14
]
, Riemannian symmetric spaces
[
28
]
and Hopf algebras
[
1
]
, to name a few. Though already studied under different guises in the literature, study of these objects gained momentum after the fundamental works of Matveev
[
29
]
and Joyce
[
25
]
, who showed that link quandles are complete invariants of non-split links up to orientation of the ambient space. Although link quandles are strong invariants, it is difficult to check whether two quandles are isomorphic. This motivated search for newer properties and invariants of quandles themselves. The curious reader may refer to the articles
[
6
,
26
,
16
,
30
,
31
]
for more on the historical development of the subject.

Recent years have seen many interesting works related to quandles and racks, their weaker analogues. A (co)homology theory for racks was introduced in
[
17
,
18
]
as homology of the rack space of a rack. This motivated development of a (co)homology theory for quandles in
[
9
]
where state-sum invariants using quandle cocycles as weights were defined. These theories have been further developed and generalized in
[
1
,
27
]
which encompasses all the previously known theories. A recent work
[
33
]
has shown that quandle cohomology is a Quillen cohomology which is the cohomology group of a functor from the category of models to that of complexes. In the cohomology theory of groups, it is well-known that 2-cocycles are closely related to extensions of groups
[
5
]
. Analogous theories have been developed for quandle cocycles
[
8
]
. Besides being of independent algebraic interest these are useful in computing cocycle knot invariants. Abelian extensions of quandles have been investigated in
[
10
]
using diagrammatic approach. Further generalizations of quandle extensions by dynamical cocycles have been given in
[
1
]
, and a more general abelian extension theory for racks and quandles, which also generalizes other variants in the literature, has been detailed in
[
24
]
. Using the generalized quandle cohomology theory of
[
1
]
some new knot invariants have been introduced in
[
7
]
. Quandle cocycle invariants have been computed from quandle coloring numbers in
[
11
]
. Some properties of quandle extensions and of invariants based on 2-cocycles are investigated in
[
12
]
. For example, it is proved that if the quandle extension is a conjugation quandle, then the state-sum invariant given by the 2-cocycle is constant. An explicit description of the second quandle cohomology of a finite indecomposable quandle has been given in
[
20
]
developing further ideas from
[
13
]
that related the second cohomology of a quandle to the first cohomology of its enveloping group.

Automorphisms of quandles reveal a lot about their internal structures and have been investigated in much detail in a series of works
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
19
,
21
]
. Study of automorphisms of quandles is important from the point of view of knot theory. The totality of the number of colorings of diagram of a knot
K
by a quandle
X
is in bijection with the set
\operatorname{Hom}(Q(K),X)
of homomorphisms from the knot quandle
Q(K)
to
X
. Composing a fixed coloring of the knot diagram by automorphisms of
X
gives rise to new colorings of the knot diagram. A recent work
[
22
]
has explored further relationship between the knot symmetries and the automorphisms of the knot quandle.

The purpose of this paper is to establish some interestingly new connections between cohomology, extensions and automorphisms of quandles. We also relate these objects to cohomology and extensions of groups.

2.
Preliminaries on quandles

This section recalls some basic definitions for the convenience of the reader.

A
quandle
is a non-empty set
X
with a binary operation
(x,y)\mapsto x*y
satisfying the following axioms:

(Q1)

x*x=x
for all
x\in X
,

(Q2)

For any
x,y\in X
there exists a unique
z\in X
such that
x=z*y
,

(Q3)

(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
.

An algebraic system satisfying only (Q2) and (Q3) is called a
rack
. Many interesting examples of quandles come from groups showing deep connections with group theory.

•

If
G
is a group and
n
an integer, then the set
G
equipped with the binary operation
a*b=b^{-n}ab^{n}
forms a quandle
\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(G)
. For
n=1
, it is the
conjugation quandle

\operatorname{Conj}(G)
.

•

If
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
is an automorphism of a group
G
, then the set
G
with the binary operation
x*y=f(xy^{-1})y
forms a quandle
\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(G)
called the
generalised Alexander quandle
of
G
. In particular, if
G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
and
\phi
is the inversion, then we get the
dihedral quandle

\operatorname{R}_{n}
.

•

If
G
is a group, then the binary operation
a*b=ba^{-1}b
turns the set
G
into the
core quandle

\operatorname{Core}(G)
.

A quandle
X
is called
trivial
if
x*y=x
for all
x,y\in X
. Unlike groups, a trivial quandle can contain arbitrary number of elements. Notice that the axioms (Q2) and (Q3) are equivalent to saying that the map
S_{x}:X\to X
given by

S_{x}(y)=y*x

is an automorphism of
X
for each
x\in X
. Each such
S_{x}
is called the
inner automorphism
of
X
induced by
x
, and the group
\operatorname{Inn}(X)
generated by such automorphisms is called the group of inner automorphisms. The group of all automorphisms of
X
is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. A quandle
X
is
connected
if
\operatorname{Inn}(X)
acts transitively on
X
. Every quandle
X
gives rise to a universal group

\operatorname{Adj}(X)=\big\{e_{x},~x\in X~|~e_{x*y}=e_{y}e_{x}e_{y}^{-1},x,y\in X\big\},

called the
adjoint
or
associated
or
enveloping group
of
X
. If
\mathcal{Q}
is the category of quandles and
\mathcal{G}
the category of groups, then the functor

\operatorname{Adj}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{G}
is left adjoint to the functor
\operatorname{Conj}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}
and the adjoint group of a knot quandle is the knot group
[
25
, Section 15]
.

3.
Quandle cocycles and extensions

Extension theory for quandles using quandle cocycles was first proposed in
[
8
,
10
]
. A far reaching generalization of quandle extensions using dynamical 2-cocycles which encompasses all the previous constructions was given in
[
1
]
. We begin with a generalisation of a construction of Andruskiewitsch and Grana in
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
and
S
be two sets,
\alpha:X\times X\to\operatorname{Map}(S\times S,S)
and
\beta:S\times S\to\operatorname{Map}(X\times X,X)
two maps. Then the set
X\times S
with the binary operation

(3.0.1)

(x,s)*(y,t)=\big(\beta_{s,t}(x,y),~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)

forms a quandle if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\beta_{s,s}(x,x)=x
and
\alpha_{x,x}(s,s)=s
for all
x\in X
,
s\in S
;

(2)

for each
(y,t)\in X\times S
, the map
(x,s)\mapsto\big(\beta_{s,t}(x,y),~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)
is a bijection;

(3)

\beta_{\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),u}\Big(\beta_{s,t}(x,y),~z\Big)=\beta_{\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)}\Big(\beta_{s,u}(x,z),~\beta_{t,u}(y,z)\Big)

and

\alpha_{\beta_{s,t}(x,y),z}\Big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),~u\Big)=\alpha_{\beta_{s,u}(x,z),\beta_{t,u}(y,z)}\Big(\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\Big)
.

Proof.

The axiom (Q1) holds if and only if
\beta_{s,s}(x,x)=x
and
\alpha_{x,x}(s,s)=s
for all
x\in X
,
s\in S
. The axiom (Q2) is equivalent to condition (2). Finally, if
(x,s),(y,t),(z,u)\in X\times S
, then

\displaystyle\big((x,s)*(y,t)\big)*(z,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\beta_{s,t}(x,y),~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)*(z,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\beta_{\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),u}\big(\beta_{s,t}(x,y),z\big),~\alpha_{\beta_{s,t}(x,y),z}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),u\big)\Big)

and

\displaystyle\big((x,s)*(z,u)\big)*\big((y,t)*(z,u)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\beta_{s,u}(x,z),~\alpha_{x,z}(s,u)\big)*\big(\beta_{t,u}(y,z),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\beta_{\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)}\big(\beta_{s,u}(x,z),~\beta_{t,u}(y,z)\big),~\alpha_{\beta_{s,u}(x,z),~\beta_{t,u}(y,z)}\big(\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\big)\Big).

Thus, axiom (Q3) is equivalent to condition (3).
∎

We denote the quandle obtained in Proposition
3.1
by
X~_{\alpha}\times_{\beta}S
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
and
S
be two sets,
\alpha:X\times X\to\operatorname{Map}(S\times S,S)
and
\beta:S\to\operatorname{Map}(X\times X,X)
two maps. Then the set
X\times S
with the binary operation

(x,s)*(y,t)=\big(\beta_{t}(x,y),\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)

forms a quandle if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\beta_{s}(x,x)=x
and
\alpha_{x,x}(s,s)=s
for all
x\in X
,
s\in S
;

(2)

\beta_{s}(-,y):X\to X
and
\alpha_{x,y}(-,t):S\to S
are bijections for all
x,y\in X
,
s\in S
;

(3)

\beta_{u}\big(\beta_{t}(x,y),~z\big)=\beta_{\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)}\big(\beta_{u}(x,z),~\beta_{u}(y,z)\big)

and

\alpha_{\beta_{t}(x,y),z}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),~u\big)=\alpha_{\beta_{u}(x,z),~\beta_{u}(y,z)}\big(\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\big)
.

Andruskiewitsch and Grana
[
1
, Section 2.1]
considered a special case of Proposition
3.1
when
X
is a quandle and

\beta_{s,t}(x,y)=x*y

for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
. In this case the map
\alpha
satisfy the
conditions

(3.0.2)

\alpha_{x,x}(s,s)=s,

(3.0.3)

\alpha_{x,y}(-,t):S\to S~\textrm{is a bijection}

and the
cocycle condition

(3.0.4)

\alpha_{x*y,z}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),~u\big)=\alpha_{x*z,y*z}\big(\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\big)

for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
. Such an
\alpha
is referred as a
dynamical 2-cocycle
. Further, in this case, the quandle operation (
3.0.1
) on
X\times S
becomes

(3.0.5)

(x,s)*(y,t)=\big(x*y,~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big).

The quandle so obtained is called an
extension
of
X
by
S
through
\alpha
and is denoted by
X\times_{\alpha}S
. It is easy to see that for each
x\in X
, the set
S
forms a quandle with the binary operation

s*_{x}t:=\alpha_{x,x}(s,t)

for all
s,t\in S
.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
X
is a connected quandle, then
(S,*_{x})\cong(S,*_{y})
for all
x,y\in X
.

Proof.

Let
x,z\in X
and
u\in S
. Define a map
\phi:(S,*_{x})\to(S,*_{x*z})
by

\phi(s)=\alpha_{x,z}(s,u)

for all
s\in S
.
Then, for
s,t\in S
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(s*_{x}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,z}(s*_{x}t,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,z}\big(\alpha_{x,x}(s,t),u\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{x*z,x*z}\big(\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~\alpha_{x,z}(t,u)\big),~\textrm{by \eqref{dynamical-cocycle-condition3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(s)*_{x*z}\phi(t),

and
\phi
is an isomorphism of quandles. Since
X
is connected, it follows that
(S,*_{x})\cong(S,*_{y})
for all
x,y\in X
.
∎

4.
Action of automorphisms on dynamical 2-cocycles

Let
X
be a quandle,
\Sigma_{S}
the symmetric group on a set
S
and
\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
the set of all dynamical 2-cocycles. For
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
and a dynamical 2-cocycle
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
, define

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha:X\times X\to\mathrm{Map}(S\times S,S)

by setting

(4.0.1)

^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t):=\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)

for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The map
\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)\times\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)\to\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
given by

\big((\phi,\theta),\alpha\big)\mapsto~^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha

defines a left action of the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
on the set
\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
.

Proof.

Let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
. We first show that
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha
is a dynamical 2-cocycle. For
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, we compute

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x*y,z}\Big(^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t),u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(y),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\Big(\theta^{-1}\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big),~\theta^{-1}(u)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(y),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big),~\theta^{-1}(u)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(z),~\phi^{-1}(y)*\phi^{-1}(z)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(u)\big),~\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(y),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\big(\theta^{-1}(t),~\theta^{-1}(u)\big)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\textrm{by the cocycle condition \eqref{dynamical-cocycle-condition3} for}~\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x*z),\phi^{-1}(y*z)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(u)\big),~\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(y),~\phi^{-1}(z)}\big(\theta^{-1}(t),~\theta^{-1}(u)\big)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x*z,y*z}\Big(^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,z}(s,u),~~^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{y,z}(t,u)\Big),

and hence
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha
satisfy (
3.0.4
). It follows easily that
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha
satisfy (
3.0.2
) and (
3.0.3
) which shows that
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
.

Given
(\phi_{i},\theta_{i})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
for
i=1,2
, we see that

{}^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi_{1}\phi_{2},~\theta_{1}\theta_{2})}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\Big(\alpha_{\phi_{2}^{-1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(x),~\phi_{2}^{-1}\phi_{1}^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta_{2}^{-1}\theta_{1}^{-1}(s),~\theta_{2}^{-1}\theta_{1}^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\Big(^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}\alpha_{\phi_{1}^{-1}(x),~\phi_{1}^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta_{1}^{-1}(s),~\theta_{1}^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}\big(^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}\alpha\big)_{x,y}(s,t),

and hence
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
acts on
\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
from the left.
∎

Two dynamical 2-cocycles
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
are said to be
cohomologous
if there exists a map
\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}
such that

(4.0.2)

\beta_{x,y}(s,t)=\lambda_{x*y}\Big(\alpha_{x,y}\big(\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s),~\lambda_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)

for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
. It can be seen easily that being cohomologous is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
, and we denote the set of cohomology classes of dynamical 2-cocycles by
\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)
. Further, we denote the equivalence class of
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
by
[\alpha]\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The map
\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)\times\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)\to\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)
given by

\big((\phi,\theta),[\alpha]\big)\mapsto~[^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha]

defines a left action of the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
on the set
\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)
.

Proof.

It only needs to be shown that cohomologous cocycles are mapped to cohomologous cocycles under the action in Lemma
4.1
. Let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
and
\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}
such that (
4.0.2
) is satisfied. Define a map
\lambda^{\prime}:X\to\Sigma_{S}
by setting
\lambda^{\prime}_{x}=\theta\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}\theta^{-1}
for
x\in X
. Then, for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, we have

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\beta_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\beta_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(y)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\Big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}\big(\theta^{-1}(s)\big),~~\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}\big(\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)\Big)\Big),

by using

(
4.0.2
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\Big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\Big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}\theta^{-1}(s),~~\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}\theta^{-1}(t)\Big)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\theta\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}\theta^{-1}\Big)\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\Big(\theta^{-1}(\theta\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}\theta^{-1})^{-1}(s),~~\theta^{-1}(\theta\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(y)}\theta^{-1})^{-1}(t)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{x*y}~\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\Big(\theta^{-1}{\lambda^{\prime}_{x}}^{-1}(s),~~\theta^{-1}{\lambda^{\prime}}_{y}^{-1}(t)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{x*y}~\Big(^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}\Big({\lambda^{\prime}_{x}}^{-1}(s),~~{\lambda^{\prime}_{y}}^{-1}(t)\Big)\Big),

and hence
[^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha]=[^{(\phi,\theta)}\beta]
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
X
be a quandle,
S=A
a group (not necessarily abelian) and
\alpha:X\times X\to A
a map. Then the map
\alpha^{\prime}:X\times X\to\mathrm{Map}(A\times A,A)
given by

\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t)=s~\alpha_{x,y},

where
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in A
, satisfy (
3.0.2
) and (
3.0.4
) if and only if
\alpha
satisfy

(4.0.3)

\alpha_{x,y}~\alpha_{x*y,z}=\alpha_{x,z}~\alpha_{x*z,y*z}

and

(4.0.4)

\alpha_{x,x}=1

for
x,y,z\in X
. If
A
is abelian, then (
4.0.3
) is the cocycle condition defining the usual second quandle cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X,A)

[
9
]
, which will be discussed in some detail in Section
6
.

5.
Exact sequence relating automorphisms and dynamical cohomology

Let
X
be a quandle,
S
a set,
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
and
E=X\times_{\alpha}S
the extension of
X
by
S
using
\alpha
. For a fixed base point
x_{0}\in X
, we set

\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)=\big\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)~|~\phi(x_{0})=x_{0}\big\},

and

\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)=\Big\{\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)~|~\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\tau(x,s)\big)~\textrm{for some}~\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)~\textrm{and}~\tau\in\mathrm{Map}(X\times S,S)\Big\}.

We write
\tau(x,s)
as
\tau_{x}(s)
for
x\in X
and
s\in S
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The following hold:

(1)

If
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)
such that
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),\tau_{x}(s)\big)
, then
\tau_{x}\in\Sigma_{S}
.

(2)

\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
.

Proof.

Assertion (1) follows from bijectivity of
\psi
. For the second assertion, let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
such that
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),\tau_{x}(s)\big)
for all
x\in X
and
s\in S
. Define
\varphi(x,s):=\big(\phi^{-1}(x),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}(s)\big)
. Since
\phi
and
\tau_{x}
are bijections for all
x\in X
, it follows that
\varphi
is also a bijection. Since
\psi
is a quandle homomorphism, for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, we have

\tau_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)=\alpha_{\phi(x),\phi(y)}\big(\tau_{x}(s),~\tau_{y}(t)\big),

which is equivalent to

(5.0.1)

\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}(s),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}(t)\big)=\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big).

Now, consider

\displaystyle\varphi\big((x,s)*(y,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\big(x*y,~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x*y),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(y),~\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}(s),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}(t)\big)\big),~\textrm{by}~\eqref{useful-in-subgroup}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}(s)\big)*\big(\phi^{-1}(y),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(x,s)*\varphi(y,t).

Thus,
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
and a direct check shows that it is the inverse of
\psi
. Further, given
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\tau_{x}(s)\big)
and
\psi^{\prime}(x,s)=\big(\phi^{\prime}(x),~\tau^{\prime}_{x}(s)\big)
, we see that

(5.0.2)

\psi\psi^{\prime}(x,s)=\psi\big(\phi^{\prime}(x),~\tau^{\prime}_{x}(s)\big)=\big(\phi\big(\phi^{\prime}(x)\big),~\tau_{\phi^{\prime}(x)}\big(\tau^{\prime}_{x}(s)\big)\big)=\big(\phi\phi^{\prime}(x),~\tau_{\phi^{\prime}(x)}\tau^{\prime}_{x}(s)\big),

and hence
\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
.
∎

Restricting the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
to that of its subgroup
\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
, let

\Omega_{[\alpha]}:\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S)

be the orbit map given by

(5.0.3)

\Omega_{[\alpha]}(\phi,\theta)=~^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha].

For simplicity of notation, we denote
\Omega_{[\alpha]}
by
\Omega
. Further, let

\Phi:\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}

be the restriction map given by

(5.0.4)

\Phi(\psi)=~(\phi,\theta),

where
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\tau_{x}(s)\big)
and
\theta(s):=\tau_{x_{0}}(s)
for all
x\in X
and
s\in S
. Define a map
\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}
by setting

\lambda_{x}:=\theta\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}.

Note that
\lambda_{x_{0}}=\mathrm{id}_{S}
and
\tau_{x}
can be uniquely written as

(5.0.5)

\tau_{x}=(\tau_{x}\theta^{-1})\theta=\lambda_{\phi(x)}^{-1}\theta

for all
x\in X
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The map
\Phi
is a group homomorphism and
{\rm Im}(\Phi)=\Omega^{-1}\big([\alpha]\big)
.

Proof.

Let
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
. Then, by (
5.0.2
), we have

\psi\psi^{\prime}(x,s)=\big(\phi\phi^{\prime}(x),~\tau_{\phi^{\prime}(x)}\tau^{\prime}_{x}(s)\big),

where
\tau_{\phi^{\prime}(x_{0})}\tau^{\prime}_{x_{0}}(s)=\tau_{x_{0}}\tau^{\prime}_{x_{0}}(s)=\theta\theta^{\prime}(s)
. This shows that
\Phi(\psi\psi^{\prime})=\Phi(\psi)\Phi(\psi^{\prime})
.

Notice that

\Omega^{-1}\big([\alpha]\big)=\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)_{[\alpha]},

the stabiliser of the action of
\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
at
[\alpha]
. Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
such that
\Phi(\psi)=(\phi,\theta)
. Since
\psi
is a quandle homomorphism, for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, we have

(5.0.6)

\tau_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)=\alpha_{\phi(x),\phi(y)}\big(\tau_{x}(s),~\tau_{y}(t)\big).

Since
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)
and
\theta\in\Sigma_{S}
, we can replace
x,y
by
\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)
and
s,t
by
\theta^{-1}(s),\theta^{-1}(t)
, respectively, in (
5.0.6
). This gives

(5.0.7)

\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)=\alpha_{x,y}\Big(\tau_{\phi^{-1}(x)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s)\big),~\tau_{\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big).

Define a map
\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}
by setting
\lambda_{x}:=\theta\tau^{-1}_{\phi^{-1}(x)}
. Then (
5.0.7
) takes the form

(5.0.8)

\lambda_{x*y}^{-1}~\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)=\alpha_{x,y}\big(\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s),~\lambda_{y}^{-1}(t)\big),

which is further equivalent to

(5.0.9)

^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),~\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)=\lambda_{x*y}\Big(\alpha_{x,y}\big(\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s),~\lambda_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)\Big).

This shows that
{\rm Im}(\Phi)\subseteq\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)_{[\alpha]}
.

Conversely, let
(\phi,\theta)\in\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)_{[\alpha]}
, that is, there exists a map
\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}
such that (
5.0.9
) holds. Define
\psi:E\to E
by setting

\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\lambda_{\phi(x)}^{-1}\theta(s)\big).

Clearly,
\psi
is bijective since
\phi
and
\theta
are so. Replacing
x,y
by
\phi(x),\phi(y)
and
s,t
by
\theta(s),\theta(t)
, respectively, in (
5.0.9
) gives

(5.0.10)

\lambda_{\phi(x)*\phi(y)}^{-1}~\theta\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,~t)\big)=\alpha_{\phi(x),\phi(y)}\big(\lambda_{\phi(x)}^{-1}\theta(s),~\lambda_{\phi(y)}^{-1}\theta(t)\big).

Now, we compute

\displaystyle\psi\big((x,s)*(y,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\big(x*y,~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(x)*\phi(y),~\lambda_{\phi(x)*\phi(y)}^{-1}\theta\big(\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(x)*\phi(y),~\alpha_{\phi(x),\phi(y)}\big(\lambda_{\phi(x)}^{-1}\theta(s),~\lambda_{\phi(y)}^{-1}\theta(t)\big)\Big),~\textrm{by}~\eqref{ext-homo-condition5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi(x),~\lambda_{\phi(x)}^{-1}\theta(s)\big)*\big(\phi(y),~\lambda_{\phi(y)}^{-1}\theta(t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(x,s)*\psi(y,t).

Thus,
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)
and
\Phi(\psi)=(\phi,\theta)
, which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)\cong\Big\{\lambda:X\to\Sigma_{S}~|~\lambda_{x_{0}}=\mathrm{id}_{S}~\textrm{and}~\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\lambda_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}\big(\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s),\lambda_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)\big)\Big\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\psi\in\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)
if and only if
\Phi(\psi)=(\mathrm{id}_{X},\mathrm{id}_{S})
. In view of (
5.0.5
), we have

\psi(x,s)=\big(x,\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s)\big),

and
\lambda_{x_{0}}=\mathrm{id}_{S}
. Further, by (
5.0.6
),
\psi
is a quandle homomorphism if and only if

\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\lambda_{x*y}\big(\alpha_{x,y}\big(\lambda_{x}^{-1}(s),\lambda_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)\big)

for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
. Thus,
\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)
is the desired set via the map
\psi\mapsto\lambda
. Here, in view of (
5.0.2
), the right hand side is a group with operation
(\lambda\lambda^{\prime})_{x}:=\lambda_{x}\lambda^{\prime}_{x}
for all
x\in X
.
∎

Combining propositions
5.2
and
5.3
, we obtain our first main result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X
be a quandle,
x_{0}\in X
,
S
a set,
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
and
E=X\times_{\alpha}S
the extension of
X
by
S
using
\alpha
. Then there exists an exact sequence

(5.0.11)

1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;S),

where exactness at
\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}
means that
{\rm Im}(\Phi)=\Omega^{-1}\big([\alpha]\big)
.

The preceding theorem can be thought of as a quandle analogue of a similar result of Wells for groups
[
34
]
. As a consequence, we obtain the following short exact sequence.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X
be a quandle,
x_{0}\in X
,
S
a set,
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
and
E=X\times_{\alpha}S
the extension of
X
by
S
using
\alpha
. Then there exists a short exact sequence of groups

(5.0.12)

1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\Sigma_{S}\big)_{[\alpha]}\longrightarrow 1.

It is interesting to find conditions for the splitting of the short exact sequence (
5.0.12
).

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
X
and
S
be quandles and
x_{0}\in X
. If
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}^{2}(X;S)
is the cocycle given by
\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=s*t
for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, then the short exact sequence

(5.0.13)

1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(S)\big)_{[\alpha]}\longrightarrow 1

splits.

Proof.

Note that for the given
\alpha
, the quandle operation (
3.0.1
) on
E=X\times_{\alpha}S
becomes

(x,s)*(y,t)=(x*y,s*t),

which is just the product quandle structure. Also, a direct check shows that, in this case,

\operatorname{Ker}(\Phi)\cong\Big\{\lambda:X\to\operatorname{Aut}(S)~|~\lambda_{x_{0}}=\mathrm{id}_{S}~\textrm{and}~\lambda_{x*y}(s*t)=\lambda_{x}(s)*\lambda_{y}(t)\Big\}

and

\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)=\Big\{\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)~|~\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\tau_{x}(s)\big)~\textrm{for some}~\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)~\textrm{and}~\tau:X\to\operatorname{Aut}(S)\Big\}.

Now, for any
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(S)
, we have

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\theta\Big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big(\theta^{-1}(s),~\theta^{-1}(t)\big)\Big)=\theta\big(\theta^{-1}(s)*\theta^{-1}(t)\big)=s*t=\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
. Thus,
\big(\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(S)\big)_{[\alpha]}=\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. Define

\zeta:\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(S)\to\operatorname{Aut}^{x_{0}}_{S}(E)

by setting

\zeta(\phi,\theta)=\psi
, where
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),\theta(s)\big)
for
x\in X
and
s\in S
. Then
\zeta
is a group homomorphism with
\Phi\circ\zeta
being the identity map, and sequence (
5.0.13
) splits.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

We believe that an analogue of Theorem
5.4
should be provable for the general cohomology theory for quandles developed in
[
1
]
.

6.
Exact sequence relating automorphisms and second quandle cohomology

Specialising to the usual quandle cohomology with coefficients in an abelian group, after some appropriate modifications, the sequence (
5.0.11
) takes a simpler and elegant form, which we derive in this section. We briefly recall the cohomology theory for quandles as in
[
9
]
. Let
X
be a quandle and
A
an abelian group. Let
S_{n}(X)
be the free abelian group generated by
n
-tuples of elements of
X
. Define a homomorphism

\partial_{n}:S_{n}(X)\to S_{n-1}(X)
by

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{i}\big((x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle-(x_{1}\ast x_{i},x_{2}\ast x_{i},\dots,x_{i-1}\ast x_{i},x_{i+1},x_{i+2},\dots,x_{n})\big)

for
n\geq 2
and
\partial_{n}=0
for
n\leq 1
. Then
\{S_{n}(X),\partial_{n}\}
forms a chain complex. For
n\geq 2
, let
D_{n}(X)
be the free abelian subgroup of
S_{n}(X)
generated by
n
-tuples
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
with
x_{i}=x_{i+1}
for some
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Set
D_{n}(X)=0
for
n\leq 1
. It can be checked that if
X
is a quandle, then
\partial_{n}\big(D_{n}(X)\big)\subseteq D_{n-1}(X)
. Setting
C_{n}(X)=S_{n}(X)/D_{n}(X)
, we see that
\{C_{n}(X),\partial_{n}\}
forms a chain complex, where by abuse of notation,
\partial_{n}
also denotes the induced homomorphism.

Define
C^{n}(X;A):=\operatorname{Hom}\big(C_{n}(X),A\big)
and
\delta^{n}:C^{n}(X;A)\to C^{n+1}(X;A)
given by

\delta^{n}(f)=(-1)^{n}f\circ\partial_{n+1}.

This turns
\{C^{n}(X;A),\delta^{n}\}
into a cochain complex, and its
n
-th cohomology group
\operatorname{H}^{n}(X;A)
is called the
n
-th quandle cohomology group of
X
with coefficients in
A
. We denote the group of cocycles and the group of coboundaries by
\operatorname{Z}^{n}(X;A)
and
\operatorname{B}^{n}(X;A)
, respectively.

As in Remark
4.3
,
\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
can be identified with the set of maps
\alpha:X\times X\to A
satisfying the 2-cocycle condition (
4.0.3
) and (
4.0.4
), that is,

\alpha_{x,y}~\alpha_{x*y,z}=\alpha_{x,z}~\alpha_{x*z,y*z}

and

\alpha_{x,x}=1

for
x,y,z\in X
. Further, two 2-cocycles
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
are cohomologous if there exists a 1-cochain
\lambda\in C^{1}(X;A)
such that
\beta~\delta^{1}(\lambda)=\alpha
, that is,

\beta_{x,y}=\lambda_{x*y}\lambda_{x}^{-1}\alpha_{x,y}

for
x,y\in X
.

For
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
\alpha\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
, we define

(6.0.1)

^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}:=\theta\big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)

for
x,y\in X
.

Proposition 6.1
.

The group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
acts by automorphisms on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
as

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]:=[^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha]

for
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
.

Proof.

That (
6.0.1
) defines an action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
on
\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
is easy and follows along the lines of proof of Lemma
4.1
. Let
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
. Then, for
x,y\in X
, we have

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}(\alpha\beta)_{x,y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big({(\alpha\beta)}_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}~\beta_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)~\theta\big(\beta_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big),~\textrm{since}~\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}~^{(\phi,\theta)}\beta_{x,y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha~^{(\phi,\theta)}\beta)_{x,y},

and hence the action is by automorphisms. Let
\alpha,\beta\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
be two cohomologous quandle 2-cocycles. Then there exists a map
\lambda:X\to A
such that

\beta_{x,y}=\alpha_{x,y}~\lambda_{x}~\lambda_{x*y}^{-1}.

Setting
\lambda^{\prime}_{x}:=\theta(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)})
, we see that

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\beta_{x,y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big(\beta_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}~\theta\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}\big)\theta\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=

{}^{(\phi,\theta)}\alpha_{x,y}~\lambda^{\prime}_{x}~{\lambda^{\prime}_{x*y}}^{-1}.

Thus,
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
acts by automorphisms on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
.
∎

Applying the orbit-stabiliser theorem to the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
yields the following bound on the size of second quandle cohomology.

Corollary 6.2
.

If
X
is a finite quandle,
A
a finite abelian group and
\alpha:X\times X\to A
a quandle 2-cocycle, then

|\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)|\geq\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)|}{|\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}|},

where
\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}
is the stabiliser of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
at
[\alpha]
.

Let
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
be a fixed cohomology class. Note that the group
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
acts freely and transitively on itself by left multiplication. For any
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
, since
[\alpha]
and
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]
are two elements of
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
, there exists a unique element, say,
\Theta_{[\alpha]}(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
such that

(6.0.2)

^{\Theta_{[\alpha]}(\phi,\theta)}\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]\big)=[\alpha].

This defines a map

\Theta_{[\alpha]}:\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A).

For convenience of notation, we denote
\Theta_{[\alpha]}
by
\Theta
. By Remark
4.3
, the map
\alpha^{\prime}:X\times X\to\mathrm{Map}(A\times A,A)
given by
\alpha^{\prime}_{x,y}(s,t)=s~\alpha_{x,y}
, where
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in A
, is a dynamical 2-cocycle. Thus, by Proposition
3.1
, the binary operation

(6.0.3)

(x,s)*(y,t):=(x*y,~s~\alpha_{x,y})

defines a quandle
E:=X\times_{\alpha}A
called the
abelian extension
of
X
by
A
using
\alpha
. We would like to understand certain group of automorphisms of the quandle
E
in terms of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
,
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
. For this purpose, we define

\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)=\Big\{\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)~|~\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\lambda_{x}\theta(s)\big)~\textrm{for some}~(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)~\textrm{and map}~\lambda:X\to A\Big\}.

Proposition 6.3
.

\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
such that
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\lambda_{x}\theta(s)\big)
for
x\in X
and
s\in A
. Define
\varphi(x,s):=\big(\phi^{-1}(x),~\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}s\big)\big)
. Clearly,
\varphi
is a bijection since
\phi
and
\theta
are so. Since
\psi
is a quandle homomorphism, for
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in S
, we have

(6.0.4)

\lambda_{x*y}~\theta(\alpha_{x,y})=\lambda_{x}~\alpha_{\phi(x),\phi(y)},

which is equivalent to

(6.0.5)

\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}~\alpha_{x,y}\big)=\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}\big)~\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}.

Now, consider

\displaystyle\varphi\big((x,s)*(y,t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\big(x*y,~s~\alpha_{x,y}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x*y),~\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x*y)}^{-1}s~\alpha_{x,y}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x)*\phi^{-1}(y),~\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}s\big)~\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big),

due to

(
6.0.5
)

and the fact that
A
is abelian

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\phi^{-1}(x),~\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(x)}^{-1}s\big)\big)*\big(\phi^{-1}(y),~\theta^{-1}\big(\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(y)}^{-1}t\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(x,s)*\varphi(y,t).

Thus,
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
and a direct check shows that
\varphi
is the inverse of
\psi
. Further, given
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),~\lambda_{x}\theta(s)\big)
and
\psi^{\prime}(x,s)=\big(\phi^{\prime}(x),~\lambda^{\prime}_{x}\theta^{\prime}(s)\big)
, we have

(6.0.6)

\psi\psi^{\prime}(x,s)=\psi\big(\phi^{\prime}(x),~\lambda^{\prime}_{x}\theta^{\prime}(s)\big)=\big(\phi\big(\phi^{\prime}(x)\big),~\lambda_{\phi^{\prime}(x)}\theta\big(\lambda^{\prime}_{x}\theta^{\prime}(s)\big)\big)=\big(\phi\phi^{\prime}(x),~\lambda_{\phi^{\prime}(x)}~\theta(\lambda^{\prime}_{x})~\theta\theta^{\prime}(s)\big),

and hence
\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(E)
.
∎

In this case, we have a natural restriction map

\Psi:\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)\to\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)

given by
\Psi(\psi)=(\phi,\theta)
.

Proposition 6.4
.

The map
\Psi
is a group homomorphism and
{\rm Im}(\Psi)=\Theta^{-1}(1)
.

Proof.

It is clear from (
6.0.6
) that
\Psi
is a group homomorphism. First notice that

\Theta^{-1}(1)=\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}

and let
(\phi,\theta)\in\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}
. Then there exists a map
\lambda:X\to A
such that

(6.0.7)

\theta\big(\alpha_{\phi^{-1}(x),\phi^{-1}(y)}\big)=\alpha_{x,y}~\lambda_{x}~\lambda_{x*y}^{-1}.

for all
x,y\in X
. Define
\psi:E\to E
by setting
\psi(x,s)=\big(\phi(x),\lambda_{\phi(x)}\theta(s)\big)
. Clearly,
\psi
is bijective and is a quandle homomorphism due to (
6.0.7
). Further,
\Psi(\psi)=(\phi,\theta)
, and hence
\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}\subseteq{\rm Im}(\Psi)
.

Conversely, if
(\phi,\theta)\in{\rm Im}(\Psi)
, then there exists a map
\lambda:X\to A
such that (
6.0.7
) holds. But this is equivalent to
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]=[\alpha]
, which is desired.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)\cong\operatorname{Z}^{1}(X;A)=\{\lambda:X\to A~|~\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{x*y}~\textrm{for all}~x,y\in X\}
.

Proof.

Note that
\psi\in\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
if and only if
\Psi(\psi)=(\mathrm{id}_{X},\mathrm{id}_{S})
. This gives
\psi(x,s)=\big(x,\lambda_{x}~s\big)
for all
x\in X
and
s\in A
. Further, by (
6.0.4
),
\psi
is a quandle homomorphism if and only if
\lambda_{x}=\lambda_{x*y}
. Thus,
\operatorname{Ker}(\Psi)
is the desired group
\operatorname{Z}^{1}(X;A)
of 1-cocycles identified via the map
\psi\mapsto\lambda
.
∎

We are now in a position to state the main result of this section that gives an exact sequence relating quandle 1-cocycles, automorphisms and second cohomology of quandles for an abelian quandle extension.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
X
be a quandle,
A
an abelian group,
\alpha\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
and
E=X\times_{\alpha}A
the abelian extension of
X
by
A
using
\alpha
. Then there exists an exact sequence

(6.0.8)

1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Z}^{1}(X;A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Theta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A),

where exactness at
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
means that
{\rm Im}(\Psi)=\Theta^{-1}(1)
.

Theorem
6.6
can be reformulated as follows.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
X
be a quandle,
A
an abelian group,
\alpha\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(X;A)
and
E=X\times_{\alpha}A
the abelian extension of
X
by
A
using
\alpha
. Then there exists a short exact sequence

(6.0.9)

1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Z}^{1}(X;A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)_{[\alpha]}\longrightarrow 1.

The second quandle cohomology is an obstruction to extension of automorphisms in quandle extensions.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
X
be a quandle and
A
an abelian group such that
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)=1
. Then every automorphism in
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
extends to an automorphism in
\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
.

Restricting the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
to that of its subgroups
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
gives the following result.

Corollary 6.9
.

Every automorphism in
\operatorname{Aut}(X)_{[\alpha]}
and
\operatorname{Aut}(A)_{[\alpha]}
can be extended to an automorphism in
\operatorname{Aut}_{A}(E)
.

7.
Properties of the map
\Theta

In this section, we study the map
\Theta
in more detail. Let
X
be a quandle and
A
an abelian group. Since the group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
acts on the group
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
, we have their semi-direct product
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
. Further, note that the group
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
acts on itself by left multiplication.

Proposition 7.1
.

For
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
[\alpha],[\beta]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
setting

{}^{[\alpha](\phi,\theta)}[\beta]=~^{[\alpha]}{(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\beta])}

defines an action of
~\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
.

Proof.

For
(\phi_{1},\theta_{1}),(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
[\alpha_{1}],[\alpha_{2}],[\beta]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
, we compute

{}^{\big([\alpha_{1}](\phi_{1},\theta_{1})\big)\big([\alpha_{2}](\phi_{2},\theta_{2})\big)}[\beta]

\displaystyle=

{}^{\big([\alpha_{1}]~^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}[\alpha_{2}]\big)\big((\phi_{1},\theta_{1})(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})\big)}[\beta]

\displaystyle=

{}^{\big([\alpha_{1}]~^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}[\alpha_{2}]\big)}{\big(^{\big((\phi_{1},\theta_{1})(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})\big)}[\beta]\big)}

\displaystyle=

{}^{[\alpha_{1}]}{\big(^{{}^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}{[\alpha_{2}]}}{\big(^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}{\big(^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}[\beta]\big)}\big)}\big)}

\displaystyle=

{}^{[\alpha_{1}]}{\big({{}^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}{[\alpha_{2}]}}{\big(^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}{\big(^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}[\beta]\big)}\big)}\big)},

\displaystyle\textrm{since}~^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1}}{[\alpha_{2}]}\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A),~\textrm{which acts on itself by left translation}

\displaystyle=

{}^{[\alpha_{1}]}{\big({{}^{(\phi_{1},\theta_{1})}{\big([\alpha_{2}]\big(^{(\phi_{2},\theta_{2})}[\beta]\big)}}\big)\big)},

\displaystyle\textrm{since}~\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)~\textrm{acts by automorphisms on}~\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)

\displaystyle=

{}^{\big([\alpha_{1}](\phi_{1},\theta_{1})\big)}{\big(^{\big([\alpha_{2}](\phi_{2},\theta_{2})\big)}[\beta]\big)},

\displaystyle~\textrm{since}~\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)~\textrm{acts on itself by left translation}.

Thus,
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
acts on
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
.
∎

We recall two basic definitions from group cohomology that we need in the subsequent discussion
[
5
, Chapter 4]
. Let
A
be an abelian group and
G
a group acting on
A
from left by automorphisms. Then the first cohomology of
G
with coefficients in
A
is defined as
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G;A):=\operatorname{Z}^{1}(G;A)/\operatorname{B}^{1}(G;A)
, where

\operatorname{Z}^{1}(G;A)=\big\{f:G\to A~|~f(xy)=f(x)~{{}^{x}}f(y)~\textrm{for all}\ x,y\in G\big\}

is the group of
derivations
or
1-cocycles
and

\operatorname{B}^{1}(G;A)=\big\{f:G\to A~|~\textrm{there exists}~a\in A~\textrm{such that}~f(x)=({{}^{x}}a)a^{-1}~\textrm{for all}~x\in G\big\}

is the group of
inner derivations
or
1-coboundaries
.

Also recall that a complement of a subgroup
H
in a group
G
is another subgroup
K
of
G
such that
G=HK
and
H\cap K=1
. The following result relating derivations and complements is well-known
[
32
, 11.1.2]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
H
be an abelian group and
G
a group acting on
H
by automorphisms. Then the map
f\mapsto\{f(g)g~|~g\in G\}
gives a bijection from the set
\operatorname{Z}^{1}(G;H)
of derivations to the set
\{K~|~G=HK~\textrm{and}~H\cap K=1\}
of complements of
H
in
G
.

Theorem 7.3
.

The map
\Theta:\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
is a derivation. Further, if
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
correspond to two quandle 2-cocycles, then
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
differ by an inner derivation.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Theta=\Theta_{[\alpha]}
for
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
and
\mathbb{g}\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
. Then for elements
[\alpha],~^{\mathbb{g}}[\alpha]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
, there exists a unique
[\beta]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
such that
~{}^{[\beta]}[\alpha]=~^{\mathbb{g}}[\alpha]
. This shows that
[\beta]^{-1}\mathbb{g}\in\mathbb{H}
, the stabiliser subgroup of
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
at
[\alpha]
, and hence

\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)=\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\mathbb{H}.

Further, since
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
acts freely on itself, it follows that
\mathbb{H}
is the complement of
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
in the group
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
. By Lemma
7.2
, let
f:\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
be the unique derivation corresponding to the complement
\mathbb{H}
of
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
in
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)\rtimes\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big)
. Then

\mathbb{H}=\big\{f(\phi,\theta)(\phi,\theta)~|~(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)\big\},

that is,

[\alpha]=~^{f(\phi,\theta)(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]=~^{f(\phi,\theta)}{\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]\big)}.

Now, by definition of
\Theta
as in (
6.0.2
), we obtain
f(\phi,\theta)=\Theta(\phi,\theta)
, and hence
\Theta
is a derivation.

Let
\Theta=\Theta_{[\alpha]}
and
\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta^{\prime}_{[\alpha^{\prime}]}
, where
[\alpha],[\alpha^{\prime}]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
. Then for any
(\phi,\theta)\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)
, we have

{}^{\Theta(\phi,\theta)}\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha]\big)=[\alpha]~\textrm{and}~^{\Theta^{\prime}(\phi,\theta)}\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha^{\prime}]\big)=[\alpha^{\prime}].

Since
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
acts transitively on itself by left multiplication, there exists a unique
[\beta]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
such that
{}^{[\beta]}[\alpha^{\prime}]=[\alpha]
. This gives

{}^{[\beta]^{-1}}{\big(^{\Theta(\phi,\theta)}\big(^{{}^{(\phi,\theta)}{[\beta]}}{\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha^{\prime}]\big)}\big)\big)}=^{\Theta^{\prime}(\phi,\theta)}\big(^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha^{\prime}]\big).

Since
[\beta]^{-1}\Theta(\phi,\theta){{}^{(\phi,\theta)}{[\beta]}}
,
\Theta^{\prime}(\phi,\theta)
and
{}^{(\phi,\theta)}[\alpha^{\prime}]
all lie in
\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
, which acts freely on itself, we must have

[\beta]^{-1}\Theta(\phi,\theta){{}^{(\phi,\theta)}{[\beta]}}=\Theta^{\prime}(\phi,\theta).

Thus,
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
differ by an inner derivation, which completes the proof.
∎

8.
Adjoint functors between categories of quandles and groups

Let
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{G}
denote the category of quandles and groups, respectively. Let
w=w(x,y)
be a word in the free group
F(x,y)
on generators
x
and
y
. For any group
G
, the word
w
defines a map
G\times G\to G
given by
(g,h)\mapsto w(g,h)
for
g,h\in G
. Setting

g*h=w(g,h)

gives an algebraic system
(Q_{w}(G),*)
. If
w(x,y)=y^{-n}xy^{n}
for some integer
n
, then
Q_{w}(G)
is the
n
-th conjugation quandle
\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(G)
, and if
w(x,y)=yx^{-1}y
, then
Q_{w}(G)
is the core quandle
\operatorname{Core}(G)
. In fact, the following result
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
shows that these are all the possibilities.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
w=w(x,y)\in F(x,y)
be such that
Q_{w}(G)
is a quandle for every group
G
. Then
w(x,y)=yx^{-1}y
or
y^{-n}xy^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For a word
w=w(x,y)\in F(x,y)
and a quandle
X\in\mathcal{Q}
, we define the group

\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X)=\big\langle e_{x},~x\in X~\mid~e_{x*y}=w(e_{x},e_{y}),~~x,y\in X\big\rangle.

We note that if
w(x,y)=y^{-1}xy
, then
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X)
is simply denoted by
\operatorname{Adj}(X)
and referred as the
adjoint group
of the quandle
X
in the literature.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
w(x,y)=yx^{-1}y
or
y^{-n}xy^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{G}
is a functor that is left adjoint to the functor
Q_{w}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{Q}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
a quandle homomorphism. Then we obtain a group homomorphism

\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\phi):F(X)\to\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(Y)
given by
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\phi)(e_{x})=e_{\phi(x)}
, where
F(X)
is the free group on the set
\{e_{x}~|~x\in X\}
. Further, for
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\phi)(e_{x*y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\phi(x)*\phi(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(e_{\phi(x)},e_{\phi(y)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\phi)\big(w(e_{x},e_{y})\big),

and hence we obtain a group homomorphism
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\phi):\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X)\to\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(Y).

Conversely, let
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
and
f\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,H)
. Defining
Q_{w}(f):Q_{w}(G)\to Q_{w}(H)
by
Q_{w}(f)(a)=f(a)
, we see that

\displaystyle Q_{w}(f)(a*b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a*b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\big(w(a,b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\big(f(a),f(b)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a)*f(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{w}(f)(a)*Q_{w}(f)(b)

for all
a,b\in G
. Thus,
Q_{w}(f)\in\operatorname{Hom}\big(Q_{w}(G),Q_{w}(H)\big)
. A direct check shows that both
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}
and
Q_{w}
are functors.

Let
X\in\mathcal{Q}
,
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}\big(X,Q_{w}(G)\big)
a quandle homomorphism. Define
\widetilde{\phi}:F(X)\to G
by setting
\widetilde{\phi}(e_{x})=\phi(x)
. Then, for
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(e_{x*y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x*y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)*\phi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\big(\phi(x),\phi(y)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}\big(w(e_{x},e_{y})\big),

and hence
\widetilde{\phi}\in\operatorname{Hom}\big(\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X),G\big)
. Similarly, let
f\in\operatorname{Hom}\big(\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X),G\big)
be a group homomorphism. Define
\widehat{f}:X\to Q_{w}(G)
by setting
\widehat{f}(x)=f(e_{x})
. Then, for
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(x*y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(e_{x*y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\big(w(e_{x},e_{y})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\big(f(e_{x}),f(e_{y})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\big(\widehat{f}(x),\widehat{f}(y)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(x)*\widehat{f}(y)

and hence
\widehat{f}\in\operatorname{Hom}\big(X,Q_{w}(G)\big)
. It follows easily that
\widehat{\widetilde{\phi}}=\phi
and
\widetilde{\widehat{f}}=f
which proves that
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}
is left adjoint to
Q_{w}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
denote the category of pairs
(X,\phi)
, where
X\in\mathcal{Q}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. If
(X_{1},\phi_{1}),(X_{2},\phi_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
, then a morphism from
(X_{1},\phi_{1})
to
(X_{2},\phi_{2})
is a quandle homomorphism
\psi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
such that

\phi_{2}\psi=\psi\phi_{1}.

In case of groups, the category
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is defined analogously. Each object
(G,f)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
gives an object
\big(\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(G),\operatorname{Alex}(f)\big)
, where
\operatorname{Alex}(f)
acts as
f
. On the other hand, for
(X,\phi)\in\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
, we define the group

\operatorname{Adj}_{\phi}(X)=\big\langle e_{x},~x\in X~\mid~e_{x*y}=e_{\phi(x)}e_{\phi(y)}^{-1}e_{y},~~x,y\in X\big\rangle.

Further, the map
\phi
extends to an automorphism
\operatorname{Adj}(\phi)
of the free group
F(X)
. For
x,y\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}(\phi)(e_{x*y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\phi(x)*\phi(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\phi^{2}(x)}e_{\phi^{2}(y)}^{-1}e_{\phi(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Adj}(\phi)(e_{\phi(x)}e_{\phi(y)}^{-1}e_{y}),

and hence
\big(\operatorname{Adj}_{\phi}(X),\operatorname{Adj}(\phi)\big)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. It follows easily that both
\operatorname{Alex}
and
\operatorname{Adj}
are functors. Imitating the proof of Proposition
8.2
, we obtain

Proposition 8.3
.

The functor
\operatorname{Adj}:\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is left adjoint to the functor
\operatorname{Alex}:\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
.

9.
From group extensions to quandle extensions

We show that extensions of groups give rise to extensions of quandles by applying the functors described in Section
8
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
1\to A\to E\to G\to 1
be an extension of groups and
w(x,y)=yx^{-1}y
or
y^{-n}xy^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
Q_{w}(E)\cong Q_{w}(G)\times_{\alpha}Q_{w}(A)
for some dynamical 2-cocycle
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:E\to G
be the quotient homomorphism and
\kappa:G\to E
a transversal such that
\kappa(1)=1
. By functoriality,
we have a surjective quandle homomorphism
Q_{w}(\pi):Q_{w}(E)\to Q_{w}(G)
such that

\pi\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)=Q_{w}(\pi)\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)=x*y=Q_{w}(\pi)\big(\kappa(x*y)\big)=\pi\big(\kappa(x*y)\big)

for all
x,y\in G
. Thus, there exists a unique element, say,
\mu(x,y)\in A
such that

\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)=\kappa(x*y)~\mu(x,y).

Note that
\mu(x,x)=1
for all
x\in G
. Every element of
E
can be written uniquely as
\kappa(x)s
for some
x\in G
and
s\in A
. For each
x\in G
, there is a bijection
\gamma_{x}:Q_{w}(\pi)^{-1}(x)\to A
given by
\gamma_{x}\big(\kappa(x)s\big)=s
. Hence, by
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
, we obtain

Q_{w}(E)\cong Q_{w}(G)\times_{\alpha}A,

where
\alpha
is the dynamical 2-cocycle given by
\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\gamma_{x*y}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)*\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)
for
x,y\in G
and
s,t\in A
.

Case 1. If
w(x,y)=yx^{-1}y
, then

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)*\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\big(\kappa(x)s\big)*\big(\kappa(y)t\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\kappa(y)t~s^{-1}\kappa(x)^{-1}~\kappa(y)t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\kappa(y)\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)~^{\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)}(ts^{-1})~t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)~^{\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)}(ts^{-1})~t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\kappa(x*y)~\mu(x,y)~^{\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)}(ts^{-1})~t\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)~^{\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)}(ts^{-1})~t.

Recall that the quandle operation in
Q_{w}(G)\times_{\alpha}A
is given by
(x,s)*(y,t)=\big(x*y,\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)\big)
for
x,y\in G
and
s,t\in A
. For each fixed
x\in G
, we have

(x,s)*(x,t)=\big(x,\alpha_{x,x}(s,t)\big)=(x,ts^{-1}t)=(x,s*t),

which agrees with the quandle operation in
Q_{w}(A)
.

Case 2. If
w(x,y)=y^{-n}xy^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)*\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\big(\kappa(x)s\big)*\big(\kappa(y)t\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big((\kappa(y)t)^{n}(\kappa(x)s)(\kappa(y)t)^{-n}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(\big(\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)\kappa(y)^{-n}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+2}}t\big)\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}}{(st^{-1})}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n-1}}{t^{-1}}\big)\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{2}}{t^{-1}}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)}{t^{-1}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+2}}t\big)\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}}{(st^{-1})}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n-1}}{t^{-1}}\big)\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{2}}{t^{-1}}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)}{t^{-1}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)~\Big(\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-n+2}}t\big)\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}\kappa(x)^{-1}}t\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n}}{(st^{-1})}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)^{n-1}}{t^{-1}}\big)\cdots\big(^{\kappa(y)^{2}}{t^{-1}}\big)~\big(^{\kappa(y)}{t^{-1}}\big)\Big).

Taking
x=y=1
and using the facts that
\mu(1,1)=1=\kappa(1)
, we obtain

(1,s)*(1,t)=\big(1,\alpha_{1,1}(s,t)\big)=\big(1,t^{n}st^{-n}\big)=(1,s*t),

which agrees with the quandle operation in
Q_{w}(A)
. Thus,
Q_{w}(E)\cong Q_{w}(G)\times_{\alpha}Q_{w}(A)
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Proposition
9.1
is the following result which generalises
[
23
, Proposition 3.9]
.

Corollary 9.2
.

If
E=G\times A
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Core}(E)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Core}(G)\times\operatorname{Core}(A)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(E)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(G)\times\operatorname{Conj}_{n}(A).

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
1\to A\to E\to G\to 1
be an extension of groups and
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)
such that
f(A)=A
. If
f_{1}\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
and
f_{2}\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)
are the automorphisms induced by
f
, then
\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(E)\cong\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{1}}(G)\times_{\alpha}\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{2}}(A)
for some dynamical 2-cocycle
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:E\to G
,
\kappa:G\to E
and
\mu:G\times G\to A
be as in Proposition
9.1
, that is,
\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)=\kappa(x*y)~\mu(x,y)
for all
x,y\in G
. Note that
\operatorname{Alex}(\pi):\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(E)\to\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{1}}(G)
is a surjective quandle homomorphism. For each
x\in G
, define a bijection
\gamma_{x}:\operatorname{Alex}(\pi)^{-1}(x)\to A
by
\gamma_{x}\big(\kappa(x)s\big)=s
. Again, by
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
, we obtain

\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(E)\cong\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{1}}(G)\times_{\alpha}A,

where
\alpha
is the dynamical 2-cocycle given by
\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)=\gamma_{x*y}\big(\gamma_{x}^{-1}(s)*\gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\big)
for
x,y\in G
and
s,t\in A
. We check that

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,y}(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\big(\big(\kappa(x)s\big)*\big(\kappa(y)t\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(f\big(\kappa(x)st^{-1}\kappa(y)^{-1}\big)\big(\kappa(y)t\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(f\big(\kappa(x)\kappa(y)^{-1}\big)\kappa(y)~^{\kappa(y)^{-1}f(\kappa(y))}{f(st^{-1})}~t\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)~^{\kappa(y)^{-1}f(\kappa(y))}{f(st^{-1})}~t\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{x*y}\Big(\kappa(x*y)~\mu(x,y)~^{\kappa(y)^{-1}f(\kappa(y))}{f(st^{-1})}~t\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)~^{\kappa(y)^{-1}f(\kappa(y))}{f_{2}(st^{-1})}~t.

Taking
x=y=1
and using the facts that
\mu(1,1)=1=\kappa(1)
, we get

(1,s)*(1,t)=\big(1,\alpha_{1,1}(s,t)\big)=\big(1,f_{2}(st^{-1})t\big)=(1,s*t),

which is the desired quandle structure on
\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{2}}(A)
.
∎

Proposition

recovers the following special case of

Corollary 9.4
.

If
E=G\times A
and
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\times\operatorname{Aut}(A)

\operatorname{Alex}_{f}(E)\cong\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{1}}(G)\times\operatorname{Alex}_{f_{2}}(A).

In the reverse direction, it is interesting to know whether an extension of quandles
E=X\times_{\alpha}S
gives rise to an extension of adjoint groups. Let
\pi:E\to X
be the surjective quandle homomorphism given by
\pi(x,s)=s
. Then, by functoriality of adjoint groups, there is a surjective group homomorphism

\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\pi):\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(E)\to\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(X)

given by
\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\pi)(e_{(x,s)})=e_{x}
. Note that
\langle e_{(x,s)}^{-1}e_{(x,t)}~|~x\in X,~s,t\in S\rangle\leq\operatorname{Ker}\big(\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\pi)\big)
. Understanding the structure of
\operatorname{Ker}\big(\operatorname{Adj}_{w}(\pi)\big)
seems difficult in general.

Example 9.5
.

Let
\operatorname{R}_{4}=\{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
be the dihedral quandle of order four. If
X=\{0,1\}
is the two element trivial quandle, then there is a surjective quandle homomorphism
\pi:\operatorname{R}_{4}\to X
given by
\pi(a_{0})=\pi(a_{2})=0
and
\pi(a_{1})=\pi(a_{3})=1
. Then, by
[
1
, Corollary 2.5]
,

\operatorname{R}_{4}\cong X\times_{\alpha}S,

where
\alpha
is a dynamical 2-cocycle and
S
is a set of two elements. Since both
X
and
S
are two element trivial quandles,
\operatorname{Adj}(X)=\operatorname{Adj}(S)=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the free abelian group of rank two. We claim that
\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})
is not an extension of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

We write
\operatorname{R}_{4}
as a disjoint union of two fibers

\operatorname{R}_{4}=Y_{0}\sqcup Y_{1},~\mbox{where}~Y_{0}=\{a_{0},a_{2}\},~~~Y_{1}=\{a_{1},a_{3}\}.

In fact, the fibers
Y_{1},Y_{2}
are the connected components and trivial subquandles of
\operatorname{R}_{4}
. We see that
S_{a_{0}}=S_{a_{2}}
with

S_{a_{0}}(a_{1})=a_{3},~~S_{a_{0}}(a_{3})=a_{1}.

Similarly,
S_{a_{1}}=S_{a_{3}}
with

S_{a_{1}}(a_{0})=a_{2},~~S_{a_{1}}(a_{2})=a_{0}.

Since
Y_{0},Y_{1}
and
X
are trivial quandles, we have

\operatorname{Adj}(Y_{0})=\big\langle e_{0},e_{2}~|~e_{0}e_{2}=e_{2}e_{0}\big\rangle,~~\operatorname{Adj}(Y_{1})=\big\langle e_{1},e_{3}~|~e_{1}e_{3}=e_{3}e_{3}\big\rangle

and

\operatorname{Adj}(X)=\big\langle f_{0},f_{1}~|~f_{0}f_{1}=f_{1}f_{0}\big\rangle.

We see that the group
\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})
is generated by its subgroups
\operatorname{Adj}(Y_{0}),\operatorname{Adj}(Y_{1})
and has relations

e_{1}^{-1}e_{0}e_{1}=e_{2},~~~e_{1}^{-1}e_{2}e_{1}=e_{0},~~~e_{0}^{-1}e_{1}e_{0}=e_{3},~~~e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}e_{2}=e_{3},~~~e_{3}^{-1}e_{0}e_{3}=e_{2},~~~e_{3}^{-1}e_{2}e_{3}=e_{0}.

The elements
b_{0}:=e_{0}e_{2}^{-1}
and
b_{1}:=e_{1}e_{3}^{-1}
lie in the kernel of the homomorphism

\operatorname{Adj}(\pi):\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})\to\operatorname{Adj}(X).

We remove the generators
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
using the equalities
e_{2}=e_{0}b_{0}^{-1}
and
e_{3}=e_{1}b_{1}^{-1}
. Then the other relations have the form

b_{0}=e_{1}^{-1}e_{0}^{-1}e_{1}e_{0},~~b_{1}=e_{0}^{-1}e_{1}^{-1}e_{0}e_{1},~~b_{1}=b_{0}^{-1},~~b_{0}^{2}=1,~~e_{0}^{-1}b_{0}e_{0}=b_{0},~~e_{1}^{-1}b_{1}e_{1}=b_{1}.

Thus,
\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})
has a presentation

\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})=\big\langle e_{0},e_{1},b_{0}~|~b_{0}^{2}=1,~~e_{1}e_{0}=e_{0}e_{1}b_{0},~~e_{1}^{-1}e_{0}=e_{0}e_{1}^{-1}b_{0},~~[e_{0},b_{0}]=[e_{1},b_{0}]=1\big\rangle

and there is an exact sequence

1\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to\operatorname{Adj}(\operatorname{R}_{4})\to\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to 1,

where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle b_{0}\rangle
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\langle f_{0},f_{1}\rangle
.

10.
Homomorphism from group cohomology to quandle cohomology

We briefly recall the idea of a quandle module due to
[
1
,
24
]
. A
trunk

\operatorname{T}(X)
of a quandle
X
is an object analogous to a category which has one object for each
x\in X
and for each ordered pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
there are morphisms
\textbf{a}_{x,y}:x\to x*y
and
\textbf{b}_{y,x}:y\to x*y
such that the diagrams

\textstyle{x\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{x,y}}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{x,z}}
\textstyle{x*y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{x*y,z}}
\textstyle{x*z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{x*z,y*z}\hskip 56.9055pt}
\textstyle{(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)}

and

\textstyle{y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{b}_{y,x}}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{y,z}}
\textstyle{x*y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{a}_{x*y,z}}
\textstyle{y*z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\textbf{b}_{y*z,x*z}\hskip 56.9055pt}
\textstyle{(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)}

commute for all
x,y,z\in X
. If
\mathcal{A}
is the category of abelian groups, then a
trunk map
is a functor
\operatorname{T}(X)\to\mathcal{A}
. Thus, a trunk map assigns an abelian groups
A_{x}
to each
x\in X
and group homomorphisms

\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}:A_{x}\to A_{x*y}
and
\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y,x}:A_{y}\to A_{x*y}

such that

(10.0.1)

\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*y,z}~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}=\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*z,y*z}~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,z}

and

(10.0.2)

\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*y,z}~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y,x}=\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y*z,x*z}~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{y,z}

for all
x,y,z\in X
. A
quandle module
over
X
is a trunk map

\operatorname{T}(X)\to\mathcal{A}
such that each

\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}
is an isomorphism and the identities

(10.0.3)

\bar{\textbf{b}}_{z,x*y}(s)=\big(\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*z,y*z}\bar{\textbf{b}}_{z,x}(s)\big)~\big(\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y*z,x*z}\bar{\textbf{b}}_{z,y}(s)\big)

and

(10.0.4)

\bar{\textbf{a}}_{z,z}(s)~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{z,z}(s)=s

hold for all
x,y,z\in X
and
s\in A_{z}
. A quandle module over
X
is called
homogeneous
if
A_{x}=A
for all
x\in X
. A homogeneous quandle module is called
trivial
if
\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}
is the identity map and
\bar{\textbf{b}}_{x,y}
is the trivial map for all
x,y\in X
.

Example 10.1
.

Any abelian group
A
can be turned into a homogeneous quandle module over a quandle
X
by taking
\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}(s)=s^{-1}
and
\bar{\textbf{b}}_{x,y}(s)=s^{2}
for all
x,y\in X
and
s\in A
.

From now onwards, we would consider only homogeneous quandle modules over a quandle. If
A
is a homogeneous quandle module over
X
, then a
factor set
is a map
\mu:X\times X\to A
satisfying

(10.0.5)

\mu(x*y,z)~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*y,z}\big(\mu(x,y)\big)=\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x*z,y*z}\big(\mu(x,z)\big)~\mu(x*z,y*z)~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y*z,x*z}\big(\mu(y,z)\big)

and

(10.0.6)

\mu(x,x)=1

for all
x,y,z\in X
. Identities (
10.0.1
)-(
10.0.4
) imply that each factor set
\mu
defines a quandle extension
E=X\times_{\alpha}A
of
X
by
A
by

(10.0.7)

(x,s)*(y,t)=\big(x*y,~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}(s)~\mu(x,y)~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y,x}(t)\big)

for all
x,y\in X
and
s,t\in A
. Conversely, by
[
24
, Proposition 3.1]
, every extension of a quandle
X
by a homogeneous quandle module
A
over
X
is determined by a factor set. Further, two factor sets
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}:X\times X\to A
are
cohomologous
if there exists a map
\lambda:X\to A
such that

(10.0.8)

\mu_{2}(x,y)=\mu_{1}(x,y)~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}\big(\lambda(x)\big)~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y,x}\big(\lambda(y)\big)~\lambda(x*y)^{-1}

for all
x,y\in X
. By
[
24
, Theorem 3.6]
, two factor sets are cohomologous if and only if the corresponding extensions are equivalent. Thus, the set
\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;A)
of cohomology classes of factor sets, which forms an abelian group with point-wise multiplication, is in bijective correspondence with the set of equivalence classes of quandle extensions of
X
by
A
. When
A
is a trivial homogeneous quandle module over
X
, then
\mathcal{H}^{2}(X;A)=\operatorname{H}^{2}(X;A)
and the quandle operation (
10.0.7
) agrees with (
6.0.3
).

Next, we recall a bit of extension theory of groups that we need for our last two results. Let
A
be an abelian group and
G
a group acting on
A
from left by automorphisms. The second cohomology of
G
with coefficients in
A
is defined as
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A):=\operatorname{Z}^{2}(G;A)/\operatorname{B}^{2}(G;A)
, where

\operatorname{Z}^{2}(G;A)=\big\{\nu:G\times G\to A~|~^{x}\nu(y,z)\nu(x,yz)=\nu(xy,z)\nu(x,y)~\textrm{for all}\ x,y,z\in G\big\}

is the group of
2-cocycles
and

\operatorname{B}^{2}(G;A)=\big\{\nu:G\times G\to A~|~\textrm{there exists}~\lambda:G\to A~\textrm{with}~\nu(x,y)=~^{x}\lambda(y)\lambda(xy)^{-1}\lambda(x)~\textrm{for all}~x,y\in G\big\}

is the group of
2-coboundaries
.

It is well-known that
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)
classifies extensions of groups
1\to A\to E\to G\to 1
that give rise to the given action of
G
on
A
. In particular,
E
is abelian if and only if any group 2-cocycle
\nu
classifying the extension is symmetric, that is,

\nu(x,y)=\nu(y,x)

for all
x,y\in G
. Since any group 2-cocycle cohomologous to a symmetric group 2-cocycle is itself symmetric, we can consider the set
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)_{sym}
of all symmetric group cohomology classes of degree 2. In fact, it turns out that
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)_{sym}
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)
.

Theorem 10.2
.

Let
G
and
A
be abelian groups with
A
viewed as a trivial
G
-module. Then there is a natural group homomorphism
\Lambda:\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)_{sym}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G);A\big)
given by
\Lambda\big([\nu]\big)=[\check{\nu}]
.

Proof.

Let
1\to A\to E\to G\to 1
be an extension of abelian groups and
\kappa:G\to E
a transversal such that
\kappa(1)=1
. Let
\nu:G\times G\to A
be a symmetric group 2-cocycle corresponding to the transversal
\kappa
, that is,

(10.0.9)

\kappa(x)\kappa(y)=\kappa(xy)~\nu(x,y)

for all
x,y\in G
. Define
\check{\nu}:G\times G\to A
by

\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)=\kappa(x*y)~\check{\nu}(x,y)

for all
x,y\in G
. Then, we have

(10.0.10)

\displaystyle\check{\nu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\kappa(x)*\kappa(y)\big)\kappa(x*y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa(y)\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(y)\kappa(yx^{-1}y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa(y)\kappa(x)^{-1}\kappa(yx^{-1})^{-1}~\nu(yx^{-1},y),~\textrm{using \eqref{tau-nu} and the fact that $E$ is abelian}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(yx^{-1},x)^{-1}~\nu(yx^{-1},y).

As in Example
10.1
, we view
A
as a homogeneous quandle module over
\operatorname{Core}(G)
by taking
\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}(s)=s^{-1}
and
\bar{\textbf{b}}_{x,y}(s)=s^{2}
for all
x,y\in\operatorname{Core}(G)
and
s\in A
. By Proposition
9.1
,
\operatorname{Core}(E)
is a quandle extension of
\operatorname{Core}(G)
by the homogeneous quandle module
A
. Further,
\check{\nu}
is the factor set of the extension
\operatorname{Core}(E)
relative to the transversal
\kappa
in the sense of (
10.0.5
).

We claim that if
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}:G\times G\to A
are two cohomologous group 2-cocycles, then the corresponding factor sets
\check{\nu_{1}},\check{\nu_{2}}
are cohomologous in the sense of (
10.0.8
). Let
\lambda:G\to A
be a map such that
\nu_{2}(x,y)=\nu_{1}(x,y)\lambda(x)\lambda(xy)^{-1}\lambda(y)
for all
x,y\in G
. Then, using (
10.0.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\check{\nu_{2}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(yx^{-1},x)^{-1}\nu_{2}(yx^{-1},y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nu_{1}(yx^{-1},x)^{-1}~\nu_{1}(yx^{-1},y)\big)\lambda(y)^{2}\lambda(x)^{-1}\lambda(yx^{-1}y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\check{\nu_{1}}(x,y)\lambda(y)^{2}\lambda(x)^{-1}\lambda(x*y)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\check{\nu_{1}}(x,y)~\bar{\textbf{b}}_{y,x}\big(\lambda(y)\big)~\bar{\textbf{a}}_{x,y}\big(\lambda(x)\big)~\lambda(x*y)^{-1}

for all
x,y\in G
. Thus, by (
10.0.8
),
\check{\nu_{1}}
and
\check{\nu_{2}}
are cohomologous and determine equivalent extensions. This defines a map

\Lambda:\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)_{sym}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G);A\big)

given by
\Lambda\big([\nu]\big)=[\check{\nu}]
. If
[\nu_{1}],[\nu_{2}]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)_{sym}
, then

\displaystyle\Lambda\big([\nu_{1}][\nu_{2}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda\big([\nu_{1}\nu_{2}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\check{(\nu_{1}\nu_{2})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\check{\nu_{1}}\check{\nu_{2}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\check{\nu_{1}}][\check{\nu_{2}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda\big([\nu_{1}]\big)\Lambda\big([\nu_{2}]\big),

and hence
\Lambda
is a group homomorphism.

For naturality of
\Lambda
, let
G_{1},G_{2},A_{1},A_{2}
be abelian groups and
f:G_{2}\to G_{1}
,
h:A_{1}\to A_{2}
be group homomorphisms. Then for each group 2-cocycle
\nu:G_{1}\times G_{1}\to A_{1}
, we define

\nu^{\prime}(x,y)=h\big(\nu\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\big)

for all
x,y\in G_{2}
. A routine check shows that
\nu\mapsto\nu^{\prime}
induces a group homomorphism
\operatorname{H}^{2}(G_{1};A_{1})_{sym}\to\operatorname{H}^{2}(G_{2};A_{2})_{sym}
given by
[\nu]\mapsto[\nu^{\prime}]
. Similarly, for each factor set
\mu:\operatorname{Core}(G_{1})\times\operatorname{Core}(G_{1})\to A_{1}
, define

\mu^{\prime}(x,y)=h\big(\mu\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\big)

for all
x,y\in\operatorname{Core}(G_{2})
. A direct check shows that
\mu^{\prime}
satisfies the factor set conditions (
10.0.5
) and (
10.0.6
). Since cohomologous factor sets are mapped to cohomologous factor sets, we obtain a group homomorphism
\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G_{1});A_{1}\big)\to\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G_{2});A_{2}\big)
given by
[\mu]\mapsto[\mu^{\prime}]
. Finally, it follows from the construction of the preceding maps that the diagram

\textstyle{\operatorname{H}^{2}(G_{1};A_{1})_{sym}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[\nu]\mapsto[\nu^{\prime}]}
\scriptstyle{\Lambda}
\textstyle{\operatorname{H}^{2}(G_{2};A_{2})_{sym}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Lambda}
\textstyle{\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G_{1});A_{1}\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[\mu]\mapsto[\mu^{\prime}]}
\textstyle{\mathcal{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Core}(G_{2});A_{2}\big)}

commutes, and the map
\Lambda
is natural.
∎

Let
G
be a group and
A
a trivial
G
-module. For a group 2-cocycle
\nu\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}(G;A)
, define
\breve{\nu}:G\times G\to A
by

(10.0.11)

\breve{\nu}(x,y)=\nu(x,y)~\nu(y,y^{-1}xy)^{-1}

for
x,y\in G
. It is shown in
[
9
, Theorem 7.1]
that
\breve{\nu}\in\operatorname{Z}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Conj}(G);A\big)
. In fact, the following analogue of Theorem
10.2
holds.

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
A
an abelian group viewed as a trivial
G
-module. Then there is a natural group homomorphism
\Gamma:\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Conj}(G),A\big)
given by
\Gamma\big([\nu]\big)=[\breve{\nu}]
.

Proof.

Let
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
be two cohomologous group 2-cocycles. Then there exists a map
\lambda:G\to A
such that
\nu_{2}(x,y)=\nu_{1}(x,y)\lambda(x)\lambda(xy)^{-1}\lambda(y)
for all
x,y\in G
. Using (
10.0.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\breve{\nu_{2}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(x,y)~\nu_{2}(y,y^{-1}xy)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\nu_{1}(x,y)~\nu_{1}(y,y^{-1}xy)^{-1}\big)\lambda(x)\lambda(y^{-1}xy)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\breve{\nu_{1}}(x,y)\lambda(x)\lambda(x*y)^{-1}.

for all
x,y\in G
. Thus,
\breve{\nu_{1}}
and
\breve{\nu_{2}}
are cohomologous quandle 2-cocycles. This defines a map

\Gamma:\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)\to\operatorname{H}^{2}\big(\operatorname{Conj}(G),A\big)

given by
\Gamma\big([\nu]\big)=[\breve{\nu}]
. If
[\nu_{1}],[\nu_{2}]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}(G;A)
, then

\displaystyle\Gamma\big([\nu_{1}][\nu_{2}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\big([\nu_{1}\nu_{2}]\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\breve{(\nu_{1}\nu_{2})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\breve{\nu_{1}}\breve{\nu_{2}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\breve{\nu_{1}}][\breve{\nu_{2}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma\big([\nu_{1}]\big)\Gamma\big([\nu_{2}]\big),

and
\Gamma
is a group homomorphism. Naturality of
\Gamma
follows similar to that of
\Lambda
in Theorem
10.2
.
∎

We conclude with the following natural problem.

Problem 10.4
.

Determine the kernel and the image of the homomorphisms
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
.

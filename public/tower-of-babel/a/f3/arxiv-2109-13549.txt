Serre-invariant stability conditions and Ulrich bundles on cubic threefolds
Soheyla Feyzbakhsh, Laura Pertusi

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove a general criterion which ensures that a fractional Calabi–Yau category of dimension at most
2
admits a unique Serre-invariant stability condition, up to the action of the universal cover of
\text{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
. We apply this result to the Kuznetsov component
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
of a cubic threefold
X
. In particular, we show that all the known stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
are invariant with respect to the action of the Serre functor and thus lie in the same orbit with respect to the action of the universal cover of
\text{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
. As an application, we show that the moduli space of Ulrich bundles of rank at least
2
on
X
is irreducible, answering a question asked by Lahoz, Macrì and Stellari in
[
LMS15
]
.

Keywords.
Cubic threefolds, stability conditions, Serre functor, Ulrich bundles

1. Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface in the complex projective space
\mathbb P^{4}
. As first noted by Kuznetsov
[
Kuz04
]
, the birational geometry of
X
is controlled by a certain full admissible subcategory
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
of the bounded derived category
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X),
defined by the semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)=\langle\operatorname{Ku}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)\rangle.

We call
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
the Kuznetsov component of
X
. It has been shown that
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
completely determines the isomorphism class of the cubic threefold
X
; see
[
BMM+12
,
BBF+20
]
.
The proof involves the theory of stability conditions for complexes in the derived category as introduced by Bridgeland
[
Bri07
]
, and the study of moduli spaces of stable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
.

Up to now there are two different constructions of stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
: one in
[
BMM+12
]
(see Section
5.2
) and one in
[
BLM+17
]
(see Section
4.2
).
One of the goals of this paper originates from the desire to understand the connection between these two families of stability conditions.

1.1. Main results

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
\mathbb C
-linear triangulated category with Serre functor
S
. Recall that the space of stability conditions on
\mathcal{T}
carries a right action of
\widetilde{\text{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
, which is the universal cover of
\text{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
;
see Section
2.1
. The first result of this paper is a general criterion which implies the existence of a unique orbit of stability conditions which are invariant with respect to the action of the Serre functor (Definition
3.1
).

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be Serre-invariant stability conditions on a linear triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
satisfying the conditions (
C1
), (
C2
) and (
C3
) in Section
3
. Then there exists a
\tilde{g}\in\widetilde{\emph{GL}}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\cdot\tilde{g}
.

Theorem
1.1
applies for instance to the Kuznetsov component of a cubic threefold
X

to show that the known stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
are identified in the stability manifold up to the action of
\widetilde{\text{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
, answering a question asked by Macrì and Stellari (see
[
PY20
, Remark 4.9]
).

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
5.7
)
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold. The stability condition
\overline{\sigma}
on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
defined in
[
LMS15
]
is in the same orbit as the stability conditions
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
introduced in
[
BLM+17
]
with respect to the
\widetilde{\emph{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
-action.

Our second result provides a detailed description of the moduli spaces of stable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
with minimal dimension with respect to any Serre-invariant stability condition. More precisely, let
M_{X}(v)
and
M_{X}(w)
be the moduli of slope-stable sheaves on
X
with Chern characters

v=\left(1,0,-\frac{1}{3}H^{2},0\right)\quad\text{and}\quad w=\left(2,-H,-\frac{1}{6}H^{2},\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\right).

Note that
v
is the numerical class of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
of a line
\ell
in
X
and in fact
M_{X}(v)
is isomorphic to the Fano variety of lines on
X
. We also denote by
M_{X}(v-w)
the moduli of large volume stable complexes on
X
of class
v-w
; see Definition
4.10
.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
4.5
)
.

We have the isomorphisms

\textstyle{M_{X}(v)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}(H))}
\textstyle{M_{X}(w)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}(H))}
\textstyle{M_{X}(v-w).}

Moreover, the above three moduli spaces are isomorphic to the moduli of
\sigma
-stable objects of class
[\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]
in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, where
\sigma
is any Serre-invariant stability condition on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
.

Finally, we apply Theorem
1.2
to study the moduli space of Ulrich bundles of rank at least
2
on
X
. Recall that an Ulrich bundle
E
on
X
is an arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay vector bundle such that the graded module
\oplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}H^{0}(X,E(mH))
has
3\operatorname{rank}(E)
generators in degree
1
(see Section
6
). Ulrich bundles all lie in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
.
Thus the moduli spaces of stable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
become a useful tool to deduce properties of certain classical moduli spaces of semistable sheaves on
X
, such as non-emptyness and irreducibility. Applying these techniques, Lahoz, Macrì and Stellari
[
LMS15
, Theorem B]
show that the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}^{sU}_{d}
of stable Ulrich bundles of rank
d
on
X
is non-empty and smooth of the expected dimension
d^{2}+1
. They leave as an open question its irreducibility, which is our last result.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold. The moduli space of Ulrich bundles of rank at least
2
on
X
is irreducible.

1.2. Related works and motivation

The interest in the study of Serre-invariant stability conditions on semiorthogonal components in the bounded derived category has grown recently, mostly due to applications to the study of moduli spaces (see for instance
[
PY20
,
LZ22
]
) and to the desire to better understand the Kuznetsov component. For Fano threefolds of Picard rank
1
and index
2
, the existence of Serre-invariant stability conditions on their Kuznetsov component is proved in
[
PY20
]
, making use of the stability conditions constructed in
[
BLM+17
]
. In the upcoming paper
[
PR22
]
, the same result is proved for the Kuznetsov component of a Gushel–Mukai threefold. On the other hand, in the recent paper
[
KP21
]
, the authors show the non-existence of Serre-invariant stability conditions on Kuznetsov components of almost all Fano complete intersections of codimension at least
2
.

The assumptions in Theorem
1.1
require the category to be fractional Calabi–Yau with numerical Grothendieck group of rank
2
generated by objects
E
with small
\hom^{1}(E,E)
. The first condition allows one to control the phase of stable objects after the action of the Serre functor, and the other conditions make the category similar to the bounded derived category of a curve. Since in the case of curves of genus at least
2
there is a unique orbit of stability conditions by Macrì’s result
[
Mac07
]
, these are very natural conditions for one to expect the category to have a unique Serre-invariant stability condition.

Theorem
1.1
applies to cubic threefolds and to other Fano threefolds (see Remark
3.8
). Note that in these cases the uniqueness result has been recently proved independently by
[
JLL+21
]
. We explain in Section
3
a related application in the case of very general cubic fourfolds.

In Proposition
5.3
we apply the method in
[
BLM+17
]
to construct stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
via the embedding of
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
in
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
; see
[
LMS15
]
. These stability conditions are Serre invariant, as shown in Section
5.3
.
Since the stability conditions constructed in
[
BLM+17
]
are also Serre invariant by
[
PY20
, Corollary 5.5]
, we deduce that all the stability conditions constructed on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
up to now are Serre invariant. Theorem
1.2
then follows from Theorem
1.1
. An interesting question would be to understand whether the property of Serre invariance characterises the stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. This fact together with Theorem
1.1
would allow one to show that there is a unique orbit of stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, in analogy to the case of curves of genus at least
2
; see
[
Mac07
]
.

Another open question is to generalise Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
6.5
to further study moduli spaces of semistable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
with respect to a Serre-invariant stability condition like their projectivity or irreducibility. Some cases of small dimension have been studied in
[
PY20
,
APR22
,
BBF+20
,
Qin21
,
LZ22
]
.

The study of Ulrich bundles is a central theme in classical algebraic geometry and commutative algebra (see for instance
[
ES03
]
and
[
Bea18
]
for a survey). The existence of Ulrich bundles have been shown on cubic threefolds by
[
LMS15
, Theorem B]
.
In the rank
2
case, namely the case of instanton sheaves of minimal charge, we know a full description of the moduli space with class
2[\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]
for cubic threefolds, see
[
LMS15
]
, and more generally for Fano threefolds of Picard rank
1
, index
2
and degree
d\geq 3
; see
[
Qin21
,
LZ22
]
. For the case of cubic fourfolds, we refer to
[
FK20
]
.

Theorem
1.4
follows from an embedding of the moduli space of Ulrich bundles of rank
d\geq 2
on
X
in a moduli space of semistable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
with class
d[\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]
, where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is the ideal sheaf of a line in
X
. By Theorem
1.2
, the latter moduli space can be described via an irreducible moduli space parametrising Gieseker-semistable sheaves which are
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-modules; it has the same dimension as

\mathfrak{M}^{sU}_{d}
.

1.3. Plan of the paper

In Section
2
we recall the definitions and basic properties of (weak) stability conditions, tilt stability, wall-crossing and a method to construct stability conditions on Kuznetsov components. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
we review the construction of the stability conditions
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
from
[
BLM+17
]
, and then we prove Theorem
1.3
. In Section
5
we extend the construction of stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
from
[
BMM+12
,
LMS15
]
, and we show they are Serre invariant. Finally, we deduce Theorem
1.2
. In Section
6
we prove Theorem
1.4
.

2. (Weak) Bridgeland stability conditions and tilting

In this section we recall the definitions and properties of (weak) Bridgeland stability conditions and wall-crossing. Our main reference is
[
BMS16
]
. We also mention the stronger Bogomolov inequalities proved in
[
Li19
]
in the case of cubic threefolds. Finally, we explain
the method to construct stability conditions on the orthogonal complement of an exceptional collection introduced in
[
BLM+17
]
.

2.1. Review on (weak) stability conditions

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
\mathbb C
-linear triangulated category, and denote by
K(\mathcal{T})
its Grothendieck group. A (weak) stability condition on
\mathcal{T}
is defined by giving the heart of a bounded t-structure and a (weak) stability function on it. Fix a finite-rank lattice
\Lambda
with a surjective morphism
v\colon K(\mathcal{T})\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda
. We denote by
\Re[-]
(resp.
\Im[-]
) the real (resp. imaginary) part of a complex number.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the heart of a bounded t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
. A group homomorphism
Z\colon\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb C
is a

(
weak
)
stability function
on
\mathcal{A}
if for any
0\neq E\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\Im Z(v(E))\geq 0
, and in the case that
\Im Z(v(E))=0
, we have
\Re Z(v(E))<(\leq)\,0
.

By abuse of notation, we will write
Z(E)
for an object
E\in\mathcal{A}
instead of
Z(v(E))
. The slope of
E\in\mathcal{A}
with respect to
Z
is defined by

\mu_{Z}(E)=\begin{cases}-\frac{\Re Z(E)}{\Im Z(E)}&\text{if }\Im Z(E)>0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

An object
F\in\mathcal{T}
is called
(
semi
\,)
stable
with respect to the pair
(\mathcal{A},Z)
if some shift
F[k]
lies in the heart
\mathcal{A}
and for every proper subobject
F^{\prime}\subset F[k]
in
\mathcal{A}
we have

\mu_{Z}(F^{\prime})<(\leq)\ \mu_{Z}(F[k]/F^{\prime}).

Definition 2.2
.

A
(
weak
)
stability condition
(with respect to
\Lambda
) on
\mathcal{T}
is a pair
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the heart of a bounded t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
and
Z
is a (weak) stability function satisfying the following properties:

(a)

HN property
: Every object of
\mathcal{A}
has a Harder–Narasimhan (HN) filtration with
\sigma
-semistable factors.

(b)

Support property
: There exists a quadratic form
Q
on
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb R
such that the restriction of
Q
to
\ker Z
is negative definite and
Q(E)\geq 0
for all
\sigma
-semistable objects
E
in
\mathcal{A}
.

We denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
the space of stability conditions on
\mathcal{T}
with respect to
\Lambda
. This space is actually a complex manifold; see
[
Bri07
]
.

If
\Lambda
is the numerical Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{T}
, we call
\sigma
a
numerical
(weak) stability condition. Any (weak) stability condition defines a slicing on
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
phase
of a
\sigma
-semistable object
E\in\mathcal{A}
is

\phi(E)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\text{arg}(Z(E))\in(0,1].

If
Z(E)=0
, then
\phi(E)=1
. For
F=E[n]
, we set

\phi(E[n]):=\phi(E)+n.

The
slicing

\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
of
\mathcal{T}
corresponding to
\sigma
is a collection of full additive subcategories
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)\subset\mathcal{T}
for
\phi\in\mathbb R
, such that:

(i)

for
\phi\in(0,1]
, the subcategory
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)
is given by the zero object and all
\sigma
-semistable objects with phase
\phi
;

(ii)

for
\phi+n
with
\phi\in(0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb Z
, we set
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi+n)\coloneqq\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)[n]
.

The HN property of (weak) stability condition
\sigma
implies that for
0\neq E\in\mathcal{T}
, there exists a unique finite sequence of real numbers

\phi^{+}(E)\coloneqq\phi_{1}>\phi_{2}>\cdots>\phi_{n}\eqqcolon\phi^{-}(E)

and a unique sequence of objects in
\mathcal{T}

0=E_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}E_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}E_{2}\xrightarrow{f_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}E_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}E_{n}=E

with cone
(f_{i})\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{i})
.

It is clear from the definition that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(0,1]
, where the latter is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of the zero object together with those objects
0\neq E\in\mathcal{T}
which satisfy
0<\phi^{-}(E)\leq\phi^{+}(E)\leq 1
.

On the stability manifold
\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
, we have:

(a)

a right action of the universal covering space
\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
of
\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
: for a stability condition
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
and
\tilde{g}=(g,M)\in\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
, where
g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R
is an increasing function such that
g(\phi+1)=g(\phi)+1
and
M\in\mathrm{GL}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
, we define
\sigma\cdot\tilde{g}
to be the stability condition
\sigma^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
with
Z^{\prime}=M^{-1}\circ Z
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(\phi)=\mathcal{P}(g(\phi))
(see
[
Bri07
, Lemma 8.2]
);

(b)

a left action of the group of linear exact autoequivalences
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
, whose induced action on
K(\mathcal{T})
is compatible with
v
: for
\Phi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
and
\sigma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Stab}}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{T})
, we define
\Phi\cdot\sigma=(\Phi(\mathcal{P}),Z\circ\Phi_{*}^{-1})
, where
\Phi_{*}
is the automorphism of
K(\mathcal{T})
induced by
\Phi
.

2.2. Tilt stability

An important tool in the construction of weak stability conditions is the procedure of tilting a heart. Let
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
be a weak stability condition on
\mathcal{T}
and
\mu\in\mathbb R
. We consider
the following subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}^{\mu}\coloneqq\{E\in\mathcal{A}|\text{ every Harder--Narasimhan factor $F$ of $E$ has $\mu_{Z}(F)>\mu$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\mu}\coloneqq\{E\in\mathcal{A}|\text{ every Harder--Narasimhan factor $F$ of $E$ has $\mu_{Z}(F)\leq\mu$}\}.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
HRS96
]
)
.

The category

\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{\mu}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}^{\mu},\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}^{\mu}[1]\rangle_{\text{\rm extension closure}}

is the heart of a bounded t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
.

We say that the heart
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}_{\sigma}
is obtained by tilting
\mathcal{A}
with respect to the weak stability condition
\sigma
at slope
\mu
.

The above construction applies to the case of the bounded derived category
\mathcal{T}=\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
of coherent sheaves on a smooth projective variety
X
, to define tilt stability conditions. Fix an ample divisor
H
on
X
, and set
n:=\text{dim}(X)
. The pair
\sigma_{H}=(\operatorname{Coh}(X),\ Z_{H})
, where
Z_{H}=-\operatorname{ch}_{1}H^{n-1}+\operatorname{ch}_{0}H^{n}
, is a weak stability condition on
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
, known as
slope stability
, with respect to the rank
2
lattice generated by the elements of the form
(H^{n}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E),H^{n-1}\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E))
for
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
; see
[
BLM+17
, Example 2.8]
. In particular, the slope of a coherent sheaf
E
on
X
is defined by

\mu_{H}(E)=\begin{cases}\frac{H^{n-1}\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E)}{H^{n}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E)}&\text{if }\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E)>0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Any
\mu_{H}
-semistable sheaf
E
satisfies the Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality

\Delta_{H}(E):=(H^{n-1}\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E))^{2}-2H^{n}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E)\cdot H^{n-2}\operatorname{ch}_{2}(E)\geq 0.

We observe that if
n=1
, then
\sigma_{H}
is a stability condition.

Now given
\beta\in\mathbb R
, we denote by
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
the heart of a bounded
t
-structure obtained by tilting the weak stability condition
\sigma_{H}
at the slope
\mu_{H}=\beta
(see Theorem
2.4
).
For
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
, the twisted Chern character is defined by
\operatorname{ch}^{\beta}(E):=e^{-\beta H}\operatorname{ch}(E)
. Explicitly, the first three terms are

\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\beta}(E):=\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E),\quad\operatorname{ch}_{1}^{\beta}(E):=\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E)-\beta H\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E)

and

\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\beta}(E):=\operatorname{ch}_{2}(E)-\beta H\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E)+\frac{\beta^{2}H^{2}}{2}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E).

Proposition 2.5

(
[
BLM+17
, Proposition 2.12]
)
.

There is a continuous family of weak stability conditions parametrised by
\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R
, given by

(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R\mapsto\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}=(\emph{Coh}^{\beta}(X),Z_{\alpha,\beta})

with

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\colon\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z^{3}\

\displaystyle\ \rightarrow\ \mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\left(H^{3}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E),H^{2}\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E),H\operatorname{ch}_{2}(E)\right)\

\displaystyle\ \mapsto\ \frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}H^{n}\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\beta}(E)-H^{n-2}\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\beta}(E)+iH^{n-1}\operatorname{ch}_{1}^{\beta}(E).

The weak stability condition
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
is called
tilt stability
, and if
n=2
, then it defines a stability condition on
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
(see
[
Bri08
,
AB13
]
). There is a region in the upper half-plane where the notions of
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-stability and
\mu_{H}
-stability are closely related.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
BMS16
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Assume
E\in\operatorname{Coh}(X)
is a
\mu_{H}
-stable sheaf of positive rank. Then
E
is an element of
\,\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
for
\beta<\mu_{H}(E)
and is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for
\alpha\gg 0
.

We now recall the notions of walls and chambers for tilt stability.

Definition 2.7
.

Fix
v\in\Lambda
. A
numerical wall
for
v
is a subset of the upper half-plane of the form

W(v,w)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb R_{>0}\times\mathbb R\colon\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(v)=\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(w)\}

with respect to a vector
w\in\Lambda
. We say a point
(\alpha,\beta)\in W(v,w)
is on an
actual wall
for
v
if and only if there is an object
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
of class
v(E)=v
which is strictly
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable and unstable on one side of the wall.

A
chamber
for class
v
is a connected component in the complement of the union of walls in the upper half-plane.

Tilt stability satisfies well-behaved wall and chamber structure, in the sense that walls with respect to a class
v\in\Lambda
are locally finite. Properties of walls are described by the following theorem, which was first proved in
[
Mac14
]
(see also
[
Sch20
]
).

Theorem 2.8

(Bertram’s nested wall theorem)
.

Fix

(a)

\alpha

(b)

If two numerical walls intersect, then they are the same. If a numerical wall contains a point defining an actual wall, then the numerical wall is an actual wall.

(c)

2.4. Induced stability conditions

We finish this section by recalling induced stability conditions on an admissible subcategory. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
\mathbb C
-linear triangulated category with Serre functor
S
. Assume
\mathcal{T}
has a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{D},E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\rangle,

where
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{m}\}
is an exceptional collection and
\mathcal{D}:=\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{m}\rangle^{\perp}
. The next proposition gives a criterion to construct a stability condition on
\mathcal{D}
from a weak stability condition on
\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
BLM+17
, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

Let
\sigma=(\mathcal{A},Z)
be a weak stability condition on
\mathcal{T}
.
Assume that the exceptional collection
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\}
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

E_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
;

(b)

S(E_{i})\in\mathcal{A}[1]
;

(c)

Z(E_{i})\neq 0
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,m
.

If moreover there are no objects
0\neq F\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{D}
with

,
then the pair
\sigma^{\prime}=(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},Z|_{K(\mathcal{A})})
is a stability condition on
\mathcal{D}
.

3. Serre-invariant stability conditions

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category which is linear of finite type over a field
K
;
i.e.

\oplus_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(E,F[i])
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
K
. Assume that
\mathcal{T}
has Serre functor
S
. Since
S
is a linear exact autoequivalence, we can have the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A stability condition
\sigma
on
\mathcal{T}
is
Serre invariant
(or
S
-invariant
) if
S\cdot\sigma=\sigma\cdot\tilde{g}
for some
\tilde{g}\in\widetilde{\text{GL}}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb R)
.

Assume
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the following conditions:

(
C1
)

The Serre functor
S
of
\mathcal{T}
satisfies
S^{r}=[k]
when
0<k/r<2
or
r=2
and
k=4
.

(
C2
)

The numerical Grothendieck group
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T})
is of rank
2
, and we have the inequality
\ell_{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\max\{\chi(v,v)\colon 0\neq v\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T})\}<0
.

(
C3
)

There is an object
Q\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying

(3.1)

-\ell_{\mathcal{T}}+1\leq\hom^{1}(Q,Q)<-2\ell_{\mathcal{T}}+2.

If
k=2r=4
, there are two objects
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
satisfying (
3.1
) such that
Q_{1}
is not isomorphic to
Q_{2}
or
Q_{2}[1]
,
\hom(Q_{2},Q_{1})\neq 0
and
\hom(Q_{1},Q_{2}[1])\neq 0
.

We can slightly relax (
C3
) in the case
k=2r=4
; see Lemma
3.7
.

The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be
S
-invariant numerical stability conditions on a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
satisfying the above conditions (
C1
), (
C2
) and (
C3
). Then there exists a
\tilde{g}\in\widetilde{\emph{GL}}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
such that
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\cdot\tilde{g}
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is easy to see that if the Serre functor satisfies
S=[2]
, then every stability condition on
\mathcal{T}
is Serre invariant.

-invariant stability conditions.

Aside: Very general cubic fourfolds

If the Serre functor of
\mathcal{T}
is
S=[2]
,
i.e.

\mathcal{T}
is a
2
-Calabi-Yau category, then clearly any stability condition is Serre invariant. An example of such a category is the Kuznetsov component of a cubic fourfold. In this section we apply Theorem
3.2
to show that if

is a very general cubic fourfold, then there is a unique
\widetilde{\text{GL}}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
-orbit of stability conditions on the Kuznetsov component of
X
.

Recall that the bounded derived category of a cubic fourfold
X
has a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)=\langle\operatorname{Ku}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle

by
[
Kuz10
]
, where
\operatorname{Ku}(X)=\langle\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\rangle^{\perp}
. The Serre functor of
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
satisfies
\mathcal{S}=[2]
. By
[
AT14
]
, the numerical Grothendieck group of
X
contains two classes
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
spanning a rank
2

A_{2}
-lattice

\langle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\rangle\cong\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{{-}}2&-1\\
-1&\hphantom{{-}}2\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the intersection pairing
(-,-):=-\chi(-,-)
. We say that

is very general if
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))=\langle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\rangle
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a very general cubic fourfold. If
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
are stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, then there exists a

\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\cdot\tilde{g}

Proof.

The Serre functor of
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
satisfies
\mathcal{S}^{2}=[4]
, as required in (
C1
).
Since
X
is very general, we have

\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))\cong\langle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\rangle\cong\begin{pmatrix}\hphantom{{-}}2&-1\\
-1&\hphantom{{-}}2\end{pmatrix}.

For every

, we have
v=a\lambda_{1}+b\lambda_{2}
. Thus

v^{2}=2a^{2}+2b^{2}-2ab\geq 2.

It follows that
\chi(v,v)=-v^{2}\leq-2
, which implies that

\ell_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}:=\text{max}\{\chi(v,v):v\neq 0\in\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))\}=-2<0,

as required in (
C2
).

We now check (
C3
). Recall that given a line
\ell\subset X
, we can define the objects
F_{\ell}
,
P_{\ell}\in\operatorname{Ku}(X)
sitting in

\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)[1]\to P_{\ell}\to\mathcal{I}_{\ell}.

Their classes in
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))
are
v(F_{\ell})=\lambda_{1}
and
v(P_{\ell})=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
as computed in
[
LLM+18
, Section 6.3]
. By
[
BLM+17
, Lemma A.5]
, they are stable with respect to every stability condition on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, and by
[
LLM+18
, (6.3.1)]
, their phases with respect to the stability conditions constructed in
[
BLM+17
]
satisfy

\phi(F_{\ell})<\phi(P_{\ell})<\phi(F_{\ell})+1.

Since
\chi(F_{\ell},F_{\ell})=-2=2-\hom^{1}(F_{\ell},F_{\ell})
and similarly for

, we have

3\leq\hom^{1}(F_{\ell},F_{\ell})=\hom^{1}(P_{\ell},P_{\ell})=4<6.

For two lines
\ell
and

, we have

\displaystyle\hom(F_{\ell},P_{\ell^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\hom(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(1),P_{\ell^{\prime}}[1])

\displaystyle=\hom(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(1),\mathcal{I}_{\ell^{\prime}}[1])

\displaystyle=\hom(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(1),\mathcal{O}_{\ell^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\hom(\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(1),\mathcal{O}_{\ell^{\prime}}[1]).

The latter is equal to

if

4. Kuznetsov component of cubic threefolds

From now on, we assume
X
is a smooth complex
cubic threefold, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)
denotes the corresponding very ample line bundle.

4.1. Kuznetsov component

The Kuznetsov component
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
is the right-orthogonal complement of the exceptional collection
\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)
in
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)

sitting in the semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)=\langle\operatorname{Ku}(X),\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)\rangle

(see
[
Kuz04
]
). By
[
BMM+12
, Proposition 2.7]
, the numerical Grothendieck group
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))
of
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
is a rank
2
lattice

\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))=\mathbb Z\,[\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]\oplus\mathbb Z\,[\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})],

where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is the ideal sheaf of a line
\ell
in
X
and
\mathcal{S}
denotes the Serre functor of
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. With respect to this basis, the Euler form
\chi_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(-,-)
on
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))
is represented by

(4.1)

\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\
\hphantom{{-}}0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that by
[
Kuz04
, Lemma 4.1, Lemma 4.2]
, the functor

\mathrm{O}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X),\quad\mathrm{O}(-)=L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))[-1]

satisfies
\mathrm{O}|_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}^{2}=\mathcal{S}^{-1}[1]
and
\mathrm{O}|_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}^{3}\cong[-1]
. Thus

\mathcal{S}[-3]=\mathcal{S}^{-2}[2]=\mathrm{O}|_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}^{4}=\mathrm{O}|_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}[-1],

which implies that

\mathcal{S}=\mathrm{O}|_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}[2]=L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))[1].

4.2. Stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)

Stability conditions on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
have been first constructed in
[
BMM+12
]
and more recently in
[
BLM+17
]
. We recall here the definition of the latter, while the former will be reviewed in Section
5.2
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb R
, denote by
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
the heart of a bounded t-structure obtained by tilting
\operatorname{Coh}(X)
with respect to slope stability at slope
\mu_{H}=\beta
. By
[
BLM+17
, Proposition 2.12]
, for
\alpha\in\mathbb R_{>0}
, the pair
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}=(\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X),Z_{\alpha,\beta})
defines a weak stability condition on
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
, known as tilt stability, with respect to the lattice
\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z^{3}
generated by the elements of the form
(H^{3}\operatorname{ch}_{0}(E),H^{2}\operatorname{ch}_{1}(E),H\operatorname{ch}_{2}(E))\in\mathbb Q^{3}
for
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
(see Section
2.2
). Here

Z_{\alpha,\beta}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}H^{3}\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\beta}-H\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\beta}+iH^{2}\operatorname{ch}_{1}^{\beta}

with
\operatorname{ch}^{\beta}\coloneqq e^{-\beta}\operatorname{ch}=(\operatorname{ch}_{0},\operatorname{ch}_{1}-\beta H\operatorname{ch}_{0},\operatorname{ch}_{2}-\beta H\operatorname{ch}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}H^{2}\operatorname{ch}_{0},\dots)
. Its associated slope is

\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=-\frac{\Re Z_{\alpha,\beta}(E)}{\Im Z_{\alpha,\beta}(E)}\quad\text{for }\Im Z_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\neq 0,

where
\Re[-]
and
\Im[-]
are the real and imaginary parts.

Now consider the tilted heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(X)=\langle\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
) is the subcategory of objects in
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
with
\mu^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}\leq 0
(resp.
\mu^{-}_{\alpha,\beta}>0
).
By
[
BLM+17
, Proposition 2.14]
, the pair
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}=\left(\operatorname{Coh}^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(X),-iZ_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
is a weak stability condition on
\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(X)
.

Sheaves supported in codimension 3 are the only objects in the heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
whose central charge
Z_{\alpha,\beta}
vanishes. We denote by
\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
the category of sheaves on
X
of codimension 3.

Proposition 4.1
.

Any
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-
(

\,)

is

-
(

where

and
T\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
. Conversely, take a
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-
(
semi
\,)
stable object
E
such that either

(a)

E\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X),E)=0
, or

(b)

E\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X),E[1])=0
.

Then

is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-
(
semi
\,)

Proof.

First assume
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-(semi)stable, so by definition it lies in an exact triangle

(4.2)

F[1]\rightarrow E\rightarrow T

for some

and
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
. If
Z_{\alpha,\beta}(T)\neq 0
,
i.e.

, we have

\Re[-iZ_{\alpha,\beta}(T)]\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\Re[-iZ_{\alpha,\beta}(F[1])]<0.

This shows that the phase of

is bigger than that of
T
with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
. Thus the exact triangle (

) implies that one of the following cases happens:

(i)

E=T\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
;

(ii)

E=F[1]\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1]
; or

(iii)

T\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
.

Case (

) is excluded in the statement, so we may assume the first two cases happen. We only consider the case
E\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
; the other one can be shown by a similar argument. Suppose for a contradiction that
E
is not
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-(semi)stable, and let

E_{1}\rightarrow E\rightarrow E_{2}

be a destabilising sequence in
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
. We may assume

is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable; thus

\mu_{\alpha,\beta}^{-}(E_{1})=\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{1})\geq\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(E_{2})\geq\mu_{\alpha,\beta}^{-}(E_{2})\geq\mu_{\alpha,\beta}^{-}(E)\geq 0.

The right inequality follows from the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
. Thus both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
lie in
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
; hence (
4.3
) is also a destabilising sequence for
E
in
\operatorname{Coh}^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
, which leads to a contradiction.

Now assume
E\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-(semi)stable and
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X),E)=0
. In the rest of the argument, all

lie in
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
and all
F_{i}
lie in
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
. Suppose
E_{1}\rightarrow E\rightarrow E_{2}
is a destabilising sequence with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
. We can assume
E_{2}
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable. Thus by the above argument, we have either

(1)

E_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1]
, or

(2)

E_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
, or

(3)

E_{2}
lies in an exact triangle
F_{2}[1]\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow T_{2}
, where
T_{2}\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
.

Since
E\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
, the phase of
E
with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
is less than or equal to
\frac{1}{2}
. But we know the phase of objects in
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1]
is bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
; thus case (
i
) cannot happen. In case (
iii
) we know the phase of
E_{2}
is equal to the phase of
F_{2}[1]
, so again this case cannot happen. Hence we may assume
E_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

By definition, we know
E_{1}
lies in an exact triangle
F_{1}[1]\rightarrow E_{1}\rightarrow T_{1}
. But the composition of the injections
F_{1}[1]\rightarrow E_{1}\rightarrow E
is zero because
\hom(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta})=0
. Thus
E_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
, and so the destabilising sequence is an exact sequence in the original heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
which (semi)destabilises
E
with respect to
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
, leading to a contradiction.

Finally, suppose
E\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-(semi)stable and we have
\hom(\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X),E[1])=0
. Let
E_{1}\rightarrow E[1]\rightarrow E_{2}
be a destabilising sequence with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
. We may assume
E_{1}
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable.
We know
E_{1}\notin\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
, and the phase of objects in
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta}\setminus\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
with respect to
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}^{0}
is less than the phase of objects in
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1]
. Thus the same argument as in case (
a
) implies that
E_{1}
lies in an exact triangle
F_{1}[1]\rightarrow E_{1}\rightarrow T_{1}
, where
T_{1}\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
and
F_{1}\neq 0
. By definition,
E_{2}
lies in an exact triangle
F_{2}[1]\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow T_{2}
. Since
\hom(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\alpha,\beta})=0
, the composition of surjections
E[1]\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow T_{2}
is zero. Thus
T_{2}=0
and
E_{2}=F_{2}[1]
. Taking cohomology from the destabilising sequence with respect to the heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
gives the long exact sequence

0\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F_{2}\rightarrow T_{1}\rightarrow 0.

Thus
F_{1}
is a subobject of
E
in
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
. We know the phase of
E_{1}
is equal to the phase of
F_{1}[1]
with respect to
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
, and it is bigger than or equal to the phase of
E[1]
. This implies that the phase of
F_{1}
is bigger than or equal to the phase of
E
with respect to
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

By restricting weak stability conditions
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
to the Kuznetsov component
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, we obtain a stability condition on it.

(

4.3. Stable objects in
\boldsymbol{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}
with minimal
\boldsymbol{\hom^{1}}

By (
4.1
), the classes in
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))
with self-intersection
-1
are

\pm[\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]\,,\quad\pm[\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]\ \quad\text{and}\quad\pm[\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]=\pm\left([\mathcal{I}_{\ell}]-[\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]\right).

In this section we investigate the moduli of semistable objects of the above classes. We denote by
M^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(v)
the moduli space of
\sigma
-semistable objects of class
v\in\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(X))
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\sigma
be an
\mathcal{S}
-invariant stability condition on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
.

(a)

The moduli space
M^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}([\mathcal{I}_{\ell}])
is isomorphic to the moduli space
M_{X}(v)
of slope-stable sheaves on
X
with Chern character
v\coloneqq\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})=\left(1,0,-\frac{1}{3}H^{2},0\right)
.

(b)

The moduli space
M^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}([\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})])
is isomorphic to the moduli space
M_{X}(w)
of slope-stable sheaves on
X
with Chern character
w\coloneqq\left(2,-H,-\frac{1}{6}H^{2},\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\right)
.

(c)

The moduli space
M^{\sigma}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}([\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})])
is isomorphic to the moduli space
M_{X}(v-w)
of large volume limit stable complexes of character
v-w
.

In particular, all the three moduli spaces above are isomorphic to the Fano variety
\Sigma(X)
of lines in
X
. This proves Theorem
1.3
. Part (
a
) of Theorem
4.5
is proved in
[
PY20
]
; however, we prove it here via a slightly different argument.

We start with a slope-stable sheaf
E
with Chern character
v
. So
E
is torsion-free with double dual
E^{\vee\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Thus it lies in the exact sequence

E\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\twoheadrightarrow Q,

where
Q
is a torsion sheaf of character
\left(0,0,\frac{1}{3}H^{2},0\right)
, which is therefore isomorphic to the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
of a line
\ell
in
X
. Thus
E
is isomorphic to the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
.

By Lemma
2.6
,
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for
\beta<0
and
\alpha\gg 0
. Applying
[
Fey22
, Lemma 3.5]
to
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
, we see that there is no wall for it crossing the vertical line
\beta=\beta_{0}=-1/{H^{3}}
. Thus
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}
-stable for any
\alpha>0
.
Since
\mu_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})<0
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}[1]\in\operatorname{Coh}_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}^{0}(X)
. For any sheaf
Q\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
, we know

\hom(Q,\mathcal{O}_{\ell})=0=\hom(Q,\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]).

Thus
\hom(Q,\mathcal{I}_{\ell}[1])=0
, and so Proposition
4.1
implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}[1]
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}
-stable. Since
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}\in\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, we immediately get that
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
is
\sigma(\alpha,\beta_{0})
-stable. Thus by Corollary
4.3
, we get the following.

Lemma 4.6
.

The ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}
for any line
\ell
in
X
is stable with respect to any
\mathcal{S}
-invariant stability condition on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
.

By definition,
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
is also stable with respect to any
\mathcal{S}
-invariant stability condition on
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. The next step is to describe the complex
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
. We know
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
is of character
\left(1,H,\frac{1}{6}H^{2},-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\right)
, so
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))=3
. Consider the short exact sequence

\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(H).

Since the map
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{\ell}(H))
is surjective
and
H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(H))=0
for
i>0
by the Kodaira vanishing theorem, we get

\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)[1])=\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)[2])=0.

Thus we obtain

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})[-1]=L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))=\mathrm{cone}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 3}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}}\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)).

Since Pic
(X)=\mathbb Z.H
, Lemma 3.5 of
[
Fey22
]
implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta=0}
-stable for any
\alpha>0
. Thus
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable along the numerical wall
W(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H),\mathcal{O}_{X}[1])
where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
have the same phase. Therefore, their extension
L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))
is also
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable along the wall. One can easily show that
W(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H),\mathcal{O}_{X}[1])
intersects the horizontal line
\alpha=0
at two points with
\beta
-values
0
and
\frac{1}{3}
. Thus by the definition of the heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
,
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
is a two-term complex with cohomology in degree -2 and -1, and

(4.5)

\mu_{H}^{+}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right)\leq 0.

Also, since
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is a quotient sheaf of
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
, we get

1\leq\mu_{H}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right).

We know
\operatorname{ch}\left(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\right)=w=\left(2,-H,-\frac{1}{6}H^{2},\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\right)
. The rank of
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is lower than
1=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))
. If the rank is
1
, then the image im
(\operatorname{ev})
in the category of coherent sheaves is of rank zero, which is not possible because
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
is torsion-free. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is of rank zero, so

(-1)^{i+1}\operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(\mathcal{H}^{i}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right).H^{2}\geq 0

for
i=-2,-1
. Thus
\operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right)
is equal to either zero or
H
. In the latter case, we will have
\operatorname{ch}_{\leq 2}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right)=(2,0,-\alpha)
for some
\alpha\geq 0
because of the Gieseker semistability of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 3}
. Then im
(\operatorname{ev})
is a subsheaf of
\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)
of rank
1
, so it is torsion-free of class
\operatorname{ch}(\text{im}(\operatorname{ev}))=(1,0,\alpha)
and so
\alpha\leq 0
; thus in total we obtain
\alpha=0
.

Applying a similar argument also implies that
\operatorname{ch}_{3}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))\right)=0
; thus
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
, which is not possible by the definition of the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is a sheaf supported in dimension at most
1
.

We also claim
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is slope stable. Otherwise, there is a subsheaf
F
of bigger slope. By (
4.5
), it must be of class
\operatorname{ch}_{\leq 1}(F)=(1,0)
. The semistability of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})[-1]
along the wall implies that
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is a reflexive sheaf. If not,
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))[1]
, and so
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})[-1]
, has a subobject supported in dimension at most
1
of phase
1
, which is not possible. Since
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is of rank
2
, its quotient sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))/F
is stable, and it must also be reflexive. Taking the double dual shows that
F^{\vee\vee}=F
; thus
F
is a line bundle, and so
F\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which is in contradiction to the definition of the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}^{-2}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is slope stable.

The next proposition implies that
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
is zero and so the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}
is surjective and the kernel is a reflexive sheaf of Chern character
w
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Take an
H
-slope-stable sheaf
E
of Chern character
(2,-H,\operatorname{ch}_{2},\operatorname{ch}_{3})
. Then
\operatorname{ch}_{2}.H\leq-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}
, and if
\operatorname{ch}_{2}.H=-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}
, then
\operatorname{ch}_{3}\leq\frac{1}{6}H^{3}
. This, in particular, implies that any
H
-slope-stable sheaf of Chern character
w
is a reflexive sheaf.

Proof.

Proposition
2.9
implies that
\operatorname{ch}_{2}.H\leq 0
. Since
c_{2}(E)=\frac{1}{2}H^{2}-\operatorname{ch}_{2}
is an integral class, we obtain
\operatorname{ch}_{2}.H\leq-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}
. Assume
\operatorname{ch}_{2}.H=-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}
. There is no wall for
E
crossing the vertical line
\beta=-1
by
[
Fey22
, Lemma 3.5]
; thus
E
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta=-1}
-stable for any
\alpha>0
. One can easily compute that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\mu_{\alpha,\,\beta=-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2H)[1])<\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\mu_{\alpha,\,\beta=-1}(E),

which implies that
\hom(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2H)[1])=0
because
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2H)[1]
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for any
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb R
; see
[
BMS16
, Corollary 3.11]
. Moreover, since

\mu_{H}(E)=\frac{-1}{2}<\mu_{H}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),

we get
\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},E)=0
. Therefore,
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},E)=\operatorname{ch}_{3}(E)-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\leq 0
, as claimed.
∎

Thus we obtain

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})=L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))[1]=K_{\ell}[2]

for a slope-stable reflexive sheaf
K_{\ell}
of Chern character
w
. The next proposition shows that any slope-stable sheaf
K
of class

is of the form
L_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H))[-2]
for a line
\ell
on
X
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Take a slope-stable sheaf
K
of Chern character
w
. There exists a line
\ell
on
X
such that
K
lies in the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 3}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{\ell}(H)\rightarrow 0.

In other words,
K=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})[-2]
.

Proof.

Lemma
4.7
implies that
K
is a reflexive sheaf; thus
K[1]
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for
\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha\gg 0
; see
[
BBF+20
, Proposition 4.17]
. Since there is no wall for
K[1]
crossing the vertical line
\beta=0
, it is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable along the numerical wall

. We have

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\mu_{\alpha,\,\beta=0}(K[1])<\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\mu_{\alpha,\,\beta=0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2H)).

Thus
\hom(K,\mathcal{O}_{X}[2])=\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2H),K[1])=0
. Also,
\hom(K,\mathcal{O}_{X}[3])\!=\!\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2H),K)\!=\!0

. Therefore,

which implies that there is a non-zero map
K[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
. Therefore,
K[1]
is unstable below the numerical wall
W(K[1],\mathcal{O}_{X}[1])
.

Choose three linearly independent sections of
K^{\vee}
:

\psi\colon K[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 3}[1].

We know
K[1]
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
are
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable of the same phase
\phi
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
is a simple object in
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
. Therefore, the map
\psi
is surjective in
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
. The kernel
\ker\psi

The wall

intersects the horizontal line
\alpha=0
at two points with
\beta=0,\frac{1}{3}
. Thus moving along the wall implies that

(4.6)

\frac{1}{6}\leq\mu_{H}^{-}\left(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\ker\psi)\right).

Moreover, the slope stability of

implies that

(4.7)

\mu_{H}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\ker\psi)\right)\leq-\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,

and
\operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(\mathcal{H}^{0}(\ker\psi)\right)-\operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\ker\psi)\right)=H

First assume
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\ker\psi)

2

\operatorname{ch}_{1}\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\ker\psi)\right)H^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}H^{3}

\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\ker\psi)
is zero and
\ker\psi
is a sheaf.

We finally claim that
\ker\psi
is torsion-free. If not, by (

1
. Since
\ker\psi
is semistable along the wall of phase lower than

. Hence its torsion part is trivial;
i.e.

\ker\psi
is a torsion-free sheaf. This immediately implies that

for a line
\ell
on

.
∎

The next step is to compute
\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})
.

Lemma 4.9
.

We have
h^{0}(K_{\ell}(H))=3

Proof.

We know

K_{\ell}(H)
crossing the vertical line
\beta=0
. Therefore,
K_{\ell}(H)
is semistable along the numerical wall

. Suppose for a contradiction that there are four linearly independent global sections:

s\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus 4}\rightarrow K_{\ell}(H).

Since
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is stable along the wall and has the same phase as
K_{\ell}(H)
, the map
s
is injective and the cokernel of
s
is also semistable of the same phase. It is of Chern character
\operatorname{ch}(\text{cok}\ s)=\left(-2,H,-\frac{1}{6}H^{2},-\frac{1}{6}H^{3}\right)
. The numerical wall
W(K_{\ell}(H),\mathcal{O}_{X})
intersects the horizontal line
\alpha=0
at two points with
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}=0,-\frac{1}{3}
. Thus by the definition of the heart, we get

(4.8)

\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{-\frac{5}{4}}}\left(f_{b}(0,1]\right)\subset\langle\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}},\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}[1]\rangle.

Also, an easy computation shows that
\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\subset\langle\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}),\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})[1]\rangle
(see for instance
[
PY20
, Lemma 3.7]
). Restricting to the heart
\mathcal{A}_{b}
, this implies

\mathcal{A}_{b}\subset\langle\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}},\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}[1]\rangle

by
[
BLM+17
, Lemma 4.3]
. Thus the stability conditions
\sigma_{b}
and
\sigma_{-\frac{5}{4}}\,.\,g_{b}
have the same central charge and satisfy the conditions of
[
BMS16
, Lemma 8.11]
; hence they are the same.
∎

5.3. Serre invariance

In this section we show the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

The stability condition
\overline{\sigma}=\left(\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}},\overline{Z}\right)
from (
5.5
) on
\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is Serre invariant.

Therefore, Proposition
5.3
implies that all stability conditions
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
from (
5.4
) are Serre invariant. By
[

]
(see also
[
Kuz04
, Proposition 3.8]
), the Serre functor
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}
of
\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
satisfies

\mathcal{S}^{-1}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\cong L_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(-\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1})[-2]\cong(-\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1})\circ L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(-)[-2].

We first describe the image of the heart after the action of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{-1}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

We have
L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}})\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\subset\langle\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}},\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}[1]\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}=\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\cap\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
, and set
E^{\prime}:=L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(E)
. We first show that

(5.6)

E^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}=L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(E)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\in\langle\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}),\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})[1]\rangle.

By Serre duality and the fact that
\mathcal{B}_{0}[1],\mathcal{B}_{-1}[1]\in\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
, we have that
\operatorname{Hom}^{i}(\mathcal{B}_{0}[1],E)

is defined by the triangle

Since
\mathcal{B}_{0}[1]

\overline{\sigma}_{b=-\frac{5}{4},w}
,
the map

:

\scriptstyle{f}

. Tensoring the third column by

By Lemma
5.6
below, we have

) follows.

Since
L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(E)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}=L_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(E\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1})

L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(E)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\in\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
. If we consider the HN filtration of

L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(E)\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\in\langle\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\cap\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}),\operatorname{Coh}^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\cap\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})[1]\rangle,

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

We have
\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\subset\langle\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}),\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})[1]\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is a flat
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module, by (
5.2
), for
F\in\operatorname{Coh}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
, we have that

\operatorname{ch}_{1}(F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1})=\operatorname{ch}_{1}(F)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}(F).

If
F\in\operatorname{Coh}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is slope semistable with
\mu(F)>b
, then
F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}
is slope semistable with slope
\mu(F)+\frac{1}{2}>b
. Then
F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}
is in
\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
. Otherwise, assume
F\in\operatorname{Coh}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is slope semistable with
\mu(F)\leq b
. If
\mu(F)+\frac{1}{2}\leq b
, then
F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}[1]\in\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
, while if
\mu(F)+\frac{1}{2}>b
, then
F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\in\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
and thus
F\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}[1]\in\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})[1]
. This implies the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{2}[-3]=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{-1}[2]
, we only need to find a
g\coloneqq(T,f)\in\widetilde{\text{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
such that

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{-1}[2]\,.\,\overline{\sigma}=\overline{\sigma}\,.\,g.

We know
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{-1}[2]\,.\,\overline{\sigma}=\left(\overline{Z}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}[-2]\,,\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}^{-1}[2]\left(\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}\cap\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})\right)\right)
. By
[
LMS15
, Remark 2.2 and Proposition 2.12]
, the classes
v_{1}\coloneqq[\Xi(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})]=[\mathcal{B}_{1}]-[\mathcal{B}_{0}]
and
v_{2}\coloneqq[\Xi(S_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))]=2[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-[\mathcal{B}_{-1}]
are the basis of
\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}))
. Also,
[
Huy06
, Lemma 1.30]
implies
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(\Xi(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))=\Xi(\mathcal{S}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell}))
and

\left[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\left(\Xi\left(\mathcal{S}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\right)\right)\right]=\left[\Xi\left(\mathcal{S}^{2}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\right)\right]=\left[\Xi\left(\mathcal{S}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\right)\right]-\left[\Xi(\mathcal{I}_{\ell})\right].

Therefore,
\overline{Z}\circ\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}=T^{-1}\circ\overline{Z}
for a linear invertible function
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

T^{-1}\left[\overline{Z}(v_{1})\right]=\overline{Z}(v_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad T^{-1}\left[\overline{Z}(v_{2})\right]=\overline{Z}(v_{2})-\overline{Z}(v_{1}).

Thus

T\left[\overline{Z}(v_{1})\right]=\overline{Z}(v_{1})-\overline{Z}(v_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad T\left[\overline{Z}(v_{2})\right]=\overline{Z}(v_{1}).

We know
\overline{Z}(v_{1})=2i
and
\overline{Z}(v_{2})=4+2i
, so one can easily check that the linear function
T
with respect to the standard basis can be represented as

\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\
\frac{1}{2}&\hphantom{{-}}0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus there exists a cover
(T,f)\in\widetilde{\text{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbb R)
such that
f(0,1)\subset(0,2)
, so

\mathcal{P}_{\overline{\sigma}}(f(0,1])\subset\langle\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}},\mathcal{A}_{-\frac{5}{4}}[1]\rangle.

Thus the stability conditions
\sigma\cdot g
and
((-\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1})\circ L_{\mathcal{B}_{0}})\cdot\sigma
have the same stability functions, and the condition of
[
BMS16
, Lemma 8.11]
holds for their hearts by Proposition
5.5
. Hence they are the same stability conditions.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

The stability condition
\overline{\sigma}
from (
5.5
) and the stability conditions
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
defined in (

) are in the same orbit as
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
defined in Theorem

with respect to the

Proof.

As noted in Corollary

, the conditions of Theorem

are satisfied by

. Then the statement follows from Theorem

, Theorem

and the uniqueness implied by Theorem

.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

Note that all the known stability conditions on

\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. A positive answer would imply that there is a unique

.

6. Ulrich bundles

Let
X
be a smooth cubic threefold as before. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{(s)U}_{d}
the moduli of (stable) Ulrich bundles. By
[
LMS15
, Theorem B]
, the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}^{sU}_{d}
of stable Ulrich bundles of rank
d
on
X
is non-empty and smooth of dimension
d^{2}+1
. In this section we apply our result on Serre-invariant stability conditions to show the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

The moduli space
\mathfrak{M}^{U}_{d}
of Ulrich bundles of rank
d
is irreducible.

Recall that an Ulrich bundle
E
of rank
d\geq 2
on
X
is a vector bundle on
X
satisfying
H^{i}(X,E(jH))=0
for all
i=1,2
,
j\in\mathbb Z
and such that the graded module
\oplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}H^{0}(X,E(mH))
has
3d
generators in degree
1
. Proposition 2.9 of
[
CHG+12
]
shows that any Ulrich bundle
E
is Gieseker semistable, and if it is Gieseker stable, then it is
\mu
-stable. Moreover, if an Ulrich bundle
E
is Gieseker strictly semistable, then its stable factors are also Ulrich bundles. Thus
[
LMS15
, Lemma 2.19]
implies that

\operatorname{ch}(E)=\left(d,0,-\frac{d}{3}H^{2},0\right)=d\operatorname{ch}(I_{\ell}).

The Gieseker semistability of
E
implies that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(kH),E)=0
for
k\geq 0
. We also have
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(kH),E)=0
for
k=0,1
; thus the definition of Ulrich bundles implies that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(kH),E[3])=0
. Therefore,
E\in\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. The first step to prove Theorem
6.1
is to show that Ulrich bundles are semistable objects in the Kuznetsov component with respect to the stability conditions
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
defined in Theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Every
E\in\mathfrak{M}^{U}_{d}
is
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
-semistable in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
for any
(\alpha,\beta)\in V
, with
V
as in (
4.4
).

Proof.

Since
\mu_{H}(E)=0
and
E
is Gieseker semistable, we get
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
for
\beta<0
, and by
[
BMS16
, Lemma 2.7]
, the Ulrich bundle
E
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable for
\alpha\gg 0
. Theorem 3.1 of
[
Fey22
]
implies that
E
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}
-semistable for any
\alpha>0
and

\beta_{0}=-\frac{1}{3d(d-1)}.

Since
E
is locally-free, Proposition
4.1
implies that
E
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta_{0}}
-semistable; thus by definition,
E
is
\sigma(\alpha,\beta_{0})
-semistable for
\alpha
small enough because
E\in\operatorname{Ku}(X)
. Hence the claim follows from Corollary
4.3
.
∎

We denote by
M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
the moduli space parametrising
\sigma(\alpha,\beta)
-semistable objects in
\operatorname{Ku}(X)
of class
d[I_{\ell}]
. Proposition
6.2
implies that we have an embedding

(6.1)

\mathfrak{M}^{U}_{d}\hookrightarrow M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}]).

The next step is to show that (
6.1
) is an open embedding. By Corollary
4.3
, we may assume the pairs
(\alpha,\beta)\in V
, with
V
as in (
4.4
), are on the curve
\alpha^{2}=\beta^{2}-2/3
, so (up to a shift) any
E\in M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
has maximum phase
1
in the heart
\mathcal{A}(\alpha,\beta)
, as in (
4.9
). Therefore,
E\in\operatorname{Coh}^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}(X)
is
\sigma^{0}_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable of phase
1
. We claim
E
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable; otherwise, Proposition
4.1
implies that
E
lies in the exact triangle

(6.2)

F[1]\rightarrow E\rightarrow T,

where
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
and
T\in\operatorname{Coh}_{0}(X)
. By our choice of
(\alpha,\beta)
, we know
\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(F)=0
. Since
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}
, we know

\mu^{+}_{\alpha,\beta}(F)\leq 0=\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(F).

Thus
F
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable of slope zero. But we know
\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2H)[1])<0
; thus

\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},F[2])=\hom(F,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2H)[1])=0.

Since
E\in\operatorname{Ku}(X)
, we know
\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},E)=0
; thus the exact triangle (
6.2
) implies that
\hom(\mathcal{O}_{X},T)=0
, which is not possible as
T
is a skyscraper sheaf, and so
E
is
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable.

Hence, up to a shift, we may assume any
E\in M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
lies in the heart
\operatorname{Coh}^{\beta}(X)
. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{i}(E)=0
if
i\neq 0,-1
, and if
\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)\neq 0
, then it is a torsion-free sheaf. So if we consider the locus of objects
E\in M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
with rank
(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E))=0
, then we get precisely the locus of sheaves which we know is non-empty and by
[
GD63
, Theorem 7.7.5]
is an open sublocus. Moreover, being Ulrich is an open property, see
[
CHG+12
]
, so
\mathfrak{M}^{U}_{d}
is an open subset of
M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
, as claimed.

By Theorem
4.2
, we have

M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])\cong M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[\mathcal{S}(I_{\ell})]),

and Corollary
5.7
gives the isomorphism

(6.3)

M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[\mathcal{S}(I_{\ell})])\cong M^{\overline{\sigma}(b,w)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})}(2d[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-d[\mathcal{B}_{-1}]),

where the stability conditions
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
are defined in Proposition
5.3
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Take an object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}}\nolimits(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
of class
2d[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-d[\mathcal{B}_{-1}]=d\left(4,-3,\frac{3}{2}\right)
. Then
E
is a shift of a Gieseker-
(
semi
\,)
stable sheaf if and only if
E
lies in
\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
and is
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
-
(
semi
\,)
stable for some
(b,w)\in U
, where
-\frac{5}{4}\leq b<-\frac{3}{4}
.

Proof.

First assume
E
is a Gieseker-(semi)stable sheaf. Then its Gieseker-slope is less than that of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, so
\hom(\mathcal{B}_{1},E)=0
. Moreover,

\hom(\mathcal{B}_{1},E[2])=\hom(E,\mathcal{B}_{0})=0;

thus
\hom(\mathcal{B}_{1},E[1])=-\chi(\mathcal{B}_{1},E)=-2d\chi(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})+d\chi(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{-1})=0
. This shows that
E\in\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
. Since
E
is Gieseker (semi)stable, it is
\overline{\sigma}_{b,w}
-(semi)stable for
b<-\frac{3}{4}
and
w\gg 0
. Then
[
BLM+17
, Remark 5.12]
implies that
E
is
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
-semistable.

Conversely, take a
\overline{\sigma}(b,w)
-(semi)stable object
E
in
\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
. We may assume
E\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
and by the uniqueness of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}
-invariant stability conditions, we can assume
w\gg 0
. Thus
[
BLM+17
, Remark 5.12]
shows that
E
is in
\operatorname{Coh}^{b}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
and is
\overline{\sigma}_{b,w}
-(semi)stable. This immediately implies that
E
is a Gieseker-(semi)stable sheaf.
∎

Thus we get the isomorphism

M^{\overline{\sigma}(b,w)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})}(2d[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-d[\mathcal{B}_{-1}])\cong M_{(\mathbb P^{2},\ \mathcal{B}_{0})}^{ss}(2d[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-d[\mathcal{B}_{1}])\eqqcolon\mathcal{M}_{d},

where
\mathcal{M}_{d}
parametrises Gieseker-semistable sheaves in
\operatorname{Coh}(\mathbb P^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0})
of class
2d[\mathcal{B}_{0}]-d[\mathcal{B}_{1}]
. The last step is to show the following.

Proposition 6.4
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is irreducible.

Proof.

The argument is the same as Step 3 in the proof of
[
LMS15
, Theorem 2.12]
; we include it for completeness.

The first claim is that
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is connected. If
d=1
, then (
6.3
) gives

\mathcal{M}_{1}\cong M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}([I_{\ell}]),

and the latter is isomorphic to the Fano surface of lines in
X
by
[
PY20
, Theorem 1.1]
. In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is connected. For
d>1
, the strictly semistable locus is covered by the images of the natural maps
\varphi_{d_{1},d_{2}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{d_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}_{d_{2}}\to\mathcal{M}_{d}
for
1\leq d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d
and
d_{1}+d_{2}=d
which send any pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
to
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
. Thus by induction, we deduce that the semistable locus of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is connected as well. Arguing exactly as in
[
KLS06
, Lemma 4.1]
, we obtain the non-existence of a connected component of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
consisting of purely stable sheaves.
We conclude that
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is connected.

Finally, note that for all
E\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
, we have Ext
{}^{2}(E,E)=\operatorname{Hom}(E,E\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{B}_{-1})^{\vee}=0
. This implies that
\mathcal{M}_{d}
is normal, combining well-known results in deformation theory and Quot schemes (see
[
LMS15
, Theorem 2.12]
).
∎

Since
\mathfrak{M}^{U}_{d}
is an open subset of the irreducible space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
,
it is irreducible. This ends the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

We also observe the following property, which is a consequence of the previous computations.

Corollary 6.5
.

The moduli space
M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])
is irreducible and projective.

Proof.

By the above computation, we have
M^{\sigma(\alpha,\beta)}_{\operatorname{Ku}(X)}(d[I_{\ell}])\cong\mathcal{M}_{d}

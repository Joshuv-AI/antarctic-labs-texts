Abstract
. Let E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} be an elliptic curve and for each prime p p , let N p N_{p} denote the number of points of E E modulo p p . The original version of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture asserts that ∏ p ≤ x N p p ∼ C ​ ( log ⁡ x ) rank ​ ( E ​ ( ℚ ) ) \prod\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\text{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q}))} as x → ∞ x\to\infty . Goldfeld [ Gol82 ] showed that this conjecture implies both the Riemann Hypothesis for L ⁡ ( E , s ) L(E,s) and the modern formulation of the conjecture i.e. that ord s = 1 L ⁡ ( E , s ) = rank ​ ( E ⁡ ( ℚ ) ) \mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)=\text{rank}(E(\mathbb{Q})) . In this paper, we prove that if we let r = ord s = 1 L ⁡ ( E , s ) r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s) , then under the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis for L ⁡ ( E , s ) L(E,s) , we have that ∏ p ≤ x N p p ∼ C ​ ( log ⁡ x ) r \prod\limits_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{r} for all x x outside a set of finite logarithmic measure. 1.
Introduction

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with conductor
N_{E}
, and for each prime
p
, let
N_{p}=\#E_{\textrm{ns}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, where
E_{\textrm{ns}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
denotes the set of non-singular
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-rational points on a minimal Weierstrass model for
E
at
p
. We denote by
\mathrm{rk}(E)
the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
E(\mathbb{Q})
. The original version of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture takes the following form.

Conjecture 1.1

(Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
BSD65
]
)
.

We have that

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)}

as
x\to\infty
for some constant
C
depending on
E
.

The modern formulation of the conjecture is expressed in terms of the
L
-function
L(E,s)
associated to
E
. If we set
a_{p}=p+1-N_{p}
if
p\nmid N_{E}
and
a_{p}=p-N_{p}
if
p|N_{E}
, then the
L
-function of
E
is defined for
\textrm{Re}(s)>3/2
by

L(E,s)=\prod_{p|N_{E}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{p\nmid N_{E}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}.

By the work of Wiles
[
Wil95
]
and Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor
[
BCDT01
]
,
L(E,s)
admits an analytic continuation to the entire complex plane and satisfies a functional equation which relates
L(E,s)
to
L(E,2-s)
. More precisely, if we define

\Lambda(E,s)=N_{E}^{s/2}(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(E,s)

the functional equation asserts that

\Lambda(E,s)=w_{E}\Lambda(E,2-s)

for some
w_{E}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
In particular, the critical strip of
L(E,s)
is the region
\frac{1}{2}<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
and the critical line of
L(E,s)
is the line
\mathrm{Re}(s)=1
. The Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
asserts that all the non-trivial zeros of
L(E,s)
lie on the line
\textrm{Re}(s)=1
. We now state the modern formulation of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve.
Then

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E).

Goldfeld
[
Gol82
]
showed that Conjecture
1.1
implies both Conjecture
1.2
and the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
; moreover, he also gave an explicit expression for the constant
C
appearing in Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3

(Goldfeld
[
Gol82
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. If

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)}

as
x\to\infty
, then
L(E,s)
satisfies the Riemann Hypothesis and
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E)
. Moreover, if we set
r:=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
, then

C=\frac{r!}{L^{(r)}(E,1)}\cdot\sqrt{2}e^{r\gamma},

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant and
L^{(r)}(E,s)
is the
r
-th derivative of
L(E,s)
.

It is natural to ask, assuming the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
if necessary, whether the converse is true
i.e.
whether Conjecture
1.2
implies Conjecture
1.1
.

1.1.
Main results of the paper

In this paper, we treat this question in a more general context by studying the behaviour of partial Euler products of
L
-functions of elliptic curves in the right-half of their critical strip. Our first main result establishes the asymptotic behaviour of these partial Euler products.

Theorem A

(Theorem
2.3
)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis holds for
L(E,s)
. Then for a complex number
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
1<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
, we have that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}=L(E,s)\exp\left(-r\cdot\mathrm{Li}(x^{1-s})-R_{s}(x)+U_{s}(x)+O\left(\frac{\log x}{x^{1/6}}\right)\right),\\

where
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
,
\mathrm{Li}(x)
is the principal value of
\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}
,
R_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-z}}{(\rho-z)^{2}}dz
and
U_{s}(x)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2s}}
. Here, the sums in
R_{s}(x)
are taken over all non-trivial zeros
\rho=1+i\gamma
of
L(E,s)
(excluding
\rho=1
) counted with multiplicity and are interpreted as
\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\sum\limits_{|\gamma|\leq T}
, and
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
denote the Frobenius eigenvalues of
E
at
p
. The implied constant in the big O term above depends only on the elliptic curve
E
and is independent of
s
.

Our theorem is an instance of the general paradigm that even though Euler products of
L
-functions are generally valid only to the right of the critical strip, there is a strong sense in which they should also continue to persist inside the critical strip. Indeed,
an analogous asymptotic for partial Euler products of the Riemann zeta function in the critical strip had been obtained by Ramanujan
[
Ram15
]
during his study of highly composite numbers (see Remark
2.4
), which was recently generalized by Kaneko
[
Kan22
]
to the case of Dirichlet
L
-functions. The key idea in the proof of Theorem
A
, as well as in the results of Ramanujan and Kaneko, is to develop a suitable version of an explicit formula (in the sense of analytic number theory) for the relevant
L
-function, that allows one to make a clear connection to the partial Euler products in question. We refer to Harper’s Bourbaki survey
[
Har19
]
(especially Principle 1.3 therein and the subsequent discussion) as well as the expository article
[
KKK23
]
by Kaneko–Koyama–Kurokawa for a more detailed discussion of the study of Euler products of
L
-functions in the critical strip.

We use Theorem
A
as a key tool to study the relations between the original and modern formulations of the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture. To state our results, we first introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
be a measurable subset of the real numbers. The logarithmic measure of
S
is defined to be

\mu^{\times}(S)=\int_{S}\frac{dt}{t}.

Theorem B

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{r},

where
\displaystyle r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)
,
C=\frac{r!}{L^{(r)}(E,1)}\cdot\sqrt{2}e^{r\gamma}
,
\gamma
is Euler’s constant and
L^{(r)}(E,s)
is the
r
-th derivative of
L(E,s)
.

We briefly explain the proof of Theorem
B
. The key idea is to let
s
tend to
1
in Theorem
A
in a controlled manner dependent on
x
; more precisely, we set
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
and let
x\to\infty
. A brief calculation shows that the left hand side of Theorem
A
is asymptotic to
\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}
. The main work in the proof is then estimating the contributions from the various terms on the right hand side. The term
-r\textrm{Li}(x^{1-s})
contributes the main term
(\log x)^{r}
in Theorem
B
, and the term
U_{s}(x)
contributes the factor of
\sqrt{2}
appearing in the constant
C
. This is the reason why we chose to keep the
U_{s}(x)
term in its exact shape, rather than absorbing it into an error term. The delicate issue in our proof is to handle the contribution coming from the zeros of
L(E,s)
in the term
R_{s}(x)
. By another explicit formula argument, we reduce the problem to estimating the sum
\psi_{E}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})\log p
, where as above
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
are the Frobenius eigenvalues at
p
. The Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
is equivalent to
\psi_{E}(x)=O(x(\log x)^{2})
. However, by a factor of
\log x
, this estimate is not good enough for our proof to carry through. Thus, in Section
3
of the paper, we use a method of Gallagher
[
Gal80
]
to obtain, conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
, the slightly refined estimate
\psi_{E}(x)=O(x(\log\log x)^{2})
outside a set of finite logarithmic measure; this is the essential reason why the set
S
of finite logarithmic measure appears in the statement of Theorem
B
. The fact that the estimate coming from the Riemann Hypothesis is inadequate for our purposes is consistent with the results of
[
Con05
]
and
[
KM05
]
; we briefly discuss this in Section
1.2
below.

As a corollary to Theorem
B
, we recover Goldfeld’s result
[
Gol82
]
that Conjecture
1.1
implies Conjecture
1.2
.

Corollary A

(Corollary
4.3
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Suppose that

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)}.

Then

\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E)}.

Moreover, we also prove a result in the direction of the converse; the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
and Conjecture
1.2
implies Conjecture
1.1
off a set of finite logarithmic measure.

Corollary B

(Corollary
4.4
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Assume the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
and that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E)
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)},

where
C=\frac{r!}{L^{(r)}(E,1)}\cdot\sqrt{2}e^{r\gamma}
,
\gamma
is Euler’s constant and
L^{(r)}(E,s)
is the
r
-th derivative of
L(E,s)
with
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
.

1.2.
Comparison with other work

Besides the work of Goldfeld
[
Gol82
]
mentioned above, there is also related work by Conrad
[
Con05
]
and Kuo–Murty
[
KM05
]
on studying Conjecture
1.1
and its consequences.
The new feature in our work is to study the asymptotic behaviour of the partial Euler products of
L(E,s)
in the right-half of the critical strip, as opposed to studying its asymptotics just at the point
s=1
, as in the statement of Conjecture
1.1
. Working in this wider region allows us to deduce not only Goldfeld’s result as a corollary of our main theorems, but also, as we briefly explain below, a result in
[
Con05
]
and
[
KM05
]
. In
[
Con05
]
and
[
KM05
]
, Conrad and Kuo–Murty independently show that
\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{r},
is equivalent to the fact that
\psi_{E}(x)=o(x\log x)
. The latter estimate, while deeper than what can at present be obtained from the Riemann Hypothesis, is indeed plausible (we refer to
[
Con05
, page 290]
for more details).
Indeed, as mentioned above, we prove in Section
3
that under the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis, this estimate holds outside a set of finite logarithmic measure . The proof of Theorem
B
works verbatim, by replacing the estimate in Section
3
with
\psi_{E}(x)=o(x\log x)
, to obtain the backward implication of the result of
[
Con05
]
and
[
KM05
]
. In particular, assuming the estimate
\psi_{E}(x)=o(x\log x)
in lieu of the Riemann Hypothesis also allows us to dispense with the set
S
appearing in Corollary
B
.

We point out that an important technique in our work that is common to many of the works cited above is the use of explicit formulas. However, as a technical comment, we mention that the explicit formulas used in our work are related directly to partial sums of Frobenius eigenvalues and in particular, unlike the explicit formula used in
[
Gol82
]
, we do not need to use an auxiliary test-function in our versions of the explicit formula.

Finally, we mention that the ideas introduced in this paper will be used in forthcoming work of the author
[
She24
]
to study the behaviour of partial Euler products of automorphic
L
-functions at the center of the critical strip, where our results will be applied towards problems concerning Chebyshev’s bias in the recently introduced framework of Aoki–Koyama
[
AK23
]
.

|f(z)|\leq c|g(z)|
for all
z
in a specified range. The constant
c
is allowed to depend on the elliptic curve
E
. The rank of

r

2.
Partial Euler products in the critical strip

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve.
Since the Euler factor at a prime
p
is quadratic for each
p\nmid N_{E}
, it will be convenient for us to linearize this Euler factor: for each
p\nmid N_{E}
, we let
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
denote the roots of the polynomial
x^{2}-a_{p}x+p
and so
each Euler factor can be written as

(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})=(1-\alpha_{p}p^{-s})\cdot(1-\beta_{p}p^{-s}).

The polynomial
x^{2}-a_{p}x+p
can be interpreted as the characteristic polynomial of the
p
-power Frobenius morphism on the the Tate-module
T_{\ell}(E)
, where
\ell\neq p
is a prime (and so
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
can be viewed as the corresponding eigenvalues).
By the Riemann Hypothesis for elliptic curves over finite fields (see for example
[
Sil09
, Theorem 2.3.1]
), we have that

(2.1)

|\alpha_{p}|=|\beta_{p}|=\sqrt{p}.

If
p|N_{E}
, it is a standard fact that
a_{p}\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)>3/2
, we have that

-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}},

where

b_{n}=\begin{cases}(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})\cdot\log p&\textrm{ if }n=p^{k}\textrm{ and }p\nmid N_{E}\\
a_{p}^{k}\cdot\log p&\textrm{ if }n=p^{k}\textrm{ and }p|N_{E}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows by a direct computation, see for example
[
Spi15
, Lemma 5.1.4]

∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus{\{1\}}
be a complex number such that
L(E,s)\neq 0
. We have that

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}=-r\cdot\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}-\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s},

where
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
, the sum over
\rho
is taken over all non-trivial zeros of
L(E,s)
(excluding
\rho=1
) counting multiplicity and is interpreted as
\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\gamma|\leq T}
, and where the last term of the sum on the left hand side is weighted by half if
x
is an integer.

Proof.

This is a standard argument to prove explicit formulas in analytic number theory. By using Perron’s formula and the previous lemma, we have that

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s+z}}\right)\frac{x^{z}}{z}dz=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\frac{x^{z}}{z}dz

for
c\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently large. By shifting the contour to the left and applying Cauchy’s residue theorem, the above integral equals the sum of residues of the integrand in the region
\mathrm{Re}(z)\leq c
.

•

When
s+z=1
,
\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z=1-s}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\right)=r}
, so
\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z=1-s}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\frac{x^{z}}{z}\right)=-r\cdot\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}}
.

•

When
z=0
,
\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z=0}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\frac{x^{z}}{z}\right)=-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}}
.

•

If
\rho\neq 1
is a non-trivial zero of
L(E,s)
,
\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z=\rho-s}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\frac{x^{z}}{z}\right)=-r_{\rho}\cdot\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}}
, where
r_{\rho}
is the order of the zero
\rho
. Thus, we obtain a total contribution of
\displaystyle{-\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}}
counting multiplicity for all the non-trivial zeros of
L(E,s)
.

•

Finally, the trivial zeros of
L(E,s)
are at
s=0,-1,-2,\ldots
(which are all simple). Thus, since
\displaystyle{\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{z=-k-s}\left(-\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s+z)}{L(E,s+z)}\frac{x^{z}}{z}\right)=\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}}
for all
k\geq 0
, we get a total contribution of
\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}}
.

∎

To prove our main theorem, we will first relate the partial Euler product

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-\alpha_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\beta_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}

to the series
\sum\limits_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}
. We begin by noting that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\right)=-\left(\text{I}(x)+\text{II}(x)+\text{III}(x)\right),

where

\text{I}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}}{p^{s}-a_{p}},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{I\hskip-0.28453ptI}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\alpha_{p}}{p^{s}-\alpha_{p}},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{I\hskip-0.28453ptI\hskip-0.28453ptI}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\beta_{p}}{p^{s}-\beta_{p}}.

We now fix
s\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\textrm{Re}(s)>1
. We write

\text{I}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}}{p^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}

\text{II}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\alpha_{p}}{p^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\alpha_{p}^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\alpha_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}

\text{III}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\beta_{p}}{p^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\beta_{p}^{2}}{p^{2s}}+\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot\beta_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}.

On the other hand, using the definition of
b_{n}
as given in Lemma
2.1
,

\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}={\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{a_{p}^{k}\cdot\log p}{p^{ks}}+{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})\cdot\log p}{p^{ks}}.

We can write the first term as

\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{a_{p}^{k}\cdot\log p}{p^{ks}}={\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}}{p^{s}}+\sum_{k\geq 2}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x^{1/k}\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}

and the second term as

\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}

\displaystyle={\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}+\beta_{p})}{p^{s}}+{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\sqrt{x}\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\sum_{k\geq 3}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x^{1/k}\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}.

Combining these observations it follows that

\displaystyle\text{I}(x)+\text{II}(x)+\text{III}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}+\sum_{k\geq 3}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 2}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}.

Thus, in summary,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left(\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\right)\newline

\displaystyle=-\ \sum_{n\leq x}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k\geq 3}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}-\sum_{k\geq 2}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis holds for
L(E,s)
. Then for a complex number
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
1<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
, we have that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}=L(E,s)\exp\left(-r\cdot\mathrm{Li}(x^{1-s})-R_{s}(x)+U_{s}(x)+O\left(\frac{\log x}{x^{1/6}}\right)\right),\\

where
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
,
\mathrm{Li}(x)
is the principal value of
\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}
,
\displaystyle R_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-z}}{(\rho-z)^{2}}dz
and
\displaystyle U_{s}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2s}}
. Here, the sums are taken over all non-trivial zeros
\rho=1+i\gamma
of
L(E,s)
(excluding
\rho=1
) counted with multiplicity and are interpreted as
\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\sum\limits_{|\gamma|\leq T}
.

Proof.

For all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
1<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
, we have by Equation
(
2
)
and Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}

\displaystyle\left(\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\right)=r\cdot\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}+\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}+\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}-\sum_{k\geq 3}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}-\sum_{k\geq 2}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}.

We now fix
s_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
with
1<\mathrm{Re}(s)<\frac{3}{2}
. Integrating this equation along the horizontal straight line from
s_{0}
to
\infty
, it follows that

\displaystyle\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s_{0}})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s_{0}}+p^{1-2s_{0}})^{-1}=-r\cdot\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}ds-\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}ds+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}ds+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}ds

(2.3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks}}ds+\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log p\cdot a_{p}^{k}}{p^{ks}}ds.

We now analyze each of these integrals in turn.

•

We write
s_{0}=\sigma_{0}+it_{0}
and parameterize the contour from
s_{0}
to
\infty
by the path
\gamma:[\sigma_{0},\infty]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by
\gamma(y)=y+it_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}ds

\displaystyle=\log x\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{(1-(y+it_{0}))\log x}}{(1-(y+it_{0}))\log x}dy=\int_{-\infty}^{(1-s_{0})\log x}\frac{e^{v}}{v}dv.

By parameterizing this last integral in a similar manner, we conclude that

\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{1-s}}{1-s}ds=\int_{0}^{e^{(1-s_{0})\log x}}\frac{dt}{\log t}=\textrm{Li}(x^{1-s_{0}}).\\

•

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{L^{\prime}(E,s)}{L(E,s)}ds=-\log L(E,s_{0}).

•

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\left(\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{-\log x(\rho-s)}\Biggr|_{s_{0}}^{\infty}+\frac{1}{\log x}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{(\rho-s)^{2}}ds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s_{0}}}{\rho-s_{0}}+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{(\rho-s)^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=R_{s_{0}}(x)\textrm{ by definition. }

•

We note that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}\ll\frac{1}{x^{\mathrm{Re}(s)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k}}{k+1}\ll\frac{1}{x^{\mathrm{Re}(s)}}.

Thus,

\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-k-s}}{k+s}ds\ll\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{d|s|}{x^{\mathrm{Re}(s)}}=\frac{1}{x^{\mathrm{Re}(s_{0})}\log x}.

•

We have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{p^{2s}}ds=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2s_{0}}}=U_{s_{0}}(x)\textrm{ by definition. }

•

We have that

\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\log p\cdot(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{p^{ks_{0}}}ds=\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{kp^{ks_{0}}}

Using Equation (
2.1
), we have that

\sum_{x^{1/k}<p\leq x}\frac{\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k}}{kp^{k}}\ll\sum_{x^{1/k}<n}\frac{1}{kn^{k/2}}\ll\int_{x^{1/k}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{kt^{k/2}}=\frac{x^{{1/k}-1/2}}{k-k^{2}/2}\ll\frac{x^{1/k}}{\sqrt{x}\cdot k^{2}}.

Now, since we have that

\sum_{k\geq 3}\frac{x^{1/k}}{k^{2}}=\sum_{3\leq k\leq\log x}\frac{x^{1/k}}{k^{2}}+O(1)\ll x^{1/3}\log x,

it follows that

\sum_{k\geq 3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/k}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{kp^{ks_{0}}}\ll\frac{\log x}{x^{1/6}}.

Finally, note that the last term in Equation (
2
) is an empty sum when
x
is large enough. Inserting all of the above estimates into (
2
) and exponentiating proves the result.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

During his study of highly composite numbers, while investigating the maximal order of the divisor function, Ramanujan established the following formula for partial Euler products of the Riemann zeta function in the right-half of the critical strip: for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\frac{1}{2}<\mathrm{Re}(s)<1
we have that

\prod_{p\leq x}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}=-\zeta(s)\exp\left(\mathrm{Li}(\vartheta(x)^{1-s})+\frac{2sx^{\frac{1}{2}-s}}{(2s-1)\log x}+\frac{S_{s}(x)}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}-s}}{\log(x)^{2}}\right)\right),

where
\mathrm{Li}(x)
is the principal value of
\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}
,
\vartheta(x)=\sum\limits_{p\leq x}\log p
is Chebyshev’s function and

S_{s}(x)=-s\sum\limits_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho(\rho-s)}
, the sum taken over all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. Ramanujan’s initial investigations were published in
[
Ram15
]
, but his complete work on the subject, including the statement and proof of the above formula, remained in his “lost notebook”, and was only published in 1997 in
[
Ram97
]
. Theorem
2.3
can be regarded as an analogous formula for partial Euler products of
L
-functions of elliptic curves.

3.
Refinement of the error term for
\psi_{E}(x)

Let
\psi_{E}(x)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{k}\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})\log p
. The Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
is equivalent to the fact that

\psi_{E}(x)=O(x(\log x)^{2})
. In this section, conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
, we use a method due to Gallagher
[
Gal80
]
to improve the error term outside a set of finite logarithmic measure. To do so, we will need a general result (see for instance
[
Sel92
]
) on the vertical distribution of zeros for a wide class of
L
-functions. For our elliptic curve
L
-function, the result is the following (see also
[
KM23
, Theorem 2]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N_{E}(t)
be the number of zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(E,s)
satisfying
0<\gamma<t
. Then

N_{E}(t)=\frac{\alpha_{E}}{\pi}t(\log t+c)+O(\log t),

where
c
is a constant and
\alpha_{E}
is a constant depending on
E
.

Corollary 3.2
.

We have that

\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}

converges, where the sum is taken over all non-trivial zeros of
L(E,s)
.

Proof.

Note that

\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\sum_{t<|\gamma|\leq t+1}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}.

By Theorem
3.1
, the number of terms
t<\gamma\leq t+1
is
\ll\log t
. Thus,

\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}\ll\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log t}{t^{2}}<\infty.

∎

We will also need the following estimate on mean values of exponential sums.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}
. For each
\nu\in\mathcal{A}
, let
c(\nu)
be a complex number such that the series

S(u)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{A}}c(\nu)e^{2\pi i\nu u}

is absolutely convergent. Then for any
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have that

\int_{-U}^{U}|S(u)|^{2}du\leq\left(\frac{\pi\theta}{\sin(\pi\theta)}\right)^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\frac{U}{\theta}\sum_{t\leq\nu\leq t+\frac{\theta}{U}}c(\nu)\right|^{2}dt.

Proof.

This lemma was originally proven by Gallagher (see
[
Gal70
, Lemma 1]
), but the version stated above is
[
Koy16
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\psi_{E}(x)=O(x(\log\log x)^{2}).

Proof.

Let
X
be such that
X\leq x\ll X
. By the Riemann von-Mangoldt explicit formula for
L(E,s)
(see
[
Fio14
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
Rub13
, Equation 1.10]
), we have that

\psi_{E}(x)=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma|\leq X\end{subarray}}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}+O(\log^{2}X).

By Theorem
3.1
, the number of terms
t<\gamma\leq t+1
is
\ll\log t
. Thus,

\sum_{t<|\gamma|\leq t+1}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\ll x\cdot\frac{\log t}{t}

and hence

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{0<|\gamma|<\log X}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\ll X\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\log X\rfloor}\frac{\log(j+1)}{j}\ll X\int_{1}^{\log X}\frac{\log t}{t}dt=\frac{X(\log\log X)^{2}}{2}\ll x(\log\log x)^{2}.

Let
T\leq X
. We now consider the integral

\int_{X}^{eX}\left|\sum_{T<\gamma\leq X}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\right|^{2}\frac{dx}{x^{3}}.

By making the substitution
x=e^{u}
and then
u=\frac{1}{2}+\log X+2\pi t
, this integral equals

\int_{\log X}^{1+\log X}\left|\sum_{T<\gamma<X}\frac{e^{i\gamma u}}{\rho}\right|^{2}du=2\pi\cdot\int_{-\frac{1}{4\pi}}^{\frac{1}{4\pi}}\left|\sum_{T<\gamma<X}\frac{e^{i\gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\log X)}}{\rho}\cdot e^{2\pi i\gamma t}\right|^{2}dt.

Setting
\mathcal{A}=\{\gamma:T<\gamma\leq X\}
,
\theta=\frac{1}{4\pi}
,
and

c(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{i\gamma(\frac{1}{2}+\log X)}}{\rho}&\text{if }T<\gamma\leq X\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

we can apply Lemma
3.3
conclude that

\int_{X}^{eX}\left|\sum_{T<\gamma\leq X}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\right|^{2}\frac{dx}{x^{3}}\ll\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T<\gamma\leq X\\
t\leq\gamma\leq t+1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\rho|}\right)^{2}dt\ll\int_{T-1}^{X}\left(\sum_{t\leq\gamma\leq t+1}\frac{1}{|\rho|}\right)^{2}dt,

Using again the fact that the number of terms
t<\gamma\leq t+1
is
\ll\log t
we get that

\displaystyle\int_{T-1}^{X}\left(\sum_{t<\gamma<t+1}\frac{1}{|\rho|}\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\ll\int_{T-1}^{X}\left(\frac{\log t}{t}\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{-\log^{2}t-2\log t-2}{t}\Biggr|_{T-1}^{X}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\log^{2}T}{T}.

Define a set
S_{T}
by setting

S_{T}=\{x\in[e^{T},e^{T+1}]:\left|\sum_{T<|\gamma|\leq e^{T}}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\right|\geq x(\log\log x)^{2}\}.

Setting
X=e^{T}
, we conclude from above that

\displaystyle\frac{\log^{2}T}{T}

\displaystyle\gg\int_{S_{T}}\left|\sum_{T<\gamma\leq X}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}\right|^{2}\frac{dx}{x^{3}}

\displaystyle\gg\int_{S_{T}}\frac{(\log\log x)^{4}}{x}dx

\displaystyle\gg(\log\log e^{T})^{4}\int_{S_{T}}\frac{dx}{x}

\displaystyle=(\log T)^{4}\cdot\mu^{\times}(S_{T}).

Thus,

\mu^{\times}(S_{T})\ll\frac{1}{T\log^{2}T}.

Define the set

S=\bigcup_{T=2}^{\infty}S_{T}.

Then

\mu^{\times}(S)\ll\sum_{T=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{T\log^{2}T}<\infty.

Thus,
S
has finite logarithmic measure and so, by definition of
S
and by Equation (
3.1
), we conclude that

\psi_{E}(x)=O(x(\log\log x)^{2})

for all
x\not\in S
.
∎

4.
Applications

In this section, we use our asymptotic for the partial Euler products of
L(E,s)
in Theorem
2.3
to study the relations between the original and modern formulations of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
U_{s}(x)
be as in Theorem
2.3
. Then have that

\lim_{x\to\infty}U_{1}(x)=\log\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

Proof.

This follows from the fact that (see
[
KM05
, page 332]
)

(4.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\log\log x+B+o(1),

where
B
is a constant. Equation (
4.1
) is a generalized version of Merten’s theorem and can also be deduced using the forthcoming work of the author
[
She24
]
, where such a formula is proven for general automorphic
L
-functions.

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{r},

where
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
,
C=\frac{r!}{L^{(r)}(E,1)}\cdot\sqrt{2}e^{r\gamma}
,
\gamma
is Euler’s constant and
L^{(r)}(E,s)
is the
r
-th derivative of
L(E,s)
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to set
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
and estimate the left-hand and right-hand sides of Theorem
2.3
as
x\to\infty
. When
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
, we have that

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-1-1/x}+p^{-1-2/x})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\left(1-a_{p}p^{-1}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log p}{x}\right)\right)+p^{-1}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log p}{x}\right)\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-1}+p^{-1})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{a_{p}\cdot\log p}{px}\right)\right),

where we used the fact that

(1+a+O(f(x)))^{-1}=(1+a)^{-1}(1+O(f(x)))\textrm{ if }f(x)=o(1)\textrm{ and }a\textrm{ is sufficiently small. }

Now

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{a_{p}\cdot\log p}{px}\right)\right)=1+O\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{|a_{p}|\cdot\log p}{px}\right)=1+O\left(\frac{\log x}{\sqrt{x}}\right)=1+o(1),

where we used the fact that
|a_{p}|\leq 2\sqrt{p}
and the classical estimate
\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log x+O(1)
.
Thus, when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
,

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\sim\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-1}+p^{-1})^{-1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{N_{p}}.

An analogous argument shows that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\sim\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-1})^{-1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{N_{p}}.

Combining the two previous equations yields that for
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
,

(4.2)

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p|N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}(1-a_{p}p^{-s}+p^{1-2s})^{-1}\sim\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{p}{N_{p}}.

We now estimate the right-hand side of Theorem
2.3
. Write

L(E,s)=a_{r}(s-1)^{r}+a_{r+1}(s-1)^{r+1}+\cdots

so that when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
,
L(E,s)\sim a_{r}\cdot\frac{1}{x^{r}}
as
x\to\infty
.

To estimate the contribution coming from the term
\mathrm{Li}(x^{1-s})
, we use the classical fact (see for instance
[
Fin03
, pp.425]
or
[
Har40
, Equation (2.2.5)]
) that

\mathrm{Li}(x)=\gamma+\log|\log x|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log x)^{n}}{n!\cdot n}\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}.

Applying this when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
yields that

\mathrm{Li}(x^{1-s})=\gamma+\log\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)+o(1).

To estimate the contribution coming from the term
U_{s}(x)
we note that
U_{1+\frac{1}{x}}(x)\to U_{1}(x)
since

\displaystyle\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2(1+\frac{1}{x})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2}}\cdot\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log p}{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{x}\sum_{\sqrt{x}<p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}\right)=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\sqrt{x}<p\leq x\\
p\nmid N_{E}\end{subarray}}}\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{2}+\beta_{p}^{2})}{2p^{2}}+o(1).

Using Lemma
4.1
, we conclude that when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
,
U_{s}(x)\to\log\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)
as
x\to\infty
.

To estimate the contribution coming from the term
R_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}+\frac{1}{\log x}\sum\limits_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-z}}{(\rho-z)^{2}}dz
when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
, we begin by noting that

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{1}{\rho-s}-\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{1}{\rho}=\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{s}{(\rho-s)\rho}

and using Corollary
3.2
, we have that

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{s}{(\rho-s)\rho}\ll\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}<\infty.

This implies that

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{1}{\rho-s}=\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho}+O(1)

and since we are assuming the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
,

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}=\frac{1}{x^{s}}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}+O(x^{\rho-s})\ll\frac{1}{x}\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}+O(1).

Using the Riemann von-Mangoldt explicit formula for
L(E,s)
(see
[
Rub13
, Equation 1.10]
)

\psi_{E}(x)=-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}+o(x^{1/2}),

it follows that

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}\ll\frac{1}{x}\psi_{E}(x)+O(1).

By Theorem
3.4
, we conclude that there exists a set
S
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}\ll\frac{1}{x}O(x(\log\log x)^{2})+O(1)

and so for all
x\not\in S
,

\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\frac{x^{\rho-s}}{\rho-s}=o(1).

To estimate the second quantity in the definition of
R_{s}(x)
, we note that since
\sum\limits_{\rho}\frac{1}{|\rho|^{2}}
converges by Corollary
3.2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{\rho\neq 1}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\rho-z}}{(\rho-z)^{2}}dz

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\cdot\int_{s}^{\infty}x^{1-\mathrm{Re}(z)}d|z|\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{x^{1-\textrm{Re}(s)}}{\log^{2}x}\right)=o(1).

Thus, in summary, when
s=1+\frac{1}{x}
, we conclude that for all
x\not\in S
,

\displaystyle L(E,s)\exp\left(-r\cdot\mathrm{Li}(x^{1-s})-R_{s}(x)+U_{s}(x)+O\left(\frac{\log x}{x^{1/6}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=L(E,s)\exp\left(-r\gamma-r\log\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+o(1)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{a_{r}}{x^{r}}\exp\left(-r\gamma-r\log\left(\frac{\log x}{x}\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)=\frac{a_{r}}{\sqrt{2}\cdot e^{r\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{(\log x)^{r}}.

Combining this with Equation (
4.2
) proves the theorem.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Suppose that

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)}.

Then

\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E).

Proof.

Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
, this follows directly from Theorem
4.2
. It is known that (see
[
Gol82
, Theorem 2(a)]
or
[
KM05
, pp.334]
) that the asymptotic
\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E(\mathbb{Q}))}
implies the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
, so the corollary is true unconditionally. For completeness, we explain the proof of this implication here. Note that

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}=\prod_{p|N_{E}}\left(1-\frac{a_{p}}{p}\right)\prod_{p\nmid N_{E}}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{\beta_{p}}{p}\right).

Taking logarithms on both sides yields and computing the remainder term (see
[
KM05
, Lemma 1]
for details) yields that

\log\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}=\sum_{n\leq x}c_{n}+O(1),

where

c_{n}=\begin{cases}-\frac{(\alpha_{p}^{k}+\beta_{p}^{k})}{kp^{k}}&\textrm{ if }n=p^{k}\textrm{ and }p\nmid N_{E}\\
-\frac{a_{p}^{k}}{kp^{k}}&\textrm{ if }n=p^{k}\textrm{ and }p|N_{E}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus, by hypothesis,
\sum\limits_{n\leq x}c_{n}=O(\mathrm{rk}(E)\cdot\log\log x)
and partial summation yields that

\log L(E,s+1)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}}{n^{s}}=-s\int_{1}^{\infty}(\sum_{n\leq x}c_{n})x^{-(s+1)}dx\textrm{ for all }s\in\mathbb{C}\textrm{ with }\mathrm{Re}(s)>1/2.

Hence,
\log L(E,s+1)
is holomorphic for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
and so
L(E,s)\neq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)=\mathrm{rk}(E)
and the Riemann Hypothesis for
L(E,s)
. Then there exists a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 2}
of finite logarithmic measure such that for all
x\not\in S
,

\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{N_{p}}{p}\sim C(\log x)^{\mathrm{rk}(E)},

where
C=\frac{r!}{L^{(r)}(E,1)}\cdot\sqrt{2}e^{r\gamma}
,
\gamma
is Euler’s constant and
L^{(r)}(E,s)
is the
r
-th derivative of
L(E,s)
with
r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
4.2
once we have identified
\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}\limits_{s=1}L(E,s)
with
\mathrm{rk}(E)
.
∎

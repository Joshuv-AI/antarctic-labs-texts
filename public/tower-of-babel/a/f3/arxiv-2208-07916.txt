Symplectic mapping class groups of blowups of tori
Gleb Smirnov

Abstract
. Let ω \omega be a Kähler form on the real 4 4 -torus T 4 T^{4} . Suppose that ω \omega satisfies an irrationality condition which can be achieved by an arbitrarily small perturbation of ω \omega . This note shows that the smoothly trivial symplectic mapping class group of the one-point symplectic blowup of ( T 4 , ω ) (T^{4},\omega) is infinitely generated.



1. Main result

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic
4
-manifold. The smoothly trivial symplectic mapping class group

K(X,\omega)
of
(X,\omega)
is defined by:

K(X,\omega)=\text{ker}\,\left[\pi_{0}\text{{Symp}}(X,\omega)\to\pi_{0}\text{{Diff}}(X)\right].

It is known that
K(X,\omega)
is not finitely generated for some
K3
surfaces. See
[
25
,
28
]
. The aim of this note is to explore new examples of
4
-manifolds with
K(X,\omega)
infinitely generated.

Let us consider
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
endowed with a constant coefficient symplectic form
\omega_{0}
. Let
X={\mathbb{R}}^{4}/L
be a real
4
-torus,
L
being a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
. Then
\omega_{0}
descends to a symplectic form on
X
, which we denote by
\omega
. Set
\kappa=[\omega]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
. We shall say that
\kappa
is non-resonant if the following holds:

(1.1)

\langle\kappa,a\rangle\neq 0\quad\text{for each $a\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}),\ a\neq 0,$}

where
\langle\phantom{\cdot},\phantom{\cdot}\rangle
denotes the cup product pairing. Choose a translation-invariant
\omega
-compatible
complex structure
J
on
X
, so that
(X,J,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold. Let
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
be the blowup of
X
at a point. Choose
\lambda>0
small enough so that
\tilde{X}
admits a Kähler form
\omega_{\lambda}
in the cohomology class
\pi^{*}[\omega]-\lambda\,e
, where
e
is the dual to the exceptional curve. (In fact, if (
1.1
) holds, then, for a suitably chosen
J
, the class
\pi^{*}[\omega]-\lambda\,e
admits a Kähler representative for all
0<\lambda<\sqrt{\langle\kappa,\kappa\rangle}
. See
[
17
,
9
]
.) What we shall prove is:

Theorem 1
.

If
\kappa
is non-resonant, then

K(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})
is infinitely generated.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
28
]
, but relies on different algebraic geometry from that of
K3
surfaces; there are two steps:

I.

Consider the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
of marked (
\kappa
-)polarized complex tori. It has the following properties:
(a)
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is a fine moduli space in the sense that there is a holomorphic fiber bundle
p\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}

whose fiber
X_{t}
over
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is a complex 2-torus; (b)
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is a contractible space; (c) each
X_{t}
may be endowed with a Kähler form
\Omega_{t}
such that
[\Omega_{t}]=\kappa
; and (d)
{\mathcal{X}}
has a canonical holomorphic cross-section
Z
.

Let
\tilde{{\mathcal{X}}}
denote the blowup of
{\mathcal{X}}
along
Z
. Composing the contraction of
Z
with
p
, we obtain a fiber
bundle
\tilde{p}\colon\tilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
whose fiber
\tilde{X}_{t}

over
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is the one-point blowup of
X_{t}
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
be the subset of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
defined as follows:
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
if
\tilde{X}_{t}
carries a Kähler form
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
in the class

[\tilde{\Omega}_{t}]=\kappa-\lambda\,e
. Let us choose a smooth-varying family of such Kähler forms
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
,
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
.

Consider the restriction
\tilde{p}\colon\tilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
. Identify

(\tilde{X},\omega_{\lambda})
with
(\tilde{X}_{t_{0}},\tilde{\Omega}_{t_{0}})
for some
t_{0}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
.
Since
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is contractible, it follows that
\tilde{p}\colon\tilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
is
C^{\infty}
-trivial. Then
\tilde{p}\colon\tilde{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
is also
C^{\infty}
-trivial.
Thus we may view
\tilde{\Omega}_{t}
as a family of symplectic forms on
(\tilde{X},\omega_{\lambda})
parametrized by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
. Moser’s theorem then gives a monodromy map

\pi_{1}({\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda},t_{0})\to K(\tilde{X},\omega_{\lambda}).

II.

Let
\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}\subset H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
be the set of all classes
\delta
that are indivisible (i.e., not a multiple) and that satisfy the following conditions:

\langle\delta,\delta\rangle=0,\quad 0<\langle\kappa,\delta\rangle\leqslant\lambda.

It will turn out that
\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}
is infinite, provided
\kappa
is non-resonant. Following Kronheimer
[
15
]
, we construct a homomorphism

q\colon K(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})\to\prod_{\delta\in\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

and then show that the composite homomorphism

\pi_{1}({\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\lambda},t_{0})\xrightarrow{\text{\ Moser\ }}K(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})\xrightarrow{q}\prod_{\delta\in\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

surjects onto
\bigoplus_{\delta\in\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\subset\prod_{\delta\in\Delta_{\kappa,\lambda}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

One would hope to find a mirror symmetry proof of Theorem
1
in the spirit of
Sheridan-Smith
[
25
]
and Hacking-Keating
[
13
]
. The present note does not contain a clear hint towards such a proof.

A recent study by Auroux and Smith
[
2
]
provides the first, albeit implicit, information about
\pi_{0}\text{{Symp}}(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})
. Let us state their result as follows:

Theorem 2

(Auroux-Smith,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
C
be a Riemann surface of genus
2
, and let it be equipped with a Kähler form
\tau
. Let
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})
be as above. Let
Z
be the product
(\Sigma\times\tilde{X},\tau\oplus\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda})
.
Choose a basis
\left\{\eta_{i}\right\}
in
H^{1}(Z;{\mathbb{Z}})
, and choose
\epsilon>0
small so that
Z
admits a Kähler form
\Omega_{\epsilon}
in the cohomology class

[\Omega_{\epsilon}]=[\tau\oplus\tilde{\omega}_{\lambda}]+\epsilon\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\eta_{t}\wedge\eta_{j}\quad\text{for some $c_{ij}\in(0,1)$.}

Suppose that
c_{ij}
are independent over
{\mathbb{Q}}
; then there exists a surjective homomorphism of

K(Z,\Omega_{\epsilon})
onto a free group of rank
N=N(\epsilon)
which tends to infinity as
\epsilon\to 0
.

There are other recent works on the symplectic mapping class groups in four dimensions: In
[
1
]
, Anjos, J. Li, T.-J. Li, and Pinsonnault studied the change of the symplectic mapping class group (and of the higher homotopies of the symplectomorphism group) under deformations of the symplectic form and obtained specific stability results. In
[
18
]
, J. Li, T.-J. Li, and Wu studied the symplectic mapping class groups of manifolds with
c_{1}(X)\cdot\omega>0
. In particular, they found manifolds with trivial symplectic mapping class groups and also manifolds whose symplectic mapping class group is the pure braid group of
S^{2}
. In
[
7
]
, Buse and J. Li investigated the symplectic mapping class groups of a one-point blow-up of irrational ruled surfaces; they found new non-trivial symplectic mapping classes that are trivial smoothly. The works above focus on the symplectomorphism groups of rational ruled surfaces and their blow-ups. The methods of those works remain highly dependent on the rationality assumption, and it is unclear if they apply to the problem considered in the present paper.

3. Family Seiberg-Witten invariants

In this section, we recall the basic properties of Seiberg-Witten invariants for families, specializing to the case of
b^{+}=3
. We refer to
[
23
,
24
]
for a comprehensive introduction to ordinary Seiberg-Witten invariants and to
[
19
]
for a detailed account of their family version. Some recent applications of family SW-invariants can be found in
[
16
,
4
,
21
]
.

Let
X
be a closed, connected, smooth oriented
4
-manifold. Let
{\mathcal{X}}\to B
be a fiber bundle whose fiber is
X
and whose base
B
is a smooth oriented manifold. In this section we insist that
B
is either the
2
-disk
D
or the
2
-sphere
S^{2}
and also that
b^{+}_{2}(X)=3
.

Let
T_{{\mathcal{X}}/B}
denote the vertical tangent bundle of
{\mathcal{X}}
. Pick a metric on
T_{{\mathcal{X}}/B}
, which we regard as a family of fiberwise metrics
\left\{g_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
. Pick also a spin
C
structure
{\mathfrak{s}}
on
T_{{\mathcal{X}}/B}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
. Let
{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
denote the restriction of
{\mathfrak{s}}
to a fiber
X_{b}
,
b\in B
. Hereafter, given any object
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, the object with subscript
b
denotes the restriction
of the given object to the fiber
X_{b}
.

Associated to
{\mathfrak{s}}
, there are
spinor bundles
W^{\pm}\to B
and determinant line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
, which we regard as
families of bundles

W^{\pm}=\bigcup_{b\in B}W^{\pm}_{b},\quad{\mathcal{L}}=\bigcup_{b\in B}{\mathcal{L}}_{b}.

We let
c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}})\in H^{2}({\mathcal{X}};{\mathbb{Z}})
and

c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})\in H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{Z}})
denote the Chern classes of
{\mathfrak{s}}
and

{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
respectively;

c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}})=c_{1}({\mathcal{L}})\in H^{2}({\mathcal{X}};{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})=c_{1}({\mathcal{L}}_{b})\in H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{Z}}).

Consider the configuration space

{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{(b,\varphi_{b},A_{b})\ |\ b\in B,\ \text{$\varphi_{b}$ is a section of $W^{\pm}_{b}$,}\ \text{$A_{b}$ is a ${\boldsymbol{U}}(1)$-connections on ${\mathcal{L}}_{b}$}\right\},

and let
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
be the fiber of
{\mathcal{C}}\to B
over
b\in B
. Choose a family
\left\{\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
of
g_{b}
-self-dual 2-forms. The (
\eta_{b}
-)perturbed Seiberg-Witten equations for a triple

(b,\varphi_{b},A_{b})\in{\mathcal{C}}
read:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{D}}_{A_{b}}\varphi_{b}=0,\\
F^{+}_{A_{b}}=\sigma(\varphi_{b})+i\,\eta_{b}.\end{cases}

Here
\sigma(\varphi)
is the squaring map, and
F^{+}_{A_{b}}
is the self-dual part of
F_{A_{b}}
, the curvature of the connection
A_{b}
, and
{\mathcal{D}}_{A_{b}}\colon\Gamma(W_{b}^{+})\to\Gamma(W_{b}^{-})
is the Dirac operator.

A solution
(b,\varphi_{b},{\mathcal{A}}_{b})
to equations (
3.1
) is said to be reducible if
\varphi_{b}=0
. Let us give a sufficient condition on
(g_{b},\eta_{b})
which ensures that (
3.1
) has
no reducible solutions. Setting
\varphi_{b}=0
, the equations take the form:

F^{+}_{A_{b}}=i\,\eta_{b}.

The latter equation has a solution
A_{b}
if and only if

\langle\eta_{b}\rangle_{g_{b}}+2\pi\langle c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})\rangle_{g_{b}}=0,

where
\langle\phantom{\eta}\rangle_{g_{b}}
stands for the self-dual part of the harmonic representative of the
2
-form in question, and, as before,

c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})
is the Chern class of
{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
;

[F_{A_{b}}]=-2\pi ic_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})\in H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}}).

Suppose that
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
satisfy

(3.2)

\langle\eta_{b}\rangle_{g_{b}}+2\pi\langle c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})\rangle_{g_{b}}\neq 0\quad\text{for all $b\in B$,}

then (
3.1
) has no reducible solutions. With (
3.2
) assumed, we consider the family moduli space

\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}=\left\{(b,\varphi_{b},A_{b})\in{\mathcal{C}}\ |\ (b,\varphi_{b},A_{b})\ \text{solves}\ \eqref{eq:sw}\right\}/\left\{\text{gauge transformations}\right\}.

By the Sard-Smale theorem, for a generic
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
, this moduli space is either empty or is a compact smooth manifold of dimension

(3.3)

d(\mathfrak{s},B)=\frac{1}{4}(c_{1}^{2}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})-3\sigma(X)-2\chi(X))+\text{dim}\,B.

Although the family invariants may be defined in general, we shall focus
on the case of
d(\mathfrak{s},B)=0
. Define the mod-2 Seiberg-Witten invariant for the spin
C
structure
{\mathfrak{s}}
and the family
\left\{(g_{b},\eta_{b})\right\}_{b\in B}
by setting

{\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}})=\text{Cardinality}\left(\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}\right)\,\text{mod}\,2.

The number depends on the choice of
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
. However, we can remove this dependence by restricting ourselves
to the families with vanishing winding number, which we now define. Observe that
\left\{H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{Z}})\right\}_{b\in B}
are all canonically isomorphic to each other, and are isomorphic to an abstract lattice
E\cong H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Such an isomorphism is indeed exists, because
B
is simply-connected. Let us consider the (punctured) positive cone
\mathbf{K}
;

\mathbf{K}=\left\{a\in E\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,a^{2}>0\right\}.

The space
\mathbf{K}
is homotopy equivalent to

S^{b_{2}^{+}(X)-1}
, which in our case would be
S^{2}
. Assuming that
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
satisfy (
3.2
),
we have a map

B\to\mathbf{K}\quad\text{defined by}\ b\to[\langle\eta_{b}\rangle_{g_{b}}+2\pi\langle c_{1}({\mathcal{L}}_{b})\rangle_{g_{b}}]\in H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}}),

and followed by the identification of
H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
with
E\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
. In the case of
B\cong S^{2}
, this map has a degree, which we call the winding number of
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that we are given two families

\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
and

\left\{g^{\prime}_{b},\eta^{\prime}_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
, each having vanishing winding number; then

{\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}})={\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g^{\prime}_{b},\eta^{\prime}_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}}).

Proof.

This is essentially proved in
[
19
, Prop. 3.6]
.
See also Lemma 2.1 in
[
4
]
. ∎

Thus, the
family SW-invariant, if being defined for a family with vanishing winding number, depends only on the (smooth) topology of
{\mathcal{X}}\to B
but not on our choice of
g_{b}
and
\eta_{b}
.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that a family
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
, a spin
C
structure
{\mathfrak{s}}
, and the conjugate spin
C
structure, written as
-{\mathfrak{s}}
, are so that
\left\{g_{b},\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in B}
has vanishing winding number w.r.t. to both
{\mathfrak{s}}
and
-{\mathfrak{s}}
; then

{\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}})={\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}(-{\mathfrak{s}}).

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
4
, Prop. 2.8]
. See also
[
28
, § 3]
and Proposition 2.2.22 in
[
24
]
. ∎

One can also weaken the dependence of a family invariant on the underlying spin
C
structure.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that we are given spin
C
structures

{\mathfrak{s}}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
such that the restrictions

{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}_{b}
have equal Chern classes;

c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{b})=c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}_{b})\in H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{Z}}).

If
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion-free, then

{\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}})={\mathsf{FSW}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}({\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}).

Proof.

This is well known, so we just sketch the proof.
For each
b\in B
, we let
W_{b}^{\pm}
and
W_{b}^{\pm^{\prime}}
be the spinor bundles for
{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}_{b}
respectively. Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{b}
be the configuration spaces for
{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}_{b}
respectively. Since
H^{2}(X_{b};{\mathbb{Z}})
has no
2
-torsion, it follows from Gompf’s theorem
[
10
, Prop. 1]
that
{\mathfrak{s}}_{b}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}_{b}
are isomorphic: that is, there is a unitary isomorphism
w_{b}\colon W_{b}^{\pm}\to W_{b}^{\pm^{\prime}}
which preserves the Clifford multiplication. Furthermore, modulo gauge transformations,
w_{b}
is unique. Associated to
w_{b}
, there is an isomorphism
{\mathcal{C}}_{b}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{b}^{\prime}
, which we also denote by
w_{b}
. This isomorphism is gauge-equivariant and it takes SW-solutions to SW-solutions.
w_{b}
descends to an isomorphism
\hat{w}_{b}\colon{\mathcal{C}}_{b}/\left\{\text{gauge transformations}\right\}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{b}/\left\{\text{gauge transformations}\right\}
. Although
w_{b}
is only defined up to gauge transformations, the map
\hat{w}_{b}
is unique. Thus we can use
\hat{w}_{b}
to identify the moduli spaces

\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}
and

\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}
, even though there is no isomorphism between the spaces of SW-solutions for
{\mathfrak{s}}
and
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}
. This completes the proof. For a proof in greater generality, see
[
3
, § 2.2]
. See also Lemma 2.7 in
[
20
]
. ∎

4. Seiberg-Witten for symplectic manifolds

The following facts are well-known; see, e.g.,
[
24
, § 3.3]
,
[
23
, Ch. 7]
for details. Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic
4
-manifold,
J
be an
\omega
-compatible almost-complex structure,
and
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)
be the associated Hermitian metric. Denote by
\kappa
the class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{R}})
. The canonical spin
C
structure
{\mathfrak{s}}
has bundles

W^{+}=\Lambda^{0,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,2},\quad W^{-}=\Lambda^{0,1},

and determinant line bundle
K^{*}_{X}

A_{0}
on
K^{*}_{X}
called the Chern connection such that the induced Dirac operator is

{\mathcal{D}}_{A_{0}}\colon\Gamma(\Lambda^{0,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,2})\to\Gamma(\Lambda^{0,1}),\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{A_{0}}=\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{*}\quad\text{up to a positive multiplier.}

See
[
23
, Ch. 3]
and Proposition 1.4.23 in
[
24
]
.

Let
\varepsilon\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
and let
L_{\varepsilon}
be a complex line bundle on
X
with
c_{1}(L_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon
. Let

be the spin

W^{+}=L_{\varepsilon}\oplus\left(\Lambda^{0,2}\otimes L_{\varepsilon}\right),\quad W^{-}=\Lambda^{0,1}\otimes L_{\varepsilon}.

Write

The Seiberg-Witten equations, with Taubes’ perturbing term

(4.2)

are as follows:

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}_{B}\ell+\bar{\partial}_{B}^{*}\beta=0,\\
2\,F^{0,2}_{B}=\dfrac{\ell^{*}\beta}{2},\\
2(F^{+}_{B})^{1,1}=\dfrac{i}{4}\,(|\ell|^{2}-|\beta|^{2}-4\,\rho)\omega.\end{cases}

In this symplectic case, (

(4.4)

For the Chern class of

for the conjugate spin

(4.5)

-{\mathfrak{s}}_{\varepsilon}={\mathfrak{s}}_{K_{X}-\varepsilon},

since if

\varepsilon^{\prime}=K_{X}-\varepsilon
.

Lemma 5
.

Equations (
4.3
) have no reducible solutions for

\rho>0
large enough.

Proof.

Reducible solutions exist only if

\langle\eta\rangle_{g}+2\pi\langle c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{\varepsilon})\rangle_{g}=0.

Substituting (

) for

and multiplying both sides on

\langle-i\,F^{+}_{A_{0}}-\rho\,\omega\rangle\wedge\omega+2\,\pi\,\langle c_{1}({\mathfrak{s}}_{\varepsilon})\rangle\wedge\omega=-\rho\,\omega\wedge\omega+\ldots=0.

But integrating over

gives:

\int_{X}-\rho\,\omega\wedge\omega+\ldots<0\quad\text{for $\rho>0$ sufficiently large. }

This finishes the proof. ∎

]

5. Kronheimer’s homomorphism

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic
4
-manifold and let again
S_{[\omega]}
be the space of symplectic forms deformation equivalent of

\omega
within forms of constant cohomology class. Pick a class
\varepsilon\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
such that:

\langle K^{*}_{X},\varepsilon\rangle+\langle\varepsilon,\varepsilon\rangle=-2,\quad\langle[\omega],\varepsilon\rangle\leqslant 0.

In particular, we have:

Here we sketch the construction of Kronheimer’s homomorphism
[

]

Q_{\varepsilon}\colon\pi_{1}(S_{[\omega]})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}.

Crucially, we need the

Let
\left\{\omega_{t}\right\}_{t\in S^{1}}
be a loop in

based at
\omega

\omega_{t}

\left\{g_{t}\right\}_{t\in S^{1}}
. We regard

Let

given

). Extend

\eta_{t}=-iF_{{A_{0}}_{t}}^{+}-\rho\,\omega_{t}.

We choose

Let
\left\{\eta_{b}\right\}_{b\in D}

g_{b}
-self-dual forms on

that agree with
\eta_{t}

on

In
[

]

\eta_{t}

,

Now the relative Sard-Smale theorem applies:
Perturbing

Q_{\varepsilon}(\left\{\omega_{t}\right\}_{t\in S^{1}})=\text{Cardinality}\left(\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}_{\varepsilon}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}\right)\,\text{mod}\,2.

[

]
for details.

6. The homomorphism
q

Let
(X,\omega)
be a symplectic
4

Lemma 6
.

The composite homomorphism

\pi_{1}\text{{Diff}}(\tilde{X})\to\pi_{1}(S_{\kappa,\lambda})\xrightarrow{q_{\delta-e}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

Proof.

Suppose that there is a family of symplectomorphisms

7. Period domains for complex tori

Most of the material in this section is found
in
[
26
,
9
]
. See also
[
5
]
for an in-depth introduction to the theory of complex tori. We consider a complex torus of dimension
2

X={\mathbb{C}}^{2}/L,

L
being is a lattice in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. By the Kodaira classification theorem, every complex surface diffeomorphic to the real
4
-torus is also biholomorphic to such a quotient. Let
u^{1},u^{2},u^{3},u^{4}
be a basis in
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Identifying
H^{4}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
with
{\mathbb{Z}}
through the natural orientation of
X
as a complex manifold, we have

u^{1}\cup u^{2}\cup u^{3}\cup u^{4}=\text{$1$ or $-1$.}

In the former case, the basis
u^{i}
is said to be admissible. We identify the cohomology
group
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
with the exterior square
\Lambda^{2}(L^{*})
by sending an element
u^{i}\wedge u^{j}
to the cohomology class

u^{i}\cup u^{j}
. The cup product pairing

H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\times H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
makes
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
into a Euclidean lattice, whose intersection form is even and has signature
(3,3)
. Letting
z_{1},z_{2}
be the coordinates on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, the differentials
\text{d}\,z_{1},\text{d}\,z_{2}
is a basis of holomorphic
1
-forms and

\phi_{X}=\text{d}\,z_{1}\wedge\text{d}\,z_{2}
is a holomorphic
2
-form on
X
. Such a holomorphic
2
-from is unique up to a scalar multiple. The class
[\phi_{X}]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{C}})
spans the subspace
H^{2,0}(X)
of
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{C}})
and satisfies the relations:

\langle[\phi_{X}],[\phi_{X}]\rangle=0,\quad\langle[\phi_{X}],[\overline{\phi_{X}}]\rangle=0.

Every complex torus is Kähler. In fact, the Kähler cone
{\mathcal{C}}(X)
of
X
is exactly one of the two connected components of the following set:

\left\{\kappa\in H^{1,1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})\ |\ \langle\kappa,\kappa\rangle>0\right\}.

Indeed, we have a one-to-one correspondence between
the real
(1,1)
-classes on
X
and the real-valued
(1,1)
-forms on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with constant coefficients. If
\omega
is a
(1,1)
-form with constant coefficients, then it descends to a Kähler form on
X
iff it tames the complex structure; i.e.
\omega(v,Jv)>0
for all
v\neq 0
. Together, all such forms sweep out exactly one of the two connected components of the set of all non-degenerate
(1,1)
-forms with constant coefficients. The other connected component is the set of all
(1,1)
-forms taming
-J
.

Given another complex torus
Y
and an isomorphism
f^{*}\colon H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
, we call
f^{*}
an admissible isomorphism if it takes an admissible basis of
H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
to an admissible basis of
H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Because of the canonical isomorphism
H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})=\Lambda^{2}(H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}))
, any isomorphism
f^{*}\colon H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
induces an isomorphism:

\penalty\ {}^{\wedge}\!f^{*}\colon H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}).

Shioda
[
26
]
proved the Torelli theorem for complex
2
-tori. It is convenient for us to formulate his result as follows:

Theorem 7

(Shioda)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two complex tori of dimension 2, and assume that
there is an admissible isomorphism
f^{*}\colon H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
. In order that
f^{*}
is induced by a biholomorphism

f\colon X\to Y
it is necessary and sufficient that
\penalty\ {}^{\wedge}\!f^{*}
takes
H^{2,0}(Y)
to
H^{2,0}(X)
.

Let
E=\Lambda^{2}(L^{*})
and
E_{{\mathbb{R}}}=E\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
,
E_{{\mathbb{C}}}=E\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
. Recalling the canonical identification

(7.1)

E=H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}),\quad E_{{\mathbb{C}}}=H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{C}}),

and letting
\langle\phantom{\cdot},\phantom{\cdot}\rangle
denote the cup product pairing on
E
, we introduce the period domain:

\Phi=\left\{\varphi\in E_{{\mathbb{C}}}\ |\ \langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle=0,\ \langle\varphi,\bar{\varphi}\rangle>0\right\}/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{5},

which is an open domain in a nonsingular quadric of dimension
4
.
Under (
7.1
), the class
[\phi_{X}]\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{C}})
gives a point in
\Phi
, called the period of
X
. More generally, if
X
varies in a complex-analytic family

p\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to S,\quad X_{s}=p^{-1}(s),\ s\in S\quad\text{with $X=X_{s_{0}}$},

and there is given an isomorphism
f^{*}\colon H^{1}(X_{s};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(X_{s_{0}};{\mathbb{Z}})
, then, by using
the Hodge decomposition for each
s\in S
,

H^{2}(X_{s};{\mathbb{C}})=H^{2,0}(X_{s};{\mathbb{C}})+H^{1,1}(X_{s};{\mathbb{C}})+H^{0,2}(X_{s};{\mathbb{C}}),

and the natural isomorphisms

\penalty\ {}^{\wedge}\!f^{*}\colon H^{2}(X_{s};{\mathbb{C}})\cong H^{2}(X_{s_{0}};{\mathbb{C}})=E_{{\mathbb{C}}},

we define a period mapping

\tau_{S}\colon S\to\Phi
by setting

\tau_{S}(s)=\penalty\ ^{\wedge}\!f^{*}\left(H^{2,0}(X_{s})\right).

Let now
L
be an abstract abelian group of rank
4
,
and let a basis
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
in

be fixed. Let
E
,
E_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, and

be as before. Let

such that

is compact and such

gives the positive orientation. Define

{\mathcal{M}}=R/\sim,\quad\text{where $r\sim r^{\prime}$ iff $r=A\circ r^{\prime}$ for some $A\in\boldsymbol{Gl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})$};

{\mathcal{X}}=R\times{\mathbb{C}}^{2}/\sim,\quad\text{where
$(r,z)\sim(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})$ iff $r=A\circ r^{\prime}$, $z=A\left(z^{\prime}+r^{\prime}(\ell)\right)$ for some $A\in\boldsymbol{Gl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})$, $\ell\in L$}.

Forgetting

with fiber a complex

-torus. Each fiber

,

[

(Shioda)

\tau_{{\mathcal{M}}}\colon{\mathcal{M}}\to\Phi

Thus we may identify
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\Phi

for
\tau_{{\mathcal{M}}}(b)

a fine moduli space of complex tori with prescribed marking. Observe that

Fix a class

with

Let

8. Generic tori

The following material is found in
[
17
,
9
]
. A complex
2
-torus

is said to generic if

\langle[\phi_{X}],a\rangle\neq 0\quad\text{for all $a\in H^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})$, $a\neq 0$.}

The term “generic” is justified
by the following fact. Let
{\mathcal{M}}^{gen}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
be defined as follows:

{\mathcal{M}}^{gen}=\left\{b\in{\mathcal{M}}\ |\ \langle\phi_{b},a\rangle\neq 0\ \text{for all $a\in E$, $a\neq 0$}\right\}.

Then for each

,

is generic. Since
{\mathcal{M}}^{gen}
is a complement to a countable union of proper algebraic subvarieties, it is an open and dense subset of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

We recall the following result of Demailly and Paun, which we state for the case of surfaces. See Theorem 0.1 in
[
8
]
for the full result.

Theorem 9

(Demailly-Paun,
[
8
]
)
.

Let

be a compact Kähler surface. The Kähler cone of
{\mathcal{C}}(X)
of

is one of the connected components of the set of all real
(1,1)
-classes

\kappa
such that
\langle\kappa,\kappa\rangle>0
and such that

\langle\kappa,[D]\rangle>0
for every effective divisor

on

.

Let again
\tilde{X}
be a blowup of

, and let

be the class of the exceptional
(-1)
-curve. Letting
\langle e\rangle
be the subspace of

generated by

H^{1,1}(\tilde{X};{\mathbb{R}})=H^{1,1}(X;{\mathbb{R}})\oplus\langle e\rangle
. The following statement is found in
[

]
:

Lemma 7
.

If

is generic, then
{\mathcal{C}}(\tilde{X})
is one of the connected component of the following set:

\left\{\kappa-\lambda\,e\in H^{1,1}(\tilde{X};{\mathbb{R}})\ |\ \sqrt{\langle\kappa,\kappa\rangle}>\lambda>0\right\}.

This follows upon applying Theorem

See Section called “The Riemann Conditions” in

Lemma 10

If
\kappa
is non-resonant, then

\delta\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.69444pt\kern-4.18054pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.36115pt\kern-3.68057pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}H^{1,1}(\tilde{X}_{b};{\mathbb{R}})

Lemma 17
.

q_{\delta_{0}-e}(\gamma_{\delta_{0}})=1
.

Proof.

If
b\in D-\left\{b_{0}\right\}
, then

\delta_{0}-e\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.69444pt\kern-4.18054pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.36115pt\kern-3.68057pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}H^{1,1}(\tilde{X}_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
. On the other hand, we have

\delta_{0}-e\in H^{1,1}(\tilde{X}_{b_{0}};{\mathbb{R}})
. Indeed,

\delta_{0}-e\in H^{1,1}(\tilde{X}_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
if and only if

\delta_{0}\in H^{1,1}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
. But

\delta_{0}\in H^{1,1}(X_{b};{\mathbb{R}})
if and only if

\langle\delta_{0},\phi_{b}\rangle=0
. This is equivalent to
\phi_{b}\in D\cap H_{\delta_{0}}
. But
D
is chosen in such a way that
D\cap H_{\delta_{0}}=\left\{\phi_{b_{0}}\right\}
.

We saw in Lemma
11
that
\delta_{0}-e
is represented by a smooth irreducible elliptic curve of self-intersection
(-1)
. By positivity of intersections, no other holomorphic curve represeting
\delta_{0}-e
can exist. Thus, by Theorem
6
, the moduli space

\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}_{\delta_{0}-e}}_{(g_{b},\eta_{b})}
consists of a single point, which lies over
b_{0}\in D
. In
[
27
, § 6]
, a sufficient condition is found that guarantees that this point is tranversally cut out; namely, if consider the infinitesimal variation of Hodge structures (
[
12
]
):

\Omega_{*}\colon T_{D}\to\text{Hom}\,(H^{1,1},H^{0,2}),\quad\text{where $H^{p,q}=H^{p,q}(\tilde{X}_{b_{0}};{\mathbb{C}})$.}

then
\mathfrak{M}^{{\mathfrak{s}}_{\delta_{0}-e}}_{g_{b},\eta_{b}}
is tranversally cut out, provided

\delta_{0}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.33334pt\kern-4.99998pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.69444pt\kern-4.18054pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.36115pt\kern-3.68057pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\in}}}\text{ker}\,\Omega_{*}(\partial_{t}),\quad\text{where
$\partial_{t}$ is a generator for $T_{D}$.}

But this is precisely the condition that
D
is transverse to
H_{\delta_{0}}
, a condition which is indeed met. It follows then that
Q^{+}_{\delta_{0}-e}(\gamma_{\delta_{0}})=1
.

Let us deform
\Omega_{b}
in exactly the same way as in Lemma
15
. We then have:

\langle[\Omega_{b}],2\,e-\delta\rangle\leqslant 0.

Then, using Theorem
5
, we get
Q^{-}_{\delta_{0}-e}(\gamma_{\delta_{0}})=0
, and hence also
q_{\delta_{0}-e}(\gamma_{\delta_{0}})=1
. ∎

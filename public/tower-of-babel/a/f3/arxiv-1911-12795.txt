Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Methods for 1D Rosenau Equation
P. Danumjaya K. Balaje

Abstract
In this paper, discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods are applied to one dimensional Rosenau equation. Theoretical results including consistency, a priori bounds and optimal error estimates are established for both semidiscrete and fully discrete schemes. Numerical experiments are performed to validate the theoretical results. The decay estimates are verified numerically for the Rosenau equation. Key words. Rosenau equation, discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods (DGFEM), semidiscrete DGFEM, completely discrete DGFEM, optimal error estimates, decay estimates. AMS Subject Classification: 65M60, 65M12

Introduction

Consider the one dimensional Rosenau equation:

u_{t}+u_{xxxxt}=f(u)_{x},\quad(x,t)\in(a,b)\times(0,T]

(1.1)

with initial condition

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in(a,b),\\

(1.2)

and the boundary conditions

\displaystyle u(a,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(b,t)=0,

\displaystyle u_{x}(a,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{x}(b,t)=0,

, here

is a real
constant and

is a positive integer.

The Rosenau equation is an example of a nonlinear partial differential
equation, which governs
the dynamics of dense discrete systems and models wave propagation in
nonlinear dispersive media.

Recently, several numerical techniques
like conforming finite element methods, mixed finite element methods,
orthogonal cubic spline collocation methods, etc., were proposed to
find the approximate solution of Rosenau equation.
The different conforming finite element techniques are used to
approximate the solution of Rosenau equation needs
C^{1}
-interelement
continuity condition and mixed finite element formulations
are required
C^{0}
-continuity condition. In this article discontinuous
Galerkin finite element methods are used to approximate the solution.

The well-posedness results of (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) was
proved by Park
[
16
]
and Atouani
et al.
in
[
2
]
.
Earlier, some numerical methods were proposed to solve the Rosenau
equation (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) using finite difference methods
by Chung
[
6
]
, conservative difference schemes by Hu and Zheng

[
11
]
and Atouni and Omrani
[
3
]
.
Finite element Galerkin method was used by

[
2
,
7
]
, a second order splitting combined with orthogonal
cubic spline collocation method was used by Manickam
et al.

[
13
]
and Chung
and Pani in
[
5
]
constructed
a
C^{1}
-conforming finite element method for the Rosenau equation
(
1.1
)-(
1.3
) in
two-space dimensions.

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in discontinuous
Galerkin finite element methods because of their flexibility in
approximating globally rough solutions and their potential for error
control and mesh adaptation.

Recently, a cGdG method was proposed by Choo.
et. al

in
[
8
]
. A subdomain finite element method using sextic
b-spline was proposed by Battal and Turgut in
[
12
]
. But constructing

and hence discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods can be used to

[

.

In this paper, we introduce discontinuous Galerkin finite element
methods (DGFEM) in space to solve the one dimensional Rosenau
equation (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). Comparitive to existing methods
our proposed method require less regularity.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we derive the
discontinuous weak formulation of the Rosenau equation. In Section 3,
we discuss the
a priori
bounds and optimal error estimates
for the semidiscrete problem. In Section 4, we discretize the
semidiscrete problem in the temporal direction using a backward Euler
method and discuss the
a priori

validate the theoretical results.

Throughout this paper,
C

3
Semidiscrete DGFEM

In this section, we discuss the
a priori
bounds and optimal
error estimates for the semidiscrete Galerkin method.

We define a finite dimensional subspace
D_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
of

H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}),\;s>7/2
as

D_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})=\left\{v\in L^{2}(a,b):v|_{I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n}),\;\;I_{n}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\right\}.

The weak formulation for the semidiscrete Galerkin method is to find

u^{h}(t)\in D_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
such that

\displaystyle(u^{h}_{t},\chi)+B(u^{h}_{t},\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(f(u^{h})_{x},\chi\right),\;\;\mbox{for
all}\;\;\chi\in D_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h}),

(3.1)

\displaystyle u^{h}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{h}_{0},

(3.2)

where
u_{0}^{h}
is an appropriate approximation of
u_{0}
which will be
defined later.

3.1
A priori
Bounds

In this sub-section, we derive the
a priori
bounds.

Define the energy norm

||u||_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\int_{x_{n}}^{x_{n+1}}u_{xx}^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{h^{\beta}}|[u(x_{n})]|^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{h}|[u_{x}(x_{n})]|^{2}.

We note from
[
9
]
that
B(.,.)
is coercive with respect to the energy
norm, i.e.,

B(v,v)\geq C||v||_{\mathcal{E}}^{2},\quad v\in D^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h}),

for sufficiently large values of
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
.

Observe that (
3.1
) yields a system of non-linear ordinary
differential equations and the existence and uniqueness of the solution can be
guaranteed locally using the Picard’s theorem. To obtain existence and
uniqueness globally, we use continuation arguments and hence we need the
following
a priori
bounds.

Theorem
3.1
.

Let
u^{h}(t)
be a solution to (
3.1
) and assume that
f^{\prime}
is
bounded. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|u^{h}(t)\|+\|u^{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq C(\|u_{0}^{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}}).

(3.3)

Proof.

On setting
\chi=u^{h}
in (
3.1
), we obtain

(u^{h}_{t},u^{h})+B(u^{h}_{t},u^{h})=(f(u^{h})_{x},u^{h}).

(3.4)

We rewrite the equation (
3.4
) as

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u^{h}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}B(u^{h}(t),u^{h}(t))=(f^{\prime}(u^{h})u^{h}_{x},u^{h}).

Integrating from
0
to
t
, we obtain

\|u^{h}(t)\|^{2}+B(u^{h}(t),u^{h}(t))=\|u^{h}(0)\|^{2}+B(u^{h}(0),u^{h}(0))+\int_{0}^{t}(f^{\prime}(u^{h})u^{h}_{x},u^{h})\,ds.

(3.5)

On using the coercivity of
B(u^{h}(t),u^{h}(t))
and the
boundedness of
f^{\prime}
, we arrive at

\|u^{h}(t)\|^{2}+C\|u^{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\|u^{h}(0)\|^{2}+B(u^{h}(0),u^{h}(0))+C\int_{0}^{t}(u^{h}_{x},u^{h})\,ds.

(3.6)

Using the Cauchy Schwarz inequality and the
Poincaré inequality on the right hand side of (
3.6
), we
obtain

\|u^{h}(t)\|^{2}+C\|u^{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq C(\|u^{h}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}})+\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{h}(s)\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\,ds.

(3.7)

An application of Gronwall’s inequality yields the desired
a
priori
bound for
u^{h}(t)
.

3.2
Error Estimates in the energy and
L^{2}
-norm

In this subsection, we derive the optimal error estimates in energy
and
L^{2}
-norm.

Often a direct comparison between
u
and
u^{h}
does
not yield optimal rate of convergence. Therefore, there is a need to introduce an
appropriate auxiliary or intermediate function
\tilde{u}
so that the
optimal estimate of
u-\tilde{u}
is easy to obtain and the
comparision between
u^{h}
and
\tilde{u}
yields a sharper estimate
which leads to optimal rate of convergence for

. In literature, Wheeler
[
20
]

for the first time introduced this technique in the context of
parabolic problem. Following Wheeler
[
20
]
, we introduce
\tilde{u}
be an
auxiliary projection of
u
defined by

B(u-\tilde{u},\chi)=0,\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\chi\in D^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h}).

(3.8)

Now set the error
e=u-u^{h}
and split as follows:

e=u-\tilde{u}-\left(u^{h}-\tilde{u}\right)=\eta-\theta
,
where
\eta=u-\tilde{u}
and
\theta=u^{h}-\tilde{u}
.
Below, we state some error estimates for
\eta=u-\tilde{u}
and its temporal
derivative.

Lemma
3.1
.

For
t\in(0,T]

then there exists a positive constant
C

such that the following error estimates for
\eta

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{l}\eta}{\partial t^{l}}\right\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\min(k+1,s)-2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}|||\frac{\partial^{m}u}{\partial t^{m}}|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}\right),

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{l}\eta}{\partial t^{l}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\min(k+1,s)}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{l}|||\frac{\partial^{m}u}{\partial t^{m}}|||\right),\quad l=0,1.

Proof.

We split
\eta
as follows:

\eta=u-\tilde{u}=\left(u-\bar{u}\right)-\left(\tilde{u}-\bar{u}\right)=\rho-\xi,

where
\rho=u-\bar{u}
,
\xi=\tilde{u}-\bar{u}
and

\bar{u}
is an interpolant of
u
satisfying good approximation
properties. Now from (
3.8
), we have

B(\xi,\chi)=B(\rho,\chi).

(3.9)

We note that
\rho
satisfies the following approximation
property
[
17
]

\|\rho\|_{H^{q}(I_{n})}\leq Ch^{\min(k+1,s)-q}\|u\|_{H^{s}(I_{n})}.

Set
\chi=\xi
in
(
3.9
) to obtain

B(\xi,\xi)=B(\rho,\xi).

A use of coercivity of
B(.,.)
and the assumption that
\bar{u}
is a
sufficiently smooth interpolant of

C\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{x_{n}}^{x_{n+1}}\rho_{xx}\xi_{xx}\,dx+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\rho_{xxx}(x_{n})\}[\xi(x_{n})]-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\rho_{xx}(x_{n})\}[\xi_{x}(x_{n})].

(3.10)

Now we estimate the first term as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{x_{n}}^{x_{n+1}}\rho_{xx}\xi_{xx}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|||\rho_{xx}|||\,|||\xi_{xx}|||\leq\frac{1}{6C}\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{6}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)-4}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}+\frac{C}{6}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

Estimating the second term using Hölder’s inequality, trace

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\rho_{xxx}(x_{n})\}[\xi(x_{n})]\leq Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)-6+\beta-1}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}+\frac{C}{6}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

(3.12)

Similarly the last term can be estimated as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\rho_{xx}(x_{n})\}[\xi_{x}(x_{n})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)-6+\beta-1}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}+\frac{C}{6}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

(3.13)

Combining (
3.11
)-(
3.13
), we obtain
the following bound for
\xi

\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq Ch^{\min(k+1,s)-2}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}.

(3.14)

Now using
\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}+\|\rho\|_{\mathcal{E}}
, we obtain the energy norm estimate
for
\eta
. For the
L^{2}
-estimate of
\eta

Nitsché duality argument. Consider the dual problem

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}\phi}{dx^{4}}=\eta,\;\;x\in(a,b),

\displaystyle\phi(a)=\phi(b)=0,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(a)=\phi^{\prime}(b)=0.\qquad

We note that
\phi
satisfies the regularity condition
\|\phi\|_{H^{4}}\leq C\|\eta\|
. Consider

(\eta,\eta)=(\eta,\phi_{xxxx})=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{x_{n}}^{x_{n+1}}\phi_{xx}\eta_{xx}\,dx+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\phi_{xxx}(x_{n})\}[\eta(x_{n})]-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{\phi_{xx}(x_{n})\}[\eta_{x}(x_{n})].

Since
B(\eta,\chi)=0\;\;\forall\chi\in D^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
,
we
can write

\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}=(\eta,\eta)-B(\eta,\tilde{\phi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\int_{x_{n}}^{x_{n+1}}(\phi-\tilde{\phi})_{xx}\eta_{xx}\,dx+\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{(\phi-\tilde{\phi})_{xxx}(x_{n})\}[\eta(x_{n})]

(3.15)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{(\phi-\tilde{\phi})_{xx}(x_{n})\}[\eta_{x}(x_{n})],

where
\tilde{\phi}
is a continuous interpolant of
\phi
and
satisfies the approximation property:

\|\phi-\tilde{\phi}\|_{H^{q}}\leq Ch^{s-q}\|\phi\|_{H^{s}}.

(3.16)

We use the approximation property (
3.16
),
the energy norm estimate for
\eta
and the regularity result to
bound each term on the right hand side of (
3.15
) and obtain
the estimate
for
\|\eta\|
as:

\|\eta\|\leq Ch^{\min(k+1,s)}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}.

For the estimates of the temporal derivative of
\eta
, we
differentiate (
3.8
) with respect to
t
and repeat the
arguments. Hence, it completes the rest of the proof.
∎

The following Lemma is useful to prove the error estimates:

Lemma
3.2
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n})
where
I_{n}=(x_{n},x_{n+1})\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
h

such that,

Ch_{n}^{-4}\|v\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}^{2}\leq\|v_{xx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{n})},

where
h_{n}=\left(x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right)
.

Proof.

We define the reference element
\hat{I_{n}}
as

\hat{I_{n}}=\left\{\left(\frac{1}{h_{n}}\right)x,\quad x\in I_{n}\right\}.

Since
v\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n})
, we have the following relation
(refer
[
4
]
) for the norms in the reference element and the
interval
I_{n}

|\hat{v}|_{H^{r}(\hat{I_{n}})}=h_{n}^{r-\frac{d}{2}}|v|_{H^{r}(I_{n})}.

In one space dimension, i.e.,
d=1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{I_{n}})}&=&h_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})},\;\;\mbox{and}\\
|\hat{v}|_{H^{2}(\hat{I_{n}})}&=&h_{n}^{\frac{3}{2}}|v|_{H^{2}(I_{n})}.\end{aligned}

(3.17)

By the equivalence of norm (refer
[
4
]
), we have

C_{0}\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{I_{n}})}\leq\|\hat{v}_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{I_{n}})}\leq C_{1}\|\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{I_{n}})}.

(3.18)

Now from (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), we obtain

C_{0}h_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}\leq h_{n}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}.

Rearranging the terms and squaring on both sides, we obtain the
desired estimate.
∎

To obtain the error estimates, we subtract
(
3.1
) from (
2.9
) and using the auxiliary projection
(
3.8
), we obtain the following error equation

(\theta_{t},\chi)+B(\theta_{t},\chi)=(\eta_{t},\chi)+(f(u^{h})_{x}-f(u)_{x},\chi).

(3.19)

Now we state and prove the following theorem.

Theorem
3.2
.

Let
u^{h}(t)
and
u(t)
be the solutions of (
3.1
) and
(
2.9
), respectively. Let
u^{h}_{0}
be the
elliptic projection of
u_{0}
, i.e.,
u^{h}_{0}=\tilde{u}(0)
. Then for

s>7/2
and
there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
h
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u^{h}(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\min(k+1,s)-2}\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))},

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u^{h}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\min(k+1,s)}\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}.

Proof.

Setting
\chi=\theta(t)
in (
3.19
), we obtain

(\theta_{t},\theta)+B(\theta_{t},\theta)=(\eta_{t},\theta)+(f(u^{h})_{x}-f(u)_{x},\theta).

(3.20)

Now, we write equation (
3.20
) as

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\theta(t)\|^{2}+B(\theta(t),\theta(t))\right)=(\eta_{t},\theta)+(f(u^{h})_{x}-f(u)_{x},\theta).

Integrating with respect to
t
from
0
to
T
and noting that

\theta(0)=0
, we obtain

\|\theta(t)\|^{2}+B(\theta(t),\theta(t))=\int_{0}^{T}(\eta_{t},\theta)\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}(f(u^{h})_{x}-f(u)_{x},\theta)\,ds.

(3.21)

We use integration by parts on the nonlinear term to obtain,

\left((f(u^{h})-f(u))_{x},\theta\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{f(u^{h})-f(u)\}[\theta(x_{n})]+[f(u^{h})-f(u)]\{\theta(x_{n})\}+\left(f(u)-f(u^{h}),\theta_{x}\right).

(3.22)

Using the Cauchy Schwarz’s and Young’s inequality, we bound the last term of
(
3.22
) as

\left(f(u)-f(u^{h}),\theta_{x}\right)\leq C(\|\eta\|^{2}+\|\theta\|^{2}+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}).

Now for the first term in (
3.22
), we use Hölder’s
inequality to write

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{f(u^{h})-f(u)\}[\theta(x_{n})]\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}|\{f(u^{h})-f(u)\}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}|[\theta(x_{n})]|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.23)

As earlier in (
3.12
), we use the penalty term to write
(
3.23
) as

\sum_{n=0}^{N}\{f(u^{h})-f(u)\}[\theta(x_{n})]\leq Ch^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\|e\|\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq C\|e\|\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}},\quad\text{since}\;\;\beta\geq 3.

A similar bound for the second term can be obtained as
follows. Using the Hölder’s inequality, we write

\sum_{n=0}^{N}[f(u^{h})-f(u)]\{\theta(x_{n})\}\leq\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}h^{\beta}|[f(u^{h})-f(u)]|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}h^{-\beta}|\{\theta(x_{n})\}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.24)

Using the trace inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}[f(u^{h})-f(u)]\{\theta(x_{n})\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(Ch^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\|e\|\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}h^{-\beta-1}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{\frac{\beta-1}{2}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}h^{-4+(3-\beta)}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e\|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N}h^{-4}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(I_{n})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e\|\;\;\leq\;\;C\|e\|\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}},

where the last step is obtained by using
Lemma

3.2
. Using the triangle
inequality together with Young’s inequality we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\left((f(u^{h})-f(u))_{x},\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\theta\|^{2}+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2})+Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}.

Now using the coercivity of
B(.,.)
and estimate of the
nonlinear term in (
3.21
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\theta\|^{2}+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}\int_{0}^{T}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}\int_{0}^{T}|||u_{t}|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}\,ds

(3.25)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(\|\theta\|^{2}+\|\theta\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2})\,ds.

An application of Gronwall’s inequality yields an estimate for

\theta
. We then use the triangle inequality to obtain the
estimates for
e=u-u^{h}
. The estimates are optimal in

L^{2}
-norm if
\beta\geq 3
.
∎

4
Fully Discrete DGFEM

In this section, we derive a fully discrete DGFEM and establish

a priori
bounds
along with optimal error estimates.

Backward Euler discretization
:
Let
\Delta t
denote the size of time discretization. Divide
[0,T]

by

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots<t_{M-1}<t_{M}=T

where

t_{i+1}=t_{i}+\Delta t,\quad i=0,1,\dots,M-1
and
\Delta t=\frac{T}{M}
. Let
u(t_{n})=u^{n}
and approximate
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
by using backward Euler difference formula as
:

\partial_{t}u^{n}=\frac{u^{n}-u^{n-1}}{\Delta t}.

Now,
the fully discrete discontinuous Galerkin finite element method is
given as follows:

\left(\partial_{t}U^{n+1},\chi\right)+B(\partial_{t}U^{n+1},\chi)=\left(f(U^{n+1}),\chi\right),\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\chi\in D^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h}),

(4.1)

U^{0}=U_{0},

where
U^{n}
is the fully discrete approximation of

u(x,t_{n})
.

4.1
A priori
bounds

In this sub-section, we prove an
a priori
bound for the fully
discrete DGFEM.

Theorem
4.1
.

Let
U^{n}
be a solution to (
4.1
) and assume that
f^{\prime}
is
bounded. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|U^{n}\|+\|U^{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq C(\|U_{0}\|).

(4.2)

Proof.

Set
\chi=U^{n+1}
in (
4.1
) to obtain

\left(\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\Delta t},U^{n+1}\right)+B\left(\frac{U^{n+1}-U^{n}}{\Delta t},U^{n+1}\right)=\left(f(U^{n+1})_{x},U^{n+1}\right).

Multiplying by
\Delta t
throughout, we arrive at

\left(U^{n+1}-U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)+B\left(U^{n+1}-U^{n},U^{n+1}\right)=\Delta t\left(f(U^{n+1})_{x},U^{n+1}\right).

(4.3)

Using the fact that for any two real numbers
x
and
y
, we have

\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-y^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}-y^{2}+(x-y)^{2})=(x-y)x.

We rewrite the equation (
4.3
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{n}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(B\left(U^{n+1},U^{n+1}\right)-B(U^{n},U^{n})\right)\leq\Delta t\left(f(U^{n+1})_{x},U^{n+1}\right).

(4.4)

Using the Cauchy Schwarz’s inequality, Poincare inequality and Young’s
inequality with the bound on
f^{\prime}(u)
, we obtain the
following inequality from (
4.4
)

\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{n}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(B(U^{n+1},U^{n+1})-B(U^{n},U^{n})\right)\leq C\Delta t\left(\frac{\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert^{2}}{2}\right).

Summing over
n=0,1,2,\dots,J-1
and using the coercivity of
B(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{J}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{J}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert U^{J}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}B(U^{0},U^{0})+C\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{J-1}\left(\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lVert U^{n+1}\rVert^{2}\right).

Rearranging the terms and applying the discrete Gronwall Inequality,
we obtain the desired
a priori
bound on
U^{J}
.
∎

4.2
Error Estimates

In this sub-section, we prove the optimal error estimates for the fully discrete
discontinuous Galerkin method.

Subtracting equation (
4.1
) from (
2.9
) and using the
auxiliary projection (
3.8
), we obtain the error equation as

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\theta^{n+1},\chi\right)+B(\partial_{t}\theta^{n+1},\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\eta^{n+1},\chi\right)+\left((f(U^{n+1})_{x}-f(u^{n+1})_{x}),\chi\right)

(4.5)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\sigma^{n+1},\chi\right)+B(\sigma^{n+1},\chi).

where
\sigma^{n+1}=u_{t}^{n+1}-\partial_{t}U^{n+1}
. Before we derive
the error estimate, we state and prove the following Lemma.

Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\sigma^{n}=u_{t}^{n}-\partial_{t}U^{n}
. Then the following
holds

\lVert\sigma^{n}\rVert^{2}\leq\Delta t\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\lVert u_{tt}(s)\rVert^{2}ds.

Proof.

Consider
I=\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(s-t_{n-1})u_{tt}(s)\>ds
. Using integration by parts, we see that
I=\Delta t\;\sigma^{n}
. A use of the Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\lVert\sigma^{n}\rVert^{2}\leq\Delta t\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\lVert u_{tt}(s)\rVert^{2}ds
.
The same inequality can be proved in the energy norm as well.
∎

Theorem
4.2
.

Let
U^{0}=\tilde{u}(0)
so that
\theta^{0}=0
. Then there exists a
positive constant
C
independent of
h
and
\Delta t
such that,

\displaystyle\lVert u(t_{n})-U^{n}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(h^{\min(k+1,s)-2}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T,H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}+\Delta t\,|||u_{tt}|||_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}\right),

\displaystyle\lVert u(t_{n})-U^{n}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(h^{\min(k+1,s)}\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T,H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}+\Delta t\,|||u_{tt}|||_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}\right).

(4.6)

Proof.

Set
\chi=\theta^{n+1}
in (
4.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\theta^{n+1}-\theta^{n},\theta^{n+1}\right)+B(\theta^{n+1}-\theta^{n},\theta^{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{n+1}-\eta^{n},\theta^{n+1}\right)+\Delta t\left(f(U^{n+1})_{x}-f(u^{n+1})_{x},\theta^{n+1}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Delta t\left(\sigma^{n+1},\theta^{n+1}\right)+\Delta tB(\sigma^{n+1},\theta^{n+1}).

Using Cauchy Schwarz’s Inequality and
constructing upper bounds similar to the semidiscrete case on the
right hand side of (
4.7
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\theta^{n+1}\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\theta^{n}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(B(\theta^{n+1},\theta^{n+1})-B(\theta^{n},\theta^{n})\right)\leq Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\|u_{t}\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\,Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)+\beta-3}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}+C\Delta t\left(\lVert\theta^{n+1}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\theta^{n+1}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma^{n+1}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\sigma^{n+1}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right).

(4.8)

Sum over
n=0,1,2,\dots,M-1
on both sides of (
4.8
) and
using
Lemma

4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\theta^{M}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\theta^{M}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}|||u|||_{H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h})}^{2}+Ch^{2\min(k+1,s)}\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{t}\|^{2}\,ds

(4.9)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}\left(\lVert\theta^{n+1}\rVert+\lVert\theta^{n+1}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\right)+C\Delta t^{2}\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}.

Using the discrete Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain the estimate
for
\theta^{M}
as

\lVert\theta^{M}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\theta^{M}\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq C\left(h^{2\min(k+1,s)}\|u\|_{H^{1}(0,T,H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}^{2}+\Delta t^{2}\|u_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathcal{E}_{h}))}\right),

provided
\beta\geq 3
.
Using the triangle inequality, we can prove the required estimate for

\lVert e\rVert_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\lVert e\rVert
.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived
a priori
bounds and optimal error
estimates for the semidiscrete problem. Next, we discretized the
semidiscrete problem in the temporal direction using a backward Euler
method, and derived
a priori
bounds and optimal error
estimates. We have validated the theoretical results by performing
some numerical experiments. Compared to the existing results, our
method requires less regularity of the original problem.

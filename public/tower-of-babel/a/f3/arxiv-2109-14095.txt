Neuron Growth Control by PDE Backstepping: Axon Length Regulation by Tubulin Flux Actuation in Soma
Cenk Demir, Shumon Koga, Miroslav Krstic

Abstract
In this work, stabilization of an axonal growth in a neuron associated with the dynamics of tubulin concentration is proposed by designing a boundary control. The dynamics are given by a parabolic Partial Differential Equation (PDE) of the tubulin concentration, with a spatial domain of the axon’s length governed by an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) coupled with the tubulin concentration in the growth cone. We propose a novel backstepping method for the coupled PDE-ODE dynamics with a moving boundary, and design a control law for the tubulin concentration flux in the soma. Through employing the Lyapunov analysis to a nonlinear target system, we prove a local exponential stability of the closed-loop system under the proposed control law in the spatial H 1 H_{1} -norm.

Boundary control of PDEs has been intensively developed by the method of “backstepping” in the last few decades for various kinds of systems

Several researchers have tackled stability analysis for nonlinear PDE systems under the backstepping design of a boundary control by restricting the region of attraction in a local sense. For instance,

In this paper, we develop a boundary control for a coupled PDE-ODE system with a moving boundary which models the dynamics of tubulin concentration and axon growth. First, we present a steady-state solution of the tubulin concentration for a given constant axon length, and obtain a reference error system to be stabilized at zero states. Next, we apply linearization to the reference error system to deal with the algebraic nonlinearity. Then, a backstepping transformation is employed to the linearized reference error dynamics. By solving the gain kernel equations that are derived from backstepping transformation, the control law is obtained. Finally, we prove local exponential stability by applying the Lyapunov method to the nonlinear target system, which ensures the local stability of the original PDE-ODE system of the axon growth model.

, and
L_{3}
such that
F(0,X(t))^{2}\leq L_{1}X^{\top}X
,
F(l(t),X(t))^{2}\leq L_{1}X^{\top}X
,
\int_{0}^{l(t)}F_{x}(x,X(t))^{2}dx\leq L_{2}X^{\top}X
,
\int_{0}^{l(t)}F(x,X(t))^{2}dx\leq L_{3}X^{\top}X
. In addition,

\dot{l}(t)
can be rewritten as
\dot{l}(t)=r_{\rm g}e_{1}X
.
By using these relations and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality, one can show that

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha V+r_{\rm g}\left(\frac{L_{1}(1+\gamma)+1}{2d_{2}\lambda_{\rm min}(P)}+(1+L_{3}+d_{1}L_{2})\right)V^{3/2}.

(69)

Thus, there exists
\beta>0
such that Lemma 1 holds.
∎

IV-D

Guaranteeing the conditions for all time

Next, we prove important lemmas to conclude with Theorem 1 ensuring the local stability of the closed-loop system.

Lemma 2
.

There exists a positive constant
M_{1}>0
such that in the region
\Omega_{1}:=\{(w,X)\in H_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|V(t)<M_{1}\}
the conditions (
50
) and (
51
) are satisfied.

Proof.

By using (
14
) and (
5
),
X(t)
can be described as

X(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\dot{l}(t)}{r_{\rm g}}&l(t)-l_{\rm s}\end{array}\right]^{\top}
.
Thus, for some
r>0
, if
|X|<r
then both the following two inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\dot{l}(t)}{r_{\rm g}}\right|<r,\ \ \big|l(t)-l_{\rm s}\big|<r.

(70)

The former inequality tells that if
r<\frac{\bar{v}}{r_{\rm g}}
then the condition (
50
) holds. Moreover, the latter inequality yields
-r+l_{\rm s}<l(t)<r+l_{\rm s}
, and thus if both
r<l_{\rm s}
and
r<\bar{l}-l_{\rm s}
hold, then the condition (
51
) holds.
Therefore, the constant
r>0
is chosen as

r=\min\left\{\frac{\bar{v}}{r_{\rm g}},l_{\rm s},\bar{l}-l_{\rm s}\right\}.

Since
|X|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\rm min}(P)}X^{\top}PX\leq\frac{d_{2}}{\lambda_{\rm min}(P)}V
, we deduce that by setting
M_{1}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm min}(P)}{d_{2}}r^{2}
, if
V(t)<M_{1}
holds then
|X|<r
and thus the conditions (
50
) and (
51
) are satisfied, by which we can conclude Lemma 2.
∎

Lemma 3
.

There exists a positive constant
M>0
such that if
V(0)<M
then the conditions (
50
) and (
51
) are satisfied and the following norm estimate holds:

\displaystyle V(t)\leq V(0)\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}t\right).

(71)

Proof.

For a positive constant
M>0
, let
\Omega:=\{(w,X)\in H_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}|V(t)<M\}
. By Lemma 2, it is easily shown that if
M\leq M_{1}
then
\Omega\subset\Omega_{1}
, and thus the conditions (
50
) and (
51
) are satisfied in the region
\Omega
. Thus, by Lemma 1, the norm estimate (
61
) holds. Moreover, by setting
M\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}
, we can see that applying
V(t)<M
to (
61
) leads to

\displaystyle\dot{V}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{2}V,

(72)

by which the norm estimate (
71
) is deduced. Since (
71
) is a monotonically decreasing function in time, by setting
M=\min\{M_{1},\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}\}

\Omega
is shown to be an invariant set. Thus, if
V(0)<M

V(t)<M
for all

, and one can conclude with Lemma 3.
∎

Finally, owing to Lemma 3, and the equivalent norm estimate in the

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, a boundary feedback control for an axonal growth model governed by a coupled PDE-ODE with a moving boundary is studied. The backstepping transformation is utilized for the original system to convert it to the target system which has a stable system matrix in ODE. The gain kernel solutions in the transformation are obtained, and the boundary feedback control law is designed explicitly. Using Lyapunov’s method, we first prove that the target system is locally exponentially stable in the spatial
H_{1}
-norm and then we prove local stability of
the original coupled PDE-ODE system of axonal growth model. Showing the local stability without applying linearization will be studied in future.

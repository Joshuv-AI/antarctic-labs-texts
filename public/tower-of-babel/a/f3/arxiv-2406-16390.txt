On the Confluence of Directed Graph Reductions Preserving Feedback Vertex Set Minimality
Moussa Abdenbi, Alexandre Blondin Massé, Alain Goupil, Odile Marcotte

Abstract




1
Introduction

In graph theory, the minimum directed feedback vertex set (FVS) problem consists in identifying the smallest subsets of vertices in a directed graph whose deletion renders the directed graph acyclic.
In other words, a FVS in a directed graph
G
with vertex set
V
is a subset of
V
with a nonempty intersection with every circuit of
G
.
Computing a minimum cardinality FVS (MFVS) is NP-hard
[
5
,
6
]
.
In this extended abstract we investigate graph reductions that preserve all or some minimum cardinality FVS and we focus on their properties, especially the Church-Rosser property, also called confluence.
The Church-Rosser property implies the irrelevance of reduction order, leading to a unique digraph
[
3
]
.
We explore graph reductions proposed for solving the MFVS problem, preserving the collection of MFVS or at least one of them
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
The study seeks the largest set of reductions with the Church-Rosser property and explores the adaptability of reductions to meet this criterion.
Addressing these questions is crucial, as it may have algorithmic implications, including potential parallelization and speeding up sequential algorithms in graph classes with polynomial algorithms
[
4
,
10
]
.

For sake of completeness, we recall some definitions and notation from graph theory.

A
directed graph
(or
digraph
) is an ordered pair
G=(V,E)
where
V
is a finite set of
vertices
and
E\subseteq V\times V
is a set of arcs.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a digraph,
u,v\in V
and
U\subseteq V
.
We denote
N_{G}^{+}(u)=\{s\in V\mid(u,s)\in E\}
and
N_{G}^{-}(u)=\{p\in V\mid(p,u)\in E\}
the set of successors and the set of predecessors of
u
respectively.
For a vertex
u\in V
,
G-u
denotes the digraph whose set of vertices is
V\setminus\{u\}
and whose set of arcs is
E\setminus\{(x,y)\in E\mid x=u\mbox{ or }y=u\}
. Accordingly, for a given arc
(u,v)\in E
,
G-(u,v)
is the digraph where the set of vertices is
V
and the set of arcs is
E\setminus\{(u,v)\}
.
Similarly, the digraph
G\circ u
is the digraph where the set of vertices is
V\setminus\{u\}
and the set of arcs is
(E\setminus\{(x,y)\in E\mid x=u\mbox{ or }y=u\})\cup N_{G}^{-}(u)\times N_{G}^{+}(u)
.

For
e=(u,v)\in E
, we say that
e
is a
2-way arc
in
G
if
(v,u)\in E
.
The set of all 2-way arcs of
G
is denoted by
E^{\leftrightarrow}=\{(u,v)\in E\mid(v,u)\in E\}
.
Given a digraph
G=(V,E)
we distinguish two special digraphs
G^{\leftrightarrow}=(V,E^{\leftrightarrow})
and
G^{\rightarrow}=(V,E^{\rightarrow})
where
E^{\rightarrow}=E\setminus E^{\leftrightarrow}
.

A
(directed) path
of
G
is a sequence
p=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k})
of vertices
v_{i}\in V
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
such that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.
Moreover, a path
p
is called a
circuit
if
v_{1}=v_{k}
.
An arc
(u,u)
is called a
loop
.
We say that
G
is
acyclic
, if there is no circuit in
G
.
Given
U\subseteq V
, we say that
U
is a
directed clique
or
diclique
of
G
if for each
u,v\in U
and
u\neq v
, we have
(u,v)\in E
and
(u,u)\notin E
.

A set
U\subseteq V
is called a
feedback vertex set
if
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
where
V^{\prime}=V\setminus U
and
E^{\prime}=E\setminus\{(u,v)\mid u\in U\mbox{ or }v\in U\}
is acyclic.
The set of all feedback vertex sets of
G
is denoted by
\mathrm{FVS}(G)
.
The set of all
minimal feedback vertex sets
, in short
MFVS(G)
, is the set of feedback vertex sets with minimal cardinality.

2
Reductions

Given a digraph
G=(V,E)
, the problem of finding a minimum feedback vertex set is NP-hard
[
5
]
.
However, in some cases we can use a set of
transformations
by which the size of the input graph can be reduced, with the guarantee that at least one minimum feedback vertex set in
G
could be constructed from a minimum feedback vertex set in the reduced graph, in polynomial time.
These transformations are called
digraph reductions
.

In this context, by digraph reduction we mean a transformation of the digraph
G=(V,E)
into a digraph
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
such that (1) either
|V^{\prime}|<|V|
, or
|V^{\prime}|=|V|
and
|E^{\prime}|<|E|
, and (2) an MFVS of
G
can be computed in polynomial time from any MFVS of
G^{\prime}
.

In the following, we give a brief description of Levy and Low’s
[
8
]
simple and straightforward reductions, followed by a generalization of two of their reductions by Lemaic
[
7
]
, and additional reductions from Lin and Jou
[
9
]
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a digraph and
u,v\in V
.

•

The precondition of the reduction
\textsc{loop}(u)
is
(u,u)\in E
.
This reduction transfoms
G=(V,E)
in
G-u
and adds
u
to the MFVS in construction.

•

The precondition of
\textsc{in0}(u)
is
N_{G}^{-}(u)=\emptyset
.
This reduction transfoms
G=(V,E)
in
G-u
.

•

The precondition of
\textsc{out0}(u)
is
N_{G}^{+}(u)=\emptyset
.
This reduction transfoms
G=(V,E)
in
G-u
.

•

The precondition of
\textsc{in1}(u)
is
(u,u)\notin E
and
|N_{G}^{-}(u)|=1
.
The transformation consists of replacing
G
by the digraph
G\circ u
.
This reduction does not necessarily preserve all the FVS of the original digraph but every MFVS of the reduced digraph is also an MFVS of the original graph.

•

The precondition of
\textsc{out1}(u)
is
(u,u)\notin E
and
|N_{G}^{+}(u)|=1
.
The transformation consists of replacing
G
by the digraph
G\circ u
.
This reduction does not necessarily preserve all the FVS of the original digraph but every MFVS of the reduced digraph is also an MFVS of the original digraph.

•

The precondition of
\textsc{pie}(u,v)

The transformation consists of replacing

•

G

G-x
.
This reduction does not necessarily preserve all the FVS of the original digraph but every MFVS of the reduced digraph is also an MFVS of the original digraph.

\textsc{dome}(u,v)
, for an arc
(u,v)
of

N_{G^{\rightarrow}}^{+}(v)\subseteq N_{G}^{+}(u)
(second case).
The transformation consists of replacing
G
with
G-(u,v)
.
This reduction preserves all the FVS of the original digraph.

3
The finite Church-Rosser property

Another way to see digraph reductions is to consider them as binary relations on the set of all digraphs
\mathcal{G}
.
More precisely, a reduction
R
can be seen as a binary relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
.
Hence for
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
if we can reduce
G
to
G^{\prime}
with the reduction
R
, then we say that
(G,G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
.
For a given reduction relation
\mathcal{R}
, we say that
G\in\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{R}
-irreducible
(or simply
irreducible
when the context is clear) if there does not exist
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(G,G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
.

Now, given some
G\in\mathcal{G}
, one might wish to
reduce

G
as much as possible by using the following procedure: (Step 1) if there is no
G^{\prime}
such that
(G,G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
, then stop; (Step 2) otherwise, pick any
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(G,G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}
; (Step 3) replace
G
by
G^{\prime}
and repeat the previous steps.
However, there is no guarantee that the final digraph is unique, since there might be more than one available candidate for
G^{\prime}
at Step 2).
An important property that could be satisfied by a set of reductions is the
Church-Rosser finiteness
property
[
3
]
also called
confluence

[
2
]
.
According to this property, the order in which a sequence of reductions is applied does not affect the final reduced graph.

In order to introduce more formally this property, we need some additional definitions.
Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}
be any binary relation on a set
\mathcal{S}
and write
x\mathcal{R}y
whenever
(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}
.
The
reflexive closure
of
\mathcal{R}
, denoted by
\mathcal{R}^{R}
, is given by
\mathcal{R}^{R}=\mathcal{R}\cup\{(x,x)\mid x\in\mathcal{S}\}
.
Its
transitive closure
is defined by
\mathcal{R}^{T}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}^{i}
where
\mathcal{R}^{i}
is the composition of
\mathcal{R}
with itself
i
times.
The
reflexive-transitive closure
of
\mathcal{R}
is then defined by
\mathcal{R}^{RT}=\mathcal{R}^{R}\cup\mathcal{R}^{T}
.
The
completion
of
\mathcal{R}
is given by
\mathcal{R}^{C}=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}^{RT}\mid\mbox{there does not exist\ $z\in\mathcal{S}$ \mbox{such that} $(y,z)\in\mathcal{R}$}\}
.
A pair
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R})
is called
finite
if for
x,y\in\mathcal{S}
, there is a constant
k
such that if
x\mathcal{R}^{i}y
, then
i\leq k
.

We say that
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R})
has the
Church-Rosser finiteness property
, if
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R})
is finite and for
x,y,z\in\mathcal{S}
, if
(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}^{C}
and
(x,z)\in\mathcal{R}^{C}
, then
y=z
.
The following theorem proved in
[
11
]
gives a simpler test for Church-Rosser finiteness property.

Theorem 1

(Sethi
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a relation on a set
\mathcal{S}
.
Then
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R})
is Church-Rosser finite if and only if
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{R})
is finite and, for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{S}
, the conditions
x\mathcal{R}y
and
x\mathcal{R}z
imply that there exists
w\in\mathcal{S}
such that
y\mathcal{R}^{T}w
and
z\mathcal{R}^{T}w
.

The Church-Rosser finiteness property has been equivalently called
confluence

[
2
]
.
From now on, for the sake of making the text shorter, we shall use that word as well.

]
, and Lemaic has shown that the set of reductions
\{

,

,

is also confluent
[

]
.
However, Lin and Jou in their article

]
did not investigate whether the confluence is preserved if one includes their three additional reductions (namely,
\textsc{pie}(u,v)
,

Moreover, for practical purposes, when proving confluence, it is convenient to exclude the reductions subsumed by other reductions.
For example,
in1
is subsumed by
indiclique
which means that if
in1
is applicable on a given vertex
u
, then
indiclique
is also applicable on
u
.
Therefore, if
\{\textsc{indiclique},\mathcal{R}\}
is confluent, then
\{\textsc{in1},\textsc{indiclique},\mathcal{R}\}
is also confluent.
The following proposition formalizes this property.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set and
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{3}
three relations on
\mathcal{S}
.
If
\mathcal{R}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{2}
and
\{\mathcal{R}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{3}\}
is confluent, then
\{\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{3}\}
is also confluent.

So according to Propostion
1
, proving that
\{
loop
,
indiclique
,
outdiclique
,
pie
\}
is confluent implies that
\{
loop
,
in0
,
out0
,
in1
,

,
pie
,
core
,
indiclique
,
outdiclique
\}
is also confluent.
Indeed, Lemaic has proved that
in1
and
out1
are subsumed by
indiclique
and
outdiclique
.
The same is true for
in0
and
out0
if we consider an empty set as a diclique.
For the
core
reduction we can subsume it with
indiclique
/
outdiclique
followed by
loop
.
Indeed, if
u\in V
is a core, then
N_{G}^{-}(u)\cup N_{G}^{+}(u)\cup\{u\}
forms a diclique.
In particular,
N_{G}^{-}(u)\cup N_{G}^{+}(u)=N_{G}^{-}(u)=N_{G}^{+}(u)
forms a diclique, so we can apply
\textsc{indiclique}(u)
or
\textsc{outdiclique}(u)
.
Hence, the neighbors of
u
will all have loops in
G\circ u
, which means that they have to be added to the minimum FVS which is equivalent to what
\textsc{core}(u)
should do, except that
\textsc{core}(u)
will isolate
u
and add its neighbors to the minimum FVS.
On the other hand,
\textsc{indiclique}(u)
or
\textsc{outdiclique}(u)
and
loop
will remove
u
and vertices in
N_{G}^{-}(u)\cup N_{G}^{+}(u)
are added to the minimum FVS.

So in order to prove that
\{
loop
,
indiclique
,
outdiclique
,

\}
is confluent, we use Lemma
1
.

Lemma 1
.

Given a digraph
G=(V,E)
, an arc
(u,v)\in E
,
s\in N_{G}^{+}(v)
and
p\in N_{G}^{-}(u)
, if
(u,v)
is acyclic in
G
, then
(u,s)
and
(p,v)
are also acyclic.

We can now state the following Theorem for the confluence of the set of binary relations

\{\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{loop}},\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{indiclique}},\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{outdiclique}},\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{pie}}\}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of all digraphs and
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{loop}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{indiclique}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{outdiclique}}\cup\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{pie}}
a binary relation on
\mathcal{G}
.
Then,
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
is confluent.

Proof.

According to Theorem
1
it is enough to prove that
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
is finite and that for
G,G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
if
(G,G_{1})\in\mathcal{R}
and
(G,G_{2})\in\mathcal{R}
, then there exist
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
(G_{1},G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}^{T}
and
(G_{2},G^{\prime})\in\mathcal{R}^{T}
.
Thanks to Proposition
1
, it is sufficient to prove this only for
\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{pie}}
and the other relations, since
\{\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{loop}},\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{indiclique}},\mathcal{R}_{\textsc{outdiclique}}\}
was proved to be confluent
[
7
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)\in\mathcal{G}
be a digraph,
x\in V
and
(u,v)\in V
.

pie
being a reduction, then its successive application are bounded by
|V|^{2}
, hence
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
is finite.
Now, assume that
\textsc{pie}(u,v)
is applicable.

If
\textsc{loop}(x)
is applicable, then it remains applicable after applying
\textsc{pie}(u,v)
, even if
x=u
or
x=v
.
So it is enough to consider
G^{\prime}=G-x
.
Otherwise, the two reductions can be applied in any order and
G^{\prime}=(G-x)-(u,v)
.

Thanks to Lemma
1
, if
\textsc{indiclique}(x)
(resp.
\textsc{outdiclique}(x)
) is applied first, and
x=u
or
x=v
, then
\textsc{pie}(p,v)
or
\textsc{pie}(u,s)
is applicable,
\forall p\in N_{G}^{-}(x=u)
or
\forall s\in N_{G}^{+}(x=v)
(the same goes if we first apply
\textsc{outdiclique}(x)
).
Otherwise, if we apply
\textsc{pie}(u,v)
first, the applicability of
\textsc{indiclique}(u)
(resp.
\textsc{outdiclique}(u)
) remains valid.
In both cases, we can get the same digraph
G^{\prime}=(G\circ x)-\{\bigcup_{p\in N_{G}^{-}(x)}(p,v)\}=(G-(u,v))\circ x
if
x=u
, or
G^{\prime}=(G\circ x)-\{\bigcup_{s\in N_{G}^{+}(x)}(u,s)\}=(G-(u,v))\circ x
if
x=v
.
Obviously, this remains true if
x\neq u
and
x\neq v
, with
G^{\prime}=(G\circ x)-(u,v)=(G-(u,v))\circ x
.

Finally, it is easy to see that
(G-(u,v))-(x,y)=(G-(x,y))-(u,v)
, if
\textsc{pie}(x,y)
is applicable for a given
(x,y)\in E
.
Therefore, we can conclude that
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R})
is confluent.
∎

4
Concluding remarks

In this extended abstract we focus on reductions for the minimum feedback vertex set problem, exploring their properties with an emphasis on confluence.
By identifying a subset of reductions with confluence property and considering their adaptability, this work contributes to the understanding of graph reductions and their potential impact on algorithmic advancements.
The exploration of the confluence property not only enhances our comprehension of algorithmic strategies but also opens avenues for parallelization and speed improvements in sequential algorithms.
In future work, we will investigate the
dome
reduction and explore how it can be modified so that it can be included in a confluent set of reductions considered in this extended abstract.

Equilibrium States for Center Isometries
Pablo D. Carrasco, Federico Rodriguez-Hertz

Abstract
We develop a geometric method to establish existence and uniqueness of equilibrium states associated to some Hölder potentials for center isometries (as are regular elements of Anosov actions), in particular the entropy maximizing measure and the SRB measure. It is also given a characterization of equilibrium states in terms of their disintegrations along stable and unstable foliations. Finally, we show that the resulting system is isomorphic to a Bernoulli scheme.

In this work we investigate the thermodynamic formalism for a generalization of the above systems, namely when instead of a single hyperbolic map we have an Anosov action. Let us recall that a differentiable action of a Lie Group on a compact Riemannian manifold
\alpha:G\curvearrowright M

and with respect to the metric on

\displaystyle v\in E^{s}_{x}\Rightarrow\forall n\geq 0\quad\|D_{x}\alpha(g)^{n}(v)\|\leq C\lambda^{n}\|v\|

\displaystyle v\in E^{u}_{x}\Rightarrow\forall n\leq 0\quad\|D_{x}\alpha(g)^{n}(v)\|\leq C\lambda^{-n}\|v\|.

In this case it is possible to modify the metric to guarantee that
\alpha(g)
is an isometry when restricted to each
G

center isometries
and will be our main focus. See the next section for the precise definition, or the classical reference
[
24
]
for further information about these systems.

Anosov flows (cf.
[
1
]

[
4
]

[
3
]

\geq 2
, we present a geometric construction of equilibrium states for that context. At this stage however we need to impose some condition on the potential (being constant along the orbits, or being the geometrical potential associated to a regular element). Besides the fact that these cases include the most interesting types of potentials (corresponding to the entropy maximizing measure and the SRB), we also believe that the techniques developed are interesting on their own, and may be applicable to more situations.

Comparison with other works

Other geometrical constructions exist in the literature, particularly the work of Climenhaga, Pesin and Zelerowicz
[
14
]
based on geometric measure theory, and the work of Spatzier and Vischer
[
49
]
studying compact extensions of Anosov systems. Our results have some intersection with the first cited work, but our methods are completely different, and allow us to have more control over the equilibrium measures, particularly in terms of their conditional measures along the invariant foliations. Climenhaga et al. obtain the equilibrium state in some more general settings that the ones considered here: they construct families of measures
\mu^{u}
as the ones described in
Theorem A
(that is, satisfying
2,3,4
) and use them to construct an unique equilibrium state for the system satisfying
6
. On the other hand, they do not seem to get much control on the transverse measures (the
\mu^{cs}_{x}
in our theorems), and their existence is deduced only locally (disintegrating in small rectangles). Our approach is different: we first construct the measures
\mu^{cs}_{x}
globally, and then use them to define the equilibrium measure in cases where a family
\mu^{u}
satisfying
2,3
of
Theorem A
exists. We remark that for the potentials considered in
[
14
]
we could use the existence of
\mu^{u}
given their work to construct
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
, and in particular verify the hypotheses of
Theorem B
, and perhaps
Theorem C
with some additional work (for the Bernoulli property some knowledge on the unstable measures is required). The extra control on the transverse measure is used in
[
13
]
to deduce an strengthening of Furstenberg-Marcus theorem on unique ergodicity of the unstable foliation of mixing Anosov flows.

In contrast, the precise characterization given in
Theorem B
for equilibrium states is (as far as we know) new in the literature. In addition,
Theorem C
establishes very fine statistical properties for the equilibrium state, using geometrical methods (julienne quasi-conformality) that probably could be used in other situations. It may be fruitful, for example, to analyze a possible synergy between these methods and the results of Climenhaga, Pesin and Zelerowicz.

We point out that in
[
31
]
it is also shown that the equilibrium measure built by
[
14
]
is Bernoulli. In this work we present two proofs of the fact the system is Kolmogorov, one essentially the same as
[
31
]
(which is due to Ledrappier), and the other more geometrical one based in arguments developed by Pugh and Shub to study (stably) ergodicity of conservative partially hyperbolic maps. For the Bernoulliness, we adapt the method of Ornstein and Weiss. This is also the approach followed in Li and Wu’s work, however the authors do not give all details, and seem to leave some not obvious parts for the reader to complete. Due to the importance of this theorem we elaborate more on the proof.

Additional related work appears in the manuscript
[
11
]
by Buzzi, Fisher and Tahzibi, containing some results similar to ours for perturbations of time-one maps of Anosov flows in dimension three in the case of the entropy maximizing measure, and in the recent preprint
[
2
]
by Bonthonneau, Guillarmou and Welch, studying SRB measures for the action, instead of a regular element as in our case. The techniques in this last cited work are of functional analytic type, and it seems that our approaches may be complementary. This remains as in interesting research line to be investigated.

Our interest however is constructing a particular

2.1
Families of quasi-invariant measures

Let
X
be a locally compact metric space and
f:X\to X
a bi-measurable map. From now on, by a measure on
X
we mean a Radon measure, and denote the set of such measures by
\mathscr{Rad}(X)
. The set of continuous functions with compact support on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{c}(X)
. The set
\mathcal{C}_{c}(X)
is equipped with the uniform topology, while
\mathscr{Rad}(X)
is equipped with the vague one.

Notation:
If
\phi
is measurable we write
f\phi:=\phi\circ f
. If
\mu\in\mathscr{Rad}(X)
the measure
f\mu=f_{\ast}\mu
is the unique measure
\nu
such that

\forall\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(X),\quad\int f\phi\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0pt\mu=\int\phi\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0pt\nu

With the above it is direct to verify that for
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{Rad}(X)
,
\mu=\rho\nu
it holds
f\mu=f^{-1}\rho f\nu
. Recall that
\mu\in\mathscr{Rad}(X)
is quasi-invariant if
f^{-1}\mu\sim\mu
; in this case we can write
f^{-1}\mu=\mathtt{h}_{\mu}\mu
where
\mathtt{h}_{\mu}=\frac{df^{-1}\mu}{d\mu}
. Similarly,
f^{-n}\mu=\mathtt{h}_{\mu}^{(n)}\mu
.

As it can be deduced directly from the uniqueness of the Radon-Nikodym derivative, the family
\{\mathtt{h}_{\mu}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a multiplicative cocycle over
f
with generator
\mathtt{h}_{\mu}
.

Multiplicative cocycles

A family
\{h^{(n)}:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a multiplicative cocycle over
f
if

\forall n,m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in M\Rightarrow h^{(n+m)}(x)=h^{(n)}(f^{m}x)\cdot h^{(m)}(x).

Given
h:X\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, it generates a multiplicative cocycle over
f
(and in this case we call
h
the generator of the cocycle) by defining

h^{(n)}(x)=\begin{cases}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}h(f^{k}x)&k>0\\
1&k=0\\
\prod_{k=n}^{-1}h^{-1}(f^{k}x)&k<0.\end{cases}

Observe that if
\{h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a positive multiplicative cocycle over
f
(with generator
h
) then
\{a^{(n)}:=\log h^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an additive cocycle (with generator
\log h
):

\forall n,m\in\mathbb{Z},x\in M\Rightarrow a^{(n+m)}(x)=a^{(n)}(f^{m}x)+a^{(m)}(x).

It will be useful to introduce the following.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M,\Lambda
be sets. We say that
M
is a bundle over
\Lambda
if can be written as

M=\bigsqcup_{i\in\Lambda}M_{i}

where
M_{i}
is a locally compact metric space, for every
i\in\Lambda
. We say that
f:M\to M
is a bundle isomorphism if there exists a bijection
q:\Lambda\to\Lambda
such that
f|:M_{i}\to M_{q(i)}
is an homeomorphism, for every
i\in\Lambda
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the definition above we are not assuming, a priori, any topology on
M
, and therefore we do not require any global continuity for bundle morphisms. In the cases of interest

will be manifold and each
M_{i}
an immersed (but not necessarily embedded) sub-manifold.

For example, if
M
is a (Riemannian) manifold and
\mathcal{F}=\{W(x)\}_{x\in M}
a foliation on
M
with smooth leaves, then the metric on
M
induces corresponding metrics, which in turn define a natural topology on each leaf, and we can write
M=\bigsqcup_{L\in\Lambda}L
, where
\Lambda=M/\mathcal{F}
; thus
M
is a bundle over
\Lambda
. If furthermore
f:M\to M
is a diffeomorphism and
\mathcal{F}
is
f

induces a bijection
q:\Lambda\to\Lambda,q(L)=f(L)
, and thus

is a bundle equivalence.

Consider the bundles over
\Lambda
given as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Con}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in\Lambda}\mathcal{C}_{c}(M_{i})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rad}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in\Lambda}\mathscr{Rad}(M_{i})

and consider also the space of sections of
\mathrm{Rad}
,

\mathrm{Meas}:=\{\nu:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathrm{Rad}:\nu_{i}:=\nu(i)\in\mathscr{Rad}(M_{i})\forall\ i\in\Lambda\}.

The natural topology of
\mathrm{Meas}
is the weak topology determined by
\mathrm{Con}
; it is a locally convex topology. Let
\mathrm{Con}^{+}:=\{\phi\in\mathrm{Con},\phi\geq 0,\phi\not\equiv 0\}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathrm{Meas}
satisfies

1.

for every
\psi\in\mathrm{Con}
there exists

with the property that for every

.

2.

For every

there exists

with the property that for every
\mu\in\mathcal{A},\mu(\psi)\geq d(\psi)
.

Then

is compact, and furthermore it does not contain the zero section.

Proof 2.4
.

This is a direct consequence of Tychonoff’s theorem.

From now we assume that
f
is a bundle equivalence.

with positive continuous Radon-Nikodym derivative

One verifies directly that if

Now suppose that

is a function such that

is continuous for each

,
and

. Consider

(1)

and let

.

Suppose that

.

Then

2.2
Quasi-invariant measures along leaves of foliations: the transverse measure

We will now apply the construction of the previous part to the following setting.

H-1

M
is a compact (closed) smooth Riemannian manifold and
\mathcal{F}=\{F(x)\}_{x\in M}
is continuous foliations with smooth leaves. We consider the bundle structure of
M=\bigsqcup_{F\in\Lambda}F
over
\Lambda=M/\mathcal{F}
, and denote
\mathrm{Con}(\mathcal{F}),\mathrm{Meas}(\mathcal{F})
the corresponding associated bundles.

H-2

f
is a diffeomorphism of
M
of differentiability class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

H-3

\mathcal{F}
is
f
-invariant; the induced map by
f
on
\Lambda
is denoted by
q
.

H-4

There exists a complementary continuous foliation
\mathcal{G}=\{G(x)\}_{x\in M}
of
\mathcal{F}
with smooth leaves, and such that

(a)

\mathcal{G}
is minimal: each leaf
G(x)
is dense.

(b)

\mathcal{G}
is
f
-invariant.

(c)

\mathcal{G}
is contracting under
f
: if
d_{\mathcal{G}}
denotes the intrinsic distance (on the corresponding leaf of
\mathcal{G}
) we have

\forall x,y\in M,y\in G(x)\Rightarrow d_{\mathcal{G}}(fx,fy)\leq\lambda d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y).

for some
0<\lambda<1
.

The idea is that if
B
is a foliated box corresponding to
\mathcal{F}
, we will use the (measurable) partition
\mathcal{F}|B
to play the role of the quotient space where we define our transverse measure. The existence of a complementary contracting foliation is used (crucially) to ensure the compactness property necessary for
Lemma

2.3
.

Notation:
If
A\subset M,\epsilon>0
let

G(A,\epsilon):=\{y\in M:\exists x\in A/d_{\mathcal{G}}(x,y)<\epsilon\},

and if
A=\{x\}
we write
G(x,\epsilon)=G(\{x\},\epsilon)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\delta>0
.

1.

Two relatively compact sets
A_{1}\subset F_{i_{1}},A_{2}\subset F_{i_{2}}
are
\delta
-equivalent if there exists an holonomy transport (Poincaré map)
\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}:F_{i_{1}}\to F_{i_{2}}
induced by
\mathcal{G}
satisfying:

(a)

\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}|A_{1}
is an homeomorphism of
A_{1}
onto
A_{2}
.

(b)

For every

.

2.

Two functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathrm{Con}(\mathcal{F})
are
\delta
-equivalent if
\mathtt{supp}(\psi_{1})
is
\delta
-equivalent to
\mathtt{supp}(\psi_{2})
and for every

,
\psi_{2}(\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle G}(x))=\psi_{1}(x)
. Here
\mathtt{supp}
denotes the support of the corresponding continuous function, while
\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle G}
is the holonomy map connecting
\mathtt{supp}(\psi_{1})
with
\mathtt{supp}(\psi_{2})
.

Observe that given
\psi\in\mathrm{Con}(\mathcal{F}),\delta>0
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(\psi,\delta)>0
such that if

x\in G(\mathtt{supp}(\psi),\gamma)

•

the transverse foliation defines an homeomorphism onto its image
\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}|:\mathtt{supp}(\psi)\to F(x)
.

•

\psi_{F(x)}:=\psi\circ\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}
is continuous, of compact support, and
\delta
-equivalent to
\psi
.

We seek to find a quasi-invariant

with regular Radon-Nikodym derivative. A natural class is obtained by fixing a Riemannian metric on

and considering

By the basic change of variables theorem,
\mathrm{Leb}
is quasi-invariant its Jacobian is
\rho_{F}=|\det Df|F|

(and therefore,

In the abstract definition of Jacobian given before we did not consider the variation of this function for points in different fibers of

4
A characterization of equilibrium states

The aim of this section is to characterize equilibrium states in terms of their conditional measures along strong unstable leaves, very much as the case of SRB measures, where they are characterized by the property of their conditionals being absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measures along unstables. 5
Kolmogorov and Bernoulli properties

The aim of this part is to establish the Bernoulli property for equilibrium states. Again, our arguments are written in some more generality than the context that we have been working, and in particular most of them do not require uniqueness of the equilibrium state (at least under very mild additional hypotheses), using that

For the remaining part of this section

5.1
Kolmogorov processes

We will prove first that

has the Kolmogorov property, and in particular it is mixing of all orders. For this we give two proofs. The first, which works without any extra assumptions, consists in verifying that our system satisfies the hypotheses of an abstract theorem due to Ledrappier
[
27
]

[
14
]
.

The second proof is more geometrical, and is based on the methods originally developed by Grayson, Pugh and Shub for establishing the stable ergodicity of the time-one map of the geodesic flow on manifolds of constant sectional curvature
[
19
]
. In this reasoning we use
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}=\tilde{\mathrm{m}}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
and ergodicity instead of uniqueness of the equilibrium state, but we require an extra technical assumption, namely the so called

, which is a common hypothesis for these type of arguments. Nonetheless, we think that there is value in giving this more geometrical proof, with the hope that it could be applied to equilibrium states of other systems, particularly because the first method seems ad-hoc to our setting.

6.1
Hyperbolic flows

Let
\Phi=(f^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be an Anosov flow with invariant splitting
TM=E^{s}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{u}
, where
E^{c}
is generated by tangents to flow direction. If this flow is transitive there is a dichotomy: either

1.

\Phi
is a suspension of an Anosov diffeomorphism, and therefore
M
is a fiber bundle over
S^{1}
, with fibers transverse to the flow line, or

2.

both
\mathcal{W}^{s},\mathcal{W}^{u}
are minimal foliations.

See
[
38
]
. In what follows we will focus in this last case.

For Anosov flows the powerful of symbolic representations is available
[
3
,
43
]
. With this one can establish the following.

Proposition 6.1
.

Given a Hölder potential
\varphi:M\to\mathbb{R}
there exists
\mu^{u}\in\mathrm{Meas}(\mathcal{W}^{u})
satisfying:

a)

\mu^{u}_{x}
has full support inside

, and is non-atomic.

b)

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},x\in M

c)

The map

is weakly continuous in the following sense: given

and
\mathrm{hol}^{s}_{y,x}:W^{u}(y)\to W^{u}(x)
the locally defined Poincaré map, it follows that for any

relatively open and pre-compact it holds

\mu_{y}^{u}(\mathrm{hol}^{s}_{y,x}(A))\xrightarrow[y\to x]{}\mu^{u}_{x}(A).

Moreover the convergence is uniform in

Proof 6.2
.

The construction is due to N. Haydn
[
21
]
, based in the original construction of Gibbs measures from Sinai
[

]
. Details are given in the Appendix of the paper of M. Pollicot and C. Waldken
[

. Part

is not explicitly stated by Haydn who only states (a stronger version of) this fact for holonomies corresponding to the strong stable foliation, although it clearly follows from this and part

.

Let

the time-one map. Given
\varphi
we denote
\tilde{\varphi}
its average along flow lines
\tilde{\varphi}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(f^{t}x)dt
. Observe that by the above proposition the family of measures
\mu^{u}=\{\mu^{u}_{x}\}_{x\in M}
satisfy the same conditions as the measures along unstables that we constructed in
Section

3.1
, therefore all our construction goes through and we can determine an equilibrium state
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\tilde{\varphi}}
for the system
(f,\tilde{\varphi})
having conditionals along unstables given by the family
\mu^{u}
. We now relate
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
and the equilibrium states for the flow.

Denote by
\mathscr{Pr}_{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\Phi}}(M)=\cap_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{Pr}_{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{f_{t}}}(M)
the set of invariant measures for the flow. The topological pressure of the system
(\Phi,\varphi)
is given by the variational principle (
[
50
]
, Corollary

)

P_{\scriptstyle\mathit{top}}(\Phi,\varphi)=\sup_{\mathclap{\nu\in\mathscr{Pr}_{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\Phi}}(M)}}\{h_{\nu}(\Phi_{1})+\int\varphi d\nu\},

(36)

and the definition of equilibrium state for this system is clear. In what follows we will write
\mathscr{Eq}(f,\varphi)
for the set of equilibrium measures of
(f,\varphi)
, and analogously
\mathscr{Eq}(\Phi,\varphi)
denotes the set of equilibrium states for
(\Phi,\varphi)
. Clearly
\mathscr{Eq}(\Phi,\varphi)\subset\mathscr{Eq}(f,\varphi)
, and although in principle these sets can be different, it is known that

P_{\scriptstyle\mathit{top}}(\Phi,\varphi)=P_{\scriptstyle\mathit{top}}(f,\varphi).

On the other hand, using the previous line it is direct to check that
P_{\scriptstyle\mathit{top}}(\Phi,\varphi)=P_{\scriptstyle\mathit{top}}(f,\tilde{\varphi})
, therefore it follows

\mathscr{Eq}(\Phi,\varphi)=\{\mu\in\mathscr{Pr}_{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\Phi}}(M):h_{\mu}(f)+\int\tilde{\varphi}d\mu\}\subset\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi}).

Proposition 6.3
.

If
\Phi
is a transitive Anosov flow, then the sets
\mathscr{Eq}(\Phi,\varphi)
and
\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi})
coincide. Therefore
\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi})=\{\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\tilde{\varphi}}\}
.

Proof 6.4
.

Let
A(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(\Phi_{s}x)ds
; then
A:M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an additive cocycle over
\Phi
(
A(t+s,x)=A(t,\Phi_{s}x)+A(s,x))
and
A(1,x)=\tilde{\varphi}
. For transitive hyperbolic flows it is known (cf.
[
7
]
)that
\mathscr{Eq}(\Phi,\varphi)=\{\mu\}
, and in particular
\mu
is ergodic for the flow (in fact
(\Phi,\mu)
is a Bernoulli flow). Fix any
\mathrm{m}\in\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi})
and note that since
f
and
f^{s}
commute,
f^{s}\mathrm{m}\in\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi}\circ f^{s})
.

Now the cocycle property tells us that

\displaystyle A(1+s,x)=A(1,f^{s}(x))+A(s,x)=A(s,f(x))+A(1,x)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\tilde{\varphi}\circ f^{s}(x)-\tilde{\varphi}(x)=A(1,f^{s}(x))-A(1,x)=A(s,f(x))-A(s,x)

and
\tilde{\varphi}\circ f^{s}
is cohomologous to
\tilde{\varphi}
, i.e. its difference is of the form
k(f(x))-k(x)
for some
k
(that in this case is Hölder). It is simple to check that cohomologous potentials have the same equilibrium states, therefore
f^{s}\mathrm{m}\in\mathscr{Eq}(f,\tilde{\varphi})
. The measure
\tilde{\mu}=\int_{0}^{1}f^{s}\mathrm{m}
is invariant under the flow, and a equilibrium state for
(f,\tilde{\varphi})
hence
\tilde{\mu}=\mu
. Since
\mu
is ergodic for the map
f=f^{1}
(
(\Phi,\mu)
is mixing thus clearly
(f,\mu)
is mixing), we deduce that

f^{s}\mathrm{m}=\mu\quad\forall s\in[0,1]

and in particular
\mathrm{m}=\mu
.

The above together with
Theorem B
implies
Corollary B
. In the article
[
13
]
use

to investigate further properties of the horocyclic flow, as uniqueness of quasi-invariant measures.

Here is the last application of these ideas.

Corollary 6.5
.

Suppose that

is such that for every

.

The quasi-invariance condition in the hypotheses permit us to compute the Jacobian of

with respect to the unstable holonomy, and this coincides with the Jacobian of

. We can thus use
\zeta^{cs}

\mathrm{m}
that is an equilibrium state for

(cf.

.

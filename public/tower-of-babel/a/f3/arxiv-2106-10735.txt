Bohr type inequality for Cesáro and Bernardi integral operator on simply connected domain
Vasudevarao Allu, Nirupam Ghosh

Abstract
. In this article, we study the Bohr type inequality for Cesáro operator and Bernardi integral operator acting on the space of analytic functions defined on a simply connected domain containing the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction

Let
D(a;r)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<r\}
and
\mathbb{D}=D(0;1)
be the unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. For a simply connected domain
\Omega
containing
\mathbb{D}
, let
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
denote the class of analytic functions on
\Omega
, and let

\mathcal{B}(\Omega)=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega):f(\Omega)\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\}.

The Bohr radius
[
8
]
for the family
\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
is defined to be the positive real number
R_{\Omega}\in(0,1)
given by

R_{\Omega}=\sup\{r\in(0,1):M_{f}(r)\leq 1~~~~\mbox{for}~~~f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega),z\in\mathbb{D}\},

where
M_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}
with
|z|=r
is the majorant series associated with
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega)
in
\mathbb{D}
. If
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
, then it is well-known that
R_{\mathbb{D}}=1/3
, and it is descried precisely as follows:

Theorem A
.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then
M_{f}(r)\leq 1
for
0\leq r\leq 1/3
. The number
1/3
is best possible.

The inequality
M_{f}(r)\leq 1
for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, fails to hold for any
r>1/3
. This can be seen by considering the function
\phi_{a}(z)=(a-z)/(1-az)
and take
a\in(0,1)
such that
a
is sufficiently close to
1
.
Theorem
A
was originally obtained by H. Bohr
[
6
]
in
1914
for
0\leq r\leq 1/6
. The optimal value
1/3
, which is called the Bohr radius for the unit disk, has been established by M. Riesz, I Schur and F. W. Weiner (see
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
). Over the past two decades there has been significant interest on the Bohr type inequalities, one may see articles
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
13
,
15
]
and the references therein.

Besides the Bohr radius, there is a notion of Rogosinski radius
[
19
,
21
]
which is described as follows: Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and the corresponding partial sum of
f
is defined by
S_{N}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then, for every
N\geq 1
, we have

\left|S_{N}(z)\right|<1
in the disk
|z|<1/2
and the radius
1/2
is sharp. Motivated by Rogosinski radius, Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
12
]
have considered the Bohr-Rogosinski sum as

R^{f}_{N}:=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}

for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and defined the Bohr-Rogosinski radius as the largest number
r>0
such that
R^{f}_{N}\leq 1
for
|z|<r
. For a significant and an extensive research in the direction of Bohr-Rogosinki radius, we referred to
[
11
,
14
,
17
]
and the references therein.

A natural question arises “Can we extend the Bohr type inequality for certain complex integral operators defined on various function spaces?" The idea has been initiated for the classical Cesáro operator in
[
16
,
17
]
and for Bernardi integral operator in
[
18
]
. In
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
the authors have studied the Bohr type and Bohr-Rogosinski type inequalities for Cesáro operator and Bernardi integral operator defined on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
.

Cesáro operator and its various generalizations have been extensively studied. For example, the boundness and compactness of Cesáro operator on different function spaces has been well studied. In the classical setting, for an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Cesáro operator is defined by
[
10
]
(see also
[
9
,
22
]
)

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}f(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(tz)}{1-tz}\,dt.

It is not difficult to show that for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
,

\left|\mathcal{C}f(z)\right|=\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\right)z^{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad|z|=r.

In 2020, Kayumov
et al.

[
16
]
have been established the following Bohr type inequality for Cesáro operator.

Theorem B
.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
, then

\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}

for
|z|=r\leq R,
where
R=0.5335\ldots
is the positive root of the equation

2x=3(1-x)\ln{\frac{1}{1-x}}.

The number
R
is the best possible.

For an analytic function
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the Bernardi integral operator (see
[
20
]
) is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{\beta}f(z):=\frac{1+\beta}{z^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{z}f(\xi)\xi^{\beta-1}\,d\xi=(1+\beta)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n},

where
\beta>-m
and
m\geq 0
is an integer.
It is worth to mention that for each
|z|=r\in[0,1)
, the integral representation for Bernardi integral operator yields the following for
f\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
:

\left|\mathcal{L}_{\beta}f(z)\right|=\left|(1+\beta)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n}\right|\leq(1+\beta)\frac{r^{m}}{m+\beta},

which is equivalent to the following expression

\left|\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\beta+n}z^{n}\right|\leq\frac{r^{m}}{m+\beta}.

Recently, Kumar and Sahoo
[
18
]
have studied the following Bohr type inequality for Bernardi integral operator.

Theorem C
.

Let
\beta>-m
. If
f(z)=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})
, then

\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{\beta+n}|z|^{n}\leq\frac{r^{m}}{m+\beta}

for
|z|=r\leq R(\beta)
. Here
R(\beta)
is the positive root of the equation

\frac{x^{m}}{m+\beta}-2\sum_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n+\beta}=0

that cannot be improved.

The maim aim of this paper is to find the sharp Bohr type inequality for Cesáro operator and Bernardi integral operator for functions in the class

\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
, where

\Omega_{\gamma}:=\bigg\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\bigg\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq\gamma<1.

Clearly the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is always a subset of
\Omega_{\gamma}
. In 2010, Fournier and Ruscheweyh
[
8
]
extended the Bohr’s inequality
for functions in
\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
.

The following lemma by Evdoridis
et al.

[
7
]
plays a crucial rule to prove our main results.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
7
]

For
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let

\Omega_{\gamma}:=\bigg\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right|<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\bigg\},

and let
f
be an analytic function in
\Omega_{\gamma}
, bounded by
1
, with the series representation
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then,

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{1-|a_{0}|^{2}}{1+\gamma}\quad\mbox{for}\quad n\geq 1.

2.
Main results

We state and prove our first main result.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then we have

(2.1)

\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\quad\mbox{for}~~~|z|=r\leq R_{\gamma}

where
R_{\gamma}
is the positive root of

(3+\gamma)(1-x)\ln{\frac{1}{1-x}}=2x.

The number
R_{\gamma}
is the best possible.

Proof.

Let
|a_{0}|=a<1
. A simple computation of the Cesáro operator in (
1.1
) shows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=a\left(1+\frac{r}{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{3}+\cdots\right)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|\right)r^{n}.

Using Lemma
1.1
in (
2.2
) we obtain the following estimation for the Cesáro operator:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{f}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n+1}r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\bigg).

Let

P_{\gamma,r}(a)=\frac{a}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\bigg).

Then twice differentiation of
P_{\gamma,r}
with respect to
a
shows that

P^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma,r}(a)=\frac{-2}{1+\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\right)\leq 0

for all
a\in[0,1)
and for all
r\in[0,1)
. Therefore,
P^{\prime}_{\gamma,r}
is a decreasing function and hence we obtain

(2.4)

P^{\prime}_{\gamma,r}(a)\geq P^{\prime}_{\gamma,r}(1)=\frac{1}{r(1-r)(1+\gamma)}\left(-2r+(3+\gamma)(1-r)\ln\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\geq 0

for all
r\leq R_{\gamma}
. Thus,
P_{\gamma,r}(a)
is increasing for
r\leq R_{\gamma}
and for all
\gamma\in[0,1)
and hence

(2.5)

P_{\gamma,r}(a)\leq P_{\gamma,r}(1)=\frac{1}{r}\ln{\frac{1}{1-r}}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad r\leq R_{\gamma}.

Therefore, the desired inequality (
2.1
) follows (
2.5
).

Now we show that the radius
R_{\gamma}
cannot be improved. In order to prove the sharpness of the result, we consider the function
G:\Omega_{\gamma}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
defined by
G(z)=(1-\gamma)z+\gamma
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
defined by

\psi(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-az}

for
a\in(0,1)
. Then
f_{\gamma}=\psi\circ G
maps
\Omega_{\gamma}
univalently onto
\mathbb{D}
. A simple computation shows that

f_{\gamma}(z)=\frac{a-\gamma-(1-\gamma)z}{1-a\gamma-a(1-\gamma)z}=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},~~~z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a\in(0,1)
,

(2.6)

A_{0}=\frac{a-\gamma}{1-a\gamma}~~~\mbox{ and}~~~A_{n}=\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\bigg(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\bigg)^{n}.

For a given
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let
a>\gamma
. Then the Cesáro operator on
f_{\gamma}
shows that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f_{\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|A_{k}|\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{0}}{r}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|A_{k}|\right)r^{n}.

By substituting
A_{0}
and
A_{n}
for
n\geq 1
in (
2.7
), we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f_{\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{r(1-a\gamma)}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{k}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{r(1-a\gamma)}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{(1-a^{2})(1-\gamma)}{(1-a\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{k-1}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{r(1-a\gamma)}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\left(1-\frac{a^{n}(1-\gamma)^{n}}{(1-a\gamma)^{n}}\right)r^{n}.

Further simplification of (
2.8
) shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}f_{\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{r(1-a\gamma)}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{(1+a)(1-\gamma)}{r(1-a\gamma)}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}-\frac{1+a}{ar}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{(1-\gamma)ar}{1-a\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\ln\frac{1}{1-r}+\frac{(1-a)}{(1-a\gamma)}\frac{2r+(3+\gamma)(1-r)\ln(1-r)}{r(1-r)}+D_{a,\gamma}(r),

where

\displaystyle D_{a,\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{(3-a)-\gamma(1+a)}{1-a\gamma}-2\frac{(1-a)}{(1-a\gamma)(1-r)}-\frac{1+a}{ar}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-\left(\frac{(1-\gamma)ar}{1-a\gamma}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(3-a)-\gamma(1+a)}{1-a\gamma}-2\frac{(1-a)}{(1-a\gamma)}-\frac{a^{n}(1+a)(1-\gamma)^{n+1}}{(1-a\gamma)^{n+1}}\right)r^{n}

\displaystyle=O((1-a)^{2})~~~\mbox{as}~~~a\rightarrow 1.

From (
2.4
), we obtain
\left(-2r+(3+\gamma)(1-r)\ln\frac{1}{1-r}\right)\geq 0

for all
r\leq R_{\gamma}
and hence

\frac{2r+(3+\gamma)(1-r)\ln(1-r)}{r(1-r)}>0\quad\mbox{for}~~~r>R_{\gamma}.

These two facts show that the number cannot be improved.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

Since for
\gamma=0
, the domain
\Omega_{\gamma}
reduces to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, Theorem
B
is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.1
when
\gamma=0
.

In the next result we study the Bohr type inequality for Bernardi integral operator for the class of analytic functions defined on
\Omega_{\gamma}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
0\leq\gamma<1
, let
f\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\gamma})
with
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then for
\beta>0

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}~~~\mbox{for}~~~r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}

where
R_{\gamma,\beta}
is the positive root of

\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}.

The number
R_{\gamma,\beta}
is the best possible.

Proof.

Let
|a_{0}|=a<1
. Then

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}=\frac{a}{\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}.

In view of Lemma
1.1
and (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}\leq\frac{a}{\beta}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}.

Let

\Phi_{\gamma,\beta}(a)=\frac{a}{\beta}+\frac{1-a^{2}}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}.

Then twice differentiation of
\Phi_{\gamma,\beta}
with respect to
a
shows that

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\gamma,\beta}(a)=-\frac{2}{1+\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\gamma}\leq 0

for all
a\in[0,1]
and for all
r\in[0,1)
. This implies that
\Phi^{\prime}_{\gamma,\beta}
is decreasing and

(2.10)

\Phi^{\prime}_{\gamma,\beta}(a)\geq\Phi^{\prime}_{\gamma,\beta}(1)=\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{(n+\beta)}\right)\geq 0

for
r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}
. Hence
\Phi_{\gamma,\beta}(a)
is increasing for
r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}
. Therefore for all
a\in[0,1]
,

\Phi_{\gamma,\beta}(a)\leq\Phi_{\gamma,\beta}(1)=\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\mbox{for}~~~r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}

and hence

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\quad\mbox{for}~~~r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}.

We now show that
R_{\gamma,\beta}
cannot be improved. In order to do this, consider the function

f_{\gamma}(z)=\frac{a-\gamma-(1-\gamma)z}{1-a\gamma-a(1-\gamma)z}=A_{0}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a\in(0,1)
, and
A_{n}(n\geq 0)
are given by (
2.6
). For a given
\gamma\in[0,1)
, let
a>\gamma
. Then for
\gamma\in[0,1)
and
\beta\geq 1
, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{0}}{\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a-\gamma}{(1-a\gamma)\beta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-a^{2}}{a(1-a\gamma)}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}.

By a simple computation, from (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|A_{n}|}{n+\beta}r^{n}=\frac{1}{\beta}-(1-a)\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}\right)+M_{a,\gamma,\beta}(r),

where

\displaystyle M_{a,\gamma,\beta}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{a-\gamma}{(1-a\gamma)\beta}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-a^{2})}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}+(1-a)\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(a-1)\left(\frac{\gamma(a+1)}{(1-a\gamma)\beta}+\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}\right)+\frac{(1-a^{2})}{a(1-a\gamma)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{a(1-\gamma)}{1-a\gamma}\right)^{n}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}.

Letting
a\rightarrow 1
, we obtain

M_{a,\gamma,\beta}(r)=O((1-a)^{2}).

Further from (
2.10
), we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{(n+\beta)}\right)\geq 0

for all
r\leq R_{\gamma,\beta}
. Therefore

\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{2}{1+\gamma}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{n+\beta}<0\quad\mbox{for }~~~r>R_{\gamma,\beta}.

These two facts show that
R_{\gamma,\beta}
cannot be improved.

∎

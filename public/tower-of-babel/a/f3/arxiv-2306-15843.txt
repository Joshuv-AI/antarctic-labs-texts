Abstract
. We examine a stochastic Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation for a frustrated ferromagnet with competing first and second order exchange interactions exposed to deterministic and random spin transfer torques in form of transport noise. We prove existence and pathwise uniqueness of weak martingale solutions in the energy space. The result ensures the persistence of topological patterns, occurring in such magnetic systems, under the influence of a fluctuating spin current.

1.
Introduction

Magnetic systems with higher order exchange interactions are known to host topological pattern in form of skyrmions and hopfions in dimension
d=2,3
, respectively, occurring as isolated solitons or condensed in a regular lattice
[
23
]
. This can be seen as an emergent phenomenon arising from competing exchange interactions beyond nearest neighbours on the atomic scale, including the case of geometric frustration with alternating (anti-)ferromagnetic coupling
[
1
]
. The controlled manipulation and transport of such structures by means of external currents is at the core of possible applications in future information technologies and a key challenge for the mathematical theory
[
21
]
. A decisive aspect on the level of such nanostructure is stability with respect to fluctuations coming from external sources.
The mathematical description of random effects in magnetism is generally based on stochastic Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equations (SLLG) for a governing micromagnetic energy and a noise. The existence of weak martingale solutions of the SLLG with heat-bath noise in dimension
d\leq 3
and pathwise uniqueness in dimension
d=1
have been studied in
[
2
,
3
,
13
]
.

The conventional mathematical theory of Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equations (LLG) is based on the Dirichlet energy arising from classical Heisenberg interaction of neighbouring spins. A crucial mathematical feature is the possibility of finite time singularities in spatial dimensions greater than one. Global regularity is only expected for small initial date, so that weak solution concepts become unavoidable. In the energy critical dimension
d=2
, the blow-up scenario of dissipative harmonic flows including LLG is well-understood
[
12
,
14
,
22
]
. The bubbling analysis singles out a suitable notion of energy decreasing weak solutions, so-called Struwe solutions, that are unique in this class. Corresponding existence and uniqueness results in the stochastic cases are just starting to emerge
[
15
]
. The occurrence of topological singularities, however, goes hand in hand with the collapse of topological patterns. Bubbling analysis extend to models for chiral skyrmions
[
8
,
16
]
, which are lower order perturbations of the classical theory, while the prediction of global regularity versus finite time blow-up remains a major open problem.

Higher order bi-harmonic exchange interaction exclude topological singularities in spatial dimensions
d\leq 3
by means of an infinite energy barrier. Global existence of topological patterns coupled to a Vlasov-Maxwell equation for the electron distributions function of an interacting current has been confirmed in
[
7
]
. In this work, we develop a stochastic framework for a LLG system with higher order exchange interactions and fluctuating currents and provide conditions under which topological patterns will persists almost surely. The models under consideration are based on
interaction energies of the form

E(M)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(|\Delta M|^{2}+\lambda|\nabla M|^{2}+h|M-e_{3}|^{2}\right)\ {\rm d}x

for magnetization fields
M:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
where
d=2,3
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
h\geq 0
and
e_{3}:=(0,0,1)
.
By scaling we can assume
\lambda=\pm 1
. The case we are focusing on is
\lambda=-1
corresponding to a frustrated magnet.
In this case the usual stability condition for the ferromagnetic state
m=e_{3}
is
h>1/4
which
implies coercivity
E(M)\gtrsim\int|\nabla^{2}M|^{2}+|M-e_{3}|^{2}
. The unperturbed Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation reads

\partial_{t}M=-M\times\bm{H}(M)-\alpha M\times M\times\bm{H}(M)

where
\alpha>0
is the damping constant and

\bm{H}(M)=-\left[\Delta^{2}M+\Delta M-he_{3}\right]

the effective field, i.e., minus the
L^{2}
energy gradient. The dissipative nature implies a uniform bound of
M-e_{3}
in
H^{2}
in terms of initial conditions.
Here we are interested in the LLG dynamics driven by a fluctuating current. The mathematical description of such non-variational effects requires careful considerations about the specific formulation of dissipation in the style of Landau-Lifshitz or Gilbert, respectively. The so-called adiabatic spin transfer torque induced by a spin velocity field
v
is introduced by adding the convection term
(v\cdot\nabla)M
to the micromagnetic torque
M\times\bm{H}(M)
in LLG (note that this gives rise to two distinct terms). The phenomenological non-adiabatic spin transfer torque is induced by subtracting a perpendicular counterpart
\beta M\times(v\cdot\nabla)M
. The
full torque becomes

\bm{\tau}(M)=M\times\left[\bm{H}(M)-\beta(v\cdot\nabla)M\right]+(v\cdot\nabla)M

so that

\partial_{t}M=-\bm{\tau}(M)-\alpha M\times\bm{\tau}(M).

In the special case
\alpha=\beta
, referred to as the Galilean invariant case, the interaction reduces, in the above formulation of LLG, to a single term
(1+\alpha^{2})(v\cdot\nabla)M
. It is known that in general the non-Galilean model is required for an adequate description of current driven magnetic microstructures, e.g. domain walls and vortices
[
17
,
18
]
.
We decompose the fluctuating spin current
v_{\rm fluc}=v+\xi
into a deterministic temporally homogeneous part
v
and a space-time noise
\xi=\dot{W}
that is formally the generalised time derivative (in the framework of Stratonovich calculus) of a Wiener process
W
with values in a vectorial Sobolev space to be specified below. We shall treat the deterministic part of the spin transfer torque in its general form including adiabatic and non-adiabatic terms without any balancing conditions.
For the random part we focus on the Galilean invariant case, i.e., we drop stochastic terms of the form
M\times(\xi\cdot\nabla)M
and leave only a linear transport term
(\xi\cdot\nabla)M
, which has been studied in models of stochastic Euler and Navier-Stokes equations
[
4
,
20
]
.
Therefore, the stochastic equation that we study in this paper is in the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}M

\displaystyle=-M\times\bm{H}(M)-\alpha M\times\left(M\times\bm{H}(M)\right)

\displaystyle-(1+\alpha\beta)(v\cdot\nabla)M-(\alpha-\beta)M\times(v\cdot\nabla)M-(\xi\cdot\nabla)M,

where the noise is understood in the Stratonovich sense to ensure
M
takes values in the unit sphere (norm constraint).
This equation is written formally later in (
2.3
) along with a precise definition of
W
.

For (
1.1
), the corresponding Stratonovich correction is in the form
\frac{1}{2}(\xi\cdot\nabla)^{2}M
, which has a lower order than the leading bi-Laplacian term that arises from the dissipation

\alpha M\times\left(M\times\Delta^{2}M\right)=-\alpha\Delta^{2}M+\alpha\left\langle M,\Delta^{2}M\right\rangle M

under the norm constraint.
Intuitively, in view of semigroup theory for mild solutions, this suggests that the noise does not need to be small or of a scale comparable to
\alpha
to guarantee existence of a solution.
This is indeed the case, since in energy estimates, Itô’s formula leads to cancellations between Stratonovich and Itô corrections which reduce the order of norm required to estimate these noise-related terms. They can be bounded by the energy norm of
M
with boundedness but not smallness conditions of the noise.

Small noises are of an independent interest in literature.
Recent studies
[
9
,
11
]
showed that certain nonlinear PDEs perturbed by spatially divergence free linear transport noises (satisfying suitable
L^{\infty}
and
L^{2}
conditions), converge weakly to parabolic deterministic equations. In these works, the noise (martingale) part vanishes but Stratonovich correction (in the form
\Delta M
) stays and acts as an additional dissipation in the limit under suitable scaling, which can delay blow-ups. It is not known whether a similar result holds for the conventional LLG model (without bi-harmonic interaction) or for our LLG model in higher dimensions.

In addition, to the best of our knowledge, nonlinear transport noise of the form
M\times(\xi\cdot\nabla)M
(which we dropped) has not been widely explored. For our model, it is inconclusive whether cancellations of highest-order terms can be achieved for Stratonovich and Itô corrections related to this kind of nonlinear noise. Those highest-order terms consist of cross products of mixed derivatives, which seem to require
H^{3}
-estimates in dimension
d\geq 2
, i.e., the energy norm is not sufficient. It is therefore another open question regarding the existence of solution when the random part is also in the general non-Galilean form.

In this paper, we show that under certain (spatial) regularity of the noise, there exists a pathwise unique solution of a stochastic LLG equation in the form (
1.1
) for frustrated magnets, with
H^{2}
-moment estimates.
We formulate the formal stochastic LLG equation (
2.3
) in Section
2.1
and provide the main results (Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
) in Section
2.2
.
The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
We first construct an approximating equation in Section
3.1
using standard mollifiers and a suitable cut-off function. The latter allows us to estimate certain nonlinear terms in the absence of norm constraint, i.e., when the approximation does not necessarily take values in the unit sphere.
This becomes clear as we derive
H^{2}
-uniform estimates of the approximation in Section
3.2
, where the norm constraint is narrowly violated due to mollifications.
Applying Skorohod theorem and compactness embedding results, we deduce strong (resp., weak) convergence in a weighted
H^{1}
(resp.,
H^{2}
) space in Section
4
.
Subsequently, we verify that the limit takes values in the unit sphere (Section
4.1
) and prove convergences of the drift and the diffusion terms of the equation separately (Section
4.3
).
In Section
5
, we show that the limit is indeed a pathwise unique solution of our stochastic LLG, concluding the proof.
For the ease of reading, we collect in Appendix
A
some preliminary estimates used for Section
3.2
.
Similar arguments hold for the equation on the torus, for which we give a brief description in Appendix
B
.

2.
Problem formulation and results

2.1.
Notation and the equation

We denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the unit sphere.
Let
d=2,3
.
Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Schwartz space of functions and the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
As usual, we denote by
\mathbb{L}^{p}
and
{\mathbb{W}}^{\sigma,p}
the Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with
\mathbb{H}^{\sigma}:={\mathbb{W}}^{\sigma,2}
.
For the weight function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}\in(0,1]
,

(2.1)

|\rho^{-1}\partial_{i}\rho|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}+|\rho^{-1}\partial_{ij}\rho|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq c,\quad\forall i,j=1,\ldots,d,

for some constant
c
.
Then we define weighted spaces:

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{p}_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\ :\ \left|f\rho^{\frac{1}{p}}\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}<\infty\right\},\quad p\in[1,\infty),

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{1}_{\rho}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}\ :\ \nabla f\in\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}\right\}.

For normed spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we write
E_{1}\hookrightarrow E_{2}
if
E_{1}
is continuously embedded in
E_{2}
, and
E_{1}\Subset E_{2}
if
E_{1}
is compactly embedded in
E_{2}
.
We will use the notation
\nabla_{g}
for the operator
\nabla_{g}\phi=g\cdot\nabla\phi
.

Let
h\geq 0
and let
v\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a divergence-free spin velocity field, i.e.,
{\rm div}(v)=0
.
Let
W
be an
H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued Wiener process with finite trace-class covariance
Q
, in the form

\displaystyle W(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{k}W_{k}(t)f_{k},

where
\{W_{k}\}
is a family of real-valued independent Brownian motions,
\{f_{k}\}
is a complete orthonormal system in
H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
consisting of eigenvectors of
Q
such that
Qf_{k}=q_{k}^{2}f_{k}
for some bounded real
q_{k}
and
{\rm Tr}(Q)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{k}^{2}<\infty
.
For every
k\geq 1
, let
g_{k}:=q_{k}f_{k}
. We assume that

(2.2)

q^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|g_{k}|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{k}^{2}<\infty.

Recall (
1.1
), for simplicity we write
v
instead of
(1+\alpha\beta)v
and let
\gamma:=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{1+\alpha\beta}
.
Now we write down the formal stochastic LLG equation.
Let
T\in(0,\infty)
. We will prove the existence of a pathwise unique solution
M:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
(see Theorem
2.1
) to the following equation:

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\rm d}M(t)

\displaystyle=M\times(\Delta M+\Delta^{2}M-he_{3})\ {\rm d}t+\alpha M\times(M\times(\Delta M+\Delta^{2}M-he_{3}))\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla_{v}M+\gamma M\times\nabla_{v}m\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}M\ {\rm d}t-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nabla_{g_{k}}M\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t),

where
M(0)=M_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
\nabla M_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
For simplicity,
we define drift and diffusion coefficients

\displaystyle\bar{F}(u)

\displaystyle:=u\times(\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u-he_{3})+\alpha u\times(u\times(\Delta u+\Delta^{2}u-he_{3}))-\gamma u\times\nabla_{v}u,

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle:=\bar{F}(u)-\nabla_{v}u,

\displaystyle S_{k}(u)

\displaystyle:=(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u,

\displaystyle G_{k}(u)

\displaystyle:=-\nabla_{g_{k}}u,

for any
u\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, where the bi-Laplacian terms are identified via their weak forms.
More explicitly,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle w\times\Delta^{2}u,\varphi\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta u,\Delta\varphi\times w+\varphi\times\Delta w+2\nabla\varphi\times\nabla w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

\displaystyle\left\langle w\times(w\times\Delta^{2}u),\varphi\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle w\times\Delta u,\varphi\times\Delta w+\Delta\varphi\times w+2\nabla\varphi\times\nabla w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\Delta u,(\varphi\times w)\times\Delta w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\left\langle\nabla w\times\Delta u,\nabla\varphi\times w+\varphi\times\nabla w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

for
u,w,\varphi\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

2.2.
Main results

In this section, we state the main theorem for the existence and uniqueness of solution, and prove the preservation of homotopy type as a corollary.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
T\in(0,\infty)
, we say that (
2.3
) has a martingale solution if there exists a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
defined on which an
H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued Wiener process and a
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-progressively measurable process
M
, such that

(i)

|M(t,x)|=1
, a.e.-
(t,x)
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. and
M\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho})
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\nabla M(t)|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}^{2}+|M\times\Delta^{2}M|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\right]<\infty,

(ii)

for every
t\in[0,T]
, the following equality holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. in
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}

(2.5)

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle=M_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}F(M(s))\ {\rm d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}G_{k}(M(s))\circ\ {\rm d}W_{k}(s),

where the first Bochner integral and the Stratonovich integral are well-defined in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a pathwise unique martingale solution
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P},W,M)
to (
2.3
) in the sense of Definition
2.1
, such that for
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\nabla M|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}^{2p}+|M\times\Delta^{2}M|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2p}\right]<\infty,

and
M-e_{3}\in\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.

The bounds in Theorem
2.1
and a simple interpolation argument implies space-time Hölder continuity almost surely.
Hence,
M(t)
defines a homotopy of maps from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In fact, for
\frac{d}{2}<\tau<2
,

\displaystyle\left|M(s)-M(t)\right|_{\mathbb{H}^{\tau}}^{2}\leq c\left|M(s)-M(t)\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2-\tau}\left|M(s)-M(t)\right|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{\tau}\lesssim|s-t|^{\sigma(2-\tau)}

and by Sobolev embedding

|M(s,x)-M(t,y)|\leq c\left(|M(s)|_{\mathbb{H}^{\tau}}|x-y|^{\tau-\frac{d}{2}}+|M(s)-M(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{\tau}}\right)

with constants
c
independent of
0\leq s<t<T
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Trajectories are continuous in space and time and preserve the homotopy type of initial data almost surely.

3.
Approximating equation

Since our desired solution
M
is not a function in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
, it is more convenient to define
m:=M-e_{3}
and to study the following equation for
m
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
, as in
[
7
]
:

(3.1)

{\rm d}m(t)=F(m(t)+e_{3})\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k}(m(t))\ {\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{k}(m(t))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t),\quad m(0)=m_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}.

3.1.
Mollification with cut-off

For (
3.1
), we construct an approximating equation using the modified Galerkin method in
[
24
, Chapter 15, Section 7]
combined with a cut-off function which controls the vector length of the approximation.

Let
J_{\epsilon}
denote a Friedrich mollifier on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that for
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle J_{\epsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle:=(j_{\epsilon}*u)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)u(y)\ {\rm d}y=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)u(y)\ {\rm d}y,

where
j\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is real-valued and compactly supported with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j(y)\ {\rm d}y=1
.

Fix
R>1
.
Let
\psi_{R}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be a smooth non-increasing cut-off function such that
\psi_{R}(y)=1
for
y\in[0,R]
and
\psi_{R}(y)=0
for
y\geq R+1
.
Then for a sufficiently smooth function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\psi_{R}(|u(t,x)|^{2})|u(t,x)|^{2}\leq R,\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
f^{(i)}
denote the
i
-th derivative of a smooth function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
We have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla\psi_{R}(|u|^{2})

\displaystyle=\psi_{R}^{(1)}(|u|^{2})\left\langle u,\nabla u\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta\psi_{R}(|u|^{2})

\displaystyle=\psi_{R}^{(1)}(|u|^{2})\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+\left\langle u,\Delta u\right\rangle\right)+2\psi_{R}^{(2)}(|u|^{2})\left\langle u,\nabla u\right\rangle^{2}.

Moreover, for
p\geq 0
and any non-negative integer
i
, there exists
c(R)=c(R,p)
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{R}^{(i)}(|u(t,x)|^{2})|u(t,x)|^{p}\leq c(R),\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

which implies that on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\nabla\psi_{R}(|u|^{2})|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c(R)|\nabla u|,

\displaystyle\Delta\psi_{R}(|u|^{2})|u|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c(R)\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\Delta u|\right).

For
\epsilon>0
, consider the approximating equation:

(3.4)

{\rm d}m_{\epsilon}(t)=F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(t))\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(t))\ {\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(t))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t),\quad m_{\epsilon}(0)=J_{\epsilon}m_{0},

where

\displaystyle F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=J_{\epsilon}\left(\psi_{R}(|J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})\bar{F}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3})-\nabla_{v}J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}\right),

\displaystyle S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=J_{\epsilon}S_{k}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}),

\displaystyle G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=J_{\epsilon}G_{k}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}).

The cut-off function
\psi_{R}
is only required to address the nonlinear term
\bar{F}
.
For the linear terms, we integrate-by-parts to obtain desired estimates as shown in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

3.2.
Uniform estimates of
m_{\epsilon}

We first show that the approximating equation (
3.4
) admits a unique solution
m_{\epsilon}
in Lemma
3.1
, and then deduce uniform estimates of
m_{\epsilon}
in Lemma
3.2
. At the end of this section, we estimate the deviation of
m_{\epsilon}+e_{3}
from the unit sphere in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a unique solution
m_{\epsilon}
of (
3.4
), where
m_{\epsilon}
is a progressively measurable process taking values in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right]<\infty,

and for every
t\in[0,T]
, the following equality holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. in
\mathbb{L}^{2}

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}(t)=J_{\epsilon}m_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(s))\ {\rm d}s+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(s))\ {\rm d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(s))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(s).

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
.
We first verify that
F_{\epsilon}^{R}
,
S_{k,\epsilon}
and
G_{k,\epsilon}
are locally Lipschitz on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.
Let
u,w\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
.
The derivatives of
j
are in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, bounded, and in
\mathbb{L}^{1}
.
Thus, for any non-negative integer
\sigma
,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\sigma}J_{\epsilon}u|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle=|(\nabla^{\sigma}j_{\epsilon})*(u+\nabla u+\Delta u)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla^{\sigma}j_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}|u+\nabla u+\Delta u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)|u|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}},

and similarly,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\sigma}J_{\epsilon}(u-w)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)|u-w|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}.

Recall that
\mathbb{H}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
. Let
f
be a locally Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with
f(0)=0
, then

\displaystyle|u\times f(u)-w\times f(w)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle=|(u-w)\times f(u)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+|w\times(f(u)-f(w))|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|u-w|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|f(u)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+|w|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}|f(u)-f(w)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}.

Similar arguments follow for scalar products.
Then with the Lipschitz property of
J_{\epsilon}
and
\psi_{R}
, it is clear that
F_{\epsilon}^{R}
,
G_{k,\epsilon}
and
S_{k,\epsilon}
are locally Lipschitz on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
for
k\geq 1
.

Next, let
\mathcal{E}
denote the space of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
-valued progressively measurable processes, with the norm

\displaystyle|u|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\left[|u(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\right].

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
F^{R}_{\epsilon,n}
,
S_{k,\epsilon,n}
and
G_{k,\epsilon,n}
denote the Lipschitz modifications of
F_{\epsilon}^{R}
,
S_{k,\epsilon}
and
G_{k,\epsilon}
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, respectively. For example, we set

\displaystyle F^{R}_{\epsilon,n}(u)=\begin{cases}F_{\epsilon}^{R}(u)&\text{ if }|u|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\leq n,\\
F_{\epsilon}^{R}\left(\frac{nu}{|u|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}}\right)&\text{ if }|u|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}>n.\end{cases}

Let
A_{n}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
be given by

\displaystyle A_{n}(u)(t)

\displaystyle=m_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(F^{R}_{\epsilon,n}(u(s))+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k,\epsilon,n}(u(s))\right)\ {\rm d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}G_{k,\epsilon,n}(u(s))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=m_{0}+I_{n}(t)+M_{n}(t),

where
I_{n},M_{n}\in\mathcal{E}
by the Lipschitz continuity of
F^{R}_{\epsilon,n}
,
S_{k,\epsilon,n}
and
G_{k,\epsilon,n}
.
In particular,

is an
\mathbb{H}^{2}
-valued continuous martingale.
Once we verify the Lipschitz property of

on

, standard arguments using Banach fixed point theorem and localisation by stopping times show that there exists a unique solution

in
\mathcal{E}
(the limit over

) to the
\epsilon
-approximating equation (
3.4
).
∎

In the following lemma, we deduce an
\mathbb{H}^{2}
-estimate of
m_{\epsilon}
which dominates the energy norm
E(m_{\epsilon}+e_{3})
but allows us to directly apply Gronwall’s inequality to obtain desired estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
|m_{0}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\leq c_{0}
.
Then for
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
c=c({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}c_{0},}p,T)
independent of
\epsilon
and
R
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}\right]\leq ce^{cR^{p}},

and for
\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}:=\psi_{R}(|J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|(\psi_{\epsilon}^{R})^{\frac{1}{2}}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}(t)\ {\rm d}t\right)^{p}\right]\leq c(1+R^{p})e^{cR^{p}}.

Proof.

Let
u_{\epsilon}:=J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}
and note that
\langle u,J_{\epsilon}w\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=\langle J_{\epsilon}u,w\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
for any
u,w\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
.
Let
\psi^{R,(i)}_{\epsilon}:=\psi_{R}^{(i)}(|J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})
.
We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Estimate
\frac{1}{2}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle m_{\epsilon},F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left(\left\langle m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k}\left\langle m_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=:{\rm I}_{1}\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}{\rm I}_{2,k}\ {\rm d}t+\sum_{k}{\rm I}_{3,k}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t).

The assumption
{\rm div}v=0
and (
A.3
) yield
\langle u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=0
.
Then for
\delta\in(0,1)
and
R>1
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\langle m_{\epsilon},F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}\bar{F}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})-\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\left(\Delta u_{\epsilon}+\alpha(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times(\Delta u_{\epsilon}-he_{3})\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\left(\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}+\alpha(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\nabla_{v}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta|(\psi^{R}_{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+c(\delta)R|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}.

The Stratonovich part has a similar term with opposite sign to the Itô correction. Integrating-by-parts,

\displaystyle\left\langle m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u_{\epsilon},(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}+({\rm div}g_{k})u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-|\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where
|G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=|J_{\epsilon}\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq|\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
, leaving

\displaystyle\sum_{k}{\rm I}_{2,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Similarly, for the diffusion term, by (
A.3
),

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{3,k}=-\left\langle u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}({\rm div}g_{k})|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

and by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, for
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|2\int_{0}^{s}\sum_{k}{\rm I}_{3,k}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(r)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq cb_{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{4}\ {\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq cb_{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u_{\epsilon}(s)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|u_{\epsilon}(s)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+c\delta^{-p}b_{p}^{2}\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|u_{\epsilon}(r)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\ {\rm d}s\right].

Step 2.
Estimate
\frac{1}{2}|\nabla m_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}\left(|\nabla m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+|\Delta m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\Delta m_{\epsilon}+\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left(\left\langle-\Delta m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left(\left\langle\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k}\left\langle-\Delta m_{\epsilon}+\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=:{\rm II}_{1}\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}{\rm II}_{2,k}\ {\rm d}t+\sum_{k}{\rm II}_{3,k}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t).

We address each term in the following calculations (i) – (iii).

(i)

Estimate
{\rm II}_{1}
.

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\Delta m_{\epsilon}+\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\Delta u_{\epsilon}+\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}\bar{F}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})-\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle-\Delta u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha|(\psi^{R}_{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\left\langle-\Delta u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\left((u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha|(\psi^{R}_{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\left((u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},h\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\left(-u_{\epsilon}+(u_{\epsilon}\times e_{3})\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-(\alpha-\delta)|(\psi^{R}_{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+c(\delta)\left(|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+|v|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+(1+R)|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|v|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

and with
{\rm div}v=0
, we have
\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}\Delta u_{\epsilon}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=0
and

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\Delta\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{v}\Delta u_{\epsilon}+\nabla_{\Delta v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\partial_{i}v_{j}\partial_{ij}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-(\alpha-\delta)|(\psi^{R}_{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\delta)(1+|v|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})})\left(|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+(1+R)|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right).

(ii)

Estimate
{\rm II}_{2,k}
.

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{2,k}

\displaystyle=\left(-\left\langle\Delta m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)+\left(\left\langle\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=:{\rm II}_{2a,k}+{\rm II}_{2b,k}.

For the Itô corrections, note that
J_{\epsilon}
commutes with
\nabla
and
\Delta
.
We have

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{2a,k}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|J_{\epsilon}\nabla(\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|g_{k}|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}.

For
{\rm II}_{2b,k}
,
we first re-write the Stratonovich term:

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Then we estimate the
\Delta G_{k,\epsilon}
part.
By Lemma
A.1
and (
A.13
),
taking
u=u_{\epsilon}
and
f=g_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\Delta G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=|J_{\epsilon}\Delta\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta^{2}\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{g_{k}}\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}+4{\rm T}_{1a}(u_{\epsilon})+2{\rm T}_{1b}(u_{\epsilon})+{\rm T}_{1c}(u_{\epsilon}),\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},({\rm div}g_{k})\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle 4{\rm T}_{1a}(u_{\epsilon})+2{\rm T}_{1b}(u_{\epsilon})+{\rm T}_{1c}(u_{\epsilon}),\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\left\langle\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+c|g_{k}|^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}{\rm II}_{2,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}|g_{k}|^{2}_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

(iii)

Estimate the diffusion part
{\rm II}_{3,k}
.

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{3,k}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta m_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\Delta^{2}m_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=:{\rm II}_{3a,k}+{\rm II}_{3b,k}.

We have

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{3a,k}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq|g_{k}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

and

\displaystyle{\rm II}_{3b,k}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\Delta\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}\Delta u_{\epsilon}+\nabla_{\Delta g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\partial_{i}g_{j}\partial_{ij}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+c|g_{k}|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle-\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}\Delta u_{\epsilon}+({\rm div}g_{k})\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Delta u_{\epsilon},({\rm div}g_{k})\Delta u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Then by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, for
p\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|2\int_{0}^{s}\sum_{k}{\rm II}_{3,k}\ {\rm d}W_{k}(r)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq cb_{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|g_{k}|^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\Delta u_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\ {\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}(s)|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+c\delta^{-p}b_{p}^{2}\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|\Delta u_{\epsilon}(r)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\ {\rm d}s\right].

Step 3.
Combine estimates of
\frac{1}{2}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}
.

Recall the assumption (
2.2
).
For

, we have

\displaystyle|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+2(\alpha-2\delta)|(\psi_{\epsilon}^{R})^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq|J_{\epsilon}m_{0}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+c\int_{0}^{t}R|u_{\epsilon}(s)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s+2\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\sum_{k}({\rm I}_{3,k}+{\rm II}_{3,k})\ {\rm d}W_{k}(r)\right|,

where

depends on

, but not on

or

.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
be sufficiently small such that
\alpha-2\delta>\frac{1}{2}
.
Then since
|u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{\sigma}}\leq|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{\sigma}}
for any

, we have for

that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}|(\psi_{\epsilon}^{R})^{\frac{1}{2}}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}(t)\ {\rm d}t\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbb{E}\left[|J_{\epsilon}m_{0}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}+\int_{0}^{T}R^{p}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|m_{\epsilon}(s)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}\ {\rm d}t\right]+2^{p}\delta^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbb{E}\left[|m_{0}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2p}\right]e^{cR^{p}}\leq ce^{cR^{p}},

for some constant

independent of
n
and

.
As a result,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|(\psi_{\epsilon}^{R})^{\frac{1}{2}}(J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}J_{\epsilon}m_{\epsilon}|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}(t)\ {\rm d}t\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c(1+R^{p})e^{cR^{p}},

concluding the proof.
∎

As a result of Lemma
3.2
,

F^{R}_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}S_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}))
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{k}G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}(t))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t)\right|_{W^{\sigma,p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|\nabla m_{\epsilon}(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}t\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]\leq c(R),

for fixed

,

,

Next we examine the vector length of

.

Thus, we first identify some remainder terms which arise from the difference between the convolution of products and the product of convolutions, before stating and proving Lemma

Again, let

\displaystyle p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left((1+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}})|m_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{\rho}}^{2}+(1+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}})|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+(1+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}})\left(|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)\right)^{2p}\ {\rm d}s\right]

For remainders of higher-order terms,

\displaystyle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|M_{\epsilon}+d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})^{2}+\left(|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}+2\langle d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}),M_{\epsilon}\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2(|m_{\epsilon}|^{2}+2\left\langle m_{\epsilon},e_{3}\right\rangle)\left(|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}+2\langle d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}),M_{\epsilon}\rangle\right),

Thus,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left|1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2}\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2}\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}+|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}_{\rho}}^{4}

\displaystyle+\left(|M_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\right)|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}})|M_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2}\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}+(1+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{3})|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}},

where the last inequality holds by (

A.5

Lemma 3.3

There exist constants

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|1-|m_{\epsilon}(t)+e_{3}|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}\right]

(3.10)

Proof.

Recall
\varphi(m_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(m_{\epsilon})u

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}(m_{\epsilon})(u,w)

\displaystyle=-\left\langle(1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})u,w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle M_{\epsilon},u\right\rangle\left\langle M_{\epsilon},w\right\rangle\rho(x)\ {\rm d}x.

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}{\rm d}|1-|M_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}\left\langle(1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})M_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}\ {\rm d}W_{k}

\displaystyle=:{\rm U}_{1}\ {\rm d}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left({\rm U}_{2,k}+{\rm U}_{3,k}-2{\rm U}_{4,k}\right)\ {\rm d}t-\sum_{k}{\rm U}_{5,k}\ {\rm d}W_{k}.

For the diffusion term
{\rm U}_{5,k}

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{5,k}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle(1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})M_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}\bar{F}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),\nabla_{v}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}),\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}\bar{F}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})-\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=:{\rm U}_{1a}+{\rm U}_{1b}+{\rm U}_{1c},

For

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{1b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}(v\rho)(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})^{2}\ {\rm d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}(v\rho)\ {\rm d}x

.
Thus, by (
3.9
) and the estimates in Lemma
3.2
, for any

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\rm U}_{1}\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left|1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2}\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{t}(1+|m_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{3})|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\psi^{R}_{\epsilon}\bar{F}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})-\nabla_{v}u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}s\right]

For
{\rm U}_{2,k}
,

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{2,k}

\displaystyle=\left\langle J_{\epsilon}\left((1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})M_{\epsilon}\rho\right),S_{k}(u_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}+\left\langle p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}),(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=:{\rm U}_{2a,k}+{\rm U}_{2b,k},

where by (
2.2
) and the estimates in Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}|{\rm U}_{2b,k}|\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq c(R)\ \mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
{\rm U}_{2a,k}
, integrating-by-parts,

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{2a,k}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),\nabla_{\rho g_{k}}\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})|\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}\rho\ {\rm d}x+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle u_{\epsilon}+e_{3},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle^{2}\rho\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle{\rm div}(g_{k}\rho)(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}),\nabla_{g_{k}}(u_{\epsilon}+e_{3})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=:-\widehat{{\rm U}}_{3,k}+2\widehat{{\rm U}}_{4,k}+{\rm V}_{2a,k},

where

-{\rm U}_{3,k}+2{\rm U}_{4,k}
.
For the remainder

, by (

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}\left({\rm div}(g_{k}\rho)g_{k}\right)\rho^{-1}\left|1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2}\right|^{2}\rho\ {\rm d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}\left({\rm div}(g_{k}\rho)g_{k}\right)\ {\rm d}x\right),

where the fact

vanish at infinity implies

\displaystyle=({\rm div}g_{k})^{2}+\left\langle\nabla_{g_{k}}g_{k}+2g_{k}{\rm div}g_{k},\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho\right\rangle+\nabla_{g_{k}}{\rm div}g_{k}+\left\langle g_{k},\rho^{-1}\nabla_{g_{k}}\nabla\rho\right\rangle

Then by (
2.2
) and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|{\rm V}_{2a,k}|\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|1-|M_{\epsilon}(s)|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s\right]+c(R)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}\ {\rm d}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now we estimate
{\rm U}_{3,k}
and
{\rm U}_{4,k}
.
Note that

\displaystyle{\rm U}_{3,k}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(1-|M_{\epsilon}|^{2})G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon}),G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(1-|u_{\epsilon}+e_{3}|^{2})\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}|{\rm V}_{3,k}|\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\left\langle M_{\epsilon},G_{k,\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\right\rangle-\left\langle u_{\epsilon}+e_{3},\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle|r_{4,k}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k}\left({\rm U}_{2,k}+{\rm U}_{3,k}-2{\rm U}_{4,k}\right)\ {\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad+c(R)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(|d_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}_{\rho}}^{2}+\sum_{k}|d_{\epsilon}(\nabla_{g_{k}}u_{\epsilon})|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}+|p_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right){\rm d}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

Overall, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|1-|M_{\epsilon}(t)|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq|1-|J_{\epsilon}m_{0}+e_{3}|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}+c\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|1-|M_{\epsilon}(s)|^{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}\ {\rm d}s\right]

\displaystyle\quad+c(R)\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}A_{\epsilon}(m_{\epsilon})\ {\rm d}s\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

for any
t\in[0,T]
.
Then we apply Gronwall’s lemma to yield the desired result.
∎

4.

R

In this section, we fix

\tilde{m}_{\epsilon}
.
These convergences and subsequent regularity properties of the limit

For Banach spaces

\displaystyle\Subset L^{p}(0,T;B_{1}),

\displaystyle W^{\sigma,p}(0,T;B_{2})

\displaystyle\Subset\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{3}),

for

and
\sigma p>1
.
We define

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle:=L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2})\cap W^{\sigma,p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}),

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle:=L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}_{\rho})\cap\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{-1}),

for
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

\sigma-\frac{1}{p}<\frac{1}{2}

\sigma p>1

Since
\mathbb{L}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}

\displaystyle E_{0}\hookrightarrow L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2})\cap W^{\sigma,p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho})\Subset E.

By Lemma
3.2
,
m_{\epsilon}
takes values in

,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any

.
Hence, the set of laws

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|m_{\epsilon}|_{E_{0}}>\lambda\right)\leq\lambda^{-2}\mathbb{E}\left[|m_{\epsilon}|_{E_{0}}^{2}\right]\leq\lambda^{-2}c(R)\to 0,\quad\text{ as }\lambda\to\infty.

Since

is a separable metric space, by Skorohod theorem there exists a probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
and a sequence
\{(\tilde{m}_{\epsilon},\tilde{W}_{\epsilon})\}
of

p\in[1,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}|F(\tilde{M}(r))|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq|t-s|^{p}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}|F(\tilde{M}(r))|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{p}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(|\nabla\tilde{m}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+|(\tilde{m}+e_{3})\times\Delta^{2}\tilde{m}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)(r)\ {\rm d}r\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{p},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k}\int_{s}^{t}|S_{k}(\tilde{m}(r))|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\nabla\tilde{m}(r)|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{2p}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\nabla\tilde{m}|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{2p},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k}\left|\int_{s}^{t}G_{k}(\tilde{m}(r))\ {\rm d}\tilde{W}_{k}(r)\right|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k}\int_{s}^{t}|G_{k}(\tilde{m}(r))|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\tilde{m}(r)|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\ {\rm d}r\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{p}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\tilde{m}(t)-\tilde{m}(s)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{p},

proving the
\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2})
-regularity for
\sigma\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.

5.2.
Pathwise uniqueness

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be two martingale solutions of the equation (
2.3
) defined on the same filtered probability space and with the same Wiener process
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P},W)
.
As usual, we set
m_{1}:=M_{1}-e_{3}
and
m_{2}:=M_{2}-e_{3}
. Then,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are martingale solutions of (
5.1
).
Note that in distributional sense

\displaystyle M_{1}\times(M_{1}\times\Delta M_{1})

\displaystyle=-|\nabla M_{1}|^{2}M_{1}-\Delta M_{1},

\displaystyle M_{1}\times(M_{1}\times\Delta^{2}M_{1})

\displaystyle=\left\langle M_{1},\Delta^{2}M_{1}\right\rangle M_{1}-\Delta^{2}M_{1}.

Let
y:=M_{1}-M_{2}=m_{1}-m_{2}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
.
By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality,

(5.2)

\displaystyle|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle y,\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{8}},

\displaystyle|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{4}}.

In addition,
|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq 2
,
y(0)=0
and we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=y\times(\Delta M_{1}+\Delta^{2}M_{1})\ {\rm d}t+M_{2}\times(\Delta y+\Delta^{2}y)\ {\rm d}t-y\times he_{3}\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\left(\left\langle\nabla y,\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})\right\rangle M_{1}+|\nabla M_{2}|^{2}y\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\left(\left\langle y,\Delta^{2}M_{1}\right\rangle M_{1}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}y\right\rangle M_{1}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}M_{2}\right\rangle y\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\left(y\times\left(M_{1}\times he_{3}\right)+M_{2}\times(y\times he_{3})\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\left(y\times\nabla_{v}M_{1}+M_{2}\times\nabla_{v}y\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\left(\Delta^{2}y+\Delta y\right)\ {\rm d}t-\nabla_{v}y\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}y\ {\rm d}t-\sum_{k}\nabla_{g_{k}}y\ {\rm d}W_{k}.

Applying Itô’s lemma to
\frac{1}{2}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle y,M_{2}\times(\Delta y+\Delta^{2}y)-\alpha M_{2}\times(y\times he_{3})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha\left\langle y,\left\langle\nabla y,\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})\right\rangle M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t-\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla M_{2}|^{2}|y|^{2}\ {\rm d}x\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha\left\langle y,\left\langle y,\Delta^{2}M_{1}\right\rangle M_{1}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}y\right\rangle M_{1}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}M_{2}\right\rangle y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\left\langle y,M_{2}\times\nabla_{v}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}t+\alpha\left(|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}-|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\ {\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left(\left\langle y,(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla_{g_{k}}y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\ {\rm d}t-\sum_{k}\left\langle y,\nabla_{g_{k}}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}W_{k},

where
\langle y,\nabla_{v}y\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=0
and by (
5.2
),

\displaystyle|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\delta^{-1}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

for
\delta\in(0,1)
.

For the Stratonovich and Itô correction terms,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left(\left\langle y,(\nabla_{g_{k}})^{2}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla_{g_{k}}y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k}\left\langle y,({\rm div}g_{k})\nabla_{g_{k}}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-1}\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Also, for the diffusion term,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\left\langle y,\nabla_{g_{k}}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\left\langle y,({\rm div}g_{k})y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}|{\rm div}g_{k}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k}|g_{k}|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

where the right-hand side has finite expectation, implying that the Itô integral is a well-defined continuous martingale.

We estimate the main drift part below.

\displaystyle\left\langle y,M_{2}\times\Delta y-\alpha M_{2}\times(y\times he_{3})-\gamma M_{2}\times\nabla_{v}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle y,M_{2}\times\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\alpha\left\langle y,M_{2}\times(y\times he_{3})\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\gamma\left\langle y,M_{2}\times\nabla_{v}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left\langle y,M_{2}\times\Delta^{2}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle y\times\Delta M_{2},\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+2\left\langle\nabla y\times\nabla M_{2},\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+2|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{4}}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{d}{8}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-d}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{\frac{16}{4-d}}\right)|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, for the
\alpha
terms,

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\left\langle\nabla y,\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})\right\rangle M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{4}}|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{8}{6-d}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\left\langle y,\Delta^{2}M_{1}\right\rangle M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(y\cdot M_{1})y,\Delta^{2}M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(\Delta y\cdot M_{1})y+2(\nabla y\cdot\nabla M_{1})y+(y\cdot M_{1})\Delta y,\Delta M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\langle(\nabla y\cdot M_{1}+y\cdot\nabla M_{1})\nabla y,\Delta M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle y,\Delta M_{1}\right\rangle^{2}\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}\right)|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+4|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left(|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3d}{8}}+|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\right)|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-d}}+|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{\frac{32}{12-3d}}\right)|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}M_{2}\right\rangle y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle|y|^{2}M_{2},\Delta^{2}M_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{2}|\Delta M_{2}|^{2}\ dx+2\left\langle(y\cdot\Delta y+|\nabla y|^{2})M_{2}+2(y\cdot\nabla y)\nabla M_{2},\Delta M_{2}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}+2|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\right)|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left(|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}+|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3d}{8}}\right)|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\left(|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{8}{4-d}}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{\frac{32}{12-3d}}\right)+\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

For the
\Delta^{2}y
term,

\displaystyle\left\langle y,\left\langle M_{2},\Delta^{2}y\right\rangle M_{1}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(y\cdot M_{1})M_{2},\Delta^{2}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle(y\cdot\Delta M_{1})M_{2}+(y\cdot M_{1})\Delta M_{2}+2(y\cdot\nabla M_{1})\nabla M_{2},\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left\langle(\nabla y\cdot\nabla M_{1})M_{2}+(\nabla y\cdot M_{1})\nabla M_{2},\Delta y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left(|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+2|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left(|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{4}}\left(|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{d}{8}}\left(|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\ {\rm d}x+c\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\delta^{-1})\left(|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right)^{\frac{8}{4-d}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\delta^{-1})\left(|\nabla M_{1}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right)^{\frac{16}{4-d}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{1}\right\rangle\left(|\nabla M_{2}|^{2}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta M_{1}\right\rangle\right)\ {\rm d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\left(|\nabla M_{1}|^{2}+\left\langle M_{1},\Delta M_{2}\right\rangle\right)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,y\right\rangle\left(|\nabla M_{2}|^{2}+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta M_{1}\right\rangle\right)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\langle\Delta y,M_{2}\right\rangle\left(\left\langle\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2}),-\nabla y\right\rangle+\left\langle M_{2},\Delta y\right\rangle-\left\langle y,\Delta M_{2}\right\rangle\right)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left(|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}^{2}+|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|\nabla y|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}+\frac{1}{2}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1+\frac{d}{4}}\left(|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{8}}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{d}{8}}|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+c\delta|\Delta y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+c(\delta^{-1})\left(\left(|\nabla M_{2}|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+|\Delta M_{1}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\Delta M_{2}|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{8}{4-d}}+|\nabla(M_{1}+M_{2})|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{\frac{16}{4-d}}\right)|y|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\rm d}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\phi(t)|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}t+\alpha\left(c_{1}\delta-\frac{1}{2}\right)|\Delta y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\ {\rm d}t-\sum_{k}\left\langle y,\nabla_{g_{k}}y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\ {\rm d}W_{k},

for some constant
c_{1}
and

\displaystyle\phi(t)=c(h,\delta^{-1})\left(1+|\nabla M_{1}|^{q}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+|\nabla M_{2}|^{q}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\right),

for some
q\in[2,\infty)
depending on
d
.
Since
\nabla M_{1},\nabla M_{2}
in
L^{2p}(\Omega;L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}))
, we have
\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)\ {\rm d}t<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
Let
\delta
be sufficiently small such that
c_{1}\delta-\frac{1}{2}<0
.

If
{\rm div}g_{k}=0
for all
k
, then applying Gronwall’s inequality directly and using the fact
y(0)=0
,

\displaystyle|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq|y(0)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}e^{\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)\ {\rm d}t}=0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Otherwise, let
X(t):=\frac{1}{2}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\rm d}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},-2\phi(t)e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{\rm d}|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},{\rm d}e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}\sum_{k}\left\langle y,\nabla_{g_{k}}y\right\rangle\ {\rm d}W_{k},

where the process
M(t):=\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\phi(s)\ {\rm d}s}\langle y,\nabla_{g_{k}}y\ {\rm d}W_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
is a martingale.
Hence,
\mathbb{E}[X(t)]\leq\mathbb{E}[M(t)]=0
, implying that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|y(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]=0,\quad t\in[0,T].

This proves the pathwise uniqueness of the solution of (
2.3
), concluding the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Appendix A
Preliminary estimates

A.1.
Useful formulae

Let
u,w\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d=2,3
.
Then

\displaystyle(\nabla_{g})(u\times w)=\nabla_{g}u\times w+u\times\nabla_{g}w,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{g})^{2}u=\sum_{i=1}^{d}g_{i}^{2}\partial_{i}^{2}u+\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{d}g_{i}g_{j}\partial_{ij}u+\nabla_{\nabla_{g}g}u,

\displaystyle\nabla_{f}(\nabla_{g}u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}f_{i}g_{j}\partial_{ij}u+\nabla_{\nabla_{f}g}u.

For
u,w
with suitable decay properties,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{f}u,w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle u,\nabla_{f}w+({\rm div}f)w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla_{f}u,\nabla_{g}w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left\langle f_{i}g_{j}\partial_{ij}u+\partial_{j}(f_{i}g_{j})\partial_{i}u,w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

By (
A.1
), for
f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(A.3)

\left\langle u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle({\rm div}f)u,u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

and

\displaystyle-\left\langle|u|^{2}u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{f}(|u|^{2}u)+({\rm div}f)|u|^{2}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=3\left\langle|u|^{2}\nabla_{f}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle({\rm div}f)|u|^{2}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left\langle({\rm div}f)|u|^{2}u,u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

which imply

(A.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle(1-|u|^{2})u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}({\rm div}f)\left(|u|^{4}-2|u|^{2}\right)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}({\rm div}f)(1-|u|^{2})^{2}\ {\rm d}x-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}f\ {\rm d}x,

where
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm div}f\ {\rm d}x=0
if
f
vanishes at infinity.

Without loss of generality, assume that
j
is supported on the (open) unit ball.
Let
B_{\epsilon}(x)
denote the open ball of radius
\epsilon>0
centred at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then for weighted
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm,

\displaystyle|J_{\epsilon}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)u(y)\ {\rm d}y\right|^{2}\rho(x)\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)\rho^{-1}(y)\ {\rm d}y\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)|u(y)|^{2}\rho(y)\ {\rm d}y\right)\rho(x)\ {\rm d}x,

where
\rho^{-1}(y)=(1+|y|^{2})^{2}
is bounded from above by
(1+(|x|+\epsilon)^{2})^{2}
in
B_{\epsilon}(x)
, implying

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)\rho^{-1}(y)\rho(x)\ {\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq(1+(|x|+\epsilon)^{2})^{2}\rho(x)\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)\ {\rm d}y

\displaystyle=(1+(|x|+\epsilon)^{2})^{2}\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq c,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Thus,

(A.5)

\displaystyle|J_{\epsilon}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)|u(y)|^{2}\rho(y)\ {\rm d}y\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}j_{\epsilon}(x-y)\ {\rm d}x\right)|u(y)|^{2}\rho(y)\ {\rm d}y

\displaystyle=c|u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}^{2}.

Similarly,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\left\langle J_{\epsilon}u,w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u,J_{\epsilon}(w\rho)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle u,J_{\epsilon}w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}+\left\langle u,J_{\epsilon}(w\rho)-w\rho\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle u,w-J_{\epsilon}w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle u,J_{\epsilon}w\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}+|u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}|J_{\epsilon}(w\rho)-w\rho|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\rho}}|J_{\epsilon}w-w|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where the last two terms converge to
0
as
\epsilon\to 0
for
u,w\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
.

A.2.
Estimates for mixed partial derivatives

Lemma A.1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a sufficiently smooth function with suitable decay properties.
Then

(A.7)

\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}^{2}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq|\Delta u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},\quad\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{ij}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq|\Delta u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Let
f\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
. Then for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{4}u,f\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{j}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

(A.9)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}u,f\partial_{i}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{i}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{j}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

(A.10)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{3}\partial_{j}u,f\partial_{i}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{i}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

(A.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{3}\partial_{j}u,f\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{j}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{j}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Proof.

For the inequalities in (
A.7
), we first observe that

\displaystyle|\partial_{ij}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{j}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}^{2}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}=|\Delta u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-2\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{d}\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{j}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=|\Delta u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-2\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{d}|\partial_{ij}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq|\Delta u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

and as a result,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}|\partial_{ij}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\partial_{i}^{2}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\partial_{j}^{2}u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq|\Delta u|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

In order to deduce the inequalities (
A.8
)-(
A.11
),
we first observe that

(A.12)

\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,f\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=-\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u+f\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

Then for (
A.8
),

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{4}u,f\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{j}u+f\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{ij}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{j}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{ij}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{j}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where the second equality holds by (
A.12
).
Similarly, for (
A.9
),

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}u,f\partial_{i}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+f\partial_{i}^{3}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle f\ \partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}^{2}\left(f\partial_{i}^{2}u\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-2\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle f\partial_{i}u,\partial_{i}^{2}\partial_{j}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{j}^{2}u-\partial_{j}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}u,\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\partial_{j}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{i}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{j}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where the fifth equality holds since the last term in the fourth equality is the negative counterpart of the left-hand side.
For (
A.10
),

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{3}\partial_{j}u,f\partial_{i}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}(f\partial_{i}u)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+f\ \partial_{i}^{3}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{3}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\left\langle\partial_{i}u,f\partial_{i}^{3}\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u+2\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}u,\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{3}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{i}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{j}f\ \partial_{i}^{2}u+\partial_{ij}f\ \partial_{i}u,\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{i}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

For (
A.11
), the derivation is similar to that of (
A.10
) and we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{i}^{3}\partial_{j}u,f\partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}^{2}f\ \partial_{j}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{ij}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\partial_{i}f\ \partial_{j}^{2}u+\partial_{ij}f\ \partial_{j}u,\partial_{i}^{2}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\partial_{j}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{i}^{2}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+|\partial_{j}^{2}u|^{2}+|\partial_{ij}u|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

concluding the proof.
∎

This lemma allows us to treat a higher-order term
\langle\Delta^{2}(\nabla_{f}u),\nabla_{f}u\rangle
.
Note that

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\nabla_{f}u)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{f}(\Delta^{2}u)+4\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}f_{j}\partial_{ij}(\Delta u)+2\left(\nabla_{\Delta f}(\Delta u)+2\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\partial_{ij}f_{k}\partial_{ijk}u\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(4\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(\Delta f_{j})\partial_{ij}u+\nabla_{\Delta^{2}f}u\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{f}(\Delta^{2}u)+4{\rm T}_{1a}(u)+2{\rm T}_{1b}(u)+{\rm T}_{1c}(u).

For
f\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, using Lemma
A.1
we have

(A.13)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\rm T}_{1a}(u),\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\partial_{i}f_{j}\partial_{ij}(\Delta u),\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|^{2}_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}},

\displaystyle\left\langle{\rm T}_{1b}(u),\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\Delta f}(\Delta u),\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+2\sum_{i,j,k}\left\langle\partial_{ij}f_{k}\ \partial_{ijk}u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\Delta u,\nabla_{\Delta f}\nabla_{f}u+({\rm div}\Delta f)\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}-2\sum_{i,j,k}\left\langle\partial_{ij}u,\partial_{k}\left(\partial_{ij}f_{k}\nabla_{f}u\right)\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}\cap W^{3,4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}},

\displaystyle\left\langle{\rm T}_{1c}(u),\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i,j}\left\langle\partial_{i}(\Delta f_{j})\partial_{ij}u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\left\langle\nabla_{\Delta^{2}f}u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}\cap W^{3,4}\cap H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}},

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta^{2}u,\nabla_{f}u\right\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}},

where
|f|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|f|_{W^{3,4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim|f|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for
d\leq 3
.
The estimates (
A.13
) help us to address Itô corrections in Step 2(ii) of the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Appendix B
On the torus

For equation (
2.3
) formulated on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Theorem
2.1
still holds with
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in place of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since the domain is bounded, the proof can be simplified in this case.
In this section, let
\mathbb{L}^{p}
and
\mathbb{H}^{\sigma}
denote the usual Lebesgue and Hilbert spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
W^{\sigma,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, respectively.
We can work with
M
directly and only need Faedo-Galerkin approximations
\{M_{n}\}
with a cut-off function
\psi_{R}
that depends on
|M(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}
instead of
|M(t,x)|^{2}
. More explicitly,
let
\{{\rm\bf e}_{i}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{L}^{2}
consisting of eigenvectors of the negative Laplacian
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and let
\mathbb{H}_{n}:=\text{span}\{{\rm\bf e}_{i},\ldots,{\rm\bf e}_{n}\}
.
Let
\Pi_{n}
be the orthogonal projection from
\mathbb{L}^{2}
onto
\mathbb{H}_{n}
, and we formulate the approximating equation:

(B.1)

{\rm d}M_{n}(t)=F^{R}_{n}(M_{n}(t))\ {\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}S_{k,n}(M_{n}(t))\ {\rm d}t+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{k,n}(M_{n}(t))\ {\rm d}W_{k}(t),\quad m_{n}(0)=\Pi_{n}m_{0},

where

\displaystyle F^{R}_{n}:\mathbb{H}_{n}\ni u

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi_{R}(|u|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}})\Pi_{n}\bar{F}(u)-\Pi_{n}\nabla_{v}u\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

\displaystyle S_{k,n}:\mathbb{H}_{n}\ni u

\displaystyle\mapsto\Pi_{n}S_{k}(u)\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

\displaystyle G_{k,n}:\mathbb{H}_{n}\ni u

\displaystyle\mapsto\Pi_{n}G_{k}(u)\in\mathbb{H}_{n},

are locally Lipschitz.
The uniform estimates can be obtained as in Lemma
3.2
, and tightness and compact embeddings hold for the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces without weight or localisation. Then, for the new processes
\tilde{M}_{n}
and
\tilde{M}
(deduced from Skorohod theorem) with strong convergence in
L^{2p}(\tilde{\Omega};L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2}))
, convergence of the cut-off function
\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{R}=\psi_{R}(|\tilde{M}_{n}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}})\to\tilde{\psi}^{R}=\psi_{R}(|\tilde{M}|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}})
in
L^{2p}(\tilde{\Omega};L^{2}(0,T))
is immediate from Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality:

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\psi}_{n}^{R}(t)-\tilde{\psi}^{R}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{R}^{\prime}(y)|\ \left||\tilde{M}_{n}(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}-|\tilde{M}(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tilde{M}_{n}(t)-\tilde{M}(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\tilde{M}_{n}(t)-\tilde{M}(t)|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}|\tilde{M}_{n}(t)-\tilde{M}(t)|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{\frac{d}{4}}.

Thus, we obtain similar convergences to Lemma
4.1
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{3})
without knowing
\tilde{\psi}^{R}(t)=1
, and then we can deduce the existence of weak martingale solution to

(B.2)

\tilde{M}(t)=\tilde{M}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tilde{\psi}^{R}\bar{F}(\tilde{M})-\nabla_{v}\tilde{M}\right)(s)\ {\rm d}s+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}S_{k}(\tilde{M})(s)\ {\rm d}s+\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}G_{k}(\tilde{M})(s)\ {\rm d}\tilde{W}_{k}(s).

Finally,
|\tilde{M}(t,x)|=1
(and thus
\tilde{\psi}^{R}(t)=1
for
R>1
) for a.e.-
(t,x)
,
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. can be shown using a similar but simpler calculation to Lemma
3.3
, and the pathwise uniqueness follows as in Section
5.2
.

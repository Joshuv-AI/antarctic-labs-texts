Constructive Combinatorics of Dickson’s Lemma
Iosif Petrakis

Abstract
We study constructively the relations between the finite cases of Dickson’s lemma. Although there are many constructive proofs of them, the novel aspect of our proofs is the extraction of a corresponding bound. We provide some new one-step unprovability results i.e., results of the form “a finite case of Dickson’s lemma does not prove in one step a stronger case of it”. Moreover, we study the infinite cases of Dickson’s lemma from the point of view of constructive reverse mathematics. We work within Bishop’s informal system of constructive mathematics BISH.

The finite and infinite cases of a combinatorial theorem

Although from such proofs we do not extract the best possible, or optimal bounds, we find them

The results included in this section are, as far as we know new, and they are motivated by our intuition
that it is not possible to prove

\mathrm{DL}(k,l)

\mathrm{DL}(1,l)

“directly

for every

\nexists_{f\in{\mathbb{F}}({\mathbb{N}}^{2},{\mathbb{N}})}\forall_{n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}}(f(n_{1},n_{2})\leq f(m_{1},m_{2})\rightarrow

(n_{1},n_{2})\leq(m_{1},m_{2})),

\forall_{f\in{\mathbb{F}}({\mathbb{N}}^{2},{\mathbb{N}})}\exists_{n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}}(f(n_{1},n_{2})\leq f(m_{1},m_{2})\ \wedge

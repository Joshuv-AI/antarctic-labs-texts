Blow-up or Grow-up for the threshold solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Stephen Gustafson, Takahisa Inui

Abstract
. We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with L 2 L^{2} -supercritical and H 1 H^{1} -subcritical power type nonlinearity. Duyckaerts and Roudenko [ 8 ] and Campos, Farah, and Roudenko [ 3 ] studied the global dynamics of the solutions with same mass and energy as that of the ground state. In these papers, finite variance is assumed to show the finite time blow-up. In the present paper, we remove the finite-variance assumption and prove a blow-up or grow-up result.

35Q55,35B44 etc.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

We consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(NLS)

where
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
1+4/d<p<(d+2)/(d-2)
. Here, we regard
(d+2)/(d-2)
as
\infty
when
d=1,2
. The nonlinearity is called
L^{2}
-supercritical and
H^{1}
-subcritical. The initial value problem is locally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
[
11
]
and the standard text books
[
4
,
26
,
20
]
). We also know that the blow-up criterion holds. That is, if the forward (resp. backward) maximal existence time
T_{\max}
(resp.
T_{\min}
) is finite then
\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
diverges at
t=T_{\max}
(resp.
t=T_{\min}
). Moreover, the mass
M
, energy
E
, and momentum
P
,
given by

\displaystyle M(u)

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

\displaystyle P(u)

\displaystyle=\im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{u(x)}\nabla u(x)dx

are conserved by the flow.
In the study of the global dynamics, the ground state solution
e^{it}Q(x)
plays a crucial role. Here,
Q
is the least-energy radial, positive solution to the elliptic equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta Q+Q-Q^{p}=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since pioneering work by Kenig and Merle
[
18
]
, many researchers have studied (
NLS
) from the viewpoint of global dynamics.
We summarize the global dynamics of solutions below the ground state:

Theorem

(Global dynamics below the ground state)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(u_{0})<M(Q)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(Q)
, where
s_{c}:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}
.

(1)

If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
, then the solution
u
exists for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and scatters in both time directions.

(2)

If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}>\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
, then the solution
u
blows up or grows up in both time directions.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
does not occur since we assume
M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(u_{0})<M(Q)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(Q)
.

(2)

We say that
u
grows up in positive time if
u
exists at least on
[0,\infty)
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
.

(3)

In the second statement, we have four possibilities. For example,
u
blows up in finite positive time and grows up in the negative time direction.

(4)

Martel and Raphaël
[
21
]
showed existence of a grow-up solution for the
L^{2}
-critical NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(i.e.,
p=3
). However, it is still an open problem whether a grow-up solution to (
NLS
) exists.

Holmer and Roudenko
[
16
]
showed the scattering part for 3d cubic NLS in the radial setting. The radial assumption was removed by Duyckaerts, Holmer, and Roudenko
[
7
]
. The blow-up or grow-up part for 3d cubic NLS is obtained by Holmer and Roudenko
[
17
]
.
Akahori and Nawa
[
1
]
and Guevara
[
13
]
proved both scattering and blow-up or grow-up results for (
NLS
) (i.e. for general power and dimension). Fang, Xie, and Cazenave
[
10
]
showed the scattering for (
NLS
). Guo
[
14
]
obtained the blow-up or grow-up result for (
NLS
). Their proofs of scattering rely on a concentration compactness and rigidity argument, which was developed by Kenig and Merle
[
18
]
. Dodson and Murphy
[
5
]
give another proof of the scattering part for 3d cubic NLS in the radial setting. They used an interaction Morawetz estimate and Tao’s scattering criterion (
[
25
]
). They removed the radial assumption in
[
6
]
. See also
[
2
,
15
]
.

While the global behavior of solutions below the ground state is of only two types, i.e., scattering or blow-up (or grow-up), another global behavior appears in the threshold case – there exist solutions converging to the ground state:

Theorem

(Existence of special solutions on the threshold)
.

There exist two radial solutions
Q^{\pm}(t)
to (
NLS
) in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

•

M(Q^{\pm})=M(Q)
,
E(Q^{\pm})=E(Q)
,
Q^{\pm}
exist at least on
[0,\infty)
, and there exist
C,c>0
such that

\displaystyle\|Q^{\pm}(t)-e^{it}Q\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ce^{-ct}

for
t\geq 0
,

•

\|\nabla Q^{+}(0)\|_{L^{2}}>\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}
and
Q^{+}
blows up in finite negative time,

•

\|\nabla Q^{-}(0)\|_{L^{2}}<\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}
and
Q^{-}
exists globally and scatters backward in time.

Moreover, we know the global dynamics on the threshold:

Theorem

(Global dynamics of threshold solutions)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfy
M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(u_{0})=M(Q)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(Q)
.

(1)

If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
, then the solution
u
exists on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, either
u
scatters in both directions or
u=Q^{-}
up to symmetries of the equation.

(2)

If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
, then
u=e^{it}Q
up to symmetries of the equation.

(3)

We assume that
u_{0}
is radial when
d\geq 2
or
|x|u_{0}\in L^{2}
. If
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}>\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}
, then either the solution
u
blows up in finite positive and negative time or
u=Q^{+}
up to symmetries of the equation.

Remark 1.2
.

The case
d=1
with radial (even) symmetry is not
included in part (3).

These result were shown by Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
8
]
for 3d cubic NLS. Recently, these were extended to general dimensions and powers by Campos, Farah, and Roudenko
[
3
]
. Global dynamics above the ground state were studied by Nakanishi and Schlag
[
22
]
, but we do not pursue that
direction here.

We are interested in the blow-up result on the threshold. While the blow-up or grow-up result without finite variance is known below the ground state, the result without finite variance (or radial symmetry) was not known at the threshold. In the present paper we remove this additional assumption.

1.2.
Main result

We define the virial functional
K
by

\displaystyle K(\varphi):=\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{d(p-1)}{2(p+1)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
NLS
) satisfying

\displaystyle M(u_{0})=M(Q),\;E(u_{0})=E(Q),\;K(u_{0})<0.

(MEN)

Then
u
must satisfy one of the following:

(1)

u
blows up in both time directions,

(2)

u
blows up in positive time and grows up in negative time,

(3)

u
blows up in negative time and grows up in positive time,

(4)

u
grows up in both time directions,

(5)

u=Q^{+}
up to symmetries.

Remark 1.3
.

The negativity of the virial functional is equivalent to
\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}<\|u\|_{L^{2}}
under the mass-energy condition
M(u)=M(Q),E(u)=E(Q)
.

By scaling and the variational structure, we get the following statement as a corollary of Theorem
1.1
(see
[
3
, Remark 1.6]
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
u
be the solution satisfying
M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(u_{0})=M(Q)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(Q)
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{s_{c}}>\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\nabla Q\|^{s_{c}}
. Then the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
holds.

1.3.
Idea of the proof

The virial identity

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)=8K(u(t))

is very useful for showing blow-up. Glassey
[
12
]
used it to show that solutions with negative energy blow up in finite time if the initial data is of finite variance. To remove the finite variance assumption in the use of the virial identity, a localized virial identity

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\right)=8K(u(t))+A_{R}(u(t))

is used, where
\varphi_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
satisfies
\varphi(x)=|x|^{2}
for
|x|\leq R
and
A_{R}
is an error term. Under the radially symmetric assumption, by using this localized virial identity and the radial Sobolev inequality to control the error term, Ogawa and Tsutsumi
[
23
]
proved blow-up of solutions with negative energy when
d\geq 2
. Ogawa and Tsutsumi also showed blow-up for 1d quintic NLS without the radial assumption by using a localized virial identity and scaling argument in
[
24
]
. Their proof relies on the
L^{2}
-critical nonlinearity. As far as the authors know, there is no finite time blow-up result for even solutions of (
NLS
) in the one dimensional case.
Akahori and Nawa
[
1
]
gave a proof of blow-up or grow-up under the assumption
\sup_{t>0}K(u(t))<0
, which holds under the mass-energy condition
M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(u_{0})<M(Q)^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}E(Q)
and negativity of
K(u_{0})
(see also
[
9
]
). They derived a contradiction by controlling the error term in the localized virial identity assuming the uniform boundedness
\sup_{t>0}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
. However, this argument does not work on the threshold. Indeed, we have the possibility that
K(u(t))\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. To overcome this difficulty, we use a concentration compactness argument, obtained by Keraani
[
19
]
and developed by Kenig and Merle
[
18
]
to show the scattering result. The
method of application of the concentration compactness argument to show blow-up was designed by Holmer and Roudenko
[
17
]
in the study of 3d cubic NLS, and was extended to general dimensions and powers by Guevara
[
13
]
and Guo
[
14
]
. By the concentration compactness argument, we can show that the orbit of the global-in-time uniformly bounded solution
u
with negative
K
is compact in
H^{1}
. More precisely, there exists
X:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\{u(t,\cdot-X(t)):t>0\}
is precompact in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In the
below-threshold case, we have
\sup_{t>0}K(u(t))<0
, the error term is controlled by the precompactness, and we get a contradiction. On the threshold,
K(u(t))\to 0
may happen. We apply a modulation argument to show that
u(t)\to e^{i\theta_{0}}Q(\cdot-X_{0})
as
t\to\infty
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This argument is similar to the case of positive
K
, done by Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
8
]
(see also
[
3
]
). Once we get the convergence, we can show that the uniformly bounded global solution
u
is of finite variance by using negativity of
K
. The solution with finite variance must be
Q^{+}
up to symmetries by
[
8
,
3
]
. This implies Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Variational structure

In this section, we collect lemmas concerning the variational structure.

Lemma 2.1

(Pohozaev identity (e.g.
[
4
, Corollary 8.1.3]
))
.

We have

\displaystyle\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}=\frac{2(p+1)}{d(p-1)}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{p+1}{p-1}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle E(Q)=\frac{d(p-1)-4}{2d(p-1)}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The next lemma follows from the variational structure of
Q
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u_{0}
satisfy
M(u_{0})=M(Q)
and
E(u_{0})=E(Q)
and
u
be the solution to (
NLS
) with
u(0)=u_{0}
. Then we have the following.

(1)

If
K(u_{0})>0
, then
K(u(t))>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

If
K(u_{0})=0
, then
u_{0}(x)=e^{i\theta_{0}}Q(x-x_{0})
for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(3)

If
K(u_{0})<0
, then
K(u(t))<0
for all
t
in the interval of existence.

See
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
for the proof when
d=3
and
p=3
.

2.2.
Localized virial identity

For a solution
u
to (
NLS
), define

\displaystyle J_{R}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx,

where
\varphi_{R}(x)=R^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right)
and
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(r)=\begin{cases}r^{2},&(r\leq 1),\\
0,&(r\geq 3).\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{dr^{2}}(r)\leq 2\text{ for }r\geq 0.

Then by standard computations we have

\displaystyle J_{R}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\varphi_{R}\nabla u\overline{u}dx,

\displaystyle J_{R}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=8K(u(t))+A_{R}(u(t)),

where

\displaystyle A_{R}(u(t))

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\geq R}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}\left(4\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi_{R}-8\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}\int_{|x|\geq R}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\left(2d-\Delta\varphi_{R}\right)dx+\int_{|x|\geq R}|u(t,x)|^{2}\Delta^{2}\varphi_{R}dx.

2.3.
Strichartz norms

Let
s\in[0,1)
. We say that a pair
(q,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
\dot{H}^{s}
-admissible and denote
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{s}
if

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d}{2}-s,\quad\frac{2d}{d-2s}<r<\frac{2d}{d-2}.

We define

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s};I)}:=\sup_{(q,r)\in\Lambda_{s}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r})}.

We set
\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s})}=\|u\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s};\mathbb{R})}
for short.

3.
Modulation

We define

\displaystyle\mu(f):=\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the Pohozaev identity, if
E(u)=E(Q)
, we have

\displaystyle K(u)=\frac{d(p-1)-4}{4}\mu(u).

See
[
8
,
3
]
for Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
below.

Lemma 3.1

(Modulation)
.

There exist a positive constant
\mu_{0}
and a function
\varepsilon(\mu):(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that, for any
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
M(f)=M(Q)
,
E(f)=E(Q)
, and
|\mu(f)|<\mu_{0}
, there exist a
C^{1}
-mapping

f\mapsto(x,\theta)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|e^{-i\theta}f(\cdot+x)-Q\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\mu(f)),

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)Q(x)dx=0\text{ and }\re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)\nabla Q(x)dx=0,

where
g=e^{-i\theta}f(\cdot+x)-Q
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
NLS
) and
I_{\mu_{0}}:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|\mu(u(t))|<\mu_{0}\}
. Then by choosing
(x(t),\theta(t))
for

as in Lemma

, we can write

\displaystyle u(t,x+x(t))=e^{i\theta(t)+it}(Q+g)=e^{i\theta(t)+it}(Q+\rho(t)Q+h),

where

\displaystyle\rho(t):=\frac{\re\int gQ^{p}dx}{\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}.

Lemma 3.2

(Estimates for the modulation parameters)
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
NLS
) satisfying
M(u)=M(Q)
and
E(u)=E(Q)
. The following estimates are valid for
t\in I_{\mu_{0}}
, taking
\mu_{0}
smaller if necessary:

\displaystyle|\rho(t)|\approx\|h(t)\|_{H^{1}}\approx\|g(t)\|_{H^{1}}\approx\left|\re\int Qhdx\right|\approx|\mu(u(t))|,

\displaystyle|\rho^{\prime}(t)|+|x^{\prime}(t)|+|\theta^{\prime}(t)|\lesssim|\mu(u(t))|.

.

(2)

For each
J_{1}+1\leq j\leq J_{2}
, we have
t_{n}^{j}=0
and
v^{j}(t)
scatters as
t\to\infty
.

(3)

For each
J_{2}+1\leq j\leq J
, we have
|t_{n}^{j}|\to\infty
. (In particular,
v^{j}(t)
scatters in at least one directions by (2) in Lemma
4.1
. )

Moreover,
J_{1}\geq 1
. (
J_{2}=J_{1}
or
J_{2}=J
may occur. In that case, there is no
j
satisfying the second and third statement, respectively.)

Proof.

Reordering as
t_{n}^{j}=0
for
1\leq j\leq J_{2}
and
|t_{n}^{j}|\to\infty
for
J_{2}+1\leq j\leq J
is done by suitable numbering. We show
J_{1}\geq 1
.

Step 1.
We show that there exists at least one
j
such that
t_{n}^{j}=0
. Suppose that
|t_{n}^{j}|\to\infty
for all
j
. By the Pohozaev identity,
E(\varphi_{n})=E(Q)
, and
\|\nabla\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}/\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}>1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{E(\varphi_{n})}{E(Q)}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\|\nabla\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\geq-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}>1.

Since
|t_{n}^{j}|\to\infty
, we have
\|\NLS(-t_{n}^{j})\psi^{j}\|_{L^{p+1}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
by (2) in Lemma
4.1
. Then we have

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{\|\NLS(-t_{n}^{j})\psi^{j}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}+\frac{\|W_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}+o_{n}(1)

and the right hand side is smaller than 1 for large
n,J
. This is a contradiction.

Step 2.
It remains to prove that there exists at least one
1\leq j\leq J_{2}
such that
v^{j}(t):=\NLS(t)\psi(t)
does not scatter in the positive time direction. Suppose the statement fails. Then we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|v^{j}(t)\|_{L^{p+1}}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq J_{2}
since they scatter. Take
t_{0}
sufficiently large such that
\|v^{j}(t_{0})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq\varepsilon/J_{2}
for all
1\leq j\leq J_{2}
. Let
u_{n}(t):=\NLS(t)\varphi_{n}
. Then the variational structure implies
\|u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{p+1}}/\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}>1
for all
n
and
t
.
By the orthogonality (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{n}(t_{0})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J_{2}}\|v^{j}(t_{0})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+\sum_{j=J_{2}+1}^{J}\|v^{j}(t_{0}-t_{n}^{j})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+\|W_{n}^{J}(t_{0})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\sum_{j=J_{2}+1}^{J}\|v^{j}(t_{0}-t_{n}^{j})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+\|W_{n}^{J}(t_{0})\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+o_{n}(1).

Taking large
J
and the limit
n\to\infty
, the right hand side goes to
\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, this is a contradiction.
∎

5.
Proof of Main Theorem

5.1.
Compactness of the uniformly bounded solution

In what follows, let
u_{0}
satisfy (
MEN
) and
u
be a solution to (
NLS
) on at least
[0,\infty)
satisfying
\mathbf{A}:=\sup_{t>0}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty
. In this section, we show that the solution

has a compactness property.

Proposition 5.1
.

Then for any time sequence
\{t_{n}\}
, there exists a subsequence, which is still denoted by
\{t_{n}\}
, a spacial sequence
\{x_{n}\}
, and a function
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u(t_{n},\cdot+x_{n})
converges to
\psi
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
4.1
with
\varphi_{n}=u(t_{n})
,
and reordering Lemma
4.3
.
Since for
j\in[J_{1}+1,J]
,
v^{j}
scatters in at least one direction,
E(\psi^{j})=E(v^{j})\geq 0
. Thus by the energy decoupling we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J_{1}}E(\psi^{j})\leq E(u(t_{n}))+o_{n}(1)=E(Q)+o_{n}(1)\to E(Q).

Thus there exists
j\in[1,J_{1}]
such that
E(\psi^{j})\leq E(Q)
. We may assume that
j=1
. We also have

\displaystyle M(\psi^{1})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J_{1}}M(\psi^{j})\leq M(u(t_{n}))+o_{n}(1)=M(Q)+o_{n}(1)\to M(Q)

and thus
M(\psi^{1})\leq M(Q)
.
By Lemma
4.3
,
v^{1}
is a forward non-scattering solution.

Case 1.

M(\psi^{1})<M(Q)
or
E(\psi^{1})<E(Q)
.

In this case, we have
M(\psi^{1})^{1-s_{c}}E(\psi^{1})^{s_{c}}<M(Q)^{1-s_{c}}E(Q)^{s_{c}}
.
It follows from
[
10
,
1
,
13
]
that
K(\psi^{1})<0
, and that
v^{1}
blows up or grows up in both directions by
[
1
,
13
,
9
,
14
]
. There exists
T_{0}>0
such that
\|\nabla v^{1}(T_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq 2\mathbf{A}
. By (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\geq\|\nabla u(T_{0}+t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\nabla v^{j}(T_{0}-t_{n}^{j})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla W_{n}^{J}(T_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\|\nabla v^{1}(T_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq 2\mathbf{A}+o_{n}(1),

where we note that
t_{n}^{1}=0
. This is a contradiction.

Case 2.

M(\psi^{1})=M(Q)
and
E(\psi^{1})=E(Q)
.

Since we have
M(\psi^{1})=M(Q)
, by the orthogonality of

-norm, we get

for all

. That is, we have

. The orthogonality of

\|W_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle E(Q)=E(u)

\displaystyle=E(\psi^{1})+E(W_{n}^{1})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=E(Q)+E(W_{n}^{1})+o_{n}(1).

By this and (4) in Lemma
4.1
, it holds that
\|\nabla W_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We get

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n},\cdot+x_{n}^{1})-\psi^{1}\|_{H^{1}}=\|W_{n}^{1}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 5.3

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}|\mu(u(t))|dt\lesssim(1+\sup_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}|X(t)|)\{|\mu(u(t_{1}))|+|\mu(u(t_{2}))|\}.

Lemma 5.7
.

The solution
u
satisfies
\int|x|^{2}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be small. For any
R>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(t)-e^{it+i\theta_{0}}Q(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|x|>R}|\partial_{x}Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}+|Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}dx

Thus taking
T_{\varepsilon}>0
and
R_{\varepsilon}>0
sufficiently large, for
t>T_{\varepsilon}
and
R>R_{\varepsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it+i\theta_{0}}Q(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{C^{-1}}{2}\varepsilon

and

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}|\partial_{x}Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}+|Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}dx\leq\frac{C^{-1}}{2}\varepsilon.

Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon=\varepsilon

for any
t>T_{\varepsilon}
and
R>R_{\varepsilon}
.
By Lemma
5.2
as
X(t)\equiv 0
, we get

\displaystyle|A_{R}(u(t))|\lesssim\varepsilon|\mu(u(t))|

for
t>T_{\varepsilon}
and
R>R_{\varepsilon}

(choosing these larger if necessary).
It follows from this estimate and the localized virial identity that

\displaystyle J_{R}^{\prime\prime}(t)=4K(u(t))+A_{R}(u(t))\leq c\mu(u(t))-\varepsilon\mu(u(t))\lesssim\mu(u(t))

for
t>T_{\varepsilon}
, where we note that
\mu(u(t))<0
.
As a consequence, we find that
J_{R}^{\prime}(t)>0
for
t>T_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, if not, there exists a time
t_{1}>T_{\varepsilon}
such that
J_{R}^{\prime}(t_{1})\leq 0
. Since

for
t>T_{\varepsilon}
,

is decreasing and thus there exists

such that

and

for

becomes negative for large

Hence we have

for

.
It holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(T_{\varepsilon},x)|^{2}dx=J_{R}(T_{\varepsilon})\leq J_{R}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

for all

.
Now we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{2}\|u(t)-e^{it+i\theta_{0}}Q(\cdot-x_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}dx

and thus

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|Q(x-x_{0})|^{2}dx<C.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(T_{\varepsilon},x)|^{2}dx\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq C.

Since

for all

, it follows from the Fatou lemma that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|u(T_{\varepsilon},x)|^{2}dx\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{R}(x)|u(T_{\varepsilon},x)|^{2}dx\leq C.

This completes the proof.
∎

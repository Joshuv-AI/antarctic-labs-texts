Truncated quantum observables and their semiclassical limit
Fabio Deelan Cunden, Marilena Ligabò, Maria Caterina Susca

Abstract
. For quantum observables H H truncated on the range of orthogonal projections Π N \Pi_{N} of rank N N , we study the corresponding Weyl symbol in the phase space in the semiclassical limit of vanishing Planck constant ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 and large quantum number N → ∞ N\to\infty , with ℏ ​ N \hbar N fixed. Under certain assumptions, we prove the L 2 L^{2} - convergence of the Weyl symbols to a symbol truncated (hence, in general discontinuous) on the classically allowed region in phase space. As an illustration of the general theorems we analyse truncated observables for the harmonic oscillator and for a free particle in a one-dimensional box. In the latter case, we also compute the microscopic pointwise limit of the symbols near the boundary of the classically allowed region.



1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and
\left(\Pi_{N}\right)_{N\geq 1}
a monotone family of orthogonal projections,
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{1}\subset\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{2}\subset\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{3}\subset\cdots
, with
\operatorname{rank}\Pi_{N}=N
for all
N\geq 1
.
If
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{N}\subset D(H)
, it makes sense to consider the
truncated quantum observables

H_{N}:=\Pi_{N}H\Pi_{N},

and, under some integrability conditions, the associated Weyl symbols
\sigma_{H_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)
, where
\hbar>0
. The question addressed in this paper is the
semiclassical limit
of
\sigma_{H_{N}}^{\hbar}
, as
\hbar\to 0
.

Truncated linear operators emerge in a variety of mathematical settings, and the study of their semiclassical limit is an interesting area in it own right, fueled mainly by applications in quantum theory.
In fact, our main motivation for this study comes from the semiclassical limit of Zeno Hamiltonians and Zeno dynamics
[
4
,
19
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
29
,
30
]
. In the particular case
H=I
(the identity operator), the semiclassical limit of the Weyl symbol is closely related to the scaling limits of determinantal point processes
[
25
,
3
]
often applied to active research topics that include eigenvalues of large random matrices, and number statistics of non-interacting fermions
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
26
,
31
,
36
]
. We also mention that spectral truncations (often interpreted energy cut-offs) of operator algebras have been recently studied in the development of the relation between non-commutative geometry and physics, see e.g.
[
5
,
14
,
28
]
.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In the next section we recall some some preliminary
background material. In Section
3
we present a general discussion and the main results on the semiclassical limit of symbols of truncated observables in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
). All Theorems in Section
3
can be extend to higher dimensions for truncated observables in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
d\geq 1
. Here we keep discussing the case
d=1
for sake of simplicity. In Section
3.1
we illustrate the main theorems for the spectral projections of the harmonic oscillator introduced in
[
8
]
and discuss the semiclassical limit of a large class of truncated observables.
In Section
4
we consider the spectral projections of a free particle in a one-dimensional box.
The model is exactly solvable and provides a rich playground to prove the semiclassical limit of truncated observables by manipulation of explicit formulae. In addition to
L^{2}
-convergence we prove in this case the pointwise convergence of the symbols at microscopic scales.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce some notation and recall definitions and basic results about Fourier transform, linear operators, Weyl symbols and Moyal product, see
[
20
,
21
,
27
,
22
,
23
]
.

Fourier transform

In this paper,
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the Fourier transform and its inverse,

(2.1)

\left(\mathcal{F}\varphi\right)(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}\varphi(p)e^{-ipx}dp,\quad\left(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi\right)(p):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\psi(x)e^{ipx}dx.

Plancherel’s theorem reads

(2.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}\overline{\psi(p)}\varphi(p)dp=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(y)}\mathcal{F}\varphi(y)dy.

For functions of several variables,
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is the partial Fourier transform in the
j
-th variable, and
\mathcal{F}_{j}^{-1}
is its inverse.

Linear operators and kernels

For a linear operator
T
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
we denote, if it exists, by
K_{T}(x,y)
its integral kernel,
Tu(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}K_{T}(x,y)u(y)dy
.
The
conjugate kernel

\widehat{K}_{T}:=\mathcal{F}K_{T}\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is

(2.3)

\widehat{K}_{T}\left(p,q\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}K_{T}(x,y)e^{-i(px-qy)}dxdy.

We have the identity

(2.4)

\widehat{K}_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}p-\frac{q}{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p+\frac{q}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\hbar K_{T}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{i(qx+py)}dxdy.

Moreover, if
K_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})
then
T
is an Hilbert-Schmidt operator and

\|T\|_{HS}^{2}=\textrm{Tr}(T^{\ast}T)=\|K_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})}.

Weyl symbol and Weyl quantisation

Given
\hbar>0
, the
Weyl symbol
of an operator
T

is defined as

(2.5)

\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}(x,p):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\hbar K_{T}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{ipy}dy.

If
T
is self-adjoint, then
K_{T}
is an Hermitian kernel, i.e.
K_{T}(y,x)=\overline{K_{T}(x,y)}
, and the symbol
\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}
is real-valued.

In terms of Fourier transform, the Weyl symbol
\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}
is

(2.6)

\left(\mathcal{F}_{2}\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}\right)(x,y)=\left(2\pi\hbar\right)K_{T}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right).

The relation with the conjugate kernel
\widehat{K}_{T}
is

(2.7)

\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{q}}\widehat{K}_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}p-\frac{q}{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p+\frac{q}{2}\right)e^{iqx}dq,

or equivalently

(2.8)

\left(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{T}^{\hbar}\right)(q,p)=\widehat{K}_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}p-\frac{q}{2},\frac{1}{\hbar}p+\frac{q}{2}\right).

From Plancherel’s theorem, if
T
is Hilbert-Schmidt, then

(2.9)

\|\sigma^{\hbar}_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=2\pi\hbar\|K_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})}=2\pi\hbar\|\widehat{K}_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{q}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}.

If
f=f(x,p)
is a real-valued function, its Weyl quantisation
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
is an operator acting (on a suitable subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
) as

(2.10)

\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\psi(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}f\left(\frac{x+y}{2},p\right)e^{i\frac{p}{\hbar}(x-y)}\psi(y)\frac{dydp}{2\pi\hbar}.

Moyal product

Given two linear operators
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\operatorname{Ran}(T_{2})\subset D(T_{1})
, with Weyl symbols
\sigma_{T_{1}}^{\hbar}
and
\sigma_{T_{2}}^{\hbar}
respectively, the
Moyal product
of
\sigma_{T_{1}}^{\hbar}
and
\sigma_{T_{2}}^{\hbar}
, denoted
\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{1}}\,\sharp\,\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{2}}
is the function defined by the equation

(2.11)

\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{1}}\,\sharp\,\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{2}}(x,p)=\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{1}T_{2}}(x,p).

Explicit formulae are:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{1}}\,\sharp\,\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{2}}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{\frac{2i}{\hbar}[(x-x_{1})(p-p_{2})-(x-x_{2})(p-p_{1})]}\prod_{i=1,2}\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{i}}(x_{i},p_{i})\frac{dx_{i}dp_{i}}{\pi\hbar}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{-i(k_{1}x_{2}-k_{2}x_{1})}\prod_{i=1,2}\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{i}}\left(x-x_{i},p-\frac{\hbar k_{i}}{2}\right)\frac{dx_{i}dk_{i}}{2\pi}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}e^{-i(p_{1}y_{2}-p_{2}y_{1})}\prod_{i=1,2}\sigma^{\hbar}_{T_{i}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y_{i}}{2},p-p_{i}\right)\frac{dy_{i}dp_{i}}{2\pi}.

Dirichlet and sine kernels

The
Dirichlet kernel
is defined as

(2.15)

D_{N}(x):=\sum_{|k|\leq N}e^{ikx}=\left(1+2\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos(kx)\right)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin\left((2N+1)\frac{x}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}&\text{if $x\neq 0$}\\
2N+1&\text{if $x=0$}\end{cases}.

for all nonnegative integers
N
. The
sine kernel
is defined as

(2.16)

S(x):=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{ikx}dk=\frac{\sin\frac{x}{2}}{\frac{x}{2}}.

(It is the Fourier transform of the indicator function
\chi_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]}
.)

3.
General discussion and main results

To get an idea of what one can expect as a limit for
\sigma_{H_{N}}^{\hbar}
, we consider the simplest setting where
H=I
is the identity operator, and so
H_{N}=\Pi_{N}
.
The orthogonal projections
\Pi_{N}
can be represented as

(3.1)

\Pi_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|u_{k}\rangle\langle u_{k}|,

for some orthonormal basis
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of an infinite dimensional subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
(hereafter we use the Dirac notation
|u_{k}\rangle\langle u_{k}|
to denote the projection onto
\textrm{span}\{u_{k}\}
).
A first remark should be made about the symbol of
\Pi_{N}
:

\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{|u_{k}\rangle\langle u_{k}|}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\overline{u_{k}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)}u_{k}\left(x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{-ipy}\,dy,

hence
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})\cap C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
, and
\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=\sqrt{2\pi\hbar N}
.
Then, for all
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
, using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have the basic estimate

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)\varphi(x,p)dxdp\right|\leq\sqrt{2\pi\hbar N}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore,
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
has a trivial weak limit, as
\hbar\to 0
. In fact, the basic estimate (
3.2
) suggests that something interesting can emerge in the simultaneous limit
\hbar\to 0
,
N\to\infty
with the product
\hbar N=\mu>0
fixed.

Indeed, when
\hbar N=\mu
, the family
(\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar})_{N\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
and therefore has a limit
\sigma\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
, upon extraction of a subsequence. If the symbols
(\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar})_{N\geq 1}
were regular, say
C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
, then

(3.3)

\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}=\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{2}}^{\hbar}=\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\,\sharp\,\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}=\left(\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\right)^{2}+O(\hbar)

as
\hbar\to 0
, see
[
21
]
. Hence, the limit must satisfy
\sigma=\sigma^{2}
: the limit symbol can only take value
0
or
1
, excluding Lebesgue negligible sets. We conclude that if the limit is not zero, then it must be the
characteristic function

\sigma=\chi_{\Omega}
of a
bounded
region
\Omega\in{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}
in the phase space. This informal discussion can be made precise when the
\Pi_{N}
’s are the spectral projections of Schrödinger operators: in that case we expect the symbols
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
to concentrate on the corresponding classically allowed region
\Omega
. (See also
[
24
]
.)

Consider the Schrödinger operator
A=\frac{1}{2}\hat{p}^{2}+V(\hat{x})
, where
\hat{p}=\frac{\hbar}{i}\frac{d}{dx}
and
V(\hat{x})
the multiplication operator by
V(x)
. We work in the convenient setting where
V\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, bounded from below, and confining,
|V(x)|\to+\infty
, if
|x|\to+\infty
. Then,
A
is essentially self-adjoint on
D(A)=H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\colon Vu\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\}
and has compact
resolvent: it admits a non-decreasing sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of eigenvalues tending to
+\infty
, and the associated eigenvectors
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
form a basis in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Note that the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
A
depend on
\hbar
, in general. By an appropriate shift of the potential
V(x)
we can assume without loss of generality that
A
is positive.

The Schrödinger operator
A
is the Weyl quantisation
A=\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(a)
of the
classical Hamiltonian function

(3.4)

a(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}{p}^{2}+V({x}),

see (
2.10
).
Let
\Pi_{N}=\chi_{(-\infty,E_{N}]}(A)
be spectral projections of
A
. By the integrated Weyl law (see for instance
[
37
, Thm. 6.8]
),

(3.5)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}E_{N}=E

with
E=E(\mu)
being given by the solution of

(3.6)

\mu=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}dxdp=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\sqrt{2\left(E-V(x)\right)_{+}}dx,

where

(3.7)

\Omega=\{(x,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}\colon a(x,p)\leq E\}

is the
classically allowed region
(we omit the dependence of
\Omega
on
E
or
\mu
). Then, in the simultaneous limit of small Planck’s constant
\hbar
and large quantum number
N
, one expects that
{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
converges to
\chi_{\Omega}
. Here is a precise statement.

Theorem 3.1
.

Set
\mu>0
. Then, under the above hypotheses on
V(x)
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\chi_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is postponed to Section
3.2
.

Remark 1
.

The limit in Theorem
3.1
does
not
hold pointwise, in general. Suppose for instance that
V(x)
is even. Then, the eigenfunctions of
A
have defined parity
u_{k}(-x)=(-1)^{k-1}u_{k}(x)
,
k\geq 1
. It is then easy to verify that
{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(0,0)=1+(-1)^{N+1}
. The strong
L^{2}
convergence only implies the existence of a subsequence
\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N_{k}}}
that converges almost everywhere to
\chi_{\Omega}
.

Remark 2
.

The fact that the ‘macroscopic’ limit of the symbols is a characteristic function is a universality result. It is the translation (in the phase space) of the universality of the
sine kernel
for the integral kernel of spectral projections
[
13
]
. The universality of the sine kernel is well-known for the
Christoffel-Darboux kernels
of families of orthogonal polynomial on the real line
[
35
]
. Theorem
3.1
can be extend to dimension
d\geq 1
(with the sine kernel replaced by more general Bessel type kernels).

Remark 3
.

Note that the limit symbol
\chi_{\Omega}
is a discontinuous function in
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}
. A natural question is what happens at the boundary
\partial\Omega
in a ‘microscopic limit’, i.e. if we consider the limit of the symbols
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x_{\hbar},y_{\hbar})
with sequences
(x_{\hbar},y_{\hbar})
converging to the boundary
\partial\Omega
as
\hbar\to 0
. An analogue of Theorem
3.1
can be stated in terms of rescaled Fourier transforms of the symbols. Suppose that
x_{0}
is a point of inversion for the classical motion, i.e.
V(x_{0})=E
. If
V^{\prime}(x_{0})\neq 0
, then for all compact sets
W\Subset{\mathbb{R}}
, there exist constants
\hbar_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in W}\left|\hbar^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\left(x_{0},\hbar^{-\frac{1}{3}}y\right)-\mathcal{F}_{2}{\chi_{\Omega}^{(N)}}\left(y\right)\right|\leq C\hbar^{\frac{1}{3}}

for all
0<\hbar<\hbar_{0}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{2}{\chi_{\Omega}^{(N)}}
is the
integrated Airy function
, see
[
8
]
. This universal result (adapted from
[
13
]
) should be compared to the key estimate (
3.29
) underlying Theorem
3.1
.
We stress that the microscopic limit of the symbol of the projections at the boundary of the classically allowed region depends on the behaviour of the potential
V(x)
at the points
x_{0}
of inversion of classical motion. When
V
is regular with non-zero derivative at
x_{0}
, it is natural to expect the
Airy scaling
and asymptotic symbols expressed in terms of Airy functions. Different behaviours of the potential
V(x)
give rise to different scaling limits. See Theorem
4.5
in Section
4.2
for an explicit calculation of the microscopic limit of the symbols for a free particle confined in a box (
V(x)
is formally infinite outside an interval). These models with ‘hard wall potentials’ were studied in detail in
[
11
]
.

If we now consider the truncated operators
H_{N}=\Pi_{N}H\Pi_{N}
, one expects the symbol
\sigma_{H_{N}}^{\hbar}
to converge to the restriction of the
principal symbol
of
H
to the classically allowed region, namely
\sigma_{H}\chi_{\Omega}
, where, rather informally,
\sigma_{H}=\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\sigma_{H}^{\hbar}
. Let us consider
H=\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
the Weyl quantisation of a real-valued function
f(x,p)
on the phase space, with
\operatorname{Ran}(\Pi_{N})\subset D(H)
. This choice simplifies the problem since the symbol of
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
is just

(3.10)

\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)}^{\hbar}(x,p)=f(x,p),

and thus coincides with its principal symbol (independent of
\hbar
).
As special cases the reader can think of
H=f(\hat{x})
being the multiplication of operator by
f(x)
in the position representation or
H=f(\hat{p})
the multiplication operator by
f(p)
in the momentum representation.

Note that in general
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
and
\Pi_{N}
do
not
commute, and therefore

\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\neq{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)}^{\hbar}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}.

Nevertheless for
\hbar\to 0
one expects the algebra of operators to reduce to a commutative algebra, so that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)+\sigma_{[\Pi_{N},\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)]\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle\sim\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)}^{\hbar}\,\sharp\,\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle=f\,\sharp\,\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle\sim f(x,p)\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p).

The first approximation symbol ‘
\sim
’ amounts to say that
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
and
\Pi_{N}
asymptotically commute on the range of
\Pi_{N}
. The second ‘
\sim
’ says that in the semiclassical limit, the noncommutative Moyal product of
f
and the symbols of
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
reduces to the commutative pointwise product.

The next Theorems provide sufficient conditions for those two ‘approximations’ to hold (recall that here
\hbar\to 0
and
N\to\infty
simultaneously) thus implying that, if the symbols of
\Pi_{N}
have a weak limit, then do so the symbols of the truncated observables
\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}
.

In the following,
\left(\Pi_{N}\right)_{N\geq 1}
is a monotone family of orthogonal projections
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{1}\subset\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{2}\subset\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{3}\subset\cdots
, with
\operatorname{rank}\Pi_{N}=N
for all
N\geq 1
. Denote by
\Pi_{N}^{\perp}:=1-\Pi_{N}
the orthogonal projection onto
\operatorname{Ran}(\Pi_{N})^{\perp}
. We assume that
H
is a (densely defined) self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\operatorname{Ran}(\Pi_{N})\subset D(H)
, for all
N\geq 1
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mu>0
, and set
\hbar=\mu/N
. Define the following Conditions on
\left(\Pi_{N}\right)_{N\geq 1}
and
H
:

(C0)

The sequence
\left({\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\right)_{N\geq 1}
weakly converges to
\sigma\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
, as
N\to\infty
, with
\hbar N=\mu
;

(C1)

The sequence
\left(\sigma_{\Pi_{N}H\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\right)_{N\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
;

(C2)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}H\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0
.

Theorem
3.1
provides a large class of orthogonal projections satisfying Condition (
C0
).
Clearly, all bounded operators
H
satisfy (
C1
), and Condition (
C2
) is trivially met if
H
and
\Pi_{N}
commute. However the class of operators obeying Conditions (
C1
)-(
C2
) is much larger. See, e.g., the guiding example in Section
3.1
. Typically, condition (
C2
) is the harder to check.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mu>0
, and set
\hbar=\mu/N
. Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be continuous real-valued function, and let

H=\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f),\quad f(x,p)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(p)

be defined by

(3.11)

H\psi(x)=f_{1}(x)\psi(x)+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}f_{2}(p)e^{i\frac{p}{\hbar}(x-y)}\psi(y)dy\right)\frac{dp}{2\pi\hbar}.

If Conditions (
C0
)-(
C1
)-(
C2
) hold,
then
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
weakly converges to
f\sigma\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
:

(3.12)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\sigma,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0,\quad\text{for all $\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})$}.

If one replaces (
C1
) by

(C1’)

\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}\to\|f\sigma\|_{L^{2}}
,

then,
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
strongly converges to
f\sigma
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
:

(3.13)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\sigma\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0.

We can extend the theorem to a class of operators
H=\mathrm{Op}^{\hbar}\left(f\right)
that ‘mix’
\hat{x}
and
\hat{p}
, under some regularity assumptions.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu>0
and set
\hbar=\mu/N
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
be a real-valued polynomial in
p
with polynomially bounded coefficients in

.
Consider

H=\mathrm{Op}^{\hbar}\left(f\right)

the Weyl quantisation of

defined by (

)
on its maximal domain

. (Namely,

is a differential operator with polynomially bounded coefficients.)

If Conditions (

)-(

)-(

) hold, then

\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
weakly converges to

.

If one replaces (
C1
) by

(C1’)

\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}\to\|f\sigma\|_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}
,

then,
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
strongly converges to
f\sigma
in

.

The same conclusions hold if
f=f(x,p)
is a real-valued polynomial in
x
with polynomially bounded coefficients in

and, in particular, if

is a real-valued polynomial in both

and

.

The proofs of Theorem

and Theorem

are postponed to Section

.

3.1.
Guiding example: the quantum harmonic oscillator

We apply here the main theorems to a specific model that can be thought of as a guiding example. The model fits in the hypotheses of Theorems
3.1
-
3.2
, and was studied in
[
8
]
motivated by an experimental proposal of a quantum Zeno dynamics in QED cavity by Raimond et al.
[
32
,
33
]
.

Let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be the Hermite wavefunctions defined as the normalised eigenfunctions of the quantum harmonic oscillator
A=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{p}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2}\right)
:

u_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{k-1}(k-1)!}}\left(\frac{1}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\hbar}}H_{k-1}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\right),\quad k\geq 1,

where
(H_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are the Hermite polynomials

H_{j}(y)=(-1)^{j}e^{y^{2}}\frac{d^{j}}{dy^{j}}(e^{-y^{2}}),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}},

and the corresponding eigenvalues are
E_{k}=\hbar(k-1/2)
,
k\geq 1
. The classical hamiltonian of the harmonic oscillator is
a(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}\left(p^{2}+x^{2}\right)
.
Throughout this section we consider the nested sequence of orthogonal projections

(3.14)

\Pi_{N}:=\chi_{(-\infty,E_{N}]}(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|u_{k}\rangle\langle u_{k}|,\quad N\geq 1.

If
\mu>0
, then in the limit
\hbar\to 0,N\to\infty
, with
\hbar N=\mu
, we have
E_{N}\to\mu
, therefore, the classically allowed region
\Omega=D
is the disk of radius
\sqrt{2\mu}
,

(3.15)

D=\left\{(x,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}\colon a(x,p)\leq\mu\right\}=\left\{(x,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}\colon x^{2}+p^{2}\leq 2\mu\right\}.

As an example of truncated observables let us consider the truncation of
H=\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
, with
f(x,p)=(ax+bp)^{n}
, with
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n
nonnegative integer, on the range of
\Pi_{N}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Set
\mu>0
.
Then,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}-f\chi_{D}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}

\displaystyle=0,

where
\chi_{D}
is the characteristic function of the disk
D
in (
3.15
).

Proof.

Since
f
is a polynomial in
x
and
p
,
to prove the limit (
3.16
) it is enough to check the conditions of Theorem
3.3
. For the harmonic oscillator the confining potential is
V(x)=x^{2}/2
; hence from Theorem
3.1
we have that the symbol
\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}}
strongly converges in
L^{2}
to the characteristic function
\chi_{D}
,

(3.17)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}}-\chi_{D}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0.

A standard calculation from (
2.10
) shows that the Weyl quantisation of
f
is

\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)=(a\hat{x}+b\hat{p})^{n}.

Using

(3.18)

\displaystyle\hat{x}u_{k}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left[\sqrt{k+1}u_{k+1}+\sqrt{k}u_{k-1}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{p}u_{k}

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left[\sqrt{k+1}u_{k+1}-\sqrt{k}u_{k-1}\right],

we can give a combinatorial formula for the matrix elements of
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
in the basis
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
.
Let
k,l
be two nonnegative integers. A
n
-steps path from
k
to
l
is a
n
-tuple

\gamma=\left((j_{0},j_{1}),(j_{1},j_{2}),\ldots,(j_{n-1},j_{n})\right)\in\underbrace{{\mathbb{N}}^{2}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}_{\textrm{$n$ times}},

such that
j_{0}=k
,
j_{n}=l
, and
|j_{m}-j_{m+1}|=1
for all
m=0,\ldots,n-1
. To a
n
-steps path
\gamma
we assign the weight

w(\gamma):=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(a+(j_{m+1}-j_{m})ib\right)\sqrt{\max(j_{m+1},j_{m})}.

Let
\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)
be the set of
n
-steps paths from
k
to
\ell
. Note that
\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)=\emptyset
whenever
|k-\ell|>n
.
Then, from (
3.18
)
we can write

(3.19)

\langle{u_{\ell},\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)u_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)}w(\gamma)&\text{if $|k-\ell|\leq n$}\\
0&\text{if $|k-\ell|>n$}\end{cases}

We have that for
k\leq\ell
, with
\ell-k\leq n
,

(3.20)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)}w(\gamma)=(a+ib)^{\ell-k}(a-ib)^{n-(\ell-k)}\#\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)k^{\frac{n}{2}}\left[1+o(1)\right],

as
k\to\infty
, where the total number of
n
-steps paths from
k
to
\ell
is

(3.21)

\#\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)=\binom{n}{\ell-k}

Hence, from (
3.19
) we see that
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
is a finite-band operator in the basis
(u_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of the eigenfunctions of
A
. This implies that
\Pi_{N}^{\perp}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}
has rank
n
and

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\left(\frac{\hbar}{2}\right)^{n}\sum_{k\leq N}\sum_{\ell=N+1}^{N+k}\left|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)}w(\gamma)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c(a^{2}+b^{2})^{n}\hbar^{n+1}N^{n},

where
c
is a constant dependent on
n
but not on
N
.
Hence Condition (
C2
) holds true. We now show that Condition (
C1’
) is also met.
We have

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{\hbar^{n+1}}{2^{n}}\sum_{k,\ell\leq N}\left|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}(k,\ell)}w(\gamma)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{\hbar^{n+1}}{2^{n}}(a^{2}+b^{2})^{n}\sum_{k\leq N}k^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}[1+o(1)],\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$}.

We have

\sum_{k\leq N}k^{n}=\frac{N^{n+1}}{n+1}[1+o(1)],\quad\text{as $N\to\infty$},

and

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{2}=\binom{2n}{n},

by Vandermonde’s identity.
Therefore,

(3.23)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=2\pi\mu^{n+1}\left(\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}}{2}\right)^{n}C_{n},

where
C_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
is the
n
-th Catalan number.
The limit is indeed equal to

\|f\sigma_{D}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=\int_{D}(ax+bp)^{2n}dxdp=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2\mu}}r^{2n+1}dr\int_{0}^{2\pi}(a\cos\theta+b\sin\theta)^{2n}d\theta,

see the Lemma below.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

(3.24)

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi}(a\cos\theta+b\sin\theta)^{2n}d\theta=2\pi\frac{\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)^{n}}{2^{2n}}\binom{2n}{n}.

Proof.

Setting
z=e^{i\theta}
we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\oint_{|z|=1}\left(\frac{a}{2}\left(z+z^{-1}\right)+\frac{b}{2i}\left(z-z^{-1}\right)\right)^{2n}\frac{dz}{iz}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2n}i}\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{\left[(a-ib)z^{2}+(a+ib)\right]^{2n}}{z^{2n+1}}dz

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\binom{2n}{n}(a-ib)^{n}(a+ib)^{n},

using Cauchy’s integral formula.
∎

Remark 4
.

The observable
H=\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)
with
f(x,p)=(ax+bp)^{n}
, when
n=1
and
a=0
corresponds to

H=\hat{p}
, and
H_{N}=\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}
is the
truncated momentum operator
on the subspace of the first
N
eigenfunctions of the harmonic oscillator. This truncated observable corresponds to the Zeno Hamiltonian constructed in
[
32
,
33
]
, and studied in
[
8
]
. The convergence of the symbols
\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}}
and
\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}
was proved in
[
8
]
in a different topology.
More precisely, the following space of test functions introduced by Lions and Paul
[
27
]
,

(3.25)

\mathcal{A}=\left\{f\in C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})\colon\mathcal{F}_{2}f\in L^{1}\left({\mathbb{R}}_{y};C_{0}({\mathbb{R}}_{x})\right)\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{A}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\sup_{x}\left|\mathcal{F}_{2}f(x,y)\right|dy
was considered. The normed space
\mathcal{A}
is a separable Banach algebra. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be the its dual. In
[
8
]
it was proved that the symbols
(\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}})_{N\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, and
(\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}})_{N\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
converge in the weak
∗
-topology, i.e.,

(3.26)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}}-\chi_{D},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}}=0,\quad\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma^{\hbar}_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}-p\chi_{D},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}}=0,

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}}
denotes the pairing between
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{A}
.
When trying to extend these results to other models we faced the problem that, while
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are well-suited for some scopes, their are not so for the general problem of truncated quantum observables. In particular, it is more natural to check
\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}H\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
,
rather than
\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}H\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\to 0
.

3.2.
Proofs of the main theorems

We will repeatedly use some classical facts about convergence in Hilbert spaces. In the following
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
denote separable Hilbert spaces with the corresponding scalar products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
and norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{K}}
, see for instance
[
23
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\sigma_{N})_{N\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{H}
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{H}
, and
T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
an isometry.

(1)

If
(\sigma_{N})
is a bounded sequence and
\langle\sigma_{N},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\to\langle\sigma,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
g
in a dense subspace
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{H}
, then
\sigma_{N}
weakly converges to
\sigma
, i.e.
\langle\sigma_{N},\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\to\langle\sigma,\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
;

(2)

If
\sigma_{N}
weakly converges to
\sigma
, and
\|\sigma_{N}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, then
\sigma_{N}
strongly converges to
\sigma
, i.e.
\|\sigma_{N}-\sigma\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
;

(3)

\sigma_{N}
(weakly) converges to
\sigma
in
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
T\sigma_{N}
(weakly) converges to
T\sigma
in
\mathcal{K}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
and
\mathcal{K}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})
. The partial Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{2}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
is (up to a factor
2\pi
) an isometry. Consider
{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
and
\chi_{\Omega}
in
\mathcal{H}
, and their images
\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}
in
\mathcal{K}
.
We report their explicit formulae.

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
N\geq 1
,
\hbar>0
, and
\mu>0
,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\hbar\right)K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right),

(3.28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{2\left(E(\mu)-V(x)\right)_{+}}S(2\sqrt{2\left(E(\mu)-V(x)\right)_{+}}y),

where
K_{\Pi_{N}}
is the integral kernel of
\Pi_{N}
, and
S
is the
sine kernel
defined in (
2.16
).

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
3.1
is the uniform convergence of
\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
to
\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}
on compact sets.

Theorem 3.8
.

For any compact sets
U\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
,
V\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{y}
, there exist constants
\hbar_{0}>0
and
C>0
(both depending on
U,V
), such that

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in U}\sup_{y\in V}\left|\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,y)-\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}\left(x,y\right)\right|\leq C\hbar,

for all
\hbar<\hbar_{0}
.

Proof.

The estimate (
3.29
), is an adapted restatement of the uniform convergence of the rescaled kernel
K_{\Pi_{N}}
to the sine kernel on compact sets, proved in
[
13
, Sec. 3]
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For all
\mu>0
,

(3.30)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})}=0.

Proof.

We simply observe that for all
N
,
\|\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}=\hbar N
and
\|\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}=\mu
. So, with
\hbar=\mu/N
, we have that
(\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar})_{N\geq 1}
is bounded in
\mathcal{K}
and
\|\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{\mathcal{K}}=\|\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\mathcal{K}}
. Therefore we only need to prove weak convergence.
Let
\mathcal{B}=C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y})\subset\mathcal{K}

be the subspace of continuous functions
g
with compact support in
{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y}
. It is a standard fact that
\mathcal{B}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}
. The estimate (
3.29
) with
\hbar=\mu/N
implies that
\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar},g\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}\to\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega},g\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
for all
g\in\mathcal{B}
. By density this proves weak convergence of
\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
to
\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{2}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
is (up to a factor
2\pi
) an isometry, the convergence of
{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
to
\chi_{\Omega}
in
\mathcal{H}
follows from the convergence of
\mathcal{F}_{2}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
to
\mathcal{F}_{2}\chi_{\Omega}
in
\mathcal{K}
obtained in Corollary
3.9
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
f(x,p)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)
.
By Condition (
C1
),
it is enough to prove that

\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\chi_{\Omega},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0,\quad\text{ for all $g\in\mathcal{B}$},

where
\mathcal{B}
is some dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. We write,

\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\chi_{\Omega},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\\
\underbrace{\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\sigma_{f(\hat{x},\hat{p})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}_{I_{1}(N)}+\underbrace{\langle\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}_{I_{2}(N)}+\underbrace{\langle f\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\chi_{\Omega},g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}_{I_{3}(N)}.

The first term can be majorized as

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}(N)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}-\Pi_{N}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}},

and tends to zero by (
C2
).

We now proceed to analyse
I_{2}(N)
and
I_{3}(N)
.
By linearity we can consider separately the cases
f_{2}\equiv 0
and
f_{1}\equiv 0
. We start from the case
f_{2}\equiv 0
, and we write
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)=f_{1}(\hat{x})
. Choose
\mathcal{B}_{1}=C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{x};\mathcal{F}^{-1}(C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{y})))\subset\mathcal{H}
. Let
g\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. The support of
\mathcal{F}_{2}g\in\mathcal{K}
is contained in
U\times V
for some
U\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
, and
V\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{y}
.

For
I_{2}(N)
we first note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{f_{1}(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\hbar f_{1}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{ipy}dy,

\displaystyle f(x,p)\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

\displaystyle=f_{1}(x)\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\hbar f_{1}\left(x\right)K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{ipy}dy.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz, we get

\displaystyle|I_{2}(N)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\left|\left(f_{1}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)-f_{1}\left(x\right)\right)\hbar K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)\mathcal{F}_{2}g(x,y)\right|dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mu}\left(\int_{U\times V}\left|\left(f_{1}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)-f_{1}\left(x\right)\right)\mathcal{F}_{2}g(x,y)\right|^{2}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,

by dominated convergence.

The last term is easily bounded

|I_{3}(N)|=\left|\langle f\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\sigma,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|=\left|\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\sigma,fg\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|\leq\sup_{x\in U}|f_{1}(x)|\left|\langle\sigma_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-\sigma,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right|

since
g(\cdot,p)
has support contained in
U\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{x}
. By Condition (
C0
), we have
|I_{3}(N)|\to 0
.

We now consider the case
f_{1}\equiv 0
, hence
\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)=f_{2}(\hat{p})
. Choose
\mathcal{B}_{2}=\mathcal{F}C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{q};C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{p}))\subset\mathcal{H}
. Let
g\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
. The support of
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{-1}g
is contained in
U\times V
for some
U\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{q}
, and
V\Subset{\mathbb{R}}_{p}
.

We notice that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\operatorname{Op}^{\hbar}(f)\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)

∎

4.
Truncated observables for a free particle in a box

We now study the family of spectral projections of the Schrödinger operator
A=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
acting as

(4.1)

Af(x)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x),

for all
f\in D(A)=H^{2}_{0}([-L,L])
. The self-adjoint operator
A
is the Hamiltonian of a free particle confined in the one-dimensional box
[-L,L]
with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions. The eigenvalues of
A
are

(4.2)

E_{k}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k\pi}{2L}\right)^{2}.

The corresponding normalised eigenfunctions

(4.3)

form an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}([-L,L])
. A useful formula is

(4.4)

U_{k}(\cos x)=\frac{\sin(k+1)x}{\sin x},

where
U_{k}

U_{0}(y)=1
,
U_{1}(y)=2y
and
U_{k}(y)=2yU_{k-1}(y)-U_{k-2}(y)
, for all
k\geq 2
). Using (
4.4
), we can rewrite the eigenfunctions of

as

(4.5)

u_{k}(x)=\frac{\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)}{\sqrt{L}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}(x+L)\right)U_{k-1}\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}(x+L)\right)\right).

Throughout this section we consider the nested sequence of orthogonal projections

(4.6)

\Pi_{N}:=\chi_{(-\infty,E_{N}]}(A)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}|u_{k}\rangle\langle u_{k}|,\quad N\geq 1.

Note that for the Schödinger operator of a particle in a box, the confining potential
V(x)
is formally infinite outside the box and thus this case is not covered by Theorem
3.1
.

In this section we will repeatedly use the following identity (an application of trigonometric formulae and integration by parts).

Lemma 4.1
.

For all

,

(4.7)

\sigma_{|u_{j}\rangle\langle u_{k}|}^{\hbar}(x,p):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{y}}\hbar u_{j}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)u_{k}\left(x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)e^{ipy}dy\\
=\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{\epsilon_{1}e^{-i\epsilon_{2}\frac{\pi}{2L}(j-\epsilon_{1}k)(x+L)}}{\frac{\pi\hbar}{4L}(j+\epsilon_{1}k)+\epsilon_{2}p}\sin\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}(L-|x|)\left(\frac{\pi\hbar}{4L}(j+\epsilon_{1}k)+\epsilon_{2}p\right)\right).

The starting point of the analysis are the explicit formulae for the integral kernel and the symbol of
\Pi_{N}

(Integral kernel and symbol of

\displaystyle K_{\Pi_{N}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}(x)u_{k}(y)

\displaystyle-2\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}\left(L+x\right)\right)\sin\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}(L-\left|x\right|)p\right)}{p}\Biggr].

4.1.
Semiclassical macroscopic limit of

(

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix

and let

and

Let
\hbar_{0}
such that

we have that:

\sup_{x\in U}\sup_{y\in V}\left|2\pi\hbar K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\frac{\hbar y}{2},x+\frac{\hbar y}{2}\right)-\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)\frac{\pi\mu}{L}S\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{L}y\right)\right|\leq C\hbar,

where

Remark 5

, we have

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

We proceed to show (

With this notation we have that the rescaled kernel
2\pi\hbar K_{\Pi_{N}}\left(x-\hbar y/2,x+\hbar y/2\right)

.
Suppose

is differentiable in
(a,b)

,

(4.15)

\left|\frac{(b-a)}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}f(x_{j})-\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx\right|\leq\frac{M_{1}(b-a)^{2}}{2n},\quad M_{1}=\sup_{x\in(a,b)}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|.

Therefore we have

\left|\frac{\pi\hbar}{2L}D_{N}\left(\frac{\hbar\pi y}{2L}\right)-\frac{\pi\mu}{L}S\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{L}y\right)\right|=\left|\frac{\pi\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=-N}^{N}e^{i\frac{\hbar\pi ky}{2L}}-\frac{\pi}{2L}\int_{-\mu}^{\mu}e^{i\frac{\pi ky}{2L}}dk\right|\\

Putting everything together, we get the claim.

{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
develops a jump, for large
N
. The second main result of this section concerns the
pointwise
asymptotics of
{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
near
\partial R
. By symmetry, it is enough to consider the left sides (
x=L
) and the upper side (
p=\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}
) of
\partial R
.

Theorem 4.5

(Pointwise convergence at microscopic scale near the boundary)
.

The following limits hold pointwise:

(4.16)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\left(L-\hbar u,p\right)=\\
\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(u)}{\pi}\left[\operatorname{Si}\left(2u\left(p+\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}\right)\right)-\operatorname{Si}\left(2u\left(p-\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}\right)\right)-\frac{\sin(2pu)}{pu}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{L}u\right)\right],

where
\operatorname{Si}(\cdot)
is the Sine integral function,

\operatorname{Si}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sin t}{t}dt,

and

(4.17)

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\left(x,\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}+\frac{\hbar\pi}{2L}q\right)=\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)}{2L}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-\left|x\right|)\frac{\pi}{2L}(j+q)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}(j+q)},

Proof.

We begin by writing the symbol of
\Pi_{N}
as

{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)\left(A+B-2C\right),

where

\displaystyle A=\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}(L-\left|x\right|)\left(\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}+p\right)\right)}{\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}+p},\quad B=\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sin\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}(L-\left|x\right|)\left(\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}-p\right)\right)}{\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}-p},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}\left(L+x\right)\right)\sin\left(\frac{2p}{\hbar}(L-\left|x\right|)\right)}{p}.

We start by considering the case
x=L-\hbar{u}
. Of course
\chi_{[-L,L]}(L-\hbar{u})\to\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(u)
pointwise, as
\hbar\to 0
. If we replace
x=L-\hbar{u}
with
u\geq 0
in
A
, we obtain a Riemann sum (
\hbar=\mu/N
) of a continuous function, and hence,

A=\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sin\left(2u\left(\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}+p\right)\right)}{\frac{\hbar\pi k}{2L}+p}\to\frac{1}{2L}\int_{0}^{\mu}\frac{\sin\left(2u\left(\frac{\pi\xi}{2L}+p\right)\right)}{2u\left(\frac{\pi\xi}{2L}+p\right)}d\xi=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2u\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}+p\right)}\frac{\sin\xi}{\xi}d\xi.

Similarly, for
B
. We now analyse
C
, for
x=L-\hbar u
,

\displaystyle C=\frac{\sin\left(2pu\right)}{p}\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\hbar k}{L}u\right)

\displaystyle\to\frac{\sin\left(2pu\right)}{p}\frac{1}{2L}\int_{0}^{\mu}\cos\left(\frac{\pi\xi}{L}u\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(2pu\right)}{p}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\mu}{L}u\right).

We now consider the case
p=\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}+\frac{\hbar\pi}{2L}q
. In this case we get

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(k+N+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(k+N+q\right)}+\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(k-N-q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(k-N-q\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{j={N+1}}^{2N}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)}+\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{j={0}}^{2N}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)}-\frac{1}{2L}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(N+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(N+q\right)},

and

\displaystyle-2C

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(N+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}(N+q)}\left(-2\sum_{k=1}^{N}\cos\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}(L+x)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2L}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(N+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}(N+q)}\left(1-\frac{\sin\left((2N+1)\frac{\pi}{4L}(L+x)\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4L}(L+x)\right)})\right).

Hence,

A+B-2C=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{j={0}}^{2N}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)}+R_{N}

where

R_{N}=-\frac{1}{2L}\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(N+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}(N+q)}\frac{\sin\left((2N+1)\frac{\pi}{4L}(L+x)\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{4L}(L+x)\right)}.

Note that
R_{N}=0
for
|x|=L
. When
|x|<L
, we have
|R_{N}|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. To see that the series in (
4.17
) is convergent, we sandwich the sequence
\left(\frac{\sin\left(2(L-|x|)\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)\right)}{\frac{\pi}{2L}\left(j+q\right)}\right)_{j\geq 0}
by two alternate sequences that satisfy the Leibniz criterion. The claim is proved.
∎

For a numerical illustration, see Figure
1
.

4.3.
Semiclassical limit of
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}H\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}

We now address the problem of genuinely truncated quantum observables
\Pi_{N}H\Pi_{N}
, with
\Pi_{N}
the spectral projection (
4.6
) onto the first
N
levels of a free particle in a box,
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{N}\subset D(H)
and
[H,\Pi_{N}]\neq 0
.

We present here two explicit examples that can be thought of as natural analogues of the truncated momentum operator in the harmonic oscillator eigenbasis of Section
3.1
.

4.3.1.
Multiplication by a tridiagonal operator

Define
H=f(\hat{x})
as a multiplication operator on
D(f(\hat{x}))=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
where

(4.18)

f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}x\right).

Note that
f(\hat{x})
does not commute with the spectral projections
\Pi_{N}
. We have instead,

(4.19)

f(\hat{x})u_{k}=-\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{L}}u_{k-1}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{L}}u_{k+1}\right).

We remark that
f(\hat{x})
is tridiagonal in the basis
\{u_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, and in this sense it is the analogue of the operator
\hat{p}
for the Hermite wavefunctions considered in Section
3.1
.
From (
4.19
) we get an explicit formula for the integral kernel of the truncated observable

(4.20)

\displaystyle K_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{L}}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left[u_{k}(x)u_{k+1}(y)+u_{k+1}(x)u_{k}(y)\right].

Equivalently, the matrix representation of
\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}
in the basis
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
is the
N\times N
real symmetric matrix

(4.21)

-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{L}}\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0&\ldots&&\\
1&0&1&0&&&\\
0&1&0&1&&&\\
0&0&1&0&&\\
\vdots&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&0&1\\
&&&&&1&0\end{bmatrix}.

We can check the Conditions of Theorem
3.2
:

(C1)

\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded, and in fact it converges to
\|f\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}}
. Indeed (see the matrix representation (
4.21
)),

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\|K_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N})^{2}=2\pi\hbar\frac{1}{4L}2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}1^{2}\to\frac{\pi\mu}{L}.

On the other hand,

\|f\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{R}f(x)^{2}dxdp=\int_{-\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}}^{\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}}dp\int_{-L}^{L}\frac{1}{L}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}x\right)dx=\frac{\pi\mu}{L};

(C2)

{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
strongly converges to
\chi_{R}\in L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
(see Theorem
4.3
);

(C3)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}=0
by the tridiagonal structure of
\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}
:

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)-\sigma_{m(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\|K_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}-K_{f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\frac{1}{4L}\|u_{N+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|u_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{\pi\hbar}{2L}\to 0;

We conclude that

(4.22)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}f(\hat{x})\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}-f\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=0.

4.3.2.
Truncated momentum

We now consider the operator
H=\hat{p}
with domain
D(\hat{p})=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, acting as

\hat{p}f(x)=-i\hbar f^{\prime}(x)
.
Note that
\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{N}\subset D(\hat{p})
, but
[\Pi_{N},\hat{p}]\neq 0
. In fact, in contrast to the previous example, the commutator
[\Pi_{N},\hat{p}]
is
not
a finite rank operator.
The integral kernel of the truncated momentum
\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}
is

(4.23)

\displaystyle K_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}K_{\Pi_{N}}(x,z)\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)K_{\Pi_{N}}(z,y)dz=\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}C^{\hbar}_{j,k}u_{j}(x)u_{k}(y),

where

(4.24)

C^{\hbar}_{j,k}=\langle u_{j}|\hat{p}|u_{k}\rangle=-\frac{i\hbar}{L}\left[1-(-1)^{j+k}\right]\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}.

The matrix representation of
\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}
in the basis
\left\{u_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
is the
N\times N
complex hermitian matrix

(4.25)

-\frac{i\hbar}{L}\begin{bmatrix}0&-\frac{4}{3}&0&-\frac{8}{15}&\ldots&\\
\frac{4}{3}&0&-\frac{12}{5}&0&&\vdots\\
0&\frac{12}{5}&0&-\frac{24}{7}&\\
\frac{8}{15}&0&\frac{24}{7}&\ddots&\\
\\
\vdots&&&&0&-\frac{2N(N-1)}{2N-1}\\
&\cdots&&&\frac{2N(N-1)}{2N-1}&0\end{bmatrix}.

The Weyl symbol of
\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}
is

(4.26)

\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)=\chi_{[-L,L]}(x)\frac{\hbar}{2L}\sum_{1\leq k<j\leq N}C^{\hbar}_{j,k}\times\\
\sum_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}=\pm 1}\frac{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}{\frac{\hbar\pi}{4L}(j+\epsilon_{1}k)+\epsilon_{2}p}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}(j-\epsilon_{1}k)(x+L)\right)\sin\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}(L-|x|)\left(\frac{\hbar\pi}{4L}(j+\epsilon_{1}k)+\epsilon_{2}p\right)\right).

To derive (
4.26
) we used (
4.7
) and the facts that
C_{j,k}
is purely imaginary,
C_{j,k}=\overline{C_{k,j}}
, and
\sigma_{|u_{j}\rangle\langle u_{k}|}^{\hbar}=\overline{\sigma_{|u_{j}\rangle\langle u_{k}|}^{\hbar}}
.

This operator is the analogue of the truncated momentum of Sec.
3.1
. Here we replace the
harmonic oscillator spectral projections with the spectral projections of the free particle in a box. This apparently innocuous replacement destroys the tridiagonal structure of the truncated momentum.

We now proceed to show that
\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
does fit in the hypotheses of Theorem
3.2
:

(C1)

\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=2\pi\hbar\|K_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}|C_{j,k}^{\hbar}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\left[1-(-1)^{j+k}\right]^{2}\left(\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2}

We change variables
m=j+k
,
n=j-k
, and bound the sum from above by

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=2\end{subarray}}^{2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=|m-N-1|-(N-1)\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{|m-N-1|+(N-1)}4\left(\frac{m}{n}-\frac{n}{m}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{2N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=-N\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{N}8\left(\frac{m^{2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}2\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{m=1}^{2N}m^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{9}\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}\zeta(2)N^{3}=\frac{2^{9}\pi^{3}\mu^{2}}{6L^{2}}.

(C2)

{\sigma}_{\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}
converges strongly to
\chi_{R}\in L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})
(see Theorem
4.3
);

(C3)

We show that
\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}=0
. Let
B_{\hbar,N}:=\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}^{\perp}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}}
. Then,

(4.27)

\displaystyle B_{\hbar,N}=2\pi\hbar\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq N\\
j>N\end{subarray}}|C_{j,k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\pi\hbar^{3}}{L^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq N\\
j>N\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi\mu^{3}}{L^{2}}\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq N\\
j>N\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2}.

We now write, for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{N}
,

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq N\\
j>N\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq N-\epsilon N\\
j>N+\epsilon N\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{jk}{j^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2}=\int_{0}^{1-\epsilon}{dv}\int_{1+\epsilon}^{\infty}{du}\left(\frac{uv}{u^{2}-v^{2}}\right)^{2}+R_{N}(\epsilon)

for some remainder
R_{N}(\epsilon)
. With the change variables
x=u+v/2
,
y=u-v/2
, the integral simplifies and we can show that

\int_{0}^{1-\epsilon}{dv}\int_{1+\epsilon}^{\infty}{du}\left(\frac{uv}{u^{2}-v^{2}}\right)^{2}\leq C\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}=C\log N,\quad|R_{N}(\epsilon)|\leq C

for some constant
C
independent of
N
. Plugging these estimates in (
4.27
) we see that
B_{\hbar,N}\to 0
.

The latter estimates can be used in fact to show the stronger condition

(C1’)

\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}\to\|p\chi_{R}\|_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}
. Indeed:

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\|K_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\,\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N})^{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\,\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{N}\hat{p}^{2}\Pi_{N})+B_{\hbar,N}

\displaystyle=2\pi\hbar\sum_{k=1}^{N}2E_{k}+B_{\hbar,N}=\frac{\pi^{3}\hbar^{3}}{2L^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}k^{2}+B_{\hbar,N}.

where we used the explicit form of
E_{k}
(
4.2
). The term
B_{\hbar,N}
tends to
0
.
Hence,
\|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}\to\frac{\pi^{3}\mu^{3}}{6L^{2}}
, and also

\|p\chi_{R}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(R_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p})}=\int_{R}p^{2}dxdp=\int_{-\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}}^{\frac{\pi\mu}{2L}}p^{2}dp\int_{-L}^{L}dx=\frac{\pi^{3}\mu^{3}}{6L^{2}}.

We conclude that there is strong convergence of the truncated momentum

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty,\hbar\to 0\\
\hbar N=\mu\end{subarray}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{p}}\left|\sigma_{\Pi_{N}\hat{p}\Pi_{N}}^{\hbar}(x,p)-p\chi_{R}(x,p)\right|^{2}dxdp=0.

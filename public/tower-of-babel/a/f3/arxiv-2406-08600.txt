1.
Introduction

Just two years after Blum
[
1
]
devised his eponymous axioms on complexity measures, McCreight and Meyer
[
5
]
proved that every such measure satisfies a “union theorem”. For deterministic time, non-deterministic time, and deterministic space, all of which are Blum measures, the McCreight-Meyer Union Theorem exhibits three total computable (but non-constructible) functions
t_{1},t_{2}
, and
t_{3}
such that
{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{1})
,
{\mathsf{NP}}={\mathsf{NTIME}}(t_{2})
, and
{\mathsf{PSPACE}}={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t_{3})
. However, since McCreight and Meyer’s proof sensitively depends on the complexity measure in question, it is not at all obvious how
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
, and
t_{3}
relate.

In this paper, we refine McCreight and Meyer’s result and exhibit a total computable and non-decreasing function
t_{\mathsf{poly}}
such that for all
k
,

\displaystyle\,\,\,\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}=\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad{\mathsf{BPP}}={\mathsf{BPTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad{\mathsf{RP}}=\mathsf{RTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad{\mathsf{UP}}=\mathsf{UTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad\quad\;\;\;

\displaystyle{\mathsf{PP}}=\mathsf{PTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad{\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}),\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathsf{PSPACE}}={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}}).\quad\quad\quad\quad

Apparently,
t_{\mathsf{poly}}
is inextricably tied to the polynomial-boundedness of all these classes while simultaneously agnostic to the exact underlying computational model. Unfortunately, this suggests that a better understanding of
t_{\mathsf{poly}}
will probably not help resolve the outstanding relationships between these classes.

The above result follows in two elementary steps. First, we show that for any finite collection of Blum measures
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{\ell}
, which define Blum complexity classes
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{\ell}}
, respectively, there is a total computable and non-decreasing function
t_{\mathsf{poly}}
such that for all
i\in[\ell]
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{poly}}\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(f),
where
\mathsf{poly}
is the set of polynomial functions. Among other things, this establishes that
{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,
{\mathsf{NP}}={\mathsf{NTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
, and
{\mathsf{PSPACE}}={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
. Next, we exploit the fact that classes like
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}},{\mathsf{BPP}},{\mathsf{UP}}
and so forth are definable by applying a particular complexity class operator to
{\mathsf{P}}
. For example,
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}=\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{poly}}\cdot{\mathsf{P}}
. Thus, by the first step,
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}=\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{poly}}\cdot{\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
, and a final elementary argument, which crucially relies on the fact that
t_{\mathsf{poly}}
is non-decreasing, establishes
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{poly}}\cdot{\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})=\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
, as desired.

A more intricate argument works for any (potentially infinite) collection of language classes, provided that each class in the collection is definable by applying a certain complexity class operator to some Blum complexity class. This more general result is the context of our main theorem. We regard it as evidence for a plausible conjecture that was raised to us by Fortnow:

Conjecture A

(Fortnow
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a partial recursive list of Blum measures and let
\mathsf{f}=\{f_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a total recursive list of functions such that if
i\leq j
, then
f_{i}(n)\leq f_{j}(n)
for almost all
n
. There exists a total computable function
t_{\mathsf{f}}
such that for all
i
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(f).

2.2.
The Blum Axioms

We now recall the Blum axioms
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{\varphi_{e}\mid e\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an acceptable Gödel numbering of
\mathscr{P}(\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\Sigma^{*})
. We call
\Phi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{N}\times\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
a
Blum complexity measure
(or
Blum measure
for short) iff:

(i)

for all
e
and
x
,
\Phi(e,x)\downarrow
iff
\varphi_{e}(x)\downarrow
,

(ii)

there exists
f\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\times\Sigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that for all
e
,
x
, and
m
,

f(e,x,m)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\Phi(e,x)=m$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Together, every
t\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
and Blum measure
\Phi
define a
Blum complexity class
(or
Blum class
for short) given by

\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(t)\coloneqq\left\{\varphi_{e}\mid\Phi(e,x)\leq t(|x|)\ \mathrm{a.e.}\right\}.

For example, the number of steps single-tape, deterministic Turing machines take on a given input defines the Blum measure
\mathtt{dtime}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{N}\times\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
, which stands for
deterministic time
. In particular, for the
e
th p.c. function
\varphi_{e}
,
\mathtt{dtime}
is such that for every
x
,
\mathtt{dtime}(e,x)=m
iff there exists a deterministic Turing machine
M
such that
M(x)=\varphi_{e}(x)
and
M
halts in exactly
m
steps. Other examples include
\mathtt{dspace}
(deterministic space) and
\mathtt{ntime}
(non-deterministic time), which are defined like
\mathtt{dtime}
but with the obvious modification.

Besides a few exceptional statements, in this paper we shall mostly reason at the level of language classes. Indeed, every Blum class
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}
defines a
Blum language class

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}
, which is got by considering those languages whose characteristic functions are in
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(t)\coloneqq\left\{L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid\chi_{L}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(t)\right\}.

For example,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathtt{dtime}}(t)={\mathsf{DTIME}}(t)
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mathtt{ntime}}(t)={\mathsf{NTIME}}(t)
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathtt{dspace}}(t)={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t)
.

2.3.
The McCreight-Meyer Union Theorem

In addition to satisfying a speedup theorem
[
1
]
, a compression theorem
[
1
]
, and a gap theorem
[
2
,
6
]
, every Blum measure also satisfies a union theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(McCreight-Meyer Union Theorem
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\Phi
be a Blum measure and let
\mathsf{f}=\{f_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a collection of functions that at least satisfies conditions (a) - (d) in the definition of an acceptable collection. There exists
t_{\mathsf{f}}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that:

(i)

t_{\mathsf{f}}
is non-decreasing,

(ii)

for all
f\in\mathsf{f}
,
f\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}
,

(iii)

\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{C}_{\Phi}(f).

If
\mathsf{f}
only satisfies conditions (a) and (b) in the definition of an acceptable collection, then there still exists
t_{\mathsf{f}}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that statements (ii) and (iii) hold in Theorem
2.1
. This fact is the usual statement of the McCreight-Meyer Union Theorem (Theorem 5.5 in
[
5
]
, see also Theorem J.3 in
[
4
]
for a more modern treatment). However, in this paper it is vital that
t_{\mathsf{f}}
is non-decreasing, which is the case if
\mathsf{f}
also satisfies conditions (c) and (d) in the definition of an acceptable collection. For more on this, we refer the reader to Remark 5.13 in
[
5
]
.

A fascinating corollary of Theorem
2.1
is the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

There exist non-decreasing
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that:

(i)

{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{1})
,

(ii)

{\mathsf{NP}}={\mathsf{NTIME}}(t_{2})
,

(iii)

{\mathsf{PSPACE}}={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t_{3})
.

We now prove a proposition that has many interesting corollaries.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in[\ell]\}
be a finite set of Blum measures and let
\mathsf{f}
be a collection of functions that at least satisfies conditions (a) - (d) in the definition of an acceptable collection. There exists
t_{\mathsf{f}}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that:

(i)

t_{\mathsf{f}}
is non-decreasing,

(ii)

for all
f\in\mathsf{f}
,
f\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}
,

(iii)

for all
i\in[\ell]
,
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, for all
i\in[\ell]
, there is a non-decreasing function
t_{\mathsf{f},i}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f},i})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
and for all
f\in\mathsf{f}
,
f\leq t_{\mathsf{f},i}
. For every
n
, define
t_{\mathsf{f}}(n)\coloneqq\min_{i\in[\ell]}t_{\mathsf{f},i}(n)
. Evidently,
t_{\mathsf{f}}
is total computable because the map
(i,n)\mapsto t_{\mathsf{f},i}(n)
is, and
t_{\mathsf{f}}
is non-decreasing because every
t_{\mathsf{f},i}
is. Additionally, for all
i\in[\ell]
,
t_{\mathsf{f}}(n)\leq t_{\mathsf{f},i}(n)
for all

. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f},i}).
Conversely, for all
i\in[\ell]
and all
f\in\mathsf{f}
,
f\leq t_{\mathsf{f},i}
, i.e., there is
N_{f,i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f(n)\leq t_{\mathsf{f},i}(n)
for all

. Therefore,
f(n)\leq\min_{i\in[\ell]}t_{\mathsf{f},i}(n)=t_{\mathsf{f}}(n)
for all

. Consequently,
f\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}
, so also
\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f},i})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
.
∎

There exists a non-decreasing function

(i)

,

(ii)

,

(iii)

Ultimately, our goal in this paper is to extend Proposition

to an infinity of classes—such as every level of the polynomial hierarchy—which is our contribution toward Fortnow’s Conjecture

Conjecture A

(Fortnow
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a partial recursive list of Blum measures and let
\mathsf{f}
be a collection of functions that at least satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) in the definition of an acceptable collection. There exists

such that for all

Towards this conjecture, it is natural to try to generalize our proof of Proposition

to the infinite case by taking

. Therefore, while establishing this latter containment in the finite case is relatively straightforward, it is not so—and in fact it appears to be rather difficult—in the infinite case.

3.
Complexity Class Operators for General Blum Language Classes

Our main result concerns language classes that are definable by applying a certain complexity class operator to some Blum language class. While there are many different complexity class operators, the bulk of them operate either (i) by alternately existentially and universally quantifying strings of a certain size that satisfy some global condition or (ii) by counting “witness strings” of a certain size that also satisfy some global condition. This observation compels us to introduce two new complexity class operators, which collectively encapsulate many of the well-known operators.

3.1.
The Operator
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}
and the Class
\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\Phi}

The levels
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}
of the polynomial hierarchy exhibit certain structure that not all language classes afford. In particular, each level
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}
is definable as the class obtained after applying the complexity class operator
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{poly}}
to the Blum language class
{\mathsf{P}}
. We define the most general form of this operator as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For a class
\mathcal{L}
of languages,
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}
is the class of languages
L
for which there exist
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathsf{f}
and
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}
such that for all
x
,

(3)

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{f_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{f_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{f_{k}(|x|)}\right)\chi_{L^{\prime}}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1,

where

is

if

is odd and

It is easily seen that

Observation 3.1

For all acceptable collections
\mathsf{f}

and

\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}

\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}

Importantly, for general

, the class
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{poly}}\cdot{\mathsf{DTIME}}(t)
is not
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t)

Definition 3.2
.

The class
\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t)
consists of all languages

(ii)

Definition 3.6

and
\mathsf{RTIME}(t)

and
\mathsf{UTIME}(t)

4.
Statement of Main Result

We now state our main theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection and let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in[\ell]\}
be a finite number of Blum measures. There exists a non-decreasing function
t_{\mathsf{f}}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that for all
i\in[\ell]
, all
k
, and all witness criteria
\omega
:

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
,

(ii)

\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
,

(iii)

\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
.

Note how this establishes an
infinity
of complexity classes that are all characterized by the
same
bound
t_{\mathsf{f}}
. We regard this as evidence for Conjecture
A
. Importantly, Theorem
A
relativizes.

Notice, with
\mathsf{f}=\mathsf{poly}
and just the single Blum measure
\mathtt{dtime}
, Theorem
A
yields our main corollary, which is an even stronger version of Corollary
2.4
.

Corollary A
.

There exists a non-decreasing function
t_{\mathsf{poly}}\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N})
such that for all
k
and all witness criteria
\omega
:

(i)

{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{DTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(ii)

{\mathsf{NP}}={\mathsf{NTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(iii)

{\mathsf{coNP}}={\mathsf{co}}{\mathsf{NTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(iv)

{\mathsf{PP}}=\mathsf{PTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(v)

{\mathsf{BPP}}={\mathsf{BPTIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(vi)

{\mathsf{RP}}=\mathsf{RTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(vii)

{\mathsf{co}}{\mathsf{RP}}={\mathsf{co}}\mathsf{RTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(viii)

{\mathsf{UP}}=\mathsf{UTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(ix)

{\mathsf{co}}{\mathsf{UP}}={\mathsf{co}}\mathsf{UTIME}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(x)

{\mathsf{PSPACE}}={\mathsf{DSPACE}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(xi)

{\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(xii)

{\mathsf{co}}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{co}}{\mathsf{Mod}}_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(xiii)

\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{P}}=\Sigma_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})
,

(xiv)

\Pi_{k}{\mathsf{P}}=\Pi_{k}{\mathsf{TIME}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})

(xv)

\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}_{\mathtt{dtime}}^{\mathsf{poly}}(t_{\mathsf{poly}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{poly}}\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}_{\mathtt{dtime}}^{\mathsf{poly}}(f)
.

Of course, save statement (xv), Corollary
A
more or less follows from our informal discussion in Section
1
and the fact that

is non-decreasing. Nevertheless, it also follows from our more general result, whose proof, as we now show, is a bit more involved.

5.
Proof of Main Result

We start by proving several things about the
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}
and
\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}
operators.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection and let
\Phi
be a Blum measure. For all
f\in\mathsf{f}
, all
k
, and all witness criteria
\omega
, there exist
g,h\in\mathsf{f}
such that:

(i)

\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)\subseteq\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{f}}(g)\subseteq\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(g)
,

(ii)

\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{f}}(h)\subseteq\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(h)
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is below; the proof of (ii) is analogous.

Fix
f\in\mathsf{f}
and let
L\in\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f).
Then, there are
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathsf{f}
and
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
such that for all
x
,

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{f_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{f_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{f_{k}(|x|)}\right)\chi_{L^{\prime}}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1.

Since
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
, for all
x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
,

\Phi(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq f(|\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle|),

where
e
is the Gödel number of
\chi_{L^{\prime}}
. To establish the first containment in (i), it remains to show that there is
g\in\mathsf{f}
such that for all
x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
,
\Phi(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq g(|\langle x\rangle|)
. To this end, note that by Eq. (
1
),

\displaystyle|\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}|w_{i}|+O\left(\log|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log|w_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle=|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(|x|)+O\left(\log|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log f_{i}(|x|)\right).

Therefore,

\Phi(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq f\left(|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(|x|)+O\left(\log|x|+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log f_{i}(|x|)\right)\right).

Thus, by property (e) of the acceptable collection
\mathsf{f}
, there is indeed
g\in\mathsf{f}
such that for all
x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
,
\Phi(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq g(|x|)\leq g(|\langle x\rangle|)
, where the latter inequality holds because
|x|\leq|\langle x\rangle|
and
g
is non-decreasing (property (c) of the acceptable collection
\mathsf{f}
). Consequently,
L\in\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{f}}(g)
, as desired.

We now establish the second containment in (i). Let
L\in\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(g).
Then there exist
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\mathsf{f}
and
\varphi_{e}\in\mathscr{P}(\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\{0,1\})
such that for all
x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
,
\Phi(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq g(|\langle x\rangle|)
and

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{g_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{g_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{g_{k}(|x|)}\right)\varphi_{e}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1.

Since
g
is non-decreasing,

\Phi(e,\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)\leq g(|\langle x\rangle|)\leq g(|\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle|).

Therefore,
L^{\prime}\coloneqq\{x\mid\varphi_{e}(x)=1\}\in\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(g)
and

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{g_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{g_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{g_{k}(|x|)}\right)\chi_{L^{\prime}}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1.

Thus,
L\in\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(g)
, as desired.
∎

The next lemma shows that the
\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}
and
\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}
operators pass through unions over
\mathsf{f}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection and let
\Phi
be a Blum measure. For all
k
and all witness criteria
\omega
:

(i)

\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
,

(ii)

\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathsf{W}_{\omega}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
.

Proof.

The proof of (i) is below; the proof of (ii) is analogous.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
for all
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}
, the monotonicity of
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}
(Observation
3.1
) implies
\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f^{\prime})\subseteq\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\bigcup_{f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)

\displaystyle=\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f).

For the other direction, let
L\in\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
. Then, there are
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathsf{f}
and
L^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
such that for all
x
, Eq. (
3
) holds. But
L^{\prime}\in\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
implies
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f^{\prime})
for some
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}
. Therefore,
L\in\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}(f)
.
∎

(ii)

for all

,

(iii)

for all
i\in[\ell]
,
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection, let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in[\ell]\}
be a finite collection of Blum measures, and let
t_{\mathsf{f}}
be as in Proposition
5.3
. For all
i\in[\ell]
, all
k
, and all witness criteria
\omega
:

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
,

(ii)

\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(f),

(iii)

\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(f).

Proof.

Proposition
5.3
proves (i). The proof of (ii) is below; the proof of (iii) is analogous.

The following is true for all
i\in[\ell]
and all
k
. By Lemma
5.2
and the definition of

,
L\in\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
iff
L\in\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
iff
L\in\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f^{\prime})
for some
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}
. By Lemma
5.1
, there is then
g\in\mathsf{f}
such that
\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f^{\prime})\subseteq\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(g)\subseteq\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(g)
, which implies

\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(f).

The result then follows from Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathsf{f}
be an acceptable collection, let
\{\Phi_{i}\mid i\in[\ell]\}
be a finite collection of Blum measures, and let
t_{\mathsf{f}}
be as in Proposition
5.3
. For all
i\in[\ell]
, all
k
, and all witness criteria
\omega
:

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\bigcup_{f\in\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(f)
,

(ii)

\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
,

(iii)

\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\omega}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})=\mathsf{W}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
.

Proof.

Proposition
5.3
proves (i). The proof of (ii) is below; the proof of (iii) is analogous.

The following is true for all
i\in[\ell]
and all
k
. First, let
L\in\Sigma^{\mathsf{f}}_{k}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
. Then, by Lemma
5.4
,
L\in\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(f^{\prime})
for some
f^{\prime}\in\mathsf{f}
. But
f^{\prime}\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}
by Proposition
5.3
, so
L\in\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}^{\mathsf{f}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
.

Now let
L\in\Sigma_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}}).
Then, there exist
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathsf{f}
and
\varphi_{e}\in\mathscr{P}(\Sigma^{*}\rightarrow\{0,1\})
such that for all
x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
,
\Phi_{i}(e,\langle x,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\rangle)\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}(|\langle x\rangle|)
and

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{f_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{f_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{f_{k}(|x|)}\right)\varphi_{e}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1.

Since
t_{\mathsf{f}}
is non-decreasing (Proposition
5.3
),

\Phi_{i}(e,\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}(|\langle x\rangle|)\leq t_{\mathsf{f}}(|\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle|).

Therefore,
L^{\prime}\coloneqq\{x\mid\varphi_{e}(x)=1\}\in\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
and

x\in L\iff\left(\exists y_{1}\in\Sigma^{f_{1}(|x|)}\right)\left(\forall y_{2}\in\Sigma^{f_{2}(|x|)}\right)\dots\left(Q_{k}y_{k}\in\Sigma^{f_{k}(|x|)}\right)\chi_{L^{\prime}}(\langle x,y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\rangle)=1.

Thus,
L\in\Sigma_{k}^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{i}}(t_{\mathsf{f}})
, as desired.
∎

Together Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
imply Theorem
A
.

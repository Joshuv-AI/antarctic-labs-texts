Local Laws for Sparse Sample Covariance Matrices without the truncation condition
F. Götze, A. Tikhomirov, D. Timushev

Abstract
. We consider sparse sample covariance matrices 1 n ​ p n ​ 𝐗𝐗 ∗ \frac{1}{np_{n}}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{*} , where 𝐗 \mathbf{X} is a sparse matrix of order n × m n\times m with the sparse probability p n p_{n} . We prove the local Marchenko–Pastur law in some complex domain assuming that n ​ p n > log β ⁡ n np_{n}>\log^{\beta}n , β > 0 \beta>0 and some ( 4 + δ ) (4+\delta) -moment condition is fulfilled, δ > 0 \delta>0 .



1.
Introduction

Sample covariance matrices are of great practical importance for problems of multivariate statistical analysis and such rapidly developing areas as the theory of wireless communication and deep learning. Another significant area of application of sample covariance matrices is graph theory. The adjacency matrix of an undirected graph is asymmetric, so the study of its singular values leads to the sample covariance matrix. If we assume that the probability
p_{n}
of having graph edges tends to zero as the number of vertices
n
increases to infinity, we get to the concept of sparse random matrices.

Sparse Wigner random matrices have been considered in a number of papers (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
) where many results have been obtained. With the symmetrization of sample covariance matrices it is possible to apply this results in the case when the observation matrix is square. However, when the sample size is greater than observation dimension, the spectral limit distribution has the singularity in zero, which requires different approaches.

The limit spectral distribution of sparse sample covariance matrices with sparsity
np_{n}\sim n^{\varepsilon}
, (
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary small) was studied in
[
5
,
6
]
. In particular, a local law was proved under the assumption that the matrix elements satisfy the moments condition
\E|X_{jk}|^{q}\leq(Cq)^{cq}
. In the paper
[
7
]
the case of the sparsity
np_{n}\sim\log^{\alpha}n
, for some
\alpha>1
was considered, assuming that the moments of the matrix elements satisfy the conditions
\E|X_{jk}|^{4+\delta}\leq C<\infty
,
|X_{jk}|\leq c_{1}(np_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}-\varkappa}
, for some
\varkappa>0
. Under this assumptions the local Marchenko–Pastur law was proved in some complex domain
z\in\mathcal{D}
with
\im z>v_{0}>0
, where
v_{0}
is of order
\log^{4}n/n
and the domain bound not depend on
p_{n}
while
np_{n}>\log^{\beta}n
.

This work is devoted to the case, when the elements
X_{jk}
are not truncated, and only the conditions
\E|X_{jk}|^{4+\delta}\leq C<\infty
,
np_{n}\sim\log^{\alpha}n
, for some
\alpha>1
are fulfilled. We prove the local Marchenko–Pastur law in some complex domain
u+iv\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
with the real part contained in the support of the Marchenko–Pastur distribution and separated from the support ends.

\displaystyle S_{y}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{-z+\frac{1-y}{z}+\sqrt{(z-\frac{1-y}{z})^{2}-4y}}{2y},

\displaystyle s_{n}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{j}-z}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{-s_{j}-z}\Big]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{z}{s_{j}^{2}-z^{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{jj}.

Put

(2.1)

b(z)=z-\frac{1-y}{z}+2yS_{y}(z)=-\frac{1}{S_{y}(z)}+yS_{y}(z).

In this paper we prove so called Marchenko–Pastur law for sparse sample covariance matrices.

.
The Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{V}_{22}
of size
(n+m-R)\times(n+m-R)
has the form

\mathbf{V}_{22}=\begin{bmatrix}&\mathbf{O}_{11}&\mathbf{A}_{2}\\
&\mathbf{A}_{2}^{*}&\mathbf{O}_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

Here the square matrices
\mathbf{O}_{11}
and
\mathbf{O}_{22}
have zero elements and the orders
m
and
n-R
respectively, and the matrix
\mathbf{A}_{2}
is
m\times(n-R)
with elements distributed by type
a
.
The resolvent
\mathbf{R}(z)=(\mathbf{V}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}
can be represented as

\mathbf{R}=\begin{bmatrix}&\mathbf{R}_{11}&\mathbf{R}_{12}\\
&\mathbf{R}_{12}^{T}&\mathbf{R}_{22}\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{11}=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{V}_{11}-z\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*})^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{12}=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*}-\mathbf{V}_{11}+z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{22}=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}+(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*}

\displaystyle\qquad\times(\mathbf{V}_{11}-z\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}.

We will be primarily interested in estimating the spectral norm of the matrix
\mathbf{R}_{11}
since it majorizes all elements of the matrix
\mathbf{R}_{11}
. Note that the dimension of the matrix
\mathbf{R}_{11}
is equal to
R\times R
, where
R<\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}
.
Introduce a random matrix

\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*}.

Note that

\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}=(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}&\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{11}&\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{12}\\
&{\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{12}}^{T}&\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

Given the form of the matrices
\mathbf{V}_{12}
and
\mathbf{V}_{22}
, we find that

\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{*}.

In these notation

\mathbf{R}_{11}=(\mathbf{V}_{11}-z\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{Y})^{-1}.

In what follows we shall assume that
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{V}}
is admissible. We prove that for the resolvent matrix
\mathbf{R}
all entries are bounded conditioning by admissible
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{V}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{V}}
be admissible. Under conditions of Theorem
2.2
there exists a constant
H
such that for
z\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}

\Pr\{\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n+m}|R_{jk}|>H;\mathcal{Q}\}\leq Cn^{-c\log\log n}.

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{L}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{L}}=[1,n+m]
,
\mathbb{J}\subset[1,n+m]
. We introduce the events

\mathcal{C}_{1}(v,k)=\bigcap_{|\mathbb{J}|\leq k}\bigg\{\max_{j,l\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{L}}}|R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{jl}(u+iv)|\leq H_{1}\bigg\}

and

\mathcal{C}_{2}(v,k)=\bigcap_{|\mathbb{J}|\leq k}\bigg\{\max_{j\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{L}},1\leq l\leq n+m}|R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{jl}(u+iv)|\leq{H_{2}}\bigg\}.

The following lemma holds.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the conditions of the Theorem
2.2
, the inequalities

(4.1)

\Pr\Big\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(v,k-1);\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,k)\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,k)\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\geq 1-Cn^{-c\log\log n}

and

(4.2)

\Pr\Big\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(v,k-1);\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,k)\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,k)\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\geq 1-Cn^{-c\log\log n}

are valid.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume that
k=1
.
We begin by proving the inequality (
4.1
). Since both indices are typical, the corresponding matrix elements in the rows (and columns) with numbers
j,k
are of type
a
.
Consider the diagonal elements.
For
j\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{L}}\cap[1,n]
the equality

R_{jj}=yS_{y}(z)\Big(1+\varepsilon_{j}R_{jj}+\Lambda_{n}R_{jj}\Big)

holds.
For
\omega\in\mathcal{Q}
we have

|\Lambda_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Hence,

|R_{jj}|\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq 2\sqrt{y}(1+|\varepsilon_{j}||R_{jj}|)\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}.

Let

\varepsilon_{j}=\varepsilon_{j1}+\varepsilon_{j2}+\varepsilon_{j3}

with

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}R^{(j)}_{l+n,l+n}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{l=1}^{m}R_{l+n,l+n},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mp}\sum_{l=1}^{m}(a^{2}_{jl}\xi_{jl}-p)R^{(j)}_{l+n,l+n},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j3}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{mp}\sum_{l,t=1}^{m}a_{jl}a_{jt}\xi_{jl}\xi_{jt}R^{(j)}_{l+n,t+n}.

Note that for admissible configurations

|\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{L}}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{n}{p}}.

By
[
7
, Lemma 1]
,

|\varepsilon_{j1}|\leq\frac{C}{nv}.

Next, note that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}|R^{(j)}_{l+n,l+n}|^{2}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq{H_{2}^{2}s^{2}}+H_{1}^{2}s^{2}

and

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{l=1}^{m}|R^{(j)}_{l+n,l+n}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq{H_{2}^{q}s^{q}}+H_{1}^{q}s^{q}.

We used here the so-called multiplicative inequality: for any
s\geq 1

|R_{jj}(u+iv)|\leq s|R_{jj}(u+isv)|.

Given the above, get

\displaystyle\E|\varepsilon_{j2}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{q}H_{2}^{q}}{(np)^{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{C^{q}s^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}H_{1}^{q}}{(np)^{\frac{q}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{q}q^{q}H_{2}^{q}s^{q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+1}}+\frac{C^{q}q^{q}H_{1}^{q}s^{q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+1}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\E|\varepsilon_{j3}|^{q}|R_{jj}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq\frac{C^{q}q^{q}}{(nv)^{q}}a_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}(z)+\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}s^{\frac{q}{2}}H_{2}^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}(np)^{\varkappa q+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}s^{\frac{3q}{2}}H_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}(np)^{\varkappa q+1}}+\frac{C^{q}q^{2q}s^{2q}H_{2}^{2q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}}+\frac{C^{q}q^{2q}s^{2q}H_{1}^{2q}p}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}}.

Here we used the fact that

\displaystyle|R_{jk}(u+iv)|\leq|R_{jk}(u+iv)|+(s-1)v|[\mathbf{R}(u+iv)\mathbf{R}(u+sv)]_{jk}|\leq|R_{jk}(u+isv)|

\displaystyle+(s-1)\sqrt{\im R_{jj}\im R_{kk}}\leq sH_{1}

for
j,k\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{L}}
, and

|R_{jk}(u+iv)|\leq{sH_{2}}

in the case
j\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{L}}
or
k\in\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{L}}
.

If
(np)^{{2\varkappa}}|b(z)|\geq Cq^{2}sH_{1}
and
(np)^{\varkappa}>CqsH_{1}
, then
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
can be chosen so that

\E|R_{jj}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq H_{1}^{p}.

Now consider the case of deviant indices. Let
j\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{L}}
and
k
be arbitrary.
Consider the matrix

\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{V}_{12}(\mathbf{V}_{22}-z\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{V}_{12}^{*}=\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{*}.

We estimate the matrix
\mathbf{Y}
elementwise. We start with off-diagonal elements. Consider
Y_{12}
.
The equality

Y_{12}=\frac{1}{mp}\sum_{l,t}a_{1l}\overline{a}_{2t}[\mathbf{R}_{22}^{(\mathbb{J})}]_{lt}

holds.
Note that
\{a_{1l}\}
and
\{a_{2t}\}
are independent. We can apply the lemma
5.1
with
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{R}_{22}^{(\mathbb{J})}
.
By the assumption
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap{\mathcal{Q}}
we get

\|\mathbf{A}\|^{2}\leq\gamma\frac{na_{n}(z)}{v}+\frac{r}{v},

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\leq\frac{H_{2}^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{\frac{q}{2}}}{pv^{\frac{q}{2}}|b(z)|^{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{H_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{\frac{q}{2}}n}{v^{\frac{q}{2}}}.

Finally,

\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\mathbb{J}}|[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{ij}|^{q}\leq\frac{H_{2}^{q-2}s^{q-2}n}{|b(z)|^{q-2}v}(\gamma a_{n}(z)+\frac{r}{nv}).

Further, we have

\mu_{\xi}^{(q)},\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\leq p(np)^{-2-\varkappa q}

for
q\geq 4+\delta
, and

\mu_{\xi}^{(q)},\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\leq p\mu_{4+\delta}^{\frac{q}{4+\delta}}/(np)^{\frac{q}{2}}

for
q\leq 4
.
Combining all the estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}}{n^{q}}\Big(\frac{1}{q^{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{q^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(np)^{\frac{q}{2}\varkappa}}+\frac{1}{(np)^{2}}\frac{q^{q}}{(np)^{q\varkappa}}\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}}{n^{\frac{q}{2}+1}(np)^{q\varkappa}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{q}q^{2q}}{n^{2}(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}}.

Finally we get, for
np\geq C\log n^{\frac{1}{\varkappa}}
,

\displaystyle\E|Y_{12}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}}\Big(a_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}(z)+\frac{r^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}}\Big)+\frac{C^{q}H_{2}^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{\frac{q}{2}}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}(np)^{q\varkappa+1}|b(z)|^{\frac{q}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}H_{1}^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{\frac{q}{2}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}(np)^{q\varkappa}}+\frac{C^{q}q^{2q}H_{1}^{q}s^{q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}}+\frac{C^{q}H_{2}^{q}s^{q}}{|b(z)|^{q}(np)^{2\varkappa q+3}}.

Applying Chebyshev’s inequality with
q\sim\log n
, we conclude that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Pr\Big\{|Y_{12}|\geq C\log n\Big(\frac{a_{n}(z)}{\sqrt{nv}}+\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}n}{\sqrt{nv}(np)^{\varkappa}|b(z)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{\log^{2}n}{(np)^{2\varkappa}|b(z)|}\Big)\Big\}\leq Cn^{-c\log\log n}.

Now consider the diagonal elements.

Y_{11}=\sum_{l,t}a_{1l}a_{1t}[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{lt}.

Represent
Y_{11}
as

Y_{11}=\sum_{l}a_{1l}^{2}[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{ll}+\sum_{l\neq t}a_{1l}a_{1t}[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{lt}=:\widehat{Y}_{11}+\widetilde{Y}_{11}.

Applying the inequality for quadratic forms, obtain

\displaystyle\E|\widetilde{Y}_{11}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{q}\Big(q^{q}(\E|a_{11}|^{2})^{q}\E\|\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}\|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}

\displaystyle+q^{\frac{3q}{2}}\mu^{(q)}(\E|a_{11}|^{2})^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{l}\E\big(\sum_{t}|[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{lt}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle+q^{2q}(\mu^{(q)})^{2}\sum_{l,t}

\displaystyle|[\mathbf{R}^{(\mathbb{J})}_{22}]_{lt}|^{q}\Big)\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}.

From here it is easy to get

\displaystyle\E|\widetilde{Y}_{11}|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}

\displaystyle\leq C^{q}\Big(\frac{q^{q}r^{\frac{q}{2}}a_{n}^{\frac{q}{2}}(z)}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}}+\frac{q^{\frac{3q}{2}C^{q}}}{(nv)^{\frac{q}{2}}(np)^{\varkappa q+2}|b(z)|^{\frac{q}{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{C^{q}q^{2q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+3}|b(z)|^{q}}+\frac{C^{q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}}\Big).

This yields

\displaystyle\Pr\Big\{|\widetilde{Y}_{11}|

\displaystyle\geq C\Big(\frac{\log^{2}n\log\log na_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(z)}{\sqrt{nv}}+\frac{\log^{\frac{5}{2}}n}{\sqrt{nv}(np)^{\varkappa}\sqrt{|b(z)|}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\log^{3}n}{(np)^{2\varkappa}|b(z)|}\Big);\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\leq Cn^{-\log\log n}.

Now consider
\widehat{Y}_{11}
. We have

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{11}=

\displaystyle\frac{y}{n}\sum_{l}R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{ll}+\sum_{l}(a_{1l}^{2}-\E a_{1l}^{2})R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{ll}

\displaystyle=yS_{y}(z)-\frac{1-y}{z}+y\Lambda_{n}(z)+\frac{r}{nv}+\sum_{l}(a_{1l}^{2}-\E a_{1l}^{2})R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{ll}.

By Rosenthal’s inequality,

\displaystyle\E\Big|\sum_{l}(a_{1l}^{2}-\E a_{1l}^{2})R^{(\mathbb{J})}_{ll}\Big|^{q}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\}\mathbb{I}\{\mathcal{Q}\}\leq

\displaystyle C^{q}\Big(\frac{q^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{q}}{(np)^{\frac{q}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{\frac{q}{2}}s^{q}}{(np)^{q}|b(z)|^{q}}+\frac{s^{q}q^{q}}{(np)^{2\varkappa q+2}|b(z)|^{q}}\Big).

The obtained bounds give

\displaystyle\Pr\Big\{

\displaystyle\Big|\widehat{Y}_{11}-\Big(-\frac{1-y}{z}+yS_{y}(z)\Big)\Big|\geq C\Big(\gamma a_{n}(z)+\frac{r}{nv}

\displaystyle+\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}n}{(np)^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(np)|b(z)|}+\frac{\log^{2}n}{(np)^{2\varkappa}|b(z)|}\Big);\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\leq Cn^{-\log\log n}.

Summing up the estimates for
\widehat{Y}_{11}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{11}
, we conclude that

\Pr\Big\{\Big|Y_{11}-\Big(yS_{y}(z)-\frac{1-y}{z}\Big)\Big|\geq\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{2};\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\leq Cn^{-c\log n},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}=

\displaystyle\gamma a_{n}(z),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}=

\displaystyle C\Big(\frac{r}{nv}+\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}n}{(np)^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}n}{(np)|b(z)|}+\frac{\log^{2}n}{(np)^{2\varkappa}|b(z)|}

\displaystyle+\frac{\log^{2}n\log\log na_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(z)}{\sqrt{nv}}+\frac{\log^{\frac{5}{2}}n}{\sqrt{nv}(np)^{\varkappa}\sqrt{|b(z)|}}+\frac{\log^{3}n}{(np)^{2\varkappa}|b(z)|}\Big).

It is easy to show that if

|b(z)|\geq C\log n^{\frac{3}{2}}n\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{nv}}+\frac{1}{(np)^{\varkappa}}\bigg),

then

\Pr\Big\{\Big|Y_{11}-\Big(yS_{y}(z)-\frac{1-y}{z}\Big)\Big|\leq\gamma|b(z)|;\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\leq Cn^{-c\log n}

with an arbitrarily small constant
\gamma
. From this and the inequality (
4.3
) it follows that

\Pr\Big\{\Big\|\mathbf{Y}-\Big(yS_{y}(z)-\frac{1-y}{z}\Big)\mathbf{I}\Big\|\geq\gamma|b(z)|;\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\geq 1-Cn^{-c\log n}.

Since the matrix
\mathbf{V}_{11}
is Hermitian (the eigenvalues are real), and

\im\Big(z-\frac{1-y}{z}+yS_{y}(z)\Big)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}|b(z)|,

we find that

\Pr\Big\{\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{V}_{11}-\Big(z-\frac{1-y}{z}+yS_{y}(z)\Big)-\mathbf{Y}\Big)^{-1}\Big\|\leq\frac{C}{|b(z)|};\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\geq 1-Cn^{-c\log\log n}.

This, in particular, implies that

\Pr\Big\{|R_{jk}|\leq{H_{2}};\mathcal{C}_{1}(sv,1)\cap\mathcal{C}_{2}(sv,1)\cap\mathcal{Q}\Big\}\geq 1-Cn^{-c\log\log n},

for
j\in\mathcal{D}
.
The last statement completes the proof of the Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

k=|\mathbb{J}|
. Lemma

and inequality

5.
Appendix

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
and
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}
be mutually independent random variables,
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. Define

\mathcal{L}_{j}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{2}.

Note that

\|A\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{2}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
q\geq 2

the inequality

\E|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\eta_{j}|^{q}\leq C^{q}\big(\mathcal{A}_{1}\|A\|^{q}+\mathcal{A}_{2}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q})+\mathcal{A}_{3}(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{q})\big)

holds, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}(\sigma_{\xi}^{2q}+\sigma_{\eta}^{2q}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}(\sigma_{\xi}^{(2q)}+\sigma_{\eta}^{2q})^{\frac{q-6}{2(q-4}}(\mu_{\xi}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{\frac{2(q-2)}{q-4}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}=

\displaystyle q^{2q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}.

Proof.

Let
A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\eta_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\eta_{j}).

Applying Rosenthal’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle A\leq C^{q}(q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\E\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}a_{ij})^{2}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}+q^{q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E\big|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\eta_{j}\big|^{q}\Big)

\displaystyle=:C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}A_{1}+C^{q}q^{q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}A_{2}.

Using the triangle inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq 2^{\frac{q}{2}}\E\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\eta_{j}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}+2^{\frac{q}{2}}\E\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle=:2^{\frac{q}{2}}(A_{11}+A_{12}).

Further,

A_{11}\leq 2^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big(\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}+\E\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\eta_{j}^{2}-\sigma_{\eta}^{2})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big).

Applying Rosenthal’s inequality again, we conclude that

\displaystyle\E\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\eta_{j}^{2}-\sigma_{\eta}^{2})\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{4}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}(\mu_{\eta}^{(4)})^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle+C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

To estimate
A_{12}
, we use the inequality for quadratic forms from
[
9
]
. We have

\displaystyle A_{12}\leq

\displaystyle C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}+C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{4}}\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})}\sigma_{\eta}^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle+C^{q}\Big(q^{q}(\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{2}\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big).

Summing up the above inequalities, we find that

\displaystyle A_{1}\leq

\displaystyle C^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2}}+C^{q}(\mu_{\eta}^{(4)})^{\frac{q}{4}}q^{\frac{q}{4}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle+C^{q}\Big(q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}+C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{4}}\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})}\sigma_{\eta}^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle+C^{q}q^{q}(\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big)+C^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

For
A_{2}
, by Rosenthal’s inequality, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle A_{2}\leq C^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{q}+C^{q}q^{q}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{q}.

Further note that

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2(q-2)}}(\|A\|^{q})^{\frac{q-4}{2(q-2)}},

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|^{q},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\big(\sum_{l=1}^{n}a_{il}a_{lj}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{q}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{(q-4)}{2(q-2)}}\big(\|A\|^{q}\big)^{\frac{q}{2(q-2)}},

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{(q-4)}{(q-2)}}\big(\|A\|^{q}\big)^{\frac{2}{(q-2)}}.

For
A
we get the estimate

A\leq C^{q}(B_{1}+\ldots+B_{8}),

where

\displaystyle B_{1}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{q}{2}}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\|A\|^{q},

\displaystyle B_{2}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\xi}^{q}(\mu_{\eta}^{(4)})^{\frac{q}{4}}q^{\frac{3q}{4}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2(q-2)}}\big(\|A\|^{q})^{\frac{q-4}{2(q-2)}},

\displaystyle B_{3}=

\displaystyle q^{q}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q-4}{q-2}}\Big(\|A\|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q-2}},

\displaystyle B_{4}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{5q}{4}}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})}\sigma_{\eta}^{\frac{q}{2}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q-4}{2(q-2)}}\Big(\|A\|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q}{2(q-2)}},

\displaystyle B_{5}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\big(\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})}\big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q-4}{(q-2)}}\Big(\|A\|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{2}{(q-2)}},

\displaystyle B_{6}=

\displaystyle q^{q}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q},

\displaystyle B_{7}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q},

\displaystyle B_{8}=

\displaystyle q^{2q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{q}.

Applying Young’s inequality, we obtain the bounds

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq Cq^{\frac{3q}{4}}\Big(\sigma_{\xi}^{4}(\mu_{\eta}^{(4)})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}+\sigma_{\xi}^{2q}\|A\|^{q}),

\displaystyle B_{3}\leq C^{q}q^{q}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}+C^{q}q^{q}\sigma_{\xi}^{q}\sigma_{\eta}^{q}\|A\|^{q}\Big),

\displaystyle B_{4}\leq C^{q}q^{\frac{5q}{4}}\Big((\sigma_{\xi}^{2q}+\sigma_{\eta}^{2q})\|A\|^{q}+(\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{\frac{2(q-2)}{q-4}}(\sigma_{\xi}^{q\frac{q-6}{q-4}}+\sigma_{\eta}^{q\frac{q-6}{q-4}})\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big),

\displaystyle B_{5}\leq C^{q}q^{\frac{3q}{2}}\Big(\sigma_{\xi}^{2q}\|A\|^{q}+\sigma_{\xi}^{\frac{q(q-6)}{q-4}}(\mu_{\eta}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{\frac{2(q-2)}{q-4}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q}\Big).

The last inequalities give

A\leq C^{q}\big(\mathcal{A}_{1}\|A\|^{q}+\mathcal{A}_{2}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{q})+\mathcal{A}_{3}(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}|^{q})\big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}(\sigma_{\xi}^{2q}+\sigma_{\eta}^{2q}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{3q}{2}}(\sigma_{\xi}^{(2q)}+\sigma_{\eta}^{2q})^{\frac{q-6}{2(q-4}}(\mu_{\xi}^{(\frac{q}{2})})^{\frac{2(q-2)}{q-4}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}=

\displaystyle q^{2q}\mu_{\xi}^{(q)}\mu_{\eta}^{(q)}.

Thus Lemma is proved.

New Way to Resum the Lattice QCD Taylor Series Equation of State at Finite Chemical Potential
Sabarnya Mitra, Prasad Hegde, Christian Schmidt

Abstract
Taylor expansion of the thermodynamic potential in powers of the (baryo)chemical potential μ B \mu_{B} is a well-known method to bypass the Sign Problem of Lattice QCD. Due to the difficulty in calculating the higher order Taylor coefficients, various alternative expansion schemes as well as resummation techniques have been suggested to extend the Taylor series to larger values of μ B \mu_{B} . Recently, a way to resum the contribution of the first N N charge density correlation functions D 1 , … , D N D_{1},\dots,D_{N} to the Taylor series to all orders in μ B \mu_{B} was proposed in Phys. Rev. Lett. 128, 2, 022001 (2022). The resummation takes the form of an exponential factor. Since the correlation functions are calculated stochastically, the exponential factor contains a bias which can be significant for large N N and μ B \mu_{B} . In this paper, we present a new method to calculate the QCD equation of state based on the well-known cumulant expansion from statistics. By truncating the expansion at a maximum order M M , we end up with only finite products of the correlation functions which can be evaluated in an unbiased manner. Although our formalism is also applicable for μ B ≠ 0 \mu_{B}\neq 0 , here we present it for the simpler case of a finite isospin chemical potential μ I \mu_{I} for which there is no Sign Problem. We present and compare results for the pressure and the isospin density obtained using Taylor expansion, exponential resummation and cumulant expansion, and provide evidence that the absence of bias in the latter actually improves the convergence.

Introduction

The Equation of State (EoS) of strongly-interacting matter is an important input in the hydrodynamical modeling of heavy-ion collisions
Bernhard et al. 2016
;
Parotto et al. 2020
;
Monnai et al. 2019
;
Everett et al. 2020
. Unfortunately Lattice
QCD, which is the preferred method of calculating observables in the non-perturbative regime of QCD, breaks down when the baryon chemical potential
\mu_{B}
is
non-zero. This is the well-known Sign Problem of Lattice QCD
Nagata 2021
; despite recent progress
Aarts 2009
;
Cristoforetti et al. 2012
;
Aarts et al. 2013
;
Sexty 2014
;
Fukuma et al. 2019
, the current state-of-the-art results for the QCD EoS have been obtained by using either
either analytical continuation from imaginary to real
\mu_{B}

Borsanyi et al. 2018
;
Ratti 2018
, or by expanding the EoS in a Taylor series in the chemical potential
\mu_{B}
and calculating the first
N
coefficients
Bazavov et al. 2017
;
Bollweg et al. 2021
.
In the latter case, a knowledge of the first several coefficients is necessary, not only to obtain the EoS for a fairly wide range of chemical
potentials but also to determine the radius of convergence of the Taylor series beyond which the Taylor expansion must break down
Gavai and Gupta 2008
;
Dimopoulos et al. 2022
;
Bollweg et al. 2022
;
Giordano and Pásztor 2019
. Unfortunately, the calculation of the higher order Taylor coefficients is computationally very challenging and it is natural to ask whether something can be learned about them from a knowledge of the first few Taylor coefficients. It turns out that this is indeed possible because
the first
N
derivatives
D_{1},\dots,D_{N}
of
\ln\det M(\mu_{B})
, where
M(\mu_{B})
is the fermion matrix, also contribute to the higher order Taylor coefficients through products such as
D_{N}^{2}
,
D_{N}D_{1}
, etc. In fact, the contribution of the
n
th derivative
D_{n}
to all higher orders can be shown to take the form of an exponential
\exp(D_{n}\mu_{B}^{n}/n!)

Mondal et al. 2022
. Thus, if
D_{n}
is known exactly, then its contribution to the Taylor series can be resummed to all orders through exponentiation. Exponential resummation, as we will refer to it from here on, can be shown to have several advantages compared to the original Taylor series: First, the resummed EoS converges faster than the Taylor series. Moreover, since the odd derivatives
D_{1},D_{3},\dots
are purely imaginary, the resummed expression directly gives us a phase factor whose expectation value approaches zero as
\mu_{B}
is increased, leading to a breakdown of the calculation. This breakdown is physical and related to the presence of poles or branch cut singularities of the QCD partition function in the complex
\mu_{B}
plane. The resummed expression for the partition function also makes it possible to calculate these singularities directly. Some of these advantages have been recently demonstrated through analytical calculations in a low-energy model of QCD
Mukherjee et al. 2022
.

Despite its advantages, a technical drawback of exponential resummation is that the derivatives
D_{1},\dots,D_{N}
are not known exactly in an actual Lattice calculation. As is easily seen from the identity
\ln\det M=\text{tr}\ln M
, the
D_{n}
can be expressed in terms of traces of various operators, all of which involve the inverse of the fermion matrix
M
. Since
M
is typically of size
10^{8}
or greater, calculating its exact inverse is prohibitively expensive. Instead the various traces, and hence the derivatives
D_{n}
, are estimated stochastically using
\mathcal{O}(10^{2}
-
10^{3})
random volume sources per gauge configuration. Now, the products of such stochastically estimated quantities e.g.
D_{N}^{2}
, need to be evaluated in an unbiased manner
i.e.
estimates coming from the same random vector must not be multiplied together. If
D_{N}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2,\dots,{N_{\text{rv}}}
are the
{N_{\text{rv}}}
stochastic estimates of the trace
D_{N}
, then the Unbiased Estimate (UE) of
D_{N}^{2}
is given by

\text{UE}\left[D_{N}^{2}\right]=\frac{2}{{N_{\text{rv}}}({N_{\text{rv}}}-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}D_{N}^{(i)}D_{N}^{(j)}.

(1)

By contrast, the naïve Biased Estimate (BE) is given by

\text{BE}\left[D_{N}^{2}\right]=\left[\frac{1}{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}D_{N}^{(i)}\right]^{2}.

(2)

Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) can both be readily generalized to any finite power or to the product of a finite number of traces.
In the Appendix, we present formulas for evaluating the unbiased estimate of such finite products in an efficient manner.
However we do not know of any corresponding formula to calculate the unbiased estimate of an infinite series such as an exponential.

In this paper, we will present a new way of calculating the QCD EoS based on the well-known cumulant expansion from statistics. The cumulant expansion method is intermediate between a strict Taylor series expansion and exponential resummation in the sense that the contribution of
D_{1},\dots,D_{N}
are resummed
only up to a maximum order
M
. However, since the order is finite it is possible to evaluate the terms of the expansion in an unbiased manner.
The cumulant expansion agrees exactly with the Taylor series expansion to
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{B}^{N})
provided that

M\geq N

1
1

1

N
is assumed to be even from here on.
. However, it also contains additional
contributions at
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{B}^{N+2},\dots,\mu_{B}^{MN})
which are exactly the contributions of
D_{1},\dots,D_{N}

to the higher order Taylor coefficients
\chi_{N+2}^{B},\dots,\chi^{B}_{MN}
.

Although the cumulant expansion method also works for
\mu_{B}\neq 0
, in this paper we will present the formalism for the simpler case of finite isospin chemical
potential
\mu_{I}
instead. For
\mu_{I}\neq 0
, the fermion determinant is real and one has no Sign Problem. Thus one only works with real quantities which in turn
simplifies the presentation. Moreover, the absence of the Sign Problem allows us to calculate observables for much larger values of

\mu_{I}
than would be possible for the
\mu_{B}
case, and it is precisely for these large values that bias can become significant. Lastly, the QCD
phase diagram in the
T
-
\mu_{I}
plane is known from several studies to be interesting in its own
right
Son and Stephanov 2001
;
Endrödi 2014
;
Brandt et al. 2018
;
Adhikari et al. 2021
;
Adhikari and Andersen 2020
, and we hope to be able to apply this formalism to its study in the future.

II
Formalism

We consider Lattice QCD with 2+1 flavors of rooted staggered quarks. The partition function at non-zero isospin chemical potential
\mu_{I}
is given by

Z(T,\mu_{I})=\int\mathcal{D}Ue^{-S_{G}(T)}\,\det M(T,\mu_{I}),

(3)

where
\det M(T,\mu_{I})
is shorthand for

\det M(T,\mu_{I})=\prod_{f=u,d,s}\big[\det M_{f}(m_{f},T,\mu_{f})\big]^{1/4},

(4)

with
m_{u}=m_{d}
,
\mu_{u}=-\mu_{d}=\mu_{I}
and
\mu_{s}=0
. The excess pressure
\Delta P(T,\mu_{I})\equiv P(T,\mu_{I})-P(T,0)
is given by

\frac{\Delta P(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{4}}=\frac{1}{VT^{3}}\,\ln\left[\frac{Z(T,\mu_{I})}{Z(T,0)}\right],

(5)

where
V
is the spatial volume and
T
is the temperature. By employing the same arguments as in Ref.
Mondal et al. 2022
, we can write

\frac{Z(T,\mu_{I})}{Z(T,0)}=\Bigg\langle\exp\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{D_{2n}^{I}(T)}{(2n)!}\left(\frac{\mu_{I}}{T}\right)^{2n}\right]\Bigg\rangle,

(6)

where the expectation value
\langle\cdot\rangle
is taken over a gauge field ensemble generated at
\mu_{u}=\mu_{d}=\mu_{s}=0
, and

D^{I}_{n}(T)=\frac{\partial^{n}\left[\ln\det M(\mu_{I})\right]}{\partial(\mu_{I}/T)^{n}}\Bigg\rvert_{\mu_{I}=0}.

(7)

The presence of only even powers is because the odd
\mu_{I}
derivatives vanish identically. Since even derivatives of the quark determinant are purely real, we see that
\det M(\mu_{I})
is purely real and hence there is no Sign Problem. Note that this is true even when
\mu_{I}
is purely imaginary.

The
D_{n}^{I}
can be expressed as traces of various operators
Allton et al. 2005
;
Gavai and Gupta 2005
. In Lattice calculations, the first
N
derivatives
D^{I}_{1},\dots,D_{N}^{I}
are calculated stochastically using
{N_{\text{rv}}}
random vectors, where
{N_{\text{rv}}}
is typically of order 10
2
- 10
3
. Then
\Delta P(T,\mu_{I})/T^{4}
is
approximately equal to

\frac{\Delta P_{N}^{R}(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{4}}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\ln\Bigg\langle\exp\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}\frac{\bar{D}_{2n}^{I}(T)}{(2n)!}\left(\frac{\mu_{I}}{T}\right)^{2n}\right]\Bigg\rangle.

(8)

Here
N_{\sigma}
and
N_{\tau}
are the number of lattice sites in the space and time directions respectively, while
\bar{D}^{I}_{2n}
is the average of the
{N_{\text{rv}}}
stochastic
estimates of
D^{I}_{2n}
. Eq. (
8
) is the
N^{\text{th}}
order exponential resummation formula for
\Delta P(T,\mu_{I})/T^{4}
. In the limit

{N_{\text{rv}}}\to\infty
, it accurately resums the contribution of the first
N
derivatives
D_{1}^{I},\dots,D_{N}^{I}
to all orders in
\mu_{I}

Mondal et al. 2022
. For

{N_{\text{rv}}}<\infty
however, the formula contains bias. This is easily seen if one writes the exponential as an infinite series. The series expansion leads to terms
such as
(\bar{D}^{I}_{2m})^{p}(\bar{D}^{I}_{2n})^{q}\cdots
, and we have already seen that such products are biased due to multiplication of estimates coming from
the same random vector.

The well-known cumulant expansion formula from statistics states that

\ln\big\langle e^{tX}\big\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\,\mathcal{C}_{k}(X).

(9)

The coefficients
\mathcal{C}_{k}(X)
are known as the cumulants of
X

Endres et al. 2011
;
Ejiri 2008
;
Ejiri et al. 2010
. The first four cumulants are given
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle X\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle X^{2}\rangle-\langle X\rangle^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle X^{3}\rangle-3\langle X\rangle\langle X^{2}\rangle+2\langle X\rangle^{3},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{4}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle X^{4}\rangle-4\langle X\rangle\langle X^{3}\rangle-3\langle X^{2}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle+12\langle X^{2}\rangle\langle X\rangle^{2}-6\langle X\rangle^{4}.

(10)

In our case
t=1
, which we assume lies within the radius of convergence of the cumulant expansion, and
X\equiv X_{N}(T,\mu_{I})
, where

X_{N}(T,\mu_{I})=\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}\frac{D_{2n}^{I}(T)}{(2n)!}\left(\frac{\mu_{I}}{T}\right)^{2n}.

(11)

Truncating Eq. (
9
) at
k=M\geq N/2

2
2

2

We have
M\geq N/2
instead of
M\geq N
since the first non-vanishing isospin derivative is
D_{2}^{I}
rather than
D_{1}^{I}
.
gives us yet another way to estimate
\Delta P/T^{4}
, namely

\frac{\Delta P^{C}_{N,M}(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{4}}=\frac{N_{\tau}^{3}}{N_{\sigma}^{3}}\;\sum_{k=1}^{M\geq N/2}\frac{1}{k!}\,\mathcal{C}_{k}\big(X_{N}(T,\mu_{I})\big).

(12)

Eq. (
12
) may be compared to the familiar Taylor series expansion of
\Delta P/T^{4}
, which in our case is given by

\frac{\Delta P^{E}_{N}(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{4}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}\frac{\chi_{2n}^{I}(T)}{(2n)!}\left(\frac{\mu_{I}}{T}\right)^{2n}.

(13)

The restriction
M\geq N/2
in Eq. (
12
) ensures that the cumulant and Taylor expansions of the pressure agree term-by-term up to

\mathcal{O}(\mu_{I}^{N})
. However, the cumulant expansion also contains additional terms proportional to
\mu_{I}^{N+2},\dots,\mu_{I}^{MN}
. These extra terms are the same terms that
appear in the calculation of the higher order Taylor coefficients
\chi_{N+2}^{I},\dots,\chi_{MN}^{I}
. The cumulant expansion thus manages to capture some of the higher
order contributions to
\Delta P/T^{4}
, although it is not an all orders resummation like Eq. (
8
).
Unlike Eq. (
8
) however, only finite products of traces appear in Eq. (
12
). As we show in the Appendix, there exist
formulas (Eqs. (
18
) and (
24
))
for evaluating these products efficiently in an unbiased manner. Thus, the cumulant expansion is free of the bias that can affect exponential resummation.

Finally, we will also present results for the net isospin density
\mathcal{N}(T,\mu_{I})
which is given by

\frac{\mathcal{N}(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{3}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(\mu_{I}/T\right)}\left[\frac{\Delta P(T,\mu_{I})}{T^{4}}\right].

(14)

The Taylor series expression
\mathcal{N}^{E}_{N}(T,\mu_{I})
for the same is straightforward. The resummed and cumulant expansion expressions
\mathcal{N}^{R}_{N}(T,\mu_{I})
and

\mathcal{N}^{C}_{N,M}(T,\mu_{I})
can be obtained by differentiating Eqs. (
8
) and (
12
) respectively. We do not write down the explicit expressions here. Note however that the resummed formula, unlike the cumulant expansion expression, involves a ratio of expectation values.

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we presented a new way of resumming the QCD Taylor series EoS based on the well-known cumulant expansion from statistics. Our approach is a
finite order truncation of the all orders resummation of the first
N
charge density correlation functions that was presented in Ref.
Mondal et al. 2022
.
The resummation presented there is susceptible to bias when the correlation functions are calculated stochastically. By contrast, the cumulant expansion
contains only finite products of traces that can be evaluated efficiently in an unbiased manner. Moreover, while not an all orders resummation, the
M
th order
cumulant expansion still captures the contributions of the lower order derivatives
D_{1},\dots,D_{N}
to the higher order Taylor coefficients up to a maximum
order
\chi_{MN}
.

Although our formalism is also applicable to
\mu_{B}\neq 0
, in this paper we presented results for finite isospin chemical potential
\mu_{I}
instead. Our reason for this was that the absence of a Sign Problem in the latter case meant that all quantities were real and this simplified the presentation. The
\mu_{I}\neq 0
case is
also of interest in its own right. We presented results for the excess pressure and net isospin density using Taylor series, resummation and cumulant expansion.
We found evidence for bias in the resummed results at large
(\mu_{I}/T)^{2}
. We showed this by (a) calculating the cumulant expansion using biased rather than
unbiased products, and (b) recalculating the resummed results using fewer random vectors. The cumulant expansion is a truncation of the resummed formula and when
the terms of the expansion were calculated using biased products of traces, they were in good agreement with the resummed result, while they were closer to the
Taylor series results when they were calculated using unbiased products.

There have been several proposals recently to extend the QCD EoS to larger values of the chemical potential
Borsányi et al. 2021
;
Bollweg et al. 2022
;
Dimopoulos et al. 2022
;
Pásztor et al. 2021
;
Giordano et al. 2020
. Exponential resummation is one such approach, which
can be connected to reweighting-based approaches. By generalizing it to resummation in
T
and
\mu_{B}
, it can be also connected with the alternative expansion
scheme proposed in Ref.
Borsányi et al. 2021
. The cumulant expansion approach that we have outlined here provides yet another way to extend the QCD EoS.
The precise relation between this approach and the other proposals remains to be studied.

All data presented in the figures of this paper can be found in Ref.
Schmidt 2022
.

\eta_{i}
, with components fulfilling the normalization condition
\lim_{{N_{\text{rv}}}\to\infty}N_{\text{rv}}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\eta_{i,x}^{\dagger}\eta^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i,y}=\delta_{x,y}
, where
x
and
y
are lattice sites. The scalars
\mathcal{O}_{i}
are then defined as
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\eta_{i}^{\dagger}\mathcal{O}\eta_{i}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\equiv\sum_{x,y}\eta_{i,x}^{\dagger}\,\mathcal{O}^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i,x,y}\,\eta^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{i,y}
.
. We have

\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}=\lim_{{N_{\text{rv}}}\to\infty}\frac{1}{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\,\mathcal{O}_{i}.

(15)

In practice, the limit
{N_{\text{rv}}}\to\infty
is usually dropped and the trace is estimated by the arithmetic mean over a finite number of estimates
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, which bears potential danger for the estimation of powers of the trace
(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O})^{n}
, with
n\geq 1
. The naïve estimate

\text{BE}\left[(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O})^{n}\right]\equiv\left[\frac{1}{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\mathcal{O}_{i}\right]^{n},

(16)

is a biased estimate since it receives contributions from products of the same estimates
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{k},k\leq n
, which lead to a finite bias in the estimator
(
16
). The bias can be quite significant in the regime of large
n
, even when we take
{N_{\text{rv}}}\sim\mathcal{O}(1000)
. An unbiased estimator for
(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O})^{n}
can be
obtained by rejecting all diagonal contributions and considering only mutually distinct estimates in each product. We define

\displaystyle\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{n!}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}({N_{\text{rv}}}-k)}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\quad\cdots\sum_{i_{n}=i_{n-1}+1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\mathcal{O}_{i_{1}}\dots\mathcal{O}_{i_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}({N_{\text{rv}}}-k)}\,e_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{n})\,,

(17)

where
e_{n}
denotes the
elementary symmetric polynomial
of degree
n\leq{N_{\text{rv}}}
, in
{N_{\text{rv}}}
variables. For later use, we define
e_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{i},\dots\mathcal{O}_{N_{\text{rv}}})\equiv 1
.

Although Eq. (
17
) provides us with a valid unbiased estimator, its evaluation requires
\mathcal{O}(N_{\text{rv}}^{n})
elementary operations due to the nested sums. Fortunately, it is possible to rearrange the above sum so that it can be evaluated in only
\mathcal{O}({N_{\text{rv}}})
operations by utilizing the
Girard-Newton identities
, which relate the elementary symmetric polynomials to the
power sums

p_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{N_{\text{rv}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\text{rv}}}}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{k}
. Suppressing the arguments of the polynomials, the Girard-Newton identities read

\displaystyle ne_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}e_{n-k}p_{k}\;,

(18)

which can be readily used to obtain a recursive schema for the evaluation of the unbiased estimators in Eq. (
17
). We can also use the tower of identities (
18
) to completely erase the elementary symmetric polynomials from the right-hand side and express the unbiased estimator by power sums only,

\displaystyle\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}\right)^{n}\right]=\frac{(-1)^{n}\,B_{n}\big(-p_{1},-1!\,p_{2},\dots,-(n-1)!\,p_{n}\big)}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}({N_{\text{rv}}}-k)},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\,B_{n}\left(p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\right)\,=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\,\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k_{i}!}\left[\frac{p_{i}}{i!}\right]^{k_{i}}.

(19)

In this case the sum is taken over all partitions of
n
, which makes the connection to the
complete exponential Bell polynomials

B_{n}
and is made explicit with the second equality. Below we give the final formulae in the form of Eq. (
19
), which are required up to
n=4
,

\displaystyle\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}}{{N_{\text{rv}}}({N_{\text{rv}}}-1)}\,,

\displaystyle\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}\right)^{3}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}^{3}-3p_{1}p_{2}+2p_{3}}{{N_{\text{rv}}}({N_{\text{rv}}}-1)({N_{\text{rv}}}-2)}\,,

\displaystyle\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}^{4}-6p_{1}^{2}p_{2}+3p_{2}^{2}+8p_{1}p_{3}-6p_{4}}{{N_{\text{rv}}}({N_{\text{rv}}}-1)({N_{\text{rv}}}-2)({N_{\text{rv}}}-3)}.

(20)

Although there are several (power) sums to be evaluated, these are not nested sums as in Eq. (
17
).
Therefore, the unbiased product can be calculated in only
\mathcal{O}({N_{\text{rv}}})
time.

Unbiased Estimators for Combinations of Traces of Multiple Operators

Quite often we encounter a situation where we need to estimate expressions involving combinations of traces of several operators
\mathcal{O}^{(1)},\mathcal{O}^{(2)},\dots,\mathcal{O}^{(m)}
, which are estimated on the same set of random vectors and are thus correlated.
The evaluation of different operators on the same set of random vectors might seem to be avoidable at the first glance but could – as in the case of the derivative operators of the pressure discussed above – gain computational advantages, e.g., due to a recursive definition of the
operators
Allton et al. 2005
;
Steinbrecher 2018
.
A biased estimator of a general expression of this kind is given by

BE

\displaystyle\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}^{(1)}\right)^{\gamma_{1}}\;\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}^{(2)}\right)^{\gamma_{2}}\cdots\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}^{(m)}\right)^{\gamma_{m}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{rv}}^{\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}}}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{k}\right\}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{k}\right\}^{\gamma_{m}}.

(21)

In order to construct an unbiased estimator for this more general case, we extend the framework of elementary symmetric polynomials and power sums presented above as follows: We introduce the multi-index notation
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
with non-negative integer coefficients and define the metric
|\alpha|\equiv\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
. We further define the two types of symmetric polynomials as

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{rv}}}\left\{\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{i}\right\}^{\alpha_{1}}\left\{\mathcal{O}^{(2)}_{i}\right\}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots\left\{\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{i}\right\}^{\alpha_{m}}

\displaystyle e_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{|\beta|}}\underbrace{\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{O}^{(1)}_{i_{\beta_{1}}}}_{\beta_{1}\text{-times}}\;\underbrace{\mathcal{O}^{(2)}_{i_{\beta_{1}+1}}\cdots\mathcal{O}^{(2)}_{i_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}}}_{\beta_{2}\text{-times}}\cdots

\displaystyle\phantom{=sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{|\beta|}}}\underbrace{\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{i_{\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{m-1}+1}}\cdots\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{i_{|\beta|}}}_{\beta_{m}\text{-times}}\,.

(22)

The analogues of the Girard-Newton identities (
18
) are than given as

|\gamma|\cdot e_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma,|\alpha|>0}(-1)^{|\alpha|-1}\binom{|\alpha|}{\alpha}p_{\alpha}e_{\beta}\,,

(23)

where the addition of multi-indices
\alpha+\beta
is understood by components, the summation is over the product of all partitions of the components of
\gamma
and
\binom{|\alpha|}{\alpha}
denote the multinomial coefficients. We can now define an unbiased version of (
21

e_{\gamma}

\text{UE}\left[\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}^{(1)}\right)^{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\left(\text{tr}\,\mathcal{O}^{(m)}\right)^{\gamma_{m}}\right]=\frac{|\gamma|!\cdot e_{\gamma}}{{N_{\text{rv}}}\cdots({N_{\text{rv}}}-|\gamma|+1)}.

(24)

e_{\gamma}
we use the recursive definition (
23

p_{\alpha}
and
e_{\beta}

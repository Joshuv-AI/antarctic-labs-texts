Abstract
. We determine convex PBW-type Lyndon bases for two-parameter quantum groups U r , s ​ ( F 4 ) U_{r,s}(F_{4}) with detailed commutation relations. We construct a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra 𝔲 r , s ​ ( F 4 ) \mathfrak{u}_{r,s}(F_{4}) , as a quotient of U r , s ​ ( F 4 ) U_{r,s}(F_{4}) by a Hopf ideal generated by certain central elements, which is pointed, and of a Drinfel’d double structure under a certain condition. All of Hopf isomorphisms of 𝔲 r , s ​ ( F 4 ) \mathfrak{u}_{r,s}(F_{4}) are determined which are important for seeking the possible new pointed objects in low order with ( ℓ , 210 ) ≠ 1 (\ell,210)\neq 1 . Finally, necessary and sufficient conditions for 𝔲 r , s ​ ( F 4 ) \mathfrak{u}_{r,s}(F_{4}) to be a ribbon Hopf algebra are singled out by describing the left and right integrals.

In the past fifteen years, a systematic study of the two-parameter

F_{4}

quantum group of type

We survey some previous related work. The constructions of convex
PBW-type Lyndon bases in the two-parameter quantum groups have been
given for types

in
[

]
, types
E

[

complicated combinatorics than those in one-parameter. The
nice bases for the corresponding nonrestricted quantum groups at
generic case are important to the studies of restricted (or small)
quantum groups as finite-dimensional Hopf algebras, even to the
liftings of small quantum groups in the classification work of
finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras due to
Andruskiewitsch-Schneider (
[

]
). Good Lyndon words and Lyndon
bases have been described by Lalonde-Ram for semisimple Lie
algebras and their universal enveloping algebras
[

]
.
Combinatorial constructions of quantum PBW-type Lyndon bases depend
not only on a fixed choice of a convex ordering on a positive root
system which is given by the longest element of (finite) Weyl group,
but also on the inserting manner of the

-bracketings with
suitable structure constants into good Lyndon words (also see

[

]

[

[

Like one-parameter quantum groups in
[

\chi_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}=\gamma_{1}^{x_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{x_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{x_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{x_{4}}
, which
is equivalent to the existence of a solution to the linear equation
Ax=I
in

\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, where

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}2(y-z)&2z&0&0\\
-2y&2(y-z)&2z&0\\
0&-2y&y-z&z\\
0&0&-y&y-z\end{array}\right),

x
is the transpose of
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
,
I
is the transpose of

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})
. Note that
\mbox{det}A=4(y^{4}+z^{4}-y^{2}z^{2})
,

A
is invertible when
(4(y^{4}+z^{4}-y^{2}z^{2}),l)=1
, so the equation
has a unique solution which proves our claim. In particular, this implies that the matrix

((\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}}))_{\overline{k},\overline{j}}

is invertible, and
\phi|_{\mathbb{K}G(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})}
is bijective.

Now we shall show for each
i
(
1\leq i\leq 4
) that the following matrix is invertible:

((\eta_{i}\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(E_{i}\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}}))_{\overline{k},\overline{j}}.

This will complete the proof that
\phi
is injective on
(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})_{1}^{\text{coop}}
, as desired. In fact,

\begin{array}[]{ll}&(\eta_{i}\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(E_{i}\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}})\\
&\quad=(\eta_{i}\otimes\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(\Delta(E_{i})\Delta(\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}}))\\
&\quad=(\eta_{i}\otimes\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(E_{i}\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}}\otimes\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}})\\
&\quad=(\gamma_{1}^{k_{1}}\gamma_{2}^{k_{2}}\gamma_{3}^{k_{3}}\gamma_{4}^{k_{4}})(\omega_{1}^{j_{1}}\omega_{2}^{j_{2}}\omega_{3}^{j_{3}}\omega_{4}^{j_{4}}),\end{array}

the matrix associated with
\phi|_{(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})_{1}^{coop}}
is
block upper-triangular with the same block each and invertible. So

\phi|_{(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})_{1}^{coop}}
is injective.

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Integrals

In this section, we determine
the left and right integrals in the Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}

of
\mathfrak{u}_{r,s}(F_{4})
, which play an important role in knot invariant theory.
Let
H
be a
finite-dimensional Hopf algebra. An element
y\in H
is a

left
(resp.,
right
)
integral
if

ay=\varepsilon(a)y
(resp.,
ya=\varepsilon(a)y
) for all
a\in H
. The left (resp., right) integrals form a one-dimensional ideal

\int^{l}_{H}
(resp.,
\int^{r}_{H}
) of
H
, and
S_{H}(\int^{r}_{H})=\int^{l}_{H}

under the antipode
S_{H}
of
H
.

When
y\neq 0
is a left integral of
H
, there exists a unique
group-like element
\gamma
in the dual algebra
H^{*}
(the so-called

distinguished group-like element
of
H^{*}
) such that

ya=\gamma(a)y
. If we had begun instead with a right integral

y^{\prime}\in H
, then we would have
ay^{\prime}=\gamma^{-1}(a)y^{\prime}
. This follows
from the fact that group-like elements are invertible, and the
following calculation, which can be found in [
M
, p.22]:
When
y^{\prime}\in\int^{r}_{H}
,
S_{H}(y^{\prime})
is a nonzero multiple of
y
, so
that
S_{H}(y^{\prime})S_{H}(a)=\gamma(S_{H}(a))S_{H}(y^{\prime})
for all
a\in H
.
Applying
S_{H}^{-1}
, we find
ay^{\prime}=\gamma(S_{H}(a))y^{\prime}
. As
\gamma
is
group-like, we have

\gamma(S_{H}(a))=S_{H^{*}}(\gamma)(a)=\gamma^{-1}(a).

Now if
\lambda\neq 0
is a right integral of
H^{*}
, then there
exists a unique group-like element
g
of
H
(the

distinguished group-like element
of
H
) such that

\xi\lambda=\xi(g)\lambda
for all
\xi\in H^{*}
. The algebra
H
is

unimodular
(i.e.,
\int^{l}_{H}=\int^{r}_{H}
) if and only if

\gamma=\varepsilon
; and the dual algebra
H^{*}
is unimodular if
and only if
g=1
.

The left and right
H^{*}
-module actions on
H
are given by

[formula]

\xi\rightharpoonup a=\sum a_{(1)}\xi(a_{(2)}),\qquad a\leftharpoonup\xi=\sum\xi(a_{(1)})a_{(2)},

for all
\xi\in H^{*}
and
a\in H
. In particular,

\varepsilon\rightharpoonup a=a=a\leftharpoonup\varepsilon
for all

a\in H
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
t=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{4}(1+\omega_{i}+\cdots+\omega_{i}^{\ell-1})
and

x=E_{\beta_{24}}^{\ell-1}E_{\beta_{23}}^{\ell-1}\cdots E_{\beta_{1}}^{\ell-1},

then
y=tx

(resp.,\ y^{\prime}=xt)
is a left
(resp.,\ right)

integral in
\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

We have to show that
by=\varepsilon(b)y

holds for all
b\in\mathfrak{b}
. It suffices to verify this for the generators
\omega_{k}
and
E_{k}

since
\varepsilon
is an algebra homomorphism.

Observe that
\omega_{k}t=t=\varepsilon(\omega_{k})t
for all
k=1,2,3,4
, as
the
\omega_{i}
’s commute and

\omega_{k}(1+\omega_{k}+\cdots+\omega_{k}^{\ell-1})=1+\omega_{k}+\cdots+\omega_{k}^{\ell-1}
.
From that, relation
\omega_{k}y=\varepsilon(\omega_{k})y
is clear, for
1\leq k\leq 4
.

Now we compute
E_{k}y
. We get

E_{k}t=\prod_{i=1}^{4}(1+\langle\omega_{k}^{\prime},\omega_{i}\rangle^{-1}\omega_{i}+\cdots+\langle\omega_{k}^{\prime},\omega_{i}\rangle^{-(\ell-1)}\omega_{i}^{\ell-1})E_{k}

from a direct computation. It remains to verify that

E_{k}x=0=\varepsilon(E_{k})x
.

We first prove a more general result: for
1\leq i\leq j\leq 24
, let

\widehat{E_{j,i}}={E}_{\beta_{j}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1},

we will prove
{E}_{\beta_{i}}\widehat{{E}_{j,i}}=0
by
induction on
j-i
.

If
j-i=0
, then

{E}_{\beta_{i}}{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1}={E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell}=0
. Assume that
the result holds for
j-i\leq n
, if
j-i=n+1
, using Lemma

3.10
for

p=\langle\omega_{\beta_{j}}^{\prime},\omega_{\beta_{j}}\rangle,q=\langle\omega_{\beta_{j}}^{\prime},\omega_{\beta_{i}}\rangle
and
Theorem
3.9
, we get

{E}_{\beta_{i}}{E}_{\beta_{j}}^{\ell-1}=q^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{i}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-1}q^{k}\left[\ell{-}1\atop k\right]_{p}{E}_{\beta_{j}}^{k}\left((ad_{q}E_{\beta_{j}})_{R}^{\ell-1-k}E_{\beta_{i}}\right),

where
(ad_{q}E_{\beta_{j}})_{R}^{\ell-1-k}E_{\beta_{i}}\in B_{i,j}
.
So

\begin{split}{E}_{\beta_{i}}\widehat{{E}_{j,i}}&={E}_{\beta_{i}}({E}_{\beta_{j}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}\cdots E_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1})\\
&=q^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{i}}({E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-2}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1})\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-1}q^{k}\left[\ell{-}1\atop k\right]_{p}{E}_{\beta_{j}}^{k}\left((ad_{q}E_{\beta_{j}})_{R}^{\ell-1-k}E_{\beta_{i}}\right)({E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-2}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1}).\end{split}

By the induction hypothesis, we have

{E}_{\beta_{i}}({E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-2}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1})=0,

for every
E_{\beta_{t}}\in B_{i,j}
,
i<t<j
. By the
induction hypothesis, we have

{E}_{\beta_{t}}({E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-2}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1})={E}_{\beta_{t}}({E}_{\beta_{j-1}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{j-2}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{t}}^{\ell-1}){E}_{\beta_{t-1}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{i}}^{\ell-1}=0,

therefore,
{E}_{\beta_{i}}\widehat{{E}_{j,i}}=0
, in
particular,
E_{k}x=0,1\leq k\leq 4
.

The proof for the right integral is similar.
∎

A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
is semisimple if and only if

\varepsilon(\int_{H}^{l})\neq 0
or
\varepsilon(\int_{H}^{r})\neq 0
. For

H=\mathfrak{b}
,
y
and
y^{\prime}
are respectively bases of

\int_{\mathfrak{b}}^{l}
and

\varepsilon(y)=0=\varepsilon(y^{\prime})

, so we

\mathfrak{b}

8.
To be a ribbon Hopf algebra

A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
is

quasitriangular
if there is an invertible element
R=\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
in
H\otimes H
such that
\Delta^{op}(a)=R\Delta(a)R^{-1}

for all
a\in H
, and
R
satisfies the relations
(\Delta\otimes id)R=R_{13}R_{23}
,
(id\otimes\Delta)R=R_{13}R_{12}
, where

R_{12}=\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\otimes 1
,
R_{13}=\sum x_{i}\otimes 1\otimes y_{i}
, and

R_{23}=\sum 1\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
.

Write
u=\sum S(y_{i})x_{i}
. Then
c=uS(u)
is central in
H
(cf.

[
Ka
]
) and is referred to as the
Casimir element
.

An element
v\in H
is a
quasi-ribbon element
of
quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
if
(i)

v^{2}=c
,
(ii)

S(v)=v
,
(iii)

\varepsilon(v)=1
,
(iv)

\Delta(v)=(R_{21}R)^{-1}(v\otimes v)
, where

R_{21}=\sum y_{i}\otimes x_{i}
. If moreover
v
is central in
H
, then

v
is a
ribbon element
, and
(H,R,v)
is called a

ribbon Hopf algebra
. Ribbon elements provide a very
effective means of constructing invariants of
3
-manifolds (cf.

[
RT
]
).

The Drinfel’d double
D(A)
of a
finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
A
is quasitriangular, and
Kauffman-Radford (
[
KR
]
) proved a criterion for
D(A)
to be
ribbon.

Theorem 8.1
.

([KR, Thm. 3])

Assume
A
is a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, let
g
and

\gamma
be the distinguished group-like elements of
A
and
A^{*}

respectively. Then

(\mathrm{i})

(D(A),R)
has a quasi-ribbon element if and only
if there exist group-like elements
h\in A
,
\delta\in A^{*}
such
that
h^{2}=g
,
\delta^{2}=\gamma
.

(\mathrm{ii})

(D(A),R)
has a ribbon element if and only if
there exist
h
and
\delta
as in
(\mathrm{i})
such that

S^{2}(a)=h(\delta\rightharpoonup a\leftharpoonup\delta^{-1})h^{-1},\ \forall\;a\in A
.

Next we determine the distinguished group-like elements in

\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{*}
, since all group-like elements
are precisely the algebra homomorphisms in

\mbox{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{b},\mathbb{K})
. To verify that
it is distinguished, it suffices to consider its values on
generators.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
2\rho=16\alpha_{1}+30\alpha_{2}+42\alpha_{3}+22\alpha_{4}
, where

\rho
is the half sum of positive roots of the root system
F_{4}
. Let
\gamma\in\mbox{Alg}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{b},\mathbb{K})
be defined by

\gamma(E_{k})=0,\quad\gamma(\omega_{k})=\langle\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{16}\langle\omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{30}\langle\omega_{3}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{42}\langle\omega_{4}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{22},

then
\gamma
is a distinguished group-like element in

\mathfrak{b}^{*}
.

Proof.

It suffices to verify
ya=\gamma(a)y
for

a=E_{k}
,
a=\omega_{k}

(1\leq k\leq 4)
and
y=tx
. It follows from the
proof of Theorem
7.1
that
xE_{k}=0

(1\leq k\leq 4)

, and

\begin{split}y\omega_{k}&=tx\omega_{k}\\
&=t({E}_{\beta_{24}}^{\ell-1}{E}_{\beta_{23}}^{\ell-1}\cdots{E}_{\beta_{1}}^{\ell-1})\omega_{k}\\
&=\langle\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{-16(\ell-1)}\langle\omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{-30(\ell-1)}\langle\omega_{3}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{-42(\ell-1)}\langle\omega_{4}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{-22(\ell-1)}t\omega_{k}x\\
&=\langle\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{16}\langle\omega_{2}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{30}\langle\omega_{3}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{42}\langle\omega_{4}^{\prime},\omega_{k}\rangle^{22}t\omega_{k}x\\
&=\gamma(\omega_{k})y,\end{split}

where
1\leq k\leq 4
.
∎

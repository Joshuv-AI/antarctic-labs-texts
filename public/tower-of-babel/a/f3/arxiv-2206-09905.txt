An Itô-Wentzell formula for rough paths
Rafael A. Castrequini, Pedro J. Catuogno, Alvaro E. Machado Hernandez

Abstract
This article shows an Itô-Wentzell type formula adapted to rough paths with α \alpha -Hölder regularity α ∈ ( 1 3 , 1 2 ] \alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}] . We improve previous results of R. Castrequini and P. Catuogno [ 5 ] for the Young integral and C. Keller and J. Zhang [ 10 ] for rough paths.

Introduction

In 1950 K. Itô
[
9
]
published a formula for the derivation of a deterministic function whose argument is a solution of a stochastic differential equation, subsequently called Itô formula. The extension of this formula from deterministic functions to random fields of Itô type was obtained by A. Wentzell
[
20
]
in 1965.

Later several generalizations of the Itô-Wentzell formula were obtained by several authors we mention between others the following: M. Bismut
[
1
]
, H. Kunita
[
12
]
, B. Rozovskii
[
19
]
to the context of semimartingales and stochastic flows, N. Krylov
[
14
]
to Schwartz distributions, D. Ocone and E. Pardoux
[
18
]
to anticipating stochastic calculus, R. Castrequini and P. Catuogno
[
5
]
to Young integral and R. Buckdhan, J. Ma and J. Zhang
[
2
]
to the pathwise functional Itô calculus.

In 1998 T. Lyons
[
16
]
introduced the rough path theory. It is a generalization of the ordinary differential equation theory to the case when the equation is driven by an irregular signal, see T. Lyons
[
16
]
, M. Gubinelli
[
8
]
, P. Friz and N. Victoir
[
7
]
and P. Friz and M. Hairer
[
6
]
. The rough path theory is powerful enough to include in its scope the stochastic differential equations. In 2004, M. Gubinelli
[
8
]
gave a new approach to the theory of rough paths with the introduction of the controlled paths. The controlled paths are the natural integrands of the rough integral, see
[
8
]
and
[
6
]
. It is important to highlight that the space of controlled paths is a Banach space closed by integration with respect to rough paths, composition with smooth functions and solutions of rough differential equation. From this viewpoint the rough path theory is formally similar to the classical semimartingale theory.

It is natural to ask for an Itô-Wentzel type formula in the context of rough paths. In 2016, C. Keller and J. Zhang
[
10
]
gave a version of the Itô-Wentzell formula for rough paths, see formula (3.14) in
[
10
]
. This formula applies to controlled paths given by a rough integral under restrictive hypotesis on the vector field
g
. In this article we give a complete proof of a Itô-Wentzell type formula in the context of rough paths in a general setting. More precisely, Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
,
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
be an
\alpha
-rough path and
(h,\partial_{X}h)
be a controlled path. Let
g
to be defined by

g(t,x)=g(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}h(s,x)d\mathbf{X}_{s}.

Assume that
g
is twice differentiable in the variable
x
and the functions
(t,x)\mapsto Dg_{t}(x)
and
(t,x)\mapsto D^{2}g_{t}(x)
are continuous.
Then under certain condition to be explained in Section 4, we have that for any controlled path
(Z,\partial_{X}Z)
holds the following Itô-Wentzell formula,

\displaystyle g(t,Z_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(0,Z_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}h(r,Z_{r})d\mathbf{X}_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}Dg(r,Z_{r})d_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{r}+

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}Dh(r,Z_{r})\circ\partial_{X}Z_{r}d[\mathbf{X}]_{r}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}D^{2}g(r,Z_{r})\partial_{X}Z_{r}\otimes\partial_{X}Z_{r}d[\mathbf{X}]_{r},

where
[\mathbf{X}]
is the bracket of
\mathbf{X}
.

2
Rough paths and Controlled paths

In this section we present the elements of rough path theory. We shall use freely concepts and notations of P. Friz and M. Hairer
[
6
]
.

Let
V
,
U
and
W
be Banach spaces,
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
and
T>0
. We will denote by
\triangle_{T}
the set
\{(s,t):0\leq s\leq t\leq T\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\alpha
-Hölder rough path on
V
is a pair
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
where
X:[0,T]\rightarrow V
and
\mathbb{X}:\ \triangle_{T}\rightarrow V\otimes V
such that

\|X\|_{\alpha}:=\sup_{s,t}\frac{|X_{t}-X_{s}|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}<\infty~;~\|\mathbb{X}\|_{2\alpha}:=\sup_{s,t}\frac{|\mathbb{X}_{st}|}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}<\infty

and satisfies the Chen’s relation

\mathbb{X}_{st}=\mathbb{X}_{su}+\mathbb{X}_{ut}+X_{su}\otimes X_{ut}

for all
0\leq s\leq u\leq t\leq T
.

We denote the set of
\alpha
-Hölder rough paths with respect to
X
by
\mathcal{C}_{X}([0,T];V)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=(\tilde{X},\tilde{\mathbb{X}})
be
\alpha
-Hölder rough paths. The
\alpha
-Hölder distance between
\mathbf{X}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
is given by

\|\mathbf{X};\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\|_{\alpha}:=\|X-\tilde{X}\|_{\alpha}+\|\mathbb{X}-\tilde{\mathbb{X}}\|_{\alpha}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
be an
\alpha
-rough path. The bracket of
\mathbf{X}
is defined by

[\mathbf{X}]_{st}=X_{st}\otimes X_{st}-2Sym(\mathbb{X}_{st})

where
Sym\mathbb{X}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{X}_{st}+\mathbb{X}_{st}^{*})
denote the symmetric part of
\mathbb{X}
. Here
∗
denotes the transpose, given by
(x\otimes y)^{*}=y\otimes x
for all
x,y\in V
.

If
[\mathbf{X}]=0
we say that
\mathbf{X}
is a weak geometric rough path.

We observe that

\|X\|_{\infty}\leq|X_{0}|+T^{\alpha}\|X\|_{\alpha}~~;~~\|[\mathbf{X}]\|_{2\alpha}\leq\|X\|^{2}_{\alpha}+2\|\mathbb{X}\|_{2\alpha}.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{C}_{X}([0,T];V)
we have that there exists an canonical
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=(X,\tilde{\mathbb{X})}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}([0,T];V)
weak geometric, we called this rough path the geometrization of
\mathbf{X}
. It is defined by

\tilde{\mathbb{X}}:=\mathbb{X}+\frac{1}{2}[\mathbf{X}].

Let

C^{\alpha}([0,T];V)=\{X:[0,T]\rightarrow V:\|X\|_{\alpha}<\infty\}

denote the set of
\alpha
-Hölder functions from
[0,T]
into
V
.

Let

C^{2\alpha}([0,T]^{2};V)=\{R:\triangle_{T}\rightarrow V:\|R\|_{2\alpha}:=\sup_{s,t}\frac{|R_{st}|}{|t-s|^{2\alpha}}<\infty\}

denote the set of
2\alpha
-Hölder functions from
[0,T]^{2}
into
V
.

We denote by
L(V,W)
the space of continuous linear mappings from
V
to
W
. We have the following natural operation from
L(V,L(U,W))\times L(V,U)
into
L(V\otimes V,U)
given by

(\psi\cdot\varphi)v\otimes v^{\prime}:=\psi(v)(\varphi(v^{\prime}))

where
\psi\in L(V,L(U,W))
,
\varphi\in L(V,U)
and
v,v^{\prime}\in V
.

For simplicity of notation, we identify
L(V,L(V,W))
with
L(V\otimes V,W)
via

\eta(x)(y)=\eta(x\otimes y)

where
\eta\in L(V,L(V,W))
and
x,y\in V
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];W)
. We say that a pair
(Y,\partial_{X}Y)
is a controlled path with respect to
X
if
Y\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];W)
,
\partial_{X}Y\in C^{\alpha}([0,T];L(V,W))
and
R^{Y}\in C^{2\alpha}(\triangle_{T};W)
where

R^{Y}_{st}:=Y_{st}-\partial_{X}Y_{s}X_{st}

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
.
We said that
\partial_{X}Y
is a Gubinelli derivative of
Y
.

We denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}=\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];W)
the space of controlled paths with respect to
X
.

We observe that
\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}
is a Banach space provided with the norm

\|Y,\partial_{X}Y\|_{\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}}:=|Y_{0}|+|\partial_{X}Y_{0}|+\|\partial_{X}Y\|_{\alpha}+\|R^{Y}\|_{2\alpha}.

To shorten notation, we write
\|Y\|_{\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}}
instead of
\|Y,\partial_{X}Y\|_{\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}}
.

Following M. Gubinelli
[
8
]
we define the rough integral of a controlled path with respect to a rough path, see Theorem 4.10 of P. Friz and M. Hairer
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{X}([0,T];V)
,
(Y,\partial_{X}Y)\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];L(V,W))
. Then the limit

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}d\mathbf{X}_{r}:=\lim_{|\pi|\rightarrow 0}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}Y_{u}X_{uv}+\partial_{X}Y_{u}\mathbb{X}_{uv}

there exists for all
t\in[0.T]
, where the limit is taken over any sequence of partitions
\pi
of the interval
[0,t]
with mesh size
|\pi|
convergent to zero. We call this limit the rough integral of the controlled path
(Y,\partial_{X}Y)
with respect to the rough path
\mathbf{X}
. Also holds the following estimative for all
s\leq t
,

|\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d\mathbf{X}_{r}-Y_{s}X_{st}-\partial_{X}Y_{s}\mathbb{X}_{st}|\leq C\{\|R^{Y}\|_{2\alpha}\|X\|_{\alpha}+\|\partial_{X}Y\|_{\alpha}\|\mathbb{X}\|_{2\alpha}\}|t-s|^{3\alpha}

where the constant
C
depends only of
\alpha
.

We observe that holds the following conversion formula type Itô-Stratonovich:

\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d\tilde{\mathbf{X}}_{r}=\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d\mathbf{X}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{X}Y_{r}d[\mathbf{X}]_{r}

(2.1)

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. Here the integral with respect to
[\mathbf{X}]
is understood in the Young sense, see M. Hairer and P. Friz
[
6
]
pp 61, and R. Castrequini and P. Catuogno
[
5
]
, M. Gubinelli
[
8
]
, P. Friz and N. Victoir
[
7
]
, T. Lyons
[
17
]
between others.

The next theorem establishes the integral of a controlled with respect to a controlled path, see
[

]

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{X}([0,T];V)
. Let
(Y,\partial_{X}Y)\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];L(W,U))
and
(Z,\partial_{X}Z)\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];W)
. Then the limit

\int_{0}^{t}Y_{r}d_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{r}:=\lim_{|\pi|\rightarrow 0}\sum_{[u,v]\in\pi}Y_{u}Z_{uv}+\partial_{X}Y_{u}\cdot\partial_{X}Z_{u}\mathbb{X}_{uv}

there exists for all
t\in[0.T]
, where the limit is taken over any sequence of partitions
\pi
of the interval
[0,t]
with mesh size
|\pi|
convergent to zero. We call this limit the integral of the controlled path
(Y,\partial_{X}Y)
with respect to the controlled path
(Z,\partial_{X}Z)
. Also holds the following estimative for all
s\leq t
,

\displaystyle|\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{r}-Y_{s}Z_{st}-\partial_{X}Y_{s}\cdot\partial_{X}Z_{s}\mathbb{X}_{st}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\{\|\partial_{X}Y\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{X}Z\|_{\alpha}\|X\|^{2}_{\alpha}+\|Y\|_{\alpha}\|R^{Z}\|_{2\alpha}

\displaystyle+\|R^{Y}\|_{2\alpha}\|\partial_{X}Z\|_{\infty}\|X\|_{\alpha}+

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{X}Y\cdot\partial_{X}Z\|_{\alpha}\|\mathbb{X}\|_{2\alpha}\}|t-s|^{3\alpha}

where the constant
C
depends only of
\alpha
.

We observe that the integrals are controlled paths, in fact

(\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{r}d\mathbf{X}_{r},Y)\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];W)~\mbox{ and }~(\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y_{r}d_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{r},Y\partial_{X}Z)\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T];U).

(2.2)

We also have the following conversion formula type Itô-Stratonovich for controlled paths

\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d_{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}Z_{r}=\int_{s}^{t}Y_{r}d_{\mathbf{X}}Z_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{X}Y_{r}\cdot\partial_{X}Z_{r}d[\mathbf{X}]_{r}

(2.3)

for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
,
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{X}([0,T];V)
and

(3.1)

4
The Itô-Wentzell formula

The Itô-Wentzell formula for rough paths is established by our next theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
,
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{X}([0,T];V)
and
(h,\partial_{X}h)\in C(U,\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T],L(V,W)))
. We assume that

1.

h:[0,T]\times U\rightarrow L(V,W)
is continuous and twice differentiable in
U
.

2.

Dh:[0,T]\times U\rightarrow L(U,L(V,W))
is continuous and differentiable in
U
.

3.

(Dh,\partial_{X}Dh)\in C(U,\mathcal{D}_{X}^{2\alpha}([0,T],L(U,L(V,W))))

Let

g(t,x)=g(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}h(s,x)d\mathbf{X}_{s}.

Assume that
g:[0,T]\times U\rightarrow W
is twice differentiable in
U
and the functions
(t,x)\mapsto Dg_{t}(x)
and
(t,x)\mapsto D^{2}g_{t}(x)
are continuous.
Then for any
Z\in\mathcal{D}_{X}^{\alpha}([0,T];U)
,

Symmetries for spherical functions of type χ \chi for quantum symmetric pairs
Stein Meereboer

Abstract
Abstract. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and let 𝐔 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) \mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) denote the associated Drinfel’d Jimbo quantized enveloping algebra. In this paper we study spherical functions of 𝐔 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) \mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) related to characters. We show invariance under the Wang-Zhang braid group operators and show relative Weyl group invariance, when restricted to the quantum torus.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background

There is a close connection between spherical functions on compact symmetric spaces and orthogonal polynomials. Our interest lies in studying quantum analogs of these objects in the framework of Gail Letzter’s quantum symmetric pairs
(\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})

[
Let99
]
.

One of the early motivations for studying quantum analogs of symmetric pairs was the study of quantum analogs of zonal spherical functions. Pioneering this study was Koornwinder’s paper
[
Koo93
]
, where zonal spherical functions related to quantum analogs of the symmetric pair
(SU(2),U(1))
are interpreted as Askey-Wilson polynomials, which are a special class of Macdonald polynomials
[
Mac03
]
. Later, Letzter gave interpretations of zonal spherical functions on quantum symmetric pairs, as Macdonald polynomials. To be precise, when the root system is reduced, the restrictions of zonal spherical functions to a Cartan subalgebra equal Macdonald polynomials
[
Let04
]
. Over two decades later, the theory of quantum symmetric pairs has flourished. In particular, insights by Huanchen Bao and Weiqiang Wang have contributed substantially to the understanding of the structure of quantum symmetric pairs with respect to various aspects of the theory. In particular, the theory allows for various tools for studying harmonic analysis of these spaces. Although the general theory of quantum symmetric pairs has come a far way, the understanding of generalized spherical functions is less well developed. In this paper we apply the recent advancements to the study of
\chi
-spherical functions.

Classically,
\chi
-spherical functions related to characters of symmetric pairs are invariant under the action of the relative Weyl group. In the quantum setting, relative Weyl group symmetries have only been systematically studied for zonal spherical functions. In
[
Let03
]
, Gail Letzter shows that for each coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
there exists a unique coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{d,t}}
such that the zonal spherical functions related to
(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}},\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{d,t}})
are invariant under the relative Weyl group when restricted to the Cartan subalgebra
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}
. These results indicate that there should be invariance properties of
\chi
-spherical functions related to characters of quantum symmetric pairs.

Central to this paper are the recent developments by Weiqiang Wang and Weinan Zhang
[
WZ23
]
, where relative braid group symmetries on the coideal subalgebras
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
are constructed. These symmetries do not preserve
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
in general, but they preserve the quantized function algebra
R_{q}(G)
.

1.2
The basic idea

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra, and let
\Theta:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be an involutive automorphism. We write
\mathfrak{k}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\Theta(x)=x\}
to denote the fixed point algebra of
\Theta
and refer to
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
as a symmetric pair. Recall that involutive automorphisms
\Theta
, up to conjugation, are determined by Satake diagrams
(\mathbf{I},\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},\tau)
. The theory of quantum symmetric pairs developed in
[
Let99
]
provides quantum group analogs of the pair of universal enveloping algebras
(\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{k}))
. A quantum symmetric pair
(\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
consists of a Drinfel’d Jimbo quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and a coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}\subset\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, that depends on a family of scalars
\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s}\in(\mathbb{F}^{\times})^{\mathbf{I}\setminus\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}}
. Such a family of scalars is said to be balanced if
\boldsymbol{s}=0
and
c_{i}=c_{\tau(i)}
for each
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}=\mathbf{I}\setminus\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
and we put
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{c,0}}=\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
.
Recent advancements show the existence of a relative braid group action
\mathrm{Br}(W^{\Sigma})\curvearrowright\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}

[
WZ23
]
.
For balanced parameters, the relative braid group operators admit a factorization

\boldsymbol{T}_{i,-1}^{\prime}(b)=\Upsilon_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i,-1}^{\prime}(b)\Upsilon_{i}^{-1}\qquad\text{for}\qquad b\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}},i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}

where
\mathcal{T}_{i,-1}^{\prime}
is a re-scaled Lusztig braid group operator
[
WZ23
,
Thm 9.9
]
and
\Upsilon_{i}
is the rank one quasi-
K
matrix related to the rank one Satake subdiagram
\{i,\tau(i)\}\cup\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
.
As the rank one quasi-
K
matrices act locally finitely on finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules, we obtain a precomposition operator on the quantized function algebra
-\circ\boldsymbol{T}_{i,-1}^{\prime}:R_{q}(G)\to R_{q}(G)
defined by
\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\boldsymbol{T}_{i,-1}^{\prime}
. The study of this operator through the representation theory of
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
will be key in understanding the braid group symmetries on spherical functions related to characters.

1.3
Results

In this subsection, we present the main results of the paper.

The first goal of the paper is to establish symmetries for characters of quantum symmetric pairs. Through the study of one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
modules and a case-by-case check, we establish the invariance of characters by the Wang-Zang braid group operators.

Theorem A

(Corollary
4.24
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be a balanced parameter. Then for each character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}
that occurs in a finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight module and each
w\in\mathrm{Br}(W^{\Sigma})
, it holds that
\chi\circ\boldsymbol{T}^{\prime}_{w,-1}=\chi.

Let
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}},\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{d,t}}\subset\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
be quantum symmetric pair coideal subalgebras, and let
\chi
and
\eta
be characters of
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{d,t}}
, respectively. We say that
\varphi\in R_{q}(G)
is a
(\chi,\eta)
-spherical function if
\varphi
transforms as

\varphi(bxb^{\prime})=\chi(b)\varphi(x)\eta(b^{\prime})\qquad\text{for all }b\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}},b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{d,t}},\text{ and }x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

We write
R_{q}(G)_{\chi}^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}}
to denote the space of
(\chi,\chi)
-spherical functions for
(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}},\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
. Through studying the structure of
\chi
-spherical functions and the factorization of relative braid group operators on
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, we show that
\chi
-spherical functions are invariant under the Wang-Zhang braid group operators.

Theorem B

(Proposition
5.2
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be a balanced parameter. Then for each
\varphi\in R_{q}(G)^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}_{\chi}
and each
w\in\mathrm{Br}(W^{\Sigma})
, it holds that
\varphi\circ\boldsymbol{T}_{w,-1}^{\prime}=\varphi
.

The main theorem of the paper generalizes Letzter’s relative Weyl group invariance
[
Let03
,
Thm 5.3
]
to general characters of the coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
. We recall the necessary notions to state the invariance. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-subalgebra generated by the
K_{h}
, where
h\in Y_{\Theta}=\{h\in Y:\Theta(h)=-h\}
, we refer to
\mathcal{A}
as the quantum torus. We write
\mathrm{Res}:R_{q}(G)\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{F})
to denote the restriction map to
\mathcal{A}
and denote
R_{q}(\mathcal{A})
by the image of
\mathrm{Res}
. Recall that there is a natural action of the relative Weyl group
W^{\Sigma}
on
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{F})
. Let
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
be a quantum symmetric pair coideal subalgebra, and let
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}
be a character. Then we show the existence of a coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\chi}
and a character
\chi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\chi}}
such that, when restricted to
\mathcal{A}
, the
(\chi^{\prime},\chi)
-spherical functions are relative Weyl group invariant.
We write
{}^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\chi}}R_{q}(G)^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}_{\chi}
to denote the space of
(\chi^{\prime},\chi)
-spherical functions.

Theorem C
.

[Theorem
5.13
]
Let
\boldsymbol{c}
be a balanced parameter. Then restriction to the quantum torus defines a
\mathbb{F}
-linear map

\mathrm{Res}:\bigoplus_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}}{{}^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\chi}}R_{q}(G)^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}_{\chi}}\to R_{q}(\mathcal{A})^{W^{\Sigma}}.

For
\chi=\epsilon
one recovers Gail Letzter’s relative Weyl group invariance for balanced parameters
[
Let03
,
Thm 5.3
]
. Note that our proof of Gail Letzter’s result is different from the original proof
[
Let03
,
Thm 5.3
]
.
In Section
4.3
, we address the problem of the existence of characters for the coideal subalgebra
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
. Recall that a symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is called irreducible if
\mathfrak{g}
cannot be written as the
direct sum of two semisimple Lie subalgebras which both admit
\Theta
as an involution. Furthermore, recall that an irreducible symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is said to be of Hermitian type if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
has a nontrivial center.

Theorem D

(
4.21
)
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s})
be a specializable parameter and let the symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
be irreducible. Then the following three statements are equivalent:

1.

There exist countably infinite many pairwise non-isomorphic one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
modules that occur in finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules.

2.

There exist non-trivial one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
modules that occur in finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules.

3.

The symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is of Hermitian type.

Consequently, for an irreducible symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
the space
\bigoplus\limits_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}}{{}^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}^{\chi}}R_{q}(G)^{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}_{\chi}}
decomposes into countably infinite many nontrivial constituents and if and only if
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k})
is of Hermitian type. Otherwise, the only constituent arises from to the trivial representation. In particular, in the irreducible Hermitian case this ensures that Theorem
C
is nontrivial.

1.4
Organization

Sections 2-3 are of preparatory nature. In Section
2
, we fix notation and conventions on quantum groups and the quantized function algebra. Section
3
introduces quantum symmetric pairs, quasi-
K
matrices and the Wang-Zhang braid group operators, which we extend to operators on
R_{q}(G)
.
The main new results of the paper are contained in Section 4 and 5.
In Section
4.1
, we study the characters of
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
that occur in irreducible left and right
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules. We establish that the characters of
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
occur multiplicity free and investigate their embedding. We show that characters of quantum symmetric pairs are invariant under the Wang-Zhang braid group operators. In Section
4.2
, we study spherical functions in some generality. We show the existence and uniqueness of
\chi
-spherical functions. In Section
4.3
we characterize the existence of one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
modules occurring in
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
highest weight modules. Section
5
is the heart of the paper and here we combine the results of the previous sections to show that spherical functions related to characters are invariant under the Wang-Zhang braid group operators. The rest of the paper is devoted to the interpretation of these symmetries when restricted to the quantum torus.
Appendix
A
is devoted to completing the proof of Proposition
4.23
and appendix
B
contains the proof of Lemma
5.4
. Both proofs involve case-by-case checks.

Quantum groups and bar involutions

2.1
Quantum groups

We introduce notation as in
[
Lus10
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be be a complex semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbf{I}}
. Let
D=\text{diag}(d_{i}\,:\,d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1},\,i\in\mathbf{I})
be a symmetrizer, meaning that
DC
is symmetric and
\gcd\{d_{i}\,:\,i\in\mathbf{I}\}=1
. Fix a set of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\,:\,i\in\mathbf{I}\}
and a set of simple coroots
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\,:\,i\in\mathbf{I}\}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{I}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbf{I}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be the root lattice. Let
(\,,\,)
be the normalized Killing form on
\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{I}
so that the short roots have length
2
. The Weyl group
W
is generated by the simple reflections
s_{i}:\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{I}\to\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{I}
, with
i\in\mathbf{I}
defined by
s_{i}(\alpha_{j})=\alpha_{j}-c_{ij}\alpha_{i},
for
j\in\mathbf{I}
.

Let
q
be an indeterminate and
\mathbb{Q}(q)
be the field of rational functions in
q
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. For each index
i\in\mathbf{I}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, set

q_{i}:=q^{d_{i}},\qquad[n]_{i}:=\cfrac{q_{i}^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad[n]_{i}!:=\prod_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{i}.

Let
(Y,X,\langle\,,\,\rangle.\dots)
be a root datum of type
(\mathbf{I},\cdot)
; cf.
[
Lus10
,
2.2
]
. By definition, there exist embedding
\mathbf{I}\to X
,
i\mapsto\alpha_{i}
and
\mathbf{I}\to Y
,
i\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. The relation between
\langle\,,\,\rangle
and
(\,,\,)
is
\langle i,j^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}
, for
i,j\in\mathbf{I}
.
The quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, associated to the root datum, is the unital associative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
, generated by the symbols
E_{i},F_{i}
for
i\in\mathbf{I}
and
K_{h}
for
h\in Y
, these symbols are subject to the following relations for
i,j\in\mathbf{I},
and
h,h_{1},h_{2}\in Y:

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle K_{h_{1}}K_{h_{2}}

\displaystyle=K_{h_{1}+h_{2}},

\displaystyle K_{h}E_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}E_{i}K_{h},

\displaystyle K_{h}F_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{-\langle h,\alpha_{i}\rangle}F_{i}K_{h},

\displaystyle E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\cfrac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},

as well as quantum Serre relations for
i\neq j

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=1-a_{i,j}}(-1)^{s}E_{i}^{(r)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(s)}=\sum_{r+s=1-a_{i,j}}(-1)^{s}F_{i}^{(r)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(s)}=0.

Here we use the notation

K_{i}:=K_{d_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}},\qquad E_{i}^{(n)}:=\frac{1}{[n]_{i}!}E_{i}^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{i}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{[n]_{i}!}F_{i}^{n}.

The quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
has the structure of a Hopf-algebra
(\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\triangle,\epsilon,\iota,S,)
via

\displaystyle\triangle(K_{i})

\displaystyle=K_{i}\otimes K_{i},

\displaystyle\triangle(E_{i})

\displaystyle=E_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}\otimes E_{i},

\displaystyle\triangle(F_{i})

\displaystyle=1\otimes F_{i}+F_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle\epsilon(E_{i})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(F_{i})=0,

\displaystyle\epsilon(K_{i})=1,

\displaystyle\iota(f)

\displaystyle=f\cdot 1,

\displaystyle S(E_{i})

\displaystyle=-K_{i}^{-1}E_{i},

\displaystyle S(F_{i})=-F_{i}K_{i},

\displaystyle S(K_{h})=K_{-h},

where
i\in\mathbf{I}
,
f\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
and
h\in Y
.

We refer to
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{+}
,
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{-}
and
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}
as the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
subalgebras generated by
E_{i}
,
F_{i}
for
i\in\mathbf{I}
and
K_{h}
for
h\in Y
respectively. We refer to involutive algebra anti-automorphisms as
anti-involutions
. Let us record the (anti)-involutions

1.

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra anti-involution
\varrho:\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

E_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\qquad F_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\qquad K_{i}\mapsto K_{i},\qquad\qquad i\in\mathbf{I}.

2.

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra anti-involution
\sigma:\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

E_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\qquad F_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\qquad K_{i}\mapsto K_{i}^{-1},\qquad\qquad i\in\mathbf{I}.

3.

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-algebra involution
\omega:\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

E_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\qquad F_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\qquad K_{i}\mapsto K_{i}^{-1},\qquad\qquad i\in\mathbf{I}.

4.

There exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra involution
\overline{\,\cdot\,}:\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

E_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\qquad F_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\qquad K_{i}\mapsto K_{i}^{-1},\qquad q\mapsto q^{-1}.

We refer to the
\mathbb{Q}(q)
involution mapping
q\mapsto q^{-1}
as
\overline{\,\cdot\,}:\mathbb{Q}(q)\to\mathbb{Q}(q)
.

We note that
\omega=\sigma\circ\varrho
.

Remark 2.1
.

If we consider
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
over the algebraic closure
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{Q}(q)
, all of these maps remain (anti)-involutions over
\mathbb{F}
.

Checking that these maps are (anti)-involutions can be done by verifying that the relations are preserved, cf.

[
Jan96
,
4.6, 11.9
]
. We refer to the involution
\overline{\,\cdot\,}
as the
bar
involution. A
\mathbb{Q}
-linear operator on a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
or
\mathbb{F}
algebra is called
anti-linear
if
q^{m}\mapsto q^{-m}
, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2
Weight modules

The goal of this subsection is to recall the terminology and structure of finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules and their bar involutions.
A module
M
is called a
weight module
if
M
has a decomposition of the form

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in X}M_{\lambda},\qquad\text{where}\qquad M_{\lambda}=\{m\in M\,:\,K_{h}m=q^{\langle h,\lambda\rangle}m,\,h\in Y\}.

We say that a vector
v\in M
is a
weight vector of weight
\lambda
if
K_{h}v=q^{\langle h,\lambda\rangle}v
for each
h\in Y.

A module
M
is called a
highest weight module
if there exists a weight vector
v\in M
with the property that
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})v=M
and
E_{i}v=0
for all
i\in\mathbf{I}.

The irreducible finite dimensional highest weight left modules are parameterized by the dominant weights
X^{+}
, cf.
[
Lus10
]
. We write
L(\lambda)
to denote the simple left module corresponding to a dominant weight
\lambda\in X^{+}
.
The theory of highest weight modules has a natural analog for right modules, cf.
[
Lus10
,
3.5.7
]
.

We denote by
\mathscr{U}
an algebraic completion of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, see
[
BK16
,
Sect 3
]
for the construction of
\mathscr{U}
.
Let
M=L(\lambda)
be an irreducible
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight module and
v_{\lambda}
be a choice of highest weight vector, then there exists a
bar involution

\overline{\,\cdot\,}^{M}:M\to M
relative to
v_{\lambda}
with the property that

\overline{X\cdot v_{\lambda}}^{M}:=\overline{X}\cdot v_{\lambda}\qquad\text{for}\qquad X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

If it is clear from the context, we denote
\overline{\,\cdot\,}^{M}=\overline{\,\cdot\,}
. Analogously, for irreducible right
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules
N
there exist bar involutions relative to a choice of highest weight vector.

2.3
Lusztig braid group operators

In this section, we recall the Lusztig braid group operators, cf.
[
Lus10
,
Ch. 5 & 37
]
. Let
e=\pm 1
, and let
M
be a
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight module. The
Lusztig braid group operators
are defined as
T^{\prime}_{i,e},T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}:M_{\mu}\to M_{\mu}
, where

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{i,e}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c;\,a-b+c=\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mu\rangle}(-1)^{b}q_{i}^{e(-ac+b)}F_{i}^{(a)}E_{i}^{(b)}F_{i}^{(c)}v

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c;\,a-b+c=\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mu\rangle}(-1)^{b}q_{i}^{e(-ac+b)}E_{i}^{(a)}F_{i}^{(b)}E_{i}^{(c)}v,

and the operators
T^{\prime}_{i,e}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}
satisfy the braid relations

\underbrace{T^{\prime}_{i,e}T^{\prime}_{j,e}T^{\prime}_{i,e}\dots}_{m_{ij}\text{ factors}}=\underbrace{T^{\prime}_{j,e}T^{\prime}_{i,e}T^{\prime}_{j,e}\dots}_{m_{ij}\text{ factors}}\quad\text{and}\quad\underbrace{T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}T^{\prime\prime}_{j,e}T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}\dots}_{m_{ij}\text{ factors}}=\underbrace{T^{\prime\prime}_{j,e}T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}T^{\prime\prime}_{j,e}\dots}_{m_{ij}\text{ factors}},

where
m_{ij}
denotes the order of
s_{i}s_{j}\in W
. If
w=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{n}}\in W
is a reduced expression, there exist well-defined operators
T^{\prime}_{w,e}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{w,e}
given by

T^{\prime}_{w,e}=T^{\prime}_{i_{1},e}\cdots T^{\prime}_{i_{n},e}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\prime\prime}_{w,e}=T^{\prime\prime}_{i_{1},e}\cdots T^{\prime\prime}_{i_{n},e}.

We also use the symbols
T^{\prime}_{i,e}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}
to denote algebra automorphisms on
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, cf.
[
Lus10
,
Ch. 37
]
. The Lusztig braid group operators on the modules and on
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
are related by

T^{\prime}_{i,e}(xv)=T^{\prime}_{i,e}(x)T^{\prime}_{i,e}(v)\,\text{and}\,T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}(xv)=T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}(x)T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}(v)\quad\text{for}\quad v\in M,x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

(2.1)

Hence, the algebra automorphisms
T^{\prime}_{i,e}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}
on
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
are given by conjugation by the invertible elements
T^{\prime}_{i,e}\in\mathscr{U}
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}\in\mathscr{U}
. Therefore, the Lusztig braid group operators on
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
also satisfy the braid relations. By
[
Lus10
,
Ch. 37.2
]
, the following relations hold.

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}=(T^{\prime}_{i,-e})^{-1}

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ T^{\prime}_{i,-e}

(2.2)

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}\circ\overline{\,\cdot\,}=\overline{\,\cdot\,}\circ T^{\prime\prime}_{i,-e}

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{i,e}\circ\overline{\,\cdot\,}=\overline{\,\cdot\,}\circ T^{\prime}_{i,-e}

\displaystyle T^{\prime\prime}_{i,e}\circ\omega=\omega\circ T^{\prime}_{i,e}.

2.4
The quantized function algebra and bilinear forms

In this subsection, we recall the definition and structure of the quantized function algebra.
The
quantized function algebra

R_{q}(G)
is the subspace of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{\ast}
spanned by matrix coefficients for finite dimensional
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight modules.
The quantized function algebra has the structure of a
Hopf-algebra
(R_{q}(G),\triangle,\epsilon,\iota,S)
which yields a Hopf-algebra duality between
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
R_{q}(G)
,
[
Jan96
,
Ch 7
]
. We continue by describing the structure of
R_{q}(G)
as a
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
bi-module. For each dominant weight
\lambda\in X^{+}
, the dual
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
is a right
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
weight module by setting

(fX)(v):=f(Xv)\qquad\text{where}\qquad X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\,f\in L(\lambda)^{\ast},\,v\in L(\lambda).

We define
c^{L(\lambda)}:L(\lambda)^{\ast}\otimes L(\lambda)\to R_{q}(G)
by

c^{L(\lambda)}_{f,v}(X):=f(X\cdot v).\qquad\text{where}\qquad f\in L(\lambda)^{\ast},v\in L(\lambda),\,X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

If the module is clear from the context, we write
c_{f,v}
. With these conventions, the quantized function algebra has the quantum Peter-Weyl decomposition

R_{q}(G)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in X^{+}}L(\lambda)^{\ast}\otimes L(\lambda)

as a
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-bi-module, cf.
[
Kas93
,
7.2
]
. The dual
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
has a bar involution defined by

\overline{f}(v):=\overline{f(\overline{v})},\qquad\text{where}\qquad f\in L(\lambda)^{\ast},v\in L(\lambda).

(2.3)

Next we describe an explicit identification of
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
via the Shapovalov form. For this, we fix notation regarding twisting modules with automorphisms.

Definition 2.2

(Twisting modules with automorphisms)
.

Let
\xi
be an automorphism of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\eta
be an anti-automorphism of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. Let
M
be a left module of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
N
be a right module of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

1.

We define the left module
{}^{\xi}M
by
{}^{\xi}M\cong M
as vector spaces and

X\cdot m:=\xi(X)\cdot m
for
X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),m\in{{}^{\xi}}M.

2.

We define the right module
{}^{\eta}M
by
{}^{\eta}M\cong M
as vector spaces and

m\cdot X:=\eta(X)\cdot m
for
X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),m\in{{}^{\eta}}M.

3.

We define the right module
{}^{\xi}N
by
{}^{\xi}N\cong N
as vector spaces and

n\cdot X:=n\cdot\xi(X)
for
X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),n\in{{}^{\xi}}N.

4.

We define the left module
{}^{\eta}N
by
{}^{\eta}N\cong N
as vector spaces and

X\cdot n:=n\cdot\eta(X)
for
X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),n\in{{}^{\eta}}N.
∎

We define
L^{r}(\lambda)
to be the simple right module
{}^{\varrho}L(\lambda)
.
In
[

,
7.1.3
]
Masaki Kashiwara notes that for each positive weight
\lambda\in X^{+}
, the irreducible weight module
L(\lambda)
has a unique non-degenerate symmetric bi-linear form, relative to a choice of highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}\in L(\lambda)
, with
(v_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})=1
and

(v,X\cdot w)=(\varrho(X)\cdot v,w)\qquad\text{where}\qquad v,w\in L(\lambda),\,X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

(2.4)

We refer to this bilinear form as the
Shapovalov form
. Via this identification we have
L(\lambda)^{\ast}\cong L^{r}(\lambda)
as right
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
modules.
There are two natural
bar involutions
on the quantized function algebra.
We define the
bar involution

\psi:R_{q}(G)\to R_{q}(G)

by

\psi(\varphi)(X):=\overline{\varphi(\overline{X})},\qquad\text{where}\qquad X\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}),\varphi\in R_{q}(G).

We define a second
bar involution

\bar{\,\,\cdot\,\,}^{R_{q}(G)}:R_{q}(G)\to R_{q}(G)
as follows: if
\varphi=c_{f,v}
with
f\in L^{r}(\lambda)
and
v\in L(\lambda)
, then set
\overline{c_{f,v}}^{R_{q}(G)}:=c_{\overline{f},\overline{v}}\in R_{q}(G)
and extend anti-linearly to
R_{q}(G)
.

3.1
Quantum symmetric pairs

In this section, we introduce quantum symmetric pairs in the sense of
[

]
.
This section mostly follows

By
\text{Aut}(C)
, we denote the set of permutations
\tau:\mathbf{I}\to\mathbf{I}
that satisfy
c_{ij}=c_{\tau(i)\tau(j)}
for all
i,j\in\mathbf{I}
. The set of permutations that satisfy
\tau(\mathbf{I}_{\bullet})=\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
is denoted
\text{Aut}(C,\mathbf{I}_{\bullet})
.
Let
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}\subset\mathbf{I}
be of finite type and
\tau\in\text{Aut}(C,\mathbf{I}_{\bullet})
. Then the pair
(\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},\tau)
is called an
admissible pair
if the following three conditions are satisfied:

1.

\tau^{2}=\text{id}_{I}
;

2.

the action of
\tau
on
\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
coincides with
-w_{\bullet};

3.

if
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}:=\mathbf{I}\setminus\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
satisfies
\tau(i)=i
then
\langle\rho_{\bullet}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z},
where
\rho^{\vee}_{\bullet}\in Y
is the half sum of the positive coroots relative to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bullet}
.

Given an admissible pair
(\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},\tau)
, we denote
\Theta:=-w_{\bullet}\circ\tau
, which naturally acts on
X
,
Y
and
\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{I}
. By
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}
, we denote the
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
subalgebra generated by
E_{i},F_{i}
and
K_{i}
with
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
.
Let us define

\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ns}}:=\{i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}\,:\,\tau(i)=i\text{ and }c_{ij}=0\text{ for all }j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}\}.

With this in place, we define parameters, which, as stated, are part of the datum for quantum symmetric pairs. A
parameter
is a tuple
(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s})
where

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{c}\in(\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\times})^{\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}\,:\,c_{i}=c_{\tau(i)}\text{ if }\tau(i)\neq i\text{ and }(\alpha_{i},\Theta(\alpha_{i}))=0\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{s}\in\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{s}\in(\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\times})^{\mathbf{I}_{\circ}}\,:\,s_{j}\neq 0\implies(j\in\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ns}}\text{ and }c_{ij}\in-2\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\forall i\in\mathbf{I}_{\mathrm{ns}}\setminus\{j\})\}.

1.

We call a parameter
(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s})

balanced
if
\boldsymbol{s}=0
and
c_{i}=c_{\tau(i)}
for each
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
.

2.

We call a parameter
(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s})

uniform
if

\displaystyle c_{i}=(-1)^{\langle 2\rho^{\vee}_{\bullet},\alpha_{i}\rangle}q^{-(\alpha_{i},w_{\bullet}\alpha_{\tau(i)}+2\rho_{\bullet})}\overline{c_{\tau(i)}}

\displaystyle\text{for each }i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}.

(3.1)

3.

We call a parameter
(\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{s})

admissible
if
\boldsymbol{c}
is balanced,
c_{i}^{-1}=\overline{c_{i}}
and

\displaystyle c_{i}=q^{-(\alpha_{i},w_{\bullet}\alpha_{\tau(i)}+2\rho_{\bullet})}\overline{c_{\tau(i)}}

\displaystyle\text{for each }i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}.

(3.2)

The
distinguished parameter

\boldsymbol{c}^{\diamondsuit}
is the unique balanced parameter defined by

\displaystyle c^{\diamondsuit}_{i}=-q^{-(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}-\Theta(\alpha_{i}))/2}\qquad\qquad\text{for }i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}.

(3.3)

Definition 3.1

(Quantum symmetric pair)
.

(\mathbf{I}_{\bullet},\tau)

4.1
Characters of

In this section we study the structure of characters that occur in restrictions of finite dimensional

\iota
-bar involution.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
. Recall that
v_{\lambda}
is a nonzero vector in
L(\lambda)_{\lambda}
and fix a nonzero bar invariant vector
v_{w_{0}\lambda}
in
L(\lambda)_{w_{0}\lambda}
. Remark that both vectors are unique up to a nonzero scalar multiple. Similarly we denote by
v_{\lambda}^{\ast}
a nonzero vector in
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
dual to
v_{\lambda}
and by
v_{w_{0}\lambda}^{\ast}\in L(\lambda)^{\ast}
a nonzero vector dual to
v_{w_{0}\lambda}
. The following proposition is based on
[
Let03
,
Lem 3.3
]
and
[
Kol14
,
Prop 8.1
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

1.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and suppose that
v\in L(\lambda)
spans a one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodule. Then, up to a nonzero scalar multiple,
v
equals
v_{\lambda}+w+av_{w_{0}\lambda}
for a nonzero scalar
a\in\mathbb{F}
and
w\in\bigoplus_{w_{0}\lambda<\mu<\lambda}L(\lambda)_{\mu}
.

2.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and suppose that
f\in L(\lambda)^{\ast}
spans a one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodule. Then, up to a nonzero scalar multiple,
f
equals

v_{\lambda}^{\ast}+w+av^{\ast}_{w_{0}\lambda}
for a nonzero scalar
a\in\mathbb{F}
and
w\in\bigoplus_{w_{0}\lambda<\mu<\lambda}L(\lambda)^{\ast}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let us write
v=\sum_{\mu}v_{\mu}
for weight vectors
v_{\mu}\in L(\lambda)_{\mu}
. We define the set
\text{Supp}(v):=\{\mu\in X\,:\,v_{\mu}\neq 0\}
. Let
\eta
be a maximal element of
\text{Supp}(v)
, i.e.
\eta+\alpha_{i}\notin\text{Supp}(v)
for simple roots
\alpha_{i}
. Denote by
\chi:\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}\to\mathbb{F}
the representation corresponding to the one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
module spanned by
v
.
For
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
, one has
E_{i}\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
and therefore

\displaystyle\chi(E_{i})v_{\eta}+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=\chi(E_{i})v

\displaystyle=E_{i}v

\displaystyle=E_{i}v_{\eta}+l.o.t.\,.

Because the weight of
E_{i}v_{\eta}
is greater than the weight of
v_{\eta}
, and because of the maximality of
\eta
, this leads to
E_{i}v_{\eta}=0
. Since the only one-dimensional weight module of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}
is
\epsilon
, we have
F_{j}v=0=E_{j}v
for each
j\in\mathbf{I}_{\bullet}
. As a result, one has
(T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1})^{-1}v=v
. Using this fact, we compute
for
i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}
that

\displaystyle\chi(B_{i})v_{\eta}+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=\chi(B_{i})v

\displaystyle=B_{i}v

\displaystyle=T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1}(E_{\tau(i)})K_{i}^{-1}v_{\eta}+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\eta\rangle}T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1}(E_{\tau(i)}(T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1})^{-1}v_{\eta})+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\eta\rangle}T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1}(E_{\tau(i)}v_{\eta})+l.o.t.\,.

Applying
(T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1})^{-1}
yields

\displaystyle(T^{\prime\prime}_{w_{\bullet},+1})^{-1}\chi(B_{i})v_{\eta}+l.o.t

\displaystyle=\chi(B_{i})v_{\eta}+l.o.t.

\displaystyle=q_{i}^{-\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\eta\rangle}E_{\tau(i)}v_{\eta}+l.o.t..

Again, because the weight of
E_{\tau(i)}v_{\eta}
is greater than the weight of
v_{\eta}
and because of maximality of
\eta
, this leads to
E_{\tau(i)}v_{\eta}=0
. We conclude that
E_{i}v_{\eta}=0
for each
i\in\mathbf{I}
, which means that
\eta
is equal to the highest weight
\lambda
. The remainder of the statements are proved analogously.
∎

If
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}}
, then we refer to the representation space of
\chi
as
V_{\chi}\cong\mathbb{F}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and
\chi\in\widehat{\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}}
be a character. Then the modules
L(\lambda)
and
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
each contain at most one
1
-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodule of type
\chi
.

Proof.

Suppose that
v,w\in L(\lambda)
span one-dimensional dimensional modules with the property that

bv=\chi(b)v\qquad\text{and}\qquad bw=\chi(b)w\qquad\text{for all }b\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}.

As a result of Proposition
4.1
, we may assume that
v=v_{\lambda}+l.o.t
and
w=v_{\lambda}+l.o.t.
. The difference
v-w
spans a one-dimensional module, yet the highest order term is not equal to a multiple of the highest weight vector. As a result of Proposition
4.1
, this forces
w-v=0
. A similar argument for
L(\lambda)^{\ast}
shows the desired conclusion.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and suppose that
V_{\chi}
is a one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodule in
L(\lambda)
. Then
\chi|_{\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}}=\epsilon
and
\chi(K_{h})=q^{\langle h,\lambda\rangle}
for each
K_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\Theta}^{0}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and suppose that
v\in L(\lambda)
spans the one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodule
V_{\chi}
. By Proposition
4.1
, we may assume that
v=v_{\lambda}+l.o.t.
. Let
K_{h}\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\Theta}^{0}
. Then we have

q^{\langle h,\lambda\rangle}v_{\lambda}+l.o.t=K_{h}v=\chi(K_{h})v,

which implies that
\chi(K_{h})=q^{\langle h,\lambda\rangle}
. The only one-dimensional weight module of the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}
is the trivial module. Hence, the action of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}
must be equal to the action of
\epsilon
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
be a dominant weight and suppose that
V_{\chi}
and
V_{\chi^{\prime}}
are one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
submodules in

. Then it holds that
V_{\chi}=V_{\chi^{\prime}}
if and only if

\chi(B_{i})=\chi^{\prime}(B_{i})\qquad\text{for all}\qquad i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, the multiplicity of a one-dimensional module is at most one. Using Lemma
4.3
we see that if
V_{\chi}
and
V_{\chi^{\prime}}
are one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}

\chi|_{\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\Theta}^{0}}=\chi^{\prime}|_{\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\bullet}\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})_{\Theta}^{0}}
. Henceforth, we have

\chi(B_{i})=\chi^{\prime}(B_{i})\quad\text{for all}\quad i\in\mathbf{I}_{\circ}\qquad\text{if and only if }\qquad V_{\chi}=V_{\chi^{\prime}}.\qed

This result will be one of our main tools for studying symmetries of characters. As a first consequence of Lemma
4.4
, we will show a relation between the bar involutions and characters. We first need a preliminary technical lemma that follows from
[
MS99
,
Prop 5.2
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}\subset\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))

We deduce that the eigenvalues of

(4.1)

Let

b\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}

By Lemma

Because

in the case that

for all

\boldsymbol{c}

Again, because

We apply Theorem

\boldsymbol{c}
be an uniform parameter, let

Proposition 4.10
.

Let

Proof.

Suppose that

\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}

\chi

The vectors

4.3
The existence of characters

In this section we study the existence of one-dimensional
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
modules, particularly those that arise as submodules of
\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
highest weight modules. Using a specialization argument we reduce this problem to the existence of one-dimensional
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{k})
modules that arise as submodules of
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g})
highest weight modules. The latter are characterized in terms of weights in
[
Sch84
,
Thm 7.2
]
.

To form the specialization argument, we recall the specialization of
\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}
and its modules. Let
\mathbf{A}
be the smallest subring of
\mathbb{F}
containing
q
such that every element in
\mathbf{A}
that is not contained in the ideal generated by
(q-1)
has a square root and is invertible, cf.
[
Let00
,
Sec 1
]
. Let
\wideparen{\mathbf{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the
\mathbf{A}
subalgebra generated by
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}
and
\frac{K_{i}-1}{q-1}
, with
i\in\mathbf{I}
.
Recall that
\wideparen{\mathbf{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})/(q-1)\wideparen{\mathbf{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, cf.
[
JL94
,
6.11
]
. For a dominant weight
\lambda\in X^{+}
the
\mathbf{A}

L(\lambda)^{1}:=L_{\mathbf{A}}(\lambda)/(q-1)L_{\mathbf{A}}(\lambda)

\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g})
module
L(\lambda)^{1}
is the highest weight module of weight
\lambda
. We write
\mathsf{cl}
for the quotient maps
\wideparen{\mathbf{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbf{U}(\mathfrak{g})

L^{\mathbf{A}}(\lambda)\to L(\lambda)^{1}
and
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbb{C}
. If
B\subset\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a subalgebra, we use
\wideparen{B}
to denote the intersection
B\cap\wideparen{\mathbf{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

\wideparen{B}\,\cdot\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{B}\subset\mathrm{span}_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{B}

A parameter

In the following lemma we use specialization results from
[

In this section we study zonal spherical functions, digressing from the overarching goal of studying spherical functions for general characters. We give a new proof of

Proposition 4.25
.

Let
\varphi
be a zonal spherical function for
(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}},\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
. Then
\varphi\circ S
is a zonal spherical function for
(S^{-2}(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}),\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
.

Proof.

Recall that for each
x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
we have
S^{-2}(x)=K_{2\rho}xK_{-2\rho}
, hence the algebra
S^{-2}(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})=\mathrm{ad}(K_{2\rho})(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
is a quantum symmetric pair coideal subalgebra. Also

\displaystyle\epsilon(x)=\epsilon(S(x))=S^{-1}(x_{(2)})x_{(1)}=x_{(1)}S(x_{(2)}).

Applying
S
yields
S(x)K_{-2\rho}=K_{-2\rho}S^{-1}(x).

For all
b,b^{\prime}\in\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
and
x\in\mathbf{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
it holds that

\displaystyle\epsilon(S^{-2}(b^{\prime}))\varphi(S(x))\epsilon(b)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(b)\varphi(S(x))\epsilon(S^{-1}(b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigg(b_{(1)}S(b_{(2)})S(x)K_{2\rho}S(b^{\prime}_{(2)})K_{-2\rho}b^{\prime}_{(1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigg(S(\epsilon(b_{(1)})b_{(2)})S(x)K_{2\rho}S(\epsilon(b^{\prime}_{(1)})b^{\prime}_{(2)})K_{-2\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigg(S(b)S(x)K_{2\rho}S(b^{\prime})K_{-2\rho}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigg(S(b)S(x)S^{-1}(b^{\prime})\bigg)

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigg(S(S^{-2}(b^{\prime})xb)\bigg).

This shows that
\varphi\circ S
is a zonal spherical function for
(S^{-2}(\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}}),\mathbf{B}_{\boldsymbol{c,s}})
. ∎

Rigid 2-step graph Lie algebras
Josefina Barrionuevo and Paulo Tirao

Abstract
. We characterize those graphs which correspond to a rigid 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra in the variety of at most 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 17B30; Secondary 17B99

In this work we assume that the field is
\mathbb{C}
and all Lie brackets are complex Lie brackets. However some results are valid for fields of caracteristic
0
or infinite fields.

Understanding which Lie algebras are rigid within different varieties of Lie algebras is a deep and difficult problem.
Already finding some rigid Lie algebras is quite challenging.
On the other hand showing that large families are not rigid sheds some light on the search for the rigid ones.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
be the variety of complex nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension
n
which are at most 2-step nilpotent,
that is either abelian or 2-step nilpotent.
On the one hand there are several cohomological characterizations of rigidity in
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
or 2-rigidity
[
A
,
BT
,
GR
]
.
On the other hand the family of
2-step graph Lie algebras

have attracted particular attention in different frameworks,
due in part to its strong combinatorial flavor.

The free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras correspond to the complete graph.
Recently we proved that all free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras are rigid in the variety
\mathcal{N}_{n,k}
, of complex nilpotent Lie algebras at most
k
-step nilpotent, and not longer rigid in
\mathcal{N}_{n,k+1}

[
BT
]
.
That is they are
k
-rigid but not
(k+1)
-rigid.

Notice that each isolated vertex of the underlying graph corresponds to an abelian 1-dimensional factor of the graph
Lie algbra. These graph algebras are, in general, not
2
-rigid
[
BT
]
.

In this short note we prove that besides the free 2-step nilpotent Lie
algebras there are only few more 2-rigid graph Lie algebras all of which correspond to graphs with at most 4 vertices and are
of dimension less than or equal to 8.

1.
2-rigidity

We say that a Lie bracket
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
is
2
-rigid if
\mu

is rigid in the variety
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
, that is its
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-orbit
is Zarisky open in
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
.

For the purpose of this paper, to prove rigidity we rely
on
[
BT
]
and its Appendix.
The results that we will use, stated for
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
, are the following:

Theorem A

(Sulca - Corollary 7.4 in
[
BT
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
. If
H^{2}_{2\textit{-nil}}(\mu,\mu)=0
, then
\mu
is
2
-rigid.

Theorem B

(Theorem 4.3 in
[
BT
]
)
.

The free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
m
generators is 2-rigid.

Theorem C

(Theorem 6.10 in
[
BT
]
)
.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{l}
is the
l
-dimensional abelian bracket, with
l\geq 1
, then

\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{l}\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
is 2-rigid if and only if
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
l=1
.

To prove non-rigidity within the class of 2-step graph Lie algebras,
Theorem D below suffices.
To prove it we use some preliminary result, that can be found in

[
A
]
.

Recall that if
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
, we can descompose
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}

with
\mathfrak{z}
the center of
\mu
and
\mathfrak{v}
any direct complement of it.

Lemma 1.1

(Alvarez)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
, with
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
. If
\mu
is
2
-rigid, then
\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{v}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{z}\subseteq B^{2}(\mu,\mu)
.

Proof.

If
\varphi\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{v}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{z}
, then

\mu_{t}=\mu+t\varphi
is a
2
-step analytic deformation and then is equivalent to the trivial deformation of
\mu

[
M
, Theorem 7.1]
.
[In characteristic zero, the analytic and geometric rigidity are
equivalent concepts.] This implies that

\varphi\in B^{2}(\mu,\mu)
.
∎

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
A
]
there is also the proof of the converse of the previous lemma (that is not necessary in this paper).

Theorem D
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
, with
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
. If there exist
v,w\in\mathfrak{v}
linearly independent such that,
[v,w]=0
and
\langle[v,\mathfrak{g}]\cup[w,\mathfrak{g}]\rangle\subsetneq\mathfrak{z}
, then
\mu
is not
2
-rigid.

Proof.

Choose
z\in\mathfrak{z}-\langle[v,\mathfrak{g}]\cup[w,\mathfrak{g}]\rangle
and consider

\varphi=v^{*}\wedge w^{*}\otimes z
.
On the one hand

\varphi\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{v}\otimes\mathfrak{z}
.
On the other hand
\varphi\notin B^{2}(\mu,\mu)
.
In fact, if
\varphi=\delta^{1}(f)
for a linear map
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
then

\displaystyle(v^{*}\wedge w^{*}\otimes z)(v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{1}f(v,w)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(v),w]+[v,f(w)]-f([v,w])

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(v),w]+[v,f(w)]

and hence
z\in\langle[v,\mathfrak{g}]\cup[w,\mathfrak{g}]\rangle
, wich is an absurd since we started this proof choosing
z\in\mathfrak{z}-\langle[v,\mathfrak{g}]\cup[w,\mathfrak{g}]\rangle
.

Therefore
\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{v}\otimes\mathfrak{z}\not\subseteq B^{2}(\mu,\mu)

and since the previous lemma
\mu
is not
2
-rigid.
∎

2.
Graph Lie algebras

Let
G=(V,A)
be a graph with vertices
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}

and edges
A\subseteq\{a_{ij}:\,1\leq i<j\leq m\}
.

The graph Lie algebra associated to
G
,
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
, is the

\mathbb{C}
-vector space generated by
V\cup A
and Lie bracket defined by

[v_{i},v_{j}]=\begin{cases}a_{ij},&\text{\ if\ }a_{ij}\in A,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for
1\leq i<j\leq m
.
If
A=\emptyset
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is the
m
-dimensional abelian Lie algebra, but in general if
A\neq\emptyset
,
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is a
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra.

Each connected component of
G
corresponds to an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}_{G}

and
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is the direct sum of these ideals.
Isolated vertices are 1-dimensional ideals and the sum
of all of them is a maximal abelian factor of
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
.

We are interested on their rigidity or non rigidity
inside
\mathcal{N}_{n,2}
.

3.
2-rigidity of graph Lie algebras

Before going to the general case, let us consider the cases

m\leq 4
.
The corresponding algebras are of small dimension, less than or equal to 10, and many have an abelian factor.
For each one of these there are more than one way to justify
its 2-rigidity or its non 2-rigidity.
To prove 2-rigidity we use the theorems A and B.
And for non 2-rigidy we use the theorems C and D.

Remark 3.1
.

To prove
2
-rigidity we distinguish two cases,
2
-step free (Theorem B) and others (Theorem A).
To prove non
2
-rigidity we will distinguish two cases, with abelian factors (Theorem C) or without (Theorem D). In the last case, the descomposition will be
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}=\langle V\rangle\oplus\langle A\rangle
.

It is worth mentioning that all degenerations that occur among all graph Lie algebras are presented in

We come now to the general case.

The Lie algebra associated to the complete graph of
m
vertices,

K_{m}
, is the free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
m
generators.
We know that it is 2-rigid and not 3-rigid
[
BT
]
.
Out main result is that besides this family,
the only 2-rigid ones have been already listed in the above tables.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
G
is a non-complete graph with
|V|>4
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is not
2
-rigid.

Proof.

Let
G
be a non-complete graph with
|V|>4
.
For each
v_{i}\in V
let
A_{i}
be the subset of
A
formed by all edges
a_{jk}
with
j=i
or
k=i
.

We assume, for the absurd, that
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is
2
-rigid.
We notice the following facts:

(1)

Since
g_{G}
is
2
-rigid, then
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
has no abelian factor (Theorem C). Then
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}=\langle V\rangle\oplus\langle A\rangle
and
\mathfrak{z}=[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}]
.

(2)

Since
G
is a non-complete graph, there exist vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, such that
a_{1,2}\notin A
. That is
[v_{1},v_{2}]=0
.

(3)

From the Theorem D, items (1) and (2) above and the fact that
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is
2
-rigid, it follows that
\langle[v_{1},\mathfrak{g}]\cup[v_{2},\mathfrak{g}]\rangle=\mathfrak{z}
. Therefore
A_{1}\cup A_{2}=A
.

Let
v_{3}
,
v_{4}
and
v_{5}
be three different vertices different from
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
(recall that
|V|>4
), we have that:

(4)

Since
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
, then
[v_{3},v_{4}]=0
.

(5)

Since
\emptyset\neq A_{5}\subseteq A_{1}\cup A_{2}
(
v_{5}
is not an abelian factor and item (3)), we can assume that
a_{15}\in A
.

Now, the hypotheses of Theorem D are satisfied by taking
v=v_{3}
,
w=v_{4}
and
z=a_{15}
and therefore
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is not
2
-rigid, wich is an absurd. Thus
\mathfrak{g}_{G}
is not
2
-rigid.
∎

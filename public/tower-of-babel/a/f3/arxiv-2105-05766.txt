Sensitivity of mixing times, an example
Itai Benjamini

Abstract
. We construct a family of growing finite bounded degree rooted graphs, G n G_{n} , in which the mixing time for simple random walk, starting at the root, is order log ⁡ | G n | \log|G_{n}| . Introduction

The question of instability of the mixing time was thoroughly studied in

. Sharp bounds were established on the ratio of the mixing times between two quasi-isometric graphs, for several notions of mixing times, total variation distance,
l^{2}

l^{\infty}

\log

In this note we construct a growing family of rooted bounded degree graphs, denoted

\log|G_{n}|
to
|G_{n}|\over{f(n)}

Such a gap can not be obtained in a vertex transitive graph. Let me advertise the remarkable, yet involved vertex transitive example in

Is this the largest possible ratio? that can be achieved?

We now recall the definition of the total variation mixing time and quasi - isometry.

For any two distributions
\mu
and
\nu
on a finite state space, e.g. the vertices of the graph, their total variation distance is defined as

1/2\sum_{x}|\mu(x)-\nu(x)|
. The total variation distance for a random walk starting at
x
at time
t
is defined
as
d^{x}(t)=||P^{t}_{x}-\pi||_{TV}
. Where by
P^{t}_{x}
we denote
the distribution of the simple random walk starting at
x
by time
t
and
\pi
is the stationary distribution.
The
\epsilon
- total variation mixing time for a random walk starting at
x
is defined as

t_{mix}(\epsilon)=\inf\{t:d^{x}(t)<\epsilon\}
.

A
quasi-isometry
between
two metric spaces
X
and
Y
is a map
\psi:X\rightarrow Y
such that for some numbers
(a,b)

we have
\forall u,v\in X

a^{-1}d(u,v)-b\leq d(\psi(u),\psi(v))\leq ad(u,v)+b
.

where
d
denotes the distance (in
X
or in
Y
, as appropriate); and further such that
for every
y\in Y
there is some
x\in X
such that
d(\psi(x),y)\leq a+b
. We say that

X
and
Y
are
(a,b)
quasi-isometric if such a
\psi
exists.

In our setup, we will have fixed
(a,b)
and a sequence of pairs
of bounded degree graphs, growing in size, in which the graphs of each pair are
(a,b)
quasi isometric.

A fact used by all the constructions cited above (

with probability that can be made arbitrarily close to one. Denote by

1.0001

Remark:
As we commented a key to all the examples is the fact that in the infinite binary tree, there is a set that absorbeds the simple random walk. Yet after a quasi - isometry simple random a.s. visits the set finitely many times. In

it was shown that such a set
exists on some amenable groups including the lamplighter over

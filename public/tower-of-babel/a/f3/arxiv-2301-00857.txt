1.
Introduction

Ever since J. von Neumann and D. Gabor claimed that the family of
time-frequency shifts (phase-space shifts)
\{e^{2\pi ilt}e^{-\pi(t-k)^{2}}:k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
spans
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the spanning properties of time-frequency shifts have
sparked the curiosity of mathematicians, physicists, and
engineers. Modern versions of this problem replace the Gaussian by
arbitrary generators and the time-frequency shift over the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
general lattices or even non-uniform sets.

To be specific, the time-frequency shift of
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is
defined as

(1)

M_{\xi}T_{x}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\xi t}g(t-x)\,.

Given lattice parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
the set

\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)=\{M_{\beta l}T_{\alpha k}g:k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is called Gabor family over the lattice
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
with window
g
. Gabor analysis studies the question under which
conditions on
\alpha,\beta
, and
g
there exist constants
A,B>0
, such that

(2)

A\|f\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f,M_{\beta l}T_{\alpha k}g\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\,.

If (
2
) holds,
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
is called a Gabor
frame. The set

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)\,\text{ is a frame }\}

is called the frame set of
g
. Knowing
\mathcal{F}(g)
amounts to a complete
characterization of all (rectangular) lattices
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
that generate a Gabor frame with a given window
g
.

Our understanding of the frame set is still very limited. The
prototypical result is due to Lyubarski
[
28
]
and
Seip-Wallsten
[
33
,
34
]
and determines the frame set
of the Gaussian to be
\mathcal{F}(e^{-\pi t^{2}})=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta<1\}
. This is usually formulated by saying
that
\mathcal{G}(e^{-\pi t^{2}},\alpha,\beta)
is a frame if and only if
\alpha\beta<1
. Subsequently, further examples of frame sets were found by
Janssen and Strohmer
[
22
,
23
,
25
]
. It was noted
in
[
19
]
that all examples known until 2010 were based on a totally positive window
g
. This observation led to a more systematic approach to
study the frame set for totally positive windows.

To explain the state-of-the-art we recall that
a measurable function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
is totally positive, if for every

n\in\mathbb{N}
and every two sets of
increasing numbers
x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{n}
and
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots<y_{n}
the matrix
\big(g(x_{j}-y_{k})\big)_{j,k=1,\dots,n}

has non-negative determinant:

(3)

\det(g(x_{j}-y_{k})\Big)_{j,k=1,\dots,n}\geq 0\,.

If in addition
g
is integrable, then
g
possesses
already exponential decay
[
31
, Lemma 2]
. Often an integrable totally positive function is called
a Polya frequency function, but we will not make this distinction
here.

By a deep theorem of Schoenberg
[
31
]
the Fourier transform of an
integrable totally positive function
g
possesses the factorization

(4)

\hat{g}(\xi)=ce^{-\gamma\xi^{2}}e^{2\pi i\nu\xi}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+2\pi i\nu_{j}\xi)^{-1}e^{-2\pi i\nu_{j}\xi}

with
c>0
,
\nu,\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\geq 0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and
0<\gamma+\sum_{j}\nu_{j}^{2}<\infty
.

Among the totally positive functions are the
Gaussian
e^{-\gamma t^{2}}
for
\gamma>0
and the one-sided exponential functions

\eta_{\gamma}(t)=e^{-\gamma t}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\gamma t)
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R},\gamma\neq 0
.

For the subclass of totally positive functions, for which the factorization (
4
)
is a
finite product, the results of
[
19
,
18
]
can be summarized as
follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a totally positive function with a finite factorization
(
4
) with
N\in\mathbb{N}
other than the one-sided exponentials
\eta_{\gamma}
, then

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta<1\}\,.

In other words, the lattice
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
generates a
frame with totally positive window
g
, if and only if
\alpha\beta<1
.

Remarkly the proof of Theorem
1.1
required different methods for
the cases
\gamma=0
(no Gaussian factor) and
\gamma>0
(with Gaussian
factor). In the former case total positivity and the
Schoenberg-Whitney conditions were used, in the latter case only the
factorization was used in conjunction with complex analysis.

The frame set of the one-sided exponential
\eta_{\gamma}
is
slightly different, since
\eta_{\gamma}
is not smooth enough and
the Balian-Low theorem does not apply
[
20
]
. In this case the
frame set is
\mathcal{F}(\eta_{\gamma})=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\}
, as was already known by Janssen
[
22
]
.

Theorem
1.1
suggests that the characterization of Gabor frames
holds for
all
totally positive generators. Our goal is to prove the
following theorem which is a step towards the full conjecture
formulated in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
g
is totally positive other than a one-sided exponential function

\eta_{\gamma}
. Further assume that
\alpha\beta
is
rational. Then
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,\beta)
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, if and only if
\alpha\beta<1
.

Theorem
1.2
implies that the frame set
\mathcal{F}(g)
is an
open,
dense
subset of
\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta<1\}
. This follows from a deformation
result of Feichtinger and Kaiblinger
[
11
]
.

Since every totally positive function is a limit of a totally positive with a finite
factorization, one would hope that some kind of limiting procedure
would work. However, so far all our “proofs” contained a
gap. Except for the example of the hyperbolic secant
(e^{at}+e^{-at})^{-1}

[
25
]
, Theorem
1.2
is the first general statement about
Gabor frames with a totally positive generator with an infinite factorization (
4
).

In
this paper we present a new approach that is independent of
Schoenberg’s factorization (
4
) and uses only the definition of
total positivity. The new proof idea comes from a deep theorem of
[
8
]
about
invertibility of infinite totally positive matrices. We also use methods from
spectral invariance of matrix algebras and the important fact that the Zak
transform of a totally positive function has only one zero. For
rational lattices this method leads also to a new proof of Theorem
1.1
.

To finish the discussion, we mention the superb work
[
3
]
,
which determines the frame set of
rational functions (Hurwitz functions)
g(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{a_{j}}{t-iw_{j}}
for
w_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. If
a_{j}>0
and
w_{j}>0
, then

\mathcal{F}(g)=\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\}
, for general coefficients the frame
set contains
\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}:\alpha\beta\leq 1\}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{c}
. Interestingly and in
contrast to our work, the authors show that all irrational lattices
belong to the frame set!

Other than this the complete frame set has been determined only for
the characteristic function of an interval
\chi_{[0,1]}

[
5
]
and the Haar function
[
6
]
. In contrast
to the results for totally positive windows and rational functions, the frame set
is extremely complicated and depends on subtle number theoretic
properties of the product
\alpha\beta
.

The case of irrational lattices remains open. There is some evidence
that generically a lattice
\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z}
with
irrational
\alpha\beta
generates a Gabor frame. Important results
pointing in this direction are contained in
[
3
]
and
in
[
6
]
. In each case an underlying dynamical system and
some ergodicity are at work. Currently we do not know how this works
for totally positive windows.

Of course, much more is known and relevant about Gabor frames. As we
are interested only in the complete characterization of Gabor frames
for a given window, we refer to the surveys
[
15
,
17
,
20
]
and
monographs
[
14
,
9
]
for the general theory of Gabor frames.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2 we review the tools
underlying Gabor analysis. These are Zak transform methods, Banach
algebra methods concerning spectral invariance, and general
characterizations of Gabor frames. All these characterizations amount to showing that
some (infinite) matrix is invertible. In our approach the matrix
G
under consideration
will a sub-matrix of the so-called pre-Gramian. This is a technical
novelty. In Section 3 we show that this matrix is onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
by using heavily the properties of spectral invariance and a
theorem from
[
8
]
. In
Section 4 we will show that
G
is also one-to-one. It is only here
that we will use the rationality of the lattice. Ultimately the proof
boils down to the fact that a square matrix is invertible, if and only if it is
onto.

2.
Tools

2.1.
Reduction.

Let
g_{\beta}(x)=\beta^{-1/2}g(x/\beta)
. Then
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\beta\mathbb{Z})
is a
frame, if and only if
\mathcal{G}(g_{\beta},\alpha\beta\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
is a
frame. It is immediate from (
3
) that the set of totally positive functions is invariant under
dilations. Thus,
if
g
is totally positive, then so is
g_{\beta}
. We may therefore always replace
g
by
g_{\beta}
,
\alpha
by

\alpha\beta
and
\beta
by
1
and thus assume without
generality that
\beta=1
.

We note that we may always assume that
\alpha\beta<1
and, after
the reduction, that
\alpha<1,\beta=1
because of the density theorem for Gabor
frames
[
20
]
.

2.2.
Spectral invariance.

Spectral invariance refers to the phenomenon that an invertible, infinite matrix with
sufficiently strong off-diagonal decay possesses an inverse with the same
off-diagonal decay. This topic has a long history with many contributions and
variations. See
[
1
,
2
,
36
,
37
,
35
,
38
]
for a sample of
contributions and the survey
[
15
]
. The following version can be attributed to
Shin-Sun
[
35
]
and Tessera
[
38
, Thm. 1.8]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Stability Theorem)
.

Let
G=(G_{kl})_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a bi-infinite matrix with off-diagonal decay

|G_{kl}|\leq C(1+|k-l|)^{-\sigma}\qquad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

for some
\sigma>1
.

(i) Spectral invariance: If
G
is invertible on some
\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
then
G
is invertible on all
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

(ii) Stability: If
G
is stable on some
\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{Z})
, i.e., if
G
satisfies

\|Gc\|_{p_{0}}\geq A\|c\|_{p_{0}}\qquad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{Z}),

then
G
is stable on all
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

We will need a slight variation of the stability (ii) for surjective
maps.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G=(G_{kl})_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
with off-diagonal decay

|G_{kl}|\leq C(1+|k-l|)^{-\sigma}\qquad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

for some
\sigma>1
. If
G
maps
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
onto
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
G
also maps
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

Note that the off-diagonal decay implies that
G
defines a
bounded operator on all
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),1\leq p\leq\infty
.

By the closed range theorem
[
30
, p. 102]
, the adjoint operator
(matrix) on the pre-dual
G^{*}:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
is one-to-one with closed range in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Consequently,
G^{*}
satisfies an inequality of the form

(5)

\|G^{*}c\|_{1}\geq A\|c\|_{1}\qquad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})

with a positive constant
A>0
.

The stability part of Proposition
2.1
then implies that (
5
)
holds for all
p
-norms, in particular, for some
A^{\prime}>0
we have

\|G^{*}c\|_{\infty}\geq A^{\prime}\|c\|_{\infty}\qquad c\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\,.

Since
G^{*}
is one-to-one with norm-closed range in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, we conclude that

G:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
must be onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

2.3.
Characterizations of Gabor frames.

We use the following characterization of Gabor frames that goes back
to Janssen
[
21
]
and the
duality theory of Gabor frames
[
29
]
. This is one of the
most successful criteria to verify that a Gabor system over a
rectangular lattice generates a frame and has been applied many
times. We refer to
[
17
]
for a survey of the known characterizations of Gabor
frames and some of their uses.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and satisfies the
condition
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g(x+k)|<\infty
. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

The family
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

For every
x\in[0,1)
, there
exist
A_{x},B_{x}>0

such that

(6)

A_{x}\|c\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}g(x+\alpha j-k)\Big|^{2}\leq B_{x}\|c\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\mbox{for all }c\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(iii)

There exist
A,B>0
such that, for all
x\in[0,1)
,

A\|c\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}g(x+\alpha j-k)\Big|^{2}\leq B\|c\|_{2}^{2}\qquad\mbox{for all }c\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

The matrix
P(x)=\big(g(x+\alpha j-k)\big)_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is often
called the pre-Gramian of the Gabor family.

Using spectral invariance, we deduce the following sufficient
condition for Gabor frames.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
g
be a function on
\mathbb{R}
with polynomial
decay
|g(t)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{-\sigma}
for some
\sigma>1
.
Assume that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
the set
x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
contains a subset
\{k+\delta_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
with the perturbation
\delta_{k}=\delta_{k}(x)

depending on
x
, such that the matrix
G
with entries
G_{kl}=g(k+\delta_{k}-l)
is invertible on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then

\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,1)
is a Gabor frame.

Proof.

The decay of
g
implies that
G
has sufficient off-diagonal decay
to apply Proposition
2.1
(i). Thus spectral invariance
asserts that the invertibility of
G
on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
implies that
G
is also invertible on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Since
x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}\supseteq\{k+\delta_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, we
have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}g(x+\alpha j-l)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}g(k+\delta_{k}-l)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|Gc\|_{2}^{2}\geq A_{x}\|c\|_{2}^{2}\,.

The upper bound in (
6
) always holds due to the
decay of
g
. This holds for all
x
, therefore the condition (ii) of
Theorem
2.3
implies that
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,1)
is a frame.

Proposition
2.4
says that a suitable submatrix of the
pre-Gramian
P(x)
is invertible. Proposition
2.4
is related to the characterizations via
sampling in shift-invariant spaces that figure prominently
in
[
18
]
. (In an equivalent formulation, every set

x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
contains a perturbation of
\mathbb{Z}
that is
interpolating in the shift-invariant space associated to
g
.)

To prove the main theorem (Theorem
1.2
), we will verify the conditions of
Proposition
2.4
.

2.4.
The Zak transform.

We recall the definition of the Zak transform
Zg(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}g(x-k)e^{2\pi ik\xi}
of a function
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. More generally, the Zak transform with period
p>0
is defined by

Z_{p}g(x,\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}g(x-pk)e^{2\pi ipk\xi}\,.

The Zak transform is
\tfrac{1}{p}
-periodic in the second variable
and quasi-periodic in the first variable, i.e., for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}

Z_{p}f\big(x+pk,\xi+\tfrac{l}{p}\big)=e^{2\pi ipk\xi}Z_{p}g(x,\xi)\,.

Zak transforms of totally positive function possess only few zeros. This important
property was discovered only recently
[
27
,
26
,
39
]
and is crucial for our
arguments.

Proposition 2.5
.

The Zak transform of a totally positive function
g
possesses a
unique zero in
[0,1)^{2}
that is assumed at
(x_{0},1/2)
for some
x_{0}\in[0,1)
.

3.
Surjectivity.

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
we verify the sufficient condition of
Proposition
2.4
for a totally positive function
g
. First we prove the
surjectivity. Notice that the arguments in this section hold for arbitrary values of

\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
g
be an arbitrary totally positive function in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and assume that
its Zak transform has its unique zero at
(x_{0},1/2)
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
M\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
(\delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq[x_{0}+M-1+\epsilon,x_{0}+M-\epsilon]
be a sequence of perturbations. Then the matrix
G

with entries

G_{kl}=g(k+\delta_{k}-l),k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, maps
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

onto

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})

onto

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

For the proof we use the following fundamental result of
de Boor, Friedland, and Pinkus
[
8
, Cor. 1]
.
Assume that
G
is an totally positive matrix that is bounded on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
1
1

1

The
original results are formulated for sign-regular matrices and more
general index sets.
. If the range of

G
contains a uniformly alternating vector, then
G
is onto
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
.
This result is the key tool in our proofs. Related results on the invertibility of infinite
totally positive matrices can be found in
[
4
,
7
,
10
]
.

Uniformly alternating means that we need to
find a sequence
c\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
u=Gc
satisfies

u(k)u(k+1)<0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(k)|>0
.

To accomplish this, we take
c_{l}=(-1)^{l}
and
compute
(Gc)_{k}
. This is

\displaystyle(Gc)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}g(k+\delta_{k}-l)(-1)^{l}

\displaystyle=Zg(k+\delta_{k},\tfrac{1}{2})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}Zg(\delta_{k},\tfrac{1}{2})\,,

where the last identity comes from the quasi-periodicity of
Zg
.
Since
Zg(x,\tfrac{1}{2})
is real-valued and by assumption
Zg(x,\tfrac{1}{2})
does not have any zeros on
the interval
[x_{0}-1+\epsilon,x_{0}-\epsilon]
, the numbers

Zg(\delta_{k},\tfrac{1}{2})
all have the same sign and
|Zg(\delta_{k},\tfrac{1}{2})|\geq\nu>0
for all
k
. Consequently
u=Gc
is
uniformly alternating.

To apply the result of
[
8
]
, we observe that the matrix
G
with
entries
g(k+\delta_{k}-l)
is indeed totally positive in the sense that all
minors of
G
of arbitrary dimension have a non-negative
determinant. But this follows from the definition (
3
) of total positivity.

Thus by
[
8
, Cor. 1]
the matrix
G
is onto

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. By Proposition
2.2

G
is also onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

To apply the above proposition, we need to select a suitable subset of

x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
. This can always be done with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha<1
,
\epsilon<(1-\alpha)/2
,
M\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x_{0}\in[0,1]

fixed, and
x\in[0,1]
arbitrary.

(i) Then
x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
contains a subset
\{k+\delta_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
with
\delta_{k}\in[x_{0}+M-1+\epsilon,x_{0}+M-\epsilon]
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii) If
\alpha
is rational,
\alpha=p/q
, then the perturbation

\delta_{k}
can be chosen to be
p
-periodic,
\delta_{k+np}=\delta_{k}
for all
k,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

(i) For arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the interval
[k+x_{0}-1+\epsilon,k+x_{0}-\epsilon]
has length
1-2\epsilon>\alpha
and thus contains at
least one point of
x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
, which we can write as
k+\delta_{k}
.

(ii) Let
\alpha=p/q\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
l=0,\dots,p-1
choose an
integer
j_{l}\in\{0,\dots,q-1\}
such that

x+\tfrac{p}{q}j_{l}\in[l+x_{0}-1+\epsilon,l+x_{0}-\epsilon]

and set

\delta_{l}=x+\tfrac{p}{q}j_{l}-l\,.

Clearly,
0\leq j_{0}<j_{1}<\dots<j_{p-1}<q
. Now let
m=l+np
with
l\in[0,p-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

x+\tfrac{p}{q}(j_{l}+nq)=x+\tfrac{p}{q}j_{l}+np\in[l+np+x_{0}-1+\epsilon,l+np+x_{0}-\epsilon]=[m+x_{0}-1+\epsilon,m+x_{0}-\epsilon]
,
and
\delta_{m}=x_{0}+\tfrac{p}{q}(j_{l}+nq)-m=x_{0}+\tfrac{p}{q}(j_{l}+nq)-(l+np)=\delta_{l}
. Thus
\delta_{k}
is

p
-periodic.

4.
Injectivity.

For the surjectivity of
G
we have
used the fact that
g
is totally positive, but nothing about the parameter

\alpha
. Now we impose in addition that
\alpha=p/q
is
rational. In this case the action of
G
is unitarily equivalent
to a
p\times p
-matrix-valued function. This facilitates the
analysis, as for square matrices surjectivity is equivalent to
injectivity and to invertibility.

Let again
G
be the matrix with entries
G_{kl}=g(k+\delta_{k}-l)
. Since
\alpha=p/q\in\mathbb{Q}
, the sequence of perturbations can
be chosen to be
p
-periodic by Lemma
3.2
. We now use the
periodicity of
\delta_{k}
to bring in a variant of
the Zibulski-Zeevi matrices
[
41
]
.
We write
k=r+mp
and
l=s+np
with
r,s=0,1,\dots,p-1
, and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\delta_{k}=\delta_{r}
and for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r=0,1,\dots,p-1
we have

\displaystyle(Gc)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}g(k+\delta_{k}-l)

(7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{s+np}g(r+mp+\delta_{r}-s-np)=d_{r+mp}\,.

For fixed
r,s
the sum over
n
is a convolution, so we may take
Fourier series and obtain a matrix-valued equation of functions.
Write
x_{s}(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{s+pn}e^{2\pi inp\xi}
for
the Fourier series of the coefficient in the residue class
s
and
x(\xi)=(x_{0}(\xi),\dots,x_{p-1}(\xi))
for the associated vector-valued
function. Likewise
y_{r}(\xi)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{r+pm}e^{2\pi imp\xi}
and
y(\xi)=(y_{0}(\xi),\dots,y_{p-1}(\xi))
. The
Fourier series of the sequence involving
g
is precisely the Zak
transform of
g
,
namely,

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}g(r+\delta_{r}-s-np)e^{2\pi inp\xi}=Z_{p}g(r+\delta_{r}-s,\xi)\,.

Consequently, after taking Fourier series, (
7
) can be
recast as

(8)

\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}x_{s}(\xi)Z_{p}g(r+\delta_{r}-s,\xi)=y_{r}(\xi)\qquad\text{ for }r=0,1,\dots,p-1\,.

Since
c\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and
G
is bounded on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
,
(
8
) holds pointwise for all
\xi\in[0,1/p]
.
Let
A(\xi)
be the
p\times p
-matrix-valued function with entries

A_{rs}(\xi)=Z_{p}g(r+\delta_{r}-s,\xi)\qquad r,s=0,1,\dots,p-1\,.

This
matrix is related to the Zeevi-Zibulski matrix that occurs in many
criteria for Gabor frames over rational lattices
[
17
,
41
,
40
]
.
The pointwise identity (
8
) can be written as

(9)

A(\xi)x(\xi)=y(\xi)\,.

We now translate the surjectivity of the infinite matrix
G
to a
property of the matrix-valued function
\det A(\xi)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\alpha=p/q
is rational,
|g(t)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{-\sigma}
for some
C,\sigma>1
, and that
G
maps

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})

onto

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
G
is one-to-one
on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and thus invertible on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

The surjectivity of
G
on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
implies in particular that
every vector
d=(d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
,
i.e,
d\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),d_{k}=0
for
k<0
and
k\geq p
is in the range of
G
. In this
case
y_{r}(\xi)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{r+pm}e^{2\pi imp\xi}=d_{r}
and the vector-valued function
y(\xi)=d
is constant. Due to the surjectivity
of
G
there exists
a sequence
c=c(d)\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
with associated Fourier series
x^{(d)}(\xi)
, such that
Gc=d
.
In view of (
9
)

A(\xi)x^{(d)}(\xi)=d\quad\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in[0,1/p]\,.

Since
d\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
was arbitrary, this means that for fixed
\xi

the
p\times p
-matrix is onto
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Consequently,
A(\xi)
is
one-to-one and thus invertible for every
\xi\in[0,1/p]
.

Now assume that
Gc=0
for some
c\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
A(\xi)x(\xi)=0
for all
\xi\in[0,1]
. Since
A(\xi)
is invertible,
this implies that
x(\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
must be the zero vector for all

\xi
. Consequently,
x\equiv 0
and thus
c=0
. We have proved that

G
is one-to-one on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, whence
G
is invertible on

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We finally summarize the steps that yield the proof of our main
theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
3.2
we can extract a set
\{k+\delta_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

from every
x+\alpha\mathbb{Z}
, such that
\delta_{k}
avoids the zero

x_{0}
of the Zak transform
Zg
. Then by Proposition
3.1
the matrix
G=\big(g(k+\delta_{k}-l)\big)_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
is onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

For rational values of
\alpha
the matrix
G
is also one-to-one on

\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
by Proposition
4.1
. Consequently,
G
is invertible on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Finally, the spectral invariance guaranteed by
Proposition
2.1
(i) implies that
G
is invertible on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. This is precisely the sufficient condition of
Proposition
2.4
to guarantee that
\mathcal{G}(g,\alpha,1)
is a
frame.

We are done!

Alternatively, one could give Proposition
4.1
a more structural
touch. We use

T_{p}
for the translation operator on sequences defined by
(T_{p}c)_{k}=c_{k-p}
and observe that the underlying matrix
G
of
Proposition
4.1
commutes with
T_{p}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G=(G_{kl})_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a bi-infinite matrix with off-diagonal decay

|G_{kl}|\leq C(1+|k-l|)^{-\sigma}\qquad k,l\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

for some
\sigma>1
.
Assume that
G
commutes with the translation operator
T_{p}
for
some
p\in\mathbb{N}
. If
G
is onto
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
G
is
one-to-one on
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and thus invertible.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

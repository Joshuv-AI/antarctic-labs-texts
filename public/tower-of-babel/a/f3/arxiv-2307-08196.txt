Long-time asymptotics of the Sawada-Kotera equation and Kaup-Kupershmidt equation on the line
Deng-Shan Wang, Xiaodong Zhu

Abstract
. Both Sawada-Kotera (SK) equation and Kaup-Kupershmidt (KK) equation are integrable systems with third-order Lax operator. Moreover, they are related with the same modified nonlinear equation (called modified SK-KK equation) by Miura transformations. This work first constructs the Riemann-Hilbert problem associated with the SK equation, KK equation and modified SK-KK equation by direct and inverse scattering transforms. Then the long-time asymptotics of these equations are studied based on Deift-Zhou steepest-descent method for Riemann-Hilbert problem. Finally, it is shown that the asymptotic solutions match very well with the results of direct numerical simulations.

Introduction

In 1974, Sawada and Kotera

proposed the so-called Sawada-Kotera (SK) equation

u_{t}+u_{xxxxx}+30\left(uu_{xxx}+u_{x}u_{xx}\right)+180u^{2}u_{x}=0,

which is also named the Caudrey-Dodd-Gibbon equation given by Caudrey, Dodd and Gibbon
[

3
]
and Kupershmidt

4

v_{t}+v_{xxxxx}+30(vv_{xxx}+\frac{5}{2}v_{x}v_{xx})+180v^{2}v_{x}=0.

(1.2)

Both SK equation (
1.1
) and KK equation (
1.2
) are completely integrable systems with third-order Lax operator of the form
\psi_{xxx}+6Q\psi_{x}+6R\psi=k^{3}\psi

and

The Lax pair of the SK equation (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi_{x}=L\Phi,\\
\Phi_{t}=Z\Phi,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
k^{3}&-6u&0\end{array}\right),\\

(1.4)

Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}36k^{3}u&6u_{xx}-36u^{2}&9k^{3}-18u_{x}\\
18k^{3}u_{x}+9k^{6}&6u_{xxx}-18k^{3}u+36uu_{x}&-12u_{xx}-36u^{2}\\
6k^{3}u_{xx}-36u^{2}k^{3}&Z_{32}&-6u_{xxx}-18k^{3}u-36uu_{x}\end{array}\right),

(1.5)

with spectral parameter

.

The Lax pair of the KK equation (
1.2
) in matrix form is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi_{x}=\tilde{L}\Phi,\\
\Phi_{t}=\tilde{Z}\Phi,\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

where

\tilde{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
k^{3}-3u_{x}&-6u&0\end{array}\right),

(1.7)

\tilde{Z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}3u_{xxx}+36k^{3}u+72uu_{x}&-3u_{xx}-36u^{2}&9k^{3}\\
\tilde{Z}_{21}&-18k^{3}u&-3u_{xx}-36u^{2}\\
\tilde{Z}_{31}&\tilde{Z}_{32}&-72uu_{x}-18k^{3}u-3u_{xxx}\end{array}\right),

(1.8)

with
\tilde{Z}_{21}=9k^{3}u_{x}+9k^{6}+3u_{xxxx}+72u_{x}^{2}+72uu_{xx}

Notice that the space parts of the operator Lax pairs for the SK and KK equations have the similar form, i.e.,
\mathscr{L}\phi=(\partial_{xxx}+6u\partial)\phi=k^{3}\phi
for the SK equation, and
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}\phi=(\partial_{xxx}+6u\partial_{x}+3u_{x})\phi=k^{3}\phi

\mathscr{L}\phi=(\partial_{xxx}+p\partial_{x}+q)

which corresponds to the matrix form Lax pair

\Phi_{x}=L\Phi

(1.10)

with

0&0&1\\

It is obvious that

for the SK equation (
1.1

1.2

In addition, both the SK equation (

(1.11)

u=\frac{1}{6}(w_{x}-w^{2})\quad{\rm and}\quad v=\frac{1}{3}(w_{x}-\frac{w^{2}}{2}).

(1.12)

It is noted that there doesn’t exist singularity in the spectral problem of the mSK-KK equation (
1.11
), thus it is practicable to study the long-time asymptotics of the equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2

1.11

An approach to normal polynomials through symmetrization and symmetric reduction * Thanks: * This work was supported by NSF REU Grant 2244488
Darien Connolly, Calvin George, Xiang-dong Hou, Adam Madro, Vincenzo Pallozzi Lavorante

Abstract
. An irreducible polynomial f ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] of degree n n is normal over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} if and only if its roots r , r q , … , r q n − 1 r,r^{q},\dots,r^{q^{n-1}} satisfy the condition Δ n ​ ( r , r q , … , r q n − 1 ) ≠ 0 \Delta_{n}(r,r^{q},\dots,r^{q^{n-1}})\neq 0 , where Δ n ​ ( X 0 , … , X n − 1 ) \Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}) is the n × n n\times n circulant determinant. By finding a suitable symmetrization of Δ n \Delta_{n} (A multiple of Δ n \Delta_{n} which is symmetric in X 0 , … , X n − 1 X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1} ), we obtain a condition on the coefficients of f f that is sufficient for f f to be normal. This approach works well for n ≤ 5 n\leq 5 but encounters computational difficulties when n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 . In the present paper, we consider irreducible polynomials of the form f = X n + X n − 1 + a ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f=X^{n}+X^{n-1}+a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] . For n = 6 n=6 and 7 7 , by an indirect method, we are able to find simple conditions on a a that are sufficient for f f to be normal. In a more general context, we also explore the normal polynomials of a finite Galois extension through the irreducible characters of the Galois group.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a finite Galois extension over
F
with Galois group
G=\text{Aut}(K/F)
. An element
\alpha\in K
is said to be
normal
over
F
(with respect to
K
) if
\{\sigma(\alpha):\sigma\in G\}
forms a basis of
K
over
F
; in this case,
\{\sigma(\alpha):\sigma\in G\}
is called a
normal basis
of
K
over
F
. Normal bases of finite Galois extension always exist
[
12
, §VI.13]
. The notion of normal bases appears in the context of Galois modules and leads to a subtle refinement in algebraic number theory
[
4
,
21
]
. If
\alpha\in K
is normal over
F
(with respect to
K
) and
f
is its minimal polynomial over
F
, then
\alpha
is separable over
F
,
F(\alpha)\subset K
, and
[F(\alpha):F]=\deg f=|G|=[K:F]
. It follows that
K=F(\alpha)
. Therefore, one may speak of a separable element
\alpha
over
F
being normal over
F
without mentioning
K
. The minimal polynomial of a normal element over
F
is called a
normal polynomial
over
F
. Therefore, a normal polynomial over
F
is a monic separable irreducible polynomial whose splitting field over
F
is generated by one its roots and whose roots are linearly independent over
F
. When
F
is a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a normal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is simply a monic irreducible polynomial whose roots are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Normal bases and normal polynomials over finite fields have applications in many areas and they constitute a well-studied topic in the theory of finite fields
[
5
,
8
,
13
,
16
]
,
[
17
, §§5.2 – 5.4]
,
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

The number of normal polynomials of a given degree over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is known
[
14
, Theorem 3.73]
. For an irreducible polynomial
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
with roots
r_{0}=r,r_{1}=r^{q},\dots,r_{n-1}=r^{q^{n-1}}
,
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if

\Delta_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})\neq 0,

where

\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\det\left[\begin{matrix}X_{0}&X_{1}&\cdots&X_{n-1}\cr X_{n-1}&X_{0}&\cdots&X_{n-2}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr X_{1}&X_{2}&\cdots&X_{0}\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0}\dots,X_{n-1}].

However, it is more desirable to tell whether
f
is normal from its coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
. This question was considered in
[
9
]
with a naive approach which we briefly describe below.

The approach in
[
9
]
is based on the notions of symmetrization and symmetric reduction. Given a polynomial
f(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
, a
symmetrization
of
f
is a multiple of
f
in
\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
which is symmetric in
X_{0},\dots X_{n-1}
. Given a symmetric polynomial
F(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
, its
symmetric reduction
is the polynomial
H\in\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\dots,s_{n}]
such that

F(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=H(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}),

where
s_{i}
is the
i
th elementary symmetric polynomial in
X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}
. (Note: The coefficient ring
\mathbb{Z}
here can be replaced with any commutative ring.)

Let
\epsilon_{n}=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n}
. The polynomial
\Delta_{n}
affords a factorization

\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\prod_{m\mid n}\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1}),

where

(1.1)

\Psi_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr)\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]

and

Y_{ni}=\sum_{j=0}^{n/m-1}X_{i+mj},\quad 0\leq i\leq m-1.

Let the symmetric group
S_{n}
act on
\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
by permuting the indeterminates
X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}
. The stabilizer of
\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
in
S_{n}
, denoted by
\text{Stab}(\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1}))
, is determined. Consequently,

(1.2)

\Theta_{n,m}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}):=\prod_{\phi}\phi(\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1})),

where
\phi
runs over a left transversal of
\text{Stab}(\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1}))
in
S_{n}
(i.e., a system of representatives of the left cosets of
\text{Stab}(\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1}))
in
S_{n}
), is a symmetrization of
\Psi_{m}(Y_{n0},\dots,Y_{n,m-1})
. The explicit formula for
\Theta_{n,m}
is given in
[
9
]
. Let
\theta_{n,m}\in\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\dots,s_{n}]
be the symmetric reduction of
\Theta_{n,m}
, that is,

(1.3)

\Theta_{n,m}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\theta_{n,m}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}).

If
p
is prime, then
\theta_{n,p^{t}l}
is a power of
\theta_{n,l}
in characteristic
p
(
[
9
, Remark 4.2]
). The main results of
[
9
]
can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be irreducible, where
p=\text{\rm char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\theta_{n,m}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
for all
m\mid n
with
p\nmid m
, then
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

To recap,
\Theta_{n,m}
are the symmetrizations of the factors of
\Delta_{n}
;
\theta_{n,m}
is the symmetric reduction of
\Theta_{n,m}
. While
\Theta_{n,m}
is explicitly given,
\theta_{n,m}
is difficult to compute. The polynomials
\theta_{n,m}
give rise to the sufficient condition that we seek for the normality of polynomials.

The usefulness of Theorem
1.1
depends whether the polynomials
\theta_{n,m}
(or their images in characteristic
p
) can be computed. In
[
9
]
,
\theta_{n,m}
were computed for all
m\mid n
with
n\leq 6
except for
\theta_{6,6}
. Hence Theorem
1.1
can be used to detect normality of irreducible polynomials of degree
\leq 5
and in cases
p=2
or
3
, also of degree 6. However, for larger
n
and
m
, the computation of the polynomial
\theta_{n,m}
, such as
\theta_{6,6}
and
\theta_{7,7}
, becomes impractical as we will see in the next section.

If
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
a_{1}\neq 0
. Since
f(a_{1}X)=a_{1}^{n}(X^{n}+X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}^{\prime})
, we may assume without loss of generality that
a_{1}=1
. In the present paper, we consider irreducible polynomials of the form

(1.4)

f=X^{n}+X^{n-1}+a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]

and we search for sufficient conditions on
a
such that
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. From Theorem
1.1
, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
f=X^{n}+X^{n-1}+a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is irreducible, where
p=\text{\rm char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
\theta_{n,m}(1,0,\dots,0,a)\neq 0
for all
m\mid n
with
m>1
and
p\nmid m
, then
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Note.
In Theorem
1.2
, there is no need to consider
m=1
since in general,
\theta_{n,1}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})=s_{1}
, and hence
\theta_{n,1}(1,0,\dots,0,a)=1\neq 0
.

In Theorem
1.2
,
\theta_{n,m}(1,0,\dots,0,a)
is a polynomial in
a
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The usefulness of Theorem
1.2
of course depends on our ability to compute
\theta_{n,m}(1,0,\dots,0,a)
. Fortunately, there is an indirect method that allows us to compute
\theta_{n,m}(1,0,\dots,0,a)
without having to compute
\theta_{n,m}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
in general. We will describe this method in the next section.
\theta_{6,6}(1,0,\dots,0,a)
and
\theta_{7,7}(1,0,\dots,0,a)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[a]
for various primes
p
are computed in Sections 3 and 4, respectively. These results give rise to practical criteria for an irreducible polynomial
f=X^{n}+X^{n-1}+a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
(
n=6,7
) to be normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; see Theorems
3.1
and
4.1
.

In Section 5, we discuss normal polynomials of an arbitrary finite Galois extension with Galois group
G
. Basic facts about normal polynomials over finite fields extend naturally to the general situation. The determinant
\Delta_{n}
played a central role for normal polynomials over finite fields. In the general case,
\Delta_{n}
is replaced by the
group determinant

\mathcal{D}_{G}
of
G
. (In fact,
\Delta_{n}
is the group determinant of the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
.) The factors of
\mathcal{D}_{G}
are given by the irreducible characters of
G
. When
G
is small, the symmetrizations of the factors of
\mathcal{D}_{G}
can be determined, and in some cases, the symmetric reductions of these symmetrizations can be computed. In such cases, we have criteria for the normality of polynomials in terms of their coefficients similar to the case of finite fields.

Let
p
be a prime and
\Theta(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
be a homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree
d
. We have

\displaystyle\Theta(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})\,

\displaystyle=\theta(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq\lfloor d/n\rfloor}b_{j}s_{1}^{d-jn}s_{n}^{j}+\text{terms involving $s_{2},\dots,s_{n-1}$},

where
\theta
is the symmetric reduction of
\Theta
,
s_{i}
is the
i
th elementary symmetric polynomial in
X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}
, and
b_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then

(2.11)

\theta(1,0,\dots,0,s_{n})=\sum_{j\leq\lfloor d/n\rfloor}b_{j}s_{n}^{j}

and hence the question is to find
b_{0},\dots,b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Given a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
of relatively small degree, one can determine its roots explicitly in its splitting field. This is a unique feature not possessed by other fields. Our method exploits this distinctive advantage of finite fields. Take a “sample” polynomial
f=X^{n}-X^{n-1}+(-1)^{n}u\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}[X]
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}(u)=\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, and let
r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1}
be the roots of
f
(in some extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
). Then

(2.12)

\Theta(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})=\sum_{j\leq\lfloor d/n\rfloor}b_{j}u^{j}.

Since
r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1}
are explicitly determined, we can compute
\Theta(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})
. Since
\Theta(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})
is symmetric,
\Theta(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(u)
. Hence

(2.13)

\Theta(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})=[u^{0},\dots,u^{k-1}]C(u)

for some
C(u)\in\text{M}_{k\times 1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. On the other hand, we can write

(2.14)

[u^{0},\dots,u^{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}]=[u^{0},\dots,u^{k-1}]A(u),

where
A(u)\in\text{M}_{k\times(\lfloor d/n\rfloor+1)}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. By (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), (
2.12
) becomes

[u^{0},\dots,u^{k-1}]C(u)=[u^{0},\dots,u^{k-1}]A(u)\left[\begin{matrix}b_{0}\cr\vdots\cr b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}\end{matrix}\right],

i.e.,

(2.15)

A(u)\left[\begin{matrix}b_{0}\cr\vdots\cr b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}\end{matrix}\right]=C(u).

Each sample polynomial
f=X^{n}-X^{n-1}+(-1)^{n}u
gives rise to a linear system (
2.15
) for
b_{0},\dots,b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}
. Enough number of such linear systems will allow us to solve for
b_{0},\dots,b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}
. More precisely, we have the following algorithm.

Algorithm 2.1
.

(An algorithm for computing
b_{0},\dots,b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}
in (

1.

Choose

(the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
) with
[\mathbb{F}_{p}(u_{i}):\mathbb{F}_{p}]=k_{i}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
over

, denoted by

, are all distinct.

(ii)

.

(iii)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}\geq\lfloor d/n\rfloor+1
.

To satisfy (ii), use the following fact: If
\prod_{j=0}^{k_{i}-1}(X^{n}-X^{n-1}+(-1)^{n}u_{i}^{p^{j}})
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then
X^{n}-X^{n-1}+(-1)^{n}u_{i}
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{i}}}
.

2.

For each

, compute the matrix

in (

) by reducing

(

) mod

. Since

is irreducible over

, its roots are

modulo

and

(

). Compute the matrix

in (

) by reducing

mod

and

\left[\begin{matrix}A(u_{1})\cr\vdots\cr A(u_{m})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}b_{0}\cr\vdots\cr b_{\lfloor d/n\rfloor}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}C(u_{1})\cr\vdots\cr C(u_{m})\end{matrix}\right]

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N,m>0
be integers and
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
(the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
) be such that their minimal polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are all distinct. Let
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(u_{i}):\mathbb{F}_{q}]=k_{i}
and write

[u_{i}^{0},u_{i}^{1},\dots,u_{i}^{N-1}]=[u_{i}^{0},\dots,u_{i}^{k_{i}-1}]A(u_{i}),

where
A(u_{i})\in\text{\rm M}_{k_{i}\times N}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then

\text{\rm rank}\left[\begin{matrix}A(u_{1})\cr\vdots\cr A(u_{m})\end{matrix}\right]=\min\{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m},N\}.

Proof.

Let
g_{i}
be the minimal polynomial of
u_{i}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
a=(a_{0},\dots,a_{N-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
. We have

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}A(u_{1})\cr\vdots\cr A(u_{m})\end{matrix}\right]a^{T}=0\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \left[\begin{matrix}u_{1}^{0}&\cdots&u_{1}^{N-1}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr u_{m}^{0}&\cdots&u_{m}^{N-1}\end{matrix}\right]a^{T}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ g_{i}\,\Bigm|\,\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{j}x^{j}\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq m

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}\,\Bigm|\,\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{j}x^{j}.

Since
\deg\prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Bigl\{(a_{0},\dots,a_{N-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}:\prod_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}\,\Bigm|\,\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_{j}x^{j}\Bigr\}=\max\Bigl\{N-\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i},\,0\Bigr\}.

Hence

\text{rank}\left[\begin{matrix}A(u_{1})\cr\vdots\cr A(u_{m})\end{matrix}\right]=N-\max\Bigl\{N-\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i},\,0\Bigr\}=\min\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i},\,N\Bigr\}.

∎

Note.
In the above lemma, if
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
have the same minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
A(u_{1})
and
A(u_{2})
are row equivalent. The reason is that for
a=(a_{0},\dots,a_{N-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}

A(u_{1})a^{T}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ [u_{1}^{0},\dots,u_{1}^{N-1}]a^{T}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ [u_{2}^{0},\dots,u_{2}^{N-1}]a^{T}=0\ \Leftrightarrow\ A(u_{2})a^{T}=0.

Remark.
More generally, the method described in this section can be used to compute the symmetric reduction

2.11

5.
Normal Polynomials of a Finite Galois Extension

Let
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\}
be a finite group. To each
g\in G
assign an indeterminate
X_{g}
. The
group determinant
of
G
is

\mathcal{D}_{G}(X_{g_{1}},\dots,X_{g_{n}})=\det(X_{g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{g_{1}},\dots,X_{g_{n}}],

where
(X_{g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}})
is the
n\times n
matrix whose
(i,j)
entry is
X_{g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}}
. Note that when
G=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}).

Let
F
be any field and
f
be a monic, separable irreducible polynomial over
F
such that
F(\alpha)=S_{F}(f)
, where
\alpha
is any root of
f
and
S_{F}(f)
demotes the splitting field of
f
over
F
. Let
G=\text{Aut}(S_{F}(f)/F)=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\}
be the Galois group of
f
over
F
. It is well known that
f
is normal over
F
if and only if

(5.1)

\mathcal{D}_{G}(g_{1}(\alpha)\,\dots,g_{n}(\alpha))\neq 0.

(See
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
.)

Let
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}
be the irreducible representations of
G
with
\deg\rho_{i}=m_{i}
. It is a fundamental fact from the representation theory that

(5.2)

On the time-domain full waveform inversion for time-dissipative and dispersive poroelastic media
Miao-jung Yvonne Ou, Petr Plecháč, Jiangming Xie

Abstract
. This paper concerns the Time-Domain Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) for dispersive and dissipative poroelastic materials. The forward problem is an initial boundary value problem (IBVP) of the poroelastic equations with a memory term; the FWI is formulated as a minimization problem of a least-square misfit function with the (IBVP) as the constraint. In this paper, we derive the adjoint problem of this minimization problem, whose solution can be applied to computed the direction of steepest descent in the iterative process for minimization. The adjoint problem has a similar numerical structure as the forward problem and hence can be solved by the same numerical solver. Because the tracking of the energy evolution plays an important role in the FWI for dissipative and dispersive equations, the energy analysis of the forward system is also carried out in this paper.

1.
Introduction

For the application of poroelastic wave equations in biomedical and geological research, a time-domain simulation is more feasible because the input wave signal is usually a pulse of finite band-width, instead of a monochromatiic signal. The dispersive nature of a poroelastic material is encoded in the memory term of the poroelastic equations. The presence of the memory term poses a significant challenge in developing a time domain numerical solver for the poroelastic equations. This challenge can be handled by the method of auxiliary variables as long as a high precision approximation of the memory kernel function can be obtained. Initiated in
[
14
]
and further developed In
[
15
]
, the link between the Laplace transform of the memory kernel and the Herglotz-Nevanlinna functions was utilized to obtain a highly accurate pole-residue approximation, which makes it possible for replacing the memory terms with sums of auxiliary variables that satisfy ordinary differential equations. The new system is termed
augmented Biot equations
because it contains the new auxiliary variables and the corresponding governing equations. By design, the augmented system has no explicit memory terms and can be solved much more efficiently than the original system.

Incorporating the dissipative and the dispersive mechanism of the fluid saturated poroelastic media in the forward wave solver is important in recovering poroelastic material properties from the probing waves (the inverse problem). The full waveform inversion (FWI) has become a powerful tool in the geological exploration since the 1980s
[
2
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
24
,
10
,
9
]
. FWI has been successfully applied in seismic exploration with (visco)-elastic models
[
22
]
in recovering certain material properties. However, certain important poroelastic properties such as porosity and relative velocity between fluid and solid cannot be recovered from these models. For example, it is reported in
[
7
]
that the measured parameters of water-saturated sand in the laboratory is better predictions by a poroelastic Biot model than a a viscoelastic model. In
[
13

[

At the macroscopic level, the viscodynamics of a poroelastic material is described by the dissipative and dispersive coefficients in the poroelastic wave equation with memory terms
[

.

FWI has been applied to the one-dimensional Biot-JKD equations to recover several prorelastic parameters from numerical simulated data in a
water tank setting

The time-domain augmented Biot solver developed in

2.
The mathematical model for wave propagation in poroelastic materials

Introduce the solid velocity
\mathbf{v}
and the relative fluid velocity
\mathbf{q}
, which are defined by

(2.1)

\mathbf{v}:=\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t},\ \mathbf{q}:=\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t},

where
\mathbf{u}
is the skeleton solid displacement vector and
\mathbf{w}=\phi(\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{u})
is the relative motion of the fluid scaled by the porosity
\phi
with
\mathbf{U}
being the fluid displacement vector. In terms of these notations, the variation in fluid content is given by
\zeta=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}
.

2.1.
The constitutive relation for the plane strain of a transversely isotropic material

Let the
z

(2.2)

\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}=\mathbf{C}\mbox{\boldmath{$\epsilon$}},

where the elastic stiffness tensor
\mathbf{C}
, the stress tensor

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{c_{11}}&{c_{13}}&{0}\\
{c_{13}}&{c_{33}}&{0}\\
{0}&{0}&{c_{55}}\end{array}\right),\quad\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}=(\tau_{11}\ \tau_{33}\ \tau_{13})^{T},\quad\mbox{\boldmath{$\epsilon$}}=(\epsilon_{11}\ \epsilon_{33}\ 2\epsilon_{13}\ )^{T},

with
\epsilon_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{i}u_{j}+\partial_{j}u_{i}\right)
, j=1,3. The constitutive relation for the poroelastic material is

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bm{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\bm{\epsilon}-\bm{\beta}p=\mathbf{C}^{u}\bm{\epsilon}-M\bm{\beta}\zeta,

(2.8)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\left(\zeta-\bm{\beta}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}\right),

where the

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}^{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}+M\bm{\beta}\bm{\beta}^{T},~~\bm{\beta}:=(\beta_{1},~\beta_{3},~0)^{T},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{c_{11}+c_{12}+c_{13}}{3K_{s}},~\beta_{3}:=1-\frac{2c_{13}+c_{33}}{3K_{s}},

\displaystyle M:

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K^{2}_{s}}{K_{s}\left[1+\phi(K_{s}/K_{f}-1)\right]-\left(2c_{11}+c_{33}+2c_{12}+4c_{13}\right)/9},

with
K_{s}
and

2.2.
Equation of motions

The equations of motion for the solid part are given by the conservation of momentum as

(2.9)

\rho\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}}{\partial t}+\rho_{f}\frac{\partial\mathbf{{q}}}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\bm{\overline{\tau}},

where
\rho_{s}
is the density of constituent solid,
\rho=(1-\phi)\rho_{s}+\phi\rho_{f}
is the bulk density of the medium and
\bm{\overline{\tau}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{\tau_{11}}&{\tau_{13}}\\
{\tau_{13}}&{\tau_{33}}\end{array}\right]
with the action
\nabla\cdot\bm{\overline{\tau}}:=\left(\partial_{x}\tau_{11}+\partial_{z}\tau_{13},\ \partial_{x}\tau_{13}+\partial_{z}\tau_{33}\right)^{T}
is the plane stress tensor.

The equations of motion for the fluid part is described by the generalized Darcy’s law

(2.10)

\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}}{\partial t}+{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\right)\check{\bm{\alpha}}\star\frac{\partial\mathbf{q}}{\partial t}=-\nabla p,

where
\star
denotes the time-convolution operator and
\check{\bm{\alpha}}=(\check{\alpha}_{1},\check{\alpha}_{3})^{T}
is the inverse Fourier-Laplace transform of the dynamic tortuosity
\bm{\alpha}(s)
with
s=-i\omega
. The Fourier-Laplace transform of a function

is defined as

(2.11)

\hat{f}(\omega):={\mathcal{L}}[f](s=-i\omega):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-st}dt.

For the well-known low-frequency Biot model
[
4
]
, the generalized Darcy’s law is

-\partial_{x_{i}}p=\rho_{f}\partial_{t}v_{i}+\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\right){\alpha_{\infty}}_{i}\partial_{t}q_{i}+\left(\frac{\eta}{K_{0i}}\right)q_{i}

, which has no memory term and corresponds to

\check{\alpha}_{j}(t)=\alpha_{\infty j}\delta(t)+\frac{\eta\phi}{K_{0j}\rho_{f}}H(t)\Longleftrightarrow\alpha_{j}(\omega)={\alpha_{\infty}}_{j}+\frac{\eta\phi/(K_{0j}\rho_{f})}{-i\omega}.

Historically, to address the discrepancy between the lab measurement and the prediction by the low-frequency Biot model, many forms of
\alpha(\omega)
have been proposed
[
5
]

[
12
]

[
1
]

[
17
]
. Among them, the JKD model by Johnson, Koplik and Dashen
[
12
]
has been widely used in applications. The most general one is presented in
[
1
]
, which does not rely on any specific form of the
permeability/tortuosity function. In
[
14
]
, an integral representation formula for
\alpha(\omega)
was derived based on the result in
[
1
]
and the Stieltjes function theory.

2.3.
The Biot-JKD equations

The JKD model

]
is

(2.12)

\alpha_{j}(s)=T_{j}(s):=\alpha_{\infty j}+\frac{\eta\phi}{s\kappa_{j}\rho_{f}}\left(1+s\frac{4\alpha^{2}_{\infty j}\kappa^{2}_{j}\rho_{f}}{\eta\Lambda_{j}^{2}\phi^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad s:=-i\omega

where

is the viscous characteristic length and
\text{P}_{j}=0.5
the Pride numberin the
j

2.12
) reads

(2.13)

\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}}{\partial t}+{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\infty j}\rho_{f}}{\phi}\right)\frac{\partial{\mathbf{q}}}{\partial t}+\nabla p=-{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}\right)\left(\partial_{t}+\lambda_{j}\right)^{1/2}\mathbf{q},~j=1,3,

where
\lambda_{j}=\frac{\eta\phi^{2}\Lambda_{j}^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}_{\infty j}\kappa_{j}^{2}\rho_{f}}
.

Taking derivative on both sides (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) with resect to time

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{\tau}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\mathbf{C}^{u}\bm{\epsilon}-M\bm{\beta}\zeta\right),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(M\left(\zeta-\bm{\beta}^{T}\bm{\epsilon}\right)\right),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\rho\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}}{\partial t}+\rho_{f}\frac{\partial\mathbf{{q}}}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot\bm{\overline{\tau}},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\rho_{f}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{v}}}{\partial t}+{\operatorname{diag}}\left(m_{j}\right)\frac{\partial{\mathbf{q}}}{\partial t}+\nabla p=-{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}\left(\partial_{t}+\lambda_{j}\right)^{1/2}\right)\mathbf{q},

where
\left(\partial_{t}+\lambda_{j}\right)^{1/2}
is a shift fractional derivative operator and
m_{j}=\alpha_{\infty j}\rho_{f}/\phi>0

2.5.
The augmented Biot equations

According to
[
14

, the tortuosity function of all porous material can be expressed as

Define the following function

Applying the inverse Fourier-Laplace transform gives

Introduce the auxiliary variables

Energy analysis of the augmented Biot equations

In this section, we conduct the energy analysis of the augmented Biot equations (

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider the augmented Biot equation (
2.44
) and define
\mathcal{E}_{1}
as (
2.22
) and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
as (
2.23
). Introduce

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1,3}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}r_{k}^{j}}{\phi(-\vartheta_{k}^{j})}\right)(\varphi_{k}^{j}-q_{j})^{2}\,dxdz,

with
\varphi_{k}^{j}=\exp({\vartheta_{k}^{j}\,t})\star\frac{\partial q_{j}}{\partial t}
.
Then the function

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{3}

is the energy function, which satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{q}^{T}{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}}\right)\mathbf{q}-\sum_{j=1,3}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}(\varphi_{k}^{j})^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r_{k}^{j}\right)\,dxdz\leq 0.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left[\alpha_{j}\star\frac{\partial q_{j}}{\partial t}\right](s)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\infty_{j}}s\hat{q}_{j}+\left(a_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}r^{j}_{k}\right)\hat{q}_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}r^{j}_{k}\vartheta_{k}\frac{\hat{q}_{j}}{s-\vartheta_{k}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\infty_{j}}s\hat{q}_{j}+a_{j}\hat{q}_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}\frac{r^{j}_{k}}{s-\vartheta_{k}}\left(s\hat{q}_{j}\right),

from which we have

(2.45)

\displaystyle-\nabla p=\rho_{f}\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+{\operatorname{diag}}(m_{j})\partial_{t}\mathbf{q}+{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}}\right)\mathbf{q}+\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{1}_{k}{\varphi}^{1}_{k}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N_{3}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{3}_{k}{\varphi}^{3}_{k}\end{pmatrix}

where

\varphi_{k}^{j}=\exp({\vartheta_{k}^{j}\,t})\star\frac{\partial q_{j}}{\partial t}.

By straightforward computing, we know

(2.46)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi_{k}^{j}}{\partial t}(\mathbf{x},t)=\vartheta_{k}^{j}\varphi_{k}^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)+\frac{\partial q_{j}}{\partial t},

(2.47)

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}^{j}(\mathbf{x},0)=0,\,j=1,3.

Multiplying (
2.45
) with
\mathbf{q}^{T}
yields

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\rho_{f}\mathbf{q}^{T}\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+\mathbf{q}^{T}{\operatorname{diag}}(m_{j})\frac{\partial\mathbf{q}}{\partial t}+\mathbf{q}^{T}\nabla p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbf{q}^{T}{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}}\right)\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{1}_{k}{\varphi}^{1}_{k}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N_{3}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{3}_{k}{\varphi}^{3}_{k}\end{pmatrix}

Similar to the proof of theorem
2.1
, we can derive

(2.48)

\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{E}_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\mathbf{q}^{T}{\operatorname{diag}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\kappa_{j}}\right)\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{q}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{1}_{k}{\varphi}^{1}_{k}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N_{3}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{3}_{k}{\varphi}^{3}_{k}\end{pmatrix}dxdz.

To calculate the second integral in the equation above, we multiply (
2.46
) with
q_{j}
and
{\varphi}^{j}_{k}
to obtain

(2.49)

\displaystyle{q_{j}}\partial_{t}{\varphi}_{k}^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)-{q}_{j}{\vartheta}^{j}_{k}{\varphi}^{j}_{k}(\mathbf{x},t)-{q}_{j}\partial_{t}{q}_{j}=0,

(2.50)

\displaystyle{\varphi_{k}^{j}}\partial_{t}{\varphi}_{k}^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)-{\varphi_{k}^{j}}{\vartheta}^{j}_{k}{\varphi}^{j}_{k}(\mathbf{x},t)-{\varphi_{k}^{j}}\partial_{t}{q}_{j}=0.

By subtracting (
2.50
) from (
2.49
) and rearranging terms, the following equation holds for every fixed
j=1,3
and
k=1,\dots,N_{j}
,

-q_{j}\vartheta^{j}_{k}\varphi_{k}^{j}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(\varphi_{k}^{j}-q_{j})^{2}-\vartheta_{k}^{j}(\varphi_{k}^{j})^{2}\Longrightarrow-q_{j}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}\right)r_{k}^{j}\varphi_{k}^{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}r_{k}^{j}}{\phi\vartheta_{k}^{j}}\right)\partial_{t}(\varphi_{k}^{j}-q_{j})^{2}-(\varphi_{k}^{j})^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r_{k}^{j}\right)

Summing over

and noting that
\vartheta_{k}^{j}<0
and integrating in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-\mathbf{q}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{1}_{k}{\varphi}^{1}_{k}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{N_{3}}\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r^{3}_{k}{\varphi}^{3}_{k}\end{pmatrix}\,dxdz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j=1,3}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}r_{k}^{j}}{\phi\vartheta_{k}^{j}}\right)\partial_{t}(\varphi_{k}^{j}-q_{j})^{2}-(\varphi_{k}^{j})^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r_{k}^{j}\right)\,dxdz

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{3}^{\prime}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j=1,3}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{j}}(\varphi_{k}^{j})^{2}\left(\frac{\rho_{f}}{\phi}r_{k}^{j}\right)\,dxdz.

3.
Full waveform inversion of the augmented Biot equations

The inverse problem is defined as a constrained minimization of the misfit function
\chi(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W})
with an
evolution PDE defining relation between the sought property field
\mathbf{M}
and the observed wave field
\mathbf{W}
, see
(
3.3
) below. A gradient type minimization algorithm for this constrained problem takes advantage of
the adjoint problem which can be solved with the same solver as the forward problem. Therefore,
in this section, we derive the adjoint problem for the FWI of the augmented Biot equation (
2.44
) with fixed
N_{1}
and
N_{3}
.

We introduce the notation used in this section. Note that in this section,
\mathcal{L}
denotes a fuctional, instead of the Fourier-Laplace transform. The Frechét derivative with respect to
\mathbf{u}
of a functional
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})
acting on a suitable test function
\delta\mathbf{u}
is denoted by

d_{\mathbf{u}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})[\delta\mathbf{u}]
.
The gradient with respect to
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
of a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is denoted by
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}f
and
represented by a row vector.
The gradient of a vector-valued function
\mathbf{F}:{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
with respect to
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}

is represented by a
q\times p
matrix defined as

[\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}]_{ij}=\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial u_{j}},\,i=1,\dots,q,\,j=1,\dots,p\,.

In terms of this notation, the usual product rule
for vector-valued functions

\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}:\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
represented
by column vectors is expressed

[\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{G})]_{j1}=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial u_{j}}G_{k}+F_{k}\frac{\partial G_{k}}{\partial u_{j}}\,,\;\;\mbox{or in matrix notation}\;\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{G})=(\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F})^{T}\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{F}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{G}\,,

and the chain rule for

\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
reads

[\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{m}))]_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F_{i}}{\partial u_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial m_{j}}\,,\;\;\mbox{or in matrix notation}\;\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{m}))=\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{u}\,.

The usual subscript notation for the partial derivative
f_{x}:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
is also used. The transpose of a matrix
\mathbf{A}
is denoted as
\mathbf{A}^{T}
and
\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{G}\equiv\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{G}
represents the scalar product of two vectors in the corresponding Euclidean space.
We introduce the time-spatial inner product of two vector-valued functions
\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{q}

(3.1)

\langle\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{h}^{T}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x},t)d\mathbf{x}dt\,.

3.1.
The misfit function for the FWI

Given the observed data
\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{y}_{r},t)
at receivers placed in the
positions
\mathbf{y}_{r}
,
r=1,\cdots,N
, the misfit functional is defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\chi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{M}):=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{R}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{M};\mathbf{x},t)-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x},t)\|^{2}\mu(dx)dt=:\langle\Phi,\mathbf{1}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}

where
\mu(dx)
is a measure representing the receivers, typically the sum of Dirac masses

\mu(dx)=\sum_{r=1}^{N}\delta_{y_{r}}
concentrated at points
y_{r}\in\Omega
. For the purpose of this derivation
we can assume that these are appropriately regularized so the integral in (
3.2
) is well defined.
We denote
\mathcal{R}
the restriction operator that sets the ’unmeasurable’ or unavailable components
in
\mathbf{W}
to be zero.
The given vector
\mathbf{d}
has the same dimension and format as
\mathbf{W}
by augmenting the data vector with
zeros for the components which have no available data. Therefore, the components in the
discrepancy
\mathcal{R}\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{d}
which correspond to the non-measurable entries in
\mathbf{W}
must equal to zero.

We consider the following constrained optimization problem for the property field
\mathbf{M}
in a suitable space
of admissible functions

(3.3)

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{M}}~\chi(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{M})\,,

(3.4)

subject to:

\displaystyle~\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}+\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W})=0\,,\;\;\;\;\mbox{ (PDE),}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x},0;\mathbf{M})-\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{0}\,,\;\;\;\;\mbox{ (IC)}

where the mapping

\mathcal{A}:(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{w},\bm{\zeta})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times n}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is defined as in (
2.44
), specifically

(3.6)

\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W})=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{x}))\mathbf{W}_{x}+\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}_{z}+\mathbf{H}_{1}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x})\mathbf{W}+\mathbf{H}_{2}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x})\mathbf{S}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x},t)\,.

For the sake of clarity we also abbreviate the equation (
3.4
) as

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{M},\partial_{t}\mathbf{W},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W})=0
.

3.2.
Adjoint problem and variation of the misfit function

A gradient type minimization iteratively updates values of the field
\mathbf{M}(x)
in the feasible
set using descent directions derived from the variation
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi
of the misfit function.
The first task is thus derivation of admissible directions that belong to the tangent space of the constraints
(
3.4
)-(
3.5
).
Given a perturbation
\delta\mathbf{M}
, the solution of the forward problem results in a perturbation
(variation)
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{W}
and thus a perturbation
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi
of the misfit function along
the constraint (
3.4
).

Assuming sufficient regularity of the misfit functional
\chi
and using the Taylor expansion for
\chi
,
the infinitesimal variation in the direction of
\delta\mathbf{M}
along the constraints is given by
the Fréchet derivative (taking into account that
\Phi
does not depend explicitly on
\mathbf{M}
)

(3.7)

\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi:=d_{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}}\chi[\delta\mathbf{M}]=\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\Phi,\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{W}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}\,,

where the function
\mathbf{V}:=\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{W}
solves the variation equation
for the constraint (
3.4
) under the perturbation
\delta\mathbf{M}
, i.e.,

(3.8)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}+\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W})\cdot\mathbf{V}+\nabla_{\bm{\zeta}}\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W}):\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{V}&=-\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{W})\cdot\delta\mathbf{M}\,,\\
\mathbf{V}(x,0)&=\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x})\,,\end{split}

along with the solution
\mathbf{W}(x,t;\mathbf{M})
of (
3.4
)-(
3.5
).
Thus solving (
3.4
)-(
3.5
), (
3.8
) for the functions
(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{V})
and taking into account the specific form of
\Phi
in (
3.2
) we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi=\sum_{r=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{R}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{x},t)-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x},t)\right)^{T}\mathbf{V}\,\delta_{y_{r}}(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x}\,dt\,.

An alternative way to compute the variation
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi
is to use the formulation of
the constrained minimization problem (
3.3
) with the Lagrangian functional

(3.10)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})=\langle\Phi,\mathbf{1}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}+\langle\bm{\lambda},\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}\mathbf{g}d\mathbf{x}\,,

where we introduced
the Lagrange multipliers
\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\bm{\mu}(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In this formulation we can vary
\delta\mathbf{M}
and
\delta\mathbf{W}
independently thus obtaining the Fréchet derivative
d\mathcal{L}
at a point
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})
acting on the increment
(\delta\mathbf{M},\delta\mathbf{W})
, i.e.,

(3.11)

\begin{split}d\mathcal{L}[(\delta\mathbf{M},\delta\mathbf{W})]=&\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\Phi,\delta\mathbf{W}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}+\langle\bm{\lambda},d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{W}]\rangle_{\Omega\times T}+\langle\bm{\lambda},d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{M}]\rangle_{\Omega\times T}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{W}]\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{M}]\,d\mathbf{x}\,.\end{split}

From the definition of the mapping
\mathcal{F}=\partial_{t}\mathbf{W}+\mathcal{A}
and
\mathbf{g}
we have

\begin{split}d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{W}]&=\partial_{t}\delta\mathbf{W}+\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{A}\cdot\delta\mathbf{W}+\nabla_{\bm{\zeta}}\mathcal{A}:\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\delta\mathbf{W}\\
d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{M}]&=\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{A}\cdot\delta\mathbf{M}\,,\\
d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{W}]&=\delta\mathbf{W}\,,\;\;\;\;\;\;d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{M}]=-\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\bm{\phi}\cdot\delta\mathbf{M}\,.\end{split}

Combining the first and second term in (
3.11
) and integrating by parts in the expression for
d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{W}]
we obtain

\begin{split}\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\Phi,\delta\mathbf{W}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}+\langle\bm{\lambda},d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathcal{F}[\delta\mathbf{W}]\rangle_{\Omega\times T}&=\langle-\partial_{t}\bm{\lambda}+\bm{\lambda}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{A}-\mathrm{div}(\bm{\lambda}^{T}\nabla_{\bm{\zeta}}\mathcal{A})+\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\Phi,\delta\mathbf{W}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}\\
&+\int_{\Omega}(\bm{\lambda}^{T}(\mathbf{x},T)\delta\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x},T)-\bm{\lambda}^{T}(\mathbf{x},0)\delta\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x},0)\,d\mathbf{x}\,,\end{split}

where we set
\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)=0
for
\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega
to remove the boundary terms from the integration by parts.
We denote the solution
\bm{\lambda}_{*}(\mathbf{x},t)
of the adjoint problem

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}^{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{A}+\mathrm{div}(\bm{\lambda}^{T}\nabla_{\bm{\zeta}}\mathcal{A})=\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}\Phi\,,\;\;\;\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\,,\;\;t<T\\
&\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},T)=0\,,\;\;\;\;\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)|_{\partial\Omega}=0\,,\end{split}

and set the multiplier
\bm{\mu}_{*}(\mathbf{x})=\bm{\lambda}_{*}(x,0)
. Hence we have

\int_{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{505486}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454239}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{454240}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{W}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{W}]\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\Omega}\bm{\mu}^{T}d_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{g}[\delta\mathbf{M}]\,d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega}\bm{\lambda}_{*}(x,0)\cdot\delta\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x},0)\,d\mathbf{x}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\lambda}_{*}(x,0)\cdot(\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\bm{\phi}\delta\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{x}))\,d\mathbf{x}\,.

Substituting back to (
3.11
) we obtain the expression for the variation
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi
of the misfit functional
\chi
evaluated at the solution
\mathbf{W}_{*}
of the forward problem (
3.4
)-(
3.5
) as

(3.13)

\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi=d\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{W}_{*},\bm{\lambda}_{*},\bm{\mu}_{*})[\delta\mathbf{M}]=\langle\bm{\lambda}_{*},\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{A}\delta\mathbf{M}\rangle_{\Omega\times T}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\lambda}_{*}(x,0)\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{m}}\bm{\phi}\delta\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{x})\,d\mathbf{x}\,.

Returning back to the specific form of
\mathcal{A}
, cf.(
3.6
), and
\Phi
, cf. (
3.2
), we have
\bm{\lambda}_{*}
solving

\displaystyle{\partial_{t}\bm{\lambda}}+\partial_{x}\left(\mathbf{A}^{T}\bm{\lambda}\right)+\partial_{z}\left(\mathbf{B}^{T}\bm{\lambda}\right)-\mathbf{H}_{1}^{T}\bm{\lambda}=\sum_{r=1}^{N}\left[\mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{x},t)-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{x},t)\right]\delta(\mathbf{y}_{r}),

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},T)=\mathbf{0},\quad\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)|_{\partial\Omega}=\mathbf{0}\,,

and
\bm{\mu}_{*}(\mathbf{x})=\bm{\lambda}_{*}(\mathbf{x},0)
.
Solving the adjoint problem for
\bm{\lambda}_{*}
eliminates the need for computing the solution
\mathbf{V}={\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\mathbf{W}}
of the variation equation (
3.8
) and it gives an explicit expression for the descent direction in the gradient-type minimization algorithm. From (
3.13
) we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k=1}^{q}\delta M_{k}(\mathbf{x})\int_{0}^{T}\bm{\lambda}_{*}^{T}\left\{\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}_{x}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{W}_{z}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{W}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{2}\mathbf{S}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathbf{H}_{2}\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{S}-\partial_{m_{k}}\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\right\}dt\,d\mathbf{x}\,,

where
\delta M_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,q
denotes components of the increment
\delta\mathbf{M}
. Thus we have an explicit formula for the directional derivative
\delta_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{464247}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262143}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{417093}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{M}$}}}}}}\chi=\langle\mathbf{G},\delta\mathbf{M}\rangle_{\Omega}
where
the vector- valued function
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
represents the Fréchet derivative and is given component-wise as

(3.14)

[\mathbf{G}]_{k}=\int_{0}^{T}\bm{\lambda}_{*}^{T}\left\{\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}_{x}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{W}_{z}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{W}+\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{2}\mathbf{S}+\mathbf{H}_{2}\partial_{m_{k}}\mathbf{S}-\partial_{m_{k}}\bm{\phi}(\mathbf{x})\right\}dt\,.

Therefore, the direction of descent for minimizing the misfit function
\chi
and updating the field

is given by

4.
Numerical method for computing
\{\vartheta_{k}\}
and
\{r_{k}\}

To make this paper selt-contained, we summarize in this section the numerical method for poles
\vartheta_{k}
and residues
r_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,M
, by using the two-sided residue method; the calculation should be carried out in an arbitrary precision arithmetic system such as Advanpix (a multiprecision Matlab toolbox) to ensure the returned poles and residues are of correct signs
[
15
]
. The two-sided residue interpolation method involves solving a linear system of size
M
from given values of
\alpha
at
M
distinct frequencies and automatically interpolates
\alpha
at
\omega=0
and
\omega=\infty
. Depending on the spectral content of the source term
\mathbf{S}
, the interpolation grid is either equally spaced or log-spaced in the frequency range.

Given the M interpolation data
(z_{k},u_{k},v_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{+}\times\mathbb{C}^{p\times q}\times\mathbb{C}^{p\times q}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{+}
denotes the upper-half complex plane, we seek a
p\times p
matrix valued function
G(z)
of the form

(4.1)

G(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\mu(t)}{t-z},

where
\mu
is a positive
p\times p
matrix valued measure, such that

(4.2)

G(z_{i})u_{i}=v_{i},i=1,\cdots,M.

Define the Hermitian matrices
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
via

(4.3)

\left(S_{1}\right)_{ij}:=\frac{u_{i}^{*}v_{j}-v_{i}^{*}u_{j}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{i}}},~\left(S_{2}\right)_{ij}:=\frac{z_{j}u_{i}^{*}v_{j}-\overline{z_{i}}v_{i}^{*}u_{j}}{z_{j}-\overline{z_{i}}},~i,j=1,\ldots,M,

then
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are positive semidefinite. Here the sup-script
*
denotes the conjugate transpose operator. Conversely, if
S_{1}
is positive and
S_{2}
is positive semidefinite, then

(4.4)

\displaystyle G(z):=

\displaystyle-C_{+}\left(zS_{1}-S_{1}A-C_{+}^{*}C_{-}\right)^{-1}C_{+}^{*}=C_{+}\left(S_{2}-zS_{1}\right)^{-1}C_{+}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{qM}\left(\frac{1}{d_{k}-z}\right)C_{+}S_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{x}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{*}S_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}C_{+}^{*},

is a solution to the interpolation problem, where
\bm{x}_{j}
is the eigenvector of
S_{1}^{\frac{-1}{2}}S_{2}S_{1}^{\frac{-1}{2}}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
d_{k}
and
C_{-}:=\left({u_{1}},{\cdots},{u_{M}}\right)
,
C_{+}:=\left({v_{1}},{\cdots},{v_{M}}\right)
,
A:=\operatorname{diag}\left(z_{i}\right)
.
Specializing the results to the function
D(s)
, with change of variables
-i\omega=:s=-\frac{1}{z}
, we have
p=q=1
and

(4.5)

D(s)=\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{1+st}=(-z)\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{d\sigma}{t-z},~s=-\frac{1}{z},

then

(4.6)

\displaystyle D(s)-\alpha_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(-z)\left(\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{t-z}-\frac{\alpha_{\infty}}{-z}\right)

\displaystyle=(-z)\left(\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{d\sigma(t)}{t-z}-\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{\alpha_{\infty}\sigma(t)}{t-z}\right):=(-z)G_{new}(z),

which is Stieltjes function owing to
\sigma
has a Dirac measure of strength
\alpha_{\infty}
.
Using (
4.4
), we can compute the poles and residues for
G_{new}
as follows, which amounts to solving a generalized eigen value problem.

(4.7)

\displaystyle-i\omega_{k}=:s_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{z_{k}},~u_{k}=\frac{1}{s_{k}},~k=1\ldots M,

(4.8)

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D\left(s_{k}\right)-\alpha_{\infty},k=1\ldots M,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left(S_{1}\right)_{pq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-s_{q}D\left(s_{q}\right)+s_{p}^{*}D^{*}\left(s_{p}\right)}{s_{p}^{*}-s_{q}}-\alpha_{\infty},~p,\,q=1\ldots M,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left(S_{2}\right)_{pq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-D\left(s_{q}\right)+D^{*}\left(s_{p}\right)}{s_{q}-s_{p}^{*}},~p,\,q=1\ldots M,

(4.11)

\displaystyle(-z)G_{new}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{C}_{+}\left(S_{1}+sS_{2}\right)^{-1}{C}_{+}^{*}.

In terms of the generalized eigenvalues/eigenvectors
[\mathbf{V},\Phi]:=eig(S_{1},S_{2})
, where
\mathbf{V}
is the matrix of generalized vectors and
\Phi
the diagonal matrix of generalized eigenvalues such that

(4.12)

S_{1}\mathbf{V}=S_{2}\mathbf{V}\Phi,

and taking into account of the simultaneous diagonalization property

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbf{V}^{*}S_{1}\mathbf{V}&=\Phi,\quad\mathbf{V}^{*}S_{2}\mathbf{V}&=\mathbf{I},\end{aligned}

we obtain

(4.14)

D(s)=\alpha_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{C_{+}\mathbf{V}_{1}(:,k)\mathbf{V}_{1}(:,k)^{*}C_{+}^{*}}{s+\Phi(k,k)}.

The poles
\vartheta_{k}
and residues
r_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,M
, are given by

(4.15)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Phi(k,k),

(4.16)

\displaystyle r_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{+}\mathbf{V}(:,k)\mathbf{V}(:,k)^{*}C_{+}^{*}.

5.
Conclusion and future work

In this paper, we consider the time-domain FWI for the poroelastic wave equations, whose dispersive and dissipative behaviors are encoded in the auxiliary variables
\Theta_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,N
. Since all possible memory kernel functions that satisfy the causality conditions can be represented as an integral presented here, the FWI framework proposed here is general enough to handle all porous material with pore geometry regular enough for the spectral theory of Stokes equation to hold true. The energy analyses for the popular Biot-JKD equations and for the most general augmented Biot equations have been also presented in this paper; they play an important role in understand the stability of the adjoint problem, which is derived in this paper. The adjoint problem involves solving a PDE backward in time for the Lagrange multiplier function
\bm{\lambda}(\mathbf{x},t)
. In this framework, the minimization of the misfit function can be solved by using the gradient calculated from using the solutions of the forward and the adjoint problem.

This paper lays the foundation for a thorough mathematical and numerical analysis for the time-domain FWI of dispersive and dissipative poroelastic wave equations. On the mathematical side, the future work includes the analysis of the minimization problem such as the uniqueness and existence of the minimizer convergence rate, the choice of regularization terms and the error estimates. The question of uniqueness of the minimizer is highly non-trivial because a given memory kernel can be approximated equally well by different choices of pole-residue sets; this line of research will involve the definition of equivalent classes of pole-residue and factor it into the iteration steps. We would also like to expand our energy analysis to the case where the initial conditions of the fluid relative velocity
\mathbf{q}
is not zero. On the numerical side, the immediate future work includes the development of an efficient solver for the adjoint problem and the strategy for handling the time integral for computing the gradient
\mathbf{G}
; for handing the memory demand, a strategy such as the CARFS
[
23
]
will be considered.

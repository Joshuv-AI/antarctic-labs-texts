1.
Introduction

We study solutions of the inequality

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\partial^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}(x,u)\geq f(x)g(|u|)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\setminus S,

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
m,n\geq 1
are integers,
S
is a
compact subset of
\Omega
, and
a_{\alpha}
are Caratheodory functions such that

|a_{\alpha}(x,\zeta)|\leq A|\zeta|,\quad|\alpha|=m,

with some constant
A>0
for almost all
x\in\Omega

and for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Also let
f:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function
and
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a twice continuously differentiable function such that

g(0)=0
and, in addition,
g(\zeta)>0
,
g^{\prime}(\zeta)>0
, and
g^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)>0
for all
\zeta\in(0,\infty)
.
As is customary, by
\alpha={(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})}
we mean a multi-index with

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}

and

\partial^{\alpha}={\partial^{|\alpha|}/(\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}})},

where
x={(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}
.

Let us denote by
B_{r}^{x}
an open ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
In the case of
x=0
, we write
B_{r}
instead of
B_{r}^{0}
.
For an arbitrary set
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a real number
r>0
by
\omega_{r}
we mean the set of the points
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

the distance from which to
\omega
is less than
r
.

The function
u
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) if

u\in L_{1}(\Omega\setminus S_{r})

and

f(x){g(|u|)}\in L_{1}(\Omega\setminus S_{r})
for any
r>0

and, moreover,

\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(-1)^{m}a_{\alpha}(x,u)\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x)g(|u|)\varphi\,dx

(1.2)

for any non-negative function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus S)
.
In so doing, we say that the singular set
S
is removable if

u\in L_{1}(\Omega)
,

f(x){g(|u|)}\in L_{1}(\Omega)
,
and inequality (
1.2
) is valid for all non-negative functions
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

The problem of removability of singularities for solutions of differential equations and inequalities is a traditional area of interest for many mathematicians [1–11].
Most of the papers in this area are devoted to the case of second-order differential operators [1–7].
For higher order differential inequalities (
1.1
), sufficient conditions for the removability of a singularity are known only in the case of the power nonlinearity
g(t)=t^{\lambda}

[
8
,
9
]
or in the case of an isolated singularity
[
11
]
.

Singular sets for solutions of linear higher order elliptic equations were studied in
[
10
]
.
For these equations, in order to remove a singularity, some growth conditions have to be imposed on the solutions when approaching a singular set.

In the case of an arbitrary nonlinearity, conditions for the removability of a singular set remained unknown.
The paper presented to your attention provides an answer to this question.
Note that we do not impose any requirements on the growth of solutions near a singularity.
We also do not impose any ellipticity conditions on the coefficients
a_{\alpha}
of the differential operator.
Thus, our results can be applied to a wide class of differential inequalities.

Consider the Legendre transformation

g^{*}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{g^{\prime}(0)}^{\xi}(g^{\prime})^{-1}(\zeta)\,d\zeta,&&\xi>g^{\prime}(0),\\
&0,&&\xi\leq g^{\prime}(0)\end{aligned}\right.

of the function
g
,
where
(g^{\prime})^{-1}
is the inverse function to
g^{\prime}
.
From the Fenchel-Young inequality, it obviously follows that

ab\leq g(a)+g^{*}(b)

for all real numbers
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 0
.
In the case of
g(t)=t^{\lambda}/\lambda
,
\lambda>1
, this inequality takes the form

ab\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}a^{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}b^{\lambda/(\lambda-1)}

for all real numbers
a\geq 0
and
b\geq 0
.

We put

\gamma(\xi)=\frac{g^{*}(\xi)}{\xi}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu(\xi)=\inf_{t\geq\xi}\frac{g(t)}{t}.

Let
N_{r}(\omega)
be the minimal number of sets of diameter at most
r
which can cover a bounded set
\omega
. The lower and upper fractal dimensions of
\omega
respectively are defined by

\operatorname{\underline{dim}}_{F}\omega=\liminf_{r\to+0}\frac{\log N_{r}(\omega)}{\log 1/r}

and

\operatorname{\overline{dim}}_{F}\omega=\limsup_{r\to+0}\frac{\log N_{r}(\omega)}{\log 1/r}.

It does not present any particular problem to verify that

\operatorname{\underline{dim}}_{F}\omega=n-\limsup_{r\to+0}\frac{\log\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\omega_{r}}{\log r}

and

\operatorname{\overline{dim}}_{F}\omega=n-\liminf_{r\to+0}\frac{\log\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\omega_{r}}{\log r}.

Therefore, the last two expressions can be used as another definition of the lower and upper fractal dimensions.

If
\operatorname{\underline{dim}}_{F}\omega=\operatorname{\overline{dim}}_{F}\omega
,
then we say that
\omega
has the fractal dimension

\operatorname{dim}_{F}\omega=\lim_{r\to+0}\frac{\log N_{r}(\omega)}{\log 1/r}.

In the literature, the fractal dimensions are sometimes called Minkowski dimensions or box-counting dimensions
[
12
,
13
]
.
The upper fractal dimension is also known as Kolmogorov’s entropy or Kolmogorov’s capacity
[
12
]
.

We say
[
10
]
that
\omega
has finite
k
-dimension if

\limsup_{r\to+0}\frac{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\omega_{r}}{r^{n-k}}<\infty.

In so doing,
\omega
is a
k
-dimensional set if

\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\omega_{r}/r^{n-k}
tends to a positive finite limit as
r\to+0

[
12
]
.
If
\omega
is a
k
-dimensional set, then it obviously has both the fractal dimension
k
and finite
k
-dimension.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any set
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

there exists a non-negative function
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

\left.\psi\right|_{\omega}=1,\quad\left.\psi\right|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\omega_{r}}=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\psi\|_{C^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq Cr^{-m},

Proof.

We construct a sequence (countable or finite) of points

Let

be an arbitrary point belonging to
\omega

Assume further that

is already constructed for all

\omega\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}B_{r/2}^{x_{j}},

|x_{i+1}|<\inf\left\{|x|:x\in\omega\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}B_{r/2}^{x_{j}}\right\}+1.

Since

B_{r/4}^{x_{i}}\cap B_{r/4}^{x_{j}}=\emptyset

, we obviously obtain

\omega\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{r/2}^{x_{i}}.

In so doing, for any point

the number of the balls

containing

does not exceed a certain value depending only on

Really, if

Therefore, the number of the balls

Consider a non-negative function

Putting

\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}(x)=0

Thus, to complete the proof, it remains to take

where

is a non-negative function such that

\left.\eta\right|_{(-\infty,0]}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left.\eta\right|_{[1,\infty)}=1.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(-1)^{m}a_{\alpha}(x,u)\psi_{i}\partial^{\alpha}\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\sum_{|\alpha^{\prime}|+|\alpha^{\prime\prime}|=m,\;|\alpha^{\prime}|>1}a_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(x,u)\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}\psi_{i}\partial^{\alpha^{\prime\prime}}\varphi\,dx

as

.

3.6
) implies (
1.2
), where

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let (

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{1}}}\int_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{1}}}|u|\,dx\leq\frac{2}{\beta}G^{-1}\left(C\beta^{1/m}(\rho_{1}-\rho_{0})\mathop{\rm ess\,inf}\limits_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S}f^{1/m}\right)

for all real numbers
0<\rho_{0}<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<r_{0}
,
where

\beta=\frac{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{1}}}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{0}}}

G^{-1}
is the function inverse to (

is a constant depending only on

A
,
n
,
m
,
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon_{0}

Consider the function

If
J(\rho_{1})=0
, then (
3.8
) is obvious;
therefore, it can be assumed that
J(\rho_{1})>0
.

We construct a finite sequence of real numbers
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
as follows.
The first term
\rho_{1}
of this sequence is defined in the conditions of the lemma.
Assume further that
\rho_{i}
is already known for some integer
i\geq 1
.
If
\rho_{i}\leq(\rho_{0}+\rho_{1})/2
, then we put
l=i
and stop; otherwise we take

\rho_{i+1}=\inf\{r\in(\rho_{0},\rho_{i}):J(r)\leq 2J(r_{i})\}.

Since
J
is a non-increasing positive function on the closed interval

this procedure must complete in a finite number of steps.

Let us denote by
C
various positive constants which can depend
only on
A
,
n
,
m
,
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon_{0}
, and the first summand in the left-hand side of (

From inequalities (

) and (
3.7
), where

\frac{1}{(\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1})^{m}}\int_{S_{\rho_{i}}\setminus S_{\rho_{i+1}}}|u|\,dx\geq C\int_{S_{\varepsilon/2}\setminus S_{\rho_{i}}}f(x)g(|u|)\,dx

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{i}}}\int_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{i}}}g(|u|)\,dx\geq g\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{i}}}\int_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S_{\rho_{i}}}|u|\,dx\right)

Combining this with (

\int_{J(\rho_{i})}^{J(\rho_{i+1})}g^{-1/m}(\beta\zeta/2)\zeta^{1/m-1}\,d\zeta\geq C(\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1})\mathop{\rm ess\,inf}\limits_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S}f^{1/m},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,l-1.

\int_{J(\rho_{1})}^{\infty}g^{-1/m}(\beta\zeta/2)\zeta^{1/m-1}\,d\zeta\geq C(\rho_{1}-\rho_{0})\mathop{\rm ess\,inf}\limits_{S_{\rho_{2}}\setminus S}f^{1/m}.

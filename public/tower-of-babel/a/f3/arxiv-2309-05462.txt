. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
p
be a prime number,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the field of
p
-adic numbers and
F
a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and uniformiser
\pi_{F}
. Let
\Omega_{F}
denote the the Drinfeld upper half-plane: it is a rigid
F
-analytic space whose underlying set is the set of
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-orbits in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{F})\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
. In
[
12
]
, Drinfeld defined a tower of
G:=GL_{2}(F)
-equivariant étale rigid
\breve{F}
-analytic coverings of
\Omega:=\Omega_{F}\times_{F}\breve{F}
, where
\breve{F}
is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
.

We consider a slight modification of Drinfeld’s construction, due to Rapoport-Zink
[
27
]
. They constructed a tower of coverings

\cdots\to\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}_{n-1}\to\cdots\to\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{0}

of rigid
\breve{F}
-analytic spaces. Their base space
\mathcal{M}_{0}
can be viewed as being the disjoint union of countably many copies of
\Omega
(cf
[
27
, Theorem 3.72]
): there is a non-canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{M}_{0}\cong\Omega\times\mathbb{Z}
. The tower comes equipped with an action of
G
, so that the maps
\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
are all
G
-equivariant. The
G
-action on the base space is given by

g\cdot(z,n)=(g\cdot z,n+v_{\pi_{F}}(\det g)).

Here the
G
-action on
\Omega
is the usual one by Möbius transformations. Thus each copy of
\Omega
is stabilised by the subgroup
G^{0}
of
G
consisting of matrices
g
in
G
such that
v_{\pi_{F}}(\det g)=0
. In this way we may view
\mathcal{M}_{0}
as being isomorphic to
G\times_{G^{0}}\Omega
.

Let
D
be the quaternion division algebra over
F
with valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{D}
and let
\Pi
denote a generator of the unique maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{D}
. The maps
\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
in the tower are all finite étale and Galois with

\Gal(\mathcal{M}_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{0})\cong\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/(1+\Pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D}).

Moreover, the actions of
G
and
\Gal(\mathcal{M}_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{0})
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
commute.

Scholze–Weinstein have proved in
[
31
, Theorem 7.3.1]
that if
\mathbf{C}
denotes a complete and algebraically closed extension of
\breve{F}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
is a finite étale
G
-equivariant cover of the base-change
\Omega\times_{\breve{F}}\mathbf{C}
, then there is an integer
m\geqslant 0
such that
\mathcal{M}_{m}\times_{\breve{F}}\mathbf{C}\to\Omega\times_{\breve{F}}\mathbf{C}
factors through
\widetilde{\Omega}
.

One way to better understand these finite étale equivariant covers is through the study of equivariant vector bundles with flat connections on the base space: the two theories are essentially equivalent. Here we briefly sketch how to obtain equivariant vector bundles with flat connection from the Drinfeld tower. Suppose that
\rho
is a smooth geometrically irreducible representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
whose character is defined over some finite extension
K
of
\breve{F}
. There is some
m\geqslant 0
such that
\rho
factors through
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/(1+\Pi^{m}\mathcal{O}_{D})
and, for all
n\geqslant m
,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{n,K}}
has a
\rho
-isotypical component
\mathcal{V}^{\rho}
independent of the choice of
n
. Then the pushforward of
\mathcal{V}^{\rho}
down to
\mathcal{M}_{0,K}
is a
G
-equivariant vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{0,K}
of rank
(\dim\rho)^{2}
, equipped with an integrable connection.

In this paper we only consider the smooth
K
-linear representations of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
of degree
1
, that is, the torsion characters
\theta:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\to K^{\times}
. Then the pushforward of
\mathcal{V}^{\theta}
is a
G
-equivariant line bundle on
\mathcal{M}_{0,K}
with an integrable connection. For example, when
\bf{1}
is the trivial representation, the corresponding line bundle is just the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
with its natural
G
-action and natural integrable connection. The set
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}
of torsion characters
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\to K^{\times}
forms a group under pointwise multiplication of characters; given a continuous action
1
1

1

see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{PicConG}
for the precise definitions
of a topological group
H
on a smooth rigid
K
-analytic space
X
, we denote by
\PicCon^{H}(X)
the group of isomorphism classes of
H
-equivariant line bundles with an integrable connection on
X
, where the group structure comes from the tensor product of these line bundles. In this way, the Drinfeld tower gives us a group homomorphism

(1)

\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\PicCon^{G}(\mathcal{M}_{0,K})_{\tors}.

1.2.
The main result

In this paper we will consider, for each complete valued field extension
K
of
F
, the subgroup
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}
of
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)
consisting of the isomorphism classes of the
G^{0}
-equivariant line bundles with integrable connection on the rigid
K
-analytic space
\Omega:=\Omega_{F}\times_{F}K
that have finite order under
\otimes
. This group is naturally isomorphic to
\PicCon^{G}(G\times_{G_{0}}\Omega)_{\tors}
via an induction map, and therefore also, non-canonically, to
\PicCon^{G}(\mathcal{M}_{0,K})_{\tors}
whenever
K
contains
\breve{F}
. Our main result is then

Theorem A
.

Suppose that
K
contains the quadratic unramified extension
L
of
F
. Then there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}.

We note that Theorem
A
cannot be true without the condition that
K
contains
L
in the statement, because
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)[p^{\prime}]
is cyclic of order
q^{2}-1
for any choice of field extension
K
, but
K^{\times}
contains no such subgroup unless
K
contains
L
and so
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})
cannot either. We also note here in passing that in fact all line bundles on
\Omega
are known to be trivial — see, e.g.
[
16
, Théorème A]
.

Our isomorphism depends on a choice of a point
z\in\Omega_{F}(L)
and an
F
-algebra embedding
\iota:L\hookrightarrow D
. However, there is a natural
G
-action on
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}
and a natural conjugation
D^{\times}
-action on
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}
: the first action factors through
G/G^{0}F^{\times}
and the second action factors through
D^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}F^{\times}
, both cyclic groups of order
2
. The isomorphisms in Theorem
A
are compatible with respect to these actions, provided we identify
G/G^{0}F^{\times}
with
D^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}F^{\times}
in the only possible way. Quotienting out by these actions on both sides, we obtain a bijection

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}/G\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}/D^{\times}

which no longer depends on any choices. We expect, but do not check it in this paper, that when
K=\breve{F}
, the isomorphism in Theorem
A
is in fact inverse to (
1
) for some choice of identification of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
with
G\times_{G^{0}}\Omega
, depending on our other choices.

1.3.
Motivation

We do not use the result of Scholze–Weinstein
[
31
]
, nor even the existence of the Drinfeld tower, to prove Theorem
A
. Instead, we give an explicit construction of the elements
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}
as finite order
G^{0}
-equivariant line bundles with connection on
\Omega
and prove directly that there are no others. More precisely: in this paper we give an
elementary construction

2
2

2

elementary in the sense of not depending on the duality of Rapoport-Zink spaces, nor on the theory of perfectoid spaces
of finite order
GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
-equivariant line bundles with connection on the
K
-affinoid subdomain
\Omega_{0}
of
\Omega
that is the inverse image of the vertex fixed by
GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
in the Bruhat-Tits tree under the reduction map, and show that each of these line bundles extends uniquely to a
G^{0}
-equivariant line bundle with connection on all of
\Omega
. We have provided full arguments for some results that already appear in the literature in order to stress the elementary nature of our work.

In our companion work
[
1
]
, we use this elementary construction to better understand the structure of the global sections
\mathscr{L}^{\theta}(\Omega)
of these equivariant line bundles as modules over the locally
F
-analytic distribution algebra of
G^{0}
. The results of this paper are used to show that if
j:\Omega\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
denotes the open inclusion, then
j_{\ast}\mathscr{L}^{\theta}
is a coadmissible
G^{0}
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-module on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
in the sense of
[
2
]
. We then use this to prove that for each
\theta\in\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}
,
\mathscr{L}^{\theta}(\Omega)
is
irreducible
as a coadmissible
D(G^{0},K)
-module when the underlying connection is non-trivial, and that it has length two otherwise. The condition that the underlying connection is non-trivial can be shown to be equivalent to
\theta
not being fixed by the
D^{\times}
-action on
\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}
discussed above.

1.4.
An overview of some related works

Because of the central position of the theory of Drinfeld coverings and Rapoport-Zink spaces in the local Langlands program
[
14
]
, it is difficult to make a comprehensive literature review. In his work
[
35
]
, Teitelbaum found explicit local equations for the first Drinfeld covering of
\Omega
: this yields explicit torsion line bundles with connection on
\Omega_{0}
together with a (non-explicit)
GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
-equivariant structure. However neither Teitelbaum, nor Lue Pan in his closely-related work
[
24
]
, consider the flat connections on these line bundles, nor do they make an attempt to classify the appropriate equivariant structures in an elementary manner.

We acknowledge our intellectual debt to the Introduction of
[
11
]
, where Dospinescu and Le Bras explain the importance of the equivariant vector bundles on
\Omega
in the context of locally analytic representations of
GL_{2}(F)
, and in particular in the
p
-adic local Langlands program. We mention here in passing that the constructions in our paper enable us to define the first Drinfeld covering of
\Omega
directly over
F
rather than over
\breve{F}
without appealing to the theory of Weil descent. We also take the opportunity to mention here the monumental works of Colmez, Dospinescu and Nizioł
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
that use Scholze’s pro-étale methods and build upon
[
11
]
to show, amongst other things, that the
p
-adic étale cohomology of the Drinfeld tower realises the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence, at least in the case when
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Finally, we mention that Junger in his recent preprint
[
17
]
has classified equivariant line bundles on Deligne’s formal model
\widehat{\Omega}
of
\Omega_{F}
. There are some formal similarities in our methods (for example his Proposition 2.10 plays a similar role to our Proposition
3.2.14
), however we cannot deduce his results from ours, nor vice versa. We expect that our results here can be naturally extended to analogues of
\Omega
in higher dimensions.

2.1.
Measures on profinite sets

We begin by adapting some definitions from

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
Z
be a profinite set and let
\mathfrak{{a}}
be an abelian group.

(1)

An
\mathfrak{{a}}
-valued measure on Z
is a function
\nu
from the set of clopen subsets of
Z
to
\mathfrak{{a}}
, satisfying
\nu(U)=\nu(V)+\nu(U\backslash V)
whenever
V\subseteq U
are clopen subsets of
Z
.

(2)

M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})
denotes the abelian group of all
\mathfrak{{a}}
-valued measures on
Z
under pointwise operations.

(3)

M_{0}(Z,\mathfrak{{a}}):=\{\nu\in M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}}):\nu(Z)=0\}
is the subgroup of
\mathfrak{{a}}
-valued measures on
Z
with total value zero
.

Examples 2.1.2
.

(1)

If
Z
is any profinite set and
\mathfrak{a}
is a unital ring then for each
z\in Z
we can define a measure
\delta_{z}
by

\delta_{z}(U)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }z\in U\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2)

If
Z
happens to be finite we can define a ‘counting measure’ in
M(Z,\mathbb{Z})
via

\Sigma_{Z}(U)=|U|\mbox{ for all }U\subset Z.

Indeed
\Sigma_{Z}=\sum_{z\in Z}\delta_{z}
.

Let
C(Z,\mathbb{Z})
be the set of continuous
\mathbb{Z}
-valued functions on
Z
, where we give
\mathbb{Z}
the discrete topology. Of course every such function is locally constant.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

Let
Z
be a profinite set and let
\mathfrak{{a}}
be an abelian group.

(1)

There is a natural additive isomorphism
M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\to\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(C(Z,\mathbb{Z}),\mathfrak{{a}})
.

(2)

Let
(Z_{i})_{i\in I}
be a filtered inverse system of finite sets. Then every isomorphism
Z\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}Z_{i}
of profinite sets induces an isomorphisms of abelian groups
M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}M(Z_{i},\mathfrak{{a}})
and
M_{0}(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}M_{0}(Z_{i},\mathfrak{{a}})
.

(3)

The functor
\mathfrak{{a}}\mapsto M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})
is exact.

Proof.

(a) Let
\nu\in M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})
and let
f:Z\to\mathbb{Z}
be locally constant. Because
Z
is profinite and hence compact, we can choose an open partition
\{U_{1},\cdots,U_{m}\}
of
Z
such that
f_{|U_{i}}
is constant for each
i
. Choose
z_{i}\in U_{i}
for each
i=1,\cdots,m
and define
\langle\nu,f\rangle:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}f(z_{i})\nu(U_{i})\in\mathfrak{{a}}
. Then
\langle\nu,f\rangle
does not depend on the choice of open partition or the choice of the
z_{i}
’s, and
\nu\mapsto(f\mapsto\langle\nu,f\rangle)
defines an additive map
M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\to\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(C(Z,\mathbb{Z}),\mathfrak{{a}})
.

Let
1_{U}
denote the characteristic function of the clopen subset
U
of
Z
. Given an additive map
g:C(Z,\mathbb{Z})\to\mathfrak{{a}}
, setting
\nu(U):=g(1_{U})\in\mathfrak{{a}}
for each clopen
U
defines an element
\nu\in M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})
such that
\langle\nu,f\rangle=g(f)
for all

because the characteristic functions
1_{U}
generate
C(Z,\mathbb{Z})
as an abelian group. The function
g\mapsto\nu
is then an inverse to
M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\to\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(C(Z,\mathbb{Z}),\mathfrak{{a}})
.

(b) The isomorphism
Z\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}Z_{i}
induces an isomorphism of abelian groups
C(Z,\mathbb{Z})\cong\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow}C(Z_{i},\mathbb{Z})
. The functor
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(-,\mathfrak{{a}})
converts colimits into limits; now apply part (a) to get
M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}M(Z_{i},\mathfrak{{a}})

(c) By a theorem of Nöbeling — see
[

—

is a free abelian group. Hence
\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(C(Z,\mathbb{Z}),-)
is exact; now apply part (a).
∎

Definition 2.1.4
.

For every abelian group
\mathfrak{{a}}
the map
Z\mapsto M(Z,\mathfrak{{a}})
from profinite sets to abelian groups extends to a functor from the category of profinite sets and continuous functions to the category of abelian groups sending a continuous function

to the group homomorphism

f_{\ast}\colon M(Z_{1},\mathfrak{{a}})\to M(Z_{2},\mathfrak{{a}});\hskip 5.69046ptf_{\ast}(\nu)(U)=\nu(f^{-1}(U)).

In particular an action of group

Remark 2.1.5

Lemma 2.1.6

Let

Consider the multiplication-by-

Let

Let

Let

3.1.
Line bundles with flat connection on smooth rigid spaces

Let
X
be a non-empty, smooth rigid

-analytic space. By a
line bundle with flat connection
we mean a
\mathcal{D}
-module
\mathscr{L}
on

which is invertible as an

-module.

If

\mathscr{M}

\mathscr{L}^{\otimes-1}
via the rule

(v\cdot f)(\ell)=v\cdot f(\ell)-f(v\cdot\ell)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad v\in\mathcal{T}_{X},f\in\mathscr{L}^{\otimes-1},v\in\mathscr{L}.

Definition 3.1.1
.

(1)

\PicCon(X)
denotes the abelian group of isomorphism classes of line bundles with flat connection on
X
under the operation
-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}
-.

(2)

\Con(X):=\ker(\PicCon(X)\to\Pic(X))
denotes the group of isomorphism classes of line bundles with flat connection on
X
that are trivial after forgetting the connection.

We now show that when
X
is connected,
\mathscr{L}
is a simple
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module for any
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon(X)
. We start with the case where
X
is
K
-affinoid.

Lemma 3.1.2
.

Suppose that
X
is a connected
K
-affinoid variety such that
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Then for every
[\mathscr{L}]\in\Con(X)
,
\mathscr{L}(X)
is a simple
\mathcal{D}(X)
-module.

Proof.

Suppose that
n=\dim X
. Following the formalism of
[
22
, §1]
we see that
\mathcal{O}(X)
satisfies the conditions found in §1.1.2 of
loc. cit.
Let
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
denote a free generating set for
\mathcal{T}(X)
as a
\mathcal{O}(X)
-module so we may consider

\mathcal{D}(X)=\mathcal{O}(X)[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}]

as a filtered
K
-algebra with associated graded ring

\gr\mathcal{D}(X)\cong\mathcal{O}(X)[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]

with
\mathcal{O}(X)
in degree
0
and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
in degree
1
, the principal symbols of
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
respectively.

The filtration of
\mathscr{L}(X)
whose
0
th filtered part is
\mathscr{L}(X)
and whose
-1
st filtered part is
0
is a good filtration, so that

\gr\mathscr{L}(X)\cong\mathcal{O}(X)[T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}]/(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}).

Since
X
is also connected,
\mathcal{O}(X)
is an integral domain by the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
. Thus any non-zero proper
\mathcal{D}(X)
-module quotient of
\mathscr{L}(X)
must have dimension
<n
. However, by
[
22
, Théorème 1.1.4, Corollaire 1.2.3]
, no such
\mathcal{D}(X)
-module can exist and so
\mathscr{L}(X)
is a simple
\mathcal{D}(X)
-module as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.1.3
.

Suppose
X
is connected and that
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon(X)
. Then
\mathscr{L}
is simple as a
\mathcal{D}
-module.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{L}
is a line bundle, there is an admissible cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
consisting of
K
-affinioid subdomains such that the line bundle
\mathscr{L}|_{U}
is trivial for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
. By passing to a refinement, we may also assume that for each
U\in\mathcal{U}
,
U
is connected and that
\mathcal{T}|_{U}
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module.

Suppose that
\mathscr{M}
is a subobject of
\mathscr{L}
as a
\mathcal{D}
-module and consider

\mathcal{V}_{1}:=\{U\in\mathcal{U}:\mathscr{M}(U)=\mathscr{L}(U)\}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{V}_{2}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}:\mathscr{M}(U)=0\}.

Then
\mathcal{U}
is the disjoint union of
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
by Lemma
3.1.2
. Now if
U\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}_{2}
with
U\cap V\neq\emptyset
, then

\mathcal{L}(U\cap V)\cong\mathcal{O}(U\cap V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(U)}\mathscr{M}(U)\cong\mathscr{M}(U\cap V)\cong\mathcal{O}(U\cap V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}(V)}\mathscr{M}(V)=0,

a contradiction. Since
X
is connected if follows that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}_{1}
or
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{V}_{2}
. Hence
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{L}
or
\mathscr{M}=0
as required.
∎

Whenever
\mathscr{M}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module on
X
, we have at our disposal the
K
-vector space of
global horizontal sections

\mathscr{M}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}:=\{m\in\mathscr{M}(X):\partial\cdot m=0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\partial\in\mathcal{T}(X)\}.

Similarly, when
x\in X
, we have the vector space of
local horizontal sections

\mathscr{M}_{X,x}^{\mathcal{T}_{X,x}=0}:=\{m\in\mathscr{M}_{X,x}:\partial\cdot m=0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\partial\in\mathcal{T}_{X,x}\}.

These vector spaces are related as follows.

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a
\mathcal{D}
-module on
X
.

(1)

There is an injective
K
-linear map

\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{O},\mathscr{M})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}

given by
\varphi\mapsto\varphi(X)(1)
. This map is functorial in
\mathscr{M}
and commutes with restriction to admissible open subspaces
U
of
X
.

(2)

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Then the map in part (a) is an isomorphism, and for every
x\in X
, restriction sends
\mathscr{M}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
into
\mathscr{M}_{X,x}^{\mathcal{T}_{X,x}=0}
.

Proof.

(a) Recall that for any
\mathcal{O}
-module
\mathfrak{F}
on
any
ringed space
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{O})
, the rule
\varphi\mapsto\varphi(\mathfrak{X})(1)
gives a functorial bijection
\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O},\mathfrak{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{F}(\mathfrak{X})
, whose inverse sends the global section
f\in\mathfrak{F}(\mathfrak{X})
to the
\mathcal{O}
-linear map
r_{f}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathfrak{F}
sending
a\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{U})
to
a\cdot f|_{\mathfrak{U}}
. In our setting, this bijection sends the
\mathcal{D}
-linear homomorphisms to the global horizontal sections, because
1\in\mathcal{O}(X)
is killed by
\mathcal{T}(X)
. The first statement follows, and the second is clear.

(b) Suppose that
f\in\mathscr{M}(X)
is such that
\mathcal{T}(X)\cdot f=0
. Then
\mathcal{T}(U)\cdot f|_{U}=0
for all admissible open
U\subseteq X
because
\mathcal{T}
is generated by global sections. It follows that the map
r_{f}(U):\mathcal{O}(U)\to\mathscr{M}(U)
is
\mathcal{D}(U)
-linear, and hence
r_{f}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{O},\mathscr{M})
.

The second statement holds because global horizontal sections of
\mathscr{M}
restrict to local horizontal sections of
\mathscr{M}
whenever
\mathcal{T}
is generated by global sections.
∎

Before we can proceed, we recall some well-known facts about the local structure of smooth rigid spaces.

Lemma 3.1.5
.

Let
K(x):=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}
denote the residue field of
x\in X
.

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is a regular local ring.

(2)

There exist local parameters
t_{1},\cdots,t_{d}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
and derivations
v_{1},\cdots,v_{d}\in\mathcal{T}_{X,x}
such that
v_{i}(t_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
for all
i,j=1,\cdots,d
.

(3)

The
\mathfrak{m}_{X,x}
-adic completion
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is isomorphic to the formal power series ring
K(x)[[t_{1},\cdots,t_{d}]]
.

(4)

The natural map
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}^{\mathcal{T}_{X,x}=0}\to K(x)
is an isomorphism.

(5)

The restriction map
\mathcal{O}(X)\to\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
is injective whenever
X
is connected.

Proof.

(a) This follows from the smoothness of
X
.

(b) This follows from the proof of
[
15
, Theorem A.5.1]
.

(c) Since
K
is a field of characteristic zero, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is equi-characteristic. Hence it admits a coefficient field by
[
21
, Theorem 28.3]
. The result now follows by using part (a) together with
[
4
, Theorem 11.22]
.

(d) This follows immediately from (b) and (c).

(e) Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)
maps to zero in
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is a regular local ring by (a), it embeds into
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
by
[
4
, Corollary 10.19]
, and hence the germ
f_{x}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
of
f
at
x
is zero. Hence there exists an affinoid subdomain
U
of
X
containing
x
such that
f|_{U}=0
. Let
Z=V(f\mathcal{O}_{X})
denote the closed
K
-analytic subspace of
X
cut out by
f
; then
U\subseteq Z
. Since
X
is smooth, it is normal in the sense of
[
10
]
. Since it is also connected,
[
10
, Lemma 2.1.4]
ensures that
Z=X
. Since
X
is reduced, it follows that
f=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.1.6
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Then

\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}=K.

Proof.

Pick a point
x\in X
and consider the completion
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
at
x
. Since
X
is connected, the restriction map
\mathcal{O}(X)\to\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
is injective by Lemma
3.1.5
(e).
This restriction map sends the
K
-subalgebra
L:=\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
of
\mathcal{O}(X)
into
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}^{\mathcal{T}_{X,x}=0}
by Lemma
3.1.4
(b), which is isomorphic to the residue field
K(x)
by Lemma
3.1.5
(d). Since
K(x)
is finite dimensional over
K
, we see that
L
is a finite field extension of
K
. If
L
was a proper field extension of
K
, then base changing
X
to
L
would yield non-trivial idempotents in
L\otimes L\subset\mathcal{O}(X)\otimes L=\mathcal{O}(X_{L})
and show that
X_{L}
is not connected. Since
X
was assumed to be geometrically connected, we conclude that
L=\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
is in fact equal to
K
.
∎

As a consequence, we can characterise the trivial line bundle with trivial connection in terms of its horizontal sections under certain assumptions on
X
.

Corollary 3.1.7
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Then for every
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon(X)
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathscr{L}
is the trivial line bundle with trivial flat connection,

(2)

\dim_{K}\mathscr{L}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}=1
,

(3)

\mathscr{L}
has a non-zero global horizontal section.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). This follows from Proposition
3.1.6
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). This is clear.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). By Lemma
3.1.4
(b), there is a non-zero
\mathcal{D}
-linear map
\mathcal{O}\to\mathscr{L}
. Since
X
is connected and since
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathscr{L}
are both simple
\mathcal{D}
-modules by Proposition
3.1.3
, this map must be an isomorphism.
∎

Proposition
3.1.6
also implies that an isomorphism between two line bundles with flat connection is unique up to scalars.

Corollary 3.1.8
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:\mathscr{L}_{1}\to\mathscr{L}_{2}
be two isomorphisms between two line bundles with flat connection on
X
. Then there is a scalar
\lambda\in K^{\times}
such that
\varphi_{2}=\lambda\varphi_{1}
.

Proof.

By tensoring
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
by
\mathscr{L}_{2}^{\otimes-1}
we may assume that
\mathscr{L}_{2}=\mathcal{O}
. But then
\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}^{-1}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism, so by Lemma
3.1.4
(a) and Proposition
3.1.6
, it is given by multiplication by a non-zero scalar in
K
.
∎

Proposition 3.1.9
.

Suppose that
(X_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
is an increasing admissible covering of
X
by geometrically connected affinoid subdomains.

(1)

X
is also geometrically connected.

(2)

If
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections, then the restriction maps
\PicCon(X)\to\PicCon(X_{n})
induce a natural isomorphism

\PicCon(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}\PicCon(X_{n}).

Proof.

(a) By base-changing to
\mathbf{C}
, it is enough to show that
X
is connected. If it isn’t, then we can find two non-empty families of non-empty admissible open subsets
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
of
X
, such that
\mathcal{U}\cup\mathcal{V}
is an admissible covering of
X
and

\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}}U\cap\bigcup_{V\in\mathcal{V}}V=\emptyset.

Since the admissible covering
(X_{n})
was assumed to be increasing, we can find
n\geqslant 0
,
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
X_{n}\cap U
and
X_{n}\cap V
are both non-empty. But then
\{U\cap X_{n}:U\in\mathcal{U}\}
and
\{V\cap X_{n}:V\in\mathcal{V}\}
together form an admissible cover of
X_{n}
that disconnects it, giving the required contradiction.

(b) Suppose that
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon(X)
is such that
[\mathscr{L}|_{X_{n}}]=0
in
\PicCon(X_{n})
for all
n
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections,
\mathscr{L}^{\mathcal{T}=0}
is a subsheaf of
\mathscr{L}
with
\mathscr{L}(X_{n})^{\mathcal{T}=0}=K
for all
n
by Proposition
3.1.6
. It follows that all restriction maps
\mathscr{L}(X_{n+1})^{\mathcal{T}=0}\to\mathscr{L}(X_{n})^{\mathcal{T}=0}
are isomorphisms. Hence by the sheaf condition on
\mathscr{L}^{\mathcal{T}=0}
,
\mathscr{L}(X)^{\mathcal{T}=0}=K
. Using part (a) together with Corollary
3.1.7
, we now see that
[\mathscr{L}]=0\in\PicCon(X)
.

To see that the homomorphism is also surjective we consider a family of line bundles with connection
[\mathscr{L}_{n}]_{n\geqslant 0}\in\prod_{n\geqslant 0}\PicCon(X_{n})
such that

[\mathscr{L}_{n}|_{X_{m}}]=[\mathscr{L}_{m}]\mbox{ in }\PicCon(X_{m})\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad n\geqslant m.

Choose isomorphisms of
\mathcal{D}
-modules
\varphi_{m}\colon\mathscr{L}_{m+1}|_{X_{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{L}_{m}
for all
m\geqslant 0
. Then whenever
n\geqslant m
we can define an isomorphism of sheaves
\varphi_{n,m}\colon\mathscr{L}_{n}|_{X_{m}}\to\mathscr{L}_{m}
by

\varphi_{n,m}:=\varphi_{n-1}|_{X_{m}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{m}|_{X_{m}}.

Then the construction in
[
13
, §4.4]
gives a sheaf
\mathscr{L}
of
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
X
together with isomorphisms of
\mathcal{D}
-modules
\mathscr{L}|_{X_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{L}_{n}
. Because each
\mathscr{L}(X_{n})
is free of rank
1
over
\mathcal{O}(X_{n})
, we see that
\mathscr{L}
must be a line bundle on
X
. Hence
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon(X)
is the required preimage of
[\mathscr{L}_{n}]\in\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}\PicCon(X_{n})
.∎

The following result will also be useful.

Corollary 3.1.10
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Then for every positive integer
d
, the abelian group
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}/K^{\times}
has no
d
-torsion.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
with
f^{d}\in K^{\times}
. Then for every
\partial\in\mathcal{T}(X)
we see that
0=\partial(f^{d})=df^{d-1}\partial(f)
. Since
df^{d-1}\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
it follows that
\partial(f)=0
. Thus
f\in K^{\times}
by Proposition
3.1.6
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.11
.

Suppose that a group
G
acts on
X
. Then
G
acts naturally on
\PicCon(X)
by abelian group automorphisms via

g\cdot[\mathscr{L}]=[g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}]

where
g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module via the ring isomorphsim
(g^{-1})^{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}g_{\ast}\mathcal{D}
.

Moreover
\Con(X)
is a
G
-stable subgroup of
\PicCon(X)
.

Proof.

We can check that
g\cdot([\mathscr{L}][\mathscr{M}])=(g\cdot[\mathscr{L}])(g\cdot[\mathscr{M}])
for any
[\mathscr{L}],[\mathscr{M}]\in\PicCon(X)
, because
g_{\ast}(\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathscr{M})
is naturally isomorphic to
(g_{\ast}\mathscr{L})\otimes_{g_{\ast}\mathcal{O}}(g_{\ast}\mathscr{M})
as a
g_{\ast}\mathcal{D}
-module.

Since
g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}
is trivial as a line bundle whenever
\mathscr{L}
is trivial as a line bundle the last part is immediate. ∎

Remark 3.1.12
.

We note more generally, in the context of Lemma
3.1.11
, that if
U
is an admissible open subspace of
X
, each
g\in G
induces a group isomorphism
\PicCon(U)\to\PicCon(g\cdot U)
;
[\mathscr{L}]\mapsto[g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}]
where again
g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}
is a
\mathcal{D}_{gU}
-module via the ring isomorphism
(g^{-1})^{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}_{gU}\to g_{\ast}\mathcal{D}_{U}
. Moreover these restrict to isomorphisms
\Con(U)\to\Con(g\cdot U)
.

We will now construct some connections on the trivial line bundle by using units.

Lemma 3.1.13
.

Suppose that
d
is a positive integer and
u\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
. Then there is a unique element
[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}]
of
\Con(X)
such that
\mathscr{L}_{u,d}
has a free generator
v
as a
\mathcal{O}
-module with
\partial(v)=\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u)}{u}v
for all
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}v
is a line bundle with a flat connection satisfying

\partial(v)=\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u)}{u}v

for all
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
. For all
f\in\mathcal{O}
and
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
, necessarily

\partial(fv)=\left(\partial(f)+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u)}{u}f\right)v

so as
\mathcal{D}
is generated by
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{T}
there is at most one element of
\Con(X)
with the property given in the statement. To prove the existence of such a line bundle with flat connection it suffices to show that for all
\partial_{1},\partial_{2}\in\mathcal{T}

(\partial_{1}\partial_{2}-\partial_{2}\partial_{1})(v)=[\partial_{1}\partial_{2}](v)

where
[--]
denotes the Lie bracket on
\mathcal{T}
.

But

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\left(\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial_{2}(u)}{u}v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\partial_{1}\left(\frac{\partial_{2}(u)}{u}\right)v+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial_{2}(u)}{u}\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial_{1}(u)}{u}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\frac{u\partial_{1}\partial_{2}(u)-\partial_{2}(u)\partial_{1}(u)}{u^{2}}v+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial_{2}(u)}{u}\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial_{1}(u)}{u}v

and so

(\partial_{1}\partial_{2}-\partial_{2}\partial_{1})(v)=\frac{1}{d}\frac{[\partial_{1}\partial_{2}](u)}{u}v

as required.
∎

Proposition 3.1.14
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. For every non-zero integer
d
, there is a homomorphism of abelian groups

\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}\to\Con(X)
given by
u\mapsto[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}]
. The kernel of this homomorphism is
K^{\times}\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times d}
and its image is
\Con(X)[d]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
. Then if, for
i=1,2
,
v_{i}
generates
\mathscr{L}_{u_{i},d}
with
\partial(v_{i})=\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u_{i})}{u_{i}}
,
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
is a generator of
\mathscr{L}_{u_{1},d}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{u_{2},d}
and

\displaystyle\partial(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial(v_{1})\otimes v_{2}+v_{1}\otimes\partial(v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{\partial(u_{1})}{u_{1}}+\frac{\partial(u_{2})}{u_{2}}\right)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u_{1}u_{2})}{u_{1}u_{2}}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})

for all
\partial\in\mathcal{T}
. Thus
u\mapsto[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}]
does define a homomorphism.

Note that
u\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
is in the kernel of the homomorphism if and only if
[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}]=[\mathcal{O}.v]=[\mathcal{O}]
in
\Con(X)
. That is,
u
is in the kernel if and only if there is
w\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
such that
\partial(wv)=0
for all
\partial\in\mathcal{T}(X)
. But

\partial(wv)=\left(\frac{\partial(w)}{w}+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\partial(u)}{u}\right)wv.

Now

\frac{\partial(uw^{d})}{uw^{d}}=\frac{\partial(u)}{u}+d\frac{\partial(w)}{w}

so
\partial(wv)=0
if and only if
\partial(uw^{d})=0
. Thus by Proposition
3.1.6
, the kernel is precisely
K^{\times}\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times d}
as claimed. Moreover
[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}^{\otimes d}]=[\mathscr{L}_{u^{d},d}]=[\mathcal{O}]
so each
[\mathscr{L}_{u,d}]
is indeed
d
-torsion.

Given
[\mathscr{L}]\in\Con(X)[d]
, we use the hypothesis that
\mathscr{L}
is trivial as a line bundle to pick a generator
v\in\mathscr{L}(X)
as an
\mathcal{O}(X)

Proposition 3.1.16
.

Suppose that
X
is geometrically connected and that
\mathcal{T}
is generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module by its global sections. Let

be a group acting on

. Then for every non-zero integer

,
\theta_{d}
is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

\theta_{d}\colon\Con(X)[d]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}/K^{\times}\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times d}.

Proof.

Let

,
[\mathscr{L}]\in\Con(X)[d]
and fix a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism
\psi:\mathscr{L}^{\otimes d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}
. The map

is a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism, so

is also a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism. After identifying

with
(g_{\ast}\mathscr{L})^{\otimes d}
and

with
\mathcal{O}
, we obtain a

-linear isomorphism

, where
\mathcal{D}
acts on

via the ring isomorphism

becomes an
\mathcal{D}

as follows: let

be such that

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a coherent
\mathcal{O}
-module on
X
. Suppose that
\{g^{\mathscr{M}}\}_{g\in G}
is a
G
-equivariant structure on

. Then for every affinoid subdomain

of

and

is continuous with respect to canonical
K
-Banach topologies on the domain and codomain.

Proof.

Let
U
be an affinoid subdomain of
X
. By Kiehl’s Theorem — see, e.g.
[

is a finitely generated module over the affinoid algebra
\mathcal{O}(U)
because

is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module. Recalling from
[

that every finitely generated module

over an affinoid algebra

-Banach-space topology, we see that
\mathscr{M}(U)
carries a canonical
K
-Banach space topology. Fixing

as an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module via a twisted action, by defining

for all

and

-module, and the structure map

is now a
\mathcal{O}(U)
-linear homomorphism between two finitely generated

-modules. It is therefore automatically continuous by
[

, Corollary 1.2.4]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Suppose that

Let

for all

gives a well-defined

on

Suppose that

is any

-Banach space. Then the

and
\mathcal{T}(X)\cdot v=0
for some
v\in\mathscr{L}(X)
, then
\partial\cdot(g\cdot v)=g\cdot((g^{-1}\cdot\partial)\cdot v)=0
for all
\partial\in\mathcal{T}(X)
so that
g\cdot v\in\mathscr{L}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
again.

Suppose that
\chi\in\Hom(G,K^{\times})
is such that
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}
as a
G
-equivariant line bundle with flat connection. Considering the global horizontal sections, we obtain an isomorphism of continuous
G
-representations

\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}\quad\cong\quad\mathcal{O}_{\chi}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}.

By Proposition
3.1.6
, both of these
K
-vector spaces are
1
-dimensional and spanned by
1\in\mathcal{O}(X)
. However the
G
-action on the first is trivial, whereas the
G
-action on the second is through the character
\chi
. Hence
\chi
is the trivial character, and the map
\Hom(G,K^{\times})\to\PicCon^{G}(X)
is injective.

If
\mathscr{L}
is
G
-equivariant, then
(g^{-1})^{\mathscr{L}}:\mathscr{L}\to g_{\ast}\mathscr{L}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism for all
g\in G
which means that the class
[\mathscr{L}]
in
\PicCon(X)
is fixed by this
G
-action i.e. the image of the map
\PicCon^{G}(X)\to\PicCon(X)
is indeed contained in
\PicCon(X)^{G}
.

Finally suppose that
\mathscr{L}
is a
G
-equivariant line bundle with flat connection on
X
which becomes trivial after forgetting the
G
-structure. Then we can find a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{O}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{L}
. Applying the functor of global horizontal sections, we deduce from Proposition
3.1.6
that the
K
-linear

-representation
\mathscr{L}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
is in fact one-dimensional. Let
v:=\varphi(X)(1)\in\mathscr{L}(X)^{\mathcal{T}(X)=0}
; since
1\in\mathcal{O}(X)
generates
\mathcal{O}
as an
\mathcal{O}
-module, we see that
v\in\mathscr{L}(X)
generates
\mathscr{L}
as an
\mathcal{O}
-module:
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}\cdot v
. Let
\chi\in\Hom(G,K^{\times})
describe the
G
-action on

, so that
g\cdot v=\chi(g)v
for all
g\in G
. This gives us a character
\chi:G\to K^{\times}
; we will first show that
\chi
is continuous. To see this, choose a non-empty affinoid subdomain
U
of
X
and let
\mathcal{A}
be an affine formal model in
\mathcal{O}(U)
, so that
\mathcal{A}\cdot v_{|U}
is the unit ball in
\mathscr{L}(U)=\mathcal{O}(U)\cdot v_{|U}
with respect to some
K
-Banach norm defining the canonical topology on
\mathscr{L}(U)
. By Definition
3.2.3
(b), for any
n\geqslant 0
we can find an open subgroup
H_{n}
of
G_{U}
such that
h\cdot v_{|U}\equiv v_{|U}\mod\pi_{F}^{n}\mathcal{A}\cdot v_{|U}
for all

. Hence
\chi(h)\equiv 1\mod\pi_{F}^{n}K^{\circ}
for all

, and
\chi:G\to K^{\times}
is therefore continuous as claimed.

With
\chi\in\Hom(G,K^{\times})
in hand, consider the
G
-equivariant line bundle with connection
\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}_{\chi^{-1}},
and the
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism
\varphi_{\chi}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}_{\chi^{-1}}
given by
\varphi_{\chi}(f):=\varphi(f)\otimes 1
. We claim that
\varphi_{\chi}
is
G
-equivariant; given this claim, it follows immediately that
\mathscr{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\chi}
as a
G
-equivariant
\mathcal{D}
-module, and then
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon^{G}(X)
lies in the image of
\Hom(G,K^{\times})
as required.

To establish the claim, we first check that
\varphi_{\chi}(X):\mathcal{O}(X)\to(\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\chi^{-1}})(X)
is

-equivariant: for

and

g\cdot\varphi_{\chi}(X)(f)=g\cdot(fv\otimes 1)=(g\cdot f)(g\cdot v)\otimes\chi^{-1}(g)=(g\cdot f)v\otimes 1=\varphi_{\chi}(X)(g\cdot f).

By replacing
\mathscr{L}
by
\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\chi^{-1}}
, it now remains to show that if
\varphi:\mathcal{O}\to\mathscr{L}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-linear isomorphism such that

is

is also

-equivariant. To this end, fix

, and consider the morphisms
g^{\ast}(\varphi)\circ g^{\mathcal{O}}:\mathcal{O}\to g^{\ast}\mathscr{L}
and

. By precomposing the

-module structure on

with the ring isomorphism

we may regard

to be an
\mathcal{O}
-module. Then it is coherent, and the two maps are

-linear. Since they have the same global sections by our computation above and since

that the two maps are equal. This means that

-equivariant.
∎

In order to understand
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)
the following result will prove to be useful.

Proposition 3.2.15
.

Suppose that
X
is quasi-Stein and geometrically connected and
G
is equal to an amalgamated product
A\ast_{C}B
of its open subgroups
A
and
B
along their common subgroup
C
. Then the homomorphism

(p_{A},p_{B})\colon\PicCon^{G}(X)\to\PicCon^{A}(X)\underset{\PicCon^{C}(X)}{\times}{}\PicCon^{B}(X)

induced by restriction of equivariant structures is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon^{G}(X)
lies in the kernel of
(p_{A},p_{B})
. Then

\omega([\mathscr{L}])=[\mathcal{O}]\in\PicCon(X).

By Proposition
3.2.14
,
[\mathscr{L}]=[\mathcal{O}^{\chi}]
for some
\chi\in\Hom(G,K^{\times})
. However, also by Proposition
3.2.14
,
\chi|_{A}
and
\chi|_{B}
are both trivial and so, as
G
is generated by
A
and
B
,
\chi
is trivial. Thus
[\mathscr{L}]=[\mathcal{O}]\in\PicCon^{G}(X)
and
(p_{A},p_{B})
is injective.

Suppose now that
[\mathscr{L}_{A}]\in\PicCon^{A}(X)
,
[\mathscr{L}_{B}]\in\PicCon^{B}(X)
and that there is an isomorphism
\theta\colon\mathscr{L}_{A}|_{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{L}_{B}|_{C}
of
C
-equivariant line bundles with flat connection obtained by restriction of equivariant structures from
A
and
B
to
C
. We transport the
B
-equivariant structure on
\mathscr{L}_{B}
along
\theta
to
\mathscr{L}_{A}
. In this way, the
\mathcal{D}
-module
\mathscr{L}:=\underline{\omega}(\mathscr{L}_{A})
can be equipped with
A
-equivariant and
B
-equivariant structures whose restrictions to
C
agree.

By Proposition
2.4.11
, for every subgroup
H
of
G
there is a bijection between the set of all
H-\mathcal{D}
-module structures on
\mathscr{L}
extending the given
\mathcal{D}
-module structure on
\mathscr{L}
, and the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(H,\mathscr{L})
. This bijection is given by

\{g^{\mathscr{L}}\}_{g\in H}\mapsto[g\mapsto(g,g^{\mathscr{L}})\in\Aut_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{L}/X/H)].

On the other hand, by Theorem
2.4.13
restriction induces a bijection

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(G,\mathscr{L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(A,\mathscr{L})\times_{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(C,\mathscr{L})}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}}(B,\mathscr{L}).

It follows that there exists a
G-\mathcal{D}
-module
\mathscr{L}_{G}=(\mathscr{L},\{g^{\mathscr{L}}\}_{g\in G})
whose restriction to
A
is
\mathscr{L}_{A}
, and whose restriction to

is the transport of
\mathscr{L}_{B}
to
\mathscr{L}_{A}
along
\theta
. Since
A
and
B
are open in

, the action map
G_{U}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{L}(U))^{\times}
is continuous for every affinoid subdomain
U
of
X
, because the restrictions of this map to both

and
B_{U}
are continuous. This shows that
[\mathscr{L}_{G}]\in\PicCon^{G}(X)
. By construction, we have
p_{A}([\mathscr{L}_{G}])=[\mathscr{L}_{A}]
and
p_{B}([\mathscr{L}_{G}])=[\mathscr{L}_{B}]
. Hence
(p_{A},p_{B})
is surjective. ∎

Lemma 3.2.16
.

If
X
is a geometrically connected quasi-Stein space with an increasing admissible covering
(X_{n})
by
G
-stable affinoid subdomains, then the restriction maps
\PicCon^{G}(X)\to\PicCon^{G}(X_{n})
induce an isomorphism of groups

\PicCon^{G}(X)\cong\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}\PicCon^{G}(X_{n}).

Proof.

Certainly the restriction maps induce a group homomorphism

\alpha\colon\PicCon^{G}(X)\to\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}\PicCon^{G}(X_{n}).

By Proposition
3.1.9
, if
[\mathscr{L}]\in\ker\alpha
then
\omega([\mathscr{L}])=[\mathcal{O}_{X}]\in\PicCon(X)
. Thus
[\mathscr{L}]=[\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\chi}]
for some continuous character
\chi\colon G\to K^{\times}
by Proposition
3.2.14
. But then
[\mathscr{L}|_{X_{n}}]=[\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}^{\chi}]
for any
n\geqslant 0
and so
\chi
is the trivial character since
\alpha([\mathscr{L}])
is trivial. Thus
\alpha
is injective.

Let
([\mathscr{L}_{n}])\in\prod\PicCon^{G}(X_{n})
be a compatible family of isomorphism classes of equivariant line bundles with connection under restriction so that for each
n
we can find an isomorphism

\theta_{n+1,n}\colon\mathscr{L}_{n+1}|_{X_{n}}\to\mathscr{L}_{n}

of
G
-equivariant line bundles with connection on
X_{n}
.

Thus each
l\geqslant m
, we can define
\theta_{m,l}\colon\mathscr{L}_{l}|_{X_{m}}\to\mathscr{L}_{m}
to be the composite of the restrictions of
\theta_{l,l-1},\theta_{l-1,l-2},\ldots,\theta_{m+1,m}
to
X_{m}
. Now
(\mathscr{L}_{l},\theta_{m,l})
forms gluing data for the cover
(X_{m})_{m\geqslant n}
of
X
. The resulting sheaf
\mathscr{L}
is an
G
-equivariant line bundle with connection on
X
with
\alpha([\mathscr{L}])=([\mathscr{L}_{n}])
and so
\alpha
is surjective.
∎

3.3.
Cocycles and equivariant line bundles on affinoids

In this technical subsection, we will explain how isomorphism classes of
G
-equivariant structures on the trivial line bundle over a
K
-affinoid variety
X
can be classified through the language of continuous
1
-cocycles of
G
acting on
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
. This material will be crucial to the proof of one of the main results in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{MainProofSec}
, namely Theorem
4.4.1
.

We assume throughout
\lx@sectionsign\ref{SecCocycELBC}
that
X
is a smooth and geometrically connected
K
-
affinoid
space, with a topological group
G
acting continuously on it.

Lemma 3.3.1
.

(1)

The set of
G
-equivariant structures on a trivial line bundle
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}\cdot v
is in natural bijection with
Z^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times})
under a function that sends
\{g^{\mathscr{L}}:g\in G\}
to the function
\alpha:G\to\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
determined by the rule

g^{\mathscr{L}}(v)=\alpha(g)v\quad\mbox{for all}\quad g\in G.

(2)

The bijection in (a) induces an isomorphism

\theta^{G}_{X}:\ker\left(\Pic^{G}(X)\to\Pic(X)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}).

Proof.

(a) Suppose that for each
g\in G
, we have a morphism of sheaves of
K
-vector spaces
g^{\mathscr{L}}:\mathscr{L}\to g^{\ast}\mathscr{L}
such that

g^{\mathscr{L}}(fv)=g^{\mathcal{O}}(f)g^{\mathscr{L}}(v)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad g\in G,f\in\mathcal{O}.

This data is completely determined by the function
\alpha\colon G\to\mathcal{O}(X)
given by

g^{\mathscr{L}}(v)=\alpha(g)v\quad\mbox{for all}\quad g\in G.

We first claim that
\{g^{\mathscr{L}}:g\in G\}
is a
G
-equivariant structure on
\mathscr{L}
if and only only
\alpha
is a
1
-cocycle with values in the group
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
.

We see that for all
g,h\in G

(8)

\displaystyle h^{\ast}(g^{\mathscr{L}})h^{\mathscr{L}}(v)=h^{\ast}(g^{\mathscr{L}})(\alpha(h)v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g\cdot\alpha(h))\alpha(g)v\mbox{ and}

(9)

\displaystyle(gh)^{\mathscr{L}}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(gh)v.

Now if
\{g^{\mathscr{L}}:g\in G\}
defines a
G
-equivariant structure on
\mathscr{L}
then
\alpha(1)=1
and
h^{\ast}(g^{\mathscr{L}})h^{\mathscr{L}}(v)=(gh)^{\mathscr{L}}(v)
. Thus by (
8
) and (
9
)
\alpha(gh)=(g\cdot\alpha(h))\alpha(g)
for all
g,h\in G
and in particular

1=\alpha(gg^{-1})=(g\cdot\alpha(g^{-1}))\alpha(g)

for all
g\in G
. Thus
\alpha
is a
1
-cocycle with values in
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
. Conversely, if
\alpha
is a
1
-cocycle then by (
8
) and (
9
) again, for all
g,h\in G
,

(gh)^{\mathscr{L}}(v)=h^{\ast}(g^{\mathscr{L}})h^{\mathscr{L}}(v)\mbox{ and so }

(gh)^{\mathscr{L}}=h^{\ast}(g^{\mathscr{L}})h^{\mathscr{L}}.

Moreover
\alpha(1^{2})=\alpha(1)^{2}
and so, since
X
is connected,
\alpha(1)=1
and
1^{\mathscr{L}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{L}}
.

It remains to observe that
\alpha
is continuous if and only if for every affinoid subdomain
U
of
X
, the action map
G_{U}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{L}(U))^{\times}
is continuous. This holds because

g\cdot(fv)=g^{\mathcal{O}}(f)\alpha(g)v\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad g\in G_{U},f\in\mathcal{O}(U)

and because
G
acts continuously on
X
.

(b) Suppose now that
\mathscr{L}_{1}=\mathcal{O}\cdot v_{1}
and
\mathscr{L}_{2}=\mathcal{O}\cdot v_{2}
are two
G
-equivariant line bundles corresponding to
1
-cocycles
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
respectively.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathscr{L}_{1}\to\mathscr{L}_{2}
be an isomorphism of the underlying line bundles. Then
\varphi(v_{1})=fv_{2}
for some
f\in\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times}
, so for all
g\in G
we have

\varphi(g^{\mathscr{L}_{1}}(v_{1}))=\varphi(\alpha_{1}(g)v_{1})=\alpha_{1}(g)fv_{2}

whereas

g^{\mathscr{L}_{2}}(\varphi(v_{1}))=g^{\mathscr{L}_{2}}(fv_{2})=g^{\mathcal{O}}(f)\alpha_{2}(g)v_{2}.

Hence
\varphi
defines an isomorphism of
G
-
equivariant
line bundles if and only if

\alpha_{2}(g)=\frac{g^{\mathcal{O}}(f)}{f}\alpha_{1}(g)\quad\mbox{ for all }\quad g\in G.

Thus the map in (a) induces a bijection

\theta^{G}_{X}\colon\ker\left(\Pic^{G}(X)\to\Pic(X)\right)\to H^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times})

which is a group homomorphism because
g\cdot(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=\alpha_{1}(g)v_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2}(g)v_{2}=(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2})(g)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})
for all
g\in G
.
∎

Recall the map
\phi_{z}
from Proposition
3.2.7
; by abuse of notation, we will also denote its pre-composition with the forgetful map
\Con^{G}(X)\to\Pic^{G}(X)
by
\phi_{z}
.

Proposition 3.3.2
.

Suppose that
L
is a finite field extension of
K
.

(1)

The isomorphism from Lemma
3.3.1
(b) induces a homomorphism

\phi^{G}_{X}\colon\Con^{G}(X)\to H^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(X)^{\times})

by pre-composition with the forgetful map

\Con^{G}(X)\to\ker(\Pic^{G}(X)\to\Pic(X)).

(2)

For every

and every

(3)

For every
z\in X(L)
and every

(4)

Let

-stable affinoid subdomain, with

. Then the following diagram is commutative:

\textstyle{\Con^{G}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{(-)|_{Y}}
\textstyle{\Con^{G}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(a) Since the forgetful map

(b) Suppose that

with

(d) If

∎

Definition 3.3.3
.

Recall from
\lx@sectionsign\ref{ConvNotn}

Recall from Lemma

-module on the

.

Let

and let

(1)

For each

, the following are equivalent:

in

(b)

there is

such that

(4)

The map

4.1.
Subdomains of the rigid analytic affine line

We will write
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{A}_{K}:=\mathbb{A}_{K}^{1,\an}
to denote the rigid
K
-analytic affine line, equipped with a fixed choice of local coordinate
x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{A})
. We write
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to denote the rigid
K
-analytic projective line.

Definition 4.1.1
.

A
K
-cheese
is an affinoid subdomain of
\mathbb{A}
of the form

C_{K}(\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{s}):=\Sp K\left\langle\frac{x-\alpha_{0}}{s_{0}},\frac{s_{1}}{x-\alpha_{1}},\cdots,\frac{s_{g}}{x-\alpha_{g}}\right\rangle

for some
\mathbf{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{g})\in K^{g+1}
and
\mathbf{s}:=(s_{0},\ldots,s_{g})\in(K^{\times})^{g+1}
, which satisfy

•

|s_{i}|\leqslant|s_{0}|
for all
i=1,\ldots,g
,

•

|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{0}|\leqslant|s_{0}|
for all
i=1,\ldots,g
, and

•

|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}|\geqslant\max\{|s_{i}|,|s_{j}|\}
whenever
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant g
.

When there is no risk of confusion, we will simplify the notation to
C(\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{s})
.

We call the open discs

\displaystyle D_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\bf{C}}):|z-\alpha_{0}|>|s_{0}|\}\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle D_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}({\bf C}):|z-\alpha_{i}|<|s_{i}|\}\mbox{ for }i=1,\ldots,g

the
holes
of
C(\alpha,\mathbf{s})
and we write

h(C(\alpha,\mathbf{s})):=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{g},D_{\infty}\}

to denote the set of holes of
C(\alpha,\mathbf{s})
.

Of course the
\bf{C}
-points of
C(\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{s})
are obtained by removing the
g+1
holes from
\mathbb{P}^{1}({\bf C})
. The conditions on the parameters
\mathbf{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{s}
are there to ensure that the holes are pairwise disjoint. We also require that
\mathbf{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{s}
are defined over
K
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

(1)

Given two open discs
D_{1},D_{2}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
with
D_{1}\cap D_{2}\neq\emptyset
, it must necessarily be the case that either
D_{1}\subseteq D_{2}
, or
D_{2}\subseteq D_{1}
.

(2)

The union and the intersection of two cheeses
C_{1},C_{2}
are also cheeses, unless
C_{1}\cap C_{2}=\emptyset
.

Lemma 4.1.3
.

Suppose that
X
and
Y
are
K
-cheeses and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a
K
-analytic automorphism such that
\varphi(Y)\subseteq X
. Then there is a unique function

\varphi^{X}_{Y}\colon h(X)\to h(Y)

such that for every

,
\varphi^{X}_{Y}(D)
is the largest hole of
Y
containing
\varphi^{-1}(D)
.

Proof.

By
[
19
, p. 33]
, the automorphism
\varphi
is necessarily a Möbius transformation. Hence
\varphi
, as well as
\varphi^{-1}
, maps open discs in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
to open discs in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})
.

Let

. Then
\varphi^{-1}(D)\cap Y(\mathbf{C})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(D\cap X(\mathbf{C}))=\emptyset
. Thus the open disc
\varphi^{-1}(D)
is contained in the union of the holes of

. Remark

(a) then implies that

is contained in a unique hole
\varphi_{Y}^{X}(D)
of

.
∎

Notation 4.1.4
.

Suppose that
X,Y
are
K
-cheeses with

. We will denote the function

Lemma 4.1.5
.

Suppose that
X
and

are
K
-cheeses with non-empty intersection. Then there is a natural bijection

\iota^{X\cup Y}_{X}\times\iota^{X\cup Y}_{Y}:h(X\cup Y)\quad\longrightarrow\quad h(X)\underset{h(X\cap Y)}{\times}{}h(Y)

given by

Proof.

Note that the map in the statement of the Lemma is well-defined because

\iota^{X}_{X\cap Y}\circ\iota^{X\cup Y}_{X}=\iota^{X\cup Y}_{X\cap Y}=\iota^{Y}_{X\cap Y}\circ\iota^{X\cup Y}_{Y}.

De Morgan’s laws imply that

is precisely the set of non-empty intersections

and

and

. But then

and

, so

or

We now show that

is surjective. Suppose that

and

are such that

. This means that

and

are contained in the same hole

. Since

is a hole of

is the union of the holes of

is a finite union of proper open subdiscs; hence

Returning to the general case, it will now be enough to show that there is some finite extension

of

4.2.
Drinfeld’s upper half-plane

In this section we study
\Omega_{F}
the
Drinfeld upper half plane
, which is a rigid
F
-analytic space whose underlying set consists of the
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-orbits in
\Omega_{F}(\overline{F})=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{F})\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
.

It is straightforward to see that for any finite extension
L
of
F
,
\Omega_{F}(L)
can be identified with
L\backslash F
and we will often silently make this identification. The rigid space
\Omega_{F}
comes naturally equipped with an action of
GL_{2}(F)
by Möbius transformations:

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}

and the same formula induces an action on each set
\Omega_{F}(L)
.

We recall from
[
6
, §I.1.2]
the
GL_{2}(F)
-equivariant reduction map
\lambda\colon\Omega_{F}(\mathbf{C})\to|\mathcal{BT}|
from
\Omega_{F}(\mathbf{C})=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbf{C})\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
to the geometric realisation
|\mathcal{BT}|
of the Bruhat-Tits tree
\mathcal{BT}
associated with
PGL_{2}(F)
. Since
\lambda\circ\sigma=\lambda
for any
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
, this map factors through the rigid
F
-analytic space
\Omega_{F}
, giving us a map

\lambda:\Omega_{F}\to|\mathcal{BT}|.

Any point in
\Omega_{F}
is the
\Gal(\overline{F}/F)
-orbit
[z]
of some
z\in\Omega_{F}(\overline{F})
; then we have
\lambda([z])=\lambda(z)
. We abuse notation and also call
\lambda:\Omega_{F}\to|\mathcal{BT}|
the
reduction map
.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Let
z\in\Omega_{F}(\overline{F})
. Then
GL_{2}(F)_{z}\leqslant GL_{2}(F)_{[z]}\leqslant GL_{2}(F)_{\lambda\left([z]\right)}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the
GL_{2}(F)
-equivariance of
z\mapsto[z]
and
\lambda
.
∎

Proposition 4.2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is a finite subtree of
\mathcal{BT}
. Then
\lambda^{-1}(|\mathcal{T}|)
is an
F
-cheese contained in
\Omega_{F}
.

Proof.

Since the union of two non-disjoint
F
-cheeses is an
F
-cheese and
\mathcal{T}
is connected, by an induction on the number of edges of
\mathcal{T}
, it suffices to prove the result when
\mathcal{T}
is a single vertex or has two vertices connected by an edge. Both of these cases can be deduced from the discussion in
[
6
, §I.2.3]
.
∎

Definition 4.2.3

Lemma 4.2.5

Lemma 4.2.8

(1)

(2)

(3)

(a) If

to the last vertex
s

\mathcal{T}_{2}

(b) Since

\iota^{\mathcal{T}_{2}}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}(e)\not\in E(\mathcal{T}_{1})

∎

Lemma 4.2.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are finite subtrees of
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{T}
such that

E(\mathcal{S})=\{(ss^{\prime})\}\mbox{ and }V(\mathcal{S})\cap V(\mathcal{T})=\{s\}.

There is a natural bijection

\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}:N(\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T})\to N(\mathcal{S})\underset{N(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T})}{\times}{}N(\mathcal{T})

given by
e\mapsto(\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e),\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}(e))
.

Proof.

The map
\xi:=\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}
in the statement is well-defined, because

\iota^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}\circ\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}=\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}=\iota^{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}\circ\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}

by Lemma
4.2.8
(a).

Next we show that
\xi
is injective. To this end, suppose that
e_{1},e_{2}
are two elements of
N(\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T})
such that
\xi(e_{1})=\xi(e_{2})
. Let
v_{i}=s_{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}(e_{i})
for
i=1,2
.
Suppose first that both of
v_{1},v_{2}
lie in
\mathcal{S}
. In this case,
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{1})=e_{1}
and
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{2})=e_{2}
, by Lemma
4.2.8
(b), and we deduce by looking at the first component of
\xi(e_{1})=\xi(e_{2})
that
e_{1}=e_{2}
. The case where both
v_{1},v_{2}
lie in
\mathcal{T}
is entirely similar. Suppose for a contradiction that
e_{1}\neq e_{2}
. Then without loss of generality, we can now assume that
v_{1}
lies in
V(\mathcal{T})\backslash V(\mathcal{S})
and
v_{2}
lies in
V(\mathcal{S})\backslash V(\mathcal{T})
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is a single leaf with
V(\mathcal{S})\cap V(\mathcal{T})=\{s\}
, this forces
v_{2}=s^{\prime}
. Therefore since
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{2})=e_{2}
by Lemma
4.2.8
(b), the only vertex of
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{2})
in
\mathcal{S}
is
s^{\prime}
. On the other hand, because

,
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{1})\in E(\mathcal{T})
by Lemma

(c). This contradicts
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{2})=\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(e_{1})
because

.

Finally we show that
\xi
is surjective. Suppose that
(e,f)\in N(\mathcal{S})\underset{N(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T})}{\times}{}N(\mathcal{T})
.

We first consider the case where
s_{\mathcal{T}}(f)\neq s
, so that

. It follows that

\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}(f)=f

4.2.8
(b) because
s_{\mathcal{T}}(f)\in\mathcal{T}
. Consider the following element

g:=\iota^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(e)=\iota^{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(f).

Since

,

(c). In particular

. Since

(d). Now

\iota^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(h)=h
by Example

(a) several times, we now see that

\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)=h=\iota^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(h)=\iota^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)=\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(f)=\iota^{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{T}}(f)=g.

Hence
\iota^{\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}}(f)=g=e
as required.

Next we consider the case where
s_{\mathcal{T}}(f)=s
so that, by Lemma

(b),
\iota^{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{T}}(f)=f
, and hence

(a,b) and the right vertical map is an inclusion map. Now the injectivity of
q_{2}
follows from the Snake Lemma. Therefore
q_{1}
is also injective, because
q_{2}(w[\mathscr{L}])=wq_{1}([\mathscr{L}])
for every
[\mathscr{L}]\in\PicCon^{B}(\Omega)
.

Finally, by Proposition
4.4.4
,
q_{2}([\mathscr{L}])=q_{1}(w[\mathscr{L}]^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{\det}^{k}})
for some integer
k
. Hence the image of
q_{2}
is contained in the image of
q_{1}
.
∎

Remark 4.4.6
.

One may wonder if it might be possible to strengthen the statement of Corollary
4.4.5
to give a similar description of
all
torsion elements in
\PicCon^{G_{0}}(\Omega)
. However when
q=2
, the restriction map

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\to\PicCon^{GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})}(\Omega)_{\tors}

is not an isomorphism in general, because the homomorphism from the abelianization of
GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
to the abelianization of
G^{0}
induced by inclusion has a kernel of order
2
and so particular the restriction map

\Hom(G^{0},K^{\times})_{\tors}\to\Hom(GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F}),K^{\times})_{\tors}

then may not be surjective.

Next we pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
to deduce the consequences of Theorem
4.4.1
for the
p^{\prime}
-torsion part of our main group of interest, namely
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)
. First we recall the Sylow pro-
p
subgroup
P_{z}
of
SL_{2}(F)_{z}
from Lemma
2.2.5
.

Lemma 4.4.7
.

Suppose that
K
contains the quadratic unramified extension
L
of
F
and let
z\in\Omega_{F,0}(L)
. Then the homomorphism

\phi_{z}[p^{\prime}]\colon\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)[p^{\prime}]\quad\longrightarrow\quad\Hom(G^{0}_{z},K^{\times})[p^{\prime}]

is an isomorphism. Moreover, every
p^{\prime}
-torsion character
\chi:G^{0}_{z}\to K^{\times}
kills
P_{z}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4.2
(a) and Corollary
4.4.5
, restriction maps induce an isomorphism

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)[p^{\prime}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathop{\underset{\scriptstyle}{\underset{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.75346pt][-1.50694pt]{$\longleftarrow$}}{\operatorname{lim}}}}\Con^{GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})}(\Omega_{n})[p^{\prime}].

Using Proposition
3.3.2
(d) together with Theorem
4.4.1
, we deduce that map
\phi_{z}[p^{\prime}]
in the statement of the Lemma is an isomorphism.

The last statement holds because
P_{z}
is a (normal) pro-
p
subgroup of
G^{0}_{z}
.
∎

With the last result in hand, it is natural to wonder about the
p
-torsion part of
\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)
. The following description of this group does not require the full force the methods employed in the proof of Theorem
4.4.1
.

Lemma 4.4.8
.

Suppose that
K
contains the quadratic unramified extension
L
of
F
and let
z\in\Omega_{F}(L)
. The homomorphism

\phi_{z}[p^{\infty}]\colon\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)[p^{\infty}]\quad\longrightarrow\quad\Hom(G^{0}_{z},K^{\times})[p^{\infty}]

is injective with image
\Hom(G^{0}_{z}/P_{z},K^{\times})[p^{\infty}]
.

Proof.

Since
K\supseteq L
and
z\in\Omega_{F}(L)
by assumption, we see that
z\in\Omega(K)
. Hence the map
\phi_{z}:\Pic^{G^{0}}(\Omega)\to\Hom(G^{0}_{z},K^{\times})
exists by Proposition
3.2.7
.

Now consider the following triangle:

\textstyle{\Hom(G^{0},K^{\times})[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\res}
\textstyle{\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)[p^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{z}[p^{\infty}]}
\textstyle{\Hom(G^{0}_{z},K^{\times})[p^{\infty}]}

Here, the horizontal map sends the character
\chi
to
[\mathcal{O}_{\chi}]
, and the diagonal arrow
\res
on the left is restriction of characters. The triangle is commutative by Lemma
3.3.2
(c), and the horizontal arrow is an isomorphism by
Proposition
3.2.14
and Corollary
4.3.8
. Hence it suffices to show that
\res
is injective, and that its image is
\Hom(G^{0}_{z}/P_{z},K^{\times})[p^{\infty}]
.

Note that
SL_{2}(F)
is a perfect subgroup of
G^{0}
and
K^{\times}
is abelian. Hence
\chi|_{SL_{2}(F)}=1
for any character
\chi:G^{0}\to K^{\times}
. In particular,
\res(\chi)
vanishes on the subgroup
P_{z}
of
SL_{2}(F)
. Now if
\chi:G^{0}\to K^{\times}
is a character such that
\res(\chi)=\chi|_{G^{0}_{z}}=1
, then Corollary
2.2.6
immediately implies that
\chi=1
. Therefore
\res
is injective as required.
∎

Proposition 4.4.9
.

Suppose that
K
contains the quadratic unramified extension
L
of
F
. Then for all
g\in GL_{2}(F)
and
z\in\Omega_{F}(L)
, there is a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{z}}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{\Hom(G^{0}_{z}/P_{z},K^{\times})_{\tors}}
\textstyle{\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{g\cdot z}}
\textstyle{\Hom(G^{0}_{g\cdot z}/P_{g\cdot z},K^{\times})_{\tors}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{c_{g}^{\ast}}

whose arrows are all isomorphisms of abelian groups.

Proof.

The diagram commutes by Proposition
3.2.13
(b), and its vertical arrows are isomorphisms with inverses
g^{-1}
and
c_{g^{-1}}^{\ast}
respectively. By Lemma
4.4.7
and Lemma
4.4.8
, the top horizontal arrow is an isomorphism in the case when
z\in\Omega_{F,0}(L)
. But since
L
is quadratic over
F
,
GL_{2}(F)
acts transitively on
\Omega_{F}(L)=L\backslash F
, so we may choose
g\in GL_{2}(F)
such that
g\cdot z\in\Omega_{F,0}(L)
and then
\phi_{g\cdot z}
is an isomorphism. The commutativity of the diagram now ensures that
\phi_{z}
is always an isomorphism.
∎

We can finally give our proof of Theorem A.

Theorem 4.4.10
.

Suppose that
K
contains the quadratic unramified extension
L
of
F
. Then there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\to\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}

that descends to a natural bijection

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}/G\to\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}/D^{\times}.

Proof.

Choose
z\in\Omega_{F}(L)
as well as an
F
-algebra homomorphism
\iota:L\hookrightarrow D
. By Lemma
2.2.5
(d,b,c),
j_{z}(P_{z})
is the Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of
\ker N_{L/F}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}
. In view of Definition
2.3.5
, we see that
j_{z}(P_{z})=P^{1}_{L}
. Now Proposition
4.4.9
together with Lemma
2.2.5
(b) shows that

j_{z}\circ\phi_{z}\colon\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\to\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\times}/P^{1}_{L},K^{\times})_{\tors}

is an isomorphism. We can now post-compose
j_{z}\circ\phi_{z}
with the inverse of the isomorphism
\overline{\varrho\circ\iota}^{\ast}
from Corollary
2.3.7
(a) to obtain the required isomorphism

(\overline{\varrho\circ\iota}^{\ast})^{-1}\circ j_{z}\circ\phi_{z}:\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}.

Although this does depend on the choice of
z\in\Omega(L)
as well as the choice of the
F
-algebra embedding
\iota:L\hookrightarrow D
, using Proposition
4.4.9
, Remark
2.2.7
and Corollary
2.3.7
(c) we see that it descends to a well-defined bijection

\PicCon^{G^{0}}(\Omega)_{\tors}/G\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\quad\Hom(\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times},K^{\times})_{\tors}/D^{\times}

which does not depend on any of the choices.
∎

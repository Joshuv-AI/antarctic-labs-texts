Hodge Theory for Polymatroids Thanks: The first author is supported by PRIN 2017YRA3LK
Roberto Pagaria, Gian Marco Pezzoli

Abstract
. We construct a Leray model for a discrete polymatroid with arbitrary building set and we prove a generalized Goresky-MacPherson formula. The first row of the model is the Chow ring of the polymatroid; we prove Poincaré duality, Hard Lefschetz, and Hodge-Riemann theorems for the Chow ring. Furthermore, we provide a relative Lefschetz decomposition with respect to the deletion of an element.



1.
Introduction

Recently, long standing conjectures about log-concavity of graphs and matroids have been brilliantly solved by studying the Chow ring of matroids
[
Huh12
,
HK12
,
Len13
,
HW17
,
AHK18
,
BHM+20
,
ADH20
,
BEST21
]
.
A natural question is whether the corresponding results hold for polymatroids
[
BEST21
, Question 1.5]
.

Discrete polymatroids
generalize arrangements of subspaces in the same way as matroids generalize hyperplane arrangements.
Moreover polymatroids codify invariants of hypergraphs as matroids do with graphs.
Polymatroids have application in linear programming
[
Edm70
,
Sch03
]
, in code theory
[
GJLR20
]
, and in commutative algebra
[
HH02
]
.
The base polytope of a polymatroids is a
generalized permutahedron
with a vertex in the origin, this yields a criptmorphism between polymatroids and generalized permutahedra
[
Pos09
,
AA17
]
.

The easiest definition of polymatroid is a pair
P=(E,\cd)
where
E
is a finite ground set and
\cd\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{N}
is a
increasing submodular function
.
If
P
is realized by a subspace arrangement, then
\cd
is the codimension of the corresponding flat.

In the case of arrangements of subspaces, De Concini and Procesi
[
DCP95
]
have constructed a
wonderful model
, i.e. a smooth projective variety that contains the complement of the arrangement as open subset.
This wonderful model
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
is obtained from
\mathbb{P}^{r}
by a sequence of blowups along some linear subspaces; this collection of subspaces is called
building set

\mathcal{G}
.
The variety
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
is used for studying the complement of the subspace arrangement, by considering the Leray spectral sequence for the inclusion of the complement in the wonderful model
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
.
The spectral sequence collapses at the third page yielding a
Leray model
(also known as Morgan algebra
[
Mor78
]
) for the rational homotopy type.

Inspired by the realizable case, we provide a combinatorial definition of
building set
for polymatroids and we introduce a
Leray model

B(P,\mathcal{G})
for a polymatroid with building set.
In the case of matroids the Leray model was recently studied by Bibby, Denham, and Feichtner
[
BDF21
]
.
The last combinatorial object that we need is the
\mathcal{G}
-nested set complex

n(P,\mathcal{G})
.
In the realizable case this complex remembers whether the intersection of the corresponding divisors in
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
is non-empty.

The problem of computing the cohomology of the complement of a subspace arrangement was solved by Goresky and MacPherson
[
GM88
]
and by De Concini and Procesi
[
DCP95
]
with different techniques.
Goresky and MacPherson have used the stratified Morse theory to describe the additive cohomology with integer coefficients of the complement in terms of a poset.
De Concini and Procesi have provided the aforementioned Morgan algebra (alias the Leray model) that describes the ring structure of the cohomology with rational coefficients.
These two results were connected by Yuzvinsky
[
Yuz02
,
Yuz99
]
constructing a smaller rational model
\CM
, however this connection was found only for the maximal building set.

We extend these results to the non-realizable setting and to arbitrary building sets, see Theorems
3.14
and
3.15
, by using the
critical monomial algebra

\CM(P,\mathcal{G})
.

Theorem
.

The inclusion
\CM(P,\mathcal{G})\hookrightarrow B(P,\mathcal{G})
is a quasi-isomorphism. Moreover

H^{{\mathbin{\mathchoice{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to4.45491pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to3.91045pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}}}}(B(P,\mathcal{G}))\cong H^{{\mathbin{\mathchoice{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to4.45491pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to3.91045pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}}}}(\CM(P,\mathcal{G}))\cong\bigoplus_{f\in L}\bigotimes_{g\in F}\tilde{H}_{2\cd(g)-2-{\mathbin{\mathchoice{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to5.87434pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to4.45491pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}{\hbox to3.91045pt{\hfil\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\scalebox{.75}{\lower 0.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}\hfil}}}}}\Bigl(n((\hat{0},g),\mathcal{G})\Bigr).

In the realizable case with maximal building set, the above decomposition specializes to the Goresky-MacPherson formula.

The Leray model contains a subalgebra
\DP(P,\mathcal{G})
as the first row of the spectral sequence, we call this algebra the
Chow ring
of the polymatroid.
For subspace arrangements,
\DP(P,\mathcal{G})
is the cohomology (indeed the Chow ring) of the wonderful model
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
.
The combinatorial Chow ring for matroids was studied by Feichtner and Yuzvinsky
[
FY04
]
and later by Huh, Katz, and Adiprasito
[
Huh12
,
HK12
,
AHK18
]
and others.

We prove that the Chow ring
\DP(P,\mathcal{G})
of a polymatroid satisfies the Kähler package (see Theorems
4.7
and
4.21
).

Theorem
.

The algebra
\DP(P,\mathcal{G})
satisfy the Poincaré duality property.
Moreover, there exists a simplicial cone
\Sigma_{P,\mathcal{G}}
contained in
\DP^{1}(P,\mathcal{G})
such that for each
\ell\in\Sigma_{P,\mathcal{G}}
the Hard-Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge-Riemann relations hold.

We prove the above theorem using methods similar to ones in
[
AHK18
]
.
A second and easier proof of the Kähler package for matroid was given in
[
BHM+22
]
using a semismall decomposition; the decomposition is the first step through the singular Hodge theory
[
BHM+20
]
.
In the realizable setting the decomposition is induced by a map between wonderful models that is semismall (for semismall maps in algebraic geometry see
[
dCM02
,
dCM09
]
).
In the case of polymatroids the corresponding map is not semismall, hence we cannot deduce the Kahler package using this method.
However, we obtain a relative Lefschetz decomposition of the Chow ring (see Theorem
5.4
).

Theorem
.

Let
\DP_{(a)}
be the Chow ring for the polymatroids
P\setminus a
where an element
a\in E
is removed from the ground set.
The Chow ring
\DP(P,\mathcal{G})
decomposes into irreducible
\DP_{(a)}
-modules as

\DP(P,\mathcal{G})=\DP_{(a)}\oplus\bigoplus_{f\in S_{a}}\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n_{f}}x_{f}^{k}\DP_{(a)}.

Moreover, these irreducibles are explicitly described by:

x_{f}^{k}\DP_{(a)}\cong\DP((P\setminus a)^{f\setminus a},(\mathcal{G}\setminus a)^{f\setminus a})\otimes\DP(P_{f},\mathcal{G}_{f})[k].

The
reduced characteristic polynomial
of a polymatroid is defined by

(1)

\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)=\frac{\sum_{A\subseteq E}(-1)^{\lvert A\rvert}\lambda^{\cd(E)-\cd(A)}}{\lambda-1}.

As final step we relate the coefficients of the reduced characteristic polynomial to the Hodge-Riemann bilinear form (see Theorem
6.4
).
In order to do that, we restrict to the case of the maximal building set and we fix an isomorphism
\deg\colon\DP^{r}(P,\mathcal{G}_{\max})\to\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem
.

There exist elements
\alpha,\beta\in\DP^{1}(P,\mathcal{G}_{\max})
such that

\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\deg(\alpha^{i}\beta^{r-i})\lambda^{i}.

The element
\alpha
belongs to the closure of the
\sigma
-cone (morally it is nef), but in general
\beta
is not in the closure of the ample cone.
Hence, the coefficients of the reduced characteristic polynomial do not form a log-concave sequence (see Remark
6.6
). Indeed every finite sequence of non-positive integers can appear as a substring of the coefficients.

Techniques

The techniques used for our proof are various and inspired by
[
FY04
,
BDF21
,
Yuz02
,
AHK18
,
BHM+22
]
.

In
Section

2
we make use of Gröbner basis to give two explicit additive bases of the Leray model.
In
Section

3
, by using algebraic Morse theory, we compute the cohomology of the Leray model generalizing the Goresky-MacPherson formula.
In
Section

4
, we use an inductive procedure
to prove the Kähler package.
The main difference with the previous methods is that we do not have partial building sets as in
[
BDF21
]
nor order filters as in
[
AHK18
]
.
Our induction is based on the cardinality of the building set, and the inductive step involved completely different polymatroids.

Section

5
is devoted to the proof of the relative Lefschetz decomposition using some lemmas from the previous sections.
The reduced characteristic polynomial is studied in
Section

6
.
We prove the claimed equality by showing that both polynomials satisfy the same recursion. In this proof we use the properties of the Möbius function for posets.

Finally,
Section

7
contains an explicit and exhaustive example that illustrates our definitions and properties.

2.
The Leray model

For general references about polymatroids we suggest
[
Wel76
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
polymatroid

P=(E,\cd)
on the ground set
E
is a
codimension
function
\cd\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying:

(C1)

\cd(\emptyset)=0
,

(C2)

if
A\subseteq B
, then
\cd(A)\leq\cd(B)
, and

(C3)

if
A,B\subseteq E
, then
\cd(A\cup B)+\cd(A\cap B)\leq\cd(A)+\cd(B).

A polymatroid is a
matroid
if the codimension of singletons are either zero or one.

The closure of a subset
A\subseteq E
is the subset

\{a\in E\ |\ \cd(A\cup\{a\})=\cd(A)\}.

A
flat
is a closed set and the collection of flats forms a lattice
L_{P}
, that we call the
poset of flats
. We use the notation
\max(X)
with
X\subseteq L_{P}
for the set of maximal elements of
X
.

Definition 2.2

(Geometric building set)
.

Let
P=(E,\cd)
be a polymatroid and let
L
be its lattice of flats. A subset
\mathcal{G}
in
L\setminus\{\hat{0}\}
is called a
geometric building set
if for any
x\in L
the morphism of lattices:

\varphi_{x}\colon\prod_{y\in\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq x})}\ [\hat{0},y]\to[\hat{0},x]

induced by the inclusions is an isomorphism and the equality

\cd(x)=\sum_{y\in\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq x})}\cd(y)

holds.

We define
F(P,\mathcal{G},x)=\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq x})
the set of
\mathcal{G}
-factors
of
x
.

Definition 2.3

(
\mathcal{G}
-nested set complex)
.

A subset
S
of
\mathcal{G}
is called
\mathcal{G}
-nested if, for any set of incomparable elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{t}
in
S
of cardinality at least two, the join
x_{1}\vee\cdots\vee x_{t}
is not contained in
\mathcal{G}
. The
\mathcal{G}
-nested sets form an abstract simplicial complex
n(P,\mathcal{G})
.

We suggest to visualize a (realizable) polymatroid
(E,\cd)
as a collection of linear subspaces
S_{e}
for
e\in E
in a fixed complex vector space
V
.
For each
A\subseteq E
, the codimension
\cd(A)
is the codimension of the corresponding flat
\cap_{a\in A}S_{a}
.
The (geometric) building set
\mathcal{G}
is a good choice of some flats to blow up, in order to obtain a wonderful model
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
with some exceptional divisors
D_{g}\subset Y_{\mathcal{G}}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
indexed by
\mathcal{G}
.
A subset
S
of
\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{G}
-nested if and only if the corresponding divisors
\{D_{W}\}_{W\in S}
have non empty intersection.

The following proposition summarizes the main properties of building and nested sets.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid with poset of flats
L
and
\mathcal{G}
be a building set. Then:

(1)

For each
g\in\mathcal{G}
,
x\in L
with
x\geq g
, there exists a unique
\mathcal{G}
-factor
p
of
x
such that
p\geq g
.

(2)

If
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
and
g\wedge h>\hat{0}
, then
g\vee h\in\mathcal{G}
.

(3)

If
S
is a
\mathcal{G}
-nested set, then the
\mathcal{G}
-factors of
\bigvee S
are the maximal elements in
S
(i.e.
F(P,\mathcal{G},\bigvee S)=\max(S)
).

(4)

Let
S
be a
\mathcal{G}
-nested set, the Hasse diagram of
S
(as subset of
L
) is a forest.

Proof.

For
(1)
see
[
FK04
, Proposition 2.5(1)]
, for
(2)
see
[
BDF21
, Proposition 2.5.3(b)]
, and for
(3)
see
[
FK04
, Proposition 2.8]
.
In order to prove
(4)
we suppose that the Hasse diagram
\Gamma_{S}
of
S
is not a forest.
Thus there exist two incomparable elements
g,h\in S
and
t\in S
such that
g\wedge h\geq t\in S
;
in particular
g\wedge h>\hat{0}
. By part
(2)
we get that
g\vee h\in\mathcal{G}
but this contradicts the definition of nested set.
Therefore
\Gamma_{S}
is a forest.
∎

Let
P=(E,\cd)
be a polymatroid,
L
be its poset of flats, and
\mathcal{G}
be a building set in
L
.
Let
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})=\mathbb{Q}[e_{g},x_{g}\mid g\in\mathcal{G}]
be the bigraded commutative algebra with exterior generators
e_{g}
in bidegree
(0,1)
and commutative generators
x_{g}
in bidegree
(2,0)
.

This algebra is equipped with a differential
d
of bidegree
(2,-1)
defined on generators by
d(e_{g})=x_{g}
,
d(x_{g})=0
.
Fix a linear extension
\succ
of the order on
\mathcal{G}
, this gives a reverse order among the
e
-variables and among the
x
-variables, i.e.
x_{h}\prec x_{g}
and
e_{h}\prec e_{g}
if and only if
h\succ g
.
We also set
x_{g}\prec e_{h}
for each
g,h
.
The algebra
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})
has a monomial basis given by:

e_{T}x_{S}^{b}:=e_{g_{1}}\cdots e_{g_{t}}x_{h_{1}}^{b_{1}}\cdots x_{h_{s}}^{b_{s}}

where
T=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
with
g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
satisfying
g_{1}\prec g_{2}\prec\cdots\prec g_{t}
,
S=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\}
with
h_{i}\in\mathcal{G}
and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{s})
is a
s
-tuple of positive integers.
We define the element:

c_{g}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\in\mathcal{G}\\
h\geq g\end{subarray}}x_{h}.

Definition 2.5

(The Leray model of a polymatroid)
.

Let
I(\mathcal{G})
be the ideal of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})
generated by

(i)

e_{T}x_{S}
whenever
S\cup T\notin n(P,\mathcal{G})
,

(ii)

e_{T}x_{S}c_{g}^{b}
whenever
S,T\subseteq\mathcal{G}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
b\geq\cd(g)-\cd(\bigvee(S\cup T)_{<g})
.

The ideal
I(\mathcal{G})
is preserved by the differential
\dd
, so the quotient

B(P,\mathcal{G})={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{I(\mathcal{G})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{I(\mathcal{G})}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{I(\mathcal{G})}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I(\mathcal{G})}$}}}

is a bigraded differential algebra, called the
Leray model
of the polymatroid.

In the realizable case, the Leray model
B(P,\mathcal{G})
is the second page of the Leray spectral sequence for the natural inclusion

V\setminus\cup_{e\in E}S_{e}\cong Y_{\mathcal{G}}\setminus\cup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}D_{g}\hookrightarrow Y_{\mathcal{G}}.

This spectral sequence collapses at the third page, hence it becomes a differential bigraded algebra also known as the Morgan algebra
[
Mor78
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
e_{T}x_{S}c_{g}^{b}
be a monomial of type
(ii)
and let

S^{\prime}=S\cap\max(S\cup T)_{<g}\quad\hbox{and}\quad T^{\prime}=\max(S\cup T)_{<g}\setminus S.

The monomial
e_{T^{\prime}}x_{S^{\prime}}c_{g}^{b}
divides
e_{T}x_{S}c_{g}^{b}
.
Thus, when we consider a monomial of type
(ii)
we can always assume that
S\cap T=\emptyset
,
S\cup T
is an antichain and
\bigvee(S\cup T)<g
.

Theorem 2.7
.

The generators of type (i) and (ii) of the ideal
I(\mathcal{G})
of Definition
2.5
form a Gröbner basis with respect to the deg-lex order.

Proof.

We adapt the method used in
[
FY04
, Theorem 2]
and in
[
BDF21
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.
We are fixing a linear extension of the order on
\mathcal{G}
with
x_{g}\prec e_{h}
for each
g,h
.
We consider the deg-lex monomial order on
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G})
and
we explicitly compute
S
-polynomials.

Case (i)-(i):

Since relations type
(i)
are monomials the
S
-polynomials is zero.

Case (i)-(ii):

Now we consider
f_{1}=e_{T}x_{S}
of type
(i)
and
f_{2}=e_{A}x_{B}c_{g}^{b}
of type
(ii)
.
We assume that

(see Remark

). Let
U=T\cup A
,

, therefore the
S
-polynomial is

S(f_{1},f_{2})=e_{U}x_{V}x_{g}^{b}-e_{U}x_{V}c_{g}^{b}=e_{U}x_{V}(x_{g}^{b}-c_{g}^{b}).

If
g\notin S
, we have that
S\subseteq V
and therefore

S(f_{1},f_{2})=\pm e_{A\setminus T}e_{T}x_{S}x_{V\setminus S}(x_{g}^{b}-c_{g}^{b})

is divisible by
e_{T}x_{S}
.

Then, assume
g\in S
, since
S\cup T
is not nested we have that
U\cup V\cup\{g\}
is not nested. If
U\cup V
is not nested, then

.

So assume

is nested, (thus we have
g\notin U\cup V

is not nested the

The set
U\cup V\cup\{g\}

\displaystyle b

Case (ii)-(ii):

Let
f_{1}=e_{T}x_{S}c_{g}^{d}

In this section we generalize the Goresky-MacPherson formula (see

The choice of the minimal building set yields a significantly smaller nested set complex and it can be useful in practical computations.

3.1.
Critical monomials

Definition 3.1
.

A
standard monomial

(resp.
\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}
) is a monomial that appears in the basis given by Corollary
2.8
(resp. by Corollary
2.12
).

For any standard monomial
\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}^{b}
we extend the function

by setting

for

.

Definition 3.2
.

Let

be a standard monomial. An element

is called

with respect to the monomial
\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}^{b}
if

and

If every element of

.

Notice that if the monomial

and so the critical monomial is uniquely determined by

.

Definition 3.3
.

The
critical monomial
associated with

is

where

.

In Theorem
3.14
we will prove that the linear span of critical monomials form a subcomplex (indeed a subalgebra) of

.
Moreover, we will show that this subalgebra is quasi-isomorphic to the Leray model.
This first lemma implies that the span of critical monomials is a sub-complex.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every critical monomial
c\mu(T)
we have

\dd(c\mu(T))=\sum_{t\in T\setminus\max(T)}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec t}\rvert}c\mu(T\setminus\{t\}).

Proof.

Let
c\mu(T)=\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}^{b}
, we have

\displaystyle\dd(c\mu(T))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in T}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec t}\rvert}\tau_{T\setminus\{t\}}\sigma_{S}^{b}\sigma_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in T\setminus\min(T)}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec t}\rvert}\tau_{T\setminus\{t\}}\sigma_{S}^{b}\sigma_{t},

because if
t\in\min(T)
then
b(t)=\cd(t)-1
and so
\sigma_{t}^{\cd(t)}=0
.

Fix
t\in T\setminus\min(T)
, the set

is nonempty.
By using relation (ii) of Theorem
2.9
and the fact that
\tau_{t}^{2}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{R}\sigma_{R}^{b}\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in R}(-1)^{\lvert R_{\prec r}\rvert}\tau_{R\setminus\{r\}}\tau_{t}\sigma_{R}^{b}\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in R}(-1)^{\lvert R_{\prec r}\rvert}\tau_{R\setminus\{r\}}\tau_{t}\sigma_{R\setminus\{r\}}^{b}\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+b(r)+1},

where in the last equality we used

0=\tau_{t}(\sigma_{r}-\sigma_{t})\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+1}\prod_{l\neq r}(\tau_{l}-\tau_{t})=\tau_{t}\tau_{R\setminus\{r\}}(\sigma_{r}-\sigma_{t})\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+1}.

Notice that

implies
\cd(\bigvee R)=\cd(\bigvee(R\setminus\{r\}))+\cd(r)
and
\cd(\bigvee(R\setminus\{r\})\vee\bigvee(T_{<r}))=\cd(\bigvee(R\setminus\{r\}))+\cd(\bigvee(T_{<r}))
; so
b_{T\setminus\{r\}}(t)=b_{T}(t)+b_{T}(r)+1
.
Therefore

\tau_{R}\sigma_{R}^{b}\sigma_{t}^{b(t)+1}=\sum_{r\in R}(-1)^{\lvert R_{\prec r}\rvert}c\mu((R\setminus\{r\})\cup\{t\})

and finally:

\displaystyle\dd(c\mu(T))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in T\setminus\min(T)}\sum_{r\in\max(T_{<t})}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec r}\rvert}\mu(T\setminus\{r\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in T\setminus\max(T)}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec r}\rvert}\mu(T\setminus\{r\}),

because

is a forest by Proposition

.
This conclude the proof.
∎

The algebraic Morse theory, together with Lemma

Lemma 3.5
.

The set

is a matching.
Moreover, a monomial is critical if and only if it is critical for the matching

Proof.

We check that each non-critical monomial appears exactly once in

Proposition 3.8

The matching

Proof.

We show that the graph

Although

First notice that:

\displaystyle d(\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}^{b})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in T}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec g}\rvert}\tau_{T\setminus{g}}\sigma_{S}^{b}\sigma_{g}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in T\\
g\textnormal{ non-critical}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\lvert T_{\prec g}\rvert}\tau_{T\setminus{g}}\sigma_{S}^{b}\sigma_{g}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in T\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in T\\

It is enough to prove that there are no alternating directed cycles, i.e. cycles such that for every pair of consecutive edges exactly one is in

Suppose that there exists a directed cycle and consider two consecutive edges.
We can assume that the first one is in

The first edge is

The second edge is

\tau_{T^{\prime}}\sigma_{S^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}

Since the value of

for some

These two edges are shown below.
\tau_{T}\sigma_{S}^{b}
\tau_{T}\tau_{g}\frac{\sigma_{S}^{b}}{\sigma_{g}}
\tau_{T\setminus\{f\}}\tau_{g}\frac{\sigma_{S}^{b}}{\sigma_{g}}\sigma_{f}

The sets of critical elements for

and for

coincide, so both

and

are non-critical for

By minimality of

and

We have proved that in every alternating path after two steps the set indexing the variable

Therefore there are no alternating cycles.

Lemma 3.12

We define the algebra of critical monomials abstractly, by generators and relations.

We summarize all the previous results of this section in the following theorem.

Lemma 4.4

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=x_{T}^{n}(x_{g}+x_{\hat{1}})^{n(g)}x_{\hat{1}}^{n(\hat{1})}

\displaystyle x_{S}^{b}\epsilon(x_{S}^{b})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\lvert S\setminus\{\hat{1}\}\rvert}x_{T}^{n}x_{g}^{n(g)}x_{\hat{1}}^{n(\hat{1})}

4.2.

This technical section is devoted to computing the annihilator
\Ann(\sigma_{g})
and
\Ann(x_{g})
for

We describe it using the Chow ring of different polymatroids:

and

. In the case of matroids these operations are known as truncation, restriction, and contraction.

The following proposition is needed for the proof of the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
A
and
B
be Poincaré duality algebra of the same dimension
n
, then:

•

for each
x\in A^{k}
,
x\neq 0
, the ring
A/\Ann(x)
is a Poincaré duality algebra of dimension

•

each surjective homomorphism

is an isomorphism.

The proof of the above proposition can be found, for example, in
[

.

Let

be a polymatroid with building set
\mathcal{G}
.
Consider

such that
\cd(g)>1
and let
\tr_{g}\cd\colon 2^{E}\to\mathbb{N}
be the function defined by:

\tr_{g}\cd(h)=\begin{cases}\cd(h)-1&\textnormal{if }\cd(h)=\cd(h\cup g),\\
\cd(h)&\textnormal{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We denote by
\tr_{g}L
the poset of flats of
\tr_{g}\cd
.
Finally, define

\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}=\{\overline{h}\in\tr_{g}L\mid h\in\mathcal{G}\},

where
\overline{h}
is the closure with respect to

of the flat

. Notice that
\tr_{g}L
is a subposet of

but with a different codimension function.

Lemma 4.9
.

For all

with
\cd(g)>1
, the pair
\tr_{g}P=(E,\tr_{g}\cd)
is a polymatroid and
\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}
is a building set for the poset of flats

Proof.

It is easy to see that
(E,\tr_{g}\cd)
is a polymatroid. Let

and notice that, for all

,
h\leq x
in

if and only if
\overline{h}\leq x
in
\tr_{g}L
.
Thus, we have
\max\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}=\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}
and it follows that

[\hat{0},x]\simeq\prod_{y\in\max(\mathcal{G}_{\leq x})}[\hat{0},y]\simeq\prod_{y\in\max(\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}_{\leq x})}[\hat{0},y].

For the second part of the definition of a building set we have two cases. Let

and assume
g\nleq x
, which implies
g\nleq y_{i}
for every

\tr_{g}\cd(x)=\cd(x)=\sum_{y\in\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}}\cd(y)=\sum_{y\in\max\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}}\tr_{g}\cd(y).

Finally, let

then by Proposition

such that

. Thus, we have the following:

\tr_{g}\cd(x)=\cd(x)-1=\Bigl(\sum_{y\in\max\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}}\cd(y)\Bigr)-1=\sum_{y\in\max\tr_{g}\mathcal{G}_{\leq x}}\tr_{g}\cd(y).

\displaystyle x_{g}\psi_{g}\Bigl(1\otimes\sigma_{g\vee h}^{n}\prod_{s\in S}(\sigma_{g\vee s}-\sigma_{g\vee h})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=x_{g}\sigma_{g\vee h}^{n}\prod_{s\in S}(\sigma_{s}-\sigma_{g\vee h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{A}x_{g}x_{A}\sigma_{g\vee h}^{n},

where the sum is taken over all sets

such that

and
a_{i}\not\geq g\vee h
.
Applying Lemma

to

and

we obtain that each term

is zero.

The map
\psi_{g}
is surjective because either

or
g\vee h\in\mathcal{G}_{g}
for all

.
We apply Proposition
4.8
,
\DP(L,\mathcal{G})/\Ann(x_{g})
is a Poincaré duality algebra of dimension
\cd(\hat{1})-2
. The algebra
\DP(P^{g},\mathcal{G}^{g})\otimes\DP(P_{g},\mathcal{G}_{g})
is Poincaré duality of dimension
(\cd(g)-1)+(\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)-1)
(here is the only point were we use

).
Since

is surjective between Poincaré duality algebras of the same dimension, it is an isomorphism.

For the last statement we have

\displaystyle x_{g}\psi_{g}(x_{g}^{\cd(g)-1}\otimes x_{\hat{1}}^{\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)-1})

\displaystyle=x_{g}\sigma_{g}^{\cd(g)-1}x_{\hat{1}}^{\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)-1}

\displaystyle=(x_{g}-\sigma_{g})\sigma_{g}^{\cd(g)-1}x_{\hat{1}}^{\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)-1}

\displaystyle=-x_{\hat{1}}\sigma_{g}^{\cd(g)-1}x_{\hat{1}}^{\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)-1}

\displaystyle=-x_{\hat{1}}^{\cd(\hat{1})-1},

so

.
∎

Lemma 4.18

Let

The following theorem provides an abstract procedure to prove the Hodge-Riemann relations inductively.

5.
The relative Lefschetz decomposition

In this section we provide a decomposition of

-module.
This is analogous to the semi-small decomposition of
[

Indeed, consider an arrangement of hyperplanes

Lemma 5.6

For all elements

such that

6.
Characteristic polynomial

In this section we study the coefficients of the (reduced) characteristic polynomial of a polymatroid.

We consider only maximal building sets, so we omit the building set from the notations.
Moreover we suppose that the polymatroid is without loops, i.e.
\cd(\{e\})>0
for all
e\in E
.

Let
\alpha=\alpha_{P}=-x_{\hat{1}}
and
\beta=\beta_{P}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{\max}}x_{g}
be two elements in
\DP^{1}(P)
.
We denote by
\mu_{L}(a,b)
the Möbius function of
L
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any polymatroid
P
with
\cd(E)>0
and
r=\cd(E)-1
we have

\deg(\beta_{P}^{r})=(-1)^{r}+\sum_{g\in L\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}}(-1)^{\cd(g)-1}\deg(\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-\cd(g)}).

Proof.

A flag with repetition is
\mathcal{F}=(F_{1}^{a_{1}}\subsetneq F_{2}^{a_{2}}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F_{l}^{a_{l}})
where
a_{i}>0
are the multiplicity of the flats
F_{i}\in L
.
We also require that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}=r
.
Define
x_{\mathcal{F}}=\prod_{i=1}^{\lvert\mathcal{F}\rvert}x_{F_{i}}^{a_{i}}
, we will prove that
x_{\mathcal{F}}=0
if
\cd(F_{1})>a_{1}
.
More generally we have
x_{\mathcal{F}}=0
if
\cd(F_{i})>\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}
for some
i
, but we prove and use the implication only for
i=1
.
From the isomorphism
\psi_{g}
of Lemma
4.14
we obtain

x_{\mathcal{F}}=x_{F_{1}}\psi_{F_{1}}((x_{F_{1}}\otimes 1-1\otimes\beta_{P_{g}})^{a_{1}-1}(1\otimes x_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})),

where
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=(F_{2}^{a_{2}}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq F_{l}^{a_{l}})
.
Notice that the degree of
x_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
is
r-a_{1}
, which is greater than
r-\cd(F_{1})
, the top degree of
\DP(P_{g})
.

Let
\binom{r}{a}
be the multinomial coefficient where
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{l})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}=r
.
Since
x_{f}x_{g}=0
if
f
and
g
are incomparable, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{P}^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{F}\textnormal{ flag of }P}\binom{r}{a}x_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in L\setminus\{\hat{0}\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{F}\textnormal{ flag of }P\\
F_{1}=F\end{subarray}}\binom{r}{a}x_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in L\setminus\{\hat{0}\}}\sum_{k=\cd(F)}^{r}\binom{r}{k}x_{F}^{k}\sum_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\textnormal{ flag of }P_{F}}\binom{r-k}{a^{\prime}}x_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in L\setminus\{\hat{0}\}}\sum_{k=\cd(F)}^{r}\binom{r}{k}x_{F}^{k}\beta_{P_{F}}^{r-k}.

The summand relative to
F=\hat{1}
is exactly
x_{\hat{1}}^{r}
and contributes
(-1)^{r}
to
\deg(\beta_{P}^{r})
.
It is enough to prove

\deg\Bigl(\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}\binom{r}{k}x_{g}^{k}\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-k}\Bigr)=\deg(\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-\cd(g)})

for every
g\in L\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
.
We use Lemma
4.14
to obtain:

\displaystyle\deg

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}\binom{r}{k}x_{g}^{k}\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-k}\Bigr)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}\binom{r}{k}\deg((x_{g}\otimes 1-1\otimes\beta_{P_{g}})^{k-1}(1\otimes\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-k}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}(-1)^{k-\cd(g)}\binom{r}{k}\binom{k-1}{\cd(g)-1}\deg(x_{g}^{\cd(g)-1}\otimes\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-\cd(g)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{r}{k}\binom{k-1}{\cd(g)-1}\deg(\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-\cd(g)})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\cd(g)-1}\deg(\beta_{P_{g}}^{r-\cd(g)}),

where in the last equality we used the identity

\sum_{k=\cd(g)}^{r}(-1)^{k}\binom{r}{k}\binom{k-1}{\cd(g)-1}=(-1)^{\cd(g)}

which follows from
[
GKP94
, eq. 5.24]
with
l=r
,
m=0
,
n=\cd(g)-1
, and
s=-1
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

For every polymatroid
P
with poset of flats
L
and
r=\cd(E)-1
with
\cd(E)>0
we have

\deg(\beta_{P}^{r})=(-1)^{\cd(E)}\mu_{L}(\hat{0},\hat{1}).

Proof.

It is known that
\mu_{L}(\hat{0},\hat{1})=\tilde{\chi}(\Delta(\hat{0},\hat{1}))
, i.e. the Möbius function coincides with the reduced Euler characteristic of the order complex of the poset
L\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
(e.g. see
[
Rot64
]
).
Let
L^{\op}
be the opposite (dual) lattice of
L
which is defined on the same set of
L
but with reversed order, i.e.,
x\leq y
in
L^{\op}
if and only if
y\leq x
in
L
.
Since the order complexes of
L
and
L^{\op}
are the same simplicial complex, we have

\mu_{L}(\hat{0},\hat{1})=\mu_{L^{\op}}(\hat{0},\hat{1}).

Define
\deg(\beta_{P}^{0})=1
for rank zero polymatroids
P
.
Therefore, the functions
(-1)^{\cd(E)}\deg(\beta_{P}^{r})
and
\mu_{L^{\op}}(\hat{0},\hat{1})
satisfy the same recurrence relation.
One is given by the definition of
\mu_{L^{\op}}(\hat{0},\hat{1})
and the other by Lemma
6.1
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Notice that if
L
is a geometric lattice (i.e. the poset of flats of a matroid), then the Möbius function has alternating sign, hence in this case
\deg(\beta^{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Definition 6.3
.

The
characteristic polynomial
of a polymatroid
P
is

\chi_{P}(\lambda)=\sum_{g\in L}\mu_{L}(\hat{0},g)\lambda^{\dim(g)},

where
\dim(g)=\cd(\hat{1})-\cd(g)
.
Since
\chi_{P}(1)=0
by the definition of Möbius function, we define the
reduced characteristic polynomial
as

\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)=\frac{\chi_{P}(\lambda)}{\lambda-1}.

This definition of reduced characteristic polynomial coincides with the one in the introduction eq. (
1
), as stated in
[
Whi93
]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

For every polymatroid
P
, we have

\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\deg_{P}(\alpha_{P}^{i}\beta_{P}^{r-i})\lambda^{i}

where
r=\cd(E)-1
.

Proof.

We show that
\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)
and the right hand side satisfy the same recurrence:

q_{P}(\lambda)-\lambda q_{\tr_{\hat{1}}P}(\lambda)=-\mu_{L}(\hat{0},\hat{1})

where
L
is the poset of flats of
P
.

Let
\tr_{\hat{1}}L
be the poset of flats of
\tr_{\hat{1}}P
and notice that
\mu_{L}(\hat{0},g)=\mu_{\tr_{\hat{1}}L}(\hat{0},g)
for all
g
such that
\dim(g)>1
.
Therefore
\chi_{P}(\lambda)-\lambda\chi_{\tr P}(\lambda)
is a polynomial of degree one divisible by
\lambda-1
.
Hence
\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)-\lambda\overline{\chi}_{\tr P}(\lambda)
is constant and equal to
\overline{\chi}_{P}(0)=-\mu_{L}(\hat{0},\hat{1})
.
This proves that
\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)
satisfies the recurrence.

Now observe that for
i>0

\deg_{P}(\alpha_{P}^{i}\beta_{P}^{r-i})=\deg_{\tr P}(\alpha_{\tr P}^{i-1}\beta_{\tr P}^{r-i})
by Lemma
4.11
.
This proves that

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\deg_{P}(\alpha_{P}^{i}\beta_{P}^{r-i})\lambda^{i}-\lambda\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{i}\deg_{\tr P}(\alpha_{\tr P}^{i-1}\beta_{\tr P}^{r-i})\lambda^{i}=\\
=(-1)^{r}\deg_{P}(\beta^{r}),\end{split}

and so Lemma
6.2
proves the recurrence.

The base case
\cd(E)=1
is trivial, so the proof follows.
∎

For an explicit example check Section
7
.

Corollary 6.5
.

The coefficient of
\lambda^{i}
of the reduced characteristic polynomial
\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)
is (up to the sign) the reduced Euler characteristic of the order complex of the poset
(\tr_{\hat{1}}^{i}L)\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}

[\lambda^{i}]\overline{\chi}_{P}(\lambda)=(-1)^{\cd(E)}\tilde{\chi}(\Delta((\tr_{\hat{1}}^{i}L)\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\})).

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
6.4
and Lemma
6.2
.

Remark 6.6
.

The coefficients of the characteristic polynomial

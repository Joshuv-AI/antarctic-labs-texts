Characteristic-free test ideals
Felipe Pérez, Rebecca R.G

Abstract
. Tight closure test ideals have been central to the classification of singularities in rings of characteristic p > 0 p>0 , and via reduction to characteristic p > 0 p>0 , in equal characteristic 0 as well. A summary of their properties and applications can be found in [ ST12 ] . In this paper, we extend the notion of a test ideal to arbitrary closure operations, particularly those coming from big Cohen-Macaulay modules and algebras, and prove that it shares key properties of tight closure test ideals. Our main results show how these test ideals can be used to give a characteristic-free classification of singularities, including a few specific results on the mixed characteristic case. We also compute examples of these test ideals.



1.
Introduction

The test ideal originated in the study of tight closure
[
HH90
]
. Since then, it has been used to define a classification of singularities in rings of characteristic
p>0

[
HH90
,
HH94
,
HH89
]
, which aligns well with the classification of singularities in equal characteristic 0
[
Smi00
,
Har01
]
. The general idea is that the larger the test ideal, the closer the ring is to being regular, and the smaller the test ideal, the singular the ring is. The gap in the literature on test ideals is the mixed characteristic case. Recent work of Ma and Schwede
[
MS18a
,
MS18b
]
has begun to fill in this gap, from the perspective of test ideals of pairs. However, most existing results are heavily dependent on the characteristic of the ring, and it is not always known whether corresponding definitions actually agree. In this paper, we study a generalization of the test ideal in a characteristic-free setting. We study test ideals from the perspective of closure operations, mimicking the approach of Hochster and Huneke
[
Hoc07
]
with regard to the tight closure test ideal but broadening our definition to include test ideals coming from arbitrary closure operations.

We are motivated by work of the second named author on the connections between closure operations given by big Cohen-Macaulay modules and algebras, and the singularities of the ring
[
R.G16b
,
RG18
]
, and encouraged by the fact that these connections hold in all characteristics. More precisely, in
[
R.G16b
]
, the second named author proved that a ring is regular if and only if all closure operations satisfying certain axioms (Dietz closures) act trivially on modules over the ring. Since big Cohen-Macaulay modules give Dietz closures, we expect further connections to hold between the singularities of the ring and the big Cohen-Macaulay module closures over the ring, and we give some of those connections in this paper. In order to do this, we define and study the test ideals given by closures coming from big Cohen-Macaulay modules and algebras. See Section
3
for details.

We prove that the test ideal of a module closure has multiple equivalent definitions, which we use to get our main results connecting singularities to big Cohen-Macaulay module test ideals.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,m,k)
be a local ring and
E=E_{R}(k)
the injective hull of the residue field.

(1)

Let cl be a residual closure operation. Then the test ideal
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\text{Ann}\,0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. (Proposition
3.9
)

(2)

Let
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{B}
be a module closure. If
R
is complete or
B
is finitely-presented, then
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\sum_{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)}f(B)
. (Theorem
3.12
)

In particular, the second result is similar to the result that the tight closure test ideal

\tau_{*}(R)=\sum_{e\geq 0}\sum_{\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{1/p^{e}},R)}\phi((cR)^{1/p^{e}})

for particular elements
c

[
HT04
]
. This perspective on the tight closure test ideal is one of the major tools used to study it, as described in
[
ST12
]
. Our second definition also coincides with the trace ideal of the module
B
, as studied in
[
Lam99
,
Lin17
]
.
By drawing this connection, we open the door for future results on test ideals using the theory of trace ideals, and vice versa. In an upcoming paper with Neil Epstein
[
ER19
]
, the second named author has generalized this to a duality between closure operations and interior operations on finitely-generated and Artinian modules over complete local rings.

One important consequence of these results is that when the ring is complete and cl is a big Cohen-Macaulay module closure,
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
is nonzero (Corollary
3.16
).

We also define a finitistic test ideal of an arbitrary closure operation and discuss cases where it is equal to the (big) test ideal of the same closure operation. In the Gorenstein case, the test ideal of an algebra closure is the whole ring if and only if the corresponding finitistic test ideal is also the whole ring (Proposition
3.10
).

One advantage to working with test ideals of module closures is that, as a consequence of Theorem
3.12
, when the module is finitely-generated, we can compute its test ideal in Macaulay2. This is in contrast to the tight closure test ideal, which is difficult to compute in general. In Section
5
, we compute examples of test ideals of finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay modules, and in some cases are able to compute or approximate the “smallest" Cohen-Macaulay test ideal.

In summary, our results on the classification of singularities via test ideals are:

.

Assume that

is a complete local domain.

(1)

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}(R)=R
for all big Cohen-Macaulay

-modules

. (Corollary

(2)

If

for all big Cohen-Macaulay algebras

. (Corollary

)

(3)

If
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}(R)=R
for some big Cohen-Macaulay module

, then

is Cohen-Macaulay. (Corollary

)

(4)

If

is a Cohen-Macaulay ring with a canonical module

is Gorenstein if and only if
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{\omega}}(R)=R
. (Corollary

)

(5)

If

(6)

If

We apply our techniques to the case of mixed characteristic rings in Section
6
. We propose a mixed characteristic closure operation that satisfies Dietz’s axioms (these guarantee that it acts like a big Cohen-Macaulay module closure–see
[
Die10
,
R.G16b
]
for details), and prove that its test ideal can be viewed in three different ways similar to those we gave for module closures earlier. In addition to demonstrating how our results can be used in mixed characteristic, this section shows how our proof techniques can be applied to a broader group of closures than module closures.

Throughout the paper,
R
will denote a commutative Noetherian ring, though some of the
R
-algebras under consideration will not be Noetherian.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the concepts of closure operations and trace ideals. We record their basic properties for later use and give the appropriate references for their proofs.

2.1.
Closure Operations

Given a submodule
N
of a module
M
, we would like to find a submodule of
M
containing
N
that also satisfies some desired properties. This idea is encoded in the following familiar definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A
closure operation

\operatorname{cl}
on a ring
R
is a map, which to each pair of modules
N\subseteq M
assigns a submodule
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
of
M
satisfying.

•

(Extension)
N\subseteq N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
,

•

(Idempotence)
(N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}})^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}=N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, and

•

(Order-Preservation)
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq{N^{\prime}}_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, for
R
-modules
N\subseteq N^{\prime}\subseteq M
.

A particularly important family of closures are Dietz closures, originally defined in
[
Die10
,
Die18
]
. A local domain has a Dietz closure if and only if it has a big Cohen-Macaulay module
[
Die10
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local domain and
N,M,
and
W
be

R
-modules with
N\subseteq M
. A closure operation
\operatorname{cl}
is called a
Dietz closure
if it satisfies the following extra axioms:

(1)

(Functoriality) Let
f:M\to W
be a homomorphism. Then
f(N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M})\subseteq f(N)_{W}^{\operatorname{cl}}
.

(2)

(Semi-residuality) If
N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}=N
, then
0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M/N}=0
.

(3)

(Faithfulness) The ideal
\mathfrak{m}
is closed in
R
.

(4)

(Generalized Colon-Capturing) Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}
be a partial system of parameters for
R
, and let
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
. Suppose that there exists a surjective homomorphism
f:M\to R/J
and
v\in M
such that
f(v)=x_{k+1}+J
. Then
(Rv)^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\cap\ker f\subseteq(Jv)^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}
.

Note that these axioms are independent of each other, and an arbitrary closure operation on any ring
R
can satisfy some subset of them.

Remark 2.3
.

The careful reader will note that the axioms, as expressed here, are set in a more general setting than in
[
Die10
]
. In
[
Die10
]
the axioms were defined only for complete rings, but this hypothesis was not needed. They were also defined only for finitely-generated modules in
[
Die10
]
, but the definitions were later used for arbitrary modules in
[
Die18
]
.

Associated to any
R
-module
B
we define a closure operation as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Given an
R
-module
B
(not necessarily finitely-generated), we define a closure operation
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
on
R
by

u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\text{ if for all }b\in B,\,b\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes N\to B\otimes M)

for any pair of
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
and
u\in M
. This is called a
module closure
.

When
B
is an
R
-algebra, the previous definition can be simplified to
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}
if and only if

1\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes N\to B\otimes M).

Remark 2.5
.

We can extend this closure operation to families of modules in certain circumstances. Let
\cal{B}=\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a collection of
R
-modules. We define
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}=\sum\operatorname{cl}_{B_{i}}
. This is not in general a closure operation (it is not necessarily idempotent), but since the ring is Noetherian, it can be extended to one by iteration as in
[
Eps12
, Construction 3.1.5]
. Alternatively, if the family is directed under generation (see Definition
2.18
), then
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}
does form a closure operation. In particular, if the
B_{i}
are
R
-algebras that form a directed family, then
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}
is a closure operation.

Definition 2.6

(
[
Hoc75
]
)
.

Let
(R,m)
be a local ring. We say that an
R
-module
B
(not necessarily finitely-generated) is a
big Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
if
mB\neq B
and every system of parameters on
R
is a regular sequence on
B
. Note that these modules are sometimes referred to as balanced big Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules (see for example
[
Sha81
]
).

Theorem 2.7

(
[
Die10
]
)
.

If
B
is a big Cohen-Macaulay module, then
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is a Dietz closure.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
R.G16b
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
R
be any ring and
B
any
R
-module (not necessarily finitely-generated). Then
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
satisfies the first two axioms of a Dietz closure, i.e.,
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is functorial and semi-residual.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that when
M=R
and
N=I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\subseteq R
is an ideal we have
u\in I^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{R}
if and only if
uB\subseteq IB
. That is, the closure of of an ideal is the collection of all elements that multiply

into

I^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{R}=(IB:_{R}B).

Alternatively, we can write
I^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{R}
as the set of elements

of

for which the equation

ub=f_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+f_{n}X_{n}

has a solution
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
in

for every

. Or in the case that

is an

u1_{B}=f_{1}X_{1}+\ldots+f_{n}X_{n}

has a solution.

We will sometimes write

when

is clear from context.

Suppose that

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
R
be a ring possessing a closure operation
\operatorname{cl}
. In the following,
N
and
N^{\prime}
are
R
-submodules of the
R
-module
M

\mathcal{I}

(a)

Suppose that

(b)

Suppose that

(c)

Let

\left(\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}N_{i}\right)^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\left(N_{i}\right)_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}

(d)

Let
\mathcal{I}

\operatorname{cl}
-closed in

\operatorname{cl}
-closed in

(e)

If

(N_{1}+N_{2})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}=((N_{1})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}+(N_{2})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
.

(f)

Suppose that
\operatorname{cl}
satisfies the functoriality axiom. Let

N_{N^{\prime}}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
.

(g)

Suppose that

\operatorname{cl}

.

(h)

Suppose that
(R,\mathfrak{m})

.

When the closure operation satisfies the functoriality and semi-residuality axioms, the elements of the ring multiplying the closure inside the original module can be seen as an annihilator. More precisely:

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\operatorname{cl}
be a closure operation that is functorial and semi-residual. Then for any
R
-module
M
and any
R
-submodule
N
of
M
, we have that
\left(N:_{R}N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M/N}\right)
. In particular, this holds for module closures.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
\left(N:N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right)=\left(0:0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M/N}\right)
. Now part (a) of Proposition
2.14
implies
N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}/N=0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M/N}
, from where the result is clear.

∎

The following proposition gives information about the behavior of module closures under ring extension.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
B,N
and
M
be
R
-modules, such that
N\subseteq M
. If
R\to S
is a ring morphism, then

\operatorname{Im}(S\otimes_{R}N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\to S\otimes_{R}M)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(S\otimes_{R}N\to S\otimes_{R}M)^{\operatorname{cl}_{S\otimes_{R}B}}_{S\otimes M}.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}
, then we have that

b\otimes x\in\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes_{R}N\to B\otimes_{R}M),

for all
b\in B
. Tensoring with
S
we get

b\otimes s\otimes x\in\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes_{R}S\otimes_{R}N\to B\otimes_{R}S\otimes_{R}M),

for all
b\in B
and all
s\in S
. But we can rewrite the previous expression as

b\otimes s^{\prime}\otimes s\otimes x\in\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes_{R}S\otimes_{S}S\otimes_{R}N\to B\otimes_{R}S\otimes_{S}S\otimes_{R}M),

for all
b\in B
and all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
. Thus
(s\otimes x)\in\operatorname{Im}(S\otimes_{R}N\to S\otimes_{R}M)^{\operatorname{cl}_{S\otimes_{R}B}}_{S\otimes_{R}M}
.
∎

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
B
be an
R
-module and
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
the associated module closure. For any ideal
I
in
R
and any prime ideal
P
,

I^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}R_{P}\subseteq(IR_{P})^{\operatorname{cl}_{B_{P}}}_{R_{P}}.

Similarly, if
R
is a local ring and
\hat{R}
is its completion at the maximal ideal, then

I^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\hat{R}\subseteq(I\hat{R})^{\operatorname{cl}_{\hat{R}\otimes B}}_{\hat{R}}.

Definition 2.18

(
[
Lam99
]
)
.

Recall that a module
B
is said to
generate
a module
D
if some direct sum of copies of
B
maps onto
D
.

The generation property enables us to compare the closures given by
B
and

. Before we give the precise result we need a lemma.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
R
be a local ring. If
M
and
N
are
R

0^{\operatorname{cl}_{M}}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in 0^{\operatorname{cl}_{M}}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)}
, then for every
m\in M
we have that
m\otimes\phi=0
in
M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)
. By means of the natural map
M\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)\to N
, given by
m\otimes\phi\mapsto\phi(m)
, we have that
\phi(m)=0
for all

∎

The following proposition is the result of a conversation with Yongwei Yao, and gives one case where we have containment of module closures.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
B
and
D
be finitely-generated
R
-modules, where
R
is complete and local. Then
\operatorname{cl}_{B}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{D}
, i.e.
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\subseteq N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{D}}
for all
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
, if and only if
B
generates
D
.

Proof.

If
B
generates
D
(see Definition
2.18
), then
\operatorname{cl}_{B}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{D}
by
[
R.G16b
, Proposition 3.6]
. For the reverse direction, assume
\operatorname{cl}_{B}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{D}
.

Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
be a generating set for
B
and
E
be the injective hull of the residue field of
R
. We have a map

h:\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)\to B^{\oplus r}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)

given by
h(f)=(b_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus b_{r})\otimes f
. The kernel of this map is the set of elements
f
of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)
such that
b_{i}\otimes f=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, which is equal to the set of
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)
such that
b\otimes f=0
for all
b\in B
. This is equal to
0^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)}
. Hence, by our assumption,
0^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)}\subseteq 0^{\operatorname{cl}_{D}}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)}
, but the latter is 0 by the preceding lemma. This implies that
h
is injective.

Since
h
is injective, its Matlis dual

h^{\vee}:\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B^{\oplus r}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)

is surjective. The map
h^{\vee}
takes a map
\phi:B^{\oplus r}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)\to E
to

\phi\circ h:\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)\to E
. By Hom-tensor adjointness, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B^{\oplus r}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B^{\oplus r},\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E))

Under this isomorphism, a map
\psi:B^{\oplus r}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)
is sent to the map
\phi:B^{\oplus r}\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)\to E
sending

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r})\otimes f\mapsto(\phi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}))(f).

Put together, this gives us a surjective map

\operatorname{Hom}(B^{\oplus r},\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)))\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E))

that sends
\psi:B^{\oplus r}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)
to
\phi\circ h:\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E)\to E
. Combining earlier information,
\phi\circ h=\psi(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
.

Since
R
is complete and
D
is finitely-generated,
D\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(D,E),E)
, and therefore the map

\operatorname{Hom}(B^{\oplus r},D)\twoheadrightarrow D.

given by
\left(\psi:B^{\oplus r}\to D\right)\mapsto\psi(b_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus b_{r})
is surjective. Hence for every
d\in D
, there is a map
B^{\oplus r}\to D
whose image contains
d
. Therefore,
B
generates
D
.
∎

The following proposition characterizes regular rings in terms of the behaviour of Dietz closures. This result describes an important connection between the behavior of big Cohen-Macaulay module closure operations and the singularities of the ring.

Theorem 2.21

(
[
R.G16b
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Suppose that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a local domain that has at least one Dietz closure (in particular
R
may be any complete local domain). Then
R
is regular if and only if all Dietz closures on
R
are trivial on submodules of finitely-generated
R
-modules.

Note that this result holds regardless of the characteristic of
R
, as by
[
HH92
,
And18
]
, we know that big Cohen-Macaulay algebras (and in particular big Cohen-Macaulay modules) exist over complete local domains of any characteristic.

In fact, the proof of this statement in
[
R.G16b
]
uses the fact that big Cohen-Macaulay modules over regular rings are faithfully flat
[
HH92
]
, and we get the following corollary to Theorem
2.21
and its proof in
[
R.G16b
]
:

Corollary 2.22
.

Suppose that
(R,m)
is a local domain with a big Cohen-Macaulay module
B
(in particular,
R
may be any complete local domain). Then
R
is regular if and only if all big Cohen-Macaulay module closures on
R
are trivial (on submodules of all
R
-modules).

Remark 2.23
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Cohen-Macaulay local domain of dimension
d
. If
R
is approximately Gorenstein (for example if
\dim(R)\neq 1
), then for all

, the

-modules
\text{syz}^{n}(k)
induce Dietz closures that are trivial
if and only if

is regular
[

]
. So when

is not regular,
\text{syz}^{n}(k)
gives an example of a nontrivial Dietz closure on

.

We also have the following:

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
R
be a local domain with a big Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module

such that
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is trivial on ideals of

. Then

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

The closure
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
captures colons, so for all partial systems of parameters

on

, we must have

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}):x_{k+1}\subseteq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{R}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}).

Hence

∎

2.2.
Trace ideals and Modules

Definition 2.25
.

Let
R
be a ring and
A,B

R
-modules. The
trace of
A
with respect to
B
is defined as

\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)=\sum_{\phi:B\to A}\phi(B)

where the sum runs over all
R
-linear maps from
B
to
A
.

That is, the trace of a module
A
with respect to another module
B
is the submodule generated by the images of all possible maps from
B
to
A
.

Remark 2.26
.

(1)

B
generates
A
if and only if
\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)=A
. One example where
B
generates
A
is when there is a surjective map from
B
to
A
, or if
B=R
.

(2)

When
A=R
, this is also referred to as the trace ideal,
\text{tr}_{B}(R)

[
Lam99
]
.

We collect some basic properties of the trace in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.27

(C.f.
[
Lin17
, c.f. Proposition 2.8 ]
)
.

Let
R
be a ring, and
A,B,C

R
-modules. The following holds.

(1)

We have

\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)=\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)\otimes B\to A)

where the map is given by
\phi\otimes b\mapsto\phi(b)
.

(2)

The behavior with respect to direct sums is given by

\operatorname{tr}_{B\oplus C}(A)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)+\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A).

(3)

More generally, if
\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an arbitrary family of
R
-modules, then

\operatorname{tr}_{\bigoplus_{i\in I}B_{i}}(A)=\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{tr}_{B_{i}}(A).

(4)

For tensor products, we have

\operatorname{tr}_{B\otimes C}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)\cap\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A).

Furthermore, if
B
generates
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)
or
C
generates
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)
, then the equality holds.

(5)

If
B
generates
C
then

\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)\supseteq\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A).

(6)

\operatorname{tr}_{A}(R)=R
if and only if
A
generates all
R
-modules. If
R
is a local ring then
\operatorname{tr}_{A}(R)=R
if and only if
A
has a free summand
[
Lin17
, Proposition 2.8, Part iii]
and
[
CR90
, Lemma 3.45]
.

(7)

\operatorname{tr}_{B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)}(A)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
. Furthemore, when
A=R
and
B
is reflexive we also have
\operatorname{tr}_{B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)}(R)
.

Proof.

(1)

This is clear from the definition.

(2)

From the definition we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{B\oplus C}(A)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi(b,c)\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B\oplus C,A),b\in B,c\in C\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{1}(b)+\phi_{2}(c)\mid\phi_{1}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A),\phi_{2}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A),b\in B,c\in C\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi_{1}(b)\mid\phi_{1}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A),b\in B\right)+\left(\phi_{2}(c)\mid\phi_{2}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A),c\in C\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)+\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A)

(3)

We proceed as in the previous case

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{\bigoplus_{i\in I}B_{i}}(A)

\displaystyle=\left(\phi((b_{i})_{i\in I})\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\oplus_{i\in I}B_{i},A\right),b_{i}\in B_{i},b_{i}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }i\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(b_{i})\mid\phi_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(B_{i},A\right),b_{i}\in B_{i},b_{i}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }i\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\left(\phi_{i}(b_{i})\mid\phi_{i}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B_{i},A),b_{i}\in B_{i},\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{tr}_{B_{i}}(A)

which is what we wanted.

(4)

Note that for any
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B\otimes C,A)
and
c\in C
, we have a map
\phi(-\otimes c):B\to A
sending
b\mapsto\phi(b\otimes c)
. Hence
\phi(b\otimes c)\in\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
for all
b\in B
. Similarly,
\phi(b\otimes c)\in\operatorname{tr}_{C}(R)
and the result follows.

To get the equality, assume that
B
generates
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)
. Then for
a\in\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)\cap\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A)
there exists
\phi:C\to A
such that
\phi(c)=a
for some
c\in C
. Now as
B
generates
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)
, there exists a map
\Psi:B\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)
and an element
b\in B
such that
\Psi(b)=\phi
. Consider the map
B\otimes C\to A
given by
y\otimes z\mapsto\Psi(y)(z)
. This map is well defined and
b\otimes c\mapsto a
. The result follows. The case where
C
generates
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)
works the same way.

(5)

This follows from the fact that every element
a
in
\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A)
can be obtained via a map
C\to A
and an element
c\in C
. This element
c
will be in the image of some map
B\to C
, and so its image
a
in
A
can be obtained via the composition
B\to C\to A
. Hence
a\in\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
.

(6)

Follows as in the references, where the hypothesis that
A
is finitely-generated used by Lindo is not needed.

(7)

By part (4) we have that
\operatorname{tr}_{B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
. On the other hand we have the map
B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)\to A
given by
b\otimes\phi\mapsto\phi(b)
. This implies
\operatorname{tr}_{B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)}(A)\supseteq\operatorname{Im}(B\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)\to A)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
. The last assertion is trivial after noting that
B=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R),R)
.

∎

The result below relates traces of modules in an exact sequence.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
0\to B\xrightarrow{\alpha}C\to D\to 0
be a short exact sequence of
R
-modules, and
A
any other
R
-module. If
J=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(D,A))
, then

J\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)+\operatorname{tr}_{D}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.27
part 5 we have that
\operatorname{tr}_{D}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{C}(A)
. Let
a\in\operatorname{tr}_{B}(A)
. Then there exist
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)
and
b\in B
such that
\phi(b)=a
. From the exact sequence

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(D,A)

we can conclude that for any
r\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(D,A)),

r\phi\in\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(C,A)\xrightarrow{\hat{\alpha}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,A))
, say
r\phi=\hat{\alpha}(\tilde{\phi})=\tilde{\phi}\circ\alpha
. This implies that
ra=r\phi(b)=(\tilde{\phi}\circ\alpha)(b)
. Setting
c=\alpha(b)
, we have
ra=\tilde{\phi}(c)
. The result follows.

∎

3.
Test ideals and Trace Ideals

In this section we define the test ideal of an arbitrary closure operation, give some of its basic properties, and prove that the test ideal of a module closure is a trace ideal.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a ring and
\operatorname{cl}
be a closure operation on
R
-modules. The
big test ideal of
R
associated to
\operatorname{cl}
is defined as

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{N\subseteq M}\left(N:N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right)

where the intersection runs over any (not necessarily finitely-generated)
R
-modules
N,M
. In the case that
\operatorname{cl}
is generated from a
R
-module
B
, (resp. a family
\mathcal{B}
) that is
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{B}
we also denote this ideal by
\tau_{B}(R)
(resp.
\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)
). We sometimes refer to the big test ideal as the test ideal.

Similarly, we define the
finitistic test ideal of
R
associated to
\operatorname{cl}
as

\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}{N\subseteq M}\\
\text{$M/N$ f.g.}\end{subarray}}\left(N:N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\right).

In the case where
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{B}
for some
R
-module
B
, we denote this ideal by
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{B}(R)
.

Note that the big test ideal is always contained in the finitistic test ideal.

When cl is tight closure, these definitions agree with the the tight closure test ideal as given in
[
HH90
, Definition 8.22]
.
As an immediate consequence we get:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\operatorname{cl}
be a closure operation. Then, the test ideal
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
is equal to
R
if and only if for every inclusion of
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
, we have
N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}=N
.

Similarly,
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
if and only if for every inclusion of
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
, with
M/N
finitely-generated, we have
N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}=N
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\operatorname{cl}
be a closure that is functorial and semi-residual. Then

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{M\text{ an $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right).

Additionally,

\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{M\text{ a f.g. $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right).

Proof.

Lemma
2.15
implies that for any
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
,
\left(N:N^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M/N}\right)
, and so

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{M\text{ an $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right).

The second result follows as the intersection will be over all
M/N
finitely-generated.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The finitistic test ideal could be taken as the intersection over all
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
where
M
is finitely-generated. If cl is functorial and semi-residual, then by the proof of Lemma
3.3
, this is equal to

\bigcap_{M\text{ a f.g. $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right),

and so it is equal to our definition of the finitistic test ideal. In particular, this holds for module closures.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
R
is a regular local ring, and cl is a Dietz closure on
R
, then
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
. In this case, if
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{B}
for some big Cohen-Macaulay module
B
, then
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
as well. In fact, if
R
is a complete local domain,
R
is regular if and only if
\tau_{B}(R)=R
for all big Cohen-Macaulay modules
B
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from the definition of a test ideal, Theorem
2.21
, and Lemma
3.3
: if
R
is regular and cl is a Dietz closure, cl is trivial on finitely-generated
R
-modules, so
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
.
By Corollary
2.22
,
R
is regular if and only if
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is trivial for all big Cohen-Macaulay modules
B
. The result follows from Corollary
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a local domain with a big Cohen-Macaulay module
B
such that
\tau_{B}(R)=R
(or
\tau_{B}^{fg}(R)=R
). Then
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.2
,
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is trivial on ideals of
R
. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay by Lemma
2.24
.
∎

Note that if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\tau_{R}(R)=R
, so the converse holds.

It follows from the definition that
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)\subseteq\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
, leading to the following question that is still open in most cases for the tight closure test ideal.

Question 3.7
.

Do the big test ideal and the finitistic test ideal coincide? More specifically, what are the conditions needed on a ring
R
or on a closure operation
\operatorname{cl}
so that
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
?

The following result answers this question in one special case. We will be able to say more once we prove Proposition
3.9
, our first result giving an alternate definition of the test ideal.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\cal{B}
be a directed family of flat
R
-algebras, or a single flat
R
-module
B
. Then
\tau_{\cal{B}}(R)=\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\cal{B}}(R)
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}
, and
\operatorname{clfg}
denote
the closure given by:

u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{clfg}}\text{ if for some }N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M,M_{0}/N\text{ finitely-generated, }u\in N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}}.

We claim that
\operatorname{clfg}=\operatorname{cl}
. To see that
\operatorname{clfg}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}
, note that by part (f) of Proposition
2.14
, for any
N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M
,

N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}.

For the other inclusion, suppose that
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Then there is some
B\in\cal{B}
such that
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}
. Since
B
is a flat
R
-module, by
[
RG16a
, Proposition III.12]
,

\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is hereditary, i.e. for any
N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M
,
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\cap M_{0}=N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}
. We have

\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M}\\
\text{$M_{0}/N$ f.g.}\end{subarray}}N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M}\\
\text{$M_{0}/N$ f.g.}\end{subarray}}\left(N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\cap M_{0}\right)=N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\bigcap\left(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M}\\
\text{$M_{0}/N$ f.g.}\end{subarray}}M_{0}\right).

Note that
M/N
is the union of its finitely-generated submodules, so
M
can be written as the union of the
M_{0}
above. Hence the final step is equal to
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\cap M=N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}.
This implies that
u\in N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}
for some
N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M
with
M_{0}/N
finitely-generated. Hence
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{clfg}}
.

Now we show that
\tau_{\cal{B}}(R)=\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\cal{B}}(R)
. The forward inclusion always holds. For the reverse inclusion, suppose that
u\in\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\cal{B}}(R)
. We would like to show that for arbitrary
R
-modules
N\subseteq M
,
uN_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq N
.
Since
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}=N_{M}^{\operatorname{clfg}}
, for every
v\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, there is some
N\subseteq M_{0}\subseteq M
with
M_{0}/N
finitely-generated such that
v\in N_{M_{0}}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Hence
uv\in N
. This implies that
u\in\tau_{\cal{B}}(R)
, which gives us the result.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\operatorname{cl}
be a closure on a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
satisfying the first two Dietz axioms, functoriality and semi-residuality, and

be the injective hull of the residue field
k
. Let
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
denote the big test ideal associated to
\operatorname{cl}
. Then
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}\right)
. Additionally,
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
is the annihilator of

0^{\operatorname{clfg}}_{E}=\{u\in E\mid\text{ for some finitely-generated\ }E^{\prime}\subseteq E,u\in 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E^{\prime}}\}.

Proof.

C.f.
[
HH90
, Proposition 8.23]
.
By Lemma
3.3
,

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{M\text{ an $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\right).

We now show that
\bigcap_{M}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}
. That the first is contained in the second is clear. For the other inclusion let
u\in R-\{0\}
such that
u0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=0
, and let
M
be an
R
-module such that
u0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\neq 0
. Then there is some
x\in 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\subseteq M
such that
ux\neq 0
in
M
. Choose

maximal with respect to not containing

. Replace

by

and

by
\bar{x}
. By
[
Hoc07
, Lecture of September 17]
, every finitely-generated submodule of

has finite length and

spans its socle. Hence

spans the socle of

, and so
Rux\cong kux\cong k
, and

is an essential extension of this copy of

.
Hence we can embed

in

, and so by part (f) of Proposition
2.14
,

u\cdot 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}\subseteq u\cdot 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=0,

which contradicts our choice of

. The result follows.

Now we show that
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{clfg}}_{E}
. We have

\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\bigcap_{M\text{ f.g.}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{M}).

To see that this is contained in
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{clfg}}_{E}

is contained in

for some finitely-generated

. So an element

for every finitely-generated

will kill

. Hence
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{clfg}}_{E}
. For the reverse inclusion, let

such that

, and let

be a finitely-generated

-module such that

. The rest of the argument follows as for the non-finitely-generated case, with the addition to the last line that since

is finitely-generated,
u0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq u0_{E}^{\operatorname{clfg}}=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

B/I_{t}B
, this implies that
uy^{r}b_{i}\in I_{t+r}B
for each
i
. But this is exactly equivalent to
uy^{r}\in(I_{t+r})^{\operatorname{cl}}_{R}
. Since by assumption
(I_{t+r})^{\operatorname{cl}}_{R}=I_{t+r}
, we have
uy^{r}\in I_{t+r}
for sufficiently large values of

. This implies that
v=0
in
E
. Hence
0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=0
, and thus
\tau_{B}(R)=R
.
∎

We can use the previous result to give a similar result for families.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let

be a Gorenstein local ring and
\cal{B}
a directed family of

-algebras or a family of finitely-generated

\tau^{\text{fg}}{B}(R)=R
if and only if
\tau_{\cal{B}}(R)=R
.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}
. The piece we need to prove is that if
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
, then
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
. Suppose that
\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=R
.
Let

. Then there is some

such that

. For every

\operatorname{cl}_{B}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{B}}
. Since

\tau^{\text{fg}}_{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}(R)=R

, this implies that
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}(R)=R

0^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}_{E}=0
, whch implies that

.
∎

The following theorem connects test ideals with trace ideals, and is the key component of many of our results. This connects the idea of the test ideal with representation theoretic ideas.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
R
be local and
\operatorname{cl}=\operatorname{cl}_{B}
for some
R
-module
B
. If
B
is a finitely presented
R
-module or
R
is complete then

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)

Proof.

Let
E=E_{R}(k)
be the injective hull of the residue field
k
of
R
. By Proposition
3.9
,
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E})=(0:0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E})
; hence
c\in\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
if and only if
c\cdot 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=0
, but

0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=\bigcap_{b\in B}\ker(E\to B\otimes E),

where the map
E\to B\otimes E
corresponding to
b\in B
is given by
e\mapsto b\otimes e
.
Since
E
is Artinian, there are elements
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in B
such that this is equal to

\bigcap_{b\in\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\}}\ker(E\to B\otimes E).

We can rewrite this as
\ker(\phi)
, where
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}):E\to(B\otimes E)^{\oplus n}
sends

e\mapsto(b_{1}\otimes e,b_{2}\otimes e,\ldots,b_{n}\otimes e).

First, suppose that
c\in\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
, so that
c\cdot 0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}=c\ker(\phi)=0
. Then

0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{E}(c),

and by Matlis duality the map

\frac{\hat{R}}{c\hat{R}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(\operatorname{Ann}_{E}(c),E)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E},E)

is surjective. But applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\_,E)
to the exact sequence

0\to\ker(\phi)\to E\xrightarrow{\phi}(B\otimes E)^{\oplus n}

gives

\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E},E)=\frac{\hat{R}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(B\otimes\hat{R},\hat{R})\to\hat{R})},

where
i
th map
\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(B\otimes\hat{R},\hat{R})\to\hat{R}
is given by
\phi\mapsto\phi(b_{i})
. From the surjection
\hat{R}/c\hat{R}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}},E)
we can now conclude that

c\hat{R}\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\hat{R}}(B\otimes\hat{R},{\hat{R}})\to\hat{R}).

In the complete case, the denominator is contained in
\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)
, so this implies that
cR\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)
. In the case that
B
is finitely presented, since
\operatorname{Hom}
commutes with flat base change, the last expression is equal to

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,{R})\to R)\right)\otimes\hat{R}

It then follows by the faithful flatness of completion that

c\in\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,{R})\to R)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R).

For the reverse containment, suppose that
c\in\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)
. Then there are
b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m}
such that

c\in\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)\to R),

where the
i
th map
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)\to R
sends
f\mapsto f(b^{\prime}_{i})
. We can enlarge the set
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
from the setup to include
b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m}
. Then

c\in\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)\to R).

Hence we have a surjection

R/cR\to\frac{R}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R))}.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\_,E)
, we get an injection

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(B,R)\to R)},E\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/cR,E)=\operatorname{Ann}_{E}c.

But the module on the left is
0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Hence
c\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, which is equal to
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
.

∎

4.
Test ideals of Families

We extend the concept of test ideal introduced in the previous setting to that of families of modules. We can make this definition even when the family of modules does not give an idempotent closure operation, which is one way to deal with the question of how large the sum of the corresponding module closure operations can be (discussed in
[
R.G16b
, Section 9.2]
). We will then discuss the test ideals of specific families of big Cohen-Macaulay modules and algebras and connect them to the singularities of the ring.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of
R
-modules, not necessarily finitely-generated. We define the test ideal associated to
\mathcal{B}
as

\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R):=\bigcap_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\tau_{B}(R)

We list an immediate set of properties

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}
families of
R
-modules, then

(a)

\tau_{\emptyset}(R)=R
.

(b)

If
(0)\in\mathcal{B}
then
\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)=0
, in particular
\tau_{R-mod}(R)=0
.

(c)

If
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, then
\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)\supseteq\tau_{\mathcal{C}}(R)
.

(d)

\tau_{\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}}(R)=\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)\cap\tau_{\mathcal{C}}(R)
.

(e)

\tau_{\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{C}}(R)\supseteq\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)+\tau_{\mathcal{C}}(R)
.

Note that if
\mathcal{B}
is a directed family of
R
-algebras or of
R
-modules directed under generation, so that it defines a closure operation, then this definition of the test ideal agrees with our prior definition:

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{B}
is a directed family of
R
-algebras, or of
R
-modules directed under generation. Let
\operatorname{cl}
be the closure operation associated to
\mathcal{B}
. Then

\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)=\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{M\text{ an $R$-module}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{M}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{M}\bigcap_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\tau_{B}(R)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R).

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.12
(i.e.,
R
is complete local, or
R
is local and every
B\in\mathcal{B}
is finitely-presented),

\tau_{\mathcal{B}}(R)=\bigcap_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain. If
\cal{S}
is a directed family of
R
-algebras or a family of
R
-modules directed under generation (so that
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{S}}
is a closure operation), then
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{S}}
is trivial if and only if for every
S\in\cal{S}
,
S
has a free summand.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
and Corollary
4.4
,

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{S}}}(R)=\bigcap_{S\in\cal{S}}\operatorname{tr}_{S}(R).

We know that
\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{S}}
is trivial if and only if
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}_{\cal{S}}}=R
. The right hand side is equal to

if and only if
\operatorname{tr}_{S}(R)=R
for all

, which holds if and only if each

has a free summand (Lemma
2.27
, part (6)).

Alternatively, this follows from Definition

and Corollary

.
∎

Ideally, we want to consider the test ideal coming from the family of all Cohen-Macaulay modules, since a ring is regular if and only if the test ideals of these modules are equal to the whole ring by Corollary
3.5
. The collection of Cohen-Macaulay modules is not generally a set, so we work with the following family instead:

Remark 4.6
.

Let
R
be any Cohen-Macaulay ring and consider the full subcategory of
Mod(R)
consisting of big Cohen-Macaulay modules over
R
. For any set
S
the module
R^{S}
is in this subcategory, hence there is an embedding of the category of sets to the category of Cohen-Macaulay modules over
R
. The former is not a small category, so the latter is not a small category either.

To avoid this complication we restrict to a single representative for each isomorphism class of Cohen-Macaulay modules and bound the size of the modules we consider.
To do this, let
R
be a local ring and
Bas
be a fixed infinite set. Let

be the full subcategory of big Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules that are quotients of free
R
-modules

with

. This is a small category, and therefore we can consider the set of objects in this category.
For the purposes of this paper, it is enough for

to have countable order, and
we denote the set of objects by

.
Since isomorphic modules give the same closure operation, test ideal, and trace ideal, studying

is sufficient for our purposes.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain. We define the singular test ideal to be

\tau_{sing}(R)=\bigcap_{B\in CM}\tau_{B}(R),

where

is defined as in Remark

.

.

Let
(R,m,k)
be a complete local domain, then

is regular if and only if
\tau_{sing}(R)=R
.

Proof.

If

is regular, by Corollary
3.5
,
\tau_{B}(R)=R
for all big Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules

. Hence
\tau_{sing}(R)=R
.

If
\tau_{sing}(R)=R
, then
\tau_{B}(R)=R
for all countably-generated big Cohen-Macaulay

-modules

. Hence for such

,
\operatorname{cl}_{B}
is trivial on all submodules of all
R
-modules. Let
\operatorname{cl}
be a Dietz closure on finitely-generated
R
-modules. By Theorem 5.1 of
[

]
there exists a countably-generated big Cohen-Macaulay

The following results connect the test ideals of big Cohen-Macaulay modules to the singular locus of the ring, and are used to get more specific results on test ideals of big Cohen-Macaulay modules over rings with finite Cohen-Macaulay type.

Remark 4.9
.

In this paper, all finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay

Let

Suppose otherwise, then there exists

The following results connect the trace ideal, and hence the test ideal, to the socle of the ring (the set of elements annihilated by the maximal ideal
m
). Rings with nonzero socle are not reduced.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
(R,m)
be a local ring and
B
an
R
-module such that

Since

,

is a nontrivial

to

. In particular we have a surjection

. If

is an element of the socle of

, then there is a map from
B\to R/m\to R
that first sends
B
onto

and then to

via multiplication by

. Some element of

maps to

in

, and this maps to

in

. From this we see that
\text{soc}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{B}(R)
.

.

Let
R
be a local ring and

an

If

Proposition 4.19
.

Let

and

Proof.

Let

be a finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay module over

is a projective module over the regular ring
\bar{R}
. It follows that there is a surjective

Therefore for

For the last part of the result, note that if

We now discuss the test ideal given by the family of big Cohen-Macaulay

-algebras. The following result of Hochster indicates that tight closure on finitely-generated

Theorem 4.20

(
[

)

Let

Definition 4.21
.

Let

be the subcategory of Cohen-Macaulay
R
-algebras with basis elements obtained from

We can also define the finitistic version,

\tau_{CMA}^{fg}=\bigcap_{B\in CMA}\tau_{B}^{fg}(R).

The following result indicates why big Cohen-Macaulay algebra test ideals are a good tight closure replacement.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let

be a complete local domain of characteristic
p>0
. Then
\tau_{CMA}^{fg}(R)
as defined above is equal to the finitistic tight closure test ideal.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.20
, for finitely-generated
R
-modules

,
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B}}\subseteq N_{M}^{*}
for every big Cohen-Macaulay algebra

. This implies that

\bigcap_{B\in CMA}\tau_{B}^{fg}(R)\supseteq\tau_{*}^{fg}(R).

For the other direction, note that for each finitely-generated
R
-module

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n_{M}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B_{i,M}}}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{*}.

\bigcap_{M\text{ f.g.}}\bigcap_{i=1}^{n_{M}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}_{B_{i,M}}}\subseteq\bigcap_{M\text{ f.g.}}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{M}^{*}.

Examples

In this section we compute test ideals and trace ideals. In these examples, we compute

Example 5.1
.

Let
R
be a complete PID. Then for any family of
R
-modules

\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=0
or
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=R
. Indeed, if

then it is a principal ideal

\operatorname{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=R

If

.

But this is not always true in the general one-dimensional case, as the following example shows.

Example 5.2

Let

The following example is of a ring with countable Cohen-Macaulay type whose singular test ideal is not primary to the maximal ideal. This indicates that Proposition
4.14
does not hold even for fairly nice rings with infinite Cohen-Macaulay type.

Example 5.5
.

Let
R=k[[x,y,z]]/(x^{2}y+z^{2})

is a field of arbitrary characteristic. This ring is known as the

hypersurface singularity and as the Whitney Umbrella. By

•

M=\operatorname{coker}\begin{pmatrix}z&y\\
-x^{2}&z\end{pmatrix},

•

N=\operatorname{coker}\begin{pmatrix}z&xy\\
-x&z\end{pmatrix}

•

M_{j}=\operatorname{coker}\begin{pmatrix}z&0&xy&0\\
0&z&y^{j+1}&-xy\\
-x&0&z&0\\
-y^{j}&x&0&z\end{pmatrix}

•

N_{j}=\operatorname{coker}\begin{pmatrix}z&0&xy&0\\
0&z&y^{j}&-x\\
-x&0&z&0\\
-y^{j}&xy&0&z\end{pmatrix}

Let’s compute the corresponding test ideals. As the ring
R
is a complete local domain, by Theorem
3.12
we only need to compute the trace ideal of
R
with respect to these modules.

•

M
: A map
\phi
from
M
to
R
is the same as a map from
R^{2}\to R
whose kernel contains
<(z,x^{2}),(-y,z)>
. That is, we must have that
z\phi(e_{1})+x^{2}\phi(e_{2})=-y\phi(e_{1})+z\phi(e_{2})=0
, or in an equivalent way, we want solutions for

\begin{pmatrix}z&-x^{2}\\
y&z\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix}=0

with
a,b\in R
. We first find the solutions in the fraction field and then determine when they are in
R
. To do this, we row reduce this matrix by multiplying the second row by
x^{2}
and then adding the
z
times the first row, which gives us

\begin{pmatrix}z&-x^{2}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix}=0.

This means that we need
az=bx^{2}
. As we want
a,b\in R
, this is equivalent to saying
a\in x^{2}:z
and
b\in z:x^{2}
. It follows that
\tau_{M}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{M}(R)=(x^{2}:z)+(z:x^{2})
. As both ideals are proper,
\operatorname{tr}_{M}(R)\neq R
. Now, note that from the equation

we have that
(x^{2},y,z)=\operatorname{tr}_{M}(R)
.

•

N: A similar procedure implies
\tau_{N}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{N}(R)=(z:x)+(x:z)
, which is equal to
(xy,z)+(z,x)=(x,z)
.

•

M_{j}
: After transposing and row reducing we obtain the system

\displaystyle az-cx-dy^{j}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle bz+dx

\displaystyle=0.

Some possibilities that satisfy this equation are (found in Macaulay2 for particular values of

\begin{array}[]{c | c | c | c}a&b&c&d\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr x&0&z&0\\
\hline\cr(-1)^{j}y^{j}&x&0&(-1)^{j}z\\
\hline\cr-z&0&xy&0\\
\hline\cr 0&x&-y^{j+1}&xy\\
\end{array}

Hence
(x,y^{j},z)\subseteq\tau_{M_{j}}(R).
(In fact, computations in Macaulay2 confirm that these choices generate all maps

, so the two ideals are equal.)

•

N_{j}
: As in the previous case, transposing and row reducing we obtain the system

\displaystyle az-cx-dy^{j}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle bz+dxy

\displaystyle=0.

In particular the following are solutions to this set of equations

\begin{array}[]{c | c | c | c}a&b&c&d\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr x&0&z&0\\
\hline\cr y^{j}&-xy&0&z\\
\hline\cr-z&0&xy&0\\

(x,y^{j},z)\subseteq\tau_{N_{j}}(R)

From this we can conclude that the intersection of
\tau_{B}(R)
over all finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay

(x^{2},y,z)\cap(x,z)\cap(x,y^{j},z)\cap(x,y^{j},z)=(x^{2},xy,z).

Notice that this is not primary to the maximal ideal, and so the singular test ideal, which is contained in this ideal, is also not

-primary.

\displaystyle\mapsto 0\text{ for all other $i$.}

To see that this is an
R
-linear map, we check that the action of
z
is compatible with the map. We have

z\psi(e_{n+1})=zx
and

\psi(ze_{n+1})=\psi(xe_{1})=x\psi(e_{1})=xz.

Additionally,
z\psi(e_{n+2})=zy
and

\psi(ze_{n+2})=\psi(ye_{1}+xe_{2})=y\psi(e_{1})+x\psi(e_{2})=yz+0=yz,

and
z\psi(e_{1})=z^{2}=0=\psi(0)=\psi(ze_{1}).

For
1<i<n
,
\psi(ze_{i})=\psi(0)=0=z\psi(e_{i}).

For
i>n+2
,
ze_{i}
is in terms of
e_{j}
for
1<j\leq n
, so
0=z\psi(e_{i})=\psi(ze_{i}).

The existence of this map shows that
(x,y,z)\subseteq\operatorname{tr}_{M_{n}}(R)
for
n\geq 2
. Hence

(x,y,z)\subseteq\bigcap_{n}\operatorname{tr}_{M_{n}}(R).

To see that these are in fact equal, suppose there is a map
\psi:M_{n}\to R
sending
e_{i}\mapsto 1
for some
i
. If
i\leq n
, we have

z=z\psi(e_{i})=\psi(ze_{i})=0,

which is a contradiction.
If
i=n+1
, we have

z=z\psi(e_{i})=\psi(ze_{i})=\psi(xe_{1})=x\psi(e_{1}),

which is also a contradiction as
z\not\in(x)R
.
If
i>n+1
, we have

z=z\psi(e_{i})=\psi(ye_{n-i-1}+xe_{n-i})=y\psi(e_{n-i-1})+x\psi(e_{n-i}),

which is a contradiction since
z\not\in(x,y)R
.
Hence
1\not\in\operatorname{tr}_{M_{n}}(R)
, which implies that

\operatorname{tr}_{M_{n}}(R)=(x,y,z).

Remark 5.9
.

Given Proposition
2.20
it is natural to ask whether
\tau_{T}(R)\subseteq\tau_{S}(R)
if and only if
S
generates
T
. Note that the “if” part follows from Proposition
2.20
. But as Example
5.10
shows the other direction is false, even in the case of finitely-generated Cohen-Macaulay modules.

Example 5.10
.

Let
R=k[[x,y,z]]/(xy-z^{4})
where
k
has characteristic 0 (or most values of
p
are also fine). We can view
R
as a subring of
S=k[[s,t]]
, via
x\mapsto s^{4}
,
y\mapsto t^{4}
, and
z\mapsto st
. The indecomposable MCM’s of
R
are
R
,
M_{1}=(s,t^{3})\cong(y,z)
,
M_{2}=(s^{2},t^{2})\cong(y,z^{2})
, and
M_{3}=(s^{3},t)\cong(x,z)

[
LW12
]
. According to Macaulay2,
H=\operatorname{Hom}(M_{1},R)=\operatorname{Im}\begin{pmatrix}y&z^{3}\\
z&x\\
\end{pmatrix}
, and using the function
\text{homomorphism}(H_{\{i\}})
for
i=0,1
, we see that the homomorphisms
M\to R
are as follows: one of them is given by
s\mapsto y
and
t^{3}\mapsto z
, and the other by
s\mapsto z^{3}
and
t^{3}\mapsto x
.

Similarly,
\operatorname{Hom}(M_{3},R)=\operatorname{Im}\begin{pmatrix}x&z^{3}\\
z&y\\
\end{pmatrix}
, and the homomorphisms send
s^{3}\mapsto x
,
t\mapsto z
, or
s^{3}\mapsto z^{3}
,
t\mapsto y
.

So
\operatorname{tr}_{M_{1}}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{M_{3}}(R)=m
. But
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
are distinct indecomposable Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules, so neither generates the other. As
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
are finitely-generated
R
-modules,
\operatorname{tr}_{M_{i}}(R)=\tau_{M_{i}}(R)
for
i=1,3
, so
M_{1}
and
M_{3}
are two
R
-modules that give the same test ideal, but neither one generates the other.

6.
Mixed Characteristic

Recently, André proved the existence of big Cohen-Macaulay algebras in mixed characteristic
[
And18
]
.
We take advantage of this result and of almost big Cohen-Macaulay algebras as defined by Roberts
[
Rob10
]
and used by André to define a closure operation in mixed characteristic, and to prove that the corresponding test ideal can be written as a variant on a trace ideal, paralleling our results in previous sections. This demonstrates that the arguments used in earlier sections can be adapted to apply to closures that are variations on module closures.

Our closure is similar to dagger closure as defined by Hochster and Huneke
[
HH91
]
. The key difference is that we have replaced
R^{+}
, the absolute integral closure of
R
, with an arbitrary almost big Cohen-Macaulay algebra. We are also using small powers of a particular element as our “test elements", as is usual in working with perfectoid algebras, rather than using arbitrary elements of small order as in
[
HH91
]
.

In this section, let
(R,m)
be a complete local domain of dimension
d>0
and mixed characteristic
(0,p)
,
T
a
p
-torsion free algebra, and
\pi\in T
a non-zerodivisor such that
T
contains a compatible system of
p
-power roots of
\pi
, i.e. a set of elements
\{\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that
(\pi^{1/{p^{n}}})^{p^{m}}=\pi^{1/{p^{n-m}}}
for all
m\leq n
.
We will denote this system of
p
-power roots of
\pi
by
\pi^{1/{p^{\infty}}}
.

Definition 6.1

(
[
And18
, Definition 4.1.1.3]
)
.

T
is an
almost (balanced) big Cohen-Macaulay algebra with respect to
\pi^{1/{p^{\infty}}}
if
T/mT
is not almost 0 with respect to
\pi^{1/{p^{\infty}}}
(i.e., it is not the case that
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}T/mT=0
for all
n>0
), and for every system of parameters
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
on
R
,

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\cdot\frac{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}):_{T}x_{i+1}}{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})}=0

for all
n>0
,
0\leq i\leq d-1
.

André proved the existence of almost big Cohen-Macaulay algebras as a step on the way to proving the existence of big Cohen-Macaulay algebras. The reason we have included this “intermediate" step in our paper (rather than focusing solely on big Cohen-Macaulay algebras) is that almost mathematics is central to major results in mixed characteristic commutative algebra, and our techniques can be applied to this case. This also connects our results to the recent work of
[
MS18a
]
on a mixed characteristic version of a test ideal for pairs in regular rings, which is defined using an almost big Cohen-Macaulay algebra.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
T
be an almost big Cohen-Macaulay algebra over
R
. We define a closure operation cl by
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
if for all
n>0
,

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes_{R}N\to T\otimes_{R}M).

Proposition 6.3
.

The closure cl defined above is a Dietz closure. Consequently,
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E_{R}(k)}
.

Proof.

First, we show that cl gives a closure operation. Let
N\subseteq M
be
R
-modules. It is clear that
N\subseteq N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Additionally, if
N\subseteq N^{\prime}\subseteq M
, and
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, then for all
n>0
,

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N^{\prime}\to T\otimes M).

Hence
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq(N^{\prime})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. It remains to show that cl is idempotent. Suppose that
u\in(N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}})_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Then for all
n>0
,

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\to T\otimes M).

So we can write
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u=\sum t_{i}\otimes m_{i}
, with the
m_{i}\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Hence

\pi^{1/{p^{n^{\prime}}}}t_{i}\otimes m_{i}\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)

for all
i
and for all
n^{\prime}>0
. This implies that for all
n,n^{\prime}>0
,

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\pi^{1/{p^{n^{\prime}}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M).

In particular,
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)
for all
n>0
. Multiplying by
\pi^{(p-2)/p^{n}}
, we get
\pi^{1/{p^{n-1}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)
for all
n>0
, so
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
.

Next we prove that cl is functorial. Suppose that
f:M\to W
is a map of
R
-modules, and
N\subseteq M
. Let
u\in N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Then
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)
for all
n>0
, i.e.
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u=\sum t_{i}\otimes n_{i}
with each
n_{i}\in N
. Applying
1\otimes f
, we get
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes f(u)=\sum t_{i}\otimes f(n_{i})
. Since each
f(n_{i})\in f(N)
, we have
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes f(u)\in(f(N))_{W}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, as desired.

To prove semi-residuality, suppose that
N_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}=N
. Let
u\in M
such that
\bar{u}\in 0_{M/N}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Then
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes\bar{u}=0
in
T\otimes M/N
, which by right exactness of tensor products implies that
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes N\to T\otimes M)
. Hence
u\in N
, which implies that
\bar{u}=0
. Hence
0_{M/N}^{\operatorname{cl}}=0
.

For faithfulness, suppose that
u\in m_{R}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Then
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}u\in mT
for all
n>0
. If
u\not\in m
, then
u
is a unit, so this implies that
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\in mT
for all
n>0
. But then
T/mT
is almost zero, which is a contradiction.

For generalized colon-capturing, suppose
f:M\twoheadrightarrow R/I
, where
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}
is part of a system of parameters for
R
, and let
v\in M
such that
f(v)={\bar{x}}_{k+1}
. Let
u\in(Rv)_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}\cap\operatorname{Ker}(f)
. Then

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes Rv\to T\otimes M)

for all
n>0
. So
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u=t_{n}\otimes v
for some
t_{n}\in T
. Hence

0=(\text{id}\otimes f)(\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u)=(\text{id}\otimes f)(t_{n}\otimes v)=t_{n}\otimes f(v)

in
T\otimes R/I
. So
t_{n}x_{k+1}\in IT
. Hence
\pi^{1/{p^{n^{\prime}}}}t_{n}\in IT
for all
n^{\prime}>0
. So

\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\pi^{1/{p^{n^{\prime}}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(IT\otimes Rv\to T\otimes M)=\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes Iv\to T\otimes M)

for all
n,n^{\prime}>0
. As in the proof of idempotence, this implies that
\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes u\in\operatorname{Im}(T\otimes Iv\to T\otimes M)
for all
n>0
. Therefore
u\in(Iv)_{M}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, which completes the proof of generalized colon-capturing.

As a corollary to Proposition
3.9
, since cl is residual,
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E_{R}(k)}
.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Let cl be the closure from Definition
6.2
. We define

\operatorname{tr}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\sum_{n>0}\sum_{\psi:T\to R}\psi(\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}T)=\sum_{n>0}\operatorname{Im}(T^{*}\otimes\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}T\to R),

where
T^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)
and the map sends
h\otimes x\mapsto h(x)
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain and let cl be the closure defined above. Then

\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{tr}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R).

Proof.

Let
E=E_{R}(k)
be the injective hull of the residue field
k
of
R
. By Proposition
3.9
,
\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E})=(0:0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E})
; hence
c\in\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
if and only if
c\cdot 0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=0
, but

0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}=\bigcap_{n>0}\ker(E\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}T\otimes E),

where
\alpha_{n}
is given by
e\mapsto\pi^{1/{p^{n}}}\otimes e
.
Since
E
is Artinian, there are elements
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}>0
such that this is equal to

\bigcap_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\ker(E\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}T\otimes E).

We can rewrite this as
\ker(\phi)
, where
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{t}):E\to(B\otimes E)^{\oplus t}
sends

e\mapsto(\pi^{1/{p^{n_{1}}}}\otimes e,\pi^{1/{p^{n_{2}}}}\otimes e,\ldots,\pi^{1/{p^{n_{t}}}}\otimes e).

First, suppose
c\in\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
, so that
c\cdot 0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}=c\ker(\phi)=0
. Then

0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{E}(c),

and by Matlis duality the map

\frac{R}{cR}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\operatorname{Ann}_{E}(c),E)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E},E)

is surjective. But applying Matlis duality to the exact sequence

0\to\ker(\phi)\to E\xrightarrow{\phi}(T\otimes E)^{\oplus t}

gives

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(0^{\operatorname{cl}}_{E},E)=\frac{R}{\sum_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R)},

where the maps
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R
are given by
\psi\mapsto\psi(\pi^{1/{p^{n_{i}}}})
for each
n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}\}
. From the surjection
R/cR\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}},E)
we can now conclude that

cR\subseteq\sum_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,{R})\to R).

This gives us the desired result.

For the reverse containment, suppose that
c\in\operatorname{tr}_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
. Then there are
n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{s}>0
such that
c\in\sum_{n\in\{n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{s}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R)
, where the
i
th map
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R
sends
f\mapsto f(\pi^{1/{p^{n^{\prime}_{i}}}})
. We can enlarge the set
n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}
to include
n^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,n^{\prime}_{s}
. Then
c\in\sum_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R)
. Hence we have a surjection

R/cR\to\frac{R}{\sum_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R)}.

Applying Matlis duality, we get an injection

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}\left(\frac{R}{\left(\sum_{n\in\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}}\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(T,R)\to R)\right)},E\right)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R/cR,E)=\operatorname{Ann}_{E}c.

But the module on the left is
0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}
. Hence
c\in\operatorname{Ann}_{R}0_{E}^{\operatorname{cl}}
, so
c\in\tau_{\operatorname{cl}}(R)
.
∎

Determinants, even instantons and Bridgeland stability
Victor do Valle Pretti

Abstract
. We provide a systematic way of calculating a quiver region associated to a given exceptional collection, which as an application is used to prove that μ \mu -stable sheaves represented by two-step complexes are Bridgeland stable. In the later sections, we focus on the case of even rank 2 2 instantons over ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} and Q 3 Q_{3} to prove that the instanton sheaves, instanton bundles and perverse instantons are Bridgeland stable and provide a description of the moduli space near their only actual wall.

Recently, a number of papers delved into the question of the Bridgeland stability of the instantons over Fano threefolds,
[
35
,
34
,
39
]
. A common theme in their proofs is the use of the Kuznetsov component and the fact that instantons of minimal charge are in the Kuznetsov component. This approach allows for not choosing a bases for the exceptional collection, which in some Fano threefolds is necessary due to the nonexistence of a full strong exceptional collection. On the other hand, our approach in this paper is able to achieve their stability for every charge for some of the cases where these exceptional collections are known to exist.

The paper is organized as follows: the first two sections describe two already established theories of stability, Bridgeland stability and quiver stability. The next section deals with a general theory of exceptional collections and a base for the theory developed later in the paper. Most of what is done in these sections is well known.

In the forth section we define and describe how to calculate the quiver region in a systematic way, this is done as consequence of Proposition
5.5

In the next sections we describe the stability of instanton sheaves, instanton bundles and of a special class perverse instantons for

such that

with relations

will be denoted by
(Q,J)

T:V_{i}\rightarrow W_{i}
for each
i\in Q_{0}
, such that
T_{t(a)}\circ f_{a}=g_{a}\circ T_{t(a)}
. Moreover, if
(Q,J)
is a quiver with relation it is said that a representation of
Q

(V,(f_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}})
satisfies the relation
J
if the compositions induced by the elements in
J
are equal to
0
as linear transformations.

With that definition it is possible to define the categories
\operatorname{Rep}(Q)
and its subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,J)
of representations of
Q
and representations satisfying the relation
J
, respectively. These categories are both abelian. A dimension vector
\underline{\dim}:\operatorname{Rep}(Q,J)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
is well defined by letting
\underline{\dim}((V,(f_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}}))=(\dim(V_{i}))_{i\in Q_{0}}
.

In
[
21
]
, King explored the idea of defining stable representations defined by a GIT quotient in terms of a linear relation on the dimension vector of subrepresentations. Fix
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
, a representation
(V,(f_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}})
is said to be
\theta
-(semi)stable if

\theta\cdot\underline{\dim}(V,(f_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}}):=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\theta_{i}\dim(V_{i})=0

and for every subrepresentation
(W,(g_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}})
of
(V,(f_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}})
,

\theta\cdot\underline{\dim}(W,(g_{h})_{h\in Q_{1}})\leq(<)0.

With this notion of stability is possible to define the moduli space of (semi)stable representations of a quiver with relations
(Q,J)
with given dimension vector
d
with respect to
\theta
as
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{Q,J,\theta}(d)
(
\mathcal{M}^{ss}_{Q,J,\theta}(d)
), where we identify the (semi)stable objects that have the same Jordan-Hölder filtration in
\operatorname{Rep}(Q,J)
. When
J=0
we will suppress the subscript
J
from the notation. Further information can be found at
[
21
,
28
]
.

One way to construct the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{Q,J,\theta}(d)
and
\mathcal{M}^{ss}_{Q,J,\theta}(d)
is by constructing a GIT quotient of the space
R_{d}=\bigoplus_{h\in Q_{1}}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{s(h)},V_{t(h)})
by the algebraic group of product of nonsingular linear transformations
G_{d}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}Gl_{i}
, acting via conjugation, which descends to
PG_{d}=G_{d}/k^{\ast}(\operatorname{Id})_{i\in Q_{0}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

[
28
, Remark 4.1.15]

For a given
\theta
and dimension vector

,
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{Q,\theta}(d)
is a smooth variety of dimension
\chi(d)=\Sigma_{h\in Q_{1}}d_{s(h)}d_{t(h)}-\Sigma_{i\in Q_{0}}d_{i}^{2}+1
. For the case of the Kronecker quiver

with dimension vector

or

, it means that
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{Q.\theta}(d)
has dimension

.

Remark 3.4
.

[
28
, Remark 4.1.15]

The moduli space of semistable representations

is a projective variety and the loci of stable representations

is an open subset of
\mathcal{M}^{ss}_{Q,J,\theta}(d)

is
\theta

Appendix: Computational Observations

As seen in the paper, the construction of examples of Ext-exceptional collections is very important to establishing exceptional regions. One way of constructing these collections is by shifting the objects in a strong exceptional collection by the appropriate degree. For
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, it is a known result by Rudakov
[
36
]
that the only strong exceptional collections we can create are mutations of the canonical exceptional collection , proved to be strong by Beilinson
[
5
]
.

Question 1
.

Is there an Ext-exceptional collection in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
satisfying the upper-half plane for any point in
\mathbb{H}
with
\alpha>2
and
s=\frac{1}{3}
?

In this appendix we describe a computational approach that lead to Question
1
. There is no known list of all full exceptional collections in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
but there exists a conjecture about the transitivity of the action of the group
B_{n}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}^{n}
over the space of strong exceptional collections, where
B_{n}
is the
n
-braid group acting by mutations and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the action by shifts, see
[
7
]
. Our idea was to use mutations to inductively define strong exceptional collections, with easy to compute Chern characters due to their additive property, and test for the upper-half plane condition using a parametrization of lines going through the origin.

We start by defining the notion of mutation of an exceptional collection and their computational properties.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\{E_{0},E_{1}\}
be a exceptional pair in
\operatorname*{D}^{b}(X)
, a left (resp. right) mutation of the exceptional pair is the object
\mathcal{L}_{E_{0}}E_{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{R}_{E_{1}}E_{0}
) defined by the following distinguished triangles

\mathcal{L}_{E_{0}}E_{1}\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(E_{0},E_{1})\otimes E_{0}\rightarrow E_{1}

(E_{0}\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(E_{0},E_{1})^{\ast}\otimes E_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{E_{1}}E_{0}).

Definition 8.2
.

For an exceptional collection
\mathcal{E}=\{E_{0},...,E_{k}\}
in
\operatorname*{D}^{b}(X)
we can define the Left and Right mutations of
\mathcal{E}
by

\mathcal{L}_{i}\mathcal{E}=\{E_{0},...,E_{i-1},\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i+1},E_{i},E_{i+2},...,E_{k}\}

\mathcal{R}_{i}\mathcal{E}=\{E_{0},...,E_{i+1},\mathcal{R}_{E_{i+1}}E_{i},E_{i+2},...,E_{k}\}

It is known that a mutation of an exceptional collection is also an exceptional collection, they also preserve fullness. That is not true for strong exceptional collections, in general, but in our case this is actually true.

The following theorem describe what happens when mutating a strong exceptional collection and the nature of the objects in a strong exceptional collection.

Theorem 8.3
.

[
6
, Section 9]

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}=\langle E_{0},...,E_{n}\rangle
is a full exceptional collection of sheaves in
\operatorname*{D^{b}}(X)
for a
n
-dimensional manifold
X
. Then
\mathcal{E}
is a strong exceptional collection and any mutation of
\mathcal{E}
is a full strong exceptional collection.

Theorem 8.4
.

[
33
]

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety for which
n=\dim\operatorname{K}_{0}(X)-1=\dim(X)
. Then for any full strong exceptional collection
E_{0},...,E_{n}
in
\operatorname*{D^{b}}(X)
, the objects
E_{i}
are shifts of locally free sheaves by the same number
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For a smooth

dimensional projective variety X, the Theorems

and

can be applied in conjunction so that

is a strong exceptional collection, because otherwise
\mathcal{L}_{E_{i}}E_{i+1}=E_{i+1}[1]

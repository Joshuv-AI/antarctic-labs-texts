Entanglement Measure Based on Optimal Entanglement Witness
Nan Yang 1 , Jiaji Wu 1 , Xianyun Dong 1 , Longyu Xiao 1 , Jing Wang 1 , Ming Li 1

Abstract
We introduce a new entanglement measure based on optimal entanglement witness. First of all, we show that the entanglement measure satisfies some necessary properties, including zero entanglements for all separable states, convexity, continuity, invariance under local unitary operations and non-increase under local operations and classical communication(LOCC). More than that, we give a specific mathematical expression for the lower bound of this entanglement measure for any bipartite mixed states. We further improve the lower bound for 2 ⊗ \otimes 2 systems. Finally, we numerically simulate the lower bound of several types of specific quantum states.



1
Introduction

Entanglement is one of the most remarkable features of quantum mechanics, which has been recognized as an essential resource in quantum information theory
[
1
,
2
]
. In recent years, quantum entanglement is increasingly the focus of people’s attention and is widely applied to quantum information processing tasks
[
3
]
.

Since entanglement is a newly discovered state of resources, it’s of vital importance to discover the mathematical structure behind its theory. Entanglement measure is the quantization of entanglement and various entanglement measures have been well defined. Arindam Lala
[
4
]
has studied various entanglement measures associated with certain non-conformal field theories. And Jacob L. Beckey et.al.
[
5
]
have proposed entanglement measures that are computable and operationally meaningful multipartite.

Entanglement witness(EW) gives a sufficient condition for detecting entanglement, which is equivalent to making a hyperplane in the quantum state space to separate some separable states from entangled states. In Ref.
[
6
]
, the authors have studied the optimal EW(OEW) based on local orthogonal observables. And the optimal entanglement witness constructed by Ref.
[
7
]
could effectively detect the entangled states produced by cooper pair splitters.

In this paper, we introduce a new entanglement measure of bipartite quantum states, which is closely related to optimal entanglement witness. In Sec.III, we prove explicitly that this new measure satisfies many properties of bipartite entanglement measures. In Sec.IV, we obtain the lower bound of this measure for any bipartite mixed states. Furthermore, considering the property that there is a double cover relationship between SU(2) group and SO(3) group, we give a better lower bound on
2\otimes 2
space. Finally, we present numerical simulations of the lower bound for some specific forms of quantum states.

2
Construction of Entanglement Measure

In Ref.
[
8
]
, we know that EW could be the operator in the form of
\alpha
I+L
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
be arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. If an EW
W_{E}
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
can be written in the following form:

\displaystyle W_{E}=\alpha(L)I-L,

(1)

where
I
is the identity operator,
L
is a self-adjoint operator, and
\alpha(L)
is the maximum of expectation values taken over all separable pure states
|\varphi\rangle
:

\displaystyle\alpha=\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|L|\varphi\rangle,

(2)

then this EW is called an OEW. We use the results of this OEW to quantify entanglement.

Definition 1
.

Let
M_{a}
be a positive semi-definite set

M_{a}=\{H\geq 0\ |\ H\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B},tr(H)=a,a\in R^{+}\}.

(3)

The entanglement measure
W_{E}
of a bipartite state
\rho\in(\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B})
is given by

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)=\max\limits_{L\in M_{a}}\{-tr(W_{E}\rho)\}.

(4)

We define
||M||_{KF}=Tr[\sqrt{MM^{\dagger}}]
,
||M||_{HS}=\sqrt{Tr[MM^{\dagger}]}
and
||M||_{2}=\sigma_{max}(M)
. They respectively represent the trace norm, Frobenius norm and 2-norm of the matrix
M
. Meanwhile, we could also get the following result according to the relevant knowledge of EW.

\displaystyle tr(W_{E}\rho)<0,

(5)

\displaystyle tr(W_{E}\sigma)\geq 0,

(6)

where
\rho
is an arbitrary entangled state acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and
\sigma
is an arbitrary separable state acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. It’s not difficult to see that
W_{E}
is one type of OEW, and we could get the above inequalities from the Ref.
[
9
]
.

3
Properties of Entanglement Measure

For
\forall\rho\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, an entanglement measure is a functional
C
defined on the set of density operators on the Hilbert space. A well-defined measure of entanglement
C
should satisfy the following requirements:

(E1)
C(\rho)=0
, for any
\rho\in Sep
(zero for all separable states).

(E2)
C(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})
\leq\lambda C(\rho_{1})+(1-\lambda)C(\rho_{2})
, for all
\rho_{1}
,
\rho_{2}
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
(convexity).

(E3) Keeping invariant under local unitary transformations, i.e.,

C((U_{1}\otimes U_{2})^{\dagger}\rho(U_{1}\otimes U_{2}))=C(\rho)

holds for all unitary operators
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
.

(E4)
C(\Lambda_{LOCC}(\rho))
\leq C(\rho)
(nonincreasing under local operations and classical communication (LOCC)).

(E5) When
\|\rho-\sigma\|\rightarrow 0
,
C(\rho)-C(\sigma)\rightarrow 0
(continuity).

Subsequently, we will prove that our entanglement measure defined in Eq.(
4
) fulfills these requirements.

Propositon 1
.

C_{w}(\rho)
satisfies property (E2).

Proof
.

Let
\rho=\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2}
, where
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. By substituting it into
C_{w}(\rho)
we have

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{w}(\lambda\rho_{1}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{2})

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+\lambda tr(L\rho_{1})+(1-\lambda)tr(L\rho_{2})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{\lambda(-\alpha(L)+tr(L\rho_{1}))+(1-\lambda)(-\alpha(L)+tr(L\rho_{2}))\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda C_{w}(\rho_{1})+(1-\lambda)C_{w}(\rho_{2}).

∎

Propositon 2
.

C_{w}(\rho)
satisfies property (E1).

Proof
.

Assume that
\rho
is a separable and pure state acting on the space of
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, i.e.
\rho
could be written as
\rho=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}=|\varphi_{1}\rangle\langle\varphi_{1}|\otimes|\varphi_{2}\rangle\langle\varphi_{2}|
, where
|\varphi_{1}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
,
|\varphi_{2}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Then we have

\displaystyle tr(W_{E}\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tr(\alpha\rho-L\rho)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tr(\alpha)-tr(L\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tr(\max\langle\varphi^{A}|\otimes\langle\varphi^{B}|L|\varphi^{A}\rangle\otimes|\varphi^{B}\rangle)-tr(L\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{\rho^{A*},\rho^{B*}}tr(L\rho^{A*}\otimes\rho^{B*})-tr(L\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}),

where
\rho^{A*}=|\varphi^{A*}\rangle\langle\varphi^{A*}|
and
\rho^{B*}=|\varphi^{B*}\rangle\langle\varphi^{B*}|
. According to the definition and Eq.(
6
) we have
-tr(W_{E}\rho)\leq 0
. Let
L=\frac{a}{n}I
, where
n
is the dimension of
L
, we have
W_{E}=0
. Thus the entanglement degree for the separable pure states is 0, which illustrate that

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)=\max\limits_{L}\{-tr(W_{E}\rho)\}=0.

(9)

The above-mentioned fact is discussed when
\rho
is a separable pure state acting on the space of
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. If
\rho
is a separable mixed state, we have a decomposition of
\rho
:

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\varphi_{1i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{1i}|\otimes|\varphi_{2i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{2i}|.

(10)

According to property (E2), we know
C_{w}(\rho)
satisfies

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\leq\sum_{i}p_{i}C(|\varphi_{1i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|\otimes|\varphi_{2i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{2i}|)=0.

(11)

Note that when
L=\frac{a}{n}I
, we have
C_{w}(\rho)=0
. Thus for
\forall\rho
that is separable,
C_{w}(\rho)=0
.
∎

Propositon 3
.

C_{w}(\rho)
has local unitary transformation invariance, satisfying property(E3).

Proof
.

For
U^{\dagger}\rho U
, we write the measure as:

\displaystyle C_{w}(U^{\dagger}\rho U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-tr(W_{E}U^{\dagger}\rho U)\}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(LU^{\dagger}\rho U)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(ULU^{\dagger}\rho)\}.

Since
L
is positive semi-definite, we have
\langle\varphi|L|\varphi\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle\varphi|{L}^{\prime}|\varphi\rangle=(\langle\varphi|U)L(U^{\dagger}|\varphi\rangle)\geq 0
for any
|\varphi\rangle
, so
{L}^{\prime}
is also positive semi-definite. Furthermore, the unitary transformation does not change the eigenvalues, so
tr({L}^{\prime})=tr(L)=a
. As
U
is a local unitary matrix,
|\varphi^{*}\rangle=U^{\dagger}|\varphi\rangle
is still a separable pure state. In addition, when
|\varphi\rangle
takes all separable states,
|\varphi^{*}\rangle
could also take all the separable states owing to the fact that
U^{\dagger}
is reversible, then

\displaystyle\alpha(ULU^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|ULU^{\dagger}|\varphi\rangle

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}(\langle\varphi|U)L(U^{\dagger}|\varphi\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle^{*}\in sep}\langle\varphi^{*}|L|\varphi^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|L|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(L).

When
L
takes all Hermitian matrices,
ULU^{\dagger}
can also take all Hermitian matrices. For
\forall L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
, we have
L=U^{\dagger}L^{\prime}U\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
which satisfies
L^{\prime}=ULU^{\dagger}
. Therefore

\displaystyle C_{w}(U^{\dagger}\rho U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(ULU^{\dagger}\rho)\}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{U^{\dagger}LU}\{-\alpha(ULU^{\dagger})+tr(ULU^{\dagger}\rho)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L^{\prime}}\{-\alpha(L^{\prime})+tr(L^{\prime}\rho)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho).

∎

Propositon 4
.

C_{w}(\rho)
is entangled monotone with respect to separable operation
\varepsilon(\rho)
, where

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\rho)=\sum_{i}(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})\rho(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger),

(15)

and operator completeness is satisfied, which means
\sum_{i}E_{i}E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}F_{i}\dagger=I_{\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}}
.

Proof
.

We have

\displaystyle C_{w}(\varepsilon(\rho))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(L\varepsilon(\rho))\}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(L\sum_{i}(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})\rho(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger))\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L}\{-\alpha(L)+tr(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})\rho)\}.

Firstly, we prove
\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))
conforms to the definition of
L
, i.e. it satisfies the properties of
L
in the definition

\displaystyle tr(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))=tr(\sum_{i}(E_{i}E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}F_{i}\dagger)L)=tr(L)=a>0.

(17)

\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))
is obviously a self-conjugate positive semi-definite matrix. So
\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))
meets the definition of
L
. Next we consider the relationship between
\alpha(L)
and
\alpha(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))
:

\displaystyle\alpha(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})|\varphi\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\sum_{i}\langle\varphi|(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})|\varphi\rangle.

Let
|\varphi^{*}_{i}\rangle=(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})|\varphi\rangle/\sqrt{p_{i}}
, where
p_{i}=tr(\langle\varphi|(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})|\varphi\rangle)
, and
\sum_{i}p_{i}=1
. We have

\displaystyle\alpha(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\sum_{i}p_{i}\langle\varphi^{*}_{i}|L|\varphi^{*}_{i}\rangle

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi^{*}_{i}|L|\varphi^{*}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}\max\limits_{|\varphi*_{i}\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi^{*}_{i}|L|\varphi^{*}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha(L).

We may clearly arrive at the conclusion that

\displaystyle C_{w}(\varepsilon(\rho))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(L)+tr(L\varepsilon(\rho))

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(L)+tr(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})\rho)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\alpha(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i}))+tr(\sum_{i}(E_{i}\dagger\otimes F_{i}\dagger)L(E_{i}\otimes F_{i})\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(L^{\prime})+tr(L^{\prime}\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho).

∎

Corollary 1
.

C_{w}(\rho)
is entangled monotone with respect to LOCC (E4).

Proof
.

LOCC belongs to separable operation, and we have proved
C_{w}(\rho)
is entangled monotone with respect to separable operation.
∎

Propositon 5
.

C_{w}(\rho)
is continuous for
\rho
, satisfying the property (E5).

Proof
.

Set
\rho^{\prime}=\rho+v_{n}
and
\rho^{\prime}=\rho-v_{n}
, we could have

\displaystyle|C_{w}(\rho)-C_{w}(\rho^{\prime})|\leq\max\limits_{L}|tr(Lv_{n})|,

(21)

where
v_{n}
is a Hermitian matrix. The spectral decompose of
v_{n}
is
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|
, and
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=0
. Thus

\displaystyle tr(Lv_{n})=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\langle\varphi_{i}|L|\varphi_{i}\rangle,

(22)

where
\langle\varphi_{i}|L|\varphi_{i}\rangle=\langle\varphi_{i}^{*}|\Lambda|\varphi_{i}^{*}\rangle\leq\lambda_{max}(L)
,
L=U^{\dagger}\Lambda U
,
U
is a unitary matrix,
\Lambda
is a real diagonal matrix with the diagonal elements being the eigenvalues of
L
,
|\varphi^{*}\rangle=U|\varphi\rangle
. According to the properties of
L
,
|tr(Lv_{n})|
satisfies

\displaystyle|tr(Lv_{n})|\leq a\sum_{i}|\lambda_{i}|=atr(\sqrt{v_{n}^{\dagger}v_{n}})=a||v_{n}||_{KF}.

(23)

When
\rho^{\prime}\rightarrow\rho
,
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
, i.e.
||v_{n}||_{KF}\rightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq|C_{w}(\rho)-C_{w}(\rho^{\prime})|\leq\max\limits_{L}|tr(Lv_{n})|\leq a||v_{n}||_{KF}\rightarrow 0,

(24)

which proves that
C_{w}(\rho)
is continuous.
∎

4
Lower Bound

4.1
Theoretical Derivation

We present a lower bound using the idea of finding a specific
L
to simplify the calculation. Without loss of generality, we discuss the situation of
L\in M_{1}
. Then we can set
L^{{}^{\prime}}=aL
to satisfy
L^{{}^{\prime}}\in M_{a}
. In the following, we discuss the lower bound in two cases:
\rho
is a pure state and
\rho
is a mixed state.

4.1.1
Pure State

We will divide the lower bound of
C_{w}(\rho)
into two steps: calculating
\alpha(L)
and
tr(L\rho)
. If we are capable to obtain the estimate of
\alpha(L)
in advance, the original problem will be simplified. As a matter of fact, J.Sperling and W.Vogel
[
9
]
have gotten the estimate of
\alpha(L)
. We also demonstrate this result by using Schmidt decomposition rather than the Lagrange multiplier.

Theorem 1
.

When
rank(L)=1
, we could regard
L
as a pure state and we have

\displaystyle\alpha(L)=\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|L|\varphi\rangle=\mu_{1},

(25)

where
\sqrt{\mu_{1}}
is the maximum Schmidt coefficient of
L
.

Proof
.

For
\forall|\varphi_{pure}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(n)}_{B}(m\leq n)
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi_{pure}\rangle=\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}}|i_{A}i_{B}\rangle,

(26)

where
\sqrt{\mu_{i}}
(
i=1,...m
) are the Schmidt coefficients, and
\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq...\geq\mu_{m}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=1
.
|i_{A}\rangle
and
|i_{B}\rangle
are the orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{H}^{(m)}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(n)}_{B}
respectively. Then
L=|\varphi_{pure}\rangle\langle\varphi_{pure}|
could be written as
\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}|i_{A}i_{B}\rangle\langle j_{A}j_{B}|
.

Similarly, we could write separable pure state
|\varphi_{sep}\rangle
in form of
\sum_{i,j}a_{i}b_{j}|i_{A}j_{B}\rangle
. By using these representations, we could conclude that

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{sep}|L|\varphi_{sep}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{A}|\otimes\langle\varphi_{B}|L|\varphi_{A}\rangle\otimes|\varphi_{B}\rangle

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}a_{i}b_{i}a_{j}^{*}b_{j}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}}|a_{i}||b_{i}|)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}\sqrt[4]{\mu_{i}}|a_{i}|\sqrt[4]{\mu_{i}}|b_{i}|)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}}|a_{i}|^{2})(\sum_{j}\sqrt{\mu_{j}}|b_{j}|^{2}).

The first inequality holds by applying the absolute value inequality and the second one holds due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Note that
\sum_{i}|a_{i}|^{2}=1
and
\sum_{j}|b_{j}|^{2}=1
. We can obtain
\langle\varphi_{sep}|L|\varphi_{sep}\rangle\leq\mu_{1}
and the equal sign works if and only if it meets the condition that
|a_{1}|=1
,
|b_{1}|=1
,
a_{i}=0
,
b_{i}=0

(i>1)
. Ultimately, we have
\alpha(L)=\mu_{1}
.
∎

After getting the estimate of
\alpha(L)
, we may substitute it into
C_{w}(\rho)
. Thus the original problem will be transformed into estimating of
tr(L\rho)
. In the pure states, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\rho
be a pure quantum state acting on the space of
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(d_{1})}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(d_{2})}
. We have

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq\frac{\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{KF}-1}{d},

(28)

where
d=min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
.

Proof
.

It is easy to deduce that
\rho=\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}|i_{A}i_{B}\rangle\langle j_{A}j_{B}|
. Then we take a special
L^{\prime}
, which has the standard Schmidt form
L^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j}\sqrt{m_{i}m_{j}}|i_{A}i_{B}\rangle\langle j_{A}j_{B}|
, where
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq...\geq m_{d}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}m_{i}=1
. We can notice that
L
is a pure state. Then

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq tr(L^{\prime}\rho)-\alpha(L^{\prime})=(\sum_{i}\sqrt{m_{i}\mu_{i}})^{2}-m_{1}.

(29)

By designating
m_{i}=\frac{1}{d}
( it meets the above restraint conditions obviously), we can obtain

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq\frac{(\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}})^{2}-1}{d}=\frac{\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{KF}-1}{d}.

(30)

From the Ref.
[
10
]
, we know that
(\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}})^{2}
is equal to
\|R(\rho)\|_{KF}
and
\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{KF}
for a pure state
\rho
, where
R(\rho)
is the realigned matrix of
\rho
. Here we write
(\sum_{i}\sqrt{\mu_{i}})^{2}
as
\|\rho^{T_{A}}\|_{KF}
. Hence, the inequality in the previous equation holds.
∎

In this way and by means of Schmidt decomposition we succeeded in obtaining the lower bound of
C_{w}(\rho)
for pure states. For mixed states, we still attain the expression of
\alpha(L)
firstly.

4.1.2
Mixed State

To solve the problem for the mixed state, we introduce a new form of density operator. An arbitrary state
\rho
acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{(d_{1})}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}^{(d_{2})}
could be Bloch represented as follows

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum_{i}{s_{i}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes I}+\sum_{j}{I\otimes t_{j}\lambda_{j}^{B}+\sum_{i,j}{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes\lambda_{j}^{B}}}),

(31)

where
I
stands for identity operator, and
\{\lambda_{i}^{A}\}_{i=1}^{d_{1}^{2}-1}
,
\{\lambda_{i}^{B}\}_{i=1}^{d_{2}^{2}-1}
are generalized Gell-Mann matrices, which satisfy
tr(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})=2\delta_{ij}
,
\lambda_{i}^{\dagger}=\lambda_{i}
. It’s clear that
s_{i}
,
t_{i}
and
r_{ij}
are all real. Hence, the value of
\alpha(L)
could be estimated again when
L
is a mixed state.

Theorem 3
.

Let
s_{i}=0
,
t_{i}=0
,
L
is written as Bloch representation as follows

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum_{i,j}{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes\lambda_{j}^{B}}).

(32)

Then the estimate of
\alpha(L)
is

\displaystyle\alpha(L)=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}\|R_{L}\|_{2},

(33)

where
R_{L}=(r_{ij})
.

Proof
.

For Hermitian matrix
L
,
L
can be written as

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum_{i}{s_{i}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes I}+\sum_{j}{I\otimes t_{j}\lambda_{j}^{B}+\sum_{i.j}{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes\lambda_{j}^{B}}}).

(34)

For separable state
\rho
, it can be written as same as
L
:

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum_{i}{s_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes I}+\sum_{j}{I\otimes t_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}\lambda_{j}^{B}+\sum_{i,j}{r_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes\lambda_{j}^{B}}}).

(35)

Assume
S
,
S_{1}
,
T
,
T_{1}
are vectors constituted by
s_{i}
,
s_{i}^{\prime}
,
t_{j}
,
t_{j}^{\prime}
respectively,
R_{L}
and
R_{1}
are the correlation matrices of
L
and
\rho
. We could prove
R_{1}=S_{1}T_{1}^{T}
as
\rho
is a separable state, then

\displaystyle\alpha(L)=\frac{1}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}(d_{1}d_{2}+\max\limits_{\|S_{1}\|_{2}=1,\|T_{1}\|_{2}=1}(2d_{2}S_{1}^{T}S+2d_{1}T_{1}^{T}T+4S_{1}^{T}R_{L}T_{1})).

(36)

Let
L=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}^{A}\otimes\lambda_{j}^{B}})
. We may simplify the above formula as

\displaystyle\alpha(L)=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\max\limits_{\|S_{1}\|_{2}=1,\|T_{1}\|_{2}=1}\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}S_{1}^{T}R_{L}T_{1}.

(37)

Assume that the singular value decomposition(SVD) form of
R_{L}
is
U\Lambda V^{\dagger}
,
\Lambda=diag(\sigma_{i})(\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq...)
. Set
S_{1}^{{}^{\prime}T}=S_{1}^{T}U
,
T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=V^{\dagger}T_{1}
,
S_{1}^{T}R_{L}T_{1}
can be written as
S_{1}^{{}^{\prime}T}\Lambda T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}
. We have the following inequality

\displaystyle S_{1}^{{}^{\prime}T}\Lambda T_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum s_{i}t_{j}\sigma_{i}\leq\sqrt{(\sum|s_{i}|^{2}\sigma_{i})(\sum|t_{i}|^{2}\sigma_{i})}\leq\sigma_{1}.

(38)

Obviously, the upper bound in Eq(
38
) is saturated. From Eq(
37
) and Eq(
38
), we could conclude that

\displaystyle\alpha(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}\sigma_{max}(R_{L})

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}\|R_{L}\|_{2}.

(40)

∎

After getting the estimate of
\alpha(L)
when

Theorem 4
.

Considering the special
L
which can be written as the following Bloch representation

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}(I\otimes I+\sum{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j}}).

(41)

Assume
R_{L}=(r_{ij})
, when
R_{\rho}\neq 0

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq\frac{2(\|R_{\rho}\|_{KF}-1)}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}\sqrt{rank(R_{\rho})}}.

(42)

When
R_{\rho}=0
, we have
C_{w}(\rho)\geq 0
.

Proof
.

From Theorem 3, the above
L
could derive
\alpha(L)=\frac{1}{d_{1}d_{2}}+\max\limits_{\|S_{1}\|_{2}=1,\|T_{1}\|_{2}=1}\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}S_{1}^{T}R_{L}T_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq tr(L\rho)-\alpha(L)=\frac{4}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}}(tr(R_{L}^{T}R_{\rho})-\|R_{L}\|_{2}).

(43)

Let
R_{\rho}=U\Sigma V^{T}
be the singular value decomposition of
R_{\rho}

and

\sigma_{i}
is the element of
\Sigma
in corresponding position. Assume

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq\frac{4(\|R_{\rho}\|_{KF}-1)}{d_{1}^{2}d_{2}^{2}c}.

(44)

Next we will prove that when

,
L\geq 0
. When

, and

\displaystyle\|\sum{r_{ij}\lambda_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j}}\|_{HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{tr(\sum{r_{ij}r_{sk}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{s}\otimes\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}})}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{4\sum{r_{ij}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\|R_{L}\|_{HS}

and

\displaystyle\|R_{L}\|_{HS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{tr(\frac{1}{c^{2}}V\Sigma^{\prime T}U^{T}U\Sigma^{\prime}V)}

Theorem 5
.

For
\rho\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}

C_{w}(\rho)\geq\frac{1}{4}\max\{0,m_{1}+m_{2}-sgn(|R_{\rho}|)m_{3}-1,\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}+sgn(|R_{\rho}|)m_{3}-1}{3}\},

is the correlation matrix of

, and

(

Proof
.

From Theorem 3, when
L^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4}(I\otimes I+\sum{r_{ij}^{\prime}\lambda_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j}})

\alpha(L^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}(1+\|R_{L^{\prime}}\|_{2})
,
R_{L^{\prime}}=(r_{ij}^{\prime})
. For
\rho\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{2}
,
\rho=\frac{1}{4}(I\otimes I+\vec{s}\cdot\vec{\lambda}\otimes I+I\otimes\vec{t}\cdot\vec{\lambda}+\sum r_{ij}\lambda_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j})
and
R_{\rho}=(r_{ij})
, we can select an appropriate local unitary transformation
U_{1}\otimes U_{2}
on
\rho
to diagonalize
R_{\rho}
, then the transformed
R_{\rho}
is
\Sigma_{\rho}=O_{1}^{T}R_{\rho}O_{2}=diag(m_{1},m_{2},sgn(|R_{\rho}|)m_{3})
(
m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq m_{3}\geq 0
), where
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
are real orthogonal matrices satisfying
|O_{1}|=|O_{2}|=1
. We can construct
L^{\prime}=\frac{1}{4}(I\otimes I+\sum{r_{ij}}^{\prime}\lambda_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j})
, where
{R}^{\prime}=({r_{ij}}^{\prime})=O_{1}^{T}\Sigma_{L^{\prime}}O_{2}
,
\Sigma_{L^{\prime}}=diag(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, then

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L^{\prime}\geq 0}\{tr(L^{\prime}\rho)-\alpha(L^{\prime})\}

(49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L^{\prime}\geq 0}\{\frac{1}{4}(tr(R_{L}^{T}R_{\rho})-\max\limits_{i}|r_{i}|)\}

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{L^{\prime}\geq 0}\{\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{m}-\max\limits_{i}|r_{i}|)\},

(51)

where
\textbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
and
\textbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},sgn(|R_{\rho}|)m_{3})
. From Ref.
[
11
]
, we know that the above matrix

is positive semi-definite if and only if
r
belongs to the tetrahedron
\Gamma
with vertices
\textbf{t}_{1}=(-1,-1,-1)
,
\textbf{t}_{2}=(1,1,-1)
,
\textbf{t}_{3}=(1,-1,1)
,
\textbf{t}_{4}=(-1,1,1)
. Since tetrahedron
\Gamma
is symmetric about three axes and three coordinate planes,
r
still belongs to tetrahedron
\Gamma
when we change the order of elements or the sign of the two elements.

When
r
satisfies
\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{m}-\max\limits_{i}|r_{i}|)=\max\limits_{L^{\prime}\geq 0}\{\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{m}-\max\limits_{i}|r_{i}|)\}
, we have
r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq|r_{3}|\geq 0
. Otherwise, For
\forall\textbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in\Gamma
, we can change

\textbf{r}^{\prime}=(r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{2},r^{\prime}_{3})\in\Gamma(r^{\prime}_{1}\geq r^{\prime}_{2}\geq|r^{\prime}_{3}|)
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}^{\prime}\cdot\textbf{m}-\max\limits_{i}|r_{i}|)-\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{m}-\max\limits_{i}|r^{\prime}_{i}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}^{\prime}-\textbf{r})\cdot\textbf{m}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}[c_{1}(m_{1}-m_{2})+c_{2}(m_{2}-m_{3})+c_{3}m_{3}]

(53)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

(54)

We can simplify the problem to

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)\geq\max\limits_{\textbf{r}\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{4}(\textbf{r}\cdot\textbf{b}),

(55)

where

5
Multipartite Entanglement

So far we focused our attention on Hilbert spaces of two finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Now we consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{n}
. The generalization of the definition of
C_{w}(\rho)
is

\displaystyle C_{w}(\rho)=\max\limits_{L}\{-tr(W_{E}\rho)\},

and it satisfies:

\displaystyle L\in M_{a},

\displaystyle W_{E}=\alpha I-L,

\displaystyle\alpha=\max\limits_{|\varphi\rangle\in sep}\langle\varphi|L|\varphi\rangle.

It is obvious that there must be a Hermitian matrix
L
which satisfies
tr(W_{E}\rho)<0
,
tr(W_{E}\sigma)\geq 0
, where
\rho
is an entangled state,
\sigma
is a separable state.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we define an entanglement measure based on entanglement witness and extend its definition to the multipartite entanglement of quantum states in arbitrary dimensional systems. This measure satisfies some necessary properties of an entanglement measure and we estimate its lower bound on bipartite system. The rationality of the entanglement measure is verified by the estimation of its lower bound by discussing pure and mixed states separately and the corresponding numerical simulation.

A duality result about special functions for Drinfeld modules of arbitrary rank
Giacomo Hermes Ferraro

Abstract
In the setting of a Drinfeld module ϕ \phi over a curve X / 𝔽 q X/\mathbb{F}_{q} , we use a functorial point of view to define Anderson eigenvectors , a generalization of the so called "special functions" introduced by Anglès, Ngo Dac and Tavares Ribeiro, and prove the existence of a universal object ω ϕ \omega_{\phi} . We adopt an analogous approach with the adjoint Drinfeld module ϕ ∗ \phi^{*} to define dual Anderson eigenvectors . The universal object of this functor, denoted by ζ ϕ \zeta_{\phi} , is a generalization of Pellarin zeta functions, can be expressed as an Eisenstein-like series over the period lattice, and its coordinates are analytic functions from X ⁡ ( ℂ ∞ ) ∖ { ∞ } X({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})\setminus\{\infty\} to ℂ ∞ {\mathbb{C}_{\infty}} . For all integers i i we define dot products ζ ϕ ⋅ ω ϕ ( i ) \zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i)} as certain meromorphic differential forms over X ℂ ∞ ∖ { ∞ } X_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\{\infty\} , and prove they are actually rational. This amounts to a generalization of Pellarin’s identity for the Carlitz module, and is linked to the pairing of the A A - motive and the dual A A - motive defined by Hartl and Juschka. Finally, we develop an algorithm to compute the forms ζ ϕ ⋅ ω ϕ ( i ) \zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i)} when X = ℙ 1 X=\mathbb{P}^{1} , and prove a conjecture of Gazda and Maurischat about the invertibility of special functions for Drinfeld modules of rank 1 1 .

Introduction

Drinfeld modules are meant to provide an analogue of complex elliptic curves—interpreted as quotients of the complex plane by a lattice—in the context of function fields over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Instead of
\mathbb{Z}
, we work with the ring
A
of functions over a projective, geometrically irreducible, smooth curve
{X}/{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
outside a closed point
\infty
. The role of the real numbers is filled by the
\infty
-
adic completion
{K_{\infty}}
of the function field of
X
, while the field of complex numbers is substituted by
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, defined as the
\infty
-
adic completion of an algebraic closure of
{K_{\infty}}
.

Inside
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, we can consider a discrete projective finitely generated
A
-
module
\Lambda
, the
period lattice
, which contrary to the theory of elliptic curves can have arbitrary rank
r
, and is never cocompact. A Drinfeld module
\phi
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear and polynomial action of
A
on
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, and Drinfeld modules are in bijection with period lattices in the following way: for each Drinfeld module
\phi
there is a unique period lattice
\Lambda_{\phi}
such that
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, endowed with the
A
-
module structure induced by
\phi
, is isomorphic to
{{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}/{\Lambda_{\phi}}
with the
A
-
module structure induced by the inclusion
A\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

Entire functions.

The Tate algebra
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
is defined as the completion of
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes A
with respect to the sup norm induced by
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
(when not specified, tensor products are assumed to be over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
); given an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
basis
\{a_{i}\}_{i}
of
A
, all elements of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
can be uniquely expressed as
\sum_{i}c_{i}\otimes a_{i}
with
\lim_{i}c_{i}=0
.

The Tate algebra
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
can be thought of as the set of analytic functions from the "unit disc"
D\subseteq X({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, where

D\coloneqq\left\{P\in X({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})\setminus\{\infty\}\text{ such that }\|a(P)\|\leq 1\text{ for all }a\in A\right\}.

In the article
Chung et al. 2023
, Chung, Ngo Dac, and Pellarin proved that, if
\infty\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the
Pellarin zeta function

\zeta_{A}\coloneqq-\sum_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}}a^{-1}\otimes a\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA

is an analytic function from
X({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})\setminus\{\infty\}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.
We generalize this result as follows to a wider class of Eisenstein-like series.

Proposition 1

(Prop.
6.8
)
.

Let
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
be an arbitrary period lattice, and define

\zeta_{\Lambda}\coloneqq-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-1}\otimes\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5mu\Lambda.

For any
A
-
linear map
f:\Lambda\to A
, the element
(1\otimes f)\zeta_{\Lambda}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
is an analytic function from
X({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})\setminus\{\infty\}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

The most interesting aspect of this proposition is that it is proven in a completely different way from
Chung et al. 2023
: it is a simple consequence of one of the main theorems of this paper, namely that
\zeta_{\Lambda}
is an "eigenvector" for the adjoint Drinfeld module associated to
\Lambda
(see Theorem
6
). This property of
\zeta_{\Lambda}
is the motivating result of this paper, and is meant to mirror the property of Anderson–Thakur special functions.

Special functions.

The simplest example of a Drinfeld module is the
Carlitz module

C
: we assume
{X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}}
, so that
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
for some rational function
\theta
, and set
\phi_{\theta}\coloneqq\theta+\tau
, where
\tau:{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
denotes the Frobenius endomorphism sending
c
to
c^{q}
. In this case, the period lattice is
\tilde{\pi}A
for some
\tilde{\pi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}
; as we said before, we can identify the Tate algebra
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
with the set of analytic functions from the unit disc of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, i.e. with the set of formal series
\sum_{i}s_{i}t^{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![t]\!]
such that
\lim_{i}s_{i}=0

Anderson and Thakur introduced in
Anderson and Thakur 1990
the function
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
as the unique element such that, if we write
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{i}t^{i}
, we have
c_{0}=1
and

\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{\theta}(c_{i})t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{i}t^{i+1}.

This series has various uses: for example, as shown in
Anglès and Pellarin 2014
by Anglès and Pellarin,
\omega
is connected to the explicit class field theory of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
, and its
{\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5mu}
-
rational values interpolate Gauss-Thakur sums.

The module of "special functions" (as defined in
Anglès et al. 2017
by Anglès, Ngo Dac, and Tavares Ribeiro) generalizes the Anderson–Thakur function to any Drinfeld module
\phi
as follows:

\Sf_{\phi}(A)\coloneqq\{\omega\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA\mid(\phi_{a}\otimes 1)(\omega)=(1\otimes a)\omega\text{ for all }a\in A\},

where
\phi_{a}\otimes 1
sends an infinite series
\sum_{i}c_{i}\otimes a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muA
to
\sum_{i}\phi_{a}(c_{i})\otimes a_{i}
.

In a recent article, Gazda and Maurischat showed (in the generality of an arbitrary anderson module
\underline{E}=(E,\phi)
) that the module of special functions is isomorphic to
\Hom_{A}(\Omega,\Lambda_{\phi})
, where
\Omega
is the module of Kähler differentials of

(
(
Gazda and Maurischat 2021
, Thm. 3.11)
).

In this paper we formulate a generalization of special functions which allows us to recover this result using the language of functors. We give the following Definition and Theorem, in the generality of Anderson modules (see Section
3

2.1
Basic statements about Pontryagin duality

In this paper, compact and locally compact spaces are always assumed to be Hausdorff.

Definition 2.1

(Pontryagin duality)
.

Call
\mathbb{S}^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\times}
the complex unit circle. For any commutative ring with unity
B

is a contravariant functor from the category of topological
B
-

\hat{M}\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M,\mathbb{S}^{1})

-

The following well known result, which we do not prove, justifies the terminology "duality".

Proposition 2.2
.

For any ring

B
-

sending

(f\mapsto f(m))
. The map

is a continuous

-

\hat{M}
is locally compact, and

is an isomorphism. Moreover, if

is discrete (resp. compact).

Let now

Remark 2.5

The topological tensor product can be endowed with the compact open topology, but we will only need to use the definition of the underlying set.

∎

We introduce some other useful terminology.

Remark 2.7

For any set

Definition 2.8

If

Remark 2.9

In the previous definition, a generic element

Remark 2.11

For any discrete projective

Definition 2.12

The open ball

Proposition 2.14
.

Let

be a prodiscrete
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space and

be a discrete
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space. There is a natural
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear bijection
\Phi:C\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muM\to C\hat{\otimes}M
.

If we fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
basis
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
of
M
with corresponding dual basis
(m_{i}^{*})_{i\in I}
of
\hat{M}
, for any function
f\in C\hat{\otimes}M=\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\hat{M},C)
we have

\Phi^{-1}(f)=\sum_{i\in I}f(m_{i}^{*})\otimes m_{i}.

Moreover, if
C
and
M
are
A
-
modules,
\Phi
is
A\otimes A
-
linear.

Proof.

Fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
basis
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
of
M
and let
\mathcal{U}
be an associated filter of
C
. Any
x\in C\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muM
can be expressed in a unique way as
\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}\otimes m_{i}
, where
x_{i}\in C
for all
i\in I
, and for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
the set

I_{U}\coloneqq\{i\in I\mid x_{i}\not\in U\}

is finite. We define
\Phi(x):\hat{M}\to C
as follows:

\forall f\in\hat{M},\;\Phi(x)(f)\coloneqq\lim_{J\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{fin}}(I)}\sum_{i\in J}f(m_{i})x_{i}.

Since
C
is complete with respect to the neighborhood filter
\mathcal{U}
, and for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
the set

\{i\in I\mid f(m_{i})x_{i}\not\in U\}\subseteq I_{U}

is finite, the map
\Phi(x)
is well defined.
For all
U\in\mathcal{U}
, the set

\{f\in\hat{M}\mid f(m_{i})=0\text{ for all }i\in I_{U}\}

is a neighborhood of
0
in
\hat{M}
, and is contained in
\Phi(x)^{-1}(U)
, hence
\Phi(x)
is continuous. Since
\Phi(x)
is also obviously
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear,
\Phi(x)\in C\hat{\otimes}M
for all
x\in C\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muM
.

The map
\Phi
is manifestly
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear, and, if

and

are
A
-
modules,
A\otimes A
-
linear, so we just need to prove bijectivity.
On one hand, if
\Phi(x)\equiv 0
, we have

0=\Phi(x)(m_{i}^{*})=x_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I,

hence

, showing injectivity. On the other hand, if

is a continuous function, for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
the set

\{i\in I\mid g(m_{i}^{*})\not\in U\}

is finite because
\hat{M}
is compact, hence

is an element of
C\mskip 1.5mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{\otimes}\mskip-1.5mu}\mskip 1.5muM
; since

\Phi(y)(m_{i}^{*})=g(m_{i}^{*})\text{ for all }i\in I,

we have
\Phi(y)=g
.
∎

3
Universal Anderson eigenvector

In this section, we will define the functor of
Anderson eigenvectors
relative to an Anderson module
(E,\phi)
, which generalizes the concept of special functions and Gauss-Thakur sums (see Definition
3.6
), and prove that under some conditions it is representable (see Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.11
). As a corollary, we get a variant of the result
(
Gazda and Maurischat 2021
, Thm. 3.11)
, in which Gazda and Maurischat described the module of special functions for any Anderson module
(E,\phi)
.

3.1
Anderson modules

Definition 3.1
.

Given an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
algebra
R
, an
R
-
module scheme over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}

G
is a group scheme over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
endowed with a compatible action of
R
, i.e. a ring homomorphism
R\to\End(G)
.

If
G
is a group scheme over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, we denote by
\Lie(G)
its tangent space at the identity, which has a natural structure of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
vector space (it also has a Lie algebra structure, but it is trivial in all group schemes of interest to us). This association can be extended to a functor from the category of group schemes over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
to that of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
vector spaces, and given
f:G\to G^{\prime}
a morphism in the first category, we denote the induced morphism
\Lie(G)\to\Lie(G^{\prime})
as
\Lie(f)
.

Let’s define Anderson
A
-
modules (see
(
Hartl and Juschka 2020
, Def. 2.5.2)
).

Definition 3.2
.

An
Anderson
A
-
module

\underline{E}=(E,\phi)
over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
of dimension
d
consists of an
A
-
module scheme
E
over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
with the following properties:

•

as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
module scheme over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
,
E
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}^{d}
;

•

the action
\phi
of
A
on
E
is such that
\Lie\phi_{a}-a:\Lie(E)\to\Lie(E)
is nilpotent for all
a\in A
.

Fix an Anderson
A
-
module
(E,\phi)
.
There is a unique
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear function

{\exp_{\phi}:\Lie(E)\to E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})},

called
exponential
of
\phi
, such that

\exp_{\phi}\circ\Lie\phi_{a}=\phi_{a}\circ\exp_{\phi}\text{ for all }a\in A

(see
(
Goss 1998
, Thm. 5.9.6)
); its kernel

\Lambda_{\phi}\coloneqq\ker(\exp_{\phi})\subseteq\Lie(E)

is an
A
-
module of finite rank with respect to the
A
-
module structure induced by
\Lie\phi
on
\Lie(E)
(see
(
Goss 1998
, Lemma 5.9.12)
).

Moreover, if we fix an isomorphism
E\cong\mathbb{G}_{a,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}^{d}
, the exponential function can be identified with a series in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d\times d}[\![\tau]\!]
—where
\tau
is the Frobenius endomorphism—whose leading term is the identity matrix.

Remark 3.3
.

Since
E
and
\mathbb{G}_{a,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}^{d}
are isomorphic group schemes over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, we can identify the set
E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})
with
\mathbb{G}_{a,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}^{d}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d}
up to an automorphism of
\mathbb{G}_{a,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}^{d}
as a group scheme over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
. Since any such automorphism is continuous as a map from
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d}
to itself,
E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})
has a natural topology, and is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d}
.

Since
\Lie(E)
is a
d
-
dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, it also has a natural topology; moreover, by the inverse function theorem applied to
\exp_{\phi}
, we get that
\Lambda_{\phi}\subseteq\Lie(E)
is a discrete subset. In light of this remark, and since for all
a\in A

{\exp_{\phi}\circ\Lie\phi_{a}=\phi_{a}\circ\exp_{\phi}}
,
\exp_{\phi}
is a morphism of topological
A
-
modules.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\underline{E}=(E,\phi)
be an Anderson
A
-
module. The discrete
A
-
module
\Lambda_{\phi}\subseteq\Lie(E)
is called the
period lattice
of
\phi
. If
\exp_{\phi}
is surjective,
\underline{E}
is said to be
uniformizable
; in this case, its
rank
is defined as the rank of
\Lambda_{\phi}
as an
A
-
module.

The following is a well-known lemma, so we just give an outline of the proof.

Lemma 3.5
.

The
A
-
module structure of
\Lie(E)
induced by
\phi
extends uniquely to a structure of topological vector space over
{K_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Since the endomorphisms
(\Lie\phi_{a})_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}}
commute and are invertible, the ring homomorphism
\Psi:A\to\End_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\Lie(E))
sending
a
to
\Lie\phi_{a}
can be extended uniquely to
K
, and we can fix a basis
\Lie(E)\cong\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d}
in which, for all
c\in K
,
\Psi_{c}
is a triangular matrix with
N_{c}\coloneqq c^{-1}\Psi_{c}-\mathrm{Id}_{d}
nilpotent—precisely,
N_{c}^{d}=0
. We can endow
\End_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{d})
with a matrix norm
|\cdot|
sending a matrix to the maximum of the norms of its coefficients; since the norm on
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
is non-archimedean,
|\cdot|
is submultiplicative. To extend continuously
\Psi
to
{K_{\infty}}
, it suffices to prove that the set
\{|c^{-1}\Psi_{c}|\}_{c\in K\setminus\{0\}}
is bounded, so that
|\Psi_{c}|
tends to
0
as
\|c\|
tends to
0
.

Since
A
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
algebra, we can pick a finite set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
such that the finite products of the
a_{i}
’s generate
A
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space.

Call
M\coloneqq\max\{1,|N_{a_{1}}|,\dots,|N_{a_{n}}|\}
. For
i=1,\dots,n
, for all
m\geq 0
, since
N_{a_{i}}^{d}=0
:

a_{i}^{-m}\Psi_{a_{i}^{m}}=(a_{i}^{-1}\Psi_{a_{i}})^{m}\in\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\{\mathrm{Id}_{d},N_{a_{i}},\dots,N_{a_{i}}^{d-1}\}\right),

hence
|\Psi_{a_{i}^{m}}|<\|a_{i}\|^{m}M^{d}
. Fix any
b\in A
: since it is a linear combination of products of powers of the
a_{i}
’s, and since the norm
|\cdot|
is non-archimedean, we have
|b^{-1}\Psi_{b}|<M^{nd}
, hence

|N_{b}|\leq\max(\{1,|b^{-1}\Psi_{b}|\})<M^{nd}.

For all
c\in K^{\times}
, if we write
c=ab^{-1}
with
a,b\in A\setminus\{0\}
, we have:

|c^{-1}\Psi_{c}|=|a^{-1}\Psi_{a}(b^{-1}\Psi_{b})^{-1}|=\left|(\mathrm{Id}_{d}+N_{a})\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(-N_{b})^{i}\right)\right|<M^{nd^{2}},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Since

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume that

is uniformizable. The functor
\Sf_{\phi}
is naturally isomorphic to
\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi}^{*}\otimes_{A}\Omega,\_)
; moreover, the universal object in
E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})\hat{\otimes}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)
corresponds to the map

\widehat{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}\cong{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}/{\Lambda_{\phi}}\to E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})

\exp_{\phi}(c)
.

Proof.

Since
E
is uniformizable, the exponential map induces and isomorphism of topological

-

{\Lie(E)}/{\Lambda_{\phi}}
and

, which sends
{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}/\Lambda_{\phi}
to
\exp_{\phi}({K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi})
. By Lemma

,
\exp_{\phi}({K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi})
is the maximal compact
A

E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})

For any discrete

is compact, hence for any function in

\Sf_{\phi}(M)=\Hom_{A}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\hat{M},E({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})),

\displaystyle\Sf_{\phi}(M)

In this section, we focus on a Drinfeld module

5.1
Poonen duality

Let
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
denote the
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
-
bimodule of unbounded formal power series in
\tau

For any formal series

s^{*}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\tau^{-i}s_{i}=\sum_{i}s_{i}^{q^{-i}}\tau^{-i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!].

Remark 5.1
.

Despite the notation for the space
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
, its structure of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
-
bimodule cannot be extended to a ring structure.

Remark 5.2
.

It’s easy to show that, since
\exp_{\phi}
has an infinite radius of convergence, the adjoint exponential
\exp_{\phi}^{*}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau^{-1}]\!]
also converges everywhere on
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

We follow a construction due to Poonen, who proved a duality result of central importance to this section (
(
Poonen 1996
, Thm. 10)
).

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
\beta\in\ker(\exp_{\phi}^{*})\setminus\{0\}
, there is a unique element
g_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!]
such that
(1-\tau)g_{\beta}=\beta\exp_{\phi}
. Moreover,
g_{\beta}
has infinite radius of convergence.

Proof.

Let’s set
h:=\sum_{i\geq 0}\tau^{i}
; since
h(1-\tau)=1
, the defining property of
g_{\beta}
is equivalent to the identity
g_{\beta}=h\beta\exp_{\phi}
: this implies both existence and uniqueness. If we call
e_{i}
the

-
th coefficient of
\exp_{\phi}
and
c_{i}
the
i
-
th coefficient of
g_{\beta}
, from the identity

we get the following:

c_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\beta^{q^{i}}e_{k-i}^{q^{i}}\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Longrightarrow\lim_{k}c_{k}^{\frac{1}{q^{k}}}=\lim_{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}e_{i}^{q^{-i}}\beta^{q^{-i}}=\exp_{\phi}^{*}(\beta)=0,

hence the radius of convergence of

is infinite.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Since
\ker((1-\tau)g_{\beta})=\ker(\beta\exp_{\phi})=\Lambda_{\phi}
,

has image in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

By convention, we set

.

(
(

5.2
Lattices

Throughout this subsection,
C
will be a complete normed
{K_{\infty}}
-
vector space (with non-archimedean norm).

Definition 5.6
.

An infinite
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear subspace
V\subseteq C
is a

if for any positive real number

there are finitely many elements of
V
of norm at most
r
.

An

of
V
is a sequence
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
with the following property: for all
m\geq 1
,
v_{m}
is an element of
V\setminus\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{i}\}_{i<m})
of least norm.

We call the sequence of real numbers
(\|v_{i}\|)_{i\geq 1}
a

of

.

The next two results aim to justify the nomenclature "ordered basis" and to prove that the norm sequence does not depend on the choice of the ordered basis.

Remark 5.7
.

If
V\subseteq C
is a lattice, every subset
S\subseteq V
has an element of least norm. In particular, we can construct an ordered basis of
V
by recursion.

Lemma 5.8
.

If
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is an ordered basis of a lattice
V\subseteq C
, it is a basis of
V
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space.

Proof.

For all
m\geq 1

v_{m}\not\in\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{i}\}_{i<m}),

hence the
v_{i}
’s are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linearly independent. Since for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
there is a finite number of elements of
V
with norm at most
r
, the norm sequence
(\|v_{i}\|)_{i\geq 1}
tends to infinity; in particular, for all
v\in V
there is an integer
m
such that
\|v_{m}\|>\|v\|
, so

v\in\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{i}\}_{i<m})

by construction of
v_{m}
.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

If
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is an ordered basis of a lattice
V\subseteq C
, and
(v^{\prime}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of elements in
V
that are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linearly independent and with weakly increasing norm, then

\|v^{\prime}_{i}\|\geq\|v_{i}\|\text{ for all }i\geq 1.

In particular, the norm sequence of
V
does not depend on the chosen ordered basis of
V
.

Proof.

By contradiction, assume
\|v^{\prime}_{m}\|<\|v_{m}\|
for some
m
. Then for all
i\leq m
we have

\|v^{\prime}_{i}\|\leq\|v^{\prime}_{m}\|<\|v_{m}\|,

so
v^{\prime}_{i}\in\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{j}\}_{j<m})
by construction of
v_{m}
; since
\{v^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i\leq m}
is a set of
m

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linearly independent vectors and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{j}\}_{j<m})=m-1
, we have reached a contradiction. If we take
(v^{\prime}_{i})_{i}
to be another ordered basis, by this reasoning we get both

\|v^{\prime}_{m}\|\geq\|v_{m}\|\text{ and }\|v_{m}\|\geq\|v^{\prime}_{m}\|,

hence the norm sequence is independent from the choice of the ordered basis.
∎

Finally, we show that the norm sequence is reasonably well behaved with regard to subspaces.

Lemma 5.10

Let
W\subseteq V\subseteq C
be lattices. The norm sequence
(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of

is a subsequence of the norm sequence
(r_{i})_{i\geq 1}

V
.

Moreover, if
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}{V}/{W}=n<\infty

we have

.

Proof.

Let
(w_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be an ordered basis of
W
. Let’s construct an ordered basis
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
V
recursively in the following way. For all
k\geq 1
let

be the least integer such that

and let

be an element of least norm. If
\|v_{k}^{\prime}\|<\|w_{f(k)}\|
, we set
v_{k}\coloneqq v_{k}^{\prime}
, otherwise we set
v_{k}\coloneqq w_{f(k)}
.

By construction
(v_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is an ordered basis of
V
, so we only need to show that for all
j\geq 1
there is some
k\geq 1
such that
v_{k}=w_{j}
. By contradiction, let
j
be the first integer such that this does not happen, and let
k
be the greatest integer such that

w_{j}\not\in\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{i}\}_{i<k}),

which exists because
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a basis of

. This means that
w_{j}=\alpha v_{k}+v
for some

and some constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, and since

, by our algorithm we must have

; as a consequence

which is a contradiction because, since
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is an ordered basis,
\|v_{k}\|\geq\|v_{i}\|
for all

, hence
\|v_{k}\|\geq\|v\|
.

If

, since the basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
of

extends the basis
\{w_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
of

, there are exactly

elements of the former which are not contained in the latter. Since, taking the order into account,
(w_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a subsequence of
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, for

, hence

.
∎

Recall that

is the degree of
\infty\in X
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
V\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
be a lattice which is also a projective
A
-
submodule of finite rank
r
, and let
(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be its norm sequence. Then:

•

for all
i\gg 0
,

s_{i+er}=q^{e}\cdot s_{i};

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, for all
i\gg 0
,

\frac{s_{i+k}}{s_{i}}\leq q^{e\left\lceil\frac{k}{er}\right\rceil};

•

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, for infinitely many

,

\frac{s_{i+k}}{s_{i}}\leq q^{\frac{k}{r}}.

Proof.

We can choose
a,b\in A\setminus\{0\}
such that
\deg(b)=\deg(a)+e
. Fix an ordered basis
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
V
: obviously,
(av_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and
(bv_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are ordered bases respectively of
aV
and
bV
. Since
V
has rank
r
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}{V}/{aV}=r\deg(a)\text{ and }\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}{V}/{bV}=r\deg(b),

therefore by Lemma
5.10
we have

\|v_{i}\|=\|av_{i-r\deg(a)}\|=\|bv_{i-r\deg(b)}\|\text{ for }i\gg 0.

Rearranging the terms, we get that, for

:

\|v_{i-r\deg(a)}\|=\|a\|^{-1}\|b\|\|v_{i-r\deg(b)}\|=q^{e}\|v_{i-r\deg(b)}\|.

Shifting the indices we get

\|v_{i}\|=q^{e}\|v_{i-r(\deg(b)-\deg(a))}\|=q^{e}\|v_{i-er}\|\text{ for }i\gg 0,

which is the first statement.

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, since the norm sequence is weakly increasing, we have the following inequality for

\frac{s_{i+k}}{s_{i}}\leq\frac{s_{i+er\left\lceil\frac{k}{er}\right\rceil}}{s_{i}}=q^{e\left\lceil\frac{k}{er}\right\rceil}.

Moreover, for all

\prod_{j=0}^{er-1}\frac{s_{i+k(j+1)}}{s_{i+kj}}=\frac{s_{i+ker}}{s_{i}}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{s_{i+e(j+1)r}}{s_{i+ejr}}=q^{ek}&\text{if }k\geq 0\\
\prod_{j=k}^{-1}\frac{s_{i+e(j+1)r}}{s_{i+ejr}}=q^{ek}&\text{if }k<0,\end{cases}

hence at least one of the factors on the left hand side has norm at most

; this implies that the inequality

\frac{s_{i+k}}{s_{i}}\leq q^{\frac{k}{r}}

holds for infinitely many values of

.

∎

Definition 5.13
.

Remark 5.14
.

For all
c\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, since
V\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
is a lattice, the infinite product
c\prod_{v\in V}\left(1-\frac{c}{v}\right)
converges, and is equal to
\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{V,n}c^{q^{n}}
. In particular,

\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{V,n}x^{q^{n}}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![x]\!]

is the only power series with infinite radius of convergence and with leading coefficient
1
such that its zeroes are simple and coincide with
V
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Fix a lattice
V\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, with norm sequence
(r_{i})_{i\geq 1}
. Fix an ordered basis
(v_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and call
V_{m}\coloneqq\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{v_{i}\}_{i\leq m})
for all
m\geq 0
. We have:

•

for all
k\geq 0
:

\|e_{V,k}\|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}^{q^{i-1}-q^{i}};

•

for all
m>0
, for all
k>0
:

\left\|\sum_{v\in V_{m}}v^{q^{k}-1}\right\|\begin{cases}=0&\text{if }k<m\\
\leq r_{m}^{q^{k}-q^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}&\text{if }k\geq m.\end{cases}

Proof.

For the first part, if
k=0
then
e_{V,k}=1
, so there is nothing to prove. If
k>0
, we have:

\|e_{V,k}\|=\left\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\setminus\{0\}\\
|I|=q^{k}-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{v\in I}v^{-1}\right\|\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\setminus\{0\}\\
|I|=q^{k}-1\end{subarray}}\left\|\prod_{v\in I}v^{-1}\right\|=\left\|\prod_{v\in V_{k}}v^{-1}\right\|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}^{q^{i-1}-q^{i}}.

For the second part, note that the element whose norm we are trying to estimate is equal to

, in the notation of Lemma
5.12
. By that lemma, if
k<m
, the element is zero, otherwise we have the following inequality:

\|S_{m,q^{k}-1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})\|\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},\dots,d_{m}\\
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{m}=q^{k}-1\\
\forall j\;d_{1}+\cdots+d_{j}\geq q^{j}-1\end{subarray}}\left\|v_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots v_{m}^{d_{m}}\right\|.

It’s easy to see that the maximum norm of the product

under the specified conditions is obtained when we set

for

and

, therefore we get the desired inequality.
∎

Proof of the identities

We can now prove the main propositions of this section.

Proposition 5.18
.

For all
\beta\in\ker(\exp_{\phi}^{*})
, the following identity holds in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}

\beta=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{g_{\beta}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Proof.

The series converges for all
\beta\in\ker(\exp_{\phi}^{*})
because the denominators belong to the lattice
\Lambda_{\phi}
and the numerators to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
\beta=0
the identity is obvious, hence we can assume
\beta\neq 0
.
Fix an ordered basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
and define

\Lambda_{m}\coloneqq\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\leq m})\text{ for all }m\geq 0.

By Lemma
5.17
,
\ker(g_{\beta})\subseteq\Lambda_{\phi}
has codimension

5.10
, if we denote by
(r_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and
(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}
the norm sequences respectively of
\Lambda_{\phi}
and
\ker(g_{\beta})
, there is a positive integer

such that

s_{i}=\begin{cases}r_{i}&\text{if }i<N\\
r_{i+1}&\text{if }i\geq N.\end{cases}

For all

, we define:

S_{m}\coloneqq\beta+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{m}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{g_{\beta}(\lambda)}{\lambda}=\beta\sum_{k\geq 1}e_{\ker(g_{\beta}),k}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{m}}\lambda^{q^{k}-1}.

By Lemma
5.15
, we have:

\displaystyle\|\beta^{-1}S_{m}\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k\geq 1}e_{\ker(g_{\beta}),k}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{m}}\lambda^{q^{k}-1}\right\|\leq\max_{k\geq m}\left\{\|e_{\ker(g_{\beta}),k}\|\left\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{m}}\lambda^{q^{k}-1}\right\|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{k\geq m}\left\{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}^{q^{i-1}-q^{i}}\right)\left(r_{m}^{q^{k}-q^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{k\geq m}\left\{\left(\prod_{i=N}^{k}r_{i+1}^{q^{i-1}-q^{i}}\right)\left(r_{m}^{q^{k}-q^{m}}\prod_{i=N}^{m}r_{i}^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{k\geq m}\left\{\left(\prod_{i=N}^{m}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r_{i+1}}\right)^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=m+1}^{k}\left(\frac{r_{m}}{r_{i}}\right)^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=N}^{m}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r_{i+1}}\right)^{q^{i}-q^{i-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r_{N}}{r_{m+1}}\right)^{q^{N}-q^{N-1}}\prod_{i=N+1}^{m}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r_{m+1}}\right)^{q^{i}-2q^{i-1}+q^{i-2}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r_{N}}{r_{m+1}}\right)^{q^{N}-q^{N-1}}.

Since this number tends to zero as

tends to infinity, we have the following identity in

:

0=\lim_{m}S_{m}=\lim_{m}\left(\beta+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{m}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{g_{\beta}(\lambda)}{\lambda}\right)=\beta+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{g_{\beta}(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Proposition 5.20
.

For all integers

, for all

with
\|c\|\leq q^{\frac{k-1}{r}}
, the following identity holds in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp_{\phi}(c\lambda)}{\lambda^{q^{k}}}=-\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}l_{k-j}^{q^{j}}c^{q^{j}},

where by convention the summation on the right hand side is

Proof.

First of all, let’s show that series on the left hand side converges. Since
\exp_{\phi}({K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi})
is homeomorphic to the compact space
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}}

, so the series converges.

Fix an ordered basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
, set
r_{i}\coloneqq\|\lambda_{i}\|
, and define

\Lambda_{m}\coloneqq\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\leq m})\text{ for all }m\geq 1;

define:

for all

5.15
, for all

\displaystyle\|S_{m}\|=

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\limsup_{m}\|S_{m}\|\leq C_{k}\cdot\limsup_{j}\prod_{i=m+1}^{j}\left(\frac{\|c\|r_{i}}{r_{i+k}}\right)^{q^{i+k}-q^{i+k-1}},

\displaystyle\|c\|\frac{r_{i}}{r_{i+k}}

-
module
M
, it induces a morphism
\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(M)\to\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{A}(\hat{M},{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})
. Fix some
\zeta\in\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(M)
, with image
\overline{\zeta}
: since
\hat{M}
is compact,
\overline{\zeta}(\hat{M})
must be a compact
A
-
submodule of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, but for any
c\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\{0\}
the set
A\cdot c
is unbounded, hence
\overline{\zeta}\equiv 0
. We deduce that the image of
\zeta:\hat{M}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\phi^{*}}
must be contained in
\ker\exp_{\phi}^{*}
, which by Theorem
5.5
is isomorphic as a topological
A
-
module to
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}
; we have the following natural isomorphisms:

\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(M)=\Hom_{A}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\hat{M},\ker\exp_{\phi}^{*})\cong\Hom_{A}^{\mathrm{cont}}\left(\widehat{\ker\exp_{\phi}^{*}},M\right)\cong\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},M),

where we used Lemma
2.6
for the second isomorphism.

The universal object
\zeta_{\phi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\phi^{*}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
is given by the natural morphism

\psi:\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}\cong\ker{\exp_{\phi}^{*}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\phi^{*}}

of Theorem
5.5
, which by Proposition
5.18
sends
g\in\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}
to

-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{g(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

If we fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
, with
(\lambda_{i}^{*})_{i}
dual basis of
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}
, by Proposition
2.14
we can write
\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}\psi(\lambda_{i}^{*})\otimes\lambda_{i}
, hence:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{*}(\lambda)}{\lambda}\right)\otimes\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\},i}\lambda^{-1}\otimes\lambda_{i}^{*}(\lambda)\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-1}\otimes\lambda.

∎

Definition 6.5
.

We define the
universal dual Anderson eigenvector

\zeta_{\phi}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
as the universal object of the functor
\Sf_{\phi^{*}}
.

Corollary 6.6
.

For all discrete
A
-
modules
M
,
\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(M)
, as an
A\otimes A
-
module, is isomorphic to
\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},M)
. In particular, for any
M
we have the following equality between subsets of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}M
:

\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(M)=\left\{\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-1}\otimes l(\lambda)\biggm|l\in\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},M)\right\}.

Remark 6.7
.

Fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
of the discrete
A
-
module
\Lambda_{\phi}
, with
(\lambda_{i}^{*})_{i}
dual basis of
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}
. By Proposition
2.14
we can express the universal object in the following alternative way as an element of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\phi^{*}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
:

\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}\psi(\lambda_{i}^{*})\otimes\lambda_{i},

where
\psi
denotes Poonen’s isomorphism
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}\cong\ker(\exp_{\phi}^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\phi^{*}}
.

6.2
A convergence result for the universal Anderson eigenvector

Let’s fix a Drinfeld module
(\mathbb{G}_{a},\phi)
of rank
1
and an ordered basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
. By Proposition
2.14
, there is a unique sequence
(z_{i})_{i}
in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, converging to
0
, such that we can write
\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}z_{i}\otimes\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
. Under the assumption
\infty\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Chung, Ngo Dac, and Pellarin proved that, for any nonnegative integer
k
,
\sum_{i}z_{i}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}
converges to the
k
-
th coefficient of the logarithm, while for any negative integer
k
it converges to
0
(
Chung et al. 2023
).

We aim to generalize this result to a Drinfeld module of arbitrary rank—without any assumption on
\infty
—by exploiting the defining property of the universal Anderson eigenvector.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
(\mathbb{G}_{a},\phi)
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
, fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear basis
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
, and write
\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}z_{i}\otimes\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
. Then, for all integers
k
the series
\sum_{i}z_{i}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}
converges; moreover, if
k\geq 0
it converges to the
k
-
th coefficient of the logarithm
l_{k}
, while if
k<0
it converges to
0
.

Proof.

Let’s fix
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
and fix an ordered basis
(\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
. By Lemma
5.11
there is some
N
such that, for all
i>N
,
\|\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}\|=\|a\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i-r\deg(a)}\|
; if we define

\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}&\text{if }i\leq N\\
a\lambda^{\prime}_{i-r\deg(a)}&\text{if }i>N,\end{cases}

we have
\|\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{i}\|=\|\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\|
for all
i
, hence
(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})_{i}
is also an ordered basis of
\Lambda_{\phi}
.

Let’s write
\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}z^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
; if we denote by
({\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*})_{i}
the corresponding dual basis of
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}
, if we call
\psi
Poonen’s isomorphism
\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}\cong\ker(\exp_{\phi}^{*})\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, by the Remark
6.7
we know that
z^{\prime}_{i}=\psi({\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*})
; in particular, for
i\gg 0
, we have

z^{\prime}_{i}=\phi_{a}^{*}(z^{\prime}_{i+r\deg(a)}).

Let’s write
\phi_{a}^{*}=\sum_{k}\tau^{-k}a_{k}
. There is some real constant
\varepsilon>0
such that that, for any
c\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
with
\|c\|<\varepsilon
,

\|\phi_{a}^{*}(c)\|=\|a_{r\deg(a)}c\|^{q^{-r\deg(a)}}<\|c\|.

Since the sequence
(z^{\prime}_{i})_{i}
converges to
0
, for
i\gg 0
we have

\|z^{\prime}_{i}\|=\|\phi_{a}^{*}(z^{\prime}_{i+r\deg(a)})\|=\|a_{r\deg(a)}z^{\prime}_{i+r\deg(a)}\|^{q^{-r\deg(a)}},

hence

\|z^{\prime}_{i+r\deg(a)}\|=\|z^{\prime}_{i}\|^{q^{r\deg(a)}}\|a_{r\deg(a)}\|^{-1}.

By recursion, for all
k\geq 0
there is a positive real constant
\varepsilon<1
and a positive integer
M
such that, for
i>M
:

\|z^{\prime}_{i+kr\deg(a)}\|=\|z^{\prime}_{i}\|^{q^{kr\deg(a)}}\|a_{r\deg(a)}\|^{-\frac{q^{kr\deg(a)}-1}{q-1}}<\varepsilon^{q^{kr\deg(a)}}

In particular, by setting
i=M+1,\dots,M+r\deg(a)
, and setting
\delta\coloneqq\varepsilon^{q^{-M-r\deg(a)}}<1
, we deduce that
\|z^{\prime}_{n}\|<\delta^{q^{n}}
for
n\geq M
. In particular, the series
\sum_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
converges in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
for any integer
k
.

For all
i
, we can write
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
as a finite sum
\sum_{j}\alpha_{i,j}\lambda_{j}
with constants
\alpha_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so we have:

\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i}z^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{i}z^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\left(\sum_{j}\alpha_{i,j}\lambda_{j}\right)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\alpha_{i,j}z^{\prime}_{i}\otimes\lambda_{j}=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i,j}z^{\prime}_{i}\right)\otimes\lambda_{j}.

For all
j
, we deduce
z_{j}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i,j}z^{\prime}_{i}
. Moreover, for any integer
k
:

\sum_{j}z_{j}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{j}=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i\geq j}\alpha_{i,j}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\right)\lambda_{j}=\sum_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\left(\sum_{j\leq i}\alpha_{i,j}\lambda_{j}\right)=\sum_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}.

For all
k
, let’s set
l^{\prime}_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{i}{z_{i}^{\prime}}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
.
If
k>0
, we have:

\displaystyle l_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}}\lambda^{1-q^{k}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}}\lambda^{-q^{k}}\sum_{i}{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*}(\lambda)\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}}\lambda^{-q^{k}}{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*}(\lambda)\right)\lambda^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}}\lambda^{-1}{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*}(\lambda)\right)^{q^{k}}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{i}g({\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}^{*})^{q^{k}}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=l^{\prime}_{k}.

Note that for all
a\in A
,
\sum_{i}z^{\prime}_{i}\otimes a\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i}\phi_{a}^{*}(z^{\prime}_{i})\otimes\lambda_{i}
, hence for any integer
k
we have the identity:

\sum_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i}\phi_{a}^{*}(z^{\prime}_{i})^{q^{k}}\lambda_{i}.

Define
\log^{\prime}_{\phi}\coloneqq\sum_{k}l^{\prime}_{k}\tau^{k}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau^{-1},\tau]\!]
. For all
a\in A
, if we write
\phi_{a}^{*}=\sum_{j}\tau^{-j}a_{j}
, we have:

\displaystyle a\log^{\prime}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=a\sum_{k}l^{\prime}_{k}\tau^{k}=\sum_{k}\sum_{i}a\lambda^{\prime}_{i}{z^{\prime}_{i}}^{q^{k}}\tau^{k}=\sum_{k}\sum_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\phi_{a}^{*}(z^{\prime}_{i})^{q^{k}}\tau^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\left(a_{j}z^{\prime}_{i}\right)^{q^{k-j}}\tau^{k}=\sum_{k}\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\tau^{k-j}z^{\prime}_{i}\right)a_{j}\tau^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{j}l^{\prime}_{k-j}\tau^{k-j}a_{j}\tau^{j}=\log^{\prime}_{\phi}\circ\phi_{a}.

Since
\log_{\phi}
has the same property,
\log_{\phi}-\log^{\prime}_{\phi}
is a series in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau^{-1}]\!]
such that
a(\log_{\phi}-\log^{\prime}_{\phi})=(\log_{\phi}-\log^{\prime}_{\phi})\phi_{a}
for all
a\in A
. Since the degrees of both sides differ if
a\not\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\log_{\phi}-\log^{\prime}_{\phi}\neq 0
, we deduce that
\log_{\phi}=\log^{\prime}_{\phi}
, hence
l^{\prime}_{k}=0
for all
k<0
and
l^{\prime}_{0}=1
.

∎

7
Pairing between Anderson eigenvectors and dual Anderson eigenvectors

Let’s fix a Drinfeld module
\phi
with exponential
\exp_{\phi}=\sum_{i\geq 0}e_{i}\tau^{i}
and assume that
\Lambda_{\phi}:=\ker\exp_{\phi}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
has rank
r
as an
A
-
module; let
\log_{\phi}=\sum_{i\geq 0}l_{i}\tau^{i}
be the inverse of
\exp_{\phi}
as an element of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!]
.

7.1
Definition of the dot product

The following rationality result (a weak version of
(
Ferraro 2022
, Thm. 6.3)
) links Anderson eigenvectors and dual Anderson eigenvectors in the rank 1 case.

Theorem 7.1

(Ferraro)
.

Assume that
\phi
is a normalized Drinfeld module of rank
1
. The product of an element in
\Sf_{\phi^{*}}(A)
and an element in
\Sf_{\phi}(A)
is a rational function over
X_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

To generalize this statement to Drinfeld modules of arbitrary rank, we need a proper way of "multiplying"
\zeta_{\phi}
and
\omega_{\phi}
, established in the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

The following
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
linear pairing is well defined:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}(\Lambda_{\phi}^{*}\otimes_{A}\Omega)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{i}c_{i}\otimes\lambda_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{j}d_{j}\otimes(\lambda^{*}_{j}\otimes\omega_{j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{i,j}(c_{i}d_{j})\otimes(\lambda^{*}_{j}(\lambda_{i})\omega_{j})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mapsto}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coloneqq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coloneqq}

Moreover, considering
g
and
f
as continuous functions respectively from
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
and
\Lambda_{\phi}\otimes_{A}{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
, for all
b\in{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
we have:

g(b)=\sum_{i}c_{i}f(\lambda_{i}\otimes b).

Proof.

The morphism is well defined because for all
\varepsilon>0
there are finitely many pairs of indices
(i,j)
such that
\|c_{i}d_{j}\|>\varepsilon
; the
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
linearity is also obvious from the definition. Call
\res:\Omega\otimes{K_{\infty}}/A\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\res_{\Lambda_{\phi}}:(\Lambda_{\phi}^{*}\otimes_{A}\Omega)\otimes\left(\Lambda_{\phi}\otimes_{A}{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}\right)\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
the two perfect pairings. By Remark
2.11
we have:

g(b)=\sum_{i,j}c_{i}d_{j}\res(\lambda^{*}_{j}(\lambda_{i})\omega_{j},b)=\sum_{i}c_{i}\sum_{j}d_{j}\res_{\Lambda}(\lambda^{*}_{j}\otimes\omega_{j},\lambda_{i}\otimes b)=\sum_{i}c_{i}f(\lambda_{i}\otimes b).

7.2
Rationality of the dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}

The pairing defined in Lemma
7.2
will be denoted by a dot product. For any element
h\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega=\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left({{K_{\infty}}}/{A},{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\right)
and for any
b\in{K_{\infty}}
with projection
\,\overline{\!{b}}\in{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
, to simplify notation we will write
h(b)
to denote
h(\,\overline{\!{b}})
. We can now state the generalization of Theorem
7.1
.

Theorem 7.3
.

For any Drinfeld module
\phi
, for all integers
k
, the dot product
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega
is a rational differential form over the base-changed curve
X_{{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}
. Moreover, for all positive integers
k
,
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\in\Omega_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

As an element of
\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\mathrm{cont}}\left({{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}/{\Lambda_{\phi}},{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\right)
,
\omega_{\phi}
sends the projection of any
c\in{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}
to
\exp_{\phi}(c)
. By Lemma
7.2
, since
\zeta_{\phi}=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\lambda^{-1}\otimes\lambda
, for all
b\in{K_{\infty}}
and for all integers
k
we have:

\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}(b)=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp(b\lambda)^{q^{k}}}{\lambda}=\left(-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp(b\lambda)}{\lambda^{q^{-k}}}\right)^{q^{k}}.

By Proposition
5.20
, for all positive integers
k
, if
b\in{K_{\infty}}
has norm at most
q^{-\frac{k+1}{r}}
,
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}(b)=0
. Let’s denote by
C\subseteq{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
the subspace generated by the projections of elements in
{K_{\infty}}
with norm at most
q^{h}
, and denote by
Q
the quotient. Since
Q
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space, we get the following:

\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\mathrm{cont}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}}},{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\right)\supseteq\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(Q,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(Q,{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}})={\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\hat{Q}.

Since
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
restricted to
C
is identically
0
, it’s contained in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\hat{Q}
, therefore it can be expressed as a finite sum:

\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}=\sum_{i}c_{i}\otimes\mu_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\hat{Q}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\widehat{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}}}}=\Omega_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}.

To prove the theorem for all integers
k
we proceed by induction. Assume that the result holds for all integers bigger than
k
, and fix some
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. From the definition of special functions we have:

\displaystyle(1\otimes a-a\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}=\frac{1}{1\otimes a-a^{q^{k}}\otimes 1}\sum_{i=1}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}^{q^{k}}\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k+i)},

hence
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
is a rational differential form over
X_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

From the previous proof we deduce that, if we can compute the dot product
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
for
r\deg(a)
consecutive integers
k
, then we can compute it for any value of
k
.

7.3
Computation of the dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
for
k\ll 0

We can expand on the previous theorem. In fact, we are able to describe explicitly the differential form
\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}
for
k
large enough by using once again Proposition
5.20
.

Theorem 7.5
.

For all
b\in{K_{\infty}}
denote by
s(b)\in{K_{\infty}}
an element of smallest norm such that
b-s(b)\in A
. For all integers
k>re\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)
, we have the following identity for all
b\in{K_{\infty}}
:

\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}(b)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}l_{k-j}^{q^{j}}s(b)^{q^{j}}.

Proof.

Recall that the norm of all elements in
{K_{\infty}}
is an integer power of
q^{e}
. Fix any
b\in{K_{\infty}}
, assume
\|s(b)\|=q^{ed}
for some integer
d
; the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
vector space
{H^{0}(X,d\infty)}/{H^{0}(X,(d-1)\infty)}
has dimension less than
e
, otherwise there would be some
a\in H^{0}(X,d\infty)\subseteq A
such that
\|s(b)-a\|<\|s(b)\|
, contradicting the minimality condition on
s(b)
. By Riemann–Roch, if
e(d-1)>2g-2
, the spaces
H^{0}(X,(d-1)\infty)
and
H^{0}(X,d\infty)
have dimension respectively
e(d-1)-g+1
and
ed-g+1
, which is a contradiction, hence
\|s(b)\|\leq q^{e\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)}
. Since
\frac{k-1}{r}\geq e\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)
, by Proposition
5.20
we have:

\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}(b)=\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}(s(b))=-\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp_{\phi}(s(b)\lambda)}{\lambda^{q^{k}}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}l_{k-j}^{q^{j}}s(b)^{q^{j}}.

Remark 7.6
.

Equivalently, for all integers
k>re\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)
and for all
b\in{K_{\infty}}
:

\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-k)}(b)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}l_{k-j}^{q^{j}}s(b)^{q^{j}}\right)^{q^{-k}}.

In principle, we can use this result to compute
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}(b)
for all
i
and all
b
, in the same way we proved rationality in Theorem
7.3
, as we observed in Remark
7.4
.

7.4
The generating series of the dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}

Using Theorem
7.5
and Remark
7.6
, we can in principle compute the dot product
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
for any
k\geq-re\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)
, but since the sketched algorithm is recursive, it’s necessary to compute all the intermediate dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}
for
i
between
-re\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)
and
k
.

The objective of this subsection is to streamline this computation by studying the generating series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\tau^{k}
.

Definition 7.7
.

Denote by
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
the subset of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!][\tau^{-1}]
given by the series with a nonzero radius of convergence on
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

Remark 7.8
.

The set
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
is closed under addition and composition, hence it is a subring of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!][\tau^{-1}]
.

Remark 7.9
.

Since the radius of convergence of
h=\sum_{i}h_{i}\tau^{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!][\tau^{-1}]
is the inverse of
\limsup_{i\to\infty}\|h_{i}\|^{q^{-i}}
, we have that
h\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
if and only if
\limsup_{i\to\infty}\|h_{i}\|^{q^{-i}}<\infty
.

Lemma 7.10
.

Every nonzero element
h\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
admits a (unique) bilateral inverse in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
.

Proof.

Since
\tau:{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
is an isomorphism, up to multiplication we can assume

h=\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{i}\tau^{i},

with
h_{0}=1
. If we call

h_{+}\coloneqq-\sum_{i\geq 1}h_{i}\tau^{i}=1-h,

the series
\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{+}^{i}
is a well defined bilateral inverse of
h
in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!]
. Since
h
has finitely many nonzero coefficients, it’s easy to see that there is some
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and some positive real constant
C<1
such that, for all
x\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
with norm less than
R
,
\|h_{i}x^{q^{i}}\|\leq C\|x\|
for all
i\geq 1
. In particular, for all
x\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
with norm less than
R
, each of the finitely many summands in the expansion of
h_{+}^{i}(x)
has norm at most
C^{i}\|x\|
, hence the series

\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{+}^{i}(x)

converges. We deduce that the series
\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{+}^{i}
has a nonzero radius of convergence, hence it belongs to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
.
∎

Definition 7.11
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
we define
\Phi_{c}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
as
\exp_{\phi}\circ c\circ\log_{\phi}
.

Remark 7.12
.

For all
a\in A
,
\Phi_{a}=\phi_{a}
. The map
\Phi:{K_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
sending
c
to
\Phi_{c}
is the unique ring homomorphism which extends
\phi:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
such that each coefficient is a continuous function.

For a series
s\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
and an integer
k
, let’s denote by
(s)_{k}
its
k
-th coefficient, so that
s=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(s)_{k}\tau^{k}
.

Proposition 7.13
.

Let
\mu:{K_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
be a function with the following properties:

1.

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}
the function sending
c
to
(\mu_{c})_{k}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear and continuous;

2.

\forall a\in A,c\in{K_{\infty}}
,
\mu_{ac}=\mu_{c}\phi_{a}
;

3.

\forall a\in A
,
\mu_{a}=0
;

4.

\forall R\in\mathbb{R}
there is some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
with
\|c\|\leq R
,
(\mu_{c})_{n}=(\Phi_{c})_{n}
.

The function
\mu
is uniquely determined; in addition, for any
c\in{K_{\infty}}
, we have:

\mu_{c}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)\tau^{k}.

Proof.

To prove uniqueness, let’s take two such functions
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
, and define

\lambda:=\mu-\mu^{\prime}.

For each element
c\in{K_{\infty}}
let
s(c)
be an element of least norm such that
c-s(c)\in A
. As we already said in the proof of Theorem
7.5
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
,

\|s(c)\|\leq q^{e\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)};

using properties
1
,
3
, and
4
with
R=q^{e\left(\left\lfloor\frac{2g-2}{e}\right\rfloor+1\right)}
, we deduce that there is some integer
n_{0}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{0}
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
:

(\lambda_{c})_{n}=(\lambda_{s(c)})_{n}+(\lambda_{c-s(c)})_{n}=(\lambda_{s(c)})_{n}=(\mu_{s(c)})_{n}-(\mu^{\prime}_{s(c)})_{n}=(\Phi_{s(c)})_{n}-(\Phi_{s(c)})_{n}=0.

If by contradiction
\lambda\not\equiv 0
, there is an element
c\in{K_{\infty}}
such that
\lambda_{c}
has the highest degree; by property
2
, for any
a\in A\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\lambda_{ac}=\lambda_{c}\phi_{a}
, which has a greater degree than
\lambda_{c}
, reaching a contradiction.

Let’s check that

\mu_{c}\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)\tau^{k}

satisfies all conditions. The properties
1
and
3
are obvious. For property
4
, note that for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}

(\Phi_{c})_{k}=(\exp_{\phi}\circ c\circ\log_{\phi})_{k}=\sum_{i+j=k}e_{i}c^{q^{i}}l_{j}^{q^{i}},

which is equal to
\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)
for all
k\geq r\cdot\log_{q}(\|c\|)+1
by Proposition
5.20
. Finally, for property
2
, since
\zeta_{\phi}
is an Anderson eigenvector, for all
a\in A
we have:

(1\otimes a)\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i=0}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}^{q^{-i}}\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}\Rightarrow\text{for all integers $k$, }(1\otimes a)\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=0}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}^{q^{k-i}}\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-i)}.

We deduce that, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
:

\displaystyle\mu_{c}\phi_{a}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)\tau^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}\tau^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r\deg(a)}(\phi_{a})_{i}^{q^{k-i}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-i)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)\right)\tau^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((1\otimes a)\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)\tau^{k}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(ac)\tau^{k}=\mu_{ac}.

∎

Remark 7.14
.

If
c\in{K_{\infty}}\setminus A
, the formal power series
\mu_{c}
has no obvious convergence properties. In fact, if
c\in K\setminus A
we can choose
a\in A
so that
ca\in A
, and we get that
\mu_{c}\phi_{a}=\mu_{ac}=0
: since
\mu_{c}\neq 0
, this implies that its radius of convergence is
0
.

Definition 7.15
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
we define
\hat{\Phi}_{c}\coloneqq(\Phi_{c}-\mu_{c})^{*}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
.

Proposition 7.16
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
, the series
\hat{\Phi}_{c}
has a nonzero radius of convergence. Moreover, the map
\hat{\Phi}:{K_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
sending
c
to
\hat{\Phi}_{c}
is the unique ring homomorphism which extends
\phi^{*}:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
such that each coefficient is a continuous function.

Proof.

Uniqueness is obvious: by multiplicativity there is at most one way to extend
\phi^{*}
to the fraction field
K
, and by continuity there is at most one way to extend it to the completion
{K_{\infty}}
. By definition of
\Phi
and
\mu
, each coefficient of
\hat{\Phi}_{c}
is a continuous function of
c
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
, by Proposition
5.20
we have
(\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{k}=\left((\Phi_{c}-\mu_{c})^{*}\right)_{k}=0
for
k\ll 0
, hence
\hat{\Phi}_{c}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!][\tau^{-1}]
.
On the other hand, for
k\gg 0
:

((\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{k})^{q^{-k}}=((-\mu_{c}^{*})_{k})^{q^{-k}}=-(\mu_{c})_{-k}=-\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-k)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(c)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\phi}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp(c\lambda)}{\lambda^{q^{-k}}};

all the numerators of the series belong to the compact space
\exp({K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi})\cong{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}/{\Lambda_{\phi}}
, and since
\Lambda_{\phi}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
is discrete all the denominators are bounded from below: this means that the set
\{((\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{k})^{q^{-k}}\}_{k\gg 0}
is bounded, hence
\hat{\Phi}_{c}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
by Remark
7.9
.
For all
a\in A
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{a}

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{a}-\mu_{a})^{*}=\phi^{*}_{a}

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{a}\circ\hat{\Phi}_{c}

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{c}\circ\phi_{a}-\mu_{c}\circ\phi_{a})^{*}=(\Phi_{ac}-\mu_{ac})^{*}=\hat{\Phi}_{ac},

which implies that
\hat{\Phi}
extends
\phi^{*}
multiplicatively.
∎

Remark 7.17
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
we have:

\mu_{c}^{*}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(c)\tau^{k}.

A posteriori, we can repackage the results of this subsection under the following theorem.

Theorem 7.18
.

Let
\Phi,\hat{\Phi}:{K_{\infty}}\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
be the unique ring homomorphisms which extend respectively
\phi,\phi^{*}:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\langle\tau\rangle
and such that their
k
-
th coefficient is a continuous function from
{K_{\infty}}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The following identity holds in the
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau,\tau^{-1}]
-
module
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau,\tau^{-1}]\!]
for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(c)\tau^{k}=\Phi_{c}^{*}-\hat{\Phi}_{c}.

This Theorem allows us to partially carry out the computation of the dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
, such as in the following Proposition.

Proposition 7.19
.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
with norm less than
1
:

\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(c)=\begin{cases}c&\text{if }k=0\\
0&\text{if }1\leq k\leq r-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

For all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
the lowest degree of
\hat{\Phi}_{c}
is
-r\deg(c)
, while the highest degree of
\Phi_{c}^{*}
is
0
. In particular, if
\|c\|<1
, i.e.
\deg(c)\leq-1
, we have:

\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(c)=(\Phi^{*}_{c}-\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{k}=\begin{cases}(\Phi^{*}_{c}-\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{0}=(\Phi^{*}_{c})_{0}=c&\text{if }k=0\\
(\Phi^{*}_{c}-\hat{\Phi}_{c})_{k}=0&\text{if }1\leq k\leq r-1.\end{cases}

7.5
Application to the case of genus
0
and arbitrary rank

Thanks to Theorem
7.18
, we can compute efficiently the dot products
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
in the case of genus
0
and rational point at infinity. In this subsection we assume
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
, and we fix a rational function
\theta
over
X
with a simple pole at
\infty
. In this case we can write
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
,
{K_{\infty}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\theta^{-1})\!)
and
\Omega=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]d\theta
, where
d\theta:{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
sends
\theta^{n}
to
\delta_{-1,n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 7.20
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
. We have the following identities in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega
:

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\theta}{\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\theta};

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=0\;\;\;\forall 1\leq k\leq r-1.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.19
, for all
n>0
we have:

\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(\theta^{-n})=\begin{cases}\theta^{-n}&\text{if }k=0\\
0&\text{if }1\leq k\leq r-1.\end{cases}

Since
\theta^{n}\in A
for all
n\geq 0
, we also have
\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}\right)(\theta^{n})=0
for all
n\geq 0
and for all
k
so, if
1\leq k\leq r-1
,
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(k)}
is identically zero. If instead
k=0
we have the following identity for all integers
n
:

\left((\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\theta)\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}\right)(\theta^{n})=\theta\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}(\theta^{n})\right)-\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}(\theta^{n+1})=\delta_{-1,n}=d\theta(\theta^{n}),

hence

\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}=\frac{d\theta}{(\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\theta)}.

We now relate the usual definition of Anderson generating functions to the universal Anderson eigenvector, by giving a basis-dependent description of the latter.

Lemma 7.21
.

Fix the
A
-
linear bases
\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}\}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
and
\{\pi_{1}^{*},\dots,\pi_{r}^{*}\}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}^{*}
, where
\pi_{i}^{*}(\pi_{k})=\delta_{i,k}
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\omega_{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j\geq 0}\exp_{\phi}\left(\frac{\pi_{i}}{\theta^{j+1}}\right)\otimes\theta^{j}\pi_{i}^{*}d\theta,

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\frac{d\theta\pi_{i}^{*}}{\theta^{j+1}}(\lambda)\lambda^{-1}\right)\otimes\theta^{j}\pi_{i}.

Proof.

When used as indices, we imply
i
to vary among the integers between
1
and
r
, extremes included, and
j
to vary among the nonnegative integers.
The chosen bases induce an isomorphism

\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)\cong\bigoplus_{i}Ad\theta\pi_{i}^{*}.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear basis
\{\theta^{j}d\theta\pi_{i}^{*}\}_{i,j}
of
\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)
induces a dual basis
\{\theta^{-j-1}\pi_{i}\}_{i,j}
of

\widehat{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Lambda_{\phi}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Lambda_{\phi}}$}}}.

Similarly, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear basis
\{\theta^{j}\pi_{i}\}_{i,j}
of
\Lambda_{\phi}
: induces the dual basis
\{\theta^{-j-1}d\theta\pi_{i}^{*}\}_{i,j}
of

\hat{\Lambda}_{\phi}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)}$}}}.

This concludes the proof, by virtue of Remark
3.14
and the proof of Theorem
6.4
.
∎

Definition 7.22
.

For
i=1,\dots,r
we define the
i
-
th Anderson generating function as:

\omega_{\phi,i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\geq 0}\exp_{\phi}\left(\frac{\pi_{i}}{\theta^{j+1}}\right)\otimes\theta^{j}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A.

Similarly, for
i=1,\dots,r
we define the
i
-
th dual Anderson generating function as:

\zeta_{\phi,i}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\frac{d\theta\pi_{i}^{*}}{\theta^{j+1}}(\lambda)\lambda^{-1}\right)\otimes\theta^{j}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A.

Remark 7.23
.

For all integers
1\leq i\leq r
,
\omega_{\phi,i}
and
\zeta_{\phi,i}
are the unique elements in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A
such that the identities
(1\otimes\pi_{i})(\omega_{\phi})=\omega_{\phi,i}d\theta
and
(1\otimes\pi^{*}_{i})(\zeta_{\phi})=\zeta_{\phi,i}
hold (in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega
and
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A

Definition 7.24
.

Let’s define
\bm{\omega}_{\phi}\coloneqq(\omega_{\phi,i}^{(j-1)})_{i,j}\in\Mat_{r\times r}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A)
. We call it the
rigid analytic trivialization
of the
t
-
motive attached to
\phi
.

The previous matrix has been studied in various articles (see for example
(
Pellarin 2008
, Section 4.2)
,
Khaochim and Papanikolas 2023
,
Gezmiş and Papanikolas 2019
). We can use it to state the following Theorem.

Theorem 7.25
.

The product of
\zeta_{\phi}\in\Mat_{1\times r}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A)
and
\bm{\omega}_{\phi}\in\Mat_{r\times r}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A)
is the vector

\frac{1}{(\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\theta)}\cdot(1,0,\dots,0)\in\Mat_{1\times r}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A).

Proof.

Note that we have interpreted
\zeta_{\phi}
as
(\zeta_{\phi,i})_{i}\in\Mat_{1\times r}({\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}A)
. If we multiply by
d\theta\in\Omega
the
j
-
th coordinate of the product, we get:

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{\phi,i}^{(j-1)}\zeta_{\phi,i}d\theta=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{\phi,i}\pi_{i}^{*}d\theta\right)^{(j-1)}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\zeta_{\phi,i}\pi_{i}\right)=\omega_{\phi}^{(j-1)}\cdot\zeta_{\phi},

which is
\frac{d\theta}{(\theta\otimes 1-1\otimes\theta)}
if

by Proposition
7.20
, and
0
otherwise by Proposition
7.19
.
∎

Remark 7.26
.

It’s a well known result
that the determinant of the matrix
\bm{\omega}_{\phi}
is nonzero (see for example
(

from

.

7.6
Application to the case of hyperelliptic curves

In the case of rank
1
normalized Drinfeld modules, the result
(
Ferraro 2022
, Thm. 7.26)
can be used to express the rational form
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}
in terms of the Drinfeld divisor. While Theorem
7.18
, in principle, completely describes the form
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}
, it’s not as explicit a result for arbitrary curves.

In this subsection we restrict ourselves to the case of a hyperelliptic curve
X
with hyperelliptic divisor
2\infty
and a Drinfeld module
\phi
of rank
1
. We use the results of the previous sections to recover an expression for the scalar product
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}
and for the shtuka function
f_{\phi}
in terms of the coefficients of
\phi
.

A curve
X
of genus
g
is hyperelliptic if and only if there is a divisor
D
of degree
2
, called hyperelliptic divisor, such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)))=2
. If we assume
D=2\infty
, there is a rational function
x\in A
of degree
2
. Let’s denote by
y
an element of
A
with the smallest odd degree.

Remark 7.27
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linear basis of
A
is
\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\{x^{i},x^{i}y\}_{i\geq 0}
. In particular, the only positive integers that are not degrees of elements in
A
are the odd positive integers smaller than
\deg(y)
; by Riemann–Roch’s theorem, this set has cardinality
g
, hence
\deg(y)=2g+1
. Expanding
y^{2}
in terms of the basis
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, we deduce that there are polynomials
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
such that
y^{2}=Q(x)y+P(x)
, where
P
has degree
2g+1
and
Q
has degree at most
g
.

If the characteristic of the base field is odd, we can also assume
Q(x)=0
using the coordinate change
y\mapsto y+\frac{Q(x)}{2}
.

Remark 7.28
.

Every element of
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
can be represented by an element of
{K_{\infty}}
with degree either negative or equal to an odd positive number smaller than
2g+1
. We deduce that the image of
\mathcal{B}:=\{yx^{-i-1},x^{-i-1}\}_{i\geq 0}
in
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
is a set of linearly independent elements which spans a dense subset of
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
.

Proposition 7.29
.

If we define
\nu\in\Omega=\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left({{K_{\infty}}}/{A},\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)
as the function sending
yx^{-1}
to
1
and all the other elements of
\mathcal{B}
to
0
, we get that
\Omega=A\nu
.

Proof.

For all
j\geq 0
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
,
(x^{j}\nu)(c)=\nu(x^{j}c)
, which is
1
when
c=yx^{-j-1}
and
0
on all the other elements of
\mathcal{B}
.

Similarly, For all
j\geq 0
, for all
c\in{K_{\infty}}
,
((y-Q(x))x^{j}\nu)(c)=\nu((y-Q(x))x^{j}c)
. If
c=x^{-1-i}
for some
i\geq 0
we have:

((y-Q(x))x^{j}\nu)(c)=\nu(yx^{j-i-1})-\nu(Q(x)x^{j-i-1})=\nu(yx^{j-i-1})=\delta_{j,i}.

If
c=yx^{-1-i}
for some
i\geq 0
we have:

((y-Q(x))x^{j}\nu)(c)=\nu((y^{2}-Q(x)y)x^{j-i-1})=\nu(P(x)x^{j-i-1})=0.

In particular, the elements

\{(y-Q(x))x^{i}\nu,x^{i}\nu\}_{i\geq 0}\subseteq\Omega=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{A}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{K_{\infty}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}}},\mathbb{F}_{q}\right)

are independent, and since
\mathcal{B}
spans a dense subset of
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
, they also generate all of
\Omega
.
∎

Lemma 7.30
.

Denote by
\left({{K_{\infty}}}/{A}\right)_{<q^{-2}}\subseteq{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
the subspace of the elements with norm less than
q^{-2}
, and call
C
the cokernel of this inclusion. Then, the image of

\{yx^{-i-1}\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{x^{-1}\}

forms a basis of
C
, and the set

\{x^{i}\nu\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{(y-Q(x))\nu\}

is the corresponding dual basis of
\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C,\mathbb{F}_{q})\subseteq\Omega
.

Proof.

On one hand, the images of
\{yx^{-i-1}\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{x^{-1}\}
span
C
because they are the only elements of
\mathcal{B}
that are not sent to
0
under the induced map
{K_{\infty}}\to C
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\deg(yx^{-i-1})

\displaystyle=2(g-i)-1\text{ { } for }0\leq i\leq g,

\displaystyle\deg(x^{-1})

\displaystyle=-2,

hence their images are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
linearly independent in
C
.

Note that the image of
\{yx^{-j-1-g},x^{-j-1}\}_{j\geq 1}
in
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
spans a dense subset of
\left({{K_{\infty}}}/{A}\right)_{<q^{-2}}
. For all
0\leq i\leq g
, for all
j\geq 1
we get:

\begin{cases}(x^{i}\nu)(yx^{-j-1-g})=\nu(yx^{i-j-1-g})=0&\text{because $i-j-1-g\leq-2$}\\
(x^{i}\nu)(x^{-j-1})=\nu(x^{i-j})=0&\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}((y-Q(x))\nu)(yx^{-j-1-g})=\nu(P(x)x^{-j-1-g})=0&\\
((y-Q(x))\nu)(x^{-j-1})=\nu(yx^{-j-1})-\nu(Q(x)x^{-j-1})=0&\text{because $-j-1\leq-2$},\end{cases}

so
\{x^{i}\nu\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{y\nu\}\in\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. On the other hand, we have the following identities for all
0\leq i\leq g
and for all
0\leq j\leq g
:

\begin{cases}(x^{i}\nu)(yx^{-j-1})=\nu(yx^{i-j-1})=\delta_{i,j}\\
(x^{i}\nu)(x^{-1})=\nu(x^{i-1})=0\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}((y-Q(x))\nu)(yx^{-j-1})=\nu(P(x)x^{-j-1})=0\\
((y-Q(x))\nu)(x^{-1})=\nu(yx^{-1})-\nu(Q(x)x^{-1})=1.\end{cases}

This implies that

\{x^{i}\nu\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{(y-Q(x))\nu\}

is the dual basis of

\{yx^{-i-1}\}_{0\leq i\leq g}\cup\{x^{-1}\},

as desired.
∎

Theorem 7.32
.

We have the following identities for the dot product

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{y\otimes 1+1\otimes(y-Q(x))}{x\otimes 1-1\otimes x}-\sum_{i=0}^{g-1}\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{0}\otimes x^{i}\right)(1\otimes\nu)

\displaystyle f_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)\left(-\sum_{i=0}^{g}\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{1}\otimes x^{i}\right)}{y\otimes 1+1\otimes(y-Q(x))-(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{g-1}\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{0}\otimes x^{i}\right)}.

For all

of norm less than

(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(c)=x(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(c)-(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(xc)=0.

In particular, by Lemma
7.30

(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})

as a function from

(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(yx^{-i})=yx^{-i}-\left((\phi^{*}_{x})^{-i}\circ\phi^{*}_{y}\right)_{0}

\displaystyle(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(yx^{-i-1})

\displaystyle=\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i}}-x\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{0};

\displaystyle(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})(x^{-1})

\displaystyle\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega^{(1)}_{\phi}\right)(yx^{-i-1})

\displaystyle\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega^{(1)}_{\phi}\right)(x^{-1})

7.30

in

\displaystyle(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{g}\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i}}-x\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{0}\otimes x^{i}+1\otimes(y-Q(x))\right)(1\otimes\nu)

\displaystyle=

7.7
Comparison with known results in the case of elliptic curves

The computations can be directly compared to the results of Green and Papanikolas, who tackled the particular case of an elliptic curve in
Green and Papanikolas 2018
. They assumed
\phi
to be normalized and the period lattice
\Lambda_{\phi}
to be isomorphic to
A
, and they set:

\displaystyle\phi_{x}=x+x_{1}\tau+\tau^{2},

\displaystyle\phi_{y}=y+y_{1}\tau+y_{2}\tau^{2}+\tau^{3}.

They proved the following identities (see
(
Green and Papanikolas 2018
, Thm. 7.1, Eqs. 18,26,27)
):

f_{\phi}=\frac{1\otimes y-y\otimes 1-((y_{2}-x_{1}^{q})\otimes 1)(1\otimes x-x\otimes 1)}{1\otimes x-x^{q}\otimes 1+(y_{1}-x_{1}(y_{2}-x_{1}^{q}))\otimes 1};

\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}=\frac{(x^{q}-y_{1}+x_{1}(y_{2}-x_{1}^{q}))^{q}\otimes 1-1\otimes x}{f_{\phi}}.

Let’s compare these results with Theorem
7.32
. First, we need to compute the coefficients
(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}})_{0},(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}})_{1},(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-2}})_{1}
. Starting from the definition of
\phi^{*}_{x}
and
\phi^{*}_{y}
we can explicitly compute the first
3
terms of
\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}}
using the identity
\phi^{*}_{x}\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}}=\phi^{*}_{y}
:

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{x}

\displaystyle=\tau^{-2}+x_{1}^{q^{-1}}\tau^{-1}+x

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{y}

\displaystyle=\tau^{-3}+y_{2}^{q^{-2}}\tau^{-2}+y_{1}^{q^{-1}}\tau^{-1}+y

\displaystyle\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{-1}+(y_{2}-x_{1}^{q})+(y_{1}^{q}-x_{1}^{q}y_{2}^{q}-x^{q^{2}}+x_{1}^{q^{2}+q})\tau+\dots,

hence
(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}})_{0}=y_{2}-x_{1}^{q}
and
(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-1}})_{1}=y_{1}^{q}-x_{1}^{q}y_{2}^{q}-x^{q^{2}}+x_{1}^{q^{2}+q}
.
Since
\deg(yx^{-2})=-1
, and since
\phi
is normalized, we have
\phi^{*}_{yx^{-2}}\in\tau+{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\![\tau]\!]\tau^{2}
, hence
(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-2}})_{1}=1
. By Theorem
7.32
, we have:

(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi})f_{\phi}=(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega^{(1)}_{\phi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{g}\left(\phi^{*}_{yx^{-i-1}}\right)_{1}\otimes x^{i}=-((y_{1}^{q}-x_{1}^{q}y_{2}^{q}-x^{q^{2}}+x_{1}^{q^{2}+q})\otimes 1+1\otimes x),

which agrees with Green and Papanikolas’ formula for
\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}
.

Remark 7.33
.

A posteriori, since the computations do not take into account the
A
-
module structure of
\Lambda_{\phi}
, it turns out that the formulas found by Green and Papanikolas hold without the assumption
\Lambda_{\phi}\cong A
.

7.8
Link with the Hartl–Juschka pairing for Drinfeld modules in genus
0

In the article
Hartl and Juschka 2020
, given an abelian and
A
-
finite Anderson
A
-
module
\underline{E}=(E,\phi)
, Hartl and Juschka define a perfect pairing of
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
modules between
N(\underline{E})
and
\tau M(\underline{E})
, where
M(\underline{E})
and
N(\underline{E})
are respectively the
A
-
motive and the dual
A
-
motive associated to
\underline{E}
.

In this section we prove that Hartl and Juschka’s perfect pairing coincides with the dot product defined in Lemma
7.2
when
\underline{E}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
-
Drinfeld module.

Let’s first give the definition of
A
-
motive and dual
A
-
motive in the special case of a Drinfeld module. Let’s denote by
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
the noncommutative ring
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]\otimes A
, and similarly for
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]
.

Definition 7.34
.

Let
\phi:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
be a Drinfeld module.

We define the
A
-
motive
M(\phi)
as the left
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
-
module
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
where for all
a\in A
,
h\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
,
m\in M(\phi)
we have
h\cdot m\coloneqq hm
and
a\cdot m\coloneqq m\phi_{a}
.

We define the dual
A
-
motive
N(\phi)
as the left
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]
-
module
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]
where for all
a\in A
,
h\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]
,
m\in N(\phi)
we have
h\cdot m\coloneqq hm
and
a\cdot m\coloneqq m\phi^{*}_{a}
.

In the following proposition, we consider
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
as a left
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes A[\tau^{-1}]
-
module, where
\tau^{-1}
sends
x\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
to
x^{(-1)}
, and we consider
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)
as a left
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes A[\tau]
-
module where
\tau
sends
x\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)
to
x^{(1)}
.

Proposition 7.35
.

The
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]
-
linear morphism

F:N(\phi)\cong{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau^{-1}]\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}

sending
1
to
\zeta_{\phi}
is an injective morphism of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes A[\tau^{-1}]
-
modules.

The
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
-
linear morphism

G:\tau M(\phi)\cong\tau{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)

sending
\tau
to
\omega_{\phi}^{(1)}
is an injective morphism of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes A[\tau]
-
modules.

Proof.

Let’s prove the
A
-
linearity of
F
. Let
x:=\sum_{i}c_{i}\tau^{-i}\in N(\phi)
, so that its image is
F(x)=\sum_{i}(c_{i}\otimes 1)\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}
; for all
a\in A
, since
\zeta_{\phi}
is a dual Anderson eigenvector we have:

F(x\circ\phi_{a}^{*})=(x\circ\phi_{a}^{*})(F(1))=x(\phi_{a}^{*}(\zeta_{\phi}))=x(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot(1\otimes a))=(1\otimes a)\cdot x(\zeta_{\phi})=(1\otimes a)F(x).

To prove injectivity, let’s fix
\sum c_{i}\tau^{-i}\in N(\phi)\setminus\{0\}
and prove that
\sum c_{i}\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}
is a nonzero element of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Lambda_{\phi}
. Let
N
be the smallest index such that
c_{N}\neq 0
. On one hand, for all
i>N
,

\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}=\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i-N)}\right)^{(-i)}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega

by Theorem
7.3
; on the other hand, by Proposition
7.19
,
\zeta_{\phi}^{(-N)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}
sends any
c\in{K_{\infty}}
with norm less than
1
to
c^{\frac{1}{q^{n}}}
, as a function from
{{K_{\infty}}}/{A}
to
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
. Since any form in
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{\mathrm{cont}}\left({{K_{\infty}}}/{A},{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\right)
has finite support, we deduce that
\zeta_{\phi}^{(-N)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}\not\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega
, hence

\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{i}\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}\right)\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}=c_{N}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-N)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}\right)+\sum_{i>N}c_{i}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(-N)}\right)\not\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega;

in particular,
\sum c_{i}\zeta_{\phi}^{(-i)}\neq 0
.

Let’s prove the
A
-
linearity of
G
. Let
y\in\tau M(\phi)
and fix
x:=\sum_{i}c_{i}\tau^{i}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
so that
x\tau=y
and
G(y)=x(G(\tau))=\sum_{i}(c_{i}\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}
; for all
a\in A
, since
\omega_{\phi}
is an Anderson eigenvector we have:

G(y\phi_{a})=(x\tau\phi_{a}\tau^{-1})G(\tau)=(x\tau)(\phi_{a}(\omega_{\phi}))=(1\otimes a)x(\omega_{\phi}^{(1)})=(1\otimes a)G(y).

To prove injectivity, let’s fix
\sum_{i}c_{i}\tau^{i}\in\tau M(\phi)\setminus\{0\}
and prove that
\sum c_{i}\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}
is a nonzero element of
{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Hom_{A}(\Lambda_{\phi},\Omega)
. Let
N
be the smallest index such that
c_{N}\neq 0
: as shown above, for all
i>N
,
\zeta_{\phi}^{(N)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega
, while
\zeta_{\phi}^{(N)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(N)}\not\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\otimes\Omega
, so
\sum c_{i}\omega^{(i)}\neq 0
.
∎

Let’s state a version of Hartl and Juschka’s theorem for Drinfeld modules.

Theorem 7.36

(
(
Hartl and Juschka 2020
, Thm. 5.13)
)
.

Let
\phi:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
be a Drinfeld module. There is a canonical
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
linear perfect pairing
HJ:N(\phi)\otimes_{A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}\tau M(\phi)\to\Omega_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

Hartl and Juschka leave as an open question the computation of the pairing
HJ
in the general case, but they carry it out in the case
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
. In particular, they show the following.

Proposition 7.37

(
(
Hartl and Juschka 2020
, Ex. 5.16)
)
.

Assume
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
and let
\phi:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
, with
\phi_{\theta}=\sum_{i}t_{i}\tau^{i}
. Let
\{\alpha_{i,j}\}_{0\leq i,j<r}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r\times r}
be the matrix with entries
\alpha_{i,j}:=-t_{i+j+1}^{q^{-i}}
, and let
\{\beta_{i,j}\}_{0\leq i,j<r}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r\times r}
be its inverse. Then for all
0\leq i,j<r
, the following identity holds:

HJ(\tau^{-j}\otimes\tau^{i+1})=\beta_{i,j}d\theta.

We prove the following Theorem.

Theorem 7.38
.

Assume
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
and let
\phi:A\to{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}[\tau]
be a Drinfeld module. The following identity holds in
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
for all
i,j\geq 0
:

HJ(\tau^{-j}\otimes\tau^{i+1})=\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.35
, we can identify
\tau M(\underline{E})=\Span_{{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}\{\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\}_{i\geq 0}
and
N(\underline{E})=\Span_{{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}}\{\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\}_{j\geq 0}
, hence we need to prove that the dot product coincides with the Hartl–Juschka pairing.

If we call
r
the rank of
\phi
, the set
\{\tau^{i+1}\}_{0\leq i<r}
generates
\tau M(\underline{E})
as an
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
module, and the set
\{\tau^{(-j)}\}_{0\leq j<r}
generates
N(\underline{E})
as an
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
module; since both the Hartl–Juschka pairing and the dot product are
A_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
-
linear, it suffices to prove the statement for all
0\leq i,j<r
.

By Proposition
7.37
, we need the following identity to hold in
\Omega_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\hat{\otimes}\Omega
for all
0\leq i,j<r
:

\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(t_{k+i+1}\otimes 1)^{q^{-k}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\right)=-\delta_{k,j}d\theta.

If
k>j
, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(t_{k+i+1}\otimes 1)^{q^{-k}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=r-k}^{r-1}(t_{k+i+1}\otimes 1)^{q^{-k}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r-1-k}(t_{k+i+1}\otimes 1)^{q^{-k}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\right)=0,

where the first sum is
0
because
t_{l}=0
if
l>r
, and the second sum is
0
because, by Proposition
7.20
,

\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}=\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+j+1)}\right)^{(-j)}=0\text{ if }0<i+j+1<r,

which is true because
i,j\geq 0
and
i+1\leq r-k<r-j
.

Since
\omega_{\phi}
is an Anderson eigenvector, the identity
\sum_{l}(t_{l}\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}^{(l)}=\omega_{\phi}(1\otimes\theta)
holds, hence if
k\leq j
we have:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(t_{k+i+1}\otimes 1)^{q^{-k}}\left(\zeta_{\phi}^{(-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+1)}\right)\right)^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-j)}\cdot\sum_{i=k+1}^{r+k}(t_{i}\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}=\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-j)}\cdot\sum_{i=k+1}^{r}(t_{i}\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes\theta-\theta\otimes 1)\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-j)}\cdot\omega_{\phi}-\zeta_{\phi}^{(k-j)}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_{i}\otimes 1)\omega_{\phi}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes\theta-\theta\otimes 1)\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(j-k)}\right)^{(k-j)}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t_{i}\otimes 1)\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(i+j-k)}\right)^{(k-j)}.

By Proposition
7.20
, since
0<i+j-k\leq j<r
, the sum on the right hand side is
0
, while
(1\otimes\theta-\theta\otimes 1)\left(\zeta_{\phi}\cdot\omega_{\phi}^{(j-k)}\right)^{(k-j)}
is
0
if
k<j
and
-d\theta
if
k=j
.
∎

Crossing estimates from metric graph and discrete GFF
Jian Ding, Mateo Wirth, Hao Wu

Abstract
We compare level-set percolation for Gaussian free fields (GFFs) defined on a rectangular subset of δ ​ ℤ 2 \delta\mathbb{Z}^{2} to level-set percolation for GFFs defined on the corresponding metric graph as the mesh size δ \delta goes to 0. In particular, we look at the probability that there is a path that crosses the rectangle in the horizontal direction on which the field is positive. We show this probability is strictly larger in the discrete graph. In the metric graph case, we show that for appropriate boundary conditions the probability that there exists a closed pivotal edge for the horizontal crossing event decays logarithmically in δ \delta . In the discrete graph case, we compute the limit of the probability of a horizontal crossing for appropriate boundary conditions. Keywords. Gaussian free field, crossing event, scaling limit, pivotal edge.

Introduction

In this note we will prove several results on level-set percolation of Gaussian free fields on the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The thrust of our results is that the probability that there exists a crossing of the domain in which the field is defined differs depending on whether we consider a discrete Gaussian free field or a metric graph Gaussian free field. Additionally, in the metric graph case we prove a bound on the probability that there exists a closed pivotal edge for the crossing. Discussions on future directions

•

The level loops of zero-boundary metric graph GFF will converge to the so-called conformal loop ensemble CLE with
\kappa=4
. From there, we believe that the optimal estimate for the crossing probability in (
1.4
) is
|\log(\delta)|^{-2}
. However, our method only provides an upper bound
|\log(\delta)|^{-1/2}
. To get the optimal estimate requires more delicate tools.

•

The level lines of zero-boundary discrete GFF will converge to the so-called branching
\text{SLE}_{4}(-1;-1)
process. Then the limit of the crossing probability in (
1.5
) should be the probability of certain crossing event of
\text{SLE}_{4}(-1;-1)
. It is an interesting question to derive an explicit formula for such probability. The limit of crossing probabilities in (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) for general
\lambda
are also unknown.

•

We provide an estimate on the existence of pivotal edge in metric graph GFF. It is more important to understand the pivotal vertices in discrete GFF and its connection to continuum GFF.

2.1
Discrete GFF and metric graph GFF

We begin with an alternate construction of the metric graph GFF which more clearly shows it is a natural analog to the discrete GFF. Namely, one can construct
\tilde{\phi}
by first defining a Brownian motion
\{\tilde{B}_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
on
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
as in
[
Lu16
, Section 2]
:
\tilde{B}
behaves like a standard Brownian motion in the interior of the edges, while on the vertices it chooses to do excursions on each incoming edge uniformly at random. For a subset
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we let
\tilde{\zeta}=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{B}_{t}\in\partial V\}
, and
\{G(u,v)\,:\,u,v\in\tilde{V}\}
be the density of the 0-potential of
\{\tilde{B}_{t}\,:\,0\leq t<\tilde{\zeta}\}
(with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\tilde{V}
), where
u
and
v
are now arbitrary points in
\tilde{V}
(not necessarily vertices). It is shown in
[
Lu16
]
that the trace of
\tilde{B}
on
V
(when parametrized by its local time at the vertices) is exactly the continuous-time simple random walk on
V
, and therefore the definition of
G
here coincides with the one in (
1.2
) for
u,v\in V
, justifying the abuse of notation. Due to this relation between the metric graph Brownian motion and the simple random walk, we will, by a slight abuse of notation, let
\mathbb{P}_{v}
be the law of
\tilde{B}_{t}
where
\tilde{B}_{0}=v
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{v}
be the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{v}
. It was also shown in
[
Lu16
]
that the value of
G
on
\tilde{V}\setminus V
can be obtained by interpolation from the value on
V
. For two pairs of adjacent vertices
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
in
V
, and two points
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
on the corresponding edges, taking the convention that either the edges are distinct or
(u_{1},v_{1})=(u_{2},v_{2})
and letting

and
r_{2}=|w_{2}-u_{2}|

[
Lu16
, Equation (2.1)]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G(w_{1},w_{2})=&(1-r_{1})(1-r_{2})G(u_{1},u_{2})+r_{1}r_{2}G(v_{1},v_{2})+(1-r_{1})r_{2}G(u_{1},v_{2})\\
&+r_{1}(1-r_{2})G(v_{1},u_{2})+4(r_{1}\wedge r_{2}-r_{1}r_{2})\mathbf{\mathbbm{1}}_{(u_{1},v_{1})=(u_{2},v_{2})}.\end{split}

(2.1)

We call a process
\{\tilde{\phi}(v)\,:\,v\in\tilde{V}\}
a Gaussian free field on
\tilde{V}
if it is a continuous Gaussian process such that there exists a function
f:\partial V\to\mathbb{R}
for which

{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}(v)]=\tilde{{\mathbb{E}}}_{v}[f(\tilde{B}_{\tilde{\zeta}})],\quad\operatorname{Cov}[\tilde{\phi}(u)\tilde{\phi}(v)]=G(u,v).

We note that this definition extends to any compact, connected subset
K\subset\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

We remark on a consequence of the construction above. For any finite subset
A\subset\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, we can define an electric network with vertex set
W=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cup A
, where any two vertices

are connected with an edge of conductance

if there is a continuous path in
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
from

to

which does not contain any other points in

(note this path is always contained in

is a connected (proper) subset and

is a metric graph GFF on

,

is a discrete GFF on (the electric network with vertex set)

.

Next, we state the FKG inequality for the GFF, which will be used repeatedly throughout the paper. Let
\tilde{\phi}
be a metric graph GFF and

be the

\tilde{\phi}
. An event

is increasing if

is increasing as a function of

. For two increasing events

[

\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)\geq\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(B).

(2.2)

By symmetry, the same holds for decreasing events.

[b_{\delta},c_{\delta})
(the bottom part of the first passage set), and by
{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}_{\delta,0}^{t}
the union of all connected components of
{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}_{\delta,0}
intersecting
[d_{\delta},a_{\delta})
(the top part of the first passage set). The metric graph first passage sets

,

,

2.3
Exploration martingales

In this section we introduce a family of martingales which form the basis of the proofs of our results. We begin with some basic definitions and some fundamental results. For a subset
W\subset\tilde{V}_{\delta}
, we let
\mathcal{F}_{W}
be the sigma algebra generated by
\{\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(w)\,:\,w\in W\}
.

Definition 2.1

(Optional set)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a random compact subset of
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
that almost surely has finitely many connected components. We say
\mathcal{K}
is an optional set for
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
if for every deterministic, open subset
W
of
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
,
\{\mathcal{K}\subset W\}\in\mathcal{F}_{W}
. Similarly, we say
\mathcal{K}
is optional for
\phi_{\delta}
if the following holds. For any deterministic open subset
W
of
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
,
\{\mathcal{K}\subset W\}\in\mathcal{F}_{W\cap V_{\delta}}
.

We note that in the definition above, we use the subspace topology on
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
considered as a subspace of
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

For an optional set
\mathcal{K}
, we define its
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}
by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}=\left\{A\in\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{V}_{\delta}}\,:\,A\cap\{\mathcal{K}\subset W\}\in\mathcal{F}_{W},\text{ for all deterministic, open $W\subset\tilde{V}_{\delta}$}\right\}.

Sometimes, it will be useful to consider the field
\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\phi})=\{\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\phi}(v))\,:\,v\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}\}
, where
\operatorname{sign}
is the function

\operatorname{sign}(x)=\begin{cases}-1,&x<0,\\
0,&x=0,\\
1,&x>0.\end{cases}

For a subset
W\subset\tilde{V}_{\delta}
, we let
\mathcal{G}_{W}
be the
\sigma
-field generated by
\{\operatorname{sign}(\tilde{\phi}(w))\,:\,w\in W\}
. We extend this notation to optional sets in the obvious way.

The strong Markov property of the metric graph GFF, stated below, will allow us to perform detailed analysis of the exploration martingales and is thus fundamental to our proofs.

Theorem 2.2

(Strong Markov property,
[
Lu16
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be optional for
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
. Given
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}
, the process
\{\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\,:\,v\in\overline{\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\}
is a metric graph GFF on
\overline{\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{K}}
with boundary condition given by the restriction of
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
to
\partial\mathcal{K}\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}
.

We introduce some notation for harmonic extension. Assume the same setup as in Theorem
2.2
, and let
\tilde{B}
be a Brownian motion on
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
. Set
\tilde{\zeta}=\inf\{t\geq 0:\tilde{B}_{t}\in\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}\}
as in Section
2.1
and
\tau=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{B}_{t}\in\mathcal{K}\}
be the hitting time of
\mathcal{K}
. We define, for
v\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}
and
w\in\mathcal{K}\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}
,

\operatorname{Hm}(v,w;\mathcal{K})=\mathbb{P}_{v}\left(\tilde{B}_{\tau\wedge\tilde{\zeta}}=w\right).

Note that the harmonic measure
\operatorname{Hm}(v,\cdot;\mathcal{K})
is always supported on a finite set of points since
|\partial\mathcal{K}|<\infty
for compact
\mathcal{K}
with finitely many connected components.
With this notation, in Theorem
2.2
, we have in fact, for
u,v\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}}\operatorname{Hm}(v,w;\mathcal{K})\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(w),

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(u)\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=G(u,v)-\sum_{w\in\mathcal{K}}\operatorname{Hm}(u,w;\mathcal{K})G(w,v).

Note also that Theorem
2.2
implies the strong Markov property of the discrete GFF since any random subset
\mathcal{K}\subset V_{\delta}
which is optional for
\phi_{\delta}
is optional for
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
.

Moreover, we define

\operatorname{Hm}(v,\mathcal{K})=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{K}}\operatorname{Hm}(v,w;\mathcal{K})={\mathbb{P}}_{v}(\tau\leq\tilde{\zeta});

for
U\subset\tilde{V}_{\delta}
a finite subset, we define

\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{K})=\sum_{u\in U}\operatorname{Hm}(u,\mathcal{K}).

We can now introduce the exploration martingales. For a finite subset
U\subset V_{\delta}
, we define the “observable”
X_{U}
by

X_{U}=\sum_{v\in U}\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v).

For
\mathcal{I}_{0}
a deterministic, compact subset of
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
(with finitely many connected components), we will let
M
be the Doob martingale for
X_{U}
as we explore
E^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
, or
\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
from
\mathcal{I}_{0}
. We will specify whether the exploration happens on the metric graph or discrete graph whenever we use an exploration martingale.

To make this precise, we specify what we mean by exploring the excursion set from
\mathcal{I}_{0}
. We begin with the exploration on
\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
as it is simpler to explain. Let
\tilde{D}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
be the metric graph distance on
\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{t}
be the ball of radius
t
around
\mathcal{I}_{0}
with respect to
\tilde{D}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
. We use the convention that for
u,v\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}
with
u\notin\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
and
u\neq v
,
\tilde{D}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}(u,v)=\infty
and
\tilde{D}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}(u,u)=0
. It is easy to show that
{\mathcal{I}}_{t}
is an optional set for each
t\geq 0

[
DW19
]
.

The exploration corresponding to
E^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
is similar in spirit but slightly more cumbersome to describe. In this case we take
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset V_{\delta}
and
\mathcal{I}_{0}
the metric graph on
\mathcal{V}_{0}
. We begin the exploration by setting
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathcal{V}_{0}\cap E^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\mathcal{V}_{0}\cap E^{<0}_{\delta}
. For
k\geq 1
an integer, we let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k}

\displaystyle=\{v\in(V_{\delta}^{o}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{k-1})\cap E^{\geq 0}_{\delta}\,:\,\exists u\in\mathcal{A}_{k-1},\,u\sim v\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{k}

\displaystyle=\{v\in(V_{\delta}^{o}\setminus\mathcal{V}_{k-1})\cap E^{<0}_{\delta}\,:\,\exists u\in\mathcal{A}_{k-1},\,u\sim v\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{k-1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{B}_{k},

and
\mathcal{I}_{k}
be the metric graph on
\mathcal{V}_{k}
(as with
M
, whether
\mathcal{I}_{k}
corresponds to the metric graph or discrete graph construction will be clear from context). In words, at each time step we explore all unexplored vertices that are adjacent to an explored vertex on which the field is non-negative. Note that
\mathcal{I}_{k}
is an optional set, and in fact
\mathcal{I}_{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{V}_{k-1}}
. To extend this definition to real
t
, we proceed by interpolation. That is, if we let
\mathcal{E}_{k}=\{(u,v)\,:\,u\in\mathcal{I}_{k-1},\,v\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{B}_{k},\,u\sim v\}
be the edges between
\mathcal{I}_{k-1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{k-1}
then for
k-1<t<k
we let

\mathcal{I}_{t}=\mathcal{I}_{k-1}\cup\left(\bigcup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}[u,(k-t)u+(t-k-1)v]\right).

Note that as before
\mathcal{I}_{t}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{V}_{k-1}}
and so
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is an optional set for any
t
.

Explorations on
E^{\leq 0}_{\delta}
and
\tilde{E}^{\leq 0}_{\delta}
are defined in the same way. As alluded to above, we take

M_{t}={\mathbb{E}}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}}].

It is straightforward to check that
M
is a continuous martingale
[
DW19
]
. We now turn to the quadratic variation of the exploration martingale.

It is straightforward to check that for a Doob martingale, the quadratic variation is equal to the decrease in the conditional variance
[
DW19
]
. That is,

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{0}}]-\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}}].

We denote by
G_{t}
the Green’s function on
\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{t}
, and write

\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(u,v)=\operatorname{Hm}(u,v;\mathcal{I}_{t}).

(2.3)

We get from Theorem
2.2
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in U}G_{t}(u,u^{\prime})=\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in U}\left[G(u,u^{\prime})-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(u,v)G(v,u^{\prime})\right].

(2.4)

This gives

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in U}\left[\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(u,v)G(v,u^{\prime})-\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(u,v^{\prime})G(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})\right],

\displaystyle=\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in U}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(u,v)G_{0}(v,u^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(U,v)G_{0}(v,U),

(2.5)

where
G_{0}(v,U)=\sum_{u\in U}G_{0}(v,u)
.

2.4
Brownian motion tools

In this section, we recall two facts about continuous martingales and Brownian motion that will be useful throughout the paper. The first is
[
RY99
, Theorem 1.7 in Chapter V]
, stated below, which is a version of the Dubins-Schwarz theorem for martingales of bounded quadratic variation.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let be
M
a continuous martingale,
T_{t}=\inf\{s\,:\,\langle M\rangle_{s}>t\}
, and
W
be the following process

W_{t}=\begin{cases}M_{T_{t}}-M_{0}&t<\langle M\rangle_{\infty}\,,\\
M_{\infty}-M_{0}&t\geq\langle M\rangle_{\infty}.\end{cases}

Then
W
is a Brownian motion stopped at
\langle M\rangle_{\infty}
.

When applying this theorem, we will generally denote by
B
a Brownian motion which satisfies
B_{t}=M_{T_{t}}-M_{0}
for
t<\langle M\rangle_{\infty}
but is not stopped at
\langle M\rangle_{\infty}
, so that
W_{t}=B_{t\wedge\langle M\rangle_{\infty}}
.
Suppose
M
is the exploration martingale in Section
2.3
. By Theorem
2.3
, the process
\{M_{T_{t}}-M_{0}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{0}}
, so we will generally take
B
to be independent of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{0}}
as well.

The second result gives the distribution of the hitting time of a line by Brownian motion.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
B
be a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion,
\Phi
be the distribution function of the standard normal distribution, and
\bar{\Phi}(x)=1-\Phi(x)
. For
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
b>0
, let
\tau=\inf\{t\geq 0\,:\,B_{t}\leq mt-b\}
. Then for
T>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}(\tau\leq T)=\bar{\Phi}\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{T}}-m\sqrt{T}\right)+e^{2bm}\bar{\Phi}\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{T}}+m\sqrt{T}\right).

Proof.

The density of
\tau
is given in
[
BS02
, Equation (2.0.2) in Part II]
. Taking an integral then gives the desired result.
∎

We note two facts that follow directly from Proposition
2.4
and will be used repeatedly throughout the paper. First, letting
m=0
we obtain that
\sup\{B_{s}\,:\,0\leq s\leq t\}
and
-\inf\{B_{s}\,:\,0\leq s\leq t\}
have the same distribution as
|B_{t}|
. Second, for
m<0
and
b>0
we have by letting
T\to\infty
that
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau<\infty)=e^{2bm}<1
.

2.5
Random walk estimates

We conclude this section with some results about Green’s functions and harmonic measures which will be used later. The first result follows from
[
LL10
, Theorem 4.4.4, Proposition 4.6.2]
. It will be useful in comparing the Green’s function in different domains. Below and throughout the paper, a subset
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is simply connected if for any loop in
V
, all the vertices in the interior of the loop (i.e. separated from infinity by the loop) are contained in
V
.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that the following holds. For
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
simply connected, let
G
be the Green’s function on
V
. For
v\in V^{o}
, let
\Delta=\operatorname{dist}(v,\partial V)
be the Euclidean distance between
v
and
\partial V
. Then

\Big|G(v,v)-\frac{\pi}{2}\log\left(\Delta+1\right)\Big|\leq C.

Next, we provide two estimates on harmonic measures for a random walk started near the boundary of a box. The first provides an upper bound on the probability that a random walk started near the left side of a box doesn’t exit the box through the left side. Recall that
S
is a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
\mathbb{P}_{v}
is the law of
S
started at
v
, and that we take
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\mathbb{C}
in the obvious way.

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds. For
N,M\geq 2
,
R=[0,N]\times[-M,M]
,
V=R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
\zeta=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in\partial V\}
. We have

\mathbb{P}_{1}(\Re(S_{\zeta})\neq 0)\leq\frac{c}{N\wedge M}.

Proof.

Let
\zeta^{\prime}=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,\Re(S_{n})=0\}
and
K=N\wedge M
. We have by
[
LL10
, Theorem 8.1.2]
that

\mathbb{P}_{1}(|\Im(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})|>K)\leq\frac{c}{K}.

Let
D=\partial V\setminus\{z\,:\,\Re(z)=0\}
and note that there exists a universal constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{v}(|\Im(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})|>K)\geq c^{\prime},\quad\forall v\in D.

Finally, the conclusion follows by noting

\mathbb{P}_{1}(|\Im(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})|>K)=\sum_{v\in D}\mathbb{P}_{1}(S_{\zeta}=v)\mathbb{P}_{v}(|\Im(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})|>K)\geq c^{\prime}\mathbb{P}_{1}(\Re(S_{\zeta})\neq 0).

∎

The second result provides a lower bound on the probability that a random walk started near the left side of a box will exit the box through the right side.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
a>0
there exists a constant
c_{a}>0
such that the following holds. For
N,M\geq 2
with
M\geq aN
,
R=[0,N]\times[-M,M]
,
V=R\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
\zeta=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in\partial V\}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{1}(\Re(S_{\zeta})=N)\geq\frac{c_{a}}{N}.

Proof.

Let
M^{\prime}=M/2
,
V^{\prime}=(-\infty,N]\times[-M^{\prime},M^{\prime}]\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
\zeta^{\prime}=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in\partial V^{\prime}\}
. For an integer
k
, let
v_{k}=1+ik
. By the invariance principle, there exists a constant
c_{a}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{v_{k}}(\Re(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})=N)\geq c_{a},\quad|k|\leq M^{\prime}/2.

Let
D=\{v_{k}\,:\,|k|\leq M^{\prime}-1\}
,
\tau=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in D\}\wedge\zeta^{\prime}
, and
G
be the Green’s function on
V^{\prime}
. By a last exit decomposition, we have for
v\in D

\mathbb{P}_{v}(\Re(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})=N)=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in D}G(v,v^{\prime})\mathbb{P}_{v^{\prime}}(\Re(S_{\tau})=N).

By
[
DL18
, Lemma 1]
,
\max\{G(v,D)\,:\,v\in D\}\leq c_{a}^{\prime}N
. Therefore, combining the last two displays and summing over
v\in D
gives

c_{a}N\leq\sum_{v\in D}\mathbb{P}_{v}(\Re(S_{\zeta^{\prime}})=N)\leq c_{a}^{\prime}N\sum_{v\in D}\mathbb{P}_{v}(\Re(S_{\tau})=N).

Finally, we note that for any
v\in D
,
\mathbb{P}_{v}(\Re(S_{\tau})=N)\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}(\Re(S_{\zeta})=N)
, so the conclusion follows.
∎

3
Estimates of crossing probabilities in the metric graph

3.1
The zero-boundary case

In this section we prove (
1.4
). The proof consists of analyzing the exploration martingale
M
, introduced in Section
2.3
, corresponding to an exploration on
\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
with
\mathcal{I}_{0}=[c^{\prime}_{\delta},d^{\prime}_{\delta})
and
U=\{u\in\partial V_{\delta}^{o}\,:\,\Re(u)\leq L/4\}
.
Recall that

M_{t}={\mathbb{E}}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{t}}]=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(U,v)\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v).

We let
\mathcal{I}_{0}^{-}=\{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}\,:\,\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)<0\}
. Since
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
is non-negative on
\mathcal{I}_{t}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{0}^{-}
, we have

M_{t}\geq\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}^{-}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(U,v)\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v).

Noting that
\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(U,v)
is decreasing in
t
for
v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}^{-}
(since
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is increasing), we conclude

M_{t}-M_{0}\geq-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U,v)(\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\vee 0).

(3.1)

Next, we turn to bounding the quadratic variation. In particular, we claim that there exists
c=c(L)>0
such that

\left\{[a_{\delta}^{\prime},b_{\delta}^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}\geq 0}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c^{\prime}_{\delta},d^{\prime}_{\delta})\right\}\subseteq\left\{\langle M\rangle_{\infty}\geq c|\log(\delta)|\right\}.

(3.2)

Before proving (
3.2
), we show how it can be used to conclude the proof of (
1.4
). Suppose
(B_{s})_{s\geq 0}
is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion.
Combining Theorem
2.3
with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left([a_{\delta}^{\prime},b_{\delta}^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}\geq 0}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c^{\prime}_{\delta},d^{\prime}_{\delta})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq s\leq c|\log(\delta)|}B_{s}\geq-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U,v)(\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\vee 0)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{c|\log(\delta)|}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U,v){\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\vee 0]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{\sqrt{|\log(\delta)|}}\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{0}),

where in the second inequality we used the fact that, for
t
and
a>0
, we have by Proposition
2.4
that
\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}B_{s}\geq-a\right)\leq a/\sqrt{t}
; and in the third inequality we used the fact that
G(v,v)\leq 4
for
v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
(since
v
is adjacent to
\partial V_{\delta}
). It remains to bound
\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{0})
. Since
U,\mathcal{I}_{0}\subset V_{\delta}
, we can consider a discrete time simple random walk
S
(on
\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
), killed on
\partial V_{\delta}
(instead of the metric graph Brownian motion
\tilde{B}
). For a vertex
u\in U
, we have
\operatorname{Hm}(u,\mathcal{I}_{0})\leq c\delta
by Lemma
2.6
. Since
|U|\leq c/\delta
, we conclude

\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{0})\leq c.

Thus,the proof will be complete once we prove (
3.2
).

Proof of (
3.2
).

We begin by noting that on
\left\{[a^{\prime}_{\delta},b^{\prime}_{\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}\geq 0}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c^{\prime}_{\delta},d^{\prime}_{\delta})\right\}
there exists a vertex
u^{*}\in[a^{\prime}_{\delta},b^{\prime}_{\delta})
such that the set
\mathcal{I}_{\infty}
contains a nearest neighbor path
\gamma\subset V_{\delta}^{o}
connecting
u^{*}
to the complement of the box of radius
L/4
around
u^{*}
. We will show that for any such
u^{*}
and any such
\gamma
, we have

\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{0}}]-\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma\cup\mathcal{I}_{0}}]\geq c|\log(\delta)|.

Recall that
G_{0}
denotes the Green’s function on
\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{0}
and that

\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{I}_{0}}]-\operatorname{Var}[X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma\cup\mathcal{I}_{0}}]=\sum_{v\in\gamma}\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U,v;\gamma)G_{0}(v,U).

Let
S
be a discrete-time simple random walk (on
\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) killed on
\partial V_{\delta}\cup\mathcal{I}_{0}
and
T_{U}
be the hitting time of
U
by
S
. It is easy to see that

G_{0}(v,U)\geq\mathbb{P}_{v}(T_{U}<\infty)\geq c,\quad\forall v\in\gamma,

for some universal constant
c>0
. Therefore, it remains to show

\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U,\gamma)\geq c|\log(\delta)|.

(3.3)

To this end, we partition
U
and
\gamma
as follows. For
n\geq 0
an integer, we let
Q_{n}
be the box of radius
r_{n}=2^{-n-2}L
centered at
u^{*}
,
A_{n}=Q_{n}\setminus Q_{n+1}
,
U_{n}=U\cap A_{n}
, and
\gamma_{n}=\gamma\cap A_{n}
. We note that
N=\max\{n\geq 1\,:\,r_{n}\geq 1000\delta\}
satisfies
N\geq c|\log(\delta)|
. Finally, we claim there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U_{n},\gamma_{n};\gamma)\geq c,\quad 0\leq n\leq N\,.

For the proof, let
u_{1}\in U_{n}
be such that
2r_{n}/3-\delta<|u_{1}-u^{*}|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 2r_{n}/3
,
u_{2}\in U_{n}
be such that
5r_{n}/6\leq|u_{2}-u^{*}|_{\ell_{\infty}}<5r_{n}/6+\delta
, and
Q_{n,2}
be a box of radius
r_{n}/12
centered at
u_{2}
. With these choices, the distances between
u_{1}
and
Q_{n,2}
, and between
\{u_{1}\}\cup Q_{n,2}
and
A_{n}^{c}
are of the same order as
r_{n}
. Therefore, if we let
S
be a simple random walk and
\mathfrak{E}
be the event that
S
hits
Q_{n,2}\cap U
and then completes a loop around
u^{*}
before exiting
A_{n}
, we have
\mathbb{P}_{u_{1}}(\mathfrak{E})\geq c
. Note that such a walk necessarily contains a path from
U_{n}
to
\gamma_{n}
which does not hit
\partial V_{\delta}
. Therefore, letting
T
be the hitting time of
U_{n}\cup\gamma_{n}
by
S
, and
\zeta
be the hitting time of
A_{n}^{c}
, we have by a last exit decomposition

\mathbb{P}_{u_{1}}(\mathfrak{E})\leq\sum_{u\in U}G^{*}_{n}(u_{1},u)\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{T\wedge\zeta}\in\gamma_{n}),

where
G^{*}_{n}(u_{1},u)
is the expected number of visits a random walk started at
u_{1}
makes to
u
after hitting
Q_{n,2}\cap U_{n}
, and before exiting
A_{n}
. It is immediate that
\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{T\wedge\zeta}\in\gamma_{n})\leq\operatorname{Hm}(u,\gamma_{n};\gamma)
, and we have that

G_{n}^{*}(u_{1},u)\leq G_{n}(u_{1},u)\wedge\max\{G_{n}(u^{\prime},u)\,:\,u^{\prime}\in Q_{n,2}\cap U_{n}\},

where
G_{n}
is the Green’s function on
A_{n}\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. It follows from
[
LL10
, Theorem 4.4.4, Proposition 4.6.2]
that
G^{*}_{n}
is uniformly bounded. That is, there exists a universal constant
c
such that

G_{n}^{*}(u_{1},u)\leq c,\quad u\in U_{n}.

Finally, this implies that

\operatorname{Hm}_{0}(U_{n},\gamma_{n};\gamma)\geq\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{T\wedge\zeta}\in\gamma_{n})\geq c\mathbb{P}_{u_{1}}(\mathfrak{E})\geq c.

This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
The alternating boundary case

In this section we prove the lower bound in (
1.6
). The proof consists of two main claims, both of which are proved using an exploration martingale.
Recall that
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}
(resp.
\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}

above zero (resp. below zero). First, letting

(3.4)

Next, we claim that conditional on this event, the probability that there is a positive crossing is bounded uniformly away from zero. That is, letting
\mathbb{P}^{+}
denote the law of

given

(3.5)

Assuming (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain the lower bound in (
1.6
):

\mathbb{P}\left([a_{\delta},b_{\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left([a_{\delta},b_{\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c_{\delta},d_{\delta}),\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}\cap\Pi=\emptyset\right)\geq c_{1}c_{2}.

Thus it remains to show (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

Proof of (

).

For this proof, we consider an exploration martingale

from

Note that we have

Note that by Lemma

We claim that for there exists a constant

\operatorname{Var}(X_{U})-\operatorname{Var}(X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})\geq c.

(3.6)

Assuming this bound for now, we have

\langle M\rangle_{T}\geq\min_{\gamma}\{\operatorname{Var}(X_{U})-\operatorname{Var}(X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})\}\geq c,

almost surely on

\sigma

2.3

2.4

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}\cap\Pi\neq\emptyset)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq c}B_{t}\leq c^{\prime}\lambda\right)\leq 1-c^{\prime\prime}.

∎

Let
\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}
be the connected component of

Finally, we let

from

with obsevable

\pi_{\epsilon,t}=\operatorname{Hm}^{+}_{t}(U_{\epsilon},[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})),\quad\mu_{\epsilon,t}=\operatorname{Hm}^{+}_{t}(U_{\epsilon},\mathcal{I}_{t}),

M_{\epsilon,t}-M_{\epsilon,0}\geq-\lambda(\mu_{\epsilon,0}+\pi_{\epsilon,0}-\pi_{\epsilon,t}),

with equality if and only if

(i.e. the exploration has stopped by time

). To conclude the proof, we need to lower bound

. Indeed, we have by Lemma

\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}\cap\Pi=\emptyset

\displaystyle\pi_{\epsilon,0}-\pi_{\epsilon,t}

4
Estimates of crossing probabilities in the discrete graph

The goal of this section is to prove (
1.5
) and (
1.8
). Both proofs begin with the observation that in the discrete graph, when we explore the excursion set
E_{\delta}^{>0}
starting from
\mathcal{I}_{0}
, the set
\partial\mathcal{I}_{\infty}\setminus\partial V_{\delta}
is contained in
E^{<0}_{\delta}
. We show that in fact, suitably defined averages of the field on this set are bounded away from 0 with high probability. Following the literature on the subject, we call this phenomenon entropic repulsion. It was previously studied in a similar context in
[
DL18
]
.

4.1
Entropic repulsion for explorations on the discrete graph

In this section we formalize the idea, stated above, that when there is no horizontal crossing the values of the field on the outer boundary of the explored set are strictly negative. We begin with some setup. For this section, we let
\tilde{K}\subset\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
be compact and simply connected and
V=\tilde{K}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For
U,I\subset V
, we let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be

D=\{v\in I\,:\,\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)>0\},\quad D^{\prime}=\{v\in I\,:\,\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I\setminus D)>0\},

where
\operatorname{Hm}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
is the harmonic measure on
W\cup\partial\tilde{K}
. That is,
D
is the subset of
\partial I
that can be reached from
U
, and
D^{\prime}
is the subset of
\partial(I\setminus D)
that can be hit from
U
(equivalently from
D
). As the notation suggests,
U
corresponds to the “observable” in the definition of the exploration martingale, while
I
corresponds to the explored set. We will assume the pair
(U,I)
satisfies some geometric conditions. First, that for any
v\in D
, all edges incident on
v
are contained in
\tilde{K}
. That is,
\operatorname{dist}(v,\partial\tilde{\mathcal{K}})\geq 1
, where
\operatorname{dist}
denotes the Euclidean distance. Second, that for any
v\in D
, there exists
w\in I\setminus D
such that
v\sim w
. We let
\xi
be given by

\xi(U,I)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)}\sup\{\operatorname{Hm}(U,w;I\setminus D)\,:\,w\in D^{\prime}\}.

The reason the supremum is over
w\in D^{\prime}
is the following. For any
v\in D
there exists
w\in D^{\prime}
such that
v\sim w
which gives

\operatorname{Hm}(U,w;I\setminus D)\geq\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I).

Therefore,
\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\leq 4\xi(U,I)\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)
for any
v\in D
. Finally, we let
\tilde{\phi}
be a metric graph GFF on
\tilde{K}
with boundary condition
f
satisfying
|f(v)|\leq\lambda
for all
v\in\partial\tilde{K}
. As usual, we let
\phi
be the restriction of
\tilde{\phi}
to
V
. Note that
\phi
is a discrete GFF on
V\cup\partial\tilde{K}
with boundary condition
f
. The goal is to show that when
\xi(U,I)
is small and we condition
\phi
to be negative on
D
, the typical value of the field on
D
(as seen from
U
) is bounded away from zero with high probability.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\tilde{K}
,
U
, and
I
satisfy the conditions above. Additionally, let
I^{+}
and
I^{-}
be a partition of
I
such that
D\subseteq I^{-}
and for every
v\in D
, there exists
w\in I^{+}
such that
v\sim w
. Let
\mathfrak{E}
be the following event

\mathfrak{E}=\{\tilde{\phi}(v)<0\,:\,v\in I^{-}\}\cap\{\tilde{\phi}(v)\geq 0\,:\,v\in I^{+}\}.

Let
Y
be the following random variable

Y=\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\phi(v).

There exists a universal function
r
satisfying
r(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
and a constant
\Delta=\Delta(\lambda)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(Y\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E})\geq 1-r(\xi(U,I)).

The proof follows along the same lines as
[
DL18
, Lemma 6]
, and consists of bounding the conditional mean and variance of
Y
given
\mathfrak{E}
and the field on
I^{+}
. We isolate three lemmas that will be used repeatedly throughout the rest of the paper.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{B_{1,v}\}_{v\in V}
and
\{B_{2,v}\}_{v\in V}
be a sequence of non-empty intervals satisfying

\displaystyle\inf B_{1,v}\leq\inf B_{2,v},\quad\sup B_{1,v}\leq\sup B_{2,v},\quad\forall v\in V.

Let
\tilde{\phi}_{1}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{2}
be metric graph GFFs on
\tilde{K}
with boundary conditions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
such that
f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v)
for
v\in\partial\tilde{K}
, and let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be the corresponding discrete graph GFFs. We assume that
B_{j,v}=\{f_{j}(v)\}
for
v\in\partial\tilde{K}\cap V
. Finally, for
j=1,2
let
\mathfrak{E}_{j}
be the event

\mathfrak{E}_{j}=\{\phi_{j}(v)\in B_{j,v},v\in V\}.

Then the law of
\phi_{1}
given
\mathfrak{E}_{1}
is stochastically smaller than the law of
\phi_{2}
given
\mathfrak{E}_{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of
[
DL18
, Equation (49)]
; we reproduce it here for completeness. Let
\nu_{1}
be the law of
\phi_{1}
given
\mathfrak{E}_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
be the law of
\phi_{2}
given
\mathfrak{E}_{2}
. Further, let
\tilde{K}^{\prime}
be the closure of an open neighborhood of
\tilde{K}
such that
\tilde{K}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}=V
. Let
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}
be a metric graph GFF on
\tilde{K}^{\prime}
with zero boundary condition, say, and
\phi^{\prime}
be the corresponding discrete GFF on the graph with vertex set
V^{\prime}=V\cup\partial\tilde{K}
. We note that
\mu
, the law of
\phi^{\prime}
, has density
\mu(dr)=\exp(-H(r))dr
(where
r
is a
|V^{\prime}|
-dimensional vector) such that for every
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V^{\prime}|}

H(r\wedge r^{\prime})+H(r\vee r^{\prime})\leq H(r)+H(r^{\prime}),

where
\wedge
and
\vee
are taken coordinate by coordinate. Additionally, we note
\nu_{j}
is simply the law of the restriction of
\phi^{\prime}
to
V
, conditioned on the event

\mathfrak{E}_{j}^{\prime}=\{\phi^{\prime}(v)=f_{j}(v),v\in\partial\tilde{K}\}\cap\{\phi^{\prime}(v)\in B_{j,v},v\in V\}.

We let
\mu_{j}
be the law of
\phi^{\prime}
conditioned on
\mathfrak{E}^{\prime}_{j}
. For
q>0
and
B\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define the function

W^{(q)}_{B}(t)=q\operatorname{dist}(t,B)^{4}.

(4.1)

We can then approximate
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
by probability measures satisfying the following

\displaystyle\mu^{(q)}_{j}(dr)

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left(-\sum_{v\in V\cup\partial\tilde{K}}W^{(q)}_{B_{j,v}}(r_{v})\right)\mu(dr),

where we set
B_{j,v}=[f_{j}(v)]
for

. It is clear that for any

and any

W^{(q)}_{B_{1,v}}(t\wedge t^{\prime})+W^{(q)}_{B_{2,v}}(t\vee t^{\prime})\leq W^{(q)}_{B_{1,v}}(t)+W^{(q)}_{B_{2,v}}(t^{\prime}).

Therefore, it follows from
[
Pr74
]
that
\mu_{2}^{(q)}
is stochastically smaller than
\mu_{1}^{(q)}

\mu_{i}^{(q)}
converges weakly to
\mu_{i}
, so it follows
\mu_{2}
is stochastically smaller than
\mu_{1}
. Finally, this implies that
\nu_{1}
is stochastically smaller than
\nu_{2}
.
∎

In light of this lemma (and the fact that
Y

and

is the set of all vertices in

I^{+}=\{v\in V\,:\,\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;D\cup\{v\})=0\}.

With this assumption, we are ready to begin the moment analysis. We start with the variance as the argument is easier.

Lemma 4.3
.

\operatorname{Var}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq\operatorname{Var}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}]\quad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of
[
DL18
, Equation (56)]
; we reproduce it here for completeness. Let
\mathbf{Y}=(\phi(v))_{v\in D}
,
\mu
be the law of
\mathbf{Y}
given
\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}
, and
\nu
be the law of
\mathbf{Y}
given
\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}
and
\mathfrak{E}
. Let
m
and
\Sigma
be the mean and variance of
\mathbf{Y}
given
\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}
and note that
\Sigma
is deterministic. Recall
\mu(dr)\propto\exp(-\frac{1}{2}(r-m)\Sigma^{-1}(r-m))dr
. For
q>0
, we approximate
\nu
by a probability measure
\nu^{(q)}
satisfying the following

\nu^{(q)}\propto\exp\left(-q\sum_{v\in D}r_{v}^{4}\mathbf{\mathbbm{1}}_{r_{v}\geq 0}\right)\mu(dr).

Since the second derivative of
f(t)=t^{4}\mathbf{\mathbbm{1}}_{t\geq 0}
is non-negative, we have that
\nu^{(q)}
is of the form
\nu^{(q)}(dr)=\exp(-H(r))dr
where
\inf_{r}\text{Hess}(H)(r)\geq\frac{1}{2}\Sigma^{-1}
. Therefore, by the Brascamp-Lieb inequality
[
BL76
]
, for a random vector
\mathbf{Y}^{(q)}\sim\nu^{(q)}
and any
l\in\mathbb{R}^{|D|}
, we have
\operatorname{Var}[l\cdot\mathbf{Y}^{(q)}]\leq\operatorname{Var}[l\cdot\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}]
. As
q\to\infty
,
\nu^{(q)}
converges weakly to
\nu
, so we have

\operatorname{Var}[l\cdot\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq\operatorname{Var}[l\cdot\mathbf{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}]\quad\text{a.s.}

Since
Y
is of the form
l\cdot\mathbf{Y}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

We obtain from this

Corollary 4.4
.

\operatorname{Var}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq 16\xi(U,I)\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)^{2},\quad\text{\emph{a.s.}}

Proof.

We note

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{v,v^{\prime}\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\operatorname{Hm}(U,v^{\prime};I)G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}(v^{\prime},v),

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}(U,v)

where
G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}
is the Green’s function on
\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}
and we have used the fact that

\sum_{v^{\prime}\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v^{\prime};I)G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}(v^{\prime},v)=G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}(U,v),\,\,\forall v\in D.

Note that

G_{\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}(U,D)\leq 4\operatorname{Hm}(U,I),

where we have used the fact that a random walk started on
D
will hit
I^{+}
with probability at least
1/4
before returning to
D
. Therefore, we have

\operatorname{Var}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}]\leq 4\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\sup_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\leq 16\xi(U,I)\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)^{2}.

∎

To conclude, we need a high-probability bound on the conditional expectation of
Y
given
\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}
and
\mathfrak{E}
. We begin with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a universal continuous, increasing function
g:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that for any
w\in\tilde{K}^{o}
and any
\epsilon>0
the following holds. Let
\mathfrak{E}_{w}
be the event

\mathfrak{E}_{w}=\{\tilde{\phi}(w)=0\}\cap\{\tilde{\phi}(u)>0\,\forall u\in\tilde{K},\,\operatorname{dist}(u,\partial\tilde{K}\cup\{w\})\geq\epsilon\}.

For
v\in\tilde{K}
such that
|v-w|\leq r
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\mid\mathfrak{E}_{w}]\leq g(r)+\lambda.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
DL18
, Lemma 6]
. First, by an argument similar to the proof of
[
DL18
, Equation (48)]
, there exists a function
R:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for any
a,b>0
, there exists a function
h_{a,b}
that is harmonic on
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{w\}
and satisfies

\Big|h_{a,b}(u)-a\log\left(|u-w|+2\right)-b\Big|\leq R(a),\quad\forall u\in\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}
be a Gaussian free field on
\tilde{K}
with boundary condition
h_{a,b}
on
\partial\tilde{K}\cup\{w\}
, and
\varphi_{a,b}
be the restriction of
\tilde{\varphi}
to
V
. Let
A\subset\tilde{K}
be a countable, dense subset of
\{u\,:\,u\in\tilde{K},\,\operatorname{dist}(u,\partial\tilde{K}\cup\{w\})\geq\epsilon\}
, and
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of finite subsets of
A
such that
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}=A
. Applying Lemma
4.2
to
A_{n}
and taking a limit, we have that for any
a,b>0
such that
h_{a,b}(u)>0
for all
u\in\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, the following holds

{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}(v)\mid\mathfrak{E}_{w}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b+\lambda}(v)\mid\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b+\lambda}(u)>0,\forall u\in\tilde{K},\,\operatorname{dist}(u,\partial\tilde{K}\cup\{w\})\geq\epsilon].

By the FKG inequality,

{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\phi}(v)\mid\mathfrak{E}_{w}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b+\lambda}(v)\mid\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b+\lambda}(u)>0,\forall u\in\tilde{K}].

Next, we claim there exist universal constants
c_{a},c_{b}>0
such that for all
a\geq c_{a}
and
b\geq c_{b}
,

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(u)>0,\,\forall\,u\in\tilde{K})\geq\frac{1}{2}.

(4.2)

Indeed, it is straightforward to check by a union bound that there exist universal constants
c_{a}^{\prime},c_{b}^{\prime}>0
such that for all
a\geq c_{a}^{\prime}
and
b\geq c_{b}^{\prime}

\mathbb{P}\left(\varphi_{a,b}(u)\geq\frac{h_{a,b}(u)}{2},\,\forall\,u\in V\right)\geq\frac{3}{4}.

Next, recall from the introduction that given
\mathcal{F}_{V}
, for each segment
e(u,v)\subset\tilde{K}
with endpoints
u,v\in V\cup\partial\tilde{K}\cup\{w\}
the restriction of
\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}
to
e(u,v)
is a Brownian bridge of length
2|u-v|
of a Brownian motion with variance 2 at time 1. Thus, applying
[
BS02
, Formula 1.3.8]
we see the following holds almost surely on
\{\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(u)\geq h_{a,b}(u)/2,\,\forall\,u\in V\}
,

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(x)>0,\,\forall\,x\in e(u,v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{V})=1-e^{-\frac{1}{2|u-v|}\varphi_{a,b}(u)\varphi_{a,b}(v)}\geq 1-e^{-\frac{1}{8}h_{a,b}(u)h_{a,b}(v)}.

Therefore, there exist universal constants
c_{a}^{\prime\prime},c_{b}^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for all
a\geq c_{a}^{\prime\prime}
and
b\geq c_{b}^{\prime\prime}

\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(u)>0,\,\forall\,u\in\tilde{K}\mid\varphi_{a,b}(u)\geq\frac{h_{a,b}(u)}{2},\,\forall\,u\in V\right)\geq\frac{2}{3}.

Combining this with the second-to-last display and letting
c_{j}=c_{j}^{\prime}\vee c_{j}^{\prime\prime}
for
j\in\{a,b\}
gives (
4.2
). Note that
\operatorname{Var}[\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(v)]\leq c\log(r+2)
for some universal constant
c>0
, and by assumption
{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\varphi}_{a,b}(v)]=h_{a,b}(v)\leq a\log(r+2)+b+R(a)
so it follows that

{\mathbb{E}}[\tilde{\varphi}_{c_{a},c_{b}+\lambda}(v)\mid\tilde{\varphi}_{c_{a},c_{b}+\lambda}(u)>0,\,\forall\,u\in\tilde{K}]\leq g(r)+\lambda,

for some universal (continuous, increasing) function
g
(which can be calculated explicitly in terms of
c
,
c_{a}
, and
c_{b}
).
∎

Finally, combining the three lemmas we can prove the following corollary, which gives a high probability bound on
{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

There exists a constant
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{1}(\lambda)>0
and a universal function
r_{1}
which satisfies
r_{1}(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
, such that the following holds

\mathbb{P}({\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq-\Delta_{1}\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E})\geq 1-r_{1}(\xi(U,I)).

Before proving this, we show how it implies Proposition
4.1
. Let
\Delta=\Delta_{1}/2
and
X=\mathbb{P}(Y\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E},\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}})
. We have

\mathbb{P}(Y\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E})={\mathbb{E}}[X\mid\mathfrak{E}].

Additionally, we have by Chebyshev’s inequality and Corollary
4.4
that there exists a function
r_{2}
such that
r_{2}(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
and

X\geq 1-r_{2}(\xi(U,I)),\quad\text{a.s. on }\{{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq-\Delta_{1}\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\}.

It follows that

\mathbb{P}(Y\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E})\geq 1-r_{1}(\xi(U,I))-r_{2}(\xi(U,I)).

Proof of Corollary
4.6
.

We have

{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]=\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I){\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}].

Let
\mathfrak{E}^{+}=\{\phi(v)\geq 0\,:\,v\in I^{+}\}
. By Lemma
4.2
, we have for any
v\in D
,

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}^{+},\phi(v)<0]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\wedge 0\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}^{+}].

Note that given
\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}}
,
\phi(v)
has a normal distribution with variance at least 1. Therefore, letting
m_{v}={\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}^{+}]
, there exist universal constants
a,b>0
such that

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(v)\wedge 0\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}^{+}]\leq-ae^{-bm_{v}^{2}}.

Thus, it suffices to show that there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)m_{v}\leq c\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E}\right)\geq 1-r_{1}(\xi(U,I)).

(4.3)

Indeed, by Markov’s inequality
\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)m_{v}\leq c\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)
implies

\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\mathbf{\mathbbm{1}}_{m_{v}\leq 2c}\geq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Hm}(U,I),

and consequently

{\mathbb{E}}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{I^{+}},\mathfrak{E}]\leq-\frac{ae^{-4bc^{2}}}{2}\operatorname{Hm}(U,I).

Turning to the proof of (
4.3
), let
Y^{\prime}
be the following random variable

Y^{\prime}=\sum_{w\in D^{\prime}}\operatorname{Hm}(U,w;I^{+})\phi(w).

It follows from the definition of the harmonic measure that

\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)m_{v}-Y^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)\sum_{w\in\partial\tilde{K}\setminus I^{+}}\operatorname{Hm}(v,w;I^{+})\tilde{\phi}(w),

where for
w\in\partial\tilde{K}
,
\operatorname{Hm}(v,w;I^{+})
is the probability that a metric graph Brownian motion started at
v
exits
\tilde{K}
through
w
without hitting
I^{+}
. In particular, we have

\sum_{v\in D}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;I)m_{v}\leq Y^{\prime}+\lambda\operatorname{Hm}(U,I).

Therefore, to prove (
4.3
) it suffices to show

\mathbb{P}\left(Y^{\prime}\leq c\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)\mid\mathfrak{E}\right)\geq 1-r_{1}(\xi(U,I)).

We show this by bounding the conditional mean and variance of
Y^{\prime}
. First, by Lemma
4.2
, we can replace
\mathfrak{E}
by
\mathfrak{E}^{\prime}=\{\phi(v)=0,\,v\in D\}\cap\mathfrak{E}^{+}
. Next, Lemma
4.3
and a straightforward adaption of the proof of Corollary
4.4
give

\operatorname{Var}[Y^{\prime}\mid\mathfrak{E}^{\prime}]\leq\operatorname{Var}[Y^{\prime}\mid\phi(v)=0,\,v\in D]\leq 4\xi(U,I)\operatorname{Hm}(U,I)^{2}.

Finally, Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.2
(and the fact that every
w\in D^{\prime}
is adjacent to a vertex in
D
) give

{\mathbb{E}}[\phi(w)\mid\mathfrak{E}^{\prime}]\leq g(1)+\lambda,\quad\forall w\in D^{\prime}.

The conclusion then follows easily.
∎

4.2
Zero boundary case

In this section we prove (
1.5
). We let

be an exploration martingale, as introduced in Section

from

with the understanding that

). With this setup, we have

. The main step of the proof consists of bounding

. In particular, we claim that there exists

\sup\{\xi_{k}\,:\,k\geq 1\}\leq\frac{c}{|\log(\delta)|},\quad\text{a.s.}

(4.4)

Before proving (
4.4

). To simplify notation, we will write

for the event that

is not connected to

in

. Assuming (
4.4
), Proposition
4.1
implies that there exists
\Delta>0
such that for any

, there exists

such that

\mathbb{P}(M_{\infty}\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{\infty})\mid D^{>0}(\mathcal{I}_{0},U)=\infty)\geq 1-\epsilon,\quad\forall\delta\leq\delta_{0}.

(4.5)

To conclude, we need to upper bound the quadratic variation. Recall that
\operatorname{Hm}_{t}
is the harmonic measure on
\mathcal{I}_{t}\cup\partial V_{\delta}
. By (
2.5
)

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{t}}\operatorname{Hm}_{t}(U,v)G(v,U)\leq 4\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{t}),

(4.6)

where we have used the fact that a random walk started on

has probability at least

of hitting

before returning to

, which implies
G(v,U)\leq 4
for all

.

Finally, by Lemma
2.7
,
\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{0})\geq c
. Combining this with (

) and (

) and applying Theorem

, we see that there exists

such that

\mathbb{P}(\exists t\geq 0\text{ s.t. }M_{t}\leq-\Delta\operatorname{Hm}(U,I_{t}))\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\exists t\geq 0\text{ s.t. }B_{t}\leq-\Delta\left(c\vee\frac{t}{4}\right)\right)\leq 1-2\epsilon.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}(D^{>0}(\mathcal{I}_{0},U)=\infty)\leq 1-\epsilon,\quad\forall\delta\leq\delta_{0}.

Proof of (
4.4
).

Let
D_{k}=\{v\in\mathcal{I}_{k}\,:\,\operatorname{Hm}_{k}(U,v)>0\}
,
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}=\mathcal{I}_{k}\setminus D_{k}
, and
\operatorname{Hm}_{k}^{\prime}
be the harmonic measure on
\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}\cup\partial V_{\delta}
. Let
S
be a random walk on
\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
killed on
\partial V_{\delta}
,
\tau_{k}=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in U\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{k}\}
, and

be the Green’s function of the random walk killed on
\partial V_{\delta}\cup\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{k}
. We have for
u\in U
and

\operatorname{Hm}_{k}(u^{\prime},v)=\sum_{u\in U}G^{\prime}_{k}(u^{\prime},u)\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{\tau_{k}}=v).

By reversibility,
\mathbb{P}_{u}(S_{\tau_{k}}=v)=\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{k}}=u)
. Noting also that
G_{k}^{\prime}
is symmetric, summing over
u^{\prime}
gives

\operatorname{Hm}_{k}(U,v)=\sum_{u\in U}\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{k}}=u)G^{\prime}_{k}(u,U)\leq 4\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{k}}\in U),

where we have used the fact that

. There exists a universal constant
p>0
such that for any integer

(here

around

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{k}
, it follows that there exist

such that the following holds for all

and

\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{k}}\in U)\leq A\left(\frac{\delta}{\operatorname{dist}(v,U)}\right)^{\alpha}.

It remains to lower bound
\operatorname{Hm}(U,\mathcal{I}_{k})
in terms of

. We have already noted that there exists
c>0
such that

for all

. On the other hand, a simple adaptation of the proof of (

) shows that there exists a constant

Combining the lower bounds on

with the upper bound on

gives the desired conclusion.
∎

\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}

[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})

\eta=\min\{\eta_{v}\,:\,v\in\partial\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0}\}>0,\quad\text{a.s.}

U

U=\left\{u\,:\,u\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+},\,\Big|\Re(u)-\frac{L}{2}\Big|\leq\frac{3L}{8},\,\operatorname{dist}(u,\tilde{{\mathchoice{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\displaystyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\textstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{A}$}}}}_{\delta,0})=\frac{\eta}{2}\right\}.

and lower bound the quadratic variation of

. In particular, we claim that there exist constants

such that the following holds

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{\eta}{\delta},

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}\left(\frac{\eta}{\delta}\right)^{2},\quad\text{a.s. on }\left\{[a_{\delta},b_{\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}\geq 0}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})\right\}.

Before proving the claim, we note that it implies by an application of Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
2.4

\mathbb{P}\left([a_{\delta},b_{\delta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}\geq 0}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq t\leq c^{\prime}}B_{t}\geq-c\lambda\right)\leq 1-c^{\prime\prime}.

For the proof, consider the electric network with vertex set
W=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cup U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}
where two vertices

are connected with an edge of conductance
C_{u,v}=(4|u-v|/\delta)^{-1}
if there exists a path in
\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
connecting

to

which does not contain any other points in
W

u
and
v

. Note that
G^{+}
(as defined above) is the Green’s function of the continuous time simple random walk on
W
killed on
\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}
. To simplify some definitions, we take
S
to be a discrete-time simple random walk on
W
(not killed on
\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}
).

We now turn to proving the upper bound on
\mu_{0}
. Let
\tau_{0}=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}\}
be the hitting time of
U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}
. We have by a last exit decomposition

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hm}^{+}(u,\mathcal{I}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u^{\prime}\in U}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}C_{u^{\prime}}G^{+}(u,u^{\prime})\mathbb{P}_{u^{\prime}}(S_{\tau_{0}}=v),

\displaystyle=\sum_{u^{\prime}\in U}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}C_{v}G^{+}(u^{\prime},u)\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{0}}=u^{\prime})

It is straightforward to show that

for all

such that
\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{0}}\in U)>0

\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau_{0}}\in U)\leq c\delta
since it is bounded by the probability that a random walk on
\delta\mathbb{Z}

contains a nearest neighbor path

\Pi^{\prime}=\left\{z\,:\,\Big|\Re(z)-\frac{L}{2}\Big|\leq\frac{L}{4}\right\}.

For the proof, note that there exists
c>0
such that

for all
v\in\gamma

c
is independent of
\gamma
and
v

\Pi^{\prime}
will exit
V_{\delta}
before exiting the strip

\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}

G^{+}(u,U)\geq G^{+}(u,u)\geq\eta/\delta
for all

\operatorname{Var}^{+}(X_{U})-\operatorname{Var}^{+}(X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})\geq c\frac{\eta}{\delta}\operatorname{Hm}^{+}(U,\gamma).

To lower bound the harmonic measure, the idea is to replace
\gamma
with a line. More precisely, we take

\gamma^{*}=\left\{w\in V_{\delta}\,:\,\Im(w)\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right),\,\Big|\Re(w)-\frac{L}{2}\Big|\leq\frac{L}{4}\right\},

and note that there exists a universal constant

such that for

. This follows from the fact that a random walk started at
w

before

V_{\delta}
before exiting

is bounded uniformly away from 0. Therefore, we have
\operatorname{Hm}^{+}(U,\gamma)\geq c\operatorname{Hm}^{+}(U,\gamma^{*})
. To bound

from below, we proceed by a last-exit decomposition. Let
\tau=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{+}\cup\gamma^{*}\}
. By the same argument given in the proof of the upper bound on
\mu_{0}
,

\operatorname{Hm}^{+}(U,\gamma^{*})=\sum_{w\in\gamma^{*}}\sum_{u\in U}C_{w}\mathbb{P}_{w}(S_{\tau}=u)G^{+}(u,U)\geq\frac{\eta}{\delta}\sum_{w\in\gamma^{*}}\mathbb{P}_{w}(S_{\tau}\in U),

where we have used the fact that
C_{w}=1
for all
w\in\gamma^{*}
and
G^{+}(u,u)\geq\eta/\delta
for all
u\in U
. Finally, by Lemma
2.7
we have

\sum_{w\in\gamma^{*}}\mathbb{P}_{w}(S_{\tau}\in U)\geq c.

This concludes the proof.

5
(Non)-Existence of closed pivotal edges for metric graph percolation

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
. First, we recall some definitions and notation. Let
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
be a metric graph GFF on
\tilde{V}_{\delta}
with alternating boundary condition (
1.3
). Denote by
E_{\delta}
the edge set of the nearest-neighbor graph on
V_{\delta}
. We let
\omega:E_{\delta}\to\{0,1\}
be such that
\omega(e)=1
if
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(u)\geq 0
for all
u\in I_{e}
and
\omega(e)=0
otherwise. In the former case, we say the edge is open, in the latter that it is closed. The notations
\omega^{e}
and
\omega_{e}
are defined in (
1.10
). An edge
e
is pivotal if there is a horizontal crossing in
\omega^{e}
but there is no such crossing in
\omega_{e}
. Note that the pivotality of an edge does not depend on the status of the edge itself. We say that
e
is a closed pivotal edge (resp. open pivotal edge) if
e
is pivotal and it is closed (resp. open). Our goal is to show that the probability that there exists a closed pivotal edge decays to 0 as
\delta\to 0
. In particular, we want to show that there exists a constant
c>0
(depending only on
L
) such that the following holds

\mathbb{P}(\text{there exists a closed pivotal edge})\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|\log(\delta)|}}.

(5.1)

By symmetry, it suffices to consider only edges
e
on the left half of
V_{\delta}
. That is, if we let

E_{\delta}^{l}=\left\{e\in E\,:\,\exists\,x\in I_{e},\,\Re(x)\leq\frac{L}{2}\right\},

it suffices to show

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{there exists a closed pivotal edge in $E_{\delta}^{l}$}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|\log(\delta)|}}.

In fact, we will show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{there exists a closed pivotal edge in $E_{\delta}^{l}$}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}}\right)\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{|\log(\delta)|}}\quad\text{a.s.}

Note that if there is a positive horizontal crossing in the metric graph (that is,
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}\cap[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
) then there are no closed pivotal edges and that this event is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{A}_{\delta,0}^{l}}
. Therefore, we assume from now on that this is not the case.

To simplify notation, we let
\mathbb{P}^{l}
denote the law of
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
given
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}}
and
{\mathbb{E}}^{l}
denote the expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}^{l}
. Similarly, we let
\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
be the connected component of
\tilde{V}_{\delta}\setminus\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}
containing
[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})
and
V_{\delta}^{l}=\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Note that, given that there is no horizontal crossing in the metric graph, the set of closed pivotal edges consists of all edges with one endpoint in
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}
and the other in
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{r}
. Therefore, given
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{A}_{\delta,0}^{l}}
the set of pivotal edges is increasing in
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{r}
and therefore increasing in
\{\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}(v)\,:\,v\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}\}
. We will therefore assume from now on that
\tilde{\phi}_{\delta}
has zero boundary condition on
[b_{\delta},c_{\delta})\cup[d_{\delta},a_{\delta})
.

The proof is essentially the same as that of (
4.7
) and consists of analyzing an exploration martingale
M
, as introduced in Section
2.3
, corresponding to an exploration on
\tilde{E}^{\geq 0}_{\delta}
from
\mathcal{I}_{0}=[c_{\delta},d_{\delta})
with observable given by the following set. For each
v\in\partial\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}\setminus\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}
, we let
\eta_{v}=\operatorname{dist}(v,V_{\delta}^{l})
. Since
|\partial\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}|<\infty
and
\partial\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}
almost surely, we have

\eta=\min\{\eta_{v}\,:\,v\in\partial\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}\setminus\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}>0\}\quad\text{a.s.}

We then set the observable set
U
to be

U=\left\{u\in\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}\,:\,\operatorname{dist}(u,\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l})=\frac{\eta}{2},\,\Re(u)\leq\frac{3L}{4}\right\}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
U
is non-empty (and indeed that there exists
u\in U
with
\Re(u)\leq(L/2)+\delta
) since otherwise there can be no pivotal edges in
E_{\delta}^{l}
. As usual, we let
\operatorname{Hm}^{l}_{t}
be the harmonic measure on
\mathcal{I}_{t}\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
and
\mu_{t}=\operatorname{Hm}^{l}_{t}(U,\mathcal{I}_{t})
. As usual, since
\mathcal{I}_{0}\subset\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
we write
\operatorname{Hm}^{l}
for
\operatorname{Hm}^{l}_{0}
. Note that
M_{0}=\lambda\mu_{0}
and
M_{t}\geq 0
for all
t
, so
M_{t}-M_{0}\geq-\lambda\mu_{0}
. We claim that there exist constants
c,c^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{\eta}{\delta},

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}\left(\frac{\eta}{\delta}\right)^{2}|\log(\delta)|,\quad\text{a.s. on }\{\text{there exists a closed pivotal edge in }E_{\delta}^{l}\}.

Assuming this claim for now, an application of Theorem
2.3
then gives (after rescaling the Brownian motion)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{l}(\text{there exists a closed pivotal edge in }E_{\delta}^{l})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{0\leq t\leq c^{\prime}|\log(\delta)|}B_{t}\geq-c\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{\prime\prime}}{\sqrt{|\log(\delta)|}}.

To prove the claim we introduce the electric network with vertex set
W=\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cup U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
where two vertices
u,v\in W
are connected with an edge of conductance
C_{u,v}=(4|u-v|/\delta)^{-1}
if there exists a path in
\delta\tilde{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
connecting
u
to
v
which does not contain any other points in
W
. For the rest of this proof, we will write
u\sim v
if
u
and
v
are connected in this network, and we let
C_{u}=\sum_{v\sim u}C_{u,v}
. We let
G^{l}
be the Green’s function corresponding to the continuous time simple random walk on
W
killed on
\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
, but take
S
to be a discrete-time simple random walk on
W
(not killed on
\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}
).

For the upper bound on
\mu_{0}
, we let
\tau=\min\{n\geq 1\,:\,S_{n}\in U\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}\}
. By the arguments given in the proof of (
4.7
),

\operatorname{Hm}^{l}(U,\mathcal{I}_{0})=\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}C_{v}\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}=u)G^{l}(u,U)\leq 2\frac{\eta}{\delta}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}\in U),

where we used the facts that
C_{v}=1
for all
v\in\mathcal{I}_{0}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}\in U)>0
and
G^{l}(u,U)\leq 2\eta/\delta
for all
u\in U
. Finally, there exists
c>0
such that
P_{v}(S_{\tau}\in U)\leq c\delta
since this probability is upper bounded by the probability that a one-dimensional simple random walk hits
L/4
before returning to zero. Since
|\mathcal{I}_{0}|\geq c^{\prime}/\delta
we conclude that
\mu_{0}\leq c\eta/\delta
as promised.

To lower bound the quadratic variation, we note that if there exists a closed pivotal edge in
E_{\delta}^{l}
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\infty}
contains a nearest-neighbor path
\gamma
in
V_{\delta}^{l}
satisfying the following conditions. First, that it crosses the following strip

\Pi^{\prime}=\left\{z\,:\,\frac{L}{2}+\delta\leq\Re(z)\leq\frac{5L}{8}\right\}.

(5.2)

Second, that there exists
v^{*}\in\gamma
and
w\in\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\delta,0}^{l}\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}
satisfying
\Re(w)\leq L/2+\delta
and
|w-v^{*}|=\delta
. We claim that there exists
c>0
such that for any such path

\operatorname{Var}^{l}(X_{U})-\operatorname{Var}^{l}(X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})\geq c\left(\frac{\eta}{\delta}\right)^{2}|\log(\delta)|.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}^{l}(X_{U})-\operatorname{Var}^{l}(X_{U}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\gamma}\operatorname{Hm}(U,v;\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l})G^{l}(v,U),

\displaystyle\geq c\frac{\eta}{\delta}\operatorname{Hm}(U,\gamma;\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}),

where we used the fact that
\operatorname{Hm}^{l}(v,U)\geq c
for all
v\in\gamma
since it is lower bounded by the probability that a simple random walk on
\delta\mathbb{Z}
started at
\delta\lceil 5L/8\delta\rceil
hits 0 before
\delta\lfloor 3L/4\delta\rfloor
and the fact that
G(u,U)\geq\eta/\delta
for all
u\in U
. Therefore, we want to show that there exists
c>0
such that for any path
\gamma
that satisfies the conditions above,

\operatorname{Hm}(U,\gamma;\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l})\geq c\frac{\eta}{\delta}|\log(\delta)|.

The proof is essentially identical to that of (
3.3
). For
n\geq 0
an integer, we let
Q_{n}
be the box of radius
r_{n}=2^{-n-2}L
centered at
v^{*}
,
A_{n}=Q_{n}\setminus Q_{n+1}
,
U_{n}=U\cap A_{n}
, and
\gamma_{n}=\gamma\cap A_{n}
. We note that
N=\max\{n\geq 1\,:\,r_{n}\geq 1000\delta\}
satisfies
N\geq c|\log(\delta)|
. Finally, we claim that there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

\operatorname{Hm}(U_{n},\gamma_{n};\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l})\geq c\frac{\eta}{\delta},\quad 1\leq n\leq N\,.

For the proof, we begin as usual with a last-exit decomposition. Take
1\leq n\leq N
and let
\tau=\min\{k\geq 1\,:\,S_{k}\in U\cup\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l}\}
(recall
S
is a random walk on
W
). We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hm}(U_{n},\gamma_{n};\gamma\cup\partial\tilde{V}_{\delta}^{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\sum_{u^{\prime}\in U}\sum_{v\in\gamma_{n}}G^{l}(u,u^{\prime})C_{u^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{u^{\prime}}(S_{\tau}=v),

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\sum_{u^{\prime}\in U}\sum_{v\in\gamma_{n}}G^{l}(u^{\prime},u)C_{v}\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}=u^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta}{\delta}\sum_{v\in\gamma_{n}}\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}\in U).

From here, the details of the proof are the same as for the proof of (
3.3
). Let
v_{1}\in\gamma_{n}
be such that
2r_{n}/3-\delta<|v_{1}-v^{*}|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 2r_{n}/3
,
v_{2}\in\gamma_{n}
be such that
5r_{n}/6\leq|v_{2}-v^{*}|_{\ell_{\infty}}<5r_{n}/6+\delta
, and
Q_{n,2}
be the box of radius
r_{n}/12
centered at
v_{2}
. With these choices, the distance between
v_{1}
and
Q_{n,2}
, and between
\{v_{1}\}\cup Q_{n,2}
and
A_{n}^{c}
are of the same order as
r_{n}
. Therefore, letting
\mathfrak{E}
be the event that
S
hits
Q_{n,2}\cap\gamma
and then hits
U
before exiting
A_{n}
, there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that
\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}(\mathfrak{E})\geq c
. By a last exit decomposition

\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}(\mathfrak{E})\leq\sum_{v\in\gamma}G^{l,*}(v_{1},v)\mathbb{P}_{v}(S_{\tau}\in U_{n}),

where
G^{l,*}(v_{1},v)
is the expected number of visits a random walk started at
v_{1}
makes to
v
after hitting
Q_{n,2}\cap\gamma
and before exiting
A_{n}
. Finally, it follows easily from
[
LL10
, Theorem 4.4.4, Proposition 4.6.2]
that
G^{l,*}(v_{1},\cdot)
is uniformly bounded. That is, there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that

G^{l,*}(v_{1},v)\leq c,\quad v\in A_{n}\cap\delta\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.

6.1
Preliminaries on SLE

We denote by
\mathbb{H}
the upper-half plane. We call a compact subset
K
of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
an
\mathbb{H}
-hull if
\mathbb{H}\setminus K
is simply connected. By Riemann’s mapping theorem, there exists a unique conformal map
g_{K}
from
\mathbb{H}\setminus K
onto
\mathbb{H}
with the normalization
\lim_{z\to\infty}|g_{K}(z)-z|=0
. With such normalization, we say that
g_{K}
is normalized at
\infty
.

We consider the following collections of
\mathbb{H}
-hulls. First, consider families of conformal maps
(g_{t},t\geq 0)
obtained by solving the Loewner equation: for each
z\in\mathbb{H}
,

\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W_{t}},\quad g_{0}(z)=z,

where
(W_{t},t\geq 0)
is a real-valued continuous function, which we call the driving function. Second, for each
z\in\mathbb{H}
, define the swallowing time
T_{z}
to be

\sup\left\{t\geq 0:\inf_{s\in[0,t]}|g_{s}(z)-W_{s}|>0\right\}.

Finally, denote by
K_{t}
the closure of
\{z\in\mathbb{H}:T_{z}\leq t\}
. Then
g_{t}
is the conformal map from
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
onto
\mathbb{H}
normalized at
\infty
. The collection of
\mathbb{H}
-hulls
(K_{t},t\geq 0)
is called a Loewner chain parameterized by the half-plane capacity.

For
\kappa\geq 0
, Schramm Loewner Evolution, denoted by
\text{SLE}_{\kappa}
, is the Loewner chain with driving function
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
where
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion. It was proved in
[
RS05
]
that
(K_{t},t\geq 0)
is almost surely generated by a continuous transient curve, i.e. there exists a continuous curve
\eta
such that, for each
t\geq 0
, the set
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
is the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta[0,t]
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}|\eta(t)|=\infty
. In this section, we focus on
\kappa\in(0,4]
when the curve is simple.

\text{SLE}_{\kappa}(\rho^{L};\rho^{R})
is a variant of
\text{SLE}_{\kappa}
process where one keeps track of two extra marked points on the boundary.
Let
y^{L}\leq 0\leq y^{R}
and
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}\in\mathbb{R}
. An
\text{SLE}_{\kappa}(\rho^{L};\rho^{R})
process with force points
(y^{L};y^{R})
is the Loewner chain driven by
W_{t}
that solves the following system of SDEs:

\displaystyle dW_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}dB_{t}+\frac{\rho^{L}dt}{W_{t}-V_{t}^{L}}+\frac{\rho^{R}dt}{W_{t}-V_{t}^{R}},\quad dV_{t}^{L}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{L}-W_{t}},\quad dV_{t}^{R}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{R}-W_{t}},

where
W_{0}=0,V_{0}^{L}=y^{L},V_{0}^{R}=y^{R}
.
It turns out that such process exists for all time if
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}>-2
.
If
\rho^{L}\leq-2
or
\rho^{R}\leq-2
, it exists up to
the first time that the process swallows
y^{L}
or
y^{R}
. Moreover, the process is generated by continuous curve up to and including the same time.

The above SLE processes are defined in
\mathbb{H}
, for other simply connected domains we define SLE process via conformal image. Suppose
\Omega
is a non-trivial simply connected domain and
a,b,c,d
are four boundary points lying on locally connected components in counterclockwise order. Then
\text{SLE}_{\kappa}(\rho^{L};\rho^{R})
in
\Omega
from
b
to
d
with force points
(a;c)
is
\varphi^{-1}(\eta)
where
\eta
is an
\text{SLE}_{\kappa}(\rho^{L};\rho^{R})
from
0
to
\infty
with force points
(y^{L};y^{R})
and
\varphi
is any conformal map from
\Omega
onto
\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi(a)=y^{L}\leq\varphi(b)=0\leq\varphi(c)=y^{R}<\varphi(d)=\infty
.

In this section, we focus on
\text{SLE}_{4}(\rho^{L};\rho^{R})
with
\rho^{L}=\rho^{R}=-2
and force points
y^{L}<0<y^{R}
. Then the above SDEs become

dW_{t}=2dB_{t}+\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{L}-W_{t}}+\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{R}-W_{t}},\quad dV_{t}^{L}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{L}-W_{t}},\quad dV_{t}^{R}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{R}-W_{t}}.

(6.1)

Denote by
\eta
the continuous curve corresponding to the Loewner chain driven by
W
.
Let
T
be the first time that the process swallows
y^{L}
or
y^{R}
. We will calculate the probability
{\mathbb{P}}(\eta(T)=y^{R})
.
Set

\hat{W}_{t}=\frac{2W_{t}-V_{t}^{L}-V_{t}^{R}}{V_{t}^{R}-V_{t}^{L}}.

Then
T=\inf\{t:\hat{W}_{t}\in\{-1,+1\}\}
. Itô’s formula gives

d\hat{W}_{t}=\frac{4dB_{t}}{V_{t}^{R}-V_{t}^{L}}.

In particular,
(\hat{W}_{t\wedge T},t\geq 0)
is a bounded martingale. Optional stopping theorem gives
{\mathbb{E}}[\hat{W}_{T}]=\hat{W}_{0}
. From there, we obtain

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\eta(T)=y^{R}\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\hat{W}_{T}=1\right)=\frac{-y^{L}}{y^{R}-y^{L}}.

(6.2)

This gives (
1.9
) assuming the convergence of the level line in Theorem
6.1
.

Below, we perform a time change in the system which will be useful in Section
6.2
. This is analog of
[
SS09
, Section 4.5]
.
Define a new time parameter, for
t\geq 0
,

s(t):=\log\left(\frac{V_{t}^{R}-V_{t}^{L}}{y^{R}-y^{L}}\right).

Set
\tilde{W}_{s}=\hat{W}_{t}
when
s=s(t)
. Then we have

d\tilde{W}_{s}=q(\tilde{W}_{s})d\tilde{B}_{s},\quad\text{where }q(x)=\sqrt{2(1-x^{2})},

(6.3)

and
(\tilde{B}_{s},s\geq 0)
is a standard one-dimensional Brownian motion. The process
\tilde{W}
starts from
\tilde{W}_{0}=\frac{-y^{L}-y^{R}}{y^{R}-y^{L}}
, evolves according to (
6.3
), and stops when it hits
\{-1,+1\}
at finite time
T
.
From above, we see how to transform from the process in (
6.1
) to the one in (
6.3
).
We will show that this is one-to-one transform up to a scaling constant.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
(\tilde{Y}_{s})
is a continuous process starting from
\tilde{Y}_{0}\in(-1,1)
, evolving according to
d\tilde{Y}_{s}=q(\tilde{Y}_{s})d\tilde{B}_{s}
, and stopped when it hits
\{-1,+1\}
. Define

t(s)=\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{s}e^{2u}(1-\tilde{Y}_{u}^{2})du,\quad s(t)=\sup\{s:t(s)\leq t\}.

Define

Y_{t}=\frac{1}{2}e^{s(t)}\tilde{Y}_{s(t)}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s(t)}e^{u}\tilde{Y}_{u}du-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{Y}_{0}.

Then
(Y_{t})
is a continuous process starting from
0
and evolving according to

dY_{t}=2dB_{t}+\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{L}-Y_{t}}+\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{R}-Y_{t}},\quad dV_{t}^{L}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{L}-Y_{t}},\quad dV_{t}^{R}=\frac{2dt}{V_{t}^{R}-Y_{t}},

(6.4)

where
V_{0}^{L}=-\frac{1}{2}(1+\tilde{Y}_{0})
and
V_{0}^{R}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\tilde{Y}_{0})
.

Proof.

Set

B_{t}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{s(t)}e^{u}q(\tilde{Y}_{u})d\tilde{B}_{u};

V^{L}_{t}=V_{0}^{L}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s(t)}e^{u}(1-\tilde{Y}_{u})du,\quad V^{R}_{t}=V_{0}^{R}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s(t)}e^{u}(1+\tilde{Y}_{u})du.

Then
(B_{t})
is a standard Brownian motion, and
(Y_{t},V_{t}^{L},V_{t}^{R})
solves (
6.4
).
∎

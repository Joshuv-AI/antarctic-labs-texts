Abstract
We study the Ohno-Zagier type relation for multiple t t -values and multiple t t -star values. We represent the generating function of sums of multiple t t -(star) values with fixed weight, depth and height in terms of the generalized hypergeometric function F 2 3 \,{}_{3}F_{2} . As applications, we get a formula for the generating function of sums of multiple t t -(star) values of maximal height and a weighted sum formula for sums of multiple t t -(star) values with fixed weight and depth.



Keywords
multiple
t
-value, multiple zeta value, generalized hypergeometric function.

Introduction

A finite sequence
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
of positive integers is called an index. The weight, depth and height of the index
\mathbf{k}
are defined respectively by
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}
,
n
and
|\{j\mid 1\leq j\leq n,k_{j}\geq 2\}|
. If
k_{1}>1
, the index
\mathbf{k}
is called admissible. For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the multiple zeta value
\zeta(\mathbf{k})
and the multiple zeta-star value
\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\zeta(\mathbf{k})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=\zeta^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

The systematic study of multiple zeta values was carried out by M. E. Hoffman
[
2
]
and D. Zagier
[
10
]
. More results of multiple zeta values can be gotten from the book
[
11
]
of J. Zhao.

In this paper, we focus on the Ohno-Zagier type relations. For nonnegative integers
k,n,s
, denote by
I_{0}(k,n,s)
the set of admissible indices of weight
k
, depth
n
and height
s
. It is easy to see that
I_{0}(k,n,s)
is nonempty if and only if
k\geq n+s
and
n\geq s\geq 1
. Using the Gaussian hypergeometric function, Y. Ohno and D. Zagier proved in
[
7
]
that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq n+s,n\geq s\geq 1}\left(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}\zeta(\mathbf{k})\right)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{s-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{uv-w}\left\{1-\exp\left(\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(n)}{n}(u^{n}+v^{n}-\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})\right)\right\},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are determined by
\alpha+\beta=u+v
and
\alpha\beta=w
.

After that, similar studies were carried out on various generalizations of multiple zeta values. For example, in
[
1
]
, a similar formula holds for the sums of multiple zeta-star values. While this time we do not meet the Gaussian hypergeometric function but the generalized hypergeometric function
\,{}_{3}F_{2}
. In fact, T. Aoki, Y. Kombu and Y. Ohno showed that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\geq n+s,n\geq s\geq 1}\left(\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}\zeta^{\star}(\mathbf{k})\right)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{2s-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-v)(1-\beta)}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({1-\beta,1-\beta+u,1\atop 2-v,2-\beta};1\right)

with
\alpha
and
\beta
determined by
\alpha+\beta=u+v
and
\alpha\beta=uv-w^{2}
. Here for a positive integer
m
and complex numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}
with
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots
, the generalized hypergeometric function
\,{}_{m+1}F_{m}
is defined as

\,{}_{m+1}F_{m}\left({a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1}\atop b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}};z\right)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}\cdots(a_{m+1})_{n}}{n!(b_{1})_{n}\cdots(b_{m})_{n}}z^{n},

with the Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{n}
defined by

(a)_{n}=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if\;}n=1,\\
a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1),&\text{if\;}n>1.\end{cases}

It is known that this formal power series converges absolutely for
|z|<1
, and it also converges absolutely for
|z|=1
if
\Re(\sum b_{i}-\sum a_{i})>0
. If
m=1
, we get the Gaussian hypergeometric function.

In
[
9
]
, Y. Takeyama studied the Ohno-Zagier type relation for a level two variant of multiple zeta values, which is called multiple
T
-values and was introduced by M. Kaneko and H. Tsumura
[
6
]
. As a consequence, a weighted sum formula of multiple
T
-values with fixed weight and depth was given in
[
9
]
. In this paper, we consider another level two variant of multiple zeta values, which is called multiple
t
-values and was introduced by M. E. Hoffman in
[
3
]
.

For an admissible index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, the multiple
t
-value
t(\mathbf{k})
and the multiple
t
-star value
t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle t(\mathbf{k})=t(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

\displaystyle t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=t^{\star}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

It is easy to obtain the following iterated integral representations:

\displaystyle t(\mathbf{k})=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}\frac{tdt}{1-t^{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{n-1}-1}\frac{tdt}{1-t^{2}}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{n}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}},

\displaystyle t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{t(1-t^{2})}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{n-1}-1}\frac{dt}{t(1-t^{2})}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{n}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t^{2}},

where
\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{i}-1}=\underbrace{\frac{dt}{t}\cdots\frac{dt}{t}}_{k_{i}-1}
, and for one forms
\omega_{i}(t)=f_{i}(t)dt
,
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, we define the iterated integral

\int_{0}^{z}\omega_{1}(t)\cdots\omega_{k}(t)=\int\limits_{z>t_{1}>\cdots>t_{k}>0}f_{1}(t_{1})\cdots f_{k}(t_{k})dt_{1}\cdots dt_{k}.

We want to study the sum of multiple
t
-(star) values with fixed weight, depth and height. For nonnegative integers
k,n,s
, define

G_{0}(k,n,s)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}t(\mathbf{k}),\quad G_{0}^{\star}(k,n,s)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n,s)}t^{\star}(\mathbf{k}).

Then we obtain Ohno-Zagier type relations which represent the generating functions of
G_{0}(k,n,s)
and
G_{0}^{\star}(k,n,s)
by the generalized hypergeometric function
\,{}_{3}F_{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For formal variables
u,v,w
, we have

\sum\limits_{k\geq n+s,n\geq s\geq 1}G_{0}(k,n,s)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{s-1}=\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\alpha,\beta,1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are determined by
\alpha+\beta=1-\frac{u}{2}+\frac{v}{2}
and
\alpha\beta=\frac{1}{4}(1-u+v-uv+w)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For formal variables
u,v,w
, we have

\sum\limits_{k\geq n+s,n\geq s\geq 1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n,s)u^{k-n-s}v^{n-s}w^{s-1}=\frac{1}{1-u-v+uv-w}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star}};1\right),

where
\alpha^{\star}
and
\beta^{\star}
are determined by
\alpha^{\star}+\beta^{\star}=3-\frac{u}{2}-\frac{v}{2}
and
\alpha\beta=\frac{1}{4}(9-3u-3v+uv-w)
.

From the above theorems and using some summation formulas of
\,{}_{3}F_{2}
, we obtain some corollaries in Section
2
. For example, we give a formula for the generating function of sums of multiple
t
-(star) values of maximal height and a weighted sum formula of sums of multiple
t
-(star) values with fixed weight and depth. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in Section
3
.

Applications

2.1
Sum of height one

To save space, we denote a sequence of
k
repeated
n
times by
\{k\}^{n}
.

Setting
w=0
in Theorem
1.1
, we find

\alpha,\beta=\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1+v}{2}.

Hence we get the following result, which gives the generating function of height one multiple
t
-values.

Corollary 2.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

We have

\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1,n\geq 1}t(k-n+1,\{1\}^{n-1})u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1+v}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right).

Similarly, setting
w=0
in Theorem
1.2
, we have

\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star}=\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3-v}{2}.

Therefore, we obtain the generating function of height one multiple
t
-star values.

Corollary 2.2
.

We have

\sum\limits_{k\geq n+1,n\geq 1}t^{\star}(k-n+1,\{1\}^{n-1})u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{(1-u)(1-v)}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3-v}{2}};1\right).

Note that using
[
3
, Lemma 5.2]
, for any integer
m\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}t(m,\{1\}^{n-1})v^{n-1}=\,_{m+1}F_{m}\left({\frac{1+v}{2},1,\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{m-1}\atop\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{m}};1\right),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{\star}(m,\{1\}^{n-1})v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{1-v}\,_{m+1}F_{m}\left({1,\left\{\frac{1}{2}\right\}^{m}\atop\frac{3-v}{2},\left\{\frac{3}{2}\right\}^{m-1}};1\right).

2.2
Sum of maximal height

Setting
v=0
in Theorem
1.1
, we get the generating function of sums of multiple
t
-values of maximal height. By the symmetric sum formula
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
, the sum of multiple
t
-values with fixed weight, depth and maximal height can be represented by
t
-values. Here we give a closed formula for the generating function of the sums of maximal height.

Corollary 2.3
.

For formal variables
u,w
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{k\geq 2n,n\geq 1}G_{0}(k,n,n)u^{k-2n}w^{n}=\exp\left\{\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t(n)}{n}(u^{n}-x^{n}-y^{n})\right\},

(2.1)

where
x
and
y
are determined by
x+y=u
and
xy=w
.

Proof.
Setting
v=0
in Theorem
1.1
, we get
\alpha+\beta=1-\frac{u}{2}
and
\alpha\beta=\frac{1}{4}(1-u+w)
. Let
x=1-2\alpha
and
y=1-2\beta
, then
x+y=u
and
xy=w
. Using the summation formula
[
8
, 7.4.4.28]

\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left({a,b,1\atop c,2+a+b-c};1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+a+b-c}{(1+a-c)(1+b-c)}\left(1-c+\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(1+a+b-c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\right)

(2.2)

with
a=\alpha
,
b=\beta
and
c=\frac{3}{2}
, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\alpha,\beta,1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right)=\frac{1}{1-u}\frac{\frac{1-u}{2}}{\left(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\beta-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{w}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{w}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-y}{2}\right)}.

Using the duplication formula

\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}2^{z}\Gamma(1-z)}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{z}{2}\right)},

and the expansion

\displaystyle\Gamma(1-z)=\exp\left(\gamma z+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(n)}{n}z^{n}\right),

(2.3)

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant, we get

\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi}2^{z}\exp\left\{\frac{\gamma z}{2}+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta(n)}{n}(1-2^{-n})z^{n}\right\}.

Since
t(n)=(1-2^{-n})\zeta(n)
, we find that (see also
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
)

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\frac{1-z}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi}2^{z}\exp\left\{\frac{\gamma z}{2}+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t(n)}{n}z^{n}\right\}.

(2.4)

Now it is easy to finish the proof.∎

Similarly, we have a formula for the generating function of the sums of multiple
t
-star values of maximal height.

Corollary 2.4
.

For formal variables
u,w
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{k\geq 2n,n\geq 1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n,n)u^{k-2n}w^{n}=\exp\left\{\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{t(n)}{n}((x^{\star})^{n}+(y^{\star})^{n}-u^{n})\right\},

(2.5)

where
x^{\star}
and
y^{\star}
are determined by
x^{\star}+y^{\star}=u
and
x^{\star}y^{\star}=-w
.

Proof.
Setting
v=0
in Theorem
1.2
, we get
\alpha^{\star}+\beta^{\star}=3-\frac{u}{2}
and
\alpha^{\star}\beta^{\star}=\frac{1}{4}(9-3u-w)
. Let
x^{\star}=3-2\alpha^{\star}
and
y^{\star}=3-2\beta^{\star}
, then
x^{\star}+y^{\star}=u
and
x^{\star}y^{\star}=-w
. Using the summation formula (
2.2
) with
a=\frac{1-u}{2}
,
b=\frac{1}{2}
and
c=\alpha^{\star}
, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u-w}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star}};1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u-w}\frac{-(\beta^{\star}-1)}{\left(\alpha^{\star}-\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\beta^{\star}-\frac{3}{2}\right)}\left(1-\alpha^{\star}+\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha^{\star}\right)\Gamma\left(\beta^{\star}-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{w}+\frac{1}{w\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-x^{\star}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-y^{\star}}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)}.

Now the result follows from (
2.4
).∎

From (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we see that

\left(1+\sum\limits_{k\geq 2n,n\geq 1}G_{0}(k,n,n)u^{k-2n}w^{n}\right)\left(1+\sum\limits_{k\geq 2n,n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n,n)u^{k-2n}w^{n}\right)=1.

Also, setting
u=0
in (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}t(\{2\}^{n})w^{n}=\exp\left\{\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}t(2n)w^{n}\right\},

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{\star}(\{2\}^{n})w^{n}=\exp\left\{\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}t(2n)w^{n}\right\}.

The above formulas can be deduced from the identities
[
4
,
5
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z_{k}w}=\exp_{\ast}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}z_{nk}w^{n}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{1+z_{k}t}\right)\ast S\left(\frac{1}{1-z_{k}t}\right)=1

in the harmonic shuffle algebra.

2.3
A weighted sum formula

Let
I_{0}(k,n)
be the set of admissible indices of weight
k
and depth
n
, and define

G_{0}(k,n)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n)}t(\mathbf{k}),\quad G_{0}^{\star}(k,n)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}\in I_{0}(k,n)}t^{\star}(\mathbf{k}),

which are the sums of multiple
t
-values and multiple
t
-star values with fixed weight
k
and depth
n
, respectively. By setting
w=uv
and then
v=2u
or
v=-2u
in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following results.

Proposition 2.5
.

For a formal variable
u
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}2^{n-1}G_{0}(k,n)\right)u^{k-2}=\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{k-n-1}2^{n-1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n)\right)u^{k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2u}t(2)\exp\left(\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{4(2^{n-1}-1)}{n(2^{n}-1)}t(n)u^{n}\right).

(2.6)

Proof.
If
w=uv
in Theorem
1.1
, we have

\alpha,\beta=\frac{1-u+v}{2},\frac{1}{2}.

Hence we get the expression for the generating function of sums of multiple
t
-values with fixed weight and depth:

\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}G_{0}(k,n)u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u+v}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right).

Setting
v=2u
, then we have

\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}2^{n-1}G_{0}(k,n)u^{k-2}=\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1+u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right).

Using Dixon’s summation formula
[
8
, 7.4.4.21]

\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left({a,b,c\atop 1+a-b,1+a-c};1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{a}}\frac{\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-b-c\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-b\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{a}{2}-c\right)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)},\quad\Re(a-2b-2c)>-2,

with
a=1
,
b=\frac{1}{2}
and
c=\frac{1+u}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1+u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-u}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3-u}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{u}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1-\frac{u}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{8}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-u}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(1-\frac{u}{2}\right)^{2}}.

Then using (
2.3
), (
2.4
), the relation
\zeta(n)=(1-2^{-n})^{-1}t(n)
and the fact
t(2)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}
, we get

\sum\limits_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}2^{n-1}G_{0}(k,n)\right)u^{k-2}=2^{2u}t(2)\exp\left(\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{4(2^{n-1}-1)}{n(2^{n}-1)}t(n)u^{n}\right).

Similarly, setting
w=uv
in Theorem
1.2
, we have

\alpha^{\star},\beta^{\star}=\frac{3-u-v}{2},\frac{3}{2}.

Hence we find

\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n)u^{k-n-1}v^{n-1}=\frac{1}{1-u-v}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3-u-v}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right).

Let
v=-2u
, then we get

\sum\limits_{k>n\geq 1}(-2)^{n-1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n)u^{k-2}=\frac{1}{1+u}\,_{3}F_{2}\left({\frac{1-u}{2},\frac{1}{2},1\atop\frac{3+u}{2},\frac{3}{2}};1\right).

Now it is easy to finish the proof.∎

Expanding the right-hand side of (

), we get the following weighted sum formula.

Corollary 2.6
.

For any integer
k\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}2^{n-1}G_{0}(k,n)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{k-n-1}2^{n-1}G_{0}^{\star}(k,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}=k-2\atop n,m\geq 0,n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\geq 2}\frac{2^{n+2m}(2^{n_{1}-1}-1)\cdots(2^{n_{m}-1}-1)}{n!m!n_{1}\cdots n_{m}(2^{n_{1}}-1)\cdots(2^{n_{m}}-1)}t(2)t(n_{1})\cdots t(n_{m})\log^{n}2.

3
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are similar as that of Ohno-Zagier relation for multiple zeta values in
[
7
]
.

As in
[
3
]
, for an index
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}(z)=\mathcal{L}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}(z)=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{z^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\star}(z)=\mathcal{L}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}^{\star}(z)=\sum\limits_{m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{n}>0\atop m_{i}:\text{odd}}\frac{z^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{n}^{k_{n}}}.

Then
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\star}(z)
converge absolutely for
|z|<1
. If
k_{1}>1
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}(1)=t(\mathbf{k})
and
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\star}(1)=t^{\star}(\mathbf{k})
. From
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
, we know that

Introduction

Motivated by Banach space geometry
[

,

and Stein’s semigroup theory
[

[

[

,

,
46
]
for symmetric Markovian semigroups. Afterwards, Betancor

developed this theory in some special cases which are not Markovian (cf.
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
3
]
), such as Schrödinger, Hermite, Laguerre semigroups
etc.
, see also
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
24
,
33
,
39
,
22

for related results.
In a recent remarkable paper
[
48
]
, Xu investigated for the first time the vector-valued Littlewood-Paley-Stein inequalities for semigroups of regular contractions
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
on
L^{p}(\Omega)
for a
fixed

1<p<\infty
. That is, for a Banach space
X
of martingale cotype
q
(
2\leq q<\infty
), he showed the Lusin cotype of
X
relative to
\{e^{-t\sqrt{L}}\}_{t>0}
, in other words, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|G_{q,\sqrt{L}}(f)\|_{p}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)},\quad\forall f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\otimes X,

where

G_{q,\sqrt{L}}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\|t\sqrt{L}{e^{-t\sqrt{L}}}(f)(x)\right\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

More importantly, by deeply exploring holomorphic functional calculus, Fendler’s dilation, Calderón-Zygmund theory and Wilson’s intrinsic square functions, he was able to obtain the sharp bounds depending on the martingale cotype constant, and the latter in turn enables him to resolve an open problem posed by Naor and Young
[
32
]
. More precisely, let
\mathsf{L}^{\sqrt{L}}_{c,q,p}(X)
be the least constant
C
in (
1.1
)—the Lusin cotype constant of
X
, and
\mathsf{M}_{c,q}(X)
the martingale cotype
q
constant of
X
, he obtained

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{\sqrt{L}}_{c,q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},p^{\prime}\right\}\mathsf{M}_{c,q}(X)

with the order
\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},p^{\prime}\right\}
being sharp. We refer the reader to Section
6
for the definition of
\mathsf{M}_{c,q}(X)
and the martingale type constant
\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)
.

By duality, the converse inequality of (
1.1
) also holds under the condition that
X
is of martingale type
q
(
1<q\leq 2
)

\|f-\mathbf{F}(f)\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq C\|G_{q,\sqrt{L}}(f)\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\otimes X,

where
\mathbf{F}
is the obvious vector-valued extension of the projection from
L^{p}(\Omega)
onto the fixed point space of
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
, and the resulting
type
bounds satisfy

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}^{\sqrt{L}}_{t,q,p}(X)\lesssim\max\left\{p,p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X).

Nevertheless the order
\max\left\{p,p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}
is now very likely to be suboptimal suggested by the special case
L=\Delta
—the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
q=2
and
X=\mathbb{C}
, where

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{t,2}(\mathbb{C})=1,\ \mathrm{and}\ \sqrt{p}\lesssim\mathsf{L}^{\sqrt{\Delta}}_{t,2,p}(\mathbb{C})\lesssim p,

see for instance
[
47

) when

is essentially equivalent to the fact that

-norm of the classical
g
-function controls that of the Lusin square function, which dominates in turn
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of the function itself; this involves the deep theory of Hardy/BMO spaces. Other than this special case, the problem of determining the optimal order of
\mathsf{L}^{\sqrt{L}}_{t,q,p}(X)
when

in (
1.3

[

has been determined in
[
49
]
for all symmetric Markovian semigroups. However it seems much harder to consider the corresponding problem for a fixed semigroup, and actually the special case

In the present paper, we will determine the optimal order of

Let
L
be a sectorial operator of type
\alpha
(

) on

, and thus it generates a holomorphic semigroup

,

.

When

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Functional calculus

We start with a brief introduction of some preliminaries around the holomorphic functional calculus (cf.
[
30
]
).
Let
0\leq\alpha<\pi
. Define the closed sector in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
as

S_{\alpha}=\left\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\arg z|\leq\alpha\right\},

and
S_{\alpha}^{0}
is denoted as the interior of
S_{\alpha}
.
Let
\gamma>\alpha
and denote by
H(S_{\gamma}^{0})
the space of all holomorphic functions on
S_{\gamma}^{0}
. Define

H_{\infty}(S_{\gamma}^{0})=\left\{b\in H(S_{\gamma}^{0}):\|b\|_{\infty}<\infty\right\},

where
\|b\|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{|b(z)|:z\in S_{\gamma}^{0}\right\}
and

\Psi(S_{\gamma}^{0})=\left\{\psi\in H(S_{\gamma}^{0}):\exists\,s>0\text{ s.t. }|\psi(z)|\leq c|z|^{s}(1+|z|^{2s})^{-1}\right\}.

A densely defined closed operator
L
acting on a Banach space
Y
is called a
sectorial operator of type
\alpha
if for each
\gamma>\alpha
,
\sigma(L)\subset S_{\gamma}
and

\sup\left\{\|z(z{\rm Id}-L)^{-1}\|_{B(Y)}:z\notin S_{\gamma}\right\}<\infty,

where
\|\cdot\|_{B(Y)}
denotes the operator norm and
{\rm Id}
the identity operator.

Assume that
L
is a sectorial operator of type
\alpha
. Let
0\leq\alpha<\theta<\gamma<\pi
and
\Gamma
be the boundary of
S_{\theta}
oriented in the positive sense. For
\psi\in\Psi(S_{\gamma}^{0})
, we define the operator
\psi(L)
as

\psi(L)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\psi(z)(z{\rm Id}-L)^{-1}\,{\rm d}z.

By Cauchy’s theorem, this integral converges absolutely in
B(Y)
and it is clear that the definition is independent of the choice of
\theta
.
For every
t>0
, denote by
\psi_{t}(z)=\psi(tz)
for
z\in S_{\gamma}^{0}
, we have
\psi_{t}\in\Psi(S_{\gamma}^{0})
. Set

h(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tz)\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},\quad z\in S_{\gamma}^{0}.

One gets that
h
is a constant on
S_{\gamma}^{0}
, hence by the convergence lemma (cf.
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
),

h(L)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(tL)x\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=cx,\quad x\in{\mathscr{D}}(L)\cap{\rm im}(L).

By applying a limiting argument, the above identity extends to
\overline{{\rm im}(L)}
. In particular, take
\psi(z)=z^{2}e^{-2z}
, then

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}-tLe^{-tL}(-tLe^{-tL})x\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=\frac{1}{4}x,\quad x\in\overline{{\rm im}(L)},

which will be useful later. We refer the reader to
[
21
]
for more information on functional calculus.

\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
on
{\mathcal{N}}
, namely

\mathbf{F}_{L}=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{N}}:e^{-tL}(f)=f,\ \forall\,t>0\right\}.

It is well-known that
\mathbf{F}_{L}
coincides with the null space of
\{tLe^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
, and the resulting quotient space is defined as
{\mathcal{N}}_{L}:={\mathcal{N}}/\mathbf{F}_{L}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the fixed point subspace of
L^{p}(\real^{d};X)
is
\mathbf{F}_{L}\cap L^{p}(\real^{d};X)=\left\{0\right\}
(see
[
16
, Theorem 6.10]
); in other words, the projection from
L^{p}(\real^{d};X)
to the fixed point subspace for all
1\leq p<\infty
is
0
. See e.g.
[
29
,
48
]
for more information on this projection.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
L^{*}
be the adjoint operator of
L
. Then
L^{*}
is also a sectorial operator with the kernels of
\{e^{-tL^{*}}\}_{t>0}
satisfying (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) (cf.
[
16
, Theorem 6.10]
).

2.3.
Vector-valued tent, Hardy and BMO spaces

In this subsection, we introduce several spaces including vector-valued tent spaces, vector-valued Hardy and BMO spaces associated with a generator
L
.

2.3.1.
Vector-valued Tent spaces

We first introduce vector-valued tent spaces. We denote by
\real{}^{d+1}_{+}
the usual upper half-space in
d+1
i.e.
\real{}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. Let
\Gamma(x)=\{(y,t)\in\real^{d+1}_{+}:|y-x|<t\}
denote the standard cone with vertex at
x
. For any closed subset
F\subset\real^{d}
, define
{\mathcal{R}}(F)=\bigcup_{x\in F}\Gamma(x)
. If
O\subset\real^{d}
is an open subset, then the tent over
O
, denoted by
\widehat{O}
, is given as
\widehat{O}=\left({\mathcal{R}}(O^{C})\right)^{C}
.

For any strongly measurable function
f:\real^{d+1}_{+}\to X
, we define two operators as follows:

{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{\Gamma(x)}\|f(y,t)\|_{X}^{q}\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)(x)=\sup_{x\in B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\widehat{B}}\|f(y,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the supremum runs over all balls
B
in
d
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. The vector-valued tent space
T_{q}^{p}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
is defined as the subspace consisting of all strongly measurable functions
f:\real^{d+1}_{+}\to X
such that

\|f\|_{T_{q}^{p}(X)}:=\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)\|_{p}<\infty,

and
T_{q}^{\infty}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
is defined as the subspace of all strongly measurable functions
g:\real^{d+1}_{+}\to X
such that

\|g\|_{T_{q}^{\infty}(X)}:=\|\mathcal{C}_{q}(g)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Let
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
be the space of finite linear combinations of elements from
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})
and
X
. The following density follows from the standard arguments (see e.g.
[
23
]
), and we omit the details.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. Then

C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
is norm dense in
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and weak-
\ast
dense in
\left(T^{1}_{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})\right)^{*}
.

2.3.2.
Vector-valued Hardy spaces

Given a function
f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{L}
, the
q
-variant of Lusin area integral function of
f
associated with
L
is defined by

S_{q,L}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{\Gamma(x)}\|tLe^{-tL}(f)(y)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}};

and the
q
-variant of Littlewood-Paley
g
-function is defined by

G_{q,L}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\|tLe^{-tL}(f)(x)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. We define the vector-valued Hardy space
H_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)
associated with
L
as

H_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{L}:S_{q,L}(f)\in L^{p}(\real^{d})\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}=\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}.

It is easy to check that
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
is a Banach space from the definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{L}
. The space
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
has deep connection with the vector-valued tent space, namely, a strongly measurable function
f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{L}
belongs to
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
if and only if
\mathcal{Q}(f)\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
where
{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t)=-tLe^{-tL}(f)(x)
. Moreover,

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}=\|{\mathcal{Q}}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}.

2.3.3.
Vector-valued BMO spaces

Definition 2.7
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
1<q<\infty
. We define the vector-valued BMO space
BMO_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)
associated with
L
as

BMO_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{L}:\|\mathcal{C}_{q}({\mathcal{Q}}(f))\|_{p}<\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{BMO_{q,L}^{p}(X)}=\|\mathcal{C}_{q}({\mathcal{Q}}(f))\|_{p}.

In particular, for
p=\infty
, we denote it by
BMO_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
for short.

It is easy to verify that
BMO^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
equipped the the norm
\|\cdot\|_{BMO^{p}_{q,L}(X)}
is a Banach space from the definition of
{\mathcal{N}}_{L}
.

The vector-valued Hardy and BMO spaces enjoy the similar relationship as the scalar-valued ones (see e.g.
[
11
]
). We collect them below with a brief explanation.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. One has for
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
,

(2.5)

\|\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)\|_{p},\quad q<p\leq\infty,

and

(2.6)

\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim q^{\frac{p}{q}}\|\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Therefore, we have for
1\leq p\leq q
,

BMO_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)\subset H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)

and for
q<p<\infty
,

H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)=BMO_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

Given an
X
-valued function
f
defined on
\real{}^{d+1}_{+}
, we consider the scalar-valued function
\widetilde{f}(x,t)=\|f(x,t)\|_{X}
. Then one may apply (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) in the case
X=\mathbb{C}
for
\widetilde{f}
(see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 3]
) to obtain (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) for general
X
. Thus by using the operator
\mathcal{Q}
and the density in Lemma
2.5
, for any
f\in BMO_{q,L}^{p}(\real^{d};X)
(
1\leq p\leq q
), we get

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}=\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}({\mathcal{Q}}(f))\|_{p}\lesssim q^{\frac{p}{q}}\|\mathcal{C}_{q}({\mathcal{Q}}(f))\|_{p}=\|f\|_{BMO_{q,L}^{p}(X)},

and the same argument works for
q<p<\infty
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

In particular,
BMO_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
is closely related to the Carleson measure. Recall that a scalar-valued measure
\mu
defined on
\real{}^{d+1}_{+}
is a Carleson measure if there exists a constant
c
such that for all balls
B
in
d
,

|\mu(\widehat{B})|\leq c|B|,

where
\widehat{B}
is the tent over
B
. The norm is defined as

\|\mu\|_{c}=\sup_{B}|B|^{-1}|\mu(\widehat{B})|,

where the supremum runs over all the balls in
d
.

For a vector-valued function
f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{L}
, we define a measure
\mu_{q,f}
as

\mu_{q,f}(x,t)=\frac{\|{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}.

Then
f
belongs to
BMO_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
if and only if
\mu_{q,f}
is a Carleson measure, and moreover

\|f\|_{BMO_{q,L}(X)}=\|\mu_{q,f}\|^{\frac{1}{q}}_{c}.

3.
Theory of vector-valued tent spaces and two key linear operators

In this section, we will first present the basic theory of vector-valued tent spaces such as atomic decomposition, interpolation and duality, and then introduce two important linear operators
\mathcal{K}
and
\pi_{L}
which enable us to exploit the basic theory of tent spaces to investigate in later sections vector-valued Wilson’s square functions and Theorem
1.1
.

Note that if the underlying Banach space
X
has some geometric property such as UMD, then the vector-valued tent space theory have been established in the literature
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
. In the present paper, we observe that the theory of vector-valued tent space holds for any Banach space; and this is quite essential for the applications in the present paper.

3.1.
Basic theory of vector-valued tent spaces

We begin this subsection by presenting the atomic decomposition of tent space in the context of vector-valued context. It has been established in
[
27
, Theorem 4.5]
, for the completeness of this article, we will attach the proof. Recall that a strongly measurable function
a:\real^{d+1}_{+}\to X
is called an
(X,q)
-atom if

(1)

\text{supp}\,a\subset\widehat{B}
where
B
is a ball in
d
;

(2)

\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|a(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq|B|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. For each
f\in T_{q}^{1}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
, there exists a sequence of complex numbers
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
(X,q)
-atoms
a_{k}
such that

f=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k},\quad\|f\|_{T_{q}^{1}(X)}\approx\sum_{k\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}|.

Proof.

Let
a
be an
(X,q)
-atom and
\text{supp}\,a\subset\widehat{B}
where
B=B(c_{B},r_{B})
with center
c_{B}
and radius
r_{B}
. If
\Gamma(x)\cap\widehat{B}\neq\emptyset
, there exists
(y,t)\in\Gamma(x)\cap\widehat{B}
. Then we have
|x-c_{B}|\leq|x-y|+|y-c_{B}|<t+r_{B}<2r_{B}
. By Hölder’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem,

\|a\|_{T^{1}_{q}(X)}=\int_{2B}\left(\int_{\Gamma(x)}\|a(y,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}{\rm d}x\lesssim|2B|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|a(y,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim 1.

Therefore any
(X,q)
-atom belongs to
T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
.

Let
0<\lambda<1/2
. We define two sequences of open sets
\left\{O_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\left\{O_{k}^{*}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
as

O_{k}=\left\{x\in\real^{d}:{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)(x)>2^{k}\right\},\quad O_{k}^{*}=\left\{x\in\real^{d}:M(\mathbbm{1}_{O_{k}})(x)>1-\lambda\right\},

where
M(\mathbbm{1}_{O_{k}})
is the centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. It is clear that both
O_{k}
and
O^{*}_{k}
have finite measure. Additionally, the following properties hold:
O_{k+1}\subset O_{k}
,
O^{*}_{k+1}\subset O^{*}_{k}
and
|O^{*}_{k}|\leq C_{\lambda}|O_{k}|
(see e.g.
[
11
]
).

We follow a similar construction as in
[
27
]
. The Vitali covering lemma and
[
28
, Lemma 4.2]
assert that for each
O_{k}^{*}
, there exist disjoint balls
B_{k}^{j}\subset O_{k}^{*}
(
j\geq 1
) such that

\widehat{O}^{*}_{k}\subset\bigcup_{j\geq 1}\widehat{5B}_{k}^{j},\quad\sum_{j\geq 1}|B_{k}^{j}|\leq|O_{k}^{*}|.

With this setup, we proceed to define a family of functions
\chi_{k}^{j}
by the partition of unity:

0\leq\chi_{k}^{j}\leq 1,\ \sum_{j\geq 1}\chi_{k}^{j}=1\text{ on }\widehat{O}_{k}^{*}\ \text{ and }\ \text{supp}\,\chi_{k}^{j}\subset\widehat{5B}_{k}^{j}.

Therefore

f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f_{k}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\chi_{k}^{j}f_{k}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{j}a_{k}^{j},

where

(3.1)

f_{k}=f\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{O}^{*}_{k}\setminus\widehat{O}^{*}_{k+1}},\ \lambda_{k}^{j}=|5B_{k}^{j}|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{5B_{k}^{j}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})^{q}(x)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\ a_{k}^{j}=\frac{\chi_{k}^{j}f_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{j}}.

Now we only need to show that each
a_{k}^{j}
is an
(X,q)
-atom and

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}^{j}|\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{1}_{q}(X)}.

It is clear that
\text{supp}\,a_{k}^{j}\subset\widehat{5B}_{k}^{j}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|a\|_{L^{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq|5B_{k}^{j}|^{1-q}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})\mathbbm{1}_{5B_{k}^{j}}\|_{q}^{-q}\left(\int_{\widehat{5B}_{k}^{j}}\|f_{k}(y,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|5B_{k}^{j}|^{1-q}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})\mathbbm{1}_{5B_{k}^{j}}\|_{q}^{-q}\left(\int_{5B_{k}^{j}}\left({\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})(x)\right)^{q}\,{\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle=|5B_{k}^{j}|^{1-q}.

Hence each
a_{k}^{j}
is an
(X,q)
-atom.

According to
[
11
, Lemma 5]
, it is known that
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})
is supported in
O^{*}_{k}\setminus O_{k+1}
, then we deduce that
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f_{k})(x)\leq 2^{k+1}
by definition. Thus

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}^{j}|\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{j\geq 1}|5B_{k}^{j}|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}2^{k+1}|5B_{k}^{j}|^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k+1}|O_{k}^{*}|\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k+1}C_{\lambda}|O_{k}|.

However,
{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)(x)>2^{(k+m)}
on
O_{k+m}
, then

2^{k}|O_{k}|=\int_{O_{k}}2^{k}\,{\rm d}x=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{O_{k+m}\setminus O_{k+m+1}}2^{k}\,{\rm d}x\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m}\int_{O_{k+m}\setminus O_{k+m+1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Hence

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k+1}C_{\lambda}|O_{k}|\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-m+1}C_{\lambda}\int_{O_{k+m}\setminus O_{k+m+1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)(x)\,{\rm d}x\lesssim\|f\|_{T^{1}_{q}(X)}.

We complete the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

From the atomic decomposition of
T^{1}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{1};X)
—Lemma
3.1
, one may conclude a molecule decomposition of the corresponding Hardy space. This might have further applications, and we include it in the Appendix.

The following lemma is the complex interpolation theory of vector-valued tent spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space,
1<q<\infty
and
1\leq p_{1}<p<p_{2}<\infty
such that
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{1}+\theta/p_{2}
with
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Then

[T^{p_{1}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)]_{\theta}=T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),

with equivalent norms, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
is the complex interpolation space. More precisely, for
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, one has

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{[T^{p_{1}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)]_{\theta}}\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}.

Proof.

For the interpolation theory, we introduce two important operators, which allow us to relate
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
with
L^{p}(\real^{d};E)
with
E
being the Banach space
L^{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
equipped with the measure
{\rm d}x{\rm d}t/t^{d+1}
. The first operator is defined as

i(f)(x,y,t)=\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma(x)}(y,t)f(y,t),

for
f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
. Then it is clear that
\|i(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}
. Denote by
\widetilde{T}_{q}^{p}
the range of the operator
i
. Now we introduce another operator
N
given by

N(f)(x,y,t)=\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma(x)}(y,t)\frac{1}{w_{d}t^{d}}\int_{|z-y|<t}f(z,y,t)\,{\rm d}z,

where
w_{d}
is the volume of the
d
-dimensional unit ball. It is known that
N
is a continuous projection from
L^{p}(\real^{d};E)
onto itself with range
\widetilde{T}_{q}^{p}
for
1<p<\infty
(cf.
[
23
]
). Consider the maximal operator

M_{1}(f)(x,y,t)=\sup_{x\in B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\|f(z,y,t)\|_{X}\,{\rm d}z,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
d
. It is known from the maximal inequalities (see e.g.
[
38
, Chapter II]
) that
M_{1}
is bounded on
L^{p}\left(\real{}^{d};L^{q}(\real^{d+1};{\rm d}y{\rm d}t/t^{d+1})\right)
for
1<p<\infty
; in particular, we view
\|f\|_{X}
as a scalar-valued function in
L^{p}\left(\real{}^{d};L^{q}(\real^{d+1};{\rm d}y{\rm d}t/t^{d+1})\right)
, then

\|M_{1}(f)\|_{L^{p}\left(\real{}^{d};L^{q}\left(\real{}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)\right)}=\|M_{1}(\|f\|_{X})\|_{L^{p}\left(\real{}^{d};L^{q}\left(\real{}^{d+1}_{+},\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)\right)}\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)},\quad q\leq p<\infty.

Then we deduce from the definition of
N
that

\|N(f)(x,y,t)\|_{X}\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma(x)}(y,t)\frac{1}{|B(y,t)|}\int_{B(y,t)}\|f(z,y,t)\|_{X}\,{\rm d}z\leq M_{1}(f)(x,y,t).

Therefore

\|N(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq\|M_{1}(f)\|_{L^{p}\left(\real{}^{d};L^{q}\left(\real{}^{d+1}_{+};\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)\right)}\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)},\quad q\leq p<\infty.

We denote by
F
the Banach space
L^{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})
equipped with the measure
{\rm d}x{\rm d}t/t^{d+1}
. Then it is clear that
F\subset E^{*}
and
F
is norming for
E
. For
1<p<q
, we have

\displaystyle\|N(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle N(f)(x,y,t),g(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(x,y,t),N(g)(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\|N(g)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(F)}\lesssim p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(F)},

where the supremum is taken over all
g
in the unit ball of
L^{p^{\prime}}(\real^{d};F)
. We conclude

(3.2)

\|N(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\|f\|_{L^{p}(E)},\quad 1<p<\infty.

Now we turn to the interpolation theory. The proof of the case
1<p_{1}<p_{2}<\infty
follows from
[
23
]
by virtue of the immersion
i
and the projection
N
.

For the case
p_{1}=1
, we adapt the classical argument as in
[
11
, Lemma 4, Lemma 5]
. Since the immersion
i
is an isometry, the exactness of the exponent
\theta
of complex interpolation functor reads that

\displaystyle\|i(f)\|_{[L^{1}(\real^{d};E),L^{p_{2}}(\real^{d};E)]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|i\|_{T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)\to L^{1}(\real^{d};E)}^{1-\theta}\|i\|_{T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)\to L^{p_{2}}(\real^{d};E)}^{\theta}\|f\|_{[T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)]_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{[T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)]_{\theta}}.

By the interpolation theory of vector-valued
L^{p}
spaces, (see e.g.
[
9
]
), we have

\|i(f)\|_{[L^{1}(\real^{d};E),L^{p_{2}}(\real^{d};E)]_{\theta}}=\|i(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}.

Thus

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\leq\|f\|_{[T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),T^{p_{2}}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)]_{\theta}}.

For the reverse direction, let
f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
and
\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}=1
. By taking into account the atomic decomposition of
T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
—Lemma
3.1
, we define the interpolation functor
F
as

F(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{k(\alpha(z)p-1)}f_{k},

where
\alpha(z)=1-z+z/p_{2}
and
f_{k}
is defined in (
3.1
). We have
F(\theta)=f
. Then the proof can be then conducted in the same way as in
[
11
, Lemma 5]
, we omit the details.
∎

We now provide a characterization of
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
-norm. It belongs to the norming subspace theory of vector-valued
L^{p}
-spaces, see e.g.
[
15
, Chapter II, Section 4]
. The proof is in spirit the same as the scalar-valued case (cf.
[
23
, Theorem 2.4]
and
[
11
, Theorem 1]
, but we include a proof here to provide explicit orders for later applications.

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mu)
be a measure space. Recall that a Banach space
X
has the
Radon-Nikodým property
with respect to
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mu)
if for each
\mu
-continuous vector-valued measure
\nu:{\mathcal{F}}\to X
of bounded variation, there exists
g\in L^{1}(\Omega;X)
with respect to the measure
\mu
such that

\nu(E)=\int_{E}g\,{\rm d}\mu,\quad\forall\,E\in{\mathcal{F}}.

In the following context, we call a Banach space has the Radon-Nikodým property for short when there is no ambiguity. We refer readers to
[
14
, Chapter III]
for more details.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. The space

T^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})
is isomorphically identified as a subspace of the dual space of
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
. Moreover, it is norming for
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
in the following sense,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(x,t),g(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|,\quad 1<p<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
such that
\|\mathcal{A}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
; and similarly,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(x,t),g(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|,\quad 1\leq p<q,

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
such that
\|\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
.
Furthermore, if
X^{*}
has the Radon-Nikodým property, then

T^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})=\left(T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)\right)^{*},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Proof.

We adopt the maps
i
and
N
used in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. We first prove the estimate (
3.3
).

For any
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
and
f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
, we denote by

g(f)=\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(x,t),g(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}.

Thus we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}|g(f)|&=\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(y,t),g(y,t)\left(w_{d}^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<t}1\,{\rm d}x\right)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right|\\
&=w_{d}^{-1}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle i(f)(x,y,t),i(g)(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|.\end{split}

Since
i(f)\in\widetilde{T}^{p}_{q}
, we have
N(i(f))=i(f)
. Then we deduce that

(3.6)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}&=\|i(f)\|_{L^{p}(E)}=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle i(f)(x,y,t),g(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|\\
&=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle N(i(f))(x,y,t),g(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|\\
&=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle i(f)(x,y,t),N(g)(x,y,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}x\right|\end{split}

where the supremum is taken over all
g
in the unit ball of
L^{p^{\prime}}(\real^{d};F)
. Notice that
N(g)=i\left(i^{-1}(N(g))\right)
and by (
3.2
)

\|i^{-1}(N(g))\|_{T^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}}(X^{*})}=\|N(g)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(F)}\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},{p^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(F)}.

Consequently, combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}},{p^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\sup_{g}|g(f)|,\quad 1<p<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
such that
\|\mathcal{A}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
, and we actually exploit a limiting argument: since not only the subset of
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
with norm
\|\mathcal{A}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
is contained in the unit ball of
T^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})
, but also its closure contains the unit sphere, and thus one concludes that this subset is still norming for
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
.

Now we deal with the estimate (
3.4
) in the case
1<p<q
. Let
g\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\real^{d};F)
. By definition we have

\displaystyle\|i^{-1}(N(g))(y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B(y,t)|}\int_{B(y,t)}\|g(z,y,t)\|_{X^{*}}\,{\rm d}z\right)^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B(y,t)|}\int_{|z-y|<t}\|g(z,y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}z.

For a ball
B
in
d
, we observe

\displaystyle\int_{\widehat{B}}\|i^{-1}(N(g))(y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\widehat{B}}\int_{|z-y|<t}\|g(z,y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}z\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2B}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{B}}(y,t)g(z,y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{2B}H^{q^{\prime}}(z)\,{\rm d}z,

where

H(z)=\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{B}}(y,t)g(z,y,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Then we have

\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[i^{-1}(N(g))\right](x)\lesssim\left({\mathcal{M}}(H^{q^{\prime}})(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

where
{\mathcal{M}}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator. Therefore when
q^{\prime}<p^{\prime}\leq\infty
, we obtain

\|\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[i^{-1}(N(g))\right]\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|{\mathcal{M}}(H^{q^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|H\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(F)}.

Thus we observe

\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{g}|g(f)|,\quad 1<p<q,

with the supremum being taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X^{*}
such that
\|\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
.

For the endpoint case
p=1
of (
3.4
), because of the failure of vector-valued Calderón-Zygmund theory, the above arguments adapted from
[
23
, Theorem 2.4]
do not work any more. Instead, by using the atomic decomposition of
T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
—Lemma
3.1
, one may carry out the classical arguments as in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
in the present vector-valued setting, and we leave the details to the interested reader.

When the Banach space
X^{*}
has the Radon-Nikodým property, one gets
F=E^{*}
(cf.
[
25
, Theorem 1.3.10]
). Then the duality follows from then an analogous argument in
[
23
]
for
1<p<\infty
. Again, the duality in the case
p=1
can be deduced as in the scalar-valued case
[
11
, Theorem 1]
, and we leave the details to the interested reader.
∎

3.2.
The two linear operators
\mathcal{K}
and
\pi_{L}

Let
{\mathcal{K}}:\real^{d+1}_{+}\times\real^{d+1}_{+}\to\real
be a reasonable real-valued function such that for
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, the linear operator
{\mathcal{K}}
is well defined as below,

{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t):=\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(x,y)f(y,s)\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}s}{s}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. Assume that the kernel
{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(x,y)
satisfies the following estimation: there exist positive constants
\kappa
,
\varepsilon
,
C
such that

(3.7)

|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{\varepsilon}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(1+\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}|x-y|\right)^{d+\kappa}}.

Then the linear operator
{\mathcal{K}}
initially defined on
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
extends to a bounded linear operator on
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
. More precisely,

\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)},\quad\forall\,f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),\ 1\leq p<\infty.

Furthermore, for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, we have

\|\mathcal{C}_{q}\left({\mathcal{K}}(f)\right)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\|\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Proof.

Fix
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
\kappa<\varepsilon
from (
3.7
).
We first deal with the case
p=q
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)f(w,s)\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|\|f(w,s)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t}.

We obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\real{}^{d}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{Cs^{\varepsilon}t^{-\varepsilon}t^{-d}}{\left(1+t^{-1}|y-w|\right)^{d+\kappa}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{Ct^{\varepsilon}s^{-\varepsilon}s^{-d}}{\left(1+s^{-1}|y-w|\right)^{d+\kappa}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{Ct^{-d}}{\left(1+t^{-1}|y-w|\right)^{d+\kappa}}\,{\rm d}w+\int_{t}^{\infty}t^{\varepsilon}s^{-\varepsilon-1}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}1.

It is clear that in the assumption of
{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)
,
(w,s)
plays the same role as
(y,t)
. Thus

(3.8)

\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)}^{q}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|f(w,s)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}=\|f\|_{L^{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)}^{q}.

Then the case
p=q
is done since
\|f\|_{T^{q}_{q}(X)}\approx{\|f\|_{L^{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)}}
. Moreover, from the proof we observe that
{\mathcal{K}}
is always bounded on
L^{p}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

For
1\leq p<q
, by the interpolation—Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to show the case
p=1
. By the atomic decomposition—Lemma
3.1
, It suffices to show that

(3.9)

\|{\mathcal{K}}(a)\|_{T^{1}_{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}1,

where
a
is an
(X,q)
-atom with
\text{supp}\,a\subset\widehat{B}
and
B=B(c_{B},r_{B})
.
One can write

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)]\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{4B}{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)](x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{(4B)^{C}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)](x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=I+II.

From (
3.8
) we obtain

(3.10)

\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)]\|_{q}^{q}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\|a(w,s)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\leq|B|^{1-q}.

Then we can estimate the term
I
:

(3.11)

I\leq|4B|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)]\|_{q}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}1.

Now we handle the second term
II
.
By Hölder’s inequality, we observe

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)](x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|y-x|<t}\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\cdot\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}\|a(w,s)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|B|^{1-q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|y-x|<t}\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}

\displaystyle=|B|^{1-q}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\int_{|y-x|<t}\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}

\displaystyle+|B|^{1-q}\int_{r_{B}}^{\infty}\int_{|y-x|<t}\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

When
x\in(4B)^{C},\ w\in B
, we have

r_{B}<|x-w|\leq|x-y|+|y-w|<t+|y-w|,

hence

|x-c_{B}|\leq|x-w|+|w-c_{B}|<2(t+|y-w|)\leq 2(\max\left\{t,s\right\}+|y-w|).

Therefore we observe from (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{K}}_{t,s}(y,w)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{\varepsilon}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(\max\left\{t,s\right\}+|y-w|\right)^{d+\kappa}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d+\kappa}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{\varepsilon}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{-\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{d+\kappa}}=\frac{\min\left\{s^{\varepsilon}t^{\kappa-\varepsilon},t^{\varepsilon}s^{\kappa-\varepsilon}\right\}}{|x-c_{B}|^{d+\kappa}}.

Then

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{|B|^{1-q}|B|^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}}{|x-c_{B}|^{q(d+\kappa)}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\left(\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\min\left\{s^{q^{\prime}\varepsilon}t^{q^{\prime}(\kappa-\varepsilon)},t^{q^{\prime}\varepsilon}s^{q^{\prime}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}\right\}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x-c_{B}|^{q(d+\kappa)}}\int_{0}^{r_{B}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}t^{q^{\prime}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}s^{q^{\prime}\varepsilon}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}+\int_{t}^{r_{B}}s^{q^{\prime}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}t^{q^{\prime}\varepsilon}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{r_{B}^{q\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{q(d+\kappa)}}.

For
J_{2}
, since
t\geq r_{B}\geq s
, and
|x-c_{B}|<2(t+|y-w|)
,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{|B|^{1-q}|B|^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}}{|x-c_{B}|^{q(d+\kappa)}}\int_{r_{B}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r_{B}}s^{q^{\prime}\varepsilon}t^{q^{\prime}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}\right)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{r_{B}^{q\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{q(d+\kappa)}}.

Thus

{\mathcal{A}}_{q}[{\mathcal{K}}(a)](x)\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\frac{r_{B}^{\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{d+\kappa}},\quad x\in(4B)^{C}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{(4B)^{C}}\frac{r_{B}^{\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{d+\kappa}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{m+1}B\setminus 2^{m}B}\frac{r_{B}^{\kappa}}{|x-c_{B}|^{d+\kappa}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{m+1}B}\frac{r_{B}^{\kappa}}{2^{m(d+\kappa)}r_{B}^{d+\kappa}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{m+1}r_{B})^{d}}{2^{m(d+\kappa)}r_{B}^{d}}\lesssim\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}2^{-m\kappa}\lesssim_{\kappa}1,

we obtain

(3.12)

II\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}1.

For the case
q<p<+\infty
, we denote by
{\mathcal{K}}^{*}
the adjoint operator. It is clear that the kernel of
{\mathcal{K}}^{*}
has the same estimation as that of
{\mathcal{K}}
. For
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, we obtain from Lemma
3.4
that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t),g(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle=p^{\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle f(x,t),{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\|{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(g)\|_{T^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}}(X^{*})}\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)},

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
such that
\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q^{\prime}}(g)\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq 1
. Consequently, we observe that
{\mathcal{K}}
extends to a bounded linear operator on
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
. More precisely,

\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)},\quad\forall\,f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X).

Now we prove the second assertion of this lemma. Fix
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, take a ball
B
in
d
, we can write

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\widehat{B}}\left\langle{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t),g(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\widehat{B}}\left\langle f(x,t),{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g}\|{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(g)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\widehat{B};X^{*})}\left(\int_{\widehat{B}}\|f(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the supremum is taken over all
g
in the unit ball of
L^{q^{\prime}}(\widehat{B};X^{*})
. From (
3.8
) we know that

\|{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(g)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X^{*})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\widehat{B};X^{*})}.

Thus for any
x\in\real^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{C}_{q}\left[{\mathcal{K}}(f)\right](x)\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\widehat{B}}\|{\mathcal{K}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\sup_{x\in B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{\widehat{B}}\|f(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}=\left(\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)(x)\right)^{q}.

Therefore we obtain

\|\mathcal{C}_{q}\left[{\mathcal{K}}(f)\right]\|_{p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\|\mathcal{C}_{q}\left(f\right)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Moreover, from (
2.5
) we also observe

\|\mathcal{C}_{q}\left[{\mathcal{K}}(f)\right]\|_{p}\lesssim_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{q}(f)\|_{p},\quad q<p\leq\infty.

The proof is completed.
∎

Now we come to the second important linear operator, which will relate the tent space
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
to the Hardy space
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
.

Recall the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t)=-tLe^{-tL}(f)(x)
. Define the operator
\pi_{L}
acting on
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
as

\pi_{L}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}(f(\cdot,t))(x,t)\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t},\quad\forall\,x\in\real^{d}.

It is easy to verify that
\pi_{L}
is well-defined.
The following lemma asserts that
\pi_{L}
extends to a bounded linear operator from
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
to
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
. We will denote it by
\pi_{L}
as well.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. The operator
\pi_{L}
initially defined on
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
extends to a bounded linear operator from
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
to
H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
. More precisely,

\|\pi_{L}(f)\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}\lesssim_{\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)},\quad\forall\,f\in T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X),\ 1\leq p<\infty.

Furthermore, for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
, we have

\|\pi_{L}(f)\|_{BMO^{p}_{q,L}(X)}\lesssim_{\beta}\|\mathcal{C}_{q}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
. Recall that
k(t,x,y)
is the kernel of the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}
, then

(3.13)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{Q}}[\pi_{L}(f)](x,t)&=\int_{\real{}^{d}}k(t,y,z)\pi_{L}(f)(z)\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\int_{\real{}^{d}}k(t,y,z)\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}k(s,z,w)f(w,s)\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left(\int_{\real{}^{d}}k(t,y,z)k(s,z,w)\,{\rm d}z\right)f(w,s)\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}.\end{split}

We denote by

\Phi_{t,s}(y,w)=\int_{\real{}^{d}}k(t,y,z)k(s,z,w)\,{\rm d}z.

Note that
k(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is the kernel of the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}=-te^{-tL}
, thus
\Phi_{t,s}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the kernel of
-tLe^{-tL}\circ(-sLe^{-sL})=tsL^{2}e^{-(t+s)L}
. On the other hand,
\partial^{2}_{r}(e^{-rL})|_{r=t+s}=L^{2}e^{-(t+s)L}
which has the kernel
\partial^{2}_{r}K(r,\cdot,\cdot)|_{r=t+s}
. Then by (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\Phi_{t,s}(y,w)|\lesssim_{d,\beta}\frac{ts}{(t+s)^{2-\beta}(t+s+|y-w|)^{d+\beta}}\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(1+\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}|x-y|\right)^{d+\beta}}.

Denote by

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=4\mathcal{Q}\circ\pi_{L}.

From Lemma
3.5
, we conclude that
\mathcal{P}
initially defined on
C_{c}(\real^{d+1}_{+})\otimes X
extends to a bounded linear operator on
T^{p}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
. Moreover,

\left\|\mathcal{P}(f)\right\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|f\right\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}.

Therefore

\|\pi_{L}(f)\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}}=4^{-1}\|\mathcal{P}(f)\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{T^{p}_{q}(X)},

which is the desired assertion.

For the second part, we obtain the desired assertion from Lemma
3.5
immediately.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

One can verify that
\mathcal{P}\circ\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}
, thus
\mathcal{P}
serves as a continuous projection from
T^{p}_{q}\left(\real{}^{d+1}_{+};X\right)
onto itself. Indeed, we can also obtain this lemma under the assumption that
L
is a sectotrial operator satisfying only (
2.3
).

4.
vector-valued intrinsic square functions

In this section, we begin with the introduction of vector-valued intrinsic square functions, originally presented by Wilson in
[
42
]
in the case of convolution operators. We then proceed to compare them with the
q
-variant of Lusin area integral associated with a generator
L
.

Recall that
L
is assumed to be a sectorial operator of type
\alpha
(
0\leq\alpha<\pi/2
) satisfying assumptions (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) with
\beta>0,0<\gamma\leq 1
. Define
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
as the family of functions
\varphi:\real^{d}\times\real^{d}\to\real
such that

(4.1)

|\varphi(x,y)|\leq\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}},

(4.2)

|\varphi(x+h,y)-\varphi(x,y)|+|\varphi(x,y+h)-\varphi(x,y)|\leq\frac{|h|^{\gamma}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta+\gamma}}

whenever
2|h|\leq 1+|x-y|
and

(4.3)

\int_{\real{}^{d}}\varphi(x,y)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\real{}^{d}}\varphi(x,y)\,{\rm d}y=0.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
, define
\varphi_{t}(x,y)=t^{-d}\varphi(t^{-1}x,t^{-1}y)
.

Let
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
. We define

A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(x,t)=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}\left\|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\varphi_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,{\rm d}y\right\|_{X},\quad\forall\,(x,t)\in\real^{d+1}_{+}.

Then the intrinsic square functions of
f
are defined as

S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{\Gamma(x)}\left(A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(y,t)\right)^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}t}{t^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and

G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(x,t)\right)^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space,
1<q<\infty
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
L
be any fixed sectorial operator
L
satisfying (
1.5
), (
1.6
) and (
1.7
). For any
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes X
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)\approx_{\gamma,\beta}G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x),

(4.5)

\displaystyle S_{q,L}(f)(x)\lesssim S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x),\quad G_{q,L}(f)(x)\lesssim G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x),

and

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}.

Remark 4.2
.

The following
g
-function version of (
4.6
) holds also

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}{p^{\frac{2}{q}}}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}.

But its proof is much more involved and depends in turn on Theorem
1.1
that will be concluded in the next section.

As in the classical case
[
41
]
, the assertions (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) can be deduced easily from the following facts on
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
. The following properties hold:

(i)
if
t\geq 1
, then
t^{-d-\gamma}\varphi_{t}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
;

(ii)
if
|z|\leq 1
,
t\geq 1
, then
(2t)^{-d-\gamma-\beta}\left(\varphi^{(z)}\right)_{t}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
, where
\varphi^{(z)}(x,y)=\varphi(x-z,y)
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case of Wilson
[
41
]
, while the present setting is non-convolutive, let us give the sketch. The claim (i) is easy by definition. For the claim (ii), notice that

2^{-1}(1+|x-y|)\leq 1+|(x-z)-y|\leq 2(1+|x-y|).

By definition, we have

|\varphi^{(z)}(x,y)|=|\varphi(x-z,y)|\leq\frac{1}{(1+|(x-z)-y|)^{d+\beta}}\leq\frac{2^{d+\beta}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}.

and

\displaystyle|\varphi^{(z)}(x+h,y)-\varphi^{(z)}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|\varphi(x-z+h,y)-\varphi(x-z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|h|^{\gamma}}{(1+|(x-z)-y|)^{d+\beta+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{d+\beta+\gamma}|h|^{\gamma}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta+\gamma}}.

The same Hölder continuity estimation holds for the variable
y
. Thus we obtain
2^{-d-\beta-\gamma}\varphi^{(z)}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
. Then the claim (ii) follows from the claim (i).
∎

With Lemma
4.3
, the assertions (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) will follow easily.
The most challenging part of Theorem
4.4
lies in (
4.6
). In addition to the interpolation and duality theory on the (vector-valued) tent space that have been built in Section
3
, the following pointwise estimate is another technical part in the proof of estimate (
4.6
).

Recall that
k(t,x,y)
is the kernel of the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}
. Let
{\theta\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}
, define

{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)=\int_{\real{}^{d}}\theta_{t}(y,z)k(s,z,w)\,{\rm d}z.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\nu=2^{-1}\min\left\{\gamma,\beta\right\}
and
\zeta=(d+2^{-1}\beta)(d+\beta)^{-1}
, then

\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{(1-\zeta)\nu}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(1+\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}|y-w|\right)^{d+\frac{1}{2}\beta}}.

Proof.

To estimate the kernel
{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)
, we follow a similar argument presented in
[
20
, Chapter 8, 8.6.3]
.

Let
\theta\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
, we have

(4.8)

|\theta_{t}(y,z)|\leq\frac{t^{-d}}{(1+t^{-1}|y-z|)^{d+\beta}},\quad\forall\,y,z\in\real^{d},\ t>0.

For
2|z-z^{\prime}|<t+|y-z|
, we have

t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|<1+t^{-1}|y-z|
, then

\displaystyle|\theta_{t}(y,z)-\theta_{t}(y,z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{-d-\gamma}|z-z^{\prime}|^{\gamma}}{(1+t^{-1}|y-z|)^{d+\beta+\gamma}}\leq\frac{\min\left\{(t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|)^{\gamma},\left(1+t^{-1}|y-z|\right)^{\gamma}\right\}}{t^{d}(1+t^{-1}|y-z|)^{d+\beta+\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\min\left\{1,(t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{t^{d}}.

For
2|z-z^{\prime}|\geq t+|y-z|
, we have
t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|\geq 1/2
, then

|\theta_{t}(y,z)-\theta_{t}(y,z^{\prime})|\leq|\theta_{t}(y,z)|+|\theta_{t}(y,z^{\prime})|\leq 2t^{-d}\lesssim\frac{\min\left\{1,(t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{t^{d}}.

Hence

|\theta_{t}(y,z)-\theta_{t}(y,z^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\min\left\{1,(t^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{t^{d}},\quad\forall\,y,z,z^{\prime}\in\real^{d},\ t>0.

On the other hand, Lemma
2.1
asserts that there exists a positive constant
C_{k}
such that
C_{k}^{-1}(k(s,\cdot,\cdot))_{s^{-1}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
(see also the Convention afterwards). Thus, one gets for all
w,z,z^{\prime}\in\real^{d},\ s>0
,

|k(s,z,w)|\lesssim\frac{C_{k}s^{-d}}{(1+s^{-1}|z-w|)^{d+\beta}},\quad|k(s,z,w)-k(s,z^{\prime},w)|\lesssim\frac{C_{k}\min\left\{1,(s^{-1}|z-z^{\prime}|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{s^{d}}.

Now we start to deal with the kernel
{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)
. By symmetry, it suffices to handle the case
s\leq t
. First we observe the following estimate,

\displaystyle\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{s^{-d}\min\left\{1,(t^{-1}|u|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{(1+s^{-1}|u|)^{d+\beta}}\,{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{|u|<t}\frac{s^{-d}(t^{-1}|u|)^{\gamma}}{(1+s^{-1}|u|)^{d+\beta}}\,{\rm d}u+\int_{|u|>t}\frac{s^{-d}}{(1+s^{-1}|u|)^{d+\beta}}\,{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|v|<t/s}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\gamma}\frac{|v|^{\gamma}}{(1+|v|)^{d+\beta}}\,{\rm d}v+\int_{|u|>t}s^{\beta}|u|^{-d-\beta}\,{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}

Taking
\nu=2^{-1}\min\left\{\gamma,\beta\right\}
, and we have
|v|^{\gamma}<(t/s)^{\gamma-\nu}|v|^{\nu}
. Then we obtain

J_{1}\leq\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\nu}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{|v|^{\nu}}{(1+|v|)^{d+\beta}}\,{\rm d}v\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\nu}.

For
J_{2}
, we have

J_{2}\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{\beta}r^{-\beta-1}\,{\rm d}r\lesssim_{\beta}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\beta}\leq\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\nu}.

Thus for
s\leq t
, by the vanishing property (
4.3
) of
k(s,\cdot,w)
, one gets

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left[\theta_{t}(y,z)-\theta_{t}(y,w)\right]k(s,z,w)\,{\rm d}z\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\frac{\min\left\{1,(t^{-1}|z-w|)^{\gamma}\right\}}{t^{d}}\frac{s^{-d}}{(1+s^{-1}|z-w|)}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}t^{-d}\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)^{\nu}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{\nu}.

On the other hand,

|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|\leq\int_{\real{}^{d}}|\theta_{t}(y,z)||k(s,z,w)|\,{\rm d}z\lesssim_{\beta}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(1+\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}|y-w|\right)^{d+\beta}}.

Let
\zeta=(d+2^{-1}\beta)(d+\beta)^{-1}
, we then get

|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|=|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{1-\zeta}|{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)|^{\zeta}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\frac{\min\left\{\frac{s}{t},\frac{t}{s}\right\}^{(1-\zeta)\nu}\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}^{d}}{\left(1+\min\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\right\}|y-w|\right)^{d+\frac{1}{2}\beta}}.

It is clear that the estimation of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\theta}_{t,s}(y,w)
is independent of the choice of
\theta
, and thus the desired estimate is obtained.
∎

Now let us prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof.

The pointwise estimate (
4.4
) follows from Lemma

|x-y|<t

; then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}\left\|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\varphi_{t}(x,z)f(z)\,{\rm d}z\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{d+\beta+\gamma}\sup_{\varphi^{(w)}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}\left\|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left(\varphi^{(w)}\right)_{t}(x,z)f(z)\,{\rm d}z\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=2^{d+\beta+\gamma}A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(y,t).

Exchanging
x
and

and taking

in place of

Now we turn to the pointwise estimates (
4.5
).
Lemma
2.1
asserts that there exists a positive constant
C_{k}
such that
C_{k}^{-1}(k(t,\cdot,\cdot))_{t^{-1}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}

Then the estimates (
4.5
) follows trivially.

Below we explain the proof of (

\displaystyle A_{\gamma,\beta}[\pi_{L}(h)](y,t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma,\beta}}\left\|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\theta_{t}(y,z)\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}k(s,z,w)h(w,s)\,\frac{{\rm d}w{\rm d}s}{s}\right)\,{\rm d}z\right\|_{X}

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

be any fixed Banach space and

. Both the spaces
BMO_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}^{p^{\prime}}(\real^{d};X^{*})

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}}\lesssim_{\beta}\max\left\{p^{\frac{1}{q}}p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},p^{\prime\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left\langle f(x),g(x)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right|,\quad 1<p<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}}\lesssim_{\beta}\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left\langle f(x),g(x)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right|,\quad 1\leq p<q,

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X^{*}
such that
\|g\|_{BMO^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(X^{*})}\leq 1
.
Furthermore, if

has the Radon-Nikodým property. Then

\displaystyle BMO^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(X^{*})=\left(H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)\right)^{*},\quad 1\leq p<q;

\displaystyle H^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(X^{*})=\left(H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)\right)^{*},\quad 1<p<\infty.

Remark 5.2
.

Indeed, we can also obtain this duality theorem under the assumption that

be a sectotrial operator satisfying only (

), see Remark

.

Proposition 5.3

Let

) and (

Proof.

Fixing

By approximation, we can assume that

is denoted by
{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}
. By the Calderón identity—(
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left\langle f(x),g(x)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle=4\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t),{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle=4\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle G^{\frac{p-q}{q}}(x,t){\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t),G^{\frac{q-p}{q}}(x,t){\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}G^{p-q}(x,t)\|{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t)\|_{X}^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}G^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,t)\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=I\cdot II.

The term
I
is estimated as below,

\displaystyle I^{q}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{p-q}(x,t)\partial_{t}\left(G^{q}(x,t)\right)\,{\rm d}t{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-q\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{p-1}(x,t)\partial_{t}G(x,t)\,{\rm d}t{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq-q\int_{\real{}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}G^{p-1}(x,0)\partial_{t}G(x,t)\,{\rm d}t{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=q\int_{\real{}^{d}}G^{p}(x,0)\,{\rm d}t{\rm d}x=q\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}^{p},

since
G(x,t)
is decreasing in
t
, and
G(x,0)=G_{q,L}(f)(x)
.

For the term
II
, we introduce two more variants of
S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)
(cf.
[
47
]
). The first is defined similarly to
G(\cdot,t)
:

S(x,t)=\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{|y-x|<s-\frac{t}{2}}\left(A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(y,s)\right)^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}s}{s^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad x\in\real^{d},\ t>0.

To introduce the second one, let
{\mathscr{D}}_{k}
be the family of dyadic cubes in
d
of side length
2^{-k}
, that is,

{\mathscr{D}}_{k}=\left\{2^{-k}\prod_{j=1}^{d}[m_{j},m_{j}+1):m_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

Denote
c_{Q}
as the center of a cube
Q
. Then, we define

\mathcal{S}(x,k)=\left(\int_{\sqrt{d}2^{-k}}^{\infty}\int_{|y-c_{Q}|<s}\left(A_{\gamma,\beta}(f)(y,s)\right)^{q}\,\frac{{\rm d}y{\rm d}s}{s^{d+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\text{if }x\in Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k},\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

By definition, we have the following properties,

(i)

\mathcal{S}(\cdot,k)
is increasing in
k
,

(ii)

\mathcal{S}(\cdot,k)
is constant on every cube
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k}
,

(iii)

\mathcal{S}(x,-\infty)=0
and
\mathcal{S}(x,\infty)=S(x,0)=S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)
.

If
s\geq t\geq\sqrt{d}2^{-k}
and
x\in Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k}
, then
B(x,s-\frac{t}{2})\subset B(c_{Q},s)
, where
B(x,t)
denotes the ball with center
x
and radius
t
. This implies

S(x,t)\leq\mathcal{S}(x,k),\quad x\in Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k}\text{ whenever }t\geq\sqrt{d}2^{-k}.

Using (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we have

G_{q,L}(f)(x)\lesssim G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x)\approx_{\gamma,\beta}S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)(x),

and similarly,

(5.2)

G(x,t)\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}S(x,t).

Now we proceed to estimate the term
B
based on these observations. Applying (
5.2
) to
II
, we have

\displaystyle II^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}S^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,t)\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k}}\int_{Q}\int_{\sqrt{d}2^{-k}}^{\sqrt{d}2^{-k+1}}S^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,t)\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{k}}\int_{Q}\int_{\sqrt{d}2^{-k}}^{\sqrt{d}2^{-k+1}}\mathcal{S}^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,k)\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}D(x,j)\int_{\sqrt{d}2^{-k}}^{\sqrt{d}2^{-k+1}}\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x

where
D(x,j)=\mathcal{S}^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,j)-\mathcal{S}^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,j-1)
. Then
D(x,j)
is constant on every cube
Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}
. Thus

\displaystyle II^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\int_{\real{}^{d}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}D(x,j)\left(\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\int_{\sqrt{d}2^{-k}}^{\sqrt{d}2^{-k+1}}\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}}\int_{Q}D(x,j)\int_{0}^{\sqrt{d}2^{-j+1}}\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}}D(x,j)\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{d}\ell(Q)}\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x,

where
\ell(Q)
denotes the length of
Q
. There exists a ball
B
such that
Q\subset B
,
Q\times(0,2\sqrt{d}\ell(Q)]\subset\widehat{B}
and
|B|\lesssim|Q|
. Then we deduce that

\int_{Q}\int_{0}^{2\sqrt{d}\ell(Q)}\|{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)(x,t)\|_{X^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}{\rm d}x\leq\inf_{y\in B}\left\{\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](y)\right\}^{q^{\prime}}|B|\lesssim\inf_{y\in Q}\left\{\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](y)\right\}^{q^{\prime}}|Q|.

Therefore

\displaystyle II^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}}D(x,j)\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(x)\inf_{y\in Q}\left\{\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](y)\right\}^{q^{\prime}}|Q|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in{\mathscr{D}}_{j}}\int_{Q}D(x,j)\left(\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](x)\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\real{}^{d}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}D(x,j)\left(\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](x)\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\real{}^{d}}\mathcal{S}^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}(x,\infty)\left(\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right](x)\right)^{q^{\prime}}\,{\rm d}x=\|S^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{r}\left\|\left(\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right]\right)^{q^{\prime}}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}^{\frac{q-p}{q-1}}\|\mathcal{C}_{q^{\prime}}\left[{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(g)\right]\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}},

where
1/r=1-q^{\prime}/p^{\prime}=(q-p)/(qp-p)
.

Combining the estimates of
I
and
II
with Theorem
4.1
, we get the desired assertion.
∎

Finally, we arrive at the proof of our main theorem.

Proof of Theorm
1.1
.

The first part (
1.8
) of Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
. Indeed, suppose
L
be a generator such that the kernels of the generating semigroup satisfy (
1.5
) , (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) with
0<\beta,\gamma\leq 1
, then the classical Poisson semigroup generated by
\sqrt{\Delta}
satisfy obviously the same assumptions. Then

\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim\|S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

Similarly we obtain

\|S_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

As for another part (
1.9
), one side is easy by Theorem
4.1
,

\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim\|G_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}\approx_{\gamma,\beta}\|S_{q,\gamma,\beta}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

For the reverse direction, by Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
, we have for
1\leq p<(1+q)/2
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{p(q-1)}{q-p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{g}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left\langle f(x),g(x)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\sup_{g}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\|g\|_{BMO^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(X^{*})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}^{\frac{p}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}^{1-\frac{p}{q}},

where the supremum is taken over all
g\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes X^{*}
such that its
BMO_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}^{p^{\prime}}(X^{*})
-norm is not more than
1
. Hence

\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\frac{1+q}{2}.

Now we deal with the case
(1+q)/2\leq p<\infty
. Let
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
, we deduce from Theorem
5.1
that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}p^{\frac{1}{q}},p^{\prime\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\right\}\sup_{h}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d}}\left\langle f(x),h(x)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{h}\left|\int_{\real{}^{d+1}_{+}}\left\langle{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(x,t),{\mathcal{Q}}^{*}(h)(x,t)\right\rangle_{X\times X^{*}}\,\frac{{\rm d}x{\rm d}t}{t}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{h}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\|G_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(h)\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}p^{\prime\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sup_{h}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\|S_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(h)\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},

where the supremum is taken over all
h\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X^{*}
such that its
H^{p^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime},L^{*}}(X^{*})
-norm is not more than
1
.

Combining the estimations above we conclude that

p^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p^{\frac{1}{q}}\|S_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

We complete the proof.
∎

6.
Applications

In this section, we first recall the previous related results in
[
33
,
29
]
. These, together with the tent space theory and Theorem
1.1
, will enable us to obtain the optimal Lusin type constants and the characterization of martingale type. In particular, this resolves partially Problem 1.8, Problem A.1 and Conjecture A.4 in the recent paper of Xu
[
48
]
.

Some notions and notations need to be presented.
We first introduce the vector-valued atomic Hardy space
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
. A measurable function
a\in L^{\infty}(\real^{d};X)
is called an
X
-valued atom if

\text{supp}\,a\subset B,\ \int_{\real{}^{d}}a(x)\,{\rm d}x=0,\ \|a\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\leq|B|^{-1},

where
B
is a ball in
d
. The atomic Hardy space
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
is defined as the function space consisting of all functions
f
which admits an expression of the form

f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty,

where
a_{j}
is an
X
-valued atom. The norm of
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
is defined as

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(X)}=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|:f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}(x)\right\}.

This is a Banach space.

The BMO space
BMO(\real^{d};X)
is defined as the space of all
f\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\real^{d};X)
equipped with the semi-norm

\|f\|_{BMO(X)}=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\|f-f_{B}\|_{X}\,{\rm d}x<\infty,

where the supremum runs over all the balls in
d
and
f_{B}
represents the average of
f
over
B
.
BMO(\real^{d};X)
is a Banach space modulo constants.

It is well-known that
BMO(\real^{d};X^{*})
is isomorphically identified as a subspace of the dual space of
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
(cf.
[
10
]
) and it is norming in the following sense

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(X)}\approx\sup\left\{|\left\langle f,g\right\rangle|:g\in BMO(\real^{d};X^{*}),\ \|g\|_{BMO(X)}\leq 1\right\}

with universal constants. Furthermore, if the Banach space
X^{*}
has the Radon-Nikodým property, then (cf.
[
8
]
)

(6.1)

\displaystyle(H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X))^{*}=BMO(\real^{d};X^{*}),

with equivalent norms.

We recall the following definitions on the geometric properties for Banach spaces. A Banach space
X
is said to be of
martingale type

q
(with
1<q\leq 2
) if there exists a positive constant
c
such that every finite
X
-valued
L^{q}
-martingale
(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the following inequality holds

\sup_{n\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}}\|f_{n}\|_{X}^{q}\leq c^{q}\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|f_{n}-f_{n-1}\|_{X}^{q},

where
{\mathbb{E}}
denotes the underlying expectation; and the least constant
c
is called the martingale type constant, denoted as
\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)
. While
X
is said to be of
martingale cotype

q
(with
2\leq q<\infty
) if the reverse inequalities holds with
c^{-1}
in place of
c
and the corresponding martingale cotype constant is denoted by
\mathsf{M}_{c,q}(X)
. Pisier’s famous renorming theorem shows that
X
is of martingale cotype (respectively, type)
q
if and only if
X
admits an equivalent
q
-uniform convex (respectively, smooth) norm. We refer the reader to
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
for more details.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<q\leq 2
. The authors in
[
29
]
showed that the assertion that
X
is of martingale type
q
is equivalent to the one that for any
1<p<\infty
, there exists a constant
c_{p}
such that for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq c_{p}\|S_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(f)\|_{p}.

Later on, in
[
33
]
the authors investigated the relationships between
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
and
H^{1}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X)
as well as the ones between
BMO(\real^{d};X)
and
BMO_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X)
, and provided insights into the geometric properties of the underlying Banach space
X
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<q\leq 2
. The followings are equivalent

(i)

X
is of martingale type
q
;

(ii)

there exists a positive constant c such that for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\rm at}(X)}\leq c\|S_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(f)\|_{1};

(iii)

there exists a positive constant c such that for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

\|f\|_{BMO(X)}\leq c\|f\|_{BMO_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(X)}.

Moreover, the constants in
(ii)
and
(iii)
are majored by
\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)
.

The following theorem follows from the interpolation theory between vector-valued tent spaces—Lemma
3.3
—and the boundedness of the projection
\pi_{L}
—Lemma
3.6
. See for instance the general interpolation theory of complemented subspaces (cf.
[
40
, Section 1.17]
), and we omit the details.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space,
1<q<\infty
and
1\leq p_{1}<p<p_{2}<\infty
such that
1/p=(1-\theta)/p_{1}+\theta/p_{2}
with
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Let
L
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then

[H^{p_{1}}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X),H^{p_{2}}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta}=H^{p}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X),

with equivalent norms, where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta}
is the complex interpolation space. More precisely, for
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
, one has

\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{[H^{p_{1}}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X),H^{p_{2}}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta}}\lesssim p^{\frac{2}{q}}\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,L}(X)}.

Now we are at the position to give the applications.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
1<q\leq 2
. Let
L
be as in Theorem
1.1
. The followings are equivalent

(i)

X
is of martingale type
q
;

(ii)

for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(X)}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{1};

(iii)

for any
1<p<\infty
and
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p};

(iv)

for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,

\|f\|_{BMO(X)}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|f\|_{BMO_{q,L}(X)}.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii). This follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
6.1
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). This is deduced from Theorem
1.1
and (
6.2
).

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). In the case
1<p<q
, by Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|S_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}\|_{p}.

Keeping in mind (
6.2
) and Theorem
6.1
(ii), we consider

[H^{1}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X),H^{q}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta}\subset[H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X),L^{q}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta};

then combining Theorem
6.2
with the interpolation between
H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X)
and
L^{q}(\real^{d};X)
(cf.
[
9
, Theorem A]
), one gets for any
f\in C_{c}(\real^{d})\otimes X
,
1/p=1-\theta+\theta/q
,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{[H^{1}_{{\rm at}}(\real^{d};X),L^{q}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta}}\lesssim\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|f\|_{[H^{1}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X),H^{q}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(\real^{d};X)]_{\theta}}\lesssim\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|f\|_{H^{p}_{q,\sqrt{\Delta}}(X)}.

This is the desired (
6.3
).
Combining it with the related result for
q\leq p<\infty
in
[
48
]
, we conclude

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}p\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X)\|G_{q,L}(f)\|_{p},\quad 1<p<\infty.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv). This follows from Remark
4.5
and Theorem
6.1
(iii).
∎

Remark 6.4
.

(1). Taking
L=\sqrt{\Delta}
in the assertion (iii), we get

\mathsf{L}_{t,q,p}^{\sqrt{\Delta}}(X)\lesssim p\mathsf{M}_{t,q}(X),\quad 1<p<\infty,

where the order is optimal as
p
tends to
1
. This solves partially
[

, Problem 1.8]
.

(2). The implication (iii)
\rightarrow
(i) says that a Banach space
X
which is Lusin type
q
relative to
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
implies the martingale type

for a large class of generators

. This answers partially
[

.

7.
Appendix

From the atomic decomposition of
T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
, we derive the following molecular decomposition for
H^{1}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
for any Banach space
X
, which might have further applications.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X
be any fixed Banach space and
1<q<\infty
. For any
f\in H^{1}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
, there exist a sequence of complex numbers
\left\{\lambda_{j}\right\}_{j\geq 1}
and corresponding molecules
\alpha_{j}=\pi_{L}(a_{j})
with
a_{j}(x,t)
being an
(X,q)
-atom such that

f=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}\alpha_{j},\quad\|f\|_{H^{1}_{q,L}(X)}\approx\sum_{j\geq 1}|\lambda_{j}|.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{1}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
. It follows that
{\mathcal{Q}}(f)\in T^{1}_{q}(\real^{d+1}_{+};X)
. Hence
{\mathcal{Q}}(f)
admits an atomic decomposition by Lemma
3.1
. More precisely, there exist a sequence of complex numbers
\{c_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
(X,q)
-atoms
a_{j}
such that

{\mathcal{Q}}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}a_{j},\quad\|f\|_{H^{1}_{q,L}(X)}=\|{\mathcal{Q}}(f)\|_{T^{1}_{q}(X)}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|.

Then by Lemma
3.6
, it follows that
\pi_{L}(a_{j})=\alpha_{j}\in H^{1}_{q,L}(\real^{d};X)
for all
j\geq 1
. Recall below the Calderón identity—(
4.10
),

f(x)=4\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}[{\mathcal{Q}}(f)(\cdot,t)](x,t)\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}.

This further deduce that

f(x)=4\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}[a_{j}(\cdot,t)](x,t))\,\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}=4\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x),

and thus we obtain the desired molecular decomposition.
∎

Encounter-based approach to diffusion with resetting
Ziyad Benkhadaj, Denis S. Grebenkov

Abstract
An encounter-based approach consists in using the boundary local time as a proxy for the number of encounters between a diffusing particle and a target to implement various surface reaction mechanisms on that target. In this paper, we investigate the effects of stochastic resetting onto diffusion-controlled reactions in bounded confining domains. We first discuss the effect of position resetting onto the propagator and related quantities; in this way, we retrieve a number of earlier results but also provide complementary insights onto them. Second, we introduce boundary local time resetting and investigate its impact. Curiously, we find that this type of resetting does not alter the conventional propagator governing the diffusive dynamics in the presence of a partially reactive target with a constant reactivity. In turn, the generalized propagator for other surface reaction mechanisms can be significantly affected. Our general results are illustrated for diffusion on an interval with reactive endpoints. Further perspectives and some open problems are discussed.

Evans and Majumdar have introduced a new aspect into this field –

II.1
Encounter-based approach

II.2
Position resetting

We start by looking at the conventional scenario of stochastic
resetting of the particle position. At each resetting, the particle
is immediately relocated to its starting position
\bm{x}_{0}
. We assume
that durations
\delta_{1}

In other words, resettings occur at random times

etc. We first present general results and then discuss the Poissonian
resetting with a rate

\phi(t)=\omega e^{-\omega t}

that the Poissonian case was recently studied in

General results

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\phi(t_{1})\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}d\bm{x}_{1}d\ell_{1}P(\bm{x}_{1},\ell_{1},t_{1}|\bm{x}_{0})\int\limits_{0}^{t-t_{1}}dt_{2}\phi(t_{2})\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell-\ell_{1}}d\bm{x}_{2}d\ell_{2}P(\bm{x}_{2},\ell_{2},t_{2}|\bm{x}_{0})

\bm{x}_{1}
,
\bm{x}_{2}
,
etc. In turn, the convolutions over
\ell_{k}
and
t_{k}
can be turned
into products by performing the double Laplace transform with respect
to variables
\ell
and
t
that is defined for a given function

f(\ell,t)
as

{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\bigl\{f(\ell,t)\bigr\}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\ell\,e^{-q\ell}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-pt}\,f(\ell,t).

(18)

Applying this transform to Eq. (
17
) and summing
the resulting geometric series, one has

{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\bigl\{P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\bigl\{\phi(t)\rho(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\,.

(19)

As
\Phi(t)
and
\phi(t)
do not depend on
\ell
, one can first
perform the Laplace transform with respect to
\ell
to get

{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\bigl\{P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\,,

(20)

where we used Eqs. (
5
,
11
), and
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
denotes
the Laplace transform with respect to
t
. An inversion of the double
Laplace transform in this equation formally yields the full propagator

P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
under resetting:

P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\biggr\}\,,

(21)

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}^{-1}
denotes the double inverse Laplace transform
with respect to
q
and
p
.
This expression outlines that position resetting breaks the symmetry
of the full propagator with respect to the exchange between
\bm{x}
and

\bm{x}_{0}
, in sharp contrast to Eq. (
16
) for the full
propagator
P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
without resetting. This is not
surprising because resetting to the starting point
\bm{x}_{0}

distinguishes it from other points. Expectedly, the form of
Eq. (
20
) resembles Eq. (1) from
Chechkin18
for
the probability density of the first-passage time. However,
Eq. (
20
) gives access to more refined information
about the diffusing particle – to its full propagator under
resetting.

In analogy with Eq. (
11
), the Laplace transform of

P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
with respect to
\ell
determines the
conventional propagator under resetting that we denote as

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
. In other words, Eq. (
20
)
can be written as

\tilde{G}_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\,,

(22)

where tilde denotes the Laplace transform
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
of

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
with respect to time
t
(we keep using this
tilde notation for other quantities in the following). The inverse
Laplace transform formally yields the propagator under resetting in
time domain:

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\biggr\}\,.

(23)

For a reactive target (
q>0
), the propagator

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
is expected to vanish in the long-time limit,
as the particle diffusing in a bounded domain cannot in general avoid
hitting the target (see the discussion below in the case of a
Poissonian resetting). In turn, if the target is inert (
q=0
), the
particle survives forever,
S_{0}(t|\bm{x}_{0})=1
, whereas the conventional
propagator approaches a steady-state uniform distribution:

G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\to 1/|\Omega|
as
t\to\infty
, where
|\Omega|
is
the volume of the confining domain
\Omega
. If the resetting time
density
\phi(t)
has a finite mean
{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}
, the denominator
of Eq. (
23
) behaves in the small-
p
limit as

1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{0}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}=1-\tilde{\phi}(p)\approx p{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}+O(p^{2})
,
that implies the long-time behavior

G_{0,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}G_{0,\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x}|\bm{x}_{0})=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt\,\Phi(t)}{{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}}G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\,.

(24)

When there was no resetting, the diffusing particle explored the
bounded confining domain and therefore equilibrated the likelihood of
its location at any point in
\Omega
(the uniform steady-state
distribution). Moreover, the information on the starting point was
lost. In contrast, resetting breaks this uniformity and preserves
information on the resetting point in the steady-state distribution

G_{0,\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x}|\bm{x}_{0})
.

The integral of the propagator in Eq. (
23
) over

\bm{x}\in\Omega
determines the survival probability under resetting:

S_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\biggr\}\,,

(25)

or, equivalently,

\tilde{S}_{q,\phi}(p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\,.

(26)

As previously, the survival probability determines the probability
density
H_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})=-\partial_{t}S_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the
first-reaction time
{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}
under resetting, as well as its moments.
In particular, the mean FRT under resetting is

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\{{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}\}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t\,H_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,S_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{q,\phi}(0|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{\int\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\Phi(t)\,S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})}{\int\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\Phi(t)\,H_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})}\,,

(27)

where we used that
\phi(t)=-\partial_{t}\Phi(t)
,
Eq. (
8
), and integrated by parts in the denominator.
This expression was earlier derived by Pal
et al.

Pal16
,
as well as by Chechkin and Sokolov
Chechkin18
, who used it to
investigate the search optimality under resetting (see also

Evans20
). Note that Eq. (
27
) is equivalent
to the general form (
1
) obtained by Pal and Reuveni

Pal17
. We also note that the relation (
9
), which
is valid under resetting, can be inverted to access the probability
density of the first-crossing time under resetting:

U_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{\tilde{H}_{q,\phi}(p|\bm{x}_{0})}{q}\biggr\}\,.

(28)

Finally, the integral of Eq. (
21
) over

\bm{x}\in\Omega
yields the probability density
\rho_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

of the boundary local time under resetting:

\rho_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\Phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}{1-{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\bigl\{\phi(t)S_{q}(t|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr\}}\biggr\}\,.

(29)

Poissonian resetting

For the Poissonian resetting with
\Phi(t)=e^{-\omega t}
, we use
again Eq. (
11
) to simplify Eq. (
22
) as

\tilde{G}_{q,\omega}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{\tilde{G}_{q}(\bm{x},p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}{1-\omega\tilde{S}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}\,.

(30)

One can invert this Laplace transform via the residue theorem by
finding the poles
\{p_{k}\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
of

\tilde{G}_{q,\omega}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})
. For
q>0
, these poles are
determined by the equation:

\tilde{S}_{q}(p_{k}+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{1}{\omega}\,.

(31)

In the limit
\omega\to 0
, the resetting is progressively switched
off, and the
k
-th pole
p_{k}
approaches
-D\lambda_{k}^{(q)}
. The
pole
p_{0}
with the largest real part determines the exponential decay
of the propagator
G_{q,\omega}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
in the long-time limit.

In turn, for the inert target (
q=0
), Eq. (
30
)
simplifies to

\tilde{G}_{0,\omega}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})=\biggl(1+\frac{\omega}{p}\biggr)\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x},p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})\,,

(32)

which in time domain reads

\displaystyle G_{0,\omega}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{-\omega t}G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle+\omega\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{-\omega t^{\prime}}G_{0}(\bm{x},t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})\,.

(33)

This renewal-type equation has been used in many earlier works (see

Evans20
and references therein). In the limit
t\to\infty
,
one gets

G_{0,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}\omega\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x},\omega|\bm{x}_{0})\,,

(34)

in agreement with Eq. (
24
).

Other general expressions are also simplified for the Poissonian
resetting. For instance, Eq. (
26
) reads

{\mathcal{L}}_{q,p}\{\rho_{\omega}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\}=\tilde{S}_{q,\omega}(p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{\tilde{S}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}{1-\omega\tilde{S}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}\,,

(35)

from which the probability density follows as

\tilde{H}_{q,\omega}(p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{(p+\omega)\tilde{H}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}{p+\omega\tilde{H}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}\,.

(36)

This relation was reported by Reuveni and used to show that the
relative standard deviation of the FPT is equal to
1
under optimal
resetting
Reuveni16
(see also
Bressloff22c
;
a similar relation for the generating function was given in

Kusmierz14
; see a more general discussion in Sec. 3.1 of the
review
Evans20
). In particular, setting
p=0
in
Eq. (
35
) yields the known expression for the mean FRT:

{\mathbb{E}}\{{\mathcal{T}}_{\omega}\}=\frac{\tilde{S}_{q}(\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}{1-\omega\tilde{S}_{q}(\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}=\frac{1-\tilde{H}_{q}(\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}{\omega\tilde{H}_{q}(\omega|\bm{x}_{0})}\,.

(37)

Expressing
\tilde{S}_{q}(p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})
in terms of

\tilde{S}_{q,\omega}(p|\bm{x}_{0})
from Eq. (
35
) and then
substituting it into Eq. (
30
) gives

\tilde{G}_{q,\omega}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})=\bigl(1+\omega\tilde{S}_{q,\omega}(p|\bm{x}_{0})\bigr)\tilde{G}_{q}(\bm{x},p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0}),

(38)

from which the double inverse Laplace transform with respect to
p

and
q
yields

\displaystyle P_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})=e^{-\omega t}P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

(39)

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\omega e^{-\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\hskip-2.84526pt\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}d\ell^{\prime}P(\bm{x},\ell-\ell^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})\,\rho_{\omega}(\ell^{\prime},t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0}).

This is a typical renewal-type representation, in which the first term
represents the contribution without resetting, while the second term
accounts for resettings; in this term, the time interval from
0
to

t
is split by time
t^{\prime}
of the
last
resetting before
t
,
which occurs with the probability density
\omega e^{-\omega(t-t^{\prime})}
.
During the period from
0
to
t^{\prime}
, resettings erase information on
the position so that
\rho_{\omega}(\ell^{\prime},t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})
determines the
boundary local time
\ell^{\prime}
acquired up to
t^{\prime}
. In turn, as the
position is reset to
\bm{x}_{0}
at
t^{\prime}
, the diffusive dynamics from
t^{\prime}

to
t
is described by
P(\bm{x},\ell-\ell^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})
.
The relation (
39
), which was also derived in

Bressloff22c
, expresses the full propagator under Poissonian
resetting in terms of
P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
(without resetting) and the
probability density
\rho_{\omega}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
with resetting.
However, its explicit form is deceptive because

\rho_{\omega}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
still has to be determined via the double
inverse Laplace transform of Eq. (
35
).

II.3
Boundary local time resetting

Now we turn to another resetting scenario, which was not studied
earlier and consists in resetting the boundary local time
\ell_{t}
,
while keeping the position
\bm{X}_{t}
unchanged. Such a resetting does
not affect the dynamics of the particle; in particular, if the target
is inert (
q=0
), the related propagator
G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
remains
unchanged by construction. However, resetting of the boundary local
time may affect the reaction mechanism on a reactive target. We aim
therefore to analyze how such a resetting modifies the full propagator

P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
, the propagator
G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
, and the
probability density
\rho(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the boundary local time.
We also discuss the (unsolved) challenges in computing the probability
density
U(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the first-crossing time.

Full propagator

Similarly to Eq. (
17
), one can write a
renewal-type relation

P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})=\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\phi(t_{1})\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\bm{x}_{1}d\ell_{1}P(x_{1},\ell_{1},t_{1}|\bm{x}_{0})\,\Phi(t-t_{1})P(\bm{x},\ell,t-t_{1}|\bm{x}_{1})+\ldots

(40)

In contrast to the previous computation in Sec.
II.2
,
convolutions over boundary local times are replaced by integrals over
their intermediate values. Using Eq. (
11
) with
q=0
to
evaluate these integrals and performing the Laplace transform with
respect to time
t
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})={\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\}

(41)

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{1}{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\phi(t)G_{0}(\bm{x}_{1},t|\bm{x}_{0})\}{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{1})\}+\ldots

To proceed, one needs to evaluate the integrals over intermediate
positions
\bm{x}_{k}\in\Omega
. For this purpose, we use the spectral
decomposition (
14
) of the propagator
G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
.
For instance, one has

{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\phi(t)G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x})[u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{0})]^{*}\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(0)}).

(42)

The orthonormality of eigenfunctions
u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x})
allows one to
compute the integral of two such functions:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{1}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\phi(t)G_{0}(\bm{x}_{1},t|\bm{x}_{0})\}\,{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\phi(t)G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{1})\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{1}\,\biggl(u_{k_{1}}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{1})\,[u_{k_{1}}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{0})]^{*}\,\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k_{1}}^{(0)})\biggr)

\displaystyle\times\biggl(u_{k_{2}}^{(0)}(\bm{x})\,[u_{k_{2}}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{1})]^{*}\,\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k_{2}}^{(0)})\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x})\,[u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{0})]^{*}\,\biggl[\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(0)})\biggr]^{2}.

More generally, the integral of over
n-1
intermediate points
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\ldots,\bm{x}_{n-1}
yields the
n
-th power of

\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(0)})
. Using this property, we can sum
the infinite number of terms in Eq. (
41
) to get

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x}^{\prime})[u_{k}^{(0)}(\bm{x}_{0})]^{*}}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(0)})}\,.

(43)

An inverse Laplace transform with respect to
p
yields a formal
solution for the full propagator
P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
under
resetting of the boundary local time:

\displaystyle P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\xi(t^{\prime})\,G_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle\times\Phi(t-t^{\prime})\,P(\bm{x},\ell,t-t^{\prime}|\bm{x}^{\prime}),

(44)

where

\xi(t)={\mathcal{L}}_{p}^{-1}\biggl\{\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p)}\biggr\}(t),

(45)

and we used again the spectral decomposition (
14
) of
the propagator
G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
.

It is instructive to look at the long-time behavior of the full
propagator. As the position of the particle is not affected by
resetting, it should reach the uniform distribution, as in the
no-resetting case. In addition, random resettings of the boundary
local time render this quantity stationary at long times as well. As
a consequence, one can expect that the full propagator reaches a
well-defined steady-state limit. This is indeed the case. To show
it, let us examine Eq. (
43
) in the limit
p\to 0
, which corresponds to the long-time behavior. It is known that the
principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}^{(0)}
of the Laplace operator with
Neumann boundary condition (
q=0
) is zero. In addition, the
corresponding eigenfunction is constant:
u_{0}^{(0)}=|\Omega|^{-1/2}
. As a consequence, the sum in
Eq. (
43
) behaves as
1/(p|\Omega|{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\})+O(1)
as
p\to 0
, where we used
\tilde{\phi}(p)\approx 1-p{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}+O(p^{2})
, under the assumption that the mean

{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}
exists. One sees that the right-hand side of
Eq. (
43
) has a pole at
p=0
that yields the
constant term in the long-time limit:

P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt\,\Phi(t)}{{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}}P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ),

(46)

where

P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\,P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}^{\prime})

(47)

can be interpreted as the full propagator averaged over the starting
point uniformly distributed in
\Omega
(here
\circ
highlights that
the starting point is uniformly distributed; we keep using this
notation for other quantities). Expectedly, the steady-state
distribution
P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)
does not depend on the
starting point
\bm{x}_{0}
.

Let us have a closer look at the steady-state limit
P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)
. On one hand, its integral over
\ell
yields the
expected uniform distribution of the position:

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\ell\,P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\Phi(t)G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\circ)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\,,

(48)

where we used that
G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\circ)=1/|\Omega|
for any time
t
(if
the initial distribution was uniform, it remains uniform for any
t

since the target is inert). On the other hand, the joint steady-state
probability density
P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)
is not factored,
revealing correlations between
\bm{X}_{t}
and
\ell_{t}
. The steady-state
probability density of the boundary local time reads

\rho^{\rm st}_{\phi}(\ell)=\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}\,P_{\phi}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{E}}\{\delta\}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dt\,\Phi(t)\rho(\ell,t|\circ),

(49)

where

\rho(\ell,t|\circ)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{0}\,\rho(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0}).

(50)

The expression (
43
) can be further simplified if
the starting point
\bm{x}_{0}
is not fixed but uniformly distributed in

\Omega
. The orthogonality of eigenfunctions
u_{k}^{(0)}
with
k>0
to
u_{0}^{(0)}=|\Omega|^{-1/2}
implies that the integral over

\bm{x}_{0}
cancels all terms in the sum except
k=0
. After
simplifications, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\circ)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{0}\,\tilde{P}_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ)\}}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p)}\,.

(51)

For the Poissonian resetting, one has
\tilde{\phi}(p)=\omega/(\omega+p)
that allows one to simplify
Eq. (
43
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})=\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad+\omega\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\,\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime},p|\bm{x}_{0})\,\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}^{\prime}),

(52)

which reads in time domain as

\displaystyle P_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})=e^{-\omega t}P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

(53)

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\omega e^{-\omega(t-t^{\prime})}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\,G_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})\,P(\bm{x},\ell,t-t^{\prime}|\bm{x}^{\prime}).

This relation has a simple probabilistic interpretation in terms of
the last resetting time, in analogy to the discussion after
Eq. (
39
). It could also be directly deduced
from Eq. (
44
).

If the starting point
\bm{x}_{0}
is distributed uniformly, the volume
average of Eq. (
52
) yields

\tilde{P}_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\circ)=\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,p+\omega|\circ)\biggl(1+\frac{\omega}{p}\biggr),

(54)

where we used that

\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{0}\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime},p|\bm{x}_{0})=\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{0}\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x}_{0},p|\bm{x}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{p}

(55)

due to the normalization of the propagator
G_{0}(\bm{x}_{0},t|\bm{x}^{\prime})
and its
symmetry with respect to the exchange of the starting and arrival
points. In time domain, Eq. (
54
) reads

P_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ)=e^{-\omega t}P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\omega e^{-\omega t^{\prime}}P(\bm{x},\ell,t^{\prime}|\circ).

(56)

In the long-time limit, one retrieves

P_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\circ)\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}P_{\omega}^{\rm st}(\bm{x},\ell)=\omega\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,\omega|\circ),

(57)

in agreement with the general relation (
46
).

The full propagator in Eq. (
43
) determines the
probability density
\rho_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the boundary local
time under resetting. In the case of Poissonian resetting, the
integral of Eq. (
52
) over
\bm{x}\in\Omega
gives

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{\omega}(\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})=\tilde{\rho}(\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad+\omega\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}\,\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime},p|\bm{x}_{0})\,\tilde{\rho}(\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}^{\prime}).

(58)

If the starting point
\bm{x}_{0}
is distributed uniformly in
\Omega
, the
average over
\bm{x}_{0}
yields

\tilde{\rho}_{\omega}(\ell,p|\circ)=\tilde{\rho}(\ell,p+\omega|\circ)\left(1+\frac{\omega}{p}\right),

(59)

which becomes in time domain

\rho_{\omega}(\ell,t|\circ)=e^{-\omega t}\rho(\ell,t|\circ)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\omega e^{-\omega t^{\prime}}\,\rho(\ell,t^{\prime}|\circ),

(60)

in analogy with Eq. (
56
).

In the long-time limit, one retrieves

\rho_{\omega}(\ell,t|\circ)\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}\rho_{\omega}^{\rm st}(\ell)=\omega\tilde{\rho}(\ell,\omega|\circ),

(61)

in agreement with the general relation (
49
).

To complete this discussion, let us check whether the symmetry of the
full propagator under the exchange of the starting and arrival points

\bm{x}_{0}
and
\bm{x}
is preserved or not. This was the case for the full
propagator
P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
without resetting, see
Eq. (
16
). As resettings of the boundary local time do
not affect the position
\bm{X}_{t}
, one might think that the symmetry is
preserved here. However, an inspection of
Eq. (
43
) reveals that this symmetry is in
general broken for the full propagator
P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
under
resetting. What does break the symmetry? Even though the random
trajectory
\bm{X}_{t}
of the diffusing particle remains unaffected,
resettings modify the boundary local time
\ell_{t}
and thus affect

correlations
between
\bm{X}_{t}
and
\ell_{t}
, which are captured by
the full propagator
P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
. To better illustrate
this point, let us consider the situation with a single resetting at
time
t_{1}
. The path from
\bm{x}_{0}
to
\bm{x}
is then split into two
parts: the path from
\bm{x}_{0}
to an intermediate position
\bm{X}_{t_{1}}=\bm{x}_{1}
, which is sampled without any constraint on the intermediate
boundary local time
\ell_{1}
(ranging from
0
to
\infty
), and the
path from
\bm{x}_{1}
to
\bm{x}
with the constraint on
\ell_{t}
to be
\ell
.
The reversed path from
\bm{x}
to
\bm{x}_{0}
(after the exchange of the
starting and arrival points) is also split into similar two parts:
from
\bm{x}
to
\bm{x}_{1}
without constraint on the boundary local time, and
from
\bm{x}_{1}
to
\bm{x}_{0}
with such a constraint. The presence of a
constraint changes the statistics and thus breaks the symmetry. This
is particularly clear from Eq. (
52
) for the
Poissonian resetting: the part without constraint is sampled with

\tilde{G}_{0}(\bm{x}_{1},p|\bm{x}_{0})
, whereas the part with constraint is
sampled with
\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}_{1})
.

Conventional propagator

In contrast to Eq. (
11
) in the no-resetting case, the full
propagator
P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
under resetting, given by
Eq. (
43
), does not allow one to access the
propagator
G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
. In fact, one cannot use anymore
the fundamental relation (
11
) because the monotonous growth of
the boundary local time is broken by resettings. Even if the survival
probability of the particle is still determined as
{\mathbb{E}}\{e^{-\mu T_{t}}\}
by the total residence time
T_{t}
of the particle in a
vicinity of the target, the latter is not given by
a\ell_{t}/D
but should include the boundary local times acquired upon
all prior resettings. If there were
k
resettings up to time
t
at
times
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
, then

T_{t}=\frac{a}{D}L_{t},\qquad L_{t}=\ell_{t_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{t_{k}}+\ell_{t},

(62)

where
\ell_{t_{j}}
is the boundary local time acquired between two
successive resettings at
t_{j-1}
and
t_{j}
, while
\ell_{t}
is the
boundary local time acquired between
t_{k}
(the last resetting) and

t
. As a consequence, the integral over all intermediate states
should be done with the survival probability

e^{-qL_{t}}=e^{-q\ell_{t_{1}}}\ldots e^{-q\ell_{t_{k}}}\,e^{-q\ell_{t}},

(63)

i.e., each resetting is “penalized” by the corresponding factor

e^{-q\ell_{j}}
:

\displaystyle G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})=\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\phi(t_{1})\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{1}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\ell_{1}e^{-q\ell_{1}}P(\bm{x}_{1},\ell_{1},t_{1}|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle\times\Phi(t-t_{1})\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\ell e^{-q\ell}P(\bm{x},\ell,t-t_{1}|\bm{x}_{1})+\ldots

(64)

The integrals over boundary local times eliminate these variables and
allow one to replace full propagators by propagators:

\displaystyle G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})=\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\phi(t_{1})

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}_{1}G_{q}(\bm{x}_{1},t_{1}|\bm{x}_{0})\Phi(t-t_{1})G_{q}(\bm{x},t-t_{1}|\bm{x}_{1})+\ldots

In turn, the Laplace transform with respect to
t
transforms time
convolutions into products. Finally, the integrals over intermediate
positions
\bm{x}_{1}
,
\bm{x}_{2}
, etc. can be calculated by using the spectral
decomposition (
14
) of the propagators and the
orthonormality of Laplacian eigenfunctions. In analogy to
Eq. (
43
), we get

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}^{\prime}{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t|\bm{x}_{0})\}

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{k}^{(q)}(\bm{x})[u_{k}^{(q)}(\bm{x}^{\prime})]^{*}}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(q)})}\,.

However, the main difference with Eq. (
43
) is
that the factor
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)G_{q}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t|\bm{x}_{0})\}
itself admits the
same spectral decomposition that allows one to compute the integral
over
\bm{x}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{k}^{(q)}(\bm{x})[u_{k}^{(q)}(\bm{x}_{0})]^{*}\frac{\tilde{\Phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(q)})}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(q)})}\,.

Finally, as
\Phi(t)
is the integral of
\phi(t)
, one has

\tilde{\Phi}(p)=(1-\tilde{\phi}(p))/p
so that the last factor is
simply
1/(p+D\lambda_{k}^{(q)})
. Inverting this Laplace transform
with respect to
p
, one gets

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})=G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

(65)

for any resetting. In essence, the above formal derivation reflects
the fact that the product of exponential penalizing factors in
Eq. (
63
) can be reduced to a single factor
e^{-qL_{t}}
, where
L_{t}
represents the total boundary local time as if
there was no resetting.

We stress that the identity (
65
) is the
consequence of the
chosen
mechanism of surface reactions. In
fact, the constant reactivity can be replaced by other surface
reaction mechanisms
Grebenkov20
that is mathematically
equivalent to replacing the exponential law for the random threshold

\hat{\ell}
by another law:
{\mathbb{P}}\{\hat{\ell}>\ell\}=\Psi(\ell)
.
The overall “penalizing” factor Eq. (
63
) is now
replaced by

\Psi(\ell_{t_{1}})\,\ldots\,\Psi(\ell_{t_{k}})\,\Psi(\ell_{t}),

(66)

which is in general not equal to
\Psi(L_{t})
. As a consequence,
splitting the total boundary local time
L_{t}
into “pieces”

\ell_{t_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{t_{k}},\ell_{t}
by resettings changes the
penalizing factor from
\Psi(L_{t})
to the product in
Eq. (
66
) and thus modifies the generalized propagator

G_{\Psi,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
under resetting so that

G_{\Psi,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})\neq G_{\Psi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

(67)

in general. It is worth noting that the generalized propagator

G_{\Psi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
, given by Eq. (
12
), does not satisfy the
Robin boundary condition (
13b
) and thus does not possess
a spectral expansion on common Laplacian eigenfunctions, like
Eq. (
14
) for the conventional propagator

G_{q}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
. In particular, one cannot evaluate the integrals
over the intermediate positions
\bm{x}_{k}
in the same way as we did for
the derivation of Eq. (
43
). Finding appropriate
tools to compute the generalized propagator
G_{\Psi,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})

under resetting presents an interesting perspective for future
research.

Probability density of the first-crossing time

Moreover, even for the constant reactivity, one can imagine other
settings, for which the boundary local time resetting would affect the
conventional propagator. For instance, resetting can model a renewal
of the reactive state of the target or of the particle, therefore
erasing former history of their interactions. For instance, in the
context of a resource depletion model introduced in

Grebenkov22f
, the particle receives a unit of resources at
each encounter with the target, while a threshold
\ell
characterizes
the amount of initially available resources. In this setting, the
first-crossing time
{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
defined by Eq. (
10
) is
the first-depletion time, at which the resources on the target are
exhausted. In turn, the boundary local time resetting can be
considered as a replenishment of resources to the initial level

\ell
. The depletion dynamics is therefore characterized by the
probability density
U_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the first-crossing time
under resetting. In contrast to the no-resetting case
(Sec.
II.1
), the density
U_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
does not
follow from the full propagator and related quantities. For instance,

\rho_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
determines the probability
{\mathbb{P}}\{\ell_{t}<\ell\}
that the boundary local time
\ell_{t}

at time

t
does
not exceed the level
\ell
; however, as
\ell_{t}
is not monotonously
increasing due to resettings, this probability says nothing about the
values of
\ell_{t^{\prime}}
at earlier times
t^{\prime}
. In other words, the
processes
\ell_{t}
and
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}=\max\limits_{0<t^{\prime}<t}\{\ell_{t^{\prime}}\}
are not identical anymore. It is
therefore the probability law for
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}
that determines
the first-crossing time
{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
. In analogy with
Eq. (
40
), one can write the renewal-type relation
for the joint probability density of
\bm{X}_{t}
and
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}
as

P_{\phi}^{\rm max}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})=\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt_{1}\phi(t_{1})\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}d\bm{x}_{1}d\ell_{1}P(x_{1},\ell_{1},t_{1}|\bm{x}_{0})\,\Phi(t-t_{1})P(\bm{x},\ell,t-t_{1}|\bm{x}_{1})+\ldots

(68)

Once this infinite series is computed, the distribution of the
first-crossing time under resetting can be determined via

{\mathbb{P}}\{T_{\ell}>t\}={\mathbb{P}}\{\ell_{t}^{\rm max}<\ell\}=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}d\ell^{\prime}\int\limits_{\Omega}d\bm{x}\,P_{\phi}^{\rm max}(\bm{x},\ell^{\prime},t|\bm{x}_{0}).

(69)

The “only” difference with Eq. (
40
) is that the
integrals over
\ell_{k}
in Eq. (
68
) have an
upper limit
\ell
instead of
\infty
, to ensure that intermediate
boundary local times
\ell_{k}
do not exceed the threshold
\ell
.
However, this change does not allow one to replace full propagators by

G_{0}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
, while the spectral expansions of the resulting
integrated full propagators are more sophisticated and do not allow to
simply evaluate integrals over intermediate positions
\bm{x}_{k}
. This
challenging problem remains unsolved, even for the Poissonian
resetting.

III
Diffusion on an interval

In order to illustrate our general results, we consider diffusion on
an interval
(0,b)
of length
b
with partially reactive endpoints
(i.e.,
\Gamma=\{0,b\}
). For this basic example, most quantities
of interest can be found explicitly and easily drawn. Note that this
one-dimensional setting is equivalent to three-dimensional diffusion
between parallel planes separated by distance
b

computations should be feasible for other common models such as, e.g.,
a disk, a cylinder, a sphere, or a spherical target surrounded by a
concentric spherical boundary (see
Grebenkov20b
;
Grebenkov20c
).
We stress that former works on resetting dealt with unbounded domains
so that “finite-size effects” have been ignored.

The conventional propagator
G_{q}(x,t|x_{0})
is well known and was
reported in different textbooks (see, e.g.,

Carslaw
;
Thambynayagam
). In particular, the Laplace transform
of the propagator is given by (see, e.g.,
Grebenkov20b

(70)

where
\alpha=\sqrt{p/D}
and

\displaystyle v_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=q\sinh(\alpha x)+\alpha\cosh(\alpha x),

(71)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}+q^{2})\sinh(\alpha b)+2\alpha q\cosh(\alpha b).

(72)

The Laplace-transformed survival probability reads then

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{q}(p|x_{0})=\frac{1}{pV}\biggl(\alpha^{2}\sinh(\alpha b)

(73)

\displaystyle+q^{2}\bigl(\sinh(\alpha b)-\sinh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))-\sinh(\alpha x_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle+\alpha q\bigl(2\cosh(\alpha b)-\cosh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))-\cosh(\alpha x_{0})\bigr)\biggr).

In the limit
q\to\infty
, one gets

\tilde{S}_{\infty}(p|x_{0})=\frac{1}{p}\biggl(1-\frac{\sinh(\alpha x_{0})+\sinh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))}{\sinh(\alpha b)}\biggr)\,.

(74)

In the limit

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the effect of stochastic resetting onto
diffusion-controlled reactions within the encounter-based approach.
On one hand, this approach disentangles the diffusive dynamics from
surface reactions, yielding a deeper insight onto the search process
and allowing one to implement more sophisticated surface reaction
mechanisms
Grebenkov20
. On the other hand, it offers richer
opportunities for implementing the stochastic resetting: while former
works were focused on resetting the position of the particle, one can
now investigate the effect of the boundary local time resetting as
well. We followed these two scenarios and derived two formal
representations (
21
,
44
) of the
full propagator
P_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
under arbitrary resetting.
These are new results that allow one to access many other quantities
under resetting such as the conventional propagator

G_{q,\phi}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
, the survival probability

S_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})
, the probability density
H_{q,\phi}(t|\bm{x}_{0})
of
the first-reaction time
{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}
and its mean value
{\mathbb{E}}\{{\mathcal{T}}_{\phi}\}
,
the probability density
\rho_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the boundary local
time
\ell_{t}
, and the probability density
U_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of
the first-crossing time
{\mathcal{T}}_{\ell}
. In the case of position resetting,
our general formalism allowed us to deduce some earlier obtained
expressions. In particular, we retrieved the general expression for
the mean first-reaction time and its asymptotic behavior for
one-dimensional diffusion. In turn, the scenario of the boundary
local time resetting has been introduced here for the first time so
that all the results that we obtained in Sec.
II.3
, were
not reported earlier. In particular, we showed that the full
propagator approaches a steady-state distribution and studied its
behavior. We also observed the asymmetry of the full propagator with
respect to the exchange of the starting and arrival points, which
originates from subtle correlations between the position and the
boundary local time. Quite counter-intuitively, we found that the
conventional propagator is not affected by any resetting of the
boundary local time. We showed however that this is a specific
feature of the constant reactivity and the related exponential
distribution in the stopping condition. This property does not
necessarily hold for other surface reaction mechanisms. The impact of
stochastic resetting in this more general setting remains to be
uncovered.

While we managed to compute most quantities of interest, at least in
the Laplace domain, the problem of finding the probability density

U_{\phi}(\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})
of the first-crossing time under resetting of
the boundary local time remains unsolved. As described in
Sec.
II.3
, such resetting events destroy the
nondecreasing character of the boundary local time
\ell_{t}
so that

\ell_{t}
is no longer equal to its maximum,
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}=\max\nolimits_{0<t^{\prime}<t}\{\ell_{t^{\prime}}\}
. As a consequence, the
knowledge of
\ell_{t}
is not enough for describing the probability law
of
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}
or, equivalently, the first-crossing time. Even
though one can still write a renewal-type equation
(
68
) for the joint probability density of

\bm{X}_{t}
and
\ell_{t}^{\rm max}
, we could not find a way to compute the
integrals over intermediate positions
\bm{x}_{k}
and thus to sum up all
the contributions. This is a challenging open problem.

Quite naturally, one can go beyond the above two types of resetting.
If both the position and the boundary local time are reset
simultaneously, the full propagator gets a particularly simple form:

\tilde{P}_{\phi}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})=\frac{{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\{\Phi(t)P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})\}}{1-\tilde{\phi}(p)}\,.

(87)

In the case of the Poissonian resetting, one has

\tilde{P}_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,p|\bm{x}_{0})=\biggl(1+\frac{\omega}{p}\biggr)\tilde{P}(\bm{x},\ell,p+\omega|\bm{x}_{0})\,,

(88)

which reads in time domain as

\displaystyle P_{\omega}(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle=e^{-\omega t}P(\bm{x},\ell,t|\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\omega e^{-\omega t^{\prime}}\,P(\bm{x},\ell,t^{\prime}|\bm{x}_{0})\,.

(89)

This relation was also derived in
Bressloff22c
, where the case
of simultaneous resettings of the position and the boundary local time
was studied in more details; in particular, the generalized propagator

\tilde{G}_{\Psi,\omega}(\bm{x},p|\bm{x}_{0})
under Poissonian resetting but
arbitrary surface reaction mechanism
\Psi(\ell)
was obtained. There
exist however more general forms of asynchronized resettings of
\bm{X}_{t}

and
\ell_{t}
, which may considerably affect the full propagator and
the related quantities. Further explorations in this direction
present an interesting perspective.

As our study was realized independently and in parallel to the work by
Bressloff
Bressloff22c
, it is instructive to highlight several
distinctions between them. (i) The analysis in
Bressloff22c

was focused on the Poissonian resetting, which can be implemented by
modifying the partial differential equations for the full propagator
and related quantities; this method presents some advantages, in
particular, Bressloff derived the governing equations for the
propagator
G_{\Psi,\omega}(\bm{x},t|\bm{x}_{0})
with a general surface
reaction mechanism; however, its extension to other resetting laws is
difficult. In turn, we employed the renewal scheme, which is
applicable to any resetting law. (ii) Bressloff considered two
resetting scenarios: resetting of the position alone and simultaneous
resetting of both
\bm{X}_{t}
and
\ell_{t}
; while we also looked at the
first scenario, our main focus was on resetting of the boundary local
time alone, which is technically more difficult. (iii) The analysis
of
Bressloff22c
was formulated for diffusion outside a compact
obstacle
{\mathcal{U}}
, i.e., for
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\backslash{\mathcal{U}}
, in contrast to our focus on bounded domains; even though many
general results are valid in both settings, we often relied on
spectral expansions, which are exclusively applicable in bounded
domains, for which the Laplace operator has a discrete spectrum. (iv)
Finally, Bressloff considered the exterior of a ball for illustrating
his results, whereas we used a bounded domain (an interval). We
conclude that these two works provide complementary insights onto the
problem of diffusion-mediated surface phenomena with resetting.

Appendix A
Computation of
U_{\omega}(\ell,t|x_{0})

In this Appendix, we detail the computation of the Laplace-transformed
probability density
\tilde{U}_{\omega}(\ell,p|x_{0})
of the
first-crossing time under Poissonian resetting of the position for
diffusion on the interval. According to Eq. (
73
), we get

\tilde{H}_{q}(p|x_{0})=q\frac{qS(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0})}{V}\,,

(90)

where
S(x_{0})=\sinh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))+\sinh(\alpha x_{0})
and
C(x_{0})=\cosh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))+\cosh(\alpha x_{0})
. We can then express

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{q,\omega}(p|x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+\omega)\tilde{H}_{q}(p+\omega|x_{0})}{p+\omega\tilde{H}_{q}(p+\omega|x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(p+\omega)(qS(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0}))}{aq^{2}+bq+c}\,,

(91)

where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=p\sinh(\alpha b)+\omega S(x_{0}),

(92a)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=2p\alpha\cosh(\alpha b)+\omega\alpha C(x_{0}),

(92b)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=p\alpha^{2}\sinh(\alpha b),

(92c)

and we set
\alpha=\sqrt{(p+\omega)/D}
. Denoting by
q_{\pm}
two
roots of the quadratic polynomial in the denominator of
Eq. (
91
),

q_{\pm}=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}\,,

(93)

one can decompose
\tilde{H}_{q,\omega}(p|x_{0})/q
into a sum of
partial fractions

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{H}_{q,\omega}(p|x_{0})}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+\omega)}{a(q_{+}-q_{-})}\biggl(\frac{q_{+}S(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0})}{q-q_{+}}

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{-}S(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0})}{q-q_{-}}\biggr)\,.

(94)

Substituting this expression into Eq. (
28
), we compute
explicitly the inverse Laplace transform with respect to
q
:

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{\omega}(\ell,p|x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{p+\omega}{a(q_{+}-q_{-})}\biggl(\bigl(q_{+}S(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0})\bigr)e^{q_{+}\ell}

\displaystyle-\bigl(q_{-}S(x_{0})+\alpha C(x_{0})\bigr)e^{q_{-}\ell}\biggr)\,.

(95)

Since both roots
q_{\pm}
are negative, this expression behaves
correctly at large
\ell
.

This is an exact fully explicit expression for the Laplace-transformed
probability density of the first-crossing time under resetting. Even
though the inverse Laplace transform with respect to
p
is needed to
get this quantity in time domain, one can investigate the asymptotic
behavior or to compute the moments of the first-crossing time via the
small-
p
expansion. For instance, one can check the correct
normalization of the probability density:

\tilde{U}_{\omega}(\ell,0|x_{0})=1
. In the limit
\omega\to 0
, one
has
q_{\pm}=-\alpha(\cosh(\alpha b)\pm 1)/\sinh(\alpha b)
that
implies

\tilde{U}_{0}(\ell,p|x_{0})=\frac{e^{-\ell\alpha\tanh(\alpha b/2)}}{\sinh(\alpha b)}\bigl(\sinh(\alpha(b-x_{0}))+\sinh(\alpha x_{0})\bigr)

(96)

without resetting.

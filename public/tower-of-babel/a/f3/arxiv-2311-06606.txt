Comment on "Continuous simultaneous measurement of position and momentum of a particle"
Adelcio C. Oliveira

Abstract
In a recent paper, [Gampel, F. and Gajda, M., Phys. Rev. A 107, 012420, (2023)], the authors claimed they are proposing a new model to explain the existence of classical trajectories in the quantum domain. The idea is based on simultaneous position and momentum measurements and a "jump Markov process". Consequently, they have interpreted the emergence of classical trajectories as sets of detection events. They successfully implemented the model for a free particle and for one under a harmonic potential. Here, we show that the continuous observation limit is a realization of a coherent semiclassical expansion; Also, as has already been demonstrated, the jump process is not necessary and is not observable. In other words, the collapse, as they propose, is a non-go theorem; even if it is real, it can not be measured under the needed assumptions to obtain Newtonian classical dynamics.



The basic idea is to consider a single particle system monitored in high frequency. This monitoring process is realized in a way that the position and momentum are observed simultaneously and independent process, it can be realized beyond the standard uncertain principle
Ozawa

Tsarev

Cimini

\mathcal{L}^{2}
set for states that can be observed in the quantum domain. After a highly accurate simultaneous position and momentum measurement (CMPM), the quantum state can not have a thickness lower than uncertain principle demands, thus the narrowest that it can be is a coherent state, this approach was first investigated in
Oliveira2014
and in reference
Oliveira2021

Analytical Approach

In order to obtain an analytical approach, we observe that for a general Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
, and initial state
\widehat{\rho}(0)
at time
t
we can write
Oliveira2003
;
Romero2004
;
Reis
its time evolution as

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)\{\widehat{\rho}(0)+\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}[\widehat{\rho}(0),\widehat{\Delta}_{s1}]dt_{1}+\left(\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}[[\widehat{\rho}(0),\widehat{\Delta}_{s1}],\widehat{\Delta}_{s1}]dt_{2}dt_{1}

(2)

\displaystyle+\left(\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\right)^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}[[[\widehat{\rho}(0),\widehat{\Delta}_{s3}],\widehat{\Delta}_{s2}],\widehat{\Delta}_{s1}]dt_{3}dt_{2}dt_{1}+...{\LARGE\}}\widehat{U}_{sc}^{\dagger}(t)

The operators
\Delta_{i}
and
\widehat{U}_{sc}
are defined by the
perturbation
\delta
, that is defined as

\delta(t)=\widehat{H}-\widehat{H}_{sc}(t)

(3)

thus we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{H}=\widehat{H}_{sc}(t)+\widehat{\delta}(t),\\
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\rho}=-[\widehat{\rho},\widehat{H}_{sc}]-[\widehat{\rho},\widehat{\delta}(t)].\end{array}\right.

In the interaction picture, in terms of
\widehat{H}_{sc},
we define

\widehat{\rho}_{I}=\widehat{U}_{sc}^{\dagger}(t)\widehat{\rho}\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)

(4)

where
\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)
is the semiclassical time evolution operator,
then we have

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{\rho}_{I}=-[\widehat{\rho}_{I},\widehat{\Delta}_{s}].

(5)

where

\widehat{\Delta}_{s}(t)=\widehat{U}_{sc}^{\dagger}(t)\widehat{\delta}(t)\widehat{U}_{sc}(t),

(6)

and
\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)
is defined as

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)=\widehat{H}_{sc}(t)\widehat{U}_{sc}(t).

(7)

If the classical hamiltonian can be writeen as

H_{\text{cls}}=\hbar\omega\,\alpha^{\ast}\alpha+\sum_{m,n}A_{m,n}\left(\alpha^{\ast}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n},

then, the semiclassical Hamiltonian, for one degree of freedom
Oliveira2003
, is

\begin{split}\widehat{H}_{\text{sc}}(\alpha(t))&=\hbar\omega\,\widehat{a}^{\dagger}\widehat{a}+\sum_{m\neq 0}m\,A_{m,m}\left(\alpha^{\ast}\right)^{m-1}\alpha^{m-1}(\widehat{a}^{\dagger}\widehat{a}-\alpha^{\ast}\alpha)+\sum_{m,n}A_{m,n}\left(\alpha^{\ast}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{m\neq n}m\,A_{m,n}\left(\alpha^{\ast}\right)^{m-1}\alpha^{n}(\widehat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{\ast})+\sum_{m,n}n\,A_{m,n}\left(\alpha^{\ast}\right)^{m}\alpha^{n-1}(\widehat{a}-\alpha).\end{split}

(8)

In the continuous simultaneous position and momentum measurements the corrections in the approximation vanish and we obtain

\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\widehat{\rho}(t)=\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)\widehat{\rho}(0)\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)^{\dagger},

(9)

where
n
is the number of measurements in the time interval, see reference
Oliveira2014
for the details. The state is a Gaussian and its centroid obeys Newtonian dynamics
Oliveira2003
. We emphasized that it is an analytical result that is valid for any Hamiltonian, for
SU2
algebra see
Romero2004
;
Reis
. As was numerically shown in
Oliveira2014
;
Oliveira2021
, it is not necessary to have continuous monitoring, if the time interval between two measurements is small, then

\widehat{\rho}(t)\approx\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)\widehat{\rho}(0)\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)^{\dagger},

(10)

and for any practical reason the Newtonian classical limit is achieved. That is valid for any general Hamiltonian, not restricted to chaotic dynamics as referred by
Gampel
.
In figure [2] of reference
Gampel
they implemented the "jump" simulation for a harmonic potential, for this potential
\widehat{\rho}(t)=\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)\widehat{\rho}(0)\widehat{U}_{sc}(t)^{\dagger}=\ket{\alpha(t)}\bra{\alpha(t)}
, where
\ket{\alpha(t)}
is a coherent state and the centroid obeys the classical equations of motion, that is exactly the reason why the coherent states belong to the set known as quasi-classical states
Klauder
.

Quantum Darwinism
Zurek
predicts that the environment and system interaction generates a proliferation of quantum states, known as pointer states, as well as shifting the probabilistic balance towards to states that are termed classical. The CMPM can then be understood as an additional selection rule in Quantum Darwinism, when observables that do not commute are measured simultaneously by independent processes, the apparatus acts on the system selecting only states that presents minimum variances of the observables. Understanding how this process takes place is the subject of future investigation.

Final remarks

In summary, the classical Newtonian limit can be achieved by high precision simultaneous measurement in an almost continuous process as stated in
Oliveira2014
;
Gampel
;
Oliveira2021
, but the procedure of adopting a non-continuous process of collapse as proposed by
Gampel
is not necessary and not an observable process. Instead of that, the semiclassical approximation gives the exact result in the continuous monitoring limit
Oliveira2014
;
Oliveira2021
and is a good approximation for the not exactly continuous measurement process.

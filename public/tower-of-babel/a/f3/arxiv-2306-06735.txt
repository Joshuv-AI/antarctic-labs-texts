Bruhat-Tits buildings, representations of p p -adic groups and Langlands correspondence
Anne-Marie Aubert

Abstract
. The Bruhat-Tits theory is a key ingredient in the construction of irreducible smooth representations of p p -adic reductive groups. We describe generalizations to arbitrary such representations of several results recently obtained in the case of supercuspidal representations, in particular regarding the local Langlands correspondence and the internal structure of the L L -packets. We prove that the enhanced L L -parameters with semisimple cuspidal support are those which are obtained via the (ordinary) Springer correspondence. Let 𝐆 \mathbf{G} be a connected reductive group over a non-archimedean field F F of residual characteristic p p . In the case where 𝐆 \mathbf{G} splits over a tamely ramified extension of F F and p p does not divide the order of the Weyl group of 𝐆 \mathbf{G} , we show that the enhanced L L -parameters with semisimple cuspidal support correspond to the irreducible smooth representations of 𝐆 ⁡ ( F ) \mathbf{G}(F) with non-singular supercuspidal support via the local Langlands correspondence constructed by Kaletha, under the assumption that the latter satisfies certain expected properties. As a consequence, we obtain that every compound L L -packet of 𝐆 ⁡ ( F ) \mathbf{G}(F) contains at least one representation with non-singular supercuspidal support.

In

In

splits over a tamely ramified extension. His construction was generalized by Yu in

[

[
FKS

by modifying Yu’s construction with a certain quadratic character.

The original Yu’s method is based on the construction of very specific pairs formed by an open compact subgroup of

and an irreducible smooth representation of the latter, called

[
BK2
]
). Yu’s construction of types has been generalized to not necessarily supercuspidal representations in
[

]

[

]
. In §
3
, we

If the group
\mathbf{G}
splits over a tamely ramified extension and the residual characteristic of

does not divide the order of the Weyl group of

-parameters for

that have trivial restriction to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. These

-packets contain only supercuspidal representations.

We generalize these notions to arbitrary smooth representations as follows: we call
an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G

regular
(resp.

) if its supercuspidal support is regular (resp. non-singular). We study the local Langlands correspondence for these representations by using the approach we developed in
[

,

,
AX1
]
.
In particular, assuming some properties of the local Langlands correspondence (proved to be satisfied in the case of classical

-adic groups in
[
AMS3
]
, in the case of the group
\operatorname{G}_{2}
in
[
AX2
]
, and, for

L
a torus in
[

,

]
), we obtain in Theorem
4.5.5
, for every
L
-parameter

, a description of the equivalence classes of the irreducible non-singular representations in a compound
L
-packet
\Pi_{\varphi}
via the Springer correspondence of a (possibly disconnected) reductive complex group attached to

the

attached to
\mathfrak{s}
. By (

\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{U}

, and let

decompose with respect

K=(K\cap U)\cdot(K\cap L)\cdot(K\cap\overline{U}

3.3.
Regular representations

Let
{\underline{\mathbb{L}}}
be a connected reductive group defined over the finite field
k_{F}
, let
{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}
be a maximal torus of
{\underline{\mathbb{L}}}
.
In
[
DL
, Def. 5.15]
, Deligne and Lusztig defined two regularity conditions for a character
\theta
of
\mathbb{T}:={\underline{\mathbb{T}}}(k_{F})
, which we now recall:

•

\theta
is said to be
in general position
if its stabilizer in
(\operatorname{N}_{\underline{\mathbb{G}}}({\underline{\mathbb{T}}})/{\underline{\mathbb{T}}})(k_{F})
is trivial.

•

\theta
is said to be
non-singular
if it is not orthogonal to any coroot.

By
[
DL
, Prop. 5.16]
, if the centre of
\mathbf{G}
is connected,
\theta
is non-singular if and only if it is in general position.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be a maximal torus of
\mathbf{L}
which defined over
F
, and let
\mathbf{T}
denote the maximal unramified subtorus of
\mathbf{S}
. Let
F^{\prime}/F
be an unramified extension splitting
\mathbf{T}
and
R_{\mathrm{res}}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{T})
denote the set of restrictions to
\mathbf{T}
of the absolute roots in
R(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{S})
.
Let
y
be a vertex in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbf{L}/{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}},F)
, and let
{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}
be an elliptic maximal torus of
{\underline{\mathbb{L}}}_{y}
. By
[
DB2
, Lem. 2.3.1]
there exists a maximally unramified elliptic maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{G}
such that the reductive quotient of the special fiber of the connected Néron model of
\mathbf{T}
is
{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}
.
In
[
Ka3
, Def.3.4.16]
, Kaletha introduced the following definition: a character
\theta
of
{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}
is called
regular
if its stabilizer in
(\operatorname{N}_{\mathbf{L}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T})(F)
is trivial. By
[
Ka3
, Fact 3.4.18]
, if
\theta
is regular, then it is in general position.

Let
\vartheta
be a depth-zero character of
S
. Let
S_{0}
and
\mathbb{T}
be defined as in (
2.0.4
) and (
2.0.6
). The character
\vartheta
is said to be
regular
if its restriction to
S_{0}
equals the inflation of a regular character of
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
Ka3
, Def. 3.4.16]
).

Let
\sigma
be any irreducible depth-zero supercuspidal representation of
L
. There exists a vertex
y\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{red}}(\mathbf{L},F)
and an irreducible cuspidal representation
\tau
of
\mathbb{L}_{y}:={\underline{\mathbb{L}}}_{y}(k_{F})
, such that the restriction of
\sigma
to
L_{y,0}
contains the inflation
\mathrm{infl}(\tau)
of
\tau
(see
[
Mor
, §1-2]
or
[
MP2
, Prop. 6.6]
). The normalizer
{\mathrm{N}}_{L}(L_{y,0})
of
L_{y,0}
in
L
is a totally disconnected group that is compact mod center, which, by
[
BT2
, proof of (5.2.8)]
, coincides with the fixator
L_{[y]}
of
[y]
under the action of
L
on the reduced building of
\mathbf{L}
. Let
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
denote an extension of
\mathrm{infl}(\tau)
to
L_{[y]}
. Then
\sigma
is compactly induced from a representation of
{\mathrm{N}}_{L}(L_{y,0})
:

(3.3.1)

\sigma=\operatorname{c-Ind}_{L_{[y]}}^{L}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}).

The representation
\sigma
is called
regular
if
\tau=\pm R_{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}^{{\underline{\mathbb{L}}}_{y,0}}(\theta)
for some pair
({\underline{\mathbb{T}}},\theta)
, where
{\underline{\mathbb{T}}}
is an elliptic maximal torus of
{\underline{\mathbb{L}}}_{y,0}
and
\theta
is a regular character of
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
Ka3
, Def. 3.4.19]
).

A supercuspidal representation of
L
is called
regular
if it arises via the construction described in §
3.1
from a cuspidal
L
-datum
\mathcal{D}^{L}
such that the representation
\sigma^{0}
of
L^{0}
is regular (see
[
Ka3
, Def. 3.7.9]
).

Definition 3.3.2
.

An irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
G
is
regular
if its supercuspidal support is regular, i.e. if
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
with
\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{L}
such that
\sigma
is regular.

Proposition 3.3.3
.

Any irreducible smooth representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
is regular.

Proof.

Let
G:=\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
, let
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathfrak{B}(G)
and let
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
. There is a partition
(N_{1},N_{2},\ldots,N_{r})
of
N
such that
\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{G}
with

L=\mathrm{GL}_{N_{1}}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{N_{2}}(F)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{N_{r}}(F)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma=\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma_{r},

with
\mathfrak{s}_{i}:=[\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}}(F),\sigma_{i}]_{\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}(F)}}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathrm{GL}_{N_{i}(F)})
. The representation
\sigma
is regular if and only if the representations
\sigma_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\sigma_{r}
are all regular. Thus, by Definition
3.3.2
, the representation
\pi
is regular if and only if the representations
\sigma_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\sigma_{r}
are all regular. Thus we are reduced to show that any supercuspidal irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
is regular. Then the result follows from
[
Ka3
, Lem.3.7.7]
. We can also recover it as follows. By applying the construction above with
L=G
, we obtain that
G^{0}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{N_{E}}(E)
, where
E/F
is finite tamely ramified extension (here
N_{E}=N/[E:F]
) and
\mathbb{G}^{0}_{y}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{E}}(k_{E})
. Any cuspidal irreducible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N_{E}}(k_{E})
is up to sign a Deligne-Lusztig character induced from a character
\theta_{E}
in general position of the
k_{E}
-points of an elliptic maximal torus, but equivalently
\theta_{E}
is regular.
∎

Remark 3.3.4
.

When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
we have also at our disposal the Bushnell-Kutzko construction of supercuspidal irreducible smooth representation in
[
BK1
]
, which works for any prime number
p
.
By
[
MY
]
, this construction coincides with the one in
[
Yu1
]
when
p
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
G
.

Proposition
3.3.3
extends to this more general setting as follows.
Let
\pi
be a supercuspidal irreducible smooth representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
. By
[
BK1
, Chap.6]
, for
p
arbitrary, the representation
\pi
is compactly induced from a representation
\Lambda
of
E^{\times}J(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})
, where
J(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})
is a compact open subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and
\Lambda
is an extension of an irreducible smooth representation
\lambda
of
J(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})
. Here
E/F
is a (possibly wildly ramified) finite extension,
\beta
an element of
\operatorname{M}_{n}(F)
and
{\mathfrak{A}}
an hereditary
\mathfrak{o}_{F}
-order such that
[{\mathfrak{A}},n,0,\beta]
is a simple stratum. There is a subgroup

such that

(3.3.5)

J(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})/J^{1}(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})\simeq G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}/G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0+},

where
G^{\beta}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{N_{E}}(E)
is the centralizer of
E
in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and
G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}
denotes the parahoric subgroup of
G^{\beta}
associated to a vertex
y_{\mathfrak{A}}
in
\mathcal{B}(G^{\beta},E)
which corresponds to
{\mathfrak{A}}\cap G^{\beta}
. Moreover,
\lambda=\mathrm{infl}(\tau_{E})\otimes\kappa
, where
\mathrm{infl}(\tau_{E})
is the inflation to
G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}
of an irreducible cuspidal representation of
G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}/G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0+}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{N_{E}}(k_{E})
, and
\kappa
is certain representation of
J(\beta,{\mathfrak{A}})
that extends a Heisenberg representation.
The normalizer of
G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}
coincides with
E^{\times}G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}
. Since
\tau_{E}
is regular, the depth-zero representation
\operatorname{c-Ind}_{E^{\times}G^{\beta}_{y_{\mathfrak{A}},0}}^{G^{\beta}}({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{E})
is a regular supercuspidal irreducible representation of
G^{\beta}
.

4.
Langlands correspondence and Bernstein Center

4.1.
Generalized Springer correspondence

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a complex (possibly disconnected) reductive group, and let
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
be the identity component of
\mathcal{G}
. If
g\in\mathcal{G}
, we denote by
(g)_{\mathcal{G}}
its
\mathcal{G}
-conjugacy class. We denote by
\mathfrak{U}(\mathcal{G})
the unipotent variety of
\mathcal{G}
, and by
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G})
the set of
\mathcal{G}
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(u,\rho)
, with
u\in\mathcal{G}
unipotent and
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{G}}(u))
, where

A_{\mathcal{G}}(u):={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)/{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)^{\circ}
denotes the component group of the centralizer of
u
in
\mathcal{G}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a parabolic subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
with unipotent radical
\mathcal{U}
, let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a Levi factor of
\mathcal{P}^{\circ}
, and let
v
be a unipotent element in
\mathcal{M}_{0}

{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)\times{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}(v)\mathcal{U}
acts on the variety

(4.1.1)

\mathcal{Y}_{u,v}:=\left\{y\in\mathcal{G}^{\circ}\;:\;y^{-1}uy\in v\mathcal{U}\right\}

by
(g,p)\cdot y:=gyp^{-1}
, with
g\in{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
,
p\in{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}(v)\mathcal{U}
, and
y\in\mathcal{Y}_{u,v}
.
The group
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)\times A_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}(v)
acts on the set of irreducible components of
\mathcal{Y}_{u,v}
of maximal dimension, and we denote by
\sigma_{u,v}
the corresponding permutation representation.

Let
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)}
be the usual scalar product of the space of class functions on the finite group
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
with values in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Let
\rho^{\circ}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u))
. The representation
\rho^{\circ}
is called
cuspidal
if

\langle\rho^{\circ},\sigma_{u,v}\rangle_{A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)}\neq 0
for any choice of
v
implies that
\mathcal{P}_{0}=\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}^{\circ}))
the set of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
-conjugacy classes of triples
\mathfrak{t}^{\circ}:=(\mathcal{M}_{0},v,\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}})
such that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a Levi subgroup of (a parabolic subgroup of)
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
, and
(v,\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}})
is a cuspidal unipotent pair in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, i.e.
v
is a unipotent element in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, and
\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
is a cuspidal irreducible representation of
A_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}(v)
.
Let
\mathfrak{t}^{\circ}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}^{\circ}))
and
W_{\mathfrak{t}^{\circ}}:=\operatorname{N}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(\mathfrak{t}^{\circ})/\mathcal{M}_{0}
. By
[
Lu2
, §9.2]
, the group
W_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}:=\operatorname{N}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(\mathcal{M}_{0})/\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a Weyl group and
W_{\mathfrak{t}^{\circ}}\simeq W_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
.

We consider now the case of the (possibly disconnected) group
\mathcal{G}
. Let
u
be a unipotent element in
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
. We first observe that
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)^{\circ}={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)^{\circ}
. (The inclusion
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)^{\circ}\subset{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)^{\circ}
is obvious. On the other side, since
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)^{\circ}
is connected, it is contained in
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
. Hence,
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)^{\circ}
is contained in
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
. But, since it is connected, it is contained in
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
). It follows that the natural inclusion
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)\subset{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
induces an injection of
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
into
A_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
. A unipotent pair
(u,\rho)
with
u\in\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
and
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{G}}(u))
is called
cuspidal
if (one of, or equivalently all) the irreducible constituents of the restriction of
\rho
to
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
are cuspidal.

We call
quasi-Levi subgroup
of
\mathcal{G}
a subgroup
\mathcal{M}
of the form

\mathcal{G}^{\circ}

\mathcal{M}^{\circ}=\mathcal{L}
.
Note that a Levi subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
is a quasi-Levi subgroup, and in the case when
\mathcal{G}
connected, both notions coincide.
The group
\mathcal{M}
is said to be

if there exists a cuspidal unipotent pair in
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{G})
be a set of representatives for the conjugacy classes of quasi-Levi subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
, and let
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\mathcal{G})
denote the subset of the cuspidal ones. Let
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))
be the set of
\mathcal{G}
-conjugacy classes of triples

,

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{G}
, and
(v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})

\mathcal{M}

Let

In

\nu_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))}\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[W_{\mathfrak{t}},\kappa_{\mathfrak{t}}])\,\overset{1-1}{\longrightarrow}\,\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}),

-cocycle and

-twisted group algebra of

-vector

We will define a canonical map, called the

(4.1.4)

\mathrm{Sc}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G})),

and analyse its fibers. We first consider the
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
-case.

[
Lu2
, § 6.2]
, there exists a triple

as above and a cuspidal irreducible representation

. Moreover

of

\mathcal{G}
-case. We set
\mathcal{M}:={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}({\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ})

\mathcal{X}_{v}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{X}_{v},\quad(x,m)\mapsto m^{-1}xm,

The bijection
\nu_{\mathcal{G}}

[

[

We recall briefly the construction of the map

Let
\mathcal{B}_{u}
be the variety of Borel subgroups of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
which contain

\mathcal{B}_{u}
have same dimension, say

W_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}
of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
acts on the top cohomology group

by permuting the irreducible components of
\mathcal{B}_{u}

and

commute, and we obtain the irreducible representations of

by taking the
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
-isotypic components of

\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G}^{\circ})
to be the set of pairs
(u,\rho^{\circ})
, where
u
is a unipotent element of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
(up to conjugacy) and
\rho^{\circ}
is an irreducible representation of
A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)
, such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u)}(\rho^{\circ},H^{2e(u)}(\mathcal{B}_{u},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}))\neq 0
.
We obtain a bijection, say
\nu_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
, from the set of all irreducible representations of
W_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}
to
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(G)
.
In particular, we have

(4.2.1)

(u,1)_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}\in\nu_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\operatorname{Irr}(W_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}))
, for any

Let
(u,\rho)\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G})
. By
[
AMS1
, Theorem 1.2]
, we can write
\rho=\rho^{\circ}\rtimes\eta
, with
\rho^{\circ}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{G}^{\circ}}(u))
. Moreover, where
\rho^{\circ}
is uniquely determined by
\rho
up to
A_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
-conjugacy.
We extend the definition of
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G}^{\circ})
to the case of disconnected groups by setting

(4.2.2)

\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G}):=\left\{(u,\rho)\,:\,\text{$(u,\rho^{\circ})\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G}^{\circ})$}\right\}/\mathcal{G}.

Definition 4.2.3
.

A triple
\mathfrak{t}=(\mathcal{M},v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))
is called
semisimple
if the unipotent element
v
is trivial.

Lemma 4.2.4
.

The semisimple triples in
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))
are those of the form
(\mathcal{M},1,\eta)
, where
\mathcal{M}={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
with
\mathcal{T}
a maximal torus of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
, and
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a maximal torus of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
and let
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ})
, where
\mathcal{M}:={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
. By definition the restriction of
\eta
to
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}=\mathcal{T}
is the trivial representation of
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
, which is obviously cuspidal. Hence the representation
\eta
is cuspidal.
Thus, the triple
(\mathcal{M},1,\eta)
belongs to
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))
. It is semisimple by definition.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{t}=(\mathcal{M},v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}))
, where
\mathcal{M}={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}({\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ})
with
\mathcal{L}
a Levi subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
and
v
the trivial element of
\mathcal{L}
. By
[
Lu2
, Prop. 2.8]
, the fact that
(v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})
is cuspidal implies that
v
is a distinguished unipotent element in
\mathcal{L}
, that is,
v
does not meet the unipotent variety of any proper Levi subgroup of
\mathcal{L}
. Since
v
is trivial, it is only possible if
\mathcal{L}
does not have proper Levi subgroups, that is,
\mathcal{L}
is a torus, say
\mathcal{T}
.
Thus we have
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{T}
, which gives
\mathcal{M}={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
. Since
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{M}}(v)=\mathcal{M}
, we get
A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)=\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
. Let
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{G}}(u))
.
By
[
AMS1
, (51)]
, we can write

(4.2.5)

\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}=\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{\circ}\rtimes\eta:=\operatorname{ind}_{A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)_{\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}}}^{A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)}(\rho^{\circ}\otimes\eta),

with
\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}}(v))
and
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{C}[A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)_{\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}^{\circ}}/A_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}}(v),\kappa^{\prime}])
,
where
A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)_{\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}}
denotes the stabilizer of
\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
in
A_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
, and
\kappa^{\prime}\colon A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)_{\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}}/A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)^{\circ}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is a certain
2
-cocycle.
Since
v
is trivial, we have

A_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}}(v)=\{1\}
. Hence
\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
is the trivial representation (which is cuspidal) of the trivial group
A_{\mathcal{M}^{\circ}}(v)
, and we have

A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)_{\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}}=A_{\mathcal{M}}(v)=\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ}
. It gives

\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}=\eta
with
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}[\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ},\kappa^{\prime}])
, where
\kappa^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is a
2
-cocycle. But, by
[
AMS1
, (25)]
, the cocycle
\kappa^{\prime}
is trivial, since
\rho^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cusp}}
is the trivial representation.
∎

Proposition 4.2.6
.

The set
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G})
is the set of
\mathcal{G}
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(u,\rho)
with semisimple cuspidal support.

Proof.

Let
(u,\rho)_{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G})
. Let
\mathfrak{t}=(\mathcal{M},v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})
be the cuspidal support of
(u,\rho)_{\mathcal{G}}
. The generalized Springer correspondence constructed in
[
Lu2
]
reduces to the ordinary Springer correspondence precisely when
\mathcal{L}
is a maximal torus of
\mathcal{G}^{\circ}
. Thus
(u,\rho)_{\mathcal{G}}
belongs to
\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G})
if and only if
\mathcal{M}={\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
with
\mathcal{T}
a maximal torus of
\mathcal{G}
. But then, since
v
is a unipotent element of
\mathcal{T}
, it has to be trivial. It follows that
A_{\mathcal{T}}(v)=\{1\}
, and hence
\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}=1
. So
\mathfrak{t}=(\mathcal{M},1,\eta)
, where
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{M}^{\circ})
. Then the result follows from Lemma
4.2.4
.
∎

4.3.
Enhanced Langlands parameters

Let
G^{\vee}
denote the Langlands dual group of
G
, i.e. the complex Lie group with root datum dual to that of
G
.
The
L
-group of
G
is defined to be
{}^{L}G:=G^{\vee}\rtimes W_{F}
. Let
W_{F}^{\prime}:=W_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the Weil-Deligne group of
F
.
A
Langlands parameter
(or
L
-parameter) for
G
and its inner twists is a continuous morphism
\varphi\colon W_{F}^{\prime}\to{}^{L}G
such that

\varphi(w)
is semisimple for each
w\in W_{F}
, the restriction
\varphi|_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
is a morphism of complex algebraic groups, and
\varphi
commutes to the projections to
W_{F}
.
The group
G^{\vee}
acts on the set of
L
-parameters. We denote by
\Phi(G)
the set of
G^{\vee}
-classes of
G
-relevant
L
-parameters as defined in
[
Bor
, §3]
(see also
[
SZ
, §2.5]
).

We define an action of
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}
on
G^{\vee}
by setting

h\cdot g:=h^{\prime}gh^{\prime-1}\quad\text{for $h\in G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and $g\in G^{\vee}$, where $p(h)=h^{\prime}{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}$.}

It induces an action of
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}
on
{}^{L}G
and we denote by
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}}(\varphi)
the stabilizer in
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}
of
\varphi(W_{F}^{\prime})
for this action.

We attach to each
L
-parameter
\varphi
several (possibly disconnected) complex reductive groups as follows.
We set
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi):={\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(W^{\prime}_{F}))
and denote by
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}}^{1}(\varphi)
the inverse image of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi)/({\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi)\cap{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}})
(viewed as a subgroup of
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{ad}}
) under the quotient map
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}\to G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{ad}}
. We define
\varphi|_{W_{F}}
to be the restriction along the embedding of
W_{F}
as the first factor of
W^{\prime}_{F}
:

(4.3.1)

\begin{matrix}\varphi|_{W_{F}}\colon&W_{F}&\to&{}^{L}G\cr&w&\mapsto&\varphi(w,1)\end{matrix}.

We set
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi|_{W_{F}}):={\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi(W_{F}))
and denote by
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}^{1}(\varphi|_{W_{F}})
the inverse image of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi|_{W_{F}})/({\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}(\varphi|_{W_{F}})\cap{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}})
in
G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}
. We set

(4.3.2)

\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}:={\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}^{1}(\varphi|_{W_{F}})\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\varphi}:={\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}^{1}(\varphi)/({\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}^{1}(\varphi))^{\circ}.

We denote by
u_{\varphi}
the image of

\left(1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\cr 0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
under
\varphi
. By
[
AMS1
, (92)]
, we have
u_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}^{\circ}
and

S_{\varphi}\simeq A_{\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}}(u_{\varphi})
, where
u_{\varphi}:=\varphi\left(1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\cr 0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)
.

An
enhancement
of
\varphi
is an irreducible representation
\rho
of
S_{\varphi}
. Pairs
(\varphi,\rho)
are called
enhanced
L
-parameters
(for
G
and its inner twists). Let
G^{\vee}
act on the set of enhanced
L
-parameters via

(4.3.3)

g\cdot(\varphi,\rho)=(g\varphi g^{-1},g\cdot\rho).

We denote by
\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes of enhanced
L
-parameters.

An
L
-parameter
\varphi
is said to be
discrete
if
\varphi(W_{F}^{\prime})
is not contained in any proper Levi subgroup of
{}^{L}G
.
An enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is called
cuspidal
if
\varphi
is discrete and
(u_{\varphi},\rho)
is a cuspidal unipotent pair in
\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}
, as defined in §
4.1
.
We denote by
\Phi_{{\mathrm{e}},\mathrm{cusp}}(G)
the subset of
\Phi_{{\mathrm{e}}}(G)
consisting of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes of cuspidal enhanced
L
-parameters.

In
[
AMS1
, Section 7]
, we attached to every enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)
its
cuspidal support

\mathrm{Sc}(\varphi,\rho)
. We recall below how it is defined.
Let
\mathfrak{t}:=(\mathcal{M},v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})
denote the cuspidal support of
(u_{\varphi},\rho)
, as defined in
4.1
.
In particular,
(v,\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})
is a cuspidal unipotent pair in
\mathcal{M}
.
Upon conjugating
\varphi
with a suitable element of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}^{\circ}}(u_{\varphi})
, we may assume that the identity component
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{M}
contains
\varphi\left(\left(1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\cr 0&z^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)\right)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Recall that by the Jacobson–Morozov theorem (see for example
[
CG
, Theorem 3.7.1]
), any unipotent element
v
of
\mathcal{M}
can be extended to a homomorphism of algebraic groups

(4.3.4)

j_{v}\colon\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{L}\text{ satisfying }j_{v}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=v.

By
[
CG
, Prop. 3.7.3]
, the homomorphism
j_{v}
is uniquely determined up to conjugation by an element of the unipotent radical of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}(v)
. In particular,
j_{1}
is the identity map. A homomorphism
j_{v}
satisfying (
4.3.4
) will be said to be
adapted to
\varphi
.

By
[
AMS1
, Lem. 7.6]
, up to
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy, there exists a unique homomorphism
j_{v}\colon\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{L}
which is adapted to
\varphi
, and moreover, the cocharacter

(4.3.5)

\chi_{\varphi,v}\colon z\mapsto\varphi\left(1,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\cr 0&z^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)\cdot j_{v}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z^{-1}&0\cr 0&z\end{smallmatrix}\right)

has image in
{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}
.
We define an
L
-parameter
\varphi_{v}\colon W_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to{\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}}({\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ})
by

\varphi_{v}(w,x):=\varphi(w,1)\cdot\chi_{\varphi,v}(|\!|w|\!|^{1/2})\cdot j_{v}(x)\quad\text{for any $w\in W_{F}$ and any $x\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})$.}

The
cuspidal support
of
(\varphi,\rho)
is defined to be

(4.3.6)

\mathrm{Sc}(\varphi,\rho):=({\mathrm{Z}}_{{}^{L}G}({\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}),(\varphi_{v},\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}}))_{G^{\vee}}.

By
[
AMS1
, Prop. 7.3]
, we can always represent
({\mathrm{Z}}_{{}^{L}G}({\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathcal{L}}^{\circ}),\varphi_{L},\rho_{\mathrm{cusp}})_{G^{\vee}}
by a triple of the form
(L^{\vee}\rtimes W_{F},\varphi_{L},\rho_{L})
, where
L
is a Levi subgroup of
G
and
(\varphi_{L},\rho_{L})
is a cuspidal enhanced
L
-parameter for
L
.
The cuspidal support of
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is said to be
semisimple
if
v=1
.

Definition 4.3.7
.

An enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is called
non-singular
if its cuspidal support is semisimple.

We recall that an
L
-parameter
\varphi_{L}\colon W^{\prime}_{F}\to{}^{L}L
is said to be
supercuspidal
if it is discrete and has trivial restriction to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Thus, an enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is non-singular if and only if its cuspidal support is of the form
(L^{\vee}\rtimes W_{F},\varphi_{L},\rho_{L})_{G^{\vee}}
, with
\varphi_{L}
a supercuspidal
L
-parameter for a Levi subgroup
L
of
G
.

Theorem 1
.

An enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is
non-singular if and only if
(u_{\varphi},\rho)_{\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}}\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}_{\varphi})
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Proposition
4.2.6
.
∎

4.4.
A Bernstein-type decomposition on the Galois side

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a Levi subgroup defined over
F
of a parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
defined over
F
and let
L^{\vee}
denote the
\mathbb{C}
-points of the Langlands dual group of
\mathbf{L}
,
We define

(4.4.1)

{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}({}^{L}L):=({\mathrm{Z}}_{L^{\vee}}^{I_{F}})_{W_{F}}^{\circ}.

The group
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}({}^{L}L)
is naturally isomorphic to the group
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)
(see
[
Hai
, p.16]
). We denote the isomorphism
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}({}^{L}L)
by
\chi\mapsto\chi^{\vee}
. We define an action of
{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}({}^{L}L)
on
\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(L)
as follows.
Given
(\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(L)
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}({}^{L}L)
, we define
(\xi\varphi,\rho)\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(L)
by

\xi\varphi:=\varphi
on
I_{F}\times\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
(\xi\varphi)(\mathrm{Fr}_{F}):=\widetilde{\xi}\varphi(\mathrm{Fr}_{F})
. Here
\widetilde{\xi}\in{\mathrm{Z}}_{L^{\vee}\rtimes I_{F}}^{\circ}
represents
z
. Let
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}:=[L^{\vee},\varphi_{\mathrm{c}},\rho_{\mathrm{c}}]_{G^{\vee}}
be the
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy class of the orbit of
(\varphi_{\mathrm{c}},\rho_{\mathrm{c}})\in\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{cusp}}(L)
under the action of

{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L^{\vee})
. Let
\mathfrak{B}^{\vee}(G)
be the set of such
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
.

By
[
AMS1
, (115)]
, the set
\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)
is partitioned into
series à la Bernstein
as

(4.4.2)

\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)=\prod_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{B}(G^{\vee})}\Phi(G)^{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}},

where for each
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
, the subset
\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)^{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}}

is defined to be the fiber of
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
under the supercuspidal map
\mathrm{Sc}
defined in (
4.3.6
).

For any
\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{G}\in\mathfrak{B}(G)
, it is expected (see
[
AMS1
, Conj. 2]
), and known for
p
-adic general linear groups and for pure inner forms of quasi-split
p
-adic classical groups (by
[
Mou
,
AMS3
]
), when
p\neq 2,3
, for the exceptional group of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
(by
[
AX1
,
AX2
]
), and for
G
arbitrary and
L
a torus (by
[
ABPS2
,
So2
]
), that some suitable notion of
\mathrm{LLC}
for supercuspidal representations of
L
, denoted by
\sigma^{\prime}\mapsto(\varphi_{\sigma^{\prime}},\rho_{\sigma^{\prime}})
, induces a bijection:

(4.4.3)

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s}}_{G}\colon&\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)&\rightarrow&\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}}(G)\cr&\pi&\mapsto&(\varphi_{\pi},\rho_{\pi})\end{matrix}

where
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}=[L^{\vee},\varphi_{\sigma},\rho_{\sigma}]_{G^{\vee}}
, and hence, by using (
3.2.2
) and (
4.4.2
), a bijection

(4.4.4)

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}_{G}\colon&\operatorname{Irr}(G)&\rightarrow&\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}(G)\cr&\pi&\mapsto&(\varphi_{\pi},\rho_{\pi})\end{matrix}.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(L,\sigma)
denote the finite subgroup of
{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(L)
defined as

(4.4.5)

{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(L,\sigma):=\left\{\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(L)\;:\;\chi\otimes\sigma=\sigma\right\}.

Let
\natural
and
{}^{L}\natural
denote the collections of
2
-cocycles, occurring respectively in
[
So1
]
and
[
AMS1
]
. In many cases these cocycles are known to be both trivial (for instance for classical groups, as proved in
[
AMS3
]
).

For every Levi subgroup
L
of
G
, there is a canonical isomorphism
w\mapsto w^{\vee}
between
W_{G}(L):=\operatorname{N}_{G}(L)/L
and
W_{G^{\vee}}(L^{\vee}):=\operatorname{N}_{G^{\vee}}(L^{\vee})/L^{\vee}
, see
[
ABPS1
, Prop. 3.1]
. For
w\in W_{G}(L)
and
\sigma\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{sc}}(L)
, let
{}^{w}\sigma
denote the representation of
L
defined by

{}^{w}\sigma(\ell):=\sigma(w^{-1}\ell w)
. If the representation
\sigma
is non-singular, then the representation
{}^{w}\sigma
is also non-singular.

.

If the following properties are satisfied for every
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{G}
:

(4.4.7)

{}^{w^{\vee}}(\varphi_{\sigma^{\prime}},\rho_{\sigma^{\prime}})=(\varphi_{{}^{w}\sigma^{\prime}},\rho_{{}^{w}\sigma^{\prime}})\quad\text{for any $w\in W(L)$.}

(4.4.8)

{}^{L}\natural_{\chi^{\vee}}=\natural_{\chi}\quad\text{for any $\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{\mathrm{nr}}(L)/{\mathfrak{X}}_{{\mathrm{nr}}}(L,\sigma)$,}

then
\sigma^{\prime}\mapsto(\varphi_{\sigma^{\prime}},\rho_{\sigma^{\prime}})

\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s}}_{G}
from

, where

4.5.
The
\mathrm{LLC}
for non-singular representations

Let
L
be Levi subgroup of
G
.
In
[
Ka4
, Def. 4.1.2]
, a supercuspidal
L
-parameter
\varphi_{L}
for
L
is said to be
torally wild
if
\varphi(P_{F})
is contained in a maximal torus of
L^{\vee}
. In particular, if
\varphi_{L}
is torally wild then
({\mathrm{Z}}_{L^{\vee}}(\varphi(I_{F})))^{\circ}
is abelian.

In
[
Ka4
]
, when the group
\mathbf{L}
splits over a tamely ramified extension and
p
is very good for
\mathbf{L}
, Kaletha has constructed the
L
-packets associated to torally wild supercuspidal
L
-parameters and has attached to each non-singular irreducible supercuspidal representation
\sigma
of
L
a cuspidal enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi_{\sigma},\rho_{\sigma})
such that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is a torally wild supercuspidal
L
-parameter for
L
. When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{N}(F)
and
p
is odd, as proved in
[
OT
]
, the map
\sigma\mapsto(\varphi_{\sigma},\rho_{\sigma})
coincides with the local Langlands correspondence defined by Harris and Taylor in
[
HT
]
and Bushnell et Henniart in
[
BH1
,
BH2
]
.

The connection index for
\mathbf{L}
dividing the order of Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}
(see for instance
[
Bou
, VI.2, Prop.7]
), if
p
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}
, then
p
is very good for
\mathbf{L}
. When
\mathbf{L}
splits over a tamely ramified extension and
p
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}
, all supercuspidal
L
-parameters are torally wild, and hence, in this case, the
L
-packets associated to all supercuspidal
L
-parameters for
L
are constructed in
[
Ka4
]
: these are exactly the
L
-packets that contain non-singular supercuspidal representations of
L
.

Let
{\mathrm{Inn}}(\mathbf{G})
be the group of inner automorphisms of
\mathbf{G}
. Recall that given an algebraic group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
over
F
, an isomorphism
\xi\colon\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{G}
defined over
F_{s}
determines a
1
-cocycle

(4.5.1)

z_{\xi}\colon\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Gamma_{F}&\to&\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G})\\
\gamma&\mapsto&\xi\gamma\xi^{-1}\gamma^{-1}.\end{array}

An
inner twist
of
G
consists of a pair
G^{\prime}_{\xi}:=(G^{\prime},\xi)
, where
G^{\prime}=\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(F)
for some connected reductive
F
-group
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
, and
\xi\colon\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\mathbf{G}
is an isomorphism of algebraic groups defined over
F_{\mathrm{s}}
such that
\text{im }(\gamma_{\xi})\subset{\mathrm{Inn}}(\mathbf{G})
. Two inner twists
(G^{\prime},\xi),(G^{\prime}_{1},\xi_{1})
of
G
are
equivalent
if there exists
f\in{\mathrm{Inn}}(\mathbf{G})
such that

(4.5.2)

z_{\xi}(\gamma)=f^{-1}z_{\xi_{1}}(\gamma)\;\gamma f\gamma^{-1}\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma_{F}.

We denote the set of equivalence classes of inner twists of
G
by
{\mathrm{InnT}}(G)
.

An inner twist of
G
is the same thing as an inner twist of the unique quasi-split inner form
G^{*}=\mathbf{G}^{*}(F)
of
G
.
Thus the equivalence classes of inner twists of
G
are parametrized by the Galois cohomology group
H^{1}(F,{\mathrm{Inn}}(\mathbf{G}^{*}))
.

Kottwitz
[
Ko
, Theorem 6.4]
found a natural group isomorphism

(4.5.3)

H^{1}(F,{\mathrm{Inn}}(\mathbf{G}^{*}))\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\operatorname{Irr}(({\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}})^{W_{F}}).

(When F has positive characteristic, see
[
Th
, Theorem 2.1]
.) In this way every
inner twist of
G
is associated to a unique character of
({\mathrm{Z}}_{G^{\vee}_{\mathrm{sc}}})^{W_{F}}
.

The basic version of the local Langlands conjecture states that there exists a surjective finite-to-one
map from the set of equivalence classes of irreducible admissible representations of
G

to the set of
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes of relevant
L
-parameters
\varphi\colon W^{\prime}_{F}\to{}^{L}G
. The fiber over
\varphi
is called an

L
-packet
. we denote it by
\Pi_{\varphi}(G)
.
Assuming the validity of the basic local Langlands conjecture, we can define for
each Langlands parameter
\varphi
the “compound
L
-packet”
\Pi_{\varphi}
as

(4.5.4)

\Pi_{\varphi}:=\bigcup_{G^{\prime}_{\xi}\in{\mathrm{InnT}}(G)}\Pi_{\varphi}(G^{\prime}_{\xi}).

The elements of
\Pi_{\varphi}
are expected to be in bijection with
\operatorname{Irr}(S_{\varphi})
, where
S_{\varphi}
is the group introduced in (
4.3.2
) (see
[
Ar
]
and
[
Ka2
, §4.6]
).

Theorem 4.5.5
.

We suppose that
\mathbf{G}
splits over a tamely ramified extension, that
p
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
, and that, for every Levi subgroup
L
of
G
, the Langlands correspondence

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Ka}}_{L}\colon\sigma\mapsto(\varphi_{\sigma},\rho_{\sigma})
defined by Kaletha for the non-singular supercuspidal representations
\sigma
of
L
satisfies (
4.4.7
), and (
4.4.8
), and that the induced maps
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s}}_{G}
, where
\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{G}
, satisfy (
4.4.9
).

Let
\varphi\colon W^{\prime}_{F}\to{}^{L}G
be an
L
-parameter. Then the equivalence classes of the irreducible non-singular representations in the compound
L
-packet
\Pi_{\varphi}
are in bijection with

\mathfrak{U}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal{G}_{\varphi})
. In particular, the
L
-packet
\Pi_{\varphi}(G^{*})
contains at least one irreducible non-singular representation.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{s}=[L,\sigma]_{G}
non-singular. Since
\mathbf{G}
splits over a tamely ramified extension,
\mathbf{L}
also splits over a tamely ramified extension, and, since
p
does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
, it does not divide the order of the Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}
.
Since we have assume that
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Ka}}_{L}
satisfies (
4.4.7
)and (
4.4.8
), it follows from Remark
4.4.6
that we have a bijection

\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s}}_{G}\colon\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)\to\Phi_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}}(G),

where
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}=[L^{\vee},\varphi_{\sigma},\rho_{\sigma}]_{G^{\vee}}
, and,
by hypothesis, the map
\mathcal{L}_{G}^{\mathfrak{s}}
satisfies (
4.4.9
).

Let
\varphi\colon W^{\prime}_{F}\to{}^{L}G
be an
L
-parameter and let
\rho\in\operatorname{Irr}(S_{\varphi})
. Let
(L^{\vee}\rtimes W_{F},\varphi_{L},\rho_{L})_{G^{\vee}}
denote the cuspidal support of the enhanced
L
-parameter
(\varphi,\rho)
. By Theorem
1
, we have
(u_{\varphi},\rho)_{\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}}\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}_{\varphi})
if and only if
(\varphi,\rho)
is non-singular. It follows from Definition
4.3.7
that
(\varphi,\rho)
is non-singular if and only if the
L
-parameter
\varphi_{L},
is supercuspidal. But, by
[
Ka4
]
, the supercuspidal
L
-parameters for
L
are exactly the ones which correspond to non-singular supercuspidal irreducible representations of
L
by the Langlands correspondence
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Ka}}_{L}
.

Since, by (
4.2.1
), we have
(u_{\varphi},1)_{\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}}\in\mathfrak{U}^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\mathrm{e}}(\mathcal{G}_{\varphi})
, there exists a non-singular irreducible representation
\pi
of the quasi-split group
G^{*}
such that
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s}}(\pi)=(\varphi,1)
.
∎

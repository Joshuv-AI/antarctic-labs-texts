Entanglement Entropy Bounds in the Higher Spin XXZ Chain
Christoph Fischbacher 1, Oluwadara Ogunkoya 2

Abstract
. We consider the Heisenberg XXZ spin- J J chain ( J ∈ ℕ / 2 J\in\mathbb{N}/2 ) with anisotropy parameter Δ \Delta . Assuming that Δ > 2 ​ J \Delta>2J , and introducing threshold energies E K := K ⁡ ( 1 − 2 ​ J Δ ) E_{K}:=K\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right) , we show that the bipartite entanglement entropy (EE) of states belonging to any spectral subspace with energy less than E K + 1 E_{K+1} satisfy a logarithmically corrected area law with prefactor ( 2 ​ ⌊ K / J ⌋ − 2 ) (2\lfloor K/J\rfloor-2) . This generalizes previous results by Beaud and Warzel [ 4 ] as well as Abdul-Rahman, Stolz and one of the authors [ 1 ] , who covered the spin- 1 / 2 1/2 case.



1.
Introduction

In the following, we are going to prove upper bounds for the bipartite entanglement entropy of the spin–
J
XXZ Heisenberg chain, where
J\in\{1/2,1,3/2,2\dots\}
. The spin–
1/2
Heisenberg XXZ chain under the presence of a random magnetic field has recently attracted significant interest in the rigorous study of many–body–localization (MBL), where MBL phenomena such as exponential clustering
of correlations, zero–velocity Lieb–Robinson bounds and area laws for the entanglement entropy (EE) for states from the lowest energy regime (“droplet” regime) have been shown recently in
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
, for a recent survey of these results, cf. also
[
21
]
.

Recall that for one-dimensional models such as the chain, we say that the EE satisfies an area law with respect to the bipartition of the chain into a “right” and a “left” subchain if it is uniformly bounded in the size of the subchain. While an area law is generally considered as an indicator of MBL, many delocalized systems seem to exhibit a logarithmic correction, which means that the EE scales like the logarithm of the subchain’s length. See the results in
[
4
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
23
]
, where such logarithmic corrections to an area for the EE have been obtained.

In what follows, we will adapt the ideas of Beaud and Warzel from
[
4
]
, where a log–corrected area law for droplet states of the spin–
1/2
Heisenberg XXZ chain for the generic case and a true area law under the presence of a disordered magnetic field was shown. With the help of technical refinements, this result was improved to show a log–corrected energy law also for higher–energy states in
[
1
]
.

While our results can certainly be viewed as a technical improvement of the spin-
1/2
case, we nevertheless believe them to be of additional interest, since they demonstrate that given a discrete many–particle Schrödinger-type operator in one dimension, a logarithmically corrected area law for states in a given energy range follows if the following four criteria are met: (i) a suitable relative bound that controls the hopping operator in terms of the potential, (ii) the potential energetically favoring configurations with a lower number of “building blocks”, (iii) a sufficiently low dimension of the space of these building blocks and (iv) a generalized Pauli principle that limits the amount of particles that can occupy any site.

We will proceed as follows:

In Section
2
, we will introduce the model and review previous results. We recall that the Spin-J XXZ model on the chain with anisotropy parameter
\Delta
is unitarily equivalent to a direct sum of discrete
N
–particle Schrödinger operators of the form
-(2\Delta)^{-1}A_{N}+V_{N}
, where
A_{N}
is a weighted adjacency operator and
V_{N}
is an interaction potential (Prop.
2.16
). Compared to the previously studied spin-
1/2
case,
V_{N}
is much more complicated and we determine the set of all its minimizing configurations for sufficiently large
N
(Prop.
2.5
).
Using that the kinetic term
A_{N}
is controlled by the potential
V_{N}
in the sense that
-4JV_{N}\leq A_{N}\leq 4JV_{N}
(Prop.
2.17
), we use a suitable Combes–Thomas estimate (Thm.
2.9
) previously shown in
[
1
]
, which allows as to obtain decay estimates on spectral projections (Thm.
2.10
). This together with some previous results on estimating the entanglement entropy (Lemma
2.68
) reduces the problem to showing bounds for the sum of all possible
N
-particle configurations in the subsystem weighted by their distance to the nearest configuration with sufficiently low potential (Cor.
3.2
).

Showing these bounds is the main goal of Section
3
: We firstly prove a suitable bound for single–cluster configurations in the spin-
1/2
case (Lemma
3.3
), which we appropriately generalize to certain low–potential configurations for the spin-
J
case (Lemma
3.5
). We then introduce the notion of “building blocks” (Def.
3.4
) out of which any
N
–particle configuration can be composed. Exploiting that if an
N
-particle configuration has potential energy of
K
or less, then it can be composed out of no more than
(\lfloor K/J\rfloor-1)
building blocks, we obtain a suitable bound for configurations of arbitrary high potential energy (Lemma
3.30
). Putting everything together, we prove our main result (Thm.
3.12
), where we show that states belonging to any spectral subspace with energy strictly less than
E_{K+1}=(K+1)\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)
satisfy a logarithmically corrected area law with prefactor
(2\lfloor K/J\rfloor-2)
.

Previous Results

For any fixed
J\in\mathbb{N}/2
, we consider the chain of length
L\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, which is described by the Hamiltonian

(2.1)

H_{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}h_{j,j+1}+J(2J-S_{1}^{3}-S_{L}^{3})

acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{L}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}\mathbb{C}^{2J+1}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{L}\mathcal{H}_{j}
, with the interpretation of
\mathcal{H}_{j}=\mathbb{C}^{2J+1}
as being the local Hilbert space describing a spin–
J
particle located at site

. For later convenience, we also introduce the notation
\Lambda_{L}=\{1,2,\dots,L\}
. The two–site Hamiltonian

is given by

(2.2)

h_{j,j+1}=J^{2}-S_{j}^{3}S_{j+1}^{3}-\frac{1}{\Delta}(S_{j}^{1}S_{j+1}^{1}+S_{j}^{2}S_{j+1}^{2})=J^{2}-S_{j}^{3}S_{j+1}^{3}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(S_{j}^{+}S_{j+1}^{-}+S_{j}^{-}S_{j+1}^{+})

and the additional term
J(2J-S_{1}^{3}-S_{L}^{3})
describes a boundary field; usually referred to as “droplet boundary condition”.
Here
S^{1},S^{2}
and
S^{3}
are the spin–
J
matrices and
S^{\pm}=(S^{1}\pm iS^{2})/2
are the spin raising and lowering operators, respectively (for the full definition of these matrices see e.g.
[
8
]
or
[
14
]
). Labeling an operator
A\in\mathbb{C}^{(2J+1)\times(2J+1)}
by a site
j
, like e.g.
A_{j}
, means that it acts as
A
on the
j
-th factor of the tensor product and as the identity on all the other factors.
In what follows, we assume that the anisotropy parameter
\Delta
satisfies
\Delta>2J
. While we emphasize that
\Delta>2J
is not necessary for the operators
h_{j,j+1}
to be non-negative (see, e.g.
[
20
, p.16]
), it is certainly sufficient (see Proposition
2.17
below).

2.1.
Equivalence to a direct sum of Schrödinger-type operators

In the following, we are going to discuss the equivalence of the spin-
J
XXZ Hamiltonian to a direct sum of discrete many-particle Schödinger-type operators. To this end, we will firstly review previous work
[
8
]
, where we described configurations of particles via functions
{\bf m}:\Lambda_{L}\rightarrow\{0,1,\dots,2J\}
, where for each
i\in\Lambda_{L}
, the value
{\bf m}(i)
represents number of particles that are located at site
i
when in configuration
\bf{m}
.

Equivalently, a configuration of
N
particles distributed over
\Lambda_{L}
can be described using ordered multisets
X
with elements in
\Lambda_{L}
. Here, the value of the
i
-th element
x_{i}\in X
represents the location of the
i
-th particle of a configuration of
N
particles.

For our purposes, it will be advantageous to use both points of view as they have their respective advantages: the first description involving occupation numbers will be more useful when it comes to analyzing the interaction potential of the Schrödinger-type operators. On the other hand, once it has been shown how the entanglement entropy of a state can be estimated using the Combes-Thomas bound for spectral projections, the problem boils down to a combinatorial problem estimating exponentially weighted sums over a large set of many-particle configurations, for which the multiset point of view will be more convenient.

2.1.1.
Previous approach using occupation numbers

In
[
8
, Prop. 2.1]
, it was shown that the Hamiltonian
H_{L}
is unitarily equivalent to a direct sum of many–body Schrödinger operators. One firstly observes that
H_{L}
preserves the total magnetization/particle number: to this end, we define the
local particle number operator

\mathcal{N}^{loc}:=(J-S^{3})
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2J+1}
which has spectrum
\sigma(\mathcal{N}^{loc})=\{0,1,\dots,2J-1,2J\}
. For any site
j\in\{1,\dots,L\}
, we interpret the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{N}^{loc}_{j}
as the number of particles located at site
j
. We then define the
total particle number operator

\mathcal{N}_{L}
as

(2.3)

\mathcal{N}_{L}:=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathcal{N}_{j}^{loc}\>,

where the eigenvalues
\sigma(\mathcal{N}_{L})=\{0,1,2,\dots,2JL-1,2JL\}
of
\mathcal{N}_{L}
are consequently interpreted as the total number of particles. It can now be verified that
[H_{L},\mathcal{N}_{L}]=0
, and thus we decompose
\mathcal{H}_{L}=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{2JL}\mathcal{H}_{L}^{N}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{L}^{N}
denotes the eigenspace of
\mathcal{N}_{L}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
N
– the space of all
N
-particle configurations in
\{1,2,\dots,L\}
with the restriction that no site can be occupied by more than
2J
particles. We also define
H_{L}^{N}:=H_{L}\upharpoonright_{\mathcal{H}_{L}^{N}}
. Now, let

(2.4)

{\bf{M}}^{N}_{L}:=\left\{{\bf{m}}:\{1,2,\dots,L\}\rightarrow\{0,1,\dots,2J\}:\sum_{j=1}^{L}{\bf{m}}(j)=N\right\}\>,

be the set of all functions from
\{1,2,\dots,L\}
to
\{0,1,\dots,2J\}
whose values add up to
N
. For convenience, we also define
{\bf M}_{L}:=\bigcup_{N=0}^{2JL}{\bf M}_{L}^{N}
– the set of
all
functions from
\{1,2,\dots,L\}
to
\{0,1,\dots,2J\}
. Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=0}^{2J}

\mathcal{N}^{loc}

This means in particular that for any

2.2.
Combes–Thomas estimate and bounds on spectral projections

One of the main ingredients of the proof of the bound for the entanglement entropy will be a bound on spectral projections. To be more specific, let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{S}_{L}
be a set of configurations. For any such
\mathcal{A}
, we define
P_{\mathcal{A}}
to be the orthogonal projection onto the subspace of functions that are supported on
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.

(2.27)

(P_{\mathcal{A}}f)(X)=\begin{cases}f(X)\quad&\mbox{if}\quad X\in\mathcal{A}\\
0\quad&\mbox{if}\quad X\in\mathbb{S}_{L}\setminus\mathcal{A}\end{cases}\>.

For later convenience, we also define
\overline{P}_{\mathcal{A}}:={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-P_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

For any
N\in\{1,2,\dots,2JL\}
, let
Y_{N}
be an arbitrary multiplication operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})
and
z\notin\sigma(H_{N}+Y_{N})
such that there exists
\kappa_{z}>0
for which

(2.28)

\left\|V_{N}^{1/2}(H_{N}+Y_{N}-z)^{-1}V_{N}^{1/2}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{z}}<\infty\>.

Then for all subsets
\mathcal{A,B}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
, we have

(2.29)

\left\|P_{\mathcal{A}}\left(H_{N}+Y_{N}-z\right)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{B}}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{V_{N,0}}\left\|P_{\mathcal{A}}V_{N}^{1/2}\left(H_{N}+Y_{N}-z\right)^{-1}V_{N}^{1/2}P_{\mathcal{B}}\right\|\leq\frac{2}{V_{N,0}\kappa_{z}}\,e^{-\eta_{z}d^{N}(\mathcal{A,B})}\>,

where

(2.30)

\eta_{z}=\log\left(1+\frac{\Delta\kappa_{z}}{4J}\right).

Proof.

An abstract result of this form for Schrödinger-type operators whose kinetic term is controlled by the potential such as in Proposition
2.17
was shown in
[
1
, Prop. 3.1]
. The theorem now follows from plugging in the constants particular to this model.
∎

Now, let
K\in\mathbb{N}
and for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, define
E_{K,\delta}:=\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)(K+1-\delta)
. Moreover, for any non-negative multiplication operator
W_{N}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})
, let
Q_{K,\delta}^{N}\equiv Q^{N}_{K,\delta}(L,W_{N}):={\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}_{[0,E_{K,\delta}]}(H_{N}(L)+W_{N})
be the spectral projection of
H_{N}+W_{N}
associated to the energy interval
[0,E_{K,\delta}]
. Lastly, for any
K\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the set
\mathbb{S}_{L,K}^{N}:=\{X_{\bf m}:{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L,K}^{N}\}
, where the sets
{\bf M}_{L,K}^{N}
were introduced in Definition
2.7
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
be a set of configurations. We then get the following estimate:

(2.31)

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\mathcal{A}}Q_{K,\delta}^{N}\right\|=\left\|Q_{K,\delta}^{N}P_{\mathcal{A}}\right\|\leq C_{N,K}e^{-\mu_{K}d^{N}(\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N})},

where

(2.32)

\displaystyle C_{N,K}

\displaystyle=C_{N,K}(\Delta,\delta,J)=\max\left\{1,\frac{8K(K+1)}{V_{N,0}\delta^{2}}\right\}

(2.33)

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mu_{K}

\displaystyle=\mu_{K}(\Delta,\delta,J)=\log\left(1+\frac{\delta(\Delta-2J)}{16J(K+1)}\right).

Proof.

We follow ideas from
[
6
, Proof of Lemma 8.2]
and
[
11
]
. If
\mathcal{A}\cap{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}\neq\emptyset
, which implies
d^{N}(\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N})=0
, then (
2.31
) immediately follows from
C_{N,K}\geq 1
. Hence, assume
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}\setminus{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}
from now on. Then, let us choose
Y_{N}=W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}
with
\gamma=\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)K
and show that the operator

H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.9
. Observe that for any
E\in[0,E_{K,\delta/2}]
one has

(2.34)

\displaystyle V_{N}^{-1/2}(H_{N}+Y_{N}-E)V_{N}^{-1/2}\geq-\frac{1}{2\Delta}V_{N}^{-1/2}A_{N}V_{N}^{-1/2}+{\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}V_{N}^{-1}-EV_{N}^{-1}

(2.35)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)+\left(\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)K-E_{K,\delta/2}\right)V_{N}^{-1}P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-E_{K,\delta/2}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}V_{N}^{-1}

(2.36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)\left({\mathchoice{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.0mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-4.5mul}}{\mathrm{1\mskip-5.0mul}}}-(1-\delta/2)V_{N}^{-1}P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-(K+1-\delta/2)V_{N}^{-1}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\right)\>.

Now, note that

(2.37)

-(1-\delta/2)V^{-1}_{N}P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\geq-(1-\delta/2)P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\quad\mbox{as well as}\quad-V^{-1}_{N}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\geq-\frac{1}{K+1}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\>,

which we use to further estimate (
2.36
):

(2.38)

\eqref{eq:estimate}\geq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)\left(\frac{\delta}{2}P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}+\frac{\delta/2}{K+1}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)\frac{\delta}{2(K+1)}\>.

From this, it can be concluded that for any
E\in[0,E_{K,\delta}]
, we have
E\notin\sigma(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}})
and moreover that

(2.39)

\|V_{N}^{1/2}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-E)^{-1}V_{N}^{1/2}\|\leq\frac{2(K+1)}{\delta\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)}

and, by a slight modification (see
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
), one gets for any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
:

(2.40)

\|V_{N}^{1/2}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-E+i\varepsilon)^{-1}V_{N}^{1/2}\|\leq\frac{4(K+1)}{\delta\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)}\>.

From Theorem
2.9
, we therefore get for any
\mathcal{A}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}\setminus{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}

(2.41)

\|P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-E+i\varepsilon)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{A}}\|\leq\frac{8(K+1)}{V_{N,0}\cdot\delta\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)}e^{-\mu_{K}d^{N}(\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N})}\>,

for any
E\in[0,E_{K,\delta/2}]
and any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
. The constant
\mu_{K}=\mu_{K}(\Delta,\delta,J)
is given by

(2.42)

\mu_{K}=\log\left(1+\frac{\delta(\Delta-2J)}{16J(K+1)}\right)\>.

Now, let
\Gamma
be the circle centered at
\frac{1}{2}E_{K,\delta}
with radius
R:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)(K+1)
. Note that this implies
\dist(\Gamma,[0,E_{K,\delta}])=\frac{\delta}{2}\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)=:\delta^{\prime}
. Moreover, it follows from Proposition
2.17
that

(2.43)

\displaystyle H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\geq-\frac{1}{2\Delta}A_{N}+V_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}\geq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)V_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}

(2.44)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)(V_{N}+K)P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}+\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)V_{N}\overline{P}_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}

(2.45)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)(K+1)\>,

which means that there is no spectrum of
H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}
inside the circle
\Gamma
, which implies

(2.46)

\oint_{\Gamma}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}\text{d}z=0\>.

We therefore get

(2.47)

\displaystyle Q_{K,\delta}^{N}=

\displaystyle(Q_{K,\delta}^{N})^{2}=\frac{i}{2\pi}Q_{K,\delta}^{N}\oint_{\Gamma}(H_{N}+W_{N}-z)^{-1}\text{d}z

(2.48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}Q_{K,\delta}^{N}\oint_{\Gamma}[(H_{N}+W_{N}-z)^{-1}-(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}]\text{d}z

(2.49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i\gamma}{2\pi}Q_{K,\delta}^{N}\oint_{\Gamma}(H_{N}+W_{N}-z)^{-1}P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}\text{d}z

We then proceed to estimate

(2.50)

\displaystyle\|Q_{K,\delta}^{N}P_{\mathcal{A}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma R\max_{z\in\Gamma}\left[\|Q_{K,\delta}^{N}(H_{N}+W_{N}-z)^{-1}\|\|P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{A}}\|\right]

(2.51)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma R}{\delta^{\prime}}\max_{z\in\Gamma}|\|P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{A}}\|\>,

where we have used
\|Q_{K,\delta}^{N}(H_{N}+W_{N}-z)^{-1}\|\leq(\delta^{\prime})^{-1}
. Note that for any
z\in\Gamma
, we have

(2.52)

\operatorname{Re}z\leq\left(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta}\right)\left(K+1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)=E_{K,\delta/2}\>,

which means that we can apply (
2.41
) to estimate
\|P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}(H_{N}+W_{N}+\gamma P_{{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N}}-z)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{A}}\|
uniformly in
z\in\Gamma
. We then get

(2.53)

\eqref{eq:specCT}\leq\frac{8\gamma R(K+1)}{V_{N,0}\delta^{\prime}\delta(1-\frac{2J}{\Delta})}e^{-\mu_{K}d^{N}(\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N})}=\frac{8K(K+1)}{V_{N,0}\delta^{2}}e^{-\mu_{K}d^{N}(\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{N})}\>,

which is the desired result.
∎

2.3.
Entanglement Entropy

In what follows, we will mainly use ideas and previous results from
[
1
, Sec. 5]
. Let
{\mathbb{S}}_{L}:=\bigcup_{N=0}^{2JL}{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
, which allows us to identify

(2.56)

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L})=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{2JL}\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})\>.

Next, let
\psi\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L})
be normalized.
We denote the associated density matrix by
\rho_{\psi}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
2
2

2

Using physicist’s notation, for any
\alpha,\beta
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the symbol “
|\alpha\rangle\langle\beta|
” denotes the rank-one operator
\psi\mapsto\alpha\langle\beta,\psi\rangle
for any
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
.

Analogous to (
2.21
), for any subset
\Gamma\subset\Lambda_{L}
, and any
N\in\{1,\dots,2J|\Gamma|\}
, we define

(2.57)

{\mathbb{S}}_{\Gamma}^{N}:=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\in\Gamma^{N}:x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{N}\mbox{ and }\min_{k\in\{1,2,\dots,N-2J\}}(x_{k+2J}-x_{k})\geq 1\right\}

and – as before – we set
{\mathbb{S}}_{\Gamma}^{0}:=\{\emptyset\}
.
Moreover,
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Gamma})
is defined analogously to (
2.56
).
Now, let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1<\ell<L
. Then,
\Lambda_{L}=\Lambda_{\ell}\cup\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}
is a spatial bipartition of
\Lambda_{L}
into two disjoint discrete subintervals, with the corresponding decomposition of the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L})=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}})\otimes\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}})
. Moreover, for any
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}
and any
Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}
, which implies that
X\cup Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}
, one naturally identifies
\phi_{X\cup Y}=\phi_{X}\otimes\phi_{Y}
.
3
3

3

For two multisets
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{j})\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{K})\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}
, the notation “
X\cup Y
” denotes the multiset
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{j},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}
. This means in particular that
{\bf m}_{X\cup Y}={\bf m}_{X}+{\bf m}_{Y}
.

The reduced state
\rho_{1}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}})\rightarrow\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}})
is the linear operator given by

(2.58)

\rho_{1}(\psi,\Lambda_{\ell})\equiv\rho_{1}=\mbox{Tr}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}(\rho_{\psi})=\sum_{X_{1},X_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}}\psi(X_{1}\cup Y)\overline{\psi(X_{2}\cup Y)}|\phi_{X_{1}}\rangle\langle\phi_{X_{2}}|

where
\mbox{Tr}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}(\cdot)
denotes the partial trace over the subsystem
\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}
.
Then, the entanglement entropy of
\rho_{\psi}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})
is given by

(2.59)

\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})=-\mbox{Tr}(\rho_{1}\log\rho_{1})=:\mathcal{S}(\rho_{1})\>,

where
\mathcal{S}(\rho_{1})
denotes the von Neumann entropy of the reduced state
\rho_{1}
.
4
4

4

We adapt the convention
0\log 0=0
.
As in
[
1
,
4
]
, we will actually show estimates for the
\alpha
-Rényi entropies
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
of
\rho_{1}
, which are given by

(2.60)

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1}):=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]\>,

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since for every
\alpha\in(0,1)
one has
\mathcal{S}(\rho_{1})\leq\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1})
, showing suitable bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}(\rho_{1})
will then readily imply the desired result for the entanglement entropy
\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})
. Let us now further analyze (
2.58

(2.61)

\rho_{1}=\sum_{X_{1},X_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\psi(X_{1})\overline{\psi(X_{2})}|\phi_{X_{1}}\rangle\langle\phi_{X_{2}}|+\sum_{X_{1},X_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}}\psi(X_{1}\cup Y)\overline{\psi(X_{2}\cup Y)}|\phi_{X_{1}}\rangle\langle\phi_{X_{2}}|\>,

where the first sum simply corresponds to the contributions in (
2.58
), for which
Y=\emptyset
. Now, let the vector
\Psi\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}})
be given by
\Psi:=\sum_{X_{3}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\psi(X_{3})\phi_{X_{3}}
, which clearly satisfies
\|\Psi\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}})}\leq\|\psi\|_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L})}
and observe that

(2.62)

\sum_{X_{1},X_{2}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\psi(X_{1})\overline{\psi(X_{2})}|\phi_{X_{1}}\rangle\langle\phi_{X_{2}}|=|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|\>.

So, we write
\rho_{1}=|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|+\hat{\rho}_{1}
with
\hat{\rho}_{1}
being equal to the second sum in (
2.61
).
Let us now focus on
\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]
:

(2.63)

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\mbox{Tr}[(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|+\hat{\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq 2\mbox{Tr}[(|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|)^{\alpha}]+2\mbox{Tr}[(\hat{\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq 2+2\mbox{Tr}[(\hat{\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}]\>,

where we have used the quasi-norm property of
\mbox{Tr}|\cdot|^{\alpha}
, cf.
[
22
, Satz 3.21]
and the fact that
|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|
is a non-negative rank-one operator with norm less than or equal to one.
Let us now further estimate

(2.64)

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[(\hat{\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\langle\phi_{X},(\hat{\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}\phi_{X}\rangle\leq\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}}\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=0}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle^{\alpha}

(2.65)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{4J-1}{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}\right|^{1-\alpha}+\sum_{j=4J}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle^{\alpha}

where we have used Jensen’s inequality for the first estimate in (
2.64
) and the second estimate (
2.65
) just follows from maximizing
\sum\sum\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle^{\alpha}
under the constraint
\sum\sum\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle\leq 1
. We estimate further by noting
|{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}|\leq\ell^{j}
and thus – assuming
\ell\geq 4J
–

(2.66)

\eqref{eq:teilzwei}\leq\ell^{4J(1-\alpha)}+\sum_{j=4J}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle^{\alpha}\>.

Now, for each
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}
with
|X|=j
, we get

(2.67)

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{X},\hat{\rho}_{1}\phi_{X}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}}|\psi(X\cup Y)|^{2}=\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\psi\|^{2}\>,

where given any
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}
, we have defined
\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\{X\cup Y:Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}\setminus\{\emptyset\}\}
.
Altogether, these considerations show the following

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\ell\geq 4J
and
\psi\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L})
be normalized. Then, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we get

(2.68)

\mbox{\emph{Tr}}[({\rho}_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq 2+2\ell^{4J(1-\alpha)}+2\sum_{j=4J}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\psi\|^{2\alpha}\>.

3.
Proof of the logarithmically corrected entanglement bound

3.1.
Local distance estimates

The following lemma, whose proof can be found in Appendix
B.2
, provides us with an estimate for the distance of a configuration in
{\mathbb{S}}_{L}
to the nearest configuration in
{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{j})\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}
with
j\geq 4J
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}^{k}
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for any
K\geq 4J^{2}
, we have the following estimate:

(3.1)

d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j})\leq d^{j+k}(X\cup Y,{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{j+k})\>.

So, the main observation made in Lemma
3.1
is that there always exists a configuration in
{\mathbb{S}}^{j}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}
which is at least as close to
X
as any configuration in
{\mathbb{S}}^{j+k}_{L,K}
might be to
X\cup Y
.
We now combine Lemmas
2.68
,
3.1
and Theorem
2.10
. To this end, for any non-negative function
W:{\mathbb{S}}_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
, let us define
Q_{K,\delta}\equiv Q_{K,\delta}(L,W):=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{2JL}Q_{K,\delta}^{N}(L,W_{N})
– the spectral projection of the full Hamiltonian onto the energy interval
[0,E_{K,\delta}]
with background potential
W
. Here,
W_{N}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})\rightarrow\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})
, denotes the multiplication operator induced by
W
, i.e.
(W_{N}f)(X):=W(X)f(X)
, for any
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N})
and any
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\ell\geq 4J
. Let
\psi\in\mbox{\emph{ran}}(Q_{K,\delta})
be normalized. Then, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have the following estimate

(3.2)

\mbox{\emph{Tr}}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq 2+2\ell^{4J(1-\alpha)}+2C_{K}^{\prime 2\alpha}\sum_{j=4J}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-2\alpha\mu_{K}d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K})}\>,

where

(3.3)

C_{K}^{\prime}=\max\left\{1,\frac{2K(K+1)}{J^{2}\delta^{2}}\right\}\>.

Proof.

Let
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}
, where
j\geq 4J
. Defining
\mathcal{A}_{X,k}=\{X\cup Y:Y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{k}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}\}
and using that

(3.4)

P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}Q_{K,\delta}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{2J(L-\ell)}P_{\mathcal{A}_{X,k}}Q_{K,\delta}^{j+k}

we get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\psi\|

\displaystyle=\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}Q_{K,\delta}\psi\|\leq\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}Q_{K,\delta}\|=\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,2J(L-\ell)\}}\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X,k}}Q_{K,\delta}^{j+k}\|

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,2J(L-\ell)\}}\left(C_{j+k,K}\cdot e^{-\mu_{K}d^{j+k}(\mathcal{A}_{X,k},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{j+k})}\right)

where we have used Theorem
2.10
for the last inequality and the constants
C_{j+k,K}
and
\mu_{K}
were given in (
2.32
). Now, since
j+k\geq 4J
by assumption, it follows from Proposition
2.5
that
V_{j+k,0}=4J^{2}
and thus

(3.7)

C_{j+k,K}=\max\left\{1,\frac{2K(K+1)}{J^{2}\delta^{2}}\right\}=C^{\prime}_{K}\>,

independently of
j
. Now, for any
k\in\{1,\dots,2J(L-\ell)\}
, observe that by Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.8)

d^{j+k}(\mathcal{A}_{X,k},{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{j+k})=\min_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}^{c}}^{k}}d^{j+k}(X\cup Y,{\mathbb{S}}_{L,K}^{j+k})\geq d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j})

and thus we may proceed to estimate (
3.6
) to get

(3.9)

\|P_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\psi\|\leq\eqref{eq:findmax}\leq C^{\prime}_{K}\cdot e^{-\mu_{K}d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K})}\>.

Using Lemma
2.68
, we consequently find

(3.10)

\mbox{Tr}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq 2+2\ell^{4J(1-\alpha)}+2C_{K}^{\prime 2\alpha}\sum_{j=4J}^{2J\ell}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-2\alpha\mu_{K}d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}^{j}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K})}\>,

which shows (
3.2
).

3.2.
Estimates using geometric series

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}^{N}=\left\{X=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{N}\right\}
be the set of spin–1/2
configurations on the infinite chain. Moreover, let
C=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{N})=(c,c+1,\ldots,c+(N-1))
be an arbitrary configuration of
N
consecutive particles (
c\in\mathbb{Z}
). Then, for any
\gamma>0
, we get

(3.11)

\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}:=\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}<\infty\>.

Proof.

Define

\mathcal{X}^{N,0}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N}:x_{1}\geq c_{1}\right\},\;\;\;\;\mathcal{X}^{N,N}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N}:x_{N}<c_{N}\right\}

and for any
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,N-1\right\}
,

\mathcal{X}^{N,j}=\left\{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N}:x_{j}<c_{j},\;x_{j+1}\geq c_{j+1}\right\}\>.

Then,
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{N}=\uplus_{j=0}^{N}\mathcal{X}^{N,j}
, where “
\uplus
” denotes a disjoint union. For any
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,N-1\right\}
, we get by an argument similar to
[
9
, Lemma A.3]
that

(3.12)

\begin{split}\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N,j}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}&=\sum_{x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{j}<c_{j}}e^{-\gamma(|x_{1}-c_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{j}-c_{j}|)}\sum_{c_{j+1}\leq x_{j+1}<x_{j+2}<\cdots<x_{N}}e^{-\gamma(|x_{j+1}-c_{j+1}|+\cdots+|x_{N}-c_{N}|)}\\
&\leq e^{-\gamma j}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{j}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N-j}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)\leq e^{-\gamma j}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}\>.\\
\end{split}

Analogously, we find

(3.13)

\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N,0}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\leq\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\leq\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}

(3.14)

\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N,N}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\leq e^{-\gamma N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\leq e^{-\gamma N}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}\>.

Hence,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{X\in\mathcal{X}^{N,j}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}e^{-\gamma j}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\right)\left(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-e^{-k\gamma}}\right)^{2}\>,

which is the desired result. The infinite product’s convergence follows from elementary facts.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

For any
{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}^{N}
, let
\supp({\bf m}):=\{j\in\{1,2,\dots,L\}:{\bf m}(j)\neq 0\}
denote the support of
{\bf m}
. Then, for any
L
, we define the set
{\bf K}_{L}
of “building bocks” in
\Lambda_{L}
as follows

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{L}

\displaystyle:=\{{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}:|\mbox{supp}({\bf m})|=1\}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\cup\{{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}:\exists\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda_{L}:{\bf m}(i)=2J\mbox{ if }\alpha\leq i\leq\beta\mbox{ and }{\bf m}(i)=0\mbox{ else}\}\>.

In other words, building blocks are configurations of either a single column of up to
2J
particles or of discrete intervals, where each site is occupied by exactly
2J
particles (rectangular blocks).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\gamma>0
. Then for any
C\in{\bf K}_{L}
, we have

(3.19)

\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\leq\mathcal{L}^{2J}_{\gamma}

Proof.

Let
C
be a rectangular block, i.e.
\;C=(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2J\;\;\text{times}}}{{\overbrace{c,\ldots,c}}},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2J\;\;\text{times}}}{{\overbrace{c+1,\ldots,c+1}}},\ldots,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2J\;\;\text{times}}}{{\overbrace{c+m,\ldots,c+m}}})
where
N=2J(m+1)
. For any
N
-particle configuration
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}
, let
Z_{0}(X)
denote the constraint

(3.20)

Z_{0}(X):x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{N}\>.

Moreover, define the additional constraints

(3.21)

Z_{i}(X):x_{i}<x_{i+2J}<x_{i+4J}<\cdots<x_{i+2JR_{i}}

where
i\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,2J\right\}
and
R_{i}=\max\left\{R\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}:i+2JR\leq N\right\}
.

Let

(3.22)

\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}:=\left\{X\in\Lambda^{N}_{L}:\;Z_{i}(X)\;\text{ holds }\;\forall\;i=0,\ldots,2J\right\}

and

(3.23)

\Gamma^{N}:=\left\{X\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:\;Z_{k}(X)\;\text{ holds }\;\forall\;k=1,\ldots,2J\right\}\>.

Obviously,
\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}\subseteq\Gamma^{N}
. To see that
\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}={\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}
(defined in (
2.21
)), note that it is obvious that
\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}\subseteq{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}
since for any
X\in\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}
, the constraints
Z_{i}(X)
imply that
\min\left\{(x_{k+2J}-x_{k}):k=1,\ldots,N-2J\right\}\geq 1
.

Conversely, suppose there exists
X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}\setminus\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}
, i.e.
X
violates at least one constraint,
Z_{t}(X)
say, where
t\neq 0
. Then there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{t+2Jr}\geq x_{t+2J(r+1)}
. This is a contradiction since
X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}
. Hence,
{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}=\mathbb{M}^{N}_{L}
.

Therefore, by Lemma (
3.3
),

(3.24)

\begin{split}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}&\leq\sum_{X\in\Gamma^{N}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\\
&=\sum_{Z_{1}(X)}e^{\displaystyle-\gamma(|x_{1}-c_{1}|+\cdots+|x_{1+2JR_{1}}-c_{1+2JR_{1}}|)}\times\cdots\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\cdots\times\sum_{Z_{2J}(X)}e^{\displaystyle-\gamma(|x_{2J}-c_{2J}|+\cdots+|x_{2J+2JR_{{}_{2J}}}-c_{2J+2JR_{{}_{2J}}}|)}\\
&\leq\mathcal{L}^{2J}_{\gamma}\>.\end{split}

If
C=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N})
is a column block, i.e.
c_{1}=\cdots=c_{N}=c
, where
c\in\Lambda_{L}
and
N\leq 2J
, then

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}}e^{-\gamma d^{N}(X,C)}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{-\infty<x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots\leq x_{N}<\infty}e^{-\gamma[|x_{1}-c|+\cdots+|x_{N}-c|]}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\gamma[|x_{1}-c|+\cdots+|x_{N}-c|]}=\left(\frac{1+e^{-\gamma}}{1-e^{-\gamma}}\right)^{N}\leq\mathcal{L}^{N}_{\gamma}\leq\mathcal{L}^{2J}_{\gamma}\>,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that for this result, we are making use of the generalized Pauli principle, which requires that no site be occupied by more than
2J
particles.

3.3.
Building blocks and potential

Given a value
K\in\mathbb{N}
and a particle number
N\geq 4J
, it seems rather cumbersome to give a full description of all configurations in
{\bf M}_{L,K}^{N}
. However, it can be shown that any configuration in
{\bf M}_{L,K}^{N}
can be composed out of no more than
K
of the “building blocks” as described in Definition
3.4
before.

Definition 3.7
.

Given a configuration
{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}
, we define the quantity
B({\bf m})
as follows:

(3.27)

B({\bf m}):=\left|\{i\in\{1,2,\dots,L-1\}:{\bf m}(i)+{\bf m}(i+1)\notin\{0,4J\}\}\right|+(2-\delta_{{\bf m}(1),0}-\delta_{{\bf m}(L),0})\>.

Moreover, for any
R\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(3.28)

{\bf B}_{L,R}:=\{{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}:B({\bf m})\leq R\}\quad\mbox{as well as}\quad{\bf B}_{L,R}^{N}:={\bf B}_{L,R}\cap{\bf M}_{L}^{N}

for any
N\in\{0,1,\dots,2JL\}
. Additionally, for multisets, we introduce
\mathbb{B}_{L,R}=\{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}:{\bf m}_{X}\in{\bf B}_{L,R}\}
and
\mathbb{B}_{L,R}^{N}=\mathbb{B}_{L,R}\cap{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
.

Remark 3.8
.

The purpose of
B({\bf m})
is to count the number of neighboring sites
\{i,i+1\}
,
i=1,2,\dots,L-1
, which are not both occupied by either
0
or
2J
particles. Each of the two additional terms in
(2-\delta_{{\bf m}(1),0}-\delta_{{\bf m}(L),0})=(1-\delta_{{\bf m}(1),0})+(1-\delta_{{\bf m}(L),0})
increases
B({\bf m})
by a value of one if the sites
1
or
L
are occupied by any particles (which formally corresponds to including the edges
\{0,1\}
and
\{L,L+1\}
in the count).

Remark 3.9
.

Observe that
B({\bf m})\leq J^{-1}V({\bf m})
for every
{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}
. This follows from the fact that
v({\bf m}(i),{\bf m}(i+1))=0
if and only if
{\bf m}(i)={\bf m}(i+1)=0
or
{\bf m}(i)={\bf m}(i+1)=2J
and
v({\bf m}(i),{\bf m}(i+1))\geq J
else, and comparing the extra term
(2-\delta_{{\bf m}(1),0}-\delta_{{\bf m}(L),0})
in (
3.27
) with the boundary field term
J({\bf m}(1)+{\bf m}(L))
in (
2.14
). For
K\geq 4J^{2}
, define
\widetilde{K}:=\lfloor K/J\rfloor
, and observe that this implies

(3.29)

{{\mathbb{S}}}_{L,K}\subset{\mathbb{B}}_{L,\widetilde{K}}\quad\mbox{and thus in particular}\quad{{\mathbb{S}}}^{N}_{L,{K}}\subset{\mathbb{B}}^{N}_{L,\widetilde{K}}\>.

It is now crucial to observe that any configuration in
{\bf B}_{L,\widetilde{K}}
can always by obtained by composing it out of at most
(\widetilde{K}-1)
“building blocks”.

Remark 3.10
.

Observe that for any
{\bf m}\in{\bf M}_{L}
, there exist
\{{\bf k}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{\tau}\subset{\bf K}_{L}
, (
\tau\leq L
), with pairwise disjoint supports, such that
{\bf m}=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}{\bf k}_{i}
. If in addition, we have
{\bf m}\in{\bf B}_{L,\widetilde{K}}
, it follows from (
3.27
) that
\tau\leq(\widetilde{K}-1)
, i.e. any configuration in
{\bf B}_{L,\widetilde{K}}
can be composed out of no more than
(\widetilde{K}-1)
building blocks. See Figure
3.1
for a pictorial representation of building blocks.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
j\in\{4J,4J+1,\dots,2J\ell\}
,
K\in\{4J^{2},4J^{2}+1,\dots,2J\ell\}
and
\widetilde{K}:=\lfloor K/J\rfloor
. Then, for any
\gamma>0
, we get the following estimate

(3.30)

\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j})}\leq(4Je)^{\widetilde{K}-2}\mathcal{L}_{2\alpha\mu_{K}}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}\ell^{2\widetilde{K}-3}\>.

Proof.

Analogously to before, we define
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}:=\mathbb{B}_{L,\widetilde{K}}^{j}\cap{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}
and observe that due to (
3.29
), we have
{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j}\subset\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}
and thus, we get

(3.31)

\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j})}\leq\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,Y)}=\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j}}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,Y)}\leq\sum_{Y\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}}\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,Y)}\>.

We now claim that for every
Y\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}
, we get

(3.32)

\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,Y)}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}\>.

Since
Y\in\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}
, observe that by Remark
3.10
, there exist
{\bf k}^{(1)},{\bf k}^{(2)},\dots,{\bf k}^{(\tau)}\in{\bf K}_{L}
with
\tau\leq(\widetilde{K}-1)
and pairwise disjoint support such that

(3.33)

{\bf k}^{(1)}+{\bf k}^{(2)}+\cdots+{\bf k}^{(\tau)}={\bf m}_{Y}\>.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that the building blocks are ordered such that
i<j
implies
\max\supp({\bf k}^{(i)})<\min\supp({\bf k}^{(j)})
.

For any
i\in\{1,2\dots,\tau\}
, let
K^{(i)}:=X_{{\bf k}^{(i)}}
denote the multiset associated with the building block
{\bf k}^{(i)}
and thus
Y=K^{(1)}\cup K^{(2)}\cup\dots\cup K^{(\tau)}
. Now, decompose any
X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}
analogously into
X=X^{(1)}\cup X^{(2)}\cup\dots\cup X^{(\tau)}
such that
|X^{(i)}|=|K^{(i)}|=:k_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\tau\}
and
i<j
implies that
\max(X^{(i)})<\min(X^{(j)})
.

We then get

(3.34)

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X^{(1)}\cup\dots\cup X^{(\tau)}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma\left(d^{k_{1}}(X^{(1)},K^{(1)})+\cdots+d^{k_{\tau}}(X^{(\tau)},K^{(\tau)})\right)}

(3.35)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\tau}\left(\sum_{X^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{k_{i}}}e^{-\gamma d^{k_{i}}(X^{(i)},K^{(i)})}\right)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{2J\tau}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}\>,

where we have used Lemma
3.5
for estimating the sum in (
3.35
). This shows (
3.32
) which together with (
3.31
) proves that

(3.36)

\sum_{X\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell}}^{j}}e^{-\gamma d^{j}(X,{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda_{\ell},K}^{j})}\leq\sum_{Y\in{\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}^{j}}}\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}=|\mathbb{B}_{{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}}^{j}|\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}\>,

which means that we need to further estimate the number of configurations in
\Lambda_{\ell}
of
(\widetilde{K}-1)
or less building blocks of
j
particles. We do this by a rather rough combinatorial argument: firstly, note that distributing
j
particles into up to
(\widetilde{K}-1)
building blocks can be estimated by
\binom{j+\widetilde{K}-2}{\widetilde{K}-2}
. This is clearly an overestimate, since it disregards the constraints in (
3.17
) which building blocks have to satisfy. Next, we have to account for all the possible ways, those
(\widetilde{K}-1)
or less building blocks can be placed in
\Lambda_{\ell}
. A trivial upper bound for this is given by
\ell^{\widetilde{K}-1}
, since there are up to
\ell
sites one could place each individual building block (disregarding the fact that the supports of the building blocks have to be disjoint and can be larger than one and thus again overestimating).
If
\widetilde{K}>2
, we therefore conclude

(3.37)

|\mathbb{B}_{{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}}^{j}|\leq\binom{j+\widetilde{K}-2}{\widetilde{K}-2}\cdot\ell^{\widetilde{K}-1}\leq\left(\frac{(2J\ell+\widetilde{K}-2)e}{\widetilde{K}-2}\right)^{\widetilde{K}-2}\ell^{\widetilde{K}-1}\leq(4Je)^{\widetilde{K}-2}\ell^{2\widetilde{K}-3}\>,

where we have used the estimate
\binom{\alpha}{\beta}\leq\left(\frac{\alpha e}{\beta}\right)^{\beta}
for the binomial coefficient as well as the fact that
j\leq 2J\ell
and
4J\leq\widetilde{K}\leq 2\ell
. For the special case
\widetilde{K}=2
, which can only occur of
J=1/2
, Equation (
3.37
) still follows since
|\mathbb{B}_{{\Lambda_{\ell},\widetilde{K}}}^{j}|\leq\ell
. This shows the lemma.

∎

3.4.
Proof of the main result

We are now prepared to show our main result:

Theorem 3.12
.

For any
K\in\{4J^{2},4J^{2}+1,\dots\}
and any
\delta>0
, we get the following estimate

(3.38)

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\>\limsup_{L\rightarrow\infty}\>\frac{\sup_{W}\>\sup_{\psi}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})}{\log\ell}\leq{2\lfloor K/J\rfloor-2}\>,

where the suprema are taken over all non-negative functions
W:{\mathbb{S}}_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and over all normalized elements
\psi\in\mbox{\emph{ran}}(Q_{K,\delta}(L,W))
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
W:{\mathbb{S}}_{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
be an arbitrary non-negative background potential and let
\psi
be an arbitrary normalized element of
\mbox{ran}(Q_{K,\delta}(L,W))
. Combining Corollary
3.2
and Lemma
3.30
(with the choice
\gamma=2\alpha\mu_{K}
) and using that for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
\alpha
-Rényi entropy is an upper bound for the von Neumann entropy, we obtain

(3.39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\operatorname{{\rm Tr}}[(\rho_{1})^{\alpha}]\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(2+2\ell^{4J(1-\alpha)}+(4Je)^{\widetilde{K}-1}C_{K}^{\prime 2\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{2\alpha\mu_{K}}^{2J(\widetilde{K}-1)}\ell^{2\widetilde{K}-2}\right)\>,

which does not depend on
L
. We therefore get

(3.40)

\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\>\limsup_{L\rightarrow\infty}\>\frac{\sup_{W}\>\sup_{\psi}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\psi})}{\log\ell}\leq\frac{2\widetilde{K}-2}{1-\alpha}=\frac{2\lfloor{K}/J\rfloor-2}{1-\alpha}\>.

Since this is true for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, one can take
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, which yields the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Note that for
J=1/2
, the constant in (
3.38
), is given by
(4K-2)
. In
[
1
]
, where only the case
J=1/2
was treated, the better bound
(2K-1)
was established. The main reason for this discrepancy is that in this special case, one can actually show that any configuration
{\bf m}
with
V({\bf m})\leq K
can actually be composed out of no more than
K
building blocks rather than out of no more than
{\widetilde{K}}-1=2K-1
building blocks.

Appendix A
Proof of the distance formula

Lemma A.1
.

The graph distance
d^{N}(X,Y)
from
X
to
Y
is given by (
2.26
).

Proof.

Let
\delta^{N}(X,Y):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}-y_{i}|
. To prove that
\delta^{N}(X,Y)
is a lower bound of the graph distance
d^{N}(X,Y)
, we first show that
{\bf m}_{X}\sim{\bf m}_{Y}
if and only if
\delta^{N}(X,Y)=1\;\;\forall\;X,Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
.

For
X,Y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{N}_{L}
, where
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
,
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{N})
, suppose
\delta^{N}(X,Y)=1
. By (
2.26
), there exists
k_{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
such that
|x_{k_{0}}-y_{k_{0}}|=1
and
x_{k}=y_{k}\;\;\forall\;k\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}\setminus\{k_{0}\}
. Define
j_{0}:=x_{k_{0}}
. Therefore
y_{k_{0}}=j_{0}\pm 1
. Now, let
{\bf m}_{X}
and
{\bf m}_{Y}
be the occupation number functions corresponding to
X
and
Y
respectively. Without loss of generality, let
y_{k_{0}}=j_{0}+1
and suppose that
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0})=r
,
r\in\{1,\ldots,2J\}
. Then
{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0})=r-1
, since
y_{k_{0}}\neq j_{0}
. Hence,
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0})-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0})=r-(r-1)=1
. Also, suppose
{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0}+1)=s,\;\;s\in\left\{1,\ldots,2J\right\}

{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0}+1)=s-1
, since

. Therefore,
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0}+1)-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0}+1)=s-1-s=-1
and
{\bf m}_{X}(j)={\bf m}_{Y}(j),\;\;\forall\;j\in\{1,\ldots L\}\setminus\{j_{0},j_{0}+1\}
. Hence,
{\bf m}_{X}\sim{\bf m}_{Y}
.

Conversely, suppose
{\bf m}_{X}\sim{\bf m}_{Y}
. By (
2.12
), there exists a unique
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}
such that
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0})-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0})=\pm 1,\;\;\;{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0}+1)-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0}+1)=\mp 1
and
{\bf m}_{X}(j)={\bf m}_{Y}(j)\;\;\forall\;j\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\setminus\{j_{0},j_{0}+1\}
. Without loss of generality, suppose
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0})-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0})=1
and
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0}+1)-{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0}+1)=-1
. Using the same definitions as before that
j_{0}:=x_{k_{0}}
and
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0})=r
, let
{\bf m}_{X}(j_{0}+1)=s^{\prime}
, i.e.
s^{\prime}=s-1
where
{\bf m}_{Y}(j_{0}+1)=s
. Let
i_{1}=\min\left\{k:x_{k}=j_{0}\right\}
, then
x_{k}=j_{0}
for
k=i_{1},\ldots,i_{1}+(r-1)
and
x_{k}=j_{0}+1
for
k=i_{1}+r,\ldots,i_{1}+r+(s^{\prime}-1)
. Therefore,

(A.1)

\begin{split}\delta^{N}(X,Y)&=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|x_{j}-y_{j}|=0+\cdots+0+|x_{i_{1}+(r-1)}-y_{i_{1}+(r-1)}|+0+\cdots+0\\
&=|j_{0}-(j_{0}+1)|=1\end{split}

\Longrightarrow\;\;

\delta^{N}(X,Y)=1
.

It then follows from the triangle inequality that
\delta^{N}(X,Y)
is a lower bound of the graph distance.

To prove equality, it suffices to show that there exists a path from
X
to
Y
such that the length of the path is
\delta^{N}(X,Y)
.
Let
X,Y\in{\mathbb{S}}_{L}^{N}
and
i_{0}=\min\{k\in\left\{1,\ldots N\right\}:x_{k}\neq y_{k}\}
. Without loss of generality, suppose

(A.2)

\begin{split}Y&=(\ldots,y_{i_{0}-1},y_{i_{0}},y_{i_{0}+1},\ldots)\longrightarrow Y_{1}=(\ldots,y_{i_{0}-1},y_{i_{0}}-1,y_{i_{0}+1},\ldots)\\

and
Y

Define
i_{1}:=\min\{k\in\left\{i_{0}+1,\ldots,N\right\}:x_{k}\neq y_{k}
. Repeating the above process for
x_{i_{1}}<y_{i_{1}}
(with a similar case for
x_{i_{1}}>y_{i_{1}}
), we get another path of length
|x_{i_{1}}-y_{i_{1}}|
. Since

, the process ends at some

such that
i_{s}:=\min\{k\in\left\{i_{0}+s,\ldots,N\right\}:x_{k}\neq y_{k}\}
and repeating the process for

to

of length

(A.3)

\begin{split}=\sum_{j:1\leq j<i_{0}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+|x_{i_{0}}-y_{i_{0}}|&+\sum_{j:i_{0}<j<i_{1}}|x_{j}-y_{j}|+|x_{i_{1}}-y_{i_{1}}|+\cdots\\

=\sum^{N}_{j=1}|x_{j}-y_{j}|=\delta^{N}(X,Y)\>,&\end{split}

which finishes the proof.

p=\min\{i:z_{i}>\ell\}
and
\xi=\max\left\{i:z_{i}\leq\ell-2\right\}
(note that
\xi
exists since if
z_{1}>\ell-2
and
j_{c}<4J-{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}(\ell-1)-{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}(\ell)
, then we would have
j=j_{0}+j_{c}<4J
, a contradiction). Again, we distinguish two cases:

(a)

If
j_{c}\leq 2J-{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}(\ell)
, then
z_{i}=f_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p-1
and
f_{i}=\ell
for
p\leq i\leq j
. Then,

(B.23)

\begin{split}d^{j}(X,Z^{\prime})&=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|x_{i}-z_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(z_{i}-x_{i})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|x_{i}-z_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(z_{i}-f_{i})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(f_{i}-x_{i})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|x_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|z_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|f_{i}-x_{i}|\\
&=d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+\sum_{i=j-j_{c}+1}^{j}|z_{i}-f_{i}|\geq d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+j_{c}\end{split}

(b)

If
j_{c}>2J-{{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}}(\ell)
, let
\eta=j_{c}-(2J-\alpha_{1})
, where
\alpha_{1}={{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}}(\ell)
and
\alpha_{2}={{\bf m}_{Z^{\prime}}}(\ell-1)
. Then, observe that

(B.24)

\left\{\begin{split}f_{i}&=z_{i},\;\;\;\;\;1\leq i\leq\xi+\alpha_{2}\\
f_{i}&=\ell-1,\;\;\;\;\;\xi+\alpha_{2}+1\leq i\leq\xi+\alpha_{2}+n\\
f_{i}&=\ell,\;\;\;\;\;i>\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta\\
z_{i}&=\ell,\;\;\;\;\;\xi+\alpha_{2}+1\leq i\leq p-1\end{split}\right.

Hence,

(B.25)

\begin{split}d^{j}(X,Z^{\prime})&=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}}|x_{i}-z_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{p-1}|x_{i}-z_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(z_{i}-x_{i})\\
\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}}|x_{i}-z_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}(z_{i}-x_{i})+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}(z_{i}-x_{i})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(z_{i}-f_{i})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(f_{i}-x_{i})\\
\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}}|x_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}(z_{i}-f_{i})+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}(f_{i}-x_{i})+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}(z_{i}-f_{i})\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}(f_{i}-x_{i})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(z_{i}-f_{i})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}(f_{i}-x_{i})\end{split}

\begin{split}&=\sum_{i=1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}}|x_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}|f_{i}-x_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}|f_{i}-x_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|f_{i}-x_{i}|\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}|z_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}|z_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|z_{i}-f_{i}|\\
\\
&=d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+1}^{\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta}|z_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=\xi+\alpha_{2}+\eta+1}^{p-1}|z_{i}-f_{i}|+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|z_{i}-f_{i}|\\
\\
&\geq d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+\sum_{i=p}^{j}|z_{i}-f_{i}|\\
&\geq d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+(j-p+1)=d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+j_{c}\end{split}

Moreover, since
{\bf e}(i)\neq{\bf f}(i)
only if
i=\ell-1,\ell
, this implies

(B.26)

d^{j}(X_{\bf e},X_{\bf f})=|{\bf f}(\ell)-{\bf e}(\ell)|\leq j_{c}\>.

We therefore get

(B.27)

d^{j}(X,X_{\bf e})\leq d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+d^{j}(X_{\bf f},X_{\bf e})\leq d^{j}(X,X_{\bf f})+j_{c}\leq d^{j}(X,Z^{\prime})\;,

and thus, we have shown (
3.1
) for Case 2.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Bounds on the Inducibility of Double Loop Graphs
S. Y. Chan, K. Morgan, J. Ugon

Abstract
In the area of extremal graph theory, there exists a problem that investigates the maximum induced density of a k k -vertex graph H H in any n n -vertex graph G G . This is known as the problem of inducibility that was first introduced by Pippenger and Golumbic in 1975. In this paper, we give a new upper bound for the inducibility for a family of Double Loop Graphs of order k k . The upper bound obtained for order k = 5 k=5 is within a factor of 0.964506 of the exact inducibility, and the upper bound obtained for k = 6 k=6 is within a factor of 3 of the best known lower bound.

A problem in extremal graph theory investigates the maximum induced density of a
k
-vertex graph
H

in 1975, which was defined as follows:

Given a graph

by defining the

\mathcal{I}(H,G)=\dfrac{\mathcal{P}(H,G)}{\binom{n}{k}}.

(1)

Let

taken over all graphs of order

\mathcal{I}(H,n)=\dfrac{\mathcal{P}(H,n)}{\binom{n}{k}}.

(2)

Note that

0\leq\mathcal{I}(H,G),\mathcal{I}(H,n)\leq 1.

(3)

Pippenger and Golumbic showed that for any graph

is non-increasing and bounded below by 0 and approaches a limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, the inducibility of

is defined as

\mathcal{I}(H)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}(H,n).

(4)

The exact inducibility

In this section, we introduce constructions for the

of order 5. Using the upper bound obtained in equation (

The inducibility of

By Lemma

\mathcal{I}(DLG(1,2))\leq 1\frac{23}{625}\approx 1.0368
for

.

For the case where
k=6
, we give a construction that gives a lower bound to the inducibility
\mathcal{I}(DLG(1,2))\geq\frac{10}{81}
. This result brings the gap between the upper bound and the lower bound to a factor of
3
. Our construction uses a partitioning method for
DLG(1,2)
for
k=6
and is as follows: Consider a subgraph
C_{4}
of a
DLG(1,2)
.

We take 3 partitions

such that each partition contains

and
P_{2}
give a complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
and
P_{3}
is the set of isolated vertices which are connected to all vertices in
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. From each partition, we pick two vertices. The graph induced by these six vertices is
DLG(1,2)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(DLG(1,2),n)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{2}\cdot\binom{n}{2}=\dfrac{n^{3}(n-1)^{3}}{8}.

(20)

Now, from Equation (
20
), we have:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\dfrac{n^{3}(n-1)^{3}}{8}}{\binom{3n}{6}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n^{3}(n-1)^{3}}{8}\cdot\dfrac{6!}{3n(3n-1)(3n-2)(3n-3)(3n-4)(3n-5)}.

Taking the limit as
N
approaches
\infty
, we obtain the lower bound on the inducibility of
DLG(1,2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(DLG(1,2))

\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{6!\cdot n^{6}}{3^{6}n^{6}}=\dfrac{10}{81}.

(21)

Recall that a graph
G
and it’s complement share the same inducibility, that is
\mathcal{I}(G)=\mathcal{I}({\overline{G}})
. The complement of the

on

gives 3 disjoint

Now for the case

for

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a proof for the upper bound on the inducibility of the family of double loop graphs
DLG(1,2)
in a given graph
G
. For the case when
k=5
, we showed that our upper bound is indeed close to the known inducibility of
K_{5}
, where
\mathcal{I}(K_{5})=1
. We further showed the lower bound for the counts of
DLG(1,2)
for
k=6
. We presented a construction for the
DLG(1,2)
. When compared to the bound given by
Pippenger and Golumbic 1975
for disjoint graphs, we showed that our construction is in fact the best possible construction that maximises the number of
DLG(1,2)
in any given graph
G
.

Future work include finding the lower bound for this class of graphs for
k\geq 8
. We may also consider finding the bounds on the inducibility for the family of graphs that have some symmetrical properties.

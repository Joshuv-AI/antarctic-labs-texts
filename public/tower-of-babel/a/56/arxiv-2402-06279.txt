Spectra of infinite Cayley graphs, examples with pure band spectra Thanks: The author acknowledges support of the Swiss NSF grant 200020-20040
Pierre de la Harpe

Abstract
. It is shown that there are groups Γ \Gamma with finite generating sets S S such that the adjacency operator of the Cayley graph Cay ⁡ ( Γ , S ) {\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S) is a disjoint union of N N intervals, for arbitrarily large integers N N .



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
05C50, 47A10.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph
with a countable (infinite or finite) set
V
of vertices
and a set
E
of edges which are unordered pairs of vertices;
graphs here are simple, i.e., without loops and multiple edges.
The degree of
v\in V
is the number
d_{v}
of edges of the form
\{u,v\}

and we assume that
G
is of bounded degree, that is
\max_{v\in V}d_{v}<\infty
.
The
adjacency operator
of
G

is the linear operator
A_{G}
acting on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(V)
defined by

(A_{G}\xi)(v)=\sum_{u\in V,\{u,v\}\in E}\xi(u)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for}\hskip 14.22636pt\xi\in\ell^{2}(V),\hskip 5.69046ptv\in V.

It is a bounded self-adjoint operator which appears for example in

[
CoSi–57
,
Bigg–93
,
Boll–98
,
BrHa–12
]
for finite graphs
and
[
Kest–59
,
Moha–82
,
MoWo–89
]
for infinite graphs.

Let
A
be a bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Denote by
\Sigma(A)
the spectrum of
A
;
it is a non-empty compact subset of the real line
(non-empty because we assume
\mathcal{H}\neq\{0\}
).
The operator
A
has
pure band spectrum

if its spectrum is the disjoint union of intervals,

\Sigma(A)=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}\mathopen{[}a_{j},b_{j}\mathclose{]}

where
N\geq 1
and
a_{j}<b_{j}<a_{j+1}
for
j=1,\ldots,N-1
and
a_{N}<b_{N}
.
Graphs
G
such that
A_{G}
has pure band spectrum with
N\leq 2
are well-known,
for example Cayley graphs of finitely generated free abelian groups with
N=1
,
or free groups with
N=1

[
Kest–59
]
,
or Grigorchuk groups with
N=2

[
DuGr–20
]
.
But, according to
[
DuGr–20
]
, it has been an open question to find examples with
N\geq 3
.
(We repeat from
[
DuGr–20
]
that it is another open question
whether there exists a Cayley graph such that the spectrum
of its adjacency operator is a Cantor set.)
The observation of this paper is that sums and products of graphs
provide a natural way to obtain graphs and
Cayley graphs

with adjacency operators having pure band spectra.

Theorem 1
.

For any
N\geq 1
, there exist Cayley graphs
G
such that
the adjacency operator
A_{G}
has a pure band spectrum,
consisting of
N
disjoint intervals.

See Section
3
for precise examples,
which are Cayley graphs of groups which are products

\Gamma_{0}\times\Gamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{N}
,
where
\Gamma_{0}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Z}^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
,
or to a free abelian group of rank
d\geq 2
,
and where
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{N}
are finite groups.

A graph
G=(V,E)
has also a Laplacian
L_{G}
and a Markov operator
M_{G}

which act on
\ell^{2}(V)
. For regular graphs of some degree
k
, in particular for Cayley graphs,
we have
L_{G}=k-A_{G}
and
M_{G}=1-\frac{1}{k}S_{G}
,
so that the spectra of
L_{G}
and
M_{G}
are images of
\Sigma(A_{G})

by affine transformations of
\mathbf{R}
.
The theorem above can therefore be repeated for
L_{G}
and
M_{G}
.

The proof of Theorem
1
, in Section
3
,
follows from standard results on spectra of operators
acting on tensor products, see Section
1
,
and on spectra on sums of graphs, see Section
2
.

1.
Spectrum of some operators acting on tensor products of Hilbert spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
be Hilbert spaces,

A
a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{H}
,
and
B
a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\mathcal{K}
.
On the tensor product Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}
,
we consider the three operators

A\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B,\hskip 14.22636ptA\otimes B,\hskip 14.22636ptA\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B+A\otimes B,

which are also bounded and self-adjoint.

Proposition 2
.

Let
A
and
B
be as above.

(i)

The spectrum of
A\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B
is

\{z\in\mathbf{R}\mid z=x+y\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for some}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\Sigma(A)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pty\in\Sigma(B)\}.

(ii)

The spectrum of
A\otimes B
is

\{z\in\mathbf{R}\mid z=xy\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for some}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\Sigma(A)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pty\in\Sigma(B)\}.

(iii)

The spectrum of
A\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B+A\otimes B
is

\{z\in\mathbf{R}\mid z=x+y+xy\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for some}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\Sigma(A)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pty\in\Sigma(B)\}.

There is one proof in
[
Sche–69
]
, or in the older
[
BrPe–66
]
for
(ii)
.
We sketch below another proof, which if needed could give more information
on spectral measures and multiplicities.
Before this, we recall the following facts.
A similar reminder can be found in
[
Harp
]
.

An operator (= bounded linear operator)
X
on
\mathcal{H}

and an operator
Y
on
\mathcal{K}
are
unitarily equivalent

if there exists an operator
U\,\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
which is unitary (= a surjective isometry)
such that
Y=UXU^{*}
.

Let
\Sigma
be a non-empty metrizable compact space,
let
\mu
be a finite measure on
\Sigma
with closed support equal to
\Sigma
,
let
\mathfrak{m}\,\colon\Sigma\to\{1,2,3,\ldots,\infty\}

be a measurable function,
and let
\varphi\,\colon\Sigma\to\mathbf{R}\in L^{\infty}(\Sigma,\mu)
be
an essentially bounded complex-valued function on
\Sigma
,
with essential supremum denoted by
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
.
Denote by
\ell^{2}_{\infty}
the Hilbert space
of square summable sequences
(z_{j})_{j\geq 1}
of complex numbers
and, for each
n\geq 1
, by
\ell^{2}_{n}
the subspace
of sequences such that
z_{j}=0
for all
j\geq n+1
.
Let
L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m})

be the Hilbert space of measurable functions
\xi\,\colon\Sigma\to\ell^{2}_{\infty}

such that
\xi(x)\in\ell^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}(x)}
for all
x\in\Sigma

and
\int_{\Sigma}\|\xi(x)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}_{\infty}}d\mu(x)<\infty
.
The
multiplication operator

M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m},\varphi}

is the operator defined on the space
L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m})
by

(M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m},\varphi}\xi)(x)=\varphi(x)\xi(x)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.69046pt\xi\in L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m})\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\Sigma.

It is well-known that this is a bounded self-adjoint operator,
with norm
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
and with spectrum
the essential range of
\varphi
.
(The arguments to prove this are standard;
see for example Sections 4.20 to 4.28 in
[
Doug–72
]
,
or any of
[
AbKr–73
,
Abra–78
,
Krie–86
]
.)
A
straight multiplication operator

M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m}}

is an operator of this type in the particular case
of a compact subset
\Sigma
of the real line
and of the function
\varphi
given by the inclusion
\Sigma\subset\mathbf{R}
,
so that
(M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m}})(x)=x\xi(x)

for all
\xi\in L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m})

and
x\in\Sigma
.

The Hahn–Hellinger multiplicity theorem establishes that:

(I)

Two straight multiplication operators

M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m}}
and
M_{\Sigma^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}

are unitarily equivalent if and only if the three following conditions are satisfied:

\Sigma^{\prime}=\Sigma
, the measures
\mu^{\prime}
and
\mu
are equivalent,
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(x)=\mathfrak{m}(x)
for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\Sigma
.

(II)

Any bounded self-adjoint operator
A
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

is unitarily equivalent to a straight multiplication operator
M_{\Sigma,\mu,\mathfrak{m}}
,
where
\Sigma
is the spectrum of
A
.

For a proof, we refer to

[
Sim4–15
, Theorem 5.4.3]
or to

[
BoSm–20
, Theorem 10.4.6]
(which applies to unbounded self-adjoint operators).
The measure
\mu
in
(II)
is called a scalar-valued spectral measure for
A

(it is well-defined by
A
up to equivalence of measures)
and the function
\mathfrak{m}
is called the spectral multiplicity function of
A

(is is well-defined up to equality
\mu
-almost everywhere).

A second proof of Proposition
2
.

Let
A
and
B
be as in the proposition.
Let
M_{\Sigma(A),\mu,\mathfrak{m}}
and
M_{\Sigma(B),\nu,\mathfrak{n}}

be two straight multiplication operators which are unitarily equivalent to
A
and
B
respectively.
Then
A\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B
is unitarily equivalent to
the multiplication operator (in general not a straight one)

M_{\Sigma,\mu\times\nu,\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{n},\varphi_{i}}

where
\Sigma
is the product
\Sigma(A)\times\Sigma(B)
,
where
\mu\times\nu
is the product measure on
\Sigma
,
where
(\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{n})(x,y)=\mathfrak{m}(x)\mathfrak{n}(y)

and
\varphi_{i}(x,y)=x+y
for all
(x,y)\in\Sigma
;
this operator acts on the Hilbert space

L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu\times\nu,\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{n})=L^{2}(\Sigma(A),\mu,\mathfrak{m})\otimes L^{2}(\Sigma(B),\nu,\mathfrak{n})

of measurable functions
\zeta\,\colon\Sigma\to\ell^{2}_{\infty}\otimes\ell^{2}_{\infty}

such that
\zeta(x,y)\in\ell^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}(x)}\otimes\ell^{2}_{\mathfrak{n}(y)}

for all
(x,y)\in\Sigma
and

\int_{\Sigma}\|\zeta(x,y)\|^{2}d(\mu\times\nu)(x,y)<\infty
.
Since the subset of
\mathbf{R}
which appears in Claim
(i)

is the essential range of the function
\varphi_{i}
,
this set is the spectrum of
M_{\Sigma,\mu\times\nu,\mathfrak{m}\times\mathfrak{n},\varphi_{i}}

by the reminder above,
and therefore also the spectrum of the unitarily equivalent operator

A\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{K}}+{\rm id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes B
.
This completes the proof of
(i)
.

The proofs of Claims
(ii)
and
(iii)

are similar, using functions
\varphi_{ii}
and
\varphi_{iii}
defined by

\varphi_{ii}(x,y)=xy
and
\varphi_{iii}(x,y)=x+y+xy
.
∎

2.
Sums and products of graphs and Cayley graphs

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(W,F)
be two simple graphs of bounded degree.
We recall the following definitions, as in
[
CvDS–80
, Section 2.5]
.

Definition 3
.

Let
G
and
H
be as above

(i)

The
sum

G+H
is the graph with vertex set
V\times W
and edge set

\displaystyle E_{G+H}=

\displaystyle\Big\{\{(v,w),(v,w^{\prime})\}\mid v\in V\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in F\Big\}

\displaystyle\bigcup\Big\{\{(v,w),(v^{\prime},w)\}\mid\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046ptw\in W\Big\}

(ii)

The
product

G\times H
is the graph with vertex set
V\times W
and edge set

E_{G\times H}=\Big\{\{(v,w),(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\}\mid\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in F\Big\}

(iii)

The
strong product

G\times_{s}H
is the graph with vertex set
V\times W
and edge set

E_{G\times_{s}H}=E_{G+H}\cup E_{G\times H}.

Note that sums and strong products of connected graphs are connected,
but products need not be.
The following proposition is straightforward.

Proposition 4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(W,F)
be two graphs as above,
with adjacency operators
A
and
B
respectively.

(i)

The adjacency operator of the sum
G+H
is
A\otimes{\rm id}_{\ell^{2}(W)}+{\rm id}_{\ell^{2}(V)}\otimes B
.

(ii)

The adjacency operator of the product
G\times H
is
A\otimes B
.

(iii)

The adjacency operator of the strong product
G\times_{s}H

is
A\otimes{\rm id}_{\ell^{2}(W)}+{\rm id}_{\ell^{2}(V)}\otimes B+A\otimes B
.

From Propositions 2 and 4, we have the spectra of sum graphs, product graphs, and strong product graphs.
In the context of finite graphs, this is classical, see for example
[
CvDS–80
, Theorem 2.23]
,
or
[
BrHa–12
, Section 1.4]
.

Here is an example of application.
Let
d\geq 1
and let
Q_{d}
the
1
-skeleton of the
d
-hypercube;
this graph is a Cayley graph of an elementary abelian
2
-group of order
2^{d}
,
and also the graph sum of
d
copies
of the the graph
Q_{1}
which has two vertices and one edge;
it follows from Propositions
2

(i)

and
4

(i)

that its spectrum has distinct eigenvalues

\lambda_{j}=d-2j
, each of multiplicity
m_{j}=\binom{d}{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,d
;
this is of course well-known
[
Bigg–93
, Result 21a]
.

Remark 5
.

For two Cayley graph
G={\rm Cay}(\Gamma,S)
and
H={\rm Cay}(\Delta,T)
,
the sum
G+H
is the Cayley graph of the group
G\times H

with respect to the generating set
S\cup T\Doteq(S\times\{e_{H}\})\cup(\{e_{G}\}\times T)

and the strong product
G\times_{s}H
is the Cayley graph of
G\times H

with respect to the generating set
S\cup T\cup(S\times T)
.
Note that
S\times T
need not generate
\Gamma\times\Delta

(example:
\Gamma=\Delta=\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}
andd
|S|=|T|=1
);
when it does,
G\times_{s}H
is the Cayley graph of
\Gamma\times\Delta

with respect to
S\times T
.

3.
Examples and a proof of Theorem 1

Let
n
be a positive integer,
K_{n}
the complete graph on
n
vertices,
and
A_{(n)}
the adjacency operator of
K_{n}
.
The spectrum of
A_{(n)}
consists of the simple eigenvalue
n-1

and of the eigenvalue
-1
of multiplicity
n-1
.
The graph
K_{n}
is the Cayley graph of a group
G
of order
n

generated by the set
G\smallsetminus\{e_{G}\}
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and let
NK_{n}

be the sum of
N
copies of
K_{n}
.
It follows from Proposition
4
that
the distinct eigenvalues of the adjacency operator of
NK_{n}

are
-(N-j)+j(n-1)
for
j=0,1,\ldots,N

(we don’t need their multiplicities here).
Note that the gap between two consecutive eigenvalues is
n
.

Let
L
be the infinite line, with vertex set
\mathbf{Z}

and edge set
\Big\{\{n,n+1\}\mid n\in\mathbf{Z}\Big\}
.
Using Fourier transform, it is standard to compute the spectrum
of the adjacency operator of
L
, which is the interval
\mathopen{[}-2,2\mathclose{]}
;
the computation is repeated in
[
Harp
]
.
By Remark
5
, the sum
L+NK_{n}

is a Cayley graph of a group product of
\mathbf{Z}
and
N
groups of order
n
.
It is now an immediate consequence of
Propositions
4

(i)

and
2

(i)
that we have:

Proposition 6
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
N\geq 1
.
The graph
L+NK_{n}

is a Cayley graph of which the spectrum of the adjacency operator
is the disjoint union of
N+1
intervals of length
4
centered at the points

-(N-j)+j(n-1)
, where
j=0,1,\ldots,N
.

We can replace
L
by any Cayley graph with spectrum an interval,
as long as
n
is large enough, for example by the standard Cayley graph
of the free abelian group of rank
d
,
which has a spectrum
\mathopen{[}-2d,2d\mathclose{]}
(where
d\geq 1
),
or by a free group of rank
d
,
which has a spectrum
\mathopen{[}-2\sqrt{d-1},2\sqrt{d-1}\mathclose{]}

(where
d\geq 2
).

We can also replace
K_{n}
by the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}

which is the Cayley graph of a group
G
or order
2n

which has a subgroup
H
of order
n
,
with respect to the generating set
G\smallsetminus H
.
The eigenvalues of
K_{n,n}
are the simple eigenvalues
-n
and
n
,
and
0
of multipicity
2n-2
.
For
n\geq 5
, the spectrum of
L+NK_{n,n}

is the disjoint union of 2N+1 intervals of length
4
centered
at the
2N+1
points
jN
, where
-N\leq j\leq N
.

Note that all groups which can be used for Proposition
6
have torsion.
Let
\Gamma
be a countable torsion-free group.
If the reduced C
∗
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
has no non-trivial projection,
the spectrum of a Cayley graph of
\Gamma
is connected,
and thus cannot be a spectrum with
N\geq 2
bands, in contrast with the proposition.
The Kadison–Kaplansky conjecture is the statement that

C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
has no non-trivial projection for any countable torsion-free group;
it is known to hold for large classes of groups,
including torsion-free hyperbolic groups
[
Pusc–02
]
,
their subgroups
[
MiYu–02
]
,
and torsion-free amenable groups
(as a consequence of heavy results on the Baum–Connes conjecture
[
HiKa–01
, Corollary 9.2]
).

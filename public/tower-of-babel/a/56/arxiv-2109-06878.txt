Mathematical structures of non-perturbative topological string theory: from GW to DT invariants
Murad Alim, Arpan Saha, Jörg Teschner, Iván Tulli

Abstract
We study the Borel summation of the Gromov–Witten potential for the resolved conifold. The Stokes phenomena associated to this Borel summation are shown to encode the Donaldson–Thomas invariants of the resolved conifold, having a direct relation to the Riemann–Hilbert problem formulated by T. Bridgeland. There exist distinguished integration contours for which the Borel summation reproduces previous proposals for the non-perturbative topological string partition functions of the resolved conifold. These partition functions are shown to have another asymptotic expansion at strong topological string coupling. We demonstrate that the Stokes phenomena of the strong-coupling expansion encode the DT invariants of the resolved conifold in a second way. Mathematically, one finds a relation to Riemann–Hilbert problems associated to DT invariants which is different from the one found at weak coupling. The Stokes phenomena of the strong-coupling expansion turn out to be closely related to the wall-crossing phenomena in the spectrum of BPS states on the resolved conifold studied in the context of supergravity by D. Jafferis and G. Moore.

Introduction

The study of the geometric structures associated to quantum field and string theories has been extremely fruitful in revealing connections between different areas of mathematics as well as in putting forward organizing principles and relations for mathematical structures and invariants.

The focus of this work is on the connection of two types of invariants associated to families of Calabi–Yau (CY) threefolds. On the one hand, the Gromov–Witten invariants are characteristics of the enumerative geometry of maps into the CY.
Their generating function is closely related to the partition function of topological string theory. The latter is a formal power series which is asymptotic in the topological string coupling constant. On the other hand, the Donaldson–Thomas or BPS invariants associated to the same geometry can be defined using the enumeration of coherent sheaves supported on holomorphic submanifolds on the same CY subject to a stability condition. Physically, the latter correspond to BPS states, which are realized by D-branes supported on subspaces of the CY geometry. The generating functions of BPS invariants are expected to correspond to physical partition functions of black holes. In physical terms, the
topological string theory is obtained from a perturbative formulation of the underlying string theory, while the BPS invariants represent data representing non-perturbative effects in string theory. Relations between the two very different types of data and mathematical invariants have long been expected both from the points of view of physics
[
INOV08
,
DVV06
,
OSV04
]
as well as mathematics
[
MNOP06a
,
MNOP06b
]
.

The link between GW and DT invariants is thus expected to be intimately related to the non-perturbative structure of topological string theory. Since the latter is defined by an asymptotic series in the topological string coupling, the most canonical path to its non-perturbative structure is to consider the theory of resurgence and Borel resummation; see
[
Mn14
]
and references therein for an overview. This has indeed been applied to topological string theory in connection with Chern–Simons theory and matrix models in
[
PS10
]
as well as for the resolved conifold in
[
HO15
]
. In particular,
[

]
used a generalization of the Borel resummation and produced via Borel resummation a partition function which matched the expectations of a proposal for the non-perturbative structure of topological string theory on non-compact CY manifolds put forward earlier in
[
HMnMO14
,
GHMn16
]
. A non-perturbative definition of the topological string free
energy for general toric CY has been proposed in
[

]
in terms of the spectral determinants of the
finite difference operators obtained by quantising the mirror curves.
In
[

]
, techniques of resurgence and transseries were applied to the study of topological string theory via the holomorphic anomaly equations of BCOV
[
BCOV94
]

[

]

These techniques have been applied to the study of the proposal of
[

]

The link to BPS structures started to emerge more clearly recently

GMn21
]
where connections between Stokes phenomena and BPS invariants have been investigated. See also
[

,

]

On the side of DT invariants and BPS structures, exciting insights are coming from the study of wall-crossing phenomena. The wall-crossing formulas of Kontsevich and Soibelman

,
MNOP06b
]
. Bridgeland

formulated a Riemann–Hilbert associated to the Donaldson–Thomas invariants of a given derived category

In this paper, we will revisit the Borel summation of the
resolved
conifold partition function from a new perspective. We will
show, on the one hand, that the Stokes jumps of the Borel

[
Bri20
]
. The Stokes jumps serve as

The Borel summations along different rays
\rho

are found to have the following structure

(1.1)

F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)=F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)+F_{\mathrm{D}}(\lambda,t;\rho),

where
\lambda
is the topological string coupling, and

the complexified Kähler parameter. The contribution denoted

is the canonical re-organisation known
from the work of Gopakumar and Vafa of the formal series in
powers of

which is
convergent for

.
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
does not depend on the
ray
\rho
. The second part,
F_{\mathrm{D}}(\lambda,t;\rho)
strongly depends
on the choice of a ray
\rho
.
F_{\mathrm{D}}(\lambda,t;\rho)
can be
represented as functions of the variables
Q^{\prime}=e^{4\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}t/\lambda}

and
q^{\prime}=e^{4\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}/\lambda}

of non-perturbative effects associated to D-branes in type II string
theory. It is known that there exist non-perturbative
effects in string theory represented by disk amplitudes associated to
stable
D-branes. Closely related effects have recently been identified with
non-perturbative corrections to the metric on the
hypemultiplet moduli space in type II string theory on CY three-folds

[
ASS21
]
.
It seems natural to interpret
the jumps of
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
across Stokes rays
as the consequences of changes of the set of stable
objects contributing to the non-perturbative effects
in the partition functions.
The explicit results for the jumps take a
particularly simple form, having a direct
relation to the Riemann-Hilbert problems associated to DT-invariants
in
[
Bri20
]
further discussed below.

The results associated to different rays
\rho

interpolate between two special functions which had previously
been proposed as candidates for non-perturbative definitions of the topological string
partition functions: Integration along the imaginary axis yields
the Gopakumar–Vafa resummation

on the one hand, while choosing

to be
the positive real axis,
\rho={\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

In the case
\rho={\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

the previously known function

can be obtained from the refined version of

[

]
in the limits studied by Nekrasov and Shatashvili
[
NS09
]
.
The combination

(1.2)

F_{\rm np}(\lambda,t):=F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)+F_{\mathrm{NS}}(\lambda,t),

appearing on the right side of (

) in the case
\rho={\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

has been studied before as a candidate for a non-perturbative
completion of the topological string partition function
[
HMnMO14
]
.
Relations to previous work studying the function
F_{\rm np}(\lambda,t)

in connection to topological string theory are further discussed in Section

2.4
.

It turns out that there is an appealing way to encode the Stokes data geometrically,
in line with the previous suggestions made in
[
CLT20
]
. It will be shown that
the Stokes jumps
can be interpreted as transition functions of a certain line bundle
canonically associated to the solution of the Riemann–Hilbert problem
considered by Bridgeland. We will show that this line bundle
is closely related to the hyperholomorphic line bundles studied in
relation to hyperkähler geometry in
[
APP11b
,
Nei11
]
.
The Borel summations of the topological string partition functions represent
local sections of this line bundle.
In the previous work
[
CPT18
,
CLT20
]
, it had been demonstrated that the Fourier transforms
of the topological string partition functions associated to a certain class of
local CY are related to the isomonodromic tau-functions which represent
local sections of this line bundle. Due to the absence of compact four-cycles,
the tau-functions simply coincide with the topological string
partition functions for the case at hand.

The Borel summation along the positive real axis
appears to be distinguished in some ways.
This function also
has an asymptotic expansion for
\lambda\to\infty
,
referred to as the strong-coupling expansion in the following. The Borel summations
of the strong-coupling expansion along different rays
\rho^{\prime}

are found to have the following structure:

(1.3)

{F}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\lambda,t)=F_{\rm BPS}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})+F_{\rm NS}(\lambda,t).

The contribution
F_{\rm NS}(\lambda,t)
is now independent of
\rho^{\prime}
, while

F_{\rm BPS}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})
exhibits jumps when
\rho^{\prime}
crosses certain rays
in the complex plane of the variable
1/\lambda
. We find that

Z_{\rm BPS}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime}):=e^{F_{\rm BPS}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})}
is closely related to the counting functions for BPS states
previously studied in the context of supergravity by Jafferis and Moore
[
JM08
]
.
The Stokes jumps of
Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})
display a precise correspondence
to the wall-crossing behaviour of the counting functions for BPS states
studied in
[
JM08
]
.
In the case of
\rho^{\prime}={\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
we recover
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
.

Mathematically one may again observe a close relation to a Riemann–Hilbert
problem associated to DT theory. However, the jumps
of
Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})
now directly coincide with the jumps of a particular
coordinate function in a close relative of Bridgeland’s Riemann–Hilbert problem,
as could have been expected from previous
computations of
Z_{\mathrm{BPS}}(\lambda,t;\rho^{\prime})

on the basis of wall-crossing formulae
[
BLR19
, Appendix A]
.
It should be stressed that both the
location of jumps, and the functional form of the jumps are different for
weak- and strong-coupling expansions. However, we find that both are determined
by Riemann–Hilbert type problems associated to DT invariants,
albeit quite
remarkably in somewhat different ways.

At least in the example studied in this paper,
we have identified two new ways to extract non-perturbative
information on DT invariants from the GW invariants
defining the topological string partition functions. Our results
suggest that these data are deeply encoded in the analytic
structures of non-perturbatively defined partition functions.
The way this happens indicates close connections
to string-theoretic
S-duality conjectures, as will be briefly discussed
in Section
6
.

Borel summations of the resolved conifold partition function

We are going to study the formal series

\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\mathrm{Li}_{3-2g}(Q),

with
Q=\exp(2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t)
, and polylogarithms
\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)
and Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
defined by

(2.1)

\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{s}}\,,\quad s\in\mathbb{C}\,,\qquad\frac{w}{e^{w}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\,.

Borel summation of this formal series will repackage the information
contained in it in an interesting way, revealing non-obvious
mathematical structures. Our goals in this section will be to state the results on the Stokes phenomena of the Borel sums of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
,
to discuss
some of its implications, and relations to previous results in the literature.

2.1
Motivation: Topological string theory on the resolved conifold

Topological string theory motivates the consideration of the topological string partition functions. One expects to be able to
associate such partition functions
to families of Calabi–Yau (CY) threefolds
X=X_{t}
, with

t=(t^{1},\dots,t^{n})
being a set of distinguished local coordinates on the
CY Kähler moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of dimension
n=h^{1,1}(X_{t})
.
The partition function is expected to be defined by
an asymptotic series in the topological string coupling
\lambda
of the form

(2.2)

Z_{\rm top}(\lambda,t)=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}{F}^{g}(t)\right).

In order to provide a rigorous mathematical basis for the definition of topological
string partition functions, one may start by defining
the GW potential of a Calabi–Yau threefold
X
as the formal power series

(2.3)

{\mathcal{F}}(Q,\lambda)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}{\mathcal{F}}^{g}(Q)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}\lambda^{2g-2}N_{\beta}^{g}\,Q^{\beta}\,,

where
Q^{\beta}=\prod_{r=1}^{n}Q_{r}^{\beta_{r}}
if
\beta=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\beta_{r}\gamma_{r}
,
with
\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\}
being an integral basis for
H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
and
Q_{r}
being formal variables for
r=1,\dots,n
.
One may note that the term associated to
\beta=0
is independent of the Kähler class

\beta
, motivating the decomposition

(2.4)

{\mathcal{F}}(Q,\lambda)={\mathcal{F}}_{0}(\lambda)+\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(Q,\lambda)\,,

where the contribution
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(\lambda)
takes the universal form
[
FP98
]

(2.5)

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}(\lambda)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}F_{0}^{g},\qquad F_{0}^{g}=\frac{\chi(X)(-1)^{g-1}\,B_{2g}\,B_{2g-2}}{4g(2g-2)\,(2g-2)!}\,,\quad g\geq 2\,,

with
\chi(X)
being the Euler characteristic of
X
.
The formal series
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(Q,\lambda)
is defined as

(2.6)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}(Q,\lambda)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma}\lambda^{2g-2}[\mathrm{GW}]_{\beta,g}\,Q^{\beta},

where
\Gamma=\{\beta\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}});\beta\neq 0\}
,
with
[\mathrm{GW}]_{\beta,g}
being the Gromov–Witten invariants.
In this way, one arrives at a precise definition of
{\mathcal{F}}(Q,\lambda)
as a formal series.

There is a class of CY manifolds where the series
{\mathcal{F}}^{g}(Q)
actually
have finite radii of convergence, allowing us to define
the functions
F^{g}(t)={\mathcal{F}}^{g}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t_{1}},...,e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t_{n}})
,
where
t=(t_{1},...,t_{n})
.
The resulting power series in
\lambda
is not expected to be convergent, in general.
One may hope, however, that there can exist analytic functions
having the series
F(\lambda,t)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}F^{g}(t)
as asymptotic expansion.

We are here considering a particular example of a CY manifold
X
called the
resolved conifold. This
CY threefold represents the total space of the rank two bundle over the projective line:

(2.7)

X:=\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\,,

and corresponds to the resolution of the conifold singularity.

GV98b

2.2
Statement of results for the Borel sum and its Stokes phenomena

Here we state a theorem collecting the results that we wish to prove. The proof of each part will be presented in Section

Before stating the theorem, we briefly recall the definition of Borel summation. Given a formal power series
a(\check{\lambda})\in\check{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[[\check{\lambda}]]

\mathcal{B}\colon\check{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[[\check{\lambda}]]\to\mathbb{C}[[\xi]],\;\;\;\;\mathcal{B}(\check{\lambda}^{n+1})=\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}.

Let

If (

by the sum over all jumps

\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)
for
k\geq 0
, leading to the decomposition

(2.19)

F_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}}(\lambda,t)=\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Big(\lambda\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{l}}{l^{2}(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\Big).

As we will see in Proposition
3.15
, this decomposition can be obtained by evaluating the integral
on the right of (
2.14
) as a sum over residues. Part (iv)
of the theorem above identifies the second term on the right of
(
2.19
) with the sum over the Stokes jumps in the lower right quadrant
of the Borel plane.

It will turn out that the Borel summation
F_{0,\rho}(\lambda)

of the formal series (
2.5
) is closely related to the value of the function

F_{\rho}(\lambda,0)
defined in Theorem
2.1
. The relation will be found to be of the form

(2.20)

F_{0,\rho}(\lambda)=-F_{\rho}(\lambda,0)-\frac{1}{12}\log\check{\lambda}+C,

where
C
is a constant independent of
\rho
which won’t be of interest for us.
Equation (
2.20
)
finally allows us to represent the Borel summation
\widehat{F}_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
of the
full free energy
F(\lambda,t)=F_{0}(\lambda)+\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
of the topological string theory by the formula

(2.21)

\widehat{F}_{\rho}(\lambda,t)=F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)-F_{\rho}(\lambda,0)-\frac{1}{12}\log\check{\lambda}+C.

In the following two subsections we will first discuss the interpretation of
Theorem
2.1
in the context of topological string theory.
This will be followed by a discussion of the relation to previous results in
this direction.

2.3
Connection to topological string theory

In the case of the resolved conifold, non-perturbative definitions of the topological string partition
functions should be analytic functions of
\lambda
and
t
such that
(
2.8
) gives an asymptotic series expansion for
\lambda\to 0
of the corresponding free energy
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
.

2.3.1

The Gopakumar–Vafa (GV) resummation of the GW potential
[
GV98a
]
re-organises the non-constant part

\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
of the GW potential in the following form:

(2.22)

\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma}[\mathrm{GW}]_{\beta,g}\,Q^{\beta}=\sum_{\beta\in\Gamma}\sum_{g\geq 0}\,[\mathrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\left(2\sin\big({\textstyle\frac{k\lambda}{2}}\big)\right)^{2g-2}Q^{k\beta}\,.

Equation (
2.22
) can be understood as an equality of
formal power series in
Q^{\beta}
with coefficients being Laurent series in
\lambda
.
One can
thereby regard (
2.22
) as a definition of the
GV invariants
[\mathrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}
in terms of the Gromov–Witten invariants

[\mathrm{GW}]_{\beta,g}
.

Using the known results for the invariants
\mathrm{GW}_{\beta,g}
of the conifold,
one finds that the right-hand side of
(
2.22
) simplifies to

(2.23)

F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

This has also been derived using the topological vertex formalism
[
AKMnV05
]
.
Assuming
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
, one may notice that the series defining
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
in
(
2.23
) is convergent for
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)>0
or
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)<0
.
One may regard
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
as a minimal summation of the divergent
series (
2.1
), in the sense that it is obtained by a
rearrangement of the formal series
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)

into a convergent series in powers of
Q=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}

that defines functions analytic in
\lambda
away from the real line
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Our results relate
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
to the limits of the Borel summations
along rays
\rho_{k}
for
k\to\pm\infty
when the rays
\rho_{k}
approach the
imaginary axis.

2.3.2

As mentioned above, the function
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
is not well-defined
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. This is one of the motivations to look for analytic
functions having the same asymptotic expansion, but larger domains of definition,
as candidates for non-perturbative definitions of the topological string partition
functions.

A general proposal has been made in
[
HMnMO14
]

for non-perturbative definitions of topological string partition
functions. This proposal was motivated by the observation
[
HMO13
]

that one can systematically add functions of
e^{(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\lambda}}

to the function
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
cancelling all the singularities that

F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
develops on the real
\lambda
-axis. The function of

e^{(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\lambda}}
having this property can be interpreted as
certain non-perturbative corrections in string theory.

Specialised to the conifold, the proposal made in

[
HMnMO14
]
yields the following function:

(2.24)

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t):=F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)+\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lambda F_{\mathrm{NS}}\big({\textstyle\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\lambda},\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(t-\frac{1}{2})}\big),

using the notations

(2.25)

F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big({\textstyle\frac{\lambda k}{2}}\big)\big)^{2}}\,,\qquad F_{\mathrm{NS}}(g,t):=\frac{1}{2{\mathrm{i}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k^{2}\sin\big({\textstyle\frac{gk}{2}}\big)}\,.

It is easy to see that the right side of (
2.24
)
coincides with the expression on the right of (
2.19
) (i.e. that
F_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}=F_{\mathrm{np}}
).

2.3.3

Using Borel
summation is another natural approach to finding
non-perturbative definitions of the topological string partition
functions, as previously investigated in

[
PS10
]
and in
[
HO15
]
. A formula for the Borel transform
had been first proposed in
[
PS10
]
, and in
[
HO15
]
it was
conjectured that the Borel transform along the real axis
is equal to (
2.24
). Extensive numerical studies provided
convincing evidence for these proposals.

Our Theorem
2.1
offers a more complete picture.
It shows that the Borel summations

F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
interpolate between
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)
and
F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)
.
All the functions
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
defined by different choices of the
ray
\rho
can be regarded as different re-packagings of the same
information, contained in the formal series (
2.8
).
Any of these summations can serve as a candidate for
a non-perturbative definition of the topological string partition
function of the resolved conifold. Additional requirements have
to be imposed to distinguish a particular choice among others.

Defining the topological string partition
functions by Borel summation whenever this possibility exists seems
to be the most canonical way to associate actual functions to the
divergent series (
2.8
).
The price to pay
is that the resulting function is only piecewise analytic,
having jumps across the rays
\pm l_{k}
. However, as will be
explained in the rest of the paper, there is interesting
information contained in these jumps. We are going to demonstrate that
the jump functions encode information on the
spectrum of BPS states on the resolved conifold in
a particularly simple and transparent way by
relating them to the Riemann–Hilbert problem formulated
in
[
Bri20
]
which takes as input data the generalised DT invariants
for the resolved conifold.

The Borel summations
F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
each have natural domains of definition,
bounded by the rays
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. It seems important to note, however,
that the functions
F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
can be analytically continued in

\lambda
to larger domains of definition containing
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. This suggests
to regard the analytically continued functions
F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
as local
sections of a line bundle defined by taking exponentials of the
jumps
\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)=F_{\rho_{k+1}}(\lambda,t)-F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)

as transition functions. This line bundle, together with the collection
of distinguished local sections
F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
is a natural geometric object
canonically associated to the formal series (
2.8
)
by Borel summation. We will see that it is a natural
analog of the line bundle proposed in
[
CLT20
]
for the case of the
resolved conifold.

2.3.4

Let us note that the differences

F_{\mathrm{D},k}(\lambda,t):=F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)-F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)

can be
represented as sums of terms which are all
proportional to an exponential
function having dependence with respect to the topological
string coupling
\lambda
of the form

e^{(const.)/\lambda}
.
It is therefore natural to associate the
differences
F_{\mathrm{D},k}(\lambda,t)
with
non-perturbative effects in string theory. They can be represented
as a sum over the Stokes jumps across the rays
l_{k}
enclosed by

\rho_{k}
and
\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}
. We will see that
these jumps are in a one-to-one correspondence with
D-branes in type II string theory on the resolved conifold.

A dependence of the form of the form

e^{(const.)/\lambda}
is characteristic
for non-perturbative effects in string theory having a
world-sheet description through disk amplitudes with boundaries
associated to D-branes. Such disk amplitudes can represent
central charge functions of D-branes in type II string theory

[
HIV00
]
.
We will see that the constants in the exponential
functions
e^{(const.)/\lambda}
appearing in the
differences
F_{\mathrm{D},k}(\lambda,t)

have a simple relation to the central charge functions of the D-branes
associated to the jumps. The functions
F_{\mathrm{D},k}(\lambda,t)

can be represented as sums over all terms which are exponentially
suppressed in the wedge of the
\lambda
-plane bounded by
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
.

These observations suggest that the non-perturbative
effects represented by the functions
F_{\mathrm{D},k}(\lambda,t)

may have a world-sheet description in terms of
disk amplitudes with boundaries associated to stable
D-branes representing states in the BPS-spectrum of the resolved
conifold. The set of D-branes contributing to the non-perturbatively defined partition
functions would then depend on the phase of
\lambda
, and jump across the rays
l_{k}
.
It would be interesting to verify this
interpretation more directly.

2.4
Previous results

Previous work on this subject had obtained several important partial results.
The first study of the Borel summability of the series
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
was performed
in
[
PS10
]
, where an explicit formula for the Borel transform was found.
While the direct comparison of the formula derived in
[
PS10
]
with (
2.12
)
is not completely straightforward,
it is easy to see that the poles and residues agree.

Another approach to the summation of the formal series
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)

has been proposed in
[
HO15
]
. The summation considered in
[
HO15
]
is
an analytic function
F^{\rm resum}_{\rm coni}(\lambda,t)
defined
through an explicit integral representation.
Numerical evidence has been presented for the conjecture that

F^{\rm resum}_{\rm coni}(\lambda,t)
is equal to the Borel summation
F_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}}(\lambda,t)

along
\rho={\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
in our notations.
We will later in Section
3.4.1
explicitly establish the relation between

F^{\rm resum}_{\rm coni}(\lambda,t)
and
F_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}}(\lambda,t)
considered in our paper.
It was furthermore proposed in
[
HO15
]
that the function

F^{\rm resum}_{\rm coni}(\lambda,t)
admits the decomposition
(
2.24
). This conjecture
has been extensively checked numerically.

Interesting relations with spectral determinants of
finite difference operators along the lines of
[
GHMn16
]

have been found in
[
BGT19
]
. Further exploration of the
relations to our results should be illuminating.

It has been demonstrated in
[
Bri20
]
that a special function closely related to the
triple sine function has (
2.1
) as its asymptotic expansion.
The relation between the triple sine function and the formal series
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)

has
stimulated the work
[
Ali20
,
AS21
,
Ali21
]
studying the function
defined on the right side of (
2.14
)
as a promising candidate for a non-perturbative definition of the topological string
partition function. It was identified in
[
AS21
]
as a solution with pleasant analytic properties of a difference equation
[
Ali20
]
which governs the topological string free energy.
In
[
Ali21
]
, the non-perturbative content of this function was extracted demonstrating
that this function admits the
decomposition
(
2.24
) and matching in particular with the results of
[
HO15
]
.

Further work on the function
F_{\rm np}(\lambda,t)

in connection with the non-perturbative structure of topological strings can be found in
[
LV18
,
KM15
]
.

3
Proofs of the results of Section
2.2

In this section we prove each of the points of Theorem
2.1
.
Our approach is strongly inspired by the paper

[
GK20
]
which has studied the analogous problem
for the non-compact quantum dilogarithm function.
Each of the four subsections below corresponds to each of the four points of the Theorem.

3.1
The Borel transform

We start by proving the first part of Theorem
2.1
, concerning the Borel transform of of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
. We remark that an alternative expression of the Borel transform was previously given in
[
PS10
]
, which we recall in Section
3.1.1
.

Recall the asymptotic expansion of the topological string free energy for the resolved conifold, which is given by (
2.8
):

(3.1)

\begin{split}\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)&=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\widetilde{F}^{g}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\,\textrm{Li}_{3-2g}(Q)\,\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)\,,\quad\check{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,,\quad Q=e^{2\pi it}\,,\end{split}

We use the property

(3.2)

\theta_{Q}\textrm{Li}_{s}(Q)=\textrm{Li}_{s-1}(Q)\,,\quad\theta_{Q}:=Q\,\frac{d}{dQ}\,,

to write

(3.3)

\widetilde{F}^{g}=\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\,\theta_{Q}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,,\quad g\geq 2\,.

Furthermore, using that
\theta_{Q}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\partial_{t}
we obtain

(3.4)

\widetilde{F}^{g}=\frac{(-1)B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!(2\pi)^{2g}}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,,\quad g\geq 2\,.

We thus have

(3.5)

\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\,\check{\lambda}^{2g-2}\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,.

We now wish to compute the Borel transform of
\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)

and specify its domain of convergence.
The Borel transform is defined as the formal power series
G(\xi,t):=\mathcal{B}(\Phi(-,t))(\xi)
, where

(3.6)

\mathcal{B}\colon\check{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[[\check{\lambda}]]\to\mathbb{C}[[\xi]],\;\;\;\;\mathcal{B}(\check{\lambda}^{n+1})=\frac{\xi^{n}}{n!}.

Namely, we wish to study

(3.7)

G(\xi,t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!(2g-3)!}\xi^{2g-3}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,.

In order to do this, it will be convenient to first recall the Hadamard product and a certain integral representation thereof. The techniques used below follow the lines of
[
GK20
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Consider two formal power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[[z]]
. Then the Hadamard product
\oast\colon\mathbb{C}[[z]]\times\mathbb{C}[[z]]\to\mathbb{C}[[z]]
is defined by

(3.8)

\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\bigg)\oast\bigg(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}z^{n}\,.

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider two holomorphic functions near
z=0
having series expansions

(3.9)

f_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\;\;\;f_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}

with radius of convergence
r_{1}>0
and
r_{2}>0
, respectively. Then
(f_{1}\oast f_{2})(z)
converges for
|z|<r_{1}r_{2}
, and for any
\rho\in(0,r_{1})
the following holds for
|z|<\rho r_{2}
:

(3.10)

(f_{1}\oast f_{2})(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}f_{1}(s)f_{2}\Big(\frac{z}{s}\Big)\,.

Proof.

By using the limsup definition of the radius of convergence, one can easily check that the radius of convergence of
(f_{1}\oast f_{2})(z)
must be bigger or equal than
r_{1}r_{2}
. On the other hand, we have that for
|z|<\rho r_{2}<r_{1}r_{2}
,

(3.11)

\begin{split}(f_{1}\oast f_{2})(z)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{|s|=\rho}\mathrm{d}s\,\frac{f_{1}(s)}{s^{n+1}}\bigg)b_{n}z^{n}\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}f_{1}(s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}\Big(\frac{z}{s}\Big)^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}f_{1}(s)f_{2}\Big(\frac{z}{s}\Big)\\
\end{split}

where the interchange of sum and integrals is justfied by the Fubini–Tonelli theorem and the absolute convergence of
(f_{1}\oast f_{2})(z)
.
∎

The idea is to write

(3.12)

G(\xi,t)=(f_{1}\oast f_{2}(-,t))(\xi)

for two functions
f_{1}(\xi)
,
f_{2}(\xi,t)
which are holomorphic near
\xi=0
, and then use the first part of the previous lemma.
We will take
f_{1}(\xi)
,
f_{2}(\xi,t)
to be the following:

(3.13)

\begin{split}f_{1}(\xi)&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2g-1)\,B_{2g}}{(2g)!}\xi^{2g-3}\\
f_{2}(\xi,t)&=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{2g-3}}{(2g-3)!}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{2g-3}}{(2g-3)!}\,(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3-2g}(Q)\,.\end{split}

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
|\mathrm{Re}(t)|<\frac{1}{2}
. Then
G(\xi,t)
converges for
|\xi|<2\pi|t|
.

Proof.

Using the fact that

(3.14)

B_{2g}\sim(-1)^{g+1}4\sqrt{\pi g}\Big(\frac{g}{\pi e}\Big)^{2g}\;\;\;\text{as}\;\;\;g\to\infty,

we find that the radius of convergence for
f_{1}(\xi)
is
2\pi
. On the other hand, using the fact that for
|\text{Re}(t)|<1/2
, we have

(3.15)

\mathrm{Li}_{3-2g}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\sim\Gamma(1-3+2g)(-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t)^{3-2g-1}\,\;\;\;\text{as}\;\;\;g\to\infty,

we find that the radius of convergence of
f_{2}(\xi,t)
is
r_{2}(t)=|t|
.

By the use of Lemma
3.2
, we find that provided
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
satisfies
|\text{Re}(t)|<\frac{1}{2}
, we have that
G(\xi,t)=(f_{1}\oast f_{2}(-,t))(\xi)
converges for
|\xi|<r_{1}r_{2}(t)=2\pi|t|
.
∎

We now wish to use the integral representation of the Hadamard product to find a more convenient representation of
G(\xi,t)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

With the same hypothesis as in Proposition
3.3
, we have

(3.16)

G(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{m^{3}}\bigg(1+\frac{\xi}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}-\frac{1}{1-e^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}\bigg)

In particular, for fixed
t
, the expression on the right allows us to analytically continue
G(\xi,t)
to a meromorphic function in
\xi
with poles at
\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)m
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

The idea is to now use the integral representation of the Hadamard product in Lemma
3.2
, together with the results in the proof of Proposition
3.3
. In particular, for
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
|\text{Re}(t)|<1/2
and
\rho\in(0,2\pi)
, we have for
|\xi|<\rho|t|

(3.17)

G(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}f_{1}(s)f_{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{s},t\Big)

where
f_{1}(\xi)
and
f_{2}(\xi,t)
are as in Proposition
3.3
.

We have, on the one hand,

(3.18)

\begin{split}f_{1}(\xi)&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2g-1)\,B_{2g}}{(2g)!}\xi^{2g-3}=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{\,B_{2g}}{(2g)!}\xi^{2g}\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,B_{g}}{g!}\xi^{g}-1+\frac{\xi}{2}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{12}\right)\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}\left(\frac{\xi}{e^{\xi}-1}-1+\frac{\xi}{2}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{12}\right)\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{3}}-\frac{1}{\xi(e^{\xi/2}-e^{-\xi/2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{12\xi}\right)\,,\end{split}

where we have used the expression for the generating function of the Bernoulli numbers

(3.19)

\frac{w}{e^{w}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{w^{n}}{n!}\,,

and the fact that except
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
, all odd Bernoulli numbers vanish. From the final expression we see that
f_{1}(\xi)
admits an analytic continuation to a meromorphic function with double poles at
\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

On the other hand, for
f_{2}(\xi,t)
, we have

\displaystyle f_{2}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{2g-3}}{(2g-3)!}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)=\partial_{\xi}^{3}\,\left(\sum_{g=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)}{(2g)!}\xi^{2g}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\xi}^{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}(\textrm{Li}_{3}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)})+\textrm{Li}_{3}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)}))-\textrm{Li}_{3}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{3}}{2}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)-\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)}\big)\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{3}}{2}\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)}}\bigg)\,,

so that
f_{2}(\xi,t)
admits an analytic continuation in
\xi
with simple poles at
\pm t+\mathbb{Z}
. The integral representation then becomes

(3.21)

G(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\frac{1}{s^{3}}-\frac{e^{s}}{s(e^{s}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{12s}\right)\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}\bigg)\,.

Notice that
f_{2}(\xi/s,t)
as a function of
s
has simple poles at
s=\pm\xi/(t+k)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. By our assumption that
|\xi|<\rho|t|
and
|\mathrm{Re}(t)|<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(3.22)

\Big|\frac{\pm\xi}{t+k}\Big|<\rho\frac{|t|}{|t+k|}\leq\rho\;,

so that all the poles of
f_{2}(\xi/s,t)
lie inside the contour. Furthermore, since
\rho<2\pi
, all the poles of
f_{1}(s)
lie outside the contour.

If, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k>1
, we denote as
\gamma_{k}
the contour given by
|s|=\pi(2k+1)
, then between
|s|=\rho
and
\gamma_{k}
, we have the poles at
\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}n
for
n=1,...,k
due to
f_{1}(s)
. We can therefore write the following for any
k>1
:

(3.23)

\displaystyle G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|s|=\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\frac{1}{s^{3}}-\frac{e^{s}}{s(e^{s}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{12s}\right)\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}:-k<m<k,m\neq 0}\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\frac{e^{s}(s-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m)^{2}}{(e^{s}-1)^{2}s^{2}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}\bigg)\right)\right|_{s=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m}

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{k}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\frac{1}{s^{3}}-\frac{e^{s}}{s(e^{s}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{12s}\right)\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi i(t+\xi/s)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}\bigg)\,,

where the terms in the sum come from the contribution of the (clockwise) contours around the poles between the two contours.

One can check that
f_{2}(\xi/s,t)=\mathcal{O}(1/s)
as
s\to\infty
, while
f_{1}|_{\gamma_{k}}=\mathcal{O}(1/k)
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, taking the limit
k\to\infty
in (
3.23
) we obtain the following expression:

\displaystyle G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}-\{0\}}\frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\frac{e^{s}(s-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m)^{2}}{(e^{s}-1)^{2}s^{2}}\bigg(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi/s)}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi/s)}}\bigg)\right)\right|_{s=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}}\left(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}\right)\right.

(3.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\frac{\xi}{2m^{4}}\left(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m})^{2}}+\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}{(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m})^{2}}\right)\right).

The result then follows by a simple rewriting of the summands.
∎

A direct check that
G(\xi,t)
is the Borel transform of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
can be found in Lemma
B.1
from Appendix
B
.

3.1.1
Previous expression for the Borel transform

In the following we review an expression for the Borel transform of the topological string free energy for the resolved conifold obtained in
[
PS10
]
, starting again with

(3.25)

\begin{split}\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)&=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\widetilde{F}^{g}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\textrm{Li}_{3}(q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(q)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\textrm{Li}_{3-2g}(q)\,\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\textrm{Li}_{3}(q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(q)+\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)\,,\quad\check{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,.\end{split}

Using the series representation of the polylogarithm

(3.26)

\textrm{Li}_{s}(e^{2\pi it})=\Gamma(1-s)\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2\pi i)^{s-1}(k-t)^{s-1}\,,

valid for
\textrm{Re}(s)<0
and
t\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
we can write

(3.27)

\begin{split}\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)&=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\,\textrm{Li}_{3-2g}(q)=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\check{\lambda}^{2g-2}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k-t)^{2-2g}.\end{split}

Taking the Borel transform of
\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)
, we find

(3.28)

\begin{split}G(\xi,t)&=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g\,(2g-2)!}\xi^{2g-3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k-t)^{2-2g}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\xi}\left(\frac{(k-t)^{2}}{\xi^{2}}-\frac{e^{\xi/(k-t)}}{(e^{\xi/(k-t)}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{12}\right),\\
\end{split}

where the second equality follows from

(3.29)

\frac{e^{w}}{(e^{w}-1)^{2}}=\frac{1}{w^{2}}-\frac{1}{12}-\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}w^{2g-2}\,,

which can be obtained by taking a derivative of the generating function of Bernoulli numbers (
3.19
)
and rearranging the outcome. We note here that this expression for the Borel transform, which was previously obtained in
[
PS10
]
has the same set of poles at

\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

as the one we have obtained in Theorem
2.1
. We will later show that it also has the same Stokes jumps.

One advantage of the expression (
3.16
) compared to (
3.28
) is that the first gives a well-defined expression for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. As we will see below, this will allow us to express the Borel transform of constant map contribution of (
2.4
) in terms of
G(\xi,0)
(see Corollary
3.13
).

Remark 3.5
.

It seems one might be able to obtain (
3.28
) from the integral representation (
3.21
) by deforming the contour to
0
instead of
\infty
, and summing over the residues of the poles inside the contour. The only technical issue is that it seems harder to show that the contour limiting to
s=0
limits to a zero contribution.

3.2
The Borel sum along
\mathbb{R}_{>0}

We now prove the second point of Theorem
2.1
. Hence, we wish to study the Borel sum of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
along
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. More generally, we define the following:

Definition 3.6
.

Given
t\in\mathbb{C}
with
\text{Im}(t)\neq 0
, and a ray
\rho\subset\mathbb{C}^{\times}
from
0
to
\infty
avoiding the poles
2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)m
of
G(\xi,t)
and which is different from
\pm{\mathrm{i}}\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we define

(3.30)

F_{\rho}(\lambda,t):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\int_{\rho}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)\,,\quad Q=e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}\,,

for
\lambda
in the half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{\rho}
centered at
\rho
. We call
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
the Borel sum of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
along
\rho
.

The integral appearing in (
2.14
) corresponds to an integral representation of a certain function
G_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
related to the triple sine function. Hence, before studying the Borel sum along
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we recall how this function is defined and some of its properties. For a convenient review of the special functions appearing below and their properties, see for example
[
Bri20
, Section 4]
and the references cited therein.

Definition 3.7
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we define

(3.31)

G_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\exp\Big(\frac{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{6}B_{3,3}(z+\omega_{1}\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\Big)\cdot\sin_{3}(z+\omega_{1}\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\,,

where
\sin_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
denotes the triple sine function, and
B_{3,3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
is the multiple Bernoulli polynomial.

What will be most important for us are the following properties:

Proposition 3.8
.

[
Bri20
, Prop. 4.2]
[
Nar04
, Prop. 2]

G_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is a single-valued meromorphic function under the assumption
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\not\in\mathbb{R}_{<0}
. Furthermore, we have

•

It is regular everywhere, and vanishes only at the points

(3.32)

z=a\omega_{1}+b\omega_{2},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

with
a<0
and
b\leq 0
, or
a>0
and
b>0
.

•

Let
\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{i})>0
and
-\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{1})<\mathrm{Re}(z)<\mathrm{Re}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
. Then

(3.33)

G_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\exp\bigg(-\int_{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{8u}\frac{e^{u(z-\omega_{2}/2)}}{\sinh(\omega_{2}u/2)(\sinh(\omega_{1}u/2))^{2}}\;\bigg).

Definition 3.9
.

For
\mathrm{Re}\,\check{\lambda}>0
and
-\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda})<\mathrm{Re}(t)<\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda}+1)
, we define

(3.34)

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t):=\log G_{3}(t\,|\,\check{\lambda},1)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{8u}\frac{e^{u(t-1/2)}}{\sinh(u/2)(\sinh(\check{\lambda}u/2))^{2}}\;,

We now wish to relate
F_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}(\lambda,t)
to
F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)
. For this, we will need the following lemma, giving a “Woronowicz form” for
F_{\mathrm{np}}
. We remark that a similar form for the triple-sine function was conjectured in
[
AP21
, Equation B.17]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
0<\mathrm{Re}(t)<1
,
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
, and let
\lambda
be in the sector determined by
l_{0}=\mathbb{R}_{<0}\cdot 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t
and
l_{-1}=\mathbb{R}_{<0}\cdot 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-1)
. Furthermore, assume that
\mathrm{Re}(t)<\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda}+1)
. Then
F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)

admits the following Woronowicz form:

(3.35)

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}}\mathrm{d}v\frac{v}{1-e^{v}}\log(1-e^{\check{\lambda}v+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\,.

Proof.

We will follow the method of
[
GK20
]
, based on the unitarity of the
Fourier transform:

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle Ff,Fg\rangle,\qquad\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\;f(x)\overline{g(x)},\qquad(F\psi)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\,xy}\psi(y).

We start by defining for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
,

(3.36)

f_{\epsilon}(x):=e^{-\epsilon x}\log\big(1-e^{\check{\lambda}x+2\pi\mathrm{i}\,t}\big),\;\;\;\;g_{\epsilon}(x):=e^{+\epsilon x}\frac{1}{1-e^{x+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}},\;\;\;\;G_{\epsilon}(x):=e^{+\epsilon x}\frac{x}{1-e^{x+\mathrm{i}\epsilon}}.

We then easily see that

(3.37)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle f_{\epsilon},G_{\epsilon}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}}dv\frac{v}{1-e^{v}}\log(1-e^{\check{\lambda}v+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\;.

We now compute the Fourier transform of
f_{\epsilon}
,
g_{\epsilon}
, and
G_{\epsilon}
. Setting
\zeta=2\pi x+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon
, we find that

(3.38)

Ff_{\epsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}\log(1-e^{\check{\lambda}y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\check{\lambda}}{\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;\frac{e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}}{1-e^{-\check{\lambda}y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\;,

where we have integrated by parts, and used that the boundary terms vanish. The last integral has simple poles at
y=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(k-t)/\check{\lambda}
, and under our assumptions for the parameters
t
and
\lambda
, it is easy to check that the poles on the upper half-plane correspond to
k>0
, while those in the lower half-plane correspond to
k\leq 0
. If
\text{Re}(x)>0
, by an application of Jordan’s lemma and the residue theorem, we can compute
Ff_{\epsilon}(x)
by summing up the residues in the upper half-plane, obtaining

(3.39)

\begin{split}\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\check{\lambda}}{\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;\frac{e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}}{1-e^{-\check{\lambda}y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}&=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\check{\lambda}}{\zeta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}}{\check{\lambda}}\Big|_{y=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(k-t)/\check{\lambda}}=-\frac{2\pi}{\zeta}e^{2\pi t\zeta/\check{\lambda}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi k\zeta/\check{\lambda}}\\
&=-\frac{\pi}{\zeta}e^{\pi\zeta(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}\Big(2e^{-\pi\zeta/\check{\lambda}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi\zeta k/\check{\lambda}}\Big)=-\frac{\pi}{\zeta}\frac{e^{\pi\zeta(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}}{\sinh(\pi\zeta/\check{\lambda})}\;,\end{split}

where in the last equality we have used the Dirichlet series representation of
1/\sinh(z)
. Similarly, if
\text{Re}(x)<0
, we can compute
Ff_{\epsilon}
summing up the residues in the lower half-plane, obtaining

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\check{\lambda}}{\zeta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;\frac{e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}}{1-e^{-\check{\lambda}y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}

\displaystyle=-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\check{\lambda}}{\zeta}\sum_{k=0}^{-\infty}\frac{e^{{\mathrm{i}}y\zeta}}{\check{\lambda}}\Big|_{y=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(k-t)/\check{\lambda}}=\frac{2\pi}{\zeta}e^{2\pi t\zeta/\check{\lambda}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi k\zeta/\check{\lambda}}

(3.40)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{\zeta}e^{\pi\zeta(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}\Big(-2e^{\pi\zeta/\check{\lambda}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{2\pi\zeta k/\check{\lambda}}\Big)=-\frac{\pi}{\zeta}\frac{e^{\pi\zeta(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}}{\sinh(\pi\zeta/\check{\lambda})}\,,

so that
Ff_{\epsilon}(x)
exists for
x\neq 0
and

(3.41)

Ff_{\epsilon}(x)=-\frac{\pi}{\zeta}\frac{e^{\pi\zeta(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}}{\sinh(\pi\zeta/\check{\lambda})}\;.

The computation of
Fg_{\epsilon}(x)
is simpler, and follows similar lines. One obtains that for
x\neq 0
,

(3.42)

\overline{Fg_{\epsilon}(x)}=\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{e^{(\epsilon-\pi)\zeta}}{\sinh(\pi\zeta)}\,.

On the other hand, since
G_{\epsilon}(x)=xg_{\epsilon}(x)
, we find

(3.43)

\overline{FG_{\epsilon}(x)}=-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\overline{Fg_{\epsilon}(x)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\Big(\frac{2\pi e^{\epsilon\zeta}}{1-e^{2\pi\zeta}}\Big)\,.

We then have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle f_{\epsilon},G_{\epsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\langle Ff_{\epsilon},FG_{\epsilon}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg(-\frac{\pi}{2\pi x}\frac{e^{\pi(2\pi x)(2t-1)/\check{\lambda}}}{\sinh(2\pi^{2}x/\check{\lambda})}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{4(\sinh(2\pi^{2}x))^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\check{\lambda}^{-1}\cdot(\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+})}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{8v}\frac{e^{v(t-1/2)}}{\sinh(v/2)(\sinh(\check{\lambda}v/2))^{2}}

(3.44)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{8v}\frac{e^{v(t-1/2)}}{\sinh(v/2)(\sinh(\check{\lambda}v/2))^{2}}=F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)\,,

where we used the fact that the range of the parameter
\lambda
allows us to deform the contour back to
\mathbb{R}+i0^{+}
. The result then follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove the second point of Theorem
2.1
. Another proof is given in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Under the same assumptions as in Lemma
3.10
, we have

(3.45)

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)\,,

where
G(\xi,t)
is the Borel transform (
3.16
) obtained in the previous section,
Q=e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}
, and
\check{\lambda}=\lambda/2\pi
.

Proof.

We start by performing the change of variables
y=\lambda v/2\pi
on (
3.35
), obtaining

(3.46)

\begin{split}F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)&=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\lambda(\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+})}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\frac{y}{1-e^{2\pi y/\lambda}}\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\frac{y}{1-e^{2\pi y/\lambda}}\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{y}{1-e^{2\pi y/\lambda-i\epsilon}}\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\,,\end{split}

where in the second equality we have used that the range of
\lambda
allows us to deform the contour back to
\mathbb{R}+{\mathrm{i}}0^{+}
. Now using

(3.47)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}(-\log(1-e^{-2\pi y/\lambda+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon}))=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda(1-e^{2\pi y/\lambda-{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon})}

and integration by parts, we find

(3.48)

\begin{split}F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\Big[&-\frac{y}{2\pi\lambda}\log(1-e^{-2\pi y/\lambda+i\epsilon})\log(1-e^{y+2\pi it})\Big|_{y=-\infty}^{\infty}\\
&+\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\log(1-e^{-2\pi y/\lambda+i\epsilon})\Big(\log(1-e^{y+2\pi it})+\frac{y}{1-e^{-y-2\pi it}}\Big)\Big]\,.\end{split}

Because
\text{Re}(\lambda)>0
, we obtain that the boundary terms vanish. Furthermore,
splitting the integration over the left and right half intervals, one then obtains

(3.49)

\begin{split}F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}&\Big[\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\log(1-e^{-2\pi y/\lambda+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon})\Big(\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\frac{y}{1-e^{-y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\log(1-e^{2\pi y/\lambda+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon})\Big(\log(1-e^{-y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})-\frac{y}{1-e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\Big]\\
=\widetilde{H}(&\lambda,t)+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}H(\lambda,t,\epsilon)\;,\end{split}

where we have defined

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(\lambda,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;(2\pi y+\pi{\mathrm{i}}\lambda)

\displaystyle\Big(\log(1-e^{-y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})-\frac{y}{1-e^{y-2\pi it}}\Big)

\displaystyle H(\lambda,t,\epsilon):=\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\log(1-e^{-2\pi y/\lambda+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon})\Big(

\displaystyle\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})

(3.50)

\displaystyle+\frac{y}{1-e^{-y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y}{1-e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\;.

One can compute
\widetilde{H}(\lambda,t)
explicitly by performing an integration by parts to get rid of the log term:

(3.51)

\begin{split}\widetilde{H}(\lambda,t)&=\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda^{2}}\bigg((\pi y^{2}+\pi{\mathrm{i}}\lambda y)\log(1-e^{-y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\Big|_{y=0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;(\pi y^{2}+\pi{\mathrm{i}}\lambda y)\frac{-1}{1-e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\bigg)\\
&\;\;\;\;-\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;(2\pi y+\pi{\mathrm{i}}\lambda)\frac{y}{1-e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\frac{y^{2}}{e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}-1}\;.\end{split}

Since
\text{Im}(t)>0
, we find that
|e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}|<1
, so that the last integral in (
3.51
) corresponds to an integral representation of
\mathrm{Li}_{3}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})/\lambda^{2}
. Hence, we conclude that

(3.52)

\widetilde{H}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\;.

On the other hand, by expanding the first log term of
H
and applying the Fubini–Tonelli theorem, we find that

(3.53)

\begin{split}H(\lambda,t,\epsilon)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi ny/\lambda+{\mathrm{i}}n\epsilon}}{n}\Big(&\log(1-e^{y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\\
&+\frac{y}{1-e^{-y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y}{1-e^{y-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\;.\end{split}

Performing a change of variables in each integral, and interchanging integral and summations again, we obtain

(3.54)

\begin{split}H(\lambda,t,\epsilon)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi y/\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{in\epsilon}}{n^{2}}\Big(&\log(1-e^{y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\\
&+\frac{y/n}{1-e^{-y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y/n}{1-e^{y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\\
\end{split}

Letting
H(\lambda,t):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}H(\lambda,t,\epsilon)
, we get

(3.55)

\begin{split}H(\lambda,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi y/\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big(&\log(1-e^{y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\\
&+\frac{y/n}{1-e^{-y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y/n}{1-e^{y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\;.\end{split}

Finally, using that
-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}e^{-2\pi y/\lambda}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}e^{-2\pi y/\lambda}
and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle H(\lambda,t)=\Bigg[\frac{e^{-2\pi y/\lambda}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big(\log(1-e^{y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt+\frac{y/n}{1-e^{-y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y/n}{1-e^{y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\Bigg]\Bigg|_{y=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\;\;\frac{e^{-2\pi y/\lambda}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\Bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big(\log(1-e^{y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})+\log(1-e^{-y/n+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})

(3.56)

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt+\frac{y/n}{1-e^{-y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}-\frac{y/n}{1-e^{y/n-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}}\Big)\Bigg]\,.

Using that the boundary term at
\infty
vanishes, and interchanging the derivative with the sum, we obtain

(3.57)

\begin{split}H(\lambda,t)&=-\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{2}}\log(1-Q)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi y/\lambda}G(y,t)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)B_{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\;\;{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi y/\lambda}G(y,t)\;,\end{split}

where we used that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}=\pi^{2}B_{2}
,
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)=-\log(1-Q)
.

Hence, putting (
3.49
), (
3.52
) and (
3.57
) together gives us (
3.45
).
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\rho
be a ray different from

.

It is related to the Borel transform
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
of
\tilde{F}(\lambda,t)
by the equation (

).

Proof.

We will consider a limit of

as

is taken to satisfy
\text{Re}(t)>0
,
\text{Im}(t)>0

\rho

and

(resp.
-l_{-1}
and
-l_{0}

3.3
Stokes phenomena of the Borel sum

In the previous section, we studied
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
for
\rho=\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. However, the ray
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a choice, and any other ray
\rho
that avoids the poles of
G(\xi,t)
in principle is an equally valid choice to perform the Borel sum. In this section we study the dependence on this choice.

Proposition 3.14
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
and for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
let
l_{k}=\mathbb{R}_{<0}\cdot 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)
. Furthermore let
\rho
be a ray in the sector determined by the Stokes rays
l_{k+1}
and
l_{k}
, and
\rho^{\prime}
a ray in the sector determined by
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. Then for
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}_{\rho}\cap\mathbb{H}_{\rho^{\prime}}
(resp.
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}_{-\rho}\cap\mathbb{H}_{-\rho^{\prime}}
) we have

(3.63)

F_{\pm\rho}(\lambda,t)-F_{\pm\rho^{\prime}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\partial_{\check{\lambda}}\Big(\check{\lambda}\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}})\Big)\,.

If
\mathrm{Im}(t)<0
, then the previous jumps also hold provided
\rho
is interchanged with
\rho^{\prime}
in the above formulas.

Proof.

Notice that

(3.64)

F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)-F_{\rho^{\prime}}(\lambda,t)=\int_{\mathcal{H}(l_{k})}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)\,,

where
\mathcal{H}(l_{k})
is a Hankel contour around
l_{k}=\mathbb{R}_{<0}\cdot 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)
.

To compute this, notice that for our range of parameters,
G(\xi,t)
has double poles at
\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with generalized residues

(3.65)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}(e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}(\xi-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k))^{2}G(\xi,t))\right|_{\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)}=-\frac{e^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}}{(2\pi)^{2}m^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\right).

In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}(l_{k})}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{m=-1}^{-\infty}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\xi}(e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}(\xi-2\pi\mathrm{i}m(t+k))^{2}G(\xi,t))\right|_{\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}}{m^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}m(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2\pi}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)+\frac{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\log\big(1-e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)\Big)

(3.66)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\partial_{\check{\lambda}}\Big(\check{\lambda}\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}})\Big)\;,

where we have used the series representation of
\mathrm{Li}_{s}(z)
for
s=1,2
; and the fact that for
\lambda
in a sufficiently small sector containing
l_{k}
, we have
|e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}|<1
.

A similar argument follows for the rest of the cases in the statement of the proposition.
∎

3.3.1
Stokes jumps of the other Borel transform

Recall the expression for the Borel transform which was obtained previously in

G(\xi,t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\xi}\left(\frac{(k-t)^{2}}{\xi^{2}}-\frac{e^{\xi/(k-t)}}{(e^{\xi/(k-t)}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{12}\right),\\

3.4
The limits
\lim_{k\to\pm\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
and
F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)

To finish the proof of Theorem

, we study the limits of
F_{\rho}(\lambda,t)
, discussed in point (iv).

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\rho_{k}
denote any ray between the Stokes rays
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. Furthermore, assume that
0<\mathrm{Re}(t)<1
,
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>0
,
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)<0
, and
\mathrm{Re}(t)<\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda}+1)

(3.69)

\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}=F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)\,.

Furthermore, we can write the sum of the Stokes jumps along
l_{k}
for

as

(3.70)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\partial_{\lambda}\Big(\lambda\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{l}}{l^{2}(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\Big),\;\;\;\;w:=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t/\check{\lambda}},\;\;\widetilde{q}:=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/\check{\lambda}}\;.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.14
we find that

(3.71)

F_{\rho_{k}}-F_{\rho_{k+1}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)+\log(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\frac{t+k}{\check{\lambda}}})\log\big(1-e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)\Big)\,.

Denoting
w=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t/\check{\lambda}}
and
\widetilde{q}=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/\check{\lambda}}
, we find

(3.72)

\begin{split}F_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)-\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)-F_{\rho_{k+1}}(\lambda,t)\\
&=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(w\widetilde{q}^{k}\big)+\log\big(w\widetilde{q}^{k}\big)\log\big(1-w\widetilde{q}^{k}\big)\Big)\,.\end{split}

We now use the following identities:

(3.73a)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log(1-w\widetilde{q}^{k})=-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l}\frac{w^{l}}{1-\widetilde{q}^{l}}\,,

(3.73b)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k\log(1-w\widetilde{q}^{k})=-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{q}^{l}}{l}\frac{w^{l}}{(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})^{2}}\,,

(3.73c)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(w\widetilde{q}^{k})=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l^{2}}\frac{w^{l}}{1-\widetilde{q}^{l}}\,.

The first two identities (
3.73a
) and (
3.73b
) are easily established by
using the Taylor expansion of the logarithm function; using that
|\widetilde{q}|<1
and
|w|<1
; and exchanging the two
summations. In order to verify (
3.73c
), one can first act on it with
w\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}w}
.
The left side of the resulting equation is easily seen to be equal to

-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log(1-w\widetilde{q}^{k})=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l}\frac{w^{l}}{1-\widetilde{q}^{l}}\,,

using (
3.73a
). It follows that (
3.73c
) holds up to addition of a term which is
constant with respect to
w
. In order to fix this freedom, it
suffices to note that (
3.73c
) holds for
w=0
.

Using the previous identities, we obtain

(3.74)

\begin{split}F_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)-\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)&=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg(\frac{1}{l}-\frac{\widetilde{q}^{l}\log\widetilde{q}}{1-\widetilde{q}^{l}}-\log w\bigg)\\
&=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)\,.\end{split}

Now notice that under our assumptions on
t
and
\lambda
, we have
F_{\rho_{0}}=F_{\mathrm{np}}
by Proposition
3.11
. We now show that
F_{\rho_{0}}
admits the following representation as sum over residues:

(3.75)

F_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

In order to see this, let us recall that by Proposition
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{8u}\,\frac{e^{u(t-\frac{1}{2})}}{\sinh(u/2)(\sinh(\lambda u/4\pi))^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u}\,\frac{e^{ut}}{1-e^{u}}\frac{1}{(2\sinh(\lambda u/4\pi))^{2}}\,.

The integrand has two series of poles, one
at
u=u_{l}:=(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\lambda}l
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and the other at

u=\widetilde{u}_{k}:=2\pi\mathrm{i}k
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We can compute the previous integral by closing the contour in the upper
half-plane. The contributions from the poles at
u=u_{l}
are calculated as

\displaystyle 2\pi\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{4\pi}{2\lambda}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\frac{e^{ut}}{(1-e^{u})u}\bigg|_{u=(2\pi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\lambda}l}

\displaystyle=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{\mathrm{i}(2\pi)^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\frac{w^{l}}{(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\frac{w^{l}}{(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})l}\,,

while the contributions of the poles at
u=2\pi\mathrm{i}k
give the remaining term.

In particular, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}(F_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)-F_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t))+F_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}

(3.76)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

The last statement follows easily by noticing that

(3.77)

∎

\displaystyle F_{-\rho_{0}}-\lim_{k\to-\infty}F_{-\rho_{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg(\frac{1}{l}-\frac{\widetilde{q}^{l}\log\widetilde{q}}{1-\widetilde{q}^{l}}-\log w^{-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(w^{-1}\big)+\log(w^{-1})\log\big(1-w^{-1})\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(w^{-1}\big)+\log(w^{-1})\log\big(1-w^{-1})\Big)\,.

On the other hand, under our conditions on the parameters

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{np}}(-\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{-l})}\bigg)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}.

Hence, we find that for

and

Joining our results together, we conclude that

(3.87)

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to-\infty}F_{-\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)&=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{-l})}\bigg)+\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)\Big)+F_{\mathrm{GV}}(\lambda,t)\\
&\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(w^{-1}\big)+\log(w^{-1})\log\big(1-w^{-1})\Big)\,.\end{split}

Finally, notice that

(3.88)

\begin{split}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{-l})}+\frac{w^{-l}}{l(1-\widetilde{q}^{l})}\bigg)\Big)&=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\bigg(\frac{w^{-l}}{l}\bigg)\Big)\\
&=\log(w^{-1})\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{-l}}{l}-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{-l}}{l^{2}}\\
&=-(\mathrm{Li}_{2}(w^{-1})+\log(w^{-1})\log(1-w^{-1}))\,,\\
\end{split}

where in the last equality we used that

Bridgeland’s Riemann–Hilbert problem and its solution

We begin by recalling the Riemann–Hilbert problem considered in

The initial data for such Riemann–Hilbert problems is the following:

Definition 4.1
.

A variation of BPS structures is given by a tuple
(M,\Gamma,Z,\Omega)

•

M
is a complex manifold.

•

Charge lattice:
\Gamma\to M

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}

•

Central charge:
Z
is a holomorphic section of
\Gamma^{*}\otimes\mathbb{C}\to M
.

•

BPS indices:
\Omega\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
is a function satisfying
\Omega(\gamma)=\Omega(-\gamma)
and the Kontsevich–Soibelman wall-crossing formula
[
KS08

Bri19

The tuple
(M,\Gamma,Z,\Omega)

•

Support property: Let
\text{Supp}(\Omega):=\{\gamma\in\Gamma\;\;|\;\;\Omega(\gamma)\neq 0\}
. Given a compact set

and a choice of covariantly constant norm
|\cdot|
on
\Gamma|_{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}

such that for any

•

Convergence property: for each
p\in M

such that

(4.5)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}}|\Omega(\gamma)|e^{-R|Z_{\gamma}|}<\infty\,.

The variation of BPS structures associated to the resolved conifold is then taken to be the tuple

•

is the complex
2

\Gamma\to M

If for

\Omega

\Omega(\gamma)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if }\gamma=\pm\beta+n\delta\quad\text{for }\;\;n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\\

A similar argument follows for

∎

Proposition 4.10

(See

It satisfies the difference equation

\frac{F(z+\omega_{1}\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}{F(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}=(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}z/\omega_{2}})^{-1}\,.

•

When

Definition 4.11

5
The strong-coupling expansion and its Stokes phenomena

In this section, we will demonstrate that the topological string partition
function has a Borel summable strong-coupling expansion for
\lambda\to\infty
.
The Stokes jumps of the strong-coupling expansion are found to reproduce
the wall-crossing behaviour of counting functions for the
framed
BPS states representing
composites of D0 and D2 branes bound to a heavy D6 brane in string theory on the resolved conifold.
This wall-crossing
has previously been studied by Jafferis and Moore
[
JM08
]
. This work in particular gave a physical derivation of the results of Szendröi on the generating function of non-commutative DT invariants
[
Sze08
]
, see also
[
NN
]
for related work in mathematics and
[
DG10
,
AOVY11
]
in physics.

Because the techniques required in this section are the same as in the previous sections, we will give less details of the intermediate computations.

5.1
Borel summation of the strong-coupling expansion

In order to derive the strong-coupling expansion we shall start with

given in (

using the notations

and

.
As before, we may rewrite this in terms of a Laplace transformation,

(5.2)

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{(v+\alpha)\log\big(1-e^{\check{\lambda}v-\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\big)}{1-e^{v+\alpha}}-\frac{(v-\alpha)\log\big(1-e^{-\check{\lambda}v-\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\big)}{1-e^{\alpha-v}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}v\;\bigg[\frac{(v+\alpha)(\check{\lambda}v+\pi\mathrm{i})}{1-e^{v+\alpha}}+\bigg(\frac{v+\alpha}{1-e^{v+\alpha}}-\frac{v-\alpha}{1-e^{\alpha-v}}\bigg)\log\big(1-e^{-\check{\lambda}v-\mathrm{i}0^{+}}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda}{(2\pi)^{3}}(2\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q^{\prime})+\alpha\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime}))-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}(\mathrm{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime})+\alpha\,\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q^{\prime}))+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}v\;e^{-\check{\lambda}v}G_{s}(v,t^{\prime})\,,

using the notations
Q^{\prime}=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}}

and

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{s}}(v,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\frac{v+2\pi\mathrm{i}\,nt^{\prime}}{1-e^{-v/n-2\pi\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}}}\,.

Having represented the function

\lambda
for which

5.2
Stokes jumps

To compute the Stokes jumps, we follow the strategy from Section
3.3
. The relevant residues are

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Res_{v=v_{kn}^{\pm}}e^{-v/\lambda^{\prime}}G_{\mathrm{s}}(v,t)=\frac{e^{-v_{kn}^{\pm}/\lambda^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{2}n^{3}}(\mp 2\pi\mathrm{i}\,nk)n=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{k}{n}\,e^{\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda n(t^{\prime}+k)}.\,

It follows that
the Stokes jumps across
\pm l_{k}^{\prime}
are explicitly given as

(5.5)

\begin{aligned} F^{\prime}_{\pm\rho_{k+1}}-F^{\prime}_{\pm\rho_{k}}&=2\pi\mathrm{i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\pm e^{\pm\lambda\mathrm{i}n(t^{\prime}+k)}\frac{\,k}{2\pi\mathrm{i}n}=\mp k\log\big(1-e^{\pm\lambda\mathrm{i}(t^{\prime}+k)}\big)\\
&=\mp k\log\big(1-e^{\pm 2\pi\mathrm{i}(t+\check{\lambda}k)}\big)=\mp k\log(1-Q^{\pm 1}{q}^{\pm k})\,,\end{aligned}\qquad\quad\begin{aligned} &Q:=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t},\\[4.30554pt]
&q:=e^{\mathrm{i}\lambda}.\end{aligned}

Note that there is no jump for
k=0
.

For the rest of the section, we will assume that
0<\mathrm{Re}(t^{\prime})<1
and
\mathrm{Im}(t^{\prime})>0
. Taking
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda^{\prime})>0
(
\iff\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)<0
), we can sum the jumps in the upper half-plane, and obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to-\infty}F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k}}-F^{\prime}_{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-1}^{-\infty}(F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k}}-F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k+1}})=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\log(1-Q{q}^{-k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{-l}}{l}\frac{Q^{l}}{(1-q^{-l})^{2}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{\big(2\sin\big(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

On the other hand, if
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda^{\prime})<0
(
\iff\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)>0
), we can sum the jumps in the lower half-plane of the variable, which leads to

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k}}-F^{\prime}_{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k+1}}-F^{\prime}_{\rho_{k}})=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\log(1-Q{q}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{l}}{l}\frac{Q^{l}}{(1-q^{l})^{2}}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kt}}{\big(2\sin\big(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

Note that the domains of definition of

and

have empty intersection.

It will be instructive to consider the normalised partition functions

Considering the functions

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{Z}}_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{k}Q)^{k},

It seems interesting to observe that the wall-crossing behaviour

causing the jumps of the BPS partition functions

\lambda_{\mathrm{D}}

As an outlook we will now

6.1
Global aspects

In the space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\text{K\"{a}h}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
with coordinates
(t,\lambda)
,
one may naturally consider two asymptotic regions, referred to as weak and

defined by sending

with constant

.
The asymptotic expansions of the non-perturbative free energy

in powers of
\lambda
and
\lambda^{-1}
are valid in the weak and
strong-coupling regions, respectively.

In order to get a more global picture, it seems natural to include the rays and jumps
of the strong coupling expansion into the definition of a refined version of the

4

More precisely:

•

On the complex
2

\begin{split}\mathcal{W}_{\text{weak},k}^{\pm}&:=\{(t,\lambda)\;|\;\lambda\in\pm\mathbb{R}_{<0}2\pi i(t+k)\},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\\

and the chambers defined by the connected components of the complement of

Taking into account the chamber structure on

6.3
Langlands modular duality

It seems finally worth pointing out that the coordinate changes
associated to Stokes jumps in weak- and strong coupling
expansions are related by the phenomenon
called Langlands modular duality in the terminology introduced by
Fock and Goncharov in the
context of quantum cluster algebras
[
FG09
]
following
[
Fad00
]
.
An essential aspect of this phenomenon, specialized to the case at hand, is the
possibility to introduce dual shift operators

({T}f)(t)=f(t+\lambda/2\pi),\qquad(\tilde{T}f)(t)=f(t+1),

which act on the variables
\tilde{Q}:=e^{4\pi^{2}\mathrm{i}\,t/\lambda}\equiv w
and
Q=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}

as

{T}Q=qQ,\qquad\tilde{T}\tilde{Q}=\tilde{q}\tilde{Q},\qquad\tilde{T}Q=Q,\qquad{T}\tilde{Q}=\tilde{Q}.

This implies in particular that the functions representing
the cluster coordinate transformations associated to the weak coupling jumps
are invariant under the shift
\tilde{T}
,
while the shift
{T}
acts trivially on
the cluster coordinate transformations associated to the strong coupling jumps.
This simple phenomenon has a natural generalisation
which is the root of some remarkable features of quantized cluster
algebras
[
FG09
]
.
We can’t help the feeling that
this manifestation of Langlands modular duality in the case of the
resolved conifold partition functions can be the tip of an iceberg.

Appendix A
Alternative proof for the Borel sum

In this section we present an alternative derivation of the Borel sum and transform of
\widetilde{F}(\lambda,t)
. The alternative proof uses the integral representation of the Hadamard product used in Section
3.1
.

Proposition A.1
.

Take
t\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
and
0<\mathrm{Re}(t)<1
. Then

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)
equals
F_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
on their common domain of definition. More specifically:

(A.1)

F_{\mathrm{np}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)\,.

Proof.

We will first write down an integral representation for
F_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}
assuming that
t\in(0,1)
, and
\check{\lambda}>0
satisfies the conditions of Proposition
3.8
. We will then deform
t
to
\text{Im}(t)>0
and show what we want.

We recall the Hadamard product representation (see Proposition
3.4
):

(A.2)

G(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}f_{1}(s)\,f_{2}\Big(\frac{\xi}{s},t\Big)\,,

where
\gamma
was an appropriate counterclockwise contour around
0
, and

(A.3)

\begin{split}f_{1}(s)&=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{3}}-\frac{1}{\xi(e^{\xi/2}-e^{-\xi/2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{12\xi}\right),\\
f_{2}(\xi,t)&=\frac{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{3}}{2}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)-\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t-\xi)}\big)\right).\\
\end{split}

Integrating
e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)
along the positive real line and swapping the integral signs, we get

(A.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}G(\xi,t)\\
&=\frac{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}{2}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,f_{1}(s)e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi i(t+\xi/s)}\big)-f_{1}(s)e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi i(t-\xi/s)}\big)\right).\end{split}

Next we simultaneously rescale
s\mapsto{\check{\lambda}}s
and
\xi\mapsto{\check{\lambda}}s\xi
on the first term, while simultaneously rescaling
s\mapsto-{\check{\lambda}}s
and
\xi\mapsto{\check{\lambda}}s\xi
on the second term to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}{2}\int_{\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s^{-1}\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,{\check{\lambda}}s\big(f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}s)-f_{1}(-{\check{\lambda}}s)\big)e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right)

(A.5)

\displaystyle=(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}\int_{\gamma}\mathrm{d}s\,\check{\lambda}f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}s)\left(\int_{0}^{s^{-1}\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right).

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
denote the contours following the real line from
-\infty
to
\infty
avoiding
0
by a small detour in the upper and lower half-planes respectively. We may in fact take them to the lines with imaginary parts
\epsilon
and
-\epsilon
respectively, for some small
\epsilon>0
. Since
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}-\mathcal{C}=\gamma
up to homology, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}\left(\int_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}-\int_{\mathcal{C}}\right)\mathrm{d}s\,{\check{\lambda}}f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}s)\left(\int_{0}^{s^{-1}\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg({\check{\lambda}}f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}(s-{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon))\left(\int_{0}^{(s+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon)\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-(s-{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon)\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right)

(A.6)

\displaystyle\qquad-{\check{\lambda}}f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}(s+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon))\left(\int_{0}^{(s-{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon)\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-(s+{\mathrm{i}}\epsilon)\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right)\Bigg).

Now taking the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
then gives us

(A.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/{\check{\lambda}}}G(\xi,t)=(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}s\,{\check{\lambda}}f_{1}({\check{\lambda}}s)\left(\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)\right),

where
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is a counterclockwise Hankel contour along the negative real axis when
s<0
, a clockwise Hankel contour along the positive real axis when
s>0
, and the imaginary axis from
-{\mathrm{i}}\infty
to
{\mathrm{i}}\infty
when
s=0
.

The poles and residues of the inner integrand are given by

(A.8)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Res}_{-(t+k)}\big(e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)})\big)=-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}e^{s(t+k)},\end{split}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We can thus deduce using Cauchy’s residue theorem that the inner integral is the sum of
2\pi{\mathrm{i}}
times the residues from the poles at
-(t+k)
with
k\geq 0
when
s<0
and minus the sum of
2\pi{\mathrm{i}}
times the residues from the poles at
-(t+k)
with
k>0
when
s>0
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{s(t+k)}=-\frac{e^{st}}{1-e^{s}}\,,\qquad\mbox{when $s<0$},

(A.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-s\xi}\mathrm{Li}_{0}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+\xi)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-1}^{-\infty}e^{s(t+k)}=\frac{e^{st}e^{-s}}{1-e^{-s}}=-\frac{e^{st}}{1-e^{s}}\,,\qquad\mbox{when $s>0$}.

Putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\frac{e^{\check{\lambda}s}}{(e^{\check{\lambda}s}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{({\check{\lambda}s})^{2}}+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{e^{st}}{e^{s}-1}

(A.10)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\left(\frac{e^{\check{\lambda}s}}{(e^{\check{\lambda}s}-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{({\check{\lambda}s})^{2}}+\frac{1}{12}\right)\frac{e^{st}}{e^{s}-1}\,,

where we remark that the integrand of the integral over

and
\check{\lambda}

\text{Im}(t)>0

\lambda

(A.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{2m+1}}\frac{e^{ts}}{e^{s}-1}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2m}}\mathrm{Li}_{2m+1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}).\end{split}

The integrand has a pole of order

for all nonzero integers

\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{2m+1}}\frac{e^{ts}}{e^{s}-1}=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}}k)^{2m+1}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2m}}\mathrm{Li}_{2m+1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\,.

Where in the last equality we used the fact that

Appendix B
Asymptotic series from Borel transforms

Lemma B.1
.

The expression

(B.1)

G(\xi,t)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!(2g-3)!}\xi^{2g-3}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,,

of the Borel transform can be obtained back from

(B.2)

\begin{split}G(\xi,t)&=-\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}}\left(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}\right)\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left.\frac{\xi}{2m^{4}}\left(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m})^{2}}+\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}{(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m})^{2}}\right)\right).\end{split}

Proof.

We first write the second expression of
G(\xi,t)
as

(B.3)

\begin{split}G(\xi,t)&=-\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{m^{3}}\left(\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}-\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t-\xi/m}}\right)\right.\right)\end{split}

we next use the Taylor expansion around
\xi=0
:

(B.4)

\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}{1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m}}=\textrm{Li}_{0}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t+\xi/m})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{k}}{m^{k}}\textrm{Li}_{-k}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\,,

which makes use of the property

(B.5)

\theta_{Q}\textrm{Li}_{s}(Q)=\textrm{Li}_{s-1}(Q)\,,\quad\theta_{Q}:=Q\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}Q}\,,

We thus obtain:

\displaystyle G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{m^{3}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}\left(\frac{\xi}{m}\right)^{2k+1}\textrm{Li}_{-2k-1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\right)\right)\right)\,

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\zeta(2k+4)\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}\xi^{2k+1}\textrm{Li}_{-2k-1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left(\frac{\xi^{2}}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+3}\frac{B_{2k+4}\,(2\pi)^{2k+4}}{2(2k+4)!}\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}\xi^{2k+1}\textrm{Li}_{-2k-1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\right)\right)\,

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathrm{i}})^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+3}\frac{B_{2k+4}\,(2\pi)^{2k+4}\,(2k+3)}{(2k+4)!(2k+1)!}\xi^{2k+1}\,\textrm{Li}_{-2k-1}(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t})\right)

(B.6)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!(2g-3)!}\xi^{2g-3}\,\partial_{t}^{2g}\textrm{Li}_{3}(Q)\,,

where in going from the first to the second line we have used the following expression for the Riemann zeta function:

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\,,\quad\textrm{Re}(s)>0\,,

and in going from the second to the third line we have used the following identity:

Lemma B.2

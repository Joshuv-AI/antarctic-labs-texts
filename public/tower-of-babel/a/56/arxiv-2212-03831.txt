Zakir Deniz

Abstract
. A vertex coloring of a graph G G is said to be a 2-distance coloring if any two vertices at distance at most 2 2 from each other receive different colors, and the least number of colors for which G G admits a 2 2 -distance coloring is known as the 2 2 -distance chromatic number χ 2 ​ ( G ) \chi_{2}(G) of G G . When G G is a planar graph with girth at least 6 6 and maximum degree Δ ≥ 6 \Delta\geq 6 , we prove that χ 2 ​ ( G ) ≤ Δ + 4 \chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4 . This improves the best known bound for 2-distance coloring of planar graphs with girth six.



1.
Introduction

All graphs in this paper are assumed to be simple, i.e., finite and undirected, with no loops or multiple edges. We refer to
[
8
]
for terminology and notation not defined here. Let
G
be a graph, we use
V(G),E(G),F(G),\Delta(G)
and
g(G)
to denote the vertex, edge and face set, the maximum degree and girth of
G
, respectively. If there is no confusion in the context, we abbreviate
\Delta(G),g(G)
to
\Delta,g
.
A 2-distance coloring is a vertex coloring where two vertices that are adjacent or have a common neighbour receive different colors, and the smallest number of colors for which
G
admits a 2-distance coloring is known as the 2-distance chromatic number
\chi_{2}(G)
of
G
.

In 1977, Wegner
[
7
]
posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For every planar graph
G
,

\chi_{2}(G)\leq\begin{cases}7&\text{if}\ \ \Delta=3\\
\Delta+5&\text{if}\ \ 4\leq\Delta\leq 7\\
\Big\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\Big\rfloor+1&\text{if}\ \ \Delta\geq 8.\end{cases}

The conjecture is still widely open. Thomassen
[
5
]
(independently by Hartke et al.
[
3
]
) proved the conjecture for planar graphs with
\Delta=3
. In general, there are some upper bounds for
2
-distance chromatic number of planar graphs. For instance, van den Heuvel and McGuinness
[
6
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 2\Delta+25
, while the bound
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\big\lceil\frac{5\Delta}{3}\big\rceil+78
was proved by Molloy and Salavatipour
[
4
]
.
Recently, Zhu and Bu
[
9
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 20
when
\Delta\leq 5
, and
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 5\Delta-7
when
\Delta\geq 6
. In addition, Zhu et al.
[
10
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 5\Delta-9
when
7\leq\Delta\geq 8
.

For planar graphs without short cycles, Dong and Lin
[
2
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+8
if
g\geq 5
. In
[
1
]
, Bu and Zhu showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+5
if
G
is a planar graph with
g\geq 6
, which confirms the Conjecture
1.1
for the planar graphs with girth six.

Our focus will be on planar graphs with
\Delta,g\geq 6
. In such a case, we improve the bound of Bu and Zhu
[
1
]
by reducing one, and the main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
G
is a planar graph with
g\geq 6
and
\Delta\geq 6
, then
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4
.

Given a planar graph
G
, we denote by
\ell(f)
the length of a face
f
and by
d(v)
the degree of a vertex
v
.

A
k
-vertex is a vertex of degree
k
. A
k^{-}
-vertex is a vertex of degree at most
k
, and a
k^{+}
-vertex is a vertex of degree at least
k
. A
k
(
k^{-}
or
k^{+}
)-face is defined similarly. A vertex
u\in N(v)
is called
k
-neighbour (resp.
k^{-}
-neighbour,
k^{+}
-neighbour) of
v
if
d(u)=k
(resp.
d(u)\leq k
,
d(u)\geq k
). A
k(d)
-vertex is a
k
-vertex adjacent to
d

2
-vertices.

For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we use
n_{i}(v)
to denote the number of
i
-vertices adjacent to
v
.
Let
v\in V(G)
, we define
D(v)=\Sigma_{v_{i}\in N(v)}d(v_{i})
. For
u,v\in V(G)
, we denote by
d(u,v)
the distance between
u
and
v
. Also, we set
N_{i}(v)=\{u\ |\ u\in V(G),\ 1\leq d(u,v)\leq i\}
for

, and so

.

2.1.
The Structure of Minimum Counterexample

Let

Our proof technique is similar to
[
1
]

We begin with outlining some structural properties that

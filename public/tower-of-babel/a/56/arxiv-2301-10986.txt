Abstract
We show the absolute continuity of the spectrum and determine the spectrum as a set for two classes of Hadamard manifolds and for specific domains and quotients of one of the classes.

1
Introduction

The spectrum of the Laplacian is a classical invariant in Riemannian geometry.
If the underlying manifold is closed,
then the spectrum of its Laplacian consists of eigenvalues of finite multiplicity,
and many studies are concerned with estimates of the eigenvalues and their multiplicities.
In this paper, we investigate the spectrum of (the Laplacian of) non-compact Riemannian manifolds.
Then the structure of the spectrum is quite different in general.
For example, the spectrum of Euclidean spaces does not have eigenvalues,
but is, what is called absolutely continuous.

For a complete and connected Riemannian manifold
M
, we view its Laplacian
\Delta

as an unbounded and symmetric operator with domain
C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)\subseteq L^{2}(M)
.
The closure of
\Delta
, which we also denote by
\Delta
, is a self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(M)
.
Its spectrum as a subset of
\mathbb{R}
will be denoted by
\sigma(M)
.

Functional analysis of self-adjoint operators yields the orthogonal decomposition

\displaystyle L^{2}(M)=H_{\mathrm{pp}}(M)\oplus H_{\mathrm{ac}}(M)\oplus H_{\mathrm{sc}}(M)

of
L^{2}(M)
into
\Delta
-invariant subspaces such that

(1)

H_{\mathrm{pp}}(M)
is spanned by eigenfunctions;

(2)

H_{\mathrm{ac}}(M)
consists of functions with absolutely continuous spectral measure;

(3)

H_{\mathrm{sc}}(M)
consists of functions with singularly continuous spectral measure.

We present some more details about these notions and decompositions in Appendix
A
,
since they are needed in our discussion of spectra of Riemannian products.

There is the well known decomposition
\sigma(M)=\sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(M)\cup\sigma_{\ess}(M)
of the spectrum
as a disjoint union of
discrete
and
essential spectrum
,
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
belongs to
\sigma_{\ess}(M)
if
\Delta-\lambda
is not a Fredholm operator.
Then
\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(M)
if and only if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\Delta

of finite multiplicity and is an isolated point of
\sigma(M)
.
Therefore, the preimage in
L^{2}(M)
of
\sigma_{\mathrm{d}}(M)
under the spectral projection associated to
\Delta

is contained in
H_{\mathrm{pp}}(M)
.
In particular, if the essential spectrum of
\Delta
is empty, then
H_{\mathrm{ac}}(M)=H_{\mathrm{sc}}(M)=\{0\}
.
The essential spectrum of
\Delta
is determined by the geometry of
M
at infinity;
see, for example,
[
4
, Proposition 3.6]
for a general formulation of this fact.
In particular, the essential spectrum vanishes if
M
is compact.
But there are also a number of results about the vanishing of the essential spectrum
for non-compact manifolds;
see, for example,
[
6
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
and
[
4
, Examples 3.7]
.

Now the vanishing of the essential spectrum implies the vanishing of the absolutely continuous spectrum,
which is not what we are aiming for.
In fact, we will obtain conditions which imply the absolute continuity of the spectrum,
H_{\mathrm{ac}}(M)=L^{2}(M)
,
or, in other words, the vanishing of the point and the singular continuous spectrum.
As a byproduct, we will also discuss the vanishing of the point spectrum,
H_{\mathrm{pp}}(M)=\{0\}
.
The underlying manifolds will be
Hadamard manifolds
, that is,
complete and simply connected Riemannian manifolds of non-positive sectional curvature.

Recall that a homogeneous Hadamard manifold can be thought of as a simply connected solvable Lie group
S

with a left-invariant Riemannian metric
and given as a semi-direct product
S=A\ltimes N
, where
A
is Abelian and
N
is nilpotent.
Our main result is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

If
M
is a homogeneous Hadamard manifold,
then the spectrum of
M
is absolutely continuous with
\sigma(M)=\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
,
where
h
is the mean curvature of
N
in
S\cong M
.

An explicit formula for
h
is given in Theorem
5.2
,
where we also show that the equality
\sigma(M)=\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
holds in greater generality,
namely for left-invariant Riemannian metrics on Lie groups
G
,
which are solvable or compact extensions of solvable Lie groups,
such that their derived subgroups are not cocompact in
G
.

Concerning the identification of homogeneous Hadamard manifolds
with certain simply connected solvable Lie groups with left-invariant Riemannian metrics,
one may ask, whether representation theory could be a tool to study the spectrum of their Laplacians.
However, in general, in our situation the underlying Lie groups are not exponential solvable,
see
[
1
, p. 26 and p. 37]
and
[
2
, Remark (ii) on p. 16]
,
so that at least the extension of Kirillov theory by Auslander and Kostant does not seem to apply.

For Euclidean and symmetric spaces of non-compact type,
there is much more precise information on their spectrum.
For example, the Fourier transform yields a unitary equivalence between the Laplacian in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})

and multiplication by
|x|^{2}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
which implies that the spectrum of the Laplacian is absolutely continuous with spectrum
[0,\infty)
.
Similar, but more involved, characterizations of the Laplacian are known
in the case of symmetric spaces of non-compact type.

We say that a Hadamard manifold
M
is
asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}

if there is a number
h\geq 0
such that each horosphere in
M
with center
\xi
has constant mean curvature
h
.
Hyperbolic spaces
H^{k}_{\mathbb{F}}
, endowed with Fubini–Study metrics, are asymptotically harmonic about any point in
M_{\infty}
, where
\lambda_{0}=h^{2}/4>0
.
Asymptotically harmonic manifolds in the usual sense are asymptotically harmonic about any point in
M_{\infty}
.
Such manifolds arise, for example, in investigations on the rigidity of geodesic flows.
Furthermore, since horospheres are limits of spheres,
harmonic Hadamard manifolds are asymptotically harmonic about any point in
M_{\infty}
.
However, homogeneous Hadamard manifolds without Euclidean factor,
which are not hyperbolic spaces, are asymptotically harmonic about some, but not any point
M_{\infty}
.
A second main result of this article is

Theorem 1.2
.

If
K_{M}<0
and
M
is asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}
,
then the spectrum of
\Delta
on
M
is absolutely continuous with
\sigma(M)\subseteq\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
,
where
h>0
is the mean curvature of the horospheres with center
\xi
.
Moreover, if the sectional curvature of
M
is negatively pinched and the covariant derivative of the curvature tensor of
M
is uniformly bounded, then
\sigma(M)=\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
.

Say that a group of isometries of a Hadamard manifold
M
is
elementary

if it is discrete and without torsion, fixes a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}
,
and such that it is of one of the following two types:

(a)

\Gamma
leaves a geodesic
\gamma
in
M
emanating from
\xi
invariant.

(b)

\Gamma
leaves Busemann functions associated to
\xi
invariant.

In the first case,
C=\Gamma\backslash\gamma
is a circle and the projection
M\to\gamma

along horospheres with center
\xi
descends to a Riemannian submersion
\pi\colon N\to C
,
where
N=\Gamma\backslash M
.
In the second case,
any Busemann function
b
associated to
\xi
is invariant under
\Gamma

and hence pushes down to
N
and defines a Riemannian submersion
\pi=b\colon N\to\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
M
as in Theorem
1.2
, if
\Gamma
is an elementary group of isometries of
M
fixing
\xi
,
then the spectrum of the quotient
N=\Gamma\backslash M
is absolutely continuous
with
\sigma(N)\subseteq\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
, where
h>0
denotes the mean curvature of the fibers of
\pi
.
Moreover,

(1)

if
N
is of type
(b)
and the fibers of
\pi
are of finite volume or

(2)

if
N
is of type
(b)
, the fibers of
\pi
are of infinite volume,
the sectional curvature of
M
is negatively pinched,
and the covariant derivative of the curvature tensor of
M
is uniformly bounded,

then
\sigma(N)=\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
.

Since Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
has vanishing curvature and a basis of parallel differential forms,
its Hodge–Laplacian
({\rm d}+{\rm d}^{*})^{2}
decomposes into parts which are unitarily equivalent to its Laplacian on functions.
In particular, its differential form spectrum is absolutely continuous.
One might ask whether the differential form spectrum of Hadamard manifolds as considered above
is also absolutely continuous.
Except for symmetric spaces,
we do not know of any such general result.
But there are results about the essential spectrum of the Hodge–Laplacian,
given sufficiently strong pinching of sectional curvature (depending on dimension and degree),
see for example
[
9
,
12
]
,
and, for asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, on the non-existence of eigenvalues
above the essential spectrum, see
[
14
, Theorem 16]
.

Regarding the absolute continuity of the spectrum,
we rely on the same integral formulae as the ones used by Xavier
[
19
]
and Donnelly–Garofalo
[
7
,
8
]
,
using, in particular, arguments from
[
19
]
.
One important difference to the latter is that we also use functions which are convex,
but not strictly convex.
The determination of the spectra as sets is by different arguments,
where we rely on
[
15
]
in the case of homogeneous Hadamard manifolds.

2
Preliminaries

For a Hadamard manifold
M
and points
\xi\in M_{\infty}
and
x\in M
,
the Busemann function
b
associated to
\xi
with
b(x)=0
is given by

\displaystyle b(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(d(x_{n},y)-d(x_{n},x)),

where
(x_{n})
is any sequence in
M
converging to
\xi
.
Recall that the limit exists and that
b
is a
C^{2}
distance function on
M

with horoballs and horospheres centered at
\xi
as sublevel and level sets;
cf.
[
3
]
for this and other facts about Hadamard manifolds.

Observation 2.1
.

If a Hadamard manifold
M
is asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}

or if
N=\Gamma\backslash M
is of type
(b)
,
then the Cheeger constant of
M
respectively
N
is at least
h
,
where
h
is the mean curvature of the horospheres with center
\xi
.
In particular, the spectrum of
M
respectively
N
is contained in
\bigl[h^{2}/4,\infty\bigr)
.

Throughout the article,
we use the absolute value sign to denote the appropriate volumes of sets under discussion.

Proof of Observation
2.1
.

Let
D
be a compact domain in
M
respectively
N
with smooth boundary.
Let
b
be a Busemann function associated to
\xi
and
X=\nabla b
.
Then
|X|=1
and
\diver X=h
and hence

\displaystyle h|D|=\int_{D}\diver X=\int_{\partial D}\langle X,\nu\rangle\leq|\partial D|,

where
\nu
is the outer normal field of
D
along
\partial D
.
The last assertion is just the Cheeger inequality.
∎

Observation 2.2
.

If a Hadamard manifold
M
is asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}

and
b
is a Busemann function of
M
centered at
\xi
, then

\displaystyle 0\leq\nabla^{2}b(v,v)\leq h|v|^{2}.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately from
h=\tr\nabla^{2}b
and
\nabla^{2}b\geq 0
.
∎

Suppose that a Lie group
G
acts properly, freely, and isometrically on a complete Riemannian manifold
M
.
Then the quotient
Q=G\backslash M
is a smooth manifold.
Moreover,
Q
inherits a Riemannian metric such that the projection
\pi\colon M\to Q
is a Riemannian submersion.
The mean curvature field
H
of the fibers is
\pi
-related to a vector field on
Q
,
which is then the push-forward
\pi_{*}H
of
H
.
Following
[
15
]
, we define a Schrödinger operator on
Q
,

\displaystyle S=\Delta+\frac{1}{4}|\pi_{*}H|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\diver\pi_{*}H.

(2.1)

For the determination of the spectrum
\sigma(M)
of a homogeneous Hadamard manifold
M
,
we will then invoke
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
in the following form.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
G
is amenable and that its component of the identity is unimodular.
Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}(M)=\lambda_{0}(S)=:\lambda_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma(S)\subseteq\sigma(M),

where
\lambda_{0}(M)
and
\lambda_{0}(S)
denote the bottom of the spectrum
\sigma(M)
of
M

and
\sigma(S)
of
S
, respectively.
In particular,
\sigma(S)=[\lambda_{0},\infty)
implies that
\sigma(M)=[\lambda_{0},\infty)
.

For a boundary condition
B
of a domain
D
in a Riemannian manifold
M
,
denote by
C^{\infty}_{c,B}(\bar{D})
the space of smooth functions on
\bar{D}
with compact support
which satisfy
B
along the boundary
\partial D
of
D
.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a boundary condition
B
for a smooth domain
D
is
non-positive

if
u\in C^{\infty}_{c,B}(\bar{D})
implies
2u\nabla u=\nabla_{\nu}u^{2}\leq 0
along
\partial D
.

Examples 2.5
.

Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are non-positive.
Robin boundary conditions
\alpha u+\beta\nabla_{\nu}u=0
are non-positive if
\alpha\beta>0
.
These kinds of boundary conditions are also self-adjoint and elliptic in the usual sense.

3
Vanishing of point spectrum

For a
C^{1}
vector field
X
and a
C^{2}
function
u
on a Riemannian manifold
M
, we have the following identity

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle=2\langle X,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u+|\nabla u|^{2}\diver X+\diver\big\{2X(u)\nabla u-|\nabla u|^{2}X\big\}.

(3.1)

In the context of spectral theory, this identity was used by Donnelly–Garofalo,
see
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
, and Xavier, see
[
19
, identity (1)]
,
but it also occurs in the earlier work of Kazdan–Warner
[
13
, identity (8.1)]
.
Donnelly and Garofalo attribute (
3.1
) to Rellich
[
17
]
in the case of Euclidean spaces;
cf. (3) respectively II on page 62 of
[
17
]
.
For the convenience of the reader, we give a short proof of the identity.

Proof of (
3.1
).

Since
X(u)=\langle\nabla u,X\rangle
and
|\nabla u|^{2}=\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle=\nabla u(u)
, we have

\displaystyle 2\diver(X(u)\nabla u)

\displaystyle=2\nabla u(X(u))+2X(u)\diver\nabla u

\displaystyle=2X(\nabla u(u))+2[\nabla u,X](u)-2X(u)\Delta u

\displaystyle=2X\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)+2(\nabla_{\nabla u}X)(u)-2(\nabla_{X}\nabla u)(u)-2X(u)\Delta u

\displaystyle=2X\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)+2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle-2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle-2X(u)\Delta u

\displaystyle=X\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)+2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle-2X(u)\Delta u

\displaystyle=\diver\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}X\bigr)-|\nabla u|^{2}\diver X+2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle-2X(u)\Delta u,

which is the assertion.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

If
\Delta u=\varphi u
for a
C^{1}
function
\varphi
on
M
, then

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle=\varphi X(u^{2})+|\nabla u|^{2}\diver X+\diver\big\{2X(u)\nabla u-|\nabla u|^{2}X\big\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\hphantom{2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle}{}=\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}-\varphi u^{2}\bigr)\diver X-X(\varphi)u^{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle=}{}+\diver\big\{2X(u)\nabla u+\bigl(\varphi u^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)X\big\}.

(3.3)

Proof.

If
\Delta u=\varphi u
, then

\displaystyle 2X(u)\Delta u

\displaystyle=2\varphi X(u)u=\varphi X\bigl(u^{2}\bigr)=X\bigl(\varphi u^{2}\bigr)-X(\varphi)u^{2}

\displaystyle=\diver\bigl(\varphi u^{2}X\bigr)-\varphi u^{2}\diver X-X(\varphi)u^{2}.

Together with (
3.1
), this gives (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
∎

In
[
8
]
, Donnelly and Garofalo consider eigenfunctions
Lu=\lambda u
of Schrödinger operators
L=\Delta+V
.
This corresponds to
\varphi=\lambda-V
in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).

To exhibit the strength of (
3.1
), we present immediate applications of the above identities.
With somewhat more elaborate techniques, we will obtain stronger results later.
Our results are reminiscent of
[
17
, Satz 2]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
K_{M}<0
and that
M
is asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}
.
Let
D
be the complement of a horoball in
M
with center
\xi
.
Then the point spectrum
H_{B,\mathrm{pp}}=H_{B,\mathrm{pp}}(\Delta,\bar{D})
vanishes for any self-adjoint elliptic
boundary condition
B
for
D
, which is non-positive.

Proof.

Let
b
be the Busemann function associated to
\xi
such that
D=b^{-1}((0,\infty))
.
By assumption, there is a number
h
, because of negative curvature strictly positive,
such that the mean curvature of the horospheres
b^{-1}(r)
is constant, equal to
h
.
In other words,
\diver\nabla b=h
.
Let

\displaystyle X=\bigl(1-{\rm e}^{-hb}\bigr)\nabla b.

(3.4)

Then

\displaystyle\diver X=h\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla X=h{\rm e}^{-hb}\nabla b\otimes\nabla b+\bigl(1-{\rm e}^{-hb}\bigr)\nabla^{2}b.

Now
\nabla^{2}b
vanishes in the direction of
\nabla b
and is positive definite perpendicular to it.
Since
1-{\rm e}^{-hb}>0
on
D
, we conclude that
\nabla X
is positive definite on
D
.
By Observation
2.2
,
\nabla^{2}b
is bounded on
M
, hence
\nabla X
on
D
.

Let
u
be a square-integrable smooth function on
D
with
\Delta u=\lambda u
,
satisfying the boundary condition
B
.
Since
X
vanishes on
\partial D=b^{-1}(0)
and
\nabla X
is bounded on
D
,
integration of (
3.3
), with
\varphi=\lambda
, is justified and implies that

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=h\int_{D}\bigl(|\nabla u|^{2}-\lambda u^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle=h\int_{D}\bigl(u\Delta u-\lambda u^{2}\bigr)+h\int_{\partial D}u\nabla_{\nu}u

\displaystyle=h\int_{\partial D}u\nabla_{\nu}u\leq 0,

(3.5)

where the inequality is implied by the boundary condition
B
.
Now
\nabla X
is positive definite on
D
, therefore
\nabla u
vanishes, and hence
u
is constant.
On the other hand, the volume of
D
is infinite and
u
is square-integrable, thus
u
vanishes.
∎

Restricting to Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions, we can allow for more general kinds of domains.
Let
M
be a Hadamard manifold. Say that a subset
D\subseteq M
is a
shadow
if there is a horosphere
H\subseteq M

and a subset
C\subseteq H
such that
D
is the set of all points
x\in M

such that the ray from
x
to the center
\xi
of
H
passes through
C
.
Then we also say that
D
is the
shadow of
C
, thinking of
\xi
as a source of light.
The exterior of a horoball considered in Theorem
3.2
above corresponds to
C=H
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
K_{M}<0
and that
M
is asymptotically harmonic about a point
\xi\in M_{\infty}
.
Let
D\subseteq M
be the shadow of a smooth domain
C
in a horosphere
H
in
M
with center
\xi
.
Then the Dirichlet and Neumann point spectra of
D
vanish.

Proof.

Let
b
be the Busemann function on
M
associated to
\xi
which vanishes on
H=b^{-1}(0)

and
X
be the vector field as in (
3.4
).
As in the previous proof, we get that
\nabla X
is positive definite on
b^{-1}((0,\infty))
,
and hence, in particular, on
D\setminus C
.

Let
u
be a square-integrable smooth function on
D
with
\Delta u=\lambda u
,
satisfying the Dirichlet or the Neumann boundary condition.
Now the boundary of
D
consists of the smooth domain
C\subseteq H

and the shadow of
\partial C
, where
X
is tangential to it.
Since
C
is smooth,
\partial C
is the singular part of
\partial D
.
Now the codimension of
\partial C
in
M
is two,
and hence we can apply the divergence formula when integrating (
3.2
).
Since
X
vanishes on
H
,
the contribution of
C
vanishes in the integrated version of (
3.3
).
As for the shadow part, the contribution of the first boundary term
2X(u)\nabla_{\nu}u
vanishes
since then
X(u)=0
in the case of the Dirichlet boundary condition
and
\nabla_{\nu}u=0
in the case of the Neumann boundary condition.
The contribution of the second term,
\bigl(\lambda u^{2}-|\nabla u|^{2}\bigr)X
,
always vanishes since
\langle X,\nu\rangle=0
in the shadow part.
Thus repeating the computation in (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla u}X,\nabla u\rangle_{2}=h\int_{\partial D}u\nabla_{\nu}u=0.

Since
\nabla X
is positive definite on
D\setminus C
, we conclude, as above, that
u=0
.
∎

4
Absolutely continuous spectrum

Let
S
be a self-adjoint operator in a separable Hilbert space
H
,
and denote by
R(z)=(S-z)^{-1}
the resolvent of
S
.
Say that a closed operator
T
in
H
is
S
-smooth
if, for each
x\in H
and
\varepsilon\neq 0
,

R(\lambda+{\rm i}\varepsilon)x\in\dom T

for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{|x|=1,\,\varepsilon\neq 0}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|TR(\lambda+{\rm i}\varepsilon)x|^{2}<\infty,

see
[
16
, p. 142]
.
In our context, the point is that then the image

\displaystyle\ran T^{*}\subseteq H_{\mathrm{ac}}(S),

(4.1)

see
[
16
, Theorem XIII.23]
.
For a Borel subset
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
say that a closed operator
T
in
H
is
S
-smooth on
B
if
TE_{B}
is
H
-smooth,
where
E_{B}
denotes the spectral projection of
S
associated to
B
.
Then

\displaystyle\ran E_{B}T^{*}\subseteq H_{\mathrm{ac}}(S),

by (
4.1
).
By
[
16
, Theorem XIII.30]
,
T
is
S
-smooth on the closure

perpendicular to
\mathfrak{n}
.
If
(X_{j})
is an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{n}
in
\mathfrak{s}
,
then we get that

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i,j}\langle\nabla_{Y_{i}}Y_{i},X_{j}\rangle X_{j}=\sum_{i,j}\langle[X_{j},Y_{i}],Y_{i}\rangle X_{j}=\sum_{j}(\tr\ad_{X_{j}}|_{\mathfrak{n}})X_{j},

which implies the first asserted equality.
The second follows since the orthogonal complement of
\mathfrak{n}
in
\mathfrak{s}

does not contribute to
\tr\ad_{X_{j}}
.
∎

6
Determination of the spectrum

In this section we aim at the determination of the spectrum
\sigma(M)

We start with the case where

K_{M}<0

\xi\in M_{\infty}

with mean curvature of horospheres equal to

Recall from the first part of the proof of Theorem

We subsume the case

From Observation

For any smooth function

\displaystyle\Delta b^{*}v=b^{*}\bigl(-v^{\prime\prime}-hv^{\prime}\bigr).

\varphi^{\prime\prime}=h^{2}\varphi/4

With

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mu)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad\Phi(v)=\varphi v,

\displaystyle(\Phi(v))^{\prime\prime}=(\varphi v)^{\prime\prime}=\varphi\biggl(\frac{h^{2}}{4}v+hv^{\prime}+v^{\prime\prime}\biggr),

In contrast to the case where the volume of the fibers of

What we will do is to carefully cut off pull-backs.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that

Since

By the upper curvature bound

\displaystyle|J(t)|\geq{\rm e}^{at}|J(0)|\qquad\text{for all}\quad t>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\nabla\chi_{t}|\leq{\rm e}^{-at}|\nabla\chi_{0}|\qquad\text{for all}\quad t>0,

For the estimate of the Laplacian, we refer to
[

, Section 6]
.
The situation there is that of a Hadamard manifold

\displaystyle|\Delta\chi|\leq c_{0}{\rm e}^{-at}\qquad\text{over}\quad b^{-1}(t)\quad\text{for all}\quad t>0,

End of proof of Theorem

Let

Appendix A
Some spectral theory

We include a short discussion of spectra of self-adjoint operators and of Riemannian manifolds.
The main aim is the description of the spectra of Riemannian products.
The latter is well-known and clear in the compact case,
but we could not single out a reference for the general case as presented in Appendix
A.3
.
The product case is important in our discussion of homogeneous Hadamard manifolds,
where it is used for the reduction to the case of vanishing Euclidean factor,
which keeps the technicalities of the discussion at a moderate level; cf. Section
5
.

A.1
Convolution of measures

Recall that a finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
is uniquely a sum of two finite Borel measures
\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}
and
\nu_{\mathrm{s}}
on
\mathbb{R}
,
where
\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}
is absolutely continuous and
\nu_{s}
is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure
\lambda
.
Then
\mathbb{R}
is the disjoint, but not unique, union of a Lebesgue measurable set
Y_{\mathrm{ac}}
and a Lebesgue nullset
Y_{\mathrm{s}}
such that
\nu_{\mathrm{s}}
is concentrated on
Y_{\mathrm{s}}
,
that is,
\nu_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus Y_{\mathrm{s}})=0
.
The countable set
Y_{\mathrm{pp}}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}\mid\nu(y)>0\}
is a subset of
Y_{\mathrm{s}}

and
\nu_{\mathrm{s}}=\nu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\nu_{\mathrm{sc}}
,
where
\nu_{\mathrm{pp}}
is concentrated on
Y_{\mathrm{pp}}
and
\nu_{\mathrm{sc}}
on
Y_{\mathrm{sc}}=Y_{\mathrm{s}}\setminus Y_{\mathrm{pp}}
.

The
convolution

\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}
of finite Borel measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
is defined to be the push-forward of the finite Borel measure
\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
under the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto x_{1}+x_{2}.

In other words, for any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle(\nu_{1}*\nu_{2})(B)

\displaystyle=(\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2})(\{(x_{1},x_{2})\mid x_{1}+x_{2}\in B\})

\displaystyle=\iint\chi_{B}(x_{1}+x_{2})\,{\rm d}\nu_{1}(x_{1}){\rm d}\nu_{2}(x_{2}).

Clearly
\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}=\nu_{2}*\nu_{1}
.
Furthermore, if
\nu_{1}=g_{1}\lambda
with a
\lambda
-integrable
g_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=g\lambda\qquad\text{with $\lambda$-integrable function}\quad g(y)=\int g_{1}(y-z)\,{\rm d}\nu_{2}(z).

(A.1)

In particular, if in addition
\nu_{2}=g_{2}\lambda
with a
\lambda
-integrable
g_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=g\lambda\qquad\text{with $\lambda$-integrable function}\quad g(y)=\int g_{1}(y-z)g_{2}(z)\,{\rm d}\lambda(z).

(A.2)

Notice that the latter
g=g_{1}*g_{2}
, the usual convolution of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.

The support of a measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
is the closed set of points
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\nu(U)>0

for any neighborhood
U
of
y
.

Proposition A.1
.

The convolution
\nu=\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}
has support

\displaystyle\supp\nu=\overline{\supp\nu_{1}+\supp\nu_{2}}.

If
\supp\nu_{1}
and
\supp\nu_{2}
are bounded from below,
then
\supp\nu=\supp\nu_{1}+\supp\nu_{2}
.

For Borel measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
,
we write
\mu\prec\nu
to indicate that
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
.

Proposition A.2
.

The components of the convolution
\nu=\nu_{1}*\nu_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathrm{pp}}=\nu_{1\mathrm{pp}}*\nu_{2\mathrm{pp}}\qquad\text{with}\quad Y_{\mathrm{pp}}=Y_{1\mathrm{pp}}+Y_{2\mathrm{pp}};

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}\succ\nu_{1\mathrm{ac}}*\nu_{2\mathrm{ac}}+\nu_{1\mathrm{ac}}*\nu_{2\mathrm{s}}+\nu_{1\mathrm{s}}*\nu_{2\mathrm{ac}}.

In particular, if
\nu_{1}
or
\nu_{2}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
, then also
\nu
.

In general,
we cannot exclude that the absolutely continuous component of the convolution of
\lambda
-singular measures vanishes.
Thus
\nu_{1\mathrm{s}}*\nu_{2\mathrm{s}}
may contribute to
\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}
.

Proof of Proposition
A.2
.

The assertion about
\nu_{\mathrm{pp}}
is clear from

\displaystyle(\nu_{1}*\nu_{2})(y)=\sum_{y_{1}+y_{2}=y}\nu_{1}(y_{1})\nu_{2}(y_{2}).

The assertion about
\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}
follows immediately from (
A.1
) and (
A.2
).
∎

A.2
Decomposition of spectra

Let
A
be a self-adjoint operator on a (real or complex, separable) Hilbert space
H
.
A second such operator
A^{\prime}
on a Hilbert space
H^{\prime}
is viewed as equivalent to
A

if there is an orthogonal respectively unitary transformation
T\colon H\to H^{\prime}

such that
T\dom A=\dom A^{\prime}
and
TA=A^{\prime}T
on
\dom A
.
We say that such an operator
T

intertwines

A
and
A^{\prime}
.
The spectral theorem in its multiplicative version says that there is a measure space
X

with a finite measure
\mu
and a measurable real function
f
on
X

such that
A
is equivalent to the multiplication operator
A_{f}
on
L^{2}(X,\mu)
,
A_{f}\varphi=f\varphi
,
with domain
\dom A_{f}=\{\varphi\in L^{2}(X)\mid f\varphi\in L^{2}(X)\}
.
Note that neither
X
nor
\mu
, given
X
, are unique.

Suppose that
T
intertwines
A
with such a multiplication operator
A_{f}
.
Consider the push-forward
\nu=f_{*}\mu
, a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
.
By the definition of
f_{*}\mu
,

\displaystyle\supp\nu=\essran f,

the
essential range of
f
.
Whereas
\nu
is not unique, not even the total mass of
\nu
,
the spectrum
\sigma(A)
, the spectral projections
\pi_{B}
, and the spectral measures
\nu_{u}
,
u\in H
,
associated to
A
are invariant respectively equivariant under
T
.
For that reason, it will be instructive to identify them in the case of
A_{f}
.

Recall that the
resolvent set

\rho(A)
of
A
consists of all
y\in\mathbb{R}
or
y\in\mathbb{C}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle A-y\colon\ \dom A\to H

is bijective.
By definition, the
spectrum

\sigma(A)
is the complement of
\rho(A)
in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, respectively,
where we actually have
\sigma(A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, by the self-adjointness of
A
.
The
point spectrum

{\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A)\subseteq\sigma(A)}
is the set of eigenvalues of
A
.

Proposition A.3
.

In the notation further up,

\displaystyle\sigma(A)=\sigma(A_{f})=\essran f\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A)=\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A_{f})=\{y\in\mathbb{R}\mid\nu(y)>0\}.

Proposition A.4
.

The spectral projection of
A_{f}
associated to a Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}

is given by multiplication in
L^{2}(X)
with
f^{*}\chi_{B}=\chi_{B}\circ f
,
where
\chi_{B}
denotes the characteristic function of
B
.

For any
u\in H
,
the
spectral measure

\nu_{u}
on
\mathbb{R}
associated to
u
(and
A
) is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{u}(B)=\langle u,\pi_{B}u\rangle_{2}

for any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
where
\pi_{B}\colon H\to H
denotes the spectral projection associated to
A
.
Since
T
intertwines the spectral measures associated to
A
and
A_{f}
,

\displaystyle\nu_{u}(B)=\nu_{\varphi}(B)=\langle\varphi,(f^{*}\chi_{B})\varphi\rangle_{2}=\int_{X}(f^{*}\chi_{B})\bar{\varphi}\varphi\,{\rm d}\mu

for
\varphi=Tu\in L^{2}(X)
and any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.

Let now again
\nu=f_{*}\mu
, decompose
\nu=\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\nu_{\mathrm{pp}}+\nu_{\mathrm{sc}}
,
and write
\mathbb{R}=Y_{\mathrm{ac}}\cup Y_{\mathrm{s}}
and
Y_{\mathrm{s}}=Y_{\mathrm{pp}}\cup Y_{\mathrm{sc}}
as disjoint unions as further up.
Then we have, respectively set,

\displaystyle\sigma(A)=\sigma(A_{f})=\essran f,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(A)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(A_{f})=Y_{\mathrm{ac}}\cap\essran f,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{s}}(A)=\sigma_{\mathrm{s}}(A_{f})=Y_{\mathrm{s}}\cap\essran f,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A)=\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A_{f})=Y_{\mathrm{pp}}\cap\essran f=Y_{\mathrm{pp}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(A)=\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(A_{f})=Y_{\mathrm{sc}}\cap\essran f.

We call the latter four the
absolutely continuous, singular, point
and
singular continuous

parts of the spectrum of
A
or
A_{f}
.

Proposition A.5
.

Let
L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{ac}}
,
L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{s}}
,
L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{pp}}
,
L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{sc}}
be the subspaces of
\varphi\in L^{2}(X)

vanishing outside
f^{-1}(Y_{\mathrm{ac}})
,
f^{-1}(Y_{\mathrm{s}})
,
f^{-1}(Y_{\mathrm{pp}})
,
f^{-1}(Y_{\mathrm{sc}})
, respectively.
Then we have orthogonal decompositions

\displaystyle L^{2}(X)=L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{ac}}\oplus L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{s}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{s}}=L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{pp}}\oplus L^{2}(X)_{\mathrm{sc}}.

Moreover, besides zero, these subspaces consist precisely of those
\varphi\in L^{2}(X)

such that the associated spectral measures
\nu_{\varphi}
are absolutely continuous and singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure
respectively are concentrated on
\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(A_{f})
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{sc}}(A_{f})
.

Proof.

The only non-trivial part is the absolute continuity of
\nu_{\varphi}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda

for
\varphi\in L^{2}(X)
vanishing outside

.
To that end, let

be a Lebesgue nullset.
Since
\varphi
vanishes outside

, we readily see that

\nu_{\varphi}(B)=\int_{f^{-1}(B)}\bar{\varphi}\varphi\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{f^{-1}(B\cap Y_{\mathrm{ac}})}\bar{\varphi}\varphi\,{\rm d}\mu.

From the fact that
\mu\bigl(f^{-1}(B\cap Y_{\mathrm{ac}})\bigr)=\nu(B\cap Y_{\mathrm{ac}})=\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}(B\cap Y_{\mathrm{ac}})=0

and the square-integrability of
\varphi
, we conclude that
\nu_{\varphi}(B)=0
,
which shows that
\nu_{\varphi}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
.
∎

In the present paper, we study Laplacians

In the case of Riemannian products,

,

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})=(\Delta u_{1})\otimes u_{2}+u_{1}\otimes(\Delta u_{2}).

Since the Laplacian is bounded from below, Proposition
A.7
yields

Corollary A.10
.

We have
\sigma(M)=\sigma(M_{1})+\sigma(M_{2})
.

The following example is well-known.

Example A.11
.

Via the Fourier transform, the Laplacian
\Delta
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
corresponds to multiplication with
|x|^{2}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Therefore, the spectrum of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is absolutely continuous with
{\sigma(\mathbb{R}^{m})=[0,\infty)}
.

Together with this example, Corollary
A.9

(2)
yields

Corollary A.12
.

For any complete and connected Riemannian manifold
M
,
the spectrum of
M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is absolutely continuous, for any
k\geq 1
.

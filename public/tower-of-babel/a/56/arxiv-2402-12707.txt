Abstract
The structure of a linear block code is pivotal in defining fundamental properties, particularly weight distribution, and code design. In this study, we characterize the Type II structure of polar codewords with weights less than twice the minimum weight w min \w_{\min} , utilizing the lower triangular affine (LTA) transform. We present a closed-form formula for their enumeration. Leveraging this structure and additionally characterizing the structure of weight 2 ​ w min 2\w_{\min} , we ascertain the complete weight distribution of low-rate and, through the utilization of dual codes properties, high-rate polar codes, subcodes of Reed–Muller (RM) codes, and RMxPolar codes. Furthermore, we introduce a partial order based on the weight distribution and briefly explore its properties and applications in code construction and analysis.



I
Introduction

Polar codes, identified as a code family capable of achieving the capacity of any binary-input discrete memoryless channel (BI-DMC)
[
1
]
, are members of a larger family of codes called decreasing monomial codes
[
2
]
, which also includes Reed–Muller codes. While significant research has been devoted to polar codes in the past decade, mainly focused on reducing the decoding complexity or improving the error correction performance, less attention has been paid to their structural aspects. Building on classical results pertaining to Reed-Muller codes
[
3
,
4
]
, recent studies
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
have delved into the algebraic properties of polar codes and, in general, decreasing monomial codes, among which a subgroup of their permutation group called the lower triangular affine group (
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
). These works shed light on the weight structure, offering closed-form formulas for the enumeration of codewords with a weight less than twice the minimum weight (
\w_{\min}
). The approach used in these works involves the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
on the maximum-degree monomials in the construction set and the cardinality of the resulting orbit of polynomials, which include
\w_{\min}
-weight codewords. Furthermore, they provide insight into the
1.5\w_{\min}
-weight codewords through the Minkowski sum of two particular orbits. The permutation group and the discovery of its properties
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
have made a significant contribution to the ensemble decoding of polar codes
[
12
,
13
]
.

In this study, we extend the findings presented in
[
6
]
to characterise larger weights. Due to space constraints, our focus centers on the Type II structure
[
6
, Thm. 2]
, for which we provide a closed-form formula to enumerate its codewords. We demonstrate that this structure yields nearly the entire weight distribution of the second-order Reed-Muller codes
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
and their subcodes. We also propose a formula for the codewords of weight
2\w_{\min}
to complete the weight distribution. Additionally, we leverage this structure and the associated closed-form formula to construct low-rate RM-polar codes
[
14
]
based on
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
subcodes. We extend this construction to high-rate codes by exploiting the properties of the corresponding dual codes. Finally, we introduce a new partial order based on weight distribution, analyse its properties, and give examples for its potential application for code construction.

Coding theory notations

We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{2}
the finite field with two elements.
Also, subsets of consecutive integers are denoted by
[\ell,u]\triangleq\{\ell,\ell+1,\ldots,u\}

|\cdot|

\backslash

\w(\bm{c})\triangleq|\supp(\bm{c})|

2

For example, the elements in

A

We usually use the short notation
(N,K)
for codes where we refer to
N

as the

of the code. The vectors in
\mathcal{C}
are called

.
Let us collect all
\w

Let

A particular basis for
{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
is the monomial basis, where any
m

\mathbf{x}^{\bin(i)}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{m-1}^{i_{m-1}},

Denote the set of all monomials in

\mathcal{M}_{m}\!\triangleq\!\left\{\mathbf{x}^{\bin(i)}\mid\bin(i)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\right\}.

One can associate to any integer its binary expansion, which is a one-to-one mapping, and thus map each integer

Given that the reverse index order was used for

\begin{array}[h]{ccccc}[0,2^{m}-1]&\to&\mathcal{M}_{m}\\

Furthermore, the

The degree induces a ranking on any monomial set

, that is,
\mathcal{I}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{j}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{j}=\{f\in\mathcal{I}\mid\deg(f)=j\}.

Decreasing monomial codes

Let “
|
” denote the divisibility between monomials, i.e.,
f|g
iff
\operatorname{ind}(f)\subseteq\operatorname{ind}(g).
Also, the greatest common divisor of two monomials is
\gcd(f,g)=h
with
\operatorname{ind}(h)=\operatorname{ind}(f)\cap\operatorname{ind}(g).

Definition 2
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}.
Then
f\preceq_{w}g
if and only if
f|g.
When
\deg(f)=\deg(g)=s
we say that
f\preceq_{sh}g
if
\forall\;1\leq\ell\leq s\;\text{ we have }\;i_{\ell}\leq j_{\ell}
, where
f=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{s}}
,
g=x_{j_{1}}\dots x_{j_{s}}
.
Define
f\preceq g\quad\text{iff}\quad\exists g^{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}\;\text{s.t.}\;f\preceq_{sh}g^{*}\preceq_{w}g
.

Note that we have
x_{0}\preceq x_{1}\preceq\dots\preceq x_{m-1}.
Also,
\preceq
is an order relation that is partial, e.g.,
x_{3}x_{4}
and
x_{1}x_{5}
are not comparable with respect to
\preceq.

Now we can define the concept of monomial decreasing sets.

Definition 3
.

A set
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
is
decreasing
if and only if (
f\in\mathcal{I}
and
g\preceq f
) implies
g\in\mathcal{I}
.

Any monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
with
\mathcal{I}
decreasing is called a
decreasing monomial code
. Both Polar and Reed-Muller codes are decreasing monomial codes
[
5
]
. For
\mathcal{R}(r,m)
we have

\mathcal{R}(r,m)=\mathcal{C}\left([1,x_{m-r}\cdots{}x_{m-1}]_{\preceq}\right).

(2)

From
[
5
]
, we use the multiplicative complement of a monomial
g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
defined by
\check{g}\triangleq\frac{x_{0}\dots x_{m-1}}{g}.
Note that while
g=x^{\bin(2^{m}-1-i)}
for a given
i
, its complement is
(\check{g})=x^{\bin(i)}.
We can extend the definition to a monomial set
\check{\mathcal{I}}=\{\check{g}\mid g\in\mathcal{I}\}.
The dual of a decreasing monomial code is still a decreasing monomial code:

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{M}_{m}\setminus\check{\mathcal{I}})
.

II-B

Permutation group

The set of permutations that globaly leave the code invariant, forms the
automorphism group
of the code
\mathcal{C}.

A bijective affine transformation over
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is represented by a pair
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
where
\mathbf{B}=(b_{i,j})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m\times m}
is an invertible matrix lying in the general linear group
{\rm GL}(m,2)
and
\bm{\varepsilon}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. The action of
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
on a monomial
g=\prod_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(g)}x_{i}
(denoted by
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot g
) replaces each variable
x_{i}
of
g
by a variable
y_{i}
as

y_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}b_{i,j}x_{j}+\varepsilon_{i}
.
This new variable
y_{i}
, is in fact a linear form (a polynomial in which all terms have a degree at most 1), where the maximum variable is
x_{i}.

In
[
5
]
it was proved that the lower triangular affine transformation denoted by
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
is a subgroup of the permutation group of any decreasing monomial code.
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
is a subgroup of the affine group, where
\mathbf{B}\in{\rm GL}(m,2)
is a lower triangular binary matrix with
b_{i,i}=1
and
b_{i,j}=0
whenever
j>i
. Therefore, the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
can be expressed as the following mapping from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
to itself:

\bm{x}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}\bm{x}+\bm{\varepsilon}.

The set of polynomials resulting from the action of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
on a monomial is collected in a set named
orbit
and denoted by

{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f=\{(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\cdot f\mid(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\}.

Since
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
acts as a permutation on
\operatorname{ev}(f)
, all elements in
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
have the same Hamming weight.

II-C

Minimum Weight Codewords

The minimum weight codewords of any monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
can be written as evaluations of particular polynomials. Indeed, letting
r=\max\{\deg(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{I}\}
we have that
\w_{\min}=2^{m-r}
and any
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
of weight
\w_{\min}
equals
\operatorname{ev}(l_{1}\cdots l_{r})
, where
l_{i}
are linear forms mutually independent. In other words one can not write
l_{i}
as a linear combination of the other forms
l_{j}
(for
i\neq j
), and each
\deg(l_{i})=1
for all
i.
Notice that any such linear form has a maximum variable w.r.t.
\preceq
, i.e., we can always write
l_{i}=x_{i_{1}}+\dots x_{i_{l}}+\epsilon_{i}
where
x_{i_{l}}\preceq\dots x_{i_{1}}
and
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}.

Minimum weight codewords are counted using a particular subgroup of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
.

Definition 4

(
[
5
,
15
]
)
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
define
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}
as the subgroup
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
by ”collecting actions
of
(\mathbf{B},\bm{\varepsilon})
that satisfy”

\varepsilon_{i}=0\text{ if }i\not\in\operatorname{ind}(g)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ \penalty\ b_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\text{ if }i\not\in\operatorname{ind}(g),\\
0\text{ if }j\in\operatorname{ind}(g).\end{array}\right.

In
[
5
]
it was demonstrated that
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f={\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f
, hence,
{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot f
being a finer estimation of the orbit. This group can be written as a semi-direct product between the group of invertible matrices having one on the diagonal and the group of translations. A variable
x_{i}
can be translated by a scalar
\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
onto
x_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}
. Hence,
f\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
admits
2^{\deg(f)}
translations.
Regarding the linear mapping,
x_{i}
can be transformed into a ”new variable” (
y_{i}
), where
y_{i}=x_{i}+\sum_{j=0,j\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)}^{i-1}b_{i,j}x_{j}
. The extra variables considered in
y_{i}
express the degree of freedom we have on
x_{i}
and are denoted by
\lambda_{f}(x_{i})=|\{j\in[0,i)\mid j\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)\}|.

Definition 5
.

Given a monomial
f=x_{i_{0}}\dots x_{i_{s-1}}
with
i_{0}<\dots<i_{s}
, we associate a partition of length
\deg(f)
defined by
\lambda_{f}=(i_{s-1}-(s-1),\dots,i_{0}-0)
(see
[
15
]
). Then, the total number of free variables on all
x_{i}
in
f
is

|\lambda_{f}(f)|=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}\lambda_{f}(x_{i})

(3)

from which

2^{|\lambda_{f}|}
possible actions on
f.
For simplicity, we use the notation
\lambda_{f}
for
\lambda_{f}(f)
.

Example 2
.

Let
f=x_{0}x_{2}x_{4}.
Then
\lambda_{f}=(4-2,2-1,0-0)=(2,1,0)
which corresponds to the degree of freedom 2 for
x_{4}
(
x_{3},x_{1}
are free variables for
x_{4}
), and the degree of freedom

for
x_{2}
(
x_{1}
is a free variable for
x_{2}
). As another example, let
g=x_{2}x_{4}.
Then
\lambda_{g}=(3,2)

x_{3},x_{1},x_{0}
are free variables for

x_{1},x_{0}

III
Structure of weights less than
2\w_{\min}

The starting point of our quest is a well-known result on the classification of codewords of weight smaller than
2\w_{\min}
of Reed-Muller codes.

Theorem 1

(
[
16
]
,
[
3
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
r<m
and
P\in{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
be such that
\deg(P)\leq r
with
0<\w(\operatorname{ev}(P))<2^{m+1-r}.
Then
P
is affine equivalent to one of the forms

1.

Type I
:
P=y_{1}\dots y_{r-\mu}(y_{r-\mu+1}\dots y_{r}+y_{r+1}\dots y_{r+\mu})
where
m\geq r+\mu,r\geq\mu\geq 3

2.

Type II
:
P=y_{1}\dots y_{r-2}(y_{r-1}y_{r}+\dots+y_{r+2\mu-3}y_{r+2\mu-2})
where
m-r+2\geq 2\mu,2\mu\geq 2.

In both cases
y_{i}
are linear independent forms and
\w(\operatorname{ev}(P))=2^{m+1-r}-2^{m+1-r-\mu}.

III-A

Classification of Type II codewords

Considering the following fact, we rewrite Theorem
1
in terms of
{\rm LTA}(m,2)
to add more depth to its impact on decreasing monomial codes: Any product of independent linear forms can be rewritten so that the maximum degree variables of these forms are all distinct
[
15
, Proposition 3.7.12]
. For example,
(x_{4}+x_{2})(x_{4}+x_{1})=(x_{4}+x_{1})(x_{2}+x_{1}+1).

Theorem 2
.

Let
r<m
and
P\in{\mathbf{R}}_{m}
be such that
\deg(P)\leq r
with
0<\w(\operatorname{ev}(P))<2^{m+1-r}.
Then

1.

Type I:
for
m\geq r+\mu,r\geq\mu\geq 3

P=y_{1}\dots y_{r-\mu}(y_{r-\mu+1}\dots y_{r}+y_{r+1}\dots y_{r+\mu})

P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
with
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
having
\deg(f)=\deg(g)=r.

2.

Type II:
for
m-r+2\geq 2\mu\geq 2

P=y_{1}\dots y_{r-2}(y_{r-1}y_{r}+\dots+y_{r+2\mu-3}y_{r+2\mu-2})

P\in\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}
with
f_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{m}
satisfying
\deg(f_{i})=r.

In both cases,
y_{i}
are linear independent forms and
\w(\operatorname{ev}(P))=2^{m+1-r}-2^{m+1-r-\mu}.

In the sequel, we shall focus on Type II codewords. Notice that such codewords are defined by
r+2\mu-2
independent linear forms.
The particular case of
\mu=2
, which corresponds to
1.5\w_{\min}
codewords, was determined in
[
6
]
. The authors classified any such codewords in terms of monomials and group action. Let us recall their result.

Theorem 3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
be a decreasing monomial code, and
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
. Any codeword of weight
1.5\w_{\min}
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
is the evaluation of a polynomial
P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\cdot\big({\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f}\cdot\frac{f}{h}+{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{g}\cdot\frac{g}{h}\big)
, with
f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{r},h=\gcd(f,g)
, and
\deg(h)=r-2
.

Therefore, the
\w_{\min}
and
1.5\w_{\min}
codewords can be described using
\mathcal{I}_{r}.
If any such codeword falls within an orbit
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f
(for the case of
\w_{\min}
) or a sum of two orbits
{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f+{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot g
(for the case of
1.5\w_{\min}
) with
f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
, the question arises whether analogous results exist for higher weight. Continuing along the same path, we prove that any Type II codeword of a decreasing monomial code resides within the sum of
\mu
orbits of monomials from
\mathcal{I}_{r}.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
be a decreasing monomial code and
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
. Then, any Type II codeword of weight
2^{m+1-r}-2^{m+1-r-\mu}
with
m-r+2\geq 2\mu\geq 2
belongs to

{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f_{i}}\cdot\frac{f_{i}}{h}

where
\forall i,f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
,
h=\gcd(f_{i},f_{j})
, for all
i,j\in[1,\mu]
(
i\neq j
) and
\deg(h)=r-2
.

Straightforward, given a decreasing monomial code defined by the monomial set
\mathcal{I}
, one can enumerate the possible weights in the interval
[\w_{\min},2\w_{\min}].
Note that for any
\mathcal{R}(r,m)
code, we have
\mu\leq\lfloor\frac{m-r+2}{2}\rfloor
given in Theorem
1
.

Example 3
.

\mathcal{R}(2,7)
has weights
\{32,48,56,64\}
which correspond to
\w_{\min}=2^{5},1.5\w_{\min}=2^{6}-2^{4},1.75\w_{\min}=2^{6}-2^{8},2\w_{\min}=2^{6}.
Considering
m-r+2=7
for Type II, observe that we can have
\mu\in\{2,3\}
corresponding to the number of terms in
x_{0}x_{1}+x_{2}x_{3}
and
x_{0}x_{1}+x_{2}x_{3}+x_{4}x_{5}.

As additional examples, for
m=7,r=3
, let
\mathcal{I}_{r}=\{x_{0}x_{1}x_{2},x_{0}x_{1}x_{3},x_{0}x_{1}x_{4}\}.
In this case, there are no pair or triplet of monomials, corresponding to
\mu\in\{2,3\}
, with
\deg(\gcd(f_{1},f_{2}))=1
or
\deg(\gcd(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}))=1
.

Now, let us change the set to

III-B

Enumeration formulae for Type II codewords

A natural question that arises is the count of polynomials within an orbit, like the one from Theorem
4
. It is worth noting that we use the term ’orbit’ with a slight abuse of language, since there is a Minkowski sum of multiple orbits.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a decreasing monomial set with
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
and
\mu\geq 2
a positive integer. Also, let
f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
for
i\in[1,\mu]
with
\gcd(f_{i},f_{j})=1
for any pair
(i,j)\in[1,\mu]\times[1,\mu]
with
i\neq j.
Then

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\cdot\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mu}{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}\right|=\\
\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{h}\cdot h\right|\times\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\mu}|{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}|}{2^{\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\alpha_{{f_{i}},{f_{j}}}}},

(7)

where given
\operatorname{ind}(f_{i})=\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
and
\operatorname{ind}(f_{j})=\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
we have
\alpha_{f_{i},f_{j}}=2
if
f_{i},f_{j}
are not comparable,
\alpha_{f_{i},f_{j}}=1
if
f_{j}\preceq f_{i},j_{2}>i_{1}
, or
\alpha_{f_{i},f_{j}}=0
if
f_{j}\preceq f_{i},i_{1}>j_{2}.

With the cardinality of an orbit at our disposal, we can proceed to enumerate Type II codewords of a given weight. The classification perspective provided by Theorem
4
, which is also constructive, gives the monomials (a pair, triplet, or more monomials) generating such codewords. Additionally, the cardinality of an orbit is determined by Proposition
1
. Lastly, we establish that any two distinct tuples of valid monomials for Type II codewords define disjoint orbits. With these components in place, our formula can be formally stated.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a decreasing monomial set and
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f).
Let
\w_{\mu}=2^{m+1-r}-2^{m+1-r-\mu}
with
2\leq 2\mu\leq m-r+2.
The number of weight
\w_{\mu}
codewords of Type II is

|W_{\w_{\mu}}(\mathcal{I})|=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\mu,\;f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{r}\\
h=\gcd(f_{i},f_{j})\in\mathcal{I}_{r-2}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{r-2+2\mu+|\lambda_{h}|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\mu}|\lambda_{f_{i}}(\frac{f_{i}}{h})|}}{2^{\sum\limits_{(f_{i},f_{j})}\alpha_{\frac{f_{i}}{h},\frac{f_{j}}{h}}}}.

(8)

IV
A partial order based on weight contribution

It is widely recognized that there are partial orders for channel reliability
[
20
,
5
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no partial order on the weight contribution. In this section, we introduce a new partial order relation with respect to weight contribution. We use the symbol
\preccurlyeq
instead of
\preceq

in Definition
2
to distinguish it from the partial order of reliability. First, we define this partial order relation for decreasing monomial codes with respect to weight distribution, and then we extend it to the order relation of monomials in the generating set of decreasing monomial codes with respect to their weight contribution.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}),\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{J})
be two decreasing monomial codes. Let
\w\in[0,2^{m}]
and define

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\preccurlyeq_{\w}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{J})\text{ if }|W_{\w}(\mathcal{J})|\leq|W_{\w}(\mathcal{I})|.

We say that
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\preccurlyeq_{[\w_{1},\w_{2}]}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{J})
if
\forall\w\in[\w_{1},\w_{2}]
we have
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\preccurlyeq_{\w}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{J}).

Unless
|\mathcal{I}|\neq|\mathcal{J}|
, we cannot have
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})\preccurlyeq_{[1,2^{m}]}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{J}).
In the case of
|\mathcal{I}|=|\mathcal{J}|
, it is thus natural to examine the smallest interval for
w
on which two codes are comparable. Having determined the structure of codewords of Type II for
\w\in[\w_{\min},2\w_{\min})
, we can now establish the order relation
\preccurlyeq_{[\w_{\min},2\w_{\min})}.
While our construction and definitions are applicable to any dimension, for consistency with our results on subcodes of
\mathcal{R}(2,m)
where we have the complete weight distribution, we shall consider this particular case.
We extend the definition
\preccurlyeq_{\w_{\min}}
to monomials as follows.

Definition 7
.

Let
r
be the maximum degree of monomials and
f,g\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
. Then, we have
f\preccurlyeq_{\w_{\min}}g
if and only if
|\lambda_{f}|<|\lambda_{g}|
.

To each degree
r

Symmetric property and non-comparable elements

The order
\preccurlyeq_{\w_{\min}}

Defining

Lemma 2
.

Let
S_{l}=\{f\in\mathcal{I}_{2}\mid|\lambda_{f}|=l\}.
Then we have

•

Symmetry:
|S_{l}|=|S_{m-2+l}|
for all
l\in[0,m-2].

•

Cardinality:
|S_{l}|=\lfloor\frac{l+2}{2}\rfloor
for any
l\in[0,m-2].

Proof:

Using Definition
5
for
\lambda_{f}
, each level
l

Indeed,
|\lambda_{x_{0}x_{l+1}}|=\dots=|\lambda_{x_{\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}x_{\lfloor\frac{l+2}{2}\rfloor}}|=l.
There are
{\lfloor\frac{l+2}{2}\rfloor}

, which finishes our proof.
∎

1.

Based on

2.

The remaining monomials, all from level
l

]

IV-C

Permutation group

The order
\preccurlyeq_{\w_{\min}}
induces new symmetries which generate properties yet to be discovered. For example, elements at the same level
S_{l}
have identical weight contribution, thus defining different codes with the same weight distribution. However, the codes constructed based on our algorithm are not permutation equivalent. While two permutation equivalent codes will have an identical weight distribution, the converse is not always true, and few results are known in this direction
[
28
]
. The following examples further clarify the matter.

Example 7
.

Let us consider the codes in Example
5
. The three codes have different permutation groups, and thus are not equivalent.
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\{x_{0}x_{5}\})
has a group of order
2^{30}\times 3^{2}
,
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\{x_{1}x_{4}\})
of order
2^{28}\times 3^{3}\times 7
, while
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}\setminus\{x_{2}x_{3}\})
of order
2^{28}\times 3\times 7.

Example 8
.

For
m=7
and the code dimensions between
9
and
16
, we find the corresponding codes and their properties based on the poset of weight contribution and reliability, as illustrated in Table
V

{\mathscr{Aut}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}))
) is obtained using MAGMA’s permutation group function. The order of the permutation group of

6

To demonstrate our result we need an intermediate result.

Lemma 6
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
l\in[0,(m-1)/2]
such that there are
l
monomials
f_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{2}
satisfying
\gcd(f_{1},\dots f_{l},x_{j})
for some variable
j.
Then we have

\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot x_{j}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}\right|\\
=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}\right|\left|{\rm LTA}(m,2)_{f_{1}\dots f_{l}}\cdot x_{j}\right|.

(38)

Proof:

We will use induction and start with the first two cases.

•

l=0\Leftrightarrow P=x_{i_{1}}
and here the result is trivial.

•

l=1\Leftrightarrow P=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}})(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+l_{i_{3}}.
Suppose
l_{i_{3}}
contains at least one variable from
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}}.
We have

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}})(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{3}}^{*}

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}+1)(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{3}}^{*}

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}+1)(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+l_{i_{3}}^{\prime},

where
l_{i_{3}}^{\prime}
does not contain any of the variables
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}}.

If
l_{i_{3}}
contains both variables we have

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}})(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+x_{i_{2}}+x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{3}}^{*}

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}+1)(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}}+1)+x_{i_{3}}+l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}}+1+l_{i_{3}}^{*}

\displaystyle=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}+1)(x_{i_{2}}+l_{i_{2}})+x_{i_{3}}+l_{i_{3}}^{\prime},

where
l_{i_{3}}^{\prime}
does not contain any of the variables
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}}.

Suppose our statement is valid up to
l-1
and let us demonstrate that it is also true for
l.
Consider
P\in{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot x_{i_{2l+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{l}{\rm LTA}(m,2)\cdot f_{i}.
By definition we have
P=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}})(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}})+\dots(x_{i_{2l-1}}+l_{i_{2l-1}})(x_{i_{2l}}+l_{i_{2l}})+x_{i_{2l+1}}+l_{i_{2l+2}}.

We thus have

P=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}})(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}})+\dots(x_{i_{2l-1}}+l_{i_{2l-1}})(x_{i_{2l}}+l_{i_{2l}})\\
+x_{i_{2l+1}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i_{2l+1}\\
j\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{2l}\}\end{subarray}}b_{i_{2l+1},j}x_{j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i_{2l+1}\\
j\in\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\end{subarray}}b_{i_{2l+1},j}x_{j}+\varepsilon_{i_{2l+1}}\\
=(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}^{*})(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}}^{*})+\dots(x_{i_{2l-1}}+l_{i_{2l-1}})(x_{i_{2l}}+l_{i_{2l}})\\
+x_{i_{2l+1}}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j<i_{2l+1}\\
j\not\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{2l}\}\end{subarray}}b_{i_{2l+1},j}x_{j}\\
+b_{i_{2l+1},i_{1}}l_{i_{2}}+b_{i_{2l+1},i_{2}}l_{i_{1}}+\varepsilon_{i_{2l+1}}^{*}+b_{i_{2l+1},i_{1}}b_{i_{2l+1},i_{2}}.

Using our induction hypothesis we can write
P-(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{1}}^{*})(x_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}}^{*})
such that
l_{i_{2l+1}}^{\prime}
does not contain any of the existing variables, and hence conclude our proof.
∎

Now, Lemma
6
combined with Proposition
4
and Theorem
5
gives the wanted result.

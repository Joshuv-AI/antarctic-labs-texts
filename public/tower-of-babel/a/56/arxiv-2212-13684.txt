Joint Receive Antenna Selection and Beamforming in RIS-Aided MIMO Systems
Chongjun Ouyang ⋆ , Ali Bereyhi † , Saba Asaad † , Ralf R. Müller † , and Hongwen Yang ⋆

Abstract
This work studies a low-complexity design for reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-aided multiuser multiple-input multiple-output systems. The base station (BS) applies receive antenna selection to connect a subset of its antennas to the available radio frequency chains. For this setting, the BS switching network, uplink precoders, and RIS phase-shifts are jointly designed, such that the uplink sum-rate is maximized. The principle design problem reduces to an NP-hard mixed-integer optimization. We hence invoke the weighted minimum mean squared error technique and the penalty dual decomposition method to develop a tractable iterative algorithm that approximates the optimal design effectively. Our numerical investigations verify the efficiency of the proposed algorithm and its superior performance as compared with the benchmark.

I
Introduction

Large multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technology, often referred to as massive MIMO, is known to boost the spectral efficiency of wireless channels. However, its fully-digital implementation with a dedicated radio frequency (RF) chain at each antenna suffers from expensive hardware costs and excessive energy consumption. Numerous potential approaches have been introduced to alleviate this issue over the past years; see
[
1
,
3
,
2
]
and the references therein. Among them is antenna selection that sets only a small subset of antennas active in each coherence time. This way it reduces the number of required RF chains, and hence the overall RF cost without significantly degrading the performance.

On a parallel track, recent advances in RF micro electromechanical systems have made programmable meta-surfaces a reality, among which reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) have received a growing attention
[
4
]
. An RIS is a planar array comprising low-cost passive reconfigurable reflecting elements (REs). The main idea behind RIS is to tune the phase responses of REs, such that the propagation environment is smartly reconfigured. Deploying an RIS introduces additional degrees of freedom to the system and improves the wireless link performance, e.g., spectral efficiency
[
5
]
.

Against this backdrop, it is natural to combine the two technologies of antenna selection and RIS to improve system performance while reducing the RF cost; see
[
6
,
8
,

,

,

]
for some recent studies in this respect. The early attempt in
[

]
studied the joint antenna selection and RIS phase-shifts design of a single-user multiple-input single-output (MISO) case. The result is extended to the single-user MIMO case in
[
7
]
. Extensions to multiuser MISO, multiuser MIMO (MU-MIMO), and multi-cell settings are further discussed in
[

,

I-A

Contributions

Despite research progresses on this topic, the available literature is still restricted to some particular scenarios. These restrictions are mainly in two respects: firstly, these earlier studies only considered single-stream transmission and ignored the design of precoders at the user terminals (UTs); Secondly, most existing works, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
10
]
, assume that RE phase-shifts are continuously tuned. From the implementational viewpoint, this is an unrealistic assumption, as most proposed realizations of RISs apply quantized phase-shifts on the receive signal.

In this paper, we consider an RIS-aided MU-MIMO system, where each RE takes its phase-shift from a discrete set, and each UT sends multiple data streams to the base station (BS). We maximize the throughput of this system by proposing a joint design that optimizes the UT precoders, RIS phase-shifts, and the switching network at the BS. The basic design problem is an NP-hard problem dealing with integer programming. Starting from this problem, we propose an iterative algorithm through the following lines of contributions: 1) We propose a penalty dual decomposition (PDD)-based method to tackle the non-convex joint design problem via capitalizing on the weighted minimum mean squared error (WMMSE) technique. 2) To further alleviate the complexity, we next propose an alternative algorithm based on sequential optimization (SO). This algorithm is shown to reduce the complexity at the expense of performance losses. This result verifies the efficiency of our PDD-based algorithm and depicts the complexity-performance trade-off. We verify our low-complexity design through numerical experiments. Our numerical results demonstrate that the proposed algorithms significantly outperform the benchmark.

We consider uplink transmission in a RIS-aided multiuser MIMO setting in which

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{G}{\bm{\Phi}}\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{H}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}+\mathbf{n}.

(1)

The terms appearing in (

•

•

•

•

\displaystyle\phi_{m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}=\left\{{\rm e}^{{\rm j}2\pi\frac{t-1}{2^{Q}}}:t\in\left[2^{Q}\right]\right\}.

(2)

The system operates in the time division duplexing (TDD) mode. The channel state information (CSI) is hence estimated in the uplink training phase via pilot sequences. We assume that the pilots are mutually orthogonal and that the estimation error is negligible. The BS thus learns perfectly the CSI. Details on channel estimation for RIS-aided MIMO systems are found in
[
11
]

II-A

Receive Antenna Selection

The BS has
T<N
RF chains and hence uses a switching network to select a subset of receive
antennas. This switching network connects the selected antennas to the available
T
RF chains at the BS. As a result, the received signal at the RF front-end of the BS is given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{r}}={\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{y}}={\mathbf{S}}\mathbf{G}{\bm{\Phi}}\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{H}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}+{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{n}},

(3)

where
{\mathbf{S}}={\{0,1\}}^{T\times N}
is the antenna selection matrix with

\displaystyle[{\mathbf{S}}]_{t,n}=\begin{cases}1&{\text{antenna}}~n~{\text{is connected to}}~{\text{RF chain}}~t\\
0&{\text{otherwise}}\end{cases}.

Since
\mathbf{S}
is a fat permutation matrix, we have
{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{S}}^{{\mathsf{H}}}={\mathbf{I}}
.

II-B

Performance Metric: Sum-Rate

The sum-rate term for this uplink multiuser MIMO setting is given by

12
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\log_{2}\!\det\!\left(\!{\mathbf{I}}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{G}}{\bm{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{S}}^{\mathsf{H}}\!\right),

(4)

where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}={\mathbbmss{E}}\left[{\mathbf{x}}_{k}{\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\right]={\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\in{\mathbbmss{C}}^{N_{k}\times N_{k}}

(5)

is the transmit covariance matrix of UT
k
subject to the power budget
{\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{Q}}_{k})\leq p_{k}
. The sum-rate is achieved via minimum mean square error (MMSE) estimation along with successive interference cancellation (SIC), referred to as MMSE-SIC.

The sum-rate expression can be further simplified: by exploiting the structure of the selection matrix
\mathbf{S}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{S}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{S}}={\mathsf{diag}}\left\{{\mathbf{s}}\right\}\triangleq{\bm{\Delta}}\in{\mathbbmss{C}}^{N\times N},

(6)

where
\mathbf{s}=[s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}]^{\mathsf{T}}
with

representing the activity of antenna

otherwise. Noting

4

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\log_{2}\det\left(\mathbf{I}+{\sigma^{-2}}{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right).

(7)

In the sequel, we consider the sum-rate as the metric which quantifies the throughput of this system.

Problem Formulation

Our ultimate goal is to find the system design that optimizes the throughput. This means that we strive to jointly design the precoding matrices

III-A

Reformulation of Problem (

P
1

The principle problem (

P
1

P
1

) as the data rate (input-output mutual information) of the hypothetical channel described by
{\mathbf{y}}_{\rm{h}}={\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{x}}_{\rm h}+{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm h}

{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}\in{\mathbbmss{C}}^{N\times L}

{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm h}\sim{\mathcal{CN}}\left({\mathbf{0}},\sigma^{2}{\mathbf{I}}\right)

{\mathbf{x}}_{\rm h}\in{\mathbbmss{C}}^{L}

{\mathbf{x}}_{\rm h}\sim{\mathcal{CN}}({\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{I}})

{\mathbf{y}}_{\rm{h}}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm{h}}

\displaystyle={\mathbbmss{E}}_{{\mathbf{s}}_{\rm h},{\mathbf{n}}_{\rm h}}\left\{\left({\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{y}}_{\rm{h}}-{\mathbf{s}}_{\rm h}\right)\left({\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{y}}_{\rm{h}}-{\mathbf{s}}_{\rm h}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}\right\}

(8a)

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}-{\mathbf{I}}\right)\left({\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}-{\mathbf{I}}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}+\sigma^{2}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}.

(8b)

Invoking the WMMSE technique

13
]

to define the following WMSE minimization problem:

\begin{split}&\min_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}},{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}},{\mathbf{s}},{\bm{\phi}},{\mathbf{P}}}~{\mathsf{tr}}\left\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm{h}}\right\}-\log\det\left({\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}\right)\\
&\quad\quad{\rm{s.t.}}~C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}.\end{split}

(
\mathcal{P}_{2}
)

,
{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}
,
{\mathbf{s}}
,
{\bm{\phi}}
, and

The marginally convex form of the objective in (

, and

&~{\rm{s.t.}}~{\overline{s}}_{n}=s_{n},s_{n}\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{n}\right)=0,{\text{for}}~n\in[N],{\mathbf{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{s}}=T,\\

III-B

The Proposed PDD-Based Algorithm

The PDD-based algorithm is characterized by an embedded double loop structure
[
14
]
. The inner loop solves the augmented Lagrangian (AL) subproblem while the outer loop updates the dual variables and the penalty parameters that correspond to constraint violation. We illustrate these concepts clearly in the sequel throughout the derivation of the PDD-based algorithm.

PDD converts problem (

P
3

) into its AL by moving the equality constraints as a penalty term to the objective function. The AL subproblem corresponding to (

P
3

) is given by

\begin{split}&\min_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}},{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}},\overline{\mathbf{s}},{\mathbf{s}},{\bm{\phi}},{\mathbf{v}},{\mathbf{P}}}~{\mathsf{tr}}\left\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm{h}}\right\}-\log\det\left({\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}\right)+f_{\rho}\\
&~{\rm{s.t.}}~{v}_{m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Q},{\text{for}}~m\in[M],\\
&~\quad~~{\mathsf{tr}}\left({\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\leq p_{k},{\text{for}}~k\in[K],\end{split}

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{4}
)

where
\rho>0
is the penalty parameter penalizing the violation of the equality constraints, and
f_{\rho}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2\rho}\left[\left({\mathbf{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{s}}-T+\rho\xi\right)^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\left(s_{n}-{\overline{s}}_{n}+\rho\mu_{n}\right)^{2}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\left(s_{n}\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{n}\right)+\rho\lambda_{n}\right)^{2}\right]+\sum_{m=1}^{M}|\phi_{m}-v_{m}+\rho\tau_{m}|^{2}\right],

(9)

with
\xi
,
{\bm{\mu}}=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{N}]^{\mathsf{T}}
,
{\bm{\lambda}}=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}]^{\mathsf{T}}
, and
{\bm{\tau}}=[\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{M}]^{\mathsf{T}}
denting the dual variables associated with the equality constraints in
{\mathbf{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{s}}=T
,
{\overline{s}}_{n}=s_{n}
,
s_{n}\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{n}\right)=0
, and
{\bm{\phi}}={\mathbf{v}}
, respectively. It is observed that as
\rho\rightarrow 0
, the penalty term is forced to zero, i.e., equality constraints are enforced. It is shown in
[
14
]
that updating the primal and dual variables, as well as the penalty factor in an alternating manner, PDD converges to a stationary-point solution.

Starting with the penalized form (

P
4

), we now discuss the underlying problems in the inner and outer loops.
The solution to the AL problem (inner loop) is iteratively approximated via the block coordinate descent (BCD) method. To this end, we minimize the objective function of (

4

) by sequentially updating
{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}
,

,
{\mathbf{s}}

III-B

Optimizing the Weights

We optimize

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}^{\star}=\argmin\nolimits_{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}}{\mathsf{tr}}\left\{{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm{h}}\right\}-\log\det\left({\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}\right).

The marginally optimal

\displaystyle{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\star}=(\sigma^{2}{\mathbf{I}}+{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}{\overline{\mathbf{H}}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}})^{-1}{\bm{\Delta}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}.

The marginal optimization for

\mathbf{s}

The marginal problem for

where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\Re\left\{\left({\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}\right)\odot(\overline{\mathbf{H}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathsf{H}})\right\}+{2^{-1}\rho^{-1}}\left({\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}+{\mathbf{I}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+{2^{-1}\rho^{-1}}{{\mathsf{diag}}\left\{\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{1}\right)^{2},\cdots,\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{N}\right)^{2}\right\}},

(14)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{g}}

\displaystyle\triangleq 2\Re\!\left\{{\mathbf{q}}\right\}\!-\!\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\left(\rho\xi\!-\!T\right){\mathbf{1}}\!+\!\left(\rho{\bm{\lambda}}\!-\!\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)\!+\!\rho\left({\mathbf{1}}\!-\!\overline{\mathbf{s}}\right)\!\odot\!{\bm{\mu}}\right),

\displaystyle\triangleq\left[[{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathsf{H}}]_{1,1},\cdots,[{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}\overline{\mathbf{H}}^{\mathsf{H}}]_{N,N}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}.

5
Optimizing the Phase-Shifts

The marginal problem for

is given by

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}^{\star}=\argmin\nolimits_{{\bm{\phi}}\in{\mathbbmss{C}}^{M}}~({\bm{\phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{B}{\bm{\phi}}-2\Re\{{\bm{\phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{b}}\}).

(17)

The terms appearing in (
17
) are defined as follows:

•

{\mathbf{b}}=\mathsf{diag}\{{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{K}}^{\mathsf{H}}\}+\frac{1}{2\rho}({\mathbf{v}}-\rho{\bm{\tau}})
.

•

{\mathbf{K}}=\left[{\mathbf{H}}_{1}{\mathbf{P}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{H}}_{K}{\mathbf{P}}_{K}\right]
.

•

{\mathbf{B}}={\mathbf{B}}_{1}\odot{\mathbf{B}}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}+\frac{1}{2\rho}{\mathbf{I}}
.

•

{\mathbf{B}}_{1}={\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm{h}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}{\mathbf{G}}\succeq{\mathbf{0}}
and
{\mathbf{B}}_{2}={\mathbf{K}}{\mathbf{K}}^{\mathsf{H}}\succeq{\mathbf{0}}
.

Using the Schur product theorem, we can conclude that
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}\odot{\mathbf{B}}_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\succeq{\mathbf{0}}
, and thus

{\mathbf{B}}\succ{\mathbf{0}}
. Therefore, problem (
17
) is an unconstrained convex quadratic optimization problem. Using its first-order optimality condition, we find the solution as

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}^{\star}={\mathbf{B}}^{-1}{\mathbf{b}}.

(18)

III-B
6
Optimizing
\mathbf{v}

The marginal optimization for this auxiliary variable
\mathbf{v}
decouples into
M
parallel scalar optimizations with the
m
th subproblem being

\displaystyle v_{m}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\argmin\nolimits_{v_{m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}}|\phi_{m}-v_{m}+\rho\tau_{m}|.

(19)

Using the fact that
|v_{m}|^{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle v_{m}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\argmax\nolimits_{v_{m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}}\Re\{v_{m}(\phi_{m}+\rho\tau_{m})^{*}\}={\rm e}^{{\rm j}{\vartheta}_{m}^{\star}},

(20)

where
{\vartheta}_{m}^{\star}
is obtained by mapping
\angle({\phi_{m}+\rho\tau_{m}})
to the nearest discrete phase in the set of discrete phases. With infinite-precision representation of phase-shifts, i.e.,
Q\rightarrow\infty
, we have
v_{m}^{\star}={\rm{e}}^{{\rm j}\angle({\phi_{m}+\rho\tau_{m}})}
.

III-B
7
Optimizing the Precoders

The marginal optimization in terms of
{\mathbf{P}}
reduces to
K
subproblems with the
k
th one being

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\star}\!=\!\argmin\nolimits_{{\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}})\leq p_{k}}({\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{C}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k})\!-\!2\Re\{{\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{D}}_{k})\}),

where
{\mathbf{C}}_{k}={\mathbf{O}}_{k}{\mathbf{W}}_{\rm h}{\mathbf{O}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}
,
{\mathbf{O}}_{k}={\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{\rm h}
,
{\mathbf{D}}_{k}={\mathbf{O}}_{k}{\mathbf{W}}_{{\rm h},k}^{\mathsf{H}}
, and
{\mathbf{W}}_{{\rm h},k}
denotes the submatrix of
{\mathbf{W}}_{{\rm h}}
obtained by extracting the elements of the rows with indices from
(1+\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}L_{k^{\prime}})
to
\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k}L_{k^{\prime}}
. This is a standard convex quadratic optimization subproblem whose solution is given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\star}=({\mathbf{C}}_{k}+\lambda{\mathbf{I}})^{-1}{\mathbf{D}}_{k}.

(21)

The regularizer
\lambda
is chosen, such that the complementarity slackness condition, i.e.,
\lambda({\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}})-p_{k})=0
, is satisfied. If
{\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}({\mathbf{C}}_{k}+\lambda{\mathbf{I}})^{-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k})=p_{k}
; then,
\lambda=0
. Otherwise, we can obtain the solution of
\lambda
from the following identity:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}})={\mathsf{tr}}({\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}({\mathbf{C}}_{k}+\lambda{\mathbf{I}})^{-2}{\mathbf{D}}_{k})=p_{k}.

(22)

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{n_{k}=1}^{N_{k}}\frac{|[{\mathbf{U}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}{\mathbf{D}}_{k}{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{U}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}^{\mathsf{H}}]_{n_{k},n_{k}}|^{2}}{([{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}]_{n_{k},n_{k}}+\lambda)^{2}}=p_{k},

(23)

where
{\mathbf{C}}_{k}={\mathbf{U}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}{\mathbf{U}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}
is the eigen-decomposition of
{\mathbf{C}}_{k}
. Since
[{\bm{\Lambda}}_{{\mathbf{C}}_{k}}]_{n_{k},n_{k}}\geq 0
for
n_{k}\in[N_{k}]
, the left-hand side of (
23
) is a monotonously decreasing function of
\lambda\geq 0
. Consequently, we can find
\lambda
by solving equation (
23
) using the bisection-based search method.

We next consider the outer loop of the PDD-based algorithm. In this loop, the dual variables
\{\xi,{\bm{\mu}},{\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{\tau}}\}
and the penalty factor
\rho
are updated. These variables are updated by optimizing the constraint violation quantity that is defined as

\displaystyle h\triangleq\max_{\forall n,m}\{|\phi_{m}\!-\!v_{m}|,|{\mathbf{1}}\!^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{s}}\!-\!T|,\left|{\overline{s}}_{n}\!-\!s_{n}\right|,\left|s_{n}\!\left(1\!-\!{\overline{s}}_{n}\right)\right|\}.

(24)

The optimization of this quantity is discussed in great detail in
[
14
]
and is skipped here, due to lack of space. The outcome of this optimization yield the update scheme as follows: if
h<\mu
, we update the dual variables via the recursive equalities

\displaystyle\xi^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\xi^{(t)}+{\rho}^{-1}\left({\mathbf{1}}^{\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{s}}-T\right),

(25a)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\mu_{n}^{(t)}+{\rho}^{-1}\left({\overline{s}}_{n}-s_{n}\right),

(25b)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{(t)}+{\rho}^{-1}s_{n}\left(1-{\overline{s}}_{n}\right),

(25c)

\displaystyle\tau_{m}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\tau_{m}^{(t)}+{\rho}^{-1}(v_{m}-\phi_{m}),

(25d)

where
t
denotes the outer iteration index. We refer the reader to
[
14
, Table I, Line 4]
for further details. The derived PDD-based algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

IV
Low-Complexity Sequential Optimization

The PDD-based algorithm proposed in Section
III
optimizes the antenna selection vector
\mathbf{s}
, phase-shift vector
\bm{\phi}
, and transmit precoders
\{{\mathbf{P}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
alternately. Although this leads to a feasible complexity, it can lead to a computational burdensome in many applications. To this end, we develop an alternative scheme based on sequential optimization approach in this section. In this respect, we first design the phase-shifts using an element-wise BCD (EWBCD) method; then, we select the antennas using a greedy search (GS). We finally obtain the optimal transmit precoders with the aid of iterative water-filling method. These three steps are illustrated in the sequel.

IV-
1
Phase-Shifts Design

At the first step of the SO-based method, we design the phase-shifts by maximizing the effective channel gain given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{tr}({\overline{\mathbf{H}}}{\overline{\mathbf{H}}}^{\mathsf{H}})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{tr}({\mathbf{G}}{\bm{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}).

(26)

Assuming
{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}={\frac{p_{k}}{N_{k}}}{\mathbf{I}}
for
k\in[K]
, the phase-shifts design problem is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{\phi}}^{\star}=\argmax({\mathsf{tr}}({\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}{\bm{\Phi}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}})={\bm{\phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}\hat{\mathbf{H}}{\bm{\phi}}),~{\rm{s.t.}}~C_{3},

(27)

where
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}\frac{p_{k}}{N_{k}}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{H}}=({\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})\odot{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}}^{\mathsf{T}}
. To handle the discretely constrained
\bm{\phi}
, we propose to optimize the elements of
\bm{\phi}
one by one, resulting in an EWBCD method comprised of
M
steps. At the
m
th step, we optimize
\phi_{m}
while treating
\phi_{m^{\prime}}
for
m^{\prime}\neq m
as constants. This leads to

\displaystyle{\phi}_{m}^{\star}=\argmax\nolimits_{\phi_{m}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{Q}}{2\Re\{\phi_{m}^{*}\varphi_{m}\}},

(28)

where
\varphi_{m}=\sum\nolimits_{m^{\prime}\neq m}[\hat{\mathbf{H}}]_{m^{\prime},m}\phi_{m^{\prime}}
. The solution of this problem is given by
{\phi}_{m}^{\star}={\rm e}^{{\rm j}\delta_{m}^{\star}}
, where
\delta_{m}^{\star}
is obtained by mapping
\angle{\varphi_{m}}
to the nearest discrete phase.

IV-
2
Antenna Selection

In the next step, we optimize the antenna selection vector
\mathbf{s}
for the updated phase-shifts. The resulting problem of antenna selection is given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{s}}^{\star}=\argmax\log_{2}\det(\mathbf{I}+{\sigma^{-2}}{\bm{\Delta}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{\Delta}}^{\mathsf{H}}),~{\rm{s.t.}}~C_{1},

(29)

where
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{G}}{\bm{\Phi}}\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}^{\mathsf{H}}
. To tackle the discrete constraint, we exploit the GS method. More details about this method can be found in
[
2
,
3
]
and are omitted here due to page limitations.

IV-
3
Transmit Precoding Design

After obtaining the phase-shifts and antenna selection vector, we find the optimal transmit precoding matrices by solving

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{P}}~\log_{2}\det(\mathbf{I}+\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{K}{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\phi}},k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}{\mathbf{P}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\phi}},k}^{\mathsf{H}}),~{\rm{s.t.}}~C_{2},

(30)

where
{\mathbf{G}}_{{\bm{\phi}},k}=\sigma^{-1}{\bm{\Delta}}{\mathbf{G}}{\bm{\Phi}}{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
. The optimization in (
30
) can be optimally solved by the iterative water-filling method
[
15
]
.

Based on
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
, we can show that the complexity of sequential optimizations with respect to
{\bm{\phi}}
,
{\mathbf{s}}
, and
\mathbf{P}
scale with
{\mathcal{O}}\left(M\right)
,
{\mathcal{O}}\left(NTM\right)
, and
{\mathcal{O}}\left(KT^{3}\right)
, respectively. Hence, the complexity of the SO-based method scales with
{\mathcal{O}}(NTM+KT^{3})
, which is lower than the PDD-based method.

VI
Conclusion

We proposed an iterative algorithm based on the PDD method for joint antenna selection and beamforming in RIS-aided MU-MIMO systems. An alternative algorithm with reduced complexity was further developed based on the SO scheme. To reduce the complexity, we also developed a SO-based algorithm. Our numerical results imply that the PDD-based method outperforms both the SO-based and benchmark schemes. The SO-based algorithm moreover tracks closely the benchmark performance while gaining considerably in terms of complexity.

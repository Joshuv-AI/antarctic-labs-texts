Wenhua Wang, Aiting Wang

Abstract




Abstract
Let
A
be an expansive dilation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a variable exponent function satisfying the globally log-Hölder continuous condition. Let
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the variable anisotropic Hardy space introduced by Liu
[
16
]
. In this paper, the authors obtain
that the Fourier transform of
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
coincides with a continuous function
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the
sense of tempered distributions. As applications, the authors further conclude a higher order convergence of the continuous function
F
at the origin and then give a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality in the setting
of anisotropic Hardy spaces with variable exponents.

Introduction

As is known to all, the real-variable theory of
Hardy space
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
plays an important role in various fields of analysis and
PDE
s; see, for examples,
[
7
,
13
,
17
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
.
A interesting and natural problem is the characterization of the Fourier transform

\widehat{f}
for
f\in H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. For examples, Coifman
[
6
]
characterized all
\widehat{f}
via entire functions of exponential type
for
n=1
, where
f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
with
p\in(0,\,1]
. Later,
Taibleson and Weiss
[
24
]
proved that, for
p\in(0,\,1]
, the
Fourier transform of
f\in H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
coincides with a continuous function
F
in the sense of tempered
distributions, and there exists a positive constant
C
, such that, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle|F(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})}|x|^{n(1/{p}-1)}.

In 2003, Bownik
[
3
]
introduced the anisotropic Hardy space
H_{A}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(0,\,\infty)
and
A
being a
general expansive matrix on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
In 2013, Bownik and Wang
[
4
]
further extended inequalities (
1.1
) to the setting of Hardy
space
H^{p}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(0,\,1]
. In 2021, Huang et al.
[
14
]
established the inequalities (
1.1
) to the anisotropic
mixed-norm Hardy space
H^{\vec{p}}_{\vec{a}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, where
\vec{p}\in(0,1]^{n}
and
\vec{a}\in[1,\,\infty)^{n}
.

On the other hand, variable exponent function spaces have their applications in fluid dynamics
[
2
]
, image processing
[
5
]
,
PDE
s and variational calculus
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
25
,
26
]
.
Let
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a variable exponent function satisfying the globally log-Hölder continuous condition (see Section
2
below for its definition). Recently, Cruz-Uribe et al.
[
10
]
and Nakai et al.
[
19
]
independently introduced
the variable Hardy space
H^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and obtained their real-variable theory. Then Sawano
[
20
]
, Yang et al.
[
27
]
and Zhuo et al.
[
28
]
further contributed to the theory.
Very recently, Liu et al.
[
16
]

introduced the variable anisotropic Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with a general expansive matrix
A
, and established its some real-variable characterizations of
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, respectively, in terms of the atomic, the maximal functions and the Littlewood-Paley functions characterization.

Inspired by the previous works,
it is a natural and interesting problem to ask whether
there is an extension to the variable exponents setting
of the inequalities (
1.1
).
In this paper we shall answer this problem affirmatively. We obtain
the characterization of the Fourier transform on

\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. In addition, we also establish its some applications.

Precisely, this article is organized as follows.

In Section

F
on

in the

In Section

at the origin and then give a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality in the setting

2
The definitions of variable anisotropic Hardy spaces

In this section, we recall the definition of variable anisotropic Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function
M_{N}(f)
.

Firstly, we recall the definition of expansive dilations
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
; see
[
3
, p. 5]
. A real
n\times n
matrix
A
is called an
expansive dilation
, shortly a
dilation
, if

\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}|\lambda|>1
, where
\sigma(A)
denotes the set of
all
eigenvalues
of
A
. Let
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
be two
positive numbers
such that

1<\lambda_{-}<\min\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}\leq\max\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}<\lambda_{+}.

If
A
is diagonalizable over

\mathbb{C}
, we can even take
\lambda_{-}:=\min\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}
and

\lambda_{+}:=\max\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}
.
Otherwise, we need to choose them sufficiently close to these
equalities according to what we need in our arguments.

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
, for a fixed dilation
A
,
there exist a number
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and a set
\Delta:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,|Px|<1\}
, where
P
is some non-degenerate
n\times n
matrix, such that

\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta,

and we can
assume that
|\Delta|=1
, where
|\Delta|
denotes the

n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
of the set
\Delta
. Let

B_{k}:=A^{k}\Delta
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Then
B_{k}
is open,

B_{k}\subset rB_{k}\subset B_{k+1}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ |B_{k}|=b^{k}.

In this paper, let
b:=|\det A|
.
An ellipsoid
x+B_{k}
for some
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
dilated ball
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the set of all such dilated balls, that is,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\{x+B_{k}:\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Throughout the whole paper, let
\sigma
be the
smallest integer
such that
2B_{0}\subset A^{\sigma}B_{0}

and, for any subset
E
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
E^{\complement}:={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus E
. Then,
for all
k,\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
k\leq j
, it holds true that

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{k}+B_{j}\subset B_{j+\sigma},

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{k}+(B_{k+\sigma})^{\complement}\subset(B_{k})^{\complement},

where
E+F
denotes the
algebraic sum

\{x+y:\ x\in E,\,y\in F\}

of sets
E,\,F\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
quasi-norm
, associated with
dilation
A
, is a Borel measurable mapping

\rho_{A}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, for simplicity, denoted by

\rho
, satisfying

(i)

\rho(x)>0
for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{\vec{0}_{n}\}
,
here and hereafter,
\vec{0}_{n}
denotes the origin of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
;

(ii)

\rho(Ax)=b\rho(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where, as above,
b:=|\det A|
;

(iii)

\rho(x+y)\leq C_{A}\left[\rho(x)+\rho(y)\right]
for
all
x,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
C_{A}\in[1,\,\infty)
is a constant independent of
x
and
y
.

In the standard dyadic case
A:=2{{\rm I}_{n\times n}}
,
\rho(x):=|x|^{n}
for
all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is
an example of homogeneous quasi-norms associated with
A
, here and hereafter,
{\rm I}_{n\times n}
denotes the
n\times n

unit matrix
,

|\cdot|
always denotes the
Euclidean norm
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
, we know that all homogeneous quasi-norms associated with a fixed dilation

A
are equivalent. Therefore, for a
fixed dilation
A
, in what follows, for simplicity, we
always use the
step homogeneous quasi-norm

\rho_{A}
defined by setting, for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho_{A}(x):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b^{k}\chi_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}(x)\ {\rm if}\ x\neq\vec{0}_{n},\hskip 8.5359pt{\mathrm{or\ else}\hskip 8.5359pt}\rho_{A}(\vec{0}_{n}):=0.

By (
2.2
), we know that, for all
x,\,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho_{A}(x+y)\leq b^{\sigma}[\rho_{A}(x)+\rho_{A}(y)].

Moreover,
({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,\rho_{A},\,dx)
is a space of
homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
7
,
8
]
,
where
dx
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Suppose
\rho_{A}
is a homogeneous quasi-norms associated with a fixed dilation

A
. Then there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}_{A}>0
such that, for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathfrak{C}_{A}}[\rho_{A}(x)]^{\lambda_{-}/\ln b}\leq|x|\leq\mathfrak{C}_{A}[\rho_{A}(x)]^{\lambda_{+}/\ln b}\ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \rho_{A}(x)\geq 1,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathfrak{C}_{A}}[\rho_{A}(x)]^{\lambda_{+}/\ln b}\leq|x|\leq\mathfrak{C}_{A}[\rho_{A}(x)]^{\lambda_{-}/\ln b}\ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \rho_{A}(x)<1,

where
\mathfrak{C}_{A}
depends only on the dilation
A
.

Now we recall that a measurable function
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
is called a
variable exponent
. For any variable exponent
p(\cdot)
, let

(2.6)

\displaystyle p_{-}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,inf\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x)\quad\mathrm{and}\ \ p_{+}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x).

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
the set of all variable exponents
p(\cdot)
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Then the
modular function
(or, for simplicity, the
modular
)
\varrho_{p(\cdot)}
, associated with
p(\cdot)
, is defined by setting

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx

and the
Luxemburg
(also called
Luxemburg-Nakano
)
quasi-norm

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
by

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\,\infty):\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}.

Moreover, the
variable Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f

satisfying that
\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)<\infty
, equipped with the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

(i)

For any

r\in(0,\,\infty)
and
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|{|f|^{r}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\left\|f\right\|^{r}_{L^{rp(\cdot)}}.

Moreover, for any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
f,g\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|\mu f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=|\mu|\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
and

\left\|f+g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq\left\|f\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}+\left\|g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}},

here and hereafter,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\underline{p}:=\min\{p_{-},\,1\}.

(ii)

From
[
9
]
, we know that, for any function
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}

with
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}>0
,

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/{\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}})=1

and, if
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq 1
, then

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

A function
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
is said to satisfy the
globally log-Hölder continuous condition
, denoted by

p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
, if there exist two positive constants
C_{\log}(p)
and
C_{\infty}
, and
p_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that, for any
x,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{\log}(p)}{\log(e+1/\rho(x-y))}

and

\displaystyle|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\rho(x))}.

A
C^{\infty}
function
\varphi
is said to belong to the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}
if,
for every integer
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and multi-index
\alpha
,

\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}:=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}[\rho(x)]^{\ell}|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|<\infty
.
The dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}
, namely, the space of all tempered distributions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
equipped with the weak-
\ast

topology, is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}:\ \|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}\leq 1,\ |\alpha|\leq N,\ \ \ell\leq N\right\}.

In what follows, for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
\varphi_{k}(x):=b^{-k}\varphi\left(A^{-k}x\right)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. The
non-tangential maximal function

M_{\varphi}(f)
with respect to
\varphi
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}(f)(x):=\sup_{y\in x+B_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f*\varphi_{k}(y)|.

Moreover, for any given
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
non-tangential grand maximal function

M_{N}(f)
of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle M_{N}(f)(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}M_{\varphi}(f)(x).

The following variable anisotropic Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
was introduced in
[
16
, Definition 2.4]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
A
be a dilation and
N\in[\lfloor({1/\underline{p}}-1)/\ln\lambda_{-}\rfloor+2,\,\infty)
, where
\underline{p}

is as in (
2.7
). The
variable anisotropic Hardy space

\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}:=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:M_{N}(f)\in L^{p(\cdot)}\right\}

and, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
, let
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}:=\|M_{N}(f)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
.

(i)

When

p(\cdot):=p
with

, the space

is reduced to the anisotropic Hardy

]

(ii)

When

,
the space

is reduced to the variable Hardy space

introduced by Nakai et al.
[

]
and also Cruz-Uribe et al.

We begin with
the following notion of anisotropic
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atoms introduced in
[

, Definition 4.1]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (

). An

(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom
is a measurable function
a
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
satisfying

(i)

(support)
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a:=\overline{\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:a(x)\neq 0\}}\subset B
, where
B\in\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{B}
is as in (

);

(ii)

(size)
\|a\|_{L^{q}}\leq\frac{|B|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}
;

(iii)

(vanishing moment)
\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+}
with
|\alpha|\leq s
.

In what follows, for convenience, we call an anisotropic
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom simply by
a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom. The following variable anisotropic atomic Hardy space
was introduced in
[
16
, Definition 4.2]

Definition 2.7
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
,

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (

),
and

be a dilation.
The

\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A,\mathrm{atom}}

is defined to be the set of all distributions

satisfying that there
exist

and a sequence of

(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atoms,
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported, respectively,
on

such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}.

Moreover, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A,\mathrm{atom}}
, let

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A,\mathrm{atom}}}:=\inf\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{{B^{(j)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(j)}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}},

where the infimum is taken over all the decompositions of
f
as above.

3
Main result

In this section, we state the main result of the article as follow:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
with
p_{-}
,
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
). Then for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
, there exists a continuous function
F
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
such that

\widehat{f}=F
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, and there exists a positive constant
C
such that
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle|F(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1/{p_{+}}-1}\right\},

where
A^{*}
denotes the transposed matrix of
A
and
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
.

Remark 3.2
.

When

p(\cdot):=p
with
p\in(0,\,1]
, this result is reduced to
[
3
, Theorem 1]
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need some technical lemmas.
The following lemma reveals the atomic decompositions of the variable anisotropic Hardy spaces (see
[
16
, Theorem 4.8]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
q\in(\max\{p_{+},\,1\},\,\infty]

with
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
),

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
) and

N\in{\mathbb{N}}\cap[\lfloor(1/{\underline{p}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor+2,\,\infty)
.
Then

\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}=\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}_{A,\mathrm{atom}}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

To state following two lemmas, let us recall two basic definitions. We define the dilation operator by
D_{A}(f)(x)=f(Ax)
, then commute with Fourier transform by following identity for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle b^{j}\left(D_{A^{*}}^{j}\widehat{D_{A}^{j}f}\right)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
,
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
a
be a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom supported on
x_{0}+B_{k}
with some
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{D_{A}^{k}(a)}(x)\right|\leq Cb^{-k/q}\|a\|_{L^{q}}\min\left\{1,\,|x|^{s+1}\right\}.

Proof.

From the assumption that
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset x_{0}+B_{k}
with
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we obtain that

\text{supp}\,D_{A}^{k}(a)\subset A^{-k}x_{0}+B_{0}.

Let
T(\xi)
be the degree
s
Taylor polynomial of the function
\xi\rightarrow e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\,\xi\rangle}
center at
A^{-k}x_{0}
. Using the vanishing of moments of the atom
a
and the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{D_{A}^{k}(a)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}D_{A}^{k}(a)(\xi)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\,\xi\rangle}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{A^{-k}x_{0}+B_{0}}D_{A}^{k}(a)(\xi)\left[e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\,\xi\rangle}-T(\xi)\right]d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{A^{-k}x_{0}+B_{0}}\left|a(A^{k}\xi)\right|\left|\xi-A^{-k}x_{0}\right|^{s+1}|x|^{s+1}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{s+1}b^{-k}\int_{x_{0}+B_{k}}\left|a(\xi)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim|x|^{s+1}b^{-k/q}\|a\|_{L^{q}},

where
i:=\sqrt{-1}
.
By the fact that
\text{supp}\,D_{A}^{k}(a)\subset A^{-k}x_{0}+B_{0}
, and the Hölder inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{D_{A}^{k}(a)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}D_{A}^{k}(a)(\xi)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\,\xi\rangle}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{-k}\int_{x_{0}+B_{k}}\left|a(\xi)\right|d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{-k/q}\|a\|_{L^{q}}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

To show Theorem
3.1
, we also need the following essential lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
with
p_{-}
,
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
),
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom
a
supported on
x_{0}+B_{k}
with
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|\leq C\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
a
be a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom with
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset x_{0}+B_{k}
, where
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then it follows from (
3.2
), (
3
) and the size condition of
a
that, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|b^{k}\left[D_{A^{*}}^{k}\widehat{D_{A}^{k}}(a)\right](x)\right|

\displaystyle=b^{k}\left|\widehat{D_{A}^{k}}(a)\left(A^{*k}x\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{(1-1/q)k}\|a\|_{L^{q}}\min\left\{1,\,\left|A^{*k}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{b^{k}}{\|\chi_{x_{0}+B_{k}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\min\left\{1,\,\left|A^{*k}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{b^{(1-{1}/{p_{+}})k},\,b^{(1-{1}/{p_{-}})k}\right\}\min\left\{1,\,\left|A^{*k}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}.

If
\rho_{A^{*}}(x)<b^{-k}
, from (
2.6
) and
s>(1/p_{-}-1)\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}-1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{a}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{b^{(1-{1}/{p_{+}})k},\,b^{(1-{1}/{p_{-}})k}\right\}\min\left\{1,\,\left|A^{*k}x\right|^{s+1}\right\}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{b^{(1-{1}/{p_{+}})k},\,b^{(1-{1}/{p_{-}})k}\right\}b^{(1/p_{-}-1)k}[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1/p_{-}-1}

\displaystyle\thicksim\max\left\{b^{(1-{1}/{p_{+}})k},\,b^{(1-{1}/{p_{-}})k}\right\}.

which implies that (
3.4
) holds true.

On the other hand, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, if
\rho_{A^{*}}(x)\geq b^{-k}
, then, by (
3.2
), we find that (
3.4
) holds true in this case. The concrete details being omitted. This finishes
the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}
. Then we have, for any
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\{B^{(j)}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
,

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|\leq\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{B^{(j)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(j)}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

where
\underline{p}
is as in (
2.7
).

Proof.

Since
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}
and the well-known inequality that,
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}}

with
p\in(0,\,1]
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|\left\|\frac{\chi_{B^{(j)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(j)}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{B^{(j)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(j)}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{B^{(j)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(j)}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.
For any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
, from Definition
2.7
and Lemma
3.3
, we know that there exist numbers
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom,
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, supported, respectively, on
\{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\thicksim\inf\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}}{\|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}.

By the continuity of the Fourier transform on
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},

In addition, by the fact that, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}},\,a_{j}\in L^{1}
, and the Hausdorff
Young inequality, we know that
\widehat{a_{j}}\in L^{\infty}
. From this, Lemmas
3.4
and

3.6
, and (
3.7
), we conclude that, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim C\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}}{\|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}<\infty.

Thus, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, the summation
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)
converges absolutely on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Without loss of generality, we may let, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle F(x):=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)

pointwisely and hence, for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle|F(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}.

Next, we prove that
\widehat{f}=F\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. By (
3.8
), it suffices to show that

F=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},

that is, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}},\,\varphi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)\varphi(x)dx<\infty.

In fact, by Lemma
3.4
, we know that there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N>1/p_{-}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)\varphi(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B^{*}_{k+1}\backslash B^{*}_{k}}\max\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}|\varphi(x)|dx+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{({1}/{p_{-}}-1)k}b^{k}\frac{1}{(1+b^{k})^{N}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

which, together with Lemma
3.6
, (
3.7
) and (
3.11
), implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)\varphi(x)dx\right|\lesssim\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|=0,

and hence
\widehat{f}=F\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. Furthermore, for any given compact set
K
, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
K
, such that, for any
x\in K,\,\rho_{A^{*}}(x)\leq C
. By this and (
3.10
), we obtain that, for any
x\in K
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}||\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|\lesssim\max\left\{C^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1},\,C^{{1}/{p_{+}}-1}\right\}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|.

Therefore, the absolute convergence of
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}\widehat{a_{j}}(x)
is uniform on compact set
K
.
By this and the fact that, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\widehat{a_{j}}
is a continuous function, we conclude that, for any compact set
K
,
F
is also a continuous function on
K
, and hence on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4
Some applications

In this section, as applications of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain a higher order convergence of the continuous function
F
in Theorem
3.1
at the origin, and establish a variant of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality on the anisotropic Hardy spaces with variable exponents. The following conclusion is the first main result of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\mathrm{log}}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
with
p_{-}
,
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
), and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
. Then there exists a continuous function
F
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
such that
\widehat{f}=F
in
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
and

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{{F}(x)}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}=0.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
. By Lemma
3.3
, we know that there exist numbers
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom,
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, supported, respectively, on
\{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\thicksim\inf\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}}{\|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}},

which, together with Lemma
3.6
, implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty.

By Theorem
3.1
, we obtain that there exists
F
such that
\widehat{f}=F
in
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
. Therefore, we have

\frac{|{F}(x)|}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|\frac{|\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}.

By (
3.5
) and the condition that
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
, it is easy to check that, for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{|\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}=0.

Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
, we know that there exists
\delta>0
such that, for any
|x|<\delta
,

\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}<\frac{\epsilon}{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{j}|+1}.

By this, we have, for any
|x|<\delta
,

\displaystyle\frac{|{F}(x)|}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}<\epsilon.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{{F}(x)}{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{{1}/{p_{-}}-1}}=0.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Now we state the second result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\mathrm{log}}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
with
p_{-}
,
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
), and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
. Then there exists a continuous function
F
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
such that
\widehat{f}=F
in
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
and

\left[\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|F(x)|^{p_{+}}\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-1-({p_{+}}/{p_{-}})},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}dx\right]^{1/p_{+}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}.

Proof.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\mathrm{log}}
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 1
, and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}
. By Lemma
3.3
, we obtain that there exist numbers
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of
(p(\cdot),\,\infty,\,s)
-atom,
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, supported, respectively, on
\{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{j}a_{j}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot)}_{A}}\thicksim\inf\left\|\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{j}|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}}{\|\chi_{{{x_{j}+B_{\ell_{j}}}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\right]^{\underline{p}}\right\}^{1/\underline{p}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}.

By Theorem
3.1
, we know that there exists continuous
F
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, such that

\displaystyle\widehat{f}=F\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime},

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|F(x)|^{p_{+}}\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-1-{p_{+}}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{j}|^{p_{+}}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\left[|\widehat{a_{j}}(x)|\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{2}/{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

By Lemma
3.6
, we only need to show that, for any
(p(\cdot),\,\infty,\,s)
-atom
a
with
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset x_{0}+B_{k}
,
x_{0}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\left\{\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{p_{+}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-1-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}\lesssim 1.

Now we can write

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{p_{+}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-1-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{B_{-k}^{*}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{p_{+}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{p_{+}-2}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left\{\int_{(B_{-k}^{*})^{\complement}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{p_{+}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{2}/{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{I+II}.

For the term
\mathrm{I}
, by (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{k[1+{\ln\lambda_{-}}(s+1)/{\ln b}]}\max\left\{b^{-k/p_{-}},\,b^{-k/p_{+}}\right\}\left\{\int_{\rho_{A^{*}}(x)<b^{-k}}\right.

\displaystyle\ \ \times\left.\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}+\left[(s+1)\ln{\lambda_{-}}/\ln b\right]},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{2}/{p_{+}}-{(s+1)\ln\lambda_{-}}/{\ln b}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{k[1+{(s+1)\ln\lambda_{-}}/{\ln b}]}\max\left\{b^{-k/p_{-}},\,b^{-k/p_{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle\ \ \times\min\left\{b^{-k[1-{1}/{p_{-}}+{(s+1)\ln\lambda_{-}}/{\ln b}]},\,b^{-k[1-{2}/{p_{+}}+{(s+1)\ln\lambda_{-}}/{\ln b}]}\right\}

\displaystyle\thicksim 1.

For the term
\mathrm{II}
, from the Hölder inequality, the Plancherel theorem, the fact that
0<p_{-},\,p_{+}\leq 1
, and the size condition of
a
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{(B_{-k}^{*})^{\complement}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{p_{+}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{2}/{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{p_{+}}dx\right\}^{1/p_{+}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{(B_{-k}^{*})^{\complement}}|\widehat{a}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\left\{\int_{(B_{-k}^{*})^{\complement}}\left[\min\left\{[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{p_{-}}},\,[\rho_{A^{*}}(x)]^{1-{2}/{p_{+}}}\right\}\right]^{{2p_{+}}/({2-p_{+}})}dx\right\}^{{(2-p_{+})}/({2p_{+}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|a(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\min\left\{b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{-}})},\,b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{+}})}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|x_{0}+B_{k}|^{1/2}}{\left\|\chi_{x_{0}+B_{k}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\min\left\{b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{-}})},\,b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{+}})}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\left\{b^{k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{-}})},\,b^{k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{+}})}\right\}\min\left\{b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{-}})},\,b^{-k({1}/{2}-{1}/{p_{+}})}\right\}

\displaystyle\thicksim 1.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

It is worth pointing out that there is a difference with Liu’s paper in the proof of Theorem

. Indeed, we use the

, while Liu proves
[

, Theorem 4.3]
by using the

(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom characterization of
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{p(\cdot)}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

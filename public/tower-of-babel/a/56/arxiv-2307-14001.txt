Time multiscale modeling of sorption kinetics I: uniformly accurate schemes for highly oscillatory advection-diffusion equation
Clarissa Astuto, Mohammed Lemou, Giovanni Russo

Abstract
In this paper we propose a numerical method to solve a 2D advection-diffusion equation, in the highly oscillatory regime. We use an efficient and robust integrator which leads to an accurate approximation of the solution without any time step-size restriction. Uniform first and second order numerical approximations in time are obtained with errors, and at a cost, that are independent of the oscillation frequency. This work is part of a long time project, and the final goal is the resolution of a Stokes-advection-diffusion system, in which the expression for the velocity in the advection term, is the solution of the Stokes equations. This paper focuses on the time multiscale challenge, coming from the velocity that is an ε − \varepsilon- periodic function, whose expression is explicitly known. We also introduce a two–scale formulation, as a first step to the numerical resolution of the complete oscillatory Stokes-advection-diffusion system, that is currently under investigation. This two–scale formulation is also useful to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the solution.

Model

In this paper we investigate the time multiscale coming from the advection term in the system. As a preliminary, work we assume that the expression for the velocity is known, and we do not have to compute the solution of the Stokes equations.

In the presence of a moving fluid, the conservation law for the local concentration of ions

\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\vec{J},\quad{\rm in}\,\mathcal{S},

Space discretization

We use a uniform square Cartesian cell-centered discretization, with

4
A two–scale formulation

In this section our goal is to provide a formal derivation of the asymptotic models for Eq. (
16
), to
1^{\rm st}
and
2^{\rm nd}
order in
\varepsilon
, and how the two–scale formulation can be used to derive uniformly accurate numerical schemes. The strategy is to formulate and analyze the equations obtained by decoupling the slow variable
t
and the fast variable
\Theta=t/\varepsilon
, following the same strategy adopted in

[
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
. In this way, we obtain the two–scale formulation of Eq. (
16
), and then we derive the asymptotic models. Note that, one could also use the two–scale formulation to derive uniformly accurate numerical schemes solving the original equations in the vein of
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
. Since in this paper we are solving a preliminary case, in which the velocity is explicitly known, adding a new variable
\Theta
is not necessary. We will follow another way to construct uniformly accurate schemes.

The use of averaging methods is very common when multiple scales are involved, and the aim of the averaging is to weaken the stiffness of the short scale
t/\varepsilon
. We will consider a reformulation of the problem, that is more general and in which we add a degree of freedom when defining the new variable
\Theta
. The general problem is easier to solve numerically because its initial condition can be chosen in such a way that the solution, and its first and second time derivatives, are bounded in
\varepsilon
.

4.1
Formal derivation of the asymptotic models: first and second order in
\varepsilon

In this subsection we derive the asymptotic models for Eq. (
16
), to
1^{\rm st}
and
2^{\rm nd}
order in
\varepsilon
. Given
h>0
, we consider the bounded linear operators
L_{h}
and
Q_{h}
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
, that were defined in Section

. Then we introduce the two–scale vector-valued function
C_{h}(t,\Theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}

1-

\Theta
, and satisfies
C_{h}(t,t/\varepsilon)=c_{\varepsilon,h}(t)
, for all

\partial_{t}C_{h}(t,\Theta)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{\Theta}C_{h}(t,\Theta)=\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(C_{h}(t,\Theta)),\qquad C_{h}(0,\Theta)=C^{0}_{h}(\Theta)

(22)

\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(C_{h}(t,\Theta))=\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\,\vec{u}_{h}(\Theta)\right)C_{h}(t,\Theta).

(23)

It is important to observe that, while the initial condition for the Eq. (

C^{0}_{h}(\Theta)

C^{0}_{h}(0)=c_{\varepsilon,h}^{0}

for the two–scale Eqs. (

-
23

-
23

Here we perform the asymptotic analysis starting from the following decomposition of

Now we search for the expression of

. To do this, we substitute the expression (
24

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{C}_{h}(t)+\partial_{t}\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{\Theta}\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(\overline{C}_{h}(t)+\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta))

while the average of the Eq. (

\partial_{t}\overline{C}_{h}(t)=\langle\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(\overline{C}_{h}(t)+\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\bullet))\rangle,

with

Now we subtract Eq. (
27

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{\Theta}\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(\overline{C}_{h}(t)+\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\Theta))-\left<\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(\overline{C}_{h}(t)+\widetilde{C}_{h}(t,\bullet))\right>,

4.2
A class of numerical schemes derived from the two–scale equation

Here we show how to derive a uniformly accurate first and second order scheme for Eq. (
16
) with a two–scale formulation, though in this paper we choose another strategy. Our approach is based on a suitable time integral formulation and avoids the additional variable
\Theta=t/\varepsilon
. This variable could indeed increase the complexity of the numerical schemes.

First we solve

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{\Theta}C_{h}(t,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(C_{h}(t,\Theta))\quad{\rm in}\,\Omega_{h},\,\forall t>0

(41)

\displaystyle{C}_{h}(0,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{0}_{h}(\Theta)=c^{0}_{\varepsilon,h}

(42)

where the solution
C_{h}(t,\Theta)
of this problem, and its first time derivative, are uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
. One can construct a first order uniformly accurate scheme for Eq. (
16
) starting from (
41
-
42
), and then setting
c_{\varepsilon,t}(t)=C_{h}(t,t/\varepsilon)
(see, for instance,
[
13
]
).

To obtain a second order scheme, we solve the following

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{C}_{h}(t,\Theta)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\partial_{\Theta}C_{h}(t,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}(C_{h}(t,\Theta))\quad{\rm in}\,\Omega_{h},\,\forall t>0

(43)

\displaystyle{C}_{h}(0,\Theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{0}_{\varepsilon,h}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\Theta}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\sigma}(c^{0}_{\varepsilon,h})-\langle\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}(c^{0}_{\varepsilon,h})\rangle\right)d\sigma

(44)

where the solution
C_{h}(t,\Theta)
of this problem and its time derivatives, to first and second order, are uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
. Therefore, one can construct a second order uniformly accurate scheme for Eq. (
16
) by first solving (
43
-
44
), and then setting
c_{\varepsilon,t}(t)=C_{h}(t,t/\varepsilon)
.

We do not apply this strategy here but we will use it in a more involved case where our model should be coupled with Stokes equations. This task is deferred to a work already under investigation.

5
First order accurate scheme, uniformly in
\varepsilon

In this section we construct a scheme to solve Eq. (
16
), that is first order accurate in time, and we prove that it is also uniformly accurate in
\varepsilon
. The only property that we use is that the first time derivative of the solution is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon
, and this property is justified since we assume that the initial condition is not oscillatory in space and the boundary conditions for the concentration are not oscillatory in time.

In this section we define the operator
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t}
as a sum of three sparse matrices, the identity
I
and the two tri-diagonal block matrices,
L_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, defined in Eqs. (
17
). In this way, it is easy to solve the linear system in Eq. (
46
) with standard numerical techniques.

Proposition 1
.

Let
L_{h}
and
Q_{h}
be bounded operators in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
defined in Section
3
, with
h>0
. Let
c_{\varepsilon,h}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
be bounded in
\varepsilon
,
\vec{u}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
be a bounded given
1-
periodic function of time. Then, there exists a constant
\Delta t_{0}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that, for all
\Delta t<\Delta t_{0}
, the following holds true:

i)

The operator
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t}:=I-\Delta tL_{h}-Q_{h}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\,ds
in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}}
is invertible.

ii)

The following:

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}(0),

(45)

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t}}^{-1}c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n},

(46)

for

is a first order scheme, uniformly accurate with respect to
\varepsilon
, solving the Eq. (

). In other words, we have
||c(t^{n})-c^{n}||\leq K\Delta t
for all

, with

independent of
\varepsilon
,
\Delta t=t_{\rm fin}/M
,
t_{\rm fin}>0
and
t^{n}=n\Delta t
.

Proof.

To prove i), we first show that
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t}\to I
, when
\Delta t\to 0
since the operators
L_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, and the function
\vec{u}_{h}
are bounded.
Thus there exists a constant
\widehat{K}>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
), such that,
\forall\Delta t<\widehat{K}
,
{\rm det}(\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t})\neq 0
, and
\mathcal{A}^{1}_{\Delta t}
is invertible.

To prove ii), we first show how to deduce the scheme defined in Eqs. (
45
-
46
). We start integrating Eq. (
16
) between
t
and
t^{n+1}
, with
[t,t^{n+1}]\subset[t^{n},t_{\rm fin}]

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t^{n+1}}\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\right)c_{\varepsilon,h}(s)ds.

(47)

Evaluating the quantity
c_{\varepsilon,h}(t)
in
t=t^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)c_{\varepsilon,h}(s)\right)ds,

(48)

and then the following

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n})\approx\left(\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\right)\,ds\right)c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1}).

(49)

Since the operators
L_{h}
and
Q_{h}
do not depend on time, Eq. (
49
) becomes

c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n})\approx L_{h}\,\Delta tc_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})+Q_{h}c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)ds

(50)

where the integral
\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\,ds
is calculated analytically. At the end, the scheme reads

c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}-c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n}=L_{h}\,\Delta t\,c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}+Q_{h}c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\,ds

where
c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n}\approx c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n})
.

Now we prove that the scheme in Eq. (

\Delta t
, uniformly in
\varepsilon

51

48

(52)

Here we substitute
\,c_{\varepsilon,h}(s)-c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}=c_{\varepsilon,h}(s)-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})+c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}\,
in Eq. (

\displaystyle e_{n+1}-e_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\right)\left(c_{\varepsilon,h}(s)-c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})\right)\,ds

(53)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{t^{n}}^{t^{n+1}}\left(L_{h}+Q_{h}\vec{u}_{h}(s/\varepsilon)\right)\,ds\left(c_{\varepsilon,h}(t^{n+1})-c_{\varepsilon,h}^{n+1}\right).

\vec{u}_{h}(t/\varepsilon)

Considering now the norm of the Eq. (

||e_{n+1}||-||e_{n}||\leq||e_{n+1}-e_{n}||\leq K_{1}K_{2}\Delta t^{2}+||e_{n+1}||K_{2}\Delta t\leq K_{3}\Delta t^{2}+||e_{n+1}||K_{3}\Delta t,

E_{n+1}+\Delta t\leq(1-K_{3}\Delta t)^{-1}(E_{n}+\Delta t).

(57)

\displaystyle E_{n}+\Delta t\leq(1-K_{3}\Delta t)^{-n}\Delta t\leq\left(1-K_{3}\frac{t_{\rm fin}}{N}\right)^{-N}\Delta t

K_{4}
independent of

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\left(\left(1-K_{3}\,{t_{\rm fin}}/{N}\right)^{-N}-1\right)=\exp{(K_{3}t_{\rm fin})}-1

To conclude the proof, we define
\Delta t_{0}=\min\{\widehat{K},1/K_{3}\}.

∎

8
Conclusions

In this work, we have presented a general strategy for constructing uniformly accurate numerical schemes for highly oscillatory advection diffusion equations, motivated by the necessity of solving the multiscale problem developed in
[
2
]
to treat the diffusion and trapping or a surfactant around a rapidly oscillatory bubble (see also
[
24
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
1
]
).

Traditional numerical time integrators, that are commonly employed, require significant time restrictions to achieve accurate solutions when the oscillation period is much smaller than the diffusion time, since the time step has to be smaller than the oscillation period.

Here we demonstrate that there are alternative choices available to achieve the desired accuracy without resolving the short oscillation period, thus making the computational cost orders of magnitude smaller that the one required by methods from the literature.

The accuracy of the proposed method actually improves when the oscillation frequency increases, providing accurate solutions with time steps much larger then the oscillation period.

Here we assume that the fluid velocity is a known function of space and time.
A natural extension of the current work
is the numerical solution of a coupled Stokes-advection-diffusion system, where the expression for the velocity in the advection term is obtained numerically by solving the Stokes equations.

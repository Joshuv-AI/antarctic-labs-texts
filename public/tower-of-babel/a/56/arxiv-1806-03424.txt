Abstract
Since the standard model contribution is virtually absent, any observation of direct CP violation in the Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays would be evidence of new physics. In this paper, we conduct a quantitative study on direct CP violation in D 0 → K − ​ π + D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+} , D s + → η ​ π + D_{s}^{+}\to\eta\pi^{+} and D s + → η ′ ​ π + D_{s}^{+}\to\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+} decays in the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L × S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) R × U ​ ( 1 ) B − L SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L} gauge extension of the standard model. In the model, direct CP violation arises mainly from the interference between the decay amplitude coming from the SM left-left current operators and that from the right-right current operators induced by W R + W_{R}^{+} gauge boson exchange. Interestingly, the strong phase between the two amplitudes is evaluable, since it stems from difference in QCD corrections to the left-left and right-right current operators, which is a short-distance QCD effect given by ∼ ( α s ​ ( M W L 2 ) / 4 ​ π ) ​ log ⁡ ( M W R 2 / M W L 2 ) \sim(\alpha_{s}(M_{W_{L}}^{2})/4\pi)\log(M_{W_{R}}^{2}/M_{W_{L}}^{2}) . We assess the maximal direct CP violation in the above decays in the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L × S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) R × U ​ ( 1 ) B − L SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L} model. Additionally, we present a correlation between direct CP violation in these modes and one in K → π ​ π K\to\pi\pi decay parametrized by ϵ ′ \epsilon^{\prime} , since W R + W_{R}^{+} gauge boson has a sizable impact on the latter.

Introduction

In the standard model (SM), direct CP violation in the Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays
is highly suppressed at the level of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})

Delepine:2012xw

because no multiple tree and/or penguin diagrams with different CP phases can interfere.
Hence, if discovered, direct CP violation in these modes would immediately be a sign of new physics.
This is in contrast to the singly-Cabibbo-suppressed decays, where tree and penguin diagrams in the SM interfere to yield direct CP violation,
and also
c\to s\bar{s}u
and
c\to d\bar{d}u
processes
interfere through long-distance effects and may lead to sizable direct CP violation in the SM
Golden:1989qx
.
In this paper, we conduct a quantitative study on direct CP violation in Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays with no final-state
K^{0}
,
namely,
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
,
D_{s}^{+}\to\eta\pi^{+}
and
D_{s}^{+}\to\eta^{\prime}\pi^{+}
decays,
in the
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
gauge extension of the SM.
Here, the absence of final-state
K^{0}
ensures that
Cabibbo-favored decay amplitudes do not interfere with doubly-Cabibbo-suppressed decay amplitudes via
K^{0}
-
\bar{K}^{0}
mixing
to induce SM contributions to direct CP violation
Bigi:1994aw
;
Xing:1995jg
.

In the
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
model, the right-right current operators,
(\bar{s}c)_{V+A}(\bar{u}d)_{V+A}
,
coming from
W_{R}^{+}
gauge boson exchange and the left-right current operators,
(\bar{s}c)_{V\pm A}(\bar{u}d)_{V\mp A}
,
induced by
W_{L}^{+}
-
W_{R}^{+}
mixing both contribute to the Cabibbo-favored decays.
However, the Wilson coefficients for the latter are suppressed by
\sim 2m_{b}/m_{t}\simeq 1/20
compared to the former
if the model naturally accommodates the bottom and top quark Yukawa couplings.
Therefore, we assume throughout this paper that the contribution of the right-right current operators
dominates over that of the left-right ones.
As a support for this assumption, we comment that the dominance of the right-right current contribution
has been observed in the study
Haba:2018byj
of direct CP violation in
K\to\pi\pi
decay in the
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
model,
where we have found that the right-right current contribution is larger by factor 5 than the left-right one,
which indicates that although the hadronic matrix elements of the left-right operators are enhanced,
this is insufficient to overcome the suppression of
2m_{b}/m_{t}\simeq 1/20
on their Wilson coefficients.

The hadronic matrix elements of the right-right current operators are simply the minus of those of the left-left current operators,
due to parity symmetry of QCD.
Nevertheless, the decay amplitude from the right-right current operators and that from the left-left ones
acquire a non-trivial relative strong phase
from difference in QCD corrections to the right-right and left-left current operators,
which manifests itself as a difference between the ratio of the Wilson coefficients for

(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})_{V+A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\beta})_{V+A}

and
(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\beta})_{V+A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\alpha})_{V+A}
operators
and the ratio of those for

(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})_{V-A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\beta})_{V-A}

and
(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\beta})_{V-A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\alpha})_{V-A}
operators
(
\alpha,\beta
denote color indices)
at a given renormalization scale.
Ultimately, this difference is because the quark-gluon-quark-
W_{L}^{+}
(
W_{R}^{+}
) box diagram in the fundamental theory
contains terms proportional to
\log M_{W_{L}}^{2}
(
\log M_{W_{R}}^{2}
),
and hence the amount of QCD corrections to
W_{L}^{+}
and
W_{R}^{+}
gauge boson exchange diagrams
differ by
\sim(\alpha_{s}(M_{W_{L}}^{2})/4\pi)\log(M_{W_{R}}^{2}/M_{W_{L}}^{2})
.
Interestingly, this fact allows us to evaluate the relative strong phase,
since the difference in QCD corrections at scales between
\mu\sim M_{W_{R}}
and
\mu\sim M_{W_{L}}

is a short-distance effect.
Also, the scale-and-scheme-independent combinations
Buras:1998ra
of Wilson coefficients and hadronic matrix elements
for
(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\alpha})_{V-A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\beta})_{V-A}

and
(\bar{s}_{\alpha}c_{\beta})_{V-A}(\bar{u}_{\beta}d_{\alpha})_{V-A}
operators can be estimated reliably
with the diagrammatic approach with
SU(3)
flavor symmetry
Zeppenfeld:1980ex
;
Chau:1982da
;
Gronau:1994bn
;
Hernandez:1994rh
;
Gronau:1994rj
,
which works successfully on the Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays into two pseudoscalars
Bhattacharya:2009ps
;
Cheng:2010ry
.

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{+}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+C_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2}+\frac{C_{-}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}-C_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2},

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{+}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2}-\frac{C_{-}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}-E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2},

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{+}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2}+\frac{C_{-}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}-E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{2}.

Numerical analysis on direct CP violation

In the SM, direct CP violation in the Cabibbo-favored decays is generated via
the interference between the tree diagram
and the box and di-penguin diagrams
Delepine:2012xw
.
CP asymmetry in
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
decay rate is estimated to be
1.4\times 10^{-10}

in Ref.
Delepine:2012xw
.
We infer that direct CP violation is suppressed similarly in all Cabibbo-favored modes,
and therefore neglect the SM contribution in all modes.
Provided the contribution of the right-right current is small,
CP asymmetry in the decay rates can be expanded as

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D\to f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma[D\to f]-\Gamma[\bar{D}\to\bar{f}]}{\Gamma[D\to f]+\Gamma[\bar{D}\to\bar{f}]}\simeq\mathrm{Re}\left[\frac{({\cal A}_{f})_{\mathrm{RR}}}{({\cal A}_{f})_{\mathrm{LL}}}-\frac{(\bar{{\cal A}}_{\bar{f}})_{\mathrm{RR}}}{(\bar{{\cal A}}_{\bar{f}})_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right].

(30)

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D\to f}=F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D\to f}\left[\left(\eta^{\mu_{W}}_{\mu_{W^{\prime}}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{7}}-\left(\eta^{\mu_{W}}_{\mu_{W^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{4}{7}}\right]\left(\frac{g_{R}}{g_{L}}\frac{M_{W}}{M_{W^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{V_{cs}^{\mathrm{R}*}V_{ud}^{\mathrm{R}}}{V_{cs}^{\mathrm{L}*}V_{ud}^{\mathrm{L}}}\right),

6
Summary

We have studied the contribution of the right-right current operators in the

SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
model
to direct CP violation in Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays, for which the SM contribution is virtually absent.
Interestingly, this contribution is evaluable,
because it stems from difference in QCD corrections to the left-left current operators induced by
W_{L}^{+}
boson
and the right-right ones induced by
W_{R}^{+}
boson,
which is a short-distance effect
\sim(\alpha_{s}(M_{W_{L}}^{2})/4\pi)\log(M_{W_{R}}^{2}/M_{W_{L}}^{2})
.
Combining a short-distance calculation of this difference with
the result of the diagrammatic approach to the Cabibbo-favored decay amplitudes,
we numerically evaluate the CP asymmetry in

D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
,
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta
and

D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
decay rates.
We have found that the asymmetry in
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta
is specially sizable,
and further suggested the measurement of the difference in the CP asymmetries in
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta
and

D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
decays.
For
M_{W^{\prime}}
(almost equal to
M_{W_{R}}
) about 4 TeV and
g_{R}=2g_{L}
,
one may hope to observe this CP asymmetry difference at Belle II with 50 ab
-1
of data or at LHCb with 50 fb
-1
of data.
Finally, we have presented a correlated prediction for the CP asymmetry difference in

D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta
and
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
decays,
and direct CP violation in
K\to\pi\pi
decay Re
(\epsilon^{\prime}/\epsilon)
,
under the assumption of ‘charge symmetry’ in the
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
model.
We have observed that if the experimental data on Re
(\epsilon^{\prime}/\epsilon)
are naturally accounted for,
the CP asymmetry difference in
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta
and
D^{+}_{s}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
decays is as small as
10^{-6}
,
and that a fine-tuning of the new CP-violating phases
is mandatory to anticipate the discovery of direct CP violation in Cabibbo-favored charmed meson decays.

Appendix B: Effect of

We study the effect of
SU(3)
flavor symmetry breaking on our prediction,
which is not discussed in the main text.
Our prediction of CP asymmetries
depends crucially on V-spin symmetry
(symmetry of
u
and
s
, which is part of
SU(3)
flavor symmetry),
since our prediction is derived from Eqs. (
17
–
20
),
which are obtained by assuming V-spin.
In particular, the isospin symmetry cannot lead to the above results.
The effect of V-spin breaking on Eqs. (

)

f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1\simeq 0.2
.

where

and also enhances penguin amplitudes (suppressed by

in the

and
c\to s\bar{s}u
-induced amplitudes

;
all these effects are absent in the Cabibbo-favored decays.

Let us see how corrections of order
f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1\simeq 0.2
to Eqs. (
17
–
20
)
affect our prediction of CP asymmetries.
First we concentrate on
T
(tree) and
C
(color-suppressed tree) diagrams.

17
,
18
) are not valid,
T_{\mathrm{LL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{LL}}
are written as

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{LL}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}\frac{\braket{Q_{1}}_{\mathrm{CE}}+\braket{Q_{2}}_{\mathrm{DE}}}{2}+C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}\frac{\braket{Q_{1}}_{\mathrm{CE}}-\braket{Q_{2}}_{\mathrm{DE}}}{2},

(36)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{LL}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}\frac{\braket{Q_{2}}_{\mathrm{CE}}+\braket{Q_{1}}_{\mathrm{DE}}}{2}-C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}\frac{\braket{Q_{2}}_{\mathrm{CE}}-\braket{Q_{1}}_{\mathrm{DE}}}{2}.

(37)

)
are individually renormalization-scale-and-scheme independent.

(39)

where we estimate the V-spin breaking parameters as

|\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}|\sim|\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}|\sim f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1\simeq 0.2
.
In the leading order of
\epsilon_{\rm{E}+},\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}
, we find

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{LL}}+(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-})C_{\mathrm{LL}}=C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}(\braket{Q_{2}}_{\rm{CE}}+\braket{Q_{1}}_{\rm{DE}})(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2),

(40)

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{LL}}-(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+})C_{\mathrm{LL}}=C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}(\braket{Q_{2}}_{\rm{CE}}-\braket{Q_{1}}_{\rm{DE}})(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2).

(41)

Consequently,
T_{\mathrm{RR}}
and
C_{\mathrm{RR}}
can be expressed in terms of
T_{\mathrm{LL}}
,
C_{\mathrm{LL}}
and the V-spin breaking parameters as

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{RR}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{+}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+C_{\mathrm{LL}}(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)}{2}

(42)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(1-\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)-C_{\mathrm{LL}}(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)}{2},

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{RR}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{+}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{+}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(1-\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+C_{\mathrm{LL}}(1-\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)}{2}

(43)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{C_{-}^{\mathrm{RR}}}{C_{-}^{\mathrm{LL}}}\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(1-\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)-C_{\mathrm{LL}}(1+\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)}{2}.

We obtain analogous expressions for
A_{\mathrm{RR}}
and
E_{\mathrm{RR}}
, with
\epsilon_{\rm{E}+},\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}

replaced with different V-spin breaking parameters
\epsilon_{\rm{A}+},\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}
.
The above V-spin breaking corrections solely affect the factor
F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D\to f}
in the formula for CP asymmetry Eq. (
31
).
For the phenomenologically interesting modes
D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta
,
D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}
and
D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}
,
this factor is altered from Table
2
to

\displaystyle F_{\rm CP}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}-2E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}-2A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]+{\rm Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)-2E_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}-2A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]

(44)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\left[\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)-2A_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}-2A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right],

\displaystyle F_{\rm CP}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]+{\rm Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+E_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]

(45)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\left[\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+A_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right],

\displaystyle F_{\rm CP}^{D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}+A_{\mathrm{LL}}}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]+{\rm Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+A_{\mathrm{LL}}(-\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]

(46)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\left[\frac{T_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{E}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{E}-}/2)+E_{\mathrm{LL}}(-\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right].

Depending on the phases of V-spin breaking parameters
\epsilon_{\rm{E}+},\epsilon_{\rm{E}-},\epsilon_{\rm{A}+},\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}
,
the second and third terms of Eqs. (
44
–
46
) can be enhanced far beyond
f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1\simeq 0.2
.
However, as we will show below, the most promising observable,

A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}-A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}
, is not much affected by the V-spin breaking.
To see this, note that this observable is proportional to
F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}-F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}
.
Since
|A_{\mathrm{LL}}|
is small, it can be approximated as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}-F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}-2E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}-2A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]-\rm{Im}\left[\frac{C_{\mathrm{LL}}+E_{\mathrm{LL}}}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}+A_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]

(47)

\displaystyle-3\,{\rm Im}\left[\frac{E_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]-3\,{\rm Im}\left[\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1.54-3\,{\rm Im}\left[\frac{E_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2+\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right]-3\,{\rm Im}\left[\frac{A_{\mathrm{LL}}(\epsilon_{\rm{A}+}/2-\epsilon_{\rm{A}-}/2)}{T_{\mathrm{LL}}}\right],

where the first term
-1.54
is the prediction in the V-spin limit, while the second and third terms represent V-spin breaking effects.
The second term is at most
\pm 3|E_{\mathrm{LL}}/T_{\mathrm{LL}}|(f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1)\simeq\pm 0.32

and the third term is at most
\pm 3|A_{\mathrm{LL}}/T_{\mathrm{LL}}|(f_{K}/f_{\pi}-1)\simeq\pm 0.07
.
Thus, we conclude that the V-spin breaking corrections do not significantly change our prediction of

F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}-F_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}
and hence of

A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta}-A_{\mathrm{CP}}^{D_{s}^{+}\to\pi^{+}\eta^{\prime}}
.

We note in passing that the fitted values of
T_{\mathrm{LL}},C_{\mathrm{LL}},E_{\mathrm{LL}},A_{\mathrm{LL}}
in Ref.
Bhattacharya:2009ps
,
which we have adopted throughout the paper,
are themselves obtained under the assumption of
SU(3)
flavor symmetry,
but we expect that the
SU(3)
breaking effects are properly
reflected in the errors of the fitted values.

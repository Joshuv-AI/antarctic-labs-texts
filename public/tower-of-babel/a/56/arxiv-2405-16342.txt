1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a ring and
\sigma
be an automorphism of
R
. An additive map
D:R\to R
that satisfies
D(ab)=\sigma(a)D(b)+D(a)b
for all
a,b\in R
is called a
\sigma
-derivation of
R
. If for every
a\in R
, there exists an integer
n=n_{a}>0
such that
\sigma^{n}(a)=a
, then
\sigma
is called locally torsion. If for every
a\in R
, there is an integer
m=m_{a}>0
such that
D^{m}(a)=0
, then
D
is called locally nilpotent.

An Ore extension
R[x;\sigma,D]
is a ring of polynomials of form
a_{n}x^{n}+\dots+a_{1}x+a_{0}
with
n\geq 0
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in R
with standard addition and multiplication given by
xa=\sigma(a)x+d(a)
for all
a\in R
. There are two important special cases of Ore extensions. When
\sigma=1
, the Ore extension is called a differential polynomial ring or a skew polynomial ring of derivation type and denoted by
R[x;D]
. When
D=0
, the Ore extension is called a skew polynomial ring of automorphism type and denoted by
R[x;\sigma]
.

Let
J(R)
be the Jacobson radical of
R
. It is well-known that every element
r_{1}
in
J(R)
is quasi-regular which means that there exists an element
r_{2}
in
J(R)
such that

0=r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{1}r_{2}=r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{2}r_{1}
.

One famous open question in ring theory is the Koethe conjecture asking whether every one-sided nil ideal is contained in a two-sided nil ideal
[
9
]
. In 1972, Krempa showed that Koethe conjecture is equivalent to whether a polynomial ring over a nil ring is Jacobson radical
[
10
]
. In 1956, Amitsur proved that the Jacobson radical of a polynomial ring,
J(R[x])
, is equal to
(J(R[x])\cap R)[x]
and
J(R[x])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R

[
1
]
.
In 1983, Ferrero, Kishimoto, and Motose proved that
J(R[x;D])=(J(R[x;D])\cap R)[x;D]

[
5
]
. They showed that if
R
is a commutative ring, then
J(R[x;D])\cap R
is nil
[
5
]
.
In 1980, Bedi and Ram proved that the Jacobson radical of a skew polynomial ring of automorphism type,
J(R[x;\sigma])
, is equal to
K\cap J(R)+K_{0}
where
K=\{r\in R:rx\in J(R[x;\sigma])\}
and
K_{0}=\{\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}x^{i}:r\in K\}

[
2
]
. In particular, they showed that if
\sigma
is locally torsion, then
K
is nil and so
J(R[x;\sigma])=K[x;\sigma]

[
2
]
.
However, Amitsur’s theorem does not hold for general Ore extensions. Bedi and Ram showed a counter-example
[
2
]
. In section 2, we will extend their result to Ore extension and show the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be an algebra over a field of characteristic
0
. Let
\sigma
and
D
be an automorphism and a
q
-skew
\sigma
-derivation on
R
. Then
R[x;\sigma,D]=I\cap J(R)+I_{0}
where
I=\{r\in R:rx\in J(R[x;\sigma,D])\}
and
I_{0}=\{\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}x^{i}:r_{i}\in I\}
.

In 2016, Madill proved that if
R
is a polynomial identity ring, then
J(R[x;D])\cap R
is nil
[
11
]
. In 2017, Smoktunowicz proved that if
R
is an algebra over a field
F
of characteristic
p>0
and
d
is a locally nilpotent derivation on
R
, then
J(R[x;D])\cap R
is a nil ideal of
R

[
15
]
. We may wonder whether such results can be gained on Ore extensions. In 2019, Greenfeld, Smoktunowicz and Ziembowski posed the question below. In section 3, we will give partial answers to the question.

Question 1
.

[
6
, Question 6.17]

Consider
J(R[x;\sigma,D])\cap R
. Is it nil if we assume that
D
is locally nilpotent?
What if we assume that
\sigma
is locally torsion?

Proposition 1

Let

be a

-derivation on

\displaystyle x^{k}r=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}_{q}\sigma^{i}D^{k-i}(r)x^{i}

\displaystyle\binom{k}{j}_{t}=\frac{(t^{k}-1)(t^{k-1}-1)\dots(t^{k-j+1}-1)}{(t^{j}-1)(t^{j-1}-1)\dots(t-1)}.

In particular, for any positive integer

Lemma 1
.

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism on

and
D
be a
q
-skew
\sigma
-derivation on

. Then

\sigma

Proof.

Define a map
\sigma^{*}:R[x;\sigma,D]\rightarrow R[x;\sigma,D]
by
\sigma^{*}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{-i}\sigma(a_{i})x^{i}
for any

. Since
\sigma
is an automorphism and
q
is in a field

,
\sigma^{*}
is well-defined and bijective. Now, we claim that
\sigma^{*}
is an automorphism. Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}
and
g(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{i}x^{i}
for
n\leq m
be polynomials in
R[x;\sigma,D]
.

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}(f(x)+g(x))

\displaystyle=\sigma^{*}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}(a_{i}+b_{i})x^{i}+\sum_{i=n+1}^{m}b_{i}x^{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{-i}\sigma(a_{i}+b_{i})x^{i}+\sum_{i=n+1}^{m}q^{-i}\sigma(b_{i})x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(q^{-i}\sigma(a_{i})+q^{-i}\sigma(b_{i}))x^{i}+\sum_{i=n+1}^{m}q^{-i}\sigma(b_{i})x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}q^{-i}\sigma(a_{i})x^{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^{-i}\sigma(b_{i})x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{*}(f(x))+\sigma^{*}(g(x)).

To show that
\sigma^{*}(f(x)g(x))=\sigma^{*}(f(x))\sigma^{*}(g(x))
, it suffices to show that
\sigma^{*}(ax^{n}bx^{m})=\sigma^{*}(ax^{n})\sigma^{*}(bx^{m})
. Since
D
is
q
-skew, by Proposition
1

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}(ax^{n}bx^{m})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{*}(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}a\sigma^{i}D^{n-i}(b)x^{i+m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}q^{-(i+m)}\sigma(a\sigma^{i}D^{n-i}(b))x^{i+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}q^{-(i+m)}\sigma(a)\sigma^{i+1}D^{n-i}(b)x^{i+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}q^{-(i+m)}q^{-(n-i)}\sigma(a)\sigma^{i}D^{n-i}\sigma(b)x^{i+m}

\displaystyle=q^{-n-m}\sigma(a)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}_{q}\sigma^{i}D^{n-i}\sigma(b)x^{i+m}\right)

\displaystyle=(q^{-n}\sigma(a)x^{n})(q^{-m}\sigma(b)x^{m})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{*}(ax^{n})\sigma^{*}(bx^{m}).

Thus,
\sigma^{*}
is an automorphism on
R[x;\sigma,D]
.
We know that Jacobson radicals are closed under isomorphisms. That means that
\sigma^{*}(J(R[x;\sigma,D]))\subseteq J(R[x;\sigma,D])
.
Then for any

,
we have that
\sigma(a)=\sigma^{*}(a)\in J(R[x;\sigma,D])\cap R
.

∎

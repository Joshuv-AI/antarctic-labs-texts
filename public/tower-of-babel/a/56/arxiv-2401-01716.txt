On the connected (sub)partition polytope
Phablo F. S. Moura, Roel Leus, and Hande Yaman

Abstract
Let k k be a positive integer and let G G be a graph with n n vertices. A connected k k -subpartition of G G is a collection of k k pairwise disjoint sets (a.k.a. classes) of vertices in G G such that each set induces a connected subgraph. The connected k k -partition polytope of G G , denoted by 𝒫 ⁡ ( G , k ) \mathcal{P}(G,k) , is defined as the convex hull of the incidence vectors of all connected k k -subpartitions of G G . Many applications arising in off-shore oil-drilling, forest planning, image processing, cluster analysis, political districting, police patrolling, and biology are modeled in terms of finding connected (sub)partitions of a graph. This study focus on the facial structure of 𝒫 ⁡ ( G , k ) \mathcal{P}(G,k) and the computational complexity of the corresponding separation problems. We first propose a set of valid inequalities having non-null coefficients associated with a single class that extends and generalizes the ones in the literature of related problems, show sufficient conditions for these inequalities to be facet-defining, and design a polynomial-time separation algorithm for them. We also devise two sets of inequalities that consider multiple classes, prove when they define facets, and study the computational complexity of associated separation problems.

Introduction

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices.
We denote by
V(G)
and

the sets of vertices and edges of
G
, respectively. For a positive integer
\ell
, we let
[\ell]=\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.
A
connected
k
-subpartition

\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
of
G
is a collection of
k
pairwise disjoint sets of vertices in
V(G)
such that each set induces a connected subgraph, that is,
V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i,j\in[k]
with
i\neq j
and the subgraph of
G
induced by
V_{i}
, denoted by

.
A collection

is a

-partition
if additionally
\bigcup_{i\in[k]}V_{i}=V(G)
.

Given a set of vertices
V^{\prime}\subseteq V(G)
and
c\in[k]
, we define the vector
e(V^{\prime},c)\in\{0,1\}^{kn}
such that its non-null entries are precisely
e(V^{\prime},c)_{v,c}=1
for all
v\in V^{\prime}
.
For the sake of simplicity, we write
e(H,c)
rather than
e(V(H),c)
when
H
is a graph.
Let
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
be a
k
-subpartition of
G
.
The non-null entries of its
incidence vector

\chi(\mathcal{V})
are precisely
\chi(\mathcal{V})_{v,c}=1
for every
c\in[k]
,
v\in V_{c}
, that is,
\chi(\mathcal{V})=\sum_{c\in[k]}e(V_{c},c)
.
The connected
k
-partition polytope of
G
, denoted by
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
, is defined as

\mathcal{P}(G,k)=\conv\{\chi(\mathcal{V})\in\{0,1\}^{nk}\colon\mathcal{V}\text{ is a connected $k$-subpartition of $G$}\}.

Many combinatorial problems deal with finding (sub)partitions of the set of vertices of a graph into classes that induce connected subgraphs, or equivalently integer points in the polytope
\mathcal{P}
.
A well-studied example is the
Maximum Weight Connected Subgraph
problem, which receives as input a vertex-weighted graph
G
(weights may be negative), and consists in finding a connected subgraph of
G
that maximizes the sum of the weights of its vertices (see Álvarez-Miranda,
Ljubić, and Mutzel
[
2
]
, and Hochbaum and Pathria
[
15
]
).

Convex Recoloring
is another problem involving partitioning graphs into connected subgraphs that has received considerable attention in the last years (see Campêlo et al.
[
6
]
, and Chopra et al.
[
10
]
).
This problem was introduced by Moran and Snir
[
22
]
, and consists in finding a (re)coloring of a given colored graph such that each color class in the recoloring induces a connected subgraph, and the amount of recolored vertices is minimized.
Note that the concept of coloring here differs from the classical definition of (proper) coloring, where pairs of vertices linked by an edge are assigned distinct colors.

In the
Balanced Connected Partition
problem, the goal is to partition a graph into a given number of connected subgraphs so that the size of every subgraph is roughly the same.
This concept of balance can be modelled, for example, as minimizing (resp. maximizing) the size of the largest (resp. smallest) subgraph in the partition, or minimizing the maximum difference of sizes between two subgraphs.
These objectives yield equivalent problems when one has to partition into two classes, but lead to distinct problems when the desired partition has more than two subgraphs (see Lucertini, Perl, and
Simeone
[
19
]
).
A related problem investigated by Lovász
[
18
]
and Györi
[
14
]
consists in partitioning a connected graph into a given number of connected subgraphs with prescribed orders.

Another example of connected partition problem is
Connected Maximum
k
-Cut
, a variant of the classical
Maximum
k
-Cut
in which the goal is to find a partition of the vertices into
k
classes inducing connected subgraphs such that the number of edges linking vertices in distinct classes is maximized.
Recently, Hojny et al.
[
16
]
proposed mixed-integer linear programming formulations for an edge-weighted version of this problem, and reported on extensive computational experiments.

The idea of dividing graphs into connected parts (classes) plays an essential role in many applications arising in off-shore oil-drilling, forest planning, image processing, cluster analysis, political districting, police patrolling, and biology.
For more details on these applications, we refer to
[
3
,
8
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

In this work, we study the facial structure of the connected

-partition polytope, which is naturally modelled as follows.
Given any pair of non-adjacent vertices

Let us denote by

Consider now the following inequalities on variables

.

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in[k]}x_{v,c}\leq 1

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\forall v\in V,

(1)

\displaystyle x_{u,c}+x_{v,c}-\sum_{z\in Z}x_{z,c}\leq 1

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\forall\{u,v\}\notin E(G),Z\in\Gamma(u,v),c\in[k],

(2)

\displaystyle x_{v,c}\geq 0

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\forall v\in V\text{ and }c\in[k].

(3)

Let us denote by

satisfying (

Observe that if two non-adjacent vertices

It follows from this remark that

.

The facial structure of the polytope

was studied before by Campêlo et al.
[
7
]
in the context of a polyhedral approach to

Chopra et al.
[

]
provided a strong extended formulation for the problem on trees, and reported on computational experiments.
We remark that

is

-hard even on paths, as shown by Moran and Snir
[

]
.
Indeed, Moura and Wakabayashi
[
23
]
proved that this problem on n-vertex bipartite graphs cannot be approximated within a factor of

for any

, unless

.

Wang et al.
[
26
]
presented a detailed polyhedral study for the case

, which is the so-called

.
More precisely, assuming that

is connected, they showed that (
2
) induces a facet of

if and only if

is minimal.
They also proved that the

[

]
),
are valid for general graphs and can be separated in linear time.
The indegree inequalities can be generalized to the case of positive

in a trivial way as follows.
Consider an orientation
\vec{E}
of the edges in
G
, and define, for each
v\in V(G)
, the

d(v)
as the number of arcs in

with head

.
The indegree inequality corresponding to

is

\sum_{v\in V(G)}(1-d(v))x_{v,c}\leq 1.

(4)

The validity of this inequality follows from the fact that
\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}d(v)\geq|E(G^{\prime})|
, and
|E(G^{\prime})|\geq|V(G^{\prime})|-1
for any connected subgraph

of

.
Unlike for the case of

, for

the indegree inequalities and nonnegativity inequalities (

) are not sufficient to describe
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
when

is a tree as

is

-hard on paths.
This simple observation suggests that the facial structure of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
with

is significantly more involved compared to the one of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
. This work is devoted to study the facial structure of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
, and the computational complexity of the separation problems associated with the families of valid inequalities.

Before concluding this section, we review the results on the dimension of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
and the trivial inequalities of the formulation previously described.

Theorem 1

(Campêlo et al.
[
7
,
5
]
)
.

The following hold for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
:

1.

The polytope is full-dimensional, i.e.,

.

2.

For every

, inequality (

) is facet-defining if

, or if

3.

For every

and

One may notice that if

and

is not connected, then there exist vertices

belonging to different components of

, and so inequalities (

This explains why one has to suppose that

is connected in Theorem

2
.
The characterization of the facet-defining inequalities (

) shown by Wang et al.
[

]
also assumes that

Contributions

We introduce, in Section
2
, a class of inequalities for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
that generalizes both the connectivity inequalities (
2
), and the indegree inequalities (
4
).
The proposed class contains all nontrivial inequalities studied by Wang et al.
[
26
]
for the connected subgraph polytope (i.e.
k=1
), and by Chopra et al.
[
10
]
and Campêlo et al.
[
5
]
for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
with
k\geq 1
.
Furthermore, it contains new strong valid inequalities.
We also prove sufficient (weak) conditions under which the proposed generalized inequalities are facet-defining for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.
If the input graph
G
is a tree, we show that all facets of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
having non-null coefficients associated with a single class in
[k]
are induced by indegree inequalities (
4
).

The separation problem associated with these inequalities is investigated in Section
3
.
More precisely, we design a polynomial-time algorithm that reduces the input instance to a quadratic number of bipartite instances of the
Minimum Weight Vertex Cover
problem, when the partition defining the inequality is fixed.
In general, this algorithm runs in
\mathcal{O}(k|V(G)||E(G)|)
time.
When separating inequalities (
2
) and (
4
), this algorithm achieves the same running times as the corresponding separation routines for these inequalities described in the literature.
The proposed routine runs in
\mathcal{O}(k(|V(G)|+|E(G)|))
time when separating a set of inequalities that strictly contains the indegree inequalities (
4
).

In Section
4
, we propose two sets of valid inequalities, namely multiway and pairing inequalities, that combine multiple classes.
We characterize the facets induced by multiway inequalities, and present sufficient conditions for the pairing inequalities to be facet-defining.
We show in Section
5
that the separation problem associated with multiway inequalities is
NP
-hard (if the stable set which defines it is given) as this separation boils down to solving the
Multiway Cut
problem.
Moreover, we show that the separation problem of the pairing inequalities can solved in polynomial time if the graph is a tree.
The proposed algorithm computes a minimum-cost flow in a network which is constructed with the vertices of the input graph and additional vertices corresponding to the classes.

Concluding remarks and possible directions for further research are discussed in Section
6
.

2
Single-class inequalities

In this section, we consider valid (non-trivial) inequalities for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
where all non-null coefficients are associated with a same class
c\in[k]
.
Let us denote by
(\pi,\pi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{R}
the inequality
\pi x\leq\pi_{0}
where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
An inequality
(\pi,\pi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{nk}\times\mathbb{R}
is said to be a
single class
inequality for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
if
(\pi,\pi_{0})
is a valid inequality for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
, and there exists
c\in[k]
such that
\pi_{v,b}=0
for all
v\in V(G)
and
b\in[k]\setminus\{c\}
.
We say that an inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
is nontrivial if it is different from
(0,0)
and from the nonnegativity constraints (
3
).

First we show that every facet-defining inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
can be naturally lifted to a single-class inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
that is also facet defining.
In particular, we shall see that the connectivity inequalities (
2
) and the indegree inequalities (
4
) induce facets of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
under the same hypothesis shown by Wang et al.
[
26
]
for
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(\pi,\pi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
be a nontrivial valid inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)

different from
x_{v}\leq 1
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Let
k\geq 2
be an integer, let
c\in[k]
, and let
(\pi^{c},\pi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{nk}\times\mathbb{R}
be a valid inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
, where the (possibly) non-null entries of
\pi^{c}
are precisely
\pi^{c}_{v,c}=\pi_{v}
for all
v\in V(G)
.
It holds that
(\pi,\pi_{0})
is facet defining for
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
if and only if
(\pi^{c},\pi_{0})
is facet defining for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
(\pi,\pi_{0})
defines a facet
F
of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
.
Let
\tilde{F}
be the face of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
induced by
(\pi^{c},\pi_{0})
, that is,
\tilde{F}=\{x\in\mathcal{P}(G,k):\pi^{c}x=\pi_{0}\}
.
By Theorem
1
, we have
\dim(F)=n-1
, and so
F
contains a set
X=\{x^{j}\}_{j\in[n]}
of affinely independent vectors such that
x^{j}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
for all
j\in[n]
.
Observe now that, for each
v\in V(G)
, there is a vector
r^{v}\in X
such that
r^{v}_{v}=0
, otherwise
F\subset\{x\in\mathcal{P}(G,1):x_{v}=1\}
, a contradiction to the assumption that
F
is a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
.

In what follows, we show how to lift the vectors in
F
to the space
\{0,1\}^{nk}
.
For each
j\in[n]
, we define a vector
\tilde{x}^{j}\in\{0,1\}^{nk}
such that, for every
v\in V(G)
,
\tilde{x}^{j}_{v,c}=x^{j}_{v}
, and
\tilde{x}^{j}_{v,b}=0
for all
b\in[k]\setminus\{c\}
.
Similarly, for each
v\in V(G)
, let
\tilde{r}^{v}=\tilde{x}^{j}
where
r^{v}=x^{j}
for some
j\in[n]
.
Note that every vector described above corresponds to a connected
k
-subpartition of
G
where class
c
is the single non-empty class, and that it belongs to
\tilde{F}
.

Let us define
R=\{\tilde{r}^{v}+e(v,b):v\in V(G)\text{ and }b\in[k]\setminus\{c\}\}
.
By the definition of
r^{v}
,
v
is not assigned to any class in the corresponding connected
k
-subpartition, and thus
r^{v}+e(v,b)
belongs to
\tilde{F}
for all
b\in[k]\setminus\{c\}
.
Let
\tilde{X}=R\cup\{\tilde{x}^{j}\}_{j\in[n]}
, and note that
|\tilde{X}|=n(k-1)+n=nk
.
One may easily verify that
\tilde{X}\subseteq\tilde{F}
.
Since
X
is an affinely independent set and by the construction of the vectors, it holds that
\tilde{X}
is also affinely independent.
As a consequence,
\dim(\tilde{F})=nk-1
and so
\tilde{F}
is a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.

Suppose now that
(\pi^{c},\pi_{0})
induces a facet
\tilde{F}
of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.
Thus, for every
v\in V(G)
,
\tilde{F}\neq\{x\in\mathcal{P}(G,k):x_{v,c}=1\}
since the valid inequality
x_{v,c}\leq 1
is dominated by (
1
) if
k\geq 2
.
Again by Theorem
1
, there are
nk
affinely independent vectors in
\tilde{F}
.
Let us denote by
M
be the matrix
nk\times nk
formed by such vectors.
Consider the submatrix of
M^{\prime}
of
M
formed by the
n
rows indexed by
c
, and observe that each column of
M^{\prime}
belongs to
F
.
As
M
contains
nk
affinely independent rows, the affine rank of
M^{\prime}
is
n
.
Hence there exist
n
affinely independent vectors in
F
.
Therefore,
\dim(F)=n-1
and so
F
is a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
.
∎

We next use Lemma
2
to characterize inequalities (
2
) and (

) that are facet-defining for

.

Corollary 3
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.
The following hold.

1.

Inequality (
2
) is facet defining for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
if and only if the separator is minimal.

2.

Inequality (
4
) is facet defining for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
if and only if

(a)

for every
u,v\in V(G)
, there exists at most one directed
u,v
-walk in the corresponding orientation of
G
; and

(b)

the orientation of
G
contains at least two vertices of indegree zero.

Proof.

Wang et al.
[
26
]
proved that (
2
), for non-adjacent vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, and
(u,v)
-separator
Z
, defines a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
if and only if
Z
is minimal. As
G
is connected, this inequality is different from
x_{v}\leq 1
for all
v\in V(G)
. Hence
1
holds using Lemma
2
.

To prove
2
, suppose first that (
4
) induces a facet
F
of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
, and let
D
denote the orientation of
G
that defines such inequality.
By Lemma
2
, the corresponding facet
F^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
is different from
\{x\in\mathcal{P}(G,1):x_{v}=1\}
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Wang et al.
[
26
]
proved that
F^{\prime}
is a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
if and only if (a) holds.
Due to (a) and
F^{\prime}\neq\{x\in\mathcal{P}(G,1):x_{v}=1\}
for all
v\in V(G)
,
D
contains at least two vertices of indegree zero.

To show the converse, consider an orientation
D
of
G
satisfying (a) and (b).
Let
F^{\prime}
be the face of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
induced by the inequality (
4
) which is associated with

.
Since

has at least two vertices with indegree zero, the inequality inducing

contains at least two coefficients equal to one, and so it is different from
x_{v}\leq 1
for all

.
Since (a) holds,

is a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
by Wang et al.
Therefore, the claimed result follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

k

Theorem 4

(Korte, Lovász, and Schrader
[

]
(see
[

]
))

Theorem 5
.

Let
G

k\geq 2
be an integer, and let
(\pi,\pi_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{nk}\times\mathbb{R}
be a nontrivial single-class inequality of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.
Then,
(\pi,\pi_{0})
induces a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
if and only if
(\pi,\pi_{0})
is equal to (
4
) defined by an orientation of

Proof.

Let
F
be the facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
induced by
(\pi,\pi_{0})
.
Applying Lemma
2

, yields a facet

of
\mathcal{P}(G,1)
that is different from
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{v}=1\}
for all

.
By Theorem

,

is induced by an indegree inequality (
4
), and so

is equal to (
4
) for some

.
Additionally, the orientation of

defining such inequality contains at least two vertices of degree zero since

for all

Consider an orientation of

This orientation defines an inequality (

2.1
Generalized connectivity inequalities

Before showing a class of inequalities that generalizes both (
2
) and (
4

Let
\ell\in[n]
.
Given a set
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|=\ell
, and an
\ell
-partition
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{\ell}\}
of
V(G)

\mathcal{W}

agrees
with
S
if each vertex of
S
belongs to exactly one class in
\mathcal{W}
.
We denote by

the set of all edges of
G
with endpoints in different classes of
\mathcal{W}

E(\mathcal{W})=\{\{u,v\}\in E(G)\colon u\in W_{i},v\in W_{j}\text{ for some }i,j\in[\ell]\text{ with }i\neq j\}

Let

be the set of arcs obtained in an arbitrary (but fixed) orientation of the edges in

\vec{E}(\mathcal{W})=\{(u,v)\in V(G)\times V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(\mathcal{W})\}

We now define a function

such that
\hat{d}(v)=|\{j\in[\ell]:(u,v)\in\vec{E}(\mathcal{W})\text{ and }u\in W_{j}\}|
.
Intuitively,
\hat{d}(v)
corresponds to the number of different classes of
\mathcal{W}
that contain a vertex that is the tail of some arc with head
v
.
Clearly, every vertex
v
that is not an endpoint of an edge in
E(\mathcal{W})
satisfies

\hat{d}(v)=0
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the design of an algorithm to solve the separation problem associated with inequalities (
5
) in polynomial time for a given (fixed) partition of
V(G)
.
Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{i}\}_{i\in[\ell]}
be a partition of
V(G)
.
Given a vector
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{nk}_{\geq}
and
c\in[k]
, the separation problem associated with (
5
) (for the partition
\mathcal{W}
) reduces to finding an orientation of the edges in
E(\mathcal{W})
that minimizes
\sum_{v\in V(G)}\hat{d}(v)x^{*}_{v,c}
as the left-hand side of (
5
) is equal to

\sum_{v\in S}x_{v,c}-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\hat{d}(v)x_{v,c}

and the choice of
S
is trivial.
We next show how to reduce this problem to
\mathcal{O}(n^{2})
bipartite instances of the
Minimum Weighted Vertex Cover
problem (VC).
Given a graph
H
and a weight function
w\colon V(H)\to\mathbb{Q}_{\geq}
, VC consists in finding a set
C\subseteq V(H)
such that
\{u,v\}\cap C\neq\emptyset
for every edge
\{u,v\}\in E(H)
, and
\val(C):=\sum_{v\in C}w(v)
is minimum.
This problem can be solved in polynomial time on bipartite graphs by computing maximum flows (see Chlebík and Chlebíková
[
9
]
).

The core of the separation procedure is described in Algorithm
1
, which uses a subroutine called
MinW-VC-Bipartite
for computing (in polynomial time) a minimum-weight vertex cover of a bipartite graph.

Theorem 10

Let

Firstly consider any orientation

The separation problem of inequalities (

4
Multiclass inequalities

We next derive strong valid inequalities for
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
that combine different classes.
In contrast to the single-class inequalities, the multiclass inequalities may have non-null coefficients associated with any subset of classes
C\subseteq[k]
.
As we shall see, the proposed inequalities generalize (
2
) in different ways.
The ones in Section
4.1
are based on stable sets in
G
instead of pairs of non-adjacent vertices (i.e., stable sets of size two) in
G
.
The inequalities in Section
4.2
are induced by a collection of pairs of non-adjacent vertices.

4.1
Multiway inequalities

First we present the definition of multiway cuts, which generalizes the concept of separators.
Given a stable set
S\subset V(G)
, a
multiway cut
of
S
in
G

Z\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
such that there is no path in
G-Z
between any pair of distinct vertices in
S
, that is, each component of
G-Z
contains at most one vertex in
S
.
Let us denote by
\mathcal{K}
the set of components of
G-Z

Proposition 12
.

Let

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{c\in C}x_{v,c}-\sum_{z\in Z}\sum_{c\in C}\beta x_{z,c}\leq|C|.

(9)

Proof.

We assume that
|S|>|C|
, otherwise the inequality holds trivially.
Consider a connected
k

of
G

.
If

) is satisfied by

.
Fix now

Since

We next introduce inequalities that also generalize (

must be type-3 which implies
V_{c}\supseteq\{u_{c},v_{c},z\}
and
\gamma_{\hat{z},c}=0
for all
\hat{z}\in(Z\cap V_{c})\setminus\{z\}
.
Thus we can remove all vertices of
V_{c}
but one of the delegates, say
u_{c}
, and still have a conforming connected
k
-subpartition.
Consequently, it holds that

\sum_{i\in[k]}e(V_{i},i)
and
\sum_{i\in[k]\setminus\{c\}}e(V_{i},i)+e(u_{c},c)
belong to
\hat{F}
.
It follows that
-\lambda_{z,c}=\lambda_{v_{c},c}=\lambda_{u_{c},c}
for all
c\in C
, and
z\in Z
with
\gamma_{z,c}=1
.

Consider now
b,c\in C
such that
c
blocks
b
.
By
2
, there is a conforming connected
k
-subpartition
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}_{i\in[k]}
such that
V_{c}\supseteq\{u_{b},v_{b},u_{c},v_{c}\}
.
Since
u_{c}\neq v_{c}
and
u_{b}\neq v_{b}
, we assume (w.l.o.g.) that
u_{b}\neq v_{c}
, and that
V_{b}=\emptyset
.
Note that one may remove the class
V_{c}
from
\mathcal{V}
, add classes
\{u_{b}\}
and
\{v_{c}\}
, and obtain a conforming connected
k
-subpartition.
As a consequence, the vectors
\sum_{i\in[k]}e(V_{i},i)
and
\sum_{i\in[k]\setminus\{c\}}e(V_{i},i)+e(u_{b},b)+e(v_{c},c)
are in
\hat{F}
, and so
\lambda_{u_{b},b}=\lambda_{v_{c},c}
.
By
3
, the class-blocking graph is connected, and so we have
\lambda_{u_{b},b}=\lambda_{v_{b},b}=\lambda_{u_{c},c}=\lambda_{v_{c},c}
for every
b,c\in C
.
Therefore, there is
\lambda_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lambda=\lambda_{*}\hat{\lambda}
, and thus inequality (
10
) induces a facet of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
.
∎

5
Separation of multiclass inequalities

We first show that the separation problem associated with inequalities (
9
) (if
S
is given) is
NP
-hard
by observing that it consists in solving the
Minimum Multiway Cut
problem (undirected node version) which is defined as follows.

Given a graph
G
, a set of terminals
S\subseteq V(G)
inducing a stable set in
G
, and weights
w\colon V(G)\setminus S\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq}
, the problem consists in finding a minimum-weight set of non-terminal vertices
Z\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
such that there is no path in
G-Z
between any pair of distinct vertices in
S
, that is, each component of
G-Z
contains at most one vertex in
S
.
Note that when
|S|=2
, this is exactly the
Minimum Vertex Cut
problem, and so it is solvable in polynomial time using a maximum flow algorithm.
For
|S|\geq 3
, Garg et al.
[
13
]
proved that
Multiway Cut
is
NP
-hard.

Let us fix
k=1
.
Given a graph
G
, a stable set
S\subseteq V(G)
of
G
with
|S|\geq 3
, and a vector
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{n}
, the separation problem of inequalities (
9
) on input
(G,S,\bar{x})
is equivalent to finding a minimum-weight set of vertices
Z^{*}\subseteq V(G)\setminus S
such that each vertex of
S
belongs to a different component of
G-Z^{*}
, where the weight of each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus S
is
\bar{x}_{v}
.
In other words, solving the separation of (
9
) is not easier than solving
Multiway Cut
.
Therefore the separation problem of inequalities (
9
) is
NP
-hard even for
k=1
.

We next describe a simple heuristic to find violated inequalities (
9
) using separating sets.
A set of vertices
Z\subset V(G)
is a
separating set
(or
vertex cut
) of a connected graph
G
if
G-Z
has more than one component.
Given
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
,
C\subseteq[k]
, a connected graph
G
, and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq}^{nk}
,
the weight of each vertex
v\in V(G)
is defined as
f(v)=\sum_{c\in C}\bar{x}^{*}_{v,c}
.
A natural heuristic approach to find inequalities (
9
) violated by
\bar{x}
is to first compute a minimum separating set
Z
of
G
with weight function
f
, and then choose a vertex of maximum weight in each component of
G-Z
to be part of
S
.
The problem of finding a minimum separating set can be solved in polynomial time by reducing it to the
Maximum Stable Set
problem on weighted bipartite graphs as shown by Balas and Souza
[
4
]
.
Regarding the subset
C\subseteq[k]
, it is not clear to us what is the best choice for this set of classes.
A simple strategy could be to consider the subsets of
[k]
in nondecreasing order of cardinality.
Of course, this enumeration approach is viable only when
k
is small.

In the remainder of this section, we focus on the following separation problem associated with inequalities (
10
).
Given
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
,
C\subseteq[k]
with
|C|\geq 1
, a graph
G
, a collection of pairs of vertices
\{\{u_{c},v_{c}\}\}_{c\in C}
with
u_{c}\neq v_{c}
such that
\{u_{c},v_{c}\}\notin E(G)
, and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nk}_{\geq}
, the separation problem of (
10
) consists in finding a multicut
Z
of the pairs
\{\{u_{c},v_{c}\}\}_{c\in C}
in
G
that minimizes

\sum_{v\in Z}\sum_{c\in C}\gamma_{v,c}\>\bar{x}_{v,c}.

At first glance, one might think that this separation problem implies solving the
Minimum Multicut
problem defined as follows.
Given a graph
G
and a collection
Q
of pairs of distinct non-adjacent vertices in
G
, the goal is to find a minimum-size
multicut

Z
of
Q
in
G
, that is, a set of vertices
Z\subseteq V(G)\setminus(\bigcup_{q\in Q}q)
such that, for each pair
\{u,v\}\in Q
, there is no path in
G-Z
between
u
and
v
.
Călinescu, Fernandes, and Reed
[
11
]
proved that
Minimum Multicut
is
NP
-hard on trees with maximum degree at most
4
.
However, we next prove that the separation problem of (
10
) is polynomial-time solvable on trees by reducing it to
Minimum-Cost Flow
.

Theorem 18
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
, let
C\subseteq[k]
with
|C|\geq 1
, let
G
be a tree, and let
Q=\{\{u_{c},v_{c}\}\}_{c\in C}
be a collection of pairs of vertices with
u_{c}\neq v_{c}
such that
\{u_{c},v_{c}\}\notin E(G)
.
The separation problem of (
10
) can be solved in polynomial time.

Proof.

For each
c\in C
, let us denote by
P_{c}
the path in
G
between
u_{c}
and
v_{c}
.
Note that if there exist distinct classes
b,c\in C
such that
V(P_{c})\subseteq V(P_{b})
, then we can simply remove the pair
\{u_{b},v_{b}\}
from
Q
since every separator
Z
of
u_{c}
and
v_{c}
is also a separator of
u_{b}
and
v_{b}
, and
\gamma_{v,b}=0
for all
v\in V(G)
as
b
blocks
c
.
From now on, assume that
V(P_{c})\not\subseteq V(P_{b})
for all
b,c\in C
with
b\neq c
.
Thus, for each
c\in C
and
u_{c},v_{c}
-separator
Z
, there is a single vertex
v\in(V(P_{c})\setminus\{u_{c},v_{c}\})\cap Z
such that
\gamma_{v,c}=1
.

Let us define
V^{\prime}=\bigcup_{q\in Q}q
. We next construct a network consisting of a directed graph
D
with vertices
V(D)=(V(G)\setminus V^{\prime})\cup C\cup\{s,t\}
and arcs

A(D)=\{(s,v):v\in V(G)\setminus V^{\prime}\}\>\cup\{(c,t):c\in C\}\cup A^{\prime},

where
A^{\prime}=\{(v,c)\in(V(G)\setminus V^{\prime})\times C:v\in V(P_{c})\}.

We define the demands in the network as
d\colon V(D)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
such that
d(s)=-|C|
,
d(t)=|C|
, and
d(v)=0
for all
v\in V(D)\setminus\{s,t\}
.
Moreover, the capacity function
r\colon A(D)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
is defined as

r(a)=\begin{cases}|C|&\text{if }a=(s,v)\text{ with }v\in V(G)\setminus V^{\prime},\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Finally, the cost of the arcs in the network is given by a function
w\colon A(D)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
such that
w(a)=\bar{x}_{v,c}
if
a=(v,c)\in A^{\prime}
, and
w(a)=0
otherwise.

Since
d
and
r
are integer-valued functions, a minimum-cost flow
f^{*}
in the network
(D,d,r,w)
such that
f^{*}(a)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
a\in A(D)
can be computed in polynomial time (see Ahuja, Magnanti, and
Orlin
[
1
]
).
It follows from the definition of
r
that the flow of every arc in
A^{\prime}
given by
f^{*}
is either zero or one.
Also the capacities guarantee that at most one unit of flow enters any vertex in
C
.
We define
Z=\{v\in V(G)\setminus V^{\prime}:f^{*}(v,c)=1\text{ for some }c\in C\}
, and observe that
Z
is a
u_{c},v_{c}
-separator in
G
for every
c\in C
as the demand
d(t)=-d(s)=|C|
.
Moreover, for all
v\in Z
and
c\in C
, we define
\gamma_{v,c}=f^{*}(v,c)
.
The cost of
f^{*}
is precisely

\sum_{a\in A(D)}w(a)f^{*}(a)=\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}w(a)f^{*}(a)=\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus V^{\prime}}\sum_{c\in C}\bar{x}_{v,c}f^{*}(v,c)=\sum_{v\in Z}\sum_{c\in C}\gamma_{v,c}\bar{x}_{v,c}.

On the other hand, given a set
Z\subseteq V(G)\setminus V^{\prime}
which is a
u_{c},v_{c}
-separator for every
c\in C
, it induces a (feasible) flow
f
in
(D,d,r,w)
where
f(v,c)=\gamma_{v,c}
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus V^{\prime}
and
c\in C
, where
\gamma_{v,c}
is defined with respect to
Z
.
Therefore, the separation problem of (
10
) can be solved in polynomial time when the input graph
G
is a tree.
∎

We conclude this section observing that the algorithm described in the proof of Theorem
18
does not seem to be adaptable to more general graphs as the size of the produced network mainly depends on the number of distinct paths between the vertices belonging to pairs in
Q
.
Indeed, we do not know the computational complexity of the separation problem of (
10
) in general.

6
Further research

The ILP formulation of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
given by (
1
), (
2
), and
x\in\{0,1\}^{nk}
is natural and it can be adapted to model additional specific constraints of many problems involving connected partitions.
Additionally, one may extend it to model connected
k
-subpartitions where a class
c\in[k]
has to be
\kappa_{c}
-connected (with
\kappa_{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}
) by simply imposing

\displaystyle\kappa_{c}(x_{u,c}+x_{v,c}-1)\leq\sum_{z\in Z}x_{z,c}

\displaystyle\quad\forall\{u,v\}\notin E(G),Z\in\Gamma(u,v).

One may also have different costs/gains when assigning a vertex to a class as in the case of the
Convex Recoloring
problem.
In general, one may easily ensure different properties to the classes in the (sub)partition using variables indexed by the vertices and classes.
If
k
is large and the classes in the subpartition have identical properties, the studied formulation suffers from symmetry in the sense that any permutation of the classes of a solution lead to an equivalent solution.
To avoid such symmetries, one could alternatively model
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
using binary variables
\tilde{x}_{uv}\in\{0,1\}
for every
u,v\in V(G)
to identify whether
u
and
v
belong to the same class in a connected
k
-subpartition.
A future research direction is to find valid inequalities to strengthen such a formulation and investigate the properties of instances for which one formulation outperforms the other.

Another natural direction for further research is to investigate new classes of valid inequalities since there exist facets of
\mathcal{P}(G,k)
different from the ones we studied.
Using PORTA
[
25
]
, we found that the following inequalities are facet-defining for the graph depicted in Figure
7
with classes
a
and
b
:

\displaystyle x_{v_{1},a}+x_{v_{2},a}+x_{v_{3},a}-x_{v_{4},a}+2x_{v_{1},b}+2x_{v_{2},b}+x_{v_{3},b}-2x_{v_{4},b}-2x_{v_{5},b}

\displaystyle\leq 3,

\displaystyle 2x_{v_{1},a}+2x_{v_{2},a}+x_{v_{3},a}-2x_{v_{4},a}-2x_{v_{5},a}+x_{v_{1},b}+x_{v_{2},b}+x_{v_{3},b}-x_{v_{4},b}

\displaystyle\leq 3.

Finally, to implement more efficient solving methods, it would be necessary to better understand the computational complexity of the separation problems associated with the proposed inequalities, and devise good heuristics to tackle such problems.

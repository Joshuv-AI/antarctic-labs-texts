Bakir FARHI

Abstract

1
Introduction and Notation

Throughout this note, we let
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
denote the set of positive integers. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we let
\lfloor x\rfloor
denote the integer part of
x
. For a given prime number
p
and a given positive integer
n
, we let
\vartheta_{p}(n)
and
s_{p}(n)
respectively denote the usual
p
-adic valuation of
n
and the sum of base-
p
digits of
n
. A well-known formula of Legendre (see e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 2.6.4, page 77]
) states that for any prime number
p
and any positive integer
n
, we have

\vartheta_{p}(n!)=\frac{n-s_{p}(n)}{p-1}.

(1.1)

Next, we let
s(n,k)
and
S(n,k)
(with
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq k
) respectively denote the Stirling numbers of the first and second kinds, which can be defined as the integer coefficients appearing in the polynomial identities:

\begin{split}X(X-1)\cdots(X-n+1)&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}s(n,k)X^{k},\\
X^{n}&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)\end{split}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

This immediately implies the orthogonality relations:

\sum_{k\leq i\leq n}s(n,i)S(i,k)=\sum_{k\leq i\leq n}S(n,i)s(i,k)=\delta_{nk}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n,k\in{\mathbb{N}},n\geq k),

(1.2)

where
\delta_{nk}
is the Kronecker delta. Among the many formulas related to the Stirling numbers, we mention the following (see e.g.,
[
1
, §1.14, page 51]
):

\frac{\log^{k}(1+x)}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}s(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}),

(1.3)

which is needed later on. We let finally
B_{n}
and
G_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) respectively denote the Bernoulli and the Genocchi numbers which can be defined by their respective exponential generating functions:

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}B_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}~~~~\text{and}~~~~\frac{2x}{e^{x}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}G_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

(1.4)

The famous Genocchi theorem
[
4
]
states that the
G_{n}
’s are all integers. There are at least two beautiful proofs of the Genocchi theorem: the first one uses the formula
G_{n}=2(1-2^{n})B_{n}
(see e.g.,
[
1
]
) together with the Fermat little theorem and the von Staudt-Clausen theorem, while the second one uses the remarkable Seidel formula
[
9
]
:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}G_{n+k}=0~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

In this note, we give a new proof of the integrality of the
G_{n}
’s by expressing them in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind. The starting point of this research is the study of the integer sequence
{(a_{n})}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, defined by:

a_{0}=0~~\text{and}~~a_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}k!(n-k-1)!~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}).

(1.5)

This sequence is closely related to the sum of the inverses of binomial coefficients which is studied by several authors (see
[
5
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
]
). It must be noted that both Stirling numbers, Genocchi numbers and the numbers
a_{n}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
) have combinatorial interpretations (see e.g.,
[
1
,
11
]
for the Stirling numbers,
[
2
,
15
]
for the Genocchi numbers and the sequence
A003149
of
[
10
]
for the
a_{n}
’s).

In another direction, by leaning on Legendre’s formula (
1.1
), an identity due to Rockett
[
8
]
, and another identity due to the author
[
3
]
, we obtain nontrivial lower bounds for the
2
-adic valuations of the rational numbers
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
).

2
The results and the proofs

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

For all natural number
n
, we have

G_{n}=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq k\leq n}(-1)^{k-1}(\ell-1)!(k-\ell)!S(n,k).

(2.1)

In particular,
G_{n}
is an integer for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

To prove this theorem, we need some intermediary results. The first one (Proposotion
2.2
below) can be immediately derived from the following identity of Rockett
[
8
]
:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}=\frac{n+1}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac{2^{k}}{k}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

(2.2)

But for convenience, we prefer reproduce its proof here.

Proposition 2.2
.

For all natural number
n
, we have

a_{n}=\frac{n!}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}.

(2.3)

Proof.

We begin by establishing a recurrent formula for the sequence
{(a_{n})}_{n}
. For any integer
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}k!(n-k-1)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}k!(n-k-1)!+(n-1)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}k!(n-k-2)!(n-k-1)+(n-1)!

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}k!(n-k-2)!-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(k+1)!(n-k-2)!+(n-1)!.

But since

\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}k!(n-k-2)!=a_{n-1}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(k+1)!(n-k-2)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell!(n-\ell-1)!~~~~~~~~~~(\text{by putting }\ell=k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\ell!(n-\ell-1)!-(n-1)!

\displaystyle=a_{n}-(n-1)!,

it follows that:

a_{n}=na_{n-1}-a_{n}+2\cdot(n-1)!.

Hence

a_{n}=\frac{n}{2}a_{n-1}+(n-1)!.

(2.4)

Further, we remark that Formula (
2.4
) also holds for
n=1
. Now, according to Formula (
2.4
), we have for any positive integer
k
:

\frac{2^{k}}{k!}a_{k}-\frac{2^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}a_{k-1}=\frac{2^{k}}{k}.

Then by summing both sides of the last equality from
k=1
to
n
, we obtain (because the sum on the left is telescopic and
a_{0}=0
) that:

\frac{2^{n}}{n!}a_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k},

which gives the required formula. The proof is achieved.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

The exponential generating function of the sequence
{(a_{n})}_{n}
is given by:

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{-2\log(1-x)}{2-x}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Using Formula (
2.3
) of Proposition
2.2
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)x^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{2^{k}}{k}x^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\left(\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{n}\right).

But since
\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{n}=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{1-\frac{x}{2}}=\frac{x^{k}}{2^{k}}\cdot\frac{2}{2-x}
, we get

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2}{2-x}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k}=\frac{2}{2-x}\left(-\log(1-x)\right),

as required. This achieves the proof.
∎

Next, from Corollary
2.3
and Formula (
1.3
), we derive the following corollary:

Corollary 2.4
.

For any natural number
n
, we have

a_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{k}s(n,k).

(2.6)

Proof.

Let us consider the following three functions (which are analytic on the neighborhood of zero):

f(x):=\frac{-2\log(1-x)}{2-x}~,~g(x):=\frac{2x}{e^{x}+1}~,~\text{ and }h(x):=\log(1-x).

We easily check that
f=-g\circ h
. Since in addition
h(0)=0
then the power series expansion of
f
about the origin can be obtained by substituting
h
in the power series expansion of
g
about the origin (which is given by (
1.4
)) and multiplying by
(-1)
. Doing so, we get

f(x)=-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}G_{k}\frac{(h(x))^{k}}{k!}=-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}G_{k}\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{k!}.

(2.7)

Further, by substituting in (
1.3
)
x
by
(-x)
, we have for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

\frac{\log^{k}(1-x)}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}(-1)^{n}s(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

So, by inserting this last into (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}G_{k}\sum_{n=k}^{+\infty}(-1)^{n}s(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}G_{k}s(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\left[(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{k}s(n,k)\right]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing this with Formula (
2.5
) of Corollary
2.3
, we conclude that:

a_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}G_{k}s(n,k)~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}),

as required.
∎

We finally derive our main result from Corollary
2.4
above by applying the well-known inversion formula recalled in the following proposition:

Proposition 1
.

Let
{(u_{n})}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
{(v_{n})}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be two real sequences. Then the two following identities
(I)
and
(II)
are equivalent:

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{k}s(n,k)~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}),

(
I
)

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{k}S(n,k)~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

(
II
)

Proof.

Use the orthogonality relations (
1.2
) (see e.g.,
[
1
]
or
[
7
]
for the details).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

It suffices to apply Proposition
1
for
u_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}a_{n}
and
v_{n}=G_{n}
(
\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}
). In view of (
2.6
), Identity
(I)
holds; so
(II)
also, that is

G_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}a_{k}S(n,k)~~~~~~~~~~(\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}).

Finally, by substituting in this last equality
a_{k}
by its expression given by (
1.5
), we get for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
:

\displaystyle G_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}i!(k-i-1)!\right)S(n,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(\ell-1)!(k-\ell)!\right)S(n,k)~~~~~~~~~~(\text{by putting }\ell=i+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq k\leq n}(-1)^{k-1}(\ell-1)!(k-\ell)!S(n,k),

as required.
∎

Now, we turn to present another type of results providing nontrivial lower bounds for the
2
-adic valuations of the rational numbers
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}
(
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
).

Theorem 2.5
.

For any positive integer
n
, we have

\vartheta_{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)\geq s_{2}(n)

(2.8)

and
(
more strongly
)
:

\vartheta_{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)\geq n-\left\lfloor\frac{\log{n}}{\log{2}}\right\rfloor.

(2.9)

Proof.

Let
n
be a fixed positive integer. Since
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
then we have
\vartheta_{2}(a_{n})\geq 0
. But, by using Formula (
2.3
) of Proposition
2.2
, this is equivalent to:

\vartheta_{2}(n!)-n+\vartheta_{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)\geq 0.

Then, using the Legendre formula (
1.1
) for the prime number
p=2
, which says that
\vartheta_{2}(n!)=\frac{n-s_{2}(n)}{2-1}=n-s_{2}(n)
, we get

\vartheta_{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)\geq s_{2}(n),

confirming (
2.8
). To establish the stronger lower bound (
2.9
), we use the Rockett formula (
2.2
) together with the identity:

\mathrm{lcm}\left\{\binom{m}{0},\binom{m}{1},\dots,\binom{m}{m}\right\}=\frac{\mathrm{lcm}(1,2,\dots,m,m+1)}{m+1}~~~~~~~~~~(\forall m\in{\mathbb{N}}),

(2.10)

established by the author in
[
3
]
. According to (
2.2
) and (
2.10
), we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}=\frac{2^{n}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}~~\text{and}~~1=\frac{n}{\mathrm{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)}\cdot\mathrm{lcm}\left\{\binom{n-1}{0},\binom{n-1}{1},\dots,\binom{n-1}{n-1}\right\}.

By multiplying side by side these last equalities, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}=\frac{2^{n}}{\mathrm{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)}\cdot\mathrm{lcm}\left\{\binom{n-1}{0},\binom{n-1}{1},\dots,\binom{n-1}{n-1}\right\}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}.

But since the rational number
\mathrm{lcm}\{\binom{n-1}{0},\binom{n-1}{1},\dots,\binom{n-1}{n-1}\}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}
is obviously an integer, then we derive that:

\vartheta_{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2^{k}}{k}\right)\geq\vartheta_{2}\left(\frac{2^{n}}{\mathrm{lcm}(1,2,\dots,n)}\right)=n-\left\lfloor\frac{\log n}{\log 2}\right\rfloor,

confirming (
2.9
) and completes the proof.
∎

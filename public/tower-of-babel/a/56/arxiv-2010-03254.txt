The Structure of Translational Tilings in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}
Terence Tao

Abstract
We obtain structural results on translational tilings of periodic functions in ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} by finite tiles. In particular, we show that any level one tiling of a periodic set in ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} must be weakly periodic (the disjoint union of sets that are individually periodic in one direction), but present a counterexample of a higher level tiling of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} that fails to be weakly periodic. We also establish a quantitative version of the two-dimensional periodic tiling conjecture which asserts that any finite tile in ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} that admits a tiling, must admit a periodic tiling, by providing a polynomial bound on the period; this also gives an exponential-type bound on the computational complexity of the problem of deciding whether a given finite subset of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} tiles or not. As a byproduct of our structural theory, we also obtain an explicit formula for a universal period for all tilings of a one-dimensional tile.

1.3
Periodicity of tiling

In one dimension it is easy to see from the pigeonhole principle that any tiling

1.4
Results

We can now state our first main theorem, which clarifies the nature of level one tilings of periodic sets in two dimensions, in particular revealing a fundamental difference between level one tilings and higher level tilings.

Theorem 1.3

(Tilings in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
)
.

(i).

(Level one tilings in the plane are weakly periodic) If
F\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is finite and
A
is a level one tiling of a periodic set by
F
, then
A
is weakly periodic.

(ii).

(Higher level tilings in the plane need not be weakly periodic) There exists an eight-element subset
F\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and a level
4
tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
which is not weakly periodic.

Theorem

1.3
(i).
In fact, we have a more quantitative version of this result. Here and in the sequel we use the asymptotic notation

Corollary 1.6

We now discuss further the proof of Theorem

Theorem 1.7

(Structure of tilings)

Let

F
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a set such that
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}
is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic for some

\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f}

\langle qf\rangle

is the least common multiple of

and all the primes less than or equal to

.

We establish this result in Section

[

]

is a tiling for a tile

of that tile, as long as

is congruent to
1

(x+y)^{p}=x^{p}+y^{p}
that is valid in any commutative ring of characteristic

1.7
is then established by averaging over all such dilations

.

Theorem
1.7
already resembles an assertion of weak periodicity of
\mathds{1}_{A}
, except that the terms on the right-hand side of (
5
)
are not indicator functions. Nevertheless, the structural theorem turns out to be particularly powerful in the case of level one tilings, when it imposes a powerful pointwise constraint

\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f}\leqslant 1

(6)

on the functions
\varphi_{f}
. Furthermore, by working modulo

to eliminate the
\mathds{1}_{A}
and

\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f}=0\hbox{ mod }1.

to conclude that the functions

The proof of Theorem
1.5
also proceeds by exploiting the dilation lemma, though in a simpler fashion (there is no need to work modulo

1.5
but with weaker bounds (of exponential type in the diameter rather than polynomial) by using the pigeonhole principle instead of the dilation lemma, but we do not present these arguments here.

of a given one-dimensional tile

We plan to investigate the applications of this theory to higher-dimensional lattice tilings in subsequent work.

In Section

If

We use

We use

to denote the projection homomorphism from

to the (additive) unit circle

. A

\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

Two vectors

are said to be

otherwise. In the two-dimensional case

to be the determinant of the

v=\frac{v\wedge h_{2}}{h_{1}\wedge h_{2}}h_{1}+\frac{v\wedge h_{1}}{h_{2}\wedge h_{1}}h_{2}

(7)

for any

(this is easily verified by first testing the cases

, then extending by linearity). In particular, if we let
\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle
denote the lattice generated by

, we have the inclusion

|h_{1}\wedge h_{2}|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle.

This inclusion can also be established from Lagrange’s theorem, since
|h_{1}\wedge h_{2}|
is the index of
\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle
. From Hadamard’s inequality one has

|h_{1}\wedge h_{2}|\leqslant\|h_{1}\|\|h_{2}\|

where

denotes the Euclidean length of an element

of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

An element

is said to be

if it cannot be written as

for some

and some integer

. Note that every non-zero element

of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be uniquely expressed as

where

is a natural number and

is primitive.

If

, we use

to denote the Kronecker delta function at

, and let

denote the discrete differentiation operator

in the direction

(or

The counterexample

We first need to locate a sign pattern on

Lemma 2.1

There exists a

3
A dilation lemma and the structure theorem

In
[
B2
, Proposition 3.1]
, some elementary commutative algebra was used to establish a dilation lemma that asserted, roughly speaking, that if
A
was a (multi-set) tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of a tile
F
at some level
k
, then
A
was also a tiling of the dilate
rF
for an arithmetic progression of
r
’s. A one-dimensional version of this lemma previously appeared in
[
T2
]
; see also
[
HIPRV
, Proposition 3.2]
(or
[
IMP
, Theorem 3.3]
) for a related Fourier-analytic dilation lemma for tilings of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{d}
. Variants of this lemma were also established in
[
S5
, Lemma 10]
and
[
KS
, Lemma 2]
. We re-prove this lemma using elementary number theory, and generalize it from tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to tilings of periodic level functions.

Lemma 3.1

(Dilation lemma)
.

Let
F
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d\geqslant 1
, and let
g\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}
be a bounded function.

(i).

If
\mathds{1}_{F}*g=k
for some integer
k
, then for any prime
p
with
p>(\sup g-\inf g)|F|
, one has
\mathds{1}_{pF}*g=k
.

(ii).

If
\mathds{1}_{F}*g=k
for some integer
k
, and
q
is the product of all primes less than or equal to
(\sup g-\inf g)|F|
, then one has
\mathds{1}_{rF}*g=k
whenever
r
is a natural number coprime to
q
.

(iii).

If
\mathds{1}_{F}*g
is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic for some
\ell\geqslant 1
, and
q
is the least common multiple of
\ell
and all primes less than or equal to
2(\sup g-\inf g)|F|
, then
\mathds{1}_{rF}*g=\mathds{1}_{F}*g

whenever
r
is a natural number with
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
.

Proof.

We begin with (i). The claim is easily verified when
g
is constant, so we may assume that
g
is non-constant, in particular
p>|F|
. We convolve the equation
\mathds{1}_{F}*g=k
by
p-1
further copies of
\mathds{1}_{F}
using the identity
\mathds{1}_{F}*1=|F|
to conclude that

(\mathds{1}_{F})^{*p}*g=|F|^{p-1}k

where
(\mathds{1}_{F})^{*p}
is the convolution of
p
copies of
\mathds{1}_{F}
. As all functions here are integer-valued, this identity also holds modulo
p
:

(\mathds{1}_{F})^{*p}*g=|F|^{p-1}k\hbox{ mod }p.

By Fermat’s little theorem we have
|F|^{p-1}=1\hbox{ mod }p
. Also, from the binomial theorem
4
4

4

Alternatively, one can apply the Frobenius endomorphism
f\mapsto f^{*p}
to the group algebra
\mathcal{F}_{p}[\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
, where
\mathcal{F}_{p}
denotes the finite field of order
p
.
we have
(f+g)^{*p}=f^{*p}+g^{*p}\hbox{ mod }p
for all finitely supported functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}
. Iterating this observation and writing
\mathds{1}_{F}=\sum_{f\in F}\delta_{f}
as the sum of Kronecker delta functions
\delta_{f}
, we see that

(\mathds{1}_{F})^{*p}=\mathds{1}_{pF}\hbox{ mod }p.

We conclude that

\mathds{1}_{pF}*g=\mathds{1}_{F}*g\hbox{ mod }p

or equivalently

(\mathds{1}_{pF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*g=0\hbox{ mod }p.

Observe that the left-hand side takes values in the integers of magnitude at most
(\sup g-\inf g)|F|
(to show this, one can first shift
g
by a constant to normalize
\inf g=0
if desired). By the assumption on
p
, we thus see that
(\mathds{1}_{pF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*g=0
, and (i) follows.

To prove (ii), observe from the fundamental theorem of arithmetic that any
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
is the product of a finite number of primes
p>(\sup g-\inf g)|F|
(possibly with repetition). The claim then follows by iterating (i).

Finally, we prove (iii). If
\mathds{1}_{F}*g
is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic, then for any
\lambda\in\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we have

\mathds{1}_{F}*\Delta_{\lambda}g=\Delta_{\lambda}(\mathds{1}_{F}*g)=0.

The discrete derivative
\Delta_{\lambda}g
takes values in the integers of magnitude at most
\sup g-\inf g
. Applying (ii), we conclude that

\mathds{1}_{rF}*\Delta_{\lambda}g=0

whenever
\lambda\in\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
. Equivalently,
\mathds{1}_{rF}*g
is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic for all
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
, which implies that

(\mathds{1}_{rF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*g

is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic. In particular, if
B_{R}
denotes a Følner sequence on
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(for instance one can take
B_{R}\coloneqq\ell\{-R,\dots,R\}^{d}
), then we have

(\mathds{1}_{rF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*g=(\mathds{1}_{rF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}*g

(16)

for any
R>0
. But as
r-1
is a multiple of
\ell
, we have
rf-f\in\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for all
f\in F
. From the Følner property we then have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|(\delta_{rf}-\delta_{f})*\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}\right\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=0

for all
f
; from Young’s inequality and the boundedness of
g
we hence have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|(\delta_{rf}-\delta_{f})*\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}*g\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=0

and hence by the triangle inequality

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left\|(1_{rF}-1_{F})*\frac{1}{|B_{R}|}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}*g\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=0.

Combining this with (
16
) one has
(\mathds{1}_{rF}-\mathds{1}_{F})*g=0
, which gives (iii).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The above proof shows that if the requirement
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
in Lemma
3.1
(iii) is relaxed to
r
merely being coprime to
q
, then
\mathds{1}_{rF}*g
is no longer necessarily equal to
\mathds{1}_{F}*g
, but will still be
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic. In
[
S5
, Theorems 10, 13]
it is also shown that if
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=1
then
\mathds{1}_{-F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=1
, and also
\mathds{1}_{rF}*\mathds{1}_{A}\leqslant 1
whenever
r
is an integer coprime to
|F|
. (In fact one can improve the inequality
\mathds{1}_{rF}*\mathds{1}_{A}\leqslant 1
to
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=1
by volume packing arguments; see, e.g.,
[
GL2
, Lemma 3.2]
.)

Now we can prove Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let the notation and hypotheses be as in that theorem. From Lemma
3.1
(iii) we have

\mathds{1}_{rF}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}

for all natural numbers
r
with
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
, where
q
being as in Lemma
3.1
(iii). As
0\in F
, we can rewrite this identity as

\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\delta_{rf}*\mathds{1}_{A}.

We can average this to obtain

\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f,N}

for any natural number
N\geqslant 1
, where
\varphi_{f,N}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to[0,1]
is the function

\varphi_{f,N}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{(1+nq)f}*\mathds{1}_{A}.

It is clear that
\varphi_{f,N}
takes values in
[0,1]
. Also from telescoping series we have

|\varphi_{f,N}(x+qf)-\varphi_{f,N}(x)|\leqslant\frac{2}{N}

(17)

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. By the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem, we can find a sequence
N_{i}\to\infty
such that for every
f\in F\backslash\{0\}
,
\varphi_{f,N_{i}}
converges locally uniformly to a limit
\varphi_{f}
, which then also takes values in
[0,1]
, and we now have

\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\varphi_{f}.

Setting
N=N_{i}
in (
17
) and taking limits, we conclude that
\varphi_{f}(x+qf)-\varphi_{f}(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, thus
\varphi_{f}
is
\langle qf\rangle
-periodic, and Theorem
1.7
follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The above argument shows that one can interpret
\varphi_{f}(x)
as a limiting density of
A
along the ray
\{x+(1+nq)f\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. A similar averaging argument (using ultrafilter limits instead of generalized limits) appears in
[
S4
, Section 7.3]
in a related context, under different assumptions.

For our application it is convenient to group together “commensurable” terms in Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 3.4

(Structure of tilings, II)
.

Let
d,\ell,k\geqslant 1
, let
F
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
E
be an
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-periodic subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and let
A
be a tiling of
E
by
F
of level
k
. We normalize
0\in F
, and assume
|F|>1
. Then there exists a decomposition

\mathds{1}_{A}=k\mathds{1}_{E}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}

(18)

where
1\leqslant m\leqslant|F|-1
, and each
\varphi_{j}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to[0,k]
is
\langle qh_{j}\rangle
-periodic, where
q
is the least common multiple of
\ell
and all the primes less than or equal to
2|F|
, and
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}
are pairwise incommensurable elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|h_{j}\|\leqslant\mathrm{diam}(F)^{|F|-1}.

(19)

In particular, we have the upper bounds

q\ll_{|F|}\ell

(20)

and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi_{j}\leqslant k.

(21)

Furthermore, each
h_{j}
is an integer multiple of an element of
F\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.7
one has

\mathds{1}_{A}=k\mathds{1}_{E}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\{0\}}\tilde{\varphi}_{f}

where each
\tilde{\varphi}_{f}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to[0,1]
is
\langle qf\rangle
-periodic. We define an equivalence relation on
F\backslash\{0\}
by declaring
f\sim f^{\prime}
if
f,f^{\prime}
are commensurable (namely,
f=pf^{\prime}
for some rational
p
). If we let
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
be the equivalence classes of this relation, then
1\leqslant m\leqslant|F|-1
and we have a decomposition (
18
) with

\varphi_{j}\coloneqq\sum_{f\in C_{j}}\tilde{\varphi}_{f}.

In particular the
\varphi_{j}
are non-negative, and then from (
18
) and the non-negativity of
\mathds{1}_{A}
we conclude that all the
\varphi_{j}
are also bounded by
k
, as well as the bound (
21
). Since each
\tilde{\varphi}_{f}
is
\langle qf\rangle
-periodic, we see on taking least common multiples that
\varphi_{j}
is
\langle qh_{j}\rangle
-periodic for some non-zero
h_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
commensurable to the elements of
C_{j}
and of magnitude at most

\|h_{j}\|\leqslant\prod_{f\in C_{j}}\|f\|\leqslant\mathrm{diam}(F)^{|C_{j}|}

(note that
\|f\|\leqslant\mathrm{diam}(F)
for all
f\in F
since
0\in F
). In particular we have (
19
). Since the
C_{j}
are pairwise incommensurable, the
h_{j}
are also pairwise incommensurable. Finally, the bound (
20
) is clear from definition of

.
∎

We have found that the bound (
21
) is particularly powerful in the level one case

, as it can be used in that case to completely rule out “equidistributed” scenarios in which at least one of the
\varphi_{j}
has values that equidistribute in the unit interval
[0,1]
. However, in higher level settings it is possible for multiple equidistributed
\varphi_{j}
to coexist, which is what led us to the counterexample constructed in Section
2
.

As another quick application of Theorem
1.7
, we obtain an explicit formula for a universal period of one-dimensional tiles:

Corollary 3.5

(Universal period of one-dimensional tiles)
.

Let
F
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}
, and let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a set such that
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}
is
\langle\ell\rangle
-periodic for some
\ell\geqslant 1
. We normalize
0\in F
. Then
A
is
\langle qn\rangle
-periodic, where
q
is the least common multiple of
\ell
and all the primes less than or equal to
2|F|
, and
n
is the least common multiple of
\|f\|
for all
f\in F\backslash\{0\}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
1.7
we see that
\mathds{1}_{A}
is the linear combination of a finite number of terms, each of which is
qn
-periodic. The claim follows.
∎

As first observed in
[
N
]
, an easy application of the pigeonhole principle already gives that all one-dimensional tilings are periodic; however, the bound produced is exponential in the diameter
\mathrm{diam}(F)
if done naively. In contrast, the bound here is polynomial in the diameter (for fixed
|F|
), and further is uniform over
all
tilings
A
of an
\langle\ell\rangle
-periodic set by a fixed tile
F
, whereas the period produced by pigeonhole principle arguments will depend on the choice of tiling. In
[

]
it was shown that if the cardinality
|F|
of the tile is not held fixed, the period of a one-dimensional tiling can grow superpolynomially in the diameter
n\coloneqq\mathrm{diam}(F)
(in fact a lower bound
\exp(\log^{2}n/4\log\log n)
is demonstrated for infinitely many

); there is also an exponential lower bound for indecomposable tilings of higher level
[
S3
]
. Conversely, the best known upper bound for the period for a tile of diameter
n
(with no restriction on
|F|
) is
\exp(O_{\varepsilon}(n^{1/3+\varepsilon}))

[
B3
]
.

Remark 3.6
.

In the special case of Corollary

5
Converting weakly periodic tilings to periodic tilings

A simple pigeonholing argument

[

, Theorem 2.3]

[

, Proposition 2.1]
). In this section we introduce a constructive way to convert any weakly periodic tiling to a periodic tiling. This constructive approach allows us to achieve the significantly better bound in Theorem

We first give a “slicing lemma” that allows one to “slice” the tile

along cosets along which the tiling set

already exhibits some periodicity, while retaining the periodicity of the set being tiled.

Lemma 5.1

(Slicing lemma)
.

Let
\ell,k\geqslant 1
be natural numbers, and let
h
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Suppose that
F
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
A
is a
\langle\ell kh\rangle
-periodic subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}
is
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic. Then for every coset
x+\langle h\rangle
of
\langle h\rangle
, the function
\mathds{1}_{F\cap(x+\langle h\rangle)}*\mathds{1}_{A}
is
qks\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic, where
q
is the least common multiple of
\ell
and all the primes less than or equal to
2|F|
, and
s
is the least common multiple of the
|h\wedge(f-f^{\prime})|
for all
f,f^{\prime}\in F
with
f-f^{\prime}
incommensurate with
h
.

Proof.

We may assume that
F
intersects
x+\langle h\rangle
, as the claim is trivial otherwise; by relabeling we may then assume
x\in F
. By translating
F
and
x
we then may assume that
x=0
and
0\in F
. From Lemma
3.1
(iii) we have

\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{rF}*\mathds{1}_{A}

whenever
r=1\hbox{ mod }q
. If we strengthen the condition on
r
to
r=1\hbox{ mod }qk
, then for each
f\in F\cap\langle h\rangle
, we have
rf-f\in\langle qkh\rangle\subset\langle\ell kh\rangle
, hence by the
\langle\ell kh\rangle
-periodicity of
A
we have

(\mathds{1}_{r(F\cap\langle h\rangle)}-\mathds{1}_{F\cap\langle h\rangle})*\mathds{1}_{A}=0.

Combining the two equations, we see that

\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F\cap\langle h\rangle}*\mathds{1}_{A}+\sum_{f\in F\backslash\langle h\rangle}\delta_{rf}*\mathds{1}_{A}

and thus

\mathds{1}_{F\cap\langle h\rangle}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\langle h\rangle}\delta_{rf}*\mathds{1}_{A}

whenever
r=1\hbox{ mod }qk
. Note that all the terms on the right-hand side are
\langle\ell kh\rangle
-periodic, since
\mathds{1}_{A}
is. Averaging over
r
and using the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem to extract a limit as in the proof of Theorem
1.7
, we see that

\mathds{1}_{F\cap\langle h\rangle}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}-\sum_{f\in F\backslash\langle h\rangle}\varphi_{f}

for some functions
\varphi_{f}
which are both
\langle\ell kh\rangle
-periodic and
\langle qkf\rangle
-periodic. By (
8
) and the definition of

and

, each
\varphi_{f}
is
qks\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic, as is
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}
, and the claim follows.

Corollary 5.3

(Improving a tiling, I)

Let
\ell,k\geqslant 1

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}+\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus\{j\}}\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}=\mathds{1}_{E}

To eliminate all the terms besides
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}
we apply discrete differentiation operators as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
(ii). From (
8
) we can write

e=a_{i,j}\ell h_{i}+b_{i,j}\ell h_{j}

for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
distinct from
j
and some integers
a_{i,j},b_{i,j}
. Then from the periodicity properties of the
A_{i}
,
A_{j}
we have

\Delta_{e-b_{i,j}\ell h_{j}}(\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{i}})=0

and

\Delta_{e-b_{i,j}\ell h_{j}}(\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}})=\Delta_{e}(\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}});

also from the
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodicity of
E
one has

\Delta_{e-b_{i,j}\ell h_{j}}\mathds{1}_{E}=0.

Applying each of the discrete derivative operators
\Delta_{e-b_{i,j}\ell h_{j}}
in turn, we conclude that

\Delta_{e}^{m-1}(\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}})=0.

Thus, for any
x
, the map
n\mapsto\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}(x+ne)
is polynomial in
n
; but it is also bounded, hence constant. Thus
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}
is
\langle e\rangle
-periodic and
\langle\ell h_{j}\rangle
-periodic, hence by (
8
) it is
\ell NM_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic for some integer
M_{j}
with
M_{j}\ll_{m}\|h_{j}\|
. We now split
h_{j}=k_{j}h^{\prime}_{j}
where
k_{j}
is a natural number and
h^{\prime}_{j}
is primitive. By Corollary
5.3
we see that we can find a
q_{j}k_{j}s_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
such that
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}_{j}}
, where
q_{j}=\ell NM_{j}C_{j}
for some
C_{j}=O_{|F|}(1)
, and
s_{j}
is a positive integer with the bound

s_{j}\leqslant(\|h^{\prime}_{j}\|\mathrm{diam}(F))^{|F|(|F|-1)/2}.

We conclude that
q_{j}k_{j}s_{j}=\ell NL_{j}
with

L_{j}\ll_{|F|}\|h_{j}\|^{1+|F|(|F|-1)/2}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{|F|(|F|-1)/2}.

Summing over
j
, we have

\mathds{1}_{F}*\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{E}.

In particular this shows that
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}_{j}}
is bounded by
1
, that is to say the
A^{\prime}_{j}
are disjoint. If we set
A^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}A^{\prime}_{j}
, we then have
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}=\mathds{1}_{E}
, and
A^{\prime}
is
\ell M\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic with

M\leqslant N\prod_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\ll_{|F|}(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|h_{i}\|)^{m+|F|(|F|-1)/2}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{m|F|(|F|-1)/2}

giving the claim.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The claim is trivial for
|F|=1
, so suppose
|F|>1
. By Theorem
1.4
, there exist
m,h_{1},\dots,h_{m},L
obeying the conclusions of that theorem, such that
A
is the disjoint union of
\langle\ell Lh_{j}\rangle
-periodic sets for
j=1,\dots,m
. Applying Theorem
5.4
(with
\ell
replaced by
\ell L
), we can find an
\ell LM^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}
with
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}=\mathds{1}_{E}
, where

M^{\prime}\ll_{|F|}(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|h_{i}\|)^{\tfrac{1}{2}(|F|+2)(|F|-1)}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{\tfrac{1}{2}|F|(|F|-1)^{2}}.

Using the bounds (
3
), (
4
), we have

LM^{\prime}\ll_{|F|}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{|F|(|F|-1)}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{\tfrac{1}{2}(|F|+2)(|F|-1)^{3}}\mathrm{diam}(F)^{\tfrac{1}{2}|F|(|F|-1)^{2}}.

The claim follows.
∎

6
One-periodic tilings

Call a set
A

one-periodic
if
A
is
\langle h\rangle
-periodic for some non-zero
h
. For any given tile
F
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we consider
8
8

8

We thank Mihalis Kolountzakis (private communication) for suggesting this question.
the following question:

Question 6.1
.

Let
F
be a finite tile of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Does there exist a tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F
which is not one-periodic?

The answer to this question is positive for some tiles and negative for others, as the following examples show:

(i).

If
F_{0}
is the square
\{0,1\}^{2}
, then every tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F_{0}
can be seen to be either
\langle(2,0)\rangle
-periodic or
\langle(0,2)\rangle
-periodic, and hence the answer to Question
6.1
is negative in this case. More generally, from
[
S5
, Theorem 19]
it follows that the answer to Question
6.1
is negative for any tile of cardinality
4
that contains zero and generates
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

(ii).

On the other hand, in Subsection
1.3
it was shown that the four-element tile
F_{1}\coloneqq\{0,2\}\times\{0,1\}
(which does
not
generate
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
) admits tilings
A
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that are not one-periodic. Note that if
A
is a tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F_{2}
, then
2A
is a tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F_{2}\coloneqq 2F_{1}+\{0,1\}^{2}=\{0,4\}\times\{0,2\}+\{0,1\}^{2}
. Thus the answer to Question
6.1
is positive for the tiles
F_{1},F_{2}
. In particular it is possible to have non-one-periodic tilings even when the tile
F
contains
0
and generates all of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as an abelian group.

(iii).

If the tile
F
is collinear (it lies in a one-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), then Corollary
3.5
implies that all tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F
are
\langle h\rangle
-periodic for some universal period
h
that is parallel to the line that
F
lies in. Hence the answer to Question
6.1
is negative in this case.

(iv).

If the tile
F
is “connected” in the sense that
F+[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{2}
is simply connected, then by
[
GN
, Theorem 5.5]
,
F
admits only one-periodic tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Hence the answer to Question
6.1
is negative in this case. This of course contains the first part of (i) as a special case.

(v).

From
[
S5
, Theorem 17]
it follows that if
F
has prime order
|F|=p
then every tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by
F
is
p\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic, so the answer to Question
6.1
is negative in this case.

We do not have a full classification of the tiles
F
which have a negative answer to Question
6.1
. However, the results presented in our paper do at least show that this question is decidable for any given tile, even if one replaces the set
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by more general periodic subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Theorem 6.2

(Decidability of Question
6.1
)
.

There is an algorithm which, when given periodic subset
E
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and a finite set
F
that tiles
E
as input
9
9

9

Note that a periodic subset
E
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
can be stored using a finite amount of memory, by first storing a pair of generators for the periodicity lattice, and then storing a coset representative for each of the cosets of that lattice that lie in
E
.
, decides in finite time whether
F
admits a non one-periodic tiling of
E
, or not.

Proof.

Suppose that
F
is a tile of an
\ell\mathbb{Z}^{2}
periodic set
E
. From Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
5.4
we can compute positive integers
m,N_{1},\dots,N_{m}
and pairwise incommensurable
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with the property that for every tiling set
A
of
E
by
F
, there exists partitions
A=A_{1}\uplus\dots\uplus A_{m}
and
E=E_{1}\uplus\dots\uplus E_{m}
, where for each
1\leqslant j\leqslant m
,
A_{j}
is
\langle h_{j}\rangle
-periodic,
E_{j}
is
N_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic, and
A_{j}
is a tiling set for
E_{j}
by
F
:
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{E_{j}}
. We write
h_{j}=k_{j}h^{\prime}_{j}
for a (computable) natural number
k_{j}
and primitive
h^{\prime}_{j}
. By Corollary
5.3
, we can then compute positive integers
M_{1},\dots,M_{m}
such that whenever
A,A_{1},\dots,A_{m},E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
are as above, one can find an
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
for each
j=1,\dots,m
such that

A_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)\equiv_{F}A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)

for all cosets
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
, where we use
A\equiv_{F}A^{\prime}
to denote the equivalence relation
\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}
. By construction we have

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{E_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{E}.

Conversely, if for each
j=1,\dots,m
we locate a
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{E}

(30)

and then for each coset
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
we find a
\langle h_{j}\rangle
-periodic subset
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
of
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
such that

A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}\equiv_{F}A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)

(31)

then the sets

A_{j}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle}A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}

are such that

A_{j}\equiv_{F}A^{\prime}_{j}

and hence

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A_{j}}=\mathds{1}_{E}.

In particular, the
A_{j}
are disjoint and their union

A\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\bigcup_{y\in\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle^{\perp}}A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}

(32)

tiles
E
by
F
. Thus, we have a way of completely describing all the tilings
A
of
E
by
F
: we first locate all tuples
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
of
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic sets
A^{\prime}_{j}
obeying (
30
), then for each such tuple, each
j=1,\dots,m
, and each coset
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
, we choose a
\langle h_{j}\rangle
-periodic subset
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
obeying (
31
), then the unions (
32
) give all the possible tilings of
E
by
F
.

Note that there are only finitely many
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(in fact there are
2^{M_{j}^{2}}
many), so there are only finitely many possible tuples
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
that can arise in this fashion. For each such tuple, the identity (
30
) can be verified or disproved in finite time. Hence we can completely enumerate all the possible tuples
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
that can arise in the above construction. Similarly, for each coset
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
and any fixed choice of
A^{\prime}_{j}
, the set of all
\langle h_{j}\rangle
-periodic subsets
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
of
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
obeying (
31
) is also finite and can be enumerated in finite time. However, there are infinitely many such cosets, and hence one may potentially need to loop over an infinite number of choices in order to enumerate all possible tilings (cf. the tilings (
1
), (
2
)).

Fortunately, for the question of whether there admit non-one-periodic tilings we do not need to exhaust over infinitely many possibilities, and can instead reason as follows. Let us call a slice
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
of an
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
by a coset
(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
a
chameleon
if there exists a
\langle h_{j}\rangle
-periodic subsets
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
of
\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y
obeying (
31
) which is not equal to
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
, in which case we say that
A^{\prime}_{j}

contains a chameleon slice
. Note that for any given
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
, the slices of
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
repeat periodically in
y
and hence one can determine whether
A^{\prime}_{j}
contains a chameleon slice or not in finite time.

Suppose first that
A
arises from a tuple
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
with the property that at most one of the
A^{\prime}_{j}
admits a chameleon slice, thus we have some
j_{0}
such that
A^{\prime}_{j}
does not admit a chameleon slice for any
j\neq j_{0}
. Then for
j\neq j_{0}
, the set
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
is necessarily equal to
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
for every
y
, hence
A_{j}
is necessarily equal to the
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic set
A^{\prime}_{j}
. Since
A_{j_{0}}
is also
\langle h_{j_{0}}\rangle
-periodic, we conclude that the set
A
constructed in (
32
) is one-periodic in this case.

Now suppose that
A
arises from a tuple
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
with the property that there are at least two sets
A^{\prime}_{j_{1}},A^{\prime}_{j_{2}}
which admit chameleon slices
A^{\prime}_{j_{1}}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j_{1}}+y_{1})
,
A^{\prime}_{j_{2}}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j_{2}}+y_{2})
respectively. Thus for
i=1,2
, we may find a
\langle h_{j_{i}}\rangle
-periodic subset
A^{(i)}
of
\langle h^{\prime}_{j_{i}}\rangle+y_{i}
that is distinct from
(A^{\prime})^{(i)}\coloneqq A^{\prime}_{j_{i}}\cap\langle h^{\prime}_{j_{i}}\rangle+y_{i}
such that

A^{(i)}\equiv_{F}(A^{\prime})^{(i)}.

In particular, if we set
A^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}A^{\prime}_{j}
, and let
A
be the set formed from
A^{\prime}
by replacing
(A^{\prime})^{(i)}
with
A^{(i)}
for
i=1,2
, so that

\mathds{1}_{A}=\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\mathds{1}_{A^{(i)}}-\mathds{1}_{(A^{\prime})^{(i)}}),

then
A
is equivalent to
A^{\prime}
:

\mathds{1}_{A}*\mathds{1}_{F}=\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}*\mathds{1}_{F}=\mathds{1}_{E}.

We claim that
A
is not one-periodic. Since
\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}
is
M\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic for some
M
, it suffices to show that
\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\mathds{1}_{A^{(i)}}-\mathds{1}_{(A^{\prime})^{(i)}})
is not one-periodic. But each summand
\mathds{1}_{A^{(i)}}-\mathds{1}_{(A^{\prime})^{(i)}}
is a non-trivial one-periodic function supported on the coset
\langle h^{\prime}_{j_{i}}+y_{i}\rangle
, and the claim is then evident from the incommensurability of
h^{\prime}_{j_{1}}
and
h^{\prime}_{j_{2}}
.

From the above discussion, we conclude that
F
admits non-one-periodic tilings if and only if there exist
M_{j}\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-periodic sets
A^{\prime}_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
obeying (
30
) such that at least two of the
A^{\prime}_{j}
admit chameleon slices. Since we can computably enumerate all the tuples
(A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m})
obeying (
30
) and test all of them for chameleon slices, the claim follows.
∎

Informally, the proof of Theorem
6.2
tells us that all the tilings of a given periodic set
E
by a given tile
F
arise from starting with a doubly periodic tiling
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}_{1}\uplus\dots\uplus A^{\prime}_{m}
(which is drawn from a finite list of such tilings) and performing a (possibly infinite) number of “slide moves” in which one or more slices
A^{\prime}_{j}\cap(\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y)
is replaced with an equivalent (one-periodic) set
A_{j,\langle h^{\prime}_{j}\rangle+y}
, in the spirit of (
1
) or (
2
). In principle, this should reduce any reasonable question about such tilings to a finite computation for any fixed choice of
E,F
, with the existence of non-one-periodic tilings serving as just one representative example of such a question.

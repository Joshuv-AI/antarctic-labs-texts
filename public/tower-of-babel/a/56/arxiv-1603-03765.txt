Infinite Series Involving Fibonacci Numbers Via Apéry-Like Formulae
Chance Sanford

Abstract
In this note, infinite series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are derived by employing formulae similar to that which Roger Apéry utilized in his seminal paper proving the irrationality of ζ ⁡ ( 3 ) \zeta(3) .



1
Introduction

In 1979 Roger Apéry published his seminal paper in which he proved the irrationality of
\zeta(3)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{n^{3}}}
. In his paper, Apéry utilizes a technique that allows one to evaluate certain infinite series telescopically.

In this note, we demonstrate how Apéry’s technique may be employed to evaluate infinite series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers; in particular of the form
F_{n^{k}}
and
L_{n^{k}}
respectively. Using Apéry-like formulae we are able to evaluate series that can be difficult to determine by other means.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, Apéry’s technique has not been utilized to evaluate infinite series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. In addition, the results obtained in this note are also believed to be new, except for Theorem 1, which is a new proof of an old result.

Before proceeding with the results, we first examine Apéry’s formula as described in Chen
[
1
]
.

For
x\neq 0
and any sequence
{a_{k}}
, let

B_{1}=\frac{1}{x},\quad B_{k}=\frac{{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\dots{a_{k-1}}}{x(x+a_{1})(x+a_{2})\dots(x+a_{k-1})},\quad k\geq 2

Then,

B_{k}-B_{k+1}=\frac{{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\dots{a_{k-1}}}{({x+a_{1}})({x+a_{2}})\dots({x+a_{k}})}

Due to telescoping, we find that:

\frac{1}{x+a_{1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\dots{a_{k-1}}}{({x+a_{1}})({x+a_{2}})\dots({x+a_{k}})}=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{{a_{1}}{a_{2}}\dots{a_{n}}}{x(x+a_{1})(x+a_{2})\dots(x+a_{n})}

Preliminaries

By modifying Apéry’s formula we are able to obtain closed form evaluations for a number of series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

But first let us establish the following notation for Binet forms of the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers:

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\sqrt{5}},\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}

where

\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\quad\beta=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}

and
F_{n}
,
L_{n}
are the
n
-th Fibonacci and Lucas number respectively.

In addition, we will need the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

For
n>0
,

1+\alpha^{2n}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{5}F_{n}\alpha^{n}&\text{for odd $n$}\\
L_{n}\alpha^{n}&\text{for even $n$}\end{cases}

(1)

This lemma can be found as Exercise 49 on page 97 of Koshy
[
4
]
, and may be verified using Binet’s formula for the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

Lemma 2
.

For
m>0
and
n\geq 1
,

F_{2^{n}m}=F_{m}L_{m}L_{2m}L_{4m}\cdots L_{2^{n-1}m}

(2)

Proof.

Using the well known identity:
F_{2nm}=F_{nm}L_{nm}
we find that:

F_{2^{n}m}=F_{2^{n-1}m}L_{2^{n-1}m}=F_{2^{n-2}m}L_{2^{n-2}m}L_{2^{n-1}m}

Repeating this process it becomes clear that:

F_{2^{n}m}=F_{m}L_{m}L_{2m}L_{4m}\cdots L_{2^{n-1}m}

∎

Lemma 3
.

For any positive integers
q
and
m
,

F_{(2m+1)q}=F_{q}\left[(-1)^{mq}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{kq}L_{2(m-k)q}}\right]

(3)

L_{(2m+1)q}=L_{q}\left[(-1)^{m(q+1)}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k(q+1)}L_{2(m-k)q}}\right]

(4)

Proof.

Only proof of (
3
) will be given, (
4
) follows an almost identical procedure.

Case 1:
q
odd

If
q
is odd then
(-1)^{jq}=(-1)^{j}
. In addition, from Binet’s formulae for the Fibonacci and Lucas numbers, along with the formula for the finite Geometric Series, we have:

\displaystyle F_{(2m+1)q}

\displaystyle=F_{q}\left[(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{2(m-k)q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}\left(\alpha^{2(m-k)q}+\beta^{2(m-k)q}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[(-1)^{m}+{\alpha^{2mq}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-\alpha^{-2q})^{k}}+{\beta^{2mq}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-\beta^{-2q})^{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[(-1)^{m}+\frac{\alpha^{2mq}(1-(-1)^{m}\alpha^{-2mq})}{1+\alpha^{-2q}}+\frac{\beta^{2mq}(1-(-1)^{m}\beta^{-2mq})}{1+\beta^{-2q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\beta^{q}(\alpha^{2q}+1)}{\sqrt{5}}\left[\frac{(-1)^{m}(1+\alpha^{2q})}{1+\alpha^{2q}}+\frac{\alpha^{2mq+2q}-(-1)^{m}\alpha^{2q}}{1+\alpha^{2q}}+\frac{\beta^{2mq}-(-1)^{m}}{1+\alpha^{2q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\alpha^{2mq+2q}+\beta^{2mq}\right)=\frac{\alpha^{2mq+q}-\beta^{2mq+q}}{\sqrt{5}}=F_{(2m+1)q}

Case 2:
q
even

If
q
is even then
(-1)^{jq}=1
.

Therefore, we find that:

\displaystyle F_{(2m+1)q}

\displaystyle=F_{q}\left[1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{L_{2(m-k)q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{\left(\alpha^{2(m-k)q}+\beta^{2(m-k)q}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[1+{\alpha^{2mq}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(\alpha^{-2q})^{k}}+{\beta^{2mq}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(\beta^{-2q})^{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{q}-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left[1+\frac{\alpha^{2mq}(1-\alpha^{-2mq})}{1-\alpha^{-2q}}+\frac{\beta^{2mq}(1-\beta^{-2mq})}{1-\beta^{-2q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\beta^{q}(1-\alpha^{2q})}{\sqrt{5}}\left[\frac{(1-\alpha^{2q})}{1-\alpha^{2q}}+\frac{\alpha^{2q}-\alpha^{2mq+2q}}{1-\alpha^{2q}}+\frac{\beta^{2mq}-1}{1-\alpha^{2q}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{-\beta^{q}}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\beta^{2mq}-\alpha^{2mq+2q}\right)=\frac{\alpha^{2mq+q}-\beta^{2mq+q}}{\sqrt{5}}=F_{(2m+1)q}

∎

Remark 1
.

As an interesting side note, by Lemma 3 we have:

\displaystyle F_{(2l+1)(2m+1)}

\displaystyle=F_{2m+1}\left[(-1)^{l}+\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{2(l-k)(2m+1)}}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{2l+1}\left[(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{2(m-k)(2l+1)}}\right]

So that for positive integers
l
and
m
,

\frac{F_{2l+1}}{F_{2m+1}}=\frac{(-1)^{l}+\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{2(l-k)(2m+1)}}}{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{2(m-k)(2l+1)}}}

and,

\frac{L_{2l+1}}{L_{2m+1}}=\frac{1+\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}{L_{2(l-k)(2m+1)}}}{1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{L_{2(m-k)(2l+1)}}}

Lemma 4
.

For odd
p
,
F_{p^{n}}
and
L_{p^{n}}
may be written as:

F_{p^{n}}=F_{(2m+1)^{n}}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]

(5)

L_{p^{n}}=L_{(2m+1)^{n}}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[{1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]

(6)

Proof.

Using (
3
) and (
4
) from Lemma 3 we are able to prove Lemma 4. We proceed by induction on
n
.

Base Case:
n=1

\displaystyle F_{1}\prod_{j=0}^{0}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{1}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{2m+1}

where we have used (
3
) with
q=1
.

Induction Step:

Assume that,

F_{p^{n}}=F_{(2m+1)^{n}}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]

We now prove that (
5
) holds for
n+1
.

That is,

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{n}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{(2m+1)^{n}}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}}\right]

\displaystyle=F_{(2m+1)^{n+1}}

where we have used (
3
) with
q=(2m+1)^{n}
.

This concludes the proof of equation (
5
), the proof of equation (
6
) proceeds in a nearly identical fashion.
∎

Results

In 1974 D.A. Millin
[
6
]
proposed a problem in
The Fibonacci Quarterly
regarding the series of reciprocal Fibonacci numbers of the form
F_{2^{n}}
(coincidentally in the same issue that Usiskin’s problems appeared). Millin’s proposal stimulated significant interest in the series, which led to the publication of numerous proofs and generalizations (see
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
for more information and references).

For the first theorem, a new proof of Millin’s Series is given using an Apéry-like formula.

Theorem 1
.

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{F_{2^{n}}}}=\frac{7-\sqrt{5}}{2}

(11)

Proof.

Let,

B_{n}=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha^{4})(1+\alpha^{8})\cdots(1+\alpha^{2^{n}})},\quad n>1

By subtracting
B_{n+1}
from
B_{n}
we observe that:

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha^{2^{n+1}}}{(1+\alpha^{4})(1+\alpha^{8})\cdots(1+\alpha^{2^{n}})(1+\alpha^{2^{n+1}})}

(12)

Setting
n=2^{n}
in Lemma 1 we obtain:

1+\alpha^{2^{n+1}}=L_{2^{n}}\alpha^{2^{n}}

Note that we are not able to begin
B_{n}
at
(1+\alpha^{2})^{-1}
since the above altered form of Lemma 1 does not hold for
n=0
.

By Lemma 1, we now find that (
12
) becomes:

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha^{2^{n+1}}}{L_{2}\alpha^{2}L_{4}\alpha^{4}\cdots L_{2^{n}}\alpha^{2^{n}}}=\frac{\alpha^{2^{n+1}}}{L_{2}L_{4}\cdots L_{2^{n}}\alpha^{2^{n+1}-2}}

Then by cancellation and Lemma 2 we obtain:

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{F_{2^{n+1}}}

Using this information, we are now able to evaluate Millin’s series telescopically.

In other words,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{F_{2^{n+1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}B_{n}-B_{n+1}

\displaystyle=B_{2}-\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\alpha^{4}}-0

Therefore,

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_{2^{n+1}}}=\sum_{n=3}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_{2^{n}}}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{6}}

(13)

Since Millin’s series begins the sum at
n=0
, we must add the missing terms.

In the end we find that:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{F_{2^{n}}}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{6}}+1+1+1/3=\frac{7-\sqrt{5}}{2}

(14)

∎

Theorem 2
.

For any positive integer
m
, and
a>0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L^{a}_{2^{n+1}m}-1}{F^{a}_{2^{n+2}m}}}=\frac{1}{F^{a}_{m}L^{a}_{m}}

(15)

Proof.

Let,

B_{0}=\frac{1}{F^{a}_{m}L^{a}_{m}},\quad B_{n}=\frac{1}{F^{a}_{m}L^{a}_{m}L^{a}_{2m}L^{a}_{4m}\cdots{L^{a}_{{2^{n}}m}}}\quad n>0

Then subtracting
B_{n+1}
from
B_{n}
and employing Lemma 2 gives,

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{L^{a}_{2^{n+1}m}-1}{F^{a}_{2^{n+2}m}}

Once again, taking advantage of the telescoping, we acquire:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L^{a}_{2^{n+1}m}-1}{F^{a}_{2^{n+2}m}}}=B_{0}-\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n+1}=\frac{1}{F^{a}_{m}L^{a}_{m}}

∎

Remark 2
.

Specifically, setting
a=m=1
, (
15
) becomes:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L_{2^{n+1}}-1}{F_{2^{n+2}}}}=1

(16)

Although since we know the closed form for Millin’s Series, we may split up the summand to obtain,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L_{2^{n+1}}}{F_{2^{n+2}}}}=1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{F_{2^{n+2}}}}=\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}

Theorem 3
.

For any positive integer
m
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{(-1)^{m}-1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}}}{F_{(2m+1)^{n+1}}}}=1

(17)

Proof.

Let,

B_{0}=1,\quad B_{n}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]},\quad n>0

Then,

\displaystyle B_{n}-B_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}}\right]-1}{\prod_{j=0}^{n}\left[{(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{j}}}}}\right]}

which by Lemma 4 gives,

\displaystyle=\frac{{(-1)^{m}-1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}}}{F_{(2m+1)^{n+1}}}

Therefore,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{(-1)^{m}-1+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}}}{F_{(2m+1)^{n+1}}}}=B_{0}-\lim_{n\to\infty}B_{n+1}=1

as desired.

∎

Remark 3
.

As a specific example, let
m=1
.

We then have,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L_{2\cdot 3^{n}}-2}{F_{3^{n+1}}}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L^{2}_{3^{n}}}{F_{3^{n+1}}}}=1

(18)

where we have used the identity
L^{2}_{n}=L_{2n}+2(-1)^{n}

[
4

.

In addition, by splitting up the summand of sum on the left side of (
18

Namely,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{F_{3^{n}}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L_{2\cdot 3^{n}}}{F_{3^{n+1}}}}+\frac{1}{2}

(19)

More generally, for all odd
m
(
17
) becomes:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{\left[\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(m-k)(2m+1)^{n}}}}\right]-2}}{F_{(2m+1)^{n+1}}}}=1

Therefore for all Fibonacci numbers of the form
F_{4m+3}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{F_{(4m+3)^{n}}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{\sum_{k=0}^{2m}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2(2m+1-k)(4m+3)^{n}}}}}}{F_{(4m+3)^{n+1}}}}+\frac{1}{2}

(20)

For instance,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{F_{7^{n}}}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{L_{6\cdot 7^{n}}-L_{4\cdot 7^{n}}+L_{2\cdot 7^{n}}}{F_{7^{n+1}}}}+\frac{1}{2}

(21)

Theorem 5

For any positive integer

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{F_{2^{n+2}}}\left[(-1)^{m}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{(-1)^{k}L_{(m-k)2^{j+1}}}\right]}{F_{(2m+1)2^{n+2}}}=\frac{1}{F_{2m+1}L_{2m+1}}

m=4
. We then have,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{L_{8\cdot 5^{n}}+L_{16\cdot 5^{n}}}{F_{4\cdot 5^{n+1}}}=\frac{1}{6765}

(32)

Theorem 8
.

For odd
p
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{(-1)^{n(p-1)/2}\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2kp^{n}}}}}{F_{p^{n+1}}}}=\frac{1}{F_{p}}

(33)

Proof.

Let,

B_{1}=1,\quad B_{n}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}{\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2kp^{j}}}}\right]}},\quad n>1

Therefore,

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2kp^{n}}}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}{\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2kp^{j}}}}\right]}}

Then utilizing Lemma 7, we have:

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{(-1)^{n(p-1)/2}F_{p}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{(p-1)/2}{(-1)^{k}{L_{2kp^{n}}}}\right]}{F_{p^{n+1}}}

(34)

which after summation produces (
33
).

∎

Remark 8
.

As an example of (

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{(-1)^{n+1}{L_{2\cdot 3^{n}}}}{F_{3^{n+1}}}}=\frac{1}{2}

(35)

Theorem 9
.

For even
p
,

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\frac{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{n}/2}}\right]^{2}-1}{L_{p^{n+1}}-2}}=\frac{1}{L_{p^{2}}-2}

(36)

Proof.

Let,

B_{2}=1,\quad B_{n}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{j+1}/2}}\right]^{2}}},\quad n>2

we then have:

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{n}/2}}\right]^{2}-1}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{j+1}/2}}\right]^{2}}}

which by Lemma 8 gives,

B_{n}-B_{n+1}=\frac{(L_{p^{2}}-2)\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{n}/2}}\right)^{2}-1\right]}{L_{p^{n+1}-2}}

Once again, by carrying out the summation, we find that:

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}{\frac{\left[\sum_{k=1}^{p/2}{L_{(2k-1)p^{n}/2}}\right]^{2}-1}{L_{p^{n+1}}-2}}=\frac{B_{2}}{L_{p^{2}}-2}=\frac{1}{L_{p^{2}}-2}

∎

Closing Remarks
.

The aim of this paper was to demonstrate how Apéry-like formulae may be employed to derive certain infinite series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. While this method is not well suited for all series of this type, it provides us with another useful tool to be used in the evaluation of series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

In addition, over the course of this paper we have restricted ourselves to examining series involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers of the form
F_{p^{k}}
and
L_{p^{k}}
. This due to the fact that terms of this form usually yield ”clean” looking summands. That being said, these are not the only series that may be evaluated using Apéry-like formulae. Series of other types may also considered, although they may not always return such pretty results.

Lastly, the techniques employed in this paper should yield similar dividends when applied to more general recursively-defined sequences, such as the Generalized Fibonacci Sequences
U_{n}
and
V_{n}
.

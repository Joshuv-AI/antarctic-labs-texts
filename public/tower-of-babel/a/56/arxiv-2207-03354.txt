Abstract
. We introduce an intermediate family of Laurent polynomials between Schur’s Q Q -functions and S. Okada’s symplectic Q Q -functions. It can also be regarded as a Q Q -function analogue of Proctor’s intermediate symplectic characters, and is named the family of intermediate symplectic Q Q -functions. We also derive a tableau-sum formula and a Józefiak-Pragacz-type Pfaffian formula of the Laurent polynomials.

Introduction

The theme of this brief note is the investigation of an
intermediate
family between symmetric and symplectic polynomials, i.e., a family of polynomials sitting in the intermediate between the two families of polynomials, one of which is invariant under the action of the Weyl group of root system of type
A
, and another is invariant under the action of the Weyl group of type
C
. A typical example of such an intermediate family is Proctor’s intermediate symplectic Schur polynomials
[
P88
]
, which was introduced as the characters of indecomposable representations of intermediate symplectic Lie groups. The resulting polynomial is denoted as

(0.1)

\displaystyle\spf^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{k}^{\pm 1},x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{W_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k}},

where
k
and
n
are non-negative integers satisfying
k\leq n
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{n})
is a partition of length
\leq n
(see
section

0.1
below for the terminology), the first group of variables
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})
is of type
C
, acted by the Weyl group
W_{k}
of type
C
, and the second group
(x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n})
is of type
A
, acted by the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n-k}
. For the special values of
(k,n-k)
, we have

\spf^{(k,0)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=\spf_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}),\quad\spf^{(0,n)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}),

where
\spf_{\lambda}
is the symplectic Schur polynomial and
s_{\lambda}
is the Schur polynomial, respectively. See
section

1.2
and
section

1.3
for the detail.

The first motivation of our study was to introduce a nice
q
- or
t
-analogue of the intermediate symplectic Schur polynomial which would be an intermediate version of the Macdonald polynomials of type
A
and
C

[
M
]
.
Our trial failed in that direction, but remained alive in the direction to find a “(
t=-1
)-analogue”. To spell out, let us recall Schur’s
Q
-function
Q_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})

[
M95
, III.8]
. It is the specialization of the Hall-Littlewood polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n};t)

[
M95
, III]
at
t=-1
:

(0.2)

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\coloneqq P_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n};-1)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}.

The specialization
t=-1
implies that the polynomial
Q_{\lambda}
is not zero only if
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{n})
is a strict partition, i.e., a strictly decreasing sequence
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dotsb
.

The polynomial (
0.2
) is of course an object of type
A
. As for the corresponding object of type
C
, let us give a brief explanation on the recent work of S. Okada
[
O21a
]
. There he studied the specialization of Macdonald’s zonal spherical polynomial of type
C

[
M71
]
, denoted as
P^{C}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};t_{s},t_{l})
, at
t_{s}=t_{l}=-1
. Here we denoted the two parameters by
t_{s}
and
t_{l}
, indicating that they are attached to the
W_{k}
-orbit of short roots and that of long roots in the root system of type
C
, respectively. The specialized Laurent polynomial is named the symplectic
Q
-function, which we denote as

Q^{C}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})\coloneqq P^{C}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};-1,-1)\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{k}^{\pm 1}]^{W_{k}}.

Here we only need to consider a strict partition
\lambda
as in the case of (
0.2
).

Now it is tempting to consider if there is an intermediate polynomial

(0.3)

\displaystyle Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{k}^{\pm 1},x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{W_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k}}

whose special cases recover Schur’s and symplectic
Q
-functions as

Q^{(k,0)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=Q^{C}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}),\quad Q^{(0,n)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=Q_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}),

so that it can be regarded as a
Q
-function analogue of the intermediate symplectic Schur polynomial (
0.1
).

The purpose of this note is to explain that there exists such a family of polynomials (
0.3
), which we call
the intermediate symplectic
Q
-polynomials
. A direct definition is given by a natural combination of tableau-sum formulas of Schur’s
Q
- and symplectic
Q
-functions (see
2.2.6
). But we make a detour, and take the infinite-variable version as the definition.

Recall that Schur, Hall-Littlewood, Macdonald polynomials and Schur’s Q-functions have their infinite-variable version, or the associated symmetric functions, as developed in
[
M95
]
. The symplectic Schur polynomial and symplectic
Q
-function also have their infinite-variable version, realized by symmetric functions
[
O21b
,
O21a
]
. We start the discussion to introduce
the intermediate symplectic Schur function

\spf^{I}_{\lambda}(X\mid Y)
, which is the infinite-variable version of the intermediate symplectic Schur polynomial (
0.1
). It has two families
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dotsc)
of infinite variables. The
X
-variables correspond to the type
C
variables, and the
Y
-variables to the type
A
variables. See
1.3.1
for the precise definition. The content in
section

1
is a preliminary of the main
section

2
of this note, and also serve complimentary material for Okada’s paper
[
O21b
]
on an application of the intermediate symplectic Schur polynomials to the counting of shifted plane partitions of shifted double staircase shape.

After that, we introduce in
2.2.1

the intermediate symplectic
Q
-function

Q^{I}_{\lambda}(X\mid Y)
which is the infinite-variable version of (
0.3
). We also introduce the skew diagram version
Q^{I}_{\lambda/\mu}(X\mid Y)
. Then we show in
2.2.6
that a particular specialization of variables
X
and
Y
yields a Laurent polynomial enjoying a tableau-sum formula, which is nothing but the finite-variable intermediate symplectic
Q
-polynomial (
0.3
).

In the main
2.3.2
, we give a Józefiak-Pragacz-type Pfaffian formula for the intermediate symplectic
Q
-polynomials (
0.3
):

Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\Pf\begin{bmatrix}M^{I}_{\lambda}&N^{I}_{\lambda,\mu}\\
-{}^{t}\!{N^{I}_{\lambda,\mu}}&O\end{bmatrix},

where
M^{I}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\bigl[Q^{(k,n-k)}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{l}
is the matrix consisting of the intermediate symplectic
Q

(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})
, and
N^{I}_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq\bigl[Q^{(k,n-k)}_{(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{m+1-j})}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\bigr]_{1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq m}
consists of those of one-row diagram
(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{m+1-j})
. Our proof is an application of the Lindström-Gessel-Viennot theorem
[
GV
,
L73
]
to a certain directed graph
\Gamma^{(k,n-k)}
(see
fig.

2.1

2.3.2

Notation and terminology

Here is the list of notations used throughout the text.

(1)

We denote by
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{0,1,2,\dotsc\}
the set of non-negative integers.

(2)

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the symbol
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denotes the symmetric group of degree
n
.

(3)

The symbol
[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
denotes the square matrix of size
n

with
(i,j)
-entry given by
a_{ij}
.

We also use the terminology of partitions, Young diagram and Young tableaux
in the sense of
[
M95
, Chap. I]
. In particular, we use the following terminology.

(4)

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})
means a non-increasing finite sequence of non-negative integers.
We identify two such sequences which differ only by a string of zeros at the end:
(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l},0)=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l},0,\dotsc,0)
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Par}
the set of all partitions.

(5)

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})
,
the non-zero entries
\lambda_{i}
are called the parts of
\lambda
.
The number of parts is called the length of
\lambda
and denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
.
We also denote
\left|{\lambda}\right|\coloneqq\lambda_{1}+\dotsb+\lambda_{l}
and call it the weight of
\lambda
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Par}_{n}\subset\mathrm{Par}
the subset of partitions of weight
n
.

(6)

We use the abbreviation
(m^{n})\coloneqq(\overbrace{m,m,\dotsc,m}^{\text{$n$ times}})
for the iterated parts in a partition.

(7)

A partition
\lambda
is called strict if all the parts are distinct.
We denote by
\mathrm{SPar}\subset\mathrm{Par}
the subset consisting of all strict partitions.

(8)

A partition
\lambda
is identified with the corresponding Young diagram

\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell(\lambda),\ 1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}\},

which is depicted by replacing the lattice points in
S(\lambda)
with unit cells.

(9)

For a partition
\lambda
, we denote by
\lambda^{\prime}
the transpose of
\lambda
.

(10)

A skew diagram is a set-theoretic difference
\lambda/\mu\coloneqq\lambda-\mu
of the Young diagrams corresponding to two partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
.

Finally, let us note:

(11)

We follow the terminology on symmetric functions and symmetric polynomials in
[
M95
, Chap. I]
. In particular, a symmetric polynomial means a finite-variable symmetric polynomial, and a symmetric function means an infinite-variable symmetric “polynomial”. The precise definition will be briefly reviews in
section

1.1
. We also use a non-standard terminology “Schur’s
Q
-polynomial” to mean the finite-variable version of Schur’s
Q
-function. See the paragraph of (
2.4
) for the detail.

1.
Intermediate symplectic Schur functions

1.1.
The ring of symmetric functions

Let us recall the ring of symmetric functions
[
M95
, I.2]
. For an infinite sequence
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
of commuting independent variables, we denote by
\Lambda(X)
the ring of symmetric functions with variables
X

with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. We can regard it as the space of symmetric polynomials of infinite variables
X
, and roughly express its definition as

\Lambda(X)=``{\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}}".

Here is the precise description. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
\Lambda^{(n)}(X)\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
be the commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of
n
-variable symmetric polynomials, where each
\sigma\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts as
x_{i}\mapsto x_{\sigma(i)}
. We denote by
\Lambda^{(n)}(X)=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda^{(n)}_{d}(X)
the grading structure with respect to the degree given by
\deg x_{i}\coloneqq 1
for each
i=1,2,\dotsc
. Then we have the projective system
\{\Lambda_{n}^{d}(X)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}
for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
\Lambda^{(n+1)}_{d}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda^{(n)}_{d}(X)
given by
x_{n+1}\mapsto 0
and other
x_{i}
’s preserved. The projective limit is denoted by
\Lambda_{d}(X)\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda^{(n)}_{d}(X)
, and the graded space

\Lambda(X)\coloneqq\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda^{d}(X)

has a natural structure of graded commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. This is the definition of
\Lambda(X)
. An element of
\Lambda(X)
will be called a symmetric function of variable
X
.
Hereafter we suppress the symbol
X
and denote
\Lambda\coloneqq\Lambda(X)
,
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq\Lambda(X)
and so on if no confusion may arise.
We also denote
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
the ring of symmetric functions with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
.

The projection to the ring of
n
-variable symmetric polynomials is denoted by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\pi^{(n)}_{A}\colon\Lambda(X)\longrightarrow\Lambda^{(n)}(X),\quad\pi^{(n)}_{A}(x_{i})=\begin{cases}x_{i}&(i\leq n)\\
0&(n<i)\end{cases},

and sometimes called the (
n
-variable) truncation. We will also use another ring homomorphism

(1.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{C}^{(n)}\colon\Lambda(X)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{W_{n}},\quad\pi_{C}^{(n)}(x_{i})\coloneqq\begin{cases}x_{i}&(i\leq n)\\
x_{i-n}^{-1}&(n<i\leq 2n)\\
0&(2n<i)\end{cases}.

Here
W_{n}=\mathfrak{S}_{n}\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
denotes the Weyl group of the root system of type
C_{n}
, which acts on
x_{i}
’s by
\sigma(x_{i})=x_{\sigma(i)}
for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
\epsilon_{i}(x_{j})=x_{j}
(
j\neq i
),
\epsilon_{i}(x_{i})=x_{i}^{-1}
for
\epsilon_{i}=(0,\dotsc,0,\overset{i}{1},0,\dotsc,0)\in(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.

Lemma 1.1.1
.

The families of ring homomorphisms
\{\pi^{(n)}_{A}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{\pi^{(n)}_{C}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
enjoy the following property.

(1)

If
f\in\Lambda
satisfies
\pi^{(n)}_{A}(f)=0
for any
n\gg 0
, then
f=0
.

(2)

If
f\in\Lambda
satisfies
\pi^{(n)}_{C}(f)=0
for any
n\gg 0
, then
f=0
.

Proof.

(1) is a consequence of the universality of the projective limit
\Lambda
.
(2) is proved in
[
O21a
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

Finally, we introduce a bivariate version of
\Lambda(X)
. Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dotsc)
be two sequences of commuting independent variables. We define

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda(X\mid Y)\coloneqq\Lambda(X)\otimes\Lambda(Y),

where
\otimes
denotes the tensor product over
\mathbb{Q}
of graded commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras.
Thus,
\Lambda(X\mid Y)
is a graded commutative ring with the grading structure

\Lambda(X\mid Y)=\bigoplus_{c\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda^{c}(X\mid Y),\quad\Lambda^{c}(X\mid Y)=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}d,e\in\mathbb{N}\\
d+e=c\end{subarray}}\Lambda^{d,e}(X\mid Y),\quad\Lambda^{d,e}(X\mid Y)\coloneqq\Lambda^{d}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\Lambda^{e}(Y),

where
\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the ordinary tensor product of linear spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus, given a basis
\{P_{\lambda}(X)\mid\lambda\colon\text{partitions}\}
of
\Lambda(X)
and another
\{Q_{\lambda}(Y)\mid\lambda\colon\text{partitions}\}
of
\Lambda(Y)
, we have the tensor product basis
\{P_{\lambda}(X)\otimes Q_{\mu}(Y)\mid\lambda,\mu\colon\text{partitions}\}
of
\Lambda(X\mid Y)
.

Let us also introduce the tensor product of the ring homomorphisms
\pi^{(n)}_{C}
and
\pi^{(n)}_{A}
.

Definition 1.1.2
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\leq n
, and define a ring homomorphism

\pi^{(k,n-k)}\colon\Lambda(X\mid Y)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{k}^{\pm 1},x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{W_{k}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k}}

by setting

\pi^{(k,n-k)}(x_{i})\coloneqq\begin{cases}x_{i}&(i\leq k)\\
x_{i-k}^{-1}&(k<i\leq 2k)\\
0&(2k<i)\end{cases},\quad\pi^{(k,n-k)}(y_{j})\coloneqq\begin{cases}x_{k+j}&(j\leq n-k)\\
0&(n-k<j)\end{cases}.

Using
1.1.1
, we can immediately show the following statement. We omit the proof.

Lemma 1.1.3
.

If
f\in\Lambda(X\mid Y)
satisfies
\pi^{(k,n-k)}(f)=0
for any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
k,n-k\gg 0
, then we have
f=0
.

1.2.
Schur and symplectic Schur functions

Here we recall some classical bases of
\Lambda
referring to
[
M95
, I.2, I.3]
for the detail. The following bases will be used:

\{s_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}\},\quad\{h_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}\},\quad\{e_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}\},\quad\{p_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par}\},

the family of Schur, completely homogeneous, elementary and power-sum symmetric functions, respectively. Let us first recall the relations

\displaystyle s_{(n)}(X)=h_{n}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\dotsb\leq i_{n}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dotsb x_{i_{n}},

\displaystyle s_{(1^{n})}(X)=e_{n}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dotsb<i_{n}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\dotsb x_{i_{n}}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
These relations are extended to the Jacobi-Trudi formulas
[
M95
, (3.4), (3.5)]
:

(1.4)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(X)=\det[h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}]_{i,j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}=\det[e_{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-i+j}]_{i,j=1}^{\ell(\lambda^{\prime})},

where
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes the transpose of
\lambda
(see
section

0.1

(9)
).

Next, recall the definitions
h_{\lambda}\coloneqq h_{\lambda_{1}}h_{\lambda_{2}}\dotsb h_{\lambda_{l}}
and
e_{\lambda}\coloneqq e_{\lambda_{1}}e_{\lambda_{2}}\dotsb e_{\lambda_{l}}
for a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})
. As for the power-sum, we have
p_{n}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{i\geq 1}x_{i}^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{\lambda}\coloneqq p_{\lambda_{1}}p_{\lambda_{2}}\dotsb p_{\lambda_{l}}
for a partition
\lambda
. Let us also recall that
s_{\lambda}
’s,
h_{\lambda}
’s and
e_{\lambda}
’s are actually bases of the free module
\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Finally, note that
p_{\lambda}
’s form a basis of
\Lambda
since it is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Now let us recall the representation-theoretic fact that the characters of irreducible polynomial modules of the general linear group
\GL_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
are given by

\bigl\{\pi^{(n)}_{A}(s_{\lambda})\in\Lambda^{(n)}(X)\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par},\ \ell(\lambda)\leq n\bigr\}.

We call
s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\coloneqq\pi^{(n)}_{A}(s_{\lambda})
the Schur polynomial as usual.

Let us also recall the Schur functions for skew diagrams
[
M95
, I.5]
. There are several equivalent definitions, and here we only show the one extending the Jacobi-Trudi formulas (
1.4
): For any pair
(\lambda,\mu)
of partitions, we have
s_{\lambda/\mu}\in\Lambda
satisfying the equalities

s_{\lambda/\mu}=\det[h_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}=\det[e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}-\mu^{\prime}_{j}-i+j}]_{i,j=1}^{m}

with
n\geq\max\{\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu)\}
and
m\geq\max\{\ell(\lambda^{\prime}),\ell(\mu^{\prime})\}
.
We also used the convention
h_{r}\coloneqq 0
for
r<0
.
We can recover the ordinary Schur functions as
s_{\lambda}=s_{\lambda/\emptyset}
.
The skew Schur polynomial is defined to be

(1.5)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\coloneqq\pi^{(n)}_{A}(s_{\lambda/\mu})\in\Lambda^{(n)}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}.

If
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfy
\lambda\supset\mu
and
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
,
then the skew Schur polynomial has the following tableau-sum formula:

(1.6)

\displaystyle s_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{T\in\SpT^{0,n}(\lambda/\mu)}x^{T}.

Here
\SpT^{0,n}(\theta)
denotes the set of all semi-standard tableaux of shape
\theta
with entries from the totally ordered set
\{1<2<\dotsb<n\}
. See
[
M95
, I.5, (5.12)]
for the detail, and also
1.3.3
for a generalization.

Finally we recall the symplectic Schur functions
[
KT87
, Definition 2.1.1]
. For a partition
\lambda
, we define

\displaystyle s^{C}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\det[h_{\lambda_{i}-i+j}+h_{\lambda_{i}-i-j+2}]_{i,j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\in\Lambda.

Note that the
(i,1)
-entry of the matrix is
2h_{\lambda_{i}}
, and we actually have
s^{C}_{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{Z}}
. By the ring homomorphism
\pi_{C}^{(n)}\colon\Lambda(X)\to\mathbb{Q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{W_{n}}
in (
1.2
), we obtain the symplectic Schur polynomials

(1.7)

\displaystyle s^{C}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\pi^{(n)}_{C}(s^{C}_{\lambda}).

More generally, we have the skew symplectic Schur polynomials
s^{C}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
for partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
. If
\lambda\supset\mu
and
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
, then we have the tableaux formula

(1.8)

\displaystyle s^{C}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{T\in\SpT^{n,0}(\lambda/\mu)}x^{T}.

Here
\SpT^{n,0}(\theta)
denotes the set of all symplectic tableaux of shape
\theta
introduced by King
[
K76
]
. See
1.3.3
below for an explanation.

The symplectic Schur function
s_{\lambda}^{C}\in\Lambda
is a infinite-variable version of the irreducible character of the symplectic group in the following sense.
Recall the ring homomorphism
\pi_{C}^{(n)}\colon\Lambda(X)\to\mathbb{Q}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dotsc,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{W_{n}}
in (
1.2
). Then the characters of irreducible rational module of the symplectic group
\Sp_{2n}
over
\mathbb{C}
are given by

\bigl\{\pi_{C}^{(n)}(s_{\lambda}^{C})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{Par},\ \ell(\lambda)\leq n\bigr\}.

1.3.
Intermediate symplectic Schur functions

Let us continue to use the symbols in the previous
section

1.1
. In particular,
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dotsc)
denote the infinite sequences of independent variables, and
\Lambda(X\mid Y)
denotes the ring of bivariant symmetric functions (
1.3
). In this subsection, we introduce the family of elements of
\Lambda(X\mid Y)
of lifting the intermediate symplectic characters.

Definition 1.3.1
.

For each partition
\lambda
,
we define an element
s^{I}_{\lambda}(X\mid Y)\in\Lambda(X\mid Y)
by

s^{I}_{\lambda}(X\mid Y)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\colon\text{partitions}\\
\mu\subset\lambda\end{subarray}}s^{C}_{\mu}(X)s_{\lambda/\mu}(Y),

and call it
the intermediate symplectic Schur function
.

The name of
s^{I}_{\lambda}(X|Y)
originates in the following
1.3.5
. To state it, we need several preparation. Let us first recall the algebraic group
\Sp_{2k,n-k}
introduced by Proctor
[
P88
]

Proposition 1.3.5
.

Let
\lambda

is uniquely characterized by the following property: For

where

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from

of

By (

which is by the tableau-sum formula (

) and (

) equal to

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\subset\lambda}\sum_{T_{C}\in\SpT^{k,0}(\mu)}x^{T_{C}}\sum_{T_{A}\in\SpT^{0,n-k}([k+1,n];\lambda/\mu)}x^{T_{A}},

Now recall that we have a natural embedding of graded rings

For any partition
\lambda

Proof.

By

, Corollary 2.6]
, for any partition

we have

2.1.
Schur’s
Q
-functions and symplectic

Here we give a summary on Schur’s
Q
-function
[
M95
, III.8]
and its symplectic analogue, the symplectic
Q
-function
[
O21a
, §3]
.

In the ring
\Lambda=\Lambda(X)
of symmetric functions with variables
X=(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc)
, we define
q^{A}_{r}\in\Lambda
(
r\in\mathbb{N}
) by the generating series as

\sum_{r\geq 0}q^{A}_{r}z^{r}=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1+x_{i}z}{1-x_{i}z}.

We denote by
\Gamma\subset\Lambda
the graded subalgebra generated by
q_{r}
’s, and set
\Gamma_{d}\coloneqq\Lambda_{d}\cap\Gamma
.

Now, we define
Q^{A}_{\lambda}\in\Lambda
for each
strict
partition
\lambda
(see
section

0.1
,
(7)
) inductively on the length
\ell(\lambda)
. In the case of length
0,1,2
, we set

(2.1)

\displaystyle Q^{A}_{\emptyset}\coloneqq 1,\quad Q^{A}_{(r)}\coloneqq q^{A}_{r}\quad(r>0),\quad Q^{A}_{(r,s)}\coloneqq q^{A}_{r}q^{A}_{s}+2\sum_{k=1}^{s}(-1)^{k}q_{r+k}q_{r-k}\quad(r>s>0).

Then, in the case of length
\geq 3
, we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q^{A}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\Pf\bigl[Q^{A}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}\bigr]_{1\leq i,j\leq m}.

Here
\Pf
denotes the Pfaffian of an even-size skew-symmetric matrix, and
m\coloneqq\ell(\lambda)
or
\ell(\lambda)+1
according whether
\ell(\lambda)
is even or odd. In the case
\ell(\lambda)
is odd, we put
\lambda_{m}\coloneqq 0
. Finally, for the entries of the matrix, we used the convention
Q^{A}_{(r,0)}\coloneqq Q^{A}_{(r)}
,
Q^{A}_{(s,r)}\coloneqq Q^{A}_{(r,s)}
for
s>r
, and
Q^{A}_{(r,r)}\coloneqq 0
for
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the family
\{Q^{A}_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\colon\text{partitions}\}
is a basis of
\Gamma
. We call
Q^{A}_{\lambda}(X)

Schur’s
Q
-function
.

Let us also recall that Schur’s
Q
-function can be extended to the skew diagrams. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{m})
be strict partitions such that
\lambda_{l}>0
and
\mu_{m}\geq 0
. We may assume that
l+m
is even since we can replace
m
by
m+1
if
l+m
is odd. Then we define

(2.3)

\displaystyle Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}\coloneqq\Pf\begin{bmatrix}M^{A}_{\lambda}&N^{A}_{\lambda,\mu}\\
-{}^{t}\!{N^{A}_{\lambda,\mu}}&O\end{bmatrix},

where
M^{A}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\bigl[Q^{A}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}\bigr]_{1\leq i,j\leq l}
as in (
2.2
), and
N^{A}_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq\bigl[q^{A}_{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{m+j-1}}\bigr]_{1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq m}
. We call
Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}

skew Schur’s
Q
-function
. We can recover (
2.2
) by
Q^{A}_{\lambda/\emptyset}=Q^{A}_{\lambda}
. The Pfaffian formula (
2.3
) is originally due to Józefiak and Pragacz
[

]
. See
[
M95
, III.8, Exercise 9]
for a brief account, and
[

, §6, Theorem 6.2]
for a combinatorial proof based on the Lindström-Gessel-Viennot theorem
[
GV
,
L73
]
.

For strict partitions
\lambda,\mu
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
, the truncation
\pi^{(n)}_{A}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{(n)}
in (

) yields
skew Schur’s
Q
-polynomial

(2.4)

\displaystyle Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\coloneqq\pi^{(n)}_{A}(Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}).

We denote
Q^{A}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\coloneqq Q^{A}_{\lambda/\emptyset}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
and call it
Schur’s
Q
-polynomial
. The polynomial

vanishes unless

.

For strict partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfying

, Schur’s

-polynomial has the tableau-sum formula

\displaystyle Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{T\in\QT^{(0,n)}(\lambda/\mu)}x^{T},

where

denotes the set of marked shifted tableaux of shape

in the sense of
[
M95
, III.8]
. We refer to

for the definition of a marked shifted tableau. As for the symbol

, we have:

.

Let

and

be partitions.

(1)

The

First we define

(

\displaystyle Q^{C}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\Pf\bigl(Q_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}\bigr)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}.

if it is even, and by

otherwise. Except the summation term for

). We call the obtained family

-functions

We also have

-function

, (3.14)]

such that

\mu_{m}\geq 0

\displaystyle Q^{C}_{\lambda/\mu}\coloneqq\Pf\begin{bmatrix}M^{C}_{\lambda}&N^{C}_{\lambda,\mu}\\
-{}^{t}\!{N^{C}_{\lambda,\mu}}&O\end{bmatrix},

where
M^{C}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\bigl[Q^{C}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}\bigr]_{1\leq i,j\leq l}

We can recover (

) by

.

Let

). For strict partitions
\lambda

Let

x^{T}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{m(i^{\prime})+m(i)-m(\overline{i}^{\prime})-m(\overline{i})}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}x_{j}^{m(j^{\prime})+m(j)},

where
m(\gamma)
denotes the multiplicity of the entry
\gamma
in
T
.
If
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
, then we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{T\in\QT^{(k,n-k)}(\lambda/\mu)}x^{T}.

Proof.

As in the proof of
1.3.5
, the left hand side of (
2.10
) is equal to

\sum_{\nu}Q^{C}_{\nu/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n}),

where the sum is taken over the strict partitions
\nu
such that
\mu\subset\nu\subset\lambda
. By the tableau-sum formulas (
2.5
) and (
2.8
), it is equal to

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}\sum_{T_{C}\in\QT^{(k,0)}(\nu/\mu)}x^{T_{C}}\sum_{T_{A}\in\QT^{(0,n-k)}([k+1,n];\lambda/\nu)}x^{T_{A}},

where
\QT^{(0,n-k)}([k+1,n];\lambda/\nu)
denotes the set of marked shifted tableaux of shape
S(\lambda/\nu)
with entries from the totally ordered set
\{k+1<(k+1)^{\prime}<\dotsb<n^{\prime}<n\}
. By
2.2.4
and
2.2.5
, the product set
\QT^{(k,0)}(\nu/\mu)\times\QT^{(0,n-k)}([k+1,n];\lambda/\nu)
is equal to the subset of
\QT^{(k,n-k)}(\lambda/\mu)
consisting of tableaux whose entries
1^{\prime},1,\overline{1}^{\prime},\overline{1},\dotsc,k^{\prime},k,\overline{k}^{\prime},\overline{k}
occupy the shifted skew diagram
S(\nu/\mu)
. Thus, the summation (
2.11
) is equal to the right hand side of (
2.10
).
∎

2.3.
Formulas of intermediate symplectic
Q
-polynomials

Here we study basic properties of the (Laurent) polynomial
Q^{k:n-k}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
. We may take (
2.10
) for its definition:

Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{T\in\QT^{(k,n-k)}(\lambda/\mu)}x^{T}.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x=(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
be as in
2.2.6
.
Also, let
\lambda
and
\mu
be strict partitions with
\lambda\supset\mu
.

(1)

For an additional indeterminate
z
,
we have the following equality of formal series.

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}Q^{(k,n-k)}_{(l)}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})z^{l}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1+x_{i}z}{1-x_{i}z}\frac{1+x_{i}^{-1}z}{1-x_{i}^{-1}z}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\frac{1+x_{j}z}{1-x_{j}z}.

(2)

We have

(2.12)

\displaystyle Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q^{C}_{\mu^{(i)}/\mu^{(i-1)}}(x_{i})\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}Q^{A}_{\mu^{(j)}/\mu^{(j-1)}}(x_{j}),

where the sum is taken over all the sequences

\mu=\mu^{(0)}\subset\mu^{(1)}\subset\dotsb\subset\mu^{(n-1)}\subset\mu^{(n)}=\lambda
.

(3)

For each single variable
x_{i}
with
i=1,\dotsc,k
, we have

Q^{C}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{i})=\begin{cases}0&(\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)>1)\\
\det\bigl[Q^{C}_{(\lambda_{l}-\mu_{m})}(x_{i})\bigr]_{l,m=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}&(\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)\leq 1)\end{cases}

with the convention
Q^{C}_{(r)}(x)=0
for
r<0
. Similarly, for
j=k+1,\dotsc,n
, we have

Q^{A}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}0&(\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)>1)\\
\det\bigl[Q^{A}_{(\lambda_{l}-\mu_{m})}(x_{j})\bigr]_{l,m=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}&(\ell(\lambda)-\ell(\mu)\leq 1)\end{cases}.

Proof.

(1)

We denote the left hand side by
q(z)
, and take
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\in\QT^{(k,n-k)}\bigl((l)\bigr)
. Then for each
i=1,\dotsc,n
, the letter
i^{\prime}
can appear at most once in
T
by the condition (QT3) in
2.2.4
,
and the letter
i
can appear in arbitrary times. Thus the variable
x_{i}
contributes

(1+x_{i}z)(1+x_{i}z+x_{i}^{2}z^{2}+\dotsb)=(1+x_{i}z)/(1-x_{i}z)
to the generating series
q(z)
.
Similarly, for each
j\in\{1,\dotsc,k\}
,
the letter
\overline{j}^{\prime}
can appear at most once in
T
by the condition (QT4) in
2.2.4
,
and the letter
\overline{j}
can appear in arbitrary times. Thus the variable
x_{j}
contributes

(1+x_{j}^{-1}z)(1+x_{j}^{-1}z+x_{j}^{-2}z^{2}+\dotsb)=(1+x_{j}^{-1}z)/(1-x_{j}^{-1}z)
to
q(z)
.
Hence we have the claim.

(2)

We obtain the formula by decomposing a tableau
T\in\QT^{(k,n-k)}(\lambda/\mu)
in the definition (
2.10
) into the subtableaux consisting of
i^{\prime},i,\overline{i}^{\prime},\overline{i}
for
i=1,\dotsc,k
, and of
j^{\prime},j
for
j=k+1,\dotsc,n
.

(3)

The first half of the statement is shown in
[
O21a
, Lemma 4.4, (2), (3)]
. The second half can also be shown by a similar argument in loc. cit.

∎

Theorem 2.3.2
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
k\leq n
, and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{l})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{m})
be strict partitions satisfying
\lambda\supset\mu
,
\lambda_{l}>0
,
\mu\geq 0

l\geq 2
and
l+m

Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda/\mu}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\Pf\begin{bmatrix}M^{I}_{\lambda}&N^{I}_{\lambda,\mu}\\
-{}^{t}\!{N^{I}_{\lambda,\mu}}&O\end{bmatrix},

where
M^{I}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\bigl[Q^{(k,n-k)}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{l}

We use an “intermediate” modification of Stembridge’s variation

3.
Concluding remarks and questions

At this moment, we only know the properties of intermediate symplectic
Q
-polynomials given in
2.3.1
and
2.3.2
. We expect several other properties from those for Schur’s and symplectic
Q
-polynomials. Below we list them in the form of open questions.

Question 1
.

Is there a good formula of
Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
with
\ell(\lambda)=2
?
Combined with (
2.12
) and
2.3.2
, it will yield a recursive definition of
Q^{(k,n-k)}_{\lambda}
for general
\lambda
.

Question 2
.

Schur’s
Q
-function
Q^{A}_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
can be presented as a ratio of Pfaffians, known as Nimmo’s formula
[
N90
, (A13)]
. A similar formula is shown for symplectic
Q
-functions by Okada
[
O21a
, Proposition 2.2]
. Is there a Nimmo-type formula for intermediate symplectic
Q
-functions?

Question 3
.

Okada established in
[
O20
]
a theory of generalized Schur’s
P
- and
Q
-functions. Actually, the theory of symplectic
Q
-functions in
[
O21a
]
is based on that theory. Also, this theory can be regarded as a
Q
-function analogue of Macdonald’s ninth variation of Schur polynomials
[
M92
]
. Is it possible to further generalize the theory to include our intermediate symplectic
Q
-functions? Such a framework will give answers to the above questions automatically.

Question 4
.

We expect that the situation is quite simplified in the case
n-k=1
, i.e., when the number of type
A
variable is one. Some of the questions above might be attacked in this case.

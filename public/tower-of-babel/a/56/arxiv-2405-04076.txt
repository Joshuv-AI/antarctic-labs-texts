2d Sinh-Gordon model on the infinite cylinder
Colin Guillarmou, Trishen S. Gunaratnam, Vincent Vargas

Abstract
. For R > 0 R>0 , we give a rigorous probabilistic construction on the cylinder ℝ × ( ℝ / ( 2 ​ π ​ R ​ ℤ ) ) \mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/(2\pi R\mathbb{Z})) of the (massless) Sinh-Gordon model. 1.
Introduction

Over the past 15 years, there has been huge progress in the rigorous understanding of two-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs) defined formally via a path integral with exponential interactions. An important class of these models are non-affine Toda theories associated to complex, semisimple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
. These models arise as the quantization of the classical Toda field theories with potentials of the form

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\gamma x\cdot e_{i}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
(or, more generally,
\mathbb{C}
) is a coupling constant,
(e_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
and
\cdot
are a special choice of basis vectors and scalar product on a
n
-dimensional vector space, related to the representation theory of
\mathfrak{g}
(in particular, their Cartan subalgebras). They are well-studied in the physics literature thanks to the emergence of higher order symmetry, so-called
W
-algebras, which contain the usual Virasoro algebra of CFT. The case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
corresponds to the simplest such theory: the celebrated Liouville CFT introduced by Polyakov
[
Pol81
]
. The probabilistic construction of the path integral formulation of these CFTs when
\gamma\in(0,2)
has been developed thoroughly (see
[
DKRV16
,
GRV19
,
CRV23
]
). In the case of Liouville, a series of works
[
KRV20
,
GKRV24
,
GKRV
]
has shown the equivalence between the probabilistic construction and the bootstrap formalism of physics
[
DO94
,
ZZ96
]
. The case of
\gamma\in i(0,\sqrt{2})
has been also recently developed
[
GKR25
]
in the compactified version.

On the other hand, in the case of affine Toda theories, much less is known on both mathematical and physical fronts. Affine models correspond to quantizations of the classical field theories with potentials of the form

(1.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e^{\gamma x\cdot e_{i}}+e^{-\gamma x\cdot e_{i}}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

i.e. including the reflected term. In this case, the quantization leads to a quantum field theory (QFT) that is not expected to exhibit conformal invariance (i.e. it is not a CFT), but their significance in physics stems from the expectation that they remain
integrable
in an appropriate sense. The simplest QFT in this class is the Sinh-Gordon model and corresponds to the choices
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Amongst its most interesting features are the existence of an isolated first eigenvalue in its spectrum, leading to a mass gap and exponential decay of correlations – this is in stark contrast to the purely continuous spectrum of Liouville. Furthermore, the study of its finer properties is an active area of research and controversy in physics. We refer to
[
KLM21
]
, and references therein, for a more in-depth survey of physics results and research directions on the Sinh-Gordon model. The Sinh-Gordon model is closely related to the Sine-Gordon model, which corresponds to choosing
\gamma=i\beta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. The Sine-Gordon model is ubiquitous in statistical physics. For example, it arises in the Coleman correspondence (see
[
BW24
]
). Let us finally also mention that affine Toda models associated to exceptional Lie algebras (
E_{8}
,
E_{7}
, etc) arise in consideration of near-critical scaling limits of planar statistical physics models, such as the Ising model, see
[
Mus10
, Chapter 16]
.

1.1.
The Sinh-Gordon model

Let
\mu>0
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{R}:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{R}
the infinite cylinder of width
R>0
, with
\mathbb{T}_{R}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi R\mathbb{Z}
the circle of radius
R
.
The Sinh-Gordon model with parameters
\mu
(called the cosmological constant) and
\gamma
on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is the probability measure
\langle\cdot\rangle
formally defined via the following path integral

(1.3)

\langle F\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int F(\phi)e^{-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\frac{1}{4\pi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\mu\cosh(\gamma\phi))dx}D\phi,

where the integral should be over some space
\Sigma
of fields (functions or distributions) on the cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{R}
equipped with a measure
D\phi
that represents the “uniform measure”,

F
is a bounded and measurable function on the space
\Sigma
of fields, and
Z
is a normalisation constant making
D\phi/Z
a probability measure.
In this context, the main objects of interest are the so-called vertex correlations associated to the Sinh-Gordon model, which are formally defined via the formula

(1.4)

\langle\prod_{j=1}^{m}V_{\alpha_{j}}(z_{j})\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int\prod_{j=1}^{m}e^{\alpha_{j}\phi(z_{j})}e^{-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\frac{1}{4\pi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\mu\cosh(\gamma\phi))dx}D\phi,

where above
z_{1},\dots,z_{m}
is a set of
m
disjoint points in
\mathcal{C}_{R}
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
some weights. In the physics literature the points
z_{j}
are called
insertions with weights
\alpha_{j}
. The vertex correlations are natural observables for the theory since, as Laplace transforms of
\phi
, they encode the distribution of the field
\phi
and we will also see that they enjoy natural scaling properties with respect to
R
.

Since
D\phi
does not exist mathematically, the above definition (
1.3
) is indeed formal and making sense of the path integral is not straightforward. The purpose of this article is to define (
1.3
) rigorously using probability theory. It turns out that the random field
\phi
underlying the construction will live in a negative Sobolev space
\Sigma=H^{-s}(\mathcal{C}_{R})
, as is customary in the probabilistic approach to quantum field theory (also called constructive quantum field theory). Therefore, the averages of the form (
1.3
) will be defined for bounded
F:H^{-s}(\mathcal{C}_{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and the correlations (
1.4
) will require a renormalisation procedure since the field
\phi
will not be defined pointwise. In order to present a concrete probabilistic construction of the above formal path integrals, we must first introduce the probabilistic construction of the Hilbert space and the Hamiltonian of the theory for
R=1
. We will see that the general case can be deduced by a scaling argument. The construction is based on the
Gaussian Free Field
on the circle.

Convention
: In the sequel of the paper, when working in the
R=1
case, we will omit the subscript or superscript
R
in the notations. For instance
\mathcal{C}_{1}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}
.

1.2.
Main result on the Sinh-Gordon Hamiltonian

The construction of the measure and of the correlation functions is based on the theory of
Gaussian multiplicative chaos
and the spectral resolution of the Hamiltonian of the Sinh-Gordon model, that we now explain.
The Hamiltonian is a self-adjoint operator acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, that can be obtained as a generator of a Markov semigroup. As we shall explain later, the theory on the cylinder
\mathcal{C}_{R}
can be reduced by a scaling argument to the theory for

for now, and we will write the general case in terms of the

The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}),\mu_{0}).

Above,
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
,

is the Sobolev space of order

on the circle

, and
\mu_{0}
is the law

For fixed

The process

When acting on the linear span

{\bf H}=-\frac{\partial_{c}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(\partial_{x_{n}}^{*}\partial_{x_{n}}+\partial_{y_{n}}^{*}\partial_{y_{n}})+\mu(e^{\gamma c}V_{+}+e^{-\gamma c}V_{-}),

Although related, we warn the reader that

When

In order to construct the Sinh-Gordon theory for

{\bf H}
.
This is no restriction since

We summarize the diagonalization result, along with other spectral properties of the Hamiltonian, in the following theorem (as above, we will fix

and work on
H^{-s}(\mathbb{T})

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu>0
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
. The Hamiltonian
{\bf H}
generating the semigroup
{\bf T}_{t}
is a self-adjoint, positive operator acting on a dense domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{H})\subset\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore, the following properties hold:

(1)

The operator
\mathbf{H}
has discrete spectrum
(\lambda_{j})_{j\;\geqslant\;0}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
with complete basis of normalised eigenfunctions
(\psi_{j})_{j\;\geqslant\;0}
, the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
is simple and strictly positive, and there is a unique associated normalised eigenfunction
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, which is positive

-almost everywhere. We call
\psi_{0}
the ground state.

(2)

For every
j\;\geqslant\;0
and
N\;\geqslant\;0
, the eigenfunctions
\psi_{j}
belong to the weighted
L^{p}
spaces
e^{-N|c|}L^{p}(H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}),\mu_{0})
.

(3)

One has the following diagonalization result

(1.8)

e^{-t{\bf H}}f(c+\varphi)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\psi_{j}(c+\varphi)\langle f,\psi_{j}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
3
. Although some of the techniques involved are reminiscent of the construction of the Hamiltonian associated to Liouville CFT in
[
GKRV24
]
, the treatment of the spectral properties is somehow different
from the Liouville case, as the analogue of

does
not
have discrete spectrum. Ultimately, the difference boils down to the fact that the potential in the Liouville Hamiltonian decays when the average of the field (the zero mode)

In the sequel, we will sometimes write
\lambda_{j}^{\mu}
to stress the dependence in
\mu

We now turn to the vertex correlations. In order to state our theorem, we will need the notion of

Insertions are disjoint points

Given a set
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{C}
of insertions and

, we write

2.
Gaussian free field and Gaussian multiplicative chaos

3.1.
Hilbert space and Free Hamiltonian

We first recall the construction of the Hilbert space and definition and basic properties of the Free Hamiltonian. Recall that

The Hilbert space of the theory is

We now rigorously define the Free Hamiltonian following
[
GKRV24

denote the operator acting on

defined by

\mathbf{P}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(\mathbf{X}_{n}^{*}\mathbf{X}_{n}+\mathbf{Y}_{n}^{*}\mathbf{Y}_{n}),

where, for

(3.4)

\mathbf{X}_{n}=\partial_{x_{n}},\qquad\mathbf{X}_{n}^{*}=-\partial_{x_{n}}+x_{n},\qquad\mathbf{Y}_{n}=\partial_{y_{n}},\qquad\mathbf{Y}_{n}^{*}=-\partial_{y_{n}}+y_{n}.

Here the adjoints are the formal adjoints on

with respect to the Gaussian measure

on the dense subspace

(

First, we define the operator

Lemma 3.2

Proof.

The norm estimate follows from the almost sure bound

To prove it is a semigroup, we first use the Markov property and the conformal invariance of the GFF on

.

Finally, let us describe the integral kernel of the propagator.

Lemma 3.7
.

1) For

can be written under the form

for

where the integral kernel is the measurable function for

and

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\mathbb{E}_{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}}\Big[e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)}\Big]

where
\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}=X_{\mathbb{A}_{t},D}+P_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c+\varphi,c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})
with

\mathbb{A}_{t}
and

\mathbb{A}_{t}

(c+\varphi,c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})
on the boundary
\mathbb{T}
and
e^{-t}\mathbb{T}
, the expectation is taken with respect to

, and the function
\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}

\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}(\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-2tn})^{-1})e^{-\frac{(c-c^{\prime})^{2}}{2t}}e^{-\sum_{n}\frac{1}{4\sinh(tn)}(q_{tn}(x_{n},x_{n}^{\prime})+q_{tn}(y_{n},y_{n}))}

where
\varphi(\theta)=\sum_{n\not=0}\frac{x_{n}+{\rm sign}(n)iy_{n}}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{in\theta}

\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)=\sum_{n\not=0}\frac{x_{n}^{\prime}+{\rm sign}(n)iy_{n}^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{in\theta}
.

2) The integral kernel
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}\in L^{2}((H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}))^{2},\mu_{0}^{\otimes 2})
, the operator

is Hilbert-Schmidt and thus compact on

.

Proof.

First, by
[

, Proposition 6.1]
(see Equation (6.9) there), the integral kernel of

\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}

satisfies
\phi(0,\cdot)=c+\varphi
, then by the Feynman-Kac representation (
3.7
), for each

we have (with

)

\begin{split}e^{-t{\bf H}}F(c+\varphi)=&\mathbb{E}[F(c+B_{t}+\varphi_{t})e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}e^{\sigma\gamma c}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(dx)}]\\
&=\mathbb{E}[F(c+B_{t}+\varphi_{t})H_{t}(c+\varphi,c+\varphi_{t})]\end{split}

with
H_{t}
defined as the conditional expectation

H_{t}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}):=\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}e^{\sigma\gamma c}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(dx)}\,\Big|\,\varphi_{0}=\varphi,B_{t}+\varphi_{t}=c^{\prime}-c+\varphi^{\prime}\Big].

Now, using the Markov property of the GFF,
X_{\mathbb{D}}|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}=X_{\mathbb{A}_{t},D}+P_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(0,X_{\mathbb{D}}|_{e^{-t}\mathbb{T}})
with

with boundary values

on
\mathbb{T}
and
f_{2}
on

, therefore
we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}}\Big[e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}e^{\sigma\gamma c}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)}\Big]=H_{t}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

since
(X_{\mathbb{D}}+P\varphi)|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}=X_{\mathbb{A}_{t},D}+P(\varphi,c^{\prime}-c+\varphi^{\prime})=\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}-c
. Thus (
3.20
) is proved.

Let us remark that for
s>0
, we have that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\sinh(s)}q_{s}(x,x^{\prime})\;\leqslant\;C_{0}\Big(\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{(x^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\Big)

for every

. This can be established by considering the matrix associated to the difference of these quadratic forms and computing the eigenvalues. The optimal such

for us will be the lower bound,
C_{0}=\frac{1}{1+e^{s}}
. Taking

, we then compute for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{p}{4\sinh(tn)}(q_{tn}(x_{n},x_{n}^{\prime}))}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2}-\frac{{x^{\prime}_{n}}^{2}}{2}}\frac{dx_{n}dx_{n}^{\prime}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2(1-pC_{0})^{-1}}-\frac{{x^{\prime}_{n}}^{2}}{2(1-pC_{0})^{-1}}}\frac{dx_{n}dx_{n}^{\prime}}{2\pi}=\Big(1-\frac{p}{1+e^{tn}}\Big)^{-1}.

This implies that for
p\in[2,1+e^{t})
, there is
C(t)>0
depending only on

and

(3.22)

\|\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\cdot,c^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}^{2})}=\frac{e^{-\frac{(c-c^{\prime})^{2}}{2t}}}{\sqrt{2\pi t}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e^{-2tn})}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(1-\frac{p}{1+e^{tn}}\Big)^{-1}\;\leqslant\;C(t)e^{-\frac{(c-c^{\prime})^{2}}{2t}}.

Let
\bar{c}=(c+c^{\prime})/2
and write
P_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,c^{\prime})=\bar{c}+P_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c-\bar{c},c^{\prime}-\bar{c})

|P_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c-\bar{c},c^{\prime}-\bar{c})|\;\leqslant\;|c-c^{\prime}|
.
Using that for each
\theta>0
, there is
C_{\theta}
such that
e^{-x}\;\leqslant\;C_{\theta}x^{-\theta}
for all

,
we can bound the potential term by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}}\Big[e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\theta}\mu^{-\theta}e^{\gamma\theta\bar{c}}e^{\gamma\theta|c-c^{\prime}|}\textbf{1}_{\bar{c}\;\leqslant\;0}\mathbb{E}_{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}}\Big[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{-,\mathbb{C}}(X_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)\Big)^{-\theta}\Big],

where

. Let

be a ball with boundary
not intersecting

. The random variable

has finite moments of any order and by Lemma
2.4

depending on

such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\prod_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int_{B}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(X_{\mathbb{A}_{t},D},dx)\Big)^{-\theta}\Big]\;\leqslant\;C_{\theta,t}.

Consequently, for all
q\in[1,\infty)
there is
C_{\theta,t,q}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}}\Big[e^{-\mu\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)}\Big]\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}\;\leqslant\;C_{\theta,t,q}e^{-\gamma\theta|\bar{c}|}e^{\gamma\theta|c-c^{\prime}|}.

Combining with (
3.22
) and using Hölder inequality with

and

, we deduce that
there is

and

such that

\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\cdot,c^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}^{2})}\;\leqslant\;C_{\theta,t}e^{-\gamma\theta|\bar{c}|}e^{\gamma\theta|c-c^{\prime}|}e^{-\frac{(c-c^{\prime})^{2}}{2t}}\;\leqslant\;C^{\prime}_{\theta,t}e^{-\gamma\theta(|c+c^{\prime}|+|c-c^{\prime}|)}.

This function is in

thus we have proved that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}\in L^{2}((H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}))^{2},\mu_{0}^{\otimes 2})
and therefore the operator

is Hilbert-Schmidt and compact on
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

We obtain as a corollary that the resolvent of
{\bf H}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}
:

Corollary 3.8
.

The operator

\mathbf{R}_{+}=({\bf H}+1)^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\mathbf{H}-t}dt

is compact on
\mathcal{H}
,
{\bf H}
has discrete spectrum and an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions
(\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}\;\geqslant\;0
.

Proof.

By the spectral theorem, it follows that

\mathbf{R}_{+}=\lim_{t\to 0}e^{-t{\bf H}}\mathbf{R}_{+}

in operator norm on
\mathcal{H}
. Since
e^{-t{\bf H}}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}
, the same is true for
e^{-t{\bf H}}\mathbf{R}_{+}
.
This proves that
\mathbf{R}_{+}
is compact since the space of compact operators is closed in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. The resolvent

\mathbf{R}(\lambda)=({\bf H}-\lambda)^{-1}
for
\lambda\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}

satisfies the identity

\mathbf{R}(\lambda)(1-(1+\lambda)\mathbf{R}_{+})=\mathbf{R}_{+}.

By the analytic Fredholm theorem we see that
(1-(1+\lambda)\mathbf{R}_{+})
is invertible outside a discrete set of poles. Therefore

\mathbf{R}(\lambda)
extends to a meromorphic family in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.
The spectrum of
{\bf H}
is then discrete and given by the poles of
\mathbf{R}(\lambda)
, all contained in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
The eigenfunctions of
{\bf H}
coincide with those of
\mathbf{R}_{+}
, which form an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

3.4.
Properties of eigenfunctions and proof of Theorem
1.1

The semigroup
(e^{-t\mathbf{H}})_{t\;\geqslant\;0}

and

such that

Lemma 3.9

The semigroup

and for all

\mu_{0}
-a.e. and

is not identically

\mu_{0}
-a.e.,

As a consequence, the multiplicity of the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
{\bf H}
is equal to

and the

\psi_{0}
associated with

Proof.

Let
F\in\mathcal{H}
such that
F\;\geqslant\;0

\mu_{0}
-almost everywhere and is not identically 0 (almost everywhere).
Thus there exists a Borel set

of strictly positive and finite measure such that

for
\mu_{0}
-a.e.

Note that

By Lemma

\begin{split}e^{-t{\bf H}}F(c+\varphi)=&\int_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{T})}\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})F(c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})d\mu_{0}(c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})\\
\;\geqslant\;&\int_{A}\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}(c,\varphi,c^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})F(c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})d\mu_{0}(c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime}).\end{split}

Since for a.e.

\displaystyle\infty=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[1_{B_{n}}(c+\varphi)1_{A_{n}}(c^{\prime}+\varphi^{\prime})\Big(\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}|x|^{-\gamma Q}M_{\gamma}^{\sigma,\mathbb{C}}(\phi_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},dx)\Big)^{p}\Big]dcdc^{\prime}

The statement about simplicity of smallest eigenvalue and the positivity of the ground state follows from the positivity improving property of

4.
Construction of the Sinh-Gordon model and one-point function

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, which address the construction of the model and its vertex correlations respectively.
Theorem
1.2
is proved in Sections
4.2
-
4.3
. Theorem
1.3
is proved in Section
4.4
. Unless stated otherwise, we fix
\mu>0
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
and drop them from the notation.

4.1.
State space for the models

Let
R>0
and
s>0
fixed. Denote by
C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
the space of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}
into
H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R})
. We are going to define the Sinh-Gordon models on this space.
Note that, for some fixed
s>0
, there is a canonical continuous injection

C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{R})

into the space of distributions. Via pushforward of these injections, we obtain a measure on
D^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{R})
. In the sequel we shall identify these measures and ignore this subtlety. In order to define these measures, we identify a sufficiently rich ring of observables, corresponding to compactly supported functions, on which we can construct a candidate premeasure by probabilistic techniques. We then extend them to a full probability measure by Carathéodory extension and controlling the normalization. We now make this precise.

Given
I\subset\mathbb{R}
an interval and
\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
, denote by
\phi|_{I}
the restricted function in
C(I,H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
.
We say that
F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
I
-measurable if there exists

F^{I}:C(I,H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle F(\phi)=F^{I}(\phi|_{I}),\qquad\forall\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R})).

Henceforth, we identify
F
and
F^{I}
. We say that
F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is of compact support if there exists a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
of finite length such that
F
is
I
-measurable. We then define the support of
F
to be the smallest closed interval
I
such that
F
is
I
-measurable. Let
\mathcal{F}^{R}
denote the ring of bounded measurable functions
F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that are of compact support. Note that
\mathcal{F}^{R}
generates the full
\sigma
-algebra in light of the topology of compact convergence on
C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
. We shall write
\mathcal{F}
for
\mathcal{F}^{R=1}
.

4.1.1.
Actions of translations, scaling, and a conformal map

In the construction of the measures, to simplify formulas it will be convenient to act on functions in
\mathcal{F}^{R}
by three mappings: translations in the
t
variable, a scaling variable between
R>0
and
R=1
, and a conformal mapping to the punctured disk.

Given
T>0
, let
\tau_{T}
denote the time-translation map
t\mapsto t+T
which acts on
C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
via
\phi\mapsto\phi(T+\cdot)
. We extend its action to functions
F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
via
\tau_{T}^{*}F(\phi):=F(\tau_{T}\phi)
for all
\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))
. In particular, for functions of compact support, this shifts the support by
+T
.

Denote by
\rho_{R}:\mathcal{C}_{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{1}
the scaling map
\rho_{R}:(t,\theta)\mapsto(t/R,\theta/R)
.
We extend its action to any function
F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}_{R}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\rho_{R}^{*}F:C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
being defined by

\rho_{R}^{*}F(\phi):=F(\phi\circ\rho_{R})
(here we view
\phi\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}))
as a distribution of
the variable
(t,\theta)
).

4.2.
Sinh-Gordon model for

.

We now construct the Sinh-Gordon model on
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. We will use the notation

\mathcal{C}_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}:=[t_{1},t_{2}]\times\mathbb{T}

(in particular
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{1,T}=\mathcal{C}_{[0,T]}
).
We first introduce our approximations which will depend on a choice of initial configuration for the underlying GFF (viewed as a stochastic process).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T>0
. Denote by

the probability measure on

with expectation values given, for any bounded measurable

with support in

Above,

is the standard Brownian motion,

is the process (

),

denotes the functions

in

,

is the GMC measure depending on

F
by

\int_{E_{c,\varphi}(\mathcal{C}^{+}_{R,T})}F(\phi)e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{C}^{+}_{R,T}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dtd\theta}D\phi:=\mathbb{E}_{\varphi}[F(\phi^{R}(t,\theta))],

where
\phi^{R}(t,\theta)
is the random variable in
E_{c,\varphi}(\mathcal{C}^{+}_{R,T})
defined by

\phi^{R}(t,\theta):=c+B_{\frac{t}{R}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{n}(\frac{t}{R})+iy_{n}(\frac{t}{R})}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{in\frac{\theta}{R}}+\frac{x_{n}(\frac{t}{R})-iy_{n}(\frac{t}{R})}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{-in\frac{\theta}{R}},

with
x_{n}(t),y_{n}(t)
the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes defined above for
R=1
, and the expectation is conditional on
\varphi
.

Now, to work on the strip
\mathcal{C}_{R,T}=[-T,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{R}
, we view it as a shift by
t\mapsto t-T
of
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{R,2T}
and therefore represent the path integral on
\mathcal{C}_{R,T}
as

\int_{E_{c,\varphi}(\mathcal{C}_{R,T})}F(\phi)e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{R,T}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dtd\theta}D\phi:=\mathbb{E}[F(\phi^{R}(t,\theta))],

where
E_{c,\varphi}(\mathcal{C}_{R,T})=\{\phi(t+T,\theta)\,|\,\phi\in E_{c,\varphi}(\mathcal{C}^{+}_{R,2T})\}
and now the random variable

\phi^{R}

used to define the path integral is

\phi^{R}(t,\theta)=c+B_{\frac{t+T}{R}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{n}(\frac{t+T}{R})+iy_{n}(\frac{t+T}{R})}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{in\frac{\theta}{R}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x_{n}(\frac{t+T}{R})-iy_{n}(\frac{t+T}{R})}{2\sqrt{n}}e^{-in\frac{\theta}{R}}.

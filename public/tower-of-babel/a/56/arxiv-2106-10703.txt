Lattice construction of exotic invertible topological phases
Ryohei Kobayashi

Abstract




In this paper, we provide state sum path integral definitions of exotic invertible topological phases proposed in the recent paper by Hsin, Ji, and Jian. The exotic phase has time reversal (
T
) symmetry, and depends on a choice of the spacetime structure called the Wu structure. The exotic phase cannot be captured by the classification of any bosonic or fermionic topological phases, and thus gives a novel class of invertible topological phases. When the
T
symmetry defect admits a spin structure, our construction reduces to a sort of the decorated domain wall construction, in terms of a bosonic theory with
T
symmetry defects decorated with a fermionic phase that depends on a spin structure of the
T
symmetry defect.
By utilizing our path integral, we propose a lattice construction for the exotic phase that generates the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
classification of the (3+1)d invertible phase based on the Wu structure.
This generalizes the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
classification of the
T
-symmetric (1+1)d topological superconductor proposed by Fidkowski and Kitaev.
On oriented spacetime, this (3+1)d invertible phase with a specific choice of Wu structure reduces to a bosonic Crane-Yetter TQFT which has a topological ordered state with a semion on its boundary.
Moreover, we propose a subclass of
G
-SPT phases based on the Wu structure labeled by a pair of cohomological data in generic spacetime dimensions. This generalizes the Gu-Wen subclass of fermionic SPT phases.

Introduction

The classification of gapped phases is an important problem in condensed matter physics. Though the classification problem is difficult and unsolved in general, one can simplify the problem by considering a simplified class of systems with a unique gapped ground state on arbitrary closed spatial manifolds. Such phases are called invertible topological phases.
In the presence of a global symmetry
G
, invertible topological phases are also called symmetry protected topological (SPT) phases protected by
G
.

1

Precisely speaking, it is more common to define SPT phases as invertible phases which become trivial when we forget the global symmetry, following
[
1
]
. For example, the (1+1)-dimensional topological superconductor (Kitaev wire) is not counted as an SPT phase based on such a definition, though it is an invertible topological phase. In this thesis, we do not make a careful distinction between the two concepts, namely invertible phases and SPT phases.

In the case of free fermions, a complete classification of SPT phases has been obtained using K-theory
[
2
,
3
]
. In the case of intrinsically interacting systems, the classification in general differs from the free phases
[
4
]
, and we have to use a completely different method to perform the classification
[

,

. For example, a large class of interacting bosonic SPT phases with the global symmetry can be classified by utilizing group cohomology
[
1
]
. For the case of fermionic systems, the SPT phases have a richer classification than the bosonic phases; for example, Gu and Wen found that a subclass of fermionic SPT phases is classified by a pair of cohomological data
[
5

, generalizing the classification of bosonic phases. These fermionic SPT phases are called Gu-Wen phases or super-cohomology phases.
Later, a comprehensive classification scheme of SPT phases utilizing cobordism group is proposed in
[
6
]
, which is thought to classify invertible field theories which effectively describe SPT phases. The cobordism group provides a generic and powerful framework that correctly predicts the classification of interacting invertible topological phases, based on onsite (0-form) global symmetries, time reversal symmetry, or higher-form symmetries
[

,

So far, almost all the invertible topological phases discussed in literature has been either bosonic or fermionic. This implies that the effective field theories describing these topological phases require either oriented or spin structure (or these spacetime structures twisted by other symmetry groups such as pin
+
or pin
-
) of the spacetime.

Here we assume that the effective field theories are Lorentz invariant, and thereby oriented
SO(d)
or spin group
Spin(d)
contains the Lorentz group. Recently, there are also classes of topological phases whose effective field theories lack the Lorentz invariance, such as fractons
[
17

or those realized by foliated field theories
[
21
]
. These theories are beyond the scope of the present paper.
As such, the classification of invertible topological phases based on the cobordism groups has been performed for the corresponding field theories with (possibly twisted) oriented or spin structure.

Recently, it was proposed in
[
22
]
that there are nontrivial invertible topological field theories based on the spacetime structure called Wu structure, which is inequivalent to any (possibly twisted) oriented or spin structure previously discussed in literature, and thus gives a new class of invertible field theories which are phrased neither as bosonic nor fermionic.
The topological phases that depend on the spacetime Wu structure are called exotic topological phases.
Wu structure in
d
spacetime dimensions corresponds to the global symmetry given by a specific nontrivial mixture of the spacetime Lorentz symmetry
O(d)
and the 1-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry.
Since the Lorentz group is taken as
O(d)
, the exotic topological phases possess the time reversal symmetry.
Mathematically, the symmetry is described by a specific 2-group that corresponds to a sort of the extension of
O(d)
by the 1-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry. This generalizes spin/pin structure required for fermionic systems, where one extends the Lorentz symmetry
SO(d)
or
O(d)
by the ordinary (0-form)
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry that corresponds to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fermion parity.

In this paper, we explore a lattice realization of exotic topological phases. We define topologically invariant path integral constructions of exotic invertible topological phases in and more than (3+1)d in terms of a state sum on a lattice. In particular, the existence of an explicit state sum for the topological path integral of exotic invertible topological phases suggests that there should also exist corresponding commuting projector Hamiltonians, tensor network descriptions, and explicit quantum circuits that prepare these exotic topological phases.
We leave it to future work to explicitly develop these descriptions.

Summary of the main results

on a spacetime
d
-manifold

-form

symmetry characterized by the

-dimensional response theory

\displaystyle(-1)^{\int\mathop{\mathrm{Sq}}\nolimits^{3}(a)}=(-1)^{\int a\cup_{d-5}a},

(1.1)

where
\mathop{\mathrm{Sq}}\nolimits^{i}
denotes the Steenrod operation of cohomology groups
[
23
]
and

is higher cup product, see Appendix
A

That is, if one considers gauge transformation and the re-triangulation of the spacetime, the partition function transforms as

\displaystyle z(M^{\prime};\eta^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(-1)^{\int_{\widetilde{M}}\mathop{\mathrm{Sq}}\nolimits^{3}(a)}z(M;\eta,a),

(1.2)

where
\widetilde{M}=M\times[0,1]
is a
(d+1)

and

(d-2)

(\eta,a)

is a

a^{\prime}
, equivalent to

in cohomology
[a]=[a^{\prime}]
.

One can further show that the partition function becomes a quadratic function of the
(d-2)
-form gauge field

which satisfies the quadratic property

\displaystyle z(\eta,a)z(\eta,b)=z(\eta,a+b)(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup_{d-4}b}.

(1.3)

The construction of the theory

is done by a sort of decorated domain wall construction, namely it involves decorating the symmetry defect with a topological phase in lower spacetime dimensions.
That is,

is given by decorating the codimension-1 defect of the time-reversal (

The Grassmann integral on the

[

In particular, when the

In the case of the spacetime dimension

Actually, when the spacetime is oriented, the Wu structure can be taken to be trivial,
so in that case the exotic topological phase reduces to a bosonic topological phase. In particular, on oriented 4-manifolds

reproduces a (3+1)d bosonic Crane-Yetter TQFT based on a unitary modular tensor category
\mathcal{C}=\{1,s\}
that has a single nontrivial anyon

which is a semion.

For the case of

, one can gauge the 1-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry of

, which gives the Arf-Brown-Kervaire (ABK) invariant based on the quadratic refinement

of

\mathbb{Z}_{8}
classification of the exotic invertible topological phase

.
This generalizes the
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
classification of the (1+1)d pin
-

[
4

In addition, by utilizing the lattice construction of

we propose a subclass of the exotic invertible topological phases with an onsite (0-form) symmetry

with

Then, we propose a subclass of exotic SPT phases labeled by a pair of the cohomological data

\displaystyle(\nu_{d},n_{d-2})\in C_{\rho}^{d}(BG,U(1))\times Z^{d-2}(BG,\mathbb{Z}_{2}),

(1.4)

which are subject to the constraint

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho}\nu_{d}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Sq}^{3}(n_{d-2})\mod 1,

(1.5)

where

In general, by utilizing the theory

2
Review on Wu structure

In this section, we briefly review for Wu structure of the spacetime in continuum. First of all, let us recall spin structure of the spacetime required for fermionic phases. Wu structure is then regarded as a natural generalization of spin structure.

spin structure

A relativistic quantum field theory in
d
spacetime dimensions possesses the Lorentz
SO(d)
symmetry. However, since fermions are spinors, fermions transform according to the double cover of
SO(d)
, which is
Spin(d)
.
To define the field theory on a generic spacetime manifold, one needs to consider a
SO(d)
bundle
\phi:M\to BSO(d)
, which is the tangent bundle
TM
of an oriented triangulated manifold
M
. In order to have fermions, the transition functions
\phi_{ij}\in SO(d)
defined on 1-simplices
\braket{ij}
must be lifted to
\widetilde{\phi}_{ij}\in Spin(d)
.
Since
Spin(d)
is the group extension

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}\to Spin(d)\to SO(d)

(2.1)

whose extension is given by
w_{2}\in H^{2}(BSO(d),\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Then,
Spin(d)
is identified as
SO(d)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

as a set
, so we can express
\widetilde{\phi}_{ij}
as a pair
(\phi_{ij},\eta_{ij})\in SO(d)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
The nontrivial group extension is reflected in the multiplication law of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
elements
\eta_{ij}
twisted by
w_{2}
. Namely, for transition functions
\widetilde{\phi}_{ij}\in Spin(d)
, we have the multiplication law

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{01}\widetilde{\phi}_{12}=(\phi_{01}\phi_{12},\eta_{01}+\eta_{12}+w_{2}(\phi_{01},\phi_{12})).

(2.2)

Due to the cocycle condition
\widetilde{\phi}_{01}\widetilde{\phi}_{12}=\widetilde{\phi}_{02}
, we find on each 2-simplex
\braket{012}

\displaystyle\eta_{01}+\eta_{12}+\eta_{02}=w_{2}(\phi_{01},\phi_{12})

(2.3)

In coordinate-free notation, this is precisely the equation
\delta\eta=w_{2}
.

Wu structure

The Wu structure is a natural generalization of the spin structure illustrated above. The symmetry groups involved are Lorentz group
O(d)
that includes time reversal symmetry, and a 1-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry denoted as
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
here. Then, the total structure of the symmetry
\mathbb{G}
is described as a nontrivial mixture called a 2-group between
O(d)
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
symmetry,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}\to\mathbb{G}\to O(d)

(2.4)

and the 2-group structure involving
O(d)
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
is specified by the cohomology class
w_{1}w_{2}\in H^{3}(BO(d),\mathbb{Z}_{2})
called the Postnikov class. Analogously to the nontrivial group extension for the spin structure, a nontrivial 2-group has a distinct algebra of symmetry generators, thereby has a distinct configuration of background gauge field from that of the direct product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}\times O(d)
.

That is, if we denote the background gauge field of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
as
\eta\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and that of
O(d)
as
\phi:M\to BO(d)
, then these background gauge fields are correlated on each 3-simplex
\braket{0123}
as

\displaystyle\eta_{012}+\eta_{013}+\eta_{023}+\eta_{123}=w_{1}(\phi_{01})w_{2}(\phi_{12},\phi_{23}).

(2.5)

In coordinate-free notation, this is precisely the equation
\delta\eta=w_{1}w_{2}
.

It is worth noting that the background
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
gauge field twisted by the Postnikov class
\delta\eta=w_{1}w_{2}
reflects the algebra for the symmetry generators of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
and
O(d)
in the 2-group. To see this, we regard the background gauge field of
p
-form symmetry as an insertion of a
(d-p-1)
-dimensional symmetry defects on the Poincaré dual of the
(p+1)
-form background gauge field. Then, a 3-simplex is understood as an associator which shifts the configuration of 0-form
O(d)
symmetry defects by the
F
-move, see Fig.
1
(a).

During the
F
-move inside a 3-simplex, the three
O(d)
symmetry defects
\phi_{01},\phi_{12},\phi_{23}
meets at the junction in the middle of the movie, which gives a codimension-3 junction of the
O(d)
symmetry defects, see Fig.
1
(b). Then, the 2-group relation
\delta\eta=w_{1}w_{2}
means that the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
symmetry generator
w_{1}(\phi_{01})w_{2}(\phi_{12},\phi_{23})
is sourced from the codimension-3 junction. The 2-group is understood as the modification of the associativity of the 0-form
O(d)
symmetry generators by a generator of the 1-form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
symmetry, controlled by the Postnikov class in
H^{3}(BO(d),\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. See
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
for an introduction of the 2-group.

Wu structure is the nontrivial 2-group between the Lorentz
O(d)
symmetry and the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
1-form symmetry. The Wu structure is specified by a choice of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
2-cochain
\eta
with
\delta\eta=w_{1}w_{2}
, where
\eta
gives a background gauge field of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
symmetry. This is understood as sourcing the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{[1]}
symmetry defect from the codimension-3 junction of
O(d)
symmetry defects on each 3-simplex.

Path integral of an exotic phase

Now we construct a path integral definition for
z(\eta,a)
on a triangulated
d
-manifold
M
that depends on the Wu structure specified by a choice of
\eta\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
with
\delta\eta=w_{1}w_{2}
, and a
(d-2)
-form gauge field
[a]\in H^{d-2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. The partition function
z(\eta,a)
is valued in
\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
. Then, the main properties of
z(\eta,a)
is summarized as follows:

1.

The quadratic property

\displaystyle z(\eta,a)z(\eta,b)=z(\eta,a+b)(-1)^{\int_{M}a\cup_{d-4}b}.

(3.1)

2.

The change of
z(\eta,a)
under the gauge transformation

a\to a+\delta\chi
or under the change of the triangulation
is controlled by ’t Hooft anomaly whose response action is given by

\displaystyle(-1)^{\int\mathop{\mathrm{Sq}}\nolimits^{3}(a)}.

(3.2)

The description of the Wu structure used for
z(\eta,a)
is based on a specific chain
S\in Z_{d-3}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
that represents the Poincaré dual of the obstruction class
w_{1}w_{2}
for the Wu structure. Then, the Wu structure is specified by a choice of a
(d-2)
-chain
E\in C_{d-2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
with
\partial E=S
, which is Poincaré dual to
\eta\in C^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. As discussed in Appendix
B
, we prepare
S
based the choices of the cochain and chain representative of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
respectively, and then take the pairing of them. In particular, when the spacetime manifold
M
is oriented, we take the representative of
w_{1}
as zero and thereby
S=0
. So, for oriented spacetime manifolds
\eta
is closed and one can specify the Wu structure by
[\eta]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. As a warm-up, we start with the description of the simplest case
z(\eta,a)
on oriented manifolds.

3.1
Oriented case

Here let
M
be an oriented manifold. In that case
\eta
is closed
\delta\eta=0
, and
z(\eta,0)
is given by

\displaystyle z(\eta,a)=e^{2\pi i\int q(a)}(-1)^{\int_{E}a},

(3.3)

where
E\in Z_{d-2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is the Poincaré dual of
\eta
, and

\displaystyle q(a)=\frac{1}{4}(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}+\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a}),

(3.4)

where
\widehat{a}\in C^{d-2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{4})
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
lift of
a
.
When
d=4
, the above
q(a)
gives a cohomology operation
q:H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to H^{4}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{4})
known as the Pontryagin square
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, and thus
z(\eta=0,a)
is topologically invariant. However, the action
z(\eta,a)
is not topologically invariant for
d>4
, because

\displaystyle\delta(q(a))=\frac{1}{2}a\cup_{d-5}a=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Sq}}\nolimits^{3}(a)\mod 1.

(3.5)

This demonstrates the ’t Hooft anomaly of
z(\eta=0,a)
represented in (
3.2
).
This can be seen by using the Leibniz rule for higher cup product

\displaystyle\delta(u\cup_{i}v)=(-1)^{p+q-i}u\cup_{i-1}v+(-1)^{pq+p+q}v\cup_{i-1}u+\delta u\cup_{i}v+(-1)^{p}u\cup_{i}\delta v,

(3.6)

with
u\in C^{p},v\in C^{q}
cochains.
According to the Leibniz rule we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a})&=2(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-5}\widehat{a}+\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}+(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\delta\widehat{a},\\
\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a})&=(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\delta\widehat{a}-\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}+\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Putting these expressions into
\delta(q(a))
shows (
3.5
).
Next, let us consider a quadratic property of
z(\eta,a)
in (
3.1
).
We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}q(a)+q(b)-q(a+b)&=\frac{1}{4}(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b}+\widehat{b}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}+\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{b}+\widehat{b}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a}).\end{split}

(3.8)

Here, the rhs can be rewritten as

\displaystyle q(a)+q(b)-q(a+b)=\frac{1}{2}{a}\cup_{d-4}{b}+\frac{1}{4}\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\widehat{b}+\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\widehat{b})\mod 1.

(3.9)

To see this, we note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{4}\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\widehat{b})&=\frac{1}{4}(\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d-1}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d}\widehat{b}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a})\\
&=\frac{1}{4}((-1)^{d-1}\widehat{b}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a}+(-1)^{d-1}\delta(\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\widehat{b})-\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\delta\widehat{b}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d-1}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d}\widehat{b}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}).\end{split}

(3.10)

Here we again used the Leibniz rule (
3.6
).
Since
\frac{1}{4}\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\delta\widehat{b}=0\mod 1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{4}\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\widehat{b})&=\frac{1}{4}((-1)^{d-1}\widehat{b}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a}+(-1)^{d-1}\delta(\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\widehat{b})\\
&\quad+(-1)^{d}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d-1}\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b}+(-1)^{d}\widehat{b}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a})\mod 1.\end{split}

(3.11)

Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b})+\frac{1}{4}\delta(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\widehat{b}+\delta\widehat{a}\cup_{d-2}\widehat{b})\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{d}}{4}(\widehat{a}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{b}+\widehat{b}\cup_{d-4}\widehat{a}+\widehat{a}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{b}+\widehat{b}\cup_{d-3}\delta\widehat{a})\mod 1.\end{split}

(3.12)

Thus we obtain (
3.9
).
When the spacetime manifold
M

Appendix A
Review on higher cup product

A branching structure on a triangulation is a local ordering of vertices, which can be specified by an arrow on each 1-simplex
\braket{ij}

d

In this appendix, we review cochain-level product operation called higher cup product, whose definitions are based on branching structure of the triangulation. See also
[
48

Let

Firstly, the cup product gives the product of cochains

\displaystyle-\cup-:C^{k}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})\times C^{l}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})\to C^{k+l}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n}),

(A.1)

whose explicit form is written as

\displaystyle(a\cup b)(0,\dots,k+l)=a(0,\dots,k)b(k,\dots,k+l).

(A.2)

Note that this definition of cup product depends on the branching structure on the triangulation, where the ordering of vertices on each
(k+l)
-simplex is specified as

The cup product satisfies the Leibniz rule at the cochain level,

\displaystyle\delta(a\cup b)=\delta a\cup b+(-1)^{k}a\cup\delta b.

(A.3)

According to the Leibniz rule, one can show that the cup product defines the product of cohomologies

. Actually, for given
a\in Z^{k}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})
,

\displaystyle(a+\delta A)\cup(b+\delta B)=a\cup b+\delta((-1)^{k}a\cup B+A\cup b+A\cup\delta B),

(A.4)

so this also shifts
a\cup b
by a coboundary, thus defines a map between cohomologies. Such a product operation defined on cohomologies is called a cohomology operation.

As a generalization of the cup product, the higher cup product
\cup_{i}

\displaystyle-\cup_{i}-:C^{k}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})\times C^{l}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n})\to C^{k+l-i}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{n}),

\displaystyle(a\cup_{i}b)(0,\dots,k+l-i)=\sum_{0\leq j_{0}<\dots<j_{i}\leq k+l-i}(-1)^{p}\cdot a(0\to j_{0},j_{1}\to j_{2},\dots)b(j_{0}\to j_{1},j_{2}\to j_{3},\dots).

(A.8)

The theory

with the above two properties is realized by a bulk-boundary version of the Grassmann integral constructed in

in this paper, we present the proof only for the oriented case for simplicity. However,
\partial S_{N}=S_{\partial N}

Now let us write down the boundary Gu-Wen integral coupled with bulk;
we simply write the integral by
\sigma(a,b)

We assign Grassmann variables
\theta_{e},\overline{\theta}_{e}

(d-2)
-simplex

\sigma(a,b)=\int\prod_{f|b(f)=1}d\theta_{f}d\overline{\theta}_{f}\int\prod_{e|a(e)=1}d\theta_{e}d\overline{\theta}_{e}\prod_{t}u(t),

u(t)

d

N

is defined in the same fashion as in the case without boundary if

3.2
of the main text. However, its definition gets modified when
t
shares a
(d-1)
-simplex with the boundary.
For simplicity, we assign an ordering on vertices of such

Now let us demonstrate (

). The quadratic part of

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime},\delta\lambda+\delta\lambda^{\prime})=\sigma(\lambda,\delta\lambda)\sigma(\lambda^{\prime},\delta\lambda^{\prime})(-1)^{\int_{\partial N}\lambda\cup_{d-3}\lambda^{\prime}}(-1)^{\int_{N}\lambda\cup_{d-3}\delta\lambda^{\prime}+\delta\lambda^{\prime}\cup_{d-3}\lambda},

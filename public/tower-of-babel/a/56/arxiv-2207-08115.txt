Optimal Re-Embeddings of Border Basis Schemes
Martin Kreuzer, Le Ngoc Long, Lorenzo Robbiano

Abstract
. Border basis schemes are open subschemes of Hilbert schemes parametrizing 0-dimensional subschemes of ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} of given length. They yield open coverings and are easy to describe and to compute with. Our topic is to find re-embeddings of border basis schemes into affine spaces of minimal dimension. Given P = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] over a field K K , an ideal I ⊆ ⟨ X ⟩ = ⟨ x 1 , … , x n ⟩ I\subseteq\langle X\rangle=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle , and a tuple Z Z of indeterminates, in previous papers the authors developed techniques for computing Z Z -separating re-embeddings of the ideal I I , i.e., of isomorphisms Φ : P / I → K ⁡ [ X ∖ Z ] / ( I ∩ K ⁡ [ X ∖ Z ] ) \varPhi:P/I\rightarrow K[X{\setminus}Z]/(I\cap K[X{\setminus}Z]) . Here these general techniques are developed further and improved by constructing a new algorithm for checking candidate tuples Z Z and by using the Gröbner fan of the linear part of I I advantageously. Then we apply this to the ideals defining border basis schemes 𝔹 𝒪 \mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}} , where 𝒪 \mathcal{O} is an order ideal of terms, and to their natural generating polynomials. The fact that these ideals are homogeneous w.r.t. the arrow grading allows us to look for suitable tuples Z Z more systematically. Using the equivalence of indeterminates modulo the square of the maximal ideal, we compute the Gröbner fan of the linear part of the ideal quickly and determine which indeterminates should be in Z Z when we are looking for optimal re-embeddings. Specific applications include re-embeddings of border basis schemes where 𝒪 ⊆ K ⁡ [ x , y ] \mathcal{O}\subseteq K[x,y] and where 𝒪 \mathcal{O} consists of all terms up to some degree.

Introduction

One of the characteristic features of Algebraic Geometry is the existence
of moduli spaces. A systematic construction of so-called Hilbert schemes
was given by A. Grothendieck in
[
4
]
.
One particularly famous and well-researched type of these moduli spaces are
the Hilbert schemes
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits_{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K})
parametrizing
0-dimensional subschemes of length
\mu
in a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K}

over a field
K
. In Computational Algebraic Geometry, Grothendieck’s construction
leads to considerable difficulties, as the defining ideals of these moduli
spaces involve too many indeterminates to allow acceptably efficient calculations
in all but the most elementary cases.

One tool which has emerged in the last 20 years to study

\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits_{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K})
is the theory of border basis schemes.
These schemes define an open affine cover of the Hilbert scheme. For an order ideal

{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
in
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, i.e., for
a set of terms (power products of indeterminates) which is closed under forming divisors,
the
{\mathcal{O}}
-border basis scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
parametrizes all 0-dimensional schemes
in
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{K}
such that the residue classes of the terms in
{\mathcal{O}}
form
a
K
-vector space basis of their coordinate ring. The various
{\mathcal{O}}
-border basis
schemes for order ideals of length
\mu
cover
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits_{\mu}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{K})
and have
easily computable defining ideals (see
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,

[
14
]
,
[
15
]
). This makes it possible to use them for explicit calculations
to check if they are non-singular (see
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
), to verify when they are
affine cells (see
[
6
]
), or to compute subschemes defining particular loci
(see
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
). But one impediment remains:
although the equations defining border basis schemes are sparse, there are still
many of them and they involve a significant number of indeterminates, making it hard
to carry out some of the necessary calculations in practice.

This leads us to the topic of this paper: we want to re-embed border basis schemes
into lower-dimensional affine spaces and to efficiently calculate those re-embeddings.
The border basis scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is an affine scheme whose coordinate ring is
of the form
K[C]/I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
, where
C=\{c_{11},\dots,c_{\mu\nu}\}
is a set of distinct
indeterminates (see Definition
3.4
).
One obstruction to the idea of re-embedding
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is given by the cotangent space at the monomial
point of the border basis scheme. The monomial point corresponds to the monomial ideal in
P
which
is generated by all terms not in
{\mathcal{O}}
. On the
{\mathcal{O}}
-border basis scheme, it corresponds
to the origin defined by the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
generated by the residue classes of the
indeterminates in
C
.

A key observation is that the
vector space dimension
\dim_{K}({\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2})
yields a lower bound for the
possible embedding dimensions, since it is an invariant under isomorphisms,
and if this bound is achieved, we say that we have found
an
optimal
re-embedding of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
(see Definition
1.3
).

The usual way re-embeddings are computed in Computational Algebraic Geometry utilizes
the calculation of elimination ideals via Gröbner bases.
However, due to the large number of indeterminates,
this method is not feasible in most situations involving border basis schemes. In two
previous papers (see
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
) the authors introduced and developed
a more efficient method based on separating tuples of indeterminates.
This method can be applied to any finitely generated
K
-algebra
P/I
,
where
I
is an ideal contained in the maximal ideal

{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
of
P
.
For a tuple
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
of indeterminates
in
P
, we say that
I
is
Z
-separating
if there exist a tuple of non-zero
polynomials
(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
of
I
and a term ordering
\sigma
such that

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. Given a tuple
Z
such that
I
is
Z
-separating,
we can eliminate the indeterminates in
Z
and get a
Z
-separating
re-embedding

\varPhi:\;P/I\longrightarrow K[Y]/(I\cap K[Y])
where
Y=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
. Moreover, we described some methods to search for such
separating tuples based on Linear Programming Feasibility (LPF) solvers and the computation
of restricted Gröbner fans (see
[
11
]
).

The first topic in this paper, treated in Sections 1 and 2, is to improve those methods
further in the general framework of finitely generated
K
-algebras, in particular
if we are looking for optimal re-embeddings of
I
.
In Section 1 we show how to check whether a given tuple of distinct indeterminates
Z

yields a
Z
-separating re-embedding for a given ideal
I
. Besides the general method,
which involves a Gröbner basis computation (see Proposition
1.10
),
we provide an algorithm which works particularly well for the type of ideals considered
later in the paper, namely defining ideals of border basis schemes
(see Algorithm
1.13
). Since this algorithm uses only linear algebra
techniques, it is suitable for finding good re-embeddings even in the case of large examples.

In Subsection 2.A we introduce the idea of using
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)
.
This is the Gröbner fan of the
linear part

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
of
I
,
i.e., the ideal generated by the
homogeneous linear parts of the elements of
I
. It is used to find tuples
Z
which may
yield separating, possibly optimal re-embeddings of
I
(see Algorithm
2.1
).
Using Remark
2.2
and Example
2.3
we show that
our methods may fail to detect
all
optimal embeddings.
In Subsection 2.B we look at the Gröbner fan of an ideal
I_{L}
generated by homogeneous
linear polynomials more closely. First we reduce the task to check whether a given
tuple of indeterminates
Z
consists of leading terms of a reduced Gröbner basis
of
I_{L}
to a linear algebra computation (see Proposition
2.5
). Then
we reduce the calculation of the Gröbner fan of
I_{L}

to the task of finding the maximal minors of a matrix (see Theorem
2.9

and Remark
2.10
). We also note that this task can be tackled via any
method for computing the bases of a linear matroid (see Remark
2.10
.b).

The actual application of these methods to finding optimal re-embeddings of
border basis schemes commences in Section 3. When we are given an order ideal of terms

{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
in a polynomial ring
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
over a field
K
,
we let
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
be its
border
, and
C=\{c_{ij}\mid i=1,\dots,\mu;\;j=1,\dots,\nu\}
the set of indeterminates which appear as coefficients
in the
generic
{\mathcal{O}}
-border prebasis

G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{\nu}\}
,
where
g_{j}=b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}c_{ij}t_{i}
for
j=1,\dots,\nu
.
Then the defining ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
of the
{\mathcal{O}}
-border basis scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is generated
in
K[C]
by two types of polynomials:

(1)

For border terms
b_{j}=x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}
, called
next-door neighbours
,
there is a tuple
\mathop{\rm ND}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})
of
\mu
polynomials which expresses the
fact that the syzygy
b_{j}-x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}=0
of border terms can be lifted to a syzygy
of
G
.

(2)

For border terms
b_{j}=x_{\ell}t_{m}
and
b_{j^{\prime}}=x_{k}t_{m}
, called

across-the-rim neighbours
, there is a tuple
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})
of
\mu
polynomials
which expresses the fact that the syzygy
x_{k}b_{j}-x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}=0

of border terms can be lifted to a syzygy of
G
.

Altogether, both types of polynomials form the set of
natural generators
of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.
In Proposition
3.8
we recall the detailed description of the linear parts
of the natural generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
given in
[
16
]
.

Then Section 4 introduces a further important structure governing the vanishing ideal
of a border basis scheme, namely the
arrow degree
. It is the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading
on
K[C]
given by
\deg_{W}(c_{ij})=\log(b_{j})-\log(t_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,\mu
and
j=1,\dots,\nu
.
The natural generators, and thus the entire ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
, are homogeneous with respect to
the arrow grading. Unfortunately, the arrow grading is in general not positive, and in fact
not even non-negative, so that we cannot avail ourselves of the many nice properties of positive
or non-negative gradings. However, the arrow degree allows us to visualize the linear parts
of the natural generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
nicely (see Remark
4.7
) and to describe
the terms in the quadratic parts of the natural generators in great detail
(see Proposition
4.9
).

Another fundamental ingredient is provided in Section 5.
If we let
{\mathfrak{m}}=\langle\bar{c}_{ij}\rangle

denote the homogeneous maximal ideal in
K[C]/I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
, the cotangent space of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
at the monomial
point is given by
\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(B_{{\mathcal{O}}})={\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
. As mentioned before,
the dimension of this vector space is a lower bound for the embedding dimension of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
.
It also yields a useful
equivalence relation on the indeterminates
c_{ij}
. We say that
c_{ij}
and
c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

are
cotangent equivalent
if
\bar{c}_{ij}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=\bar{c}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
.
Then there are three main types of cotangent equivalence classes.
The indeterminates
c_{ij}
such that
\bar{c}_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
are called
trivial
and form
the
trivial equivalence class
. A non-trivial indeterminate
c_{ij}
is called
basic

if its cotangent equivalence class (which is then also called basic) consists only of
c_{ij}
.
Finally, a non-trivial indeterminate
c_{ij}
is called
proper
if its equivalence class
(which is then also called proper) contains at least two elements.
An interesting property of this equivalence relation is that it provides a
beautiful description of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))\rangle)
and a
method to compute it (see Theorem
5.5
) which is more
efficient than the general method described in Subsection 2.A.

Moreover, an important application of these equivalence classes to the task of
finding optimal
Z
-separating re-embeddings of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is that,
to find a suitable tuple
Z
, we have to put into
Y=C{\setminus}Z

all basic indeterminates
c_{ij}
and exactly one from each proper equivalence
class (see Theorem
5.6
). Since the cotangent equivalence
classes can be calculated very quickly (see
[
16
]
), we obtain a rather fast
method of checking for optimal
Z
-separating re-embeddings of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
in
Algorithm
5.7
.

The final three sections of the paper are devoted to examining special cases
in which we can (or cannot) find optimal re-embeddings of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
. Section 6 starts
with the case of
planar
border basis schemes, i.e., the case
n=2
.
Most of the results in this section are inspired by, improve upon, or make explicit
the excellent and thorough study of this setting in
[
6
]
.
A key concept here is the notion of an
x_{\ell}
-exposed
indeterminate
c_{ij}
.
When we form a product
x_{\ell}g_{j}
as part of lifting a neighbour syzygy or an across-the-rim
syzygy, where
g_{j}
is an element of the generic
{\mathcal{O}}
-border prebasis, some coefficients
of the result will be coefficients of a border term, and the indeterminates appearing
in them are called the
x_{\ell}
-exposed indeterminates. By varying
\ell
and
j
,
we obtain the set
C^{\rm ex}
of all exposed indeterminates in
C

(see Definition
6.1
and Proposition
6.4
).

The main result of the section is that all non-exposed indeterminates can be eliminated,
i.e., that the exposed indeterminates generate
K[C]/I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
. In
[
6
]
, Prop. 7.1.2,
this is shown via the Hilbert-Burch Theorem. Instead, here we construct an explicit
Weight Assignment Algorithm
6.8
which allows us to find,
for
Z=C\setminus C^{\rm ex}
, a
Z
-separating tuple of natural generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})

and the corresponding elimination ordering
\sigma
. Thus all non-exposed indeterminates

c_{ij}
can be eliminated (see Theorem
6.9
). Consequently, we can also improve
Algorithm
5.7
by restricting the choice of indeterminates for
Y=C{\setminus}Z

in each proper cotangent equivalent class to exposed indeterminates only
(see Proposition
6.17
).
However, as for instance Example
6.18
shows, eliminating the
non-exposed indeterminates is in general not sufficient to achieve optimal embeddings.
Furthermore, Remark
6.2
shows that all exposed indeterminates are rim
indeterminates (see Definition
4.11
), but in
Example
6.18
some rim indeterminates are not exposed.
An explicit application of the Weight Assignment Algorithm
6.8

is to the family of order ideals called
planar boxes
(see Remark
6.14
).

Another family of examples in which we can compute optimal separating re-embeddings
of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is examined in Section 7, where we treat
simplicial
order ideals
{\mathcal{O}}

of type
d
, i.e., order ideals consisting of all terms of degree
\leq d
for some
d>0
.
Here we prove that one can optimally re-embed
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
by using the tuple
Z

of all interior indeterminates
c_{ij}
, i.e., all indeterminates
c_{ij}
such that
no multiple
x_{k}t_{i}
is in
\partial{\mathcal{O}}
(see Proposition
7.3
).
As an immediate consequence, we see that
border basis schemes of simplicial order ideals are affine cells if
n=2
and singular
schemes if
n\geq 3
(see Corollary
7.5
).

The last section comprises a small collection of interesting further examples
of re-embeddings of border basis schemes. In Example
8.1

we look at the order ideal
{\mathcal{O}}=\{1,\,y,\,x,\,y^{2},\,x^{2}\}
in two indeterminates
which we call the
L-shape
. Its border basis scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is shown to be a
10-dimensional smooth variety which can be embedded as a complete intersection
of codimension 2 in
\mathbb{A}^{12}
. This embedding is the best
Z
-separating re-embedding,
and we conjecture that 12 is in fact the embedding dimension of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
in this case.
In particular, this would imply that
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is not an affine cell.

Two similar looking further examples exhibit very different behaviors. For the order ideal

{\mathcal{O}}=\{1,\,z,\,y,\,x,\,z^{2}\}
we get a singular border basis scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}

which has an optimal
Z
-separating re-embedding (see Example
8.3
),
while for
{\mathcal{O}}=\{1,\,z,\,y,\,x,\,xy\}
we get a smooth border basis scheme
for which we cannot find an optimal
Z
-separating re-embedding.

1.
Z
-Separating Re-embeddings of Affine Schemes

In this paper we let
K
be an arbitrary field, let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
and let
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. The tuple formed by the indeterminates
of
P
is denoted by
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Moreover, let
1\leq s\leq n
,
let
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}
be pairwise distinct indeterminates in
X
,
and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
.
Denote the remaining indeterminates by

\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s}\}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
,
and let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s})
. Committing a slight abuse of notation,
we shall also write
Y=X\setminus Z
.
The monoid of terms in
P
is denoted by
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mid\alpha_{i}\geq 0\}
.
Given a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
its restriction to
\mathbb{T}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s})
is denoted by
\sigma_{Y}
.

1.A.
Z
-Separating Tuples and
Z
-Separating
Re-embeddings

In
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, the authors examined re-embeddings of affine algebras,
i.e., isomorphisms with presentations requiring fewer
K
-algebra
generators. In particular, it was shown that the following situation leads to
such re-embeddings.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
P
which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
, and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

be a tuple of distinct indeterminates in
X
. We say that the ideal
I

is
Z
-separating
if there exist a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}

and
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}
such that

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. In this situation
\sigma
is called a

Z
-separating term ordering
for
I
, and the tuple
(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
is called
a
Z
-separating tuple.

Recall that, in the setting of the definition, the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis
of the ideal
\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
is
coherently
Z
-separating
,
i.e., it is of the form
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\}
where we have
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=z_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,s
, and where
z_{i}
divides no other term in
g_{i}

or in
g_{j}
for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
and
j\neq i
(see
[
11
]
, Prop. 2.2).
Furthermore, recall that
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f)
denotes the homogeneous component of degree 1
of a polynomial
f\in{\mathfrak{M}}
and
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)=\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f)\mid f\in I\rangle_{K}

is the
{\mathfrak{M}}
-linear part
of
I
. Moreover, if we denote the image of
{\mathfrak{M}}
in
P/I

by
{\mathfrak{m}}
, it was shown in
[
10
]
, Prop. 1.8.b, that

P_{1}/\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\cong{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, where
\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(B_{\mathcal{O}})={\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}

is the
cotangent space
of
P/I
at the origin, and in
[
10
]
, Prop. 1.9
that
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
is easy to compute, since it is equal
to
\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f_{1}),...,\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f_{s})\rangle_{K}
, where
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\}

is any set of generators of
I
.

In this setting we obtain the following kind of re-embedding of
I
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
P
which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
. Assume that there
exists a tuple of distinct indeterminates
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
in
X
such that
I

is
Z
-separating. Let
\sigma
be a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
,
let
Y=X\setminus Z
, and let
S_{\sigma}
be the minimal set of terms generating

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)
.

(a)

The reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
is of the form

G=\{z_{1}-h_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\,z_{s}-h_{s},\allowbreak g_{1},\,\dots,\,g_{r}\}

with
h_{i},g_{j}\in K[Y]
.

(b)

The
K
-algebra homomorphism
\varPhi:P/I\longrightarrow K[Y]/(I\cap K[Y])
given by
\varPhi(\bar{x}_{i})=\bar{x}_{i}
for

x_{i}\in Y
and
\varPhi(\bar{x}_{i})=\bar{h}_{j}

for
x_{i}=z_{j}\in Z
is an isomorphism of
K
-algebras, called the

Z
-separating re-embedding
of
I
.

(c)

We have
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)
,
and hence
Y\supseteq X\setminus S_{\sigma}
.

(d)

The residue classes of the indeterminates in
Y=X\setminus Z

generate the cotangent space
P_{1}/\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\cong{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
of
P/I
at the origin.

Proof.

Claims (a) and (b) follow from
[
10
]
, Theorem 2.13. Let us prove (c).
By assumption, there exist
polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I
such that they form the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of
\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
and such that
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})

for
i=1,\dots,s
. Then we have
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(f_{i}))
for
i=1,\dots,s
,
and thus
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)
.
Claim (d) follows from (c), since the set of the residue classes
of the elements in
X\setminus S_{\sigma}
is a
K
-basis of
P_{1}/\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
.
∎

Next we recall a definition which plays a fundamental role in this paper
(see
[
10
]
, Definition 3.3).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I

be a proper ideal in
P
.

(a)

A
K
-algebra isomorphism
\varPsi:\;P/I\longrightarrow P^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
,
where
P^{\prime}
is a polynomial ring over
K
and
I^{\prime}
is an ideal in
P^{\prime}
, is
called a
re-embedding
of
I
.

(b)

A re-embedding
\varPsi:\;P/I\longrightarrow P^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
of
I
is called

optimal
if every
K
-al
gebra isomorphism
P/I\longrightarrow P^{\prime\prime}/I^{\prime\prime}

with a polynomial ring
P^{\prime\prime}
over
K
and an ideal
I^{\prime\prime}
in
P^{\prime\prime}

satisfies the inequality
\dim(P^{\prime\prime})\geq\dim(P^{\prime})
.

When we are only looking for
Z
-separating re-embeddings of
I
which are optimal,
the above proposition yields the following characterization.

Corollary 1.4
.

In the setting of the proposition, assume that
{s=\#Z}
is equal to the
K
-vector space
dimension of
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
. Then the following claims hold.

(a)

The map
\varPhi
is an optimal re-embedding of
I
.

(b)

We have
\langle Z\rangle=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)
,
and hence
Y=X\setminus S_{\sigma}
.

(c)

The residue classes of the indeterminates in
Y=X\setminus Z

form a
K
-vector space basis of the cotangent space

P_{1}/\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\cong{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
of
P/I
at the origin.

Proof.

Claim (a) is a consequence of
[
10
]
, Cor. 4.2. Let us prove (b).
By claim (c) of the proposition, we have

\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)
.
Since we have
s=\dim_{K}(\langle Z\rangle_{K})=\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I))
, we deduce
that
Z
minimally generates
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)\rangle)

which concludes the proof.

Finally, let us prove (c). By (b), we have
Z=S_{\sigma}
. As mentioned in
the proof of claim (d) of the proposition, the set of the residue classes
of the elements in
X\setminus S_{\sigma}
is a
K
-basis
of
P_{1}/\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
. This implies the claim.
∎

An important situation in which we obtain an optimal re-embedding is described
by the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

In the setting of the proposition, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The map
\varPhi:\;P/I\longrightarrow K[Y]/(I\cap K[Y])

is an optimal re-embedding, and the ring
P_{{\mathfrak{M}}}/I_{{\mathfrak{M}}}
is a regular local ring.

(b)

We have
I\cap K[Y]=\{0\}
.

In particular, if these conditions are satisfied, then
\varPhi
is an isomorphism
with a polynomial ring, the scheme
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(P/I)
is isomorphic to an affine space,
and the set
G=\{z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s}\}
is a minimal set of generators of
I
.

Proof.

The claims follow from
[
11
]
, Proposition 6.7.
∎

Sometimes an optimal separating re-embedding
cannot be achieved using a given set of generators of
I
,
as the following easy example shows.

Example 1.6
.

Let

, and let

If the conditions of Corollary
1.5
are satisfied,
we have the following additional information.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
be a tuple of non-zero polynomials in
{\mathfrak{M}}
,
and let
I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle

a regular local ring and that, for

, we have a tuple

of

-separating.

(a)

The tuple

is a Gröbner basis of

for

.

(b)

The tuple

(c)

Proof.

First we prove (a). Since

for

To prove (b) we note that the fact that the leading terms of

Finally, to prove claim (c), we use the definitions and results of

By Thm. 5.5, the map

has cardinality one.
∎

The entries of every

.

.

Let

.

1.B. Checking
Z
-Separating Tuples

An important algorithmic problem is to determine whether a given tuple
of distinct indeterminates
Z
is separating for an ideal
I
in
P

which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
and given by a set of generators. The following
proposition provides a general answer.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
P
which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
, and
let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates in
X
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The ideal
I
is
Z
-separating.

(b)

For every elimination ordering
\sigma
for
Z
,
we have
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I)
.

(c)

There exists an elimination ordering
\sigma
for
Z
such that

\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I)
.

Proof.

To show that (a) implies (b), we note that, by definition, there exists
a
Z
-separating term ordering
\sigma
for
I
. By
[
11
]
, Remark 4.3, it
follows that any elimination ordering for
Z
is then also a

Z
-separating term ordering for
I
. Now the claim follows
from
[
11
]
, Proposition 4.2.

Since (b) obviously implies (c), it remains to prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(a).
The fact that
\sigma
is an elimination ordering for
Z
implies that
the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
has the shape given in
[
11
]
,
Proposition 4.2.d. Thus the claim follows from this proposition.
∎

Clearly, condition (b) of this proposition allows us to check effectively whether
I

is
Z
-separating for a given tuple
Z
, but the required Gröbner basis computation
may be too costly, in particular if the given ideal is not
Z
-separating.
However, the following example shows that apparently
there is no generally applicable better method.

Example 1.11
.

Consider the ring
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, the tuple
Z=(x)
, and the ideal

I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{10}\rangle
, where

\begin{array}[]{lcl}f_{1}&=&xy^{2}+\frac{1}{2}y^{3}-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}z-x^{2}-\frac{1}{2}xy-y^{2}+\frac{1}{2}xz+x,\cr f_{2}&=&y^{2}z^{2}+3y^{3}-4y^{2}z-xz^{2}-3xy+4xz,\cr f_{3}&=&y^{3}z-xyz-y^{2}z+xz,\cr f_{4}&=&y^{4}-xy^{2}-y^{3}+xy,\cr f_{5}&=&x^{2}y^{2}-x^{3},\cr f_{6}&=&x^{3}+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}y+xy^{2}+\frac{1}{2}y^{3}-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}z-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}z-x^{2}-y^{2},\cr f_{7}&=&x^{2}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{2}+3x^{2}y+3y^{3}-4x^{2}z-4y^{2}z,\cr f_{8}&=&x^{2}yz+y^{3}z-x^{2}z-y^{2}z,\cr f_{9}&=&x^{2}y^{2}+y^{4}-x^{2}y-y^{3},\cr f_{10}&=&x^{4}+x^{2}y^{2}\end{array}

At first glance, the ideal
I
does not appear to be
Z
-separating, even if we use
linear combinations of the generators. However, for an elimination ordering
\sigma
for
Z
, the
reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of the ideal
I
is
\{x-y^{2},\,y^{4}+y^{2}\}
,
and this proves that
I
is indeed
Z
-separating.

To have more efficient methods for checking if a given ideal is
Z
-separating
for a given tuple
Z
, which may or may not work in special cases, we can try to use one of
the following techniques.

Remark 1.12
.

Let
I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle
be an ideal in
P
which is generated by polynomials

f_{i}\in{\mathfrak{M}}
, and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of indeterminates in
X
.

(a)

To check condition (b) of the proposition, it may suffice to compute a partial
Gröbner basis of
I
, or to use a timeout for the calculation.

(b)

We can also hope to find suitable polynomials
f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{s}}\in I

for which the tuple
(f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{s}})
is
Z
-separating among the given
generators of
I
. More precisely, we may proceed as follows:

(1)

For all tuples
F_{Z}=(f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{s}})

such that
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
are
s
distinct indices
and
z_{j}\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i_{j}})
for
j=1,\dots,s
, perform the following step.

(2)

Using an LPF-solver as in
[
11
]
, Cor. 3.5, check whether the tuple
F_{Z}

is
Z
-separating. If it is, return the tuple of weights calculated by the
LPF-solver and stop.

(c)

The loop suggested in (b) may have too many iterations to be practically
feasible. In our implementation and experiments, we therefore used
the following heuristics: create suitable tuples
(f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{s}})
randomly and perform step (2) for every tuple
after it has been created. If the LPF-solver is not successful,
try another random example until one tuple
F_{Z}
works or
until a certain number of unsuccessful tries has been performed.

Even for large examples, these random choices
lead to success in many cases, and setting
the number of random tries to several hundred (or several thousand in
the case of really large examples) resulted in a high hit rate of
correct answers.

In view of the difficulties involved in computing Gröbner
bases for the ideals considered in this paper, the following algorithm offers
an efficient method for finding
Z
-separating re-embeddings
in the case of ideals generated by polynomials of degree two. This case will be
exactly the one we encounter when we study re-embeddings of border basis schemes.

Algorithm 1.13
.

(Checking for
Z
-Separating Re-embeddings)

Let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, let
I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle
be an ideal in
P

generated by polynomials of the form
f_{i}=\ell_{i}+p_{i}\in{\mathfrak{M}}
, where
\ell_{i}\in P_{1}

and
p_{i}\in P_{2}
for
i=1,\dots,r
, and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct
indeterminates in
X
. Consider the following sequence of instructions.

(1)

Let
Y=X\setminus Z
, set
w_{i}=0
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

such that
x_{i}\in Y
, let
\widehat{Z}=Z
, let
\hat{f}_{i}=f_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,r
, and let
d=1
.

(2)

For
i=1,\dots,r
, delete in
\hat{f}_{i}
every term which is not divisible by an
indeterminate in
\widehat{Z}
and write the resulting polynomial in the form

\hat{f}_{i}=\hat{\ell}_{i}+\hat{p}_{i}
where
\hat{\ell}_{i}\in P_{1}
and
\hat{p}_{i}\in P_{2}

for
i=1,\dots,r
.

(3)

If
\dim_{K}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{1},\dots,\hat{\ell_{r}}\rangle_{K})<s
then return
"No Weights Found"
and stop.
Otherwise, interreduce the polynomials
\hat{f}_{i}

K
-linearly such that their
linear parts are distinct indeterminates of
\widehat{Z}
.
Rename the resulting polynomials and write them in the form

\hat{f}_{i}=z_{i}+\hat{p}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
and
\hat{f}_{j}=q_{j-s}

for
j=s+1,\dots,r
. Here we have
\hat{p}_{i},q_{j}\in P_{2}
,
and we let
u=r-s
as well as
\hat{s}=s
.

(4)

Interreduce the polynomials
q_{1},\dots,q_{u}

K
-linearly
such that they have distinct leading terms w.r.t.
Lex
. Remove
all zero polynomials, redefining
u
if necessary, and call the resulting polynomials

q_{1},\dots,q_{u}
again.

(5)

Reduce the polynomials
\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{\hat{s}}

K
-linearly
against
q_{1},\dots,q_{u}
and get the polynomials
\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{\hat{s}}
.

(6)

If none of the resulting polynomials
\tilde{f}_{j}

is an indeterminate from
\widehat{Z}
, return
"No Weights Found"
and stop.
Otherwise, let
\widetilde{Z}
be the set of all indeterminates
z_{i}
which equal one of the
polynomials
\tilde{f}_{j}
, set
w_{k}=d
for each of these indeterminates
z_{i}=x_{k}
,
and remove from
\widehat{Z}
all indeterminates from
\widetilde{Z}
.
Let
\hat{s}=\#\widehat{Z}
, let
\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{\hat{s}}

be the remaining polynomials
\tilde{f}_{j}
, and denote
2d+1
by
d
.

(7)

Repeat the following steps until
\widehat{Z}=\emptyset
. Then return

W=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
and stop.

(8)

For
i=1,\dots,\hat{s}
and
j=1,\dots,u
, remove from
\hat{f}_{i}
and
q_{j}

every term which is not divisible by one of the indeterminates in
\widehat{Z}
.

(9)

Perform steps (4), (5), and (6).

This is an algorithm which, if it is successful, returns a tuple of non-negative
weights
W=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that every term ordering
\sigma

which is compatible with the grading
given by
W
is an elimination ordering for
Z
with the property that there exists a
coherently
Z
-separating tuple of polynomials
(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})
in
I
satisfying

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

Proof.

In view of the two cases examined in step (6), the algorithm either stops or
the number of elements in
\widehat{Z}
decreases in each iteration of the loop
in steps (7)–(9). Hence the algorithm is finite, and it remains to prove correctness
if it returns a tuple
W=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
.

Let
\sigma
be a term ordering which is compatible with the grading given by
W
.
We want to show that for every indeterminate
z_{i}\in Z
there exists
a polynomial
g_{i}\in I
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=z_{i}
and such that
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\}

is the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\rangle
.
In step (1), the indeterminates in
Y=X\setminus Z
are given weight zero,
and in step (2) all terms which are products of indeterminates from
Y
are removed.
Since we are only performing
K
-linear combinations of the given generators

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in I
, and since every indeterminate in
Z
will get a positive weight,
the deleted terms will surely be smaller than any term which still needs to get a weight.

Clearly, if the linear part of
\hat{I}=\langle\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{r}\rangle

has a dimension smaller than
s
, then the desired
polynomials
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
do not exist in
I
. Therefore step (3) correctly stops
the algorithm in this case. If this linear part has dimension
s
, then there exist

K
-linear combinations
\hat{g}_{i}
of
\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{r}
such that

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(\hat{g}_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
and
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(\hat{g}_{i})=0
for
i>s
.
Such polynomials are correctly found in step (3), and the homogeneous quadratic polynomials

\hat{g}_{s+1},\dots,\hat{g}_{r}
among them are interreduced in step (4).
Every polynomial
h_{i}
in
\hat{I}
which satisfies
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(h_{i})=z_{i}
is of the
form
h_{i}=\hat{g}_{i}+k_{i}
where
k_{i}\in\langle\hat{g}_{s+1},\dots,\hat{g}_{r}\rangle_{K}
.
In order to have a polynomial of this kind for which
z_{i}
can be assigned the smallest
available weight, it is necessary that its homogeneous component of degree two is zero.
The check whether such polynomials exist is done in steps (5) and (6) where the
quadratic parts of the polynomials
\hat{f}_{j}
are
K
-linearly reduced against
the homogeneous polynomials of degree two.

If the quadratic part of no polynomial
\hat{f}_{j}
reduces to zero, our attempt to
assign weights fails and step (6) gives the correct answer. Otherwise, we assign those
indeterminates, namely the ones in
\widetilde{Z}
, the next available weight
d
.
In the loop of steps (7)–(9) we repeat this procedure. Setting the terms divisible only
by indeterminates in
\widetilde{Z}
to zero and increasing the lowest available weight
to
2d+1
makes sure that any remaining indeterminate will get a bigger weight than any
of the deleted terms. Once again, interreducing the quadratic polynomials and then
reducing the polynomials
z_{i}+\hat{p}_{i}
against these quadratic polynomials
decides whether there exists an indeterminate
z_{i}
that can get the next lowest
available weight.

If this loop reaches
\widehat{Z}=\emptyset
, we have assigned
weights to all indeterminates in
Z
such that for
i=1,\dots,s
there exist polynomials

\tilde{g}_{i}=z_{i}+p_{i}
in
I
for which
z_{i}
is the unique term of highest weight
in
\tilde{g}_{i}
and
p_{i}
is homogeneous of degree two.
In particular, we can renumber these polynomials and the indeterminates in
Z

such that
z_{1}<_{\sigma}\cdots<_{\sigma}z_{s}
and
p_{i}\in K[Y][z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1}]_{2}

for
i=1,\dots,s
. Starting with
\tilde{g}_{1}=z_{1}+p_{1}
, we can then substitute
z_{i}

in all polynomials
\tilde{g}_{j}
, where
j>i
, by
-p_{i}
and get the reduced

\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
\langle\tilde{g}_{1},\dots,\tilde{g}_{s}\rangle

we were looking for. Altogether, Proposition 2.6 in
[
10
]
implies that

(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})
is coherently
Z
-separating, as we wanted to show.
∎

Several variants of Algorithm
1.13
are possible,
as the following remark shows.

Remark 1.14
.

Suppose we are in the setting of Algorithm
1.13
.

(a)

The hypothesis that the polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I
have degree
two is not strictly necessary. Using some care, one can write down a version
of the algorithm which replaces the quadratic parts
\hat{p}_{i},\,q_{j}
by the
non-linear parts of the respective polynomials. Since this more general situation
is not relevant to this paper, we leave the details to the interested readers.

(b)

For certain types of ideals, some of the polynomials
\hat{f}_{1},\dots,\hat{f}_{r}
in step (2) of Algorithm
1.13
are already of the form

c\,z_{i}
with
c\in K\setminus\{0\}
and
z_{i}\in\widehat{Z}
. In this case we can
include an execution of step (6) and replace step (3) by the following steps.

(3a)

If
\dim_{K}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{1},\dots,\hat{\ell_{r}}\rangle_{K})<s
,
return
"No Weights Found"
and stop.

(3b)

Let
\widetilde{Z}
be the set of all
z_{i}\in\widehat{Z}

such that
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(\hat{f}_{i})=\{z_{i}\}
. If we have
\widetilde{Z}\neq\emptyset
then
perform the following step (3c) and afterwards return to step (2). Otherwise, continue
with step (4).

(3c)

Let

(4a)

Form all products

2.
Separating Re-embeddings via Gröbner Fans of Linear Ideals

In this section we introduce a novel method for finding optimal

Z
-separating re-embeddings which improves on the approach in
[
11
]
.
Let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
,
let
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, and let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}

be an ideal of
P
.
In the first subsection we show that the knowledge of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)

i.e., the Gröbner fan of the linear part of

Z
-separating
re-embedding of

compute Gröbner fans of homogeneous linear ideals.

2.A. Finding
Z
-Separating Re-embeddings

Given a tuple of distinct indeterminates
Z
in
X
, the methods explained in
Subsection 1.B allow us to check whether the ideal
I
is
Z
-separating.
This raises the question how to find such tuples

.
In
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
we gave some answers
to this question which use the computation of the Gröbner fan of
I
.
Unfortunately, this computation may be infeasible for large examples.
However, a way out is suggested by Proposition
1.2
.c.
Indeed, our next algorithm says that we can get away with knowing

\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)
.
As mentioned previously, if we have the equality

I=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle
with
f_{i}\in{\mathfrak{M}}
then
we can calculate the linear part of

via

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)=\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(f_{r})\rangle_{K}

(see
[
10
]
, Proposition 1.9).

Algorithm 2.1
.

(
Z
-Separating Re-embeddings
via
{\boldsymbol{\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits}(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)}

Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal of
P
, and let
s\leq\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I))
.
Consider the following sequence of instructions.

(1)

Compute
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)
.

(2)

Form the set
S
of all tuples
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
such that
there is marked reduced Gröbner basis
\overline{G}
in

\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\rangle)
for which
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}
are among the marked terms.

(3)

If
S=\emptyset
, return

"No re-embedding found"
.
While

, perform the following steps.

(4)

Choose a tuple

and remove it from
S
.

(5)

Using one of the methods suggested in Subsection 1.B, check whether the ideal
I
is

Z
-separating. If it is, return
Z
and stop. Otherwise, continue with the next iteration.

This is an algorithm which, if successful, finds a tuple of distinct indeterminates

Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
in
X
such that
I
is
Z
-separating.

Moreover, if
s=\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I))
and the algorithm is successful then
the output tuple

defines an optimal re-embedding of

.

Proof.

Every tuple

such that there exists an
Z
-separating re-embedding
of

is contained in the tuple of leading terms of a marked reduced Gröbner basis
of
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
by Proposition

.c.
The set of all possible such tuples

is computed in steps (1) and (2).
If the loop in steps (4)-(5) finds a tuple

such that
I
is
Z
-separating,
we are done.

In addition, if
s=\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I))
and the algorithm is successful,
then
[

]
, Corollary 4.2, shows that the

-separating re-embedding
of

is optimal.
∎

If we are looking for optimal re-embeddings and
use the method of Proposition

to perform step (5),

is able to certify that no optimal

-separating
re-embedding of

Remark 2.2

Notice that Algorithm

On one side, it can happen that an optimal re-embedding is obtained using a subset

On the other side, it can happen that an optimal re-embedding cannot

The following Example

Example 2.3

Let

2.B. The Gröbner Fan of a Homogeneous Linear Ideal

In the remainder of this subsection we study ways how to perform step (1) of
Algorithm
2.1
. Therefore we now let
I_{L}
be an ideal in
P

generated by linear forms, and our goal is to compute
the Gröbner fan
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I_{L})
, i.e., the set of all marked reduced Gröbner
bases of
I_{L}
.

More precisely, let
L=(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{r})
be a tuple of
linear forms in
P
, and let
I_{L}=\langle L\rangle
.
For
i=1,\dots,r
, we write
\ell_{i}=a_{i1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{in}x_{n}

with
a_{ij}\in K
. Then the matrix
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{r,n}(K)
is
called the
coefficient matrix
of
L
.
To compute
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I_{L})
, the following proposition will be useful.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
L=(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{r})
be a tuple of
K
-linearly independent linear forms in
P
,
let
I_{L}=\langle L\rangle
, and let
A=(a_{ij})
be the coefficient matrix of
L
.
Moreover, let
s\leq r
, let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates in
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
and let
Y=X\setminus Z
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

There exists a term ordering
\sigma
such that

\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
.

(b)

There exists a term ordering
\sigma
such that
the residue classes of the elements of
Y
generate the
K
-algebra
P/\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
.

(c)

The residue classes of the elements of
Y
generate the

K
-algebra
P/I_{L}
.

(d)

The residue classes of the elements of
Y
generate the

K
-vector space
P_{1}/\langle L\rangle_{K}
.

(e)

Let
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
be the indices such that

z_{j}=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,\dots,s
. Then the columns
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}
of
A

are linearly independent.

Proof.

To show (a)
\Rightarrow
(b), it suffices to note that
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
implies that the canonical map
K[Y]\cong P/\langle Z\rangle\longrightarrow P/\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
is surjective.
The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(c) follows
from Macaulay’s Basis Theorem (see
[
12
]
, Theorem 1.5.7).

To prove (c)
\Rightarrow
(d), we note that
I_{L}

is generated by linear forms, and therefore
P/I_{L}
is isomorphic to a polynomial ring.
Thus the residue classes of a tuple of indeterminates
Y
are a
K
-algebra
system of generators of the ring
P/I_{L}
if and only if they are a system of generators
of the
K
-vector space given by its homogeneous component
P_{1}/(I_{L})_{1}
of degree one,
where
(I_{L})_{1}=\langle L\rangle_{K}
.
The assumption in (d) implies that the indeterminates in
Z
can be expressed as linear
combinations of the indeterminates in
Y
. Consequently, any elimination ordering
\sigma

for
Z
satisfies
\langle Z\rangle\subseteq\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
. This proves (d)
\Rightarrow
(a).

Finally, we show that (d) and (e) are equivalent. Notice that both conditions
imply
s\leq r
. The tuple
Y=X\setminus Z

is a system of generators of the vector space
P_{1}/\langle L\rangle_{K}

if and only if
\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{r}\}

together with
Y
is a system of generators of
P_{1}
. This means that, if we extend
A
with

n-s
rows that are unit vectors having their non-zero entries at the positions
of the indeterminates in
Y
, the resulting matrix
\overline{A}
of size
(r+n-s)\times n

has the maximal rank
n
. Now we consider the matrix
A_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})}

consisting of columns
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}
of
A
. By renumbering the
indeterminates, we may assume that
i_{1}=1
,
\dots
,
i_{s}=s
, and hence
that the extended matrix is upper block triangular of the form

\overline{A}\;=\;\begin{pmatrix}A_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})}&\ast\\
0&I_{n-s}\end{pmatrix}

where

Now it is clear that the rows of

of the proposition.
∎

The following lemma collects some preparatory results.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

(b)

Claim (a) follows from the fact that every tuple of

To prove (b) we observe that a minimal Gröbner basis of a linear homogeneous ideal

Now we are ready to present the key result for computing

Theorem 2.9
.

(The Gröbner Fan of a Homogeneous Linear
Ideal)

Let
L=(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{s})
be a tuple of
K
-linearly independent linear forms in
P
,
let
I_{L}=\langle L\rangle
, and let
A=(a_{ij})
be the coefficient matrix of
L
.
Furthermore, let
M
be the set of tuples
(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})

such that

and such that the corresponding
maximal minor of the matrix

(a)

The map
{\varphi}:\;\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I_{L})\longrightarrow M
given by

{\varphi}(Z)=(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})
for
Z=\{x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{s}}\}\in\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I_{L})

and
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}
is well-defined and bijective.

(b)

The map
\psi:\;\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I_{L})\longrightarrow M
given by

\psi(G)={\varphi}(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(G))
for every
G\in\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I)
is well-defined and bijective.

Proof.

First we prove (a).
To begin with, let us check that
{\varphi}
is well-defined. For an element

G\in\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I_{L})
, the tuple
Z=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(G)
satisfies
\langle Z\rangle=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{L})
. Let
Z=\{x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{s}}\}
with
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq n
.
Then Corollary
2.6
.e shows
\det(A_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})})\neq 0
,
and therefore
{\varphi}
is well-defined.

Since the map
{\varphi}
is clearly injective, we still need to show that it is surjective.
Given
(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})\in M
, part (b) of the lemma implies that

L^{\prime}=A_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})}^{-1}\cdot L
is a reduced Gröbner basis of
I_{L}

with leading term set
Z=\{x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{s}}\}
.
Therefore
{\varphi}(Z)=(i_{1},\dots,i_{s})
proves the desired surjectivity.

To show (b), we note that the map
\psi
is clearly well-defined and injective.
By the definition of
\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I_{L})
, it is also surjective.
∎

4.
The Arrow Degree

Let us continue to use the notation and definitions of the
preceding section. In particular, let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}

be an order ideal in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, let
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
, and let
C=\{c_{ij}\mid i=1,\dots,\mu;\;j=1,\dots,\nu\}
be the set of coefficients of the generic

{\mathcal{O}}
-border prebasis
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{\nu}\}
, where

g_{j}=b_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}c_{ij}\,t_{i}
for
j=1,\dots,\nu
.

For the very detailed analysis of the natural set of generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})

performed in the subsequent parts of this paper, the following grading
will be very useful (see
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}\!=\!\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be an order ideal in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, let

\partial{\mathcal{O}}\!=\!\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
, and let

K[C]=K[c_{ij}\mid i=1,\dots,\mu;\;j=1,\dots,\nu]
, where
K
is a field.

(a)

The
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading on
K[C]
defined by

W\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n,\mu\nu}(\mathbb{Z})
, where we let

\deg_{W}(c_{ij})=\log(b_{j})-\log(t_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,\mu

and for
j=1,\dots,\nu
, is called the
arrow grading
.

(b)

The
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
K[C]
defined by
letting
\deg(c_{ij})=\deg(b_{j})-\deg(t_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,\mu

and
j=1,\dots,\nu
is called the
total arrow grading
.

The following result is shown in
[
16
]
, Lemma 3.4.

Proposition 4.2
.

The ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is homogeneous with respect to the arrow grading
on
K[C]
.
In particular, since the total arrow grading is coarser than the arrow grading,
the ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is also homogeneous with respect to the total arrow grading.

As for the arrow degrees of the indeterminates
c_{ij}
, we have the
following easy observation (see also
[
7
]
, Lemma 4.1.1).

Proposition 4.3
.

For all
i\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, the arrow degree

\deg_{W}(c_{ij})
has at least one positive component.

Proof.

Suppose that
i\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}

such that all components of
\log(b_{j})-\log(t_{i})
are negative.
This means that
b_{j}\mid t_{i}
, and therefore the fact that
{\mathcal{O}}

is an order ideal implies
b_{j}\in{\mathcal{O}}
, a contradiction.
∎

This proposition inspires the following definition which is in accordance
with
[
7
]
, Section 4.1.

Definition 4.4
.

For
i\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, the
indeterminate
c_{ij}
is said to be a
standard indeterminate
,
and
\deg_{W}(c_{ij})
is called a
standard arrow
, if

\deg_{W}(c_{ij})
contains exactly one positive component.

If
\deg_{W}(c_{ij})
contains at least two positive entries,
we say that
c_{ij}
is a
positive indeterminate
in
C

and that
\deg_{W}(c_{ij})
is a
positive arrow.

Our next result yields important restrictions for the indeterminates
appearing in the quadratic parts of the natural generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.
It uses the following notion (see also
[
6
]
, Section 4.1).

Definition 4.8
.

Let
j\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, and let
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Then the border term
b_{j}
is called
x_{\ell}
-exposed

if it is of the form
b_{j}=x_{\ell}\,t_{i}
with

i\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
. In this case we also say that
t_{i}

x_{\ell}
-exposes
the border term
b_{j}
.

Now we are ready to formulate the proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be an order ideal in
\mathbb{T}^{n}

with border
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
.

(a)

Let
j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
be such that
b_{j},b_{j^{\prime}}

are next-door neighbours with
b_{j}=x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}
, and let
i\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
.
Assume that
b_{\lambda_{1}},\dots,b_{\lambda_{s}}\in\partial{\mathcal{O}}
are the

x_{\ell}
-exposed border terms, and let
b_{\lambda_{p}}=x_{\ell}\,t_{\varrho_{p}}

for
p=1,\dots,s
. Then the quadratic terms in the support of
\mathop{\rm ND}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{i}

are the products
c_{i\lambda_{p}}\,c_{\varrho_{p}j^{\prime}}
with
p=1,\dots,s
.

(b)

Let
j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
be such that
b_{j},b_{j^{\prime}}

are across-the-rim neighbours with
b_{j}=x_{\ell}t_{m^{\prime}}
and
b_{j^{\prime}}=x_{k}t_{m^{\prime}}

for some
m^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
. For every
m\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
,
the quadratic terms in the support of
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
are of the following types.

(1)

Let
b_{\kappa_{1}},\dots,b_{\kappa_{s}}\in\partial{\mathcal{O}}
be the

x_{k}
-exposed border terms, and let us write
b_{\kappa_{p}}=x_{\ell}\,t_{\varrho_{p}}

for
p=1,\dots,s
.
Then the terms
c_{m\kappa_{p}}\,c_{\varrho_{p}j}
may appear in the support of
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
.

(2)

Let
b_{\lambda_{1}},\dots,b_{\lambda_{u}}\in\partial{\mathcal{O}}
be the

x_{\ell}
-exposed border terms, and let us write
b_{\lambda_{q}}=x_{\ell}t_{\sigma_{q}}

for
q=1,\dots,u
. Then the terms
c_{m\lambda_{q}}\,c_{\sigma_{q}j^{\prime}}

may appear in the support of
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
.

Proof.

Let
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{\nu}\}
be the generic
{\mathcal{O}}
-border prebasis.

First we prove (a). The polynomials in
\mathop{\rm ND}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})
are the coefficients of the
terms
t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}
in the reduction
x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}-g_{j}
, viewed as a polynomial in

K[C][x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. The leading terms
x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}
and
b_{j}
cancel by
definition. Hence we only have to reduce the
x_{\ell}
-exposed border terms
in
x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}
. The coefficient of
b_{\lambda_{p}}
in
x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}

is
c_{\varrho_{p}j^{\prime}}
. The coefficient of
t_{i}
in
g_{\lambda_{p}}
is
c_{i\lambda_{p}}
.
Therefore the coefficient of
t_{i}
in
c_{\varrho_{p}j^{\prime}}\,g_{\lambda_{p}}

is
c_{\varrho_{p}j^{\prime}}c_{i\lambda_{p}}
and it will be a part of the coefficient
of
t_{i}
in the final result of the reduction, i.e., in
\mathop{\rm ND}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{i}
.

Next we prove (b). Again the entries of
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})
are obtained
as the coefficients of
t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}
in the reduction of
x_{k}g_{j}-x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}
,
and again the highest terms
x_{k}b_{j}
and
x_{\ell}b_{j^{\prime}}
cancel.
To reduce
x_{k}g_{j}
, we have to reduce the
x_{k}
-exposed border terms

b_{\kappa_{1}},\dots,b_{\kappa_{s}}
. The coefficient of
b_{\kappa_{p}}
in
x_{k}g_{j}
is

c_{\varrho_{p}j}
. The polynomial
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
has arrow degree
\deg_{W}(c_{ij})+e_{k}
.
It is therefore the coefficient of
t_{m}
in the reduction of
x_{k}g_{j}-x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}
. The coefficient of
t_{m}
in
g_{\kappa_{p}}
is
c_{m\kappa_{p}}
.
Hence the coefficient of
t_{m}
in
c_{\varrho_{p}j}\,g_{\kappa_{p}}
is

c_{\varrho_{p}j}c_{m\kappa_{p}}
, and it may appear in
\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
.
These quadratic terms are the ones listed in case (1).

The analysis of the terms in the reduction of
x_{\ell}g_{j^{\prime}}

is completely analogous and leads to the quadratic terms in case (2).

5.
Cotangent Equivalence Classes and Optimal Embeddings

As before, we let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
,
let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be an order ideal in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
let
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
be its border, and let

C=\{c_{ij}\mid i=1,\dots,\mu;\;j=1,\dots,\nu\}
be the
set of coefficients of the generic
{\mathcal{O}}
-border prebasis.
Recall that the affine coordinate ring of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is

B_{\mathcal{O}}=K[C]/I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
, and let
{\mathfrak{m}}=\langle c_{ij}+I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})\mid i=1,\dots,\mu;\;j=1,\dots,\nu\rangle
be the maximal ideal of
B_{\mathcal{O}}

generated by the residue classes of the indeterminates
c_{ij}
.

In Section
2

we discussed how to use
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))\rangle)
to
detect
Z
-separating, possibly optimal, re-embeddings of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.
In the framework of border basis schemes further tools are available.
Example
5.8
at the end of this section illustrates
their applicability.

Definition 5.1
.

For every indeterminate
c_{ij}\in C
, let
\bar{c}_{ij}
denote
its residue class in the cotangent space
{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}

of
B_{\mathcal{O}}
at the origin.

(a)

The relation
\sim
on
C
defined
by
c_{ij}\sim c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\Leftrightarrow\bar{c}_{ij}=\bar{c}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

is an equivalence relation called
cotangent equivalence
.

(b)

An indeterminate
c_{ij}\in C
is called
trivial

if
\bar{c}_{ij}=0
. The trivial indeterminates form the
trivial
cotangent equivalence class
in
C
.

(c)

A non-trivial indeterminate
c_{ij}\in C
is called
basic
if
its cotangent equivalence class consists only of
c_{ij}
. In this case,
the cotangent equivalence class
\{c_{ij}\}
is also called
basic
.

(d)

A non-trivial indeterminate
c_{ij}\in C
is called
proper

if its cotangent equivalence class contains at least two elements. In this case,
the cotangent equivalence class of
c_{ij}
is also called
proper
.

The meaning of these notions will become clear in the next theorems.
First we need a lemma which provides further information about the above definition.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let us assume to be in the above setting.

(a)

The union
U
of the supports of the elements in
a minimal set of generators of the ideal
\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))\rangle

does not depend on the choice of a minimal set of generators.

(b)

The set of basic indeterminates is
C\setminus U
.

(c)

The union of the sets of trivial and proper indeterminates is
U
.

(d)

The set of basic indeterminates is contained in any set of indeterminates
whose residue classes modulo
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
generate the
K
-algebra
B_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

To prove (a) we note that any minimal set of generators of

\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))\rangle
is also a minimal set of generators
of the
K
-vector space
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))
.
Let
A
and
B
be two such sets. Since every linear form
\ell
in
A

is a linear combination of linear forms in
B
, each indeterminate in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(\ell)

is in the support of some linear form in
B
. By interchanging the roles of
A
and
B
,
the conclusion follows.

Since
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
C
, to prove claims (b) and (c) it suffices
to show that a basic indeterminate is not in
U
, while trivial and proper indeterminates
are in
U
.
For a contradiction, assume that
c_{ij}\in U
is a basic indeterminate.
From
c_{ij}\in U
and the fact that
c_{ij}
is the only element in its equivalence class,
we deduce that there is a polynomial in
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
of the form
c_{ij}+q

with
q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{2}
. Hence we get
\bar{c}_{ij}=0
, a contradiction.
Next let
c_{ij}
be trivial. Then there is a polynomial in
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
of the form
c_{ij}+q

with
q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{2}
, and hence we get
c_{ij}\in U
. Next, let
c_{ij}
be proper.
Then there exist another indeterminate
c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
and a polynomial in
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
of the form

c_{ij}-c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+q
with
q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{2}
. Hence we get
c_{ij}\in U
.

Finally, we prove (d). From (b) we know that the basic indeterminates do not belong to the
support of any generator of
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))
. Hence the claim follows from
Proposition
1.2
.c.
∎

Definition 5.3
.

Let
E=\{c_{i_{1}j_{1}},\dots,c_{i_{p}j_{p}}\}
be a proper equivalence class in
C
,
and let
\sigma
be a term ordering on

Notation 5.4
.

Let
\sigma
be a term ordering on
{\mathbb{T}}^{\mu\nu}
.
In accordance with the notation introduced in Proposition

the unique minimal set of terms generating the ideal

In the following theorem we give an explicit representation of

This implies a description of

For instance, it does not have to deal with huge matrices.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
E_{0}
be the trivial equivalence class and
E_{1},\dots,E_{q}

the proper equivalence classes in
C
. Let
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathop{\rm lin}\nolimits}=\langle\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))\rangle

be the ideal generated by the linear parts of the polynomials in
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.

(a)

Let
\sigma
be a term ordering on
\mathbb{T}^{\mu\nu}
.
Then we have
S_{\sigma}=E_{0}\cup E_{1}^{\sigma}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}^{\sigma}
,
and hence
\#S_{\sigma}=\#E_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\#E_{i}-q
.

(b)

For
i=1,\dots,q
, let
E_{i}^{\ast}
be a set obtained from
E_{i}

by deleting one of its elements. Then there exists a term ordering
\sigma

on
\mathbb{T}^{\mu\nu}
such that we have

S_{\sigma}=E_{0}\cup E_{1}^{\ast}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}^{\ast}
.

(c)

Let
\Sigma
be the set of all sets of the form

E_{0}\cup E_{1}^{\ast}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}^{\ast}
, where
E_{i}^{\ast}
is obtained
from the set

by deleting one of its elements.
Then the map
{\varphi}:\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathop{\rm lin}\nolimits})\longrightarrow\Sigma
given by

{\varphi}(S_{\sigma})=E_{0}\cup E_{1}^{\sigma}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}^{\sigma}

is well-defined and bijective.

(d)

We have

\#\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathop{\rm lin}\nolimits})=\#\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathop{\rm lin}\nolimits})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{q}\#E_{i}
.

Proof.

To prove claim (a) we observe that the inclusion
E_{0}\subseteq S_{\sigma}
follows from

E_{0}\subseteq\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))
, and that the inclusion

S_{\sigma}\subseteq E_{0}\cup E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}

follows from Lemma
5.2
.c.

For

, we write the proper equivalence class

such that
c_{i_{1}j_{1}}>_{\sigma}\cdots>_{\sigma}c_{i_{p}j_{p}}
.
Using the definition of a proper equivalence class, it follows that

c_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}-c_{i_{p}j_{p}}\in\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(c_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}-c_{i_{p}j_{p}})=c_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}

for every
\ell\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
.
Hence we have proved

E_{0}\cup E_{1}^{\sigma}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}^{\sigma}\;\subseteq\;S_{\sigma}\;\subseteq\;E_{0}\cup E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{q}

Therefore the
\sigma
-smallest
element in each proper equivalence class does not belong to

,
which finishes the proof of (a).

Claim (b) follows from (a) if we show that there exists a term ordering
\sigma

such that

for

.
By definition, the sets

are pairwise disjoint. Consequently, a term ordering
which solves the problem can be chosen as a block term ordering, and hence it
suffices to consider the case

.
So, let

and w.l.o.g. assume
that

for
\ell=2,\dots,p
.
To finish the proof it suffices to take a term ordering
\sigma
such that

c_{i_{\ell}j_{\ell}}>_{\sigma}c_{i_{1}j_{1}}
for
\ell=2,\dots,p
.

Claim (c) follows immediately from (b).
To prove claim (d) we note that the first equality is obvious.
To show
\#\mathop{\rm LTGFan}\nolimits(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})_{\mathop{\rm lin}\nolimits})=\prod_{i=1}^{q}\#E_{i}
, it suffices to
deduce from (b) that the number of the leading term ideals of

equals the number of
q
-tuples of indeterminates
c_{ij}
, exactly one chosen in
each proper equivalence class.

To prove (a), let

be the minimal set of terms generating

Claim (b) follows from the mentioned inclusion

.a.

For the proof of (c), we assume that

is a basic indeterminate. Thus no next-door

Since the border

.
Thus we may assume that there are

is a trivial indeterminate,

has to be divisible by

.
The term

cannot be in the border of
{\mathcal{O}}
, because then also

would be outside

, in contradiction to the hypothesis

.
Therefore there exists an index

such that

.
By Proposition
3.8
, we get

to the hypothesis that
c_{ij}
is basic.

Now we show (d). For a contradiction, assume that
c_{ij}\in C
is proper
and that every element in the cotangent equivalence class of

is an interior indeterminate. By Proposition

, there exists

7.
Simplicial Order Ideals

In this section we continue to examine re-embeddings of border basis schemes
in special cases. We use the notation introduced previously.
Let
K
be a field, let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and let

{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
be the homogeneous maximal ideal of
P
.
The order ideals we are looking at in this section are defined as follows.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. The order ideal

{\mathcal{O}}=\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus{\mathfrak{M}}^{d+1}=\{t\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\mid\deg(t)\leq d\}

is called the
simplicial order ideal
of type
d
in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Let us collect some basic numerical information about simplicial order ideals.

Remark 7.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be the simplicial order ideal of type
d

in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and let
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
be its border.
Then the following formulas hold:

(a)

\mu=\binom{d+n}{n}

(b)

\nu=\binom{d+n}{n-1}

(c)

\#{\mathcal{O}}^{\circ}=\binom{d+n-1}{n}

(d)

\#\nabla{\mathcal{O}}=\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}

(e)

\#C=\binom{d+n}{n}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}

(f)

\#C^{\circ}=\binom{d+n-1}{n}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}

(g)

\#C^{\nabla}=\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}

These formulas follow immediately from the
affine Hilbert function of a polynomial ring (cf.
[
13
]
, Propositions 5.1.13
and 5.6.3.c).

The following proposition allows us to find optimal re-embeddings of border basis
schemes in the case of simplicial order ideals.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
, and let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be the simplicial order ideal of type
d

in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and let
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
be its border.
Furthermore, let
s=\#C^{\circ}=\#{\mathcal{O}}^{\circ}\cdot\#(\partial{\mathcal{O}})=\binom{d+n-1}{n}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}
.
Then there exists a term ordering
\sigma
such that for each indeterminate
c_{ij}\in C^{\circ}
there
is a natural generator
f
of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
with
c_{ij}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f)
.
Consequently,
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for the ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.

Proof.

Let
Z
be the tuple of indeterminates in
C^{\circ}
, ordered arbitrarily.
We define an elimination ordering
\sigma
for
Z
by constructing a suitable matrix
M
.
In the first row of
M
and the column corresponding to an indeterminate
c_{ij}
,
we put the number
2\delta-1
, where
\delta
is the
total arrow degree
\deg(c_{ij})=\deg(b_{j})-\deg(t_{i})=d+1-\deg(t_{i})
.

Next we define the second row of the matrix
M
as follows. For each arrow degree
\delta
,
let
S_{\delta}
be the set of all indeterminates
c_{ij}\in C^{\circ}
with

\deg_{W}(c_{ij})=\delta
. Let
D
be the set of all arrow degrees
\delta
such that

S_{\delta}\neq\emptyset
. For each
\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})
in
D
,
we choose a fixed, for instance the first, number
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\delta_{k}>0
,
and note that this is possible by Proposition
4.3
.
For
c_{ij}\in S_{\delta}
, we write

t_{i}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
with
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we put the number
\alpha_{k}
into the column corresponding to
c_{ij}

of the second row of the matrix
M
. In the columns corresponding to
c_{ij}\in C^{\nabla}

we put zeros into the second row of
M
. Finally, we complete the matrix
M
to
a matrix defining an elimination ordering
\sigma={\rm ord}(M)
for
Z
.

Our goal is to show that
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.
For this purpose we prove that for each
c_{ij}\in C^{\circ}
there exists a
generator
f_{ij}\in\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{O}}}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

The linear part of
f_{ij}
is (up to sign)
c_{ij}
or of the form

c_{ij}-c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
with
c_{ij}>_{\sigma}c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
.

(2)

For every term
c_{\alpha\beta}c_{\gamma\delta}
of degree two in

\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{ij})
, we have
c_{ij}>_{\sigma}c_{\alpha\beta}c_{\gamma\delta}
.

Then it follows that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{ij})=c_{ij}
and hence
\sigma
is a

Z
-separating term ordering for the ideal
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
.

To prove the existence of
f_{ij}
with the desired properties,
we let
c_{ij}\in C^{\circ}
, and we let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})

be the arrow degree of
c_{ij}
. Let
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
be the component of
\delta

with
\delta_{k}>0
chosen in the above construction, and let
t_{i}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. Now we distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

\alpha_{k}=0
. By assumption, the exponent of
b_{j}
with respect to
x_{k}

is positive. Let
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}{\setminus}\{k\}
. Then
x_{\ell}\,b_{j}/x_{k}

is an element of
\partial{\mathcal{O}}
, and hence equal to
b_{j^{\prime}}
for some
j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
.
Notice that
(b_{j},b_{j^{\prime}})
is an across-the-rim neighbour pair.
Moreover, the term
t_{m}=x_{\ell}t_{i}
is contained in
{\mathcal{O}}
, since
t_{i}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\circ}
.
However
t_{m}
is not divisible by
x_{k}
. Next, consider the polynomial

f_{ij}=\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
. The linear part of
f_{ij}
is (up to sign)
c_{ij}
. Furthermore, the
terms in the quadratic part are of the form
c_{m\kappa}c_{\varrho j^{\prime\prime}}
where
j^{\prime\prime}\in\{j,j^{\prime}\}

and
c_{\varrho j^{\prime\prime}}\in C^{\nabla}
. Here
\deg(t_{m})=\deg(t_{i})+1
implies that the entry
in the first row of
M
corresponding to
c_{m\kappa}
is two less than the entry
corresponding to
c_{ij^{\prime\prime}}
.
Since the entry corresponding to
c_{\varrho j^{\prime\prime}}
is one, it follows that we have

c_{ij}>_{\sigma}c_{m\kappa}c_{\varrho j^{\prime\prime}}
. Thus we get
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{ij})=c_{ij}
.

Case 2:

\alpha_{k}>0
. In this case, we choose an index
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}{\setminus}\{k\}
and note that
x_{\ell}b_{j}/x_{k}
is in
\partial{\mathcal{O}}
. Let
j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}

such that
x_{k}b_{j^{\prime}}=x_{\ell}b_{j}
. Since
t_{i}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\circ}
, we know that
t_{m}=x_{\ell}t_{i}

is in
{\mathcal{O}}
. Since
\alpha_{k}>0
, it follows that
x_{k}
divides
t_{m}
and there exists an
index
i^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,\mu\}
with
x_{k}t_{i^{\prime}}=t_{m}=x_{\ell}t_{i}
.
Then
(b_{j},b_{j^{\prime}})
is an across-the-rim neighbour pair, and we consider the polynomial

f_{ij}=\mathop{\rm AR}\nolimits(j,j^{\prime})_{m}
. The linear part of
f_{ij}
is (up to sign) given by

c_{ij}-c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
. Here the facts that the total arrow degree of
c_{ij}
and
c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

are the same and that the
x_{k}
-exponent of
t_{i^{\prime}}
is
\alpha_{k}-1
imply

c_{ij}>_{\sigma}c_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
.

The terms of degree two in the support of
f_{ij}
are seen to be smaller than
c_{ij}

with respect to
\sigma
in exactly the same way as in Case 1. Altogether, it follows
that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{ij})=c_{ij}
, and the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

As mentioned above, this proposition has a number of useful consequences.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
d\geq 1
, and let
{\mathcal{O}}=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\}
be the simplicial order ideal of type
d

in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with border
\partial{\mathcal{O}}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\nu}\}
.

(a)

The linear part of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is given by

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))=\langle C^{\circ}\rangle_{K}
.

(b)

We have
\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{\mathcal{O}}))=\#C^{\nabla}=\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}
.

(c)

We have
\mathop{\rm edim}\nolimits(B_{\mathcal{O}})=\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(B_{\mathcal{O}}))
.

In particular, for the term ordering
\sigma
constructed in the proposition,
the
Z
-separating re-embedding

\varPhi:K[C]/I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})\longrightarrow K[C^{\nabla}]/(I\cap K[C^{\nabla}])

defined by the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
is optimal.

Proof.

To prove (a), we note that the indeterminates of
C^{\circ}
are in

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))
by the proposition. By
[
16
]
, Cor. 2.8.b, there is no
linear part of one of the natural generators of
I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})
which contains
an indeterminate of
C^{\nabla}
in its support. Hence we get

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}))=\langle C^{\circ}\rangle_{K}
.

The first equality in (b) follows directly from the proposition and (a).
Since we have the equality
\#C=\#{\mathcal{O}}\cdot\#(\partial{\mathcal{O}})=\binom{d+n}{n}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}
, we get

\#C^{\nabla}=\#C-\#C^{\circ}={\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n}}\cdot{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n-1}}-{\textstyle\binom{d+n-1}{n}}\cdot{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n-1}}={\textstyle\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}}\cdot{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n-1}}

Finally, we prove (c). As an application of
[
10
]
, Cor. 4.2, we know that
the embedding dimension of
B_{\mathcal{O}}
is
\#C-\#C^{\circ}=\#C^{\nabla}
,
and this number is equal to
\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{\mathcal{O}}))
by (b).
∎

This proposition allows us to check when a simplicial order ideal
has a border basis scheme which is an affine cell.

Corollary 7.5
.

In the setting of the proposition, let
p=(0,\dots,0)
be the monomial point of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

(a)

If
n=2
then
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is isomorphic to an affine space.

(b)

If
n\geq 3
then
p
is a singular point of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

For
n=2
, it follows from the proposition that

\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
can be embedded into an affine space of dimension

\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{\mathcal{O}}))=\binom{d+1}{1}\cdot\binom{d+2}{1}=(d+1)(d+2)
.
It is also known that the scheme
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}
is irreducible and its dimension is given by
the formula

\dim(\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}})=2\cdot\#({\mathcal{O}})=2\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2}=(d+1)(d+2)
.
The conclusion follows.

To prove (b), we observe that the dimension of the principal component of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{O}}

is
n\cdot\#{\mathcal{O}}=n\cdot\binom{d+n}{n}
.
Moreover, it follows from part (c) of the proposition that

\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Cot}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{\mathcal{O}}))=\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}\cdot\binom{d+n}{n-1}
.
Hence it suffices to show that for
n\geq 3
we have

[formula]

n\cdot{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n}}<{\textstyle\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}}\cdot{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n-1}}

To prove (1), we note that
n\cdot\binom{d+n}{n}{=}(d+n)\cdot\binom{d+n-1}{n-1}
.
Hence (1) is equivalent to

[formula]

d+n<{\textstyle\binom{d+n}{n-1}}

Therefore we need to show that for
n\geq 3
we have

1<{\textstyle\frac{(d+n-1)\cdot(d+n-2)\cdots(d+2)}{(n-1)!}}={\textstyle\frac{d+n-1}{n-1}}\cdot{\textstyle\frac{d+n-2}{n-2}}\cdots{\textstyle\frac{d+2}{2}}

which is obvious, since
d\geq 1
.

Notice that for
n=2
we have
d+n=\binom{d+n}{n-1}
. Thus we have equality in (2),
and hence also equality in (1). This shows

On the Smallest Number of Functions Representing Isotropic Functions of Scalars, Vectors and Tensors
M.H.B.M. Shariff

Abstract
In this paper, we address the open problem (stated in Pennisi and Trovato, 1987. Int. J. Engng Sci., 25(8), 1059-1065) associated with the irreducibility of representations for isotropic functions. In particular, we prove that for isotropic functions that depend on P P vectors, N N symmetric tensors and M M non-symmetric tensors (a) the number of irreducible invariants for a scalar-valued isotropic function is 3 ​ P + 9 ​ M + 6 ​ N − 3 3P+9M+6N-3 (b) the number of irreducible vectors for a vector-valued isotropic function is 3 3 and (c) the number of irreducible tensors for a tensor-valued isotropic function is at most 9 9 . The irreducible numbers in given (a), (b) and (c) are much lower than those obtained in the literature. This significant reduction in the number of irreducible scalar/vector/tensor-valued functions have the potential to substantially simplify modelling complexity.

3.2
Vector

The vector function
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\,

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{m}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{m}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i}^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{m}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{j}-\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{j}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i})\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{m}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}i,j=1,2,\ldots,N\,:i<j\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}m=1,2,\ldots,P\,.

Theorem 1

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}g_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,,

\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}

\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{1}

\displaystyle S=\{\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{2},\ldots\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{N},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{2},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{P}\}\,.

(21)

Proof:

(a) If (
20

g
is clearly a vector-valued isotropic function, since the coefficients
g_{i}
are isotropic invariants of the set

S
(
21

(b) For
N\geq 1

P\geq 0
. Let
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}
be unit eigenvectors of the symmetric tensor
\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{1}
(see (
4

Hence we can write

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}[\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}]\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}r=1,2,\ldots,N,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}s=1,2,\ldots,P

(22)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}[\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}]\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,.

(23)

Let scalar function

\displaystyle g_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,.

(24)

We then have

\displaystyle g_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,.

(25)

In view of (

\displaystyle g_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=g_{i}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\,,

In the case when

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}g_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}g_{i}=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,.

Theorem 2

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i,j=1}t_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\,,

(a) If (

(b) Using the basis

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i,j=1}t_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\,,

\displaystyle t_{ij}=g_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\,.

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i,j=1}\bar{t}_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{t}_{ij}=\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}

\displaystyle=g_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\,.

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}\bar{t}_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}=\sum_{i,j=1}t_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\,.

(36)

Since
Q
is arbitrary, we have

\displaystyle g_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=g_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T},\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})\,

(37)

which implies that the functions
t_{ij}=g_{ij}
must depend on
P
-scalar-valued isotropic functions of
S
. Since

g_{ij}=g_{ji}
, all tensor-valued isotropic functions can be written as a linear combination of only six symmetric tensors

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}(i=1,2,3)\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}(i=1,2;j=2,3,i<j)\,.

(38)

Hence, we can express

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{r},\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{s})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}g_{ii}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}+\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i})\,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}(i=1,2;j=2,3)\,.

(39)

All symmetric tensors in (
30
) generated by Smith
[
24
]
can be expressed in terms of the six symmetric tensors given in
(
38
), for example, the symmetric tensor

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{j}+\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{j}\mbox{\boldmath$A$}_{i}=\sum_{p=1}^{3}g_{pp}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{p}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{p}+\sum_{p<q}g_{pq}(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{p}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{q}+\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{q}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{p})\,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}(p=1,2;q=2,3)\,,

(40)

where

\displaystyle g_{pp}=2\sum_{m=1}^{3}{A}^{(i)}_{pm}{A}^{(j)}_{mp}\,,{\mbox{\hskip 7.22743pt}}g_{pq}=\sum_{m=1}^{3}({A}^{(i)}_{pm}{A}^{(j)}_{mq}+{A}^{(i)}_{qm}{A}^{(j)}_{mp})\,.

(41)

Hence, when a tensor-valued function is expressed in terms of a linear combinations of Smith’s functions given in (
30
), it can then be
expressed in terms of a linear combination of the six symmetric spectral tensors given in (
38
); this further validates our claim
that the irreducible basis contains only six symmetric tensors.

The above theorem proves that the irreducible set contains only six linearly independent symmetric tensors. This drastically
reduce the complexity in physical modelling. For example, Merodio and Rajagopal
[
3
]
modelled viscoelastic solids, where the Cauchy stress

\mbox{\boldmath$a$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}

36

omission of invariants and tensors. However,

result in an incomplete representation of the full range of mechanical response subjected to a continuum. However, using the results obtained here,

Theorem 3

If
\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})
is an isotropic tensor function then
\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})
is coaxial with
V
and hence

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}=\mbox{\boldmath$G$}\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\,.

(42)

Proof

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}
be an eigenvector of
V
and choose

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}=2\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$I$}=\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\,.

(43)

In view of
\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=\lambda_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$V$}=\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\rightarrow\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}=\mbox{\boldmath$V$}\,.

(44)

From (
29
) we get

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}=\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\,.

(45)

Hence

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}t_{ij}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{j}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\,.

(46)

Note that
\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}
and hence we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}t_{i1}\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}t_{i1}(2\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$I$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}=2t_{11}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}-\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\,.

(47)

Hence

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}=t_{11}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}\,,

(48)

which implies that
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{1}
is an eigenvector of
\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})
and
t_{11}
is an eigenvalue of
G
. In a similar fashion,
choosing
\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}=2\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{r}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{r}-\mbox{\boldmath$I$}=\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{T}
,

, we can easily derive that

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$G$}(\mbox{\boldmath$V$})=\sum_{i=1}t_{ii}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\,,

(49)

and the theorem is proved.

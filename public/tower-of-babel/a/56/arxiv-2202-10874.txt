Groebner fan and embedded resolutions of ideals on toric varieties
F. Aroca, M. Gómez-Morales, H. Mourtada

Abstract
We consider the notions of Groebner fan and Newton non-degeneracy for an ideal on a toric variety, extending the two existing notions for ideals on affine spaces. We prove, without assumptions on the characteristic of the base fields, that the ”Groebner fan” of such an ideal is actually a polyhedral fan and that a sub-variety defined by a Newton non-degenerate ideal on a toric variety X σ X_{\sigma} admits a toric embedded resolution of singularities Z ⟶ X σ . Z\longrightarrow X_{\sigma}.



Keywords–
Embedded resolution of singularities, Newton non-degenerate ideals, Groebner fan, Toric varieties, Tropical Geometry.

Introduction

Resolution of singularities is a fundamental tool in Algebraic Geometry, which also has applications
in several other domains like Differential Geometry, Analysis and Number Theory. While its existence
when working over fields of characteristic zero was proved by Hironaka in 1968
[

]
, in positive characteristics this (and its super local couterpart: local uniformization) remains a widely open problem,

In characteristic zero, a huge amount of work has been done to simplify Hironaka’s proof and to make it functorial
[

,

,

]
. The majority
of this work follows one philosophy which is about making ”very” small improvements to the singularity
by blowing up smooth centers. See also
[

,

]
for resolution of singularities using a sequence of weighted blowing ups.

In this article, we are concerned with toric resolutions of singularities which, when they exist, are obtained
with one toric morphism. Such resolutions are then very useful in applications and allow to effectively compute
many important invariants of singularities,

[

,

,

,

For hypersufaces in affine spaces, the so called condition of being Newton non-degenerate, introduced in the seventies by Khovanski, Kuchnirenko and Varchenko
[

, guaranties the existence of toric resolution of singularities. Recently, this was generalized to higher codimensions in
[

,

]
via the equivalent notions (but expressed in different languages) of Schon varieties and Newton non-degenerate ideals.

In this article, we extend this last notion to ideals on toric varieties and prove that their associated varieties admit toric embedded resolutions of singularities

[

,

,

. We insist here that in the context of this article the embedding is fixed. We now explain the results of this article; the reader who is unfamiliar with toric geometry

Since the problem is of local nature, we will be considering affine normal toric varieties. Let
K
be a perfect field. Let

be a free abelian group of rank

Let

,\sigma\cap-\sigma=\{0\}\$).

The initial form of

0

Theorem 1.1

The closures (with respect to the topology of

The fan in theorem

Let

Another main result of this article is that a non-degenerate ideal admits a toric embedded resolution in the following sense.

Let

is a proper birational morphism, in which irreducible components of the total transform

are smooth (in particular, the strict transform of

Note that the variety

is smooth since it is associated with a regular fan. The main result of

is the special case of theorem

In the special case where

. The full generality of theorem

).

Remark 1.4
.

The embedded resolution of singularities in theorem

\mathrm{I}n_{v}(\mathscr{J})=\mathrm{I}n_{v^{\prime}}(\mathscr{J})\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{if and only if}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)=\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J).

(5)

In particular the equivalence classes of the relation
\sim
defines a polyhedral fan which is a subdivision of
\sigma
and which is obtained from the Groebner fan
of
J
intersected with
\sigma.

Proof.

The reverse implication of
(\ref{fan})
is a direct consequence of proposition
3.4
. Let us prove the direct implication. For that, we suppose that
\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)\not=\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J).
Since,
I_{\sigma}=Ker(\Psi)\subset J
, we have by proposition
3.2
(parts 1 and 2)

\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(I_{\sigma})=I_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(I_{\sigma})=I_{\sigma}\subset\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J).

So we can choose basis of the forms

\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(I_{\sigma})\subset\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{l},H_{1},\ldots,H_{k})

\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(I_{\sigma})\subset\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J)=(G^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,G^{\prime}_{l^{\prime}},H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}),

where
I_{\sigma}=(H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}).

Now, since we assumed that
\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)\not=\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J),
there exist an
i
such that
G_{i}\not\in\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J).

We have
\Psi(G_{i})\not\in\Psi(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J)):
indeed, otherwise we will have
\Psi(G_{i}-G^{\prime})=0
for some
G^{\prime}\in\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J);
this implies that
G_{i}-G^{\prime}=H\in I_{\sigma}
and hence
G_{i}=G^{\prime}+H\in\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J),
which is a contradiction. We deduce, since
\Psi(G_{i})\not\in\Psi(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J))
, that
\mathrm{I}n_{v}(\mathscr{J})\not=\mathrm{I}n_{v^{\prime}}(\mathscr{J}).
So we have proved that
if
\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J))\not=\mathrm{I}n_{\omega^{\prime}}(J))
then
\mathrm{I}n_{v}(\mathscr{J})\not=\mathrm{I}n_{v^{\prime}}(\mathscr{J});
this ends the proof.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

We keep the notation of theorem
3.5
. Using the main result of
[
FJT
]
, we know how to compute the Groebner fan of
J
(i.e. there is an algorithm for this); hence, applying theorem
3.5
, we obtain an algorithm to compute the (toric) Groebner fan of
\mathscr{J}.
At the same time, in practice and for dimension reasons, it should be much easier to compute directly the (toric) Groebner fan of
\mathscr{J}
; indeed, in general
s
is much greater than
n.

We now are ready to prove theorem
1.3
.

Proof of theorem
1.3
.

For
\mathscr{J}\in\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}
satisfying the Newton non-degeneracy property, we will prove that
J:=\Psi^{-1}(\mathscr{J})\subset\textbf{K}[\textbf{y}]
is Newton non-degenerate. For that, we need to prove that for any
\omega\in\textbf{R}_{+}^{s},
the singular locus of
V(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J))\subset V((y_{1}\cdots y_{s})).

Thanks to proposition
3.2
, we only need to consider the case where
\omega\in\phi(\sigma)
; otherwise, for
\omega\not\in\phi(\sigma)=\textrm{Trop}\,(I_{\sigma}),
since
I_{\sigma}\subset J,
we have that
\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J)
contains monomials and hence
V(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J))\subset V((y_{1}\cdots y_{s})),
in particular its singular locus is. Now, assume that
\omega\in\phi(\sigma);
by proposition
3.4
, we have that
\Psi(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J))=\mathrm{I}n_{v}(\mathscr{J});
hence we have

V(\mathrm{I}n_{v}(\mathscr{J}))=V(\mathrm{I}n_{\omega}(J))\subset X_{\sigma}\subset\textbf{A}^{s}.

We hence know, by the Newton non-degeneracy condition defined in
1.2
that the singular locus is included in the complement of the torus
(\textbf{K}^{*})^{n}
in
X_{\sigma}.
So we need to prove that the complement of the torus
(\textbf{K}^{*})^{n}
in
X_{\sigma}
is sent by the embedding
X_{\sigma}\subset\textbf{A}^{s}
to the complement of the torus
(\textbf{K}^{*})^{s}.
Note that the embedding
(\textbf{K}^{*})^{n}\hookrightarrow(\textbf{K}^{*})^{s}
is given by

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto(\textbf{t}^{\alpha^{1}},\ldots,\textbf{t}^{\alpha^{s}}),

where
\textbf{t}^{\alpha^{i}}=t_{1}^{\alpha^{i}_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{\alpha^{i}_{n}}.
Now, we know (
e.g.
section 3.2 in
[
Cox
]
) that

X_{\sigma}\setminus(\textbf{K}^{*})^{n}=\bigsqcup_{0\not=\tau\subseteq\sigma}\mathbb{O}_{\tau},

where
\mathbb{O}_{\tau}
is
the orbit associated to
\tau;
note that the orbit
\mathbb{O}_{0}
is equal to
(\textbf{K}^{*})^{n}.
Then, by the definition of
\mathbb{O}_{\tau}
we know that if
\alpha^{i}\not\in\tau^{\perp},
any point
p
which is in
\mathbb{O}_{\tau}
will belong to
V(y_{i})\subset\textbf{A}^{s}.
Hence, we deduce that
J
is Newton non-degenerate as an ideal in
\textbf{K}[\textbf{y}].

By the main theorem of
[
AGS
]
, we know that any regular subdivision
\widetilde{\Sigma}
of the Groebner fan of
J
induces
a proper birational map
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\longrightarrow\textbf{A}^{s}
(where
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
is the toric variety associated
with the fan
\widetilde{\Sigma}
), which is an embedded resolution of
V(Y)\subset\textbf{K}^{s}.
Now, by theorem
3.5
, the fan
\tilde{\Sigma}
induces a
regular subdivision of the (toric) Greobner fan of
\mathscr{J}
(actually, one can go the other way and construct
\tilde{\Sigma}
from a regular subdivision of the -toric- Groebner fan of
\mathscr{J}).
The morphism
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\longrightarrow\textbf{A}^{s}
is an embedded resolution of
X_{\sigma}\subset\textbf{A}^{s}
(see
[
GPT
]
) and the strict transform of
X_{\sigma}
is
X_{\Sigma}.
The strict transform of
V(\mathscr{J})\subset X_{\sigma}
in
X_{\Sigma}
is the same
as the strict transform
Y^{\prime}
of
V(J)\subset\textbf{A}^{s}
in
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}.
In particular, this strict transform is smooth, and since it is transverse to the irreducible components of the exceptional locus of
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\longrightarrow\textbf{A}^{s}
, we deduce that
Y^{\prime}
is transverse to the exceptional locus of
X_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow X_{\sigma}
, which is nothing but the transverse intersection of the exceptional locus of
Z_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
with
X_{\Sigma}
and the theorem follows.

Abstract
. In this paper we consider random dynamical systems formed by concatenating maps acting on the unit interval [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] in an iid fashion. Considered as a stationary Markov process, the random dynamical system possesses a unique stationary measure ν \nu . We consider a class of non square-integrable observables ϕ \phi , mostly of form ϕ ⁡ ( x ) = d ​ ( x , x 0 ) − 1 α \phi(x)=d(x,x_{0})^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}} where x 0 x_{0} is a non-recurrent point (in particular a non-periodic point) satisfying some other genericity conditions, and more generally regularly varying observables with index α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) . The two types of maps we concatenate are a class of piecewise C 2 C^{2} expanding maps, and a class of intermittent maps possessing an indifferent fixed point at the origin. Under conditions on the dynamics and α \alpha we establish Poisson limit laws, convergence of scaled Birkhoff sums to a stable limit law and functional stable limit laws, in both the annealed and quenched case. The scaling constants for the limit laws for almost every quenched realization are the same as those of the annealed case and determined by ν \nu . This is in contrast to the scalings in quenched central limit theorems where the centering constants depend in a critical way upon the realization and are not the same for almost every realization.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider non square-integrable observables
\phi:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}

on two simple classes of random dynamical system. One consists of randomly
choosing in an iid manner from a finite set of maps which are strictly
polynomially mixing with an indifferent fixed point at the origin, the
other consisting of randomly choosing from a finite set of maps which are
uniformly expanding and exponentially mixing. The main type of observable
we consider is of the form
\phi(x)=|x-x_{0}|^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
,

\alpha\in(0,2)
which in the IID case lies in the domain of attraction of
a stable law of index
\alpha
. For certain results the point
x_{0}
has to
satisfy some nongenericity conditions and in particular not be a
periodic point for almost every realization of the random system (see
Definition
2.3
). Some of our results, particularly those involving convergence to
exponential and Poisson laws hold for general observables
that are regularly varying with index
\alpha
.

The settings for investigations on stable limit laws for observables on dynamical systems tend to be of two broad types: (1) “good observables” (typically Hölder) on
slowly mixing non-uniformly hyperbolic systems; and (2) “bad” observables (unbounded with fat tails) on fast mixing dynamical systems.
As illustrative examples of both settings we give two results.

Example of (1):

The LSV intermittent map
T_{\gamma}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

T_{\gamma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x(1+2^{\gamma}x^{\gamma})&\mbox{if $0\leq x\leq\frac{1}{2}$};\\

T_{\gamma}

(

Example of (2):

[

5.
A Poisson Point Process Approach to random and sequential
dynamical systems

Our results are based on the Poisson point process approach developed by
Marta Tyran-Kamińska
[
TK10a
,
TK10b
]
adapted to our random
setting (see Theorems
5.1

and
5.3
). Namely, convergence to a stable law
or a Lévy process follows from the convergence of the corresponding
(Poisson) jump processes, and control of the small jumps.

A key role is played by Kallenberg’s Theorem
[
Kal76
, Theorem 4.7]

to check convergence of the Poisson point processes,

N_{n}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\,}N_{(\alpha)}
. Kallenberg’s theorem does not assume stationarity
and hence we may use it in our setting.

In this section, we provide general conditions ensuring weak convergence to
Lévy stable processes for non-stationary dynamical systems, following
closely the approach of Tyran-Kamińska
[
TK10b
]
. We start from
the very general setting of non-autonomous sequential dynamics and then
specialize to the case of quenched random dynamical systems, which will be
useful to treat iid random compositions in the later sections.

5.3.
The annealed transfer operator

We assume that the random dynamical system

F:\Omega\times[0,1]\to\Omega\times[0,1]
,

F(\omega,x)=(\sigma\omega,T_{\omega}(x))

which can also be viewed as
a Markov process on
[0,1]
, has a stationary measure
\nu
with density

h
. The map

will preserve

{\mathbb{P}}\times\nu
. Recall that
{\mathbb{P}}:=\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We use the notation
P_{\mu,i}
for the transfer operator of

with respect to a measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
, i.e.

\int f\cdot g\circ T_{i}d\mu=\int(P_{\mu,i}f)gd\mu,\text{ for all
$f\in L^{1}(\mu)$, $g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)$}.

The annealed transfer operator is defined by

P_{\mu}(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}P_{\mu,i}(f)

with adjoint

U(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}f\circ T_{i}

which satisfies the duality relation

\int f(g\circ U)d\mu=\int(P_{\mu}f)gd\mu,\text{ for all
$f\in L^{1}(\mu)$, $g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)$}.

As above, we assume there are sample measures

d\nu^{\omega}=h_{\omega}dx
on each fiber
[0,1]
of the skew product
such that

P_{\omega}h_{\omega}=h_{\sigma\omega}

where
P_{\omega}
is the transfer operator of
T_{\omega_{0}}
with respect
to the Lebesgue measure.

Therefore

\nu(A)=\int_{\Omega}[\int_{A}h_{\omega}dx]d{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)

for all Borel sets
A\subset[0,1]
.

7.1.
Two useful lemmas

We now proceed to the proofs of the main results. We will use the following
technical propositions which are a form of spatial ergodic theorem which
allows us to prove exponential and Poisson limit laws.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume Condition U and let
\chi_{n}:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence
of functions in
L^{1}(m)
such that

{\mathbb{E}}_{m}(|\chi_{n}|)={\mathcal{O}}(n^{-1}\widetilde{L}(n))
for some slowly
varying function
\widetilde{L}
. Then, for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.

\omega\in\Omega
and for all
\ell\geq 1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq k\leq\ell}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\nu^{\sigma^{j}\omega}}(\chi_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(\chi_{n})\right)\right|=0.

Therefore, given
(s,t]\subset[0,\infty)
and
\varepsilon>0
, for

{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.
\omega
there exists
N(\omega)
such that

\left|\sum_{r=\lfloor ns\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\nu^{\sigma^{j}\omega}}(\chi_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(\chi_{n})\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon

for all
n\geq N(\omega)
.

Proof.

We obtain the second claim by taking the difference between two values of

\ell
in the first claim.

Fix
\ell\geq 1
. For
\delta>0
, let

U_{k}^{n}(\delta)=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\nu^{\sigma^{j}\omega}}(\chi_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(\chi_{n})\right)\right|\geq\delta\right\},

and

B^{n}(\delta)=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,\sup_{0\leq k\leq\ell}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left({\mathbb{E}}_{\nu^{\sigma^{j}\omega}}(\chi_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}_{\nu}(\chi_{n})\right)\right|\geq\delta\right\}.

Note that

B^{n}(\delta)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\ell}U_{k}^{n}(\delta).

We define
f_{n}(\omega)={\mathbb{E}}_{\nu^{\omega}}(\chi_{n})
and

\overline{f}_{n}={\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}(f_{n})
. We claim that
f_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is
Hölder with norm
\|f_{n}\|_{\theta}={\mathcal{O}}(n^{-1}\widetilde{L}(n))
. Indeed,
for
\omega\in\Omega
, we have

|f_{n}(\omega)|=\left|\int_{Y}\chi_{n}(x)d\nu^{\omega}(x)\right|\leq\|h_{\omega}\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}\|\chi_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{m}}\leq\frac{C}{n}\widetilde{L}(n),

and for
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{n}(\omega)-f_{n}(\omega^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Y}\chi_{n}(x)d\nu^{\omega}(x)-\int_{Y}\chi_{n}(x)d\nu^{\omega^{\prime}}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Y}|\chi_{n}(x)|\cdot|h_{\omega}(x)-h_{\omega^{\prime}}(x)|dm(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{\omega}-h_{\omega^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{m}}\|\chi_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\widetilde{L}(n)d_{\theta}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}),

since
\omega\in\Omega\mapsto h_{\omega}\in L^{\infty}(m)
is Hölder continuous. In particular, we also have that
\overline{f}_{n}={\mathcal{O}}(n^{-1}\widetilde{L}(n))
.

We have, using Chebyshev’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(U_{k}^{n}(\delta))

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega\,:\,\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left(f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}\right)\right|\geq\delta\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\left(\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left(f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}({\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}|f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}|^{2}+2\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq kn-1}{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}((f_{n}\circ\sigma^{i}-\overline{f}_{n})(f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}))\right].

By the
\sigma
-invariance of
{\mathbb{P}}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}|f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}|^{2}={\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}|f_{n}-\overline{f}_{n}|^{2},

and, since
(\Omega,{\mathbb{P}},\sigma)
admits exponential decay of correlations for Hölder observables, there exist
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}((f_{n}\circ\sigma^{i}-\overline{f}_{n})(f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j}-\overline{f}_{n}))

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}((f_{n}-\overline{f}_{n})(f_{n}\circ\sigma^{j-i}-\overline{f}_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{j-i}\|f_{n}-\overline{f}_{n}\|_{\theta}^{2}.

We then obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(U_{k}^{n}(\delta))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}\left[kn\|f_{n}-\overline{f}_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{m}}^{2}+2\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq kn-1}\lambda^{j-i}\|f_{n}-\overline{f}_{n}\|_{\theta}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{nk}{\delta^{2}}\|f_{n}\|_{\theta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{k}{n\delta^{2}}(\widetilde{L}(n))^{2},

which implies that

{\mathbb{P}}(B^{n}(\delta))\leq C\frac{\ell^{2}}{n\delta^{2}}(\widetilde{L}(n))^{2}.

Let
\eta>0
. By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, it follows that for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.

\omega\in\Omega
, there exists
N(\omega,\delta)\geq 1
such that

\omega\notin B^{\lfloor p^{1+\eta}\rfloor}(\delta)
for all

p\geq N(\omega,\delta)
.

Let now

P:=\lfloor p^{1+\eta}\rfloor<n\leq P^{\prime}=\lfloor(p+1)^{1+\eta}\rfloor
for
p
large enough. Let
0\leq k\leq\ell
. Then, since

\|f_{n}\|_{\infty}={\mathcal{O}}(n^{-1}\widetilde{L}(n))
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kP-1}\left(f_{n}(\sigma^{j}\omega)-\overline{f}_{n}\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{kn-1}\left(f_{n}(\sigma^{j}\omega)-\overline{f}_{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=kP}^{kn-1}\left|f_{n}(\sigma^{j}\omega)-\overline{f}_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{P^{\prime}-P}{P}\widetilde{L}(n)\leq C\frac{\widetilde{L}(p^{1+\eta})}{p},

because on the one hand

\frac{P^{\prime}-P}{P}=\frac{\lfloor(p+1)^{1+\eta}\rfloor-\lfloor p^{1+\eta}\rfloor}{\lfloor p^{1+\eta}\rfloor}={\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{p}\right),

and on the other hand, by Potter’s bounds, for
\tau>0
,

\widetilde{L}(n)\leq C\widetilde{L}(P)\left(\frac{n}{P}\right)^{\tau}\leq C\widetilde{L}(P)\left(\frac{P^{\prime}}{P}\right)^{\tau}\leq C\widetilde{L}(P).

Since

\left|\sum_{j=0}^{kP-1}\left(f_{n}(\sigma^{j}\omega)-\overline{f}_{n}\right)\right|<\delta

for all
0\leq k\leq\ell
, it follows that for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.
\omega
, there exists
N(\omega,\delta)
such that
\omega\notin B^{n}(2\delta)
for all
n\geq N(\omega,\delta)
, which concludes the proof.
∎

9.1.
Uniformly expanding maps: proof of Theorem

Recall Theorem
6.3
: under the conditions of
Section

, in particular
(LY)
,
(Min)
and
(Dec)
, if

x_{0}\notin{\mathcal{D}}
is non-recurrent, then for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.

\omega\in\Omega

N_{n}^{\omega}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\,}N_{(\alpha)}

under the probability measure
\nu^{\omega}
.

Our proof of Theorem
6.3
uses the existence of a
spectral gap for the associated transfer operators
P^{n}_{\omega}
, and
breaks down in the setting of intermittent maps. The use of the
spectral gap is encapsulated in the following lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

Assume
(LY)
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all

\omega\in\Omega
, all
f,f_{n}\in{\rm BV}
with

\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\leq 1\>\text{ and }\>\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}<\infty,

we have

\sup_{n\geq 0}\left\|P_{\omega}^{n}\left(f\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\circ T_{\omega}^{j}\right)\right\|_{\rm BV}\leq C\|f\|_{\rm BV}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right)

Proof.

We proceed in four steps.

Step 1.
We define

g_{\omega}^{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n}f_{j}\circ T_{\omega}^{j},

where we have set
f_{0}={\mathbf{1}}
. We observe that for all
n\geq 0
, there exists
C_{n}>0
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega
,

(9.1)

\|g_{\omega}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\leq\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\right)^{n+1}\leq 1\>\text{
and }\>\|g_{\omega}^{n}\|_{\rm BV}\leq C_{n}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right).

The first estimate is immediate, and the second follows, because

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n})\|f_{n+1}\circ T_{\omega}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}+\|g_{\omega}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}{\rm Var}(f_{n+1}\circ T_{\omega}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n})+{\rm Var}(f_{n+1}\circ T_{\omega}^{n+1})

\displaystyle={\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n})+\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n+1}}{\rm Var}_{I}(f_{n+1}\circ T_{\omega}^{n+1})

\displaystyle={\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n})+\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n+1}}{\rm Var}_{T_{\omega}^{n+1}(I)}(f_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Var}(g_{\omega}^{n})+\left(\#{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n+1}\right){\rm Var}(f_{n+1}),

and so we can define by induction
C_{n+1}=C_{n}+\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\#{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n+1}
which is finite, as there are only finitely many maps in
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Step 2.
We first prove the lemma in the case where
r=1
in the condition
(LY)
. Before, we claim that for
f\in{\rm BV}
and sequences
(f_{j})\subset{\rm BV}
as in the statement, we have

(9.2)

{\rm Var}\left(P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\right)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\rho^{j}\|P_{\omega}^{n-j}(fg_{\omega}^{n-j-1})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|f_{n-j}\|_{\rm BV}\\
+D\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\rho^{j}\|P_{\omega}^{n-1-j}(fg_{\omega}^{n-1-j})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\|f_{n-j}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}.

This implies the lemma when
r=1
, since

\|P_{\omega}^{n-j}(fg_{\omega}^{n-j-1})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\leq\|g_{\omega}^{n-j-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|P_{\omega}^{n-j}|f|\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\leq C\|f\|_{\rm BV},

and

\|P_{\omega}^{n-j}(fg_{\omega}^{n-j})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\|fg_{\omega}^{n-j}\|_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|g_{\omega}^{n-j}\|_{L^{1}(m)}\leq\|f\|_{\rm BV}.

We prove the claim by induction on
n\geq 0
. It is immediate for
n=0
, and for the induction step, we have, using
(LY)
,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}

\displaystyle(P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n+1}))

\displaystyle={\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n}f_{n+1}\circ T_{\omega}^{n+1}))={\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n})f_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n}))\|f_{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}+\|P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}{\rm Var}(f_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\rho{\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n}))+D\|P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\right)\|f_{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}+\|P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}{\rm Var}(f_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\rho{\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n}))+D\|P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\|f_{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}+\|P_{\omega}^{n+1}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|f_{n+1}\|_{\rm BV},

which proves (
9.2
) for
n+1
, assuming it holds for
n
.

Step 3.
Now, we consider the general case
r\geq 1
and we assume
that
n
is of the particular form
n=pr
, with
p\geq 0
. We note that
the random system defined with

{\mathcal{T}}=\left\{T_{\omega}^{r}\right\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies the
condition
(LY)
with
r=1
. Consequently, by the second step and
(
9.1
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\right\|_{\rm BV}

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{\sigma^{r-1}\omega}^{r}\circ\ldots\circ P_{\omega}^{r}\left(f\prod_{j=1}^{p}g_{\sigma^{jr}\omega}^{r}\circ T_{\omega}^{jr}\right)\right\|_{\rm BV}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\rm BV}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|g_{\sigma^{jr}\omega}^{r}\|_{\rm BV}\right)\leq CC_{r}\|f\|_{\rm BV}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right).

Step 4.
Finally, if
n=pr+q
, with
p\geq 0
and

(LY)

\displaystyle\|P_{\omega}^{n}(fg_{\omega}^{n})\|_{\rm BV}

\displaystyle=\|P_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr}g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}\circ T_{\omega}^{pr})\|_{\rm BV}

\displaystyle=\|P_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}(P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q})\|_{\rm BV}\leq C\|P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}\|_{\rm BV}.

But, from Step 3, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}\|_{L^{1}(m)}

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})\|_{L^{1}(m)}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})\|_{L^{1}(m)}\leq C\|f\|_{\rm BV}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right),

and, using (
9.1
),

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q})

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr})\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}{\rm Var}(g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q})+{\rm Var}(P_{\omega}^{pr}(fg_{\omega}^{pr}))\|g_{\sigma^{pr}\omega}^{q}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}

\displaystyle\leq\left[C_{q}\|g_{\omega}^{pr}\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}\|P_{\omega}^{pr}|f|\|_{L^{\infty}(m)}+C\|f\|_{\rm BV}\right]\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\max_{q=0,\ldots,r-1}C_{q}\right)\|f\|_{\rm BV}\left(\sup_{j\geq 1}\|f_{j}\|_{\rm BV}\right),

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Uniformly expanding maps: proof of Theorem

For this purpose, we consider first some technical lemmas regarding short returns. For
\omega\in\Omega

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(\varepsilon)=\left\{x\in[0,1]\,:\,|T_{\omega}^{n}(x)-x|\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

Lemma 10.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
n\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0

m({\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(\varepsilon))\leq C\varepsilon.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
[
HNT12
, Lemma 3.4]
, conveniently adapted to our setting of random non-Markov maps. Recall that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n}
is the partition of monotonicity associated to the map
T_{\omega}^{n}
. Consider
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n}
. Since
\inf_{I}|(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}|\geq\lambda^{n}>1
, there exists at most one solution
x_{I}^{\pm}\in I
to the equation

(10.1)

T_{\omega}^{n}(x_{I}^{\pm})=x_{I}^{\pm}\pm\varepsilon,

and since there is no sign change of
(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}
on
I

(10.2)

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{\omega}(\varepsilon)\cap I\subset[x_{I}^{-},x_{I}^{+}].

We have

T_{\omega}^{n}(x_{I}^{+})-T_{\omega}^{n}(x_{I}^{-})=x_{I}^{+}-x_{I}^{-}+2\varepsilon,

and by the mean value theorem,

\left|T_{\omega}^{n}(x_{I}^{+})-T_{\omega}^{n}(x_{I}^{-})\right|=\left|(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}(c)\right|\,\left|x_{I}^{+}-x_{I}^{-}\right|,\>\text{ for some }c\in I.

Consequently,

(10.3)

\left|x_{I}^{+}-x_{I}^{-}\right|\leq\left(\sup_{I}\frac{1}{|(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}|}\right)\left[\left|x_{I}^{+}-x_{I}^{-}\right|+2\varepsilon\right]\leq\lambda^{-n}\left|x_{I}^{+}-x_{I}^{-}\right|+2\varepsilon\sup_{I}\frac{1}{|(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}|}.

Note that if there is no solutions to (
10.1
), then the estimate (
10.3
) is actually improved.
Rearranging (
10.3
) and summing over
I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n}
, we obtain thanks to (

The fact that

(10.4)

\sum_{I\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\omega}^{n}}\sup_{I}\frac{1}{|(T_{\omega}^{n})^{\prime}|}\leq C

for a constant
C>0
independent from
\omega
and

Appendix

The observation that our distributional limit theorems hold for any measures
\mu\ll\nu^{\omega}
follows
from Theorem 1, Corollary 1 and Corollary 3 of Zweimüller’s work
[
Zwe07
]
.

Let

S_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{b_{n}}[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ T_{\omega}^{j}(x)-a_{n}].

and suppose

S_{n}\rightarrow_{\nu_{\omega}}Y

where
Y
is a Lévy random variable.

We consider first the setup of intermittent maps. We will show
that for any measure
\nu
with density
h
i.e.
d\nu=hdm
in the cone

L
, in particular Lebesgue measure
m

with
h=1
,

S_{n}\rightarrow_{\nu}Y

We focus on
m
. According to
[
Zwe07
, Theorem 1]
it is enough to show
that

\int\psi(S_{n})d\nu_{\omega}-\int\psi(S_{n})dm\to 0.

for any
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
which is bounded and uniformly Lipschitz.

Fix such a
\psi
and consider

\int\psi(\frac{1}{b_{n}}[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ T_{\omega}^{j}(x)-a_{n}])(h_{\omega}-1)dm

\leq\int\psi(\frac{1}{b_{n}}[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ T_{\sigma^{k}\omega}^{j}(x)-a_{n}])P_{\omega}^{k}(h_{\omega}-1)dm

\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|P_{\omega}^{k}(h_{\omega}-1)\|_{L^{1}(m)}.

Since
\|P_{\omega}^{k}(h_{\omega}-1)\|_{L^{1}_{m}}\to 0
in case of
Example
2.2
and maps satisfying (LY), (Dec) and (Min) the
assertion is proved. By
[
Zwe07
, Corollary 3]
, the proof for
continuous time distributional limits follows immediately.

Rational points in the Noether-Lefschetz locus of moduli spaces of K3 surfaces
Domenico Valloni

Abstract
. In this paper, we study maps between moduli spaces of lattice-polarized K3 surfaces induced by sublattices of prime index. We show that these maps can be used to determine if a rational point of the moduli space belongs to the Noether-Lefschetz locus. As an application, we prove that the Bombieri-Lang conjecture implies non-density statements for the rational points in the Noether-Lefschetz locus, as predicted by a conjecture of Shafarevich.



1.
introduction

Let
L
be a lattice of signature
(1_{+},n_{-})
that embeds primitively into the K3 lattice. Nikulin
[
Nik79a
]
introduced the moduli space
\mathcal{F}_{L}
of
L
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces, which parametrizes isomorphism classes of pairs
(X,\iota)
, where
X
is a complex K3 surface and
\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
is a primitive embedding satisfying an ampleness condition. The spaces
\mathcal{F}_{L}
have been extensively studied in the literature, particularly for specific choices of
L
, and they are normal quasiprojective varieties of dimension
20-\mathrm{rank}(L)
. They also have a natural model over
\mathbf{Q}
and are closely related to Shimura varieties.

In this paper, we are interested in the Noether-Lefschetz locus
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
, which consists of all the points
(X,\iota)
for which
\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
is a proper inclusion. We remark that determining whether a given point lies in
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
amounts to know something about
\operatorname{NS}(X)
, which is in general a difficult problem (see e.g.,
[
vL07
]
,
[
EJ08
]
and
[
Cha14
]
). To distinguish points in
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
we use the negative definite lattice
\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X)\coloneqq\iota(L)^{\perp}
, and we remark that there are only finitely many subvarieties of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
whose very general point is a K3 surface
(X,\iota)
with a given
\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X)
.

Let now
K
be a number field, and let
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}(K)
denote the set of
K
-rational points lying in the Noether-Lefschetz locus. According to
[
And96
, Thm.5.2.]
, the subset
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}(K)
is always thin. This is in fact proved by André for general families of motives, with a key ingredient being a version of Hilbert irreducibility for infinite extensions proved in
[
Ser89
]
. In this work, we shall use related ideas to describe the set
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}(K)
. To state our main result, for any prime
p
coprime to
\mathrm{discr}(L)
, we construct three branched coverings
\pi_{p}^{\bullet}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{L}(p)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{L}
, where
\bullet\in\{+,-,0\}
. Each covering corresponds to an isometry class of a sublattice
T^{\prime}\subset T
such that
T/T^{\prime}\cong\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}
, and if
4\leq\mathrm{rank}(L)\leq 18
, then each
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{\bullet}(p)
is a moduli space of lattice polarized K3 surfaces (in general, at least one of them always is). Under some mild conditions on
L
the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{\bullet}(p)
also have one or two components, and
\deg(\pi_{p}^{\bullet})\sim p^{22-\mathrm{rank}(L)}
on each of them. Finally, one defines
\OO_{\Delta}(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))\subset\OO(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))
as the subgroup which respects effective
(-2)
-classes in
\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X)
. We note that
|\OO(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))|\leq 3^{21^{3}}
always, and in practice,
\OO_{\Delta}(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))\subset\mu_{2}
for most of the lattices. We can now state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be an hyperbolic lattice which is not
2
-elementary, and let
U\subset\mathcal{F}_{L}
be the Zariski open which supports a universal family (see Proposition
2.31
). Let
K
be a number field and let
x\in U(K)
be a rational point representing
(X,\iota)
. Then:

(1)

If
x\in\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
, there is an extension
K^{\prime}/K
with
[K^{\prime}\colon K]\leq|\OO_{\Delta}(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))|
and a rational point
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{L}(p)(K^{\prime})
such that
\pi_{p}(x^{\prime})=x
, at least for one choice
\bullet\in\{+,-,0\}
.

(2)

In fact,
x
is in the Noether-Lefschetz locus if and only if point (1) holds for
p
sufficiently large;

(3)

If the cokernel of
\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X)\perp\iota(L)\subset\operatorname{NS}(X)
is not
2
-torsion and
\OO_{\Delta}(\operatorname{NS}_{\iota}(X))\subset\mu_{2}
(which is automatic for instance if
\rho(X)=\mathrm{rank}(L)+1
) then we can choose
K^{\prime}=K
in point (1).

For a closed point
x\in\mathcal{F}_{L}
and a closed point
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}}
such that
\pi^{\bullet}_{p}(x^{\prime})=x
, one expects in general that
\mathrm{deg}(x^{\prime})\sim\mathrm{deg}(x)\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\pi^{\bullet}_{p})
, which can be made more precise using Hilbert irreducibility. Since the numbers
|\OO_{\Delta}(K)|
are universally bounded and the degrees of
\pi_{p}^{\bullet}
are arbitrarily large, this shows that the closed points in
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
behave in a special way with respect to the coverings
\pi_{p}^{\bullet}
. We remark that these coverings already appeared in the literature; for instance, they were studied in
[
Kon93
]
when
\mathrm{rank}(L)=1
.

Point (2) can be made uniform assuming the following conjecture of Várilly-Alvarado:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
VA17
]
)
.

Let
L
be an even hyperbolic lattice and let
X/K
be a K3 surface over a number field such that
\operatorname{NS}(\overline{X})\cong L
. Then,
|\Br(\overline{X})^{G_{K}}|
can be bounded only in terms of
[K\colon\mathbf{Q}]
.

This and similar finiteness have also been extensively studied for instance in
[
SZ12
]
,
[
HKT13
]
,
[
New16
]
,
[
VAV17
]
,
[
CFTTV18
]
,
[
CC20
]
,
[
Val21
]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume that Conjecture
1.1
is true for a lattice
L
, and let
N>0
be an integer. Then, there is a number
C=C(N,L)>0
such that, for any number field
K/\mathbf{Q}
satisfying
[K\colon\mathbf{Q}]\leq N
, a
K
-rational point
x\in U(K)
belongs to the Noether-Lefschetz locus if and only if point (1) of Theorem
1.1
holds for one (and hence any)
p\geq C
.

In the final part of the paper, we prove our main application. We recall that the Bombieri-Lang conjecture is a generalization of the Mordell conjecture, or Faltings’ theorem, which states that rational points on varieties of general type are never Zariski dense.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume the Bombieri-Lang conjecture and let
K
be a number field.

(1)

If
1\leq\mathrm{rank}(L)\leq 11
then the set of all the
K
-rational points in the Noether-Lefschetz locus satisfying (3) from Theorem
1.1
is not Zariski-dense.

(2)

Assume that
\mathrm{rank}(L)=3
. Then
\mathcal{F}^{nl}(K)
is not Zariski dense.

Theorem
1.1
readily yields the proof of (1) once we know that the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{\bullet}(p)
are of general type for sufficiently large
p
, which follows from the results in
[
Ma15
]
. On the other hand, the proof of (2) does not directly follow from our main theorem, but on other similar constructions. We remark that (2) holds without any further assumption if we have an analogue of Theorem 1.1 in
[
Ma15
]
for any rank.

The statement above is relevant only when
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is not of general type. There are numerous examples where it is shown that
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is either rational or unirational, see
[
Muk92a
]
,
[
Muk92b
]
,
[
Muk96
]
,
[
Ma15
]
,
[
FM21
]
,
[
FV21
]
, as well as the references therein. Similarly, there are also examples where
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is unirational and
\mathrm{rank}(L)=3
, see for instance
[
FHM23
]
. Nonetheless, it is expected that for all but finitely many
L
, the space
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is of general type, as suggested by the results in
[
GHS07
]
,
[
Ma15
]
and
[
FM21
]
.

Finally, the only unconditional result like Theorem
1.3
is proved for any one-dimensional family in
[
CT13
, Thm.1.1]
. After the author had written the first version of this paper, he was pointed out to the preprint
[
Cad22
]
which contains similar arguments for
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
-local systems on any variety. In particular, in
[
Cad22
]
it is explained how non-density results like Theorem
1.3
would follow from the Bombieri-Lang conjecture together with certain geometric conjectures. In our case, thanks to Theorem
1.1
, we are able to replace such conjectures with the results of
[
Ma15
]
, allowing us to prove Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Moduli spaces of lattice polarized K3 surfaces

In this chapter, we review the construction of the moduli spaces of lattice-polarized K3 surfaces. We follow
[
Dol96
]
as a primary reference, along with the more recent paper
[
AE21
]

2.1.
Stable orthogonal groups

A lattice
L
is an abelian group
L\cong\mathbf{Z}^{n}
together with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
L\times L\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}
. All the lattices in this paper will be even, meaning that
(x,x)\in 2\mathbf{Z}
for any
x\in L
. The signature of
L
is the signature of
L_{\mathbb{R}}
and the discriminant form of an even lattice
L
is the group
A_{L}=L^{\vee}/L
endowed with its natural quadratic form
q\colon A_{L}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}/2\mathbf{Z}
(see
[
Nik79b
]
). One denotes by
\OO(A_{L})
the finite group of isometries of
A_{L}
. The isometry group of
L
is denoted as
\OO(L)
. We denote by
\ell(A_{L})
the length of the abelian group
A_{L}
(minimal number of generators) and we say that
L
is unimodular if
\mathrm{discr}(L)=\pm 1,
that is, if the natural inclusion
L\hookrightarrow L^{\vee}
is an isomorphism.

Definition 2.1
.

The stable isometry group of
L
is
\OO^{*}(L)\coloneqq\ker(\OO(L)\rightarrow\OO(A_{L}))
.

The group
\OO^{*}(L)
enjoys some well-known properties:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
L\hookrightarrow U
be a primitive embedding of even lattices, where
U
is unimodular. Define
\Gamma(L,U)\coloneqq\{g\in\OO(U)\colon g_{|L^{\perp}}=\operatorname{Id}\}
. Then, the natural map
\Gamma(L,U)\rightarrow\OO(L)
induces an isomorphism
\Gamma(L,U)\cong\OO^{*}(L).

Proof.

Let
K
be the orthogonal complement of
L
in
U
. By
[
Nik79b
, Prop.1.6.1]
, there is a natural isomorphism
A_{L}\cong-A_{K}
where
-A_{K}
denotes the group
A_{K}
with opposite quadratic form. It follows that if
g\in\Gamma(L,U)
then the induced isometry
g_{|L}\in\OO(L)
must act trivially on
A_{L}
. On the other hand, let
\tilde{g}\in\OO^{*}(L)
be a stable isometry. Due to
[
Nik79b
]
[Cor.1.5.2] one sees that
\tilde{g}\oplus\operatorname{Id}_{K}\colon L\oplus K\xrightarrow{\sim}L\oplus K
can always be extended to a global isometry
g\in\Gamma(L,U)
.
∎

In other words, an isometry of
L
is stable if and only if it can be extended to an isometry of
U
by requiring it to be the identity on
L^{\perp}
. Note that this works for every unimodular lattice
U
which contains
L
primitively.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
L\hookrightarrow N
be any inclusion of lattices that respects the quadratic form. Then, there is a natural map
\mathrm{O}^{*}(L)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{O}^{*}(N)
induced by extension by
1
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{L}
be the saturation of
L
in
N
. The map
L\hookrightarrow N
factorizes as
L\hookrightarrow\tilde{L}\hookrightarrow N
where the first arrow exhibits
\tilde{L}
as an overlattice of
L
(that is, the quotient
\tilde{L}/L
is finite) and the second arrow is a primitive embedding of lattices. It is sufficient to prove the proposition for these two kinds of maps. So assume that
N
is an overlattice of
L
. As explained in
[
Nik79b
, Prop.1.4.1]
,
N
is determined by an isotropic subgroup
H\subset A_{L}
, that is, under the natural quotient map
\pi\colon L^{\vee}\rightarrow A_{L}
one has that
\pi^{-1}(H)=N
. Clearly, if an isometry
g\in\OO(L)
acts trivially on
A_{L}
then
g
extends uniquely to an isometry of
N
. Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism
A_{N}\cong H^{\perp}/H
, and it follows that such an extension must belong to
\OO^{*}(N)
. Consider now a primitive embedding
L\hookrightarrow N
, so that
N/L
is torsion free. Let
U
be a unimodular lattice with a primitive embedding
N\hookrightarrow U
. We obtain a chain
L\subset N\subset U
. Let
g\in\OO^{*}(L)
be a stable isometry. By the lemma above, we can extend
g\oplus\operatorname{Id}_{L^{\perp}}
to an isometry
\tilde{g}\in\OO(U)
. This isometry clearly belongs to
\Gamma(N,U)\cong\OO^{*}(N)
, and the proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

All these properties hold in general for lattices over PIDs, e.g., for
p
-adic lattices.

2.2.
Moduli spaces

Let
\Lambda
denote the K3 lattice, the unique even, unimodular lattice of rank 22 and of signature
(3_{+},19_{-})
. An even lattice
L
is called hyperbolic if its signature pair is of the form
(1_{+},n_{-})
. Two primitive embedding
f_{1},f_{2}\colon L\hookrightarrow\Lambda
are equivalent if there exists
g\in\OO(\Lambda)
such that
f_{1}=g\circ f_{2}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}(L)
the set of equivalence classes of primitive embedding of
L
into
\Lambda
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is known that
\mathcal{E}(L)
is always finite, e.g., it follows from Nikulin’s description of this set
[
Nik79b
, Prop.1.6.1]
and the finiteness of the genus of a lattice. Moreover, for any even hyperbolic lattice
L
of rank
\mathrm{rank}(L)\leq 20
, if
\ell(A_{L})\leq 20-\mathrm{rank}(L)
then
\mathcal{E}(L)=\{*\}
due to
[
Nik79b
]
[Cor.1.13.3], so that every lattice of rank smaller than
10
embeds uniquely into the K3 lattice.

For an embedding
f
we denote by
[f]
its class in
\mathcal{E}(L)
. For an even hyperbolic lattice
L
, write
V=\{x\in L_{\mathbb{R}}\colon(x,x)>0\}
and fix a primitive embedding
f\colon L\hookrightarrow\Lambda
. Consider the set
\Delta(f)\coloneqq\{\delta\in\Lambda\setminus L^{\perp}\colon(\delta,\delta)=-2\text{ and }\langle\delta,L\rangle\text{ is hyperbolic }\}
. For each
\delta\in\Delta(f)
we obtain a hyperplane
V_{\delta}\subset L_{\mathbb{R}}
given by the condition
(x,\delta)=0
. A small cone of
L
relative to
f
is a connected component of the complement
V\backslash\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta(f)}V_{\delta}
(see
[
AE21
, Def.2.21]
). Note that the set
\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta(f)}V_{\delta}
depends only on the class
[f]\in\mathcal{E}(L)
.

Definition 2.4
.

A small cone of
L
is a connected component
\mathcal{C}
of

V\setminus\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\Delta(e)\\
e\in\mathcal{E}(L)\end{subarray}}V_{\delta}.

Since the set
\mathcal{E}(L)
is finite, it follows from
[
AE21
, Prop.2.23]
that the small cones are locally rational polyhedral, that is, they are given locally by the intersection of finitely many rational half spaces.

Fix an even hyperbolic lattice
L
that embeds primitively into the K3 lattice and a small cone
\mathcal{C}
of
L
. The following is
[
AE21
, Def.2.16]
and
[
AE21
, Prop.2.24]
.

Definition 2.5
.

For
e\in\mathcal{E}(L)
, an
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surface is a pair
(X,\iota)
where
X
is a complex K3 surface and
\iota
is a primitive embedding
\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
such that
\iota(\mathcal{C})
contains a big and nef class, and such that the composition
L\rightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))\cong\Lambda
represents
e
(this does not depend on the last isometry). Two
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces
(X,\iota),(X^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism
h\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}X^{\prime}
such that
\iota=h^{*}\circ\iota^{\prime}
where
h^{*}\colon\operatorname{NS}(X^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
is the natural pullback map on Néron-Severi lattices (in particular, the isomorphism is not required to preserve the small cone.)

Note that
\iota(\mathcal{C})
contains automatically an ample class whenever
\iota(L)^{\perp}
does not contain
(-2)-
classes. This is due to the definition of small cones: if
\ell\in\iota(\mathcal{C})
is a big and nef class, then it is ample if and only if
(\ell,\delta)>0
for any effective
(-2)
-class
\delta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
. If
\delta\notin L^{\perp}
, then
(\ell,\delta)\neq 0
because
\ell\in\mathcal{C}
, so it follows that
(\delta,\ell)>0
.

For a hyperbolic lattice
L
, its roots are given by
\Delta(L)=\{\delta\in L\colon(\delta,\delta)=-2\}
. The Weyl group
W(L)\subset\OO(L)
is the group generated by the reflections in elements of
\Delta(L)
. Note that
W(L)\subset\OO^{*}(L)
always, because any such reflection can be extended (as a reflection) on any even unimodular lattice
U
which contains
L
primitively. A Weyl chamber is a connected component of
V\setminus\bigcup_{\delta\in\Delta(L)}V_{\delta}
. Clearly, small cones are contained in Weyl chambers. It is a classical fact that
W(L)
acts freely on
V
with a Weyl chamber
\mathcal{K}
as a fundamental domain, so that the set of Weyl chambers forms a torsor under
W(L)
. If
X
is a projective K3 surface, then its Kähler cone
\mathcal{K}_{X}
is a Weyl chamber of
\operatorname{NS}(X)
.

The construction of the moduli space of
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces then follows Dolgachev’s paper. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the fine moduli space of marked K3 surfaces. This is a
20
-dimensional complex manifold which is not Hausdorff, and which parametrizes K3 surfaces
X
together with a marking
\phi\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda
, up to the natural notion of isomorphism. Over
\mathcal{M}
one has a universal family
(\mathcal{X},\phi)
, and the period map

p\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}})\colon(\sigma,\sigma)=0,\,\,(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})>0\}

takes a point
m\in\mathcal{M}
to the line generated by
\phi_{m}(H^{2,0}(\mathcal{X}_{m}))
. The open subset
\mathcal{D}
is the period domain of
\Lambda
and for any
x\in\mathcal{D}
seen as a line in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
, let
W_{x}\subset\OO(\Lambda)
be its Weyl group, that is, the group generated by reflection in elements
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap x^{\perp}
such that
(\lambda,\lambda)=-2
. The period map
p
is surjective
[
Tod80
]
and its fibers
p^{-1}\{x\}
are naturally torsors under
W_{x}
, where
g\in W_{x}
acts by
(X,\phi)\mapsto(X,g\circ\phi)
. It follows that the fibers are in one-to-one correspondence to Weyl chambers in
\operatorname{NS}(X)
whenever
X
is projective.

One then defines marked
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces. Fix an embedding
L\hookrightarrow\Lambda
which represents
e
and a small cone
\mathcal{C}
on
L
.

Definition 2.6
.

A marked
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surface is a K3 surface
X
together with a marking
\phi\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda
such that
L\subset\phi(\operatorname{NS}(X))
and such that the induced map
L\rightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
is quasipolarized (the image of
\mathcal{C}
contains a big and nef divisor). Two marked
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces
(X,\phi),(X^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism,
h\colon X\xrightarrow{\sim}X^{\prime}
such that
\iota=h^{*}\circ\iota^{\prime}
where
h^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{Z}(1))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))
is the natural pullback map in cohomology.

To any marked
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surface
(X,\phi)
one can associate a
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surface by putting
\iota_{\phi}=\phi^{-1}_{|L}\colon L\hookrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)
. Consider now the period domain

\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathbb{P}(L^{\perp}_{\mathbb{C}})\colon(\sigma,\sigma)=0\text{ and }(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})>0\}.

There is a natural embedding
\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}\subset\mathcal{D}
, and we put
\mathcal{M}_{L}=p^{-1}\{\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}\}
; this set consists of all the marked K3 surfaces
(X,\phi)
such that
L\subset\phi(\operatorname{NS}(X))
. Restricting
\mathcal{M}_{L}
further

\mathcal{M}^{qp}_{L}\coloneqq\{(X,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}_{L}\colon(X,\phi^{-1}_{|L})\text{ is quasipolarized}\}

yields the fine moduli space of marked
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces. For a point
x\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}
denote by
W_{x}(L^{\perp})\subset\OO(\Lambda)
the group generated by reflections in vectors
\delta\in x^{\perp}\cap L^{\perp}
such that
(\delta,\delta)=-2
. Note that
x^{\perp}\cap L^{\perp}
is a definite lattice (it corresponds to the orthogonal of
L
inside
\operatorname{NS}(X)
) so that
W_{x}(L^{\perp})
is finite.

Theorem 2.7
.

The restriction of the period map to
\mathcal{M}^{qp}_{L}
is surjective, and the fibers
p^{-1}\{x\}
are naturally torsors under the finite groups
W_{x}(L^{\perp})
.

Proof.

In fact, let
x\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}
be any period and pick a preimage
(X,\phi)
. Since we have that
W(\operatorname{NS}(X))\subset\OO^{*}(\operatorname{NS}(X))
we get a natural embedding
W(\operatorname{NS}(X))\subset\OO(\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}))
so that we let
W(\operatorname{NS}(X))
act on the fibers of
\mathcal{M}_{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{L^{\perp}}
. Then we simply choose
w\in W(L)
so that
\phi(w(\mathcal{K}_{X}))
contains the fixed small cone
\mathcal{C}
so that
(X,\phi\circ w)\in\mathcal{M}_{L}^{qp}
. This proves surjectivity.

For the other statement, let
(X,\phi)
and
(X,\psi)
be two different markings that yield the same
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surface. Consider the Hodge isometry
\phi^{-1}\circ\psi
of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))
, which is the identity on
L
. If this map is not induced by an isomorphism, then there must exists an effective
(-2)
-class
\delta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
such that
\phi^{-1}\circ\psi(\delta)
is not effective. But then
\delta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)

then

. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If in Definition

In order to construct the space

Proposition 2.8
.

The period map induces an isomorphism

The points of

-quasipolarized K3 surfaces.

It follows that for any other choice of the embedding

The manifold

The definition of

The space

has a natural model over

Proof.

The correct way to prove this would be to follow Rizov’s steps

Although

2.3.
Hodge theoretic interpretation

We begin with the following definition. Let
T
be an even lattice of signature
(2_{+},n_{-})
.

Definition 2.12
.

A
T
-polarized K3 structure is a tuple
(T^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
where
T^{\prime}
is an even lattice in the same genus of
T
endowed with a Hodge structure of weight zero of K3 type, for which the quadratic form of
T^{\prime}_{\mathbf{Q}}
induces a polarization, and
\gamma^{\prime}\colon A_{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{T^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism of finite quadratic forms. Another tuple
(T^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime})
is isomorphic to the first one, if there is an integral Hodge isometry
f\colon T^{\prime}\rightarrow T^{\prime\prime}
such that, if
\bar{f}\colon A_{T^{\prime}}\rightarrow A_{T^{\prime\prime}}
denotes the natural induced isometry on the discriminant forms, then
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}\circ\bar{f}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)
be the set of all isomorphism classes of
T
-polarized K3 structures. We define
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
as the isomorphism classes of pairs
(T^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
where
T^{\prime}
now has no Hodge structure, but it is only an even lattice in the same genus of
T^{\prime}
(the rest of the definition remains the same).

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
L
be an even hyperbolic lattice,
f\colon L\hookrightarrow\Lambda
a primitive embedding, and
T
its orthogonal complement. There is a natural isomorphism
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))\cong\mathcal{E}(L)
.

Proof.

Due to
[
Nik79b
, Prop.1.6.1]
we can identify
\mathcal{E}(L)
with isomorphism classes of pairs
(T^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
where
\rho^{\prime}\colon A_{T^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\sim}-A_{L}
is an isometry and
T^{\prime}
is a lattice in the same genus as
T
. Two such pairs give isomorphic primitive embeddings if and only if there is an isometry
f\colon T^{\prime}\rightarrow T^{\prime\prime}
such that
\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime\prime}\circ\bar{f}
. Using the natural isomorphism
g\colon A_{T}\xrightarrow{\sim}-A_{L}
induced by the identification
T=L^{\perp}
we can associate to any
(T^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*}(T)
the element
(T^{\prime},g\circ\gamma^{-1})\in\mathcal{E}(L).
This is a bijection.
∎

One has a natural surjective map
\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
. We now show that
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})
also parametrizes
T
-polarized Hodge structures of K3 type:

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
L
be an even hyperbolic lattice with a primitive embedding
L\hookrightarrow\Lambda
such that
L^{\perp}=T
. There is a natural identification

\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)\cong\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})

such that the following square commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}(L).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

To any
L
-quasipolarized K3 surface
(X,\iota)
we let
(\iota(L)^{\perp},\gamma_{X})
be the pair where
\gamma_{X}
is given by the composition
A_{\iota(L)^{\perp}}\cong-A_{L}\cong A_{T}.
It is easy to see that this defines an injection
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{K}^{*}_{T}
.

To go the other way around, choose any
(T^{\prime},\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}^{*}_{T}
. We can glue the direct sum
T^{\prime}\oplus L
into the K3 lattice, using the isomorphism
A_{T^{\prime}}\cong-A_{L}
induced by
\gamma
. We then endow the K3 lattice
\Lambda
with this Hodge structure and, by the surjectivity of the period map and the global Torelli theorem, we obtain a unique K3 surface
X
together with an isometry
f\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda
. Now, choose a small cone
\mathcal{C}
on
L
, and consider the embedding
L\hookrightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z}(1))
given by the composition of
L\subset L\oplus T^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
and
f^{-1}
. Pick any
w\in W(L)
such that
\iota\coloneqq j\circ w
is quasipolarized with respect to
\mathcal{C}
and consider the corresponding
L
-quasipolarized K3 surface
(X,\iota)
. We need to show that
(X,\iota)
does not depend on these choices (the K3 surface
X
is not naturally associated to the Hodge structure, and
f
is arbitrary). Let
(X^{\prime},\iota^{\prime})
be another
L
-quasipolarized K3 surface obtained via the same procedure. Choose any Hodge isometry
f\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{Z})
such that
f\circ\iota=\iota^{\prime}
. Assume that
L^{\perp}
does not contain
(-2)
-classes, and let
\delta\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
be any effective
(-2)
-class and
c\in\mathcal{C}
be any element. Then
(\iota(c),\delta)>0
and therefore
(\iota^{\prime}(c),f(\delta)>0
. This shows that

is induced by a unique isomorphism (by the global Torelli) of
L
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces. If

contains
(-2)
-classes, then

is not necessarily induced by an isomorphism. On the other hand, we can pick any element

in the finite group

such that

is induced by a unique isomorphism. In this case, the two

∎

Thus, in the end, one could rephrase the notion of

-polarized K3 surface as follows:

Definition 2.15
.

An

-polarized K3 surface is a pair
(X,\iota)
where

is a K3 surface and

is a primitive embedding of lattices (without any ampleness condition). Two pairs

On

.

.

The quotient of

parametrizes Hodge structures

where

is in the same genus as

.

In particular, since when

the transcendental lattice of

For any

2.4.
Double quotient interpretation

Let
T
be any even lattice of signature
(2_{+},n_{-})
, and let
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{T}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{\mathbb{C}})
be the associated period domain. Let
\OO_{T}^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
be the kernel of
\OO_{T}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\rightarrow\OO(A_{L}).
Consider the following double quotient

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}_{T}\coloneqq\OO_{T}(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathcal{D}_{T}^{\pm}\times\OO_{T}(\mathbf{A}_{f})/\OO_{T}^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}),

where the action of
q\in\SO_{T}(\mathbf{Q})
is given by
q(\sigma,g)=(q\sigma,qg)
and the action of
K
is the right multiplication on the right coordinate. The product can be taken both with
\OO(\mathbf{A}_{f})
endowed with the adelic topology, or with the discrete topology: this does not make a difference when one goes to the quotient.
Note that
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is not a Shimura variety because
\OO_{T}
is not connected. We now show that
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}_{L}
when

embeds primitively into the K3 lattice with orthogonal complement isomorphic to
T

\mathcal{M}_{T}
parametrizes
T
-polarized Hodge structures of K3-type.

Lemma 2.18
.

There is a natural isomorphism
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))\cong\OO_{T}(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\OO_{T}(\mathbf{A}_{f})/\OO_{T}^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
.

Proof.

Consider
g\in\OO_{T}(\mathbf{A}_{f})
. Then,
T^{g}\coloneqq g(T\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\cap T_{\mathbf{Q}}
is the unique lattice that satisfies
T^{g}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}=g(T\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
where the equality is taken in
T\otimes\mathbf{A}_{f}
. The map
g
also induces a natural isomorphism
\gamma_{g}\colon A_{T}\rightarrow A_{T^{g}}
, so we obtain a map
\OO(\mathbf{A}_{f})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
sending
g
to
(T^{g},\gamma_{g})
. The surjectivity of this map is due to the fact that
\OO(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\rightarrow\OO(A_{T})
is surjective
[
Nik79b
]
[Cor.1.9.6] and it is straightforward to check that it induces a bijection between the double coset and
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
.
∎

Proposition 2.19
.

For any even lattice
T
of signature type
(2_{+},n_{-})
there is a natural identification

\mathcal{M}_{T}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)

and a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{T}(\mathbb{C})\cong\bigsqcup_{(T^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))}\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}}^{\pm}/\OO^{*}(T^{\prime}).

We put
V=T_{\mathbf{Q}}
. Let

and denote by
[\sigma,g]\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
its equivalence class. Denote by

the polarized Hodge structure induced by

, that is,

and

. If there is a Hodge isometry

then

is an element of

such that

. Consider now the unique lattice

such that

with equality occurring in

. Denote by

the integral Hodge structure induced by the inclusion

. Note that

induces an isomorphism

, so we obtain an element

. Clearly

for any

and

for any

This shows that the map is well defined. To construct the inverse, one picks an element

and one chooses any isometry

. The image of

is a lattice in the same genus of

, because

has the same signature and the same discriminant form of

. We can find both a Hodge structure

and an element

such that

as integral polarized Hodge structures. We only need to show that we can choose

such that

. This follows from the fact that the map

is surjective

Nik79b
]
[Cor.1.9.6.] and from the fact that for any

one has
T^{gh}=T^{g}
. These two constructions are one the inverse of the other.

We now prove the last part of the proposition. The discussion above shows how to any
[\sigma,g]\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(\mathbb{C})
we can associate an element
(T^{g},\gamma_{g})\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)).
Assume that for another
[\sigma^{\prime},g^{\prime}]
we have that
(T^{g^{\prime}},\gamma_{g^{\prime}})\cong(T^{g},\gamma_{g})
. Note that we are now considering only lattices, without Hodge structures. This means that we can find
q\in\OO(\mathbf{Q})
and
k\in\OO^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
such that
qg^{\prime}k=g
; hence, the fiber of the map
\mathcal{M}_{T}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
are represented by
(\sigma,g)
for a fixed
g\in\OO(\mathbf{A}_{f})
and
\sigma
varying in
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{T}
. Now, two elements
(\sigma,g)
and
(\sigma^{\prime},g)
map to the same one in
\mathcal{M}_{T}
if and only if there is
q\in\OO(\mathbf{Q})
and
k\in\OO^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
such that
\sigma^{\prime}=q\sigma
and
qgk=g
, i.e., if and only there is
q\in\OO_{T}(\mathbf{Q})
such that
g^{-1}qg\in\OO_{T}^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
and
\sigma^{\prime}=q\sigma
. We can view
g
as an isometry between
T\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
and
T^{g}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
, so the equation above says that
q
must act integrally on
T^{g}
, i.e.,
q\in\OO_{T^{g}}(\mathbf{Z})
. Moreover, since
k
acts trivially on
A_{T}
one can deduce from the same equation that
q\in\OO_{T^{g}}^{*}(\mathbf{Z})
. Now, note that for any
q\in\OO_{T^{g}}^{*}(\mathbf{Z})
there is
k\in\OO_{T}^{*}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
such that
qgk=g
, simply by putting
k=gqg^{-1}
. So the fiber over
(T^{g},\gamma_{g})\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}^{*}(T))
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{\pm}/\OO^{*}(T^{g})
.
∎

2.5.
Fields of moduli and rigidity

Let
(X,\iota)
be a
L
-quasipolarized K3 surface over
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
. The field generated by the point in
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}})
representing

is called the field of moduli of
(X,\iota)
. This can also be defined as the fixed field of

\{\sigma\in G_{\mathbf{Q}}\colon(X,\iota)\cong(X^{\sigma},\iota^{\sigma})\}\subset G_{\mathbf{Q}}.

In general, the field of moduli is smaller than (any) field of definition. For a lattice polarized K3 surface, most of the times the two fields coincide due to the scarcity of automorphisms. In general, an argument of Galois descent shows that the two fields coincide whenever the parametrized object has no non-trivial automorphisms. Denote by
\mu(X)
the set of Hodge isometries of
T(X_{\mathbb{C}})
. Note that this corresponds to the roots of unity in the field
E_{X}=\End_{\operatorname{Hdg}}(T(X_{\mathbb{C}})_{\mathbf{Q}})
and, in particular,
\mu(X)=\{\pm 1\}
whenever
E_{X}
is totally real.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
(X,\iota)
be a
L
-quasipolarized K3 surface, and assume that
X
can be defined over
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
. Assume that
\iota
is an isomorphism between
L
and
\operatorname{NS}(X)
. If the natural map
\mu(X)\rightarrow\OO(A_{X})
is injective, then
(X,\iota)
can be defined over its field of moduli.

Proof.

Let
(X,\iota)
and
(Y,\iota)
be
(L,e)
-quasipolarized K3 surfaces, and assume that both
\iota
and
\iota^{\prime}
are isomorphisms and that
(X,\iota)\cong(X^{\prime},\iota^{\prime}).
We begin by showing that there is a unique isomorphism between
(X,\iota)
and
(X^{\prime},\iota^{\prime}).
Pick two isomorphisms
f,f^{\prime}
and consider
g=f^{\prime}\circ\pi^{-1}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Then,

is an automorphism of
X
which acts trivially on
\operatorname{NS}(X)
and therefore on

. Its action on

is given by a integral Hodge isometry, that is, by an element of
\mu\in\mu(X)
. Since the action of

must be the identity due to the fact that

and

are naturally isomorphic, we conclude thanks to the assumption that
\mu(X)\rightarrow\OO(A_{X})

such that

there is a unique isomorphism

of polarized K3 surfaces

. One checks that this gives a Galois descent data for

over its field of moduli (see
[

]

∎

This automatically holds when

is not
2
-elementary (that is,

is
2

Our aim now is to show that this always happens outside a proper closed subset of
\mathcal{F}_{L}

Definition 2.25
.

For a negative definite lattice
K
, let
\Delta(K)
be the set of roots of
K

\Delta(K)=\Delta^{+}(K)\sqcup\Delta^{-}(K).
We denote by

which respects this decomposition.

This is well-defined up to conjugation by an element of the Weyl group
W(K)
. In the next, with a triple
(T,K,L)

\Lambda
which are pairwise orthogonal, their orthogonal sum
T\oplus K\oplus L
is of finite index in
\Lambda
and
T
has signature
(2_{+},n_{-})
and

Definition 2.26
.

We say that
(T,K,L)
is

if every isometry of the form
\pm\mathrm{Id}_{T}\oplus q\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L}
with

is the identity (note that this is well-defined and does not depend on the chosen decomposition of
\Delta(K)
).

Remark 2.5
.

Rigidity is easy to characterize when
\mathrm{rank}(K)=1.
In this case
\OO(K)=\mu_{2}
and therefore rigidity is automatic if
K=\langle-2\rangle
, otherwise it is implied by the fact that none of the isometries
\pm\mathrm{Id}_{T}\oplus\pm\mathrm{Id}_{K}\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L}
other than the identity extend to an isometry of
\Lambda
. This happens in particular whenever each
A_{L},A_{K},A_{T}
is not
2
-torsion.

Now assume that
A_{K}
is not
2
-torsion and let
g\in\OO(\Lambda)
be an isometry which extends one of the form
\pm\mathrm{Id}_{T}\oplus q\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L}
. Let
M
be the saturation of
T\oplus L
in
\Lambda
. Then
M=K^{\perp}
and
K=M^{\perp}
since
K
is primitive in
\Lambda
. We assume that
\OO(K)\rightarrow\OO(A_{K})
is injective (see later about this condition). Now, if
g
extends an isometry of the form
\mathrm{Id}_{T}\oplus q\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L}
we see that
q
acts as the identity on

, and therefore,
q
maps to the identity of
\OO(A_{K})\cong\OO(A_{M})
. Thanks to the injectivity of
\OO(K)\rightarrow\OO(A_{K})
we conclude that

is itself the identity. So if

is not trivial it must extend an isometry of the form
-\mathrm{Id}_{T}\oplus q\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L}
. Using the same argument, this shows that

is the identity. We have thus proved:

Proposition 2.27
.

If
(X,\iota)
is not rigid, i.e. the triple
(T(X),\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\iota},\iota(L))
is not rigid, then there is a non-symplectic involution
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
which acts trivially on
\iota(L)
.

Nikulin has classified non-symplectic involutions of K3 surfaces. He showed among other things that a K3 surface
X
has a non-symplectic involution if and only if
\operatorname{NS}(X)
embeds primitively into a certain
2
-elementary lattice

, where

is the rank and

is the length of the discriminant form and

. We do not need to explain what these lattices are, we just need to know that they are finitely many.

Now we will comment on the injectivity of the map

for even definite lattices. In the reasoning above, we actually only needed the weaker statement that
\OO^{*}(K)\cap\OO_{\Delta}(K)=\{1\}

[

, Rmk. 1.14.7]

. More precisely,

is clearly contained in

\OO^{*}_{\Delta}(K)=\OO^{*}(K)\cap\OO_{\Delta}(K)
.

Definition 2.28

The lattice
\mathcal{L}(K)\coloneqq(K^{\OO^{*}_{\Delta}(K)})^{\perp}
is a Leech-type lattice.

It follows that if
\OO^{*}_{\Delta}(X)
is non-trivial, then

primitively contains a Leech-type lattice. The point is that Leech-type lattices are very rare:

Proposition 2.29
.

The only non-trivial Leech-type lattice of rank smaller than

is

.

Using Nikulin’s results, it is easy to show that Leech-type lattices are finite when the rank is bounded.

Proposition 2.30
.

There are only finitely many Leech-type lattices of rank smaller than a given

Proof.

This is basically the same proof as in Theorem 1.14.9 in
[

]

which is a multiple of

and bigger than

be the finite set of isometry classes of even, negative definite unimodular lattice of rank

each definite lattice of rank smaller than

as well. Then ones uses the second point of Proposition 1.14.8 in

to show that there is a finite subgroup

with

∎

Proposition 2.31
.

Assume that

is not
2

From the discussion in the next chapter, one has that if

. Now, consider all the points

such that

(1)

;

(2)

is not

(3)

(4)

There are no lattices of the form

sandwiched between

and

.

We know that all these points correspond to rigid points by our previous discussion, so we only need to show that the corresponding locus is open. Pairs satisfying (1) and (2) are contained in proper closed subsets (due to the finiteness of

3.
Maps between moduli spaces

Consider two lattices
T,S
of signature type
(2_{+},-)
and let
T\rightarrow S
be a map that respects the pairing (and therefore, necessarily injective). Then we have natural maps
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{S}
and
\OO^{*}(T)\hookrightarrow\OO^{*}(S)
from Proposition
2.3
and, therefore, we have an induced map
\mathcal{M}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{S}
which is analytic. The fact that this is also algebraic follows from an application of a result Borel (see Theorem 3.14
[
Mil05b
]
).

At the level of Hodge structures, the map
\mathcal{K}_{T}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}^{*}_{S}
works as follows. Let
K
be the orthogonal complement of
T
in
S
. Then,
S
is an overlattice of
T\oplus K
, meaning
T\oplus K\subset S
is of finite index. We recall that an overlattice is determined by an isotropic subgroup
H\subset A_{T}\oplus A_{K}=A_{T\oplus K}
, in the sense that
S
is the preimage of
H
via the natural quotient
T^{\vee}\oplus K^{\vee}\rightarrow A_{T}\oplus A_{K}
. We also have a natural isometry
A_{S}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\perp}/H
(since
H
is isotropic, the quotient on the left has a natural induced quadratic form). Given an element
(T^{\prime},\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}^{*}(T)
we thus obtain an isotropic subgroup
H^{\prime}\subset A_{T^{\prime}}\oplus A_{K}
simply by putting
H^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime}\oplus\operatorname{Id}_{A_{T}})(H)
. This determines an overlattice
S^{\prime}
of
T^{\prime}\oplus K
which belongs to the same genus of
S
, since they have the same signature and discriminant form. Moreover, we have isomorphisms
A_{S}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\perp}/H\cong(H^{\prime})^{\perp}/H^{\prime}\cong A_{S^{\prime}}
thus obtaining an element of
\mathcal{K}^{*}(S)
- once we endow
S^{\prime}
with the Hodge structure induced by the one on
T
and the trivial one on
K
.

Two maps of lattices
f,f^{\prime}\colon S\rightarrow
induce the same map
\mathcal{M}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T}
if and only if there is
g\in\OO^{*}(T)
such that
f=g\circ f^{\prime}
. This implies that there are only finitely many possible maps associated with some lattice
S
which embeds primitively into
T
. Similarly, the two maps have the same image if there is
g\in\OO^{*}(T)
such that
g(f(S))=f^{\prime}(S)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that there is
g\in\OO^{*}(T)
such that
g(f(S))=f^{\prime}(S)
, and let
f_{*},f^{\prime}_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T}
be the two maps induced respectively by
f
and
f^{\prime}
. Then, there is
q\in\OO(A_{T^{\prime}})
such that
f_{*}=f_{*}^{\prime}\circ q
, where we recall that
\OO(A_{T^{\prime}})
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}}
as previously noted.

Proof.

In fact,
f^{-1}\circ g^{-1}\circ f^{\prime}
is an isometry
\phi\in\OO(S)
and
q
is the unique element of
\OO(A_{S})
which acts as
\phi^{-1}
on
A_{S}
.
∎

From the point of moduli spaces of K3 surfaces, we choose a primitive embedding
S\hookrightarrow\Lambda
and we let
L
be its orthogonal complement. We distinguish two cases.

3.0.1.
Maps induced by primitive embeddings

Suppose

is a primitive embedding. Then, we obtain another primitive embedding

given by the composition
T\rightarrow S\rightarrow\Lambda
. If we denote by
N
the orthogonal complement of

then we obtain yet another primitive embedding

. Now, all of these primitive embeddings induce identifications
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{K}^{*}_{T}
and
\mathcal{F}_{N}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{K}^{*}_{S}
due to
2.2
. The proof of the following fact is just a verification that we omit:

Lemma 3.2
.

Under these identification, the map
\mathcal{F}_{L}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to
\mathcal{K}_{T}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}^{*}_{S}
sends

to

.

Using this description it is also easy to see that
\mathcal{F}_{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{N}

(f_{*}(X,\iota))^{\sigma}=(X,\iota\circ f)^{\sigma}=(X^{\sigma},(\iota\circ f)^{\sigma})=(X^{\sigma},\sigma_{*}\circ\iota\circ f)=f_{*}((X,\iota)^{\sigma}).

In general, all the maps induced by maps of lattices are defined over
\mathbf{Q}
. This is also mirrored by the fact that the corresponding Shimura datums have
\mathbf{Q}
as their reflex field since the lattice is indefinite, and that the maps above are maps of Shimura varieties induced by the embedding
\SO_{T}\hookrightarrow\SO_{S}
.

Remark 3.1
.

One can write the Noether-Lefschetz locus as the union of all the images of
\mathcal{M}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T}
for
S
varying among all the possible choices. It also suffices to make
S
vary among the sublattices of
T
of corank one, thus expressing the Noether-Lefschetz locus as union of divisors.

The next proposition is a consequence of the fact that
\OO(K)
is universally bounded for definite lattices of a given rank. This is in fact the reason why the main result holds in full generality, i.e., for any modular orthogonal variety.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any primitive embedding
T\hookrightarrow S
and any connected component
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{T}
of
\mathcal{M}_{T}
, the degree of the induced map
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{S}
onto its image can be bounded only in terms of
\mathrm{rank}(S)-\mathrm{rank}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{S}
be the connected component of
\mathcal{M}_{S}
to which
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{T}
is mapped. As explained at the beginning of this section, this can determined via lattice theory: if
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{T})
is determined (up to an indeterminacy of at most two) by an isomorphism class of a pair
(T^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{T})
, then the construction carried out before above determines another tuple
(S^{\prime},\gamma_{S}^{\prime})\in\pi_{0}(\mathcal{M}_{S})
. Note that
S^{\prime}
is constructed as an overlattice of
T^{\prime}\oplus K
. This means that there is a primitive embedding
T^{\prime}\hookrightarrow S^{\prime}
such that the map
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{0}_{S}
is induced by
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{\pm}/\OO^{*}(T^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{S}^{\pm}/\OO^{*}(S^{\prime}).

Let
\OO^{*}(T)^{+}\subset\OO^{*}(T)
be the stabilizer of
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}
. This acts on
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}
, and we consider the induced map
\mathcal{D}_{T}^{+}/\OO^{*}(T)^{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{+}_{S}/\OO^{*}(S)^{+}
. Let
\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T)\coloneqq\{g\in\OO^{*}(S)\colon g(T)=T\}
and write
\Gamma\subset\OO(T)
for the image of
\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T)

Remark 4.1
.

In fact, the number of rational preimages is related to the Picard number of

4.1.
Consequences of Bombieri-Lang

We begin by proving point (1) of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof.

By
[
Ma18
, Thm.1.3]
, we know that in this case the (components of the) space
\mathcal{M}_{T}(p)^{\bullet}
are of general type for almost all

. This implies that
\mathcal{M}_{T}(p)^{\bullet}(K)
is not Zariski dense for every number field
K
under the Bombieri-Lang conjecture, and then we conclude by point (2) of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

To prove point (2), we slightly generalize the ideas behind Theorem
1.1
and use the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
Ma18
, Thm.1.1.]
)
Up to scaling, there are only finitely many lattices
T
of signature
(2,17)
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{T}/\OO(T)
is not of general type.

Note that the quotient here is by the full orthogonal group. Let
p
be a prime number as in Corollary
3.7
and consider the surjective map
\OO(T)\rightarrow\OO(A_{T})\times\OO_{T}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
. For any sublattice
pT\subset T^{\prime}\subset T
, let
\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T^{\prime})
be its stabilizer in
\OO^{*}(T)
and
\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T^{\prime}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})\subset\OO^{*}_{T}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})
be the stabilizer of
T^{\prime}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
in
T\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}
. Then we have natural inclusions

(4.2)

\OO^{*}(T^{\prime})\subset\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T^{\prime})\subset\OO^{*}(T)\text{ , }\OO^{*}(T^{\prime})\subset\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T^{\prime})\subset\OO(T^{\prime})

and similarly for their adelic counterparts. Now, consider the varieties

\mathcal{M}_{T/T^{\prime}}\coloneqq\OO_{T^{\prime}}(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}}^{\pm}\times\OO_{T^{\prime}}(\mathbf{A}_{f})/\mathrm{Stab}^{*}(T^{\prime}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{Z}})

and

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T^{\prime}}\coloneqq\OO_{T^{\prime}}(\mathbf{Q})\backslash\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}}^{\pm}\times\OO_{T^{\prime}}(\mathbf{A}_{f})/\OO_{T^{\prime}}(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}).

Then we have the coverings
\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T/T^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T}
and
\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{T/T^{\prime}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T^{\prime}}
induced by
4.2
.
Note that both
\mathcal{M}_{T/T^{\prime}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T^{\prime}}
are quotients of
\mathcal{M}_{T^{\prime}}
by subgroups of
\OO(A_{T^{\prime}})

Corollary 4.2
.

If
p
is big enough and
pT\subsetneq T^{\prime}\subsetneq T
then the components of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{T^{\prime}}
are all of general type.

Proof.

In fact, each connected component dominates a variety of the form
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{T^{\prime\prime}}/\OO(T^{\prime\prime})^{+}
for some lattice
T^{\prime\prime}
in the same genus as
T^{\prime}
. But then we use Theorem
4.1
and choose
p
big enough so that, for any lattice
S
whose quadratic form is primitive (i.e., the value group of
S
is
\mathbf{Z}
) and of rank
19
with
p\leq\mathrm{discr}(T)
, we have that
\mathcal{D}_{T}/\OO(T)^{+}
is of general type. Then since
p
clearly divides the discriminant of
T^{\prime}
whenver

, we conclude.
∎

Now we prove point (2) of Corollary
1.3
. Let
x\in U(K)
be a rational point of
\mathcal{M}_{T}
which belongs to the Noether-Lefschetz locus, and let
(X,\iota)/K
be the corresponding K3 surface. We can view
T(X_{\mathbb{C}})\subset T
as a proper primitive sublattice. But then the Galois representation associated with

must stabilize

4.2.
Final remarks

The following conjecture of Shafarevich implies that
\mathcal{F}_{L}(K)
is never Zariski-dense:

Conjecture 4.1
.

Let
N>0
be an integer. Then, there are only finitely many lattices that can appear as
\operatorname{NS}(\overline{X})
where
X/K
is a K3 surface and
[K\colon\mathbf{Q}]\leq N
.

To attempt to deduce this from the Bombieri-Lang conjecture and the ideas above, one has first to answer the following highly non-trivial question:

Question 4.1
.

Is it true that the positive dimensional components of the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}(K)
are contained in
\mathcal{F}_{L}^{nl}
?

Finally, there is an analogue of Conjecture
4.1
for abelian varieties proposed by Coleman (unpublished) which is known to imply many uniform bounds for the arithmetic of abelian varieties and K3 surfaces (see
[
R1́8
]
and
[
OSZ19
]
).

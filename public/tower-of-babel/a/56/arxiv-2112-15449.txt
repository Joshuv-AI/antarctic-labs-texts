Certain properties of the class of univalent functions with real coefficients
Milutin Obradović, Nikola Tuneski

Abstract
. Let 𝒰 + {\mathcal{U}}^{+} be the class of analytic functions f f such that z f ⁡ ( z ) \frac{z}{f(z)} has real and positive coefficients and f − 1 f^{-1} be its inverse. In this paper we give sharp estimates of the initial coefficients and initial logarithmic coefficients for f f , as well as, sharp estimates of the second and the third Hankel determinant for f f and f − 1 f^{-1} . We also show that the Zalcman conjecture holds for functions f f from 𝒰 + {\mathcal{U}}^{+} .

A collection of the more significant once can be found in

Lemma 1

f\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)\Leftrightarrow\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}\leq\lambda.

Proof.

If

\begin{split}\quad\left|\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(z)-1\right|=\left|\frac{z}{f(z)}-z\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{\prime}-1\right|=\left|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}z^{n}\right|<\lambda,\end{split}

z\in{\mathbb{D}}.
For real
z
and
z\rightarrow 1
from left, the last inequality gives
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}\leq\lambda.

If
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}\leq\lambda

\left|\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(z)-1\right|=\left|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}z^{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}|z|^{n}<\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)b_{n}\leq\lambda,

z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, which mens that

∎

2.
Coefficient estimates

The famous Koebe 1/4 theorem guaranties that each function
f
from
{\mathcal{S}}
has an inverse at least on a disk with radius 1/4. Let the inverse function has an expansion

(5)

f^{-1}(w)=w+A_{2}w^{2}+A_{3}w^{3}+\cdots

on the disk
|w|<\frac{1}{4}
. By using the identity
f(f^{-1}(w))=w
, and representations (
1
) and (
5
), we obtain the following relations:

(6)

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{2}=-a_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
A_{3}=-a_{3}+2a_{2}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
A_{4}=-a_{4}+5a_{2}a_{3}-5a_{2}^{3}.\end{array}

We now give sharp bounds of
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
and
A_{4}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
, and

is given by (

\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq A_{2}\leq 1+\lambda,\\[5.69054pt]
0\leq A_{3}\leq 1+3\lambda+\lambda^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
0\leq A_{4}\leq(1+\lambda)(1+5\lambda+\lambda^{2}).\end{array}

Proof.

Since
f\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
, then by definition

f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots=\frac{z}{1+b_{1}z+b_{2}z^{2}+\cdots},

where
b_{n}\geq 0,\quad n=1,2,3,\ldots.
After comparing the coefficients we receive,

(8)

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{2}=-b_{1},\\[5.69054pt]
a_{3}=-b_{2}+b_{1}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
a_{4}=-b_{3}+2b_{1}b_{2}-b_{1}^{3},\\[5.69054pt]
a_{5}=-b_{4}+b_{2}^{2}+2b_{1}b_{3}-3b_{1}^{2}b_{2}+b_{1}^{4},\end{array}

and by combining (
8
) with (
6
),

(9)

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{2}=b_{1},\\[5.69054pt]
A_{3}=b_{2}+b_{1}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
A_{4}=b_{3}+3b_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}^{3}.\end{array}

Since
a_{2}
is real (so are
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
,…), and

for all functions from
{\mathcal{U}}(\lambda)
(see

From

Further, from Lemma

which implies

(11)

\begin{split}0\leq b_{2}&\leq\lambda\\
b_{2}+2b_{3}&\leq\lambda\quad\left(\Leftrightarrow b_{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda-b_{2})\right)\\
b_{2}+2b_{3}+3b_{4}&\leq\lambda\quad\left(\Leftrightarrow b_{4}\leq\frac{1}{3}(\lambda-2b_{3}-b_{2})\right).\end{split}

Combining (

) and (

and for

\begin{split}0\leq A_{4}&\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda-b_{2})+3(1+\lambda)b_{2}+(1+\lambda)^{3}\\
&=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\left(\frac{5}{2}+3\lambda\right)b_{2}+(1+\lambda)^{3}\\
&\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}+\left(\frac{5}{2}+3\lambda\right)\lambda+(1+\lambda)^{3}\\
&=(1+\lambda)(1+5\lambda+\lambda^{2}).\end{split}

For the sharpness of the upper bounds, for the function

defined by (

\begin{split}f_{\lambda}(z)&=\frac{z}{1+(1+\lambda)z+\lambda z^{2}}\\

i.e.,

For the sharpness of the lower bounds it is enough to consider the inverse function of the function

∎

3.
The second and the third Hankel determinant

Another concept, rediscovered few years ago, attracting the attention of mathematicians working in the field of univalent functions is so called Hankel determinant
H_{q}(n)(f)
of a given function
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots,
, for
q\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
, defined by

H_{q}(n)(f)=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\ldots&a_{n+q-1}\\
a_{n+1}&a_{n+2}&\ldots&a_{n+q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
a_{n+q-1}&a_{n+q}&\ldots&a_{n+2q-2}\\
\end{array}\right|.

Main interest is to find upper bound (preferably sharp) of the modulus of
H_{q}(n)(f)
. The general Hankel determinant is hard to deal with, so the second and the third ones,

H_{2}(2)(f)=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{2}&a_{3}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{array}\right|=a_{2}a_{4}-a_{3}^{2}

and

H_{3}(1)(f)=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a_{2}&a_{3}\\
a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{4}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{5}\\
\end{array}\right|=a_{3}(a_{2}a_{4}-a_{3}^{2})-a_{4}(a_{4}-a_{2}a_{3})+a_{5}(a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}),

respectively, are studied instead. The research is focused on the subclasses of univalent functions (starlike, convex,
\alpha
-convex, close-to-convex, spirallike,…) since the general class of normalised univalent functions is also hard to deal with. Some of the more significant results can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
9
,
11
,
13
,
25
]
.

In this section we will give sharp bounds of the second and the third Hankel determinant for the functions in
{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)
and for their inverse.

Using (
8
), (
10
) and (
11
), after some calculations, we receive

(14)

\begin{split}H_{2}(2)(f)&=b_{1}b_{3}-b_{2}^{2},\\
H_{3}(1)(f)&=b_{2}b_{4}-b_{3}^{2}.\end{split}

Theorem 3
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)
,
0<\lambda\leq 1
. Then the following estimates are sharp:

(
i
)

-\lambda^{2}\leq H_{2}(2)(f)\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\frac{\lambda}{2}
;

(
ii
)

-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\leq H_{3}(1)(f)\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}
.

Proof.

(
i
)

First, let note that from (
10
) and (
11
), we have
b_{1},b_{3}\geq 0
and
b_{2}\leq\lambda
, which easily implies

H_{2}(2)(f)=b_{1}b_{3}-b_{2}^{2}\geq-b_{2}^{2}\geq-\lambda^{2}.

The estimate is sharp due to the function
f(z)=\frac{z}{1+\lambda z^{2}}
.

For the upper bound, from (
14
) we have
H_{2}(2)(f)=b_{1}b_{3}-b_{2}^{2}\leq b_{1}b_{3}
, and further from
0\leq b_{1}\leq 1+\lambda
and
0\leq b_{3}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}
(see (
10
) and (
11
)) we need to find
\max\{b_{1}b_{3}\}
. For
b_{3}=\frac{\lambda}{2}
(
\Rightarrow

b_{2}=b_{4}=\cdots=0
) we have

H_{2}(2)(f)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}b_{1}.

If we consider the function
f
defined by

\frac{z}{f(z)}=1+b_{1}z+\frac{\lambda}{2}z^{3},

then
f
is in
{\mathcal{U}}^{+}(\lambda)
if
1+b_{1}z+\frac{\lambda}{2}z^{3}\neq 0
. Since

\left.\frac{z}{f(z)}\right|_{z=1}=1+b_{1}+\frac{\lambda}{2}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\left.\frac{z}{f(z)}\right|_{z=-1}=1-b_{1}-\frac{\lambda}{2},

then it is necessary
1-b_{1}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\geq 0
. In contrary, if
1-b_{1}-\frac{\lambda}{2}<0
, then for
z=r
real in
-1<r<1
, we have

\left.\frac{z}{f(z)}\right|_{z=r}=1+b_{1}r+\frac{\lambda}{2}r^{3},

has zero in
(-1,1)
, i.e.,
f
is not analytic on the unit disk.

The previous condition is also a sufficient one. Namely, for
0\leq b_{1}\leq 1-\frac{\lambda}{2}
and
0\leq b_{3}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}
:

\begin{split}\left|\frac{z}{f(z)}\right|&=\left|1+b_{1}z+\frac{\lambda}{2}z^{3}\right|\geq 1-b_{1}|z|-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z|^{3}\\
&>1-\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)-\frac{\lambda}{2}=0.\end{split}

So,
0\leq b_{1}\leq 1-\frac{\lambda}{2}
and
H_{2}(2)(f)\leq\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\frac{\lambda}{2}
. The upper bound is sharp due to the function
f(z)=\frac{z}{1+(1-\lambda/2)z+\lambda/2z^{3}}
.

(
ii
)

Similarly as in the proof of part (a), from (
14
) and (
11
),

\begin{split}H_{3}(1)(f)&\leq b_{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}(\lambda-b_{2}-2b_{3})-b_{3}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\lambda b_{2}-\frac{1}{3}b_{2}^{2}-\frac{2}{3}b_{2}b_{3}-b_{3}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{3}\lambda b_{2}-\frac{1}{3}b_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}.\end{split}

Also,
H_{3}(1)(f)\geq-b_{3}^{2}\geq-\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}
, since by (
11
),
b_{3}\leq\frac{\lambda-b_{2}}{2}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}.
The functions
f(z)=\frac{z}{1+\lambda/2z^{3}}
and
f(z)=\frac{z}{1+\lambda/2z^{2}+\lambda/6z^{4}}
show that the estimates are sharp.

∎

where

is an increasing function of

, and

Also,

(

For the third Hankel determinant of

4.
Zalcman conjecture

In the early 1970’s Zalcman posed the following conjecture for the class of univalent functions:

|a_{n}^{2}-a_{2n-1}|\leq(n-1)^{2}\quad\quad(n\in{\mathbb{N}},n\geq 2).

There is a manuscript by Krushkal (
[
8
]
) that uses complex geometry of the universal Teichmüller space claiming to have proven the conjecture, but this work is not widely reckognized to be correct.
Later, in 1999, Ma (
[
12
]
) proposed a generalized Zalcman conjecture,

|a_{m}a_{n}-a_{m+n-1}|\leq(m-1)(n-1)\quad\quad(m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},m\geq 2,n\geq 2),

and closed it for the class of starlike functions and for the class of univalent functions with real coefficients. The general case is still an open problem.

The Zalcman conjecture for the class
{\mathcal{U}}

Variance of the k k -fold divisor function in arithmetic progressions for individual modulus
David T. Nguyen

Abstract
. In this paper, we confirm a smoothed version of a recent conjecture on the variance of the k k -fold divisor function in arithmetic progressions to individual composite moduli, in a restricted range. In contrast to a previous result of Rodgers and Soundararajan [ 28 ] , we do not require averaging over the moduli. Our proof adapts a technique of S. Lester [ 23 ] who treated in the same range the variance of the k k -fold divisor function in the short intervals setting, and is based on a smoothed Voronoï summation formula but twisted by multiplicative characters. The use of Dirichlet characters allows us to extend to a wider range from previous result of Kowalski and Ricotta [ 22 ] who used additive characters. Smoothing also permits us to treat all k k unconditionally. This result is closely related to moments of Dirichlet L L -functions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background of the problem

Barban-Davenport-Halberstam-type inequalities involve upper bounding quantities of the shape

(1.1)

\sum_{d\leq D}\ \sum_{a\pmod{d}}|\Delta(f;X,d,a)|^{2},

where
|\Delta(f;X,d,a)|
is being squared, with
\Delta(f;X,d,a)
a certain error term depending on the function
f
, parameters
X,d
, and
a
coprime to
d
. These types of inequalities originate from the works of Barban
[
1
,
2
]
(1963, 1964), Davenport-Halberstam
[
13
]
(1966), and they yield a wilder range for
D
in terms of
X
as compared to Bombieri-Vinogradov-type inequalities which bound expressions roughly of the form

(1.2)

\sum_{d\leq D}\ \max_{a\pmod{d}}|\Delta(f;X,d,a)|.

Non-trivial bounds for (
1.1
) have many applications in number theory–we give two recent instances below.

1. A version of the Barban-Davenport-Halberstam-type inequality, among with other novel ideas, with the function
f
replaced by related convolutions over primes, were skillfully used by Zhang
[
31
, Lemma 10]
(2014) in his spectacular proof that there are bounded gaps between primes.

2. In their work in 2017, Heath-Brown and Li
[
18
]
proved a version of Barban-Davenport-Halberstam inequality in their Corollary 2 as an ingredient to show that the sparse sequence
a^{2}+p^{4}
, where
a
is a natural number and
p
is a prime, contains infinitely many primes.

In this paper, we study the asymptotic of a related quantity to (
1.1
). Let
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
be integers. Let
\tau_{k}(n)
denote the
k
-fold divisor function

(1.3)

\tau_{k}(n)=\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}=n}1,

where the sum runs over ordered
k
-tuples
(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k})
of positive integers for which
n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}=n
, so its Dirichlet generating function is

(1.4)

\zeta(s)^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{k}(n)}{n^{s}},\quad(\Re s>1).

Explicitly, if
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
is the prime factorization of
n
, then

(1.5)

\tau_{k}(n)=\binom{k+\alpha_{1}-1}{k-1}\cdots\binom{k+\alpha_{r}-1}{k-1};

(see Lemma
5
). There is a precise prediction on the asymptotic of the variance

(1.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{d}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\textrm{mod }d)\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)\right|^{2},

(c.f. (
1.1
)), as
d,X\to\infty
at a certain rate, which is the main object of study in this paper. We state this conjecture (in our notation) below.

Conjecture A

(Keating–Rodgers–Roditty-Gershon–Rudnick-Soundararajan)
.

Fix
k\geq 2
. For
X,d\to\infty
such that

, we have

(1.7)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
(a,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{d}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\textrm{mod }d)\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)\right|^{2}\sim a_{k}(d)\gamma_{k}(c)X(\log d)^{k^{2}-1},

where

is the arithmetic constant

(1.8)

a_{k}(d)=\lim_{s\to 1^{+}}(s-1)^{k^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{k}(n)^{2}}{n^{s}},

and
\gamma_{k}(c)
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
k^{2}-1
defined by

(1.9)

\gamma_{k}(c)=\frac{1}{k!G(k+1)^{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\delta_{c}(w_{1}+\cdots w_{k})\Delta(w)^{2}d^{k}w,

where

This conjecture originates from the work of Keating, Rodgers, Roditty-Gershon, and Rudnick (or, KRRR, for short) in 2018
[

for prime moduli, with general form for composite moduli put forward by Rodgers and Soundararajan in

Firstly, Conjecture

Outline of the proofs

We briefly explain here the wider range (

\chi

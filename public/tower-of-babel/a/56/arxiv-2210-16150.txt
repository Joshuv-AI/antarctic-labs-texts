The centroid Banach-Mazur distance between the parallelogram and the triangle


Abstract




Marek Lassak

Abstract.

Let
C
and
D
be convex bodies in the Euclidean space
E^{d}
.
We define the centroid Banach-Mazur distance
\delta_{BM}^{\rm cen}(C,D)
similarly to the classic Banach-Mazur distance
\delta_{BM}(C,D)
, but with the extra requirement that the centroids of
C
and an affine image of
D
coincide.
We prove that for the parallelogram
P
and the triangle
T
in
E^{2}
we have
\delta_{BM}^{\rm cen}(P,T)=\frac{5}{2}
.

Keywords:
Banach-Mazur distance, centroid Banach-Mazur distance, convex body, centroid, parallelogram, triangle

Primary: 52A21, Secondary 46B20, 52A10

Introduction

The classical definition of the Banach-Mazur distance of centrally symmetric convex bodies of the Euclidean

Namely, for convex bodies

where

denotes a homothety with a positive ratio

For the relationship of them see Claim of
[

A survey on the Banach-Mazur distance is given in the book
[

Moreover, in Sections 3.2 and 3.3 of the book

Here is the notion of the centroid Banach-Mazur distance of convex bodies

of

:

\delta_{\rm BM}^{\rm cen}(C,D)=\inf_{a,\,h_{\lambda}}\{\lambda;\,a(D)\subset C\subset h_{\lambda}a(D)\ {\rm and}\ {\rm cen}(a(D))={\rm cen}(C)\},

where

again stands for an affine transformation, but

means a homothety with the ratio

whose center is at the centroids

Observe that the centroids of

We easily show that

for every

and

.
Recall that pioneer research on the centroid was provided by Neumann

In Theorem we prove that

.
Our effort is put in order to show that

At the end of the paper we present a few remarks.
The first concerns the positions of our triangle with respect to the parallelogram for which the ratio

The second comments the dual version of Theorem.
The third shows the following generalization of Theorem for a centrally-symmetric convex body

We also propose a more general task to consider an arbitrary convex body instead of

Generalised Spin r Structures on Homogeneous Spaces
Diego Artacho ∗ and Marie-Amélie Lawn ∗

Abstract
. Spinorial methods have proven to be a powerful tool to study geometric properties of spin manifolds. Our aim is to continue the spinorial study of manifolds that are not necessarily spin. We introduce and study the notion of G G -invariance of spin r structures on a manifold M M equipped with an action of a Lie group G G . For the case when M M is a homogeneous G G -space, we prove a classification result of these invariant structures in terms of the isotropy representation. As an example, we study the invariant spin r structures for all the homogeneous realisations of the spheres.

1.
Introduction

The field of spin geometry has proven to be a fruitful area of study with numerous applications in various areas of mathematics. It has been applied to a wide range of problems, from the study of topology and differential equations to the theory of quantum mechanics. However, by their nature, spinorial considerations are limited to manifolds that admit a spin structure. To overcome this limitation, considerable efforts have been made in recent decades to extend spin geometry to non-spin manifolds. Recent developments have led to the introduction of spin
C
and spin
H
structures, which come, respectively, from the complexification and quaternionification of the classical spin groups
[
11
,
24
,
6
,
13
]
.

The idea of extending spin geometry to non-spin manifolds was introduced by Friedrich and Trautmann
[
12
]
, who started the theory of spinorial Lipschitz structures. These ideas have been developed further in
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

While every spin manifold is spin
C
and every spin
C
manifold is in turn spin
H
, the converses are not true. For example, even-dimensional complex projective spaces
\mathbb{CP}^{2n}
are not spin, but they are spin
C
. Similarly, the Wu manifold
W=\SU(3)/\SO(3)
is not spin
C
, yet it is spin
H

[
7
]
. Moreover, there exist examples of manifolds which are not even spin
H
, such as
W\times W

[
2
]
.

It is a well-known fact that every almost-complex Riemannian manifold is spin
C
, and every almost-quaternionic manifold is spin
H
. Furthermore, it is known that every oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
\leq 3
is spin. In 2005, Teichner and Vogt
[
26
]
completed the work started by Hirzebruch and Hopf in 1958
[
17
]
, showing that every oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
\leq 4
admits a spin
C
structure. These structures are crucial for Seiberg-Witten theory, which has become an essential tool in the study of smooth
4
-manifolds
[
23
]
. In 2021, Albanese and Milivojević
[
2
,
3
]
showed that every oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
\leq 5
is spin
H
, and for closed manifolds, this is true for dimensions
\leq 7
. In
[
25
]
, in analogy to Seiberg-Witten theory, the authors construct quaternionic monopoles, which are associated to spin
H
structures on
4
-manifolds.

Espinosa and Herrera
[
10
]
introduced the concept of spin
r
structure for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the cases
r=1,2,3
corresponding to spin, spin
C
and spin
H
respectively. They call these structures
spinorially twisted spin structures

[
10
,
15
,
14
]
. Albanese and Milivojević
[
2
]
call these structures
generalised spin
k
structures
. An oriented Riemannian manifold
M
is spin
r
if, and only if, it can be embedded into a spin manifold with codimension
r
. Consequently, if
r<s
, every spin
r
manifold is spin
s
. They proved that there is no
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that every oriented Riemannian manifold is spin
r
. This result can be interpreted as ensuring that this chain of structures does not
stabilise
. As pointed out by Lawson in
[
18
]
, this is remarkable, as every manifold embeds into an orientable manifold with codimension
1
.

For a general spin
r
manifold, there is not a preferred spin
r
structure to work with. In
[
10
]
, the authors give a canonical construction of a spin
n
structure for every oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold in terms of its holonomy. We show that this structure is not induced by a spin
s
structure for any
s<n
for a wide class of manifolds.

For
r>3
, there are presently no known topological obstructions to the existence of a spin
r
structure, and the problem of their classification is beyond our reach. In this paper, we give a complete study of invariant spin
r
structures on homogeneous spaces.

Homogeneous spaces are of great interest in this context, because there is a simple way of looking at spin structures provided they are invariant, namely as lifts of the isotropy representation to the spin group
[
5
,
4
,
9
]
. However, not all homogeneous spaces are spin, and indeed, all the examples provided above are homogeneous. It is therefore natural to ask whether this isotropy-lifting criterion can be extended to invariant spin
r
structures. It turns out that this is the case, as we show in Section
3
, along with a characterisation theorem (Theorem
3.7
).

Another feature of homogeneous spaces is that they provide an ideal setting for explicit spinorial computations. For instance, in
[
1
]
, the authors explore the relationship between certain invariant spinors on homogeneous spheres and various
G
-structures.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we provide simple examples of
n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian homogeneous
G
-spaces
M
for which the minimal
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M
admits a
G
-invariant spin
r
structure is exactly
n
(Theorem
4.1
). This establishes a
G
-invariant analogue of the fact that, for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an oriented Riemannian manifold that does not admit a spin
r
structure
[
2
]
.

Additionally, we study a family of examples. The compact and connected Lie groups that act transitively and effectively on spheres were classified by Montgomery and Samelson in
[
22
]
. For each of these homogeneous realisations of the spheres, we compute the minimal
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that the corresponding sphere admits an invariant spin
r
structure, refining the picture presented in
[
9
]
, where only spin structures were considered. Finally, we show the beautiful relationship between the existence of invariant spin
r
structures on homogeneous spheres and lifts of the holonomy representation of an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold to
\Spin^{r}(n)
, via Berger’s classification of holonomy groups.

Generalised spin
r
structures

2.1.
Spin
r
groups

Denote by
\SO(n)
the special orthogonal group, and let
\lambda_{n}\colon\Spin(n)\to\SO(n)
be the standard two-sheeted covering. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the group

\Spin^{r}(n)\coloneqq\left(\Spin(n)\times\Spin(r)\right)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\,,

where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle(-1,-1)\rangle\subseteq\Spin(n)\times\Spin(r)
. Note that
\Spin^{1}(n)=\Spin(n)
. Moreover, as
\Spin(2)\cong\U(1)
and
\Spin(3)\cong\Sp(1)
, it is clear that
\Spin^{2}(n)=\Spin^{\mathbb{C}}(n)
and
\Spin^{3}(n)=\Spin^{\mathbb{H}}(n)
. There are natural Lie group homomorphisms

\begin{aligned} \lambda^{r}_{n}\colon&\Spin^{r}(n)\to\SO(n)\\
&[\mu,\nu]\mapsto\lambda_{n}(\mu)\end{aligned}
,

\begin{aligned} \xi^{r}_{n}\colon&\Spin^{r}(n)\to\SO(r)\\
&[\mu,\nu]\mapsto\lambda_{r}(\nu)\end{aligned}
.

r<s
, there is a natural inclusion
\iota^{rs}_{n}\colon\Spin^{r}(n)\hookrightarrow\Spin^{s}(n)

We need to make some topological considerations first, which we shall use later. Define the map

\displaystyle\varphi^{r,n}\colon

\displaystyle\Spin^{r}(n)\to\SO(n)\times\SO(r)\,.

\displaystyle[\mu,\nu]\mapsto(\lambda_{n}(\mu),\lambda_{r}(\nu))

The following result shows that this map is a two-sheeted covering, and describes it at the level of fundamental groups. We will use this result to apply the lifting criterion.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\varphi^{r,n}_{\sharp}
be the map induced by
\varphi^{r,n}
at the level of fundamental groups. Then,

(1)

\varphi^{r,n}
is a
2
-sheeted covering map;

(2)

\varphi^{2,2}_{\sharp}\left(\pi_{1}\left(\Spin^{2}(n)\right)\right)=\langle(1,\pm 1)\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{1}\left(\SO(2)\times\SO(2)\right)
;

(3)

if
n\geq 3
, then
\varphi^{2,n}_{\sharp}\left(\pi_{1}\left(\Spin^{2}(n)\right)\right)=\langle(1,1)\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{1}\left(\SO(n)\times\SO(2)\right)
;

(4)

if
r,n\geq 3
, then
\varphi^{r,n}_{\sharp}\left(\pi_{1}\left(\Spin^{r}(n)\right)\right)=\langle(1,1)\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong\pi_{1}\left(\SO(n)\times\SO(r)\right)
.

Proof.

Consider the composition

\Spin(n)\times\Spin(r)\xrightarrow{p}\Spin^{r}(n)\xrightarrow{\varphi^{r,n}}\SO(n)\times\SO(r)\,,

where
p
is the obvious projection.

Point (1) follows from the fact that
\lambda_{n},\lambda_{r}
and
p
are
2
-sheeted covering maps.

For (2), suppose
r=n=2
. Recall that
\Spin(2)\cong\SO(2)\cong S^{1}
, and that the map
\varphi^{2,2}\circ p
is given by
(z,w)\mapsto(z^{2},w^{2})
. Consider the loop
\alpha_{\pm}\colon[0,1]\to\Spin^{2}(2)
defined by
\alpha_{\pm}(t)=p\left(e^{i\pi t},e^{\pm i\pi t}\right)
. It is clear that the image of
\alpha_{\pm}
in the fundamental group of
\SO(2)\times\SO(2)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, is
(1,\pm 1)
. Moreover, the lift of every loop
\beta
based at
[1,1]
in
\Spin^{2}(2)
along
p
starting at
(1,1)
is of the form
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
, where either both
\beta_{i}
are loops based at
1
or both are paths from
1
to
-1
in the corresponding factors of
\Spin(2)\times\Spin(2)
. In the first (resp. second) case, the image of
\beta
in the fundamental group of
\SO(2)\times\SO(2)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, is of the form
(2r,2s)
(resp.
(2r+1,2s+1)
), for some
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. In both cases, the image of
\beta
is an element of
\langle(1,\pm 1)\rangle
. This proves (2).

Let us prove (3). Suppose
n\geq 3
. Let
\beta
be a loop in
\Spin^{2}(n)
based at
[1,1]
. Its lift along
p
starting at
(1,1)
is a path with endpoint either
(1,1)
or
(-1,-1)
. Hence, this lift is of the form
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
, where either both
\beta_{i}
are loops based at
1
or both are paths from
1
to
-1
in the corresponding factors of
\Spin(n)\times\Spin(2)
. In the first case, the image of
\beta
in the fundamental group of
\SO(n)\times\SO(2)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}
, is of the form
(0,2m)
, for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In the other case, the image of
\beta
is of the form
(1,2l+1)
, for some
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. This proves (3).

The proof of (4) is analogous.

∎

2.2.
Spin
r
structures on manifolds

As in the classical spin, spin
C
and spin
H
cases, one can define a family of structures on oriented Riemannian manifolds:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with principal
\SO(n)
-bundle of positively oriented orthonormal frames
FM
. A spin
r
structure on
M
is a lift of the structure group of
FM
along the Lie group homomorphism
\lambda^{r}_{n}
. In other words, it is a pair
(P,\Phi)
consisting of

•

a principal
\Spin^{r}(n)
-bundle
P
over
M
, and

•

a
\Spin^{r}(n)
-equivariant bundle homomorphism
\Phi\colon P\to FM
, where
\Spin^{r}(n)
acts on
FM
via
\lambda^{r}_{n}
.

The associated principal
\SO(r)
-bundle to
P
along
\xi^{r}_{n}
is called the auxiliary bundle of the spin
r
structure, and it is denoted by
\hat{P}
. The natural map
\theta\colon P\to FM\tilde{\times}\hat{P}
, where
\tilde{\times}
denotes fibre product, is a two-sheeted covering.

If
(P_{1},\Phi_{1})
and
(P_{2},\Phi_{2})
are spin
r
structures on
M
, an equivalence of spin
r
structures from
(P_{1},\Phi_{1})
to
(P_{2},\Phi_{2})
is a
\Spin^{r}(n)
-equivariant diffeomorphism
f\colon P_{1}\to P_{2}
such that
\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{2}\circ f
.

Remark 2.3
.

For
r<s
, a spin
r
structure naturally induces a spin
s
structure, namely via the associated bundle construction along
\iota^{rs}
.

Given an oriented Riemannian manifold
M
, it is therefore natural to ask which is the minimal
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M
has a spin
r
structure.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
M
be an oriented Riemannian manifold. The spin type of
M
, denoted by
\Sigma(M)
, is the smallest
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M
admits a spin
r
structure.

The spin type measures the failure of a manifold to be spin. For example, every spin manifold has spin type
1
, and
\Sigma\left(\mathbb{CP}^{2n}\right)=2
for all
n\geq 1
. Every oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
\leq 4
has spin type
\leq 2

[
26
]
. In
[
2
,
3
]
, the authors show that every (compact) oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
\leq 5
(
\leq 7
) has spin type
\leq 3
, and that, in every dimension
\geq 8
, there is a closed manifold with spin type
\geq 4
. For example, the product of the Wu manifold with itself is a
10
-dimensional manifold with spin type
\geq 4
– see
[
2
]
.

Being spin
r
turns out to be a topological property, as shown in the next result.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
2
]
, Proposition 3.2)
.

The following are equivalent for an orientable Riemannian manifold
M
:

(1)

M
is spin
r
,

(2)

there is an orientable rank-
r
real vector bundle
E\to M
such that
TM\oplus E
is spin,

(3)

M
immerses in a spin manifold with codimension
r
,

(4)

M
embeds in a spin manifold with codimension
r
. ∎

This lets us prove that Definition
2.4
is a good definition, in the sense that the spin type is defined for all oriented Riemannian manifolds:

Corollary 2.6
.

An oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
has spin type
1\leq\Sigma(M)\leq n
, and so the spin type is well-defined.

Proof.

By Whitney’s embedding theorem, such a manifold can be smoothly embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, which is spin. Now apply Proposition
2.5
. Equivalently, one can take
E=TM
in point (2) of Proposition
2.5
, and note that
w_{1}(TM\oplus TM)=0
and
w_{2}(TM\oplus TM)=0
, as
TM
is oriented.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Note that, if
n>1
and
M
is a closed oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold, one can improve the bound in Corollary
2.6
to
1\leq\Sigma(M)\leq n-\alpha(n)
, where
\alpha(n)
is the number of ones in the binary expression of

, by Cohen’s theorem
[
8
]

There is a way of constructing an explicit spin

n

G
be the holonomy group of

at
x

be the holonomy representation at

admits a reduction of the structure group from
\SO(n)
to

\Spin^{r}(n)

via the associated bundle construction. This is always the case for

Proposition 2.8

(see
[
10

, Proposition 3.3)

Let

3.1.
Classification

The main result of this section is Corollary
2.5
, which gives a classification of
G
-invariant spin
r
structures up to
G
-equivariant equivalence. Its proof relies on Lemmata
3.2
and
3.4
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G/H
be a homogeneous space. Let
K
be a Lie group and
\hat{\pi}\colon P\to G/H
a principal
K
-bundle over
G/H
. Suppose there exists a smooth left
G
-action on
P
by principal
K
-bundle homomorphisms lifting the
G
-action on
G/H
, i.e., such that
g\cdot(p\cdot k)=(g\cdot p)\cdot k
and
\hat{\pi}(g\cdot p)=g\cdot\hat{\pi}(p)
, for all
g\in G
,
k\in K
and
p\in P
. Then, there exists a Lie group homomorphism
\varphi\colon H\to K
such that:

(1)

P
is
G
-equivariantly bundle-isomorphic to
\pi\colon G\times_{\varphi}K\to G/H
, defined by
\pi([g,k])\to gH
.

(2)

P
is a trivial bundle if, and only if,
\varphi
extends to a Lie group homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}\colon G\to K
.

Moreover,
\varphi
is unique up to conjugation by elements of
K
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists a smooth
G
-action on
P
by
K
-bundle homomorphisms lifting the
G
-action on
G/H
. Then, the Lie group
G\times K
acts on
P
by

(g,k)\cdot p\coloneqq g\cdot p\cdot k^{-1}\,,

for every
g\in G
,
k\in K
and
p\in P
. Note that, as the
G
-action on
P
is by
K
-bundle homomorphisms, this is a well-defined action of
G\times K
on
P
. Moreover, this action is transitive, as the base space is a homogeneous
G
-space and
K
acts transitively on each fibre. Fix
p_{0}\in P
with projection
o\coloneqq e_{G}H\in G/H
. Let
H_{p_{0}}\subseteq G\times K
be the stabiliser of
p_{0}
. The first projection
H_{p_{0}}\to G
lands in
H
, because the right
K
-action does not change the fibre. And the resulting homomorphism
H_{p_{0}}\to H
is in fact an isomorphism, since the
K
-action is simply transitive on the fibres. Denote by
\hat{\varphi}
the inverse isomorphism, and define
\varphi\coloneqq\pr_{2}\circ\hat{\varphi}\colon H\to K
. Now, it is straightforward to check that the map

\displaystyle\begin{split}G\times_{\varphi}K&\to P\\
[g,k]&\mapsto gp_{0}k\end{split}

is a
G
-equivariant
K
-bundle isomorphism.

Suppose now that
\varphi
extends to
\widetilde{\varphi}:G\to K
. Then, we can trivialise the bundle
G\times_{\varphi}K
via the map
\iota:G/H\times K\to G\times_{\varphi}K,(gH,k)\mapsto[g,\widetilde{\varphi}(g)^{-1}k]
. This map is well-defined because

\iota(ghH,k)=[gh,\widetilde{\varphi}(gh)^{-1}k]=[gh,\varphi(h)^{-1}\widetilde{\varphi}(g)^{-1}k]=[g,\widetilde{\varphi}^{-1}k]=(gH,k)=\iota(gH,k)\,.

The inverse of
\iota
is given by

\iota^{-1}[g,k]=(gH,\widetilde{\varphi}(g)k)
, which is well-defined for the same reason:

\iota^{-1}[gh,k]=(ghH,\widetilde{\varphi}(gh)k)=(gH,\widetilde{\varphi}(gh)k)=\iota^{-1}[g,\phi(h)k]\,.

It is clear that
\iota
and
\iota^{-1}
are bundle morphisms. However, note that they are not
G
-equivariant.

Conversely, if
P
is a trivial bundle, it is straightforward to show that
\varphi
extends to
G
.

For the last assertion, suppose
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\colon H\to K
are Lie group homomorphisms, and suppose that there exists a
G
-equivariant
K
-bundle isomorphism
f\colon G\times_{\varphi_{1}}K\to G\times_{\varphi_{2}}K
. Then, for each
g\in G
and
k\in K
,

f([g,k])=f(g\cdot[e_{G},e_{K}]\cdot k)=g\cdot f([e_{G},e_{K}])\cdot k\,,

and so
f
is determined by
f([e_{G},e_{K}])
. As
f
is a bundle homomorphism,
f([e_{G},e_{K}])
lies over
o
, and hence there exists
t\in K
such that
f([e_{G},e_{K}])=[e_{G},t]
. Now, for every
g\in G
,
k\in K
and
h\in H
, we have that

\displaystyle[g,tk]

\displaystyle=g\cdot[e_{G},t]\cdot k=g\cdot f([e_{G},e_{K}])\cdot k=f(g\cdot[e_{G},e_{K}]\cdot k)

\displaystyle=f([g,k])=f([gh,\varphi_{1}(h)^{-1}k])=[gh,t\varphi_{1}(h)^{-1}k]=[g,\varphi_{2}(h)t\varphi_{1}(h)^{-1}k]\,.

Therefore, for each
h\in H
,
\varphi_{2}(h)=t\varphi_{1}(h)t^{-1}
.
∎

Example 3.3
.

If
G/H
is an
n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian homogeneous space, the smooth action of
G
on
G/H
induces a smooth action of
G
on the positively oriented orthonormal frame bundle of
G/H
by
\SO(n)
-bundle homomorphisms, covering the
G
-action on
G/H
. The Lie group homomorphism
\varphi\colon H\to\SO(n)
given by Lemma
3.2
is precisely the isotropy representation.

We can generalise Lemma
3.2
:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G/H
be a homogeneous space. Let
K,L
be Lie groups. Let
\lambda\colon K\to L
and
\eta\colon H\to L
be Lie group homomorphisms, with
\lambda
surjective. Let
P
be a
G
-equivariant lift of the structure group of
G\times_{\eta}L
to
K
along
\lambda
. Then, there exists a Lie group homomorphism
\tilde{\eta}\colon H\to K
such that
P
is
G
-equivariantly bundle-isomorphic to
G\times_{\tilde{\eta}}K
covering the identity on
G\times_{\eta}L
, and
\eta=\lambda\circ\tilde{\eta}
. Moreover,
\tilde{\eta}
is unique up to conjugation by elements of
\ker(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon P\to G_{\times_{\eta}}L
be a
G
-equivariant reduction of the structure group along
\lambda
. As
\lambda
is surjective, there exists
p_{0}\in P
such that
f(p_{0})=[e_{G},e_{L}]
. Applying the same argument we applied in the proof of Lemma
3.2
with this choice of
p_{0}
, one obtains a Lie group homomorphism
\widetilde{\eta}\colon H\to K
such that
P
is
G
-equivariantly bundle-isomorphic to
G\times_{\tilde{\eta}}K
, and we can take this isomorphism to send
p_{0}
to
[e_{G},e_{K}]
. Hence, we have a
G
-equivariant reduction of the structure group

\displaystyle\begin{split}G\times_{\widetilde{\eta}}K&\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}P\to G\times_{\eta}L\\
[g,k]&\mapsto gp_{0}k\mapsto[g,\lambda(k)]\,.\end{split}

One readily checks that this map is well-defined if, and only if,
\lambda\circ\widetilde{\eta}=\eta
.

Now, suppose that
\widetilde{\eta}_{1},\widetilde{\eta}_{2}
are such that

\lambda\circ\widetilde{\eta}_{1}=\lambda\circ\widetilde{\eta}_{2}=\eta\,.

Suppose further that there exists a
G
-equivariant
K
-bundle isomorphism
f
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times_{\widetilde{\eta}_{1}}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times_{\widetilde{\eta}_{2}}K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\times_{\eta}L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

commutes. Then, there exists
t\in\ker(\lambda)
such that

f\left(\left[e_{G},e_{K}\right]\right)=\left[e_{G},t\right]\,.

Hence, for every
h\in H
,

\left[e_{G},t\right]=f\left(\left[e_{G},e_{K}\right]\right)=f\left(\left[h,\widetilde{\eta}_{1}(h)^{-1}\right]\right)=\left[h,t\widetilde{\eta}_{1}(h)^{-1}\right]=\left[e_{G},\widetilde{\eta}_{2}(h)t\widetilde{\eta}_{1}(h)^{-1}\right]\,.

This shows that

\widetilde{\eta}_{2}=t\widetilde{\eta}_{2}t^{-1}\,.

Conversely, if
\widetilde{\eta}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\eta}_{2}
are conjugate by an element
t\in\ker(\lambda)
, we can define
f
by

f\left(\left[g,k\right]\right)=\left[g,tk\right]\,.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

We recover Lemma
3.2
taking
L
to be the trivial one-element Lie group.

Example 3.6
.

Taking
r=1
,
\lambda=\lambda_{n}\colon\Spin(n)\to\SO(n)
and
\eta=\sigma
the isotropy representation in Lemma
3.4
, we recover
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
.

Finally, we obtain our main result:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G/H
be an
n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian homogeneous space with
H
connected and isotropy representation
\sigma\colon H\to\SO(n)
. Then, there is a bijective correspondence between

•

G
-invariant spin
r
structures on
G/H
modulo
G
-equivariant equivalence of spin
r
structures, and

•

Lie group homomorphisms
\varphi\colon H\to\SO(r)
such that
\sigma\times\varphi\colon H\to\SO(n)\times\SO(r)
lifts to
\Spin^{r}(n)
along
\lambda_{n}^{r}
modulo conjugation by an element of
\SO(r)
.

Proof.

A
G
-invariant spin
r
structure is, by definition, a
G
-equivariant reduction of the structure group of the positively oriented orthonormal frame bundle
G\times_{\sigma}\SO(n)
. Hence, by Lemma
3.4
,
G
-invariant spin
r
structures on
G/H
modulo
G
-equivariant equivalence of spin
r
structures are in bijection with Lie group homomorphisms
\widetilde{\sigma}\colon H\to\Spin^{r}(n)
such that
\lambda_{n}^{r}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}=\sigma
modulo conjugation by elements of
\ker(\lambda_{n}^{r})
. But, as
H
is connected and
\lambda_{n}^{r}
is a covering map, these are in bijection with Lie group homomorphisms
\varphi\colon H\to\SO(r)
such that
\sigma\times\varphi
lifts to
\Spin^{r}(n)
modulo conjugation by elements of
\SO(r)
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

For the case
r=1
, as
\SO(1)
is trivial, we get two cases: either
\sigma
lifts to
\Spin(n)
or it does not. So we have either a unique such structure or none. This recovers the well-known result that, if a
G
-invariant spin structure exists, it is unique (see
e.g.

[
9
]
).

In the light of Theorem
3.7
, it is clear that, if
r<s
, a
G
-invariant spin
r
structure naturally induces a
G
-invariant spin
s
structure. So it makes sense to introduce a
G
-invariant analogue of the spin type of Definition
2.4
:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acting smoothly by orientation-preserving isometries on an oriented Riemannian manifold
M
. The
G
-invariant spin type of
M
, denoted by
\Sigma(M,G)
, is the least
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M
admits a
G
-invariant spin
r
structure.

For example, we know that the unique spin structure of
S^{n}
is not
G
-invariant for
G=\SO(n+1),\U((n+1)/2)

[
9
]
,
i.e.
,

\Sigma(S^{n},\SO(n+1)),\,\Sigma(S^{n},\U((n+1)/2))>1\,.

But there might exist a
G
-invariant spin
C
structure. Let us explore this in the next section.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G

, which exists, by Berger’s classification. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a lift of the holonomy representation to
\Spin^{r}(n+1)
.

(2)

S^{n}
has a

r

Introduction and main results

In this article, we aim to establish the existence
and concentrating behavior of nontrivial solutions for the following
p
-Laplacian equations
with logarithmic nonlinearity of type

(1.1)

-\varepsilon^{p}\Delta_{p}u+V(x)|u|^{p-2}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u\log|u|^{p}\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

under the constraint

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx=a^{p}\varepsilon^{N},

where
a,\varepsilon>0
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is known as the Lagrange multiplier,
\Delta_{p}\cdot=\text{div}(|\nabla\cdot|^{p-2}\nabla\cdot)

denotes the usual

p
-Laplacian operator with
2\leq p<N
and
V\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the potential
which satisfies

(V_{1})

V\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
-\infty<V_{0}=\inf\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)<V_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}V(x)<+\infty
;

(V_{2})

V^{-1}(\{V_{0}\})=\{x^{1},x^{2},\ldots,x^{l}\}
with
x^{1}=0
and
x^{i}\neq x^{j}
if
i\neq j

for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\cdots,l\}
.

In the case
p=2
, Eq. (
1.1
) also comes from the study of solitary waves for the following nonlinear
Schrödinger equation

(1.3)

\text{i}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\Delta\psi-V(x)\psi+h(|\psi|^{2})\psi=0\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

where i is the imaginary unit,
\psi:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,

V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is the potential,

h(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i\theta}}z)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}h(z)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is not difficult to see that any solution
\psi
of Eq. (
1.3
) with the Cauchy initial
function
\psi(0,x)
preserves the
L^{2}
-mass, namely

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\psi(0,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\penalty\ \forall t\in(0,\infty).

Actually, Eq. (
1.3
) is usually adopted in the study of nonlinear optics and Bose-Einstein condensates,
where
\psi
describes the state of the condensate and the
L^{2}
-mass is the total number of atoms,
see e.g.
[
31
,
51
,
60
]
. One significant motivation associated with Eq. (
1.3
) is the search for its standing wave solutions.
The standing wave is a solution of the form
\psi(t,x)=e^{-\text{i}\lambda t}u(x)
,
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a time-independent function that satisfies
the nonlinear elliptic equation

(1.4)

-\Delta u+V(x)u=\lambda u+g(u)\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

with
g(u)=h(|u|^{2})u
.

Usually, there are two directions
to the study of standing waves of the Schrödinger equation
(
1.4
). On the one hand, one can choose the frequency
\lambda
to be fixed and look for the
existence of nontrivial solutions for it by investigating critical points of the variational functional

J_{\lambda}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+(V(x)-\lambda)|u|^{2}\right)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)dx,

where
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)ds
.
When
g(t)=t\log t^{2}
, we refer to the article
[
62
]

and its references therein to acquaint the
significant physical
applications in quantum mechanics, quantum optics, nuclear physics, transport and diffusion
phenomena, open quantum systems, effective quantum gravity, superfluidity theory
and Bose-Einstein condensation for Eq. (
1.4
).
Owing to the logarithmic type nonlinearity,
it presents some challenging mathematical problems. For instance, the
associated variational functional is not
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth
since one can find a function below

\left.u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(|x|^{N/2}\log(|x|))^{-1},&\quad|x|\geq 3,\\
0,&\quad|x|\leq 2,\end{array}\right.\right.

such that

, however it holds that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx=-\infty

The initial work to deal with this difficulty is due to Cazenave in
[
16
]

where the author considered the following
logarithmic Schrödinger equation

\text{i}u_{t}+\Delta u+u\log u^{2}=0,\penalty\ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

in the space
W\triangleq\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u^{2}\log u^{2}|dx<\infty\}

with a suitable Luxemburg norm. Speaking it more clearly, by introducing the
N
-function

(1.5)

\left.A(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-s^{2}\log s^{2},&\quad 0\leq s\leq e^{-3},\\
3s^{2}+4e^{-3}s-e^{-6},&\quad s\geq e^{-3},\end{array}\right.\right.

\|u\|_{A}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\},

and
(\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\cdot\|_{A})
as the Luxemburg norm,
then the author obtained the existence of infinitely many critical
points for the variational functional

\mathcal{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx,\penalty\ u\in W,

on the set
\Sigma\triangleq\{u\in W:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}dx=1\}
.
Very recently, Alves and his collaborators

It should be stressed here that Theorem

In order to avoid this obstacle, we continue to make use of the decomposition in (

Whereas, the biggest challenge in the proof of Theorem

Alternatively, we successfully put forward a totally different argument to look for a minimizer of

Finally, we begin paying attention to the case

which leads to the Problem (

and prove the following result.

Theorem 1.6

Suppose that

and

Given a

To get around this obstacle,

Let us introduce it step by step.
For every

In this section, we would like to recommend some preliminary results.
First of all, let us introduce
some fundamental concepts and properties concerning the Orlicz
spaces. For the more details, please refer to
[
45

for example.

Definition 2.1
.

An
N
-function is a continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty)
that satisfies the following
conditions:

(i)

\Phi
is a convex and even function;

(ii)

\Phi(t)=0\Longleftrightarrow t=0
;

(iii)

\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}=0
and
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}=+\infty
.

We say that an
N
-function
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, denoted by
\Phi\in(\Delta_{2})
, if

\Phi(2t)\leq k\Phi(t),\penalty\ \forall t\geq t_{0},

for some constants
k>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
.

The conjugate function
\tilde{\Phi}
associated with
\Phi
is obtained through the Legendre’s transformation, defined as

\tilde{\Phi}(s)=\max_{t\geq 0}\{st-\Phi(t)\},\penalty\ \text{for}\>s\geq 0.

It can be shown that that
\tilde{\Phi}
is also an
N
-function. The functions
\Phi
and
\tilde{\Phi}
are mutually complementary that is,

\tilde{\tilde{\Phi}}=\Phi
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define the Orlicz space associated with the
N
-function
\Phi
as follows

L^{\Phi}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx<+\infty,\penalty\ \text{for some}\>\lambda>0\right\},

which is a Banach space endowed with the Luxemburg norm given by

\|u\|_{\Phi}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dx\leq 1\right\}.

Associated with the Orlicz Spaces, there also holds the Hölder and Young type inequalities, namely

st\leq\Phi(t)+\tilde{\Phi}(s),\quad\forall s,t\geq 0

and

\left|\int_{\Omega}uvdx\right|\leq 2\|u\|_{\Phi}\|v\|_{\tilde{\Phi}},\mathrm{\penalty\ for\penalty\ }\forall u\in L^{\Phi}(\Omega)\mathrm{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\forall v\in L^{\bar{\Phi}}(\Omega).

The space
L^{\Phi}(\Omega)
is reflexive and separable provided that

\Phi,\tilde{\Phi}\in(\Delta_{2})
. Moreover, the
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies that

L^{\Phi}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\Phi(|u|)dx<+\infty\right\}

and

u_{n}\rightarrow u\mathrm{\penalty\ in\penalty\ }L^{\Phi}(\Omega)\Longleftrightarrow\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(|u_{n}-u|\right)dx\rightarrow 0.

We then recall an significant relation involving
N
-functions that will be adopted later. Let
\Phi
be
an
N
-function of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
class and
\tilde{\Phi}
is its conjugate function. Suppose that

(2.1)

1<l\leq\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(t)t}{\Phi(t)}\leq m,\>t\neq 0,

then
\Phi,\tilde{\Phi}\in(\Delta_{2})
.
Finally, we consider the functions

\xi_{0}(t)=\min\{t^{l},t^{m}\}\mathrm{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\xi_{1}(t)=\max\{t^{l},t^{m}\},\>t\geq 0,

it is possible to verify that, using (
2.1
), the function
\Phi
satisfies the inequality below

(2.2)

\xi_{0}\left(\|u\|_{\Phi}\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi(u)\leq\xi_{1}\left(\|u\|_{\Phi}\right),\>\forall u\in L^{\Phi}(\Omega).

Inspired by
[
8
,
3
,
4
]
, we define the functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
as follows

\left.F_{1}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}F_{1}(-s),&s\leq 0,\\
-\frac{1}{p}s^{p}\log s^{p},&0<s<(p-1)\delta,\\
-\frac{1}{p}s^{p}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p\delta s^{p-1}-\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p},&s\geq(p-1)\delta,\end{array}\right.\right.

and

\left.F_{2}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&|s|\leq(p-1)\delta,\\
\frac{1}{p}|s|^{p}\log\big(|s|^{p}/((p-1)\delta)^{p}\big)+p\delta|s|^{p-1}-\frac{p+1}{p}|s|^{p}-\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p},&|s|\geq(p-1)\delta,\end{array}\right.\right.

where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small but fixed,
then we reach the decomposition (
1.9
).
Moreover,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
satisfy the following properties:

(\textbf{P}_{1})

F_{1}
is even with
F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s\geq 0
and
F_{1}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore,

is convex if
\delta\approx 0^{+}
;

(\textbf{P}_{2})

F_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}((\delta,+\infty),\mathbb{R})
and for each

(\textbf{P}_{4})

\operatorname*{lim}\limits_{s\to\infty}\frac{F_{2}^{\prime}(s)}{s^{p-1}}=\infty

Lemma 2.2
.

The function
F_{1}
is an
N

,
it holds that
F_{1},\tilde{F}_{1}\in(\Delta_{2})

Proof.

Exploiting some elementary calculations, one could easily certify that
F_{1}
satisfies (I)-(III)
of Definition
2.1
. To arrive at the proof, we shall verify that
F_{1}
satisfies a similar relation in (
2.1
)
and so it reveals that
F_{1},\tilde{F}_{1}\in(\Delta_{2})
. Firstly, we see that

\left.F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}-(1+\log s^{p})s^{p-1},&0<s<(p-1)\delta,\\
-s^{p-1}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p(p-1)\delta s^{p-2},&s\geq(p-1)\delta.\end{array}\right.\right.

Next, we shall analyze the cases
0<s<(p-1)\delta
and
s\geq(p-1)\delta
separately.

Case 1.

0<s<(p-1)\delta
.

In this case, it is simple to calculate that

\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}=p+\frac{1}{\log s},

which indicates that there is an

such that

1<l_{1}\leq\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}\leq m_{1}\triangleq\sup_{0<s<\delta}\left(p+\frac{1}{\log s}\right)\leq p,

for some sufficiently small
\delta>0
.

Case 2.

s\geq(p-1)\delta
.

In this case, we continue to calculate that

\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}=\frac{-s^{p}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p(p-1)\delta s^{p-1}}{-\frac{1}{p}s^{p}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p\delta s^{p-1}-\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}}

From which, we derive that
\sup\limits_{s\geq(p-1)\delta}\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}\leq p

since for all
s\geq(p-1)\delta
, there holds

\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}\leq\frac{-s^{p}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p(p-1)\delta s^{p-1}+\big[p\delta s^{p-1}-\frac{1}{p-1}\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}\big]}{-\frac{1}{p}s^{p}\big[\log\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}+p+1\big]+p\delta s^{p-1}-\frac{1}{p(p-1)}\big((p-1)\delta\big)^{p}}.

Obviously, one can deduce that

\operatorname*{lim}_{s\to+\infty}\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}=p\mathrm{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}>p-1,\penalty\ \forall s>0,

and so we obtain

p-1<\inf_{s>0}\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}.

The last inequality together with
p>2
guarantees the existence of an

such that

1<l\leq\frac{F_{1}^{\prime}(s)s}{F_{1}(s)}\leq p,\>\forall s>0.

Since
F_{1}
is an even function, then the inequality holds true for any

The proof is completed.

Next, we shall introduce the Pohoz̆aev identity

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

are constants and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx=\frac{N}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx+\mu N\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx.

for all

u(x)>0
for all

Proof.

The proof is divided into the following three steps.

Step 1.

u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\tau\in(0,1)

We start by assuming that
u\geq 0
. For all

Taking

with

as a test function in (

(2.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(u_{L}^{kN+1})dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[F_{2}^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime}_{1}(u)+(\lambda-1)|u|^{p-2}u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u]u_{L}^{kp+1}dx.

It is easy to observe that

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla(u_{L}^{kp+1})dx=\frac{kp+1}{(k+1)^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla(u_{L})^{k+1}|^{p}dx,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}uu_{L}^{kp+1}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(u_{L})^{k+1}|^{p}dx.\end{array}\right.

Adopting property-
(\textbf{P}_{1})
and property-
(\textbf{P}_{2})
with
\tilde{q}=q
, there holds

(2.7)

F_{2}^{\prime}(u)u-F^{\prime}_{1}(u)u+\lambda|u|^{p}+\mu|u|^{q}\leq(C_{q}+|\lambda|+|\mu|)|u|^{q}\triangleq C_{q,\lambda,\mu}|u|^{q}\penalty\ \text{if}\penalty\ |u|\geq 1.

Without loss of generality, we shall suppose that
|u|\geq 1
.
Combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (

3.
The semiclassical problem

In this section, we shall contemplate the existence and concentration behavior

Nevertheless, first of all, let us consider

\left.\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u+\mu|u|^{p-2}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u+|u|^{p-2}u\log|u|^{p},\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx=a^{p},\end{array}\right.\right.

In general, to solve Problem (

As a consequence, we are derived from Section

\displaystyle I_{\mu}^{\prime}(u)v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla v+(\mu+1)|u|^{p-2}v\right]dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)vdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F_{2}^{\prime}(u)vdx,\forall v\in X.

Next, we will prove the following result for Problem (

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

. Then, there is a constant

Lemma 3.2

Let

Proof.

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(u)

Lemma 3.3

Lemma 3.4

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
2\leq p<N
and
a>a_{*}
. Assume that

\left(\int_{|x|\geq R}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p^{*}\vartheta}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}}\leq\tilde{C}_{p,a}\vartheta^{p}\left(\int_{|x|\geq R-r}|\bar{v}_{\varepsilon}^{j}|^{\frac{p\vartheta t}{t-1}}dx\right)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}.

Setting
\chi=\frac{p^{*}(t-1)}{pt}
and
s=\frac{pt}{t-1}
,
we are derived from the above inequality that

|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|_{\chi^{m+1}{s(|x|\geqslant R)}}\leqslant\tilde{C}_{p,a}^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi^{-i}}\chi^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}i\chi^{-i}}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|_{p^{*}(|x|\geqslant R-r)}

and so

|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|_{L^{\infty}(|x|\geqslant R)}\leqslant\tilde{C}_{p,a}^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\chi^{-i}}\chi^{\sum_{i=1}^{m}i\chi^{-i}}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|_{p^{*}(|x|\geqslant R-r)}.

Since
\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\to\bar{v}^{j}
in
X
, the last inequality completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3.

Let
(\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon},\lambda^{j}_{\varepsilon})\in X\times\mathbb{R}

be a couple of weak solution to the Problem
(
3.14
), then there are
C_{0}^{j},c_{0}^{j}>0
such that

\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\leq C^{j}_{0}\exp(-c^{j}_{0}|x|)

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Since we have derived that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}\to\lambda^{j}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}

and
\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
we apply Lemma A.2 to deduce that

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}+|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\log|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}}{|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}}=-\infty\penalty\ \text{uniformly in}\penalty\ \varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*}).

So, there is an
R>0
which is independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
such that

\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}+|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\log|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\leq\frac{V_{0}-2}{2}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1},\penalty\ \forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ |x|\geq R.

Denoting the constant
\hat{V}_{0}=V_{0}+2\in[1,+\infty)
, then for all
|x|\geq R
, there holds

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\hat{V}_{0}}{2}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}+|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\log|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}-\left[V(\varepsilon x+\varepsilon x_{\varepsilon}^{j})-\frac{V_{0}+2}{2}\right]|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}+|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\log|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}-\frac{V_{0}-2}{2}|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Let
\psi^{j}(x)=C_{0}^{j}\exp(-c_{0}^{j}|x|)

with
C_{0}^{j},c_{0}^{j}>0
such that
(c^{j}_{0})^{p}(p-1)<\frac{\hat{V}}{2}

and
\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq C_{0}^{j}\exp(-c_{0}^{j}R)
for all
|x|=R
.
It follows from some simple calculations that

-\Delta_{p}\psi^{j}+\frac{\hat{V}}{2}(\psi^{j})^{p-1}=(\psi^{j})^{p-1}\left(\frac{\hat{V}}{2}-(c^{j}_{0})^{P}(p-1)+\frac{N-1}{|x|}(c^{j}_{0})^{p-1}\right)>0,\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ |x|\geq R.

Define
\Sigma=\{|x|\geq R\}\cap\{\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}>\psi^{j}\}
, adopting the following inequality

(|x|^{s-2}x-|y|^{s-2}y)\cdot(x-y)\geq 0\text{ for all }s>1\text{ and }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

and choosing
\phi=\max\{\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\psi^{j},0\}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R})\cap X

as a test function in

-\Delta_{p}(\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\psi^{j})+\frac{\hat{V}}{2}\big[(\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon})^{p-1}-(\psi^{j})^{p-1}\big]\leq 0,\penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ |x|\geq R

to conclude that

0\geq\int_{\Sigma}(|\nabla\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}-|\nabla\psi^{j}|^{p-2}\psi)\nabla\phi dx+\frac{\hat{V}}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\left[(\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon})^{p-1}-(\psi^{j})^{p-1})\right]\phi dx\\
\geq 0.

Therefore, the set
\Sigma\equiv\emptyset
. From which, we know that
\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\leq\psi^{j}(x)
for all
|x|\geq R
and

\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}\leq\psi^{j}(x)=C^{j}_{0}\exp(-c^{j}_{0}|x|)\text{ for all }|x|\geq R.

Exploiting Lemma A.2 again,
|\bar{v}^{j}_{\varepsilon}|_{\infty}\leq C
and so
the above inequality holds true for the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by increasing
C_{0}^{j}
to be
large. The proof is completed.
∎

Invariant integrals on coideals and their Drinfeld doubles
K. De Commer and J. R. Dzokou Talla

Abstract
. Let A A be a CQG Hopf ∗ * -algebra, i.e. a Hopf ∗ * -algebra with a positive invariant state. Given a unital right coideal ∗ * -subalgebra B B of A A , we provide conditions for the existence of a relatively invariant integral on the stabilizer coideal B ⟂ B^{\perp} inside the dual discrete multiplier Hopf ∗ * -algebra of A A . Given such a relatively invariant integral, we show how it can be extended to a relatively invariant integral on the Drinfeld double coideal. We moreover show that the representation theory of the Drinfeld double coideal has a monoidal structure. As an application, we determine the relatively invariant integral for the coideal ∗ * -algebra 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) ) \mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})) constructed from the Podleś spheres.

Introduction

Let
(A,\Delta)
be a CQG (compact quantum group) Hopf
*
-algebra
[
DK94
]
, to be interpreted as the algebra
A=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
of regular functions (=matrix coefficient functions) on a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
. Let
B\subseteq A
be a unital right coideal
*
-subalgebra, so we interpret
B=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{X})
as an algebra of functions on a compact quantum space
\mathbb{X}
with transitive right action of
\mathbb{G}
. As we have an embedding
B\subseteq A
, we can say that
\mathbb{X}
has a classical point
\bullet
induced by the counit, so that we can view
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{H}
the stabilizer of
\bullet
. In general,
\mathbb{H}
will not exist as an actual quantum subgroup, i.e. we do not have a Hopf
*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
with surjective Hopf
*
-algebra morphism
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
. However,
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
still exists as a special type of coalgebra.

Under the above assumptions,
A
is cosemisimple, i.e. as a coalgebra it is simply a direct sum of finite-dimensional coalgebras dual to finite-dimensional matrix algebras. Hence dual to
A
one has the
*
-algebra
\mathcal{U}=\widehat{A}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{G}]
consisting of functionals vanishing on all but a finite number of the above coalgebras. It can according to the context be viewed as a convolution algebra of
\mathbb{G}
or as the algebra of continuous functions of compact support on the discrete quantum group
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
which is Pontryagin-dual to
\mathbb{G}
. When
A
is infinite-dimensional,
\mathcal{U}
will not be a Hopf
*
-algebra but a
multiplier Hopf
*
-algebra
, or more precisely an
algebraic quantum group

[
VD94
,
VD96
,
VD98
]
. Because of its concrete nature however as a direct sum of matrix algebras, this structure can also be dealt with in a more ad hoc way. Dual to the coideal
B=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
is a left coideal
\mathcal{I}=B^{\perp}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{H}]
inside the
multiplier
algebra of
\mathcal{U}
, which we call the
stabilizer coideal
.

Recently, there has been a lot of activity in determining properties of coideals in the context of invariant integrals
[
Tom07
,
FS09
,
KK17
,
Kas18
,
Chi18
,
CKS20
]
. For example, a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an invariant integral on
\mathcal{I}
turns out to be that the coideal
B
(or its stabilizer
\mathcal{I}
) is invariant under the antipode squared. Unfortunately, this is not always the case in all examples of interest. An important situation where this condition is not satisfied is for the
Podleś spheres
at generic parameter. We will show however that the situation can be amended by considering
relatively
invariant integrals (cf.
[
Kas18
]
for similar notions). We then show that a
tracial
relatively invariant integral on
\mathcal{I}
exists when
B\subseteq A
forms a
co-Gelfand pair
, meaning that the associated unital
*
-algebra of spherical functions is commutative.

We then consider also the Drinfeld double of a CQG coideal, which gives an analytic version of the purely algebraic construction presented in
[
DCDz21
]
. This construction is important, as it gives an answer to the longstanding open problem on how to quantize real semisimple Lie groups. Indeed, it can be motivated that if
L
is a real semisimple Lie group, realized as the real form of a simply connected, connected complex Lie group
G
, then a quantization of its convolution algebra
C_{c}(L)
can be obtained as the Drinfeld double of the CQG coideal
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U/K)
of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U)
, where
U
is the maximal compact subgroup of
G
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U/K)
is the quantum homogeneous space associated to the Letzter-Kolb coideal
*
-subalgebra
[
Let99
,
Let02
,
Kol14
,
DCNTY19
,
DCM20
]
for the symmetric pair
K=L\cap U\subseteq U
. Note that this Drinfeld double coideal is
a priori
only a coideal, so its category of representations would seem to lack a monoidal structure. As we demonstrate however, there
is
a monoidal structure present, although its construction is not straightforward. In this paper, we do not provide the details of the above construction in general, but as a guiding example we explain the constructions in the case of quantum
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. For further motivation, we refer to
[
DCDz21
]
.

The contents of this paper are as follows. In the
first section
, we recall the main preliminaries on quantum groups, and prove equivalent conditions for a coideal associated to a discrete quantum group to admit a relatively invariant integral. In the
second section
, we introduce the Drinfeld double of a CQG coideal and its stabilizer dual, and show that this Drinfeld dual coideal admits as well a relatively invariant integral and associated regular representation when the original coideal does. We further show how the representation theory of the Drinfeld double coideal admits a monoidal structure. In the
third section
, we apply our results to the case of quantum
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, constructed as the quantum double of a Podleś sphere and its stabilizer coideal.

1.
Invariant integrals on coideals

1.1.
CQG Hopf
*
-algebras and their duals

Let
(A,\Delta)
be a CQG (compact quantum group) Hopf
*
-algebra, so
A
is generated as an algebra by the matrix coefficients of its unitary corepresentations
[
DK94
]
. Equivalently,
A
is a Hopf
*
-algebra with a (necessarily unique) invariant state
\Phi_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, so

(\id\otimes\Phi_{A})\Delta(a)=\Phi_{A}(a)1=(\Phi_{A}\otimes\id)\Delta(a),\qquad\forall a\in A,

\Phi_{A}(1)=1,\qquad\Phi_{A}(a^{*}a)\geq 0,\qquad\forall a\in A.

One can interpret
A=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
with
\mathbb{G}
a compact quantum group. We denote by
\varepsilon
the counit of
A
, and by
S_{A}
its antipode. We further use the (unsummed) Sweedler notation
\Delta(a)=a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
.

We refer to
[
NT14
, Chapter 1]
for all information on CQG Hopf
*
-algebras we will need. We recall the essentials in what follows. First of all,
A
is cosemisimple as a coalgebra, and we choose a maximal family of mutually inequivalent irreducible (right) comodules
(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha},\delta_{\alpha})
. We can assume moreover that these comodules are unitary, so the
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and, using the usual Sweedler notation
\delta_{\alpha}(v)=v_{(0)}\otimes v_{(1)}
, we have

\langle v_{(0)},w_{(0)}\rangle v_{(1)}^{*}w_{(1)}=\langle v,w\rangle 1,\qquad\forall v,w\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}.

Let
\mathscr{U}=\Lin(A,\mathbb{C})
be the vector space dual of
A
, which is a unital
*
-algebra under the convolution product and
*
-structure given by

(\omega\chi)(a)=(\omega\otimes\chi)\Delta(a),\qquad\omega^{*}(a)=\overline{\omega(S(a)^{*})},\qquad a\in A,\omega,\chi\in\mathscr{U}.

We view
\mathscr{U}=\Lin(A,\mathbb{C})
as a topological space with the topology of pointwise convergence. It is sometimes convenient to use the abstract notation

\tau:A\times\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad(a,\omega)\mapsto\tau(a,\omega)=\omega(a).

for the natural pairing between
\mathscr{U}
and
A
.

Any finite-dimensional unitary right
A
-comodule
(\mathcal{H},\delta)
leads to a unital
*
-representation of
\mathscr{U}
on
\mathcal{H}
via

\pi(x)v=\tau(v_{(1)},x)v_{(0)}.

We call these
\mathscr{U}
-representations
admissible
. If conversely
\pi
is an admissible
*
-representation of
\mathscr{U}
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, there exists a unique
\pi_{\xi,\eta}=\pi(\xi,\eta)\in A
such that

\tau(\pi(\xi,\eta),x)=\langle\xi,\pi(x)\eta\rangle,\qquad x\in\mathscr{U}.

We refer to
\pi(\xi,\eta)
as a
matrix coefficient of
\pi
. We then also interpret

\pi\in B(\mathcal{H})\otimes A,\qquad(\id\otimes\tau(-,h))\pi=\pi(h),\qquad h\in\mathscr{U}.

We choose a maximal family
(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\alpha})
of mutually inequivalent irreducible admissible unital
*
-representations of
\mathscr{U}
, and we write
\Irr_{\adm}(\mathscr{U})=\{\alpha\}
for the index set. We can concretely identify
\mathscr{U}
with a direct product
*
-algebra

\mathscr{U}\cong\Prod_{\alpha\in\Irr_{\adm}(\mathscr{U})}B(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}),\qquad\pi_{\alpha}:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}),\quad\omega\mapsto(v\mapsto\pi_{\alpha}(\omega)v=\tau(v_{(1)},\omega)v_{(0)}).

(1.1)

The topology of
\mathscr{U}
then coincides with the product topology on
\Prod_{\alpha}\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
. In the following, we will switch between these two pictures whenever convenient.

We write

\mathscr{U}\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{U}=\Prod_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})\otimes\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha^{\prime}})\cong\Lin(A\otimes A,\mathbb{C})

(1.2)

for the topologically completed tensor product. Then the product on
A
dualizes to a unital
*
-homomorphism

\Delta:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{U}.

This coproduct allows to take the tensor product of admissible representations.

There further exists a unique
\delta_{A}\in\mathscr{U}
such that, for each
\alpha\in\Irr_{\adm}(\mathscr{U})
, the operator
\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
is positive and invertible with
\Tr(\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})^{1/2})=\Tr(\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})^{-1/2})
and, writing this number as
\dim_{q}(\alpha)
, one has the following Peter-Weyl orthogonality relations between matrix coefficients of irreducible admissible
*
-representations of
\mathscr{U}
:

\Phi_{A}(\pi_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\pi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})^{*})=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle\langle\eta_{2},\delta_{A}^{-1/2}\eta_{1}\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\alpha)},

(1.3)

\Phi_{A}(\pi_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})^{*}\pi_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1}))=\delta_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{\langle\xi_{1},\delta_{A}^{1/2}\xi_{2}\rangle\langle\eta_{2},\eta_{1}\rangle}{\dim_{q}(\alpha)}.

(1.4)

In general, we can make sense of
\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})^{z}
for any complex
z
by positivity of
\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})
, and there exists then a unique
\delta_{A}^{z}\in\mathscr{U}
so that
\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A}^{z})=\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A})^{z}
for all
\alpha\in\Irr_{\adm}(\mathscr{U})
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

It follows immediately from the orthogonality relations that
\Phi_{A}
is faithful, i.e. both maps
A\rightarrow\mathscr{U}
given by

a\mapsto\Phi_{A}(a-),\qquad a\mapsto\Phi_{A}(-a)

(1.5)

are injective. Moreover, they have the same range which we will write
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
. Then
\mathcal{U}
is a (non-unital)
*
-subalgebra of
\mathscr{U}
, equal under the identification (
1.1
) to
\oplus_{\alpha}\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
. In particular,
\mathcal{U}
is an
A
-bimodule by

\tau(b,x\lhd a)=\tau(ab,x),\qquad\tau(b,a\rhd x)=\tau(ba,x),\qquad x\in\mathcal{U},a,b\in A.

The linear map

\sigma_{A}:A\rightarrow A,\qquad\sigma_{A}(\pi_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))=\pi_{\alpha}(\delta_{A}^{-1/2}\xi,\delta_{A}^{-1/2}\eta)

(1.6)

satisfies
\Phi_{A}(ab)=\Phi_{A}(b\sigma_{A}(a))
for all
a,b\in A
, and hence is an automorphism of
A
called the
modular automorphism
. We can then identify

\delta_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\quad a\mapsto\varepsilon(\sigma_{A}^{-1}(a)),

(1.7)

so that it is clear that
\delta_{A}
is a character on
A
, and in particular
S_{A}(\delta_{A})=\delta_{A}^{-1}
. We further have that the antipode squared acts as follows on matrix coefficients:

S_{A}^{2}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\delta_{A}^{-1/2}\xi,\delta_{A}^{1/2}\eta).

(1.8)

Note now that the antipode
S_{A}
of
A
dualizes to an anti-isomorphism of algebras
S:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}
satisfying
S(x)^{*}=S^{-1}(x^{*})
for
x\in\mathscr{U}
. One then has

S^{2}(x)=\delta_{A}^{-1/2}x\delta_{A}^{1/2},\qquad\forall x\in\mathscr{U}.

(1.9)

This allows one to modify
S
as to become
*
-preserving and involutive: we define the
unitary antipode
as

R:\mathscr{U}\rightarrow\mathscr{U},\qquad x\mapsto\delta_{A}^{1/4}S(x)\delta_{A}^{-1/4}.

Then for all
x\in\mathscr{U}
one has

R(x)^{*}=R(x^{*}),\qquad R^{2}(x)=x,\qquad S(x)=\delta_{A}^{-1/4}R(x)\delta_{A}^{1/4},\qquad S^{-1}(x)=\delta_{A}^{1/4}R(x)\delta_{A}^{-1/4}.

(1.10)

This allows to give a direct formula for the
dual

\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\pi}}
of an admissible unitary
\mathscr{U}
-representation
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
as being the usual dual Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{*}
with the
*
-representation

\overline{\pi}(x)\xi^{*}=(R(x)^{*}\xi)^{*}.

(1.11)

It follows that we can write

\pi(\xi,\eta)^{*}=\pi((\delta_{A}^{1/4}\xi)^{*},(\delta_{A}^{-1/4}\eta)^{*})=\pi(\delta_{A}^{-1/4}\xi^{*},\delta_{A}^{1/4}\eta^{*}).

(1.12)

To end, note that
\sigma_{A}
and
S_{A}^{2}
are part of one-parameter groups of automorphisms called respectively the
modular
and
scaling
automorphisms groups:

(\sigma_{A})_{z}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\delta_{A}^{i\overline{z}/2}\xi,\delta_{A}^{-iz/2}\eta),\qquad(\tau_{A})_{z}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\delta_{A}^{i\overline{z}/2}\xi,\delta_{A}^{iz/2}\eta),

so that
\sigma_{A}^{n}=(\sigma_{A})_{-in}
and
S_{A}^{2n}=(\tau_{A})_{-in}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We then also write
R_{A}:A\rightarrow A
for involutive the anti-
*
-(co)algebra isomorphism

R_{A}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\eta^{*},\xi^{*}),\qquad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H},\qquad S_{A}=R_{A}\circ(\tau_{A})_{-i/2}=(\tau_{A})_{-i/2}\circ R_{A}.

Remark 1.1
.

In practice, for example in the setting of quantized enveloping algebras, the Hopf
*
-algebra
A
is obtained through the unitary pairing with another Hopf
*
-algebra
U
,

\tau:A\otimes U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

This pairing is called
non-degenerate
if it induces inclusions
A\subseteq\Lin(U,\mathbb{C})
and
U\subseteq\Lin(A,\mathbb{C})
. One then calls a
*
-representation of
U
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
admissible
if it is implemented by a unitary corepresentation of
A
through
\tau
. The non-degeneracy condition provides a dense embedding
U\subseteq\mathscr{U}
, compatible with the coproduct.

1.2.
Coideals of CQG Hopf
*
-algebras and their stabilizer duals

Let
B\subseteq A
be a (unital) right coideal
*
-subalgebra, so
B
is a unital
*
-subalgebra of
A
with

\Delta(B)\subseteq B\otimes A.

As mentioned in the introduction, for
A=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})
we interpret
B=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
. With
B_{+}=B\cap\Ker(\varepsilon)
, we can then define

C=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})=A/AB_{+}.

By
[
Tak79
, Proposition 1]
,
C
has a unique structure of a left
A
-module coalgebra such that the projection map
\pi_{C}:A\mapsto C
is a left
A
-module coalgebra map.

Write
1_{C}=\pi_{C}(1_{A})
for the canonical grouplike element in
C
. By
[
Chi18
, Theorem 3.1]
(see also, in light of Remark
1.1
, the closely related
[
MS99
, Theorem 2.2]
), we have that
C
is cosemisimple, and we can obtain
B
back from
C
via

B=\{b\in A\mid\pi_{C}(b_{(1)})\otimes b_{(2)}=1_{C}\otimes b\}.

(1.13)

The quotient coalgebra
C
moreover inherits an anti-linear involution via

\pi_{C}(a)^{{\dagger}}:=\pi_{C}(S(a)^{*}),

using e.g. that
S(AB_{+})^{*}=AB_{+}
via an observation in
[
Ko93
]
(see also
[
MS99
, Lemma 1.4]
). Writing
\mathscr{I}=\Lin(C,\mathbb{C})
, we obtain through
\pi_{C}
an inclusion of unital
*
-algebras

\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathscr{U},

where
\mathscr{I}
is provided with the convolution algebra structure and the
*
-structure
\omega^{*}(c)=\overline{\omega(c^{{\dagger}})}
.

Definition 1.2
.

We call a
*
-representation of
\mathscr{I}
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
admissible
if it arises as a subrepresentation of an admissible
\mathscr{U}
-representation.

We can again choose a maximal set of irreducible admissible
\mathscr{I}
-representations, and write them
(\mathcal{G}_{\beta},\pi_{\beta})
for
\beta
in some indexing set
\Irr_{\adm}(\mathscr{I})
. We have

\mathscr{I}=\Prod_{\beta}\End(\mathcal{G}_{\beta}).

(1.14)

The coproduct of
\mathscr{U}
restricts to a unital
*
-homomorphism

\Delta:\mathscr{I}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{I}=\prod_{\alpha,\beta}\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})\otimes\End(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})\cong\Lin(A\otimes C,\mathbb{C}),

dual to the left
A
-module structure on
C
. This allows to take, within the class of admissible
*
-representations of
\mathscr{I}
, the tensor product of an admissible
\mathscr{U}
-representation with an admissible
\mathscr{I}
-representation.

Let now
\Phi_{C}\in\mathscr{I}
be the self-adjoint support projection of the admissible
*
-representation

\varepsilon_{\mid\mathscr{I}}:\mathscr{I}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\omega\mapsto\omega(1_{C}),

so interpreted as an element in
\mathscr{U}
we have that
(\Phi_{C})_{\alpha}\in\End(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})
is the orthogonal projection onto the
\mathscr{I}
-fixed vectors of
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
, i.e. the vectors
\xi
satisfying
h\xi=\varepsilon(h)\xi
for all
h\in\mathscr{I}
. By abuse of notation, we interpret
\Phi_{C}
both as a linear map on
A
and on
C
,

\Phi_{C}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\Phi_{C}:C\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

We have the invariance property

(\Phi_{C}\otimes\id)\Delta_{C}(c)=\Phi_{C}(c)1_{C}=(\id\otimes\Phi_{C})\Delta_{C}(c),\qquad\forall c\in C.

(1.15)

From (
1.13
) and (
1.15
), we obtain the surjective right
B
-linear projection map

E_{B}:A\rightarrow B,\quad a\mapsto(\Phi_{C}\otimes\id)\Delta(a),

(1.16)

so
\Phi_{C}=\varepsilon\circ E_{B}
. In particular,
B
is generated as a vector space by the
\pi(\xi,\eta)
with
\Phi_{C}\xi=\xi
. Using (
1.12
) and the fact that
B
is
*
-invariant, we find that

\Phi_{C}S_{A}(\Phi_{C})=S_{A}(\Phi_{C}),\qquad\Phi_{C}S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C})=\Phi_{C},\\
S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C})\Phi_{C}=S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C}),\qquad S_{A}(\Phi_{C})\Phi_{C}=\Phi_{C},

(1.17)

where the second identity follows from the first by applying
S_{A}^{-1}
, and where the third and fourth identities follow from the first two by applying the
*
-operation. In particular, we obtain also a map

F_{B}:A\rightarrow B,\quad a\mapsto(S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C})\otimes\id)\Delta(a).

(1.18)

A direct calculation using again (
1.12
) shows that

F_{B}(a)=E_{B}(a^{*})^{*},\qquad a\in A,

(1.19)

and in particular
F_{B}
is left
B
-linear.

Let us record the following observation (see also
[
LVD07
, Proposition 1.6]
).

Lemma 1.3
.

Inside
\mathscr{U}\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{U}
, the following identities hold:

\Delta(\Phi_{C})(1\otimes\Phi_{C})=\Phi_{C}\otimes\Phi_{C}=(S_{A}(\Phi_{C})\otimes 1)\Delta(\Phi_{C})=\Delta(\Phi_{C})(S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C})\otimes 1).

(1.20)

Proof.

The identity
\Delta(\Phi_{C})(1\otimes\Phi_{C})=\Phi_{C}\otimes\Phi_{C}
is immediate upon realizing that
\Delta(\Phi_{C})\in\mathscr{U}\widehat{\otimes}\mathscr{I}
by the left coideal property of
\mathscr{I}
, and
\Phi_{C}x=x\Phi_{C}=\varepsilon(x)\Phi_{C}
for
x\in\mathscr{I}
by construction of
\Phi_{C}
.

On the other hand, for
a,b\in A
we have

\pi_{C}(b_{(1)})\Phi_{C}(ab_{(2)})=S_{A}(a_{(1)})\pi_{C}((a_{(2)}b)_{(1)})\Phi_{C}((a_{(2)}b)_{(2)})=\pi_{C}(S_{A}(a_{(1)}))\Phi_{C}(a_{(2)}b).

(1.21)

Applying
\Phi_{C}
, we see that

\Phi_{C}(b)\Phi_{C}(a)=\Phi_{C}(b_{(1)})\Phi_{C}(a\pi_{C}(b_{(2)}))=\Phi_{C}(b_{(1)})\Phi_{C}(ab_{(2)})=\Phi_{C}(S_{A}(a_{(1)}))\Phi_{C}(a_{(2)}b).

As this holds for all
a,b\in A
, we find
(S_{A}(\Phi_{C})\otimes 1)\Delta(\Phi_{C})=\Phi_{C}\otimes\Phi_{C}
. Applying the
*
-operation, we obtain
\Delta(\Phi_{C})(S_{A}^{-1}(\Phi_{C})\otimes 1)=\Phi_{C}\otimes\Phi_{C}
.
∎

Recall now from
[
BDRV06
, Proposition 2.10]
that
B
allows a (unique) modular automorphism

\sigma_{B}:B\rightarrow B,\qquad\Phi_{A}(bc)=\Phi_{A}(c\sigma_{B}(b)),\qquad b,c\in B.

Note that
\sigma_{B}
is in general different from
(\sigma_{A})_{\mid B}
. Indeed, the equality
\sigma_{B}=(\sigma_{A})_{\mid B}
only holds if
B
is invariant under
\sigma_{A}
, which is not always the case.

Lemma 1.4
.

The modular automorphism
\sigma_{B}
of
B
is given by
\sigma_{B}(b)=F_{B}(\sigma_{A}(b))
for
b\in B
, so

\sigma_{B}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\delta_{A}^{-1/4}R(\Phi_{C})\delta_{A}^{-1/4}\xi,\delta_{A}^{-1/2}\eta),\qquad\xi\in\Phi_{C}\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

(1.22)

Its inverse
\sigma_{B}^{-1}
is given by
\sigma_{B}^{-1}(b)=E_{B}(\sigma_{A}^{-1}(b))
for
b\in B
, so

\sigma_{B}^{-1}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\Phi_{C}\delta_{A}^{1/2}\xi,\delta_{A}^{1/2}\eta),\qquad\xi\in\Phi_{C}\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

(1.23)

Proof.

Since

Definition 1.5

Lemma 1.6

Proof.

Consider the intertwining map

Assume

Definition 1.11

Let

be a character on

The following is the main theorem of this section.

Let

•

•

\mathcal{I}
if and only if
\Phi_{C}\delta_{A}^{1/2}\Phi_{C}=\Phi_{C}
, and in this case
\psi_{\mathcal{I}}
is tracial.

A particular case to which the above corollary applies is the following.

Definition 1.19
.

We say that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
is a
Gelfand pair
if
\Phi_{C}\mathscr{U}\Phi_{C}
is commutative. We say that
B\subseteq A
is a
co-Gelfand pair
if the unital
*
-algebra of
*
-spherical functions

\Xi=\{\pi(\Phi_{C}\xi,R(\Phi_{C})\eta)\mid\pi\textrm{ admissible},\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\}.

is commutative.

Note that by the formula for the
*
-structure on
A
together with (
1.17
), we have indeed that
\Xi
is a
*
-algebra, which can also be written as

\Xi=\{\pi(\Phi_{C}\xi,\delta_{A}^{1/4}\Phi_{C}\eta)\mid\pi\textrm{ admissible},\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\}.

Hence we have shifted the algebra of spherical functions as defined in (say)
[
Let03
, Section 4]
with the character

to make it
*
-invariant. This coincides with the definition in other sources such as
[

, Chapter 11]
, where one looks for functions invariant with respect to a (2-sided) coideal in

interacting with the

-structure in a different way then by

-invariance (and the shift with

is implemented there then already on the

Corollary 1.20
.

If
B\subseteq A
is a co-Gelfand pair, then
\mathcal{I}
admits a
\delta_{A}^{-1/2}
-invariant trace.

Proof.

We are to show that
\delta_{B}^{1/2}=\Phi_{C}
. However, it follows immediately from the orthogonality relations (

) and (

) that
(\Phi_{A})_{\mid\Xi}
admits (in general) the modular automorphism

\sigma_{\Xi}(\pi(\xi,\eta))=\pi(\delta_{B}^{-1/2}\xi,R(\delta_{B}^{1/2})\eta).

Since by assumption

∎

2.
Invariant functionals on the Drinfeld double coideal

2.1.
Drinfeld double coideals

Let
A
be a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra with dual
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
. We can construct a
*
-algebra
\mathcal{D}(A,\mathscr{U})
generated by
A
and
\mathscr{U}
with commutation relations given by

xa=a_{(2)}(S_{A}^{-1}(a_{(1)})\rhd x\lhd a_{(3)}),\qquad x\in\mathscr{U},a\in A.

Inside, we have the
Drinfeld double
\mathcal{D}(A,\mathcal{U})
of
A
, spanned by elements of the form
ax
for
a\in A
and
x\in\mathcal{U}
. The multiplication maps

A\otimes\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(A,\mathcal{U}),\qquad\mathcal{U}\otimes A\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(A,\mathcal{U})

are then linear isomorphisms. One can make
\mathcal{D}(A,\mathcal{U})
into a
multiplier Hopf
*
-algebra with (positive) invariant integrals

[
VD98
,
DrVD01
,
DeVD04
]
containing, suitably interpreted,
(A,\Delta)
and
(\mathcal{U},\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})
as copies. As we will not explicitly need this extra structure, we refrain from spelling out the details.

Assume now that
B\subseteq A
is a coideal
*
-subalgebra
B
, and let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
be the stabilizer dual. Then
\mathcal{D}(A,\mathscr{U})
contains a subalgebra
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathscr{I})
generated by
B
and
\mathscr{I}
. The ‘orthogonality’ between
B
and
\mathscr{I}
gives that the commutation relations simplify to

yb=b_{(1)}(y\lhd b_{(2)}),\qquad by=(y\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(2)}))b_{(1)},\qquad b\in B,y\in\mathscr{I}.

The
*
-subalgebra
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
spanned by elements of the form
yb
with
y\in\mathcal{I}
and
b\in B
is called the
quantum double coideal
of
B
, and one can easily check that also in this case the multiplication maps

B\otimes\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}),\qquad\mathcal{I}\otimes B\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})

are linear isomorphisms. The
*
-algebra
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
can be considered as a right coideal
*
-subalgebra in (the multiplier
*
-algebra of)
\mathcal{D}(A,\mathcal{U})
, but again we will not have any need to spell this out explicitly. Note however that
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is naturally an
\mathscr{U}
-
A
-bimodule via

x\rhd(yb)=y(x\rhd b),\qquad(yb)\lhd a=(y\lhd a)b,\qquad x\in\mathscr{U},a\in A,y\in\mathcal{I},b\in B,

where
x\rhd b=(\id\otimes\tau(-,x))\Delta(b)
.

We observe the following easy lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Any
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
on a pre-Hilbert space is bounded.

Proof.

If
(V,\pi)
is a
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
, then it is in particular an
\mathcal{I}
-representation. Since
\mathcal{I}
is a direct sum of matrix algebras, it follows that this is necessarily a bounded
*
-representation. From the commutation relations it follows immediately that
\mathcal{I}\mathcal{H}
carries a
*
-representation
\pi
of
B
such that
\pi(b)\pi(x)=\pi(bx)
for all
b\in B,x\in\mathcal{I}
. It is now sufficient to observe that any
*
-representation of
B
on a pre-Hilbert space is bounded, which follows from the fact that, for
e_{i}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
,

\sum_{i}\pi_{\alpha}(\xi,\delta_{A}^{1/4}e_{i})^{*}\pi_{\alpha}(\xi,\delta_{A}^{1/4}e_{i})=\|\delta_{A}^{1/4}\xi\|^{2}1_{A},\qquad\xi\in\Phi_{C}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha},

cf.
[
Boc95
]
.
∎

We further keep all notation as in the previous section. Assume that
g\in\mathscr{U}
is a positive grouplike element, and that
\Phi_{C}
is
g
-balanced. Let
\psi_{\mathcal{I}}=\sum_{\beta}\Tr(\mu_{\beta}-)
be the associated
g
-invariant positive functional on
\mathcal{I}
, with associated one-parametergroup
\kappa_{z}(x)=\mu^{z}x\mu^{-z}
for
x\in\mathscr{I}
. We also write again
\kappa=\kappa_{-i}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The functional

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad xb\mapsto\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x)\Phi_{A}(b),\qquad b\in B,x\in\mathcal{I}

is a positive functional on
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
. Moreover,
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
is modular with modular automorphism given by

\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}(yb)=\kappa(y)\sigma_{B}(g^{-1}\rhd b),\qquad y\in\mathcal{I},b\in B.

(2.1)

The functional
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
is
g
-invariant in the sense that

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(x\rhd(yb)\lhd a)=\varepsilon(x)\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(yb)\tau(a,g).

Modularity of
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
means that

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(xy)=\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(y\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}(x)),\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}).

Proof.

We easily compute for
x,y\in\mathcal{I}
and
b,c\in B
that

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}((cy)^{*}bx)=\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(y^{*}(x\lhd(S_{A}^{-1}(c^{*}b)_{(2)}))(c^{*}b)_{(1)})=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(y^{*}x)\Phi_{A}(c^{*}b),

(2.2)

from which positivity of
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
follows.

The modularity of
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
with respect to the proposed modular automorphism follows from the following computations for
x,y,z\in\mathcal{I}
and
b,c,d\in B
:

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(yxb)=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(yx)\Phi_{A}(b)=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x\kappa(y))\Phi_{A}(b),

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(xbz)=\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(x(z\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(2)}))b_{(1)})=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x(z\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(2)})))\Phi_{A}(b_{(1)})=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(xz)\Phi_{A}(b),

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(cxb)=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(c_{(2)}))\Phi_{A}(c_{(1)}b)=\tau(S_{A}^{-1}(c_{(2)}),g)\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x)\Phi_{A}(c_{(1)}b)=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x)\Phi_{A}(b\sigma_{B}(g^{-1}\rhd c)),

\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(xbd)=\psi_{\mathcal{I}}(x)\Phi_{A}(bd).

The
g
-invariance of
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
is immediate.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If
\Phi_{C}
is
\delta_{A}^{-1/2}
-balanced, then
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
is a positive
\delta_{A}^{-1/2}
-invariant trace.

Proof.

We only need to check that
\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}
is tracial. But since
\psi_{\mathcal{I}}
is tracial in this case,
\kappa=\id
. Moreover,
\sigma_{B}(\delta_{A}^{1/2}\rhd b))=b
for
b\in B
since
\delta_{B}=1
. The result follows.
∎

Let now
L^{2}(B)
be the GNS-space for
B
with respect to
\Phi_{A}
, so
L^{2}(B)
is the completion of
B
with respect to the inner product
\langle b,c\rangle=\Phi_{A}(b^{*}c)
. Write
\Lambda_{B}:B\rightarrow L^{2}(B)
for the GNS map. Similarly, let
L^{2}(\mathcal{I})
be the GNS-space for
\mathcal{I}
with respect to
\psi_{\mathcal{I}}
, with GNS-map
\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

There exists a unique non-degenerate
*
-representation

\pi_{\mathrm{reg}}:\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})\rightarrow B(L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{I}))

such that for
b,c\in B
and
x,y\in\mathcal{I}

\begin{split}\pi_{\mathrm{reg}}(c)(\Lambda_{B}(b)\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}(x))&=\Lambda_{B}(cb)\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}(x),\\
\pi_{\mathrm{reg}}(y)(\Lambda_{B}(b)\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}(x))&=\Lambda_{B}(b_{(1)})\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}((y\lhd b_{(2)})x).\end{split}

(2.3)

Proof.

Consider the map

\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}}:\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})\rightarrow L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{I}),\qquad bx\mapsto\Lambda_{B}(b)\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}(x).

Then (
2.2
) shows
\langle\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x),\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(y)\rangle=\varphi_{\mathcal{D}}(x^{*}y)
. It follows immediately that we can define a non-degenerate
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
on
\Lambda_{B}(B)\otimes\Lambda_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathcal{I})
such that
x\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(y)=\Lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(xy)
. This extends to a bounded
*
-representation on
L^{2}(B)\otimes L^{2}(\mathcal{I})
by Lemma
2.1
. An easy computation shows that the formulas (
2.3
) hold.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

We call
\pi_{\mathrm{reg}}
the
regular
*
-representation
of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
(with respect to
g
).

2.2.
Monoidal structure for
\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,I))
and categorical interpretation

Let
A
be a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra with dual
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
. Let
B\subseteq A
be a right coideal
*
-subalgebra with stabilizer dual
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathscr{I}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
. The goal of this section is to look for a natural tensor product for
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
. We thank A. Brochier, D. Jordan and D. Nikshych for discussions on this subject.

We motivate first the existence of a monoidal structure from purely algebraic considerations. Assume in fact that
A
is a general bialgebra, and
B\subseteq A
a right coideal subalgebra. Write
C=A/AB_{+}
for the associated left
A
-module coalgebra with coproduct
\Delta_{C}
and projection map

\pi_{C}:A\rightarrow C,\qquad a\mapsto[a],

where we use the bracket-notation also for more general quotient maps in what follows.

We can then consider the categories

{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C},\qquad{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C},\qquad{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}

(2.4)

of (bi)modules relative to the comodule structure(s). For example,
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
consists of
Doi-Koppinen modules

[
Doi92
,
Kop95
]
, which are left
B
-modules
M
with a right
C
-comodule
\delta_{M}
structure such that

\delta_{M}(bm)=\Delta(b)\delta_{M}(m),\qquad b\in B,m\in M.

One calls
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
the category of
A
-relative
C
-bicomodules, and
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}
the category of
A
-relative
B
-bimodules.

There is a natural adjoint functor pair

F_{B}:{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}\rightarrow{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C},\quad V\mapsto{}^{\bullet}_{\bullet}A^{\bullet}\underset{B}{\otimes}V^{\bullet}\quad\dashv\quad G_{B}:{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}\rightarrow{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C},\quad M\mapsto{}^{\coinv_{C}}\!\!\!{}_{\bullet}M^{\bullet},

(2.5)

where the bullets indicate the natural places where the (co)algebras (co)act. The unit/counit adjunction maps are given by

\varepsilon_{M}^{B}:A\underset{B}{\otimes}\left({}^{\coinv_{C}}M\right)\rightarrow M,\quad a\otimes m\mapsto am,\qquad\eta_{V}^{B}:V\rightarrow{}^{\coinv_{C}}(A\underset{B}{\otimes}V),\quad v\mapsto 1\otimes v.

(2.6)

Similarly, with
\overset{C}{\square}
denoting the cotensor product, there is an adjoint functor pair

F_{C}:{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}\rightarrow{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C},\quad M\mapsto M/MB_{+}\quad\dashv\quad G_{C}:{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}\rightarrow{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A},\quad V\mapsto{}_{\bullet}V\overset{C}{\square}{}_{\bullet}A_{\bullet}^{\bullet},

(2.7)

with unit/counit adjunction maps

\varepsilon_{V}^{C}:(V\overset{C}{\square}A)/(V\overset{C}{\square}A)B_{+}\rightarrow V\quad[\sum_{i}v_{i}\otimes a_{i}]\mapsto\sum_{i}\varepsilon(a_{i})v_{i},\\
\eta_{M}^{C}:M\rightarrow(M/MB_{+})\overset{C}{\square}A,\quad m\mapsto[m_{(0)}]\otimes m_{(1)}.

(2.8)

Moreover,
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
and
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}
are monoidal through respectively the cotensor product
\overset{C}{\square}
with the diagonal module structure and
\underset{B}{\otimes}
with the diagonal comodule structure, and the composite adjunction functors
F_{B}\circ F_{C}
and
G_{C}\circ G_{B}
are resp. oplax/lax monoidal through the maps

A\underset{B}{\otimes}\left(M\underset{B}{\otimes}N/(M\underset{B}{\otimes}N)B_{+}\right)\rightarrow(A\underset{B}{\otimes}M/MB_{+})\overset{C}{\square}(A\underset{B}{\otimes}N/NB_{+}),\\
a\otimes[m\otimes n]\rightarrow(a_{(1)}\otimes[m_{(0)}])\otimes(a_{(2)}m_{(1)}\otimes[n]),

(2.9)

(({}^{\coinv_{C}}M)\overset{C}{\square}A)\underset{B}{\otimes}({}^{\coinv_{C}}N)\overset{C}{\square}A)\rightarrow{}^{\coinv_{C}}(M\overset{C}{\square}N)\overset{C}{\square}A,\\
(\sum_{i}m_{i}\otimes a_{i})\otimes(\sum_{j}n_{j}\otimes a_{j}^{\prime})\mapsto\sum_{j}(\sum_{i}m_{i}\otimes a_{i(1)}n_{j})\otimes a_{i(2)}a_{j}^{\prime}.

(2.10)

Assume now that
A
is a Hopf algebra, with
{}_{B}A
faithfully flat and
A^{C}
faithfully coflat. Then it follows from
[
Tak79
, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2]
that the above adjoint pairs are equivalences,

{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}\cong{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}\cong{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}.

(2.11)

Since (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are easily checked to correspond to each other under the adjunction, it then follows automatically that the ensuing equivalence between
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}
and
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
is in fact a strong monoidal equivalence.

Assume now again that
A
is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra and
B
a right coideal
*
-subalgebra. Then indeed
{}_{B}A
is faithfully flat
[
Chi18
, Corollary 3.5]
and
A^{C}
is faithfully coflat (as
C
is cosemisimple). There is then a one-to-one correspondence between Doi-Kopinnen modules and non-degenerate
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-modules through the correspondence

{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}\ni V\mapsto V\in{}_{\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})}\mathscr{M},\qquad(b\omega)v=\tau(v_{(-1)},\omega)bv_{(0)},\qquad\omega\in\mathcal{I},b\in B,v\in V.

It follows that the category of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-modules obtains a uniquely defined monoidal structure through either its identification with
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}
or
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
.

Consider now however the C
∗
-category
\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
of non-degenerate
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
on Hilbert spaces. We have a C
∗
-equivalence of this category with
\Rep_{0}(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
, the C
∗
-category of non-degenerate
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-modules with a pre-Hilbert space structure making the module structure into a
*
-representation. Indeed, one easily checks that

\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))\rightarrow\Rep_{0}(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})),\qquad\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{I}\mathcal{H}

does the job, the quasi-inverse being given by completing a pre-Hilbert space into a Hilbert space (by Lemma
2.1
, we can extend the
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
to this completion).

Hence, to obtain a monoidal structure on
\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
, we only need to see how the Hilbert space structure can be transported via the equivalences in (
2.11
). Unfortunately, this seems not as straightforward as one would hope, and one quickly runs into trouble with either questions concerning which C
∗
-completions to use, or how to invoke the appropriate modular structure to preserve the compatibility with the
*
-operation. In what follows, we will for this reason abandon the
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}_{B}^{A}
-picture, and focus on the
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
-side, considered however from a dual point of view.

Consider the von Neumann algebras
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
and
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}=\mathscr{I}\cap\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
of uniformly bounded elements in the direct products (
1.1
) and (

). We then obtain normal maps

\Delta:\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathscr{U}_{\infty},\qquad\Delta:\mathscr{I}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathscr{I}_{\infty},

where
\overline{\otimes}
denotes the von Neumann algebraic tensor product (which just corresponds to bounded sequences in the algebraic completed product (

)). Hence
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
can be seen as a left coideal von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A right

-Hilbert
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
-bimodule
(\mathscr{E},\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}})
will be called
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}

\Delta:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathscr{E}

(2.12)

where the latter
W^{*}
-valued inner product is determined by
\langle x\otimes\xi,y\otimes\eta\rangle_{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}=x^{*}y\otimes\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}
.

Note that the equivariance conditions are meaningful as
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
is a left coideal von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
. Whenever convenient, we continue to use the Sweedler notation

\Delta(\xi)=\xi_{[-1]}\otimes\xi_{[0]}.

We denote by
{}^{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}
the C
∗
-category of
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
-relative right W
∗
-Hilbert
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
-bimodules (with the obvious choice for morphisms). Note also for future reference that a right W
∗
-Hilbert
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
-module
\mathcal{E}
can always be written in the form
\mathcal{E}\cong\Prod_{\beta}^{b}B(\mathcal{G}_{\beta},V_{\beta})
for Hilbert spaces
V_{\beta}

\langle(x_{\beta})_{\beta},(y_{\beta})_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}=(x_{\beta}^{*}y_{\beta})_{\beta}\in\mathscr{I}_{\infty}=\Prod_{\beta}^{b}B(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
-relative right

-Hilbert
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}
-bimodule, we can equip it with a left

a\blacktriangleright\xi=\tau(S_{A}^{-1}(a),\xi_{[-1]})\xi_{[0]},

which is well-defined since

is a normal functional on
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
for

. Similarly, we equip
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
with the left and right

\tau(c,a\blacktriangleright x)=\tau(S_{A}^{-1}(a)c,x),\qquad\tau(c,x\blacktriangleleft a)=\tau(cS_{A}^{-1}(a),x),\qquad x\in\mathscr{U}_{\infty},a,c\in A.

Recall further the map
S_{B}^{2}
introduced in (

).

Theorem 2.7
.

There is an equivalence of C
∗
-categories

\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))\cong{}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}^{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}.

More precisely, let
\mathcal{E}\in{}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}^{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}
, and consider
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{E}\Phi_{C}
with the scalar product

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\tau(1_{C},\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}),\qquad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.13)

Then
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space with a non-degenerate
*

\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
through

\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(b)\xi=S_{A}^{2}(S_{B}^{-2}(b))\blacktriangleright\xi,\qquad\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(x)\xi=x\xi,\qquad b\in B,x\in\mathcal{I},

and the association

becomes an equivalence of tensor C
∗

F_{\infty}:{}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}^{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}\rightarrow\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})).

A quasi-inverse functor is given by

G_{\infty}:\Rep(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))\rightarrow{}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}}^{\mathscr{U}_{\infty}}\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{I}_{\infty}},\qquad\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\square}\mathcal{H},

\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\square}\mathcal{H}=\{z\in\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{H}\mid\forall b\in B,(1\otimes\pi_{\mathcal{H}}(b))z=z(\blacktriangleleft b\otimes\id)\}

with the

-relative
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}

and with the Hilbert
\mathscr{I}_{\infty}

-valued one on

(i.e. the

).

The adjoint equivalence between

and

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{E}\Phi_{C},x\in\mathscr{U}_{\infty}
. However, it is not hard to show that the
\Delta(\xi)(x\otimes\Phi_{C})
are
\sigma
-weakly closed in
\mathscr{U}_{\infty}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{H}
, leading to
z=0
, a contradiction.
∎

It is further straightforward to check that the maps
\epsilon_{\mathcal{H}},\eta_{\mathcal{E}}
in Proposition
2.14
and Proposition
2.15
indeed define an adjoint pairing between
F_{\infty}
and
G_{\infty}
, and hence turn
G_{\infty}
into a quasi-inverse for
F_{\infty}
, finishing the proof of Theorem
2.7
.

3.
Relatively invariant functionals on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{t})

Let
0<q<1
, and let
A=\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
be the universal Hopf algebra generated by
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
such that

\displaystyle\alpha\beta=q\beta\alpha,\quad\alpha\gamma=q\gamma\alpha,\quad\beta\gamma=\gamma\beta,\quad\beta\delta=q\delta\beta,\quad\gamma\delta=q\delta\gamma,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\alpha\delta-q\beta\gamma=1,\qquad\delta\alpha-q^{-1}\gamma\beta=1.

(3.1b)

It is well-known that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra by the
*
-structure

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{*}&\gamma^{*}\\
\beta^{*}&\delta^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta&-q^{-1}\beta\\
-q\gamma&\alpha\end{pmatrix}

and the coproduct making
U=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}
a corepresentation
[
Wor87a
,
Wor87b
]
. Moreover, it is non-degenerately paired with the Hopf
*
-algebra
U=U_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))
generated by elements
e,f,k^{\pm 1}
with universal relations

ke=q^{2}ek,\qquad kf=q^{-2}fk,\qquad ef-fe=\frac{k-k^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}

and Hopf
*
-algebra structure determined by

e^{*}=fk,\qquad f^{*}=k^{-1}e,\qquad k^{*}=k,

\Delta(e)=e\otimes 1+k\otimes e,\qquad\Delta(f)=f\otimes k^{-1}+1\otimes f,\qquad\Delta(k)=k\otimes k.

The admissible
*
-representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))
are labeled by half-integers. Concretely, we write
(V_{n/2},\pi_{n/2})
for the
n+1
-dimensional unitary representation of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))
, with orthonormal basis
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
and action

\pi_{n/2}(k)\xi_{p}=q^{n-2p}\xi_{p},

(q^{-1}-q)\pi_{n/2}(e)\xi_{p}=\sqrt{(q^{-n+p-1}-q^{n-p+1})(q^{-p}-q^{p})}q^{\frac{n}{2}-p+1}\xi_{p-1},

(q^{-1}-q)\pi_{n/2}(f)\xi_{p}=\sqrt{(q^{-n+p}-q^{n-p})(q^{-p-1}-q^{p+1})}q^{-\frac{n}{2}+p}\xi_{p+1}.

We denote by
U_{n/2}\in\End(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
the associated unitary corepresentation of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
, and for
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
by
U_{n/2}(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
its matrix coefficients.

Write
\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))=\Lin(\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2)))
for the dual convolution
*
-algebra. It is well-known that we can express the modular element
\delta_{A}\in\mathscr{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{su}(2))
for
\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
by

\delta_{A}^{1/2}=k.

(3.2)

Consider now for
t\in\mathbb{R}
the element

B_{t}=q^{-1/2}(e-fk)-i(q-q^{-1})^{-1}tk.

(3.3)

Then
B_{t}^{*}=-B_{t}
and

\Delta(B_{t})=q^{-1/2}(e-fk)\otimes 1+k\otimes B_{t},

so that
B_{t}
generates a
*
-invariant left coideal
I=U_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{t})
of
U
. The stabilizer dual
I^{\perp}=B=\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2})
can be identified with one of the
Podleś spheres

[
Pod87
]
. We recall from
[
Koo93
, Theorem 4.3]
(see also
[
DCDz21
, Theorem 2.1]
) that the spectrum of
\pi_{n/2}(iB_{t})
equals the set

\Spec(\pi_{n/2}(iB_{t}))=\left\{[a+n-2p]\mid p=0,1,\ldots,n\right\}

when
t=q^{a}-q^{-a}
and where
[x]=\frac{q^{x}-q^{-x}}{q-q^{-1}}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. It hence follows that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{t})
will be isomorphic to the
*
-algebra of functions with finite support on the set
\{[a+n]\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. We denote by
e_{[a+n]}
the Dirac function at
[a+n]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a
\delta_{A}^{-1/2}
-invariant integral
\psi_{t}:\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{t})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, concretely determined by

\psi_{t}(e_{[a+n]})=\frac{q^{a+n}+q^{-a-n}}{q^{a}+q^{-a}},\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

To see that a
\delta_{A}^{-1/2}
-invariant integral exists, it is by Theorem
1.12
(and the identity
k=\delta_{A}^{1/2}
) sufficient to verify that

\tau(b,k^{-1})=\varepsilon(\sigma_{A}\sigma_{B}^{-1}(b)),\qquad b\in\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2}).

(3.4)

Now
\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2})
is generated by its spin one spectral subspace, which is spanned by the
U_{1}(\xi^{(t;1)},\eta)
with
\xi^{(t;1)}=\begin{pmatrix}q^{-1/2}\\
(q+q^{-1})^{-1/2}it\\
q^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}
. Noting that the
V_{n/2}
are self-dual by the unitary transformation
u_{n}:\overline{\xi}_{p}\mapsto(-1)^{p}\xi_{n-p}
, it follows that
R(\Phi_{C})
is the orthogonal projection onto

u_{2}\mathbb{C}\overline{\xi^{(t;1)}}=\mathbb{C}\begin{pmatrix}q^{1/2}\\
(q+q^{-1})^{-1/2}it\\
q^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix},

and hence
\delta_{A}^{-1/4}R(\Phi_{C})\delta_{A}^{-1/4}
leaves
\xi^{(t;1)}
invariant. From (
1.22
) and (
1.7
) we then find that (
3.4
) holds.

Write now
\psi_{t}(e_{[a+n]})=\mu_{[a+n]}
. It then follows from (
1.33
) (with
c_{\beta}=1
) that

\mu_{[a+n]}=\langle\xi,k\xi\rangle

for
\xi
any normalized
[a+n]
-eigenvector of
iB_{t}
. Now it is easily computed that for
n\geq 0
we have in
V_{n/2}
the
[a+n]
-eigenvector

\xi_{n/2}^{+}=\sum_{p=0}^{n}(-iq^{-(a+n)})^{p}\sqrt{\frac{(q^{2n};q^{-2})_{p}}{(-1)^{p}(q^{-2};q^{-2})_{p}}}\xi_{p},

where we use the Pochhammer symbol
(x;q^{2})_{n}=(1-x)(1-q^{2}x)\ldots(1-q^{2n-2}x)
. By the
q
-binomial theorem, we compute the norm of
\xi_{n/2}^{+}
to be

\|\xi_{n/2}^{+}\|^{2}=(-q^{-2a};q^{-2})_{n}.

Another application of the
q
-binomial theorem then leads to

\mu_{[a+n]}=\frac{\langle\xi_{n/2}^{+},k\xi_{n/2}^{+}\rangle}{\|\xi_{n/2}^{+}\|^{2}}=\frac{q^{a+n}+q^{-a-n}}{q^{a}+q^{-a}}.

The proof for
n<0
is similar.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The previous theorem can also be proven by observing that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
is a co-Gelfand pair. In fact, this is true also in higher rank for any coideal obtained from quantum symmetric pair coideals, using
[
Let03
, Theorem 4.2]
(note that the construction there needs to be tweaked a little to obtain
*
-invariance, see
[
DCNTY19
, Appendix B]
). As we do not develop this result further in this paper, we refrain from spelling out further details.

By the theory developed in Section
2
, we can consider the Drinfeld double coideal
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2}),\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{t}))
. As motivated in
[
DCDz21
]
and in the second section of this paper (see also
[
Pus93
,
PW94
,
BR99
]
for similar motivations in the context of realifications of complex quantum groups), we can think of this
*
-algebra as a
q
-deformation of the convolution
*
-algebra of compact support continuous functions on (a conjugated version of)
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. To be in accordance with earlier notation, we will the write this
*
-algebra as

\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{t})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O}_{q}(S_{t}^{2}),\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{k}_{t})).

From Corollary
2.3
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Phi_{A}
be the Haar state on
\mathcal{O}_{q}(SU(2))
. Then

\varphi_{t}:\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{t})\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad e_{[a+n]}b\mapsto\frac{q^{a+n}+q^{-a-n}}{q^{a}+q^{-a}}\Phi(b)

k^{-1}

Let

be the GNS-space of

with respect to

*

.

We have thus obtained a non-trivial

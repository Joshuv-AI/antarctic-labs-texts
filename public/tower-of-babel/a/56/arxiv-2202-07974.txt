Generic transporters for the linear time dependent quantum Harmonic oscillator on ℝ {\mathbb{R}}
A. Maspero

Abstract
In this paper we consider the linear, time dependent quantum Harmonic Schrödinger equation i ∂ t u = 1 2 ( − ∂ x 2 + x 2 ) u + V ( t , x , D ) u {\rm i}\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2})u+V(t,x,D)u , x ∈ ℝ x\in{\mathbb{R}} , where V ⁡ ( t , x , D ) V(t,x,D) is classical pseudodifferential operator of order 0, selfadjoint, and 2 ​ π 2\pi periodic in time. We give sufficient conditions on the principal symbol of V ⁡ ( t , x , D ) V(t,x,D) ensuring the existence of solutions displaying infinite time growth of Sobolev norms. These conditions are generic in the Fréchet space of symbols. This shows that generic, classical pseudodifferential, 2 ​ π 2\pi -periodic perturbations provoke unstable dynamics. The proof builds on the results of [ 36 ] and it is based on pseudodifferential normal form and local energy decay estimates. These last are proved exploiting Mourre’s positive commutator theory.

Introduction and main result

In this paper we study the perturbed quantum harmonic oscillator on
{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle{\rm i}\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2})u+V(t,x,D)u,\hskip 10.00002ptx\in{\mathbb{R}}\ .

(1.1)

We shall always assume that
V(t,x,D)
is a bounded operator, selfadjoint and
2\pi
-periodic in time.
Our goal is to construct solutions exhibiting unstable behavior in the form of forward energy cascade.
Precisely, we shall exhibit solutions of (
1.1
) whose
{\mathcal{H}}^{r}
-Sobolev norms,
r>0
, grows unbounded in time:

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{r}=+\infty\ ;

(1.2)

here we denoted, for any
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\mathcal{H}}^{r}:=\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}})\colon\ \ \|u\|_{r}:=\|H_{0}^{r}\,u\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\},\hskip 20.00003ptH_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2})\ .

(1.3)

Note that, when
V=0
, the unperturbed evolution
e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}
preserves all norms
\|\cdot\|_{r}
for all times and no energy cascade occurs.
So the question is whether one can construct an operator
V
producing unbounded orbits.

To formalize this concept we shall say (following
[
36
]
) that
V(t,x,D)
is a
transporter

if (
1.1
) has at least one solution fulfilling (
1.2
) for some
r>0
.

In the last few years several transporters for (
1.1
) were constructed by Delort
[

For the moment, let us note that all these above are
examples
of transporters, and it is not clear neither how to determine if a given operator
V(t,x,D)
is a transporter nor what happens for generic operators.
More precisely, the following questions are open and, we believe, of great interest (not only for the linear theory, but also for applications to nonlinear systems):

(Q1)

Given an operator
V(t,x,D)
, can we identify sufficient conditions guaranteeing it to be a transporter?

(Q2)

Are transporters rare or common? In other words, how generic are transporters?

In this paper we answer both questions, at least in case
V(t,x,D)
belongs to the class of classical pseudodifferential operators of order 0.
We identify, for the first time, explicit, sufficient conditions on the principal symbol of
V(t,x,D)
which guarantee the operator to be a transporter.
We show that these conditions are fulfilled for
generic
symbols, meaning for a set which is open and dense in the Fréchet topology of symbols.

As a conclusion, we obtain that generic,
2\pi
-time periodic, classical pseudodifferential perturbations of order 0 produce unbounded orbits – a fact which is, in our opinion, somewhat surprising.

The conditions we identify on
V(t,x,D)
are actually very simple.
Let
v_{0}(t,x,\xi)
be its principal symbol.
Assume it to be a positively homogeneous function of degree 0 (see Definition
1.1
), and denote by

\langle v_{0}\rangle(x,\xi)
its
resonant average
with respect to the classical flow

\phi^{t}(x,\xi):=(x\cos t+\xi\sin t,-x\sin t+\xi\cos t)

(1.4)

of the harmonic oscillator
h_{0}(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+\xi^{2})
, i.e.

\langle v_{0}\rangle(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}v_{0}(t,\phi^{t}(x,\xi))\,{\rm d}t\ .

(1.5)

Our main Theorem
1.3
shows that, if the Poisson bracket between
\langle v_{0}\rangle

does not vanish identically outside the origin, i.e.

\{\langle v_{0}\rangle,h_{0}\}\not\equiv 0\ \ \ \ \mbox{ in }x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1\ ,

(1.6)

then
V(t,x,D)
is a transporter.
Here we use the convention that

\{f,g\}:=\partial_{\xi}f\cdot\partial_{x}g-\partial_{x}f\cdot\partial_{\xi}g\ .

(1.7)

The proof of this result, that we will describe at the end of the section, builds on the theory developed in

The dispersion is quantitatively described by local energy decay estimates, which in turn are proved exploiting Mourre’s theory of positive commutators.

1.1
Main result

We first define the class of symbols we use.

Definition 1.1
.

(i)
A function
f
is a symbol of order
\rho\in{\mathbb{R}}
if
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}})
and

\forall\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there exists
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
such that

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\,\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}f(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\ (1+x^{2}+\xi^{2})^{\rho-\frac{\beta+\alpha}{2}}\ .

We will write
f\in S^{\rho}_{{\,}}
.

(ii)
We shall say that
f
is a classical symbol of order 0,
f\in S^{0}_{\rm cl}
, if there exists
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2},{\mathbb{C}})
positively homogeneous of degree
0
, i.e.

f_{0}(\lambda x,\lambda\xi)=f_{0}(x,\xi)\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\lambda\geq 1,\ \ \ \forall\,x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1,

(1.8)

and
\mu<0

such that
f-f_{0}\in S^{\mu}
. We shall call
f_{0}
the principal symbol of
f
.

Remark 1.2
.

(i)
It is easy to see that
S^{0}_{\rm cl}\subset S^{0}
.

(ii)
With our numerology, the symbol
\frac{1}{2}({x^{2}+\xi^{2}})
of the harmonic oscillator
H_{0}
is of order 1, and not of order 2 as typically in the literature.

We shall also consider symbols depending periodically from time.
We will denote by
C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, the space of
C^{k}
maps
f\colon{\mathbb{T}}\ni t\mapsto f(t,\cdot)\in S^{\rho}
with finite seminorms

\wp^{k,\rho}_{j}(f):=\sum_{\alpha+\beta\leq j\atop 0\leq\ell\leq k}\ \ \sup_{x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}\atop t\in{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\,\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\ell}f(t,x,\xi)\right|}{\left(1+x^{2}+\xi^{2}\right)^{\rho-\frac{\beta+\alpha}{2}}}\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\ .

(1.9)

Such seminorms turn
C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho}_{\,})
into a Fréchet space, with distance

{\mathtt{d}}^{k,\rho}(f,g):=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\,\frac{\wp^{k,\rho}_{j}(f-g)}{1+\wp^{k,\rho}_{j}(f-g)},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f,g\in C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho}_{\,})\ .

(1.10)

Similarly we define the space
C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})
, which we endow with the seminorms and distance in (
1.9
), (
1.10
).
Finally we denote by

C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})
the subset of
C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{{\rm cl}})
of real valued symbols.

To a symbol
f\in C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho})
we associate the operator

F(t,x,D)
by standard Weyl quantization

\big(F(t,x,D)\psi\big)(x):=\Big({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(f(t,\cdot)\right)\psi\Big)(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{y,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(x-y)\xi}\,f\left(t,\frac{x+y}{2},\xi\right)\,\psi(y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}\xi\ .

We shall say that an operator
F
is a
pseudodifferential operator
of order
\rho
if
F={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(f\right)
for some
f\in S^{\rho}
and shall write
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\rho}
.
If the symbol
f\in C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho})
we shall write
F\in C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{\rho})
.

Our main result is the following one.

Theorem 1.3
.

Denote by

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}:=\Big\{v\in C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})\colon

\displaystyle\mbox{ the principal symbol }v_{0}\mbox{ fulfills }\{\langle v_{0}\rangle,\,h_{0}\}\not\equiv 0\mbox{ in }x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1\Big\}\ \ ,

(1.11)

where
\langle v_{0}\rangle
is the resonant average (
1.5
) of
v_{0}
and
h_{0}(x,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+\xi^{2})
.
Then:

(i)

For any
v\in\mathscr{V}
, the operator
V(t,x,D):={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right)
is a transporter for (
1.1
).
More precisely,
\forall r>0
there exist a
solution
\psi(t)\in{\mathcal{H}}^{r}
of (
1.1
)
and constants
C,T>0
such that

\|\psi(t)\|_{r}\geq C\langle t\rangle^{r},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall t>T.

(1.12)

(ii)

The set
\mathscr{V}
is generic in
C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})
; precisely it is open and dense with respect to the metric
{\mathtt{d}}^{0,0}
in (
1.10
).

Let us comment the result.

1.

The set
\mathscr{V}
is strictly contained in
C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})
. For example, any symbol constant in time does not belong to
\mathscr{V}
. Indeed if
v_{0}(x,\xi)
is time independent, then its resonant average
\langle v_{0}\rangle
commutes with
h_{0}
, as it is easily checked.

However, given a time independent, non constant symbol
v_{0}(x,\xi)\in S^{0}_{{\rm cl}}
, it is always possible to find
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that

\cos(nt)v_{0}(x,\xi)\in\mathscr{V}
, see Lemma
6.1
.

2.

The open property of item
(ii)
guarantees that transporters are stable under perturbations.
In particular if
v\in\mathscr{V}
, any sufficiently small perturbation of
v
still belongs to
\mathscr{V}
.

The density instead guarantees that given
any
symbol in
C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{\rm cl})
, it is always possible to perturb it so that the new symbol belongs to
\mathscr{V}
.

3.

The property of belonging to
\mathscr{V}
involves only the principal symbol. In particular if
v\in\mathscr{V}
, one can add arbitrarily large symbols in
C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho})
,
\rho<0
, and still be in
\mathscr{V}
.

4.

The growth of Sobolev norms of Theorem
1.3
is truly an energy cascade phenomenon; indeed the
L^{2}
-norm of any solution of
(
1.1
) is preserved for all times,
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}
,
\,\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. This is due to the fact that
H_{0}+V(t,x,D)
is selfadjoint.

5.

Estimate (
1.12
) is optimal, since it is proved in
[
13
]
(see also
[
34
]
) that
any
solution of (
1.1
) fulfills the upper bounds

\forall r>0\ \ \ \exists\,{\widetilde{C}}_{r}>0\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\|\psi(t)\|_{r}\leq{\widetilde{C}}_{r}\langle t\rangle^{r}\,\|\psi(0)\|_{r}.

6.

Energy cascade is a resonant phenomenon; here it happens because
V(t,x,D)
oscillates at frequency
\omega=1
which resonates with the spectral gaps of the harmonic oscillator.
In
[
4
]
we proved that if
V(\omega t,x,D)
is quasiperiodic in time with a Diophantine frequency vector
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then the Sobolev norms of the solutions grow at most as
\langle t\rangle^{\epsilon}
,
\forall\epsilon>0
(see
[
6
]
for recent results on
\langle t\rangle^{\epsilon}
growth and references therein).

Moreover, with additional restrictions on
\omega
(typically belonging to some Cantor set of large measure) and assuming
V(t,x,D)
to be small in size, then all solutions have uniformly in time bounded Sobolev norms
[
3
,
5
]
.
Therefore the stability/instability of the system depends strongly on the resonant properties of the frequency
\omega
.

Let us briefly describe the main ingredients of the proof.
The first step is to use resonant pseudodifferential normal form, analogous to the one of Delort
[
13
]
, to conjugate the original equation (
1.1
) to

{\rm i}\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\big({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T+R(t)\big){\varphi}

(1.13)

where
\langle v_{0}\rangle
is the resonant average (
1.5
) of the principal symbol of
V(t,x,D)
,
T
is a selfadjoint, time independent pseudodifferential operator of negative order, and
R(t)
is an arbitrary regularizing perturbation. This is done in Section
3
.

The second step is the analysis of the

effective
Hamiltonian

{\rm i}\partial_{t}\psi=\big({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T\big)\psi

(1.14)

obtained removing
R(t)
from (
1.13

We construct solutions of (
1.14
) exhibiting dispersion in the energy space, i.e. solutions
\psi(t)
whose negative
{\mathcal{H}}^{-r}
-Sobolev norm,

\|\psi(t)\|_{-r}\leq\frac{C}{\langle t\rangle^{r}}\|\psi(0)\|_{r},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}\ .

(1.15)

Hence, by the unitarity of the flow, these solutions have unbounded growth of positive Sobolev norms.
Then it is not difficult to construct solutions of the complete system (
1.13
) exhibiting Sobolev norms explosion.

To prove (
1.15
) we study the spectral properties of
\mathsf{V}_{0}:={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)
.
Exploiting Mourre’s commutator theory
[
37
]
,
we show that

\mathsf{V}_{0}
has absolutely continuous spectrum in an interval
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, over which it fulfills the strict Mourre estimate

g_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})\,{\rm i}[\mathsf{V}_{0},A]\,g_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})\geq\rho\,g_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})^{2}

(1.16)

for some
\rho>0
,
A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{1}
and
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})
with
g\equiv 1
on
{\mathcal{I}}
.
This in turn implies, by Sigal-Soffer theory
[
40
]
, that

\mathsf{V}_{0}
fulfills
dispersive estimates in the frequency space in the form of local energy decay estimates.
The same is true also for
\mathsf{V}_{0}+T
, as Mourre estimates are stable by pseudodifferential operators of negative order.

The key difficulty in applying Mourre-Sigal-Soffer theory is that the operator
A
entering the Mourre estimate (
1.16
) is not given,
but has to be constructed.
In particular we produce
A
so that the principal symbol of the operator on the left of (
1.16
) is

g_{\mathcal{I}}(\langle v_{0}\rangle)^{2}\,\{\langle v_{0}\rangle,h_{0}\}^{2}\ .

(1.17)

Then condition (
1.6
) allows to select an interval
{\mathcal{I}}
so that the function in (
1.17
) is strictly positive, and then we deduce
(
1.16
) exploiting the strong Gårding inequality.
This is done in Section
4
.

Pseudodifferential operators

Symbolic calculus.

This first class of results regards very basic properties of pseudodifferential operators and can be found in classical texts such as

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a\in S^{\rho}_{\,}
,
b\in S^{\mu}
,
\rho,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

(i)

Action: For any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there are
C,M>0
such that

\|{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right)u\|_{s-\rho}\leq C_{s}\,\wp^{\rho}_{M}(a)\,\|u\|_{s}.

(ii)

Symbolic calculus:
One has
{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right)^{*}={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\overline{a}\right)
.

There exists a symbol
c\in S^{\rho+\mu}
such that
{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right)\circ{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(b\right)={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(c\right)
. Moreover
c-ab\in S^{\rho+\mu-1}
and

\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt\exists N,C>0\mbox{ s.t. }\ \ \wp^{\rho+\mu-1}_{j}(c-ab)\leq C\,\wp^{\rho}_{N}(a)\,\wp^{\mu}_{N}(b)\ .

There exists a symbol
d\in S^{\rho+\mu-1}
such that

{\rm i}[{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right),{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(b\right)]={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(d\right)
.
Moreover
d-\{a,b\}\in S^{\rho+\mu-3}
and

\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\hskip 20.00003pt\exists N,C>0\mbox{ s.t. }\ \ \wp^{\rho+\mu-3}_{j}(d-\{a,b\})\leq C\,\wp^{\rho}_{N}(a)\,\wp^{\mu}_{N}(b)\ .

(iii)

Exact Egorov theorem: One has

e^{{\rm i}\tau H_{0}}\,{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right)\,e^{-{\rm i}\tau H_{0}}={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\circ\phi^{\tau}\right)\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}

where
\phi^{\tau}
is the flow (
1.4
).
In particular
t\mapsto a\circ\phi^{t}\in C^{k}({\mathbb{T}},S^{\rho})
,
\forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and

\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\hskip 10.00002pt\exists N,C>0\ s.t.\ \ \wp^{\rho}_{j}(a\circ\phi^{t})\leq C\,\wp^{\rho}_{N}(a)\ .

(2.1)

(iv)

Compactness: Let
\rho<0
. Then
{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(a\right)
is compact.

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
X\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{1})
is selfadjoint.
Then the following holds true.

(i)

Flow:
e^{-{\rm i}\tau X(t)}
extends to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}^{r})

\,\forall r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and moreover there exist
c_{r},C_{r}>0
s.t.

c_{r}\|\psi\|_{r}\leq\|e^{-{\rm i}\tau X(t)}\psi\|_{r}\leq C_{r}\|\psi\|_{r}\ ,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\tau\in[0,1]\ .

(2.3)

(ii)

Conjugation: Let
H(t)
be a time dependent selfadjoint operator. Assume that

\psi(t)=e^{-{\rm i}X(t)}{\varphi}(t)
then

{\rm i}\partial_{t}\psi=H(t)\psi\ \hskip 10.00002pt\iff\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm i}\partial_{t}{\varphi}=H^{+}(t){\varphi}

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle H^{+}(t):=e^{{\rm i}X(t)}\,H(t)\,e^{-{\rm i}X(t)}-\int_{0}^{1}e^{{\rm i}sX(t)}\,(\partial_{t}X(t))\,e^{-{\rm i}sX(t)}\ {\rm d}s\ .

(2.5)

(iii)

Lie expansion: Let
X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\rho}
with
\rho<1
and
H\in{\mathcal{S}}^{m}
,

m\in{\mathbb{R}}
, both selfadjoint.
Then
e^{{\rm i}X}\,H\,e^{-{\rm i}X}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{m}
, it is selfadjoint and for any
M\geq 1
we have

e^{{\rm i}X}\,H\,e^{-{\rm i}X}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}\frac{{\rm i}^{\ell}}{\ell!}{\rm ad}_{X}^{\ell}(H)+R_{M},

(2.6)

where
{\rm ad}_{X}(H):=[X,H]
and

R_{M}:=\frac{{\rm i}^{M+1}}{M!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{M}\,e^{{\rm i}\tau X}\,{\rm ad}_{X}^{M+1}(H)\,e^{-{\rm i}\tau X}\,{\rm d}\tau\ \in{\mathcal{S}}^{{m-(M+1)(1-\rho)}}

is selfadjoint.

Remark 2.4
.

If

The next results concern the functional calculus of pseudodifferential operators in

[

12
]

Given

as follows: for any

{\widetilde{g}}_{N}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}},\hskip 20.00003pt{\widetilde{g}}_{N}(x,y):=\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}g^{(\ell)}(x)\frac{({\rm i}y)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\right)\,\tau\left(\frac{y}{\langle x\rangle}\right)

The operator

is independent of

, see
[

]

.

3
Pseudodifferential normal form

The first step of our proof is to use pseudodifferential normal form to extract from the original equation (

{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)
.
Precisely we shall prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider equation (
1.1
) with
V(t,x,D)={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v(t,\cdot)\right)
and
v\in C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{0}_{cl})
.
There exists
\delta>0
such that for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
the following holds true.
There exists a change of coordinates

{\mathcal{U}}_{N}(t)
, unitary in
L^{2}
and fulfilling

\forall r\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\exists\,c_{r},C_{r}>0\colon\hskip 20.00003ptc_{r}\|{\varphi}\|_{r}\leq\|{\mathcal{U}}_{N}(t)^{\pm}{\varphi}\|_{r}\leq C_{r}\|{\varphi}\|_{r},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},

such that
u(t)
solves (
1.1
) if and only if

{\varphi}(t):={\mathcal{U}}_{N}(t)u(t)
solves

{\rm i}\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\big({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T_{N}+R_{N}(t)\big){\varphi}

(3.2)

where
\langle v_{0}\rangle
is the resonant average of the principal symbol
v_{0}
of

,

T_{N}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{-\delta}
is time independent and selfadjoint and

R_{N}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-N-\delta})
is selfadjoint
\forall t
.

The proof of the proposition is based on a series of change of coordinates, and at each step we shall solve a simple homological equation, which we now describe:

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
H\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{m})
,
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
. There exists
X\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{m})
such that

H(t)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}H(s)\,{\rm d}s=\partial_{t}X(t)\ .

(3.3)

If
H(t)
is selfadjoint for any
t
, so is
X(t)
.

Proof.

We solve (
3.3
) at the level of symbols; we put
H(t)={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(h(t,\cdot)\right)
with
h\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},S^{m})
and look for

X(t)={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\chi(t,\cdot)\right)
with a symbol
\chi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},S^{m})
to be determined.
Then (
3.3
) reads

h(t,x,\xi)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(s,x,\xi)\,{\rm d}s=\partial_{t}\chi(t,x,\xi)\ ,

(3.4)

whose solution is readily given by

\chi(t,x,\xi):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(h(s,x,\xi)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(s_{1},x,\xi)\,{\rm d}s_{1}\right)\,{\rm d}s\ .

(3.5)

One verifies that
\chi\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},S^{m})
.
Finally if
H(t)
is selfadjoint, its symbol
h(t,\cdot)
is real valued, so is
\chi(t,\cdot)
and thus
X(t)
is selfadjoint.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

First we gauge away
H_{0}
, performing the change of variables

u=e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}\psi
. Then
\psi
solves

{\rm i}\partial_{t}\psi=e^{{\rm i}tH_{0}}\,{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v(t,\cdot)\right)\,e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}\psi
.

Next recall that,
by definition of the class
S^{0}_{\rm cl}
, the symbol
v
decomposes as

v(t,\cdot)=v_{0}(t,\cdot)+v_{-\mu}(t,\cdot)

(3.6)

with
v_{0}(t,\cdot)
positively homogeneous of degree 0 and
v_{-\mu}\in C^{0}_{r}({\mathbb{T}},S^{-\mu})
for some

.
So we decompose

(3.7)

with

\mathsf{V}(t):=e^{{\rm i}tH_{0}}\,{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v_{0}(t,\cdot)\right)\,e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}\ ,\hskip 20.00003ptR(t):=e^{{\rm i}tH_{0}}\,{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v_{-\mu}(t,\cdot)\right)\,e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}\ .

(3.8)

Note that
\mathsf{V}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{0})
and
R\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-\mu})
, both selfadjoint
\forall t
.

Now we proceed inductively, performing several changes of coordinates which remove the oscillating part order by order.
Let us describe the first step.
We perform the change of variables
\psi=e^{-{\rm i}X_{1}(t)}{\varphi}
where
X_{1}(t)\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{0})
, selfadjoint
\forall t
,
to be determined.
By Lemma
2.3
,
{\varphi}
fulfills the Schrödinger equation

{\rm i}\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\mathsf{H}_{1}(t){\varphi}
with

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=e^{{\rm i}X_{1}(t)}\,(\mathsf{V}(t)+R(t))\,e^{-{\rm i}X_{1}(t)}-\int_{0}^{1}e^{{\rm i}sX_{1}(t)}\,(\partial_{t}X_{1}(t))\,e^{-{\rm i}sX_{1}(t)}\ {\rm d}s\ .

Then a Lie expansion, see (
2.6
), gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{V}(t)-\partial_{t}X_{1}(t)+R_{1}(t),\hskip 20.00003ptR_{1}\in C^{0}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-\delta}),\mbox{ selfadjoint }\forall t\ ,

(3.9)

with

\delta:=\min(1,\mu)>0\ .

(3.10)

Lemma
3.2
allows us to choose
X_{1}\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}},{\mathcal{S}}^{0})
, selfadjoint
\forall t
, s.t.

\mathsf{V}(t)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathsf{V}(s)\,{\rm d}s=\partial_{t}X_{1}(t)\ .

(3.11)

With this choice, and exploiting Egorov’s Theorem, equation (
3.9
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathsf{V}(s)\,{\rm d}s+R_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v_{0}(s,\phi^{s}(x,\xi))\right)\,{\rm d}s+R_{1}(t)

\displaystyle={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+R_{1}(t)\ .

(3.12)

Note also that the map
e^{-{\rm i}X_{1}(t)}
fulfills an estimate like (
3.1
) thanks to Lemma
2.3
.

Then we iterate this procedure. Assume that at the
N
-th step we have the equation

{\rm i}\partial_{t}{\varphi}=\left({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T_{N}+R_{N}(t)\right){\varphi}=:\mathsf{H}_{N}(t){\varphi}

(3.13)

with

selfadjoint and time independent, and

\forall t
.
The change of variables
{\varphi}=e^{-{\rm i}X_{N+1}(t)}w
, with a certain

selfadjoint and to be determined, conjugates

) to
{\rm i}\partial_{t}w=\mathsf{H}_{N+1}(t)w
with

\mathsf{H}_{N+1}(t)={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T_{N}+R_{N}(t)-\partial_{t}X_{N+1}(t)+R_{N+1}(t)\ ,

We exploit again Lemma

to find

Thus

in (

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{N+1}(t)

\displaystyle={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)+T_{N+1}+R_{N+1}(t),\hskip 20.00003ptT_{N+1}:=T_{N}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}R_{N}(s)\,{\rm d}s\ .

Clearly

, it is

Finally we put
{\mathcal{U}}_{N}(t):=e^{{\rm i}X_{N}(t)}\,\cdots e^{{\rm i}X_{1}(t)}e^{{\rm i}tH_{0}}

∎

4
Mourre estimates

In this section we study the spectral properties of the operator

{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)
, which is the leading term of equation (
3.2
).
In particular we prove that its essential spectrum contains a nontrivial interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{0}
and, over a subinterval
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{0}
, it fulfills a Mourre estimate.
Then Mourre theory
[
37
]
guarantees that
{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right)
has absolutely continuous spectrum in
{\mathcal{I}}
, and
therefore we might expect dispersive behavior.
More precisely we prove the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
v\in\mathscr{V}
(see (
1.11
)) and
v_{0}
be its principal symbol.
Denote by
\langle v_{0}\rangle\in S^{0}
its resonant average (see (
1.5
)) and

\mathsf{V}_{0}:={\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(\langle v_{0}\rangle\right).

The following holds true.

(i)

The spectrum
\sigma\big(\mathsf{V}_{0}\big)
contains a closed interval
{\mathcal{I}}_{0}
.

(ii)

There exist
A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{1}
selfadjoint, an open interval
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{0}

with
g_{{\mathcal{I}}}\equiv 1
over
{\mathcal{I}}

such that

g_{{\mathcal{I}}}\big(\mathsf{V}_{0}\big)\ {\rm i}[\mathsf{V}_{0},A]\ g_{{\mathcal{I}}}\big(\mathsf{V}_{0}\big)\geq\rho\,g_{{\mathcal{I}}}\big(\mathsf{V}_{0}\big)^{2}+\mathsf{K}

for some
\rho>0
and
\mathsf{K}
a compact operator.

Notation:

In the following, we shall denote by
\mathsf{K}
a general compact operator on
L^{2}
, which might change from line to line.
We shall also constantly use that pseudodifferential operators in
{\mathcal{S}}^{-\mu}

In order to prove Proposition

we start with some preparation. We shall denote by

{\mathcal{A}}\colon{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
,
(\vartheta,I)\to(x,\xi)
the action-angle change of coordinates defined by

(4.2)

Given a function

the function

expressed in action angle coordinates (
4.2
).
Note that since (

{\widetilde{\{f,g\}}}=\{{\widetilde{f}},{\widetilde{g}}\}=:\partial_{I}{\widetilde{f}}\,\partial_{\vartheta}{\widetilde{g}}-\partial_{\vartheta}{\widetilde{f}}\,\partial_{I}{\widetilde{g}}\ .

(4.3)

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that

is positively homogeneous of degree 0, i.e. it fulfills (

Then

{\widetilde{f}}(\vartheta,I)={\widetilde{f}}(\vartheta,1),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall I>\frac{1}{2}\ .

Being

Now we begin the proof of Proposition

, which is

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

(i)

\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\equiv\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,1)

of zero measure.
Since the image of

is the nontrivial interval

(see (

is a positive measure set and contains only regular values.

\underline{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{0}\setminus\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}({\mathcal{C}})
.
The set
\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}^{-1}(\underline{\lambda})
is a compact set of isolated points, thus finitely many; denote them by

.
Since
\partial_{\vartheta}\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta_{a})\neq 0
for any
a=1,\ldots,d
, we
can find
neighbors

of
\vartheta_{a}
and a neighbor

of

such that

(i)

\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}\colon{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\to{\mathcal{V}}
is a diffeomorphism for any
a=1,\ldots,d
,

(ii)

\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}^{-1}({\mathcal{V}})=\cup_{a}{\mathcal{U}}_{a}
,

there exists

such that
\min\limits_{\vartheta\in\cup_{a}\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}_{a}}\left|\partial_{\vartheta}\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta)\right|\geq\sqrt{\rho}
.

Now take an interval
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
with
\underline{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{I}}
.
Take also
g_{\mathcal{I}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})

g_{\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
on

and
\textup{supp }g_{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
.
Using (ii) above, we have that

(\vartheta,I)\in\textup{supp }\left(g_{\mathcal{I}}\big(\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\big)\right)\cap\{I>\frac{1}{2}\}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \vartheta\in\overline{\bigcup_{a=1}^{d}{\mathcal{U}}_{a}}\ .

(4.17)

In particular in the set

\textup{supp}\left(g_{\mathcal{I}}\big(\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\big)\right)\cap\{I>\frac{1}{2}\}

the function
\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\equiv\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta)
fulfills (iii) above.
We
deduce that

g_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}\left(\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\right)\,\left(\partial_{\vartheta}\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\right)^{2}\,\geq\rho\ g_{\mathcal{I}}^{2}\left(\widetilde{\langle v_{0}\rangle}(\vartheta,I)\right)\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\vartheta,I)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times\{I>\frac{1}{2}\}\ ,

proving (
4.16
).
∎

We can finally prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

By Lemmata
4.4
and
4.5
, we have

\langle g_{{\mathcal{I}}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})\,{\rm i}[\mathsf{V}_{0},A]\,g_{{\mathcal{I}}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})u,u\rangle\geq\rho\langle{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(g_{\mathcal{I}}(\langle v_{0}\rangle)^{2}\right)u,u\rangle+\langle(\mathsf{K}-CH_{0}^{-2})u,u\rangle

with
\mathsf{K}
compact.
Now by symbolic and functional calculus

\displaystyle{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(g_{\mathcal{I}}(\langle v_{0}\rangle)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(g_{\mathcal{I}}(\langle v_{0}\rangle)\right)\right)^{2}+{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{g.dec}}}{{=}}g_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathsf{V}_{0})^{2}+\mathsf{K}_{1}

Inserting in (

).

(6.9)

where

{\mathtt{v}}^{+}_{n}(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(nt)\,\,{\mathtt{v}}(\phi^{t}(x,\xi))\,{\rm d}t,\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathtt{v}}^{-}_{n}(x,\xi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(nt)\,{\mathtt{v}}(\phi^{t}(x,\xi))\,{\rm d}t,\hskip 10.00002ptn\geq 0.

They are all symbols positively homogeneous of degree 0.
The claim is equivalent to verify that

\exists\ n\geq 1\colon\ \ \{h_{0},{\mathtt{v}}^{+}_{n}\}\not\equiv 0\ \ \mbox{ in }x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1\ .

(6.10)

First note that, by integration by parts and the periodicity of the flow
\phi^{t}
,

\displaystyle\{h_{0},{\mathtt{v}}^{+}_{n}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(nt)\,\{h_{0},{\mathtt{v}}\circ\phi^{t}\}\,{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(nt)\,\left(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\mathtt{v}}\circ\phi^{t}\right)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(nt)\,({\mathtt{v}}\circ\phi^{t})\,{\rm d}t=n\,{\mathtt{v}}^{-}_{n}\,,

(6.11)

so it is sufficient to check that
\exists n\geq 1
so that
{\mathtt{v}}^{-}_{n}\not\equiv 0
in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1
.
Actually, since

\displaystyle{\mathtt{v}}_{n}^{-}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sin(nt)\,{\mathtt{v}}\big(\phi^{t}(x,\xi)\big)\,{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos\big(n(t-\frac{\pi}{2n})\big)\,{\mathtt{v}}\big(\phi^{t}(x,\xi)\big)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(nt)\,{\mathtt{v}}\big(\phi^{t+\frac{\pi}{2n}}(x,\xi)\big)\,{\rm d}t={\mathtt{v}}^{+}_{n}\big(\phi^{\frac{\pi}{2n}}(x,\xi)\big),

(6.12)

it is enough to check that
\exists n\geq 1
so that one among
{\mathtt{v}}^{\pm}_{n}
is not identically zero in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1
; this is what we show next.

Assume by contradiction that
{\mathtt{v}}^{\pm}_{n}\equiv 0
in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1
for any
n\geq 1
; then from (
6.9
) we get
{\mathtt{v}}\circ\phi^{t}={\mathtt{v}}^{+}_{0}
for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and any
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1
. Then

0=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\mathtt{v}}\circ\phi^{t}=\{h_{0},{\mathtt{v}}\}\circ\phi^{t}\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1;

so, at
t=0
, one gets
\{h_{0},{\mathtt{v}}\}\equiv 0
in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq 1
, contradicting the assumption.

Then at least one couple of the
{\mathtt{v}}_{n}^{\pm}
is not identically zero,
and (
6.10
) follows.
∎

A.1
The strong Gårding inequality in
{\mathcal{S}}^{0}

Our proof involves the Anti-Wick quantization of a symbol, which we now introduce.
First let us define coherent states:
for
z=(q,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

(A.1)

Note that the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{z}

.
Now, given a symbol

(A.2)

, then

.

\Phi_{0}

A.1
). Moreover

Proof.

A.2

Proof of Theorem

Denote by
\mathscr{A}
the set on the right of (

We first show that
\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))\subseteq\mathscr{A}

Assume by contradiction that

\lambda\in\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))
does not belong to

\mathscr{A}
.
Then there exist
c,R>0
such that
|v(x,\xi)-\lambda|\geq c

for any

.
Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with
\chi\equiv 1
in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\leq R
and
\chi\equiv 0
in
x^{2}+\xi^{2}\geq R+1

Put

b(x,\xi)=\dfrac{1-\chi(x,\xi)}{v(x,\xi)-\lambda}\in S^{0}_{\,}
.
Then by symbolic calculus there are operators
K_{1},K_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}
(and therefore compact) so that

\left({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right)-\lambda\right)\,{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(b\right)=\textup{Id}+K_{1},\hskip 20.00003pt{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(b\right)\,\left({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right)-\lambda\right)=\textup{Id}+K_{2}\ .

Thus
{\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right)-\lambda
is a Fredholm operator, and its
spectrum in a neighbourhood of zero must be discrete.
In particular
\lambda\not\in\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))
, contradicting the assumption.
This shows that
\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))\subseteq\mathscr{A}
.

We show now the inverse inclusion
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))
. Given
\lambda\in\mathscr{A}
, we shall
exhibit a Weyl’s sequence for
\lambda
, i.e. a sequence of functions

with
\|\varphi_{j}\|=1
,
\varphi_{j}\rightharpoonup 0

\|({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right)-\lambda)\varphi_{j}\|\to 0
; then
Weyl’s theorem guarantees that

\lambda\in\sigma_{ess}({\rm Op}^{W}\!\left(v\right))
.
We construct such a Weyl’s sequence using the coherent states in (
A.1
).
If
\lambda\in\mathscr{A}
we have a sequence

))

We claim that, up to subsequences,

\lambda
.
It is clear that
\|\Phi_{j}\|=1

.
It is not difficult to show that

We show now that

Lemma A.2
.

Let
z_{j}:=(q_{j},p_{j})\to\infty
.
Then, up to a subsequence,
\Phi_{j}:={\mathcal{T}}_{z_{j}}\Phi_{0}\rightharpoonup 0
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases:

(i)
the sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j}
is bounded and

(ii)
up to a subsequence
|q_{j}|\to\infty
.

In case
(i)
, up to a subsequence we can assume
q_{j}\to q_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p_{j}\to\infty
.
Take an arbitrary
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\overline{\Phi_{j}(x)}\,{\rm d}x=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\overline{\Phi_{(q_{0},p_{j})}(x)}{\rm d}x}_{=:{\mathtt{I}}_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,\overline{\left(\Phi_{(q_{j},p_{j})}(x)-\Phi_{(q_{0},p_{j})}(x)\right)}{\rm d}x}_{=:{\mathtt{I}}_{2}}\ .

(A.6)

Using Riemann-Lebesgue lemma

{\mathtt{I}}_{1}=\frac{e^{\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{0}}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,e^{-\frac{(x-q_{0})^{2}}{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}xp_{j}}{\rm d}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{j}\to\infty}}{{\to}}0\ .

(A.7)

To estimate the second integral in (
A.6
) we write

\displaystyle{\mathtt{I}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\pi^{-\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}xp_{j}}\,\left(f(x+q_{j})e^{-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{j}}-f(x+q_{0})e^{-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{0}}\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{e^{-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{j}}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}xp_{j}}\,\big(f(x+q_{j})-f(x+q_{0})\big)\,{\rm d}x}_{{\mathtt{I}}_{21}}+\underbrace{\frac{e^{-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{j}}-e^{-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}p_{j}q_{0}}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}xp_{j}}\,f(x+q_{0})\,{\rm d}x}_{{\mathtt{I}}_{22}}

In case

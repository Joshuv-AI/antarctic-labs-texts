Boundedness of n n -complements on surfaces
Xiangze Zeng

Abstract
. In light of Shokurov’s result [ 16 , Theorem 3] in 2020, the main purpose of this paper is to prove the boundedness of n n -complements for surface pairs ( X / Z ∋ o , B ) (X/Z\ni o,B) under the same setting, without the asssumptions that X / Z X/Z is of weak Fano type or ( X / Z ∋ o , B ) (X/Z\ni o,B) has a klt ℝ \mathbb{R} -complement.



1.
Introduction

The concept of
n
-complements naturally arises from the study on the anticanonical linear system
|-nK_{X}|
, and was introduced by V.V.Shokurov in
[
14
]
. Later this concept of
n
-complements was generalized to
\mathbb{R}
-complements and Shokurov asked: does the existence of an
\mathbb{R}
-complement necessarily imply the existence of an
n
-complement for some integer
n
? And if so,what kind of restrictions can we put on this
n
? The widely expected result is that there exists a finite set
\mathcal{N}
of positive integers such that, for a certain class of relative pairs
(X/Z\ni o,B)
, as long as
(X/Z\ni o,B)
has an
\mathbb{R}
-complement, it also has an
n
-complement for some
n\in\mathcal{N}
. We’ll refer to this statement as ”boundedness of
n
-complements” throughout this paper. Boundedness of
n
-complements on surfaces was established by V.V.Shokurov in
[
15
]
. In
[
1
]
C.Birkar proved boundedness of
n
-complements for pairs
(X,B)
of Fano type with hyperstand multiplicities in arbitrary dimensions, and later Shokurov generalized this boundedness result to all relative pairs of weak Fano type in
[
16
]
. In this paper, we’ll generalize Shokurov’s boundedness result Theorem 3 in
[
16
]
to all surface pairs
(X/Z\ni o,B)
, without the weak Fano type assumption on
X/Z
and without the restrictions on the multiplicities of
B
. To better state our main theorem
1.4
, we recall the formal definitions of complements first:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X/Z\ni o,D)
be a pair with a proper local morphism
X/Z\ni o
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
. Then a pair
(X/Z\ni o,D^{+})
with the same local morphism is called an

\mathbb{R}
-complement
of
(X/Z\ni o,D)
if the following holds:

(1)

D^{+}\geq D
;

(2)

(X,D^{+})
is lc;

(3)

K_{X}+D^{+}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z\ni o
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X/Z\ni o,D)
be a pair with a proper local morphism
X/Z\ni o
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D=\sum d_{i}D_{i}
, and
n
is a positive integer. Then a pair
(X/Z\ni o,D^{+}=\sum d_{i}^{+}D_{i})
with the same local morphism is called an

n
-complement
of
(X/Z\ni o,D)
if the following holds:

(1)

for every prime divisor
D_{i}
,

d_{i}^{+}\geq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $d_{i}=1$;}\\
\frac{\lfloor(n+1)d_{i}\rfloor}{n}&\text{otherwise;}\end{cases}

(2)

(X,D^{+})
is lc;

(3)

K_{X}+D^{+}\sim_{n}0/Z\ni o
.

Now we are ready to state our main result Theorem
1.4
, with the complementary index
n
under the restrictions given by Definition
1.3
below.

Definition 1.3
.

Given these data: a positive integer
I
, a nonrational vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, a direction
e
of the rational affine span
\langle v\rangle
and a real number
\epsilon>0
. We say an integer
n
is under
complementary restrictions
with respect to
I,v,e,\epsilon
if there exists a vector
v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
satisfying the following:

(1)

Divisibility:
I|n
;

(2)

Denominator with
n
:
nv_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}
;

(3)

Approximation:
||v_{n}-v||<\epsilon/n
;

(4)

Directional Approximation:
||\frac{v_{n}-v}{||v_{n}-v||}-e||<\epsilon
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose we have the data
I,v,e,\epsilon
as in Definition
1.3
. Then we can find a finite set
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(I,v,e,\epsilon)
such that:

(1)

Restrictions:

\mathcal{N}
consists of integers under complementary restrictions;

(2)

Existence of
n
-complements:
suppose we have a surface pair
(X/Z\ni o,B)
with a proper local morphism
X/Z\ni o
, connected central fiber
X_{o}
and an
\mathbb{R}
-boundary
B
. Then if
(X/Z\ni o,B)
has an
\mathbb{R}
-complement, it also has an
n
-complement for some
n\in\mathcal{N}
.

I would like to thank Professor Shokurov for his help and guidance. This work wouldn’t be completed without his deep insights and infinite patience.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic Properties of Complements

In this section we list some basic properties of
n
-complements frequently used throughout this paper. Lemma
2.1
implies that
n
-complements are preserved by birational pushforwards, while Lemma
2.2
is a monotonicity result for
n
-complements.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
12
, Proposition 4.3.1]
). Let
f:Y/Z\to X/Z
be a birational contraction,
D
a subboundary on
Y
. If
(Y/Z,D)
has an
n
-complement
(Y/Z,D^{+})
, then
(X/Z,f(D))
has an
n
-complement
(X/Z,f(D^{+}))
.

Remark 1
.

If we put certain restrictions on the coefficients of the boundary
D
, we can also pull back complements along a birational contraction. See
[
12
, Proposition 4.3.2]
for one of such result.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
16
, Proposition 1]
). Let
(X/Z,D)
and
(X/Z,D\,^{\prime})
be two pairs on
X
with
D\geq D\,^{\prime}
. If
(X/Z,D)
has an
n
-complement
(X/Z,D^{+})
, then
(X/Z,D^{+})
is also an
n
-complement of
(X/Z,D\,^{\prime})
.

2.2.
Rational Approximation of Coefficients

We’ll quote and use certain known boundedness results on
n
-complements for the main proof. Since these positive results are established under the assumption that coefficients of
B
are contained in a hyperstandard set, or more generally, a dcc set of rational numbers in [0,1], we briefly introduce the definition of hyperstandard sets and a procedure of lower rational approximation of coefficients using hyperstandard sets here. For more details of related constructions, properties and proofs, see Construction 1 from
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
11
, 3.2]
).
Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a set of rational numbers in
[0,1]
. Then a hyperstandard set is

\Phi=\Phi(\mathfrak{R})=\{1-\frac{r}{l}|r\in\mathfrak{R},\text{$l$ is a positive integer}\}\cap[0,1].

Remark 2
.

This definition of hyperstandard sets is a generalization of the set
S
of standard multiplicities where
S=\{1\}\cup\{1-1/l|l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\}=\Phi(\{0,1\})
. Hence to make sure that the hyperstandard set
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
contains
S
, we’ll always assume
\{0,1\}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.

For a finite set
\mathcal{N}
of positive integers, and a hyperstandard set
\Phi=\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
as in Definition
2.3
, we can construct a new set
\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
of rational numbers in [0,1] as follows:

\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)=\{1-\frac{r}{l}+\frac{1}{l}(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\frac{m_{n}}{n+1})|r\in\mathfrak{R},l\in\mathbb{N}^{+},m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\}\cap[0,1].

\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
is again a hyperstandard set by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
16
, Corollary 4]
). Let
\mathcal{N}
be a nonempty finite set of positive integers and
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite set of rational numbers in [0,1]. Then there exists a new finite set
\mathfrak{R}\,^{\prime}
of rational numbers in [0,1] such that

\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)=\Phi(\mathfrak{R}\,^{\prime}).

Moreover,
\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
is a dcc set of rational numbers in [0,1] with only one accumulation point
1\in\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
.

Construction 1
.

Using the fact
\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
is a dcc set with only one accumulation point 1 from Lemma
2.4
, for any
b\in[0,1]
, there exists a unique largest rational approximation
b_{\mathcal{N},\Phi}\leq b
in
\Gamma(\mathcal{N},\Phi)
. For simplicity, in the following text we’ll always take
\mathfrak{R}=\{0,1\}
and denote this
b_{\mathcal{N},\Phi}
by
b_{\mathcal{N}}
. Similarly, for a boundary
B
on
X
, we can construct its low approximation
B_{\mathcal{N}}
.

The following lemma shows that this low approximation of the boundary
B
preserves
n
-complements when
n\in\mathcal{N}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
16
, Corollary 6]
). Let
(X/Z,B)
be a pair with a boundary
B
. If
(X/Z,B_{\mathcal{N}})
has an
n
-complement
(X/Z,B^{+})
with
n\in\mathcal{N}
, then
(X/Z,B^{+})
is also an
n
-complement of
(X/Z,B)
.

2.3.
Connectedness Lemma

In this section we prove a modified version of the well-known Connectedness Lemma, which is Lemma
2.7
. Let’s first recall the concept of locus of lc singularities in Definition
2.6
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a normal variety,
(X,D)
a log pair. Then a subvariety
W\subset X
is a
center of lc singularities,
if there exists a birational contraction
f:Y\to X
and a divisor
E
(not necessarily exceptional) on
Y
, such that the discrepancy
a(E,D,X)\leq-1
and
f(E)=W
. The union of all centers of lc singularities is called the
locus of lc singularities of
(X,D)

and denoted by
\operatorname{LCS}(X,D)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
14
, 5.7]
,
[
9
, Theorem 17.4]
). Let
(X/Z\ni o,D)
be a pair with a proper local morphism
g:X\to Z\ni o
and connected central fiber
g^{-1}(o)
,
D=\sum d_{i}D_{i}
a divisor on
X
such that:

(1)

if
d_{i}<0
, then
D_{i}
is very
g
-exceptional in the sense that
g_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}))=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
;

(2)

-(K_{X}+D)
is big and nef over
Z
.

Then
\operatorname{LCS}(X,D)
is connected in a neighborhood of the fiber
g^{-1}(o)
.

Proof.

Let
X\to Z\,^{\prime}\to Z
be the Stein factorization of
g
. Since the central fiber
g^{-1}(o)
is connected and
X\to Z\,^{\prime}
has connected fibers, the fiber of
Z\,^{\prime}\to Z
over
o
is a single point
o\,^{\prime}
. Then we can apply the well-known connectedness lemma to the contraction
X\to Z\,^{\prime}
, and conclude that
\operatorname{LCS}(X,D)
is connected near the fiber of
X\to Z\,^{\prime}
over
o\,^{\prime}
, which is the same as
g^{-1}(o)
set-theoretically.
∎

The connectedness principle might not work for pairs
(X/Z\ni o,D)
without the big assumption. However, we have a generalization of
2.7
for surface 0-pairs:

Lemma 2.8
.

(
[
14
, Theorem 6.9]
). Let
X
be a surface,
g:X\to Z\ni o
a proper local morphism with connected central fiber
g^{-1}(o)
,
D
an effective divisor on
X
such that
K_{X}+D\equiv 0
. If
\operatorname{LCS}(X,D)
is disconnected in a neighborhood of
g^{-1}(o)
, then

(1)

(X,D)
is plt in a neighborhood of
g^{-1}(o)
, and

(2)

\operatorname{LCS}(X,D)\cap g^{-1}(o)
has two connected components.

Remark 3
.

For a detailed discussion on Lemma
2.8
in higher dimensions and for pairs with
-(K_{X}+D)
nef, see
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
.

2.4.
Inversion of Adjunction

In this section we recall the fact that inversion of adjunction holds for log canonical surface pairs, which is Lemma
2.9
below.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(X,S+D)
be a log pair with
X
a smooth surface,
S\subset X
a smooth curve, and
D
an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
S\not\subset\operatorname{Supp}D
. Then
(S,D|_{S})
is lc if and only if
(X,S+D)
is lc in a neighborhood of
S
.

The following is a special generalization of Lemma
2.9
which we’ll use in the proof of Theorem
4.4
later.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(X,S+D)
be a log pair with
X
a smooth surface,
S\subset X
a smooth curve,
D=\sum d_{i}D_{i}
an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
S\not\subset\operatorname{Supp}D
, and
D_{i}.S=0
for all
i
with
d_{i}<0
. Then if
(S,D|_{S})
is lc,
(X,S+D)
is lc in a neighborhood of
S
.

Proof.

Since
D_{\geq 0}|_{S}=D|_{S}
by our assumption where
D_{\geq 0}=\sum_{d_{i}>0}d_{i}D_{i}
, we can apply Lemma
2.9
to the pair
(X,S+D_{\geq 0})
. Hence
(X,S+D_{\geq 0})
is lc in a neighborhood of
S
. Since
D\leq D_{\geq 0}
,
(X,S+D)
is also lc in a neighborhood of
S
.
∎

Remark 4
.

One can find a more general version of Lemma
2.9
in higher dimensions and for a wider range of varieties
X
in
[
7
]
. For inversion of adjunction in the log terminal case, see
[
10
, Theorem 5.50]
.

2.5.
Minimal Dlt Modification

In some of our proofs, we need to lift lc pairs to a higher model with better singularities. That’s where Minimal Dlt Modification of a lc pair as follows comes in handy.

Proposition 2.11
.

(
[
12
, Proposition 3.1.2]
). Let
(X,D)
be a lc normal surface pair where
D
is a boundary. Then there exists a blow-up, namely the
minimal dlt modification

f:Y\to X
such that

(1)

f^{*}(K_{X}+D)=K_{Y}+D_{Y}+\sum E_{i}
, where
D_{Y}
is the birational transform of
D
and
E_{i}
are all the exceptional divisors over
X
;

(2)

(Y,D_{Y}+\sum E_{i})
is dlt;

(3)

Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

2.6.
Zariski Decomposition

Traditionally Zariski decomposition are defined for effective divisors on nonsingular surfaces, on which the existence and uniqueness of Zariski decomposition is purely a linear algebraic result. There are generalizations of this classical concept to a wider range of algebraic varieties and divisors in higher dimensions, and here in this section we recall Zariski decomposition for pseudo-effective divisors on normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial surfaces in the relative setting (
[
13
, Section 2]
), where the 2-dimensional intersection theory works well.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
X/Z
be a relative projective normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial surface, and
D
be any divisor on
X
. Then a decomposition
D=M+F
is called a
Zariski decomposition
of
D
if the following holds:

(1)

F=\sum f_{i}D_{i}
, where
D_{i}
are distinct vertical prime divisors over
Z
, and
f_{i}>0
;

(2)

M
is nef over
Z
;

(3)

the intersection matrix
(d_{ij})_{i\times j}
is negative definite, where
d_{ij}=D_{i}.D_{j}
;

(4)

M.D_{i}=0
for all
i
.

If such a decomposition exists and is unique, we usually call
M
the
mobile part
and
F
the
fixed part
of
D
.

Remark 5
.

See
[
13
]
for a generalization of Zariski decomposition to higher dimensions.

It is well-known that Zariski decomposition exists and is unique for surface pairs:

Theorem 2.13
.

(
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
). Let
X/Z
be a projective relative normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial surface and
D
be a pseudo-effective divisor on
X
. Then there exists a unique Zariski decomposition
D=M+F
of
D
.

Proposition 2.14
.

(
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
). Assumptions the same as in Theorem
2.13
and let
L
be a nef divisor over
Z
. If
L\leq D
, we have
L\leq M
.

Proof.

Let
M-L=(D-L)-F=M\,^{\prime}-F\,^{\prime}
, where
M\,^{\prime}
and
F\,^{\prime}
are effective divisors with no common components. Then to show
L\leq M
, it suffices to show
F\,^{\prime}=0
. On one hand, since
D-L
is effective, by our construction
F\,^{\prime}\leq F
. Hence
M.F\,^{\prime}=0
by Definition
2.12
(4), and it follows that
(M-L).F\,^{\prime}=-L.F\,^{\prime}\leq 0
since
L
is nef over
Z
. On the other hand,
(M-L).F\,^{\prime}=(M\,^{\prime}-F\,^{\prime}).F\,^{\prime}\geq-(F\,^{\prime})^{2}\geq 0
, where
M\,^{\prime}.F\,^{\prime}\geq 0
by construction,
(F\,^{\prime})^{2}\leq 0
by Definition
2.12
(3) and the fact
F\,^{\prime}\leq F
. Combining these, we have
(F\,^{\prime})^{2}=0
, and
F\,^{\prime}=0
follows from Definition
2.12
(3).
∎

Remark 6
.

This property can be rephrased as:
M
is the biggest nef divisor
L
such that
L\leq D
. Another useful result from Proposition
2.14
is: if
D\,^{\prime}\leq D
are two pseudo-effective divisors on a surface
X
, then
M\,^{\prime}\leq M
. Here
M\,^{\prime}
and
M
are the mobile parts of
D\,^{\prime}
and
D
respectively.

In general the mobile part
M
is not necessarily semi-ample. But we can establish this useful property in the following special case.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
(X/Z,B)
be a lc pair, where
X/Z
is a projective relative normal surface,
B
is effective and
D=K_{X}+B
is pseudo-effective. Then the mobile part
M
of
D
is semi-ample over
Z
, and there exists a birational contraction
f:X/Z\to X\,^{\prime}/Z
such that the fixed part
F
of
D
is
f
-exceptional. Additionally, if
D
is effective
M
is also effective.

Proof.

Run
(K_{X}+B)
-MMP on the pair
(X/Z,B)
, and suppose at each step we have an extremal contraction
f_{i}:(X_{i},B_{i})\to(X_{i+1},B_{i+1})
. Set
E_{i}:=K_{X_{i}}+B_{i}-f_{i}^{*}(K_{X_{i+1}}+B_{i+1})
, then
-E_{i}
is
f_{i}
-nef and
f_{i}
-exceptional, hence by Negativity Lemma
E_{i}
is effective. Let
X\,^{\prime}/Z
be the end result of this MMP, and
f:X/Z\to X\,^{\prime}/Z
be the composition of all
f_{i}
with
B\,^{\prime}=f_{*}B
,
D\,^{\prime}=f_{*}D
, then
E:=K_{X}+B-f^{*}(K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime})
is also effective and
f
-exceptional. Note
D=K_{X}+B=f^{*}D\,^{\prime}+E
is a Zariski Decomposition of
D
, since
f^{*}D\,^{\prime}
is nef over
Z
, and
E\geq 0
is
f
-exceptional. By uniqueness we then have
M=f^{*}D\,^{\prime}
and
F=E
. Now
f
is indeed a birational contraction for which
F
is
f
-exceptional. The semi-ampleness of
D\,^{\prime}
over
Z
follows from the relative abundance theorem in dimension 2:
(X\,^{\prime},B\,^{\prime})
is lc, and
K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}=D\,^{\prime}
is nef over
Z
by construction. Hence
M=f^{*}D\,^{\prime}
is also semi-ample over
Z
.

When
D
is effective, we can apply Proposition
2.14
to our case with
L=0
, then we get
M
is also effective.
∎

2.7.
0-contractions

In this section we briefly review the definition of
0
-contractions. For more detailed definitions and properties, we refer to
[
16
, Section 7]
.

Definition 2.16
.

Suppose
f:X\to Z
is a contraction of normal algebraic varieties,
B
is an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
. Then in the usual sense
f
is a
0
-contraction
if it satisfies (1) and (2) in the following:

(1)

(X,B)
is lc generically over
Z
;

(2)

K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
over
Z
;

(3)

the horizontal part
B^{h}
of
B
with respect to
f
is generically a boundary over
Z
.

But in this paper we always assume
0
-contractions also have property (3).

Definition 2.17
.

Under the same setting as in Definition
2.16
, we have two divisors on
Z
naturally related to
B
. One is called the
divisorial part of adjunction

B_{\operatorname{div}}
of
f
, which is uniquely determined by this
0
-contraction; the other is the
moduli part of adjunction

B_{\operatorname{mod}}
of
f
, which is defined up to
\sim_{\mathbb{R}}
. For convenience we’ll also use the b-divisor form
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
in this section. For explicit constructions of these two divisors, we refer to
[
16
, Section 7.2]
or
[
11
, 7.2]
. The divisors
B_{\operatorname{div}}
and
B_{\operatorname{mod}}
satisfy the following
canonical bundle formula
:

K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{\operatorname{div}}+B_{\operatorname{mod}}).

Definition 2.18
.

For a
0
-contraction
f:(X,B)\to Z
, we call a positive integer
I
its
adjunction index
if

(1)

\mathbb{K}_{X}+\mathbb{B}\sim_{I}f^{*}(\mathbb{K}_{Z}+\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{div}}+\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}})
, in particular,
K_{X}+B\sim_{I}f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{\operatorname{div}}+B_{\operatorname{mod}})
;

(2)

I\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is b-Cartier and
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is defined modulo
\sim_{I}
, in particular,
\mathbb{K}_{X}+\mathbb{B}\sim_{I,Z}f^{*}(\mathbb{K}_{Z}+\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{div}})
.

Actually, (2) implies (1). See
[
16
, 7.3]
for this result and more details.

A well-known result about the moduli part
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.19
.

(
[
16
, Theorem 8]
). Let
f:(X,B)\to Z
be a
0
-contraction as in Definition
2.16
, and
B
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor generically over
Z
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is a b-
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor of
Z
, defined up to a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalence. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is b-nef.

Proposition
2.20
below guarantees us that we can pull back
n
-components on the base
Z
back to
X
along a
0
-contraction
f:(X,B)\to Z
if
n
is divisible by the adjunction index
I
.

Proposition 2.20
.

(

2.9.
Known Boundedness Results

Now we are ready to restate two known results for a certain class of pairs
(X/Z,B)
where
X/Z
is of Fano type and
B
has hyperstandard multiplicities. We’ll use their special versions for surface pairs in the proof of the main Theorem
1.4
later. To begin with, we state the following result about the existence of adjunction index for such pairs:

Theorem 2.26
.

(
[
16
, Theorem 9]
). Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite set of rational numbers in
[0,1]
and
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
I=I(d,\mathfrak{R})
such that, for any contraction
f:(X,B)\to Z
with

(1)

f
is a 0-contraction;

(2)

X
is of Fano type over
Z
with
\dim X=d
;

(3)

the coefficients of
B^{h}
are in
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
.

Then
f
has an adjunction index
I
.

Corollary 2.27
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite set of rational numbers in
[0,1]
. Then there exists a natural number
I=I(\mathfrak{R})
such that, for any 0-contraction
f:(X,B)\to Z
from a surface to a curve with coefficients of
B^{h}
in
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
,
f
has an adjunction index
I
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a general fiber of
f
. By the adjunction formula
(K_{X}+B+F)|_{F}=K_{F}+B_{F}
. Taking degrees both sides,
0=(K_{X}+B+F).F\geq\deg K_{F}
, hence
F
is either rational or elliptic.

Case 1
: In the case
f
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration, we see
B_{F}\geq 0
and hence
B^{h}\neq 0
. We can then run a
K_{X}
-MMP over
Z
and get
g:(X,B)\to(X\,^{\prime},B\,^{\prime}=g_{*}B)
.
(X\,^{\prime},B\,^{\prime})
can’t be a minimal model, otherwise
-B\,^{\prime}\equiv K_{X\,^{\prime}}
is nef over
Z
, which contradicts that
B^{h}\neq 0
. Hence we get a Mori fiber space
X\,^{\prime}/Z
, and
X\,^{\prime}
is of Fano type over
Z
. We can then apply Theorem
2.26
to the 0-contraction
(X\,^{\prime},B\,^{\prime})\to Z
(
B\,^{\prime}=g_{*}B
also has coefficients in
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
) and conclude that it has an adjunction index
I_{1}=I(\mathfrak{R})
. Since
K_{X}+B=g^{*}(K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime})
is crepant,
I_{1}
is also an adjunction index for
(X,B)\to Z
.

Case 2
: In the case
f
is an elliptic fibration,
B^{h}=0
. Then Proposition
2.25
concludes that
f
has an adjunction index
I_{2}=12
.

Combining the two cases above, we can take
I=I(\mathfrak{R})=lcm(I_{1},I_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 2.28
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 5.5]
,
[
1
, Proposition 6.3]
) Assumptions the same as in Corollary
2.27
. Then there exists another finite set
\mathfrak{S}\subset[0,1]
of rational numbers depending only on

such that the discriminant

on

has coefficients in
\Phi(\mathfrak{S})
.

Remark 7
.

When the relative dimension of

to all cases without the assumption

is of Fano type. Also see

Now let’s recall the index conjecture for pairs

, which is established as Theorem

below in the case

Theorem 2.29
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 1.8]
). Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite set of rational numbers in
[0,1]
and
d
be a positive integer. Then there exists a positive integer
n=n(d,\mathfrak{R})
such that, for any pair
(X/Z\ni o,B)
and a contraction
f:(X,B)\to Z
with

(1)

(X,B)
is lc of dimension

;

(2)

;

(3)

the coefficients of

(4)

-(K_{X}+B)

There is an
n
-complement
(X/Z\ni o,B^{+})
of
(X/Z\ni o,B)
with

. Moreover, this complement is also an
mn
-complement for any positive integer

Theorem 2.30
.

(
[
5
, Proposition 5.3]
,
[
11
, Corollary 1.11]
). Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a finite set of rational numbers in
[0,1]
. Then there exists a positive integer
I=I(\mathfrak{R})
with the following. If
(X,B)
is a surface pair such that

(1)

(X,B)
is lc;

(2)

K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
;

(3)

the coefficients of
B
are in
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
.

Then
I(K_{X}+B)\sim 0
.

Proof.

By taking a dlt modification (Proposition
2.11
), we can suppose
(X,B)
is dlt. Then we have the following cases:

Case 1
:
B=0
and
X
has canonical singularities. We take a minimal resolution
g:Y\to X
. Then
K_{Y}-\sum a_{i}E_{i}=g^{*}K_{X}
where
E_{i}
are
g
-exceptional prime divisors. On one hand,
a_{i}\geq 0
since
X
has canonical singularities; on the other hand, since
K_{Y}
is nef over
X
, by Negativity Lemma
a_{i}\leq 0
. Hence
a_{i}=0
and
K_{Y}=g^{*}K_{X}
. We can then assume
X
is smooth. In this case we can quote the classical result that
12K_{X}\sim 0
if
K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
and set
I_{1}=12
.

Case 2
:
B=0
and
X
has klt singularities. By the classification of log terminal surface singularities
[
10
, Proposition 4.18]
,
X
has quotient singularities and the existence of an index
n
follows. A classical result from
[
4
]
has that
nK_{X}\sim 0
for some
n\leq 21
in this case. Hence for our result we can take
I_{2}=21!
.

Case 3
:
B\neq 0
. Run
K_{X}
-MMP. Let
h:X\to X\,^{\prime}
be the composite of all contractions and
B\,^{\prime}=h_{*}B
. Note
B\,^{\prime}
also has coefficients in
\Phi(\mathfrak{R})
. If
X\,^{\prime}
is a minimal model,
K_{X\,^{\prime}}
is nef,
B\,^{\prime}\geq 0
and
K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
. Hence
B\,^{\prime}=0
and by the previous case,
K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}
has an index
I_{2}
.

If
X\,^{\prime}\to T
is a Mori fiber space and
T
is a curve, from our assumption we see that
f:(X\,^{\prime},B\,^{\prime})\to T
is a 0-contraction, hence by Corollary
2.27

f
has an adjunction index
J=J(\mathfrak{R})
and

K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}\sim_{J}f^{*}(K_{T}+B\,^{\prime}_{T}).

Here
B\,^{\prime}_{T}=B\,^{\prime}_{\operatorname{div}}+B\,^{\prime}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is taken effective. By Lemma
2.28
,
B\,^{\prime}_{\operatorname{div}}
has coefficients in some hyperstandard set
\Phi(\mathfrak{S})
and since
JB\,^{\prime}_{\operatorname{mod}}
is integral,
B\,^{\prime}_{\operatorname{mod}}
has coefficients of the type
i/J
for
i=0,1,...,J
. Hence by adding
i/J
,
i=1,2,...,J-1
to
\mathfrak{S}
we get
\mathfrak{R}\,^{\prime}
and the coefficients of
B\,^{\prime}_{T}
are in the hyperstandard set
\Phi(\mathfrak{R}\,^{\prime})
, which depends only on
\mathfrak{R}
. Note
\deg K_{T}\leq 0
,
T
is of Fano type and
K_{T}+B\,^{\prime}_{T}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
. We can then apply Theorem
2.29
to
(T,B\,^{\prime}_{T})
to conclude that it has an index
N
,
N
depends only on
\mathfrak{R}
. Then take
I_{3}:=\operatorname{lcm}(N,J)
, we have
I_{3}(K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime})\sim f^{*}(I_{3}(K_{T}+B\,^{\prime}_{T}))\sim 0
,
I_{3}
is an index for
K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}
, and
I_{3}
depends only on
\mathfrak{R}
.

If
T
is a point,
X\,^{\prime}
is klt log del Pezzo and
\rho(X\,^{\prime})=1
, in particular
X\,^{\prime}
has Fano type. Then by Theorem
2.29
,
K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime}
has an index
I_{4}=I_{4}(\mathfrak{R})
.

Finally, since
K_{X}+B=h^{*}(K_{X\,^{\prime}}+B\,^{\prime})
is crepant, an index
I
for

is also an index for
K_{X}+B
. We can then take our

in the theorem to be the least common multiple of

,

. Notice that all

depend only on

, hence so does

.
∎

Remark 8
.

As in the theorem, we call

an

for the 0-pair
(X/Z\ni o,B)
if

.

Remark 9
.

It is known that the index conjecture holds true for non-klt 0-pairs

when

(
[

). Using this result, we can avoid the use of Theorem

Remark 10
.

The index conjecture is widely expected to be true in arbitrary dimensions, especially in the case

is not klt. The klt case is much more difficult and remains a mystery even in dimension 3. By Theorem

, the index conjecture is true in the case

3.
Boundedness for Curves

3.1.
Classification of
\mathbb{R}
-complementary curves

To find
n
-complements for a surfaces pair
(X,B)
, a natural thought is to lift
n
-complements of the restriction of the pair on some curves. Hence we need a boundedness result of
n
-complements for certain kinds of curves
C
. In view of Connectedness Lemma
2.7
, we focus on the case
C
is a connected complete nodal curve. Proposition
3.1
below gives a complete classification of
\mathbb{R}
-complementary curve pairs
(C,B)
in this case.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(C,B)
be a pair, where
C
is a connected complete nodal curve and
B
is a boundary. If
(C,B)
has an
\mathbb{R}
-complement, then one of the following occurs:

(1)

C
is a nonsingular rational curve and
\deg B\leq 2
;

(2)

C
is a nonsingular elliptic curve and
B=0
;

(3)

C
is an irreducible rational curve with only one nodal singularity and
B=0
;

(4)

C=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
is a cycle of smooth rational curves
C_{i}
, namely
C_{i}
are the irreducible components and
C_{i}.C_{j}=1
if
|i-j|=1
or
\{i,j\}=\{1,n\}
,
C_{i}.C_{j}=0
otherwise. And
B=0
;

(5)

C=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
is a chain of smooth rational curves
C_{i}
, namely
C_{i}
are the irreducible components and
C_{i}.C_{j}=1
if
|i-j|=1
,
C_{i}.C_{j}=0
otherwise. If
B_{i}
is the restriction of
B
on
C_{i}
, then
\deg{B_{1}}\leq 1
,
\deg{B_{n}}\leq 1
and
B_{i}=0
otherwise.

We need a lemma before the proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
C
is a connected complete curve with only nodal singularities and
C_{i}
are the irreducible components. Consider the composition
\pi:\tilde{C}_{i}\to C_{i}\to C
where
\tilde{C}_{i}\to C_{i}
are the normalization of
C_{i}
, then we have

K_{\tilde{C}_{i}}=\pi^{*}K_{C}-\sum P_{j}.

Here
P_{j}
are all the points on
\tilde{C}_{i}
mapped to singularities of
C
.

Proof.

By the existence of an
\mathbb{R}
-complement
(C,B^{+})
we have
K_{C}+B^{+}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
and
B^{+}\geq B\geq 0
. Taking degree both sides, we have
\deg{K_{C}}\leq 0
. Moreover, since
B^{+}
is supported on the smooth locus of
C
,
B^{+}
doesn’t affect singularities and we can assume
(C,0)
is lc.

Now assume
C
is nonsingular. Then
\deg{K_{C}}=2g(C)-2\leq 0
,
g(C)\leq 1
. If
g(C)=1
,
K_{C}\sim 0
,
B=B^{+}=0
and we get case 1). If
C
is rational,
K_{C}+B^{+}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
is equivalent to
\deg{B^{+}}=2
. Existence of such
B^{+}
is equivalent to
\deg B\leq 2
and we get case 2).

If
C
is singular and irreducible, consider its normalization
f:\tilde{C}\to C
. By Lemma
3.2
we have
f^{*}K_{C}=K_{\tilde{C}}+\sum P_{j}
. Since
\deg{K_{C}}\leq 0
,
\deg{(K_{\tilde{C}}+\sum P_{j})}=2g(\tilde{C})-2+l\leq 0
. Here
l
is the number of points on
\tilde{C}
mapped to singularities of
C
. Suppose
C
has
r
nodal singularities, then
l=2r\leq 2
,
r=1
and
\tilde{C}
is rational. As a result
K_{C}\sim 0
,
B=B^{+}=0
and this is case 3).

For the rest of cases
C
is reducible. For
\pi_{i}:\tilde{C}_{i}\to C
defined in Lemma
3.2
, we have
\deg{(K_{\tilde{C}_{i}}+\sum P_{j})}=2g(\tilde{C}_{i})-2+l_{i}\leq 0
, where
l_{i}
is the number of points on
\tilde{C}_{i}
mapped to singularities of
C
. There are at least one singularity of
C
lying on
C_{i}
by the connectedness assumption, so
l_{i}\geq 1
. Hence
g(\tilde{C}_{i})=0
and
l_{i}\leq 2
. If
C_{i}
has a nodal singularity itself, we’ll get
l_{i}\geq 3
and contradiction. Hence
C_{i}
are rational and smooth for all
i
. Now
l_{i}\leq 2
means every
C_{i}
has at most 2 intersections with other
C_{j}
. Starting with
C_{1}
, we can rearrange
C_{i}
such that
C_{i}
intersects
C_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. If
C_{n}
intersects
C_{1}
, then
l_{i}=2
for all
i
, and
K_{C_{i}}+\sum P_{j}+B^{+}_{i}\sim 0
implies
B_{i}=B^{+}_{i}=0
, hence
B=0
. This is case 4). Otherwise
C_{1}
doesn’t meet
C_{n}
,
l_{1}=l_{n}=1
and
l_{i}=0
for
2\leq i\leq n-1
. By the same reasoning
B_{i}=0
for
2\leq i\leq n-1
. For
i=1
or
n
,
K_{C_{i}}+\sum P_{j}+B^{+}_{i}\sim 0
is equivalent to
\deg{B^{+}_{i}}=1
, so
\deg{B_{i}}\leq 1
. This is case 5).
∎

Lemma 3.3

For arbitrary n real numbers

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose we have a continuous map

||\frac{\lambda(e)}{||\lambda(e)||}-\frac{\lambda(e\,^{\prime})}{||\lambda(e\,^{\prime})||}||<\epsilon
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose we have

as in Definition

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(I,v,e,\epsilon,d,c_{1},...,c_{m})
such that the following holds:

(1)

\mathcal{N}
consists of positive integers under complementary restrictions with respect to

(2)

For

satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{ij}\leq c_{i}
and
b_{ij}\in[0,1]
for
i=1,2,...,m
,
j=1,2,...,d
, there exists an
n\in\mathcal{N}
such that
\sum^{d}_{j=1}b_{ij}^{[n]}\leq c_{i}
for
i=1,2,...,m
.

Proof.

Consider the following subset of
\mathbb{R}^{md}
:

S:=\{(B_{1},...,B_{m})|\sum^{d}_{j=1}b_{ij}\leq c_{i},b_{ij}\in[0,1],i=1,2,...,m,j=1,2,...,d\}.

is then compact. For every point

in

we will define an open neighborhood
U_{B}
of
B
and an integer

satisfying the complementary restrictions, such that for any point
(A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{m})\in U_{B}
and this

for

. The sets

form an open cover of

\cup U_{B_{q}}

We fix a point

. Without loss of generality we can assume
b_{1j}<1
if

and
b_{1j}=1
otherwise. Then we set the positive constants
\delta_{1}=\min_{j\leq r_{1}}\{1-b_{1j},1/(2r_{1})\}
and
\delta_{i}
similarly; in the special case

let
\delta_{i}=\infty
. Define
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{l+md}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
(b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{l+md})\mapsto(b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{l})
to be the projection onto first

and choose some direction

of the rational affine span

such that
\lambda(e\,^{\prime})=e
. For this

,

and
\epsilon
we have a

. Since we can make this

. Now we can choose some
n
under complementary restrictions with respect to
I\,^{\prime},u,e\,^{\prime},\delta
where
I\,^{\prime}=lcm(I,q_{i})
and
q_{i}
are the denominators of

. Then we have an approximation
u_{n}=(v_{n},B_{n})
(

Notation 2

For any divisor

Lemma 4.1

Let

Proof.

If

and use induction on the number of components of
D_{\leq 0}
. Since

is contractible, by negative definiteness we have
(D_{\leq 0})^{2}<0

(K+S).E\leq(K+S+D_{\geq 0}).E\leq D_{\leq 0}.E<0.

Since

-curve and

Now let

be the contraction of

and

on

Here the total transform

and the exceptional part

Proposition 5.1

After a log terminal modification (Proposition

of

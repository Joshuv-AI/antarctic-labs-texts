Bridgeland Stability Conditions on Fano Threefolds
Marcello Bernardara, Emanuele Macrì, Benjamin Schmidt, Xiaolei Zhao

Abstract
We show the existence of Bridgeland stability conditions on all Fano threefolds, by proving a modified version of a conjecture by Bayer, Toda, and the second author. The key technical ingredient is a strong Bogomolov inequality, proved recently by Chunyi Li. Additionally, we prove the original conjecture for some toric threefolds by using the toric Frobenius morphism.



Keywords.
Stability conditions, Derived categories, Fano threefolds

Résumé.

Nous établissons l’existence de conditions de stabilité de Bridgeland sur toute variété de Fano de dimension 3 en montrant une version modifiée d’une conjecture de Bayer, Toda et du deuxième auteur. L’ingrédient technique essentiel est une inégalité forte de Bogomolov, démontrée récemment par Chunyi Li. En outre, nous montrons la conjecture originale pour certaines variétés toriques de dimension 3 via l’utilisation du morphisme de Frobenius torique.

1.
Introduction

In the recent article
[
Li15
]
, Chunyi Li proved the existence of Bridgeland stability conditions on Fano threefolds of Picard number 1. The purpose of this paper is to extend Li’s method to any Fano threefold, when we choose the polarization to be the anti-canonical divisor.

Tilt stability was introduced in
[
BMT14
]
as an intermediate notion to define Bridgeland stability on threefolds.
Its definition mimics Bridgeland’s original construction in the surface case
[
Bri08
,
AB13
]
.
Let
X
be a smooth projective complex threefold, and let
H
be a polarization on
X
. Also, let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
.
The usual notion of slope stability (with respect to
H
) induces a family of torsion pairs on the category of coherent sheaves
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
, parameterized by
\beta
, roughly obtained by truncating Harder-Narasimhan filtrations at the slope
\beta
.
We denote the corresponding family of tilted abelian categories by
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
.
Tilt stability is the (weak) stability condition defined on
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
with respect to the slope

\nu_{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}}{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}},

where, as usual, dividing by
0
equals to
+\infty
.
Here
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}
denotes the twisted Chern character
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits\cdot e^{-\beta H}
.
We will review all of this in Section
2.
.

The intuition is that tilt stability on threefolds should play the role of usual slope stability of sheaves on surfaces.
By keeping this idea in mind, we should look for a generalization of the classical Bogomolov inequality for slope stable sheaves on surfaces to tilt stability.
This is exactly the content of our main theorem.
An analogous result, with a similar proof, appears independently in
[
Piy16
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective Fano threefold of index
i_{X}
and let
H=-\frac{K_{X}}{i_{X}}
.
There exists a cycle
\Gamma\in A_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
and, for any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E
with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)\leq 0.

In
[
BMT14
]
(see also
[
BMS16
]
) a version of Theorem
1.
was conjectured without the class
\Gamma
. That original conjecture turned out to be imprecise in the case of the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at a point
[
Sch16
]
.
Therefore, modifying the conjecture in
[
BMT14
]
by adding
\Gamma
seems like a necessary step to understand Bridgeland stability on threefolds.
In fact, Theorem
1.
does still imply existence of Bridgeland stability conditions
[
Bri07
]
on all Fano threefolds exactly as in
[
BMT14
]
. Indeed, similarly to what was done in
[
BMS16
]
, we get the following quadratic inequality (see Proposition
2.
):

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\,\left(\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)+3\,\frac{\Gamma\cdot H}{H^{3}}(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\,(H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E))(2\,H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E)-3\,\Gamma\cdot H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))

\displaystyle-6(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))\geq 0,

for any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E
, where

\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))(H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E)).

When the Picard rank of
X
is
1
, Theorem
1.
was proved in
[
Li15
]
with
\Gamma=0
(the cases of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and of the quadric threefold were proved earlier, with a completely different proof, in

]

[
Sch14
]
). The idea of the proof of Theorem

[

The key result is a slight generalization of
[

to the higher Picard rank case.
This is the content of Theorem

, whose proof will take the whole Section

.

There are examples in which the class
\Gamma
is indeed
0
, even if the Picard number is greater than
1
(see Theorem

). More generally, it would be interesting to know what the optimal class
\Gamma
is.
For example, all known counter-examples (
[

]
) to the original conjecture from
[

]
on Fano threefolds do satisfy a stronger inequality for some choice of

It is interesting to observe that when such a strong version of the conjecture holds, then all possible applications of such an inequality
either to the birational geometry of threefolds or to counting invariants (pointed out in a sequence of papers; e.g.,
[

Finally, we remark that the original conjecture is also proved for abelian threefolds (and their étale quotients) in

In the case of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

Let

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)\leq 0.

It holds for all polarizations on

On the blow-up of

2.
Background on tilt stability

In
[
BMT14
]
the notion of tilt stability was introduced as an auxiliary notion in between slope stability and Bridgeland stability on threefolds. Let
X
be a smooth projective threefold over the complex numbers and
H
be a fixed ample divisor on
X
. The classical slope for a coherent sheaf
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
is defined as

\mu(E):=\frac{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)}{H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)},

where division by zero is interpreted as
+\infty
. As usual a coherent sheaf
E
is called
slope-(semi)stable
if for any non trivial proper subsheaf
F\subset E
the inequality
\mu(F)<(\leq)\mu(E/F)
holds.

Let
\beta
be an arbitrary real number. Then the twisted Chern character
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}
is defined to be
e^{-\beta H}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits
. Explicitly:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}-\beta H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}-\beta H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0},

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}-\beta H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}-\frac{\beta^{3}}{6}H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}.

The process of tilting is used to construct a new heart of a bounded t-structure. For more information on the general theory of tilting we refer to
[
HRS96
]
and
[
BVdB03
]
. A torsion pair is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X):\text{any quotient $E\twoheadrightarrow G$ satisfies $\mu(G)>\beta$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\beta}

\displaystyle:=\{E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X):\text{any subsheaf $F\subset E$ satisfies $\mu(F)\leq\beta$}\}.

The heart of a bounded t-structure is given as the extension closure
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X):=\langle\mathcal{F}_{\beta}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\rangle
. Let
\alpha>0
be a positive real number. The tilt slope is defined as

\nu_{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{0}}{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{1}}.

As before, an object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
is called
tilt-(semi)stable
(or
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-(semi)stable
) if for any non trivial proper subobject
F\subset E
the inequality
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(F)<(\leq)\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E/F)
holds.

Any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
satisfies

\displaystyle\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)

\displaystyle=(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))(H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E))

\displaystyle=(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E))^{2}-2(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E))(H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E))\geq 0.

Let
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
be the image of the map
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}
. Notice that
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
factors through
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}
. Varying
(\alpha,\beta)
changes the set of stable objects. A
numerical wall
in tilt stability with respect to a class
v\in\Lambda
is a non trivial proper subset
W
of the upper half plane given by an equation of the form
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(v)=\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(w)
for another class
w\in\Lambda
. A subset
S
of a numerical wall
W
is called an
actual wall
if the set of semistable objects with class
v
changes at
S
. The structure of walls in tilt stability is rather simple. Part (1) - (5) is usually called Bertram’s Nested Wall Theorem and appeared first in
[
Mac14a
]
, while part (6), (7), and (8) are in Lemma 2.7 and Appendix A of
[
BMS16
]
. The last part of (8) about reflexivity is to be found in
[
LM16
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Let
v\in\Lambda
be a fixed class. All numerical walls in the following statements are with respect to
v
.

1.

Numerical walls in tilt stability are either semicircles with center on the
\beta
-axis or rays parallel to the
\alpha
-axis.

2.

If two numerical walls given by classes
w,u\in\Lambda
intersect, then
v
,
w
and
u
are linearly dependent. In particular, the two walls are completely identical.

3.

The curve
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(v)=0
is given by a hyperbola. Moreover, this hyperbola intersects all semicircular walls at their top point.

4.

If
v_{0}\neq 0
, there is exactly one numerical vertical wall given by
\beta=v_{1}/v_{0}
. If
v_{0}=0
, there is no actual vertical wall.

5.

If a numerical wall has a single point at which it is an actual wall,
then all of it is an actual wall.

6.

If there is an actual wall numerically defined by an exact sequence of tilt semistable objects
0\to F\to E\to G\to 0
such that
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(E)=v
, then

\overline{\Delta}_{H}(F)+\overline{\Delta}_{H}(G)\leq\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if either
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(F)=0
,
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(G)=0
, or both
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=0
and
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(F)
,
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(G)
, and
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{\leq 2}(E)
are all proportional.

7.

If
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=0
for a tilt semistable object
E
, then
E
can only be destabilized at the unique numerical vertical wall.

8.

If
E
is a tilt stable object for fixed
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\alpha\gg 0
, then
E
is one of the following.

•

If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
, then
E
is a slope semistable sheaf.

•

If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)<0
, then
H^{0}(E)
is a torsion sheaf supported in dimension smaller than or equal to
1
and
H^{-1}(E)
is a reflexive slope semistable sheaf with positive rank.

A generalized Bogomolov type inequality involving third Chern characters for tilt semistable objects with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}=0
has been conjectured in
[
BMT14
]
. Its main goal was the construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on arbitrary threefolds.

For any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
the inequality

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{3}(E)\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{\beta}_{1}(E)

holds.

The conjecture has been proved for
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in
[
Mac14b
]
and for the smooth quadric hypersurface
Q\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
in
[
Sch14
]
. All other Fano threefolds of Picard rank one were handled in
[
Li15
]
. Finally, it is known to hold for abelian threefolds with independent proofs in
[
BMS16
]
and
[
MP15
,
MP16
]
. It turns out that the conjecture fails on the blow up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a single point as shown in
[
Sch16
]
. In this article we give an affirmative answer to the following natural follow up question in case
X
is a Fano threefold and the polarization
H
is given by the anticanonical divisor.

Is there a cycle
\Gamma\in A_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
depending at most on
H
such that
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
and for any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E
with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)\leq\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)?

The condition
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
is crucial for the reduction to the case
\alpha=0
. In order to state it precisely, we first need the notion of
\overline{\beta}
-stability.

For any object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
, we define

\overline{\beta}(E)=\begin{cases}\frac{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)-\sqrt{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}}{H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)}&\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\neq 0,\\
\frac{H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E)}{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)}&\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)=0.\end{cases}

Moreover, we say that
E
is
\overline{\beta}
-(semi)stable
, if it is (semi)stable in a neighborhood of
(0,\overline{\beta}(E))
.

By this definition we have
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\overline{\beta}(E)}(E)=0
.

Assume
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
is
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-semistable with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\neq\infty
. Then there is a
\nu_{\alpha,-\beta}
-semistable object
\tilde{E}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{-\beta}(X)
and a sheaf
T
supported in dimension
0
together with a triangle

\tilde{E}\to\mathop{\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om}\nolimits(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})[1]\to T[-1]\to\tilde{E}[1].

The following lemma was first proved for the original conjecture in
[
BMS16
]
, but it works out in our case as well without any change in the proof.

Let
\Gamma\in A_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
be a cycle such that
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
. Assume that for any
\overline{\beta}
-stable object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
with
\overline{\beta}(E)\in[0,1)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
the inequality

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\leq\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)

holds. Then Question
2.
has an affirmative answer with this cycle
\Gamma
.

By Proposition
2.
we can use the derived dual to reduce to the case
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
. Tensoring with lines bundles
\mathcal{O}(aH)
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}
makes it possible to further reduce to
\overline{\beta}(E)\in[0,1)
.

A straightforward computation shows that

\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)-\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)

is decreasing along the hyperbola
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
as
\alpha
decreases, because
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
. By using Theorem
2.
, (6), we can then proceed by induction on
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)
to show that it is enough to prove the inequality for
\overline{\beta}
-stable objects.

Indeed, if
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=0
, then
E
is
\overline{\beta}
-stable implying the claim.
Assume
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)>0
. If
E
is
\overline{\beta}
-stable, we are done. Otherwise,
E
is destabilized along a wall between
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(0,\overline{\beta}(E))
. Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
be the stable factors of
E
along this wall. By induction, the inequality holds for
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
and so it does for
E
by linearity of the Chern character.

\Box

As in
[
BMS16
]
, we get a quadratic inequality for any tilt semistable object. This still implies the support property for Bridgeland stability conditions, as in
[
BMS16
, Section 8]
.

Assume that Question 2.4 has an affirmative answer.
Then any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E
satisfies

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\,\left(\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)+3\,\frac{\Gamma\cdot H}{H^{3}}(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\,(H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E))(2\,H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E)-3\,\Gamma\cdot H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E))

\displaystyle-6(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E))\geq 0.

Observe that if
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
, then
Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\geq 0
is equivalent to
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)\leq\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)+\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)
. Let
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
be a
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object with
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits(E)=v
.
If
(\alpha,\beta)
is on the unique numerical vertical wall for
v
, then
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)=0
,
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\beta}(E)\geq 0
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\beta}(E)\leq 0
. Therefore, since
\Gamma\cdot H\geq 0
, we have

Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\geq 0
.

As a consequence,
(\alpha,\beta)
lies on a unique numerical semicircular wall
W
with respect to
v
. One computes that there are
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow\delta\alpha^{2}+\delta\beta^{2}+x\beta+y\alpha\geq 0.

Moreover, the equality
Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
holds if and only if

\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=\nu_{\alpha,\beta}\left(H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E),2H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E)-3H\cdot\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E),3\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)-3\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E),0\right).

In particular, the equation
Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)\geq 0
defines either the complement of a semi-disc with center on the

\beta
-axis or a quadrant to one side of a vertical line. Moreover,
Q_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
is a numerical wall
with respect to
v
. Since numerical walls do not intersect, the inequality holds on either any or no point of
W
. Computing it at the top point of

3.
Li’s Bogomolov inequality

Let
X
be a Fano threefold of index
i_{X}
. Consider tilt stability with respect to the polarization
H=-\frac{K_{X}}{i_{X}}
. For any tilt semistable object
E
with
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\neq 0
, we define

\displaystyle\beta_{-}(E)

\displaystyle=\mu(E)-\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E))^{2}}}

\displaystyle\beta_{+}(E)

\displaystyle=\mu(E)+\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E))^{2}}}.

If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)>0
, we have
\beta_{-}(E)=\overline{\beta}
. If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)<0
, then
\beta_{+}(E)=\overline{\beta}
. Note that
\beta_{-}(E)\leq\beta_{+}(E)
are the two solutions to the equation
\nu_{0,\beta}(E)=0
. The main result of this section is a slight modification of
[
Li15
, Proposition 3.2]
beyond Picard rank one.

Let
E
be a tilt stable object other than a shift of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
or an ideal sheaf of points. If additionally
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\neq 0
and
0\leq\beta_{-}(E)\leq\beta_{+}(E)<1
, then the inequality

\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E))^{2}}\geq\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\nolimits\left\{\frac{1}{(H^{3})^{2}},\frac{3}{2i_{X}H^{3}}\right\}

holds.

Note, that when
X
has index
1
or
2
and its Picard rank is at least
2
, then
H^{3}\geq 2
, and

\mathop{\mathrm{min}}\nolimits\left\{\frac{1}{(H^{3})^{2}},\frac{3}{2i_{X}H^{3}}\right\}=\frac{1}{(H^{3})^{2}}.

That turns out to be the only case in which we use this theorem in the following sections.

The idea of the proof is as follows. Assuming there exists an object
E
contradicting the theorem, we will show that
E
can be chosen such that either
E
or
E[1]
is tilt stable for all
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. The conditions on
\beta_{\pm}(E)
will then allow to prove
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}(E,E)=0
. A contradiction can be obtained from estimating the Euler characteristic
\chi(E,E)\leq 1
. We will fill the details in a series of lemmas.

If
E
is a tilt stable object with rank
\pm 1
,
0\leq\beta_{-}(E)\leq\beta_{+}(E)<1
, and
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=0
, then
E
is a shift of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
or an ideal sheaf of points. That means
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
. In particular, Theorem
3.
holds for rank
\pm 1

The Hodge Index Theorem implies

H\cdot(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)^{2}-2\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E))\leq\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{H^{3}}=0

with equality if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
is numerically equivalent to a multiple of
H
. Part (7) of the Structure Theorem for Tilt Stability shows that
E
is stable for
\alpha\gg 0
, and therefore, part (8) applies. In any of the two cases,
E
satisfies the classical Bogomolov inequality and we get
H\cdot(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)^{2}-2\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E))=0
. In particular,
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
is numerically equivalent to a multiple of
H
.

If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)=-1
, then

is reflexive of rank one, i.e., a line bundle. Since line bundles have no extensions with skyscraper sheaves, we must have

. The hypotheses on
\beta_{\pm}(E)
directly imply

Assume

is an ideal sheaf of a subscheme of dimension smaller than or equal to one. Its second Chern character equals

\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)^{2}}{2}-\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E).

Since

is ample and it intersects this curve class as zero, we must have that

Again, the hypotheses on

directly imply the claim.

. Note that in this range we have

.

We will prove Theorem
1.
in this case with

It is not hard to compute

C_{0}=\frac{10\sqrt{30}}{1323}-\frac{3}{98}.

Assume we have an object

\overline{\beta}
-stable with

. Then Lemma

implies

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\frac{\overline{\beta}^{2}}{2}+C_{0}\right)H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-\frac{\overline{\beta}}{6}(7\overline{\beta}^{2}-1)\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)

\displaystyle=-\left(7\left(\frac{\overline{\beta}^{2}}{2}+C_{0}\right)\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{\left(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\right)^{2}}}+\frac{\overline{\beta}}{6}(7\overline{\beta}^{2}-1)\right)\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E),

where the second expression is only valid for
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\neq 0
and follows from the definition of
\overline{\beta}
. Since both

and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\geq 0

\overline{\beta}\geq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{7}}

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)=0

If

\Box

\Box

We are now in position to prove Theorem
1.

\overline{\beta}
-stable object with

In this situation Lemma

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)

\displaystyle\leq-f_{X,C_{0}}(\overline{\beta})H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-g_{X}(\overline{\beta})H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)

\displaystyle=-\left(f_{X,C_{0}}(\overline{\beta})\sqrt{\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{\left(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\right)^{2}}}+g_{X}(\overline{\beta})\right)H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E),

where the second expression is only valid for
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\neq 0
and follows from the definition of
\overline{\beta}
.
As in the index two case, we will show that the right hand side is non-positive. Since both

and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\geq 0
we are done if
\overline{\beta}\geq\beta_{0}
, or
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)=0
.
If
0<\overline{\beta}<\beta_{0}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\neq 0
we will split the two cases
\beta_{+}(E)\geq 1
and
\beta_{+}(E)<1
.

If
\beta_{+}(E)\geq 1
, we get

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\leq-h_{X,C_{0}}(\overline{\beta})H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\leq 0.

If
\beta_{+}(E)<1
, we can use Theorem
3.
to obtain

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\leq-l_{X,C_{0}}(\overline{\beta})H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}^{\overline{\beta}}(E)\leq 0.

We are left to deal with the case of a
\overline{\beta}
-stable object
E
with
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
and
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
.
If
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)=0
then, by Theorem
3.
,
E
is a shift of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
or an ideal of points. In those cases, Theorem
1.
is obvious.

Assume that there is a
\overline{\beta}
-stable object
E
satisfying
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
,
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)>{\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)}
, i.e.,
E
contradicts Theorem
1.
. Then
E
can be chosen such that additionally
\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
.

Pick a
\overline{\beta}
-stable object
E
such that
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
is minimal with the properties
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
,
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)>0
. Then
\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)\neq 0
, and equivalently
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)\neq 0
. If
E
could be chosen with
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)
arbitrarily large, the conditions of Theorem
3.
would be fulfilled. This would imply

\left(\frac{H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)}{H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)}\right)^{2}=\frac{\overline{\Delta}_{H}(E)}{(H^{3}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E))^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{(H^{3})^{2}}

even in the limit

such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)
is maximal for objects with these properties.

If
\mathop{\mathrm{Ext}}\nolimits^{1}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X})\neq 0
, we can get a non trivial triangle

E^{\prime}\to E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]\to E^{\prime}[1].

We want to show that
E^{\prime}
is in
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{0}(X)
, i.e., that the morphism
E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
is surjective. Let
T
be the cokernel of this map in
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{0}(X)
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]
is semistable, we get
\nu_{0,\alpha}(T)=\infty
. Since it is even stable, this means
T
a sheaf supported on points (this is because those are the only objects that are mapped to the origin by
Z_{0,\alpha}
). But then
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X}[1],T)=0
, a contradiction unless
T=0
.

We will show that
E^{\prime}
is also
\overline{\beta}
-stable to get a contradiction to the maximality of
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)
. Let

0=E_{0}\hookrightarrow E_{1}\hookrightarrow\ldots\hookrightarrow E_{n}=E^{\prime}

be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
E^{\prime}
for
\beta=0
and
\alpha\ll 1
. The fact that
E
is
\overline{\beta}
-stable and that
E^{\prime}\to E
is injective in
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{0}(X)
implies

\nu_{0,\alpha}(E^{\prime})\leq\nu_{0,\alpha}(E_{i})\leq\nu_{0,\alpha}(E).

Taking the limit
\alpha\to 0
implies
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(E_{i})=0
for all
i
. For every semistable factor
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
we choose a Jordan Hölder filtration and call the stable factors of all those filtrations
F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}
. Then
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}(F_{i})=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. The values
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(F_{i})-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{i})
add up to
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E^{\prime})-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E^{\prime})=\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)>0
. Choose
j
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(F_{j})>\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{j})
. By definition of
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{0}(X)
we have
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{i})\leq H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E^{\prime})=H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Assume
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{j})<H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
.
If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(F_{j})\geq 0
, then this contradicts the minimality of
E
.
If
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(F_{j})<0
, then we can use the derived dual via Proposition
2.
to reduce to the positive rank case.
Hence, we must have
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{j})=H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)
and
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(F_{i})=0
for

. Since
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}(E)\neq 0
, the existence of the morphism
F_{1}\hookrightarrow E
shows that

. But the slopes of the factors in the Harder-Narasimhan filtration are strictly decreasing. Therefore, we must have

and

is indeed
\overline{\beta}
-stable.

\Box

We can now finish the proof of Theorem
1.
in the case of a
\overline{\beta}
-stable object
E
with
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{0}(E)\geq 0
and
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
. Assume there is
E
as in Lemma

contradicting the theorem, i.e.,

\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}(\mathcal{O}(H),E)=\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{1}(E,\mathcal{O})=0.

By stability we have

as before.

5.
Toric threefolds

In this section, we use a variant of the method in
[
BMS16
]
to prove Conjecture
2.
in some toric (not necessarily Fano) cases, with respect to certain polarizations. The results presented here arose from discussions together with Arend Bayer and Paolo Stellari.

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex toric threefold.
Let
H
be an ample divisor such that, for all effective divisors
D
on
X
, we have
H\cdot D^{2}\geq 0
, and
H\cdot D^{2}=0
implies
that
D
is an extremal ray of the effective cone.
Then, for any
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable object
E\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta}(X)
with
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(E)=0
, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(E)\leq 0.

Since the statement of Theorem
5.
is independent of scaling
H
, we will assume throughout this section that
H
is primitive.
The extra condition on the extremality of divisors with
H\cdot D^{2}=0
is probably not necessary.
It is trivially satisfied by
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundles over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
For us, it will simplify the proof, since it directly implies (see e.g.,
[
CLS11
, Lemma 15.1.8]
) that a primitive such
D
is the class of an irreducible torus-invariant divisor.
Also, since
X
is a threefold, there cannot be more than
3
such irreducible torus-invariant divisors with the same class.
Indeed, if
D
is not movable, then there is only one irreducible divisor with that class. If
D
is movable, then since it is extremal in the effective cone, it cannot be big and so it induces a rational morphism on a lower dimensional toric variety of Picard rank
1
, namely
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

The positivity condition
H\cdot D^{2}\geq 0
is instead necessary in the proof.
As proved in
[
BMS16
, Corollary 3.11]
, this implies that all (shifts of) line bundles in
X
are
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
.

The assumptions are satisfied for any polarization on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
In the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a line, if we denote the pull-back of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1)
by
h
and the pull-back of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
by
f
, then the assumption is satisfied by any polarization of the form
ah+bf
, with
a,b>0
and
a\leq b
. The class of the anticanonical bundle is
3h+f
, and so it is not covered by the above result.

In order to prove Theorem
5.
, we use
the toric Frobenius and the formula from Theorem
5.A.
below as follows.
First, as in the previous sections, we can work only with
\overline{\beta}
-stable objects.
In the case where
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
, assuming
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)>0
, the Euler characteristic

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)
grows as a polynomial in
m
of degree
3
. On the other hand, by using adjointness, Theorem
5.A.
,
and stability of line bundles, we can bound both
\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)
and show
that
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)
has to grow at most as a polynomial in
m^{2}
, thus giving a contradiction to
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)>0
.
The basic idea is then to reduce to this case.
When
\overline{\beta}(E)
is an integer, this is easy to do by tensoring with line bundles. When it is a rational number, we pull-back again via the toric Frobenius morphism to simplify denominators.
Finally, the irrational case can be dealt with by elementary Dirichlet approximation.

5.A.
Toric Frobenius morphism

Let
X
be a smooth projective toric threefold.
We denote the irreducible torus-invariant divisors by
D_{\rho}
(corresponding to the rays
\rho
of the fan associated to
X
).
The canonical line bundle is then
\omega_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(-\sum_{\rho}D_{\rho}\right)
.

For an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the
toric Frobenius morphism
by

\underline{m}\colon X\to X.

It is defined via multiplication by
m
on the lattice and is a finite flat map of degree
m^{3}
. The main property we will need is the following result from
[
Tho00
]
on direct images of line bundles (see also
[
Ach15
]
for a short proof).

Let
D\in\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}\nolimits(X)
be a line bundle on
X
.
Then

\underline{m}_{*}D=\bigoplus_{j}\left(L_{j}^{\vee}\right)^{\oplus\eta_{j}},

where

•

the line bundles
L_{j}
are given by all the possible integral divisors in the formula

\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\frac{1}{m}\left(-D+\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}\right)\right),

where
a_{\rho}
varies in between
0,\ldots,m-1
,

•

the multiplicity
\eta_{j}
counts the number of
a_{\rho}
’s giving the same line bundle
L_{j}
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and let us denote the pull-backs of the hyperplane classes from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by
h
and
f
respectively, as before. There are five torus-invariant irreducible divisors, three of them lie in the class
h
and two of them in the class
f
.
It follows that

\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-f)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-h)^{\oplus r_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-h-f)^{\oplus r_{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-2h)^{\oplus r_{3}}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-2h-f)^{\oplus r_{4}},

where
r_{1},r_{3}
grow like
m^{2}
and
r_{2},r_{4}
grow like
m^{3}
.

Notice that Frobenius pull-back in general does not preserve
\overline{\beta}
-stability. Indeed, in the case of the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a point, we can consider
\mathcal{O}(h)
. It is not too hard to check that
\mathcal{O}(h)
is
\overline{\beta}
-stable, while for any
m\geq 2
, the pull-back

\underline{m}^{*}\mathcal{O}(h)=\mathcal{O}(mh)
is not
\overline{\beta}
-semistable (see
[
Sch16
]
and Section
6.
for more details).

5.B.
Proof of Theorem
5.
: the integral case

We use the statement in
[
BMS16
, Conjecture 5.3]
(or Lemma
2.
in the present note).
Let
E
be a
\overline{\beta}
-stable object.
In this section, we assume that
\overline{\beta}(E)\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By tensoring by multiples of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(H)
, we can assume that
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
.
We want to show
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)\leq 0
.

Assume the contrary
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)>0
.
By using the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch Theorem we can compute

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)=m^{3}\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}(E)+O(m^{2}).

If
\underline{m}^{*}E
were
\overline{\beta}
-semistable, we could easily get a contradiction.
Unfortunately, in general, it is not true, as remarked in Example
5.A.
.
But we can still use the push-forward
\underline{m}_{*}
and Theorem
5.A.
.
Indeed, by adjointness, we have

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)=\chi\left((\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee},E\right)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee},E\right)+\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}\left((\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee},E\right).

The last inequality follows since, given a line bundle
L
on
X
, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(L,E[i])=0
if
i<-1
or
i>3
.
We want to prove that the right hand side has order
\leq m^{2}
.
In the notation of Theorem
5.A.
, we have
\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\oplus(L_{j}^{\vee})^{\oplus\eta_{j}}
, where
L_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}D_{\rho})
.

First of all, since
H
is ample and
L_{j}
is effective, we have

H^{2}\cdot L_{j}\geq 0

and
H^{2}\cdot L_{j}=0
if and only if
L_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Hence,
L_{j}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta=0}(X)
, when
L_{j}\neq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and as remarked before, by
[
BMS16
]
, they are all
\nu_{\alpha,0}
-stable, for all
\alpha>0
.
If
H\cdot L_{j}^{2}>0
, then

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,0}(L_{j})>0=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,0}(E)

and so
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(L_{j},E)=0
.
If
H\cdot L_{j}^{2}=0
, then by our assumption
a_{\rho}=0
for all but at most
3
rays
\rho
. Hence,
\eta_{j}
has order at most
m^{2}
: there are at most three non-trivial coefficients
a_{\rho}
, with the property
0\leq a_{\rho}<m
, and related by a linear equation defining
L_{j}
in Theorem
5.A.
.
Summing up, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{m}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\vee},E\right)=\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X},E)+\sum_{j\,:\,H\cdot L_{j}^{2}=0}\eta_{j}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(L_{j},E)=O(m^{2}).

To bound the
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}
, by Serre duality, we have

\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}(L_{j},E)=\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(E,L_{j}\otimes\omega_{X}[1]).

Since
\omega_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\sum_{\rho}D_{\rho})
, we have

L_{j}^{\prime}:=L_{j}\otimes\omega_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\rho}(m-a_{\rho})D_{\rho}\right).

Since
0\leq a_{\rho}<m
, then
m-a_{\rho}>0
, for all
\rho
.
Therefore,
H^{2}\cdot L_{j}^{\prime}<0
and
H\cdot(L_{j}^{\prime})^{2}>0
.
Hence,
L_{j}^{\prime}[1]\in\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\beta=0}(X)
and

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,0}(L_{j}^{\prime}[1])<0=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,0}(E).

Again by stability of
L_{j}^{\prime}[1]
we have

\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(E,L_{j}\otimes\omega_{X}[1])=0

for all
j
.
It follows that

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}E)=O(m^{2}),

giving the required contradiction.

5.C.
Proof of Theorem
5.
: the rational case

In this section, we assume that
\overline{\beta}(E)\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
.
We write
\overline{\beta}(E)=\frac{p}{q}
, with
p
and
q
coprime,
q>0
.
We want to show that
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{p/q}(E)\leq 0
.
As before, by the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch Theorem, we have

\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}\left(\underline{q}^{*}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-pH)\right)\right)=m^{3}q^{3}\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{p/q}(E)+O(m^{2}),

and, by adjointness,

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{m}^{*}\left(\underline{q}^{*}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-pH)\right)\right)=\chi\left((\underline{mq}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mpH))^{\vee},E\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{mq}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mpH))^{\vee},E\right)+\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}\left((\underline{mq}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mpH))^{\vee},E\right).

By Theorem
5.A.
,

\underline{mq}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mpH)=\bigoplus_{j}\left(L_{j}^{\vee}\right)^{\oplus\eta_{j}},

where

L_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\frac{1}{mq}\left(mpH+\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}\right)\right),

and
0\leq a_{\rho}<mq
.
Therefore,

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{p/q}_{1}(L_{j})=L_{j}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(-\frac{p}{q}H\right)=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\frac{1}{mq}\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}D_{\rho}\right)

is an effective divisor and cannot be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
because
p/q
is not an integer.
It follows that
H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p/q}(L_{j})>0
for all
j
.
Moreover

H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{p/q}(L_{j})=\frac{1}{2}H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p/q}(L_{j})^{2}\geq 0.

As in the
\overline{\beta}(E)=0
case, the equality holds if and only if the corresponding
\eta_{j}
has order at most
m^{2}
, since
q
is constant.
If
H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{p/q}(L_{j})>0
, then

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,p/q}(L_{j})>0=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\nu_{\alpha,p/q}(E)

and so
\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(L_{j},E)=0
.
As in the integral case, this shows that

\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{mq}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mpH))^{\vee},E\right)=O(m^{2}).

The vanishing of
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}
follows as in the integral case, by using Serre duality and stability.

5.D.
Proof of Theorem
5.
: the irrational case

Finally, we assume that
\overline{\beta}(E)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
By assumption, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
E
is
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}
-stable for all
(\alpha,\beta)
in

V_{\epsilon}:=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,0<\alpha<\epsilon,\,\overline{\beta}(E)-\epsilon<\beta<\overline{\beta}(E)+\epsilon\right\}.

By the Dirichlet approximation theorem, there exists a sequence
\left\{\beta_{n}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of rational numbers such that

\left|\overline{\beta}(E)-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right|<\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}}<\epsilon

for all
n
, where
q_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
.

We compute, for
n\gg 0
,

\chi\left(\mathcal{O}_{X},\underline{q_{n}}^{*}E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H)\right)=q_{n}^{3}\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}(E)+O(q_{n}^{2})\geq q_{n}^{3}\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}(E)}(E)+O(q_{n}^{2}).

The last inequality follows since, by definition,
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(E)
has a local minimum at
\beta=\overline{\beta}(E)
.

As in the previous cases, we assume for a contradiction that
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}(E)}(E)>0
, and we want to bound

\chi\left((\underline{q_{n}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H))^{\vee},E\right)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{q_{n}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H))^{\vee},E\right)+\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}\left((\underline{q_{n}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H))^{\vee},E\right)

for
n\gg 0
.
By Theorem
5.A.
,

\underline{q_{n}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H)=\bigoplus_{j}\left(\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)^{\vee}\right)^{\oplus\eta_{j}^{(n)}},

where

L_{j}^{(n)}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n}}\left(p_{n}H+\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)\right),

and
0\leq a_{\rho}^{(n)}<q_{n}
.
Notice that, since
L_{j}^{(n)}
is an integral divisor,
\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
and
\frac{a_{\rho}^{(n)}}{q_{n}}
are both universally bounded with respect to
n
, there is only a finite number of isomorphism classes of
L_{j}^{(n)}
for all
n
.

We have that

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits^{p_{n}/q_{n}}_{1}(L_{j}^{(n)})=L_{j}^{(n)}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}H\right)=\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n}}\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)

is an effective divisor, and cannot be
\mathcal{O}_{X}
for
n\gg 0
.
Since
\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}
is an integral divisor,
we have

H^{2}\cdot\sum_{\rho}a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\geq 1,

so that

H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)\geq\frac{1}{q_{n}}.

Now,

H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)=H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)+H^{3}\left(\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}-\overline{\beta}\right)\geq\frac{1}{q_{n}}-\frac{H^{3}}{q_{n}^{2}}>0

for
n\gg 0
. Therefore
L_{j}^{(n)}
belongs to
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits^{\overline{\beta}}(X)
for
n\gg 0
.
By definition,

\displaystyle H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=H\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)+\left(\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}-\overline{\beta}\right)H\right)^{2}

\displaystyle>H\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}-\overline{\beta}\right)H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{q_{n}^{3}}\,H\cdot\left(q_{n}\left(\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}-2H\cdot\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right).

If

f_{n,j}:=H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{2}^{\overline{\beta}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}H\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\overline{\beta}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)^{2}>0,

we can argue as in the previous cases to show that

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(L_{j}^{(n)},E)=0
by stability. Since
q_{n}\to\infty
, we
can have
f_{n,j}\leq 0
only if the ratio

\frac{2H^{2}\cdot\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}}{H\cdot\left(\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}}

is not bounded from above for
n\gg 0
, since
H\cdot\left(\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}\geq 0
by assumption.
But

2H^{2}\cdot\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\leq K\cdot\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)},

for a constant
K>0
which is independent on
n
and
j
, and

H\cdot\left(\sum a_{\rho}^{(n)}D_{\rho}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\rho,\tau}a_{\rho}^{(n)}a_{\tau}^{(n)}(H\cdot D_{\rho}\cdot D_{\tau}).

Fix
\rho_{0}
such that
a_{\rho_{0}}^{(n)}\neq 0
.
Then

a_{\rho_{0}}^{(n)}\leq\sum_{\tau}a_{\rho_{0}}^{(n)}a_{\tau}^{(n)}(H\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}\cdot D_{\tau})

unless
a_{\tau}^{(n)}=0
for all
\tau
for which
H\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}\cdot D_{\tau}\neq 0
. That is, we can have
a_{\tau}^{(n)}\neq 0
possibly only for
\tau
such that
H\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}\cdot D_{\tau}=0
.
By our assumption, the latter equality means that
D_{\tau}
has the same divisor class as
D_{\rho_{0}}
,
H\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}^{2}=0
.
Indeed, we can show first that
H\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}\cdot D_{\tau}=0
implies that
H\cdot D_{\tau}^{2}\leq 0
. Assume not and set
\lambda=\tfrac{H^{2}\cdot D_{\rho_{0}}}{H^{2}\cdot D_{\tau}}
. The Hodge Index Theorem says

0\geq H\cdot(D_{\rho_{0}}-\lambda D_{\tau})^{2}=\lambda^{2}H\cdot D_{\tau}^{2}>0,

a contradiction.

By the assumption in the theorem this implies
H\cdot D_{\tau}^{2}=0
. Thus, we get
H\cdot(D_{\tau}+D_{\rho_{0}})^{2}=0
. By the extremality assumption, we then have that
D_{\tau}
is in the same class as
D_{\rho_{0}}
.
It follows that
H\cdot\left(\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{p_{n}/q_{n}}\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)\right)^{2}=0
.

Moreover, as in the integral case, we can bound the number of torus-invariant divisors in the same class of such a
D_{\rho_{0}}
. Summing up, if
f_{n,j}\leq 0
, then the multiplicity
\eta_{j}^{(n)}
has order at most
q_{n}^{2}
.
Together with the finiteness of the isomorphism classes of

L_{j}^{(n)}
, we have that

\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits\left((\underline{q_{n}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p_{n}H))^{\vee},E\right)=\sum_{j\,:\,H.\left(L_{j}^{(n)}\right)^{2}=0}\eta_{j}^{(n)}\mathop{\mathrm{hom}}\nolimits(L_{j}^{(n)},E)=O(q_{n}^{2}).

As before, the vanishing of
\mathop{\mathrm{ext}}\nolimits^{2}
follows as in the integral case, by using Serre duality and stability.
This shows that
\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\overline{\beta}(E)}(E)\leq 0
also in this case, and therefore completes the proof of Theorem
5.
.

6.
Details about the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a point

Theorem
1.
implies the existence of Bridgeland stability conditions on all Fano threefolds. However, it would be interesting to know what the optimal class
\Gamma
is. A condition that is coherent with the case of Picard rank
1
would be
\Gamma\cdot H=0
.

In this section we will study the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a point more carefully. We use the notations for divisors on
X
which were introduced in Section
4.B.
. In
[
Sch16
]
, it was shown that the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(h)
does not satisfy Theorem
1.
with
\Gamma=0
.
In
[
Mar16
]
, it was shown that the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{e}
of the exceptional divisor also does not satisfy Theorem
1.
with
\Gamma=0
.
We will do the following computation.

Line bundles on the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a point and
\mathcal{O}_{e}
satisfy Theorem
1.
with
\Gamma=k(h^{2}+2e^{2})
, for
\frac{1}{48}\leq k\leq\frac{3}{98}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{147}
.

Note that here
\Gamma\cdot H=0
.

Recall that
H=2h-e
, so by twisting a suitable choice of
\mathcal{O}(H)
, we can assume that the line bundle is of the form
\mathcal{O}(mh)
for an integer
m
.

The hyperbola
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))=0
is given by the equation

7\alpha^{2}-7\beta^{2}+8m\beta-2m^{2}=0.

So when
m\leq 0
, then
\overline{\beta}=\frac{4+\sqrt{2}}{7}m
.
Evaluating the left hand side of the inequality in Theorem
1.
, we get

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{3}}{6}-\overline{\beta}m^{2}+2\overline{\beta}^{2}m-\frac{7}{6}\overline{\beta}^{3}-km

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{98}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{147}\right)m^{3}-km.

So when
k\leq\frac{3}{98}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{147}
, the above function is less than or equal to
0
for any integer
m\leq 0
, and the theorem holds.

When
m=1
holds, then
\overline{\beta}=\frac{4-\sqrt{2}}{7}
. A similar computation shows that the inequality holds for
m=1
when
k\geq\frac{3}{98}-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{147}
.

Now we focus on the case when
m\geq 2
. In this case
\mathcal{O}(mh)
is not
\overline{\beta}
-stable. To see this, just note that there always exists a nontrivial map
\mathcal{O}(mh-e)\to\mathcal{O}(mh)
. The wall induced by this map is given by the equation

7\alpha^{2}+7\beta^{2}-7\beta-2m^{2}+4m=0.

When
m\geq 2
, this wall is non-empty, and
\mathcal{O}(mh)
is destabilized on the wall. So in order to show the theorem, it suffices to check the inequality at this wall.

Note that the top point of the wall, which is also the intersection with the hyperbola
\nu_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))=0
, has coordinates
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha^{2}=\frac{2m^{2}-4m+7/4}{7}
. So we have

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{6}H^{2}\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{\beta}(\mathcal{O}(mh))

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{3}}{6}-\beta m^{2}+2\beta^{2}m-\frac{7}{6}\beta^{3}-km-\frac{2m^{2}-4m+7/4}{42}(4m-7\beta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{m^{3}}{42}+\frac{m^{2}}{21}-km.

It is easy to see that this function is negative for
m\geq 2
and
k>0
.

For the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{e}
, we have
\overline{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}
and

\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{3}^{1/2}(\mathcal{O}_{e})-\Gamma\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{ch}}\nolimits_{1}^{1/2}(\mathcal{O}_{e})=\frac{1}{24}-2k,

from which the result follows.

\Box

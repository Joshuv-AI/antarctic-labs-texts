Sharp density discrepancy for cut and project sets An approach via lattice point counting Keywords: Cut and project sets, model sets, aperiodic order, lattice point counting, diophantine approximation, discrepancy MSC(2020): Primary: 52C23, 11P21, 11H06. Secondary: 52C45.
Henna Koivusalo, Jean Lagacé

Abstract
Cut and project sets are obtained by taking an irrational slice of a lattice and projecting it to a lower dimensional subspace. We seek to quantify fluctuations from the asymptotic mean for point counts. We obtain uniform upper bounds on the discrepancy depending on the diophantine properties of the lattice. In an appendix, Michael Björklund and Tobias Hartnick obtain lower bounds on the L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} norm of the discrepancy also depending on the diophantine class; these lower bounds match our uniform upper bounds and both are therefore sharp. This also allows us to find sharp bounds for the number of lattice points in thin slabs. Using a sufficient criteria of Burago–Kleiner and Aliste-Prieto–Coronel–Gambaudo we find an explicit full-measure class of cut and project sets that are biLipschitz equivalent to lattices; our lower bounds indicate that this is the largest class of cut and project sets for which those criteria can apply.

Introduction

1.1
Cut and project sets

A
cut and project set

\Lambda
is a discrete subset of euclidean space
which, despite being aperiodic, still exhibits repetitive structure and
long-term order. Fix an orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{d}\cong\mathbb{E}:=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d_{\triangledown}}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{d_{\triangleleft}},

(1.1)

with associated projections

\pi_{\triangledown}
and
\pi_{\triangleleft}
. Fix a lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
,
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and a bounded

window

\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. The cut and project set associated with this
data is

\Lambda:=\Lambda(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown},\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft};\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft}):=\pi_{\triangledown}((\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap\pi_{\triangleleft}^{-1}(\Omega_{\triangleleft}));

(1.2)

it is said to be
regular
if
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
is an open set whose boundary
has zero measure, and
s
-
regular
if, in addition
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
has a
finite
(d_{\triangleleft}-s)
-Minkowski content for some
0<s\leqslant 1
(for definitions, see Section
3.1
). We also make the standard assumption that
\pi_{\triangleleft}(\Gamma)
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and

\pi_{\triangledown}\big|_{\Gamma}
is injective; we give a characterisation of these via
complete irrationality
of
\Gamma
in Section

2.3
.
Since the decomposition (
1.1
) is fixed we generally drop them from the
notation and write
\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft})
.
Cut and project sets have received an increasing amount of interest of late as
mathematical models for
quasicrystals
.
Also referred
to as
model sets
, they originated in mathematics as
generalisations of lattices by Meyer in 1970
[
Mey70
]
.

Many of the standard questions about lattices extend naturally to
cut and project sets; amongst which is the counting problem consisting of finding a
good asymptotic description for the quantity
\#(\Lambda\cap\Omega_{\triangledown})

where the search region
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
is large, for example a large ball
\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t)\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
or cube
[0,t]^{d_{\triangleleft}}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
. It is well-known (see, for example,
[
HKWS16
]
, also see
[
BG13
, Theorem 7.2]
) that

\#(\Lambda\cap\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t))=\frac{\vol(\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,1))\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t^{d_{\triangledown}}+\Delta(\Lambda;\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t)),

(1.3)

where the discrepancy is smaller than the main term, i.e.

\Delta(\Lambda;\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t))=o\left(t^{d_{\triangledown}}\right)
for every
\Lambda
. Here, the volumes are computed in the
appropriate subspace and the covolume of a lattice
\Gamma
is defined as

\covol(\Gamma):=\vol(\mathbb{E}/\Gamma)
.

The purpose of the current work is to find estimates for the discrepancy
\Delta

depending on the search region
\Omega_{\triangledown}
, the window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and the diophantine properties of

\Gamma
. We establish a direct,
explicit link between a quantifiable diophantine property of the lattice and a
discrepancy bound for the corresponding cut and project set by reducing the
problem to lattice point counting, see Theorem

1.2
. We apply the discrepancy estimates to the problem of identifying
new classes of cut and project sets that can be mapped by a biLipschitz map to
a
lattice, see Theorem
1.5
. In particular, we note that for windows
of finite perimeter the sharp classes we obtain depend only on the diophantine
class of the lattice
\Gamma
and nothing else.

1.2
Uniform upper bounds for discrepancy

We will first state our main theorem on discrepancies of cut and project sets.
We begin by defining what it means for a lattice to be
\psi
-repellent

(for the origin of this term, see
[
BH23
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Given an increasing

\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
so that
\psi(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
,
we call a lattice
\Gamma

\psi
-repellent (with respect to the decomposition

\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
), if for every
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{0\}
,

|\pi_{\triangleleft}(\gamma)|>\psi\left(|\pi_{\triangledown}(\gamma)|\right)^{-1}.

If there is some
\mu>0
so that
\psi(r)\asymp r^{\mu}
at
\infty
, we say
that
\psi

grows at speed
\mu
at infinity
. If on the other hand

\psi(r)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}r^{\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
, for instance
\psi(r)=\log(1+r)
, we say that
\psi

grows slowly at infinity
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
is completely irrational with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and let
0<s\leqslant 1
. Let the window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
have boundary with finite

(d_{\triangleleft}-s)
-Minkowski content, and assume that the dual lattice

\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\psi
-repellent for some
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
that grows slowly. Let
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
have finite perimeter.

Then, for any
\delta>0
there are

C_{\Lambda,\delta},t_{0}>0
so that for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and every
t>t_{0}
, the cut and project set

\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})

satisfies,

\left\lvert\#(\Lambda\cap t\Omega_{\triangledown})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\right\rvert\leqslant C_{\Lambda,\delta}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-s(1-\delta)}.

(1.4)

In Section
4
we show that proving Theorem
1.2

reduces to a lattice point count in
anisotropically expanding domains — this is the content of Lemma

4.1
. Our main theorems in the lattice point
counting context are Theorems
4.3
and
4.5
of which Theorem

1.2
is then a special case.
Note that by the Khintcine-Groshev Theorem (also see

[
BH23
]
) the property of being
\psi
-repellent for

\psi(t)\asymp t^{\mu}
, and hence our results, hold in a conull set of
lattices.

In Theorem
4.3
we assume that the search
region is a strictly convex domain satisfying some boundary curvature
assumption described later, whereas Theorem
4.5
merely assumes that the
search region has finite perimeter, so that balls
and cubes are special cases, respectively. A
simple
self-contained proof of the asymptotic (
1.3
) is included along the way to Theorem
1.2
.
We also formulate in Section
3.3
a corollary of Theorem
1.2
for discrepancies of frequencies of
r
-patterns in cut and project sets, see Theorem
3.16
.

The main interest of the discrepancy bounds in Theorem
1.2
is that
generically in a complementary set of lattices we obtain a matching lower bound
along a subsequence, see Subsection
1.3
and the appendix of
this text. This means that the class of lattices for which we obtain
these upper bounds is sharp. Of course other upper bounds on the discrepancy have been proved in the past by
Haynes, Julien, Koivusalo, and Walton
[
HJKW19
]
and
Rühr, Smilansky and Weiss
[
RSW23
]
1
1

1

To be more precise, these results are concerned with discrepancy to pattern frequencies. However, as we will explain in Section
3.3
, there is no reason to make a distinction between the two problems.
. Haynes
et al.
had a slightly different
parametrisation of the cut and project scheme as compared to the one we have
here, and one crucial difference is that they take both the window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}

and the search region
\Omega_{\triangledown}
to be axes-aligned cubes. This, together
with a diophantine condition on the lattice
\Gamma
allows them to deduce a
very small discrepancy, in a similar way to the anomalous small remainders proven
for lattices in
[
KY15
, Theorem 3.5]
. Namely,
they show that for any
\varepsilon>0
, and almost
every choice of
\Gamma
,

\left\lvert\#(\Lambda\cap t\Omega_{\triangledown})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\right\rvert\leqslant C_{\varepsilon,\Gamma}(\log t)^{d_{\triangleleft}-\varepsilon}.

(1.5)

That these anomalously low bounds hold can also be deduced from an earlier work of Haynes, Kelly and Weiss
[
HKW14
]
, but their parametrisation is yet different. The aim of both of these results was to obtain a conull set of lattices in which the discrepancy bounds hold. In particular, they did not work out the discrepancy bounds for classes broader than what was necessary for the statement on full measure, which amounts to a type of weak badly approximable condition on
\Gamma
. The weak bad approximation properties appearing in these works differ from the
\psi
-repellent condition of Theorem
1.2
.

The result of Rühr
et al.
is also for
\Gamma
belonging to a full
measure subset of the set of lattices, but they
do allow a wider choice of
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and
\Omega_{\triangledown}
. In particular,
they assume that
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
has box dimension bounded from above
by
d_{\triangleleft}-s
and prove that for almost every lattice

\left\lvert\#(\Lambda\cap\Omega_{\triangledown})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})}{\covol(\Gamma)}\right\rvert=O\left(\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})^{1-\frac{s}{d_{\triangledown}-2s}}\right).

(1.6)

For this result also, an underlying generic diophantine condition is key,
but the techniques obscure it and it seems difficult to track it down
explicitly.

All of these results give a growth rate for the discrepancy which is smaller
than that of Theorem
1.2
, but there is no clarity on the behaviour of
the discrepancy on the measure zero (but comeagre) set of lattices on which
their result does not hold. Theorem
1.2
differs from these
results in two ways. Foremost, it uses lattice point counting, a technique which is new to the study of
discrepancies of cut and project sets. Further, in Theorem
1.2
, the
connection between the established discrepancy bound and the function
\psi
is
explicit and, in comparison to
[
HJKW19
]
and

[
HKW14
]
, we give a new class of cut and project sets with uniform upper
bounds for discrepancy, as the diophantine condition of Theorem
1.2

is explicit and on the dual lattice and not the lattice
\Gamma
itself.

1.3
Averaging, and lower bounds for discrepancy

It is
natural to ask what is the best possible bound for the discrepancy that one can
expect.
The reader may also have
noticed that the translation parameter
\mathbf{z}
seems spurrious, as none of the
previous results depended on it. This is the case because the methods we use are
translation invariant. At the same time, translating
\Gamma
by
\mathbf{z}
is the same
as translating the window by
\mathbf{z}_{\triangleleft}
and translating the search region by

\mathbf{z}_{\triangledown}
; and up to a small set of parameters this should not affect the
actual statistics for the distribution of the points in a cut and project set.
With this heuristic in mind, we average the absolute value of the discrepancy
over a fundamental domain of
\Gamma
to obtain lower bounds.
The next theorem
should be interpreted as saying that this is the best upper bound on the
discrepancy that one can obtain on a full measure set of
\mathbf{z}
. We note that these lower
bounds hold with even weaker assumptions on the lattice and the window. Indeed,
no irrationality at all is assumed on
\Gamma
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
is any lattice,
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
is any bounded
window with nonzero volume. There is
C,t_{0}>0
so that the cut and project
sets
\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft})
satisfy for all
t>t_{0}

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\left\lvert\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft});\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t))\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}\geqslant Cf(t)t^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

(1.7)

where for some
A>0
the function
f(t)
is defined as

f(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }d\not\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,4)\\
\exp(-A\log\log(t)^{4})&\text{if }d\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,4).\end{cases}

(1.8)

Furthermore,

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft});\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=0.

(1.9)

Just as Theorem
1.2
was a special case of Theorem

4.3
through a characterisation as a lattice point counting
problem as proved in Section
4
, Theorem
1.9
is
a consequence of a corresponding lattice result, Theorem
4.7
.
What Theorem
1.9
indicates is that for every cut and project set and every
t

large enough, there are positive measure subsets of
\mathbb{E}/\Gamma
so that the
remainder terms are as big as indicated in (
1.7
), both in the
positive and the negative direction. This is, in a way, as best as one can hope
to do on full measure sets. In the Appendix, written by Michael Björklund and Tobias Hartnick,
it is instead the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm in the parameter
\mathbf{z}
which is considered, also
known as the number variance. More precisely, define a lattice to be

\psi
-Liouvillean if it satisfies the complementary property to being

\psi
-repellant; in other words if there is a sequence
\gamma^{(n)}
so that

|\pi_{\triangleleft}(\gamma^{(n)})|\leqslant\psi(|\pi_{\triangledown}(\gamma^{(n)})|^{-1}).

(1.10)

Björklund and Hartnick show that for almost every
r>0
, every cut and
project set
\Lambda
originating from the window
\mathbb{B}_{\triangleleft}(0,r)
and
lattice whose dual is
\psi
-Liouvillean satisfies the asymptotics

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}|\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\mathbb{B}_{\triangleleft}(0,r));t\Omega_{\triangledown})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}}{t^{2d}\psi(t)^{-d_{\triangleleft}-1-\delta}}=\infty.

for any
\delta>0
. This means that, at least along subsequences, the
discrepancy can be very large. In fact, they also show that there exist
explicit
\psi
-Liouvillean lattices and
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
so that cut and project
sets
\Lambda
with this data satisfies

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}|\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft});t\Omega_{\triangledown})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}}{t^{2d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-2-\delta}}=\infty

(1.11)

for any
\delta>0
; see Theorems
A.5
and
A.6
. For those lattices satisfying
(
1.11
) Hölder’s inequality implies that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{|\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft});t\Omega_{\triangledown})|}{t^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-1+\delta}}=\infty.

(1.12)

so that Theorem
1.2
is sharp when the boundary of the window has
Minkowski dimension
d-1
.

1.4
Alternative interpretation: lattice points in thin slabs

There is another geometric interpretation of our results in terms of lattice
point counts which we describe now. Counting the number of points in the cut and
project set
\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft})
within dilates
t\Omega_{\triangledown}

of a search region is the same as counting the number of points in the lattice

\Gamma
in a thin slab
(t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})-\mathbf{z}
. Our main
result, Theorem
1.2
combined with Theorem
A.6
can be rewritten in this language.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, and
\Gamma
be a lattice completely
irrational with respect to this decomposition. Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
have finite perimeter, and assume
furthermore that the dual lattice
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\psi
-repellent for
some slowly growing
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
. Then, for any

\delta>0
there is
C_{\Gamma,\delta}
so that

\left|\#\Gamma\cap(t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\right|\leqslant C_{\Gamma,\delta}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-1+\delta}.

(1.13)

Furthermore, there are
\psi
-Liouvillean lattices and sets
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
so that for every
\delta>0
,

\left|\#\Gamma\cap(t_{n}\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t_{n}^{d_{\triangledown}}\right|\geqslant t_{n}^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t_{n})^{-1-\delta}.

(1.14)

We note that this refines and corrects the lattice point counting results in
anisotropically expanding domains found in any of

[
KY11
,
KY15
,
Lag20
]
, where the
contribution from Diophantine properties of the lattice was overlooked.

A classical problem in number theory is to prove existence of approximate
integer solutions to polynomial equations, for instance Davenport and Heilbronn
proved in
[
DH46
]
that there are infinitely many solutions to

\sum_{j=1}^{5}\lambda_{j}n_{j}^{2}\leqslant\varepsilon

(1.15)

provided the
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{5}
are not zero, do not all share the same sign, and are
pairwise rationally independent. Freeman obtained asymptotics for the number
of solutions where every coordinate
n_{i}
is below some value
T

[
Fre02
]
. This
corresponds to counting the number of points of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
\ker L\oplus[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
where
L
is the linear map given by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{4}&\lambda_{5}\end{pmatrix}
under the additional constraint that all coordinates are squares. After a
linear change of variable, this can be interpreted in our setting, up to the additional
constraint on the coordinates being squares.

Recently, in
[
Wal20
]
, Walker has obtained results of this type for general matrices
L
in the case where the
constraint is that all the coordinates belong to some set
A
of positive
density, as opposed to the situation in (
1.15
) where the set of
squares has density zero.
In this situation, he obtains an asymptotic relation where the first term is given by the
count of
all
lattice points in
\ker L\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
, plus an
error term which is bounded in terms of the set
A
and Diophantine properties
of the (dual) lattice
[
Wal20
, Lemma 3.4]
; see also
[
Wal21
, Lemma 8.4]
where
the coefficients are assumed algebraic. As such, Theorem
1.4
makes
more precise, and sharp, the contribution of the principal term in Walker’s results.

1.5
BiLipschitz equivalence to lattices

We now turn our attention to the question of mapping cut and project sets onto
lattices with a biLipschitz map
\varphi:\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If such a map exists,
we say that
\Lambda
is
biLipschitz (BL) equivalent
to a lattice. The
question of BL equivalence to lattices of relatively dense and uniformly
discrete sets (
separated nets
) is an old problem originating in
geometry: if two metric spaces have separated nets that
are BL equivalent to each other, then they are quasi-isometric. Gromov
[
Gro93
]
asked
whether there are choices of separated nets in the same metric space that are
not BL equivalent to each other. The constructions of Burago and Kleiner

[
BK98
]
and McMullen
[
McM98
]
were the first to show that the answer is
‘yes’ in euclidean space: there are
separated nets that are not BL equivalent to a lattice.
Later it was found that in fact the collection of BL classes of separated nets
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has the size of the continuum
[
Mag11
]
. Burago and Kleiner

[
BK02
]
(in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), and later, Aliste-Prieto, Coronel and Gambaudo

[
ACG13
]
(in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) also showed that BL
equivalence can be reduced to uniform point counting in large cubes.

In the case of cut and project sets, Burago and Kleiner
[
BK02
]
were able to prove that for almost every choice of
\Gamma
, a regular cut and project set in
dimension
d=3
is BL equivalent to a lattice. This prompted them to
ask whether every regular cut and project set has this property. Their result was
extended to
s
-regular cut and project sets in any dimension
d
by Haynes,
Kelly and Weiss
[
HKW14
]
. Proofs of this kind rely on a result given in
[
ACG13
, Theorem 3.1]
, see also

[
BK02
]
, which establishes a link between discrepancy estimates of cut and project sets,
and their BL equivalence class. In Theorem
1.2
we established an explicit connection between a diophantine property of
\Gamma^{\dagger}
and the discrepancy of the corresponding cut and project set. We can now apply it to find the following new class of cut and project sets which are BL equivalent to lattices.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
is completely irrational with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
, and assume that
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is
s
-regular for some
0<s<1
. Assume, furthermore, that
there exists some
\eta>0
such that
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\log(1+t)^{(1+\eta)/s}
-repellant. Then, the cut and project set
\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is BL equivalent to a lattice.

As pointed out above, the property of being
\psi
-repellent for
\psi(t)\asymp t^{\mu}
holds in a conull set of lattices, and in particular the above result
establishes that almost every lattice
\Gamma
corresponds to a cut and project
set which is BL equivalent to a lattice. We note that Theorems

A.5
and
A.6
of the Appendix show that this is
more or less the largest class of cut and project to which the sufficient conditions of
Burago–Kleiner and Aliste-Prieto–Coronel–Gambaudo can be applied, up to a
very small (both null and meagre) set. In other words, if we want to determine
the
biLipschitz class of more cut and project sets a weaker sufficient condition
needs to be found, or an example which is not biLipschitz equivalent
to a lattice.

Plan of the paper

In Section
2
and
3
, we cover the background on
lattices, the Fourier transform and cut and project sets. There, we also fix
some notation and discuss the irrationality assumptions that are necessary for
our results to hold. Section
4
connects the distribution of
points in cut and project sets to lattice point counting, and states the main
general theorems. Section
5
is a preparation section; we are
aiming to use Poisson summation for the lattice point counts. As such, it is
necessary to find smooth approximations to the functions being summed in order
to ensure sufficient decay of the Fourier transforms.
Section
6
is concerned with the proof of Theorems
4.3

which gives upper bounds to discrepancy. Section

7
is again a preparation section, this time providing
uniform diophantine estimates for lattice projections. In Section

8
we prove the Theorem
4.7
on the average lower
bound for the discrepancy. Finally, the appendix of Björklund and Hartnick
gives the lower bounds on the number variance.

2
Notation, definitions and assumptions

In this section we collect notations, definitions that we use in this paper, as
well as recall some facts about them. The results stated in this section are
standard or folklore, we state them for convenience of reference. As such, the reader who is
short on time may wish to pass directly to Section
4
.

2.1
Euclidean space

Total space is denoted by
\mathbb{E}
, and
\dim(\mathbb{E})=d
. The canonical decomposition
with respect to which cut and project sets are defined is always denoted

\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft},

(2.1)

with
\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown})=d_{\triangledown}
and
\dim(\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft})=d_{\triangleleft}=d-d_{\triangledown}
. Sometimes, we require
other subspaces or decompositions of
\mathbb{E}
, in which case we denote them
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
, or
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
. The projection on
\mathbb{F}
is denoted
by
\pi_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Given a decomposition, whether the canonical one or an auxiliary one, we always
indicate with subscripts
\triangledown
or
\triangleleft

2.2
Asymptotic notation

We make frequent use of the Landau and Vinogradov asymptotic notation. To wit,
given two real-valued functions
f,g
, we say that

•

indiscriminately,
f=O\left(g\right)
,
f\lesssim g
, or
g\gtrsim f
to indicate that
there exists
C>0
so that
\left\lvert f\right\rvert\leqslant C\left\lvert g\right\rvert
;

•

we use
f\asymp g
to indicate that
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

In either cases, an index on the asymptotic notation indicates dependence of the
constants on this index. For instance, writing

means
that the constant
C
in the definition above may depend on
\Gamma
and
s
.

2.3
Lattices

Definition 2.3
.

Given
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
with potentially
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{E}
, a

\mathbb{F}
is
a discrete cocompact subgroup
\Gamma
of the group of translations
in
\mathbb{F}
. Through the identification of
\mathbb{F}
with its group of translations, we
view a lattice as a subset
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}
, and
\mathbb{F}/\Gamma
is a torus which
we can identify with any parallelotope (called a fundamental cell) generated by a basis of
\Gamma
. Given
a lattice this identification is fixed once and for all, the fundamental cell is
also denoted
\mathbb{F}/\Gamma
, and every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}
can therefore be written uniquely as
\mathbf{x}=\gamma+\mathbf{k}
, for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{F}/\Gamma
.
The covolume of a lattice

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}
is defined as

\covol(\Gamma):=\vol(\mathbb{F}/\Gamma).

(2.3)

The space
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{E}}
of all lattices in
\mathbb{E}
is identified with

\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{E}}:=\GL(d,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\GL(d,\mathbb{R})/S_{d},

(2.4)

where
S_{d}
is the symmetric group acting by permuting the columns of a
d\times d
matrix. Note that
this may differ from the standard description found in the litterature where

would be replaced by
\mathrm{SO}(d,\mathbb{R})
; this is because for our purposes
we do
not
want to identify lattices which are equivalent up to orthogonal
transformations. From this identification we see that
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{E}}
is an orbifold of
dimension
d^{2}
, and it
inherits a (Haar) measure and a topology from
\GL(d,\mathbb{R})
, on which we put the
topology induced by the operator norm.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice. Its dual lattice

is defined as

\Gamma^{\dagger}:=\left\{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{E}:\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}.

(2.5)

The dual lattice is itself a lattice, and
(\Gamma^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=\Gamma
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
be a subspace. We denote
\Gamma(\mathbb{F}):=\Gamma\cap\mathbb{F}

and we say that
\mathbb{F}
is a
\Gamma
-subspace
if
\Gamma(\mathbb{F})
spans

\mathbb{F}
.

We note the important distinction between
\Gamma^{\dagger}(\mathbb{F}):=\Gamma^{\dagger}\cap\mathbb{F}
and

\Gamma(\mathbb{F})^{\dagger}:=\left\{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\spn(\Gamma(\mathbb{F})):\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(2.6)

There is a correspondence between
\Gamma
-subspaces of dimension
m
and

\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspaces of codimension
m
. Indeed, if
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
is a

\Gamma
-subspace,
\mathbb{F}^{\perp}
is a
\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspace, see

[
KY15
, Lemma 3.2]
. This correspondence is
showcased in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice. For every
x\in\mathbb{E}
,
\spn(x)
is a

\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspace if and only if there exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
so that

\left\{y\in\mathbb{E}:x\cdot y\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)\right\}\cap\Gamma=\varnothing.

(2.7)

Proof.

We first suppose
\spn(x)
is a
\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspace, so that there
exists
a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
ax\in\Gamma^{\dagger}
. By definition of

\Gamma^{\dagger}
,

\left\{y\in\mathbb{E}:ax\cdot y\in(1/4,3/4)\right\}\cap\Gamma=\varnothing.

(2.8)

Therefore we can take
t=(2a)^{-1}
and
\delta=(4a)^{-1}
.

Suppose on the other hand that (
2.7
) holds for some
x\in\mathbb{E}
. This implies we cannot make the quantity
\left\lvert x\cdot\gamma\right\rvert
arbitrarily
small. In particular, this implies that
\left\lvert x\cdot\gamma\right\rvert
for
\gamma\in\Gamma
is quantised:
there exists
a>0
such that for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
{x\cdot\gamma}=an
. But then

, in other words
\spn(x)
is a

\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspace.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice and
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
a subspace. We say
that
\Gamma
is
irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
if
\Gamma(\mathbb{F})=\left\{0\right\}
. If
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is also irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
we say that

\Gamma
is
completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
.

Given a subspace
\mathbb{F}
of codimension at least
1
, the set of lattices which are
not completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
is dense in the set of lattices, but
is still small, as seen in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}
be a subspace of dimension
m<d
. Then, the set of
lattices completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
is a comeagre set whose
complement has Hausdorff dimension
dm
.

Proof.

The projection by the quotient map of a comeagre set is comeagre, so we can
identify
\Gamma
with any of its (unordered) bases in

\GL(d,\mathbb{R})/S_{d}
. In turn, this is viewed as an open subset
of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}/S_{d}
. The first basis element
\theta_{1}
of
\Gamma
can be chosen
arbitrarily outside
\mathbb{F}
, that is, outside a set of dimension
m
. Recursively, to ensure that
\mathbb{F}\cap\Gamma=\left\{0\right\}
,
the
j
th basis vector needs to be chosen outside

\spn_{\mathbb{Z}}(\theta_{1},\dotsc,\theta_{j-1})+\mathbb{F}
. This is a
d
-fold product of
a countable union of dimension
m<d
affine conditions, hence the set of
irrational lattices with respect to
\mathbb{F}
is a comeagre set whose complement
has Hausdorff dimension
dm
.

To see that the same holds for completely irrational lattices, we observe
that the involution
\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma^{\dagger}
corresponds in
\GL(d,\mathbb{R})/S_{d}
to the map
A\mapsto(A^{-1})^{\top}
. This is a locally Lipschitz homeomorphism, hence it preserves
comeagreness and Hausdorff dimension. By the same construction as above, the set
of lattices whose dual is irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}
is also a comeagre set
whose complement has Hausdorff dimension
dm
, taking their union
yields our claim.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

In our setting we will use (complete) irrationality with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. Irrationality of
\Gamma
with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
implies that the projection
\pi_{\triangledown}
restricted to
\Gamma
is injective; this allows
us to translate the problems at hand into lattice point counting problems
(see Lemma
4.1
).
Irrationality of
\Gamma^{\dagger}
with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
implies that

\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
(see Lemma

). This latter fact is a
standard condition in the definition of cut and project sets and it will
appear technically in our use of the Poisson summation formula.

2.4
Fourier Transforms of indicator functions

Define
\mathbf{e}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
as

\mathbf{e}(x):=e^{-2\pi ix}.

(2.9)

The Fourier transform of a function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined as

[\mathcal{F}f](\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi})f(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

(2.10)

Given a decomposition
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
, we also define partial
Fourier transforms relative to the decomposition via the formulae

[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}f](\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}):=\int_{\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})f(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown},\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}

(2.11)

and

[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}f](\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangleleft},\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}):=\int_{\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}}\mathbf{e}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangleleft}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})f(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown},\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}.

(2.12)

A direct computation tells us that
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}=\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}
.
We now provide three estimates on the decay of the Fourier transform of indicator
functions, depending (in decreasing order of regularity) on whether the boundary
of a set is strictly convex, has finite perimeter, or finite upper Minkowski
content. We note that the second estimate could be proved by means of the third,
but since the proof is simpler we keep it for clarity of exposition, in
particular for readers who are only interested in the case where the boundary is
regular.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
be a decomposition of Euclidean space with

\dim(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown})=m
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{E}
be compact and assume that for every

\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
,
\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})
either has
zero
m
-dimensional measure or is a convex set with
\mathrm{C}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}

boundary and principal curvatures uniformly bounded away from
0
. There exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega}](\boldsymbol{\xi})\right\rvert\leqslant C(1+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert)^{\frac{-m-1}{2}}.

(2.13)

Remark 2.11
.

We note that the hypotheses of this statement differ slightly from those
found in
[
KY11
, Theorem 4]
,
[
KY15
, Theorem
2.3]
(where this lemma is used in their
proof without explicitly stating it separately) and
[
Lag20
, Lemma
6.4]
. We add here the hypotheses that the boundary of
\Omega
is sufficiently
regular and that the principal curvatures are uniformly bounded away from

0
. These conditions are necessary to ensure the decay
(
2.13
): if the principal curvatures vanish at some point
then the optimal order of decay is worse, see e.g.
[
Ran66
]
for

\mathrm{L}^{p}
balls in
\mathbb{E}
and
[
CdV77
, Théorème 1]
for
general sets. As such, the hypotheses on regularity and geometry of the
previous lemma should be added to the statements of the
aforementioned theorems for their proof to go through in their current
state.

Proof.

We write the Fourier transform as

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangleleft})\int_{\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\chi(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown},\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}]](\boldsymbol{\xi}).

By the assumptions on
\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})
, it follows from

[
IL14
, Theorem 2.29]
with
\alpha=0
in their notation that under the hypotheses of the lemma

\left\lvert[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\right\rvert\lesssim(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|)^{\frac{-m-1}{2}},

(2.15)

uniformly with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}
. The claim then follows by bounding the
Fourier transform with respect to the
\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
direction by its value at

0
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
be a decomposition of Euclidean space with

\dim(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown})=m
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{E}
be compact and assume that for
every
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
,
\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})
either has
zero measure or is a

m
-dimensional domain with finite perimeter, and assume that this perimeter
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}
. There
exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega}](\boldsymbol{\xi})\right\rvert\leqslant C(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|)^{-1}.

(2.16)

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert\geqslant 1
. Write the
Fourier transform again as

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangleleft})\int_{\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\chi(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown},\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}

(2.17)

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}]](\boldsymbol{\xi}),

where we interpret the indicator function of
\Omega
as a family
parametrised by
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}
of indicators in
\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}
. Applying the
Gauss–Green theorem
we have that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\Omega\cap(F_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\Omega\cap(F_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|^{2}}\operatorname{div}(\grad_{\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{\partial\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})}\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\cdot\nu}{\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert^{2}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert^{-1}\vol_{m-1}(\partial\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})),

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal to the boundary. Uniform boundedness
of the perimeter implies our claim.
∎

For our final estimate we start with a definition of (upper) Minkowski content.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{E}
be a bounded set and let
0\leqslant s\leqslant d
. The

s
-codimensional (upper) Minkowski content of
\Upsilon
with respect to
\mathbb{E}
is
defined as

\mathfrak{M}_{s}(\Upsilon;\mathbb{E}):=\limsup_{r\searrow 0}r^{-s}\vol(\Upsilon_{r}),

(2.19)

where
\Upsilon_{r}
is the
r
-neigbourhood of
\Upsilon
in
\mathbb{E}
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
be a decomposition of Euclidean space with

\dim(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown})=m
. Let
0<s<1
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{E}
be compact and assume that for
every
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
,
\Omega\cap(\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}+\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})
either has
zero measure or is a

m
-dimensional domain whose boundary has finite
s
-codimensional Minkowski
content with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}
, and assume furthermore that this
Minkowski content
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}
. There
exists
C>0
such that

\left\lvert[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega}](\boldsymbol{\xi})\right\rvert\leqslant C(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|)^{-s}.

(2.20)

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}
be a set whose boundary has finite

s
-codimensional Minkowski content with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}
. For
r>0
, cover the
boundary of
\Omega_{\triangledown}
with finitely many disjoint cubes of sidelength
r>0
, of which we need at most
\lesssim r^{s-m}
. Define
\Omega^{(r)}
to
be
\Omega
with those cubes removed; this is a set with Lipschitz boundary,
hence to which we can apply the Gauss–Green theorem. Furthermore,

\vol_{m-1}(\partial\Omega^{(r)})\lesssim r^{s-1}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lvert\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{(r)}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{\partial\Omega^{(r)}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\cdot\nu}{\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert^{2}}\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown}\right\rvert

(2.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{s}+|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|^{-1}r^{s-1}.

We now assume without loss of generality that
|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|\geqslant 1
is large
enough and we
take
r=|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|^{-1}
to obtain

r^{s}+|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|^{-1}r^{s-1}\lesssim|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown}|^{-s}.

(2.22)

Uniformity of the Minkowski content allows us to decompose the integral in
the
\triangleleft
and
\triangledown
direction as in the proof of Lemma

2.12
, whence we obtain our desired result.
∎

3
Cut and project sets

In this section we introduce the definitions and preliminary results for cut and
project sets, and prove Theorem
1.5
as a consequence of Theorem

1.2
. We reserve the letter
\Lambda
for cut and project sets, and

\mathrm{M}
for subsets
of
\Lambda
which are themselves a cut and project set.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be a decomposition of
d
-dimensional
Euclidean space. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice,
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be a window and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
. The cut and project set associated
with this data is

\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft}):=\pi_{\triangledown}((\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap\pi_{\triangleleft}^{-1}(\Omega_{\triangleleft}))

(3.1)

We fix the decomposition
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
throughout and this is
why it has been dropped from the notation in this definition of cut and project
sets.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then
\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})=\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z}+\gamma;\Omega_{\triangleleft})
, in other words the map
\mathbf{z}\mapsto\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is
\Gamma
-periodic, so that we may
consider
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}/\Gamma
is an element of the torus rather than of
\mathbb{E}
. In addition, we can
observe that for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
,

\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})=\Lambda(\Gamma;0;\Omega_{\triangleleft}+\mathbf{z}_{\triangleleft})+\mathbf{z}_{\triangledown},

(3.2)

in other words every cut and project set with a parameter
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
can
be realised as the translation of a cut and project set with a parameter

\mathbf{z}=0
by translating the window appropriately.

3.1
Assumptions on the cut and project set

We do not need to give any conditions on the parameter
\mathbf{z}
for our results to hold. We will require some
conditions on the other pieces of data which we list now.

(O)

The decomposition
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is
orthogonal.

(D)

The image
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
.

(I)

The projection
\pi_{\triangledown}|_{\Gamma}
is injective,

(
s
-Reg)

The window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
is regular, meaning
that it is relatively compact and has non-empty interior, and its
boundary is of measure
0
. We assume further that the
s
-codimensional
Minkowski content of
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is finite.

The condition
(O)
is simply stated
for convenience; every cut and project set can be realised as a cut and project
set satisfying
(O)
, see for example the discussion in
[
RSW23
, p. 9]
.
The conditions
(I)
and
(D)
below are standard assumptions in
the area (see, for example,
[
RSW23
, Section 2]
), but we will prove as Lemma

3.4
below that they are equivalent to the assumption of

\Gamma
being completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, a formulation
which is more convenient for
our purposes. Finally, some condition on the regularity on the boundary of the
window is needed; otherwise, not even the patch counting function behaves as
expected
[
JLO19
]
. Condition
(
s
-Reg)
is the weakest condition
that allows for our techniques. Notice that, given these assumptions the inverse

(\pi_{\triangledown})^{-1}:\Lambda\to\Gamma
is well defined. For any subset
A\subset\Lambda
, we set the notation
A^{\triangle}=(\pi_{\triangledown})^{-1}(A)
, and the notation
A^{\star}=\pi_{\triangleleft}((\pi_{\triangledown})^{-1}(A))
.

The following lemmas tell us that
complete irrationality of the lattice with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is equivalent to conditions
(D)
and

(I)
. We note that even outside our current purposes, complete
irrationality is
a priori
a much easier condition to check directly on a given lattice than
density of the projection on
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, say.

Lemma 3.3
.

The lattice projection
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
is dense in

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
if and only if for every
r>0
, the intersection
\mathbb{B}(0,r)\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
spans
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
.

Proof.

We see immediately that density of
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
implies the existence of
arbitrarily small subsets of
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
spanning
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
; we now
prove the other direction. We observe that
\pi_{\triangleleft}:\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is a group homomorphism. In particular, for every linearly independent

\Sigma\subset\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
of cardinality
d_{\triangleleft}
,
\spn_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma)
is a lattice
in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. In order to prove our claim, it is sufficient to show that for every

x_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\Sigma\subset\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
such that
\dist(x_{\triangleleft},\spn_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma))<\varepsilon
. We note that for
any full rank lattice
\Theta\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,

\sup_{x_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}}\dist(x_{\triangleleft},\Theta)<\diam(\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}/\Theta).

(3.3)

Now, if
\Sigma
is a basis for a lattice
\Theta
, then identifying

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}/\Theta
with the parallelotope generated by
\Sigma
we see
that

\diam(\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}/\Theta)<d_{\triangleleft}\max\left\{\left\lvert\theta\right\rvert:\theta\in\Sigma\right\}.

(3.4)

In particular, by hypothesis for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{B}_{\triangleleft}(0,d_{\triangleleft}^{-1}\varepsilon)\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
of cardinality
d_{\triangleleft}

spanning
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, so that we deduce from (
3.3
) that

\dist(x,\spn_{\mathbb{Z}}(\Sigma_{\varepsilon}))<\varepsilon
, and indeed existence of arbitrarily small subsets
of
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
spanning
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
implies density of
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
in

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The lattice
\Gamma
is completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,
if and only if conditions
(D)
and
(I)
hold.

Proof.

The proof is split in two: we show that irrationality of
\Gamma
with
respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is equivalent to
(I)
, whereas irrationality of

\Gamma^{\dagger}

with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is equivalent to
(D)
. The first assertion
is direct:
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}=\ker(\pi_{\triangledown})
, so that we see directly
that
\Gamma
being irrational with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is equivalent to

\pi_{\triangledown}
being injective when restricted to
\Gamma
.

Now, suppose
(D)
, that
\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
is dense in

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. Then, for every
x_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma

such that

0<\left\lvert x_{\triangleleft}\cdot\gamma\right\rvert=\left\lvert x_{\triangleleft}\cdot\gamma_{\triangleleft}\right\rvert<1/2,

(3.5)

in particular
x_{\triangleleft}\not\in\Gamma^{\dagger}
and
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is irrational with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. On the other hand, if

\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
is not dense in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, use Lemma
3.3
to find
r>0
such that

r=\inf\left\{t:\spn(\mathbb{B}(0,t)\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft})=\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}\right\}.

(3.6)

Choose a codimension
1
subspace
\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
spanned by elements of

\overline{\mathbb{B}(0,r)}\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
and containing every
\gamma\in\mathbb{B}(0,r)\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft}
. (In particular for
d_{\triangleleft}=1
take
\mathbb{F}^{\perp}
to be the whole
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
.) Let
x\in\mathbb{F}^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
have norm
1
. By
construction,

\left\{y\in\mathbb{E}:x\cdot y\in(r/4,3r/4)\right\}\cap\Gamma=\varnothing,

(3.7)

so that Lemma
2.6
implies that
\spn(x)
is a

\Gamma^{\dagger}
-subspace, in other words
\Gamma^{\dagger}\cap\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}\neq\left\{0\right\}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We observe that complete irrationality of
\Gamma
with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
does not preclude
\Lambda
from containing periodic subsets
of rank strictly smaller than
d
, so that these are also included in our
analysis.

We call a window satisfying Condition
(
s
-Reg)
a
s
-regular
window. Boundedness of the window ensures that
\Lambda
is uniformly discrete, and non-empty interior of the window guarantees that
\Lambda
is relatively dense. The
Minkowski content of
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
will determine how good of an upper
bound we are able to get on the discrepancy.

3.2
BL equivalence classes

Recall that we call a subset
Y
of euclidean space a
separated net
if
it is uniformly discrete and relatively dense. Two separated nets
Y,Y^{\prime}
are
said to be
biLipschitz (BL) equivalent
if there is a biLipschitz map

\varphi:Y\to Y^{\prime}
. We now describe sufficient conditions under which we can obtain
BL equivalence
of a separated net
Y
,
a fortiori
of a cut and project set, to a lattice. We note
that since lattices are all in the same BL equivalence class, these
characterisations are often given in terms of equivalence to
\mathbb{Z}^{d_{\triangledown}}
.

Given a separated net
Y\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
, and a
search region
\Omega_{\triangledown}
and
\alpha>0
, put

\zeta_{\alpha}(\Omega_{\triangledown})=\max\left\{\frac{\alpha\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})}{\#(Y\cap\Omega_{\triangledown})},\frac{\#(Y\cap\Omega_{\triangledown})}{\alpha\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})}\right\}.

(3.8)

Set
Q_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
to be the unit cube and for
t>0
put

Z_{\alpha}(t)=\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{\triangledown}}}\zeta_{\alpha}(\mathbf{n}+tQ_{\triangledown})

(3.9)

The following lemma appears first as
[
BK02
]
for
d_{\triangledown}=2
and
[
ACG13
, Theorem
3.1]
for any
d_{\triangledown}\geqslant 2
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
be a separated net, and suppose that there is

\alpha>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log Z_{\alpha}(2^{n})<\infty.

(3.10)

Then,
Y
is BL-equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}^{d_{\triangledown}}
.

We use this lemma as well as our Theorem
1.2
in order to prove
Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Recall from Remark

3.2
that it is equivalent to translate
\Omega_{\triangledown}
or

\Gamma
by
\mathbf{z}_{\triangledown}
. Therefore, since Theorem
1.2
is uniform in
the parameter
\mathbf{z}
, we can read from (
1.4
) that for any

s
-regular window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
, any lattice
\Gamma

completely irrational with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and so that
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is

\psi
-repelled by
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
, any
\mathbf{z},\mathbf{z}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}
and
\delta>0
:

\frac{\#(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})\cap(\mathbf{z}^{\prime}_{\triangledown}+[0,t]^{d}))}{\alpha t^{d}}=1+O_{\Gamma,\Omega_{\triangleleft}}\left(\psi(t)^{-s+\delta s}\right),

(3.11)

where we have set
\alpha=\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}
. In
particular, if there is
\eta>0
so that
\psi(t)\gtrsim\log(t)^{\frac{1+\eta}{s}}
then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log Z_{\alpha}(2^{n})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log\left(1+\log(2^{-n})^{(-1+\delta)(1+\eta)}\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\delta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\eta+\delta+\eta\delta}.

In particular, taking
\delta
sufficiently small ensures that this is a
convergent sum. Therefore, by Lemma
3.6
the cut and project set

\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is BL equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Suppose that
\psi(r)
has a slower growth at infinity, that is that for every

\eta>0
, as
r\searrow 0
,
\psi(r)\lesssim\psi(r)^{\frac{1+\eta}{s}}
, and that

\Gamma^{\dagger}
is not
\psi^{\prime}
-repelled by
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
for any
\psi^{\prime}
with a
faster growth at infinity than this. Then, our methods
cannot prove that the associated cut and project set is BL equivalent to a
lattice from Lemma
3.6
. Indeed, in that situation the sum
at the end of (
3.12
) diverges. This means that from the
point of view of our results on discrepancy this is the largest class of
lattices which can be shown to be BL equivalent to a lattice; in order to
improve it one needs to either find weaker sufficient conditions or
improved results on the discrepancy. It is indicated in
[
RSW23
]
that the
bound
t^{-d}\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft});\mathbf{n}+[0,t]^{d})=o\left(1\right)
cannot be improved to a different decay rate on the right-hand
side that would hold for every cut and project set. In the appendix, Michael
Björklund and Tobias Hartnick give variance estimates proving that our
diophantine conditions is the weakest under which we can use Lemma

3.6
to prove BL equivalence of a cut and project set to a
lattice.

3.3
Acceptance domains

The goal of this section is to describe
r
-patterns in cut and project sets as
cut and project sets themselves. The upshot of this procedure is that any
theorem we prove for the statistics of cut and project sets is also valid for
statistics of
r
-patterns.

Definition 3.8
.

For
r>0
, define

\Gamma(r):=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma_{\triangledown}\in\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,r)\right\}.

(3.13)

For every
P\subset\Gamma(r)
, the
acceptance domain

A_{P}
is defined as

A_{P}:=\bigcap_{\gamma\in P}(\Omega_{\triangleleft}-\gamma_{\triangleleft})\cap\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma(r)\setminus P}(\inte((\Omega_{\triangleleft})^{c})-\gamma_{\triangleleft}),

(3.14)

where the interior and the complement of
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
are taken relative to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
.
An
r
-admissible pattern
is a subset
P\subset\Gamma(r)
containing the
origin and such that
A_{P}\neq\varnothing
.
The set of all acceptance domains of size
r
is denoted

\mathcal{A}(r):=\left\{A_{P}:P\text{ is a $r$-admissible pattern}\right\}.

(3.15)

Remark 3.9
.

•

We note a difference in our definition with the one from

[
KW21
, Definition 3.3]
: There patches
P
are thought to be subsets of
\Lambda
, but here they are subsets of
\Gamma(r)
.

•

We take the interior of the complements rather than of
the window as our windows are open whereas in
[
KW21
, Definition 3.3]
they are
closed; the advantage of our choice is that it makes acceptance
domains windows in their own right, see Lemma
3.10
.

•

Another difference to
[
KW21
, Definition 3.3]
is that we consider in the intersections
all

\gamma\in\Gamma(r)
rather than considering only the ’slab’ of them so that

\gamma_{\triangleleft}\in\Omega_{\triangleleft}-\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. This has no bearing on the
definition: since
0\in P
for all
r
-admissible patterns,

\gamma_{\triangleleft}\not\in\Omega_{\triangleleft}-\Omega_{\triangleleft}
implies that

\Omega_{\triangleleft}\cap(\Omega_{\triangleleft}-\gamma_{\triangleleft})=\varnothing
. In other words, if such a
\gamma\in P
, then
A_{P}=\varnothing
. If such a
\gamma\not\in P
,
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\inte((\Omega_{\triangleleft})^{c})-\gamma_{\triangleleft}
so that intersecting with that set changes nothing. In
the end, the intersections in (
3.14
) can always be
supposed to be intersections over a finite set.

•

In
[
KW21
, Definition 3.3]
the
windows are assumed to be in a
generic
position (see
[
BG13
, Definition
7.2]
), so that
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}\cap\Gamma_{\triangleleft}=\emptyset
. We will not make such an assumption.

•

For every
r
-admissible pattern
P
the acceptance domain
A_{P}
has non-empty interior since it
is open and non-empty. As such, by the assumption
(D)
, there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
P_{r}(\lambda)=P_{\triangledown}
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
is a
s
-regular window and let
r>0
. Then for every

r
-admissible pattern
P\in\Gamma(r)
,
A_{P}
is a
s
-regular window.

Proof.

The domain
A_{P}
is a non-empty intersection of translates of
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and of the interior
of its complement. As such it is
bounded, open, and its
boundary is a union of boundary pieces for
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. Finiteness of
the upper
Minkowski content is stable under finite unions, so the boundary of
A_{P}
satisfies the same
condition as that of
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and we conclude that
A_{P}
is a
s
-regular
window.
∎

The following lemma completely characterises pattern equivalence in terms of
maximal
r
-admissible patterns and their acceptance domains. It has appeared in the literature multiple times in various forms. For a proof we refer to
[
KW21
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

For every
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

1.

P_{r}(\lambda)^{\triangle}-\lambda^{\triangle}
is the unique
r
-admissible pattern
P\subset\Gamma(r)
such that
P_{r}(\lambda)=P_{\triangledown}
.

2.

If
\zeta\in\Lambda
is such that
\zeta^{\star}\in A_{P_{r}(\lambda)^{\triangle}}
, then
\zeta
is
r
-pattern equivalent to

\lambda
. Conversely, if
\zeta
is
r
-pattern equivalent to

\lambda
, then
\zeta^{\star}
is in the closure of
A_{P_{r}(\lambda)^{\triangle}}
.

Remark 3.12
.

Let us make a few remarks about the statement, as
well as other definitions of acceptance domains in the literature.

•

For a window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
in a generic position, the statement

2
in the previous lemma does not
need to refer to the closure, and becomes an equivalence. In either
case, our results are the same
whether or not some

\gamma_{\triangleleft}
hit
\partial\Omega_{\triangleleft}
.

•

The previous lemma implies that our definition of acceptance
domains makes them a tiling of
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. If, rather, we are interested in
the frequency of
r
-clusters
of points in
\Lambda
, that is
subsets of
\Lambda
of diameter at most
2r
, the acceptance
domains take the form of (
3.14
) without the
intersection over translates of
\inte((\Omega_{\triangleleft})^{c})
. In particular, this turns
the relation “belongs to an acceptance domain” into a partial
order (through reverse inclusion) rather than an equivalence, with
that partial order expressing whether a cluster is a subset of
another up to translation. This corresponds to the definition of
acceptance domains found in, say
[
BG13
, Section
7.2]
and since acceptance domains are still

s
-regular windows (with the same proof as in Lemma

3.10
), our results on asymptotic frequency also
apply to them.

We finish this section with the statement of a theorem on discrepancies for the
frequences of
r
-patterns. From the above discussion, Theorem

3.16
follows directly from Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
, let
\mathrm{M}\subset\Lambda
be
the set of representatives for a
r
-pattern equivalence class. Then, there
is a regular
\Omega_{\triangleleft,\mathrm{M}}\subset\Omega_{\triangleleft}
so that
\partial(\Omega_{\triangleleft,{\mathrm{M}}})
has finite
(d_{\triangleleft}-s)
-dimensional Minkowski content so
that
\mathrm{M}=\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft,{\mathrm{M}}})
. In
particular, for every
\delta>0
there is
C_{\mathrm{M},s,\delta}
so that

\left\lvert\#(\mathrm{M}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,t))-\frac{\vol(\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,1))\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft,{\mathrm{M}}})}{\covol(\Gamma)}\right\rvert\leqslant C_{\mathrm{M},s,\delta}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-s+\delta s}

(3.16)

Of course, any improvement to Theorem
1.2
obtained when

grows at
speed

also applies here.

4
Lattice point counts and the statement of the general theorems

In this section, we reduce Theorem
1.2
to a lattice point count for

\Gamma
in an anisotropically expanding set. Counting lattice points in ever
expanding sets is a standard problem in the geometry of numbers, we refer to the
methods and results in

[
KY11
,
KY15
,
Lag20
]
, where lattice points
are counted in anisotropically expanding domains. The main difference in our
situation compared to these works is that the relevant
domains are products of domains in
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
; any expansion occurs
only along
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
. In particular, this allows us to consider sets whose

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
slices have very bad boundary regularity.

In that spirit, we start with the following
general lemma which tells us that we can reduce the count of elements
in a cut and project set to a sum over the lattice
\Gamma
in the total space.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice which is irrational with
respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
. Then

\#\left(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})\cap\Omega_{\triangledown}\right)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\gamma-\mathbf{z}).

(4.1)

Proof.

Irrationality of
\Gamma
with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
implies that

\pi^{\triangle}:\Lambda\to(\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap(\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is a bijection. By definition,
\lambda\in\Lambda\cap\Omega_{\triangledown}
if and only if
(\lambda^{\triangle})_{\triangledown}\in\Omega_{\triangledown}
and

(\lambda^{\triangle})_{\triangleleft}\in\Omega_{\triangleleft}
; in other words
\lambda^{\triangle}\in\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. We deduce that

\#\left\{\Lambda\cap\Omega_{\triangledown}\right\}=\#\left\{\Lambda^{\triangle}\cap(\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})\right\}=\#\left\{(\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap(\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})\right\}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\gamma-\mathbf{z}).

(4.2)

This is our claim.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We note that irrationality of
\Gamma
with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
is only
used to ensure that
\pi^{\triangle}
is well defined (and in particular that

\pi_{\triangledown}
is injective). This can also be ensured from taking a window
whose diameter is smaller than the element of
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
with smallest norm.

In order to simplify the statement of theorems, we now set the notation, for

\Omega=\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
,
t>0
,
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
a lattice, and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
,

N(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t):=\#((\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap(t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\gamma-\mathbf{z}).

(4.3)

We also slightly abuse notation and use the same symbol for the discrepancy in
the cut and project counting function and for the discrepancy in the lattice
point counting function, that is

\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)=N(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)-\frac{\vol(\Omega)t^{d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)}.

(4.4)

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that any estimates on the
discrepancy as defined in (
4.4
) transfers to estimates
on the discrepancy for cut and project sets as defined in
(
1.3
). The next theorems in this sections are
equivalent reinterprations of Theorems
1.2
and
1.9
in the
language of lattice point counting.
They are a refinement of
[
KY15
, Theorems
2.1–2.3]
and
[
Lag20
, Theorem 1.11]
, where looking at product sets
allows us to be more precise in the dependence of the remainder estimates on the
regularity of the window, and with precise control on diophantine properties of

\Gamma
, see Definition
1.1
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice completely irrational with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
so that
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\psi
-repellent with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be a
s
-regular
window and
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
be a convex set with

\mathrm{C}^{\frac{d_{\triangledown}-1}{2}}
boundary and principal curvatures uniformly bounded
away from
0
. If
\psi
grows slowly at infinity, for every

\delta>0
there are
C_{\Lambda,\delta},t_{0}>0
, such that for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and
every
t>t_{0}
,

\left\lvert\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)\right\rvert\leqslant C_{\Lambda,\delta}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-s(1-\delta)}.

(4.5)

If
\psi
grows at speed
\mu
at infinity, then for every

\delta>0
there are
C,t_{0}>0
such that for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and
t>t_{0}
,

\left\lvert\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)\right\rvert\leqslant Ct^{d_{\triangledown}-\frac{2sd_{\triangledown}}{(d_{\triangledown}+1)(s+\frac{1}{\mu})+2d_{\triangleleft}}+\delta}.

(4.6)

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
1.2
is an immediate corollary of the first part of Theorem

4.3
in the case where
\Omega_{\triangledown}
is the unit ball – note that by the above considerations

|\#(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})\cap t\Omega_{\triangledown})-\frac{\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}t^{d_{\triangledown}}|=|\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)|.

Further, Theorem

3.16
is also an immediate consequence, after observing that by
Lemma
3.10
, the representatives of an
r
-pattern form
cut and project set with a
s
-regular window.

Even when the boundary of the search region merely has finite perimeter we can
still obtain estimates on the discrepancy.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be a lattice completely irrational with respect to

\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
so that
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\psi
-repellent with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
. Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be a
s
-regular
window and
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
be a set with finite perimeter.
If
\psi
grows slowly at infinity, for every

\delta>0
there are
C,t_{0}>0
, such that for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and
every
t>t_{0}
,

\left\lvert\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)\right\rvert\leqslant Ct^{d_{\triangledown}}\psi(t)^{-s(1-\delta)}.

(4.7)

If
\psi
grows at speed
\mu
at infinity, then for every

\delta>0
there are
C,t_{0}>0
such that for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{E}
and
t>t_{0}
,

\left\lvert\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)\right\rvert\leqslant Ct^{d_{\triangledown}-\frac{d_{\triangledown}s}{d_{\triangledown}s+d_{\triangleleft}+\mu^{-1}}+\delta}.

(4.8)

Remark 4.6
.

We first observe that when
\psi
grows slowly at infinity, the
estimate on the discrepancy does not depend on convexity properties of the search
region. In fact, it depends only on the Diophantine properties of
\Gamma

and the regularity of the boundary of the window.

Theorems
4.3
,
4.5
(and hence Theorems
1.2
and
3.16
) are proved in Section
6
.
The two previous theorems are uniform bounds on the discrepancy
\Delta
with
respect to the parameter
\mathbf{z}
. When we average over this parameter
\mathbf{z}
we
obtain lower bounds without conditions on the lattice
\Gamma
. We
note that when the dimension
d_{\triangledown}\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,4)
there are arithmetic
obstructions which make our lower bounds slightly worse. This is similar to what is observed
in
[
PS01
,
KSS03
,
LP16
]
,
and is not an artifact of the proof methods. We note that for these lower bounds
it is only required that the window be bounded with finite volume, no other
regularity assumptions is made. Even the boundedness assumption can be weakened,
see Remark
8.2

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
be any bounded window with non-zero volume,
\Omega_{\triangledown}=\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,1)
,
\Omega=\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
be any lattice. Then, there exists
A,C,t_{0}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=0

(4.9)

and for every
t>t_{0}

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\left\lvert\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)\right\rvert\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}\geqslant Cf(t)t^{\frac{d_{\triangledown}-1}{2}},

(4.10)

where
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is defined as

f(t):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }d_{\triangledown}\not\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,4)\\
\exp(-A\log\log(t)^{4})&\text{if }d_{\triangledown}\equiv 1\,({\rm mod}\,4).\end{cases}

(4.11)

For the proof, see Section
8
. From this theorem we immediately obtain Theorem
1.9
and the following lower bounds on the best
possible upper bounds.

Corollary 4.8
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.7
, there are
C,t_{0}>0
such
that for every
t>t_{0}
there are two
subsets
\mathcal{S}_{+}(t)
and
\mathcal{S}_{-}(t)
of
\mathbb{E}/\Gamma
of positive measure so that

\forall\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{\pm}\qquad\qquad\pm\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)\geqslant Cf(t)t^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

(4.12)

5
Interlude I: approximation and mollification

5.1
Approximation of sets and volume estimates

In this section we will provide a smoothing of sets well-adapted to the products
of sets appearing in the study of pattern statistics. We use
\bullet\in\left\{\triangledown,\triangleleft\right\}
to
represent either direction since the construction itself is symmetric with
respect to the decomposition, but different choices end up being made in either
direction.

For
\Omega_{\bullet}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}
and
a_{\bullet}>0
we define

\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}:=\Omega_{\bullet}+\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(0,a_{\bullet})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}:=\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus(\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}.

(5.1)

These are exterior and interior approximations to

\Omega_{\bullet}
, respectively. We note that since
\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(0,a_{\bullet})
is an open
convex set containing
0
,

\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}=\Omega_{\bullet}\cup(\partial\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}=\Omega_{\bullet}\setminus(\partial\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}.

(5.2)

In particular we see that if
\Omega_{\bullet}
has empty interior then

\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}
is empty.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\bullet\in\left\{\triangledown,\triangleleft\right\}
, let
a_{\bullet},t_{\bullet}>0

and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}
. We have the identity

(a_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}t_{\bullet}}^{\pm}=a_{\bullet}\Omega_{t_{\bullet}}^{\pm}.

(5.3)

Proof.

The equality for
+
can be proven by observing that by linearity

a_{\bullet}(\Omega_{t_{\bullet}}^{+})=a_{\bullet}(\Omega_{\bullet}+\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(0,t_{\bullet}))=a_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet}+\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(0,a_{\bullet}t_{\bullet})=(a_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet})^{+}_{a_{\bullet}t_{\bullet}}.

(5.4)

Similarly and using this first result we have that

a_{\bullet}\Omega_{t_{\bullet}}^{-}=\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus a_{\bullet}((\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus\Omega_{\bullet})_{t_{\bullet}}^{+})=\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus(\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus a_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}t_{\bullet}}^{+}=(a_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet})_{a_{\bullet}t_{\bullet}}^{-}.

(5.5)

∎

We need the following volume estimate.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega_{\bullet}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}
be a bounded set whose boundary has
finite
s
-codimensional Minkowski content with respect to
\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}
, for
some
0\leqslant s\leqslant d_{\bullet}
. Then, there exists

C,\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that for every
0<a_{\bullet}<\varepsilon_{0}
we have

\vol\left(\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}\right)\leqslant Ca_{\bullet}^{s}.

(5.6)

Proof.

It follows from identity (
5.2
) that
\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}=(\partial\Omega_{\bullet})^{+}_{a_{\bullet}}
, i.e. is the tubular neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega_{\bullet}
of radius

a_{\bullet}
. The claim then follows from the definition of Minkowski
content.
∎

5.2
Anisotropic mollifiers of indicators

Now that we have found approximations of sets, it is time to find smooth
approximations to the indicator functions of those sets.

Let
\widetilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a smooth nonnegative bump function supported
in
[-1,1]
.
Define
\rho_{\bullet}\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{E}_{\bullet})
as

\rho_{\bullet}(\mathbf{x}_{\bullet})=\frac{\widetilde{\rho}(|\mathbf{x}_{\bullet}|)}{\int_{\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}}\rho(|\mathbf{x}_{\bullet}|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{\bullet}},

(5.7)

and
\rho\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{E})
as

\rho(\mathbf{x})=\rho_{\triangledown}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown})\rho_{\triangleleft}(\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft}).

(5.8)

For
a_{\bullet}>0
,

\bullet\in\left\{\triangledown,\triangleleft\right\}
define the bidisk

\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}):=\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,a_{\triangledown})\times\mathbb{B}_{\triangleleft}(0,a_{\triangleleft})\subset\mathbb{E},

(5.9)

and
the mollifier
\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}

to be

\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{a_{\triangledown}^{d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{d_{\triangleleft}}}\rho(a_{\triangledown}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{\triangleleft})

(5.10)

We observe that
\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}
is supported in

\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})
, is smooth and has unit mass. By separation of variables,
its Fourier transform
satisfies

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\rho_{\triangledown}\right](a_{\triangledown}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\rho_{\triangleleft}\right](a_{\triangledown}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangledown})

(5.11)

For any measurable
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
we define

f^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}:=[f*\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}](\mathbf{x}):=\int_{\mathbb{E}}f(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}.

(5.12)

Proposition 5.3
.

For
\bullet\in\left\{\triangledown,\triangleleft\right\}
, let
\Omega_{\bullet}\in\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}
and

a_{\bullet}>0
, and put
\Omega=\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. For all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{E}
,

\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{-}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x})\leqslant\chi_{\Omega}(\mathbf{x})\leqslant\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x}).

(5.13)

Proof.

We start with the rightmost inequality. Since
0\leqslant\chi_{\Upsilon}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}\leqslant 1
for any
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{E}
, it suffices to
show that for every
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
,

\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x})=1
. But by
definition of
\Omega^{+}_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}
,
\mathbf{x}-\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})\subset\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}
. Since
\supp(\rho_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}})\subset\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})
we deduce that

\displaystyle\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\rho_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}(\mathbf{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}\rho_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}(\mathbf{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle=1.

Similarly, to prove the leftmost inequality it suffices to show that for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{E}\setminus\Omega
, we have

\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{-}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x})=0
.
This time, observe that for
\bullet\in\left\{\triangledown,\triangleleft\right\}

\mathbf{x}-\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(0,a_{\bullet})\subset\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus\Omega_{\bullet}\right)_{a_{\bullet}}^{+}=\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{-}

(5.15)

so that
\mathbf{x}-\mathbb{D}(A_{\triangledown},A_{\triangleleft})\cap\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{-}=\varnothing
. This implies that

\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{-}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{D}(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}}^{-}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\rho_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}(\mathbf{y})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}=0,

(5.16)

proving our claim.
∎

6
Uniform upper bounds for the discrepancy

We now aim at proving Theorem
4.3
. Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be an open
set whose boundary has finite
s
-codimensional Minkowski content, and
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
be an open convex set satisfying the hypothesis of either Lemma

2.10
or
2.12
, and put
\Omega=\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. We
suppose without loss of generality that the parameter
\mathbf{z}=0
; as mentioned in
Remark
3.2
this can be achieved by translating
\Omega
instead
of
\Gamma
. For

t>0
, we put

N(\Omega;\Gamma;t)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\gamma)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{\Omega}(t^{-1}\gamma_{\triangledown},\gamma_{\triangleleft}).

(6.1)

Since our goal is to use the Poisson summation formula, we need to smooth out
this sum. For
a_{\bullet}>0
, define

\displaystyle N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}(\Omega;\Gamma;t)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{(t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}(\gamma)

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}[\chi_{t\Omega^{\pm}_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}\times\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}*\rho^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}](\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}[\chi_{\Omega^{\pm}_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}\times\Omega^{\pm}_{a_{\triangleleft}}}*\rho^{(a_{\triangledown}t^{-1},a_{\triangleleft})}](t^{-1}\gamma_{\triangledown},\gamma_{\triangleleft}),

where we went from the first to the second line using Lemma

5.1
, and the second to third changing variables in the
integral defining convolution.
It follows from Proposition
5.3
that

N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{-}(\Omega;\Gamma;t)\leqslant N(\Omega;\Gamma;t)\leqslant N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}(\Omega;\Gamma;t)

(6.3)

as such we now aim to find an asymptotic expression for

N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}(\Omega;\Gamma;t)
. We will
choose
a_{\triangledown}
and
a_{\triangleleft}
at the end of the process, as functions of
t
, the dimensions

d_{\triangledown}
and
d_{\triangleleft}
, as well as the parameter
s
. Recall from Section

2.3
that
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is the dual lattice of
\Gamma
,
that is
the lattice

\Gamma^{\dagger}:=\left\{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{E}:\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma\right\}.

(6.4)

From the Poisson summation formula, we have that

\displaystyle N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}(\Omega;\Gamma,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\covol(\Gamma)}\sum_{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}}[\mathcal{F}\chi_{(t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft})_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}^{(a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})}](\gamma^{\dagger})

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)}\sum_{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega_{t^{-1}A_{\triangledown}}^{\pm}}\right](t\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}\right](\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\rho_{\triangledown}\right](a_{\triangledown}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\rho_{\triangleleft}\right](a_{\triangleleft}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}).

This last sum can be further split into two terms, the term at
0
and the remainder:

N_{a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}(\Omega;\Gamma;t)=V(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})+R(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})

(6.6)

Here
V
, the volume term, is the term at
0
in the sum:

V(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})=\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{\pm}}\right](0)\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}\right](0)\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\rho_{\triangledown}\right](0)\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\rho_{\triangleleft}\right](0).

(6.7)

On the other hand
R
, the remainder term, is the sum over all other elements of
the dual lattice:

R(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown};a_{\triangleleft})=\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)}\sum_{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{\pm}}\right](t\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{\pm}}\right](\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\rho_{\triangledown}\right](a_{\triangledown}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})\left[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\rho_{\triangleleft}\right](a_{\triangleleft}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}).

(6.8)

6.1
The volume term

We first analyse
V
. The mollifiers
\rho_{\bullet}
were constructed
specifically to have unit mass so that
[\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\rho_{\bullet}](0)=1
.
On
to the Fourier transforms of indicators, we have that for any
s_{\bullet}>0

[\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\chi_{\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{\pm}}](0)=\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{\pm}).

(6.9)

Since

\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{-}\subset\Omega_{\bullet}\subset\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{+}

(6.10)

we deduce that

\left\lvert\vol(\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{\pm})-\vol(\Omega_{\bullet})\right\rvert\leqslant\vol(\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{s_{\bullet}}^{-}),

(6.11)

and inserting into (
6.7
) with
t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}
and
a_{\triangleleft}
in place
of
s_{\triangledown}
and
s_{\triangleleft}
gives us

\displaystyle\left\lvert V(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})-\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}\vol(\Omega)}{\covol(\Gamma)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol(\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{-})+\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})\vol(\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{-})

(6.12)

\displaystyle+\vol(\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{-})\vol(\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{-})\bigg)\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}\vol(\Omega)}{\covol(\Gamma)}.

Since we end up choosing
a_{\triangledown}=o\left(t\right)
and
a_{\triangleleft}=o\left(1\right)
in such a way that

\vol(\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{-})\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vol(\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{-})\to 0

as

t\to\infty
, it is sufficient to
bound only the first two terms in (
6.12
).
Lemma
5.2
tells us that as
t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}\to 0
and

a_{\triangleleft}\to 0
,

\vol(\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}}^{-})\lesssim t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\vol(\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{+}\setminus\Omega_{a_{\triangleleft}}^{-})\lesssim a_{\triangleleft}^{s}.

(6.13)

Putting together the last two displays we deduce that

\left\lvert V(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown};a_{\triangleleft})-\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}\operatorname{Vol}(\Omega)}{\covol(\Gamma)}\right\rvert\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\left(a_{\triangleleft}^{s}\vol(\Omega_{\triangledown})+t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}\vol(\Omega_{\triangleleft})\right)

(6.14)

6.2
The remainder term

Since

\rho_{\bullet}
is smooth
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\rho_{\bullet}
is a Schwartz function. That is, for every
K_{\bullet}>0

\left\lvert[\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\rho_{\bullet}](a_{\bullet}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet})\right\rvert\lesssim_{K_{\bullet}}\left(1+\left\lvert a_{\bullet}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet}\right\rvert\right)^{-K_{\bullet}}.

(6.15)

For
\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\chi_{\Omega_{a_{\bullet}}^{\pm}}
we use the estimate, uniform in

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet}

\left\lvert\left[\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}\chi_{\Omega^{\pm}_{a_{\bullet}}}\right](\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\triangleleft})\right\rvert\lesssim_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(1+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet}\right\rvert)^{-L_{\bullet}}

(6.16)

where

L_{\bullet}=\begin{cases}\frac{d_{\bullet}+1}{2}&\text{if $\Omega_{\bullet}$ is strictly convex
with principal curvatures bounded away from zero};\\
1&\text{if $\Omega_{\bullet}$ has finite perimeter};\\
s&\text{if $\Omega_{\bullet}$ is $s$-regular, $0<s<1$},\end{cases}

(6.17)

Combining (
6.16
) and
(
6.17
), we have for every
K_{\bullet}>d_{\bullet}-L_{\bullet}
(so that the sum converges)

\displaystyle\left\lvert R(\Omega;\Gamma;t;a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)}\sum_{\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{(1+|t\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}(1+|\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{\left(1+|a_{\triangledown}\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|\right)^{K_{\triangledown}}\left(1+|a_{\triangleleft}\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|\right)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}

(6.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}t^{d_{\triangledown}}\left(\underbrace{\sum_{0<|\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown}|<t^{-\sigma}}\frac{(1+|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{(1+|a_{\triangleleft}\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}}_{\Sigma_{1}:=}+\underbrace{\sum_{|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|>t^{-\sigma}}\frac{(1+|t\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}(1+|\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{\left(1+|a_{\triangledown}\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|\right)^{K_{\triangledown}}\left(1+|a_{\triangleleft}\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|\right)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}}_{\Sigma_{2}:=}\right),

where
\sigma>0
is a parameter to determine later.

First region: small
\gamma_{\triangledown}
.

To estimate
\Sigma_{1}
, we first use Peetre’s inequality to see that for every

\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0<|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|<t^{-\sigma}}\frac{(1+|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{(1+|a_{\triangleleft}(\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}+\xi_{\triangleleft}))^{K_{\triangleleft}}}\frac{(1+|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{L_{\triangleleft}}}{(1+|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-K_{\triangleleft}}}

(6.19)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+\diam(\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}))^{L_{\triangleleft}+K_{\triangleleft}}\sum_{0<|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|<t^{-\sigma}}\frac{(1+|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{(1+|a_{\triangleleft}(\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}+\xi_{\triangleleft})|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}.

Integrating both sides over
\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}
therefore gives us

\Sigma_{1}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\sum_{0<|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|<t^{-\sigma}}\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}}\frac{(1+|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}}{(1+|a_{\triangleleft}(\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi}.

(6.20)

Since
\Gamma^{\dagger}
is
\psi
-repulsive, in
this region
|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|>\psi(t^{\sigma})
, and in particular since

\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}
is bounded we see that
|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|>\psi(t^{\sigma})/2
as soon as
t
is large enough, whereas in this region

\gamma_{\triangledown}+\xi_{\triangledown}
is merely uniformly bounded. Therefore, we can unfold
the previous sum in that region to obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}\int_{|\xi_{\triangledown}|<2\diam(E/\Gamma^{\dagger})}\int_{|\xi_{\triangleleft}|>\psi(t^{\sigma})/2}(1+|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}(1+a_{\triangleleft}|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{-K_{\triangleleft}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{\triangleleft}\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{\triangledown}

(6.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}a_{\triangleleft}^{-K_{\triangleleft}}\int_{|\xi_{\triangleleft}|>\psi(t^{\sigma})/2}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi_{\triangleleft}}{\left\lvert\xi_{\triangleleft}\right\rvert^{L_{\triangleleft}+K_{\triangleleft}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}a_{\triangleleft}^{-K_{\triangleleft}}\psi(t^{\sigma})^{d_{\triangleleft}-L_{\triangleleft}-K_{\triangleleft}}.

Second region: larger
\gamma_{\triangledown}
.
In this region, we estimate
(1+|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}|)^{-L_{\triangleleft}}\lesssim 1
, and use Peetre’s inequality,
assuming that we choose
a_{\bullet}\leqslant 1
small, to get

\displaystyle\left(1+|a_{\bullet}\gamma^{\dagger}_{\bullet}|\right)^{-K_{\bullet}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+|a_{\bullet}(\gamma_{\bullet}^{\dagger}+\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet})|\right)^{-K_{\bullet}}\left(1+|a_{\bullet}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet}|\right)^{K_{\bullet}}

(6.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K,\Gamma}\left(1+|a_{\bullet}(\gamma^{\dagger}_{\bullet}+\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\bullet})|\right)^{-K_{\bullet}}.

We refine Peetre’s inequality on the
region

|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|>t^{-\sigma}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1+|t(\gamma_{\triangledown}+\xi_{\triangledown})|}{1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+t(|\gamma_{\triangledown}|+|\xi_{\triangledown}|)}{1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}|}

(6.23)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1+t(|\gamma_{\triangledown}|+\diam(\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}))}{1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}|}.

It is a straightforward calculus exercise to see that for every
t,c>0
,
the function

x\mapsto\frac{1+t(x+c)}{1+tx}

(6.24)

is strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, so that the right-hand side in
(
6.23
) is bounded by the value at
|\gamma_{\triangledown}|=t^{-\sigma}
. Evaluating gives us

(1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}\lesssim_{L_{\triangledown},\Gamma}t^{L_{\triangledown}\sigma}(1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}+\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}.

(6.25)

Putting all these estimates back into
\Sigma_{2}
, integrating over

\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}
then unfolding the sum gives us

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K_{\bullet},\Gamma,L_{\bullet}}t^{L_{\triangledown}\sigma}\sum_{|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}|>t^{-\sigma}}\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma^{\dagger}}\frac{(1+t|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}}{(1+a_{\triangledown}|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{K_{\triangledown}}(1+a_{\triangleleft}|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{\dagger}+\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi}

(6.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K_{\bullet},\Gamma,L_{\bullet}}t^{L_{\triangledown}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{E}}\frac{(1+t|\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{-L_{\triangledown}}}{(1+a_{\triangledown}|\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{K_{\triangledown}}(1+a_{\triangleleft}|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi}.

Changing variables as
(\xi_{\triangledown},\xi_{\triangleleft})\mapsto(a_{\triangledown}^{-1}\xi_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft}^{-1}\xi_{\triangleleft})
gives us

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K_{\bullet},\Gamma,L_{\bullet}}\frac{t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}}}{a_{\triangledown}^{d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{d_{\triangleleft}}}\int_{\mathbb{E}}\frac{|\xi_{\triangledown}|^{-L_{\triangledown}}}{(1+|\xi_{\triangledown}|)^{K_{\triangledown}}(1+|\xi_{\triangleleft}|)^{K_{\triangleleft}}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\xi}

(6.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K_{\bullet},\Gamma,L_{\bullet}}t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{-d_{\triangleleft}},

as long as
K_{\triangleleft}>k-d
, and we take
K_{\triangledown}
arbitrary large
(since it plays no role in the asymptotics appart from the constant in
front).

Combining the remainder terms.
Summing up
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}

we obtain in the end that

|R(\Omega,\Gamma,t,a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft})|\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,K_{\bullet},L_{\bullet}}t^{d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{-K_{\triangleleft}}\psi(t^{\sigma})^{d_{\triangleleft}-L_{\triangleleft}-K_{\triangleleft}}+t^{d_{\triangledown}-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{-d_{\triangleleft}}.

(6.28)

6.3
A balancing act

Along the proof, we have introduced arbitrary parameters
a_{\triangledown},a_{\triangleleft},K_{\triangleleft}
and
\sigma
; it is now time to choose them carefully in order to obtain
the required bounds on the discrepancy. Note that
a_{\bullet}
will be functions
of
t
, which is our asymptotic parameter; as such any constants in asymptotic
estimates is not allowed to depend on them. On the other hand,
\sigma
and

K_{\triangleleft}
will depend only on
\psi
which is part of the geometry of the
cut and project set, so our asymptotic estimates may depend without issue from
them. Technically,
K_{\triangledown}
is also arbitrary but it plays no
role in the asymptotics appart from needing to be large enough for some integral
to converge. Summing up the contributions from
\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}
and the
remainder term and factoring
t^{d_{\triangledown}}
out we arrive to

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,K_{\triangleleft}}\,\,a_{\triangleleft}^{s}+t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}+a_{\triangleleft}^{-K_{\triangleleft}}\psi(t^{\sigma})^{d_{\triangleleft}-L_{\triangleleft}-K_{\triangleleft}}+t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}a_{\triangleleft}^{-d_{\triangleleft}}.

(6.29)

Since
d_{\triangleleft}-L_{\triangleleft}>0
and
\psi(t^{\sigma})\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
, we see immediately that if we choose
a_{\triangleleft}(t)\lesssim\psi(t^{\sigma})^{-1}
the third term in (
6.29
) is unbounded. As
such, we choose
a_{\triangleleft}(t)=\psi(t^{\sigma})^{-1+\delta}
for some fixed

0<\delta<1
(recalling that we assumed in our earlier analysis that

a_{\triangleleft}
should be sufficiently small). Once
\delta
is fixed, taking
K_{\triangleleft}

arbitrarily large will make the the third term smaller than the first. From
these choices we are now left with

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,K_{\triangleleft},\delta}\psi(t^{\sigma})^{-s+\delta s}+t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}+t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}}\psi(t^{\sigma})^{-d_{\triangleleft}(-1+\delta)}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}.

(6.30)

There are now two situations, depending on the growth of
\psi
at infinity.

Case 1:
\psi
grows slowly at infinity

In this situation
\psi(t^{\sigma})\lesssim_{\varepsilon}t^{\varepsilon}

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Here, the first term on
the right-hand side of (
6.30
) would preclude any polynomial
bound on
t^{-d}\Delta
, but choosing
a_{\triangledown}=t^{1/2}
and
\sigma=1
, we
see that the second and third term are bounded above by a negative power of
t
.
Therefore, in this situation we obtain

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,K_{\triangleleft},\delta}\psi(t)^{-s+\delta s}

(6.31)

for any
\delta>0
.

Case 2:
\psi
grows at speed
\mu
at infinity.

In this case
\psi(t^{\sigma})\asymp t^{\sigma\mu}
and we can rewrite (
6.30
) as

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,K_{\triangleleft},\delta}t^{-\sigma\mu s+\delta\sigma\mu s}+t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}+t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangleleft}\sigma\mu(-1+\delta)}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}.

(6.32)

From this we see that the discrepancy will be made as small as possible if we
choose the parameters
0<\sigma<1
,
0<a_{\triangledown}\ll t
, and
0<\delta<1
in such a way that

t^{\sigma\mu(-s+\delta s)}=t^{-1}a_{\triangledown}=t^{-(1-\sigma)L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangleleft}\sigma\mu(-1+\delta)}a_{\triangledown}^{L_{\triangledown}-d_{\triangledown}}.

(6.33)

Indeed, the first two terms are independent of each other and have opposite
monotonicity with the third term in every parameter. This leads directly to
taking
a_{\triangledown}=t^{1+\sigma\mu(-s+\delta s)}
.
Replacing in the third term and equating with the first we choose

\sigma=\frac{d_{\triangledown}}{s\mu(1-\delta)(d_{\triangledown}-L_{\triangledown}+1)+L_{\triangledown}+(1-\delta)d_{\triangleleft}\mu}

(6.34)

so that

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,\delta}\,\,t^{\frac{-d_{\triangledown}(1-\delta)}{(1-\delta)(d_{\triangledown}-L_{\triangledown}+1+\frac{d_{\triangleleft}}{s})+\frac{L_{\triangledown}}{\mu s}}}

(6.35)

We see that this exponent is smaller when

\delta
approaches
0
, which means that in the end, for any
\delta>0
we
get

t^{-d_{\triangledown}}\Delta(\Gamma;t;\Omega)\lesssim_{\Omega,\Gamma,\delta}\,\,t^{\frac{-d_{\triangledown}}{d_{\triangledown}-L_{\triangledown}+1+\frac{d_{\triangleleft}}{s}+\frac{L_{\triangledown}}{\mu s}}+\delta}.

(6.36)

∎

8
Averaged lower bounds for the discrepancy

In this section, we prove the averaged bounds on the discrepancy. Our first
lemma proves that for every cut and project set, no matter how bad the window or
the search region is, the average of the discrepancy
over translates of the lattice is zero.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\Omega_{\triangleleft}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
,
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
and put

\Omega=\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. For every lattice
\Gamma
and
t>0

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\Delta(\Omega;\Gamma+\mathbf{z};t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=0.

(8.1)

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{\Omega}(\gamma-\mathbf{z})-\frac{\vol(\Omega)}{\covol(\Gamma)}.

(8.2)

Integrating over
\mathbb{E}/\Gamma
a sum over elements of

\Gamma
is the same as integrating over
\mathbb{E}
without taking the sum, in
other words:

\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=-\vol(\Omega)+\int_{\mathbb{E}}\chi_{\Omega}(-\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}=0

(8.3)

∎

Lower bounds on the averaged discrepancy, Proof of Theorem

4.7

When the dimension
d_{\triangledown}=1
, the claim is simply that there is a constant bound below
for the average of the discrepancy, which is readily seen to hold. We now assume
that
d_{\triangledown}\geqslant 2
.
This proof is in the same spirit as
[
DT82
, Lemma 1]

and
[
LP16
, Section 4]
. Recall that for this theorem

\Omega_{\triangleleft}
is any bounded window with nonzero volume, and
\Omega_{\triangledown}=\mathbb{B}_{\triangledown}(0,1)
.
We first observe that
\Delta(\Omega,\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)

is a
\Gamma
-periodic function in the parameter
\mathbf{z}
, it therefore makes
sense to compute the coefficients at
\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}
of its Fourier series:

\widetilde{\Delta}(\Omega,\Gamma,t)_{\gamma^{\dagger}}:=\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma;\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft}))\mathbf{e}(\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

(8.4)

Lemma
8.1
tells
us that the zero’th Fourier coefficient vanishes, for other
\gamma^{\dagger}\in\Gamma^{\dagger}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we obtain
in the same way as
in (

)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}(\Omega,\Gamma,t)_{\gamma^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{E}/\Gamma}\left(-\frac{\vol(\Omega)}{\covol(\Gamma)}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\gamma-\mathbf{z})\right)\mathbf{e}(\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{E}}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\mathbf{z})\mathbf{e}(-\gamma^{\dagger}\cdot\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{F}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}\times\Omega_{\triangleleft}}](-\gamma^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}}](-\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}](-\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft})

\displaystyle=t^{d}[\mathcal{F}_{\triangledown}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}](-t\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangledown})[\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}](-\gamma^{\dagger}_{\triangleleft}).

It follows from the triangle inequality that,

\left\|\Delta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{E}/\Gamma)}\geqslant\left\|\widetilde{\Delta}\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\dagger})},

(8.6)

so that finding large Fourier coefficients implies lower bounds on the

Let us first examine the term
\mathcal{F}_{\triangleleft}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}
. We assumed

we denote by

\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft}):=\pi_{\triangledown}((\Gamma+\mathbf{z})\cap\pi_{\triangleleft}^{-1}(\Omega_{\triangleleft}))

(A.1)

the associated cut and project set. If
\Lambda
is such a cut and project set and
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
is a bounded Borel subset, then we define the
discrepancy
of
\Lambda
with respect to
\Omega_{\triangledown}
as

\Delta(\Lambda;\Omega_{\triangledown})=\#(\Lambda\cap\Omega_{\triangledown})-\frac{\vol_{\triangledown}(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol_{\triangleleft}(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)}.

(A.2)

In the study of the discrepancy of cut and project sets one can vary the
underlying lattice
\Gamma
, underlying window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and the bounded set

\Omega_{\triangledown}
. We will always assume that
\Omega_{\triangledown}
is a fixed set of bounded perimeter and study its dilates

t\Omega_{\triangledown}
. In this specific case we have
\vol_{\triangledown}(t\Omega_{\triangledown})=t^{d_{\triangledown}}\vol_{\triangledown}(\Omega_{\triangledown})
, and hence formula (
A.2
) reduces to the one from
Theorem
1.2
in the introduction. Moreover we have
\Delta(\Lambda;\Omega_{\triangledown})=o(t^{d_{\triangledown}})
which means that the number of points in dilates of

\Omega_{\triangledown}
is
asymptotically proportional to the volume of
\Omega_{\triangledown}
with an error term given
by the discrepancy.
If we fix the window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
and only vary the lattice by translations,
then we can consider the discrepancy as a function

\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}:\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\quad(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)\mapsto\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t):=\Delta(\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft}),t\Omega_{\triangledown}).

In the context of the bi-Lipschitz problem for model sets one is mostly interested in estimates of the form

\varphi^{-}_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(t)\leq\sup_{\Gamma+\mathbf{z}\in\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}}|\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)|\leq\varphi^{+}_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(t),

which are
uniform
in the torus variable. However, there is also some
interest in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-estimates
of the form

\varphi^{p,-}_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(t)\leq\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}}|\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}\leq\varphi^{p,+}_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(t).

This appendix is specifically concerned with lower
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-bounds for the
discrepancy for specific choices of
\Gamma
and
t
(and
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
given by
Euclidean balls of some “generic” radius or cylinders of some “generic”
sidelength), i.e. we will provide lower bounds for the
number variance

\mathrm{NV}_{t}(\Gamma,\Omega_{\triangleleft}):=\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}}|\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\Gamma+\mathbf{z},t)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}.

(A.3)

As the term “number variance” suggests, this
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm can be interpreted
as the variance of a certain random variable and hence can be estimated using
probabilistic tools. Before we discuss this probabilistic interpretation, we
state our main result. For this we need the following notion. We denote by
\|x\|
the nearest distance from a real number
x
to an integer and
say that an increasing function
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is
increasing
slowly at infinity
if
\psi(r)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}r^{\varepsilon}
for every

\varepsilon>0
as
r\to\infty
. Typical examples of such functions are given by
\psi(r)=\log(1+r)^{\beta}
for some fixed
\beta>0
or
\psi(r)=\log\log(e+r)
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a function which is increasing
slowly at infinity.

(i)

a
is a
Liouville number
if there is a sequence of integers
(q_{n})
so that

q_{n}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|q_{n}a\|^{-1/n}\geqslant q_{n}.

(ii)

a
is a
\psi
-Liouville number
if there is a sequence of integers
\left\{q_{n}\right\}
so that

q_{n}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(\|q_{n}a\|^{-1})\geqslant q_{n}.

(A.4)

Remark A.2
.

Every
\psi
-Liouville number is a Liouville number, and conversely every Liouville number is
\psi
-Liouville for
some

\psi
. Indeed, we can just set

\psi(\|q_{n}a\|^{-1}):=\|q_{n}a\|^{-1/n}
, and
interpolate linearly. There exist
\psi
-Liouville numbers for any
\psi
which increases slowly at infinity.

There is a similar notion for lattices:

Definition A.3
.

Let
\psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be slowly increasing at infinity. A
lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}

is said to be
\psi
-Liouvillean
(with respect to the splitting
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
) if it is irrational with respect
to
\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
and there is a sequence
\left\{\gamma^{(n)}\right\}=\left\{(\gamma_{\triangledown}^{(n)},\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{(n)})\right\}\subset\Gamma\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma_{\triangledown}^{(n)}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{(n)}|=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad|\gamma_{\triangleleft}^{(n)}|\leq\psi(|\gamma_{\triangledown}^{(n)}|^{-1})\quad\textrm{for all $n$}.

(A.5)

It is called a
Liouvillean
if it is
\psi
-Liouvillean for some
\psi
.

Example A.4
.

We can construct
\psi
-Liouvillean lattices for any slowly increasing
\psi
: Let
e_{\triangledown}\in\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown},e_{\triangleleft}\in\mathbb{E}_{\triangleleft}
be
unit vectors and further decompose the splitting

(A.6)

where
\mathbb{F}_{\bullet}=\mathbb{E}_{\bullet}\ominus\spn(e_{\bullet})
. Putting
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
, let
\widetilde{\Gamma}\subset\mathbb{F}
be a lattice which is irrational with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{\triangledown}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{\triangleleft}
. If

is a
\psi
-Liouville number and

(A.7)

is a

so that

\psi(\|aq_{n}\|^{-1})\geqslant q_{n}

m_{n}
to be the nearest integer to

\gamma^{(n)}=q_{n}(ae_{\triangledown}+e_{\triangleleft})-m_{n}(e_{\triangledown}+e_{\triangleleft})\in\Gamma

and this sequence makes

A.2
The diffraction formula for the number variance

It will be convenient for us to use the language of point processes; see e.g.
[
Kal17
]
for background. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
M(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by
\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of locally finite (i.e. closed and discrete) subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We consider
\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as a subset of
M(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by identifying each set
\Lambda
with its Dirac comb
\delta_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\delta_{x}
. Every bounded Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
with bounded support defines a
linear statistic

\mathcal{P}f:M(G)\to\mathbb{C},\quad p\mapsto p(f),

and we equip
\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset M(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the smallest
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
for which all of these linear statistics are measurable. Now assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is some auxiliary probability space on which
\mathbb{R}^{n}
acts measurably, preserving
\mathbb{P}
; then an equivariant measurable map

\Lambda:\Omega\to\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\omega\mapsto\Lambda_{\omega}.

is called a
stationary simple point process
with
distribution

\mu=\Lambda_{*}\mathbb{P}
. We will only consider processes which are
locally square-integrable
in the sense that the real-value random variable

\Lambda\cap B:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\quad z\mapsto\#(\Lambda_{z}\cap B)

is square-integrable for every bounded Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
; note that this is automatically the case if
\Lambda_{\omega}
is almost surely
r
-uniformly discrete for some fixed
r>0
.

Definition A.7
.

If
\Lambda:\Omega\to\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is locally square-integrable, then the random variable

\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda):\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\quad\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda)(z):=\#(\Lambda_{z}\cap B)-E[\Lambda\cap B].

is called the
discrepancy
of
\Lambda
on
B
, and the variance

N_{B}(\Lambda):=\mathrm{Var(\Lambda\cap B)}=\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda)(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)

If
\Lambda:\Omega\to\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is locally square-integrable, then the random variable

\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda):\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\quad\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda)(z):=\#(\Lambda_{z}\cap B)-E[\Lambda\cap B].

is called the
discrepancy
of
\Lambda
on
B
, and the variance

N_{B}(\Lambda):=\mathrm{Var(\Lambda\cap B)}=\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{disc}_{B}(\Lambda)(\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)

is called the
number variance
of
\Lambda
at
B
.

Note that the number variance is finite, since
\Lambda\cap B
is square-integrable.

Remark A.8

(Diffraction formula)
.

According to
[
BH23
, Prop. 2.3]
there exists a positive-definite signed Radon measure
\eta_{\Lambda}
, the
(reduced) autocorrelation measure
of
\Lambda
, such that

N_{B}(\Lambda)=\eta_{\Lambda}(\chi_{B}\ast\chi_{B}^{*}),

(A.10)

where
\chi_{B}
denotes the characteristic function of
B
. The Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\eta_{\Lambda}
is a positive Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, called the

(reduced) diffraction
of
\Lambda
, and it follows from (
A.10
)
that for sufficiently regular
B
we have

N_{B}(\Lambda)=[\mathcal{F}\eta_{\Lambda}](|\mathcal{F}\chi_{B}|^{2}).

(A.11)

This is called the
diffraction formula
for the number variance.

Example A.9
.

We return to the general setting of Subsection
A.1
. If we fix the lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{E}
and the window
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
then the map

\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft}):\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}\to\mathcal{LF}(\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}),\quad\Gamma+\mathbf{z}\mapsto\Lambda(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangleleft})

is a stationary simple point process with respect to the unique
\mathbb{E}
-invariant probability measure on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{E}
, called the
cut and project process
associated with
\Gamma
and
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
. For a bounded Borel set
\Omega_{\triangledown}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\triangledown}
we have

E[\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft})\cap B]=\frac{\vol_{\triangledown}(\Omega_{\triangledown})\vol_{\triangleleft}(\Omega_{\triangleleft})}{\covol(\Gamma)},

and hence the discrepancy of
\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is given by

\mathrm{disc}_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}(\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft}))(\Gamma+\mathbf{z})=\Delta_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}(\Gamma+\mathbf{z};\Omega_{\triangledown}).

On the other hand, it follows from classical results of Meyer
[
Mey70
]
that the reduced diffraction of
\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft})
is given by
[
BH23
, Thm. 2.9]

\mathcal{F}\eta=\frac{1}{\covol(\Gamma)^{2}}\,\sum_{\xi=(\xi_{\triangledown},\xi_{\triangleleft})\in\Gamma^{\dagger}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}|[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}](\xi_{\triangleleft})|^{2}\cdot\delta_{\xi_{\triangledown}}.

The diffraction formula (
A.11
) for the number variance of

\Lambda(-;\Omega_{\triangleleft})
may thus be stated as follows; here we use that since

\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}}(x_{\triangledown})=\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}(x_{\triangledown}/t)
we have

[\mathcal{F}\chi_{t\Omega_{\triangledown}}](\xi_{\triangledown})=t^{d_{\triangledown}}[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}](t\xi_{\triangledown})
.

Proposition A.10
.

If
\Omega_{\triangleleft}
has sufficient Fourier decay, then for all
t>0
we have

\mathrm{NV}_{t}(\Gamma,\Omega_{\triangleleft})=\frac{t^{2d_{\triangledown}}}{\covol(\Gamma)^{2}}\,\sum_{\xi=(\xi_{\triangledown},\xi_{\triangleleft})\in\Gamma^{\dagger}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}|[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangledown}}](t\xi_{\triangledown})|^{2}|[\mathcal{F}\chi_{\Omega_{\triangleleft}}](\xi_{\triangleleft})|^{2}.\qed

.

If we define

.

Since

\displaystyle=\left|J_{d_{\triangleleft}/2}(r\tau_{n})-\left(\frac{2}{\pi r\tau_{n}}\right)^{1/2}\cos(r\tau_{n}-\varphi)+\left(\frac{2}{\pi r\tau_{n}}\right)^{1/2}\cos(r\tau_{n}-\varphi)\right|

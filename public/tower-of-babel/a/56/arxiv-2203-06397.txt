Brownian fluctuations of the interface in a system with two linear attracted components and white noises
Tran Hoa Phu

Abstract
We concern the analysis of the long time behavior of interfaces in systems with two components. Each component evolves according to 1-d Allen-Cahn equation with Neumann boundary conditions, perturbed by small space-time white noise and with symmetric double well potential in the interval [ − ϵ − 1 , ϵ − 1 ] [-\epsilon^{-1},\epsilon^{-1}] . The two components interact with each other by an attractive linear force. Instantons are defined as the stationary solution of the Allen-Cahn equation without noise which connects two pure phases. We prove that for time t = ϵ − 1 t=\epsilon^{-1} , in the limit ϵ → 0 \epsilon\rightarrow 0 , when initial states are close to an instanton, two components stay close to the same instanton, whose center moves as a Brownian motion.

\displaystyle+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}H_{t-s}\bigg(|m_{1,s}-m_{1,s}^{*}|+|m_{2,s}-m_{2,s}^{*}|\bigg)(x)ds

Since
e^{-2\lambda t}
and
e^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\;(s<t)
are bounded by
1
, we get

|q_{t}(x)|\leq(H_{t}|q_{0}|)(x)+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}\bigg(|m_{1,s}-m_{1,s}^{*}|+|m_{2,s}-m_{2,s}^{*}|\bigg)(x)ds

(6.29)

Since
m_{1,t}-m_{1,t}^{*}=\dfrac{1}{2}(p_{t}+q_{t})
and
m_{2,t}-m_{2,t}^{*}=\dfrac{1}{2}(p_{t}-q_{t})
, it is easy to derive

|m_{1,t}-m_{1,t}^{*}|+|m_{2,t}-m_{2,t}^{*}|\leq|p_{t}|+|q_{t}|

(6.30)

As a result, let
w_{t}=|p_{t}|+|q_{t}|
, there is
c_{3}>0
such that

w_{t}(x)\leq(H_{t}w_{0})(x)+c_{3}\int_{0}^{t}ds(H_{t-s}w_{s})(x)ds

(6.31)

Iterating
M
times (
6.31
),

\displaystyle w_{t}(0)

\displaystyle\leq(H_{t}w_{0})(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{M}\dfrac{(c_{3}t)^{n}}{n!}(H_{t}w_{0})(0)+2\dfrac{(c_{3}t)^{M}}{M!}

(6.32)

\displaystyle\leq e^{c_{3}t}(H_{t}w_{0})(0)+2\dfrac{(c_{3}T)^{M}}{M!}\quad\forall t\leq T

By the Stirling formula

n!=\sqrt{2\pi}e^{-n}n^{n+1/2}\bigg(1+0(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}})\bigg)

(6.33)

The last term in (
6.32
) is bounded as follows, choosing
M\geq e^{2}c_{3}t
, then

\displaystyle\dfrac{(c_{3}T)^{M}}{M!}

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\bigg(M\log(c_{3}T)-M(\log{M}-1)\bigg)\leq C\exp\bigg({-M\log{\dfrac{M}{ec_{3}T}}}\bigg)

(6.34)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-e^{2}c_{3}T}

Furthermore,

\displaystyle(H_{t}w_{0})(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{t}(0,y)w_{0}(y)dy=\int_{|y|\geq VT}H_{t}(0,y)w_{0}(y)dy

(6.35)

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\int_{|y|\geq VT}H_{t}(0,y)dy\leq 2c_{4}\dfrac{1}{V\sqrt{T}}e^{-V^{2}T}\quad\forall t\leq T

for some
c_{4}>0
. This combines with (
6.32
) and (
6.34
), we get

w_{t}(0)\leq 2c_{4}\dfrac{1}{V\sqrt{T}}e^{-T(V^{2}-c_{3})}+2Ce^{-e^{2}c_{3}T}

(6.36)

Finally, by choosing
V^{2}\geq c_{3}+1
and note that (
6.30
), we obtain (
6.22
).
∎

Corrolary 6.1
.

If

m_{i,0}(x)=m_{i,0}^{*}(x)\quad\forall\;|x|\leq L+VT\quad\forall i=1,2

(6.37)

then

\sup_{t\leq T}\sup_{|x|\leq L}|m_{i,t}(x)-m_{i,t}^{*}(x)|\leq ce^{-T}\quad\forall i=1,2

(6.38)

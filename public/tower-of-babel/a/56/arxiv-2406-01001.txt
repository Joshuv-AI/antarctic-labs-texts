Discreteness Effects in the Post-Reconstruction Galaxy Power Spectrum
Naonori Sugiyama

Abstract
Recent studies have increasingly recognized the value of analyzing the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum for investigations into redshift space distortion (RSD) effects. In this paper, we present a novel theoretical model for the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum, designed for RSD analyses. In particular, we emphasize the importance of accounting for discrete effects arising from the reconstruction displacement vector, which have been overlooked. We specifically calculate these discrete effects, i.e., shot noise terms, within the framework of standard perturbation theory at the 1-loop level. In addition, we adopt a formulation that accounts for infrared (IR) effects to accurately model the non-linear damping of the Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) signal. Our model comprehensively integrates key physical phenomena relevant to the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum, such as gravitational non-linearities, RSD effects, bias effects, reconstruction effects, reconstruction-specific shot-noise effects, and the non-linear damping of the reconstructed BAO signal, thereby making it applicable to post-reconstruction RSD analyses.

II
Pre-reconstruction case

In preparation for the post-reconstruction case, we summarize the discrete effects in terms up to the fourth-order of the density fluctuations, including the power spectrum, bispectrum, and trispectrum.

II.1
Galaxy density fluctuations

Following
[
56
]
, we divide the space into the infinitesimal grid cells of volume
\delta V
. Each cell is allowed to contain at most one galaxy, which can be described by the occupation number
n_{i}
such that
n_{i}=1
if the
i
th cell contains a galaxy, and
n_{i}=0
otherwise. Therefore, for any natural number
n
, the occupation number satisfies

\displaystyle n_{i}=n_{i}^{2}=\dots=n_{i}^{n}\;.

(1)

The galaxy number density can be represented as

\displaystyle n_{\rm gal}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i}\,n_{i}\,\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{i})\;,

(2)

where
\bm{x}_{i}
denotes the position of the
i
th galaxy, and
\delta_{\rm D}
represents the three-dimensional delta function. The occupation number
n_{i}
is defined using the galaxy number density as

\displaystyle n_{i}=n_{\rm gal}(\bm{x}_{i})\delta V\;,

(3)

because the delta function is discretized as

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j})\xrightarrow[\rm discretized]{}\delta_{\rm K}(i,j)\,/\,\delta V\;,

(4)

where
\delta_{\rm K}(i,j)
represents the Kronecker delta, which equals
1
when
i=j
and
0
otherwise. The total number of galaxies is then calculated by summing over the occupation numbers,

\displaystyle N_{\rm gal}=\int d^{3}x\,n_{\rm gal}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i}n_{i}\;.

(5)

The galaxy mean number density is given by

\displaystyle\bar{n}=\frac{N_{\rm gal}}{V}\;,

(6)

where
V
is the survey volume.

In a manner analogous to the case with galaxies, the random number density is described by

\displaystyle n_{\rm ran}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i}\,m_{i}\,\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{i})\;,

(7)

where the
m_{i}=n_{\rm ran}(\bm{x}_{i})\delta V
represents the occupation number corresponding to random particles. The total number of random particles is then given by

\displaystyle N_{\rm ran}=\int d^{3}x\,n_{\rm ran}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i}m_{i}\;.

(8)

We define the ratio between
N_{\rm gal}
and
N_{\rm ran}
as

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{N_{\rm gal}}{N_{\rm ran}}\;.

(9)

To ensure that the discretization effects arising from random particles are sufficiently small compared to those originating from galaxies, the value of
\alpha
is conventionally chosen to be of the order of
10^{-2}
.
Furthermore, we assume that galaxies and random particles are never in the same position, leading to

\displaystyle n_{i}m_{i}=0\;.

(10)

Using the galaxy and random number densities, the galaxy density fluctuation is represented as

\displaystyle\delta(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\left[n_{\rm gal}(\bm{x})-\alpha n_{\rm ran}(\bm{x})\right]

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\sum_{i}\,(n_{i}-\alpha m_{i})\,\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{i})\;,

In Fourier space, this becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}xe^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}\delta(\bm{x})

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\sum_{i}\,(n_{i}-\alpha m_{i})\,e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}_{i}}\;,

where the tilde denotes a Fourier-transformed quantity.

Throughout the remainder of this paper, for the convenience of subsequent calculations, we introduce

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})=\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\,(n_{i}-\alpha m_{i})\,e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}_{i}}\;,

(13)

and rewrite the Fourier-transformed density fluctuations as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k})=\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\;.

(14)

This notation allows for the explicit description of the discrete effects in density fluctuations.

II.2
Power spectrum

Using the notation presented in Eq. (
13
), the power spectrum in the discrete representation is represented as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})P(\bm{k})\;,

(15)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes the ensemble average. In the left-hand side of the above equation, note that the summation is performed under the condition
i\neq j
. This is because the power spectrum is a measure for computing the correlation between two different galaxies, so it is necessary to avoid counting the same galaxy.

The effect of counting the same galaxy where
i=j
is called the shot-noise effect. In the case of
i=j
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(-\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{2}\left(1+\alpha\right)\,N_{\rm gal}\;,

(16)

where we used
n_{i}m_{i}=0
. Since
\alpha
in Eq. (
9
) is usually chosen to be sufficiently smaller than
1
, e.g., on the order of
10^{-2}
, neglecting it, this equation can be approximated as

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(-\bm{k})\approx\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{2}\,N_{\rm gal}\;.

(17)

Consequently, we derive the power spectrum with the shot-noise term as

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})}{V}\left\langle\left(\sum_{i\neq j}+\sum_{i=j}\right)\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(-\bm{k})\right\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})P_{\rm N}(\bm{k})\;,

where

\displaystyle P_{\rm N}(\bm{k})=P(\bm{k})+\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\;.

(19)

From Eq. (
18
), when density fluctuations are calculated from discrete particles, it is understood that the power spectrum computed from the product of two density fluctuations invariably contains the shot noise term.

II.3
Bispectrum

Although the main focus of this paper is on the power spectrum, the bispectrum is used to express the post-reconstruction power spectrum in Section
III.2
.

The bispectrum in the discrete representation is represented as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{123})B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})\;,

(20)

where
\bm{k}_{123}=\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{3}
.

As in the case of the power spectrum, when calculating the bispectrum from the product of three density fluctuations, shot-noise terms for the bispectrum appear.

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i,j,k}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{123})B_{\rm N_{\rm all}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})\;.

To calculate
B_{\rm N_{\rm all}}
, we decompose
\sum_{i,j,k}
into the following five components:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}=\sum_{i\neq j\neq k}+\sum_{i\neq j,i=k}+\sum_{i\neq j,j=k}+\sum_{i\neq k,i=j}+\sum_{i=j=k}\;.

(22)

The first term on the right-hand side of the above equation leads to the bispectrum, according to the definition in Eq. (
20
). To calculate the second term, using Eq. (
13
), we derive the following relation for two different wave vectors
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})

(23)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{2}\alpha\left(1+\alpha\right)\widetilde{n}_{{\rm ran}}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})\;.

Ignoring the term proportional to
\alpha
, this relation can be approximated as

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})\;.

(24)

Using Eq. (
24
), the second term is calculated as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j,i=k}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{123}\right)\frac{1}{\bar{n}}P(\bm{k}_{2})\;.

(25)

The third and fourth terms can be similarly calculated. Finally, the fifth term can be calculated as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{123})\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{2}\left(1-\alpha^{2}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{123})\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{3}\;,

where
\alpha^{2}
is ignored in the final line. Consequantly, we obtain

\displaystyle B_{\rm N_{\rm all}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})

(27)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\left[P(\bm{k}_{1})+P(\bm{k}_{2})+P(\bm{k}_{3})\right]+\frac{1}{\bar{n}^{2}}\;.

In the computation of the post-reconstruction power spectrum in Section
III.2
, the bispectrum with shot noise terms appears in a form different from
B_{\rm N_{\rm all}}
in Eq. (
27
). In this paper, we define the bispectrum with shot noise for the following special case as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j,k}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{123})B_{\rm N_{12}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})\;,

(28)

where

\displaystyle B_{\rm N_{12}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})=B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})+\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\left[P(\bm{k}_{1})+P(\bm{k}_{2})\right]\;.

(29)

In this
B_{\rm N_{12}}
, it is important to note that the exchange symmetry among
\bm{k}_{1}
,
\bm{k}_{2}
, and
\bm{k}_{3}
is no longer preserved. Only
\bm{k}_{1}
and
\bm{k}_{2}
are exchangeable.

II.4
Product of four density fluctuations, and trispectrum

In this subsection, we calculate the discrete effects that arise from the product of four density fluctuations. In doing so, Wick’s theorem allows for the decomposition into terms consisting of products of power spectra and terms originating from the trispectrum.

In our notation, the trispectrum is represented as follows:

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j\neq k\neq l}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\widetilde{\delta}_{l}(\bm{k}_{4})\right\rangle_{\rm c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{1234})T(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})\;,

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\rm c}
signifies that only the connected part is extracted when calculating the ensemble average.

Similar to the case of the bispectrum in Eq. (
28
), this paper considers the following special case.

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i\neq j,k,l}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{k}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{k}(\bm{k}_{3})\widetilde{\delta}_{l}(\bm{k}_{4})\right\rangle

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{2}\right)(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{3}+\bm{k}_{4}\right)P(\bm{k}_{1})P_{\rm N}(\bm{k}_{3})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3}\right)(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4}\right)P_{\rm N}(\bm{k}_{1})P_{\rm N}(\bm{k}_{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{4}\right)(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}\left(\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{3}\right)P_{\rm N}(\bm{k}_{1})P_{\rm N}(\bm{k}_{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}_{1234})T_{\rm N_{12}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})\;.

The first term on the right-hand side represents the product of the power spectra with and without the shot noise effect (
P\times P_{\rm N}
). The second and third terms are the products of two
P_{\rm N}
terms (
P_{\rm N}\times P_{\rm N}
). The last term corresponds to the trispectrum with shot noise terms. By performing calculations similar to those for the bispectrum in Section
II.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{\rm N_{12}}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\Big[B(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{4})+B(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{4},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3})

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt+B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{4},\bm{k}_{3})+B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{3},\bm{k}_{4})

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt+B(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{k}_{3}+\bm{k}_{4})\Big]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(1/\bar{n}\right)^{2}\Big[P(\bm{k}_{1})+P(\bm{k}_{2})+P(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{3})+P(\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{k}_{4})\Big]\;.

Here, note that only the exchange of
\bm{k}_{1}
and
\bm{k}_{2}
, as well as the exchange of
\bm{k}_{3}
and
\bm{k}_{4}
, are symmetric.

III
Post-reconstruction case

In this section, we calculate discrete effects in the post-reconstruction power spectrum.

III.1
Galaxy density fluctuations

To reconstruct the distribution of galaxies, the displacement vector used for reconstruction is derived from the observed galaxy density fluctuations as follows
[
28
]
:

\displaystyle\bm{s}(\bm{x})=i\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}}\bm{R}(\bm{p})\,\widetilde{\delta}(\bm{p})

(33)

with

\displaystyle\bm{R}(\bm{p})=\left(\frac{\bm{p}}{p^{2}}\right)\left(-\frac{W_{\rm G}(pR_{\rm s})}{b_{1,\rm fid}}\right)\;,

(34)

where
b_{1,\rm fid}
is the fiducial linear bias parameter input for reconstruction,
W_{\rm G}(pR_{\rm s})=\exp\left(-p^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}/2\right)
is a Gaussian filter function, and
R_{\rm s}
is the input smoothing scale.

The reconstruction of the galaxy distribution is the operation of moving the positions of galaxies and random particles by using
\bm{s}(\bm{x})
. The post-reconstruction density fluctuation is then given by
[
23
,
32
]

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm rec}(\bm{x})=\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\,\delta(\bm{x}^{\prime})\,\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}-\bm{s}(\bm{x}^{\prime}))\;.

(35)

Substituting Eq. (
11
) into Eqs. (
33
) and (
35
) results in

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm rec}(\bm{x})=\left(1/\bar{n}\right)\sum_{i}\,\left(n_{i}-\alpha m_{i}\right)\,\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{s}_{i})\;,

(36)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\bm{s}_{i}=i\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\bm{p}\cdot\bm{x}_{i}}\bm{R}(\bm{p})\sum_{j}\,\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{p})\;.

(37)

In Fourier space, Eq. (
36
) becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\rm rec}(\bm{k})=\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}}\;.

(38)

In Section
IV
, we will calculate the discretization effect included in the 1-loop correction of the power spectrum. For this purpose, we expand
e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}}
in Eq. (
38
) to the second order of
\bm{s}_{i}
:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\widetilde{\delta}_{\rm rec}(\bm{k})\approx\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\left[1+\left(-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}\right)^{2}\right]\;,

(39)

where each term is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\left(-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{p}_{2})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\Big[\left[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{p}_{2})\right]\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{p}_{1})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{p}_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{p}_{1})\right]\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{p}_{2})\widetilde{\delta}_{j}(\bm{p}_{1})\Big]\;,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}(\bm{k})\left(-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{s}_{i}\right)^{2}

(41)

\displaystyle=

IV.1
Density fluctuations

Z_{1}

\displaystyle 2\,Z_{2}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2})Z_{1}(\bm{k}_{1})Z_{1}(\bm{k}_{2})P_{\rm lin}(k_{1})P_{\rm lin}(k_{2})

IV.3
IR Limit

The infrared (IR) effect refers to the non-linear effects that arise from scales significantly larger than the scale of interest
k
, namely, from smaller wave numbers
p
. At the 1-loop level, it is possible to extract only the IR effect by taking the limit of
p/k\to 0
in Eq. (
60

k
limit. In the pre-reconstruction power spectrum, the 1-loop correction terms, given by
P_{22}
and
P_{13}
, cancel out in the IR limit
[
57
,
58
]
. This IR cancellation is known to hold at any order in perturbation theory
[
59
,
60
,
61
,
62
,
63
,
50
,
64
,
65
,
66
]
.

Furthermore, this non-perturbative IR cancellation has been shown to remain effective even after reconstruction
[
34
]
. However, this previous study on reconstruction did not take into account the discrete effects specific to reconstruction. In this section, as a preparation for the construction of the IR-resummed model in Section
V
, we compute the 1-loop correction term in the IR limit when discrete effects are included after reconstruction and show the occurrence of IR cancellation.

In Eqs. (
56

57

60

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\bm{k}-\bm{p})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[p/k\to 0]{}

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\bm{k})\;,

\displaystyle P_{\rm lin}(|\bm{k}-\bm{p}|)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[p/k\to 0]{}

\displaystyle P_{\rm lin}(k)\;.

(61)

In addition, the non-linear kernel functions are related to lower-order functions in the IR limit as follows:

\displaystyle Z_{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{p},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[p/k\to 0]{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{M}\cdot\bm{p}}{p^{2}}\right)Z_{1}(\bm{k})\;,

\displaystyle Z_{2}(-\bm{k},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[p/k\to 0]{}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{M}\cdot\bm{p}}{p^{2}}\right)Z_{1}(\bm{k})\;,

\displaystyle Z_{3}(\bm{k},\bm{p},-\bm{p})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[p/k\to 0]{}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3!}\left(\frac{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{M}\cdot\bm{p}}{p^{2}}\right)^{2}Z_{1}(\bm{k})\;,

where

\displaystyle M_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+f\hat{n}_{i}\hat{n}_{j}\;.

Leaving only terms of the order of

\displaystyle P_{\rm rec,22,IR}(\bm{k})=X(\bm{k})Z_{1}^{2}(\bm{k})P_{\rm lin}(k)\;,

\displaystyle P_{\rm rec,13,IR}(\bm{k})=-X(\bm{k})Z_{1}^{2}(\bm{k})P_{\rm lin}(k)\;,

\displaystyle X(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\left(\frac{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{M}\cdot\bm{p}}{p^{2}}\right)+\left[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{p})\right]Z_{1}(\bm{p})\right]^{2}P_{\rm lin}(p)

(65)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{p})\right]^{2}\;.

\displaystyle P_{\rm rec,1\mathchar 45loop,IR}(\bm{k})=P_{\rm rec,22,IR}(\bm{k})+P_{\rm rec,13,IR}(\bm{k})=0\;.

The function

In this section, we examine the discrete effects within the IR-resummed model for the post-reconstruction power spectrum, as proposed by

V.1
IR-resummed model construction

In this subsection, we give an overview of how to construct the IR-resummed power spectrum model. For a detailed discussion, see
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
45
,
53
,
54
,
55
,
23
,
34
]
. To simplify the explanation, we focus on the case of dark matter in real space, though the same approach applies to galaxies in redshift space.

The non-linear power spectrum can be generally decomposed into a term proportional to the linear dark matter power spectrum and a term consisting of mode coupling integrals
[
4
,
48
]
:

\displaystyle P(\bm{k})=G^{2}(\bm{k})P_{\rm lin}(k)+P_{\rm MC}(\bm{k})\;,

(70)

where
G
is called the propagator and
P_{\rm MC}
is the mode-coupling term.

In the IR limit, the propagator and the mode-coupling terms are calculated as

\displaystyle G^{2}(\bm{k})P_{\rm lin}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)\;,

\displaystyle P_{\rm MC}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)\,P_{\rm lin}(k)\;,

(71)

where
\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}_{\rm pp,\,\perp}
is given in Eq. (
69
). At the SPT 1-loop level,
P_{13}
corresponds to the propagator term
G^{2}P_{\rm lin}
, while
P_{22}
corresponds to the mode-coupling term
P_{\rm MC}
. Expanding
e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}
, we find that
P_{13}=-k^{2}\sigma^{2}P_{\rm lin}
and
P_{22}=k^{2}\sigma^{2}P_{\rm lin}
. Substituting Eq. (
71
) into Eq. (
70
), the non-linearities cancel in the IR limit, leaving only the linear power spectrum:

\displaystyle P(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)+\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)\,P_{\rm lin}(k)

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\rm lin}(k)\;.

The
P_{\rm MC}
in the IR limit is proportional to
P_{\rm lin}
and has the BAO signal, but the actual behavior of the BAO within
P_{\rm MC}
is negligible. For example, the
P_{22}
term is known to have smooth curves in numerical calculations (e.g., see Figure
1
). Based on these numerical and experimental findings, we apply a procedure to remove the BAO signal from the mode coupling term in the IR limit. To achieve this, we decompose the linear matter power spectrum into a “wiggle” part (
P_{\rm w}
) containing only the BAO signal, and a “no-wiggle” part (
P_{\rm nw}
) without the BAO signal:
P_{\rm lin}(k)=P_{\rm w}(k)+P_{\rm nw}(k)

[
68
,
69
,
70
]
. We then replace
P_{\rm lin}
in
P_{\rm MC}
with
P_{\rm nw}
and construct the no-wiggle mode-coupling term:

\displaystyle P_{\rm MC,\,nw}(k)=\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm nw}(k)\;.

(73)

Using this no-wiggle mode-coupling term, we obtain the IR-resummed model at the linear level,

\displaystyle P(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)+\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm nw}(k)

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\rm nw}(k)+e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}P_{\rm w}(k)

This method of constructing an IR-resummed power spectrum model was first proposed by
Sugiyama and Spergel [50]
.

Subsequent studies
[
52
,

,

,

,
53
,
54
,
55
]
have highlighted the significance of the wiggle part in mode couplings. By decomposing the mode-coupling term into its wiggle and no-wiggle components, we obtain the following expression in the IR limit:

\displaystyle P_{\rm MC}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm nw}(k)

(75)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(e^{-k^{2}\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}}-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm w}(k)\;.

The parameter
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi^{2}}\left(1-j_{0}(pr_{\rm BAO})\right)P_{\rm lin}(p),

(76)

where
j_{0}
is the
0
th order spherical Bessel function and
r_{\rm BAO}\sim 110\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}
is the BAO scale. A version including
j_{2}
also exists, but it is not included here. This expression shows that the wiggle part from the propagator term cancels out completely with that from the mode-coupling term, and the remaining factor
e^{-k^{2}\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}}
describes the BAO non-linearity. Thus, the final expression for the power spectrum becomes

\displaystyle P(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\left(P_{\rm w}(k)+P_{\rm nw}(k)\right)+\left(1-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm nw}(k)

(77)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(e^{-k^{2}\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}}-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right)P_{\rm w}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\rm nw}(k)+e^{-k^{2}\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}}P_{\rm w}(k)\;.

Eq. (
74
) and Eq. (
77
) are similar, but the parameter
\sigma^{2}
describing the BAO non-linearity in Eq. (
74
) is replaced by
\sigma^{2}_{\rm BAO}
in Eq. (
77
). However,
Blas et al. [45]
point out that in a
\Lambda
CDM model, the numerical difference between
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
and
\sigma^{2}
is small. In fact,
Sugiyama [34]
shows that the difference between
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
and
\sigma^{2}
affects the power spectrum by about
0.2\%
, which is why the wiggle part in the mode-coupling term,
(e^{-k^{2}\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}}-e^{-k^{2}\sigma^{2}})P_{\rm w}
, is negligible in numerical calculations, as seen in Figures
1
. Hence, ignoring the wiggle part in the mode-coupling term, as in Eq. (
74
), is a good approximation for constructing a model that fits the observed power spectrum.

In the post-reconstruction case,
Sugiyama [34]
showed that the IR effect in the density fluctuations is mathematically similar to the pre-reconstruction case. This means that the method of constructing the pre-reconstruction IR-resummed model can be applied in the same way as in the post-reconstruction case. As a result, the non-linear damping of the BAO in the post-reconstruction power spectrum can be described by a single Gaussian damping function, as in the pre-reconstruction case
[
34
,
67
]
. It has long been thought that the post-reconstruction BAO behavior can be described by two exponential damping functions
[

,
44
,

,

]
, based on the Zel’dovich approximation (ZA)
[

. However,
Sugiyama [34]
showed that ZA cannot account for all post-reconstruction IR effects. These theoretical developments have provided the theoretical basis for the recent BAO analysis performed by the DESI project
[
72
]
.

Furthermore,
Sugiyama [34]
finds that the difference between
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
and
\sigma^{2}
after reconstruction is significantly smaller than before reconstruction. The post-reconstruction
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm BAO,\,rec}^{2}

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\int\frac{dp}{2\pi^{2}}\left(1-W(pR_{\rm s})\right)^{2}\left(1-j_{0}(pr_{\rm BAO})\right)P_{\rm lin}(p)\;.

As
p\to 0
, the factor
(1-W(pR_{\rm s}))^{2}
approaches zero, suppressing the contribution from large scales. This reduces the large-scale contribution around
r_{\rm BAO}\sim 110\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}
, meaning the impact of
j_{0}(pr_{\rm BAO})
becomes negligible. In fact,
Sugiyama [34]
shows that when

, the impact of the difference between
\sigma_{\rm BAO}^{2}
and
\sigma^{2}
on the post-reconstruction power spectrum is extremely small, on the order of

. This result demonstrates that the approximation of neglecting the wiggle part in the mode-coupling term holds very well after reconstruction.

V.2
IR-resummed model at the 1-loop level

Based on the discussion in the previous subsection, we construct an IR-resummed model of the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum at the 1-loop level. This model equivalent to that presented by
Sugiyama [34]
, except that it includes the discrete effects.

First, it was confirmed in Section

VI
Discussion and Conclusions

This paper constructs a theoretical model of the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum in anticipation of future data analyses of the RSD effect using the galaxy distribution after reconstruction. To explain the observed galaxy power spectrum, it is necessary to consider various effects such as the non-linear gravitational effect, the RSD effect, and the bias effect. In particular, we point out the need to add shot noise terms due to discrete effects in the case of post-reconstruction analysis. Specifically, we present the shot noise terms that appear in the 1-loop corrections of the power spectrum in Standard Perturbation Theory (see Eq.
60
). Furthermore, by applying this calculation to the resummation model of infrared (IR) effects, we show a power spectrum model applicable to small scales due to the 1-loop corrections, while describing the non-linear damping effects before and after the reconstruction of the BAO signal (see Eq.
83
).

We have provided a discrete representation of the density fluctuations in the post-reconstruction galaxy distribution and calculated the associated power spectrum. Furthermore, we have demonstrated that the differences in the power spectrum before and after reconstruction can be described using higher-order statistics such as the bispectrum and trispectrum, in addition to the combination of the pre-reconstruction power spectra. These correction terms include shot-noise terms arising from discrete effects specific to the reconstruction. At the 1-loop level, contributions from the trispectrum can be ignored, yielding two types of terms in the post-reconstruction 1-loop power spectrum: one derived from the product of the pre-reconstruction power spectrum and another based on the bispectrum.

The shot noise term appears alongside the Gaussian filtering function,
W_{\rm G}(pR_{\rm s})=\exp\left(-p^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}/2\right)
, introduced for reconstruction. This suggests that when the smoothing scale
R_{\rm s}
is small, the shape of the post-reconstruction power spectrum is significantly affected by the shot-noise term specific to the reconstruction. This effect is particularly pronounced when
R_{\rm s}
is less than
5\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}
(see Figures
1
and
3
). Even when
R_{\rm s}=10\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}
or
15\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}
is selected, Figure
2
indicates that there is a
2-4\%
impact on the power spectrum at
k=0.2\,h\,{\rm Mpc^{-1}}
. In other words, it is crucial to properly account for the discrete effects in order to accurately model the post-reconstruction galaxy power spectrum.

Furthermore, we have derived the solution in the IR (high-
k
) limit of the post-reconstruction 1-loop power spectrum. The calculations in the IR limit are used to construct the IR-resummed model, and our calculations can reproduce the result shown by
White [44]
, i.e., the shot noise term that appears in the smoothing parameters that characterize the exponential damping function of the BAO signal.

The post-reconstruction power spectrum model presented in this paper includes almost all the effects that should be applied to actual observational data, such as gravitational non-linear effects, RSD effects, bias effects, exponential damping effects of BAO, and discrete effects unique to the reconstruction. Thus, it is immediately applicable to actual cosmological data analysis. Nonetheless, for more precise analyses, several improvements could be considered, summarized as follows:

1.

While our model incorporates both resummed IR effects and SPT 1-loop non-linear effects, considering higher order perturbation theory terms could extend its applicability to smaller scales.

2.

Given our findings, building emulators for the post-reconstruction power spectrum (e.g., see
Wang et al. [73]
) should be designed to account for changes in galaxy number densities due to the reconstruction-specific discrete effect. In other words, the galaxy number density input to the emulator should be set to exactly match the observed galaxy number density.

3.

The calculation of the displacement vector for reconstruction should include the window function effects of the observed region, necessitating a post-reconstruction power spectrum model that includes such window effects.

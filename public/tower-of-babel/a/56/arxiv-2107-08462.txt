Cohomology of configuration spaces of surfaces as mapping class group representations
Andreas Stavrou

Abstract
. We express the rational cohomology of the unordered configuration space of a compact oriented manifold as a representation of its mapping class group in terms of a weight-decomposition of the rational cohomology of the mapping space from the manifold to a sphere. We apply this to the case of a compact oriented surface with one boundary component and explicitly compute the rational cohomology of its unordered configuration space as a representation of its mapping class group. In particular, this representation is not symplectic, but has trivial action of the second Johnson filtration subgroup of the mapping class group.



1.
Introduction

The
ordered configuration space
of a manifold
M
is the space

F_{n}(M)=\{(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathring{M}^{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ if }i\neq j\}

of
n
distinct points in the
interior
of
M
, topologised as a subspace of
M^{n}
.
The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acts on
F_{n}(M)
by permuting the coordinates, and the
(unordered) configuration space
of
M
is the quotient

C_{n}(M)=F_{n}(M)/\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

Henceforth, we will assume that the manifold
M
is
compact, connected
and
oriented
of dimension
d
, possibly with boundary. Furthermore, all (co)homology will be with
\Q
coefficients unless explicitly stated.

The
diffeomorphism group

\Diffd(M)
is the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of
M
pointwise preserving a neighbourhood of the boundary
\partial M
, with the Whitney
C^{\infty}
-topology. The
mapping class group

\MCG(M)
of
M
is the group of connected components
\pi_{0}(\Diffd(M))
, i.e. diffeomorphisms up to isotopy.

The group
\Diffd(M)
acts naturally on
C_{n}(M)
giving rise to an action of
\MCG(M)
on the homology
H_{*}(C_{n}(M))
. We are interested in

Question 1.1
.

What is
H_{*}(C_{n}(M))
as a
\MCG(M)
-representation?

1.1.
General manifolds

We reduce Question
1.1
to computing the rational cohomology of the space
\mapd(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q})
of continuous maps of pairs

(1)

and

(2)

, i.e. the action of the group

on

arising from postcomposition of maps.

For
k\in\Z
, the
k
-weightspace,

, of a
\Q^{*}
-representation

is the subspace of
V
on which every
q\in\Q^{*}
acts via scalar multiplication by

.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

Let

and

. Under the sphere action,
H^{*}(\map_{\partial}(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q}))
splits as a direct sum of its weightspaces and there is a

-equivariant isomorphism

1.2.1.
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representations

We fix
g\geq 1
and recall some representation theory of
\Gamma_{g,1}
. The action of
\Gamma_{g,1}
on
H_{\Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};\Z)\simeq\Z^{2g}
preserves the intersection product
\lambda_{\Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lambda^{2}H_{\Z}\to\Z
and thus induces the short exact sequence of groups

(1.2)

1\to I_{g,1}\to\Gamma_{g,1}\to\sptg\to 1,

where the
symplectic group

\sptg
is the subgroup of the linear group
\GL_{2g}(\Z)
preserving
\lambda_{\Z}
, and the
Torelli group

I_{g,1}
is the kernel of this action. The rationalisation
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};\Q)\simeq\Q^{2g}
is the
standard symplectic representation
of
\Gamma_{g,1}
. The symplectic form
\lambda=\lambda_{\Z}\otimes\Q
is
\Gamma_{g,1}
-invariant, so provides a
\Gamma_{g,1}
-equivariant isomorphism
H\cong H^{\vee}
, under which
\lambda
dualises itself to an invariant tensor
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}H
. Any
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representation that factors through
\sptg
, or equivalently with trivial
I_{g,1}
-action, is called
symplectic
. The standard representation
H
is symplectic, and so are all the subquotients of its tensor powers.

We fix a
standard symplectic basis

\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{2g}\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})=\Z^{*2g}
, write
[-]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})\to H
the abelianisation and obtain the standard symplectic basis
e_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[\alpha_{i}]
of
H
. In this notation
\omega=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+...+e_{2g-1}\wedge e_{2g}\in\Lambda^{2}H
.

The
content
(Definition
4.1
) is the function

c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})\to\Lambda^{2}H

defined on each word

, in the alphabet
\{\alpha_{i}\}
, by

(1.3)

c(w)=\sum_{\mathclap{1\leq i<j\leq k}}\hskip 6.0pt[w_{i}]\wedge[w_{j}].

The content depends on the choice of basis. Some examples of how it evaluates are: 1.
c(\alpha_{i}^{\pm})=0
for
i=1,...,2g
; 2. on commutators ,
c([a,b])=c(aba^{-1}b^{-1})=2[a]\wedge[b]
for
a,b\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})
, and 3. on the boundary word
c([\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}]...[\alpha_{2g-1},\alpha_{2g}])=2(e_{1}\wedge e_{2}+...+e_{2g-1}\wedge e_{2g})=2\omega
(Proposition

).

We use the content

to define function

\xi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma_{g,1}\to\hom(H,\Lambda^{2}H)
(Definition
4.7
) by setting

\xi(\phi)(e_{i})=c(\phi(\alpha_{i}))

for each
\phi\in\Gamma_{g,1}
and

, and extending linearly on
H
. The function
\xi
is
not
a group homomorphism, but rather a crossed homomorphism that agrees on

with the Johnson homomorphism
\tau
from
[

]
(see proof of Proposition

).

2.
The group
\Q^{*}
, sphere actions, and weights

Let
\Q^{*}
denote the group of non-zero rationals with multiplication. For a simply connected space
X
, a map
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to X_{\Q}
is a
rationalisation
if it induces rationalisations on homotopy or, equivalently, homology groups. The rationalisation
X_{\Q}
is uniquely defined up to homotopy equivalence.

Definition 2.1
.

A based
\Q^{*}
-action on a rational sphere
(S^{n}_{\Q},*)
,
n\geq 1
, by homeomorphisms is called a
sphere action
if the induced action on
\pi_{n}(S^{n}_{\Q})=\Q
is by multiplication by
q
for every
q\in\Q^{*}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Sphere actions exist (see Section
4.6
for an example), but we will not specify one yet.
In fact, for any
d,n\geq 1
, since
S^{d}\wedge S^{n}_{\Q}\simeq_{\Q}S^{d+n}_{\Q}
, specifying a sphere action on
S^{n}_{\Q}
induces a sphere action on
S^{d}\wedge S^{n}_{\Q}
.

Definition 2.3

(Weightspaces)
.

For a
\Q^{*}
-representation
V
and
k\in\Z
, the
k
-weightspace

V^{(k)}
is the subspace of
V
on which each
q\in\Q^{*}
acts by multiplication by
q^{k}
. We say that
\Q^{*}
acts on
V

purely by weights
if
V
splits as the direct sum of its weightspaces.

Definition 2.4

(Weighted algebras)
.

A
weighting
on a (differential) multi-graded commutative algebra
A^{*,...,*}
is an extra grading denoted by
A^{*,...,*,(*)}
that is compatible with the product of
A
, but
does not
contribute a Koszul sign in the graded commutativity rule (nor in the Leibniz rule). If
A
has a weighting, we call
A

weighted
.

If
V
is a weighted multigraded vector space, then
S(V)
is the free graded commutativity algebra on
V
that ignores the weight in the graded commutativity.

Once defined, the weighting index will be usually omitted to alleviate notation.

Remark 2.5
.

A multi-graded algebra
A^{*,...,*}
on which
\Q^{*}
acts purely by weights inherits a
weighting

A^{*,...,*,(*)}
. Cohomology of a space defined functorially in
S^{n}_{\Q}
will usually be naturally weighted by the sphere action.

3.
Configuration spaces in terms of mapping spaces, equivariantly

The cohomology of mapping spaces
H^{*}(\mapd(M,X))
is covariantly functorial in the manifold
(M,\partial M)
with boundary preserving maps and contravariant in the based space
(X,x_{0})
with based maps.
Therefore, the algebra
H^{*}(\mapd(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q}))
inherits commuting actions of
\MCG(M)
by precomposition of maps and of
\Q^{*}
by postcomposition with the sphere action. The aim of this section is proving

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
m\geq 1
, the sphere action on
H^{*}(\map_{\partial}(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q}))
is purely by weights, and there is a
\MCG(M)
-equivariant isomorphism

(3.1)

H^{i}(C_{k}(M))\cong\widetilde{H}^{i+2mk}(\map_{\partial}(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q}))^{(k)}.

We interpolate the claimed isomorphism using the auxiliary space
\Gamma_{\partial}(M;S^{2m}_{\Q})
which we define now.
Let
\tau^{+}M
be the fibrewise one point compactification of the tangent bundle of the manifold
M
. For a based space
(X,x_{0})
, let
\tau^{+}X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\tau^{+}M\wedge_{f}X
be the fibrewise smash product of
(\tau^{+}_{x}M,0)
with
(X,x_{0})
. This has a canonical section
s_{0}
defined by the smash point. Denote by
\Gamma_{\partial}(M;X)
the space of continuous sections of
\tau^{+}_{X}
defined on
M
that agree with
s_{0}
on
\partial M
. The diffeomorphism group
\Diffd(M)
acts on
\Gamma_{\partial}(M;X)
via bundle maps on
\tau M
. For
X=S^{2m}_{\Q}
, there is also an induced sphere action on
\Gamma_{\partial}(M;S^{2m}_{\Q})
which commutes with the
\Diffd(M)
action, just like in the case of mapping spaces.

Our argument is divided in two steps, each giving one of the
\MCG(M)
-equivariant isomorphisms

(3.2)

H^{i}(C_{k}(M))\cong_{1}\widetilde{H}^{i+2mk}\big(\Gamma_{\partial}(M;S^{2m}_{\Q})\big)^{(k)}\cong_{2}\widetilde{H}^{i+2mk}(\map_{\partial}(M,S^{d+2m}_{\Q}))^{(k)}.

for
i,k,m\geq 1
. Theorem
3.2
proves isomorphism 1 and Theorem
3.4
proves isomorphism 2.

3.1.
From configuration spaces to section spaces

In this proof, we give an equivariant version of an argument from
[
BCT89
]
, that uses the Thom space of a bundle over
C_{n}(M)
, by applying an equivariant stable splitting result from
[
MT14
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For

4.1.
The content of free words

Denote by
\Z^{*n}
the free product of
n
copies of
\Z
, i.e. the free group of rank
n
with a choice of basis
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
, coming from each copy of
\Z
. The

l(w)
of a word

is measured with respect to this basis.
Write
[-]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Z^{*n}\to\Z^{n}
for the abelianisation map, giving
\Z^{n}
the standard basis
e_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[\alpha_{i}]
,
i=1,...,n
. Recall that the
second exterior power
of a
\Z
-module

is the
\Z
-module
\Lambda^{2}M=M\otimes_{\Z}M/\langle v\otimes v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v\in M\rangle
in which the image of
v\otimes u
is written as
v\wedge u
.
Write
\A=\{\alpha_{1}^{\pm},...,\alpha_{n}^{\pm}\}
for the set of length-one words of
\Z^{*n}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
content
is the function

c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Z^{*n}\to\Lambda^{2}\Z^{n}

defined by

(4.1)

c(w)=\sum_{\mathclap{1\leq i<j\leq k}}\hskip 6.0pt[w_{i}]\wedge[w_{j}]

for each word
w=w_{1}w_{2}...w_{k}\in\Z^{*n}
with
k\geq 0
and
w_{i}\in\A
for all
i
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The content is well-defined.

Proof.

It suffices to check that if two consecutive letters are inverse, say
w_{p}^{-1}=w_{p+1}
, then
c(w)
equals the content of the reduced word
w^{\prime}=w_{1}...w_{p-1}w_{p+2}...w_{k}
after cancelling
w_{p},w_{p+1}
. The difference
c(w)-c(w^{\prime})
is by definition

c(w)=\sum_{\mathclap{1\leq i<p}}\hskip 6.0pt([w_{i}]\wedge[w_{p}]+[w_{i}]\wedge[w_{p+1}])+[w_{p}]\wedge[w_{p+1}]+\sum_{\mathclap{p+1<j\leq k}}\hskip 6.0pt([w_{p}]\wedge[w_{j}]+[w_{p+1}]\wedge[w_{j}])

and
[w_{p+1}]=[w_{p}^{-1}]=-[w_{p}]
so all three summands vanish.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The content depends on the choice of basis of the free group
\Z^{*n}
. For example, if we wrote
c_{\alpha}
for the content defined above and
c_{\beta}
for the content defined using the basis
\beta_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
,
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}
for
i=2,...,n
, then we would have
c_{\alpha}(\beta_{1})=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}
whereas
c_{\beta}(\beta_{1})=0
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The content
c
satisfies:

(1)

c(a)=0
if
a=1
or
a\in\A
;

(2)

c(a^{-1})=-c(a)
for all
a\in\Z^{*n}
;

(3)

c(ab)=c(a)+c(b)+[a]\wedge[b]
for all
a,b\in\Z^{*n}
;

(4)

c([a,b])=2[a]\wedge[b]
where
[a,b]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
is the commutator;

(5)

c([a,[b,c]])=0
for all
a,b\in\Z^{*n}
i.e.
c
vanishes on double commutators.

Proof.

Properties (1),(2),(3) follow immediately from the definition by rearranging indices and using
[w^{-1}]=-[w]
for all
w\in\Z^{*n}
. Property (4) follows by writing
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
, using property (3) three times to obtain

\displaystyle c([a,b])=c(a)+c(b)+c(a^{-1})+c(b)

\displaystyle+[a]\wedge[b]+[a]\wedge[b^{-1}]+[a]\wedge[a^{-1}]+[b]\wedge[a^{-1}]+[b]\wedge[b^{-1}]+[a^{-1}]\wedge[b^{-1}]

and cancelling using property (2). Finally, (5) follows from by applying (4) to get
c([a,[b,c])=2[a]\wedge[[b,c]]=0
because commutators vanish in the abelianisation.
∎

5.3.
Bi-graded algebras and filtered graded algebras

Our aim here is to state and prove the
lifting lemma

5.17
.

Definition 5.12
.

A
filtered algebra

(A^{*},F^{*}_{*})
is a graded algebra
A^{*}
with a filtration

0=F^{k}_{k}\subset...\subset F^{k}_{i+1}\subset F^{k}_{i}\subset...\subset F^{k}_{0}=A^{k},

that is multiplicative in the sense that
F^{k}_{i}\cdot F^{k^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}}\subset F^{k+k^{\prime}}_{i+i^{\prime}}
. The
associated graded
algebra
\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}
is given by the filtration quotients

\gr^{F}(A)^{p,q}=F_{q}^{p+q}/F_{q+1}^{p+q}

and inherits a product from
A^{*}
.
A morphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(A^{*},F^{*}_{*})\to(B^{*},G^{*}_{*})
of graded algebras is
filtered
if
\phi(F^{k}_{i})\subset G^{k}_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq k
. In that case,
\phi
has
associated graded
morphism

\gr(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\gr^{F}(A)\to\gr^{G}(B)

which is also a morphism of bigraded algebras.

Definition 5.13
.

For a bigraded algebra
B^{*,*}
, the
tautological filtration

F_{*}^{*}(\Tot)
of
\Tot(B)^{*}
is

F_{i}\Tot(B)^{k}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k,q\leq i-k}B^{p,q}.

Remark 5.14
.

Clearly,
\gr^{F}(\Tot(B))^{*,*}\cong B^{*,*}
naturally, but in general
\Tot(\gr^{F}(A))^{*}

\not\cong A^{*}
.

For a subset
S\subset\Z_{\geq 0}\times\Z_{\geq 0}
define
B^{S}=\oplus_{(p,q)\in S}B^{p,q}
the bigraded vector subspace of
B^{*,*}
. Write this inclusion as
i_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B^{S}\hookrightarrow B^{*,*}
. Clearly if
S
is additively closed and contains
(0,0)
, then
B^{S}\subset B^{*,*}
and
B^{S}
is, in fact, a subalgebra.

Definition 5.15
.

For
k\geq 0
, we say that
(p,q)\in\Z_{\geq 0}\times\Z_{\geq 0}
is
a bottom entry in total degree
k
of
B^{*,*}
if
p+q=k
and, for
p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}=k
and
q^{\prime}<q
,
B^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}=0
.

Definition 5.16
.

For a filtered algebra
(A^{*},F^{*}_{*})
, a set of indices
S\subset\Z_{\geq 0}\times\Z_{\geq 0}
is
bottom and closed
if it satisfies

(1)

(0,0)\in S
,

(2)

each
(p,q)\in S
is a bottom entry in its total degree for
\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}
and

(3)

for each
(p,q),(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\in S
, then
(p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime})
is also a bottom entry of
\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}
and furthermore, if
\gr^{F}(A)^{p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime}}\neq 0
, then
(p+p^{\prime},q+q^{\prime})\in S
.

Lemma 5.17

(Lifting Lemma)
.

Let
(A^{*},F^{*}_{*})
be a filtered graded algebra with a bottom and closed set of indices
S\subset\Z_{\geq 0}\times\Z_{\geq 0}
.
Then,
\gr^{F}(A)^{S}\subset\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}
is a bigraded subalgebra, and this inclusion of algebras,
i_{S}
, has a canonical lift

(5.22)

\phi_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\big(\Tot(\gr^{F}(A)^{S}),F^{*}_{*}(\Tot)\big)\to(A^{*},F^{*}_{*}),

in the sense that
\phi_{S}
is a filtered monomorphism of algebras whose associated graded

(5.23)

\gr(\phi_{S})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\gr^{F(\Tot)}\big(\Tot(\gr^{F}(A)^{S})\big)=\gr^{F}(A)^{S}\to\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}

is the inclusion
i_{S}
.

Proof.

We first construct the linear map
\phi_{S}
and prove it is a lift. The idea is that if
(p,q)
is a bottom entry then
F^{p+q}_{i}=0
for all
i>q
and so
\gr^{F}(A)^{p,q}
is canonically isomorphic to
F^{p+q}_{q}
. Composing this isomorphism with the inclusion
F^{p+q}_{q}\subset A^{p+q}
, provides a canonical linear monomorphism
\phi_{p,q}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\gr^{F}(A)^{p,q}\to A^{p,q}
, and the monomorphisms
\phi_{p,q}
piece together to form the graded linear monomorphism
\phi_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Tot(\gr^{F}(A)^{S})\to A^{*}
. It is then an easy tautological check that
\phi_{S}
is filtered with associated graded,
\gr(\phi_{S})
, equal to
i_{S}
using that
S
has at most one non-trivial entry in each diagonal, as it contains only bottom entries.

That
\gr^{F}(A)^{S}\subset\gr^{F}(A)^{*,*}
is a subalgebra follows from Definition
5.16
since
S
being bottom and closed: from 1,
\gr^{F}(A)^{S}
contains the unit; from 3, it is multiplicatively closed. It remains to prove that
\phi_{S}
is multiplicative.

We use that
S
consists of bottom entries. For
i=1,2
, pick
(p_{i},q_{i})\in S
and
z_{i}\in F^{p_{i}+q_{i}}_{q_{i}}
and write

[z_{i}]\in\gr^{F}(A)^{p_{i},q_{i}}=F^{p_{i}+q_{i}}_{q_{i}}/F^{p_{i}+q_{i}}_{q_{i}+1}=F^{p_{i}+q_{i}}_{q_{i}}.

The last equality holds because
(p_{i},q_{i})
is bottom. Observe that
\phi_{S}([z_{i}])=\phi_{p_{i},q_{i}}([z_{i}])=z_{i}
. Denote by
\cdot_{A}
the product in
A
and
\cdot_{\gr}
the product in
\gr(A)
. Then by definition,

[z_{1}]\cdot_{\gr}[z_{2}]=[z_{1}\cdot_{A}z_{2}]\in\gr(A)^{p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+q_{2}}.

By part 3 of Definition
5.16
,
(p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})
is also a bottom entry, so we have two cases:

(1)

\gr(A)^{p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+q_{2}}\neq 0
and so
(p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+q_{2})\in S
, therefore

\phi_{S}([z_{1}]\cdot_{\gr}[z_{2}])=\phi_{S}([z_{1}\cdot_{A}z_{2}])=z_{1}\cdot_{A}z_{2}=\phi_{S}(z_{1})\cdot\phi_{S}(z_{2}),

or

(2)

\gr(A)^{p_{1}+p_{2},q_{1}+q_{2}}=0
, so
F^{p_{1}+p_{2}+q_{1}+q_{2}}_{q_{1}+q_{2}}=0
and
z_{1}\cdot_{A}z_{2}=0
, and therefore
\phi_{S}([z_{1}]\cdot_{\gr}[z_{2}])=0=\phi_{S}(z_{1})\cdot\phi_{S}(z_{2})
.

In either case,
\phi_{S}
is multiplicative.
∎

6.
Cohomology of Configuration spaces of surfaces as
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representation

Theorem 6.1
.

For
g,i,n\geq 0
, the
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representation
H^{i}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1}))

is isomorphic to the bidegree
(i,(n))
part of the weighted graded commutative
\Gamma_{g,1}
-algebra

\Q[y_{1},...,y_{2g},w]\otimes H^{*,*}(\Lambda[x_{1},...,x_{2g},v],d(v)=2\omega)

with

•

bidegrees
|x_{i}|=(1,(1))
,
|y_{i}|=(2,(2))
,

|w|=(0,(1))
and
|v|=(1,(2))
,

•

differential
d
given on
v
by

d(v)=2\omega=2(x_{1}x_{2}+...+x_{2g-1}x_{2g})

and vanishing on the
x_{i}
, and extended using the Leibniz rule, and

•

the
\Gamma_{g,1}
-action trivial on
w
and
v
, symplectic on the
x_{i}
and the Johnson on the
y_{i}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
, we have isomorphism

(6.1)

H^{i}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1}))\cong H^{i+2m^{\prime}n}(\map_{\partial}(\Sigma_{g,1},S^{2(m^{\prime}+1)}_{\Q}))^{(n)}

for any
m^{\prime}\geq 1
.
The right hand side is, by Theorem
5.5
and Definition
5.4
, isomorphic to

H^{i+2m^{\prime}n,(n)}\big(R_{m^{\prime}+1},d_{R}\big),

which, by Remark
5.6
, is isomorphic to
H^{i,(n)}\big(R_{1},d_{R}\big)
. All these isomorphisms are
\Gamma_{g,1}
-equivariant.
Finally, by treating the weighting as a usual grading, the bigraded algebra
H^{*,*}\big(R_{1},d_{R}\big)
is by definition the weighted graded algebra

\Q[y_{1},...,y_{2g},w]\otimes H^{*,*}(\Lambda[x_{1},...,x_{2g},v],d(v)=2\omega)

with the desired
\Gamma_{g,1}
-action.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We can drop the generator
w
, write
A^{i,(\leq n)}=\oplus_{j\leq n}A^{i,(j)}
and rephrase the theorem as the isomorphism of
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representations

(6.2)

H^{\bullet}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1}))\cong\Big(\Q[y_{1},...,y_{2g}]\otimes H^{*,*}(\Lambda[x_{1},...,x_{2g},v],d(v)=2\omega)\Big)^{\bullet,(\leq n)}

Conjecture 6.3
.

This is an isomorphism of graded
\Gamma_{g,1}
-algebras with the truncated algebra structure on the right.

6.1.
The action of the Johnson filtration

We determine how the Johnson filtration
J(i)\subset\Gamma_{g,1}
(see (
1.7
)) acts on
H^{*}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1}))
.

Proposition 6.4
.

The action of
J(2)
on the representation
J
is trivial.

Proof.

Since
J(2)\subset J(1)=I_{g,1}
,
J(2)
acts on each of
H,\Lambda^{2}H
trivially, therefore by the definition of
J
, it suffices to check that for all
\phi\in J(2)
,
\xi(\phi)(y)
vanishes for
y\in H
. It suffices to check on the basis
y_{i}
,
i=1,...,2g
.

From the definition of
J(2)
, a mapping class
\phi\in J(2)
maps every
g\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})
to
\phi(g)=gh
for some
h\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})^{(2)}
.

Then by definition
4.7
,
\xi(\phi,y_{i})=c(\phi(a_{i}))=c(a_{i}h)
some
h\in(\Z^{*2g})^{(2)}
, i.e. a double commutator. From proposition
4.4
, we get
c(a_{i}h)=c(a_{i})+c(h)+[a_{i}]\wedge[h]
,
c(a_{i})=0
,
c(h)=0
and
[h]=0
because
h
is a commutator.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

The action of
J(2)
on
H^{*}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1}))
is trivial for all
n\geq 0
and
g\geq 0
.

Proof.

The vector space
H^{i}(C_{n}(\Sigma_{g,1});\Q)
is a
\Gamma_{g,1}
-subrepresentation of
H^{*}(R_{1},d_{R})
which is a subquotient of the free tensor power on the direct sum of
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representation
J\oplus H\oplus\Q\oplus\Q
with appropriate gradings (see Remark
5.7
) all of which have trivial action of
J(2)
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

This is in constrast with

acting non-trivially on

for

[

]
.

On the other hand, the
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representation

]
. We compute this representation explicitly and conclude this result independently.

Definition 6.7
.

The

is the quotient
\widetilde{J}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=J/\langle\omega\rangle
, fitting in an extension of
\Gamma_{g,1}
-representations

(6.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Proposition 6.8
.

If
g\geq 2
, the reduced Johnson representation
\widetilde{J}
is not symplectic and the extension (
6.3
) is not
\Gamma_{g,1}
-split. In particular,
J
is also not symplectic.

Proof.

If
J
were symplectic, then so would be its quotient
\widetilde{J}
.
If the sequence were
\Gamma_{g,1}
-split, then
\widetilde{J}
would be a direct sum of symplectic representations, thus symplectic. So it suffices then to prove that
\widetilde{J}
is not symplectic. It is enough to show that there are
\phi\in I_{g,1}
and
y\in H
so that
\xi(\phi,y)\not\in\Q\langle\omega\rangle
. We will prove that the image of
\xi|_{I_{g,1}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I_{g,1}\to\hom(H,\Lambda^{2}H)
is not contained in
\hom(H,\Q\langle\omega\rangle)
.

We consider the crossed homomorphism
\xi
with homomorphism
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}I_{g,1}\to\hom(H_{\Z},\Lambda^{2}H_{\Z})
from
[
Joh80
]
. Let
a_{1},...,a_{2g}\in\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})
and
x_{1},...,x_{2g}\in H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1};\Z)
be the standard bases and define homomorphism

the map
j([a,b])=[a]\wedge[b]
. Then
\tau
is defined for
\phi\in I_{g,1}
, by

(6.4)

\tau(\phi)(x_{i})=j(\phi(a_{i})a_{i}^{-1}).

Now by definition of
I_{g,1}
, for each
i=1,...,n
there is a conjugator
c=[a,b]\in[\pi,\pi]
a conjugator so that
\phi(a_{i})=a_{i}c
. Then

(6.5)

\tau(\phi)(x_{i})=j(a_{i}ca_{i}^{-1})=j([a_{i},c]c)=j([a_{i},c])+j(c)=[a]\wedge[b]

since
[c]=0
, being a commutator.
On the other hand,

(6.6)

\xi(\phi)(x_{i})=c(\phi(a_{i}))=c(ca_{i})=c(c)+c(a_{i})+[c]\wedge[a_{i}]=c(c)=2[a]\wedge[b],

by Proposition
4.4
. Extending linearly we conclude
\xi|_{I_{g,1}}=2\tau
.

Finally, from
[

]
, the image of

is

. Thus
\im(\xi|_{I_{g,1}})=\Lambda^{3}H\subset H\otimes\Lambda^{2}H
, which is not contained in

, provided that

, so
\dim(\Lambda^{3}H)={2g\choose 3}>1
.
∎

6.2.
A comment on closed surfaces

Let
\Sigma_{g}
be a closed oriented genus
g
surface and
\Gamma_{g}
its mapping class group.
As briefly mentioned in the introduction, in
[
Loo20
]
it is shown that the
\Gamma_{g}
representation
H^{3}(C_{3}(\Sigma_{g}))
is non-symplectic for
g\geq 3
. We briefly explain how the same representation arises from results of our paper.

The inclusion
\Diffd^{+}(\Sigma_{g,1})\subset\Diff^{+}(\Sigma_{g})
gives rise to surjection
\Gamma_{g,1}\to\Gamma_{g}
, and so we can speak of
\Gamma_{g,1}
representations instead. By applying Theorem
3.1
,
H^{3}(C_{3}(\Sigma_{g}))
is isomorphic to the
\Gamma_{g,1}
representation

(6.7)

\widetilde{H}^{3\mu}(\map(\Sigma_{g},S^{2m}_{\Q}))^{(3)}.

We can compute this by analysing the Serre spectral sequence of the evaluation fibration

(6.8)

\mapd(\Sigma_{g,1},S^{2m}_{\Q})\cong\map_{*}(\Sigma_{g},S^{2m}_{\Q})\to\map(\Sigma_{g},S^{2m}_{\Q})\to S^{2m}_{\Q},

and comparing it with the Serre spectral sequence from Section
4.7
by deformation retracting
\vee_{2g}S^{1}
to
\Sigma_{g,1}
. One of the relevant entries of the Serre spectral sequence of (
6.8
) is then

\displaystyle E^{0,3\mu,(3)}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\Q\{x_{i}\wedge x_{j}\wedge x_{k},x_{i}y_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i,j,k=1,...,2g\}/\langle\omega\wedge x_{i},x_{i}y_{j}=x_{j}y_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\Lambda^{3}H/(\omega\wedge H)\oplus\Sym^{2}H

The direct sum here is not
\Gamma_{g,1}
-equivariant, but rather, the action is again the symplectic on the
x_{i}
and the “Johnson on the
y_{i}
”. We defer a more detailed elaboration of this to future work.

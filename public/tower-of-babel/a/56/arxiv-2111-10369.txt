Abstract
Prior work [ 1 ] shows that the Standard Model (SM) naturally arises near a gapless quantum critical region between Georgi-Glashow (GG) s ​ u ​ ( 5 ) su(5) and Pati-Salam (PS) s ​ u ​ ( 4 ) × s ​ u ​ ( 2 ) × s ​ u ​ ( 2 ) su(4)\times su(2)\times su(2) models of quantum vacua (in a phase diagram or moduli space), by implementing a modified s ​ o ​ ( 10 ) so(10) Grand Unification (GUT) with a Spin(10) gauge group plus a new discrete Wess-Zumino Witten term matching a 4d nonperturbative global mixed gauge-gravity w 2 ​ w 3 w_{2}w_{3} anomaly. In this work, we include Barr’s flipped u ⁡ ( 5 ) u(5) model into the quantum landscape, showing these four GUT-like models arise near the quantum criticality near SM. The SM and GG models can have either 15 or 16 Weyl fermions per generation, while the PS, flipped u ⁡ ( 5 ) u(5) , and the modified s ​ o ​ ( 10 ) so(10) have 16n Weyl fermions. Highlights include: First, we find the precise GG or flipped u ⁡ ( 5 ) u(5) gauge group requires to redefine a Lie group U(5) ≡ q ^ ( SU ( 5 ) × q ^ U ( 1 ) ) / ℤ q ^ {}_{\hat{q}}\equiv({\rm SU}(5)\times_{\hat{q}}{\rm U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_{\hat{q}} with q ^ = 2 \hat{q}=2 or 3 (instead of non-isomorphic analog q ^ = 1 \hat{q}=1 or 4), and different q ^ \hat{q} are related by multiple covering. Second, for 16n Weyl fermions, we show that the GG and flipped u ⁡ ( 5 ) u(5) are two different symmetry-breaking vacua of the same order parameter separated by a first-order Landau-Ginzburg transition. We also show that analogous 3+1d deconfined quantum criticalities, both between GG and PS, and between the flipped u ⁡ ( 5 ) u(5) and PS, are beyond Landau-Ginzburg paradigm. Third, topological quantum criticality occurs by tuning between the 15n vs 16n scenarios. Fourth, we explore the generalized higher global symmetries in the SM and GUTs. .

(2)
Beyond Landau-Ginzburg
type: Cannot be characterized via merely symmetry-breaking order parameters. e.g., phase transitions involving topological terms (e.g., WZW term),
SPTs, intrinsic topological orders, or the ’t Hooft anomaly matching differently on two sides of phases, etc.

(3)
Wilson-Fisher

\langle\Phi\rangle\neq 0

especially via
r\Phi^{2}+\lambda\Phi^{4}
type potential. However, Wilson-Fisher fixed point requires the beyond-mean-field

(4)
Gross-Neveu type

\chi

\Phi

condensation
\langle\Phi\rangle\neq 0

(5)
Order of phase transition
:

For a minimal positive
N
, if the
N
th derivative of the free energy (of QFT)
with respect to the driving parameter is discontinuous at the transition,
it is called the
N
th-order phase transition.

\bullet
First-order transition is also called a discontinuous transition,
while the correlation length remains finite and no additional gapless excitations appear at the transition.

\bullet
Second-order and higher-order is called a continuous transition,
while the correlation length diverges to infinite
18
18

18

Here the correlation function for Landau-Ginzburg paradigm
is typically the two-point correlator
\langle{\cal O}(x_{1}){\cal O}(x_{2})\rangle
of local order parameters of individual spacetime points.
In contrast, the correlation function for topological order beyond the Landau-Ginzburg paradigm
is the correlator of strings or higher-dimensional extended operators.

and additional gapless excitations (thus called critical, sometimes described by conformal field theory)
appear at the transition.

This above definition is applicable to Landau-Ginzburg as well as beyond Landau-Ginzburg paradigm.

If the transition happens to be within Landau-Ginzburg paradigm, then:

\bullet
First-order means the order parameter has a discontinuous jump at the transition.

\bullet
Second-order and higher-order means the order parameter changes continuous without a jump at the transition.

Einstein Lorentzian solvable unimodular Lie groups
Oumaima Tibssirte

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to show that many key results found in the study of Einstein Lorentzian nilpotent Lie algebras can still hold in the more general settings of unimodular Lie algebras and (completely) solvable Lie algebras.

Introduction

Left-invariant pseudo-Riemannian Einstein metrics on nilpotent Lie groups is a research area that has known a significant progress, especially in the last decade. While the celebrated theorem of Milnor (see
[
16
]
) has among its consequences that in the Riemannian setting no such metrics could exist, except when the underlying Lie group is abelian, the indefinite case is an entirely different story with a handful of examples available in the literature (see
[
19
]
) and a systematic study of the Lorentzian case (see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
). Although, for the time being, there is still no decisive theorem that settles the debate concerning the exact conditions for such metrics to exist, the abundance of results on this particular subject is more apparent now than ever before.

In contrast, there is little to no information when it comes to the investigation of left-invariant Einstein pseudo-Riemannian, indefinite metrics, on general Lie groups, and a line of study that could potentially lead to a similar development as in the nilpotent case is yet to be found, furethermore it is not known whether the results obtained for nilpotent Lie groups could hold under weaker conditions or admit a useful generalization, even solvable Lie groups which represent in some sense a natural extension of the nilpotent scene, remain a question mark.

The purpose of this paper is therefore to initiate such an inspection by expanding the context of the main theorems provided by the study of Einstein left-invariant Lorentzian metrics on nilpotent Lie groups to more general situations with an emphasis on the solvable case, and at the same time to shed light on the limitations of these results. The main reference for this paper is
[
6
]
, and an analogy will be drawn between the different situations throughout the paper.

2
Preliminaries

A
pseudo-Euclidean vector space
is a real vector space of finite dimension
n

endowed with a
nondegenerate symmetric inner product of signature
(q,n-q)=(-\ldots-,+\ldots+)
. When
the
signature is
(0,n)

(resp.
(1,n-1)
) the space is called
Euclidean
(resp.
Lorentzian
).

Let
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean vector space of signature
(q,n-q)
. A vector
u\in V
is called
spacelike
if
\langle u,u\rangle>0
,
timelike
if
\langle u,u\rangle<0
and
isotropic
if
\langle u,u\rangle=0
.
A family

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{s})
of vectors in
V
is called
orthogonal
if, for
i,j=1,\ldots,s

and
i\not=j
,
\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle=0
. An orthonormal basis of
V
is an orthogonal basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
such that
\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle=\pm 1
.
A
pseudo-Euclidean basis
of
V
is a basis
(e_{1},\bar{e}_{2},\ldots,e_{q},\bar{e}_{q},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-2q})
for which the non vanishing products are

\langle\bar{e}_{i},e_{i}\rangle=\langle f_{j},f_{j}\rangle=1
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n-2q\}
.
When
V
is Lorentzian, we call such a basis

Lorentzian
. Pseudo-Euclidean basis always exist.

For any endomorphism
F:V\longrightarrow V
, we denote by
F^{*}:V\longrightarrow V
its adjoint with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. We shall make use of the following Lemmas whose proofs can be found in
[
6
]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Lorentzian vector space,
e
an isotropic vector and
A
a skew-symmetric endomorphism. Then
\langle Ae,Ae\rangle\geq 0
. Moreover,
\langle Ae,Ae\rangle=0
if and only if
Ae=\alpha e
with
\alpha\in\hbox{\bb R}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(V,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Lorentzian vector space,
e
an isotropic vector and
A
a skew-symmetric endomorphism such that
A(e)=0
. Then:

1.

{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{2})\leq 0
,

2.

{\mathrm{tr}}(A^{2})=0
if and only if for any
x\in e^{\perp}
,
A(x)=\lambda(x)e
and in this case
{\mathrm{tr}}(A\circ B)=0
for any skew-symmetric endomorphism satisfying
B(e)=0
.

A Lie group
G
together with a left invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
is called a

pseudo-Riemannian Lie group
. The metric
g

defines a symmetric nondegenerate inner
product
\langle\;,\;\rangle
on the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=T_{e}G
of
G
, and conversely, any nondegenerate symmetric
inner product on
{\mathfrak{g}}

gives rise
to an unique left invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric on
G
.

We will refer to a Lie
algebra endowed with a nondegenerate symmetric inner
product as a
pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra
.

Levi-Civita connection of
(G,g)
defines a product
\mathrm{L}:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
called
Levi-Civita product
given by Koszul’s
formula

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathrm{L}_{u}v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle.

(1)

For any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\mathrm{L}_{u}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is skew-symmetric and
[u,v]=\mathrm{L}_{u}v-\mathrm{L}_{v}u
.
The curvature on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by

K(u,v)=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}].

The Ricci curvature
\mathrm{ric}:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\hbox{\bb R}
and its Ricci operator
{\mathrm{Ric}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
are defined by
\langle{\mathrm{Ric}}(u),v\rangle=\mathrm{ric}(u,v)=\mathrm{tr}\left(w\longrightarrow K(u,w)v\right)
. A pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra is called
flat
(resp.
Ricci-flat
) if
K=0

(resp.
{\mathrm{ric}}=0
). It is called Einstein if there exists a constant
\lambda\in\hbox{\bb R}
such that
{\mathrm{Ric}}=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.
For any
u\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, put
\mathrm{R}_{u}=\mathrm{L}_{u}-{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
.
It is well-known that
\mathrm{ric}
is given by

{\mathrm{ric}}(u,v)=-{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{R}_{u}\circ\mathrm{R}_{v})-\frac{1}{2}\left({\langle}{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}u,v{\rangle}+{\langle}{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}v,u{\rangle}\right),

(2)

where
H
is the vector given by
\langle H,u\rangle=\mathrm{tr({\mathrm{ad}}_{u})}
.
Let us transform this formula to get some
useful formulas. To do so, we consider
{\mathrm{ad}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
J:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{so}({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
the adjoint representation and the endomorphism given by
J_{u}(v)={\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{*}u
. It is clear that
J_{u}
is skew-symmetric,
\ker{\mathrm{ad}}=Z({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\ker J=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]^{\perp}
.
One can deduce easily from (
1
) that

\mathrm{R}_{u}=-\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}\right)-\frac{1}{2}J_{u}.

Thus

{\mathrm{ric}}(u,v)=-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{*})-\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(J_{u}\circ J_{v})-\frac{1}{2}{\langle}{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}u,v{\rangle}-\frac{1}{2}{\langle}{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}v,u{\rangle}.

(3)

Define now the auto-adjoint endomorphisms
\widehat{B}
,
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}

and

{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{B}u,v\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}),\;\langle{\mathcal{J}}_{1}u,v\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{*}),\;\langle{\mathcal{J}}_{2}u,v\rangle=-{\mathrm{tr}}(J_{u}\circ J_{v})={\mathrm{tr}}(J_{u}\circ J_{v}^{*}).

Thus (
3
) is equivalent to

\mathrm{Ric}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\widehat{B}+{\mathcal{J}}_{1}\right)+\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{J}}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}^{*}).

(4)

When
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a unimodular Lie algebra then
H=0
and hence
(
4
) becomes

\mathrm{Ric}=-\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{B}+{\mathcal{J}}_{1})+\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{J}}_{2},

(5)

Since we will deal with nilpotent Lie algebras, only
{\mathcal{J}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
will be relevant so we are going to express them in an useful way. This is based on the notion of structure endomorphisms we introduce now.

Let

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
be a basis of

{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then, for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the Lie bracket can be written

[u,v]=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\langle S_{i}u,v\rangle e_{i},

(6)

where
S_{i}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
are skew-symmetric
endomorphisms with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.
The family
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{p})
will be called

structure endomorphisms
associated to
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
. One can see easily from the definition of
J
and (
6
) that for
any

u\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

J_{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\langle u,e_{i}\rangle S_{i}

(7)

The structure endomorphisms
(K_{1},\ldots,K_{p})
associated to a new basis

(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})
are given by

K_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}p^{ji}S_{i},\quad j=1,\ldots,p,

where
(p^{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq p}
is the passage matrix from
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})
to
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
.
The following proposition will be useful later, its proof is a matter of simple computation and can be found in
[
6
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra,

(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
a basis of

{\mathfrak{g}}
and
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{p})
the corresponding structure endomorphisms. Then

{\mathcal{J}}_{1}=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle S_{i}\circ S_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{J}}_{2}u=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\langle e_{i},u\rangle{{\mathrm{tr}}}(S_{i}\circ S_{j})e_{j}.

(8)

In particular,
{\mathrm{tr}}{\mathcal{J}}_{1}={\mathrm{tr}}{\mathcal{J}}_{2}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a real Lie algebra and denote
\mathcal{D}^{0}({\mathfrak{g}}):={\mathfrak{g}}
and for all
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{D}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}):=[\mathcal{D}^{k-1}({\mathfrak{g}}),\mathcal{D}^{k-1}({\mathfrak{g}})]
.Recall that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is said to be solvable if it satisfies
\mathcal{D}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})=0
for some
k\geq 1
. We say that the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is completely solvable if it has a chain of ideals

0=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset\dots\subset I_{n}={\mathfrak{g}},

such that
\dim(I_{i})=i
for all
0\leq i\leq n
. In this case,
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
in which every endomorphism
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}
with
u\in{\mathfrak{g}}
is represented by an upper triangular matrix
M_{u}
, in particular the Killing form
B
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies :

B(u,u)={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{2})={\mathrm{tr}}(M_{u}^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(M_{u})_{ii}^{2}\geq 0,

i.e the Killing form of a completely solvable real Lie algebra is positive semi-definite.Finally note that by Engel’s Theorem any nilpotent Lie algebra is completely solvable.

Results on solvable and unimodular Lorentzian Einstein Lie algebras

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebra and let
Q
denote the symmetric endomorphism
Q=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{J}_{2}
. Then for any orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and any endomorphism
E
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

{\mathrm{tr}}(QE)=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\langle E([e_{i},e_{j}])-[E(e_{i}),e_{j}]-[e_{i},E(e_{j})],[e_{i},e_{j}]\rangle,

(9)

where
\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle=\epsilon_{i}
.

Proof.

We denote by
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{p})
the structures endomorphisms associated to
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{p})
. By combining (
8
) and (
7
) we get

QE(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}J_{e_{i}}^{2}E(u)-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\langle e_{i},E(u)\rangle{{\mathrm{tr}}}(J_{e_{i}}\circ J_{e_{j}})e_{j}.

Let compute:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}(QE)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\langle QE(e_{j}),e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\langle J_{e_{i}}E(e_{j}),J_{e_{i}}(e_{j})\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\langle e_{i},E(e_{j})\rangle{{\mathrm{tr}}}(J_{e_{i}}\circ J_{e_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}\langle e_{i},[E(e_{j}),J_{e_{i}}(e_{j})]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{j}\langle e_{i},E(e_{j})\rangle\langle J_{e_{j}}e_{l},J_{e_{i}}e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{l}\langle J_{e_{i}}(e_{j}),e_{l}\rangle\langle e_{i},[E(e_{j}),e_{l}]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{j}\langle J_{e_{j}}e_{l},J_{E(e_{j})}e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{l}\langle{e_{i}},[e_{j},e_{l}]\rangle\langle e_{i},[E(e_{j}),e_{l}]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}\langle J_{e_{j}}e_{l},e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i},J_{E(e_{j})}e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}\langle[e_{j},e_{l}],[E(e_{j}),e_{l}]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{i}\langle{e_{j}},[e_{l},e_{i}]\rangle\langle[e_{l},e_{i}],{E(e_{j})}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}\langle[e_{j},e_{l}],[E(e_{j}),e_{l}]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{i}\langle[e_{l},e_{i}],{E([e_{l},e_{i}])}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}\langle[e_{j},e_{l}],[E(e_{j}),e_{l}]\rangle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{j}\epsilon_{l}\langle[e_{j},e_{l}],[e_{j},E(e_{l})]\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,l=1}^{p}\epsilon_{l}\epsilon_{i}\langle[e_{l},e_{i}],{E([e_{l},e_{i}])}\rangle,

and the formula follows.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean unimodular Lie algebra having a derivation with non null trace. Then
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is Einstein if and only if it is Ricci flat.

Proof.

Denote
Q:=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{1}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{J}_{2}
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is unimodular, equation (
5
) gives that :

\mathrm{Ric}=Q-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{B}.

Next, recall that the Killing form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies
B(\tau(u),\tau(v))=B(u,v)
for any
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}({\mathfrak{g}})
and any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, therefore
B(Du,v)=-B(u,Dv)
for any
D\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{g}})
, in particular
B(Du,u)=0
. Fix an orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
of
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
and set
\epsilon_{i}:=\langle e_{i},e_{i}\rangle
, then for any
D\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathfrak{g}})
:

{\mathrm{tr}}(\widehat{B}\circ D)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}\langle\widehat{B}(D(e_{i})),e_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}B(D(e_{i}),e_{i})=0,

on the other hand Lemma
3.1
gives that
{\mathrm{tr}}(Q\circ D)=0
, this shows that
{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{Ric}\circ D)=0
and so if
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
\lambda
-Einstein we get that
\lambda{\mathrm{tr}}(D)=0
, which proves the claim.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an Einstein Lorentzian solvable unimodular Lie algebra. If
Z({\mathfrak{g}})
is nondegenerate then it is either Euclidean or
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is flat, and
{\mathfrak{g}}
can be written as
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{b}}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
where
{\mathfrak{b}}
is an abelian ideal and
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an abelian subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, moreover the Levi-Civita product of
{\mathfrak{g}}
has the form

\mathrm{L}_{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c r}{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}&&u\in{\mathfrak{a}}\\
0,&&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Suppose that
Z({\mathfrak{g}})
is nondegenerate Lorentzian and choose a vector
e\in Z({\mathfrak{g}})
such that
\langle e,e\rangle=-1
. We have
{\mathfrak{g}}=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}=e^{\perp}
.
For any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, put
[u,v]=\langle Ku,v\rangle e+[u,v]_{0}
, where
K:{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and
[u,v]_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
.
It is obvious that
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a Euclidean solvable Lie algebra, moreover
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is unimodular. We claim that if
({\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is Einstein i.e
\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
then
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(K^{2})
and

{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}}=\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(K^{2})\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}K^{2}.

This implies that the Ricci curvature of
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is negative. But since
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is a unimodular solvable Euclidean Lie algebra,
[
18
, Corollary
3.3
]
gives that its Ricci curvature cannot be negative definite, therefore
\lambda=0
and so
K=0
which implies that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
is an ideal since
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]=[{\mathfrak{g}}_{0},{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}]
. Moreover
[
18
, Theorem

3.1
]
shows that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}={\mathfrak{b}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}
where
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an abelian subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{0}
is an abelian ideal of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, and if
\mathrm{L}^{0}
denotes the Levi-Civita product of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
then for any
u\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}

\mathrm{L}^{0}_{u}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c r}{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}&&u\in{\mathfrak{a}}\\
0,&&u\in{\mathfrak{b}}_{0}\end{array}\right.

Thus if we set
{\mathfrak{b}}:=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus[{\mathfrak{g}}_{0},{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}]
we get the desired result. Let prove our claim. Indeed, we choose an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{d})
of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and we denote by
(\widetilde{K},\widetilde{S}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{S}_{d})
the structure endomorphisms associated to the basis
(e,e_{1},\dots,e_{d})
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Note that all these endomorphisms vanishes at
e
and hence leave
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
invariant. We have
{\widetilde{K}}_{|{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}=K
and if
S_{i}={(\widetilde{S}_{i})}_{|{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}
then
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})
are the structure endomorphisms of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
corresponding to
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{d})
. If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is Einstein, according to (
8
),

-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{B}-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{K}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\widetilde{S}_{i}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{J}_{2}=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}.

[formula]

If we evaluate this expression at
e
using the expression of
\mathcal{J}_{2}
given in (
8
), we get

\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(\widetilde{K}^{2})e-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathrm{tr}}(\widetilde{K}\circ\widetilde{S}_{i})e_{i}=\lambda e.

This is equivalent to
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(\widetilde{K}^{2})=\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(K^{2})
and
{\mathrm{tr}}(\widetilde{K}\circ\widetilde{S}_{i})=0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we also observe that the Killing form of
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},[\;,\;]_{0})
is equal to the restriction to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
of the Killing form of
({\mathfrak{g}},[\;,\;])
. By taking the restriction of
(*)
to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
we finish the proof of our claim. ∎

Remark 1
.

The previous result can be generalized to arbitrary Einstein pseudo-Euclidean, solvable and unimodular Lie algebra, the proof is essentially the same.

4
The Double Extension Theorem For Solvable Unimodular Lorentzian Einstein Lie algebras

Let
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},[\;,\;]_{0},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0})
be a Euclidean Lie algebra and set
{\mathfrak{g}}:=\hbox{\bb R}e\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\hbox{\bb R}\bar{e}
. Let
\langle\;,\;\rangle
be the Lorentzian metric on
{\mathfrak{g}}
given by
\langle e,e\rangle=\langle\bar{e},\bar{e}\rangle=0
,
\langle e,\bar{e}\rangle=1
and
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{|{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\times{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}}:=\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}
then define on

{\mathrm{tr}}(\overline{K}\circ K)={\mathrm{tr}}(\overline{K}\circ S_{i})=0
, in particular

\lambda=\langle\lambda e,\overline{e}\rangle=\dfrac{1}{4}\langle\mathcal{J}_{2}(e),\overline{e}\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}(\overline{K}\circ K)=0,

i.e
{\mathfrak{g}}
is Ricci-flat. Next we have that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is completely solvable which means that its Killing form is positive semi-definite, hence using the formula

-\mathcal{J}_{1}=K\circ\overline{K}+\overline{K}\circ K+\sum_{i=1}^{d}S_{i}^{2},

and the fact that
\hat{{\cal B}}(e)=0
we obtain that

{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal{J}_{1})=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathrm{tr}}(S_{i}^{2})=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\langle S_{i}(f_{j}),f_{k}\rangle^{2}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat{\mathcal{B}})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle\hat{{\cal B}}(f_{i}),f_{i}\rangle\geq 0,

thus by taking the trace of the Ricci operator and using that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is Ricci-flat we get that :

0=-\dfrac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathcal{J}_{1})-\dfrac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}(\hat{\mathcal{B}}),

which shows in particular that
\langle S_{i}(f_{j}),f_{k}\rangle=0
for all
i,j,k=1,\dots,d
and
S_{i}((\hbox{\bb R}e)^{\perp})\subset\hbox{\bb R}e
, i.e

[f_{i},f_{j}]=\langle K(f_{i}),f_{j}\rangle e,\quad i,j=1,\dots,d.

(22)

Now set
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}:={\mathrm{span}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})
, since
e\in\mathrm{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
we get that the bilinear map
[\;,\;]_{0}:{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\times{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
given by

[u,v]:=[u,v]_{0}+\langle K(u),v\rangle\quad u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0},

defines a Lie bracket on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
and from (
22
) we deduce that
({\mathfrak{g}}_{0},[\;,\;]_{0})
is an abelian Lie algebra. Finally using that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is unimodular we obtain that for any
u\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
:

0={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u})=\langle[u,\overline{e}],e\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\underset{=0}{\underbrace{\langle[u,f_{i}],f_{i}\rangle}}=\langle\overline{K}(u),\overline{e}\rangle=-\langle\overline{K}(\overline{e}),u\rangle.

Therefore
\overline{K}(\overline{e})\in\hbox{\bb R}e
and the only non-vanishing Lie brackets of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
are of the form :

[\overline{e},u]=D(u)+\langle b,u\rangle e,\quad[u,v]=\langle K(u),v\rangle e,\quad\text{for}\;u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}},

where
D(u)=\sum_{i}\langle S_{i}(\overline{e}),u\rangle f_{i}
and
b=K(\overline{e})
. In other words
{\mathfrak{g}}
is obtained by a double extension process from an abelian Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
with parameters
(K,D,0,b)
and
{\mathrm{tr}}(D)=0
.
∎

Convergence Rate for Degenerate Partial and Stochastic Differential Equations via weak Poincaré Inequalities
Alexander Bertram ,, Martin Grothaus ,

Abstract
Abstract . We employ weak hypocoercivity methods to study the long-term behavior of operator semigroups generated by degenerate Kolmogorov operators with variable second-order coefficients, which solve the associated abstract Cauchy problem. We prove essential m-dissipativity of the operator, which extends previous results and is key to the rigorous analysis required. We give estimates for the L 2 L^{2} -convergence rate by using weak Poincaré inequalities. As an application, we obtain estimates for the (sub-)exponential convergence rate of solutions to the corresponding degenerate Fokker-Planck equations and of weak solutions to the corresponding degenerate stochastic differential equation with multiplicative noise.

In order to obtain the rate of convergence to their stationary solution and to their equilibrium measure for solutions to degenerate stochastic differential equations, respectively, it is assumed that a Poincaré inequality exists for the measure induced by the potential giving the force. The same is necessary for the complementary result

H
.
Invariance and conservativity imply that

T_{t}(\mu(f))=\mu(f)T_{t}(1)=\mu(f)=\mu(T_{t}f)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all }f\in L^{2}(\mu).

Since
f-\mu(f)\in D(L^{H})
for all
f\in D(L)
, this yields

\mu((T_{t}f)^{2})-\mu(T_{t}f)^{2}=\mu((T_{t}^{H}(f-\mu(f)))^{2})\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\xi(t)(\mu((f-\mu(f))^{2})+\|f\|_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2})

for all
f\in D(L)
and
t\geq 0
.
Since
|f-\mu(f)|\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{osc}}
, this shows the wanted estimate with
c_{1}=2\tilde{c}_{1}
for all
f\in D(L)
.
This extends to
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
by approximation as in the proof of
Assumption
(WH5).
.

\square

5
Applications

We briefly present direct applications of the hypocoercivity result to PDEs and SDEs.
Since the principles used are the same, we refer to
[
7
]
for details.
We assume the conditions stated in
1.2
.

5.1
The abstract Cauchy problem

We consider the abstract Cauchy problem associated with the operator
L
on
X=L^{2}(\mu)
:

u(0)=u_{0},\hskip 18.49988pt\partial_{t}u(t)=Lu(t),\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all }t\geq 0,

(5.1)

where
(L,D(L))
is the closure of
(L,\mathcal{C})
on
X
,
u:[0,\infty)\to X
and
u_{0}\in X
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote the semigroup on
X
generated by
(L,D(L))

and set
u(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T_{t}u_{0}
. Then
u
is the unique mild solution to (
5.1
) for all
u_{0}\in X

and the unique classical solution for all
u_{0}\in D(L)
, so in particular for all
u_{0}\in\{f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}):\nabla f\text{ has compact support}\}
.

Furthermore,
1.2
shows that for all bounded measurable
u_{0}
,
we obtain convergence of the unique solution
u(t)
to a constant as
t\to\infty
. More precisely, there are explicit constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending on the choice of
\Sigma
,
\Phi
and
\Psi
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

\left\|u(t)-\int_{E}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq c_{1}\xi(t)\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2},

with
\xi
given by (
1.6
).

5.2
The associated Fokker-Planck equation in divergence form

Consider the Hilbert space
\widetilde{X}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{2}(E;\widetilde{\mu})
, where

\widetilde{\mu}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=Z(\Phi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}Z(\Psi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\Phi(x)+\Psi(y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

and define the differential operator
(L^{\mathrm{FP}},\mathcal{C})
on
\widetilde{X}
for all
f\in\mathcal{C}=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\otimes C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
as in (
1.4
).
The abstract Cauchy problem associated with the closure
(L^{\mathrm{FP}},D(L^{\mathrm{FP}}))
is given by
(
1.3
).
Using the unitary transformation

T:X\to\widetilde{X},\hskip 9.24994ptTg=\rho g\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ with }\hskip 9.24994pt\rho(x,v)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\Phi(x)-\Psi(y)},

as well as the result on the adjoint semigroup
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
stated at the end of the proof of
1.1
,
we obtain essential m-dissipativity of
(L^{\mathrm{FP}},\mathcal{D})
,
where
\mathcal{D}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=C_{c}^{2}(E)
is a
T
-invariant core of
(L^{*},D(L^{*}))
.
A simple approximation argument gives essential m-dissipativity of
(L^{\mathrm{FP}},\mathcal{C})
on
\widetilde{X}
, and the semigroup
(\widetilde{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(T(T_{t}^{*})T^{-1})_{t\geq 0}
generated by the closure
(L^{\mathrm{FP}},D(L^{\mathrm{FP}}))
induces solutions of the Cauchy problem as seen in
Section

5.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in\widetilde{X}
such that
\frac{u_{0}}{\rho}
is bounded and set
u(t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\widetilde{T}_{t}u_{0}
. Then
1.2
yields the existence of constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
depending on
\Sigma
,
\Phi
and
\Psi
such that for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)-\rho\int_{E}u_{0}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}\right\|_{\widetilde{X}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|T(T_{t}^{*})T^{-1}u_{0}-T(1)\int_{E}\frac{u_{0}}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right\|_{\widetilde{X}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(T_{t}^{*})T^{-1}u_{0}-\int_{E}T^{-1}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq c_{1}\xi(t)\left\|\frac{u_{0}}{\rho}\right\|_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2},

with
\xi
given by (
1.6
). This shows convergence of the solution
u
to the stationary solution
\rho\int_{E}u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)
in
\widetilde{X}
as
t\to\infty
with the rate
\xi
.

5.3
Degenerate Diffusion with multiplicative noise

Finally, we connect the convergence result to the initially stated Itô SDE (
1.1
).
Under the assumptions of
1.1
, the theory of Generalized Dirichlet Forms developed by Stannat (see
[
16
]
) yields the existence of a Hunt process

\mathbf{M}=(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},(X_{t},Y_{t}),(P_{(x,y)})_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}})

with state space
E=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}
and
P_{(x,y)}
-a.s. continuous sample paths for all
(x,y)\in E
,
which is properly associated in the resolvent sense with the semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
generated by
(L,D(L))
.
In particular, we obtain that for each
f\in\widetilde{X}
and all
t>0
,
T_{t}f
is a
\mu
-version of
p_{t}f
, where
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the transition semigroup of
\mathbf{M}
given by

p_{t}f:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{R},\hskip 18.49988pt(x,y)\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}[f(X_{t},Y_{t})].

Due to
[
17
, Proposition 1.4]
, for any
\mu
-probability density
h\in L^{1}(\mu)
(including
h\equiv 1
),
the probability law
P_{h}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{E}P_{(x,y)}h(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}\mu
on the space of continuous paths solves the martingale problem for
(L,D(L))
on
C_{c}^{2}(E)
, i.e. for each
f\in C_{c}^{2}(E)
, the stochastic process
(M_{t}^{[f]})_{t\geq 0}
given by

M_{t}^{[f]}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f(X_{t},Y_{t})-f(X_{0},Y_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}Lf(X_{s},Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

is a martingale with respect to
P_{h}
. Additionally, by
[
14
, Lemma 2.1.8]
, for
f\in D(L)
with
f^{2}\in D(L)
and
Lf\in L^{4}(\mu)
, the process
(N_{t}^{[f]})_{t\geq 0}
defined by

N_{t}^{[f]}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(M_{t}^{[f]})^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}L(f^{2})(X_{s},Y_{s})-2(fLf)(X_{s},Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\hskip 9.24994ptt\geq 0,

is also a martingale wrt.
P_{h}
. Analysis of the coordinate processes together with Lévy’s characterization
of Brownian motion shows that
(X_{t},Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a weak solution to the SDE (
1.1
), since
\sigma=\sqrt{\Sigma}
is invertible with locally bounded inverse.

Under the assumptions of
1.2
, we therefore obtain the existence of constants
c_{1},c_{2}
depending on
\Sigma
,
\Phi
and
\Psi
such that

\left\|p_{t}f-\mu(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(E;\mu)}^{2}\leq c_{1}\xi(t)\|f\|_{\mathrm{osc}}^{2}

for all bounded measurable
f:E\to\mathbb{R}
. This implies that the law
P_{1}
with initial distribution
\mu
is strongly mixing, i.e. for any two Borel sets
A_{1},A_{2}
on the space of continuous paths, it holds

P_{1}(\varphi_{t}A_{1}\cap A_{2})\to P_{1}(A_{1})P_{1}(A_{2})\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ as }t\to\infty,

where
\varphi_{t}A_{1}
denotes the set of shifted paths

\varphi_{t}A_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{(Z_{s})_{s\geq 0}\in C([0,\infty),E)\mid(Z_{s+t})_{s\geq 0}\in A_{1}\}.

Discretisable quasi-actions I: Topological completions and hyperbolicity Thanks: This research was partially supported by the Israel Science Foundation (grant No. 1562/19 ).
Alex Margolis

Abstract
. We define and develop the notion of a discretisable quasi-action. It is shown that a cobounded quasi-action on a proper non-elementary hyperbolic space X X not fixing a point of ∂ X \partial X is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action on either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite graph. Topological completions of quasi-actions are also introduced. Discretisable quasi-actions are used to give several instances where commensurated subgroups are preserved by quasi-isometries. For example, the class of ℤ \mathbb{Z} -by-hyperbolic groups is shown to be quasi-isometrically rigid. We characterise the class of finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to either ℤ n × Γ 1 \mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\Gamma_{1} or Γ 1 × Γ 2 \Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2} , where Γ 1 \Gamma_{1} and Γ 2 \Gamma_{2} are non-elementary hyperbolic groups.



1.
Introduction

One of the central problems of geometric group theory is to understand to what extent the large-scale geometry of a finitely generated group determines its algebraic structure.
A
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic group
is a finitely generated group
G
containing an infinite cyclic normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}\cong H\vartriangleleft G
such that the quotient
G/H
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group. The following theorem is an application of the methods developed in this article:

Theorem A

(Corollary
7.13
)
.

A finitely generated group that is quasi-isometric to a
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic group is also
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic.

Theorem
A
is complemented by earlier work of Gersten
[
Ger92
]
and Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb
[
KKL98
]
, from which it can be deduced that two
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic groups are quasi-isometric if and only if their hyperbolic quotients are quasi-isometric.

Special cases of Theorem
A
were already known. Kapovich–Leeb and Rieffel independently proved that a finitely generated group is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
if and only if it is of the form
\mathbb{Z}
-by-
Q
, where
Q
is a finite extension of a cocompact Fuchsian group
[
KL97a
,
Rie01
]
. This was an important step in understanding finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to the fundamental groups of 3-manifolds. Kleiner–Leeb later proved a special case of Theorem
A
for groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}
-by-
Q
, where
Q
is a finite extension of a uniform lattice in a symmetric space
[
KL01
]
.
Mosher–Sageev–Whyte showed that a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}\times F_{2}
is virtually
\mathbb{Z}\times F_{m}
for some
m\geq 2

[
Why01a
,
MSW03
]
, proving another special case of Theorem
A
.

The common thread needed to prove these special cases of Theorem
A
is the notion of a quasi-action. A
quasi-action
of a group
G
on a metric space
X
, denoted
G\qa X
, consists of a uniform collection of quasi-isometries
\{f_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of
X
such that
f_{\id}
is uniformly close to the identity map, and the distance between
f_{gh}
and
f_{g}\circ f_{h}
in the sup metric is uniformly bounded for all
g,h\in G
. Two quasi-actions
G\qa X
and
G\qa Y
are
quasi-conjugate
if there is a coarsely
G
-equivariant quasi-isometry from
X
to
Y
.
To characterise groups quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
, Kapovich–Leeb and Rieffel use the fact that a quasi-action on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
can be quasi-conjugated to an isometric action on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Mosher–Sageev–Whyte prove that any cobounded quasi-action on a locally finite bushy tree can be quasi-conjugated to an isometric action on a possibly different locally finite bushy tree.

This article introduces a framework for studying quasi-actions on more general spaces, including all proper non-elementary hyperbolic spaces and all Cayley graphs of finitely presented groups of cohomological dimension two. We introduce the notion of a discretisable quasi-action and give numerous applications, including Theorem
A
.
The central definition of this article is the following:

Definition
.

Let
X
be a metric space. A cobounded quasi-action
G\qa X
is said to be
discretisable
if it is quasi-conjugate to an action
G\curvearrowright Y
, where
Y
is a connected locally finite graph and
G
acts on
Y
by graph automorphisms. We say the space
X
is
discretisable
if every cobounded quasi-action on
X
is discretisable.

As we shortly see, the notion of a discretisable quasi-action is much more natural, robust and useful than the previous definition might initially suggest. To explain why this is the case, we first note that in general, there is nothing particularly remarkable about the graph
Y
in the preceding definition — all we know about
Y
is that it is a locally finite graph quasi-isometric to
X
. The crux of the matter is not so much the intrinsic local geometry of
Y
, but rather what one can say about
G
by knowing that it acts on a locally finite graph.
Here are two such properties that will motivate the subsequent discussion:

(1)

Corestricting
\phi:G\rightarrow\Aut(Y)
to the closure of the image gives a homomorphism
G\rightarrow\hat{G}
with dense image, where
\hat{G}
is a totally disconnected locally compact group.

(2)

The stabiliser
H
of a vertex of
Y
is a commensurated subgroup of
G
, i.e. every conjugate of
H
is commensurable to
H
.

The homomorphism
G\rightarrow\hat{G}
in
1
is an example of what we call a topological completion, whilst
the subgroup
H
in
2
is an example of what we call a coarse stabiliser.
Discretisability can be reformulated in terms of topological completions and coarse stabilisers, which we formally define shortly.

Proposition B

(Proposition
4.14
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G\qa X
is discretisable;

(2)

G\qa X
has a coarse stabiliser;

(3)

the topological completion of
G\qa X
exists and is compact-by-(totally disconnected).

Switching between these equivalent formulations is essential for both proving and applying discretisability. We now define and discuss the terms in Proposition
B
, indicating how discretisability can be used to prove Theorem
A
and similar theorems.

Topological completions of quasi-actions

Given a quasi-action
G\qa X
, we say a subset
T\subseteq G
is
bounded
if for some (hence every)
x\in X
, the quasi-orbit
T\cdot x\coloneqq\{t\cdot x\mid t\in T\}
is bounded. If a topological group
G
acts continuously, properly and isometrically on a proper metric space
X
, then
T\subseteq G
has compact closure if and only if it has bounded orbits. This motivates the following definition:

Definition
.

A
topological completion of a quasi-action

G\qa X
is a pair
(\hat{G},\rho)
where:

(1)

\hat{G}
is a locally compact group;

(2)

\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
is a homomorphism with dense image;

(3)

for every
T\subseteq G
,
T
is bounded if and only if
\overline{\rho(T)}
is compact.

The following theorem shows that topological completions are unique. We refer the reader to Theorem
4.8
for a more precise statement.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.8
)
.

Topological completions are unique modulo a compact normal subgroup.

It thus makes sense to talk about
the
topological completion of a quasi-action. Topological completions need not exist. Indeed, in Section
4.5
we show that non-trivial quasi-morphisms give rise to cobounded quasi-actions on
\mathbb{R}
that do not have topological completions. Nonetheless, we demonstrate that for a very large class of spaces, including proper non-elementary hyperbolic spaces, cobounded quasi-actions
always
have topological completions. The following theorem is inspired by work of Furman
[
Fur01
]
and Whyte
[
Why01b
]
.

Theorem D

(Corollary
4.22
)
.

If
X
is a cocompact proper geodesic metric space whose Morse boundary contains at least three points, then any cobounded quasi-action on
X
has a topological completion.

More generally, cobounded quasi-actions on tame metric spaces always have topological completions; see Section
4.4
for details. Armed with the structure theory of locally compact groups, topological completions are a powerful new tool in geometric group theory.

Coarse stabilisers of quasi-actions

Discretisability can be characterised in terms of coarse stabilisers, which are coarse analogues of compact open subgroups.

Definition
.

A subgroup
H\leq G
is a
coarse stabiliser
of
G\qa X
if:

(1)

H
is bounded;

(2)

every bounded subset of
G
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets.

If
G\qa X
has a coarse stabiliser, then it is unique up to commensurability (Proposition
3.5
).
A subgroup
H\leq G
is said to be
commensurated
, denoted
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
, if every conjugate of
H
is commensurable to
H
. Commensurated subgroups are also known as almost normal, inert or near normal subgroups. If
G
is finitely generated relative to a commensurated subgroup
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
, the
quotient space
is the set of left cosets
G/H
equipped with the relative word metric; see Section
2.2
for details. The quotient space is a proper discrete metric space that is well-defined up to quasi-isometry. Moreover, if
H
is normal, then the quotient space coincides with the quotient group when equipped with the word metric.

A basic fact of Lie theory is that if a Lie group
G
acts smoothly and transitively on a smooth manifold
M
with isotropy subgroup
H
, then the homogeneous space
G/H
is diffeomorphic to
M
via a diffeomorphism that conjugates the action of
G
on
G/H
to the action of
G
on
M
.
The following proposition is a coarse geometric analogue of this fact in the setting of cobounded quasi-actions admitting a coarse stabiliser, where the associated homogeneous space
G/H
is discrete. This proposition also generalises the Milnor–Schwarz lemma, which can be recovered in the case
H
is trivial.

Proposition E

(Proposition
3.7
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action with coarse stabiliser
H
. Then:

(1)

G
is finitely generated relative to
H
;

(2)

H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
;

(3)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to the natural left action
G\curvearrowright G/H
. In particular,
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
X
.

Proposition
E
furnishes us with a blueprint for proving Theorem
A
and similar theorems. Indeed, suppose
G
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to a group of the form
\mathbb{Z}
-by-
Q
, where
Q
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group. A theorem of Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb ensures
G
admits a cobounded quasi-action on
Q

[
KKL98
]
. If this quasi-action were discretisable (see Theorem
F
), then Propositions
B
and
E
would imply that
G\qa Q
has a coarse stabiliser
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
such that the associated quotient space
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
. All that remains is to deduce that
H
— which is necessarily a two-ended commensurated subgroup — is commensurable to a normal subgroup.

Discretisable quasi-actions and spaces

A hyperbolic metric space is said to be
non-elementary
if its Gromov boundary contains at least three points. We exhibit the following trichotomy for quasi-actions on proper hyperbolic spaces, partially resolving a conjecture of Gromov
[
Gro87
, 0.3.C]
.

Theorem F

(Theorem
5.6
)
.

Let
X
be a proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action, then one of the following holds:

(1)

(connected case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a homogeneous
simply-connected negatively curved manifold of dimension at least two.

(2)

(mixed case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a pure millefeuille space.

(3)

(totally-disconnected case)

G\qa X
is discretisable.

We briefly elaborate on the continuous and mixed cases of Theorem
F
. Homogeneous negatively curved manifolds were classified by Heintze as a particular family of connected solvable Lie groups equipped with left-invariant metrics
[
Hei74
]
. Caprace–Cornulier–Monod–Tessera define a class of
\CAT(-1)
spaces known as
millefeuille spaces
, which are a certain sort of warped product of a tree with a homogeneous negatively curved manifold
[
CCMT15
,
Cor18
]
. A millefeuille space is said to be
pure
if it is neither a tree nor a homogeneous negatively curved manifold. Such spaces exhibit a mixture of continuous behaviour coming from the transitive action on the manifold, and discrete behaviour coming from the action on the tree with discrete orbits.

A quasi-action
G\qa X
on a proper hyperbolic space
X
naturally extends to an action of
G
on the Gromov boundary
\partial X
. Using work of Caprace–Cornulier–Monod–Tessera
[
CCMT15
]
, we have a complete understanding of the case where
G
fixes a point of
\partial X
, which can occur only when
X
is quasi-isometric to a millefeuille space (see Corollary
5.8
). Under the additional hypothesis that
G
does not fix a point of
\partial X
, we can sharpen the conclusion of Theorem
F
:

Corollary G

(Corollary
5.7
)
.

Let
X
be a proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action that does not fix a point of
\partial X
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

(connected case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a rank one symmetric space of non-compact type.

(2)

(totally-disconnected case)

G\qa X
is discretisable.

An iconic theorem of Mosher–Sageev–Whyte asserts one can quasi-conjugate a cobounded quasi-action on a finite valance bushy tree to an isometric action on such a tree
[
MSW03
, Theorem 1]
.
Mosher–Sageev–Whyte’s proof proceeds in two stages. In the first stage, which is the heart of their argument, they use
quasi-edges
to quasi-conjugate the quasi-action to an isometric action on a locally finite graph. In other words, they show precisely that cobounded quasi-actions on finite valance bushy trees are discretisable. For the second stage, which is more classical, they add 2-cells to this graph and then apply Dunwoody’s tracks technology to obtain an action on a tree
[
Dun85
]
.
Theorem
F
thus provides a far-reaching generalisation of the crucial first step of Mosher–Sageev–Whyte’s argument.

Discretisability is not a phenomenon particular to groups exhibiting coarse negative curvature, and can often be deduced via group cohomology. We will not pursue this line of investigation in this article, but state a result, to appear in future work, that further illustrates the ubiquity of discretisable groups. A
generalised Baumslag–Solitar group
is the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups in which every vertex and edge group is infinite cyclic, and the associated Bass–Serre tree is infinite-ended.

Theorem H

(
[
Mar22
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely presented group of cohomological dimension two. Then exactly one of the following holds:

(1)

(connected case)

\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a closed surface of non-positive Euler characteristic.

(2)

(mixed case)

\Gamma
is a generalised Baumslag–Solitar group.

(3)

(totally-disconnected case)
every cobounded quasi-action
G\qa\Gamma
is discretisable.

A natural problem in geometric group theory, raised by the author in
[
Mar21
]
, is to find examples of groups
G
and subgroups
H
such that the following question has a positive solution:

Question 1
.

Suppose a finitely generated group
G
contains an infinite normal (or commensurated) subgroup
H
. If
G^{\prime}
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
G
, does
G^{\prime}
contain a normal (or commensurated) subgroup
H^{\prime}
such that
H^{\prime}
is quasi-isometric to
H
and
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
?

Normal and commensurated subgroups give rise to a
coarse bundle
structure on the group. A key step in finding positive solutions to Question
1
is showing that fibres of this coarse bundle structure are preserved by quasi-isometries.
Positive answers to special cases of Question
1
formed a key step in several quasi-isometric rigidity results, including work of Farb–Mosher, Mosher–Sageev–Whyte, Eskin–Fisher–Whyte, Peng and the author
[
FM98
,
FM99
,
FM00
,
FM02
,
MSW03
,
Why01a
,
Why10
,
EFW12
,
EFW13
,
Pen11a
,
Pen11b
,
Mar21
]
.
Although Question
1
is of course false in full generality, discretisability provides a framework to prove there are many further instances where this question has a positive answer. In particular, we use discretisability to prove Theorems
J
and
M
, providing a positive solution to Question
1
for groups quasi-isometric to central extensions of hyperbolic groups and products of hyperbolic groups respectively.

Groups quasi-isometric to central extensions of hyperbolic groups

By a
central extension of a hyperbolic group
we mean a finitely generated group
G
that is a central extension of the form
H
-by-
Q
, where
H
is a finitely generated abelian central subgroup of
G
, and
Q=G/H
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group. Without loss of generality, we can always assume
H
is free abelian. These groups are a well-studied class of groups that were shown to be biautomatic by Neumann–Reeves
[
NR97
]
.

The “internal” quasi-isometry classification of central extensions of hyperbolic groups is already well-understood. Indeed, work of Neumann–Reeves
[
NR97
]
shows a central extension of a hyperbolic group of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-by-
Q
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
. Combined with a result of Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb
[
KKL98
]
, this implies that if
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are non-elementary hyperbolic groups, then the central extensions
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-by-
Q
and
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
-by-
Q^{\prime}
are quasi-isometric if and only if
n=m
and
Q
is quasi-isometric to
Q^{\prime}
. We thus investigate the class of finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to central extensions of hyperbolic groups, or equivalently, the class of finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
.

We first focus on
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic groups, which — up to passing to an index two subgroup if necessary — are central extensions. The class is a rich source of groups that are quasi-isometric but have different algebraic properties. For instance, Gersten showed that uniform lattices in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
\widetilde{\PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
are quasi-isometric but not commensurable, demonstrating that two of Thurston’s eight model geometries are quasi-isometric
[
Ger92
]
. The class of
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic groups also contains two quasi-isometric groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
such that
G
has Kazhdan’s property (T) but
G^{\prime}
does not, thus demonstrating property (T) is not a quasi-isometry invariant
[
DK18
, §19.9]
.

Theorem
A
says the class of
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic groups is quasi-isometrically rigid, giving many instances in which Question
1
has a positive solution.
Somewhat surprisingly, the technology developed in this article ensures the proof of Theorem
A
is actually easier in the generic case when the hyperbolic quotient is
not
virtually isomorphic to a uniform lattice in a rank one symmetric space. This is because in the discretisable case, after applying
[
KKL98
]
, one gets a two-ended commensurated subgroup for free via Propositions
B
,
E
and Corollary
F
. Such a subgroup can then be shown to be commensurable to a normal subgroup. For the case in which the quotient is not discretisable, one has to work much harder to construct a two-ended normal subgroup via a growth estimate
[
KL01
, §5–6]
.

For higher rank free abelian central subgroups, we obtain an analogous result if we assume residual finiteness. We recall that a group is
residually finite
if the intersection of all finite index subgroups is trivial.

Theorem I

(Theorem
7.15
)
.

A finitely generated residually finite group that is quasi-isometric to a central extension of a hyperbolic group of the form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-by-
Q
is virtually a central extension of a hyperbolic group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-by-
Q^{\prime}
, where
Q
is quasi-isometric to
Q^{\prime}
.

Leary–Minasyan recently described a family of CAT(0) groups that are not biautomatic
[
LM21
]
. Such groups are quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times F_{2}
, but are not virtually central extensions of hyperbolic groups and are not residually finite. These groups demonstrate that the residual finiteness hypothesis in Theorem
I
is necessary. The groups constructed in
[
LM21
]
are HNN extensions of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that the stable letter acts on a finite index subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as an orthogonal matrix in
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
. Theorem
J
demonstrates that similar behaviour occurs for all groups quasi-isometric to central extensions of hyperbolic groups, thus providing a complete algebraic characterisation of such groups.

A subgroup
H\leq G
is said to be
weakly separable
if for every
g\in G\setminus H
, there exists a homomorphism
\phi:G\rightarrow Q
such that
\phi(H)
is finite and
\phi(g)\notin\phi(H)
; we note
Q
is not assumed to be finite. A separable subgroup is necessarily weakly separable. The notion of a weakly separable subgroup is due to Caprace–Kropholler–Reid–Wesolek
[
CKRW20
]
.
If
H\vartriangleleft G
is a normal subgroup, the action of
G
by conjugation on
H
induces a homomorphism
G\rightarrow\Aut(H)
. More generally, if
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
is a commensurated subgroup, then conjugation induces the
modular homomorphism

G\rightarrow\Comm(H)
, where
\Comm(H)
is the abstract commensurator of
H
. The abstract commensurator of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorem J

(Theorem
7.1
and Proposition
7.14
)
.

Let
Q
be a non-elementary locally finite vertex-transitive hyperbolic graph and let
n\geq 1
. A finitely generated group
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
if and only if both the following hold:

(1)

there is a subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
such that the quotient space
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
;

(2)

the image of the modular homomorphism
G\rightarrow\Comm(H)\cong\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

Moreover, the following are equivalent:

•

H
is weakly separable;

•

H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup of
G
;

•

a finite index subgroup of
G
centralises a finite index subgroup of
H
;

•

G
is biautomatic.

Remark
.

It follows from Lemma
2.23
that weak separability of
H
in Theorem
J
is equivalent to separability of
H
under the additional hypothesis that all finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to
Q
are residually finite. It is a well-known open question whether all hyperbolic groups are residually finite.

We briefly outline the proof of Theorem
J
. If
G
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
, then a result of Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb implies the quasi-action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
descends to a quasi-action of
G
on
Q
. We show that this quasi-action is discretisable, hence has some coarse stabiliser
H
. We now apply the formalism of
coarse bundles
as described in Section
2.3
. In particular, Proposition
3.10
can be applied to show the coarse bundle
p:G\to G/H
is fibre-preserving quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q\to Q
. This implies
H
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, hence has a finite index subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Properties
(1)
and
(2)
readily follow.
Conversely, if
(1)
and
(2)
hold, we can apply a result of Mineyev concerning
\ell_{\infty}
-cohomology to deduce the coarse bundle
p:G\to G/H
has a coarse Lipschitz section
[
Min00
]
. Together with
(1)
and
(2)
, this can be used to show
G
is quasi-isometric to
H\times G/H
, hence to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
.

Groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces

We characterise finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to the product of finitely many non-elementary hyperbolic spaces. A group acting isometrically on a product
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
is said to
preserve the product structure
if every isometry splits as a product of isometries, possibly permuting the factors. The following theorem generalises results of Kleiner–Leeb
[
KL97b
,
KL09
]
and Ahlin
[
Ahl02
]
, who prove special cases where the hyperbolic factors are quasi-isometric to symmetric spaces or trees.

Theorem K

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. Then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, preserving the product structure, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a rank one symmetric space of non-compact type or a locally finite graph.

It is well-known that there are finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to the direct product of two infinite hyperbolic groups that do not virtually split as a direct product. Examples include uniform irreducible lattices in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
\Aut(T)\times\Aut(T)
for
T
a finite valence regular tree. Such examples demonstrate that the conclusion of Theorem
K
cannot in general be strengthened to conclude
\Gamma
virtually splits as a direct product. The reason why such lattices exist is that both
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
T
are spaces with a large amount of symmetry. We expect most hyperbolic groups and spaces do not possess such symmetry, and in such situations, Theorem
K
would imply direct products of hyperbolic groups are virtually preserved by quasi-isometries; see also Theorem
M
.

A graph is
Helly
if any family of pairwise intersecting combinatorial balls have non-trivial intersection. A group is
Helly
if it acts geometrically on a Helly graph
[
CCG+20
]
. Helly groups have a robust nonpositive-curvature-like structure, possessing many properties also possessed by
\CAT(0)
groups. Moreover, Helly groups are biautomatic even though
\CAT(0)
groups might not be
[
LM21
]
. We refer the reader to
[
CCG+20
]
for additional properties of Helly groups. An isometric action on a locally finite hyperbolic graph can be conjugated to an action on a Helly graph; see Proposition
6.21
. Combining this observation with Theorem
K
, we show the following:

Theorem L

(Theorem
6.24
)
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space
not
quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space, then
\Gamma
is Helly. In particular,
\Gamma
is biautomatic.

Intriguingly, Theorem
L
is false if we drop the hypothesis that no
X_{i}
is quasi-isometric to a symmetric space. Hughes and Valiunas have an example of a group that is not biautomatic acting geometrically on
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times T
, where
T
is a finite valence regular tree
[
HV22
]
. This example illustrates an essential difference between discretisable and non-discretisable hyperbolic groups. In addition to the existence of coarse stabilisers, Theorem
L
gives compelling further evidence why in some circumstances, the discrete and combinatorial nature of locally finite graphs — a priori with rather unexciting local geometry — has concrete advantages over spaces with richer local geometry such as symmetric spaces.

We now refine Theorem
K
by deducing the existence of natural commensurated subgroups and quotient spaces that capture the algebraic structure of groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces, providing further instances in which Question
1
(virtually) has a positive solution. For simplicity, we restrict to products of two factors. The case where both factors are quasi-isometric to symmetric spaces is well-understood by work of Kleiner–Leeb
[
KL97b
]
, so we assume at least one factor is not quasi-isometric a symmetric space.

Theorem M

(Theorem
6.10
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric spaces such that
Y
is
not
quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space. Suppose
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
X\times Y
.
Then there exists a finitely generated subgroup
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
, commensurated by a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{*}\leq\Gamma
such that:

(1)

\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
X
;

(2)

\Gamma^{*}/\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
Y.

Moreover,
\Lambda
is weakly separable in
\Gamma^{*}
if and only if
\Gamma
is virtually isomorphic to the direct product
\Gamma_{X}\times\Gamma_{Y}
, where
\Gamma_{X}
and
\Gamma_{Y}
are finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to

and

respectively.

Remark
.

By Lemma
2.23
, the weak separability of
\Lambda
in Theorem
M
is equivalent to separability of
\Lambda
under the additional hypothesis that all finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to

are residually finite.

We recall that subgroup separability was used by Wise to study irreducible lattices in products of trees. Wise showed that such lattices are virtually products if and only if certain “horizontal” and “vertical” subgroups are separable
[
Wis96
, Theorem 5.5]
; see also
[
CKRW20
, Corollary 32]
. The subgroup

in the preceding theorem plays an analogous role.
We note that in the case

is also not quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space, we can reverse the roles of

and

in Theorem

to obtain a commensurated subgroup quasi-isometric to

.

We state two theorems that allow the conclusion of Theorem
K
to be strengthened under additional algebraic hypotheses. These results rely on “abstract arithmeticity” theorems of Caprace–Monod
[
CM09a
, §5]
which assert that under suitable hypotheses, irreducible lattices in products of locally compact groups are arithmetic lattices in semisimple algebraic groups.
In order to rule out irreducible arithmetic lattices, we say a hyperbolic space is
of coarse algebraic type
if it is quasi-isometric to either a rank one symmetric space of non-compact type, or to a rank one Bruhat–Tits building, i.e. to a

-regular tree for

.

Theorem N

(Theorem

)
.

Let

Boundary rigidity of groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces

A

2.1.
Coarse geometric preliminaries

In this subsection we give some basic definitions of coarse metric geometry. We develop terminology and notation relating to quasi-actions, and prove a generalisation of the Milnor–Schwarz lemma for non-proper quasi-actions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For any
r\geq 0
and
A\subseteq X
, let

N_{r}(A)\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid d(x,y)\leq r\text{ for some $y\in A$}\}.

The
Hausdorff distance
between any two subsets
A,B\subseteq X
is defined to be

d_{\text{Haus}}(A,B)\coloneqq\inf\{r\mid A\subseteq N_{r}(B)\text{ and }B\subseteq N_{r}(A)\}.

There are various notions of coarse maps between metric spaces that we use.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a map between metric spaces.

•

Given constants
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
, we say that
f
is
(K,A)
-coarse Lipschitz
if

d(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq Kd(x,x^{\prime})+A

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
. We say that
f
is
coarse Lipschitz
if there exist constants
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
such that
f
is
(K,A)
-coarse Lipschitz.

•

We say that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometric-embedding
if

\frac{1}{K}d(x,x^{\prime})-A\leq d(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq Kd(x,x^{\prime})+A

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.
We say that
f
is a
quasi-isometric embedding
if there exist constants
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
such that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometric embedding.

•

We say that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
if
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometric embedding such that
Y=N_{A}(f(X))
. We say
f
is a
quasi-isometry
if there exist constants
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
such that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry.

•

We say that
f
is a
K

, if it is a
(K,0)
-quasi-isometry. We say that
f
is a
bi-Lipschitz equivalence
if it is a
K
-bi-Lipschitz equivalence for some

.

•

Given a proper non-decreasing function
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and constants

and
A\geq 0
, we say that

is an
(\eta,K,A)
-coarse embedding
if

\eta(d(x,x^{\prime}))\leq d(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq Kd(x,x^{\prime})+A

for all

. We say that

is a

if there exist
\eta
,

and

as above such that

is an
(\eta,K,A)
-coarse embedding.

Remark 2.3
.

For each proper non-decreasing function
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we define

\tilde{\eta}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by
\tilde{\eta}(S):=\sup(\eta^{-1}([0,S]))
. We observe that
whenever
\eta(S)\leq R
, then
S\leq\tilde{\eta}(R)
. Conversely, if
S<\eta(R)
, then
\tilde{\eta}(S)\leq R
.

Being quasi-isometric is an equivalence relation among metric spaces. If
G
is a finitely generated group, then any two word metrics on
G
with respect to arbitrary finite generating sets are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. Moreover, any two Cayley graphs of
G
with respect to finite generating sets are quasi-isometric. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we assume finitely generated groups are equipped with a word metric with respect to a finite generating set. If
G
is finitely generated and
H\leq G
is finitely generated, then the inclusion
H\to G
is a coarse embedding when
H
and
G
are equipped with word metrics with respect to finite generating sets.

If
X
and
Y
are metric spaces, two functions
f,g:X\to Y
are said to be
A
-close
if
\sup_{x\in X}d(f(x),g(x))\leq A
, and are

if they are
A
-close for some

. An
A
-coarse inverse
to

is a function

such that
h\circ f
and

are
A
-close to
\id_{X}
and
\id_{Y}
respectively. We say that

is a
coarse inverse
to

if it is an
A
-coarse inverse for some
A
.
If
X
is a metric space, then its

consists of equivalence classes of quasi-isometries from

to

, where

are equivalent if they are close. The quasi-isometry group
\QI(X)
forms a group with multiplication defined by
[f][g]=[f\circ g]
. Moreover, if

is a coarse inverse to

[f]^{-1}=[h]
.

In a metric space

, an
A
-chain of length

between

consists of a sequence
x=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
in

for

. The following classes of metric spaces will be used throughout this article.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a metric space.

•

We say that
X
is

if closed balls in

are compact.

•

We say that
X
is

if for every

and

\lvert N_{r}(x)\rvert<\infty
.

•

We
X

if there exists a constant

•

We say that
X
is
(K,A)
-quasi-geodesic
if any

. We say that

A metric space is quasi-geodesic if and only if it is quasi-isometric to a geodesic metric space, i.e. to a space in which any pair of points can be joined by an isometrically embedded arc. The following lemma is well-known; see e.g.
[
CdlH16
, Proposition 1.A.1.]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group equipped with the word metric with respect to a finite generating set. Let
H\leq G
be a subgroup equipped with the subspace metric. Then
H
is finitely generated if and only if
H
is coarsely connected.

A connected graph can be considered a metric space when equipped with the induced path metric in which each edge has length one. A graph is
locally finite
if each vertex is incident to only finitely many edges. A connected graph is a locally finite graph if and only if it is a proper metric space. We caution that a locally finite graph with at least one edge is not locally finite as a metric space.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
CdlH16
, Proposition 3.D.11]
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space. The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is quasi-isometric to a proper quasi-geodesic metric space;

(2)

X
is quasi-isometric to a quasi-geodesic locally finite metric space;

(3)

X
is quasi-isometric to a locally finite connected graph.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X,Y
be metric spaces such that
X
is
(K,A)
-geodesic. Suppose

is a map and

is a constant such that
d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq B
whenever
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\leq A
. Then

is coarse Lipschitz.

Proof.

Let

. Let

be an

with

∎

(

)
.

Let

,

,

We now prove the following coarse analogue of the Arzelà–Ascoli Theorem.

Lemma 2.9

(Coarse Arzelà–Ascoli Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a locally finite metric space. Suppose that for some
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
, there exist a sequence of
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries
(f_{i})

of
X
such that
\{f_{i}(x_{0})\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded for some
x_{0}\in X
.
Then there exists a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
and a subsequence
(f_{n_{i}})
such that for all
x\in X
, there exists an
N_{x}
such that
f_{n_{i}}(x)=f(x)
for all
i\geq N_{x}
. In particular,
(f_{n_{i}})
converges pointwise to
f
.

Proof.

We first show that for every
x\in X
,
T_{x}\coloneqq\{f_{i}(x)\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is finite. By hypothesis
T_{x_{0}}\subseteq B_{R}(f_{0}(x_{0}))
for some sufficiently large
R
. For each
x\in X
, we thus see that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

d(f_{0}(x_{0}),f_{i}(x))\leq d(f_{0}(x_{0}),f_{i}(x_{0}))+d(f_{i}(x_{0}),f_{i}(x))\leq R+Kd(x,x_{0})+A,

and so
T_{x}
is bounded. Since
X
is locally finite, each
T_{x}
is finite.

As
X
is locally finite and hence is countable, we can pass to a subsequence
(f_{n_{i}})
such that for each
x\in X
, the sequence
(f_{n_{i}}(x))
is eventually constant and so eventually equal to some
f_{x}\in X
. We define
f:X\to X
by
f(x)=f_{x}
and claim that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry. Indeed, for any
x,y\in X
,
f(x)
and
f(y)
are equal to
f_{n_{i}}(x)
and
f_{n_{i}}(y)
respectively for
i
sufficiently large. Since each
f_{n_{i}}
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry, it follows that
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometric embedding.

Suppose
y\in X
and let
y_{0}\coloneqq f(x_{0})
. Since each
f_{n_{i}}
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry, there exists a
z_{i}\in X
such that
d(f_{n_{i}}(z_{i}),y)\leq A
. Thus for
i
sufficiently large,
f_{n_{i}}(x_{0})=y_{0}
and so

\displaystyle d(z_{i},x_{0})\leq K\Big(d(f_{n_{i}}(z_{i}),f_{n_{i}}(x_{0}))+A\Big)\leq K\Big(d(y,y_{0})+2A\Big)\eqqcolon R_{y}.

Since
N_{R_{y}}(x_{0})
is finite, there exists an
x\in N_{R_{y}}(x_{0})
such that
d(f_{n_{i}}(x),y)\leq A
for all

sufficiently large. Thus
d(f(x),y)\leq A
and so

is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
G

-quasi-action
of

(1)

(2)

(3)

We say that a quasi-action

, we use the terms
\rho
-quasi-conjugacy and
(K,A)
-quasi-conjugacy as appropriate. If
G=G^{\prime}
and no homomorphism
\rho:G\to G
is explicitly mentioned, a quasi-conjugacy is always assumed to be an
\id_{G}
-quasi-conjugacy.

Quasi-conjugacies arise naturally in the context of group actions. Suppose
\rho:G\rightarrow\Isom(X)
is an isometric action and
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a quasi-isometry with coarse inverse
\overline{f}
. Then
f
is easily seen to be a quasi-conjugacy from the action
\rho:G\rightarrow\Isom(X)
to the quasi-action
g\mapsto f\circ\rho(g)\circ\overline{f}
on
Y
.
Moreover, quasi-conjugacy is an equivalence relation among the collection of quasi-actions of a fixed group
G
.

The following proposition is a variant of the well-known Milnor–Schwarz lemma, which has been called the
fundamental observation of geometric group theory
. Similar generalisations to actions that are not proper are present in the literature — see
[
CdlH16
, Theorem 4.C.5]
and
[
ABO19
, Lemma 3.11]
— although the following theorem is more general as it is phrased in terms of quasi-actions.

Proposition 2.13
.

Suppose
(X,d)
is a quasi-geodesic metric space and
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action. Fix a basepoint
b\in X
. Then there exists an
R_{0}
such that the following holds:

(1)

For any
R\geq R_{0}
,
G
is generated by any set
S\subseteq G
such that

\{g\in G\mid d(g\cdot b,b)\leq R_{0}\}\subseteq S\subseteq\{g\in G\mid d(g\cdot b,b)\leq R\}.

(2)

Let
d_{S}
denote the word metric on
G
with respect to some
S
as above.
Then the isometric left action
G\curvearrowright(G,d_{S})
is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa X
via the
quasi-orbit map

g\mapsto g\cdot b
.
In particular,
(G,d_{S})
is quasi-isometric to
X
.

Proof.

We can choose constants
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
sufficiently large such that the following hold:

(1)

X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-geodesic metric space;

(2)

G\qa X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-action;

(3)

for every
x\in X
, there exists a
g_{x}\in G
such that
d(g_{x}\cdot b,x)\leq A
.

Set
R_{0}\coloneqq 3KA+3A
and fix
R\geq R_{0}
and
S\subseteq G
as in the statement. Let
f:G\rightarrow X
be the quasi-orbit map
g\mapsto g\cdot b
.

Let
g,k\in G
. As
X
is quasi-geodesic, there exists an
A
-chain
g\cdot b=x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}=k\cdot b
, where
m\leq Kd(g\cdot b,k\cdot b)+A
.
Let
g_{0}=g
,
g_{m}=k
and
g_{i}\coloneqq g_{x_{i}}
for
i\neq 0,m
. Thus for every
0\leq i\leq m
, we have
d(g_{i}\cdot b,x_{i})\leq A
.
Using Lemma
2.11
, we see that

\displaystyle d(b,g_{i-1}^{-1}g_{i}\cdot b)

\displaystyle\leq Kd(g_{i-1}\cdot b,g_{i}\cdot b)+3A

\displaystyle\leq Kd(x_{i-1},x_{i})+2KA+3A\leq 3KA+3A=R_{0},

and so
g_{i-1}^{-1}g_{i}\in S
. Since
g^{-1}k=(g_{0}^{-1}g_{1})(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2})\dots(g_{m-1}^{-1}g_{m})
, we deduce that
G
is generated by
S
.
Moreover, since
m\leq Kd(g\cdot b,k\cdot b)+A
, we see

(1)

\displaystyle d_{S}(g,k)\leq Kd(f(g),f(k))+A,

for all
g,k\in G
.

Now suppose
d_{S}(g,k)=n
, so we can write
g^{-1}k=s_{1}\dots s_{n}
for some
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in S
. Setting
t_{i}=s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{i}
for each
i
, we see by another application of Lemma
2.11
that

d(gt_{i-1}\cdot b,gt_{i}\cdot b)\leq Kd(b,s_{i}\cdot b)+3A\leq KR+3A\eqqcolon R^{\prime}

for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus

(2)

\displaystyle d(f(g),f(k))=d(g\cdot b,k\cdot b)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}d(gt_{i-1}\cdot b,gt_{i}\cdot b)\leq R^{\prime}n=R^{\prime}d_{S}(g,k)

for all
g,k\in G
. Together, (
1
) and (
2
) imply that
f:G\rightarrow X
is a quasi-isometric embedding.

Since for every
x\in X
,
d(f(g_{x}),x)\leq A
, we see
f
is a quasi-isometry.
Finally, for all
g,k\in G
, we have
d(k\cdot f(g),f(kg))=d(k\cdot g\cdot b,kg\cdot b)\leq A.
Therefore,
f
is a quasi-conjugacy.
∎

By considering the action of
G
on its Cayley graph with respect to a generating set
S
as in Proposition
2.13
, we deduce:

Corollary 2.14
.

An arbitrary cobounded quasi-action
G\qa X
on a quasi-geodesic metric space is quasi-conjugate to an isometric and vertex-transitive action of
G
on a connected (perhaps locally infinite) graph.

Corollary
2.14
is a general nonsense argument that cannot — as far as the author is aware — be used to say anything nontrivial about either the group
G
or the space
X
.
A more general statement of this sort was proven by Kleiner–Leeb
[
KL09
, Corollary 1.1]
. In contrast, quasi-conjugating a quasi-action to an isometric action on a
locally finite
graph is a non-trivial matter, and as this article demonstrates, has several non-trivial consequences.

2.2.
Commensurated subgroups and quotient spaces

In this subsection, we define and investigate the relative word metric and relative Cayley graphs of pairs
(G,H)
, where
G
is a group and
H
is a subgroup. In the case
G
is finitely generated relative to
H
, the relative Cayley graph is well-defined up to quasi-isometry. The relative Cayley graph is shown to be locally finite if and only if
H
is commensurated. We also characterise when a commensurated subgroup is commensurable to normal subgroup.
The results and proofs in this subsection are minor variations of statements already in the literature, typically stated under additional hypothesis that
G
is finitely generated or has the structure of a topological group; see e.g.
[
KM08
,
CM13
]
. We do not make these assumptions here.

Definition 2.15
.

If
G
is a group and
H\leq G
is a subgroup, a
relative generating set
of the pair
(G,H)
is a set
S\subseteq G
such that
S\cup H
generates
G
. We say that
G
is finitely generated relative to
H
if
(G,H)
has a finite relative generating set.

Remark 2.16
.

If
G
is finitely generated, then it is finitely generated relative to any subgroup
H\leq G
.

Given a group
G
, a subgroup
H
and a non-empty set
S\subseteq G
, we define a simplicial graph
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
with vertex set

\{gH\mid g\in H\}
and edge set

\{(gH,gsH)\mid g\in G,s\in S\}.

In particular,
gH
and
kH
are joined by an edge if and only there exist
h,h^{\prime}\in H
and
s\in S
such that either
ghs=kh^{\prime}
or
gh=kh^{\prime}s
. It is clear
G
acts on
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
by graph automorphisms.

Proposition 2.17
.

The graph
\Gamma=\Gamma_{G,H,S}
is connected if and only if
S
is a relative generating set of
(G,H)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
S
is symmetric, i.e.
S=S\cup S^{-1}
.
We first suppose that
\Gamma
is connected. For every
g\in G
,
H
and
gH
can be joined by an edge path
g_{0}H,g_{1}H,\dots,g_{n}H
in
\Gamma
where
g_{0}=1_{G}
and
g_{n}=g
. For each
i
, there exist
h_{i}\in H
and
s\in S
such that
g_{i}h_{i}sH=g_{i+1}H
, and so
g_{i}^{-1}g_{i+1}
is contained in the subgroup
\langle H,S\rangle_{G}
. Thus
g=(g_{0}^{-1}g_{1})(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2})\dots(g_{n-1}^{-1}g_{n})
is also contained in
\langle H,S\rangle_{G}
.

Conversely, suppose
G=\langle H,S\rangle_{G}
. Then every
g\in G
can be written in the form
h_{1}s_{1}h_{2}s_{2}\dots h_{n}s_{n}h_{n+1}
, where for each
i
,
h_{i}\in H
and
s_{i}\in S
. Setting
g_{i}=h_{1}s_{1}h_{2}s_{2}\dots h_{i}s_{i}
, we see that

H,g_{1}H,g_{2}H,\dots,g_{n}H=gH

is a path in
\Gamma
from
H
to
gH
, so
\Gamma
is connected.
∎

If
S
is a relative generating set, we call
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
the
relative Cayley graph
of the pair
(G,H)
.
The
relative word metric
on
G/H
is the metric
d_{S}
such that
d_{S}(gH,kH)
is the minimal number of edges from
gH
to
kH
in the graph
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
.
Although the relative Cayley graph is sometimes called a (left) Schreier graph, we do not use this terminology here to avoid confusion with the graph whose edge set is
\{(gH,sgH)\mid s\in S,g\in G\}
, also called the Schreier graph.

Proposition 2.18
.

If
S
and
S^{\prime}
are two finite relative generating sets of the pair
(G,H)
, then
(G/H,d_{S})
and
(G/H,d_{S^{\prime}})
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. Moreover, the relative Cayley graphs
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
and
\Gamma_{G,H,S^{\prime}}
are quasi-isometric.

Proof.

We pick
K\geq 1
sufficiently large such that
d_{S}(H,s^{\prime}H)\leq K
for every
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and
d_{S^{\prime}}(H,sH)\leq K
for every
s\in S
. It follows from the triangle inequality that

\frac{1}{K}d_{S^{\prime}}(gH,kH)\leq d_{S}(gH,kH)\leq Kd_{S^{\prime}}(gH,kH)

for every
gH,kH\in G/H
.
Since
(G/H,d_{S})
and
(G/H,d_{S^{\prime}})
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent nets in
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
and
\Gamma_{G,H,S^{\prime}}
respectively, the relative Cayley graphs are quasi-isometric.
∎

One consequence of Proposition
2.18
is that geometric properties of the relative word metric that are invariant under bi-Lipschitz equivalence depend only on the pair
(G,H)
and not the choice of relative generating set.

We recall two subgroups
H,K\leq G
are
commensurable
if the intersection
H\cap K
has finite index in both
H
and
K
. Commensurability is an equivalence relation among subgroups of
G
. A subgroup
H\leq G
is said to be
commensurated
, denoted
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
, if every conjugate of
H
is commensurable to
H
. A subgroup commensurable to a commensurated subgroup is also commensurated.
Commensurated subgroups can be characterised as follows:

Proposition 2.19

(cf.
[
CM13
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Suppose
S
is a finite relative generating set of the pair
(G,H)
. Then
\Gamma=\Gamma_{G,H,S}
is locally finite if and only if
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
S
is symmetric. The set
\Lambda\coloneqq\{hsH\mid h\in H,s\in S\}
is the set of vertices of
\Gamma
that are adjacent to
H
. Since
G
acts transitively on
\Gamma
, we see that
\Gamma
is locally finite if and only if
\Lambda_{s}\coloneqq\{hsH\mid h\in H\}
is finite for every
s\in S
. Now
\Lambda_{s}
is finite if and only if
s^{-1}Hs
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets. We thus see
\Gamma
is locally finite if and only if
s^{-1}Hs
and
H
are commensurable for every
s\in S
. Since
H
and
S
generate
G
,
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
if and only if
s^{-1}Hs
is commensurable to
H
for every
s\in S
. Thus
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
if and only if
\Gamma
is locally finite.
∎

Remark 2.20
.

When
G
is finitely generated,
\Gamma_{G,H}
is quasi-isometric to the
coned–off Cayley graph
as defined by Farb
[
Far98
]
. The relative Cayley graph
\Gamma_{G,H}
is thus hyperbolic precisely when
G
is weakly hyperbolic relative to
H
, i.e.
G
is hyperbolic relative to
H
in the sense of
[
Far98
]
. If
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
, then the bounded coset penetration property of
[
Far98
]
fails, so
G
is never hyperbolic relative to
H
in the sense of
[
Bow12
]
.

Definition 2.21
.

If
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
and
G
is finitely generated relative to
H
, we call the left coset space
G/H
, equipped with the relative word metric, a
quotient space
. We call the map
G\to G/H
given by
g\mapsto gH
the
quotient map
.

By Proposition
2.18
, the quotient space is well-defined up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence. We caution the reader that the quotient map is not a homomorphism unless
H
is normal.

Given a commensurated subgroup
H
, a natural question to consider is how close
H
is to a normal subgroup. A subgroup
H\leq G
is
weakly separable
if it is the intersection of virtually normal subgroups of
G
containing
H
. Using a result of Caprace et al.
[
CKRW20
]
, we show the following:

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
G
and
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
be finitely generated groups. The following are equivalent:

(1)

H
contains a finite index subgroup that is normal in
G
;

(2)

H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup of
G
;

(3)

H
is commensurable to a subgroup that is normal in a finite index subgroup of
G
;

(4)

H
is weakly separable in
G
.

Proof.

(
1
)
\implies
(
2
) and (
2
)
\implies
(
3
) are obvious.

(
3
)
\implies
(
2
): Suppose
H
is commensurable to a subgroup
H^{\prime}
that is normal in some finite index subgroup of
G
. Then
H^{\prime}
has finitely many conjugates in
G
, each of which is commensurable to
H^{\prime}
as
H^{\prime}
is commensurated in
G
. Therefore
H^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\cap_{g\in G}gH^{\prime}g^{-1}
is a finite index subgroup of
H^{\prime}
that is normal in
G
, hence
H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup.

(
2
)
\implies
(
4
): Suppose
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
commensurable to
H
. As
H
is finitely generated, so is
K
. Moreover,
H\cap K
is a finite index subgroup of
K
. Let
K_{0}
be the intersection of all subgroups of
K
of index
[K:H\cap K]
. Since
K
is finitely generated,
K_{0}
is a finite index characteristic subgroup of
K
, hence is normal in
G
. Moreover,
H
contains
K_{0}
as a finite index subgroup, so that
H
is virtually normal. Thus
H
is weakly separable.

(
4
)
\implies
(
1
): If
H
is weakly separable, then
[
CKRW20
, Corollary 7]
ensures
H
contains a finite index subgroup that is normal in
G
.
∎

Recall that a subgroup
H\leq G
is
separable
if it is equal to the intersection of finite index subgroups of
G
containing
H
. Moreover,
H
is separable if and only if it is closed in the profinite topology of
G
, i.e. the topology on
G
with basis the cosets of finite index normal subgroups.
The relation between weak separability and separability in the present context is encapsulated in the following lemma:

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
G
and
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
be finitely generated groups.

(1)

If
H
is separable, then it is weakly separable.

(2)

Suppose
H
is weakly separable. Then
H
is separable if and only if
G/H_{0}
is residually finite, where
H_{0}
is the normal core of
H
.

Note that by Lemma
2.22
, the normal core
H_{0}
in (
2
) is a finite index subgroup of
H
.

Proof.

(
1
): This is immediate from the definitions.

(
2
): It follows from the definitions that
H_{0}
is separable if and only if
G/H_{0}
is residually finite. Thus it is enough to show
H
is separable if only if
H_{0}
is. First suppose
H
is separable. Since
H_{0}
is the intersection of conjugates of
H
, it follows
H_{0}
is separable. Conversely, suppose
H_{0}
is separable. As
H_{0}
is a finite index subgroup of
H
and
H_{0}
is closed in the profinite topology,
H
is also closed in the profinite topology, hence is separable. ∎

If
H
is a group then a
commensuration
of
H
is an isomorphism
\phi:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
between finite index subgroups of
H
. Two commensurations are
equivalent
if they agree on some finite index subgroup of
H
. Let
\Comm(H)
be the set of equivalence classes of commensurations. We give
\Comm(H)
the structure of a group by defining for
[\phi],[\psi]\in\Comm(H)
,
[\phi][\psi]=[\phi^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime}]
for suitable representatives
\phi^{\prime}\in[\phi]
and
\psi^{\prime}\in[\psi]
such that
\phi^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime}
is defined.

There is a homomorphism
\Delta:G\to\Comm(H)
associated to a group
G
containing a commensurated subgroup
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
, defined as follows. For every
g\in G
, there exist finite index subgroups
H_{1},H_{2}\leq H
such that
gH_{1}g^{-1}=H_{2}
, and so conjugation by
g
induces an element
\Delta(g)\in\Comm(H)
. The map
\Delta:G\rightarrow\Comm(H)
is readily verified to be a well-defined homomorphism called the
modular homomorphism
.

2.3.
Coarse bundles and quasi-isometries

In this subsection we recall the definition and basic properties of coarse bundles as used in
[
Why10
,
Mar21
]
.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
,
(B,d_{B})
and
(F,d_{F})
be proper quasi-geodesic metric spaces. We say
X
is a coarse bundle over
B
with fibre
F
if there exist constants
K\geq 1
,
A\geq 0
, proper non-decreasing functions
\eta,\phi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and a map
p:X\rightarrow B
such that:

(1)

p
is
(K,A)
-coarse Lipschitz, i.e. for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,

d_{B}(p(x),p(x^{\prime}))\leq Kd_{X}(x,x^{\prime})+A;

(2)

for all
b\in B
,
D_{b}\coloneqq p^{-1}(b)
is the
fibre
of
X
at
b
and there is an
(\eta,\phi)
-coarse embedding
s_{b}:F\rightarrow X
with
\im(s_{b})=D_{b}
;

(3)

for all
b,b^{\prime}\in B
,
d_{\text{Haus}}(D_{b},D_{b^{\prime}})\leq Kd_{B}(b,b^{\prime})+A
.

When the above holds, we say that
p:X\rightarrow B
is a
(K,A)
-coarse bundle
, or simply a
coarse bundle
. We call
X
the
total space
,
B
the
base space
and
F
the
fibre
.

Our main source of coarse bundles is the following:

Proposition 2.25

(
[
Mar21
, Proposition 3.14]
)
.

Let
G
and
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
be finitely generated groups equipped with the word metric. Let
G/H
be the quotient space equipped with the relative word metric. Then the quotient map
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is a coarse bundle with fibre
H
.

Another (trivial) source of coarse bundles are spaces that split as direct products. We assume the direct product of metric spaces
X
and
Y
is equipped with the
\ell_{2}
-metric
d((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))=\sqrt{d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})^{2}+d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})^{2}}
unless stated otherwise. The following is easy to verify:

Lemma 2.26
.

Suppose
B
and
F
are proper geodesic metric spaces, and
\pi_{B}:F\times B\rightarrow B
is the projection onto the
B
factor. Then
\pi_{B}:F\times B\rightarrow B
is a coarse bundle with fibre
F
.

We are particularly interested in quasi-isometries of the total space that preserve fibres in the following sense.

Definition 2.27
.

Let
p:X\rightarrow B
and
p^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
be coarse bundles. We say that a quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is
A
-fibre-preserving
if for every
b\in B
, there exists a
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
such that
d_{\text{Haus}}(f(D_{b}),D^{\prime}_{b^{\prime}})\leq A
, where
D_{b}
and
D^{\prime}_{b^{\prime}}
are fibres of
p:X\rightarrow B
and
p^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
respectively.
We say a quasi-action
\phi:G\to X^{X}
is
fibre-preserving
if there exists an
A
such that every
\phi(g)
is an
A
-fibre-preserving quasi-isometry.

Proposition 2.28
.

If
p:X\rightarrow B
is a coarse bundle and
G\qa X
is a fibre-preserving quasi-action, then there is an
induced quasi-action

G\qa B
satisfying

\sup_{b\in B}d_{\text{Haus}}(D_{g\cdot b},g\cdot D_{b})<\infty.

Moreover, if
G\qa X
is cobounded, then the induced quasi-action
G\qa B
is also cobounded.

Proof.

We pick constants
K
and
A
such that
p:X\rightarrow B
is a
(K,A)
-coarse bundle,
G\qa X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-action and every
g\in G
is an
A
-fibre-preserving quasi-isometry. Lemma 3.6 of
[
Mar21
]
ensures there exist constants
K_{1}
and
A_{1}
such that for every
g\in G
, there exists a
(K_{1},A_{1})
-quasi-isometry
\lambda(g):B\rightarrow B
such that
d_{\text{Haus}}(D_{\lambda(g)(b)},g\cdot D_{b})\leq A_{1}
for all
g\in G
and
b\in B
.
Using Lemma
2.11
, this implies

\displaystyle d_{\text{Haus}}(D_{\lambda({gh})(b)},D_{(\lambda(g)\circ\lambda(h))(b)})

\displaystyle\leq d_{\text{Haus}}(gh\cdot D_{b},g\cdot D_{\lambda(h)(b)})+2A_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Kd_{\text{Haus}}(h\cdot D_{b},D_{\lambda(h)(b)})+2A_{1}+3A

\displaystyle\leq KA_{1}+2A_{1}+3A\eqqcolon A_{3}

for all
g,h\in G
and
b\in B
. It follows from the definition of a
(K,A)
-coarse bundle that
\sup_{b\in B}d_{B}((\lambda(g)\circ\lambda(h))(b),\lambda(gh)(b))\leq KA_{3}+A
, and so
\lambda
defines a quasi-action of
G
on
B
.

Now suppose
G\qa X
is cobounded and choose
r
such that for all
x,y\in X
, there exists
g\in G
with
d_{X}(x,g\cdot y)\leq r
. Let
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and pick
x\in D_{b}
and
y\in D_{b^{\prime}}
. Choose
g\in G
such that
d_{X}(x,g\cdot y)\leq r
, and note by the definition of
\lambda(g)
there exists
z\in D_{\lambda(g)(b^{\prime})}
such that
d(z,g\cdot y)\leq A_{1}
. Since
d_{X}(x,z)\leq r+A_{1}
,
x\in D_{b}
and
z\in D_{\lambda(g)(b^{\prime})}
, we have
d_{B}(b,\lambda(g)(b^{\prime}))\leq K(r+A_{1})+A
. Thus the induced quasi-action
G\qa B
is cobounded.
∎

Definition 2.29
.

A coarse bundle
p:X\rightarrow B
with fibre
F
is
quasi-isometrically trivial
if there is a quasi-isometry
X\rightarrow B\times F
that is
A
-fibre preserving with respect to the projection
\pi:B\times F\rightarrow B
.

We note that if
p:X\rightarrow B
is a quasi-isometrically trivial coarse bundle with fibre
F
, then
X
is quasi-isometric to
F\times B
.
A useful invariant to determine whether a coarse bundle is quasi-isometrically trivial is the
fibre-distortion function
of a coarse bundle, introduced by the author and summarised below
[
Mar21
]
. The fibre-distortion function is encoded in a more sophisticated coarse invariant called the
holonomy
due to Whyte
[
Why10
]
.

Let
p:X\rightarrow B
be a coarse bundle with fibre
F
. For each
b\in B
, it follows from Definition
2.24
that there exist coarse embeddings
s_{b}:F\rightarrow D_{b}
and
t_{b}:D_{b}\rightarrow F
, with uniform distortion, such that
t_{b}\circ s_{b}
and
s_{b}\circ t_{b}
are uniformly close to the identity on
F
and
D_{b}
respectively.
For each
b_{0},b\in B
, since
D_{b_{0}}
and
D_{b}
are at finite Hausdorff distance, there is a closest point projection map
p_{b_{0}}^{b}:D_{b_{0}}\rightarrow D_{b}
. We thus define a map
\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b}:F\rightarrow F
given by
t_{b}\circ p_{b_{0}}^{b}\circ s_{b_{0}}
.
We now define the fibre-distortion function as follows:

Definition 2.30
.

If
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a quasi-isometry, let

\kappa(f)\coloneqq\inf\{K\geq 1\mid\textrm{$f$ is a $(K,A)$-quasi-isometry for some $A\geq 0$}\}.

Suppose
p:X\rightarrow B
is a coarse bundle with fibre
F
. Pick a basepoint
b_{0}\in B
. For each
b\in B
, we define a function
\Lambda:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
b\mapsto\log(\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b}))
, which we call the
fibre distortion function
.

Before proving properties about the fibre-distortion function, we first give some examples.

Example 2.31
.

Let
X=F\times B
be a direct product of proper geodesic metric spaces. As in Lemma
2.26
, the projection
X\rightarrow B
defines a coarse bundle structure. The fibre distortion function is trivial, since the closest point projection
p_{b}^{b^{\prime}}:D_{b}\rightarrow D_{b^{\prime}}
is of the form
(f,b)\mapsto(f,b^{\prime})
and so
\kappa(p_{b}^{b^{\prime}})=1
.

Example 2.32
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with
\mathbb{Z}\cong H=\lvert a\rvert\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
. We can define a homomorphism
h:G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for each
g\in G
, if there exist non-zero integers
m,n
such that
ga^{n}g^{-1}=a^{m}
, then
h(g)=\log(\lvert\frac{m}{n}\rvert)
. This gives a well-defined homomorphism on
G
called the
height function
. (See
[
FM98
]
,
[
Why01a
]
.) It follows from
[
Mar21
, Lemma 5.8]
that the height function descends to a well-defined map
G/H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is equal to the fibre distortion function of
G\rightarrow G/H
.

Example 2.33
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
. Let
B
be a free abelian basis of
H
. Since
\Comm_{G}(H)\cong\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, for each
g\in G
, there is a matrix
A_{g}\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
such that conjugation by
g
is equal to multiplication by
A_{g}
on some finite index subgroup of
H
. Moreover,
A_{g}=A_{g^{\prime}}
if
g
and
g^{\prime}
are in the same (left or right)
H
-coset.
By Proposition
2.25
, the quotient map
q:G\rightarrow G/H
is a coarse bundle. As shown in
[
Mar21
, §5]
, the fibre-distortion map is given by
gH\mapsto\lvert\log(\lVert A_{g}\rVert)\rvert
, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the
\ell_{1}
-operator norm.

A subgroup of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded in the operator norm if and only if it is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus if
G
is a group,
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
is a commensurated subgroup and
\Delta:G\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is the modular homomorphism, then
G\rightarrow G/H
has bounded fibre distortion if and only if
\im(\Delta)
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

We now justify the use of the definite article in Definition
2.30
.

Proposition 2.34
.

If
p:X\rightarrow B
is a coarse bundle with fibre
F
, the fibre-distortion function
\Lambda
is well-defined up to uniform additive error, independently of the choice of the basepoint
b_{0}
or the maps
\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b}
.

To prove this, we make use of the following lemmas. The first follows easily from the definition of
\kappa
above.

Lemma 2.35
.

If
f,g:X\rightarrow Y
are quasi-isometries that are close, then
\kappa(f)=\kappa(g)
.

Lemma 2.36

(
[
Mar21
, Lemma 5.6]
)
.

Suppose that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a quasi-isometry. If
g:W\rightarrow X
and
h:Y\rightarrow Z
are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries, then

\frac{\kappa(f)}{K^{2}}\leq\kappa(h\circ f\circ g))\leq\kappa(f)K^{2}.

Proof of Proposition
2.34
.

It is easy to verify
p_{b_{0}}^{b}
and
t_{b}
are coarsely well-defined up to some additive error, so Lemma
2.35
ensures
\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b})
and hence
\Lambda
, depends only on
b_{0}
and
b
and not on the choice of
t_{b}
and
p_{b_{0}}^{b}
.
Now let
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
be fibre distortion functions with respect to basepoints
b_{0}
and
b_{1}
. Then for all
b\in B
,
\lambda_{b_{1}}^{b}=t_{b}\circ p_{b_{1}}^{b}\circ s_{b_{1}}
is close to

\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b}\circ\lambda_{b_{1}}^{b_{0}}=t_{b}\circ p_{b_{0}}^{b}\circ s_{b_{0}}\circ t_{b_{0}}\circ p_{b_{1}}^{b_{0}}\circ s_{b_{1}}.

Therefore, Lemma
2.36
implies
\frac{\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b})}{K^{2}}\leq\kappa(\lambda_{b_{1}}^{b})\leq\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b})K^{2}
, where

. Thus for all

,
\lvert\Lambda_{0}(b)-\Lambda_{1}(b)\rvert\leq 2\log(K)
.
∎

We now show fibre-preserving quasi-isometries preserve the fibre-distortion function, which is a slight generalisation of
[
Mar21
, Proposition 5.5]
.

Proposition 2.37
.

Suppose that
p:X\rightarrow B
and
p^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}
are coarse fibre bundles with fibres
F
and
F^{\prime}
, and with fibre distortion functions
\Lambda:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
respectively. If
f:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a fibre-preserving quasi-isometry and
\hat{f}:B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
is the induced quasi-isometry as in Lemma 3.6 of
[
Mar21
]
, there is a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\lvert\Lambda^{\prime}(\hat{f}(b))-\Lambda(b)\rvert\leq C.

Proof.

Suppose
f:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is
A
-fibre-preserving. The induced quasi-isometry
\hat{f}:B\rightarrow B^{\prime}
can therefore be chosen so that
d_{\text{Haus}}(f(D_{b}),D_{\hat{f}(b)})\leq A
. For each
b\in B
, pick
f_{b}:D_{b}\rightarrow D_{\hat{f}(b)}
such that
d(f(x),f_{b}(x))\leq A
for all
x\in D_{b}
. Define
\mu_{b}\coloneqq t_{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b}s_{b}:F\rightarrow F^{\prime}
. Since
p_{b_{0}}^{b}
and
p_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)}
are closest point projections, it follows that

\sup_{x\in F}d((t_{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b}p_{b_{0}}^{b}s_{b_{0}})(x),(t_{\hat{f}(b)}p_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b_{0}}s_{b_{0}})(x))<\infty.

Lemma
2.35
ensures
\kappa(t_{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b}p_{b_{0}}^{b}s_{b_{0}})=\kappa(t_{\hat{f}(b)}p_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b_{0}}s_{b_{0}})
. Since
t_{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b}p_{b_{0}}^{b}s_{b_{0}}
is close to
\mu_{b}\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b}
and
t_{\hat{f}(b)}p_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)}f_{b_{0}}s_{b_{0}}
is close to
\lambda_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)}\mu_{b_{0}}
, we use Lemma

to deduce

\frac{1}{A}\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b})\leq\kappa(\lambda_{\hat{f}(b_{0})}^{\hat{f}(b)})\leq A\kappa(\lambda_{b_{0}}^{b})

for some constant

. The result now follows from the definition of
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
.
∎

We are particularly interested in the case where the fibre-distortion function is bounded:

Corollary 2.38
.

Suppose that
p:X\rightarrow B
and
p^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}
are coarse fibre bundles with fibres
F
and
F^{\prime}
, and with fibre distortion functions
\Lambda:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. If
f:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is a fibre-preserving quasi-isometry, then
\Lambda
is bounded if and only if
\Lambda^{\prime}
is.

We mention a theorem of Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb which says that if spaces split as direct products and the factors satisfy a form of coarse non-positive curvature, then quasi-isometries preserve the direct product structure
[
KKL98
]
. Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb define two classes of spaces that they call
coarse type I
and
coarse type II
(
[
KKL98
, Definition 3.5]
) which include:

(1)

cocompact non-elementary hyperbolic spaces;

(2)

cocompact geodesically complete CAT(0) spaces containing a rank one geodesic;

(3)

irreducible symmetric spaces of non-compact type;

(4)

thick Euclidean buildings.

We will not give the definition of spaces of coarse type I or coarse type II, and will only use the fact that the previous four examples are of coarse type I or II; see
[
KKL98
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.39

(
[
KKL98
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let
X=Z\times\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is either of coarse type I or coarse type II and
Z
is a geodesic metric space all of whose asymptotic cones are homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
K,A
, there exist constants
K^{\prime},A^{\prime},D
such that the following hold.
If
f:X\rightarrow X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry, there is a homomorphism
\sigma:G\rightarrow\Sym(n)
so that for every
i
, there exists a
(K^{\prime},A^{\prime})
-quasi-isometry
f_{i}:X_{i}\rightarrow X_{\sigma(g)(i)}
such that the following diagram commutes up to error at most
D

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\sigma(g)(i)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\sigma(g)(i)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}}

where
\pi_{i}:X\rightarrow X_{i}
is the projection onto
X_{i}
.

We first reformulate Theorem
2.39
in the language of quasi-actions and coarse bundles.

Proposition 2.40
.

Let
X=Z\times\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
be as in Theorem
2.39
. Suppose
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
,
X_{I}\coloneqq\Pi_{i\in I}X_{i}
, and
\pi_{I}:X\rightarrow X_{I}
is the projection onto
X_{I}
. Let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action, and let
\sigma:G\rightarrow\Sym(n)
be as in Theorem
2.39
. Let
G_{I}=\{g\in G\mid\sigma(g)(I)=I\}
, which is a finite index subgroup of
G
. Then the restricted quasi-action
G_{I}\qa X
is fibre-preserving with respect to the projection
\pi_{I}:X\rightarrow X_{I}
.

Proof.

After reordering the factors, we assume
I=\{1,\dots,r\}
.
We show
G_{I}\qa X
is fibre-preserving with respect to the coarse bundle
\pi_{I}:X\rightarrow X_{I}
. Suppose
G_{I}\qa X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-action and fix some
g\in G_{I}
. By Theorem
2.39
, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, there is a quasi-isometry
g_{i}:X_{i}\rightarrow X_{\sigma(g)(i)}
, such that
d(\pi_{\sigma(g)(i)}(g\cdot x),g_{i}\cdot\pi_{i}(x))\leq D
for all
x\in X
, where
D
is a constant depending only on
X
,
K
and
A
.

For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
p_{i}:X_{I}\to X_{i}
be the projection map. Let
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{r})\in X_{I}
and
g\in G_{I}
. We define

so that
p_{\sigma(g)(i)}(b^{\prime})=g_{i}\cdot b_{i}
. Let
x\in\pi_{I}^{-1}(b)
, so that
\pi_{i}(x)=b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Then

d_{X_{\sigma(g)(i)}}(\pi_{\sigma(g)(i)}(g\cdot x),p_{\sigma(g)(i)}(b^{\prime}))=d(\pi_{\sigma(g)(i)}(g\cdot x),g_{i}\cdot\pi_{i}(x))\leq D

for

. This implies

and so

, where

and

are the fibres over

and

respectively. A similar argument, replacing

with

and

ensures

and

are uniformly finite Hausdorff distance apart. This demonstrates that every

is

-fibre-preserving for some uniform

.
∎

We will now use Theorem
2.39
to quasi-conjugate quasi-actions on products to isometric actions on products.
We say a quasi-action
G\qa\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}

coarsely preserves the product structure
if up to uniformly bounded error, each

splits as a product
(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
, possibly permuting factors. Note that Theorem
2.39
ensures if each

is coarse type I or coarse type II (in particular, there is no “

preserves the product structure.

Proposition 2.41
.

Let
X=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is either of coarse type I or of coarse type II. Suppose
G\qa X
is a (cobounded) quasi-action and
G_{i}
is the finite index subgroup of

that stabilises the factor

, i.e.
G_{i}=\{g\in G\mid\sigma(g)(i)=i\}

is as in Theorem

. Suppose every

is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action

. Then
G\qa\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action

, where each

is isometric to some

and the quasi-conjugacy

The proof of Proposition

will follow from the following theorem of Kleiner–Leeb; see also an argument of Ahlin

Theorem 2.42

(
[

]
)
.

Let

be a group,
H\leq G
be a finite index subgroup and

be a quasi-action. Then there is a quasi-action
\alpha:G\qa\Pi_{i\in G/H}X_{i}
preserving the product structure such that

•

is quasi-isometric to

;

•

•

to

is quasi-conjugate to

is an isometric action, then we may assume each

is isometric to

is an isomeric action.

2.41
.

By Theorem

, the quasi-action

induces a homomorphism

We conclude with an application of Theorem
2.39
that will be used when studying groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces. We recall a hyperbolic metric space is of
coarse algebraic type
if it is quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite regular tree of valence at least three.

Lemma 2.43
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper geodesic non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If
\Gamma
is virtually a uniform lattice in a semisimple algebraic group, then each of the
X_{i}
is of coarse algebraic type.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in a semisimple algebraic group, it acts geometrically on a space
\Pi_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
, where each
Y_{i}
is either an irreducible symmetric space or an irreducible thick Euclidean building. Note that all the
Y_{i}
and
X_{i}
are either of course type I or II, or are Euclidean spaces. Since
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
and
\Pi_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
are quasi-isometric, we can thus apply Theorem
2.39
to deduce
\Pi_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}
contains no Euclidean factors,
n=m
and after reordering factors, each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
. Thus
Y_{i}
cannot contain an isometrically embedded Euclidean plane, hence has rank one. Since rank one Euclidean buildings are locally finite infinite-ended trees, each
X_{i}
is either quasi-isometric to such a tree, or is quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space.
∎

2.4.
Topological groups and metric spaces

In this section we recall some definitions and conventions regarding topological groups. Classical references for the theory of topological groups are
[
HR79
,
Bou98
]
. We refer to
[
CdlH16
]
for a thorough exposition of the metric geometry of locally compact groups.

A
topological group
is a group which is simultaneously a topological space, such that the multiplication and inverse maps are continuous. We assume topological groups are Hausdorff unless stated otherwise.
A topological group is
locally compact
if it is locally compact as a topological space, i.e. every point has a compact neighbourhood.
Under some mild hypotheses, locally compact groups can be thought of as geometric objects in their own right. A locally compact group
G
is
compactly generated
if there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq G
that generates
G
. Note that a discrete group is finitely generated if and only if it is compactly generated.

If
G
is a compactly generated locally compact group, then the word metric with respect to any compact generating set is unique up to quasi-isometry. More generally, Cornulier–de la Harpe define the notion of a geodesically adapted metric on a compactly generated locally compact group
G
.
A metric
d
on
G
is said to be
geodesically adapted
if it is:

(1)

left-invariant;

(2)

proper, i.e. all balls
N_{r}(1)\coloneqq\{g\in G\mid d(1,g)\leq r\}
have compact closure;

(3)

locally bounded, i.e.
N_{r}(1)
is a neighbourhood of
1
for
r
sufficiently large;

(4)

quasi-geodesic.

For any two geodesically adapted metrics on
G
, the identity map is a quasi-isometry (
[
CdlH16
, Corollary 4.B.11]
). We remark that a geodesically adapted metric is in general not assumed to be
compatible
, i.e. the topology on
G
is not necessarily the topology induced by the metric. For instance, the word metric with respect to a compact generating set is always discrete, so cannot induce the original topology of
G
unless
G
is also discrete.

A theorem of Kakutani–Kodaira implies that one can quotient out a compactly generated group by a compact normal subgroup to obtain a second-countable metrisable group
[
KK44
]
; see also
[
CdlH16
, Theorems 2.A.10 and 2.B.6]
.

Corollary 2.44
.

Let
G
be a locally compact compactly generated group. Then there exists a compact normal subgroup
K\vartriangleleft G
such that
G/K
is metrisable and second-countable.

If
G
is a locally compact group and
X
is a proper quasi-geodesic space, an isometric action
\rho:G\to\Isom(X)
is said to be
geometric
if:

(1)

\rho
is continuous;

(2)

it is
cocompact
: there is a compact subset
K\subseteq X
such that
\rho(G)K=X
;

(3)

it is
proper
: for all compact
K\subseteq X
, the set
\{g\in G\mid\rho(g)K\cap K\neq\emptyset\}
has compact closure.

We note that a less restrictive notion of geometric action — which applies to actions that are not necessarily continuous — is considered in
[
CdlH16
]
.

Lemma 2.45

(
[
CdlH16
, Theorem 4.C.5]
)
.

Suppose
G
is a locally compact group,

is a proper quasi-geodesic metric space and

is geometric. Then

is compactly generated and the orbit map

is a quasi-conjugacy from

.

Every locally compact group

Proposition 2.46

(
[

, Remark 4.C.13]
)
.

Let

be compactly generated locally compact groups and let

is a quasi-isometry. In particular:

(2)

The following lemma shows a continuous extension of a geometric action of a uniform lattice is geometric.

Lemma 2.47
.

Let

be quasi-geodesic metric space, let

be a finitely generated group, and let

be a geometric action. If

is also geometric.

Proof.

Since

for some compact set

be compact. Since
\hat{\rho}
is continuous, the natural map

given by
(g,x)\mapsto\hat{\rho}(g)(x)
is continuous. Therefore

is also compact. Since

is proper, the set

is finite. Thus

is contained in

∎

If

We also require the following lemma concerning centralisers in topological groups:

Lemma 2.49
.

Let
G
be a topological group let
\Gamma\leq G
be a finitely generated discrete subgroup. If
\Gamma
is normalised by a dense subgroup of

, then its centraliser

is open.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\leq G
be a dense subgroup normalising

. For each

, we define

by

. As

3.
Discretisable quasi-actions and coarse stabilisers

In this section we develop the basic theory of coarse stabilisers. We characterise discretisability in terms of coarse stabilisers, and prove a generalisation of the Milnor–Schwarz lemma for quasi-actions admitting a coarse stabiliser.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a quasi-action
G\qa X
, a subset
T\subseteq G
is
bounded
if
\{t\cdot x_{0}\mid t\in T\}\subseteq X
is bounded for some (equivalently any)
x_{0}\in X
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
G\qa X
be a quasi-action and let
S\subseteq G
be a generating set that satisfies the conclusions of Proposition
2.13
. Then
T\subseteq G
is bounded in the preceding sense if and only if it is bounded as a subspace of the metric space
(G,d_{S})
.

We recall the definition of a coarse stabiliser:

Definition 3.3
.

A subgroup
H\leq G
is a
coarse stabiliser
of
G\qa X
if:

(1)

H
is bounded;

(2)

every bounded subset of
G
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets.

The motivating example of a coarse stabiliser is the following:

Example 3.4
.

Suppose
Y
is a connected locally finite graph and
G\curvearrowright Y
is an action by graph automorphisms. Let
v
be a vertex of
Y
and let
H\coloneqq\stab_{G}(v)
. We claim that
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\curvearrowright Y
. Firstly, as
H\cdot v=v
,
H
is bounded, so
1
holds. We now show
2
holds.
If
T\subseteq G
is bounded, then
T\cdot v
is bounded in
Y
, hence consists of finitely many vertices. We note if
g,k\in G
, then
gv=kv
if and only if
k\in gH
. Thus
T
consists of finitely many left
H
-cosets.

We now demonstrate the robustness of coarse stabilisers: they are well-defined up to commensurability.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
H
be a coarse stabiliser of the quasi-action
G\qa X
. Then
H^{\prime}\leq G
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
if and only if
H
and
H^{\prime}
are commensurable.

Proof.

First suppose
H^{\prime}
is a coarse stabiliser. Let
x\in X
. By
1
, we can choose
R
sufficiently large such that
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq\{g\in G\mid d(g\cdot x,x)\leq R\}\eqqcolon B
. As
B
is bounded,
2
implies
B
is contained in both finitely many left
H
-cosets and in finitely many left
H^{\prime}
-cosets. Since
H,H^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
H
is contained in finitely many left
H^{\prime}
-cosets, and
H^{\prime}
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets; thus
H
and
H^{\prime}
are commensurable.

For the converse, suppose
H
and
H^{\prime}
are commensurable. It follows from
2
that every bounded subset of
G
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets, and hence is contained in finitely many left
H^{\prime}
-cosets. Thus we need only show
H^{\prime}
is bounded. Since
H^{\prime}\subseteq FH
for some finite
F=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subseteq G
, we see that for any
x\in X
,
H^{\prime}\cdot x\subseteq x_{1}H\cdot x\cup\dots\cup x_{n}H\cdot x
. As
H\cdot x
is bounded, so is
H^{\prime}\cdot x
. Thus
H^{\prime}
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
H\leq G
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
, then it is commensurated.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
. For all
V\subseteq G
and
g\in G
, note that
gVg^{-1}\cdot x
has finite Hausdorff distance from
g\cdot V\cdot g^{-1}\cdot x
. Thus
V
is bounded if and only if
gVg^{-1}
is bounded.
Suppose
G\qa X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-action with coarse stabiliser
H
. By Proposition
3.5
, to show
H
is commensurated it is sufficient to show that for every
g\in G
,
gHg^{-1}
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\curvearrowright X
. Fix
g\in G
. Since
H
is bounded, so is
gHg^{-1}
. If
T\subseteq G
is bounded, then
g^{-1}Tg
is also bounded. Since
H
is a coarse stabiliser,
g^{-1}Tg\subseteq FH
for some finite
F\subseteq G
. Thus
T\subseteq(gFg^{-1})gHg^{-1}
and so every bounded subset is contained in finitely many left
gHg^{-1}
-cosets. Therefore
gHg^{-1}
is a coarse stabiliser.
∎

We now prove a relative version of the Milnor–Schwarz lemma for quasi-actions with coarse stabilisers.

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose
X
is a quasi-geodesic metric space and
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action with coarse stabiliser
H
.
Then:

(1)

G
is finitely generated relative to
H
.

(2)

H
is a commensurated subgroup of
G
.

(3)

The natural left action
G\curvearrowright G/H
is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa X
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. Since
H
is a coarse stabiliser, there exists an
R
such that
H\cdot x\subseteq N_{R}(x)
.
By increasing
R
if necessary, Proposition
2.13
ensures we may assume
G
is generated by
S\coloneqq\{g\in G\mid d(g\cdot x,x)\leq R\}
and
G\curvearrowright(G,d_{S})
is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa X
. Note that
H\subseteq S
. Since
S
is bounded and
H
is a coarse stabiliser, there is a finite subset
F\subseteq G
such that
S\subseteq FH
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F\subseteq S
. We thus deduce that
F
is a finite generating set of the pair
(G,H)
. Let
d_{S}
be the word metric on
G
with respect to
S
and let
d_{G,H,F}=d_{F}
be the relative word metric of the pair
(G,H)
with respect to
F
. We show that the quotient map
(G,d_{S})\rightarrow(G/H,d_{F})
given by
g\mapsto gH
is a quasi-conjugacy. The map is surjective and equivariant, so we need only show it is a quasi-isometric embedding.

Let
g,k\in G
and suppose
d_{S}(g,k)=n
. Then
g^{-1}k=s_{1}\dots s_{n}
for some
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in S
.
Since
S\subseteq FH
, we have
s_{i}=f_{i}h_{i}
, for some
f_{i}\in F
and
h_{i}\in H
and so
d_{F}(gH,kH)\leq n
.
Now suppose
d_{F}(gH,kH)=m>0
.
Then there exist
f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{m}\in F
and
h^{\prime}_{1},\dots,h^{\prime}_{m}\in H
such that
g^{-1}k=f^{\prime}_{1}h^{\prime}_{1}\dots f^{\prime}_{m}h^{\prime}_{m}
. Thus
d_{S}(g,k)\leq 2m
. If
gH=kH
, then
g^{-1}k\in H
, and so
d_{S}(g,k)\leq 1
. Thus
d_{S}(g,k)\leq 2m+1=2d_{F}(gH,kH)+1
as required.
∎

We can use Proposition
3.7
to deduce a necessary and sufficient condition for two quasi-actions, at least one of which has a coarse stabiliser, to be quasi-conjugate.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
X
and
Y
are quasi-geodesic metric spaces and that
G\qa X
and
G\qa Y
are cobounded quasi-actions. Suppose
H\leq G
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. Then
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa Y
if and only if
H
is also a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa Y
.

Proof.

Suppose
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a quasi-conjugacy and
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. Let
x\in X
and
T\subseteq G
. Then it is straightforward to see that
d_{\text{Haus}}(T\cdot f(x),f(T\cdot x))<\infty
. Thus
T\cdot x\subseteq X
is bounded if and only
T\cdot f(x)\subseteq Y
is. It readily follows that
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa Y
. Conversely, suppose
H
is a coarse stabiliser of both
G\qa X
and
G\qa Y
. Then Proposition
3.7
implies that
G\qa X
and
G\qa Y
are both quasi-conjugate to
G\curvearrowright G/H
, hence are quasi-conjugate to one another.
∎

We recall from the introduction that a cobounded quasi-action is discretisable if it can be quasi-conjugated to an action on a locally finite graph. We characterise discretisable quasi-actions as precisely those that have coarse stabilisers:

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space. A cobounded quasi-action
G\qa X
is discretisable if and only if it has a coarse stabiliser.

Proof.

First suppose
G\qa X
is discretisable. By definition,
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an action
G\curvearrowright Y
on a locally finite graph
Y
. Let
v
be a vertex of
Y
and let
H\coloneqq\stab_{G}(v)
. As in Example
3.4
,
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\curvearrowright Y
, hence by Proposition
3.8
,
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
.
Conversely, suppose
H\leq G
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. By Proposition
3.7
, there exists a finite relative generating set
S
of
(G,H)
. Proposition
2.19
ensures the relative Cayley graph
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
is locally finite. Since
H
is the stabiliser of a vertex of
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
, Example
3.4
ensures
H
is also a coarse stabiliser of
G\curvearrowright\Gamma_{G,H,S}
. Thus Proposition
3.8
ensures
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to
G\curvearrowright\Gamma_{G,H,S}
, and so
G\qa X
is discretisable.
∎

We conclude this section with a useful application of Proposition

to fibre-preserving cobounded quasi-actions on coarse bundles. We recall that if

Let

4.
Topological completions of quasi-actions

4.1.
Geometry of topological completions

We now define the notion of a topological completion of a quasi-action and establish its basic properties.
In Proposition
4.3
we show that the topological completion
\hat{G}
of
G\qa X
is compactly generated and the left action of
\hat{G}
on itself is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa X
. In Proposition
4.5
, we show the topological completion of a cobounded quasi-action exists if and only if we can quasi-conjugate it to an isometric action on a proper metric space.
The results of this subsection hold not just for topological completions, but for the more general notion of a weak topological completion.

Definition 4.1
.

The
weak topological completion of a quasi-action

G\qa X
is a homomorphism
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
such that:

(1)

\hat{G}
is a locally compact topological group;

(2)

\rho(G)
is cocompact;

(3)

for every
T\subseteq G
,
T
is bounded if and only if
\overline{\rho(T)}
is compact.

A
topological completion
of
G\qa X
is a weak topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
in which a stronger form of (
2
) holds:

(1)

\rho(G)
is dense in
\hat{G}
.

For ease of exposition, we sometimes say that
\hat{G}
is a (weak) topological completion of
G\qa X
, where the homomorphism
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
is implicit.
For instance, we say
G\qa X
has a totally disconnected topological completion if there exists a topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
such that
\hat{G}
is totally disconnected.

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose
G\qa X
has a (weak) topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
with
K\leq\hat{G}
a compact normal subgroup. If
\pi:\hat{G}\rightarrow\hat{G}/K
is the quotient map, then
\pi\circ\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}/K
is a (weak) topological completion of
G\qa X
.

Given a finitely generated group
\Gamma
equipped with a (left-invariant) word metric
d
,
\Gamma
acts on itself by left-multiplication; we call this action
\phi:\Gamma\to\Isom(\Gamma,d)
the
regular representation
of
\Gamma
. More generally, if
G
is a locally compact compactly generated group, there is a regular representation
G\to\Isom(G,d)
when
G
is equipped with a geodesically adapted metric. Although the regular representation depends on the choice of generating set, it is unique up to quasi-conjugacy.
The following proposition tells us that given a weak topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
of
G\qa X
,
\hat{G}
is quasi-isometric to
X
and the quasi-action of
G
on
X
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to the regular representation of
\hat{G}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
X
is a quasi-geodesic metric space and
\phi:G\rightarrow X^{X}
is a cobounded quasi-action. Let
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
be a weak topological completion.
Then
\hat{G}
is compactly generated. Moreover,
\phi
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to the regular representation of
\hat{G}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho(G)
is cocompact, there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq\hat{G}
such that
\rho(G)K=\hat{G}
. Applying Proposition
2.13
to the quasi-action
\phi:G\rightarrow X^{X}
, there exists a bounded generating set
S\subseteq G
, and so
\overline{\rho(S)}
is compact. Since
\rho(S)
generates
\rho(G)
and
\rho(G)K=\hat{G}
, we deduce
\overline{\rho(S)}\cup K
is a compact generating set of
\hat{G}
.

Since
\hat{G}
is compactly generated, there exists a geodesically adapted metric
d_{\hat{G}}
on
\hat{G}
.
As
d_{\hat{G}}
is left invariant, we can define an isometric action
\psi:G\rightarrow\Isom(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
by
\psi(g)(k)\coloneqq\rho(g)k
. Using the definition of a topological completion and a geodesically adapted metric, we see that for arbitrary
T\subseteq G
,
x_{0}\in X
and
g_{0}\in\hat{G}
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\{\phi(t)(x_{0})\mid t\in T\}
is bounded in
X
;

(2)

\overline{\rho(T)}
is compact;

(3)

\{\psi(t)(g_{0})=\rho(t)g_{0}\mid t\in T\}
is bounded in
(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
.

Applying Proposition
2.13
to both
\phi:G\rightarrow X^{X}
and
\psi:G\rightarrow\Isom(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
, we deduce that there is a sufficiently large bounded generating set
S^{\prime}\subseteq G
such that
\phi
and
\psi
are both quasi-conjugate to
G\curvearrowright(G,d_{S^{\prime}})
, hence are quasi-conjugate to one another. It follows from the definition of
\psi:G\curvearrowright(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
that it is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to the regular representation
\hat{G}\curvearrowright(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
. Thus
\phi
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to the regular representation of
\hat{G}
.
∎

We will also require the following consequence of the Milnor–Schwarz lemma for topological groups
[
CdlH16
, Theorem 4.C.5.]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action with weak topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
. Suppose
Y
is a proper quasi-geodesic metric space and
\phi:\hat{G}\rightarrow\Isom(Y)
is a geometric action. Then
G\qa X
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to
\hat{G}\curvearrowright Y
.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem 4.C.5 of
[
CdlH16
]
implies that for some fixed
y_{0}\in Y
,
d_{\hat{G}}(g,h)\coloneqq d_{Y}(\phi(g)(y_{0}),\phi(h)(y_{0}))
defines a geodesically adapted pseudo-metric
d_{\hat{G}}
on
\hat{G}
. This ensures the regular representation
\hat{G}\curvearrowright(\hat{G},d_{\hat{G}})
is quasi-conjugate to
\hat{G}\curvearrowright Y
. Proposition
4.3
thus implies that
G\qa X
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to
\hat{G}\curvearrowright Y
as required.
∎

We note that if
X
is a proper metric space,
\Isom(X)
can be equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence, making it a second-countable locally compact group acting continuously and properly on
X
(see
[
CdlH16
, Lemma 5.B.4]
). Moreover, if
\Isom(X)
acts cocompactly on
X
, this action is geometric. We give the following geometric characterisation for a quasi-action to have a topological completion:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action. The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a proper metric space
Y
such that
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
G\curvearrowright Y
;

(2)

G\qa X
has a weak topological completion;

(3)

G\qa X
has a topological completion.

Moreover, if
Y
is as in (
1
), then the action
\rho:G\rightarrow\Isom(Y)
is a weak topological completion, and the corestriction
G\rightarrow\overline{\im(\rho)}
of
\rho
is a topological completion.

Proof.

(\ref{item:TCvsQC_metric})\implies(\ref{item:TCvsQC_wTC})
: Suppose
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
\rho:G\rightarrow\Isom(Y)\eqqcolon\hat{G}
for some proper metric space
Y
. We claim that
\rho
is a weak topological completion of
G\qa X
. Fix some
y_{0}\in Y

4.2.
Uniqueness of topological completions

When studying the large-scale geometry of finitely generated groups, it is often convenient to study groups up to passing to and from finite index subgroups and quotienting out by finite normal subgroups. Similarly, one frequently studies locally compact groups up to quotienting out by a compact normal subgroup. This is the case for topological completions, where elements in such a subgroup correspond to quasi-isometries that are uniformly close to the identity map:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a metric space and
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action. Suppose
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
is a weak topological completion and

is a compact normal subgroup. Then there is a constant

such that for every

and

.

Proof.

Suppose
G\qa X
is an
(K,A)
-quasi-action.
We set

and choose
x_{0}\in X
. Since
\overline{\rho(J)}\subseteq L
is compact,
J
is bounded. Pick
R
sufficiently large such that
d(x_{0},g\cdot x_{0})\leq R
for all
g\in J
.
Since
G\qa X
is cobounded, there is a constant
C
such that for all
x\in X
, there exists a
g_{x}\in G
such that
d(g_{x}\cdot x,x_{0})\leq C
. Using the fact that
J
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we see that for all
g\in J
and
x\in X
, setting

and applying Lemma

,

\displaystyle d(g\cdot x,x)

\displaystyle\leq d(g_{x}^{-1}h\cdot g_{x}\cdot x,x)+A

\displaystyle\leq Kd(h\cdot g_{x}\cdot x,g_{x}\cdot x)+4A

\displaystyle\leq Kd(h\cdot x_{0},x_{0})+K^{2}C+KA+KC+4A

\displaystyle\leq KR+K^{2}C+KA+KC+4A\eqqcolon D

as required.
∎

(1)

G
is finitely generated relative to
H
if and only if
G
is compactly generated.

(2)

Any two compact open subgroups of
G
are commensurable. In particular,
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
.

Proof.

(
1
): Suppose
G
is generated by a compact subset
K
. Since
\{gH\}_{g\in G}
is an open cover of
G
, there is a finite subset
S\subseteq G
such that
K\subseteq\bigcup_{s\in S}sH
; thus
S
is a finite generating set of the pair
(G,H)
. Conversely, if
S
is a finite generating set of
(G,H)
, then
\bigcup_{s\in S}sH
is a compact generating set of
G
.

(
2
): Suppose
H
and
H^{\prime}
are compact open subgroups of
G
. The preceding argument implies
H
is contained in finitely many left cosets of
H^{\prime}
, and
H^{\prime}
is contained in finitely many left cosets of
H
. Thus
H
and
H^{\prime}
are commensurable. Since a conjugate of a compact open subgroup is also compact and open, we deduce that
H
is commensurated.
∎

If
G
is a compactly generated locally compact group,
H\leq G
is a compact open subgroup and
S
is a relative generating set of
(G,H)
, then the relative Cayley graph
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
, as defined in Section
2.2
, is known as a
Cayley–Abels graph
or a
rough Cayley graph
; see for instance
[
KM08
]
. Such a graph is always connected and locally finite, and the natural action of
G
on
\Gamma_{G,H,S}
is continuous, proper and vertex-transitive with coarse stabiliser
H
.
The preceding lemma, coupled with Proposition
3.8
, implies the following:

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
G
be a compactly generated locally compact group containing a compact open subgroup. Then
G
acts geometrically on its Cayley–Abels graph. Moreover, up to quasi-conjugacy, this action is independent of the choice of compact open subgroup and finite relative generating set.

We can thus talk about
the
Cayley–Abels graph of a compactly generated locally compact group containing a compact open subgroup.
Modulo a compact normal subgroup, the converse to Theorem
4.10
holds:

Lemma 4.13
.

A locally compact group is compact-by-totally disconnected if and only if it contains a compact open subgroup.

Proof.

Suppose
G
contains a compact open subgroup
H
. Let
K\coloneqq\cap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}
. Since
K
is a compact normal subgroup of
G
, it is sufficient to show
G/K
is totally disconnected. Let
\pi:G\to G/K
be the quotient map and suppose
g_{1}K\neq g_{2}K
. Then there is some
g\in G
such that
g_{1}gH\neq g_{2}gH
. Now
\pi(g_{1}gH)
and
\pi(g_{2}gH)
are distinct cosets of the open subgroup
\pi(H)
, hence are clopen. Thus
g_{1}K
and
g_{2}K
lie in disjoint clopen subsets of
G/K
. It follows that
G/K
is totally disconnected.

Conversely, suppose
G
has a compact normal subgroup
K\vartriangleleft G
such that
G/K
is totally disconnected. By Theorem
4.10
, there is a compact open subgroup
H_{0}\leq G/K
. Since
K
is compact, the preimage of
H_{0}
under the quotient map
G\rightarrow G/K
is a compact open subgroup of
G
.
∎

We can now prove the following:

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G\qa X
is discretisable;

(2)

G\qa X
has a coarse stabiliser;

(3)

the topological completion of
G\qa X
exists and is compact-by-(totally disconnected).

Proof.

(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2)
: This is Corollary
3.9
.

(1)\Longrightarrow(3)
: By definition, if
G\qa X
is discretisable, there exists a locally finite graph
Y
such that
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an action
\rho:G\rightarrow\Aut(Y)
. Since
\Aut(Y)
is totally disconnected, every closed subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
is also totally disconnected. It follows from Proposition
4.5
that
G\qa X
has a totally disconnected topological completion. By Theorem
4.8
, every topological completion of
G\qa X
is compact-by-(totally disconnected).

(3)\Longrightarrow(2)
: Suppose
G\qa X
has a topological completion
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
with
\hat{G}
compact-by-(totally disconnected). By Proposition
4.3
and Lemma
4.13
,
\hat{G}
contains a compact open subgroup
\hat{H}
. Let
H\coloneqq\rho^{-1}(\hat{H})
. We claim that
H
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. Firstly, since
\hat{H}
is compact it follows
H
is bounded. Secondly, if
T\subseteq G
is bounded, then
\overline{\rho(T)}
is compact so is contained in finitely many left
\hat{H}
-cosets. It is evident that if for some
g,k\in T
,
\rho(g)
and
\rho(k)
are contained in the same left
\hat{H}
-coset, then
g
and
k
are contained in the left
H
-coset. Thus
T
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets, verifying
H
is indeed a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
.
∎

The following corollary gives a sufficient condition for a finitely generated group to admit a quasi-action that is not discretisable.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group that is a uniform lattice in a locally compact group
G
that is not compact-by-(totally disconnected). Then there is a cobounded quasi-action
G\qa\Gamma
that is not discretisable.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, there is a quasi-action
G\qa\Gamma
with topological completion
G
. Since
G
is not compact-by-(totally disconnected), Proposition
4.14
implies
G\qa\Gamma
is not discretisable.
∎

We conclude with a criterion determining when a topological completion is discrete:

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
X
be a quasi-geodesic metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action with coarse stabiliser
H
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup of
G
;

(2)

G\qa X
has a discrete topological completion;

(3)

every topological completion of
G\qa X
is compact-by-discrete.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
: Suppose
H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup
H_{1}
. Then
G
acts on the Cayley graph
Y
of
G/H_{1}
with coarse stabiliser
H_{1}
by Example
3.4
. Propositions
3.5
and
3.8
ensure
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to the natural action
G\to\Aut(Y)
. Since the image of
G
in
\Aut(Y)
is discrete, Proposition
4.5
ensures
G\qa X
has discrete topological completion
G/H_{1}
.

(2)\implies(1)
: Suppose
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
is a topological completion of
G\qa X
with
\hat{G}
discrete. Let
H^{\prime}\coloneqq\ker(\rho)
. We claim
H^{\prime}
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. Since
\rho
is a topological completion and
\rho(H^{\prime})=\{e\}
is compact,
H^{\prime}
is bounded and so
1
holds. Moreover, if
T\subseteq G
is bounded, then as
\hat{G}
is discrete,
\rho(T)
is finite hence
T\subseteq\rho^{-1}(\rho(T))
is contained in finitely many left
H^{\prime}
-cosets; thus
2
holds. Therefore
H^{\prime}
is a coarse stabiliser of
G\qa X
. By Proposition
3.5
,
H
is commensurable to the normal subgroup
H^{\prime}
.

(2)\iff(3)
: Theorem
4.8
implies that if some topological completion is discrete, then every topological completion is compact-by-discrete. Conversely, Remark
4.2
ensures if some topological completion is compact-by-discrete, then there exists a discrete topological completion.
∎

4.4.
Tame spaces

In this subsection we investigate tame spaces, which were introduced by Whyte (see
[
Why01b
,
Why10
]
). See also the related notion of
nontranslatable
spaces due to Kapovich–Kleiner–Leeb
[
KKL98
]
. Our interest in tameness is due to Theorem
4.20
, which states that cobounded quasi-actions on tame spaces necessarily have topological completions. We show in Proposition
4.21
that spaces which satisfy a rather weak form of coarse negative curvature are tame.

We first establish some notation that will be used in this subsection.
Given functions
f,g:X\rightarrow Y
between metric spaces, a subset
F\subseteq X
and
r\geq 0
, we write:

(1)

f\overset{F,r}{\sim}g
if
d(f(x),g(x))\leq r
for all
x\in F
;

(2)

f\overset{r}{\sim}g
if
f\overset{X,r}{\sim}g
;

(3)

f\overset{}{\sim}g
if
f\overset{r}{\sim}g
for some
r\geq 0
.

Definition 4.17
.

We say that a space
X
is
tame
if for any
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
, there exists a constant
B
such that for every
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
such that
f\sim\id_{X}
, then
f\overset{B}{\sim}\id_{X}
.

We emphasise that unless
X
is bounded, the constant
B
in the preceding definition depends on the constants
K
and
A
.
It is clear from the definition that tameness is a quasi-isometry invariant. The following example shows groups with infinite centre are not tame:

Example 4.18
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group equipped with a word metric
d_{S}
with respect to some finite generating set
S
. Let
a\in G
be central in
G
and let
L_{a}:G\rightarrow G
be left multiplication by
a
, which is an isometry of
(G,d_{S})
. Then for all
g\in G
,

d_{S}(L_{a}(g),g)=d_{S}(ag,g)=d_{S}(ga,g)=d_{S}(a,1_{G})=\lvert a\rvert_{S},

so that
L_{a}\sim\id_{G}
. It follows that if
G
has infinite centre, then it cannot be tame.

We require the following reformulation of tameness, which is easily seen to be equivalent to Definition
4.17
:

Lemma 4.19
.

A metric space
X
is tame if and only if for every
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
, there is a constant
B\geq 0
such that the following holds: if

f,g:X\rightarrow X
are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries such that
f\sim g
, then
f\overset{B}{\sim}g
.

Our interest in tame metric space comes from the following theorem, whose proof is delayed till later in this section.

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
X
be a tame proper quasi-geodesic metric space. Then every cobounded quasi-action on
X
has a topological completion.

It is shown in
[
Why10
]
that non-elementary hyperbolic groups are tame. We will shortly see that a much larger class of groups are tame, namely groups whose Morse boundary contains at least three points. We first explain how the Morse boundary is defined.

Let
X
be a proper geodesic metric space. Recall that a
(K,A)
-quasi-geodesic
is the image of a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometrically embedded interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. The
endpoints
of a quasi-geodesic are the images of the endpoints of
I
(if they exist). Given a function
\phi:[1,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, we say that a geodesic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow X
is
\phi
-Morse
if for any
(K,A)
-quasi-geodesic
\lambda
with endpoints on
\gamma
, then
\lambda\subseteq N_{\phi(K,A)}(\gamma)
. A geodesic is said to be
Morse
if it is
\phi
-Morse for some
\phi
, where
\phi
is called the
Morse gauge
.

The
Morse boundary
of
X
, denoted
\partial_{*}X
, is defined to be the set of Morse geodesic rays in
X
up to equivalence, where two rays are equivalent if they are at finite Hausdorff distance. Although the Morse boundary can be given the structure of a topological space, studying the Morse boundary as a set is sufficient for our purposes. The Morse boundary was first defined by Cordes for arbitrary proper geodesic metric spaces in
[
Cor17
]
, and agrees with the Gromov boundary on hyperbolic spaces and the contracting boundary on CAT(0) spaces. Quasi-isometries preserve Morse geodesic rays up to finite Hausdorff distance
[
Cor17
, Lemma 2.9]
, so a quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
induces a bijection
\partial_{*}f:\partial_{*}X\rightarrow\partial_{*}X
.

Although the Morse boundary can be empty, for a large class of groups it is not. In particular, it follows from work of Sisto that if a group is acylindrically hyperbolic, then its Morse boundary contains at least three points
[
Sis16
]
. Our interest in groups with nontrivial Morse boundary comes from the following:

Proposition 4.21
.

A cocompact proper geodesic metric space whose Morse boundary contains at least three points is tame.

Coupled with Theorem
4.20
, this implies the following:

Corollary 4.22
.

Let
X
be a cocompact proper geodesic metric space whose Morse boundary contains at least three points, e.g. an acylindrically hyperbolic group. Then every cobounded quasi-action on
X
has a topological completion.

To prove Proposition
4.21
, we make use of some lemmas from
[
Cor17
]
and
[
CCM19
]
. The following lemma ensures that quasi-isometries send Morse geodesics to Morse geodesics up to some uniform Hausdorff distance. This lemma is implicit in the proof of Theorem 3.7 in
[
CCM19
]
, and can easily be deduced by adapting the proof of Lemma 2.9 in
[
Cor17
]
.

Lemma 4.23
.

Given
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
and a Morse gauge
\phi
, there is a constant
B\geq 0
such that for any
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
and any
\phi
-Morse bi-infinite geodesic
\gamma\subseteq X
,
f(\gamma)
is Hausdorff distance at most
B
from a Morse geodesic.

Given a Morse gauge
\phi
, Charney et al. define
\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
to be the set of distinct triples
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
in
\partial_{*}X
such that for each
i\neq j
, every bi-infinite geodesic from
\xi_{i}
to
\xi_{j}
is
\phi
-Morse
[
CCM19
]
. As noted in
[
CCM19
]
, if
\partial_{*}X
contains three points, then
\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
is non-empty for some
\phi
large enough.

Let
T(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
denote an ideal triangle with vertices
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\in\partial_{*}X
.
It is well-known that for a triple of distinct points
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
in the boundary of a hyperbolic group, the centre of
T(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
is well-defined up to some uniform error. For some fixed Morse gauge
\phi
, the same is true for triples in
\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
:

Lemma 4.24

(
[
CCM19
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Given a Morse gauge
\phi
, there is a constant
r_{0}
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
, there is an
R=R(r,\phi)
such that for all
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
, the set

\displaystyle E_{r}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\coloneqq\left\{x\in X\mid\text{$x$ lies within $r$ of all three sides of some $T(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})$}\right\}

is non-empty and has diameter at most
R
.

We are now in position to apply these lemmas to prove Proposition
4.21
:

Proof of Proposition
4.21
.

Since
\partial_{*}X
has at least three points, say
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{3}
, we can choose a Morse gauge
\phi
such that
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})\in\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
. By Lemma
4.24
, we can choose
r_{1}
large enough so that
E_{r_{1}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
is non-empty and thus contains some
x_{0}\in X
. Since
X
is cocompact, we can pick
r_{2}
such that for every
x\in X
, there is an isometry
f_{x}:X\rightarrow X
such that
d(f_{x}(x_{0}),x)\leq r_{2}
. Let
\xi^{x}_{i}\coloneqq(\partial_{*}f_{x})(\xi_{i})
for
i=1,2,3
and
x\in X
. Since each
f_{x}
is an isometry,
(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})\in\partial_{*}X^{(3,\phi)}
and
x\in E_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
. Choose
B
as in Lemma
4.23
such every
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry sends a
\phi
-Morse bi-infinite geodesic to within Hausdorff distance
B
from another Morse geodesic. Let
f:X\rightarrow X
be a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry such that
f\overset{}{\sim}\id_{X}
. Let
x\in X
.
Since
x\in E_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
, it follows
x
has distance at most
r_{1}+r_{2}
from a point on a geodesic from
\xi_{1}^{x}
to
\xi_{2}^{x}
. Since
f
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry with
f\overset{}{\sim}\id_{X}
,
f(x)
has distance at most
r_{3}\coloneqq K(r_{1}+r_{2})+A+B
from a point on a (possibly different) Morse geodesic from
\xi_{1}^{x}
to
\xi_{2}^{x}
. Repeating this argument for the other sides of the triangle
T(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
allows us to deduce that
f(x)\in E_{r_{3}}(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
. By Lemma
4.24
, we can pick
R
large enough so that for every
x\in X
,
E_{r_{3}}(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
has diameter at most
R
. Since
x
and
f(x)
are both contained in
E_{r_{3}}(\xi_{1}^{x},\xi_{2}^{x},\xi_{3}^{x})
, it follows
d(f(x),x)\leq R
for all
x\in X
. This shows
X
is tame.
∎

We now begin our proof of Theorem
4.20
. Our main ingredient is unpublished work of Whyte
[
Why01b
]
, which we give a full account of. See also
[
Dym15
, Section 4]
for a published account of this work.
The notion of coarse convergence of functions plays a fundamental role in this subsection.

Definition 4.25
.

Let
X
be a metric space. For a sequence of functions
f_{i}:X\rightarrow X
and a function
f:X\to X
, we write
f_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}f
if for every
x\in X
, there is an
N_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
d(f_{i}(x),f(x))\leq r
for all
i\geq N_{x}
. We say
(f_{i})

coarsely converges
to
f:X\rightarrow X
, written
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
, if there exists an
r\geq 0
such that
f_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}f
. We say that
(f_{i})
is
coarsely convergent
if it coarsely converges to some
f:X\rightarrow X
, in which case we say that
f
is the
coarse limit
of
(f_{i})
.

In order to prove Theorem
4.20
, we first need to develop some preliminary lemmas concerning coarse convergence of quasi-isometries of tame spaces.
In what follows, it will simplify the argument if we assume that
X
is locally finite. The following remark ensures this can always be done:

Remark 4.26
.

Suppose
X
is a proper quasi-geodesic tame metric space and
G\qa X
is a quasi-action. Proposition
2.6
ensures that
X
is quasi-isometric to a locally finite tame metric space
Y
, and hence the quasi-action
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a quasi-action
G\qa Y
. Clearly
G\qa X
has a topological completion if and only if
G\qa Y
does, so it suffices to prove that every cobounded quasi-action on a locally finite tame quasi-geodesic metric space has a topological completion.

We first show coarse convergence of quasi-isometries of locally finite tame spaces satisfies the following uniformity property:

Lemma 4.27
.

Let
X
be a tame locally finite metric space and let
K\geq 1
and
A\geq 0
. There exists
B\geq 0
such that whenever
(f_{i})
is a coarsely convergent sequence of
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries of
X
, the following hold:

(1)

There exists a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
such that
f_{i}\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}f
.

(2)

If
f:X\rightarrow X
is any
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry such that
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
, then
f_{i}\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}f
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X
. Since
(f_{i})
is coarsely convergent,
(f_{i}(x_{0}))
is a bounded sequence. By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a subsequence
(f_{n_{i}})
and a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f:X\rightarrow X
such that for each
x\in X
,
f_{n_{i}}(x)=f(x)
for
i
sufficiently large. We claim
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
. Indeed, since
(f_{i})
is coarsely convergent, there exists some
g:X\rightarrow X
and
r\geq 0
such that
f_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. Thus for each
x\in X
,
d(f_{n_{i}}(x),g(x))\leq r
for
i
sufficiently large, and hence
d(f(x),g(x))\leq r
. It follows that
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
as required.

We now choose
B
as in Lemma
4.19
so that if
h,g:X\rightarrow X
are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries such that
h\overset{}{\sim}g
, then
h\overset{B}{\sim}g
. Suppose
g:X\rightarrow X
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry such that
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. We claim that
f_{i}\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. Indeed, there exists some
r
such that
f_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. Thus for all
x\in X
, there exists an
N_{x}
such that
d(f_{i}(x),g(x))\leq r
for all
i\geq N_{x}
. Let
I_{x}\coloneqq\{f_{i}(x)\mid i\geq N_{x}\}\subseteq N_{r}(g(x))
. Since
X
is locally finite,
I_{x}
is finite. An element of
y\in I_{x}
is
live
if
\{i\in\mathbb{N}\mid f_{i}(x)=y\}
is infinite. We can thus pick
M_{x}\geq N_{x}
such that every element of
\{f_{i}(x)\mid i\geq M_{x}\}
is live. For every live
y\in I_{x}
, we pick a subsequence
(f_{m_{i}})
such that
(f_{m_{i}}(x))
is eventually constant and equal to
y
. Applying Lemma
2.9
again we can pass to a further subsequence
(f_{k_{i}})
that converges pointwise to a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
h:X\rightarrow X
. Arguing as in the previous paragraph, we deduce
g\overset{}{\sim}h
.
As
h(x)=y
and both
g
and
h
are
(K,A)
quasi-isometries, it follows that
d(g(x),y)\leq B
. Thus
d(f_{i}(x),g(x))\leq B
for all
i\geq M_{x}
, and so
f_{i}\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}g
.
∎

In order to use coarse convergence to define a topology, we show that coarse convergence of sequences of quasi-isometries are ‘continuous’ with respect to multiplication and taking inverses.

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
X
be a metric space and suppose
f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
and
g_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
, where all maps are self quasi-isometries of
X
with uniform constants. Then

(1)

g_{i}\circ f_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g\circ f
;

(2)

\overline{f_{i}}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}\overline{f}
, where
\overline{f_{i}}
and
\overline{f}
are coarse inverses to
f_{i}
and
f
with uniform constants.

Proof.

We pick
K
and
A
large enough such that
\overline{f}\circ f\overset{A}{\sim}\id_{X}
,
\overline{f_{i}}\circ f_{i}\overset{A}{\sim}\id_{X}
for all
i
, and all maps under consideration are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries. Choose
r
large enough such that
f_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}f
and
g_{i}\overset{r}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. We claim
g_{i}\circ f_{i}\overset{R}{\rightsquigarrow}g\circ f
, where
R\coloneqq Kr+A+r
. Indeed, let
x\in X
and choose
N
large enough such that
d(f_{i}(x),x)\leq r
and
d(g_{i}f(x),gf(x))\leq r
for all
i\geq N
. Then

\displaystyle d(g_{i}f_{i}(x),gf(x))\leq d(g_{i}f_{i}(x),g_{i}f(x))+d(g_{i}f(x),gf(x))\leq Kr+A+r=R

for all
i\geq N
, so
g_{i}f_{i}\overset{R}{\rightsquigarrow}gf
as required.

We also claim
\overline{f_{i}}\overset{R^{\prime}}{\rightsquigarrow}\overline{f}
, where
R^{\prime}\coloneqq K(r+2A)+5A
. Indeed, let
y\in X
and choose
x\in X
such that
d(f(x),y)\leq A
. We pick
M
large enough so that
d(f_{i}(x),f(x))\leq r
for
i\geq M
. Thus

\displaystyle d(\overline{f_{i}}(y),\overline{f}(y))

\displaystyle\leq d\Big(\overline{f_{i}}(f(x)),\overline{f}(f(x))\Big)+2KA+2A

\displaystyle\leq d\Big(\overline{f_{i}}(f(x)),x\Big)+2KA+3A\leq d\Big(\overline{f_{i}}(f_{i}(x)),x\Big)+Kr+2KA+4A

\displaystyle\leq Kr+2KA+5A=R^{\prime}

for all
i\geq M
. Thus
\overline{f_{i}}\overset{R^{\prime}}{\rightsquigarrow}\overline{f}
.
∎

If
\phi:G\to X^{X}
is a quasi-action, we would like to define a topology on
G
so that
g_{i}\to g
if and only if
\phi(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. Doing so directly gives a topology that might be neither Hausdorff nor locally compact. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 4.29
.

A quasi-action
\phi:G\rightarrow X^{X}
is said to be:

(1)

coarsely faithful
if whenever
\phi(g)\overset{}{\sim}\id_{X}
, then
g=e_{G}
.

(2)

coarsely complete
if whenever
\phi(g_{i})
is a coarsely convergent sequence, there exists a
g\in G
such that
\phi(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}\phi(g)
.

Remark 4.30
.

We caution the reader that the term coarsely faithful is at variance with the usual rule of thumb that prefixing a mathematical property with the adjective ‘coarsely’ weakens the property in question. Indeed, an isometric action
\phi:G\rightarrow\Isom(X)
might be faithful but not coarsely faithful.

The following proposition allows us to quasi-conjugate a quasi-action to one that is coarsely faithful and coarsely complete:

Proposition 4.31
.

Let
X
be a locally finite quasi-geodesic tame metric space and let
\phi:G\rightarrow X^{X}
be a cobounded quasi-action. Then there exists a coarsely faithful and coarsely complete cobounded quasi-action
\hat{G}\qa X
and a homomorphism
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
such that the
\id_{X}
is a
\rho
-quasi-conjugacy from
G\qa X
to
\hat{G}\qa X
. Moreover, every
g\in\hat{G}
is the coarse limit of a sequence in
\rho(G)
.

To prove an action is coarsely complete, we use the following coarse analogue of Lemma
4.9
.

Lemma 4.32
.

Let
X
be a tame locally finite metric space.
Suppose that
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
and
(f_{i,j})_{i=1}^{\infty}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f_{j}
for each
j
, where all these maps are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries of
X
. Then there exist sequences
(n_{j})
and
(m_{j})
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
(f_{m_{j},n_{j}})_{j=1}^{\infty}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
.

Proof.

Since
X
is a locally finite metric space, hence is countable, we may enumerate
X
as
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,\}\subseteq X
. Choose
B
as in Lemma
4.27
. Since
(f_{j})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose
n_{j}
such that
d(f(x_{l}),f_{k}(x_{l}))\leq B
for each
l\leq j
and
k\geq n_{j}
. Similarly, for each
j
we can choose
m_{j}
such that
d(f_{n_{j}}(x_{l}),f_{k,n_{j}}(x_{l}))\leq B
for each
l\leq j
and
k\geq m_{j}
. We claim
(f_{m_{j},n_{j}})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
.
Indeed, let
x\in X
so that
x=x_{l}
for some
l
. Therefore

\displaystyle d(f_{m_{j},n_{j}}(x),f(x))\leq d(f_{m_{j},n_{j}}(x),f_{n_{j}}(x))+d(f_{n_{j}}(x),f(x))\leq 2B

for all
j\geq l
, so that
(f_{m_{j},n_{j}})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}f
as required.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.31
.

Let
\hat{G}
be the subset of
\QI(X)
consisting of equivalence classes of quasi-isometries that arise as coarse limits of coarsely convergent sequences in
\{\phi(g)\mid g\in G\}
, and let
\rho:G\rightarrow\hat{G}
be the map
g\mapsto[\phi(g)]
. It follows from Lemma
4.28
that
\hat{G}
is a subgroup of
\QI(X)
. By Lemma
4.27
there exists constants
K
and
A
such that each
g\in\hat{G}
can be represented by a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
f_{g}:X\rightarrow X
. Pick
B\geq A
as in Lemma
4.19
such that if
f,g:X\to X
are
(K,A)
-quasi-isometries such that
f\overset{}{\sim}g
, then
f\overset{B}{\sim}g
. Since
f_{g}\circ f_{h}\overset{B}{\sim}f_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
, the map
\hat{\phi}:\hat{G}\rightarrow X^{X}
given by
g\mapsto f_{g}
is a
(K,B)
-quasi-action
\hat{G}\qa X
. Since
\phi(g)\overset{B}{\sim}\hat{\phi}(\rho(g))
for all
g\in G
,
\id_{X}
is a
\rho
-quasi-conjugacy.

As
\hat{G}
is a subgroup of
\QI(X)
and
g=[\hat{\phi}(g)]=[f_{g}]
, it is clear
\hat{\phi}
is coarsely faithful. Moreover, Lemma
4.32
ensures the coarse limit of a coarsely convergent sequence in
\{\hat{\phi}(g)\mid g\in\hat{G}\}
is a coarse limit of a coarsely convergent sequence in
\{\phi(g)\mid g\in G\}
, implying
\hat{\phi}
is coarsely complete.
∎

We now prove the following:

Proposition 4.33
.

Let
X
be a locally finite quasi-geodesic metric space and let
\phi:G\to X^{X}
be a cobounded quasi-action that is coarsely faithful and coarsely complete. Then
G
can be given the structure of a second-countable locally compact compactly generated topological group such that
(g_{i})
converges to
g
if and only if
\phi(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}\phi(g)
. Moreover,
G
has no nontrivial compact normal subgroups and the identity map
G\to G
is a topological completion of
\phi
.

Remark 4.34
.

Since a first countable topological space is completely determined by its convergent sequences, any topology on
G
satisfying the properties in Proposition
4.33
is unique.

For the remainder of this section, which is devoted to proving Proposition
4.33
, we fix
\phi:G\to X^{X}
as in the statement of Proposition
4.33
. We also fix
K
and
A
such that
\phi
is a
(K,A)
-quasi-action, and a constant
B\geq 0
as in Lemma
4.27
.
We define a topology on
G
and show it satisfies the required properties.
For ease of notation, when unambiguous we will write
g\overset{}{\sim}g^{\prime}
to denote
\phi(g)\overset{}{\sim}\phi(g^{\prime})
,
g_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
to denote
\phi(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}\phi(g)
, etc. We recall
g\cdot x
can be used to denote
\phi(g)(x)
.

Proposition 4.35
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be the set of all subsets
U\subseteq G
that satisfy the following property:
if
g\in U
and
(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
, there exists an
N
such that
g_{i}\in U
for all
i\geq N
. Then
\mathcal{U}
form the open sets of a topology on
G
called the
topology of coarse convergence
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\emptyset,G\in\mathcal{U}
. Suppose
(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
and
g\in\cup_{j\in J}U_{j}
, where each
U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
. Then
g\in U_{j}
for some
j
, so there exists an
N
such that
g_{i}\in U_{j}\subseteq\cup_{j\in J}U_{j}
for all
i\geq N
. Therefore
\cup_{j\in J}U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
.
Now suppose
(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
and
g\in U_{1}\cap\dots\cap U_{n}
, where each
U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
. Since
g\in U_{j}
and
U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists an
N_{j}
such that
g_{i}\in U_{j}
for all
i\geq N_{j}
. Thus
g_{i}\in U_{1}\cap\dots\cap U_{n}
for all
i\geq\max(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})
and so
U_{1}\cap\dots\cap U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
.
∎

We now define a basis of the topology of coarse convergence.

Lemma 4.36
.

For each
h\in G
and finite
F\subseteq X
, we define

U_{h,F}\coloneqq\{g\in G\mid\text{if $(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g$, $\exists N$ such that $g_{i}\overset{F,B}{\sim}h$ for all $i\geq N$}\}.

Then
h\in U_{h,F}
for all
h\in G
and
F\subseteq X
, and
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{U_{h,F}\mid h\in G,F\subseteq G\text{ is finite}\}
is a countable basis of open sets in
G
.

Proof.

We first show each
U_{h,F}
is open. Suppose for contradiction that
U_{h,F}
is not open. Then there exists some
g\in U_{h,F}
and a sequence
(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
such that
g_{i}\notin U_{h,F}
for all
i
.
Since
g_{i}\notin U_{h,F}
, there is a sequence
(g^{i}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g_{i}
such that
g^{i}_{j}\overset{F,B}{\not\sim}h
for all
j
. By Lemma
4.32
, there is a sequence
(k_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
such that
k_{i}\overset{F,B}{\not\sim}h
for all
i
, contradicting our assumption that
g\in U_{h,F}
. Thus
U_{h,F}
is open.

We now show
h\in U_{h,F}
for every finite
F\subseteq G
. Indeed, suppose
(h_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}h
. It follows from Lemma
4.27
and our choice of
B
that
(h_{i})\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}h
, so there exists an
N
such that
h_{i}\overset{F,B}{\sim}h
for all
i\geq N
. Thus
h\in U_{h,F}
.

We now show
\mathcal{B}
is a basis of
G
. Let
U\subseteq G
be open and let
h\in U
. We claim there is some finite
F_{h}\subseteq G
such that
U_{h,F_{h}}\subseteq U
. It then follows that
U=\cup_{h\in U}U_{h,F_{h}}
, so that
\mathcal{B}
is indeed a basis of
G
. To prove the claim, we assume for contradiction that
U_{h,F}\not\subseteq U
for any finite
F\subseteq G
. Since
X
is locally finite, we enumerate
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots\}
and set
X_{i}\coloneqq\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i}\}
for each
i
. For each
i
, we can thus choose
g_{i}\in U_{h,X_{i}}\setminus U
. By considering the constant sequence
(g_{i})_{j=1}^{\infty}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g_{i}
, it follows that
g_{i}\overset{X_{i},B}{\sim}h
. Thus
(g_{i})\overset{B}{\rightsquigarrow}g
but
g_{i}\notin U
for all
i
, contradicting our assumption that
U
is open.

All that remains is to show
\mathcal{B}

is countable. We first note that
U_{h,F}=U_{h^{\prime},F^{\prime}}
if
h|_{F}=h^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}
. Thus even though
G
might be uncountable, since
X
is countable (as it is locally finite) there are only countably many functions from finite subsets of
X
to
X
, and hence countably many
U_{h,F}
for
h\in G
and finite
F\subseteq X
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.33
.

It follows from Proposition
4.35
and Lemma
4.36
that when equipped with the topology of coarse convergence,
G
is a second-countable topological space and that
(g_{i})\to g
if and only if
(\phi(g_{i}))\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
. Moreover, Lemma
4.28
implies multiplication and inverses are continuous. Since topological groups are assumed to be Hausdorff, we also verify
G
is Hausdorff.
Indeed, as
G\qa X
is coarsely faithful,
g\not\overset{}{\sim}h
for
g\neq h\in G
, so there exists some
x\in X
such that
d(g\cdot x,h\cdot x)>2B
. It follows that
U_{g,\{x\}}
and
U_{h,\{x\}}
are disjoint neighbourhoods of
g
and
h
, and so
G
is Hausdorff. Thus
G
is a second-countable topological group.

As
G
is a Hausdorff and second-countable topological group, the Birkhoff–Kakutani theorem implies
G
is metrisable. Thus a subspace of
G
is compact if and only if it is sequentially compact. To show local compactness of
G
, we show every
g\in G
has a sequentially compact neighbourhood
V_{g}
. Fix
x_{0}\in X
and
g\in G
and let
V_{g}
be the closure of
U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}
in
G
. We claim that
V_{g}
is sequentially compact.

Indeed,
h\in V_{g}
if and only if there is a sequence
(h_{i})
in
U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}
such that
h_{i}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}h
. For every
h_{i}\in U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}
,
d(g\cdot x_{0},h_{i}\cdot x_{0})\leq B
. Thus Lemma
4.27
implies that for every
h\in V_{g}
,
d(h\cdot x_{0},g\cdot x_{0})\leq 2B
. It thus follows from Lemma
2.9
that every sequence
(h_{i})
in
V_{g}
has a coarsely convergent subsequence
(h_{n_{i}})
. Since
G\qa X
is coarsely complete, there exists some
h\in G
such that
h_{n_{i}}\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}h
. By Lemma
4.32
,
h
is the coarse limit of some sequence in
U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}
, and hence
h\in V_{g}
. Thus for each
g\in G
,
V_{g}
is a sequentially compact set containing an open neighbourhood
U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}
of
g
. Therefore
G
is locally compact.

To show the identity map
\id_{G}
is a topological completion of
G\qa X
, we need only show
T\subseteq G
has compact closure if and only if it is bounded. Suppose
T\subseteq G
has compact closure and fix
x_{0}\in X
. Since
\{U_{g,\{x_{0}\}}\mid g\in\overline{T}\}
is an open cover of
\overline{T}
, there are finitely many
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in\overline{T}
such that
T\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}U_{g_{i},\{x_{0}\}}
. It follows that
\{g\cdot x_{0}\mid g\in T\}\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}N_{B}({g_{i}\cdot x_{0}})
, and so
T
is bounded. Conversely, suppose that
T
is bounded, so that for some
x_{0}\in X
,
\{g\cdot x_{0}\mid g\in T\}
is bounded. It follows from Lemma
2.9
and the coarse completeness of
G\qa X
that any infinite sequence
(g_{i})
in
T
has a subsequence
(g_{n_{i}})
that coarsely converges to some
g\in G
. Since
(g_{i})\overset{}{\rightsquigarrow}g
,
(g_{i})\to g
and so
g\in\overline{T}
; therefore
\overline{T}
is compact.
The final claim that
G
has no nontrivial compact normal subgroups follows easily from Proposition
4.7
and the hypothesis that
G
is coarsely faithful.
∎

We can now use the topology of coarse convergence to prove Theorem
4.20
:

Proof of Theorem
4.20
.

Let
X
be a proper quasi-geodesic tame metric space and let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action. By Proposition
2.6
(see also Remark
4.26
) there is a locally finite metric space
Y
such that
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to
G\qa Y
. Combining Propositions
4.31
and
4.33
, we deduce
G\qa Y
has a topological completion
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
.
∎

4.5.
Quasi-morphisms and quasi-actions on
\mathbb{R}

In this section, we give many examples of quasi-actions on
\mathbb{R}
that do
not
have topological completions. Whilst not directly used or needed elsewhere in this article, these examples serve to contextualise our main results by demonstrating that, despite the plethora of spaces
X
such that every cobounded quasi-action on
X
does have a topological completion (e.g. Theorem
4.20
and Theorem
H
), quasi-actions on more general spaces need not have topological completions. To do this, we make use of the well-known notion of a quasi-morphism.

A
quasi-morphism
is a map
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{g,h\in G}\lvert f(g)+f(h)-f(gh)\rvert\leq D

for some
D
called the
defect
of
f
. A map
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
bounded
if
\sup_{g\in G}\lvert f(g)\rvert<\infty
and
unbounded
otherwise. Two quasi-morphisms
f,f^{\prime}:G\to\mathbb{R}
are said to be
equivalent
if their difference is bounded. A quasi-morphism is said to be
trivial
if it is equivalent to a homomorphism. A quasi-morphism
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
homogeneous
if
f(g^{n})=nf(g)
for all
g\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Every quasi-morphism is equivalent to a homogeneous quasi-morphism, which is necessarily unique; see for instance
[
Fri17
, Proposition 2.10]
.

A quasi-morphism
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
induces a quasi-action
\phi_{f}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}
via the formula
\phi_{f}(g)(x)=f(g)+x
. We call
\phi_{f}
the
quasi-action induced by
f
. Since we are primarily interested in cobounded quasi-actions, we first determine when the induced quasi-action is cobounded:

Lemma 4.37
.

Let
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
be a quasi-morphism. The induced quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
is cobounded if and only if
f
is unbounded.

Proof.

If
f
is bounded, then every quasi-orbit of the induced quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
is bounded, so the quasi-action is not cobounded. Conversely, suppose
f
is unbounded. As noted above, there is a homogeneous quasi-morphism
\hat{f}:G\to\mathbb{R}
and a constant
B
such that
\lvert\hat{f}(g)-f(g)\rvert\leq B
for all
g\in G
. Since
f
is unbounded,
\hat{f}
is not identically zero and so there is some
g\in G
such that
\hat{f}(g)\neq 0
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
n_{x}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\hat{f}(g)}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}
; thus,
\lvert x-\hat{f}(g)n_{x}\rvert\leq\lvert\hat{f}(g)\rvert
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
It follows that

\lvert f(g^{n_{x}})-x\rvert\leq\lvert\hat{f}(g^{n_{x}})-x\rvert+B=\lvert n_{x}\hat{f}(g)-x\rvert+B\leq\lvert\hat{f}(g)\rvert+B

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, which implies the induced quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
is
(\lvert\hat{f}(g)\rvert+B)
-cobounded.
∎

We prove the following:

Theorem 4.38
.

Let
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
be an unbounded quasi-morphism. Then the induced cobounded quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
has a topological completion if and only if
f
is trivial.

Proof.

First suppose that
f
is trivial. Then there is a homomorphism
f^{\prime}:G\to\mathbb{R}
and a constant
B
such that
\lvert f^{\prime}(g)-f(g)\rvert\leq B
. The quasi-action induced by
f^{\prime}
is an isometric action on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus the identity map is a
(1,B)
-quasi-conjugacy from the quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
induced by
f
to the isometric action
G\curvearrowright\mathbb{R}
induced by
f^{\prime}
. It follows from Proposition
4.5
that the quasi-action
G\qa\mathbb{R}
induced by
f
has a topological completion.

Conversely, suppose the quasi-action
\phi_{f}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{R}}
has a topological completion
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
. Let
d
be a geodesically adapted metric on
\hat{G}
, which exists by Proposition
4.3
. Proposition
4.3
also ensures
\phi_{f}
is
\rho
-quasi-conjugate to the regular representation
\hat{G}\curvearrowright(\hat{G},d)
. In particular
(\hat{G},d)
is two-ended and so a result of Abels, refined by Cornulier
[
Cor18
, Corollary 19.39]
, states that
\hat{G}
acts geometrically on
\mathbb{R}
. Corollary
4.4
thus implies
\phi_{f}
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
\rho:G\to\Isom(\mathbb{R})
. Since the original quasi-action
\phi_{f}
consists of translations,
\im(\rho)
consists of translations and so there is a homomorphism
\psi:G\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\rho(g)(x)=\psi(g)+x
.

As a consequence of the preceding argument, there exist constants
K\geq 1
,
A\geq 0
and a
(K,A)
-quasi-isometry
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\lvert h(\phi_{f}(g)(x))-\rho(g)(h(x))\rvert\leq A

for all
g\in G
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. There is a constant
B
and a homogeneous quasi-morphism
\hat{f}:G\to\mathbb{R}
of defect
B
such that
\lvert\hat{f}(g)-f(g)\rvert\leq B
for all
g\in G
. Therefore

\lvert h(\hat{f}(g))-\psi(g)\rvert\leq\lvert h(\phi_{f}(g)(0))-\rho(g)(0)\rvert+KB+A\leq KB+2A\eqqcolon C

for all
g\in G
. Thus for all
g,k\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle n\lvert\hat{f}(gk)-\hat{f}(g)-\hat{f}(k)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\hat{f}((gk)^{n})-\hat{f}(g^{n})-\hat{f}(k^{n})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\hat{f}((gk)^{n})-\hat{f}(g^{n}k^{n})\rvert+B

\displaystyle\leq K\lvert h(\hat{f}((gk)^{n}))-h(\hat{f}(g^{n}k^{n}))\rvert+KA+B

\displaystyle\leq K\lvert\psi((gk)^{n})-\psi(g^{n}k^{n})\rvert+2KC+KA+B

\displaystyle=2KC+KA+B

as
\hat{f}
has defect
B
and
\psi
is a homomorphism. Since this holds for every
n
, it follows
\hat{f}
is a homomorphism. As
\hat{f}
is equivalent to
f
, we deduce
f
is trivial.
∎

We conclude by noting that there are many examples of nontrivial quasi-morphisms. For instance, Brooks gave examples of non-trivial quasi-morphisms on the free group of rank two
[
Bro81
]
; see also
[
EF97
]
. Coupled with Theorem
4.38
, this implies:

Corollary 4.39
.

There exist many cobounded quasi-actions on
\mathbb{R}
with no topological completion.

5.
Quasi-actions on hyperbolic spaces

In this section we prove Theorem
5.6
, classifying cobounded quasi-actions on proper hyperbolic metric spaces. We make use of structure theorems for locally compact hyperbolic groups due Caprace–Cornulier–Monod–Tessera. We combine these with results from Section
4
to deduce Theorem
5.6
.

For definiteness, we say that a metric space
X
is
hyperbolic
if it is a geodesic metric space and there is a
\delta\geq 0
such that for any triple of geodesic segments
[x,y]
,
[y,z]
and
[x,z]
in
X
,
[x,y]\subseteq N_{\delta}([y,z]\cup[x,z])
. This definition is a quasi-isometry invariant in the sense that if a geodesic metric space is quasi-isometric to a hyperbolic space, then it is also hyperbolic.

A locally compact group
G
is said to be
hyperbolic
if it is admits a compact generating set such that the associated Cayley graph is hyperbolic. Hyperbolicity of
G
is independent of the choice of compact generating set. Henceforth, locally compact hyperbolic groups are always equipped with such a metric. Similarly, if
G
is finitely generated relative to
H
, we say
G/H
is hyperbolic if some (hence every) relative Cayley graph
\Gamma_{G,H}
is hyperbolic.
If
G
is a locally compact hyperbolic group, it has a well-defined boundary
\partial G
on which
G
acts by homeomorphisms. We remark that although the Cayley graph of
G
is not proper (unless
G
is discrete),
G
acts geometrically on some proper geodesic hyperbolic metric space
[
CCMT15
, Corollary 2.6]
.

Definition 5.1
.

A hyperbolic metric space is said to be
non-elementary
if its boundary has at least three points. In particular, if
G
is a locally compact hyperbolic group, then
G
is non-elementary if and only if
\partial G
has at least three points.

We now recall some structural results about hyperbolic locally compact groups. Firstly, we can characterise when such groups are amenable:

Lemma 5.2

(
[
CCMT15
, Proposition 3.1 and Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic locally compact group. If
G
is elementary, it is amenable. If
G
is non-elementary, then it is amenable if and only if it fixes a point of
\partial G
. Moreover, a non-elementary amenable hyperbolic group is not unimodular.

We will make use of the following structural result concerning compact normal subgroups of hyperbolic groups. Recall that the
amenable radical
of a locally compact group is the maximal amenable normal subgroup.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
CCMT15
, Lemmas 3.6, 5.1 and 5.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a non-elementary locally compact hyperbolic group. Then
G
has a unique maximal compact normal subgroup
K\vartriangleleft G
which is precisely the kernel of the action of
G
on
\partial G
. Moreover, if
G
is non-amenable, then
K
is the amenable radical of
G
.

To understand non-amenable locally compact hyperbolic groups, we require the following result of Caprace et al.
[
CCMT15
, Proposition 5.10]
, which is itself a refinement of a result of Mineyev–Monod–Shalom
[
MMS04
]
. The proof of Theorem
5.4
makes use of the solution to Hilbert’s fifth problem due to Gleason and Yamabe.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G
be a non-amenable locally compact hyperbolic group and let
K\vartriangleleft G
be the maximal compact normal subgroup. Then either:

(1)

G/K
is a virtually connected centre-free simple rank one Lie group, in which case
G
acts geometrically on a rank one irreducible symmetric space of non-compact type;

(2)

G/K
is totally disconnected, in which case
G
acts geometrically on a locally finite graph.

Millefeuille spaces
were introduced by Caprace et al. in order to characterise amenable hyperbolic groups
[
CCMT15
]
. We briefly summarise their construction, referring the reader to
[
CCMT15
]
and
[
Cor18
]
for additional details. Let
X
be a homogeneous simply connected negatively curved
Riemannian manifold. Then
X
is isometric to a solvable Lie group
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
equipped with a left-invariant metric, where
N
is a nilpotent Lie group and
\alpha:N\rightarrow N
is a contracting automorphism of
N
. Projecting to the
\mathbb{R}
factor defines a Busemann function
b:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Fix
k\geq 1
, and let
T_{k+1}
be a
(k+1)
-regular tree with a surjective Busemann function
b^{\prime}:T_{k+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
taking integer values on vertices. The associated millefeuille space is the topological space

X[k]=\{(x,y)\mid b(x)=b^{\prime}(y)\},

which can naturally be equipped with a
\CAT(-1)
metric. We note that
X[k]
depends (up to isometry) only on
X
and
k
, and not the Busemann functions
b
and
b^{\prime}
. The millefeuille space
X[k]
is said to be
pure
if neither
X
nor
T_{k+1}
is a line.
The following theorem is the main result of
[
CCMT15
]
. See also a summary of the work described by Cornulier
[
Cor18
, §19.2.5]
.

Theorem 5.5

(
[
CCMT15
, Theorem 7.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a non-elementary amenable locally compact hyperbolic group. Then either:

(1)

G
acts geometrically on a simply connected homogeneous negatively curved
Riemannian manifold;

(2)

G
acts geometrically on a pure millefeuille space;

(3)

G
acts geometrically on a regular tree of finite valency greater than two.

We can now use Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
to prove:

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
X
be a proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If

is a cobounded quasi-action, then one of the following holds:

(1)

(connected case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a homogeneous
simply-connected negatively curved manifold of dimension at least two.

(2)

(mixed case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a pure millefeuille space.

(3)

(totally-disconnected case)

G\qa X
is discretisable.

Proof.

Let
G\qa X
be a cobounded quasi-action on a proper non-elementary hyperbolic space. By Corollary
4.22
,
G\qa X
has a topological completion
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\hat{G}
is a compactly generated locally compact group that is quasi-isometric to

, hence
\hat{G}
is a non-elementary locally compact hyperbolic group. By Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
,
\hat{G}
acts geometrically on

, where

is either a homogeneous negatively curved manifold, a pure millefeuille space, or a locally finite graph. Corollary

thus implies
G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to
G\curvearrowright Y
.
∎

As mentioned in the introduction, we can sharpen the conclusions of Theorem
5.6
under an additional hypothesis determining whether
G\qa X
extends to an action

that either does or does not fix a point of
\partial X
. We thus have the following two corollaries:

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
X
be a proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action that does not fix a point of
\partial X
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

(connected case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to a cocompact isometric action on a rank one symmetric space of non-compact type.

(2)

(totally-disconnected case)

G\qa X
is discretisable.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
X
be a proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. If
G\qa X
is a cobounded quasi-action that fixes a point of
\partial X
, then one of the following holds:

(1)

(continuous case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
G\curvearrowright Y
, where
Y
is a simply connected homogeneous negatively curved
Riemannian manifold.

(2)

(mixed case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
G\curvearrowright Y
, where
Y
is a pure millefeuille space.

(3)

(discrete case)

G\qa X
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
G\curvearrowright Y
, where
Y
is a regular tree of finite valency greater than two.

Proofs of Corollaries
5.7
and
5.8
.

As in the proof of Theorem
5.6
, the quasi-action
G\qa X
has a topological completion
\rho:G\to\hat{G}
. We claim
\hat{G}
is amenable if and only if the quasi-action
G\qa X
fixes a point of
\partial X
. Indeed, Proposition
4.3
ensures there is a
\rho
-quasi-conjugacy

. This induces a homeomorphism

that conjugates the
G
action on
\partial X
to a
\rho(G)
action on
\partial\hat{G}
. Since
\hat{G}
acts continuously on
\partial\hat{G}
(see for instance
[
Fur01
, Lemma 3.4]
) and
\rho(G)\leq\hat{G}
is dense in

\rho(G)
fixes a point of

if and only if
\hat{G}
does. The claim now follows from Lemma

.
We now proceed as in the proof of Theorem

, noting that when

does not fix a point of
\partial X
we can apply Theorem
5.4
, and when
G
does fix a point of
\partial X
we can apply Theorem
5.5
.
∎

We will use the following property of pure millefeuille spaces:

Proposition 5.9

(
[
Cor18
, Corollary 19.22]
)
.

A pure millefeuille space is not quasi-isometric to any vertex-transitive locally finite graph. In particular, a pure millefeuille space is not quasi-isometric to a finitely generated group.

We recall that a metric space
X
is discretisable if every cobounded quasi-action on
X
is discretisable. We can thus use Theorem
5.6
to deduce:

Corollary 5.10
.

A finitely generated hyperbolic group
\Gamma
is discretisable if and only if it is not quasi-isometric to a simply connected negatively curved homogeneous space. In particular, if
\partial\Gamma
is not homeomorphic to a sphere (of any dimension), then it is discretisable.

The following question, raised by Cornulier
[
Cor18
, Conjecture 19.99]
, is relevant to refining our description of non-discretisable finitely generated hyperbolic groups: Do there exist negatively curved homogeneous spaces other than symmetric spaces that are quasi-isometric to finitely generated groups? Since the isometry groups of such manifolds are not unimodular, they cannot contain lattices. In particular, a finitely generated group cannot act geometrically on a negatively curved homogeneous space other than a symmetric space.
A positive answer to this conjecture is implied by the Pointed Sphere Conjecture
[
Cor18
, Corollary 19.104]
, which asserts that if
X
is a negatively curved homogeneous spaces other than a symmetric space, there is a distinguished point in
\partial X
that is fixed by every self quasi-isometry of

. Xie, Carrasco and others have proven special cases of this conjecture
[
Xie14
]
,
[
Pia17
]
. We refer to
[
Cor18
]
for more details.

6.
Products of hyperbolic groups

In this section we study the class
\mathcal{C}
of finitely generated groups quasi-isometric to products of non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric spaces. Theorem
6.1
, the key result of this section, shows any group in
\mathcal{C}
acts geometrically on a product of symmetric spaces and locally finite graphs. This implies Corollary
6.6
, which says a group in
\mathcal{C}
is virtually isomorphic to a lattice in a product of locally compact hyperbolic groups with trivial amenable radical. In Theorem
6.10
, we show that in non-exceptional cases, groups in
\mathcal{C}
contain commensurated subgroups, and split as direct products precisely when these commensurated subgroups are weakly separable.
We prove Theorems
6.11
and
6.15
, which show that under the presence of additional algebraic hypotheses, namely residual finiteness and linearity, groups in
\mathcal{C}
are either arithmetic lattices in semisimple algebraic groups, or split as direct products of hyperbolic groups. Theorem
6.26
proves that
\CAT(0)
groups in
\mathcal{C}
are boundary rigid.

6.1.
A structure theorem for groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces

An isometric action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
is said to
preserve the product structure
if every isometry splits as a product of isometries, possibly permuting the factors.
If
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
is an isometric action preserving the product structure, we let
\Gamma^{*}\leq\Gamma
denote the maximal finite index subgroup that does not permute factors.
We note that
\Gamma^{*}
acts isometrically on each
Y_{i}
and hence induces an action on the boundary
\partial Y_{i}
by homeomorphisms.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper geodesic non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. Then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, preserving the product structure, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite graph.
Moreover,
\Gamma^{*}
does not fix a point of
\partial Y_{i}
for any
i
.

We first prove a weaker version of Theorem
6.1
:

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper geodesic non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. Then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, preserving the product structure, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a negatively curved homogeneous space, a pure millefeuille space, or a locally finite graph. Moreover, if
\Gamma^{*}
fixes a point of
\partial Y_{i}
for some locally finite graph
Y_{i}
, then
Y_{i}
is a regular tree of finite valency greater than two.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, it has a cobounded quasi-action on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. Noting each
X_{i}
is of coarse type I, we can combine Proposition
2.41
and Theorem
5.6
to deduce
\Gamma\qa\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is quasi-conjugate to an isometric action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, where each
Y_{i}
is one of the three desired types of space.
Proposition
2.41
and Theorem
5.5
ensure that if the subgroup
\Gamma^{*}
fixes a point of some
\partial Y_{i}
, then
Y_{i}
can be assumed to be either a homogeneous space, a pure millefeuille space, or a regular tree of finite valency greater than two. Without loss of generality, we may assume that if

fixes a point of some

and

is a graph, then

is such a tree.
∎

Theorem

will follow from Lemma

and the following proposition, which is a generalisation of the fact that a finitely generated non-elementary hyperbolic group cannot fix a point of its boundary.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group acting geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
such that the conclusions of Lemma

are satisfied. Then
\Gamma^{*}
does not fix a point of
\partial Y_{i}
for any
i
.

A
\CAT(0)
space

admitting an isometric action of

is said to be

-minimal
if there is no non-empty proper

-invariant closed convex subset of

. A proper

if it admits no isometric splitting as

for

.
To prove Proposition

, we make use of the following result of Caprace–Monod:

Theorem 6.4

(
[
CM09a
, Theorems 3.11 and 3.14]
)
.

Suppose a finitely generated group acts geometrically on a proper
\CAT(0)
space
X
.
Then
X
contains a canonical closed convex non-empty
\Isom(X)
-invariant

\Isom(X)

such that
\Isom(X^{\prime})
fixes no point of

.

If
\Gamma

\Gamma

.

Throughout this section, we use the following lemma about groups acting on products of spaces such that at least one factor is a locally finite graph. It is a geometric analogue of Lemma

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group acting cocompactly by isometries on a proper metric space
X=X_{1}\times X_{2}
, preserving the product structure and without permuting factors. Suppose
X_{2}
is a locally finite graph and
\Gamma
acts on
X_{2}
by graph automorphisms. If
v
is a vertex of
X_{2}
, then the subgroup
\Gamma_{1}\leq\Gamma
stabilising
X_{1}\times\{v\}
acts cocompactly on
X_{1}\times\{v\}
and is commensurated in
\Gamma
.
In particular, if
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
X
, then
\Gamma_{1}
acts geometrically on
X_{1}\times\{v\}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X_{1}
. Since
\Gamma
acts cocompactly on
X_{1}\times X_{2}
, there is an

such that the
\Gamma
-orbits of
K\coloneqq N_{r}(x_{0},v)
cover

. For each

, there is a
g_{x}\in\Gamma
such that
g_{x}(x,v)\in K
, and so
d_{X_{1}}(g_{x}x,x_{0}),d_{X_{2}}(g_{x}v,v)\leq r
. As
X_{2}
is a locally finite graph, the set
T=\{g_{x}v\mid x\in X_{1}\}
is finite, so we may pick

such that

. For each

, we have

for some

, and so
g_{x}^{-1}g_{x_{i}}\in\Gamma_{1}
. Since

d(g_{x}^{-1}g_{x_{i}}x_{i},x)=d(g_{x_{i}}x_{i},g_{x}x)\leq d(g_{x_{i}}x_{i},x_{0})+d(x_{0},g_{x}x)\leq 2r,

we deduce

is covered by

projects to a cocompact action on

and

is the stabiliser of a vertex, it follows

is commensurated.

We are now ready to prove Proposition

Proof of Proposition

.

By rearranging the factors of
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, we may assume that

if and only if the following both hold:

•

does not fix a point of

.

Applying Lemma

has a subgroup

is a CAT(0) space with isometry group acting cocompactly. Since

is geodesically complete, it is

. Since the de Rham decomposition of

is essentially unique — see

has no Euclidean de Rham factor. Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

We first note that
\Gamma

We also have the following algebraic consequence of Theorem

.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric space. There is a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{*}\leq\Gamma
and a finite normal subgroup

such that
\Gamma^{*}/F
has trivial amenable radical and is a uniform lattice in
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
, whose projection to each factor is dense. Moreover, each

is a hyperbolic locally compact group with trivial amenable radical that is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
, and is either a virtually connected simple rank-one Lie group or is totally disconnected.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
,
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite graph. Moreover, the finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{*}
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, preserving the product structure, not permuting the factors, and not fixing a point of
\partial Y_{i}
for any

. This geometric action induces a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma^{*}\to\Isom(Y_{1})\times\dots\times\Isom(Y_{n})
. Since this action is geometric,
\ker(\rho)
is finite and
\rho(\Gamma^{*})
is a uniform lattice.
Letting
H_{i}
be the closure of the projection of
\rho(\Gamma^{*})
to
\Isom(Y_{i})
, we have a map
\phi:\Gamma^{*}\rightarrow H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{n}
with finite kernel and whose image is a uniform lattice with dense projections to each factor.

Each

is a closed cocompact subgroup of
\Isom(Y_{i})
, hence is a non-elementary locally compact hyperbolic group that is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
. Since
\Gamma^{*}
does not fix a point on the boundary of

, Lemma

ensures

is non-amenable. Lemma

thus implies

has compact amenable radical

and so the quotient

has trivial amenable radical and is also a locally compact hyperbolic group quasi-isometric to

. By Theorem

,

is either a simple rank one Lie group or is totally disconnected. Composing

with the quotient map in each factor gives a map

, with finite kernel and whose image is a uniform lattice.
∎

6.2.
Algebraic splitting for groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces

We use Corollary
6.6
to deduce further structural results about groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic groups. We first establish some general facts about products of hyperbolic groups with trivial amenable radical.

Lemma 6.7
.

Suppose
G
is a locally compact hyperbolic group with trivial amenable radical. If
H\leq G
is nontrivial and either normal or cocompact (but not necessarily closed), then its centraliser
C_{G}(H)
is trivial.

Proof.

By
[
CCMT15
, Corollary 2.6]
,
G
acts geometrically on a proper geodesic hyperbolic metric space
X
. By Corollary
4.4
, there is a quasi-conjugacy
G\to X
that induces a homeomorphism
\partial G\cong\partial X
conjugating the action of
G
on
\partial G
to the action of
G
on
\partial X
. We assume
G
is nontrivial otherwise there is nothing to prove. Thus
G
is non-amenable, and Lemma
5.2
ensures
X
is non-elementary and
G
does not fix a point of
\partial X
.
Recall an element
g\in G
is
hyperbolic
if its limit set consists of precisely two distinct points
\{g^{+\infty},g^{-\infty}\}\subseteq\partial X
, see e.g.
[
Gro87
]
for details. The centraliser of a hyperbolic element
g
fixes
g^{+\infty}
and
g^{-\infty}
, and we use this observation to prove the lemma.

First suppose
H
is a cocompact subgroup. As
G
is non-amenable and
H\leq G
is cocompact, the set of endpoints of hyperbolic elements of
H
is dense in
\partial X

[
Gro87
, 8.2.D, 8.2.G]
). Therefore
C_{G}(H)
fixes
\partial X
pointwise. Since
G
has trivial amenable radical, Lemma
5.3
ensures the set of elements of
G
fixing
\partial X
is trivial, hence
C_{G}(H)
is trivial.

Now suppose
H
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
. As
H
is normal, any
H
-invariant subset of
\partial X
is also
G
-invariant. Since
G
is non-amenable,
H
cannot stabilise a finite subset of
\partial X
. The classification of groups acting isometrically on hyperbolic space implies
H
contains hyperbolic elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
whose limit sets are disjoint. It follows that
C_{G}(H)
fixes at least four points in the boundary, hence has bounded orbits. As
C_{G}(H)
is a closed subgroup with bounded orbits, it is compact. As
H
is normal, so is
C_{G}(H)
, and hence is trivial as
G
has trivial amenable radical.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Suppose
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
, where each
G_{i}
is a locally compact hyperbolic group with trivial amenable radical. If
H\leq G
is either normal or cocompact, then its centraliser
C_{G}(H)
is trivial.

Proof.

Normal and cocompact subgroups are both preserved by continuous epimorphisms. Therefore, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the projection of
H
to
G_{i}
has trivial centraliser in
G_{i}
by Lemma
6.7
. Thus
C_{G}(H)
projects to the trivial subgroup in each factor, so is also trivial.
∎

The following result gives a sufficient criterion for a group quasi-isometric to a product of hyperbolic spaces to split as a direct product modulo a finite normal subgroup. Caprace–Monod proved an analogous result for CAT(0) groups
[
CM09a
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
each be the product of finitely many (possibly one) hyperbolic locally compact groups with trivial amenable radical, and let
\rho:\Gamma\to G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a homomorphism with finite kernel and whose image is a uniform lattice.
If the projection of
\rho(\Gamma)
to one of
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
is discrete, then the projection onto the other factor is also discrete.
When this is the case,
\Gamma/\ker(\rho)
virtually splits as
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is a uniform lattice in
G_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Set
\Lambda\coloneqq\rho(\Gamma)
and let
p_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
be the projection for
i=1,2
. Let
H_{i}\leq G_{i}
be the closure of
p_{i}(\Lambda)
. Note
\Lambda
is a uniform lattice in
H_{1}\times H_{2}
. Suppose
H_{1}
is discrete. Then
\{1\}\times H_{2}
is open in
H_{1}\times H_{2}
, and so Lemma
2.48
ensures
\Lambda\cap(\{1\}\times H_{2})
is a uniform lattice in
\{1\}\times H_{2}
. Therefore,
\Lambda_{2}\coloneqq p_{2}(\Lambda\cap(\{1\}\times H_{2}))
is a uniform lattice of
H_{2}
normalised by the dense subgroup
p_{2}(\Lambda)
. As
\Lambda_{2}
is a cocompact subgroup of
G_{2}
, Corollary
6.8
ensures its centraliser
C_{H_{2}}(\Lambda_{2})
is trivial. Since Lemma
2.49
says
C_{H_{2}}(\Lambda_{2})
is open,
H_{2}
is discrete. As
\Lambda
is a uniform lattice of the discrete group
H_{1}\times H_{2}
, it is of finite index and so
\Lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq\Lambda^{\prime}_{1}\times\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}
is a finite index subgroup of
\Lambda
, where
\Lambda^{\prime}_{1}\coloneqq p_{1}(\Lambda\cap(H_{1}\times\{1\}))
and
\Lambda^{\prime}_{2}\coloneqq p_{2}(\Lambda\cap(\{1\}\times H_{2}))
.
∎

We now present a refinement of Theorem
6.1
, algebraically characterising when such a group splits as a direct product. We assume at least one factor is not quasi-isometric to a symmetric space; the case where all the factors are quasi-isometric to symmetric spaces is considered by Kleiner–Leeb
[
KL97b
]
.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
X\times Y
, where:

•

X
is a product of finitely many cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric spaces;

•

Y
is a cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric space not quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space.

Then there exists a finitely generated subgroup
\Lambda\leq\Gamma
such that:

(1)

\Lambda
is commensurated by a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{*}\leq\Gamma
;

(2)

\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
X
;

(3)

the quotient space
\Gamma^{*}/\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
Y
;

(4)

\Lambda
is weakly separable in
\Gamma^{*}
if and only if
\Gamma^{*}
contains a finite normal subgroup
F
, contained in
\Lambda
, such that
\Gamma^{*}/F
is virtually isomorphic to
\Gamma_{X}\times\Gamma_{Y}
, where
\Gamma_{X}
is commensurable to
\Lambda/F
and
\Gamma_{Y}
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
Y
.

Proof.

Suppose
X=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
and
Y=X_{n+1}
. Applying Theorem
6.1
,
\Gamma^{*}
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n+1}Y_{i}
preserving the product structure, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite graph. Since
Y
is not quasi-isometric to a symmetric space,
Y_{n+1}
is a locally finite graph.
Set
X^{\prime}=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
and
Y^{\prime}\coloneqq Y_{n+1}
. Then
X^{\prime}
is quasi-isometric to
X
,
Y^{\prime}
is quasi-isometric to
Y
, and
\Gamma^{*}
acts geometrically on
X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
, preserving the product structure and not permuting the factors.

Let
v\in V(Y^{\prime})
be an arbitrary vertex. Applying Lemma
6.5
, we deduce
\Lambda\coloneqq\stab_{\Gamma^{*}}(X^{\prime}\times\{v\})
is commensurated by
\Gamma^{*}
and acts geometrically on
X^{\prime}\times\{v\}
; in particular
\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
X
. Moreover, since
\Lambda
is a coarse stabiliser of the natural action
\Gamma^{*}\curvearrowright Y^{\prime}
by Example
3.4
, the quotient space
\Gamma^{*}/\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to

by Proposition
3.7
. Thus
\Gamma^{*}/\Lambda
is quasi-isometric to
Y
.

Arguing as in the proof of Corollary
6.6
, the action of
\Gamma^{*}
on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n+1}Y_{i}
induces a homomorphism
\Gamma^{*}\to G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n+1}
, where
G_{i}
is obtained by taking the closure of the projection of
\Gamma^{*}
to
\Isom(Y_{i})
and quotienting out by the maximal compact normal subgroup. In particular, each
G_{i}
is a locally compact hyperbolic group with trivial amenable radical. By Remark
4.2
and Proposition
4.5
,
G_{i}
is a topological completion of
\Gamma^{*}\curvearrowright Y_{i}
.
Let
H
be the closure of the projection of
\Gamma^{*}
to
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
. Therefore we have a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma^{*}\to H\times G_{n+1}
with finite kernel whose projection to each factor is dense.

Suppose
\Lambda
is weakly separable. By Lemma
2.22
,
\Lambda
is commensurable to a normal subgroup. Proposition
4.16
implies every topological completion of
\Gamma^{*}\qa Y^{\prime}
is compact-by-discrete. Since
G_{n+1}
is a topological completion of
\Gamma^{*}\qa Y^{\prime}
with trivial amenable radical,
G_{n+1}
is discrete. Proposition
6.9
tells us that
H
is also discrete and
\Gamma^{*}/\ker(\rho)
has a finite index subgroup that splits as
\Gamma_{X}\times\Gamma_{Y}
, where
\Gamma_{X}
and
\Gamma_{Y}
are commensurable to
H
and
G_{n+1}
. Thus
\Gamma_{X}
is quasi-isometric to
X
and
\Gamma_{Y}
is quasi-isometric to
Y
. Since
\Lambda/\ker(\rho)
projects to a finite index subgroup of
H
and to a finite subgroup of
G_{n+1}
,
\Lambda/\ker(\rho)
is commensurable to
\Gamma_{X}\times\{1\}\cong\Gamma_{X}
as required.

Conversely, suppose
\Gamma^{*}
contains a finite normal subgroup

, contained in
\Lambda
, such that
\Gamma^{*}/F
virtually splits as
\Gamma_{X}\times\Gamma_{Y}
with
\Gamma_{X}
commensurable to
\Lambda/F
. Since
\Gamma_{X}
is normalised by a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma^{*}/F
, Lemma

implies
\Lambda/F
is weakly separable in

. Thus

is weakly separable in
\Gamma^{*}
.
∎

We now prove two theorems that show groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces split as direct products under additional algebraic hypotheses, namely residual finiteness and linearity respectively. We use arithmeticity results of Caprace–Monod
[
CM09a
, §5]
.

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite group quasi-isometric to
X_{1}\times X_{2}
, where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are cocompact proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric spaces. If
X_{1}
is quasi-isometric to an irreducible rank one symmetric space and
X_{2}
is not, then either:

(1)

\Gamma
is virtually an arithmetic lattice in a semisimple algebraic group;

(2)

\Gamma
virtually splits as
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
.

Moreover, if
X_{2}
is not quasi-isometric to a tree, then (
2
) holds.

Before beginning the proof, we recall the notion of an irreducible lattice.

Definition 6.12
.

A lattice
\Gamma\leq G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
in a product of locally compact groups is
irreducible
if:

(1)

the projection of
\Gamma
to each
G_{i}
is dense;

(2)

the projection of
\Gamma
to any proper subproduct of
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
is non-discrete.

We caution the reader that outside the world of semisimple algebraic groups, there are many different notions of irreducibility that are not in general equivalent; see the discussion in
[
CM12
, §2.B]
.
To prove Theorem
6.11
, we will apply the following theorem of Caprace–Monod; see also
[
BFS20
, Theorem 4.6]
.

Theorem 6.13

(
[
CM09a
, Theorem 5.18]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq S\times D
be a finitely generated irreducible lattice, where
S
is connected semisimple Lie group and
D
is totally disconnected with trivial amenable radical. Assume the projection
\Gamma\rightarrow S
is injective. Then
D
is a semisimple algebraic group and the image of
\Gamma
in
S\times D
is an arithmetic lattice.

Note that Caprace–Monod prove a more general theorem where the projection
\Gamma\rightarrow S
is not necessarily injective, and

need not have trivial amenable radical
[
CM09a
, Theorem 5.18]
. This more general statement will not be needed here. In order to show injectivity of the projection to the Lie group factor, we appeal to the following theorem of Caprace–Kropholler–Reid–Wesolek.

Lemma 6.14

(
[
CKRW20
, Corollary 33]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice in the product
G_{1}\times G_{2}
of two locally compact
groups. Assume
G_{2}
is totally disconnected and non-discrete and that every infinite
closed normal subgroup of
G_{2}
has trivial centraliser in
G_{2}
. Suppose further that
\Gamma
is finitely
generated and that the canonical projection
\Gamma\rightarrow G_{2}
has a dense image. If
\Gamma
is residually
finite, then the projection
\Gamma\rightarrow G_{1}
is injective.

Proof of Theorem
6.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated residually finite group quasi-isometric to
X_{1}\times X_{2}
. Corollary
6.6
says that there is a finite normal subgroup
F\vartriangleleft\Gamma
such that
\Gamma/F
is a uniform lattice in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
satisfy the conclusions of Corollary
6.6
. In particular,
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are quasi-isometric to
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
respectively. Since
\Gamma
is residually finite, we can replace it with a finite index subgroup if necessary so that
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in
G_{1}\times G_{2}
. If either
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
is discrete, Proposition
6.9
implies
\Gamma
virtually splits as a product
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
, where
\Gamma_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
. We therefore suppose

and
G_{2}
are both non-discrete.

Since both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are hyperbolic and have trivial amenable radical, Theorem
5.4
ensures each of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is either a virtually connected rank one simple Lie group, or is totally disconnected. We claim
G_{1}
is the former and
G_{2}
is the latter. Indeed, as
X_{2}
is not quasi-isometric to a symmetric space,
G_{2}
must be totally disconnected. As
G_{1}
is quasi-isometric to an irreducible rank one symmetric space

,

acts continuously properly and cocompactly on

; see
[
Cor18
, Theorem 19.25]
. As

has trivial amenable radical, this action is faithful. Suppose for contradiction

is totally disconnected. Since

is a closed subgroup of the Lie group

,

is also a Lie group, hence it satisfies the

4.10
, a totally disconnected group satisfying the no small subgroup property is discrete, contradicting our hypothesis that

is not discrete. Therefore,

is a virtually connected rank one simple Lie group.

Combining Lemmas
6.7
and
6.14
, we deduce that the projection
\Gamma\rightarrow G_{1}
is injective. Passing to finite index subgroups of

and

as needed, we may assume

is connected. We can thus apply Theorem
6.13
to deduce

is an arithmetic lattice in a semisimple algebraic group. By Lemma

, this can only occur when

is quasi-isometric to a

∎

We conclude this section with a theorem in a similar vein to Theorem

, where strengthening our algebraic assumption on

is arithmetic or virtually splits. We recall from the introduction that a group is

Theorem 6.15
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated linear group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each

is a cocompact proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space and

(1)

\Gamma
is virtually a uniform arithmetic lattice in a semisimple algebraic group, in which case each

is of coarse algebraic type;

(2)

After permuting the factors of
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, there is an

virtually splits as a direct product

, where

is quasi-isometric to

We prove Theorem

by invoking the following theorem of Caprace–Monod. The

of a topological group is the subgroup consisting of elements with open centraliser.

Theorem 6.16

(
[
CM09a
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
be a finitely generated uniform irreducible lattice, where

admits a faithful Zariski-dense representation into a semisimple group over a field of characteristic

.
After replacing

and

by a finite index subgroups,

.

.

The definition of irreducibility used here is a priori weaker than the notion used in
[

]
, so technically Theorem

is not a special case of
[

, Theorem 5.1]
.

To ensure the hypotheses of Theorem

.

Let
\Gamma\leq G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
be a uniform irreducible lattice, where each

is a nontrivial hyperbolic locally compact group with trivial amenable radical. If
\Gamma
is residually finite, then

Lemma 6.19

(
[
CM09a
, Proposition 4.9]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
be a uniform lattice in a product of compactly

is totally disconnected and that the

.

As each projection

is open, the projection of

is contained in

. Therefore, it is enough to show each

is trivial. By Theorem

, each

is either a virtually connected simple rank one Lie group, or is totally disconnected. In the former case

is trivial, so it remains to show

is totally disconnected.

We assume

is totally disconnected and write

. Note that

is a uniform lattice in

and that both

and

are non-discrete since

is irreducible. By Lemmas

and

, the projection

and Lemma

imply

has amenable closure; see e.g.
[

. As

is trivial.
∎

We now prove Theorem
6.15
, closely following the proof of
[
CM09a
, Theorem 6.6]
.

Proof of Theorem
6.15
.

By Corollary
6.6
and using the fact
\Gamma
is linear, hence residually finite, we replace
\Gamma
with a finite index subgroup that is a uniform lattice in a product
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
as in Corollary
6.6
. In particular,
\Gamma
has trivial amenable radical, each
G_{i}
is a hyperbolic locally compact group with trivial amenable radical quasi-isometric to

, and the projection of
\Gamma
to each

is dense.

If
\Gamma
is not irreducible, then Proposition

ensures
\Gamma
virtually splits as
\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
, where each
\Gamma_{i}

, and we have finished proving the first part of Theorem

. We thus assume

By hypothesis,

has a faithful finite-dimensional linear representation

over a field of characteristic

and quotienting out by the (solvable) radical gives a representation of

thus implies that if at most one

is of coarse algebraic type, then there are no higher rank arithmetic factors, and so

.

6.3.
Biautomaticity of groups quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic spaces

It is a well known open problem to determine whether hyperbolic groups act geometrically on a
\CAT(0)
space. The motivation behind this is that hyperbolic metric spaces typically have poor local geometry, e.g. finite subgroups acting isometrically on such a space need not fix a point. To partially remedy this, the notion of a Helly group was recently introduced by Chapolin et al.
[
CCG+20
]
, a class of groups that satisfy similar properties to
\CAT(0)
groups.

Definition 6.20
.

A
Helly graph
is a graph in which any family of pairwise intersecting combinatorial balls have non-trivial intersection. A
Helly group
is a finitely generated group acting geometrically on a Helly graph.

It was shown by Lang and Chapolin et al. that hyperbolic groups are Helly
[
Lan13
,
CCG+20
]
. Our interest in Helly graphs comes the following proposition, which follows easily from the theory developed by Lang and Chapolin et al.

Proposition 6.21
.

Let
X
be a locally finite hyperbolic graph. There exists a Helly graph
\Helly(X)
and a graph monomorphism
\sigma:X\to\Helly(X)
, which is also an isometric embedding, satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\Helly(X)
is locally finite;

(2)

\Helly(X)=N_{A}(\im(\sigma))
for some
A\geq 0
;

(3)

There is a monomorphism
\Psi:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(\Helly(X))
such that
\sigma\circ\alpha=\Psi(\alpha)\circ\sigma
for all
\alpha\in\Aut(X)
.

Proof.

Let
V=V(X)
be the vertex set of
X
equipped with the subspace metric. We recall the construction of the
injective hull
of
V
, following the notation in
[
Lan13
]
and
[
CCG+20
]
, which we refer to for proofs. Let
E(V)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{V}
be the set of functions
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

f(x)+f(y)\geq d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in V
;

(2)

f
is extremal in the following sense: if
g\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
satisfies:

•

g(x)+g(y)\geq d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in V
and

•

g(x)\leq f(x)
for all
x\in V
,

then
f=g
.

It is shown that
E(V)
is a metric space when equipped with the metric
d_{\infty}(f,g)\coloneqq\sup_{y\in Y}\lvert f(y)-g(y)\rvert
. There is an isometric embedding
e:V\to E(V)
defined by
y\mapsto d_{y}
, where
d_{y}(x)=d(x,y)
.

As in
[
CCG+20
, §4]
, the subspace
E^{0}(V)\coloneqq E(V)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{V}
is called the
discrete injective hull
. The graph
\Helly(X)
is the graph with vertex set
E^{0}(V)
, where two vertices
f,g\in E^{0}(V)
are joined by an edge if
d_{\infty}(f,g)=1
. As suggested by the terminology, the graph
\Helly(X)
is in fact a Helly graph
[
CCG+20
, Theorem 4.4]
. Since
e:V\to E(V)
is an isometric embedding with image contained in
E^{0}(V)
,
e
induces a graph monomorphism
\sigma:X\to\Helly(X)
agreeing with
e
on
V=V(X)
. It is shown in Theorem 4.4 of
[
CCG+20
]
that
f,g\in E^{0}(V)
can be joined by a path in
\Helly(X)
of length

, hence
\sigma
is an isometric embedding.

It is shown in
[
Lan13
, Proposition 3.7]
that there is a monomorphism
\Phi:\Aut(X)\to\Isom(E(V))
given by
\Phi(\alpha)(f)\coloneqq f\circ\alpha^{-1}
. Thus
\Phi
satisfies
\Phi(\alpha)\circ e=e\circ\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\Aut(X)
. All elements of
\Phi(\Aut(X))
preserve the subspace
E^{0}(V)\subseteq E(V)
of integer valued functions, hence
\Phi
induces a homomorphism
\Psi:\Aut(X)\to\Aut(\Helly(X))
such that
\sigma\circ\alpha=\Psi(\alpha)\circ\sigma
for all
\alpha\in\Aut(X)
, showing (
3
) holds.

All that remains is to show (
1
) and (
2
) hold. We now assume the graph
X
, or equivalently its vertex set
V
, is hyperbolic. By
[
CCG+20
, Propositions 3.12 and 5.11]
, there exists some
A\geq 0
such that
N_{A}(\im(e))=E(V)
. It follows that
N_{A^{\prime}}(\im(\sigma))=\Helly(X)
for some
A^{\prime}\geq 0
. For
x,y\in V
, we define the interval
I(x,y)
to be
\{z\in V\mid d(x,z)+d(z,y)=d(x,y)\}
. We say that
V
has
\beta
-stable intervals
if for every triple
x,y,y^{\prime}\in V
with
d(y,y^{\prime})=1
, we have
d_{\text{Haus}}(I(x,y),I(x,y^{\prime}))\leq\beta
. As noted in
[
Lan13
]
, if
V
is
\delta
-hyperbolic then it has
(\delta+1)
-stable intervals. A theorem of Lang now ensures that if
V
is hyperbolic, then
E(V)
is a proper metric space
[
Lan13
, Theorem 1.1]
. Since
E(V)
is proper, it follows that
\Helly(X)
is locally finite.
∎

We use this to deduce:

Corollary 6.22
.

Let
X
be a locally finite hyperbolic graph. Then the map
\sigma:X\to\Helly(X)
is a quasi-isometry that conjugates a group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
by graph automorphisms to an action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\Helly(X)
on a Helly graph.

We can thus refine Theorem
6.1
as follows:

Corollary 6.23
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric space. Then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, preserving the product structure, where each
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
and is either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite Helly graph.

Recall the direct product
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
in Corollary
6.23
is a direct product of metric spaces equipped with the
\ell_{2}
-metric. However, if each
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
is a graph, we can instead define a direct product
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
to be a graph with vertex set
V(X_{1})\times\dots\times V(X_{n})
, where two distinct vertices
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
are joined by an edge if for all
i
, either
v_{i}=w_{i}
or
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
are joined by an edge. This induces the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric on the vertex set. If
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
preserving the product structure and acting via a graph isomorphism on each factor, then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
. Moreover, if each
X_{i}
is Helly, then
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is also Helly
[
CCG+20
, Proposition 5.1]
. Combining these observations with Corollary
6.23
shows the following:

Theorem 6.24
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric space not quasi-isometric to a rank one symmetric space. Then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
\boxtimes_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, where each
Y_{i}
is a locally finite Helly graph quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
. Therefore,
\Gamma
is Helly and satisfies the conclusions of
[
CCG+20
, Theorem 1.5]
. In particular,
\Gamma
is biautomatic.

As mentioned in the introduction, Hughes–Valiunas show the conclusion of Theorem
6.24
is false if we weaken the hypothesis that each
X_{i}
is not quasi-isometric to a symmetric space
[
HV22
]
. Nonetheless, we can partially remedy the situation by relaxing the Helly property to its non-discrete analogue. A metric space

is
injective
if a collection of pairwise intersecting metric balls have non-empty intersection. Groups acting geometrically on injective metric spaces possess many natural properties in common with
\CAT(0)
groups; see
[
Hae21
, Theorem 1.1]
for a list of such properties.

Theorem 6.25
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact non-elementary proper hyperbolic metric space, then
\Gamma
acts geometrically on an injective metric space. In particular,
\Gamma
is semihyperbolic.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
,
\Gamma
acts geometrically preserving the product structure on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, where
Y_{i}
is either a rank one symmetric space or a locally finite hyperbolic graph. As in the proof of Proposition
6.21
, for each
i
there is an isometric embedding
e_{i}:Y_{i}\to E(Y_{i})
where
E(Y_{i})
is the injective hull of
Y_{i}
; see for instance
[
Lan13
]
. We write
(\Pi_{\infty})_{i=1}^{n}E(Y_{i})
to denote the product metric space equipped with the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric. Since each
E(Y_{i})
is an injective space, so is
(\Pi_{\infty})_{i=1}^{n}E(Y_{i})
. By
[
Lan13
, Proposition 3.7]
, the isometric action of
\Gamma
on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
canonically extends to an isometric of
\Gamma
on
(\Pi_{\infty})_{i=1}^{n}E(Y_{i})
. It remains to show this action is geometric.

The result follows readily by showing each
e_{i}:Y_{i}\to E(Y_{i})
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

N_{A_{i}}(e(Y_{i}))=E(Y_{i})
for some
A_{i}\geq 0
;

(2)

E(Y_{i})
is proper.

As noted in the proof of Proposition
6.21
, these properties follow from work of Lang if

is a locally finite hyperbolic graph
[
Lan13
]
. In the case where
Y_{i}
is a rank one symmetric space, these properties were shown by Haettel
[
Hae21
, Proposition 4.6]
. Finally, we note that groups acting geometrically on injective spaces are semihyperbolic; see e.g.
[
Hae21
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

6.4.
Boundary rigidity of
\CAT(0)
spaces quasi-isometric to products of hyperbolic groups

Combining Corollary
6.6
with results of Monod and Caprace–Monod, we prove the following:

Theorem 6.26
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated quasi-isometric to
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, where each
X_{i}
is a cocompact proper non-elementary hyperbolic metric space. Suppose
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a proper
\CAT(0)
space
Y
. Then
Y
contains a canonical closed convex non-empty
\Gamma
-invariant
subset
Y^{\prime}
that splits as
Y_{1}\times\dots\times Y_{n}
, where
Y_{i}
is quasi-isometric to
X_{i}
.
Moreover, the visual boundary
\partial Y
is homeomorphic to the join
\partial X_{1}*\dots*\partial X_{n}
, where
\partial X_{i}
is the Gromov boundary of
X_{i}
. In particular,
\partial Y
depends only on
\Gamma
and not the choice of
\CAT(0)
space
Y
.

We first show a
\CAT(0)
space quasi-isometric to a product of hyperbolic spaces has no Euclidean de Rham factor.

Lemma 6.27
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to a product of proper non-elementary hyperbolic spaces and acting geometrically on a proper
\CAT(0)
space
Y
. Then
Y
has no Euclidean de Rham factor.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is quasi-isometric to a product of proper non-elementary hyperbolic spaces, it acts geometrically on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
as in Theorem
6.1
, preserving the product structure. Moreover, Theorem
6.1
ensures the finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{*}
does not fix a point of
\partial Y_{i}
.
Suppose for contradiction
Y
splits as a direct product
Y=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Y^{\prime}
with
n>0
maximal. It follows from
[
CM19
, Theorem 2]
that
\Gamma
contains a commensurated subgroup
\Lambda\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Note that
\Lambda^{*}\coloneqq\Lambda\cap\Gamma^{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is commensurated in
\Gamma^{*}
.

Since
\Lambda^{*}
is an amenable subgroup acting isometrically on each
Y_{i}
, it either has bounded orbits or fixes a non-empty subset
S_{i}\subseteq\partial Y_{i}
of cardinality at most two. In the latter case, as
\Lambda^{*}\leq\Gamma^{*}
is commensurated, it follows that
S_{i}
is also
\Gamma^{*}
-invariant. This contradicts the fact that
Y_{i}
is non-elementary and
\Gamma^{*}
acts on
Y_{i}
cocompactly without fixing a point of
\partial Y_{i}
. Therefore
\Lambda^{*}
acts on each
Y_{i}
with bounded orbits, so
\Lambda^{*}
acts with bounded orbits on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
. As
\Lambda^{*}
is infinite, this contradicts the fact that
\Lambda^{*}\leq\Gamma^{*}
acts properly on
\Pi_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
.
∎

We will use the following theorems, which are special cases of results of Monod and Caprace–Monod respectively:

Theorem 6.28

(
[
Mon06
, Corollary 10]
)
.

Let

be a proper CAT(0) space. Suppose

acts isometrically and minimally on

and does not fix a point of
\partial X
.
If
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
is any product of groups

, then
X
splits
G
-equivariantly as a product
X=X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{n}
, where
G_{i}
acts isometrically on
X_{i}
.

Theorem 6.29

(
[
CM09b
, Theorem 8.4]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\leq G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
be an irreducible uniform lattice, where each
G_{i}
is a locally compact compactly generated group. Let
X
be a proper
\CAT(0)
space with finite-dimensional boundary and no Euclidean de Rham factor. If
\Gamma
acts isometrically and minimally on
X
without fixing a point of
\partial X
, then the
\Gamma
-action on

extends to a continuous

-action by
isometries.

Proof of Theorem
6.26
.

We proceed by induction on

, noting that when

, there is nothing to prove as the visual boundary and Gromov boundary of a
\CAT(0)
space coincide. By Corollary
6.6
, after replacing
\Gamma
with a finite index subgroup and quotienting out by a finite normal subgroup, we can assume
\Gamma
is a uniform lattice in a product
G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
, where
G_{i}
is a hyperbolic locally compact group with trivial amenable radical and the projection of
\Gamma
to each

is dense.

Suppose
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a proper CAT(0) space
Y
. By Theorem
6.4
, there exists a canonical closed convex non-empty
\Isom(Y)
-invariant
\Isom(Y)
-minimal subset

. Theorem
6.4
also ensures

is
\Gamma
-minimal. By Lemma

,

has no Euclidean de Rham factor, so Theorem

implies

does not fix a point of

.

First suppose

is an irreducible lattice. Then Theorem

implies the action of

on

extends continuously to an isometric action of

on

. Lemma

ensures the action of

on

ensures

is quasi-isometric to

∎

7.
Groups quasi-isometric to central extensions of hyperbolic groups

In this section we prove the following theorem and discuss its consequences:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
Q
be a locally finite vertex-transitive non-elementary hyperbolic graph and let
n\geq 1
. A finitely generated group
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
if and only if both the following hold:

(1)

there is a subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
such that the quotient space
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
;

(2)

the image of the modular homomorphism
G\rightarrow\Comm(H)\cong\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
O_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

7.1.
Quasi-isometrically trivial coarse bundles

Consider a finitely generated group
G
containing a commensurated subgroup
H\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Proposition
2.25
ensures the quotient map
p:G\to G/H
is a coarse bundle. In this subsection we prove Proposition
7.2
, which gives necessary and sufficient conditions for
p:G\rightarrow G/H
to be quasi-isometrically trivial in the sense of Definition
2.29
. Using a theorem of Mineyev concerning
\ell_{\infty}
-cohomology of hyperbolic simplicial complexes, we show in Lemma
7.5
that one of the conditions of Proposition
7.2
is automatically satisfied when the quotient space
G/H
is hyperbolic.

A
coarse Lipschitz section
of a coarse bundle
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is a coarse Lipschitz map
s:G/H\rightarrow G
that is a section to
p
, i.e.
p\circ s=\id_{G/H}
. The following proposition partially generalises the equivalence of (1) and (2) in
[
KL01
, Proposition 8.3]
, which proves the special case in which
H
is a central subgroup in
G
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group containing a commensurated subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
for
n\geq 1
. Then
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is quasi-isometrically trivial if and only if both of the following conditions hold:

(1)

p:G\rightarrow G/H
has a coarse Lipschitz section;

(2)

the image of the modular homomorphism
\Delta:G\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof of the
\implies
direction.

If
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is quasi-isometrically trivial, then
G
is quasi-isometric to
H\times G/H
via a fibre-preserving quasi-isometry. Therefore,
p
has a coarse Lipschitz section, and
p:G\rightarrow G/H
has bounded fibre distortion by Corollary
2.38
. It follows from Example
2.33
that the image of the modular homomorphism
\Delta:G\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
as required.
∎

The remainder of the proof of Proposition
7.2
is the content of the following lemma:

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
G
and
H
be as above and suppose
p:G\rightarrow G/H
satisfies (1) and (2) of Proposition
7.2
. Then
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is quasi-isometrically trivial.

Throughout the remainder of this subsection, we fix the following constants and notation. Let
G
be a finitely generated group containing a commensurated subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
. Let
S
be a symmetric generating set of
G
and let
d_{G}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{S}
be the word metric and word norm with respect to
S
.
We also equip
G/H
with the relative word metric
d_{G/H}
with respect to
S
.
We equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a standard Euclidean inner product
\lVert\cdot\rVert
and fix a monomorphism
\iota:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose image is a uniform lattice. We equip
H
with the metric
d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})=\lVert\iota(h)-\iota(h^{\prime})\rVert
.

Let
\Delta:G\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be the modular homomorphism. We assume the image of
\Delta
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. The modular homomorphism
\Delta
satisfies the property that for all
g\in G
and
h\in H\cap g^{-1}Hg
,
\Delta(g)(\iota(h))=\iota(ghg^{-1})
. Indeed, the definition of
\Delta
ensures that for all
g\in G
, there is a finite index subgroup
H_{g}\leq H\cap g^{-1}Hg
such that
\Delta(g)(\iota(h))=\iota(ghg^{-1})
for all
h\in H_{g}
. However, a homomorphism between groups isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is determined by its restriction to a finite index subgroup, hence we can assume
H_{g}=H\cap g^{-1}Hg
as above.

We can choose a proper non-decreasing function
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and constants
K\geq 1
,
A\geq 0
so that the following hold.

•

N_{A}(\iota(H))=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

•

The inclusion
H\rightarrow G
is an
(\eta,K,A)
-coarse embedding.

•

The map
p:G\rightarrow G/H
is a
(K,A)
-coarse bundle.

•

\lVert\Delta(g)v\rVert\leq A\lVert v\rVert
for all
g\in G
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The last point follows from the fact that
\im(\Delta)
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, hence its elements have uniformly bounded operator norm.
We first show the following technical lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

For each
r\geq 0
, there is a constant
B_{r}
such that the following hold for all
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in H
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
.

(1)

If
\lvert g_{1}\rvert_{S}\leq r
and
\lvert h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}\rvert_{S}\leq r
, then

\lVert\iota(h_{1})+\Delta(g_{1})(\iota(h_{2}))\rVert\leq B_{r}.

(2)

If
\lvert g_{1}\rvert_{S},\lvert g_{2}\rvert_{S}\leq r
and
\lvert h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2}h_{3}\rvert_{S}\leq r
, then

\lVert\iota(h_{1})+\Delta(g_{1})\iota(h_{2})+\Delta(g_{1}g_{2})\iota(h_{3})\rVert\leq B_{r}.

(3)

If
\lvert g_{1}\rvert_{S}\leq r
and
\lVert\iota(h_{1})+\Delta(g_{1})(\iota(h_{2}))\rVert\leq r
, then
\lvert h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}\rvert_{S}\leq B_{r}
.

Proof.

Fix
r\geq 0
. Let
H_{0}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\lvert g\rvert_{S}\leq r}H^{g}
, noting that
H_{0}
is a finite index subgroup of
H
. Let
\pi:H\rightarrow H_{0}
be a closest point projection. We can pick a constant

such that both
\sup_{h\in H}d_{G}(\pi(h),h)
and
\sup_{h\in H}\lVert\iota(h)-\iota(\pi(h))\rVert
are bounded by

. We set
B_{r}\coloneqq\max(\tilde{\eta}(3r+2D)+2AD,K(r+AD)+r+D)
, where
\tilde{\eta}
is as in Remark
2.3
.

(
2
): We observe

\displaystyle\lvert h_{1}g_{1}\pi(h_{2}g_{2}\pi(h_{3})g_{2}^{-1})g_{1}^{-1}\rvert_{S}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2}\pi(h_{3})g_{2}^{-1}\rvert_{S}+D+r

\displaystyle\leq\lvert h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2}h_{3}\rvert_{S}+2D+2r

\displaystyle\leq 3r+2D

Since

We argue as in case 2 of the proof of
[
KL01
, Proposition 5.3]
. Namely, we define
Z_{k}
to be the term in the upper central series of
G^{\circ}
at which
\dim(Z_{k})
achieves its maximum value. Then
G/Z_{k}
has discrete centre and
\dim(Z_{k})<\dim(G)
as
G^{\circ}
is not nilpotent. We can therefore apply
[
KL01
, Lemma 5.5]
to
\Gamma\leq G
.
∎

We also need to following lemma for the case in which
Q
is a tree.

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
T
be a locally finite infinite-ended tree and let
G
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times T
. Then the induced quasi-action
G\qa T
as in Lemma
7.8
does not fix a point of
\partial T
.

Proof.

First note that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times T
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times F_{2}
, where
F_{2}
is a free group of rank two. Thus
G
is quasi-isometric to a non-amenable finitely generated group, hence is non-amenable. We assume for contradiction that
G
fixes a point of
\partial T
.

By
[
MSW03
, Theorems 1 and 2]
, there exists a locally finite bushy tree
T^{\prime}
such that the induced action
G\qa T
can be quasi-conjugated to an isometric action
G\qa T^{\prime}
, and the stabiliser of every vertex and edge of
T^{\prime}
is virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. As
G
fixes a point of
\partial T
,
G
also fixes a point of
\partial T^{\prime}
. Replacing
T^{\prime}
with a minimal subtree and considering the quotient graph
G\backslash T^{\prime}
, we deduce
G
must be a strictly ascending HNN extension of a group
H
that is virtually
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and defined by an injective endomorphism
\alpha:H\rightarrow H
. Since
H
is amenable, so is the normal subgroup
H_{\infty}\coloneqq\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha^{-i}(H)
. Since
G/H_{\infty}\cong\mathbb{Z}
is an infinite cyclic subgroup generated by the image of the stable letter,
G
is amenable. This is the desired contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.9
.

We suppose for contradiction
G\qa Q
fixes a point of
\partial Q
. By Corollary
5.8
,
G\qa Q
can be quasi-conjugated to an isometric action
\rho:G\rightarrow\Isom(Z)
, where
Z
is either a negatively curved homogeneous space, a pure millefeuille space, or a regular tree of finite valency greater than two. Since
Q
is a locally finite vertex-transitive graph, Proposition
5.9
says
Z
is not a pure millefeuille space. Moreover, Lemma
7.11
precludes the case
Z
is a tree. Therefore,
Z
must be a negatively curved homogeneous space.

To apply Lemma
7.10
, we need to fix some constants. Let
X\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
and suppose
G\qa X
is an
A_{1}
-cobounded proper quasi-action on
X
. We also assume the projection
\pi:X\rightarrow Q
is
A_{2}
-coarsely equivariant and that
f:Q\rightarrow Z
is a
(K_{3},A_{3})
-quasi-conjugacy from the quasi-action
G\qa Q
to the isometric action
G\curvearrowright Z
.

Pick a basepoint
x_{0}\in X
, set
q_{0}\coloneqq\pi(x_{0})
and
z_{0}\coloneqq f(\pi(x_{0}))
. Set
A_{4}\coloneqq\max(K_{3}(2A_{3}+1)+A_{2},A_{1})
and let

V\coloneqq\{g\in G\mid d_{Q}(\pi(g\cdot x_{0}),q_{0})\leq A_{4}\}.

We claim
V\subseteq G
, equipped with the subspace metric, is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since the map
h:G\rightarrow X
given by
g\mapsto g\cdot x_{0}
is a quasi-isometry, it is sufficient to show that
h(V)
has finite Hausdorff distance from
D_{q_{0}}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(q_{0})
, which is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By definition
h(V)\subseteq N_{A_{4}}(D_{q_{0}})
. Since
G\qa X
is
A_{1}
-cobounded, it follows for each
x\in D_{q_{0}}
, there is some
g\in G
such that
d(g\cdot x_{0},x)\leq A_{1}
. Therefore,
d_{Q}(\pi(g\cdot x_{0}),q_{0})\leq A_{1}\leq A_{4}
, so
g\in V
and hence
x\in N_{A_{1}}(h(V))
. As
D_{q_{0}}\subseteq N_{A_{1}}(h(V))
,
h(V)
and
D_{q_{0}}
are at finite Hausdorff distance. It follows that
r(k)\coloneqq\left|\{g\in V\mid\lvert g\rvert_{G}\leq k\}\right|
grows polynomially with
k
.

Let
U\coloneqq\{\phi\in\Isom(Z)\mid d_{Z}(\phi(z_{0}),z_{0})<1\}
, which is an identity neighbourhood in
\Isom(Z)
. We claim
\rho^{-1}(U)\subseteq V
. Indeed, suppose
g\in\rho^{-1}(U)
. Then

\displaystyle d_{Q}(\pi(g\cdot x_{0}),q_{0})

\displaystyle\leq d_{Q}(g\cdot q_{0},q_{0})+A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}(d_{Z}(f(g\cdot q_{0}),z_{0})+A_{3})+A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}(d_{Z}(\rho(g)(z_{0}),z_{0})+2A_{3})+A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{3}(1+2A_{3})+A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq A_{4}

so that
g\in V
. Lemma
7.10
then guarantees that
r(k)
grows faster than any polynomial, contradicting the previous paragraph.

We have thus shown that
G\qa Q
cannot fix a point of
\partial Q
. By Corollary
5.7
, either
G\qa Q
is discretisable or
G\qa Q
is quasi-conjugate to a non-discrete isometric action on a rank one symmetric space
Z
. The latter cannot occur, as shown in the proof of
[
KL01
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
KL01
, Proposition 5.3]
).
∎

7.3.
Consequences of Theorem
7.1

Theorem 7.12
.

Let
Q
be a locally finite vertex-transitive non-elementary hyperbolic graph. A finitely generated group
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times Q
if and only if
G
contains an infinite cyclic normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}\cong H\vartriangleleft G
such that
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
.

Proof.

\implies
: Suppose
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times Q
. Then Theorem
7.1
ensures
G
contains an infinite cyclic commensurated subgroup
\langle a\rangle=H
such that the quotient space
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
, and the image of
\Delta:G\rightarrow\GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
\Orth_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\{\pm 1\}
is a normal subgroup of
\GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, it follows that
\Delta(G)
is contained in
\{\pm 1\}
. Thus for each
g\in G
, there is some sufficiently large
N_{g}
such that
ga^{N_{g}}g^{-1}=a^{\pm N_{g}}
. Let
S=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
be a generating set of
G
, and let
N=\lcm(N_{s_{1}},\dots,N_{s_{n}})
. Therefore
s_{i}a^{N}s_{i}^{-1}=a^{\pm N}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, whence
ga^{N}g^{-1}=a^{\pm N}
for all
g\in G
. Therefore
H_{0}\coloneqq\langle a^{N}\rangle
is a normal subgroup of
G
. As
H_{0}
is a finite index subgroup of
H
, the quotient group
G/H_{0}
is quasi-isometric to the quotient space
G/H
, hence to
Q
.

\impliedby
: Suppose
G
contains an infinite cyclic normal subgroup
H\vartriangleleft G
with
G/H
quasi-isometric to
Q
. Since
\Aut(H)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, the image of the map
G\to\Aut(H)\cong\{\pm 1\}
is conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore Theorem
7.1
implies
G
is quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times Q
.
∎

Theorem
7.12
implies:

Corollary 7.13
.

A finitely generated group that is quasi-isometric to a
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic group is also
\mathbb{Z}
-by-hyperbolic.

The groups constructed by Leary–Minasyan demonstrate that Theorem
7.12
cannot in general be strengthened to deduce that groups quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
contain a normal free abelian subgroup of rank
n
. The following proposition gives necessary and sufficient criteria under which the commensurated subgroup in Theorem
7.1
contains a finite index normal subgroup. The author would like to thank Sam Hughes for pointing out a result of Valiunas used in the proof of the following proposition.

Proposition 7.14
.

Let
Q
be a locally finite vertex-transitive non-elementary hyperbolic graph and let
G
be a finitely generated group quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
. Let
H\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a commensurated subgroup as in Theorem
7.1
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

H
is weakly separable;

(2)

H
has a finite index subgroup that is normal in
G
;

(3)

a finite index subgroup of
G
centralises a finite index subgroup of
H
;

(4)

G
is biautomatic.

Proof.

(
1
)
\iff
(
2
) and (
3
)
\implies
(
2
) follow from Lemma
2.22
.

(
2
)
\implies
(
3
): Suppose
H^{\prime}\leq H
is a finite index subgroup of
H
that is normal in
G
. The image of the modular homomorphism
\Delta:G\to\Aut(H)\cong\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is both a discrete subgroup of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and conjugate to a subgroup of
\Orth_{n}(\mathbb{R})
by Theorem
7.1
. Thus
\im(\Delta)
is finite, ensuring
H^{\prime}
is centralised by the finite index subgroup
\ker(\Delta)\leq G
.

(
3
)
\implies
(
4
): This follows from a theorem of Neumann–Reeves, which shows a virtually central extension of a hyperbolic group is biautomatic
[
NR97
, Theorem 1.3]
.

(
4
)
\implies
(
3
): Valiunas showed if
G
is biautomatic and
H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
is a finitely generated commensurated abelian subgroup, then a finite index subgroup of
G
centralises a finite index subgroup of
H

[
Val21
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Finally, we show the conclusion of Theorem
7.1
can be strengthened under the additional hypothesis that
G
is residually finite:

Theorem 7.15
.

Let
Q
be a locally finite vertex-transitive non-elementary hyperbolic graph. Let
G
be a finitely generated residually finite group quasi-isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times Q
. Then
G
contains a normal subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\vartriangleleft G
such that
G/H
is quasi-isometric to
Q
. Moreover,
H
is centralised by a finite index subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.1
,
G
contains a commensurated subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong H\text{\;\cusfont Q\; }G
. It follows from the proof of Theorem
7.1
that the induced quasi-action
G\qa Q
as in Lemma
7.8
has coarse stabiliser
H
and does not fix a point of
\partial Q
. By Proposition
3.7
,
G\qa Q
is quasi-conjugate to
G\curvearrowright G/H
and the action of
G
on
G/H
does not fix a point of
\partial(G/H)
. It follows that any abelian normal subgroup of
G
has bounded orbits in
G/H
, hence is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets.

Let
\overline{H}
be the profinite closure of
H
, i.e. the smallest separable subgroup of
G
containing
H
. Since
G
is residually finite, the profinite topology is Hausdorff. Therefore,
\overline{H}
is the closure of an abelian subgroup of a Hausdorff topological group, hence is also abelian; see e.g.
[
HR79
, Corollary 5.3]
. Moreover,
[
CKRW20
, Corollary 6]
ensures
\overline{H}
contains a finite index subgroup
N
that is normal in
G
. Thus
N
is a normal abelian subgroup of
G
, so by the previous paragraph,
N
is contained in finitely many left
H
-cosets. Since
H\leq\overline{H}
,
N\cap H
is a finite index subgroup of
H
, hence is commensurable to
H
. Since
H
is commensurable to a normal subgroup of
G
, the result follows from Proposition
7.14
.
∎

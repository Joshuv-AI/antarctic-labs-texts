Approximations of Rockafellians, Lagrangians, and Dual Functions
Julio Deride, Johannes O. Royset

Abstract
Solutions of an optimization problem are sensitive to changes caused by approximations or parametric perturbations, especially in the nonconvex setting. This paper shows that solutions of substitute problems, constructed from Rockafellian functions, can be less sensitive to such changes. Unlike classical stability analysis focused on local changes around (local) minimizers, we employ epi-convergence to examine whether approximating or perturbed problems suitably approach an actual (unperturbed) problem globally. We demonstrate that solutions derived from the Rockafellian-based substitute problems converge to solutions of the actual optimization problem under suitable conditions, providing a rigorous alternative to potentially unstable direct approximations. We quantify the rates of convergence that often lead to Lipschitz-kind stability properties for the substitute problems.



keywords

stability, approximation theory, epi-convergence, Rockafellian, Lagrangian, duality

Introduction

Given the problem of minimizing
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=[-\infty,\infty]
, we are often compelled to consider one or more approximating problems. The approximations may stem from imprecise implementation of conceptual operations such as integration of nontrivial functions and maximization over uncountable sets or from smoothing methods and penalty functions. Approximations also arise in sensitivity analysis as model parameters are perturbed, for example in an effort to identify the problem’s robustness to data manipulation. These approximations may cause disproportionally large changes in minimizers, minimum values, and also level-sets; see, e.g.,
[
43
,
46
]
. Fortunately, every optimization problem can be associated with many
substitute problems
and they may possess more desirable stability properties. This paper develops sufficient conditions for epigraphical and hypographical stability of substitute problems defined by Rockafellian relaxations, Lagrangian relaxations, and dual functions.

Under broad conditions involving epi-convergence and exactness, cluster points of minimizers from these relaxations are, in fact, minimizers of the actual underlying problem, a property not generally shared by direct approximations.

While stability results near a local solution leverage concepts from second-order subdifferentiability, supporting calculus rules, and notions of “regularity” (cf.

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\delta(u)\geq-\bar{\eta}-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}(p_{i}+u_{i})\bar{\eta}\geq-2\bar{\eta}.}}

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle~~+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}\big(\min\{0,p_{i}+u_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}\}\big)^{2}\leq f(u,x).

\displaystyle=f(u,x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}\big(\min\{p_{i}^{\nu},p_{i}+u_{i}\}-(p_{i}+u_{i})\big)g_{i}(x)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}(\theta^{\nu}\min\{0,p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}\}^{2}-\theta u_{i}^{2})

The proof of part (a) is constructive and thus furnishes a formula for

3.4
Epi-Convergence and Quantification under
Augmentation

A Rockafellian
f
for
\varphi
that is not (strictly) exact can be augmented with a function of
u
to produce another Rockafellian for
\varphi
with that property. One approach relies on norms to define augmenting functions, which relates closely to augmented Lagrangians
[
41
, Sec. 11.K]
. We concentrate on the augmenting function
\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}
. Trivially, the resulting Rockafellian
f+\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}
for
\varphi
is strictly exact, supported by any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The computational challenges associated with this augmenting function are mitigated by considering an approximating function
a^{\nu}
, which may only introduce minor inaccuracies compared to those anticipated from approximating
f
by
f^{\nu}
.
This is formalized next, with a proof in Section
6
.

Theorem 12
.

(epi-convergence under augmentation).

For
\{a,a^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,\infty],

\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
a(0)=a^{\nu}(0)=0
and proper
\{f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},

\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}

\varphi,\varphi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively, and let

\bar{f}(u,x)=f(u,x)+a(u)~~\mbox{ and }~~\bar{f}^{\nu}(u,x)=f^{\nu}(u,x)+a^{\nu}(u).

Then,
\bar{f}
and
\bar{f}^{\nu}
are Rockafellians for
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\nu}
, respectively, and the following hold:

(a)

If

,

(b)

(c)

and
(u,x,\alpha)\mapsto\max\{\|u\|_{2},\|x\|_{2},|\alpha|\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

leads to a quantifiable rate of convergence in Theorem

4
Convergence of Lagrangians

We next focus on Lagrangians and the middle arrow in Figure
1
. Given that
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
and
y^{\nu}\to y
, what additional properties would suffice for
l^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,y^{\nu})\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptl(\,\cdot\,,y)
to hold? In the convex setting, this is well understood even in reflexive Banach spaces and for the broader question of epi/hypo-convergence of
l^{\nu}
to
l

[
4
]
; see also
[
2
,
3
,
14
]
, with rates of convergence results in
[
11
]
. (The general theory of epi/hypo-convergence as pioneered in
[
6
]
does not require convex-concave bifunctions.) In this section, we focus on the nonconvex setting and the “primal” variables, with the multipliers being viewed as parameters. We recall that the role of multipliers tends to be somewhat diminished in the nonconvex case when the underlying Rockafellian is often constructed via augmentation. This motivates the present focus, which brings forth
tightness
as the key property.

Theorem 14
.

(Lagrangian relaxations; epi-convergence).

For Rockafellians
f
,
f^{\nu}:

\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
and multiplier vectors
y^{\nu}\to y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. If
\{f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight for all convergent sequence
\{x^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
, then
l^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,y^{\nu})\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptl(\,\cdot\,,y)
.

Proof.
We first confirm the liminf-condition in (
2.1
). Let
x^{\nu}\to x
. Since
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf_{y}
by Theorem
5
(a), it follows from (
2.1
) that
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\geq f_{y}(u,x)
whenever
u^{\nu}\to u
. We can then invoke Proposition
26
(a) to conclude that
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})=\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(\inf f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}))\geq\inf f_{y}(\,\cdot\,,x)=l(x,y)
.

Second, we confirm the limsup-condition in (
2.2
). Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We construct
x^{\nu}\to x
such that

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\leq l(x,y)

(4.1)

and consider three cases. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
. (i) Suppose that
l(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, there exists
u\in\varepsilon\mbox{-}\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y}(\,\cdot\,,x)
. Since
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf_{y}
, there are points
(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\to(u,x)
such that
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq f_{y}(u,x)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\big(\inf f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})\big)\leq\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq f_{y}(u,x)\leq\inf f_{y}(\,\cdot\,,x)+\varepsilon=l(x,y)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, (
4.1
) holds. (ii) The case with
l(x,y)=-\infty
follows by a similar argument. (iii) Suppose that
l(x,y)=\infty
. Then, (
4.1
) holds trivially with
x^{\nu}=x
for all
\nu
.

A consequence of the theorem is that
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits(\inf l^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,y^{\nu}))

\leq

\inf f(0,\,\cdot\,)

=

\inf\varphi
; see Proposition
26
(b) below. Consequently, under the assumptions of Theorem
14
the approximating Lagrangians provide in the limit a lower bound on the minimum value of the actual problem.

We note that tightness is a necessary assumption in the theorem in the following sense. If there exists a convergent sequence
x^{\nu}\to x
for which
\{f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is not tight, then
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})<l(x,y)
and the epi-convergence of

to

will not hold.

A result related to Theorem

appears as Exercise 7.57 in
[

. It asserts the following: For proper lsc

Theorem 15
.

(Lagrangian relaxations; error bounds).

For Rockafellians
f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that
\epi f
and
\epi f^{\nu}
are nonempty,
\rho\in[0,\infty)
, and

\hat{\rho}\in[\rho,\infty)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ with }\|x\|_{2}\leq\rho,~l(x,y)\leq\rho:

\displaystyle~~~~\exists\hat{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ with }\|\hat{u}\|_{2}\leq\hat{\rho},~~f_{y}(\hat{u},x)\leq\max\big\{-\rho,~l(x,y)\big\},

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mbox{ with }\|x\|_{2}\leq\rho,~l^{\nu}(x,y^{\nu})\leq\rho

\displaystyle~~~~\exists\hat{u}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ with }\|\hat{u}^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\hat{\rho},~f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\hat{u}^{\nu},x)\leq\max\big\{-\rho,~l^{\nu}(x,y^{\nu})\big\},

then, using the norms
(u,x,\alpha)\mapsto\max\{\|u\|_{2},\|x\|_{2},|\alpha|\}
and
(x,\alpha)\mapsto\max\{\|x\|_{2},|\alpha|\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

Proof.
Set
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
. Let
(x^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})\in\epi l^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,y^{\nu})\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho)
. Since
\|x^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\rho
and
l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\leq\alpha^{\nu}\leq\rho
, there is
\hat{u}^{\nu}\in\mathbb{B}(\hat{\rho})
with
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\hat{u}^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq\max\{-\rho,~l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\}
.

We consider two cases. First, suppose that

. Then,
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\hat{u}^{\nu},x^{\nu})=l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y^{\nu})\leq\alpha^{\nu}
. This means that
(\hat{u}^{\nu},x^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})

\in

\epi f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\cap\mathbb{B}(\hat{\rho})
.
Second, suppose that

, where
\eta^{\nu}=d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\hat{\rho}}(\epi f_{y},\epi f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}})+\varepsilon

. Moreover,

Since

Proposition 16
.

(composite optimization; tightness).

In the setting of Example
1
, suppose that the functions
\{h^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are proper, lsc, and convex, and both
\{x^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{y^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are convergent sequences. If
\{G^{\nu}(x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded and there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\partial h^{\nu*}(y^{\nu})\cap K\neq\emptyset
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\{f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight.

Proof.
We can limit the focus to
x^{\nu}\in X^{\nu}
because otherwise tightness holds trivially. Thus, without loss of generality, suppose that
x^{\nu}\in X^{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
49
, Thm. 2.19]
and
[
49
, Prop. 5.37]
,
using the short-hand notation
w^{\nu}=G^{\nu}(x^{\nu})+u^{\nu}
,
one has

u^{\nu}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})~\Longleftrightarrow~y^{\nu}\in\partial h^{\nu}(w^{\nu})~\Longleftrightarrow~w^{\nu}\in\partial h^{\nu*}(y^{\nu}).

(4.2)

By assumption, there exist points
\{v^{\nu}\in\partial h^{\nu*}(y^{\nu})\cap K,\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Then,
\{u^{\nu}=v^{\nu}-G^{\nu}(x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded because
v^{\nu}\in K
and
\{G^{\nu}(x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded. By (
4.2
),
u^{\nu}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})
and there is a compact set
B
such that
\inf f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})=f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})=\inf\{f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})~|~u\in B\}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.

The assumption about a compact set
K
in Proposition
16
holds broadly. For example, suppose that
h^{\nu}
is of the form
h^{\nu}(z)=\sup_{y\in Y^{\nu}}\langle y,z\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle y,Q^{\nu}y\rangle

is nonempty, closed, and convex, and

is a symmetric positive semidefinite

-by-

matrix; see, e.g.,
[
49
, Sec. 5.I]
. Then, for

,
\partial h^{\nu*}(y^{\nu})=Q^{\nu}y^{\nu}+N_{Y^{\nu}}(y^{\nu})
. Since

, the assumption of Proposition

holds if

is bounded. This is indeed common. For example, the case

(i.e., inequality constraints) has

and

.

For fixed

Proposition 17
.

(composite optimization; epi-convergence).

In the setting of Example
1
, suppose that
X^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle s$}}\hskip 7.0ptX

, which holds in particular when

\{h,h^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}

Proof.
Suppose that
h^{\nu*}(y)\to h^{*}(y)
. First, we consider the liminf-condition (
2.1
) and let
x^{\nu}\to x
. We observe that
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(\iota_{X^{\nu}}(x^{\nu})-h^{\nu*}(y))\geq\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{X^{\nu}}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(-h^{\nu*}(y))

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits l^{\nu}(x^{\nu},y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\iota_{X^{\nu}}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits g_{0}^{\nu}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\big\langle G^{\nu}(x^{\nu}),y\big\rangle+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\big(-h^{\nu*}(y)\big)

\{f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
holds if there is a bounded set containing a minimizer of
p_{i}^{\nu}g_{i}^{\nu}(x^{\nu}+u_{i})-y_{i}^{\nu}u_{i}
for each
i
and
\nu
.

4.3
Tightness from
Augmentation

Formulas for Lagrangians under augmention are available in many cases; see
[
41
, Sec. 11.K]
. Trivially, any Rockafellian
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
augmented with the indicator function
\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}
produces the Rockafellian given by
\bar{f}(u,x)=f(u,x)+\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}(u)
, which in turn defines a Lagrangian of the form
\bar{l}(x,y)=f(0,x)
for all
y
. We concentrate on two sufficient conditions for tightness as required in Theorem
14
. Augmentation is also discussed in
[
3
, Thm. 4.2]
, but for the convex case.

Proposition 21
.

(tightness under proximal augmentation).

For
\theta\in(0,\infty)
,
\{\theta^{\nu}\in[\theta,\infty),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and
\{f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
, suppose that for each
\nu
,
f^{\nu}
is a Rockafellian for
\varphi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and let

\bar{f}^{\nu}(u,x)=f^{\nu}(u,x)+\theta^{\nu}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.

Suppose that
\{x^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is convergent,
\{y^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded, and there exist
\bar{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in(0,\theta)
, and
\gamma,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that for each
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
with
\inf f^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})<\infty
one has

\displaystyle\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:

\displaystyle~~f^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})\geq\gamma-\beta\|u\|_{2}^{2}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\exists u^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ with }\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\tau:

\displaystyle~~f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq\tau.

(4.4)

Then,
\{\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight.

Proof.
Since
\{y^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded, there is
\rho<\infty
such that
\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\|y^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\rho
. Without loss of generality, we consider
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
with
\inf f^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})<\infty
because otherwise
\inf\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})=\infty
and the tigthness condition holds trivially. For such
\nu
, one has

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u,x^{\nu})

\displaystyle=f^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})+\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle\geq\gamma+(\theta^{\nu}-\beta)\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\rho\|u\|_{2}~~~\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle=f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})+\theta^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}-\langle y^{\nu},u^{\nu}\rangle\leq\tau+\theta^{\nu}\tau^{2}+\rho\tau.

Since
\theta^{\nu}-\beta>0
,
there exists
\bar{\rho}>\rho
, independent of
\nu
, such that
\gamma+(\theta^{\nu}-\beta)\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\rho\|u\|_{2}\geq\tau+\theta^{\nu}\tau^{2}+\rho\tau
when
\|u\|_{2}>\bar{\rho}
. Consequently,
\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u,x^{\nu})
when
\|u\|_{2}>\bar{\rho}
and
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
. This implies that

\inf_{u\in\mathbb{B}(\bar{\rho})}\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u,x^{\nu})

\leq

\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(u,x^{\nu})
for all
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
.

Proposition 22
.

(tightness under sharp augmentation).

For
\{\theta^{\nu}\in(0,\infty),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
, suppose that for each
\nu
,
f^{\nu}
is a Rockafellian for
\varphi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and let

\bar{f}^{\nu}(u,x)=f^{\nu}(u,x)+\theta^{\nu}\|u\|,

where the norm is arbitrary but equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{2}
in the sense that for some
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
one has
\|u\|_{2}\leq\kappa\|u\|
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Suppose that
\{x^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is convergent,
\{y^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfies
\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\|y^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\rho
for some
\rho\in[0,\infty)
, and there exist
\bar{\nu}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\in(0,\infty)
, and
\gamma,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that for each
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
with
\inf f^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})<\infty
one has

\displaystyle\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:

\displaystyle~~f^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})\geq\gamma-\beta\|u\|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\exists u^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ with }\|u^{\nu}\|\leq\tau:

\displaystyle~~f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq\tau.

(4.6)

If
\theta^{\nu}\geq\beta+\kappa\rho+1
for all
\nu\geq\bar{\nu}
, then
\{\bar{f}^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight.

Proof.
The conclusion follows by arguments similar to those for Proposition
21
.

The conditions (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) are mild as they simply impose lower bounding concave functions on the Rockafellians
f^{\nu}
uniformly for all

. Conditions (

The path to analyzing many Lagrangians constructed by augmentation is now clear. For example, consider the Rockafellians
f
and

in Example

for composite optimization. Proposition

furnishes sufficient conditions for
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
.
Suppose that
f
is augmented with
\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}
to produce
\bar{f}
, which then is strictly exact, and

is augmented by some norm to produce

5
Convergence of Dual Functions

Next, we focus on hypo-convergence of
\psi^{\nu}
to
\psi
and its quantification as represented by the top arrow in Figure
1
. There are three possible perspectives when examining a dual problem of maximizing
\psi
and its approximation involving
\psi^{\nu}
:
(i) One could develop sufficient conditions for
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
expressed in terms of the underlying Rockafellians. (ii) One could focus on the corresponding Lagrangians and the max-inf problems
\mathop{\rm maximize}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}l(x,y)
and
\mathop{\rm maximize}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}l^{\nu}(x,y)
. (iii) One could focus on the bifunctions
k,k^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m+n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

k\big(y,(u,x)\big)=f(u,x)-\langle y,u\rangle~~~\mbox{ and }~~~k^{\nu}\big(y,(u,x)\big)=f^{\nu}(u,x)-\langle y,u\rangle,

(5.1)

and the resulting max-inf problems

\mathop{\rm maximize}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}k\big(y,(u,x)\big)~~\mbox{ and }~~\mathop{\rm maximize}_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}}k^{\nu}\big(y,(u,x)\big).

We adopt perspective (i) in the following because it directly addresses the dual problems without passing through some equivalent (in some sense) max-inf problems. Perspectives (ii) and (iii) land us in the territory of lopsided convergence as pioneered in
[
5
]
and refined in
[
48
]
. That general approach to analyzing max-inf (or min-sup) problems furnishes sufficient conditions for the convergence of max-inf points and max-inf values. However, the conditions do not explicitly leverage the special structure of the bifunctions
l,l^{\nu}
and
k,k^{\nu}
, in particular concavity in the
y
-arguments. Still, we compare below with (iii) in detail. Perspective (ii) would result in conditions on the Lagrangians, which is counter to our focus on the more fundamental Rockafellians.

The next theorem provides the key building block for examining dual functions in applications.

Theorem 23
.

(hypo-convergence of dual functions).

For Rockafellians
f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
. Then, the following hold:

(a)

If for each
y\in\dom(-\psi)
there exists
y^{\nu}\to y
such that
\{f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight, then
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
.
In fact, for all such
y^{\nu}\to y
, one has
\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\to\psi(y)
.

(b)

If
\dom(-\psi)
has a nonempty interior,
Y
is a dense subset of
\dom(-\psi)
, and
\{f^{\nu}_{y},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight for each
y\in Y
, then
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
.

Proof.
For (a), it suffices to show that
-\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0pt-\hskip-0.99594pt\psi
. First, we consider (
2.1
) and let
y^{\nu}\to y
. By Theorem
5
(a),
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf_{y}
. In view of Proposition
26
(b), this implies that
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}})\leq\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits f_{y}
. Thus,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\geq-\psi(y)
. Second, we consider (
2.2
). Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. If
y\not\in\dom(-\psi)
, then
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits-\psi^{\nu}(y)\leq-\psi(y)
holds trivially because the right-hand side is infinity. Thus, we concentrate on
y\in\dom(-\psi)
. By assumption, there exists
y^{\nu}\to y
such that
\{f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight. By Theorem
5
(a),
f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf_{y}
. Proposition
26
(c) then implies that

\inf f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\to\inf f_{y}.

(5.2)

Thus,
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\leq-\psi(y)
and the first part of (a) follows. Since (
5.2
) simply states that
\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\to\psi(y)
, the remaining claim in (a) holds immediately.

For (b), we start by showing that
-\psi
is lsc. We consider two cases. (i) Suppose that
f(u,x)=-\infty
for some
(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,
-\psi(y)=\infty
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. This means that
\dom(-\psi)=\emptyset
, which is ruled out by assumption. (ii) Suppose that
f(u,x)>-\infty
for all
(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
-\psi
can be written as
-\psi(y)=\mathop{\rm sup}\nolimits\{\langle y,u\rangle-f(u,x)|(u,x)\in\dom f\}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we invoke
[
49
, Prop. 6.27]
to conclude that
-\psi
is lsc. That proposition requires
y\mapsto\langle y,u\rangle-f(u,x)
to be real-valued and lsc for each
(u,x)\in\dom f
, which indeed hold.

Since
-\psi^{\nu}
and
-\psi
are convex functions with
\dom(-\psi)
having a nonempty interior and
-\psi
being lsc, it suffices by
[
49
, Thm. 7.17]
to show that
\psi^{\nu}(y)\to\psi(y)
for all
y\in Y\cup(\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\dom(-\psi))
. Let
y\in Y\cup(\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\dom(-\psi))
.

We invoke Theorem
5
(a) to establish that
f^{\nu}_{y}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf_{y}
and consider two cases. (i) Suppose that
y\in Y
. Then,
\{f^{\nu}_{y},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight and Proposition
26
(c) implies that
\inf f^{\nu}_{y}\to\inf f_{y}
, which is identical to having
\psi^{\nu}(y)\to\psi(y)
. (ii) Suppose that
y\not\in\dom(-\psi)
. Then,
\psi(y)=-\infty
, which is equivalent to having
\inf f_{y}=-\infty
. By Proposition
26
(b),
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits(\inf f_{y}^{\nu})\leq\inf f_{y}=-\infty
and
\inf f_{y}^{\nu}\to\inf f_{y}
.

Comparing the assumption in Theorem
23
(a) with that of Theorem
23
(b), we see that the former requires a check for each
y\in\dom(-\psi)
while the latter only considers a dense subset of such points. But, the former permits
y^{\nu}\neq y
while the latter needs
y^{\nu}=y
in the tightness verification.

The connection between Theorem
23
and perspective (iii), with bifunctions
k^{\nu},k
defined in (
5.1
), is as follows. By
[
49
, Propostion 9.28]
, if
k^{\nu}
lop-converges to
k
ancillary-tightly, then
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
. A close examination of the respective definitions (cf. Def. 9.14, 9.19, and 9.21 in
[
49
]
) reveals that
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
if and only if
k^{\nu}
lop-converges to
k
. However, the tightness assumptions in Theorem
23
(a,b) are slightly weaker than ancillary-tightness, which requires examining all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
; see
[
49
, Def. 9.21]
. The direct proof of Theorem
23
has the merit that it completely bypasses the intricacies of lopsided convergence. Theorem
23
(a) also furnishes additional insight about continuous convergence.

The property
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
together with tightness of
\{-\psi^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
ensure via Proposition
26
(c) that
\sup\psi^{\nu}\to\sup\psi
. If
\sup\psi=\inf f(0,\,\cdot\,)
also holds, then we conclude that the maximum values of the approximating dual functions tend to the minimum value of the actual problem.

It is well-known that maximizers of the dual function
\psi
are subgradients of the min-value function
u\mapsto\inf f(u,\,\cdot\,)
under certain assumptions that typically include convexity of the underlying Rockafellian
f
; see, e.g.,
[
49
, Thm. 5.44]
. The property
\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle h$}}\hskip 7.0pt\psi
, therefore, helps us to confirm that maximizers of
\psi^{\nu}
can only converge to such subgradients.

Even in the convex case, tightness is hard to avoid. If
f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are proper, lsc, and convex Rockafellians, then Wijsman’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
41
, Thm. 11.34]
) ensures that
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
if and only if
f^{\nu*}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf^{*}
. Since
-\psi(y)=f^{*}(y,0)
and
-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})=f^{\nu*}(y^{\nu},0)
, we have as a consequence of
f^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0ptf
that
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\geq-\psi(y)
whenever
y^{\nu}\to y
. This provides “one-half” of the requirement for
-\psi^{\nu}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-10.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle e$}}\hskip 7.0pt-\psi
; see (
2.1
). For the other “half” (
2.2
), we seek for each
y
a sequence
y^{\nu}\to y
such that
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\leq-\psi(y)
or equivalently
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu*}(y^{\nu},0)\leq f^{*}(y,0)
, which is identical to
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(\inf f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}})\geq\inf f_{y}
. We are again compelled to bring in tightness as in Proposition
26
(a).

The truncated Hausdorff distance between
\epi f
and
\epi f^{\nu}
also leads to a bound for the dual problems. We note that
d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\hypo\psi,\hypo\psi^{\nu})
directly quantifies the distance between maximum values and near-maximizers as we see from Theorem
2
after a re-orientation from minimization to maximization.

The bifunction perspective (iii) discussed above can also lead to estimates of
d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\hypo\psi^{\nu},\hypo\psi)
as seen in
[
47
, Sec. 5]
. However, these estimates rely on the pointwise difference between
k^{\nu}
and
k
, Lipschitz properties, and also fail to utilize the specific structure of these bifunctions. Thus, the following bound is tighter and more versatile.

Theorem 24
.

(error bounds for dual functions).

For Rockafellians
f,f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that
\epi f
,
\epi f^{\nu}
,
\dom(-\psi)
, and
\dom(-\psi^{\nu})
are nonempty, and
\rho\in[0,\infty)
. Let
\|\cdot\|_{2}
,
(u,x)\mapsto\max\{\|u\|_{2},\|x\|_{2}\}
,
(y,\alpha)\mapsto\max\{\|y\|_{2},|\alpha|\}
, and
(u,x,\alpha)\mapsto\max\{\|u\|_{2},\|x\|_{2},|\alpha|\}
be the norms on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, respectively. Then, the following hold:

(a)

If
\hat{\rho}>\rho+d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\dom(-\psi),\dom(-\psi^{\nu}))
and
\rho^{\prime}\in[1,\infty)
satisfies

\displaystyle\forall y\in\dom(-\psi)\cap\mathbb{B}(\hat{\rho}):

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\psi(y)\in[-\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\forall y^{\nu}\in\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\cap\mathbb{B}(\hat{\rho}):

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\in[-\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}],

then, provided that
\bar{\rho}\geq\rho^{\prime}(1+\hat{\rho})
, one has

d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\hypo\psi^{\nu},\hypo\psi)\leq(1+\hat{\rho})d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\bar{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})+\rho^{\prime}d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}\big(\dom(-\psi),\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\big).

(b)

If
Y(\rho)=\dom(-\psi)\cap\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho)
is nonempty and
\rho^{\prime}\in[0,\infty)
satisfies for all
y\in Y(\rho)

\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\psi(y),\psi^{\nu}(y)\in[-\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}],

then, provided that
\bar{\rho}\geq\rho^{\prime}(1+\rho)
, one has

\sup_{y\in Y(\rho)}\big|\psi(y)-\psi^{\nu}(y)\big|\leq(1+\rho)d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\bar{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu}).

Proof.
For (a), let
(y^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})\in\hypo\psi^{\nu}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho)
. Then,
y^{\nu}\in\dom(-\psi^{\nu})
. We consider two cases.
First, suppose that
y^{\nu}\in\dom(-\psi)
. Set
y=y^{\nu}
. Since
\hat{\rho}>\rho
, the assumptions establish that

\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho^{\prime})\neq\emptyset,~~~\inf f_{y},\inf f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}\in[-\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}].

(5.3)

We can therefore apply Proposition
2
(a) and establish

\big|\psi(y)-\psi^{\nu}(y^{\nu})\big|=|\inf f_{y}-\inf f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}}|\leq d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho^{\prime}}(\epi f_{y},\epi f^{\nu}_{y^{\nu}})\leq(1+\rho)d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\breve{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu}),

with
\breve{\rho}\geq\rho^{\prime}(1+\rho)
, where the last inequality follows from Theorem
5
(b). Thus,
(y,\min\{\alpha^{\nu},\psi(y)\})\in\hypo\psi
and
|\alpha^{\nu}-\min\{\alpha^{\nu},\psi(y)\}|\leq(1+\rho)d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\breve{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})
. This means that the distance between
(y^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})
and
(y,\min\{\alpha^{\nu},\psi(y)\})
in the chosen norm is at most
(1+\rho)d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\breve{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})
.

Second, suppose that
y^{\nu}\hskip-1.13791pt\not\in\dom(-\psi)
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\hat{\rho}-\rho-d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\dom(-\psi),\dom(-\psi^{\nu}))]
. Since
y^{\nu}\in\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\cap\mathbb{B}(\rho)
, there is
y\in\dom(-\psi)
such that

\|y-y^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}\big(\dom(-\psi),\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\big)+\varepsilon.

Thus,

\displaystyle\leq(1+\hat{\rho})d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\bar{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})+\rho^{\prime}\|y-y^{\nu}\|_{2},

with
\bar{\rho}\geq\rho^{\prime}(1+\hat{\rho})
. Thus,

and, with

\eta_{\varepsilon}=(1+\hat{\rho})d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\bar{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})+\rho^{\prime}d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}\big(\dom(-\psi),\dom(-\psi^{\nu})\big)+\rho^{\prime}\varepsilon,

\big|\alpha^{\nu}-\min\{\alpha^{\nu},\psi(y)\}\big|\leq(1+\hat{\rho})d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\bar{\rho}}(\epi f,\epi f^{\nu})+\rho^{\prime}\|y-y^{\nu}\|_{2}\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}.

\bar{\rho}\geq\breve{\rho}

Moreover,
(y^{\nu},\alpha^{\nu})
is arbitrary and this yields

and

. Since

For (b), let

Proposition 25
.

(composite optimization; tightness).

In the setting of Example

, suppose that
\{h,h^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are proper, lsc, and convex functions with

(b)

Proof.
For (a), suppose that

that
\{(y,v)\,|\,v\in\partial h^{\nu*}(y)\}\,{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\rightarrow$}}\kern-11.0pt\hbox{\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$\,\scriptstyle s$}}\hskip 7.0pt\{(y,v)\,|\,v\in\partial h^{*}(y)\}

v\in\partial h^{*}(y)
and thus also
v^{\nu}\to v
and
y^{\nu}\to y
such that
v^{\nu}\in\partial h^{\nu*}(y^{\nu})
. Moreover, there is a bounded set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
G^{\nu}(x^{\nu})\in D
for all
x^{\nu}\in X^{\nu}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\rho=\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\|v^{\nu}\|_{2}
, which is finite because
\{v^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a convergent sequence. We construct the compact set

.
Let
\{x^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an arbitrary sequence. We consider two cases.

First, suppose that
x^{\nu}\in X^{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, we construct

. Consequently,
u^{\nu}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})
by (
4.2
). Moreover,
\{u^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset B
. This means that

\inf\big\{f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})~\big|~u\in B\big\}\leq\inf f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(\,\cdot\,,x^{\nu})~~~~\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.4)

Second, suppose that
x^{\nu}\not\in X^{\nu}
for one or more
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. For such
\nu
,
f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(u,x^{\nu})=\infty
regardless of
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Thus, (
5.4
) still holds because each side of that inequality is
\infty
for such
\nu
.

Let
\bar{X}=\cl\cup_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}X^{\nu}
and fix
\nu
in (
5.4
). Since
x^{\nu}
is arbitrary, we obtain from that inequality the relation
\inf\{f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}(u,x)~|~u\in B,x\in\bar{X}\}\leq\inf f_{y^{\nu}}^{\nu}
. Since
\nu
is arbitrary, this proves the claim.

For (b), suppose that
y
,
K
, and
\bar{\nu}
are as stipulated in the proposition. By assumption, there is
\{v^{\nu}\in\partial h^{\nu*}(y)\cap K,\nu\geq\bar{\nu}\}
. We can then mimic the proof of (a) and reach the conclusion.

Proposition
25
(a) helps to confirm the assumption of Theorem

(a) because when

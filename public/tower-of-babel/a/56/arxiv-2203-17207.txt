Thresholds have been a central subject in the study of random discrete structures since its initiation by Erdős and Rényi
[
6

7
]
, the study of which has flourished in random graph theory, computer science
[
17
,
23

[

if

when

and
\mu_{p}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow 1
when
p\gg p^{*}
. That

is always an Erdős-Rényi threshold follows from
[
4

, in which it was observed that

\mathcal{F}
admits a threshold in the Erdős-Rényi sense. While much work has been done identifying thresholds for specific properties (see
[

,
14
]
), another intensively studied direction in the study of thresholds is “sharpness” of thresholds: we refer interested readers to
[

,
11
]
.

To emphasize the strength of Theorem
1.1
, we point out that, in
[
16
]
, Kahn and Kalai wrote that “It would probably be more sensible to conjecture
that it is not true.” The expectation threshold is the most naive (and often the easiest) approach to estimating the threshold, and Theorem
1.1
says that for
every
nontrivial increasing property, its threshold is only within a logarithmic factor of this naive estimate. In particular, many important works in this area have been on thresholds for
specific properties,
and Theorem
1.1
easily implies some of those very hard results on the location of thresholds, for example, the appearance of perfect matchings in random
r
-uniform hypergraphs in seminal work of Johansson, Kahn and Vu
[
15
]
, and the appearance of a given bounded degree
spanning tree in a random graph in recent work of Montgomery
[
19
]
(we note that Montgomery shows a stronger “universality” result that above the correct threshold we actually have containment of all bounded degree spanning trees, which is not recoverable from Theorem
1.1
). For more discussion on the significance and applications of this theorem, we refer the readers to
[
8
]
, in which a weaker fractional relaxation of Theorem
1.1
was proved.

Two recent breakthroughs related to Theorem
1.1
are the significant progress on the Erdős-Rado sunflower conjecture by Alweiss, Lovett, Wu and Zhang
[

and the resolution of the fractional Kahn-Kalai conjecture by Frankston, Kahn, Narayanan and the first author

, the authors observed the relevance of the developments in
[

and a weaker version of Theorem

There is a universal

Our proof of Theorem

Importantly, even to address the weaker fractional relaxation version of these conjectures and questions of Talagrand, we need to use the full strength of the ideas in this paper as well as additional ideas in

Before going through the proof in detail, we first give an informal overview of our strategy. A hypergraph

towards the last iterations). We will show that there is a sub-hypergraph

of

that admits a cover

which has small cost with high probability, as well as a hypergraph

with the following key properties:

(\bigcup_{j\leq i}W_{j})\cup S_{i}\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle\,\,\textrm{for all }S_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i},\textrm{ and}

The choices of

and

. In the

is empty, or

and a cover

Proposition 2.3
.

We have that either
W\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle
or
\mathcal{U}:=\bigcup_{i\leq\gamma}\mathcal{U}(W_{i})
covers
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Note that
\ell_{\gamma}<1
, hence either
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\emptyset
or
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\{\emptyset\}
.

In the former case (
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\emptyset
), we show that
\mathcal{U}
covers
\mathcal{H}
. Indeed, for
S\in\mathcal{H}
, let
S=S_{0},S_{1},S_{2},\ldots
(
S_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
) be the evolution of
S
in the iteration process, i.e.,
S_{i}:=T(S_{i-1},W_{i})
. In each iteration, either
S_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}
and is covered by
\mathcal{U}_{i}
, or otherwise,
S_{i+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\emptyset
, there exists
j<\gamma
for which
S_{j}\in\mathcal{G}_{j}
. Hence,
S
is covered by
\mathcal{U}
.

In the latter case (
\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}=\{\emptyset\}
), we show that
W\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle
. Indeed, by Property (
9
),
W_{1}\cup\ldots\cup W_{\gamma}\cup\emptyset\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle
, and hence
W\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
\mathcal{U}
covers
\mathcal{H}
. By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\mathbb{P}(W\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle)+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1;

equivalently,

\mathbb{P}(W\in\langle\mathcal{H}\rangle)\geq 1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}).

To bound
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
, note that the expected “cost” for the cover
\mathcal{U}
is

(20)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}(W)}p^{|U|}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{3.1}}}{{<}}\sum_{i\leq{\gamma}}L_{i}^{-0.8\ell_{i}}&=\sum_{i<\gamma-\sqrt{\log_{0.9}(1/\ell)}}L_{i}^{-0.8\ell_{i}}+\sum_{i=\gamma-\sqrt{\log_{0.9}(1/\ell)}}^{\gamma}L_{i}^{-0.8\ell_{i}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{gamma1},\eqref{i.bound}}}{{\leq}}2L^{-0.8\exp(c\sqrt{\log\ell})}+O((L\sqrt{\log\ell})^{-c^{\prime}})=(\log\ell)^{-c^{\prime\prime}}\end{split}

for some constant
c^{\prime},c^{\prime\prime}>0
. Also note that, by the assumption that
\mathcal{H}
is not
p
-small,

(21)

if
\mathcal{E}
occurs, then
\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}(W)}p^{|U|}>1/2
.

Therefore, by combining (
21
) and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}(W)}p^{|U|}>1/2\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star)}}{{\leq}}2\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}(W)}p^{|U|}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{exp.cost}}}{{=}}(\log\ell)^{-c^{\prime\prime}}=o_{\ell\to\infty}(1).

where
(\star)
follows from Markov’s Inequality. This gives the desired conclusion.

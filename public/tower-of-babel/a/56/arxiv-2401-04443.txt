Transposed Poisson structures on solvable Lie algebras with filiform nilradical
Kobiljon Abdurasulov, Jobir Adashev and Sabohat Eshmeteva

Abstract
In this article, we describe 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -derivations of solvable Lie algebras with a filiform nilradical. Nontrivial transposed Poisson algebras with solvable Lie algebras are constructed. That is, by using 1 2 \frac{1}{2} -derivations of Lie algebras, we have established commutative associative multiplication to construct a transposed Poisson algebra with an associated given Lie algebra.

\frac{1}{2}
-derivation.

A transposed Poisson algebra defined this way not only shares some properties of a Poisson algebra, such as the closedness under tensor products and the Koszul self-duality as an operad but also admits a rich class of identities

1
Preliminaries

In this section, we present the concepts and known results.
All the algebras we present in this section are given over the field
\mathbb{C}
unless otherwise stated.

We first recall the definition of a Poisson algebra.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a vector space equipped with two bilinear operations

\cdot,\;[-,-]:\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}.

The triple
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot,[-,-])
is called a

Poisson algebra
if
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and

(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra which satisfies the compatibility condition

[x,y\cdot z]=[x,y]\cdot z+y\cdot[x,z].

(1)

Eq. (
1
) is called the
Leibniz
rule
since the adjoint operators of the Lie algebra are
derivations of the commutative associative algebra.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a vector space equipped with two bilinear operations

\cdot,\;[-,-]:\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}.

The triple
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot,[-,-])
is called a
transposed Poisson algebra
if
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and
(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra which satisfies the following compatibility condition

2z\cdot[x,y]=[z\cdot x,y]+[x,z\cdot y].

(2)

Eq. (
2
) is called the
transposed
Leibniz rule
because the roles played by the two binary operations in the Leibniz rule in a Poisson algebra are switched. Further, the resulting operation is rescaled by introducing a factor 2 on the left-hand side.

Transposed Poisson algebras were first introduced in a paper by Bai, Bai, Guo and Wu
[
2
]
. A transposed Poisson structure
\cdot
on
\mathfrak{L}
is called
trivial
, if
x\cdot y=0
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
.

The next result shows that the
compatibility relations of the Poisson algebra and those of the Transposed Poisson algebra are independent in the following sense.

Proposition 1.3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot,[-,-])
is both a
Poisson algebra and a transposed Poisson algebra if and only
if

x\cdot[y,z]=[x\cdot y,z]=0.

(3)

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
be an algebra with a multiplication
[-,-],
and
\varphi
be
a bilinear map. Then
\varphi
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-
derivation
if it satisfies:

\varphi([x,y])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(x),y]+[x,\varphi(y)]).

(4)

Observe that
\frac{1}{2}
-derivations are a particular case of
\delta
-derivations introduced by Filippov in
[
7
]

(see,
[
11
,
12
]
and references therein). It is easy to see from Definition
1.4
that
[\mathfrak{L},\mathfrak{L}]
and
Ann(\mathfrak{L})
are invariant under any
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of
\mathfrak{L}
. Definitions
1.2
and
1.4
immediately implies the following key Lemma.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
be a Lie algebra and
\cdot
a new binary (bilinear) operation on
\mathfrak{L}
. Then

(\mathfrak{L},\cdot,[-,-])
is a transposed Poisson algebra if and only if
\cdot
is commutative and associative and for
every
z\in\mathfrak{L}
the multiplication by
z
in
(\mathfrak{L},\cdot)
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation of
(\mathfrak{L},[-,-])
.

Definition 1.6
.

An
n
-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}
is said to be
filiform
if
\dim\mathfrak{L}^{i}=n-i
, for
2\leq i\leq n
.

Now let us define a natural gradation for the nilpotent Lie algebras.

Definition 1.7
.

Given a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}
with nilindex
s
, put
\mathfrak{L}_{i}=\mathfrak{L}^{i}/\mathfrak{L}^{i+1},\ 1\leq i\leq s-1
, and
Gr(\mathfrak{L})=\mathfrak{L}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{L}_{2}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{L}_{s-1}
.
Define the product in the vector space
Gr(\mathfrak{L})
as follows:

[x+\mathfrak{L}^{i+1},y+\mathfrak{L}^{j+1}]:=[x,y]+\mathfrak{L}^{i+j+1},

where
x\in\mathfrak{L}^{i}\setminus\mathfrak{L}^{i+1},

y\in\mathfrak{L}^{j}\setminus\mathfrak{L}^{j+1}.
Then
[\mathfrak{L}_{i},\mathfrak{L}_{j}]\subseteq\mathfrak{L}_{i+j}
and we obtain the graded algebra
Gr(\mathfrak{L})
. If
Gr(\mathfrak{L})
and
\mathfrak{L}
are isomorphic, then we say that the algebra
\mathfrak{L}
is
naturally graded
.

It is well known that there are two types of naturally graded filiform Lie algebras. In fact, the second type will appear only in the case when the dimension of the algebra is even.

Theorem 1.8

(
[
26
]
)
.

Any naturally graded filiform Lie algebra is isomorphic to one of the following non isomorphic algebras:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathfrak{n}_{n,1}:&[e_{i},e_{1}]=e_{i+1},\quad 2\leq i\leq n-1;\\[2.84526pt]
\mathfrak{Q}_{2n}:&[e_{i},e_{1}]=e_{i+1},\ 2\leq i\leq 2n-2,\quad[e_{i},e_{2n+1-i}]=(-1)^{i}e_{2n},\ 2\leq i\leq n.\end{array}

All solvable Lie algebras whose nilradical is the naturally graded filiform Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{n,1}
are classified in
[
25
]
(
n\geq 4
). Furthermore, solvable Lie algebras whose nilradical is the naturally graded filiform Lie algebra
\mathfrak{Q}_{2n}
are classified in
[
1
]
.
It is proved that the dimension of a solvable Lie algebra whose nilradical is isomorphic to an
n
-dimensional naturally graded filiform Lie algebra is not greater than
n+2
.

2

-derivations of solvable Lie algebras with filiform nilradical

In this section, we calculate
\frac{1}{2}

Theorem 2.1
.

Any
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation
\varphi
of the algebra
\mathfrak{s}^{1}_{n,1}(\beta)

for

\varphi(e_{1})=\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{2}e_{2}+\alpha_{3}e_{3}+\alpha_{4}e_{4},\ \varphi(e_{2})=\alpha_{1}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3}+\beta_{4}e_{n},\ \varphi(e_{3})=\alpha_{1}e_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{3}e_{4},

(\beta-2)\alpha_{2}=(\beta-2)\beta_{3}=(2-\beta)\beta_{4}=0;

Proof 2.2
.

It is easy to see that from the multiplication table of the algebra
\mathfrak{s}^{1}_{n,1}(\beta)

and

are the generator

-derivation.

Now consider the condition of
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation for the elements
e_{1}
and
e_{2}:

\varphi(e_{3})=\varphi([e_{2},e_{1}])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{2}),e_{1}]+[e_{2},\varphi(e_{1})])=

=\frac{1}{2}([\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}e_{i}+\beta_{n+1}x,e_{1}]+[e_{2},\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}+\alpha_{n+1}x])

Now consider the condition of
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation for the elements
e_{1}
and

\varphi([e_{1},x])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{1}),x]+[e_{1},\varphi(x)])=\frac{1}{2}([\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}+\alpha_{n+1}x,x]+[e_{1},\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}e_{i}+\delta_{n+1}x])

On the other hand

\varphi([e_{1},x])=\varphi(e_{1})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}+\alpha_{n+1}x.

Comparing coefficients of the basis elements we obtain that

-derivation for the elements

and

\varphi([e_{3},x])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{3}),x]+[e_{3},\varphi(x)])

On the other hand

We have the following

\varphi(e_{1})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i},\ \varphi(e_{2})=\beta_{1}e_{1}+\alpha_{1}e_{2}+\beta_{3}e_{3}+\beta_{n}e_{n}+\beta_{n+1}x,

\varphi(e_{3})=\frac{1}{2}(-\beta_{n+1}e_{1}+2\alpha_{1}e_{3}+\beta_{3}e_{4}),\ \varphi(x)=\beta_{3}e_{1}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n-1}(i-3+\beta)\alpha_{i+1}e_{i}+\delta_{n}e_{n}+\alpha_{1}x.

Using property of the
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation for the product

-derivation for the elements
e_{i},e_{1}:

\varphi(e_{i+1})=\varphi([e_{i},e_{1}])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{i}),e_{1}]+[e_{i},\varphi(e_{1})])

=\frac{1}{2}\Big([\alpha_{1}e_{i}+\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{2}\alpha_{2n+2-i}e_{2n},e_{1}]+[e_{i},\alpha_{1}e_{1}+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{2n-1}\alpha_{i}e_{i}]\Big)

=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\alpha_{1}e_{i+1}+\alpha_{1}e_{i+1}+(-1)^{i}\alpha_{2n+1-i}e_{2n})\Big)=\alpha_{1}e_{i+1}+\frac{(-1)^{i}}{2}\alpha_{2n+1-i}e_{2n}

and

\varphi(e_{2n})=\varphi([e_{2},e_{2n-1}])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{2}),e_{2n-1}]+[e_{2},\varphi(e_{2n-1})])

=\frac{1}{2}\Big([\alpha_{1}e_{2}+\beta_{2n}e_{2n},e_{2n-1}]+[e_{2},\alpha_{1}e_{2n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{3}e_{2n}]\Big)=\alpha_{1}e_{2n}.

From the
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation property (
1.4
) for
3\leq i\leq 2n-1
we have

\varphi([e_{i},x_{2}])=\frac{1}{2}([\varphi(e_{i}),x_{2}]+[e_{i},\varphi(x_{2})])

=\frac{1}{2}\Big([\alpha_{1}e_{i}+\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{2}\alpha_{2n+2-i}e_{2n},x_{2}]+[e_{i},\sum\limits_{i=2}^{2n-2}\alpha_{i+1}e_{i}+\gamma_{2n}e_{2n}+\alpha_{1}x_{2}]\Big)=

=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\alpha_{1}e_{i}+(-1)^{i-1}\alpha_{2n+2-i}e_{2n}+(-1)^{i}\alpha_{2n+2-i}e_{2n}+\alpha_{1}e_{i}\Big).

On the other hand

\varphi([e_{i},x])=\varphi(e_{i})=\alpha_{1}e_{i}+\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{2}\alpha_{2n+2-i}e_{2n}.

Comparing coefficients of the basis elements we obtain that

\alpha_{i}=0,\ 3\leq i\leq 2n-1.

By using the property of
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation for the product
[x_{1},x_{2}]=0
, we have

2\delta_{2n}=(2n+1)\gamma_{2n}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

{\bf TP}(\mathfrak{r}_{2n+2}):\ x_{1}\cdot x_{1}=(2n+1)^{2}e_{2n},\ x_{1}\cdot x_{2}=2(2n+1)e_{2n},\ x_{2}\cdot x_{2}=4e_{2n},

where it is taken into account that the transposed Poisson algebra has its products with respect to the bracket
[-,-]
, and the remaining products are equal to zero.

Proof 3.22
.

Let
(\mathfrak{r}_{2n+2},\cdot,[-,-])
be a transposed Poisson algebra structure defined on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}_{2n+2}
. Then according to Lemma
1.5
for each element of
x\in\mathfrak{r}_{2n+2}
the linear operator
\varphi_{x}(y)=x\cdot y
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-derivation. Hence, by using Theorem
2.15
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
we can put

\begin{array}[]{lll}\varphi_{e_{i}}(e_{j})=a_{i}e_{j},&\varphi_{e_{i}}(x_{1})=(2n+1)b_{i}e_{2n}+a_{i}x_{1},&\varphi_{e_{i}}(x_{2})=2b_{i}e_{2n}+a_{i}x_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\varphi_{x_{1}}(e_{j})=a_{x_{1}}e_{j},&\varphi_{x_{1}}(x_{1})=(2n+1)b_{x_{1}}e_{2n}+a_{x_{1}}x_{1},&\varphi_{x_{1}}(x_{2})=2b_{x_{1}}e_{2n}+a_{x_{1}}x_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\varphi_{x_{2}}(e_{j})=a_{x_{2}}e_{j},&\varphi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})=(2n+1)b_{x_{2}}e_{2n}+a_{x_{2}}x_{1},&\varphi_{x_{2}}(x_{2})=2b_{x_{2}}e_{2n}+a_{x_{2}}x_{2}.\\[2.84526pt]
\end{array}

By checking the equalities of
\varphi_{e_{i}}(e_{j})=e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=e_{j}\cdot e_{i}=\varphi_{e_{j}}(e_{i}),\ \varphi_{e_{i}}(x_{k})=e_{i}\cdot x_{k}=x_{k}\cdot e_{i}=\varphi_{x_{k}}(e_{i})
and
\varphi_{x_{1}}(x_{2})=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}=x_{2}\cdot x_{1}=\varphi_{x_{2}}(x_{1})
for all
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,2n\}
and
k\in\{1,2\},
we have the following restrictions:

a_{t}=0,\ a_{x_{1}}=0,\ a_{x_{2}}=0,\ b_{t}=0,\ 1\leq t\leq 2n,\ 2b_{x_{1}}=(2n+1)b_{x_{2}}.

Thus, we obtain

x_{1}\cdot x_{1}=(2n+1)^{2}\alpha e_{2n},\ x_{1}\cdot x_{2}=2(2n+1)\alpha e_{2n},\ x_{2}\cdot x_{2}=4\alpha e_{2n}.

We have non-trivial transposed Poisson algebra structures only in the case when
\alpha\neq 0
. Further, by using the transformation

\phi(x)=x,\ \phi(e_{1})=e_{1},\ \phi(e_{i})=\sqrt{\alpha^{-1}}e_{i},\ 2\leq i\leq 2n-1,\ \phi(e_{2n})=\alpha^{-1}e_{2n},

we get the algebra
{\bf TP}(\mathfrak{r}_{2n+2})
.

Wavefront Sets of Unipotent Representations of Reductive p p -adic Groups I
Dan Ciubotaru, Lucas Mason-Brown, Emile Okada

Abstract
. The wavefront set is a fundamental invariant arising from the Harish-Chandra-Howe local character expansion of an admissible representation. We prove a precise formula for the wavefront set of an irreducible Iwahori-spherical representation with ‘real infinitesimal character’ and determine a lower bound for this invariant in terms of the Deligne-Langlands-Lusztig parameters. In particular, for the Iwahori-spherical representations with real infinitesimal character, we deduce that the algebraic wavefront set is a singleton, as conjectured by Mœglin and Waldspurger. As a corollary, we obtain an explicit description of the wavefront set of an irreducible spherical representation with real Satake parameter.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The local Langlands classification

We begin with a brief overview of the local Langlands classification.
Let
\mathsf{k}
be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic
0
with ring of integers
\mathfrak{o}
, finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of cardinality
q
and valuation
\mathsf{val}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Fix an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
of
\mathsf{k}
and let
K\subset\bar{\mathsf{k}}
be the maximal unramified extension of
\mathsf{k}
in
\bar{\mathsf{k}}
.

Let
W_{\mathsf{k}}
be the Weil group of
\mathsf{k}

[
Tat79
, (1.4)]
. Let
\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})
be the group of
\mathsf{k}
-rational points of a connected reductive algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
defined and split over
\mathsf{k}
. Let
G^{\vee}
denote the complex Langlands dual group associated to
\mathbf{G}
, see
[
Bor79
, §2.1]
. (Since we assume throughout that
\mathbf{G}
is
\mathsf{k}
-split, we can work with
G^{\vee}
in place of the L-group.) Let
W_{\mathsf{k}}^{\prime}=W_{\mathsf{k}}\ltimes\mathbb{C}
denote the Weil-Deligne group associated to
\mathsf{k}

[
Del73
, §8.3.6]
.
The semidirect product is defined via the action

wxw^{-1}=\|w\|x,\qquad x\in\mathbb{C},\ w\in W_{k},

where
\|w\|
is the norm of
w\in W_{k}
.

Definition 1.1.1
.

A
Langlands parameter
is a continuous homomorphism

(1.1.1)

\phi:W_{\mathsf{k}}^{\prime}\rightarrow G^{\vee}

which respects the Jordan decompositions in
W_{\mathsf{k}}^{\prime}
and
G^{\vee}
(
[
Bor79
, §8.1]
).

For any Langlands parameter
\phi
, let
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lan}}_{\phi}(\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k}))
denote the associated
L
-packet of irreducible admissible
\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})
-representations
[
Bor79
, §10]
. The
L
-packets have not yet been defined in general. A discussion of this problem is beyond the scope of this paper—we refer the reader to
[
Vog93
]
,
[
Art13
]
, or
[
Kal22
]
.

Of particular interest for us is the following special case. We say that a Langlands parameter is
unramified
if it is trivial when restricted to the inertia subgroup
I_{\mathsf{k}}
of
W_{\mathsf{k}}
. If we fix a generator
\mathsf{Fr}
of the infinite cyclic group
W_{\mathsf{k}}/I_{\mathsf{k}}
, we get a bijection between the set of unramified Langlands parameters and pairs of the form

(1.1.2)

(s,u)\in G^{\vee}\times G^{\vee},\qquad s\text{ semisimple},\ u\text{ unipotent},\ sus^{-1}=u^{q}.

This bijection is defined by
\phi\mapsto(s,u)=(\phi(\mathsf{Fr}),\phi(1))
, where
1\in\mathbb{C}\subset W^{\prime}_{\mathsf{k}}
. Replacing
u
with
n\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
such that
q^{n}=u
, we get a further bijection onto pairs of the form

(1.1.3)

(s,n)\in G^{\vee}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},\qquad s\text{ semisimple},\ n\text{ nilpotent},\ \Ad(s)n=qn.

If
\phi
is an unramified Langlands parameter which corresponds to the pair
(s,n)
, the
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi}^{\mathsf{Lan}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is in bijection with the set of irreducible representations
\rho
of the component group
A(s,n)
of the mutual centralizer in
G^{\vee}
of
s
and
n
, such that the center
Z(G^{\vee})
acts trivially on
\rho
. This is known as the Deligne-Langlands-Lusztig correspondence, see Theorem
2.5.2
below.

Of fundamental importance are the
tempered
Langlands parameters. For unramified parameters, ‘tempered’ means that the semisimple element
s
in (
1.1.2
) is ‘relatively compact’, see Theorem
2.5.2
(1) for a more precise condition.

1.2.
Unipotent representations: wavefront sets

The main result of the paper is a formula for the wavefront set in terms of the Langlands parameters for the class of representations with
unipotent cuspidal support
introduced by Lusztig
[
Lus95
]
(see Section
2.5
for the precise definition). In terms of the Langlands correspondence, see Section
1.1
, these are exactly the representations which correspond to unramified Langlands parameters. Hence, via (
1.1.3
), the set of irreducible representations with unipotent cuspidal support is in bijection with
G^{\vee}
-conjugacy classes of triples
(s,n,\rho)
. Write
X(s,n,\rho)
for the irreducible representation corresponding to
(s,n,\rho)
. Since triples are considered up to conjugation by
G^{\vee}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
s
belongs to a fixed maximal torus
T^{\vee}
of
G^{\vee}
. There is a polar decomposition

T^{\vee}=T_{c}^{\vee}T_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
,
where
T^{\vee}_{c}
is compact and
T^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a vector group, see (
2.0.1
). We say that
s
is
real
if
s\in T^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and in this case, we say that
X(s,n,\rho)
has
real infinitesimal character
.

As is well-known, the category of smooth
{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})
-representation decomposes as a product of full subcategories, called
Bernstein blocks
, see
[
BD84
, (2.10)]
. The category of representations with unipotent cuspidal support is a finite product of Bernstein blocks. The block containing the trivial representation (known as the
principal block
) is precisely the category of
Iwahori-spherical representations
(i.e. smooth representations generated by their Iwahori-fixed vectors). Under the Deligne-Langlands-Lusztig correspondence, the irreducible Iwahori-spherical representations correspond to the triples
(s,n,\rho)
for which
\rho
is of
Springer type
. This means that
\rho
occurs in the permutation representation of
A(s,n)
in the top cohomology group of the variety of Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
that contain
n
and are invariant under
\mathrm{Ad}(s)
. Notably, the set of irreducible representations with unipotent cuspidal support is the smallest class of representations which is a union of
L
-packets and contains all Iwahori-spherical representations, see Theorem
2.5.2
.

An important role will be played by the
Aubert-Zelevinsky
involution
[
Aub95
]
, denoted
X\mapsto\mathrm{AZ}(X)
, see Section
2.7
. This is an involution on the Grothendieck group of finite-length smooth
\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}})
-representations which preserves the Grothendieck group of each Bernstein block and carries irreducibles to irreducibles (up to a sign). In the case of representations with unipotent cuspidal support, it preserves the semisimple parameter
s
. For example,
\mathrm{AZ}
takes the trivial representation to the Steinberg discrete series representation, and more generally, a spherical representation to the unique generic representation (in the sense of admitting Whittaker models) with the same semisimple parameter.

A fundamental invariant attached to an admissible representation
X
is its
wavefront set
. In its classical form, the wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}(X)
of
X
is a collection of nilpotent
{\mathbf{G}}({\mathsf{k}})
-orbits in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathsf{k}})
: these are the maximal orbits for which the Fourier transforms of the corresponding orbital integrals contribute to the Harish-Chandra-Howe local character expansion of the distribution character of
X
,
[
Har99
, Theorem 16.2]
. In this paper, we consider two coarser invariants (see Section
2.8
for the precise definitions). The first of these invariants is the
algebraic wavefront set
, denoted
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
. This is a collection of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}({\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
, see for example
[
Wal18
, p. 1108]
(where it is simply referred to as the ‘wavefront set’ of
X
). The second invariant is a natural refinement
{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)
of
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
called the
canonical unramified wavefront set
, defined recently in
[
Oka21
]
. This is a collection of nilpotent orbits
\mathfrak{g}(K)
(modulo a certain equivalence relation
\sim_{A}
). The relationship between these three invariants is as follows: the algebraic wavefront set
\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)
is deducible from the usual wavefront set
\mathrm{WF}(X)
as well as the canonical unramified wavefront set
{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)
. It is not known whether the canonical unramified wavefront set is deducible from the usual wavefront set, but we expect this to be the case, see
[
Oka21
, Section 5.1]
for a careful discussion of this topic.

The final ingredient that we need in order to state our main result is the duality map
d
defined by Spaltenstein in
[
Spa82
, Proposition 10.3]
(see also
[
Lus84
, §13.3]
and
[
BV85
, Appendix A]
), and its refinement
d_{A}
due to Achar
[
Ach03
]
. Suppose
G
is the complex reductive group with dual
G^{\vee}
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{o}
be the set of nilpotent orbits in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and let
\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
be the set of pairs
(\mathbb{O},\bar{C})
consisting of a nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{o}
and a conjugacy class
\bar{C}
in
\bar{A}(\mathbb{O})
, Lusztig’s canonical quotient of the
G
-equivariant fundamental group
A(\mathbb{O})
of
\mathbb{O}
. Let
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,\bar{c}}
be the corresponding sets for
G^{\vee}
. The duality
d
is a map
d:\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o}
whose image consists of Lusztig’s special orbits. The duality
d_{A}
is a map

d_{A}:\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{o,\bar{c}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}

satisfying certain properties, see section
2.1
. One of these properties is:

d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)=(d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}),\bar{C}^{\prime}),

where
\bar{C}^{\prime}
is a conjugacy class in
\bar{A}(d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}))
(which is trivial in the case when
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
is special in the sense of Lusztig).

In
[
Oka21
, Section 5.1]
, it is shown that there is bijection between
\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
and the set of
\sim_{A}
-classes of unramified nilpotent orbits
\mathcal{N}_{o}(K)
of
{\mathbf{G}}(K)
. This is recalled in section
2.1
. This way, we can think of
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},\bar{C})
as a class in
\mathcal{N}_{o}(K)/\sim_{A}
.

Theorem 1.2.1

(See Theorems
3.0.8
,
3.0.10
below)
.

Let
X=X(s,n,\rho)
be an irreducible smooth Iwahori-spherical
{\mathbf{G}}(\mathsf{k})
-representation with real infinitesimal character and let
\mathsf{AZ}(X)=X(s,n^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
.

(1)

The canonical unramified wavefront set
{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)
is a singleton, and

{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)},1),

where
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)}
is the
G^{\vee}
-orbit of
n^{\prime}
and
d_{A}
is the duality map defined by Achar. In particular,

\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{\mathsf{AZ}(X)}).

(2)

Suppose
X=X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n,\rho)
where
h^{\vee}
is the neutral element of a Lie triple attached to a nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. Then

d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)\leq_{A}\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X),

where
\leq_{A}
is the partial order defined by Achar. In particular,

d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})\leq\hphantom{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X).

The proof relies on the local character expansion ideas of Barbasch and Moy
[
BM97
]
and the subsequent refinements of the third-named author
[
Oka21
]
, together with a detailed analysis of the Springer correspondence, nilpotent orbits, and the combinatorics of the Achar duality.

We believe that Theorem
1.2.1
should remain true for all irreducible representations with unipotent cuspidal support with real infinitesimal character. However, the combinatorics in the more general case are more involved as they rely on the generalized Springer correspondence; moreover, in order to obtain the necessary branching results to parahoric subgroups, one needs to use the correspondences with Lusztig’s graded affine Hecke algebras, and this adds another layer of complexity.
Additionally, there are examples that show that the statement of Theorem
1.2.1
(1) does not hold if
s
is not real. We intend to address these generalizations in future work.

There is a vast literature on the computation of the (mainly algebraic) wavefront set of representations of
p
-adic groups. We mention only two examples related to the unipotent representations. In
[
MW87
]
, Mœglin and Waldspurger introduced the notion of generalized Whittaker models, which they used to compute the wavefront sets for representations of
\mathrm{GL}(n)
, small-rank (in the sense of Howe) representations of
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
, and, in the notation of Theorem
1.2.1
, the case
\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}=\rho
, the infinitesimal character where the trivial
\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})
-representation occurs. For
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{SO}(2n+1)
, Waldspurger has computed the algebraic wavefront sets of all irreducible tempered representations with unipotent cuspidal support
[
Wal20
, Théorème 2]
and of their
\mathsf{AZ}
-duals
[
Wal18
, Théorème, p.1108]
.

To use Theorem
1.2.1
as a tool for computing wavefront sets, we need an algorithm for computing the
\mathsf{AZ}
-dual of an irreducible representation.
For example, if
X
is an irreducible spherical representation (in the sense of having nonzero fixed vectors under the action of
\mathbf{G}(\mathfrak{o})
) with Satake parameter
s
), then
\mathsf{AZ}(X)=X(s,n^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
admits nonzero Whittaker models, and it is known that this is equivalent to the condition that
G^{\vee}(s)n^{\prime}
is the unique open orbit in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
(section
2.5
). Denote
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{s}=G^{\vee}n^{\prime}
, i.e., the
G^{\vee}
-saturation of the open
G^{\vee}(s)
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
. Then an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2.1
is
the following.

Corollary 1.2.2
.

The algebraic wavefront set of the irreducible spherical
\mathbf{G}(\mathsf{k})
-representation
X(s)
with real Satake parameter
s
is

\hphantom{}{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X(s))=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{s}).

While this result was expected for a long time (by analogy with real groups for example), as far as we know, this is the first proof for all split
p
-adic groups.

In general, it is a difficult problem to compute the
\mathsf{AZ}
-dual. Evens and Mirković
[
EM97
, Theorem 0.1]
proved that for Iwahori-spherical representations,
\mathsf{AZ}
admits a geometric description in terms of the Fourier-Deligne transform on irreducible perverse sheaves which is computable in principle via the algorithms in
[
Lus10
, §2]
. For
{\mathbf{G}}=\mathrm{GL}(n)
, an algorithm involving Zelevinsky multisegments was given in
[
MW86
]
.
For split symplectic and odd orthogonal groups, an explicit combinatorial algorithm for computing
\mathsf{AZ}
was recently announced in
[
AM20
]
. We also mention the recent progress by Waldspurger
[
Wal19
]
on the generalized Springer correspondence for
\mathrm{Sp}(2n)
which provides another perspective on
\mathsf{AZ}
-duality (more in line with our methods in this paper).

In a sequel to this paper, we will use the wavefront set results in order to give a new characterization of the anti-tempered unipotent Arthur packets and a
p
-adic analogue of the weak Arthur packets defined for real groups in
[
ABV92
]
.

2.1.
Nilpotent orbits

Let
\mathcal{N}

{\mathbf{G}}(F)
, see
[
DeB02
, Section 2]
.
For
F
algebraically closed this coincides with all the usual notions of nilpotence.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{o}
be the functor which takes
F
to the set of orbits in
\mathcal{N}(F)
under the adjoint action of
{\mathbf{G}}(F)
.
When
F
is
{\mathsf{k}}
or
K
, we view
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F)
as a partially ordered set with respect to the closure ordering in the topology induced by the topology on
F
.
When
F
is algebraically closed, we view
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F)

For brevity we will write
\mathcal{N}(F^{\prime}/F)
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F^{\prime}/F)
) for
\mathcal{N}(F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}F^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}F^{\prime})
) where

is a morphism of fields.
For
(F,F^{\prime})=({\mathsf{k}},K)
(resp.
({\mathsf{k}},{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
,
(K,{\bar{\mathsf{k}}})
), the map
\mathcal{N}_{o}(F^{\prime}/F)
is strictly increasing (resp. strictly increasing, non-decreasing).
We will simply write
\mathcal{N}
for
\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{N}_{o}
for
\mathcal{N}_{o}(\mathbb{C})
.
In this case we also define
\mathcal{N}_{o,c}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
) to be the set of all pairs

(resp. Lusztig’s canonical quotient

[

\mathfrak{Q}:\mathcal{N}_{o,c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}},\qquad(\mathbb{O},C)\mapsto(\mathbb{O},\bar{C})

Lemma 2.1.2
.

Let

Then the following are true:

(1)

\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}\leq\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
if and only if
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1)\geq_{A}d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1)
,

(2)

\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
if and only if
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1)=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1)
.

Proof.

(1)

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}\leq\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
.
Then
d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1})\geq d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2})
.
So we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{1}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1))

\displaystyle=d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1})\geq d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2})=\mathrm{pr}_{1}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1))

\displaystyle d_{S}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}\leq\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}=d_{S}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1)).

Thus indeed
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1)\geq_{A}d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1)

(\Leftarrow)
If
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1)\geq_{A}d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1)
then
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}=d_{S}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},1))\leq d_{S}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2},1))=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
.

(2)

This follows from part (i).

3.
Computation of wavefront sets

Our main result is a general formula for the (canonical unramified) wavefront set of an irreducible Iwahori-spherical representation with real infinitesimal character. To prove this result, it will be convenient to define the
wavefront set
of a Weyl group representation (analogous to the invariant

2.1
and

\mathrm{WF}_{J}(E)=\max\{\overline{\mathbb{L}}(J,\mathbb{O}(\phi(E^{\prime}),\mathbb{C}))\mid E^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}(W_{J}),\ \Hom(E^{\prime},E|_{W_{J}})\neq 0\}\subset\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}.

Definition 3.0.1
.

The
wavefront set
of

is

\mathrm{WF}(E)=\max\{\mathrm{WF}_{J}(E)\mid J\subsetneq\tilde{\Delta}\}\subset\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}.

Lemma 3.0.2
.

Let

and
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E,\mathbb{C})
. Then for any

we have that

(3.0.1)

\mathrm{WF}_{J}(E)\leq_{A}d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1).

Proof.

If
E^{\prime}
is an irreducible constituent of

, then by
[
AA07
, Proposition 4.3]
we have that
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\leq d_{S}(\mathbb{L}(J,\mathbb{O}(\phi(E^{\prime}),\mathbb{C})))

Thus for all

and so by
[

.
∎

We now introduce the notion of a
faithful nilpotent orbit

.

Definition 3.0.3
.

Let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent orbit. We say that
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}

faithful
if there is a pair
(J,\phi)\in\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\Delta}}
such that

\overline{\mathbb{L}}(J,\mathbb{O}(\phi))=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)

(ii)

If

W

such that

\Hom_{W_{J}}(F,E|_{W_{J}})\neq 0
.

Theorem 3.0.4
.

Every nilpotent orbit
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\vee}
is faithful.

The proof of Theorem
3.0.4
will be postponed until Section
4
. Using this result, and Lemma
3.0.2
, we can compute the the wavefront set of any irreducible
W
-representation.

Corollary 3.0.5
.

Let
E\in\mathrm{Irr}(W)
. Then

\mathrm{WF}(E)=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E,\mathbb{C}),1).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.0.2
,
\mathrm{WF}(E)
is bounded above by

. It suffices to show that this bound is achieved.
For this let
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E,\mathbb{C})

By Theorem

\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
is faithful. So there is a pair
(J,\phi)\in\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\Delta}}
such that

and there is a representation

But since

∎

\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
be a nilpotent orbit. Choose an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple
(e^{\vee},f^{\vee},h^{\vee})
with
e^{\vee}\in\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
. The semisimple operator
\ad(h^{\vee})
induces a Lie algebra grading

\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[n],\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[n]:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\mid[h^{\vee},x]=nx\}.

Write
L^{\vee}
for the connected (Levi) subgroup corresponding to the centralizer
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{0}
of
h^{\vee}
. If we set
s:=q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}
, then

L^{\vee}=G^{\vee}(s),\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]=\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}

where
G^{\vee}(s)
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{q}
are as defined in Section
2.5
. Note that
L^{\vee}
acts by conjugation on each
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[n]
, and in particular on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]
. We will need the following well-known facts, see
[
Kos59
, Section 4]
or
[
Lus95b
, Prop 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.0.9
.

The following are true:

(i)

L^{\vee}e^{\vee}
is an open subset of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]
(and hence the unique open
L^{\vee}
-orbit therein).

(ii)

L^{\vee}e=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]
.

(iii)

G^{\vee}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}[2]\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}^{\vee}}
.

Corollary 3.0.10
.

Suppose
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
is Iwahori-spherical. Then

(3.0.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)&\leq_{A}\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)\\
d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})&\leq\hphantom{}^{\bar{{\mathsf{k}}}}\mathrm{WF}(X)\end{split}

Proof.

The second inequality follows from the first by applying
\mathrm{pr}_{1}
to both sides. So it suffices to show that
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)\leq_{A}\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)
.

Let
X\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
be Iwahori-spherical. Since
\mathsf{AZ}
is an involution on the Iwahori-spherical representations in
\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))
, we have

X=\mathsf{AZ}(X^{\prime})\text{ for some Iwahori-spherical }X^{\prime}=X(q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}},n^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})\in\Pi^{\mathsf{Lus}}_{q^{\frac{1}{2}h^{\vee}}}(\mathbf{G}({\mathsf{k}}))

By definition,

. Now (iii) of Lemma
3.0.9
implies that
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X^{\prime}}:=G^{\vee}n^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
. In particular

and so

since

is order-reversing. But by Theorem
3.0.8
,
d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{X^{\prime}},1)=\hphantom{}^{K}\mathrm{WF}(X)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of faithfulness

Fix the notation of Sections
2.1
-
2.4
, i.e.
G
,
G^{\vee}
,
\mathcal{N}
,
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}
,
\mathcal{F}_{\tilde{\Delta}}
,
\bar{s}
, and so on. Recall that to each irreducible representation
E
of
W
, we associate nilpotent orbits
\mathbb{O}(E,\mathbb{C})\subset\mathcal{N}
and
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E,\mathbb{C})\subset\mathcal{N}^{\vee}
. In this section all of our objects will be defined over
\mathbb{C}
—so we will simplify the notation by writing
\mathbb{O}(E):=\mathbb{O}(E,\mathbb{C})
,
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E):=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E,\mathbb{C})
. Similarly, for a family
\phi
in
\mathrm{Irr}(W)
, we will write
\mathbb{O}(\phi)
,
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(\phi)
for
\mathbb{O}(\phi,\mathbb{C})
,
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(\phi,\mathbb{C})
.

Recall the notion of a
faithful
nilpotent orbit, see Definition
3.0.3
. In this section we will prove that all nilpotent orbits are faithful (this is Theorem
3.0.4
).

First, note that conditions (i) and (ii) of Definition
3.0.3
depend only on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
. Moreover, if
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1},\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
are faithful (for
(J_{1},\phi_{1})
,
(J_{2},\phi_{2})
respectively), then
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{1}\times\mathbb{O}^{\vee}_{2}
is faithful (for
(J_{1}\times J_{2},\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})
). Thus, it suffices to prove Theorem
3.0.4
for
\mathfrak{g}
a simple Lie algebra. In many cases, we can appeal to the following proposition.

Lemma 4.0.1
.

Suppose
\overline{\mathbb{L}}(J,\mathbb{O}(\phi))=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)
,
E=E(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)
(as representations of
W
), and
F=\mathrm{sp}(\phi\otimes\mathrm{sgn})
. Then
j^{W}_{W_{J}}F=E
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbb{O},C):=\mathbb{L}(J,\mathbb{O}_{\phi})
.
Then, by definition of
d_{S}
we have that
E(d_{S}(\mathbb{O},C),1)=j_{W_{J}}^{W}F
.
But
d_{S}(d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1))=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
as required.
∎

Proposition 4.0.2
.

Suppose there is a unique irreducible representation
E
of
W
such that
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}(E)=\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
. Then
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
is faithful.

Proof.

By the uniqueness condition, we must have
E=E(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)
. Recall that
\overline{\mathbb{L}}:\mathcal{K}_{\widetilde{\Delta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}\mathcal{N}_{o,\bar{c}}
is surjective. Choose any
(J,\phi)
such that
\overline{\mathbb{L}}(J,\mathbb{O}_{\phi})=d_{A}(\mathbb{O}^{\vee},1)
and let
F=\mathrm{sp}(\phi\otimes\mathrm{sgn})
. By Lemma
4.0.1
, we have
j^{W}_{W_{J}}F=E
, and hence
\Hom_{W_{J}}(F,E|_{W_{J}})\neq 0
by Frobenius reciprocity.
∎

Proposition
4.0.2
implies Theorem
3.0.4
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n)
and for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(2n)
and
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
a very even orbit (see Section
4.3
). The simple exceptional Lie algebras are handled in Section
4.10
(where we make repeated use of Proposition
4.0.2
to simplify the arguments). It remains to prove Theorem
3.0.4
for
\mathfrak{g}
a simple Lie algebra of type B/C/D (and

not very even in the type D case). The proof will require a fairly lengthy detour into the combinatorics of partitions and symbols.

4.1.
Preliminaries on partitions

Let
\mathcal{P}(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
, we will typically write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq...\geq\lambda_{k})
, and we assume
\lambda_{k}\neq 0
(unless otherwise stated). We will write
\#\lambda
for the number of (nonzero) parts of
\lambda
and
|\lambda|
for their sum. If
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we will write
m_{\lambda}(x)
for the multiplicity of
x
in
\lambda
and
\mathrm{ht}_{\lambda}(x)
for its ‘height,’ i.e.
\mathrm{ht}_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{y\geq x}m_{\lambda}(y)
. A partition
\lambda
is
very even
if all parts are even, occuring with even multiplicity.

There is a partial order on partitions, defined by the formula

\lambda\leq\mu\iff\sum_{i\leq j}\lambda_{i}\leq\sum_{i\leq j}\mu_{i}.

Given an arbitrary partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})
, we define two new partitions (of
|\lambda|+1
and
|\lambda|-1
)

\lambda^{+}:=(\lambda_{1}+1,\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{k}),\qquad\lambda^{-}:=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{k}-1)

and we write
\lambda^{t}
for the transpose of
\lambda
. Given
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(m)
, we define
\lambda\cup\mu\in\mathcal{P}(m+n)
by ‘adding multiplicities’

m_{\lambda\cup\mu}(x)=m_{\lambda}(x)+m_{\mu}(x),\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

We write
\alpha=\lambda\cup_{\geq}\mu
if
\alpha=\lambda\cup\mu
and the smallest part of
\lambda
is at least as large as the largest part of
\mu
. We write
\mu\subseteq\lambda
if
m_{\mu}(x)\leq m_{\lambda}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. In this case, there is a unique subpartition partition
\lambda\setminus\mu\subseteq\lambda
such that
\lambda=\mu\cup(\lambda\setminus\mu)
.

A
decorated
partition of
n
is a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
together with a decoration
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
. We write
(\lambda,\kappa)
as
\lambda^{\kappa}
. If
\lambda
is
not
very even, we declare
\lambda^{0}=\lambda^{1}
. Otherwise we regard
\lambda^{0}
and
\lambda^{1}
as distinct. Write
\mathcal{P}^{d}(n)
for the set of decorated partitions of
n
, with the equivalence relation just defined.

An
(ordered) bipartition
of
n
is a pair of partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
such that
|\lambda|+|\mu|=n
. An
unordered bipartition
of
n
is an unordered pair
\{\lambda,\mu\}
with the same property. Write
\overline{\mathcal{P}}(n)
(resp.
\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{u}(n)
) for the set of ordered (resp. unordered) bipartitions of
n
.

A
decorated unordered bipartition
of
n
is an unordered bipartition
\{\lambda,\nu\}
of
n
together with a decoration
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
.
We write
(\{\lambda,\nu\},\kappa)
as
\{\lambda,\mu\}^{\kappa}
.
If
\lambda\neq\mu
we declare
\{\lambda,\mu\}^{0}=\{\lambda,\mu\}^{1}
. Otherwise we regard
\{\lambda,\mu\}^{0}
and
\{\lambda,\mu\}^{1}
as distinct.
Write
\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{d,u}(n)
for the set of decorated unordered bipartition of
n
, with the equivalence relation just defined.

.

\displaystyle X=B:

\displaystyle X=C:

\displaystyle X=D:

has defect 0.

and

\Lambda=((a_{1},\dots,a_{k});(b_{1},\dots,b_{l}))

\displaystyle X=C

\displaystyle:((a_{1},\dots,a_{s});(b_{1},\dots,b_{t}))\approx((0,a_{1}+2,\dots,a_{s}+2);(1,b_{1}+2,\dots,b_{t}+2)).

Lemma 4.8.4
.

The following are true:

(i)

\pi\subseteq d(\lambda)
.

(ii)

\mu\subseteq d(\lambda)
.

(iii)

{}^{\langle\pi\rangle}d(\lambda)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n)
.

(iv)

{}^{\langle\mu\rangle}d(\lambda)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n)
.

Proof.

We will prove (i)-(iv) for
X=C
(the proofs for
X=B,D
are analogous, and are omitted).

Since
\pi\subseteq\mu
, (ii) implies (i). And since all parts of
\pi\setminus\mu
have even multiplicity, (iv) implies (iii). So it is enough to prove (ii) and (iv).

Choose
\nu
so that
\mu\cup\nu=\lambda^{t}
. Note that
|\nu|
is odd. We will first show that

(4.8.1)

d(\lambda)=\mu\cup(\nu^{-})_{C}

There are two cases to consider.

(a)
\mu\subseteq(\lambda^{t})^{-}
. In this case we have
(\lambda^{t})^{-}=\mu\cup\nu^{-}
. Since
\mu
consists only of even parts, equation (
4.3.2
) implies

d(\lambda)=((\lambda^{t})^{-})_{C}=\mu\cup(\nu^{-})_{C},

as desired.

(b)
\mu\nsubseteq(\lambda^{t})^{-}
. Write

\nu=\nu^{\prime}\cup_{\geq}(x,y_{1},...,y_{r}),

where
x
is the smallest odd part of
\nu
(note that
r
might be
0
). Then by (
4.3.2
) we have

(4.8.2)

(\nu^{-})_{C}=(\nu^{\prime})_{C}\cup_{\geq}(x-1,y_{1},...,y_{r})

Now let
z
be the smallest part of
\mu
and choose
\mu^{\prime}
such that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}\cup(z)
. Then

(\lambda^{t})^{-}=\mu^{\prime}\cup[\nu^{\prime}\cup_{\geq}(x,y_{1},...,y_{r},z-1)].

So by (
4.3.2
) we have

(4.8.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d(\lambda)&=((\lambda^{t})^{-})_{C}\\
&=\mu^{\prime}\cup[(\nu^{\prime})_{C}\cup_{\geq}(x-1,y_{1},...,y_{r},z)]\\
&=\mu\cup[(\nu^{\prime})_{C}\cup_{\geq}(x-1,y_{1},...,y_{r})]\end{split}

Substituting (
4.8.2
) into the right hand side of (
4.8.3
), we obtain

d(\lambda)=\mu\cup(\nu^{-})_{C},

as desired.

Thus, we have proved (
4.8.1
). (ii) follows immediately. In view of (
4.8.1
), (iv) amounts to the assertion that
(\nu^{-})_{C}
is special. This will require some notation.

For
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(2n)
, define

e_{\alpha}(i,j):=\#\{k\mid i\leq k\leq j\text{ and }\alpha_{k}\text{ is even}\}

Let
\alpha_{t_{1}},...,\alpha_{t_{p}}
be the odd parts of
\alpha
(for
t_{1}<t_{2}<...<t_{p}
). We say that

•

\alpha
is
special
if
e_{\alpha}(1,t_{1})
is even and
e_{\alpha}(t_{i},t_{i+1})
is even for
1\leq i\leq p-1
.

•

\alpha
is

if
e_{\alpha}(1,t_{1})
is even,
e_{\alpha}(t_{i},t_{i+1})
is even for
1\leq i\leq p-2
, and the smallest part of
\alpha
is odd, with multiplicity
1
.

By (
4.3.2
), the odd parts of
\alpha_{C}
are precisely those of the form
(\alpha_{C})_{t_{2i-1}}
or
(\alpha_{C})_{t_{2i}}
for
i

\alpha_{t_{2i-1}}=\alpha_{t_{2i}}
. Choose
i<j
such that

\alpha_{t_{2i-1}}=\alpha_{t_{2i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha_{t_{2j-1}}=\alpha_{t_{2j}}.

Then by (

)

e_{\alpha_{C}}(t_{2i},t_{2j-1})=e_{\alpha}(t_{2i},t_{2j-1})+2(j-i-1).

In particular,

e_{\alpha_{C}}(t_{2i},t_{2j-1})\equiv e_{\alpha}(t_{2i},t_{2j-1})\mod{2}

The following implications are immediate

•

\alpha\text{ special}\implies\alpha_{C}\text{ special}
.

•

\alpha\text{ quasi-special}\implies\alpha_{C}\text{ special}.

Returning to the situation at hand, there are two cases to consider. First, suppose the smallest part of
\nu
is even. In this case,
\nu^{-}
is quasi-special. So
(\nu^{-})_{C}
is special. Next, suppose the smallest part of
\nu
is odd. In this case,
\nu^{-}
is special. So
(\nu^{-})_{C}
is special. In either case,
(\nu^{-})_{C}
is special, as desired.
∎

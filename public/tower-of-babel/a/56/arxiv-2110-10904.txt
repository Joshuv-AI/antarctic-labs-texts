Discrete and free groups acting on locally finite trees
Matthew J. Conder

Abstract
. We present an algorithm to decide whether or not a finitely generated subgroup of the isometry group of a locally finite simplicial tree is both discrete and free. The correctness of this algorithm relies on the following conjecture: every ‘minimal’ n n -tuple of isometries of a simplicial tree either contains an elliptic element or satisfies the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. We prove this conjecture for n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 , and show that it implies a generalisation of Ihara’s Theorem.



1.
Introduction

Let
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries of a simplicial tree
T
. A theorem attributed to Weidmann (see
[
1
, Theorem 4]
) asserts that the group generated by
X
is either free, or contains an elliptic isometry of
T
. The proof, however, gives no practical method of distinguishing between these two cases.

In this paper, we conjecture that every
n
-tuple
X
which is ‘minimal’ (in the sense that certain product replacements do not decrease a particular sum of translation lengths) either contains an elliptic isometry or satisfies the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. We prove this conjecture for
n=2,3
, and report significant computational evidence that it holds for
n>3
. It implies the existence of a practical method to decide between the two outcomes of the theorem of Weidmann, and to exhibit such an elliptic isometry in the second case.

If
T
is locally finite and its isometry group
{\rm Isom}(T)
is equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence, then this conjecture additionally gives an algorithm to decide whether or not a finitely generated subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is both discrete and free. This significantly generalises the algorithm given in
[
3
]
for 2-generated subgroups of
{\rm Isom}(T)
. In fact, our conjecture implies the following generalisation of Ihara’s Theorem
[
7
, Theorem 1]
: a finitely generated subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is both discrete and free if and only if it contains no elliptic element. It immediately follows that a discrete subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is locally free if and only if it contains no elliptic element.

2.
Main Results

Let
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries of a simplicial tree
T
. A
product replacement
is where we replace some
g_{i}
by either
g_{j}g_{i}
or
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
(for
i\neq j
). This is an example of a Nielsen transformation, and it preserves the subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
generated by
X
. Given
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and subsets
S_{1},S_{2}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}\backslash\{j\}
, we will denote by
X^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}
the
n
-tuple obtained from
X
by performing the following product replacements:

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto g_{j}g_{i}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(\textup{if }i\in S_{1});

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;(\textup{if }i\in S_{2}).

Note that if
i\in S_{1}\cap S_{2}
, then the
i
-th element of
X^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}}
is
g_{j}g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of isometries of a simplicial tree. Define the following sum of translation lengths:

L(X)=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}l(g_{i})+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}l(g_{i}g_{j})+l(g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}).

Definition 2.2
.

An
n
-tuple
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
of isometries of a simplicial tree is
minimal
if
L(X)\leq L(X^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}})
for every
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and every
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\backslash\{j\}
.

Conjecture 2.3
.

Let
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be a
n
-tuple of isometries of a simplicial tree
T
. If
X
is minimal, then either some
g_{i}
is elliptic, or
X
satisfies the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma.

Many different statements of the Ping Pong Lemma appear in the literature, but the version we use here is stated in
Section

3
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Conjecture
2.3
holds for
n=2,3
.

The proof of
2.3
for
n=3
relies on a case-by-case analysis of the possible interactions between the axes of three hyperbolic elements. The main obstacle to obtaining a full proof of
2.3
is the vast increase in the number of possible interactions between the axes as
n
increases. However, as discussed in
Section

6
, there is significant computational evidence that
2.3
holds.

When equipped with the topology of pointwise convergence, the isometry group
{\rm Isom}(T)
has the structure of a topological group
[
6
, Section 5.B]
. A subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is
discrete
if the corresponding topology is the discrete topology.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
T
be a locally finite simplicial tree. If Conjecture
2.3
holds, then there is an algorithm to decide whether or not a finitely generated subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is both discrete and free.

We present such an algorithm in
Section

4
and discuss its implementation in
Magma

[
2
]
in
Section

5
. As a consequence of this algorithm, we obtain the following generalisation of Ihara’s Theorem
[
7
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
T
be a locally finite simplicial tree. If Conjecture
2.3
holds, then a finitely generated subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is both discrete and free if and only if it contains no elliptic element.

A group is
locally free
if every finitely generated subgroup is free. Thus we immediately deduce the following from
2.6
:

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
T
be a locally finite simplicial tree. If Conjecture
2.3
holds, then a discrete subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is locally free if and only if it contains no elliptic element.

3.
Background

Let
T
be a simplicial tree. We will assume (by subdividing edges, if necessary) that every isometry
g
of
T
acts without inversions and hence can be classified based on its
translation length
. This is the integer

l(g)=\min_{x\in V(T)}d(x,gx),

where
V(T)
denotes the vertex set of
T
, and
d
is the standard path metric on
T
. Note that
l(g)=l(g^{-1})
and
l(hgh^{-1})=l(g)
for all such isometries
g,h
of
T
. If
l(g)=0
, then
g
is
elliptic
and it fixes a vertex of
T
. If
l(g)>0
, then
g
is
hyperbolic
and there is an infinite path in
T
(called the
axis
of
g
) upon which
g
acts by translations of length
l(g)
. For further background information, see
[
11
]
.

Suppose
g_{i},g_{j}
are hyperbolic isometries of
T
with respective axes
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
. As in
[
1
]
, we define the
projection of
\gamma_{j}
onto
\gamma_{i}
to be

{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{i}}(\gamma_{j})=\{x\in\gamma_{i}:d(x,\gamma_{j})=d(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})\}.

Note that
{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{i}}(\gamma_{j})
is either the unique vertex of
\gamma_{i}
that is closest to
\gamma_{j}
(if
\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}=\varnothing
), or the path
\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}
(if
\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}\neq\varnothing
).

We present a version of the Ping Pong Lemma specifically for isometries of a simplicial tree. It is essentially a reformulation of
[
8
, Proposition 1.6]
using the notation of
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(The Ping Pong Lemma)
.

Let
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be an
n
-tuple of hyperbolic isometries of a simplicial tree with axes
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
. Suppose that
for each
1\leq i\leq n
there is an open segment
P_{i}\subseteq\gamma_{i}
of length
l(g_{i})
such that

\bigcup_{i\neq j}{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{i}}(\gamma_{j})\subseteq P_{i}.

Then the group
G
generated by
X
is free of rank
n
. If
T
is locally finite, then
G
is also discrete with respect to the topology of pointwise convergence on
{\rm Isom}(T)
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

implies that two hyperbolic isometries of

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
. Precisely one of the following holds:

(1)

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
do not intersect and

l(g_{1}g_{2})=l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})+2d(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

(2)

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
intersect with the same orientation and

l(g_{1}g_{2})=l(g_{1})+l(g_{2}).

(3)

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
intersect with opposite orientations along a (possibly infinite) path of length
\Delta=\Delta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\geq 0

(i)

\Delta<\min\{l(g_{1}),l(g_{2})\}

l(g_{1}g_{2})=l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})-2\Delta

(ii)

\Delta>\min\{l(g_{1}),l(g_{2})\}

l(g_{1}g_{2})=|l(g_{1})-l(g_{2})|
;

(iii)

\Delta=\min\{l(g_{1}),l(g_{2})\}
, either
\gamma_{2}
and
g_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}
(if

) or
\gamma_{1}
and
g_{2}\cdot\gamma_{1}
(if
l(g_{1})>l(g_{2})
) intersect along a (possibly infinite) path of length
\Delta^{\prime}\geq 0

\hskip 56.9055ptl(g_{1}g_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|l(g_{1})-l(g_{2})|-2\Delta^{\prime}&\textup{ if }\Delta^{\prime}<\frac{|l(g_{1})-l(g_{2})|}{2}\\
0&\textup{ otherwise. }\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Example 3.4
.

Let

p

v_{p}(x)=r
, where

4.
The algorithm

In this section we prove Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
by presenting an algorithm which, subject to
2.3
, decides whether or not a finitely generated subgroup of the isometry group of a locally finite simplicial tree
T
is both discrete and free. The algorithm requires a method of computing translation lengths on
T
. In particular, using
Equation

3.1
, this algorithm can be applied to finitely generated subgroups of
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We first note the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T
be a locally finite simplicial tree. If a subgroup of
{\rm Isom}(T)
is both discrete and free, then it contains no elliptic element.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Proposition 5.1]
.
∎

Algorithm 4.2
.

Let
T
be a locally finite simplicial tree. Given a finitely generated subgroup
H=\langle h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\rangle\leq{\rm Isom}(T)
, we proceed as follows.

If
H
is both discrete and free of rank
n
, then the algorithm returns
true
and outputs an
n
-tuple of isometries which generates
H
and satisfies the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. Otherwise, the algorithm returns
false
and outputs an elliptic element of
H
.

(1)

Set
g_{i}=h_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and initialise
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
.

(2)

If
l(g_{i})=0
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then return
false
and the element
g_{i}
.

(3)

If
L(X^{j}_{S_{1},S_{2}})<L(X)
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\backslash\{j\}
, then replace
g_{i}
with
g_{j}g_{i}
if
i\in S_{1}
and
g_{i}
with
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
if
i\in S_{2}
, and return to
(2)
.

(4)

Return
true
and the
n
-tuple
X=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

It suffices to show that, subject to
2.3
,
4.2
terminates and produces the correct output.

The only recursive step in
4.2
is step
(3)
. Since
L(X)
is a positive integer which strictly decreases upon each iteration, the algorithm must eventually terminate.

If the algorithm returns false, then
H
is not both discrete and free by
4.1
. Otherwise, we must reach step
(4)
and hence
X
is minimal. If
2.3
holds, then the elements of
X
must satisfy the hypotheses of
3.1
and hence
H
is both discrete and free of rank
n
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

If
H
contains an elliptic element, then it cannot be both discrete and free by
4.1
. If
H
contains no elliptic element, then
4.2
will terminate and return
true
.
∎

Note that
4.2
also gives a constructive proof of Weidmann’s Theorem
[
1
, Theorem 4]
for locally finite simplicial trees: by recording the product replacements performed in step
(3)
, the elliptic element returned at step
(2)
can be written as a word in the input generators
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}
.

6.
Comments on the conjecture

There is significant computational evidence that
2.3
holds for
n>3
: using
Magma
, we have generated millions of examples of minimal
n
-tuples of rational matrices of determinant 1 (viewed as elements of
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and hence isometries of
T_{p}
) for
4\leq n\leq 10
and primes
p\leq 11
. In every case, the conclusion of
2.3
holds.

We now discuss some reasons why our definition of minimality cannot be simplified. An algorithm given in
[
3
]
takes as input a pair of isometries
(g_{1},g_{2})
of a simplicial tree
T
and performs single product replacements which strictly decrease the sum
l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})
until a ‘minimal’ Nielsen-equivalent pair is obtained. Such a ‘minimal’ pair either contains an elliptic isometry or satisfies the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. The following example shows that this notion of minimality does not immediately generalise to
n
-tuples of isometries.

Example 6.1
.

Consider the following matrices in
{\rm SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{5})
:

g_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{5}&-\frac{1}{5}\\[4.0pt]
-\frac{1}{5}&\frac{26}{5}\end{array}\right],\;\;g_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&1\\[4.0pt]
-\frac{1}{5}&-\frac{4}{5}\end{array}\right],\;\;g_{3}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}5&0\\[4.0pt]
0&\frac{1}{5}\end{array}\right].

Since
v_{5}({\rm tr}(g_{i}))=-1
for each
i
, it follows from
Equation

3.1
that each
g_{i}
is a hyperbolic isometry of the Bruhat-Tits tree
T_{5}
with translation length 2. Observe from
3.2
that
{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{3}}(\gamma_{1})\cup{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{3}}(\gamma_{2})
is a subpath of
\gamma_{3}
of length 2 and hence the 3-tuple
X=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
does not satisfy the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. Moreover, no product replacements can strictly reduce the sum
l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})+l(g_{3})
.

We also present an example which motivated the notion of minimality given in
2.2
.

Example 6.2
.

Let
X=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4},g_{5})
be a 5-tuple of hyperbolic isometries of the Bruhat-Tits tree
T_{7}
, where

g_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{129}{49}&-\frac{178}{49}\\[4.0pt]
\frac{6}{49}&\frac{31}{147}\end{array}\right],\;\;g_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{688}{49}&-\frac{1}{7}\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1031}{49}&\frac{1}{7}\end{array}\right],\;\;g_{3}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{1}{49}&-\frac{3}{49}\\[4.0pt]
2&-43\end{array}\right],

g_{4}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{9}{7}&-\frac{25}{21}\\[4.0pt]
-\frac{60}{49}&\frac{281}{147}\end{array}\right],\;\;g_{5}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}7&7\\[4.0pt]
-\frac{3}{7}&-\frac{2}{7}\end{array}\right].

Each isometry is hyperbolic, with
l(g_{1})=l(g_{2})=l(g_{3})=l(g_{4})=4
and
l(g_{5})=2
. Of the twenty products of the form
g_{i}g_{j}
or
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
for
1\leq i<j\leq 5
, ten have translation length 8, four have translation length 6 and six have translation length 4. Thus

.

Observe from
3.2
that
{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{5}}(\gamma_{1})\cup{\rm Proj}_{\gamma_{5}}(\gamma_{2})
is a subpath of
\gamma_{5}
of length 2; it follows that
X
does not satisfy the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma. There is also no single product replacement which strictly reduces the sum
L(X)
. Moreover, for each
j\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
, there is no subset
S
of
\{1,2,3,4,5\}\backslash\{j\}
for which replacing every
g_{i}
with
i\in S
by
g_{j}g_{i}
(respectively
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}
) strictly reduces the value of

. However, the replacements

g_{1}\mapsto g_{5}g_{1},\;\;g_{3}\mapsto g_{5}g_{3}g_{5}^{-1}

produce the 5-tuple

with
L(X^{5}_{\{1,3\},\{3\}})=144<L(X)
.

7.
Proof of
2.4

In this final section, we prove
2.4
. We split the proof into several lemmas. The first lemma proves that
2.3
holds when
n=2
. The remaining six lemmas together prove that
2.3
holds when

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X=(g_{1},g_{2})
be a pair of hyperbolic isometries of a simplicial tree. If
X
does not satisfy the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma, then it is not minimal.

Proof.

Since
X
does not satisfy the hypotheses of the Ping Pong Lemma, the axes
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
overlap in a path of length
\Delta(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\geq\min\{l(g_{1}),l(g_{2})\}.
Without loss of generality, we may further assume that
l(g_{1})\leq l(g_{2})
and that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
have the same orientation. We will show that
L(X^{1}_{\{\},\{2\}})<L(X)
, so
X
is not minimal.

First observe that replacing
g_{2}
by
g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}
preserves
l(g_{1})
and changes the other terms in the expression of
L(X)
as follows:

\displaystyle l(g_{1}g_{2})\mapsto l(g_{2})\mapsto l(g_{1}g_{2}^{-1})\mapsto l(g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{-1}).

3.2

(2)
shows that
l(g_{1}g_{2})=l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})
, so it remains to find
l(g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{-1})
. Since
g_{1}^{2}
has the same axis as
g_{1}
but double the translation length,
3.2
(applied to
g_{1}^{2}
and
g_{2}^{-1}
) shows that

\displaystyle l(g_{1}^{2}g_{2}^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq 2l(g_{1})+l(g_{2})-2\Delta

\displaystyle\leq l(g_{1}g_{2})-l(g_{1}),

and it follows that
L(X^{1}_{\{\},\{2\}})<L(X)
as required.
∎

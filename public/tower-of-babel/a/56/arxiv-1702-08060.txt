Elliptic Dynamical Quantum Groups and Equivariant Elliptic Cohomology This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Elliptic Hypergeometric Functions and Their Applications. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/EHF2017.html
Giovanni FELDER †, Richárd RIMÁNYI ‡ and Alexander VARCHENKO ‡

Abstract
We define an elliptic version of the stable envelope of Maulik and Okounkov for the equivariant elliptic cohomology of cotangent bundles of Grassmannians. It is a version of the construction proposed by Aganagic and Okounkov and is based on weight functions and shuffle products. We construct an action of the dynamical elliptic quantum group associated with 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{gl}_{2} on the equivariant elliptic cohomology of the union of cotangent bundles of Grassmannians. The generators of the elliptic quantum groups act as difference operators on sections of admissible bundles, a notion introduced in this paper.

Introduction

Maulik and Okounkov
[
12
]
have set up a program to realize representation theory of quantum groups of various kinds on torus equivariant (generalized) cohomology of Nakajima varieties. A central role is played by the stable envelopes, which are maps from the equivariant cohomology of the fixed point set of the torus action to the equivariant cohomology of the variety. Stable envelopes depend on the choice of a chamber (a connected component of the complement of an arrangement of real hyperplanes) and different chambers are related by
R
-matrices of the corresponding quantum groups. The basic example of a Nakajima variety is the cotangent bundle of the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
of
k
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The torus is
T=U(1)^{n}\times U(1)
, with
U(1)^{n}
acting by diagonal matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
U(1)
acting by multiplication on the cotangent spaces. Then the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
acts on
H_{T}(\sqcup_{k=0}^{n}T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
and the action of generators is described geometrically by correspondences. It turns out that this representation is isomorphic to the tensor products of
n
evaluation vector representations with the equivariant parameters of
U(1)^{n}
as evaluation points and the equivariant parameter of
U(1)
as the deformation parameter of the quantum group. The choice of a chamber is the same as the choice of an ordering of the factors in the tensor product. The same holds for the affine quantum universal enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2})
if we replace equivariant cohomology by equivariant
K
-theory. As was shown in
[
8
,
14
]
, the stable envelopes, which realize the isomorphisms, are given by the
weight functions
, which originally appeared in the theory of integral representations of solutions of the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equation, see
[
19
,
20
]
. Their special values form transition matrices from the tensor basis to a basis of eigenvectors for the Gelfand–Zetlin commutative subalgebra.

The recent preprint
[
1
]
of Aganagic and Okounkov suggests that the same picture should hold for equivariant elliptic cohomology and elliptic dynamical quantum groups and this is the subject of this paper. The authors of
[
1
]
define an elliptic version of the stable envelopes and show, in the example of the cotangent bundle of a projective space, stable envelopes corresponding to different orderings are related to the fundamental elliptic dynamical
R
-matrices of the elliptic dynamical quantum group
E_{\tau,y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
. Our paper is an attempt to understand the elliptic stable envelope in the case of cotangent bundles of Grassmannians. In particular we give a precise description of the space in which the stable envelope takes its values. Our construction of stable envelopes is based on elliptic weight functions. In Appendix
A
we also give a geometric characterization, in terms of pull-backs to the cohomology of fixed points, in the spirit of
[
12
]
.

While our work is inspired by
[
1
]
, we do not know whether the two constructions are equivalent or not. The interesting project of understanding the exact relation between our construction and the construction of Aganagic–Okounkov requires more work.

Compared to equivariant cohomology and
K
-theory, two new features arise in the elliptic case. The first new feature is the occurrence of an additional variable, the dynamical parameter, in the elliptic quantum group. It also appears in
[
1
]
, under the name of Kähler parameter, in an extended version of the elliptic cohomology of Nakajima varieties. The second is a general feature of elliptic cohomology: while
T
-equivariant cohomology and
K
-theory are contravariant functors from
T
-spaces to supercommutative algebras, and can thus be thought of as covariant functors to affine superschemes,
1
1

1

The reader may safely ignore the super prefixes, as we only consider spaces with trivial odd cohomology, for which one has strictly commutative algebras.
in the elliptic case only the description as covariant functor to (typically non-affine) superschemes generalizes straightforwardly.

Our main result is a construction of an action of the elliptic quantum group associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
on the extended equivariant elliptic cohomology scheme
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n})
of the union
X_{n}=\sqcup_{k=0}^{n}X_{k,n}
of cotangent bundles
X_{k,n}=T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
of Grassmannians. The meaning of this is that we define a representation of the operator algebra of the quantum group by difference operators acting on sections of a class of line bundles on the extended elliptic cohomology scheme, which we call admissible bundles: up to a twist by a fixed line bundle, admissible bundles on
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
are pull-backs of bundles on
\hat{E}_{U(n)\times U(1)}(\mathrm{pt})
(by functoriality there is a map corresponding to the map to a point and the inclusion of the Cartan subalgebra
T\to U(n)\times U(1)
). The claim is that there is a representation of the elliptic quantum group by operators mapping sections of admissible bundles to sections of admissible bundles.

This paper may be considered as an elliptic version of the paper
[
16
]
where analogous constructions are developed for the rational dynamical quantum group
E_{y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
.

2.1
Dynamical Yang–Baxter equation

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a complex abelian Lie algebra and
V
an
\mathfrak{h}
-module with a weight decomposition
V=\oplus_{\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}V_{\mu}
and finite dimensional weight spaces
V_{\mu}
. A dynamical
R
-matrix with values in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V\otimes V)
is a meromorphic function
(z,y,\lambda)\mapsto R(z,y,\lambda)\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V\otimes V)
of the spectral parameter
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the deformation parameter
y\in\mathbb{C}
and the dynamical parameter
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, obeying the dynamical Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle R\big(z,y,\lambda-yh^{(3)}\big)^{(12)}R(z+w,y,\lambda)^{(13)}R\big(w,y,\lambda-yh^{(1)}\big)^{(23)}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=R(w,y,\lambda)^{(23)}R\big(z+w,y,\lambda-yh^{(2)}\big)^{(13)}R\big(z,y,\lambda-yh^{(3)}\big)^{(12)}

(2.1)

in
\operatorname{End}(V\otimes V\otimes V)
and the inversion relation

\displaystyle R(z,y,\lambda)^{(12)}R(-z,y,\lambda)^{(21)}=\mathrm{Id}

(2.2)

in
\operatorname{End}(V\otimes V)
. The superscripts indicate the factors in the tensor product on which the endomorphisms act non-trivially and
h
is the element in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{End}(V)
defined by the action of
\mathfrak{h}
: for example
R\big(z,y,\lambda-yh^{(3)}\big)^{(12)}
acts as
R(z,y,\lambda-y\mu_{3})\otimes\mathrm{Id}
on
V_{\mu_{1}}\otimes V_{\mu_{2}}\otimes V_{\mu_{3}}
.

2.2
Duality and gauge transformations

Suppose that
R(z,y,\lambda)
is a dynamical
R
-matrix with
\mathfrak{h}
-module
V
. Let
V^{\vee}=\oplus_{\mu}(V^{\vee})_{\mu}
with weight space
(V^{\vee})_{\mu}
the dual space to
V_{\mu}
. Then
R^{\vee}(z,y,\lambda)=\big(R(z,y,\lambda)^{-1}\big)^{*}
, the dual map to

R
-matrix with values in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\vee}\otimes V^{\vee})
. It is called the dual
R
-matrix to

.

Another way to get new
R
-matrices out of old is by a gauge transformation. Let
\psi_{V}(\lambda)
be a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with values in
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V)
. Let

\psi_{V\otimes V}(\lambda)=\psi_{V}\big(\lambda-yh^{(2)}\big)^{(1)}\psi_{V}(\lambda)^{(2)}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V\otimes V)
. Then

\displaystyle R_{\psi}(z,y,\lambda)=\psi_{V\otimes V}(\lambda)^{-1}R(z,y,\lambda)\psi_{V\otimes V}(\lambda)^{(21)}

is another dynamical
R
-matrix. The corresponding representations of the symmetric group are related by the isomorphism

\displaystyle\psi_{V^{\otimes n}}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{V}\bigg(\lambda-y\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}h^{(j)}\bigg)^{(i)}.

Shuffle products and weight functions

Weight functions are special bases of spaces of sections of line bundles on symmetric powers of elliptic curves. They appear in the theory of hypergeometric integral representation of Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations. In
[
4

[

]

There remains to prove that the pairing vanishes also if

As above the presence of the vanishing factors in

such that at least

\geq n^{\prime}
and the corresponding variables

are arguments of

and at least
k_{2}^{\prime}
indices are
\leq n^{\prime}
and the corresponding variables are arguments of

. If
k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{2}^{\prime}=1
there is one variable left and we can write the pairing as a sum of one-dimensional integrals over this variable:

\displaystyle I_{A,B}=\int_{\gamma}\frac{f_{1}(z_{A},t)g_{1}(z_{B})f_{2}(z_{A})g_{2}(t,z_{B})}{h(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y,t)}{\rm d}t.

(3.3)

Here

and

with

. The point is that in

\displaystyle h(z,y,t)=C(z,y)\prod_{c\in\overline{A\cup B}}\theta(t-z_{c})\prod_{c\in A\cup B}\theta(t-z_{c}+y),

for some

. Because of the vanishing condition, the integrand in (

and the only poles are at

. By the residue theorem

.

Finally, let us reduce the general case to the case where

. We use induction on

. By Lemma

the pairing vanishes unless

so there is nothing to prove in this case. Assume that the claim is proved for

. By Proposition
3.10
, we can write

and
g_{2}=h_{2}*m_{2}
with

. By Lemma
3.24
we can assume that

. By the associativity of the shuffle product we can use the result for

to obtain that the pairing vanishes unless

and

\displaystyle\langle f_{1}*g_{1},f_{2}*g_{2}\rangle=\langle f_{1}*h_{1},f_{2}*h_{2}\rangle\langle m_{1},m_{2}\rangle.

By the induction hypothesis, this vanishes unless

.
∎

3.9
Normalized weight functions

By construction the weight functions

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}_{I}(z_{a},z_{a}-y,t_{3},\dots,t_{k};z,y,\lambda)=0,\qquad a=1,\dots,n.

Definition 3.27

The

\displaystyle w_{I}^{-}(t;z,y,\lambda)=\frac{\omega^{-}_{I}(t;z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{1\leq j\neq l\leq k}\theta(t_{j}-t_{l}+y)},

3.10
Eigenvectors of the Gelfand–Zetlin algebra

The normalized weight functions
w^{-}_{I}
evaluated at

provide the (triangular) transition matrix between the standard basis of
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)^{\otimes n}
and a basis of eigenvectors of the Gelfand–Zetlin
algebra. The Gelfand–Zetlin algebra is generated by the determinant
\Delta(w)
, see (
6.2
), and
L_{22}(w)
. The determinant acts by multiplication by

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\theta(w-z_{i}+y)}{\theta(w-z_{i})}.

We thus need to diagonalize

.

Lemma 3.33
.

Let
0\leq k\leq n
,
[k]=\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then

\displaystyle\xi_{[k]}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\theta(\lambda+(n-k-i)y)v_{[k]}\in V(z_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes V(z_{n})

is an eigenvector of
L_{22}(w)
with eigenvalue

\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{k}\frac{\theta(w-z_{a})}{\theta(w-z_{a}-y)}.

Proof.

(See
[
8
,
15
,
16
]
.) Since
L_{21}(w)v_{1}=0=L_{12}(w)v_{2}
, the action of
L_{22}(w)
on
v_{1}^{\otimes k}\otimes v_{2}^{\otimes n-k}
is simply the product of the action on all factors, with the appropriate shift of
\lambda
. Since
L_{22}(w)
acts diagonally in the basis
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
one gets the result by straightforward calculation.
∎

For
I\subset[n]
,
|I|=k
, define

\displaystyle\xi_{I}=\xi_{I}(z,y,\lambda)=\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{w^{-}_{J}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}v_{J}.

(3.5)

By Lemma
3.32
this definition is consistent with the one for

\xi_{[k]}
above.

Proposition 3.34

(cf.
[
8
,
15
,
16
]
)
.

The vectors
\xi_{I}
,
I\subset[n]
,
|I|=k
form a basis of eigenvectors of the operators of the Gelfand–Zetlin algebra on

\displaystyle\Delta(w)\xi_{I}=\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\theta(w-z_{a}+y)}{\theta(w-z_{a})}\xi_{I},\qquad L_{22}(w)\xi_{I}=\prod_{a\in I}\frac{\theta(w-z_{a})}{\theta(w-z_{a}-y)}\xi_{I}.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.18
(i), we have that

\displaystyle\xi_{I}(z,y,\lambda)=S_{i}(z,y,\lambda)\xi_{s_{i}\cdot I}(s_{i}z,y,\lambda).

Thus

is related to

by a morphism of representations of the elliptic dynamical quantum group. If

then there is a permutation

such that

and thus

is an eigenvector with eigenvalue

∎

3.11
An explicit formula for weight functions

Let

and recall the definition (

and of

in Corollary

\displaystyle l^{+}_{I}(r,a;t,z,y,\lambda)=\begin{cases}\theta(t_{r}-z_{a}+y),&\text{if $a<i_{r}$},\\

\theta(t_{r}-z_{a}),&\text{if $a>i_{r}$},\end{cases}

, the coefficients

must thus vanish if at least one
k_{a}
is
\geq 2
which is what we had to prove.
∎

4
Equivariant elliptic cohomology of Grassmannians

Let
E
be an elliptic curve and
G
a compact group. Equivariant elliptic cohomology was postulated by Ginzburg, Kapranov and Vasserot in
[
7
]
as a functor
E_{G}
from pairs of finite
G
-CW complexes to superschemes satisfying a set of axioms, generalizing those satisfied by equivariant cohomology and equivariant
K
-theory. One of them being that for a point pt,
E_{G}(\mathrm{pt})
is a suitable moduli scheme of
G
-bundles on the dual elliptic curve. For example
E_{U(n)}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{(n)}=E^{n}/S_{n}
and for an abelian group
A
with group of characters
X(A)=\operatorname{Hom}(A,U(1))
,
E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})=\operatorname{Hom}(X(A),E)
. By functoriality, the scheme
E_{G}(M)
comes with a structure map

\displaystyle p_{G}\colon\ E_{G}(M)\to E_{G}(\mathrm{pt}).

For a complex elliptic curve, the case we consider here, a construction of equivariant elliptic cohomology was given by Grojnowski
[
7
,
10
]
. It has the property that for a connected Lie group
G
with maximal torus
A
and Weyl group
W
then
W
acts on
E_{A}(M)
and
E_{G}(M)=E_{A}(M)/W
.

4.1
Tautological bundles and Chern classes

Let
E
be a complex elliptic curve. The unitary group
U(n)
and its maximal torus
A\cong U(1)^{n}
act on the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The
A
-equivariant cohomology of
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
was described in
[
7
, Section 1.9]
, and is analogous to the classical description of ordinary equivariant cohomology in terms of Chern classes of tautological bundles. The Grassmannian has two tautological equivariant vector bundles of rank
k
and
n-k
, respectively. They give rise to a characteristic map
[
7
]

\displaystyle\chi\colon\ E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\to E_{U(k)}(\mathrm{pt})\times E_{U(n-k)}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}.

Here
E^{(k)}=E^{k}/S_{k}
denotes the symmetric power of the elliptic curve, which is the
U(k)
-equivariant cohomology of a point. Together with the structure map to
E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})
we have a description of
the equivariant elliptic cohomology as the fiber product of
E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}
and
E^{n}
over
E^{(n)}
, namely, we have the Cartesian square:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{@{}ccc}E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))&\longrightarrow&E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
E^{n}&\longrightarrow&E^{(n)}.\end{array}

The left vertical arrow is the structure map to
E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})
; the maps
E^{n}\to E^{(n)}
and
E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\to E^{(n)}
are the canonical projections. Thus
E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))=(E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)})\times_{E^{(n)}}E^{n}
.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
(the Weyl group of
U(n)
) acts on the diagram above (with trivial action on the right column) and the
U(n)
-equivariant cohomology is the quotient by this action:

\displaystyle E_{U(n)}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))=E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))/S_{n}=E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}.

4.2
Moment graph description

An alternative useful description of the equivariant elliptic cohomology is via the localization theorem, proved by Goresky, Kottwitz and MacPherson
[
9
]
for equivariant cohomology and generalized to elliptic cohomology by Knutson and Roşu
[
18
]
. For partial flag varieties such as Grassmannians it is described explicitly in
[
6
, Example 4.4]
. The action of
A
on the Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
has isolated fixed points labeled by subsets of
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
with
k
elements. The fixed point
x_{I}
labeled by
I\subset[n]
is the
k
-plane spanned by the coordinate axes indexed by
I
. The inclusion of the fixed points
x_{I}
induces a map
\iota_{I}\colon E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})\to E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
and it turns out that
E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
is the union of the
\iota_{I}E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})\simeq E^{n}
where
I
runs over the subsets of
[n]
with
k
elements.

Let
\Gamma
be the graph with vertex set
\Gamma_{0}
the set of subsets
I\subset[n]
with
|I|=k
elements and an edge connecting
I
with
I^{\prime}
for each pair of vertices such that
|I\cap I^{\prime}|=k-1
. In this case
I=J\cup\{a\}
and
I^{\prime}=J\cup\{b\}
with
|J|=k-1
and we set
\Delta_{I,I^{\prime}}=\{z\in E^{n},z_{a}=z_{b}\}
. We then have inclusion maps
\Delta_{I,I^{\prime}}\to\iota_{I}E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{n}
,
\Delta_{I,I^{\prime}}\to\iota_{I^{\prime}}E_{A}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{n}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have the coequalizer diagram

\displaystyle\sqcup_{|I\cap I^{\prime}|=k-1}\Delta_{I,I^{\prime}}\rightrightarrows\sqcup_{I\subset[n],|I|=k}E^{n}\to E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))).

In other words,
E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
is the union of copies of
E^{n}
labeled by subsets
I\subset[n]
of size
k
, glued along the diagonals
\Delta_{I,I^{\prime}}
. The structure map
E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\to E^{n}
is the identity on each copy. The isomorphism between the two descriptions of
E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))
is induced by the map

\displaystyle\sqcup_{I\subset[n],|I|=k}E^{n}\to\big(E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\big)\times_{E^{(n)}}E^{n},

whose restriction to the copy
E^{n}
labeled by
I
is

\displaystyle z\mapsto(z,z_{I},z_{\bar{I}}),\qquad z_{I}=(z_{i})_{i\in I},\qquad z_{\bar{I}}=(z_{j})_{j\in\bar{I}}.

It is easy to check directly Proposition
4.1
using the fiber product as a definition of the equivariant elliptic cohomology.

4.3
Cotangent bundles and dynamical parameter

The action of
U(n)
on the Grassmannian induces an action on its cotangent bundle
X_{k,n}=T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
. Additionally we have an action of
U(1)
on the cotangent bundle by scalar multiplication on the fibers, so we get an action of

\displaystyle G=U(n)\times U(1)

and its Cartan torus

\displaystyle T=A\times U(1)\cong U(1)^{n+1}.

Since the cotangent bundle is equivariantly homotopy equivalent to its zero section, the equivariant elliptic cohomology is simply

\displaystyle E_{T}(X_{k,n})=E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\times E,

a scheme over
E_{T}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{n}\times E
, and

\displaystyle E_{G}(X_{k,n})=E_{U(n)}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\times E,

a scheme over
E_{G}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{(n)}\times E
.

We will consider, as in
[
1
]
, an extended version of elliptic cohomology to accommodate for dynamical variables in quantum group theory, namely

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}):=E_{T}(X_{k,n})\times(\mathrm{Pic}(X_{k,n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}E)\cong E_{T}(X_{k,n})\times E,

a scheme over
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{n}\times E\times E
(the Picard group of the Grassmannian is infinite cyclic generated by the top exterior power of the tautological bundle). Similarly, we set

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{G}(X_{k,n})=E_{G}(X_{k,n})\times E,

which is a scheme over
\hat{E}_{G}(\mathrm{pt})=E^{(n)}\times E\times E
.

The fixed points
x_{K}
for the
A
-action on the Grassmannian are also isolated fixed points in the cotangent bundle of the Grassmannian for the
T
-action and we have maps
\iota_{K}=\hat{E}_{T}(i_{K})\colon\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})

\to\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
induced by the inclusion
i_{K}\colon\mathrm{pt}\mapsto x_{K}
. Then
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
consists of the components
\iota_{K}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
, where
K
runs over the subsets of
[n]
with
k
elements. By Section
4.1
, we have a description of
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
as a fiber product:

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})\cong\big(E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\big)\times_{E^{(n)}}E^{n}\times E\times E.

In particular we have the
characteristic embedding

\displaystyle c\colon\ \hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})\to E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\times E^{n}\times E\times E

(4.1)

of the extended
T
-equivariant elliptic cohomology scheme into a non-singular projective variety.

5
Admissible line bundles on
\boldsymbol{\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})}

5.1
Line bundles on
\boldsymbol{E^{p}}

Line bundles on complex tori are classified by the Appel–Humbert theorem, see
[
13
, Section I.2]
. In the special case of powers of generic elliptic curves this reduces to the following explicit description: let
E=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
with
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}+\tau\mathbb{Z}
so that
E^{p}=\mathbb{C}^{p}/\Lambda^{p}
. For each pair
(N,v)
consisting of a symmetric integral
p\times p
matrix
N
and
v\in(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})^{p}
let
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
be the line bundle
(\mathbb{C}^{p}\times\mathbb{C})/\Lambda^{p}\to E^{p}
with action

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot(x,u)=(x+\lambda,e_{\lambda}(x)u),\qquad\lambda\in\Lambda^{p},\qquad x\in\mathbb{C}^{p},\qquad u\in\mathbb{C},

and cocycle

\displaystyle e_{n+m\tau}(x)=(-1)^{n^{t}Nn}(-{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\pi\tau})^{m^{t}Nm}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}m^{t}(Nx+v)},\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}.

Proposition 5.1
.

(i)

\mathcal{L}(N,v)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}(N^{\prime},v^{\prime})
if and only if
N=N^{\prime}
and
v\equiv v^{\prime}\mod\Lambda^{p}
.

(ii)

For generic
E
, every holomorphic line bundle on
E^{p}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
for some
(N,v)
.

(iii)

\mathcal{L}(N_{1},v_{1})\otimes\mathcal{L}(N_{2},v_{2})\cong\mathcal{L}(N_{1}+N_{2},v_{1}+v_{2})
.

(iv)

Let
\sigma\in S_{p}
act by permutations on
E^{p}
and
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Denote also by
\sigma
the corresponding
p\times p
permutation matrix. Then

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,v)=\mathcal{L}\big(\sigma^{t}N\sigma,\sigma^{t}v\big).

To an integral symmetric
p\times p
matrix
N
and a vector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
we associate the integral quadratic form
N(x)=x^{t}Nx
and the linear form
v(x)=x^{t}v
on the universal covering
\mathbb{C}^{p}
of
E^{p}
and we call them the quadratic form and the linear form of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
. The linear form is defined up to addition of an integral linear form.

Remark 5.2
.

Exceptions to (ii) are elliptic curves with complex multiplication, in which case there are additional line bundles that are not isomorphic to those of the form
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
.

Remark 5.3
.

The map
E^{p}\to\mathrm{Pic}(E^{p})
sending
v
to
\mathcal{L}(0,v)
is an isomorphism onto the subgroup
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(E^{p})
of classes of line bundles of degree 0. If
E
is a generic elliptic curve, the Néron–Severi group
\mathrm{NS}(E^{p})=\mathrm{Pic}(E^{p})/\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(E^{p})
is a free abelian group of rank
n(n+1)/2
identified with the group of integral symmetric matrices via
N\mapsto\mathcal{L}(N,0)
.

Remark 5.4
.

Sections of
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
are the same as functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{p}
such that
f(x+\lambda)=e_{\lambda}(x)^{-1}f(x)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda^{p}
,
x\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Explicitly, a function on
\mathbb{C}^{p}
defines a section of
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
if and only if

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}+1,\dots,x_{p})=(-1)^{N_{jj}}f(x),

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}+\tau,\dots,x_{p})=(-1)^{N_{jj}}{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(\sum_{k}N_{jk}x_{k}+v_{j})-\pi{\rm i}\tau N_{jj}}f(x),

for all
x\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
,
j=1,\dots,p
.

Remark 5.5
.

The factors of
-1
in the cocycle can be removed by going to an equivalent cocycle. With the present convention the line bundles
\mathcal{L}(N,0)
correspond to divisors whose irreducible components are subgroups. Let
\theta(z)
be the odd Jacobi theta function in one variable, see (
1.1
). Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\theta(r^{t}x+z)=\theta(r_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+r_{p}x_{p}+z)

is a holomorphic section of
\mathcal{L}(N,v)
with quadratic form

\displaystyle N(x)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}x_{i}\right)^{2},

and linear form

\displaystyle v(x)=z\sum_{i=1}^{p}r_{i}x_{i}.

If
z=0
this section vanishes precisely on the subtorus
\mathrm{Ker}(\phi_{r})
, the kernel of the group homomorphism
\phi_{r}\colon E^{p}\to E
,
x\mapsto\sum_{i}r_{i}x_{i}
. Since an integral quadratic form is an integral linear combination of squares of integral linear forms,
\mathcal{L}(N,0)
has a meromorphic section which is a ratio of products of theta functions
\theta(r^{t}x)
with
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

5.2
Admissible line bundles

The elliptic dynamical quantum group acts on sections of admissible line bundles, which are, up to a twist by a fixed line bundle, those coming from the base scheme
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
. Let

be the structure map

\displaystyle p_{T}\colon\ \hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})\to\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt}),

and

the characteristic map

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}\colon\ \hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})=E_{A}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n))\times E\times E\to E^{(k)}\times E^{(n-k)}\times E\times E.

Let

be coordinates on the universal covering of

and

the quadratic form

\displaystyle N_{k,n}(t,s,y,\lambda)=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}(\lambda+(n-k)y)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}(t_{i}-s_{j})^{2}.

(5.1)

Clearly

is symmetric under permutation of the coordinates
t_{i}
and of the coordinates

and thus
\mathcal{L}(N_{k,n},0)

Definition 5.6
.

The

on

\mathcal{T}_{k,n}=\hat{\chi}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N_{k,n},0)

Definition 5.7
.

An

on

\displaystyle p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n},

for some line bundle
\mathcal{L}

5.3
Holomorphic and meromorphic sections

We will consider meromorphic sections of line bundles on elliptic cohomology schemes. Since these schemes are singular, we need to be careful about the definition. Recall that
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
has components
Y_{K}=\iota_{K}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
, corresponding to the inclusion of the fixed points
x_{K}
, labeled by subsets
K\subset[n]
of cardinality
k
. We say that a meromorphic section on a complex manifold
restricts to a meromorphic section
on a submanifold if it is defined at its generic point, i.e., if the divisor of poles does not contain a component of the submanifold.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
. A
meromorphic section
of
\mathcal{L}
is a collection of meromorphic sections
s_{I}
of
\mathcal{L}|_{Y_{I}}
, labeled by
I\subset[n]
with
|I|=k
and restricting to meromorphic sections on all intersections
Y_{I_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap Y_{I_{s}}
and such that

\displaystyle s_{I}|_{Y_{I}\cap Y_{J}}=s_{J}|_{Y_{I}\cap Y_{J}},

for all
I
,
J
. A
holomorphic section
is a meromorphic section whose restriction to each
Y_{I}
is holomorphic. We denote by
\Gamma(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}),\mathcal{L})
the space of holomorphic sections of
\mathcal{L}
and by
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}),\mathcal{L})
the space of meromorphic sections of
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let

be the union of the hypertori

,
1\leq a\neq b\leq n
and
\lambda=jy
,
-n\leq j\leq n

\varphi\colon f\mapsto(\varphi_{I}f)_{I\subset[n],|I|=k}
given by formula (

) is a well-defined injective morphism of

-modules

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\ \bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k,n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{T}(X_{k,n}),

which is an isomorphism on

Proof.

We first prove that the morphism is well-defined. The function
\varphi_{I}f
of
z
,
y
,
\lambda
defines a section of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}(Q,0)
with quadratic form
Q=\Big(N^{\Theta}_{k,n}-\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j}+y)^{2}\Big)\big|_{t=z_{I}}
. An explicit calculation shows that

\displaystyle Q=N_{k,n}|_{t=z_{I},\,s=z_{\bar{I}}}.

It follows that
\varphi f
is a meromorphic section of
p_{T*}\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
. By Lemma
3.29
(which applies to any symmetric theta function obeying the vanishing condition),
\varphi f
is actually holomorphic.

To show that the morphism is injective, we use the fact that the weight functions
\omega_{I}^{+}
form a basis of

at the generic point of
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
, see Proposition

. Thus every local section of
\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k,n}
can be written as linear combination of normalized weight functions with meromorphic coefficients. If this section is in the kernel of our morphism then its restriction to each component vanishes. By the triangularity property of weight functions of Lemma
3.32
all coefficients must vanish and the kernel is trivial.

We now construct the inverse map on the complement of
D
. A section
s
of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{T}(X_{k,n})
on an open set
U
is a collection of sections

of
\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
on the various components of

and agreeing on intersections. Then

is

\displaystyle f(t;z,y,\lambda)=\sum_{I,K}\psi_{K}(y,\lambda)^{-1}\frac{w_{K}^{-}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)s_{I}(z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b})\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}\omega_{K}^{+}(t,z,y,\lambda).

It is easy to check that this is a meromorphic section of

on
U
with poles at

,
1\leq a\neq b\leq n
and at the zeros of
\psi_{K}
. It is regular at the apparent poles at

since the sections

agree on intersections of the components. Let us compute
\varphi f
:

\displaystyle\frac{f(t;z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{i\neq j}\theta(t_{i}-t_{j}+y)}=\sum_{I,K}\frac{w_{K}^{-}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)s_{I}(z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b})\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}w_{K}^{+}(t,z,y,\lambda).

The orthogonality relations, see Corollary

\displaystyle\varphi_{J}f=\sum_{I,K}\frac{w_{K}^{-}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)s_{I}(z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b})\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}w_{K}^{+}(z_{J},z,y,\lambda)=s_{J}(z,y,\lambda).

∎

5.8
Admissible difference operators

Recall that
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
has a factor

corresponding to the

\displaystyle\tau_{j}(y,\lambda)=(y,\lambda+jy).

Denote also by

\displaystyle\tau^{*}_{j}\colon\ \Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}),\mathcal{L})\to\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}),\tau^{*}_{j}\mathcal{L}).

Definition 5.19
.

Let
k=0,\dots,n
and
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
,
\mu\in 2\mathbb{Z}
,

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{2}
is the admissible bundle
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k+\mu/2,n}
with
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

(ii)

For each section
s
of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and fixed point

,

\displaystyle\iota_{K}^{*}\varphi(s)=\sum_{K^{\prime}}\varphi_{K,K^{\prime}}\tau_{\nu}^{*}\iota_{K^{\prime}}^{*}s

(5.6)

for some sections

\displaystyle\iota_{K}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{2}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\iota_{K^{\prime}}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{-1}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\iota^{*}_{K}\mathcal{T}_{k+\mu/2,n}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\iota_{K^{\prime}}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{k,n}^{-1}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k,n}(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu),\qquad k=0,\dots,n,\qquad 0\leq k+\mu\leq n,

is then

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n})=\sqcup_{k=0}^{n}\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}).

It comes with a map
p_{T}\colon\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n})\to\sqcup_{k=0}^{n}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})

Definition 5.20
.

An
admissible line bundle
on
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n})

\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
is admissible. Let
\mathcal{L}=\big(\mathcal{L}^{0},\dots,\mathcal{L}^{n}\big)

of degree
(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)

\mathcal{M}_{1}

\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n}),\mathcal{M}_{1})\to\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n}),\mathcal{M}_{2})

5.9

Examples of admissible difference operators are multiplication operators by sections of pull-backs of line bundles on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
. A subclass of these operators appear as coefficients in the defining relations of
the quantum group: they are the entries of the
R
-matrix and are functions of the dynamical and deformation parameter, and appear in the relations in two different guises: with and without “dynamical shift”. We borrow the terminology of
[

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})

\mu_{\ell}\mathcal{L}
,
\mu_{r}\mathcal{L}
on
\sqcup_{k=0}^{n}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
:

•

\mu_{r}\mathcal{L}
is the line bundle
(\mathcal{L},\dots,\mathcal{L})
;

•

\mu_{\ell}\mathcal{L}
is the line bundle
(\tau_{n}^{*}\mathcal{L},\tau_{n-2}^{*}\mathcal{L},\dots,\tau_{-n}^{*}\mathcal{L})
.

Definition 5.22
.

The

is the map

\displaystyle\mu_{\ell}\colon\ \Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}(\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt}),\mathcal{L})\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mu_{\ell}\mathcal{L},0,0),

sending a section
s
to the operator whose restriction to
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}
is the multiplication by
\tau_{n-2k}^{*}p_{T}^{*}s
.

The

is the map
\mu_{r}\colon\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}\big(\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt}),\mathcal{L}\big)\to\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mu_{r}\mathcal{L},0,0)
sending

to the operator whose component in
\mathcal{A}_{k,n}
is
the multiplication by
p_{T}^{*}s
.

5.10

Let
\mathcal{L}\!\in\!\mathrm{Pic}\big(\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})\big)
and set

to

for any admissible line bundle

in the following sense.

.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}\big(\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})\big)
. Every section

can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{I\subset[n],\,|I|=k}a_{I}\operatorname{Stab}(v_{I}),

for some
a_{I}\in\mathcal{A}^{0}_{n}\big(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{I}^{-1}\big)
, where

is the line bundle of Proposition
5.9
.

Proof.

Denote by
Y_{I}=\iota_{I}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
the component labeled by
I
. Suppose that

is a meromorphic section vanishing on

for all

and such that

. By Lemma
3.32
, we can subtract from
\omega
a multiple of

to get a section that vanishes on

,

. By induction we may subtract from
\omega
a suitable linear combination of weight functions to get

.

5.12
Graded algebras, graded modules

Let
Q
be a group. Recall that an
Q
-graded algebra
A
over
\mathbb{C}
is a collection
(A_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in Q}
of complex vector spaces with associative linear multiplication maps

,
a\otimes b\mapsto a\cdot b
. Let
P
be a set with a left action of
Q
. A
P
-graded (left) module over
A
is a collection
(M_{p})_{p\in P}
of complex vector spaces indexed by
P
together with linear action maps
A_{\gamma}\otimes M_{p}\to A_{\gamma\cdot p}
,
a\otimes m\mapsto a\cdot m
, obeying
(a\cdot b)\cdot m=a\cdot(b\cdot m)
. A unital
Q
-graded algebra is an
Q
-graded algebra with an identity element

in the component indexed by the identity element

of
Q
. We require
1
to act as the identity on
P
-graded modules.

5.13

2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
with group law

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})=(\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau^{*}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu+\mu^{\prime},\nu+\nu^{\prime}).

Proposition 5.26
.

The collection
(\mathcal{A}_{n}(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu))_{(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)\in Q}
with the composition of operators is a unital
Q
-graded algebra.

Proof.

An admissible difference operator of degree
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})
sends a section of an admissible line bundle
\mathcal{M}_{1}=p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
to a section of
\mathcal{M}_{2}=p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k+\mu^{\prime}/2,n}
with
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes\tau^{*}_{\nu^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}
. An operator of degree
(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)
sends this section to a section of
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{3}\otimes T_{k+\mu/2+\mu^{\prime}/2,n}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{3}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau^{*}_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\otimes\tau^{*}_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}.

It is clear that the
\mu
-components of the degree add, so the composition has degree
(\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu+\mu^{\prime}
,
\nu+\nu^{\prime})
, as required. The identity element is the multiplication by constant function 1, a section of the trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Remark 5.27
.

There is a slight abuse of notation, since
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)
is defined for a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
and not for its equivalence class. The point is that
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)
for equivalent bundles

are canonically isomorphic: if

is an admissible difference operator of degree
(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)
and

is an isomorphism then

is an difference operator of degree
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu,\nu)
. This establishes the isomorphism

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\colon\ \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)\to\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu,\nu),

which we claim is independent of
\psi
. Indeed any two choices of
\psi
differ by the composition with an automorphism of
\mathcal{L}
. Since
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{L})=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
differ by multiplication by a nonzero scalar which does not affect
\bar{\psi}
.

Let
P
be the set of pairs
(\mathcal{L},\mu)
with
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n}))
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
Q
acts on

via

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L},\mu,\nu)\cdot(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})=(\mathcal{L}\otimes\tau_{\nu}^{*}\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\mu+\mu^{\prime}).

Let
\mathcal{M}=p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
be an admissible bundle on
X_{k,n}
.

Admissible difference operators map sections of admissible line bundles to sections of admissible line bundles. This is formalized as follows.

Proposition 5.28
.

The collection of vector spaces
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mer}}\big(\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n}),p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes T_{k,n}\big)
, labeled by

, with

is a

-graded module over the

-graded unital algebra

of admissible difference operators.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the definition, see Definition
5.19
(i).

6
Action of the elliptic dynamical quantum group

In this section we construct an action of the elliptic dynamical quantum group associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
on the extended equivariant elliptic cohomology
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{n})
of the union of cotangent bundles of the
Grassmannians of planes in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The action is by
S_{n}
-equivariant admissible difference operators acting on admissible line bundles on the cohomology scheme. Thus each generator
L_{ij}(w)

(
i,j\in\{1,2\})
of the elliptic dynamical quantum group acts on sections of any admissible line bundle by an admissible difference operator of some degree
(\mathcal{L}_{ij}(w),\mu_{ij},\nu_{ij})
which we give below. We also compute the action on
T
-equivariant elliptic cohomology classes and use the
S_{n}
-equivariance to show that the action descends to an action on
G
-equivariant classes, with
G=U(n)\times U(1)
.

We construct the action in such a way that at the generic fibre of

(i.e., for fixed

) defines a morphism of representations from the tensor product of evaluation representations. In other words, suppose that

\displaystyle L_{ij}(w)v_{I}=\sum_{K}L_{ij}(w,z,y,\lambda)_{I}^{K}v_{K},

for some meromorphic coefficients
L_{ij}(w,z,y,\lambda)_{I}^{K}
. Then we want that

\displaystyle L_{ij}(w)\operatorname{Stab}(v_{I})=\sum_{K}L_{ij}(w,z,y,\lambda)_{I}^{K}\operatorname{Stab}(v_{K}).

(6.1)

The matrix coefficients

are certain meromorphic functions of

with theta function-like transformation properties and can thus be considered as meromorphic sections line bundles on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
. Therefore each summand on the right-hand side is a meromorphic section of an admissible line bundle. The content of the following theorem is that the sum defines uniquely an admissible difference operator.

Theorem 6.1
.

(i)

The formula (
6.1
) uniquely defines admissible difference operators
L_{ij}(w)
,
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, of degree
(\mathcal{L}_{ij}(w),2(i-j),\epsilon(j))
with
\epsilon(1)=-1
,
\epsilon(2)=1
, for some
S_{n}
-equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{ij}(w)
on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
.

(ii)

These operators obey the RLL relations of the elliptic dynamical quantum group in the form

\displaystyle\mu_{\ell}R(w_{1}\!-w_{2},y,\lambda)^{(12)}L(w_{1})^{(13)}L(w_{2})^{(23)}=L(w_{2})^{(23)}L(w_{1})^{(13)}\mu_{r}R(w_{1}\!-w_{2},y,\lambda)^{(12)}.

Here the coefficients of the quadratic relations are in
\mathcal{A}^{0}_{n}
and the action of
\mu_{\ell}
,
\mu_{r}
is on each matrix element of

.

The proof of this theorem is by explicit description of the action and is parallel to the case of Yangians and affine quantum enveloping algebras,
[

15
,
16
]
. We give the formulae for the action in Sections

,
6.2
and
6.3
. The proof of Theorem

is in Section

.

By Theorem

, the generators
L_{ij}(w)
send meromorphic sections of admissible bundles to meromorphic sections of admissible bundles. The next result gives a more precise control on the poles of coefficients. We give the action on holomorphic sections, i.e., equivariant elliptic cohomology classes, both for the torus

(i)

Let

as

-equivariant line bundles on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})

induce well-defined operators from

Remark 6.3
.

Let

be the projection onto the first factor. Since the action of the generators

is by admissible difference operators it preserves the fiber of

6.1
Action of the Gelfand–Zetlin subalgebra

The Gelfand–Zetlin subalgebra is the commutative subalgebra generated by
L_{22}(w)
and the determinant
\Delta(w)
. As shown in Section

these operators act diagonally in the basis

of

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}_{I}=\sum_{|J|=k}\frac{w^{-}_{J}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}\operatorname{Stab}(v_{J}),

(cf. (

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
I,K\subset[n]
,
|I|=|K|=k
. The restriction of
\hat{\xi}_{I}
to the component of
\hat{E}(X_{k,n})
labeled by

\displaystyle\iota_{K}^{*}\hat{\xi}_{I}=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}),&$I=K$,\\
0,&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

From the definition of
\hat{\xi}_{I}
and
\operatorname{Stab}(v_{J})
we have

\displaystyle\iota_{K}^{*}\hat{\xi}_{I}=\iota_{K}^{*}\bigg(\sum_{J}\frac{w_{J}^{-}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}w^{+}_{J}(t,z,y,\lambda)\bigg)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\iota_K^*\hat{\xi}_I}{}=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)}\sum_{J}w_{J}^{-}(z_{I},z,y,\lambda)w^{+}_{J}(z_{K},z,y,\lambda),

which, using Corollary
3.31
, proves our statement.
∎

Thus we can write any section
(s_{I})_{I\subset[n]}
of an admissible bundle as linear combination

\displaystyle\sum\frac{s_{I}\hat{\xi}_{I}}{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b})}.

Since the

are eigenvectors the action of the Gelfand–Zetlin algebra is given by admissible difference operator with diagonal matrices of coefficients.

The action of the determinant
\Delta(w)
is easiest to describe: it is given by an admissible difference operator of degree
(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}(w),0,0)
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}(w)=\mathcal{L}(N_{\Delta},v_{\Delta}(w))

\displaystyle N_{\Delta}(z,y,\lambda)=\sum_{a=1}^{n}(2z_{a}+y)y,\qquad v_{\Delta}(w;z,y,\lambda)=-wy.

Since

and

are symmetric under permutations of the variables

, the corresponding bundle is naturally

-equivariant. The determinant acts on sections of any admissible line bundle

on

it acts by multiplication by the section

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\theta(w-z_{i}+y)}{\theta(w-z_{i})}

of
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}(w)
.

The action of
L_{22}(w)
is by an operator of degree
(\mathcal{L}_{22}(w),0,1)
. It is defined on the components by

\displaystyle\iota^{*}_{K}L_{22}(w)s=\prod_{i\in K}\frac{\theta(w-z_{i})}{\theta(w-z_{i}-y)}\tau_{1}^{*}\iota_{K}^{*}s,

and

, which is an

-equivariant line bundle.

Lemma 6.5
.

These formulae define
S_{n}
-equivariant admissible difference operators
\Delta(w)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}(w),0,0)
and
L_{22}(w)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L}_{22}(w),0,1)

Both operators are defined by diagonal matrices

in the notation of Definition
5.19

\displaystyle\varphi_{K,K}|_{z_{a}=z_{b}}=\varphi_{\tilde{K},\tilde{K}}|_{z_{a}=z_{b}},\qquad\tilde{K}=K\smallsetminus\{a\}\cup\{b\}.

7
Shuffle products and stable envelopes for subgroups

The stable envelope of
[
12
]
is a map from the equivariant cohomology of the fixed point set for a torus action on a Nakajima variety to the equivariant cohomology of the variety. The goal of this section is to extend this interpretation of the stable envelope to the elliptic case for cotangent bundles of Grassmannians. In our construction the stable envelope is built out of weight functions, which in turn are obtained from shuffle products of elementary weight functions associated with the one-point spaces
T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(0,1)
,
T^{*}\mathrm{Gr}(1,1)
. Thus the first step is to extend the fiber-by-fiber construction of the shuffle product of Section
3
to a shuffle product defined on sections of the coherent sheaf
\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k,n}
on
\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
. By using the isomorphism (outside the divisor
D
) of Theorem
5.16
, we get a shuffle product on the sections of the sheaf of elliptic cohomology classes
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{T}(X_{k,n})
. The
n
-fold shuffle product of classes in
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{ell}}_{T}(X_{k,1})
,
k=0,1
is then essentially the stable envelope.

We propose to view shuffle products of factors of an arbitrary number of factors as stable envelopes corresponding to subgroups of
T
. Their geometric interpretation is that they correspond to maps from the cohomology of the fixed point set for the action of a subgroup of the torus
T
, cf.
[
12
, Section 3.6]
.

The basic case, which as we shall see corresponds to the shuffle product of two factors, is the subgroup
B_{m}\subset U(1)^{n}

\displaystyle B=B_{m}=\{(\underbrace{z,\dots,z}_{m},1,\dots,1)\in A\colon z\in U(1)\},

isomorphic to
U(1)
.

Fixed points for the action of this subgroup on
\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)
are
k
-planes of the form
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, with
V_{1}
in the span of the first
m
coordinate axes and
V_{2}
in the span of the last
n-m
coordinate axes. Thus the fixed point set decomposes into connected components according to the dimension of
V_{1}
. Each of these components is a product of Grassmannians. Similarly, the
B_{m}
-invariant part of the cotangent space at a fixed point splits as a direct sum of cotangent spaces at the factors and we get an isomorphism

\displaystyle X_{k,n}^{B_{m}}\cong\sqcup_{d=0}^{k}X_{d,m}\times X_{k-d,n-m}.

As above we consider the action of
A_{n}=U(1)^{n}
on
X_{k,n}
. Then the embedding
X_{d,m}\times X_{k-d,n-m}\hookrightarrow X_{k,n}
is
A_{m}\times A_{n-m}=A_{n}
-equivariant. The Künneth formula
[
7
]
predicts that this embedding induces a map

\displaystyle E_{A_{m}}(X_{d,m})\times E_{A_{n-m}}(X_{k-d,n-m})\to E_{A_{n}}(X_{k,n}),

(7.1)

In the description as a fiber product,

\displaystyle E_{A_{m}}(X_{d,m})=E^{(d)}\times E^{(m-d)}\times_{E^{(m)}}E^{m},

and the map is the obvious one:
((t^{\prime},s^{\prime},z^{\prime}),(t^{\prime\prime},s^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime\prime}))\mapsto(p(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}),p(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}),z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})
. Here
t^{\prime}\in E^{(d)}
,
t^{\prime\prime}\in E^{(k-d)}
,
p\colon E^{(d)}\times E^{(k-d)}\to E^{(k)}
is the canonical projection and similarly for the other factors.

As in Section
4.3
, we consider the extended equivariant elliptic cohomology
\hat{E}_{T_{n}}(X_{k,n})=E_{A_{n}}(X_{k,n})\times E^{2}
for the torus
T_{n}=A_{n}\times U(1)
where the additional
U(1)
factor acts by multiplication on each cotangent space. We then have the corresponding embedding

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{T_{m}}(X_{d,m})\times_{E^{2}}\hat{E}_{T_{n-m}}(X_{k-d,n-m})\to\hat{E}_{T_{n}}(X_{k,n}),

(7.2)

where the map to
E^{2}
is the projection onto the second factor. Both are schemes over
\hat{E}_{T_{n}}(\mathrm{pt})=\hat{E}_{T_{m}}(\mathrm{pt})\times_{E^{2}}E_{T_{n-m}}(\mathrm{pt})
.

Proposition 7.1
.

The shuffle product of Proposition
3.10
defines a map

\displaystyle*\colon\ \tau^{*}_{n^{\prime\prime}-2k^{\prime\prime}}\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\boxtimes\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}\to\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k,n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}

of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})}
-modules, where
k=k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}
,
n=n^{\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}=\mathcal{L}\Big(k^{\prime\prime}y\Big((n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})y-2\sum\limits_{a=1}^{n^{\prime}}z_{a}\Big),0\Big)\in\mathrm{Pic}\big(\hat{E}_{T_{n}}(\mathrm{pt})\big)
.

Proof.

The sheaf
\Theta^{+}_{k,n}
is defined by the quadratic form
N^{\Theta}_{k,n}(t,z,y,\lambda)
, see (
5.4
). Let us write
t=(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})
,
z=(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})
, with
t^{\prime}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{k^{\prime}})
,
t^{\prime\prime}=(t_{k^{\prime}+1},\dots,t_{k})
and similarly for
z
. Then
\tau^{*}_{n^{\prime\prime}-2k^{\prime\prime}}\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\boxtimes\bar{\Theta}^{+}_{k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
is associated with the quadratic form

\displaystyle M(t,z,y,\lambda)=N^{\Theta}_{k^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(t^{\prime},z^{\prime},y,\lambda+y(n^{\prime\prime}-2k^{\prime\prime}))+N^{\Theta}_{k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime\prime},y,\lambda).

The shuffle product maps a section of this bundle to a section of a bundle associated with the sum of this quadratic form and the quadratic forms of the theta functions in
\varphi^{+}
, see Proposition
3.6
, namely

\displaystyle M(t,z,y,\lambda)+\sum_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}\sum_{l=k^{\prime}+1}^{k}\big((t_{j}-t_{l}+y)^{2}-(t_{j}-t_{l})^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{M(t,z,y,\lambda)}{}+\sum_{l=k^{\prime}+1}^{k}\sum_{a=1}^{n^{\prime}}(t_{l}-z_{a}+y)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}\sum_{b=n^{\prime}+1}^{n}(t_{j}-z_{b})^{2}.

It is straightforward to verify that this is equal to

\displaystyle N^{\Theta}_{k,n}(t,z,y,\lambda)+k^{\prime\prime}y\left((n^{\prime}-k^{\prime})y-2\sum_{a=1}^{n^{\prime}}z_{a}\right).

This shows that the shuffle product takes values in
\Theta^{+}_{k,n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime},n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime}}
. The fact that it actually takes values in the subsheaf defined by the vanishing condition follows from Proposition
3.10
.
∎

Two special cases give the stable envelope of Section

Appendix A
Axiomatic definition of elliptic stable envelopes

In this section we give an axiomatic definition of the elliptic stable envelopes in the spirit of Maulik–Okounkov
[
12
, Section 3.3]
, see also
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Recall that
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
is a meromorphic section (with controlled denominators) of an appropriate line bundle over
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
. The scheme
\hat{E}_{T}(X_{k,n})
has components
Y_{J}=\iota_{J}\hat{E}_{T}(\mathrm{pt})
, and the restriction of a section to
Y_{J}
is the result of substituting the variables
t_{i}
by
z_{J}=(z_{i})_{i\in J}
.

A meromorphic section of an admissible line bundle
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
restricted to
Y_{J}
can be written as a meromorphic function
F\colon\C^{n+2}\to\C
whose transformation properties with respect to the lattice
\Z^{n+2}+\tau\Z^{n+2}
are determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, see Remark
5.4
. Below we will consider special forms of such functions.

Theorem A.1
.

For any
I
the section
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
satisfies the following properties.

•

It is a meromorphic section of an admissible line bundle
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
.

•

The restriction of
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
to
Y_{I}
, written as a function
\C^{n+2}\to\C
with transformation properties determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, is

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{a\in I,\,b\in\bar{I}}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+\epsilon(a,b)y)}{\prod\limits_{a\in I}\theta(\lambda-(w(a,I)+1)y)},

where
\epsilon(a,b)
is defined in Lemma
3.32
and
w(a,I)
is defined in (
3.4
).

•

The restriction of
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
to any
Y_{J}
, written as a function
\C^{n+2}\to\C
with transformation properties determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N,0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, is of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi_{I}}\prod_{a\in J}\prod_{b\in\bar{J},\,b<a}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)\cdot F_{I,J},

(A.1)

where
F_{I,J}
is a holomorphic function.

Moreover, these three properties uniquely determine
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
.

Remark A.2
.

From the second property one can calculate the quadratic form

\displaystyle N_{I}=-2\sum_{a\in\bar{I}}n(a,I)z_{a}y-2\sum_{a\in I}z_{a}(\lambda+n(a,\bar{I})y)

\displaystyle\hphantom{N_I =}{}+(k(n-k)-\sum_{a\in I}n(a,\bar{I}))y^{2}-\sum_{a\in I}(\lambda-(n(a,I)+1)y+n(a,\bar{I})y)^{2},

cf. (
5.2
).

Remark A.3
.

Lemma
3.32
(ii) implies the triangularity property

•

the restriction of
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
to
Y_{J}
is 0 unless
J\leq I
.

According to Theorem
A.1
this property is a consequence of the three properties listed.

Remark A.4
.

The third listed property is a local version of a support condition used in the axiomatic description of cohomological stable envelopes in
[
12
, Theorem 3.3.4(i)]
; see also the corresponding axiom in K-theory in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1(I)]
.

Proof.

The first two properties of
c^{*}w^{+}_{I}
are claimed in Proposition
5.9
and Lemma
3.32
(ii). Inspecting the explicit formula for
w^{+}_{I}
in Section
3.11
one finds that, after substitution
t_{i}
by
z_{a}
,
a\in J
, all non-zero terms are of the form (
A.1
), which proves the third property.

Now we prove that the three properties uniquely determine
c^{*}w_{I}^{+}
. Let a section satisfy the three listed properties, and let
\kappa_{I}
be the difference of that section and
c^{*}w_{I}^{+}
.
Assume that
\kappa_{I}
is not 0. Then there exists a
J
such that
\kappa_{I}
restricted to
Y_{J}
is not 0. For a total ordering
\prec
refining the partial order
<
on the cardinality
k
subsets of
[n]
let us choose
J
to be the largest with the property
\kappa_{I}|_{Y_{J}}\not=0
. We have
J\not=I
because of the second property.

We claim that
\kappa_{I}|_{Y_{J}}
, written as a function
\C^{n+2}\to\C
with transformation properties determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N_{I},0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, is of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi_{I}}\prod_{a\in J}\prod_{b\in\bar{J},\,b<a}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)\cdot\prod_{a\in J}\prod_{b\in\bar{J},\,b>a}\theta(z_{b}-z_{a})\cdot F_{1},

(A.2)

where
F_{1}
is holomorphic. The fact that this function can be written in the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\psi_{I}}\prod_{a\in J}\prod_{b\in\bar{J},\,b<a}\theta(z_{a}-z_{b}+y)\cdot F_{2},

(A.3)

with
F_{2}
holomorphic, is explicit from the third property. We need to prove that
F_{2}
is the product of
\prod\limits_{a\in J}\prod\limits_{b\in\bar{J},\,b>a}\theta(z_{b}-z_{a})
and a holomorphic function. Let
a\in J
,
b\in\bar{J}
and
b>a
. Denote
J^{\prime}=(J-\{a\})\cup\{b\}
. Observe that
J<J^{\prime}
and hence
J\prec J^{\prime}
. From the choice of
J
therefore it follows that
\kappa_{I}
restricted to
Y_{J^{\prime}}
is 0. The diagonal
\Delta_{J,J^{\prime}}=\{z_{a}=z_{b}\}
is included both in
Y_{J}
and
Y_{J^{\prime}}
, hence we obtain that the substitution of
z_{a}=z_{b}
into
\kappa_{I}|_{Y_{J}}
vanishes. It follows that the function
F_{2}
vanishes on the hyperplane
z_{a}=z_{b}
and its translates by the lattice
\Z^{n+2}+\tau\Z^{n+2}
. The zeros of
\theta(z_{b}-z_{a})
are exactly these hyperplanes and are of first order, therefore
F_{2}
can be written as a product of
\theta(z_{b}-z_{a})
times a holomorphic function. Iterating this argument for all
(a,b)
with
a\in J
,
b\in\bar{J}
,
b>a
we obtain that (
A.3
) is in fact of the form (
A.2
), what we claimed.

Observe that the product of theta functions in (
A.2
) is the numerator of
c^{*}w_{J}^{+}|_{Y_{J}}
. Hence we obtain that (
A.2
) further equals

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{a\in J}\theta(\lambda-(w(a,J)+1)y)}{\psi_{I}}\cdot c^{*}w_{J}^{+}|_{Y_{J}}\cdot F_{1}.

Since the transformation properties of
\kappa_{I}|_{Y_{J}}
are determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N_{I},0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, and those of
c^{*}w_{J}^{+}|_{Y_{J}}
are determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N_{J},0)\otimes\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
, we have that the transformation properties of

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{a\in J}\theta(\lambda-(w(a,J)+1)y)}{\psi_{I}}\cdot F_{1}

(A.4)

are determined by
p_{T}^{*}\mathcal{L}(N_{I}-N_{J},0)
– the factor
\mathcal{T}_{k,n}
canceled.

Let
a\in J\cap\bar{I}
, and consider (
A.4
) as a function of
z_{a}
, let us call it
f(z_{a})
. Since the first factor (the fraction) only depends on
\lambda
and
y
,
f
is a
holomorphic
function of
z_{a}
for generic

. Comparing the
z_{a}
dependence of

and

we obtain that

\displaystyle f(z_{a}+\tau)={\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(\lambda+sy)}f(z_{a}),\qquad f(z_{a}+1)=f(z_{a}),

(A.5)

for some integer

. Using the 1-periodicity, we expand

\displaystyle f(z_{a})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}mz_{a}},

and using the first transformation property of (

) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m}a_{m}{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}mz_{a}}\big({\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}m\tau}-{\rm e}^{-2\pi{\rm i}(\lambda+sy)}\big)=0,

implying

for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. We obtained

, and in turn,
\kappa_{I}|_{Y_{J}}=0
. This is a contradiction proving that
\kappa_{I}
is 0 on all
Y_{J}
.
∎

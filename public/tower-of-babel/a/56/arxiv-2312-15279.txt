Hamiltonian for Weyl Transverse Gravity


Abstract
In this short note we determine Hamiltonian for Weyl transverse gravity. We find primary, secondary and tertiary constraints and calculate Poisson brackets between them. We also show that gauge fixing in Weyl transverse gravity leads to the Hamiltonian for unimodular gravity.

Introduction and Summary

Diffeomorphism invariance is fundamental postulate of theoretical physics that says that the basic laws of nature should be formulated using action functional which is manifestly invariant under diffeomorphism transformations. On the other hand we can still keep in mind that nature at fundamental level does not have to respect this presumption. Famous example of such proposal is Hořava-Lifshitz gravity

3
Preservation of primary constraints

Theory with constraints is well defined when the primary constraints
are preserved during the time evolution of system. In case of Weyl transverse gravity these constraints are given in (
20

The time evolution of any phase-space function is given by its Poisson bracket with the bare Hamiltonian
H_{B}
. Note that we have following non-zero canonical Poisson
brackets

\displaystyle\left\{h_{ij}(\mathbf{x}),\pi^{kl}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{i}^{k}\delta_{j}^{l}+\delta_{i}^{l}\delta_{j}^{k})\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\left\{N^{i}(\mathbf{x}),\pi_{j}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\delta^{i}_{j}\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{N(\mathbf{x}),\pi_{N}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,\quad\left\{\phi(\mathbf{x}),p_{\phi}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ ,

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda(\mathbf{x}),p_{\lambda}(\mathbf{y})\right\}=\delta(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\ \quad

so that time evolution of the constraint

\partial_{0}\pi_{i}=\left\{\pi_{i},H_{B}\right\}=-\mathcal{H}_{i}

(22)

and we see that the constraint

\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0\ .

(23)

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\frac{1}{\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega}\mathbf{H}(t)+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\ ,\quad\mathbf{H}=\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\ ,

(44)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
obeys condition

\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=0

(45)

as follows from definition (
44
). Then (
43
)
does not restrict
\mathbf{H}
while there are
(n-1)\times\infty-1
constraints

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\approx 0\ .

(46)

Before we will discuss
separation of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
in more details we should determine Poisson brackets between
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\mathbf{x})
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(\mathbf{y})
.
It is again convenient to introduce smeared form of

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

\mathbf{T}_{T}(X)=\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega X\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\ .

(47)

Then after some calculations that are similar to the calculations when
we checked stability of constraint
\mathcal{D}\approx 0
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{T}_{T}(X),\mathbf{T}_{T}(Y)\right\}=\mathbf{T}_{S}((X\partial_{i}Y-Y\partial_{i}X)h^{ij}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h})+

\displaystyle+\mathbf{D}(\frac{2}{n-1}(Y\partial_{l}\partial_{k}X-X\partial_{l}\partial_{k}Y)h^{kl}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h})

which shows that Poisson brackets between smeared form of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
vanish on the constraint surface
\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0\ ,\mathcal{D}\approx 0
.
Now we can return to the definition of constraint
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. We introduce its smeared form

\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{T}(X)=\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega\hat{X}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\ ,

(49)

where
\hat{X}
obeys the condition
\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\hat{X}\omega=0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{T}(\hat{X}),\mathbf{H}\right\}=\left\{\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega X\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0},\mathbf{H}\right\}=

\displaystyle=\left\{\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega\hat{X}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}-\frac{1}{V}\mathbf{H}),\mathbf{H}\right\}=

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{T}_{S}(\partial_{i}Xh^{ij}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h})+\mathbf{D}(\frac{2}{n-1}(\partial_{l}\partial_{k}Xh^{kl}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h}))\approx 0.

Finally we will calculate Poisson bracket of smeared form of the constraints
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
using the Poisson brackets (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{T}(\hat{X}),\hat{\mathbf{T}}_{T}(\hat{Y})\right\}=\mathbf{T}_{S}((\hat{X}\partial_{i}\hat{Y}-\hat{Y}\partial_{i}\hat{X})h^{ij}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h})+

\displaystyle+\mathbf{D}(\frac{2}{n-1}(\hat{Y}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}\hat{X}-\hat{X}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}\hat{Y})h^{kl}\frac{\phi^{2}\omega^{2}}{h})

that shows that
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
are first class constraints. However it is important to stress an existence of global mode
\mathbf{H}
. In fact, the time evolution of
\mathbf{H}
is equal to

\frac{d\mathbf{H}}{dt}=\left\{\mathbf{H},H_{B}\right\}\approx\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}\omega\frac{1}{\kappa}\lambda\phi^{\frac{2}{n}}\left\{\mathbf{H},\mathcal{U}\right\}=0

(52)

using the fact that
H_{B}=\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{T}_{S}(N^{i})+\int d^{n-1}\mathbf{x}(\frac{1}{\kappa}\lambda\omega\phi^{\frac{2}{n}}\mathcal{U}+v_{N}\pi_{N}+v^{i}\pi_{i}+v^{\lambda}p_{\lambda})+\mathbf{D}(\Sigma)

and the fact that
\mathbf{D}(\Omega)
is the first class constraint.

The result (
52
) shows
that
\mathbf{H}=\mathrm{const}
and corresponds to the cosmological constant of theory which is the same situation as in the case of unimodular gravity. In fact, we show in the next section how unimodular gravity arises from WTG by gauge fixing
Weyl symmetry.

3.2
Gauge Fixing of Weyl Symmetry

As we argued in previous section Weyl transverse gravity possesses gauge symmetry which is Weyl rescaling of metric. Reflection of this fact is the presence of the first class constraint
\mathcal{D}\approx 0
. We can fix this symmetry by introducing gauge fixing function. In principle there are many such functions where the only requirement that this function has to obey is that it has to have non-zero Poisson bracket with
\mathcal{D}
. We choose the simplest one when the gauge fixing function
\mathcal{F}
has the form

\mathcal{F}\equiv\phi-1\approx 0\ .

(53)

It is easy so see that
\left\{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{D}\right\}=\frac{n}{2}\phi\neq 0
and hence
\mathcal{F}
is legitimate gauge fixing function. Then
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{D}
are second class constraints
that are strongly zero where
\mathcal{F}
means that
\phi=1
while from
\mathcal{D}=0
we obtain
p_{\phi}
to be equal to

p_{\phi}=-\frac{2}{n}\pi\ .

(54)

Inserting this relation to the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
we obtain gauge fixed form of
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}(fixed)=\frac{\kappa}{h}(\pi^{ij}h_{ik}h_{jl}-\frac{1}{n-2}\pi^{2})-\frac{1}{\kappa}r

using the fact that
\Pi^{ij}(p_{\phi}=-\frac{2}{n}\pi)
is equal to

\Pi^{ij}(p_{\phi}=-\frac{2}{n}\pi)=\pi^{ij}-\frac{1}{n-1}\pi h^{ij}\ .

(56)

It is important to stress that we fixed the gauge

after
complete canonical analysis of WTG was performed
with explicit identification of the zero mode part of Hamiltonian as
cosmological constant and we also identified
(n-1)\times\infty-1

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\approx 0
as first class constraints.
In other words we have explicitly shown that gauge fixing
in Weyl transverse gravity leads to unimodular theory of gravity which
is nice consistency check.

The p p -CurlCurl : Spaces, traces, coercivity and a Helmholtz decomposition in L p L^{p}
M arc L aforest

Abstract
This work provides the foundation for the finite element analysis of an elliptic problem which is the rotational analogue of the p p -Laplacian and which appears as a model of the magnetic induction in a high-temperature superconductor operating near it’s critical current. Whereas the function theory for the p p -Laplacian requires standard results in L p L^{p} Sobolev spaces, this problem requires an extension to L p L^{p} spaces of the well-known L 2 L^{2} theory for divergence free vector fields, as used in the finite element method applied to incompressible flows and electromagnetic radiation. Among other things, the analysis requires extensions to L p L^{p} of the well-known H ⁡ ( div , Ω ) H(\operatorname{div};\Omega) and H ⁡ ( curl , Ω ) H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega) , extensions of traces and Green’s theorem, a Helmholtz decomposition and finally a Friedrich’s inequality. In this paper, we provide a proof of the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of our so-called p p -CurlCurl problem. In a companion paper, the analysis is extended to treat continuous and finite element solutions of the nonlinear parabolic problem whose spatial term is the p p -CurlCurl operator. Many of the results presented here are either already known, known in slightly different forms or are proven with the help of techniques that are already well-known. The main novelty of this paper appears to be the structured form of this L p L^{p} theory and our form of the Helmholtz decomposition and of the Friedrich’s inequality. In this respect, we note that some of these results can be found in the works of M. Dauge, M. Mitrea, I. Mitrea and …

\displaystyle W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\Big|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\Big\},

(13)

\displaystyle W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\Big|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\Big\}.

(14)

It is important to observe that these are again Banach spaces with norms, respectively

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)}&:=&\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\\
\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}&:=&\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\end{array}

(15)

and that there exist continuous injections
W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3}\hookrightarrow W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
and

W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3}\hookrightarrow W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
. On the other hand, these injections are not surjective.
We define the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
in
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
and
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)

to be respectively
W^{p}_{0}({\text{div}};\Omega)
and
W^{p}_{0}({\text{curl}};\Omega)
.

We need to know how to interpret the values along the boundary of functions in either
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
or
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
.
We begin the analysis of
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
with a density result which will allow us to extends many operators quite naturally to
all elements in these spaces. Theorem
12
is similar to Theorem 3.22 of
[
27
]
and its proof is simply an adaptation
to
L^{p}
spaces.

Theorem 12
.

Assuming that
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary and
1<p<\infty
, then the set
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
is dense in

W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)
.

The proof of this result requires three elementary lemmas.

Lemma 13
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the space
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
is dense in both
W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that the norms in
W^{p}(\text{div};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
W^{p}(\text{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})

are bounded by the norm in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The result then follows by the density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, Corollary 3.23 of
[
1
]
.
∎

Lemma 14
.

For
1<p<\infty
and any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\,\phi\,d\mathbf{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{u}\,\nabla\phi\,d\mathbf{x}=0.

(16)

For any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\,\boldsymbol{\phi}\,d\mathbf{x}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{u}\,\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi}\,d\mathbf{x}=0.

(17)

Proof.

The result follows immediately by density using the classical Divergence Theorem.
∎

We now have all the tools to tackle the proof of the density of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
inside
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
.
The main idea of the proof is that
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
will always be dense in the dual of a ”smooth” function space. The proof proceeds
by identifying this fact without having to obtain a complete characterization of the dual.

Proof of Theorem
12
.

The idea for this proof is taken from
[
10
]

where it was used to study a slightly different space. The techniques presented here will be reused
in the proof of Theorem
18
.
We will show that if
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)^{\prime}
is identically zero over
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}

then
\ell\equiv 0
. By Theorem 3.5 of
[
31
]
, this suffices to show that the closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
equals
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
.

Define
\Omega^{(4)}
to be the union of four distinct copies of
\Omega
and consider the obvious embedding

\displaystyle I:W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega^{(4)})

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\mathbf{u},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}).

This embedding is an isometry onto it’s image. Any linear functional
\ell
over
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
defines
a linear functional over the closed image
I(W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega))
and therefore, by the Hahn-Banach Theorem,
extends to a linear functional over all of
L^{p}(\Omega^{(4)})
. Since the dual of
L^{p}
is
L^{q}
, for
any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
this extension can be written in the form

\ell(\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega}+(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},w)_{\Omega}

for some (non-unique)
\mathbf{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}
and
w\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. Let
E_{0}
be the extension by
zero of any element in
L^{q}(\Omega)
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(Theorem 2.30 of
[
1
]
) and let
E
be any extension operator
from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then

\ell(\mathbf{u})=(E\mathbf{u},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},w)_{\Omega}.

Assuming that
\ell(\boldsymbol{\phi})=0
for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
, then for all
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}

we have

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\psi},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi},E_{0}w)_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=0.

In a distributional sense, we therefore have
E_{0}\mathbf{v}=\nabla E_{0}w
. This allows us to first recognize that

E_{0}w\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, both
E_{0}w
and
\nabla E_{0}w=E_{0}\mathbf{v}
vanish outside
of
\Omega
and therefore by Theorem 5.29 of
[
1
]
,
E_{0}w\big|_{\Omega}=w\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
.
This implies that there exists a sequence
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
converging to
w
in the space

W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using identity (
16
), we can now conclude that for any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)

\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle(E\mathbf{u},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(E(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}),E_{0}w)_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(E\mathbf{u},\nabla w_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(E(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}),w_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mathbf{u},\nabla w_{n})_{\Omega}+(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},w_{n})_{\Omega}=0

where the second to last step required that
w_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

The next step is to study the traces of functions in
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
. For all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
, we can define
the normal trace
\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\boldsymbol{\phi}\cdot\mathbf{n}
where
we have assumed that the domain is sufficiently smooth to define the unit outwards normal
\mathbf{n}
.
The question is to know where this quantity will belong when we extend this to
the weaker space
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, unit outwards normal
\mathbf{n}
, and
1<p<\infty
.
Then

(i)

the mapping
\gamma_{n}
defined on
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
can be extended
to a continuous and surjective map
\gamma_{n}:W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)\longrightarrow B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)^{\prime}\equiv B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)
.

(ii)

the following Green’s formula holds for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)
and
v\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)
,

(\mathbf{u},\nabla v)+(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},v)=\langle\gamma_{n}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{0}(v)\rangle.

(18)

Proof.

Given the elliptic regularity results in
L^{p}
, the proof is a natural adaptation of the argument used in
[
27
]
for the
proof of their Theorem 3.24.

We begin by examining Green’s formula. For any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and

\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
, we have

(\boldsymbol{\psi},\nabla\phi)+(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi},\phi)=\langle\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\psi}),\gamma_{0}({\phi})\rangle.

By density, this must hold for all
\phi\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
. For
v\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)
,
using the previous identity as a weak definition of
\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\psi})
, we therefore have

\displaystyle\big|\langle\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\psi}),\gamma_{0}(v)\rangle\big|\leq

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\cdot\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\cdot\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)}\cdot\|v\|_{W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)^{3}.

Pick any
\mu\in B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)
and consider
v\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)
the unique weak solution to

-\Delta v+v=0\quad\text{on $\Omega$}\qquad\gamma_{0}(v)=\mu\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}

guaranteed by Theorem
4
. Using the fact that this solution depends continuously on the
boundary conditions, estimate (
6
), we have the bound

\big|\langle\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\psi}),\mu\rangle\big|\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)}.

According to the definition of the dual norm (
5
), we may conclude that the map
\gamma_{n}
can
be extended by continuity to all of
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)

with image in the dual of
B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)
, also known as
B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)

(since
\partial\Omega
is closed).
Green’s formula (
18
) follows immediately by density since
\gamma_{0}(v)=\mu\in B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)
.

To prove the surjectivity of
\gamma_{n}
, choose any
\eta\in B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)
and construct
the unique weak solution
\phi
to

-\Delta\phi+\phi=0\quad\text{on $\Omega$}\qquad\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\phi=\eta\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$.}

(19)

By Theorem
6
, the solution
\phi
exists and belongs to
W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)
. If we define
\mathbf{u}=\nabla\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}

then the weak form of (
19
) gives the identity

(\mathbf{u},\nabla\psi)+(\phi,\psi)=0,\qquad\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

In a distributional sense, we therefore have
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
. Moreover,

\gamma_{n}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\phi=\eta
and the map is surjective.
∎

Lemma 16
.

For a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
, we have

W^{p}_{0}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)=\Big\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)\,\Big|\,\gamma_{n}(\mathbf{u})=0\Big\}.

Proof.

The proof given here is a non-trivial adaptation of the argument used in
[
27
]
, where duality was simpler
to handle because Hilbert spaces were involved. The trace
\gamma_{n}
clearly vanishes on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}

and therefore, by density,
\gamma_{n}
vanishes on
W^{p}_{0}(\text{div};\Omega)
. This proves the inclusion of

W^{p}_{0}(\text{div};\Omega)
into the set on the right. The other inclusion
can be demonstrated if we show that when
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)^{\prime}
vanishes on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
\ell
must also vanish
on any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
for which
\gamma_{n}(\mathbf{u})=0
.

Repeating the trick used in the proof of Theorem
12
, we exploit the embedding of

W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
into
L^{p}(\Omega^{(4)})
and the induced representation of every
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)^{\prime}
as

\ell(\boldsymbol{\psi})=(\boldsymbol{\psi},\mathbf{v})+(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi},w)

for some
\mathbf{v}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}
and
w\in L^{q}(\Omega)
. For all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
,

\ell(\boldsymbol{\phi})=0
and therefore
\mathbf{v}=\nabla w
in a distributional sense. This implies that
w\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)

and that
\ell
over
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
can be written as

\displaystyle\ell(\boldsymbol{\psi})=

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\psi},\nabla w)+(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi},w)=\langle\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\psi}),\gamma_{0}(w)\rangle.

If
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
is such that
\gamma_{n}(\mathbf{u})=0
then
\ell(\mathbf{u})=0
and
\mathbf{u}
belongs to the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
.
∎

We now turn to the set of spaces
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
and
W^{p}_{0}(\text{curl};\Omega)
. The analysis is very similar to the
one used for
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
and in fact, a suitable generalization of the differential operators involved here would have allowed us to treat both cases
simultaneously, as Mitrea and Mitrea did in
[
25
]
. The objective is to prove Green’s formula over these spaces and, in
particular, to characterize the traces of these spaces. In the case of
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
, we will show that the tangential trace operators,
defined for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
as
\gamma_{t}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\mathbf{n}\times\boldsymbol{\phi}
and

\gamma_{T}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=(\mathbf{n}\times\boldsymbol{\phi})\times\mathbf{n}
, are well-defined. We begin with a simple extension of
Theorem 5.29 from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 17
.

Assume
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p<\infty
and
E_{0}u
is the extension by zero of
u
to all
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
u
belongs to
W^{p}_{0}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
if and only if
E_{0}u
belongs to

W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

Theorem 5.29 in
[
1
]
is stated only for
W^{{s},{p}}(\Omega)
but the same proof shows that
this result also holds for
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem 18
.

Consider a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
.
Then the set
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
is dense in
W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
.

Proof.

We will show that if
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)^{\prime}
vanishes over
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
then
\ell
must be
identically zero.

As we did in the proof of Theorem
12
, there exists an isometric
embedding
I:W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega^{(6)})
,
I(\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{u})
.
Given any linear functional over
I(W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega))\subset L^{p}(\Omega^{(6)})
, the Hahn-Banach Theorem states that
can it be extended to a linear functional over
L^{p}(\Omega^{(6)})
, that is to an element in
L^{q}(\Omega^{(6)})
. The
linear functional can therefore be represented as

\ell(\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})_{\Omega}+(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})_{\Omega},\quad\forall\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega),

for some
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}
.

Let
E_{0}
be the extension by zero operator and let
E
be any extension operator over
L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
. Then

\ell(\mathbf{u})=(E\mathbf{u},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(E(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}),E_{0}\mathbf{w})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

For any
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
, Lemma
14
implies that

(\boldsymbol{\psi},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\psi},E_{0}\mathbf{w})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}=0.

In other words,
E_{0}\mathbf{v}=-\nabla\times E_{0}\mathbf{w}
in a distributional sense. Not only does this imply that

E_{0}\mathbf{w}\in W^{q}(\text{curl};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, but Lemma
17
shows that

\mathbf{w}\in W^{q}_{0}(\text{curl};\Omega)
.

We can therefore construct a sequence of
\mathbf{w}_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
which converges to
\mathbf{w}

with respect to the norm in
W^{q}(\text{div};\Omega)
. For any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
, we
combine these facts with formula (
17
) to deduce

\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle(E\mathbf{u},E_{0}\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(E(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}),E_{0}\mathbf{w})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-(E\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{w}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+(E(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}),\mathbf{w}_{n})_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}-(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{w}_{n})_{\Omega}+(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w}_{n})_{\Omega}=0.

This shows that the closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
must be
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega).

∎

Theorem 19
.

Consider a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
. Then

(i)

the mapping
\gamma_{t}
defined on
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
can be extended
to a continuous map
\gamma_{t}:W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)\longrightarrow(B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)^{3})^{\prime}\equiv B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
.

(ii)

the following Green’s formula holds for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
and
\mathbf{v}\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)^{3}
,

(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})=\langle\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{0}(\mathbf{v})\rangle.

(20)

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward extension of the one used in
[
27
]
.
For any
\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
, we have

(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi},\boldsymbol{\psi})-(\boldsymbol{\phi},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\psi})=\langle\gamma_{t}(\boldsymbol{\phi}),\gamma_{0}(\boldsymbol{\psi})\rangle.

(21)

By the density of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
in
W^{1,q}(\Omega)^{3}
, this formula holds for
all
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)^{3}
. For any
\mu\in B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
, let

v\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
be the weak solution to
-\Delta v+v=0,

\gamma_{0}(v)=\mu
, as provided by Theorem
4
.
Using (
21
) and estimate (
6
), for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
we have

\big|\langle\gamma_{t}(\boldsymbol{\phi}),\gamma_{0}(\mu)\rangle\big|\leq\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\cdot\|v\|_{W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)}.

According to the definition of the dual norm (
5
), the previous estimate
implies that
\gamma_{t}:W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\longrightarrow(B^{{1-1/q}}_{{q},{q}}(\partial\Omega)^{3})^{\prime}=B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)^{3}

can be defined by continuous extension and that

\|\gamma_{t}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\|_{B^{{-1/p}}_{{p},{p}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}.

Since
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
is dense in
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
, we conclude that the identity
(
21
) continues to hold for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)^{3}
.
∎

An intrinsic definition of
\gamma_{t}(W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega))
exists and is described in Mitrea
[
Mitrea
]
. For our purposes, a simpler approach
will be sufficient. Writing
Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)=\gamma_{t}(W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega))
, we define the following norm on the image

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\,\big|\,\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\text{ and }\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{w}\right\}.

(22)

Although the previous Green’s formula (
20
) used
\gamma_{0}
, we will be using the more precise expression
\gamma_{T}

since, for smooth functions, only the tangential component of
\mathbf{v}
appears in the inner product and

\langle\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{0}(\mathbf{v})\rangle=\langle\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{T}(\mathbf{v})\rangle
.

Theorem 20
.

Consider a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega
and
1<p<\infty
. Then

Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)
is a normed topological vector space, the map
\gamma_{t}:W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)\longrightarrow Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)
is continuous
and surjective and

\gamma_{T}:W^{q}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)\longrightarrow Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{\prime}
is well-defined and continuous.
Moreover, for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
,
\mathbf{v}\in W^{q}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
, we have

(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})-(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})=\langle\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{T}(\mathbf{v})\rangle.

(23)

Proof.

The image
Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)
equipped with the norm (
22
)
is easily seen to be a normed topological vector space since
\gamma_{t}
is linear.
To show that
\gamma_{t}
is continuous, it suffices to observe that for any
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)

\|\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})\|_{Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\\
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{v})=\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\leq\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}.

We now prove that
\gamma_{T}
is continuous. First of all, note that for any
s\in Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)
and any

\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
such that
s=\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})
, we have

\langle s,\gamma_{T}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\rangle=(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\phi})-(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi}),\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}.

Note that the right hand side is well-defined for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)
. Moreover,
the value on the right-hand side is independent of the choice of
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
so long as
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})=s
.
Therefore, for any fixed
s\in Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)
, the linear functional

L(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\langle s,\gamma_{T}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\rangle

is well-defined for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)
. In fact,

\displaystyle\big|L(\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|=

\displaystyle\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\phi})-(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\\
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{v})=s\end{subarray}}\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{v},\boldsymbol{\phi})-(\mathbf{v},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\\
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{v})=s\end{subarray}}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|s\|_{Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)},

which shows that
L
is continuous over
W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)
. Considering the definition
of the norm on
Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{\prime}
, equation (
5
), we immediately see that the previous
identity also implies that

\|\gamma_{T}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\|_{Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{\prime}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\phi}\|_{W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)}.

Hence, the map
\gamma_{T}
is continuous with image in
Y^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 21
.

For a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
, we have

W^{p}_{0}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)=\Big\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)\,\Big|\,\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})=0\Big\}.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{t}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=0
for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
, it suffices to prove the opposite inclusion. Given that

W^{p}_{0}(\text{curl};\Omega)
is the intersection of all
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)^{\prime}
which vanish on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
,
we must therefore show that such an
\ell
also vanishes on
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
when
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})=0.

As we did in the proof of Lemma
16
, any functional
\ell\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)^{\prime}
can be written in the form

\ell(\mathbf{u})=(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{u})+(\mathbf{h},\nabla\times\mathbf{u}),\qquad\forall\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega),

for some
\mathbf{g},\mathbf{h}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{3}
. If
\ell
vanishes for all
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
, then
this representation shows that
\mathbf{g}=-\nabla\times\mathbf{h}
in a distributional sense and therefore that
\mathbf{h}\in W^{q}(\text{curl};\Omega)
.
For
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
such that
\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u})=0
, we then apply identity (
23
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle-(\nabla\times\mathbf{h},\mathbf{u})+(\mathbf{h},\nabla\times\mathbf{u})=\langle\gamma_{t}(\mathbf{u}),\gamma_{T}(\mathbf{h})\rangle=0.

Hence
\mathbf{u}
belongs to the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
.
∎

We complete this section with a simple result concerning the spaces which we have introduced.

Lemma 22
.

For a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
, the spaces

W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)
and
W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
are locally convex and reflexive Banach spaces.

Proof.

Local convexity is a triviality since the spaces are normed. Recalling the isometries

\displaystyle I_{\text{div}}(\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})\in L^{p}(\Omega^{(4)},

\displaystyle I_{\text{curl}}(\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{u})\in L^{p}(\Omega^{(6)},

introduced in the proofs of Theorems
12
and
18
, then we see that

W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
and
W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
is isometric to a closed subspace of a reflexive space and are therefore, by Theorem 1.22 of
[
1
]
,
also reflexive.
∎

4
Divergence and rotational free vector fields

The objective of this section is to derive fundamental results concerning the existence of divergence free and rotational free vector fields.
These results appear to be well-known in the literature but we have either not found the results in the form we
needed or within the same reference. The fundamental result of this section
is a Friedrich’s inequality for problems with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. This result
is the only one which appears to be entirely new to the literature. Give more detailed citations for the
two theorems in this section : Dauge …

Theorem 23
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded simply connected Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
. Then

\nabla\times\mathbf{u}=0
in
\Omega
if and only if there exists
\phi\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)
such that
\mathbf{u}=\nabla\phi
. If this is the case, then

\phi
is unique up to an additive constant and

\|\phi\|_{W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)\setminus\mathbb{R}}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}.

Proof.

The proof follows the one for Theorem 3.37 from
[
27
]
which itself goes back to a proof in
[
3
]
.
The only differences are the use of convolutions in
L^{p}
and of the
L^{p}
version of Lemma
9
.

Given that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
,
\mathbf{u}
can be extended by zero to all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which we denote by

\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}
. With the help of a mollifier
\rho_{\epsilon}
, a suitably smooth approximation can be constructed

\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}=\rho_{\epsilon}\star\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}\cap C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3};

see Lemma 3.16 in
[
1
]
. Moreover,
\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}\to\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}
as
\epsilon\to 0
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
.

Under our assumptions on
\Omega
, it is possible to produce a sequence of simply connected Lipschitz domains
\{\mathcal{O}_{j}\}_{j}
satisfying

\mathcal{O}_{j}\subset\mathcal{O}_{j+1}
,
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\subset\Omega
and
\Omega=\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{j}
.
Fixing
\mathcal{O}_{j}
, since
\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}
is smooth and

\nabla\times\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}=\nabla\times\big(\rho_{\epsilon}\star\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}\big)=\rho_{\epsilon}\star\big(\nabla\times\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}\big)=\rho_{\epsilon}\star\big(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\big)=0,

by integration we can construct
\phi^{j}_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}=\nabla\phi^{j}_{\epsilon},\qquad\int_{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\phi^{j}_{\epsilon}\,d\mathbf{x}=0,\qquad\text{and }\qquad\|\phi^{j}_{\epsilon}\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O}_{j})}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O}_{j})^{3}}.

We now observe that since
\nabla\phi^{j}_{\epsilon}=\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}_{\epsilon}\to\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}
in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
, then
Poincaré’s inequality implies that

\phi^{j}_{\epsilon}\to\phi^{j}\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O}_{j})
. Even in the limit, we have
\|\phi^{j}\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O}_{j})}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}
.
The average of
\phi^{j}
over
\mathcal{O}_{j}
also vanishes.
At the cost of modifying
\phi^{j}
by a constant, we may assume that
\phi^{j}=\phi^{j-1}
over
\mathcal{O}_{j-1}
.
For any compact
K\subset\Omega
,
there exists
k
such that
K\subset\mathcal{O}_{k}
and therefore
by setting
\phi=\phi_{k}
over
\mathcal{O}_{k}
, we can define
\phi\in L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
.
In fact, since
\mathbf{u}|_{\mathcal{O}_{j}}=\nabla\phi^{j}
we have that
\nabla\phi=\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}\subset W^{{-1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
.
Using Lemma
9
, we conclude that
\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem 24
.

Consider an exponent
1<p<\infty
and a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

such that the boundary
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}}
with
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
open
and connected subset of
\partial\Omega
. If
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)
satisfies

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\qquad\text{on $\Omega$, and }\qquad\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\,d\mathbf{x}=0,\,\,i=D,N,

(24)

then there exists a vector potential
\mathbf{A}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{3}
such that
\mathbf{u}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}
and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}=0.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C
for which

\|\mathbf{A}\|_{W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one given for Theorem 3.38 in
[
27
]
except for the application of the Marcinkiewicz-Mihlin multiplier theorem;
see Chapter 4, subsection 3.2 of
[
32
]
. This ingredient is not new since it was already used by Dauge
[
12
]
in a proof of
the same theorem for domains with polyhedral boundaries. Again for completeness, we provide the proof.

The problem will be solved using Fourier transforms and therefore, it will be necessary to construct an extension of
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega).

Our assumptions on the domain imply that
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
is formed of two components
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
, one of
which is unbounded, say
\Omega_{0}
.
Consider the following problem over
\Omega_{1}
:

\Delta v_{1}=0\qquad\text{on $\Omega_{1}$, and }\qquad\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v_{1}=\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\qquad\text{on $\partial\Omega_{1}$.}

By Theorem
15
, the boundary condition
\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\in B^{-1/p}_{p,p}(\partial\Omega_{1})
possesses sufficient regularity
to guarantee, by Theorem
7
, a solution
v_{1}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega_{1})
. Clearly
\nabla v_{1}
is divergence free inside
\Omega_{1}
.

To extend
\mathbf{u}
into
\Omega_{0}
, choose a large closed ball
K
whose interior contains the closure of
\Omega
. Over

K\setminus\Omega
, consider the problem

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\Delta v_{0}&=&0&\text{on $K\setminus\Omega$,}\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v_{0}&=&\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}&\text{on $\partial\Omega_{0}$,}\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v_{0}&=&0&\text{on $\partial K$.}\end{array}

Again, there exists a unique solution
v_{0}\in W^{1,p}(K\setminus\Omega)
. The new vector field
\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}

obtained by patching together
\mathbf{u},\nabla v_{0}
and
\nabla v_{1}
according to

\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega$,}\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v_{0}(\mathbf{x})&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_{0}$,}\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v_{1}(\mathbf{x})&\text{if $\mathbf{x}\in(K\setminus\Omega)$,}\\
0&\text{if $\mathbf{x}$ is in the complement of $K$,}\end{cases}

then belongs to
W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
since the normal components are equal along the boundaries. The
proof that this new function belongs to
L^{p}
requires an obvious extension of Lemma 5.3 in
[
27
]
.
Furthermore, this extension of
\mathbf{u}
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfies
\nabla\times\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}=0
.

With the extension
\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}
belonging to
L^{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
, the Fourier
transform of
\mathbf{\widetilde{u}}
is well-defined. As in the
L^{2}
case, we want to consider the vector
potential
\mathbf{A}
defined by

\widehat{A}_{1}=\frac{\xi_{3}\widehat{u}_{2}-\xi_{2}\widehat{u}_{3}}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}},\qquad\widehat{A}_{2}=\frac{\xi_{1}\widehat{u}_{3}-\xi_{3}\widehat{u}_{1}}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}},\qquad\widehat{A}_{3}=\frac{\xi_{2}\widehat{u}_{1}-\xi_{1}\widehat{u}_{2}}{|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}},

but a priori, it is not clear that the inverse Fourier transform of
\mathbf{A}
can be taken. In this vein, write

\widehat{\mathbf{A}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{A}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})+\big(1-\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\widehat{\mathbf{A}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is identically
1
outside a large ball centered at the origin while being
identically
0
in a neighborhood of the origin. The inverse Fourier transform of
(1-\chi)\widehat{\mathbf{A}}
is analytic
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and therefore in
L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
. On the other hand, the inverse Fourier transform of
\chi\widehat{\mathbf{A}}

and
\xi_{j}\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}
can be written as

\displaystyle\big(\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{A}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}=\big(W(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}

\displaystyle\big(\xi_{j}\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{A}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}=\big(\xi_{j}W(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}

where the matrix
W(\boldsymbol{\xi})
possesses terms that are asymptotically of the form
\xi_{k}/|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}
.
A simple computation shows that the multipliers
W(\boldsymbol{\xi})
and
\xi_{j}W(\boldsymbol{\xi})
satisfies the conditions of the Marcinkiewicz-Mihlin
multiplier theorem and therefore, that there exists a constant
C
such that

\|\big(\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{\mathbf{A}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\big(\xi_{j}\chi(\boldsymbol{\xi})\widehat{A}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big)\check{}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}.

This shows that
\mathbf{A}
in fact belongs to
W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
.
Using the fact that
\mathbf{u}
is divergence free, it is easy to verify that
\mathbf{A}
is also divergence free
and that
\mathbf{u}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}
.
∎

5
Friedrich’s inequality

The objective of this section is to provide
detailed estimates concerning the regularity of the spaces described in the preceding sections, in particular
with regard to the boundary conditions. We will show that the boundary condition
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{B}=0\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)

provides some additional smoothness inside the domain.
The most important result will be the Friedrich’s inequality, Theorem
27
.

We begin our analysis with the following space

W^{p}_{\text{imp}}(\text{curl};\Omega)=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\,\Big|\,\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)\right\},

(25)

where
L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)=\{\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}|\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\}
.
The norm over
W^{p}_{\text{imp}}(\text{curl};\Omega)
will be

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{p}_{\text{imp}}(\text{curl};\Omega)}:=\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}.

The next result shows that this strengthened requirement at the boundary produces a non-trivial amount of regularity
inside the domain. The result comes from Costabel
[
9
]

where it was presented in
L^{2}
. We also add that this result bears a close resemblance to a result mentioned
in Remark 2.19 of
[
3
]
and attributed to Dauge
[
13
]
. Although the next bound is interesting in it’s
own right, its true purpose is to demonstrate Corollary
26
.

Theorem 25
.

Consider a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
.
If
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{\emph{div}};\Omega)\cap W^{p}(\text{\emph{curl}};\Omega)
and
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
, then

\mathbf{u}\in W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
and for some constant
C
,

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq C\left\{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}\right\}.

(26)

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one given by Monk
[
27
]
for his Theorem 3.47 except that his elliptic regularity result, Theorem 3.17, must
be replaced by the generalization, Theorem
8
.

To prove estimate (
26
), it suffices to prove a local version of this estimate and then to use a partition of unity to extend the estimate
to all of
\Omega
. Following
[
27
]
, the regularity of the boundary allows us to assume that locally
\Omega
is a bounded simply connected
domain with a connected
C^{1}
boundary. Since
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)
and
\nabla\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{u}=0
, then
there exists a vector potential
\mathbf{w}\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
such that
\nabla\times\mathbf{w}=\nabla\times\mathbf{u}
and
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}=0

(Note that the constraint on the boundary is satisfied because of formula (
23
)). Secondly, for

\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)
, we have
\nabla\times\mathbf{z}=0
and by Theorem
23
there exists
\xi\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)
such
that
\mathbf{z}=\nabla\xi
. Finally, Theorem 5.4 of
[
34
]
states that we can construct
\eta\in W^{{2},{p}}(\Omega)
satisfying

\Delta\eta=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
(in constrast to the previous
\xi
, to obtain
\eta
we need to extend
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}
by zero outside of
\Omega

and thereby use smoother boundary conditions).

To obtain the increased reguarity, we will find some additional
regularity at the boundary and invoke Theorem
8
to lift the regularity to all of
\Omega
.
Since
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
and
\mathbf{w}\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
, then

\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{n}\times(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})\in L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega).

Using the fact that
\xi\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in W^{{2},{p}}(\Omega)
we have that the function
\nu=\xi-\eta

satisfies
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\xi=\mathbf{n}\times\nabla(\eta+\nu)

and
\nabla\nu\times\mathbf{n}\in L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)
. Therefore
\nu\in W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)
,

\Delta\nu=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\xi-\nabla\eta)=0

and Theorem
8
implies that
\nu\in W^{{1+1/p},{p}}(\Omega)
.

We begin with an estimate for
\nu
. First of all, notice that since
\xi
is a consequence of Theorem
23
, it is therefore
defined up a to constant. The function
\nu
is thus also defined up to a constant and we may assume that it’s average value along the boundary
vanishes. Using the elliptic regularity result, Theorem
8
, and Poincaré’s inequality on the boundary, we deduce the following.

\displaystyle\|\nabla\nu\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle\|\nu\|_{W^{{1+1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\nu\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\nu\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\nu\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}+\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu\,d\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right|\right\}=C\|\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\nu\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}

Using the identity
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{w}+\nabla\eta+\nabla\nu
and the stability estimates of
Theorems
20
,
24
and
23
for
\mathbf{w}
and
\eta
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla\nu\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\eta\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{w}\|_{W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\eta\|_{W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right\}.

It is already known that
\mathbf{u}\in W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
and we can estimate its norm
by exploiting the previous estimate.

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\eta\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\nu\|_{W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}\right\}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We introduce the spaces

\displaystyle X^{p}_{N}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)\cap W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\,\big|\,\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}=0\text{ on $\partial\Omega$}\right\},

(27)

\displaystyle X^{p}_{N,0}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in X^{p}_{N}\,\big|\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\text{ in $\Omega$}\right\},

(28)

\displaystyle W^{p}_{N}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)\cap W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\,\big|\,\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)\text{ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0$}\right\},

(29)

\displaystyle X^{p}_{T}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)\cap W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\,\big|\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\text{ on $\partial\Omega$}\right\},

(30)

\displaystyle X^{p}_{T,0}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in X^{p}_{T}\,\big|\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\text{ in $\Omega$}\right\},

(31)

\displaystyle W^{p}_{T}=

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}(\text{div};\Omega)\cap W^{p}(\text{curl};\Omega)\,\big|\,\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\text{ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0$}\right\}.

(32)

The previous theorem implies that these spaces imbed continuously into
W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)^{3}
.
Theorem 1.4.3.2 in
[
20
]
states that
W^{{1/p},{p}}(\Omega)
imbeds compactly into
L^{p}(\Omega)
and therfore, we have
the following important corollary which generalizes Corollary 3.49 of Monk
[
27
]
.

Corollary 26
.

Consider a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1<p<\infty
.
Then the spaces
X^{p}_{N},X^{p}_{N,0},W^{p}_{N},X^{p}_{T},X^{p}_{T,0}
and
W^{p}_{T}
imbed compactly into
L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}.

Theorem 27

(Friedrich’s inequality)
.

Consider a bounded
C^{1}
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a connected boundary. Assume the
exponent satisfies
1<p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\mathbf{u}\in W_{N}^{p}

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq C\left\{\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}_{t}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

Similarly, there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\mathbf{u}\in W^{p}_{T}

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}\leq C\left\{\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

Note: to prove the second estimate, we need the analogue of Theorem
25
when
\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

The proof is an extension of the one given for Corollary 3.51 in
[
27
]
.

Proceeding by contradiction with the first inequality.
Suppose that there exists a sequence
\mathbf{u}_{n}
of functions in
W^{p}_{N}
such that

\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}+\|\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)^{3}}\leq 1/n,

while
\|\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}}=1
for all

. Since
W^{p}_{N}
imbeds compactly into
L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
, then we can extract a subsequence (renamed
\mathbf{u}_{n}
)
which converges to some
\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
. On the other hand, for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}
the curl
of
\mathbf{u}
as a distribution satisfies

\displaystyle\big|L_{\nabla\times\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|=\big|(\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|(\mathbf{u}_{n},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{u}_{n},\boldsymbol{\phi})\big|=0.

In other words,
\mathbf{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{3}
is such that
\nabla\times\mathbf{u}=0
.

According to Theorem
23
, there exists
\psi\in W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)
such that

\mathbf{u}=\nabla\psi
. Since
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}=0,
then
\Delta\psi=0
and along the boundary

In conclusion,
\psi
is constant along the connected boundary and, by uniqueness of solutions to Poisson’s problem,
\psi=0
and
\mathbf{u}=0

This contradicts our earlier hypothesis concerning the first inequality. A similar argument could be used to demonstrate the estimate over
W^{p}_{T}

∎

Rather than attempt to immediately demonstrate that

is dense in

Lemma 28

The functions in the subset

of

Theorem 29

The set
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})^{3}
is dense in

Proof.

The proof given here is a straightforward adaptation of Theorem 3.54 in

Choose

and consider the variational problem : find

such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi\,d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,d\mathbf{x},\quad\forall\psi\in W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}.

It is an exercise to show that the bilinear form
B:W^{{1},{p}}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\times W^{{1},{q}}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is continuous, non-degenerate and satisfies an
\inf
-
\sup
condition of the form (
12

By the Babuška-Lax-Milgram Theorem, Theorem

, this problem possesses a unique solution.

Evaluating the divergence of
\nabla\phi
in weak form, we find that

-(\nabla\phi,\nabla\xi)=-(\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\xi)=0,\quad\forall\xi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega),

or in other words
\nabla\cdot\nabla\phi=0

By adjusting the constant term of

, we can satisfy condition (

and deduce the existence of

is

Finally, we will show that the second term in the decomposition

in

and therefore

The bilinear form is easily seen to be continuous since, by Theorem (

We will only demonstrate the

Now let

be the function in

Returning to property (

\displaystyle\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\boldsymbol{\psi})_{\Omega}\big|=

\displaystyle\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\chi}+\nabla\xi)_{\Omega}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\chi})_{\Omega}-(\nabla\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\xi)_{\Omega}+(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\xi)_{\partial\Omega}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|(\nabla\times\mathbf{A}_{n},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\chi})_{\Omega}\big|.

By the density result of

Lemma
28
.

As we mentionned earlier in Theorem
25
, the proof of a density result can be demonstrated locally and we may assume
that
\Omega
is a bounded simply connected domain with a connected

boundary. Choose
\mathbf{u}\in\widetilde{W}^{p}_{\text{imp}}(\text{curl};\Omega)

Lemma

For any
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in X^{q}_{T}
, Green’s formula shows that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\,d\mathbf{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\,d\boldsymbol{\sigma}=0.

Hence, Theorem

states that we can construct

\Delta\xi=\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\quad\text{on $\Omega$, and }\quad\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\xi=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$.}

Then any

can be decomposed as
\nabla\xi+\big(\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\xi\big)

two components are such that

\mathbf{A}
satisfies equation (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\times\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\psi}\,d\mathbf{x}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\times\mathbf{A}\cdot\nabla\times\big(\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\xi\big)\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\times\big(\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\xi\big)-\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\xi\big)\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\,d\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\psi}-\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}\,d\mathbf{x}.

(35)

By Theorems
12

For the original

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\xi_{n}

\displaystyle\to\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{A})\text{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)^{3},

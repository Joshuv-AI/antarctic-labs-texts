On higher Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-functions
Alexander Alexandrov, Saswati Dhara

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the higher Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-functions, given by the matrix integrals. For these tau-functions we construct the canonical Kac–Schwarz operators, quantum spectral curves, and W ( 3 ) W^{(3)} -constraints. For the simplest representative we construct the cut-and-join operators, which describe the algebraic version of the topological recursion. We also investigate a one-parametric generalization of the higher Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-functions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
37K10, 14N35, 81R10, 05A15

Keywords: tau-functions, KP hierarchy, W-constraints, BGW tau-function, cut-and-join operator, enumerative geometry, moduli spaces.

1.
Introduction

Cohomological field theories
[
KM94
]
provide a universal description of a huge family of enumerative geometry invariants.
The Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on the regular spectral curves
[
EO07
,
EO09
]
is closely related to the Givental–Teleman description of cohomological field theories and for many cases
[
DBOSS14
]
it allows us to describe the generating functions in terms of certain structures on the spectral curve. A generalization of this construction for the irregular spectral curves with
simple ramification points, associated with degenerate cohomological field theories, was developed by Chekhov and Norbury
[
CN19
]
. This generalization describes Givental’s decomposition formula for the partition functions on all, possibly irregular, spectral curves which near ramification points are similar to the Airy curve

(1.1)

2x=y^{2}

or the Bessel curve

(1.2)

2xy^{2}=1.

Basic ingredients of this construction are the Kontsevich–Witten
[
Wit91
,
Kon92
]
and Brézin–Gross–Witten (BGW)
[
BG80
,
GW80
]
tau-functions.
Because of the special role, played by these functions in the modern mathematical physics, these functions are very well studied and many properties are known. In particular, these functions are solutions of the KdV hierarchy, and satisfy the Virasoro constraints. These constraints can be solved with the cut-and-join operators
[
Ale11
,
Ale18
]
. Moreover, these functions are related to the intersection theory on the moduli spaces of the Riemann surfaces
[
Wit91
,
Kon92
,
Nor23
,
Nor20
]
. In many respects these two tau-functions are very similar to each other.

A general description of decomposition of the non-semi-simple cohomological field theories is not available yet. However, it is expected that it is closely related to a version of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion on the spectral curves with
higher ramification points
[
BBC+24
]
locally described by the equation

(1.3)

(m+1)x=y^{m+1}

for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
Associated partition functions were intensively investigated in the framework of matrix models, integrable systems, and associated string models in the early 90’s of the last century, see

[
KS91
,
DVV91
,
KMM+92a
,
KMM+92b
,
FKN92
,
AvM92
,
IZ92
,
Kon92
,
Wit93
]
and references therein. Canonical Kac–Schwarz (KS) operators, associated to this family, were recently constructed in
[
Ale21
]
.

In this paper we investigate the generating functions which we claim to be associated with the spectral curves

(1.4)

(m+1)xy^{m+1}=1

for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.
The simplest representative of this family is the Bessel curve (
1.2
).
These generating functions are given by the asymptotic expansion of the higher BGW matrix models, introduced by Mironov, Morozov and Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
. They are tau-functions of the
(m+1)
-reduction of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy.
For these tau-functions we construct a complete description of the corresponding KS algebra. In particular, we derive the canonical pair of the KS operators. One of them plays the role of the quantum spectral curve, and another is the raising operator which generate the point of the Sato Grassmannian. Here we see an essential difference with the generalized Kontsevich models, associated to the curves (
1.3
). Namely, for the family (
1.3
) the canonical KS operators are polynomials, while for the higher BGW tau-functions one of them is given by an infinite series.

Using these KS operators we construct the
W^{(3)}
-constraints satisfied by the higher BGW tau-functions.
For
m=2
, these constraints completely specify the tau-function. We solve these constraints in terms of the cut-and-join operators. This solution provides an algebraic version of the topological recursion.

The tau-functions, associated to the spectral curves (
1.3
), have a very nice enumerative geometry interpretation. Namely, according to Witten’s conjecture
[
Wit93
]
, proved by Faber, Shadrin, and Zvonkine
[
FSZ10
]
,
they are the generating functions of
r
-spin intersection theory, given by the intersection numbers of Witten’s and psi classes. In the same time, the original BGW model corresponding to the Bessel spectral curve (
1.2
) describes the intersection theory of Norbury’s classes
[
Nor23
,
Nor20
,
CGFG22
]
. The geometric interpretation for the higher BGW tau-functions is addressed in the recent paper of Chidambaram, Garcia-Failde, and Giacchetto
[
CGFG22
]
.

We also consider the one-parametric deformation of the higher BGW tau-functions by a logarithmic term introduced in
[
MMS96
]
.
For this deformation we construct a complete set of the KS operators, the quantum spectral curve, the
W^{(3)}
-constraints and for the simplest case with
m=2
we construct the cut-and-join description. For the case of the original BGW tau-function (
m=1
) this deformation was investigated in
[
Ale18
]
, where it was called the generalized BGW tau-function, so we call the deformed family the generalized higher BGW tau-functions.
This deformation is very natural and we believe that it has a natural geometric interpretation. However, this interpretation is unclear yet.

We expect that the tau-functions, investigated in this paper, will play an important role in the topological expansion/Givental decomposition for the irregular spectral curves/non-semi-simple degenerate cohomological field theories.

KP hierarchy and its symmetries

Let us briefly summarize by now standard description of the KP hierarchy in terms of the Sato Grassmannian and KS operators; for more detail see, e.g.,
[
SS83
,
SW85
,
KS91
,
AvM92
,
Mul94
,
MJD00
,
Ale21
]

and references therein.

2.1.
KP hierarchy and Sato Grassmannian

The KP hierarchy was introduced by Sato
[
SS83
]
. It
can be represented in terms of a tau-function
\tau({\bf t})
by the Hirota bilinear identity

(2.1)

\oint_{\infty}e^{\sum_{k>0}(t_{k}-t_{k}^{\prime})z^{k}}\tau({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])\tau({\bf t^{\prime}}+[z^{-1}])dz=0,

which encodes all nonlinear equations of the KP hierarchy.
Here we use the standard short-hand notation

(2.2)

{\bf t}\pm[z^{-1}]:=\bigl\{t_{1}\pm z^{-1},t_{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}z^{-2},t_{3}\pm\frac{1}{3}z^{-3},\ldots\bigr\}.

Let us consider the description of the space of solutions for the KP hierarchy, introduced by Sato in
[
SS83
]
and further developed by Segal and Wilson in
[
SW85
]
. We work within the formal series setup,
\tau({\bf t})\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\dots]\!]
. Hence,
we focus on Sato’s version of the construction. Let us consider the space
H=H_{+}\oplus H_{-}
, where the subspaces

(2.3)

H_{-}=z^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[\![z^{-1}]\!]

and

(2.4)

H_{+}={\mathbb{C}}[z]

are generated by negative and nonnegative powers of
z
respectively. Then, the Sato Grassmannian
\rm{Gr}
consists of all closed linear spaces
\mathcal{W}\in H
, which are compatible with
H_{+}
. Namely, an orthogonal projection
\pi_{+}:\mathcal{W}\to H_{+}
should be a Fredholm operator, i.e. both the kernel
{\rm ker}\,\pi_{+}\in\mathcal{W}
and the
cokernel
{\rm coker}\,\pi_{+}\in H_{+}
should be finite-dimensional vector spaces.
The Grassmannian
\Gr
consists of components
\Gr^{(k)}
, parametrized by an index of the operator
\pi_{+}
. We need only the component
\Gr^{(0)}
; other components have an equivalent description.

Moreover, we will consider only the big cell
\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
of
\Gr^{(0)}
, which is defined by the constraint
{\rm ker}\,\pi_{+}={\rm coker}\,\pi_{+}=0
. We call
\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
the
Sato Grassmannian
for simplicity. There exists a bijection between the points of the Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
and the tau-functions with
\tau({\bf 0})=1
. Below for simplicity we consider only the tau-functions normalized by
\tau({\bf 0})=1
.

A point of the Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}\in\rm{Gr}^{(0)}_{+}
can be described by an
admissible basis

\{\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{2}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{3}^{\mathcal{W}},\dots\}
,

(2.5)

{\mathcal{W}}={\rm span}\,\{\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{2}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{3}^{\mathcal{W}},\dots\}.

The crucial property of the admissible bases is that if
\{\Phi_{j}^{\mathcal{W}}\}
and
\{{\Phi^{\prime}}_{j}^{\mathcal{W}}\}
are two admissible bases of
{\mathcal{W}}
, then the matrix which relates them is of the kind that has a determinant, or, equivalently, this matrix differs from the identity by an operator of trace class
[
SW85
]
:

Definition 1
.

\{\Phi_{j}^{\mathcal{W}}\}
is an admissible basis for
{\mathcal{W}}\in\rm{Gr}^{(0)}_{+}
, if

(1)

the linear map
H_{+}\rightarrow H
which takes
z^{j-1}
to
\Phi_{j}^{\mathcal{W}}
is injective and has image
\mathcal{W}
, and

(2)

the matrix, relating
\pi_{+}(\Phi_{j}^{\mathcal{W}})
to
z^{j-1}
differs from the identity by an operator of trace class.

We call an element of
H

monic
if its leading coefficient is equal to 1. Any point of the Sato Grassmannian has an admissible basis of the monic elements of the form

(2.6)

\Phi_{j}^{\mathcal{W}}=z^{j-1}\left(1+O(z^{-1})\right),

of course, such basis is not unique.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
\Lambda={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M})
be a diagonal matrix.
For any function
f({\bf t})
, dependent on the infinite set of variables
{\bf t}=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\dots)
, let

(2.7)

f\left(\left[\Lambda^{-1}\right]\right):=f({\bf t})\Big|_{t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}{\rm Tr}\,\Lambda^{-k}}

be the
Miwa parametrization
. For any basis (
2.6
) the tau-function of the KP hierarchy in the Miwa parametrization is equal to the ratio of determinants

(2.8)

\tau_{\mathcal{W}}([\Lambda^{-1}])=\frac{\det_{i,j=1}^{M}\Phi^{\mathcal{W}}_{i}(\lambda_{j})}{\Delta(\lambda)},

where

(2.9)

\Delta(z)=\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})

is the
Vandermonde determinant
.
Moreover, if for some function
\tau_{\mathcal{W}}
equation (
2.8
) holds for all
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, then
\tau_{\mathcal{W}}
is a tau-function of the KP hierarchy.

2.2.
Kac–Schwarz operators

Consider the ring of differential operators with coefficients formal Laurent series in the variable
z^{-1}
,

(2.10)

{\mathcal{D}}:={\mathbb{C}}(\!(z^{-1})\!)[\![\frac{\partial}{\partial z}]\!]

and its subrings
{\mathcal{D}}_{\pm}:=H_{\pm}[\![\frac{\partial}{\partial z}]\!]
. Operators from
{\mathcal{D}}
we will call differential operators. Below we allow the coefficients of operators in
{\mathcal{D}}
to depend also on parameters, in particular, on
\hbar
.
A natural direct sum decomposition holds

(2.11)

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{+}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{-}.

Definition 2
.

For any point of the Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}
the KS algebra

(2.12)

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{W}}:=\left\{{\mathtt{a}}\in{\mathcal{D}}\,\big|\,{\mathtt{a}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathcal{W}}\right\}

is the algebra of the differential operators which stabilize this point.

For a point of the Sato Grassmannian the KS algebra is isomorphic to
w_{1+\infty}
algebra
[
Ale21
]
. The KS operators provide an efficient tool for investigation of tau-functions
[
KS91
,
KMM+92a
,
FKN92
,
AvM92
,
MMS96
,
Ale15a
,
Ale21
]
. For instance, sometimes they allow to construct a complete set of linear Virasoro/W-constraints satisfied by the tau-functions. Moreover, they help to describe matrix models associated with tau-functions.
They are also closely related to the quantum spectral curves and relations between the Lax and the Orlov–Schulman operators.

However, there is no universal way to construct the KS algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{W}}
explicitly for a given point of the Sato Grassmannian. For the tau-functions related to the enumerative geometry problems such algebras are often generated by relatively simple operators. In this paper we construct such a description for an interesting family of tau-functions.

Consider the space of the pairs of differential operators

(2.13)

\Gr_{\mathcal{D}}:=\left\{\left({\mathtt{P}},{\mathtt{Q}}\right)\in{\mathcal{D}}^{2}\Big|\left[{\mathtt{P}},{\mathtt{Q}}\right]=1,{\mathtt{P}}\in\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+z^{-1}{\mathcal{D}}_{-},{\mathtt{Q}}\in z+{\mathcal{D}}_{-}\right\}.

In
[
Ale21
]
it is proven that
\Gr_{\mathcal{D}}
is isomorphic to the Sato Grassmannian
\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
. For any point of the Sato Grassmannian there is a unique pair of KS operators
{\mathtt{P}}_{\mathcal{W}},{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{W}}
such that
({\mathtt{P}}_{\mathcal{W}},{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}})\in\Gr_{\mathcal{D}}
. This pair can be constructed explicitly for a given Sato group element.

Definition 3
.

For a point
{\mathcal{W}}
of the Sato Grassmannian we call the pair of KS operators
({\mathtt{P}}_{\mathcal{W}},{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}})\in\Gr_{\mathcal{D}}
the canonical KS operators, associated to
{\mathcal{W}}
or the corresponding tau-function.

In
[
Ale21
, Corollary 2.2]
it was shown that for any
{\mathcal{W}}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
there are no non-trivial KS operators in
{\mathcal{D}}_{-}
,
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{W}}\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{-}=0
. Therefore, to find the canonical pair of KS operators it is sufficient to find any pair of KS operators, one from
z+{\mathcal{D}}_{-}
and another from
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+{\mathcal{D}}_{-}
, by uniqueness this pair is canonical.

The canonical pair of KS operators describes the point of the Sato Grassmannian in the following simple way. The first basis vector satisfies

(2.14)

{\mathtt{P}}_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}}=0.

This equation can be interpreted as the quantum spectral curve equation, it has a unique monic solution in
H
. Then, for this solution the vectors
{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}}^{j-1}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}}=z^{j-1}\left(1+O(z^{-1})\right)
generate an admissible basis

(2.15)

{\mathcal{W}}={\rm span}\,\{\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},{\mathtt{Q}}_{\mathcal{W}}^{2}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},\dots\}.

2.3.
Symmetries of the KP hierarchy

Using the KS operators one can construct linear equations for the tau-functions. These equations are described by operators, which belong to a certain representation of the central extension of the algebra
\gl(\infty)
, for more details see, e.g.,
[
KS91
,
FKN92
,
Ale15a
,
Ale21
]
.
Let us remind the reader a description of the
W^{(3)}
symmetry algebra of the KP hierarchy.
The
Heisenberg–Virasoro subalgebra
of
\gl(\infty)
is generated by the operators

(2.16)

\widehat{J}_{k}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\quad k>0},\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle{0}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\quad k=0,\\[2.0pt]
\displaystyle{-kt_{-k}\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\quad k<0,}\end{cases}

unit, and

(2.17)

\begin{split}\widehat{L}_{m}&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=m}{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\widehat{J}_{a}\widehat{J}_{b}{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=-m}abt_{a}t_{b}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+m}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a+b=m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{a}\partial t_{b}}.\end{split}

Here the normal ordering
{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\dots{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}
puts all
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}
to the right of all
t_{k}
, and we always assume that
t_{k}=0
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}=0
for negative
k
.
To extend the Heisenberg–Virasoro algebra to

W^{(3)}
algebra it is necessary to consider also operators cubic in
\widehat{J}_{k}
,

(2.18)

\widehat{M}_{k}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a+b+c=k}{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\widehat{J}_{a}\widehat{J}_{b}\widehat{J}_{c}{\scriptstyle{{*}\atop{*}}}\\
=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a+b+c=-k}a\,b\,c\,t_{a}\,t_{b}\,t_{c}+\sum_{c-a-b=k}a\,b\,t_{a}\,t_{b}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{c}}+\sum_{b+c-a=k}a\,t_{a}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{b}\partial t_{c}}+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{a+b+c=k}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial t_{a}\partial t_{b}\partial t_{c}}.

The operators
\widehat{J}_{k}
,
\widehat{L}_{k}
, and
\widehat{M}_{k}
satisfy the following commutation relations

(2.19)

\begin{split}\left[\widehat{J}_{k},\widehat{J}_{m}\right]&=k\,\delta_{k,-m},\\
\left[\widehat{J}_{k},\widehat{L}_{m}\right]&=k\widehat{J}_{k+m},\\
\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{L}_{m}\right]&=(k-m)\widehat{L}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{12}k(k^{2}-1)\delta_{k,-m},\\
\left[\widehat{J}_{k},\widehat{M}_{m}\right]&=2k\,\widehat{L}_{k+m},\\
\left[\widehat{L}_{k},\widehat{M}_{m}\right]&=(2k-m)\widehat{M}_{k+m}+\frac{1}{6}k(k^{2}-1)\widehat{J}_{k+m},\end{split}

and generate the
W^{(3)}
-algebra.
A commutator of
\widehat{M}_{k}
’s contains the terms of fourth power in the current components
\widehat{J}_{m}
, so it can not be represented as a linear combination of
\widehat{J}_{k}
,
\widehat{L}_{k}
, and
\widehat{M}_{k}
.

To describe the action of the algebra (
2.19
) on the Sato Grassmannian let us introduce

(2.20)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{j}}_{k}&=z^{k},\\
{\mathtt{l}}_{k}&=-z^{k}\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{k+1}{2}\right),\\
{\mathtt{m}}_{k}&=z^{k}\left(\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{2}+(k+1)z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{6}\right).\end{split}

These operators belong to
{\mathcal{D}}
and satisfy the commutation relations (
2.19
) with the omitted central term.

With any KS operator one can associate and describe explicitly a linear differential equation, satisfied by the tau-function.
The following lemma provides a particular instance of this general relation between the KS operators and linear constraints, satisfied by the tau-functions.

Lemma 2.1
.

If a point of the Sato Grassmannian
\mathcal{W}
is stabilized by a KS operator

(2.21)

\sum_{k}\left(a_{k}{\mathtt{m}}_{k}+b_{k}{\mathtt{l}}_{k}+c_{k}{\mathtt{j}}_{k}\right)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{W}},

where parameters
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
, and
c_{k}
do not depend on
{\bf t}
,
then the corresponding tau-function
\tau_{\mathcal{W}}
satisfies a linear constraint

(2.22)

\sum_{k}\left(a_{k}\widehat{M}_{k}+b_{k}\widehat{L}_{k}+c_{k}\widehat{J}_{k}\right)\cdot\tau_{\mathcal{W}}=\mu\,\tau_{\mathcal{W}}

for some eigenvalue
\mu
independent of
{\bf t}
.

3.
BGW tau-function

In this section, we remind the reader some basic properties of the BGW tau-function, for more detail see
[
MMS96
,
Ale18
]
. Here we basically follow
[
Ale18
]
. We also derive a canonical pair of KS operators for the BGW tau-function.

3.1.
BGW tau-function

The BGW model
[
BG80
,
GW80
]
, also known as the Gross–Witten–Wadia model
[
Wad12
]
, is given by the unitary matrix integral

(3.1)

Z_{\BGW}=\int_{U(M)}[dU]e^{\frac{1}{\hbar}{\rm Tr}\,\left(A^{\dagger}U+AU^{\dagger}\right)}.

Here
M\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and
[dU]
is the Haar measure on the unitary group
U(M)
normalized by
\int_{U(M)}[dU]=1
;
A
and
A^{\dagger}
are two external matrices. Using the invariance properties of the Haar measure it is easy to show that the partition function depends on their product, so it is convenient to introduce

(3.2)

\Lambda=(A^{\dagger}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For simplicity we assume that
\Lambda={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M})
is a positive defined diagonal matrix and
\hbar
is positive.

Let us focus on the expansion of the partition function for large values of the eigenvalues of the matrix
\Lambda
. To describe this expansion it is more convenient to use another matrix integral representation of the BGW partition function. Namely, according to Mironov–Morozov–Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
the partition function can be described by a particular generalized Kontsevich model

(3.3)

Z_{\BGW}=\frac{\displaystyle{\int[d\Phi]\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\text{Tr}\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}\Phi}{2}+\frac{1}{2\Phi}-\hbar M\log\Phi\right)\right)}}{\displaystyle{\int[d\Phi]\exp\left(\text{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{2\hbar\Phi}-M\log\Phi\right)\right)}}.

The measure of integration here

(3.4)

[d\Phi]:=\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{M-1}k!}\prod_{i<j}d\Im\Phi_{ij}\,d\Re\Phi_{ij}\,\prod_{i=1}^{M}d\Phi_{ii}

is a flat measure associated with the space of
M\times M
Hermitian matrices.

In equation (
3.3
), the integration is taken over
M\times M
normal matrices
\Phi
,

(3.5)

\Phi=U~\text{diag}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{M})U^{\dagger},\quad\quad\varphi_{i}\in\gamma,

where
U
is a unitary matrix and the contour
\gamma
(aka Hankel’s loop) runs from
-\infty
around the origin counter clockwise and returning to
-\infty
. Then, the measure of integration can be expressed in terms of
U
and the eigenvalues
\varphi_{i}
in the standard way

(3.6)

[d\Phi]=\Delta(\varphi)^{2}[dU]\prod_{i}^{M}d\varphi_{i}.

Using the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber formula we reduce the expression (
3.3
) to the integral over the eigenvalues

(3.7)

Z_{\BGW}=(-1)^{\frac{M(M-1)}{2}}\prod_{1}^{M}(j-1)!\frac{\text{det}_{i,j=1}^{M}\,\mathcal{I}_{M-i}(\lambda_{j})}{\Delta\left(\frac{{\lambda}^{2}}{2\hbar}\right)},

where

(3.8)

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{z^{2}{\varphi}}{2\hbar}+\frac{1}{2\hbar{\varphi}}}\frac{d{\varphi}}{{\varphi}^{\nu+1}},

and
\iota=\sqrt{-1}
.
Function
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}(z)
can be written as

(3.9)

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}(z)=z^{\nu}I_{\nu}(z/\hbar),

where
I_{\nu}(z)
is the modified Bessel function defined as

(3.10)

I_{\nu}(z)=\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\nu}\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{z^{2}{\varphi}}{4}+\frac{1}{{\varphi}}}\frac{d{\varphi}}{{\varphi}^{\nu+1}}.

Let
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\hbar}
, we consider a diagonal matrix,

(3.11)

\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda/\hbar={\rm diag}\,(\tilde{\lambda}_{1},\tilde{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\tilde{\lambda}_{M}).

We introduce

(3.12)

\tau_{\BGW}([\Lambda^{-1}])=\mathcal{C}^{-1}_{\BGW}Z_{\BGW},

where

(3.13)

\mathcal{C}_{\BGW}=\frac{{\Delta(\lambda)e^{\text{Tr}~\tilde{\Lambda}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}(j-1)!}}{{\Delta\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\hbar}\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\right)^{\frac{M}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}}.

Then from (
3.7
) we have

(3.14)

\tau_{\BGW}([\Lambda^{-1}])=\frac{\text{det}_{i,j=1}^{M}\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}(\lambda_{i})}{\Delta(\lambda)},

where
\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}
can be written in terms of the modified Bessel function

(3.15)

\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}(\lambda)=\sqrt{2\pi\tilde{\lambda}}\lambda^{j-1}e^{-\tilde{\lambda}}I_{j-1}(\tilde{\lambda}).

Here we consider the asymptotic expansion of
\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}(z)
for large values of
|z|
, which can be obtained by the stationary phase method in integral (
3.10
),

(3.16)

\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}(z)=z^{j-1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\hbar)^{k}}{z^{k}}\frac{a_{k}(j)}{8^{k}k!}\right),

where
a_{k}(j)=(4(j-1)^{2}-1^{2})(4(j-1)^{2}-3^{2})\ldots(4(j-1)^{2}-(2k-1)^{2})
.

Comparing (
3.16
) and (
3.14
) with (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) respectively we conclude, that (
3.14
) describes a tau-function of the
KP hierarchy in the Miwa parametrization. The vectors
\Phi_{j}^{\BGW}(z)
define a point of the Sato Grassmannian, associated with the BGW tau-function

(3.17)

{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}={\rm span}\,\{\Phi_{1}^{\BGW},\Phi_{2}^{\BGW},\dots\}\in\rm{Gr}^{(0)}_{+}.

Moreover, as we will see below, it describes the KdV reduction of the KP hierarchy. The KdV integrability of the BGW model was established by Mironov–Morozov–Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
.

3.2.
KS description of the BGW tau-function

Operators

(3.18)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}={z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{z}{\hbar},\quad\quad{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}=z^{2}\end{split}

stabilize the point
{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}
of the Sato Grassmannian
[
MMS96
]
,

(3.19)

{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW},\quad\quad{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}.

Therefore, they are the KS operators for this point. These operators satisfy the commutation relation
\left[{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW},{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}\right]=2{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}
.

Then, Lemma
2.1
allows one to construct linear constraints for the BGW tau-function.
The KS operators
{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}^{k}
describe a KdV reduction of the tau-function

(3.20)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2k}}\tau_{\BGW}=0.

KS operators
{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}^{k}{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}
imply the Virasoro constraints

(3.21)

\left(\frac{1}{2}\widehat{L}_{2k}-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2k+1}}+\frac{\delta_{k,0}}{16}\right)\cdot\tau_{\BGW}=0

for
k\geq 0
. These Virasoro constraints for the unitary matrix integral description of the BGW tau-function (
3.1
) are derived by Gross and Newman
[
GN92
]
.

The BGW tau-function also satisfies the dimension constraint

(3.22)

\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}{\tau}_{\BGW}=\widehat{L}_{0}\cdot{\tau}_{\BGW}.

Let us consider the operator

(3.23)

\begin{split}\widehat{W}_{\BGW}=\sum_{k,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}^{\infty}\left(kmt_{k}t_{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k+m-1}}+\frac{1}{2}(k+m+1)t_{k+m+1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{k}\partial t_{m}}\right)+\frac{t_{1}}{8}.\end{split}

From the constraints (
3.20
), (
3.21
), and (
3.22
), satisfied by
\tau_{\BGW}
, it follows that the BGW tau-function satisfies the equation

(3.24)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}\tau_{\BGW}=\widehat{W}_{\BGW}\cdot\tau_{\BGW}.

The solution normalized by
\tau_{\BGW}({\bf 0})=1
is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(
[
Ale18
]
)
.

(3.25)

\tau_{\BGW}=\exp\left({\hbar\widehat{W}_{\BGW}}\right)\cdot 1.

Let us consider the
topological expansion
of the tau-function,

(3.26)

\tau_{\BGW}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tau_{\BGW}^{(k)}\hbar^{k}.

The coefficients
\tau_{\BGW}^{(k)}
are homogenous polynomials in
{\bf t}
.
They satisfy a linear recursion relation

(3.27)

\tau_{\BGW}^{(k)}=\frac{\widehat{W}_{\BGW}}{k}\cdot\tau_{\BGW}^{(k-1)}.

We call it the
algebraic topological recursion
to distinguish it from the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion.

Remark 3.1
.

According to Norbury’s conjecture
[
Nor23
]
, recently proven in
[
CGFG22
]
, the BGW tau-function is the generating function of certain intersection numbers on the moduli spaces of punctured Riemann surfaces. For this interpretation of the BGW tau-function the parameter
\hbar
describes the expansion in the Euler characteristic of the punctured Riemann surface, and the operator
\widehat{W}_{\BGW}
describes the change of topology.
That is why slightly abusing notation we call such operators the cut-and-join operators.

The operator
\widehat{W}_{\BGW}
consists of an infinite number of terms, however, on each step of the recursion (
3.27
) only a finite number of them contribute. Hence, the recursion is given by the action of the polynomial differential operators on polynomials.

3.3.
Canonical KS operators

In
[
Ale18
]
it was shown that the KS operators
{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}
and

(3.28)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}&=\frac{1}{{\mathtt{b}}_{\BGW}}{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}^{2}\\
&=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{2}{\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}+\frac{1}{4z^{2}}\end{split}

completely specify the point
{\mathcal{W}}_{\BGW}
of the Sato Grassmannian.
In particular, it means that the canonical KS operators
{\mathtt{P}}_{\BGW}
and
{\mathtt{Q}}_{\BGW}
can be expressed in terms of
{\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}
and
{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}
.
Let us find the canonical pair of KS operators for the BGW tau-function.

Proposition 3.1
.

For the BGW tau-function the canonical pair of KS operators is given by

(3.29)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{P}}_{\BGW}&=\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}-\frac{1}{\hbar},\\
{\mathtt{Q}}_{\BGW}&=\left({\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}}.\end{split}

Proof.

To prove the first equation we need to show that
\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}-\frac{1}{\hbar}\in\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+{\mathcal{D}}_{-}
and that it is a KS operator for
\mathcal{W}_{\BGW}
.
Consider the operator

(3.30)

{\mathtt{c}}_{0}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\right)^{2}.

This is not a KS operator for
\mathcal{W}_{\BGW}
. We have

\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{0}}-\frac{1}{\hbar}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
.
Since
{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}-{\mathtt{c}}_{0}=\frac{1}{4z^{2}}
, we have

(3.31)

\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}-\frac{1}{\hbar}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+{\mathtt{d}},

where
{\mathtt{d}}\in z^{-1}{\mathcal{D}}_{-}
.
Moreover,

(3.32)

\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\sqrt{1+{\mathtt{A}}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(1+\frac{{\mathtt{A}}}{2}-\frac{{\mathtt{A}}^{2}}{8}+\dots\right)

where

(3.33)

{\mathtt{A}}=\hbar^{2}{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}-1=\hbar^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+2\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4z^{2}}

is a KS operator. Therefore,
\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}
is a KS operator, and from (
3.31
) we have the first equation in (
3.29
).

We also have

(3.34)

\left({\mathtt{a}}_{\BGW}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}}=z\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\right)\frac{1}{\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\hbar}+{\mathtt{d}}}\in z+{\mathcal{D}}_{-}.

The operator in (
3.34
) is a KS operator, therefore it coincides with
{\mathtt{Q}}_{\BGW}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The canonical pair of KS operators completely specify the point of the Sato Grassmannian
[
Ale21
]
. In particular,
the operator
{\mathtt{P}}_{\mathcal{W}}
annihilates the first basis vector, hence it defines the quantum spectral curve. For
\mathcal{W}_{\BGW}
we have

(3.35)

{\mathtt{P}}_{\BGW}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\BGW}=0,

or, equivalently,

(3.36)

\sqrt{{\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\BGW}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\Phi_{1}^{\BGW}.

This equation has a unique monic solution in the space of formal Laurent series
{\mathbb{C}}(\!(z^{-1})\!)
. From (
3.36
) we have

(3.37)

\left({\mathtt{c}}_{\BGW}-\frac{1}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\BGW}=0,

or

(3.38)

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\frac{2}{\hbar}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{4z^{2}}\right)\cdot\Phi_{1}^{\BGW}=0.

This is the quantum Bessel curve equation
[
Ale18
,
DN18
]
. All higher basis vectors can be obtained by the action of the raising operator
{\mathtt{Q}}_{\BGW}
, see (
2.15
).

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
Ale21
]
the canonical KS operators were constructed for the Kontsevich–Witten tau-function, a close cousin of the BGW tau-function. Here we see a difference in the KS description of two models. While the canonical KS operators for the Kontsevich–Witten tau-function are polynomial in
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
,
z
and
z^{-1}
, they are infinite sums in
z^{-1}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
for the BGW case.

4.
Higher BGW tau-functions

In this section, we investigate the higher BGW tau-functions, introduced by Mironov–Morozov–Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
.
In particular, we construct a complete set of the KS operators, the quantum spectral curve, the
W^{(3)}
-constraints and the cut-and-join description for the simplest case

.

4.1.
Matrix models and tau-functions

Following Mironov–Morozov–Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
we consider the generalized Kontsevich models with antipolynomial (to be precise, antimonomial) potential

(4.1)

Z^{(m)}=\frac{\displaystyle{\int[d\Phi]\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\text{Tr}\left(\frac{1}{m+1}\Lambda^{m+1}\Phi+\frac{1}{m(m+1)\Phi^{m}}-\hbar M\log\Phi\right)\right)}}{\displaystyle{\int[d\Phi]\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\text{Tr}~~\left(\frac{1}{m(m+1)\Phi^{m}}-\hbar M\log\Phi\right)\right)}}.

Below we call them the
higher BGW
models. Original BGW model (
3.3
) corresponds to
m=1
,

(4.2)

Z_{\BGW}=Z^{(1)},

and below we assume that
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. In (
4.1
) we integrate over the
M\times M
normal matrices
\Phi
associated to the Hankel’s loop
\gamma
, see (
3.5
). For simplicity we again assume that
\Lambda={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{M})
is a positive defined diagonal matrix and
\hbar
is positive. For arbitrary complex
\lambda_{j}
and
\hbar
the contours defining the normal matrix
\Phi
would be rotated correspondingly.

After the integration over the unitary group with the help of the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber formula the matrix model (
4.1
) reduces to

(4.3)

Z^{(m)}=(-1)^{\frac{M(M-1)}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{M}(j-1)!\frac{\text{det}_{i,j=1}^{M}\left(\mathcal{I}_{M-i}^{(m)}(\lambda_{j})\right)}{\Delta\left({\frac{{\lambda}^{m+1}}{(m+1)\hbar}}\right)},

where

(4.4)

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}^{(m)}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)\hbar}\left(mz^{m+1}\varphi+\frac{1}{\varphi^{m}}\right)}\frac{d\varphi}{\varphi^{\nu+1}}.

Note that,
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}^{(1)}(z)=\mathcal{I}_{\nu}(z)
given by equation (
3.8
).

In this paper we are focused on the asymptotic expansion of the partition function
Z^{(m)}
for the large eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
. This allows us to associate a tau-function of the KP hierarchy with a higher BGW model.
With the quasi-classical prefactor

(4.5)

{\mathcal{C}}_{m}=\frac{{\Delta(\lambda)e^{\frac{\text{Tr}{\Lambda}^{m}}{m\hbar}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}(j-1)!}}{{\Delta\left(\frac{\lambda^{m+1}}{(m+1)\hbar}\right)\left(\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\right)^{\frac{M}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{m}{2}}}},

we have the determinant formula

(4.6)

{\mathcal{C}}_{m}^{-1}Z^{(m)}=\frac{\text{det}_{i,j=1}^{M}\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(\lambda_{i})}{\Delta(\lambda)}.

Here

(4.7)

\begin{split}\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)&=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi{z}^{m}}{\hbar}}e^{-\frac{z^{m}}{m\hbar}}\mathcal{I}_{j-1}^{(m)}({z})\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi{z}^{m}}{\hbar}}e^{-\frac{z^{m}}{m\hbar}}\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{1}{m(m+1)\hbar}\left(mz^{m+1}\varphi+\frac{1}{\varphi^{m}}\right)}\frac{d\varphi}{\varphi^{j}},\end{split}

and in the last line we assume the asymptotic expansion of the integral at large positive
z
.

Let us consider this asymptotic expansion in more detail. For the potential

(4.8)

V(\varphi)=\frac{1}{m(m+1)\hbar}\left(mz^{m+1}\varphi+\frac{1}{\varphi^{m}}\right)

we consider the critical points of the potential satisfying

(4.9)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}V(\varphi)=0.

The solutions
\varphi_{k}=z^{-1}e^{\iota\frac{2\pi k}{m+1}}
,
k\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}
correspond to the roots of the unity. The contour of integration
\gamma
can be smoothly deformed without crossing these points in a way that the new contour contains an arc
(z^{-1}-\iota\epsilon,z^{-1}+\iota\epsilon)
with small
\epsilon
. Here
z
is assumed to be positive. The steepest descent method allows us to find the asymptotic expansion of the integral by the expansion in the neighborhood of the stationary point
\varphi_{0}=z^{-1}
. This point corresponds to the dominant contribution in (
4.7
), contributions of the other critical points are suppressed and can be neglected.
We have
V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})>0
, therefore it is convenient to consider a change of variables
\varphi\mapsto\varphi_{0}+\iota\frac{\varphi}{V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})}
. Then, the steepest descent method gives the asymptotic expansion

(4.10)

\mathcal{I}_{j}^{(m)}(z)=\frac{\varphi_{0}^{-j}e^{V(\varphi_{0})}}{2\pi\sqrt{V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\varphi\left(1+\frac{\iota\varphi}{\varphi_{0}\sqrt{V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})}}\right)^{-j}e^{-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}t_{\ell}^{*}\varphi^{\ell}}.

Here

(4.11)

t_{\ell}^{*}=\frac{(\iota)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\frac{V^{(\ell)}(\varphi_{0})}{V^{\prime\prime}(\varphi_{0})^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}.

More explicitly, we have

(4.12)

\mathcal{I}_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}e^{\frac{z^{m}}{m\hbar}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\varphi\left(1+\iota\varphi\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}}\right)^{-j}e^{-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}t_{\ell}^{*}\varphi^{\ell}}

with

(4.13)

t_{\ell}^{*}=\frac{(-\iota)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\frac{(m+\ell-1)!}{(m+1)!}\left(\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}.

and
t_{\ell}^{*}=0
for
\ell<3
.
Using this asymptotic expansion of (
4.7
) we introduce

Definition 4
.

(4.14)

\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\varphi\left(1+\iota\varphi\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}}\right)^{-j}e^{-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}t_{\ell}^{*}\varphi^{\ell}}.

Remark 4.1
.

These formal series are well defined for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}(1+O(z^{-1}))
.
Moreover,
z^{N}\Phi_{j-N}^{(m)}(z)\in z^{j-1}(1+O(z^{-1}))
is a well defined formal series of
z^{-1}
and
N
for arbitrary
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Consider the expansion

e^{\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}t_{\ell}^{*}\varphi^{\ell}}=1+\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}p_{k}({\bf t^{*}})\varphi^{k}
, where
p_{k}
is the elementary Schur function. From Definition
4
we have

(4.15)

\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}j+2k-1\\
j-1\end{pmatrix}\left(-\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}\right)^{k}(2k-1)!!\right.\\
\left.+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}p_{2\ell}({\bf t^{*}})(2\ell-1)!!+g_{j}(m,z)\right),

where

(4.16)

\begin{split}g_{j}(m,z)&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}p_{2\ell+1}({\bf t}^{*})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}j+2k\\
j-1\end{pmatrix}\left(\frac{-\iota\hbar^{\frac{1}{2}}}{z^{m/2}}\right)^{2k+1}(2\ell+2k+1)!!\\
&+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}p_{2\ell}({\bf t}^{*})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}j+2k+1\\
j-1\end{pmatrix}\left(-\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}\right)^{k+1}(2\ell+2k+1)!!\end{split}

and
\begin{pmatrix}n\\
r\end{pmatrix}=\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}
are the binomial coefficients.

4.2.
Higher BGW model as a tau-function of KP hierarchy

The prefactor (
4.5
) is chosen in such a way that

(4.17)

\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}\left(1+O(z^{-m})\right).

These
\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)
constitute an admissible basis for a point in the Sato Grassmannian

(4.18)

\mathcal{W}_{m}={\rm span}\,\{\Phi_{1}^{(m)},\Phi_{2}^{(m)},\Phi_{3}^{(m)},\ldots\}\in\rm{Gr}^{(0)}_{+}.

Therefore, the higher BGW models

(4.19)

\tau^{(m)}\left(\left[\Lambda^{-1}\right]\right)={\mathcal{C}}_{m}^{-1}Z^{(m)}

define tau-functions of the KP hierarchy in the Miwa parametrization

(4.20)

\tau^{(m)}\left(\left[\Lambda^{-1}\right]\right)=\frac{\text{det}_{i,j=1}^{M}\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(\lambda_{i})}{\Delta(\lambda)}.

We call them the
higher BGW tau-functions
. For
m=1
the tau-function coincides with the BGW tau-function considered in Section
3
,
\tau^{(1)}=\tau_{\BGW}
and
\Phi_{k}^{(1)}=\Phi_{k}^{\BGW}
. From (
4.17
) we have
\tau^{(m)}({\bf 0})=1
. Below we investigate these tau-functions.

Let us introduce the Euler operator

(4.21)

\widehat{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}},

which coincides with
\widehat{L}_{0}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The tau-function
\tau^{(m)}
satisfies the homogeneity condition

(4.22)

\widehat{D}\cdot\tau^{(m)}=m\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial\hbar}\tau^{(m)}.

Proof.

Up to a simple prefactor, the functions
\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)
depend only on a combination of
z
and
\hbar
, namely
\frac{z^{m}}{\hbar}
.
Indeed, if we change the variable of integration
\varphi\mapsto\varphi/z
in (
4.7
), we have

(4.23)

\Phi_{j}^{(m)}(z)=z^{j-1}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi{z}^{m}}{\hbar}}e^{-\frac{z^{m}}{m\hbar}}\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{z^{m}}{m(m+1)\hbar}\left(m\varphi+\frac{1}{\varphi^{m}}\right)}\frac{d\varphi}{\varphi^{j}}=z^{j-1}f_{j}\left(\frac{\hbar}{z^{m}}\right)

for some functions
f_{j}
. Then the statement of the lemma follows immediately from the determinant formula (
4.20
) and the definition of the Miwa parametrization (
2.7
).
∎

Using the expansion (
4.15
) it is easy to find explicitly the leading coefficients of the expansion of basis vectors

(4.24)

\Phi_{j}^{(m)}=z^{j-1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Phi_{j,k}^{(m)}\left(\frac{\hbar}{z^{k}}\right)^{m}\right),

they are presented in Appendix
A
. They are polynomials,
\Phi_{j,k}^{(m)}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[j,m]
.
For
m=1
the coefficients are given by (
3.16
).

Let us consider the perturbative expansion of the functions
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}^{(m)}(z)
, given by the contour integrals (

) for small
|z|
.
We put

(4.25)

\chi=\frac{z^{m+1}}{(m+1)\hbar}.

Let us change the variable of integration

. Then

(4.26)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{\nu}^{(m)}(z)&=\chi^{\nu}\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\varphi+\frac{\chi^{m}}{m(m+1)\hbar}\frac{1}{\varphi^{m}}}\frac{d\varphi}{\varphi^{\nu+1}}\\
&=\chi^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{\chi^{m}}{m(m+1)\hbar}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{\Gamma(km+\nu+1)}\\
&=\frac{\chi^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}{}_{0}F_{m}\left(;\left\{\frac{1+\nu}{m},\frac{2+\nu}{m}\ldots,\frac{m+\nu}{m}\right\};\frac{\chi^{m}}{m^{m+1}(m+1)\hbar}\right),\end{split}

where we use the Hankel’s loop integral formula for the reciprocal gamma function

(4.27)

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}=\frac{1}{2\pi\iota}\int_{\gamma}e^{\varphi}\frac{d\varphi}{\varphi^{\nu+1}}.

Here the generalized hypergeometric function
{}_{0}F_{m}\left(;\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\};x\right)
is defined by

(4.28)

\displaystyle{}_{0}F_{m}\left(;\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{q}\};x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(b_{1})_{k}(b_{2})_{k}\ldots(b_{m})_{k}}\frac{x^{k}}{k!},

where
(b)_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(b+k)}{\Gamma(b)}
. For

this expression is closely related to the expansion of the modified Bessel function (
3.10
).

For large values of
|z|
the coefficients of

’s also coincide with the coefficients of the assymptotic expansion of the of hyper-geometric functions. We have compared the first three terms of these expressions for
m=1
and
m=2
case and found a perfect agreement.

4.3.
KS algebra for higher BGW tau-functions

In the previous section, we construct the points of the Sato Grassmannian associated with the higher BGW tau-functions. In this section, we discuss the KS algebras for these points. Description of the higher BGW tau-functions by the KS operators was initiated by Mironov–Morozov–Semenoff
[
MMS96
]
, however, their description is incomplete. Here we provide a complete description, in particular, construct a canonical pair of KS operators.

Consider the operators

(4.29)

{\mathtt{a}}_{m}={z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{m}{2}+\frac{z^{m}}{\hbar},\quad\quad{\mathtt{b}}_{m}=z^{m+1}

satisfying the commutation relation

(4.30)

[{\mathtt{a}}_{m},{\mathtt{b}}_{m}]=(m+1){\mathtt{b}}_{m}.

Using the integration by parts in the integral representation of
\Phi_{j}^{(m)}
, see (
4.7
), one can show that

(4.31)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{a}}_{m}\cdot\Phi_{j}^{(m)}&={(j-1)(m+1)}\Phi_{j}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\Phi_{j+m}^{(m)},\\
{\mathtt{b}}_{m}\cdot\Phi_{j}^{(m)}&=j(m+1)\hbar\Phi_{j+1}^{(m)}+\Phi_{j+m+1}^{(m)}.\end{split}

Therefore, the operators
{\mathtt{a}}_{m}
and
{\mathtt{b}}_{m}
stabilize the point (
4.18
) of the Sato Grassmannian

(4.32)

{\mathtt{a}}_{m}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m}\in\mathcal{W}_{m},\quad\quad{\mathtt{b}}_{m}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{m}\in\mathcal{W}_{m},

and
{\mathtt{a}}_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{m}}
,
{\mathtt{b}}_{m}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{m}}
are the KS operators.

However, it easy to see that the operators
{\mathtt{a}}_{m}
and
{\mathtt{b}}_{m}
do not generate the KS algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{W}}_{m}}
, therefore they do not
specify the point
\mathcal{W}_{m}
in the Sato Grassmannian. To specify this point uniquely we need to consider some other KS operators.

Remark 4.2
.

Using integration by parts of the basis vectors (
4.7
) we have

(4.33)

\frac{\hbar}{{\mathtt{b}}_{m}}{\mathtt{a}}_{m}\cdot\Phi_{j}^{(m)}=\Phi_{j-1}^{(m)},

which implies

(4.34)

\frac{\hbar}{{\mathtt{b}}_{m}}{\mathtt{a}}_{m}\cdot\Phi_{1}^{(m)}=\Phi_{0}^{(m)}\notin\mathcal{W}_{m}.

Hence,
{\mathtt{b}}_{m}^{-1}{\mathtt{a}}_{m}
is not a KS operator for
\mathcal{W}_{m}

[

4.6.

In the previous section, we construct the

[

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{3k+1}}\tau^{(2)}&=\left(2\hbar\widehat{L}_{3k-1}-\hbar^{2}\left(\widehat{M}_{3k-3}+\frac{2}{3}\widehat{J}_{3k-3}\right)\right)\cdot\tau^{(2)},\end{split}

for

\widehat{W}_{2}&=-2t_{3}t_{1}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(3k+1)t_{3k+1}\widehat{M}_{3k-3},\end{split}

and

is the Euler operator (

Remark 4.7

In this section, we introduce a one-parametric deformation of the higher BGW tau-functions, considered in the previous section. In particular, for this deformation we construct a complete set of the KS operators, the quantum spectral curve, the

5.1.
Matrix models and tau-functions

Let us consider the matrix model
[

(5.1)

For

Using the Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber formula for the integrals (

The functions

The coefficients of the expansion of the basis vectors

Constraining Quadratic f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) Gravity from Astrophysical Observations of the Pulsar J0704+6620
G. G. L. Nashed, Waleed El Hanafy

Abstract
We apply quadratic f ⁡ ( R ) = R + ϵ ​ R 2 f(R)=R+\epsilon R^{2} field equations, where ϵ \epsilon has a dimension [L 2 ], to static spherical stellar model. We assume the interior configuration is determined by Krori-Barua ansatz and additionally the fluid is anisotropic. Using the astrophysical measurements of the pulsar PSR J0740+6620 as inferred by NICER and XMM observations, we determine ϵ ≈ ± 3 \epsilon\approx\pm 3 km 2 . We show that the model can provide a stable configuration of the pulsar PSR J0740+6620 in both geometrical and physical sectors. We show that the Krori-Barua ansatz within f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) quadratic gravity provides semi-analytical relations between radial, p r p_{r} , and tangential, p t p_{t} , pressures and density ρ \rho which can be expressed as p r ≈ v r 2 ​ ( ρ − ρ 1 ) p_{r}\approx v_{r}^{2}(\rho-\rho_{1}) and p r ≈ v t 2 ​ ( ρ − ρ 2 ) p_{r}\approx v_{t}^{2}(\rho-\rho_{2}) , where v r v_{r} ( v t v_{t} ) is the sound speed in radial (tangential) direction, ρ 1 = ρ s \rho_{1}=\rho_{s} (surface density) and ρ 2 \rho_{2} are completely determined in terms of the model parameters. These relations are in agreement with the best-fit equations of state as obtained in the present study. We further put the upper limit on the compactness, which satisfies the f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) modified Buchdahl limit. Interestingly, the quadratic f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity with negative ϵ \epsilon naturally restricts the maximum compactness to values lower than Buchdahl limit, unlike the GR or f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity with positive ϵ \epsilon where the compactness can arbitrarily approach the black hole limit C → 1 C\to 1 . The model predicts a core density a few times the saturation nuclear density ρ nuc = 2.7 × 10 14 \rho_{\text{nuc}}=2.7\times 10^{14} g/cm 3 , and a surface density ρ s > ρ nuc \rho_{s}>\rho_{\text{nuc}} . We provide the mass-radius diagram corresponding to the obtained boundary density which has been shown to be in agreement with other observations.

II
Basic formalism of

gravity

Let the pair (
\mathcal{M},g

\mathcal{M}

{{\mathcal{S}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa}f(R)+{L}_{m}\right]},

(1)

1

{f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}f_{R}+g_{\mu\nu}\square f_{R}=\kappa\mathfrak{T}_{\mu\nu}},

(2)

where
{f_{R}\equiv df(R)/dR}

{\square\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}}
with

{f(R)=R}

As in the GR theory, the left-hand side of Eq. (
2

{ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-e^{\psi}c^{2}dt^{2}+e^{\lambda}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})},

(3)

r

\phi

On the other hand, for the right-hand side of Eq. (
2

{\mathfrak{T}{{}^{\mu}}{{}_{\nu}}}={(p_{t}+\rho c^{2})v{{}^{\mu}}v{{}_{\nu}}+p_{t}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}+(p_{r}-p_{t})w{{}^{\mu}}w{{}_{\nu}}}\,,

(4)

{3\square f_{R}(R)+Rf_{R}(R)-2f(R)=\kappa\mathfrak{T}}.

(5)

In this sense, the

As in the GR theory, the matter divergence-free energy-momentum tensor provides the conservation law of energy and momentum. For the spacetime (

VI
Conclusion

In the present study, we investigated the quadratic
f(R)=R+\epsilon R^{2}
gravity by confronting the theory with astrophysical observations of pulsars. We assumed the more realistic case of anisotropic fluid as expected for high dense matter inside pulsars. We additionally assumed the interior of the static spherically symmetric stellar model is governed by KB ansatz which guarantee the regularity of the interior spacetime. In particular, we used the accurate measurements of the mass and radius of the PSR J0740+6620,
M=2.07\pm 0.11M_{\odot}
and radius
R=12.34^{+1.89}_{-1.67}
km, as inferred by NICER+XMM observations
(
Legred et al. 2021

to constraint the parameter space of the
present model
of quadratic
f(R)
gravity in the
present model
, i.e. {
\epsilon
,
C
}. On the other hand, the PSR J0740+6620 is one of the heaviest pulsars which makes it a perfect choice to test modified gravity.

In principal, strong anisotropy induces an extra repulsive force in the TOV equation which increases the size of the star and consequently allows for stable stars with higher compactness and higher maximum mass values relative to the isotropic case. Therefore strong anisotropic star could provide a better framework to deal with heavy compact stars
M\sim 2M_{\odot}
. On the other hand, for the heavy compact stars, it lowers the radial sound speed in comparison to the isotropic case and consequently predicts softer EoS in agreement with the deformabilities of the observed gravitational wave signals.

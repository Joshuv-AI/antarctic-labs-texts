Factorization of a prime matrix in even blocks
Haoming Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, a matrix is said to be prime if the row and column of this matrix are both prime numbers. We establish various necessary and sufficient conditions for developing matrices into the sum of tensor products of prime matrices. For example, if the diagonal of a matrix blocked evenly are pairwise commutative, it yields such a decomposition. The computational complexity of multiplication of these algorithms is shown to be O ⁡ ( n 5 / 2 ) O(n^{5/2}) . In the section 5, a decomposition is proved to hold if and only if every even natural number greater than 2 is the sum of two prime numbers.

1.
Introduction

In this note, a prime matrix is a
p\times q
matrix with both
p
and
q
prime numbers. Assume
m
and
n
are natural numbers with the prime decomposition. The aim of this paper is two-fold:

•

Reducing an
m\times n
matrix into the sum of tensor products of prime matrices.

•

Reducing an
n\times n
Hermite matrix into the sum of tensor products of prime matrices.

An
m\times n
matrix might be developed into the sum of tensor products of prime matrices,
In linear algebra, an
m\times n
matrix
A
has the spectral decomposition in the sense that

(1.1)

A=\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{m,n\}}\sigma_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{*},

where
U=[u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m}]
and
V=[v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}]
are orthogonal matrices. It’s quite hard to compute the
\sigma_{i},u_{i}
and
v_{i}
when the size of matrix is large. The usual method relies on the computation of eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
AA^{*}
and
A^{*}A
. If
n=m
, with the aid of usual elimination algorithm, the computational complexity is
O(n^{3})
, which increases the same as a cubic polynomial as the size of matrix gets large. However, this could be remedied provided a tensor form exists by the prime number decomposition theorem.

If
m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}p_{2}^{r_{2}}\dots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
and
n=q_{1}^{s_{1}}q_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots q_{l}^{s_{l}}
are the prime decomposition of

and

respectively, the

matrix

could be blocked according to the prime decomposition. For example, an
m^{\prime}p\times n^{\prime}q
matrix

(1.2)

A=\begin{bNiceMatrix}q&\dots&q&\\
A_{11}&\dots&A_{1n^{\prime}}&p\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\

\end{bNiceMatrix}

where each

has

consists of a pairwise commutative family of matrices of

(1.3)

AB^{*}=BA^{*},\quad A^{*}B=B^{*}A,

for each

\{b_{i}\}
and
\{c_{j}\}
which diagonalizes each matrix

, say,

(1.4)

A_{ii}=\sigma_{ii}^{k}b_{k}c_{k}^{*},

where the sum is taken over repeated index. Therefore, the
m^{\prime}p\times n^{\prime}q
block matrix can be developed into the sum of tensor products

(1.5)

(1_{m^{\prime}\times m^{\prime}}\otimes B)^{*}A(1_{n^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}}\otimes C)=\begin{bNiceMatrix}q&\dots&q&\\
\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{11}^{k})&\dots&A^{\prime}_{1n^{\prime}}&p\\

\end{bNiceMatrix}

The second aim of this paper is to reduce an

2.

.

Let

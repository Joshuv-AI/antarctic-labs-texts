1
Introduction

KLR algebras
, also called
(affine) quiver Hecke algebras
, were introduced by Khovanov–Lauda
[
KL09
]
and Rouquier
[
Rou08
]
to give a categorification of the negative half of quantum groups. KLR algebras have natural finite-dimensional quotients
R^{\Lambda}(\beta)
for a fixed dominant integral weight
\Lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
and varying
\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}
a non-negative integral linear combination of simple roots, called the
cyclotomic KLR algebras
, or
cyclotomic quiver Hecke algebras
. The module categories of cyclotomic KLR algebras
R^{\Lambda}(\beta)
for various
\beta
, together with the induction and restriction functors between them give a categorification for the irreducible highest weight module
V(\Lambda)
over a quantum group. Brundan and Kleshchev
[
BK09a
]
showed that the block algebras of the cyclotomic Hecke algebras are isomorphic to the cyclotomic KLR algebras of type
A_{\ell}^{(1)}
, and so cyclotomic KLR algebras are a vast generalization of certain cyclotomic Hecke algebras, which are well understood.

In affine type
A^{(1)}_{\ell}
, further advances have been made in understanding the structure of cyclotomic KLR algebras. In addition to the Brundan–Kleshchev isomorphism theorem, we also have a categorification theorem due to Ariki
[
Ari96
]
, Brundan and Kleshchev which implies that in characteristic zero the simple objects in these categories correspond to the dual canonical basis of the integral highest weight module
V_{\mathbb{A}}(\Lambda)
over the affine type
A
Kac–Moody Lie algebra, using a Fock space construction.
Beyond type
A
, there is still much to discover about cyclotomic KLR algebras and their block algebras.
Classically, the representation type of block algebras of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of the symmetric group was described by Erdmann and Nakano. Beyond type
A
, a general representation type classification in the style of Erdmann–Nakano for the block algebras of cyclotomic KLR algebras is a subject of active research.
For the special case
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}
, this has been investigated in a series of recent papers by Ariki–Iijima–Park for type
A^{(1)}_{\ell}
and Ariki–Park for types
A_{2l}^{(2)},D^{(2)}_{\ell+1}
and
C^{(1)}_{\ell}

[
AIP15
,
AP14
,
AP16a
,
AP16b
]
, resulting in a Lie theoretic classification of representation type for
R^{\Lambda_{0}}(\beta)
in the spirit of Erdmann–Nakano.
In affine types
A
(both twisted and untwisted) and
D^{(2)}_{\ell+1}
, the representation types turned out to be governed by the weight, also called defect, introduced by Fayers
[
Fay06
]
, as a natural generalisation of the classification by Erdmann–Nakano.
In affine type
C
Ariki–Park showed that
R^{\Lambda_{0}}(\delta)
is no longer of finite representation type; in fact the algebra
R^{\Lambda_{0}}(\delta)
is wild except in type
C^{(1)}_{2}
, where it is tame
[
AP16b
]
.
Also, for arbitrary fundamental weight
\Lambda=\Lambda_{k}
(i.e. level
1
) in untwisted affine ADE type, the set of maximal weights
\max(\Lambda)
of
V(\Lambda)
form a single Weyl group orbit
[
Kac90
, Lem. 12.6]
.
The same is not true for affine type
C
; as in this case
\max(\Lambda_{k})
consists of several Weyl group orbits, whose maximal representatives were given by Ariki and Park in
[
AP16b
]
when
k=0
.
Nevertheless, we can show in this paper that the defect governs the representation type in affine type
C
for arbitrary
\Lambda=\Lambda_{k}
.
As a first step, we enumerate a set of maximal weight representatives

\displaystyle\xi_{k,i}

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{k+1}+2\alpha_{k+2}+\dots+(i-1)\alpha_{k+i-1}+i(\alpha_{k+i}+\alpha_{k+i+1}+\dots+\alpha_{\ell-1})+\frac{i}{2}\alpha_{\ell};

\displaystyle\xi_{k,-i}

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{k-1}+2\alpha_{k-2}+\dots+(i-1)\alpha_{k-i+1}+i(\alpha_{k-i}+\alpha_{k-i-1}+\dots+\alpha_{1})+\frac{i}{2}\alpha_{0}

for
k\pm i\in I
. Thus, we need only investigate
R^{\Lambda_{k}}(m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i})
for
m\geq i/2
, a fact we will also use to show that the defect is non-negative in level one.

We explain the cases where we need different arguments than
[
AP16b
]
.
First, we consider
i=0
and
m=1
, that is the representation type of
R^{\Lambda_{k}}(\delta)
.
For the case
\ell=2
and
k=1
, we give an explicit description of the indecomposable projective modules and prove that the algebra has tame representation type.
For larger
\ell
and
k
, we are able to apply a recent interesting result of Ariki (
[
Ari17
]
, see also
2.16
) which reduces the problem to finding two appropriate idempotents.
We show that if
\ell\neq 2
and
0\leq k\leq\ell
,
R^{\Lambda_{k}}(\delta)
is of wild representation type.
Then, we will show that
R^{\Lambda_{k}}(\delta-\xi_{k,\pm 2})
is of finite representation type, and hence that block algebras of defect one are Brauer tree algebras whose Brauer tree is a straight line with no exceptional vertex. We will also see that depending on
k
, there can be two inequivalent Morita equivalence classes of blocks of defect one, with distinct number of simple modules
k+1
or
\ell-k+1
respectively.
Next, we deal with the representation type of
R^{\Lambda_{k}}(2\delta-\xi_{k,\pm 4})
and using
2.16
again, we arrive at the result that this algebra has wild representation type as well.
Finally, we handle the remaining cases by using the same arguments as in
[
AP16b
]
with some slight modifications.
Our results are summarised in
6.5
.

Background

2.1
Lie theory notation

We mostly follow
[
Kac90
]
and use standard notation for the root datum.

Let
\ell\in\{2,3,\dots\}
and
I=\{0,1,2,\ldots,\ell\}
.

The affine Cartan matrix of type
C^{(1)}_{\ell}
is given by

\mathsf{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
-2&2&-1&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&-1&2&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&2&-1&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-1&2&-2\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}.

We have
simple roots

\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}\mid i\in I\}
and
fundamental weights

\{\Lambda_{i}\mid i\in I\}
in the
weight lattice

\mathsf{P}
, and simple coroots
\Pi^{\vee}=\{\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\mid i\in I\}
in the
dual weight lattice

\mathsf{P}^{\vee}
.
Each
\alpha_{i}
gives rise to a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear transformation
r_{i}
acting on
\mathsf{P}
by

r_{i}\Lambda=\Lambda-\langle h_{i},\Lambda\rangle\alpha_{i}
, for
\Lambda\in\mathsf{P}
. Let
d\in P^{\vee}
be the element such that
\alpha_{i}(d)=\delta_{0,i}
.
We denote by
\mathsf{W}
the Weyl group, the group generated by
\{r_{i}\mid i\in I\}
.

There is a
\mathsf{W}
-invariant symmetric bilinear form
(-,-)
on
\mathsf{P}
satisfying the following:

1.

(\Lambda_{i},\alpha_{j})=d_{j}\delta_{ij}
; and

2.

(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=d_{i}a_{ij}

where
d=(2,1\dots,1,2)
if
\ell<\infty
.

We denote the set of
dominant integral weights
by

\mathsf{P}^{+}=\{\Lambda\in\mathsf{P}\mid\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\Lambda\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }i\in I\},

where
\langle
,
\rangle
is the natural pairing.
We call
\mathsf{Q}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
the
root lattice
and
\mathsf{Q}^{+}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{i}
the
positive cone
of the root lattice.
For
C^{(1)}_{\ell}
, we have the
null root
given by

\delta=\alpha_{0}+2\alpha_{1}+\dots+2\alpha_{\ell-1}+\alpha_{\ell}.

The
defect
of
\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}
(relative to
\Lambda
) is given by

\operatorname{def}_{\Lambda}(\beta)=(\Lambda,\beta)-\frac{1}{2}(\beta,\beta).

When it is clear from context, we will omit
\Lambda
from the subscript and write
\operatorname{def}(\beta)
instead of
\operatorname{def}_{\Lambda}(\beta)
.

We set
\xi_{k,0}:=0
, and for
k+i\in I
or
k-i\in I
we set

\displaystyle\xi_{k,i}

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{k+1}+2\alpha_{k+2}+\dots+(i-1)\alpha_{k+i-1}+i(\alpha_{k+i}+\alpha_{k+i+1}+\dots+\alpha_{\ell-1})+\frac{i}{2}\alpha_{\ell};

(1)

\displaystyle\xi_{k,-i}

\displaystyle:=\alpha_{k-1}+2\alpha_{k-2}+\dots+(i-1)\alpha_{k-i+1}+i(\alpha_{k-i}+\alpha_{k-i-1}+\dots+\alpha_{1})+\frac{i}{2}\alpha_{0}

respectively.

Note that if
i\neq 0
then

\xi_{k,\pm i}(\alpha_{j}^{\vee})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1&\hbox{ if }j=k,\\
1&\hbox{ if }j=k\pm i,\\
0&\hbox{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

(2)

and they form a basis for
\sum_{i\in I\setminus\{k\}}\mathbb{Q}\alpha_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
m\geq i/2
and so
m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i}\in\mathsf{Q}^{+}
, we have that

\operatorname{def}(m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i})=2m-\frac{1}{2}i
(relative to
\Lambda_{k}
).

Proof.

We have the following calculation:

\displaystyle\operatorname{def}_{\Lambda_{k}}(m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i})

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{k},m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i})-\frac{1}{2}(m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i},m\delta-\xi_{k,\pm i})

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{k},m\delta)-\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{k,\pm i},\xi_{k,\pm i})

\displaystyle=2m-\frac{1}{2}i

as required.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(\mathsf{A})
be the affine Kac–Moody algebra associated with the Cartan datum
(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{P},\Pi,\Pi^{\vee})
and let
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
be its quantum group.
The quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-algebra generated by
f_{i}
,
e_{i}

(i\in I)
and
q^{h}

(h\in\mathsf{P}^{\vee})
with certain relations (more details can be found in
[
HK02
, Chap. 3]
).
Let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
and denote by
U_{\mathbb{A}}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
the subalgebra of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by
f_{i}^{(n)}:=f_{i}^{n}/[n]_{i}!
for
i\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
where
q_{i}=q^{d_{i}}
and

\displaystyle[n]_{i}=\frac{q^{n}_{i}-q^{-n}_{i}}{q_{i}-q^{-1}_{i}},\

\displaystyle[n]_{i}!=\prod^{n}_{k=1}[k]_{i}.

For some
\Lambda\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
, let
V(\Lambda)
be the irreducible highest weight
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module with highest weight
\Lambda

and
V_{\mathbb{A}}(\Lambda)
the
U_{\mathbb{A}}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
-submodule of
V(\Lambda)
generated by the highest weight vector.

The Fock space representation for
U_{q}(C_{\ell}^{(1)})
was first constructed in
[
KMM93
]
by folding the Fock space representation for
U_{q}(A_{2\ell-1}^{(1)})
via the Dynkin diagram automorphism (see
eq.

3
).
Later, the combinatorics of the Fock space and its crystal base were described in terms of tableaux and Young diagrams (
[
KS04
,
Pre04
]
).
The next section focuses on explaining this combinatorial realisation for
V(\Lambda_{k})
.

2.2
Partitions and tableaux

Definition 2.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, a
partition
of
n
is a weakly decreasing sequence of non-negative integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
such that the sum

|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots
is equal to
n
. If
\lambda
is a partition of
n
we write
\lambda\vdash n
.
We write
\varnothing
for the unique partition of 0.
We will denote the set of partitions of
n
by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, we define its
Young diagram

[\lambda]
to be the set

\{(r,c)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\mid c\leq\lambda_{r}\}.

Note that we will depict a Young diagram of a partition using the English convention (i.e. successive rows of the diagram are lower down the page).

We define
f_{\ell}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow I
by
k\mapsto|k|
if
\ell=\infty
and, if
\ell\neq\infty
,
f_{\ell}:\mathbb{Z}/2\ell\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow I
by

\displaystyle f_{\ell}(0+2\ell\mathbb{Z})=0,\quad f_{\ell}(\ell+2\ell\mathbb{Z})=\ell,

(3)

\displaystyle f_{\ell}(k+2\ell\mathbb{Z})=f_{\ell}(2\ell-k+2\ell\mathbb{Z})=k\quad\text{ for }1\leq k\leq\ell-1.

Let
p
be the natural projection
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\ell\mathbb{Z}

.
We set

\pi_{\ell}=f_{\ell}\circ p:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow I
.
If there is no confusion, we will denote
\pi_{\ell}(k)
by
\overline{k}
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Given a
charge

\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
\Lambda_{\kappa}\in\mathsf{P}^{+}
by
\Lambda_{\kappa}:=\Lambda_{\overline{\kappa}}.

If
\lambda
is a partition of
n
, then to any node
A=(r,c)\in[\lambda]
we can associate its
residue
defined by

\mathrm{res}(A)=\overline{\kappa+c-r}.

If
\mathrm{res}(A)=i
, we call
A
an
i
-node.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
EN02
, Prop. 2.3]
,
[
AP16a
, Rem. 5.10]
)
.

Let

and

be finite dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-algebras and suppose that

is a direct summand of
GF(M)
as an
A
-module,

(2)

\dim F(M)\leq C\dim M
.

Then, if

is wild, so is

The next lemma is the analogous statement of
[

Lemma 6.2
.

(1)

If

is wild and

(2)

Suppose that

is wild,

(b)

Proof.

(1) Considering the functors

the statement follows from
6.1
and

.

(2) First, we will consider

.
Notice that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i+2}-(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i}+\alpha_{k+i+1}+2\sum^{\ell-1}_{j=k+i+2}\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{\ell}-(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i}-\alpha_{0}-2\sum^{k+i}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k+i+1}-n\delta.

For
0\leq k+i\leq\ell-1
and

, we compute

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i+2}-(n+1)\delta+\alpha_{k+i+1}

\displaystyle=r_{k+i}r_{k+i-1}\dots r_{1}r_{0}r_{1}\dots r_{k+i}(\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i}-n\delta),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i}-(n+1)\delta+\alpha_{\ell}

\displaystyle=r_{\ell-1}r_{\ell-2}\dots r_{1}r_{0}r_{1}\dots r_{k+i}(\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,i}-n\delta).

and now we apply (1) to arrive at the desired conclusion.

Using

(1)

Next, we look at

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,-(i+2)}-(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,-i}+\alpha_{0}+2\sum^{k-(i+2)}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{k-(i+1)}-(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,-i}-(n+1)\delta+\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=r_{1}r_{2}\dots r_{\ell-1}r_{\ell}r_{\ell-1}\dots r_{k-i}(\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,-i}-n\delta).

\displaystyle\langle h_{k-(i+1)},\Lambda_{k}+\xi_{k,-(i+2)}-(n+1)\delta+\alpha_{k-(i-1)}\rangle=2,

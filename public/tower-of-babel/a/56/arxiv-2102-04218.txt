1.
Introduction

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d>0
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
A
. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be an

I
-good filtration of
A
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{I}}
is a
sequence of ideals
{\mathcal{I}}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfying
I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
,
I_{n}I_{m}\subseteq I_{n+m}

and there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-k}
for all
n,m\geq 0
.
The associated graded ring
G({\mathcal{I}})=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n+1}
, the Rees ring
R(\mathcal{I})=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n}t^{n}
and the special fiber ring
F_{\mathfrak{m}}(I)=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/\mathfrak{m}I^{n}
are referred to as the blow-up algebras. When
{\mathcal{I}}
is the
I
-adic filtration
\{I^{n}\}
, we write
G(I)
and
R(I)
for the associated graded ring and the Rees ring respectively.

The structural properties of the local ring and the blow-up algebras are greatly connected to each other. The local ring
A
inherits many important properties, such as reducedness, normality, Cohen-Macaulayness, from the associated graded ring.
However, this is not true for Buchsbaum rings. See
[
10
, Example 4.10]
for an example where
G(I)
is Buchsbaum and
A
is not Buchsbaum. A finite
A
-module
M
is said to be a
Buchsbaum module
if the invariant
\ell_{A}(M/QM)-e_{0}(Q,M)
is independent of the choice of parameter ideals
Q
of
M
where
\ell_{A}(M/QM)
and
e_{0}(Q,M)
denote the length of
M/QM
and the multiplicity of
M
relative to
Q
respectively. The above condition is equivalent to saying that every system
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\dim M}
of parameters of
M
forms a weak
M
-sequence, that is

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M:_{M}x_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}
for
1\leq i\leq\dim M.
A module is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if it is Buchsbaum with
\ell_{A}(M/QM)-e_{0}(Q,M)=0
for every parameter ideal
Q
. Buchsbaum modules appear in abundance. In fact, it was proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
that for given integers
d>0
and
h_{i}\geq 0
for
0\leq i\leq d-1
, there exists a Buchsbaum local ring
A
with
\dim A=d
and
h_{i}=\ell_{A}(\h_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(A))
for
0\leq i\leq d-1
where
\h_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(A)
denote the
i
-th local cohomology module of
A
with support in
\mathfrak{m}
.

We are interested in the inheritance of ring theoretic properties while passing from
A
to the blow-up algebras. For example, suppose
A
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. If the reduction number of
{\mathcal{I}}
is at most one, then
G({\mathcal{I}})
is Cohen-Macaulay. In a Noetherian ring, it is true that

e_{1}({\mathcal{I}})-e_{1}(Q)\geq 2e_{0}({\mathcal{I}})-2\ell(A/I_{1})-\ell(I_{1}/(I_{2}+Q))

(1.1)

where
Q=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\subseteq I
is a minimal reduction of
{\mathcal{I}}
, see

[
23
, Theorem 2.4]
. When
A
is Cohen-Macaulay, we have

e_{1}({\mathcal{I}})\geq 2e_{0}({\mathcal{I}})-2\ell(A/I_{1})-\ell(I_{1}/(I_{2}+Q)).

In
[
4
]
and
[
14

, authors proved that the equality
e_{1}(I)=2e_{0}(I)-2\ell(A/I)-\ell(I/(I^{2}+Q))

In

f_{0}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{1}(Q)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/I)+\mu(I)-d+1

[

]

[

If

[

[

Theorem A

Let

3.1.
Sally module of filtration

To study blow-up algebras, Vasconcelos in
[

introduced the notion of Sally modules. Let
Q
be a minimal reduction of
{\mathcal{I}}
, then the Sally module of
{\mathcal{I}}
with respect to
Q

S_{Q}({\mathcal{I}})=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 1}I_{n+1}/I_{1}Q^{n}
, is a graded

-module. In Cohen-Macaulay local rings, Vasconcelos recovered many results using the method of Sally modules, such as Northcott’s inequality
e_{1}({\mathcal{I}})\geq e_{0}({\mathcal{I}})-\ell(A/I_{1})
and its boundary conditions.
The Sally module,
S=S_{Q}({\mathcal{I}})
can be seen as the cokernel of the following map of graded
T
-modules:

0\longrightarrow I_{1}T\longrightarrow{\mathcal{R}}_{+}(+1)=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{j\geq 1}I_{j}t^{j-1}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore,
S=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{j\geq 1}I_{j+1}/I_{1}Q^{j}
is a finitely generated graded
T
-module and
\dim_{T}S\leq d.
In
[
3

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that

T(-1)^{\mu}\xrightarrow{\phi}R(\mathcal{I})\setminus T\to S(-1)\to 0

T
-modules.

Proof.

Let
I_{1}=Q+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\mu})
. Consider the map

T(-1)^{\mu}\xrightarrow{\phi}R(\mathcal{I})/T

defined as
\phi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\mu})=\overline{\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{\mu}\alpha_{i}x_{i}t}\in R(\mathcal{I})/T.
Then

[\coker\phi]_{n}=I_{n}/(Q^{n}+Q^{n-1}I_{1})=I_{n}/Q^{n-1}I_{1}=S(-1)_{n}
for
n\geq 1
and

[\coker\phi]_{0}=0
. Therefore,

\coker\phi\cong S(-1)
as graded
T
-module.
∎

For an

Now we state and prove our main results.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local ring and
Q=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\subseteq I
a reduction of
{\mathcal{I}}
such that the conditions
(C_{1})
and
(C_{2})
are satisfied . Suppose the equality

and
\depth G({\mathcal{I}})=\depth A

By Proposition
7.1
,

. It remains to find the Hilbert coeficients. If

[

.
Since

) and (

Suppose

.
We also have that

,
e_{i}(QC)=e_{i}(Q)
for

e_{d}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{d}({\mathcal{I}}C)+(-1)^{d}\ell_{A}(W)

and
\ell_{A}(A/I_{1})=\ell_{A}(C/I_{1}C)

. Therefore, we may pass to the ring

and assume that

(C_{1})
,
(C_{2})
and
(C_{3})
are satisfied. By Corollary
3.6
,

e_{i}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{i-1}(Q)+e_{i}(Q)+e_{i-1}(S)

(7.9)

for

For

\displaystyle\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(S)\binom{n+d-2-i}{d-1-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{0}(S)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}(e_{i}(S)+e_{i-1}(S))\binom{n+d-1-i}{d-1-i}.

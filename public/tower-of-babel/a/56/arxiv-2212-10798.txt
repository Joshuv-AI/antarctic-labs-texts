On the Existence and Uniqueness of Ancient Rescaled Mean Curvature Flows
Letian Chen

Abstract
. We show existence of ancient solutions to the rescaled mean curvature flow starting from a given asymptotically conical self-expander. These are examples of mean curvature flows coming out of cones that are not self-similar. We also show a strong uniqueness theorem when the cone is generic and use it to classify mean curvature flows coming out of generic cones of small entropy in low dimensions.



1.
Introduction

A family of properly embedded hypersurface
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}\}_{s\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a
rescaled mean curvature flow
(RMCF) if it satisfies the following equation:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial s}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}}-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}.

Here
\mathbf{H}_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}}
is the mean curvature vector of
\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}
and
\perp
denotes the normal component. The RMCF equation comes from the
mean curvature flow
(MCF) equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{x}}{\partial t}\right)^{\perp}=\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{t}}.

via the change of variables

(1.3)

\displaystyle s=\log t\text{ and }\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}=t^{-1/2}\Sigma_{t}.

We emphasize that there are (at least) two different flows that are referred to as the RMCF, namely
Equation

1.1
and
Equation

1.1
with its right hand side replaced by
\mathbf{H}_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}}+\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}
. The latter flow arises naturally in the study of self-shrinkers. In this paper, however, we are only concerned with the former equation.

The static solutions to
Equation

1.1
are known as the
self-expanders
; that is, smooth embedded hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma}=\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}.

Equivalently,
\Sigma
is a self-expander if and only if
\{\sqrt{t}\Sigma\}_{t\in(0,\infty)}
is a solution to the MCF equation
Equation

1.2
. Self-expanders are modeled on MCFs coming out of (hyper)cones and serve as potential continuations of MCFs past conical singularities. Given a smooth cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a hypersurface
\Sigma
is
C^{k,\alpha}
-asymptotic
to
\mathcal{C}
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho\Sigma=\mathcal{C}\text{ in }C^{k,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}).

Asymptotically conical self-expanders can be thought naturally as MCFs coming out of their asymptotic cones in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner(\sqrt{t}\Sigma)=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\mathcal{C}.

As cones are singular at the origin, their evolution by MCF needs not be unique. In particular, there may be multiple distinct smooth self-expanders asymptotic to the same cone. This nonuniqueness was first confirmed by Angenent–Ilmanen–Chopp
[
2
]
, who showed numerically that there exist at least two self-expanders coming out of a sufficiently wide rotationally symmetric double cone. The argument was later made rigorous by
[
26
]
(cf.
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
). It turns out that there exist MCFs coming out of
\mathcal{C}
that are not self-similar, provided there are more than one smooth self-expanders asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. One class of examples of non-self-similar MCFs is given by
monotone Morse flow lines
connecting an unstable self-expander and a stable self-expander. These solutions are constructed in
[
3
]
using ideas from
[
13
]
and turn out to possess more regularity than a general MCF coming out of
\mathcal{C}
. We also refer to
[
15
, Section 5]
for a (simpler) low entropy version of the construction, and to
[
14
, Section 7]
for a related construction for an asymptotically conical self-shrinker.

Monotone Morse flow lines are a special case of
Morse flow lines
, which are RMCFs connecting two (smooth) self-expanders. More precisely,
\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}
is a Morse flow line if there exist smooth self-expanders
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma\text{ and }\lim_{s\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Gamma.

In particular, a Morse flow line is an ancient solution of
Equation

1.1
(that is, flows that are defined on
(-\infty,S)
). We shall also distinguish between the notions of a
smooth
Morse flow line, which is smooth on
(-\infty,\infty)
, and a
singular
Morse flow line (which must be described using a suitable notion of weak MCF - see
Section

2
). Even though the self-expanders
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
are smooth, the flow lines are not going to be smooth in general (see
[
15
, Proposition 5.7]
for an explicit example). The biggest difficulty in the study of singular flow lines is the possibility that they may not be canonically continued past a singularity due to the aforementioned nonuniqueness phenomenon with singular initial data. In
[
3
]
, we constructed a natural singular Morse flow line when the flow is expander mean convex (see also
4.8
).

In this article, we are interested in another special class of MCFs coming out of
\mathcal{C}
. Fix a smooth self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, we consider RMCFs
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}\}
on
(-\infty,0)
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}=\Sigma\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

Such a flow will be called a
tame ancient
RMCF (in general there could exist very wild ancient RMCFs that do not have a well-defined backward limit or even arise as MCFs out of a cone). It is easy to see that a tame ancient RMCF gives rise to a MCF coming out of
\mathcal{C}
in view of
Equation

1.3
. The converse is, in general, not true - see
Section

5
for some related discussion.

Our first result is an existence theorem for tame ancient RMCFs when
\Sigma
is unstable. In particular, this gives examples of many non-self-similar flows coming out of a cone with more than one asymptotically conical self-expanders (see
Section

2
for terminologies).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth cone and suppose
\Sigma
is an unstable smooth self-expander of index
I
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, then there exists an
I
-parameter family of distinct tame ancient RMCFs
\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}\}_{s\in(-\infty,0]}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}=\Sigma\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

In particular, there exists an
I
-parameter family of smooth MCFs coming out of
\mathcal{C}
.

1.1
is a non-compact analogue of the general existence theorem of ancient gradient flows of certain elliptic functionals proven by Choi–Mantoulidis
[
20
]
. The method is also adapted to self-shrinkers
[
14
, Section 6]
. We also mention the recent work of Sun–Wang
[
36
]
where they constructed an
I
-parameter family of translators asymptotic to
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
for a self-shrinker
\Sigma
of index
I
. The space of solutions will correspond to the space of eigenfunctions of negative eigenvalues.

If, in addition, the cone
\mathcal{C}
is generic, in the sense that any self-expander asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
has nontrivial Jacobi field, we also have the following strong uniqueness result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a generic smooth cone. Suppose
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}\}_{s\in(-\infty,0)}
is a smooth RMCF such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}=\Sigma\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

for some smooth self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\Sigma
is unstable and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is either the static flow of
\Sigma
or coincides with one of the tame ancient RMCFs constructed from
1.1
.

The proof of
1.2
relies on the Merle–Zaag ODE lemma
A.1
, which has found much recent success in various classification problems for ancient geometric flows, see for example
[
1
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
, and
[
22
]
. The genericity of
\mathcal{C}
is used to rule out the scenario in
A.1
where the neutral mode (i.e., nontrivial Jacobi fields) become dominant as
s\to-\infty
. We suspect that
1.2
is not true without the genericity assumption. For instance, if there exists a 1-parameter family of weakly stable self-expanders
\{\Sigma^{a}\}_{a\in(0,1)}
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, it is possible that there exists a Morse flow line between
\Sigma^{a_{1}}
and
\Sigma^{a_{2}}
, which will violate both conclusions of the theorem.

Since every Morse flow line is a tame ancient RMCF, we immediately have the following consequence of
1.2

Corollary 1.3
.

Every Morse flow line starting from a smooth self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to a generic cone agrees with one of the tame ancient RMCF starting from
\Sigma
constructed in
1.1
.

The above corollary gives a reasonably complete picture of the flow lines asymptotic to a generic cone. We point out that the converse does not hold, as being a tame ancient flow tells nothing about the forward limit
\Gamma
. In fact, the forward limit
\Gamma
appears to be much more complicated to analyze. One particular case in which we can deduce regularity of
\Gamma
is when
\mathcal{C}
is generic and
\Sigma
has index 1. Indeed, by
1.3
, any Morse flow line belongs to the 1-parameter family of tame ancient RMCF starting from
\Sigma
corresponding to its first eigenfunction. This implies that the flow actually lies on one side of
\Sigma
, and thus
\Gamma
is stable (see
[
13
, Proposition 5.1]
). In particular,
\Gamma
has a singular set of codimension at least 7 and is smooth in low dimensions.

Recall that a tame ancient RMCF starting from an asymptotically conical self-expander
\Sigma
gives rise to a MCF starting from its asymptotic cone. It is therefore natural to ask when the same conclusion of
1.2
holds for a general MCF coming out of the cone.
This problem turns out to be more subtle as a MCF coming out of the cone is not necessarily a tame ancient RMCF. The failure of this correspondence is essentially the failure of the uniqueness of tangent flows, which, in turn, is due to the lack of regularity of the backward limit. To elaborate, given a MCF,
\mathcal{M}
, coming out of
\mathcal{C}
, the corresponding RMCF,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
(via
Equation

1.3
), only satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{s_{i}}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma,

for some subsequence
s_{i}\to-\infty
and self-expander
\Sigma
(not necessarily smooth - see
5.3
). To upgrade the subsequential convergence to full convergence, the standard tool is the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality
[
32
]
(cf.
[
31
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
19
]
for various applications in MCF). In our case, we will prove a Łojasiewicz inequality when the cone is generic.

Theorem 1.4

(Łojasiewicz inequality for generic cones,
5.4
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth self-expander asymptotic to a generic cone
\mathcal{C}
. There is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Sigma)
such that the following holds: suppose
v\in C^{2,\alpha}\cap W^{2}(\Sigma)
satisfies
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
, then

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}(v)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2},

where
\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}
is the Euler-Lagrange operator associated to the relative expander entropy
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
.

Remark
.

Without the genericity assumption, one instead expects a constant
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}(v)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|^{1-\gamma/2},

which is the usual Łojasiewicz inequality for
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
proved by Park–Wang
[
30
]
(here, for technical reasons, the two relative expander entropies
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
and
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
are defined differently - see
Section

4
). This is, of course, also enough to deduce the uniqueness of tangent. However, as
1.2
requires genericity anyways, we have opted to prove the inequality in the generic case separately. We also emphasize that the proof of the theorem makes no use of the classical Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality from
[
32
]
and, therefore, also does not rely on the analyticity of the relative expander functional (which is indeed true). See
[
25
]
for some related discussion on analyticity vs. Morse-Bott conditions.

The smoothness of
\Sigma
is, unfortunately, essential in the above theorem. For this reason we only have a partial converse in the MCF case:

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
2\leq n\leq 6
and
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a generic cone. Suppose
\mathcal{M}=\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in(0,T)}
is a smooth MCF such that the corresponding RMCF satisfies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\tilde{\Sigma}_{s_{i}}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma

for some sequence
s_{i}\to-\infty
and some
smooth
self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{M}
agrees with one of the tame ancient RMCF starting from
\Sigma
constructed in
1.1
.

Remark
.

Here the dimension restriction is due to the trapping phenomenon, i.e. we need the flow
\mathcal{M}
to be trapped between two asymptotically conical self-expanders, which is only known in low dimensions
[
14
, Proposition 8.21]
.

Remark
.

The regularity of the subsequential limit is in general hard to check, but there are natural geometric conditions (i.e. entropy bounds) that force the limit to be smooth. In these cases,
1.5
results in a classification of MCFs coming out of generic cones of low entropy - see
5.6
and
5.7
.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In
Section

3
we prove
1.1
. In
Section

4
we first define a version of the relative expander entropy which gives the equation
Equation

1.1
a gradient flow structure, and then carry out the spectral analysis to prove
1.2
. In
Section

5
we study MCFs coming out of cones and prove the Łojasiewicz inequality
5.4
. We then prove a uniqueness of tangent flows result for generic cones and combine it with
1.2
to show
1.5
. Finally, we give some applications of
1.5
in the low entropy case.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

By a
(hyper)cone
we mean a set
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
that is invariant under dilations, i.e.
\rho\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}
for all
\rho>0
. We say
\mathcal{C}
is smooth if it is smooth away from the origin. We say a hypersurface
\Sigma
is asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
if it is smoothly asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, in view of
Equation

1.5
.

Given a (smooth) hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and any function
v:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
, let
\Sigma_{v}
be the normal graph of
v
over
\Sigma
, i.e.
\Sigma_{v}=\mathbf{f}_{v}(\Sigma)
where

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{v}(p)=\mathbf{x}(p)+v(p)\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}(p),\;\;p\in\Sigma.

The time variable
t
is always used in the unrescaled setting (i.e. MCF), and
s
is always used in the rescaled setting (i.e. RMCF).

2.2.
Integral Brakke flow

We will be using notations from geometric measure theory below, and we refer to
[
33
]
and
[
27
]
for the relevant definitions. Let
V(\mu)
denote the associated integral varifold for an integral
n
-rectifiable Radon measure
\mu
. Let
\mathbf{H}
be the generalized mean curvature vector of
V(\mu)
given by the first variation formula: for any compactly supported
C^{1}
vector field,

\displaystyle\int\Div_{V(\mu)}Xd\mu=-\int\mathbf{H}\cdot Xd\mu.

Given an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, by an
integral
n
-Brakke flow
in
U
we mean a family of integral
n
-rectifiable Radon measures
\mathcal{M}=\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in I}
such that:

(a)

For a.e.
t\in I
,
V(\mu)
has locally bounded first variation and its generalized mean curvature vector
H
is orthogonal to the approximating tangent space of
V(\mu)

x
-a.e.

(b)

For any bounded interval
[a,b]\subset I
and compact set
K\subset U
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\int_{K}(1+\left|\mathbf{H}\right|^{2})d\mu_{t}dt<\infty.

(c)

For
[a,b]\subset I
and every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(U\times[a,b],\mathbb{R}^{+})
,

\displaystyle\int\phi d\mu_{b}-\int\phi d\mu_{a}\leq\int_{a}^{b}\int\left(-\phi\left|\mathbf{H}\right|^{2}+\mathbf{H}\cdot\nabla\phi+\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right)d\mu_{t}dt.

Throughout the article, a Brakke flow is assumed to be integral, cyclic, and unit-regular (see eg.
[
38
]
). This ensures that there is no sudden loss of mass along the flow. Any Brakke flow produced by elliptic regularization
[
27
]
will automatically have these properties.

Under the rescaling
s=\log t
and
\tilde{\mu}_{s}=\mu_{t}\circ t^{\frac{1}{2}}
, a Brakke flow becomes a rescaled Brakke flow, which satisfies the same conditions as above, with condition (c) replaced by

(c’)

For
[a,b]\subset I
and every
\phi\in C_{c}^{1}(U\times[a,b],\mathbb{R}^{+})
,

\displaystyle\int\phi d\mu_{b}-\int\phi d\mu_{a}\leq\int_{a}^{b}\int\left(-\phi H\cdot(-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}+\mathbf{H})+(-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}+\mathbf{H})\cdot\nabla\phi+\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right)d\mu_{t}dt.

Of course, a smooth rescaled Brakke flow satisfies the RMCF equation,
Equation

1.1
.

2.3.
Function Spaces

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth hypersurface. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
we define the Hölder seminorm

\displaystyle[f]_{\alpha;\Sigma}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\neq q\in\Sigma\\
q\in B_{\delta}^{\Sigma}(p)\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|f(p)-f(q)\right|}{d_{\Sigma}(p,q)^{\alpha}},

where
\delta
is such that
B_{\delta}^{\Sigma}(p)
is strictly geodesically convex. For
k
an integer, define
C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma)
to be the Hölder space with norm

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sup_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}f\right|+[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{k}f]_{\alpha;\Sigma}.

The parabolic Hölder seminorm is defined similarly as

\displaystyle[f]_{\alpha;\Sigma\times I}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(p,t_{1})\neq(q,t_{2})\in\Sigma\times I\\
q\in B_{\delta}^{\Sigma}(p)\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|f(p)-f(q)\right|}{d_{\Sigma}(p,q)^{\alpha}+\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|^{\alpha/2}}.

The corresponding parabolic Hölder space
C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times I)
consists of functions
f
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times I)}=\sum_{i+2j\leq k}\sup_{\Sigma\times I}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\nabla_{t}^{j}f\right|+\sum_{i+2j=k}[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\nabla_{t}^{j}f]_{\alpha;\Sigma\times I}.

We shall write
C^{k,\alpha}=C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma)
and
C^{k,\alpha}_{P}=C^{k,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times(-\infty,0])
when
\Sigma
is clear from context.

Finally we define the weighted Sobolev space. For an integer
k
we say
f\in W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}(\Sigma)
if

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}(\Sigma)}=\left(\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}f\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Moreover, we say a map
f(x,s)\in W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}(\Sigma\times(-\infty,0])
if
f(\cdot,t)\in W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}(\Sigma)
and
f(x,\cdot)\in L^{2}((-\infty,0))
. In this article we are only interested in the weight
e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}
, so we will write
W^{k}=W^{k}_{\frac{1}{4}}
and further write
W^{0}=W
. The square bracket
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
will denote the standard inner product in
W
, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle=\int_{\Sigma}fge^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}},

whereas
\cdot
will denote the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

2.4.
Self-expanders

We recall some basic facts about self-expanders from
[
10
]
and
[
8
]
. It is useful to use the following variational characterization of self-expanders: A hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a self-expander if it is a (formal) critical point of the functional

(2.1)

\displaystyle E[\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

The Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional
Equation

2.1
is precisely
Equation

1.4
. A simple consequence of equation
Equation

1.4
is that the second fundamental form
A_{\Sigma}
satisfies
\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|=O(\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{-1})
.

Given a self-expander
\Sigma
and a compactly supported variation
\Phi_{t}(\Sigma)
such that
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi_{t}(\Sigma)=X
, the second variation formula of the expander functional
Equation

2.1
is (see eg.
[
10
, Proposition 4.2]
):

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}E[\Phi_{s}(\Sigma)]=-\int_{\Sigma}(X\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma})L_{\Sigma}(X\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma})e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Here
L_{\Sigma}
is the
stability operator
of
\Sigma
given by

\displaystyle L_{\Sigma}=\Delta_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}+\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

For a connected self-expander
\Sigma
, the
Morse index
of
\Sigma
,
\mathrm{ind}(\Sigma)
, is the biggest dimension of a linear subspace
V\subset C_{0}^{2}(\Sigma)
such that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}vL_{\Sigma}ve^{\frac{\left|x\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}<0\text{ for all }v\in V\setminus\{0\}.

A number
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of
-L_{\Sigma}
if there exists
f\in W^{2}(\Sigma)
such that
-L_{\Sigma}f=\mu f
. By works of Bernstein–Wang
[
8
]
, when
\Sigma
is smooth,
L_{\Sigma}
has a discrete spectrum and we can therefore order the eigenvalues of
-L_{\Sigma}
. It follows that the index of
\Sigma
is equal to the number of negative eigenvalues of
-L_{\Sigma}
. Let
\mathrm{nul}(\Sigma)
denote the
nullity
of the operator
-L_{\Sigma}
, i.e. the multiplicity of eigenvalue 0.
f\in W^{2}
is called a
Jacobi field
if
f\in\ker L_{\Sigma}
.

One of the features of self-expanders is that the eigenfunctions have very fast exponential decay to offset the large exponential weight (this is in contrast with the self-shrinker case where one has to be very careful about the decay rate). Indeed, by
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{\frac{\beta}{2}\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}f\right\rVert_{C^{k,\alpha}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0))}<\infty

for any
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
and
R
large depending on
\Sigma
. Moreover, the first eigenfunction
\phi_{1}
corresponding to the lowest eigenvalue of
-L_{\Sigma}
has the fastest decay rate of
(1+\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)^{-\frac{1}{2}(n-1-2\lambda_{1})}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}
by
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
.

In our study, we will often be working with normal graphs over a given self-expander
\Sigma
. Suppose
v\in C^{2}(\Sigma)
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2}}<\varepsilon
is such that
\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{x}|_{\Sigma}+v\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}
is an embedding, then it follows from a computation of Bernstein and Wang
[
12
, Proposition A.1]
that

(2.3)

\displaystyle H_{\Sigma^{\mathbf{h}}}+\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma^{\mathbf{h}}}=-L_{\Sigma}v+Q(v,\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma}v),

where
\Sigma^{\mathbf{h}}=\mathbf{h}(\Sigma)
,
H
is the scalar mean curvature and
Q
is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial with bounded coefficients.
Q
can be written as

(2.4)

\displaystyle Q(s,\rho,d,T)=a(s,\rho,d,T)\cdot d+b(s,d,T)s,

where
a,b
are homogeneous polynomial of degree 1 with bounded coefficients. Moreover,
a
and
b
satisfy the estimates

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2+j-\ell}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\nabla_{s}^{j}\nabla_{d}^{k}\nabla_{T}^{\ell}b(s,d,T)\right|\leq C(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{-1}\left|s\right|+\left|d\right|+\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\left|T\right|),

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{1+j-\ell}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{i}\nabla_{s}^{j}\nabla_{d}^{k}\nabla_{T}^{\ell}a(s,\rho,d,T)\right|\leq C(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{-1}\left|s\right|+\left|d\right|+\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\left|T\right|).

This follows from the estimates that, on a self-expanding end,
\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|=O(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{-1})
and
\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}A_{\Sigma}\right|=O(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{-2})
. See
[
14
, Lemma 3.6]
for a proof in the self-shrinker case, which carries almost identically to our case (see also
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
).

3.
Existence of tame ancient flows

In this section we establish the existence of tame ancient RMCFs coming out of an unstable, asymptotically conical self-expander. Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth cone and fix a smooth self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{\tilde{\mu}_{s}\}_{s\in(-\infty,0)}
be a rescaled Brakke flow such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\tilde{\Sigma}_{s}=\Sigma\text{ in }C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

We first establish some general properties satisfied by such flows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be as above. There is
R=R(\Sigma)>0
such that
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\setminus B_{R}(0)
is a smooth RMCF. Moreover,
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\setminus B_{2R}(0)
can be written as a normal graph of
v
over an open set
V\subset\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)
such that

\displaystyle\left|v(p,s)\right|<C\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{-1}\text{ for }p\in\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)

Proof.

The distance estimate follows from using small balls as barriers. See
[
15
, Proposition A.1]
or
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
. This can then be upgraded to smoothness using standard pseudolocality arguments. See
[
15
, Proposition A.2]
or
[
7
, Proposition 4.4]
.
∎

The key fact we need to use in the subsequent analysis is that all such RMCF, up to a time translation, can be written as an entire normal graph over
\Sigma
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Given
\delta>0
, there is
s_{\delta}
such that
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
can be written as a normal graph of a smooth function
v
over
\Sigma
on
(-\infty,s_{\varepsilon})
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}}<\varepsilon\text{ for all }s<s_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Since the varifold convergence
Equation

3.1
has multiplicity 1, we may apply the Brakke regularity theorem (
[
5
]
or
[
37
]
) to conclude that the convergence is in fact smooth on compact sets. Moreover,
3.1
shows that there exists
R=R(\Sigma)
such that
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{s}\setminus B_{2R}(0)
can be written as a normal graph
v
over
\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v(p,s)\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0))}\leq C\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{-1}.

Now given
\delta
we choose
R
sufficiently large so that
CR^{-1}<\varepsilon
and then
s_{\varepsilon}
sufficiently negative such that
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{s}\cap B_{R}(0)
is a normal graph over
\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0)
with
C^{2,\alpha}
norm bounded by
\varepsilon
as well. A similar argument applied to
\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}
using the fact that
v
satisfies the graphical RMCF equation gives the full parabolic estimate (up to decreasing
s_{\varepsilon}
).
∎

Remark
.

Arguing as in
[
14
, Lemma 7.18]
, it is possible to obtain a weighted estimate as well, but in the self-expander case, we do not need to work with weighted Hölder space in the following due to the rapid decay of eigenfunctions.

Now suppose further that
\Sigma
is unstable. Since
\Sigma
has a discrete spectrum,
\Sigma
has finite index which we will denote by
I
. By considering the Rayleigh quotient

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}}{\int_{\Sigma}\left|v\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}}

for
v\in C^{2,\alpha}\cap W^{2}(\Sigma)\setminus\{0\}
, it is easy to see that the first eigenvalue is indeed achieved.

We establish, using PDE method similar to
[
20
]
, the existence of an
I
-parameter family of ancient solutions to the RMCF starting from
\Sigma
. Each one of these solutions will correspond to a MCF coming out of
\mathcal{C}
that is not self-similar.

We work with the RMCF equation
Equation

1.1
. The tame ancient flows will be constructed as limits of suitable perturbations of the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
by its eigenfunctions. To this end, we fix an orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset W
consisting of eigenfunctions of
-L_{\Sigma}
, where
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{I}
correspond to the negative eigenvalues,
\phi_{I+1},\ldots,\phi_{I+K}
correspond to the 0 eigenvalue and
\phi_{I+K+1},\ldots
correspond to positive eigenvalues. Define the map
\tau_{-}:\mathbb{R}^{I}\to W(\Sigma\times[-\infty,0))
by

\displaystyle\tau_{-}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}\phi_{i}(x).

Let
\Pi_{-}:W\to W
be the projection onto the negative eigenspace, i.e.

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\langle v,\phi_{i}\right\rangle\phi_{i}(x).

Using
Equation

2.3
,
Equation

1.1
reduces to the following nonlinear problem:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v=L_{\Sigma}v+Q(v,\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma}v).

To solve this nonlinear PDE, we first consider the inhomogeneous linear PDE:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v=L_{\Sigma}v+h(p,s),\;\;(p,s)\in\Sigma\times(-\infty,0),

where
h(p,s)
is a smooth function. We will assume in addition that
h(p,s)
is exponentially decaying in time, i.e. there exists some
\delta>0
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|e^{-\delta s}\left\lVert h(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{W}\right|^{2}ds<\infty.

Using separation of variables, we can write a solution to
Equation

3.3
as

\displaystyle v(p,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}v_{i}(p)\phi_{i}(s),

where
v_{i}
formally solves the ODE

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\prime}(s)=-\lambda_{i}v_{i}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h_{j}(s)\phi_{j}(p),

where
h_{j}(s)=\left\langle h(\cdot,s),\phi_{j}\right\rangle
. This allows us to establish the existence of the
L^{2}
-solutions to
Equation

3.3
.

Proposition 3.3

(cf. Lemma 3.1 of
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
and suppose
h
satisfies
Equation

3.4
. There exists a unique solution
v
to
Equation

3.3
such that
\Pi_{-}(v(\cdot,0))=\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}\phi_{i}
and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|e^{-\delta^{\prime}s}\left\lVert v(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{W^{1}}\right|^{2}ds<\infty,

for some
0<\delta^{\prime}<\min\{\delta,-\mu_{I}\}
.
v
can be written as

\displaystyle v(p,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}v_{i}(s)\phi_{i}(p),

where

\displaystyle v_{i}(s)=\begin{cases}a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}-\int_{s}^{0}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma&i=1,\ldots,I,\\
\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma&i=I+1,I+2,\ldots\end{cases}.

Here
h_{i}(s)=\left\langle h(p,s),\phi_{i}(x)\right\rangle
. Additionally,
v
satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle e^{-\delta^{\prime}s}\left\lVert v(\cdot,s)-\tau_{-}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})\right\rVert_{W}\leq C\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|e^{-\delta\sigma}\left\lVert h(\cdot,\sigma)\right\rVert_{W}\right|^{2}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
s<0
.

Proof.

Let us check that
v
solves
Equation

3.3
. Indeed, differentiating under the integral sign gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left[-\lambda_{i}a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}+h_{i}(s)+\lambda_{i}\int_{s}^{0}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma\right]\phi_{i}

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}\left[h_{i}(s)-\lambda_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma\right]\phi_{i}

Using
h(p,s)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h_{i}(s)\phi_{i}(p)
and
\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}=-L_{\Sigma}\phi_{i}
we get that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v(s)=

\displaystyle\phantom{{}+{}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}L_{\Sigma}\phi_{i}\left(a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}-\int_{s}^{0}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=I+1}^{\infty}L_{\Sigma}\phi_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma-s)}h_{i}(\sigma)d\sigma+h(\cdot,s).

Evidently this is
Equation

3.3
. The fact that
\Pi_{-}(v(\cdot,0))=\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}\phi_{i}
is a straightforward computation. The uniqueness of the solution follows from the uniqueness of the linear parabolic equation
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v=L_{\Sigma}v
(with 0 initial data).
Equation

3.5
follows from the assumption on
h
and the fact that, if
\lambda_{i}<0
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-2\delta^{\prime}s-2\lambda_{i}s}ds<\infty

as long as
\delta^{\prime}<-\lambda_{i}
. Finally the last estimates follow from Hölder’s inequality as the subtraction kills the
a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}
terms.
∎

We now follow the ideas of
[
10
]
to establish higher regularity of the solutions obtained above. First notice that for a given initial data
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})
,
\tau_{-}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})
solves the linear homogeneous equation
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}v=L_{\Sigma}v
, hence by replacing
v
by
v-\tau_{-}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})
we will WLOG assume that
v
is a solution to
Equation

3.3
with

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}(v(\cdot,0))=0.

We will prove a Hölder estimate of the following type:

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
h\in C^{0,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times(-\infty,0])
. Let
v
be the solution to
Equation

3.3
with
Equation

3.6
constructed in
3.3
, then for
s<0
,

\displaystyle[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}v]_{\alpha;\Sigma\times[s-1,s]}+\sup_{\Sigma\times[s-1,s]}\left(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|+\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}v\right|\right)\leq C\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times(-\infty,0])}.

Remark
.

We emphasize that, in the self-expander case, all eigenfunctions are decreasing rapidly in the spatial variables by
Equation

2.2
, and therefore we do not need to invoke any weighted Schauder estimates. This is in contrast to the self-shrinker case in
[
14
]
as the eigenfunctions of the stability operator of self-shrinkers can potentially have polynomial growth in space, for which weighted estimates are necessary.

Proof.

WLOG we assume
s=0
. In this proof it is often convenient to work with the original MCF. To this end let
\Sigma_{t}=\sqrt{t}\Sigma
denote the MCF associated to the self-expander
\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is asymptotically conical, we can find
R>0
such that, for any
p\in\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)
and
t\in[e^{-1},1]
,
\Sigma_{t}\cap B_{2}(p)
can be parametrized as a normal graph over some open subset of
T_{p}\Sigma
with small
C^{3}
norm, i.e. there exists
\psi_{p}:\Omega\times[e^{-1},1]\subset T_{p}\Sigma\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi_{p}(0,1)=0
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{3}\left|\nabla^{i}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi_{p}\right|+\sum_{i=0}^{1}\left|\partial_{t}\nabla^{i}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi_{p}\right|\leq\varepsilon,

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{p}(x,t)=\mathbf{x}(p)+\mathbf{x}(x)+\psi_{p}(x,t)\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}(p)

parametrizes
\Sigma_{t}\cap B_{2}(p)
,
t\in[e^{-1},1]
.

We now fix
p\in\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)
and consider, on
\Omega\times[e^{-1},1]

\displaystyle w(x,t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Psi_{p}(x,t),\log t).

By
Equation

3.3
and the chain rule we see that
w
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}w-\left|A_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}w=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Psi_{p}(x,t),\log t),

Since
h\in C_{P}^{0,\alpha}
,
\left|\Psi_{p}(x,t)\right|<2\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|
, and
t\in[e^{-1},1]
which is compact, it follows that the right hand side
g_{p}=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}h(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Psi_{p}(x,t),\log t)
is also Hölder continuous in spacetime with

\displaystyle\left|g_{p}(x,t_{1})-g_{p}(y,t_{2})\right|\leq C\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}}(\left|x-y\right|^{\alpha}+\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}).

We may now invoke interior Schauder estimates (see eg.
[
28
]
) to conclude that (note that
\left|A_{\Sigma_{t}}\right|^{2}\leq C\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{-2}
on
[e^{-1},1]

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega\times[e^{-1},1]}\left(\left|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w\right|+\left|\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}w\right|\right)+[\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}w]_{\alpha;\Omega\times[e^{-1},1]}\leq C\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times(-\infty,0])}.

Rescaling back and using
Equation

3.7
, we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[-1,0]}\left(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v(p,s)\right|+\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}v(p,s)\right|\right)+[\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}v]_{\alpha;\Omega\times[-1,0]}\leq C\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}}.

As
p\in\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0)
is arbitrary, we see the desired estimates hold on
(\Sigma\setminus B_{R}(0))\times[-1,0]
. The estimates on
\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0)
follows from standard parabolic Schauder estimates for compact domains.
∎

It is standard to bootstrap
3.4
to obtain higher order Schauder estimates, and a simple modification of the proof gives the corresponding estimates for
\partial_{s}v
. These lead to the following exponentially weighted variant, which follows easily from
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose that there is
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{s<0}e^{-\delta s}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}<\infty.

Let
v
be the solution to
Equation

3.3
with
Equation

3.6
constructed in
3.3
. Then for every

\displaystyle e^{-\delta^{\prime}s}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}\leq C\sup_{\sigma<0}e^{-\delta\sigma}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[\sigma-1,\sigma])}.

We are now in position to prove the existence and uniqueness theorem for
Equation

3.2
.
1.1
follows easily from the following.

Theorem 3.6

(cf. Theorem 3.3 of
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\delta_{0}\in(0,-\lambda_{I})
. For every sufficiently large

such that the following holds: for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})\in B_{\varepsilon}^{I}(0)

\Pi_{-}v(\cdot,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}\phi_{i}

(3.8)

\displaystyle e^{-\delta_{0}s}\left\lVert v-\tau_{-}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}\leq\beta\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}^{2}

.

Proof.

In this proof we will also treat the nonlinear term
Q
as a function of
s
and
p\in\Sigma
, and will write
Q(p,s)
whenever we do so. Let us fix
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})\in B_{\varepsilon}^{I}(0)

\displaystyle C^{*}=

\displaystyle\{v:\Sigma\times(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}\mid

\displaystyle e^{-\delta_{0}s}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}<\infty\text{ for all }s\leq 0\text{, and }\Pi_{-}(v(\cdot,0))=\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}\phi_{i}\}.

4.
Relative expander entropy and uniqueness

In this section we define a version of the relative expander entropy adapted to a normal graph, which will be suitable in our setting, and prove several useful properties. We will use the relative expander entropy to deduce the uniqueness theorem,
1.2
. Throughout the section, fix a smooth cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and a self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\chi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth cutoff functions supported on
B_{R+2}(0)
and identically 1 on
B_{R}(0)
. For any function
v\in C^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Sigma)
, define the
relative expander entropy
of
\Sigma_{v}
to be

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]=\int_{\Sigma_{v}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\int_{\Sigma}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

For a general function
v\in W^{1}\cap C^{2,\alpha}(\Sigma)
, define
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]
by

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]=\lim_{R\to\infty}E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{\chi_{R}v},\Sigma]

whenever the limit exists.

We note importantly that this notion of relative expander entropy is slightly different from the usual definition of the relative entropy from
[
12
]
and
[
23
]
. We will denote by
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
the quantity defined by Bernstein–Wang;
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
is given by the formula

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}]=\lim_{R\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{1}\cap B_{R}(0)}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\int_{\Sigma_{2}\cap B_{R}(0)}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right),

for two hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, whenever the limit is defined (possibly
\infty
). In particular, they showed in
[
12
]
that when
\Sigma_{1}
is a hypersurface trapped between two self-expanders asymptotic to the same cone
\mathcal{C}
, then
E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{1},\Gamma]
is well-defined (possibly
\infty
, but not
-\infty
) for any self-expander
\Gamma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
. Because of this,
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
is the natural and more suitable quantity to study in the trapped case (and, in fact,
E_{\mathrm{rel}}=E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
in the trapped case - see
5.1
). Unfortunately, in order for a graph
\Sigma_{v}
to be trapped,
v
needs to have a very good spatial decay near infinity:

\displaystyle v(p)=O(\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{-n-1}e^{-\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{2}}{4}}).

As we are working with normal graphs that a priori do not have the sharp decay (rather only an energy bound), we will have to use
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
instead of
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
for now. See
Section

5
for further discussion on the trapping assumption and the difference between
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
and
E^{*}_{\mathrm{rel}}
.

We will show that
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
is well-defined if the function
v
has small
C^{2,\alpha}
norm.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
v\in C^{2,\alpha}_{0}(\Sigma)
function. There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Sigma)

Proof.

By Huisken’s monotonicity formula, any singularity of the flow must have entropy less than

∎

Remark
.

We suspect that the entropy bound can be relaxed, with a suitable surgery procedure, to
\lambda[\mathcal{C}]<\lambda[\mathbb{S}\times\mathbb{R}]+\delta

In a different direction, if we know that the flow is expander mean convex at some time, it is also possible to extend an ancient RMCF to a Morse flow line. Recall that a hypersurface
\Sigma
is expander mean convex if

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma}(p)+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}(p)\cdot\mathbf{x}(p)>0.

In view of

, a graphical RMCF over a self-expander

is expander mean convex if and only if

; that is, the RMCF lies on one side of
\Sigma

, we extended the notion of expander mean convexity past singularities. In particular, we showed that a smooth expander mean convex RMCF can be extended in some appropriate weak sense to stay expander mean convex in all future time, regardless of singularities, and that the extended flow is a rescaled Brakke flow. Moreover, the forward limit of such a flow is always a stable self-expander. This limit is unique due to expander mean convexity, and is smooth in low dimensions.

Using a slight modification of the argument used in

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

Since

The prototypical example of an expander mean convex RMCF is the ancient RMCF corresponding to

We end the section by recording the following uniqueness theorem for expander mean convex RMCFs asymptotic to a generic cone, which might be of independent interest.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is generic, then, up to time translation, there is a unique solution
v
to
Equation

3.2
such that
v>0
on
(-\infty,0)

Proof.

Let
v>0
be a positive solution to
Equation

3.2

(-\infty,0)
. As
\mathcal{C}
is generic, we can follow the spectral analysis above to get the sharp asymptotic decay

(4.15)

\displaystyle\delta(s)\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{I_{*}}s}.

We claim that
\lambda_{I_{*}}=\lambda_{1}
. This follows from the fact that

\displaystyle V_{\neq\lambda_{I_{*}}}(s)\leq C\delta(s)V_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}

when
s\leq s_{0}
for some
s_{0}<0
(indeed if the above were true, it will violate the sharpness of the estimate
Equation

4.15

implies that

\displaystyle-\min\{0,\Pi_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}v\}\leq\left|\Pi_{\neq\lambda_{I_{*}}}v\right|\implies\left\lVert-\min\{0,\Pi_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}v\}\right\rVert\leq C\delta(s)V_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}

Now let
h^{s}=V_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}^{-1}(s)\Pi_{=\lambda_{I_{*}}}v(\cdot,s)
, then as

converges to an
\lambda_{I_{*}}
-eigenfunction

\displaystyle\left\lVert-\min\{0,h\}\right\rVert_{W}=0,

and so

. By standard spectral theory the only eigenfunction that does not change sign corresponds to the lowest eigenvalue. Hence
\lambda_{I_{*}}=\lambda_{1}
. In particular, for any positive solution

\alpha_{1}\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}e^{s\lambda_{1}}v(\cdot,s)=\alpha_{1}\phi_{1}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is generic, we can apply the strong uniqueness theorem

to conclude that

for some

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}s}\phi_{i}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}\leq e^{\delta_{0}s}\beta\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}^{2}

where

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert e^{s\lambda_{1}}v-\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}e^{(-\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{1})s}\phi_{i}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}_{P}(\Sigma\times[s-1,s])}\leq e^{\delta_{0}+\lambda_{1}s}\beta\sum_{i=1}^{I}a_{i}^{2}

Since
v(\cdot,s)\leq Ce^{-\lambda_{1}s}
and
\delta_{0}+\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{I}\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{i}
for all

∎

Remark
.

By modifying a beautiful iteration argument of Chodosh–Choi–Mantoulidis–Schulze

5.
Mean curvature flows

All of our analysis so far has been on the level of RMCF, and essentially only relies on the fact that any such flow, up to a time translation, is an entire graph over the self-expander
\Sigma
. In this section, we study mean curvature flows coming out of cones and indicate when such a flow fits into our discussion above. We will work in low dimensions, i.e.
2\leq n\leq 6
., where the structure theory of self-expanders is best known, thanks to a series of works of Bernstein and Wang
[
10
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
.

We say an asymptotically conical hypersurface
\Gamma
is
trapped
if there exist two self-expanders
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
such that there is some radius
R_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Gamma\setminus B_{R_{0}}(0)\subset\Omega_{1}^{+}\cap\Omega_{2}^{-},

where
\Omega_{1}^{\pm},\Omega_{2}^{\pm}
denote the connected components of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\Sigma_{1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\Sigma_{2}
respectively, oriented in a way such that
\Omega_{1}^{+}\subset\Omega_{2}^{+}
. In the special case
\Gamma=\Sigma_{v}
, using the asymptotic structure of self-expanders at infinity
[
4
]
, we deduce that
v
satisfies the sharp decay estimates

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left|v(p)\right|\leq C\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{-n-1}e^{-\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|^{2}}{4}}\text{ when }\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|>R_{0}

First of all, under the trapping assumption, we can prove that the relative expander entropy
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
defined in
Section

4
indeed coincides with the usual notion
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
from
[
12
]
. This seemingly innocent fact has to do with the failure of the normal graph map
\mathbf{f}_{v}
being bijective in the annulus
B_{R+2}(0)\setminus B_{R}(0)
. Due to the large weight
e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}
, this difference cannot be killed unless the function
v
has a very good decay.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
v:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is a
W^{1}
function such that
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]<\infty
. If
\Sigma_{v}
is trapped between two self-expanders
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
then

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]=E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]<\infty.

Proof.

We fix
R
sufficiently large and write
\chi_{R}=\chi
for simplicity. Let
A_{r,R}=B_{R}(0)\setminus\bar{B}_{r}(0)
. Since
\mathbf{f}_{\chi v}
is an embedding, it is a diffeomorphism between
\Sigma_{\chi v}\cap B_{R+2}(0)
and
\Sigma\cap B_{R+2}(0)
. Let
Y_{R}=\mathbf{f}^{-1}_{\chi v}(\Sigma_{\chi v}\cap B_{R}(0))\subset\Sigma\cap B_{R+2}(0)
and
Z_{R}=(\Sigma\cap B_{R+2}(0)\setminus Y_{R})
. By the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{f}_{\chi v}(p)\right|\leq\left|\mathbf{x}(p)\right|+\left|v(p)\right|,

consequently the sets
Y_{R}
satisfy

(5.2)

\displaystyle Y_{R}\Delta(\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0))\subset\Sigma\cap A_{R-\bar{v}_{R},R+\bar{v}_{R}}

where
\bar{v}_{R}=\sup_{\Sigma\cap A_{R,R+2}}\left|v\right|
, and
\Delta
denotes the symmetric difference of two sets. Since
\Sigma
is trapped, it follows from
Equation

5.1
that

\displaystyle\left|Y_{R}\Delta(\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0))\right|=O(R^{-2}e^{\frac{-R^{2}}{4}}).

Now write

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{\chi v}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{\Sigma}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Sigma_{v}\cap B_{R}(0)}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{Y_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{{}={}}+\left(\int_{\Sigma{\chi v}\cap A_{R,R+2}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{Z_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}\right).

We can estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Y_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0)}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}\right|\leq\int_{Y_{R}\Delta(\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0))}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}=O(R^{-2}),

where we used the fact that, when
R
is sufficiently large

\displaystyle e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}\leq e^{\frac{(R+\bar{v}_{R})^{2}}{4}}\leq e^{\frac{R^{2}}{4}}\left(1+\bar{v}_{R}R+2\bar{v}_{R}^{2}\right)\leq e^{\frac{R^{2}}{4}}+CR^{-n}\leq e^{\frac{R^{2}}{4}}+1.

Thus

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{Y_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{\Sigma\cap B_{R}(0)}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}=0.

Moreover, using
Equation

4.5
, we get that

\displaystyle\phantom{{}\leq{}}\int_{\Sigma{\chi v}\cap A_{R,R+2}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{Z_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{Z_{R}}(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}\chi\right|\chi(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}+v^{2})+(\frac{3}{2}-\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|^{2})v^{2}+C\varepsilon(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}+v^{2}))e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}.

As
v\in W^{1}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{Z_{R}}(\left|\nabla_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}+v^{2})e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}=0.

Since
\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|
is bounded, the above implies that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma{\chi v}\cap A_{R,R+2}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}-\int_{Z_{R}}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}=0

Equation

5.3
and
Equation

5.4
imply the desired equality.
∎

The above equivalence means that we have all the tools from
[
12
]
in our disposal, and from now on we will write unambiguously
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
for the relative entropy.

Now let
\mathcal{M}=\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in(0,T]}
be an integral Brakke flow coming out of
\mathcal{C}
in the sense that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\mu_{t}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\mathcal{C}.

\mathcal{M}
is contained in the level set flow of
\mathcal{C}
(which necessarily fattens as long as there are more than one self-expanders asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
). The key fact is that, when
2\leq n\leq 6
, by
[
14
, Theorem 8.21]
, the two outermost flows of the cone
\mathcal{C}
corresponding to the boundary of the level set flow are given by two stable self-expanders (which are smooth when
2\leq n\leq 6
). Hence
\mathcal{M}
is, in fact, trapped between two asymptotically conical self-expanders. In particular the following forward monotonicity formula holds (here we have taken
f=1
):

Proposition 5.2

(Proposition 6.5 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be as above, and let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
denote the corresponding rescaled Brakke flow defined on
(-\infty,S)
. Then for any
-\infty<s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq S
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\tilde{\mu}_{s_{1}},\Sigma]\geq E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\tilde{\mu}_{s_{2}},\Sigma]+\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\left|\mathbf{H}_{\tilde{\mu}_{s}}-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\tilde{\mu}_{s}ds.

The following backward convergence to a self-expander is the starting point of the analysis.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be as above. Up to passing to a subsequence
s_{i}\to-\infty
, there exists a self-expander
\Sigma
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{s_{i}}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma.

Proof.

Fix a reference self-expander
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Then
E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\tilde{\mu}_{s},\Sigma^{\prime}]
is finite by appealing to the trapping and
[
12
]
. Consider the translated flow
\tilde{\mu}_{s}^{s_{0}}=\tilde{\mu}_{s+s_{0}}
defined on
s\in(-\infty,S-s_{0})
. By compactness of Brakke flows, up to passing to a subsequence
\{s_{i}\}
, as
s_{i}\to-\infty
, the sequence of translated flows converges to a rescaled Brakke flow
\bar{\mu}
defined on
(-\infty,\infty)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\bar{\mu}_{s},\Sigma^{\prime}]=\lim_{i\to\infty}E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\tilde{\mu}_{s}^{s_{i}},\Sigma^{\prime}]<\infty.

In view of the forward monotonicity formula
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int\left|\mathbf{H}_{\bar{\mu}_{s}}-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}d\bar{\mu}_{s}ds=0

for all
-\infty<s_{1}<s_{2}<\infty
. Hence
\bar{\mu}
is

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, so is
\bar{\mu}
.
∎

In general, however,
\Sigma
is only an
E
-stationary varifold and can have very large singular sets even in low dimensions. This is, in some sense, the key obstruction in the correspondence between tame ancient RMCFs and MCFs coming out of
\mathcal{C}
. One way to resolve this issue is to impose a low entropy condition, which forces the expanders to be smooth.

Another powerful consequence of the trapping is the uniqueness of tangent flows, which is proved using the Łojasiewicz inequality. Let
\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}
be the Euler-Lagrange operator associated with
E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\cdot,\Sigma]
given by

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v+sw},\Sigma]=\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v,w\right\rangle.

As
0
is a critical point for
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
, the operator
\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v=L_{\Sigma}v+Q(v),

which, in fact, agrees with the expander mean curvature of the hypersurface
\Sigma_{v}
. Here we record a version of the Łojasiewicz inequality for generic cones, which is good enough for our applications. Since we do not use the trapping assumption in the following proof, the same argument will work for
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
if we do not know a priori that
E_{\mathrm{rel}}
and
E_{\mathrm{rel}}^{*}
agree. However, in the proof of the uniqueness of tangent flows,
5.5
, trapping is necessary.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Sigma
be a self-expanders asymptotic to a generic cone
\mathcal{C}
. There is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Sigma)
such that the following holds: suppose
v\in C^{2,\alpha}\cap W^{2}(\Sigma)
satisfies
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
, then

(5.5)

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}(v)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}
denote the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}v=\Delta_{\Sigma}v+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}v-\frac{1}{2}v=L_{\Sigma}v-\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}v.

By
[
8
, Proposition 3.4]
,
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}
is an isomorphism between
W^{2}
and
W
, so there exists a constant
C=C(\Sigma)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}\leq C\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}+CC^{\prime}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W},

where
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\Sigma)>0
depends only the bound on
\left|A_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}
. As
\mathcal{C}
is generic, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}\geq c\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W}

for some constant
c>0
depending on the spectral gap
\bar{\lambda}=\min\{\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\}>0
. Thus

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}\leq C\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}+c^{-1}CC^{\prime}\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}\implies C_{\Sigma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}\leq\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W},

where the constant
C_{\Sigma}>0
in the last inequality depends only on
\Sigma
. Using the expansion
Equation

2.4
, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that when
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert Q(v,\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\mathbf{x}\cdot\nabla_{\Sigma}v,\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}v)\right\rVert_{W}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\Sigma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\Sigma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}.

Hence it follows from the triangle inequality that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}=\left\lVert L_{\Sigma}v+Q(v)\right\rVert_{W}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{\Sigma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}

On the other hand,
4.1
implies that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|\leq C_{0}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{0}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}^{2}

where
C_{0}=C_{0}(\Sigma)>0
. Combining
Equation

5.6
and
Equation

5.7
, we see that

\displaystyle 4C_{0}C_{\Sigma}^{-2}\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert^{2}_{W}\geq C_{0}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}^{2}\geq\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|

holds whenever
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon
.
∎

Remark

Here we shall explain that the exponent

Using

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
and
\Sigma

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma.

Proof.

The proof is standard (see eg.
[

[
34

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s)},\Sigma]

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s)}}\left|\mathbf{H}_{\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s)}}-\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\perp}}{2}\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left|\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}\right|^{2}e^{\frac{\left|\mathbf{x}+v(x)\mathbf{n}_{\Sigma}\right|^{2}}{4}}\mathrm{Jac}(v)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}\left\lVert\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}\right\rVert_{W},

where
C=C(\Sigma)
. Hence, by
5.4
,

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{ds}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s)},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2}\geq\frac{C}{2}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s)},\Sigma]\right|^{-1/2}\left\lVert\mathcal{N}_{\Sigma}v\right\rVert_{W}\left\lVert\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}\right\rVert_{W}\geq C\left\lVert\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}\right\rVert_{W}

Integrating the above gives, for
s_{2}\geq s_{1}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\left\lVert\frac{\partial v}{\partial s}\right\rVert_{W}ds\leq C_{0}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s_{1})},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2},

and therefore

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\lVert v(\cdot,s_{2})\right\rVert_{W}\leq\left\lVert v(\cdot,s_{1})\right\rVert_{W}+C_{0}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s_{1})},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2}.

for
C_{0}=C_{0}(\Sigma)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
be such that the nearest point projection onto
\Sigma
is smooth in a tubular neighborhood of radius
\varepsilon_{1}
. Let
\delta
be such that the extension lemma
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
holds with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\sigma=\varepsilon_{1}
(strictly speaking we need a slightly modified version with

norm replaced by the

norm). Choose
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\delta,\Sigma)
such that
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon_{0}
implies
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}\leq\delta/(3C_{0})
(as
\mathcal{M}
is trapped) and

\displaystyle C_{0}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v},\Sigma]\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2C_{0}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{W^{2}}\leq\frac{2\delta}{3}

in view of
4.1
. Now let
s_{i}\to-\infty
be a convergent subsequence, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\tilde{\mu}_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner\Sigma.

As
\Sigma
is a smooth self-expander, by Brakke regularity theorem
[
5
]
, for each
i
there is
\eta_{i}
such that
\tilde{\mu}_{s}
converges to the static flow of
\Sigma
on
(s_{i}-\eta_{i},s_{i}+\eta_{i})
. By subsequential convergence, given
\varepsilon>0
we may assume
i
is taken large enough so that the flow can be written as a normal graph
v
over
\Sigma
with
\left\lVert v(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon_{0}
on
(s_{i}-\eta_{i},s_{i}+\eta_{i})
. By the interior estimates of Ecker–Huisken
[
24
]
we may assume
\eta_{i}>1
when
i
is sufficiently large.

Fix an
i
such that all of the above is satisfied.
Equation

5.10
implies that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v(\cdot,s_{i}-\log(2))\right\rVert_{W}\leq\left\lVert v(\cdot,s_{i})\right\rVert_{W}+C_{0}\left|E_{\mathrm{rel}}[\Sigma_{v(\cdot,s_{i})},\Sigma]\right|^{1/2}<\delta.

Applying the extension lemma
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
, we get that
v
can be extended to a solution
v
to
Equation

3.2
on
(s_{i}-2\log(2),s_{i}+\log(2))
with
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha}}<\varepsilon_{1}
. Iterating the above using the forward monotonicity formula

(note that

, so

is increasing from

), we get a solution

to

3.2
on

Arguing as before using interior Schauder estimates together with

converges backwards to a (smooth) static solution

∎

Remark
.

As a byproduct of
5.4
, we can determine the rate of decay of the flow to the static solution, similar to the ODE case. Indeed, the rate (in the rescaled setting) is exponential if

holds.

In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, under suitable low entropy conditions, we can guarantee the smoothness of the self-expanders that arise as blow up limits of smooth MCFs (note that singular self-expanders do exist, but they do not arise as blow-up limits). Consequently
1.5
gives a complete picture of MCFs coming out of a generic cone of low entropy.

Corollary 5.6
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a generic cone with
\lambda[\mathcal{C}]<2
, then any smooth MCF coming out of
\mathcal{C}
is either a (smooth) self-expander or a tame ancient RMCF starting from a self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, as constructed in
1.1
.

Proof.

It suffices to establish that any blow up limit of such flow is automatically smooth. This argument is essentially carried out in
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
(cf.
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
). Let
\Sigma
be a subsequential limit from
Equation

1.8
and let
p\in\mathrm{sing}(\Sigma)
. By lower-semicontinuity of the entropy, any tangent cone
\nu
at
p
is a stationary cone with entropy less than 2. We claim that any such cone must be flat. Take an iterated tangent cone
\nu^{\prime}
at a singular point
q\in\supp\nu
(that is not the vertex).
\nu^{\prime}
is then a 2-dimension stationary cone with entropy at most 2 that splits off a line, which we write as
\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime\prime}\times\mu_{\mathbb{R}}
for some 1-dimensional stationary cone
\nu^{\prime\prime}
. Any such
\nu^{\prime\prime}
is a union of rays. Since the entropy of
\nu^{\prime\prime}
is less than 2,
\nu^{\prime\prime}
is either a flat line or a triple junction. However, as the MCF is smooth, it is cyclic as a mod 2 flat chain, which cannot encounter any singularity modeled on a triple junction by works of White
[
38
]
. Hence
\nu^{\prime\prime}
is a flat line, and
\nu^{\prime}
is flat. This implies
\nu
is smooth away from the origin. Since
\nu
is stationary and has entropy less than 2,
\supp\nu\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a closed geodesic of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which must be a multiplicity 1 great circle. This implies that
\nu
is flat and
\Sigma
is smooth by Allard regularity.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
is a generic cone with
\lambda[\mathcal{C}]<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then any smooth MCF coming out of
\mathcal{C}
is either a (smooth) self-expander or a tame ancient RMCF starting from a self-expander
\Sigma
asymptotic to
\mathcal{C}
, as constructed in
1.1
.

Proof.

The proof is similar except one uses
[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
instead of
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
. Essentially, the same argument follows through until the conclusion
\supp\nu\cap\mathbb{S}^{3}
is a closed smooth minimal surface in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. It follows from the resolution of Willmore conjecture
[
29
]
that
\supp\nu\cap\mathbb{S}^{3}
must be an equatorial sphere as any other such minimal surface has (Gaussian) area ratio at least
\frac{2\pi^{2}}{4\pi}=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Hence
\nu
is flat and
\Sigma
is smooth.
∎

Appendix A
The ODE Lemma

Lemma A.1

(Lemma B.1 in
[
20
]
)
.

Suppose
x,y,z:(-\infty,0]\to[0,\infty)
are absolutely continuous functions such that
x+y+z>0
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to-\infty}y(s)=0.

If there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
x,y,z
satisfy the following system of differential inequalities

\displaystyle\left|x^{\prime}\right|\leq\varepsilon(x+y+z),

\displaystyle y^{\prime}+y\leq\varepsilon(x+z),

\displaystyle z^{\prime}-z\geq-\varepsilon(x+y).

Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,
y\leq 2\varepsilon(x+z)
. Moreover, either there exists
-\infty<s_{*}\leq 0
such that
z\leq 8\varepsilon x
on
(-\infty,s_{*})
or
x\leq c\varepsilon z
on
(-\infty,0]
for some
c
depending on
\varepsilon_{0}
.

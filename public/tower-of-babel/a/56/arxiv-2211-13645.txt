Generalised higher-order Freud weights
Peter A. Clarkson, Kerstin Jordaan, Ana Loureiro, [2.5pt] School of Mathematics, Statistics, Actuarial ScienceUniversity of Kent, Canterbury, CT2 7FS, UK, [2.5pt] Department of Decision SciencesUniversity of South Africa, Pretoria, 0003, South Africa[2.5pt] Email: P.A.Clarkson@kent.ac.uk; jordakh@unisa.ac.za; A.Loureiro@kent.ac.uk

Abstract
We discuss polynomials orthogonal with respect to a semi-classical generalised higher order Freud weight ω ⁡ ( x , t , λ ) = | x | 2 ​ λ + 1 ​ exp ⁡ ( t ​ x 2 − x 2 ​ m ) , x ∈ ℝ , \omega(x;t,\lambda)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\left(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}, with parameters λ > − 1 \lambda>-1 , t ∈ ℝ t\in\mathbb{R} and m = 2 , 3 , … m=2,3,\dots . The sequence of generalised higher order Freud weights for m = 2 , 3 , … m=2,3,\dots , forms a hierarchy of weights, with associated hierarchies for the first moment and the recurrence coefficient. We prove that the first moment can be written as a finite partition sum of generalised hypergeometric F m 1 {}_{1}F_{m} functions and show that the recurrence coefficients satisfy difference equations which are members of the first discrete Painlevé hierarchy. We analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the recurrence coefficients and the limiting distribution of the zeros as n → ∞ n\to\infty . We also investigate structure and other mixed recurrence relations satisfied by the polynomials and related properties.

Introduction

In this paper we consider polynomials orthogonal with respect to the
generalised higher order Freud weight

\omega(x;t,\lambda)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\left(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\right),\qquad x,t\in\mathbb{R},\qquad m=2,3,\dots,

(1.1)

with
\lambda>-1
a parameter.
The main goal of this paper is to bring a comprehensive self-contained analysis of these polynomials when the parameter
m
takes integer values higher than
1
and for any values of
\lambda>-1
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The analysis for the particular cases of
m=2,3
was considered in
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
32
,
33
]
, with an emphasis on the study of the corresponding recurrence coefficients. We significantly extend existing studies on Freud type weights whilst providing a coherent and consistent approach, using techniques which are also likely to be adopted in the study of other semi-classical type weights. Throughout, we link and explain the connections to existing theory. After giving a short mathematical background in §2, in §
3
we give a closed form expression for the moments with respect to the weight (
1.1
), which correspond to a finite partition sum of generalised hypergeometric
{}_{1}F_{m}
functions.
The corresponding recurrence coefficients in the three term recurrence relation are investigated in §
4
. Therein we prove a recursive method that gives nonlinear recurrence relations satisfied by these recurrence coefficients (see Proposition
4.4
) and give them explicitly for the cases where
m=4,5
, whilst recovering the already known relations for
m=2,3
. We prove structure relations and mixed recurrence relations satisfied by generalised higher order Freud polynomials in §
5
.
The asymptotic behaviour of the recurrence coefficients proved in §

6

7
.

2
Mathematical background

Let
\nu
be a positive measure on the real line for which the support is not finite and all the moments

\mu_{k}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{k}\,{\rm d}\nu(x),\qquad k=0,1,\dots,

(2.1)

exist. The corresponding monic orthogonal polynomial sequence
{\big\{P_{n}(x)\big\}_{n\geq 0}}
is defined by

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P_{m}(x)P_{n}(x)\,{\rm d}\nu(x)=h_{n}\delta_{m,n},\qquad h_{n}>0

where
\delta_{m,n}
denotes the Kronekar delta. A fundamental property of orthogonal polynomials is that they satisfy a three-term recurrence relation of the form

P_{n+1}(x)=(x-\alpha_{n})P_{n}(x)-\beta_{n}P_{n-1}(x),

(2.2)

with
\beta_{n}>0
and initial values
P_{-1}(x)=0
and
P_{0}(x)=1
. The recurrence coefficients
\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
are given by the integrals

\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xP_{n}^{2}(x)\,{\rm d}\nu(x),\qquad\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}xP_{n-1}(x)P_{n}(x)\,{\rm d}\nu(x).

Relevant for this paper is the case of a measure that admits a representation via a positive weight function
\omega(x)
on the real line as follows
{\rm d}\nu(x)=\omega(x)\,{\rm d}x
. Henceforth, we will only work with a weight function representation.

The coefficient
\beta_{n}
in the recurrence relation (
2.2
) can also be expressed in terms of the Hankel determinant

\Delta_{n}=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits\big[\mu_{j+k}\big]_{j,k=0}^{n-1}=\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\ldots&\mu_{n-1}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-2}\end{matrix}\right|,\qquad n\geq 1,

(2.3)

with
\Delta_{0}=1
,
\Delta_{-1}=0
, whose entries are given in terms of the moments (
2.1
) associated with the weight
\omega(x)
. Specifically

\beta_{n}=\frac{\Delta_{n+1}\Delta_{n-1}}{\Delta_{n}^{2}}.

(2.4)

The monic polynomial
P_{n}(x)
can be uniquely expressed as the determinant

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}}\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\ldots&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-1}\\
1&x&\ldots&x^{n}\end{matrix}\right|,

and the normalisation constants as

h_{n}=\frac{\Delta_{n+1}}{\Delta_{n}},\qquad h_{0}=\Delta_{1}=\mu_{0}.

(2.5)

Also from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we see that the relationship between the recurrence coefficient
\beta_{n}
and the normalisation constants
h_{n}
is given by

h_{n}=\beta_{n}h_{n-1}.

For symmetric weights, since
\omega(x)=\omega(-x)
, it follows that
\alpha_{n}=0
in (
2.2
). Hence, for symmetric weights, the sequence of monic orthogonal polynomials
{\big\{P_{n}(x)\big\}_{n\geq 0}}
, satisfy the three-term recurrence relation

P_{n+1}(x)=xP_{n}(x)-\beta_{n}P_{n-1}(x).

(2.6)

The monic orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)
associated with symmetric weights are also symmetric, i.e.
P_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}(x)
. This implies that each
P_{n}
contains only even or only odd powers of
x
and we can write

\displaystyle P_{2n}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{2n-2k}^{(2n)}x^{2n-2k}=x^{2n}+c_{2n-2}^{(2n)}x^{2n-2}+\dots+c_{0}^{(2n)},

\displaystyle P_{2n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2n+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{2n-2k+1}^{(2n+1)}x^{2n-2k+1}=x^{2n+1}+c_{2n-1}^{(2n+1)}x^{2n-1}+\dots+c_{1}^{(2n+1)}x.

Substituting these expressions into the recurrence relation (
2.6
) and comparing the coefficients on each side, we obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{2n}

\displaystyle=c_{2n-2}^{(2n)}-c_{2n-1}^{(2n+1)},\qquad\beta_{2n+1}=-\frac{c_{0}^{(2n+2)}}{c_{0}^{(2n)}}=-\frac{P_{2n+2}(0)}{P_{2n}(0)}.

(2.7)

It follows from (
2.1
) that, for symmetric weights,
\mu_{2k-1}=0
,
k=1,2,\ldots\
and hence
it is possible to write the Hankel determinant
\Delta_{n}
given by (
2.3
) in terms of the product of two Hankel determinants obtained by matrix manipulation, interchanging columns and rows. The product decomposition, depending on
n
even or odd, is given by

\Delta_{2n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n},\qquad\Delta_{2n+1}=\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{n},

(2.8)

where
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{B}_{n}
are the Hankel determinants

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}

\displaystyle{=\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-2}\\
\mu_{2}&\mu_{4}&\ldots&\mu_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{2n-2}&\mu_{2n}&\ldots&\mu_{4n-4}\end{matrix}\right|,}\qquad\mathcal{B}_{n}{=\left|\begin{matrix}\mu_{2}&\mu_{4}&\ldots&\mu_{2n}\\
\mu_{4}&\mu_{6}&\ldots&\mu_{2n+2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{2n}&\mu_{2n+2}&\ldots&\mu_{4n-2}\end{matrix}\right|},

(2.9)

with
\mathcal{A}_{0}=\mathcal{B}_{0}=1
. Consequently, for a symmetric weight, substituting (
2.8
) into (
2.4
), the recurrence coefficient
\beta_{n}
is given by

\beta_{2n}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{n-1}}{\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n}},\qquad\beta_{2n+1}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}\mathcal{B}_{n+1}}{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{n}}.

Semiclassical orthogonal polynomials are natural generalisations of classical orthogonal polynomials and were introduced by Shohat in
[
43
]
. Maroni provided a unified theory for semiclassical orthogonal polynomials (cf.
[
36
,
37
]
). The weights of classical orthogonal polynomials satisfy a first-order ordinary differential equation, the
Pearson equation

\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}\{\sigma(x)\,\omega(x)\}=\tau(x)\,\omega(x),

(2.10)

where
\sigma(x)
is a monic polynomial of degree at most
2
and
\tau(x)
is a polynomial with degree
1
. For semiclassical orthogonal polynomials, the weight function
\omega(x)
satisfies a Pearson equation
(
2.10
)
with either deg
(\sigma(x))>2
or deg
(\tau(x))\neq 1
(cf.
[
22
,
36
]
).
The generalised higher order Freud weight given by (
1.1
)
is a symmetric weight that satisfies the Pearson equation (
2.10
)
with
\sigma(x)=x
and
\tau(x)=2(tx^{2}-mx^{2m}+\lambda+1)
and therefore is a semiclassical weight.

3
Moments of the generalised higher order Freud weights

The existence of the first moment
\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)
associated with the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
) follows from the fact that, at
\infty
, the integrand behaves like
\exp(-x^{2})
and, at
x=0
, the integrand behaves like
x^{\lambda}
which, for
\lambda>-1
, is integrable.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R},~\lambda>-1,~t\in\mathbb{R}
and
m=2,3,\dots
. Then, for the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
),
the first moment is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp(tx^{2}-x^{2m})\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda}\exp(ts-s^{m})\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{t^{k-1}}{{(k-1)}!}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m}\right)\;{}_{2}F_{m}\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m},1;\frac{k}{m},\frac{k+1}{m},\dots,\frac{m+k-1}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{m}\right)\;{}_{1}F_{m-1}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{m};\frac{1}{m},\dots\frac{m-1}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=2}^{m-1}\frac{t^{k-1}}{{(k-1)}!}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times{}_{1}F_{m-1}\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m};\frac{k}{m},\frac{k+1}{m},\dots,\frac{m-1}{m},\frac{m+1}{m},\dots,\frac{m+k-1}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\displaystyle\frac{t^{m-1}}{m!}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda}{m}+1\right)\;{}_{1}F_{m}\left(\frac{\lambda}{m}+1;\frac{m+1}{m},\frac{m+2}{m},\dots,\frac{2m-1}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right),

where
{}_{p}F_{q}(a;b;z)
is the generalised hypergeometric function (cf.
[
41
, eq. 16.2.1]
.

Proof.

Using the power series expansion of the exponential function, we obtain

\begin{split}\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp(tx^{2}-x^{2m})\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda}\exp(ts-s^{m})\,{\rm d}s\\
&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda}\exp(-s^{m})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(ts)^{n}}{n!}\,{\rm d}s\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n+\lambda}\exp(-s^{m})\,{\rm d}s\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{(n+\lambda-m+1)/m}\exp(-y)\,{\rm d}y\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+n+1}{m}\right),\end{split}

where
\Gamma(x)
denotes the Gamma function defined in
[
41
, eq. 5.2.1]
and the fourth equal sign follows from the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem.
Letting
n=mk+j
for
j=0,1,\dots,m-1
, we can write

\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}+k\right)\;\frac{t^{mk+j}}{(mk+j)!}.

Using the Gauss multiplication formula
[
41
, eq. 5.5.6]
yields

(mk+j)!=j!\,m^{mk}\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(\frac{j+\ell}{m}\right)_{k}

where
(a)_{k}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol (cf.
[
41
, §5.2(iii)]
, while it follows from
[
41
, eq. 5.5.1]
that

\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}+k\right)=\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)_{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right),

and hence we have

\begin{split}\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)_{k}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)}{m^{mk}\left(\frac{j+1}{m}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{j+2}{m}\right)_{k}...\left(\frac{j+m}{m}\right)_{k}}\,\frac{t^{mk+j}}{j!}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)_{k}}{\left(\frac{j+1}{m}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{j+2}{m}\right)_{k}...\left(\frac{j+m}{m}\right)_{k}}\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{mk}\\
&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m}\right)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\;{}_{2}F_{m}\left(\frac{\lambda+j+1}{m},1;\frac{j+1}{m},\frac{j+2}{m},\dots,\frac{m+j}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right),\end{split}

as required.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In our earlier studies of semi-classical orthogonal polynomials, we proved special cases of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
, namely for
m=2
in
[
11
]
and for
m=3,4,5
in
[
10
]
.

In the following theorem we derive a differential equation satisfied by the first moment
\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)
. It is often much easier to derive properties of a function from the differential equation it satisfies rather than from an integral representation or, as this case, a sum of generalised hypergeometric functions.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R},~\lambda>-1,~t\in\mathbb{R}
and
m=2,3,\dots
. Then, for the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
),
the first moment

\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp(tx^{2}-x^{2m})\,{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\lambda}\exp(ts-s^{m})\,{\rm d}s,

satisfies the ordinary differential equation

m\frac{{\rm d}^{m}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{m}}-t\frac{{\rm d}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}-(\lambda+1)\,\varphi=0.

(3.1)

Proof.

Following
[
39
]
and
[
10
]
, we look for a solution of (
3.1
) in the form

\varphi(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{st}\,v(s)\,{\rm d}s.

(3.2)

In order for (
3.2
) to satisfy (
3.1
), it is necessary that

\frac{{\rm d}^{m}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{m}}-{\frac{t}{m}}\frac{{\rm d}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}-\frac{\lambda+1}{m}\varphi=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{st}\left(s^{m}-{\frac{ts}{m}}-\frac{\lambda+1}{m}\right)v(s)\,{\rm d}s=0.

Using integration by parts, this is equivalent to

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{st}\left\{s^{m}v(s)+\frac{1}{m}v(s)+\frac{s}{m}\frac{{\rm d}{v}}{{{\rm d}{s}}}-\frac{\lambda+1}{m}v(s)\right\}{\rm d}s=0,

under the assumption that
\lim_{s\to\infty}sv(s){\rm e}^{st}=0
.
Hence, for
\varphi(t)
to be a solution of (
3.1
), we need to choose
v(s)
so that

(ms^{m}-\lambda)v(s)+s\frac{{\rm d}{v}}{{{\rm d}{s}}}=0.

One solution of this equation is
v(s)=s^{\lambda}\exp(-s^{m})
.∎

For the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
), the even moments can be written in terms of derivatives of the first moment, as follows

\displaystyle\mu_{2k}(t;\lambda,m)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2k}|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp(tx^{2}-x^{2m})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp(tx^{2}-x^{2m})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots\ ,

(3.3)

where the interchange of integration and differentiation is justified by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem.
Furthermore, from the definition we have

\displaystyle\mu_{2k+2}(t;\lambda,m)

\displaystyle=\mu_{2k}(t;\lambda+1,m),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots\ ,

(3.4)

and therefore

\mu_{2k+2}(t;\lambda,m)=\mu_{0}(t;\lambda+k+1,m),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots\ ,

which illustrates the importance of the first moment.

Recurrence coefficients for generalised higher order Freud weights

Theorem 4.1
.

For the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
), the recurrence coefficient
\beta_{n}
is given by

\beta_{2n}=\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}\ln\frac{\mathcal{B}_{n}}{\mathcal{A}_{n}},\qquad\beta_{2n+1}=\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}\ln\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}{\mathcal{B}_{n}}.

(4.1)

with
A_{0}=B_{0}=1
and

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\operatorname{Wr}\left(\mu_{0},\frac{{\rm d}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n-1}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{n-1}}\right),\qquad\mathcal{B}_{n}=\operatorname{Wr}\left(\frac{{\rm d}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}},\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{2}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{n}}\right),

(4.2)

where

\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\lambda}\exp(ts-s^{m})\,{\rm d}{x},

and
\operatorname{Wr}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{n})
denotes the Wronskian given by

\operatorname{Wr}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{n})=\left|\begin{matrix}\varphi_{1}&\varphi_{2}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}\\
\varphi_{1}^{(1)}&\varphi_{2}^{(1)}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}^{(n-1)}&\varphi_{2}^{(n-1)}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}^{(n-1)}\end{matrix}\right|,\qquad\varphi_{j}^{(k)}=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{\varphi_{j}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.9
) and (
3
) that
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{B}_{n}
can be written in terms of the Wronskians given by (

). Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{B}_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}-\mathcal{B}_{n}\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{A}_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}=\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{n-1},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{n}\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}-\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\frac{{\rm d}{\mathcal{B}_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}=\mathcal{A}_{n+1}\mathcal{B}_{n}

(4.3)

(cf.
[
47
, §6.5.1]
) and (
4.3

) yields (

).
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

See, for example, Van Assche

Theorem 4.3
.

For the generalised higher order Freud weight (

), the associated monic polynomials

satisfy the recurrence relation

and

, where

\displaystyle\beta_{2n}(t;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}(t;\lambda)\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(t;\lambda+1)}{\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda)\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda+1)}=\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}\ln\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda+1)}{\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda)},

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda)\mathcal{A}_{n+1}(t;\lambda+1)}{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}(t;\lambda)\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda+1)}=\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}\ln\frac{\mathcal{A}_{n+1}(t;\lambda)}{\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda+1)}.

where
\mathcal{A}_{n}(t;\lambda)
is the Wronskian given by (
4.2
)
with

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(t;\lambda,m)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{t^{k-1}}{{(k-1)}!}\Gamma\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m}\right)\;{}_{2}F_{m}\left(\frac{\lambda+k}{m},1;\frac{k}{m},\frac{k+1}{m},\dots,\frac{m+k-1}{m};\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{m}\right).

It follows from substituting (
3.4
) into the expression for
\mathcal{B}_{n}(t;\lambda)

) and (

). ∎

4.1
Nonlinear recursive relations

We follow the approach found in
[
37
, §7]
whose key results are summarised in
[
38
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Note that for a given
m\geq 1
, we can write

x^{2m}P_{n}(x)=\sum_{\ell=-m}^{m}C^{(2m)}_{n,n+2\ell}P_{n+2\ell},

(4.5)

where

C^{(2m)}_{n,n+2\ell}=\frac{1}{h_{n+2\ell}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{2m}P_{n+2\ell}(x)P_{n}(x)\,\omega(x)\,{\rm d}x\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=-m,\ldots,m.

Observe that

C^{(2m)}_{n,n+k}=C^{(2m)}_{n+k,n}=0
for
|k|\geq 2m+1

and

C^{(2m)}_{n,n+2\ell}=\frac{h_{n}}{h_{n+2\ell}}C^{(2m)}_{n+2\ell,n}=\frac{1}{\beta_{n+1}\cdots\beta_{n+2\ell}}C^{(2m)}_{n+2\ell,n}\,\quad\text{for}\quad\ell=1,\ldots,m.

From the recurrence relation (
2.6

The recurrence coefficient

Alternatively, (

Proof.

For any monic polynomial sequence
\big\{P_{n}(x)\big\}_{n\geq 0}

x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\rho_{n,j}P_{n-j}(x),\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1,

with
\rho_{n,0}=n

\omega(x)
satisfying the differential equation (

\sigma(x)=x

\tau(x)=2(tx^{2}-mx^{2m}+\lambda+1)
gives, using integration by parts,

\displaystyle\rho_{n,j}h_{n-j}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)P_{n-j}(x)\,\omega(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\{\tau(x)P_{n-j}(x)+x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n-j}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)\right\}P_{n}(x)\omega(x)\,{\rm d}x,

Therefore

Proposition 5.1
.

The polynomial sequence
\big\{P_{n}(x)\big\}_{n\geq 0}
orthogonal with respect to the generalised higher order Freud weight (

J(x;n)\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{P_{n+1}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}^{2}}(x)+K(x;n)\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n+1}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)+L(x;n)P_{n+1}(x)=0,

\displaystyle K(x;n)=C_{0}(x)D_{n+1}(x)-x\frac{{\rm d}{D_{n+1}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)+D_{n+1}(x),

\displaystyle L(x;n)=\mathcal{W}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(C_{n+1}(x)-C_{0}(x)),D_{n+1}(x)\right)-D_{n+1}(x)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}D_{j}(x),

D_{0}(x)=2x\left\{m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{2j-2}(t,\lambda)x^{2m-2j}-t\mu_{0}(t,\lambda)\right\}.

5.2
Mixed recurrence relations

We first consider the connection formula between the corresponding sequences of generalised higher order Freud orthogonal polynomials in the framework of Christoffel transformations when the measure is modified by multiplying with a polynomial. In our case, the measure is modified by a quadratic factor.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\big\{P_{n}(x;\lambda)\big\}_{n\geq 0}}
be the sequence of monic generalised higher order Freud polynomials orthogonal with respect to the weight (
1.1
), then, for
m,n
fixed,

\displaystyle xP_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda),

(5.1a)

\displaystyle x^{2}P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=xP_{2n}(x;\lambda)-\left\{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+\displaystyle\frac{P_{2n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}{P_{2n-1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}\right\}P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda).

(5.1b)

Proof.

Let
P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)
be the polynomials associated with the even weight function

\displaystyle\omega(x;\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp\big(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\big)=x^{2}\omega(x;\lambda),\quad m=2,3,\dots\ .

The factor
x^{2}
by which the weight
\omega(x;\lambda)
is modified has a double zero at the origin and therefore Christoffel’s formula (cf.
[
44
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
24
, Theorem 2.7.1]
), applied to the monic polynomials
P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)
, is

x^{2}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)=\frac{1}{P_{n}(0;\lambda)P_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)-P_{n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)P_{n+1}(0;\lambda)}\left|\begin{matrix}P_{n}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+1}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+2}(x;\lambda)\\
P_{n}(0;\lambda)&P_{n+1}(0;\lambda)&P_{n+2}(0;\lambda)\\
P_{n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)&P_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)&P_{n+2}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)\\
\end{matrix}\right|.

Since the weight
\omega(x;\lambda)
is even, we have that
P_{2n+1}(0;\lambda)=P_{2n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)
while
P_{2n}(0;\lambda)\neq 0
and
P_{2n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)\neq 0
, hence

x^{2}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)=\frac{-1}{P_{n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)P_{n+1}(0;\lambda)}\left|\begin{matrix}P_{n}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+1}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+2}(x;\lambda)\\
0&P_{n+1}(0;\lambda)&0\\
P_{n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)&0&P_{n+2}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)\\
\end{matrix}\right|,

for
n
odd, while, for
n
even,

x^{2}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)=\frac{1}{P_{n}(0;\lambda)P_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}\left|\begin{matrix}P_{n}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+1}(x;\lambda)&P_{n+2}(x;\lambda)\\
P_{n}(0;\lambda)&0&P_{n+2}(0;\lambda)\\
0&P_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)&0\\
\end{matrix}\right|.

This yields

x^{2}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)=P_{n+2}(x;\lambda)-a_{n}P_{n}(x;\lambda),

(5.2)

where

a_{n}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{P_{n+2}(0;\lambda)}{P_{n}(0;\lambda)},\quad&\text{for}\quad n\quad\text{even},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{P_{n+2}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}{P_{n}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)},\quad&\text{for}\quad n\quad\text{odd}.\end{cases}

Using the three-term recurrence relation (
2.6
) to eliminate
P_{n+2}(x;\lambda)
in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle x^{2}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)=xP_{n+1}(x;\lambda)-(\beta_{n+1}(\lambda)+a_{n})P_{n}(x;\lambda).

It follows from (
2.7
) that, for
n
even,
\beta_{n+1}(\lambda)+a_{n}=0
and the result follows. ∎

Theorem 5.3
.

For a fixed

, let

be the sequence of monic generalised higher order Freud polynomials orthogonal with respect to the weight (

\displaystyle P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle=P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda+1)+\beta_{2n}(\lambda+1)P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1),

(5.3a)

\displaystyle P_{2n}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle=P_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)\beta_{2n-1}(\lambda+1)P_{2n-1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}{P_{2n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}P_{2n-2}(x;\lambda+1).

(5.3b)

Proof.

Substitute (
5.1a
) into the three term recurrence relation

P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda)=xP_{2n}(x;\lambda)-\beta_{2n}(\lambda)P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda),

(5.4)

to eliminate
P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda)
and obtain

xP_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)=xP_{2n}(x;\lambda)-\beta_{2n}(\lambda)P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda).

Let
\displaystyle a_{2n}=\frac{P_{2n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}{P_{2n-1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}
. Substitute (
5.1b
) into (
5.4
) to eliminate
P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda)
and obtain

\displaystyle xP_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=xP_{2n}(x;\lambda)-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}\left(xP_{2n}(x;\lambda)-x^{2}P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1)\right).

(5.5)

Simplification and rearrangement of terms in (
5.5

\left(1-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}\right)P_{2n}(x;\lambda)=P_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}xP_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1),

then, using the three term recurrence relation to eliminate
xP_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}\right)P_{2n}(x;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}\right)P_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)}{\beta_{2n}(\lambda)+a_{2n}}\beta_{2n-1}(\lambda+1)P_{2n-2}(x;\lambda+1),

which simplifies to (
5.3b
).
Substituting (
5.1a
) into the three term recurrence relation

P_{2n+1}(x;\lambda+1)=xP_{2n}(x;\lambda+1)-\beta_{2n}(\lambda+1)P_{2n-1}(x;\lambda+1),

yields (
5.3a

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose

. For each fixed

Proof.

Suppose
-2<\lambda<-1

\lambda+1>-1
.
When
n

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle x^{k}P_{n}(x;\lambda)\,\,|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp\big(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\big)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{k}P_{n}(x;\lambda+1)\,\,|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp\big(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\big)\,{\rm d}x

(5.6)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\beta_{n}(\lambda)\beta_{n-1}(\lambda+1)P_{n-1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}{P_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;\lambda)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{k}P_{n-2}(x;\lambda+1)\,\,|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp\big(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\big)\,{\rm d}x,

while, for

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\qquad+\beta_{n}(\lambda+1)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{k}P_{n-2}(x;\lambda+1)\,\,|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp\big(tx^{2}-x^{2m}\big)\,{\rm d}x.

6.1
Asymptotic zero distribution

The asymptotic behaviour of the recurrence coefficients of generalised higher order Freud polynomials orthogonal with respect to (
1.1
), satisfying Freud’s conjecture, given by (
4.19
), is independent of the values of
t
and
\lambda
. The asymptotic behaviour implies that the recurrence coefficients are regularly varying, irrespective of
t
and
\lambda
. To consider the asymptotic distribution of the zeros of generalised higher order Freud polynomials orthogonal with respect to the weight (
1.1
) as
n\to\infty
, we use an appropriate scaling and apply the property of regular variation as detailed in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\phi(n)=n^{1/(2m)}
and assume that
n,N
tend to infinity in such a way that the ratio
n/N\to\ell
. Then, for the sequence of scaled monic polynomials
P_{n,N}(x)=(\phi(N))^{-n}P_{n}(\phi(N)x)
associated with the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
), the asymptotic zero distribution, as
n\to\infty
, has density

a_{m}(\ell)=\frac{2m}{c\pi(2m-1)}\left(1-{x^{2}}/{c^{2}}\right)^{\!1/2}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,1-m;\tfrac{3}{2}-m;{x^{2}}/{c^{2}}\right),

(6.1)

where

c=2a\ell^{1/(2m)}\quad\text{with}\quad a=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(m-1)!}{\!(\tfrac{1}{2})_{m}}\right)^{\!1/(2m)},

defined on the interval
(-2a\ell^{1/(2m)},2a\ell^{1/(2m)})
.

Proof.

The scaled monic polynomials
P_{n,N}(x)=(\phi(N))^{-n}P_{n}(\phi(N)x)
associated with the generalised higher order Freud weight (
1.1
) have recurrence coefficient
\beta_{n,N}(t;\lambda)=\frac{\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)}{\left(\phi(N)\right)^{2}}
. Since
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and, for every
\ell>0
, we have

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\phi(x\ell)}{\phi(x)}=\ell^{1/(2m)},

\phi
is regularly varying at infinity with exponent of variation
\tfrac{1}{2m}
(cf.
[
46
]
). Since it follows from (
4.6
) that

\lim_{n/N\to\ell}\sqrt{\beta_{n,N}(t;\lambda)}=\lim_{n/N\to\ell}\frac{\sqrt{\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)}}{\phi(n)}\frac{\phi(n)}{\phi(N)}=a\ell^{1/(2m)},

the recurrence coefficients
\beta_{n.N}(t;\lambda)
are said to be regularly varying at infinity with index
\tfrac{1}{2m}
(cf.
[
27
, Section 4.5]
). From the property of regular variation, using
[
27
, Theorem 1.4]
, it follows that the asymptotic zero distribution has density

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell}s^{-1/(2m)}

\displaystyle\left(2a-xs^{-1/(2m)}\right)^{\!-1/2}\left(2a+xs^{-1/(2m)}\right)^{\!-1/2}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell^{1/(2m)}}y^{2m-2}\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{2ay}\right)^{\!2}\right)^{\!-1/2}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi\ell}\int_{0}^{\ell^{1/(2m)}}y^{2m-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{2ay}\right)^{\!2k}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{2a}\right)^{\!2k}\int_{0}^{\ell^{1/(2m)}}y^{2m-2k-2}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi\ell^{1/(2m)}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!}\frac{1}{2m-2k-1}\left(\frac{x}{2a\ell^{1/(2m)}}\right)^{\!2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi\ell^{1/(2m)}(2m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)_{k}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-m\right)_{k}}{\left(\tfrac{3}{2}-m\right)_{k}k!}\left(\frac{x}{2a\ell^{1/(2m)}}\right)^{\!2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{a\pi(2m-1)\ell^{1/(2m)}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}-m;\tfrac{3}{2}-m;\left(\frac{x}{2a\ell^{1/(2m)}}\right)^{\!2}\right).

∎

7
Quadratic decomposition of the generalised higher order Freud weight

We apply known results
[
7
, Chapter 1, Theorem 9.1]
on the quadratic decomposition of any symmetric polynomials to this particular case of generalised higher-order Freud weights. Precisely, if

P_{2n}(x;t,\lambda)=B_{n}(x^{2};t,\lambda,)\qquad P_{2n+1}(x;t,\lambda)=xR_{n}(x^{2};t,\lambda),\qquad\text{for all}\quad n\geq 0,

then from the recurrence relation (
2.6
) we have

\displaystyle B_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=R_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)+\beta_{2n+2}R_{n}(x;t,\lambda),

\displaystyle xR_{n}(x;t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=B_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)+\beta_{2n+1}B_{n}(x;t,\lambda),

and this gives second order recurrence relations for both
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
as follows

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)=\left(x-\beta_{2n}-\beta_{2n+1}\right)B_{n}(x;t,\lambda)-\beta_{2n-1}\beta_{2n}B_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda),\ n\geq 1,\\
B_{1}(x;t,\lambda)=x-\beta_{1},\ B_{0}(x;t,\lambda)=1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}R_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)=\left(x-\beta_{2n+2}-\beta_{2n+1}\right)R_{n}(x;t,\lambda)-\beta_{2n+1}\beta_{2n}R_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda),\\
R_{1}(x;t,\lambda)=x-\beta_{1}-\beta_{2},\ R_{0}(x;t,\lambda)=1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,
\{B_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{R_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfy the orthogonality relations

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}B_{k}(x;t,\lambda)B_{n}(x;t,\lambda)\,x^{\lambda}\exp(tx-x^{m})\,{\rm d}x=h_{2n}(t,\lambda)\delta_{n,k},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{k}(x;t,\lambda)R_{n}(x;t,\lambda)\,x^{\lambda+1}\exp(tx-x^{m})\,{\rm d}x=h_{2n+1}(t,\lambda)\delta_{n,k},\quad n,k\geq 0.

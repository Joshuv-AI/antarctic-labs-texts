Trace mappings on quasi-Banach modulation spaces and applications to pseudo-differential operators of amplitude type
Joachim Toft, Divyang G. Bhimani, Ramesh Manna

Abstract
. We deduce trace properties for modulation spaces (including certain Wiener-amalgam spaces) of Gelfand-Shilov distributions. We use these results to show that Ψ \Psi dos with amplitudes in suitable modulation spaces, agree with normal type Ψ \Psi dos whose symbols belong to (other) modulation spaces. In particular we extend and improve the style of trace results for modulation spaces in [ 9 , 45 ] to include quasi-Banach modulation spaces. We also apply our results to obtain Schatten-von Neumann and nuclearity properties for Ψ \Psi dos with amplidudes in modulation spaces, extending earlier work in [ 12 , 41 , 45 , 51 ] .

Introduction

In the paper we deduce continuity properties of trace mappings when acting
on extended classes of modulation spaces. Thereafter we apply these
properties to get continuity and identification properties for amplitude type
pseudo-differential operators with amplitudes in modulation spaces. In contrast
to most of earlier approaches, e. g.
[
21
,
45
]

we allow the Lebesgue exponents of the involved modulation spaces to stay
in the full inverval
(0,\infty]

investigations are quasi-Banach spaces, which might not be Banach spaces.
Note that these Lebesgue exponents in
[
45
]

should belong to the smaller interval
[1,\infty]
, while in
[
21
]

they should belong to
(0,\infty)
and thereby not allowed to attain
\infty

In particular, our investigations include trace mapping properties of the
modulation space
M^{\infty,q}
,

, while analogous investigations
in
[

,

do not host these spaces.

A trace map is an operator which reduce the dimension of the domain for
functions or distributions, by fixing some coordinates. For example, let

f(x_{1},x_{2})
be a function which depends on
x_{1}\in\Omega_{1}
and

x_{2}\in\Omega_{2}
, let
z\in\Omega_{2}
be fixed. Then the
map
\operatorname{Tr}_{z}
which takes
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto f(x_{1},x_{2})
into

x_{1}\mapsto f(x_{1},z)
, i. e.,

(\operatorname{Tr}_{z}f)(x_{1})=f(x_{1},z),

can be considered as the archetype of trace mappings, provided

x_{1}\mapsto f(x_{1},z)
makes sense as a function.
(See
[
34
]
and Section
1
for notations.)

Trace mappings
appear in natural ways in different kinds of problems, e. g. in boundary
value problems of partial differential equations. If

P(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})
is a partial differential operator, and
f
and

u_{0}
are fixed functions or distributions, then

\begin{cases}P(t,x,D_{t},D_{x})u(t,x)=f(x,t),&t>0,\\[4.30554pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

is an example of an initial value problem. It is expected that
the solution
u(t,x)
should possess suitable continuity
and differentiability properties as well as the trace map
which takes
u(t,x)
into
u_{0}(x)=u(0,x)
is well-defined.
See e. g.
[
40
]
and the references therein for more facts on this.

Another example where
trace mappings appear naturally concerns pseudo-differential operators
of amplitude types.
For any amplitude
a\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{3d})
, the pseudo-differential operator

\operatorname{Op}(a)
is the linear and continuous map from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, given by

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Op}(a)f)(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a(x,y,\zeta)e^{i\langle x-y,\zeta\rangle}f(y)\,dyd\zeta.

Any such operator may in a unique way be expressed as a
pseudo-differential operator of standard
or Kohn-Nirenberg type with symbol in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
. That is, there is
a unique
a_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
such that
\operatorname{Op}(a)
above is equal to

a_{0}(x,D)=\operatorname{Op}_{0}(a_{0})
, where

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Op}_{0}(a_{0})f)(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}a_{0}(x,\zeta)e^{i\langle x-y,\zeta\rangle}f(y)\,dyd\zeta.

The drop of variable

when passing from
a(x,y,\zeta)
to
a_{0}(x,\zeta)

implies that somewhere in the process, a trace map appear. Indeed,

a_{0}
is obtained by the formula

a_{0}(x,\zeta)=(e^{i\langle D_{\zeta},D_{y}\rangle}a)(x,y,\zeta)\Big|_{y=x}=(e^{i\langle D_{\zeta},D_{y}\rangle}a)(x,x+y,\zeta)\Big|_{y=0}

(see e. g.
[
34
, Section 18.2]
).

Trace mappings often possess convenient continuity
properties when acting on spaces of continuous functions. For example, the mappings

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{z}

\displaystyle:C^{N}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

\displaystyle\to C^{N}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}}),

1.3.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

Let
0<h,s\in\mathbf{R}
be fixed. Then
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
consists of
all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s;h}}\equiv\sup\frac{|x^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|}{h^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\alpha!^{s}\,\beta!^{s}}

is finite. Here the supremum is taken over all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Obviously
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}
is a Banach space which increases with
h
and
s
. Here and
in what follows we use the notation
A\hookrightarrow B
when the topological
spaces
A
and
B
satisfy
A\subseteq B
with continuous inclusion.

The
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are the inductive and projective limits respectively
of
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect to
h>0
. This implies that

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(1.9)

and that the topology for
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is the
strongest possible one such
that the inclusion map from
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is continuous, for every choice of
h>0
. The space
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is a
Fréchet space with semi norms

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathcal{S}_{s;h}}
,
h>0
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}
,
if and only if
s>1/2
, and
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\neq\{0\}

if and only if
s\geq 1/2

(cf.
[
23
,
36
]
).

Let
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the dual of
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
Then the
Gelfand-Shilov distribution spaces

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are the projective and inductive limit
respectively of
\mathcal{S}_{s;h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with respect to
h>0
. This means that

\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s;h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathcal{S}_{s;h}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(
1.9
)
′

We remark that
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

is the (strong) dual of
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
when
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is the (strong) dual of
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

when
s>\frac{1}{2}
(cf.
[
36
]
).
We also remark that the form

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{L^{2}}=(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}

restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) is uniquely extendable to
a continuous map from
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\times\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) to
\mathbf{C}
.

We have

\mathcal{S}_{1/2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s_{1}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s_{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\\[4.30554pt]
\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s_{2}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{s_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{s_{1}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1/2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\qquad\frac{1}{2}<s_{1}<s_{2},

(1.10)

with dense embeddings.

The Gelfand-Shilov spaces are invariant under several basic transformations.
For example they are invariant under translations, dilations
and under (partial) Fourier transformations. We also note that the map

(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto f_{1}\otimes f_{2}
is continuous from
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})\times\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

each
\mathcal{S}_{s}
are replaced by
\Sigma_{s}
,
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}
or by

\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}
. (See also
[
52
]
.)

We let
\mathscr{F}
be the Fourier transform which takes the form

(\mathscr{F}f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)\equiv(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\,dx

when

denotes the usual scalar product
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The map
\mathscr{F}
extends
uniquely to homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

homeomorphisms on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and to a unitary operator on

\displaystyle(V_{\phi}f)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(y)\overline{\phi(y-x)}e^{-i\langle y,\xi\rangle}\,dy.

(
1.12
)

(2)

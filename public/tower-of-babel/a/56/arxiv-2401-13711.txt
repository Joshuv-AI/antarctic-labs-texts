Abstract
. The present work studies deeply quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras, obtaining, in particular, that they are all nilpotent. Consequently, we provide classifications in low dimensions and identify the double extensions that maintain symplectic structures. By means of both elementary odd double extensions and generalized double extensions of quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras, we obtain an inductive description of quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras of filiform type. Keywords : Quadratic Lie superalgebras; symplectic Lie superalgebras; double extension; generalized double extension; filiform. 2010 MSC : 17A70; 17B05; 17B30; 17B40.



1.
Introduction

The theory of quadratic Lie algebras has gained much attention in recent years, particularly since they have arisen in a number of other disciplines such as geometry and physics. Moreover, quadratic Lie algebras play a crucial role in conformal field theory, and with respect to these algebras, there is a Sugawara construction that exists specifically in the case of quadratic Lie algebras

[
19
]
. Nowadays, finite-dimensional quadratic Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero are well known (see
[
8
,
11
,
18
,
21
,
24
]
). The procedure of double extension was introduced by Medina
and Revoy in
[
24
]
, which enabled them to give a certain description of
quadratic Lie algebras. More precisely, it is showed that every quadratic Lie algebra can be constructed as a direct sum of irreducible ones, and the latter by a sequence of double extensions. Likewise, the
T^{*}
-extension, i.e. a construction that generalizes the semidirect product, was given in
[
11
]
to describe all solvable quadratic Lie algebras. A different approach, on the other hand, was given recently by Kath and Olbrich in
[
23
]
and the first attempt to generalize it to the context of quadratic Lie superalgebras can be found in
[
7
]
. Note that it is more difficult to apply the concept of double extension to classify inductively quadratic Lie superalgebras than to describe inductively quadratic Lie algebras. There are
a number of reasons for this, some of which include a
1
-dimensional subspace of a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is not necessarily a Lie subsuperalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and there is no analog to the Lie Theorem for solvable Lie superalgebras. In spite of the difficulties, the authors in
[
7
]
showed that every irreducible quadratic Lie superalgebra whose centre intersects the even part in a non-trivial way is a double extension. Nevertheless, such a condition is rather restrictive. Later, in
[
17
]
, the structure of the Lie superalgebras with reductive even part and the action of the even part on the odd part completely reducible is studied. Afterward,
Benayadi in
[
9
]
obtained an inductive description of quadratic Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is a reductive Lie algebra, and the action of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is completely reducible. Additionally, the same author gave an inductive description of the solvable quadratic Lie superalgebras such that the odd part is a completely reducible module on the even part. More recently, the authors in
[
3
]
generalized the notion of double extension of quadratic Lie superalgebras in order to present an inductive description of solvable quadratic Lie superalgebras. In particular, it was showed that all solvable quadratic Lie superalgebras are obtained by a sequence of generalized double extensions by
1
-dimensional Lie superalgebras. In
[
1
]
were presented some non-trivial examples of quadratic Lie superalgebras such that the even part is a reductive Lie algebra and the action of the even part on the odd part is not completely reducible, and was given an inductive description of this class of quadratic Lie superalgebras. More recently, in
[
6
]
, the authors characterized in any dimension, via double extensions, a very special family of quadratic Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is a reductive Lie algebra and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is not a completely reducible
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module. In particular, these superalgebras verify that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is a filiform
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module (filiform type). They showed that the study of quadratic Lie superalgebras of filiform type can be reduced to those that are solvable and obtained an inductive description of solvable quadratic Lie superalgebras of filiform type via both double extensions and odd double extensions of quadratic ones.

The purpose of our study is to investigate quadratic Lie superalgebras with symplectic structures. Prior to that, however, we analyzed quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras in general and obtained some significant results. More specifically, we answered some open questions raised in
[
5
]
.

Basic concepts and previous results

Throughout this work, we consider an arbitrary field of characteristic zero.

2.1.
Basic concepts and some results

We shall establish in this section some definitions needed later on. For an integer
i
we denote by
\bar{i}
its correspondent equivalence class in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
. For two integers
i,j
we use the well-defined notation
(-1)^{\overline{ij}}
for
(-1)^{ij}\in\{-1,1\}
. For a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
over the field
\mathbb{K}

for its

and
V_{\bar{1}}
for its
odd part
. An element
X
of

is called

if either
X\in V_{\bar{0}}
or
X\in V_{\bar{1}}
. In this work, all elements will be supposed to be homogeneous unless indicated otherwise. A linear map

-graded vector spaces is called

if
\phi(V_{\bar{0}})\subseteq W_{\bar{0}}
and
\phi(V_{\bar{1}})\subseteq W_{\bar{1}}
. While, it is called

if
\phi(V_{\bar{0}})\subset W_{\bar{1}}
and
\phi(V_{\bar{1}})\subset W_{\bar{0}}
. Clearly,
{\rm Hom}(V,W)={\rm Hom}(V,W)_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\rm Hom}(V,W)_{\bar{1}}
, where the first summand comprises all the even linear maps, and the second all the odd. Tensor products

are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector spaces, where its even part is
(V\otimes W)_{\bar{0}}:=(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}})
and odd part
(V\otimes W)_{\bar{1}}:=(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}})
.
We shall write

to mean that

is a homogeneous element of the Lie
superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of degree

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie superalgebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}

,

ii.

(-1)^{xz}[X,[Y,Z]]+(-1)^{xy}[Y,[Z,X]]+(-1)^{yz}[Z,[X,Y]]=0

super Jacobi identity
)

for any
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{x},Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{y},Z\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{z}
, with
x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

The general background on Lie superalgebras can be found in
[
27
]
.
From the previous definition,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is a Lie algebra and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-module. The Lie superalgebra structure also contains the
symmetric pairing

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
-morphism and satisfies the super Jacobi identity applied to three
elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}

The definition below gathers some properties of bilinear forms on Lie superalgebras.

3.
Symplectic extensions of nilpotent Lie superalgebras of filiform type

Here, we will introduce the concept of a symplectic double extension of a symplectic Lie superalgebra
as introduced in
[
13
]
.

It is a very well-known fact that if
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is a non-zero nilpotent Lie superalgebra then the center of the superalgebra,
\mathfrak{z}({{\mathfrak{g}}})=\mathfrak{z}({{\mathfrak{g}}})_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{z}({{\mathfrak{g}}})_{\bar{1}}
, is not trivial. If
\mathfrak{z}({{\mathfrak{g}}})_{\bar{1}}\neq\{0\}
then there exists at least an odd element
e\in({\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{g}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover, if we consider a symplectic structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, denoted by
\omega
, and the condition
\omega(e,e)=0
holds true, then
we can apply the process of
\delta_{\bar{1}}
-extension of symplectic Lie superalgebras
as described in
[
13
]
. Analogously, and for an even central element it can be applied other extensions of symplectic Lie superalgebras
[
13
]
.

We will use this framework extensively in this work since, as we will see in the following section, all symplectic Lie superalgebras with invertible derivations are always nilpotent. Further, if we take the quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras of filiform type, we
will be able to guarantee the existence of such an
e\in({\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{g}})\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})\setminus\{0\}
verifying
\omega(e,e)=0
.

3.1.
\delta_{\bar{1}}
-extension of symplectic Lie superalgebras

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be symplectic Lie superalgebra.
Let
\delta\in{\rm Der}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{1}}
be a derivation such that the map
\Omega:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathbb{K}
defined as

\Omega(X,Y):=\omega\bigl((\delta^{2}-(\delta^{*})^{2})(X),Y\bigr),

(3.1)

X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, is an element of
{\mathcal{B}}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},\mathbb{K})
, i.e., a
2
-coboundary. Since
\omega
is non-degenerate, there exists
Y_{0}\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
such that
\Omega(X,Y)=\omega(Y_{0},[X,Y])
, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Next, we recall the concept of the
\delta_{1}
-extension of symplectic Lie superalgebras.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
13

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
be an even symplectic Lie superalgebra. Let
\delta\in{\rm Der}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{1}}
be a derivation such that Condition (
3.1
) is satisfied together with

\delta^{2}=ad_{Y_{0}}\hskip 17.07182pt{\rm{\it and}}\hskip 17.07182pt\delta(Y_{0})=0.

Then, there exists a Lie superalgebra structure on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}:=\mathbb{K}e\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus(\mathbb{K}e)^{*}
for
e
odd, defined for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
as

[e,\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}]=0,\hskip 11.38092pt[X,Y]=[X,Y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+(\omega(\delta(X),Y)+(-1)^{x}\omega(X,\delta(Y))e,

[e^{*},e^{*}]=2Y_{0},\hskip 11.38092pt[e^{*},X]=\delta(X)-\omega(X,Y_{0})e.

There exists a symplectic form
\widetilde{\omega}
on
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}
defined as follows: for any

\widetilde{\omega}|_{{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}}=\omega,\hskip 5.69046pt\widetilde{\omega}(X,e)=\widetilde{\omega}(X,e^{*})=0,\hskip 5.69046pt\widetilde{\omega}(e^{*},e)=1,\hskip 5.69046pt\widetilde{\omega}(e,e)=\widetilde{\omega}(e^{*},e^{*})=0.

The even symplectic Lie superalgebra
\displaystyle(\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathbb{K}e^{*},\widetilde{\omega})
is called an
\delta_{\bar{1}}
-extension of
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
by the

-dimensional Lie superalgebra
(\mathbb{K}e)_{\bar{1}}
(by means of the odd superderivation
\delta
and

).

By

, the converse of Theorem

is also valid. That is, let

be an symplectic Lie superalgebra. If there exists a non-zero

.

4.
Quadratic Symplectic Lie superalgebras: General case.

Let us recall the following result (see
[
5
, Proposition 4.2]
). A quadratic Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
is a symplectic Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}},\omega)
if and only if there is an even invertible skew-supersymmetric superderivation
\delta
of
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
verifying
\omega(X,Y)=B(\delta(X),Y)
for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Moreover, in
[
5
]
it was remarked that since the Lie superalgebra admits an invertible even superderivation then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
is nilpotent (as it admits an invertible derivation
[
22
]
) which leads to the fact that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is solvable
[
27
]
. Furthermore, the following open question was posed:

Open question.

[
5
]
Is a quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra nilpotent? More generally, if a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits an even invertible superderivation, is
{\mathfrak{g}}
nilpotent?

The answer to both of these questions is yes, as we shall show next.

Theorem 4.1
.

If a Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits an even invertible superderivation then
{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra and
\delta
an even invertible superderivation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. There is no loss of generality in supossing we have an homogeneous basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\}
, where
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
is a basis of

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}
and
\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}\}
is a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
. Then, the law of
{\mathfrak{g}}
will be determined, in particular, by three sets of bracket products, two skew-symmetrical an one symmetrical, i.e.

{\mathfrak{g}}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{k}X_{k},&C_{ij}^{k}=-C_{ji}^{k}\\
{}[X_{i},Y_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}E_{ij}^{k}Y_{k},&E_{ij}^{k}=-E_{ji}^{k}\\
{}[Y_{i},Y_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{ij}^{k}X_{k},&F_{ij}^{k}=F_{ji}^{k}\end{array}\right.

verifying all of them the super Jacobi identity.

Let us consider now the Lie algebra
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
considering the
Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
by forgetting the super structure. So, let
\{\overline{X}_{1},\dots,\overline{X}_{n},\overline{Y}_{1},\dots,\overline{Y}_{m}\}
be a basis of
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and with the following multiplication table

{\mathfrak{g}}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\overline{X}_{i},\overline{X}_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{k}\overline{X}_{k},&\\
{}[\overline{X}_{i},\overline{Y}_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{m}E_{ij}^{k}\overline{Y}_{k},&\end{array}\right.

with
C_{ij}^{k}
and
E_{ij}^{k}
as above. It can be easily checked that
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}={\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{1}}
is in fact a Lie algebra, in particular a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Lie algebra. By defining

\overline{\delta}(\overline{X}_{i}):=\delta(X_{i}),\ 1\leq i\leq n\mbox{ and }\overline{\delta}(\overline{Y}_{j}):=\delta(Y_{j}),\ 1\leq j\leq m

it can be seen that
\overline{\delta}
is an invertible derivation of the Lie algebra
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and then
\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra (see
[
22
]
). Therefore,
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}
is nilpotent and also the adjoint representation of
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}
over
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{1}}
, that is, if we denote by
\rho_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
the representation of Lie algebra
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}
on
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{1}}
defined by:

\rho_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\overline{X})(\overline{Y}):=[\overline{X},\overline{Y}],\ \mbox{ for any }\overline{X}\in{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}\mbox{ and }\overline{Y}\in{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{1}}

then for every
\overline{X}\in{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}
,
\rho_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}(\overline{X})\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}({\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{1}})
is nilpotent. Let us remark that the structure constants
C_{ij}^{k}
determine the structure of a nilpotent Lie algebra on
{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}_{\bar{0}}
and
E_{ij}^{k}
make the representation
\rho_{\overline{{\mathfrak{g}}}}
nilpotent. Consequently,
{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\bar{0}}
is nilpotent and also the corresponding adjoint representation of
{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\bar{0}}
over
{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\bar{1}}
, which leads to the fact that the Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is nilpotent
[
26
]
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We can generalize the previous result to odd superderivations. Indeed, if
\delta
is an odd invertible superderivation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, then
\delta^{2}=\frac{1}{2}[\delta,\delta]
is an even invertible superderivation of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, so
{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent.

Corollary 4.1
.

Any quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra is nilpotent.

Next, and thanks to the previous theorem, we explore classifications in low dimension of quadratic Lie superalgebras in order to obtain the corresponding classification of symplectic quadratic Lie superalgebras, see
[
15
,
14
]
.

Let us recall first the concept of i-isomorphism for Lie superalgebras
[
14
]
. Thus, we say that two quadratic Lie superalgebras
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
and
({\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},B^{\prime})
are
isometrically isomorphic
(or
i-isomorphic
, for short) if there exists a Lie superalgebra isomorphism
A:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
satisfying
B^{\prime}(A(X),A(Y))=B(X,Y)
for all
X
,
Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. In this case,
A
is called an
i-isomorphism
. Therefore, we have the following results.

We classify some quadratic Lie superalgebras over the base field
\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a non-abelian quadratic Lie superalgebra of dimension
4
. We have:

(i)

If
\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=0
then there is no quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra.

(ii)

If
\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}})=\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=2
then there exists only one quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra, up to i-isomorphism, named
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}={{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\bar{1}}
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{X_{0},X_{1}\}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
such that the non-zero bilinear form
B(X_{0},X_{1})=B(Y_{2},Y_{1})=1
and the non-trivial Lie super-brackets:

{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[X_{1},Y_{1}]=-[Y_{1},X_{1}]=-2Y_{2}\\
{}[Y_{1},Y_{1}]=-2X_{0}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, any symplectic structure
\omega
on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\omega(X_{0},X_{1})=2b_{1}\\
\omega(Y_{2},Y_{1})=-b_{1}\\
\omega(Y_{1},Y_{1})=b_{2}\end{array}\right.

where
b_{1},b_{2}
are parameters in
\mathbb{C},
with
b_{1}\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i)
If
\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=0
, then we have the Lie algebra case, and it is a well-known fact that up to dimension 4, a nilpotent quadratic Lie algebra must be abelian
[
4
]
. Therefore, there is no non-abelian quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra.

(ii)
If
\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}})=\dim({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}})=2
, according to
[
15
]
and also
[
14
, Subsetion
2.1.
, page
129
]
, there are, up to i-isomorphism, two quadratic Lie superalgebras:
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
(described in the statement of the proposition) and

{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,2}^{s}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{X_{0},X_{1}\}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}=span_{\mathbb{C}}\{Y_{1},Y_{2}\}
such that the non-zero bilinear form
B(X_{0},X_{1})=B(Y_{2},Y_{1})=1
and the non-trivial Lie super-brackets:

{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,2}^{s}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[X_{1},Y_{1}]=-[Y_{1},X_{1}]=-Y_{1}\\
{}[X_{1},Y_{2}]=-[Y_{2},X_{1}]=Y_{2}\\
{}[Y_{2},Y_{1}]=[Y_{1},Y_{2}]=X_{0}\end{array}\right.

Let us note that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,2}^{s}
after applying the ismorphism given by

X_{0}=b,\ X_{1}=a,\ Y_{1}=\beta,\ Y_{2}=\alpha

is exactly the Lie superalgebra:
[a,\alpha]=\alpha
,
[a,\beta]=-\beta
and
[\alpha,\beta]=b
, which is noted by
(C^{2}_{-1}+A)
, Jordan-Wigner quantization, in Backhouse’s classification list
[
2
]
. It can be noted also that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
and the Lie superalgebra:
[a,\beta]=\alpha
,
[\beta,\beta]=b
, which is noted by
(C^{3}+A)
in Backhouse’s list are isomorphic by means of the isomorphism that follows:

a=X_{1},\ b=-2X_{0},\ \alpha=-2Y_{2},\ \beta=Y_{1}

On the other hand, it can be easily checked that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,2}^{s}
is not nilpotent and therefore it does not admit a symplectic structure. Thus, the only superalgebra candidate to be symplectic is
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
. Since a necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
to be a symplectic Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s},\omega)
is the existance of an even invertible skew-supersymmetric superderivation
\delta
of
({\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s},B)
verifying:
\omega(X,Y)=B(\delta(X),Y)
for all
X
,
Y
, we look for such
\delta

\delta(X_{1})=a_{0}X_{0}+a_{1}X_{1},\quad\delta(Y_{1})=b_{1}Y_{1}+b_{2}Y_{2}

From the condition of being an even superderivation we get

\delta([Y_{1},Y_{1}])=2[Y_{1},\delta(Y_{1})]\Longleftrightarrow\delta(X_{0})=2b_{1}X_{0}

\delta([X_{1},Y_{1}])=[\delta(X_{1}),Y_{1}]+[X_{1},\delta(Y_{1})]\Longleftrightarrow\delta(Y_{2})=(a_{1}+b_{1})Y_{2}

Thus, any even invertible skew-supersymmetric superderivation
\delta

\delta(X_{1})=-2b_{1}X_{1},\ \delta(Y_{1})=b_{1}Y_{1}+b_{2}Y_{2},\ \delta(X_{0})=2b_{1}X_{0},\ \delta(Y_{2})=-b_{1}Y_{2},\ b_{1}\neq 0.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let us remark that the symplectic quadratic Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{6,4}^{s}
were mentioned in
[
1
]
as examples of quadratic Lie superalgebras with a reductive even part and the action of the even part on the odd part is not completely reducible.

4.1.
Double extensions of quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras

Next, we start with double extensions of quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras in order to obtain the conditions under which we get another quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra as a result of the double extension. For that purpose we start with double extensions by one-dimensional Lie algebras.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{a}},B,\omega)
be a finite-dimensional quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebra. Let
({\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{B})
be a double extension of the quadratic Lie superalgebra
({\mathfrak{a}},B)
by the
1
-dimensional Lie algebra
(\mathbb{K}e)_{\bar{0}}
by means of
\psi:\mathbb{K}e\to{\rm Der}_{a}({\mathfrak{a}},B),
defined by
\psi(e):=D,
and
D
an even skew-supersymmetric superderivation of
({\mathfrak{a}},B)
. If
({\mathfrak{g}},\widetilde{B})
admits a symplectic structure then
D
is nilpotent.

Proof.

Thanks to Corollary
4.1

{\mathfrak{a}}
is a nilpotent Lie superalgebra, i.e.
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra and the adjoint representation of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}
over
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{1}}
is nilpotent: for every
X\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}
,
\rho_{{\mathfrak{a}}}(X)\in{\mathfrak{g}}{\mathfrak{l}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{1}})
is nilpotent. Applying Theorem
2.1
in our case we have the double extension
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}\oplus\mathbb{K}e^{*}
with
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}=\mathbb{K}e\oplus{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathbb{K}e^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}={\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{1}}
. By construction it can be seen the following conditions

\begin{array}[]{lll}[e,X]&=&D(X)\\[2.84526pt]
[X,Y]&=&[X,Y]_{{\mathfrak{a}}}+\varphi(X,Y)\\[2.84526pt]
[e^{*},X]&=&[e,e^{*}]=0\end{array}

for
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{a}}.

Moreover, the fact that
[e,X]=D(X)
implies that the action of
\mathbb{K}e
over
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bar{1}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
is nilpotent if and only if the linear map
D
is nilpotent. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
admits a symplectic structure,
{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent and in particular the action of
\mathbb{K}e
over
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}
must be nilpotent which concludes the proof of the statement.
∎

In what follows we are going to apply the aforementioned double extensions to the quadratic symplectic Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}_{4,1}^{s}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{6,4}^{s}
. For both of them we consider the double extensions obtained by all the possible even nilpotent skew-supersymmetric superderivations. It can be checked that all Lie superalgebras double extensions obtained are symplectic. Therefore, a straightforward computation leads to the following results.

Proof.

Let us note that
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{g}}}

\begin{array}[]{lll}[e,e]&=&X_{0}\\[2.84526pt]

\displaystyle=[X,[X,Y]]

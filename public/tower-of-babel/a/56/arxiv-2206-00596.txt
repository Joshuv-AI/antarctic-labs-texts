Families of skew-symmetric matrices of even size
J.W. Bruce, V.V. Goryunov, G.J. Haslinger

Abstract
The main result of the paper is a classification of singularities of complex skew-symmetric matrix families of even size which are simple under a natural equivalence relation. The classification is obtained by appropriate suspensions of simple families of arbitrary square matrices classified by Bruce and Tari. The suspensions we are using are based on the embedding of a space of arbitrary square matrices into the space of skew-symmetric matrices of twice the size. We also show that similar relations embed Bruce’s classification of simple symmetric matrix families into the Bruce-Tari simple list. Our constructions introduce a unified approach to all three simple classifications.



Matrices depending on parameters are traditional objects of numerous mathematical areas ranging from the most applied to the most abstract. So, an understanding of the local behaviour of matrix families has always been a natural and important problem. On the other hand, a starting point of any local understanding is a classification of singularities which are simple (that is, have no moduli) with respect relevant equivalences.

This paper is a continuation
(promised quite a while ago)
of work on the classification of simple singularities of three types of families of square matrices — arbitrary, symmetric and skew-symmetric — under their natural equivalences (see Section
1
for details). We denote the equivalences and family types respectively
\operatorname{Sq}
,
\operatorname{Sym}
and
\operatorname{Sk}
.

The work was started by the first author with his paper
[
5
]
on the classification of
\operatorname{Sym}
-simple families. It was followed by his paper with Tari
[
6
]
in which
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple singularities were classified, and by a thesis of the third author
[
10
]
where a list of two-parameter simple
4\times 4
skew-symmetric matrix families was obtained.

This paper gives a complete classification of complex
\operatorname{Sk}
-simple families of arbitrary even size. This classification turns out to be very closely related to the
\operatorname{Sq}
-classification from
[
6
]
. The relation is based on the embeddings
M\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&M\\
-M^{\top}&0\end{array}\right)
of spaces of square matrices into the spaces of skew-symmetric matrices of double the size. We introduce three ways — we call them
trivial, full
and
intermediate suspensions
— to produce a skew-symmetric family from an arbitrary square one, and it emerges from the calculations that these are sufficient to obtain our list from that in
[
6
]
. Our suspensions work like stabilisations of functions by addition of squares of new variables. The trivial suspension approach already appeared in
[
10
]
.

On the linear level, the trivial suspensions relate pencils of arbitrary
k\times k
matrices and of skew-symmetric
2k\times 2k
matrices (see
[
11
]
), while the duals of such pencils are related by the full suspensions. However, existence of extensions of these correspondences from the 1-jets to the entire simple classifications was quite unexpected.

Comparison of the simple lists from
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
shows that the standard embeddings of spaces of symmetric matrices into the spaces of arbitrary square matrices of the same size provide two further suspension types which embed the
\operatorname{Sym}
-simple classification into the
\operatorname{Sq}
one. Thus, the set of all five types of suspensions provides a unified point of view on all three simple classifications.

The paper is organised as follows.

Section
1
recalls the equivalences of arbitrary square, symmetric and skew-symmetric matrix families with which we are working in this paper.

Section
2
reminds the reader of the classification of simple square matrix families obtained in
[
6
]
.

In Section
3.1
we introduce our three types of the
\operatorname{Sq}
-to-
\operatorname{Sk}
suspensions.
We establish relations between the classes of the initial and suspended singularities. In Section
3.2
, we formulate our main result, Theorem
3.7
, which gives a complete classification of simple complex even-size skew-symmetric matrix families in terms of the suspensions of simple arbitrary square matrix singularities.

In Section
4
we take our first step towards a proof of Theorem
3.7
and derive constraints on the size of skew-symmetric matrices and on the number of parameters in a family that may allow existence of simple singularities.
All the conditions obtained are collected in Corollary
4.3
.

Section
5
completes our proof of Theorem
3.7
. There we reduce the classification of simple skew-symmetric map germs of the fixed matrix corank
in each of the cases allowed by Corollary
4.3
to the suspensions of half-the-size simple square matrix classification according to the claims of Theorem
3.7
. In the final subsection we check that none of the elements of these skew-simple classifications is adjacent to non-simple classes of a smaller matrix corank.
At this step the criterion that simplicity of a matrix family is equivalent to the simplicity of its determinantal or Pfaffian function, in the dimensions where these functions have isolated singularities, turns out to be very useful.

Finally, in Section
6
, we comment on the suspension embedding of the
\operatorname{Sym}
-simple classification into the
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple, and bring all three simple classifications together.

We work throughout over the complex numbers. Lists of simple matrix singularities over the reals can be derived using the same approach.

1
Matrix equivalences

A
matrix family

in this paper will be a holomorphic mapping
M:U\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n},
where
U
is an open domain in
\mathbb{C}^{s},
and
\operatorname{Mat}_{n}
is one of three spaces of complex
n\times n
matrices:

\operatorname{Sq}_{n},
arbitrary square matrices,

\operatorname{Sym}_{n},
symmetric matrices,

\operatorname{Sk}_{n},

n=2k
, skew-symmetric matrices.

Our principal aim is to derive a classification of simple skew-symmetric families from the known simple classification of arbitrary square families. On some occasions we will make comparisons with the symmetric case too.

The simple families we are looking for are with respect to the following equivalences.

In the
\operatorname{Sq}_{n}
case, we say that two matrix families,
M_{1}
and
M_{2},
are
\operatorname{Sq}
-equivalent
if there exist a biholomorphism
\varphi
of the source and two holomorphic maps
A,B:U\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that

M_{1}\circ\varphi=A^{\top}M_{2}B\,.

(1)

For the
\operatorname{Sym}
- and
\operatorname{Sk}
-equivalences
,
we require the existence of only one holomorphic
s
-parameter family
A
of invertible
n\times n
matrices such that

M_{1}\circ\varphi=A^{\top}M_{2}A\,.

(2)

We use a subscripted version of the terminology, like
\operatorname{Mat}_{n}
-equivalence, if we want to emphasise the size of the matrices.

Germ versions of the above definitions are straightforward.
An equivalence class of germs of matrix families will be called a
matrix singularity
. We denote by
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}
the space of all holomorphic function germs on
(\mathbb{C}^{s},0),
and identify the space of all holomorphic map germs
M:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n}
with the
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}
-module
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{N}
where
N={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{Mat}_{n}.
We write
{\mathcal{O}}_{x}
instead of
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}
when we want to emphasise the coordinates
x
introduced on
\mathbb{C}^{s}.
As usual,
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
will be the maximal ideal in
{\mathcal{O}}_{x}.

Our matrix classification will make use of right (
{\mathcal{R}}
) and contact (
{\mathcal{K}}
) equivalence of holomorphic functions, and of their versions
{\mathcal{R}}_{\partial}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\partial}

for functions on manifolds with boundary
[
2
]
.
In the
\operatorname{Sq}_{1}
,
\operatorname{Sym}_{1}
and
\operatorname{Sk}_{2}
cases, our equivalences are the same as the
{\mathcal{K}}
-equivalence of functions, and, therefore, our classification theorems do not mention these lowest size cases. The
{\mathcal{K}}
-equivalence class of the determinantal or respectively Pfaffian function of a matrix family is an invariant of the
\operatorname{Mat}
-equivalence.

Let
E_{j\ell}
be the
n\times n
matrix with the
j\ell
-entry 1, and all other entries zero. The extended tangent spaces to the
equivalence classes of germs
M:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n},
with the source coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{s},
are

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T_{\operatorname{Sq}}M&=&{\mathcal{O}}_{s}\left<\partial M/\partial x_{i},\ E_{j\ell}M,\ ME_{pq}\right>_{i=1,\dots,s;\ j,\ell,p,q=1,\dots,n},\qquad{\rm and}\\
\\
T_{\operatorname{Mat}}M&=&{\mathcal{O}}_{s}\left<\partial M/\partial x_{i},\ E_{j\ell}M+ME_{\ell j}\right>_{i=1,\dots,s;\ j,\ell=1,\dots,n}\quad{\rm for}\ \ \operatorname{Mat}=\operatorname{Sym},\operatorname{Sk}.\end{array}

We will denote by
\tau_{\operatorname{Mat}}(M)

the
Tjurina number
of
M,
that is, the codimensions of the above extended tangent spaces in
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{N}.
When dealing with skew-symmetric matrices we will write
{\mathcal{E}}_{ij}
for
E_{ij}-E_{ji}.

All our groups of matrix equivalences are in Damon’s class of geometric subgroups of the
{\mathcal{K}}
-equivalence group (see
[
7
]
), and hence a
\operatorname{Mat}
-miniversal deformation of a matrix family germ
M
may be written as

M+\lambda_{1}\varphi_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{\tau}\varphi_{\tau}\,,

where
\tau=\tau_{\operatorname{Mat}}(M),

and the
\varphi_{i}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{N}
form a basis of the quotient
{\mathcal{O}}_{s}^{N}/T_{\operatorname{Mat}}M.

We call a matrix family
M:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to(\operatorname{Mat}_{n},0)

quasi-homogeneous
if it is possible to assign positive weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{s}
to the source coordinates so that each entry
m_{ij}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})
would be a quasi-homogeneous function of degree
d_{ij}
and each
2\times 2
minor would also be quasi-homogeneous, that is,
d_{ij}+d_{pq}=d_{iq}+d_{pj}
must hold for the entire range of the indices. The last condition implies in particular that
\det\circ M
or
\operatorname{Pf}\circ M
are quasi-homogeneous too. All normal forms of matrix families appearing in this paper are quasi-homogeneous.

By the
matrix corank
of a germ
M:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n}
we will understand the corank of the matrix
M(0).
In our symmetric and arbitrary square settings, a family of the matrix corank
c
is equivalent to a family

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M^{\prime}&0\\
0&I_{n-c}\end{array}\right)
where
M^{\prime}
is a germ of a
c\times c
matrix family of the same type and
M^{\prime}(0)
is the zero matrix.
Similar reduction exists in the skew-symmetric case, with the only difference that the identity corner should be replaced by the block-diagonal matrix
J_{n-c}
with the elementary blocks

J_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
along the diagonal.

Two germs of matrix families
M_{i}:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to\operatorname{Mat}_{n_{i}},

i=1,2,
will be called
stably
\operatorname{Mat}
-equivalent

if there exists
n
such that the two ‘extended’ families

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{i}&0\\
0&I_{n-n_{i}}\end{array}\right)
, or respectively
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}M_{i}&0\\
0&J_{n-n_{i}}\end{array}\right)
, are
\operatorname{Mat}_{n}
-equivalent. All our
\operatorname{Mat}
-simple classifications are up to the stable
\operatorname{Mat}
-equivalences. All classification tables will be for
M(0)=0.

For a classification process, it is useful to have an a priori weaker intermediate notion of simplicity:

Definition 1.1

We will call a map germ
M:(\mathbb{C}^{s},0)\to(\operatorname{Mat}_{c},0)
(that is, that of the matrix corank
c
)
\operatorname{Mat}_{c}
-simple if it is simple within the set of all germs of the same matrix corank
c.

Adjacencies of such an
M
to germs of lower matrix coranks are ignored by the
\operatorname{Mat}_{c}
-simplicity. This notion allows us to first find lists of
\operatorname{Mat}_{c}
-simple singularities for various
s
and
c,
and only after that to check for the existence or non-existence of adjacencies to non-simple singularities of lower matrix coranks. One of the results of the classifications carried out in
[
5
,
6
]
is

Proposition 1.2

In the symmetric and arbitrary square settings, a matrix family germ is
\operatorname{Mat}
-simple if and only if it is
\operatorname{Mat}_{c}
-simple, where
c
is the matrix corank of the germ.

Thus the lists of
\operatorname{Mat}
-simple singularities in each of these two cases is the union of all the
\operatorname{Mat}_{c}
-simple lists. We will show that the same is true in the skew-symmetric case as well.

3
Correspondence between
\operatorname{Sq}_{k}
- and
\operatorname{Sk}_{2k}
-equivalences

3.1
Suspensions

The classification of simple
2k\times 2k
skew-symmetric families
M
turns out to be directly related to that of
k\times k
arbitrary square families
\check{M}
mostly by two kinds of suspensions which split
M
into four
k\times k
blocks:

Definition 3.1

The skew-symmetric matrix family
M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\check{M}\\
-\check{M}^{\top}&0\end{array}\right)
will be called the trivial suspension of a square matrix family
\check{M}.
The full suspension of
\check{M}
is the family
M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}V&\check{M}\\
-\check{M}^{\top}&W\end{array}\right),
where the blocks
V
and
W
are given by germs of diffeomorphism of two different copies of
(\mathbb{C}^{k(k-1)/2},0)
with
(\operatorname{Sk}_{k},0).

Thus, the number of parameters in the trivial case stays the same, and in the full case it increases by
k(k-1).

We take the set of all upper-triangle entries of the blocks
V
and
W
for the additional parameters of a fully suspended map.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
be the subgroup of the
\operatorname{Sk}_{2k}
-equivalence group which preserves the 4-block structures of the trivial suspensions. The right group of
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
is that of diffeomorphisms of the domain — let it be
(\mathbb{C}^{\check{s}},0)
— of
\check{M}.
The group of row-column operations
allowed in
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
has two connected components. In terms of (
2
), its identity component consists of all
matrix families

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{2}\end{array}\right)
,

A_{1},A_{2}:(\mathbb{C}^{\check{s}},0)\to\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{C})
,
acting on the trivial suspensions by

A^{\top}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\check{M}\\
-\check{M}^{\top}&0\end{array}\right)A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&A_{1}^{\top}\check{M}A_{2}\\
-(A_{1}^{\top}\check{M}A_{2})^{\top}&0\end{array}\right).

The other connected component contains the constant family
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I\\
-I&0\end{array}\right)
that sends
\check{M}
to
\check{M}^{\top}.

Definition 3.2

The
\operatorname{Sq}
-equivalence of arbitrary square matrix families which additionally considers a pair of mutually transposed families as equivalent will be called the

Sq^{\!\top}
-equivalence.

This is exactly the equivalence of the families
\check{M}
up to the action of the group
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}.

Thus we have:

Proposition 3.3

The
\operatorname{Sk}
-classification of those maps
M:(\mathbb{C}^{\check{s}},0)\to(\operatorname{Sk}_{2k},0)
which are
\operatorname{Sk}
-equivalent to trivial suspensions of maps
\check{M}:(\mathbb{C}^{\check{s}},0)\to(\operatorname{Sq}_{k},0)
coincides with the
\operatorname{Sq}^{\!\top}
-classification of the maps
\check{M}
.

A similar observation turns out to be valid for the full suspensions as well. To see this, we consider the same group
{\mathcal{D}}_{k},
whose right group is still that of diffeomorphisms of
(\mathbb{C}^{\check{s}},0).
The action of the
\operatorname{GL}_{k}\times\operatorname{GL}_{k}
part of

Proposition 4.1

(cf.

5

6

Analysis of the non-simplicity cutoffs in the
\operatorname{Sq}_{k}
-classification trees in

shows that
in each of these classifications one can take for a fencing set of singularities the set of all map germs
{\check{M}}
such
\det\circ{\check{M}}\in P_{8},X_{9},J_{10}.
Under all three types of suspensions we have used within Table 4, the stable
{\mathcal{K}}
-equivalence classes of
\det\circ{\check{M}}
and
\operatorname{Pf}\circ M
coincide.

During the reduction of the
\operatorname{Sk}_{2k}
-simple classifications to the suspensions of the
\operatorname{Sq}_{k}
-simple

, we also made non-simplicity cutoffs. Each of them was once again to avoid

having or being adjacent to the fencing function singularities.

Thus, for
s\leq 6
and
k\geq 1
, each
\operatorname{Sk}_{2k}
-fencing set is formed by all equivalence classes of maps whose Pfaffian is a fencing function singularity.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Sk}_{2k}
-simple map germs cannot have any adjacencies to non-simple germs of lower matrix coranks.

In fact, the same reason is behind the validity of Proposition
1.2
when the determinantal function has an isolated singularity. We have already clarified that above in the
\operatorname{Sq}
-setting, and it may also be checked that
\operatorname{Sym}_{k}
-fencing sets in the classifications of symmetric matrix families in at most three variables done in

are again formed by classes whose determinant is a
{\mathcal{K}}
-fencing function.

To formulate a general property,
we shall call the number of variables of a matrix family
low
if this number is at most the codimension in the corresponding matrix space of the singular locus of the variety of all degenerate matrices.
Then we have

Corollary 5.7

Assume the number of variables of a matrix family is low.
Then, in all three
\operatorname{Mat}
-classifications,
a matrix family is
\operatorname{Mat}
-simple if and only if its determinantal or respectively Pfaffian function is
{\mathcal{K}}
-simple.

6
Suspending symmetric families to arbitrary square

The similarities between the lists of
\operatorname{Sym}
- and
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple singularities obtained in respectively
[
5
]
and
[
6
]

indicate two types of
\operatorname{Sym}
-to-
\operatorname{Sq}
suspensions which relate these two classifications. To introduce these suspensions, consider a map germ
\hat{M}:(\mathbb{C}^{\hat{s}},0)\to(\operatorname{Sym}_{k},0),
the standard embedding
i_{k}:\operatorname{Sym}_{k}\to\operatorname{Sq}_{k}
and the standard direct sum representation
\operatorname{Sq}_{k}=\operatorname{Sym}_{k}\oplus\operatorname{Sk}_{k}.

Definition 6.1

The trivial suspension of a map
\hat{M}
is
the map germ
i_{k}\circ\hat{M}:(\mathbb{C}^{\hat{s}},0)\to(\operatorname{Sq}_{k},0).

The full suspension of
\hat{M}
is
the map germ
\hat{M}\oplus V:(\mathbb{C}^{\hat{s}},0)\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{k(k-1)/2},0)\to(\operatorname{Sym}_{k},0)\oplus(\operatorname{Sk}_{k},0)=(\operatorname{Sq}_{k},0),
where
V
is a diffeomorphism germ.

These two constructions embed the
\operatorname{Sym}
-simple classification of
[
5
]
into the
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple of
[
6
]
quoted in Section
2
. The only
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple singularities which cannot be obtained in these ways are the transposed pairs of 2-variable
\operatorname{Sq}_{2}
families in Table 1 of Theorem
2.1
singled out by the braces. Parts (2, 3, 4, 6) of that theorem are provided by the full suspensions, and the rest by the trivial ones. Some normal forms given in the theorem must be changed to make these relations obvious.

Example 6.2

The multiplicity of the determinant of an
\operatorname{Sq}
-simple map germ from
(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
to
(\operatorname{Sq}_{2},0)
must be 2. Therefore, such a map may be written as a two-parameter deformation induced from an
\operatorname{Sq}
-miniversal deformation of
the one-variable family
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&z\\
-z&0\end{array}\right),
for example as

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{3}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&z\\
-z&0\end{array}\right),\ \lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
.

In general, the reduction of the
\operatorname{Sq}
-equivalence to the
\operatorname{Sym}
-equivalence in the full suspension situation comes from the following.
Assume the
\operatorname{Sq}_{k}
-equivalence has brought a family of arbitrary square matrices of the matrix corank
k
to the form
\check{M}=\hat{M}\oplus V,
with
\hat{M}
and
V
as in Definition
6.1
: all upper triangle entries of the skew-symmetric matrix
V
are
k(k-1)/2
of the source coordinates, and the symmetric part
\hat{M}
does not depend on these entries. An
\operatorname{Sq}
-equivalence move

\check{M}\to A^{\top}\check{M}B=A^{\top}\hat{M}B+A^{\top}VB

(see (
1
)) transforms
\hat{M}
and
V
to respectively symmetric and skew-symmetric families if
A=B.
If we do not want entries of
V
to be involved in the new symmetric part then the invertible family
A
should not depend on them. After the move, the upper triangle entries of
A^{\top}VB
replace those of
V
as some of the source coordinates.

Thus, similar to what we had in Section
3.1
for the
\operatorname{Sq}
-to-
\operatorname{Sk}
full suspensions, we now have

Proposition 6.3

The
\operatorname{Sq}
-classification of those maps
\check{M}:(\mathbb{C}^{\hat{s}+k(k-1)/2},0)\to(\operatorname{Sq}_{k},0)
which are
\operatorname{Sq}
-equivalent to full suspensions of maps
\hat{M}:(\mathbb{C}^{\hat{s}},0)\to(\operatorname{Sym}_{k},0)
coincides with the
\operatorname{Sym}
-classification of the maps
\hat{M}.

Thus, one may say that — modulo our
\operatorname{Sym}
-to-
\operatorname{Sq}
and
\operatorname{Sq}
-to-
\operatorname{Sk}
suspensions — all three simple classifications considered in this paper fit together in one injective-surjective sequence

\operatorname{Sym}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sq}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Sk},

with bijectivity failing exactly at the pairs of 2-parameter families of arbitrary
2\times 2
matrices which do not have symmetric
\operatorname{Sq}
-normal forms while corresponding to
\operatorname{Sk}
-equivalent skew-symmetric families. In particular, the
\operatorname{Sym}
-simple list is embedded modulo the suspensions into the
\operatorname{Sk}
-simple list.

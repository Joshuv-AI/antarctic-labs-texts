On μ \mu -Zariski pairs of links
Mutsuo Oka

Abstract
. The notion of Zariski pairs for projective curves in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} is known since the pioneer paper of Zariski [ 25 ] and for further development, we refer the reference in [ 3 ] . In this paper, we introduce a notion of Zariski pair of links in the class of isolated hypersurface singularities. Such a pair is canonically produced from a Zariski (or a weak Zariski ) pair of curves C = { f ( x , y , z ) = 0 } C=\{f(x,y,z)=0\} and C ′ = { g ( x , y , z ) = 0 } C^{\prime}=\{g(x,y,z)=0\} of degree d d by simply adding a monomial z d + m z^{d+m} to f f and g g so that the corresponding affine hypersurfaces have isolated singularities at the origin. They have a same zeta function and a same Milnor number ( [ 16 ] ). We give new examples of Zariski pairs which have the same μ ∗ \mu^{*} sequence and a same zeta function but two functions belong to different connected components of μ \mu -constant strata (Theorem 14 ). Two link 3-folds are not diffeomorphic and they are distinguished by the first homology which implies the Jordan form of their monodromies are different (Theorem 23 ). We start from weak Zariski pairs of projective curves to construct new Zariski pairs of surfaces which have non-diffeomorphic link 3-folds. We also prove that hypersurface pair constructed from a Zariski pair give a diffeomorphic links (Theorem 24 ).

2.8.
Zeta multiplicity

By A’Campo formula, the zeta function
\zeta_{f}(t)

\prod_{j=1}^{s}(1-t^{d_{j}})^{\nu_{j}}

The zeta multiplicity of

. Suppose

. We call the factor

the zeta multiplicity factor
.
In general,

where

is the multiplicity of
f
, the lowest degree of the Taylor expansion of
f
at
\bf 0
.
This follows from the following observation.

Proposition 11
.

Assume that
\hat{\pi}:X\to\mathcal{U}_{0}
is a good resolution of an analytic function

of multiplicity
m

and put
\hat{\pi}^{-1}(V)=\tilde{V}\cup_{i=1}^{s}E_{i}
where

is the strict transform of

. Let
m_{i}
be the multiplicity of
\hat{\pi}^{*}f
along

. Then

.

2.9.
Lê-Ramunujam result for zeta-functions

Consider a piecewise analytic family
f_{s}(\mathbf{z}),\,0\leq s\leq 1
of functions with isolated singularity at the origin and suppose that the Milnor number
\mu(f_{s})
of
f_{s}
at
\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is constant for
s
. (
f_{t}
can be piecewise
C^{\infty}
.)
Then

Lemma 12
.

The zeta function
\zeta_{f_{s}}(t)
of

is independent of

and
coincides with
\zeta_{f_{0}}(t)
.

Proof.

For
n\neq 3
, the assertion follows from the result of Lê-Ramanujam
[
12
]
.
For
n=3
, we consider the family
g_{s}(x,y,z,w)=f_{s}(x,y,z)+w^{m}
.
Consider the reduced zeta function

\tilde{\zeta}(t):=\zeta(t)(1-t)
. For an isolated singularity case,
(-1)^{n}\tilde{\zeta}(t)
is equal to the divisor of the characteristic polynomial of the monodromy automorphism
h_{*}:H_{n-1}(F)\to H_{n-1}(F)
where
F
is the Milnor fiber.
For a fixed
s
, assume that

\rm{div}(\tilde{\zeta}_{f_{s}})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mu_{j}\Lambda_{e_{j}}
.
By Join theorem (
[
22
,
21
]
), we have the equality

\rm{div}(\tilde{\zeta}_{g_{s}})=\rm{div}(\tilde{\zeta}_{f_{s}})(-\Lambda_{m}+1)
. Taking

to be mutually prime for each

,
we have

\rm{div}(\tilde{\zeta}_{g_{s}})=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\Lambda_{e_{j}m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\Lambda_{e_{j}}

Note that this divisor does not depend on the parameter
s
by Lê-Ramanujam (
[
12
]
).
Assume that
{\rm{div}}(\tilde{\zeta}_{f_{0}})=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{0}}\nu_{j}d_{j}
.
We assume
d_{1}>d_{2}>\dots>d_{k_{0}}
and
e_{1}>\dots>e_{k}
and

. By the above equality, we get the equality

-\sum_{j=1}^{k_{0}}\nu_{j}\Lambda_{d_{j}m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{0}}\nu_{j}\Lambda_{d_{j}}=-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\Lambda_{e_{j}m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}\Lambda_{e_{j}}

for any

which is coprime to any

we conclude that

and

for

∎

3.1.
Zariski pairs and weak Zariski pairs

A pair of projective curves
\{C,C^{\prime}\}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is called
a Zariski pair
if
they have the same degree and there is a bijective correspondence

\phi:S(C)\to S(C^{\prime})

where
S(C)
and
S(C^{\prime})
are the sets of the singular points of
C
and
C^{\prime}
respectively
and the local topological type of the singularities of
(C,p)
and
(C^{\prime},\phi(p))
is the same for any
p\in S(C)
and
\phi
extends to a homeomorphism
\tilde{\phi}:N(C)\to N(C^{\prime})
of a tubular neighborhood
N(C)
of
C
to a tubular neighborhood
U(C^{\prime})
of
C^{\prime}
but this does not extend to a homeomorphism
of the ambient spaces

(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)
and
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C^{\prime})
for any
\tilde{\phi}
.

We say
\{C,C^{\prime}\}
is
a weak Zariski pair
if
they have the same degree and there is a bijective correspondence
\phi:S(C)\to S(C^{\prime})
of the singular points of
C
and
C^{\prime}
and the local topological type of the singularities of
(C,p)
and
(C^{\prime},\phi(p))
is the same for any
p\in S(C)
.
However there does not exist any homeomorphism

\tilde{\phi}:N(C)\to N(C^{\prime})
of their tubular neighborhoods which extends
\phi
.
This implies in particular that the pairs
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)
and
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C^{\prime})
are not homeomorphic.

5.5.
Link pairs constructed from Zariski pairs

Consider a Zariski pair of projective curves

C:f_{d}(x,y,z)=0
and
C^{\prime}:g_{d}(x,y,z)=0
of degree
d
with simple singularities. We assume thet
C,C^{\prime}
are irreducible for simplicity.
Consider the isolation

\begin{split}f(x,y,z):=f_{d}(x,y,z)+z^{d+m}\\
g(x,y,z):=g_{d}(x,y,z)+z^{d+m}.\end{split}

We assume
f
and
g
are convenient polynomial as before.
Take the simplest toric modification
\hat{\pi}:X\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
and

\hat{\pi}^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
with
\Sigma^{*}
with 4 vertices

with

\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
are written as

\begin{split}\hat{f}(\mathbf{u}_{\sigma})&=u_{\sigma 3}^{d}(f_{d}(u_{\sigma 1},u_{\sigma 2},1)+u_{\sigma 3}^{m}),\\
\hat{g}(\mathbf{u}_{\sigma})&=u_{\sigma 3}^{d}(g_{d}(u_{\sigma 1},u_{\sigma 2},1)+u_{\sigma 3}^{m})\end{split}

Let
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{s}
be the singular points of
E(P)
. Then choose an admissible coordinates system
\mathbf{w}_{i}=(w_{i,1},w_{i,2},w_{i,3})
for each
i=1,\dots,s

with
w_{i,3}=u_{\sigma 3}
. As
E(P)
is projective space
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and

f_{d}(u_{\sigma 1},u_{\sigma 2},1)=0

g_{d}(u_{\sigma 1},u_{\sigma 2},1)=0

\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}

\hat{f},\hat{g}

\hat{f}(\mathbf{w}),\hat{g}(\mathbf{w})=u_{\sigma 3}^{d}\left(\psi_{i}(w_{1},w_{2})+u_{\sigma 3}^{m}\right)

(E(P),\rho_{i})=(C,\rho_{i})
at

.

at each
\rho_{i}

\omega_{i}:Y_{i}\to X
or
\omega_{i}^{\prime}:Y_{i}^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}

\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
,

f
and

Existence and structure of P-area minimizing surfaces in the Heisenberg group
Amir Moradifam, Alexander Rowell

Abstract
We study existence and structure of P − P- area minimizing surfaces in the Heisenberg group under Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions. We show that there exists an underlying vector field N N that characterized existence and structure of P P -area minimizing surfaces. This vector field exists even if there is no P P -area minimizing surface satisfying the prescribed boundary conditions. We prove that if ∂ Ω \partial\Omega satisfies a so called Barrier condition, it is sufficient to guarantee existence of such surfaces. Our approach is completely different from previous methods in the literature and makes major progress in understanding existence of P P -area minimizing surfaces.

Let
\Omega

\R^{2n}

X=(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1},x_{2},x^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n})\in\Omega

\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla u-X^{*}}{|\nabla u-X^{*}|}\right)=H,

\mathbb{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u-X^{*}|+Hu\right)dx_{1}\wedge dx^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dx^{\prime}_{n}.

One of the main challenges in studying the equation (

\{X\in\Omega:|\nabla u(X)-X^{*}|=0\}.

On the other hand, since the energy functional

[

[
7

\mathbb{E}

In this paper, we study existence and structure of minimizers of the energy functional
\mathbb{E}
from a different point of view, using the Rockafellar-Fenchel duality. We prove various existence results that are new, even for the case
a\equiv 1
. Consider the following weighted form of the functional

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(a|\nabla u-X^{*}|+Hu\right)dx_{1}\wedge dx^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dx^{\prime}_{n},

2
Existence of P-area minimizing surfaces with Neumann boundary condition

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open region in
\R^{n}
,
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function,
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, and consider the minimization problem

\inf_{u\in\mathring{BV}(\Omega)}I(u):=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu,

(5)

where

\mathring{BV}(\Omega)=\{u\in BV(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u=0\}.

In order to study the minimizers of the least gradient problem (
17
) we first analyze the dual of this problem using Rockefeller-Fenchel duality. Define
E(b):L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\R
and
G(u):\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow\R
as follows

E(b)=\int_{\Omega}a\left|b+F\right|\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636ptG(u)=\int_{\Omega}Hu,

where
\mathring{H}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u=0\}
.
Then (
17
) can be rewritten as

(P)\hskip 14.22636pt\inf_{u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\{E(\nabla u)+G(u)\}.

(6)

By Rockafellar-Fenchel duality
[
10
]
, the dual problem associated to (
6
) is

(D)\hskip 14.22636pt-\min_{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}}\{E^{*}(b)+G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)\}=\max_{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}}\{-E^{*}(b)-G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)\},

(7)

where
E^{*}
and
G^{*}
are the convex conjugates of the convex functions
E
and
G
, and
\nabla^{*}
is the adjoint of the gradient operator
\nabla:\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let us first compute
G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)
.

\displaystyle G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\{\langle u,-\nabla^{*}b\rangle_{\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times(H^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}-G(u)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\left\{\langle u,-\nabla^{*}b\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega)\times(H^{1}(\Omega))^{*}}-\int_{\Omega}Hu\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\left\{-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot b-\int_{\Omega}Hu\right\}.

Since
cu\in\mathring{H}^{1}
for any
u\in\mathring{H}^{1}
and any
c\in\R
,

G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)=\begin{cases}\infty&\text{ if }u\not\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)\\
0&\text{ if }u\in\mathcal{D}_{0},\par\end{cases}

(8)

where

\mathcal{D}_{0}:=\left\{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}:\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot b+Hu=0,\ \ \hbox{for all}\ \ u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)\right\}.

(9)

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma 2.1 in
[
21
]
that

E^{*}(b)=\begin{cases}-\langle F,b\rangle&\text{ if }|b|\leq a\ \ \hbox{a.e.}\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

(10)

Thus, the dual problem
(D)
can be written as

(D)\hskip 14.22636pt\sup\{\langle F,b\rangle:b\in\mathcal{D}_{0}\ \ \hbox{and}\ \ |b|\leq a\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega\}.

(11)

Let
\nu_{\Omega}
denote the outer unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. Then for every
b\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}
with
\nabla\cdot b\in L^{n}(\Omega)
there exists a unique function
[b,\nu_{\Omega}]\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}(\partial\Omega)
such that

\int_{\partial\Omega}[b,\nu_{\Omega}]u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}u\nabla\cdot bdx+\int_{\Omega}b\cdot Dudx,\quad u\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}).

(12)

Moreover, for
u\in BV(\Omega)
and
b\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}
with
\nabla\cdot b\in L^{n}(\Omega)
, the linear functional
u\mapsto(b\cdot Du)
gives rise to a Radon measure on
\Omega
, and (
12
) is valid for every
u\in BV(\Omega)
(see
[
1
,
3
]
for a proof). The following lemma is an immediate consequence of (
12
).

Lemma 2.1

Let
b\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}\cap\mathcal{D}_{0}.
Then

\nabla\cdot b=H-\int_{\Omega}Hdx\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega,

and

[b,\nu_{\Omega}]=0\ \ \mathcal{H}^{n-1}-a.e.\ \ \hbox{ on }\ \ \partial\Omega.

Indeed, it follows from the above lemma that for any solution
N
of the dual problem (D)
\nabla\cdot N=H
a.e. in
\Omega
, and
N
is orthogonal to the unit normal vector on
\partial\Omega
in a weak sense. We are now ready to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.2

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\R^{n}
,
F,H\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a positive function. Then the duality gap is zero and the dual problem
(D)
has a solution, i.e. there exists a vector field
N\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
with
|N|\leq a
,
|Du+F|-a.e.
in
\Omega
, such that

\inf_{u\in\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left(a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu\right)dx=\langle F,N\rangle

(13)

Moreover

\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}=N,\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega,

(14)

for any minimizer
u
of (
6
).

Proof.
It is easily verified that
I(v)=\int_{\Omega}a|Dv|
is convex, and
J:(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}\rightarrow\R
with
J(p)=\int_{\Omega}a|p|dx
is continuous at
p=0
. Hence, it follows from Theorem III.4.1 in
[
10
]
that the duality gap is zero and the dual problem
(D)
has a solution
N
, and consequently (
13
) holds.

Now let
u\in\mathcal{H}
be a minimizer of (
6
). Then

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|N||Du+F|+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}N\cdot(Du+F)+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle+\int_{\Omega}N\cdot Du+Hu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle,

since
N\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Therefore, both the inequalities above are equalities, and hence (
14
) holds.
\Box

Remark 2.3

The primal problem
(P)
may not have a minimizer in
H^{1}
, but the dual problem
(D)
always has a solution
N\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}
. Note also that the functional
I(u)
is not strictly convex, and it may have multiple minimizers (see
[
16
]
). Theorem
2.2
asserts that if
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are both minimizers of
(P)
, then

\frac{Du_{1}+F}{|Du_{1}+F|}(x)=\frac{Du_{2}+F}{|Du_{2}+F|}(x)=N(x),

(15)

for a.e. point
x\in\Omega
where
|Du_{1}+F|
and
|Du_{2}+F|
do not vanish.

Next we show that if the primal problem
(P)
is bounded below, then it has a solution in
BV(\Omega)
. The proof follows from standard facts about
BV
functions, and we sketch it out for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.1

There exists a constant
C
, depending on
\Omega
, such that if

\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}|H(x)|<C,

(16)

then the primal problem (P) has a minimizer.

Proof.
Let
u_{n}
be the minimizing sequence for
I(u)
. Then

\int|\nabla u_{n}|-\int|F|-\int|H||u_{n}|\leq\int|\nabla u_{n}|-\int F+\int Hu_{n}\leq\int|\nabla u_{n}+F|+Hu_{n}<c,

for some constant
c
independent of
n
. Hence

\int|\nabla u_{n}|\leq C+\int|H||u_{n}|+\int|F|.

It follows from the Poincaré’s inequality that there exists a constant
C_{\Omega}
, independent of
n
, such that

\int|\nabla u_{n}|\leq C+||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}C_{\Omega}\int|\nabla u_{n}|+\int|F|

\Rightarrow\left(1-C_{\Omega}||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\int|\nabla u_{n}|\leq C+\int|F|.

\int|\nabla u_{n}|\leq C^{\prime}=\frac{C+\int|F|}{\left(1-C_{\Omega}||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)}

provided that
1-C_{\Omega}||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}>0
or equivalently

||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C:=\frac{1}{C_{\Omega}}.

It follows from standard compactness results for
BV
functions that
u_{n}
has a subsequence, denoted by
u_{n}
again, such that
u_{n}
converges strongly in
L^{1}
to a function
\hat{u}\in BV
, and
Du_{n}
converges to
D\hat{u}
is the sense of measures. Since the functional
I(u)
is lower semicontinuous,
\hat{u}
is a solution of the primal problem (
5
).
\Box

Corollary 2.4

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\R^{n}
,
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a positive function. There exists a constant
C
such that if
||H||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<C
, then the equation

\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}\right)=H-\int_{\Omega}H

has a solution
u\in BV(\Omega)
, i.e. there exists
N\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
such that

\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}=N.

3
Existence of P-area minimizing surfaces with Dirichlet boundary condition

In this section we study existence of
p
-area minimizing surfaces with a given Dirichlet boundary condition on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded open region in
\R^{n}
,
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function,
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, and consider minimization problem

\inf_{u\in BV_{f}(\Omega)}I(u):=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu,

(17)

where

BV_{f}(\Omega)=\{u\in BV(\Omega):u|_{\partial\Omega}=f\}.

The function
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
can be extended to a function in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
(denoted by
f
again), and the weighted least gradient problem (
17
) can be written as

\inf_{u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)}I(u):=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+\tilde{F}\right|+Hu+\int_{\Omega}Hfdx,

where
\tilde{F}=F+\nabla f
, and
\int_{\Omega}Hfdx
is a constant. Hence the minimization problem (
17
) is equivalent to the least gradient problem

\inf_{u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)}I(u):=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu.

(18)

It is easy to verify that the minimizers of (
17
) in
BV_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation

\nabla\cdot\left(a\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}\right)=H,

(19)

with
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
However, the minimization problems (
17
) and (
18
) do not necessarily have minimizers even if they are bounded below. This is in contrast with our results in Section 2 where boundedness of the functional
I(u)
in (
5
) from below automatically implies existence of a minimizer. To see this suppose
u_{n}
is a minimizing sequence for (
18
) that converges in
L^{1}(\Omega)
to a function
\hat{u}\in BV(\Omega)
. Then it follows from lower semicontinuity of the functional
I(u)
that

I(\hat{u})\leq\inf_{u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)}I(u).

However, the trace of
\hat{u}
on
\partial\Omega
may not necessarily be equal to zero. This is the main reason for nonexistence of minimizers for (
18
). Indeed it is well known that (
18
) may not have a minimizer, and proving existence of minimizers for (
18
) is a challenging problem that we aim to tackle in this section.

Similar to the our approach in Section 2, we first analyze the dual of the minimization problem (
5
) which will be a crucial tool in our analysis.

3.1
The Dual Problem

As in Section 2, let
E(b):(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}\rightarrow\R
and
G(u):H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\R
as

E(b)=\int_{\Omega}a\left|b+F\right|\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636ptG(u)=\int_{\Omega}Hu,

we can rewrite (
29
) as

(P^{\prime})\hskip 14.22636pt\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\{E(\nabla u)+G(u)\}.

(20)

By Rockafellar-Fenchel duality
[
10
]
, the dual problem associated to (
20
) is

(D^{\prime})\hskip 14.22636pt-\min_{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}}\{E^{*}(b)+G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)\}=\sup_{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}}\{-E^{*}(b)-G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)\},

(21)

where
E^{*}
and
G^{*}
are the convex conjugates of the convex functions
E
and
G
, and
\nabla^{*}
is the adjoint of the gradient operator
\nabla:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
. Due to the change in our function space, we update the computation of
G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)
.

\displaystyle G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{\langle u,-\nabla^{*}b\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}-G(u)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{\langle u,-\nabla^{*}b\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}}-\int_{\Omega}Hu\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\left\{-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot b-\int_{\Omega}Hu\right\}.

Since
cu\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and any
c\in\R
,

G^{*}(-\nabla^{*}b)=\begin{cases}\infty&\text{ if }u\not\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(\Omega)\\
0&\text{ if }u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0},\par\end{cases}

(22)

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}:=\left\{b\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}:\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot b+Hu=0,\ \ \hbox{for all}\ \ u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{0}.

(23)

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma 2.1 in
[
21
]
that

E^{*}(b)=\begin{cases}-\langle F,b\rangle&\text{ if }|b|\leq a\ \ \hbox{a.e.}\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}

(24)

Thus the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
can be written as

(D^{\prime})\hskip 14.22636pt\sup\{\langle F,b\rangle:b\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}\ \ \hbox{and}\ \ |b|\leq a\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega\}.

(25)

It follows from the integration by parts formula (
12
) that
b\in(L^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
if and only if

\nabla\cdot b=H\ \ \hbox{a.e. in}\ \ \Omega.

We are now ready to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\R^{n}
,
F,H\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
a\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a positive function, and assume
(P^{\prime})
is bounded below. Then the duality gap is zero and the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
has a solution, i.e. there exists a vector field
N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
with
|N|\leq a
,
|Du+F|-a.e.
in
\Omega
, such that

\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\left(a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu\right)dx=\langle F,N\rangle

(26)

Moreover

\frac{Du+F}{|Du+F|}=N,\ \ \ \ |Du+F|-a.e.\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \Omega,

(27)

for any minimizer
u
of (
20
).

Proof.
It is easily verified that
I(v)=\int_{\Omega}a|Dv|
is convex, and
J:(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}\rightarrow\R
with
J(p)=\int_{\Omega}a|p|dx
is continuous at
p=0
. Hence, it follows from Theorem III.4.1 in
[
10
]
that the duality gap is zero and the dual problem
(D)
has a solution
N
, and consequently (
26
) holds.

Now let
u\in A_{0}
be a minimizer of (
20
). Since
N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|N||Du+F|+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}N\cdot(Du+F)+\int_{\Omega}Hu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle+\int_{\Omega}N\cdot Du+Hu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F,N\rangle.

Therefore, both the inequalities above are equalities, and (
27
) holds.
\Box

Remark 3.2

Note that the primal problem
(P^{\prime})
may not have a minimizer in
H^{1}_{0}
, but the dual problem
(D^{\prime})
always has a solution
N\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}
. Note also that the functional
I(u)
is not strictly convex, and it may have multiple minimizers (see
[
16
]
). Theorem
2.2
asserts that if
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are both minimizers of
(P)
, then

\frac{Du_{1}+F}{|Du_{1}+F|}(x)=\frac{Du_{2}+F}{|Du_{2}+F|}(x)=N(x),

(28)

for a.e. point
x\in\Omega
where
|Du_{1}+F|
and
|Du_{2}+F|
do not vanish.

3.2
The relaxed problem

Here we study existence of minimizier for the relaxed least gradient problem

\inf_{u\in A_{0}}I(u)=\inf_{u\in A_{0}}\int_{\Omega}(a|Du+F|+Hu)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|ds,

(29)

where

A_{0}:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\R^{n}):u=0\text{ in }\Omega^{c}\right\}.

Unlike the problem (
18
), any minimizing sequence for (
29
) converges to a minimizer in
A_{0}
. Indeed the following proposition holds.

Proposition 3.1

There exists a constant
C
, depending on
\Omega
, such that if

\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}|H(x)|<C,

(30)

then the primal problem (
18
) has a minimizer in
A_{0}
.

Proof.
The proof follows from an argument similar to the one used in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, and the observation that if
u_{n}\in A_{0}
converges to
\hat{u}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, then
\hat{u}\in A_{0}
.
\Box

The next theorem characterizes the relationship between these two problems and sheds light on the challenging problem of existence of minimizers for (
18
).

Theorem 3.3

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary,
F\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{n}
, and
H\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. If the minimization problem (
18
) is bounded below, then

\min_{u\in A_{0}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(a|Du+F|+Hu)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|ds\right)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu

(31)

Moreover, if
u
is a minimizer of (
29
), then

u[N,\nu_{\Omega}]=|u|\ \ \mathcal{H}^{n-1}-a.e.\ \ \hbox{on}\ \ \partial\Omega.

(32)

Proof.
Since
BV_{0}(\Omega)
can be continuously embedded in
A_{0}
, we have

\min_{u\in A_{0}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(a|\nabla u+F|+Hu)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|ds\right)\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla u+F|+Hu.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that there exists a vector field
N
with
|N|\leq a
a.e. in
\Omega
and

N=\frac{Du+F}{\left|Du+F\right|}.

Now let
u
be a minimizer of the relaxed problem with
u|_{\partial\Omega}=g|_{\partial\Omega}
, where
g\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
. Since
u-g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{u\in A_{0}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(a|Du+F|+Hu)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|ds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla u+F|+Hu+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|N||\nabla u+F|+Hu+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}N(\nabla u+F)+Hu+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}N\cdot F+\int_{\Omega}N\cdot\nabla u+Hu+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|u|

\displaystyle=\langle N,F\rangle+\int_{\Omega}N\cdot\nabla(u-g)+H(u-g)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}N\cdot\nabla g+Hg+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|g|

\displaystyle=\langle N,F\rangle++\int_{\Omega}N\cdot\nabla g+Hg+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|g|

\displaystyle=\langle N,F\rangle+\int_{\partial\Omega}g[N\cdot\nu_{\Omega}]+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|g|

\displaystyle\geq\langle N,F\rangle

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in BV_{0}(\Omega)\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}a|Du+F|+Hu.

We used integration by parts, and
|N|\leq a
a.e. in
\Omega
, to obtain the last inequality, and hence (
31
) holds. Moreover, all the inequalities in the above computation are equalities. In particular, (
32
) holds.
\Box

4
Existence of minimizers under the Barrier condition

Let
F\in(L^{1}(\Omega)^{n})
and
a,H\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a>0
in
\Omega
, and define
\psi:\R^{n}\times BV_{0}(\Omega)
as follows

\psi(x,u):=a(x)|Du+F\chi_{E_{u}}|+Hu,

(35)

where
E_{u}
is the closure of the support of
u
in
\Omega
.

Define the
\psi
-perimeter of
E
in
A
, as

P_{\psi}(E;A):=\int_{A}a(x)\left|D\chi_{E}+F\chi_{E}\right|+H\chi_{E}.

Definition 1

1.

A function
u\in BV(\R^{n})
is
\psi
-total variation minimizing in
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
if

\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,u)\leq\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,v)\text{ for all }v\in BV(\R^{n})\text{ such that }u=v\text{ a.e. in }\Omega^{c}.

2.

A set
E\subset\R^{n}
of finite perimeter is
\psi
-area minimizing in
\Omega
if

P_{\psi}(E;\Omega)\leq P_{\psi}(\tilde{E})

for all
\tilde{E}\subset\R^{n}\text{ such that }\tilde{E}\cap\Omega^{c}=E\cap\Omega^{c}\text{ a.e.}
.

We will show that the super level sets of
\psi
-total variation minimizing functions in
\Omega
are
\psi
-area minimizing in
\Omega
. In order to achieve this, we shall first prove some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 4.1

Let
\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}
be defined as in (
38
). Then

P_{\psi}(E,\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}+F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}.

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}+F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{E}+F\chi_{E}|+H\chi_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{\lambda-\epsilon<u<\lambda+\epsilon\}}a|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}+F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-a|D\chi_{E}+F\chi_{E}|-H\chi_{E}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{\lambda-\epsilon<u<\lambda+\epsilon\}}a|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}|-a|F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-a(x)|D\chi_{E}|-a|F\chi_{E}|-H\chi_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{\lambda-\epsilon<u<\lambda+\epsilon\}}a|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}|-a(x)|D\chi_{E}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-H\chi_{E}-a|F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|-a|F\chi_{E}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}|-\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{E}|+\int_{\Omega}(H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-H\chi_{E})

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega\cap\{\lambda-\epsilon<u<\lambda+\epsilon\}}a|F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+a|F\chi_{E}|.

It is easy to see that the last two integrals converge to zero as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. Hence

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}+F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-P_{\psi}(E,\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}+F\chi_{\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}}|+H\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}-\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{E}+F\chi_{E}|+H\chi_{E}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}a|D\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}|-\int_{\Omega}a(x)|D\chi_{E}|\geq 0,

where we have used the lower semi-continuity of
\int_{\Omega}a|Dv|
to obtain the last inequality (see
[
16
]
). The proof is complete.
\Box

If
w\in BV(\R^{n})
and
\Omega
is an open set with Lipschitz boundary, we will write
w^{+}
and
w^{-}
to denote the outer and inner trace of
w
on
\partial\Omega.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
be bounded and open, with Lipschitz boundary.
Given
g\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
, define

I_{\psi}(v;\Omega,g):=\ \int_{\partial\Omega}a|g-v^{-}+F_{\chi_{v}}|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Omega}\psi(x,Dv).

Then
u\in BV(\R^{n})
is
\psi
-total variation minimizing in
\Omega

if and only if
u|_{\Omega}
minimizes

I_{\psi}(\,\cdot\,;\Omega,g)
for some
g
, and moreover
g=u^{+}
.

Proof:

First note that if
v\in BV(\R^{n})
then
v^{+},v^{-}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{n-1})
, and conversely, for every
g\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{n-1})

there exists some
v\in BV(\R^{n})
such that
g=v^{+}
. Also

\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi(x,Dv)=\int_{\partial\Omega}a|Dv+F_{\chi_{v}}|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\ \int_{\partial\Omega}a|v^{+}-v^{-}+F_{\chi_{v}}|d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(36)

To see this, note that
|Dv|
can only concentrate on a
set of dimension
n-1
if that set is a subset of the jump set of
v
,
so (
36
) follows from standard descriptions of
the jump part of
Dv
.

Now if
u,v\in BV(\R^{n})
satisfy
u=v
a.e. in
\Omega^{c}
, then
\int_{\bar{\Omega}^{c}}\varphi(x,Du)=\int_{\bar{\Omega}^{c}}\varphi(x,Dv)
. In
addition,
u^{+}=v^{+}
, so
using (
36
)
we deduce that

\int_{\R^{n}}\psi(x,Du)-\int_{\R^{n}}\psi(x,Dv)\ =\ I_{\varphi}(u;\Omega,u^{+})-I_{\varphi}(v;\Omega,u^{+}).

The lemma easily follows from the above equality.

\Box

Theorem 4.3

Consider the bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\R^{n}
and a
\psi
-total variation minimizing function in
\Omega
,
u\in BV(\R^{n})
. Let the super level sets of
u
to be defined as

E_{\lambda}:=\left\{x\in\R^{n}:u(x)\geq\lambda\right\}.

(37)

Then
E_{\lambda}
is
\psi
-area minimizing in
\Omega
.

Proof.
This proof closely mirrors that of Theorem 2.6 in
[
16
]
. Consider an arbitrary
\lambda\in\R
, and let
u_{1}=\max(u-\lambda,0),u_{2}=u-u_{1}
. For any
g\in BV(\R^{n})
such that
\text{supp}(g)\subset\overline{\Omega},
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{1}+F\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}}\right|+Hu_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{2}+F\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}}\right|+Hu_{2}=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du+F\right|+Hu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}a\left|D(u+g)+F\right|+H(u+g)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{1}+D(g\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}})+F\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}}\right|+H(u_{1}+g)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{2}+D(g\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}})+F\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}}\right|+Hu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{1}+D(g\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}})+F\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}}\right|+H(u_{1}+g)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}a|D(g\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}})|+\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{2}+F\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}}\right|+Hu_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a\left|D(u_{1}+g)+F\chi_{\{u\geq\lambda\}}\right|+H(u_{1}+g)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{2}+F\chi_{\{u<\lambda\}}\right|+Hu_{2}.

Thus

\int_{\Omega}a\left|Du_{1}+F\chi_{u_{1}}\right|+Hu_{1}\leq\int_{\Omega}a\left|D(u_{1}+g)+F\chi_{u_{1}}\right|+H(u_{1}+g),

for all
g\in BV(\R^{n})
with
\text{supp}(g)\subset\overline{\Omega}
. Hence
u_{1}
is also
\psi
-total variation minimizing. By the same process, we can verify that the function defined below is also
\psi
-total variation minimizing,

\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}:=\min\left(1,\frac{1}{\epsilon}u_{1}\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }u\leq\lambda,\\
\frac{1}{\epsilon}(u-\lambda)&\text{ if }\lambda<u\leq\lambda+\epsilon,\\
1&\text{ if }u>\lambda+\epsilon.\end{cases}

(38)

For a.e.
\lambda\in\R
the boundary of the super level set
E_{\lambda}
is a set of measure zero, that is,

\mathcal{L}^{n}\left(\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=\lambda\}\right)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\{x\in\partial\Omega:u^{\pm}(x)=\lambda\}\right)=0.

(39)

It follows that

\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}\rightarrow\chi_{\lambda}:=\chi_{E_{\lambda}}\text{ in }L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\R^{n}),\hskip 14.22636pt\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{\pm}\rightarrow\chi_{\lambda}^{\pm}\text{ in }L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}),

as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
via quite standard arguments that

P_{\psi}(\chi_{\lambda},\Omega)\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}P_{\psi}(\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda},\Omega);

(40)

and this, with the

convergence of the traces, implies that

I_{\varphi}(\chi_{\lambda};\Omega,\chi_{\lambda}^{+})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}I_{\varphi}(\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda};\Omega,\chi_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{+}).

(41)

Now for any
F\subset\R^{n}
such that
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi_{F}
a.e. in
\Omega^{c}
,

\displaystyle I_{\varphi}(\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda};\Omega,\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq I_{\varphi}(\chi_{F};\Omega,\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{+})

\displaystyle\leq I_{\varphi}(\chi_{F};\Omega,\chi_{\lambda}^{+})+\int_{\partial\Omega}a|\chi_{\lambda}^{+}-\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{+}|\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq I_{\varphi}(\chi_{F};\Omega,{\chi_{\lambda}}^{+})+C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\chi_{\lambda}^{+}-\chi_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{+}|\ d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

It follows from this, (
41
),
and

in

that

I_{\varphi}(\chi_{\lambda};\Omega,\chi_{\lambda}^{+})\leq I_{\varphi}(\chi_{F};\Omega,\chi_{\lambda}^{+}),

which proves that

is
{\varphi}
-area minimizing in
\Omega
.

In the case where

does not satisfy (

that satisfies (

\chi_{\lambda_{k}}\rightarrow\chi_{\lambda}\text{ in }L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\R^{n}),\hskip 14.22636pt\chi_{\lambda_{k}}^{\pm}\rightarrow\chi_{\lambda}^{\pm}\text{ in }L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}).

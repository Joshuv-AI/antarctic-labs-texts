Avoidance of the Lavrentiev gap for one-dimensional non autonomous functionals with state constraints
Carlo Mariconda

Abstract
Let F ⁡ ( y ) := ∫ t T L ⁡ ( s , y ⁡ ( s ) , y ′ ​ ( s ) ) ​ 𝑑 s F(y):=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}L(s,y(s),y^{\prime}(s))\,ds be a positive functional (the “energy”), unnecessarily autonomous, defined on the space of Sobolev functions W 1 , p ​ ( [ t , T ] , ℝ n ) W^{1,p}([t,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{n}) ( p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 ). We consider the problem of minimizing F F among the functions y y that possibly satisfy one, or both, end point conditions. In many applications, where the lack of regularity or convexity or growth conditions does not ensure the existence of a minimizer of F F , it is important to be able to approximate the value of the infimum of F F via a sequence of Lipschitz functions satisfying the given boundary conditions. Sometimes, even with some polynomial, coercive and convex Lagrangians in the velocity variable, thus ensuring the existence of a minimizer in the given Sobolev space, this is not achievable: this fact is know as the Lavrentiev phenomenon. The paper deals on the avoidance of the Lavrentiev phenomenon under the validity of a further given state constraint of the form y ⁡ ( s ) ∈ 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ n y(s)\in\mathcal{S}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} for all s ∈ [ t , T ] s\in[t,T] . Given y ∈ W 1 , p ​ ( [ t , T ] , ℝ n ) y\in W^{1,p}([t,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{n}) with F ⁡ ( y ) < + ∞ F(y)<+\infty we give a constructive recipe for building a sequence ( y h ) h (y_{h})_{h} of Lipschitz reparametrizations of y y , sharing with y y the same boundary condition(s), that converge in energy to F ⁡ ( y ) F(y) . With respect to previous literature on the subject, we distinguish the case of (just) one end point condition from that of both, enlarge the class of Lagrangians that satisfy the sufficient conditions and show that ( y h ) h (y_{h})_{h} converge also in W 1 , p W^{1,p} to y y . Moreover, the results apply also to extended valued Lagrangians whose effective domain is bounded. The results gives new clues even when the Lagrangian is autonomous, i.e., of the form L ⁡ ( s , y , y ′ ) = Λ ⁡ ( y , y ′ ) L(s,y,y^{\prime})=\Lambda(y,y^{\prime}) . The paper follows two recent papers [ 23 , 24 ] of the author on the subject.

Introduction

We consider here a one-dimensional, vectorial functional of the calculus of variations

F(y)=\int_{t}^{T}L(s,y(s),y^{\prime}(s))\,ds

(1.1)

defined on the space of Sobolev functions
W^{1,p}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
on
I:=[t,T]
with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for some
p\geq 1
. In the paper the
Lagrangian

L(s,y,u)
is Borel and is assumed to have values in
[0,+\infty[\cup\{+\infty\}
. Following the terminology of control theory we will refer to
s
as to the
time
variable, to
y
as to the
state
variable and to
u
as to the
velocity
variable.
Unless there is a minimizer, it may be desirable to approximate the infimum of the
energy

F
through a sequence of values
F(y_{h})
along a sequence
(y_{h})_{h}
of Lipschitz functions, that possibly share some desired boundary values and constraints. Though the Lipschitz functions are dense in
W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, unless some a priori growth assumptions from above are satisfied, this approximation is not always possible; this fact is summarized saying that the Lavrentiev phenomenon occurs.
Of course, the gap does not occur if the minimizers exist and are Lipschitz: we refer the interested reader to
[
18
,
17
,

,

Conditions ensuring the non-occurrence of the phenomenon, other than Lipschitz regularity of the minimizers, were established by Alberti and Serra Cassano in
[
1
]
in the
autonomous case
(i.e.,
L(s,y,u)=L(y,u)
) where they showed that, for the problem with
one end point
condition, the phenomenon does not occur under a suitable local boundedness condition. When the Lagrangian is non autonomous, the gap may occur (though being quite rare if
L
is coercive, see
[
32
]
), even with innocent looking Lagrangians, like in Manià’s
[
22
]
problem

\min F(y):=\int_{0}^{1}(y^{3}-s)^{2}(y^{\prime})^{6}\,ds:\,y\in W^{1,1}(I),\,y(0)=0,\,y(1)=1.

(1.2)

Several results concerning the
non autonomous
case are present in the literature (see
[
20
,
29
,
31
]
), but require some additional regularity of the Lagrangian on the state variable, not present in the autonomous case: as Carlson shows in
[
10
]
many do actually derive from Angell – Cesari Property (
\mathcal{D}
) introduced in
[
16
]
.
An extension of
[
1

for non autonomous Lagrangians of the form

, without requiring further regularity on the state or velocity variables of
\Lambda
, was recently provided by the author in
[
24
]
where it is pointed out that the conditions that ensure the non-occurrence of the gap for problems with just
one end point
condition may, even in the autonomous case, not be sufficient for those with
both
end point conditions: the difficulty of preserving the boundary condition was noticed also in the multidimensional setting (see
[

Another direction was followed by Cellina and his collaborators Ferriero, Marchini, Treu, Zagatti in
[

. Here, given

Notation and Basic assumptions

2.1
Basic Assumptions

Let
p\geq 1
. The functional
F
(sometimes referred as to the “energy”) is defined by

\forall y\in W^{1,p}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\qquad F(y):=\int_{I}L(s,y(s),y^{\prime}(s))\,ds,

(2.1)

where
L(s,y,v)
is of the form
L(s,y,v)=\Lambda(s,y,v)\Psi(s,y)
.

Basic Assumptions
.

We assume the following conditions.

•

I=[t,T]
is a closed, bounded interval of
{\mathbb{R}}
;

•

\Lambda:I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,+\infty[\cup\{+\infty\},\,(s,y,u)\mapsto\Lambda(s,y,u)
(
n\geq 1
) is Borel measurable;

•

\Psi:I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is Borel.

•

The
effective domain
of
\Lambda
, given by

\Dom(\Lambda):=\{(s,y,u)\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\,\Lambda(s,y,u)<+\infty\}

(2.2)

is of the form
\Dom(\Lambda)=I\times D_{\Lambda}
, with
D_{\Lambda}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

;

•

The Lebesgue measure of a subset

of

(no confusion may occur with the Euclidean norm);

•

If

The complement of a set

The characteristic function of a set

.

2.3
Two variational problems

We shall consider different variational problems associated to the functional
F
, with different end-point conditions and/or state constraints.
Let
X,Y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\mathcal{S}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We define

•

If
X\in\mathcal{S}
we set
\Gamma_{X}^{\mathcal{S}}:=\{y\in W^{1,p}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\,y(t)=X,\,y(I)\subset\mathcal{S}\}
, and the corresponding variational problem

\text{Minimize }\{F(y):\,y\in\Gamma_{X}^{\mathcal{S}}\},

(
\mathcal{P}_{X}^{\mathcal{S}}
)

whenever
\inf(\mathcal{P}_{X}^{\mathcal{S}})<+\infty
.

•

If
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}
we set

\Gamma_{X,Y}^{\mathcal{S}}:=\{y\in W^{1,p}(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\,y(t)=X,\,y(T)=Y,\,y(I)\subset\mathcal{S}\},

(2.3)

and the corresponding variational problem

\text{Minimize }\{F(y):\,y\in\Gamma_{X,Y}^{\mathcal{S}}\}.

(
\mathcal{P}_{X,Y}^{\mathcal{S}}
)

whenever
\inf(\mathcal{P}_{X,Y}^{\mathcal{S}})<+\infty
.

For the problems with one end point constraint, there is no privilege in considering the initial condition

instead of the final one

2.4
Lavrentiev gap at a function and Lavrentiev phenomenon

In this paper we consider different boundary data for the same integral functional.

Definition 2.1

(
Lavrentiev gap at
y\in W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
)
.

Let
y\in W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be such that
F(y)<+\infty
and let
\Gamma\in\{\Gamma_{X}^{\mathcal{S}},\Gamma_{X,Y}^{\mathcal{S}}\}
.

We say that the
Lavrentiev gap
does not occur at
y
for the variational problem corresponding to
\Gamma
if
there exists a sequence
\left(y_{h}\right)_{h\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of functions in
\Lip(I,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfying:

1.

\forall h\in\mathbb{N}\quad y_{h}\in\Gamma
;

2.

\displaystyle\limsup_{h\to+\infty}F(y_{h})\leq F(y)
;

3.

y_{h}\to y
in
W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We say that the
Lavrentiev phenomenon
does not occur for the variational problem corresponding to
\Gamma
if

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\\
y\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}F(y)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\Lip(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})\\
y\in\Gamma\end{subarray}}F(y).

(2.4)

Remark 2.2

(Gap and phenomenon)
.

AA

•

Condition 2 in Definition
2.1
is less restrictive than the one that is usually considered, i.e., that
\displaystyle\lim_{h\to+\infty}F(y_{h})=F(y)
. We believe that this one here is more appropriate to describe the Lavrentiev gap.

•

Let
y\in W^{1,p}(I;{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. The non-occurrence of the phenomenon at
y
ensures that, given
\varepsilon>0
, there is a
Lipschitz
function
\overline{y}
satisfying the same boundary data and/or constraints such that
F(\overline{y})\leq F(y)+\varepsilon
. If
L(s,y,\cdot)
is convex for all
(s,y)\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and
L(s,\cdot,\cdot)
is lower semicontinuous, the non-occurrence of the Lavrentiev gap at
y
implies the convergence of
(y_{h})_{h}
to
y
in
energy
, i.e.,

\lim_{h\to+\infty}F(y_{h})=F(y):

(2.5)

Indeed in that case
F
is weakly lower semicontinuous.

•

Of course, the non-occurrence of the Lavrentiev gap along a minimizing sequence implies the non-occurrence of the Lavrentiev phenomenon for the same variational problem.

The following celebrated example motivates the need to distinguish problems with just one end point condition from problems with both end points conditions.

Example 2.3

(Manià’s example
[
22
]
)
.

Consider the problem of minimizing

Then

0=\min F=F(y)<\inf\{F(y):\,y\in\Lip([0,1]),\,y(0)=0,y(1)=1\}.

However, as it is noticed in

s^{1/3}&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

Therefore, no Lavrentiev phenomenon occurs for the variational problem

\min F(y)=\int_{0}^{1}(y^{3}-s)^{2}(y^{\prime})^{6}\,ds:\,y\in W^{1,1}(I),\,\,y(1)=1.

.

For every

2.7
Distance-like functions and the compatibility condition (D)

In all the paper one can replace a distance-like function with the Euclidean distance
\dist_{e}
and Condition (D) with the requirement that
\Dom(\Lambda)
is open in
I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. It might be convenient, however, to consider other functions than
\dist_{e}
.
A distance-like function is a positive function that behaves like the Euclidean distance on pairs of
I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
having the same two first components.

Definition 2.6

(
Distance-like function
\dist
)
.

Let

\mathcal{W}
be the subset of pairs of elements of
I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
whose two first components coincides. A
distance-like function
is a function
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
with values in
[0,+\infty]
, defined on a suitable symmetric subset of pairs of
I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
containing
\mathcal{W}
, that coincides with the Euclidean distance on
\mathcal{W}
, i.e., for all
(s,z)\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

\dist((s,z,v_{1}),(s,z,v_{2}))=|v_{2}-v_{1}|.

(2.16)

For all
(s,z,v)\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
A\subset I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we set

\dist((s,z,v),A):=\inf\{\dist((s,z,v),\omega):\,\omega\in A\}.

(2.17)

Here are some examples of distance-like functions.

Example 2.7

(
u
-distance, Euclidean distance and infinity-distance)
.

AA

•

We shall denote by
\dist_{e}
the usual

in
I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

•

\dist_{u}
is the function defined on the pairs of points
\omega_{i}=(s,z,v_{i})\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n},i=1,2
with
the same first two components

4.1
A fundamental Lemma

The proof of Theorem
3.2
relies on the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
7
,
24
]

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a bounded set and let
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
be a distance-like function. Assume that:

a)

There is
{\lambda}>0
such that
\Lambda
is bounded on the subsets of
[0,T]\times\mathcal{K}\times B_{\lambda}
that are well-inside
\Dom(\Lambda)
w.r.t.
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
;

b)

There is
\nu_{0}>0
such that
\Lambda
is bounded on
([0,T]\times\mathcal{K}\times B_{\nu_{0}})\cap\Dom(\Lambda)
.

Let, for any
(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda)
,
P(s,z,v)\in\partial_{\mu}\Big(\Lambda\Big(s,z,\dfrac{{v}}{\mu}\Big){\mu}\Big)_{{\mu}=1}
. Then:

i)

P
is bounded on the bounded subsets of
I\times\mathcal{K}\times B_{\lambda}
that are well-inside
\Dom(\Lambda)
w.r.t.
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
;

ii)

For all
\rho>0
,

-\infty<\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in I,z\in\mathcal{K},|v|\leq\lambda\\
(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda)\\
\dist((s,z,v),(\Dom{\Lambda})^{c})\geq\rho\end{subarray}}P(s,z,v).

(4.1)

iii)

There is
\nu_{0}>0
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in I,z\in\mathcal{K},|v|\geq\nu_{0}\\
(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}P(s,z,v)<+\infty.

(4.2)

The proof of Lemma
4.1
follows narrowly the arguments given in
[
23
, Lemma 4.18, Proposition 4.24]
and the new arguments involved in
[
7
, Proposition 3.15]
in a different framework; we give the full details due to its importance in the proof of Theorem
3.2
for the convenience of the reader.

Proof.

We will use the fact that
P(s,z,v)=\Lambda(s,z,v)-Q(s,z,v)
, for some
Q(s,z,v)\in\partial_{r}\Lambda(s,z,ru)_{r=1}
.

i)
Let
(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda)
and
Q(s,z,v)\in\partial_{\mu}\Lambda(s,z,ru)_{\mu=1}
. Suppose that, for some
\rho>0
,
z\in\mathcal{K}
,
|v|\leq\lambda
and
\dist((s,z,v),(\Dom(\Lambda))^{c})\geq\rho
. The fact that
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
is a distance-like function implies that

\dist\left(\left(s,z,v+\dfrac{\rho}{2\lambda}v\right),(\Dom(\Lambda))^{c}\right)\geq\dfrac{\rho}{2}.

(4.3)

Assuming that

\partial_{r}\Lambda(s,z,ru)_{r=1}\not=\emptyset

(4.4)

we obtain

\Lambda\left(s,z,v+\dfrac{\rho}{2\lambda}v\right)-\Lambda(s,z,v)\geq\dfrac{\rho}{2\lambda}Q(s,z,v).

(4.5)

The boundedness assumption of
\Lambda
implies that
Q(s,z,v)
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
\lambda
and
\rho
.
Similarly, from

\Lambda\left(s,z,v-\dfrac{\rho}{2\lambda}v\right)-\Lambda(s,z,v)\geq-\dfrac{\rho}{2C}Q(s,z,v),

(4.6)

we deduce an upper bound for
Q
.

ii)
The set

\{(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda):\,z\in\mathcal{K},|v|\leq\lambda,\dist((s,z,v),(\Dom{\Lambda})^{c})\geq\rho\}

(4.7)

is contained in
[0,T]\times\mathcal{K}\times B_{\lambda}
and is well-inside
\Dom(\Lambda)
.
The claim follows immediately from
i)
.

iii)
Let
(s,z,v)\in\Dom(\Lambda)
with
z\in\mathcal{K}
and
|v|\geq{\nu_{0}},v\in\mathcal{U}
.
The assumption that
\Dom(\Lambda)
is star-shaped in the control variable implies that

\Big(s,z,{\nu_{0}}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)\in\Dom(\Lambda)

(4.8)

and thus

\Lambda\Big(s,z,{\nu_{0}}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)-\Lambda(s,z,v)\geq Q(s,z,v)\Big(\dfrac{{\nu_{0}}}{|v|}-1\Big),

(4.9)

from which we deduce that

\Lambda(s,z,v)-Q(s,z,v)\leq\Lambda\Big(s,z,{\nu_{0}}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)-\dfrac{{\nu_{0}}}{|v|}Q(s,z,v).

(4.10)

The assumptions imply that
\Lambda\Big(s,z,{\nu_{0}}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)\leq C_{1}(\mathcal{K},\nu_{0})
for some constant
C_{1}(\mathcal{K},\nu_{0})
depending only on
\mathcal{K},\nu_{0}
.
We now provide un upper bound for
-Q(s,z,v)
. Since
\Big(s,z,\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)\in\Dom(\Lambda)
, then

\Lambda\Big(s,z,\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)-\Lambda(s,z,v)\geq Q(s,z,v)\left(\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2|v|}-1\right),

(4.11)

so that the fact that
\Lambda
is bounded from below by
-d
gives

\displaystyle-Q(s,z,v)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\left(1-\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2|v|}\right)}\left[\Lambda\Big(s,z,\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)-\Lambda(s,z,v)\right]

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left[\Lambda\Big(s,z,\dfrac{\nu_{0}}{2}\dfrac{v}{|v|}\Big)+d\right]\leq C_{2}(\mathcal{K},\nu_{0})

for some constant
C_{2}(\mathcal{K},\nu_{0})
depending only on
\mathcal{K}
and
\nu_{0}
.
It follows from (
4.11
) – (
4.12
) that the right-hand side of (
4.10
) is bounded above by a constant depending only on
\mathcal{K}
and
\nu_{0}
.
∎

4.2
Change of variables and approximations

We shall often make use of the following change of variables formula for Lebesgue integrals.

Proposition 4.2

(
Change of variables for Lebesgue integrals
)
.

[
3
, Corollary 3.16]

Let
f\geq 0
be measurable and
\gamma:I\to I
be bijective, absolutely continuous with
\gamma^{\prime}>0
on
I
. Then, for every
A\subset I
,

f\in L^{1}(A)\Leftrightarrow(f\circ\gamma)\gamma^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\gamma^{-1}(A))
and

\int_{A}f(s)\,ds=\int_{\gamma^{-1}(A)}f(\gamma(\tau))\gamma^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau.

(4.13)

The following approximation argument will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(I)
and
(\varphi_{\nu})_{\nu}
be a sequence of bijiective, absolutely continuous functions
\varphi_{\nu}:I\to I
with, for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
:

•

\varphi_{\nu}^{\prime}>0
on
I
,

•

Lipschitz inverse
\psi_{\nu}
;

•

(\|\psi_{\nu}\|_{\infty})_{\nu}
bounded;

•

\varphi_{\nu}(t)\to t
uniformly.

Then

f\circ\varphi_{\nu}\to f\text{ in }L^{1}(I)
.

Proof.

Consider a sequence
(g_{m})_{m}
of smooth functions on
I
such that
g_{m}\to f
in
L^{1}(I)
. For each
m,\nu
in
{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\|f\circ\varphi_{\nu}-f\|_{1}\leq\|f\circ\varphi_{\nu}-g_{m}\circ\varphi_{\nu}\|_{1}+\|g_{m}\circ\varphi_{\nu}-g_{m}\|_{1}+\|g_{m}-f\|_{1}.

(4.14)

Clearly, for each
m
we have
g_{m}\circ\varphi_{\nu}\to g_{m}
uniformly as
\nu\to+\infty
.
Moreover, from Proposition
4.2
, the change of variable
\tau=\varphi_{\nu}(s)
gives

\displaystyle\|f\circ\varphi_{\nu}-g_{m}\circ\varphi_{\nu}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{I}|f(\tau)-g_{m}(\tau)|\psi_{\nu}^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f-g_{m}\|_{1},

forv a suitable constant
C
. The conclusion follows.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.2
ensures that
y_{\nu}
is Lipschitz for big values of
\nu
and
(y_{\nu})_{\nu}
converges to
y
both in
W^{1,1}
and in energy. Let us check these facts directly.

•

Lipschitzianity.

For all
s\in[0,1]
we have

y_{\nu}^{\prime}(s):=\begin{cases}\nu\phantom{AAA}\text{ if }0\leq s<s_{0}(\nu)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}},\\
y^{\prime}\left(s-\dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}\right)=\dfrac{1}{3}\left(s-\dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}\right)^{-2/3}\text{ if }s_{0}(\nu)\leq s\leq 1.\end{cases}

(5.14)

Now, if
s\geq s_{0}(\nu)
,

s-\dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}\geq\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}
, whence

\|y_{\nu}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\nu
on
[0,1]
.

•

Convergence in
W^{1,1}
.

We have

\displaystyle\|y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s_{0}(\nu)}|y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\,ds+\int_{s_{0}(\nu)}^{1}|y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\,ds

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq y_{\nu}(s_{0}(\nu))+y(s_{0}(\nu))+\int_{s_{0}(\nu)}^{1}|y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\,ds.

Now,

y_{\nu}(s_{0}(\nu))+y(s_{0}(\nu))=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3\nu}}+\dfrac{1}{3^{1/6}\sqrt{\nu}}\to 0,

(5.16)

and, since
y_{\nu}^{\prime}(s)=y^{\prime}\left(s-\dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}\right)
on
[s_{0}(\nu),1]
and
y
is concave,

\displaystyle\int_{s_{0}(\nu)}^{1}|y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{0}(\nu)}^{1}(y_{\nu}^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\,ds

(5.17)

\displaystyle=(y_{\nu}(1)-y(1))-(y_{\nu}(s_{0}(\nu))-y(s_{0}(\nu))

\displaystyle=\left(\left(1-\dfrac{2}{3\sqrt{3}\nu^{3/2}}\right)^{1/3}-1\right)-\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3\nu}}-\dfrac{1}{3^{1/6}\sqrt{\nu}}\right)

tends to 0 as
\nu\to+\infty
.

•

Failure of the convergence in energy.

Since
\Lambda(s,y,y^{\prime})\notin L^{1}([0,1])
this part of the proof of Theorem
3.2
is not justified. Indeed, we have

F(y_{\nu})\geq\int_{0}^{1}(y_{\nu}^{3}(s)-s)^{2}(y_{\nu}^{\prime}(s))^{6}\,ds=\frac{68\nu^{3/2}}{945\sqrt{3}}\to+\infty

(5.18)

as
\nu\to+\infty
.

Remark 6.1
.

AAA

1.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
relies on the fact that, for some
\lambda>\dfrac{\|y\|_{1}}{T-t}
,
\rho>0
and

-\infty<\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in I,z\in y(I),|v|<\lambda\\

(6.2)

This property is called Growth Condition (M
{}_{B}^{t}

]
, and is compared in the quoted paper to other growth conditions.
In view of Lemma

, Hypotheses (
{\rm B}^{w}_{y,\Lambda}
) and (

) provide a sufficient condition for the validity of (

) – (

2.

Following the proof of
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
, it appears that the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
is still valid by replacing the radial convexity condition (A

) on the last variable of

with the existence, at any point

D_{v}\Lambda(s,z,v)

with respect to

of

). In this framework, however, (

) – (

) do not follow for free as in the convex case: one has to add some further regularity conditions on
\Lambda
w.r.t. the last variable, e.g., that

[
23

). Are there some other sufficient conditions, other than radial convexity or differentiability, that guarantee the validity of Condition (M
{}_{B}^{t}

3.

The conclusions of the paper may be easily extended to Lagrangians of the form
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\Lambda_{i}(s,z,v)\Psi_{i}(s,z)
, assuming that each pair

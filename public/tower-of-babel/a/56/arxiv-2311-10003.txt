Suppression of Chemotactic Singularity via Viscous Flow with Large Buoyancy
Zhongtian Hu

Abstract
In this work, we study the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes equation with low Reynolds number and subject to large buoyancy force. We show that for initial cell density with arbitrarily large mass (i.e. the L 1 L^{1} norm), the solution remains regular for all times in the regime of sufficiently large buoyancy and viscosity. The major blowup suppression mechanism is a norm-stabilizing property possessed by a “static problem,” where the full problem can be seen as a perturbation of this quasi-stationary model.

Introduction

We consider the parabolic-elliptic Keller-Segel equation in a periodic channel
\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times[0,\pi]
subject to the influence of the buoyancy-driven Navier-Stokes equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))=0,\\
\rho_{m}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho(t,x)dx,\\
\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\frac{1}{\re}\Delta u+\nabla p=\ra\rho(0,1)^{T},\\
\divv u=0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

We equip the system with initial data
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)
,
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
, where
\rho_{0}
is a nonnegative scalar function and
u_{0}
is a divergence-free vector field. We also consider the following set of boundary conditions:

\partial_{2}\rho=\nabla\rho\cdot n=0,\quad u_{2}=u\cdot n=0,\quad\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u=0,\quad\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.

(1.2)

Here,
\mathbb{T}:=[-\pi,\pi)
is the one dimensional torus, and a function

denotes the unit normal derivative along
\partial\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times\{0,\pi\}
;
\nabla^{\perp}
denotes the differential operator
(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})

The first equation in (
1.1
) is the classical parabolic–elliptic Keller-Segel equation with advection. This equation characterizes a population of bacteria with the density
\rho
that moves in response to an attractive chemical that the bacteria themselves secrete. Specifically, the chemical-induced aggregation effect is modeled by the term
\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))
, indicating a scenario where chemicals homogenize much faster than the motion of micro-organisms. Here,
-\Delta_{N}
denotes homogeneous Neumann Laplacian that represents the classical condition of zero chemical flux across boundary
\partial\Omega
. Furthermore, chemotaxis usually takes place in ambient viscous fluids, which are classically modeled by Navier-Stokes equations with velocity
u
and pressure
p
. In nature, the micro-organisms and the fluid can interact through various means (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
). The main interaction on which we focus is the coupling by buoyancy, which originates from the variation of bacterial density in the domain. Mathematically, such interaction appears in the fluid equation through the forcing term
\ra\rho(0,1)^{T}
, where
\ra
denotes the Rayleigh number measuring relative buoyancy strength due to density variation.

In this work, we focus on the study of chemotaxis-fluid interaction in a canonical domain
\mathbb{T}\times[0,\pi]
. We remark that previous studies classically consider domains without boundaries, e.g.
\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, in order to study chemotaxis-fluid interaction in the bulk of fluid (see
[

,
24

,
25

). Our work, in contrast, intends to investigate the buoyancy effect along the vertical direction. From this perspective, it is interesting to consider a domain with a top and bottom boundary, and study the chemotaxis-fluid interaction via buoyancy when taking boundary effects into account. We also emphasize the boundary conditions that we insist for the rest of this work: on one hand, we assert the classical homogeneous Neumann boundary condition for the cell density. On the other hand, we impose the Lions boundary condition onto the fluid equation:
u\cdot n|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\omega|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. This particular boundary condition, as a special case of the more general Navier boundary condition, is prevalent in simulations of flows in the presence of rough boundaries, such as in hemodynamics. It was first introduced and rigorously studied by J.-L. Lions
[

and P.-L. Lions
[

. We refer the readers to works such as

It is also curious to understand the behavior of Keller-Segel equation under the influence of fluid advection, given the fact that most chemotactic processes take place in ambient fluid. The presence of fluid advection can bring complicated effects to chemotaxis. Among these effects, we would like to focus on the
regularization effects
induced by fluid advection. That is, we would like to understand how the transport term
u\cdot\nabla\rho
could prevent potential singular behaviors in (

In the regime of passive advection (i.e. the fluid velocity
u
is given and is not coupled to the Keller-Segel equation), Kiselev and Xu in
[

]

\rho_{0}

There also have been numerous attempts to investigate the regularity properties of the Keller-Segel equation coupling to active fluid models, and many of which address the global regularity of solutions to such coupled systems. We highlight that, among those results, either there is smallness assumption on initial data (e.g.

Recently in a series of works
[

,

]
by the author joining with Kiselev and Yao, they analyze how buoyancy effects in fluid equations suppress chemotactic singularities in a genuinely nonlinear setting. In
[

The authors demonstrated that a coupling with the porous media equation via an arbitrarily weak buoyancy constant suffices to arrest any potential chemotactic blowup. The key argument in
[

is a careful analysis of a potential energy and the coercive term
\|\partial_{1}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}
in its time derivative. The authors observed that this

norm has to be small, and induces an anisotropic mixing effect along
x_{1}

[

studies how Stokes-Boussinesq flow with strong buoyancy suppresses blowup of a Keller-Segel equation equipped with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. A rather soft argument in
[
32

shows that the flow quenches the
L^{2}
norm of cell density to be sufficiently small in the regime of large buoyancy.

The other motivation of studying the regularization by active fluid advection in the Keller-Segel equation is its connection to the global regularity of other equations that potentially have singular behaviors, such as some fluid equations. In fact, such regularizing phenomenon is the main factor that competes with main blowup mechanisms in some of the most classical fluid equations. For 3D Euler equations, a lack of/a weakened advection term is sufficient to induce finite-time singularities, see
[
15
,
21
]
. The same is also observed in 3D Navier-Stokes equations, see
[

,
30
]
. The crucial role played by advection stands out even more when one considers certain 1D models capturing Euler and Navier-Stokes dynamics, see
[
11
,
37
]
. The Keller-Segel equation much resembles these fluid equations since it is also a critical equation that exhibits potential singularities. Hence, studying the regularization by fluid advection in Keller-Segel equation contributes to a more substantial understanding in the problem of global existence for many fluid equations.

In this work, we plan to investigate (
1.1
) in the case where the fluid has large viscosity and exerts significant buoyancy onto the bacteria. We remark that not only micro-organisms generically live in viscous environment (see
[
50
]
), but also intriguing biological phenomena occur in a viscous fluid and in a buoyancy-dominating regime. For example,
[
51
]
studies an interesting phenomenon called chemotactic Boycott effect in this regime, which describes the sedimentation process of both bacteria and fluid. In numerical experiments, the canonical choice of the coupling fluid is the Navier-Stokes flow with parameters
\ra\sim 10^{6}
and
\re^{-1}\sim 10^{3}
. Hence, while the chemotaxis model used in
[

is different from (

\ra\gg\frac{1}{\re}\gg 1
is still highly relevant in the study of chemotaxis in viscous environment. Moreover, from an analytical perspective, this is also a first step to showing the blowup suppression by Navier-Stokes flow with only large buoyancy and moderate viscosity.

To make the problem more amenable to analysis, it is convenient to write
B=\re^{-1}
and

u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\omega|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

To sum up, we will work with the following equivalent, yet more convenient form of (

\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))=0,\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

\partial_{2}\rho\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.4c)

\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0,\;u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

1.2
Main Strategy of Proof

In this section, we would like to first emphasize the main difficulty of proving Theorem
1.1
, after which we briefly sketch the idea of proving this main result.

The main obstacle is that, given the appearance of time derivative and advection term in the fluid equation (
1.4b
), the problem suffers from a loss of explicit velocity law, which directly connects density
\rho
and fluid velocity
u
. In general, such loss prevents us from obtaining precise control of
u
in a way similar to
[
33
]
. In the parameter regime specified in Theorem
1.1
, however, we will show that (
1.4
) is a suitable perturbation of a static problem (
1.6
), which actually has a velocity law that can be analyzed with tools developed in
[
33
]
. Thus, we arrange the proof into the following steps.

Step 1. Study of Static Problem (
1.6
).
We first discuss the intuition of deriving the static problem (
1.6
). We start with dividing on both sides of (
1.4b
) by
B
. This yields

\frac{1}{B}(\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u)-\Delta u+\frac{1}{B}\nabla p=g\rho(0,1)^{T},\quad\divv u=0.

In the regime of large
B
, it is natural to treat the term
\frac{1}{B}(\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u)
perturbatively. In fact, it is reasonable to guess that the following velocity law dominates the velocity evolution:

-\Delta u+\nabla P=g\rho(0,1)^{T},\;\divv u=0,

(1.5)

where we write
P=B^{-1}p
. We remark that the scaling
B^{-1}
in front of the pressure is irrelevant due to incompressibility. This motivates us to first understand the following system, which we will later refer to as the
static problem
:

\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))=0,

(1.6a)

-\Delta u+\nabla p=g\rho(0,1)^{T},\quad\divv u=0,

(1.6b)

\partial_{2}\rho\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0.

(1.6c)

We remark that we do not specify the initial datum for
u
. Indeed, the problem (
1.6
) is closed by itself, due to a remarkable property of (
1.6b
)-(
1.6c
) that one may reduce them to the following compact form:

u=-g\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}\partial_{1}\rho,

(1.7)

thanks to Proposition
2.2
. Here
-\Delta_{D}
denotes homogeneous Dirichlet Laplacian, and
(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}=(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\circ(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}
. This renders no degree of freedom over choosing the initial datum for
u
in problem (
1.6
). We will work with the more convenient form (
1.7
) instead of the original formulation.

We also remark that the static problem (
1.6
) is reminiscent of the Keller-Segel equation coupling to Darcy’s law studied in
[
33
]
, whose velocity law is given by:

u=g\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho.

(1.8)

In
[
33
]
, the authors exploited an anisotropic mixing effect that is sufficient to prevent chemotactic blowup when
\rho
achieves a large
L^{2}
norm. However, the velocity law (
1.7
) yields a more regular fluid flow. The extra regularity induces a weaker mixing effect comparing to (
1.8
). To remedy this issue, we introduce a large
g
so that the flow has large amplitude to generate sufficient mixing effect. This treatment differs our work from
[
33
]
. In the large amplitute setting, we can achieve a crucial norm stabilizing property of (
1.6
). That is, the
L^{2}
norm of
\rho
stabilizes around a level
N_{0}
, whose size only depends on the initial datum
\rho_{0}
in the regime of
g
sufficiently large. The abovementioned property is formalized in the statement below:

Proposition 1.3

(Norm-Stabilizing Property)
.

Consider problem (
1.6
) with nonnegative initial datum
\rho_{0}\in H^{1}
. Assume that
t_{0}\geq 0
is inside the lifespan of the unique regular local solution
\rho(t,x)
. Then there exist
N_{0}=N_{0}(\rho_{m},\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}})
, a time
T_{*}=T_{*}(N_{0},\rho_{m})
, and
g_{0}:=g_{0}(N_{0},\rho_{m})>0
, such that the following statement holds: if
\|\rho(t_{0})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq{\frac{N_{0}}{2}}
, then for all
g\geq g_{0}
, the regular solution
\rho(t,x)
can be continued in interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+T_{*}]
with estimate

\sup_{t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T_{*}]}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq{N_{0}}.

(1.9)

Moreover, there exists a time instance
T\in\left[t_{0}+\frac{T_{*}}{2},t_{0}+T_{*}\right]
such that

\|\rho(T)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{N_{0}}{8}.

(1.10)

We will devote Section
4
to the proof of this key result. As a direct consequence, we will also see that the static problem (
1.6
) is globally regular.

Step 2. Continuation Argument.
In fact, one may view Proposition
1.3
as a damping mechanism induced by the static problem (
1.6
). Keeping in mind that the full problem (
1.4
) is a perturbation of (
1.6
), we employ a bootstrap argument to show that the solution to the full problem is

-close to the solution of the static problem for all times. Fixing initial condition
\rho_{0}
and

, we choose large

as in Proposition

. Define
\mathcal{T}_{0}
to be the largest time such that the following bootstrap assumption holds:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 9N_{0}.

Our goal is to prove the following improved estimate:

Proposition 1.4
.

Consider initial data
\rho_{0}\in H^{1}
that is nonnegative and

. There exists a couple
(g_{1},B_{1})=(g_{1}(\rho_{m},\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}),B_{1}(\rho_{m},\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{0}\|_{1}))

The main ingredient of this step is based on various nonlinear estimates pertaining to Navier-Stokes equation equipped with Lions boundary conditions. We devote Section

Step 3. Closing the Proof of Theorem

In the final step, we finish the proof of Theorem

.

2.1
Notation and Conventions

We will denote the usual
L^{p}
norm by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
or
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\cdot)}
. The second notation will be adopted if we would like to emphasize the domain. Similarly, we will denote Sobolev

\|\cdot\|_{s}
for

We define the space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to be the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respect to norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, and consider its dual space
H^{-1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the following equivalence is standard:

\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}=C\left(\int_{\Omega}f(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}fdx\right)^{1/2},

where
C
is a constant that only depends on domain
\Omega
. We also recall that
\Delta_{D}
denotes homogeneous Dirichlet Laplacian. We simply regard
C=1
as the exact value of this constant is inessential.

Recall the mean of
\rho
on
\Omega:

\rho_{m}(t)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho(t,x)\,dx.
We remark that
\rho_{m}(t)
is nonnegative and a conserved quantity along evolution given the initial datum
\rho_{0}
being nonnegative, due to a parabolic maximum principle and the divergence structure of the Keller-Segel equation (see Proposition
4.1
for the proof of this fact). Thus, we will omit the time dependence of the mean and write
\rho_{m}
.

We also consider the following decomposition to any function
\rho\in L^{2}(\Omega)
that would play a crucial role in our analysis of the static problem: we write
\rho=\bar{\rho}+\tilde{\rho}
, where

\bar{\rho}(x_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1},\;\tilde{\rho}=\rho-\bar{\rho}.

Note that
\bar{\rho}
is exactly the projection onto the zeroth mode corresponding to direction
x_{1}
, and
\tilde{\rho}
the orthogonal complement. We remark that
\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}
and
\tilde{\rho}
are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

\int_{\Omega}(\bar{\rho}(x_{2})-\rho_{m})\tilde{\rho}(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}=0.

One can connect the 1D and 2D
L^{2}
norm for
\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}
by a simple relation
\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=2\pi\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}([0,\pi])}^{2}
. In the rest of the paper, we will only use the 1D
L^{2}
norm for
\bar{\rho}
.
We always refer to the 1D
L^{2}
norm when we write
\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}
.

We will denote a universal constant in the upper bounds by uppercase letters, and that in lower bounds by lowercase letters. Constants without subscripts, i.e.
c,C,\mathcal{C}
, are subject to change from line to line. Constants with subscripts, i.e.

\Omega

2.2
Steady Stokes Equation with Lions Boundary Condition

In this subsection, we introduce suitable functional-analytic settings for our study of Stokes equation equipped with Lions boundary condition. We shall first study the following static Stokes equation and investigate its well-posedness:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\nabla p=f,&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\divv u=0,&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(2.1)

In particular, we will derive suitable elliptic estimates similar to those satisfied by the classical Stokes operator that corresponds to no-slip boundary condition for velocity (see
[
16
]
).

We introduce the following function spaces following the spirit of
[

,
12

H=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\;:\;\divv v=0,\;\int_{\Omega}v_{1}(x)dx=0\},

where we equip

with

topology,

with topology induced by the inner product
(u,v)_{V}=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{i}v_{j}dx
, and
W
with
H^{2}
topology. For

, the following Poincaré inequality holds:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u\in V
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},

(2.2)

where

is a positive constant that only depends on the domain
\Omega
.

Moreover, we have the following well-posedness and regularity result concerning (
2.1
):

Proposition 2.2
.

Assuming
f\in H
, then (
2.1
) admits a unique solution

with estimate

\|u\|_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.3)

In fact,

assumes the following explicit formula:

u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}(\partial_{2}f_{1}-\partial_{1}f_{2}),

(2.4)

where
\nabla^{\perp}:=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})
. In addition, if

,

\|u\|_{s+2}\leq C\|f\|_{s}.

(2.5)

We delay the proofs of the Propositions above to the appendix.

In this section, we perform a detailed analysis of (

4.1
Key

As a first step, we establish several key energy estimates concerning the system (

4.1.1
Estimates for a General Keller-Segel Equation with Advection

In this section, we derive appropriate estimates for the Keller-Segel equation advected by a general incompressible flow
u
, which may or may not depend on the cell density
\rho
itself:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))=0,\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0,\;\partial_{2}\rho|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{split}

(4.1)

Here,
u
is a smooth, divergence-free vector field that satisfies no-flux boundary condition (i.e.
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
). We allow such generality in (
4.1
) so that all results in this subsection hold for both the static problem (
1.6
) and the full problem (
1.4
). This is because an incompressible, viscous flow satisfying Lions boundary condition necessarily satisfies all requirements prescribed in (
4.1
).

Our first result reveals that (
4.1
) conserves mass.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\rho(t,x)
is a regular solution to (
4.1
) on
[0,T]
with
u
being
C^{1}
and divergence-free. Then
\rho(t,x)\geq 0
and
\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

First,
\rho(t,x)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
comes from the facts that
\rho_{0}\geq 0
and parabolic maximum principle. To prove the conservation of mass, we use nonnegativity of
\rho(t,x)
to obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho(t,x)dx=\int_{\Omega}(-u\cdot\nabla\rho+\Delta\rho-\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})))(t,x)dx=0,

where we integrated by parts and used that
u
is divergence-free,
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, and
\partial_{2}\rho|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.
∎

The next result, originally proved in
[
33
]
, gives two variants of
L^{2}
energy estimates of (
4.1
), which play a major role in establishing the norm-stabilizing property of (
1.6
). We point out that
[
33
, Proposition 4.2 and Corollary 4.3]
are proved where

is governed by Darcy’s law (
1.8
). However, their proofs only used that

is divergence-free and satisfies no-flux boundary condition. Hence, the proof of the result below follows verbatim from that of
[
33
, Proposition 4.2 and Corollary 4.3]
.

Proposition 4.2

(Essentially Proposition 4.2, Corollary 4.3 of
[
33
]
)
.

Assume
\rho(t,x)
to be a regular, nonnegative solution to (
4.1
) on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
for some
t_{0}\geq 0
and
T>0
. Then for any
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+T)
, there exists a constant
C_{0}
depending only on the domain
\Omega
such that

\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{0}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+2\rho_{m}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.2)

Furthermore, if
\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\rho_{m}/4
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
, then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
depending only on the domain
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}+C_{1}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

(4.4)

for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
.

The next proposition shows that one can upgrade the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
control of
\rho
to an
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
control:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\rho(t,x)
be a regular solution to (
4.1
) on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
for some
t_{0}\geq 0
and
N\geq\max(1,\rho_{m})
be an arbitrary number. If
\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2N
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
, then we also have
\sup_{t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T}\|\rho(t,\cdot)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CN^{2}
, where
C
is a domain dependent constant.

Proof.

The proof follows verbatim from that of
[
40
, Proposition 9.1]
, where it is handled for
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. We can do this since all integrations by parts do not introduce any boundary terms due to the Neumann boundary condition that
\rho
satisfies as well as the no-flux boundary condition satisfied by
u
.
∎

Finally, we establish a corollary shows a doubling time for the
L^{2}
norm
\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose
\rho(t,x)
is a regular solution to (
4.1
) on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
for some
t_{0}\geq 0
,
T>0
, and
N>0
being an arbitrary number. If
\|\rho(t_{0})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq N
, then there exists a time
T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})=\frac{\min(N^{-1},\rho_{m}^{-1})}{8(1+C_{0})}=:c_{0}\min(N^{-1},\rho_{m}^{-1})
such that

\sup_{t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2N,

(4.5)

and

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq 2N.

(4.6)

Here,
C_{0}
is fixed as in Proposition
4.2
.

Proof.

To show (
4.5
), we employ a standard barrier argument: suppose that there exists a time
\tau\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})]
such that
\|\rho(\tau)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2N
, and
\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<2N
for any
t\in[t_{0},\tau)
. Integrating (
4.2
) from
t_{0}
to
\tau
, we have

\displaystyle 2N

\displaystyle=\|\rho(\tau)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq N+2\rho_{m}\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dx+C_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{\tau}\|\rho(s)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}ds

\displaystyle\leq N+4\rho_{m}NT_{*}+4N^{2}C_{0}T_{*}<2N,

where we used the definition of
T_{*}
in the final inequality. However, this yields a contradiction.

To show (
4.6
), we integrate (
4.2
) from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})
, which yields:

\displaystyle\|\rho(t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m}))

\displaystyle-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq N+2\rho_{m}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+C_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}dt

\displaystyle\leq N+2\rho_{m}(2N)T_{*}+C_{0}(4N^{2})T_{*}<N+\frac{N}{2(1+C_{0})}+\frac{C_{0}N}{2(1+C_{0})}<2N.

The proof is thus complete.
∎

4.1.2
Estimates for Static Problem (
1.6
)

Now, we turn to some estimates concerning the system (
1.6
), which crucially exploit the structure of the Biot-Savart-type relation (
1.7
). As our first step, we prove the following key estimate which displays the smallness of a mixing-type norm in the regime of large parameter
g
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\rho(t,x)
be a regular solution to equation (
1.6
) on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T]
for some
t_{0}\geq 0
and
T>0
. Let
N>0
be an arbitrary number. If
\|\rho(t_{0})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq N
, the following bound holds:

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m})g^{-1}.

(4.7)

Proof.

On one hand, we compute that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}\rho(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}\partial_{1}\rho dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{1}(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}\partial_{1}\rho dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{2}dxdt.

(4.8)

Note that we have used the self-adjointness of
(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}
in the second equality, integration by parts in the third equality, and (
1.7
) in the last equality. On the other hand, we test the
\rho
-equation of (
1.6
) by
x_{2}
, which yields:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{2}dx&=-\int_{\Omega}x_{2}(u\cdot\nabla\rho)dx=\int_{\Omega}x_{2}\left(\partial_{t}\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))\right)dx=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}I_{i},\end{split}

(4.9)

where the first equality follows from integration by parts and no-flux boundary condition satisfied by
u
. Integrating in time from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})
and using Proposition
4.1
as well as the bound (
4.6
), we estimate time integrals of
I_{1},I_{2}
by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}I_{1}dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}x_{2}(\rho(t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m}))-\rho(t_{0}))dx\right|\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}{\leq C\rho_{m}},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}I_{2}dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{2}\rho dxdt\right|\leq CT_{*}(N,\rho_{m})^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq C.

Here, we used the definition of
T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})
and (
4.6
) in the final inequality of
I_{2}
estimate. To estimate the term
I_{3}
in (
4.9
), we use Sobolev embedding, elliptic estimates, and interpolation inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}+{\sqrt{2\pi}}\rho_{m})\|\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})\|_{W^{1,6/5}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}+{\sqrt{2\pi}}\rho_{m})\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho_{m}^{2/3}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/3}(\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}+\rho_{m})

\displaystyle={C(\rho_{m}^{5/3}N^{1/6}+\rho_{m}^{2/3}N^{2/3}).}

Then we may estimate the time integral of
A_{3}
by

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}I_{3}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\min(N^{-1},\rho_{m}^{-1})(\rho_{m}^{5/3}N^{1/6}+\rho_{m}^{2/3}N^{2/3})

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\rho_{m}^{2/3}+\rho_{m}^{-1/3},&N\leq 1,\\
\rho_{m}^{5/3}N^{-5/6}+\rho_{m}^{2/3}N^{-1/3},&N>1\end{cases}\leq C(\rho_{m}).

Collecting the estimates above yields:

\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{2}dxdt\right|\leq C(\rho_{m}),

(4.10)

Therefore by (
4.1.2
):

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}(N,\rho_{m})}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m})g^{-1},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Note that the norm
\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
could be viewed as a quantity that encodes information on mixing. This is because, formally, this quantity has the same scaling as the classical mix-norm
\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}
(see
[
34
]
for example). Since generally smallness of
\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{H^{-1}}
induces enhanced dissipation by creating large gradients, it is tempting to uncover a similar effect by exploiting the smallness of
\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
in our setting. In order to achieve this goal, we follow the approach taken in
[
33
]
, which studies a similar quantity
\|\partial_{1}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}
. The technical ingredient that we need is a Nash’s inequality with improved coefficient: let us recall the following result from
[
33
]
, which is valid for any function
\rho\in L^{2}([s,r],H^{1}(\Omega))
,
0\leq s<r\leq\infty
:

Proposition 4.6

(Corollary 6.2 of
[
33
]
)
.

Assume that for some
K>0
, there is

\int_{s}^{r}\|\partial_{1}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}dt\leq K^{-1}\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

(4.11)

Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
such that

\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq CK^{-1/4}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\nabla\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\right)^{1/2}.

(4.12)

Note that the original statement in Corollary 6.2 of
[
33
]
only considers the case when
K\geq 1
. However, one could extend the result for any
K>0
in a rather trivial way: for
K\in(0,1)
, we simply invoke the original Nash’s inequality for
\tilde{\rho}
without using the assumption (
4.11
). The fact that
K^{-1/4}>1
immediately yields the result.

With the proposition above, we may conclude the following crucial lemma, which precisely characterizes the creation of large gradients when the norm
\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
is small in an appropriate sense.

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix
g>0
. Assume that there is some constant
C>0
so that

\int_{s}^{r}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq Cg^{-9/10}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{2}.

(4.13)

Then the following estimate holds:

\int_{s}^{r}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\geq\tilde{C}g^{9/50}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}dt\right)^{-4/5}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{8/5},

(4.14)

for some constant
\tilde{C}>0
that only depends on the domain
\Omega
and constant
C
appearing in (
4.13
).

Proof.

Using definition of
H^{-1}_{0}
norm and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\|\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}dx\leq\|\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}},

where we used the fact that
\partial_{1}\rho=\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}
. Integrating the above inequality from
s
to
r
and applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality again, we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{r}\|\partial_{1}\tilde{\rho}\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{1/2}g^{-9/20}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\int_{s}^{r}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

Then an application of Proposition
4.6
via replacing
N
by
C^{-1/2}g^{9/20}\left(\int_{s}^{r}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{-1/2}>0
implies (
4.14
) after some rearrangements of the inequality.
∎

4.2
Norm-Stabilizing Property of the Static Problem

With the
a priori
estimates set up in previous sections, we are ready to prove the norm-stabilizing property (i.e. Proposition
1.3
), from which the global existence of the static problem (
1.6
) follows.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

We first fix our choice of
N_{0}
and
T_{*}
as follows: choose
N_{0}
so that

\displaystyle N_{0}\geq\max\{1,2\rho_{m},2\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\},

(4.15)

\displaystyle N_{0}\geq\frac{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}(15/8+2C_{1}c_{0})}{4c_{0}},

(4.16)

and set
T_{*}=c_{0}\min(2N_{0}^{-1},\rho_{m})=2c_{0}N_{0}^{-1}
. Recall that
C_{0},C_{1}
are universal constants fixed in Proposition
4.2
;
c_{0}
is also a universal constant fixed in Corollary
4.4
. Moreover, we fix
g\geq g_{0}(N_{0},\rho_{m})
, where the threshold
g_{0}
will be chosen sufficiently large in the course of the proof.

We first observe that by Corollary
4.4
, given
\|\rho(t_{0})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=N_{0}/2
, we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq N_{0},

(4.17)

and in particular (
1.9
) holds.

We thus focus on the proof of (
1.10
). Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>N_{0}/8
for any
t\in\left[t_{0}+\frac{T_{*}}{2},t_{0}+T_{*}\right]
. For
g\geq g_{0}
, we discuss the following dichotomy:

Case 1: Small
\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}
.

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq c_{0}g^{-1/20}.

(4.18)

In this case, we first observe that due to the contradiction assumption, there is a lower bound:

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\geq\frac{T_{*}N_{0}}{16}=\frac{c_{0}}{16}.

Combining with (
4.18
) and using orthogonality between
\bar{\rho}
and
\tilde{\rho}
, we have

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\geq\left(\frac{1}{16}-g^{-1/20}\right)c_{0}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{32},

(4.19)

if we choose

g_{0}\geq 32^{20}.

(4.20)

On the other hand, an application of Hölder’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{T_{*}}{2}\right)^{2/3}\left(\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}dt\right)^{1/3}.

Combining the inequality above with (
4.19
), we have

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}dt\geq\frac{4}{T_{*}^{2}}\left(\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{3}\geq\frac{4c_{0}^{3}}{32^{3}T_{*}^{2}}=\frac{4c_{0}N_{0}^{2}}{32^{3}}.

(4.21)

Now, we shall invoke energy estimate (
4.4
) and integrate the differential inequality from
T_{*}/2
to
T_{*}
. Note that we can do this since
\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>N_{0}/8\geq\rho_{m}/4
for any
t\in\left[t_{0}+\frac{T_{*}}{2},t_{0}+T_{*}\right]
by our contradiction assumption, and our choice of
N_{0}
in (
4.15
). Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\rho(t_{0}+T_{*})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho(t_{0}+T_{*}/2)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\bar{\rho}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{6}dt+C_{1}\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2N_{0}-\frac{4c_{0}}{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}N_{0}^{2}+C_{1}\sup_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2\leq t\leq t_{0}+T_{*}/2}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2N_{0}-\frac{4c_{0}}{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}N_{0}^{2}+2C_{1}c_{0}N_{0}g^{-1/20}\leq 2N_{0}-\frac{4c_{0}}{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}N_{0}^{2}+2C_{1}c_{0}N_{0}.

Here, we used (
4.21
) in the second inequality, (
4.18
) in the third inequality, and
g\geq g_{0}>1
in the last inequality.
Then by (
4.16
), a straightforward computation yields

\|\rho(t_{0}+T_{*})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2N_{0}-\frac{4c_{0}}{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}}N_{0}^{2}+2C_{1}c_{0}N_{0}<\frac{N_{0}}{8},

which is a contradiction to our assumption.

Case 2: Large
\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}
.

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt>c_{0}g^{-1/20}.

(4.22)

In this case, we first use estimate (
4.7
) to obtain that

\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\partial_{1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m})g^{-1}<C(\rho_{m})c_{0}^{-2}g^{-9/10}\left(\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{2}.

Then, we can invoke Lemma
4.7
with
C=C(\rho_{m})c_{0}^{-2}
to conclude that

\begin{split}\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt&\geq C(\rho_{m})g^{9/50}\left(\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}dt\right)^{-4/5}\left(\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\right)^{8/5}\\
&\geq C(\rho_{m})T_{*}^{-4/5}g^{9/50}g^{-2/25}\geq C(\rho_{m})N_{0}^{4/5}g^{1/10}.\end{split}

(4.23)

Here, we applied Proposition
4.1
, (
4.22
) in the second inequality, and used the definition of
T_{*}
in the final inequality. Now choose
g_{0}
sufficiently large that

C(\rho_{m})N_{0}^{4/5}g^{1/10}>N_{0}.

(4.24)

Then together with (
4.23
), we would arrive at
\int_{t_{0}+T_{*}/2}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt>N_{0}
. However, this contradicts the
a priori
estimate (
4.17
).

Up until this point, both cases in the dichotomy lead to a contradiction and we have completed the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

For arbitrary nonnegative initial data
\rho_{0}\in H^{1}
, there exists
g_{0}=g_{0}(\rho_{m})>0
sufficiently large that the solution of the static problem (
1.6
) is globally regular for all

.

Proof.

Let
g_{0},N_{0}
be chosen as in Proposition
1.3
(and therefore
T_{*}

t_{0}\geq 0
be the first time such that
\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=N_{0}

t_{0}=\infty

\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq N_{0}
for all
t\geq 0
as
N_{0}\geq\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Hence by the regularity criterion stated in Theorem
3.3
, we are already done. We may then suppose that
t_{0}<\infty

1.3

∎

5.1
Preliminary Estimates

For the rest of this section, we will consider a solution
(\rho,u)
to system (
1.4
) with initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
. We begin with several preliminary estimates for
\rho
,
u
, and some of their derivatives. Such estimates will be instrumental in later estimates of
\partial_{t}u
and
u\cdot\nabla u
under the bootstrap assumption. First, we introduce a time-integrated bound for
\nabla\rho
on any time interval of scale
T_{*}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For arbitrary
t_{0}\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}]
and given bootstrap assumption (
1.11
), we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq 18N_{0}.

(5.2)

Here, we recall that
[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}]
is the bootstrap horizon.

Proof.

This lemma follows directly from applying Corollary
4.4
with
M=9N_{0}
.
∎

We then give an estimate of
u
that is uniform over the time interval
[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
, which is slightly longer than the bootstrap horizon. In the mean time, we also show a bound on
\nabla u
on an interval of scale
T_{*}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Given bootstrap assumption (
1.11
), the following estimate holds:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}.

(5.3)

Moreover, for arbitrary
t_{0}\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}]
, we have the following gradient bound:

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}

(5.4)

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
. Testing (
5.1b
) by
u
and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}u_{i}dx+B\int_{\Omega}u\Delta udx=Bg\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{2}dx\leq Bg\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.5)

To treat the second and third term on LHS above, we use
\divv u=0
and
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
to deduce that

\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}u_{i}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\partial_{j}|u|^{2}dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\divv u|u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{2}|u|^{2}\Big|_{x_{2}=0}^{x_{2}=\pi}dx_{1}=0.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u\Delta udx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{\pi}u_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{i}u_{j}dx_{2}dx_{1}=-\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{j}\partial_{2}u_{j}\Big|_{x_{2}=0}^{x_{2}=\pi}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=-\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}(u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}+u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2})\Big|_{x_{2}=0}^{x_{2}=\pi}dx_{1}=-\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the Lions boundary condition in the final equality. Combining the two identities above with (
5.5
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Bg\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}

(5.6)

On the other hand, we apply Poincaré inequality (
2.2
) and apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to (
5.6
), which yields the following differential inequality:

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{B}{C}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4CBg^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Further invoking Duhamel’s formula, we deduce from the above estimate that:

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq e^{-\frac{B}{C}t}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4CBg^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{B}{C}(t-s)}\|\rho(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds,

(5.7)

for
t\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
. To estimate (
5.7
), we first use bootstrap assumption (
1.11
) and Corollary
4.4
to conclude that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 18N_{0}.

Then, we deduce from (
5.7
) that:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4CBg^{2}(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\pi^{2}\rho_{m}^{2})\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{B}{C}(t-s)}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4CBg^{2}(18N_{0}+2\pi^{2}\rho_{m}^{2})\cdot B^{-1}(1-e^{-\frac{Bt}{C}})

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\rho_{m})g^{2}N_{0}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}

where third inequality follows from
\rho_{m}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{m}N_{0}
due to our choice of
N_{0}\geq 2\rho_{m}
. We also used
g,N_{0}\geq 1
in the final inequality. This concludes the proof of (
5.3
).

To show (
5.4
), we start with (
5.6
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg(18N_{0}+2\pi^{2}\rho_{m}^{2})^{1/2}gN_{0}^{1/2}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg^{2}N_{0},

where we invoked the bootstrap assumption and (
5.3
) in the second inequality. Fixing
t_{0}\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}]
, and integrating the above from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+T_{*}/9
, we obtain that

\displaystyle B\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg^{2}N_{0}\cdot c_{0}N_{0}^{-1}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg^{2},

where the last inequality holds as
g,B\geq 1
. Now, the desired estimate follows from dividing on both sides of the above inequality by
B
.
∎

We conclude this subsection by an estimate on the time derivative of cell density
\rho
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
). For any
t_{0}\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}]
, we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{2}

(5.8)

Proof.

Picking arbitrary
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}
and using (
5.1a
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\rho\varphi dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla\rho\varphi dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\Delta\rho\varphi dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\varphi\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))dx\right|=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}I_{i}.

Using incompressibility of
u
and integrating by parts, we first see that

I_{1}=\left|\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla\varphi(\rho-\rho_{m})dx\right|\leq\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}},

Applying integration by parts in a similar fashion and using Sobolev embeddings as well as Hölder’s inequality, we also have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\varphi dx\right|\leq\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})dx\right|\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining estimates of
I_{i}
above and using duality, we conclude that for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+T_{*}/9]
:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})(g^{2}N_{0}^{3}+N_{0}^{5/2})+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{3}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
), Proposition
4.3
, and Lemma
5.2
in the second inequality. Finally, we integrate over
[t_{0},t_{0}+T_{*}/9]
and invoke Lemma
5.1
to conclude that

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}dt\leq\frac{c_{0}N_{0}^{-1}}{9}\cdot C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{3}+18N_{0}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{2}

since
g\geq 1
and
N_{0}\geq 1
.
∎

5.2
Control of the Advection Term

In this subsection, we estimate the advection term
u\cdot\nabla u
on any time interval of length
T
in the bootstrap horizon.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
) holds and fix arbitrary
T\in(0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9)
. There exists a constant
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}_{0}(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
such that for
B\geq\mathcal{C}_{0}gN_{0}^{1/2}
, the following estimate holds:

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|u\cdot\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})g^{2}N_{0}(g^{2}N_{0}T+1),

(5.9)

for any
t_{0}\in[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9-T]
.

Proof.

First using Hölder inequality and Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality in 2D, we observe that

\displaystyle\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|u\|_{1}\|\nabla u\|_{1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}})(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2})

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}

We show how to estimate the most singular term
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}
, and the rest of the terms follow from a similar argument. Using the interpolation inequality
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}
for
u\in W
, and the calculus lemma
A.3
, we have

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.10)

Integrating (
5.10
) in time from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+T
and using (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt,

where we used (
5.3
) in the second inequality. Proceeding similarly with other terms, we can conclude the following bound:

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

(5.11)

Thus to close the estimate, we need to obtain a control of
\Delta u
over an interval of length
T
. Testing (
5.1b
) by
-\Delta u
, we obtain that for any
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+T)
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)(u\cdot\nabla u)dx+Bg\int_{\Omega}\rho\Delta u_{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{B}{4}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Bg^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{B}{4}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Bg^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})gN_{0}^{1/2}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{B}{4}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Bg^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(5.12)

where we used (
5.10
) in the second inequality and (
5.3
) in the last inequality above. Choose

B\geq 4C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})gN_{0}^{1/2}=:\mathcal{C}_{0}gN_{0}^{1/2},

(5.13)

where constant
C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
is the one appearing the last inequality of (
5.12
). Plugging (
5.13
) into (
5.12
) and rearranging, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2Bg^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2Bg^{2}(18N_{0}+2\pi^{2}\rho_{m}^{2}),

for
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+T)
.
Here, we used the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
) and Corollary
4.4
.

Now integrating from
t_{0}
to
t_{0}+T
, we obtain that

\displaystyle B\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\rho_{m})Bg^{2}N_{0}T\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})(Bg^{2}N_{0}T+1).

After dividing by
B
,

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})(g^{2}N_{0}T+1),

where we used
B\geq 1
in the inequality. Combining with (
5.11
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

5.3
Control of
\partial_{t}u

In this section, our ultimate goal is to show both a pointwise-in-time and a time-integrated bound on the time derivative
\partial_{t}u
. As the first step, we show an estimate of
\partial_{t}u
near time zero, which is presented in Lemma
5.5
. The fundamental reason that we prove this lemma is rather technical: we do not assume appropriate compatibility condition on initial datum
u_{0}
. For such general datum, one generally cannot obtain a pointwise-in-time control of
\|\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
by naively taking the limit of
t\to 0
using the equation. (We refer to
[
22
]
for a more detailed discussion on compatible data for parabolic problems.) While a control of
\partial_{t}u
exactly at time zero is not available in general, we may instead control
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}
at some later time due to parabolic smoothing. More precisely, we have:

Lemma 5.5
.

Define a time instance
s_{0}:=c_{0}\min(\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{-2},\rho_{m}^{-1})
, where
c_{0}
is fixed as in Corollary
4.4
. Then for
B\geq g\max(\mathcal{C}_{0}N_{0}^{1/2},N_{0})
, where
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is defined in Lemma
5.4
, we have:

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.14)

In particular, there exists
\tau_{0}\in(0,s_{0})
such that

\|\partial_{t}u(\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})B^{2}g^{2}}{s_{0}}=:\mathcal{C}_{1}(\rho_{m},\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{0}\|_{1})B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.15)

Proof.

Note that given (
5.14
), (
5.15
) directly follows from Chebyshev’s inequality. Hence, we focus on showing (
5.14
). Multiplying
\partial_{t}u
on both sides of (
5.1b
) and integrating in space, we have

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+B\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\cdot(-\Delta u)dx=Bg\int_{\Omega}\rho\partial_{t}u_{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u(u\cdot\nabla u)dx.

(5.16)

Since
u\in W
, we can further compute that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{i}u_{j}dx=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\cdot(-\Delta u)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{j}\partial_{2}u_{j}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\cdot(-\Delta u)dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}dS+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}dS=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\cdot(-\Delta u)dx,

where we used the boundary conditions
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
in the third inequality above. Combining with (
5.16
) and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain the following energy inequality:

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{B}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\left(B^{2}g^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),\quad t\in[0,s_{0}].

Rearranging and integrating in time from
0
to
s_{0}
, we have

B\|\nabla u(s_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq B\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left(B^{2}g^{2}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)dt.

(5.17)

Invoking Lemma
5.4
with
t_{0}=0
,
T=s_{0}
, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\|u\cdot\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})g^{2}N_{0}(g^{2}N_{0}s_{0}+1).

Note that we can indeed invoke Lemma
5.4
here due to
s_{0}<\mathcal{T}_{0}
and our assumption on
B
. Combining this bound with (
5.17
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq B\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})(B^{2}g^{2}\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot s_{0}+g^{4}N_{0}^{2}s_{0}+g^{2}N_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})(B+B^{2}g^{2}+g^{4}N_{0}^{2}s_{0}+g^{2}N_{0})\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})B^{2}g^{2},

where the last inequality follows from choosing
B^{2}\geq g^{2}N_{0}^{2}\geq N_{0}
, as well as the fact that
s_{0}\leq\rho_{m}^{-1}
. Hence, we have shown (
5.14
).
∎

With the
L^{2}
estimate (
5.15
) of
\partial_{t}u
near time zero above, we may further derive the following crucial estimates of
\partial_{t}u
up to
\mathcal{T}_{0}
thanks to the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
). More precisely:

Lemma 5.6
.

Assume the bootstrap assumption (
1.11
) holds. There exists
\tau_{0}<s_{0}
and
B_{0}(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})
large so that for any
B\geq B_{0}g^{2}
,

\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5\mathcal{C}_{1}(\rho_{m},\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}},\|u_{0}\|_{1})B^{2}g^{2},

(5.18)

where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is defined in (
5.15
). Moreover, for any
t_{0}\in[\tau_{0},\mathcal{T}_{0}]
, we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})Bg^{2}.

(5.19)

Additionally, if
t_{0}\in[\tau_{0},\mathcal{T}_{0}-\frac{8}{9}T_{*}]
, then

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+T_{*}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})Bg^{2}.

(5.20)

Remark 5.7
.

We remark that (
5.19
) does not follow directly from integrating (
5.18
) in time. In fact, doing so will only lead to an insufficient upper bound of size
B^{2}g^{2}N_{0}^{-1}
.

Proof.

The proof for this lemma is rather lengthy, so we split the proof in several steps.

Step 1: Energy Estimate for
\partial_{t}u
.
We start with the evolution of
\partial_{t}u
. By differentiating (
5.1b
) in time, it is straightforward to check that
\partial_{t}u
satisfies the following equation:

\partial_{t}^{2}u+\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla u+u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u-B\Delta\partial_{t}u+\nabla\partial_{t}p=Bg(\partial_{t}\rho)(0,1)^{T}.

Testing by
\partial_{t}u
, using
\divv(\partial_{t}u)=0
and the fact that
\partial_{t}u
still satisfies the Lions boundary condition, we have for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&+B\|\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u_{j})(\partial_{j}u_{i})(\partial_{t}u_{i})dx+Bg\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\rho\partial_{t}u_{2}dx\\
&\leq-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u_{j})(\partial_{j}u_{i})(\partial_{t}u_{i})dx+\epsilon B\|\partial_{t}u_{2}\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}+C(\epsilon)Bg^{2}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}\\
&\leq-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u_{j})(\partial_{j}u_{i})(\partial_{t}u_{i})dx+\epsilon CB\|\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\epsilon)Bg^{2}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.21)

where we used the
H^{1}_{0}
–
H^{-1}_{0}
duality in the first inequality, and Poincaré inequality in the final inequality. The use of duality is indeed valid since the Lions boundary condition implies that
u_{2}
, and thus
\partial_{t}u_{2}
, satisfies zero Dirichlet boundary condition.

To estimate the first term on the RHS of the estimate above, an application of Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality in 2D and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u_{j})(\partial_{j}u_{i})(\partial_{t}u_{i})dx&\leq\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}(\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{B}{4}\|\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{B}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(5.22)

Now choosing
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small in (
5.21
), combining with (
5.22
), and rearranging, we have the following differential inequality:

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq-B\|\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{B}\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}+CBg^{2}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{C}{B}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}-\frac{B}{C_{P}}\right)\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CBg^{2}\|\partial_{t}\rho\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.23)

where we used the Poincaré inequality (
2.2
) in the second inequality.

Choose
\tau_{0}
as in Lemma
5.5
. We write (
5.23
) in Duhamel form and use (
5.15
) to obtain that, for any
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9]
,

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-\tau_{0})}{C_{P}}+C\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{B}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\right)ds\right)\\
&\quad+CBg^{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-s)}{C_{P}}+C\int_{s}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{B}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\right)d\tau\right)\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds\\
&\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-\tau_{0})}{C_{P}}+C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})\left(\frac{g^{2}}{B}+g(t-\tau_{0})^{1/2}\right)\right)\\
&\quad+CBg^{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-s)}{C_{P}}+C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})\left(\frac{g^{2}}{B}+g(t-s)^{1/2}\right)\right)\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds.\end{split}

(5.24)

Here, we used (
5.4
) in the second inequality above. We also utilized the following consequence of (
5.4
): for any
\tau_{0}\leq a\leq b\leq a+T_{*}/9
,

\int_{a}^{b}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds\leq(b-a)^{1/2}\left(\int_{a}^{a+T_{*}/9}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})g(b-a)^{1/2}.

Then, we choose
B
sufficiently large that

B\geq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{2}}{\log 2}g^{2},

(5.25)

where
\mathcal{C}_{2}:=C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{1})
is the constant appeared in (
5.24
). This reduces (
5.24
) to

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq 2\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}\exp\left({-\frac{B(t-\tau_{0})}{C_{P}}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}g(t-\tau_{0})^{1/2}\right)\\
&\quad+CBg^{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-s)}{C_{P}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}g(t-s)^{1/2}\right)\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(5.26)

This concludes the proof of energy estimates that we will use in later steps.

Step 2: Proof of (
5.18
).
The plan of proving (
5.18
) is as follows: we first show a uniform bound similar to the desired control (
5.18
), yet on a shorter time interval, together with an improved bound at the endpoint of this interval. Namely, we will show that

\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2},

(5.27)

and additionally

\|\partial_{t}u(\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}=\|\partial_{t}u(\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.28)

With (
5.27
) and (
5.28
), we can prove (
5.18
) using an iteration argument.

Step 2.1: Proof of (
5.27
) and (
5.28
)
.
To prove the desired estimates (
5.27
) and (
5.28
), we study the inequality (
5.26
). The key point is that we have to estimate (
5.26
) in two different time scales. Roughly speaking, we will choose
B^{-1}\ll T_{*}
i.e. the dissipation time for
u
being much smaller than that for
\rho
. In this scenario,
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}
may grow on time interval
[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+B^{-1}]
, but it will not grow much due to smallness of
B^{-1}
. Yet on
[\tau_{0}+B^{-1},\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9]
, the dissipation effect becomes significant and is sufficient to damp growth generated by nonlinear terms.

More precisely, let us choose
B
such that

\begin{split}\frac{100C_{P}}{B}<\frac{c_{0}}{9N_{0}}=\frac{T_{*}}{9},&\quad B\geq\frac{100C_{P}\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}}{(\log 2)^{2}}g^{2},\end{split}

(5.29)

where
C_{P}
is the Poincaré constant appeared in (
5.26
). From (
5.29
), we obtain

\exp\left({-\frac{B(t-a)}{C_{P}}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}g(t-a)^{1/2}\right)\leq\exp\left({-\frac{B(t-a)}{C_{P}}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{10\sqrt{C_{P}}\mathcal{C}_{2}g}{\sqrt{B}}\right)\leq 2,

(5.30)

for any time
a
so that
0\leq t-a\leq\frac{100C_{P}}{B}
.

Estimate on
[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B}]
.
If
t\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B}]
, we may invoke (
5.30
) to estimate
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
by:

I_{1}\leq 4\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2},\;I_{2}\leq CBg^{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg^{4}N_{0}^{2},

where we used Lemma
5.3
in the last inequality. Choosing
B
sufficiently large that

C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{2}<\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}B}{2},

(5.31)

where
C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
is the constant appearing in the previous estimate. We then conclude that

\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<5\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.32)

Estimate on
(\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B},\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9]
.
Suppose that
t\in(\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B},\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9]
. In this case, we further choose
B
large so that

B\geq\frac{(2C_{P}\mathcal{C}_{2})^{2}}{100C_{P}}g^{2}.

(5.33)

Such choice guarantees that for any time
a\in[\tau_{0},t-\frac{100C_{P}}{B}]
, we have

-\frac{B(t-a)}{C_{P}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}g(t-a)^{1/2}\leq-\frac{B(t-a)}{2C_{P}}\leq-50.

(5.34)

Therefore using (
5.34
), we can bound
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in (
5.26
) by

I_{1}\leq 2\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-\tau_{0})}{2C_{P}}\right)\leq e^{-50}\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}<\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.35)

\begin{split}I_{2}&=CBg^{2}\left(\int_{\tau_{0}}^{t-\frac{100C_{P}}{B}}+\int_{t-\frac{100C_{P}}{B}}^{t}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{B(t-s)}{C_{P}}+\mathcal{C}_{2}g(t-s)^{1/2}\right)\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds\\
&\leq CBg^{2}(e^{-50}+2)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}\rho(s)\|_{H^{-1}_{0}}^{2}ds\leq C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})Bg^{4}N_{0}^{2},\end{split}

(5.36)

where we used (
5.34
) to estimate the first integral and (
5.30
) to estimate the second integral in the first inequality above. Then we choose
B
even larger that

C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})g^{2}N_{0}^{2}\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}}{2}B,

(5.37)

where
C(\rho_{m},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
is the constant appearing in the last line of (
5.36
). Combining (
5.37
) with (
5.36
), we conclude that
I_{2}
in (
5.26
) can be bounded as follows:

I_{2}\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1}}{2}B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.38)

Finally, we combine (
5.35
) and (
5.38
) to deduce that

\sup_{\tau_{0}+\frac{100C_{P}}{B}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}.

(5.39)

Collecting both (
5.32
) and (
5.39
) directly yields (
5.27
) and (
5.28
).

Step 2.2: Iterative Procedure.
With (
5.27
) and (
5.28
), we may start to iterate Step 2.1 and 2.2 for subsequent time intervals
[\tau_{0}+iT_{*}/9,\tau_{0}+(i+1)T_{*}/9]
,
i\geq 1
. Notice that all estimates involved in Step 2.1 and 2.2 only used that
[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T_{*}/9]\subset[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
and
\|\partial_{t}u(\tau_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}
. Hence, we may define
i_{*}
to be the largest natural number so that
[\tau_{0}+i_{*}T_{*}/9,\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+1)T_{*}/9]\subset[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
. The above arguments will conclude that

\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+1)T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}.

We may without loss of generality consider
\mathcal{T}_{0}<\infty
. In this case, by definition of
i_{*}
, we must have
\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+1)T_{*}/9>\mathcal{T}_{0}
, since otherwise
[\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+1)T_{*}/9,\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+2)T_{*}/9]\subset[0,\mathcal{T}_{0}+T_{*}/9]
, contradicting the maximality of
i_{*}
. We may then conclude that

\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sup_{\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+(i_{*}+1)T_{*}/9}\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5\mathcal{C}_{1}B^{2}g^{2}.

This concludes the proof of (
5.18
), after we choose threshold
B_{0}
so large that (
5.25
), (
5.29
), (
5.31
), (
5.33
), and (
5.37
) hold for all
B\geq B_{0}g^{2}
. Note that we can indeed choose such
B_{0}
, since all conditions of choosing
B
assume the form
B\geq Cg^{2}
with some
C
.

Step 3: Proof of (
5.19

In this section, we show the improved estimate as stated in Proposition
1.4
. Consider a time instance
\mathcal{T}_{1}
to be the first time such that

Now, we set up the following comparison scheme: let
\rho_{s},u_{s}
be the (global) regular solution to the following static problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{s}+u_{s}\cdot\nabla\rho_{s}-\Delta\rho_{s}+\divv(\rho_{s}\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho_{s}-(\rho_{s})_{m}))=0,\\
-\Delta u_{s}+\nabla p_{s}=g\rho_{s}(0,1)^{T},\quad\divv u_{s}=0,\\
\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;(u_{s})_{2}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}(u_{s})_{1}\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;\rho_{s}(0,x)=\rho(\mathcal{T}_{1},x)\geq 0.\end{cases}

(5.43)

Define
r(t,x)=\rho(t,x)-\rho_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1},x)
,
v(t,x)=u(t,x)-u_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1},x)
for

(5.44a)

-\Delta v+\nabla(B^{-1}p-p_{s})=-\frac{1}{B}\partial_{t}u-\frac{1}{B}(u\cdot\nabla u)+gr(0,1)^{T},\;\divv v=0,

(5.44b)

r(\mathcal{T}_{1},x)=0,\;v(\mathcal{T}_{1},x)=u(\mathcal{T}_{1},x)-u_{s}(\mathcal{T}_{1},x),

(5.44c)

\partial_{2}r|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\;v_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}v_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(5.44d)

Notice that in the derivation of (
5.44a
), we used the fact that

\rho(t,x)-\rho_{m}-(\rho_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1},x)-(\rho_{s})_{m})=\rho(t,x)-\rho_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1},x)=r(t,x),

thanks to the observation that

(\rho_{s})_{m}=(\rho(\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdot))_{m}=\rho_{m}.

Our plan is to establish appropriate controls of
\|r(t)\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\rho_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}
on time interval
[\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}+T_{*}]

Step 1: Control of
\|r(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
To control the remainder
r
, we study (
5.44a
) by testing this equation by
r
. By incompressibility of
u_{s}
, we deduce that for any
t\in[\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}+T_{*}]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}rv\cdot\nabla\rho-\int_{\Omega}r\divv(r\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))-\int_{\Omega}r\divv(\rho_{s}\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}r)

\displaystyle=K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}.

We first treat
K_{2},K_{3}
. Integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(r^{2})\cdot\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}r^{2}(\rho-\rho_{m})dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.45)

To estimate
K_{3}
, we integrate by parts and obtain

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho_{s}\nabla r\cdot\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}rdx\leq\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}r\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}r\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(5.46)

where we used the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\subset L^{4}
and elliptic estimates. Finally, we treat
K_{1}
, which potentially contributes the growth of
\|r\|_{L^{2}}
due to the appearance of

in the term. In fact, we have

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}rv\cdot\nabla\rho dx=\int_{\Omega}\divv(rv)\rho dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}rv_{2}\rho d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla r\cdot v\rho dx\leq\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{8}\|v\|_{2}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used

in the third equality, and Sobolev embedding

in the final inequality.

Now, we claim that
-B^{-1}\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)-B^{-1}u\cdot\nabla u+gr(t,\cdot)(0,1)^{T}\in H

\int_{\Omega}\left(-B^{-1}\partial_{t}u+gr(0,1)^{T}\right)_{1}dx=\int_{\Omega}-B^{-1}\partial_{t}u_{1}dx=-B^{-1}\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}u_{1}(x)dx=0.

Moreover, we observe that using Lions boundary condition satisfied by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u\cdot\nabla u)_{1}dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{1}dx=-\int_{\Omega}\divv uu_{1}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{2}u_{1}dS(x)=0.

The two computations above readily verify the claim. Hence, we are eligible to apply the elliptic estimate (
2.3
) and obtain

\|v\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{B^{2}}(\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+g^{2}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We therefore estimate
K_{1}
by

K_{1}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\frac{1}{B^{2}}(\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+g^{2}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(5.47)

Combining (
5.47
), (
5.45
), and (
5.4
), we have the following differential inequality: for any
t\in[\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}+T_{*}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(g^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho_{s}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{B^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F(t)\|r\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{B^{2}}(\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\pi^{2}\rho_{m}^{2})\left(\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

. Note that by the bootstrap assumption, Proposition
4.3

1.3

\|r(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
on

(5.48)

B\geq\max(B_{0}g^{2},\mathcal{C}_{0}gN_{0}^{1/2}),

(5.49)

where
B_{0}
and

are chosen as in Lemma
5.6
and Lemma
5.4

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}^{\mathcal{T}_{1}+T_{*}}\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\cdot\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds

Step 2: Control of
\|\rho_{s}(t-\mathcal{T}_{1})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}
.

satisfies the static problem (

), we shall use Proposition
1.3
to demonstrate that
\rho_{s}
produces sufficient damping so as to close the bootstrap argument. However, there is a technical caveat:
N_{0}
is chosen according to initial datum
\rho_{0}
, while
\rho_{s}
corresponds to initial datum
\rho(\mathcal{T}_{1},\cdot)
, which has a different

norm from
\rho_{0}
. Therefore, applying Proposition
1.3

\rho_{s}
will produce new thresholds, which we call
\widetilde{N_{0}}
,
\widetilde{T_{*}}
, and
\widetilde{g_{0}}

Recall that
(\rho_{s})_{m}=\rho_{m}
, and
\|\rho_{s}(0,\cdot)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=N_{0}
by the definition of

. Then by Proposition
1.3
, setting

\widetilde{N_{0}}=\max\left(1,2\rho_{m},2N_{0},\frac{32^{3}C_{1}\rho_{m}^{4}(15/8+2C_{1}c_{0})}{4c_{0}}\right),

\widetilde{T_{*}}=2c_{0}\widetilde{N_{0}}^{-1}
, there exists
\widetilde{g_{0}}=\widetilde{g_{0}}(\widetilde{N_{0}},\rho_{m})
such that for any

An elementary but crucial observation is that

to satisfy (

g_{1}=g_{1}(\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}},\rho_{m}):=\widetilde{g_{0}}(\widetilde{N_{0}},\rho_{m}).

With the discussions above, we conclude the following: assuming

\|\rho_{s}(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\widetilde{N_{0}}=2N_{0}

).
By triangle inequality and (

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathcal{T}_{1}\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{1}+T_{*}/2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\|\rho(\mathcal{T}_{1}+S_{1})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{N_{0}}{2}+\frac{N_{0}}{4}<N_{0}.

5.20
) in Lemma
5.6
. For any
i>1
, with
\mathcal{T}_{i-1}
and
S_{i-1}\in[T_{*}/4,T_{*}/2]

\mathcal{T}_{i}
to be the first time instance after
\mathcal{T}_{i-1}+S_{i-1}

\mathcal{T}_{i}
, we have
\mathcal{T}_{i}-\mathcal{T}_{i-1}\geq S_{i-1}\geq T_{*}/4

\mathcal{T}_{i}
still verifies that
\mathcal{T}_{i}>s_{0}

\mathcal{T}_{i}
in

, and the second inequality holds true since
T_{*}
is the least doubling time of
\|\rho-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
at level
N_{0}
. So we may repeat Step 2 and 3 above on interval

\sup_{\mathcal{T}_{i}\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{i}+T_{*}/2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5N_{0},

and

\|\rho(\mathcal{T}_{i}+S_{i})-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq N_{0},

for some
S_{i}\in[T_{*}/4,T_{*}/2].

j_{*}:=\lfloor\frac{4\mathcal{T}_{0}}{T_{*}}\rfloor+1
times, we reach the estimate that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{j_{*}}+T_{*}/2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5N_{0}.

However, we must have
\mathcal{T}_{j_{*}}+T_{*}/2\geq\mathcal{T}_{0}
, since
\mathcal{T}_{i+1}-\mathcal{T}_{i}\geq T_{*}/4
for every

. From this fact, we conclude that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{0}}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sup_{0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}_{j_{*}}+T_{*}/2}\|\rho(t)-\rho_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 5N_{0},

6
Discussions

In this section, we discuss extensions of Theorem
1.1
and some open problems.

1.

By performing a similar, but less laborious, analysis, one could prove a similar result to Theorem
1.1
to the following Keller-Segel-Stokes system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho-\Delta\rho+\divv(\rho\nabla(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(\rho-\rho_{m}))=0,\\
\rho_{m}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\rho(t,x)dx,\\
\partial_{t}u-\frac{1}{\re}\Delta u+\nabla p=\ra\rho(0,1)^{T},\\
\divv u=0.\end{cases}

(6.1)

equipped with boundary conditions

\partial_{2}\rho=0,\quad u_{2}=0,\quad\partial_{2}u_{1}=0,\quad\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.

(6.2)

The proof of this result was contained in an earlier ArXiv version of this work, but we only presented the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes case here due to considerable redundancies in the proof of the Keller-Segel-Stokes case.

2.

It is natural to attempt to generalize our main result to the Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system equipping classical
no-slip
boundary condition on the Navier-Stokes equation. However, in this case, the existence of more singular boundary layers might complicate the analysis. Such effect is manifested in a different leading order problem. Instead of considering the Biot-Savart law
u=-g\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}\partial_{1}\rho
as we did in this work, one needs to use
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
, where
\psi
solves the following bi-harmonic equation (see
[
18
,
48
,
47
]
for a more detailed discussion):

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\psi=\partial_{1}\rho,\\
\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(6.3)

A.1
Stokes Operator Equipped with Lions Boundary Condition

In this section, we first rigorously prove Proposition
2.1
and Proposition

, which concern Stokes operator corresponding to Lions boundary condition. For the convenience of the readers, we reiterate the statements of the propositions as follows:

Proposition A.1
.

Let
u\in V
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}},

(A.1)

where
C_{P}
is a positive constant that only depends on the domain
\Omega

Proof.

Since
\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we will estimate each velocity component separately. Since
u\in V
, then in particular
u_{1}
has zero spatial mean and
u_{2}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus, (
A.1
) follows from applying Poincaré inequality to
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
separately.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

Assuming
f\in H
, then (
2.1
) admits a unique solution
u\in W
with estimate

\|u\|_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(A.2)

In fact,
u
is given by the following explicit formula:

u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta_{D})^{-2}(\partial_{2}f_{1}-\partial_{1}f_{2}).

(A.3)

More generally, if
f\in H^{s}\cap H
,
s\geq 1
, we have the following improved regularity estimate:

\|u\|_{s+2}\leq C\|f\|_{s}.

(A.4)

Proof.

To show well-posedness of problem (
2.1
), it is convenient to show on the level of stream function. Since we would find
u
such that
\int_{\Omega}u_{1}dx=0
and
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, there exists a unique stream function
\psi
such that
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
by Hodge decomposition. Moreover,
\psi
satisfies the following:

\Delta\psi=\omega,\quad\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Taking
\nabla^{\perp}\cdot
on (
2.1
) and denoting
\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u
, we must have

-\Delta\omega=\partial_{1}f_{2}-\partial_{2}f_{1},\quad\omega|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Therefore, the discussion above motivates us to study the following auxiliary coupled system:

\begin{cases}\Delta\psi=\omega,\quad\psi|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\\
-\Delta\omega=\partial_{1}f_{2}-\partial_{2}f_{1},\quad\omega|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

(A.5)

First, note that since
f\in L^{2}
, we have
\partial_{1}f_{2}-\partial_{2}f_{1}\in H^{-1}_{0}
in the sense of distribution. Then by standard elliptic theory, there exists a unique
\omega\in H^{1}_{0}
such that the
\omega
equation holds, with estimate
\|\omega\|_{1}\leq C(\Omega)\|f\|_{L^{2}}
. Applying standard elliptic theory again to
\psi
equation, we have a unique solution
\psi\in H^{3}\cap H^{1}_{0}
, with estimate
\|\psi\|_{3}\leq C(\Omega)\|\omega\|_{1}
. Define
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
. Then
u
strongly solves (
2.1
) with boundary conditions
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
satisfied in the trace sense. Moreover, we have the estimate

\|u\|_{2}\leq\|\psi\|_{3}\leq C(\Omega)\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover from the discussion above, we obtain formula (
A.3
). Finally, to show the higher regularity estimates (
A.4
), we note that given
f\in H^{s}\cap H
,
s\geq 1
,
\nabla f\in H^{s-1}
. Using the explicit formula (
A.3
) and standard elliptic estimate, we conclude (
A.4
).
∎

Finally, we present the following calculus lemma:

Lemma A.3
.

\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}
for
u\in W\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Integrating by parts twice, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{k}\partial_{k}u_{j}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}u_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}u_{j}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta u_{j}\partial_{2}u_{j}dx_{1}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{k}\partial_{2}u_{j}\partial_{k}u_{j}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=:\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+BT_{1}+BT_{2}.

We moreover notice that

\displaystyle BT_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}dx_{1}+\Delta u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}dx_{1}=0,

Now we observe that for any
x\in\partial\Omega
, since
u
is smooth and belongs to the class
W
, we have

\Delta u_{2}=\partial_{1}^{2}u_{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}=\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}=-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}=0,

where we used
u_{2}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
in the second equality,
\divv u=0
in the third equality, and
\partial_{2}u_{1}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
in the last equality. Using this fact and
\partial_{2}u_{1}=0
for
x\in\partial\Omega
, we expand the sum and obtain:

\displaystyle BT_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}dx_{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{1}u_{2}dx_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}dx_{1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}dx_{1},

where we used the boundary conditions of
u
in the last equality. Since
\partial_{2}^{2}u_{2}=-\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}=0
for
x\in\partial\Omega
, we finally conclude that
BT_{2}=0
, and thus
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

Analytic continuation of Toeplitz operators and commuting families of C ∗ − C^{*}- algebras
Khalid Bdarneh, Gestur Ólafsson

Abstract
. We consider the Toeplitz operators on the weighted Bergman spaces over the unit ball 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} and their analytic continuation. We proved the commutativity of the C ∗ − C^{*}- algebras generated by the analytic continuation of Toeplitz operators with a special class of symbols that satisfy an invariant property, and we showed that these commutative C ∗ − C^{*}- algebras with symbols invariant under compact subgroups of S ​ U ​ ( n , 1 ) SU(n,1) are completely characterized in terms of restriction to multiplicity free representations. Moreover, we extended the restriction principal to the analytic continuation case for suitable maximal abelian subgroups of S ​ U ​ ( n , 1 ) SU(n,1) , we obtained the generalized Segal-Bargmann transform and we showed that it acts as a convolution operator. Furthermore, we proved that Toeplitz operators are unitarly equivalent to a convolution operator and we provided integral formulas for their spectra.

1.
introduction

Commutative
C^{*}-
algebras provide a framework for studying spectral properties of operators. The Gelfand-Naimark theorem states that every commutative
C^{*}-
algebra is
*-
isomorphic to
C_{0}(X)
for some locally compact Hausdorff space
X
.

One interesting problem is to find families of commutative
C^{*}-
algebras generated by Toeplitz operators. The algebra of Toeplitz operators is dense in the algebra of bounded operators under the strong operator topology. However, restricting the class of symbols ensures the existence of rich commutative families of Toeplitz operators. For example, consider the Bergman space on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, the
C^{*}-
algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with essentially bounded symbols is commutative if and only if there is a pencil of hyperbolic geodesics such that the symbols of the Toeplitz operators are constant on the cycles of this pencil
[
11
]
. In other words, we need the symbols to be invariant under the maximal abelian subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. This result was extended for the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
for the case
n>1
in
[
17
]
. In particular, the
C^{*}-
algebra generated by Toeplitz operators with essentially bounded symbols that are invariant under maximal abelian subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is commutative. Moreover, the authors in
[
4
]
used tools from representation theory to extend this result to bounded symmetric domains.

In this work we will construct commuting families of
C^{*}-
algebras generated by the analytic continuation of the Toeplitz operators, and we will develop the restriction principal for suitable maximal abelian subgroups of
SU(n,1)
which will allow us to find the spectral decomposition of the analytic continuation of the Toeplitz operators.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and consider the weighted Bergman space

with respect to the probability measure

d\mu_{\lambda}(z)=\dfrac{\Gamma(\lambda)}{\pi^{n}\Gamma(\lambda-n)}(1-|z|^{2})^{\lambda-n-1}dz,\quad\lambda>n

the Toeplitz operator is the operator

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}^{(\lambda)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}

\displaystyle\,A_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})\rightarrow A_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}^{(\lambda)}=P_{\lambda}(\varphi f)

where

This definition of the Toeplitz operator works when

. However, the weighted Bergman spaces

when we consider

. The kernel of

So, a natural question would be, for which values of

The authors in

constructed Hilbert spaces of holomorphic functions with reproducing kernel

In

On the unit ball

5.
Maximal Abelian Subgroups of
SU(n,1)

Our main goal in this section is to describe the spectral decomposition of Toeplitz operators with symbols that are invariant under the maximal abelian subgroups of
SU(n,1)
. In particular, we will apply the restriction principle to write the Toeplitz operators as a convolution operator on certain sections of line bundles. In fact, we will show that the work in
[
5
]
can be generalized to the analytic continuation case.

The group
SU(n,1)
realizes the group of all automorphisms of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. As mentioned before, there are n+2 conjugacy classes of maximal abelian subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
. It is easier to describe their action on an unbounded realization of the unit ball

. Let

be the unbounded domain defined by

D_{n}=\{(z^{{}^{\prime}},z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}\mid Im(z_{n})-|z^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2}>0\}

The Cayley transform defines a biholomorphism
\mathbb{B}^{n}\rightarrow D_{n}
, which is given by

z\mapsto\dfrac{i}{1+z_{n}}(z^{{}^{\prime}},1-z_{n})

with inverse

z\mapsto\dfrac{1}{1-iz_{n}}(-2iz^{{}^{\prime}},1+iz_{n})

Moreover, if we define

C\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}i\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&i\\
&&&&-i&&i\\
&&&&1&&1\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}

then the group
CSU(n,1)C^{-1}
realizes the group of biholomorphisms of
D_{n}
.
Each maximal abelian subgroup is conjugate to one of the following representative
[
19
,
5
]
:

Quasi-elliptic
: This maximal abelian subgroup corresponds to the maximal compact torus in
SU(n,1)
, it is given by the
\mathbb{T}^{n}-
action on the unit ball

t.z=(t_{1}z_{1},...,t_{n}z_{n})

where
t\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Quasi-parabolic
: This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, and it acts on
D_{n}
by

(t,y).(z^{{}^{\prime}},z_{n})=(tz^{{}^{\prime}},z_{n}+y)

Quasi-hyperbolic
: This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
and acts on
D_{n}
by

(t,r).(z^{{}^{\prime}}.z_{n})=(rtz^{{}^{\prime}},r^{2}z_{n})

Nilpotent
: This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and acts on
D_{n}
by

(b,s).(z^{{}^{\prime}},z_{n})=(z^{{}^{\prime}}+b,z_{n}+2i<z^{{}^{\prime}},b>+s+i|b|^{2})

Quasi-nilpotent
: This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
where

, this group acts on
D_{n}
by

(t,b,s).(z^{{}^{\prime}},z^{{}^{\prime\prime}},z_{n})=(tz^{{}^{\prime}},z^{{}^{\prime\prime}}+b,z_{n}+2i<z^{{}^{\prime\prime}},b>+s+i|b|^{2})

We will restrict our work to the first three classes of subgroups. In particular, when we say a maximal abelian subgroup, we mean it is either Quasi-elliptic, Quasi-parabolic, or Quasi-hyperbolic.

Let

be a maximal abelian subgroup of

, and

be the subgroup of

that covers

Let

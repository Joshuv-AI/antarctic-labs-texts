1.
Introduction and main results

Recently, the author co-published an article
[
1
]
titled ”Asymptotic behavior of small solutions of quadratic congruences in three variables modulo prime powers”. In this work, we studied small solutions of diagonal quadratic congruences of the form

(1.1)

ax^{2}+cy^{2}+fz^{2}\equiv 0\bmod{q},

where
a,c,f\in\mathbb{Z}
,
q=p^{n}
is a power of a fixed odd prime
p
and
n\rightarrow\infty.
More precisely, we investigated the asymptotic behavior of small solutions of the congruence (
1.1
) with max
\{|x|,|y|,|z|\}\leq N
and
(xyz,p)=1.
We also assumed
(acf,p)=1.
For convenience we considered a smooth version of this problem (i.e. the solutions are suitably weighted) and studied the problem both for arbitrary and fixed coefficients
a,c,f.
In the case of fixed coefficients
a,c,f
and
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtained an asymptotic formula (
[
1
, Theorem 1]
) if
N\gg q^{1/2+\varepsilon}
, and in the case of coefficients which are allowed to vary with
n
, we obtained such a formula (
[
1
, Theorem 2]
) for
N\gg q^{11/18+\varepsilon}
.

Let

(1.2)

Q(x,y,z)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}+dxz+eyz+fz^{2}

be a quadratic form in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
and
\Delta:=\Delta_{Q}
be the determinant of the associated matrix

A_{Q}=\begin{pmatrix}a&\frac{b}{2}&\frac{d}{2}\\
\frac{b}{2}&c&\frac{e}{2}\\
\frac{d}{2}&\frac{e}{2}&f\end{pmatrix}.

In this present paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of the congruence

(1.3)

Q(x,y,z)\equiv 0\bmod{q},

with fixed coefficients and
(a(4ac-b^{2})\Delta,p)=1
(Note that
a
,
\frac{4ac-b^{2}}{4}
and
\Delta
are leading principal minors of the associated matrix
A_{Q}
). Here, for a fixed point
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we consider the set of solutions
(x,y,z)
with
(z,p)=1,
and max
\{|x-x_{0}|,|y-y_{0}|,|z-z_{0}|\}\leq N
obtaining a similar asymptotic formula as in
[
1
]
if
N\gg q^{1/2+\varepsilon}
. The condition
(z,p)=1
is slightly different than the condition
(xyz,p)=1
and it also excludes the trivial solution (0,0,0). The author is in a search of a suitable way to generalize
[
1
, Theorem 2]
to general quadratic forms. Readers are requested to see
[
1
]
for a more detailed introduction.
To state our result, we define the following quantity

C_{p}(Q):=\frac{(p-s_{p}(Q)))(p-1)}{p^{2}},

where

(1.4)

S_{p}(Q):=\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right).

We note that if
q=p
is an odd prime, then the total number of solutions
(x,y,z)
to the congruence (
1.3
) satisfying
(z,p)=1
turns out to be
(p-1)(p-S_{p}(Q))
. So a solution exists as
p-s_{p}(Q)\geq 2
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed,
p
be a fixed odd prime, and
Q(x,y,z)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}+dxz+eyz+fz^{2}
be a fixed quadratic form in
\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
with
(a(4ac-b^{2})\Delta,p)=1.
Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a Schwartz class function. Set
q:=p^{n}
. Then as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have the asymptotic formula

(1.5)

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
(z,p)=1\\
Q(x,y,z)\equiv 0\bmod{q}\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{N}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{y-y_{0}}{N}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sim\hat{\Phi}(0)^{3}\cdot C_{p}(Q)\cdot\frac{N^{3}}{q},

provided that
N\geq q^{1/2+\varepsilon}
.

One can see that the right-hand side of (
1.5
) does not depend on the choice of the fixed point
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})
, so the solutions
(x,y,z)
of
Q(x,y,z)\equiv 0\bmod{q}
, with
(z,p)=1
are equally distributed throughout
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Our method is a generalization of the method that is used in the proof of
[
1
, theorem 1]
. Key ingredients in our method are a parametrization of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
- rational points
(x,y)
on the conic

ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}+dx+ey+f=0,

repeated use of Poisson summation and an explicit evaluation of complete exponential sums with rational functions to prime power moduli due to Cochrane
[
3
]
. This transforms the problem into a dual problem which amounts to counting solutions of quadratic Diophantine
equations
(rather than
congruences
).
Then we use a result due to Heath-Brown
[
2
, corollary 2]
for counting the number of the solutions of the obtained dual quadratic equation.

Preliminaries

We will use the notation

e_{q}(z):=e\left(\frac{z}{q}\right)=e^{2\pi iz/q}

for
q\in\mathbb{N}
and denote by
G_{q}
the quadratic Gauss sum

G_{q}=\sum\limits_{x=1}^{q}e_{q}(x^{2}).

We recall that if
q=p^{k}
for some odd prime
p
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(2.1)

G_{q}=\begin{cases}p^{\frac{k}{2}}~~~&\text{if}~k~~\text{even,}\\
p^{\frac{k-1}{2}}G_{p}~~&\text{if}~k~~\text{odd,}\end{cases}

where
\left(\frac{y}{q}\right)
is the Jacobi symbol. We also denote by
q(x,y)
the de-homogenized form of the quadratic form
Q(x,y,z),

(2.2)

q(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}+dx+ey+f.

Throughout the sequel, we will write
\overline{\alpha}
for a multiplicative inverse of
\alpha
to the relevant modulus, which will always be apparent from the context.

The following preliminaries will be needed in the course of this paper.

Proposition 1

(Parametrization of points on a conic)
.

Let
K
be a field,
(\alpha,\beta)\in K^{2}
and
q(x,y)\in K[x,y]
be as in (
2.2
) with
a\Delta\neq 0
such that

q(\alpha,\beta)=0,

and
q_{x}(x,y),q_{y}(x,y)
be the partial derivatives of
q(x,y)
with respect to
x
and
y
respectively.
Then, the map

(2.3)

s:\left\{t\in K:at^{2}+bt+c\not=0\right\}\cup\{\infty\}\longrightarrow\left\{(x,y)\in K^{2}:q(x,y)=0\right\}

defined by

(2.4)

s(t):=\begin{cases}\left(\alpha-tM(t),\beta-M(t)\right)~~\text{if}~~t\in K,\\
\left(\alpha-\frac{A}{a},\beta\right)~~\text{if}~~t=\infty,\end{cases}

is bijective, where

M(t)=\frac{At+B}{at^{2}+bt+c},

with
A=q_{x}(\alpha,\beta)
and
B=q_{y}(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

We use a standard method of parametrization. Given a
K
-rational point
P=(\alpha,\beta)
on the conic and
t\in K\cup\{\infty\}
, by Bézout’s theorem, the line
\mathcal{L}(t)
through
P
given by the equation
x-\alpha=t(y-\beta)
intersects the conic in
P
and at most one more point
s(t)
(where by
\mathcal{L}(\infty)
we mean the line
y=\beta
). This point may be
P
itself, in which case
\mathcal{L}(t)
is the tangent to the conic at
P
. Conversely, if
Q
is a
K
-rational point on the conic, then there exists precisely one line through
P
and
Q
(which is the tangent in the case
P=Q
, and the tangent is unique because
\Delta\neq 0
) with rational slope
t
or
\infty
. Hence, we have a bijection between the
K
-rational points on the conic and the set of
t\in K\cup\{\infty\}
for which
s(t)
exists. To find
s(t)
, we write the Taylor series expansion around
(\alpha,\beta)
in the form

\displaystyle q(x,y)=

\displaystyle q(\alpha,\beta)+q_{x}(\alpha,\beta)(x-\alpha)+q_{y}(\alpha,\beta)(y-\beta)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{2}q_{xx}(\alpha,\beta)(x-\alpha)^{2}+q_{xy}(\alpha,\beta)(x-\alpha)(y-\beta)+\frac{1}{2}q_{yy}(\alpha,\beta)(y-\beta)^{2}.

Now we plug in
x-\alpha=t(y-\beta)
and get

(At+B)(y-\beta)+(at^{2}+bt+c)(y-\beta)^{2}=0.

Therefore, if
y\neq\beta
(i.e when
t\in K
)

\begin{split}y=&\beta-\frac{At+B}{at^{2}+bt+c}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}x&=\alpha+(y-\beta)t\\
&=\alpha-t\frac{At+B}{at^{2}+bt+c},\end{split}

which exist if
at^{2}+bt+c\neq 0.

If
y=\beta
(i.e when
t=\infty
), we have

ax^{2}+b\beta x+c\beta^{2}+dx+e\beta+f=0,

which is a quadratic polynomial in
x
(as
a\not=0
) having two roots
x=\alpha
and
\alpha-\frac{A}{a}.

This gives the desired parametrization in (
2.4
), and the map in (
2.3
) is bijective.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
p>2
be a prime,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be as in (
2.2
) with
(a\Delta,p)=1
. Then the number of solutions
(x,y)\bmod p^{n}
of the congruence

q(x,y)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}

equals

N_{p^{n}}(q)=p^{n-1}\left(p-\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)\right).

Proof.

Since
a\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
, the number of solutions of the congruence
at^{2}+bt+c\equiv 0\bmod{p}
is
1+\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)
.
So using Proposition
1
with
K=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we get that the number of solutions of the congruence

q(x,y)\equiv 0\bmod{p}

is
N_{p}(q)=p-\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)
. Now, we use a Hensel-type argument as follows. Let
(x,y)
be a solution of the congruence

(2.5)

q(x,y)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{k}}.

We want to lift it to a solution
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
of the congruence

(2.6)

q(x,y)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{k+1}}.

So we write
\tilde{x}=x+k_{1}p^{k},\tilde{y}=y+k_{2}p^{k}
and use the Taylor series expansion around
(x,y)
to get

\frac{q(x,y)}{p^{k}}+q_{x}(x,y)k_{1}+q_{y}(x,y)k_{2}\equiv 0\bmod{p}.

Now
(\Delta,p)=1
implies
(q_{x}(x,y),q_{y}(x,y))\not\equiv(0,0)\bmod{p}
. So the congruence in
k_{1},k_{2}
has exactly
p
solutions.
Thus,

N_{p^{k+1}}(q)=pN_{p^{k}}(q).

Hence, using mathematical induction the Proposition is proved.
∎

Proposition 3
.

Let
p>2
be a prime,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
be as in (
2.2
) with
(a\Delta,p)=1
. Assume that
q(\alpha,\beta)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}.
Then the solutions
(x,y)\bmod p^{n}
of the congruence

q(x,y)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}

are parametrized as

M:=\bigcup\limits_{s=0}^{n}M_{s},

where

M_{s}:=\left\{\left(\tilde{x}\left(\frac{t}{p^{s}}\right),\tilde{y}\left(\frac{t}{p^{s}}\right)\right):t=1,..,p^{n-s},at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s}\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}\right\}\mbox{ for }s=0,1,2...,n

and
\tilde{x}(t),\tilde{y}(t)
are defined as below,

(2.7)

\begin{split}\tilde{x}(t)&:=\alpha-t\frac{At+B}{at^{2}+bt+c},\\
\tilde{y}(t)&:=\beta-\frac{At+B}{at^{2}+bt+c},\end{split}

with
A=q_{x}(\alpha,\beta)
and
B=q_{y}(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rational points
(x,y)
on the conic
q(x,y)
are parametrized as
in (
2.7
) and
q(\alpha,\beta)=0
(in particular,
q(\alpha,\beta)=0\bmod{p^{n}}
). This equation is soluble in
(\alpha,\beta)
as a consequence of Proposition
2
.
Noting that
\tilde{x}(t/p^{s})
and
\tilde{y}(t/p^{s})
are
p
-adic integers, we will view
\tilde{x}(t/p^{s})
and
\tilde{y}(t/p^{s})
as elements of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

It can be seen that the pairs
\left(\tilde{x}(t/p^{s})\right),\tilde{y}(t/p^{s})
in the above sets
M_{s}
(
s=0,...,n
) are distinct and
M_{i}\cap M_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
by the following argument.
Suppose that

\left(\tilde{x}\left(\frac{t_{1}}{p^{s_{1}}}\right),\tilde{y}\left(\frac{t_{1}}{p^{s_{1}}}\right)\right)=\left(\tilde{x}\left(\frac{t_{2}}{p^{s_{2}}}\right),\tilde{y}\left(\frac{t_{2}}{p^{s_{2}}}\right)\right),

which implies

\begin{split}\alpha-t_{1}\frac{At_{1}+Bp^{s_{1}}}{at_{1}^{2}+bt_{1}p^{s_{1}}+cp^{2s_{1}}}=&\alpha-t_{2}\frac{At_{2}+Bp^{s_{2}}}{at_{2}^{2}+bt_{2}p^{s_{2}}+cp^{2s_{2}}},\\
\beta-p^{s_{1}}\frac{At_{1}+Bp^{s_{1}}}{at_{1}^{2}+bt_{1}p^{s_{1}}+cp^{2s_{1}}}=&\beta-p^{s_{2}}\frac{At_{2}+Bp^{s_{2}}}{at_{2}^{2}+bt_{2}p^{s_{2}}+cp^{2s_{2}}}.\end{split}

Then a short calculation gives

(2.8)

t_{2}p^{s_{1}}\equiv t_{1}p^{s_{2}}\bmod{p^{n}}.

So if
0\leq s_{1}=s_{2}\leq n
then
p^{s_{1}}(t_{2}-t_{1})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}
, which implies
t_{2}-t_{1}\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n-s_{1}}}
. Hence
t_{2}=t_{1}
because
1\leq t_{1},t_{2}\leq p^{n-s_{1}}
.
If
s_{2}>s_{1}
then we have
t_{2}\equiv 0\bmod{p^{s_{2}-s_{1}}}
, which contradicts the fact that
t_{2}\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
.
Now since
a\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
, the number of solutions of
at^{2}+bt+c\equiv 0\bmod{p}
in the range
1\leq t\leq p^{n}
is
\left(1+\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)\right)p^{n-1}.

Therefore, we get that
|M_{0}|=\left(p-1-\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)\right)p^{n-1},|M_{n}|=1
,
|M_{s}|=p^{n-s}-p^{n-s-1}
for
s=1,2...n-1
.
Thus

\begin{split}|M|&=\left|\bigcup_{s=0}^{n}M_{s}\right|\\
&=\left(p-1-\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)\right)p^{n-1}+(p^{n-1}-p^{n-2})+....+(p^{2}-p)+(p-1)+1=\left(p-\left(\frac{b^{2}-4ac}{p}\right)\right)p^{n-1}.\end{split}

Now Proposition
2
tells us that
M=\bigcup_{s=0}^{n}M_{n}
is a complete set of solutions of
q(x,y)\equiv 0
mod
p^{n}
.

Proposition 5

(Evaluation of exponential sums with rational functions)
.

Let
p>2
be a prime,
n\geq 2
be a natural number and
f=F_{1}/F_{2}
be a rational function where
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. For a polynomial
G
over
\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mbox{ord}_{p}(G)
be the largest power of
p
dividing all of the coefficients of
G
, and for a rational function
g=G_{1}/G_{2}
with
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mbox{ord}_{p}(g):=\mbox{ord}_{p}(G_{1})-\mbox{ord}_{p}(G_{2})
. Set

r:=\mbox{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}),

and

S_{\alpha}(f;p^{n}):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x=1\\
x\equiv\alpha\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}(f(x)),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then we have the following if
r\leq n-2
and
(F_{2}(\alpha),p)=1
.

(i) If
p^{-r}f^{\prime}(\alpha)\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
, then
S_{\alpha}(f,p^{n})=0
.

(ii) If
\alpha
is a root of
p^{-r}f^{\prime}(x)\equiv 0\bmod{p}

S_{\alpha}(f;p^{n})=\begin{cases}e_{p^{n}}\left(f(\alpha^{\ast})\right)p^{(n+r)/2}&\mbox{ if }n-r\mbox{ is even,}\\

where
\alpha^{\ast}
is the unique lifting of
\alpha
to a solution of the congruence
p^{-r}f^{\prime}(x)\equiv 0\bmod p^{[(n-r+1)/2]}
and

A(\alpha):=2p^{-r}f^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{\ast}).

Proof.

This is
[
3
, Theorem 3.1(iii)]
.

3.1.
Double Poisson summation

We start by writing

\begin{split}T=&\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
(z,p)=1\\
Q(x,y,z)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{N}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{y-y_{0}}{N}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\\

q(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\equiv\tilde{x}z\bmod{p^{n}}\\
y\equiv\tilde{y}z\bmod{p^{n}}\end{subarray}}\Phi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{N}\right)\Phi\left(\frac{y-y_{0}}{N}\right).\end{split}

Now we apply Poisson summation, Proposition
4
, after a linear change of variables to the inner double sum over
x
and
y
, obtaining

T=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{1}N}{p^{n}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{2}N}{p^{n}}\right)e_{p^{n}}\left(-k_{1}x_{0}-k_{2}y_{0}\right)\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\bmod{p^{n}}\\
q(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n}}\end{subarray}}e_{p^{n}}\left(k_{1}z\tilde{x}+k_{2}z\tilde{y}\right).

Using the parametrization in Proposition
3
, we deduce that

T=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{1}N}{p^{n}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{2}N}{p^{n}}\right)e_{p^{n}}\left(-k_{1}x_{0}-k_{2}y_{0}\right)\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in M_{s}\end{subarray}}e_{p^{n}}\left(k_{1}z\tilde{x}+k_{2}z\tilde{y}\right).

We decompose
T
into

(3.1)

T=T_{0}+U,

where
T_{0}
is the main term contribution of
(k_{1},k_{2})=(0,0)
. Hence,

\begin{split}T_{0}&=\hat{\Phi}(0)^{2}\cdot\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\cdot\left|M\right|\\
&=\hat{\Phi}(0)^{2}\cdot\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\cdot p^{n-1}(p-s_{p}(Q)),\end{split}

where

is defined as in (

) and

is defined as in Proposition
3
.

If

for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, then the term
T_{0}
can be simplified as

(3.2)

\begin{split}T_{0}=&\hat{\Phi}(0)^{2}\cdot\frac{p-s_{p}(Q)}{p}\cdot\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n}}\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{z}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)-\sum\limits_{z}\Phi\left(\frac{pz-z_{0}}{N}\right)\right)\\
=&\hat{\Phi}(0)^{2}\cdot\frac{p-s_{p}(Q)}{p}\cdot\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n}}\cdot\left(N\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}\cdot\hat{\Phi}(0)+\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left(N\hat{\Phi}(Nw)\cdot e(-wz_{0})-\frac{N}{p}\cdot\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{Nw}{p}\right)\cdot e_{p}(-wz_{0})\right)\right)\\
=&\hat{\Phi}(0)^{3}\cdot\frac{(p-s_{p}(Q))(p-1)}{p^{2}}\cdot\frac{N^{3}}{p^{n}}\cdot\left(1+o(1)\right)=\hat{\Phi}(0)^{3}\cdot C_{p}(Q)\cdot\frac{N^{3}}{p^{n}}\cdot\left(1+o(1)\right)\end{split}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
where we again use Poisson summation for the sums over
z
above and the rapid decay of
\hat{\Phi}
.

3.2.
Evaluation of exponential sums

Now we look at the error contribution

(3.3)

U=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{1}N}{p^{n}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{k_{2}N}{p^{n}}\right)e_{p^{n}}\left(-k_{1}x_{0}-k_{2}y_{0}\right)\cdot E\left(k_{1},k_{2},z;p^{n}\right)

with

\begin{split}E\left(k_{1},k_{2},z;p^{n}\right):=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in M_{s}}e_{p^{n}}\left(z(k_{1}\tilde{x}+k_{2}\tilde{y})\right).\end{split}

Assume that

(k_{1},k_{2},p^{n})=p^{r}.

Set

l_{1}:=\frac{k_{1}}{p^{r}},\quad l_{2}:=\frac{k_{2}}{p^{r}}.

The contribution of
r=n-1,n
to the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
if
N\geq p^{n\varepsilon}
by the rapid decay of
\hat{\Phi}
since
(k_{1},k_{2})=(0,0)
is excluded from the summation. In the following, we assume that
r\leq n-2
so that Proposition
5
is applicable.

Let

f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)=z\left(k_{1}\tilde{x}_{s}(t)+k_{2}\tilde{y}_{s}(t)\right),

where
\tilde{x}_{s}(t)=\tilde{x}\left(\frac{t}{p^{s}}\right),\tilde{y}_{s}(t)=\tilde{y}\left(\frac{t}{p^{s}}\right)
and
\tilde{x}(t),\tilde{y}(t)
is defined in (
2.7
).
We have
f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\equiv f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t+wp^{n-s})\bmod{p^{n}}
for
w=1,2...,p^{s}
.

So we deduce

(3.4)

\begin{split}E\left(k_{1},k_{2},z;p^{n}\right):=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right).\end{split}

The derivative of the amplitude function turns out to be

\begin{split}f^{\prime}_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)&=z\frac{k_{1}\left((aB-bA)t^{2}p^{2s}-2Actp^{3s}-Bcp^{4s})\right)+k_{2}\left(aAt^{2}p^{2s}+2aBtp^{3s}+(Bb-Ac)p^{4s}\right)}{(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{s}}\\
&=zp^{s}\frac{\left(k_{1}(aB-bA)+k_{2}aA\right)t^{2}+2(aBk_{2}-cAk_{1})tp^{s}+\left((bB-cA)k_{2}-cBk_{1}\right)p^{2s}}{(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})^{2}}.\end{split}

For
(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}\neq 0,
we define

r^{\prime}=r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2}):=\frac{\ln(\mbox{ord}_{p}((aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}))}{\ln{p}}.

Note that

aB^{2}-bAB+cA^{2}=-4\Delta.

So

cA((aB-bA)k_{1}+aAk_{2})+(aB-bA)(aBk_{2}-cAk_{1})=-4a\Delta k_{2},

and

B((aB-bA)k_{1}+aAk_{2})-A(aBk_{2}-cAk_{1})=-4\Delta k_{1}.

Thus, it can be shown that
\left(k_{1},k_{2},p^{n}\right)=\left(\left((aB-bA)k_{1}+aAk_{2})\right),(aBk_{2}-cAk_{1}),p^{n}\right)
as
a\Delta\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}.

Therefore,

\mbox{ord}_{p}(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}p^{2s+r}&\text{if}~~r^{\prime}\geq s,\\
p^{s+r+r^{\prime}}&\text{if}~~r^{\prime}<s.\end{cases}

We split the second sum over
t
on the right-hand side of (
3.4
) into

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=1\\
(ap^{2s}+b\alpha p^{s}+c\alpha^{2})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p}S_{\alpha}\left(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}};p^{n}\right),\end{split}

where

S_{\alpha}(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}};p^{n}):=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
t\equiv\alpha\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)).

We see that if
r^{\prime}=0
and
s>0
then
\mbox{ord}_{p}(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot f^{\prime}_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\equiv 0\bmod{p}
implies
t\equiv 0\bmod{p}
. Then using Proposition
5
, we have
S_{\alpha}=0
if
\alpha\neq 0
, which implies that

\sum_{s=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)=0.

If
r^{\prime}\neq 0
and
s\neq r^{\prime}
then
\mbox{ord}_{p}(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot f^{\prime}_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\equiv 0\bmod{p}
implies
t\equiv 0\bmod{p}
. Then again using Proposition
5
, we have
S_{\alpha}=0
if
\alpha\neq 0
, which implies that

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=0\\
s\neq r^{\prime}\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{s}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+btp^{s}+cp^{2s})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{s,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)=0.

Therefore

(3.5)

\displaystyle E(k_{1},k_{2},z,p^{n})=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+bt+c)\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)&\text{if }r^{\prime}=0,\\
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+bt+c)\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)+E(r^{\prime})\frac{1}{p^{r^{\prime}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\\
(at^{2}+btp^{r^{\prime}}+cp^{2r^{\prime}})\not\equiv 0~~\text{mod}~~p\end{subarray}}^{p^{n}}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{r^{\prime},k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\right)&\text{if }r^{\prime}\neq 0,\end{cases}

where

E(r^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}~~r^{\prime}\leq n,\\
0&\text{if}~~r^{\prime}>n.\end{cases}

So, we may write

U=U_{1}+U_{2}+U_{3}+O_{\varepsilon}(1),

where

(3.6)

\begin{split}U_{1}&=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(l_{1},l_{2},p)=1\\
r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2})=0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{1}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{2}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-l_{1}x_{0}-l_{2}y_{0}\right)E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right),\\
U_{2}&=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(l_{1},l_{2},p)=1\\
(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}\neq 0\\
r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2})\neq 0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{1}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{2}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-l_{1}x_{0}-l_{2}y_{0}\right)E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right),\\
U_{3}&=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{2n}}\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(l_{1},l_{2},p)=1\\
(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}=0\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{1}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{2}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-l_{1}x_{0}-l_{2}y_{0}\right)E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right).\end{split}

Set

D:=(al_{2}^{2}-bl_{1}l_{2}+cl_{1}^{2})(aB^{2}-bAB+cA^{2})\bmod{p^{n}},

and

C_{s}(t):=\left(l_{1}(aB-bA)+l_{2}aA\right)t^{2}+2(aBl_{2}-cAl_{1})tp^{s}+\left((bB-cA)l_{2}-cBl_{1}\right)p^{2s}.

For
r^{\prime}=0
, we consider the congruence

(3.7)

C_{0}(\alpha)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n-r}.}

If
D
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
p
, then the congruence (
3.7
) has no solution. So by Proposition
5
,
E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right)=0.

Assuming
D
to be quadratic residue modulo
p
, we calculate the roots of the congruence (
3.7
) as

\frac{-(l_{2}Ba-l_{1}Ac)\pm\sqrt{D}}{(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}}\bmod{p^{n-r}},

where
\sqrt{D}
denotes one of the two roots of the congruence

x^{2}\equiv D\bmod{p^{n-r}}.

For simplicity, we fix one root

\alpha^{\ast}=\frac{-(l_{2}Ba-l_{1}Ac)+\sqrt{D}}{(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}}\bmod{p^{n-r}}.

So, we have

(3.8)

a(\alpha^{\ast})^{2}+b\alpha^{\ast}+c=\frac{2aD-\sqrt{D}[l_{2}(2a^{2}B-abA)+l_{1}(b^{2}A-abB-2acA)]}{[(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}]^{2}}.

The congruence (
3.7
) has a double root
\alpha\bmod{p}
iff
D\equiv 0\bmod{p}
, and in this case, we get
a\alpha^{2}+b\alpha+c\equiv 0\bmod{p}
because of (
3.8
) and such
\alpha
is already excluded from the summation. Hence, only the case
D\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
occurs in which we have no root if
D
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
p
and two roots of multiplicity one if
D
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
. Therefore, we may assume from now on that
D\not\equiv 0\bmod{p}
and
D
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
. Then using Proposition
5
, if
\alpha
satisfies (
3.7
), we obtain

S_{\alpha}\left(f_{p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}},p^{n}\right)=\begin{cases}e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha^{\ast})\right)\cdot p^{(n+r)/2}&\mbox{ if }n-r\mbox{ is even,}\\
e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha^{\ast})\right)\cdot\left(\frac{A(\alpha)}{p}\right)\cdot\frac{G_{p}}{\sqrt{p}}\cdot p^{(n+r)/2}&\mbox{ if }n-r\mbox{ is odd,}\end{cases}

where

A(\alpha)=\frac{2f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)}{p^{r}}.

Set

J_{l_{1},l_{2}}:=l_{1}(be-2cd)+l_{2}(bd-2ae).

Then, a short calculation gives

e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha^{\ast})\right)=e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D})\right).

Further, we calculate

f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha^{\ast})=z\cdot\frac{\sqrt{D}}{(a+b\alpha^{\ast}+c(\alpha^{\ast})^{2})^{2}}\cdot p^{r},

Therefore,

\left(\frac{A(\alpha)}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{2z\sqrt{D}}{p}\right).

Similarly, for the other root of the congruence (
3.7
), we get

e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha^{\ast})\right)=e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D})\right),

and

\left(\frac{A(\alpha)}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-2z\sqrt{D}}{p}\right).

So altogether, we obtain

\begin{split}U_{1}&=\frac{N^{2}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2},p)=1\\
r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2})=0\\
D=\Box\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{1}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{2}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-l_{1}x_{0}-l_{2}y_{0}\right)\sum\limits_{(z,p)=1}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)C_{n-r}(z,D)\times\\
&\left(e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D})\right)+\left(\frac{-1}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D})\right)\right)+O_{\varepsilon}(1),\end{split}

where
D=\Box\bmod{p}
means that
D
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
and

C_{n-r}(z,D):=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }n-r\mbox{ is even,}\\
\left(\frac{-2z\sqrt{D}}{p}\right)\cdot\frac{G_{p}}{\sqrt{p}}&\mbox{ if }n-r\mbox{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Now we calculate
E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right)
when
r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2})>0
from (
3.5
).
In this case,
\mbox{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}_{0,k_{1},k_{2}})=r
and
p^{-r}f^{\prime}_{0,k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\equiv 0\bmod{p}
implies

\left((bB-cA)l_{2}-cBl_{1}\right)+2(aBl_{2}-cAl_{1})t\equiv 0\bmod{p}.

Therefore, the congruence

(3.9)

C_{0}(\alpha_{1})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n-r}}

has exactly one root which is given by

\alpha_{1}^{\ast}=\frac{\frac{-(l_{2}Ba-l_{1}Ac)+\sqrt{D}}{p^{r^{\prime}}}}{\frac{(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}}{p^{r^{\prime}}}}\bmod{p^{n-r}},

where
\sqrt{D}
is the root of the congruence

x^{2}\equiv D\bmod{p^{n-r+r^{\prime}}},

which satisfies
\sqrt{D}\equiv(l_{2}Ba-l_{1}Ac)\bmod{p^{r^{\prime}}}.
We now observe that

e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{0,p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha_{1}^{\ast})\right)=e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D})\right),

and

\left(\frac{A(\alpha_{1})}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{2z\sqrt{D}}{p}\right).

We see that
\mbox{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}_{r^{\prime},k_{1},k_{2}})=r+2r^{\prime}
and
p^{-r-2r^{\prime}}f^{\prime}_{r^{\prime},k_{1},k_{2}}(t)\equiv 0\bmod{p}
implies

2(aBl_{2}-cAl_{1})t+\frac{\left((bB-cA)k_{2}-cBk_{1}\right)}{p^{r^{\prime}}}t^{2}\equiv 0\bmod{p}.

Therefore, the congruence

(3.10)

p^{-2r^{\prime}}C_{r^{\prime}}(\alpha_{2})\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n-r}}

has two roots, one of them being
\alpha=0,
which has been excluded from the summation, and the other root is given by

\alpha_{2}^{\ast}=\frac{-(l_{2}Ba-l_{1}Ac)-\sqrt{D}}{\frac{l_{2}(Bb-Ac)-l_{1}Bc}{p^{r^{\prime}}}}\bmod{p^{n-r}.}

We now calculate that

e_{p^{n}}\left(f_{r^{\prime},p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}(\alpha_{2}^{\ast})\right)=e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D})\right).

We further calculate that

f_{r^{\prime},p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2}}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{2}^{\ast})=-z\cdot\frac{\sqrt{D}}{(a+b\alpha^{\ast}+c(\alpha^{\ast})^{2})^{2}}\cdot p^{r+2r^{\prime}}.

Therefore,

\left(\frac{A(\alpha_{2})}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-2z\sqrt{D}}{p}\right).

So by (
3.5
) if
r^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2})\neq 0,

(3.11)

\begin{split}E\left(p^{r}l_{1},p^{r}l_{2},z;p^{n}\right)&=C_{n-r}(z,D)\times\\
&\left(e_{p-r}(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D}))p^{\frac{(n+r)}{2}}+E(r^{\prime})\frac{1}{p^{r^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{-1}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D}))p^{\frac{(n+r+2r^{\prime})}{2}}\right)\\
&=C_{n-r}(z,D)\times\\
&\left(e_{p-r}(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D}))+E(r^{\prime})\left(\frac{-1}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D}))\right)p^{\frac{(n+r)}{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore,
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are of almost same form. If
(zD,p)=1
, using (
2.1
) we write
C_{n-r}(z,D)
more compactly as

C_{n-r}(z,D)=\frac{G_{p^{n-r}}}{p^{(n-r)/2}}\cdot\left(\frac{-2z\sqrt{D}}{p^{n-r}}\right).

Now, using Proposition
5
and (
3.5
),
U_{3}
can be written as

\begin{split}U_{3}=&\frac{N^{2}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum_{(z,p)=1}\phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l,p)=1\\
l\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{aAlN}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{(bA-aB)lN}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-aAlx_{0}-(bA-aB)ly_{0}\right)p^{r/2}\times\\
&e_{p^{n-r}}\left(l\cdot\frac{z(-16a\Delta+(4ac-b^{2})(2af-Ad+ad\alpha+ae\beta))}{4ac-b^{2}}\right)\cdot\frac{G_{p^{n-r}}}{p^{(n-r)/2}}\cdot\left(\frac{az\Delta}{p^{n-r}}\right).\end{split}

So, trivially

\begin{split}U_{3}&\ll\frac{N^{2}}{p^{3n/2}}\cdot N\cdot\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}\frac{p^{n-r}}{N}\cdot p^{n\varepsilon}\cdot p^{r/2}\\
&\ll\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n/2}}\cdot p^{n\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Thus, one can get

(3.12)

\begin{split}U\leq&\frac{N^{2}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\cdot\frac{G_{p^{n-r}}}{p^{(n-r)/2}}\cdot\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}D=1\\
D\equiv\Box\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}F_{n-r}(D)\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{z}{p^{n-r}}\right)\times\\
&\left(e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}+2\sqrt{D})\right)+\left(\frac{-1}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}-2\sqrt{D})\right)\right)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\varepsilon)}}\right),\end{split}

where

(3.13)

F_{n-r}(D):=\left(\frac{-2\sqrt{D}}{p^{n-r}}\right)\cdot\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2},p)=1\\
(aB-bA)l_{1}+aAl_{2}\neq 0\\
-4\Delta(al_{2}^{2}-bl_{1}l_{2}+cl_{1}^{2})=D\end{subarray}}\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{1}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{l_{2}N}{p^{n-r}}\right)e_{p^{n-r}}\left(-l_{1}x_{0}-l_{2}y_{0}\right).

3.3.
Single Poisson summation and final count

Now we split the sum over
z
in (
3.12
) into sub-sums over residue classes modulo
p
and perform Poisson summation, getting

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\cdot&\left(\frac{z}{p^{n-r}}\right)\cdot e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})\right)\\
=&\sum\limits_{u=1}^{p}\left(\frac{u}{p^{n-r}}\right)\sum\limits_{z\equiv u\bmod{p}}\Phi\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{N}\right)\cdot e_{p^{n-r}}\left(z\cdot\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})\right)\\
=&\frac{N}{p}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum\limits_{u=1}^{p}\left(\frac{u}{p^{n-r}}\right)\cdot e_{p}\left(uv-z_{0}\left(v-\frac{\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})}{p^{n-r-1}}\right)\right)\right)\times\\
&\hat{\Phi}\left(\frac{N}{p}\left(v-\frac{\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})}{p^{n-r-1}}\right)\right).\end{split}

Using the rapid decay of
\hat{\Phi}
, the above is
O(N)
if
||\frac{\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})}{p^{n-r-1}}||\leq p^{1+n\varepsilon}N^{-1}
and negligible otherwise, when
n
is sufficiently large. We may therefore constraint
\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})
to the range
[0,p^{n-r})
and then write
\overline{4ac-b^{2}}(J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\pm 2\sqrt{D})=wp^{n-r-1}+l_{3}
, where
w=0,...,p-1
and
|l_{3}|\leq L_{r}
with

L_{r}:=p^{n-r+n\varepsilon}N^{-1}.

Moreover, the summations over
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
in (
3.13
) can be cut off at
|l_{1}|,|l_{2}|\leq L_{r}
at the cost of a negligible error if
n
is large enough. It follows that

\begin{split}U\ll&\frac{N^{3}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\sum\limits_{w=0}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
|l_{1}|,|l_{2}|,|l_{3}|\leq L_{r}\\
\left((b^{2}-4ac)(wp^{n-r-1}+l_{3})-J_{l_{1},l_{2}}\right)^{2}\equiv 4D\bmod{p^{n-r}}\end{subarray}}1+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\varepsilon)}}\right).\end{split}

Now the congruence above implies

(3.14)

Ml_{1}^{2}+Nl_{1}l_{2}+Ol_{2}^{2}+Pl_{1}l_{3}+Ql_{2}l_{3}+Rl_{3}^{2}\equiv 0\bmod{p^{n-r-1}},

where
M=16a\Delta+(be-2cd)^{2}
,
N=-16b\Delta+2(be-2cd)(bd-2ae)
,
O=16c\Delta+(bd-2ae)^{2}
,
P=2(4ac-b^{2})(2cd-be)
,
Q=2(4ac-b^{2})(2ae-bd)
and
R=(4ac-b^{2})^{2}.

Let
H:=\max\{|M|,|N|,|O|,|P|,|Q|,|Q|\}
.
Now if
6HL_{r}^{2}<p^{n-r-1}
, then the congruence above can be replaced by the equation

Q^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}):=Ml_{1}^{2}+Nl_{1}l_{2}+Ol_{2}^{2}+Pl_{1}l_{3}+Ql_{2}l_{3}+Rl_{3}^{2}=0.

Clearly, this is the case when
N\geq p^{n/2+2n\varepsilon}
.
We calculate the determinant of the associated matrix of
Q^{\prime}
as

\Delta_{Q^{\prime}}=64\Delta^{2}(4ac-b^{2})^{3}.

Therefore by our assumptions,
Q^{\prime}
is non-singular. So we use Proposition
6
,
obtaining

(3.15)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
|l_{1}|,|l_{2}|,|l_{3}|\leq L_{r}\\
Q^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})=0\end{subarray}}1&\ll\sum_{d=1}^{L_{r}}\tau(|\Delta_{Q^{\prime}}|)\left(1+\frac{L_{r}\delta_{Q^{\prime}}^{1/2}}{|\Delta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{1/3}}\cdot\frac{1}{d}\right)\\
&\ll_{\varepsilon}\tau(|\Delta_{Q^{\prime}}|)\left(L_{r}+\frac{L_{r}^{1+\varepsilon}\delta_{Q^{\prime}}^{1/2}}{|\Delta_{Q^{\prime}}|^{1/3}}\right)\\
&\ll_{\varepsilon}L_{r}^{1+\varepsilon}.\end{split}

Thus, in the case when
N\geq p^{n/2+2n\varepsilon}
we get

(3.16)

\begin{split}U&\ll\frac{N^{3}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\\
|l_{1}|,|l_{2}|,|l_{3}|\leq L_{r}\\
Q^{\prime}(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3})=0\end{subarray}}1+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\epsilon)}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{N^{3}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\cdot L_{r}^{1+\varepsilon}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\epsilon)}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{N^{3}}{p^{3n/2}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-2}p^{r/2}\cdot p^{n-r+2n\varepsilon-r\varepsilon+n\varepsilon^{2}}N^{-1-\varepsilon}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\epsilon)}}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{N^{2-\varepsilon}}{p^{n/2}}\cdot p^{n(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})}+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{N^{2}}{p^{n(1/2-\epsilon)}}\right).\end{split}

This needs to be compared to the main term which is of size

T_{0}\asymp\frac{N^{3}}{p^{n}}.

If
N\geq p^{(1/2+2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})n}
, then
U=o(T_{0})
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1
upon changing
2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}
into
\varepsilon
.

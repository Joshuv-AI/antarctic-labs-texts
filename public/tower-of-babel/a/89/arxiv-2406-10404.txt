1.
Introduction

It was discovered by Moser in
[
4
]
that the equation

\binom{2n}{n}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{2b}{b}

has no solutions. This result was further extended by P. Erdos in
[
1
]
, where he proved that

\binom{2m}{m}\nmid\binom{2n}{n}

for
2m>n
.
Following the line investigated by Moser in
[
4
]
and Erdos in
[
1
]
, the purpose of this paper is to prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k,x,b
be positive integers and
a
be a nonnegative integer such that
k=a+b
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b},\qquad\text{ if and only if }x=a=1.

To prove this result, we need to overcome the difficulty that integers
a
and
b
are no longer symmetric in the equation
\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}
, unlike the case discussed in
[
4
]
and
[
1
]
. The key tool used in our proof is an analysis of the existence of prime numbers in the product of consecutive integers, which was extensively investigated in
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
.

2.
Proof of Main Result

To prove our main result, we will break the proof up into different cases. We will state these cases as propositions, and prove them throughout the paper by proving smaller lemmas. First, we will prove the result when
x\geq a
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
k,x,b
be positive integers and
a
be a nonnegative integer such that
k=a+b
. Assume that
x\geq a
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}\leq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
x=a=1
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b}{b}}{\binom{2k}{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2a)!}{a!\cdot a!}\cdot\frac{(a+2b)!}{b!(a+b)!}\cdot\frac{k!\cdot k!}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2a)(2a-1)\cdots(a+1)}{a(a-1)\cdots 1}\cdot\frac{k(k-1)\cdots(b+1)}{(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(a+2b+1)}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\frac{2a-1}{a-1}\frac{2a-2}{a-2}\cdots\frac{a+1}{1}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{k-1}{2k-1}\frac{k-2}{2k-2}\cdots\frac{k-a+1}{2k-a+1}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2a-1}{a-1}\frac{k-1}{2k-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{2a-2}{a-2}\frac{k-2}{2k-2}\Big)\cdots\Big(\frac{a+1}{1}\frac{k-a+1}{2k-a+1}\Big)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &>1\quad\text{ for }1<a<k\\
&=1\quad\text{ for }a=0,1\end{aligned}\right.

Now, if
x>a
, then

\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}>\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b}{b}\geq\binom{2k}{k}.

Furthermore, if
x=a>1
, then

\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b}{b}>\binom{2k}{k}.

When
x=a=1
, we have

\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b}{b}=\binom{2}{1}\binom{1+2b}{b}=\binom{2k}{k}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k,x,a,b
be positive integers such that
k=a+b
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b-1}{b},\qquad\text{ for }\ 4<2a\leq k.

Proof.

Let
k,x,a,b
be positive integers such that
k=a+b
. Assume that

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b-1}{b}\ \ \text{ for }4<2a\leq k.

(2.1)

So,

\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}=\frac{(2a)!}{(a!)^{2}}\cdot\frac{(2k-a-1)!}{(k-a)!(k-1)!}.

Thus,

\frac{2k(2k-1)(2k-2)\cdots(2k-a)}{k\cdot k\cdot(k-1)\cdots(k-a+1)}=\frac{(2a)(2a-1)\cdots(a+2)(a+1)}{a(a-1)\cdots 2\cdot 1}.

We have, for
k>a
,

\frac{2k-1}{k}<2,\ \frac{2k-2}{k-1}<\frac{2a-1}{a-1},\ \frac{2k-3}{k-2}<\frac{2a-2}{a-2},\cdots,\frac{2k-a+1}{k-a+2}<\frac{a+2}{2}

and

2\cdot\frac{2k-a}{k-a+1}=2\Big(2+\frac{a-2}{k-a+1}\Big)<6\leq a+1\qquad\text{ for }a\geq 5.

Thus, if
a\geq 5
, then

\binom{2k}{k}<\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a+2b-1}{b}\qquad\text{ for }k=a+b.

When
a=4
, the assumption (
2.1
) becomes

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{8}{4}\binom{2k-5}{k-4},\qquad\text{ for }k\geq 8,

or

\frac{(2k)(2k-1)(2k-2)(2k-3)(2k-4)}{k\cdot k(k-1)(k-2)(k-3)}=\binom{8}{4}.

A simple computation shows that

8(2k-1)(2k-3)=70k(k-3),

which has a unique integer solution
k=4
. Thus

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{8}{4}\binom{2k-5}{k-4},\qquad\text{ for }k\geq 8.

When
a=3
, the assumption (
2.1
) becomes

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{6}{3}\binom{2k-4}{k-3},\qquad\text{ for }k\geq 6.

Equivalently, we have,

\frac{(2k)(2k-1)(2k-2)(2k-3)}{k\cdot k(k-1)(k-2)}=\binom{6}{3}.

It induces that

4(2k-1)(2k-3)=20k(k-2),

where
k=-1,3
are integer solutions. Thus,

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{6}{3}\binom{2k-4}{k-3},\qquad\text{ for }k\geq 6.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
k,x,a,b
be positive integers such that
k=a+b

If

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

Proof.

We follows the strategy used in
[
4
]
. Suppose that
a\geq\frac{1}{2}k
.
Since
b\geq 3
, we know
k\geq 6
. Assume
\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.
Then

\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}}=\frac{(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2a+1)}{(k(k-1)\cdots(a+1))^{2}}=\binom{x+2b}{b}

is an integer. By Lemma
2.6
, there exists a prime divisor
p
of the product
(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2a+1)
such that

We claim that
p
is not a divisor of

. In fact assume that
p
divides
k-i
for some

and
\alpha
is the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
k-i
. Then
\alpha
is also the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
2(k-i)
, and
p
is not a divisor of other terms

, with
j\neq 2i
, in the numerator. In other words,
\alpha
is the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2a+1)
. However,
p^{2\alpha}
divides
(k(k-1)\cdots(a+1))^{2}
. This contradicts with the assumption that
\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}}
is an integer. Hence
p
doesn’t divide the denominator
k(k-1)\cdots(a+1)
.

So, we have found that
p
divides
\binom{x+2b}{b}
. Since

. By Lemma

, we obtain, for

However,

\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}}=\frac{(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2a+1)}{(k(k-1)\cdots(a+1))^{2}}=2^{b}\cdot\frac{(2k-1)(2k-3)\cdots(2a+1)}{k(k-1)\cdots(a+1)}<4^{b}.

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
k,x,a,b
be positive integers such that
k=a+b
.
If
a\geq\frac{1}{2}k

, then

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
, we might assume that
1\leq b<3
. When

x\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\binom{2(k-1)}{k-1}\binom{x+2}{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2(k-1))!}{((k-1)!)^{2}}(x+2)

\displaystyle=\binom{2k}{k}(x+2)\cdot\frac{k^{2}}{2k(2k-1)}

\displaystyle>\binom{2k}{k}.

When

\frac{\binom{2(k-2)}{k-2}\binom{x+4}{2}}{\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{(x+4)(x+3)}{2}\frac{k^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{(2k)(2k-1)(2k-2)(2k-3)}.

\displaystyle\frac{(x+4)(x+3)}{2}\cdot\frac{k^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k(2k-1)(2k-2)(2k-3)}

\displaystyle>1.

If

\frac{(2+4)(2+3)}{2}\cdot\frac{k^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{2k(2k-1)(2k-2)(2k-3)}=1

\binom{2(k-2)}{k-2}\binom{x+4}{2}\neq\binom{2k}{k}.

We will now prove our main result when

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
k,a,b
be positive integers such that
k=a+b
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{1+2b}{b}\quad\text{ if and only if }a=1.

Proof.

First, assume
a=1
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{1+2b}{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k)!\cdot b!\cdot(b+1)!}{(1+2b)!\cdot k!\cdot k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+2b)(1+2b)\dots(b+2)}{(1+2b)(2b)\dots(b+2)}\cdot\frac{b(b-1)\dots 1}{(b+1)b(b-1)\dots 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1+b)}{1+b}

\displaystyle=2

\displaystyle=\binom{2a}{a}

Next, assume
\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{1+2b}{b}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we might assume that
k>10
. Lemma
2.8
implies that, if
a\geq\frac{1}{2}k
and
b\geq 3
, then
\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{1+2b}{b}
. Moreover, when
b=1,2
, the equation

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{1+2b}{b}

has no integer solution for
k>10
. Therefore, we might assume that
a<\frac{1}{2}k
, so
b>\frac{1}{2}k
. For the purpose of the contradiction, we further assume that
a>1
.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{1+2b}{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2b+2)}{(k(k-1)\cdots(b+1))(k(k-1)\cdots(b+2))}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2b+3)\cdot 2}{(k(k-1)\cdots(b+2))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\binom{2a}{a}

is an integer. By Lemma
2.6
, there exists a prime divisor
p
of the product
(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2b+3)
such that
p>\frac{3}{2}(2k-2b-2)=3a-3>2.
We claim that
p
is not a divisor of
k(k-1)\cdots(b+2)
. In fact assume that
p
divides
k-i
for some
0\leq i\leq a-1
and
\alpha
is the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
k-i
. Then
\alpha
is also the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
2(k-i)
, and
p
is not a divisor of other terms
2k-j
, with
j\neq 2i
, in the numerator. In other words,
\alpha
is the largest positive integer such that
p^{\alpha}
divides
(2k)(2k-1)\cdots(2b+3)\cdot 2
. However,
p^{2\alpha}
divides
(k(k-1)\cdots(b+2))^{2}
. This contradicts with the assumption that
\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{1+2b}{b}}
is an integer. Hence
p
doesn’t divide the denominator
k(k-1)\cdots(b+2)
.

So, we must have
p
divides
\binom{2a}{a}
, whence
2a\geq p>3a-3
. So
a<3
. From the assumption that
a>1
, we have
a=2
. Now that equation
\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{1+2b}{b}
becomes

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{4}{2}\binom{2k-3}{k-2},\qquad\text{ when }a=2.

Equivalently, we get,

\frac{(2k)(2k-1)(2k-2)}{k\cdot k(k-1)}=6,

Therefore,

,

\displaystyle\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}\binom{2b}{b}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}k^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{2k+\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{8a}}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{2a+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{8b}}b^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{2b+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{8a}+\frac{1}{8b}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi ab}{k}}

\displaystyle<e^{1/4}\sqrt{\frac{3\pi x}{2}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{(2b+1)(2b+2)\cdots(2b+x)}{(b+1)(b+2)\cdots(b+x)}

\displaystyle=\Big(2-\frac{1}{b+1}\Big)\Big(2-\frac{2}{b+2}\Big)\cdots\Big(2-\frac{x}{b+x}\Big)

\displaystyle>\Big(2-\frac{x}{b+x}\Big)^{x}>\Big(2-\frac{a}{b+a}\Big)^{x}\geq\Big(2-\frac{1}{5}\Big)^{x}.

Note that
\big(\frac{9}{5}\big)^{x}-e^{1/4}\sqrt{3\pi x/2}
is an increasing function for
x\geq 2
and

\big(\frac{9}{5}\big)^{3}>e^{1/4}\sqrt{9\pi/2}
. So, for
x\geq 3
and
k\geq 150
,

\frac{(2b+1)(2b+2)\cdots(2b+x)}{(b+1)(b+2)\cdots(b+x)}>\Big(\frac{9}{5}\Big)^{x}>e^{1/4}\sqrt{\frac{3\pi x}{2}}>\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}\binom{2b}{b}}

which gives us

\binom{x+2b}{b}=\binom{2b}{b}\cdot\frac{(2b+1)(2b+2)\cdots(2b+x)}{(b+1)(b+2)\cdots(b+x)}>\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{\binom{2a}{a}}

and therefore,

\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}>\binom{2k}{k}

which is a contradiction to our assumption.
Also, we know that
x<a<\frac{3}{2}x
, so when
x=2
, we must have
2<a<3
, a contradiction to
a
an integer. Therefore, for
x\geq 2
and
k\geq 150
,

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

∎

Finally, we will prove our main result when
x\geq 2
and
x<k<150
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
k,x,a,b
be positive integers such
x\geq 2
,
k=a+b
, and
x<k<150
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b}.

Proof.

Using Maple, we verified that, for
x\geq 2
and
x<k<150
,

\binom{2k}{k}\neq\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2k}{k}.

∎

Now, we can prove the following, main theorem of the paper.

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
k,x,b
be positive integers and
a
be a nonnegative integer such that

k=a+b
. Then

\binom{2k}{k}=\binom{2a}{a}\binom{x+2b}{b},\qquad\text{ if and only if }x=a=1.

Proof.

When
x=1
, we obtain the result from Proposition
2.10
.

When
x\geq 2
, and
k\geq 150
, we obtain the result from Proposition
2.15
.

When
x\geq 2
, and
x<k\leq 150
, we obtain the result from Proposition
2.16
.

When
x\geq 2
, and
x\geq k
, we obtain the result from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

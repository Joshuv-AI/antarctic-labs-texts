Introduction

Throughout this article,
R
denotes a commutative Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}.
Let
J^{*}
denote the tight closure of an ideal

In this paper, we study some properties of the tight closure of powers of an ideal.
The depth of the associated graded ring

of the filtration
\mathcal{T}=\{(I^{n})^{*}\}
where
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal is an important invariant for understanding the tight Hilbert polynomial

for the tight Hilbert function

By a theorem of D. Rees,

[
17

, if

is analytically unramified Noetherian
local ring, then the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}^{*}(I)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(I^{n})^{*}t^{n}
is a finite module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}t^{n}.
This means that
G^{*}(I)
is a finitely generated graded module over the associated graded ring

Therefore the

coincides with a polynomial,

, of degree

. We write

P_{I}^{*}(n)=e_{0}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}^{*}(I)

Here

The vanishing of the Chern number of

Recall that a Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic is called an

-rational ring if all ideals generated by parameters are tightly closed. It is proved in

Let

be an ideal generated by a system of parameters and

be analytically unramified Cohen-Macaulay local ring.
If the depth of

then by a result of

[
12
]
the tight Hilbert coefficients of

e_{j}^{*}(I)=\sum_{n\geq j}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\ell((I^{n})^{*}/I(I^{n-1})^{*}).

Therefore, it is useful to know the depth of
G^{*}(I).

If

are elements of a commutative Noetherian ring and

We prove the following result about depth of

.

Let

\displaystyle(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}R^{+}\cap R.

2.
Some properties of the tight closure of powers of parameter ideals

In this section, we assume that
R
is a Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
p.
For a ring
R,
the subset of
R
consisting of all the elements which are not contained in any minimal prime ideals of
R,
is denoted by
R^{0}.
Let
q=p^{e}.
If
I=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
is an ideal of
R
then
I^{[q]}=(a_{1}^{q},\ldots,a_{r}^{q}).
For any positive integer
n,

(I^{n})^{[q]}=(I^{[q]})^{n}.
An element
x\in R
is said to be in the
tight closure
I^{*}
of
I
if there exists
c\in R^{0}
such that
cx^{q}\in I^{[q]},
for all sufficiently large
q.

An ideal
I
is called tightly closed if
I=I^{*}.
The tight filtration of
I
is given by
\mathcal{T}=\{(I^{n})^{*}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.
We note that
(I^{n})^{*}\subset\overline{I^{n}},
for all
n\geq 1.

We begin by recalling a few results about big Cohen-Macaulay algebras and modules needed later.
Let
R
be an integral domain with fraction field
K.
Let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K.
The integral closure of
R
in
\overline{K},
denoted
R^{+},
is called the
absolute integral closure of
R.
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local Noetherian ring and
M
be a non-zero
R
-module, we say that
M
is a
big Cohen-Macaulay

R
-module if some system of parameters for
R
is a regular sequence on
M.
We say that

M
is a
balanced big Cohen-Macaulay

R
-module if every system of parameters for
R
is a regular sequence on
M.

We need the following results for the proof of the Main Theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Colon capturing,
[
4
, Theorem 10.1.9]

Let
R
be an equidimensional residue class ring of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring

, and

be parameters of

Then for

j=0,1,\ldots,t-1.

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}):_{R}x_{j+1}\subset(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})^{*}.

A very useful version of colon capturing is described in the next result.

.

[

Let

be an equidimensional excellent local ring. Let

be a system of parameters for

Let

\displaystyle(IR)^{*}:_{R}JR)

\displaystyle=

.

[

Let

be a complete local domain of prime characteristic. Then for any

-ideal

,

if and only if

[

Let

Theorem 2.5
.

[

Let

be a Noetherian locally excellent domain of prime characteristic

If

The above theorem holds for powers of

It follows from

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(R,m)
be an excellent local domain of prime characteristic

Let

be any parameter ideal of

Then

(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}R^{+}\cap R.

Proof.

(
Karen Smith.
)
Use induction on

The

case is the Theorem
2.5
.
Let
n\geq 2.
By Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, it is enough to show that

(I^{n})^{*}\subset I^{n}R^{+}\cap R.

We assume that the result is true for
n-1.
Let
z\in(I^{n})^{*}.

Since
(I^{n})^{*}\subset(I^{n-1})^{*},
by induction hypothesis,
z=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}x^{\mu_{i}}
where the coefficients
a_{i}\in R^{+}
and

ranges through all degree

Let

Then

a_{i}x^{\mu_{i}}\in((x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{t})^{n}+J_{i})^{*}

in

where

is the ideal generated by all the degree

except

Since

and
a_{i}\in((x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{t})^{n}+J_{i})^{*}:x^{\mu_{i}},

Therefore

∎

There are several interesting consequences of Theorem
2.6
.
These may be proved without using it. However, Theorem

,
enables us to provide short unified proofs.

Huneke
[
13
, Theorem 4.7]
and Itoh
[

independently proved that in a

-dimensional local ring, for an ideal generated by a regular sequence, for all

I^{n}\cap\overline{I^{n+1}}=I^{n}\overline{I}.

The analogue of Huneke-Itoh intersection theorem for tight closure of an ideal generated by regular sequence has been proved in
[

, Theorem 8.20]
. However, we prove it in an excellent local Noetherian domain where

is a parameter ideal. This result plays an important role in the study of the tight Hilbert polynomial.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent local Noetherian domain of prime characteristic. Let
I
be an ideal generated by parameters in
R.
Then for all
n\geq 0,

I^{n}\cap(I^{n+1})^{*}=I^{n}I^{*}.

Proof.

Let
I
be the ideal generated by
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{t}.

The inclusion
I^{n}I^{*}\subset I^{n}\cap(I^{n+1})^{*}
is clear since
I^{*}J^{*}\subset(I^{*}J^{*})^{*}=(IJ)^{*}.
For the reverse inclusion, let
a\in I^{n}\cap(I^{n+1})^{*}=I^{n}\cap I^{n+1}R^{+}
where the equality follows from Theorem
2.6
.
We write

a=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}y_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;\;|\beta|=n+1}z_{\beta}x^{\beta}

for some
y_{\alpha}\in R
and
z_{\beta}\in R^{+}.
As
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{t}
forms a regular sequence in
R^{+}
we have
y_{\alpha}\in IR^{+}.
Hence
y_{\alpha}\in IR^{+}\cap R=I^{*}.
This implies that
a\in I^{n}I^{*}.

∎

The next result is due to K.-i. Watanabe for regular sequences in local rings of prime characteristic
[
6
, Corollary 4.3]
. We generalise it for parameter ideals in excellent local domains of prime characteristic.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional excellent local Noetherian domain of prime characteristic. Let
I
be an ideal generated by parameters in
R.
If
I
is tightly closed then
I^{n}
is tightly closed for all
n\geq 1.

Proof.

Use induction on
n.
For
n=1
the result is true. Suppose that
I,I^{2},\ldots,I^{n-1}
are tightly closed. Let
x\in(I^{n})^{*}.
Since
(I^{n})^{*}\subset(I^{n-1})^{*}=I^{n-1},
using Corollary
2.7
it follows that
x\in(I^{n})^{*}\cap I^{n-1}=I^{*}I^{n-1}=I^{n}.
Hence
I^{n}
is also tightly closed.
∎

3.
Cohen-Macaulay and Buchsbaum properties of
G^{*}(I)

In this section we discuss the Cohen-Macaulay property of the associated graded ring
G^{*}(I)
for an ideal
I
generated by a system of parameters in an excellent Cohen-Macaulay local domain. We also consider the Buchsbaum property of
G^{*}(I)
if
R
is an excellent Buchsbaum local domain.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent Cohen-Macaulay local domain of prime characteristic and
I=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{h})
be a parameter ideal. Then
0ptG^{*}(I)\geq h.
In particular, if
I
is generated by a system of parameters then
G^{*}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

By Valabrega-Valla
[
20
]
it is enough to show that for all
n\geq 0,
the
VV_{n}
property

I\cap(I^{n+1})^{*}=I(I^{n})^{*}

is satisfied.
We prove
VV_{n}
by induction on
n.
The statement is clearly true for
n=0.
Suppose that
n\geq 1
and

{VV}_{n-1}
is true. We prove
VV_{n}.
Let

z=\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{i}x_{i}\in(I^{n+1})^{*}=I^{n+1}R^{+}\cap R.

We show that
a_{i}\in(I^{n})^{*}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,h.
Since
z\in I\cap(I^{n})^{*},
by induction hypothesis,
a_{i}\in(I^{n-1})^{*}\subset I^{n-1}R^{+}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,h.
We can write

a_{i}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{h},\;\;|\alpha|=n-1}a_{i\alpha}x^{\alpha}\text{ for some }a_{i\alpha}\in R^{+}.

Since

z=\sum_{i=1}^{h}a_{i}x_{i}\in(I^{n+1})R^{+}
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{h}
is an
R^{+}
-regular sequence,
a_{i\alpha}\in IR^{+}
for all
i
and all
\alpha.
Hence
a_{i}\in(I^{n})R^{+}\cap R=(I^{n})^{*}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,h.
This proves
VV_{n}
for all
n.
Hence
0ptG^{*}(I)\geq h.
In particular if
h=d
then
G^{*}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})

Using

Consider the following statement:

(3.2.1)

\text{Let }z=\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}a_{i}^{2}\in(I^{n})^{*}\text{ for some }b_{i}\in R\text{ then }b_{i}\in(I^{n-2})^{*}.

We prove (

) for

using induction on

Let

Let

Since,
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
is an

-regular sequence, it follows that

Hence

Now, we prove that (

Let

By induction hypothesis it follows that

Therefore,

\displaystyle b_{i}=\sum_{|\alpha|=n-2}c_{\alpha}a^{\alpha},\text{ where }a^{\alpha}=a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots a_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}.

Note that

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\operatorname{Soc}(S/J)\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{r}k(-b_{i}+de).

Consider the graded

The

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}:0\longrightarrow S(-de)\xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}&\cdots&X_{d}\end{bmatrix}}S^{d}(-(d-1)e)\longrightarrow\cdots\rightarrow S^{d}(-e)\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow S/\mathfrak{m},

f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
is a homogeneous system of parameters,
R
is a finite
S
-module. By Auslander-Buchsbaum formula it follows that
\operatorname{pd}_{S}R+0pt_{S}R=d.
Since

R
is a free
S
-module. Let a graded free resolution of

be given by

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}S(-b_{d_{i}})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}S(-b_{1_{i}})\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow S/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\longrightarrow 0.

Since

is a flat
S
-module, tensoring the above gives free resolution for

as

where the twists remain unchanged as the maps are induced.

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}R(-b_{d_{i}})\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\oplus_{i}R(-b_{1_{i}})\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow R/I^{n}\longrightarrow 0.

Using Lemma

we conclude that

which implies the result by comparing degrees on either side of the graded isomorphism.

Next result gives a formula for

when

is the ideal generated by homogeneous system of parameters of the same degree.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
R=\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]}{(X_{0}^{r}+X_{1}^{r}+\cdots+X_{d}^{r})},

Let

be homogeneous system of parameters in

such that

(a)

(J^{n})^{*}=J^{n}+\mathfrak{m}^{(n-1)e+de},

(b)

G^{*}(J)
is Cohen Macaulay,

(c)

r^{*}(J)\leq\left[\frac{r-1-d}{e}\right]+1.

(d)
If
\left[\frac{r-1-d}{e}\right]\leq d-2

\mathcal{R}^{*}(J)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

(a) We begin by observing that

[

, Theorem 6.1]

\operatorname{Soc}(S/\mathfrak{n}^{n})=\frac{\mathfrak{n}^{n}:\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{n}^{n}}=\frac{\mathfrak{n}^{n-1}}{\mathfrak{n}^{n}}.
Since the Socle of

is generated by all the monomials of degree
n-1
in

it implies that degree of any minimal generator of the socle is
(n-1)e.
Using Lemma
3.4
for the pair

we get a one-to-one correspondence between the set degrees of minimal generators of

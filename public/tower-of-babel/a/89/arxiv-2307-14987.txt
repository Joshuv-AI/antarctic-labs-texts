Helical Separation Effect and helical heat transport for Dirac fermions
Victor E. Ambru s ,, Maxim N. Chernodub

Abstract
An ensemble of massless fermions can be characterized by its total helicity charge given by the sum of axial charges of particles minus the sum of axial charges of antiparticles. We show that charged massless fermions develop a dissipationless flow of helicity along the background magnetic field. We dub this transport phenomenon as the Helical Separation Effect (HSE). Contrary to its chiral cousin, the Chiral Separation Effect, the HSE produces the helical current in a neutral plasma in which all chemical potentials vanish. In addition, we uncover the Helical Magnetic Heat Effect which generates a heat flux of Dirac fermions along the magnetic field in the presence of non-vanishing helical charge density. We also discuss possible hydrodynamic modes associated with the HSE in neutral plasma.



I
Introduction

Massless or nearly massless fermions can be found in various areas of physics. They are present in theories of fundamental interactions and reveal themselves in cosmological models of the early Universe, ultra-hot relativistic plasmas, superfluids, and many other physical systems
Volovik 2003
. Discoveries of Dirac and Weyl semimetals, where the massless (chiral) fermions manifest as quasiparticle excitations, have opened up intriguing possibilities for experimental verification of relativistic phenomena associated with chiral fermions
Armitage et al. 2018
.

The pivotal properties of these excitations are typically linked to their vector (gauge), axial (chiral) and conformal (scale) symmetries. In the case of semimetals, these symmetries impact the electromagnetic
Vazifeh and Franz 2013
;
Chernodub et al. 2018a
, thermal
Gooth et al. 2017
;
Chernodub et al. 2022
, and elastic
Cortijo et al. 2015
responses of the materials. The peculiar characteristics of these semimetals are closely tied to the quantum anomaly that disrupts a continuous symmetry of an underlying classical theory due to quantum fluctuations
Fujikawa and Suzuki 2004
. In fundamental field theories, the vivid example is represented by the axial anomaly which gives rise to exotic transport phenomena of quarks, mediated by the evolving gluon fields’ topology in the expanding quark-gluon plasma of heavy-ion collisions
Kharzeev 2014
.

The continuous symmetries of massless fermions, leaving aside their conformal properties, are usually restricted to vector and axial symmetries, which lead to the conservation of vector (electric) and axial (chiral) charges. The excess of the number of particles over antiparticles, regardless of their spins, gives the vector charge. The sign of the spin projection on the momentum of a fermion or an anti-fermion, regardless of their vector charges, determines the chirality of the (anti-)particle in question. However, an ensemble of massless fermions can be characterized by a third, familiar, and, at the same time, intermittently disregarded quantity: helicity.

The similarity of fermionic helicity to fermion chirality often leads to the identification of these quantities, even though they represent distinct physical properties of fermions
Pal 2011
. The crucial difference – and the source of the persistent confusion – is rooted in the fact that the chirality of a single fermion identically coincides with its helicity while the chirality and helicity of a single
anti
-fermion are precisely opposite to each other. These properties lead to erroneous generic statements like “chirality is the same as helicity,” which is invalid for systems containing both particles and antiparticles. For example, a system made of a fermion and an anti-fermion can reside in a state with double total chirality and vanishing helicity (when the chiralities of the fermion and anti-fermion are the same), or in another state in which the total chirality equals zero, while helicity is non-vanishing (when the chiralities of the fermion and anti-fermion are opposite).

The helicity and chirality numbers coincide only for ensembles of massless fermions that consist only of particles with vector charges of the same sign. However, in various physically relevant environments – for example, in neutral finite-temperature plasmas or systems at high temperatures – this condition is not met, and the helicity should be treated on the same footing as chirality.

The vector, axial, and helical charges form a triad of quantities that characterize an ensemble of massless fermions and anti-fermions. The conservation of the vector
Q_{V}
, axial
Q_{A}
and helical
Q_{H}
charges in closed systems of free massless fermions allows us to introduce the thermodynamically conjugated chemical potentials that determine these charges in thermodynamic system. In particular, we introduce the helical chemical potential
\mu_{H}
to encode the helicity imbalance, alongside the more familiar vector and axial chemical potentials,
\mu_{V}
and
\mu_{A}
, respectively. As a result, the fermion ensemble is described by the Fermi-Dirac distribution at finite temperature and chemical potentials
\mu_{\ell}
associated with the vector (
\ell=V
), axial (
\ell=A
), and helical (
\ell=H
) charges. The presence of the helical charge in interacting fermionic matter affects its thermodynamics, leading to a rich phase diagram
Chernodub and Ambrus 2021
;
Wan and Ruggieri 2021
, in clear distinction with the thermodynamic effects of the axial charge density
Chernodub and Nedelin 2011
;
Ruggieri 2011
. Moreover, in addition to the standard vector
{\bm{j}}_{V}
and axial
{\bm{j}}_{A}
currents, the helical degree of freedom allows us to construct the helical current
{\bm{j}}_{H}
which determines how the helicity is transferred in the medium by fermions.

In our paper, we concentrate on anomalous transport phenomena associated with helical properties of massless fermions in the presence of the background magnetic field following our earlier study to the fermion helicity in vortical backgrounds
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2022
. The main result of this paper is represented by the following formula for the helical current, which we dub the Helical Separation Effect (HSE):

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{H}=\frac{T\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}q{\bm{B}}\qquad\text{for}\quad\mu_{V}=\mu_{A}=\mu_{H}=0\,,

(1)

which shows that in the background of a time-independent uniform magnetic field
{\bm{B}}
, the neutral matter develops a helical current along the direction of the magnetic field. The associated helical magnetic conductivity is proportional to the temperature
T
, implying that the higher the temperature, the stronger the helical current. Equation (
1
) is invariant under time reversal transformation,
t\to-t
, implying that the generated helical current is a dissipationless quantity.

Notice that magnetic fields can also generate dissipationless (persistent) electric current in small metallic rings via the Aharonov-Bohm effect
Byers and Yang 1961
;
Deaver and Fairbank 1961
;
Cotăescu et al. 2016
. This effect, however, has a different nature than the anomalous transport effects of the kind (
1
) since the persistent current in a ring (i) emerges in the transverse direction to the magnetic field and (ii) disappears in the thermodynamic limit, when the radius of the ring becomes infinite.

As the helicity of massless fermions is similar to the zilch quantum number for photons
Lipkin 1964
, the HSE (
1
) is a counterpart of the Zilch Vortical Effect which shows how the polarization of photons is transferred in the rotating photon gas
Chernodub et al. 2018b
. We have shown in Ref.
Ambrus and Chernodub 2022
that the helicity and axial vortical effects play a complementary role that elegantly accounts for the discrepancy between the polarization of particles and antiparticles. Similarly, one can expect that the new HSE will also play a complementary role to the influence of the CSE on the polarization of particles in heavy-ion collisions discussed in Ref.
Buzzegoli 2023
.

The HSE (
1
) has a simple generalization to the case of dense matter characterized by the triad of the chemical potentials
\mu_{\ell}
(
\ell=V,A,H
):

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{H}=\frac{T\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\kappa_{\beta}({\bm{\mu}})q{\bm{B}}\,,

(2)

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{\beta}({\bm{\mu}})=\frac{1}{4\ln 2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c=\pm 1\\
abc=1\end{subarray}}\ln\left(1+e^{\beta(a\mu_{V}+b\mu_{A}+c\mu_{H})}\right)\,,

(3)

is a thermodynamic factor normalized as
\kappa_{\beta}({\bm{0}})=1
.

The name “Helical Separation Effect” (
1
) is inspired by its similarity with the Chiral Separation Effect (CSE)
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
:

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{A}=\frac{\mu_{V}}{2\pi^{2}}q{\bm{B}}\,.

(4)

The CSE implies that the dense matter with
\mu_{V}\neq 0
develops the current of axial (chiral) charge
{\bm{j}}_{A}
along a background magnetic field
{\bm{B}}
. Contrary to the CSE (
4
), the HSE generates the helical current (
1
) in the absence of matter imbalance (with all chemical potentials vanishing). Notice that, while the strength of the CSE (
4
) is determined by the axial anomaly
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
, the relevance of this anomaly to the helical transport (
1
) is not certain due to the
\ln 2
factor in (
1
). A relevant discussion on the existence of possible helical anomalies in the context of the helical vortical conductivity is given in Ref.
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
. Finally, we mention that the Helical Separation Effect (
1
) should not be confused with the Helical Magnetic Effects which appear in the context of Weyl semimetals
Kharzeev et al. 2018
or in hydrodynamic transport
Yamamoto and Yang 2021
.

Another important result of our article is that the magnetic field in fermionic matter with nonzero helical density generates a heat current. Namely, the background magnetic field
\bm{B}
in the presence of a nonvanishing helical density encoded in the helical chemical potential
\mu_{H}
, produces a non-zero heat flux
j_{\epsilon}^{i}\equiv T^{0i}
along the axis of the magnetic field:

{\bm{j}}_{\epsilon}=\frac{\mu_{H}T\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}q{\bm{B}},\qquad

(5)

where
T^{0i}
is an off-diagonal component of the energy momentum tensor for massless Dirac fermions. The Helical Magnetic Heat Effect (
5
) represents the leading term in the strong field limit,
|qB|\gg T^{2}
, with higher-order corrections in chemical potentials of the order of
O({\bm{\mu}}^{3})
being neglected. Notice that the heat current (
5
) is unexpectedly proportional to the first power of temperature
T
, contrary to the
T^{2}
heat current produced by the mixed axial-gravitational anomaly
Landsteiner et al. 2011
. A similar unusual feature was also noted for the helical vortical effects which produce a heat current in vortical helical matter
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the conserved helicity operator for massive Dirac fermions in a magnetic field. A disadvantage of considering massive fermions is that, in addition to the explicit breaking of chirality conservation, they have a frame-dependence in the definition of helicity. Therefore, while discussing the applications of the Helical Separation Effect at the end of the paper, we will turn to the massless fermionic limit, similar to the generic tactics applied to the chiral degrees of freedom. We start our discussion from the discussion of the formal definition of the helicity operator in magnetic field, Sec.
II
, which leads us to the construction of the common mode solutions for the helicity operator and the Hamiltonian in Sec.
III
. Using the derived eigenmodes, the finite-temperature field theory at finite helical density is developed in Sec.
IV
which brings us to the anomalous transport in Sec.
V
. We derive not only the Helical Separation Effect (
1
), but also the corrections of the helical charges to the chiral magnetic and chiral vortical conductivities as well as to the heat transport. The interplay of the fluctuations of the helical charge density and the energy density – supported by the Helical Separation Effect (
1
) and the Helical Magnetic Heat Effect (
5
) – could, in principle, lead to the appearance of new hydrodynamic excitations. In Sec.
VI
we analyse this possibility for a
neutral
plasma and show that the helical degree of freedom does not generate a new hydrodynamic wave if all chemical potentials vanish. Our conclusions are summarized in the last section. Throughout this paper, Planck units (
\hbar=c=k_{B}=1
) and the
(+---)
metric signature are adopted.

II
Helicity operator in magnetic field

The symmetric Lagrangian for the Dirac field
\psi
with mass
M
coupled to an external electromagnetic potential reads as follows:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}\overleftrightarrow{\not{\partial}}\psi-q\overline{\psi}\not{A}\psi-M\overline{\psi}\psi,

(6)

where we use the standard convention that
q=-e<0
is the
(negative) electron charge. The Dirac equation (
6
) can be obtained using the Euler-Lagrange formalism for both
\psi
and
\overline{\psi}
:

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-q\not{A}-M)\psi

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(i\overleftarrow{\not{\partial}}+q\not{A}+M)

\displaystyle=0.

(7)

The equation for
\psi
can be cast in the form

i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=H\psi,\qquad H=\gamma^{0}(-i\bm{\gamma}\cdot\bm{\nabla}+q\not{A}+M),

(8)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian operator.

For the case of an electron in a magnetic field, the four-potential
A^{\mu}
can be taken in the Coulomb gauge,

A^{\mu}=(0,{\bm{A}}),

(9)

where
{\bm{A}}\equiv{\bm{A}}({\mathbf{x}})
is a time-independent vector field. In this case, the Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=M\gamma^{0}+\gamma^{0}{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}

\displaystyle=M\gamma^{0}+2\gamma^{5}{\bm{S}}\cdot{\bm{\pi}},

(10)

where

{\bm{\pi}}=-i\bm{\nabla}-q{\bm{A}}

(11)

is the generalized momentum of a charged particle in an electromagnetic field and
{\bm{S}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{0}{\bm{\gamma}}
is the spin matrix.
Due to the time-independence of the Hamiltonian,
\partial_{t}H=0
, we can take particle normal-mode solutions
U_{j}
which are eigenfunctions of the operator
H
:

HU_{j}=E_{j}U_{j}\Rightarrow U_{j}=e^{-iE_{j}t}\widetilde{U}_{j},

(12)

where
\widetilde{U}_{j}\equiv\widetilde{U}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
is a time-independent spinor field and the cumulative symbol
j
labels distinct solutions to be discussed in detail later. Similarly, the antiparticle solutions of Eq. (
7
) can be obtained by charge conjugation,

V_{j}=i\gamma^{2}U_{j}^{*}{\Big|}_{q\rightarrow-q}=e^{iE_{j}t}\widetilde{V}_{j},

(13)

with
\widetilde{V}_{j}\equiv\widetilde{V}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})=i\gamma^{2}\widetilde{U}_{j}^{*}{\big|}_{q\rightarrow-q}
. Clearly,
HV_{j}=-E_{j}V_{j}
.

In order to describe the polarization degree of freedom, we employ the helicity operator, defined as the projection of the spin along the generalized momentum:

h=\frac{{\bm{S}}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}}{\sqrt{H^{2}-M^{2}}}.

(14)

Taking into account that
[\gamma^{5},{\bm{S}}]=[\gamma^{0},{\bm{S}}]=0
, as well as
[H,{\bm{\pi}}]=0
, it is not difficult to show that
[H,{\bm{S}}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}]=0
, from which we directly conclude that
H
commutes with the helicity operator
h
:

[H,h]=0.

(15)

Writing conversely

h=\frac{\gamma^{5}}{2}\frac{H-M\gamma^{0}}{\sqrt{H^{2}-M^{2}}},

(16)

it is easy to see that
h^{2}=1/4
.

We can thus take the Hamiltonian eigenfunctions
U_{j}
,
V_{j}
to be simultaneously eigenfunctions of
h
, satisfying

hU_{j}=\lambda_{j}U_{j},\qquad hV_{j}=\lambda_{j}V_{j},

(17)

with the helicity eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}=\pm 1/2
such that
\lambda_{j}^{2}=1/4
. Note that, at the level of the classical theory, the helicity operator
h
preserves its eigenvalue under charge conjugation, in contradistinction to the Hamiltonian
H
or chirality
\gamma^{5}
(in the case of massless fermions). Using Eq. (
12
), the eigenvalue relations (
17
) become as follows:

\displaystyle h_{j}\widetilde{U}_{j}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}\widetilde{U}_{j},\qquad\ h_{j}^{c}\widetilde{V}_{j}=\lambda_{j}\widetilde{V}_{j},

(18)

where the helicity operator and its conjugated form reduce, respectively, to the following expressions:

\displaystyle h_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{j}+M\\
E_{j}-M&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle h_{j}^{c}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{j}-M\\
E_{j}+M&0\end{pmatrix}.

(19)

The above equations are automatically solved by taking

\displaystyle\widetilde{U}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi_{j},

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\\
-{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi_{j}^{c},

(20)

where we introduced the notation

{\cal K}^{\pm}_{j}=\sqrt{1\pm\frac{M}{E_{j}}},

(21)

The Pauli two-spinors
\phi_{j}
and
\phi_{j}^{c}
satisfy

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}\phi_{j},

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bm{\pi}}\phi^{c}_{j}

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}\phi^{c}_{j},

(22)

being related through charge conjugation:

\phi_{j}^{c}=i\sigma^{2}\phi_{j}^{*}{\Big|}_{q\rightarrow-q}.

(23)

The helicity operator (
14
) shares similarity with the zilch quantum number which – despite a seemingly non-local definition – is a well-defined quantity associated with the polarization of photons
Lipkin 1964
.

III
Mode solutions in a constant magnetic field

In this section, we review the eigensystem of a charged spinor particle in the background of the uniform homogeneous magnetic field, with an emphasis on the role of the helical degrees of freedom.

III.1
Energy levels

We seek to describe a particle moving in a constant magnetic field, oriented along the
z
axis:

{\bm{B}}=B\mathbf{e}_{z},

(24)

where
\mathbf{e}_{z}
is a unit vector pointing in the positive direction along the
z
axis and
B
is a constant real number.
The above magnetic field can be implemented via the asymmetric four-potential

A^{\mu}=Bx\delta^{\mu}_{y},

(25)

giving rise to the Faraday tensor

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}=-B(g_{\mu x}g_{\nu y}-g_{\mu y}g_{\nu x}).

(26)

We note that the symmetric four-potential
A_{\rm sym}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}B(x\delta^{\mu}_{y}-y\delta^{\mu}_{x})
can be obtained from the four-potential
A^{\mu}
in Eq. (
25
) via the gauge transformation
A^{\mu}_{\rm sym}=A^{\mu}+\partial^{\mu}\Lambda
, with
\Lambda=-\frac{1}{2}xyB
. For simplicity, we will continue the discussion in the context of the asymmetric choice (
25
), bearing in mind that the choice of gauge has no impact on the expectation values that form the main objectives of our paper.

It is easy to check that when
A^{\mu}
is given by Eq. (
25
), the Hamiltonian commutes with both
P^{y}=-i\partial_{y}
and
P^{z}=-i\partial_{z}
, such that
\phi_{j}
and
\phi_{j}^{c}
can be taken as eigenfunctions of these operators,

\phi_{j}=\frac{e^{ip^{y}_{j}y+ip^{z}_{j}z}}{2\pi}\begin{pmatrix}f^{+}_{j}\\
f^{-}_{j}\end{pmatrix},\quad\phi_{j}^{c}=\frac{e^{-ip^{y}_{j}y-ip^{z}_{j}z}}{2\pi}\begin{pmatrix}f^{c;+}_{j}\\
f^{c;-}_{j}\end{pmatrix},

(27)

where
f_{j}^{\pm}\equiv f_{j}^{\pm}(x)
are scalar functions depending only on the transverse spatial coordinate
x
. The charge conjugates
f_{j}^{c;\pm}
are related to
f_{j}^{\pm}
via

f^{c;\pm}_{j}=\pm f^{\mp;*}_{j}{\Big|}_{q\rightarrow-q}\,.

(28)

Focussing now on the particle modes only, substituting Eq. (
27
) into Eq. (
22
) leads to

\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}-p_{j}^{z}&i\sqrt{2|qB|}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\xi_{j})\\
i\sqrt{2|qB|}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}+\frac{\sigma}{2}\xi_{j})&2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}+p^{z}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f^{+}_{j}\\
f^{-}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\\
=0,

(29)

where we introduced

\xi_{j}=\sqrt{2|qB|}\left(x-\frac{\sigma p^{y}_{j}}{|qB|}\right),\quad\sigma={\rm sgn}(qB).

(30)

With the above notation, the functions
f^{\pm}_{j}(\xi_{j})
satisfy the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\xi_{j}^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\xi_{j}^{2}+\nu_{j}^{\pm}+\frac{1}{2}\right)f_{j}^{\pm}=0,

\displaystyle\nu_{j}^{\pm}=\frac{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}-(p_{j}^{z})^{2}}{2|qB|}-\frac{1\mp\sigma}{2}.

(31)

As is well known, the above equation has regular solutions given in terms of the Hermite polynomials when
\nu_{j}^{\pm}
is a non-vanishing integer:

f^{\pm}_{j}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\pm}e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/4}H_{\nu_{j}^{\pm}}(\xi_{j}),

(32)

where
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{j}
are integration constants. From the above discussion, it is easy to write the quantization relation for the energy:

E_{j}^{2}=M^{2}+(p_{j}^{z})^{2}+2n_{j}|qB|,\quad\nu^{\pm}_{j}=n_{j}-\frac{1\mp\sigma}{2}.

(33)

Using now the recurrence relations
H^{\prime}_{\nu}(z)=\nu H_{\nu-1}(z)
and
zH_{\nu}(z)=H_{\nu+1}(z)+\nu H_{\nu-1}(z)
, it is possible to derive the relation

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{j}}-\frac{1\mp\sigma}{2}\xi_{j}\right)H_{\nu}(\xi_{j})\\
=\nu\frac{1\pm\sigma}{2}H_{\nu-1}(\xi_{j})-\frac{1\mp\sigma}{2}H_{\nu+1}(\xi_{j}),

(34)

which implies that
the coefficients
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{j}
satisfy the following matrix equation:

\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}-\sigma p_{j}^{z}&-i\sqrt{2|qB|}\\
in_{j}\sqrt{2|qB|}&\hskip-10.0pt2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}+\sigma p_{j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\sigma}\\
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{-\sigma}\end{pmatrix}\\
=0.

(35)

The above expression gives one relation between the two normalization constants
\mathcal{C}^{\pm}_{j}
. The second relation allowing these constants to be determined is provided by the requirement of unit norm of the modes
U_{j}
under the Dirac inner product,

\displaystyle\langle U_{j},U_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}x\overline{U}_{j}(x)\gamma^{0}U_{j^{\prime}}(x)=\delta(j,j^{\prime})

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{n_{j}n_{j^{\prime}}}\delta_{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}}\delta(p_{j}^{y}-p_{j^{\prime}}^{y})\delta(p_{j}^{z}-p_{j^{\prime}}^{z}).

(36)

The integration with respect to
y
and
z
leads to the
\delta(p^{y}_{j}-p^{y}_{j^{\prime}})
and
\delta(p^{z}_{j}-p^{z}_{j^{\prime}})
factors, while the integration with respect to
x
can be performed using the orthogonality relation for the Hermite polynomials,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi_{j}e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}H_{n_{j}}(\xi_{j})H_{n_{j}^{\prime}}(\xi_{j})=\sqrt{2\pi}n_{j}!\delta_{n_{j},n_{j}^{\prime}}.

(37)

In the following subsections, we will discuss independently the cases
n_{j}=0
(the so-called lowest Landau level, LLL) and
n_{j}>0
(the higher Landau levels, HLL).

III.2
Lowest Landau level (LLL)

On the lowest Landau level (LLL) corresponding to
n_{j}=0
, we avoid the divergent solution with
\nu_{j}^{-\sigma}=-1
by setting

\mathcal{C}_{j}^{-\sigma}{\Big|}_{n_{j}=0}=0.

(38)

The only non-trivial solution corresponds to the polarization satisfying

2\sigma\lambda_{j}={\rm sgn}(p_{j}^{z}),

(39)

where we remind the reader that
\sigma={\rm sgn}(qB)
, cf. Eq. (
30
).

Normalizing now the modes using the Dirac inner product in Eq. (
36
), we find the normalization
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\sigma}=(|qB|/\pi)^{1/4}
corresponding to the LLL mode:

\displaystyle U_{j}(x){\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z})\frac{e^{-iE_{j}t}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi^{\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}}){\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0},

\displaystyle\phi^{\sigma}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ip_{j}^{y}y+ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{\xi_{j}^{2}}{4}}\left(\frac{|qB|}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1+\sigma}{2}\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1-\sigma}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(40)

Similarly, the antiparticle modes can be found as

\displaystyle V_{j}(x){\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\theta(-\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z})\frac{e^{iE_{j}t}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\\
-{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi^{c;\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}}){\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0},

\displaystyle\phi^{c;\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}}){\Bigl|}_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ip_{j}^{y}y-ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{\xi_{j;c}^{2}}{4}}\left(\frac{|qB|}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\frac{1+\sigma}{2}\\[2.84526pt]
-\frac{1-\sigma}{2}\end{pmatrix},

(41)

where
\xi_{j;c}
is the charge conjugate of
\xi_{j}
,

\xi_{j;c}=\xi_{j}{\Bigl|}_{q\rightarrow-q}=\sqrt{2|qB|}\left(x+\frac{\sigma p^{y}_{j}}{|qB|}\right).

(42)

These expressions allow us to establish a link between
\phi^{c;\sigma}_{j}
and its charge conjugate as follows:

\phi^{c;\sigma}_{0,p^{y},p^{z},\lambda}({\mathbf{x}})=\sigma\phi^{\sigma}_{0,-p^{y},-p^{z},\lambda}({\mathbf{x}}).

(43)

III.3
Higher energy levels

For
n_{j}>0
, we have

\mathcal{C}^{-\sigma}_{j}=-\frac{in_{j}\sqrt{2|qB|}}{2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}+\sigma p^{z}_{j}}\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}_{j},

(44)

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\sigma}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{j}}{\sqrt{n_{j}!}}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}\mathfrak{p}^{2\sigma\lambda_{j}}_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{-\sigma}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i\mathcal{N}_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}}}{2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}!}}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}\mathfrak{p}^{-2\sigma\lambda_{j}}_{j},

(45)

where
\mathcal{N}_{j}
is a phase factor,
|\mathcal{N}_{j}|=1
, while

\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}_{j}=\sqrt{1\pm\frac{p_{j}^{z}}{\sqrt{E_{j}^{2}-M^{2}}}}.

(46)

Specifically, we have

U_{j}(x)=\frac{e^{-iE_{j}t}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi^{\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}}),

(47a)

where the Pauli spinors are given by

\displaystyle\phi^{+}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ip_{j}^{y}y+ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi\sqrt{n_{j}!}}e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/4}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\times\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}^{2\lambda_{j}}_{j}H_{n_{j}}(\xi_{j})\\
-2i\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{-2\lambda_{j}}_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}}H_{n_{j}-1}(\xi_{j})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\phi^{-}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ip_{j}^{y}y+ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi\sqrt{n_{j}!}}e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/4}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\times\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{p}^{2\lambda_{j}}_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}}H_{n_{j}-1}(\xi_{j})\\
2i\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{-2\lambda_{j}}_{j}H_{n_{j}}(\xi_{j})\end{pmatrix}.

(47b)

In the above, we used
\mathcal{N}_{j}=1
for
\sigma=1
and
\mathcal{N}_{j}=2i\lambda_{j}
for
\sigma=-1
.
The antiparticle modes

V_{j}=i\gamma^{2}U_{j}^{*}{\bigl|}_{q\rightarrow-q}

can be obtained through the charge conjugation operation, being given explicitly by

\displaystyle V_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{iE_{j}t}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}^{-}_{j}\\
-{\cal K}^{+}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\phi^{c;\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}}),

\displaystyle\phi^{c;+}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ip_{j}^{y}y-ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi\sqrt{n_{j}!}}e^{-\xi_{j;c}^{2}/4}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\times\begin{pmatrix}-2i\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{-2\lambda_{j}}_{j}H_{n_{j}}(\xi_{j;c})\\
-\mathfrak{p}^{2\lambda_{j}}_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}}H_{n_{j}-1}(\xi_{j;c})\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\phi^{c;-}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ip_{j}^{y}y-ip_{j}^{z}z}}{2\pi\sqrt{n_{j}!}}e^{-\xi^{2}_{j;c}/4}\left(\frac{|qB|}{4\pi}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\times\begin{pmatrix}2i\lambda_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{-2\lambda_{j}}_{j}\sqrt{n_{j}}H_{n_{j}-1}(\xi_{j;c})\\
-\mathfrak{p}^{2\lambda_{j}}_{j}H_{n_{j}}(\xi_{j;c})\end{pmatrix}.

(48a)

It can be seen that the charge-conjugated Pauli spinors
\phi^{c;\sigma}_{j}({\mathbf{x}})
corresponding to higher levels satisfy the relation

\phi^{c;\sigma}_{n,p^{y},p^{z},\lambda}({\mathbf{x}})=-2i\lambda\sigma\phi^{\sigma}_{n,-p^{y},-p^{z},\lambda}({\mathbf{x}}),

(48b)

which shares certain similarity with Eq. (
43
).

IV
Finite-temperature field theory at finite helical density

IV.1
Second quantization

The full solution of the Dirac equation reads

\psi(x)=\sum_{j}[U_{j}(x)a_{j}+V_{j}(x)b^{\dagger}_{j}],

(49)

where the symbol
j
, which labels the eigenmodes, collectively denotes the eigenvalues
p^{y}_{j}
,
p^{z}_{j}
,
n_{j}
and
\lambda_{j}
. The sum over the eigenmodes is shorthanded as follows:

\sum_{j}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}=\pm\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{z}_{j}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty},

(50)

where the
n_{j}=0
term corresponds to the contribution coming from the lowest Landau level (LLL), which exists only when
2\sigma\lambda_{j}p^{z}_{j}>0
.

Demanding that the equality (
49
) is exact and noting that
a_{j}=\braket{U_{j},\psi}
and
b_{j}^{\dagger}=\braket{V_{j},\psi}
, we arrive to the completeness relation for the set of modes
U_{j}
and
V_{j}
:

\sum_{j}[U_{j}(t,{\mathbf{x}})U^{\dagger}_{j}(t,{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})+V_{j}(t,{\mathbf{x}})V_{j}^{\dagger}(t,{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})]=\delta^{3}({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}).

(51)

Promoting now
\psi(x)
to a Fock-space operator
\hat{\psi}
, the canonical anti-commutation relation

\{\hat{\psi}(t,{\mathbf{x}}),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(t,{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\}=\delta^{3}({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}),

(52)

can be achieved by imposing

\{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}\}=\{\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j},\hat{b}_{j^{\prime}}\}=\delta(j,j^{\prime}),

(53)

where
\delta(j,j^{\prime})=\delta(p^{y}_{j}-p^{y}_{j^{\prime}})\delta(p^{z}_{j}-p^{z}_{j^{\prime}})\delta_{n_{j},n_{j^{\prime}}}\delta_{\lambda_{j},\lambda_{j^{\prime}}}
, as in Eq. (
36
).

IV.2
Conserved charges

In this paper, we describe fermionic ensembles at finite temperature in the background of a constant homogeneous magnetic field. We will investigate states which exhibit imbalance with respect to the electric and helical charges, as well as with respect to the axial charge (in the case of massless fermions). These conserved charges can be obtained by considering the vector, axial and helical charge currents, defined, respectively, as follows:

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{V}

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\,,

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{A}

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\psi\,,

(54)

\displaystyle J^{\mu}_{H}

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}h\psi+\overline{h\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\,.

We now compute the divergences of the above currents. In the case of the vector current, we have

\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{V}=\overline{\not{\partial}\psi}\psi+\overline{\psi}\not{\partial}\psi.

(55)

Using the Dirac equation (
7
), the derivatives
\not{\partial}\psi
and
\overline{\not{\partial}\psi}
can be replaced via

\displaystyle\not{\partial}\psi

\displaystyle=-i(q\not{A}+M)\psi,

\displaystyle\overline{\not{\partial}\psi}

\displaystyle=i\overline{\psi}(q\not{A}+M).

(56)

Substituting the above into Eq. (
55
) gives the conservation of the vector current,
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{V}=0
.

Employing the same steps as above for the derivative of the axial four-current
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{A}
reveals the partial conservation of the axial current (PCAC),

\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{A}=2M(i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{5}\psi),

(57)

where the expression between the parentheses on the right-hand side is the pseudoscalar condensate.

Finally, in the case of the helicity current, we have

\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{H}=\overline{\not{\partial}\psi}h\psi+\overline{\psi}\not{\partial}h\psi+\text{h.c.},

(58)

where h.c. denotes the Hermitian conjugate. Taking advantage of the commutation relation (
15
), it is easy to show that if
\psi
is a solution of the Dirac equation, then
h\psi
also satisfies the Dirac equation. This leads to the relation

\overline{\psi}\not{\partial}h\psi=-i\overline{\psi}(q\not{A}+M)h\psi=-\overline{\not{\partial}\psi}h\psi,

(59)

implying that the helical current is identically conserved even for massive fermions:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{H}=0.

(60)

Note however that the conservation of the helicity current is broken perturbatively by helicity-violating pair annihilation processes, as discussed in Ref.
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
. In a setting of real physical systems, such as the quark-gluon plasma, the non-conservation of helicity due to perturbative scattering has the same timescale as the non-conservation of chirality due to dynamical mass generation
Astrakhantsev et al. 2020
, which allows us to treat the helicity and chirality on equal basis
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
.

The above discussions prompt us to introduce the total vector, axial and helical charges,

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}=\int d^{3}x\,J^{0}_{\ell}\,,\qquad\ell=V,A,H\,,

(61)

which are identically conserved for the vector and helical degrees of freedom:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q_{V}=\partial_{t}Q_{H}=0\qquad\text{[for fermions with any mass]},

(62)

for both chiral and massive fermions. However, due to the PCAC property (
57
), the axial charge does not represent a conserved quantity for a massive particle:

\partial_{t}Q_{A}=2M\eta,\quad\eta\equiv\int d^{3}x\,i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{5}\psi.

(63)

Thus, while at finite mass
M\neq 0
, the axial charge
Q_{A}
is not a good conserved number, the vector and helical charges are identically conserved.

Thanks to the helicity eigenmode solutions discussed in Sec.
III
, both the vector and the helicity charge operators
\widehat{Q}_{V/H}
are diagonal and, together with the Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}
, they admit the following Fock space representation:

\displaystyle:\widehat{H}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}E_{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}),

\displaystyle:\widehat{Q}_{V}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}),

\displaystyle:\widehat{Q}_{H}:

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}2\lambda_{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}),

(64)

where the colons

:\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}:\equiv\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}-\braket{0|\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}|0}

denote Wick (normal) ordering of an operator
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
.
It is worth pointing out that, while
\widehat{H}
is even under charge conjugation (both particles and antiparticles contribute with the same sign to
\widehat{H}
), both the vector and the helical charge operators are odd and therefore discriminate between particles and antiparticles. The CPT properties of
\widehat{Q}_{H}
are discussed in Ref.
Ambrus 2020
.

As a side remark, the Hamiltonian
H
can be obtained from the Dirac energy-momentum tensor,
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
, defined via Noether’s theorem as

\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\psi)}\partial^{\nu}\psi+\partial^{\nu}\overline{\psi}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi})}-g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L},

(65)

where the Dirac Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
is given in Eq. (
6
). Taking into account that
\mathcal{L}
vanishes when
\psi
satisfies the Dirac equation, we arrive at the following relation for the energy-momentum tensor:

\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\nu}}\psi.

(66)

Due to the presence of interactions,
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
is, in general, not conserved:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}=qJ^{\mu}_{V}\partial^{\nu}A_{\mu}.

(67)

The right-hand side of the above expression is not gauge invariant. To restore gauge invariance, it is customary to alter
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
by adding a gauge-fixing total divergence term,
\Delta\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\rho}(F^{\mu\rho}A^{\nu})=-qJ_{V}^{\mu}A^{\nu}+F^{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}A^{\nu}
, where we used Maxwell’s equations,
\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=qJ_{V}^{\nu}
. Adding the first term,
-qJ^{\mu}A^{\nu}
, to
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
yields
\widetilde{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{\partial^{\nu}}\psi-qJ^{\mu}A^{\nu}
. Its divergence,
\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=-qJ_{V;\mu}F^{\mu\nu}
, represents the relativistic Lorentz force acting on the electric charge and is a gauge-invariant quantity. The second term,
F^{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}A^{\nu}
, serves to make the electromagnetic contribution to the energy-momentum tensor,
\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{\rm e.m.}=-F^{\mu\lambda}\partial^{\nu}A_{\lambda}
, as well as its divergence, gauge-invariant. However, the gauge-fixing term becomes position-dependent, since
A^{\nu}=Bx\delta^{\nu}_{y}
, as shown in Eq. (
25
). For this reason, we will not consider the gauge-fixing term and we will focus instead on the form of the energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
given in Eq. (
66
).

The static homogeneous magnetic field considered in this paper,
A_{\mu}=Bxg_{\mu y}
, does not produce work as the Lorentz force is always normal to the direction of the current. Therefore, for the energy component of the conservation relation (
67
),
\nu=0
, we have
\partial_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu 0}=0,
so that the energy is conserved, as expected. This property allows the Hamiltonian to be defined as the total conserved energy,

H=\int d^{3}x\,\Theta^{00}.

(68)

Upgrading
H
to the Fock space operator
\widehat{H}
and employing the explicit mode decomposition (
49
) gives the expression in Eq. (
64
).

Finally, in the case of chiral (massless) fermions, the axial charge is conserved,
\partial_{t}Q_{A}=0
, and the corresponding Fock-space operator
\widehat{Q}_{A}
admits the decomposition

:\widehat{Q}_{A}:=\sum_{j}2\lambda_{j}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j})\qquad\text{[for $M=0$]}.

(69)

Note that, contrary to the vector and helical charge operators
\widehat{Q}_{V}
and
\widehat{Q}_{H}
, the axial charge operator
\widehat{Q}_{A}
is even with respect to charge conjugation (see Table 1 in Ref.
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
).

IV.3
Thermal expectation values

We now construct thermal states at finite vector and helical chemical potentials, using the statistical operator
Canuto and Chiu 1968a
;
Canuto and Chiu 1968b
;
Canuto and Chiu 1968c

\hat{\rho}=e^{-\beta(:\widehat{H}:-:\bm{\mu}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}:)},

(70)

where
\widehat{H}
is the Hamiltonian, while the conserved charges sector contains the vector and helical charges for massive fermions. In the case of massless fermions, we allow also for a chiral charge, so that we introduce a generic shift of the Fermi energy levels controlled by the whole triad of the corresponding chemical potentials:

\bm{\mu}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}=\mu_{V}\widehat{Q}_{V}+\mu_{A}\widehat{Q}_{A}+\mu_{H}\widehat{Q}_{H}\,.

(71)

One should bear in mind that the axial chemical potential
\mu_{A}
vanishes for a massive particle, since the axial charge is not conserved if the particle mass is nonzero, cf. (
63
).

The statistical operator (
70
) describes a system at rest, having four-velocity

u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{t}.

(72)

With the above four-velocity vector, we can construct the covariant magnetic field four-vector,

B^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}u_{\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}=B\delta^{\mu}_{z},

(73)

where
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the Levi-Civitta tensor and we took the sign convention such that
\varepsilon^{0123}=1
.

The thermal expectation value of an operator
\widehat{A}
is defined as

\braket{\widehat{A}}\equiv\mathcal{Z}^{-1}{\rm tr}(\hat{\rho}\widehat{A}),\qquad\mathcal{Z}={\rm tr}(\hat{\rho}).

(74)

In the following, we focus only on operators which are quadratic with respect to the field operator
\hat{\psi}
, thus allowing for the following decomposition:

\widehat{A}=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}\left[\mathcal{A}(U_{j},U_{j^{\prime}})\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}+\mathcal{A}(V_{j},V_{j^{\prime}})\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}}\right.\\
\left.+\mathcal{A}(U_{j},V_{j^{\prime}})\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}}+\mathcal{A}(V_{j},U_{j^{\prime}})\hat{b}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}\right],

(75)

where
\mathcal{A}(\psi,\chi)
is a sesquilinear form in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\alpha\psi+\beta\phi,\chi)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{*}\mathcal{A}(\psi,\chi)+\beta^{*}\mathcal{A}(\phi,\chi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\psi,\alpha\chi+\beta\phi)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathcal{A}(\psi,\chi)+\beta\mathcal{A}(\psi,\phi).

(76)

Evaluating
\braket{\widehat{A}}
essentially boils down to evaluating the thermal expectation values of quadratic products of the one-particle operators, which we discuss below.

First, we note that the decompositions (
64
) imply the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[\widehat{H},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=E_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},

\displaystyle[\widehat{H},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=E_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{J},

\displaystyle[\widehat{Q}_{V},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},

\displaystyle[\widehat{Q}_{V},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=-\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j},

\displaystyle[\widehat{Q}_{H},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},

\displaystyle[\widehat{Q}_{H},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}]

\displaystyle=-2\lambda_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}.

(77)

A fourth line can be added for massless fermions, namely

M=0:\quad[\widehat{Q}_{A},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]=2\lambda_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},\quad[\widehat{Q}_{A},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}]=2\lambda_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}.

(78)

With the above relations, it can be established that

\hat{\rho}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{\rho}^{-1}=e^{-\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}^{+}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j},\quad\hat{\rho}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{\rho}^{-1}=e^{-\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}^{-}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j},

(79)

where we introduced the shifted energy levels

\mathcal{E}_{j}^{\varsigma_{j}}=E_{j}-\varsigma_{j}\mu_{V}-2\lambda_{j}\mu_{A}-2\varsigma_{j}\lambda_{j}\mu_{H},

(80)

where the quantum number
\varsigma_{j}=\pm 1
distinguishes particles and antiparticles:

\varsigma_{j}=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{rcl}$+1$,&&\text{for particles},\\[2.84526pt]
$-1$,&&\text{for antiparticles}.\end{tabular}\right.

(81)

Equation (
80
) takes into account the difference in the positions of the Fermi levels for particles with vector, axial, and helical charges which are anchored in the corresponding chemical potentials
\mu_{V}
,
\mu_{A}
, and
\mu_{H}
, respectively. Despite the seemingly complicated form of this equation, it has a simple meaning, consistent with Eq. (
71
): the vector chemical potential
\mu_{V}
controls the excess of particles over antiparticles which, in turn, are distinguished by the quantum number
\varsigma_{j}
. The axial chemical potential
\mu_{A}
applies equally to particles and antiparticles but distinguishes the polarization states described by
\lambda_{j}
. Finally, the helical chemical potential
\mu_{H}
is sensitive to the helicity of the particle which is opposite for particles and antiparticles, hence the product
\varsigma_{j}\lambda_{j}
in the last term of Eq. (
80
).

The thermal expectation value of the quadratic form
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}
thus evaluates to

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta\mathcal{E}^{+}_{j}}\langle\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\rangle=\frac{\delta(j,j^{\prime})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{+}_{j}}+1},

(82)

where on the last line we used
\braket{\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}}=\delta(j^{\prime},j)-\braket{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{b}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta(j,j^{\prime})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{-}_{j}}+1}.

(83)

Finally, it is not difficult to see that

\braket{\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}}}=\braket{\hat{b}_{j}\hat{a}_{j^{\prime}}}=0.

(84)

We are now in a position to evaluate the thermal expectation value of the operator
\widehat{A}
in Eq. (
75
). Focussing on thermal effects, we consider the expectation value of the normal-ordered operator
:\widehat{A}:=\widehat{A}-\braket{0|\widehat{A}|0}
, which evaluates to

A\equiv\braket{:\widehat{A}:}=\sum_{j}\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}(U_{j},U_{j})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{+}_{j}}+1}-\frac{\mathcal{A}(V_{j},V_{j})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{-}_{j}}+1}\right].

(85)

For simplicity, we will often consider separately the contributions due to the LLL (
n_{j}=0
) and to the higher-energy modes (
n_{j}>0
) by writing

A=A_{0}+\sum_{n_{j}=1}^{\infty}A_{n_{j}},

(86)

where
A_{0}
corresponds to the lowest Landau level (with
n_{j}=0
). The terms
A_{n_{j}}
are obtained after performing the
p^{y}_{j}
and
p^{z}_{j}
integrations, as well as the sum over polarization
\lambda_{j}
:

A_{n_{j}}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\int dp^{y}_{j}\int dp^{z}_{j}\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}(U_{j},U_{j})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{+}_{j}}+1}-\frac{\mathcal{A}(V_{j},V_{j})}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}^{-}_{j}}+1}\right].

(87)

IV.4
Scalar and pseudoscalar condensates

Let us apply the previously-discussed formalism to the computation of the scalar and pseudoscalar condensates,
\bar{\psi}\psi
and
i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{5}\psi
. In the latter case, it is not difficult to see that the mode solutions in Eqs. (
40
) and (
47
) give vanishing contributions to the pseudoscalar condensate,

i\overline{U}_{j}\gamma^{5}U_{j}=0,

(88)

and similarly
i\overline{V}_{j}\gamma^{5}V_{j}=0
.

In the case of the scalar condensate, the antiparticle contribution can be obtained via charge conjugation, as follows:

\overline{V}_{j}V_{j}=-(\overline{U}_{j}U_{j})^{*}_{q\rightarrow-q}.

(89)

On the LLL, we have

\overline{U}_{j}U_{j}{\Big|}_{n_{j}=0}=\frac{Me^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p^{z}_{j}),

(90)

while for
n_{j}>0
,

\overline{U}_{j}U_{j}{\Big|}_{n_{j}>0}=\frac{Me^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{8\pi^{2}E_{j}n_{j}!}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\\
\times(\mathfrak{p}_{j;2\sigma\lambda_{j}}^{2}H_{n_{j}}^{2}+n_{j}\mathfrak{p}_{j;-2\sigma\lambda_{j}}^{2}H^{2}_{n_{j}-1}),

(91)

where it is understood that the Hermite polynomials take the argument
\xi_{j}
. In order to evaluate the chiral condensate
\braket{\bar{\psi}\psi}
, the integration with respect to
p^{y}_{j}
can be performed using the orthogonality relation (
37
) for the Hermite polynomials,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}\,e^{-\xi^{2}/2}H_{n}(\xi)H_{m}(\xi)=\sqrt{\pi|qB|}\,n!\delta_{nm}.

(92)

Specifically, we have

\overline{U}_{j}U_{j}=\frac{M|qB|}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}}\begin{cases}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p^{z}_{j}),&n_{j}=0,\\
1,&n_{j}>0.\end{cases}

(93)

The final result reads

\bar{\psi}\psi=\frac{M|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)},

(94)

where the summation runs over the helicity quantum number
\lambda_{j}=\pm 1/2
, particle-antiparticle number
\varsigma_{j}=\pm 1
and
\mathcal{E}_{j}\equiv\mathcal{E}^{\varsigma_{j}}_{j}
was introduced in Eq. (
80
). The degeneracy factor evaluates to
g_{j}=1
for
n_{j}=0
and
g_{j}=2
for
n_{j}>0
.

IV.5
Charge currents

The sesquilinear forms corresponding to the charge current operators read

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{V}(\psi,\chi)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{A}(\psi,\chi)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}\chi,

(95)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{H}(\psi,\chi)

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}h\chi+\overline{h\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\chi\,.

Replacing now
\psi=\chi=U_{j}
or
\psi=\chi=V_{j}
in terms of the helicity eigenmodes discussed in Sec.
III
, it can be seen that the sesquilinear forms for the helical charge current can be obtained from the ones for the vector charge current via

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{H}(U_{j},U_{j})

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{V}(U_{j},U_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{H}(V_{j},V_{j})

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{V}(V_{j},V_{j}).

(96)

The expectation values
J^{\mu}_{\ell}(x)\equiv\braket{:\widehat{J}^{\mu}_{\ell}(x):}
with
\ell\in\{V,A,H\}
can be evaluated using Eq. (
85
), where the sesquilinear forms
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{\ell}(V_{j},V_{j})
corresponding to the antiparticle modes can be obtained from those corresponding to the particle modes by using the following properties:

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}V_{j}

\displaystyle=(\overline{U}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}U_{j})^{*}_{q\rightarrow-q},

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}V_{j}

\displaystyle=-(\overline{U}_{j}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}U_{j})^{*}_{q\rightarrow-q}.

(97)

In the following, we will focus on the explicit computation of the sesquilinear forms corresponding to the particle modes, which we will denote in shorthand notation as

\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{\ell;j}\equiv\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{\ell}(U_{j},U_{j}).

(98)

On the LLL, an explicit computation shows that

\left.\phi^{\sigma;\dagger}_{j}\sigma^{x}\phi^{\sigma}_{j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}=\left.\phi^{\sigma;\dagger}_{j}\sigma^{y}\phi^{\sigma}_{j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}=0,

(99)

such that

\left.\mathcal{J}^{x}_{\ell;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}=\left.\mathcal{J}^{y}_{\ell;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}=0.

(100)

The temporal and vertical components can be computed as follows:

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{J}^{t}_{V;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}),

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{J}^{z}_{V;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda_{j}\sigma|p_{j}^{z}|e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}),

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{J}^{t}_{A;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\lambda_{j}|p_{j}^{z}|e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}),

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{J}^{z}_{A;j}\right|_{n_{j}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}\theta(\sigma\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}).

(101)

Using Eq. (
92
), the
p^{y}_{j}
integral can be evaluated, leading to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}J^{t}_{V;0}\\
J^{t}_{A;0}\\
J^{t}_{H;0}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}\begin{pmatrix}\varsigma_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}/E_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}\varsigma_{j}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}J^{z}_{V;0}\\
J^{z}_{A;0}\\
J^{z}_{H;0}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{qB}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}/E_{j}\\
\varsigma_{j}\\
p^{z}_{j}/E_{j}\end{pmatrix},

(102)

where
E_{j}=\sqrt{M^{2}+p_{z;j}^{2}}
on the LLL.

At higher Landau levels with
n_{j}>0
, we compute
\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{\ell;j}

as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{V;j}^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{8\pi^{2}n_{j}!}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{j;2}_{2\sigma\lambda_{j}}H_{n_{j}}^{2}+\mathfrak{p}^{j;2}_{-2\sigma\lambda_{j}}n_{j}H_{n_{j}-1}^{2}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{V;j}^{y}

\displaystyle=-\frac{qBe^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}(n_{j}-1)!}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}H_{n_{j}}H_{n_{j}-1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{A;j}^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{8\pi^{2}n_{j}!}\sqrt{\frac{|qB|}{\pi}}(\mathfrak{p}^{j;2}_{2\sigma\lambda_{j}}H_{n_{j}}^{2}-\mathfrak{p}^{j;2}_{-2\sigma\lambda_{j}}n_{j}H_{n_{j}-1}^{2}),

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{J}^{t}_{A;j}\\
\mathcal{J}^{y}_{A;j}\\
\mathcal{J}^{z}_{V;j}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{j}\sqrt{1-\frac{M^{2}}{E_{j}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{J}^{t}_{V;j}\\
\mathcal{J}^{y}_{V;j}\\
\mathcal{J}^{z}_{A;j}\end{pmatrix},

(103)

while
\mathcal{J}_{V;j}^{x}=\mathcal{J}_{A;j}^{x}=0
. As before, the Hermite polynomials take the argument
\xi_{j}
.
Performing the
p^{y}_{j}
integral using Eq. (

) leads to

\displaystyle\int dp^{y}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{J}_{V;j}^{t}\\
\mathcal{J}_{A;j}^{t}\\
\mathcal{J}_{H;j}^{t}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}_{j}^{+}{\cal K}_{j}^{-}\\
2\lambda_{j}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\int dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{J}_{\ell;j}^{x}

\displaystyle=\int dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{J}_{\ell;j}^{y}=0,

\displaystyle\int dp^{y}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{J}_{V;j}^{z}\\
\mathcal{J}_{A;j}^{z}\\
\mathcal{J}_{H;j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}

(104)

where the quantities

). It can be seen that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}J^{t}_{V;n_{j}>0}\\

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}\begin{pmatrix}\varsigma_{j}\\

\displaystyle J^{z}_{\ell;n_{j}>0}

IV.6
Energy-momentum tensor

In the case of the energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
, the sesquilinear forms
\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(V_{j},V_{j})
corresponding to the antiparticle modes can be related to those pertinent to the particle modes via

\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(V_{j},V_{j})=-[\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(U_{j},U_{j})]^{*}_{q\rightarrow-q}.

(106)

The sesquilinear forms
\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}_{j}\equiv\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}(U_{j},U_{j})
corresponding to the non-vanishing entries of the canonical energy-momentum tensor (
66
) can be written in terms of those encountered for the vector current,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{tt}_{j}=E_{j}\mathcal{J}^{t}_{V;j},\quad\mathcal{T}^{yy}_{j}=p^{y}_{j}\mathcal{J}^{y}_{V;j},\quad\mathcal{T}^{zz}_{j}=p^{z}_{j}\mathcal{J}^{z}_{V;j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{tz}_{j}=p^{z}_{j}\mathcal{J}^{t}_{V;j},\qquad\mathcal{T}^{zt}_{j}=E_{j}\mathcal{J}^{z}_{V;j}.

(107)

The other components vanish, except for

\mathcal{T}^{xx}_{j}
, which receives non-vanishing contributions only for
n_{j}>0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{xx}_{j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{2|qB|}\,\overline{U}_{j}\gamma^{x}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_{\!\!\xi_{j}}U_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|^{3/2}e^{-\xi_{j}^{2}/2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}(n_{j}-1)!\sqrt{\pi}}W_{\xi_{j}}(H_{n_{j}-1},H_{n_{j}}),

(108)

where
W_{z}(f,g)\equiv f(z)g^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(z)g(z)
is the Wronskian. Due to symmetry constraints, we expect that
\Theta^{xx}=\Theta^{yy}
.

On the LLL, when
n_{j}=0
, we have
\mathcal{T}^{xx}_{j}\rfloor_{n_{j}=0}=\mathcal{T}^{yy}_{j}\rfloor_{n_{j}=0}=0
, while the non-vanishing components of
\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{0}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\Theta^{tt}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}E_{j}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1},

\displaystyle\Theta^{tz}_{0}=\Theta^{zt}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{qB}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}2\varsigma_{j}\lambda_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}p_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1},

\displaystyle\Theta^{zz}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}p_{z;j}^{2}}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)}.

(109)

In order to compute the contributions due to the higher energy levels (
n_{j}>0
), we replace
p^{y}_{j}=xqB-\sigma\xi_{j}\sqrt{|qB|/2}
and use the relation
H_{n_{j}}^{\prime}(\xi_{j})=n_{j}H_{n_{j}-1}(\xi_{j})
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{tt}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{j}|qB|}{4\pi^{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{tz}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{zt}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}=\frac{p^{z}_{j}|qB|}{4\pi^{2}},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{xx}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{yy}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}=\frac{n_{j}(qB)^{2}}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp^{y}_{j}\mathcal{T}^{zz}_{j}{\Bigl|}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p^{z}_{j})^{2}|qB|}{4\pi^{2}E_{j}}.

(110)

It can be seen that
\mathcal{T}^{tz}_{j}|_{n_{j}>0}=\mathcal{T}^{zt}_{j}|_{n_{j}>0}
are odd with respect to
p_{j}^{z}\rightarrow-p_{j}^{z}
, thus
\Theta^{tz}_{n_{j}}=\Theta^{zt}_{n_{j}}=0
for
n_{j}>0
.
The nonvanishing components of the contribution of the higher Landau levels to the expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{n_{j}>0}
are given by

\displaystyle\Theta^{tt}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}E_{j}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1},

\displaystyle\Theta^{xx}_{n_{j}>0}=\Theta^{yy}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{j}|qB|^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)},

\displaystyle\Theta^{zz}_{n_{j}>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,p_{z;j}^{2}}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)}.

(111)

V
Anomalous transport

For a classical (non-quantum) fluid in thermodynamic equilibrium, the charge currents
J^{\mu}_{\ell}
and energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
take the ideal fluid form,

J^{\mu}_{\ell;{\rm ideal}}=Q_{\ell}u^{\mu},\quad\Theta^{\mu\nu}_{\rm ideal}=\epsilon u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-P\Delta^{\mu\nu},

(112)

where
u^{\mu}
is the local fluid four-velocity, given by Eq. (
72
) for the present case, while
\Delta^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-u^{\mu}u^{\nu}
is the projector on the hypersurface orthogonal to
u^{\mu}
. The thermodynamic pressure
P\equiv P(T,\mu_{\ell})=-\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{\mu\nu}\Theta_{\rm ideal}^{\mu\nu}
is related to the charge densities
Q_{\ell}=u_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{\ell}
and the entropy density
s
via

Q_{\ell}=\frac{\partial P}{\partial\mu_{\ell}},\qquad\ s=\frac{\partial P}{\partial T},

(113)

while the energy density
\epsilon=u_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}
is given via the Euler relation by

\epsilon=sT-P+\bm{\mu}\cdot\mathbf{Q}.

(114)

The purpose of this section is to establish the expressions for the quantities appearing above in the presence of a constant magnetic field, as well as to highlight deviations from the perfect fluid form, which we will interpret as a signature of an anomalous transport phenomenon associated with helical, rather than axial degrees of freedom. It is worth mentioning that the term “anomalous transport” denotes dissipationless – often off-equilibrium – transport effects that appear due to anomalous breaking of a continuous symmetry. The CSE (
4
) results from the anomalous breaking of the axial symmetry
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
. The presence of an anomaly for helical degrees of freedom has been speculated in Refs.
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
and below we will show that the magnetic field produces the Helical Separation Effect which appears to be very similar to its famous chiral magnetic counterpart, the CME.

V.1
Hydrodynamic decomposition

In general, the charge currents
J^{\mu}_{\ell}
(
\ell\in\{V,A,H\}
) can be decomposed with respect to the fluid four-velocity
u^{\mu}
as follows:

J_{\ell}^{\mu}=Q_{\ell}u^{\mu}+j_{\ell}^{\mu},\qquad Q_{\ell}=J^{t}_{\ell},

(115)

where
j^{\mu}_{\ell}=\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}J^{\alpha}_{\ell}
represents the charge flow in the fluid rest frame. Since the only vector orthogonal to
u^{\mu}
available in this problem is
B^{\mu}
, we have the following charge flows:

j^{\mu}_{\ell}=\sigma^{B}_{\ell}B^{\mu},

(116)

with

\sigma^{B}_{\ell}=\frac{1}{B}J^{z}_{\ell},

(117)

being the magnetic conductivity of the
\ell
’th charge.

We now consider the fluid described by a symmetric energy-momentum tensor
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
.
Once the velocity
u^{\mu}
is fixed (we take
u^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{t}
),
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
can be decomposed as

\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-(P+\Pi)\Delta^{\mu\nu}+\pi^{\mu\nu}+j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}u^{\nu}+u^{\mu}j_{\epsilon}^{\nu}.

(118)

Besides the energy density
\epsilon
and thermodynamic pressure
P
, the above decomposition introduces as deviations from the ideal fluid form the dynamic (bulk) pressure
\Pi=-P-\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{\mu\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}
, the shear-stress tensor
\pi^{\mu\nu}=\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}\Theta^{\alpha\beta}
and the heat flux in the fluid rest frame
j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}\equiv\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\Theta^{\alpha\beta}
. In the above, we employed the projector
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}
defined as

\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\Delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\Delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\Delta^{\nu}_{\alpha})-\frac{1}{3}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\alpha\beta}.

(119)

Specializing the above decomposition to the present case with
u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{t}
, the scalar quantities
\epsilon
,
P
and
\Pi
can be obtained as

\epsilon=T^{tt},\qquad P+\Pi=\frac{1}{3}(\Theta^{xx}+\Theta^{yy}+\Theta^{zz}).

(120)

In classical fluids, the dynamic pressure
\Pi
is usually related to the fluid expansion rate
\theta\equiv\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}
, which vanishes in the present case. However, as we will see later, the Dirac fluid in a magnetic field exhibits non-vanishing dynamic pressure.

As in the case of the charge flow
j^{\mu}_{\ell}
, the requirement of orthogonality to the fluid velocity
u^{\mu}
restricts the heat flux
j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}
to the form

j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}=\sigma_{\epsilon}^{B}B^{\mu}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\sigma_{\epsilon}^{B}=\frac{1}{B}\Theta^{tz},

(121)

which we dub the Helical Magnetic Heat Effect [cf. Eq. (
5
)].
The shear-stress tensor
\pi^{\mu\nu}
can be written in terms of the available tensors of the problem,

\pi^{\mu\nu}=au^{\mu}u^{\nu}+bB^{\mu}B^{\nu}+c(u^{\mu}B^{\nu}+u^{\nu}B^{\mu})+d\Delta^{\mu\nu},

(122)

where
a
,
b
,
c
, and
d
are certain coefficients.
Imposing
\pi^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}=au^{\mu}+cB^{\mu}=0
reveals that
a=c=0
. Furthermore, the tracelessness condition
\pi^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=a-bB^{2}+3d=0
gives
b=3d/B^{2}
, such that

\displaystyle\pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=d\left(\Delta^{\mu\nu}+3\frac{B^{\mu}B^{\nu}}{B^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi_{B}\times{\rm diag}\left(0,-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2},1\right),

(123)

where we defined
\pi_{B}=2d
for notational convenience.

Before ending this section, we remind the reader that in non-equilibrium relativistic hydrodynamics, the mass-energy equivalence gives rise to an ambiguity in defining the energy density, in the sense that the heat flux can be in part or totally absorbed into a new timelike vector that plays the role of the fluid velocity. In the above discussion, we have implicitly employed the so-called beta (or thermometer) velocity frame
Ván and Biró 2012
;
Ván and Biró 2013
;
Landsteiner et al. 2013
;
Becattini et al. 2015
, which gives the velocity in terms of the temperature four-vector defining the density operator
\hat{\rho}
. Another frequently used hydrodynamic frame is the so-called Landau (or energy) frame, defined by the eigenvalue equation

\Theta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}u^{\nu}_{\rm L}=\epsilon_{\rm L}u^{\mu}_{\rm L}.

(124)

In the present case, it is not difficult to establish that

u^{\mu}_{\rm L}\partial_{\mu}=\Gamma_{\rm L}(\partial_{t}+\beta_{\rm L}\partial_{z}),\qquad\beta_{\rm L}=\frac{\Theta^{tz}}{\epsilon_{\rm L}+\Theta^{zz}},

(125)

where
\Gamma_{\rm L}=(1-\beta_{\rm L})^{-1/2}
is the Lorentz factor of the Landau velocity and the Landau energy density is

\epsilon_{\rm L}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Theta^{tt}-\Theta^{zz}+\sqrt{(\Theta^{tt}+\Theta^{zz})^{2}-4\Theta_{tz}^{2}}\right].

(126)

It is easy to see that if
\Theta^{tz}\neq 0
then, in general,
\epsilon_{\rm L}\neq\epsilon
.

V.2
Thermometer frame analysis

Let us now write down the full expressions for the charge densities
Q_{\ell}=J^{t}_{\ell}
, energy density
\epsilon=\Theta^{tt}
and isotropic pressure computed in the thermometer frame:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}Q_{V}\\
Q_{A}\\
Q_{H}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}\begin{pmatrix}\varsigma_{j}\\
2\lambda_{j}{\cal K}_{j}^{+}{\cal K}_{j}^{-}\\
2\lambda_{j}\varsigma_{j}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}E_{j}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1},

\displaystyle P+\Pi

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{12\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}(E_{j}^{2}-M^{2})}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)},

(127)

where
g_{j}=1
for
n_{j}=0
and
g_{j}=2
for
n_{j}>0
,
{\cal K}_{j}^{\pm}=\sqrt{1\pm M^{2}/E_{j}^{2}}
[cf. Eq. (
21
)], and
E_{j}\sqrt{p_{z;j}^{2}+M^{2}+2n_{j}|qB|}
as in Eq. (
33
), while
\mathcal{E}_{j}=E_{j}-\varsigma_{j}\mu_{V}-2\lambda_{j}\mu_{A}-2\lambda_{j}\varsigma_{j}\mu_{H}
according to Eq. (
80
).
Similarly,
\pi_{B}=\Theta^{zz}-(P+\Pi)
can be obtained as

\pi_{B}=\frac{|qB|}{6\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}(p_{z;j}^{2}-n_{j}|qB|)}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)}.

(128)

The above expressions do not explicitly distinguish between the LLL and the higher energy-level contributions. On the contrary, the charge conductivities
\sigma^{B}_{\ell}
and the heat conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}
are fully determined by the contribution from the LLL, receiving vanishing contributions from the
n_{j}>0
energy levels:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{B}_{V}\\
\sigma^{B}_{A}\\
\sigma^{B}_{H}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{j}p_{j}^{z}\\
\varsigma_{j}E_{j}\\
p^{z}_{j}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}2\varsigma_{j}\lambda_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}p_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1},

(129)

where it is understood that
n_{j}=0
in the above relations.

It is worth stressing that the property that only the single lowest Landau level determines the value of a current, while the degenerate higher level contributions mutually cancel each other, is the characteristic feature of the anomalous currents. At the level of the charged currents, it is an expected feature for the chiral magnetic conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{V}
, responsible for the Chiral Magnetic Effect
Fukushima et al. 2008
, and the axial magnetic conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{A}
, which gives the Chiral Separation Effect
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
. These conductivities are determined by the axial anomaly (
133
). What is less expected is that a similar statement is also true for the helical conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{H}
, which, apparently, is not connected to the axial anomaly but may be related to another form of an anomaly associated with the helical degrees of freedom
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
.

In the limit of massless fermions,
E_{j}=|p_{j}^{z}|
on the LLL and the
p_{j}^{z}
integral can be performed analytically, when

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\left(1+e^{\beta\mathbf{q}_{j}\cdot\bm{\mu}}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,p_{j}^{z}}{e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}{\rm Li}_{2}(-e^{\beta\bm{q}_{j}\cdot\bm{\mu}}),

(130)

with
\mathbf{q}_{j}\cdot\bm{\mu}=\varsigma_{j}\mu_{V}+2\lambda_{j}\varsigma_{j}\mu_{H}+2\lambda_{j}\mu_{A}
and
{\rm Li}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}z^{k}/k^{n}
being the polylogarithm function
Olver et al. 2010
. Thus, in the high-temperature limit we obtain the following conductivities for massless fermions:

\displaystyle\sigma^{B}_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\mu_{A}}{2\pi^{2}}+\frac{q\beta\mu_{V}\mu_{H}}{4\pi^{2}}[1+O(\beta^{2})],

(131a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{B}_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\mu_{V}}{2\pi^{2}}+\frac{q\beta\mu_{A}\mu_{H}}{4\pi^{2}}[1+O(\beta^{2})],

(131b)

\displaystyle\sigma^{B}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{\pi^{2}\beta}\ln 2+\frac{q\beta\bm{\mu}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}+O(\beta^{3}),

(131c)

where the higher-order terms appearing in
\sigma^{B}_{V}
and
\sigma^{B}_{A}
vanish when
\mu_{V}\mu_{H}=0
and
\mu_{A}\mu_{H}=0
, respectively. It is remarkable that the helicity current appears even in a neutral plasma.

Let us now discuss the conductivities (
131
) in more detail. The meaning of these formulas is straightforward: in the background of a magnetic field
{\bm{B}}
, the fermionic system produces electric (
\ell=V
), axial (
\ell=A
), and helical (
\ell=H
) currents [cf. Eq. (
116
)],

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}_{\ell}=\sigma^{B}_{\ell}{\bm{B}}\,,\qquad\ \ell=V,A,H\,,

(132)

with the appropriate conductivities (
131
).

The first term in Eq. (
131a
) gives us the well-known expression for the chiral magnetic conductivity for the chiral separation effect (
4
) in the absence of the helical chemical potential,
\mu_{H}=0
. In particular, the coefficient
1/(2\pi^{2})
in this equation is determined by the axial anomaly which dictates, in turn, the non-conservation of the axial charge in the electromagnetic background:

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}j_{A}^{\mu}=\frac{q^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\,,

(133)

where
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the Levi-Civita tensor with
\epsilon^{0123}=+1
. The same statement is true for the first term in the axial magnetic conductivity (
131b
) which determines the chiral separation effect (
4
). The next-to-leading terms in both these expressions, Eqs. (
131a
) and (
131b
), give the new high-temperature contributions from the helical imbalance to the appropriate conductivities which are suppressed by the first power of temperature,
O(1/T)
. These conductivities vanish in the electrically neutral and chirally neutral plasmas, at
\mu_{V}=\mu_{A}=0
.

Surprisingly, the helical magnetic conductivity (
131c
) differs substantially from its known CME (
131a
) and CSE (
131b
) counterparts. First of all, the coefficient of proportionality does not show an obvious association with the axial anomaly (
133
) due to the presence of the
\ln 2
factor (see, however, the discussion on suspected role of a new helical anomaly in Refs.
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
). Secondly, the generation of the helical current is possible in the absence of any (electric, axial or helical) imbalance with all chemical potential set to zero. This property makes it somewhat similar to the mixed axial-gravitational contribution to the heat conductivity which also operates in neutral matter being proportional to the (second power of) temperature
Landsteiner et al. 2011
. The helical conductivity is given by the first term in Eq. (
131c
) proportional to temperature
T=1/\beta
and we recover our principal result (
1
). Finally, similarly to the CME and the CSE effects, the Helical Separation Effect (
1
) does not change sign under time reversal,
t\to-t
, which is a characteristic feature of anomalous dissipationless transport.

The magnetic heat conductivity evaluates to

\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}=\frac{q\mu_{H}}{\pi^{2}\beta}\ln 2+\frac{q\mu_{V}\mu_{A}}{2\pi^{2}}\\
+\frac{q\beta\mu_{H}}{24\pi^{2}}\left[3\bm{\mu}^{2}-2\mu_{H}^{2}+O(\beta^{2})\right],

(134)

where the higher-order terms vanish when
\mu_{H}=0
. In deriving the above expression, we employed the following expansion of the polylogarithm:

{\rm Li}_{2}(-e^{x})=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-x\ln 2-\frac{x^{2}}{4}-\frac{x^{3}}{24}+\frac{x^{5}}{960}+O(x^{7}).

(135)

As mentioned already, the magnetic conductivities related to anomalous transport receive contributions only from the LLL.
The other quantities appearing in Eqs. (
127
)–(
128
) receive contributions also from the HLL.
However, in the limit of a strong magnetic field,
|qB|\gg T^{2}
, the HLL contributions are exponentially suppressed. Moving as before to the case of massless fermions, we find

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|\mu_{\ell}}{2\pi^{2}}+\frac{|qB|\beta}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{\partial\mu_{\times}^{3}}{\partial\mu_{\ell}}\left[1+O(\beta^{2})\right],

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=3(P+\Pi)=\frac{3}{2}\pi_{B}=\frac{|qB|}{12\beta^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{|qB|\bm{\mu}^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}+\frac{|qB|\beta}{4\pi^{2}}\mu_{\times}^{3}\left[1+O(\beta^{2})\right],

(136)

where we denoted
\bm{\mu}^{2}=\mu_{V}^{2}+\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{H}^{2}
and
\mu_{\times}^{3}=\mu_{V}\mu_{A}\mu_{H}
.

V.3
Thermodynamic analysis

Let us consider the grand canonical potential of the system,

\Phi=-\frac{|qB|V}{4\pi^{2}\beta}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}\ln(1+e^{-\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}),

(137)

where
V
is the (infinite) system size.
The grand potential
\Phi
is related to the partition function
\mathcal{Z}={\rm tr}(\hat{\rho})
via

\mathcal{Z}=e^{-\beta\Phi}.

(138)

Given the thermodynamic relation

d\Phi=\beta^{-2}\mathcal{S}d\beta-PdV-\bm{\mathcal{Q}}\cdot d\bm{\mu},

(139)

we can identify the total entropy
\mathcal{S}
, thermodynamic pressure
\mathcal{P}
and total charge
\bm{\mathcal{Q}}
as

\mathcal{S}=\beta^{2}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\beta},\quad\mathcal{P}=-\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial V},\quad\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}=-\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\mu_{\ell}}.

(140)

Since the grand potential is linearly proportional to the system volume,
\Phi=\phi V
with
\phi
being a volume-independent quantity, it is clear that

P=-\phi=\Theta^{zz}.

(141)

It is easy to check that
Q_{V/H}\equiv\mathcal{Q}_{V/H}/V=\partial\Theta^{zz}/\partial\mu_{V/H}
. A similar relation holds for
Q_{A}
only in the case of massless fermions (
M=0
and
{\cal K}^{+}_{j}={\cal K}^{-}_{j}=1
), confirming that the chiral charge is conserved only for massless fermions.

The total energy
\mathcal{E}=\langle\widehat{H}\rangle=\mathcal{Z}^{-1}{\rm tr}(\hat{\rho}\widehat{H})
can be obtained from
\mathcal{Z}
via

\mathcal{E}=\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{Q}}-\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal{Z}}{\partial\beta}.

(142)

Since
\ln\mathcal{Z}=-\beta\Phi
, it is easy to uncover the Euler relation

s=\beta(\epsilon+P-\bm{\mu}\cdot\bm{Q}),

(143)

where
s=\mathcal{S}/V
,
\epsilon=\mathcal{E}/V
and
\bm{Q}=\bm{\mathcal{Q}}/V
.

The above discussion shows that the entropy density can be obtained as

s=\beta\left(\Theta^{tt}+\Theta^{zz}-\bm{\mu}\cdot\frac{\partial\Theta^{zz}}{\partial\bm{\mu}}\right)=\frac{\partial\Theta^{zz}}{\partial T}.

(144)

Having identified
P=\Theta^{zz}
, we are in a position to evaluate the dynamical pressure
\Pi
using Eq. (
120
):

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}(\Theta^{xx}+\Theta^{yy}-2\Theta^{zz})=-\pi_{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{6\pi^{2}}\sum_{n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j},\varsigma_{j}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{j}^{z}\,g_{j}(n_{j}|qB|-p_{z;j}^{2})}{E_{j}(e^{\beta\mathcal{E}_{j}}+1)}.

(145)

On the LLL and for massless fermions, the relation
\Pi=-\pi_{B}
can be used in conjunction with Eq. (
136
) to obtain:

\epsilon=P,\quad\pi_{B}=-\Pi=\frac{2}{3}P.

(146)

VI
Search for helical excitations in neutral plasma

Let us consider a globally-neutral plasma, characterized by vanishing chemical potentials
{\bar{\mu}}_{\ell}=0
(
\ell=V,A,H
) and temperature
{\bar{T}}
, where the bar over the symbol indicates that we consider a mean global coordinate-independent quantity. We work in the hydrodynamic approximation, which assumes small departures from local thermodynamic equilibrium.
We also assume the limit of a strong magnetic field,
|qB|\gg T^{2}
.

In a strong magnetic field, the excess in vector or axial densities generates a coherent propagation of these charges along the direction of the magnetic field in the form of a linear hydrodynamic excitation, known as the Chiral Magnetic Wave
Kharzeev and Yee 2011
. In this wave, an excess in vector (axial) charge generates an axial (vector) current along the magnetic field, which leads to a build-up of an axial (vector) charge and a cyclic repetition of the process.

We look for a similar excitation in terms of helical degrees of freedom. To this end we notice that the helical current
{\bm{j}}_{H}=\sigma^{B}_{H}{\bm{B}}
and the heat current
{\bm{j}}_{\epsilon}=\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}{\bm{B}}
, can be expressed, respectively, via Eq. (
131c
) for the helical conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{H}
and Eq. (
134
) for the heat conductivity
\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}
. These relations show that helical fluctuations are coupled to the fluctuations in temperature similarly to the coupling of vector and axial charges (currents) in the Chiral Magnetic Wave. Therefore, one could expect that the helical degrees of freedom may propagate in terms of a hypothetical “helical heat wave,” which would corresponds to a coherent hydrodynamic excitation that combines energy density and helical fluctuations.

To assess the existence of the helical heat wave as a linear hydrodynamic excitation, we consider the equations of motion derived from the charge (for all
\ell=V,A,H
), energy, and momentum conservation, namely:

\displaystyle\dot{Q}_{\ell}+Q_{\ell}\theta+\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=0,

(147a)

\displaystyle\dot{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+P+\Pi)\theta-\pi^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}-j_{\epsilon}^{\nu}\dot{u}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(147b)

\displaystyle(\epsilon+P+\Pi)\dot{u}^{\mu}-\nabla^{\mu}(P+\Pi)+\Delta^{\mu}_{\lambda}\partial_{\nu}\pi^{\nu\lambda}

\displaystyle+j_{\epsilon}^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}u^{\mu}+Dj_{\epsilon}^{\langle\mu\rangle}+j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}\theta

\displaystyle=0,

(147c)

where
D
and the overhead dot denote the comoving derivative,
Da\equiv\dot{a}\equiv u^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}a
,

\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\Delta^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}u_{\beta}
is the shear tensor with
\Delta^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}
introduced in Eq. (
118
) and
\theta=\partial_{\mu}u^{\mu}
is the expansion scalar. In the following, we focus on the dissipationless transport phenomena supported by the above set of equations and ignore dissipative corrections to the non-ideal terms
\Pi
,
\pi^{\mu\nu}
,
j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}
, and
j_{\ell}^{\mu}
. Also, we consider that the electric field is negligible. In reality, one should allow for fluctuations in
B^{\mu}
and in the electric field
E^{\mu}
, coming from the Maxwell equations. The backreaction from fluctuating electromagnetic field usually leads to a damping effect
Kharzeev and Yee 2011
which we neglect below.

We now consider infinitesimal fluctuations in a quiescent, neutral fluid of massless particles, at background temperature
\overline{T}
, in a strong magnetic field
|qB|\gg\overline{T}^{2}
. We consider the generic split of a hydrodynamic quantity
a
into its global average
\bar{a}
and fluctuation
\delta a
as follows,

a=\bar{a}+\delta a,\quad\delta a=\int d^{3}k\widetilde{\delta a}_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega)e^{-i\omega t+i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{x}}},

(148)

where
\widetilde{\delta a}_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega)
represents the amplitude of its Fourier mode of wave vector
\mathbf{k}
. For brevity, we henceforth suppress the explicit
{\mathbf{k}}
and
\omega
dependencies of fluctuations.

We take as independent parameters the fluid temperature
T
, chemical potentials
\mu_{\ell}
, and macroscopic velocity
u^{\mu}
, with
\bar{\mu}_{\ell}=0
and
\bar{u}^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}
.
The velocity fluctuations lead to a modification
u^{\mu}=\bar{u}^{\mu}+\delta u^{\mu}
that affects also the covariant magnetic field
B^{\mu}=\overline{B}^{\mu}+\delta B^{\mu}
, such that:

\overline{B}^{\mu}=B\delta^{\mu}_{z},\quad\delta B^{\mu}=B\delta u^{z}\delta^{\mu}_{z}.

(149)

Also, the comoving derivative
\dot{a}
becomes

\dot{a}\simeq\partial_{t}\delta a\rightarrow-i\omega\widetilde{\delta a},

(150)

where the arrow indicates that the Fourier transform was taken as in Eq. (
148
). More generally, the derivative leads simply to the replacement

\partial_{\mu}a^{\nu}\rightarrow-ik_{\mu}\widetilde{\delta a^{\nu}},

(151)

and in particular,
\theta\rightarrow-ik_{\mu}\widetilde{\delta u^{\mu}}=i\mathbf{k}\cdot\widetilde{\delta\mathbf{u}}
.

In the case of the charge conservation equation, Eq. (
147a
), we have

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta Q_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|}{2\pi^{2}}\delta\mu_{\ell},

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{V/A}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta\sigma^{B}_{V/A}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2\pi^{2}}\delta\mu_{A/V},

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\overline{T}\ln 2}{\pi^{2}},

\displaystyle\delta\sigma^{B}_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\delta T.

(152)

Taking into account that
\bar{\theta}=0
, the second term
Q_{\ell}\theta
in Eq. (
147a
) becomes negligible. The third term evaluates to

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=B\partial_{z}\delta\sigma^{B}_{\ell}+B\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{\ell}\partial_{t}\delta u^{z}

\displaystyle\rightarrow ik^{z}B\widetilde{\delta\sigma}^{B}_{\ell}-i\omega B\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{\ell}\widetilde{\delta u^{z}}.

(153)

Since
\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{V/A}=0
, the equations giving the conservation of
Q_{V}
and
Q_{A}
reduce to the closed set

\begin{pmatrix}\omega&-k^{z}\sigma\\
-k^{z}\sigma&\omega\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{V}\\
\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{A}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(154)

The above equations are solved when either
\omega=\pm k^{z}
or when both
\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{V}
and
\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{A}
cancel. Hence, we have uncovered the chiral magnetic wave which comprises coherent oscillations of vector and axial charges propagating, in strong magnetic field background, with the speed of light
Kharzeev and Yee 2011
.

Since
\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{H}\neq 0
, the conservation equation for the helicity charge
Q_{H}
leads to a coupling between the helicity chemical potential
\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{H}
and the fluctuations in the hydrodynamic sector,

\omega\widetilde{\delta u}{}^{z}-k^{z}\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}+\frac{\sigma\omega}{2\ln 2}\frac{\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{H}}{\overline{T}}=0.

(155)

Moving now to the
T^{\mu\nu}
sector, Eq. (
136
) shows that
\epsilon=P
and
\Pi=-\pi_{B}=-\frac{2}{3}P
. Considering that these equalities hold also in the perturbed system, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|\overline{T}^{2}}{12},

\displaystyle\delta\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{|qB|\overline{T}}{6}\delta T,

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}^{B}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\delta\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\overline{T}\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\delta\mu_{H}.

(156)

In the case of the shear-stress tensor
\pi^{\mu\nu}
, we ignore dissipative corrections and employ the form in Eq. (
123
). Writing
\pi^{\mu\nu}=\bar{\pi}^{\mu\nu}+\delta\pi^{\mu\nu}
, we have
\bar{\pi}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\pi}_{B}\times{\rm diag}(0,-1,-1,2)
with
\bar{\pi}_{B}=\frac{2}{3}\overline{P}
and

\delta\pi^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\delta\pi_{B}{\rm diag}(0,-1,-1,2)\\
+\frac{\bar{\pi}_{B}}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\delta u^{x}&-\delta u^{y}&2\delta u^{z}\\
-\delta u^{x}&0&0&0\\
-\delta u^{y}&0&0&0\\
2\delta u^{z}&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(157)

The shear tensor
\sigma^{\mu\nu}
has vanishing average,
\bar{\sigma}^{\mu\nu}=0
, while
\delta\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{(\mu}\delta u_{\nu)}-\frac{1}{3}\Delta_{\mu\nu}\theta
has vanishing components when either
\mu=t
or
\nu=t
, i.e.
\delta\sigma^{00}=\delta\sigma^{0i}=\delta\sigma^{i0}=0
. On the spatial part, we have
\delta\sigma_{ij}=\partial_{(i}\delta u_{j)}-\frac{1}{3}g_{ij}\theta
, or in Fourier space,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta\sigma}^{ij}=-\frac{i}{2}(k^{i}\widetilde{\delta u}^{j}+k^{j}\widetilde{\delta u}^{i})-\frac{i}{3}g^{ij}\mathbf{k}\cdot\widetilde{\delta\mathbf{u}}.

(158)

In the case of the energy conservation equation, we employ
\pi^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\pi}_{B}(\delta\sigma^{xx}+\delta\sigma^{yy}-2\delta\sigma^{zz})\rightarrow\frac{i}{3}\overline{P}(\mathbf{k}\cdot\widetilde{\delta\mathbf{u}}-3k^{z}\widetilde{\delta u^{z}})
,
j_{\epsilon}^{\nu}\dot{u}_{\nu}\simeq 0
, and

\partial_{\mu}j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}=B\partial_{z}\delta\sigma^{B}_{\epsilon}\rightarrow\frac{ik\overline{P}}{\overline{T}}\frac{12\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{\delta\mu}_{H}

(159)

to arrive at

2\omega\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}-{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\widetilde{\delta\mathbf{u}}-k^{z}\widetilde{\delta u}{}^{z}-\frac{12\sigma\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}k^{z}\frac{\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{H}}{\overline{T}}=0.

(160)

Now we move on to the momentum conservation equation, i.e. Eq. (
147c
).
The terms
j_{\epsilon}^{\lambda}\nabla_{\lambda}u^{\mu}
and
j_{\epsilon}^{\mu}\theta
are of higher order and can thus be ignored. Furthermore, the projector
\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}
together with
\dot{u}^{0}\simeq 0
ensures that the
\mu=0
relation reduces to a tautological
0=0
.
For
\mu=i\in\{x,y\}
and
\mu=z
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{i}_{\lambda}\partial_{\nu}\pi^{\nu\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{i}\delta\pi_{B}+\frac{\bar{\pi}_{B}}{2}\partial_{t}\delta u^{i}\rightarrow-\frac{i\overline{P}}{3}\left(2k^{i}\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}-\omega\widetilde{\delta u^{i}}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta^{z}_{\lambda}\partial_{\nu}\pi^{\nu\lambda}

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}\delta\pi_{B}+\bar{\pi}_{B}\partial_{t}\delta u^{z}\rightarrow\frac{2i\overline{P}}{3}\left(2k^{z}\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}-\omega\widetilde{\delta u^{z}}\right).

(161)

We thus arrive at:

\displaystyle\omega\widetilde{\delta u}{}^{i}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\omega\widetilde{\delta u}{}^{z}-k^{z}\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}+\omega\frac{6\sigma\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{H}

\displaystyle=0.

(162)

The first relation above shows that the plasma does not support oscillations in the plane orthogonal to the magnetic field. The second relation in Eq. (
162
) can be used in conjunction with Eqs. (
155
) and (
160
) to derive

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\omega&-k^{z}&{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\omega}{2\ln 2}}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
-k^{z}&\omega&{\displaystyle-\frac{6\sigma\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}k^{z}}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\omega&-k^{z}&{\displaystyle\frac{6\sigma\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\omega}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\delta u}{}^{z}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\delta T}}{\overline{T}}}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\delta\mu}{}_{H}}{\overline{T}}}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(163)

In order to have non-trivial solutions of the above equation, the determinant of the matrix on the left-hand side must vanish. This leads to the equation

\sigma\omega\left(\frac{1}{2\ln 2}-\frac{6\ln 2}{\pi^{2}}\right)(\omega^{2}-k_{z}^{2})=0,

(164)

where the solution
\omega=0
corresponds to the mode that supports helicity chemical potential fluctuations. The gapless modes
\omega=\pm k^{z}
have identically
\widetilde{\delta\mu}_{H}=0
and correspond to the standard sound modes present in the ideal fluid, which in the present case propagate at the speed of light.

VII
Conclusion

In our paper, we have found the Helical Separation Effect (HSE) which implies that charged massless fermions develop a dissipationless flow of helicity along the background magnetic field (
1
). This dissipationless effect is similar to the Chiral Separation Effect (CSE)
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
, which generates the flow of the chiral charge along magnetic field lines in dense fermionic matter. Contrary to the CSE, the HSE produces the helical current in a neutral plasma in which all chemical potentials vanish. Moreover, the expression for the HSE current (
1
) contains a “
\ln 2
” factor thus making it difficult to associate this effect to a known anomaly (see, however, the discussions in Ref.
Ambrus 2020
;
Ambrus and Chernodub 2023
on possible existence of helical anomalies). The associated helical conductivity is determined by temperature which makes it distantly similar to the axial-gravitational effects
Landsteiner et al. 2011
. The presence of matter plays an auxiliary role enhancing the helical current (
2
), (
3
) generated by the HSE. The same remarks are also valid for the Helical Magnetic Heat Effect (
5
) which generates, in the presence of non-vanishing helical charge density, the fermionic heat flux along the magnetic field.

Since the helical current generated by the HSE is linearly proportional to the background temperature and magnetic field, the helical current (
1
) and the heat current (
5
) become coupled together via the HSE in a striking similarly with the coupling – mediated by the CME and CSE – of the vector and chirality currents that result in the appearance of the chiral magnetic wave
Kharzeev and Yee 2011
. However, a detailed analysis shows that the fluctuations of the helical charge in a neutral plasma do not lead to the appearance of a new hydrodynamic mode. The helical degree of freedom may, however, lead to the appearance of additional hydrodynamic waves in dense plasma, which are left beyond the scope of this paper.

The helicity plays a complementary role to chirality, as the total helicity of a particle-antiparticle ensemble is given by the sum of axial charges carried by particles minus the sum of the axial charges possessed by antiparticles. As the helicity is different from chirality, its role in phenomenological applications in Quark-Gluon plasma may lead to new observational effects which remain to be explored.

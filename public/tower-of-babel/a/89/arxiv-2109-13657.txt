Global Well-Posedness For Half-Wave Maps With S 2 S^{2} and ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} Targets For Small Smooth Initial Data
Yang Liu

Abstract
. We prove global well-posedness for the half-wave map with S 2 S^{2} target for small H ˙ n 2 × H ˙ n 2 − 1 \dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1} initial data. We also prove the global well-posedness for the equation with ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} target for small smooth B ˙ 2 , 1 n 2 × B ˙ 2 , 1 n 2 − 1 \dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{2,1} initial data.



1.
Introduction

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
M=\mathbb{H}^{2}
or
S^{2}
, be smooth and bounded with the property that
\nabla_{t,x}u(t,\cdot)\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
r\in(1,\infty)
, and furthermore
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}u(t,x)=Q
for some fixed
Q\in M
, for each
t
. We define the operator
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-\triangle)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{j}(\partial_{j}u)
, and we say that
u
is a
half-wave map
if it satisfies

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u=u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u.

We define
a\times_{\eta}b:=(\eta,1,1)(a\times b)
, where
\eta=\pm 1
for
M=S^{2},\ \mathbb{H}^{2}
respectably.

The model is formally related to the Schrödinger maps equation

u_{t}=u\times\triangle u,

and the classical wave equation

\Box u=\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}u=-u\partial_{\alpha}u^{T}\partial^{\alpha}u.

Therefore, one way to consider the problem is to reformulate (
1.1
) into a nonlinear wave type equation as in Krieger and Sire
[
25
]
:

(1.2)

\begin{split}\Box u=(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta)u=&(\nabla u\cdot\nabla u-\partial_{t}u\cdot\partial_{t}u)u\\
&+\Pi_{u_{\perp}}\left[\ (u\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u)\ (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right]\\
&+\Pi_{u_{\perp}}\left[u\times(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(u\times(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)-u\times\left(u\times(-\Delta)u\right)\right]\end{split}

The advantage of this reformulation is that we can use the well-established wave equation theory to solve the problem (e.g.
[
32
]
,
[
30
]
). We first prove the global well-posedness of (
1.2
) with small critical initial data, then we will show that such a solution
u
also solves the original half-wave map equation (
1.1
) in Theorem
5.8
.

The global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with target
S^{2}
for small
\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{2,1}
initial data was established by Krieger and Sire
[
25
]
for
n\geq 5
. The result was later improved by Kiesenhofer and Krieger
[
15
]
to
n=4
.

In this work, the author establishes the global well-posedness with
S^{2}
target (
1.1
) for small
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
initial data and the global well-posedness problem for small smooth
\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{2,1}
initial data with target
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

1.1.
General Kähler Manifold

We propose a general formulation of the half-wave equation with a Kähler manifold target. Consider a Kähler manifold
(M,g,J,\omega)
, where
M

is a smooth
m
-dimensional complex manifold. It also has a structure of a real
2m
-dimensional smooth manifold.
(M,g)
is a smooth
2m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold embeds into
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
k\geq 2m+1
, by the Nash embedding theorem. For a Kähler manifold, we have a compatible triple
(g,\omega,J)
, where
J
is an integrable almost-complex structure of the complex manifold
M
such that
J^{2}=-id
and
\omega
is a closed symplectic two-form defined by

\omega(X,Y):=g(JX,Y),\ \forall X,Y\in TM.

Moreover, a Kähler manifold is also Hermitian, hence we have
g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y)
,
\forall X,Y\in TM
.

The half-wave map
u
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with a Kähler manifold
M
target satisfies

(1.3)

\partial_{t}u=J(u)\left[P_{T}(u)(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right].

Here
P_{T}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathcal{M}_{k}(\mathbb{R}))
is the orthogonal projection onto
T_{z}M
as in the work of Da Lio-Rivière
[
6
]
defined in the following:

1.1 Definition
.

Let
P_{T},P_{N}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k},M_{k}(\mathbb{R}))
where

M_{k}(\mathbb{R})
is the set of
k\times k
matrices with entries taking values in

\mathbb{R}
. Let
P_{T}
be the orthogonal projection of
T_{z}M
and

P_{N}
be the orthogonal projection onto
T_{z}^{\perp}M
s.t.

(i)

P_{T}\circ P_{T}=P_{T}

(ii)

P_{N}\circ P_{N}=P_{N}

(iii)

P_{T}+P_{N}=1_{k\times k}

(iv)

\|\partial_{z}P_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty

(v)

\langle P_{T}(z)U)\cdot(P_{N}(z)V\rangle=0\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{k},\ \forall U,V\in T_{z}{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

We can view
S^{2}
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}
as Kähler manifolds with the almost complex structure
J(u):=u\times_{\eta}
. In this paper, we focus on the half-wave map equation with these two targets,

\partial_{t}u=u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u.

1.2.
Critical
S^{2}
Case

We study the global well-posedness problem of the half-wave map with
smooth initial data
u[0]:=\big(u(0,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\big)=(u_{0},u_{1}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow S^{2}\times TS^{2}
s.t.

u_{1}=u_{0}\times(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}
, and

\|u[0]\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}<\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon\ll 1
is sufficiently small.

We improve the result from
\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2,1}\subseteq L^{\infty}
to the slightly larger space
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}
which does not embed into
L^{\infty}
. It is worth noting that both spaces share the same scaling invariance property.
As discussed above, the half-wave map can be reformulated into a nonlinear wave-type equation (
1.2
). The global well-posedness problem for the wave map with critical initial data has been studied by Tao
[
32
]
, Klainerman-Rodnianski
[
19
]
and
Shatah-Struwe
[
29
]
. In particular, we adopt the frequency fold localization method and the approximately parallel transport argument from the work of Tao
[
32
]
. In Section
5
, we will show that all the additional nonlinear terms introduced in the reformulated half-wave equation (
1.2
) are “ well-behaved ” to apply such methods.

Our result is as follows.

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
u:\mathbb{R}^{1+n}\rightarrow S^{2}
, such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ u(t,x)=Q
for a fixed
Q\in S^{2}
, for all t. Let
u[0]:=\big(u(0,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\big)=(u_{0},u_{1}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow S^{2}\times TS^{2}
s.t.

u_{1}=u_{0}\times(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}
be a smooth pair of data satisfying (
1.1
) with
u(0,\cdot)
is constant outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(this ensure that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u(0,\cdot)
is well defined ) and

(1.4)

\|u[0]\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}<\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon\ll 1
is sufficiently small.

Then the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) with initial data
u[0]
admits a smooth global solution.

1.3 Remark
.

We restrict to
n\geq 5
because we want to take advantage of the Strichartz estimate
L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}
for the nonlinearities, which is not available when
n=4
. In that case, we refer readers to techniques used in Kiesenhofer and Krieger
[
15
]
.

In order to prove this theorem, we first establish the local well-posedness of (
1.2
) with more regular
\dot{H}^{s}\times\dot{H}^{s-1},\ s>\frac{n}{2},
initial data in Section
4
. Then we adopt the ideas from Tao
[
31
]
to extend this result to global well-posedness with small critical initial data in Section
5
. In the end, we show that the solution of (
1.2
) solves the original equation (
1.1
) to close the argument.

1.3.
Critical Besov
\mathbb{H}^{2}
Case

The non-compactness of
\mathbb{H}^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
presents difficulties when working with
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
initial data as we lose the
L^{\infty}
bound. In the context of the wave equation with target
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, the global well-posedness problem has been solved by Krieger
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
. Dealing with the non-local terms of the half-wave equation is more delicate. Instead, we extend the global well-posedness result of
[
25
]
and
[
15
]
for the
S^{2}
target to the
\mathbb{H}^{2}
target with the help of the intrinsic distance function of the hyperbolic plane.

The intrinsic hyperbolic distance on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is defined by

(1.5)

d_{H}(p,q)=\text{arccosh}(-p\cdot_{\eta}q)=\ln(-p\cdot_{\eta}q+\sqrt{(p\cdot_{\eta}q)^{2}-1}),

for
p,q\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Note here that
p\cdot_{\eta}q\leqslant-1
,
\forall p,q\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
. We also have the induced Riemannian metric
ds^{2}=-dx_{0}^{2}+dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

For a given fixed point
Q\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, we define the
L^{p}
spaces taking values in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
by

L_{Q}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\mathbb{H}^{2}):=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}^{1+n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}\big|\ d_{H}(f(x),Q)\in L^{p}\right\},

as in the work of
[
21
]
and
[
13
]
.

We make similar definitions for the Sobolev spaces, Besov spaces, and others.
Adopting the intrinsic distance function, we have a similar theorem for the
\mathbb{H}^{2}
targeted half-wave map:

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
n\geqslant 4
,
u:\mathbb{R}^{1+n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
bounded such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ u(t,x)=Q
for a fixed
Q\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, for all t. Let
u[0]:=\big(u(0,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(0,\cdot)\big)=(u_{0},u_{1}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}\times T\mathbb{H}^{2}
where

u_{1}=u_{0}\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}
be a smooth pair of data satisfying (
1.1
) s.t.
u(0,\cdot)
is constant outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

(1.6)

\|u[0]\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2,1;Q}\times\dot{B}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{2,1;Q}}<\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon\ll 1
is sufficiently small.

Then (
1.1
) admits a global smooth solution.

It is worth noting that if the target is a compact subset of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, we can establish the global-well posedness for small
\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}
initial data as in the
S^{2}
case .

1.4.
Physical Background

The half-wave map equation (
1.1
) admits a conserved energy

E(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}dx.

This gives the a priori condition that
u(t)\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which implies that the
half-wave map
is energy-critical in dimension
n=1
. In the work of Lenzmann and Schikorra
[
27
]
, the authors give a full classification of the travelling solitary waves for the energy-critical problem with target
S^{2}
for
n=1
. However, there has not been much progress for more general global well-posedness of (
1.1
) in low dimensions
n\leq 3
. This is partly due to the lack of the Strichartz estimates used in the higher dimensional case for the null structure. We also point out that the conserved energy is closely related to the study of fractional harmonic maps by Da Lio and Rivière (see
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
5
]
).

The one-dimensional energy-critical half-wave maps are of notable physics interest. It’s intensively studied in the works of
[
2
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
26
]
, and
[
8
]
. The one-dimensional half-wave maps arise as a continuum limit of the discrete

Calogero-Moser (CM) spin system.
For the CM systems, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
and
[
3
]
in which the authors study the theory of completely integrable systems. In addition, the classical CM spin systems can be obtained by taking a suitable semiclassical limit of the quantum spin chains related to the well-known
Haldane-Shastry (HS) spin chains
, see e.g.
[
12
]

[
28
]
, which are exactly solvable quantum models.

2.
Transform to Wave Equation Form

We first reformulate the half-wave equation

u_{t}=u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u

as a nonlinear wave equation involving additional non-local terms. We focus on the target
\mathbb{H}^{2}
variant here. The
S^{2}
case is in the work
[
25
]
.

By using the cross product rule of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
:

a\times_{\eta}b:=\eta(a\times b),

a\cdot_{\eta}b:=(\eta a)\cdot b,

and

(a\times_{\eta}b)\times_{\eta}c=(c\cdot_{\eta}b)\ a-(a\cdot_{\eta}b)\ b.

We compute

\displaystyle\Box u=(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta)u=

\displaystyle\left((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\ u-\left(u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\ (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u

\displaystyle+u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)-u\times_{\eta}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)u\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla u\cdot_{\eta}\nabla u\right)u.

Additionally, we have the relation

\displaystyle\left(((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u)\ u-\left(u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\ (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\cdot_{\eta}u

\displaystyle\quad=-\left((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)-\left(u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\text{}-\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\times_{\eta}u\|_{\eta}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=-(\partial_{t}u\cdot_{\eta}\partial_{t}u).

Hence, we can deduce

\begin{split}\Box u=(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta)u=&\left((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\ u-\left(u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)\ (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\\
&+u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)-u\times_{\eta}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)u\right)\\
&-\left(\nabla u\cdot_{\eta}\nabla u\right)u\\
=&(\partial_{t}u\cdot_{\eta}\partial_{t}u-\nabla u\cdot_{\eta}\nabla u)u\\
&+\Pi_{u_{\perp}}\left[\ (u\cdot_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u)\ (-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right]\\
&+\Pi_{u_{\perp}}\left[u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\right)-u\times_{\eta}\left(u\times_{\eta}(-\Delta)u\right)\right]\end{split}

where
\Pi_{u_{\perp}}
is the projection onto the orthonormal complement of
u
. Note here that
\nabla
only represents the spatial derivatives. So we reformulate the half-wave map as a wave-type equation with two additional non-local terms. In the following sections, readers will see that these two additional non-local terms formally behave like
u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u
.

3.
Technical Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the function spaces and technical tools we are going to use.
Let
P_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, be standard Littlewood-Paley multipliers on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
Q_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
be the multipliers which localize a space-time function
f(t,x)
to dyadic distance
\sim 2^{j}
from the light cone
\big|\tau\big|=\big|\xi\big|
on the frequency domain.

For a smooth cutoff function
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
s.t.

\sum_{k\in Z}\chi(\frac{x}{2^{k}})=1\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

we set

\widehat{P_{k}f}(\xi)=\chi\Big(\frac{|\xi|}{2^{k}}\Big)\widehat{f}(\xi),

\widehat{Q_{j}f}(\tau,\xi)=\chi\big(\frac{\big||\tau|-|\xi|\big|}{2^{j}}\big)\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi).

where
\widehat{f}(\tau,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}e^{-i(t\tau+x\cdot\xi)}f(t,x)\ dtdx
is the space-time Fourier transform of
f
.

With these definitions, we define the homogenous hyperbolic sobolev space
X^{s,\theta}
,
s\geq\frac{n}{2}
(see
[
16
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
7
]
) as

\dot{X}^{s,\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}):=\big\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1});\ \|u\|_{\dot{X}^{s,\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\theta}\|\nabla_{x}^{s}Q_{j}u\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}<\infty\big\},

where we choose
\theta
such that

s-\frac{n}{2}=\theta-\frac{1}{2}.

Furthermore, for the critical space, when
s=\frac{n}{2}
, we introduce the additional Besov type structure to the hyperbolic Sobolev space. We set

\begin{split}&\big\|u\big\|_{\dot{X}^{\frac{n}{2},\frac{1}{2},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}:=\sup_{j\in Z}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\big\|\nabla_{x}^{\frac{n}{2}}Q_{j}u\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})},\\
&\big\|F\big\|_{\dot{X}^{\frac{n}{2}-1,-\frac{1}{2},1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}:=\sum_{j\in Z}2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\big\|\nabla_{x}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}Q_{j}F\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.\end{split}

We also have the following bound for the localization in time operation on
X^{s,\theta}
spaces(see
[
7
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
4
]

3.1 Lemma
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\varphi(t)=\begin{cases}1\ \text{for}\ |t|\leq 1,\\
0\ \text{for}\ |t|\geq 2.\end{cases}

For a given
T>0
, we define a scaling function

\varphi_{T}:=\varphi\big(\frac{t}{T}\big),

then we have

(3.1)

\|\varphi_{T}u\|_{\dot{X}^{s,\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim\max\{1,T^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}\}\ \|u\|_{\dot{X}^{s,\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}.

Given the wave admissible condition

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{n-1}{2q}\leq\frac{n-1}{4},\ 2\leq p,q\leq\infty,\ \text{for }n\geq 4,

we define our solution spaces with the classical Strichartz norms. For the global critical case, we set

(3.2)

\begin{split}\big\|u\big\|_{S}&:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{(p,q)\,\text{admissible}}2^{(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{n}{q}-1)k}\big\|\nabla_{t,x}P_{k}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\\
&\quad+\big\|\nabla_{t,x}P_{k}u\big\|_{\dot{X}^{\frac{n}{2}-1,\frac{1}{2},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\\
&=:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|P_{k}u\big\|_{S_{k}}.\end{split}

For the local sub-critical case, when
s>\frac{n}{2}
, we set

(3.3)

\begin{split}\big\|u\big\|_{S_{\text{loc}}}&:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{(p,q)\,\text{admissible}}2^{(s-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{n}{q}-1)k}\big\|\nabla_{t,x}P_{k}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\quad+\big\|\nabla_{t,x}\varphi_{T}P_{k}u\big\|_{\dot{X}^{s-1,\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\\
&=:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|P_{k}u\big\|_{S_{\text{loc}}}.\end{split}

Further, we define the spaces to control the nonlinearity respectably as

(3.4)

\big\|F\big\|_{N}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|P_{k}F\big\|_{L_{t}^{1}\dot{H}_{x}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}+\dot{X}^{\frac{n}{2}-1,-\frac{1}{2},1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

(3.5)

\big\|F\big\|_{N_{\text{loc}}}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\varphi_{T}P_{k}F\big\|_{L_{t}^{1}\dot{H}_{x}^{s-1}+\dot{X}^{s-1,\theta-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

From the works of wave maps
[
22
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
34
]
, we have the Strichartz estimate,

3.2 Proposition
.

For
s\geq\frac{n}{2}
, we have

(3.6)

\big\|u\big\|_{S}\lesssim\big\|u[0]\big\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\times\dot{H}^{s-1}}+\big\|\Box u\big\|_{N}.

Additionally, we introduce the following useful multilinear lemma to estimate the difference between non-local terms.

3.3 Lemma

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
25
]
)
.

For the following bilinear expression (where
\chi_{k_{j}}(\cdot)
is the smooth cutoff to the annulus
|\xi|\sim 2^{k_{j}}
)

F(u,v)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(\xi,\eta)e^{ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}\chi_{k_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)\chi_{k_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{v}(\eta)d\xi d\eta

where the multiplier
m(\xi,\eta)
is
C^{\infty}
with respect to the coordinates on the support of
\chi_{k_{1}}(\xi)\cdot\chi_{k_{2}}(\eta)
, and satisfies point-wise bounds

\big|m(\xi,\eta)\big|\leq\gamma\lesssim 1,\,\big|(2^{k_{1}}\nabla_{\xi})^{i}(2^{k_{2}}\nabla)^{j}m(\xi,\eta)\big|\lesssim_{i,j}1,\,\forall i,,j.

Then if
\big\|\cdot\big\|_{Z},\big\|\cdot\big\|_{Y},\big\|\cdot\big\|_{X}
are translation invariant norms with the property that

\big\|u\cdot v\big\|_{Z}\leq\big\|u\big\|_{X}\cdot\big\|v\big\|_{Y},

it follows that

\big\|F(u,v)\big\|_{Z}\lesssim\gamma^{(1-)}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u\big\|_{X}\big\|P_{k_{2}}v\big\|_{Y},

where the implied constant only depends on the size of finitely many derivatives of
m
.

In particular, for
k_{1},k_{2}\lesssim 1
, we have

(3.7)

\big\|P_{0}\left((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{k_{1}}\cdot u_{k_{2}})-u_{k_{1}}\cdot(-\triangle^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})\right)\big\|_{Z}\lesssim 2^{k_{1}}\big\|u_{k_{1}}\big\|_{X}\big\|u_{k_{2}}\big\|_{Y},

We show that the sub-critical Cauchy problem of (

,

, initial data on the domain

. The local and global well-posedness problems of the sub-critical wave maps have been studied by Klainerman-Machedon
[

]
for

, Klainerman-Selberg
[
20
]
for

, and Keel-Tao
[
14
]
for

Accounting on the higher regularity of initial data, we first prove a multilinear estimate proposition for the non-local terms of the (
1.2
). Then we use an iterative argument and the fixed point theorem to prove the local well-posedness of the sub-critical problem. In particular, we focus on the case when
s
is close to
\frac{n}{2}
i.e.
0<s-\frac{n}{2}\ll 1
. For larger

, the result follows by the persistence of higher regularity argument.

We first build the multilinear estimates:

4.1 Proposition
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow S^{2}
, such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ u(t,x)=Q
for a fixed
Q\in S^{2}
, for all t. For some
\beta,\sigma>0
, we have the following estimates.

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\big\|P_{k}\big[u(\partial_{t}u\cdot\partial_{t}u-\nabla u\cdot\nabla u)\big]\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}(1+\big\|u\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big).\end{split}

Furthermore, for
\widetilde{u}
maps into a small neighborhood of

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\big\|P_{k}\big(\Pi_{\widetilde{u}}\big((-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u\big)(u\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u)\big)\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}\prod_{v=u,\widetilde{u}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\end{split}

and

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\big\|P_{k}\big(\Pi_{\widetilde{u}}\big[u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u)-u\times(u\times(-\triangle)u)\big]\big)\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}\prod_{v=u,\widetilde{u}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big).\end{split}

Using the notation

\triangle_{1,2}F^{(j)}:=F^{(1)}-F^{(2)},

we have the difference estimates:

(4.4)

\begin{split}&\big\|\triangle_{1,2}P_{k}\big[u^{(j)}(\nabla u^{(j)}\cdot\nabla u^{(j)}-\partial_{t}u^{(j)}\cdot\partial_{t}u^{(j)})\big]\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}(1+\max_{j}\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})(\max_{j}\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(1)}-P_{k}u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\\
&+T^{\beta}(1+\max_{j}\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})(\big\|u^{(1)}-u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big(\max_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big),\\
\end{split}

and the two similarly estimates

(4.5)

\begin{split}&\big\|P_{k}\triangle_{1,2}\big(\Pi_{\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}\big((-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{(j)}\big)(u^{(j)}\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{(j)})\big)\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}\max_{j}\prod_{v=u^{(j)},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(1)}-P_{k_{2}}u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\\
&+T^{\beta}\max_{j}\prod_{v=u^{(j)},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u^{(1)}-u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\max_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\\
&+T^{\beta}\max_{j}(1+\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|\widetilde{u}^{(1)}-\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\max_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big),\\
\end{split}

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\big\|P_{k}\triangle_{1,2}\big(\Pi_{\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}\big[u^{(j)}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u^{(j)}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{(j)})-u^{(j)}\times(u^{(j)}\times(-\triangle)u^{(j)})\big]\big)\big\|_{N_{loc}}\\
&\lesssim T^{\beta}\max_{j}\prod_{v=u^{(j)},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(1)}-P_{k_{2}}u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\\
&+T^{\beta}\max_{j}\prod_{v=u^{(j)},\widetilde{u}^{(j)}}(1+\big\|v\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|u^{(1)}-u^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\max_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big)\\
&+T^{\beta}\max_{j}(1+\big\|u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}})\big\|\widetilde{u}^{(1)}-\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big(\max_{j}\sum_{k_{1}\in Z}2^{-\sigma|k-k_{1}|}\big\|P_{k_{1}}u^{(j)}\big\|_{S_{loc}}\big).\\
\end{split}

The basic technics of the proof are analogous to the work of Krieger-Sire

, hence we omit the detailed proof here. We refer the reader to Appendix

With the multilinear estimates, we then have the local well-posedness result.

\big\|u[t]\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\big(\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{x}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{x}\big)}\lesssim\big\|u[0]\big\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}}_{x}\times\dot{H}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}_{x}}.

Firstly, we introduce the frequency fold localization concept from Tao

5.2 Definition
.

A

One can easily verify that for a pair of function
(f,g)
lies underneath an envelope

(5.5)

\|(f,g)\|_{\dot{H}^{n/2}\times\dot{H}^{n/2-1}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

With the definition of frequency envelope, we now define
error

5.3 Definition

For a function

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim C_{0}^{3}c_{0}\varepsilon

For the half-wave equation (

(5.6)

\Box u_{0}=-2u_{\leq-10}\nabla_{t,x}u_{\leq-10}\nabla_{t,x}u_{0}+\textit{error}.

Instead of proving the global well-posedness Proposition

)

We use the localized version of the standard Strichartz estimate Proposition

The initial condition

&\partial_{t}w(0)=U^{-1}(0)\partial_{t}u(0)-U^{-1}(0)\partial_{t}U(0)U^{-1}(0)u(0)\end{cases}

By choosing

5.8 Theorem
.

For a smooth initial data

as in the Theorem
1.2
. For the global smooth solution

1.1

Proof.

In order to show the solution obtains by Proposition

also solves (
1.1

and the energy-type functional

Firstly, since the initial data

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}

Because the solution
u
is smooth, thus the energy-type functional is differentiable with respect to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X=-X\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u-u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}X-u\big(X\cdot(u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u_{t})\big),

\end{split}

By the multilinear estimate of Lemma
3.3
and Sobolev inequality
\dot{H}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2n}{3}}

\displaystyle\big\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}\big(X\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}X\big)-u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}X\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\big\|u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-1}{4}}X-(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}X\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}X\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Hence, for the first term of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}\big(X\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}X\big)\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}X\,dx

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}X\|^{2}_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\|u\|)\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}X\|^{2}_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\big\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{n-3}{4}}\big(u\big(X\cdot(u\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u_{t})\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

Combining these two estimates, we know (

By the Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{k(s-1)}\big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}P_{k}\big[u_{<k_{1}-10}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{k_{1}}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})

\displaystyle-(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{<k_{1}-10}\times(u_{k_{1}}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})\big)\big]\big\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(s-1)}T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}\big\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{<k_{1}-10}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\big\|u_{k_{1}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2+\gamma}L_{x}^{\infty}}\big\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\|u_{k}\|_{S_{loc}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{3}<k_{1}-10\end{subarray}}2^{(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{2}-s)k_{3}}\|u_{k_{3}}\|_{S_{loc}}\ 2^{(-\frac{1}{2+\gamma}+\frac{n}{2}-s)k_{1}}\|u_{k_{1}}\|_{S_{loc}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\|u\|_{S_{loc}}^{3}

Therefore, we can consider
P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{<k_{1}-10}\times(u_{k_{1}}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})\big)\big]
instead. Using the cross product rule, we reformulate the expression as

(A.28)

\begin{split}&P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{<k_{1}-10}\times(u_{k_{1}}\times(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})\big)-u_{<k_{1}-10}\times(u_{k_{1}}\times(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}})\big]\\
&=P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})-(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)\big]\\
&-P_{k}[\big(u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}})-(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)]\\
&=P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})-\big(u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}\big)\big]\\
&+P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)\big].\end{split}

Furthermore, we also know the differences

\displaystyle\|u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\|u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}-(-\triangle)(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}}

are all desirable bounds so that we can replace them respectably.

Additionally, by putting
u_{k_{1}}
into
L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}
, we also know that

\displaystyle P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}})\big)\big]-P_{k}\big[u_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}})\big]

\displaystyle\simeq P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{k_{1}}(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{<k_{2}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\big]-P_{k}\big[u_{k_{1}}(-\triangle)(u_{<k_{2}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\big]

Then we can use the geometric property (
A.23
) to replace

P_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{2}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})=-\frac{1}{2}P_{k_{2}}(u_{k_{2}+10}\cdot u_{k_{2}+10}).

We conclude that

\displaystyle 2^{k(s-1)}\big\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{k_{1}}(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}(u_{<k_{2}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\big]

\displaystyle-P_{k}\big[u_{k_{1}}(-\triangle)(u_{<k_{2}-10}\cdot u_{k_{2}})\big]\big\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(s-1)}T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}\big\|(-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{1}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\infty}}\ 2^{k_{2}}\ \big\|u_{>k_{2}+10}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2+\gamma}L_{x}^{\infty}}\big\|u_{>k_{2}+10}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{1}<k+10}\ 2^{(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-s)k_{1}}\|u_{k_{1}}\|_{S_{loc}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\\
k_{3}>k_{2}+10\end{subarray}}\ 2^{ks}2^{(\frac{n}{2}-2s-\frac{1}{2+\gamma})k_{3}}\|u_{k_{3}}\|^{2}_{S_{loc}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\|u\|^{3}_{S_{loc}}.

It only remains to bound the difference

P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})\big)\big].

As before, we replace

P_{k_{1}}(u_{<k_{1}-10}\cdot u_{k_{1}})=-\frac{1}{2}P_{k_{1}}(u_{>k_{1}+10}\cdot u_{>k_{1}+10}).

Then we deduce

\displaystyle 2^{k(s-1)}\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}P_{k}\big[(-\triangle)u_{k_{2}}P_{k_{1}}(u_{>k_{1}+10}\cdot u_{>k_{1}+10})

\displaystyle-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big((-\triangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{k_{2}}P_{k_{1}}(u_{>k_{1}+10}\cdot u_{>k_{1}+10})\big)\big]\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(s-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\|P_{k_{1}}(u_{>k_{1}+10}\cdot u_{>k_{1}+10})\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{\infty}}\|u_{k_{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\|u_{k_{2}}\|_{S_{loc}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<k+10\\
k_{2}\in[k-10,k+10]\end{subarray}}2^{k_{1}}2^{(-\frac{1}{2+\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}+n-2s)k_{3}}\|u_{k_{3}}\|^{2}_{S_{loc}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\frac{\gamma}{2(2+\gamma)}}\|u\|^{3}_{S_{loc}}.

Hence, we conclude the multilinear estimates.

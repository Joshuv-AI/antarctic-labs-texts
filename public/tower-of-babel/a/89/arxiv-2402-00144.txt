Abstract
. We have revised the softness property introduced by Jörg Brendle and Haim Judah (perfect sets of random reals. Israel J. Math., 83(1-2):153–176,18 1993), to present a new definition of a class of posets called σ \sigma -soft-linked. Our work demonstrates that these posets work well to preserve the evasion number as well as the bounding number small in generic extensions. Furthermore, we establish a connection between our concept and the Fréchet-linked notion introduced by Diego A. Mejía (Matrix iterations with vertical support restrictions. In Proceedings of the 14th1 and 15th Asian Logic Conferences, pages 213–248. World Sci. Publ., Hackensack, NJ, 2019).

Introduction

Blass’s
[
Bla94
]
study of set-theoretic aspects of the Specker phenomenon in Abelian group theory led him to introduce the concept of evasion and prediction, which is a combinatorial concept. He investigated several evasion numbers and how large they are compared to other cardinal characteristics. In particular, with the cardinals in Cichoń’s diagram. Brendle
[
Bre95
]
extended these ideas and studied them more closely.

Fix
b\in((\omega+1)\smallsetminus 2)^{\omega}
. An
\prod b
-predictor
is a pair
\pi=(D,\langle\pi_{n}\mid n\in D\rangle)
such that
D\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
and
\pi_{n}\colon\prod_{k<n}b(k)\to b(n)
for
n\in D
. Let
\Pi_{b}
be the collection of
\prod b
-predictors.

For
\pi\in\Pi_{b}
and
f\in\prod b
write

\pi\sqsubset^{\textbf{pr}}f\text{\ iff \ }f(n)=\pi_{n}(f{\upharpoonright}n)\text{\ for all but finitely many\ }n\in D

in which case we say that
\pi
predict
f
; otherwise
f
evades
\pi
. We define the corresponding
evasion number

\mathfrak{e}_{b}:=\mathfrak{e}_{\prod b}=\min\{|F|\mid\,F\subseteq\prod b\textrm{\ and\ }\forall\pi\in\Pi_{b}\,\exists f\in F\,(f\text{\ evades\ }\pi)\}.

It is clear that
b\leq c
we have that
\mathfrak{e}_{c}\leq\mathfrak{e}_{b}
. In the case that
b
eventually equals
\omega
, we get
\mathfrak{e}:=\mathfrak{e}_{b}
the
evasion number
; in case that
\prod b=n^{\omega}
(
n\geq 2
), we set
\mathfrak{e}_{n}:=\mathfrak{e}_{b}
. Finally, we let
\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}:=\min\{\mathfrak{e}_{b}\mid\,b\in\omega^{\omega}\}
denote the
unbounded evasion number
and let
\mathfrak{e}_{\textbf{fin}}:=\min\{\mathfrak{e}_{n}\mid\,n\in\omega\}
the
finite evasion number
. It is well-known that
\mathfrak{e}\leq\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}\leq\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{fin}}
. Below we summarize some ZFC results related to the evasion numbers.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
Bre95
, Sec. 4.2]
)
.

(1)

\mathfrak{e}\geq\min\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}\}
. Moreover,
\min\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{e}\}=\min\{\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}\}
, so
\mathfrak{b}>\mathfrak{e}
implies
\mathfrak{e}=\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{fin}}=\mathfrak{e}_{n}
for all
n\geq 2
.

(3)

\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{fin}}\geq\mathfrak{s}
, where
\mathfrak{s}
donotes the
splitting number
.

(4)

\mathfrak{e}\leq\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})\leq\mathfrak{d}
.

(5)

\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{fin}}\leq\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})
.

(6)

\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathfrak{e}
and
\min\{\mathfrak{e},\mathfrak{b}\}\leq\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{M})
.

Here, given an ideal
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
containing all singletons, let

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|\mid\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\,\bigcup\mathcal{J}\notin\mathcal{I}\};

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|\mid\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\,\bigcup\mathcal{J}=X\};

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|A|\mid\,A\subseteq X,\,A\notin\mathcal{I}\};

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm cof}(\mathcal{I})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathcal{J}|\mid\,\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{I},\ \forall\,A\in\mathcal{I}\ \exists\,B\in\mathcal{J}\,(A\subseteq B)\}.

\mathfrak{d}
denotes, as usual the
dominating number
, that is, the size of the
smallest family of functions
D\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
such that for all
f\in\omega^{\omega}
there is
g\in D
with

g(n)\geq f(n)
for almost all
n
. In future, we shall abbreviate
for almost all
n
by
\forall^{\infty}n
, and write
f\leq^{*}g
for
\forall^{\infty}n\,(f(n)\leq g(n))
. Call a family
of functions

unbounded if for all
f\in\omega^{\omega}
there is
g\in F
which lies infinitely often above
f
. Then

\mathfrak{b}=\min\{F\subseteq\omega^{\omega}\mid\,F\text{\ is unbounded}\}

is the
unbounding number
which is dual to
\mathfrak{d}
. Finally, denote by
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
the
\sigma
-ideals of Lebesgue measure sets and of meager sets of
2^{\omega}
, respectively, and let
\mathcal{E}
be the ideal generated by
F_{\sigma}
measure zero subsets of
2^{\omega}
.

(

)

Drawing inspiration from the property labeled as (

), the objective of this note is to establish a class of partially ordered sets (posets) referred to as
\sigma

(

\mathfrak{e}
. Additionally, we will demonstrate that our notion is connected to the concept of “Fréchet-linked in
\mathds{P}

Notation.
A
forcing notion
is a pair
\langle\mathds{P},\leq\rangle
where
\mathds{P}\neq\emptyset
and
\leq
is a relation on
\mathds{P}
that satisfies reflexivity and
transitivity. We also use the expression pre-ordered set (abbreviated p.o. set or just poset) to refer to a forcing notion. The elements of
\mathds{P}
are called conditions and we say that a condition
q
is
stronger
than a condition
p
if
q\leq p
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathds{P}
be a forcing notion.

(1)

Say that
p,q\in\mathds{P}
are
compatible
(in
\mathds{P}
), denoted by
p\,\|_{\mathds{P}}\,q
, if
\exists r\in\mathds{P}\,(r\leq p\textrm{\ and\ }r\leq q)
. Say that
p,q\in\mathds{P}
are
incompatible
(in
\mathds{P}
) if they are not compatible in
\mathds{P}
, which is
denoted by
p\perp_{\mathds{P}}q
.

When
\mathds{P}
is clear from the context, we just write
p\,\|\,q
and
p\perp q
.

(2)

Say that
A\subseteq\mathds{P}
is an
antichain
if
\forall p,q\in\mathds{P}\,(p\neq q\Rightarrow p\perp q)
.
A
is a
maximal antichain
on
\mathds{P}
iff
A
is an antichain and
\forall p\in\mathds{P}\,\exists q\in A\,(p\,\|_{\mathds{P}}\,q)
.

(3)

Say that
D\subseteq\mathds{P}
is
dense
(in
\mathds{P}
) if
\forall p\in\mathds{P}\,\exists q\in D\,(q\leq p)
.

(4)

Say that
G\subseteq\mathds{P}
is a
\mathds{P}
-filter
if it satisfies

(a)

G\neq\emptyset
;

(b)

for all
p,q\in G
there is some
r\in G
such that
r\leq p
and
r\leq q
; and

(c)

if
p\in\mathds{P}
,
q\in G
and
q\leq p
, then
p\in G
.

(5)

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a family of dense subsets of
\mathds{P}
. Say that
G\subseteq\mathds{P}
is
\mathds{P}
-generic over
V
if
G
is
a
\mathds{P}
-filter and
\forall D\in\mathcal{D}\,(G\cap D\neq\emptyset)
. Denote by
\dot{G}_{\mathds{P}}
the canonical name of the generic set. When
\mathds{P}
is clear from the context, we just
write
\dot{G}
.

(

]
)
.

Let
\mathds{P}
be a forcing notion. Let

.

(1)

p\perp q

.

(2)

.

Define the order

, i.e,

.

We now review some basic notation about relational systems. A
relational system
is a triple
\mathbf{R}=\langle X,Y,R\rangle
where
R
is a relation and
X
and
Y
are non-empty sets. Such a relational system has two cardinal characteristics associated with it:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})

\displaystyle:=\min\{|F|\mid\,F\subseteq X\text{\ and }\neg\exists y\in Y\,\forall x\in F\colon xRy\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})

\displaystyle:=\min\{|D|\mid\,D\subseteq Y\text{\ and }\forall x\in X\,\exists y\in D\colon xRy\}.

Given another relational system
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}=\langle X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},R^{\prime}\rangle
, say that a pair
(\Psi_{-},\Psi_{+})\colon\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
is a
Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
into
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
if

\Psi_{-}\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{+}\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
are functions such that
\forall\,x\in X\ \forall\,y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\colon\Psi_{-}(x)R^{\prime}y^{\prime}
then
xR\Psi_{+}(y^{\prime})
. Say that
\mathbf{R}
is
Tukey below

\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, denoted by
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, if there is a Tukey connection from
\mathbf{R}
to
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
.
Say that
\mathbf{R}
is
Tukey equivalent
to
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, denoted by
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, if
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}
. It is well-known that
\mathbf{R}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\leq\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
. Hence,
\mathbf{R}\cong_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
implies
\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{R})
and
\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R})=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})
.

For instance, the cardinal characteristics associated with an ideal can be characterized by relational systems.

Example 1.4
.

For
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
, define the relational systems:

(1)

\mathcal{I}:=\langle\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I},\subseteq\rangle
, which is a directed preorder when
\mathcal{I}
is closed under unions (e.g. an ideal).

(2)

\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\langle X,\mathcal{I},\in\rangle
.

It is well-known that, whenever
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal on
X
containing

,

(a)

\mathfrak{b}(\mathcal{I})=\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{I})
.

(b)

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{I})=\mbox{\rm cof}(\mathcal{I})
.

(c)

\mathfrak{b}(\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{I}})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{I})
.

(d)

\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{I}})=\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{I})
.

Here, as usual, given a formula
\phi
,
\exists^{\infty}\,n<\omega\colon\phi
means that infinitely many natural numbers satisfy
\phi
.

Lastly, for
x\in\mathds{R}
let
\parallel x\parallel
denote the distance of
x
to the nearest integer.

Evasion number and localization

This section aims to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Bre95
]
)
.

\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})\leq\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}
.

On the other hand, recently, the author, along with Diego Mejía and Ismael Rivera-Madrid
[
CMRM24
]
proved that
\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})
, so we obtain
\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})\leq\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}
. Before entering into the details of the proof, we review some basic notions and notation:

Definition 2.2
.

A set
X\subseteq 2^{\omega}
is termed
\mathcal{N}
-additive
if,
A+X\in\mathcal{N}
for every
A\in\mathcal{N}
. Denote by
\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A}
the collection of the
\mathcal{N}
-additive subsets of
2^{\omega}
. Notice that
\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A}
is a
\sigma
-ideal and
\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.

We now introduce some terminology associated with slaloms.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a sequence of non-empty sets
b=\langle b(n)\mid n\in\omega\rangle
and
h\colon\omega\to\omega
, define

\displaystyle\prod b

\displaystyle:=\prod_{n\in\omega}b(n),

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(b,h)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{n\in\omega}[b(n)]^{\leq h(n)}.

For two functions
x\in\prod b
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(b,h)
and
w\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
, we define

(1)

x\,\in^{*}\varphi\textrm{\ iff\ }\forall^{\infty}n\in\omega\,(x(n)\in\varphi(n))
,

(2)

x\in^{\circ}(\varphi,w)
iff
\forall^{\infty}n\in w\,(x(n)\in\varphi(n))
.

We define the
localization cardinal

\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc}}_{b,h}=\min\{|F|\mid\,F\subseteq\prod b\;\&\;\neg\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(b,h)\,\forall x\in F\,(x\in^{*}\varphi)\},

and a
variant of the localization cardinal

\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc_{0}}}_{b,h}=\min\{|F|\mid\,F\subseteq\prod b\;\&\;\neg\exists(\varphi,w)\in\mathcal{S}(b,h)\times[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}\,\forall x\in F\,(x\in^{\circ}(\varphi,w))\}.

It is not hard to see that
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc}}_{b,h}\leq\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc_{0}}}_{b,h}
holds. Let
\mathrm{minLc}:=\min\{\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc}}_{b,\mathrm{id}_{\omega}}\mid\,b\in\omega^{\omega}\}
where
\mathrm{id}_{\omega}
denotes the identity function on
\omega
.

Pawlikowski provided a characterization of the uniformity of
\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A}
in terms of localization cardinals. Namely, he proved:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Paw85
]
)
.

\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{minLc}
.

On the other hand, it was proved that
\mathrm{minLc}=\min\{\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc}}_{b,h}\mid\,b\in\omega^{\omega}\}
when
h
goes to infinity in
[
CM19
, Lemma 3.8]
. Therefore,
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N}\!\mathcal{A})=\min\{\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{Lc}}_{b,h}\mid\,b\in\omega^{\omega}\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
b,h
be as in

.

(1)

Define

(2)

\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{0}}}_{b,h}:=\mathfrak{d}(\mathbf{Lc}_{0}(b,h))

(3)

Assume

\mathbf{E}_{b}

Remark 2.6
.

Notice that, for a fixed

Proof of
Theorem 2.1
.

In view of

, it suffices to prove
\mathrm{minLc}\leq\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{ubd}}
.
Let
b\in\omega^{\omega}
. Define
c_{b}\in\omega^{\omega}
by

is a partition of

n

has size

. Hence, it is enough to prove that
\mathbf{E}_{b}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Lc}_{0}(c_{b},\mathrm{id}_{\omega})

\Psi_{-}\colon\prod b\to\prod c_{b}

\Psi_{+}\colon\mathcal{S}(c_{b},\mathrm{id}_{\omega})\to\Pi_{b}

.

Let
x\in F
. Define
y_{x}\in\prod c_{b}
by
y_{x}(n)=x{\upharpoonright}I_{n}
for each

. Put
\Psi_{-}(x)=y_{x}
. On the other hand, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(c_{b},\mathrm{id}_{\omega})
and for
w\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
. We can interpret

as a subset of the

-dimensional

. Since

, there is a non-zero functional

Now, define a predictor
\Psi_{+}(\varphi)=\langle\pi_{d}\mid d\in D\rangle

D:=\{d_{n}\mid\,n\in\omega\}\textrm{\ and\ }\pi_{d}(\sigma)=-\frac{1}{d_{n}}\sum_{i\in I_{n}\cap d_{n}}a_{i}^{n}\cdot\sigma(I).

It is not hard to see that

-linked”. Additionally, we will investigate the correlation between

-linked and

3.1.
Soft-linkedness

We now proceed to acquaint our linkedness property.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathds{P}
be a forcing notion and
\theta
be a cardinal number.

(1)

A set
Q\subseteq\mathds{P}
is
leaf-linked
if, for any maximal antichain
\{p_{n}\mid\,n\in\omega\}\subseteq\mathds{P}
, there is some
n\in\omega
such that
\forall p\in Q\,\exists j<n\,(p\,||\,p_{j}).

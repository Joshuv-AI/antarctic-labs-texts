Hardy-Rellich and second order Poincaré identities on the hyperbolic space via Bessel pairs
Elvise BERCHIO, Debdip Ganguly, Prasun Roychowdhury

Abstract
. We prove a family of Hardy-Rellich and Poincaré identities and inequalities on the hyperbolic space having, as particular cases, improved Hardy-Rellich, Rellich and second order Poincaré inequalities. All remainder terms provided considerably improve those already known in literature, and all identities hold with same constants for radial operators also. Furthermore, as applications of the main results, second order versions of the uncertainty principle on the hyperbolic space are derived.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{H}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
denote the most important example of Cartan-Hadamard manifold, namely the hyperbolic space and let
\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
denote the bottom of the spectrum of
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
which is explicitly given by

(1.1)

\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=\inf_{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}}{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}}=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}\,.

The present paper takes its origin from the following family of Hardy-Poincaré inequalities recently proved in
[
5
]
: for all
N-2\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
and all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)\,h_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,u^{2}\emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,

where
\gamma_{N}(\lambda):=\sqrt{(N-1)^{2}-4\lambda}
,
h_{N}(\lambda):=\frac{\gamma_{N}(\lambda)+1}{2}
and
r:={\rm d}(x,x_{0})
is the geodesic distance from a fixed pole
x_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
. We notice that the function
\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}
is positive while the map
[N-2,\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})]\ni\lambda\mapsto h_{N}(\lambda)
is decreasing. Furthermore, for
N\geq 3
, there holds
\frac{1}{4}\leq h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
and, for all
\lambda
, one locally recovers the optimal Hardy weight:
\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{r^{2}}
. Besides, denoted with
V_{\lambda}
the positive potential at the r.h.s. of (
1.2
), the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}-V_{\lambda}(r)
is
critical
in
\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\}
in the sense that the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}Vu^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
is not valid for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
if
V\gneqq V_{\lambda}
.

The interest of (
1.2
) relies on the fact that it provides in a single inequality, proved by means of a unified approach, an optimal improvement (in the sense of adding nonnegative terms in the right side of the inequality) of the Poincaré inequality (
1.1
) and an optimal improvement of the Hardy inequality. Indeed, for
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(
\gamma_{N}=0
) inequality (
1.2
) becomes the improved Poincaré inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ {\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\ {\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

(1.3)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\ {\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{(N-1)(N-3)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,,

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
with
N\geq 2
. Instead, for
\lambda=N-2
(
\gamma_{N}=N-3
) (
1.2
) becomes the improved Hardy inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

(1.4)

\displaystyle+(N-2)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{(N-2)(N-3)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,u^{2}\ \emph{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,,

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
with
N\geq 3
. As concerns inequality (
1
), we recall that it has been shown first in
[
1
]
and then, with different methods, adapted to larger classes of manifolds in
[
4
]
where criticality has also been shown. Very recently, a further development has been done in
[
16
]
where, by using the notion of Bessel pairs, it has been proved that a further positive term of the form
\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r}{\sinh^{N-1}r}\big|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\big(u\frac{\sinh^{\frac{N-1}{2}}r}{r}\big)\big|^{2}dv_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
can be added at the r.h.s. of (
1
) so that the inequality becomes an equality. Clearly, this is not in contrast with the criticality proved in
[
4
]
since the added term is not of the form
Vu^{2}
. We refer the interested reader to
[
2
]
for the
L^{p}
version of (
1
), and to
[
7
]
for remainder terms of (
1.1
) involving the Green’s function of the Laplacian.

Regarding (
1
), it’s worth recalling that generalizations to Riemannian manifolds of the classical euclidean Hardy inequality have been intensively pursued after the seminal work of Carron
[
9
]
. In particular, on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds the optimal constant is known to be
\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
and improvements of the Hardy inequality have been given e.g., in
[
11
,
16
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
31
]
. This is in contrast to what happens in the Euclidean setting where the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}-\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}
is known to be
critical
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
(see
[
12
]
). In particular, in inequality (
1
) the effect of the curvature allows to provide a remainder term of
L^{2}
-type, therefore of the same kind of that given in the seminal paper by Brezis-Vazquez
[
8
]
for the Hardy inequality on euclidean
bounded
domains.

The above mentioned results make it natural to investigate the existence of a family of inequalities extending (
1.2
) to the second order, that is an inequality including either improvement of the second order Poincaré inequalities:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u)^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\bigg(\frac{N-1}{2}\bigg)^{2(2-l)}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{l}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\quad(l=0\text{ or }l=1)

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(
N\geq 2
), and improvement of the second order Hardy inequalities:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u)^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\bigg(\frac{N-4}{2}\bigg)^{2(1-l)}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}^{l}u|^{2}}{r^{4-2l}}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\quad(l=0\text{ or }l=1)

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(
N\geq 5
), i.e. the Rellich inequality which comes for
l=0
and the Hardy-Rellich inequality for
l=1
. We recall that inequalities (
1.5
) are known from
[
27
]
and
[
30
]
with optimal constants, while improvements have been provided in
[
3
]
-
[
4
]
and, for radial operators, in
[
6
]
-
[
29
]
. Instead, inequalities (
1.6
) were firstly studied in
[
19
]
and in
[
31
]
, where the optimality of the constants was proved together with the existence of some remainder terms. More recently, a stronger version of (
1.6
), only involving radial operators and still holding with same constants, has been obtained in
[
28
]
. See also
[
22
]
for improved versions of (
1.6
) in the general framework of Finsler-Hadamard manifolds.

In the present paper we complete the picture of results in
\mathbb{H}^{N}
by proving a family of inequalities including either an improved version of (
1.5
) and an improved version of (
1.6
) when
l=1
, therefore extending (
1.2
) to the second order, see Theorem
2.2
below. Furthermore, in Theorem
2.1
, we show that the obtained family of inequalities reads as a family of
identities
for radial operators (also for non radial functions) giving a more precise understanding of the remainder terms provided. A fine exploitation of these results also allows to obtain improved versions of (
1.5
) and of (
1.6
) for
l=0
in such a way to exhaust the second order scenario, see Corollaries
2.3
and
2.4
. As far we are aware, all the improvements provided have a stronger positive impact, on the r.h.s. of (
1.5
) and of (
1.6
), than those already known in literature, see Remark
2.2
in the following.

We notice that (
1.2
) was proved by means of a unified approach based on criticality theory, well established for
second
order operators only (see
[
12
]
), together with the exploitation of a family of explicit radial solutions to the associated equations. Therefore, a similar approach seems not applicable in the higher order case. Here, drawing primary motivation from the seminal paper
[
17
]
, we extend (
1.2
) to the second order by using the notion of
Bessel pair
. This notion has been very recently developed in
[
16
]
on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds to establish several interesting Hardy identities and inequalities which, in particular, generalise many well-known Hardy inequalities on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. By combining some ideas from
[
16
]
-
[
17
]
, and through delicate computations with spherical harmonics, in the present article we develop the method of Bessel pairs to derive general abstract Rellich inequalities and identities on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
that we employ to prove our main results, i.e., Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
. In this way, we get either Poincaré and Hardy-Rellich identities, and improved inequalities, by means of a unified proof where the key ingredient is the clever construction of a family of Bessel pairs, see (
4
) in the following. Finally, as applications of the obtained inequalities, we derive quantitative versions of the second order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle, see Section
2.3
. As far as we know, the results provided represent the first examples of second order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle in the Hyperbolic context.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we introduce some of the notations and we state our main results, i.e. Poincaré and Hardy-Rellich identities and related improved inequalities; furthermore, in this section, we also state second order versions of the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle. In Section
3
we provide abstract Rellich identities and inequalities via Bessel pairs together with a related Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle. Section
4
is devoted to the proofs of the results stated in Section
2
by exploiting the results stated in Section
3
, while Section
5
contains the proofs of the results stated in Section
3
. Finally, in the Appendix we present a family of improved Hardy-Poincaré identities which follows as a corollary from

, see Lemma

) and include
[
16

as a particular case.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Notations

From now onward, if nothing is specified, we will always assume
N\geq 2
.
It is well known that the
N
-dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{N}
admits a polar coordinate decomposition structure. Namely, for
x\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
we can write
x=(r,\Theta)=(r,\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N-1})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, where
r
denotes the geodesic distance between the point
x
and a fixed pole
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{H}^{N}
and
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
is the unit sphere in the
N
-dimensional euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Recall that the Riemannian Laplacian of a scalar function
u
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is given by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(r,\Theta)=\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[(\sinh r)^{N-1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(r,\Theta)\right]\\
+\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}r}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u(r,\Theta),

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
is the Riemannian Laplacian on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. In particular,
the radial contribution of the Riemannian Laplacian, namely the operator involving only on
r
,
\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u
, reads as

\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u=\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[(\sinh r)^{N-1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right]=u^{\prime\prime}+(N-1)\coth r\,u^{\prime},

where from now on a prime will denote, for radial functions, derivative w.r.t .
r
. Also, let us recall the Gradient in terms of the polar coordinate decomposition is given by

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(r,\Theta)=\bigg(\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(r,\Theta),\frac{1}{\sinh r}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u(r,\Theta)\bigg),

where
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
denotes the Gradient on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. Again, the radial contribution of the Gradient,
\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u
, is defined as

\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u=\bigg(\frac{\partial u}{\partial r},0\bigg).

2.2.
Hardy-Rellich and Poincaré identities and improved inequalities

Our main result for radial operators reads as follows

Theorem 2.1
.

For all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}
and all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)\,h_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u_{r}}{\Psi_{\lambda}(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

where
\gamma_{N}(\lambda):=\sqrt{(N-1)^{2}-4\lambda}
,
h_{N}(\lambda):=\frac{\gamma_{N}(\lambda)+1}{2}
and
\Psi_{\lambda}(r):=r^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\sinh r}{r}\right)^{-\frac{N-1+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)}{2}}
. Furthermore, for
N\geq 5
and
\lambda
given, the constants
h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)
and
\left[\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]
are jointly sharp in the sense that, fixed
h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)
, the inequality does not hold if we replace
\left[\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]
with a larger constant.

Remark 2.1
.

We remark that the the function
\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}
is positive, strictly decreasing and satisfies

\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{3}\ \mbox{ as }\ r\rightarrow 0^{+}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{r}\ \mbox{ as }\ r\rightarrow+\infty\,.

Furthermore, the map
[0,\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})]\ni\lambda\mapsto h_{N}(\lambda)
is decreasing and
\frac{1}{4}\leq h_{N}(\lambda)\leq\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}
.

Furthemore, for non radial operators we obtain the second order analogous to (
1.2
):

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 5.
For all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}
and all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)\,h_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u_{r}}{\Psi_{\lambda}(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

where
\gamma_{N}(\lambda),h_{N}(\lambda)
and
\Psi_{\lambda}(r)
are as given in Theorem
2.1
. Furthermore, for any given
\lambda
, the constants
h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)
and
\left[\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right]
are jointly sharp in the sense explained in Theorem
2.1
.

We notice that the dimension restriction
N\geq 5
in Theorem
2.2
comes from assumption (
3.4
) in Theorem
3.2
below where we state our abstract Rellich inequalities, see also Remark
3.1
for some comments about this assumption that naturally comes when passing from the radial to the non radial framework. Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
yield a number of improved Poincaré and Hardy-Rellich inequalities that we state here below; a comparison with previous results is provided in Remark
2.2
. More precisely, for
\lambda=0
we readily got the following improved Hardy-Rellich identity and inequality:

Corollary 2.1
.

For all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r^{N}}{(\sinh r)^{2(N-1)}}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}\,u_{r}}{r^{\frac{N}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,.

Moreover, if
N\geq 5
, for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r^{N}}{(\sinh r)^{2(N-1)}}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}\,u_{r}}{r^{\frac{N}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,,

and the constant
\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}
appearing in the L.H.S of both equations is the sharp constant.

For
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
we got an improvement of the second order Poincaré identity (
1.5
) with
l=0
, and the related inequality:

Corollary 2.2
.

For all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r}{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}u_{r}}{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

Moreover, if
N\geq 5
, for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\,\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r}{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}u_{r}}{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

The constant
\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}
appearing in the L.H.S of both equations is the sharp constant. Moreover, for
N\geq 5,
the constants
\frac{1}{4}
and
\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}
are jointly sharp in the sense explained in Theorem
2.1
.

By combining Corollary
2.1
with
[
16
, Corollary 3.2]
we also get an improved Rellich inequality:

Corollary 2.3
.

For all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\bigg(\frac{N-4}{2}\bigg)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{4}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{2}(N-4)(N-1)}{8}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{4}}\,u^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{1}{r^{N-2}}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(r^{\frac{N-4}{2}}u\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r^{N}}{(\sinh r)^{2(N-1)}}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}\,u_{r}}{r^{\frac{N}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,.

Moreover, if
N\geq 5,
for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,\emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\bigg(\frac{N-4}{2}\bigg)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{4}}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{2}(N-4)(N-1)}{8}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{4}}\,u^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\ \emph{d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{1}{r^{N-2}}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(r^{\frac{N-4}{2}}u\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r^{N}}{(\sinh r)^{2(N-1)}}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{N-1}\,u_{r}}{r^{\frac{N}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,,

and the constant
\frac{N^{2}}{4}\,\left(\frac{N-4}{2}\right)^{2}
appearing in the L.H.S of both equations is the sharp constant.

Instead, by combining Corollary
2.2
with
[
16
, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 3.2]
, we improve (
1.5
) with
l=0
, i.e. we complete the second order scenario about Poincaré identities and inequalities :

Corollary 2.4
.

For all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{N-1}{4}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{(N-1)^{3}(N-3)}{16}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}+1\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r}{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}u_{r}}{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

Moreover, if
N\geq 5
, for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{N-1}{4}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{(N-1)^{3}(N-3)}{16}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}+1\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r}{(\sinh r)^{N-1}}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{(\sinh r)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}u_{r}}{r^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

The constant
\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{4}
appearing in the L.H.S of both equations is the sharp constant. Moreover, for

, the constants
\frac{1}{4}
and
\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}
in both equations are jointly sharp in the sense explained in Theorem

.

Remark 2.2
.

As far as we are aware, improved second order Poincaré and Hardy-Rellich

in

were not known in literature; besides, the above inequalities yield improvements of Poincaré and Hardy-Rellich inequalities which are considerably stronger than those already known in literature. As concerns the Hardy-Rellich and Rellich inequalities, improved versions were already known from

]
,

, therefore it decays more rapidly, either as

2.3.
Second order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle

Another remarkable consequence of Theorem

is the following quantitative version of HPW principle in

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

For all

and all

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\left(|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}-\lambda|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\right){\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}r^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)^{2}

where
h_{N}(\lambda)
is as defined as in Theorem
2.1
. In particular, for
\lambda=0,
we obtain

(2.2)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}r^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)^{2}\,,

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
.

In the Euclidean context the second order Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle has been only recently studied in

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\left(|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}-\lambda|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\right){\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}r^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}r^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)^{2}

3.
Abstract Rellich identities and inequalities via Bessel pairs

Ghoussoub-Moradifam in
[
17
]
provided a very general framework to obtain various Hardy-type
inequalities and their improvements on the Euclidean space (or bounded domain). It was based on the notion of Bessel pairs that we recall in the following

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a pair
(V,W)
of
C^{1}
-functions is a Bessel pair on
(0,R)
for some
0<R\leq\infty
if the ordinary differential equation:

(Vy^{\prime})^{\prime}+Wy=0

admits a positive solutions
f
on the interval
(0,R)
.

In
[
17
]
the authors proved the following inequality for some positive constant
C>0:

(3.1)

\int_{B}V(x)|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq C\int_{B}W(x)\,|u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B),

subject to the constraints that the functions
V
and
W
are positive radial functions defined on the euclidean ball
B
and such that:
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
is a Bessel pairs
\int_{0}^{R}\frac{1}{r^{N-1}V(r)}\,{\rm d}r=\infty
and
\int_{0}^{R}r^{N-1}V(r)\,{\rm d}r<\infty
where
0<R\leq\infty
is the radius of the ball
B
.

In view of (
3.1
), with particular choices of
(V,W)
, the results in
[
17
]
simplified and improved several known results concerning Hardy inequalities
and theirs improvements. Recently, the notion of Bessel pairs has been exploited: in
[
23
]
to establish improved Hardy inequalities
involving general distance functions, in
[
25
]
to sharpen several
Hardy type inequalities on upper half spaces, and in
[
24
]
to prove Hardy inequalities on Homogeneous
groups.

Regarding Cartan-Hadamard manifolds, the notion of Bessel pairs has been very recently exploited to obtain improved Hardy inequalities in
[
16
]
; to our future purposes, we recall their Theorem 3.2 on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

[
16
, Theorem 3.2]

Let
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
be a Bessel pair on
(0,R)
with positive solution
f
on
(0,R)
. Then for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}V(r)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}W(r)|u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\int_{B_{R}}V(r)(f(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u}{f(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle-(N-1)\int_{B_{R}}V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)u^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}V(r)|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}W(r)|u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\int_{B_{R}}V(r)(f(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u}{f(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle-(N-1)\int_{B_{R}}V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{f(r)}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)u^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}.

In view of Lemma
3.1
a natural question is to study higher order Hardy type inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{N}
using the notion of Bessel pairs. In the Euclidean space (or in bounded euclidean domains) these questions were studied in
[
17
]
. One of their results read as follows: let
0<R\leq\infty,V\text{ and }W
be positive
C^{1}
-functions on
B_{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
forms a Bessel pair.
Then for all radial functions
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
there holds

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}V(x)|\Delta u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{B}W(x)\,|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+(N-1)\int_{B_{R}}\bigg(\frac{V(x)}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{V_{r}(x)}{|x|}\bigg)|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

where
r=|x|
. In addition, if
W(x)-2\frac{V(x)}{|x|^{2}}+2\frac{V_{r}(x)}{|x|}-V_{rr}(x)\geq 0
on
(0,R)
, then the above is true for non radial function as well (we refer
[
17
, Theorem 3.1-3.3]
for more insight).
We also refer to
[
14
,
15
,
26
]
for recent results on Hardy-Rellich inequalities and their improvements on the Euclidean space using the approach of Bessel pairs.

In the present article, we extend (
3.2
) to
\mathbb{H}^{N}
by showing first the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
be a Bessel pair on
(0,R)
with positive solution
f
on
(0,R)
. Then for all radial function
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}V(r)|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}W(r)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+(N-1)\int_{B_{R}}\bigg(\frac{V(r)}{\sinh^{2}r}-\frac{V_{r}(r)\cosh r}{\sinh r}\bigg)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle-(N-1)\int_{B_{R}}V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}}V(r)(f(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u_{r}}{f(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,.

As a direct consequence of the above result, we tackle the non-radial scenario by spherical harmonic method and we prove:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
be a Bessel pair on
(0,\infty)
with positive solution
f
on
(0,\infty)
. Then for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}W(r)|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+(N-1)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{V(r)}{\sinh^{2}r}-\frac{V_{r}(r)\cosh r}{\sinh r}\bigg)|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle-(N-1)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)(f(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u_{r}}{f(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

Now it is natural to ask whether there is a counterpart of Theorem
3.1
for any function, not necessarily radial. We give an affirmative answer in below provided that
V
satisfies the extra condition (
3.4
) below:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
be a Bessel pair on
(0,\infty)
with positive solution
f
on
(0,\infty)
. Also assume
N\geq 5
and
V
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle(N-5)\frac{V(r)}{\sinh^{2}r}+3\frac{V_{r}(r)\cosh r}{\sinh r}-V_{rr}(r)+(N-4)V(r)\geq 0.

Then for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}W(r)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+(N-1)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{V(r)}{\sinh^{2}r}-\frac{V_{r}(r)\cosh r}{\sinh r}\bigg)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle-(N-1)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)(f(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u_{r}}{f(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,.

Remark 3.1
.

We remark that assumption (
3.4
) in Theorem
3.2
is not too restrictive to our purposes: we shall provide a remarkable family of
(V,W)
for which the assumption holds true in the proof of Theorem
2.1
. On the other hand, an analogous assumption was even required in the Euclidean space as well, see (
3.2
) and the comments just below; this seems the natural prize to pay in order to pass to the higher order case.

We conclude the section by stating an abstract version of Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle involving Bessel pairs which follows as a corollary from Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.1
:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(r^{N-1}V,r^{N-1}W)
be a Bessel pair on
(0,\infty)
with positive solution
f
on
(0,\infty)
and set

\tilde{W}(r):=W(r)+(N-1)\bigg(\frac{V(r)}{\sinh^{2}r}-\frac{V_{r}(r)\cosh r}{\sinh r}\bigg)-(N-1)V(r)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg).

Furthermore, let
N\geq 5
and assume that
V
satisfies
3.4
and that
\tilde{W}(r)>0

. Then for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\tilde{W}(r)}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\geq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)^{2}

and

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\tilde{W}(r)}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\geq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)^{2}.

We want to mention that for the second inequality we do not require condition
3.4
, whereas the other conditions and Corollary
3.1
are enough.

Remark 3.2
.

A non trivial example of pairs satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
is given by the family of Bessel pairs
(r^{N-1},r^{N-1}W_{\lambda})
, for all

. Indeed, they satisfy condition (

) and, in this case, the function
\tilde{W}

\tilde{W}_{\lambda}(r)=\lambda+h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\left(\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}-h_{N}^{2}(\lambda)\right)\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}r}+\frac{\gamma_{N}(\lambda)\,h_{N}(\lambda)}{r}\bigg(\coth r-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)

which is positive in
(0,+\infty)
for all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
. In particular, with this pair, taking
\lambda=0
for simplicity, Theorem
3.3

Spherical harmonics.

Before going to prove Corollary
3.1
and Theorem
3.2

Let
u(x)=u(r,\Theta)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
,

and
\Theta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we can write

(5.2)

in

, where
\{P_{n}\}
is an orthonormal system of spherical harmonics and

a_{n}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}u(r,\Theta)P_{n}(\Theta)\ {\rm d}\Theta\,.

A spherical harmonic
P_{n}
of order
n
is the restriction to
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
of a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree
n.
Moreover, it
satisfies

-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}P_{n}=\lambda_{n}P_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, where
\lambda_{n}=(n^{2}+(N-2)n)
are the eigenvalues of Laplace Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
with corresponding eigenspace dimension
c_{n}
. We note that
\lambda_{n}\geq N-1
for
n\geq 1
,
\lambda_{0}=0
,
c_{0}=1
,
c_{1}=N
and for
n\geq 2

c_{n}=\binom{N+n-1}{n}-\binom{N+n-3}{n-2}.

In a continuation let us also describe the Gradient and Laplace Beltrami operator in this setting. Now onward, to shorten the notations, we will always assume
\psi(r)=\sinh r.
They will look like as follows:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}^{\prime}}^{2}P_{n}^{2}+\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{\psi^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}P_{n}|^{2}

and

(5.3)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}a_{n}^{\prime}\right)^{2}P_{n}^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{\psi^{4}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}P_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}a_{n}^{\prime}\right)\frac{a_{n}}{\psi^{2}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}P_{n})P_{n}.

\displaystyle|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a_{n}^{\prime}}^{2}P_{n}^{2}

and

\displaystyle(\Delta_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{\psi}a_{n}^{\prime}\right)^{2}P_{n}^{2}\,.

Proof of Theorem

.

The idea of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.3
. First, exploiting the given conditions into Theorem
3.2
, we deduce that for all

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\tilde{W}(r)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,.

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}}{\tilde{W}(r)}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}V(r)|\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg)\,.

The proof of Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle involving radial part of the operator is in a similar line.

Appendix: a family of improved Hardy-Poincaré equalities

In this appendix we present a family of improved Hardy-Poincaré equalities which follows as a corollary from
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
, i.e. Lemma
3.1
above, by exploiting the family of Bessel pairs
(r^{N-1},r^{N-1}W_{\lambda})
introduced in Section
4
for all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
. If
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
the identity we got is already known from
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
while for
0\leq\lambda<\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
it is new and improves (
1.2
), i.e.
[

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
N\geq 2.
For all
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)^{2}
and for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{x_{0}\})
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+h^{2}_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}-h^{2}_{N}(\lambda)\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)h_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,u^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\Psi_{\lambda}(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

and for the radial operator we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}u|^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+h^{2}_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}-h^{2}_{N}(\lambda)\right]\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)h_{N}(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\frac{r\coth r-1}{r^{2}}\,u^{2}\,{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}(\Psi_{\lambda}(r))^{2}\bigg|\nabla_{r,\mathbb{H}^{N}}\bigg(\frac{u}{\Psi_{\lambda}(r)}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\>{\rm d}v_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\,,

where
\gamma_{N}(\lambda):=\sqrt{(N-1)^{2}-4\lambda}
,
h_{N}(\lambda):=\frac{\gamma_{N}(\lambda)+1}{2}
and
\Psi_{\lambda}(r):=r^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(\frac{\sinh r}{r}\right)^{-\frac{N-1+\gamma_{N}(\lambda)}{2}}

Proof.

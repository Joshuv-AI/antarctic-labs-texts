Dual Dictionaries in Linear Programming
Patrick T. Perkins, Xiang Gao

Abstract
. In order to use the Dual Simplex Method, one needs to prove a certain bijection between the dictionaries associated with the primal problem and those associated with its dual. We give a short conceptual proof of why this bijection exists.



1.
Introduction

Chvátal
[
1
]
introduces the notion of a
dictionary
associated to a Linear Programming problem (LP). In order to use the Dual Simplex Method, one needs to prove a certain bijection between the dictionaries associated with the primal problem and those associated with its dual. Chvátal leaves the proof as an exercise, involving a long computation. Vanderbei
[
2
]
gives a short and elegant proof. Our contribution is a short proof that, we feel, gives a clear conceptual reason for why this beautiful bijection exists.

First, we set up some notation we will use throughout the paper. Consider a general LP problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{max}}

\displaystyle z=\mathbf{c}^{T}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle A_{0}\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\geq 0

The dual problem is

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{max}}

\displaystyle-w=-\mathbf{b}^{T}\mathbf{y}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle-A_{0}^{T}\mathbf{y}\leq-\mathbf{c}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}\geq 0

Here
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
A_{0}
is an
m\times n
matrix.
But we immediately introduce slack variables and, for the rest of the paper, take
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
. Write
A=[\,A_{0}\,I\,]
for the larger matrix with an
m\times m
identity matrix appended to
A_{0}
. As usual,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are the decision variables for the primal problem and
x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{m+n}
are its slack variables. But, following Chvátal, we use
y_{n+1},\ldots,y_{n+m}
as the decision variables for the dual problem and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
for its slacks. This makes the bijection easier to see.

Example 1
.

If the initial dictionary for a primal problem is

\displaystyle x_{4}

\displaystyle=18-4x_{1}-2x_{2}+2x_{3}

\displaystyle x_{5}

\displaystyle=-3+x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=8x_{1}+11x_{2}-10x_{3}

then the initial dictionary for the dual problem is

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=-8+4y_{4}-y_{5}

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle=-11+2y_{4}-y_{5}

\displaystyle y_{3}

\displaystyle=10-2y_{4}-2y_{5}

\displaystyle-w

\displaystyle=-18y_{4}+3y_{5}

Pivoting once in the primal, letting
x_{1}
enter the basis and
x_{5}
leave, gives

\displaystyle x_{4}

\displaystyle=6-4x_{5}+2x_{2}+10x_{3}

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=3+x_{5}-x_{2}-2x_{3}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=24+8x_{5}+3x_{2}-26x_{3}

The corresponding pivot in the dual lets
y_{5}
enter and
y_{1}
leave.

\displaystyle y_{5}

\displaystyle=-8+4y_{4}-y_{1}

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle=-3-2y_{4}+y_{1}

\displaystyle y_{3}

\displaystyle=26-10y_{4}+2y_{1}

\displaystyle-w

\displaystyle=-24-6y_{4}-3y_{1}

Note that each dictionary for the dual LP is, in some sense, the negative transpose of the corresponding dictionary for the primal.

To be more precise, let
B\cup N
be an ordered partition of
\{1,2,...,m+n\}
such that
|B|=m
and the columns of
A=[A_{0}\,I\,]
indexed by
B
are linearly independant. Let
\mathbf{x}_{B}
be the vector of variables indexed by
B
, and similarly for
\mathbf{x}_{N}
. Then the dictionary of the primal LP associated to this partition is of the form

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{B}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{p}-Q\,\mathbf{x}_{N}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=z^{*}+\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{N}

where
Q
is an
m\times n
matrix,
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Given this set up, we will prove that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{N}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{q}+Q^{T}\mathbf{y}_{B}

\displaystyle-w

\displaystyle=-z^{*}-\mathbf{p}^{T}\mathbf{y}_{B}

is a dictionary for the dual LP. This means that every solution to (1.4) is a solution to the initial dual dictionary, and vice versa.

2.
Orthogonal Subspaces

We first recast our pair of LPs in terms of orthogonal subspaces. This formulation is well known, we first encountered it in Todd
[
3
]
. Add two new variables,
x_{0}
and
x_{m+n+1}
, and set
\overline{\bf{x}}=[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots x_{m+n},x_{m+n+1}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+2}
. Define the matrix
R
by

R=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&A_{0}&I&-\mathbf{b}\\
1&-\mathbf{c}^{T}&{\bf 0}&0\end{bmatrix}

Then the primal LP can be formulated

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{max}}

\displaystyle x_{0}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots x_{m+n}\geq 0

\displaystyle x_{m+n+1}=1

\displaystyle\overline{\bf x}\in\ker(R)

Now consider the row space of
R
. Let
[\mathbf{u}^{T},u_{0}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. Every vector in the row space is of the form

(2.2)

[\mathbf{u}^{T},u_{0}]\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&A_{0}&I&-\mathbf{b}\\
1&-\mathbf{c}^{T}&{\bf 0}&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}&\mathbf{u}^{T}A_{0}-u_{0}\mathbf{c}^{T}&\mathbf{u}^{T}&-\mathbf{u}^{T}\mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}

Let
\overline{\bf{y}}=[y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m+n},y_{m+n+1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n+2}
. Then we can reformulate the dual LP as

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\text{max}}

\displaystyle y_{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle y_{1},\ldots y_{m+n}\geq 0

\displaystyle y_{0}=1

\displaystyle\overline{\bf{y}}\in{\rm row\ space}(R)

Thus
\mathbb{R}^{m+n+2}
splits into two orthogonal subspaces, one associated with the primal LP and one with the dual. Note that this naturally makes
y_{n},\ldots,y_{m+n}
the decision variables for the dual LP.

3.
The Proof

Let
{\bf y}=[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m+n}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
. Then
[{\bf y},w]^{T}
is a solution to (1.4) if and only if

[1,{\bf y}_{N}^{T},{\bf y}_{B}^{T},w]\in{\rm row\ space}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&Q&I&-\mathbf{p}\\
1&-\mathbf{q}^{T}&{\bf 0}&-z^{*}\end{bmatrix}\right)

Now we rewrite (1.3) using the notation from
[
1
]
page 100. Extend
{\bf c}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
by adding
m
zeroes at the end. Let
A_{B}
be the submatrix of
A=[A_{0}\,I\,]
with columns indexed by
B
, and similarly for
A_{N}
. Then
A_{B}
is non-singular and (1.3) is of the form

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{B}

\displaystyle=A^{-1}_{B}\mathbf{b}-A^{-1}_{B}A_{N}\mathbf{x}_{N}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A^{-1}_{B}\mathbf{b}+(\mathbf{c}_{N}^{T}-\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A^{-1}_{B}A_{N})\mathbf{x}_{N}

It follows that
[{\bf y},w]
is a solution to (1.4) if an only if

\displaystyle\in{\rm row\ space}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&A_{B}^{-1}A_{N}&I&-A_{B}^{-1}\mathbf{b}\\
1&\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A_{B}^{-1}A_{N}-\mathbf{c}_{N}^{T}&{\bf 0}&-\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A_{B}^{-1}\mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm row\ space}\left(\begin{bmatrix}A_{B}^{-1}&{\bf 0}^{T}\\
\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A_{B}^{-1}&1\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&A_{N}&A_{B}&-\mathbf{b}\\
1&-\mathbf{c}_{N}^{T}&-\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm row\ space}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\bf 0}^{T}&A_{N}&A_{B}&-\mathbf{b}\\
1&-\mathbf{c}_{N}^{T}&-\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}&0\end{bmatrix}\right)

because

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}A_{B}^{-1}&{\bf 0}^{T}\\
\mathbf{c}_{B}^{T}A_{B}^{-1}&1\end{bmatrix}

is nonsingular.

But this is equivalent to
[1,{\bf y}^{T},w]\in{\rm row\ space}(R)
, which is what we wished to prove.

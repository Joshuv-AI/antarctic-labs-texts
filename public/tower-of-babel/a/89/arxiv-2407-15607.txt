Abstract
. Given a Grothendieck opfibration p : 𝒯 → ℬ p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B} , we describe a method to construct a Waldhausen category structure on the total category 𝒯 \mathcal{T} via combining Waldhausen category structures on the fibers 𝒯 A \mathcal{T}_{A} for A ∈ 𝖮𝖻 ⁡ ( ℬ ) A\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{B}) and the basis category ℬ \mathcal{B} . As an application, we show that if 𝖤 \mathsf{E} is a Waldhausen category with small coproducts such that the class of cofibrations is the left part of a weak factorization system in 𝖤 \mathsf{E} , then the representation category 𝖱𝖾𝗉 ⁡ ( Q , 𝖼𝗈𝖤 ) {\sf Rep}(Q,\mathsf{coE}) of a left rooted quiver Q Q is a Waldhausen category, where 𝖼𝗈𝖤 \mathsf{coE} is the subcategory of 𝖤 \mathsf{E} whose morphisms are cofibrations.

1.
Introduction

Given a pseudo functor
\mathscr{F}
from a category
\mathcal{B}
to the (meta) 2-category
\mathsf{Cat}
of categories, the lax colimit of
\mathscr{F}
is represented by its
Grothendieck construction

\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathscr{F}
. The association of
\mathscr{F}
to the canonical projection

p:\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathscr{F}\to\mathcal{B}
exhibits one of the most fundamental relations in category theory, namely, the following equivalence between pseudo functors from
\mathcal{B}
to
\mathsf{Cat}
and Grothendieck opfibrations over
\mathcal{B}
:

(
1
.1)

\begin{gathered}\int:\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{ps}}(\mathcal{B},\mathsf{Cat})\to\mathsf{opFib}(\mathcal{B}),\end{gathered}

which is now known as
Grothendieck correspondence

[
7
]
.

A natural question one may consider is to establish similar correspondences for categories with extra structure. This question has been extensively studied in the literature, see for instances
[
16
,
1
,
15
]
for monoidal categories, enriched categories and tangent categories. For model categories, Harpaz and Prasma
[
8
]
showed that a covariant pseudo functor satisfying certain conditions from a model category
\mathcal{M}
to
\mathsf{ModCat}
, the category of model categories and Quillen adjunctions, induces the integral model structure on its Grothendieck construction, and established an equivalence between these pseudo functors and model bifibrations over
\mathcal{M}
. Inspired by
[
8
]
as well as Roig
[
19
]
and Stanculescu
[
21
]
, Cagne and Melliès identified in
[
3
]
necessary and sufficient conditions on a Grothendieck bifibration
p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}
to ensure that model structures on the fibers
\mathcal{T}_{A}
for
A\in\mathsf{Ob}(\mathcal{B})
and the basis category
\mathcal{B}
combine into a model structure on the total category
\mathcal{T}
. Furthermore, via this Grothendieck construction, they revisited the traditional definition of Reedy model structures and exhibited their bifibrational nature.

Waldhausen categories
were introduced in
[
22
]
to extend higher algebraic
K
-theory brought by Quillen
[
17
]
. Note that Waldhausen categories only involve cofibrations and weak equivalences, and hence in general are different from model categories. Thus one may ask the following question:

1.1 Question.

Given a pseudo functor
\mathscr{F}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathsf{WaldCat}
, where
\mathcal{B}
is a Waldhausen category and
\mathsf{WaldCat}
is the category of Waldhausen categories and exact functors, how to endow a Waldhausen category structure on its Grothendieck construction
\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathscr{F}
?

Let
\mathsf{Wald.opFib}(\mathcal{B})
be the subcategory of
\mathsf{opFib}(\mathcal{B})
consisting of Grothendieck opfibrations
p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}
satisfying the following conditions:

•

the fiber
\mathcal{T}_{A}
is a Waldhausen category
for each object
A
in
\mathcal{B}
;

•

the reindexing functor
u_{!}:\mathcal{T}_{A}\to\mathcal{T}_{B}
is exact
for each morphism
u
in
\mathcal{B}
.

One may refer to objects in
\mathsf{Wald.opFib}(\mathcal{B})
as
Waldhausen opfibrations
. It is clear that (
.1
) restricts to an equivalence

\int_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{ps}}(\mathcal{B},\mathsf{WaldCat})\to\mathsf{Wald.opFib}(\mathcal{B}),

where
\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{ps}}(\mathcal{B},\mathsf{WaldCat})
is the subcategory of
\mathsf{Fun}^{\mathsf{ps}}(\mathcal{B},\mathsf{Cat})
spanned by pseudo functors whose domain lies in
\mathsf{WaldCat}
. This equivalence allows us to answer the above question from the point of view of Waldhausen opfibrations, which is also beneficial to giving further applications in representation categories of left rooted quivers.

We would like to show that for a Waldhausen opfibration
p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}
with
\mathcal{B}
a Waldhausen category, the total category
\mathcal{T}
inherits a Waldhausen category structure from the ones assigned to the basis category
\mathcal{B}
and to the fibers
\mathcal{T}_{A}
for each object
A
in
\mathcal{B}
. Note that any morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
that is above a morphism

u:A\to B
in
\mathcal{B}
can be factored uniquely as a cocartesian lifting of
X
along
u
followed by a morphism
f_{\triangleright}
in the fiber
\mathcal{T}_{B}
by the cocartesian universal property of the lifting (see
.1
). One can always equip
\mathcal{T}
with two classes of total cofibrations and total weak equivalences described as:

•

A
total cofibration
(resp.,
total weak equivalence
) is a morphism

f:X\to Y
in
\mathcal{T}
that is above a cofibration (resp.,
weak equivalence
)

u:A\to B
in
\mathcal{B}
such that
f_{\triangleright}
is a cofibration
(resp.,
weak equivalence
) in the fiber
\mathcal{T}_{B}
.

The following is the first main result of the paper.

Theorem A
.

Let
p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{B}
be a Waldhausen opfibration. If the basis category
\mathcal{B}
is a Waldhausen category, then the classes of total cofibrations and total weak equivalences given above endow the total category
\mathcal{T}
with a Waldhausen category structure.

Since the canonical projection
p:\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathscr{F}\to\mathcal{B}
associated to the pseudo functor
\mathscr{F}
in Question
1.1
is clearly a Waldhausen opfibration, Theorem
A
implies that the Grothendieck construction
\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathscr{F}
considered in Question
1.1
forms a Waldhausen category, where a morphism
(u,\phi):(A,X)\to(B,Y)
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) if
u:A\to B
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
\mathcal{B}
and

\phi:\mathscr{F}_{u}(X)\to Y
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
\mathscr{F}_{B}
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a Waldhausen category and
\mathsf{coE}
its subcategory with the same objects whose morphisms are cofibrations. Waldhausen showed in
[

that both the morphism category

and its full subcategory

. As illustrations of Theorem

, we reobtain the Waldhausen category structures on
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathsf{E})

\mathsf{Mor}(\mathsf{E})

\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{E})
(resp.,
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{coE})
), where
\mathcal{I}
is the free category associated to the quiver with two vertexes and one arrow, one may ask the following question:

1.2 Question.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a small index category and
\mathsf{E}
a Waldhausen category. Is there a canonical Waldhausen structure on
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{E})
or
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{coE})
generalizing the above two examples?

The answer for the first functor category is transparent. Indeed, define a morphism
f:X\to Y
in
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{E})
to be a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) if
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
\mathsf{E}
for each object
i
in
\mathcal{I}
. Since isomorphisms and pushouts in
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{E})
are determined componentwise, it is easy to check that
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{E})
forms a Waldhausen category in this way. However, the situation for the second functor category becomes much more complicated, and at this moment we cannot figure out a completely satisfactory solution. Instead, in this paper we will focus on the special case that
\mathcal{I}
is the free category associated to a left rooted quiver
Q
(see Section
4
for the definition of left rooted quivers). Using Theorem
A
and the approach of Cagne and Melliès
[
3
]
for Reedy model structures, we show that if
\mathsf{E}
has small coproducts and the class of cofibrations is the left part of a weak factorization system in
\mathsf{E}
, then
{\sf Rep}(Q,\mathsf{coE})=\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{I},\mathsf{coE})
admits a Waldhuasen category structure.

Before displaying the explicit statement of the result,
let us describe the key steps and necessary notation.
As one might expect, the proof of the result goes through a transfinite induction and
the most technical part of the proof is the induction step for successor ordinals.
Let

be the transfinite sequence of subsets of vertexes in

denote the subquiver of

spanned by

for each ordinal

It is shown that the restriction functor

with respect to the inclusion
\iota_{\mu}:Q_{\mu}\to Q_{\mu+1}

is a Waldhausen opfibration (see Proposition
4.10
)
under which the induction step for a successor ordinal
can be completed by Theorem
A
;
see
4.11

The key role playing in the proof of the above result is that
the fiber

of a representation

is isomorphic to the product category

(see Proposition

),

is indeed the latching object of

is the full subcategory of the undercategory

For any representation

Hence, for any morphism

we get a pushout appearing as the inner square of the commutative diagram

\scriptstyle{\lrcorner}

Now, we can state the second main result of the paper.

Theorem B
.

Let
Q
be a left rooted quiver and
\mathsf{E}
a Waldhausen category with small coproducts. Denote by
\mathsf{C}

(

\mathsf{E}

If further

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give necessary preliminaries on Waldhausen categories and
Grothendieck opfibrations.

2.1.
Waldhausen categories

In the original definition of a Waldhausen category
[
22
]
, the underlying category is required to be
pointed
. However, some categories we discuss in the paper admit although both initial and terminal objects, they are not necessary isomorphic; see for instance Example
2.3
. But they still admit classes of cofibrations and weak equivalences satisfying the required axioms. Hence, in this paper, we consider the following slight generalisation of the usual notion of a Waldhausen category, where only an initial object is required.

2.1 Definition.

A
Waldhausen category
consists of a category
\mathsf{E}
with an initial object “
0
” and a Waldhausen structure, that is, a class
\mathsf{C}
of morphisms called
cofibrations
(denoted by “
\rightarrowtail
”) and a class
\mathsf{W}
of morphisms called
weak equivalences
(denoted by “
\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}
”), such that both
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{W}
are closed under compositions and the following axioms are satisfied:

(C1)

All isomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
are cofibrations.

(C2)

For every object
A
in
\mathsf{E}
, the morphism
0\to A
is a cofibration.

(C3)

If
A\rightarrowtail B
is a cofibration, then for any morphism
A\to C
,
the pushout

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lrcorner}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\sqcup_{A}C}

exists in
\mathsf{E}
and the morphism
C\to B\sqcup_{A}C
is a cofibration.

(W1)

All isomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
are weak equivalences.

(W2) “Gluing Lemma”:

Given a commutative diagram

\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{C^{\prime}}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

in
\mathsf{E}
, where the horizontal morphisms on the right are cofibrations and all vertical morphisms are weak equivalences, the induced morphism

B\sqcup_{A}C\to B^{\prime}\sqcup_{A^{\prime}}C^{\prime}

is also a weak equivalence.

A functor between Waldhausen categories is said to be
exact
if it preserves the initial objects, cofibrations, weak equivalences, and the pushout diagrams of axiom (C3).

There are many examples of Waldhausen categories. Furthermore, given a Waldhausen category
\mathsf{E}
, one can equip Waldhausen structures on a few categories associated to
\mathsf{E}
. The following examples are taken from
[
22
]
.

2.2 Example.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a Waldhausen category.

(1)

The morphism category
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathsf{E})
admits an initial object, and is a Waldhausen category: a morphism
(u,a):(f:A\rightarrowtail X)\to(g:B\rightarrowtail Y)
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathsf{E})
if both
u
and

are cofibrations (resp., weak equivalences) in
\mathsf{E}
.

(2)

Let
\mathsf{coMor}(\mathsf{E})
be the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mor}(\mathsf{E})
whose objects are cofibrations in
\mathsf{E}
. For any morphism
(u,a):(f:A\rightarrowtail X)\to(g:B\rightarrowtail Y)

\mathsf{coMor}(\mathsf{E})
, by considering the following commutative diagram, where the inner square is a pushout that exists by the axiom (C3), we obtain a natural morphism

\mathsf{coMor}(\mathsf{E})
admits an initial object, and is a Waldhausen category, where

2.3 Example.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a Waldhausen category with
0
the initial object and
A
an object in
\mathsf{E}
.

(1)

The overcategory
\mathsf{E}/A
admits an initial object
(0\to A)
as well as a terminal object
\mathrm{id}_{A}:A\to A
. Thus
\mathsf{E}/A
is not pointed unless
A=0
. We define a morphism in
\mathsf{E}/A
to be a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) if it as a morphism in
\mathsf{E}
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence). It is routine to check that
\mathsf{E}/A
forms a Waldhausen category with this construction.

(2)

The undercategory
A/\mathsf{E}
admits an initial object
(\mathrm{id}_{A}:A\to A)
. However, the construction for overcategories in general does not work for undercategories. Indeed, the unique morphism in
A/\mathsf{E}
from the initial object
(\mathrm{id}_{A}:A\to A)
to any object
(f:A\rightarrow X)
is
f
, which might not be a cofibration in
\mathsf{E}
, so in this case the axiom (C2) fails. However, if we consider the full subcategory
\overline{A/\mathsf{E}}
consisting of cofibrations
(A\rightarrowtail X)
in
\mathsf{E}
, then this problem is overcome: one can define a morphism in
\overline{A/\mathsf{E}}
to be a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) if it as a morphism in
\mathsf{E}
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence). With this construction
\overline{A/\mathsf{E}}
forms a Waldhausen category.

2.4 Weak factorization systems.

Let

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{D}

commutes.
For a class
\mathsf{C}
of morphisms in
\mathsf{E}
, let
\mathsf{C}^{\Box}
denote the class of morphisms in
\mathsf{E}

\mathsf{C}

if

\mathsf{C}^{\Box}=\mathsf{F}
,

and every morphism

\mathsf{E}

\mathsf{C}
and

in
\mathsf{F}
.

2.5 Remark.

Let
(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{F})
be a weak factorization system in
\mathsf{E}
.
Then

(a)

both
\mathsf{C}
and
\mathsf{F}
are closed under compositions, and contain all isomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
;

(b)

if
\mathsf{E}
has pushouts along morphisms in
\mathsf{C}
, then
\mathsf{C}
is closed under pushouts;

(c)

if
\mathsf{E}
has small coproducts, then
\mathsf{C}
is closed under small coproducts.

For details, see
[
13

.

The following result describes a method to construct a Waldhausen structure from a weak factorization system satisfying certain conditions.

2.6 Proposition.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be a category with finite coproducts and an initial object 0, and let
(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{W})
be a pair of classes of morphisms in
\mathsf{E}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C}^{\Box})
forms a weak factorization system in
\mathsf{E}
such that

\mathsf{C}^{\Box}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
;

(2)

\mathsf{W}
contains all isomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
and satisfies the 2-out-of-3 property, that is, if two of the three morphisms
f
,
g
and
gf
are in
\mathsf{W}
, then so is the third one;

(3)

\mathsf{C}\cap\mathsf{W}
is closed under pushouts;

(4)

0\to X\in\mathsf{C}
for all objects
X
in
\mathsf{E}
.

Then
(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{W})
forms a Waldhausen structure on
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.5
,
\mathsf{C}
contains all isomorphisms in
\mathsf{E}
and is closed under pushouts. It remains to check the Gluing Lemma. This could be done by showing that
\mathsf{E}
is a category of cofibrant objects in the sense of Kamps and Porter
[
14
, page. 80]
, as any category of cofibrant objects satisfies the Gluing Lemma by
[
14
, Theorem II 2.27]
. However, according to the axioms for
\mathsf{E}
being a category of cofibrant objects, we only need to show that any object
X\in\mathsf{E}
admits a cylinder object. Indeed, since
(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{C}^{\Box})
forms a weak factorization system in
\mathsf{E}
, we can factor the fold map
\mathrm{id}_{X}\amalg\mathrm{id}_{X}:X\amalg X\to X
as

X\amalg X\overset{e_{0}\amalg e_{1}}{\longrightarrow}X\times I\overset{\delta}{\longrightarrow}X

for some object
X\times I
in
\mathsf{E}
with
e_{0}\amalg e_{1}\in\mathsf{C}
and
\delta\in\mathsf{C}^{\Box}
. But
\mathsf{C}^{\Box}\subseteq\mathsf{W}
, so
\delta\in\mathsf{W}
, and hence
(X\times I,e_{0},e_{1},\delta)
is the desired cylinder object for
X
.
∎

We illustrate the application of this proposition in the following examples.

\mathsf{M}

Let
\mathsf{A}

4.11 Proof of Theorem
B
.

It is clear that
{\sf Rep}(Q,\mathsf{coE})
admits an initial object, the zero functor. We proceed by a transfinite induction. Let
\mu
be an ordinal with
\mu\leqslant\zeta
. If
\mu=1
, then for any morphism
f:X\to Y\in{\sf Rep}(Q_{1},\mathsf{coE})
, one has
\mathsf{L}_{i}(X)=0=\mathsf{L}_{i}(Y)
by Remark
4.1
. Thus the induced morphism

\rho_{i}:\mathsf{L}_{i}(Y)\sqcup_{\mathsf{L}_{i}(X)}X_{i}\to Y_{i}

in (

∗

‣
4
) is precisely
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}
. In this case, one defines
f
as a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
{\sf Rep}(Q_{1},\mathsf{coE})
if
f_{i}
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
\mathsf{E}
for each vertex
i\in V_{1}
. It is clear that
{\sf Rep}(Q_{1},\mathsf{coE})
forms a Waldhausen category.

Now we carry our the induction procedure. There are two cases. If
\mu
is limit ordinal, then each vertex
i\in V_{\mu}
lies in some
V_{\lambda}
with
\lambda<\mu
. By the induction hypothesis, one checks routinely that
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
forms a Waldhausen category via the construction described in the statement of Theorem
B
.

For the case of non-limit ordinals, suppose that
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
is a Waldhausen category by the construction given in the statement of Theorem
B
. Since
\iota_{\mu}^{*}:{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu+1},\mathsf{coE})\to{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
is a Waldhausen opfibration by Proposition
4.10
, it follows from Theorem
A
that
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu+1},\mathsf{coE})
is a Waldhausen category. Moreover, because the fiber morphism
f_{\triangleright}
, up to identification through
\mathscr{R}
, is the family

\{\rho_{i}:\mathsf{L}_{i}(B)\sqcup_{\mathsf{L}_{i}(A)}X_{i}\longrightarrow Y_{i}\}_{i\in V_{\mu+1}\backslash V_{\mu}}

(see Remark
4.9
),
\mathsf{L}_{i}(A)=\mathsf{L}_{i}(X)
and
\mathsf{L}_{i}(B)=\mathsf{L}_{i}(Y)
, it follows that a morphism
f:X\to Y
that is above
u:A\to B
in
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) if and only if
u
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
and the induced morphism
\rho_{i}:\mathsf{L}_{i}(Y)\sqcup_{\mathsf{L}_{i}(X)}X_{i}\to Y_{i}
lies in
\mathsf{C}
(resp.
\mathsf{W}
) for each vertex
i\in V_{\mu+1}\backslash V_{\mu}
. However, by the induction hypothesis,
u
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu},\mathsf{coE})
if and only if
\rho_{i}
lies in
\mathsf{C}
(resp.
\mathsf{W}
) for each vertex
i\in V_{\mu}
. Consequently,
f
is a cofibration (resp., weak equivalence) in
{\sf Rep}(Q_{\mu+1},\mathsf{coE})
if and only if
\rho_{i}
lies in
\mathsf{C}
(resp.
\mathsf{W}
) for all vertexes
i\in V_{\mu+1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

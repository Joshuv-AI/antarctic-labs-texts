Storage and retrieval of von Neumann measurements
Paulina Lewandowska, Ryszard Kukulski, Łukasz Pawela, Zbigniew Puchała

Abstract
This work examines the problem of learning an unknown von Neumann measurement of dimension d d from a finite number of copies. To obtain a faithful approximation of the given measurement we are allowed to use it N N times. Our main goal is to estimate the asymptotic behavior of the maximum value of the average fidelity function F d F_{d} for a general N → 1 N\rightarrow 1 learning scheme. We show that F d = 1 − Θ ⁡ ( 1 N 2 ) F_{d}=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right) for arbitrary but fixed dimension d d . In addition to that, we compared various learning schemes for d = 2 d=2 . We observed that the learning scheme based on deterministic port-based teleportation is asymptotically optimal but performs poorly for low N N . In particular, we discovered a parallel learning scheme, which despite its lack of asymptotic optimality, provides a high value of the fidelity for low values of N N and uses only two-qubit entangled memory states.



I
Introduction

In the general approach of storage and retrieval (SAR) of quantum operations,
we want
to approximate a given, unknown operation, which we
were able to perform
N
times experimentally. Such a scheme is called
N\rightarrow 1

learning scheme.

This strategy usually consists of preparing some initial quantum state, applying the unknown
operation
N
times,
which allows us to store the unknown operation for later use, and finally, a
retrieval operation that
applies an approximation of the black box on some arbitrary quantum state.
Additionally, each application
of the operation contained within the black box can be followed by
some arbitrary
processing operations. If that is the case, the optimal strategy should also
contain their
description. The scheme is optimal when it achieves the highest possible
fidelity of the
approximation
raginsky2001fidelity
;
belavkin2005operational
.

The obstacle standing in the way of achieving unit fidelity lies in the
no-cloning
theorem
wootters1982single
and even further, the no-programming
theorem
nielsen1997programmable
. It states that a general processor,
which performs a program based on some input
state is not possible. There is no doubt that programmable devices would
represent an instrumental
piece of quantum technology. Hence, their approximate realizations are of
common interest
buvzek1996quantum
;
hillery2002probabilistic
;
yang2020optimal
;
gschwendtner2021programmability
;
peres2002unspeakable
.
Nevertheless, one should keep in mind the difference
between programming and learning. A learning protocol can be used to generate a
program, but the program does not have to be generated by learning. Although,
in some cases, the performances of programming and learning are equal

yang2020optimal
.

The seminal work in this field was the paper by Bisio and
Chiribella
bisio2010optimal
. It was devoted
to learning an unknown unitary transformation. Therein, the authors focused on
storing the unitary
operation in a quantum memory while having limited resources. They
proved that unitary
operations could be learned optimally in the parallel scheme, meaning there
is no additional
processing after using the unknown unitary transformation. Hence, all
the required uses of
the black box can be performed in parallel. They also provide an upper bound on
the fidelity of

N\rightarrow 1
learning scheme that is equal

1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)
. A
probabilistic version of
SAR (PSAR) problem was also considered in
sedlak2019optimal
;
sedlak2020probabilistic
. There,
they showed the optimal success probability of
N\rightarrow 1
PSAR of
unitary channels on

d
-dimensional quantum systems is equal to
N/(N-1+d^{2})
.

It is worth mentioning that the SAR of unitary operations is also closely
related to port-based teleportation (PBT)
ishizaka2008asymptotic
;
ishizaka2009quantum
. One may take the PBT protocol to define a learning scheme. In
particular, the entanglement fidelity
of the deterministic PBT protocol matches the fidelity of learning unitary
operations
bisio2010optimal
;
christandl2021asymptotic
. Similarly, the
probability of successful teleportation is equal to the performance of PSAR

sedlak2019optimal
.
Finally, the task of learning unknown operations
fits into the paradigm of quantum machine learning, when both the
data and the algorithms are quantum
sasaki2002quantum
;
sentis2012quantum
;
dunjko2016quantum
;
monras2017inductive
;
alvarez2017supervised
;
amin2018quantum
;
sentis2019unsupervised
.

Subsequent works build upon these results but focus on different classes of
operations, for example,
the von Neumann measurements
bisio2011quantum
. In contrast to previous
works, they showed
that, in general, the optimal algorithm for quantum measurement learning cannot
be parallel and found
the optimal learning algorithm for arbitrary von Neumann measurements for the
case
1\rightarrow 1

and
2\rightarrow 1
. Nevertheless, a general optimal scheme
N\rightarrow 1

of measurement
learning still remains an open problem, even for low-dimensional quantum
systems. Hence, despite some partial results, the investigation of SAR for von
Neumann measurements is still an open question.

In this work, we address the unsolved problem of learning an unknown von
Neumann measurement
defined in
bisio2011quantum
. We focus on
N\rightarrow 1
learning scheme of von Neumann measurements. We investigate a
value of the average fidelity
in the asymptotic regime. By using the deterministic PBT protocol we state a
lower bound which behaves as
1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)
. Moreover,
we provide an upper bound for the average fidelity function, which matches the
lower bound and hence provides a solution to the problem.

Additionally, we compare different learning
schemes for the qubit case. Although, the learning scheme based on PBT is
asymptotically optimal, it can be outperformed for low values of
N
. To show this, we introduce
a scheme, which we call
pretty good learning scheme
(PGLS).
This scheme is a particular case of a parallel learning scheme
which uses only two-qubit entangled memory states. The fidelity function
calculated for the pretty good learning
scheme is uniform over all qubit von Neumann measurements and behaves as

1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
we
formulate the problem of von
Neumann measurement learning. In Section
II.1
we introduce
necessary mathematical
concepts. Our main result is then presented in Section
II.2

(Theorem
1
). To prove this theorem, we first address the case of
lower bound (Section
III.1
), and subsequently, upper bound
(Section
III.2
). In Section
IV
we compare the
performance of different learning schemes for a qubit case. In particular, we
introduce the pretty good learning scheme and present numerical results about the most efficient parallel and adaptive learning schemes.
Finally,
Section
V
concludes the article with a summary of the main
results. In the Appendix, we
provide technical details of proofs.

, along with its

.

II.1
Mathematical framework

Let us introduce the following notation. Consider a
d
-dimensional complex
Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
denote it by
\mathcal{H}_{d}
. Let
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}},\mathcal{H}_{d_{2}})
be the set of all
matrices of dimension

d_{1}\times d_{2}
. As a shorthand we put
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d})\coloneqq\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d},\mathcal{H}_{d})
. The
set of quantum states defined on space
\mathcal{H}_{d}
, that is the set of positive
semidefinite operators having unit trace,
will be denoted by
\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})
. We will also need a linear mapping
transforming

\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}})
into
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{2}})
as
\mathcal{T}:\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}})\mapsto\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{2}}).
There exists a bijection between introduced
linear mappings

\mathcal{T}
and set of matrices
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}d_{2}})
, known as the
Choi-Jamiołkowski
isomorphism
choi1975completely
;
jamiolkowski1972linear
. Its explicit
form is
\text{T}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d_{1}-1}\mathcal{T}(\outerproduct{i}{j})\otimes\outerproduct{i}{j}
. We will denote
linear mappings with calligraphic font
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T}
etc., whereas
the
corresponding Choi-Jamiołkowski matrices as plain symbols:
L,S,T
etc.
Moreover, we introduce the vectorization operation of a matrix
X\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d_{1}},\mathcal{H}_{d_{2}})
, defined by

\left|\left.{X}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{d_{2}-1}\left(X\ket{i}\right)\otimes\ket{i}
.

A general quantum measurement (POVM)
\mathcal{Q}
can be viewed as a set of
positive semidefinite
operators
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i}
such that
\sum_{i}Q_{i}={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
. These operators
are usually called
effects. The von Neumman measurements,
\mathcal{P}_{U}
,
are a special subclass of measurements whose all effects are rank-one
projections given by
\mathcal{P}_{U}=\{P_{U,i}\}_{i=0}^{d-1}=\{U\outerproduct{i}{i}U^{\dagger}\}_{i=0}^{d-1}
for some
unitary matrix
U\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d})
.

Quantum channels are completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP) linear
maps. Generally,
\mathcal{C}

is a quantum channel which maps
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}^{(in)})
to

\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}^{(out)})
if its
Choi-Jamiołkowski operator
C
is a positive semidefinite and

\tr_{\mathcal{H}^{(out)}}(C)={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
, where
\tr_{\mathcal{H}^{(out)}}
denotes a partial trace over the output
system
\mathcal{H}^{(out)}
.
Given a von Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}
, it can be seen as a
measure-and-prepare quantum channel

\mathcal{P}_{U}(\rho)=\sum_{i}\tr\left(P_{U,i}\rho\right)\outerproduct{i}{i}
,
\rho\in\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})
. The
Choi matrix of
\mathcal{P}_{U}
is
P_{U}=\sum_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\overline{P_{U,i}},
which will be
utilized throughout this work. Finally, we will use the notation
\Phi_{U}
to
indicate
unitary channel given by
\Phi_{U}(X)=UXU^{\dagger}
and the shortcut

\mathcal{I}_{d}\coloneqq\Phi_{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d}}
for the identity channel.

II.2
Learning setup

Figure 1
:
Schematic representations of the setup for learning of von Neumann
measurements
\mathcal{P}_{U}
in the
N\rightarrow 1
scheme.

Imagine we are given a black box with the promise that it contains some von
Neumann measurement,

\mathcal{P}_{U}
, parameterized by a unitary matrix
U
. The exact value of
U

is unknown to us. We
are allowed to use the black box
N
times. Our goal is to prepare some initial
memory state

\sigma
, some intermediate processing channels
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{N-1}
and
a measurement
\mathcal{R}

such that we are able to approximate
\mathcal{P}_{U}
on an arbitrary state
\rho
. This
approximation will
be denoted throughout this work as
\mathcal{Q}_{U}
. We would like to point out that,
generally,
\mathcal{Q}_{U}
will

not
be a von Neumann measurement.

The initial memory state
\sigma
and entire sequence of processing channels

\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}
can be
viewed as
storing
the unknown operation and will be denoted as
\mathcal{S}

whereas the measurement

\mathcal{R}
we will call as
retrieval
. During the storing stage, we apply

\mathcal{S}
on
N
copies of

\mathcal{P}_{U}
. As a result, the initial memory state
\sigma
is transferred to the
memory state

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{S}}
. After that, we measure an arbitrary quantum state
\rho

and the memory state

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{S}}
by using
\mathcal{R}
. Equivalently, we can say that during
retrieval stage, we apply
the measurement
\mathcal{Q}_{U}
on the state
\rho
. The entire learning scheme will be
denoted by
\mathcal{L}

and considered as a triple
\mathcal{L}=\left(\sigma,\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N-1},\mathcal{R}\right)
. We emphasize
that the procedure allows us to use as much quantum memory as necessary.

As a measure of quality of approximating a von Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}=\{P_{U,i}\}_{i}
with a POVM
\mathcal{Q}_{U}=\{Q_{U,i}\}_{i}
we choose the fidelity function
raginsky2001fidelity
, which is
defined as follows

\mathcal{F}_{d}(\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{Q}_{U})\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}\tr(P_{U, i} Q_{U, i}),

(1)

where
d
is the dimension of the measured system.
Note that in the case when
\mathcal{P}_{U}
is a von Neumann measurement we obtain the
value of fidelity
function
\mathcal{F}_{d}
belongs to the interval
[0,1]
and equals one if
and
only if
P_{U,i}=Q_{U,i}

for all
i
. As there is no prior information about
\mathcal{P}_{U}

provided, we assume that
U
is sampled from a distribution pertaining to the
Haar measure.
Therefore, considering a von Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}
and its approximation

\mathcal{Q}_{U}
we introduce
the
average fidelity function

bisio2016quantum
with respect to
Haar measure as

\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L})\coloneqq\int_{U}dU\mathcal{F}_{d}(\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{Q}_{U}).

(2)

Our main goal is to maximize
\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}
over all possible
learning schemes
\mathcal{L}=\left(\sigma,\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N-1},\mathcal{R}\right)
. We introduce the
notation of the
maximum value of the average fidelity function

F_{d}\coloneqq\max_{\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L}).

(3)

In this work, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of
F_{d}
with
N\to\infty
.
Our main result can be summarized as the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
F_{d}
be the maximum value of the average fidelity function, defined in
Eq. (
3
) for
N\rightarrow 1
learning scheme of
von Neumann measurements. Then, for arbitrary but fixed dimension
d
we
obtain

F_{d}=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(4)

Lower bound

The proof of the lower bound for

is constructive. We
will construct the learning scheme
\mathcal{L}
of von Neumann measurements, which

\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L})=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)

The construction is based on deterministic port-based teleportation (DPBT)

N

\mathcal{H}_{d^{N}}^{(B)}=\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(N)}

Their goal is to teleport an
unknown state
\rho\in\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(in)}
from Alice to Bob in a way that this

of Bob’s ports.
To achieve this, Alice performs appropriate measurement
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

\{1,\ldots,N\}
and communicates this label to Bob. By using the label
i
, Bob
chooses
i
-th system of his state
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}_{B}
as the one which contains
the state
\rho
. The output of this procedure can be
written as

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(in)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d^{N}}^{(A)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d^{N-1}}^{(\bar{B}_{i})}}\left((Q_{i}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d^{N}})(\rho\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}_{A,B})\right),

(5)

where
\mathcal{H}_{d^{N-1}}^{(\bar{B}_{i})}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(i-1)}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(i+1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}^{(N)}
. In short, the
output of this procedure will be
denoted as
\mathcal{T}(\rho)
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a channel describing DPBT, depending on
the choice of
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}_{A,B}
and
\mathcal{Q}
.
It is known
christandl2021asymptotic
, that the best teleportation
procedure
\mathcal{T}_{0}
approximates
\mathcal{I}_{d}
with the entanglement fidelity

F_{*}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d^{2}}\tr\left(T_{0}\left|\left.{{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d}}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\!\left\langle{{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d}}}\right.\right|\right)=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(6)

We can use DPBT to construct a learning scheme (see

). Let
\outerproduct{\psi_0}{\psi_0}_{A,B}

\mathcal{Q}_{0}
realize
the optimal teleportation strategy
\mathcal{T}_{0}
. We take

with
N
copies of the von Neumann
measurement

. The result of the storage is a memory state

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{U},S}=\left(\mathcal{I}_{d^{N}}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{U}^{\otimes N}\right)(\sigma)

of the remaining quantum state
\tau
, and a tuple of measurements’ results

. The retrieval

is a composition of a measurement

and classical
postprocessing. In details, first, we
apply
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
on

to obtain the label

. Second, we return the result
j_{i}
as the output of
\mathcal{R}
.
This procedure determines a learning scheme
\mathcal{L}
which achieves the average fidelity

\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L})=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(7)

IV
Qubit case

In this subsection we investigate more deeply the behavior of

for different types of learning schemes

IV.1
Pretty good learning scheme

The first scheme which we analyze will be called the
pretty good learning
scheme
. Despite its lack of optimality, it provides a relatively high value for
the average fidelity function asymptotically behaving as

\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L}_{PGLS})=1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
. This scheme employs a simple storage strategy,
which uses only two-qubit entangled memory states and the
learning process is done in parallel. Moreover, the achieved value of the
fidelity function is uniform over all qubit von Neumann measurements.

Let us consider a parallel learning scheme with
N

copies of the von Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}
. A sketch of
our scheme is shown in Fig.
5
and here we present the
algorithm describing the procedure:

Figure 5
:
Schematic representations of the pretty good learning
scheme for
N=3
. In the learning process we obtained three labels:

0,1,0
. As labels “
0
” are in majority, we reject the label
“
1
” and
the associated quantum part.

1.

We prepare the initial memory state
\sigma
as a tensor product of

N
maximally entangled states
\ket{\omega}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|\left.{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
.

2.

We partially measure each state
\ket{\omega}
using
\mathcal{P}_{U}
,
obtaining the state
(\mathcal{P}_{U}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{2})(\outerproduct{\omega}{\omega}).

3.

For each measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}
, we obtain one of two possible
measurement results: “
0
” or “
1
”. In consequence, we get
N_{0}

outcomes “
0
”
and
N_{1}
outcomes “
1
”,
N_{0}+N_{1}=N
. The state of the remaining
quantum
part is equal to
\overline{P_{U,0}}^{\otimes N_{0}}\otimes\overline{P_{U,1}}^{\otimes N_{1}}
(up to permutation of subsystems).
Without loss of a generality (w.l.o.g.), we may assume that
N_{0}\geq N_{1}
.

4.

By majority vote we reject minority report,
i.e.
we reject all
outcomes “
1
” and
quantum states associated with them. As a result the memory state is given
by

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{S}}=\overline{P_{U,0}}^{\otimes N_{0}}
.

5.

We prepare an arbitrary state
\rho\in\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.

6.

We perform a binary retrieval measurement
\mathcal{R}=\{R,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}-R\}

.

To construct the effect
R
, let us fix
N_{0}
and let
n=N_{0}-1
. We introduce
the family of Dicke
states
mukherjee2020preparing
. The Dicke state
\ket{ D_k^n}
is the

n
-qubit state, which
is equal to the superposition state of all
{n\choose k}
basis states of
weight
k

The approximation

In the pretty good learning scheme, to keep the calculation simple, we

We have observed in Section

V
Conclusions and discussion

In this work, we studied the problem of learning an unknown von
Neumann measurement of dimension
d
from a finite number of copies.
Our goal was to find the asymptotic behavior of the maximum value for the
average fidelity
function
F_{d}
. This value was maximized over all possible learning schemes,
and the average was taken over all von Neumann
measurements. By using the deterministic PBT protocol, we were able
to state the lower bound
1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)
, which matched
the obtained upper bound and hence, solved the given problem.

In the qubit case, we introduced a scheme called the pretty good learning
scheme. This scheme was a particular case of a parallel learning protocol, and
it used only two-qubit entangled memory states. The average fidelity function
calculated for the pretty good learning scheme behaved as

1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
. Moreover, we compared the performance of
different learning schemes: adaptive, parallel, based on DPBT, based on PPBT
and the pretty good
learning scheme for the qubit case. Although, the learning scheme based on PBT
were asymptotically optimal, we showed that the pretty good learning scheme
outperforms it for low values of
N
.

This work paves the way toward a complete description of the capabilities of von
Neumann measurement learning schemes. One potential way forward is the
probabilistic storage and
retrieval approach,
widely studied for unitary operations and phase rotations in

sedlak2019optimal
;
sedlak2020probabilistic
. According to our numerical results, the
probability of retrieval of a
quantum measurement in a parallel scheme is exactly
N/(N+3)
, which
corresponds to the value
obtained in
sedlak2019optimal
for unitary channels, while adaptive
strategies for quantum
measurements learning to provide slightly higher probability, starting from
N\geq 3
.

2.5)
for the network presented in

9

\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{2}^{(out)}}L={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}S.
Therefore, using
the property
Eq. (

[\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}S,({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes U)^{\otimes N}]=0.

(32)

Let us define the memory state
\sigma_{\Phi_{U^{\dagger}},\mathcal{S}}
as an

\mathcal{S}
on

\Phi_{U^{\dagger}}
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\sigma_{\Phi_{U^{\dagger}},\mathcal{S}}&=\tr_{\mathcal{X}_{I}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{O}}\left[\left|\left.{{\sqrt{\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}S}}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\!\left\langle{{\sqrt{\tr_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}S}}}\right.\right|\left(\left|\left.{{U^{\top}}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\!\left\langle{{U^{\top}}}\right.\right|^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{4^{N}}\right)\right]\\

&=\left({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes\bar{U}\right)^{\otimes N}\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\left({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes U^{\top}\right)^{\otimes N},\end{split}

(33)

where the last equality uses the property Eq. (

)
and introduced

. It means that an arbitrary storage

\mathcal{S}

N

. By exploiting

\begin{split}F_{2}&\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{\mathcal{L}}\int_{U}dU\tr\left[L^{\top}\left(({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes U)J_{\Delta}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}\otimes U^{\dagger})\otimes\left|\left.{{U^{\dagger}}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\!\left\langle{{U^{\dagger}}}\right.\right|^{\otimes N}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}\\
\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\int_{U}dU\sum_{i=0}^{1}\tr\left[R_{i}(U\outerproduct{i}{i}U^{\dagger}\otimes\sigma_{\Phi_{U^{\dagger}},\mathcal{S}})\right]\\

(34)

∎

B.2
Objective function simplification

The aim of this section is to simplify the maximization of the fidelity
function
\widetilde{F_{2}}
defined in
Eq. (
27
). Let us consider a binary measurement

\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}

taken from the maximization domain in Eq. (
27
). It
holds that
R_{0}+R_{1}={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{2N+1}}
, and hence we may write

\begin{split}\widetilde{F_{2}}&=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\int_{U}dU\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{1}\tr\left[R_{i}\left((U\otimes{\bar{U}}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})(\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi})(U^{\dagger}\otimes{U^{\top}}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\int_{U}dU\tr\left[R_{0}\left((U\otimes{\bar{U}}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})(\sigma_{z}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi})(U^{\dagger}\otimes{U^{\top}}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1}\}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\tr\left[\int_{U}dU(U^{\dagger}\otimes{U^{\top}}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})R_{0}(U\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})(\sigma_{z}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi})\right],\end{split}

(35)

where
\sigma_{z}=\outerproduct{0}{0}-\outerproduct{1}{1}
. Observe that, taking the average value of the matrix
R_{0}

over the unitary
group
\{U\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a}\}_{U}
is equivalent to
taking
R
such that
0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{2N+1}}
and
[R,U\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a}]=0
for any qubit
unitary matrix
U
. Equivalently, we may write
[R^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}},U^{\otimes N+1}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a}]=0
,
where
\cdot^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}
represents the partial transposition over subsystem

\mathcal{Z}
. According to

(
watrous2018theory
, Theorem 7.15)
the matrix
R^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}
commutes with

U^{\otimes N+1}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a}
if and only if it is of the form

R^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}\otimes M_{\pi},

(36)

where matrices
W_{\pi}\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{X})
represent subsystem
permutation matrices
acting on
N+1
qubit systems, according to the equation

W_{\pi}\ket{b_0, b_1,\ldots, b_N}=\ket{b_{\pi(0)}, b_{\pi(1)}, \ldots,
b_{\pi(N)}},\,b_{k}\in\{0,1\}.

(37)

The matrices
M_{\pi}
belong to the set
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{Y})
and the index
\pi

goes over all
permutations of the set
\{0,\ldots,N\}
. Hence, we may simplify calculation
of

\widetilde{F_{2}}

\widetilde{F_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{2N+1}}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\tr\left[R(\sigma_{z}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi})\right].

(38)

To simplify the calculation of
\widetilde{F_{2}}
even further, we introduce the
following
notation of basis
states defined on
N+1
qubit system with fixed weight. We enumerate qubit
subsystems with numbers

0,1,\ldots,N
. For any subset
A_{k}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
, such that
|A_{k}|=k
we define:

\mathcal{H}_{2^{N}}\ni\ket{A_k}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}(\delta(i\in A_{k})\ket{1}+\delta(i\not\in A_{k})\ket{0}).

(39)

Consider the following subspaces of the
N+1
qubit space:

\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\coloneqq\text{span}\left(\ket{0}\ket{A_k},\ket{1}\ket{A_{k + 1}}:A_{k},A_{k+1}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}\right)

(40)

for
k=-1,\ldots,N
, where the vectors exist if and only if the expression is
well-defined (for instance,
the vectors
\ket{A_{-1}},\ket{A_{N + 1}}
do not exist). In this notation,
subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{(k)}

constitute a decomposition of
N+1
qubit space,
\mathcal{H}_{2^{N+1}}=\bigoplus_{k=-1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{(k)}
.
One may observe, that the matrix
R
appearing in the maximization domain
of
Eq. (
38
) is block diagonal in the introduced
decomposition (in the
partition
\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{Y}
). For such retrieval
R
, let us consider

H_{R}=\tr_{\mathcal{Z}}\left(R(\sigma_{z}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{4^{N}})\right).

(41)

Observe that the matrix
H_{R}
is block diagonal in the decomposition

\mathcal{H}_{2^{N}}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}\text{span}(\ket{A_k}:A_{k}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}).

(42)

Hence, we will write
H_{R}
as

H_{R}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{N}H_{R,k}.

(43)

Utilizing the above observations, the maximization problem
Eq. (
38
) can be
written as

\begin{split}\widetilde{F_{2}}&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\tr\left[R(\sigma_{z}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi})\right]=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\\
\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}\in\Omega(\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y})\end{subarray}}\bra{\psi}H_{R}\ket{\psi}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{k=0,\ldots,N}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{1}(H_{R,k})\end{split}

(44)

where
\lambda_{1}(\cdot)
stands for the largest eigenvalue and we used
shortcut

{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{2N+1}}
. Finally, we observe that
H_{R}=-(\sigma_{x}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})H_{R}(\sigma_{x}^{\otimes N}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})
, where
\sigma_{x}=\outerproduct{0}{1}+\outerproduct{1}{0}
. It implies
that
H_{R,k}
is unitarily equivalent to
-H_{R,N-k}
for any
k
. We use
this
fact to write the
final simplification of
\widetilde{F_{2}}
. The following lemma sums up all the
considerations presented in this
section.

Lemma 4
.

For the fidelity function
\widetilde{F_{2}}
defined in
Eq. (
27
)
it holds that

\widetilde{F_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{k=0,\ldots,\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\end{subarray}}\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}.

(45)

B.3
Technical lemmas

In the following lemma we will observe that optimization problem in
Eq. (
45
) can be reduced to the case
k\in\mathbb{N},N=2k
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and take
k
, such that
k\leq N/2
. It holds that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\end{subarray}}\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{R}:\,\,0\leq\widetilde{R}\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
\widetilde{R}=\sum_{\pi}\widetilde{W}_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes\widetilde{M}_{\pi}\end{subarray}}\|\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},N-k}\|_{\infty},

(46)

where the matrix
\widetilde{R}
is defined for
\widetilde{N}=2(N-k)
and
hence the number of
systems on which the matrix
\widetilde{W}_{\pi}
acts is
\widetilde{N}+1
.

Proof.

Let us fix
R
such that
0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
and
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}
.
Define

\widetilde{R}\coloneqq\sum_{\pi}\left(W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{N-2k}}\right)\otimes\left(M_{\pi}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{N-2k}}\right).

(47)

We see that matrix
\widetilde{R}
is in the maximization domain of the
right-hand side of
Eq. (
46
). Then, we have
\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R}}=\tr_{\mathcal{Z}}\left(\widetilde{R}(\sigma_{z}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})\right)=\bigoplus_{l}\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},l}
. The matrix
\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},N-k}
is defined on the space spanned by the
vectors
\ket{A_{N - k}}\in\mathcal{H}_{2^{\widetilde{N}}}
for
A_{N-k}\subset\{1,\ldots,\widetilde{N}\}
.
These vectors can be
expressed in the form
\ket{A_{N - k}}=\ket{B_i}\ket{B_{N-k-i}},
where

\ket{B_i}\in\mathcal{H}_{2^{N}}
for
B_{i}
such that
|B_{i}|=i
,
B_{i}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
and

\ket{B_{N-k-i}}\in\mathcal{H}_{2^{N-2k}}
,
B_{N-k-i}\subset\{N+1,\ldots,\widetilde{N}\}
. Then, we
have

\begin{split}(\bra{A_{N-k}}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},N-k}(\ket{A'_{N-k}}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})=\innerproduct{B_{N-k-i}}{B'_{N-k-i'}}\left(\bra{B_{i}}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\right)H_{R}\left(\ket{B'_{i'}}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\right)\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}.\end{split}

(48)

The non-zero blocks exist if and only if
i=i^{\prime}
and
B_{N-k-i}=B^{\prime}_{N-k-i^{\prime}}
,
so

\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},N-k}=\bigoplus_{i=k}^{N-k}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{N-k-i}:\\
B_{N-k-i}\subset\{N+1,\ldots,\widetilde{N}\}\end{subarray}}H_{R,i}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}.

(49)

That means

\|\widetilde{H}_{\widetilde{R},N-k}\|_{\infty}=\max_{i=k,\ldots,N-k}\|H_{R,i}\|_{\infty}\geq\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}.

(50)

∎

In the next lemma we will find the upper bound for
Eq. (
45
) in the case
N=2k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
N=2k
. For matrices
R
and
H_{R,k}
defined in
Subsection
B.2
we have

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}R:\,\,0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\\
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}\end{subarray}}\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}\leq 1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right).

(51)

Proof.

Let us fix
R
such that
0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
and
R=\sum_{\pi}W_{\pi}^{\top_{\mathcal{Z}}}\otimes M_{\pi}
.
Through
the rest of the proof, by
B_{l}
we denote subsets of
\{1,\ldots,2k\}
,
such that
|B_{l}|=l
, for

l=0,\ldots,2k
. Following the notation introduced in
Subsection
B.2
, we
define four types of vectors:

1.

\ket{+_{A_k}}=x\ket{0}\ket{A_k}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1}:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=k\end{subarray}}\ket{1}\ket{B_{k+1}}
,

2.

\ket{-_{A_k}}=x\ket{1}\ket{A_k}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1}:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=k-1\end{subarray}}\ket{0}\ket{B_{k-1}}
,

3.

\ket{\oplus_{A_k}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1}:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=1\end{subarray}}\ket{1}\ket{B_{k+1}}
,

4.

\ket{\ominus_{A_k}}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1}:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=0\end{subarray}}\ket{0}\ket{B_{k-1}}
,

for each
A_{k}\subset\{1,\ldots,2k\}
and some
x>0
. Now we define the
following matrices:

1.

I_{+}=\sum_{A_{k}}\outerproduct{+_{A_k}}{A_k}
,

2.

I_{-}=\sum_{A_{k}}\outerproduct{-_{A_k}}{A_k}
,

3.

I_{\oplus}=\sum_{A_{k}}\outerproduct{\oplus_{A_k}}{A_k}
,

I_{\ominus}=\sum_{A_{k}}\outerproduct{\ominus_{A_k}}{A_k}
.

For arbitrary

\innerproduct{-_{A_k}}{-_{A'_k}}=x^{2}\delta(A_{k}=A^{\prime}_{k})+|\{B_{k-1}:|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=k-1,|B_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1\}|,

\innerproduct{\oplus_{A_k}}{\oplus_{A'_k}}=|\{B_{k+1}:|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=1,|B_{k+1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=1\}|,

4.

\innerproduct{\ominus_{A_k}}{\ominus_{A'_k}}=|\{B_{k-1}:|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=0,|B_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=0\}|.

We can observe that if
A_{k}=A^{\prime}_{k}
, then the above inner products are
x^{2}+k
,
x^{2}+k
,
k
,
k
, respectively. If
|A_{k}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1
then all
the inner products are
equal to one. Finally, if
|A_{k}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|<k-1
then we obtain all the
inner products are equal
to zero. We note two useful facts about matrices
I_{+},I_{-},I_{\oplus},I_{\ominus}
. Firstly, we have

I_{+}^{\dagger}I_{+}+I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}I_{\oplus}=I_{-}^{\dagger}I_{-}+I_{\ominus}^{\dagger}I_{\ominus}.

(52)

Secondly, one can show that

\|I_{+}^{\dagger}I_{+}+I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}I_{\oplus}\|_{\infty}=x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}.

(53)

As far as the first equality is straightforward, to show the second one,
note that for each
A_{k}

there is exactly
k^{2}
sets
A^{\prime}_{k}
such that
|A_{k}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1
. This
means that by the Birkhoff’s
Theorem we can express
I_{+}^{\dagger}I_{+}+I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}I_{\oplus}
in the
basis given by vectors

\ket{A_k}
as
I_{+}^{\dagger}I_{+}+I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}I_{\oplus}=(x^{2}+2k){\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}+2\sum_{i=1}^{k^{2}}\Pi_{i},
where
\Pi_{i}
are permutation matrices. By the triangle inequality
we have that the spectral
norm is no greater than
x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}
. By taking the normalized vector

\ket{x}\propto\sum_{A_{k}}\ket{A_k}
we get
\bra{x}\left(I_{+}^{\dagger}I_{+}+I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}I_{\oplus}\right)\ket{x}=x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}.

To state the upper bound for
\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}
we will use the
definition of
H_{R}
from
Eq. (
41
) and the decomposition from
Eq. (
43
).
For a given
A_{k},A_{k}^{\prime}\subset\{1,\ldots,2k\}
we have that

\begin{split}(\bra{A_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})H_{R,k}(\ket{A'_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(A_{k})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(0)\neq 0,\\
\pi(A_{k})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(0)\neq 0,\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}.\end{split}

(54)

Let us now define

\begin{split}G_{R,k}&=(I_{+}^{\dagger}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})R(I_{+}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})+(I_{\oplus}^{\dagger}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})R(I_{\oplus}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})-(I_{-}^{\dagger}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})R(I_{-}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})-(I_{\ominus}^{\dagger}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})R(I_{\ominus}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a}).\end{split}

(55)

Taking
A_{k},A_{k}^{\prime}\subset\{1,\ldots,2k\}
we have:

\begin{split}&(\bra{A_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})G_{R,k}(\ket{A'_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})\\
=&\left(x^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(A_{k})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B^{\prime}_{k+1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k,\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=B^{\prime}_{k+1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=k,\\
\pi(B_{k+1})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1},B^{\prime}_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=k,\\
|B^{\prime}_{k+1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k,\\
\pi(0,B_{k+1})=0,B^{\prime}_{k+1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1},B^{\prime}_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=1,\\
|B^{\prime}_{k+1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=1,\\
\pi(0,B_{k+1})=0,B^{\prime}_{k+1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}\right)\\
-&\left(x^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B^{\prime}_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1,\\
\pi(A_{k})=0,B^{\prime}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=k-1,\\
\pi(0,B_{k-1})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1},B^{\prime}_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=k-1,\\
|B^{\prime}_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1,\\
\pi(B_{k-1})=B^{\prime}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1},B^{\prime}_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=0,\\
|B^{\prime}_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=0,\\
\pi(B_{k-1})=B^{\prime}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}\right).\end{split}

(56)

This can be simplified to

\begin{split}&(\bra{A_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})G_{R,k}(\ket{A'_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})\\
=&\left(x^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(A_{k})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B^{\prime}_{k+1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k,\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=B^{\prime}_{k+1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k+1},\pi:\\
|B_{k+1}\cap A_{k}|=k,\\
\pi(B_{k+1})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}\right)\\
-&\left(x^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi:\\
\pi(0,A_{k})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B^{\prime}_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B^{\prime}_{k-1}\cap A^{\prime}_{k}|=k-1,\\
\pi(A_{k})=0,B^{\prime}_{k-1}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{k-1},\pi:\\
|B_{k-1}\cap A_{k}|=k-1,\\
\pi(0,B_{k-1})=A^{\prime}_{k}\end{subarray}}M_{\pi}\right).\end{split}

(57)

Let us write the above as
(\bra{A_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})G_{R,k}(\ket{A'_k}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{a})=\sum_{\pi}c_{\pi}M_{\pi}
, where
c_{\pi}
are some constants.
For
each
\pi
, let us determine the value of
c_{\pi}
:

•

For
\pi
such that
\pi(0)=0,\pi(A_{k})=A_{k}^{\prime}
we have
c_{\pi}=x^{2}-x^{2}=0
.

•

For
\pi
such that
\pi(0)=0,\pi(A_{k})\neq A_{k}^{\prime}
we have

c_{\pi}=0
.

•

For
\pi
such that
\pi(0)\neq 0,\pi(A_{k})=A_{k}^{\prime}
we have

c_{\pi}=x^{2}+x+x=x^{2}+2x
.

•

For
\pi
such that
\pi(0)\neq 0,\pi(A_{k})\neq A_{k}^{\prime},\pi(0,A_{k})\neq 0,A_{k}^{\prime}
there exists
a_{0}\not\in\{0\}\cup A_{k}
, such that
\pi(a_{0})\in\{0\}\cup A^{\prime}_{k}
. Therefore, we
consider two sub-cases:

–

If for each
a\not\in\{0\}\cup A_{k}
it holds
\pi(a)\not\in A^{\prime}_{k}
, then
\pi(a_{0})=0
,
\pi(0)\in A^{\prime}_{k}
and
A^{\prime}_{k}\subset\pi(0,A_{k})
. Then,
c_{\pi}=x-x=0
.

–

If
\pi(a_{0})\in A^{\prime}_{k}
, then we have two options:

*

If
\pi(a_{0},A_{k})=0,A^{\prime}_{k}
, then
c_{\pi}=x-x=0
.

*

If
\pi(a_{0},A_{k})\neq 0,A^{\prime}_{k}
, then
c_{\pi}=0
.

•

For
\pi
such that
\pi(0)\neq 0,\pi(0,A_{k})=0,A_{k}^{\prime}
we
have
c_{\pi}=-x^{2}-x-x=-(x^{2}+2x)
.

Therefore, we can see that
G_{R,k}=(x^{2}+2x)H_{R,k}
. Then, utilizing
Eq. (
52
),
Eq. (
53
) and Eq. (
55
) we get

-(x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}){\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\leq G_{R,k}\leq(x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}){\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl},

(58)

and finally we obtain
\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{x^{2}+2k+2k^{2}}{x^{2}+2x}.
Minimizing over
x>0
, we get for
x\approx 2k^{2}
that

\|H_{R,k}\|_{\infty}\leq 1-\Theta(1/k^{2})
, which
finishes this case of the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of upper bound

Lemma 7
.

The maximum value of the average fidelity
function, defined in
Eq. (
3
) is upper bounded by

F_{d}\leq 1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

(59)

Proof.

The thesis is true for
d=2
which follows from Lemma
1
.
Let us fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Take the optimal learning scheme
\mathcal{L}
such
that it achieves
\mathcal{F}_{d}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L})=F_{d}
. Without loss of
the generality we assume that
\mathcal{L}
satisfies

[L,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d}\otimes U\otimes({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d}\otimes\bar{U})]^{\otimes N}]=0

(60)

for any unitary matrix
U\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d})
.
Then, for any
U
, we have

F_{d}=\mathcal{F}_{d}(\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{Q}_{U})\leq\frac{d-1}{d}+\frac{1}{d}\min_{i}\tr(P_{U, i} Q_{U, i}).

(61)

Moreover, for
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
it holds

\tr(P_{U, j_1} Q_{U, j_2})\leq\tr(P_{U, j_1} (\Id_d - Q_{U, j_1}))\leq 1-\min_{i}\tr(P_{U, i} Q_{U, i}).

(62)

Now, we use
\mathcal{L}
to construct a new learning scheme
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of qubit von
Neumann measurements in the following way.

Let
\Pi\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d})

be a projector onto

\ket{0},\ket{1}
and define an isometry matrix
V=\sum_{i=0}^{1}\outerproduct{i}{i}\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{d},\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Having access to unknown qubit von
Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U}
we may use it to implement von
Neumann measurement
\mathcal{P}_{U\oplus{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d-2}}
acting on
\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})
.
To do that, we take

\sigma\in\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{d})
and measure it in the following way

\outerproduct{0}{0}\otimes\Pi\sigma\Pi+\outerproduct{1}{1}\otimes\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}\tr(\sigma\proj{i})\outerproduct{i}{i}.

(63)

If on the first system we measure “
0
”, then we can project the state

V^{\dagger}(\Pi\sigma\Pi)V
into
\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{2})
and measure it by using

\mathcal{P}_{U}
. Otherwise, we do nothing. As a result we implemented the
measurement of the form

\sum_{i=0}^{1}\tr(V^\dagger\sigma V U \proj{i} U^\dagger)\outerproduct{i}{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}\tr(\sigma\proj{i})\outerproduct{i}{i}=\mathcal{P}_{U\oplus{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{d-2}}(\sigma).

(64)

During the retrieval stage, we project the input state
\rho\in\Omega(\mathcal{H}_{2})

into
V\rho V^{\dagger}
. Moreover, as the output of
\mathcal{L}
is a classical
label “
0
”,
\ldots
, “
d-1
”, to finalize the construction of
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
,
all the labels “
1
”,
\ldots
, “
d-1
” are returned as “
1
”.

For a given unitary matrix
U\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
and the learning
network
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
we may calculate

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathcal{P}_{U},\mathcal{Q}_{U})&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tr(VP_{U,0}V^\dagger Q_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, 0 } )+\tr(VP_{U,1}V^\dagger(\Id_d -
Q_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, 0 }) )\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{i}\tr(P_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, i} Q_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, i})+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\tr(P_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, 1} Q_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2},
0})\right)\\
&\geq\min_{i}\tr(P_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, i} Q_{U \oplus\Id_{d-2}, i})\geq dF_{d}-(d-1).\end{split}

(65)

Therefore, we get

dF_{d}-(d-1)\leq\mathcal{F}_{2}^{\text{avg}}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\leq F_{2}\leq 1-\Theta\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right),

(66)

which ends the proof.

Appendix D
Pretty good learning scheme

The pretty good learning scheme
\mathcal{L}_{PGLS}=\left(\sigma,\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N-1},\mathcal{R}\right)
consists
of the initial state
\sigma
, which is a tensor product of
N
copies of the
maximally entangled
state
\ket{ \omega}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left|\left.{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
, processing channels

\{\mathcal{C}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
that are responsible for majority voting (see
Section
IV.1
) and
the measurement
\mathcal{R}=\{R,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}-R\}
. To construct the effect
R
, we fix

N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and take

n=N_{0}-1
. Let us define

s_{n}(k,m)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\delta_{i+j-m}{k\choose i}{n-k\choose j}(-1)^{n-k-j},

(67)

being the convolution of binomial coefficients. We consider the effect
R
of
the form

\begin{split}R&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\outerproduct{R_k}{R_k},\quad\text{ such that}\ket{R_k}=\frac{\left|\left.{M_{k}}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}{||M_{k}||_{2}},\\
\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{2^{n+1}})\ni M_{k}&=\sum_{m=0}^{n+1}\frac{s_{n}(k,n-m)\ket{0}+s_{n}(k,n+1-m)\ket{1}}{\sqrt{n+1\choose m}}\bra{ D_m^{n+1}},\end{split}

(68)

for
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\ket{x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a\\
b\end{array}\right]
,
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, we have
M_{k}\ket{x}^{\otimes n+1}=(a+b)^{k}(a-b)^{n-k}\ket{x}
.

Proof.

Direct calculations reveal

\begin{split}M_{k}\ket{x}^{\otimes n+1}&=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+1\choose n-m}\cdot\frac{s_{n}(k,m)}{{n+1\choose n-m}}a^{m+1}b^{n-m}\\
\sum_{m=0}^{n}{n+1\choose n+1-m}\cdot\frac{s_{n}(k,m)}{{n+1\choose n+1-m}}a^{m}b^{n+1-m}\end{array}\right]=\sum_{m=0}^{n}s_{n}(k,m)a^{m}b^{n-m}\ket{x}\\
&=(a+b)^{k}(a-b)^{n-k}\ket{x}.\end{split}

(69)

∎

To prove that
R
is a valid effect, let us now define

M\coloneqq\left[s_{n}(k,m)\right]_{k,m=0}^{n}

(70)

and
a diagonal matrix

D\coloneqq\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{1}{{n\choose m}}\outerproduct{m}{m}.

(71)

Lemma 9
.

With the notation given above, it holds that
M^{2}=2^{n}{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{n+1}
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}=\text{span}\left([x^{k}]_{k=0}^{n}:x\in\mathbb{C}\right).
Let us take any vector of the form
\ket{x}\coloneqq[x^{k}]_{k=0}^{n}
, where
x\in\mathbb{C}
. We have

\begin{split}M\ket{x}&=\left[\sum_{m=0}^{n}s_{n}(k,m)x^{m}\right]_{k=0}^{n}=\left[(x+1)^{k}(x-1)^{n-k}\right]_{k=0}^{n}=(x-1)^{n}\left[\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}\right)^{k}\right]_{k=0}^{n}.\end{split}

(72)

Finally, we calculate

\begin{split}M^{2}\ket{x}&=(x-1)^{n}\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1}-1\right)^{n}\left[\left(\frac{\frac{x+1}{x-1}+1}{\frac{x+1}{x-1}-1}\right)^{k}\right]_{k=0}^{n}=2^{n}\ket{x}.\end{split}

(73)

∎

Lemma 10
.

Using the notation presented above, we have the following equation
MD=(MD)^{\top}
.

Proof.

We will show that
\bra{k}MD\ket{m}=\bra{m}MD\ket{k}
for any
m,k=0,\ldots,n
. W.l.o.g. we can assume that
k<m
. On the one hand, it holds
that

\begin{split}\bra{k}MD\ket{m}&=\frac{s_{n}(k,m)}{\binom{n}{m}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots,k\\
j=0,\ldots,n-k\\
i+j=m\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-j}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{j}}{\binom{n}{m}}=\sum_{i=\max\left(0,m+k-n\right)}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-m+i}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n-k}{m-i}}{\binom{n}{m}}\\
&=(-1)^{n-k-m}\sum_{i=\max\left(0,m+k-n\right)}^{k}(-1)^{i}\frac{k!m!(n-k)!(n-m)!}{n!i!(k-i)!(m-i)!(n-k-m+i)!}.\end{split}

(74)

On the other hand, we can calculate

\begin{split}\bra{m}MD\ket{k}&=\frac{s_{n}(m,k)}{\binom{n}{k}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=0,\ldots,m\\
j=0,\ldots,n-m\\
i+j=k\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{n-m-j}\binom{m}{i}\binom{n-m}{j}}{\binom{n}{k}}=\sum_{i=\max\left(0,m+k-n\right)}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-m+i}\binom{m}{i}\binom{n-m}{k-i}}{\binom{n}{k}}\\
&=(-1)^{n-k-m}\sum_{i=\max\left(0,m+k-n\right)}^{k}(-1)^{i}\frac{k!m!(n-k)!(n-m)!}{n!i!(k-i)!(m-i)!(n-k-m+i)!},\end{split}

(75)

which gives us the desired equality and completes the proof.

Lemma 11
.

The operator
R
defined in Eq. (
68
) satisfies
0\leq R\leq{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}_{2^{n+2}}
and

\mathcal{R}=\{R,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}-R\}

Proof.

Let us fix
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

n=N_{0}-1

9

, we

\begin{split}\tr\left(M_{k}^{\dagger}M_{k^{\prime}}\right)&=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{s_{n}(k,m)s_{n}(k^{\prime},m)}{{n+1\choose n-m}}+\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{s_{n}(k,m)s_{n}(k^{\prime},m)}{{n+1\choose n+1-m}}\\

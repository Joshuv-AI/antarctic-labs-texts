1.
Introduction and statements of results

Let
f_{n}(x)=1+x+\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
denote the
n

[
6
]
proved that the Galois group of

is
A_{n}
, the alternating group on
n
letters, if
4
divides
n
and is
S_{n}
, the symmetric group on
n
letters, otherwise. In 1987, Coleman
[
2
]
gave another proof of this result using the theory of Newton polygons. He also provided a simple proof of the irreducibility of
f_{n}(x)
over the field
{\mathbf{Q}}
of rational numbers. Let
K={\mathbf{Q}}(\theta)
be an algebraic number field and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}
denote the ring of algebraic integers of
K
. In the present paper, we would like to characterise all the primes dividing the index of the subgroup
{\mathbf{Z}}[\theta]
in
{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}
, where
\theta
a root of
f_{n}(x)
.

In 1878, Dedekind gave a simple criterion, known as Dedekind Criterion (cf.
[
1

,
[
3
]
), which provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a polynomial

to be satisfied so that

does not divide
[{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}:Z[\theta]]
, where
\theta
is a root of
f(x)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Dedekind Criterion)
Let

be an

as the minimal polynomial of
the algebraic integer
\theta

\mathbb{Q}.
Let

be a

\overline{f}(x)=\overline{g}_{1}(x)^{e_{1}}\ldots\overline{g}_{t}(x)^{e_{t}}
be

as a product of powers of
distinct irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},

with each

denote the polynomial

\frac{1}{p}(f(x)-g_{1}(x)^{e_{1}}\ldots g_{t}(x)^{e_{t}})
with

Then

does not divide

[{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}[\theta]]
if and only if for each

we have
either

or
\bar{g}_{i}(x)
does not divide

\overline{M}(x).

Using Dedekind Criterion, Jakhar, Khanduja and Sangwan have given necessary and sufficient conditions for a prime

Theorem 1.2

Let

Let

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
L={\mathbf{Q}}(\xi)
with
\xi
a root of an irreducible polynomial
g(x)
and
d_{L}
denote the discriminant of the field
L
, then it is well known that the discriminant
D_{g}
of
g(x)
and the index
[{\mathbf{Z}}_{L}:{\mathbf{Z}}[\xi]]
is connected by the following formula

(2.1)

D_{g}=[{\mathbf{Z}}_{L}:{\mathbf{Z}}[\xi]]^{2}d_{L}.

It is given in
[
2
]
that the discriminant of
f_{n}(x)=1+x+\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
is given by

(2.2)

D_{f_{n}}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}(n!)^{n}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By abuse of language, we take
f_{n}(x)
as

f_{n}(x)=x^{n}+nx^{n-1}+\frac{n!}{(n-2)!}x^{n-2}+\cdots+\frac{n!}{2}x^{2}+n!x+n!\in{\mathbf{Z}}[x].

Keeping in mind (
2.2
), it is easy to check that the absolute value of the discriminant of

is given by
|D_{f_{n}}|=(n!)^{n}.
In view of (
2.1
), we see that if a prime
p
does not divide
n!
, then
p
does not divide
[{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}:{\mathbf{Z}}[\theta]]
. So assume that
p
is a divisor of
n!
. Suppose that
i

0\leq i\leq n-2
is the smallest index such that

. We see that

f_{n}(x)\equiv x^{n}+nx^{n-1}+\cdots+\frac{n!}{(n-i)!}x^{n-i}\equiv x^{n-i}(x^{i}+nx^{i-1}+\cdots+\frac{n!}{(n-i)!})\mod p.

Note that
p
can not divide
i
, because if
p
divides
i
, then in view of
p|(n-i)
, we have
p|n
, which contradicts the definition of
i
. So keeping in mind that
p\nmid i
, the polynomial
x^{i}+\bar{n}x^{i-1}+\cdots+\overline{\frac{n!}{(n-i)!}}
belonging to
{\mathbf{Z}}/p{\mathbf{Z}}[x]
is a separable polynomial. Hence applying Dedekind Criterion, we see that

does not divide
[{\mathbf{Z}}_{K}:{\mathbf{Z}}[\theta]]
if and only if
x
does not divide
\overline{M}(x)
, where

is given by

M(x)=\frac{1}{p}[\frac{n!}{(n-i-1)!}x^{n-i-1}+\cdots+\frac{n!}{2}x^{2}+n!x+n!].

Thus

does not divide

. This completes the proof of the theorem.

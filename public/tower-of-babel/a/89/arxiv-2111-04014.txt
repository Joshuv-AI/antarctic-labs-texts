Construction of a new three boson non-hermitian Hamiltonian associated to deformed Higgs algebra : real eigenvalues and Partial 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 {\mathcal{PT}} -symmetry
Arindam Chakraborty

Abstract
A γ \gamma -deformed version of 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{su}(2) algebra has been obtained from a bi-orthogonal system of vectors in 𝐂 𝟐 \bf{C^{2}} . Fusion of Jordan-Schwinger realization of complexified 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( 2 ) \mathfrak{su}(2) with Dyson-Maleev representation gives a 3-boson realization of Higgs algebra of cubic polynomial type. The non-hermitian Hamiltonian thus obtained is found to have real eigenvalues and eigen states with symmetry induced orthogonality. The notion of partial 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 {\mathcal{PT}} -symmetry (henceforth ∂ 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \partial_{\mathcal{PT}} ) has been introduced as a characteristic feature of these multi-boson realizations. The Hamiltonian along with its eigenstates have been studied in the light of ∂ 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \partial_{\mathcal{PT}} -symmetry. The possibility of ∂ 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \partial_{\mathcal{PT}} -symmetry breaking is also discussed. The deformation parameter γ \gamma plays a crucial role in the entire formulation and non-trivially modifies the eigenfunctions under consideration.

1
Introduction

The present communication attempts to discuss three interrelated issues regarding multi-boson realization of deformed linear and non-linear (polynomial) algebras.

I. Implementing the idea of boson realization to a deformed version of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
algebra involving non-hermitian generators obtained from a bi-orthogonal system of vectors. Subsequent complexification of the said algebra with the introduction of a new set of ladder operators gives the so called spectrum generating algebra (SGA).

II. Obtaining a version of deformed Higgs algebra from fusion of pair of such SGAs. As a consequence a 3-boson non-hermitian Hamiltonian with real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenstates becomes available.

III. Understanding the non-hermitian Hamiltonian and its eigenstates in the light of
\partial_{\mathcal{PT}}
-symmetry.

The present article stems from a few of recent studies done by Brody
[
brody16
,
brody14
]
regarding the construction of non-hermitian su(2) generators with real eigenvalues starting from a bi-orthogonal set of vectors. It has been claimed
[
brody14
]
that a parallel formalism of quantum mechanics, at least in the framework of finite dimensional Hilbert space, is possible even for relaxing the requirement of hermiticity of the observable. In our case a bi-orthogonal system has been formulated starting from
a pair of orthonormal bases and subsequent construction of a new set of vectors following
[
baga15
]
with the help of a suitably chosen transformation. Such a bi-orthogonal system of vectors can be used to construct a set of generators of a Lie algebra which in the present
setting comes out as a parametric deformation of su(2) algebra. The related Jordan-Schwinger
operators
\{J_{0}^{\gamma},J_{\pm}^{\gamma}\}
have been constructed where the operator
J_{0}^{\gamma}
becomes a non-hermitian operator.

It is interesting to note that the complexification of the deformed algebra is not spectrum generating in general for all non-zero values of
\gamma
. However, a
\gamma
-deformed version of complexification is possible at the level of Jordan-Schwinger realization which is spectrum generating in character and resembles the corresponding realization of su(2) for
\gamma=0
.

Fusion of two such algebras
[

]
can produce the so called Higgs algebra which is one of the earliest candidates of Polynomial Angular Momentum Algebra(PAMA)
[
ruan06

higgs79
,
debergh98
]

[

]
to establish the existence of hidden symmetry for coulomb and oscillator potential in a space of constant curvature and also understood as a second order approximation of
su_{q}(2)
[
zhed92
]
. Here, we have defined a deformed version of Higgs algebra and considered the fusion of
\mathfrak{su}_{\gamma}(2)
and a deformed version of Dyson-Maleev Realization. To the best of our knowledge such a
three boson realization of Higgs algebra
is not available in literature.
The related commutation relations result to a 3-boson Hamiltonians involving central elements of the algebras, all of which are non-hermitian operators. A theorem has been proposed to show the possibility of a
block-diagonal representation
of the Hamiltonian acting on a homogeneous polynomial space of three indeterminates. The present article considers only two such spaces with degree of homogeneity n=2 and 3. The eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian are found to be real. The orthogonality of the eigenstates are discussed (in a typical Bargmann-Fock type setting) in view of a
symmetry induced indefinite inner-product
.

Since the identification of non-hermitian operators with real spectrum and Bender and Boettcher’s

In a recent article by Beygi et. al.

the issue of partial

The interpretation of all such symmetries can be understood both at the level of Hamiltonian as well as in

It is also observed that in the present situation a choice of eigenvector space is possible where the states can be classified into symmetry conforming, symmetry breaking and symmetry adopting states.

2
Jordan-Schwinger map from bi-orthogonal system

Given a pair of orthogonal vectors

\{|u_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
(-1)^{j-1}\end{array}\right):j=1,2\}
,
\langle u_{j}|u_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
one can construct conventional
\mathfrak{su}(2)
generators (which are hermitian operators in the usual sense of inner-product in
\mathbf{C^{2}}
) :

\sigma_{m}=\frac{i^{m+1}}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}c^{(m)}_{jk}|u_{j}\rangle\langle u_{k}|:m=1,2,3.
. Here,
c_{jk}^{(1)}=(-1)^{j}\delta_{jk}
and
c_{jk}^{(3)}=(-1)^{j}c_{jk}^{(2)}=(1-(-1)^{j}c_{jk}^{(1)})
. The generators follow the commutation :
[\sigma_{l},\sigma_{m}]=i\sum_{n=1}^{3}\epsilon_{lmn}\sigma_{n}
. Now considering boson operators
\{a_{j},a^{\dagger}_{j}:j=1,2\}
with
[a_{j},a_{k}^{\dagger}]-\delta_{jk}=[a_{j},a_{k}]=[a_{j}^{\dagger},a_{k}^{\dagger}]=0
, the following Jordan-Schwinger (JS) bi-linear map is obtained as a Lie algebra isomorphism
[
bieden81
]

J_{m}=\frac{i^{m-1}}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}c_{jk}^{(4-m)}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k}:m=1,2,3.

(1)

Defining
J_{\pm}=J_{1}\pm iJ_{2}
and using
[a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\beta}
,
a^{\dagger}_{\mu}a_{\nu}]=a^{\dagger}_{\alpha}a_{\nu}\delta_{\beta\mu}-a^{\dagger}_{\mu}a_{\beta}\delta_{\alpha\nu}
we obtain a spectrum generating algebra(SGA)(taking
J_{3}=J_{0}
):

[J_{0},J_{\pm}]=\pm J_{\pm}

and
[J_{+},J_{-}]=2J_{0}
.

Now, it is possible to extend this procedure in the non-hermitian regime starting from the following definition of bi-orthogonal vectors:

Definition 1
.

Two pairs of vectors
\{|\phi_{j}\rangle:j=1,2\}
and
\{|\chi_{j}\rangle:j=1,2\}
are called bi-orthogonal if
\langle\phi_{j}|\chi_{k}\rangle=0\forall j\neq k
.

Such a bi-orthogonal system can be obtained from straightforward verification of the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Given any pair of vectors

\{|v_{j}\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{j}^{(1)}\\
c_{j}^{(2)}\end{array}\right):j=1,2\}\in\mathbf{C^{2}}
two pairs of vectors
|\phi_{j}\rangle=\omega_{0}T|v_{j}\rangle
and
|\chi_{j}\rangle=(T^{-1})^{\dagger}|v_{j}\rangle
constitute a bi-orthogonal system under the action of a transformation
T=(\cos\frac{\theta}{2}){\bf 1}_{2}+2(\cos\frac{\phi}{2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2})\sigma_{1}-2(\sin\frac{\phi}{2}\sin\frac{\theta}{2})\sigma_{2}
with
\omega_{0}=\cos\theta
provided
\langle v_{j}|v_{k}\rangle=(c_{j}^{(1)})^{\star}c_{k}^{(1)}+(c_{j}^{(2)})^{\star}c_{k}^{(2)}=0\forall j\neq k
.

Proof.

As
T=T^{\dagger}
with the present sense of inner-product
\langle\phi_{j}|\chi_{k}\rangle=\langle v_{j}|\omega_{0}T(T^{-1})^{\dagger}|v_{k}\rangle=\langle v_{j}|\omega_{0}T(T^{\dagger})^{-1}|v_{k}\rangle=\omega_{0}\langle v_{j}|v_{k}\rangle
. Hence the theorem follows from the definition-1.
∎

Now, taking
|v_{j}\rangle=|u_{j}\rangle
,
\phi=\pi
and
\gamma=\sqrt{1-\omega_{0}^{2}}=\sin\theta
a deformed algebra
\mathfrak{su}_{\gamma}(2)
is obtained with the generators :
\sigma_{m}^{\gamma}=\frac{i^{m+1}}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}\frac{c_{jk}^{(m)}}{\omega_{0}^{\delta_{m2}}}|\phi_{j}\rangle\langle\chi_{k}|:m=1,2,3
with the commutator
[\sigma_{l}^{\gamma},\sigma_{m}^{\gamma}]=i\sum_{n=1}^{3}\epsilon_{lmn}(1-\gamma^{2}\delta_{n2})\sigma_{n}^{\gamma}
. The corresponding JS-map is given by
J_{m}^{\gamma}=\frac{i^{m-1}}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{2}[c_{jk}^{(4-m)}+i^{m}(1-\delta_{m2})\gamma c_{jk}^{(m)}]a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{k}:m=1,2,3.
.

Introducing a new set of deformed ladder operators with exponent
p
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
(set of real numbers),

J_{\pm}^{\gamma}=\sum_{r=0}^{1}(\pm i)^{r}\omega_{0}^{r-p}J^{\gamma}_{r+1}
and considering
J_{3}^{\gamma}=J_{0}^{\gamma}

the following spectrum generating algebra becomes possible

[J_{0}^{\gamma},J_{\pm}^{\gamma}]=\pm\omega_{0}(\gamma)J_{\pm}^{\gamma}

[J_{+}^{\gamma},J_{-}^{\gamma}]=2\omega_{0}^{-2p+1}(\gamma)J_{0}^{\gamma}.

(2)

It is to be noted that
J_{0}^{\gamma}
is no longer hermitian and
(J^{\gamma}_{+})^{\dagger}\neq J^{\gamma}_{-}
. Casimir of this algebra
{\mathcal{C_{J}^{\gamma}}}=\omega_{0}^{-2}J_{0}^{\gamma}(J_{0}^{\gamma}\pm\omega_{0})+\omega_{0}^{2p-2}J^{\gamma}_{\mp}J_{\pm}^{\gamma}
.

Three-boson realization of Higgs algebra

Definition 2
.

A
\gamma
-deformed Higgs algebra
\mathfrak{H^{\gamma}}
in present sense involving the generators
\{H^{\gamma}_{0},H^{\gamma}_{\pm}\}
is defined by the following commutation relations

[H^{\gamma}_{0},H^{\gamma}_{\pm}]=\pm\omega_{0}H^{\gamma}_{\pm}

(3)

and

[H^{\gamma}_{+},H^{\gamma}_{-}]=4\left[\alpha\omega_{0}H^{\gamma}_{0}+\frac{\beta}{\omega_{0}}(H^{\gamma}_{0})^{3}\right]

(4)

where,
\alpha
and
\beta
are functions of central elements of the algebra and
\alpha=\frac{\beta}{8}-\mathcal{H}
.

Remark 1
.

Infact Higgs algebra has been introduced in the context of non-relativistic Kepler problem in spaces with constant curvature
\pm\beta
corresponding to hyperboloid and sphere respectively.
\mathcal{H}
represents the Hamiltonian of the system. It is important to note that all the operators in definition-2 commute with
\mathcal{H}
.

The function
\alpha
represents the Hamiltonian of a system and being a central element all the generators commute with it by definition.

\mathfrak{su}_{\gamma}(2)
and a deformed Dyson-Maleev realization.

A three boson realization can be obtained from the fusion of
\mathfrak{su}_{\gamma}(2)
and Dyson-Maleev single boson representation (
\mathfrak{dm}

\displaystyle H^{\gamma}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(J_{0}^{\gamma}-M^{\gamma}_{0})

\displaystyle H^{\gamma}_{\pm}=sJ_{\pm}^{\gamma}M^{\gamma}_{\mp}

(5)

and

L_{0}^{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}(J_{0}^{\gamma}+M^{\gamma}_{0})

(6)

where,

,

and

The operators in equation-
3.1

3

4

3.2

{\mathcal{H}_{1}\doteq\mathcal{H}_{1}(a_{j},a_{j}^{\dagger}\mid j=1,2,3;\omega_{0})}

The Hamiltonian has the following expression (taking
\beta=1

\mathcal{H}_{1}=\omega_{0}^{-2}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}B_{ij}a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{j}+\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{3}V_{ijkl}a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{j}a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{l}\right)+\rm{const.}

Theorem 2
.

\mathcal{H}_{1}
has a block diagonal representation in the basis
\{|n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle\Leftrightarrow\zeta_{1}^{n_{1}}\zeta_{2}^{n_{2}}\zeta_{3}^{n_{3}}:n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}=n\}
of the homogeneous polynomial space of degree
n
with
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{Z^{+}}\cup\{0\}
. For a given
n
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}B^{(n)}_{(j+1)\times(j+1)}
, where
B^{(n)}_{j+1\times j+1}
is a block diagonal matrix of order
(j+1)\times(j+1)
.

Proof.

It is obvious to note that for a given
n
the dimension of the eigenvector space of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is
\frac{1}{2}(n+1)(n+2)
. Now the action of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
on a typical basis element

can be given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}|n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}^{-2}[A_{(0,0,0)}|n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\rangle+A_{(2,-2,0)}|n_{1}+2,n_{2}-2,n_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle+A_{(-2,2,0)}|n_{1}-2,n_{2}+2,n_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle+A_{(1,-1,0)}|n_{1}+1,n_{2}-1,n_{3}\rangle+A_{(-1,1,0)}|n_{1}-1,n_{2}+1,n_{3}\rangle]

(8)

where,

\displaystyle A_{(0,0,0)}=\left[\frac{1}{2}(n_{1}-n_{2})+\omega_{0}(c-n_{3})\right]^{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}(n_{1}+n_{2}+2n_{1}n_{2})

\displaystyle A_{(2,-2,0)}=A_{(-2,2,0)}=-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle A_{(1,-1,0)}=\frac{i\gamma}{2}n_{2}(n_{1}-n_{2}+1+2\omega_{0}(c-n_{3}))

\displaystyle A_{(-1,1,0)}=\frac{i\gamma}{2}n_{1}(n_{1}-n_{2}-1+2\omega_{0}(c-n_{3}))

(9)

Now, in view of equation-
3.2
it is observed that the index
n_{3}
is not modified on the right hand side under the action of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
. Hence, for a fixed value of

the invariant subspace spanned by
\{\zeta_{1}^{n_{1}}\zeta_{2}^{n_{2}}:n_{1},n_{2}=0\dots n-n_{3}\}

representing a block of same dimension. As the largest value of

1

∎

ℒ \mathscr{L} -invariants of Artin motives
Mladen Dimitrov, Alexandre Maksoud

Abstract
. We compute Benois ℒ \mathscr{L} -invariants of weight 1 1 cuspforms and of their adjoint representations and show how this extends Gross’ p p -adic regulator to Artin motives which are not critical in the sense of Deligne. Benois’ construction depends on the choice of a regular submodule which is well understood when the representation is p p -regular, as it then amounts to the choice of a “motivic” p p -refinement. The situation is dramatically different in the p p -irregular case, where the regular submodules are parametrized by a flag variety and thus depend on continuous parameters. We are nevertheless able to show in some examples, how Hida theory and the geometry of the eigencurve can be used to detect a finite number of choices of arithmetic and “mixed-motivic” significance.

Given a field

its absolute Galois group. When

\rI_{F}\subset\rG_{F}

For any rational prime

\iota_{\ell}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

We also fix an embedding

2.1.
Bloch–Kato Selmer groups

2.1.1.
Local and global Selmer groups

Let
V
be a
p
-adic representation of
\rG_{F}
.

If
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, the local Bloch–Kato subgroup is defined as

\rH^{1}_{f}(F,V)=\begin{cases}\ker\left(\rH^{1}(F,V)\to\rH^{1}(\rI_{F},V)\right)&\text{ , if } \ell\neq p,\\
\ker\left(\rH^{1}(F,V)\to\rH^{1}(F,B_{\mathrm{crys}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}V)\right)&\text{ , if } \ell=p,\end{cases}

where
B_{\mathrm{crys}}
is the Fontaine ring of crystalline periods (see
[
10
, §3]
).

If
F
is a number field and
S
a finite set of finite places of
F
, then the Selmer group is defined as

\rH^{1}_{S}(F,V)=\ker\left(\rH^{1}(F,V)\to\bigoplus_{v\notin S}\frac{\rH^{1}(F_{v},V)}{\rH^{1}_{f}(F_{v},V)}\right).

Here, the map
\rH^{1}(F,V)\to\rH^{1}(F_{v},V)
is given by the restriction of cocycles to
\rG_{F_{v}}
and shall be denoted by
\mathrm{loc}_{v}
.
We will be particularly interested in the Bloch–Kato Selmer group

\rH^{1}_{f}(F,V)=\rH^{1}_{\varnothing}(F,V)
and the
group
\rH^{1}_{f,p}(F,V)=\rH^{1}_{\{p\}}(F,V)\supset\rH^{1}_{f}(F,V)

consisting of cocycles that are unramified outside
p
.

2.1.2.
Kummer Theory

When
V=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)
, Kummer theory provides an explicit description of the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups. For all integers

n\geqslant 1
, the Kummer map
\kappa_{F,n}:F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{p^{n}}\to\rH^{1}(F,\mu_{p^{n}})
is an isomorphism sending

to the class of the cocycle given by
g\mapsto g(a^{1/p^{n}})/a^{1/p^{n}}
, where
a^{1/p^{n}}\in\overline{F}^{\times}
is any
p^{n}
-th root of

(see
[
33
, Chap. X, §3.b)]
). It is clear from the definition that
\kappa_{F,n}
is functorial in
F
and Galois equivariant. Taking an inverse limit on

and extending scalars to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
yields a Galois equivariant isomorphism

\kappa_{F}:\widehat{F^{\times}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}\rH^{1}(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)),

where
\widehat{A}=\varprojlim_{n}A/A^{p^{n}}
denotes the
p
-adic completion of an abelian group

. Moreover, when
F
is local,
\kappa_{F}
restricts to an isomorphism

\kappa_{F}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}\rH^{1}_{f}(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)),

(see e.g.
[
10
, Example 3.9]
).
Note that if
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and
\ell\neq p
, then
\rH^{1}_{f}(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1))
vanishes, as

\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
is finite. In contrast, for a finite extension
F
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,

\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}}
is the subgroup
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{1}
of principal units of
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
.
Finally, when
F
is a number field and
S
a finite set of finite places of
F
, the global Kummer map restricts to a Galois equivariant isomorphism

(1)

\kappa_{F}:\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{F}\left[\tfrac{1}{S}\right]^{\times}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}\rH^{1}_{S}(F,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)),

where
\mathcal{O}_{F}\left[\tfrac{1}{S}\right]^{\times}
denotes the group of
S
-units of
F
.

Our next task is to give an explicit description of Bloch–Kato Selmer groups for (duals of) Artin representations.

.

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

, or if we assume the Weak

Proof.

(i) follows from Lemma

.

(ii) By definition

, for

∎

.

Lemma 2.13

Let

. The
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-linear map

(see §

∎

Let us fix a basis

of

Fix also a basis

(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d^{+}},\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\kappa^{\prime}_{e})

Our choices allow us to construct global units and

•

Theorem 2.15
.

Assume

is non-zero, so that the

-submodule

-invariant associated with

A^{-}&B^{-}\end{pmatrix}}{\det A^{+}\cdot\det O^{-}}

where
A^{\pm}
,
B^{\pm}
and
O^{-}
are the matrices defined in Notation

.

Proof.

We first define convenient bases
\widetilde{\kappa}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\kappa}_{e}
and
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{e}
of
\rH^{1}(D,V)
and
D_{1}
respectively and then express the matrices of
j_{D,f}
and
j_{D,c}
in these bases in terms of
A^{\pm},B^{\pm}
and
O^{-}
.

Let us complete
(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d^{+}},\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\kappa^{\prime}_{e})
into a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-basis
(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{d^{+}},\kappa^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\kappa^{\prime}_{f})
of

such that the image of
(\kappa^{\prime}_{e+1},\ldots,\kappa^{\prime}_{f})
under the surjective map in Lemma

is the dual basis of
(w^{+}_{1},\ldots,w^{+}_{f-e})
of

and

Take

and write

where

and

and

The matrices of

and

is invertible.

u_{0}
in
U_{H}^{(p)}
generates
U_{H}^{(p)}/U_{H}
as a
G
-module and can be used to determine the
\kappa^{\prime}_{j}
’s. In contrast,
determining a basis of
\rH^{1}(D,V)
would involve linear equations with
possibly transcendental coefficients.

The situation is particularly nice when

d^{+}=e=f=1
and
W^{+}=W^{\Frob_{p}=\beta}
. Let
e_{\rho}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G]
be the idempotent attached to
\rho
and define
e_{W^{+}},e_{W_{1}}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[G_{p}]
as

e_{W^{+}}=\sum_{i=1}^{|G_{p}|}\beta^{-i}\cdot\Frob_{p}^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{W_{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{|G_{p}|}\Frob_{p}^{i}.

We can obtain
\varepsilon_{1,1}^{\pm}
from
\varepsilon_{0}
by letting
\varepsilon_{1,1}^{+}=e_{W^{+}}\cdot e_{\rho}\cdot\varepsilon_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{1,1}^{-}=e_{W_{1}}\cdot e_{\rho}\cdot\varepsilon_{0}
and similarly we can take
u_{1,1}^{+}=e_{W^{+}}\cdot e_{\rho}\cdot u_{0}
and
u_{1,1}^{-}=e_{W_{1}}\cdot e_{\rho}\cdot u_{0}
.
Such numerical computations have been performed for certain
3
-dimensional representations in
[
16
]
.

2.5.
The dual
\mathscr{L}
-invariant

In this section, we give an alternative expression of the
\mathscr{L}
-invariant that derives from a dual construction given in
[
5
, §2.3]
which will be used in §
3.4
. Perrin-Riou’s definition of regularity actually applies to
D_{\mathrm{crys}}(W)
and one checks that a submodule
D\subseteq D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
is regular if and only if its orthogonal complement
D^{\perp}
for the natural pairing
D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)\times D_{\mathrm{crys}}(W)\to D_{\mathrm{crys}}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1))
is a regular submodule of
D_{\mathrm{crys}}(W)
. We now fix a regular submodule
D
of
D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
and we keep the same notations as in the previous sections.

By Class Field Theory and Frobenius reciprocity one has

\rH^{1}_{f,p}(W)=\ker\left(\Hom_{G_{p}}\left(E^{\times},W\right)\longrightarrow\Hom_{G}({\mathcal{O}_{H}\left[\tfrac{1}{p}\right]^{\times}},W)\right).

Consider the linear subspace
\rH^{1}(D^{\perp},W)
of
\rH^{1}_{f,p}(W)
,
consisting of elements
\kappa
such that
\kappa(E^{\times})\subset W^{+}+W_{1}
. Computations dual to those in Section
2.3
show that
\rH^{1}(D^{\perp},W)
is the module introduced by Benois-Horte in
[
5
, §2.3.6]
and that it has dimension
e
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
For any
\kappa\in\rH^{1}(D^{\perp},W)
we denote by
\kappa^{-}
its composition with the
projection
(W^{+}+W_{1})\twoheadrightarrow W_{1}^{-}
and we put

j_{D^{\perp},f}(\kappa)=\kappa^{-}(p),\qquad j_{D^{\perp},c}(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa^{-}(1+p)}{\log_{p}(1+p)}.

This defines two linear maps
j_{D^{\perp},f},j_{D^{\perp},c}:\rH^{1}(W,D^{\perp})\to W_{1}^{-}
such that
j_{D^{\perp},c}
is an isomorphism.
When specialized to our setting, the construction of the dual
\mathscr{L}
-invariant
\mathscr{L}(W,D^{\perp})
given in
[
5
, Definition 2.3.7]
, leads to the following formula:

\mathscr{L}(W,D^{\perp})=(-1)^{e}\det\left(j_{D^{\perp},f}\circ\left(j_{D^{\perp},c}\right)^{-1}|W_{1}^{-}\right).

The following proposition is
[
5
, Prop. 2.3.8]
. Note that the sign
(-1)^{e}
corresponds to the sign in the conjectural functional equation for
p
-adic
L
-functions, as explained in detail in
[
3
, §2.2.6, §2.3.5]
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Assume that
D
is a regular submodule of
D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
. Then

\mathscr{L}(W,D^{\perp})=(-1)^{e}\mathscr{L}(V,D).

3.
Examples of computation of
\mathscr{L}
-invariant

3.1.
Gross’
p
-adic regulator

We assume throughout this paragraph that
d^{+}=0
, i.e. any complex conjugation in
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
acts on
W
as
-1
. Then the Galois extension
H/\mathbb{Q}
cut out by
\varrho
is a CM field. Conversely, if
\varrho
is irreducible and cuts out a CM field
H
, then
any complex conjugation defines a non-trivial central element in
G=\Gal(H/\mathbb{Q})
which is necessarily scalar, hence equal to
-1
, by Schur’s lemma. The unique
p
-refinement
W^{+}=\{0\}
of
W
is automatically regular
and the corresponding regular submodule is
D=D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
. Note that we have

e=\dim\rH^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W).

One may attach to
\varrho
a
p
-adic
L
-function by using Deligne-Ribet’s construction
[
19
]
together with Brauer’s induction theorem (see
[
24
]
). Gross
[
25
]
has proposed a conjectural formula for the leading term of this
p
-adic
L
-function at
s=0
in terms of a certain
\mathscr{L}
-invariant which is nowadays referred to as Gross’s
p
-adic regulator. We briefly recall its definition and relate it to Benois’
\mathscr{L}
-invariant.

Let
Y
be the
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-vector space
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cdot\mathfrak{p}
, where the direct sum runs over the set
S_{p}(H)
of all primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
H
above
p
. We denote by
X\subset Y
the kernel of the augmentation map
\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}a_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}a_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Note that
G
naturally acts on both
X
and
Y
(see
e.g.

[
34
, Chap. I §3]
), and
\Hom_{G}(W,X)=\Hom_{G}(W,Y)
as
Y=X\oplus\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
For
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}(H)
, Gross
[
25
, (1.8)]
defines the local absolute value of
\alpha\in H_{\mathfrak{p}}
by letting
||\alpha||_{\mathfrak{p},p}=p^{-\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)f_{p}}\textbf{N}_{H_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\alpha)
, where
f_{p}
is the residual degree of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\textbf{N}_{H_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is the norm map for the field extension
H_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Gross’
p
-adic regulator map is given by

\lambda_{p}:U_{H}^{(p)}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad u\mapsto\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}(H)}\log_{p}(||u||_{\mathfrak{p},p})\cdot\mathfrak{p},

whose image lies in
X
by the product formula.
Gross introduces another
G
-equivariant map

o_{p}:U_{H}^{(p)}\longrightarrow Y,\qquad u\mapsto\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in S_{p}(H)}f_{p}\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(u)\cdot\mathfrak{p},

(see
loc. cit.
, (1.12) and (1.20)). Since
\dim\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H})=d^{+}=0
, the map
\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H}^{(p)})\to\Hom_{G}(W,Y)=\Hom_{G}(W,X)
induced by
o_{p}
is injective, and it is in fact an isomorphism of
e
-dimensional
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-vector spaces.
Gross’ regulator for
W^{*}=\Hom_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}(W,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is then defined as

R_{p}(W^{*})=\det\left(\lambda_{p}\circ o_{p}^{-1}\ \big|\ \Hom_{G}(W,X)\right).

Here, we still denote by
\lambda_{p}
and
o_{p}
the maps obtained by post-composing by
\lambda_{p}
and
o_{p}
respectively. Note that, under the canonical identification
(W^{*}\otimes-)^{G}=\Hom_{G}(W,-)
, these maps coincide with the maps
1\otimes\lambda_{p},1\otimes o_{p}\ \colon\ (W^{*}\otimes U_{H}^{(p)})^{G}\to(W^{*}\otimes X)^{G}
appearing in Gross’ definition (
loc. cit.
, (2.10)).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D=D_{\mathrm{crys}}(V)
. One has
\mathscr{L}(V,D)=(-1)^{e}\cdot R_{p}(W^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{0}\in S_{p}(H)
be the prime above
p
determined by
\iota_{p}
and put
W_{1}=\rH^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},W)
. Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{\Hom_{G}(W,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\Hom_{G_{p}}(W,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom(W_{1},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}),}
\textstyle{\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H}^{(p)})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{p}}
\scriptstyle{o_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\log_{p}}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ord}_{p}}

where the map
\pi
is induced by the projection
\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}a_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\mathfrak{p}\in X\mapsto a_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}
. By Frobenius reciprocity
\pi
is an isomorphism because
W
does not contain the trivial representation. We claim that the above diagram becomes commutative when we multiply the maps
\log_{p}
and
\mathrm{ord}_{p}
by the same factor
f_{p}
, that is, the maps
\pi\circ\lambda_{p}
and
\pi\circ o_{p}
respectively coincide with
f_{p}\cdot\log_{p}
and
f_{p}\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{p}
.
Indeed, if
\kappa\in\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H}^{(p)})
, then
\kappa_{|W_{1}}
takes values in
(U_{H}^{(p)})^{G_{p}}
, hence
\iota_{p}\circ\kappa_{|W_{1}}
lands in
(H_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}^{\times}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})^{G_{p}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Our claim then follows from the fact that the map
\log_{p}\circ||\cdot||_{\mathfrak{p}_{0},p}
coincides with
f_{p}\cdot\log_{p}
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
. This proves that

R_{p}(W^{*})
is equal to the determinant of the map
\log_{p}\circ\mathrm{ord}_{p}^{-1}
acting on
\Hom(W_{1},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. In terms of Notation
2.14
, it is then clear that
R_{p}(W^{*})=\det(B^{-})\cdot\det(O^{-})^{-1}
, so
\mathscr{L}(V,D)=(-1)^{e}\cdot R_{p}(W^{*})
by Theorem
2.15
.
∎

3.2.
Weight

Given a weight

Since

The

-regular case

Assume that

(see Definition

We let

Assuming

The

3.2.3.
The CM case

\psi

3.3.
Adjoint of a weight
1

p
-regular cuspform

Let
\varrho
be the adjoint of the Artin representation
\varrho_{f}
attached by Deligne and Serre to a weight one cuspform
f
of level relatively prime to
p
. As in the introduction, we assume that
\varrho_{f}
has
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients and we view it via
\iota_{p}
as a
p
-adic representation.
Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the eigenvalues of
\varrho_{f}(\Frob_{p})
and assume that
\alpha\neq\pm\beta
(so
f
is in particular
p
-regular). We let
(w_{1},w_{\beta/\alpha},w_{\alpha/\beta})
be a basis of
W
on which
\Frob_{p}
acts with the eigenvalue prescribed in the index and we denote by
(w^{*}_{1},w^{*}_{\beta/\alpha},w^{*}_{\alpha/\beta})
its dual basis. The
p
-adic realization
V
of the motive
M=(\ad^{0}(f))(1)
has a unique decomposition as
\rG_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-modules

V=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)\cdot w^{*}_{1}\bigoplus\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)\cdot w^{*}_{\beta/\alpha}\bigoplus\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(1)\cdot w^{*}_{\alpha/\beta}
.

We now apply the results of §
2.4
.
Since one has
d^{+}=e=f=1
and
\varrho(\Frob_{p})
acts with pairwise distinct eigenvalues, the line
W^{+}
generated by
w_{\beta/\alpha}
is a motivic
p
-refinement (so it is regular by Proposition
2.9
(iii)) and
W_{1}
is a line with basis
w_{1}
. Let
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
be a
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-basis of
\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H}^{(p)})
such that
\kappa
takes values in
U_{H}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
w_{\beta/\alpha},w_{1}
(resp.
\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
) belong to the canonical
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-structure of
W
(resp. of
\rH^{1}_{f,p}(V)
described in §
2.2.1
). By Theorem
2.15
, Benois’
\mathscr{L}
-invariant for the regular submodule
D=\Hom(W/W^{+},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
is given by

\mathscr{L}(V,D)=\frac{\log_{p}(\varepsilon_{1})\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})-\log_{p}(\varepsilon_{\beta/\alpha})\log_{p}(u_{1})}{\log_{p}(\varepsilon_{\beta/\alpha})\mathrm{ord}_{p}(u_{1})},

where
\varepsilon_{1}=\kappa(w_{1})
,
\varepsilon_{\beta/\alpha}=\kappa(w_{\beta/\alpha})
,
u_{1}=\kappa^{\prime}(w_{1})
and
u_{\beta/\alpha}=\kappa^{\prime}(w_{\beta/\alpha})
.

Let us assume that
\varrho_{f}
is residually irreducible and
p
-distinguished, the latter condition meaning that
\alpha
and
\beta
are distinct modulo the maximal ideal of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. We suppose that
\varrho_{f}
is not induced by a character of a real quadratic field in which
p
splits, hence by
[
2
]
the eigencurve is étale over the weight space at both
p
-stabilizations
f_{\alpha}
and
f_{\beta}
of
f
.
Using the automorphic deformation of
f_{\alpha}
, Hida defined an analytic
\mathscr{L}
-invariant
\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(f_{\alpha}))
for which Darmon, Lauder and Rotger proposed in
[
16
, Conj. 1.2]
a conjectural description in terms of the
p
-adic logarithms of
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{\beta/\alpha},u_{1},u_{\beta/\alpha}
(see also
[
30
, §5.1]
). In light of our computation of Benois’
\mathscr{L}
-invariant in this setting, the main theorem of Rivero and Rotger
[
30
, Thm. A’]
can then be recast as

\mathscr{L}(V,D)=\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(f_{\alpha}))\mod\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}.

3.4.
Adjoint of a weight
1

p
-irregular CM cuspform

We consider the case of the Artin motive
W=\ad^{0}\varrho_{f}
, where
f
is a weight one cuspform
irregular at
p
and having CM by an imaginary quadratic field
K
in which
p
splits.
Letting
\varrho_{f}=\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\psi
, where
\psi
is a finite order Hecke character of
K
, the above assumptions translate into the fact that the anti-cyclotomic (or ring class) character
\varphi=\psi/\bar{\psi}
is non-trivial, but is locally trivial at (places above)
p
. Let
H
denote the fixed field of
\ker(\varphi)
.

3.4.1.
Regular submodules

Fix a dihedral basis of
\varrho_{f}
as in §
3.2.3
, so that
\varrho_{f|\rG_{K}}=\psi\oplus\bar{\psi}
. This choice yields a matrix representation
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&-a\end{pmatrix}
of
\ad^{0}\varrho_{f}
with basis
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}
given by

w_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
and

w_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
,
w_{3}=\tau(w_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

Moreover, this basis is adapted to the
\rG_{\mathbb{Q}}
-decomposition
\ad^{0}\varrho_{f}=\varepsilon_{K}\oplus\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi
, where
\varepsilon_{K}
is the Dirichlet character associated with the quadratic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
. If
\varphi
is quadratic then

\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi=\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}\oplus\varepsilon_{F}
, where
K^{\prime}
and
F
are the other two quadratic subfields of
H
. Otherwise
\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi
is irreducible and we have

(\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi)_{|\rG_{K}}=\varphi\oplus\bar{\varphi}
in the dihedral basis
\{w_{2},w_{3}\}
.

By Lemma
2.2
(ii) and Frobenius reciprocity one has a natural isomorphism

\rH^{1}_{f}(V)=\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H})=\Hom_{G}(\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi,U_{H})\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{H}[\varphi],

where
\kappa\in\Hom_{G}(W,U_{H})
sends
w_{1}
to
1
, sends
w_{2}
to
a
\varphi
-unit
u_{\varphi}\in U_{H}[\varphi]
and sends
w_{3}=\tau(w_{2})
to

\tau(u_{\varphi})\in U_{H}[\bar{\varphi}]
.

We have
d=3
,
d^{+}=1
and any line
W^{+}\subset W
is
G_{p}
-stable.
By Proposition
2.9
(ii),
W^{+}
is regular if and only if it is not contained in the plane defined by
the equation

\log_{p}(u_{\varphi})b+\log_{p}(\tau(u_{\varphi}))c=0\text{, i.e. }\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\cdot b=c,

where the slope

\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}
is defined in (
8
). In particular, the only non-regular submodule of

\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi
is the line generated by
w_{2}+\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}w_{3}
.

3.4.2.
A modular basis of
\rH^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\ad^{0}\varrho_{f})

Letting
\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}=-\frac{\log_{p}(u_{\mathfrak{p}})}{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(u_{\mathfrak{p}})}
for any non-torsion element
u_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}[\tfrac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\times}
, the anti-cyclotomic
\mathscr{L}
-invariant is defined as
\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)=\mathscr{L}(\varphi)-2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(see (
9
)).

The space
\rH_{f,p}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\ad^{0}\varrho_{f})=\rH^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\ad^{0}\varrho_{f})
is two-dimensional (see
[
2
, Lem. 3.2]
). We will now give a modular description of this space and use it to write down an explicit basis, under the assumption that

(11)

\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)\cdot\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})\cdot(\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)+\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi}))\neq 0.

The condition (
11
) holds for
\varphi
quadratic, whereas in all other cases the Weak
p
-adic
Schanuel Conjecture predicts that
\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)
and
\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})
are algebraically independent.

Fix a square root
\xi
of
\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)^{-1}\mathscr{S}_{\bar{\varphi}}
.

It was conjectured in
[
17
]
and proven in
[
7
]
that there is a natural isomorphism between
\rH^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\ad^{0}\varrho_{f})
and the space
S^{\dagger}_{1}\lsem f\rsem_{0}

of genuine overconvergent modular forms belonging to the generalized eigenspace of
f
. Furthermore one computes in
[
8
]
the
q
-expansions of a basis
\{f_{\mathscr{F}}^{\dagger},f_{\Theta}^{\dagger}\}
of the latter space.
The corresponding basis
\{\kappa_{\mathscr{F}},\kappa_{\Theta}\}
of
\rH^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\ad^{0}\varrho_{f})
is characterized by
\kappa_{\mathscr{F}}(\tau)=\kappa_{\Theta}(\tau)=0
and

\kappa_{\Theta|\rG_{K}}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}-\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}&0\\
0&\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}-\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}\end{pmatrix},\text{ and }\kappa_{\mathscr{F}|\rG_{K}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\eta_{\varphi}\\
\bar{\eta}_{\varphi}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Here
\eta_{\varphi}:\rG_{K}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
represents the cocycle
[\eta_{\varphi}]\in\rH^{1}(K,\varphi)
,
normalized so that
\mathrm{loc}_{p}([\eta_{\varphi}])=\log_{p}\in\rH^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
, whereas

(\eta_{\mathfrak{p}},\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}})
is a basis of
\rH^{1}(K,\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
with
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}

is unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathrm{loc}_{p}(\eta_{\mathfrak{p}})=\log_{p}
, and
\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}=\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}\circ\tau
.

3.4.3.
The
\mathscr{L}
-invariant

As in §
3.2.3

we parameterize the lines of
W
by
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\setminus{\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}}
and
t\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

by letting
W^{+}_{s,t}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(tw_{1}+w_{2}+sw_{3})
with the convention that

W^{+}_{\infty,t}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(tw_{1}+w_{3})
. The resulting projection
W\to W^{-}=W/W^{+}_{s,t}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&-a\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a-tb\\
c-sb\end{pmatrix}
.

It is proven in
[
7
, Lem. 1.4]
that
\eta_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Frob_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}})=-\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Using Local Class Field Theory, the fact that
\eta_{\mathfrak{p}}
is unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
allows us to compute:

\eta_{\mathfrak{p}|K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}}(p)=\frac{\eta_{\mathfrak{p}|K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}}(u_{\mathfrak{p}}\bar{u}_{\mathfrak{p}})}{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(u_{\mathfrak{p}})}=0+\frac{\log_{p}(\bar{u}_{\mathfrak{p}})}{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(u_{\mathfrak{p}})}=\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Using also
[
7
, Prop 1.5]
, one finds:

(12)

\displaystyle j_{D^{\perp},f}(\kappa_{\Theta})=\begin{pmatrix}2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle j_{D^{\perp},f}(\kappa_{\mathscr{F}})=\begin{pmatrix}-t\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\\
\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\mathscr{L}(\varphi)-s\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\end{pmatrix},

(13)

\displaystyle j_{D^{\perp},c}(\kappa_{\Theta})=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad

\displaystyle j_{D^{\perp},c}(\kappa_{\mathscr{F}})=\begin{pmatrix}-t\\
\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}-s\end{pmatrix}.

As
e=2
, applying Proposition
2.17
yields

(14)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}(V,D_{s,t})=(-1)^{2}\mathscr{L}(W,W^{+}_{s,t})\\
&=\frac{\det\begin{pmatrix}2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}&-t\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\\
0&\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\mathscr{L}(\varphi)-s\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\end{pmatrix}}{\det\begin{pmatrix}1&-t\\
0&\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}-s\end{pmatrix}}=\frac{2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\left(\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\mathscr{L}(\varphi)-s\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\right)}{\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}-s}.\end{split}

We would like to conclude by determining the regular submodules of arithmetic significance.
As already noted
\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f})
being locally trivial at
p
, it does not point out in any particular direction.
Nevertheless, according to
[
7
]
the form
f
belongs to exactly
4
Hida families, i.e. the representation

\varrho_{f}
admits four non-trivial deformations to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\lsem X\rsem
, each determining an ordinary line.
If this line is given by
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(e_{1}+se_{2})
then the line of the corresponding full flag in
\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f})

is given by
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-s&1\\
-s^{2}&s\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(-sw_{1}+w_{2}-s^{2}w_{3})
.

When
s=0
one finds
\mathscr{L}(V,D_{0,0})=2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\mathscr{L}(\varphi)
, corresponding to a
p
-adic variation along the CM family
\Theta=\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})
containing
f
whose ordinary line is given by
W^{+}_{\Theta}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}e_{1}
. Hence this

\mathscr{L}
-invariant may be denoted by
\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f}),W^{+}_{\Theta})
.

Analogously, when
s=\infty
one finds
\mathscr{L}(V,D_{\infty,0})=2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})
, corresponding to a
p
-adic variation along the CM family
\bar{\Theta}=\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar{\psi}\chi_{\mathfrak{p}})
containing
f
whose ordinary line is given by
W^{+}_{\bar{\Theta}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}e_{2}
. Hence this
\mathscr{L}
-invariant may be denoted by
\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f}),W^{+}_{\bar{\Theta}})
.

Finally, when
s=\xi^{-1}
corresponds to the ordinary line in one of the the non-CM families
\mathscr{F}
containing
f
, then
W_{\mathscr{F}}^{+}=W^{+}_{-s^{2},-s}
, and similarly
W_{\mathscr{F}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}}^{+}=W^{+}_{-s^{2},s}
.
Note that these are regular submodules as
-\xi^{-2}=-\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})^{-1}\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\neq\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}
by (
11
). Using formula (
14
), we get

(15)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f}),W_{\mathscr{F}}^{+})=\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f}),W_{\mathscr{F}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}}^{+})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\left(\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\mathscr{L}(\varphi)+\frac{\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)}{\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})}\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\right)}{\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}+\frac{\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)}{\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})}\mathscr{S}_{\varphi}}=\frac{2\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\left(\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})\mathscr{L}(\varphi)+\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)\mathscr{L}(\bar{\varphi})\right)}{\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\bar{\varphi})+\mathscr{L}_{\mbox{-}}(\varphi)}.

Note that although
W_{\mathscr{F}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}}^{+}
differs from
W_{\mathscr{F}}^{+}
, the
resulting
\mathscr{L}
-invariant is the same.

3.4.4.
Compatibility with
p
-adic
L
-functions

The most promising approach for the construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions for Artin motives is via
p
-adic deformation on an eigenvariety containing sufficiently many points of regular weight. This is explained by Bellaïche
[
1
]
in the case of weight one forms using the modular symbol construction of the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve. His construction of an adjoint

p
-adic
L
-function on the eigencurve is very relevant to our context, except that it misses the cyclotomic variable.
The upshot is that the Perrin-Riou
p
-adic
L
-function should be easier to construct and arithmetically more significant to study for the regular submodules
W^{+}_{\Theta}
,
W^{+}_{\bar{\Theta}}
,
W^{+}_{\mathscr{F}}
and
W^{+}_{\mathscr{F}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}}
, as this would allow them to vary in families.

We now discuss potential candidates for such
p
-adic
L
-functions and the compatibility with our expression for the
\mathscr{L}
-invariants.

First consider the CM families
\Theta
and
\bar{\Theta}
. Note that
W^{+}_{\Theta}
and
W^{+}_{\bar{\Theta}}
actually lie in the first factor of the Galois decomposition
\ad^{0}(f)=\mathrm{Ind}_{K}^{\mathbb{Q}}\varphi\bigoplus\varepsilon_{K}
. Since Perrin-Riou’s conjecture is compatible with the Artin formalism, a natural candidate for the
p
-adic
L
-function in the direction
W^{+}_{\Theta}
, resp.
W^{+}_{\bar{\Theta}}
, is

L_{p}(\varphi,s)\cdot L_{p}(\varepsilon_{K}\cdot\omega_{p},s),\quad\text{ resp. }\quad L_{p}(\bar{\varphi},s)\cdot L_{p}(\varepsilon_{K}\cdot\omega_{p},s),

where
L_{p}(\varphi,s)
, resp.
L_{p}(\bar{\varphi},s)
, is the restriction to the cyclotomic line of Katz’s
p
-adic
L
-function for
\varphi
, resp. for
\bar{\varphi}
(e.g. see
[
7
, §4.3]
) and
L_{p}(\varepsilon_{K}\cdot\omega_{p},s)
is the Kubota-Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function attached to
\varepsilon_{K}
. Note that Katz’s construction indeed allows us to vary this
p
-adic
L
-function along the families
\Theta
and
\bar{\Theta}
. The expression for
\mathscr{L}(\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f}),W^{+}_{\Theta})
also factors as a product of two
\mathscr{L}
-invariants, namely,
\mathscr{L}(\varphi)
and
\mathscr{L}(\varepsilon_{K})=2\cdot\mathscr{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the latter being the
\mathscr{L}
-invariant introduced by Ferrero and Greenberg
[
21
]
in their proof of the Trivial Zero Conjecture for
L_{p}(\varepsilon_{K}\cdot\omega_{p},s)
. The term
\mathscr{L}(\varphi)
is the
\mathscr{L}
-invariant appearing in the Trivial Zero Conjecture for
L_{p}(\varphi,s)
proposed in
[
7
, (45)]
. When
\varphi
is quadratic, this conjecture is deduced from Gross’s factorization formula in
loc. cit.
, end of §4.3. A proof of this conjecture for general
\varphi
(under mild hypotheses on
p
) based on the equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture proven by Bley
[
9
]
has been announced by Büyükboduk and Sakamoto
[
12
]
.
Meanwhile Chida and Hsieh
[
14
]
provided an entirely different proof removing those assumptions.

Given a Hida family
\mathscr{G}
and letting
\mathscr{G}^{*}
be its dual family, Hida
[
27
]
attaches to the pair
(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{G}^{*})
a three-variable
p
-adic meromorphic function
L_{p}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{G}^{*},s)
interpolating the special values of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions
L(g,h,s)
of crystalline classical members
g\in\mathscr{G}
,
h\in\mathscr{G}^{*}
of weights
\ell,m\geqslant 2
at integers
m\leqslant s<\ell
. Let
L_{p}(g,h,s)
be the specialization of
L_{p}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{G}^{*},s)
at
(g,h)
. When
g=h^{*}
is a crystalline classical point of
\mathscr{G}
of weight
\ell=m\geqslant 2
, Dasgupta’s factorization theorem
[
18
]
asserts that
L_{p}(g,g^{*},s)=\zeta_{p}(s-\ell+1)L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g),s)
where
\zeta_{p}(s)
is the
p
-adic zeta function and
L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g),s)
is Hida-Schmidt’s
p
-adic
L
-function
[
32
]
for the adjoint motive of
g
. It makes sense to apply the above construction to
\mathscr{G}\in\{\Theta,\bar{\Theta},\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}\otimes\varepsilon_{K}\}
, in which case the weight
1
specialiazation of the
p
-adic meromorphic function
L_{p}(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{G}^{*},s)\cdot\zeta_{p}(s-\ell+1)^{-1}
should be related to Perrin-Riou’s
p
-adic
L
-function attached to
\ad^{0}(\varrho_{f})
and
W^{+}_{\mathscr{G}}
. Some evidence of this fact for
\mathscr{G}=\Theta,\bar{\Theta}
lies in the analogy with
[
30
, Theorem 6.2]
which treats the case where
f
is
p
-regular and the weight map is étale at
f
. Note that the simple (resp. double) trivial zero in the
p
-regular (resp.
p
-irregular) case is heuristically related to the smoothness (resp. non-Gorensteinness) of the eigencurve at
f
.

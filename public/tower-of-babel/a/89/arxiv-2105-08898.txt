Leray’s plane stationary solutions at small Reynolds numbers
Mikhail Korobkov, Xiao Ren

Abstract
In the celebrated 1933 paper [ 22 ] by J. Leray, the invading domains method was proposed to construct D D -solutions for the stationary Navier-Stokes flow around obstacle problem. In two dimensions, whether Leray’s D D -solution achieves the prescribed limiting velocity at spatial infinity became a major open problem since then. In this paper, we solve this problem at small Reynolds numbers. The proof builds on a novel blow-down argument which rescales the invading domains to the unit disc, as well as the ideas developed in the recent works [ 19 , 20 ] .

. Instead of this, now we have extra-smallness of the Dirichlet integrals and extra-smallness of the pressure:

D_{k}=\int\limits_{\Omega_{k}}|\nabla\mathbf{w}_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{C\lambda^{2}}{|\log\lambda|}

(6.4)

\bigl|p_{k}(z)\bigr|\leq C\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|\log\lambda|}\qquad\mbox{\rm if \ }R_{k1}\leq|z|\leq R_{k\delta}=(1-\delta_{0})R_{k}

(6.5)

(see above (
3.1
), (
4.14
) ). It is easy to check that these smallness and near-boundary condition

\bigl|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}(z)-\lambda\mathbf{e}_{1}\bigr|<\frac{C\lambda}{|\log\lambda|}\qquad\ \forall z\in S_{R_{k\delta}}

(6.6)

(see (
4.9
) ) are sufficient to produce the desired contradiction with (
6.3
)  (for sufficiently small
\lambda

, even with some simplifications. (For a reader’s convenience, we repeat the main ideas of

6.2
Case II: the estimate from below

\biggl|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{S_{r}}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}\biggr|\geq\frac{1}{5}\lambda\qquad\forall r\in\bigl[R_{k1},R_{k}\bigr]

(6.7)

holds.
In particular, now we have

\lambda_{0}=|{\mathbf{w}}_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{5}\lambda>0.

(6.8)

This case is much more delicate and subtle. Again we have extra-smallness
conditions (
6.4
)–(
6.5
). The subsequent proof is split into a number of steps. Below we will see, that the assumption (
6.7
) plays the crucial role in order to control the direction of the vector
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}
(see Step 4), and, finally, reduce this case to the situation, similar to the symmetric case (see Steps 6–7).

Step 1.

We claim, that the estimate from above

\lambda_{0}\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}

(6.9)

holds
.

Indeed, under our assumptions, the Bernoulli pressure
\Phi_{L}=p_{L}+\frac{1}{2}|{\mathbf{w}}_{L}|^{2}
of the Leray solution goes to zero as well:

\lim\limits_{|z|\to+\infty}\Phi_{L}(z)=0.

(6.10)

Then the desired estimate follows immediately from the smallness of the Dirichlet integral
D_{L}=\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla{\mathbf{w}}_{L}|^{2}<\frac{C\lambda^{2}}{|\log\lambda|}
and from Lemmas 11,13 of paper
[
20
]
(note, that cited Lemma 13 of
[
20
]
is a reformulation of
[
1
, Theorem 11]
).

Of course, the obtained estimate (
6.9
) is far from being optimal, but it has very important implications.

Step 2.

If
\lambda
is small enough, then the Leray solution
{\mathbf{w}}_{L}
coincides with the Finn–Smith solution
{\mathbf{w}}_{FS}(z,{\mathbf{w}}_{0})
.

This claim follows directly from the uniqueness result of
[
20
]
. Now we can obtain the formula for the force.

Step 3.

If
\lambda
is small enough, then the force
\mathbf{F}_{L}
satisfies the asymptotic identity

\mathbf{F}_{L}=\bigl(4\pi\mathbf{e}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\bigr)\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}.

(6.11)

Here
\mathbf{e}_{0}=\frac{{\mathbf{w}}_{0}}{|{\mathbf{w}}_{0}|}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}{\mathbf{w}}_{0}
is the corresponding unit vector, and the vector

\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}
tends to zero uniformly as
\lambda\to 0
. This important identity was established in
[
6
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
6
, page 27]
).

Denote by
\varphi_{0}
the angle direction of the vector
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}
, i.e.,

{\mathbf{w}}_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\cos\varphi_{0},\sin\varphi_{0})=\lambda_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0}.

Step 4.

The estimate

|\varphi_{0}|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{2}}D_{*}\leq\frac{C}{|\ln\lambda|}

(6.12)

holds.

This important estimate follows immediately form Lemma
4
and formulas (
1.7
), (
1.5
), (
6.7
), (
0.2
4
).

Recall, that

D_{*}=\mathbf{F}_{L}\cdot(\mathbf{w}_{\infty}-\mathbf{w}_{0})=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}\bigl(4\pi\mathbf{e}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\bigr)\cdot(\lambda\mathbf{e}_{1}-\lambda_{0}\mathbf{e}_{0})

(6.13)

(see Lemma
13
).
In particular, if
D_{*}=0
, then from (
6.12
)–(
6.13
) we obtain that
\varphi_{0}=0
and
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
, i.e.,
{\mathbf{w}}_{0}={\mathbf{w}}_{\infty}
, a contradiction. So below we always assume that

D_{*}>0.

We can rewrite (
6.13
) as

D_{*}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}\biggl(4\pi(\lambda\cos\varphi_{0}-\lambda_{0})+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\cdot\bigl[(\lambda,0)-\lambda_{0}(\cos\varphi_{0},\sin\varphi_{0})\bigr]\biggr).

(6.14)

Then we have, in particular, that

Step 5.

The estimate

\lambda_{0}\leq 2\lambda

(6.15)

holds for sufficiently small
\lambda
.

Thus we can rewrite (
6.12
) as

|\varphi_{0}|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{2}+\lambda_{0}^{2}}D_{*}.

(6.16)

Now we a ready to obtain the most important inequality for this section (cf. with (
5.5
) ):

Step 6.

The estimate

D_{*}\leq 5\pi\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})

(6.17)

holds for sufficiently small
\lambda
.

Proof.

Indeed, rewrite (
6.13
) as

D_{*}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})\bigl(4\pi\mathbf{e}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\bigr)\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}\lambda\bigl(4\pi\mathbf{e}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\bigr)\cdot(\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{0})=I+II.

(6.18)

Since
|\mathbf{e}_{1}-\mathbf{e}_{0}|\leq|\varphi_{0}|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{2}+\lambda_{0}^{2}}D_{*}
, the second term is of size
o(D_{*})
(it means, that

II=\epsilon_{\lambda}\cdot D_{*}
, where the value
\epsilon_{\lambda}\to 0
uniformly as
\lambda\to 0
) . Therefore,

D_{*}(1+\epsilon_{\lambda})=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})\bigl(4\pi\mathbf{e}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\bigr)\cdot\mathbf{e}_{0}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\ln\lambda_{0}|}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})\bigl(4\pi+\epsilon_{\lambda}\bigr).

(6.19)

So the desired estimate (
6.17
) holds when
\lambda
is small enough.
∎

In particular, now we have

\lambda-\lambda_{0}>0.

(6.20)

Recall, that
\Phi_{k}=p_{k}+\frac{1}{2}|{\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}
means the Bernoulli pressure. The next property sums up the final result of our struggle: it is a reward, in a sense, for all previous labors.

Step 7.

The estimates

\max\limits_{z\in S_{R_{k1}}}\Phi_{k}(z)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\lambda_{0}}^{2}+\epsilon_{k},

(6.21)

and

\min\limits_{z\in S_{R_{k\delta}}}\Phi_{k}(z)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\lambda_{0}}^{2}+\frac{(\lambda-\lambda_{0})}{2}(\lambda+\lambda_{0})(1+\epsilon_{\lambda}).

(6.22)

hold for sufficiently small
\lambda
.

These estimates follow directly from inequality (
6.17
) of the Step 6 and from previous estimates (
4.11
)–(
4.12
). In particular, we have

\frac{\lambda}{2}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})+\max\limits_{z\in S_{R_{k1}}}\Phi_{k}(z)<\min\limits_{z\in S_{R_{k\delta}}}\Phi_{k}(z)

(6.23)

for
k
sufficiently large. This means, that we are faced with the situation, considered in the paper
[
19
]
, and we can obtain the desired contradiction just by repeating the corresponding arguments of that paper. For a reader’s convenience, we recall the main ideas of the proof in
[
19
]
adapted for the present paper.

From (
6.23
) it follows immediately, that there are two closed regular level sets
S^{\prime}_{k}
and
S^{\prime\prime}_{k}
of
\Phi_{k}

such that:

(i)

\Phi_{k}|_{S^{\prime}_{k}}\equiv t^{\prime}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}+\epsilon_{k}
,
\Phi_{k}|_{S^{\prime\prime}_{k}}\equiv t^{\prime\prime}_{k}\geq t^{\prime}_{k}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})
.

(ii)

S^{\prime}_{k}
,
S^{\prime\prime}_{k}
are smooth closed curves (homeomorphic to the circle) surrounding the origin, both of them lie between circles
S_{R_{k1}}
and
S_{R_{k\delta}}
.

(iii)

the vorticity
\omega_{k}(z)
does not change sign between the curves
S^{\prime}_{k}
,
S^{\prime\prime}_{k}
; for definiteness, we can assume without loss of generality that

\omega_{k}(z)>0\qquad\mbox{ for all $z$ between $S^{\prime}_{k}$ and $S^{\prime\prime}_{k}$}

(6.24)

(for the last assertion, see
[
19
, Step 6]
).

Now denote
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{S_{r}}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}=\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k}(r)=|\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{k}(r)|\,\bigl(\cos\varphi_{k}(r),\sin\varphi_{k}(r))
. Then by the same reasons as for inequality (
6.12
) of the present paper, we have

|\varphi_{k}(r)|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda^{2}}D_{*}=\epsilon_{\lambda}\qquad\forall r\in[R_{k1},R_{k\delta}].

(6.25)

Hence, it is not difficult to prove, that there exists a unit vector
\tilde{\mathbf{e}}=(\cos\tilde{\theta},\sin\tilde{\theta})
such that the segment

L=\{s\tilde{\mathbf{e}}:s\in[R_{k1},R_{k\delta}]\}
satisfies the following properties:

(iv)

{\mathbf{w}}^{\bot}_{k}(z)\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{e}}<0
for any
z\in L
, where we denote
(a,b)^{\bot}=(-b,a)
;

(v)

\int\limits_{L}|\nabla\omega_{k}|\,ds\leq\epsilon_{k}
.

Take two points
A\in L\cap S^{\prime}_{k}
and
B\in L\cap S^{\prime\prime}_{k}
such that the line segment
[A,B]
lies between the curves
S^{\prime}_{k}
and
S^{\prime\prime}_{k}
.
Recall, that the gradient of the Bernoulli pressure satisfies the identity

\nabla\Phi_{k}\equiv-\nabla^{\bot}\omega_{k}+\omega_{k}\cdot{\mathbf{w}}^{\bot}_{k}.

Then we have

\begin{array}[]{c}t^{\prime\prime}_{k}-t^{\prime}_{k}=\Phi_{k}(B)-\Phi_{k}(A)=\int\limits_{[A,B]}\nabla\Phi_{k}\cdot{\tilde{\mathbf{e}}}\,ds\\[12.0pt]
=-\int\limits_{[A,B]}\nabla^{\bot}\omega_{k}\cdot{\tilde{\mathbf{e}}}\,ds+\int\limits_{[A,B]}\omega_{k}{\mathbf{w}}_{k}^{\bot}\cdot{\tilde{\mathbf{e}}}\,dr:=I+II.\end{array}

(6.26)

Estimate the terms
I
and
II
separately. From the above property (v) we have

I\leq\epsilon_{k}.

(6.27)

On the other hand, from (iii)–(iv) we obtain

II<0.

(6.28)

Therefore,

t^{\prime\prime}_{k}-t^{\prime}_{k}\leq\epsilon_{k},

a contradiction with (i).

Of course, the above items (i)–(v) are only short description. In case of interest, a reader can find a detailed justification for all these steps in
[
19
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is finished completely.

at a real number
x
by
\mathsf{IH}_{h}(x)
. Letting
x_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max\{0,x\}
denote the positive part of the real number
x
, this density is given by

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathsf{IH}_{h}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(h-1)!}\sum_{j=0}^{h}(-1)^{j}\binom{h}{j}(x-j)_{+}^{h-1}&x\leq h,\\
0&x>h.\end{cases}

We now record those conventions that we assume, unless stated otherwise, throughout the rest of the paper. Throughout the analysis, we assume that we have fixed an arbitrary integer
h\geq 2
and a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear form
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{h}\to\mathbb{Z}
with coefficients
u_{1},\dots,u_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
satisfying
\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})=1
. We employ standard asymptotic notation throughout the article: for the sake of completeness, we describe this notation in Appendix
A
. We also assume that
N
is a large positive integer which tends to infinity, and that all asymptotic notation is with respect to
N
. As such, we henceforth abbreviate much of the notation introduced here by dropping arguments or subscripts of
L
,
N
, and
h
. In this section, we were careful to make it clear what the quantities we defined depended on; whenever we use such an abbreviation, it will be unambiguous what the abbreviated expression is referring to. We omit floor and ceiling symbols when doing so does not affect asymptotics. In Sections
2
-
7
, we will assume that (
1.4
) holds, and frequently abbreviate
p(N)
to
p
.

1.3.
Main Results

Our primary interest is to understand what happens to
|L(A)|
and
|L(A)^{c}|
as we vary the rate at which the inclusion probability
p(N)
decays as
N
grows. Our techniques yield two broad classes of results.

1.3.1.
Phase Transition

At the more “global” scale, we observe the following phase transition phenomenon concerning the sizes of
L(A)
and its complement.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\to(0,1)
be a function satisfying (
1.4
). Fix an integer
h\geq 2
and a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear form
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{h}\to\mathbb{Z}
with coefficients
u_{1},\dots,u_{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
such that
\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})=1
. Let
A\subseteq I_{N}
be a random subset where each element of
I_{N}
is independently included in
A
with probability
p(N)
. The following three situations arise.

(i)

If
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

(1.15)

\displaystyle|L(A)|\sim\frac{\left(N\cdot p(N)\right)^{h}}{\theta_{L}}.

(ii)

If
p(N)=cN^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
for some constant
c>0
, then

(1.16)

\displaystyle|L(A)|\sim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|-2\int_{0}^{m/2}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{h}\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{|u_{1}|-1}\cdots\sum_{t_{h}=0}^{|u_{h}|-1}\mathsf{IH}_{h}\left(x-\sum_{i=1}^{h}t_{i}\right)}{\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)dx\right)N,

(1.17)

\displaystyle|L(A)^{c}|\sim\left(2\int_{0}^{m/2}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{h}\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{|u_{1}|-1}\cdots\sum_{t_{h}=0}^{|u_{h}|-1}\mathsf{IH}_{h}\left(x-\sum_{i=1}^{h}t_{i}\right)}{\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)dx\right)N.

(iii)

If
p(N)\ggg N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then (where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the Gamma function)

(1.18)

\displaystyle|L(A)^{c}|\sim\frac{2\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p(N)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}.

Theorem
1.1
, which identifies
N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
as a threshold function (in the sense of
[
JLR11
]
), asserts that the following macroscopic behavior holds. It is not hard to see that the number of
L
-expressions we can form in
L(A)
is
\sim|A|^{h}/\theta_{L}\sim(Np)^{h}/\theta_{L}
. Thus, if we are below the threshold, it follows that
L(A)
is asymptotically almost surely “basically Sidon,” i.e., that almost all pairs of such
L
-expressions generated in
L(A)
correspond to distinct values. If we are above the threshold, it follows from
p(N)^{-\frac{h}{h-1}}\lll N
that asymptotically almost surely, almost all possible values are generated in
L(A)
. On the threshold, as
c
increases from zero to infinity, it follows from the expressions given in Theorem
1.1
(ii) that the asymptotic almost sure behavior of
|L(A)|
shifts from
L(A)
missing almost all sums to
L(A)
having almost all sums.

Theorem
1.1
may also be interpreted as corroboration for some of the ideas presented in our opening discussion on MSTD sets. Indeed, say that we are given two
\mathbb{Z}
-linear forms
L,\tilde{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}^{h}\to\mathbb{Z}
. It follows in all three cases of Theorem
1.1
that if
\theta_{L}<\theta_{\tilde{L}}
, then
|L(A)|
is asymptotically almost surely larger than
|\tilde{L}(A)|
. In particular, consider the setting in which the coefficients of
L
and
\tilde{L}
all have absolute value
1
, and it is true for both
L
and
\tilde{L}
that there are at least as many instances of
1
as
-1
amongst its coefficients. Then this statement on the asymptotic relative size of
L(A)
and
\tilde{L}(A)
is consistent with the heuristic that for two generalized sumsets which have at least as many pluses as minuses, the generalized sumset with more minus signs will usually be larger.

Theorem
1.1
also completely resolves
[
HM09
, Conjecture 4.2]
. Specifically, replacing their conjectured threshold function of
N^{-\frac{1}{h}}
with
N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, we derive their conjectured statement in the regime where
p(N)
is below the threshold. By taking

\displaystyle R(x_{0},\dots,x_{h})

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{0}^{h}}{\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|x_{i}|},

\displaystyle g_{u_{1},\dots,u_{h}}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sum_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\exp\left(-y\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{|u_{1}|-1}\cdots\sum_{t_{h}=0}^{|u_{h}|-1}\mathsf{IH}_{h}\left(x-\sum_{i=1}^{h}t_{i}\right)\right)dx,

we observe that Theorem
1.1
(ii) establishes
[
HM09
, Equation (4.4)]
, so we also derive their conjectured statement in the regime where
p(N)
is on the threshold. In the regime where
p(N)
is above the threshold, we disprove their conjecture and replace it with the correct statement.

By considering
\mathbb{Z}
-linear forms
L
for which all the coefficients
u_{1},\dots,u_{h}
have absolute value
1
, we extract the following result, which is Theorem
1.1
specifically for the setting of generalized sumsets.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\to(0,1)
be any function satisfying (
1.4
). Fix nonnegative integers
s,d,h
such that
h\geq 2
and
s+d=h
. Let
A\subseteq I_{N}
be a random subset where each element of
I_{N}
is independently included in
A
with probability
p(N)
. Then the following three situations arise.

(i)

If
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

\displaystyle|A_{s,d}|\sim\frac{(N\cdot p(N))^{h}}{s!d!}.

(ii)

If
p(N)=cN^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
for some constant
c>0
, then

\displaystyle|A_{s,d}|\sim\left(h-2\int_{0}^{h/2}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{h}}{s!d!}\mathsf{IH}_{h}(x)\right)dx\right)N,

\displaystyle|A_{s,d}^{c}|\sim\left(2\int_{0}^{h/2}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{h}}{s!d!}\mathsf{IH}_{h}(x)\right)dx\right)N.

(iii)

If
p(N)\ggg N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

\displaystyle|A_{s,d}^{c}|\sim\frac{2\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!s!d!}}{(h-1)\cdot p(N)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}.

Theorem
1.2
generalizes
[
HM09
, Theorem 1.1]
from sum and difference sets with two summands to generalized sumsets with
h
summands, under any number
0\leq s\leq h
of sums. It is also naturally of interest to understand how the relative asymptotic almost sure sizes of generalized sumsets and their complements change when we modify their structure, i.e., the number of sums amongst their
h
summands. In this direction, we have Corollary
1.3
as an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.2
(i, iii). We note that Corollary
1.3
(i) is a strictly more general statement than the first part of
[
HM13
, Theorem 1.2]
. We omit the corresponding result for Theorem
1.2
(ii) (which would yield a strictly more general statement than the second part of
[
HM13
, Theorem 1.2]
), as it is less natural.

Corollary 1.3
.

Fix nonnegative integers
s_{1},d_{1},s_{2},d_{2},h
such that
h\geq 2
and
s_{1}+d_{1}=s_{2}+d_{2}=h
. Let
A\subseteq I_{N}
be a random subset where each element of
I_{N}
is independently included in
A
with probability
p(N)
. Then the following two situations arise.

(i)

If
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{s_{1},d_{1}}|}{|A_{s_{2},d_{2}}|}\sim\frac{s_{2}!d_{2}!}{s_{1}!d_{1}!},

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{s_{1},d_{1}}^{c}|}{|A_{s_{2},d_{2}}^{c}|}\sim 1.

(ii)

If
p(N)\ggg N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{s_{1},d_{1}}|}{|A_{s_{2},d_{2}}|}\sim 1,

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{s_{1},d_{1}}^{c}|}{|A_{s_{2},d_{2}}^{c}|}\sim\sqrt[h-1]{\frac{s_{1}!d_{1}!}{s_{2}!d_{2}!}}.

1.3.2.
Poisson Convergence

In order to estimate the probability that a particular candidate value is included in
L(A)
, we invoke the Stein-Chen method based on dependency graphs. We refer the unfamiliar reader to
[
AGG89
,
AGG90
]
and the references therein for an introduction to this method and to
[
CS04
,
CX04
,
CX11
]
for more recent central results of a similar spirit. As such, it is very natural to ask if there is something noteworthy to be said about limiting Poisson behavior, both at the level of Poisson convergence and at the point process level. In this direction, the tools and arguments used to prove Theorem
1.1
quickly lend themselves to the following results. At the level of Poisson convergence, Theorem
1.4
presents a “local” phase transition for the number of representations of a candidate value in
L(A)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume the same setup as in Theorem
1.1
. For
k\in[0,mN]
, we let
W_{k}
denote the number of
L
-expressions
\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}
such that
S(\Lambda)\subseteq A
. Let
\mu_{k}=\mathbb{E}[W_{k}]
. Let
C>0
be a constant. The following two situations arise.

(i)

If
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}
, then uniformly over
k\in\left[0,mN\right]
,
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(W_{k}),\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)\lll 1
.

(ii)

If
p(N)\gtrsim N^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}
, then uniformly over
k\in\left[CN,(m-C)N\right]
,
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(W_{k}),\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)=\Omega(1)
.

Theorem
1.4
, which identifies
p(N)=N^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}
as a threshold function (which dominates the threshold function
N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
in Theorem
1.1
), presents a sharp result concerning when the number of representations of a candidate value in
L(A)
obeys a Poisson limit. A more interesting inquiry, however, results from asking if a similar threshold phenomenon may be obtained at the point process level. Indeed, for the sake of discussion, let us assume that we had an analogue of Theorem
1.4
which identifies when the process
\Xi_{k}
may be thought of as a Poisson point process. Then if we are below the threshold, we may think of the number of ways that
-dN+k
is generated in
L(A)
as being modeled by a collection of several independent ways of representing
-dN+k
under
L
. On the threshold, the extent to which this interpretation is faithful to the truth weakens as the constant
C
grows, until it collapses everywhere outside of the fringes of
\left[0,mN\right]
as we continue to move above the threshold. Such a result would be of conceptual interest, as such local dependencies have served as the main rigidity in many of the papers on MSTD sets that were mentioned in Subsection
1.1
.

Lifting to the point process level, our techniques immediately yield the following result.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume the setup of Theorem
1.1
. Let
C>0
be a constant. The following two situations arise.

(i)

If
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then uniformly over
k\in\left[0,mN\right]
,
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(\Xi_{k}),\textup{Po}(\pi_{k})\right)\lll 1
.

(ii)

If
p(N)\gtrsim N^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}
, then uniformly over
k\in\left[CN,(m-C)N\right]
,
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(\Xi_{k}),\textup{Po}(\pi_{k})\right)=\Omega(1)
.

1.3.3.
Some Remarks

We make a number of important comments concerning our main results.

Remark 1.6
.

The condition
\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})=1
assumed in the statements of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
can be relaxed. Indeed, let
L(x_{1},\dots,x_{h})=u_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+u_{h}x_{h}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear form on
h
variables for which
\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})>1
. Let
\tilde{L}(x_{1},\dots,x_{h})=v_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+v_{h}x_{h}
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear form on
h
variables for which
v_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})}
for all
i\in[h]
. It is straightforward to show that

(1.19)

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{L}(N,k)=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})\nmid k,\\
\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{L}}\left(N,k/\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})\right)&\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})\mid k,\end{cases}

and that

\displaystyle|L(A)|=|\tilde{L}(A)|,

\displaystyle|L(A)^{c}|=|\tilde{L}(A)^{c}|-\left(\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})-1\right)\sum_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|.

In this sense, we will have solved the problem for all
\mathbb{Z}
-linear forms once we have solved it under the setting in which
\gcd(u_{1},\dots,u_{h})=1
. We have thus assumed this condition in our main results.
∎

Remark 1.7
.

By fixing
h=2
, we observe that Theorem
1.1
(i) and Theorem
1.1
(iii) are respectively consistent with
[
HM09
, Theorem 3.1(i)]
and
[

, Theorem 3.1(iii)]
. Letting

be a

-linear form for which

,

, and

(ii) with

produces the following identity:

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\frac{|u_{1}|+|u_{2}|}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{c^{2}\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{|u_{1}|-1}\sum_{t_{2}=0}^{|u_{2}|-1}\mathsf{IH}_{2}\left(x-t_{1}-t_{2}\right)}{|u_{1}u_{2}|}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2|u_{1}u_{2}|\left(1-e^{-c^{2}/|u_{1}|}\right)}{c^{2}}+\left(|u_{1}|-|u_{2}|\right)e^{-c^{2}/|u_{1}|}.

∎

Remark 1.8
.

There are other natural choices for the definitions of our main objects of study in Subsection
1.2
; we have proceeded as such to be consistent with the presentation of
[
HM09
]
, which pursues the

case of our problem. We mention some of these alternatives here, and discuss the changes they would induce to our main results. One natural modification is to define a coarser equivalence relation on
\mathbb{N}^{h}
for which two

which generate a candidate sum
-dN+k
in

instead of distinct representations in
L(A)
generating

by

\displaystyle L(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{u_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+u_{h}a_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a_{i}\in A,a_{1},\dots,a_{h}\text{ distinct}\right\}.

By tracing our arguments, it can be checked that all of our main results follow over without issue to these two settings. (Indeed, in the latter case, much of the analysis becomes simpler, as we no longer have to deal with repeated summands.)
∎

2.2.
Poisson Approximation

Over the course of our calculations in Sections
5
and
6
, we will make use of the following definition and theorems. Note that it is very natural to suspect some kind of Poisson weak limit in this setting: loosely speaking, the possible ways in which a candidate sum
-dN+k
may be generated in

correspond to a collection of rare and weakly dependent events, rendering the Stein-Chen method a promising line of attack. We refer the interested reader to
[
Ald13
,
BHJ92
]
and
[

, Chapter 8]
for classical treatments of this paradigm.

Definition 2.4

(
[
BHJ92
]
, Equation (1.1))
.

Let
I
be a finite index set. For each
\alpha\in I
, let
X_{\alpha}
be a Bernoulli random variable. We say that the random variables
\left\{X_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\alpha\in I\right\}
are
positively related
if, for every
\alpha\in I
, there exist random variables
\left\{Y_{\beta\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\beta\in I\setminus\{\alpha\}\right\}
defined on the same probability space such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(Y_{\beta\alpha};\beta\in I\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(X_{\beta};\beta\in I\ |\ X_{\alpha}=1\right),

\displaystyle Y_{\beta\alpha}\geq X_{\beta}\text{ for all }\beta\in I\setminus\{\alpha\}.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
AGG89
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
I
be a finite index set. For each
\alpha\in I
, let
X_{\alpha}
be a Bernoulli random variable with parameter
p_{\alpha}
, and choose a subset
B_{\alpha}
of
I
. Define

\displaystyle b_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{\beta\in B_{\alpha}}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta},

\displaystyle b_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta\in B_{\alpha}}\Pr[X_{\alpha}X_{\beta}=1],

\displaystyle b_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{E}[X_{\alpha}\ |\ X_{\beta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\beta\notin B_{\alpha}]-p_{\alpha}\right|\right].

Let
W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\alpha\in I}X_{\alpha}
, and let
Z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\textup{Po}(\lambda)
with rate
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{E}[W]=\sum_{\alpha\in I}p_{\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\Pr[W=0]-e^{-\lambda}\right|<\min\{1,\lambda^{-1}\}(b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}).

Whenever Theorem
2.5
is invoked in this article, we will take
B_{\alpha}
to be the
dependency set
of
\alpha
for every
\alpha\in I
:

if and only if

and

are dependent. With this choice of the subsets

, we may interpret

as measuring the total size of the dependence sets and

as twice the expected number of dependent pairs that arise. It also follows immediately that

(in general,

might be thought of as measuring how honest we were in selecting

to be the dependency set of
\alpha
). We now state the theorems that we will invoke when proving our results which specifically concern Poisson convergence.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
AGG89
]
, Theorems 1 and 2)
.

Assume the setup of Theorem
2.5
, with the additional assumption that for each
\alpha\in I
, we take

to be the dependency set of
\alpha

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|L(A)^{c}|\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[(-d+\tau)N,(s-\tau)N\right]\right|\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[(-d+\tau)N,(s-\tau)N\right]^{c}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[(-d+\tau)N,(s-\tau)N\right]^{c}\right|\right],

which provides the desired reduction.

Remark 6.3
.

It is straightforward to adapt Lemma
6.2
if we were to include the condition that some particular value
n\in I_{N}
cannot be in any subset of
\mathscr{S}_{k}
. Indeed, it is not hard to see that the asymptotic claim implicit in (
6.7
) would still hold uniformly over
k\in\left[\tau N,mN/2\right]
, since for any such
k
, the number of
h
-tuples
(a_{1},\dots,a_{h})
with at least one instance of
n
such that
L(a_{1},\dots,a_{h})=-dN+k
is
O(k^{h-2})
. More specifically, by tracing and adapting the proof of Lemma
6.2
, we can show that there exists a constant
C>0
(also independent of
n
) for which it holds for all
k\in\left[(1/p)^{\frac{h}{h-1}},mN/2\right]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right]\leq e^{-Ckp},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[sN-k\notin L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right]\leq e^{-Ckp}.

We make use of this remark in the proof of Theorem
6.6
.
∎

6.2.
Expectation

In this subsection, we will compute
\mathbb{E}\left[|L(A)^{c}|\right]
. We begin with the following observation, which is the analogue of Lemma
5.1
for this regime.

Lemma 6.4
.

Uniformly over all
k\in\left[g(N),\tau N\right]
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L(A)\right]-e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right|\lll e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
. Uniformly over
k\in\left[g(N),\tau N\right]
,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}\sim|\mathscr{D}_{k}|\cdot p^{h}\sim\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}=\frac{p^{h}k^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}.

Here, (
6.12
) follows as in Lemma
5.1
, with the final equality due to the definition of
\mathsf{IH}_{h}
. It now follows from Theorem
2.5
that uniformly over
k\in\left[g(N),\tau N\right]
,

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L(A)\right]-e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right|

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\left|(\mathbb{P}\left[W_{k}=0\right]-e^{-\mu_{k}})+(e^{-\mu_{k}}-e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{p^{h}k^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)\left(b_{1}(k)+b_{2}(k)+b_{3}(k)\right)+e^{\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\left|e^{-\mu_{k}}-e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(\frac{\left(\tau Np^{\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)\left(k^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}+k^{h-1}p^{h}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\exp\left(\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{k}}{\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right)\right)-1\right|

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{f(N)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)k^{h-1}p^{h}\left(k^{h-2}p^{h-1}+1\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{f(N)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)\left(\tau Np^{\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)^{h-1}\left(\left(\tau Np^{\frac{h-1}{h-2}}\right)^{h-2}+1\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{f(N)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)f(N)^{h-1}\left(\left(f(N)p^{\frac{h-1}{h-2}-\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)^{h-2}+1\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{f(N)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)f(N)^{2h-3}\left(p^{\frac{1}{(h-1)(h-2)}}+p\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(\frac{f(N)^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)f(N)^{2h-3}p^{\frac{1}{(h-1)(h-2)}}+o(1)\lll 1.

Here, (
6.13
) follows since uniformly over
k\in\left[g(N),\tau N\right]
,

(6.14)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{k}}{\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right)\right|\leq f(N)^{h-1}\delta(N)\lll 1.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Equipped with Lemma
6.4
, we are ready to compute the expectation that we seek.

Proposition 6.5
.

We have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|L(A)^{c}|\right]\sim\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)\cap\left[(-d+\tau)N,(s-\tau)N\right]^{c}\right|\right]\sim\frac{2\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p(N)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}.

Proof.

The first asymptotic equivalence is simply a restating of (
6.11
). We prove the latter. We begin with the left fringe. Using (
5.2
), we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[-dN,(-d+\tau)N\right]\right|\right]

(6.15)

\displaystyle=o\left((1/p)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[-dN+g(N),(-d+\tau)N\right]\right|\right].

We express the latter term as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[-dN+g(N),(-d+\tau)N\right]\right|\right]=\sum_{k=g(N)}^{\tau N}\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L(A)\right]

(6.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=g(N)}^{\tau N}\left(\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L(A)\right]-e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}+e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}\right)\sim\sum_{k=g(N)}^{\tau N}e^{-\lambda_{k}N^{h-1}p^{h}}

(6.17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=g(N)}^{\tau N}\exp\left(-\frac{p^{h}k^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)\sim\int_{g(N)}^{\tau N}\exp\left(-\frac{p^{h}x^{h-1}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}\right)\ dx

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt[h-1]{\frac{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}{p^{h}}}\int_{g(N)\sqrt[h-1]{\frac{p^{h}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}}^{\tau N\sqrt[h-1]{\frac{p^{h}}{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}}e^{-x^{h-1}}\ dx

(6.18)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{h-1}}\ dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{h-2}x^{-(h-2)}e^{-x^{h-1}}\ dx

(6.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}e^{-x}\ dx=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}.

We used Lemma
6.4
in (
6.16
) and (
6.17
), and the asymptotic equivalence in (
6.18
) follows from the dominated convergence theorem, since (
5.2
) and (
6.2
) imply that the lower and upper limits of the integral respectively tend to zero and infinity. It follows from (
6.15
) and (
6.19
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[-dN,(-d+\tau)N\right]\right|\right]\sim\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}.

We now handle the right fringe. Proceeding like (
6.15
) in (
6.20
) and invoking (
5.3
),

(6.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[(s-\tau)N,sN\right]\right|\right]=o\left((1/p)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)+\sum_{k=g}^{\tau N}\mathbb{P}\left[sN-k\notin L(A)\right]

(6.21)

\displaystyle=o\left((1/p)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}\right)+\sum_{k=g}^{\tau N}\mathbb{P}\left[-sN+k\notin\tilde{L}(A)\right]\sim\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}},

where we have applied (
6.19
) to
\tilde{L}(A)
. Using (
6.19
) and (
6.21
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|L(A)^{c}\cap\left[(-d+\tau)N,(s-\tau)N\right]^{c}\right|\right]\sim\frac{2\cdot\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{h-1}\right)\sqrt[h-1]{(h-1)!\cdot\theta_{L}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{h}|u_{i}|}}{(h-1)\cdot p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}},

which was the desired result.
∎

6.3.
Concentration

We now show that the random variable
|L(A)^{c}|
is strongly concentrated about its expectation. Together with Proposition
6.5
, this will prove Theorem
1.1
(iii).

Proposition 6.6
.

If (
6.1
) holds, then
|L(A)^{c}|\sim\mathbb{E}\left[|L(A)^{c}|\right]
.

Proof.

We proceed exactly as in the proof of Proposition
5.3
, but with the following modifications. First, unlike in the proof of Proposition
5.3
, we will need to show that
\Delta_{n,1}(A)
is modest with high probability. The expectation of
\Delta_{n,1}(A)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta_{n,1}(A)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left(L\left(A\cup\{n\}\right)\setminus L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right)\cap(\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap[-dN+\tilde{g},sN-\tilde{g}])\right|\ \Big|\ a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left(L\left(A\cup\{n\}\right)\setminus L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right)\cap(\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap[-dN+\tilde{g},sN-\tilde{g}])\right|\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{n}\cap[-dN+\tilde{g},sN-\tilde{g}]}\mathbb{P}\left[\left(k\in L\left(A\cup\{n\}\right)\right)\land\left(k\notin L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right)\right]

(6.22)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}}^{mN-\tilde{g}}\mathbb{P}\left[-dN+k\notin L\left(A\setminus\{n\}\right)\right]\leq\sum_{k=\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}}^{mN-\tilde{g}}e^{-Ckp}

(6.23)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1/p)\exp\left(-Cp\left(\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1/p)\exp\left(-(C/2)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right).

We appealed to Remark
6.3
to observe the latter inequality in (
6.22
) (we may do so, since it follows from (
5.15
) and (
5.2
) that
\tilde{g}\ggg(1/p)^{\frac{h}{h-1}}
), where
C>0
is an appropriate constant. The former inequality in (
6.23
) follows from computations entirely analogous to those performed in the proof of Lemma
6.2
. Therefore, we have from (
6.23
) and Markov’s inequality that

(6.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{n,1}(A)\gtrsim(1/p)\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(1/p)\exp\left(-(C/2)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)}{(1/p)\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)}\lesssim\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right).

By a union bound, it now follows from (
6.24
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{n,1}(A)\geq\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)\text{ for some }n\in I_{N}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{N}\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\tilde{g}}\exp\left(-\frac{Cp\tilde{g}}{4}\right)+\sum_{n=N-\tilde{g}}^{N}\exp\left(-\frac{Cp\tilde{g}}{4}\right)+\sum_{n=\tilde{g}+1}^{N-\tilde{g}-1}\exp\left(-\frac{Cpn}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\log(1/p)}{p^{\frac{h}{h-1}}}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{4p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+\int_{\tilde{g}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{Cpx}{2}\right)\ dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\left((1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}\log(1/p)\right)^{h}\exp\left(-(1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}(C/4)\log(1/p)\right)+(1/p)\exp\left(-(1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}(C/4)\log(1/p)\right)

\displaystyle\leq o(1)+\left((1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}\log(1/p)\right)^{h-1}\exp\left(-(1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}(C/2)\log(1/p)\right)\lll 1.

So with probability
1-o(1)
,

(6.25)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,1}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-(C/4)p\max\left\{\tilde{g},\min\{n,N-n\}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-(1/p)^{\frac{1}{h-1}}(C/4)\log(1/p)\right)\lll 1\text{ for all }n\in I_{N}.

Furthermore, on this event with
1-o(1)
probability, it holds that

(6.26)

\displaystyle p\sum_{n=0}^{N}\left(\Delta_{n,1}(A)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\tilde{g}}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{2p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+\sum_{n=N-\tilde{g}}^{N}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{2p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+\sum_{n=\tilde{g}+1}^{N-\tilde{g}}\exp\left(-Cpn\right)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim p\tilde{g}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{2p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+p\cdot\frac{1}{p}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=p\tilde{g}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{2p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+o(1)\lesssim\frac{\log(1/p)}{p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\exp\left(-\frac{C\log(1/p)}{2p^{\frac{1}{h-1}}}\right)+o(1)\lll 1.

Finally, if we assume (
6.1
),
(
5.29
) and Lemma
6.1
together imply the sufficiency of showing (
5.30
). With these adjustments, tracing the proof of Proposition
5.3
proves the desired.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

7.
Poisson Convergence

The methods we used to prove Theorem
1.1
lend themselves quickly to a number of observations concerning the asymptotic behavior of certain local dependent Bernoulli processes of interest, which are captured in Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
. In the following computations, we assume that
k\in\left[0,mN/2\right]
. As remarked in Section
3
, we may extend these results to
k\in\left[mN/2,mN\right]
by appealing to (
3.1
). We let
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a function satisfying

(7.1)

\displaystyle p^{\frac{2}{2h-3}}\lll F(N)\lll 1.

We begin by proving an upper bound for the total variation distance between the law of
W_{k}
and
\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})
. By invoking Theorem
2.6
and substituting results from Section
3
, it follows from entirely straightforward manipulations that if
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}
(this assumption is needed only for the latter case), noting that it follows from (
7.1
) that
F(N)(1/p)^{\frac{2h-1}{2h-3}}\ggg 1/p
,

\displaystyle d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(W_{k}),\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min\{1,\mu_{k}^{-1}\}\left(b_{1}(k)+b_{2}(k)\right)

(7.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\sum_{\ell=h}^{2h-1}k^{\ell-2}p^{\ell}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{h-1}k^{\ell-1}p^{\ell}&k\leq F(N)(1/p)^{\frac{2h-1}{2h-3}}\\
\frac{k^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}+k^{h-2}p^{h-1}}{k^{h-1}p^{h}}&k>F(N)(1/p)^{\frac{2h-1}{2h-3}}\end{cases}\lll 1.

This proves Theorem
1.4
(i). We now turn to proving a lower bound for this total variation distance, which will show that the regime of
p(N)
for which we established the limiting Poisson behavior of
W_{k}
for all
k\in\left[0,mN\right]
is sharp. In what follows, we assume that

(7.3)

\displaystyle p(N)\geq cN^{-\frac{h-2}{h-1}}

for some constant
c>0
. We fix another constant
C>0
, and we study values
k\in\left[CN,mN/2\right]
, for which we will be able to obtain a meaningful lower bound. We note that

(7.4)

\displaystyle N\gtrsim(1/p)^{\frac{h-1}{h-2}},

\displaystyle k\asymp N.

To derive such a lower bound on the total variation distance, we invoke Theorem
2.7
to bound
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(W_{k},\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)
from below. First, observe that the collection of random variables
\{X_{\Lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}\}
is positively related. Indeed, for
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}
, let
Y_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}
be the Bernoulli random variable corresponding to the event
S(\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus S(\Lambda)\subseteq A
; if
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}
, then
Y_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}\equiv 1
. It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(Y_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda};\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(X_{\Lambda^{\prime}};\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}\ |\ X_{\Lambda}=1\right),

\displaystyle Y_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}\geq X_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\text{ for all }\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}\setminus\{\Lambda\}.

We define the following. The statements for
\epsilon_{k}
hold uniformly over
k\in\left[CN,mN/2\right]
and follow quickly from computations in Section
3
, notably using (
3.5
) to prove the
\gtrsim
implicit in the
\asymp
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[(W_{k}-\mathbb{E}[W_{k}])^{4}\right]}{\mu_{k}}-1;

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\textup{Var}(W_{k})}{\mu_{k}}-1\asymp N^{h-2}p^{h-1}=\Omega(1).

Thus, we may invoke Theorem
2.7
. Appealing to (
7.4
), we proceed with the relevant computations.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}+1}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\lesssim\frac{N^{4h-6}p^{4h-2}+N^{3h-4}p^{3h-1}+N^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}+N^{h-1}p^{h}}{N^{3h-4}p^{3h-1}}

\displaystyle=N^{h-2}p^{h-1}+1+\frac{1}{N^{h-1}p^{h}}+\frac{1}{N^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}}\asymp N^{h-2}p^{h-1}+1\asymp\epsilon_{k};

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}p_{\Lambda}^{2}}{\mu_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}p_{\Lambda}}{\mu_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}}=\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\asymp\frac{1}{N^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}}\lll 1;

\displaystyle\frac{3\textup{Var}(W_{k})\max_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}p_{\Lambda}}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{3pN^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}}{N^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}}=3p\lll 1.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\psi_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{\gamma_{k}}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\right)_{+}+3\epsilon_{k}+\frac{\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}p_{\Lambda}^{2}}{\mu_{k}^{2}\epsilon_{k}}+\frac{3\textup{Var}(W_{k})\max_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{D}_{k}}p_{\Lambda}}{\mu_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\asymp\epsilon_{k}.

Altogether, we conclude that uniformly over
k\in\left[CN,mN/2\right]
,

\displaystyle d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(W_{k}),\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)\geq\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{11+3\psi_{k}}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{11+O(1)\epsilon_{k}}=\Omega(1),

which proves Theorem
1.4
(ii). From here, Corollary
1.5
(i) quickly follows from Theorem
2.6
since if we have that
p(N)\lll N^{-\frac{h-1}{h}}
, then

\displaystyle d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(\Xi_{k}),\textup{Po}(\pi_{k})\right)\leq b_{1}(k)+b_{2}(k)\lesssim\sum_{\ell=h}^{2h-1}N^{\ell-2}p^{\ell}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{h-1}N^{\ell-1}p^{\ell}\lesssim N^{2h-3}p^{2h-1}+N^{h-2}p^{h-1}\lll 1.

On the other hand, Corollary
1.5
(ii) follows since
d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(\Xi_{k}),\textup{Po}(\pi_{k})\right)\geq d_{\textup{TV}}\left(\mathcal{L}(W_{k}),\textup{Po}(\mu_{k})\right)
.

8.
Future Directions

We conclude by suggesting a number of open problems related to the content of this article.

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
, we introduced and leveraged a number of tools in probabilistic combinatorics for a systematic study of linear mappings of random subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
. In a similar direction, the second author has been involved in several projects which investigate MSTD sets, many of which were mentioned in the introduction. These projects took place in a variety of settings, ranging from working with different groups (e.g., we may generalize our present setting by assuming that we draw elements independently from finite subsets
G_{N}
of a group
G
; here, we took
G_{N}=I_{N}
and
G=\mathbb{Z}
) to working with different binary operations. Though the projects themselves are varied in scope, the core themes are consistent throughout. Indeed, in many of these settings, the desired outcome was to show that a positive proportion of sets were MSTD or to show that asymptotically almost all sets failed to be MSTD, and the main rigidity arose due to dependencies resulting from overlapping representations. It may be worthwhile to revisit these works and see if mirroring our techniques might yield quick improvements on known results or settle some of the conjectures contained within.

A slightly less immediate generalization is to introduce dependencies into the binomial model itself, rather than modifying the context under which it operates. Specifically, we might assume that
A
is drawn from a probability measure
2^{\Omega_{N}}
for which the marginal laws are all
\textup{Ber}\left(p(N)\right)
, but the joint law need not be the product measure. Natural generalizations of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
for such settings would be interesting, but obtaining them will likely require substantially different ideas and techniques to deal with the additional dependence that is introduced.

Finally, recall that there is a gap between the two situations that Corollary
1.5
addresses. We leave an investigation of what happens in this unaddressed regime for future work.

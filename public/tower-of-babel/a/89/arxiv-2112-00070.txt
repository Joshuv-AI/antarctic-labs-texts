Naidan Ji, Dong Ye

Abstract

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are simple. Let
G
be a graph and and
N_{s}(G)
be the total number of
s
-cliques in
G
(a complete subgraph with
s
vertices). Particularly,
N_{2}(G)
is the number of edges of
G
, which is often denoted by
e(G)
.
The well-known Erdős-Gallai theorem
[
1
]
states that if a graph with
n
vertices has no cycle of length at least
k
where
n\geq k\geq 3
, then
e(G)\leq(k-1)(n-1)/2
, which was originally conjectured by Turán (cf.
[
6
]
). The Erdős-Gallai theorem was improved by Kopylov
[
9
]
for 2-connected graphs.

Before presenting Kopylov’s result, we need some extra notations. Let
H_{n,k,a}
be an
n
-vertex graph whose vertex set can be partitioned into three sets
A
,
B
and
C
such that
|A|=a
,
|B|=n-(k-a)
and
|C|=k-2a
, where integers
n,k
and
a
satisfy
n\geq k\geq 4
and
k/2>a\geq 1
, and whose edge set consists of all edges between
A
and
B
, and all edges in
A\cup C
. Note that
H_{n,k,a}
is 2-connected if
a\geq 2
and has no cycle longer than
k-1
. For
s\geq 2
, define

f_{s}(n,k,a)={k-a\choose s}+(n-k+a){a\choose s-1}.

Then
N_{s}(H_{n,k,a})=f_{s}(n,k,a)
and, particularly,
e(H_{n,k,a})=N_{2}(H_{n,k,a})=f_{2}(n,k,a)
. The following is Kopylov’s result for 2-connected graphs.

Theorem 1.1

(Kopylov,
[
9
]
)
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected graph on
n
vertices, and let
n\geq k\geq 5
and
t=\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor
. If
G
has no cycle of length at least
k
, then

e(G)\leq\max\{f_{2}(n,k,2),f_{2}(n,k,t)\},

and the equality holds only if
G=H_{n,k,2}
or
G=H_{n,k,t}
.

It is worth mentioning that Fan, Lv and Wang
[
4
]
proved a result slightly stronger than the above theorem for
n\geq k\geq 2n/3
. Together with a result of a result of Woodall
[
13
]
, it provided an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
. Recently, a

1.1
has been proven by Luo
[
10
]
as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Luo,
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected
n
-vertex graph, and let
n\geq k\geq 5
and
t=\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor
. If
G
has circumference less than
k
, then the number of
s
-cliques of
G
satisfies

N_{s}(G)\leq\max\{f_{s}(n,k,2),f_{s}(n,k,t)\}.

A stability result of the Theorem

is obtained Ma and Yuan
[
11
]
, which also can be viewed as the clique version of a stability result of Theorem
1.1
given by Füredi, Kostochka and Verstraëte
[
5
]
.

Another result of Erdős and Gallai in
[
1
]
shows that a graph without a path of length at least

. The result of Erdős and Gallai for paths was strengthened by Fan for 2-connected graphs (Theorem 5 in
[

]

edges has length at least

. Fan’s result is sharp when

is divisible by

, which was further sharpened by Wang and Lv
[
12
]
for all possible values

. The sharpness of the results of Fan
[
2
]
, Wang and Lv
[
12
]
can be shown by the following constructions.

Let
X_{n,k}
to be an
n
-vertex graph defined as follows. Assuming
n-2=r(k-3)+t
where
0\leq t\leq k-4
, the graph
X_{n,k}
consists of three disjoint parts

,
B
and

such that

is an edge
uv
, and

is a union of
r
vertex disjoint
(k-3)
-cliques, and

is a

-clique, and all edges between

and

. For

, define

&r{k-3\choose 2}+{t\choose 2}+2(n-2)+1&\mbox{ if }s=2.\end{aligned}\right.

Then

, and

.
These graphs

.

Theorem 1.3

(Fan
[
2
]
, Wang and Lv
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected
n
-vertex graph with
n\geq 3
. If
G
has an edge

such that
G
has no cycle of length at least
k\geq 4
containing

In
[
11
]
, Ma and Yuan made the following conjecture, which can be treated as a clique version of Theorem

. As indicated in
[
11
]
, the conjecture (if it is true) is a key tool to prove a more general stability result of of Theorem

Conjecture 1.4

(Ma and Yuan,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected
n
-vertex graph with
n\geq 3
and let

be an edge in

. Let

and

then there is a cycle on at least

Note that, the bound of the above conjecture is not the best possible for the case

because of Theorem

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be a 2-connected

-vertex graph with

. If

has an edge

has no cycle containing

,

The bound in Theorem

Let

. If

.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

-vertex graph with

. If

, then the number of

First, assume that

has a cut-vertex

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle N_{s}(G)

Another ingredient we need to prove Theorem
1.5
is
edge-switching
operation, which was introduced by Fan
[
3
]
to study subgraph covering. This operation appears in an earlier paper
[
8
]
of Klemans which studied the probabilities of the number of connected components under this operation.

Let
G
be a graph and
v
be a vertex of
G
. Let
N(v)=\{u|uv\in E(G)\}
and let
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
. The degree of
v
in

is denoted by
d_{G}(v)
which is equal to
|N(v)|
. For a given edge

,
an

from

to

is to replace each edge
vx
by a new edge

for every

. The resulting graph is called the

of
G
from
v
to
u
, denoted by
G[v\to u]
. The following lemma is a trivial observation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected graph and let
uv
be an edge of
G
.

(i)
If
N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset
and
G/uv
is not 2-connected, then
\{u,v\}
is a vertex cut of
G
.

(ii)
If
N(u)\cap N(v)\neq\emptyset
and the edge-switching graph
G[v\to u]
is not 2-connected, then
\{u,v\}
is a vertex cut of
G
.

The following lemma shows that the edge-switching operation does not increase the length of longest cycles through certain edges.

Lemma 2.4

(Ji and Chen,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
uv
be an edge. For any edge
ux
, let
k
be the length of a longest cycle of
G
containing
ux
. Then the length of a longest cycle containing
ux
in the edge-switching graph

is at most
k
.

The contraction and the edge-switching operations do not reduce the number of
s
-cliques except small values of
s
as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
uv
be an edge of
G
.

(i)
If
N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset
and
s\geq 3
, then
N_{s}(G/uv)\geq N_{s}(G)
;

(ii)
For
s\geq 2
, it holds that
N_{s}(G[v\to u])\geq N_{s}(G)
.

Proof.

(i) Since
N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset
, the graph
G
has no
s
-cliques containing
uv
for
s\geq 3
. Hence, every
s
-clique of
G
remains as an
s
-clique in
G/uv
. Hence (1) follows.

(ii) Let
S(G)
be the set of unlabeled copies of
K_{s}
in a graph
G
. Consider an edge-switching from
v
to
u
, and let
G^{\prime}=G[v\to u]
. Denote
W=\{vx|x\in N(v)\backslash N[u]\}
. Define a map
\pi:S(G)\to S(G^{\prime})
as follows,
for each
Q\in S(G)
,

\pi(Q)=\begin{cases}Q&\text{if $E(Q)\cap W=\emptyset$,}\\
Q^{\prime}&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
V(Q^{\prime})=(V(Q)\backslash\{v\})\cup\{u\}
and
E(Q^{\prime})=(E(Q)\backslash W)\cup\{ux\,|\,vx\in W\cap E(Q)\}
. If

, then

and it follows that

. All neighbors of
v
in

are neighbors of

in
Q^{\prime}
. Hence

is indeed an
s
-clique of

. So

is well-defined. Note that,
\pi(Q_{1})\neq\pi(Q_{2})
for two different

and

of

Proof of Theorem
1.5

Now, we are ready to prove our main theorem. Note that Theorem
1.5
follows from Theorem
1.3
directly for the case
s=2
. In the following, we only need to prove it for

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.
Suppose to the contrary that
G
is a counterexample. For an edge
e
of
G
, let
c_{e}(G)
be the maximum length of cycles containing
e
. Then
G
is a 2-connected
n
-vertex graph with
N_{s}(G)>g_{s}(n,k)
but does have an edge
e
such that
c_{e}(G)<k
. Let

\ell(G)=\max\{d_{G}(v)|v\mbox{ is an end-vertex of some edge }e\mbox{ with }c_{e}(G)<k\}.

Among all the counterexamples, choose
G
such that: (1) the number of vertices of
G
is as small as possible, and (2) subject to (1),
\ell(G)
is as large as possible.

Note that the theorem holds trivially for
n=3
. If
k=4
, then
G
consists of
n-2
triangles which share a common edge. Then
N_{3}(G)=n-2=g_{3}(n,4)
and
N_{s}(G)=0
for
s\geq 4
, a contradiction to that
G
is a counterexample. So in the following, assume that
n\geq 4
and
k\geq 5
.

Claim 1.

The graph
G
does not have a 2-vertex cut
\{x,y\}
such that
xy
is an edge.

Proof of Claim 1.
If not, assume that
\{x,y\}
is a vertex cut of
G
with

.
Let
H_{1}
a connected component of
G-\{x,y\}
. Further, let
G_{1}=G[V(H_{1})\cup\{x,y\}]
and
G_{2}=G-H_{1}
.
Then both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are 2-connected. For convenience, let
|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}\geq 3
, and

n_{i}-2=r_{i}(k-3)+t_{i}
and
0\leq t_{i}\leq k-4
for
i\in[2]
.

If
N_{s}(G_{i})>g_{s}(n_{i},k)
for some
i\in[2]
, without loss of generality assume
N_{s}(G_{1})>g_{s}(n_{1},k)
. Since
G
is a counterexample with the smallest number of vertices, the subgraph
G_{1}
is smaller and hence not a counterexample. Therefore,
c_{e}(G_{1})\geq k
for any edge
e\in E(G_{1})
. So
c_{e}(G)\geq c_{e}(G_{1})\geq k
for each
e\in E(G_{1})
.
For an edge
e^{\prime}\in E(G_{2})
and
e^{\prime}\neq xy
, it follows from 2-connectivity of

that
G_{2}
has a cycle

containing both

and

. Let
C
be a longest cycle of
G_{1}
containing

. Then

is a cycle of

and

c_{e^{\prime}}(G)\geq|V(C)|+|V(C^{\prime})|-2>|V(C)|\geq c_{xy}(G_{1})\geq k.

Thus,

for any edge

, a contradiction to that

e
with
c_{e}(G)<k
. Hence
N_{s}(G_{i})\leq g_{s}(n_{i},k)
holds for both

and

. Therefore,

\displaystyle N_{s}(G)

\displaystyle=N_{s}(G_{1})+N_{s}(G_{2})\leq g_{s}(n_{1},k)+g_{s}(n_{2},k)

(2)

\displaystyle=r_{1}{k-1\choose s}+{t_{1}+2\choose s}+r_{2}{k-1\choose s}+{t_{2}+2\choose s}

\displaystyle=(r_{1}+r_{2}){k-1\choose s}+{t_{1}+2\choose s}+{t_{2}+2\choose s}.

For
s\geq 3
, the following inequality holds,

{t_{1}+2\choose s}+{t_{2}+2\choose s}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &{t_{1}+t_{2}+2\choose s}\mbox{\hskip 85.35826pt if }t_{1}+t_{2}\leq k-4;\\
&{k-1\choose s}+{t_{1}+t_{2}-k+5\choose s}\mbox{\hskip 17.07182pt if }k-3\leq t_{1}+t_{2}\leq 2(k-4).\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

Note that,

and hence

, which implies

and

if

, and

N_{s}(G)\leq g_{s}(n,k).

This yields a contradiction to that
N_{s}(G)>g_{s}(n,k)
, which completes the proof of Claim 1.

Choose an edge
uv
of
G

c_{uv}(G)<k
and

Claim 2.

The vertex

Proof of Claim 2.
Suppose to the contrary that there exists a vertex

Cut finite element method for divergence free approximation of incompressible flow: a Lagrange multiplier approach
Erik Burman, Peter Hansbo, Mats G. Larson

Abstract
In this note we design a cut finite element method for a low order divergence free element applied to a boundary value problem subject to Stokes’ equations. For the imposition of Dirichlet boundary conditions we consider either Nitsche’s method or a stabilized Lagrange multiplier method. In both cases the normal component of the velocity is constrained using a multiplier, different from the standard pressure approximation. The divergence of the approximate velocities is pointwise zero over the whole mesh domain, and we derive optimal error estimates for the velocity and pressures, where the error constant is independent of how the physical domain intersects the computational mesh, and of the regularity of the pressure multiplier imposing the divergence free condition.



1
Introduction

We consider the Stokes’ equations of creeping incompressible flow with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
In this work the aim is to develop a robust and accurate cut finite element method
[
3
,
8
]
for the approximation of the Stokes’ equations using pointwise divergence free velocities. This means that the computational mesh does not respect the physical geometry, but can cut it in a quite general fashion. The combination of divergence free approximation spaces and CutFEM is known to be problematic due to the coupling of velocity and pressure on the boundary and the perturbations of the incompressibility induced by so called ghost penalty terms that are frequently used to enhance the stability of cut discretizations
[
5
,
23
,
12
]
. Unfitted finite element methods for the interface problem in incompressible elasticity was introduced in
[
1
]
using Nitsche’s method and further developed in
[
19
,
14
]
. Fictitious domain methods for the Stokes’ problem using cut elements were introduced in
[
10
,
23
]
. For an analysis of inf sup stability of unfitted FEM we refer to
[
18
]
. Unfitted FEM using stabilized Lagrange multiplier methods for Stokes’ was discussed in
[
16
]
, drawing on earlier results from
[
20
]
and
[
9
]
. None of these references treat the case of pointwise divergence free approximation. Only in the recent paper
[
22
]
an unfitted finite element method was proposed in this context using Nitsche’s method and ghost penalty. Optimal error estimates where shown and also that the solution was pointwise divergence free in the interior, but not up to the boundary. The pressure regularity also polluted the error constant unlike what one expects when using divergence free approximation. For a Darcy flow interface problem, in
[
17
]
, a new variant of ghost penalty was introduced to allow for cut elements and divergence free approximation. In this case the divergence free condition was satisfied globally.

In this contribution, we will entirely avoid ghost penalty for the velocity and pressure approximations. The idea is to instead impose the divergence-free condition over the whole computational mesh. Hence there is no need for a penalty on the pressure. The boundary condition is applied either using Nitsche’s method or a stabilized Lagrange multiplier technique of interior penalty type. In either case, the pressure term typically appearing on the boundary is decoupled from the multiplier imposing the divergence-free constraint. Either as a normal stress variable or as a separate boundary pressure. This makes the imposition of the divergence-free constraint the sole purpose of the pressure variable, unlike the classical Nitsche method for Stokes’ equations, where it also appears as a multiplier for the normal velocity component. To ensure pointwise divergence-free approximation this coupling must be broken
[
13
,
17
]
, resulting in a lack of skew symmetry of the pressure velocity coupling for Nitsche’s method. In our Nitsche variant, the bulk and boundary pressures are represented by different variables and hence completely decoupled. The Lagrange multiplier method that we propose is similar to the standard CutFEM using stabilized Lagrange multipliers
[
9
,
16
]
. For both cases, the key modification is that the velocity pressure coupling terms are integrated over the whole mesh domain; that is, these integrals are not restricted to the physical domain and do not contain any cut elements. This results in a conformity error for the pressure approximation that can be shown to only affect the pressure locally in the case of divergence-free approximation.
Hence a globally accurate pressure approximation can be constructed using post-processing with nearest neighbor extension. To keep down the technicalities, we will work with the minimal element introduced in
[
15
]
, but with some added effort, the ideas carry over to other divergence-free spaces.

2
Model problem: the Stokes’ equations

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with smooth,
non self-intersecting
, boundary
\Gamma
. Let
\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}
denote the outward pointing normal on
\Gamma
. We look for a
velocity-pressure couple
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{0}\times Q
, where
\boldsymbol{V}_{0}:=[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{d}
and
Q:=L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the set of square
integrable functions with mean zero, such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla p&=&\boldsymbol{f}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}&=&0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\boldsymbol{u}&=&0\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{array}

(2.1)

Here
\boldsymbol{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
. The weak formulation can be written, find
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{0}\times Q
such that

a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})-b(p,\boldsymbol{v})+b(q,\boldsymbol{u})=l(\boldsymbol{v}),\mbox{ for all }(\boldsymbol{v},q)\in\boldsymbol{V}_{0}\times Q,

(2.2)

where

l(\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}~\mbox{d}x,

(2.3)

a(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\boldsymbol{w}:\nabla\boldsymbol{v}~\mbox{d}x,\mbox{ with }\nabla\boldsymbol{w}:\nabla\boldsymbol{v}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla w_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}

and

b(q,\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}q\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}~\mbox{d}x.

The formulation (
2.2
) admits a unique solution with additional regularity

\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\Omega}.

(2.4)

Here and below we use the notation
a\lesssim b
for
a\leq Cb
, where
C
is a constant independent of the local mesh size and the mesh-domain configuration.

We will consider two formulations for the discretization of (2.1). In both cases, the boundary condition will be imposed weakly. To separate the effect of the pressure in the bulk from its interaction with the velocity on the boundary, we introduce a Lagrange multiplier that represents the normal stress on the boundary or the pressure part thereof. This can be done in different ways on the discrete level; but, to give details on the continuous level, it is convenient to introduce a multiplier for all components of the stress. Therefore we will instead look for
\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{V}:=[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}
and the multiplier expressing the fluid stress on the solid wall
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\boldsymbol{\Sigma}:=[H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}
. Formally, if
\nabla\boldsymbol{u}
is the matrix with columns
\nabla u_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
,

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=-(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})^{T}\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}+p\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}\mbox{ on }\Gamma.

The corresponding weak formulation reads: find
(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\lambda})\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
such that:

A[(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu})]=l(\boldsymbol{v}),\mbox{ for all }(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu})\in\boldsymbol{V}\times Q\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}.

Here the bilinear form
A
is given by

A[(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu})]:=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})-b(p,\boldsymbol{v})+b(q,\boldsymbol{u})+c(\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{v})-c(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\mu})

(2.5)

with

c(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{v})=\int_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu}~\mbox{d}s.

Unique existence of
\boldsymbol{u}
is ensured through the application of the
Lax-Milgram lemma in the space
\boldsymbol{V}_{0}\cap H^{div}_{0}
, where

H^{div}_{0}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}:\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0\}.

A unique bulk pressure
p
and boundary force
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is then guaranteed by the
Ladyzhenskaya-Babuska-Brezzi condition
[
4
]
.

3
The finite element space

To define the unfitted finite element method we let
S
denote an open polytopal domain such that
\bar{\Omega}\subset S
and
\mbox{dist}(\partial S,\Gamma)=1
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{S}
denote a quasi-uniform, conforming, shape regular tesselation
of simplexes
T
of
S
. We let
h_{T}:=\mbox{diam}(T)
. Then we extract the set of elements intersecting the physical domain

\mathcal{T}_{h}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{S}\mbox{ such that }\Omega\cap T\neq\emptyset\}.

We define the index
h
to be the mesh parameter,
h:=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
.

To distinguish the elements intersected by
\Gamma
from those in the bulk of
\Omega
we define

\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}:T\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset\}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{T}_{I}:=\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}.

The domains covered by the simplexes in the different sets are then denoted

\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}:=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}T,\quad\Omega_{\Gamma}:=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}}T,\quad\Omega_{I}:=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{I}}T.

The boundary of the mesh domain is denoted
\partial\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Clearly
\Omega\subset\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Since
\Gamma
is smooth there exists a tubular neighbourhood of
\Gamma
,
\Gamma\subset U_{\delta}
with thickness
\delta
, that does not self intersect. We assume that
h<\delta
, so that
\Omega_{\Gamma}\subset U_{\delta}
.

We denote the set of faces of the
simplexes in the set
\mathcal{T}_{X}
by
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{X})
and the subset of interior faces
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{X})
, that is faces such that
F=T\cap T^{\prime}
for
T,\,T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{X}
.
We let
Q_{h}
denote the space of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
that are
constant on each element,

Q_{h}:=\{x\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}):x|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T);\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

We let
\boldsymbol{W}_{h}
denote the space of
vectorial piecewise affine functions on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

\boldsymbol{W}_{h}:=\{v\in[H^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}:v|_{T}\in[\mathbb{P}_{1}(T)]^{d};\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

To define a finite element space for the Lagrange multiplier we
define the extended trace space as the restriction of
Q_{h}
to the elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}
,

\Sigma_{h}:=\{x\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\Gamma}):x|_{T}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(T);\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}\}\mbox{ and }\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}=[\Sigma_{h}]^{d}.

It is well known that the space
\boldsymbol{W}_{h}

is not robust for nearly incompressible elasticity and that the
velocity-pressure space
\boldsymbol{W}_{h}\times Q_{h}
is unstable for
incompressible flow problems on general meshes.
To rectify this we will enrich the space with vectorial piecewise affine bubbles, defined on a subgrid, following the design in
[
15
,
7
]
, that allows us to remain
conforming in
H^{1}
. The bubbles then allow us to define degrees of freedom on the faces. This results in an extended space, that we will denote
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
. We provide a detailed
construction of the finite element space, in
d
dimensions, in Appendix
7.1
. The
degrees of freedom are the vectorial velocities in the vertices of the macro elements and the velocity component in each face,
F=T\cap T^{\prime}
, in the direction pointing from the barycenter of triangle
T
to that of
T^{\prime}
.
We will apply
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
in the finite element method for the Stokes’ equations.
Observe that by
construction all functions
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
satisfy
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in Q_{h}
and thus the divergence is constant on each macro element.

We will need to bound quantities on
\Gamma
using quantities in the bulk. To this end we recall the
following trace inequality
[
25
]
, for all
v\in H^{1}(T)
there holds

\|v\|_{\partial T}+\|v\|_{\Gamma\cap T}\lesssim h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{T}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{T}.

(3.1)

3.1
Interpolants and approximation estimates

We recall from
[
7
]
the following interpolant that commutes with the divergence operator.
For every
\boldsymbol{v}\in[H^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}
there exists
\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}(x_{i})=i_{h}\boldsymbol{v}(x_{i})
in the vertices
x_{i}
of the macro elements, where
i_{h}
denotes the Clément
interpolant on
\boldsymbol{W}_{h}
, and for all
F\in\mathcal{F}

\int_{F}\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}~\mbox{d}s=\int_{F}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}~\mbox{d}s.

Note that the interpolant
\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}
satisfies the approximation error estimate, for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{l}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})
,

\|\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim h|\boldsymbol{u}|_{[H^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}},\quad h\|\nabla(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}+\|\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim h^{2}|\boldsymbol{u}|_{[H^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}}.

(3.2)

The proof of the existence of
\pi_{h}
is identical to that of the
interpolant for the Bernardi-Raugel element
[
2
]
, see also
[
7
]
.
Since we are interested in unfitted finite element methods we recall from
[
22
]
a stable divergence free extension from
\Omega
to
S
that we denote by
\boldsymbol{u}^{e}
. It was shown that
\boldsymbol{u}^{e}
satisfies the stability

\|\boldsymbol{u}^{e}\|_{[H^{l}(S)]^{d}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{l}(\Omega)]^{d}},\quad l=1,2.

(3.3)

It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h|\boldsymbol{u}^{e}|_{[H^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}}&\leq&C_{1}h\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}},\\[8.53581pt]
h\|\nabla(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\Omega}+\|\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h^{2}|\boldsymbol{u}^{e}|_{[H^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}}&\leq&C_{2}h^{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}},\\[8.53581pt]
\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h|\boldsymbol{u}^{e}|_{[H^{2}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}}&\leq&C_{3}h\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\end{array}

(3.4)

where the last inequality follows by applying (
3.1
) on each element in
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}
followed by the second inequality.
For the pressure variable, we will in principle extend by zero below and use standard element wise projections on piecewise constants in the bulk. We will also extend the multiplier variable
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
so that we can use approximation on the bulk mesh. For every
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}
there exists
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e}\in[H^{1}(U_{\delta})]^{d}
with
\|\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e}\|_{[H^{1}(U_{\delta})]^{d}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}}
(harmonic extension). Since
h<\delta
and
\Omega_{\Gamma}\subset U_{\delta}
we can define the approximation of
\boldsymbol{\mu}
by
(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e})|_{\Gamma}
, where
\pi_{\Gamma}:L^{2}(\Omega_{\Gamma})\rightarrow\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
is the standard
L^{2}
-projection on piecewise constant functions in
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
. Then there holds

\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e}-\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e})\|_{\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma})}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{e})\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{[H^{1/2}(\Gamma)]^{d}}.

(3.5)

The inequality (
3.5
) also follows by applying (
3.1
) on each element in
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}
followed by standard error estimates for
\pi_{\Gamma}
and finally the stability of the extension.

Below we will make use of the local average of a function
y
over some subdomain
X\subset\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
we will use the following definition,

\bar{y}^{X}:=|X|^{-1}\int_{X}y~\mbox{d}x,\mbox{ where }|X|:=\int_{X}~\mbox{d}x.

4
Finite element discretization of the model problem

We consider the finite element spaces
\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\,Q_{h}
, that were defined in the previous section, and we will propose two different approaches to the weak imposition of boundary conditions. First a full Lagrange multiplier method where the normal stress on the boundary is introduced as an independent variable and then a Nitsche type method, where the pressure on the boundary is decoupled from the pressure in the bulk in the form of an additional multiplier controlling the normal component of the velocity. The tangential component of the velocity is then imposed using Nitsche’s method. The former method, which stays very close to the continuous problem, has fewer terms that need to be evaluated and only one user defined parameter, while the latter has fewer unknowns, since only one multiplier field is added, but requires the evaluation of more boundary terms and three different stabilization parameters need to be set. Both methods can be analysed in the same fashion
and we will give full detail only for the Lagrange multiplier method. The modifications necessary for the analysis of the Nitsche type method will be outlined in remarks.

4.1
A Lagrange multiplier method

In the first method simply discretize the form (
2.5
) directly, this means adding three unknowns for the normal stress on
\Gamma
. In this case only the Lagrange multiplier needs to
be stabilized. The
finite element discretization of the problem (
2.5
) takes the form find
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
such
that

A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h})]=l(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\mbox{ for all }(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}.

(4.1)

where the bilinear form is now defined by

\displaystyle A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h})]

\displaystyle:=a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})-b_{h}(p_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b_{h}(q_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle+c(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-c(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+\gamma j(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h})

where
\gamma>0
is a stabilization parameter.
The modified form with subscript
h
is defined by

b_{h}(q_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=(q_{h},\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}.

We also introduce the vector valued stabilization term

j(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma})}\int_{F}h_{T}[\boldsymbol{v}]\cdot[\boldsymbol{w}]~\mbox{d}s.

(4.2)

Here
[x]|_{F}
denotes the jump of
x
over face
F
. For future reference we define
[x]|_{F}=x|_{F}
on all
F
in the mesh boundary, i.e. such that
F\cap\partial\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}=F
.

4.2
A Nitsche method

Here the boundary conditions on the velocities on
\Gamma
are imposed using Nitsche’s method. The velocity is enforced to be divergence free everywhere in
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
through a bulk pressure variable and an additional unknown
\varrho
is introduced for the pressure term on the boundary.
This unknown is a Lagrange multiplier imposing
\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}=0
. We formally define the discrete flux,

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\varpi_{h})=(\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h})^{T}\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\varpi_{h}\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma},

where
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a parameter that must be chosen large enough.
Replacing
\boldsymbol{u}
,
p
,
\boldsymbol{v}
and
q
in (
2.5
) by
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
,
p_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
and
q_{h}
and using the above definition of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\varrho_{h})
and

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},-\varpi_{h})
instead of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}
we propose the formulation find
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\varrho_{h})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\Sigma_{h}
such that:

A_{Nit}[(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\varrho_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\varpi_{h})]=l(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\mbox{ for all }(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\varpi_{h})\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\Sigma_{h}

(4.3)

where the bilinear form is defined by

\displaystyle A_{Nit}[(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h},\varrho_{h}),(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h},\varpi_{h})]:=a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v})-b_{h}(p_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+b_{h}(q_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})

\displaystyle-c(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\varrho_{h}),\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-c(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},-\varpi_{h}),\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+\gamma_{1}j(\varrho_{h},\varpi_{h})+\gamma_{2}g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

The stabilization terms are given by

j(y_{h},z_{h}):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma})}\int_{F}h_{T}[y_{h}][z_{h}]~\mbox{d}s

and

g(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma})}\int_{F\setminus\partial\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}h_{T}[\nabla\boldsymbol{u}_{h}]:[\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}]~\mbox{d}s.

The stabilization operator
j
is necessary to stabilize the Lagrange multiplier and
g
is a ghost penalty term that is needed to make Nitsche’s method robust independently of the mesh element intersection. Observe that compared to the method proposed in
[
22
]
the term
b_{h}
acts on the whole mesh domain
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
. The pressure appearing in the stress approximation on the other hand is an independent variable and not coupled to the bulk pressure. Note that the above formulation includes three penalty parameters, one for the penalty term in Nitsche’s method,
\gamma_{0}
, one for the ghost penalty
\gamma_{1}
and one to stabilize the boundary pressure variable,
\gamma_{2}
.

Remark 4.1

(Ghost penalty)
The formulation (
4.1
) does not have any ghost penalty term for the velocities and since the pressure velocity coupling is integrated over the whole mesh domain none is needed for the pressure or the divergence of the velocities. Optimal error estimates are obtained below also in the absence of ghost penalty. However, the linear system can be very ill-conditioned. This requires either a weakly consistent stabilizing term, whose only design condition is that
g(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}
, or an efficient preconditioner. The formulation (
4.3
) on the other hand requires control of the gradient of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
over the whole mesh domain to counter the well known instability of Nitsche’s method on unfitted meshes
[
8
]
leading to the need of a ghost penalty term in standard fashion. Since this term only acts on the velocity variable it does not perturb the divergence free property. Finally, in both cases the stabilization of the Lagrange multiplier acts on the bulk faces and therefore it both stabilizes the multiplier and provides stability for small cuts (see
[
9
]
).

Remark 4.2

(Average pressure)
Note that in the space
Q_{h}
we have not imposed the zero average condition on the pressure. This is indeed not necessary, as we shall see below, since an artificial homogeneous Dirichlet condition is imposed on the pressure variable on
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
(c.f. Remark
5.1
and Theorem
5.1
).
This fixes the constant as a function of
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
and the boundary multiplier
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}
or
\rho_{h}
. This artificial boundary condition destroys the accuracy in the cut elements, but does not influence the accuracy on the interior elements. Therefore a globally accurate pressure with zero average can be constructed using post processing (c.f. equation
5.12
and Corollary
5.1
).

5
Stability and error analysis

For the stability analysis it will be convenient to introduce the triple norm

\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|:=\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,h}+\|q\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}+j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1,h}:=\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}+\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Gamma}+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}},

and

\|q\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}:=\|q-\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}.

Remark 5.1

Observe that
\|\cdot\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}
is a norm. This is easily seen since if
q=\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}
, then
\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}=\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{\partial\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}
and hence
\|\cdot\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}=0
only if
\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}=0
. For the Lagrange multiplier method we have control of the full
H^{1}
-norm of the velocity only in
\Omega
. An extension to the mesh domain
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
can be obtained in a standard fashion using ghost penalty. This is needed in the Nitsche method both in the error analysis and for the conditioning of the linear system, whereas in the Lagrange multiplier method the role of ghost penalty is only to enhance the conditioning of the system and could in principle be replaced by preconditioning.

5.1
Stability of the Lagrange multiplier

The key difficulty in the present stability analysis is to obtain sufficient control of the Lagrange multiplier for the boundary condition without perturbing the pressure stability. First we introduce four assumptions, in the spirit of
[
6
]

Proposition 5.1

The velocity pressure pair
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
satisfies Assumption
5.1
.

Proof.

Under the assumption on the spaces (see
[
7
, Section 3.3]
and Appendix) we can choose
\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\cap[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\mathcal{T}})]^{d}
such that

\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{1}=\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}-q\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}\mbox{ and }\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\lesssim\|q-\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}},

to get

-b_{h}(q,\boldsymbol{w}_{1})=\|q-\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}.

We obtain the bound
\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\|_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}
using the trace inequality (
3.1
) followed by the scaled Poincaré inequality (see
[
11
, Appendix]
),

h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\|_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{1}\|_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}.

Using integration by parts we also have for all
q\in Q_{h}
,

b_{h}(q,\boldsymbol{w})=\sum_{T}\int_{\partial T}q\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\partial T}~\mbox{d}s.

By the construction of
\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
, we can set the vertex degrees of freedom to zero, and using only the vector degrees of freedom on the faces, we can construct
\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
so that
\int_{\partial T}q\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{\partial T}~\mbox{d}s=-\int_{\partial T}hq[q]~\mbox{d}s
and
\|\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\lesssim\|h[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
. Summing over all the elements it follows that

-b_{h}(q,\boldsymbol{w}_{2})=\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}^{2}.

Note that by applying an inverse inequality, an inverse trace inequality and by the choice of face degrees of freedom we have

\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim\|h^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\lesssim\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

We conclude by taking
\boldsymbol{w}_{q}=\boldsymbol{w}_{1}+\boldsymbol{w}_{2}
.

5.2
The global infsup condition

Here we prove a global infsup condition under the Assumptions
5.2
-
5.4
and that the velocity-pressure spaces are inf-sup stable with
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\subset Q_{h}

Theorem 5.1

Let the spaces
\boldsymbol{V}_{h},\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
satisfy Assumptions
5.2
-
5.3
.
There exists
h_{0}>0
,
\alpha>0
such that for all
\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
,
h<h_{0}
there exists
\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\times\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
such that

\alpha\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|^{2}\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]

(5.9)

and

\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|\lesssim\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|.

(5.10)

Proof.

The proof proceeds by choosing different test functions to get control of the various terms in the norm
\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|
.

1.

Control of the
H^{1}
-norm of velocities, the divergence on
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
and stabilization.

First note that since
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{V}_{h}\subseteq Q_{h}
we have

A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{v},q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\mu})]=\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\gamma j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu})+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}.

2.

Control of the
L^{2}
-norm of the pressure on
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Using Assumption
5.1
we can choose
\boldsymbol{w}=2\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}

to get

A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{w},0,0)]\geq\|q\|_{0,\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}-C(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}).

Here we used that

\displaystyle|a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w})+c(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{w})|

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma})(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\Omega}+\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\Gamma})

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma})\|q\|_{0,\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|q\|_{0,\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}+C(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}).

3.

Control of the Lagrange multiplier for the boundary condition.

Turning now to the Lagrange multiplier we use Assumption
5.2
to choose
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
such that

\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}+a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\varphi})-b_{h}(q,\boldsymbol{\varphi})\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{\varphi},0,0)]+C_{\lambda}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}-\pi_{H}\boldsymbol{\mu})\|^{2}_{\Gamma}

Using the stability of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, (
5.3
) it is straightforward to show that,

|a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\varphi})-b_{h}(q,\boldsymbol{\varphi})|\leq(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}+\|q\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}})\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\leq(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}+\|q\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}})C_{H/h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}

and hence applying also Assumption
5.3
we have

\frac{3}{4}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}-C_{H/h}(\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\|q\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}})-C_{\lambda}C_{s}j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu})\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{\varphi},0,0)].

4.

Control of
\boldsymbol{v}
on the boundary.
We use the Assumption
5.4
to choose
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
such that

\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}+\gamma j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\xi})-C_{u}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(0,0,\boldsymbol{\xi})].

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
5.5
) it follows that

\frac{3}{4}\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}-C_{u}\gamma^{2}j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu})-C_{u}\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(0,0,\boldsymbol{\xi})].

5.

Proof of the bound (
5.9
).

To get the first bound we take
\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{v}+\epsilon_{1}\boldsymbol{w}_{q}+\epsilon_{2}\boldsymbol{\varphi}
and
y=q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}
,
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\boldsymbol{\mu}+\epsilon_{3}\boldsymbol{\xi}
leading to

\displaystyle A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}),(\boldsymbol{v}+\epsilon_{1}\boldsymbol{w}_{q}+\epsilon_{2}\boldsymbol{\varphi},q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\mu}+\epsilon_{3}\boldsymbol{\xi})]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq(1-\epsilon_{1}C_{p}-\epsilon_{2}C_{H/h}-C_{u}\epsilon_{3}))\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}C_{H/h})\|q\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega}^{2}+(\gamma-\epsilon_{2}C_{\lambda}C_{s}-\epsilon_{3}C_{u}\gamma^{2})j(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(3\epsilon_{2}/4-\epsilon_{1}C_{p})\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+3\epsilon_{3}/4\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}.

First fix
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}/(4C_{p})
so that

(3\epsilon_{2}/4-\epsilon_{1}C_{p})=\epsilon_{2}/2\mbox{ and }1-\epsilon_{1}C_{p}-\epsilon_{2}C_{H/h}-C_{u}\epsilon_{3}=1-\epsilon_{2}(1/4+C_{H/h})-C_{u}\epsilon_{3}.

Now fix

sufficiently large so that
C_{H/h}<\min((8C_{p})^{-1},1/4)
then

(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}C_{H/h})\geq\epsilon_{2}(8C_{p})^{-1}

and

1-\epsilon_{2}(1/4+C_{H/h})-C_{u}\epsilon_{3}\geq 1-\epsilon_{2}/2-C_{u}\epsilon_{3}.

Since we have fixed
H/h
to make

small enough,

and

are also fixed and we can conclude by choosing
\epsilon_{2}=\min(\gamma/(4C_{\lambda}C_{s}),1/2)
and
\epsilon_{3}=1/(4C_{u})\min(1,1/\gamma)
to obtain

(\gamma-\epsilon_{2}C_{\lambda}C_{s}-\epsilon_{3}C_{u}\gamma^{2})\geq\gamma/2.

The first equality then holds with
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{4}\min_{i}\alpha_{i}

where

\alpha_{1}=1,\,\alpha_{2}=2/\gamma,\,\alpha_{3}=\gamma/(2C_{\lambda}C_{s}),\,\alpha_{3}=3/(4C_{u})\min(1,1/\gamma)),\,\alpha_{4}=\tfrac{1}{2C_{p}}\min(1,(C_{\lambda}C_{s})^{-1}).

6.

Proof of the stability (
5.10
).
We want to establish that

\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v}+\epsilon_{1}\boldsymbol{w}_{q}+\epsilon_{2}\boldsymbol{\varphi},q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\mu}+\epsilon_{3}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|\lesssim\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|.

Using the triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v}+\epsilon_{1}\boldsymbol{w}_{q}+\epsilon_{2}\boldsymbol{\varphi},q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\mu}+\epsilon_{3}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{v},q,\boldsymbol{\mu}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|+\epsilon_{1}\|\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{1,h}+\epsilon_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{1,h}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\epsilon_{3}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\Gamma}+\epsilon_{3}j(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\xi})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We consider the terms in the right hand side one by one

\|\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}+\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}+\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}_{[q]}\|_{\Gamma}.

Now observe that by definition
\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{q}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim\|q-\bar{q}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\lesssim\|q\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}
and for the boundary term we have using trace inequalities and the definition of
\boldsymbol{w}_{[q]}
,

\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}_{[q]}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim\|\nabla\boldsymbol{w}_{[q]}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}+\|h^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}_{[q]}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}}\lesssim\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[q]\|_{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma})}\lesssim\|q\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

The divergence is controlled using the stability of the average and trace inequalities,

\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\lesssim\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}},

Then observe that by (
5.2
) as seen above,

\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{1,h}\lesssim\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_{\Gamma}

and by (
5.5
)

\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\xi}\|_{\Gamma}+j(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\xi})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Gamma}.

The claim follows by collecting the bounds.

Remark 5.3

Observe that there are no constraints on
\gamma
, but the constant
\alpha
degenerates if
\gamma
becomes too small or too large. The key constraint is that the mesh must be sufficiently fine so that it is possible to satisfy both the divergence free condition and the boundary condition at the same time.

Essentially, making
H/h
larger reduces the strength of the imposition of the boundary condition on the divergence free space.

Since the method is unfitted there is no reason both the divergence free condition and the Dirichlet condition can be imposed exactly without locking. It follows that although the approximation satisfies the divergence free condition pointwise, the associated pointwise zero flow condition on the boundary is only be satisfied asymptotically.

Remark 5.4

A similar result can be proven for the Nitsche method (
4.3
), there are two main differences. First we note that for
\gamma_{0}
large enough we have using standard arguments

\|\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}+\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+\gamma_{1}j(\mu,\mu)+\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}^{2}\lesssim A_{Nit}[(\boldsymbol{v},q,\mu),(\boldsymbol{v},q+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v},\mu)].

This time we see that coercivity holds over all of
\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}
thanks to the ghost penalty term. The second difference is that since the Lagrange multiplier is a scalar, it must be paired with a scalar function for stability, the form however is
(\mu_{h}\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{\Gamma}
. To see how this can be handled it is enough to consider one
\Gamma_{i}
. We add and subtract
\bar{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma_{i}}
to obtain

(\mu\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma},\boldsymbol{v})_{\Gamma_{i}}=(\mu(\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}-\bar{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma_{i}}),\boldsymbol{v})_{\Gamma_{i}}+(\mu\bar{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma_{i}},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})_{\Gamma_{i}}.

We may now choose
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
such that for
\overline{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{\Gamma_{i}}_{i}=h\bar{\mu}^{\Gamma_{i}}\bar{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma_{i}}
and recall that
\|\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma}-\bar{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\Gamma}^{\Gamma_{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{i})}\leq C_{\Gamma}h
to obtain

(\mu\boldsymbol{n}_{\Gamma},\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{i})_{\Gamma_{i}}\geq(1-C_{\Gamma}h)\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\bar{\mu}^{\Gamma_{i}}\|_{\Gamma_{i}}^{2}-C_{\Gamma}hj(\mu,\mu).

Using this test function yields control of the boundary pressure variable
\mu
as before.
The pressure analysis carries over verbatim and of course no action needs to be taken for the control of
\boldsymbol{v}
on
\Gamma
, which is controlled through the Nitsche penalty term.

5.3
Error analysis

We now prove an error bound.
Let
\boldsymbol{u}^{e}
denote a stable divergence free extension of
\boldsymbol{u}
(see
[
22
]
). Let
\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}\in\boldsymbol{V}_{h}
be the interpolant introduced above which preserves the divergence free property.
Let
\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}
be the
L^{2}
-projection of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}
on
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{h}
. Finally we define
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}
to be the local

-projection on piecewise constants in every element in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, defined by

(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p,q)_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}=(p,q)_{\Omega},\quad\forall q\in Q_{h}.

(5.11)

Note that this projection does not produce an accurate approximation of
p
in the cells in
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}
. This is of no importance below, because the role of the projection is to make the pressure disappear, not to approximate it. An optimal global pressure is then obtained using post processing. Note however that by definition
\overline{\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}=\bar{p}^{\Omega}=0
.

Theorem 5.2

(Error estimate for the discrete error)
Under our assumptions on
\Omega
the exact solution satisfies
\boldsymbol{u}\in[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}
. There holds

\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h},\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|\lesssim h(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}}).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{e}_{h}=\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}=\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}
and
\omega_{h}=\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h}
. Using the inf-sup stability of Theorem
5.1
we have

\displaystyle\alpha\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{e}_{h},\omega_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A_{h}[(\boldsymbol{e}_{h},\omega_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}),(\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]

\displaystyle=A_{h}[(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e},\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p,\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]-l(\boldsymbol{w})

\displaystyle=A_{h}[(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e},\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p,\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]-A[(\boldsymbol{u},p,\boldsymbol{\lambda}),(\boldsymbol{w},y,\boldsymbol{\zeta})]

\displaystyle=a(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})-b_{h}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}p,\boldsymbol{w})+b(p,\boldsymbol{w})+c(\boldsymbol{w},\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle-c(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\zeta})+\gamma j(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e},\boldsymbol{\zeta}).

By the definition (
5.11
) of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}
we see that, since
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\in Q_{h}
,

b_{h}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p,\boldsymbol{w})-b(p,\boldsymbol{w})=(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w})_{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}-(p,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{w})_{\Omega}=0.

We now bound the remaining terms of the right hand side using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the approximation bounds (
3.4
), (
3.5
). First consider the weak Laplacian,

a(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})\lesssim\|\nabla(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\Omega}\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{w},0,0\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{w},0,0\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|.

For the multiplier terms we have

c(\boldsymbol{w},\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda})\lesssim\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda})\|_{\Gamma}\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}}\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{w},0,0\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|,

c(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\zeta})\lesssim\|h^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}-\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\Gamma}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|0,0,\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|,

and since
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}|_{\Omega_{\Gamma}}\in[H^{1}(\Omega_{\Gamma})]^{d}
,

j(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e},\boldsymbol{\zeta})=j(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e},\boldsymbol{\zeta})\lesssim j(\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e},\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e})^{\frac{1}{2}}j(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\zeta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim h\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}}\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|0,0,\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|.

Collecting the above bounds we obtain

\alpha\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{e}_{h},\omega_{h},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|^{2}\lesssim h(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}})\left\|{\hskip-2.6pt}\left|\boldsymbol{w},0,\boldsymbol{\zeta}\right|{\hskip-2.6pt}\right\|.

The claim follows using the second inequality of Theorem
5.1
.

Observe that this does only lead to an optimal error estimate uniformly in the domain for the continuous variables
\boldsymbol{u}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. The pressure approximation deteriorates on the boundary due to the nonconsistency of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p
. However, using local postprocessing we obtain an approximation of the pressure that has optimal order of approximation. To this end we define the canonical extension of a polynomial
x
defined on a simplex

to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
E_{T}x
. For all triangles

we introduce a mapping

that to each element in

associates an element in
\mathcal{T}_{I}
a distance

away. For a detailed discussion of the mapping
S_{h}
we refer to

we then define

q_{h}^{e}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega_{I}\\
E_{S_{h}(T)}q_{h}-\bar{q}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\mbox{ for }x\in T\subset\Omega_{\Gamma}\end{array}\right..

(5.12)

Now for each pair

Corollary 5.1

There holds

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h})\|_{\Gamma}\lesssim h(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}})

\|p-p_{h}^{e}\|_{\Omega}\lesssim h(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}}+\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)})

Proof.

The first two terms on the left hand side are bounded using the triangle inequality,

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}-\pi_{h}\boldsymbol{u}^{e}\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}}

and

\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\|_{\Gamma}\leq\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}-\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}\|_{\Gamma}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\pi_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{e}\|_{\Gamma}

and we see that the first terms of the right hand sides satisfies the bound thanks to Theorem
5.2
. The second is bounded by the interpolation estimate (
3.4
) and (
3.5
). For the bound on the pressure we see that by the definition of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}
there holds

\|p-\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|_{\Omega_{I}}\lesssim h\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{I})}

and the interior bound

\|p-p^{e}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{I}}\lesssim\|p-\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|_{\Omega_{I}}+\underbrace{\|p^{e}_{h}-\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|_{\Omega_{I}}}_{\lesssim\|p_{h}-\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\lesssim h(\|p\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{I})}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{[H^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}}+\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\|_{[H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)]^{d}})

follows by using a triangle inequality and the discrete error bound of Theorem
5.2
.
By the definition of
p_{h}^{e}
we see that

\|p-p_{h}^{e}\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\|p-p_{h}^{e}\|_{\Omega_{I}}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}}\|p^{e}-p_{h}^{e}\|^{2}_{T}.

For each term of the sum in the right hand side we add and subtract
(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p)^{e}
,

\|p^{e}-p_{h}^{e}\|_{T}\leq\|E_{S_{h}(T)}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h})-\bar{p}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{T}+\|p^{e}-E_{S_{h}(T)}\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|_{T}.

(5.13)

For the first term in the right hand side we see that

\|E_{S_{h}(T)}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h})-\bar{p}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{T}\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h}-\bar{p}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{S_{h}(T)}

and summing over the elements we obtain

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}}\|E_{S_{h}(T)}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h})-\bar{p}_{h}^{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}}\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h}^{e}\|_{S_{h}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\underbrace{\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p^{e}_{h}\|_{\Omega_{I}}}_{\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-p_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}.

The right hand side is once again bounded by Theorem
5.2
.
For the second term on the right hand side of (
5.13
) we add and subtract
\pi_{B}(p^{e})
to obtain

\|p^{e}-E_{S_{h}(T)}\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|^{2}_{T}\lesssim\|p^{e}-\pi_{B}(p^{e})\|_{B_{T,S_{h}}}^{2}+\|E_{S_{h}(T)}\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-\pi_{B}(p^{e})\|_{T}^{2}.

For the second term of the right hand side we have using the definition of
E_{S_{h}(T)}
and the local stability of
\pi_{\mathcal{T}}
for elements in
\mathcal{T}_{I}
,

\|E_{S_{h}(T)}\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-\pi_{B}p\|_{T}^{2}=\|E_{S_{h}(T)}(\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p-\pi_{B}p)\|_{T}^{2}\lesssim\|\pi_{\mathcal{T}}(p-\pi_{B}(p^{e}))\|_{S_{h}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|p^{e}-\pi_{B}(p^{e})\|_{B_{T,S_{h}}}^{2}.

It follows that

\|p^{e}-E_{S_{h}(T)}\pi_{\mathcal{T}}p\|^{2}_{T}\lesssim\|p-\pi_{B}p\|_{B_{T,S_{h}}}^{2}\lesssim h^{2}|p^{e}|_{H^{1}(B_{T,S_{h}})}^{2}

and the conclusion follows using the stability of the extension and the finite overlap of the balls
B_{T,S_{h}}
.

Remark 5.6

Observe that the estimate on the stresses is in an h-weighted

6.2
Pressure robustness

We consider a problem with Coriolis force, following John et al.

7
Conclusions

We developed two different cut finite element methods for the approximation of incompressible viscous flow using pointwise divergence free elements. The key observations was to enforce the divergence free condition globally on the computational mesh and separate the bulk pressure approximation from the boundary pressure approximation. Optimal error bounds were derived with upper bounds for the velocity error independent of the pressure regularity. The addition of degrees of freedom for the boundary pressure leads to a slightly more complicated method, on the other hand fewer integrals over cut elements have to be evaluated so the assembly of the system may not necessarily be more expensive. In view of the rather complete analysis obtained we consider this a small price to pay. One possible extension of the present approach is to
H(div)
-conforming approximation using the Raviart-Thomas space. This element has similar stability structure as the
H^{1}
-conforming element considered herein and one would therefore expect to obtain robustness and optimality for similar unfitted approximations of Darcy’s equation.

\boldsymbol{x}_{F}

\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{T}=-\frac{|T|-|T_{F}|}{|T_{F}|}\quad\text{on $T_{F}$}.

Using again the same observation we have

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{F}=\frac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\nabla\cdot((\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T})\varphi_{F})=\frac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\quad\text{on $\mathcal{S}(T_{F})$}

and by definition

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{F}=0\quad\text{on $T\setminus T_{F}$}.

Since
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{T}=\nabla\cdot(\alpha(\boldsymbol{x}_{T}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T,F})\varphi_{T})=\alpha
on

T\setminus T_{F}
we seek
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}-\alpha\frac{|T|-|T_{F}|}{|T_{F}|}=\alpha,

which gives

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{|T_{F}|}{|T|}\frac{1}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}

and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{F}=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{F}\varphi_{F}+\frac{|T_{F}|}{|T|}\frac{\boldsymbol{x}_{T}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T,F}}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\varphi_{T},

with divergence

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{F}=\alpha\quad\text{on $T$}.

In the case when
\boldsymbol{x}_{T}
is the barycenter of
T
we have the identity

\displaystyle|T_{F}|=\frac{1}{d+1}|T|

(7.1)

and we get the simplified expression

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{F}=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{F}\varphi_{F}+\frac{1}{d+1}\frac{\boldsymbol{x}_{T}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T,F}}{\|\boldsymbol{x}_{F}-\boldsymbol{x}_{T}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\varphi_{T}.

This is the explicit expression for the finite element face bubbles and coincides with the discussion of
[
7
, Section 3]
.

Finally, to obtain a global continuous finite element space we chose
\boldsymbol{x}_{F}
on an interior face
F
shared by two
elements
T
and
T^{\prime}
as the intersection between the line that passes through the barycenters of
T
and
T^{\prime}
and the face
F
, see Figure
3
. Then we have
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{F}=-\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\prime}_{F}
and therefore we can define a global continuous basis function associated with the face
F

For faces on the boundary we take
\boldsymbol{x}_{F}
to be the barycenter of
F
.

7.2
Proof of Estimate (
5.6
)

It is enough to consider one component
\Gamma_{i}
. Define
\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}}:=\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}:T\cap\Gamma_{i}\neq\emptyset\}
. By the definition of the average we have for all
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\bar{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{\Gamma_{i}}\|_{\Gamma_{i}}\leq h^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\Gamma_{i}}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}}}.

Now fix
\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{\mu}|_{T}
for some
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}}
. Clearly then for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}}

\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{y}\|_{T}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}})}\|h^{\frac{1}{2}}[\boldsymbol{\mu}]\|_{F}^{2}
. Since
M
is bounded we conclude by summing over
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma_{i}}
.

7.3
Construction of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}

It is sufficient to consider one component of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
. Let
\varphi
denote any component of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
.
Let
B_{i}
be the largest ball with center
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
on
\Gamma_{i}
, such that
B_{i}\cap\Omega_{\mathcal{T}}\subset P_{i}
, see Figure
1
. We assume that its radius is
r_{i}
. By the construction of
P_{i}
we may assume that
r_{i}=O(Mh)
. Define the function
\phi(\boldsymbol{x})=max(1-|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}|/r_{i},0)
. It follows that
\phi(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})=1
and
\phi(\boldsymbol{x})=0
for
\boldsymbol{x}\neq B_{i}
. It follows by construction that
\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in P_{i}}|\nabla\phi(\boldsymbol{x})|\lesssim(Mh)^{-1}
and there exists
c_{0}>0
so that
\overline{\phi}^{\Gamma_{i}}>c_{0}
. Let
\psi_{h}=i_{h}\phi
, where
i_{h}
is the nodal interpolant in the vertices of the mesh, and normalize with
0<c_{1}=\overline{\psi}_{h}^{\Gamma_{i}}
so that
\phi_{h}:=c_{1}^{-1}\psi_{h}
and
\overline{\phi}_{h}^{\Gamma_{i}}=1
. We may then take
\varphi=ha\phi_{h}
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
. It remains to prove that
\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\Gamma_{i}}+(Mh)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{P_{i}}\lesssim M^{-1}\|a\|_{\Gamma_{i}}
.
By the construction of
\varphi
we have since
\|\nabla\phi_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{i})}\lesssim c_{1}^{-1}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{i})}
,

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\Gamma_{i}}\lesssim c_{1}^{-1}ah\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{i})}(Mh)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\lesssim c_{1}^{-1}ah(Mh)^{-1}(Mh)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

Since
a(Mh)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\lesssim\|a\|_{\Gamma_{i}}
we conclude that

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\Gamma_{i}}\lesssim M^{-1}\|a\|_{\Gamma_{i}}.

On the patch
P_{i}
we have similarly

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{P_{i}}\lesssim c_{1}^{-1}ah\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(P_{i})}(Mh)^{\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim c_{1}^{-1}h^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|a\|_{\Gamma_{i}}.

The Strongly Coupled Polaron on the Torus: Quantum Corrections to the Pekar Asymptotics
Dario Feliciangeli and Robert Seiringer

Abstract
. We investigate the Fröhlich polaron model on a three-dimensional torus, and give a proof of the second-order quantum corrections to its ground-state energy in the strong-coupling limit. Compared to previous work in the confined case, the translational symmetry (and its breaking in the Pekar approximation) makes the analysis substantially more challenging.



1.
Introduction

The underlying physical system we are interested in studying is that of a charged particle (e.g., an electron) interacting with the quantized optical modes of a polar crystal (called phonons). In this situation, the electron excites the phonons by inducing a polarization field, which, in turn, interacts with the electron.
In the case of a ‘large polaron’ (i.e., when the De Broglie wave-length of the electron is much larger than the lattice spacing in the medium), this system is described by the Fröhlich Hamiltonian
[
10
]
, which represents a simple and well-studied model of non-relativistic quantum field theory (see
[
1
,
8
,
11
,
20
,
27
,
28
]
for properties, results and further references).

A key parameter that appears in the problem is the coupling constant, usually denoted by
\alpha
. We study the strong coupling regime of the model, i.e., its asymptotic behavior as
\alpha\to\infty
. In this limit, the ground state energy of the Fröhlich Hamiltonian agrees to leading order with the prediction of the Pekar approximation
[
24
]
, which assumes a classical behavior for the phonon field.
This was first proved in
[
4
]
, using a path integral approach (see also
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, for recent work on the Pekar process
[
28
]
). Later, the result was improved in
[
18
]
, by providing explicit bounds on the leading order correction term.

The object of our study is, precisely, the main correction to the classical (Pekar) approximation of the polaron model, i.e., the leading error term in the aforementioned asymptotics for the ground state energy. Such correction is expected to be of order
O(\alpha^{-2})
smaller than the leading term, and arises from the quantum fluctuations about the classical limit
[
2
]
. This claim was first verified rigorously in
[
9
]
, where both the electron and the phonon field are confined to a bounded domain (of linear size adjusted to the natural length scale set by the Pekar ansatz) with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Such restriction breaks translation invariance and simplifies the structure of the Pekar problem in comparison with the unconfined case, guaranteeing, at least in the case of the domain being a ball
[
6
]
, uniqueness up to phase of the Pekar minimizers and non-degeneracy of the Hessian of the Pekar functional.
We build upon the strategy developed in
[
9
]
to treat the ultraviolet singularity of the model, which in turn relies on multiple application of the Lieb–Yamazaki commutator method
[
19
]
and a subsequent use of Nelson’s Gross transformation
[
13
,
23
]
.

The key novelty of the present work is to deal with a translation invariant setting.
We investigate the quantum correction to the Pekar approximation of the polaron model on a torus, and prove the validity of the predictions in
[
2
]
also in this setting. As a first step, we analyze the structure of the set of minimizers of the corresponding Pekar functional, proving uniqueness of minimizers up to symmetries, which
was so far known to hold only in the unconfined case
[
16
,
15
]
and on balls with Dirichlet boundary conditions
[
6
]
. The translation invariance leads to a degeneracy of the Hessian of the Pekar functional and corresponding zero modes, substantially complicating the analysis of the quantum fluctuations. In order to ‘flatten’ the surface of minimizers, we introduce a convenient diffeomorphism inspired by formal computations in
[
14
]
, which effectively allows us to decouple the zero modes.

2.
Setting and Main Results

2.1.
The Model

We consider a
3
-dimensional flat torus of side length
L>0
. We denote by
\Delta_{L}
the Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
and by
(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)
the integral kernel of its ‘inverse’, which we define by

(2.1.1)

\begin{cases}&(-\Delta_{L})\left[(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(\,\cdot\,,y)\right]=\delta_{y}\\
&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)dx=0.\end{cases}

An explicit formula for
(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)
is given by

(2.1.2)

(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}\frac{e^{ik\cdot(x-y)}}{L^{3}},

which, for any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
, yields an
L^{2}
function of
y
, its Fourier coefficients being in
\ell^{2}
. Analogously we define
(-\Delta_{L})^{-s}
for any
s>0
. In the following, we identify
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
with the box
[-L/2,L/2]^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and the Laplacian with the corresponding one on
[-L/2,L/2]^{3}
with periodic boundary conditions.

Let

(2.1.3)

\displaystyle v_{L}(y):=(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}(0,y)=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{|k|}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot y}}{L^{3}},

and
v^{x}_{L}(y):=v_{L}(y-x)
. The Fröhlich Hamiltonian
[
10
]
for the polaron is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{L}

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta_{L})\otimes 1\!\!1+1\!\!1\otimes\mathbb{N}-a(v^{x}_{L})-a^{\dagger}(v^{x}_{L})

(2.1.4)

\displaystyle\;=(-\Delta_{L})\otimes 1\!\!1+1\!\!1\otimes\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{k}\right)-\frac{1}{L^{3/2}}\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{|k|}\left(a_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}+a^{\dagger}_{k}e^{-ik\cdot x}\right),

acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
, where
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
denotes the bosonic Fock space over
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. The number operator, denoted by
\mathbb{N}
, accounts for the field energy, whereas
-\Delta_{L}
accounts for the electron kinetic energy. The creation and annihilation operators for a plane wave of momentum
k
are denoted by
a_{k}^{\dagger}
and
a_{k}
, respectively, and they are assumed to satisfy the rescaled canonical commutation relations

(2.1.5)

\displaystyle[a_{k},a^{\dagger}_{j}]=\alpha^{-2}\delta_{k,j}.

In light of (
2.1.5
),
\mathbb{N}
has spectrum
\sigma(\mathbb{N})=\alpha^{-2}\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. We note that the definition (
2.1
) is somewhat formal, since
v_{L}\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. It is nevertheless possible to define
\mathbb{H}_{L}
via the associated quadratic form, and to find a suitable domain on which it is self-adjoint and bounded from below (see
[
12
]
, or Remark
4.4
in Section
4
below).

We shall investigate the ground state energy of
\mathbb{H}_{L}
, for fixed
L
and
\alpha\to\infty
.

Remark 2.1
.

By rescaling all lengths by
\alpha
,
\mathbb{H}_{L}
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
\alpha^{-2}\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{L}
, where
\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{L}
can be written compactly as

(2.1.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{L}=(-\Delta_{\alpha^{-1}L})\otimes 1\!\!1-\sqrt{\alpha}\left[\tilde{a}(v_{\alpha^{-1}L}^{x})+\tilde{a}^{\dagger}(v_{\alpha^{-1}L}^{x})\right]+1\!\!1\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{N}},

with the creation and annihilation operators
\tilde{a}^{\dagger}
and
\tilde{a}
now satisfying the (un-scaled) canonical commutation relations
[\tilde{a}(f),\tilde{a}^{\dagger}(g)]=\bra{f}\ket{g}
, and
\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
the corresponding number operator. Large
\alpha
hence corresponds to the strong-coupling limit of a polaron confined to a torus of side length
L\alpha^{-1}
. We find it more convenient to work in the variables defined in (
2.1
), however.

Remark 2.2
.

The Fröhlich polaron model is typically considered without confinement, i.e., as a model on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with electron-phonon coupling function given by
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1/2}(x,y)=(2\pi^{2})^{-1}|x-y|^{-2}
. In the confined case studied in
[
9
]
,
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was replaced by a bounded domain
\Omega
, and thus the electron-phonon coupling function was given by
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1/2}(x,y)
, where
\Delta_{\Omega}
denotes the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
. The latter setting, similarly to ours, has the advantage of guaranteeing compactness for the corresponding inverse Laplacian, which is a key technical ingredient both for
[
9
]
and our main results. In addition, for generic domains
\Omega
the Pekar functional has a unique minimizer up to phase (which is proved in
[
6
]
for
\Omega
a ball, and enters the analysis in
[
9
]
for general
\Omega
as an assumption).
Compared with
[
9
]
, setting the problem on the torus (or on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
) introduces the extra difficulty of having to deal with translation invariance and a whole continuum of Pekar minimizers. Hence the present work can be seen as a first step in the direction of generalizing the results of
[
9
]
to the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

2.2.
Pekar Functional(s)

For
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,
\|\psi\|_{2}=1
, and
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, we introduce the classical energy functional corresponding to (
2.1
) as

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi):=\expectationvalue{h_{\varphi}}{\psi}+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2},

where
h_{\varphi}
is the Schrödinger operator

(2.2.2)

\displaystyle h_{\varphi}:=-\Delta_{L}+V_{\varphi},\quad V_{\varphi}:=-2(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\varphi.

We define the Pekar energy as

(2.2.3)

\displaystyle e_{L}:=\min_{\psi,\varphi}\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi).

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it was shown in
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
that the infimum of the spectrum of the Fröhlich Hamiltonian converges to the minimum of the corresponding classical energy functional as
\alpha\to\infty
. In
[
9
]
, it was shown that the same holds for the model confined to a bounded domain with Dirichlet boundary conditions and the subleading correction in this asymptotics was computed. Our goal is to extend the results of
[
9
]
to the case of
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
.

We define the two functionals

(2.2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi):=\min_{\varphi}\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi),\quad\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi):=\min_{\psi}\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi),

and their respective sets of minimizers

(2.2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\,|\,\|\psi\|_{2}=1,\;\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)=e_{L}\right\},

(2.2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}:=\{\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\,\,|\,\,\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)=e_{L}\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is invariant under translations and changes of phase and
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is invariant under translations. It is thus useful to introduce the notation

(2.2.7)

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\psi):=\{e^{i\theta}

\displaystyle\psi^{y}(\,\cdot\,):=e^{i\theta}\psi(\,\cdot\,-y)\,\,|\,\,\theta\in[0,2\pi),\,y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\},

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle\Omega_{L}(\varphi)=\{\varphi^{y}\,\,|\,\,y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\},

for
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, respectively.

Our first result, Theorem
2.3
(or, more precisely, Corollary
2.4
) is a fundamental ingredient to prove our main result, Theorem
2.5
. It concerns the uniqueness of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
up to symmetries and shows the validity of a quadratic lower bound for
\mathcal{E}_{L}
in terms of the
H^{1}
-distance from the surface of minimizers. We shall prove these properties for sufficiently large
L
.

Theorem 2.3

(Uniqueness of Minimizers and Coercivity for
\mathcal{E}_{L}
)
.

There exist
L_{1}>0
and a positive constant
\kappa_{1}
independent of
L
, such that for
L>L_{1}
there exists
0<\psi_{L}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
such that

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle e_{L}<0,\quad\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}=\Theta_{L}(\psi_{L}).

Moreover
\psi_{L}^{y}\neq\psi_{L}
for any
0\neq y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
and, for any
L^{2}
-normalized
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,

(2.2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(f)-e_{L}\geq\kappa_{1}\dist^{2}_{H^{1}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L},f\right).

These properties of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
translate easily to analogous properties for the functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
, as stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4

(Uniqueness of Minimizers and Coercivity for
\mathcal{F}_{L}
)
.

For
L>L_{1}
(where
L_{1}
is the same as in Theorem
2.3
) there exists
\varphi_{L}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
such that

(2.2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}=\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}).

Moreover, with
\psi_{L}
as in Theorem
2.3
, we have

(2.2.12)

\displaystyle\varphi_{L}=\sigma_{\psi_{L}}:=(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}|\psi_{L}|^{2},\quad\psi_{L}=\text{unique positive g.s. of }h_{\varphi_{L}}.

Finally, there exists
\kappa^{\prime}>0
independent of
L
such that, for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,

(2.2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)-e_{L}

\displaystyle\geq\min_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\expectationvalue{\unit-(\unit+\kappa'(-\Delta_L)^{1/2})^{-1}}{\varphi-\varphi_L^y}+\left|L^{-3/2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\varphi\right|^{2},

and this implies

(2.2.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)-e_{L}\geq\tau_{L}\dist_{L^{2}}^{2}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L},\varphi)

with
\tau_{L}:=\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(2\pi/L)^{2}}{1+\kappa^{\prime}(2\pi/L)^{2}}
.

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, similar results are known to hold. In particular, the analogue of (
2.2.9
) was shown in
[
16
]
and the analogue of (
2.2.10
) follows from the results in
[
15
]
. In the case of a bounded domain with Dirichlet boundary conditions, an equivalent formulation of Theorem
2.3
was taken as working assumption in
[
9
]
. In the case of a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with Dirichlet boundary conditions, the analogue of Theorem
2.3
was proved in
[
6
]
. In both the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and of balls, rotational symmetry plays a key role in the proof of these results. Rotational symmetry is not present in our setting, hence a different approach is required. Our method of proof of Theorem
2.3
relies on a comparison of the models on
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, for large
L
. As a consequence, our analysis does not easily yield quantitative estimates on
L_{1}
.

To state our main result, which also holds in the case
L>L_{1}
, we need to introduce the Hessian of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
at its unique (up to translations) minimizer
\varphi_{L}
,

(2.2.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon\phi)-e_{L}\right)=:\expectationvalue{\HF}{\phi}\quad\forall\phi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}).

An explicit computation gives (see Proposition
3.15
)

(2.2.16)

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}=1\!\!1-4(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\psi_{L}

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{h_{\varphi_{L}}-\infspec h_{\varphi_{L}}}\psi_{L}(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2},

where
h_{\varphi_{L}}
is defined in (
2.2.2
),
\psi_{L}
is interpreted as a multiplication operator and
Q_{\psi_{L}}:=1\!\!1-\ket{\psi_L}\bra{\psi_L}
. Clearly, by minimality of
\varphi_{L}
,
H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}\geq 0
, and it is also easy to see that
H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}\leq 1
. We shall show that
H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}
has a three-dimensional kernel, given by
\spn\{\partial_{j}\varphi_{L}\}_{j=1}^{3}
,
corresponding to the invariance under translations of the functional. Note that we could define the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
at any other minimizer
\varphi_{L}^{y}
, obtaining a unitarily equivalent operator
H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}^{y}}
.

2.3.
Main Result

Recall the definition (
2.2.3
) for the Pekar energy
e_{L}
as well as (
2.2.16
) for the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
at its minimizers, for
L>L_{1}
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
L>L_{1}
, as
\alpha\to\infty

(2.3.1)

\displaystyle\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}=e_{L}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr\left(1\!\!1-\sqrt{H_{\varphi_{L}}^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}}\right)+o(\alpha^{-2}).

More precisely, the bounds

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle-C_{L}\alpha^{-1/7}\leq\alpha^{2}\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}-\alpha^{2}e_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left(1\!\!1-\sqrt{H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}}\right)\leq C_{L}\alpha^{-2/11}

hold for some
C_{L}>0
and
\alpha
sufficiently large.

The trace appearing in (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
) is over
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. Note that, since
H^{\mathcal{F}_{L}}_{\varphi_{L}}\leq 1
, the coefficient of
\alpha^{-2}
in (
2.3.1
) is negative.

In the case of bounded domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions, an analogue of Theorem
2.5
was proven in
[
9
]
(where logarithmic corrections appear in the bounds that correspond to (
2.3.2
) as a consequence of technical complications due to the boundary). Showing the validity of an analogous result on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
still remains an open problem, however. Indeed, the constant
C_{L}
appearing in the lower bound in (
2.3.2
) diverges as
L\to\infty
. This is mainly due to the lack of compactness of the resolvent of the full-space Laplacian (which leads, for instance, to a zero lower bound in (
2.2.14
) and, in particular, a divergence of the effective number of modes in (
4.3.2
)). On the other hand, our method of proof used in Section
4.1
to show the upper bound in (
2.3.2
) does apply, with little modifications, to the full space case. In any case, both the upper and lower bound are expected to hold in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as well
[
2
,
14
,
9
,
27
]
.

Compared to the results obtained in
[
9
]
, Theorem
2.5
deals with the additional complication of the invariance under translations of the problem, which implies that the set of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is a three-dimensional manifold. This substantially complicates the proof of the lower bound in (
2.3.2
), as we shall see in Section
4.3
. In particular, we need to perform a precise local study around the manifold of minimizers
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
, which we carry out by introducing a suitable diffeomorphism (inspired by
[
14
]
).

Remark 2.6

(Small
L
Regime)
.

As we show in Lemma
3.2
, there exists
L_{0}>0
such that the analogue of Theorem
2.3
for
L<L_{0}
can be proven with a few-line-argument. In this case,
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is simply non-negative and is therefore minimized by the constant function. In particular,
e_{L}=0
and
\varphi_{L}=0
.

Also an analogue of Theorem
2.5
can be proven in the regime
L<L_{0}
, i.e., it is possible to show that for
L<L_{0}
there exists
C_{L}>0
such that

(2.3.3)

\displaystyle-C_{L}\alpha^{-1/7}\leq\alpha^{2}\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{L^{3}|k|^{4}}}\right)\leq C_{L}\alpha^{-2/11}

for large
\alpha
.
In this case (unlike the regime
L>L_{1}
where the set of minimizers
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}
is a three-dimensional manifold)
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}
only consists of the
0
function, and this allows to follow essentially the same arguments of
[
9
]
(with only small modifications, which are also needed in the regime
L>L_{1}
and hence are discussed in this paper). We shall therefore not carry out the details of this analysis here.

Whether uniqueness of Pekar minimizers up to symmetries holds for
all

L>0
(i.e., also in the regime
L_{0}\leq L\leq L_{1}
) remains an open problem.

Throughout the paper, we use the word
universal
to describe any constant (which is generally denoted by
C
) or property that is independent of all the parameters involved and in particular independent of
L
, for
L\geq L_{0}
(for some fixed
L_{0}>0
). Also, we write
a\lesssim b
whenever
a\leq Cb
for some universal and positive
C
. We write
C_{L}
whenever a constant depends on
L
but is otherwise universal with respect to all other parameters. Finally, we write
a\lesssim_{L}b
whenever
a\leq C_{L}b
for some positive
C_{L}
.

2.4.
Proof Strategy and Structure of the Paper

In Section
3
we study the properties of the Pekar functionals
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}
defined in (
2.2.4
). We start by recalling the relevant properties of the Pekar functionals on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in Section
3.1
.
In the long Section
3.2
we give the proof of Theorem
2.3
. Our method of proof relies on showing the convergence, as
L\to\infty
, of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
to its full-space counterpart
\mathcal{E}
. Proposition
3.5
in Section
3.2.1
formalizes the precise meaning of this convergence.
Then, in Section
3.2.2
, we prove a stronger notion of convergence, namely that the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
at any minimizer converges to the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at a corresponding minimizer (in the sense of Proposition
3.7
); in particular, it is strictly positive above its trivial zero modes for large
L
. By combining the results obtained in Sections
3.2.1
and
3.2.2
, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.3
in Section
3.2.3
. Section
3.3
is dedicated to the investigation of the properties of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
. First, in Section
3.3.1
, we show the validity of Corollary
2.4
.
Subsequently we compute the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
(in Proposition
3.15
in Section
3.3.2
) and characterize its kernel (in Proposition
3.18
in Section
3.3.3
). Finally, in Section
3.3.3
we introduce a family of weighted norms (see (
3.3.46
)) which is of key importance in Section
4
and we show, in Lemma
3.17
, that the surface of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
locally admits a unique projection w.r.t. any of these norms.

In Section
4
we prove Theorem
2.5
. First of all, in Section
4.1
we construct a trial state and use it to obtain an upper bound to the ground state energy of
\mathbb{H}_{L}
.
This is carried out using the
Q
-space representation of the bosonic Fock space
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
(see
[
25
]
) and follows ideas contained in
[
9
]
, with only small modifications. The remaining sections are devoted to the lower bound. In Section
4.2
, we show that it is possible to apply an ultraviolet cutoff on momenta of size larger than some
\Lambda
to
\mathbb{H}_{L}
at an expense of order
\Lambda^{-5/2}
(see Proposition
4.5
). This is proven following closely the approach used in
[
9
]
: as a first step we apply a triple Lieb–Yamazaki bound
[
19
]
(in Section
4.2.1
) and then make use of a Gross transformation
[
13
,
23
]
(in Section
4.2.2
). In Section
4.3
we show the validity of the lower bound in (
2.3.2
), thus completing the proof of Theorem
2.5
. With Proposition
4.5
at hand, we have good estimates on the cost of applying an ultraviolet cutoff to
\mathbb{H}_{L}
and this allows to reduce the problem to a finite dimensional one (with dimension
N
diverging as
\alpha\to\infty
). We adopt a similar strategy to
[
9
]
, using IMS localization to split the space into an inner region close to the surface of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
and an outer region far away from it. The goal is to extract the relevant quantum correction to the ground state energy from the inner region and to show, using the bound (
2.2.14
), that the outer region contributes only as an error term. Compared to
[
9
]
, the translation invariance substantially complicates the analysis. In contrast to the case considered in
[
9
]
, the set of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is a three-dimensional manifold and does not only consist of a single function. Hence, in order to treat the inner region and decouple the zero-modes of the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
, we have to introduce a suitable diffeomorphism (see Definition
4.7
in Section
4.3.1
) that ’flattens’ the manifold of minimizers and the region close to it. It is here where we make use Lemma
3.17
, which allows us to understand the local structure of the tubular neighborhood of the surface of minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
. Another technical complication relates to the metric used to distinguish between the inner and outer region, as simply considering the
L^{2}
-norm is not sufficient for our purposes, and we need the weighted norms defined in (
3.3.46
) (in particular we apply the IMS localization with respect to a metric which depends on
\alpha
).

3.
Properties of the Pekar Functionals

In this section we derive important properties of the functionals
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}
, introduced in Section
2.2
and defined in (
2.2.4
). In Section
3.2
, we show the validity of Theorem
2.3
, relying on the comparison of the models on
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for large
L
. In Section
3.3
, we study the functional
\mathcal{F}_{L}
. In particular, we prove Corollary
2.4
and compute the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
at its minimizers.

Given a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and
k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, we denote by
f_{k}
the
k
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
. We also denote

(3.0.1)

\displaystyle\hat{f}:=f-L^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}f.

We shall use the following definition of
fractional Sobolev semi-norms
for functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,
0\neq s\in\mathbb{R}
:

(3.0.2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathring{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}=\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L)^{s}}{f}=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|k|^{2s}|f_{k}|^{2}.

Before moving on with the discussion, we recall in the following subsection the definition and relevant properties of the full-space Pekar functional.

3.1.
The Full-Space Pekar Functional

Let
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-normalized function and
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\psi,\varphi):=\expectationvalue{h^{\Rtre}_\varphi}{\psi}+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}

where
h_{\varphi}^{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
is the Schrödinger operator

(3.1.2)

\displaystyle h_{\varphi}^{\mathbb{R}^{3}}:=-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+V_{\varphi},\quad V_{\varphi}:=-2(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1/2}\varphi.

Comparing with (
2.2.1
) and (
2.2.2
), we note the analogy between the definitions and observe that we are slightly abusing notation by denoting both potentials with the same symbol (we do this for simplicity and since ambiguity does not arise). Analogously to (
2.2.4
), we define

(3.1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\psi):=\inf_{\varphi}\mathcal{G}(\psi,\varphi),\quad\mathcal{F}(\varphi):=\inf_{\psi}\mathcal{G}(\psi,\varphi).

In analogy with (
2.2.3
), we denote

(3.1.4)

\displaystyle e_{\infty}:=\inf_{\psi,\varphi}\mathcal{G}(\psi,\varphi)=\inf_{\psi}\mathcal{E}(\psi)=\inf_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}(\varphi).

For our purposes, in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it is sufficient to focus our discussion on the functional
\mathcal{E}
, of which we now recall the main properties. As shown in
[
16
]
,
\mathcal{E}
admits a
unique
positive and radially decreasing minimizer
\varPsi
which is also smooth, the set of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}
coincides with

(3.1.5)

\displaystyle\Theta(\varPsi):=\{e^{i\theta}\varPsi^{y}\,\,|\,\,\theta\in[0,2\pi),\,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\},

and
\varPsi
satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation

(3.1.6)

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+V_{\sigma_{\varPsi}}-\mu_{\varPsi}\right)\varPsi=0,

with

(3.1.7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varPsi}:=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1/2}|\varPsi|^{2},\quad V_{\sigma_{\varPsi}}=-2(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}|\varPsi|^{2},\quad\mu_{\varPsi}=T(\varPsi)-2W(\varPsi),

where
T
and
W
are defined in (
3.2.1
) below.
Furthermore, as was shown in
[
15
]
, the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at its minimizers is strictly positive above the trivial zero modes resulting from the invariance under translations and changes of phase. This implies the validity of the following Theorem, which is not stated explicitly in
[
15
]
but can be obtained by standard arguments (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Appendix A]
or
[
7
]
) as a consequence of the results therein contained.

Theorem A
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, such that, for any
L^{2}
-normalized
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.1.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f)-e_{\infty}\geq C\dist_{H^{1}}^{2}\left(\Theta(\varPsi),f\right).

Our strategy to prove Proposition
3.5
relies on Theorem
A
and in comparing
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for large
L
.

3.2.
Study of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and Proof of Theorem
2.3

To compare
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and
\mathcal{E}
, we prefer to write both of them in the following form, which can be obtained from (
2.2.4
) and (
3.1.3
), respectively, by a simple completion of the square,

(3.2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\psi}(x)(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}(x,y)\rho_{\psi}(y)dxdy=:T(\psi)-W(\psi),

(3.2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\rho_{\psi}(x)(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)\rho_{\psi}(y)dxdy=:T_{L}(\psi)-W_{L}(\psi).

The next ingredient, needed for the comparison of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
and
\mathcal{E}
, is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that

(3.2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\left|(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)-(4\pi)^{-1}(\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y))^{-1}\right|\leq\frac{C}{L}.

Proof.

We define
F_{L}(x):=-\Delta^{-1}_{L}(x,0)
and
F(x)=(4\pi)^{-1}|x|^{-1}
and observe that our statement is equivalent to showing that

(3.2.4)

\displaystyle\|F_{L}-F\|_{L^{\infty}([-L/2,L/2]^{3})}\leq\frac{C}{L}.

By definition, we have
F_{L}(x)=\frac{1}{L}F_{1}(\frac{x}{L})
. Hence, (
3.2.4
) is equivalent to

(3.2.5)

\displaystyle\|F_{1}-F\|_{L^{\infty}([-1/2,1/2]^{3})}\leq C.

Again by definition,
F_{1}-F
is harmonic (distributionally and hence also classically) on
\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{\mathbb{Z}^{3}\}\right)\cup\{0\}
(when
F_{1}
, and only
F_{1}
, is extended to the whole space by periodicity). Thus we conclude that
F_{1}-F
is in
C^{\infty}\left((-1,1)^{3}\right)
and, in particular, bounded on
[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
.
∎

The analogy between (
3.2.2
) and (
3.2.1
), combined with Lemma
3.1
, clearly suggests that
\mathcal{E}_{L}
formally converges to
\mathcal{E}
as
L\to\infty
. Hence, we set out to show that this convergence can be made rigorous and allows to infer properties of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
by comparing it to
\mathcal{E}
, in the large
L
regime.

In Section
3.2.1
we derive an important preliminary result, namely Proposition
3.5
. It formalizes in a mathematical useful way the concept of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
converging to
\mathcal{E}
. In Section
3.2.2
, we study the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
, showing that it converges (in the sense of Proposition
3.7
) to the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
and therefore is strictly positive above its trivial zero modes for large
L
. Finally, in Section
3.2.3
we use the results obtained in Sections
3.2.1
and
3.2.2
to show the validity of Theorem
2.3
.

We remark that our approach differs from the one used on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and on balls to show, for the related
\mathcal{E}
-functional, uniqueness of minimizers and strict positivity of the Hessian (see
[
16
]
and
[
15
]
for the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
[
6
]
for the case of balls). In those cases, rotational symmetry allows to first show uniqueness of minimizers and then helps to derive the positivity of the Hessian at the minimizers. We take somewhat the opposite road: comparing
\mathcal{E}_{L}
to
\mathcal{E}
, we first show that minimizers (even if not unique) all localize around the full-space minimizers (see Proposition
3.5
) and that the Hessian at each minimizer is universally strictly positive (see Proposition
3.7
) for large
L
. We then use these two properties to derive, as a final step, uniqueness of minimizers.

3.2.1.
Preliminary Results

The next Lemma proves the existence of minimizers for any
L>0
. Moreover, it shows that there exists
L_{0}>0
such that, for
L<L_{0}
,
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is strictly positive on any non-constant
L^{2}
-normalized function, as already mentioned in Remark
2.6
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
L>0
,
e_{L}
in (
2.2.3
) is attained, and there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that
e_{L}>-C
. Moreover, there exists
L_{0}>0
such that, for
L<L_{0}
,
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)>0
for any non-constant
L^{2}
-normalized
\psi
.

Proof.

We consider any
L^{2}
-normalized
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and begin by observing that in terms of the Fourier coefficients we have

(3.2.6)

\displaystyle W_{L}(\psi)=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|(\rho_{\psi})_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}},

(3.2.7)

\displaystyle(\rho_{\psi})_{k}=\sum_{j\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{\bar{\psi}_{j}\psi_{j+k}}{L^{3/2}}=(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}+\frac{\bar{\psi}_{0}\psi_{k}}{L^{3/2}}+\frac{\bar{\psi}_{-k}\psi_{0}}{L^{3/2}}.

By Parseval’s identity
|\psi_{0}|\leq 1
and thus, using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we can deduce that

(3.2.8)

\displaystyle|(\rho_{\psi})_{k}|^{2}\leq\begin{cases}L^{-3},\\
3|(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}|^{2}+\frac{3}{L^{3}}(|\psi_{k}|^{2}+|\psi_{-k}|^{2}).\end{cases}

Therefore

\displaystyle W_{L}(\psi)

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\right)+\frac{6}{L^{3}}\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|\psi_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\right)

(3.2.9)

\displaystyle\leq 3W_{L}(\hat{\psi})+\frac{6}{(2\pi)^{2}L}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

We can bound both terms on the r.h.s. in two different ways, one which is good for small
L
and one which is good for all the other
L
. Indeed, by applying estimate (
3.2.8
) and using the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality (see
[
17
]
, chapter 8) on the zero-mean function
\hat{\psi}
, we get

\displaystyle W_{L}(\hat{\psi})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim L^{2}\|(\rho_{\hat{\psi}})_{k}\|_{l^{\infty}}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}

(3.2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{1/2}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\lesssim L\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\lesssim LT_{L}(\hat{\psi})=LT_{L}(\psi).

Moreover,

(3.2.11)

\displaystyle L^{-1}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\lesssim LT_{L}(\hat{\psi})=LT_{L}(\psi).

Therefore, we can conclude that

(3.2.12)

\displaystyle W_{L}(\psi)\lesssim LT_{L}(\psi)\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)\geq(1-CL)T_{L}(\psi).

Thus, for
L<L_{0}:=C^{-1}
, either
{\psi\equiv\const}
and
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)=0
or
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)\gtrsim T_{L}(\psi)>0
. Moreover, this also implies

(3.2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)\gtrsim T_{L}(\psi)\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{2L_{0}^{2}}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}T_{L}(\psi)\gtrsim\dist^{2}_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}\left(\frac{1}{L^{3/2}}\right),\psi\right),

which is the analogue of (
2.2.10
) from Theorem
2.3
in the case
L<L_{0}
.

We now proceed to study the more interesting regime
L\geq L_{0}
. By Lemma
3.1
, splitting
\dist^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,\cdot)
into an
L^{3/2}
part and the remaining
L^{\infty}
part (whose norms can be chosen to be proportional to
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
, respectively, for any
\varepsilon>0
), and by applying again the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality, we obtain

(3.2.14)

\displaystyle W_{L}(\hat{\psi})\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\frac{\rho_{\hat{\psi}}(x)\rho_{\hat{\psi}}(y)}{4\pi\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y)}dxdy+\frac{C}{L}\lesssim\varepsilon\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}+1\leq\frac{T_{L}(\psi)}{6}+C.

Moreover, since
L\geq L_{0}
, trivially
L^{-1}\|\hat{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\lesssim 1
and we can conclude that for any
L^{2}
-normalized
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})

(3.2.15)

\displaystyle W_{L}(\psi)\leq\frac{T_{L}(\psi)}{2}+C\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)\geq\frac{T_{L}(\psi)}{2}-C.

From this we can infer that
e_{L}\geq-C
for any
L
. To show existence of minimizers, we observe that by (
3.2.15
) any minimizing sequence
\psi_{n}
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
must be bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. Therefore, there exists a subsequence (which we still denote by
\psi_{n}
for simplicity) that converges weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, for any
1\leq p<6
to some
\psi
(by the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem and the Rellich-Kondrachov embedding Theorem). The limit function
\psi
is
L^{2}
-normalized and

(3.2.16)

\displaystyle T_{L}(\psi)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_{L}(\psi_{n})

by weak lower semicontinuity of the norm. Using the
L^{4}
-convergence of
\psi_{n}
to
\psi
and the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\lesssim L\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle|W_{L}(\psi_{n})-W_{L}(\psi)|

\displaystyle=\left(\|\rho_{\psi}\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}+\|\rho_{\psi_{n}}\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)\left|\|\rho_{\psi}\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}-\|\rho_{\psi_{n}}\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim L\|\rho_{\psi_{n}}-\rho_{\psi}\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\lesssim L^{2}\|\rho_{\psi_{n}}-\rho_{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

(3.2.17)

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\|\psi_{n}-\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\left(\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)\to 0.

This implies that

(3.2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi_{n})=e_{L},

and thus that
\psi
is a minimizer. Note that, since
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi_{n})\to e_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)
by definition of
\psi_{n}
and, as shown,
W_{L}(\psi_{n})\to W_{L}(\psi)
, it also holds

(3.2.19)

\displaystyle T_{L}(\psi_{n})=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi_{n})+W_{L}(\psi_{n})\to\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)+W_{L}(\psi)=T_{L}(\psi)

which implies that
\psi_{n}
actually converges to
\psi
strongly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
.
∎

Once we have shown existence of minimizers, we need to investigate more carefully their properties. Some of them are derived in the following Lemma. Recall that

(3.2.20)

\displaystyle V_{\psi}=-2(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\psi,\quad\sigma_{\psi}=(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}|\psi|^{2},

and that, as stated above, we call any property universal which does not depend on
L\geq L_{0}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
(as defined in (
2.2.5
)). Then
\psi
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation

(3.2.21)

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{L}+V_{\sigma_{\psi}}-\mu^{L}_{\psi})\psi=0,\quad\text{with}\quad\mu^{L}_{\psi}=T_{L}(\psi)-2W_{L}(\psi).

Moreover,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, is universally bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
(and therefore in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
), has constant phase and never vanishes. Finally, any
L^{2}
-normalized sequence
f_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L_{n}})
such that
\mathcal{E}_{L_{n}}(f_{n})
is universally bounded, is universally bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L_{n}})
.

Proof.

The fact that sequences
f_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L_{n}})
of
L^{2}
-normalized functions for which
\mathcal{E}_{L_{n}}
is universally bounded are universally bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L_{n}})
follows trivially from estimate (
3.2.15
). This immediately yields a universal bound on the
H^{1}
-norm of minimizers.

The Euler–Lagrange equation (
3.2.21
) for the problem is derived by standard computations omitted here. By Lemma
3.1
and by splitting
(\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(0,\,\cdot\,))^{-1}
in its
L^{3/2}
and
L^{\infty}
parts, we have

(3.2.22)

\displaystyle|V_{\sigma_{\psi}}(x)|\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\frac{1}{\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y)}|\psi(y)|^{2}dy+\frac{C}{L}\lesssim\left(\|\psi\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}+1\right)\lesssim\left(T_{L}(\psi)+1\right).

Therefore, by the universal
H^{1}
-boundedness of minimizers,
V_{\sigma_{\psi}}
is universally bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
. This immediately allows to conclude universal
\mathring{H}^{2}
(and hence
H^{2}
) bounds for functions in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
, using the Euler–Lagrange equation (
3.2.21
), Lemma
3.2
and the universal
H^{1}
-boundedness of minimizers, which guarantee that

\displaystyle 0\geq\mu^{L}_{\psi}=2\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)-T_{L}(\psi)\geq-C.

Since
L\geq L_{0}
, universal
H^{2}
-boundedness also implies universal
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of minimizers by the Sobolev inequality.

For any
L>0
, any
\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
satisfies
(
3.2.21
)
, is in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and is such that
V_{\sigma_{\psi}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. Therefore
\psi
also satisfies, for any
\lambda>0

(3.2.23)

\displaystyle\psi=(-\Delta_{L}+\lambda)^{-1}(-V_{\sigma_{\psi}}+\mu^{L}_{\psi}+\lambda)\psi.

In particular, by a bootstrap argument we can conclude that
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
. Moreover, picking
\lambda>-\mu^{L}_{\psi}+\|V_{\sigma_{\psi}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}
and using that
(-\Delta_{L}+\lambda)^{-1}
is positivity improving, we can also conclude that if
\psi\geq 0
then
\psi>0
. By the convexity properties of the kinetic energy (see
[
17
]
, Theorem 7.8), we have that
T_{L}(|\psi|)\leq T_{L}(\psi)
which implies that if
\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
then
T_{L}(\psi)=T_{L}(|\psi|)
and also
|\psi|\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
. Hence both
\psi
and
|\psi|
are eigenfunctions of the least and
simple
(by positivity of one of the eigenfunctions) eigenvalue
\mu^{L}_{\psi}=\mu^{L}_{|\psi|}
of the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta_{L}+V_{\sigma_{\psi}}
, which allows us to infer that
\psi
has constant phase and never vanishes.
∎

We now proceed to develop the tools that will allow to show the validity of Theorem
2.3
. We begin with a simple Lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,

(3.2.24)

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\psi}\|_{\mathring{H}^{1/8}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\psi}\|^{2}_{\mathring{H}^{1/8}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}=|\langle\nabla\rho_{\psi}|\nabla((-\Delta_{L})^{-7/8}\rho_{\psi})\rangle|

\displaystyle=2\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}|\psi(x)|\nabla(|\psi(x)|)\cdot\nabla_{x}\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{(\rho_{\psi})_{k}}{|k|^{7/4}}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{L^{3/2}}\right)dx\right|

(3.2.25)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}|\psi(x)|\partial_{i}\left(|\psi(x)|\right)\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{k_{i}(\rho_{\psi})_{k}}{|k|^{7/4}}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{L^{3/2}}dx\right|.

We define

(3.2.26)

\displaystyle g_{i}(x):=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{k_{i}(\rho_{\psi})_{k}}{|k|^{7/4}}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{L^{3/2}},

and observe that
(g_{i})_{0}=0
and
|(g_{i})_{k}|=\frac{|k_{i}(\rho_{\psi})_{k}|}{|k|^{7/4}}\leq\frac{|(\rho_{\psi})_{k}|}{|k|^{3/4}}
for
k\neq 0
. These estimates on the Fourier coefficients of
g_{i}
imply that, for
i=1,2,3
,

(3.2.27)

\displaystyle\|g_{i}\|_{\mathring{H}^{3/4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|k|^{3/2}|(g_{i})_{k}|^{2}\leq\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|(\rho_{\psi})_{k}|^{2}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{4}.

Moreover, using the fractional Sobolev embeddings (see, for example,

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\psi}\|^{2}_{\mathring{H}^{1/8}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\|g^{1/8}_{i}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\|\nabla(|\psi|)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq\|\psi\|^{3}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\|\psi\|_{\mathring{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

(3.2.29)

∎

Our next goal is to show that

as

, and that in the large

We recall that the full-space Pekar functional, defined in (

), admits a unique positive and radial minimizer

which is also smooth (see (

)), and we introduce the notation

\displaystyle\varPsi_{L}:=\varPsi\chi_{[-L/2,L/2]^{3}}.

Note that

, by radiality and regularity of

.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have

(3.2.31)

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}e_{L}=e_{\infty}.

Moreover, for any

\displaystyle\dist_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),\varPsi_{L}\right)\leq\varepsilon,\quad|\mu^{L}_{\psi}-\mu_{\varPsi}|\leq\varepsilon,

where
\Theta_{L}(\psi)
,
\varPsi_{L}
,

Proof.

We first show that
\limsup_{L\to\infty}e_{L}\leq e_{\infty}

as a trial state for

. Observe that
{\|\varPsi_{L}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\to 1}
and

as

. To estimate the difference of the interaction terms we note that
\varPsi_{L}(\varPsi-\varPsi_{L})=0

\displaystyle|W_{L}(\varPsi_{L})-W(\varPsi)|\leq|W_{L}(\varPsi_{L})-W(\varPsi_{L})|+W(\varPsi-\varPsi_{L})+2\bra{(\Emininf-\Emininf_L)^2}\ket{\Delta_{\Rtre}^{-1} \Emininf_L^2}.

By dominated convergence, the last two terms converge to zero as

(3.2.34)

\displaystyle|W_{L}(\varPsi_{L})-W(\varPsi_{L})|\leq\frac{C}{L}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{[-L/2,L/2]^{6}}\varPsi_{L}(x)^{2}\varPsi_{L}(y)^{2}\left|\frac{1}{\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y)}-\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right|dxdy.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\int_{[-L/2,L/2]^{6}}\varPsi_{L}(x)^{2}\varPsi_{L}(y)^{2}\left|\frac{1}{\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y)}-\frac{1}{|x-y|}\right|dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{[-L/2,L/2]^{3}}\varPsi_{L}^{2}(x)\left(\int_{[-L/2,L/2]^{3}}\frac{(\varPsi_{L}-\varPsi_{L/2})^{2}(y)}{\dist_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}(x,y)}dy\right)dx.

Finally, by splitting

Putting the pieces together, we conclude that

\displaystyle|W_{L}(\varPsi_{L})-W(\varPsi_{L})|=o_{L}(1).

This shows our first claim, since

\displaystyle e_{L}\leq\mathcal{E}_{L}(\varPsi_{L}/\|\varPsi_{L}\|_{2})=\frac{1}{\|\varPsi_{L}\|_{2}^{2}}\left(T_{L}(\varPsi_{L})-\frac{1}{\|\varPsi_{L}\|_{2}^{2}}W_{L}(\varPsi_{L})\right)\to e_{\infty}.

We now proceed to show that

\displaystyle\liminf_{L\to\infty}e_{L}\geq e_{\infty}

and the validity of (
3.2.32
) using IMS localization. We shall show that for any

Pick

\displaystyle\psi_{R}^{y}:=\psi\eta_{R}^{y}/\|\psi\eta_{R}^{y}\|_{2}.

By standard properties of IMS localization, for any

, we have

(3.2.42)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}T_{L}(\psi_{R}^{y})\|\psi\eta_{R}^{y}\|_{2}^{2}dy=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}T_{L}(\psi\eta_{R}^{y})dy=T_{L}(\psi)+\frac{\int|\nabla\eta|^{2}}{R^{2}}.

Moreover, by using that

\displaystyle W_{L}(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\left[W_{L}(\psi_{R}^{y})-\left\||\psi_{R}^{y}|^{2}-|\psi|^{2}\right\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\right]\|\psi\eta_{R}^{y}\|_{2}^{2}dy.

Combining (
3.2.42

3.2.43

(3.2.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)+\frac{C}{R^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\left[\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi_{R}^{y})+\left\||\psi_{R}^{y}|^{2}-|\psi|^{2}\right\|_{\mathring{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\right]\|\psi\eta_{R}^{y}\|_{2}^{2}dy.

Since the integrand on the r.h.s. is equal to the l.h.s. on average (indeed

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}_{L_{n}}(\psi_{n})+\frac{C}{L_{n}^{2}}\leq e_{L_{n}}+o_{L_{n}}(1),

In other words, we can localize any element of our sequence

\displaystyle|W_{L_{n}}(\bar{\psi}_{n})-W(\bar{\psi}_{n})|\lesssim\frac{1}{L_{n}}.

\displaystyle e_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{E}(\bar{\psi}_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}_{L_{n}}(\bar{\psi})+\frac{C}{L_{n}}\leq e_{L_{n}}+o_{L_{n}}(1),

3.2.2.
Study of the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}

In this section we study the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
at its minimizers, showing that it is strictly positive, universally, for
L
big enough. Positivity is of course understood up to the trivial zero modes resulting from the symmetries of the problem (translations and changes of phase). This is obtained by comparing
\mathcal{E}_{L}
with
\mathcal{E}
and exploiting Theorem
A
.

For any minimizer
\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
, the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
at
\psi
is defined by

(3.2.60)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{L}\left(\frac{\psi+\varepsilon f}{\|\psi+\varepsilon f\|_{2}}\right)-e_{L}\right)=H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(f)\quad\forall f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}).

An explicit computation gives

(3.2.61)

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(f)=\expectationvalue{\LL_{\psi}}{\Im f}+\expectationvalue{Q_{\psi}(\LL_{\psi}-4\XL_{\psi}) Q_{\psi}}{\Re f},

with
Q_{\psi}=1\!\!1-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
and

(3.2.62)

\displaystyle L^{L}_{\psi}:=-\Delta_{L}+V_{\sigma_{\psi}}-\mu^{L}_{\psi}\ ,\quad X^{L}_{\psi}(x,y):=\psi(x)(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}(x,y)\psi(y).

(We use the same notation for the operator
X^{L}_{\psi}
and its integral kernel for simplicity.)
We recall that
\mu^{L}_{\psi}=T_{L}(\psi)-2W_{L}(\psi)
(see (
3.2.21
)) and that
V_{\sigma_{\psi}}=-2(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}\rho_{\psi}
(see (
3.2.20
)) and we note that
L^{L}_{\psi}\psi=0
is exactly the Euler–Lagrange equation derived in Lemma
3.3
.

By minimality of
\psi
, we know that
\infspec L=\infspec Q(L-4X)Q=0
, since both operators are clearly nonnegative and
\psi
is in the kernel of both of them. Moreover,
\ker L^{L}_{\psi}=\spn\{\psi\}
, since it is a Schrödinger operator of least (simple) eigenvalue
0
. The situation is more complicated for
Q_{\psi}(L^{L}_{\psi}-4X^{L}_{\psi})Q_{\psi}
, whose kernel contains at least
\psi
and
\partial_{i}\psi
(by the translation invariance of the problem). Since both
L^{L}_{\psi}
and
Q_{\psi}(L^{L}_{\psi}-4X^{L}_{\psi})Q_{\psi}
have compact resolvents (they are given by bounded perturbations of
-\Delta_{L}
), they both have discrete spectrum. Our aim is two-fold: first we need to show that the kernel of
Q_{\psi}(L^{L}_{\psi}-4X^{L}_{\psi})Q_{\psi}
is exactly spanned by
\psi
and its partial derivatives, secondly we want to show that the spectral gap (above the trivial zero modes) of both operators is bounded by a universal positive constant.

Before stating the main result of this section, we introduce the relevant full-space objects: let again
\varPsi
be the unique positive and radial full-space minimizer of the Pekar functional (
3.2.1
) and, analogously to (
3.2.62
), define

(3.2.63)

\displaystyle L_{\varPsi}:=-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}+V_{\sigma_{\varPsi}}-\mu_{\varPsi}\ ,\quad X_{\varPsi}(x,y):=\varPsi(x)(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{-1}(x,y)\varPsi(y).

We introduce

\displaystyle h_{\infty}^{\prime}:=\inf_{f\in H_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\|f\|_{2}=1\atop f\in(\spn\{\varPsi\})^{\perp}}\expectationvalue{L_{\Emininf}}{f},

(3.2.64)

\displaystyle h_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}:=\inf_{f\in H_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\|f\|_{2}=1\atop f\in(\spn\{\varPsi,\partial_{1}\varPsi,\partial_{2}\varPsi,\partial_{3}\varPsi\})^{\perp}}\expectationvalue{L_{\Emininf}-4X_{\Emininf}}{f}.

We emphasize that the results contained in
[
15
]
imply that
\min\{h_{\infty}^{\prime},h_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}\}>0
. Moreover, it is easy to see, using that
V_{\sigma_{\varPsi}}(x)\lesssim-|x|^{-1}
for large
x
, that
L_{\varPsi}
has infinitely many eigenvalues between
0
, its least and simple eigenvalue with eigenfunction given by
\varPsi
, and
-\mu_{\varPsi}
, the bottom of its continuous spectrum. Since furthermore
X_{\varPsi}
is positive, this implies, in particular, that

(3.2.65)

\displaystyle h_{\infty}^{\prime\prime},h_{\infty}^{\prime}<-\mu_{\varPsi},

which we shall use later.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any
L>0
, we define

(3.2.66)

\displaystyle\quad\,h_{L}^{\prime}:=\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}}\inf_{f\in H_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}),\|f\|_{2}=1\atop f\in(\spn\{\psi\})^{\perp}}\quad\expectationvalue{\LL_{\psi}}{f},

(3.2.67)

Proof of Theorem

In this section we first prove universal local bounds for

3.5

In this section, for any

Proposition 3.10

(Universal Local Bounds)
.

There exist universal constants
K_{1}>0
and
K_{2}>0
and

such that, for any
L>L^{**}
, any

and any

-normalized

with

(3.2.103)

\displaystyle\dist_{H^{1}}(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f)\leq K_{1},

we have

(3.2.104)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(f)-e_{L}\geq K_{2}\|P^{L^{2}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)-f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\geq K_{2}\dist_{H^{1}}^{2}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right).

We can restrict to

\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
and normalized

such that

(3.2.105)

\displaystyle P^{L^{2}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)=\psi,

which clearly implies

(3.2.106)

\displaystyle\bra{\psi}\ket{f}\geq 0,\quad\bra{\Re f}\ket{\partial_i\psi}=0.

Under this assumption, we prove that if (
3.2.103
) holds then

(3.2.107)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(\phi)-e_{L}\geq K_{2}\|\psi-f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}\geq K_{2}\dist^{2}_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right).

The general result follows immediately by invariance of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
under translations and changes of phase.

We denote
\delta:=f-\psi
and proceed to expand
\mathcal{E}_{L}
around
\psi
:

(3.2.108)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(f)=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi+\delta)=e_{L}+H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(\delta)+\Err_{\psi}(\delta).

We recall that

is simply the quadratic form associated to the Hessian of
\mathcal{E}_{L}
at
\psi
and it is defined in (
3.2.61
). We denote
P_{\psi}:=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
. The last term, which we see as an error contribution, is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\Err_{\psi}(\delta)=

\displaystyle-8\bra{\Re\delta}X^{L}_{\psi}\ket{P_{\psi} \Re\delta}+4\expectationvalue{\XL_{\psi}}{P_{\psi}\Re\delta}

(3.2.109)

\displaystyle-4\bra{|\delta|^2}(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}\ket{\psi\Re\delta}+W_{L}(\delta).

Our first goal is to estimate

. By (

) and the normalization of both
\psi

(3.2.110)

\displaystyle\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}=2-2\bra{\psi}\ket{f}.

Therefore, also using the positivity of
\psi

(3.2.111)

\displaystyle P_{\psi}\real\delta=\psi(\bra{\psi}\ket{f}-1)=-\frac{1}{2}\psi\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}.

We now apply Lemma
3.8
to obtain

\displaystyle|\bra{\Re\delta}X^{L}_{\psi}\ket{P_{\psi} \Re\delta}|\lesssim\|\real\delta\|_{2}\|P_{\psi}\real\delta\|_{2}\lesssim\|\delta\|_{2}^{3},

\displaystyle|\expectationvalue{\XL_{\psi}}{P_{\psi}\Re\delta}|\lesssim\|P_{\psi}\real\delta\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|\delta\|_{2}^{4},

(3.2.112)

\displaystyle|\bra{|\delta|^2}(-\Delta_{L})^{-1}\ket{\psi\Re\delta}|\lesssim\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}\|\real\delta\|_{2}\leq\|\delta\|_{2}^{3}.

Finally, by (

),

(3.2.113)

\displaystyle W_{L}(\delta)=\|\delta\|_{2}^{4}W_{L}\left(\frac{\delta}{\|\delta\|_{2}}\right)\leq\|\delta\|_{2}^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}T_{L}\left(\frac{\delta}{\|\delta\|_{2}}\right)+C\right)\lesssim\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

Recalling (
3.2.103
), we can estimate

(3.2.114)

\displaystyle\|\delta\|_{2}=\dist_{L^{2}}(f,\Theta_{L}(\psi))\leq\dist_{H^{1}}(f,\Theta_{L}(\psi))\leq K_{1},

and this implies, combined with (
3.2.3
) and (
3.2.113
), that

(3.2.115)

\displaystyle|\Err_{\psi}(\delta)|\lesssim\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{3}.

We now want to bound

. We fix

, where
h_{\infty}^{\prime}
and
h_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}
are defined in (
3.2.2
). Proposition

implies that there exists

such that for

and

\displaystyle L^{L}_{\psi}\geq\tau Q_{\psi},\quad Q_{\psi}(L^{L}_{\psi}-4X^{L}_{\psi})Q_{\psi}\geq\tau Q_{\psi}^{\prime},

Therefore, recalling the definition of

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\expectationvalue{\LL_{\psi}}{\Im f}+\expectationvalue{Q_{\psi}(\LL_{\psi}-4\XL_{\psi} )Q_{\psi}}{\Re f- \psi}

\displaystyle\geq\tau(\|Q_{\psi}\imaginary f\|_{2}^{2}+\|Q_{\psi}^{\prime}(\real f-\psi)\|_{2}^{2})=\tau\|Q_{\psi}\delta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}.

Moreover, applying (

(3.2.119)

\displaystyle\|Q_{\psi}\delta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}=\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}-\bra{\psi}\ket{\delta}^{2}=\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2},

and we can thus conclude that

(3.2.120)

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(\delta)\geq\frac{\tau}{2}\|\delta\|_{2}^{2}.

On the other hand, by the universal boundedness of

in

and the universal boundedness of

(see Proposition
3.5

(3.2.121)

\displaystyle L^{L}_{\psi}\geq-\Delta_{L}-C_{1}.

Similarly, also using Lemma
3.8
, for some universal

(3.2.122)

\displaystyle Q(L^{L}_{\psi}-4X^{L}_{\psi})Q\geq-\Delta_{L}-C_{2}.

If we then define
C:=(\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}+1)
, we can conclude the validity of the universal bound

(3.2.123)

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(\delta)\geq\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}-C\|\delta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

By interpolating between (
3.2.120
) and (

), we obtain

(3.2.124)

\displaystyle H^{\mathcal{E}_{L}}_{\psi}(\delta)\geq\frac{\tau}{\tau+2C}\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

Using (
3.2.115
) and (
3.2.124
) in (
3.2.108
), we can conclude that there exists a universal constant
C
such that for any
L>L^{**}
, any
0<\psi\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
and any normalized

satisfying (
3.2.105

(3.2.125)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{L}(f)-e_{L}\geq\frac{1}{C}\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}-C\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{3}.

In particular, for
K_{2}
sufficiently small, we can find a universal constant

such that (
3.2.107
) holds,
as long as

(3.2.126)

\displaystyle\|\delta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}=\|P^{L^{2}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)-f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq c.

To conclude the proof, it only remains to show that there exists a universal
K_{1}
such that (
3.2.126
) holds as long as (
3.2.103
) holds. This can be achieved as follows. We have, using that both
\psi
and

are in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{L}
and thus are universally bounded in

(by Lemma

) and recalling (see (

\psi=P^{L^{2}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)

\displaystyle\|\psi-P^{H^{1}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)\|_{\mathring{H}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi-P^{H^{1}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\|(-\Delta_{L})(\psi-P^{H^{1}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f))\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi-P^{H^{1}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\dist_{L^{2}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right)+\|f-P^{H^{1}}_{\Theta_{L}(\psi)}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dist^{1/2}_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right).

Therefore, for some universal

(3.2.128)

\displaystyle\|f-\psi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq\dist_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right)+C\dist^{1/2}_{H^{1}}\left(\Theta_{L}(\psi),f\right),

and it suffices to take

to conclude our discussion.
∎

3.3.1.
Proof of Corollary
2.4

In this section, we show the validity of Corollary
2.4
.
We need the following Lemma. Recall that in our discussion constants are universal if they are independent of
L
for
L\geq L_{0}>0
.

Lemma 3.11
.

For
\psi,\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
,
\|\psi\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}=1
,

(3.3.1)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}}{\rho_{\psi}-\rho_{\phi}}\lesssim\||\psi|-|\phi|\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}.

Proof.

We define
f(x):=|\psi(x)|+|\phi(x)|
and
g(x):=|\psi(x)|-|\phi(x)|
. By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev and the Sobolev inequality (see for example
[
3
]
for a comprehensive overview of such results on the torus), and using the normalization of
\phi
and
\psi
we have

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}}{\rho_{\psi}-\rho_{\phi}}

\displaystyle=\|(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/4}(fg)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\|fg\|_{3/2}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{2}^{2}\|g\|_{6}^{2}

(3.3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\|g\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}=C^{\prime}\||\psi|-|\phi|\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2},

which proves the Lemma.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.4
.

With
\psi_{L}
as in Theorem
2.3
, let
\varphi_{L}:=\sigma_{\psi_{L}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
.
Observing that

(3.3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi)=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)+\|\sigma_{\psi}-\varphi\|_{2}^{2},

and using Theorem
2.3
we can immediately conclude that in the regime
L>L_{1}

(3.3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}=\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}).

It is also immediate, recalling the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{L}
in (
2.2.1
) and that
\psi_{L}>0
(as proven in Theorem
2.3
), to conclude that
\psi_{L}
must be the unique positive ground state of
h_{\varphi_{L}}
.

To prove (
2.2.13
), we first of all observe that if
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, we have

(3.3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)=|(\varphi)_{0}|^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{L}(\hat{\varphi}).

Therefore, it is sufficient to restrict to
\varphi
with zero-average and show that in this case

(3.3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)-e_{L}\geq\min_{y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}\expectationvalue{\unit-(\unit+\kappa'(-\Delta_L)^{1/2})^{-1}}{\varphi-\varphi_L^y}.

Using Theorem
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi)-e_{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{L}(\psi)-e_{L}+\|\varphi-\sigma_{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\mathcal{E}_{L}(|\psi|)-e_{L}+\|\varphi-\sigma_{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{1}\dist_{H^{1}}^{2}(|\psi|,\Theta(\psi_{L}))+\|\varphi-\sigma_{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}

(3.3.7)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}\||\psi|-\psi_{L}^{y}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}^{2}+\|\varphi-\sigma_{\psi}\|_{2}^{2},

for some
y\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
. We now apply Lemma
3.11
and use that
\varphi_{L}^{y}=\sigma_{\psi_{L}^{y}}
(see (
3.3.3
)), obtaining with a simple completion of the square

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{L}(\psi,\varphi)-e_{L}

\displaystyle\geq\kappa^{\prime}\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}}{\rho_{\psi}-\rho_{\psi_L^y}}+\|\varphi-\sigma_{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|F^{1/2}(\sigma_{\psi}-\varphi_{L}^{y})+F^{-1/2}(\varphi_{L}^{y}-\varphi)\|_{2}^{2}

(3.3.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\expectationvalue{\unit-F^{-1}}{\varphi-\varphi_L^y},

where
F=1\!\!1+\kappa^{\prime}(-\Delta_{L})^{1/2}
. Dropping the first term and minimizing over
\psi
yields our claim. Finally, (
2.2.14
) immediately follows from (
2.2.13
) and the spectral gap of the Laplacian, using the fact that
\varphi_{L}
and all its translates have zero average since
\varphi_{L}=\sigma_{\psi_{L}}
.
∎

3.3.2.
The Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}

For any
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, we introduce the notation

(3.3.9)

\displaystyle e(\varphi):=\infspec h_{\varphi},

and observe that
\mathcal{F}_{L}
, defined in (
2.2.4
), can equivalently be written as

(3.3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)=\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}+e(\varphi),\quad\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}).

We compute the Hessian of
\mathcal{F}_{L}
at its minimizers using standard arguments in perturbation theory, showing the validity of expression (
2.2.16
). We need the following two Lemmas.

Lemma 3.12
.

For
L\geq L_{0}>0
, any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and any
T>0

(3.3.11)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}\varphi\|=\|\varphi(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}\|\leq C_{T}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

for some constant
C_{T}>0
with
\lim_{T\to\infty}C_{T}=0
.
Here
\varphi
is understood as a multiplication operator,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, and

(3.3.12)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}:=\inf_{\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}=\varphi\atop\varphi_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}),\,\varphi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right).

Note that

(3.3.13)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq L^{3/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq L^{3/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})},

which clearly makes the two norms equivalent. Nevertheless, we find it more natural to work with a bound of the form (
3.3.11
), where
C_{T}
is independent of
L
.

Lemma
3.12
implies that, for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, the multiplication operator associated with
\varphi
is infinitesimally relatively bounded with respect to
-\Delta_{L}
. More precisely, for any
\delta>0
, there exists
C\left(\delta,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)
depending on
\varphi
only through
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}
, such that for any
f\in\text{Dom}(-\Delta_{L})

(3.3.14)

\displaystyle\|\varphi f\|\leq\delta\|\Delta_{L}f\|+C\left(\delta,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)\|f\|.

Whenever infinitesimal relative boundedness holds with a constant
C(\delta)
uniform over a class of operators, we will say that the class is uniformly infinitesimally relatively bounded. In this case, Lemma
3.12
ensures that multiplication operators associated to functions in
(L^{2}+L^{\infty})
-balls are uniformly infinitesimally relatively bounded with respect to
-\Delta_{L}
.

Proof.

We first observe that, by self-adjointness of
(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}
, it is sufficient to show that the claimed bound holds for
\|\varphi(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}\|
. For any
f,\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and any decomposition of the form
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
with
\varphi_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and
\varphi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}\|(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{\infty}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}\|(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{2}

(3.3.15)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}\|(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{\infty}+T^{-1}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{L^{3/2}(|k|^{2}+T)}|f_{k}|\leq\left(\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{(|k|^{2}+T)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}

(3.3.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(|x|^{2}+T)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}=CT^{-1/2}\|f\|_{2}.

Therefore, picking
C_{T}:=\max\left\{T^{-1},CT^{-1/2}\right\}
yields

(3.3.17)

\displaystyle\|\varphi(-\Delta_{L}+T)^{-1}f\|_{2}\leq C_{T}\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{2}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{\infty}\right)\|f\|_{2},

optimizing over
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

For
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})

(3.3.18)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})+L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\lesssim\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}.

Proof.

We write
f_{1}=\chi_{[0,1)}
and
f_{2}=\chi_{[1,+\infty)}
and

(3.3.19)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}=f_{1}\left[(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\right]\varphi,\quad\varphi_{2}=f_{2}\left[(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\right]\varphi.

Clearly
(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
.
Moreover

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}+L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}+\|\varphi_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|<1}\frac{1}{L^{3}|k|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|<1}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|\geq 1}\frac{|\varphi_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|<1}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|\geq 1}\frac{|\varphi_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

(3.3.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{1}{|k|^{2}+1}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=C\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

. By translation invariance of the problem, it is clearly sufficient to perform the computation with respect to

Proposition 3.15
.

For

let

(3.3.22)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\leq\varepsilon_{L}

and

, is understood, in the expressions for

Note that this implies that

We shall in the following denote by

\displaystyle\spn\{\partial_{j}\varphi_{L}\}_{j=1}^{3}\subset\ker(1\!\!1-K_{L}).

We show in Section

.

Proof.

We shall denote
h_{0}:=h_{\varphi_{L}}
. By assumption (

) and since
\varphi_{L}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})

to
\varphi_{L}
and to
(\varphi-\varphi_{L})
. This way we see that

is uniformly infinitesimally relatively bounded with respect to
h_{0}
for any
\varphi

It is clear that

admits a simple and isolated least eigenvalue

. Standard results in perturbation theory then imply that there exist
\varepsilon_{L}>0
and a contour

around

such that for any

is the only eigenvalue of

inside

(see

We can thus write

(3.3.26)

\displaystyle e(\varphi)=\Tr\int_{\gamma}\frac{z}{z-(h_{0}+V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}})}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}.

Moreover, by the uniform infinitesimal relative boundedness of
V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}
with respect to
h_{0}

(3.3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\gamma}\|V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}(z-h_{0})^{-1}\|<1,

for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z-h_{0}-V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}}=

\displaystyle\left(1\!\!1-\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\right)^{-1}\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}

(3.3.28)

\displaystyle+\left(1\!\!1-\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\right)^{-1}\frac{P_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}\left(1\!\!1-V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\frac{1}{z-h_{0}}\right)^{-1}.

The first term is analytic inside the contour

3.3.2
), which is rank one, in (
3.3.26

(3.3.29)

\displaystyle e(\varphi)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{z}{z-e(\varphi_{L})}\left\langle\psi_{L}\left|\left(1\!\!1-V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\frac{1}{z-h_{0}}\right)^{-1}\left(1\!\!1-\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\right)^{-1}\right|\psi_{L}\right\rangle\frac{dz}{2\pi i}.

For simplicity, we introduce the notation

(3.3.30)

\displaystyle A=V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\frac{1}{z-h_{0}},\quad B=\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{z-h_{0}}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}.

Because of (

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{1}{1\!\!1-B}=

\displaystyle 1\!\!1+A+A(A+B)+\frac{B}{1\!\!1-B}

(3.3.31)

\displaystyle+\frac{A^{3}}{1\!\!1-A}+\frac{A^{2}}{1\!\!1-A}B+\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{B^{2}}{1\!\!1-B}.

We insert the various terms in (
3.3.29

. The term
A
, recalling (see (

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{V_{\varphi-\varphi_L}}{\psi_L}=-2\bra{\varphi-\varphi_L}\ket{\varphi_L}.

A standard calculation shows that the term
A(A+B)
gives

(3.3.33)

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{L}\left|V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\frac{Q_{\psi_{L}}}{e(\varphi_{L})-h_{0}}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\right|\psi_{L}\right\rangle=-\expectationvalue{K_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}.

Furthermore, since

yields zero. Recalling that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi_{L})-\expectationvalue{\unit-K_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\frac{z}{z-e(\varphi_{L})}\left\langle\psi_{L}\left|\frac{A^{3}}{1\!\!1-A}+A\left(\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}+\frac{1}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{B}{1\!\!1-B}\right)B\right|\psi_{L}\right\rangle\frac{dz}{2\pi i}.

We observe that, since

\displaystyle(I):=\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left|\left\langle\psi_{L}\left|\frac{A^{3}}{1\!\!1-A}\right|\psi_{L}\right\rangle\right|,

(3.3.35)

\displaystyle(II):=\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left|\left\langle\psi_{L}\left|A\left(\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}+\frac{1}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{B}{1\!\!1-B}\right)B\right|\psi_{L}\right\rangle\right|,

with the r.h.s. of (
3.15

(3.3.36)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}=\left\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}\psi_{L}\right\|_{2}^{2},

and that, by infinitesimal relative boundedness of

with respect to
(-\Delta_{L})
and since

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}(z-h_{0})^{-k}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}\right\|\leq C_{L}\quad\text{for}\,\,k=1,2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left|(z-e(\varphi_{L}))^{-1}\expectationvalue{(z-h_0)^{-1}A (\unit-A)^{-1}}{V_{\varphi-\varphi_L} \psi_L}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}(z-h_{0})^{-1}\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}\right\|\expectationvalue{J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}

(3.3.38)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}\right\|\expectationvalue{J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L},

\displaystyle(II)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left\|\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}+\frac{1}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{B}{1\!\!1-B}\right\|\expectationvalue{AA^{\dagger}}{\psi_L}^{1/2}\expectationvalue{BB^{\dagger}}{\psi_L}^{1/2}

(3.3.39)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\sup_{z\in\gamma}\left\|\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}+\frac{1}{1\!\!1-A}\frac{B}{1\!\!1-B}\right\|\expectationvalue{J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}.

\displaystyle A(1\!\!1-A)^{-1}=V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}(z-h_{\varphi})^{-1},

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\frac{A}{1\!\!1-A}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}V_{\varphi-\varphi_{L}}(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\|\|(-\Delta_{L})^{1/2}(z-h_{\varphi})^{-1}(-\Delta_{L})^{1/2}\|

where we used the relative boundedness of

. This yields the right bound for

\|A(1\!\!1-A)^{-1}\|
and
\|(1\!\!1-A)^{-1}B(1\!\!1-B)^{-1}\|\lesssim_{L}\|B\|
, concluding the proof. ∎

and

.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
K_{L}
and
J_{L}
be the operators defined in (

). We have

(3.3.42)

\displaystyle K_{L},J_{L}\lesssim_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-2}.

Proof.

We prove the result for

. By the relative boundedness of

with respect to

the same proof applies to
K_{L}
. We shall show that
(-\Delta_{L}+1)(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\psi_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}
is bounded as an operator on

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\psi_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left(\frac{|k|^{2}+1}{|k|}\right)^{2}\left|\sum_{\xi\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(\psi_{L})_{k-\xi}\frac{f_{\xi}}{(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{1/2}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{3/2}\psi_{L}\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left(\frac{|k|^{2}+1}{|k|}\right)^{2}\sum_{\xi\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|f_{\xi}|^{2}}{(|k-\xi|^{2}+1)^{3}(|\xi|^{2}+1)}

(3.3.43)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\sum_{\xi\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{|f_{\xi}|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}+1}\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{(|k|^{2}+1)^{2}}{|k|^{2}(|k-\xi|^{2}+1)^{3}}\lesssim_{L}\|f\|_{2}^{2},

where we used that

and that
\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{(|k|^{2}+1)^{2}}{|k|^{2}(|k-\xi|^{2}+1)^{3}}\lesssim|\xi|^{2}+1
. Therefore

(3.3.44)

J_{L}\leq\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)(-\Delta_{L})^{-1/2}\psi_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\|^{2}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-2}\lesssim_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-2},

as claimed.

3.3.3.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}

For

we introduce the notation

(3.3.45)

\displaystyle\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}:=\text{$L^{2}$-projection onto}\,\,\spn\{\partial_{j}\varphi_{L}\}_{j=1}^{3},

which is going to be used throughout this section and Section

.
According to Theorem
2.3
, the condition

guarantees that
\psi_{L}^{y}\neq\psi_{L}
for any

is three dimensional (i.e that the partial derivatives of

are linearly independent); if not, there would exist

such that

and this would imply
\psi_{L}=\psi_{L}^{y}
for any

parallel to

For technical reasons, we also introduce a family of weighted norms which will be needed in Section
4

(3.3.46)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{W_{T}}:=\expectationvalue{W_T}{\varphi}^{1/2},

where

\displaystyle W_{T}(k)=\begin{cases}1&|k|\leq T\\

Note that
\|\varphi\|^{2}_{W_{0}}=\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L+1)^{-1}}{\varphi}
and
\|\varphi\|_{W_{\infty}}=\|\varphi\|_{2}
.

For the purpose of this section we could formulate the following Lemma only with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{2}=\|\cdot\|_{W_{\infty}}
, but we opt for this more general version since we shall need it in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.17
.

For any
L>L_{1}
, there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{L}
(independent of
T
) such that for any
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
with
\dist_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{L}
there exist a
unique
couple
(y_{\varphi},v_{\varphi})
, depending on
T
, with
y_{\varphi}\in\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}
and
v_{\varphi}\in(\spn_{i=1,2,3}\{W_{T}\partial_{i}\varphi_{L}\})^{\perp}
, such that

(3.3.48)

\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi_{L}^{y_{\varphi}}+(v_{\varphi})^{y_{\varphi}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v_{\varphi}\|_{W_{T}}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{L}.

As Proposition
3.15
above, we opt for an
L
-dependent version of Lemma
3.17
for simplicity, as it is sufficient for our purposes. We nevertheless believe it is possible to prove a corresponding statement that is uniform in
L
. Note that Lemma
3.17
is equivalent to the statement that there exists a
T
-independent
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{L}
such that the
W_{T}
-projection onto
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
is uniquely defined in an
\varepsilon_{L}^{\prime}
-neighborhood of

with respect to the

-norm, and that, for any
\varphi
therein,

characterizes the

-projection of
\varphi
onto
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})

(3.3.49)

\displaystyle\dist_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))=\|\varphi-\varphi_{L}^{y_{\varphi}}\|_{W_{T}}=\|v_{\varphi}\|_{W_{T}}.

Proof.

We begin by observing that the Lemma is equivalent to showing that for any
\|\cdot\|_{W_{T}}
-normalized
{v\in(\spn_{i=1,2,3}\{W_{T}\partial_{i}\varphi_{L}\})^{\perp}}
, any
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{L}^{\prime}
and any

we have

(3.3.50)

\displaystyle\varepsilon<\|\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon v-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}.

Indeed, if the Lemma holds then
\varphi=\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon v
does not admit other decompositions of the form (
3.3.48
), which implies that, for any
y\neq 0
, (
3.3.50
) holds (otherwise there would exist
y\neq 0
minimizing the
W_{T}
-distance of
\varphi
from
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
and such

would necessarily yield a second decomposition of the form (
3.3.48
)). On the other hand, if the statement (
3.3.50

\varphi
such that

and also such that
(y_{1},v_{1})
and
(y_{2},v_{2})
yield two different decompositions of the form (
3.3.48
) for
\varphi
(note that at least one decomposition of the form (
3.3.48
) always exist, as there exist at least one element of
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
realizing the

-distance of
\varphi
from
\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
). By considering

(respectively

) we find
\|v_{1}\|_{W_{T}}>\|v_{2}\|_{W_{T}}
(respectively
\|v_{2}\|_{W_{T}}>\|v_{1}\|_{W_{T}}
), which is clearly a contradiction. We shall hence proceed to prove the statement (

Taylor’s formula and the regularity of
\varphi_{L}
imply the existence of a
T
-independent constant

such that

(3.3.51)

\displaystyle\varphi_{L}^{y}=\varphi_{L}+y\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{L})+g_{y},\quad\text{with}\quad\|g_{y}\|_{W_{T}}\leq\|g_{y}\|_{2}\leq C^{1}_{L}|y|^{2}.

As remarked after (

\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}
is three-dimensional, hence there exists a constant
C^{2}_{L}
independent of

such that

(3.3.52)

\displaystyle\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\|\nu\cdot\nabla\varphi_{L}\|_{W_{T}}\geq\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\|\nu\cdot\nabla\varphi_{L}\|_{W_{0}}\geq C^{2}_{L}.

Therefore, using that

in combination with (

\displaystyle|y|<(C^{2}_{L}-2\varepsilon C^{1}_{L})^{1/2}(C^{1}_{L})^{-1},

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon v-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}

\displaystyle=\|\varepsilon v-y\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{L})-g_{y}\|_{W_{T}}\geq\left(\varepsilon^{2}+|y|^{2}C_{L}^{2}\right)^{1/2}-C_{L}^{1}|y|^{2}>\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon v-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}+\|\varphi_{L}-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}\left(\|\varphi_{L}-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}-2\varepsilon\right),

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{L}-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}>2\varepsilon.

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{L}-\varphi_{L}^{y}\|_{W_{T}}=\|y\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{L})

.
Combining (

or

(3.3.58)

\displaystyle|y|>2\varepsilon(C^{3}_{L})^{-1}.

Recalling the definition of

\displaystyle 1\!\!1-K_{L}\geq\tau_{L}(1\!\!1-\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}).

We need to show that for all normalized

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\unit-K_L}{v}\geq\tau_{L}

with respect to

\displaystyle\tau_{L}\varepsilon^{2}\leq\mathcal{F}_{L}(\varphi_{L}+\varepsilon v)-e_{L}\leq\varepsilon^{2}\expectationvalue{\unit-K_L}{v}+\varepsilon^{3}\expectationvalue{J_L}{v}.

4.
Proof of Main Results

In this Section we give the proof of Theorem
2.5
. In Section
4.1
we prove the upper bound in (
2.3.2
). In Section
4.2
we estimate the cost of substituting the full Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{L}
with a cut-off Hamiltonian depending only on finitely many phonon modes, a key step in providing a lower bound for
\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}
. Finally, in Section
4.3
, we show the validity of the lower bound in (
2.3.2
).

The approach used in Sections
4.1
and
4.2
follows closely the one used in
[
9
]
, even if, in our setting, minor complications arise in the proof of the upper bound due the presence of the zero modes of the Hessian. For the lower bound in Section
4.3
, however, a substantial amount of additional work is needed to deal with the translation invariance, which complicates the proof significantly.

4.1.
Upper Bound

In this section we construct a trial state, which will be used to obtain an upper bound on the ground state energy of
\mathbb{H}_{L}
for fixed
L>L_{1}
. This is carried out using the
Q
-space representation of the bosonic Fock space
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
(see
[
25
]
). Even though the estimates contained in this section are
L
-dependent, we believe it is possible, with little modifications to the proof, to obtain the same upper bound with the same error estimates uniformly in
L
.

Our trial state depends non-trivially only on finitely many phonon variables, and we proceed to describe it more in detail. If one picks
\Pi
to be a
real
finite rank projection on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, then

(4.1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))\cong\mathcal{F}(\Pi L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))\otimes\mathcal{F}((1\!\!1-\Pi)L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})).

The first factor
\mathcal{F}(\Pi L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
can isomorphically be identified with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
N
is the complex dimension of
\ran\Pi
. In particular, there is a one-to-one correspondence between any real
\varphi\in\ran\Pi
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, explicitly given by

(4.1.2)

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\varphi_{i}\cong(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})=\lambda,

where
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
denotes an orthonormal basis of
\ran\Pi
consisting of real-valued functions. The trial state we use corresponds to the vacuum in the second factor
{\mathcal{F}((1\!\!1-\Pi)L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))}
and shall hence be written only as a function of
x
(the electron variable) and
\lambda
(the finitely many phonon variables selected by
\Pi
). We begin by specifying some properties we wish
\Pi
to satisfy. Consider
\varphi_{L}
from Corollary

and define

, where

\Pi^{\prime}

. Our trial state is of the form

\displaystyle\Psi(x,\varphi)=G(\varphi)\eta(\varphi)\psi_{\varphi}(x),

where

•

and
\varphi

\ran\Pi
(identified with

as in (

)),

•

\displaystyle G(\varphi)=\exp(-\alpha^2\expval{\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2}}{\varphi-\varphi_L}),

•

\eta
is a ‘localization factor’ given by

(4.1.5)

\displaystyle\eta(\varphi)=\chi\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right),

for some
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{L}
(with
\varepsilon_{L}
as in Proposition
3.15
), where
0\leq\chi\leq 1
is a smooth cut-off function such that
\chi(t)=1
for

and
\chi(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
,

•

\psi_{\varphi}
is the unique positive ground state of
h_{\varphi}=-\Delta_{L}+V_{\varphi}
.

We note that our state actually depends on two parameters (
N
and
\varepsilon
) and, of course, on the specific choice of
\Pi^{\prime}
. We choose
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,N}
to be a real orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
[\Pi(1\!\!1-K_{L})\Pi]
corresponding to eigenvalues
\mu_{i}=0
for

and
\mu_{i}\geq\tau_{L}>0
for

. Recalling Proposition

, this amounts to choosing
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
to be a real orthonormal basis of
\ran\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}
and
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=4,\dots,N}
to be a real orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
[\Pi^{\prime}(1\!\!1-K_{L})\Pi^{\prime}]
. With this choice,
we have (with a slight abuse of notation)

(4.1.6)

\displaystyle G(\varphi)=G(\lambda_{4},\dots,\lambda_{N})=\exp(-\alpha^2\sum_{i=4}^{N} \mu_i^{1/2}(\lambda_i-\lambda^L_i)^2),

where
\varphi_{L}\cong\lambda_{L}=(0,0,0,\lambda^{L}_{4},\dots,\lambda_{N}^{L})
, since
\varphi_{L}\in\ran\Pi
by construction, and the first three coordinates are
0
since
\varphi_{L}\in\left(\ran\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\right)^{\perp}
.

We first show that even if
G
does not have finite

-norm,
\Psi
does due to the presence of
\eta
. We define

(4.1.7)

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}:=\{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|\leq\varepsilon\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

and

(4.1.8)

\displaystyle\gamma_{L}:=\inf_{\varphi\in\ran\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\atop\|\varphi\|_{2}=1}\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L+1)^{-1}}{\varphi}>0.

Then, on
T_{\varepsilon}
, noting that
\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi_{L}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{L}^{1/2}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}}=\gamma_{L}^{1/2}\|\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi\|\leq\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{2}

(4.1.9)

\displaystyle\leq\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{\prime}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{2}+\varepsilon\leq\|\Pi^{\prime}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|+\varepsilon=\left(\sum_{i=4}^{N}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i}^{L})^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\varepsilon

and this implies, using the normalization of
\psi_{\varphi}
, that

\displaystyle\|\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(\lambda_{4},\dots,\lambda_{N})^{2}\eta(\lambda)^{2}d\lambda_{1}\dots d\lambda_{N}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(\lambda_{4},\dots,\lambda_{N})^{2}1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda)d\lambda_{1}\cdots d\lambda_{N}

(4.1.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-3}}G(\lambda_{4},\dots,\lambda_{N})^{2}\gamma_{L}^{-3/2}\left[\left(\sum_{i=4}^{N}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i}^{L})^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\varepsilon\right]^{3}d\lambda_{4}\cdots d\lambda_{N}<\infty.

We spend a few words to motivate our choice of
\Psi
. The absolute value squared of
\Psi
has to be interpreted as a probability density over the couples
(\varphi,x)
, with
\varphi
being a classical state for the phonon field and
x
the position of the electron. In the electron coordinate, our
\Psi
corresponds to the ground state of
h_{\varphi}
for any value of
\varphi
. This implies, by straightforward computations, that the expectation value of the Fröhlich Hamiltonian in
\Psi
equals the one of
e(\varphi)+\mathbb{N}
,
e(\varphi)
being the ground state energy of
h_{\varphi}
and
\mathbb{N}
the number operator. Moreover, because of the factor
\eta
, we are localizing our state to the regime where the Hessian expansion of
e(\varphi)
from Proposition
3.15
holds. To leading order, this effectively makes our system formally correspond to a system of infinitely many harmonic oscillators with frequencies given by the eigenvalues of
(1\!\!1-K_{L})^{1/2}
, with a Gaussian ground state. To carry out this analysis out rigorously, we need to choose a suitable finite rank projection
\Pi
, as detailed in the remainder of this section.

We are now ready to delve into the details of the proof. It is easy to see that the interaction term appearing in the Fröhlich Hamiltonian acts in the
Q
-space representation as the multiplication by
V_{\varphi}(x)
. Therefore, since
\Psi
corresponds to the vacuum on
(1\!\!1-\Pi)L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
and only depends on
x
through the factor
\psi_{\varphi}(x)
, the g.s. of
h_{\varphi}
, it follows that

(4.1.11)

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\HL}{\Psi}=\expectationvalue{e(\varphi)+\numero}{\Psi}

where
\varphi=\Pi\varphi\cong\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the inner product on the r.h.s. is naturally interpreted as the one on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In the
Q
-space representation, the number operator
takes the form

(4.1.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(-\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}\partial_{\lambda_{n}}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}(-\Delta_{\lambda})+|\lambda|^{2}-\frac{N}{2\alpha^{2}}.

Using the fact that
\eta
is supported on the set
T_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
4.1.7
), we can use the Hessian expansion from Proposition
3.15
to obtain bounds on
e(\lambda)
. Consequently, for a suitable positive constant
C_{L}
,

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{\HL}{\Psi}

\displaystyle\leq\expectationvalue{\eL+\expval{\unit-K_L+\varepsilon C_L J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}}{\Psi}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\Psi\left|\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}(-\Delta_{\lambda})-\frac{N}{2\alpha^{2}}\right|\Psi\right\rangle

(4.1.13)

\displaystyle=\left(e_{L}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\Pi)\right)\|\Psi\|^{2}+A+B,

with

(4.1.14)

\displaystyle A=\left\langle\Psi\left|\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}(-\Delta_{\lambda})+\sum_{i=4}^{N}\mu_{i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda^{L}_{i})^{2}\right|\Psi\right\rangle,

(4.1.15)

\displaystyle B=\varepsilon C_{L}\expectationvalue{\expval{J_L}{\varphi-\varphi_L}}{\Psi}.

We shall now proceed to first show that
B
only contributes as an error term and then to rewrite
A
as the sum of a leading order energy correction term and an error term. We recall that by Lemma
3.16

(4.1.16)

\displaystyle J_{L}\lesssim_{L}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-2}.

Therefore, since
\eta
is supported on
T_{\varepsilon}
, we have

(4.1.17)

\displaystyle B\lesssim_{L}\varepsilon^{3}\|\Psi\|^{2}.

To treat
A
a bit more work is required. A direct calculation shows that

(4.1.18)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}(-\Delta_{\lambda})+\sum_{i=4}^{N}\mu_{i}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda^{L}_{i})^{2}\right]G=\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2})G.

The previous identity, together with straightforward manipulations involving integration by parts, shows that

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\alpha^{4}}\left(\langle\psi_{\varphi}G\eta|\psi_{\varphi}(-\Delta_{\lambda}G)\eta\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{2}|\nabla_{\lambda}(\eta\psi_{\varphi})|^{2}\right)+\left\langle\Psi\left|\sum_{i=4}^{N}\mu_{i}(\lambda-\lambda^{L}_{i})^{2}\right|\Psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2})\|\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\alpha^{4}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{2}\eta^{2}|\nabla_{\lambda}\psi_{\varphi}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{2}|\nabla_{\lambda}\eta|^{2}|\psi_{\varphi}|^{2}\right]

(4.1.19)

\displaystyle=:\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2})\|\Psi\|^{2}+A_{1}+A_{2},

where the first term is clearly a leading order energy correction whereas
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
have to be interpreted as error terms, as we now proceed to show. By standard first order perturbation theory (using that the phase of
\psi_{\varphi}
is chosen so that it is the unique positive minimizer of
h_{\varphi}
) we have

(4.1.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\psi_{\varphi}=-\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}V_{\varphi_{n}}\psi_{\varphi},

where we recall that
Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}=1\!\!1-\ket{\psi_{\varphi}}\bra{\psi_{\varphi}}
. This implies that, for fixed
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}}|\nabla_{\lambda}\psi_{\varphi}(x)|^{2}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left\|\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}V_{\varphi_{n}}\psi_{\varphi}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\expectationvalue{(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2} \psi_{\varphi} \left(\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}\right)^2 \psi_{\varphi}(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}}{\varphi_n}

(4.1.21)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\Pi(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2} \psi_{\varphi} \left(\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}\right)^2 \psi_{\varphi}(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}\Pi),

where
\psi_{\varphi}
is interpreted as a multiplication operator in the last two expressions. Since
(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}\right)^{2}(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{1/2}
is uniformly bounded over the support of
\eta
(the potential
V_{\varphi}
being uniformly infinitesimally relatively bounded with respect to
-\Delta_{L}
by Corollary
3.14
) and recalling that
\psi_{\varphi}
is normalized by definition, we get

\displaystyle\Tr(\Pi(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2} \psi_{\varphi} \left(\frac{Q_{\psi_{\varphi}}}{h_{\varphi}-e(\varphi)}\right)^2 \psi_{\varphi}(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}\Pi)

(4.1.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{L}\Tr(\Pi(-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}\psi_{\varphi} (-\Delta_L+1)^{-1} \psi_{\varphi} (-\Delta_L)^{-1/2}\Pi)\lesssim_{L}1.

In summary, we conclude that

(4.1.23)

\displaystyle A_{1}\lesssim_{L}\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}\|\Psi\|^{2}.

Finally, we proceed to bound
A_{2}
. Recalling the definition of
\eta
and
T_{\varepsilon}
, we see that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\lambda}\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla_{\lambda}\left[\chi\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right)\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2}1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}(\varphi)\left|\nabla_{\lambda}\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\right|^{2}

(4.1.24)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2}1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}(\varphi)\frac{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|^{2}}{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|^{2}}\leq 1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}(\varphi)\varepsilon^{-2},

where we used that
\eta
is supported on
T_{\varepsilon}
and that
\chi
is smooth and compactly supported. Therefore, using also the normalization of
\psi_{\varphi}
, we obtain

(4.1.25)

\displaystyle A_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}\varepsilon^{2}}\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

We now need to bound
\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
in terms of
\|\Psi\|=\|\eta G\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. We define

(4.1.26)

\displaystyle S_{\nu}:=\{\varphi\in\ran\Pi\,|\,\|\Pi^{\prime}(\varphi-\varphi_{L})\|_{2}\leq\nu\}

and observe that on
S_{\nu}\cap T_{\varepsilon}
we have, by the triangle inequality,

(4.1.27)

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon+\nu,

and that on
S_{\nu}^{c}

(4.1.28)

\displaystyle G(\lambda)\leq\exp(-\alpha^2 \tau_L^{1/2} \nu^2),

where we used that
\left[\Pi(1\!\!1-K_{L})\Pi\right]^{1/2}\geq\tau_{L}^{1/2}\Pi^{\prime}
(with
\tau_{L}
being the constant appearing in Proposition
3.18
). We then have, using (
4.1.27
), that

\displaystyle\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}G\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}\cap S_{\nu}}G\|_{2}^{2}+\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}\cap S^{c}_{\nu}}G\|_{2}^{2}

(4.1.29)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon+\nu\}\cap S_{\nu}}G^{2}d\lambda_{1}\dots d\lambda_{N}+\int_{T_{\varepsilon}\cap S^{c}_{\nu}}G^{2}d\lambda_{1}\dots d\lambda_{N}.

We now perform the change of variables
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})=3(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{2},\lambda^{\prime}_{3})
in the first integral and the change of variables
\lambda-\lambda_{L}=2(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{L})
in the second integral and fix
\nu=\varepsilon/8
, obtaining

\displaystyle\|1\!\!1_{T_{\varepsilon}}G\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 27\int_{\{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi\|_{2}\leq(\varepsilon+\nu)/3\}\cap S_{\nu}}G^{2}d\lambda+2^{N}\int_{T_{\varepsilon/2}\cap S_{\nu/2}^{c}}G(\lambda^{\prime})^{8}d\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left(27+2^{N}\exp(-6\alpha^2 \tau_L^{1/2} \nu^2/4)\right)\int_{T_{\varepsilon/2}}G^{2}d\lambda

(4.1.30)

\displaystyle\leq\left(27+2^{N}\exp(-6\alpha^2 \tau_L^{1/2} \nu^2/4)\right)\|\Psi\|^{2},

where in the second step we used that
\{\|(-\Delta_{L}+1)^{-1/2}\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}\varphi\|_{2}\leq(\varepsilon+\nu)/3\}\cap S_{\nu}\subset T_{\varepsilon/2}
by the triangle inequality if
\nu=\varepsilon/8
, and (
4.1.28
) to estimate the Gaussian factor on
S_{\nu/2}^{c}
. Therefore, as long as
\sqrt{N}\leq C_{L}^{1}\alpha\varepsilon
for a sufficiently small
C_{L}^{1}
, we conclude that

(4.1.31)

\displaystyle A_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}\varepsilon^{2}}\|\Psi\|^{2}.

Plugging estimates (
4.1.17
), (
4.1
), (
4.1.23
), and (
4.1.31
) into (
4.1
), we infer, for
\sqrt{N}\leq C_{L}^{1}\alpha\varepsilon
, that for a sufficiently large
C_{L}^{2}

(4.1.32)

\displaystyle\frac{\expectationvalue{\HL}{\Psi}}{\bra{\Psi}\ket{\Psi}}\leq e_{L}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\Pi-\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2})+C_{L}^{2}(\varepsilon^{3}+\alpha^{-4}\varepsilon^{-2}).

We now proceed to choose a real orthonormal basis for
\ran\Pi
which is convenient to bound the r.h.s. of (
4.1.32
). Let
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
K_{L}
with corresponding eigenvalue
k_{j}
, ordered such that
k_{j+1}\geq k_{j}
. By Proposition
3.18
we have
k_{j}=1
for
j=1,2,3
and
k_{j}<1
for
j>3
. Moreover,
\Pi^{L}_{\nabla}
coincides with the projection onto
\spn\{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\}
. We pick
\Pi^{\prime}
to be the projection onto
\spn\{g_{4},\dots,g_{N}\}
if
\varphi_{L}
is spanned by
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{N}\}
and onto
\spn\{g_{4},\dots,g_{N-1},\varphi_{L}\}
otherwise. With this choice the eigenvalues
\mu_{i}
of
\Pi(1\!\!1-K_{L})\Pi
appearing in the Gaussian factor
G
are equal to

(4.1.33)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}=1-k_{j},\,\,\,\,j=1,\dots,N-1,\quad\mu_{N}=\begin{cases}1-k_{N}&\text{if}\,\,\varphi_{L}\in\spn\{g_{1},\dots,g_{N}\},\\
\expectationvalue{\unit-K_L}{\tilde{\varphi}_L}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with
\tilde{\varphi}_{L}:=\frac{\varphi_{L}-\sum_{j=4}^{N-1}g_{j}\bra{g_j}\ket{\varphi_L}}{\|\varphi_{L}-\sum_{j=4}^{N-1}g_{j}\bra{g_j}\ket{\varphi_L}\|_{2}}
.
In any case

\displaystyle\Tr(\Pi-\left[\Pi(\unit-K_L)\Pi\right]^{1/2})

(4.1.34)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-(1-k_{j})^{1/2})=\Tr(\unit-(\unit-K_L)^{1/2})-\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}(1-(1-k_{j})^{1/2}).

In order to estimate
\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}(1-(1-k_{j})^{1/2})
, we note that Lemma
3.16
implies that
k_{j}\lesssim_{L}(l_{j}+1)^{-2}
, where
l_{j}
denotes the ordered eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{L}
. Since
l_{j}\sim j^{2/3}
for
j\gg 1
, we have

(4.1.35)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=N}^{\infty}(1-(1-k_{j})^{1/2})\lesssim_{L}N^{-1/3}.

This allows us to conclude that

(4.1.36)

\displaystyle\frac{\expectationvalue{\HL}{\Psi}}{\bra{\Psi}\ket{\Psi}}\leq e_{L}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\unit-(\unit-K_L)^{1/2})+C_{L}^{3}(\varepsilon^{3}+\alpha^{-4}\varepsilon^{-2}+\alpha^{-2}N^{-1/3}),

as long as
\sqrt{N}\leq C_{L}^{1}\alpha\varepsilon
. The error term is minimized, under this constraint, for
\varepsilon\sim\alpha^{-8/11}
and
N\sim\alpha^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sim\alpha^{6/11}
, which yields

(4.1.37)

\displaystyle\frac{\expectationvalue{\HL}{\Psi}}{\bra{\Psi}\ket{\Psi}}\leq e_{L}-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\Tr(\unit-(\unit-K_L)^{1/2})+C_{L}\alpha^{-24/11},

as claimed in (
2.3.2
).

4.2.
The Cutoff Hamiltonian

As a first step to derive the lower bound in (
2.3.2
), we show that it is possible to apply an ultraviolet cutoff of size
\Lambda
to
\mathbb{H}_{L}
at an expense of order
\Lambda^{-5/2}
(this is proven in Proposition
4.5
in Section
4.2.3
). Our approach follows closely the one in
[
9
]
. It relies on an application of a triple Lieb–Yamazaki bound (extending the method of
[
19
]
) which we carry out in Section
4.2.1
, and on a consequent use (in Section
4.2.2
) of a Gross transformation
[
13
,
23
]
.

We shall in the following, for any
real-valued

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})
, denote

(4.2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(f):=a^{\dagger}(f)+a(f),

(4.2.2)

\displaystyle\Pi(f):=\Phi(if)=i(a^{\dagger}(f)-a(f)).

We recall that (see (
2.1
)) the interaction term in the Fröhlich Hamiltonian is given by

(4.2.3)

\displaystyle-a^{\dagger}(v^{x}_{L})-a(v^{x}_{L})=-\Phi(v^{x}_{L}),

where
v_{L}
was defined in (
2.1.3
) and
a
and
a^{\dagger}
satisfy the rescaled commutation relations (
2.1.5
). We shall apply an ultraviolet cutoff of size
\Lambda
in
k
-space, which amounts to substituting the interaction term with

(4.2.4)

\displaystyle-a^{\dagger}(v^{x}_{L,\Lambda})-a(v^{x}_{L,\Lambda})=-\Phi(v^{x}_{L,\Lambda}),

where

(4.2.5)

\displaystyle v_{L,\Lambda}(y):=\sum_{0\neq k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|<\Lambda}\frac{1}{|k|}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot y}}{L^{3}}.

To quantify the expense of such a cutoff we clearly need to bound

(4.2.6)

\displaystyle-a^{\dagger}(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda})-a(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda})=-\Phi(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda}),

where

(4.2.7)

\displaystyle w_{L,\Lambda}(y)=v_{L}(y)-v_{L,\Lambda}(y)=\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|\geq\Lambda}\frac{1}{|k|}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot y}}{L^{3}}.

4.2.1.
Triple Lieb–Yamazaki Bounds

Let us introduce the notation
p=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})=-i\nabla_{x}
for the electron momentum operator. Note that on any function of the form
f(x,y)=f(y-x)
, such as
w^{x}_{L,\Lambda}
for example, the operator
p
simply acts as multiplication by
k
in
k
-space and agrees, up to a sign, with
-i\nabla_{y}
.

The purpose of this section is to prove the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
w_{L,\Lambda}
be defined as in (
4.2.7
) and
\Lambda>1
. Then

(4.2.8)

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda})+a(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda})=\Phi(w^{x}_{L,\Lambda})\lesssim(|p|^{2}+\mathbb{N}+1)^{2}(\Lambda^{-5/2}+\alpha^{-1}\Lambda^{-3/2}),

as quadratic forms on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
.

We first need the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
w_{L,\Lambda}
be defined as in (
4.2.7
) and
\Lambda>1
. Then for any
j,l,m\in\{1,2,3\}

(4.2.9)

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}\left[(\partial_{j}\partial_{l}\partial_{m}(-\Delta_{L})^{-3}w_{L,\Lambda})^{x}\right]a\left[(\partial_{j}\partial_{l}\partial_{m}(-\Delta_{L})^{-3}w_{L,\Lambda})^{x}\right]\lesssim\Lambda^{-5}\mathbb{N},

(4.2.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{j}\partial_{l}(-\Delta_{L})^{-2}w_{L,\Lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})}\lesssim\Lambda^{-3},

(4.2.11)

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}\left[(\partial_{j}\partial_{l}(-\Delta_{L})^{-2}w_{L,\Lambda})^{x}\right]a\left[(\partial_{j}\partial_{l}(-\Delta_{L})^{-2}w_{L,\Lambda})^{x}\right]\lesssim\Lambda^{-5}(|p|^{2}+L^{-3}\Lambda^{-1})\mathbb{N},

as quadratic forms on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
.

Proof.

For any
j,l,m\in\{1,2,3\}
, (
4.2.9
) follows from

a^{\dagger}(g)a(g)\leq\|g\|_{2}^{2}\mathbb{N}
for
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}),

and then proceeding along the same lines of the proof of (
4.2.10
). To prove (
4.2.10
) we estimate

(4.2.12)

If we denote

with Fourier coefficients

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi\Big|\ket{f_{j,l}^x}\bra{f^x_{j,l}}\Big|\Psi\right\rangle=\int dx\left|\int dy\overline{f_{j,l}^{x}(y)}\Psi(x,y)\right|^{2}=\sum_{q\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left|\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|\geq\Lambda}\frac{k_{j}k_{l}}{L^{3/2}|k|^{5}}\Psi_{q-k,k}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|k|\geq\Lambda,\,\,k\neq q}\frac{1}{L^{3}|k|^{6}|q-k|^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}}|q-k|^{2}|\Psi_{q-k,k}|^{2}\right)+\sum_{q\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3}\atop|q|\geq\Lambda}\frac{|\Psi_{0,q}|^{2}}{L^{3}|q|^{6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{\Lambda}^{c}}\frac{1}{|x|^{6}|q-x|^{2}}dx=\Lambda^{-5}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{1}{|x|^{6}|\Lambda^{-1}q-x|^{2}}

(4.2.15)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda^{-5}\left(\int_{B_{1}(\Lambda^{-1}q)}\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{-1}q-x|^{2}}+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}|x|^{-6}\right)\leq\frac{16\pi}{3}\Lambda^{-5}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Final Lower Bound

In this section we show the validity of the lower bound in (
2.3.2
), thus completing the proof of Theorem
2.5
. With Proposition
4.5
at hand, we have good estimates on the cost of substituting
\mathbb{H}_{L}
with
\mathbb{H}_{L}^{\Lambda}
and, in particular, we know that the difference between the ground state energies of the two is negligible for
\Lambda\gg\alpha^{4/5}
. We are thus left with the task of giving a lower bound on
\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}^{\Lambda}
.

While the previous steps in the lower bound follow closely the analogous strategy in
[
9
]
, the translation invariance of our model leads to substantial complications in the subsequent steps, and the analysis given in this subsection is the main novel part of our proof. In contrast to the case considered in
[
9
]
, the set of minimizers
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{L}=\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L})
is a three-dimensional manifold, and in order to decouple the resulting zero-modes of the Hessian of the Pekar functional we find it necessary introduce a suitable diffeomorphism that ’flattens’ the manifold of minimizers and the region close to it. Special attention also has to be paid on the metric in which this closeness is measured, necessitating the introduction of the family of norms in (
3.3.47
).

We emphasize that the non-uniformity in
L
also results from the subsequent analysis, where the compactness of resolvent of
-\Delta_{L}
enters in an essential way.

Let
\Pi
denote the projection

(4.3.1)

\displaystyle\ran\Pi=\spn\left\{L^{-3/2}e^{ik\cdot x},\,\,k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{3},\,\,|k|\leq\Lambda\right\},\quad N=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\ran\Pi.

For later use we note that

(4.3.2)

\displaystyle N\sim\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{3}\Lambda^{3}\quad\text{as}\,\,\Lambda\to\infty.

The Fock space
\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
naturally factorizes into the tensor product
\mathcal{F}(\Pi L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))\otimes\mathcal{F}((1\!\!1-\Pi)L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
and
\mathbb{H}_{L}^{\Lambda}
is of the form
\mathbb{A}\otimes 1\!\!1+1\!\!1\otimes\mathbb{N}^{>}
, with
\mathbb{A}
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L})\otimes\mathcal{F}(\Pi L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
and
\mathbb{N}^{>}
being the number operator on
\mathcal{F}((1\!\!1-\Pi)L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
. In particular,
\infspec\mathbb{H}_{L}^{\Lambda}=\infspec\mathbb{A}
.

As in Section
4.1
, we can, for any
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of real-valued functions
\{f_{n}\}
of
\ran\Pi
, identify
\mathcal{F}(\Pi L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}_{L}))
with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
through the

-space representation (see
[

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=j_{1}\mathbb{K}j_{1}+j_{2}\mathbb{K}j_{2}-\mathbb{E},

Lemma 4.6
.

(4.3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\lesssim\alpha^{-4}\varepsilon^{-2}

Proof.

To bound

Likewise, for any

such that

It is easy to see, using again Lemma

for any

. Therefore, using that

\displaystyle 2\bra{f_n}W_{T}\ket{\varphi-\varphi_L^{y_{\varphi}}}=\lim_{h\to 0}2\bra{f_n}W_{T}\ket{\varphi-\varphi_L^{y_{h,n}}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\left(\dist_{W_{T}}^{2}(\varphi+hf_{n},\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))-\dist_{W_{T}}^{2}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\bra{f_n}W_{T}\ket{\varphi-\varphi_L^{y_{\varphi}}},

which shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\dist^{2}_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))=2\bra{f_n}W_{T}(\varphi-\varphi_{L}^{y_{\varphi}})\rangle.

Using that

\displaystyle\left|\left[\partial_{\lambda_{n}}j_{k}\right](\varphi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}\left|\partial_{\lambda_{n}}\dist^{2}_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))\right|^{2}1\!\!1_{\left\{\dist_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))\leq\varepsilon\right\}}

(4.3.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}|\bra{f_n}W_{T}(\varphi-\varphi_{L}^{y_{\varphi}})\rangle|^{2}1\!\!1_{\left\{\dist_{W_{T}}(\varphi,\Omega_{L}(\varphi_{L}))\leq\varepsilon\right\}}.

A note on almost everywhere convergence along tangential curves to the Schrödinger equation initial datum
Javier Minguillón

Abstract
In this short note, we give an easy proof of the following result: for n ≥ 2 , n\geq 2, lim t → 0 ​ e i ​ t ​ Δ ​ f ​ ( x + γ ⁡ ( t ) ) = f ⁡ ( x ) \underset{t\to 0}{\lim}\,e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\gamma(t)\right)=f(x) almost everywhere whenever γ \gamma is an α − \alpha- Hölder curve with 1 2 ≤ α ≤ 1 \frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1 and f ∈ H s ​ ( ℝ n ) f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) , with s > n 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)} . This is the optimal range of regularity up to the endpoint.

Introduction

Consider the linear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 1,
given by

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta_{x}u(x,t)=0,\\
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{cases}

(1)

Its solution can be formally expressed as

e^{it\Delta}f\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}e^{2\pi it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

(2)

It was first proposed by Carleson in 1980
[
3
]
to find the values of
s>0
for which

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f\left(x\right)=f(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3)

holds true for all functions
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Carleson
[
3
]
proved this convergence when
n=1
and
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
. Later, in 2006, Dahlberg and Kenig
[
6
]
showed that
(
3
)
was false whenever
s<\frac{1}{4}.

Many researchers have worked in this problem throughout the years. Authors such as Carbery, Cowling, Vega, Sjölin, Moyua, Vargas, Tao, S.Lee and Bourgain to name a few.
More recently, the problem has been solved in higher dimensions, except for the endpoint. In 2016, Bourgain
[
1
]
proved the necessity of
s\geq\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
in order to have
(
3
). In 2017, Du, Guth and Li
[
7
]
proved the sufficiency of the condition
s>\frac{1}{3}
when
n=2.

Later, in 2019, Du and Zhang
[
8
]
proved the sufficiency of
s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
for general
n\geq 3.
A more detailed history of the problem can be found in
[
8
]
and the references therein.

Take a solution of (
1
). Consider a set of curves
\rho(x,t)=x+\gamma(t)
that are bi-Lipschitz in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha-
Hölder in
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Cho, Lee and Vargas
[
4
]

proved in 2012 that
u\left(\rho(x,t),t\right)
converges to
f(x)
almost everywhere as
t\to 0

in
n=1
when
s>\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha,\frac{1}{4}\right\}
. They also found this to be sharp up to the endpoint. Later, in 2021, Li and Wang
[
11
]
proved that convergence in dimension
n=2
, for index
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
and the range
s>\frac{3}{8}
.
In 2023, Cao and Miao
[
2
]
gave a proof for general dimension
n
, index
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
, and
s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
. Their proof followed the argument presented in
[
8
]
and relied on techniques such as
dyadic pigeonholing, broad-narrow analysis
and induction on scales.

Our objective is to give an easy proof of the result in
[
2
]
without using the aforementioned techniques.

Fix
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
\tau\geq 1.
We consider the family of curves,

\Gamma_{\tau}^{\alpha}:=\left\{\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for all }t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1],\;|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t^{\prime})|\leq\tau|t-t^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\right\}.

(4)

The convergence result follows from the maximal bound below.
Let
B_{r}^{n}(x_{0})
denote the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Fix
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
and
\tau\geq 1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon,\tau}
such that, for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\tau}^{\alpha}
,

\left\|\sup_{0<t<1}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\gamma(t)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{1}(0)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\tau}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}{\color[rgb]{0.12,0.56,1}}+\epsilon}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)},

(5)

holds for all

f

\in H^{\frac{1}{2(n+1)}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 1.2
.

A change of variables shows that it is enough to consider the case
\tau=1
. From now on we assume
\tau=1.

Then, we can reduce Theorem
1.1
as in
[
8
]
.
We begin with a definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Fixed
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
R>1
, we define

\Gamma^{\alpha}\left(R^{-1}\right):=\left\{\gamma:[0,R^{-1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for all }t,t^{\prime}\in[0,R^{-1}],\;|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t^{\prime})|\leq|t-t^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\right\}.

(6)

By Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the time localization lemma (e.g. Lemma 3.1 in S.Lee
[
9
]
) and parabolic rescaling, Theorem
1.1
can be reduced to the following Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}\left(R^{-1}\right)
,

\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{R}(0)\right)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}+\varepsilon}{\color[rgb]{0.12,0.56,1}}\|f\|_{2}.

(7)

holds for all
R\geq 1
and all
f
with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset A(1)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\sim 1\right\}
.

2
Intermediate results

We consider the following result from
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 1.7 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 1
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that the following holds for all
R\geq 1
and all
f
with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset B_{1}^{n}(0)
. Suppose that
X=\cup_{k}B_{k}
is a union of lattice unit cubes in
B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
. Let
1\leq\beta\leq n+1
and

\phi:=\phi_{X,\beta}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{r}^{n+1}(x^{\prime})\subset B_{R}^{n+1}(0)\\
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},r\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\#\left\{B_{k}:B_{k}\subset B^{n+1}_{r}\left(x^{\prime}\right)\right\}}{r^{\beta}}.

(8)

Then

\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\right\|_{L^{2}(X,dxdt)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\phi^{\frac{1}{n+1}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2(n+1)}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{2}.

(9)

We generalize the above result to include
\alpha-
Hölder curves.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that the following holds for any
R\geq 1
, every
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}\left(R^{-1}\right)
and all
f
with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset B_{1}^{n}(0)
. Suppose that
X=\cup_{k}B_{k}
is a union of lattice unit cubes in
B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
. Let
1\leq\beta\leq n+1
and
\phi
be given by
(
8
).
Then

\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(X,dxdt)}\leq{\color[rgb]{0.12,0.56,1}}C_{\varepsilon}\phi^{\frac{1}{n+1}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2(n+1)}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{2}.

(10)

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Denote

\theta(t):=\theta_{R}(t):=R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right).

(11)

We begin with

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(X,dxdt)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int_{B_{k}}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\theta(t)\right)\right|^{2}dxdt.

(12)

Denote
(x_{k},t_{k})
to be the center of
B_{k}.
Then,

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\int_{t_{k}-1{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}}^{t_{k}+1{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}}\int_{B^{n}_{1}(x_{k})}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\theta(t)\right)\right|^{2}dxdt

(13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int_{t_{k}-1}^{t_{k}+1}\int_{B^{n}_{1}\left(x_{k}+\theta(t)\right)}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(y\right)\right|^{2}dydt.

(14)

Recall that
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}\left(R^{-1}\right)
, and
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, if
t\in(t_{k}-1,t_{k}+1),
then
\left|\theta(t)-\theta(t_{k})\right|\leq 1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\int_{t_{k}-1}^{t_{k}+1}\int_{B^{n}_{3}\left(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k})\right)}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(y\right)\right|^{2}dydt

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k}\chi_{B^{n+1}_{4}\left(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k}\right)}(y)\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(y\right)\right|^{2}dydt

(16)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\bigcup B^{n+1}_{4}(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k})}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(y\right)\right|^{2}dydt,

(17)

for some
C>0.
Note that, if
B_{4}(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k})\cap B_{4}(x_{i}+\theta(t_{i}),t_{i})\neq\emptyset,
then
|t_{k}-t_{i}|\leq 8.
Since
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2},
we have
|\theta(t_{k})-\theta(t_{i})|\leq 8
. Hence,
|x_{k}-x_{i}|\leq 16.
Therefore, the balls
\{B_{4}(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k})\}_{k}
have finite overlap
C=C_{n}
.

Define

Y=\bigcup_{l}Q_{l}
to be the minimal union of lattice unit cubes satisfying that
\bigcup_{k}B_{4}^{n+1}(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k})\subset Y.
We have proven that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\theta(t)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(X,dxdt)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(Y,dxdt)}.

(18)

Hence by Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,n}\phi_{Y,\beta}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}R^{\frac{\beta}{2(n+1)}+\epsilon}\|f\|_{2}.

(19)

We
claim
that

\phi_{Y,\beta}\leq c_{n}\phi_{X,\beta},

(20)

for some
C>0.
This would conclude the proof.

To prove (
20
), note that, if
Q_{l}\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y_{0},s_{0})
,
r\geq 1,
and
Q_{l}\cap B_{4}^{n+1}(x_{k}+\theta(t_{k}),t_{k})\neq\emptyset
, then
B_{k}=B_{1}^{n+1}(x_{k},t_{k})\subset B_{r+5}^{n+1}(y_{0}-\theta(s_{0}),s_{0}).

Therefore,

\dfrac{\#\{Q_{l}:Q_{l}\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y_{0},s_{0})\}}{r^{\beta}}\leq c_{n}\dfrac{\#\left\{B_{k}:B_{k}\subset B_{r+5}^{n+1}(y_{0}-\theta(s_{0}),s_{0})\right\}}{(r+5)^{\beta}}\cdot\dfrac{(r+5)^{\beta}}{r^{\beta}}\leq c_{n}\phi_{X,\beta}.

(21)

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Before the proof, let us introduce a stability property of the Schrödinger operator. More general versions of the following appeared in an article of Tao
[
12
]
from 1999 and an article of Christ
[
5
]
from 1988.

Suppose that

\widehat{f}
is supported inside a ball of radius 1.
If
|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\leq 4
and
|t^{\prime}-s^{\prime}|\leq 4,
then,

\left|e^{it^{\prime}\Delta}f(x^{\prime})\right|\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\left|e^{is^{\prime}\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y^{\prime})\right|,

(22)

where
\widehat{f_{\mathfrak{l}}}(\xi)=e^{2\pi i\mathfrak{l}\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)
.

Now, fix
\alpha\geq 1/2
and
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}\left(R^{-1}\right)
. Define
\theta
as in (
11
). Whenever
|x-y|\leq 2
and
|t-s|\leq 2
, we have that

\left|x+\theta(t)-(y+\theta(s))\right|\leq 4.
Thus,

\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x+\theta(t))\right|\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y+\theta(s))\right|.

(23)

Therefore, if
|x-x_{0}|\leq 1
and
|t-t_{0}|\leq 1
, then,

\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x+\theta(t))\right|\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}\int_{B_{1}(x_{0})}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y+\theta(t))\right|dyds.

(24)

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For the sake of briefness, given
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
let us denote

E^{\prime}f(x,t):=E^{\prime}_{\gamma,R}f(x,t):=e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right).

(25)

Now, we can write

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{R}(0)\right)}^{2}=\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{R}(0)\right)}^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{n}_{R}(0)}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t\leq R\end{subarray}}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|^{2}\right)dx

(27)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}\int_{B^{n}_{1}(x_{0})}\left(\sup_{0<t\leq R}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|^{2}\right)dx

(28)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}\left[\sup_{|x-x_{0}|\leq 1}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t\leq R\end{subarray}}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|^{2}\right)\right].

(29)

For each
x_{0}
, there exists
\widetilde{t_{0}}=\widetilde{t_{0}}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,R]
such that the supremum on each term of the above sum is almost attained inside
B^{n+1}_{1}(x_{0},\widetilde{t_{0}})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|\right\|_{L^{2}(B^{n}_{R}(0))}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}\sup_{(x,t)\in B_{1}^{n+1}(x_{0},\widetilde{t_{0}})}\left|E^{\prime}f(x,t)\right|^{2},

(30)

which is, by (
24
),

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{N}}\int_{B^{n}_{1}\left(x_{0}\right)}\int_{\widetilde{t_{0}}}^{\widetilde{t_{0}}+1}\left|E^{\prime}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y,s)\right|dsdy\right|^{2}.

(31)

Therefore, denoting
C_{\mathfrak{l}}=\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{N}}
and using Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}C_{\mathfrak{l}}\left\|E^{\prime}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y,t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B^{n+1}_{2}(x_{0},\widetilde{t_{0}}))}.

(32)

Let us choose
X=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|x_{0}|<R\end{subarray}}B^{n+1}_{2}\left(x_{0},\widetilde{t_{0}}\right)
. The above lets us deduce
that

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|\right\|_{L^{2}(B^{n}_{R}(0),dx)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}C_{\mathfrak{l}}\left\|E^{\prime}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y,t)\right\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}.

(33)

By Theorem
2.2
, this is,

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\epsilon}{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}C_{\mathfrak{l}}(\phi_{X,n})^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\|f_{\mathfrak{l}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}R^{\frac{2n}{2(n+1)}+\epsilon}.

(34)

Recall that, given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{R}^{n}(0),
we have chosen exactly one
\widetilde{t_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,R]
. Consequently,
\phi_{X,n}\leq 1
. Therefore, the above inequalities yield

\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq R}|E^{\prime}f(x,t)|\right\|_{L^{2}(B^{n}_{R}(0),dx)}^{2}\lesssim C_{\epsilon}R^{\frac{2n}{2(n+1)}+\epsilon}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

(35)

Integrable boundary conditions for staggered vertex models
Holger Frahm, Sascha Gehrmann

Abstract
Yang-Baxter integrable vertex models with a generic ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -staggering can be expressed in terms of composite ℝ \mathbb{R} -matrices given in terms of the elementary R R -matrices. Similarly, integrable open boundary conditions can be constructed through generalized reflection algebras based on these objects and their representations in terms of composite boundary matrices 𝕂 ± \mathbb{K}^{\pm} . We show that only two types of staggering yield a local Hamiltonian with integrable open boundary conditions in this approach. The staggering in the underlying model allows for a second hierarchy of commuting integrals of motion (in addition to the one including the Hamiltonian obtained from the usual transfer matrix), starting with the so-called quasi momentum operator. In this paper, we show that this quasi momentum operator can be obtained together with the Hamiltonian for both periodic and open models in a unified way from enlarged Yang-Baxter or reflection algebras in the composite picture. For the special case of the staggered six-vertex model, this allows constructing an integrable spectral flow between the two local cases.



I
Introduction

Integrable lattice models based on representations of the Yang-Baxter algebra have proven to be extremely useful in the understanding of non-perturbative phenomena in one-dimensional many-body systems. Given a particular
R
-matrix, it is possible to consider variations in the spectral parameter leading to local inhomogeneities preserving the integrability of such models. This has first been used by Baxter in the context of the six-vertex model
Baxter 1971
. In particular, models with periodically repeating inhomogeneities (or staggered models) have proven to be applicable to a wide range of problems. Apart from the construction of integrable spin chains with larger unit cells
Popkov and Zvyagin 1993
;
Frahm and Rödenbeck 1996
inhomogeneous vertex models have been used, e.g., to formulate the Potts model as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-staggered six-vertex model
Baxter 1973
, for the lattice regularization of field theories such as the principal chiral model
Faddeev and Reshetikhin 1986
;
Destri and de Vega 1989
, in the quantum transfer matrix approach to the thermodynamics of integrable models
Klümper 1992
, and to study integrable perturbations of conformal field theories
Reshetikhin and Saleur 1994
.
Alternatively, the staggering can be realized by choosing alternating local representations of the underlying symmetry algebra. Such a staggering appears quite naturally in the superspin formulation of network models describing the disorder induced plateau transition in integer quantum Hall systems
Chalker and Coddington 1988
;
Zirnbauer 1997
which can be made integrable by fine-tuning of the coupling constants
Gade 1999
;
Essler et al. 2005
. Extensions of such staggered models to open boundary conditions with their integrability encoded in representations of the corresponding reflection algebra
Cherednik 1984
;
Sklyanin 1988
allow for the construction of spin chains with soliton non-preserving boundary conditions
Doikou 2000
. Similar algebraic structures emerge in the study of certain AdS/CFT-type integrability theories
Prinsloo et al. 2015
;
Bai et al. 2019
.

Interestingly, finite-size studies of certain staggered models based both on variations of the spectral parameter or the local representations have revealed that their continuum limit – in spite of the compact formulation as a spin chain – is described by conformal field theories with a non-compact target space
Essler et al. 2005
;
Ikhlef et al. 2008
;
Frahm and Martins 2011
;
Frahm and Martins 2012
.
Among these the most studied example is the periodically staggered six-vertex model whose low energy effective theory has been identified to be the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/U(1)
black hole CFT
Ikhlef et al. 2008
;
Ikhlef et al. 2012
;
Candu and Ikhlef 2013
;
Frahm and Seel 2014
;
Bazhanov et al. 2021a
;
Bazhanov et al. 2021b
. More recently, the influence of open
U_{q}(sl(2))
-invariant boundary conditions in this model has been studied, both for the self-dual staggering related to the Potts model
Robertson et al. 2020a
;
Robertson et al. 2021
and also away from the self-dual line
Frahm and Gehrmann 2022
. These studies have shown that boundary conditions have a profound effect: depending on their choice the symmetry of the ground state may be spontaneously broken or the continuous component of the conformal spectrum disappears completely.

A conserved quantity existing in these models which has been particularly useful for the identification of the conformal field theory is the so-called quasi momentum operator. Its role in staggered models without a non-compact continuum limit, however, has not been studied yet.
The definition of the quasi momentum relies on the possibility to introduce a staggering in the vertical direction of the vertex model which is compatible with the horizontal one
Ikhlef et al. 2012
;
Frahm and Seel 2014
;
Frahm and Hobuß 2017
;
Frahm and Gehrmann 2022
.
That such an operator cannot be defined in the homogeneous case has impeded progress in the analysis of the spectrum other models where indications for a continuous spectrum of conformal weights have been observed, namely the
a_{N-1}^{(2)}
models and a family of orthosymplectic superspin chains

Vernier et al. 2014
;
Vernier et al. 2016
;
Martins et al. 1998
;
Frahm and Martins 2015
;
Frahm and Martins 2018
;
Frahm et al. 2019
;
Frahm and Martins 2022
.

In this work, we will use a different perspective on the staggered models to address the question of whether the quasi momentum can be constructed in an alternative approach which may be applicable for homogeneous models, too.
After a brief review of the construction of integrable models with periodic and open boundary conditions based on an ’elementary’
R
-matrix solving the Yang-Baxter equation and corresponding boundary matrices, we construct ’composite’
\mathbb{R}
-matrices using the co-multiplication property of the Yang-Baxter algebra. These
\mathbb{R}
-matrices satisfy a generalized Yang-Baxter equation (
18
) and depend on the staggering parameters through additional arguments. For periodic boundary conditions this allows to define a homogeneous transfer matrix generating both the local integrals of motions such as the Hamiltonian and the quasi momentum operator.

In Section
III
we generalize this procedure to the open case, where we express composite boundary matrices in terms of the elementary ones. Depending on the properties of the elementary
R
-matrices (and unlike in the periodic case) we identify two different choices of the staggering parameters leading to a transfer matrix constructed from the composite
\mathbb{R}
- and boundary matrices which generates a Hamiltonian with local interactions in the bulk (similar as in Refs.
Nepomechie and Retore 2021a
;
Li et al. 2022
). For one of these choices a second homogeneous transfer matrix with boundary matrices satisfying a different reflection equation generates the quasi momentum. The commutativity of these objects is guaranteed by a set of intertwining relations between the two sets of boundary matrices.

Finally we apply our findings to the self-dual staggered six-vertex model. Based on this construction the spectral flow between the models with compact and non-compact continuum limits can be studied in a family of integrable models. Although one has to give up locality at the intermediate steps one finds that the two endpoints of this scheme are separated by two first-order transitions where massive degeneracies lead to a reordering of levels. Based on the numerical solution of the Bethe equations we provide some insights into the role of the quasi momentum in the model with compact continuum limit.

For the physical interpretation as a lattice model with local (i.e. finite range) interactions additional conditions have to be satisfied. Typically, locality can be derived from the regularity property of the
R
-matrix, i.e. that it becomes a permutation operator at a shift point

), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{0\overline{0}}=\Delta_{12}\equiv\Delta\,.

where we have assumed, in addition, that
R
is differentiable at
\pm\Delta

The staggering in the auxiliary direction allows to construct another operator, generating a family of commuting integrals deriving from (
7
): instead of (
20
) we can consider the so-called quasi shift operator, given by the quotient of single row transfer matrices

\displaystyle\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(u+\delta_{\overline{0}},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(u+\delta_{0},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}\,.

As done for the product of transfer matrices, we shift the spectral parameter
u\to u-\delta_{\overline{0}}-\delta_{1}
, leading to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathrm{pbc}}(u,\{\delta_{0},\delta_{\overline{0}},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})=\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(u-\delta_{1},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(u+\Delta_{0\overline{0}}-\delta_{1},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}\,.

Restricting the staggering parameters
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{0}

\delta_{\overline{0}}

u_{0}=0
, we obtain the ’quasi momentum’

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{pbc}}=\log\left[\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathrm{pbc}}(0,\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})\right]=\log\left[\frac{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(-\delta_{1},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}{\tau^{\mathrm{pbc}}(-\delta_{2},\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})}\right]\,.

;
Candu and Ikhlef 2013

Frahm and Seel 2014

Frahm and Hobuß 2017

Frahm and Gehrmann 2022

Remarkably, the choice of boundary conditions has a profound influence on the low energy properties of the staggered models:
the effective theory of (
64
) with anisotropy
\gamma<2\delta_{0}<\pi-\gamma
has been identified to be the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})/U(1)
sigma model at level
k=\pi/\gamma
with a non-compact spectrum of conformal weights
Robertson et al. 2021
;
Frahm and Gehrmann 2022
. On the contrary, Robertson
et al.
found that the continuum limit of the model (
65
) is compact
Robertson et al. 2020b
.
The boundary RG flow between these two critical fixed points has been studied numerically: based on finite size estimates of the gap between the ground state and the lowest excitation when the amplitude of the boundary term in (
65
) is varied between the two integrable points it has been concluded that the fixed points corresponding to (
64
) and (
65
) are unstable and stable, respectively
Robertson et al. 2021
.

In the setting established in this paper both (
64
) and (
65
) originate from a staggered vertex model with the
A_{1}^{(1)}

R
-matrix (
56
). This allows to study the spectral flow between (
64
) and (
65
) in an integrable setting with a fixed choice of the boundary matrices under the variation of the bulk inhomogeneities.
The price to pay for integrability is giving up locality of the Hamiltonian at the intermediate points. Using the same staggering in the vertical and horizontal directions of the vertex model, i.e.
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{\overline{0}}\}=\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
, we tune
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
to interpolate between the integrable models with local interactions.
We choose the following normalization of the non-local ’Hamiltonian’

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\frac{q\det\left(T\left(-\delta_{1}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma}{2}\right)\right)q\det\left(T\left(-\delta_{2}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma}{2}\right)\right)}{2\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f(\delta_{1})f(\delta_{2})}

(66)

\displaystyle\times\left(\left.\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}u}\right|_{u=0}\mathcal{T}(u,\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\})-\left.\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}u}\right|_{u=0}f(u+\delta_{1})f(u+\delta_{2})\right)

with

\displaystyle f(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(2u+2\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)}{\sinh(2u+\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)}\sinh^{L}(u-\delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)\sinh^{L}(u-\delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)

(67)

\displaystyle\times\sinh^{L}(u+\delta_{1}+\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)\sinh^{L}(u+\delta_{2}+\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\gamma)\,.

Specifically, we choose for the remaining inhomogeneities the parameterization

\displaystyle\delta_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\vartheta}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}\,,\qquad\delta_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\vartheta}{2}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}\,,\qquad-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\vartheta\leq 0\,,

(68)

resulting in alternating (quasi periodic) staggering for
\vartheta=0
and
-\pi/2
, respectively.

The eigenvalues of the ’Hamiltonian’ (
66
) in this parameterization are given in terms of the Bethe roots
\{v_{m}\}
solving (
60
) as

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\left(-4L\cot(2\gamma)+L\frac{2\sin(2\vartheta)}{\sin(2\gamma)\sin(2(\gamma+\vartheta))}-\frac{2\sin(\vartheta)}{\cos(\gamma)\cos(\gamma+\vartheta)}+\frac{2\sin(\vartheta)}{\cos(2\gamma)\cos(2\gamma+\vartheta)}\right.

(69)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2\tan(\gamma)}{\cos(2\gamma)}\right)\times\left(\prod^{M}_{m=1}\frac{\cos(2(\gamma-\vartheta))+\cosh(4v_{m})}{\cos(2(\gamma+\vartheta))+\cosh(4v_{m})}-1\right)

\displaystyle-4\sin(2\gamma)\sum^{M}_{k=1}\left\{\frac{\cos(2\gamma)+\cos(\vartheta)\cosh(4v_{k})}{(\cos(2(\gamma+\vartheta))+\cosh(4v_{k}))^{2}}\right\}\times\prod^{M}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1{}\\
m\neq k\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(2(\gamma-\vartheta))+\cosh(4v_{m})}{\cos(2(\gamma+\vartheta))+\cosh(4v_{m})}\,.

As expected, this expression reduces to a sum of bare quasi-particle energies
\epsilon_{0}(v_{m})
for
\vartheta=0,\pm\pi/2
where the Hamiltonian becomes local.
Away from these points, the normalization of (
69
) leads to singularities at particular values of the flow parameter:
the one at
\vartheta=\frac{\pi}{2}-2\gamma
can be removed by multiplying the Hamiltonian by the
\vartheta
-dependent factor
\cos(2\gamma+\vartheta)
while
\vartheta=\frac{\pi}{2}-\gamma>0
does not lie on the spectral flow (
68
). The remaining singularity at
\vartheta_{c1}=-\gamma
depends on the state considered. In terms of the corresponding Bethe root configuration this can be related to the low energy root configurations of the quasi periodic model (
65
). These consists of pairs of complex conjugate roots with imaginary part
\pm\frac{\pi}{4}
and an additional root at
\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}
for
M^{\mathrm{qper}}
odd
Robertson et al. 2020b
:

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{qper}}\in\left\{x_{m}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4},x_{m}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}\bigg|x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\,,m=1,...,\left\lfloor\frac{M^{\mathrm{qper}}}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}\right\}\,.

(70)

Here
\lfloor\dots\rfloor
denotes the Gaussian bracket. Exact diagonalization of the Hamiltonian for small systems together with the determination of the corresponding Bethe roots shows that root patterns of this type persist throughout the interval
\vartheta=-\pi/2\dots\vartheta_{c1}
. At
\vartheta_{c1}
, however, several roots become purely imaginary,
v_{m}=\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi/4
, changing the order of the pole in (

\sin^{\nu}(\vartheta+\gamma)
with an appropriate choice of an integer
\nu
.

The spectral flow starting from the alternating model (
64
),
\vartheta=0
, can be studied in a similar way: here a class of low energy states (including the ground state) is known
Frahm and Gehrmann 2022
;
Robertson et al. 2021
to be described by configurations consisting of real roots and ones having an imaginary part of
\frac{\pi}{2}
:

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{alt}}=\left\{x_{m},y_{n}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{2}\bigg|x_{m},y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\,m=1,...,M_{0},\,n=1,...,M_{\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{2}}\right\}\,.

(71)

Configurations of this type exist in the interval
\vartheta=\vartheta_{c2}\dots 0
where roots with vanishing real parts appear for
\vartheta_{c2}=\gamma-\pi/2
. These do not, however, lead to singularities in the eigenvalues of (
69
).

Both at
\vartheta_{c1}
and
\vartheta_{c2}
the appearance of purely imaginary Bethe roots leads to degeneracies involving many states, as shown in results from exact diagonalization for a spin chain with
2L=8
sites, anisotropy

\vartheta_{c1}
or
\vartheta_{c2}

Support for this interpretation is obtained by studying the spectral flow within the root density formalism

\displaystyle\rho^{a}(v)=\sigma^{a}(v)+\frac{1}{L}\tau^{a}(v)\,,\quad a=x,y\,,

with bulk and surface contributions

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\left(\frac{\pi\,\vartheta}{\pi-2\gamma}\right)}{\pi-2\gamma}\left[\frac{1}{\cosh\left(\frac{2\pi v}{\pi-2\gamma}\right)+\sin\left(\frac{\pi\,\vartheta}{\pi-2\gamma}\right)}+\frac{1}{\cosh\left(\frac{2\pi v}{\pi-2\gamma}\right)-\sin\left(\frac{\pi\,\vartheta}{\pi-2\gamma}\right)}\right]\,,

\bar{\rho}(x)

\sigma^{x,y}(v)
(

V.2
Role of the quasi momentum in the quasi periodic model

As mentioned above the continuum limit of the alternating model (
64
) is described by a non-compact conformal field theory. In the lattice model this is manifest in the finite size gaps closing as

\Delta E^{\mathrm{alt}}_{n}=E^{\mathrm{alt}}_{n}-Le^{\mathrm{alt}}_{\infty}-f^{\mathrm{alt}}_{\infty}\sim\frac{\pi v^{\mathrm{alt}}_{F}}{L}\left(\text{const.}+\text{const.}\frac{(dN^{\mathrm{alt}})^{2}}{\log(L)^{2}}\right)\,,

(74)

where
Le^{\mathrm{alt}}_{\infty}
and
f^{\mathrm{alt}}_{\infty}
are the bulk and surface contributions to the energy and
v^{\mathrm{alt}}_{F}
is the Fermi velocity. On the level of the Bethe configurations (
71
) logarithmic corrections arise when the numbers
M_{0}
(
M_{\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{2}}
) of roots with
\text{Im}(v_{m}^{alt})=0
(
\frac{\pi}{2}
) are different, i.e.
dN^{\mathrm{alt}}=M_{0}-M_{\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{2}}\neq 0
. For large
L
the eigenvalues of the quasi momentum operator are proportional to
dN^{\mathrm{alt}}/\log(L)
, which allows a direct identification of the underlying CFT
Ikhlef et al. 2012
;
Frahm and Seel 2014
;
Frahm and Gehrmann 2022
.

In this section, we reconsider the finite-size analysis of the quasi periodic chain using the definition (
55
).
Motivated by the insights gained in the alternating model we consider Bethe configurations (
70
) with different numbers
M_{\pm\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}}
of roots on the lines
\text{Im}(v_{m}^{alt})=\pm\frac{\pi}{4}
.
To investigate the scaling behavior of those states, we consider the rescaled energy gaps

\displaystyle h^{n}_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{\pi v^{\mathrm{qp}}_{F}}\left(E^{\mathrm{qp}}_{n}-Le^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}-f^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}\right)\,.

(75)

The energies
e^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}
,
f^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}
and Fermi velocity of the quasi periodic model are obtained in the root density formalism using (
73
) with
\vartheta=-\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}=2f^{\mathrm{qp}}_{\infty}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\text{d}\omega\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{\gamma\omega}{2}\right)}{\sinh\left(\frac{\pi\omega}{4}\right)\cosh\left(\frac{1}{4}(\pi-2\gamma)\omega\right)}\,,\qquad v^{\mathrm{qp}}_{F}=\frac{2\pi}{\pi-2\gamma}\,.

(76)

Using the Bethe-Ansatz we have calculated
h^{n}_{\text{eff}}
for states with various
dN^{\mathrm{qp}}=M_{-\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}}-M_{\frac{\mathrm{i}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\pi}{4}}
as displayed in Fig.
10

VI
Conclusion

Starting from an elementary solution
R
of the YBE (
1
) we have constructed the composite
\mathbb{R}
-matrix (
17
). In addition to the spectral parameter the composite matrix depends on two free parameters related to the staggering of the elementary vertices (see Figure
2
).
PT
-symmetry, unitarity, regularity and crossing unitarity are inherited from the elementary
R
-matrix. Most importantly, the composite
\mathbb{R}
-matrix obeys a generalized YBE (
18
). In this picture the commuting transfer matrices of arbitrary
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-staggered models can be rewritten as homogeneous ones where the staggering parameters enter through the additional arguments of the composite
\mathbb{R}
-matrices (and reflection matrices in the case of open boundary conditions). Integrals of motion (including the quasi momentum operator) whose natural definition relies on the staggering of the model have been described in the
homogeneous
picture based on the composite
\mathbb{R}
-matrix. In the case of open boundary conditions the Hamiltonian and the quasi momentum are defined in terms of
different
representations of the reflection algebra intertwined by (
52
).
This construction may provide insights into a definition of this operator in homogeneous models (lacking a known factorization of the transfer matrix). This is of particular interest for such models featuring a continuous component of the conformal spectrum at criticality
Vernier et al. 2014
;
Vernier et al. 2016
;
Frahm and Martins 2018
;
Frahm et al. 2019
. Knowing the quasi momentum operator in these models is expected to foster the identification of the CFT describing the low energy regime.

Demanding locality in the Hamiltonian limit leads to constraints on the staggering parameters: in the case of periodic boundary conditions they have to be tuned to satisfy (
22
). For open boundary conditions the staggering has to satisfy Eq. (
33
) for ’alternating staggering’. Moreover, for quasi periodic
R
- (and
\mathbb{R}
-) matrices the inequivalent choice of ’quasi periodic staggering’ (
37
) leads to a different Hamiltonian with local interactions. For both cases we have identified the corresponding composite boundary matrices which generalizes the findings of the factorization
Li et al. 2022
for the
D^{(2)}_{2}
boundary matrices
Martins and Guan 2000
;
Nepomechie et al. 2017
;
Nepomechie and Pimenta 2018
to arbitrary algebras.

Applying our construction to this model we have studied the spectral flow between the alternating and the quasi periodic model. Following this flow along a line of integrable models we find that the endpoints are separated by two first-order transitions which is consistent with the different properties of the corresponding spectra observed previously. The different role of the quasi momentum operator in the alternating and quasi periodic model are briefly discussed.

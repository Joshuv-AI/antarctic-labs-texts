Virtual Knot Groups
Heather A. Dye, Aaron Kaestner

Abstract
. For a knot diagram K K , the classical knot group π 1 ​ ( K ) \pi_{1}(K) is a free group modulo relations determined by Wirtinger-type relations on the classical crossings. The classical knot group is invariant under the Reidemeister moves. In this paper, we define a set of quotient groups associated to a knot diagram K K . These quotient groups are invariant under the Reidemeister moves and the set includes the extended knot groups defined by Boden et al and Silver and Williams.

The Gauss code of a knot diagram records the order of the crossings.
To find the Gauss code of the diagram, we select a base point and traverse the knot in the direction of orientation and record the label of each crossing as it is traversed. In figure
1
, the Gauss code is

abcacb.

The
parity of a classical crossing
in the knot is determined by whether there are an even or odd number of labels between the two occurences of the crossing label in the Gauss code
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
. The crossing has
even parity
if there is an even number of terms between the two labels. Otherwise, the crossing has
odd parity
. In figure
1
, the crossing
a
has even parity and the crossings
b
and
c
are oddly intersticed and have odd parity.

In a diagram with no virtual crossings, all classical crossings have even parity.
The parity of a crossing is unchanged by Reidemeister moves and virtual Reidemeister moves that do not act on that crossing. Only Reidemeister I and II moves introduce or remove new classical crossings. A Reidemeister I move introduces a single crossing with even parity. The Reidemeister II move introduces either two odd crossings or two even crossings with opposite signs. The Reidemeister III move does not change the parity of any crossing in the diagram.

For more information about parity, refer to works on parity by Manturov et al
[
6
]
.

1.1.
Knot Groups

In virtual knot theory, researchers have considered a variety of different groups associated with virtual knots
[
1
]
. These groups are quotients of a free group obtained by associating a generator with each arc or semi-arc of diagram and a set of Wirtinger-type relations based on the crossings.

In the paper,
Virtual knot groups and almost classical knots

[

, the virtual knot group,

, is defined using this method. Positive crossings (as labeled in figure

relations:
z=y^{-1}xsys^{-1}
and

. Virtual crossings define the relations

a=qyq^{-1}
and
z=qxq^{-1}

The quandle group,
QG(K)
, is obtained by letting
s=1

[
8
]
. If
q=s
, the welded group,
WG(K)
, (defined by Fenn and Rourke
[

EG(K)

[

is obtained by letting
q=1

s=q=1
.

Previously, the authors of this paper incorporated parity information in the knot group structure in
Virtual Parity Alexander Polynomial

[
3
]
. This resulted in a knot group where even positive crossings determined the relations
z=y^{-1}xsys^{-1}
and
a=sys^{-1}
. The odd crossings give rise to the relations

a=\theta y\theta^{-1}
and
z=\theta x\theta^{-1}
. The virtual crossings define the relations

a=qyq^{-1}
and
z=qxq^{-1}
. This quotient group contains parity information, but at the cost of the information about the sign of the odd crossings.

In section
2
, we define a set of quotient groups associated to a virtual knot. In section
3
, we discuss the structure of this set of knot groups and its relationship to previously studied knot groups. In section
4
, we use this structure to determine if signed parity information can be incorporated into the group structure.

2.
Knot groups defined via elements of
Aut(F_{2n+j})

Given a virtual knot diagram
K
, we construct two different quotients of the free group
F_{2n+j}
that are invariant under the oriented Reidemeister moves and the detour move.

Let
n
be the number of classical crossings in
K
and choose
j\geq 0
. Then
K

has
2n
semi-arcs that begin and end at classical crossings. Each semi-arc is associated with one of the generators
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{2n}
of
F_{2n+j}
. Then choose two automorphisms of

F_{2n+j}
,
\phi
and
\theta
. We quotient
F_{2n+j}
by the commutator relations determined by
\theta
and
\phi
and relations determined by the set of classical crossings. In the second group structure, we let
n
equal the number of classical and virtual crossings. We then choose three automorphisms of the free group
F_{2n+j}
. We quotient
F_{2n+j}
by commutator relations defined by the automorphisms and relations determined by the classical and virtual crossings.

Within this set of quotient groups, we recover
the knot groups described in
[
1
]
. Within the set of quotient groups defined, we are able to obtain a variety of algebraic structures that have been motivated by knot diagrams and the Reidemeister moves.

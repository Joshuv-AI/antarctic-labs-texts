Robustness of the derivative expansion in Asymptotic Safety
Alessio Baldazzi, Kevin Falls, Yannick Kluth, Benjamin Knorr

Abstract
We analyse the renormalisation group flow of quantum gravity at sixth order in the derivative expansion within the background field approximation. Non-linear field redefinitions are used to ensure that only essential couplings flow. Working within the universality class of General Relativity, with a vanishing cosmological constant, redundant couplings are fixed to their values at the Gaussian fixed point. This reduces the theory space to two dynamical essential couplings given by Newton’s and the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling. Furthermore, it implements the condition that no extra degrees of freedom are present beyond those of General Relativity, in contrast to higher derivative theories and derivative expansions in a conventional renormalisation scheme. We find a unique ultraviolet fixed point with a single relevant direction and analyse the phase diagram of the theory. Our results suggest resilience of the gravitational Reuter fixed point under the inclusion of higher order curvature invariants and show several signs of near-perturbativity. The regulator dependence of our results is investigated in detail and shows that qualitative and quantitative features are robust to a large extent.



I
Introduction

Finding a quantum theory of gravity is one of the most fascinating open problems in theoretical physics. Such a theory is supposed to shine light on fundamental questions about the origin of the universe and the micro-structure of black holes.
However, pure Einstein gravity is well-known to be perturbatively non-renormalisable due to Newton’s coupling having negative mass dimension. As a result, perturbative renormalisability fails from two loops onwards as signalled by the presence of the Goroff-Sagnotti counterterm
Goroff and Sagnotti 1985
;
Goroff and Sagnotti 1986
;
van de Ven 1992
. This indicates that pertubative Einstein quantum gravity has to include an infinite number of parameters, rendering the theory non-predictive. Below the Planck scale, predictivity can be regained by treating gravity as an
EFT
(
EFT
)
Donoghue 1994
. In this regime, higher order couplings are suppressed by powers of the Planck mass and can be neglected. The remaining finite number of couplings can be fixed by observations, at least in principle
Donoghue 1994
. However, this approach breaks down at the Planck scale and requires an
UV
(
UV
) completion.

The Asymptotic Safety scenario has emerged as one of the key contenders for a viable
UV
completion of quantum gravity
Weinberg 1976
;
Weinberg 1980
;
Parisi 1977
. It is based entirely on standard
QFT
(
QFT
), and suggests that quantum gravity becomes scale-invariant at short distances. This can be realised by an interacting fixed point of the
RG
(
RG
) flow. Evidence for the existence of such a fixed point has accumulated, both in pure gravity, where it is known as the Reuter fixed point
Reuter 1998
, and when coupled to matter, see
e.g.
the collection of book chapters
Knorr et al. 2022
;
Eichhorn and Schiffer 2022
;
Morris and Stulga 2023
;
Martini et al. 2022
;
Wetterich 2022
;
Platania 2023
;
Saueressig 2023
;
Pawlowski and Reichert 2023
for an up-to-date overview of the field. For some recent work in the field see
e.g.

Martini et al. 2023
;
Bonanno et al. 2023a
;
Bonanno et al. 2023b
;
D’Angelo 2023
;
Pawlowski and Tränkle 2023
;
Eichhorn and Ray 2023
;
Bonanno et al. 2023c
;

;
Saueressig and Wang 2023
;
Kawai and Ohta 2023
;
Knorr 2023
;
de Brito et al. 2023b
;
de Brito et al. 2023c
.

Since Asymptotic Safety is a non-perturbative phenomenon, small coupling expansions cannot be relied on and a mathematical proof for the existence of a fixed point seems out of reach for the moment. Nevertheless, there are strong indications that Asymptotic Safety is realised in a near-perturbative regime: it is as non-perturbative as it needs to be, but as perturbative as it can be
Falls et al. 2013
;
Falls et al. 2016
;
Eichhorn et al. 2018
;
Eichhorn et al. 2019
. This gives some evidence that central aspects of the theory are already well-captured by current approximations.

In this work, we provide more evidence for this observation. Specifically, we investigate Asymptotic Safety in a derivative expansion up to sixth order on a general background, for the first time considering a complete basis of operators at this order. This goes significantly beyond previous work, and breaks a barrier in terms of technical feasibility. Previous computations were either limited to fourth order in derivatives
Falls et al. 2020
;
Knorr 2021
;
Sen et al. 2022
, or to selecting very specific backgrounds. Consequently, this lead to either an identification of operators with an equal number of derivatives, or to a straight neglect of certain operators
Codello et al. 2009
;
Benedetti et al. 2010
;
Falls et al. 2016
;
Gies et al. 2016
;
Falls et al. 2018
;
Kluth and Litim 2023
;
Kluth and Litim 2022
.

In our endeavour, we rely on the freedom to perform field redefinitions of the metric to fix all inessential couplings at all scales. This follows recent technical progress in the field
Pawlowski 2007
;
Baldazzi et al. 2022a
;
Baldazzi and Falls 2021
;
Knorr 2022a
. Apart from technical simplifications, the resulting clear distinction between essential and inessential couplings is beneficial when counting the number of free parameters in the theory. Moreover, it imposes that no extra degrees of freedom are present apart from those included in
GR
(
GR

;
Platania and Wetterich 2020
;
Platania 2022
.

II
Essential renormalisation group for gravity

In this section, we discuss some basics of implementing field redefinitions in non-perturbative
RG
flows as well as our setup and technical choices.

II.1
Generalised flow equations

In order to study the non-perturbative renormalisability of quantum gravity, we employ the
FRG
(
FRG
)
Wetterich 1993
;
Morris 1994
;
Ellwanger 1994
.
It realises Wilson’s formulation of the
RG
whereby fluctuations are integrated out with respect to a fiducial momentum scale
k
.
For every scale
k
, this gives rise to an
EAA
(
EAA
)
\Gamma_{k}[\Phi]
that describes physics at the scale
k
, where modes with momentum
p^{2}>k^{2}
have been integrated out.
In the generalised formulation
Pawlowski 2007
;
Wegner 1974
;
Baldazzi et al. 2022a
, we allow for the freedom to change the description of physics at different scales
k
by means of local field redefinitions.
Thus, the dependence on
k
originates from the Wilsonian
IR
(
IR
) cutoff and from the
k
-dependent choice of field variables. Explicitly, the
EAA
can be defined by the integro-differential equation

e^{-\Gamma_{k}[\Phi]}=\int[D\chi]e^{-S[\chi]+(\hat{\Phi}_{k}[\chi]-\Phi)^{A}\frac{\delta}{\delta\Phi^{A}}\Gamma[\Phi]-\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\Phi}_{k}[\chi]-\Phi)^{A}[\mathcal{R}]_{AB}(\hat{\Phi}_{k}[\chi]-\Phi)^{B}}\,.

(1)

We use DeWitt notation with a repeated index implying both a sum over field indices and an integration over spacetime.
The argument of the
EAA
is the expectation value of the microscopic field
\Phi=\langle\hat{\Phi}_{k}\rangle_{k,\Phi}
. Exploiting the freedom to choose the microscopic field variables
\hat{\Phi}_{k}[\chi]
as a function of
k
allows for general non-linear local field redefinitions. These enter the flow equation for the
EAA
via the
RG
kernel
\Psi_{k}
, which is defined as the expectation value of the
k
-derivative of the microscopic field,

\Psi_{k}=\langle\partial_{t}\hat{\Phi}_{k}\rangle_{k,\Phi}\,,

(2)

where
t=\log(k/k_{0})
is the
RG
time and
k_{0}
an arbitrary reference scale.

The
EAA
obeys the exact functional flow equation
Pawlowski 2007

\left(\partial_{t}+\Psi^{A}\frac{\delta}{\delta\Phi^{A}}\right)\Gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{[A]}\left[\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right]^{AB}\left(\partial_{t}\delta^{C}_{\ B}+2\frac{\delta\Psi^{C}}{\delta\Phi^{B}}\right)\left[\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]_{CA}\,,

(3)

where
[A]
takes values
1
or
-1
depending on whether the field corresponding to the index
A
is commuting or anti-commuting.
The flow equation depends on the
IR
cutoff
\mathcal{R}_{k}
, which implements a gradual integration of fluctuations in the path integral, and the
RG
kernel
\Psi_{k}
, which parameterises field redefinitions.

In the case of pure gravity, the fields
\Phi^{A}
include both the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
and the Faddeev-Popov ghosts
c_{\mu}
and
\bar{c}_{\mu}
. Furthermore, the
EAA
depends on a background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
introduced both to fix the gauge and to employ a covariant regularisation. The total metric is split into the background plus a fluctuation field
h_{\mu\nu}
,

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(4)

In this paper, we use the background field approximation, where
\Gamma_{k}[\Phi]
is approximated by a diffeomorphism invariant functional
\bar{\Gamma}_{k}[g]
in addition to gauge fixing and ghost terms,

\Gamma_{k}=\bar{\Gamma}_{k}[g]+S_{\textrm{gf}}[g;\bar{g}]+S_{\textrm{gh}}[g,c,\bar{c};\bar{g}]\,.

(5)

To close the background field approximation, we identify the full metric
g_{\mu\nu}
with the background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
by setting
h_{\mu\nu}=0
in (
3
).
One then obtains the
\beta
-functions for the couplings in
\bar{\Gamma}_{k}[g]
which multiply diffeomorphism invariant operators.
Importantly, as we indicate below, the gauge fixing and ghost terms in the action depend on
k
only through Newton’s coupling
G_{N}=G_{N}(k)
. This reflects that we study the universality class with the same degrees of freedom as
GR
.

II.2
Minimal Essential Scheme at sixth order

In this paper, we implement the
MES
(
MES
) for gravity
Baldazzi et al. 2022a
;
Baldazzi and Falls 2021
, which consists of putting to zero all terms that are proportional to the vacuum Einstein equations,
R_{\mu\nu}=0
, apart from the Einstein-Hilbert term.
This can be achieved within a consistent approximation where the flow equation is expanded in derivatives with an appropriate choice of the
RG
kernel
\Psi_{k}
. In particular, if we include terms with up to
n
derivatives of the metric in the action, we include terms with
(n-2)
derivatives in the
RG
kernel. Previously, this scheme has been carried out to order
n=4
for both pure gravity
Baldazzi and Falls 2021
and gravity coupled to a shift-symmetric scalar field
Knorr 2022a
. We extend this to the sixth order (
n=6
), whence the
EAA
reads

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{k}=\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{g}\bigg[\,\frac{\rho}{8\pi}\,-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}R+\sigma_{\mathfrak{E}}\mathfrak{E}

(6)

\displaystyle+G_{C^{3}}C^{\rho\sigma}_{\phantom{\rho\sigma}\mu\nu}C^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\alpha\beta}C^{\alpha\beta}_{\phantom{\alpha\beta}\rho\sigma}\bigg]\,.

Here,
\rho=\Lambda/G_{N}
gives the vacuum energy of the field,
\mathfrak{E}=R^{2}-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}R^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
is the topological Gauss-Bonnet term, and
C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}
is the Weyl tensor. In addition to Newton’s coupling
G_{N}
and the topological Gauss-Bonnet coupling
\sigma_{\mathfrak{E}}
, the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling
G_{C^{3}}

Goroff and Sagnotti 1985
;
Goroff and Sagnotti 1986
enters as the second dynamical essential coupling. All other couplings to operators with up to six derivatives are set to zero.

An important additional element of the
MES
for gravity is the treatment of the cosmological constant
Baldazzi and Falls 2021
. The scheme specifies that
\rho
is fixed to be proportional to the fourth power of the cutoff scale
k
. The constant of proportionality is fixed at the Gaussian fixed point and depends on the regulator. This imposes that the physical cosmological constant vanishes,
i.e.
when
k
goes to zero. Moreover, we note that this condition also dictates that the dimensionless product
G_{N}\Lambda
is given by
G_{N}\Lambda=\tilde{\rho}\,k^{4}G_{N}^{2}
, where the proportionality constant
\tilde{\rho}
depends on the regularisation. The vacuum energy has also been fixed in an alternative fashion in
Kawai and Ohta 2023
which involves rescaling the metric and modifying the regulator.
We also note that using certain gauges and parameterisation of the metric the cosmological constant can be removed from the trace part flow equation
Ohta et al. 2015
;
Ohta et al. 2016
;
Falls 2015
. Furthermore in unimodular gravity the Newton’s constant is essential and the cosmological constant appears to play no role
Benedetti 2016
.
For a more general discussion on the redundant nature of the cosmological constant see
Hamber and Toriumi 2013
.

The
MES
for gravity fixes the inessential couplings to zero as they are identified at the Gaussian fixed point, defined by taking each coupling
G_{N}
,
\rho
and
G_{C^{3}}
to zero. Away from the Gaussian fixed point we continue to fix the inessential couplings which are those conjugate to redundant operators that are given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}[\Xi_{g}]&:=\,\Xi_{g}^{A}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{A}}\bar{\Gamma}_{k}\\
&\qquad-\left[\left(\bar{\Gamma}_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)^{-1}\right]^{AB}\frac{\delta\Xi^{C}}{\delta g^{B}}\left[\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]_{CA}\,,\end{split}

(7)

where
\Xi_{g}
are symmetric covariant tensors composed of the metric and its derivatives.

Maintaining the form of the action (

\displaystyle+\gamma_{R^{2}}\,R^{2}\,g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\gamma_{S^{2}}\left(S_{\rho\sigma}S^{\rho\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}+(R-4\Lambda)S_{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{C^{2}}\left(C_{\rho\sigma\alpha\beta}C^{\rho\sigma\alpha\beta}g_{\mu\nu}-8\Lambda S_{\mu\nu}\right)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{SSTL}\left(S_{\mu\rho}S^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\,g_{\mu\nu}S^{\rho\sigma}S_{\rho\sigma}\right)

The explicit dependence on the cosmological constant

\begin{split}\Psi_{\mu\nu}^{g}\frac{\delta\bar{\Gamma}_{k}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}=&\,\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\times\\

(10)

From this expression, we can see explicitly that we can choose the

II.3
Symmetries of the RG kernel

The
RG
kernel as defined in (
9
) includes terms that represent symmetries of the underlying action. Due to this fact, they do not lead to contributions to the left-hand side of the essential
RG
equation (
3
), as seen in (
10
). One example for such terms is
\gamma_{DDR}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}R
. Since

\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}R\,,

(11)

with
\xi_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}R/2
, this term is a diffeomorphism and does not contribute to the left-hand side of (
3
). However, it does lead to a non-vanishing contribution on the right-hand side. Moreover, its contribution to the left-hand side of (
3
) only vanishes within the background field approximation. If non-diffeomorphism invariant terms are included in the action, there is also a non-trivial contribution to the left-hand side. We conclude that the corresponding tree-level redundant operator is outside of our approximation. Therefore, we set the term
\gamma_{DDR}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}R
to zero in our ansatz for
\Psi_{k}
. The same reasoning applies to other diffeomorphism invariant contributions to the
RG
kernel that would be encountered at higher orders of the derivative expansion.

Another example for a term of the
RG
kernel that does not contribute to the left-hand side of (
3
) is

\gamma_{S^{2}}\left(S_{\rho\sigma}S^{\rho\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}+(R-4\Lambda)S_{\mu\nu}\right)\,.

(12)

In contrast to
\gamma_{DDR}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}R
, it is not a diffeomorphism term, but rather an example of another class of symmetries
DeWitt 2003
.
Any contribution to the
RG
kernel of the form

\Psi^{\text{sym}}_{\mu\nu}(x)=\int\text{d}^{4}y\,\sqrt{g}\,T_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(x,y)\,\frac{\delta\Gamma}{\delta g_{\rho\sigma}(y)}

(13)

is a symmetry, provided that the two-point function
T_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(x,y)
satisfies
T_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(x,y)=-T_{\rho\sigma,\mu\nu}(y,x)
. Note that such contributions are not specific to gravity, but also arise in other theories,
e.g.
, in scalar field theories. In the case of (
12
), we have

T_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(x,y)=\delta(x-y)\left[g_{\mu\nu}(x)S_{\rho\sigma}(y)-S_{\mu\nu}(x)g_{\rho\sigma}(y)\right]\,,

(14)

up to proportionality factors. Similarly to diffeomorphisms, the contribution of (
12
) vanishes on the left-hand side of (
3
) within the background field approximation, while it leads to a non-vanishing contribution on the right-hand side.
However, such contributions on the right-hand side are proportional to the equations of motion of the
EAA
for any term of the form (
13
). This can be shown in general by noting that the contribution is proportional to the field derivative of the
RG
kernel. To see this, we write the general form of (
13
) in DeWitt notation as

\Psi^{\text{sym}\,C}=T^{CD}\Gamma^{(1)}_{D}\,,\,\,\,\,T^{CD}=-T^{CD}\,.

(15)

Using that
T^{CD}
is an anti-symmetric two-point function while the regulator and propagator are symmetric, it can be shown that

\mathcal{G}^{AB}\frac{\delta\Psi^{\text{sym}\,C}}{\delta\Phi^{B}}\left[\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]_{CA}=\mathcal{G}^{AB}\frac{\delta T^{CD}}{\delta\Phi^{B}}\left[\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]_{CA}\Gamma^{(1)}_{D}\,.

(16)

In addition to this observation, we note that in the absence of a regulator, redundant operators of the effective action only have the tree-level term, and hence the redundant operator associated with (
13
) vanishes, but we expect the expectation values of local operators to be non-local. As a result, non-local terms in
\Psi
can arise from local field redefinitions.

II.4
Gauge Fixing and Regularisation

For practical computations utilising the
FRG
in gravity, we require to fix the gauge and implement a regulator. In the
MES
, we can find convenient choices for both by only considering the Einstein-Hilbert part of the action. This is due to the fact that, within the
MES
, the propagator on a conformally flat spacetime coincides with that derived from
GR
.
Hence, we implement a standard background field harmonic gauge of the form

S_{\textrm{gf}}[g;\bar{g}]=\frac{1}{2}\int\text{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\chi_{\mu}[h,\bar{g}]\chi^{\mu}[h,\bar{g}]\,,

(17)

with

\chi_{\mu}[h;\bar{g}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{16\pi G_{N}}}\left[\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}h^{\nu}_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}h\right]\,.

(18)

Bars denote that respective quantities are constructed from the background metric
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
.
The resulting Hessian including the Einstein-Hilbert term together with the gauge fixing is given by

\delta^{2}\left(S_{\text{EH}}+S_{\text{gf}}\right)=\frac{1}{32\pi G_{N}}\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,h_{\mu\nu}\bar{\Delta}^{\phantom{\text{EH}}\mu\nu}_{\text{EH}\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}h^{\rho\sigma}\,.

(19)

The gauge fixing (
17
) together with (
18
) leads to a particularly simple form for the Einstein-Hilbert Laplacian
\bar{\Delta}_{\text{EH}}
,

\begin{split}\bar{\Delta}^{\phantom{\text{EH}}\mu\nu}_{\text{EH}\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}&=\,\left[-\bar{\nabla}^{2}-2\Lambda\right]\bar{K}^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\\
&\quad+\left[-2\,\bar{C}^{\mu\phantom{\rho}\nu}_{\phantom{\mu}\rho\phantom{\nu}\sigma}+\frac{2}{3}\,\bar{R}\,\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\right]\,,\end{split}

(20)

where the matrix
\bar{K}^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}
is given by

\bar{K}^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}=\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}-\bar{\Pi}^{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\,,

(21)

and
\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}}
and
\bar{\Pi}^{\text{Tr}}
denote the projectors on the traceless and trace modes of the graviton, respectively,

\begin{split}\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}&=\,\delta^{\mu}_{\ (\rho}\delta^{\nu}_{\ \sigma)}-\frac{1}{4}\,\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{g}_{\rho\sigma}\,,\\
\bar{\Pi}^{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}&=\,\frac{1}{4}\,\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{g}_{\rho\sigma}\,.\end{split}

(22)

Other choices for the gauge fixing term typically lead to non-minimal, or higher order differential operators in (
20
). Both are avoided in this gauge and in the
MES
.

The gauge fixing (
17
) is accompanied by the usual Faddeev-Popov ghosts in the path integral. Their contribution reads

S_{\textrm{gh}}=\int\text{d}^{4}x\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,\bar{c}_{\mu}\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}c_{\nu}\,,

(23)

where
c_{\mu}
and
\bar{c}_{\mu}
are complex Grassmann fields, and the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}
is given by

\mathcal{M}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\delta\chi^{\mu}[\bar{g},h[\xi]]}{\delta\xi_{\nu}}\,.

(24)

Within the background field approximation, it suffices to set
h_{\mu\nu}=0
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mu\nu}
. This gives rise to

\bar{\mathcal{M}}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{16\pi G_{N}}}\left(\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\bar{\nabla}^{2}+\bar{R}^{\mu\nu}\right)\,.

(25)

Next, we choose a regulator for the graviton. Motivated by the form of (
20
), we pick

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}^{g\,\mu\nu}_{k\,\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}&=\,R_{k}^{g}\left(\bar{\Delta}\right)\bar{K}^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\\
&=\,R_{k}^{g}\left(\bar{\Delta}\right)\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}-R_{k}^{g}\left(-\bar{\nabla}^{2}\right)\bar{\Pi}^{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{Tr}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\,.\end{split}

(26)

The Laplacian
\bar{\Delta}
contains a non-trivial endomorphism,

\begin{split}\bar{\Delta}^{\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}&=\,-\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\bar{\nabla}^{2}\\
&\quad+\beta\bigg[-2\,\bar{C}^{(\mu\phantom{\rho}\nu)}_{\phantom{(\mu}\rho\phantom{\nu)}\sigma}+\frac{2}{3}\,\bar{R}\,\bar{\Pi}^{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}_{\phantom{\text{TL}\,\mu\nu}\rho\sigma}\bigg]\,,\end{split}

(27)

that only contributes to the traceless, but not the trace mode of the graviton. The parameter
\beta
controls the magnitude of the endomorphism. For
\beta=0
it vanishes, and we recover a type I cutoff. For
\beta=1
, the Laplacian
\bar{\Delta}
becomes equal to the Laplacian of the Einstein-Hilbert action, see (
19
). We also refer to this choice as the type II cutoff. Most of our analysis below is focused on
\beta=1
, however, we also consider the dependence of our results on
\beta
below.

We derive the flow equation for general shape functions
R_{k}^{g}(z)
and arbitrary endomorphism parameters
\beta
. To solve the threshold functions in the resulting
\beta
-functions explicitly, we mostly use the Litim cutoff
Litim 2001
with a prefactor
\alpha
,

R_{k}^{g}(z)=\frac{\alpha k^{2}}{32\pi G_{N}}\left(1-\frac{z}{k^{2}}\right)\theta\left(1-\frac{z}{k^{2}}\right)\,.

(28)

While analytical expressions for the threshold functions are obtained for general
\alpha
, they take a particularly simple form for
\alpha=1
.

A second shape function that still allows obtaining analytical results for all threshold functions is the “square” of the Litim cutoff,

R_{k}^{g}(z)=\frac{\alpha k^{2}}{32\pi G_{N}}\left(1-\frac{z}{k^{2}}\right)^{2}\theta\left(1-\frac{z}{k^{2}}\right)\,,

(29)

which we also refer to as Litim-2 cutoff. This additional shape function allows us to study some of the dependence of our results on the shape function.

III.2
RG Flow and IR Behaviour

To visualise the flow of asymptotically safe trajectories and their
IR
properties,
Figure 1
shows the phase diagram within the
MES
for
\alpha=\beta=1
and Litim cutoff.
Since we require Newton’s coupling to be small and positive at low energies, any physical
RG
trajectory must reach the vicinity of the Gaussian fixed point with a positive Newton coupling in the
IR
. How couplings arrive at the Gaussian fixed point from the
UV
can be read off from
Figure 1
. Among these trajectories that have an
IR
limit corresponding to perturbative

, there are two possibilities for their

The first and generic option consists of trajectories for which the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling diverges at high energies. These trajectories begin in the UV at
g_{N}=0
and
g_{C^{3}}=\pm\infty
and therefore do not correspond to asymptotically safe theories. Based on scaling arguments
Weinberg 1980
, one expects that observables such as scattering amplitudes have a divergent high energy behaviour.

Finite values for the couplings are only reached on the separatrix between the regions where the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling grows towards
+\infty
and
-\infty
. This is the asymptotically safe trajectory which originates from the fixed point (
37
) in the
UV
, and it is the only trajectory that has a well-defined behaviour at all energy scales and connects to the region of perturbative quantum
GR
at low energy scales. There are no free parameters that need to be fixed and no second non-trivial fixed point is available as a
UV
completion for trajectories that are connected to the Gaussian fixed point.

The

fixed point. The irrelevant deformation spans separatrices which split the phase diagram into two parts:
all trajectories with smaller Newton coupling than these separatrices reach the Gaussian fixed point in the
IR
, while trajectories with larger Newton coupling do not. The latter eventually end up in a divergence of the
RG

Such divergences arise when denominators of the
\beta
-functions develop a zero. The zeros of those denominators form hypersurfaces in coupling space at which the
\beta
-functions diverge. In
Figure 1
we see that this happens whenever Newton’s coupling becomes to large, and we attribute these to a breakdown of our approximation.

The asymptotically safe trajectory that connects the non-trivial fixed point in the
UV
with the Gaussian fixed point in the
IR
leads to a non-trivial prediction at low energies. For small

, we can solve the

g_{C^{3}}=\left[G_{N}k^{2}\right]\left(A-\frac{43}{645120\pi^{3}}\ln G_{N}k^{2}\right)\,.

(42)

The prefactor of the logarithm is identical for all trajectories reaching the Gaussian fixed point. By contrast, the Wilson coefficient
A
depends on the trajectory. For the asymptotically safe trajectory, the value of
A
is predicted by the fixed point. Numerically, we find

A=-3.988\cdot 10^{-6}\,,

(43)

once again for the choice
\alpha=\beta=1
and the Litim shape function.

In principle, the
RG
running in the
IR
can be compared to perturbative
\beta
-functions. On the perturbative side, additional attention must be paid to the fact that the Gauss-Bonnet term acts as an evanescent operator which leads to a non-trivial relation between the perturbative two-loop divergence and its
RG
running
Bern et al. 2015
;
Bern et al. 2017
. Taking this into account, the perturbative
\beta
-function of the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling is given by

\partial_{t}g_{C^{3}}=2g_{C^{3}}+\frac{1}{7680\pi^{3}}\,g_{N}+\dots\,.

(44)

Our result using the type II cutoff is

\partial_{t}g_{C^{3}}=2g_{C^{3}}-\frac{43}{322560\pi^{3}}\,g_{N}+\dots\,.

(45)

Although the two results for the quantum corrections do not agree, it should be noted that beyond the one-loop order, a comparison with perturbative results requires approximation schemes other than a derivative expansion in the
FRG
. Indeed, in
Papenbrock and Wetterich 1995
;
Baldazzi et al. 2021a
it is shown that two-loop results for the quartic coupling of an
O(N)
symmetric scalar field can be obtained with an infinite number of terms in the derivative expansion. Thus, it is reasonable to think that also the case of gravity requires an infinite expansion in derivatives to reproduce the perturbative result (
44
).

In the intermediate range,

Compared to the relevant critical exponent

Let us note that an analysis of the

IV
Discussion

In this work, we have provided further evidence for the existence of a non-trivial fixed point in quantum gravity. Employing the
FRG
, we have performed a consistent derivative expansion to sixth order on general backgrounds. This is the first complete work at this order in four dimensions,
4
4

4

For a recent discussion of one-loop
\beta
-functions of cubic gravity in its critical dimension,
d=6
, see
Knorr 2022b
.
and marks a considerable step forward compared to previous approaches that were limited to fourth order of the derivative expansion, or the use of simpler background geometries. Moreover, we have used recent insights on the use of field redefinitions in the
FRG
by implementing the
MES
. In this scheme, only essential couplings are retained in the action, while redundant couplings are fixed at the expense of non-trivial field redefinitions. In this way, it is possible to eliminate the inessential couplings from the flow equations, which then depends only on essential couplings. Two main advantages of this approach are (i) a simpler structure for the propagator, which makes computations to this order of the derivative expansion feasible, and (ii) focusing on the essential couplings of the theory when searching for non-trivial fixed points and evaluating their critical exponents. The latter point is important for the Asymptotic Safety scenario since redundant couplings do not affect physical observables, and consequently they should not be taken into account when counting the number of free parameters of the theory. Furthermore, the assumptions of the
MES
, which identifies the inessential couplings at the free fixed point, restrict the theory space to the subspace where those couplings remain inessential. Namely, we focus on the universality classes connected to
GR
, and characterized by the same redundant operator structure. The simple form of the propagator then suggests that fixed points found in the
MES
have the same propagating degrees of freedom as
GR
, as also indicated by recent results using momentum-dependent field redefinitions
Knorr 2023
. More technically, on any conformally flat background, the propagator has the same pole structure as
GR
.

Although a non-trivial gravitational fixed point has previously been identified at second and quartic orders in derivatives within the
MES

Baldazzi and Falls 2021
at sixth order a second dynamical essential coupling enters which could have strongly altered results obtained at lower orders. Nevertheless, as seen in
Figure 1
, the results of our analysis suggest that the fixed point survives the inclusion of terms at sixth order in derivatives, and moreover, that it is remarkably stable. Newton’s coupling and its critical exponent are essentially unaffected by the extension, even though a new essential coupling enters the system. Between fourth and sixth order of the derivative expansion, the relevant critical exponents receives corrections of only
\approx 0.1\%
, taking a value of
\theta_{1}=2.225
at sixth order, see
Table 1
. Furthermore, the Goroff-Sagnotti term stays irrelevant at the fixed point, which confirms previous observations in the standard scheme
Gies et al. 2016
. Therefore, its Wilson coefficient is a prediction of the theory, see (
42
) and (
43
). The fact that the sixth order still gives rise to a unique non-trivial fixed point with just one relevant direction points towards an Asymptotic Safety scenario of pure quantum gravity that features no free parameters. Compared to the standard scheme which usually gives rise to three to four relevant directions
Codello et al. 2009
;
Benedetti et al. 2010
;
Falls et al. 2016
;
Denz et al. 2018
;
Falls et al. 2018
;
Kluth and Litim 2023
;
Falls et al. 2020
;
Sen et al. 2022
, we see that identifying redundant couplings is key to understand the predictivity of the fixed point within Asymptotic Safety.

We have also observed several features of near-perturbativity at the non-trivial fixed point. The clearest indication is the very small value of the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling at the fixed point,
g_{C^{3}}=4.5\cdot 10^{-7}
, see (
37
). Additionally, we have shown in (
39
) that the
RG
kernel which parameterises the field redefinitions takes small absolute values as well, with the sole exception of
\gamma_{g}
taking values of the order of one. Lastly, critical exponents receive minor corrections when compared to their canonical counterparts. All these indications line up with previous observations
Falls et al. 2013
;
Falls et al. 2016
;
Eichhorn et al. 2018
;
Eichhorn et al. 2019
, and suggest a near-perturbative behaviour of quantum gravity. It is as non-perturbative as necessary, but as perturbative as it gets.

This observation can lead to possible simplifications for future investigations: a possible way to compute higher order corrections is to keep all essential couplings in the action to a given order, while only retaining
\gamma_{g}
in the
RG
kernel. Remarkably, implementing this seemingly crude approximation, the results at sixth order in the derivative expansion are virtually unchanged, see (
41
). This is to be contrasted with the standard scheme, where such an approximation leads to a strongly non-canonical irrelevant critical exponent, see
Gies et al. 2016
and appendix
A
.

Finally, we have checked the dependence of our results on technical parameters. This serves as an important tool to estimate truncation errors that are present in applications of the
FRG
. Our analysis shows that our results are largely independent of such technical choices. We have tested this for two different shape functions and with two different technical parameters:
\alpha
, which enters as a prefactor in front of the shape function of the regulator (
28
), and
\beta
, which parameterises the endomorphism of the Laplacian (
27
). A sophisticated numerical analysis shows that qualitative properties are independent of technical parameters. As seen in
Figure 6
, a large area is found in which critical exponents plateau within which we find a point of minimum sensitivity with respect to both parameters, see (
46
) and (
47
). Outside of this area, stronger fluctuations are present. We expect that in this regime, our approximation scheme breaks down. Most importantly, the critical exponent of the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling is always irrelevant, and the relevant critical exponent takes mostly values of
\theta_{1}\approx 2.1\pm 0.2
. These results also hold for different shape functions. In this work, we compared the Litim cutoff and its “square”, and find qualitative agreement. This analysis also showed the special role played by the type II cutoff
(\beta=1)
. For this choice, we find the strongest indications of near-perturbativity. Except for
\gamma_{g}
, all
\gamma
-functions that parameterise the
RG
kernel develop a zero close to
\beta=1
, see
Figure 5
.
Figure 4
shows that this also applies to the fixed point value of the Goroff-Sagnotti coupling which develops a zero close to
\beta=1
as well.

It will be of great interest to apply our findings to future endeavours, including even higher orders in the derivative expansion. This might be particularly feasible due to the near-perturbativity of the fixed point which potentially allows for much simplified yet accurate approximations. Similarly, the application of this scheme to gravity-matter systems is intriguing. First steps have been made in
Knorr 2022a
. Observing the fixed point to persist the inclusion of matter would be a strong indication for a unitary and
UV
-complete theory of quantum gravity.
By studying gravity-matter systems it would also be interesting to compute the scaling dimension of relational observables
Baldazzi et al. 2022b
at the higher orders of the derivative expansion.
Last but not least, a critical open question is the extension of the
MES
to setups beyond the background field approximation. This can be explored within the fluctuation approach
Pawlowski and Reichert 2023
, within perturbation theory
Martini et al. 2022
or within a manifestly background independent approach such as
Falls 2021
.

Appendix B
The Topological Term

We have already mentioned in the main text that the fourth order of the derivative expansion features the topological Gauss-Bonnet term,

\mathfrak{E}=R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}R^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}+R^{2}\,,

(54)

whose spacetime integral is related to the Euler characteristic,

\chi=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int\text{d}^{d}x\sqrt{g}\,\mathfrak{E}\,.

(55)

Due to its topological nature,
\mathfrak{E}
is locally a total derivative. Hence, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling does not appear in any
\beta
-functions of the theory. A consequence of this is that the
\beta
-function of the Gauss-Bonnet (
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
) is independent of itself and receives only contributions from other couplings in the theory. Even though these other couplings can have a non-trivial fixed point, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling only stops running if its
\beta
-function vanishes due to non-trivial cancellations between the contributions of other couplings. However, we are not aware of any arguments why such a cancellation would take place.

If
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
does not vanish at the non-trivial fixed point, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling either runs to
+\infty
or
-\infty
. In
Knorr 2021
, it was argued that this leads to a suppression/enhancement of certain topologies in the path integral. Depending on the sign of
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
, the contribution of topologies with a given sign of the Euler characteristic is enhanced over the other.

We present our findings for
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
at the non-trivial fixed point in
Figure 7
in dependence on
\alpha
and
\beta
. To understand the convergence of the result for
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\ast}
, we show the results at fourth and sixth order in the derivative expansion. Comparing both orders, we conclude that the biggest stability for
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\ast}
is found for the type II cutoff,
i.e.

\beta=1
. This is in line with our findings in the main text, see
e.g.

Figure 5
. In this regime, we find a positive value for
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\ast}
. For
\alpha=\beta=1
we have

16\pi^{2}\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\ast}\approx 1.273\,.

(56)

This indicates that the Gauss-Bonnet coupling runs towards
+\infty
in the
UV
. In the Euclidean path integral, this means that the weight of topological configurations with positive Euler characteristic is suppressed, while topologies with negative Euler characteristics are enhanced. We also find parameter regions with vanishing or negative
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\ast}
, however, comparing fourth and sixth orders, we find that our approximation is less stable in these ranges. Thus, our results suggest a positive
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
at the non-trivial fixed point, in line with previous findings
Knorr 2021
;
Knorr 2022a
;
Knorr 2023
.

These findings can also be compared to the value of
\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}
at the Gaussian fixed point. There, we rediscover the known result

16\pi^{2}\beta_{\mathfrak{E}}^{\text{Gauss}}=\frac{53}{45}\approx 1.178\,.

(57)

Within the uncertainties of our computation, this is identical to (
56
) at the non-trivial fixed point. The topological
\beta

Baldazzi and Falls 2021

Abstract
. Variational inequality problems have extensive and important applications. In this paper, we used penalty method and the method of invariant set of descending flow to obtain the existence results for solutions of a variational inequality. In particular, we obtain the existence results for sign-changing solutions of variational inequalities for the first time based on the method of invariant set of descending flow. 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we will study the following variational inequality

\mbox{(VI)}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)&\\
\int_{\Omega}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nabla\left(v(x)-u(x)\right)\geqslant\int_{\Omega}p\left(x,u(x)\right)\left(v(x)-u(x)\right),$${\forall}$$v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)&\\
u(x),v(x)\leqslant\psi(x),x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega,\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is the obstacle,
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\psi|_{\partial\Omega}\geqslant 0
.

Variational inequalities (VIs) were initially introduced and studied by Stampacchia [7] in
1964. Since then, VIs have extensively been studied and applied to a large variety of applied
problems arising from structural analysis, economics, optimization, management science,
operations research and engineering sciences. Many results have been obtained for the existence of positive solutions or non-trivial solutions to VIs in the past three decades. For example, for the above variational inequality, M.Girardi, L.Mastroeni and M.Matzeu gave the existence result of its positive solutions. They proved in [6] the existence of non-negative solutions for variational inequality (VI) through the use of some estimates for the Mountain-Pass critical points obtained for the penalized equations associated with the (VI), and then proved the positivity of solution by a regularity result and the strong maximum principle. For more results on the existence of positive solutions or non-trivial solutions to VIs, one can refer to literature [1-6].

The main purpose of the present paper is to give some results for the existence of sign-changing solutions of the (VI). In the last two decades, people have extensively studied the existence of sign-changing solutions for various kinds of differential boundary value problems, see [9-13]. However, to the best of our knowledge, no one has studied the sign-changing solutions of (VIs). We need to point out that the study of the sign-changing solutions of VIs has extensive and important practical significance. Now, let’s look at a practical example of studying the sign-changing solution of a variational inequality. Let us suppose that we have a beam that is fixed at one end, and there is a support underneath it, which exerts a force on the other end. When the force is small, the beam simply bends, and the variational inequality describing the beam bending would have a positive solution. On the other hand, when the force is very large, the beam will have a complex bending shape, and the variational inequality describing the beam bending would have a sign-changing solution.

To show the existence of sign-changing solution to the variational inequality (VI), we will use the penalty method, and the method of invariant set of descending flow proposed by Sun Jingxian; see [8]. In the last two decades, people have extensively studied the existence of sign-changing solutions for various kinds of differential boundary value problems by using the method of invariant set of descending flow. This method has a simple and intuitive geometric meaning, and is especially suitable for proving the existence of the sign-changing solution.

2.
Sign-changing solutions for penalty problems

In this section, we construct a penalty problem
(E)_{\varepsilon}
related to (VI), and use the method of invariant set of descending flow to study the existence of sign-changing solutions for the penalty problem
(E)_{\varepsilon}
. During the proof process, we also give the existence results for positive and negative solutions for the problem
(E)_{\varepsilon}
.

First, we introduce the penalty problem related to the variational inequality
(\mbox{VI})
, that is, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mbox{(E)}_{\varepsilon}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)&\\
\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\left(u-\psi\right)^{+}v=\int_{\Omega}p\left(x,u(x)\right)v,$${\forall}$$v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\left(u-\psi\right)^{+}
denotes the positive part of the function
u-\psi
.

Let
Z_{\varepsilon}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with the new norm:

\|u\|_{\varepsilon}=\left(\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u^{2})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Evidently
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
Z_{\varepsilon}
are homeomorphic,
\|u\|
is equivalent to
\|u\|_{\varepsilon}
.
Related to the penalty problem
(E)_{\varepsilon}
, we consider the following functional:

I_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(u-\psi(x)\right)^{+}\right)^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}P(x,u)-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|u|_{2}^{2}.

(2.1)

Let us define the nonlinear operator
A_{\varepsilon}:X\rightarrow X
by
A_{\varepsilon}=K_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon}
, where
K_{\varepsilon}:C(\bar{\Omega})\rightarrow X
is the solution operator of the following boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u=v,&x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

and
F_{\varepsilon}:X\to C(\bar{\Omega})
is a nonlinear operator defined by

F_{\varepsilon}u=p(x,u)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}u-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(u-\psi\right)^{+},\forall u\in X.

Obviously, the gradient operator of
I_{\varepsilon}(u)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
is
I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)=u-A_{\varepsilon}u
for any
u\in X
.

Consider the initial value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{\,du}{\,dt}}=A_{\varepsilon}u-u,&\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in X\backslash K,\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
K=\{u\in X:u=A_{\varepsilon}u\}
. Since
A_{\varepsilon}
is Lipschitz continuous on
X
, the solution of (
2.2
) uniquely exists. Let
\left[0,\eta(u_{0})\right)
be the maximal right existence interval of the solution
u(t,u_{0})
, here
\eta(u_{0})\leqslant+\infty
.

It follows from [8] we have the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

The nonempty subset
M
of
X
is called a descending flow invariant set of (
2.2
) if

\{u(t,u_{0}):t\in\left[0,\eta(u_{0})\right)\}\subset M,\forall u_{0}\in M\backslash K.

We say that the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the
(PS)
condition, if for any sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset Z_{\varepsilon}
,
\{I_{\varepsilon}(u_{n})\}
is bounded, and
I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0

\{u_{n}\}
has at least one convergent subsequence in

.

By a usual way we can easily show the following Lemma 2.1.

Lemma 2.1
.

I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the
(PS)
condition on

for each

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda(u_{0})=\left\{u(t,u_{0}):t\in\left[0,\eta(u_{0})\right)\right\}

2.2
) emitting from the initial point

According to the theories of ordinary equation in Banach spaces, we see

This implies that

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u(t))\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t_{2}-t_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u(t))\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\,dI_{\varepsilon}(u(t))}{\,dt}\,dt

i_{\varepsilon}
from
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
onto
Z_{\varepsilon}
be the identity mapping. Obviously,
i_{\varepsilon}
is a homeomorphic mapping between
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
Z_{\varepsilon}
. Let
O=i_{\varepsilon}\left(B(0,\rho_{0})\right)
, where
B(0,\rho_{0})=\{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\|u\|<\rho_{0}\}
. Then
O
is an open set in
Z_{\varepsilon}
, and so an open set in
X
since
X\hookrightarrow Z_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover,
I_{\varepsilon}(u)\geqslant\alpha_{0}
for all
u\in\partial O
, where
\partial O
denotes the boundary of
O
in
Z_{\varepsilon}
. Let

G=\{u\in Y:\|u\|<r_{1}\},

obviously,
G\supset B(\theta,\rho_{0})\cap Y
. Let
G_{1}=i_{\varepsilon}(G)
, then
G_{1}
is a bounded open set in
Z_{\varepsilon}\cap Y
, and
G_{1}
is an open set of
X\cap Y
containing the origin. Take
\delta_{1}\in(0,\alpha_{0})
small enough, and let
D_{\varepsilon}
be the connected component of
I_{\varepsilon}^{\delta_{1}}:=\{u\in X:I_{\varepsilon}(u)<\delta_{1}\}
containing the origin point of
X
. Then
D_{\varepsilon}
is an invariant set of descending flow of (2.2), and so
\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon},-\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon}
are two nonempty invariant sets of descending flow of (2.2). It follows from (
2.9
) that
C_{P}(\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon})\neq P
. Thus,
\partial_{P}(\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon})\neq\emptyset
is a closed invariant set of descending flow of (2.2). By Lemma
2.5
we have

c_{+}=\inf\limits_{u\in\partial_{P}C_{P}\left(\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon}\right)}I_{\varepsilon}(u)\geqslant\inf\limits_{u\in\partial_{P}\left(\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon}\right)}I_{\varepsilon}(u).

It follows from Lemma
2.3
that there exists
u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\in\partial_{P}C_{P}(\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon})\cap K
such that
I_{\varepsilon}(u_{+}^{\varepsilon})=c_{+}
. Then
u_{+}^{\varepsilon}
is a positive solution of
(E)_{\varepsilon}
.

Similarly,
(E)_{\varepsilon}
has at least one negative solution
u_{-}^{\varepsilon}\in\partial_{-P}C_{-P}(-\mathring{P}\cap D_{\varepsilon})
.

Next we shall prove the existence of the sign-changing solution of
(E)_{\varepsilon}
. It follows from (
2.9
) that
C_{X}(D_{\varepsilon})\cap Y
is a bounded open subset of
Y
containing the origin of
Y
. According to Lemma
2.4
, there exists a connected component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\partial C_{X}(D_{\varepsilon})\cap Y
such that each one sided ray
l
through the origin of
Y
satisfies
l\cap\Sigma^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\Sigma
be the connected component of
\partial_{X}C_{X}(D_{\varepsilon})
containing
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Obviously,
C_{\Sigma}(\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma),C_{\Sigma}(-\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma)
) are two nonempty open subsets of
\Sigma
. By the connectedness of
\Sigma
we see that

\Lambda:=\Sigma\backslash\left(C_{\Sigma}(\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma)\cup C_{\Sigma}(-\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma)\right)\neq\emptyset.

Since
\Sigma
is an invariant set of descending flow of (
2.2
),
C_{\Sigma}(\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma)
and
C_{\Sigma}(-\mathring{P}\cap\Sigma)
are two complete invariant set of descending flow of (
2.2
) in
\Sigma
,
\Lambda
is a closed invariant set of descending flow of (
2.2
) in
\Sigma
. Moreover,

\tilde{c}:=\inf\limits_{u\in\Lambda}I_{\varepsilon}(u)\geqslant\inf\limits_{u\in\partial B(0,\rho_{0})}I_{\varepsilon}(u)>-\infty.

It follows from Lemma
2.3
that
\tilde{c}
is a critical value of
I_{\varepsilon}
, and
I_{\varepsilon}(\omega_{\varepsilon})=\tilde{c}
for some
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\Lambda\cap K
. Obviously,
\omega_{\varepsilon}\notin P\cup(-P)
and
\omega_{\varepsilon}\notin B(0,\rho_{0})
. Thus,
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is a sign-changing solution of
(E)_{\varepsilon}
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Sign-changing solutions of the variational inequality

In this section, in order to obtain the sign-changing solution of (VI), we take a sequence of positive numbers
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. By the method of section 2 we can obtain a sign-changing solution sequence
\{\omega_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
of the penalty problem
(E)_{\varepsilon_{n}}
. Then, in this section we can show that, up to a subsequence if necessary,
\omega_{\varepsilon_{n}}\rightarrow\omega
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
\omega\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\omega
is a sign-changing solution of (VI). For convenience, in this section, for each
\varepsilon_{n}>0
, the penalty equation
(E)_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is denoted as
(E)_{n}
, the corresponding functional is denoted as
I_{n}
, and the sign-changing solution
\omega_{\varepsilon_{n}}
is denoted as
\omega_{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\sigma>0
, such that for any
\varepsilon_{n}>0
, one has
0\leqslant I_{n}(\omega_{n})\leqslant\sigma
.

Proof.

According to the proof process in Lemma
2.6
, there must be a ray passing through the origin and passing through a point
u\in\Lambda
, and so,

I_{n}(\omega_{n})\leqslant\max\limits_{(t,u)\in[0,1]\times(B(0,r_{1})\cap Y)}I_{n}(tu).

Since
u\leqslant\psi
for any
u\in\bar{B}(0,r_{1})\cap Y
, one has

\int_{\Omega}\left((tu-\psi)^{+}\right)^{2}\,dx=0~~~~(t\in[0,1]).

Then,

I_{n}(\omega_{n})\leqslant\max\limits_{(t,u)\in[0,1]\times(B(0,r_{1})\cap Y)}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}P(x,tu(x))\,dx\right).

Since
[0,1]\times(\bar{B}(0,r_{1})\cap Y)
is compact, by (
1.3
), one has
0\leqslant I_{n}(\omega_{n})\leqslant\sigma
for some
\sigma>0
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\omega_{n}
be the sign-changing solution of problem
(E)_{n}
given in Lemma
2.6
. If
s<2
, then the solution sequence
\{\omega_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, that is, there is a positive constant
c
(
c
does not depend on
\varepsilon_{n}
), such that
\|\omega_{n}\|\leqslant c,n=1,2,\cdots
.

Proof.

By Lemma 3.1 we have

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}(x)}(s-\psi(x))^{+}ds\Big)dx\leqslant\sigma+\int_{\Omega}P(x,\omega_{n}(x))dx.

So, by (H
{}_{4})
,

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}(x)}(s-\psi(x))^{+}ds\Big)dx\leqslant\mbox{const}+\frac{1}{s+1}\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n}(x))\omega_{n}(x)dx.

Thus, as
\omega_{n}
is a solution of
(E)_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s+1}\big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\mbox{const}+\frac{1}{(s+1)\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}(\omega_{n}(x)-\psi(x))^{+}\omega_{n}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{\omega_{n}(x)}(s-\psi(x))^{+}ds\Big)dx.

Set
\Omega_{n}=\{x\in\Omega:\omega_{n}(x)\geqslant\psi(x)\}
we have

\displaystyle\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s+1}\big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\mbox{const}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\Big\{\frac{1}{(s+1)}\int_{\Omega_{n}}(\omega_{n}(x)-\psi(x))\omega_{n}(x)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{n}}(\omega_{n}(x)-\psi(x))^{2}ds\Big\}.

Since
s>1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\mbox{const}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega_{n}}(\omega_{n}(x)-\psi(x))\psi(x).

(3.1)

Taking
v=\psi
as a test function in
(E)_{n}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega_{n}}(\omega_{n}(x)-\psi(x))\psi(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\nabla\omega_{n}\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega_{n}}p(x,\omega_{n}(x))\psi(x).

(3.2)

By (H
{}_{2})
, (3.1) and (3.2) and the fact that
\psi\in L^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in(2,2^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{2}\leqslant\mbox{const}\left(1+\left(\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|^{2^{*}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2^{*}}}\right)

and by the continuous embedding of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
into
L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)
,
\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}\leqslant\mbox{const}(1+\|\omega_{n}\|^{s})
, so
\{\omega_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
since
s<2
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
\rho_{1}>0,~\alpha_{+}>0,\alpha_{-}>0
, such that for any
\varepsilon_{n}>0,~\|\omega_{n}\|\geqslant\rho_{1},~\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|\geqslant\alpha_{+},~\|\omega_{n}^{-}\|\geqslant\alpha_{-}
.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{n}
is the sign-changing solution of
(E)_{n}
, taking
\omega_{n}
as the test function, we get

\displaystyle\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|^{2}+C_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|^{s+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\mathcal{S}_{2}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+C_{\delta}\mathcal{S}_{s+1}\|\omega_{n}\|^{s+1}.

Note that
s+1>2
in the above equation, then there exists
\rho_{1}>0
, so that for any
n=1,2,\cdots
,

\|\omega_{n}\|\geqslant\rho_{1}.

Take
\omega_{n}^{+}
as the test function of
(E)_{n}
, then

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}\cdot\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n}^{+})\omega_{n}^{+}.

therefore,

\displaystyle\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}^{+}|\cdot\omega_{n}^{+}+C_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}^{+}|^{s}\cdot\omega_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}^{+}|^{2}+C_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}^{+}|^{s+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\mathcal{S}_{2}\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{2}+C_{\delta}\mathcal{S}_{s+1}\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{s+1}.

Since
s+1>2
, there exists
\alpha_{+}>0
, so that for any
\varepsilon_{n}>0
,

\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|\geqslant\alpha_{+}.

Similarly, we know that there exists
\alpha_{-}>0
, such that for any
\varepsilon_{n}>0
,

\|\omega_{n}^{-}\|\geqslant\alpha_{-}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that condition
{\rm{(}}{{\rm{H}}_{\rm{1}}}{\rm{)}}

\sim~
{\rm{(}}{{\rm{H}}_{\rm{5}}}{\rm{)}}
hold and
s<2
, then (VI) has at least one sign-changing solution.

Proof.

Let
\{\omega_{n}\}
be the solution of
(E)_{n}
given in Lemma
2.6
. According to Lemma
3.2
,
\{\omega_{n}\}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, so we may assume that on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\omega_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega,n\to\infty.

(3.3)

First, prove
\omega
is the solution of
(\mbox{VI})
. Combining Lemma
3.1
,
3.2
and (
1.3
), we can get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\|(\omega_{n}-\psi(x))^{+}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\|\omega_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}P(x,\omega_{n})\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\sigma+\delta\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|^{2}\,dx+C_{\delta}\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|^{s+1}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\sigma+\delta\mathcal{S}_{2}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+C_{\delta}\mathcal{S}_{s+1}\|\omega_{n}\|^{s+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bar{c},

that is
|(\omega_{n}-\psi(x))^{+}|_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{\bar{c}\varepsilon_{n}}
. Hence, when

\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
,

\left(\omega_{n}-\psi(x)\right)^{+}\to 0.

By (
3.3
), we can know on
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\to\left(\omega-\psi\right)^{+}\ \mbox{as}\ ~n\to\infty.

So that we deduce that
\left(\omega-\psi\right)^{+}=0
a.e. in
\Omega
, that is
\omega\leqslant\psi
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Now we claim
\{\omega_{n}\}
strongly converges to
\omega
as
n\to\infty.
Taking
\omega_{n}
as a test function in
(E)_{n}
we have

\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega_{n}.

(3.4)

While, taking
\omega
as a test function in
(E)_{n}
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}\cdot\nabla\omega+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega.

(3.5)

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
in the latter two relations and taking into account (
3.3
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega)\omega-\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\|\omega\|^{2}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega-\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\|\omega\|^{2}+\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}(\omega-\psi)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\omega\|^{2}\leqslant\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\|\omega_{n}\|^{2},

here we also use the weak l.s.c of the norm and the fact that
\omega\leqslant\psi
a.e. in
\Omega
. Hence,

\|\omega_{n}\|\to\|\omega\|\ \mbox{as}\ ~n\to\infty.

Since
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a separable Hilbert space, the claim is proved.

Now we are ready to show that
\omega
is a solution of (VI). Since
\omega_{n}
is the sign-changing solution of
(E)_{n}
, taking
v-\omega_{n}
, with
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
v\leqslant\psi
a.e.in
\Omega
, as a test function, we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}\nabla(v-\omega_{n})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}(v-\omega_{n})=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})(v-\omega_{n}).

Thanks to the choice of
v
, one has

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}(v-\omega_{n})\leqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}(\psi-\omega_{n})\leqslant 0,

Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}\nabla(v-\omega_{n})\geqslant\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})(v-\omega_{n}).

(3.6)

Passing to the limit in (
3.6
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega\nabla(v-\omega)\geqslant\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega)(v-\omega).

Hence,
\omega
is a solution of (VI).

Since

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}\cdot\nabla\omega_{n}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}\cdot\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}^{-}\cdot\nabla\omega_{n}^{-},

that is

\|\omega_{n}\|^{2}=\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{2}+\|\omega_{n}^{-}\|^{2},

\{\omega_{n}^{+}\},\{\omega_{n}^{-}\}
are bounded.

Next, we shall prove
\omega_{n}^{+}\rightharpoonup\omega^{+},\omega_{n}^{-}\rightharpoonup\omega^{-}.
In fact, since
\omega_{n}\to\omega
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, there is also
\omega_{n}\to\omega
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. For any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, using Green’s formula and the dominated convergence theorem, one has

\displaystyle\left\langle\omega_{n},\varphi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla|\omega_{n}|\cdot\nabla\varphi=-\int_{\Omega}|\omega_{n}|\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle\to-\int_{\Omega}|\omega|\Delta\varphi=\int_{\Omega}\nabla|\omega|\cdot\nabla\varphi

\displaystyle=\left\langle|\omega|,\varphi\right\rangle.

Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, as known from Riesz theorem,
|\omega_{n}|\rightharpoonup|\omega|
. Due to

\omega^{+}=\frac{1}{2}(|\omega|+\omega),\omega^{-}=\frac{1}{2}(|\omega|-\omega),

and
\omega_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega
, we can get

\omega_{n}^{+}\rightharpoonup\omega^{+},\omega_{n}^{-}\rightharpoonup\omega^{-}.

Taking
\omega_{n}^{+}
as a test function in
(E)_{n}
we have

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}\cdot\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega_{n}^{+}.

(3.7)

Taking
\omega^{+}
as a test function in
(E)_{n}
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\omega_{n}^{+}\cdot\nabla\omega^{+}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega^{+}=\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega^{+}.

(3.8)

Passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
in the latter two relations, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}p(x,\omega_{n})\omega_{n}^{+}-\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=\|\omega^{+}\|^{2}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}-\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\omega_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=\|\omega^{+}\|^{2}+\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\omega_{\varepsilon}-\psi\right)^{+}(\omega^{+}-\psi)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(\omega_{n}-\psi\right)^{+}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\omega^{+}\|^{2}\leqslant\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\|\omega_{n}^{+}\|\to\|\omega^{+}\|
. Since
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a separable Hilbert space, we get

\omega_{n}^{+}\to\omega^{+}~~\text{as}~~n\to\infty.

Similarly, it can be proved that on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\omega_{n}^{-}\to\omega^{-}~~\text{as}~~n\to\infty.

Also
\|\omega^{+}\|\geqslant\alpha_{+}>0,~\|\omega^{-}\|\geqslant\alpha_{-}>0
, so
\omega=\omega^{+}-\omega^{-}
is a sign-changing solution of (VI). The proof is complete.
∎

\bm{{Proof~~of~~Theorem~~1.1.}}

Proof.

From Lemma
3.4
, we can see that problem (VI) has at least one sign-changing solution. For the proof process of the existence of its positive solution; see [6]. The existence of negative solution can be directly proved using the Mountain Pass Lemma on closed convex sets. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In [6], M.Grardi, L.Mastroeni and M.Matzeu gave the regularity result of positive solution for (VI). In [1], Michele Matzeu and Raffaella Servadeil also gave the regularity results of positive solution for variational inequality problems with gradient terms, while the regularity results for negative solution and sign-changing solution have not yet been obtained.

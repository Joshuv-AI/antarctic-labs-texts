Gauge Symmetry of the Chiral Schwinger model from an improved Gauge Unfixing formalism
Gabriella V. Ambrósio, Cleber N. Costa, Paulo R. F. Alves, Everton M. C. Abreu, Jorge Ananias Neto, Ronaldo Thibes

Abstract
In this paper, the Hamiltonian structure of the bosonized chiral Schwinger model (BCSM) is analyzed. From the consistency condition of the constraints obtained from the Dirac method, we can observe that this model presents, for certain values of the α \alpha parameter, two second-class constraints, which means that this system does not possess gauge invariance. However, we know that it is possible to disclose gauge symmetries in such a system by converting the original second-class system into a first-class one. This procedure can be done through the gauge unfixing (GU) formalism by acting with a projection operator directly on the original second-class Hamiltonian, without adding any extra degrees of freedom in the phase space. One of the constraints becomes the gauge symmetry generator of the theory and the other one is disregarded. At the end, we have a first-class Hamiltonian satisfying a first-class algebra. Here, our goal is to apply a new scheme of embedding second-class constrained systems based on the GU formalism, named improved GU formalism, in the BCSM. The original second-class variables are directly converted into gauge invariant variables, called GU variables. We have verified that the Poisson brackets involving the GU variables are equal to the Dirac brackets between the original second-class variables. Finally, we have found that our improved GU variables coincide with those obtained from an improved BFT method after a particular choice for the Wess-Zumino terms.

II
The improved gauge unfixing formalism

The improved GU formalism developed by Neto
A_18
;
A_14
;
proto
is based on the initial idea of the GU formalism
A_13
;
A_15
;
A_16
which is to convert the second-class Hamiltonian into first-class one. Here we consider any second-class function of the phase space, namely,
G(A_{\mu},\pi_{\mu})
. Thus, our strategy is to write a first-class function
\tilde{G}(\tilde{A}_{\mu},\tilde{\pi}_{\mu})
from the second-class function
G
by redefining the original phase space variables. At the end, the function
\tilde{G}
need to satisfy the following variational condition

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{G}=\varepsilon\left\{\tilde{G},\chi\right\}=0\,,

(3)

where
\varepsilon
is an infinitesimal parameter and
\chi
is a scaled second-class constraint chosen to be the gauge symmetry generator.
Here we are considering that the system has only two second-class constraints which are denoted by
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
. Any function of
\tilde{G}
will be gauge invariant since

\displaystyle\left\{\tilde{G},\chi\right\}=\left\{\tilde{A},\chi\right\}\frac{\partial\tilde{G}}{\partial\tilde{A}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{G}}{\partial\tilde{\pi}}\left\{\tilde{\pi},\chi\right\}=0\,.

(4)

Consequently, we can obtain a gauge invariant function from the replacement of

\displaystyle G(A_{i},\pi_{i})\rightarrow G(\tilde{A_{i}},\tilde{\pi_{i}})=\tilde{G}(\tilde{A_{i}},\tilde{\pi_{i}})\,.

(5)

The gauge invariant phase space variables
\tilde{G}
are constructed by the series in powers of the discarded constraint
Q_{2}

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(x)=G(x)+\int dyb_{1}(x,y)Q_{2}(y)+\iint dydzb_{2}(x,y,z)Q_{2}(y)Q_{2}(z)+...\,,

(6)

which has the following boundary condition on the constraint surface
Q_{2}=0

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{(Q_{2}=0)}=G\,,

(7)

that is, we need to recover the original second-class system when
Q_{2}=0
. The coefficients
b_{n}
in the relation (
6
) are then determined by the variational conditional, Eq. (
3
). The general equation for
b_{n}
is

\displaystyle\delta\tilde{G}(x)=\delta G(x)+\delta\int dyb_{1}(x,y)Q_{2}(y)+\delta\iint dydzb_{2}(x,y,z)Q_{2}(y)Q_{2}(z)+...=0\,,

(8)

where

\displaystyle\delta G(x)=\int dy\varepsilon(y)\left\{G(x),\chi(y)\right\},

(9)

\displaystyle\delta b_{1}(x)=\int dy\varepsilon(y)\left\{b_{1}(x),\chi(y)\right\},

(10)

\displaystyle\delta Q_{2}(x)=\int dy\varepsilon(y)\left\{Q_{2}(x),\chi(y)\right\}=-\varepsilon(x)\,.

(11)

So, for the first order correction term
(n=1)
we have from Eq. (
8
)

\displaystyle\delta G(x)+\int dyb_{1}(x,y)\delta Q_{2}(y)=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad b_{1}(x,y)=\frac{\delta G(x)}{\varepsilon(x)}\delta(x-y)\,.

(12)

For the second order correction term
(n=2)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int dy\delta b_{1}(x,y)Q_{2}(y)+2\iint dydzb_{2}(x,y,z)\delta Q_{2}(y)Q_{2}(z)=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow b_{2}(x,y,z)=\frac{\delta\delta G(x)}{2\varepsilon^{2}(x)}\delta(x-y)\delta(y-z)\,.

(13)

For
n\geq 2
, the general relation is

\displaystyle b_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},...,x_{n})=\frac{\delta^{n}G(x_{1})}{n!\varepsilon^{n}(x_{1})}\delta(x_{1}-x_{2})\delta(x_{2}-x_{3}).......\delta(x_{n-1}-x_{n})\,.

(14)

Once the coefficients
b_{n}
are all determined, we can substitute them in Eq. (
6
), resulting in the expression for the gauge invariant function
\tilde{G}(x)
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(x)=\left(1+Q_{2}(x)\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon(x)}+\frac{1}{2!}Q_{2}^{2}(x)\frac{\delta\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}(x)}+...\right)G(x)\,.

(15)

Eq. (
15
) can also be written in terms of a projection operator on
G
in the following form

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(x)=e^{Q_{2}(x)\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}}:G(x)\,,

(16)

where an ordering prescription must be adopted:
Q_{2}
must come before
\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}
.
Therefore, from the GU variables defined in Eq. (
15
) or (
16
) we can derive a corresponding gauge invariant theory.

III
The bosonized Chiral Schwinger Model

The BCSM, being a (1 + 1) dimensional field theory, is described by the following Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)^{2}+e(g^{\mu\nu}-\epsilon^{\mu\nu})(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)A_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}e^{2}\alpha A_{\mu}^{2}\,,

(17)

where
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the electromagnetic field tensor,
g_{\mu\nu}=(+1,-1)
,
\epsilon^{01}=-\epsilon^{10}=1
and
\alpha
is the regularisation parameter. From the Lagrangian density (
17
) we can obtain the canonical momenta which are given by

\pi_{0}=-F_{00}=0\,,

(18)

\pi_{1}=-F_{01}=-(\partial_{0}A_{1}-\partial_{1}A_{0})=-\dot{A}_{1}+\partial_{1}A_{0}\,,

(19)

\pi_{\varphi}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{0}\varphi)}=\partial_{0}\varphi+e(A_{0}-A_{1})\,.

(20)

From Eq. (
18
) we have the primary constraint

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}\equiv\pi_{0}\approx 0\,.

(21)

Using the Legendre transformation

\displaystyle H_{c}=\int dx\left(\pi^{\mu}\dot{A}_{\mu}+\pi_{\varphi}\dot{\varphi}-\mathcal{L}\right)\,

(22)

we can obtain the canonical Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{c}=\int dx

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{1}{2}\pi_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\pi_{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}\varphi)^{2}+e\big(\partial_{1}\varphi+\pi_{\varphi}\big)A_{1}+\frac{1}{2}e^{2}\big(\alpha+1\big)A_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle-A_{0}\big[-\partial_{1}\pi_{1}+\frac{e^{2}(\alpha-1)}{2}A_{0}+e(\partial_{1}\varphi+\pi_{\varphi})+e^{2}A_{1}\big]\bigg]\,.

(23)

By writing the primary Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H_{p}=H_{c}+\int dx\lambda_{1}\Omega_{1}\,,

(24)

where
\lambda_{1}
is the Lagrange multiplier, and demanding the time stability condition of the primary constraint, Eq. (
21
), we have the secondary constraint

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}\equiv-\partial_{1}\pi_{1}+e^{2}(\alpha-1)A_{0}+e(\partial_{1}\varphi+\pi_{\varphi})+e^{2}A_{1}\approx 0\,,

(25)

which is identified as being a modified Gauss law. We note that no more constraints are produced by this iterative process. Therefore
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are the total constraints of
the model. Calculating the Poisson brackets of these constraints, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{12}=\left\{\Omega_{1}(x),\Omega_{2}(y)\right\}=-e^{2}(\alpha-1)\delta(x-y)\,.

(26)

From Eq. (
26
) we can see that for
\alpha=1
the constraints
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
satisfy a first-class algebra and for
\alpha>1
the constraints

\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
satisfy a second-class algebra. We shall only consider
the case
\alpha>1
where the BCSM has two second-class constraints.

IV
Discussion of results

In this section, we will reveal the hidden symmetries of the BCSM with the use of the improved GU formalism. As we have seen, the BCSM presents two second-class constraints for
\alpha>1

21

Case I

\Omega_{1}=\pi_{0}

\psi

\displaystyle\psi\equiv-\frac{\Omega_{1}}{e^{2}(\alpha-1)}=-\frac{\pi_{0}}{e^{2}(\alpha-1)}\,,

\displaystyle=\delta A_{1}(x)=\delta\varphi(x)=\delta\pi_{\varphi}(x)=0\,,

Applying the variational condition

\displaystyle\delta A_{0}(x)+\int dyb_{1}(x,y)\delta\Omega_{2}(y)=0\,.

\displaystyle b_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{e^{2}(\alpha-1)}\delta(x-y)\,.

V
A comparison with the Batalin-Fradkin-Tyutin (BFT) method

In this section, we will make a brief comparison of our results with the gauge invariant variables of the BCSM
obtained with the help of the newly improved BFT formalism. For more details about this procedure see reference

A_22
. This method, in stark contrast to the improved GU
formalism, makes an enlargement of the phase space with extra variables while extracting a gauge theory from a second-class constrained system.

Initially we will consider the original second-class function
G=(A^{\mu},\pi_{\mu},\varphi,\pi_{\varphi})
in the BCSM. Then, the new
first-class function
\tilde{G}=(\tilde{A}^{\mu},\tilde{\pi}_{\mu},\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\pi}_{\varphi})
in the extended phase space
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(A^{\nu},\pi_{\nu},\varphi,\pi_{\varphi};\Phi^{j})=G+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tilde{G}^{(n)},\qquad\tilde{G}^{(n)}\sim(\Phi^{j})^{n}

(68)

where
\Phi^{j}
are the two extra variables. The function
\tilde{G}
satisfies the following boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}|_{\Phi^{i}=0}=G\,.

(69)

The first order correction terms in
\Phi^{j}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{\mu(1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{e\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\Phi^{2},\frac{1}{e\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\partial^{1}\Phi^{1}\right)\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{\mu}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\left(e\sqrt{\alpha-1}\Phi^{1},-\frac{e}{\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\Phi^{1}\right)\,,

(71)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\Phi^{1},\,

(72)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{\varphi}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\partial^{1}\Phi^{1}\,.

(73)

In the sequence we have that the higher order correction terms are null. Then, the BFT gauge invariant variables can be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=A^{\mu}+\tilde{A}^{\mu(1)}=\left(A^{0}+\frac{1}{e\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\Phi^{2},A^{1}+\frac{1}{e\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\partial^{1}\Phi^{1}\right)\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\pi^{\mu}+\tilde{\pi}^{\mu(1)}=\left(\pi^{0}+e\sqrt{\alpha-1}\Phi^{1},\pi^{1}+\frac{e}{\sqrt{\alpha-1}}\Phi^{1}\right)\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}

VI
Final Remarks

In this work we have converted the BCSM with
\alpha>1
into a first-class system by using the improved GU formalism.
Initially, the canonical structure of this model was analyzed by the Dirac formalism for constrained systems where it was observed that for
\alpha>1
the system presents two second-class constraints. Subsequently, the improved GU formalism was applied which has provided two distinct gauge theories. In case I, the rescaled primary constraint, Eq. (
27
), was chosen to be the gauge symmetry generator and the corresponding GU variables are derived. In case II, the rescaled secondary constraint, Eq. (
46
), was chosen to be the gauge symmetry generator and we also obtain the respective GU variables. Furthermore, we have shown precisely
that the improved GU procedure leads to the same Poisson brackets
for both cases I and II. This result can also indicate the consistency of our formalism. Finally, we have compared our results obtained by using the improved GU formalism with those obtained by using the improved BFT method.
We have observed that the gauge invariant variables and the gauge invariant Hamiltonian, both derived with the aid of the improved BFT formalism, coincide with the GU variables and the GU Hamiltonian of case II when we take particular values for the Wess-Zumino fields. Here we can mention that the improved GU formalism leads to less extensive calculations compared to the usual GU method which the canonical Hamiltonian is directly converted into a gauge invariant Hamiltonian. Therefore, as we have observed throughout the work, once the GU variables are determined then the corresponding gauge theory is obtained in a very straightforward way.

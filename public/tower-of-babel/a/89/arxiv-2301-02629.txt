Intersection theory on non-archimedean analytic spaces
Yulin Cai

Abstract
. We develop the intersection theory of non-archimedean analytic spaces and prove the projection formula and the GAGA principle. As an application, we naturally define the category of finite correspondences of analytic spaces.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14G22; Secondary 14A99

1.
Introduction

The intersection theory of non-archimedean analytic spaces has been studied in
[
Gub98
, Section 2]
and
[
Ayo15
, Section 2.2]
, and the author believes that some experts have concrete idea about such a theory.

In
[
Gub98
]
, Gubler considers the Cartier divisors on rigid analytic spaces and formal schemes, and define their intersection with irreducible analytic subsets. This theory allows him to define the local height of subvarieties over non-archimedean fields.

In
[
Ayo15
]
, Ayoub develops the theory of motives on rigid analytic spaces using homotopy theory. He uses the presheaves on the category of affinoid spaces to construct the category of finite correspondence (for rigid analytic space)
\mathbf{RigCor}(K)
. Such construction avoids the intersection theory of analytic spaces.

In this paper, we develop the intersection theory of non-archimedean analytic spaces following the idea similar to the case of algebraic varieties. We will show the flat base change formula, the projection formula and the GAGA principle to relate the intersection theories of analytic spaces and of algebraic varieties. As an application, we give a direct construction of
\mathbf{RigCor}(K)
like
[
MVW06
, Lecture 1]
does, see
Remark

8.10
for precise statement. In fact, we can define the higher Chow groups of analytic spaces as
[
Blo86
]
for algebraic varieties, and this definition is different from Ayoub’s in
[
Ayo15
, Introduction générale]
.

In
Section

2
, we give some basic notion in the theory of Berkovich spaces, e.g. support of a coherent sheaf, Zariski image and codimension. We also extend
[
Duc09
, Proposition 4.12]
into an abstract form, i.e.
Lemma

2.17
which is a key lemma for this paper. With this lemma, we can solve the compatibility problems in our theory, e.g. see
Lemma

4.7
and
Lemma

5.4
.

In
Section

3
, we define and study the Cartier divisors on an analytic space
X
, which form a group
\mathrm{Div}(X)
. The group of divisors up to linear equivalence is denoted by
\mathrm{CaCl}(X)
. As in the theory of schemes, we have an injective homomorphism
\mathrm{CaCl}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, and it is an isomorphism if
X
is affinoid and reduced.

In
Section

4
, we give the notion of cycles, and associate a coherent sheaf with a cycle.
In particular, we can associate a closed subspace with a cycle. As in the theory of algebraic varieties, the flat pull-backs and proper push-forwards of cycles are defined. We prove the following flat base change formula.

Proposition 1.1

(
Proposition

4.31
)
.

Let

\textstyle{Y^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{X}

be a Cartesian diagram of separated, strictly
K
-analytic spaces. Assume that
f
is proper, and that
g
is flat, has of relative dimension
r
. Then
f^{\prime}
is proper,
g^{\prime}
is flat and has relative dimension
r
. Moreover,
g^{*}\circ f_{*}=f^{\prime}_{*}\circ g^{\prime*}
on
Z^{*}(Y)
.

In
Section

5
, we define intersection product of proper intersection. We will give two definitions, meaning a local one using the scheme theory and a global one using
\mathrm{Tor}
formula. For a flat morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of
K
-analytic spaces of pure dimension, the pull-back

preserves intersection product.

Since we have the flat pull-backs, proper push-forwards and intersection products, the expected projection formula is proved in
Section

.

Theorem 1.2

(Projection formula)
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a flat, proper morphism of regular, separated, strictly
K
-analytic spaces. Let
\alpha\in Z^{*}(Y)
and
\beta\in Z^{*}(X)
. Assume that
\alpha
and
f^{*}\beta
intersect properly. Then
f_{*}(\alpha)
and
\beta
intersect properly and

f_{*}(\alpha)\cdot\beta=f_{*}(\alpha\cdot f^{*}\beta).

In
Section

7
, we compare the intersection theories of algebraic varieties and of non-archimedean analytic spaces. We prove the GAGA principle, i.e.
Proposition

7.2
.

In
Section

8
, we define the category of finite correspondence
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}
. This category is also defined by Ayoub
[
Ayo15
]
using another definition.

Notation and terminology

Throughout this paper, we fix a complete non-archimedean field
K
with a non-trivial valuation. For a
K
-analytic space, we mean a Berkovich space over
K
, see
[
Ber93
, Definition 1.2.3]
. The structure sheaf on a
K
-analytic space
X
with respect to the G-topology is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. If it is necessary, we will use the notation
X_{G}
for the G-topology instead of the ordinary topology on
X
. The (
K
-analytic) dimension of
X
is denoted by
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}X
, or
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits X
when there is no confusion with the fields.

Given a point
x\in X
,
\mathscr{H}(x)
denotes its complete residue field and
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{x}X
denotes the local dimension of
X
at
x
.

We shall simply say ”coherent sheaf on
X
” for ”coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module (with respect to G-topology)”, and denote
\mathcal{C}oh(X)
the category of coherent sheaves on
X
. We also denote
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
the group of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on
X
. Assume that
X
is good, let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
and
x\in X
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{x}
the stalk at
x
of
\mathcal{F}
viewed as a sheaf of the underlying ordinary topology of
X
, i.e.

\mathcal{F}_{x}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
U\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}(U)=\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
V\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}(V).

where
U
runs through open neighborhoods of
x
, and
V
runs through affinoid neighborhoods of
x
.

We will write
{\mathrm{Irr}(X)}
for the set of all irreducible components of
X
, see
[
Duc09
, § 4.3.2]
, and write
\overline{\mathrm{Irr}(X)}
for the set of all irreducible Zariski-closed subsets of
X
. Notice that
\overline{\mathrm{Irr}(X)}
has a partial order:
W\leq Z
if
W\subset Z
.

By an algebraic variety over
K
, we mean a separated scheme of finite type over
K
.

For a commutative ring
A
,
R(A)
denotes the set of all regular elements of
A
and
\mathrm{Frac}(A)=R(A)^{-1}A
, the maximal localization containing
A
as a subring. For an
A
-module
M
, we denote
\mathrm{Ann}(M)\coloneq\{a\in A\mid a\cdot M=0\}
.

2.
Preliminary

For the convenience of the reader and further uses, in the section, we provide some basic concepts and results that are either given somewhere, or formulated easily.

In this section, we fix a
K
-analytic space
X
.

2.1.
Support of a coherent sheaf

(cf.
[
Duc18
, Section 2.5]
)

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})
the (coherent) annihilator ideal of
\mathcal{F}
(on the site
X_{G}
). The
support of
\mathcal{F}
is the closed analytic subspace of
X
defined by
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})
, denoted by
\mathrm{Supp}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Recall the annihilator
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as follows: for any analytic domain
V
,

\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})(V)\coloneqq\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)\mid a\cdot\mathcal{F}(V)=0\},

which is a coherent ideal. In particular, for any analytic domain
V
, we have
\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})|_{V}=\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F}|_{V})
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
X=\mathcal{M}(A)
is affinoid and
\mathcal{F}=\widetilde{M}
, the coherent sheaf associated to some finitely generated
A
-module
M
, then it is easy to see that

\mathrm{Ann}(\mathcal{F})=\widetilde{\mathrm{Ann}(M)}.

From the definition, we can easy deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
Z=\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{F})
. Then there is a unique coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
Z
such that
\mathcal{F}=i_{*}\mathcal{G}
, where
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
is the canonical immersion.

Proof.

By uniqueness, we can glue coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}
from local parts, so we can assume that
X=\mathcal{M}(A)
. It is not hard to see the lemma in this case.
∎

2.2.
Zariski image of a morphism

As in the theory of schemes, we can define the Zariski image of a morphism of analytic spaces, which has a natural structure of analytic space. We follow the idea in
[
Sta22
, Subsection 29.6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, and
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule. Then there is a unique coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}
with the following property: for any coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{H}
, the canonical map

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})

is bijective. In particular,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is the largest coherent sheaf contained in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\mathcal{G}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of coherent sheaves contained in
\mathcal{G}
.
We consider the morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

\varphi\colon\bigoplus\limits_{i\in I}\mathcal{G}_{i}\to\mathcal{F}.

We claim its image
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}
is coherent. Let
{{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{G}
be the image of
\varphi
as presheaves. Then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is the sheafification of
{{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
, and for any affinoid domain
V=\mathcal{M}(A)
,
{{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(V)=\sum\limits_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}(V)\subset\mathcal{F}(V)
is a finitely generated
A
-module. For any analytic domain
W\subset V
, we have
{{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(W)={{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(V)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)}\mathcal{O}_{X}(W)
, so
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(V)={{}^{p}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}(V)
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is coherent. It is the largest coherent sheaf contained in
\mathcal{G}
.

The map

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{G})

is obviously injective. For any homomorphism
\psi\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
, the image
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits(\psi)\subset\mathcal{G}
is a coherent sheaf, so
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits(\psi)\subset\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, so
f
factor through
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. This implies that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is the one we want.

The uniqueness is from the Yoneda lemma. ∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of
K
-analytic spaces. Then there is a closed analytic subspace
Z
of
X
such that

(a)

the morphism
f
factors through
Z
;

(b)

(Universal property) if
f
factors through a closed analytic subspace
Z^{\prime}
of
X
, then
Z^{\prime}
contains
Z
as a closed analytic subspace.

The closed analytic space
Z
of
X
is called the
Zariski image
of
f
, denoted by
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)
.

Proof.

By (b), if
Z
exists, then it is unique. It remains to show the existence. Let
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. By
Lemma

2.5
, we take the largest coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{I}
and set
Z=V(\mathcal{J})
. It remains to check (a) and (b).

(a) For any affinoid domain
V=\mathcal{M}(A)\subset X
and any affinoid domain
U=\mathcal{M}(B)\subset f^{-1}(V)
, we have
\mathcal{J}(V)\subset\mathcal{I}(V)\subset\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(A\rightarrow B)
, so
U\rightarrow V
factors through
\mathcal{M}(A/\mathcal{J}(V))=Z\cap V
and
U\rightarrow X
factors through
Z
. Hence
f
factors through
Z
.

(b) If
f
factors through a closed subspace
Z^{\prime}
of
X
with
Z^{\prime}=V(\mathcal{J}^{\prime})
, then
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{I}
. By the choice of
\mathcal{J}
, we have
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{J}
, so
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

If
X,Y
are affinoid,
f\colon\mathcal{M}(B)\to\mathcal{M}(A)
is given by
\varphi\colon A\to B
, then
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)=\mathcal{M}(A/\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\varphi))
. However, the Zariski image is not compatible with the G-topology.

We may expect the Zariski image is exactly the usual image as sets. It is almost true if
Y
is reduced or
f
is quasi-compact.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of
K
-analytic space. If
Y
is reduced, then
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)=\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}
with the reduce closed subspace structure.

Proof.

As a map,
f
factor through
\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}
. Since
Y
is reduced, so
f
factors through
\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}
with the reduced structure, see
[
Duc09
, PROPOSITION 4.2 (iii)]
. It remains to show the universal property of
Y\rightarrow\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}
. If
f
factors through a closed subspace
Z
of
X
, then
\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}\subset Z
as a subset. The containment is also a morphism of analytic spaces since
\overline{f(Y)}^{X_{\text{zar}}}
is endowed with the reduced structure.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of
K
-analytic spaces. Assume that
f
is proper. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mathcal{I}=\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})
is coherent. In particular,
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)=V(\mathcal{I})
.

(2)

f(Y)=\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)
.

(3)

For any analytic domain
V\subset X
, the subspace
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)\cap V
is the Zariski image of
f|_{f^{-1}(V)}\colon f^{-1}(V)\to V
.

Proof.

(1) Since
f
is proper, by Kiehl’s theorem, see
[
Ber90
, Proposition 3.3.5]
or
Theorem

4.21
, we have that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is coherent. Then

\mathcal{I}=\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})
which is coherent. Notice that
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)
is defined by the coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule of
\mathcal{I}
, so (1) holds.

(3) This is from (1).

(2) By (3), it suffices to assume that
X=\mathcal{M}(A)
is affinoid. We take a G-covering
Y=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
by affinoid domains, and set
Y^{\prime}=\coprod\limits_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
,
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
the canonical morphism which is surjective,
f^{\prime}\coloneq f\pi
. Notice that
f(Y)=f^{\prime}(Y^{\prime})
,
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)=\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})
(by definition,
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})\subset\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)
, conversely,
f
factors through
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f)
since the morphism
Y^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})
factors through
Y
). Write
Y^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}(B)
, and
f^{\prime}
is induced by
\varphi\colon A\to B
. We have that
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})=\mathcal{M}(A/\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\varphi))
. So the morphism
Y^{\prime}\rightarrow\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})
is induced by an injective homomorphism
A/\operatorname{Ker}\nolimits(\varphi)\rightarrow B
, hence it is dominant, and
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits_{\text{zar}}(f^{\prime})=\overline{f^{\prime}(Y^{\prime})}^{X_{\text{zar}}}=f(Y)
. This proves (2).
∎

2.3.
Codimension

We recall the definition of codimension in
[
Duc18
, 1.5.15]
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
Y
be a Zariski-closed subset of
X
. The
codimension
\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,X)
of
Y
in
X
is defined as follows.

•

If both
Y
and
X
are irreducible,
\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,X)\coloneqq\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}X-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Y
.

•

If
Y
is irreducible,
\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,X)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathrm{Irr}(X)\\
Y\subset Z\end{subarray}}\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,Z)
.

•

In the general case,
\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,X)\coloneqq\inf\limits_{Z\in\mathrm{Irr}(Y)}\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Z,X)
.

For
x\in X
, we define the
codimension of
Y
in
X
at
x
as

\operatorname{codim}\nolimits_{x}(Y,X)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\in\mathrm{Irr}(Y)\\
x\in Z\end{subarray}}\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Z,X)&\text{ if $x\in Y$;}\\
+\infty&\text{ if $x\not\in Y$.}\end{cases}

Remark 2.12
.

Let
W\subset Z\subset Y\subset X
be irreducible closed analytic subspaces. Then

\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(W,Y)=\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(W,Z)+\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Z,Y),

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}(Z)+\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Z,Y)=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}(Y).

Example 2.13

(
[
Duc07
]
Proposition 1.11)
.

Assume that
X=\mathcal{M}(A)
is a
K
-affinoid space,
Y=V(I)
for some ideal
I\subset A
, and
x\in X
with image
\xi\in\mathrm{Spec}(A)
. Then

(1)

\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(Y,X)=\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(\mathrm{Spec}(A/I),\mathrm{Spec}(A))
.

(2)

\operatorname{codim}\nolimits_{x}(Y,X)=\operatorname{codim}\nolimits_{\xi}(\mathrm{Spec}(A/I),\mathrm{Spec}(A))
.

Remark 2.14
.

In particular, (1) implies that

\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(\mathrm{Spec}(A_{L}/I_{L}),\mathrm{Spec}(A_{L}))=\operatorname{codim}\nolimits(\mathrm{Spec}(A/I),\mathrm{Spec}(A))

for any complete field extension
L/K
. Or we can write

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}X-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Y=\operatorname{codim}\nolimits_{\mathrm{Krull}}(Y,X).

2.4.
A key lemma

Definition 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of objects (e.g. morphisms, irreducible analytic spaces) that we care about. Let
Q
be a property of objects in
\mathcal{A}
, so we have a disjoint union

\mathcal{A}=\{S\in\mathcal{A}\mid\text{$S$ satisfies $Q$}\}\cup\{S\in\mathcal{A}\mid\text{$S$ doesn't satisfy $Q$}\}=\colon\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}.

Furthermore, let
P
be a property of objects in
\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Then for an object
S\in\mathcal{A}
, we say

•

P
is
well-defined
on
S
if
S\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
;

•

S

satisfies
P
if
P\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
S
satisfies
P
in the usual sense.

•

S

doesn’t satisfy
P
if
P\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
S
doesn’t satisfy
P
in the usual sense.

Remark 2.16
.

In this paper, the property
Q
is implied when we talk about the property
P
, so we will omit
Q
.

The following generalized result from
[
Duc09
, Proposition 4.12]
is crucial for extending a local result on irreducible closed subsets to be global.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
P
be a property of irreducible components of affinoid domains of
X
satisfying the following properties:

(a)

there is a
G
-covering
X=\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}V_{i}
by affinoid domains, the property
P
is well-defined on each irreducible component of
V_{i}
(or simply say that
P
is well-defined on
V_{i}
);

(b)

if
P
is well-defined on an irreducible component
Z
of an affinoid domain
V
, then
P
is well-defined on each irreducible component of
W
for any affinoid domain
W\subset V
. Moreover, in this case, for any irreducible component
T
of
W\cap Z
, we have
T
satisfies
P
if and only if
Z
satisfies
P
.

Then there exist Zariski-closed subsets
X^{+}_{P},X_{P}^{-}
of
X
which are characterized by the following properties: for any affinoid domain
V
on which
P
is well-defined, we have

X_{P}^{+}\cap V=\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathrm{Irr}(V),\\
T\text{ satisfies $P$}\end{subarray}}T,

X_{P}^{-}\cap V=\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathrm{Irr}(V),\\
\text{$T$ doesn't satisfy $P$}\end{subarray}}T.

Notice that
X=X_{P}^{+}\cup X_{P}^{-}
.

Proof.

For any affinoid domain
V
on which
P
is well-defined, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{+}(V)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{T\in\mathrm{Irr}(V)\mid T\text{ satisfies $P$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{-}(V)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{T\in\mathrm{Irr}(V)\mid T\text{ doesn't satisfy $P$}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}(V)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{T\in\mathcal{C}^{+}(V)}T,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-}(V)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{T\in\mathcal{C}^{-}(V)}T.

Let
V
be an affinoid domain on which
P
is well-defined, and
W\subset V
an affinoid domain. Let
Z
be an irreducible component of
V
and
T
an irreducible component of
W
contained in
Z
. By our assumption,
T\in\mathcal{C}^{+}(W)
\Longleftrightarrow

Z\in\mathcal{C}^{+}(V)
. By
[
Duc09
, COROLLAIRE 4.11]
, we have
\mathcal{E}^{+}(W)=\mathcal{E}^{+}(V)\cap W
and
\mathcal{E}^{-}(W)=\mathcal{E}^{-}(V)\cap W
.

Let
X_{P}^{+}
(resp.
X_{P}^{-}
) be the union of
\mathcal{E}^{+}(V)
(resp.
\mathcal{E}^{-}(V)
) where
V
is an affinoid domain on which
P
is well-defined. Then for any affinoid domain
V
of
X
on which
P
is well-defined, we have
X_{P}^{+}\cap V=\mathcal{E}^{+}(V)
and
X_{P}^{-}\cap V=\mathcal{E}^{-}(V)
. Since
P
is well-defined on
V_{i}
for some G-covering

by affinoid domain, and

are Zariski-closed, so

Remark 2.18
.

In our application, (b) is always from the fact that for an affinoid domain

, the homomorphism

is flat.

3.
Meromorphic functions and Cartier divisors

The sheaf of meromorphic functions and Cartier divisors are defined on a ringed space in
[
Gro67
, Section 20, Section 21]
. However, the definition is not correct, thanks to referee for pointing this out to me, see
[
Kle79
]
. It doesn’t work since the restriction of a regular element is not necessarily regular. Fortunately, this can be remedied on analytic spaces (cf.
[
Gub98
, Section 2]
). In this section and next section, we will following the idea in
[
Gro67
, Section 20, Section 21]
to discuss meromorphic functions, Cartier divisors and cycles.

Throughout this section, we fix a
K
-analytic space
X
.

3.1.
Meromorphic functions

A reference for meromorphic functions is
[
Duc21
, § 2.23]
. For convenience of readers and further uses in our paper, we study meromorphic functions here.

Definition 3.1
.

For any affinoid domain
V=\mathcal{M}(A)\subset X
, we set
K^{\prime}_{X}(V)\coloneqq\mathrm{Frac}(A)
, this will define a presheaf on affinoid domains on
X
. The associated sheaf
K_{X}
with respect to the G-topology on
X
is called the
sheaf of meromorphic functions
on
X
. An element of
K_{X}(X)
is called a
meromorphic function
on
X
. The subsheaf of invertible elements of
K_{X}
is denoted by
K_{X}^{*}
.

Remark 3.2
.

For affinoid domains
U=\mathcal{M}(B)\subset V=\mathcal{M}(A)
of
X
, and
f\in R(A)
, the restriction of
f
on
U
is in
R(B)
, this implies that
K_{X}
is well-defined. Indeed, this is from the fact
A\rightarrow B
is flat.

Remark 3.3
.

For any analytic domain
V\subset X
, we have

K_{X}(V)=\left\{(s_{i})_{i}\in\prod\limits_{i}K_{X}^{\prime}(V_{i})\,\middle|\,\parbox[c]{200.95274pt}{$V=\bigcup\limits_{i}V_{i}$ is a G-covering of $V$ with $V_{i}$ affinoid and $s_{i}|_{V_{ijk}}=s_{j}|_{V_{ijk}}$ for some G-covering \ \ \ \ $V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\bigcup\limits_{k}V_{ijk}$ with $V_{ijk}$ affinoid}\right\}\bigg/\sim,

where
(s_{i})_{i}\sim(s_{j}^{\prime})_{j}
if for any
i,j
, there exists a G-covering
V_{i}\cap V_{j}^{\prime}=\bigcup\limits_{k}V_{ijk}
with
V_{ijk}
affinoid such that
s_{i}|_{V_{ijk}}=s^{\prime}_{j}|_{V_{ijk}}
.

If
X
is separated, then it can be simplified as

K_{X}(V)=\left\{(s_{i})_{i}\in\prod\limits_{i}K_{X}^{\prime}(V_{i})\,\middle|\,\parbox[c]{200.95274pt}{$V=\bigcup\limits_{i}V_{i}$ is an G-covering of $V$ with $V_{i}$ affinoid and $s_{i}|_{V_{i}\cap V_{j}}=s_{j}|_{V_{i}\cap V_{j}}$}\right\}\bigg/\sim,

where
(s_{i})_{i}\sim(s_{j}^{\prime})_{j}
if for any
i,j
,
s_{i}|_{V_{i}\cap V^{\prime}_{j}}=s^{\prime}_{j}|_{V_{i}\cap V^{\prime}_{j}}
.

Remark 3.4
.

For any affinoid domain
V\subset X
, the canonical map
K_{X}^{\prime}(V)\rightarrow K_{X}(V)
is injective. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{X}\subset K_{X}
.

Proof.

Given an affinoid domain
V
and any finite G-covering
V=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
by affinoid domains, let
A=\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)
and
A_{i}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(V_{i})
. We consider the restriction map
\mathrm{Frac}(A)\rightarrow\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Frac}(A_{i})
.
Let
a/b\in\mathrm{Frac}(A)
be such that its restriction on
\mathrm{Frac}(A_{i})
is
0
for any
i
, i.e.
a=0\in A_{i}
. This implies that
a=0\in A
by Tate’s acyclic theorem. Hence
K_{X}^{\prime}(V)\hookrightarrow K_{X}(V)
.
We take a G-covering
X=\bigcup\limits_{i\in I}V_{i}
by affinoid domains. Then the injective map
\mathcal{O}_{X}(V_{i})\hookrightarrow K_{X}^{\prime}(V_{i})
will induce
\mathcal{O}_{X}\hookrightarrow K_{X}
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

For an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
, we call
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}K_{X}
the
sheaf of meromorphic sections of
\mathcal{F}
, and we have a canonical map

\operatorname{id}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes i\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}K_{X}.

The sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is called
strictly without torsion
if
\operatorname{id}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes i
is injective.

A global section of
\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}K_{X}
is called a

of
\mathcal{F}
on
X
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is coherent on

, we say a meromorphic section

on
X
is defined on a Zariski-open subset

is in the image of
\mathcal{F}(V)
via
\operatorname{id}\nolimits_{\mathcal{F}}\otimes i
. If moreover,
\mathcal{F}
is strictly without torsion, then there is a maximal Zariski-open subset

on which s is defined, such

is called the

of

, denoted by

(i.e.

).

Remark 3.6
.

Notice that
\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}K_{X}
is the sheafification of the presheaf given by

for any affinoid domain

Remark 3.7
.

A locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X_{G}}
-module

-module, here, we view

If

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}=\lim\limits_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{V}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)

.

Proposition 3.13
.

(1)

For any divisor
D=\{(U_{i},f_{i})\}_{i\in I}\in\mathrm{CaCl}(X)
, we can associate a subsheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\subset K_{X}
defined by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)|_{U_{i}}=f_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}}
, which is an invertible sheaf and independent of the choice of representative. Moreover,
D\geq 0

\Longleftrightarrow

\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

(2)

The construction above gives a homomorphism of groups
\rho\colon\mathrm{Div}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X),\ \ D\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.

(3)

The homomorphism
\rho
induces an injective homomorphism
\mathrm{CaCl}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(X)
with image
\operatorname{Im}\nolimits\rho
corresponding to invertible sheaves contained in
K_{X}
.

(4)

If
X
is affinoid and reduced, then
\rho\colon\mathrm{CaCl}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We follow the idea of the proof of
[
Liu02
, Proposition 7.1.18]
.

(1) Assume
D=\{(V_{j},g_{j})\}_{j\in J}
is another representative. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)|_{U_{i}\cap V_{j}}=f_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}\cap V_{j}}=(g_{j}u)^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}\cap V_{j}}=g_{j}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}\cap V_{j}}

where
u\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i}\cap V_{j})^{*}
, this implies
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is independent of the choice of representative. By construction,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
, and
D\geq 0
if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

(2) The map is a homomorphism. Indeed, let
D_{1}=\{(f_{i},U_{i})\}_{i\in I}
and
D_{2}=\{(g_{i},U_{i})\}_{i\in I}
, then

\rho(D_{1}+D_{2})|_{U_{i}}=f_{i}^{-1}g_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}}\simeq f_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}}}g_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U_{i}},

and this isomorphism is compatible on the intersection
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
.

(3) If
D=\{(U_{i},f_{i})\}_{i\in I}=\mathrm{div}(f)
is a principal divisor with
f\in H^{0}(X_{G},K_{X}^{*})
and
f_{i}=f|_{U_{i}}\in K^{\prime}_{X}(U_{i})
, where

Definition 4.1

\alpha
is

\mathrm{mult}_{Z}(\alpha)\geq 0
for any

. The set of cycles (resp. positive cycles) is denoted by
Z(X)
(resp.
Z_{+}(X)
).

The union of the
Z
such that

is called the

\alpha
, denoted by
\mathrm{Supp}(\alpha)
. It is a Zariski-closed subset of

(resp. dimension

For a positive cycle

4.4.
Flat pull-backs

We have introduced Cartier divisors, cycles. Next we consider their pull-backs via flat morphisms.

Recall the definition of flatness in sense of
[
Duc18
, Definition 4.1.8]
, a morphism
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
of
K
-analytic spaces is
naively flat
if for any
y\in{Y}
, there exists a good analytic domain
V\subset Y
containing
y
and a good analytic domain

containing

such that
\mathcal{O}_{V,y}
is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{U,f(y)}

is

if moreover

is naively flat for any morphism

is flat, then

is flat over
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
for any affinoid domains

and

with

. The converse is not true in general unless

is locally finite. Note that for any analytic domain
V
of

, the natural morphism

is flat.

Definition 4.15
.

A morphism
f\colon Y\rightarrow X
of
K
-analytic spaces has

r
if for any

,
f^{-1}(Z)=\emptyset
or any irreducible component

of

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Z^{\prime}=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Z+r
.

Remark 4.16
.

The notion of relative dimension

is an analogue of the one in algebraic geometry, see
[

. Our definition is different from the one in
[
Duc18
, §1.4.13]

Lemma 4.17
.

Let

be a flat morphism of
K
-analytic spaces. Then

has relative dimension

if and only if

or

is of pure dimension

for any

. In particular, if

is flat with

equidimensional, then f has relative dimension
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Y-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}X
.

Proof.

We apply
[
Duc18
, Lemma 4.5.11]
saying that
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y_{x}+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{x}X
for any

.

Assume that
f
has relative dimension
r
. If
x\in X
such that
Y_{x}\not=\emptyset
, then for any

containing

, we have
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}f^{-1}(Z)-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}Z=r
. This implies that
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y_{x}=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{x}X=r
for any

since

.

Conversely, for any

with
f^{-1}(Z)\not=\emptyset

. We take

and

. Then
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{x}X+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y_{x}=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{K}X+r
. This implies that

has relative dimension
r
.

If

are equidimensional, then
\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y_{x}=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{y}Y-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{x}X
implies that

is of equidimension for any

.

For an affinoid space

Recall a theorem of Kiehl.

Theorem 4.21

(

]
Proposition 3.3.5)

Let

be a proper morphism of

-analytic spaces, and

-modules.
In particular, we have Remmert’s mapping theorem, saying that

is an Zariski-closed subset of

We have the following equivalent conditions.

Let

(i)

(ii)

is proper, and for any

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{\mathscr{H}(x)}Y_{x}=0

(iii)

-analytic space.

Obviously, (i) implies (ii), and (ii) implies (iii). It remains to show that (iii) implies (i). The separatedness ensure that

are also rigid

[

. Then the result is from
[

Let

(iii)

(iv)

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{\mathscr{H}(x)}Y_{x}=0

Proof.

The equivalence of (i) and (iii) is

Assume that (iii) holds. By

, there is a maximal Zariski-open subset

Assume that (iv) holds. By

\deg(Y/X)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{y\in Y_{x}}[\mathscr{H}(y)\colon\mathscr{H}(x)]

is independent of the choice of

, called the
generic degree of

.

Proof.

By

and

, we can assume that

is finite.
For any (strictly) affinoid domain

and

, set

f(Q)=T\end{subarray}}[\mathrm{Frac}(A_{Q})\colon\mathrm{Frac}(A_{T})].

where

are the affinoid algebras corresponding to

with reduced structure. If

, set

and

. Notice that

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{y\in Y_{x}}\mathscr{H}(y)=

\displaystyle B\otimes_{A}\mathscr{H}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B\otimes_{A}\mathrm{Frac}(A_{T}))\otimes_{\mathrm{Frac}(A_{T})}\mathscr{H}(x)

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathrm{Irr}(f^{-1}(V))\\

and

and we know that

5.2.

8.
The category of finite correspondences

In this section, we will define the additive category
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}
of finite correspondences of
K
-analytic spaces. We will follow the notation in
[
Ayo15
]
and the idea in
[
MVW06
, Lecture 1]
.

For the
K
-analytic spaces in this section, we always mean separated, quasi-paracompact (see
[
Bos14
, Definition 8.12]
for quasi-paracompactness), strictly
K
-analytic spaces, the category of such spaces is exactly the category of separated, quasi-paracompact,
K
-rigid spaces by
[
Ber93
, Theorem 1.6.1]
.

A
K
-analytic space is said to be
quasi-smooth
if it is geometrically regular at each point, see
[
Duc18
, Corollary 5.3.5]
. In particular, a quasi-smooth space is regular.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
X
be a quasi-smooth, connected
K
-analytic space, and
Y
a
K
-analytic space. An
elementary correspondence
from
X
to
Y
is an irreducible closed subset
W
of
X\times Y
whose associated integral subspace is finite and surjective over
X
.

For an elementary correspondence from a quasi-smooth non-connected
K
-analytic space
X
to
Y
, we mean an elementary correspondence from a connected component of
X
to
Y
.

The group
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}(X,Y)
is the free abelian group generated by the elementary correspondences from
X
to
Y
, so
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}(X,Y)\subset Z^{*}(X\times Y)
. An element of
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}(X,Y)
are called a
finite correspondence
. By definition, if
X=\coprod\limits_{i}X_{i}
is the decomposition into its connected components, we have
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}(X,Y)=\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathrm{Cor}_{K}(X_{i},Y)
.

Remark 8.2
.

Let
X
be a quasi-smooth
K
-analytic space, and
Y
a
K
-analytic space. Every closed subspace
Z
of
X\times Y
which is finite and surjective over
X
determines a finite correspondence
[Z]
from
X
to
Y
. Indeed, we consider the case where
X
is connected (hence irreducible). We can write
[Z]=\sum\limits_{i}n_{i}[Z_{i}]
, where
Z_{i}
are irreducible component of
Z
such that
Z_{i}\rightarrow X
is surjective, and
n_{i}
is the geometric multiplicity of
Z_{i}
of
Z
.

To define the composition of morphism in the category
\mathrm{Cor}_{K}
, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
f\colon T\to T^{\prime}
be a morphism of
K
-analytic spaces over another
K
-analytic space
S
. Let
W
be an irreducible Zariski-closed subset of
T
which is finite and surjective over
S
. Then
f(W)
is irreducible, Zariski-closed in
T^{\prime}
and finite, surjective over
S
.

Proof.

Since
T\rightarrow S
is separated (
T
and
S
are separated),
W\rightarrow S
is finite, hence proper by
[
Ber90
, Corollary 3.3.8]
, we have that
W\rightarrow T^{\prime}
is proper, see
[
BGR84
, § 9.6.2, Proposition 4]
. So
f(W)
is irreducible and Zariski-closed in

.

We replace
T,T^{\prime}
by
W,f(W)
respectively, so we assume that
T\to S
is finite and surjective, and
T\to T^{\prime}
is proper and surjective. By
[
Ber90
, Corollary 3.3.8]
, it remains to show that
T^{\prime}
is proper over
S
. Obviously
T^{\prime}\rightarrow S
is quasi-compact since
T\rightarrow T^{\prime}
is surjective and
T^{\prime}\rightarrow S
quasi-compact. By
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.5.8 (iii)]
, we have

T=\mathrm{Int}(T/S)=\mathrm{Int}(T/T^{\prime})\cap f^{-1}(\mathrm{Int}(T^{\prime}/S))=f^{-1}(\mathrm{Int}(T^{\prime}/S)),

this implies that
\mathrm{Int}(T^{\prime}/S)=T^{\prime}
, i.e.

. So

is proper over

.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

Let

be a morphism of

-analytic spaces with

Lemma 8.5

Let

Proof.

This is in fact an algebraic result from

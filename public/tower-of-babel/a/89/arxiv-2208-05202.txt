Abstract
In this paper, a proof-theoretic method to prove uniform Lyndon interpolation for non-normal modal and conditional logics is introduced and applied to show that the logics 𝖤 \mathsf{E} , 𝖬 \mathsf{M} , 𝖤𝖭 \mathsf{EN} , 𝖬𝖭 \mathsf{MN} , 𝖬𝖢 \mathsf{MC} , 𝖪 \mathsf{K} , and their conditional versions, 𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{CE} , 𝖢𝖬 \mathsf{CM} , 𝖢𝖤𝖭 \mathsf{CEN} , 𝖢𝖬𝖭 \mathsf{CMN} , 𝖢𝖬𝖢 \mathsf{CMC} , 𝖢𝖪 \mathsf{CK} , in addition to 𝖢𝖪𝖨𝖣 \mathsf{CKID} have that property. In particular, it implies that these logics have uniform interpolation. Although for some of them the latter is known, the fact that they have uniform Lyndon interpolation is new. Also, the proof-theoretic proofs of these facts are new, as well as the constructive way to explicitly compute the interpolants that they provide. On the negative side, it is shown that the logics 𝖢𝖪𝖢𝖤𝖬 \mathsf{CKCEM} and 𝖢𝖪𝖢𝖤𝖬𝖨𝖣 \mathsf{CKCEMID} enjoy uniform interpolation but not uniform Lyndon interpolation. Moreover, it is proved that the non-normal modal logics 𝖤𝖢 \mathsf{EC} and 𝖤𝖢𝖭 \mathsf{ECN} and their conditional versions, 𝖢𝖤𝖢 \mathsf{CEC} and 𝖢𝖤𝖢𝖭 \mathsf{CECN} , do not have Craig interpolation, and whence no uniform (Lyndon) interpolation. keywords. non-normal modal logics, conditional logics, uniform interpolation, uniform Lyndon interpolation, Craig interpolation.

Introduction

Uniform interpolation (UIP), a strengthening of interpolation in which the interpolant only depends on the premise or the conclusion of an implication, is an intriguing logical property. One of the reasons is that it is hard to predict which logic does have the property and which does not. Well-behaved logics like
K
and
KD
have it, but then, other well-known modal logics, such as
K4
, do not. Early results on the subject were by Shavrukov
[
24
]
, who proved UIP for the modal logic
GL
, and by Ghilardi
[
6
]
and Visser
[
27
]
, who independently proved the same for
K
, followed later by Bílková, who showed that
KT
has the property, as well
[
2
]
. Surprisingly,
K4
and
S4
do not have UIP, although they do have interpolation
[
2
,
6
]
. Pitts provided the first proof-theoretic proof of UIP, for intuitionistic propositional logic,
IPC
, the smallest intermediate logic
[
19
]
. Results from
[
7
,
13
]
imply that there are exactly seven intermediate logics with interpolation and that they are exactly the intermediate logics with UIP. Pitts’ result is especially important to us, as also in our paper the approach is proof-theoretic.

In this paper we investigate UIP in the setting of non-normal modal and conditional logics. For these classes of logics, the study of UIP has a more recent history, and the property is less explored than in the setting of normal logics. Before we discuss our own contributions, we briefly describe the logics and the results from the literature that are relevant to our investigation.

Non-normal modal logics are modal logics in which the
K
-axiom, i.e. the axiom
\Box(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow(\Box\varphi\rightarrow\Box\psi)
, does not hold, but a weaker version that is given by the following
E
-rule does:

\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi

\Box\varphi\leftrightarrow\Box\psi

Thus the minimal non-normal modal logic,
E
, is propositional logic plus the
E
-rule above.
Over the last decades non-normal modal logics have emerged in various fields, such as game theory and epistemic and deontic logic
[
3
]
. Two well-known non-normal modal logics that are investigated in this paper are natural weakenings of the principle

\Box(\varphi\wedge\psi)\leftrightarrow(\Box\varphi\wedge\Box\psi)
that implies
K
over
E
. Namely, the two principles:

(M)\ \ \ \Box(\varphi\wedge\psi)\rightarrow(\Box\varphi\wedge\Box\psi)\ \ \ \ \ (C)\ \ \ (\Box\varphi\wedge\Box\psi)\rightarrow\Box(\varphi\wedge\psi).

Because the
K
-axiom holds in the traditional relational semantics for modal logic, non-normal modal logics require different semantics, of which the most well-known is neighborhood semantics. As we do not need semantics in this paper, we refer the interested reader to the textbook
[
16
]
.

Conditional logics formalize reasoning about conditional statements, which are statements of the form
if
\varphi
were the case, then
\psi
, denoted as
\varphi\triangleright\psi
. This includes formal reasoning about counterfactuals, which are instances of conditional statements in which the antecedent is false. Examples of well-known conditional logics are the logics
CE
and
CK
. Logic
CE
consists of classical propositional logic plus the following
CE
-rule, that could be seen as the conditional counterpart of the
E
-rule above (by reading the
\varphi_{i}
as
\top
):

\varphi_{0}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{1}

\psi_{0}\leftrightarrow\psi_{1}

(\varphi_{1}\triangleright\psi_{1})\leftrightarrow(\varphi_{0}\triangleright\psi_{0})

Conditional logic
CK
is the result of adding to classical propositional logic the following
CK
-rule, which derives the conditional counterpart of
K
:

\{\varphi_{0}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{i}\}_{i\in I}

\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rightarrow\psi_{0}

\{\varphi_{i}\triangleright\psi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rightarrow(\varphi_{0}\triangleright\psi_{0})

where
I
is a possibly empty set. Conditional logics have played an important role in philosophy ever since their introduction by Lewis in 1973
[
12
]
. Another area where they appear is artificial intelligence, in the setting of nonmonotonic reasoning
[
5
]
.
The first formal semantics and axiomatization of conditionals appeared in
[
25
]
and in the years after that the logics were further investigated in
[
3
,
12
,
4
]
.

For several well-known non-normal logics UIP has been established, for example, for the monotone logic
M

[
20
]
, a result later extended in
[
17
,
22
]
to other non-normal modal and conditional logics, such as
E
and
CK
.
Recently the proof theory of conditionals has been explored in
[
11
,
14
,
18
,
21
]
where sequent calculi and an automated theorem prover for several conditional logics have been developed.
Particularly relevant for this paper are the results in
[
17
,
18
]
, where in the former it is shown that the conditional logic
CK
has UIP, and in the latter, sequent-style systems for various conditional logics are provided.

Our interest in the property of UIP for non-normal modal and conditional logics lies in the fact that it can be used as a tool in what we would like to call
universal proof theory
, the area where one is concerned with the general behavior of proof systems, investigating problems such as the existence problem (when does a theory have a certain type of proof system?) and the equivalence problem (when are two proof systems equivalent?). The value of UIP for the existence problem has been addressed in a series of recent papers in which a method is developed to prove UIP that applies to many intermediate, (intuitionistic) modal, and substructural (modal) logics
[
1
,
8
,
9
]
. The proof-theoretic method makes use of
sequent calculi, and shows that general conditions on the calculi imply UIP for the corresponding logic. Thus implying that any logic without UIP cannot have a sequent calculus satisfying these conditions. The generality of the conditions, such as closure under weakening, makes this into a powerful tool, especially for those classes of logics in which UIP is rare, such as intermediate logics.
Note that in principle other regular properties than UIP
could be used in this method, as long as the property is sufficiently rare to be of use.

In this paper, we do not focus on the connection with the existence problem as just described, but rather aim to show the flexibility and utility
of our method to prove UIP by showing that it can be extended to two other classes of logics, namely the class of non-normal modal logics and the class of conditional logics. And by furthermore showing that it is constructive and can be easily adapted to prove not only UIP but even uniform Lyndon interpolation. Uniform Lyndon interpolation (ULIP) is a strengthening of UIP
in which the interpolant respects the polarity of propositional variables (a definition follows in the next section). It first occurred in
[
10
]
, where it was shown that several normal modal logics, including
\mathsf{K}
and
\mathsf{KD}
, have that property. In this paper we show that the non-normal modal logics
\mathsf{E}
,
\mathsf{M}
,
\mathsf{EN}
,
\mathsf{MN}
,
\mathsf{MC}
,
\mathsf{K}
, their conditional versions,
\mathsf{CE}
,
\mathsf{CM}
,
\mathsf{CEN}
,
\mathsf{CMN}
,
\mathsf{CMC}
,
\mathsf{CK}
in addition to
\mathsf{CKID}
have uniform Lyndon interpolation and
the interpolant can be constructed explicitly from the proof. In the last part of this paper we show that the non-normal modal logics
\mathsf{EC}
and
\mathsf{ECN}
and the conditional logics
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
do not have Craig interpolation, and whence no uniform (Lyndon) interpolation either. This surprising fact makes
\mathsf{EC}
,
\mathsf{ECN}
,
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
potential candidates for our approach to the existence problem
discussed above, but that we have to leave for another paper. Interestingly, the conditional logics
CKCEM
and
CKCEMID
have UIP but not ULIP, as we will show at the end of the paper. Some of these results have already been obtained in the literature.
That the logics
\mathsf{E}
,
\mathsf{M}
, and
\mathsf{MC}
have UIP has already been established in
[
17
,
20
]
, but that they have uniform Lyndon interpolation is, as far as we know, a new insight. In
[
15
]
it is shown that
\mathsf{E}
,
\mathsf{M}
,
\mathsf{MC}
,
\mathsf{EN}
,
\mathsf{MN}
have Craig interpolation. The proof that these logics have UIP is not a mere extension of the proof that they have interpolation but requires a different approach.

Our proof-theoretic method to prove UIP makes use of sequent calculi for non-normal modal and conditional logics that are equivalent or equal to calculi introduced in
[
15
]
. Only for the five logics
CE
,
CM
,
CEN
,
CMN
, and
CMC
, we had to develop cut-free sequent calculi ourselves.
Interestingly, for logics with Lyndon interpolation (LIP),
that fact does not always follow easily from the proof that they have Craig interpolation (CIP),
as is for example the case for
GL

[
23
]
. But for our method this indeed is the case: a proof of UIP using this method easily implies ULIP. Thus, the hard work lies in proving the former. Until now such proofs have always been semantical in nature.
For these reasons, we consider the proof-theoretic method to prove uniform interpolation for non-normal modal and conditional logics the main contribution of this paper. In
[
22
]
the search for proof-theoretic techniques to prove uniform interpolation in the setting of non-normal modal logics is explicitly mentioned in the conclusion of that paper.

The paper is build up as follows. In Section
2
, the logics and their sequent calculi are defined, except for the five logics for which we develop sequent calculi ourselves, which are treated in Section
3

and
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
have UIP (but not ULIP). Section

\mathsf{EC}
and
\mathsf{ECN}
and the corresponding conditional logics
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}

if it has all the above properties, omitting the superscripts

, everywhere. Specifically, for any set of rules

of

, called a

, and

Remark 4.2
.

As the formula

is provably unique, using the functional notation of writing

,
p
and

The following theorem connects ULIP and UIP for sequent calculi to the original version defined for logics, as we expect:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be one of the sequent calculi introduced in this paper and
L
be its logic. Then,

G
has ULIP (resp., UIP)
iff
L
has ULIP (resp., UIP).

Proof.

We only provide the sketch of the proof for ULIP. The proof for the other case is identical and only requires omitting the superscripts
\circ,\diamond\in\{+,-\}
. If
G
has ULIP,
set
\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905ptA=\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905pt(\,\Rightarrow A)
and
\exists^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905ptA=\neg\forall^{\diamond}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905pt\neg A
.
Conversely, if

has ULIP, set
\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905pt(\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta)=\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905pt(\bigwedge\Gamma\to\bigvee\Delta)
.
∎

Our strategy to prove ULIP or UIP for a logic is to prove the same property for its corresponding sequent calculus. We will explain the strategy for the harder case of ULIP. However, the proofs also work for UIP and the general constructions must be read as the argument towards proving UIP, as well. We will use this general strategy and the corresponding theorems in Subsection
4.1
to prove ULIP for the logics mentioned at the beginning of this section and in Subsection
4.2
to prove UIP for the logics
\mathsf{CKCEM}
and
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
.

To explain the main strategy, let
G
be the system for one of the logics mentioned at the beginning of this section and recall that the backward application of any of the rules in
G
decreases the weight of the sequent. Using this property and recursion on the weight of the sequents, for any given sequent
S=(\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta)
, any atom
p
and any
\circ\in\{+,-\}
, we first define a
p^{\circ}
-free formula
\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
and then by induction on the weight of
S
, we prove that
\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
meets the conditions in Definition
4.1
. Towards that end, both in the definition of
\forall^{\circ}\hskip-0.85358ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
and in the proof of its properties, we must address all the rules of the system
G
, one by one. To make the presentation uniform, modular, and more clear, we divide the rules of
G
into two families: the rules of
\mathbf{G3W}
and the modal or the conditional rules specific for
G
. The rules in the first class has one of the following forms:

\{\Gamma,\bar{\varphi}_{i}\Rightarrow\bar{\psi}_{i},\Delta\}_{i}

\Gamma,\varphi\Rightarrow\Delta

\{\Gamma,\bar{\varphi}_{i}\Rightarrow\bar{\psi}_{i},\Delta\}_{i}

\Gamma\Rightarrow\varphi,\Delta

where
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are free for all multiset substitutions, and
\bar{\varphi}_{i}
’s and
\bar{\psi}_{i}
’s are multisets of formulas (possibly empty). The rules have the
variable preserving condition
, i.e., given
\circ\in\{+,-\}
, for the left rule

\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{\theta\in\bar{\varphi}_{i}}V^{\circ}(\theta)\cup\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{\theta\in\bar{\psi}_{i}}V^{\diamond}(\theta)\subseteq V^{\circ}(\varphi),

and for the right one

\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{\theta\in\bar{\varphi}_{i}}V^{\diamond}(\theta)\cup\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{\theta\in\bar{\psi}_{i}}V^{\circ}(\theta)\subseteq V^{\circ}(\varphi).

Rather than addressing each rule in
\mathbf{G3W}
, we simply address these two forms that cover all the rules in
\mathbf{G3W}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any sequent
S
, atom
p
and
\circ\in\{+,-\}
, a uniform
\forall^{\circ}_{p}
-interpolant (resp., a uniform
\forall_{p}
-interpolant) of
S
with respect to the set of all axioms of
G

Proof.

We only prove the existence of a
\forall^{\circ}_{p}
-interpolant. The other case is similar. It is just enough to omit all the occurrences of
\circ
,
\diamond
and
\dagger
in the proof.
Let us define the formula
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
in the following way: if
S
is provable, define it as
\top
, otherwise, define it as the disjunction of all
p^{\circ}
-free formulas in
S^{s}
and the negation of all
p^{\diamond}
-free formulas in
S^{a}
. We show that
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
is the uniform
\forall_{p}^{\circ}
-interpolant of
S
with respect to the set
\mathcal{A}
of axioms of
G
. It is easy to see that
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
is
p^{\circ}
-free,
V^{\dagger}(\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS)\subseteq V^{\dagger}(S)
, for
\dagger\in\{+,-\}
and
S\cdot(\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS\Rightarrow)
is provable in
G
. To prove the condition
(ii^{\prime})
in Definition
4.1
, if
S
is provable, then as
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS=\top
, we have
\bar{C}\Rightarrow\forall^{\circ}_{\mathcal{A}}pS,\bar{D}
. If
S
is not provable, then let
S\cdot(\bar{C}\Rightarrow\bar{D})
be an axiom. There are two cases to consider. First, if
S\cdot(\bar{C}\Rightarrow\bar{D})
is in the form
\Gamma,q\Rightarrow q,\Delta
, where

is an atomic formula. Then, if
q\notin\bar{C}
and
q\notin\bar{D}
, we have

and hence the sequent

is provable which contradicts our assumption. Therefore, either
q\in\bar{C}
or
q\in\bar{D}
. If
q\in\bar{C}\cap\bar{D}
, then
\bar{C}\Rightarrow\forall^{\circ}_{\mathcal{A}}pS,\bar{D}
is provable. Hence, we assume either

and
q\notin\bar{D}
or
q\notin\bar{C}
and
q\in\bar{D}
. In the first case, if
q\in\bar{C}
, it is
p^{\circ}
-free and since it occurs in
\Delta
, it is a disjunct in
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
. Hence,
\bar{C}\Rightarrow\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS,\bar{D}

-free and as

, its negation occurs in
\forall^{\circ}\!\!\!_{{\cal A}}\hskip-0.22762ptp\hskip 0.56905ptS
. Therefore
\bar{C}\Rightarrow\forall^{\circ}_{\mathcal{A}}pS,\bar{D}
is provable.

If

is in the form
\Gamma,\bot\Rightarrow\Delta
, then
\bot\in\bar{C}
, because otherwise,
\bot\in\Gamma
and hence
S
will be provable. Now, since
\bot\in\bar{C}
, we have
\bar{C}\Rightarrow\forall^{\circ}_{\mathcal{A}}pS,\bar{D}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\circ}_{p}(S)

-interpolants.” A calculus

Note that in Definition

If a sequent calculus

5
Negative Results

In this section, we present some negative results on the failure of some interpolation properties for a number of modal and conditional logics. First, we prove that none of the logics
\mathsf{EC}
,
\mathsf{ECN}
,
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
enjoy Craig interpolation. Then, we show that for the extensions of
\mathsf{CKCEM}
, uniform Lyndon interpolation property leads to some peculiar behavior of the conditional. As a consequence, we show that the logics
\mathsf{CKCEM}
and
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
do not enjoy uniform Lyndon interpolation property.

We first investigate the modal logics
\mathsf{EC}
and
\mathsf{ECN}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Logics
\mathsf{EC}
and
\mathsf{ECN}
do not have CIP. As a consequence, they do not have UIP or ULIP.

Proof.

Set

\Box(p\wedge q),\Box(\neg q\wedge r)\Rightarrow\Box\bot

\mathbf{GEC}

\mathbf{G3cp}

’s again. Therefore,

(\Sigma,\Box\alpha_{1},\cdots,\Box\alpha_{n}\Rightarrow\Box\beta,\Lambda)

For the conditional logics
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
, let us first introduce the following two translations

and

that map atomic formulas and
\bot
to themselves, commute with the propositional connectives and
(\Box\varphi)^{t}=\top\triangleright\varphi^{t}
and

, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
and
\theta,\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\triangleright}
. Note that
(\varphi^{t})^{s}=\varphi
,

, and

, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\triangleright}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

(i)

If
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\varphi
then
\mathsf{CEC}\vdash\varphi^{t}
.

(ii)

If
\mathsf{CEC}\vdash\psi
then
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\psi^{s}
.

The same also holds for the pair
\mathsf{ECN}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
.

Proof.

The proof is by a simple induction on the length of the proofs. As it is easy, we will only spell out one case. Suppose in
(i)
, we have
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\varphi
and we want to show that
\varphi^{t}
is provable in
\mathsf{CEC}
, where
\varphi
is an instance of the axiom
(C)
. By assumption,
\varphi
is of the form
\Box A\wedge\Box B\to\Box(A\wedge B)
, where the formulas
A
and
B
are in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
. By definition, as
t
commutes with the propositional connectives,
\varphi^{t}
will be of the form
(\top\triangleright A^{t})\wedge(\top\triangleright B^{t})\to(\top\triangleright A^{t}\wedge B^{t})
. The latter is an instance of the axiom
(CC)
, as the formulas
A^{t}
and
B^{t}
are in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\triangleright}
. Therefore,
\varphi^{t}
is provable in
\mathsf{CEC}
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Logics
\mathsf{CEC}
and
\mathsf{CECN}
do not enjoy Craig interpolation.
As a consequence, they do not have uniform or uniform Lyndon interpolation property.

Proof.

We prove the claim for
\mathsf{CEC}
. The other is similar. Assume
\mathsf{CEC}
has CIP. We prove that
\mathsf{EC}
also enjoys CIP which contradicts Theorem
5.1
.
Set
\varphi=\Box(\neg q\wedge r)
and
\psi=\Box(p\wedge q)\to\Box\bot
, as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, where it was shown that
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\varphi\to\psi
, while there is no formula
\theta
such that
V(\theta)\subseteq\{q\}
and both formulas
\varphi\to\theta
and
\theta\to\psi
are provable in
\mathsf{EC}
.
Since
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\varphi\to\psi
, by the part
(i)
of Theorem
5.2
, we have
\mathsf{CEC}\vdash\varphi^{t}\to\psi^{t}
. Hence, using the assumption that
\mathsf{CEC}
has CIP, there is a formula
\chi\in\mathcal{L}_{\triangleright}
such that
V(\chi)\subseteq V(\varphi^{t})\cap V(\psi^{t})=V(\varphi)\cap V(\psi)
and
\mathsf{CEC}\vdash\varphi^{t}\to\chi
and
\mathsf{CEC}\vdash\chi\to\psi^{t}
. Now, apply the translation
s
. By the part
(ii)
of Theorem
5.2
, we have
\mathsf{EC}\vdash(\varphi^{t})^{s}\to\chi^{s}
and
\mathsf{EC}\vdash\chi^{s}\to(\psi^{t})^{s}
. As
(\varphi^{t})^{s}=\varphi
,
(\psi^{t})^{s}=\psi
, and
V(\chi^{s})\subseteq V(\chi)
, the formula
\chi^{s}
would be the interpolant for

in

, which is a contradiction.
∎

Now, to prove the last contribution of this section, namely that the logics
\mathsf{CKCEM}
and
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
do not enjoy uniform Lyndon interpolation property, first, let us make the following observation on the peculiarity of the combination of uniform Lyndon interpolation property and the axiom
(CEM)
:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
L\supseteq\mathsf{CKCEM}
be a logic. Then, if
L
enjoys uniform Lyndon interpolation property, then
L\vdash(p\triangleright r)\vee(q\triangleright\neg r)
.

Proof.

Let
p
,
q
and
r
be three distinct atoms. Using the axiom
(CEM)
, we have
\mathsf{CKCEM}\vdash(q\triangleright r)\vee(q\triangleright\neg r)
, and therefore

L\vdash(q\triangleright r)\vee(q\triangleright\neg r)
. Then,
L\vdash\neg(q\triangleright r)\to(q\triangleright\neg r)
. Note that
V^{+}(q\triangleright\neg r)=\varnothing
and
V^{-}(\neg(q\triangleright r))=\{r\}
. Set
\varphi=\forall^{-}q\,(q\triangleright\neg r)
. By the definition of uniform Lyndon interpolation,
\varphi
is
q^{-}
-free,
L\vdash\varphi\to(q\triangleright\neg r)
and
V^{+}(\varphi)\subseteq V^{+}(q\triangleright\neg r)=\varnothing
. By the latter,
\varphi
is
q^{+}
-free. Hence,
\varphi
is
q
-free which means that
q
does not occur in
\varphi
. Again, by the definition of uniform Lyndon interpolation and the facts that
\neg(q\triangleright r)
is
q^{-}
-free and
L\vdash\neg(q\triangleright r)\to(q\triangleright\neg r)
, we have
L\vdash\neg(q\triangleright r)\to\varphi
, using the property
(ii)
in Definition
2.4
. As
\varphi
is
q
-free, we can substitute
q
by
p
, keeping
\varphi
intact. Hence,
L\vdash\neg(p\triangleright r)\to\varphi
. Finally, as
L\vdash\varphi\to(q\triangleright\neg r)
, we have
L\vdash\neg(p\to r)\to(q\triangleright\neg r)
. Hence,
L\vdash(p\triangleright r)\vee(q\triangleright\neg r)
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Logics
\mathsf{CKCEM}
and
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
do not enjoy uniform Lyndon interpolation property.

Proof.

Let
L=\mathsf{CKCEM}
(resp.,
\mathsf{CKCEMID}
) and
G
be the corresponding sequent system for
L
provided in Section
2
. As
L\supseteq\mathsf{CKCEM}
, by Theorem
5.4
, if
L
enjoys uniform Lyndon interpolation property, then
L\vdash(p\triangleright r)\vee(q\triangleright\neg r)
which implies the provability of the sequent
(\,\Rightarrow p\triangleright r,q\triangleright\neg r)
in
G
. Call this proof
{\mathcal{D}}
. As the sequent is not an axiom, the last rule in
{\mathcal{D}}
is either the weakening rule or
(CKCEM)
(resp.,
(CKCEMID)
). In the latter case, the proof looks like the following

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}

p\Leftrightarrow q

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime\prime}

\Rightarrow r,\neg r

CKCEM

\Rightarrow p\triangleright r,q\triangleright\neg r

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}

p\Leftrightarrow q

{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime\prime}

p,q\Rightarrow r,\neg r

CKCEMID

\Rightarrow p\triangleright r,q\triangleright\neg r

By a simple combinatorial case check on the possible cases of the last rule in the proof
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
, we can easily show that such a proof cannot exist. Therefore, the last rule in the proof
{\mathcal{D}}
cannot be
(CKCEM)
(resp.,
(CKCEMID)
). In the case that the last rule in
{\mathcal{D}}
is the weakening rule, either
\Rightarrow(p\triangleright r)
or
\Rightarrow(q\triangleright\neg r)
is provable in
G
.
As none of these sequents is an axiom and the last rule of their proofs cannot be the weakening rule, then the last rule must be
(CKCEM)
(resp.,
(CKCEMID)
). Therefore, since in the premises of the rule
(CKCEM)
(resp.,
(CKCEMID)
), both
I
and
J
are empty, then we either have
G\vdash(\,\Rightarrow r)
or
G\vdash(\,\Rightarrow\neg r)
(resp.,
G\vdash(p\Rightarrow r)
or
G\vdash(q\Rightarrow\neg r)
). Again, it is easy to check that none of theses cases can take place.
∎

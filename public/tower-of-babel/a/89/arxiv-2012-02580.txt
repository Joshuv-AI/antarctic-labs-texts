On Extending Unipotent Representations to their Stabilizers
Matthias Klupsch

Abstract
We show that irreducible unipotent representations of split Levi subgroups of finite groups of Lie type extend to their stabilizers inside the normalizer of the given Levi subgroup. For this purpose, we extend the multiplicity-freeness theorem from regular embeddings to arbitrary isotypies.



1
Introduction

Given a normal subgroup
N
of a finite group
G
, one obtains
an action of the quotient group
G/N
on the set of irreducible
representations of
N
. If an irreducible representation of
N

is fixed under this action, it is straightforward to ask if
this representation might extend to a representation of
G
.

That this is not always the case is nicely explained by Schur’s
theory of projective representations.
The obstruction for an irreducible representation of
N
to extend
to a representation of
G
lies in (the cohomology class of) a certain

2
-cocycle associated with this representation.

When considering representations of finite groups of Lie type,
the question of whether irreducible cuspidal representations of split Levi subgroups
extend to their stabilizers inside the normalizer of the given Levi subgroup
is of much interest (cf.
[
4
, Cor. 3.13]
).

In the case of representations in characteristic
0
, this question was answered by Lusztig and Geck to the affirmative (see
[
9
, Thm. 8.6.]
and
[
3
]
).

Our goal with this paper is to better understand which irreducible representations of Levi subgroups extend to their stabilizers.
Among the irreducible representations of finite groups of Lie type, we have singled out the unipotent ones (in arbitrary non-defining characteristic) to serve as a first working example.

In fact, we shall show that every irreducible unipotent representation of a split Levi subgroup of a finite group of Lie type extends to its stabilizer.

In order to reduce this extension problem to a situation we can handle easily
we need to generalize some results on unipotent representations especially
with regards to their behaviour concerning restriction along isotypies.
In particular, we show that restriction along isotypies maps arbitrary irreducible
representations to multiplicity-free representations (known previously only for regular embeddings, cf.
[
6
, 1.7.15]
).

This way, we are able to reduce the extension problem for unipotent representations
to a problem which only concerns semidirect products with wreath products
which is in some sense a generalization of
[
4
, Prop. 4.3]
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. First we describe the purely representation theoretic problem of extending an irreducible representation
to a semidirect product of a group with a wreath product.

Then we shall show that the normalizers of Levi subgroups are of this form
modulo a central subgroup.

Afterwards, we prove that restriction along isotypies of irreducible representations
leads to multiplicity-free representations reducing this problem to the already known multiplicity-freeness theorem
[
6
, 1.7.15]
for regular embeddings. Specializing this to unipotent
representations, we find that the restriction of an irreducible unipotent
representation along an isotypy remains irreducible. This was already well-known
in characteristic
0
(see
[
6
, 2.3.14]
).

Finally, we will be able to solve the extension problem for irreducible unipotent representation.

2
Some Clifford theory

2.1.

In this section, we will consider the property of extending
representations to their stabilizer and establish connections
to multiplicity-freeness of restriction functors.

2.2.

Let
G
be a finite group,
N\trianglelefteq G
a normal subgroup of
G
and
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\ell\geq 0
.
We shall denote by
\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(G)
the set of irreducible
k
-characters of
G
.

2.3.

The
k
-linear categories
kN
-mod and
kG
-mod of finitely generated
kN
-modules and

kG
-modules are related via
induction and restriction functors

\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{N}:kN\mathrm{-mod}\to kG\mathrm{-mod}

and

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}:kG\mathrm{-mod}\to kN\mathrm{-mod}

which are biadjoint exact functors.
Recall that Clifford theory tells us that
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}
maps semisimple modules to semisimple modules.

2.4.

For any
g\in G
, conjugation with
g
defines an automorphism of
N
and thus
an autoequivalence of categories

\mathrm{ad}(g):kN\mathrm{-mod}\to kN\mathrm{-mod}.

If
M
is a
kN
-module, then
n\in N
acts on
\mathrm{ad}(g)M
like
g^{-1}ng
acts on
M
.
We denote by

S_{G}(M)=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(M)=\{g\in G\>|\>\mathrm{ad}(g)M\cong M\}

the stabilizer of
M
in
G
.

Lemma 2.5.

Let
X_{0}
be a simple
kN
-module. The following
statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists a simple
kG
-module
X
for which

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
is multiplicity-free and has
X_{0}
as a direct summand.

(ii)

There exists a simple
kS_{G}(X_{0})
-module

X_{1}
such that
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X_{1}\cong X_{0}
.

In fact, if
X
is as in (i), then any simple submodule of
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X
whose restriction to
N
has
X_{0}
as a direct summand satisfies (ii) and
any
kS_{G}(X_{0})
-module
X_{1}
as in (ii) satisfies that
\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X_{1}
is simple and satisfies (i).

Proof.

Suppose first, that (i) holds and let
X_{1}
be a simple
kS_{G}(X_{0})
-submodule of
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X
for which
X_{0}
is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X_{1}
.

By usual Clifford theory,
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X_{1}
is a direct sum of simple
kN
-modules which are conjugate to
X_{0}
under
S_{G}(X_{0})
, hence isomorphic to
X_{0}
.
By (i), however,
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X_{1}
is multiplicity-free as a direct summand of a multiplicity-free module. We thus obtain (ii).

Let us assume (ii) now and let
X=\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X_{1}
.
Using the Mackey formula, we see that

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X\cong\bigoplus_{g\in S_{G}(X_{0})\backslash G/S_{G}(X_{0})}\mathrm{ad}(g)X_{0}

which is clearly multiplicity-free.

If
T
is a simple submodule of
X
, then
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}T
is a direct sum of conjugates of
X_{0}
with each conjugate occurring at least once. Since
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
is multiplicity free, it follows that we have
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X=\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}T
and so
X=T

is simple.
∎

2.6.

A very prominent example for multiplicity-freeness
in the context of finite groups of Lie type is the well-known
result that restriction along regular embeddings is multiplicity-free (see
[
6
, 1.7.15]
). We shall later return to this result and generalize it to arbitrary isotypies. For the moment, we just note that in this situation, the relevant quotient group
G/N
is abelian which makes this special case very important for us. The following result will later be used
to show that unipotent representations in positive characteristic
remain irreducible under restriction along isotypies.

Lemma 2.7.

Suppose that
G/N
is abelian and that
X
is a simple
kG
-module.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The
kN
-module
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
is simple.

(ii)

The
kN
-module
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
is multiplicity-free and for every nontrivial
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(G/N)
we have
\lambda\otimes X\not\cong X

Proof.

Suppose (i) holds and that
f:\lambda\otimes X\to X
is an isomorphism of
kG
-modules for some
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(G/N)
.

It follows that
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}f:\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X\to\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
is an
automorphism of a simple
kN
-module and Schur’s Lemma now implies
that
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}f=\alpha\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X}
for some
\alpha\in k^{*}
.

It follows that we have

\alpha\lambda(g)gx=f(\lambda(g)gx)=gf(x)=\alpha gx

for all
g\in G
and
x\in X
and so
\lambda
is trivial.

Conversely, let us now assume (ii) and let
X_{0}
be a simple direct summand of
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
and
S_{G}(X_{0})\subseteq G
its stabilizer.
Also let
X_{1}
be a simple submodule of
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X
such that
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X_{1}\cong X_{0}
, which exists
by Lemma
2.5.
since we assumed

\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{N}X
to be multiplicity-free.

Moreover, we have
X\cong\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X_{1}
by the same Lemma.
Now, since
G/N
is abelian, the restriction
\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X
is a direct sum of conjugates of
X_{1}
and thus

\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}X\cong kG/S_{G}(X_{0})\otimes X

is semisimple which implies that the order of
G/S_{G}(X_{0})
is prime to
\ell
.

Now, if
S_{G}(X_{0})
were a proper subgroup of
G
, then there would exist a nontrivial
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}_{k}(G/S_{G}(X_{0}))
and we would obtain

\lambda\otimes X\cong\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}(\mathrm{Res}^{G}_{S_{G}(X_{0})}(\lambda)\otimes X_{1})\cong X

contrary to our assumptions. Thus, we have
G=S_{G}(X_{0})
and
X\cong X_{1}
which gives us (i).
∎

Remark 2.8.

Suppose that
G=N\rtimes W
for some subgroup
W\subseteq G
.
Note that the centralizer
C=C_{W}(N)\subseteq W
is a normal subgroup of
G
. We let
G^{\prime}=N\rtimes(W/C)\cong G/C
.

In this situation, if
X_{0}
is a simple
kN
-module, then we have
C\subseteq S_{G}(X_{0})
and
S_{G^{\prime}}(X_{0})=S_{G}(X_{0})/C
.
Moreover, if there exists a simple
kS_{G}^{\prime}(X_{0})
-module
X^{\prime}

such that
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G^{\prime}}(X_{0})}_{N}X^{\prime}\cong X_{0}
, then the inflation
X

of
X^{\prime}
to
G
satisfies
\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X_{0})}_{N}X\cong X_{0}
.

2.9.

In the next section, we shall investigate the structure of the normalizer of a standard Levi subgroup of a finite group of Lie
type. We shall see that the following result on extending
representations for certain semidirect products is exactly
what we need.

Theorem 2.10.

Let
H
and
A
be finite groups with
A
acting on
H
via group automorphisms. For a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we set

G=(H\rtimes A)\wr S_{n}=H^{n}\rtimes(A\wr S_{n})

as well as
N=H^{n}\trianglelefteq G
.

If every simple
kH
-module
X_{0}
extends to a

kS_{H\rtimes A}(X_{0})
-module,
then every simple
kN
-module
X
extends to a
kS_{G}(X)
-module.

Proof.

Let
X
be a simple
kN
-module.
We can write

X=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}

where
X_{i}
is a simple
kH
-module.

We identify
H^{n}
with
H^{\{1,\dots,n\}}
and for a subset
J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
we shall denote by
H^{J}
the subgroup
of
H^{n}
which under the
i
th projection
H^{n}\to H
has image
H
if
i\in J
and
\{1\}
else.

We have the equivalence relation

i\sim j
if and only if
X_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{ad}(a)X_{j}
for some
a\in A
and write
\{1,\dots,n\}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{r}I_{j}
for the associated partition. The stabilizer
S_{G}(X)
is a subgroup of

\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left(H^{I_{j}}\rtimes(A\wr S_{I_{j}})\right)

where we denote the
i
th factor by
G_{I_{j}}
.

Let us set
X_{I_{j}}=\bigotimes_{i\in I_{j}}X_{i}
so that we have

X=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}X_{I_{j}}
.
It follows that we have
S_{G}(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}S_{G_{I_{j}}}(X_{I_{j}})
.
Clearly, there exists a
kS_{G}(X)
-module
Y
with

\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G}(X)}_{N}Y\cong X
if only if for every

1\leq i\leq r
we have

\mathrm{Res}^{S_{G_{I_{j}}}(X_{I_{j}})}_{N}Y_{I_{j}}\cong X_{I_{j}}

for some
kS_{G_{I_{j}}}(X_{I_{j}})
-module
Y_{I_{j}}
.

We may thus assume that
r=1
.
For each
2\leq i\leq n
let
a_{i}\in A
be such that

\mathrm{ad}(a_{i})X_{i}\cong X_{1}
. With
a=(a_{i})_{i}\in A^{n}
,
we get
\mathrm{ad}(a)X\cong X_{1}^{\otimes n}
.

Clearly, if
\mathrm{ad}(a)X
extends to a
kS_{G}(\mathrm{ad}(a)X)
-module,
then
X
extends to a
kS_{G}(X)
-module.
We may thus assume that
X\cong X_{1}^{\otimes n}
.
Note that we then have
S_{G}(X)=S_{H\rtimes A}(X_{1})\wr S_{n}
.

Let
\widetilde{X_{1}}
be an extension of
X_{1}
to
S_{H\rtimes A}(X_{1})
. The

k(H\rtimes A)^{n}
-module
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{X_{1}}^{\otimes n}
becomes
a
kS_{G}(X)
-module in a natural way and we are done.
∎

3
Representatives for the relative Weyl group

3.1
Root data and isotypies

3.1.

Recall that a root datum is a quadruple
\mathcal{R}=(X,\Phi,X^{\vee},\Phi^{\vee})
where
X
and
X^{\vee}
are free abelian groups of finite rank together with a perfect pairing
X\times X^{\vee}\to\mathbb{Z}

\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
are finite subsets of
X
and
X^{\vee}
, respectively, together with a bijection
\Phi\to\Phi^{\vee},\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}

such that

(i)

\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=2
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,

(ii)

2\alpha\notin\Phi
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,

(iii)

\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
are invariant under the simple reflections
s_{\alpha}:X\to X
and
s_{\alpha}^{\vee}:X^{\vee}\to X^{\vee}
defined
by

s_{\alpha}(\chi)=\chi-\langle\chi,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\alpha

and

s_{\alpha}^{\vee}(\gamma)=\gamma-\langle\alpha,\gamma\rangle\alpha^{\vee},

respectively.

Given a set
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
of simple roots, the quintuple

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=(X,\Phi,X^{\vee},\Phi^{\vee},\Delta)
is called a
based root datum
associated with
\mathcal{R}
.

3.2.

Let
\mathcal{R}=(X,\Phi,X^{\vee},\Phi^{\vee})
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=(X^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime},X^{\prime\vee},\Phi^{\prime\vee})
be two root data and
p
a prime number.
Like in
[
11
]
, a
p
-morphism of root
data
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{R}
is a triple
(f,q,\tau)
where
f:X^{\prime}\to X

is a morphism of groups,
q:\Phi\to p^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
a map and

\tau:\Phi\to\Phi^{\prime}
a bijection such that

f(\tau(\alpha))=q(\alpha)\alpha

for all
\alpha\in\Phi
and

f^{\vee}(\alpha^{\vee})=q(\alpha)\tau(\alpha)^{\vee}

where
f^{\vee}:X^{\prime\vee}\to X^{\vee}
is the transposed map of
f

with respect to the perfect pairings between
X
and
X^{\vee}

as well as
X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime\vee}
.
As the map
f
already determines
q
and
\tau
, we shall also sometimes call
f
a
p
-morphism of root data.

3.3.

If
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
and
\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi^{\prime}
are sets
of simple roots such that
\tau(\Delta)=\Delta^{\prime}
, then we shall say that

(f,q,\tau)
(or simply
f
) is a
p
-morphism of based root data

\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}
.

3.4.

We shall write
\mathrm{Hom}_{p}(\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\mathcal{R})
for the set of
p
-morphisms of root data
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{R}
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}},\widehat{\mathcal{R}})
for the set of
p
-morphisms of based root data
\widehat{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
.

3.5.

In the following, all our algebraic groups will be defined over
\mathbb{F}=\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, the algebraic closure of the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p
elements.
The algebraic groups
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
are just the additive group
(\mathbb{F},+)
and the multiplicative group
(\mathbb{F}-\{0\},\cdot)
, respectively.

3.6.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group and
\mathbf{T}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
a maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
.
The root datum of
\mathbf{G}
associated to
\mathbf{T}

will be denoted by

\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})=(X(\mathbf{T}),\Phi(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T}),X^{\vee}(\mathbf{T}),\Phi^{\vee}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T}))=(X,\Phi,X^{\vee},\Phi^{\vee}).

Here,
X(\mathbf{T})
is the character group of
\mathbf{T}
and
X^{\vee}(\mathbf{T})
the cocharacter group.

If
\mathbf{B}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is a Borel subgroup containing
\mathbf{T}
and
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
the corresponding set of simple roots, we let
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T})
denote the corresponding based root system.

3.7.

For any element
g\in\mathbf{G}
conjugation with
g
will be denoted by
c_{g}:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
.

3.8.

For every
\alpha\in\Phi
, there exists a morphism

u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}
where
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is a minimal connected unipotent subgroup of
\mathbf{G}

normalized by
\mathbf{T}
such that

tu_{\alpha}(x)t^{-1}=u_{\alpha}(\alpha(t)x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
t\in\mathbf{T}
.
For
\mathbf{B}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
a Borel subgroup containing
\mathbf{T}
and
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
its corresponding set of simple roots fixing morphisms
\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
as above,
we shall call the tuple
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})

a
pinning
of
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
.

3.9.

Let
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be another connected reductive group and
\varphi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
a morphism
of algebraic groups. Recall that
\varphi
is called an
isotypy

if
\varphi(\mathbf{G})
contains the derived subgroup
[\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}]
and
\ker(\varphi)
is central.
Since we have
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})[\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{G}^{\prime}]
, it follows that
one has
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})\varphi(\mathbf{G})
in this case.

3.10.

Moreover, if
\mathbf{T}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{G}^{\prime}

are maximal tori such that
\varphi(\mathbf{T})\subseteq\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
,
then we shall write
\varphi:(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
. If
\varphi
is an isotypy
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
, then we obtain a
p
-morphism of root data

\mathcal{R}(\varphi)=(f_{\varphi},q,\tau):\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})

where
f_{\varphi}(\chi^{\prime})=\chi^{\prime}\circ\varphi
for
\chi^{\prime}\in X(\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
.
Clearly, if
\psi^{\prime}:(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime\prime})

is a second isotypy, then we have
\mathcal{R}(\psi\circ\varphi)=\mathcal{R}(\varphi)\circ\mathcal{R}(\psi)
.

3.11.

Furthermore, if
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})
and
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime},\{u_{\alpha^{\prime}}\}_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}})
are pinnings of
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})
and
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})
, respectively, we shall say that
\varphi
is compatible with these pinnings and write

\varphi:(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime},\{u_{\alpha^{\prime}}\})
if we have
\varphi(\mathbf{B})\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
and
\varphi(\mathbf{T})\subseteq\mathbf{T}^{\prime}

as well as

\varphi(u_{\alpha}(x))=u_{\tau(\alpha)}(x^{q(\alpha)})

for all
x\in\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\alpha\in\Delta
.

3.12.

We write
\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T}),(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime}))

for the set of isotypies
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime})

and likewise

\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}),(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime},\{u_{\alpha}^{\prime}\}_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}}))

for the set of isotypies described
in
3.11.

Proposition 3.13.

(
[
11

)

The map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T}),(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Hom}_{p}(\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime}),\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})),

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{R}(\varphi)

\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{1})=\mathcal{R}(\varphi_{2})

The map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}((\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}),(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\{u_{\alpha}^{\prime}\}_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\prime}}))\to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{p}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},\mathbf{T}^{\prime}),\widehat{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{T})),

\displaystyle\varphi\to

Remark 3.14.

Remark 3.15.

For
i=1,2
, let
\varphi_{i}:(\mathbf{G}_{i},\mathbf{T}_{i})\to(\mathbf{G}_{i+1},\mathbf{T}_{i+1})
be an isotypy between connected reductive groups and set
\varphi_{3}=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}

Suppose that we have pinnings
(\mathbf{G}_{i},\mathbf{B}_{i},\mathbf{T}_{i},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta_{i}})
of
(\mathbf{G}_{i},\mathbf{T}_{i})
(

Then if two of the three morphisms

3.2
Steinberg morphisms and isotypies

3.16.

Recall from
[
6
, 1.4.1 – 1.4.5]
that a Frobenius morphism of
\mathbf{G}
is
a morphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
of algebraic groups that encapsulates an

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational structure on
\mathbf{G}
for some
p
-power
q
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points are the set of fixed points
\mathbf{G}^{F}
.
More generally, a Steinberg morphism of
\mathbf{G}
is a
morphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
of algebraic groups
for which some power
F^{m}
is a Frobenius morphism.
Steinberg morphisms are bijections and thus, in particular,
isotypies.

3.17.

We say that a pinning

(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})

is compatible with a Steinberg morphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}

if
F
is an isotypy

(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})\to(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})
.
Note that
\mathbf{T}
is then a maximally split torus
in the sense of
[
6
, 1.4.10]
.

Lemma 3.18.

Let

be a Steinberg morphism.
Then there exists an

-stable maximal torus
\mathbf{T}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
and a pinning

of

.

Proof.

By
[
6
, 1.4.10]
there exists an
F
-stable maximal torus

and an
F
-stable

containing

.
Let
(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})
be some pinning. It follows from Remark (

) that there exists

such that we have

and

associated with the isotypy

∎

Lemma 3.19.

3.3
Normalizers of standard Levi subgroups

3.21.

As before, we fix a connected reductive group
\mathbf{G}

with maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
and assume that we have a Steinberg morphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}

(\mathbf{G},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{T},\{u_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta})

We denote the corresponding based root system by

will be identified with

3.22.

For any closed
F
-stable subgroup
\mathbf{H}\subseteq\mathbf{G}

F
-fixed points
\mathbf{H}^{F}

by the corresponding non-boldfaced letter

3.23.

The Steinberg morphism

induces an automorphism of

which will also be denoted by
F
.
The group

of

-fixed points of

of standard parabolic subgroups

where

is a
\tau
-orbit (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 1]

3.24.

Furthermore, the Steinberg morphism

induces a

-endomorphism of based root data
(F,q,\tau)
on

and we have

\tau(w\alpha)=F(w)\tau(\alpha)

for all

3.25.

For any subset

we have a standard parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}_{I}
with Levi decomposition

and all three groups are

-stable if

is
\tau
-stable. The standard Levi subgroup

3.26.

We shall identify the Weyl group of
\mathbf{L}_{I}
with the standard parabolic subgroup
W_{I}
of

generated by the reflections associated with the simple roots of
I
.
It follows from
3.23.
that if

is
\tau
-stable, then
W_{I}^{F}
is a standard parabolic subgroup of
W^{F}

3.27.

The Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}_{I}
has finite index in its normalizer
N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L}_{I})
and one has an isomorphism

N_{W}(W_{I})/W_{I}\cong N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L}_{I})/\mathbf{L}_{I}

as every representative
{\dot{w}}\in N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
of an element

is an element of
N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{L}_{I})
.

3.28.

Moreover,
[
7
, Cor. 3]
tells us that the group
N_{W}(W_{I})
is a semidirect product

N_{W}(W_{I})=W_{I}\rtimes N_{W}(I)

where
N_{W}(I)=\{w\in W\>|\>w(I)=I\}
. Suppose now that
I
is
\tau
-stable.
Taking
F
-fixed points, we obtain

N_{W^{F}}(W_{I})={W_{I}}^{F}\rtimes N_{W^{F}}(I)

where
N_{W^{F}}(I)=\{w\in W^{F}\>|\>w(I)=I\}=W^{F}\cap N_{W}(I)

and so

N_{W^{F}}(W_{I})/{W_{I}}^{F}\cong N_{G}(\mathbf{L}_{I})/{L}_{I}.

Recall that we write
G
for the
F
-fixed points
\mathbf{G}^{F}
and similarly
L_{I}
for
\mathbf{L}_{I}
.

3.29.

On the other hand,
W_{I}^{F}
is a standard parabolic subgroup
of
W^{F}
and thus
[
7
, Cor. 3]
implies that
N_{W^{F}}(W_{I}^{F})

N_{W^{F}}(W_{I}^{F})=W_{I}^{F}\rtimes N_{W^{F}}(I,\tau)

where, in view of
3.23.
, the group

is defined to consist of those

for which for every

ww_{J}w^{-1}=w_{J^{\prime}}
for some
\tau

Lemma 3.30.

We have

Proof.

It is clear that
N_{W^{F}}(I)\subseteq N_{W^{F}}(I,\tau)
.
To show equality, it suffices to show that

.

So let

and
\alpha\in I

Let

be the
\tau
-orbit of

\alpha

\tau

We then have

Corollary 3.31.

We have

4.1.

In the following we consider an algebraically closed field

\mathbf{G}
will be assumed to be equipped with a Steinberg morphism

4.2.

Let

We shall write

4.4.

Most of the properties of an isotypic morphism
\varphi

and thus from the induced maps between character and cocharacter

-torsion if and only if

\varphi

It is clear from the definition of isotypies that
it suffices to consider the case
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}=\mathbf{T}^{\prime}

We consider the factorization

We have thus obtained (i), (ii) and (iii) and (iv) is just
a combination of (i) and (iii).
∎

4.6.

The concept of duality (see
[
6
, 1.5.17]
) plays a very important role in the context of isotypies.
From the description via
p
-morphisms of root data and the Existence Theorem
[
11
, Theorem 3.8]
, it follows
that every isotypy
\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}

admits a dual morphism
\varphi^{*}:G^{\prime*}\to G^{*}

such that
f_{\varphi}
corresponds to
f_{\varphi^{*}}^{\vee}
under duality and likewise
f_{\varphi}^{\vee}
to
f_{\varphi^{*}}
.
In accordance with Proposition
3.13.
,
a dual morphism is only unique up to conjugation.

Corollary 4.7.

Let
\varphi:(G,F)\to(G^{\prime},F^{\prime})
be an isotypy and
let
G^{*}
and
G^{\prime*}
be groups in duality with
G
and
G^{\prime}
,
respectively. Let
F^{*}:G^{*}\to G^{*}
and
F^{\prime*}:G^{\prime*}\to G^{\prime*}

be Steinberg morphisms dual to
F
and
F^{\prime}
, respectively.
Then there exists a dual morphism
\varphi^{*}:(G^{\prime*},F^{\prime*})\to(G^{*},F^{*})
of
\varphi
and
the following statements hold:

1.

The kernel of
\varphi
is connected if and only if the kernel of
\varphi^{*}
is connected.

2.

\varphi
is surjective if and only if
\ker(\varphi^{*})
is finite.

3.

\varphi
is injective if and only if
\varphi^{*}
is surjective and
\ker(\varphi^{*})
is connected.

Proof.

Once we have established the existence of
\varphi^{*}
,
Lemma

4.5.

gives us the remaining claims.

If
\psi:G^{\prime*}\to G^{*}
is any dual morphism of
\varphi
,
then
F^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ F
implies

c_{t^{\prime}}\circ\psi\circ F^{\prime*}=F^{*}\circ\psi
for some

t^{\prime}\in T^{*}
where
T^{*}\subseteq G^{*}
is the dual torus of
T
.

By
[
6
, 1.4.8]
, there exists
t\in T^{*}

such that
t^{\prime}=F^{*}(t^{-1})t
, so that
\varphi^{*}=c_{t}\circ\psi:(G^{\prime*},F^{\prime*})\to(G^{*},F^{*})
is a dual morphism of
\varphi
.
∎

4.8.

If
\varphi:(\mathbf{G},F)\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
is an isotypy, we have an induced morphism

\varphi:G\to G^{\prime}
and so for every field
k
, we have
induction and restriction functors

\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}:kG^{\prime}\mathrm{-mod}\to kG\mathrm{-mod}

and

\mathrm{Ind}_{\varphi}:kG\mathrm{-mod}\to kG^{\prime}\mathrm{-mod}

which are biadjoint.

4.9.

Note that
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
can be viewed as the composition
of restriction from
G^{\prime}
to
\varphi(G)
with inflation from

\varphi(G)\cong G/\ker(\varphi)^{F}
to
G
.
In particular,
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
maps semisimple modules to semisimple ones.

4.10.

If
\varphi^{\prime}:(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime},F^{\prime\prime})
is another
isotypy, then we have transitivity

\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi}=\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}\circ\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi^{\prime}}

of restriction and similarly for induction.

4.11.

The following result is a generalization of
[
6
, 1.7.15]
from regular embeddings to arbitrary isotypies.
We shall abbreviate its conclusion by saying that
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
is multiplicity-free.

Theorem 4.12.

Let
\varphi:(\mathbf{G},F)\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
be an isotypy and
X
a simple
kG^{\prime}
-module. Then
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X

is multiplicity-free.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{T}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be a maximally split maximal torus and let
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
be the maximally split torus
of
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
given by
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})^{\circ}\varphi(\mathbf{T})
.

Let
(\mathbf{G}^{*},F^{*})
and
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime*},F^{\prime*})
be groups in duality
with
(\mathbf{G},F)
and
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
with
\mathbf{T}^{*}\subseteq\mathbf{G}^{*}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime*}\subseteq\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}
the maximally split tori corresponding to
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
, respectively.

By
4.7.
, there exists a dual morphism
\varphi^{*}:(\mathbf{G}^{\prime*},F^{\prime*})\to(\mathbf{G}^{*},F^{*})
of
\varphi
.

We consider the factorization

\textstyle{\mathbf{T}^{\prime*}\times_{\ker(\varphi^{*})}\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\varphi^{*}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{G}^{*}}

where the morphisms are given by

\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}\to\mathbf{T}^{*}\times_{\ker(\varphi^{*})}\mathbf{G}^{\prime*},g^{\prime}\mapsto(1,g^{\prime})

and

\mathbf{T}^{\prime*}\times_{\ker(\varphi^{*})}\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}\to\varphi(\mathbf{G}^{\prime*}),(t^{\prime},g^{\prime})\mapsto\varphi^{*}(g^{\prime})

as well as the inclusion
\varphi^{*}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime*})\to\mathbf{G}^{*}
.

All three of these are isotypies compatible with the
naturally induced Steinberg morphisms.

Note that the first and third is an isomorphism onto its image
while the second one is a morphism with connected kernel.

By duality, we obtain a factorization

\textstyle{\mathbf{G}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{\mathbf{G}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{G}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{G^{\prime}}}

where

\psi_{1}:(\mathbf{G},F)\to(\mathbf{G}_{1},F_{2})
and
\psi_{2}:(\mathbf{G}_{2},F_{2})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})

are surjective with connected kernel and
\psi:(\mathbf{G}_{1},F_{1})\to(\mathbf{G}_{2},F_{2})
is injective by Corollary (
4.7.
).

In particular, the functors
\mathrm{Res}_{\psi_{1}}
and
\mathrm{Res}_{\psi_{2}}
are just inflations and map simple objects to simple ones.

It thus suffices to show that
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}
is multiplicity-free, when
\varphi
is injective. In this case,

\varphi:G\to\varphi(G)=\varphi(\mathbf{G})^{F}
is an isomorphism, so that we may as well replace
\mathbf{G}
by
\varphi(\mathbf{G})
and thus assume that
\varphi
is an isomorphism
onto its image.

Now, let
\iota:(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime},F^{\prime\prime})
be a regular embedding and note that
\iota\circ\varphi
is then a regular embedding, too.
By
[
6
, 1.7.15]
, we know that
\mathrm{Res}_{\iota\circ\varphi}X^{\prime}
is multiplicity-free for every simple
kG^{\prime\prime}
-module
X^{\prime}
. As
X^{\prime}
runs through the isomorphism classes of simple
kG^{\prime\prime}
-modules, every simple
kG^{\prime}
-module
X
occurs as a direct summand in some
\mathrm{Res}_{\iota}X^{\prime}
.
It follows that
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X
is a summand of
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}\mathrm{Res}_{\iota}X^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{Res}_{\iota\circ\varphi}X^{\prime}
and thus multiplicity-free.
∎

5
Unipotent representations

5.1.

From now on let
(K,R,k)
denote an
\ell
-modular system
which is split for all finite groups under consideration.
In particular, all the results from previous sections (where
k
was assumed to be algebraically closed) descent to this situation. We let
\Lambda\in\{K,k\}
.

5.2.

Let
(\mathbf{G},F)
be a connected reductive group and
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
a Steinberg morphism.
We have a decomposition

\mathrm{Irr}_{K}(G)=\bigsqcup_{[s]}\mathcal{E}(G,[s])

of the set of irreducible characters into Lusztig series
(cf.
[
6
, 2.6.2]
) as well as a decomposition

\mathrm{IBr}_{\ell}(G)=\bigsqcup_{[s]}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}(G,[s])

of the set of irreducible Brauer characters into
\ell
-modular
Lusztig series (cf.
[
2
, 9.12]
).
Here, the first union is over
G^{*}
-conjugacy classes of
F^{*}
-stable semisimple elements in
a group
(\mathbf{G}^{*},F^{*})
in duality with
(\mathbf{G},F)
.
The second union is over
G^{*}
-conjugacy classes of
F^{*}
-stable semisimple
\ell^{\prime}
-elements.

5.3.

Representations and modules admitting characters in
\mathcal{E}(G,[1])

or
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}(G,[1])
are called
unipotent
.

5.4.

The two decompositions are compatible with reduction modulo
\ell
. In fact, if
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(G,[s])
and
\psi\in\mathrm{IBr}_{\ell}(G)
is a constituent of the reduction
\widehat{\chi}
of

\chi
modulo
\ell
, then
\psi\in\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}(G,[s_{\ell^{\prime}}])

where
s_{\ell^{\prime}}
denotes the
\ell^{\prime}
-part of
s
.

5.5.

Moreover, the decomposition into Lusztig series is compatible with the action of the group of linear characters.
In fact, every linear character
\lambda:G\to K^{*}
whose kernel contains
every
F
-stable unipotent element corresponds to a unique

z_{\lambda}\in Z(G^{*})
(cf.
[
6
, 2.5.20]
) with the same order as
\lambda

and one has bijections

\mathcal{E}(G,[s])\to\mathcal{E}(G,[zs]),\chi\mapsto\lambda\chi

and

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}(G,[s_{\ell^{\prime}}])\to\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell}(G,[z_{\ell^{\prime}}s_{\ell^{\prime}}]),\psi\mapsto\widehat{\lambda}\psi

where the first one comes from
[
6
, 2.5.21]

and the second is a consequence of the first and

5.4.

Lemma 5.6.

Let
\varphi:(\mathbf{G},F)\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
be an isotypy.
For every simple unipotent
\Lambda G^{\prime}
-module
X^{\prime}
, its restriction
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}
is a simple unipotent
\Lambda G
-module and every simple unipotent
\Lambda G
-module
X

is of the form
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}
for a simple unipotent
\Lambda G^{\prime}
-module
X^{\prime}
which is unique up to isomorphism.

In particular,
Z(G)
acts trivially
on every simple unipotent
\Lambda G
-module.

Proof.

These statements are well-known for the case
\Lambda=K

(see
[
6
, 2.3.14]
).
We already know from
4.12.
, the

\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}
is multiplicity-free.
Since

\varphi(G)
contains all
F^{\prime}
-stable unipotent
elements of
G^{\prime}
, the kernel of
every
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\Lambda}(G^{\prime}/\varphi(G))
contains
all
F^{\prime}
-stable unipotent
elements of
G^{\prime}
.
It follows from
5.5.
that
\lambda\otimes X^{\prime}
is not unipotent
unless
\lambda
is trivial.

It follows that
2.7.

applies and so
\mathrm{Res}^{G^{\prime}}_{\varphi(G)}X^{\prime}
is simple.
Since
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}
is just the inflation of

\mathrm{Res}^{G^{\prime}}_{\varphi(G)}X^{\prime}
to
G
along
\varphi
,
the first claim follows.

Furthermore, this also implies that distinct simple unipotent
\Lambda G^{\prime}
-modules restrict to distinct

\Lambda\varphi(G)
-modules, because
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime\prime}
implies that we have
X^{\prime\prime}\cong\lambda\otimes X^{\prime}
for some
\lambda\in\mathrm{Irr}_{\Lambda}(G^{\prime}/\varphi(G))

(cf.
[
8
, Section 2.8]
).

It remains to show that every simple unipotent

\Lambda G
-module is of the form
\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X^{\prime}

for a simple unipotent
\Lambda G^{\prime}
-module
X^{\prime}
.
This follows essentially from
[
6
, 3.3.24]
together with our previous
remarks in
5.4.
.
Note that the assumption of a connected kernel in

[
6
, 3.3.24]
is redundant
as one can write any isotypy as a composition of isotypies
which have connected kernels (see, for instance, the proof of Theorem
4.12.
).

The last claim about the center
Z(G)
acting trivially
on simple unipotent representations follows by
considering the isotypy
\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}/Z(\mathbf{G})
.
∎

5.7.

Finally, we have all the tools to obtain our main result.

Theorem 5.8.

Let
\mathbf{L}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be a split Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, let
X
be
a simple unipotent
\Lambda L
-module.

Then
X
extends to a module over the stabilizer
N_{G}(\mathbf{L},X)
of
X
inside the normalizer

N_{G}(\mathbf{L})
.

Proof.

First, let
\iota:(\mathbf{G},F)\to(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
be a regular
embedding and set
\mathbf{L}^{\prime}=Z(\mathbf{G}^{\prime})\varphi(\mathbf{L})
which is a
split Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
.

According to Lemma
5.6.
there exists a simple unipotent
\Lambda L^{\prime}
-module
X^{\prime}

such that
\mathrm{Res}_{\iota}X^{\prime}=X
.

Clearly, we have
\iota(N_{G}(\mathbf{L},X))\subseteq N_{G^{\prime}}(\mathbf{L}^{\prime},X^{\prime})
and thus if
X^{\prime}
extends to a module over

N_{G^{\prime}}(\mathbf{L}^{\prime},X^{\prime})
, then
X
extends to a module over

N_{G}(\mathbf{L},X)
.

We thus may assume that
Z(\mathbf{G})
is connected. According to
[
6
, 1.7.13]

there exists a surjective isotypy
\varphi:(\mathbf{G}_{0},F_{0})\to(\mathbf{G},F)

such that
Z(\mathbf{G}_{0})
and
\ker(\varphi)
are connected
and that the derived subgroup
[\mathbf{G}_{0},\mathbf{G}_{0}]
is simply-connected.

We set
\mathbf{L}_{0}=\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{L})
as well as
X_{0}=\mathrm{Res}_{\varphi}X
which is just the inflation of
X
to
L_{0}
.
We have

\varphi(N_{G_{0}}(\mathbf{L}_{0},X_{0}))=N_{G}(\mathbf{L},X)
and if
X_{0}

extends to a
\Lambda N_{G_{0}}(\mathbf{L}_{0},X_{0})
-module, then

\ker(\varphi)^{F_{0}}
acts trivially on this module (as it acts trivially on
X_{0}
) and thus induces a
\Lambda N_{G}(\mathbf{L},X)
-module which extends
X
.

We may thus also assume that the derived subgroup of
\mathbf{G}

is simply connected. Moreover, we may assume that
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}_{I}
is a standard Levi subgroup so that we are in the situation
described in Remark
3.35.
and
we shall make use of the notation which was used there.

The center
Z(\mathbf{L})
acts trivially on
X
by Lemma
5.6.
and its restriction
X_{0}=\mathrm{Res}^{L_{I}}_{H_{I}}X
to
H_{I}

is simple by the same Lemma because the inclusion
\mathbf{H}_{I}\to\mathbf{L}_{I}
is an isotypy. In particular, we can consider
X
as a
\Lambda L_{I}/Z
-module and it suffices to show that

X
extends to a
N_{G}(\mathbf{L}_{I},X)/Z
-module.

Theorem
2.10.
implies that
X_{0}
extends to a
\Lambda(H_{I}\rtimes N_{W}(I,X))
-module.
Finally, as we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda L}(\mathrm{ad}(w)X,X)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda H_{I}}(\mathrm{ad}(w)X_{0},X_{0}),

the action of
N_{W}(I,X)
on
X_{0}
is compatible with the action of
L_{I}
on
X_{0}
. Thus the extension of
X_{0}

to
H_{I}\rtimes N_{W}(I,X)
can be considered as an extension
of
X
as a
\Lambda(L_{I}/Z\rtimes N_{W}(I,X))
-module which is the same as a
\Lambda N_{G}(\mathbf{L}_{I},X)/Z
-module
by Remark
3.35.
and so the claim follows.
∎

Abstract
In this paper, we revisit the backward Euler method for numerical approximations of random periodic solutions of semilinear SDEs with additive noise. Improved L p L^{p} -estimates of the random periodic solutions of the considered SDEs are obtained under a more relaxed condition compared to literature. The backward Euler scheme is proved to converge with an order one in the mean square sense, which also improves the existing order-half convergence. Numerical examples are presented to verify our theoretical analysis. AMS subject classification: 37H99, 60H10, 60H35, 65C30. Keywords: Backward Euler method, Random periodic solution, Stochastic differential equations, Additive noise, Pull-back.

Introduction

Many phenomena in the real world exhibit both periodic and random nature, for instance, the daily temperature, energy consumption, and airline passenger volumes.
To understand the long-time behaviour of these laws of random motion, where the underlying dynamics are usually modeled via stochastic differential equations (SDEs), it is crucial to study their random periodic solutions
[
18
,
6
]
.

Though the periodic solution has been a central concept
in the field of deterministic dynamical systems since Poincaré’s seminal work
[
11
]
, its stochastic counterpart has not been properly defined or studied until the last decade.
Zhao and Zheng
[
18
]
first formulated the definition of the pathwise random periodic solutions for
C^{1}
-cocycles of random dynamical systems and Feng, Zhao and Zhou
[
6
]
further developed the definition for semiflows.
This pioneering study boosts a series of work, including the study of anticipating random solutions of SDEs with multiplicative linear noise
[
3
]
,
the existence of random solutions generated by non-autonomous SPDEs with additive noise
[
4
]
, periodic measures and ergodicity
[
5
]
, etc.
Note that almost all the sequel works are based on the definition of pathwise random periodic solutions for semiflows of random dynamical systems.

As the random periodic solution is not constructed explicitly, it is useful to study its numerical approximation. This is an infinite time horizon problem. The relevant research on the numerics of random periodic solutions for SDEs has made rapid progress recently
[
8
,
1
,
15
,
14
,
10
,
9
,
12
]
.
The first paper
[
2
]
to approximate the random period trajectory considered an Euler-Maruyama method and a modified Milstein method for a dissipative system with a global Lipschitz condition.
Wei and Chen
[
16
]
later generalised the Euler-Maruyama method to the stochastic theta method by showing that the approximation converges to the exact one at an order
1/4
.
Wu
[
17
]
studied the existence and uniqueness of the random periodic solution
for an additive SDE with a one-sided Lipschitz condition and gave the analysis of the order-half convergence of its numerical approximation via the backward Euler method.

In this paper, we mainly consider the strong convergence rate of the backward Euler method for the random periodic solution of semilinear SDE with a one-sided Lipschitz condition. Our contribution is two-folded:

•

Improved
L^{p}
-estimates of the random periodic solutions of the considered SDEs
can be guaranteed under a more relaxed condition compared to
[
17
]
;

•

The order of convergence of the backward Euler method used to approximate random periodic solutions can be lifted from half in
[
17
]
to one for SDEs with an additive noise.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
In section
2
, we present some standard notation and assumptions that will be employed in our proofs,
and give the existence and uniqueness of the random periodic solution.
In Section
3
we consider the
L^{p}
-estimates of the random periodic solutions under a relaxed condition compared to
[
17
]
.
Section
4
is devoted to the error analysis of order-one convergence of the backward Euler method, where the supporting evidence is shown via
numerical experiments in Section
5
.

Random Periodic Solutions of SDEs

Let us recall the definition of the random periodic solution for stochastic semi-flows given in
[
6
]
.
Let
X
be a separable Banach space.
Denote by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\theta_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}

a metric dynamical system and

\theta_{s}:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega

is assumed to be a measurably invertible
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Denote
\Delta:=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},s\leq t\}
.
Consider a stochastic periodic semi-flow

u:\Delta\times\Omega\times X\rightarrow X
of period
\tau
,
which satisfies the following standard condition

u(t,r,\omega)=u(t,s,\omega)\circ u(s,r,\omega)\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}a.e.\ \omega\in\Omega,}

(2.1)

for all
r\leq s\leq t,r,s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\tau>0
. A random periodic solution of period
\tau
of a semi-flow

u:\Delta\times\Omega\times X\rightarrow X

is an
\mathcal{F}
-measurable map

Y:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow X

such that

u(t-s,s,Y(s,\omega),\omega)=Y(t,\omega)\text{ and }Y(t+\tau,\omega)=Y(t,\theta_{\tau}\omega)

(2.2)

for any
(t,s)\in\Delta
and a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

Throughout this paper the following notation
is frequently used. First, we have
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Denote
[d]:=\{1,...,d\}

C
to denote a generic positive
constant independent of the time and temporal stepsize of the numerical scheme we will introduce later.
Let
|\cdot|
,
\|\cdot\|
and

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle

be the absolute value of a scalar, the Euclidean norm and the inner product of vectors
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
By
A^{T}
we denote the transpose of vector or matrix.
Given a matrix
A

\|A\|:=\sqrt{trace(A^{T}A)}
to denote the trace norm of
A
.
On a canonical probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
,
we use
\mathbb{E}
to denote the expectation and

L^{p}(\Omega;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R}^{d}})

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\|\xi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R}^{d}})}=(\mathbb{E}[\|\xi\|^{p}])^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

For a integrable random variable
X

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and for
\mathcal{G}
a
\sigma

\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}

\mathbb{E}[X|\mathcal{G}]

Let
W:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

The filtration is defined as

\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}:=\sigma\{W_{u}-W_{v}:s\leq v\leq u\leq t\}

\mathcal{F}^{t}=\mathcal{F}^{t}_{-\infty}=\bigvee_{s\leq t}\mathcal{F}^{t}_{s}

Denote the standard
\mathbb{P}

\theta:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\rightarrow\Omega

\theta_{t}(\omega)(s):=W_{t+s}-W_{t}

Given a standard two-sided Wiener process

\theta

\mathbb{P}

\left\{\begin{aligned} \text{d}X_{t}^{t_{0}}&={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big(}-\Lambda X_{t}^{t_{0}}+f(t,\,X_{t}^{t_{0}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big)}\text{d}t+g(t)\,\text{d}W(t),\quad t\in(t_{0},T],\\
X_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}}&=\xi,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

where

f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda
is
d\times d

X_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}}
to emphasise a process
X

t_{1}

t_{0}
.
The random initial value
\xi

\mathcal{F}^{t_{0}}

Assumption 2.2
.

Suppose the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

\Lambda
is self-adjoint and positive definite operator and
there exists a non-decreasing sequence
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in[d]}\subset\mathbb{R}
of positive real numbers and an orthonormal basis
(e_{i})_{i\in[d]}
, such that

\Lambda e_{i}=\lambda_{i}e_{i},

(2.4)

for every
i\in[d]
.

(ii)
The drift coefficient functions
f
is continuous and
f(t,x)=f(t+\tau,x)
.
There exists a constant
0<c_{f}<\lambda_{1}
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[0,\tau)

\begin{split}\langle x-y,f(t,x)-f(t,y)\rangle&\leq c_{f}\|x-y\|^{2},\\
\langle x,f(t,x)\rangle&\leq c_{f}(1+\|x\|^{2}).\end{split}

(2.5)

(iii)
The diffusion coefficient functions
g
is continuous and
g(t)=g(t+\tau)
and there exists a constant
c_{g}>0
such that
\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}\|g(t)\|\leq c_{g}

\begin{split}\|g(t_{1})-g(t_{2})\|\leq c_{g}|t_{2}-t_{1}|,\quad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,\tau).\end{split}

(2.6)

(iv)
There exists a constant
C^{*}>0

such that

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi\|^{2}\big]\leq C^{*}
.

Assumption 2.3
.

There exists a constant
\tilde{c_{f}}
such that

\begin{split}\Bigg\|f(t,x)-\frac{\langle f(t,x),x\rangle}{\|x\|^{2}}x\Bigg\|\leq\tilde{c_{f}}(1+\|x\|^{2}),\end{split}

(2.7)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},t\in[0,\tau)
.

Following a similar argument as in
[
13

, the SDE (
2.3
) admits a unique global semiflow under Assumption
2.2
and Assumption
2.3
.
Note that by the variation of constant formula, the solution of (
2.3
) is written as

X^{t_{0}}_{t}(\xi)=e^{-\Lambda(t-t_{0})}\xi+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}f(s,X^{t_{0}}_{s})\,\text{d}s+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}.

(2.8)

These assumptions can ensure the existence and uniqueness of the random periodic solution, which has been proposed in
[
17
, Theorem 7]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let Assumptions
2.2

and Assumptions
2.3
be hold, then there exists a unique random periodic solution
X_{t}^{*}(\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with the form

X^{*}_{t}=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}f(s,X^{*}_{s})\,\text{d}s+\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\Lambda(t-s)}g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}

(2.9)

such that
X^{*}
is a limit of the pull-back

X^{-k\tau}_{t}(\xi)
of (
2.3
) when
k\rightarrow\infty
, ie,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}(\xi)-X_{t}^{*}\|^{2}\big]=0.

(2.10)

3
Improved
L^{p}
-estimates of the random periodic solutions

In this section, our objective is to demonstrate the time-uniform moment boundedness of the random periodic solutions.
While the work of Wu (2022) established the uniform boundedness for the
p
-th moment of the SDE solution as referenced in
[
17
]
, our contribution lies in surpassing the limitation imposed on the drift functions (see Remark
3.3
for details).
To accomplish this objective, we begin by deriving a generalized lemma, building upon the principles outlined in
[
7
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m:[a,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
,

\psi:[a,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be nonnegative continuous functions for
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
If there exists a positive constant
\delta
such that

m(t)-m(s)\leq-\delta\int_{s}^{t}m(u)\,\text{d}u+\int_{s}^{t}\psi(u)\,\text{d}u\qquad

(3.1)

for any
a\leq s<t<\infty
, then

m(t)\leq m(a)+\int_{a}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-u)}\psi(u)\,\text{d}u.

(3.2)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Denote

m_{1}(t):=m(a)+\int_{a}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-u)}\psi(u)\,\text{d}u
. It is easy to derive that

\begin{split}m_{1}^{\prime}(t)&=-\delta\int_{a}^{t}e^{-\delta(t-u)}\psi(u)\,\text{d}u+\psi(t)\\
&=-\delta(m_{1}(t)-m(a))+\psi(t).\end{split}

(3.3)

So we get

m_{1}(t)-m_{1}(s)=-\delta\int_{s}^{t}(m_{1}(u)-m(a))\,\text{d}u+\int_{s}^{t}\psi(u)\,\text{d}u.

(3.4)

Now set
m_{2}(t)=m(t)-m_{1}(t)
.

Due to (
3.1
), (
3.4
) and noting
m(a)\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}m_{2}(t)-m_{2}(s)&=m(t)-m(s)-(m_{1}(t)-m_{1}(s))\\
&\leq-\delta\int_{s}^{t}(m(u)-m_{1}(u)+m(a))\,\text{d}u\\
&\leq-\delta\int_{s}^{t}(m(u)-m_{1}(u))\,\text{d}u\\
&=-\delta\int_{s}^{t}m_{2}(u)\,\text{d}u.\end{split}

(3.5)

To prove (
3.2
), it suffices to show that
m_{2}(t)\leq 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
m_{2}(t)>0
for some
t
,
then
m_{2}(a)=0
implies that there exists an interval
[s_{1},t_{1}]\subset[a,\infty],\,m_{2}(t_{1})>m_{2}(s_{1})
and
m_{2}>0
on
[s_{1},t_{1}]
, which contradict (
3.5
).
∎

We now give the time-uniform moment boundedness of the exact solution of SDE (
2.3
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let Assumptions

2.2
hold. Let
X^{-k\tau}_{t}
be the solution of SDE (
2.3
) with the initial value
X^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau}=\xi
obeying

\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi\|^{2p}\big]<\infty
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\mathbb{E}\big[{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2p}\big]\leq C\big(1+\mathbb{E}[\|\xi\|^{2p}]\big).

(3.6)

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Using the Itô formula gives

\begin{split}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}=&\big(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2}\big)^{p}+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},-\Lambda X^{-k\tau}_{s}\rangle\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},f(s,X^{-k\tau}_{s})\rangle\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\rangle\\
&+p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p(p-1)\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-2}\|(X^{-k\tau}_{s})^{T}g(s)\|^{2}\,\text{d}s.\end{split}

(3.7)

This directly leads to

\begin{split}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}\leq&\big(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2}\big)^{p}+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},-\Lambda X^{-k\tau}_{s}\rangle\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},f(s,X^{-k\tau}_{s})\rangle\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\rangle\\
&+p(2p-1)\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\|g(s)\|^{2}\,\text{d}s.\\
\end{split}

(3.8)

Combining this with (
2.4
)
and (
2.5
)
we can get

\begin{split}\big(1+\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|^{2}\big)^{p}\leq&\big(1+\|\xi\|^{2}\big)^{p}+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|^{2}\big)^{p-1}\big(-\lambda_{1}(1+\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|^{2})\big)\,\text{d}s\\
&+2pc_{f}\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|^{2}\big)^{p}\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\rangle\\
&+p\big((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}\big)\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\,\text{d}s\\
\leq&\big(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2}\big)^{p}-2p(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\rangle\\
&+p\big((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}\big)\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\,\text{d}s,\end{split}

(3.9)

where

\begin{split}&p\big((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}\big)\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\\
&\quad=p(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\times\tfrac{(2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}-c_{f}}\\
&\quad\leq p(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\times\tfrac{p-1}{p}\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}+p(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\times\tfrac{1}{p}\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p}}\\
&\quad=(p-1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}+\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}\end{split}

(3.10)

by the Young inequality

a^{p-1}b\leq\frac{p-1}{p}a^{p}+\frac{1}{p}b^{p}
.
Therefore

\begin{split}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}\leq&\big(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2}\big)^{p}-(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}\,\text{d}s\\
&+\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1}))^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}\,\text{d}s\\
&+2p\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\big(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p-1}\langle X^{-k\tau}_{s},g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\rangle.\\
\end{split}

(3.11)

For every interval
n\geq 1
, define the stopping time

\tau_{n}=\inf\{s\in[-k\tau,t]:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|}\geq n\}.

(3.12)

Clearly,
\tau_{n}\uparrow t
a.s.. Moreover,
it follows from (
3.11
) and the property of the Itô integral that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|}^{2})^{p}]\leq&\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2})^{p}]-(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\big(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2}\big)^{p}\,\text{d}s\Big]\\
&+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau}^{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t\wedge\tau_{n}}\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}\,\text{d}s\Big].\end{split}

(3.13)

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and by the Fatou’s lemma, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2})^{p}]\leq&\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2})^{p}]-(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[(1+\|{X^{-k\tau}_{s}\|}^{2})^{p}\Big]\,\text{d}s\\
&+\int_{-k\tau}^{t}\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}\,\text{d}s.\end{split}

(3.14)

Now applying Lemma
3.1
to (
3.14
) with
\delta=(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})>0
and
\psi=\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}
gives

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|X^{-k\tau}_{t}\|}^{2})^{p}]&\leq\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2})^{p}]+\int_{-k\tau}^{t}e^{-(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})(t-u)}\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p-1}}\,\text{d}u\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}[(1+{\|\xi\|}^{2})^{p}]+\tfrac{((2p-1)c_{g}^{2}+2\lambda_{1})^{p}}{(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})^{p}}(1-e^{-(p+1)(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})(t+k\tau)})\\
&\leq C\big(1+\mathbb{E}[\|\xi\|^{2p}]\big),\end{split}

(3.15)

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The above time-uniform moment boundedness was obtained under more relaxed conditions, compared to

[
17
, Proposition 14]
,
where the author additionally required
(c_{f}+\tfrac{(p-1)c_{g}}{2})(2+p+2^{p+1})<p\lambda_{1}
for some
positive number
p\geq 4\gamma-2
(cf.
[
17
, Assumption 13]
).
Instead, here we just require
0<c_{f}<\lambda_{1}
, which significantly relaxes
the aforementioned condition.

4
The order-one convergence of the backward Euler method

This section is devoted to the error analysis of the strong convergence rate of the backward Euler approximation to SDE (
2.3
), where we lift the order of convergence to one from half as obtained in
[
17
]
.
Take an equidistant partition

\mathcal{T}^{h}:=\{jh,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,
such that
h\in(0,1)
.
Note that
\mathcal{T}^{h}
stretch along the real line because we are dealing with an infinite time horizon problem.
The backward Euler method applied to SDE (
2.3
) takes the following form:

{\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh}={\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}-\Lambda h{\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh}+hf\big(jh,{\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh}\big)+g((j-1)h)\Delta W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}

(4.1)

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
where

\Delta W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}:=W_{-k\tau+jh}-W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}
,
and the initial value

\tilde{X}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau}=\xi
.
Because of the periodicity of
f
and
g
, we have that
f(-k\tau+jh,{\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh})=f(jh,{\tilde{X}}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh})
,

g(-k\tau+jh)=g(jh)
.

Before proceeding to the assumptions and the main proof of the error analysis, we present some existing and essential results regarding (
4.1
) from literature. The uniform bounds for the second moment of the numerical approximation have been established in
[
17
, Lemma 17]
as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
be satisfied.
Then there exists a constant
\tilde{C}>0
such that

\sup_{k,j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{X}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh}\|^{2}]\leq\tilde{C},

(4.2)

where
\{\tilde{X}^{-k\tau}_{-k\tau+jh}\}_{k,j\in\mathbb{N}}
is given by (
4.1
).

The existence and uniqueness of random periodic solutions associated to the backward Euler method has been guaranteed in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
be satisfied. For
h\in(0,1)
, the time domain is divided as
\mathcal{T}^{h}
.
The backward Euler method (
4.1
) admits a random period solution
\tilde{X}^{*}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}(\xi)-\tilde{X}^{*}\|^{2}\big]=0.

(4.3)

In order to establish a strong convergence rate as high as one of the backward Euler method, we need the following conditions on the drift as well as the initial condition besides Assumption
2.2
.

Assumption 4.3
.

Assume the drift coefficient functions
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
are continuously differentiable, and
there exists a constant
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(t,x)-\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(t,\bar{x}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}\big)y\big\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|x\|+\|\bar{x}\|)^{\gamma-2}\|x-\bar{x}\|\|y\|,\quad\forall x,\bar{x},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|f(t,x)-f(s,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|x\|^{\gamma})|t-s|,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},s,t\in[0,\tau),

(4.5)

and

\|\xi\|_{L^{\max\{4\gamma,8\gamma-8\}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
.

Note that the condition (
4.4
)
implies

\|\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(t,x)y\|\leq C(1+\|x\|)^{\gamma-1}\|y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\

(4.6)

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\|f(t,x)-f(t,\bar{x})\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|x\|+\|\bar{x}\|)^{\gamma-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|,\quad\forall x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|f(t,x)\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|x\|)^{\gamma},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.8)

We now present two lemmas with estimates which
play important role in proving the order of convergence of the backward Euler method in Theorem
4.6
.
The first one is the
Hölder continuity of the exact solution of
(
2.3
) with respect to the norm in

L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the second one is about the regularity of
f
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
Assumptions
2.2
and
4.3

be hold.
Then there exists a positive constant
C

which depends on
\gamma,d,\Lambda,f,g
only,
such that

\|X_{t_{1}}^{-k\tau}-X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{p\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|+C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(4.9)

for all
t_{1},t_{2}\geq-k\tau
and

p\in[2,\infty)
,
where
X_{t}^{-k\tau}
denotes the exact solution to the SDE
(
2.3
).

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Without loss of generality we set
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
and get

\begin{split}\|X_{t_{1}}^{-k\tau}-X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}&=\Big\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big(-\Lambda X_{r}^{k\tau}+f(r,X_{t}^{-k\tau}\big)\,\text{d}r+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\Big\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big(\Lambda X_{r}^{-k\tau}+f(r,X_{t}^{-k\tau})\big)\,\text{d}r\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+\Big\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

(4.10)

For the first term,
we can get the following estimate using the Hölder inequality, (
3.6
) and
(
4.8
)

\begin{split}\Big\|&\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big(-\Lambda X_{r}^{-k\tau}+f(r,X_{t}^{-k\tau})\big)\,\text{d}r\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\Lambda X_{r}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\text{d}r+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|f(r,X_{t}^{-k\tau})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\text{d}r\\
&\quad\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{p\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|.\end{split}

(4.11)

Applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality to the last term of (
4.10
) gives

\Big\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|g(r)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\text{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.12)

This completes proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let Assumptions
2.2
and
4.3
be
satisfied.
Consider the exact solution
X^{-k\tau}_{t}
of SDE (
2.3
) over
[-k\tau,T]
for arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\geq-k\tau
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C

which depends on
\gamma,d,\Lambda,f,g
only,
such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[-k\tau,T]

and
s\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
,
it holds that

\begin{split}\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{2\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|.\end{split}

(4.13)

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

For all
s,t_{2}\in[-k\tau,T]
,
it follows from
(
4.7
) and
(
4.8
)
that

\begin{split}\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|&=\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(s,X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})+f(s,X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|\\
&\leq\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(s,X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|+\big\|f(s,X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|\\
&\leq C(1+\|X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|+\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|)^{\gamma-1}\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}-X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|\\
&\quad+C(1+\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma})|t_{2}-s|.\end{split}

(4.14)

Taking the expectation on both sides and
using the Hölder inequality

\|f^{\gamma-1}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|^{\gamma-1}_{L^{2\rho_{1}(\gamma-1)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
+\|g\|_{L^{2\rho_{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,(\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{2}}=1)

with exponents

\rho_{1}=:\frac{2\gamma-1}{\gamma-1}
and

\rho_{2}=:\frac{2\gamma-1}{\gamma}
yield that

\begin{split}&\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad\leq C\big\|(1+\|X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|+\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|)^{\gamma-1}\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}-X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\\
&\qquad+C\big\|(1+\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma})|t_{2}-s|\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R})}}\\
&\quad\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma-1}_{L^{2\rho_{1}(\gamma-1)}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\Big)\|X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}-X_{s}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{2\rho_{2}}(\Omega;{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R}^{d}})}\\
&\qquad+C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{2\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|.\\
\end{split}

(4.15)

Moreover, through Lemma

4.4

with
p=2\rho_{2}
we have that

\begin{split}\|X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{2\rho_{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{2\gamma\rho_{2}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-s|+C|t_{2}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|+C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}.\\
\end{split}

(4.16)

Note that
2\rho_{1}(\gamma-1)=4\gamma-2
. Altogether, it follows that for
s\in[t_{1},t_{2}]

\begin{split}&\big\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(t_{2},X_{t_{2}}^{-k\tau})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad+C\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\big(\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{2\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)|t_{2}-t_{1}|.\end{split}

(4.17)

This thus finishes
the proof of the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to give the main result of this section that reveals the order-one convergence of the backward Euler scheme to the SDE (
2.3
) in the long run.

Theorem 4.6
.

Under Assumptions

2.2
and

4.3
.
For
h\in(0,1)
, the time domain is divided as
\mathcal{T}^{h}
.
If
X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}
and

\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}

are the exact and the numerical solutions given by (
2.3
) and (
4.1
), respectively,
then
there exists a constant
C
that depends on the
\gamma,\Lambda,f,g
and
d
such that

\sup_{k,j}\|X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}-\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ch.

(4.18)

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

First note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}&=X_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau}-\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\Lambda X_{s}^{-k\tau}\,\text{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s+\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}g(s)\,\text{d}W_{s}\\
&=X_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau}-\Lambda hX_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}+hf(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\\
&\quad+g((j-1)h)\Delta W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}+\mathcal{R}_{j},\end{split}

(4.19)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{j}&:=-\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\Lambda(X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\\
&\quad+\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}g(s)-g((j-1)h)\,\text{d}W_{s}.\\
\end{split}

(4.20)

Subtracting (
4.1
) from this yields

\begin{split}X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}-\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}&=X_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau}-\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau}-\Lambda h(X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}-\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\\
&\quad+h\big(f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\big)\\
&\quad+g((j-1)h)\Delta W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}-g((j-1)h)\Delta W_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}+\mathcal{R}_{j}.\\
\end{split}

(4.21)

For brevity, we denote

\begin{split}D_{j}:=X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}-\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau},\quad\Delta f_{j}:=f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,\tilde{X}_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}).\end{split}

(4.22)

We emphasize that
D_{j}
and
\Delta f_{j}
are
\mathcal{F}_{jh-k\tau}
-measurable.

Using (
4.22
), (
4.21
) can be now rewritten as

D_{j}=D_{j-1}-\Lambda hD_{j}+h\Delta f_{j}+\mathcal{R}_{j}.

(4.23)

This leads to

\|D_{j}+\Lambda hD_{j}-h\Delta f_{j}\|^{2}=\|D_{j-1}+\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}.

(4.24)

Using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) gives

\begin{split}\|&D_{j}+\Lambda hD_{j}-h\Delta f_{j}\|^{2}\\
&=\|D_{j}\|^{2}+2h\langle D_{j},\Lambda D_{j}\rangle-2h\langle D_{j},\Delta f_{j}\rangle+h^{2}\|\Lambda D_{j}-\Delta f_{j}\|^{2}\\
&\geq\|D_{j}\|^{2}+2\lambda_{1}h\|D_{j}\|^{2}-2c_{f}h\|D_{j}\|^{2}\\
&=\big(1+2(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})h\big)\|D_{j}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(4.25)

Meanwhile,

\|D_{j-1}+\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}=\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}+2\langle D_{j-1},\mathcal{R}_{j}\rangle+\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}.

(4.26)

As a result,

\big(1+2(\lambda_{1}-c_{f})h\big)\|D_{j}\|^{2}\leq\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}+2\langle D_{j-1},\mathcal{R}_{j}\rangle+\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}.

(4.27)

Denoting
v:=\lambda_{1}-c_{f}
and taking expectation yield,

(1+2vh)\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j}\|^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}[\langle D_{j-1},\mathcal{R}_{j}\rangle]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}].

(4.28)

Recalling
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D_{j-1}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}
-measurable, we deduce

\mathbb{E}[\langle D_{j-1},\mathcal{R}_{j}\rangle]=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}[\langle D_{j-1},\mathcal{R}_{j}\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\langle}D_{j-1},\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]\rangle\big].

(4.29)

Further, noting
v>0
by (
2.5
), and using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
2ab\leq vha^{2}+\frac{1}{vh}b^{2}
for arbitrary positive
h
,
we obtain

\begin{split}(1+2vh)\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j}\|^{2}]&\leq\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}]+2\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\sqrt{vh}D_{j-1},\frac{1}{\sqrt{vh}}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]\rangle\big]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}]\\
&\leq(1+vh)\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}]+\dfrac{1}{vh}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{{(j-1)h-k\tau}}]\|^{2}\big].\end{split}

(4.30)

Hence,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j}\|^{2}]&\leq\tfrac{1+vh}{1+2vh}\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}]+\tfrac{1}{1+2vh}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}]+\tfrac{1}{(1+2vh)vh}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]\|^{2}\big]\\
&=(1-\tfrac{v}{1+2vh}h)\mathbb{E}[\|D_{j-1}\|^{2}]+\tfrac{1}{1+2vh}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}]+\tfrac{1}{(1+2vh)vh}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{{(j-1)h-k\tau}}]\|^{2}\big].\end{split}

(4.31)

Therefore, we only need to estimate two error terms
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|^{2}]
and
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{{(j-1)h-k\tau}}]\|^{2}\big]
.
Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{j}
given by (
4.20
)
and using an triangle inequality yield

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\bigg\|\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\Lambda(X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\,\text{d}s}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+\bigg\|\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+\bigg\|\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}g(s)-g((j-1)h)\,\text{d}W_{s}\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&=:\mathbb{I}_{1}+\mathbb{I}_{2}+\mathbb{I}_{3}.\end{split}

(4.32)

For the term

\mathbb{I}_{1}
,
following the Hölder inequality and (
4.9
) shows

\begin{split}\mathbb{I}_{1}&\leq\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\|\Lambda(X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\text{d}s\\
&\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{2\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).\end{split}

(4.33)

With the help of
the Hölder inequality
and (
4.14
),
one can obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{I}_{2}&\leq\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\bigg\|f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,\text{d}s\\
&\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{2\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).\end{split}

(4.34)

In view of the Itô isomery and by Assumption
2.2
(iii)

\begin{split}\mathbb{I}_{3}=\Big(\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\|g(s)-g((j-1)h)\|^{2}\,\text{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.35)

Putting all the above estimates together we derive from (
4.32
) that

\|\mathcal{R}_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big(1+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\geq-k\tau}\|X_{t}^{-k\tau}\|^{2\gamma-1}_{L^{4\gamma-2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).\\

(4.36)

Note that

\mathbb{E}[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}g(s)-g((j-1)h)\,\text{d}W_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]=0
.
Thus

\begin{split}&\|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq\Big\|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}-\Lambda(X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\quad+\Big\|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}f(s,X_{s}^{-k\tau})-f(jh,X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&=:\mathbb{I}_{4}+\mathbb{I}_{5}.\end{split}

(4.37)

In order to estimate
\mathbb{I}_{4}
,
we first show that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\int_{s}^{-k\tau+jh}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\\
&\quad=\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{-k\tau+jh}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s}\\
&\quad=\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{s}^{-k\tau+jh}g(r)\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}W_{r}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\Big]\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\,\text{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s}\\
&\quad=0.\end{split}

(4.38)

As a result,
we derive from (
4.19
) that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}-\Lambda(X_{s}^{-k\tau}-X_{-k\tau+jh}^{-k\tau})\,\text{d}s\Big|\mathcal{F}_{(j-1)h-k\tau}\Big]\\
&\quad=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{-k\tau+(j-1)h}^{-k\tau+jh}\int_{s}^{-k\tau+jh}(-\Lambda^{2}X_{r}^{-k\tau}+\Lambda f(r,X_{r}^{-k\tau}))\,\text{d}r\,\text{d}s\Big].\\
\end{split}

(4.39)

Based on the Jensen inequality and
the Hölder inequality,
according to
(
3.6
) and
(
4.8
), one can infer

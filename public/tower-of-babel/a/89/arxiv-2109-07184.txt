unbounded M M -weakly and unbounded L L -weakly compact operators
Zahra Niktab, Kazem Haghnejad Azar, Razi Alavizadeh, Saba Sadeghi Gavgani

Abstract
We introduce the class of unbounded M M -weakly operators and the class of unbounded L L -weakly compact operators. We investigate some properties for these new classification of operators and we study relation between them and M M -weakly compact and L L -weakly compact operators. We also present an operator characterization of Banach lattices with order continuous norm.

Introduction

A continuous operator
T:E\to X
from a Banach lattice
E
to a Banach space
X
is said to be
M
-weakly compact if
\left\lVert Tx_{n}\right\rVert\to 0
holds for every norm bounded disjoint sequence
\{x_{n}\}
of
E
.
A continuous operator
T:X\to E
from a Banach space
X
to a Banach lattice
E
is said to be
L
-weakly compact if
\left\lVert y_{n}\right\rVert\to 0
holds for every sequence
\{y_{n}\}
of
solid hull of
T(U)
, where
U
is
the closed unit ball of the Banach space
X
.
The classes of
L
-weakly and
M
-weakly compact operators were introduced by
Meyer-Nieberg in
meyer1974uber
.
He proved some interesting properties for these classifications of operators. For example, he proved that
L
-weakly and
M
-weakly compact operators are weakly compact,
(
aliprantis2006positive
, Theorem 5.61)
.
In this paper, we introduce unbounded version for these classifications of operators and we prove some of their properties.

Before we state our results, we need to fix some notations and recall some definitions. Let
E
and
F
be two vector lattices, let
x,y\in E
with
x\leq y
, and let the order interval
[x,y]
be the subset of
E
defined by
[x,y]=\{z\in E:x\leq z\leq y\}
.
A subset of
E
is called order bounded if it is included in an order interval.
Let
T:E\rightarrow F
be an operator between two vector lattices
E
and
F
.
T
is order bounded if it maps order bounded subsets of
E
to order bounded subsets of
F
.
If
E
is a normed space, then by
E^{{}^{\prime}}
and
E^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
we will denote the topological dual and topological bidual of
E
, respectively.
The vector space
E^{\sim}
of all order bounded linear functionals on
E
is called the order dual of
E
. The vector space
E^{\sim\sim}=(E^{\sim})^{\sim}
will denote the order bidual of
E
.
The algebraic adjoint of
T
will be denoted by
T^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}
, and its order adjoint will be denoted by
T^{\sim}:F^{\sim}\rightarrow E^{\sim}
.
A Banach lattice is a Banach space
(E,\left\lVert.\right\rVert)
such that
E
is a vector lattice and its norm satisfies the following property: for each
x,y\in E
, if
\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert y\right\rvert
, then we have
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert y\right\rVert
. A norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
of a Banach lattice
E
is order continuous, if for each net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
such that
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
, (i.e.
(x_{\alpha})
is decreasing and
\inf\{x_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda\}=0
) we have
\left\lVert x_{\alpha}\right\rVert\to 0
.
A Banach lattice
E
is said to be a Kantorovich–Banach
space (
KB
-space) whenever every increasing norm bounded
sequence of
E^{+}
is norm-convergent.
The solid hull of a subset
A
of vector lattice
E
is the smallest solid set including
A
. It is easy to see that

\sol(A)=\left\{x\in E:\exists y\in A\mbox{ with }\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert y\right\rvert\right\}.

A net
(x_{\alpha})
in
E
is said to be

unbounded order
convergent

(
uo
-convergent, for short) to
x
if for every
u\in E^{+}
the net (
|x_{\alpha}-x|\wedge u
)
converges to zero in order.

unbounded norm convergent

(
un
-convergent, for
short) to
x
if
\||x_{\alpha}-x|\wedge u\|\to 0
for every
u\in E^{+}
.

For sequences in
L^{p}(\mu)
, where
1\leq p<\infty
and
\mu
is a finite measure, it is easy to see that
uo
-convergence agrees with convergence almost everywhere, see
gao2017uo
.
Under the same assumptions,
un
-convergence agrees with convergence in measure, see

(
troitsky2004measure
, Example 23)
.
It is clear that if
X
is an order continuous normed lattice then
uo
-convergence implies
un
-convergence.
For an operator
T:E\rightarrow F
between two vector lattices we say that its modulus
\left\lvert T\right\rvert
exists whenever

\left\lvert T\right\rvert:=T\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}(-T)

is a well defined operator from
E
into
F
.
An operator
T:E\rightarrow F
between two vector lattices is said to
be a lattice (or Riesz) homomorphism whenever
T(x\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}y)=T(x)\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\vee$}}}}T(y)

holds for all
x,y\in E
.
An operator
T:E\rightarrow F
between two vector lattices is called disjointness-preserving
if
Tx\perp Ty
for all
x,y\in E
satisfying
x\perp y
.
By Meyer’s theorem
(
meyer2012banach
, Theorem 3.1.4)
, we know that, if an order bounded operator
T:E\rightarrow F
between two Archimedean vector lattices preserves disjointness, then its modulus exists, and

\left\lvert T\right\rvert(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)=\left\lvert T(\left\lvert x\right\rvert)\right\rvert=\left\lvert Tx\right\rvert

holds for all
x\in E
. Moreover,
\left\lvert T\right\rvert
is a lattice homomorphism.

We refer the reader to
aliprantis2006positive
and
meyer2012banach
for any unexplained terms from Banach lattice theory.

Main Results

In this section, we introduce two new concepts as unbounded

-weakly compact and unbounded

-weakly compact operators and we investigate some of their properties. We establish their relationships with
M
-weakly compact and

-weakly compact operators and we study lattice properties of these new concepts.

Definition 1

A continuous operator
T:E\to F
between two Banach lattices
E
and

is said to be unbounded

-weakly compact (or

-

Tx_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0

A continuous operator

-

y_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
holds for every disjoint sequence
\{y_{n}\}
of
solid hull of

is
the closed unit ball of the Banach space

.

We do not use net in above definitions since we have the following propositions.

Proposition 1

A continuous operator
T:E\to F
between two Banach lattices

and

is

-

-weakly compact iff

Tx_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{un}0
holds for every norm bounded disjoint net
\{x_{\alpha}\}
of

.

Proposition 2

A continuous operator
T:X\to E
from a Banach space

to a Banach lattice

is
u
-
L
-weakly compact
iff
y_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{un}0
holds for every disjoint net
\{y_{\alpha}\}
of
solid hull of
T(U)
, where
U
is
the closed unit ball of the Banach space
X
.

In the rest of this paper, we denote by

the class of all continuous operators between two normed vector spaces

and

.

MW(E,X)

the class of all

-weakly compact operators from a Banach lattice

to a Banach space

LW(X,E)

the class of all

-weakly compact operators from a Banach space

to a Banach lattice

MW_{u}(E,F)

the class of all

-

-weakly compact operators between two Banach lattices

and

the class of all

-

-weakly compact operators from a Banach space

For Banach lattices

and

, and a Banach space

we have the following inclusions

LW(X,E)\subset LW_{u}(X,E),\quad MW(E,F)\subset MW_{u}(E,F).

In the next theorem we give a condition that the reverse inclusions hold. But, in general the above inclusions are proper as shown in the following example.

Example 1

Let
T:\ell_{1}\to c_{0}
be the inclusion operator.
The sequence
\{e_{n}\}
of the
standard unit vectors is a norm bounded disjoint sequence of
\ell^{1}
.
We have
\|T(e_{n})\|=1
for each

, therefore
T
is not
M
-weakly compact. On the other hand, let
\{a_{n}\}
be a norm bounded disjoint sequence in
\ell_{1}
. Clearly for each
u\in c_{0}^{+}
,
\{a_{n}\wedge u\}
is a disjoint sequence in
c_{0}
, so it follows from order continuity of

that
a_{n}\wedge u\xrightarrow{\|\cdot\|}0
. Therefore,
T
is
u
-
M
-weakly compact.
Also, we have
\{e_{n}\}\subset T(U)
, where
U
is
the closed unit ball of
\ell_{1}
, since
\left\lVert e_{n}\right\rVert=1
for each

, therefore
T
is not
L
-weakly compact. But every norm bounded disjoint sequence in solid hull of

is

-convergent to zero. Hence

is

-
L
-weakly compact.

Remark 1

If

is a Banach lattice with strong unit, then it follows from Theorem 2.3 of

that

.
That is, for a sequence

. So,

for each Banach lattice

2.

for each Banach space

-

Example 2

Let

On the other hand, we can easily see

Lemma 1

Let
T:E\rightarrow X
be a continuous operator from a Banach
lattice
E
to a Banach space
X
, let
A
be a norm bounded solid subset of
E
,
and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a norm continuous subadditive function. If
f(Tx_{n})\rightarrow 0
holds
for each disjoint sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
A
, then for each
\epsilon>0
there exists some

u\in E^{+}
lying in the ideal generated by A such that

f(T(|x|-u)^{+})<\epsilon

holds for all
x\in A
.

Theorem 2.1

Let
T:E\to F
be a continuous operator between two Banach lattices
E
and
F
, let
A
be a norm bounded solid subset of
E
. If
Tx_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
holds for each disjoint sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
A
, then for each
w\in F^{+}
and each
\epsilon>0
there exists some
u\in E^{+}
lying in the ideal generated by
A
such that

\|\left\lvert T(|x|-u)^{+}\right\rvert\wedge w\|<\epsilon

holds for all
x\in A
.

Proof

Let
w\in F^{+}
and
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary but fixed.
Define map
f_{w}:F\to\mathbb{R}
as

f_{w}(x)=\left\lVert\left\lvert x\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert,

for each
x\in F
. It is obvious that
f_{w}
is subadditive and norm continuous.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
be a disjoint sequence in
A
. It follows from assumption and

f_{w}(Tx_{n})=\left\lVert\left\lvert Tx_{n}\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

that
f_{w}(Tx_{n})\to 0
.
Therefore, by Lemma
1
there exists some
u\in E^{+}
lying in the ideal generated by
A
such that

f_{w}(T(|x|-u)^{+})<\epsilon

for all
x\in A
. That is,

\left\lVert\left\lvert T(|x|-u)^{+}\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert<\epsilon

for all
x\in A
. Thus, the proof is complete.

and for each

-

-

-weakly compact operators are closed subspaces of the vector space of continuous operators.

Theorem 2.2

For Banach lattice
E
and
F
, and a Banach space
X
the following hold.

LW_{u}(X,E)
and
MW_{u}(E,F)
are closed vector subspaces of
L(X,E)
and
L(E,F)
, respectively.

Proof

At first we show that
LW_{u}(X,E)
is a subspace of
L(X,E)
.
Obviously,
LW_{u}(X,E)
is closed under the scalar multiplication. Let
T,S\in LW_{u}(X,E)
we prove that
T+S\in LW_{u}(X,E)
.
Let
U
be the closed unit ball of
X
. We claim that
\sol((T+S)(U))^{+}\subseteq\sol(T(U))^{+}+\sol(S(U))^{+}
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sol((T+S)(U))^{+}=

\displaystyle\quad\{y\in E^{+}:y\leq\left\lvert(T+S)(u)\right\rvert\text{ for some }u\in U\}

\displaystyle=\{y_{1}+y_{2}:y_{1}+y_{2}\leq\left\lvert Tu+Su\right\rvert\text{ for some }u\in U

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ such that }0\leq y_{1}\leq\left\lvert Tu\right\rvert,0\leq y_{2}\leq\left\lvert Su\right\rvert\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{y_{1}+y_{2}:y_{1}\in\sol(T(U))^{+},y_{2}\in\sol(S(U))^{+}\}

\displaystyle=\sol(T(U))^{+}+\sol(S(U))^{+}

Now, assume that
\{y_{n}\}\subset\sol((T+S)(U))
be a disjoint sequence. Clearly,
\{\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert\}
is a disjoint sequence in

\sol((T+S)(U))
. By the above argument there exist
\{y_{1,n}\}\subset\sol(T(U))^{+}
and
\{y_{2,n}\}\subset\sol(S(U))^{+}
such that

\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert=y_{1,n}+y_{2,n}
for each
n
. If
n\neq m
then

y_{i,n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}y_{i,m}\leq(y_{1,n}+y_{2,n})\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}(y_{1,m}+y_{2,m})=\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}\left\lvert y_{m}\right\rvert=0
for
i=1,2
. Thus,
\{y_{i,n}\}
is a disjoint sequence for
i=1,2
. Since
T
and
S
are
u
-
L
-weakly compact we have
y_{i,n}\xrightarrow{un}0
for
i=1,2
.
Now, for
w\in E^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert(y_{1,n}+y_{2,n})\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert(y_{1,n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w)+(y_{2,n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert y_{1,n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert+\left\lVert y_{2,n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert,

therefore,
y_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
. That is,
T+S\in LW_{u}(X,E)
.

Now, to prove that
LW_{u}(X,E)
is closed in
L(X,E)
we show that

T\in\overline{LW_{u}(X,E)}
is
u
-
L
-weakly compact.
Let
\epsilon>0
and
w\in E^{+}
be arbitrary but fixed from now on and let
S\in LW_{u}(X,E)
such that
\left\lVert T-S\right\rVert<\frac{\epsilon}{3}
. Assume that
\{y_{n}\}\subset\sol(T(U))
be a disjoint sequence we want to show that
y_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
choose some
u_{n}\in U
such that
\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert T(u_{n})\right\rvert
. By using Part
2
of Corollary
1
choose
u\in E^{+}
lying in the ideal generated by
S(X)
such that

\|(|Sx|-u)^{+}\wedge w\|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}
for all
x\in U
.
We have

\displaystyle 0\leq\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lvert(S-T)u_{n}\right\rvert+\left\lvert Su_{n}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lvert(S-T)u_{n}\right\rvert+(\left\lvert Su_{n}\right\rvert-u)^{+}+\left\lvert Su_{n}\right\rvert.

It follows from
(

, Theorem 1.13)
that for each

there exist

with

and

such that
\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert=v_{n}+w_{n}+z_{n}
. Clearly,
\{z_{n}\}
is a disjoint sequence in
\sol(S(U))
.
Since

is
u
-
L
-weakly compact,

.
Specifically, there exists

such that we have
\left\lVert z_{n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert<\frac{\epsilon}{3}

.
On the other hand,

\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert(S-T)u_{n}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert S-T\right\rVert<\frac{\epsilon}{3},

and since

for all

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle 0\leq\left\lVert\left\lvert y_{n}\right\rvert\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert(v_{n}+w_{n}+z_{n})\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert+\left\lVert w_{n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert+\left\lVert z_{n}\mathrel{\text{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\wedge$}}}}w\right\rVert

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}=\epsilon.

As
w\in E^{+}
is arbitrary hence
y_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
. Therefore,
T\in LW_{u}(X,E)
.

.

-

. Let
T
be in the closure of the set of all
u
-
M
-weakly compact operators, and let

be a disjoint sequence of
E

\|x_{n}\|\leq 1

Tx_{n}\xrightarrow{un}0
. Let

\epsilon>0

We have

\displaystyle|Tx_{n}|\wedge w

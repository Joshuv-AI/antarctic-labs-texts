Uniform Degeneration of hyperbolic surfaces with boundary along harmonic map rays
Kento Sakai

Abstract
. We study the degeneration of hyperbolic surfaces along a ray given by the harmonic map parametrization of Teichmüller space. The direction of the ray is determined by a holomorphic quadratic differential on a punctured Riemann surface, which has poles of order ≥ 2 \geq 2 at each puncture. We show that the rescaled distance functions of the universal covers of hyperbolic surfaces uniformly converge, on a certain non-compact region containing a fundamental domain, to the intersection number with the vertical measured foliation given by the holomorphic quadratic differential determining the direction of the ray. This implies that hyperbolic surfaces along the ray converge to the dual ℝ \mathbb{R} -tree of the vertical measured foliation in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff. As an application, we determine the limit of the hyperbolic surfaces in the Thurston boundary.



1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a connected and oriented surface.
We suppose that
S
admits a complete, finite area hyperbolic structure with geodesic boundary.
The
Teichmüller space
of
S
is the space of marked hyperbolic structures on
S
.
There are many kinds of parametrizations for the Teichmüller space (for example, Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates, Teichmüller’s parametrization, the earthquake parametrization, the Fricke coordinate, and so on), and each of them demonstrates geometric properties of Teichmüller spaces.
In this paper, we are interested in the parametrization by the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials via harmonic maps between hyperbolic surfaces.

We here review the history of the parametrization given by harmonic maps.
If the surface is closed,
a harmonic diffeomorphism uniquely exists in the homotopy class of a homeomorphism
[
ES64
,
Har67
,
SY78
,
Sam78
]
.
Based on this result, Wolf
[
Wol89
]
proved that the Teichmüller space is homeomorphic to the vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on a Riemann surface by taking the associated Hopf differentials.
Wolf’s parametrization of Teichmüller space was also found independently and by different means by Hitchin
[
Hit87
]
and Wan
[
Wan92
]
.
For hyperbolic surfaces with cusps, Lohkamp
[
Loh91
]
established an analogous parametrization by the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials with poles of order at least
1
.
For surfaces with closed boundaries, the parametrization is provided with the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials with poles of order 2 and fixed residues.
Gupta
[
Gup21
]
similarly parametrized the Teichmüller space for hyperbolic surfaces with crown ends using the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials with poles of order at least 3 and fixed principal parts.
Recently, Allegretti
[
All24
]
addressed surfaces with punctures and boundaries of both types, and some exceptional types of surfaces, which had never been dealt with.

Figure 1.
Hyperbolic surface with a closed boundary (left side) and a crown end (right side).

In the case that a hyperbolic surface is closed or with cusps,
the interesting fact of this parametrization is that, if we deform a hyperbolic surface along a ray from the origin in the vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on a Riemann surface, these hyperbolic surfaces converge to a point in the Thurston boundary
[
Wol89
,
Sak23
]
.
Moreover, the limit point is the projective class of the vertical measured foliation determining the direction of the ray.
In terms of metric geometry, Wolf studied the degenerations of hyperbolic structures along a ray, by realizing them as metric spaces via harmonic maps.
As he mentioned in
[
Wol95
]
, the distance functions of the metric lifted to the universal covering of the hyperbolic surfaces uniformly converge on compact sets.
Wolf also described degenerations of hyperbolic structures by using the notion of the equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff convergence, which was introduced by Paulin
[
Pau88
]
. From these results, Wolf recovered the result of Morgan-Shalen
[
MS84
]
in the case of
2
-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds.

In this paper, we consider the degeneration of hyperbolic surfaces with closed boundaries or crown ends (see
Figure

1
).
For the sake of simplicity, first we explain our result in the case that
S
is an ideal
k
-gon for
k\geq 3
.
In this case, we deal with harmonic maps from the complex plane
\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}P^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
to the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with polynomial Hopf differentials.
Harmonic maps of this type were first studied in
[
HTTW95
]
where it was shown that their images form ideal polygons.
Let
\varphi
be a polynomial quadratic differential of degree
k-2
on
\mathbb{C}
, which has a pole of order
k+2
at
\infty\in\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.
Then, there exists a unique marked hyperbolic ideal
k
-gon
[C_{\varphi},f_{\varphi}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
satisfying the following condition: there exists a harmonic diffeomorphism
h_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{C}\to C_{\varphi}
such that the
Hopf differential
of
h_{\varphi}
is equal to
\varphi
,
where the Hopf differential of
h_{\varphi}
is the
(2,0)
-part of the pullback metric
{h_{\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{\varphi}
.
Therefore, for each
t>0
, we obtain the hyperbolic surface
[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]
and the harmonic map
h_{t\varphi}
corresponding to
t\varphi
.
The one-parameter family
\{[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]\}_{t>0}
of the hyperbolic surfaces is called the
harmonic map ray
in the direction of
\varphi
.
The pullback metric
{h_{t\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{t\varphi}
rescaled by
\frac{1}{4t}
defines a distance function on
\mathbb{C}
.
For each
t>0
, let
d_{t}
denote the distance function.
On the other hand, another metric space is obtained from
\varphi
.
Let
T(\varphi)
denote the leaf space of the vertical measured foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
.
The transverse measure of
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
defines a distance
d_{T(\varphi)}
on
T(\varphi)
.
The metric space
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
is called the
dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree
to
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
.
If
\varphi
is a polynomial quadratic differential on
\mathbb{C}
, it is a simplicial metric tree.
Our main result in the case of an ideal
k
-gon is the following.

Theorem A

(
Corollary

3.3
)
.

The rescaled metric spaces
(\mathbb{C},d_{t})
uniformly converge to the simplicial metric tree
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff as
t
tends to
\infty
.

This theorem is obtained by showing that, for every
\varepsilon>0
and every sufficiently large
t>0
, the projection map
\pr_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{C}\to T(\varphi)
to the leaf space defines an
\varepsilon
-approximation
between
(\mathbb{C},d_{t})
and
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
, namely, for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
,
|d_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})-d_{T(\varphi)}(\pr_{\varphi}x_{1},\pr_{\varphi}x_{2})|<\varepsilon
.
In other words, the distance functions
d_{t}
uniformly converge to
d_{T(\varphi)}\circ\operatorname{Pr}_{\varphi}
on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, which is a non-compact domain.
Here, we define the map
\operatorname{Pr}_{\varphi}
by
\operatorname{Pr}_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})=(\pr_{\varphi}x_{1},\pr_{\varphi}x_{2})
.

Next, we present the general case.
Let
X
be a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures and let
\varphi
be a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
with poles only at the punctures.
We suppose that the order of
\varphi
at each puncture is at least
2
.
The orders of
\varphi
at punctures determine a topological surface
S
with closed boundaries or crown ends.
Then, there exists a unique hyperbolic structure
[C_{\varphi},f_{\varphi}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
satisfying the same condition as the ideal polygon case. Thus, having the hyperbolic surface
[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]
associated with
t\varphi
for each
t>0
, we obtain the harmonic map ray
\{[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]\}
.

To describe the limit of a harmonic map ray, we take the universal covering
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
.
Since
\widetilde{X}
is conformally equivalent to the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}
(or the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{E}
), we identify
\widetilde{X}
with
\mathbb{H}
(or
\mathbb{E}
).
Let
\Gamma
be the discrete free subgroup of the orientation-preserving isometry group
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H})
of the hyperbolic plane (or
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{E})
of the Euclidean plane) with
X=\widetilde{X}/\Gamma
.
Taking the lift of
{h_{t\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{t\varphi}
to
\widetilde{X}
and rescaling the metric by
\frac{1}{4t}
, we get the distance function
d_{t}\colon\widetilde{X}\times\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
from the rescaled metric on
\widetilde{X}
, which is invariant under the action of
\Gamma
.
To state our main theorem, we consider the hyperbolic plane (or the Euclidean plane) with the Gromov boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
of
\Gamma
attached in an equivariant manner.
We denote this union with a natural topology by
\widetilde{X}\cup\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
.
Its formal definition is introduced in
Section

4
.
Let
T(\varphi)
be the dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree to the vertical measured foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{\varphi},\mathrm{vert}}
of
\widetilde{\varphi}
.
The transverse measure of
\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{\varphi},\mathrm{vert}}
defines a distance on
T(\varphi)
.
We define a map
\pr_{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{X}\to T(\varphi)
as the projection to the leaf space of
\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{\varphi},\mathrm{vert}}
.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

5.1
)
.

Let
U\subset\widetilde{X}\cup\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
be a neighborhood of
\partial_{\infty}{\Gamma}
.
The sequence of the rescaled metric spaces
(\widetilde{X}\setminus U,d_{t})
uniformly converges to
(\pr_{\varphi}(\widetilde{X}\setminus U),d_{T(\varphi)})
in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff as
t
tends to
\infty
.

By introducing the complement of a neighborhood of
\partial_{\infty}\Gamma
, we can characterize all domains where
d_{t}
converge uniformly.
Note that the complement
\widetilde{X}\setminus U
can be a non-compact subset.

Theorem

B
implies that, in the sense of
[
Wol95
, Definition 5.1]
, the identity maps
\id\colon\widetilde{X}\to(\widetilde{X},d_{t})
converge to
\pr_{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{X}\to T(\varphi)
.
The identity map
\id\colon\widetilde{X}\to(\widetilde{X},d_{t})
is a harmonic map to the metric space
(\widetilde{X},{\widetilde{h}_{t\varphi}}\!^{\ast}\widetilde{C}_{t\varphi}/\sqrt{4t})=(\widetilde{X},d_{t})
, and the limit
\pr_{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{X}\to T(\varphi)
is also a harmonic map as in
[
Wol95
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lastly, we describe an application of
Theorem

B
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{A}
be the sets of isotopy classes of simple closed curves and properly embedded arcs on
S
, respectively.
We consider the hyperbolic structures in terms of the length function
\ell_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}
, and describe the limit along a ray.

Corollary C

(
Corollary

6.4
)
.

(4t)^{-1/2}\ell_{\ast}[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]\to I_{\ast}\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}
as
t\to\infty
.

From this corollary, we find that the direction of the ray determines the limit point in the projective space
P(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}})
of functions.
Since the limit is an accumulation point of the sequence of hyperbolic surfaces
\{[C_{t\varphi},f_{t\varphi}]\}_{t>0}
, there is a point in the
Thurston boundary
corresponding to the limit.
In
Section

6.1
, we define the Thurston boundary of the Teichmüller space of a surface with closed boundaries or crown ends in a similar way to
[
ALPS16
]
.

We briefly remark on some relevant results.

Using the notion of ultralimit, we obtain the limit metric space
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty})
of the sequence
(\widetilde{X},d_{t})
as described in
[
KL97
]
.
Furthermore, it follows from
[
KS97
]
that harmonic maps
h_{t}\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{C}_{t\varphi}
converge uniformly on compact subsets of
\widetilde{X}
to a harmonic map
h_{\infty}\colon\widetilde{X}\to(X_{\infty},d_{\infty})
.
These results are obtained under a general framework.
In contrast, the result in
Theorem

B
is more desirable, as it provides explicit descriptions of the limits of both metric spaces and harmonic maps, with convergence valid even on the noncompact domains
\widetilde{X}\setminus U
.
Additionally, our conclusion is achieved without relying on significant tools in
[
KL97
,
KS97
]
.

Recently, for the results on which this paper is based, analogous results have been obtained for the Hitchin component of surface group representations into
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
.
For instance, the paper
[
LTW22
]
demonstrates that harmonic maps arising from rays of cubic differentials converge to a harmonic map into a building and the associated holonomy representations into
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
also converge to a representation into the isometry group of the building.
Moreover, the paper
[
Nie23
]
establishes an analogue of Gupta’s work
[
Gup21
]
for convex
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
-structures.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Harmonic maps

Let
X
be a Riemann surface with a local holomorphic coordinate
z
and
(S,\rho(w)|dw|^{2})
be a surface with a Riemannian metric
\rho
, where
w
denotes a isothermal coordinate of
\rho
.
A
C^{2}
map
f\colon X\to(S,\rho)
is
harmonic
if
f
satisfies

f_{z\overline{z}}+\frac{\rho_{w}}{\rho}f_{z}f_{\overline{z}}=0.

For a map
f\colon X\to(S,\rho)
, the
(2,0)
-part of the pullback metric
(f^{\ast}\rho)^{2,0}
is a quadratic differential on
X
, which is called the
Hopf differential
of
f
.
In the Teichmüller theory of harmonic maps, the following is fundamental: if the Jacobian of
f
is nowhere vanishing,
f
is a harmonic map if and only if its Hopf differential is a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
(see
[
Sam78
]
).

2.2.
Teichmüller space

The
Teichmüller space
is the space of hyperbolic structures with marking on a topological surface.
In this subsection, we will define a
marking
as a map from a punctured Riemann surface to a hyperbolic surface with closed geodesic boundaries or crown ends.

Let
\overline{S}
be an oriented, closed surface of genus
g
.
From
\overline{S}
, we remove
n
open disks
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}\subset\overline{S}
whose closures are pairwise disjoint.
In addition, for each
1\leq i\leq n
, we remove
m_{i}\geq 0
distinct points on
\partial D_{i}
.
We denote the resulting surface by
S
.
Except for 6 cases (see
Figure

2
),
S
admits a complete hyperbolic structure of finite area with totally geodesic boundary.

Figure 2.
Exceptional surfaces

Let
X
be a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures.
At each puncture
p_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq n)
, we pick
m_{i}
tangent directions
v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,m_{i}}\in(T_{p_{i}}X\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
counterclockwise.
The data
\theta=(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of tuples
\theta_{i}=(v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,m_{i}})
is called
direction data
.
Taking a real oriented blow-up at each puncture
p_{i}
and removing the points corresponding to the directions in the direction data, we obtain the surface
S
.
Thus, we can regard
X
as a subset of
S
.

Let
C
be a complete, finite area hyperbolic surface homeomorphic to
S
with totally geodesic boundary.
A
marking
of
C
from
(X,\theta)
is a diffeomorphism
f\colon X\to C\setminus\partial C
which is the restriction of a diffeomorphism
\bar{f}\colon S\to S
.
For a hyperbolic surface
C
homeomorphic to
S
and its marking
f
from
(X,\theta)
, we call the pair
(C,f)
the
marked hyperbolic surface
.

Let
(C_{1},f_{1})
and
(C_{2},f_{2})
be marked hyperbolic surfaces from
(X,\theta)
.
We define the equivalence relation
(C_{1},f_{1})\sim(C_{2},f_{2})
,
if there exists an isometry
g\colon C_{1}\to C_{2}
such that
g\circ f_{1}
is isotopic to
f_{2}
relative to the direction data
\theta
.
The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(S)
is the quotient space of equivalence classes of marked hyperbolic surfaces homeomorphic to
S
.
The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
is homeomorphic to the Euclidean space of dimension

6g-6+\sum_{i=1}^{l}(m_{i}+3).

2.3.
Principal part, analytic residue

Let
X
be a closed Riemann surface with punctures
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
and
\varphi
a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
with poles at punctures.
We take a coordinate disk
(D_{i},z_{i})
around each puncture
p_{i}
.
Let
k_{i}
be the order of
\varphi
at
p_{i}
.
The 1-form
\pm\sqrt{\varphi(z_{i})}\,dz_{i}
(up to the multiplication by
\pm 1
) can be expanded in a Laurent series.
Then the principal part of the Laurent series (i.e. terms of degree at least
-1
) is

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\pm(c_{r}z_{i}^{-r}+c_{r-1}z_{i}^{-r+1}+\cdots+c_{1}z_{i}^{-1})\,dz_{i}&(k_{i}\text{ is even})\\
\pm z^{-1/2}(c_{r}z_{i}^{-r}+c_{r-1}z_{i}^{-r+1}+\cdots+c_{1}z_{i}^{-1})\,dz_{i}&(k_{i}\text{ is odd}),\end{cases}

where
k_{i}=2r
or
2r+1
, and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}
are complex numbers.

Definition 2.1

(analytic residue)
.

We call the form in
Equation

2.1
the
principal part
of
\varphi
at
p_{i}
with respect to the coordinate disk
(D_{i},z_{i})
.
The coefficient of degree
-1
term in
Equation

2.1
is determined up to the multiplication by
\pm 1
. It is called the
analytic residue

\Res(\varphi,p_{i})
of
\varphi
at
p_{i}
.

By this definition, if
k_{i}
is odd, the analytic residue of
\varphi
at
p_{i}
is
0
, and if
k_{i}=2
, the analytic residue of
\varphi
at
p_{i}
equals to the square root of the coefficient of
z_{i}^{-2}
in the Laurent series of
\varphi
.
By the residue theorem, we have

\pm\int_{\gamma}\sqrt{\varphi}=\pm 2\pi i\Res(\varphi,p_{i}),

where
\gamma
is a loop around
p_{i}
.
The principal part depends on the choice of the coordinate disk, but the analytic residue does not.

Conversely, if a form
P_{i}(z_{i})\,dz_{i}
which is expressed as
Equation

2.1
is given on each coordinate disk
(D_{i},z_{i})
, we call the data
P=(\pm P_{i}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}
the
prescribed principal part data
.
Let
\mathrm{QD}(X,P)
denote the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
X
whose principal part at each puncture
p_{i}
with respect to
(D_{i},z_{i})
is equal to
P_{i}(z_{i})
.
The analytic residue
\Res(P,p_{i})
of
P
at
p_{i}
is the coefficient of
z_{i}^{-1}
in
P_{i}(z_{i})
.

2.4.
Direction data obtained from principal parts

We consider a prescribed principal part data
P=(P_{i}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}
on a closed Riemann surface
X
of genus
g
with
n
punctures
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
.
We suppose the quadratic differential
P_{i}^{2}=P_{i}(z_{i})^{2}dz_{i}^{2}
has a pole of order
m_{i}+2
at each puncture
p_{i}
.
In the case of
m_{i}\geq 1
, if each leaf of horizontal foliation of
P_{i}^{2}
on
(D_{i},z_{i})
tends to
p_{i}
, its tangent direction converges to one of the
m_{i}
asymptotic directions
v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,m_{i}}
determined by
P_{i}^{2}
(e.g. see
[
GW19
]
).
We set
\theta_{i}=(v_{i,1},\ldots,v_{i,m_{i}})
and
\theta(P)=(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

In the following, we suppose that the marking of every
[C,f]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is from
(X,\theta(P))
to
C
.

2.5.
Length data

Following
[
All24
]
, we here define the
length data
of the marked hyperbolic surface
C\in\mathcal{T}(S)
.
Let
\partial_{i}
denote the closed boundary or crown end of
C
corresponding to
p_{i}
by the marking of
C
.
We first consider the case that the number of cusps of
\partial_{i}
is a positive even number.
On the hyperbolic surface
C
, we put horocycles centered at each ideal vertex
p_{i,j}\ (1\leq j\leq m_{i})
of
\partial_{i}
, and truncate ideal vertices along the horocycles.
If the horocycles are disjoint each other, a geodesic segment is left on each geodesic boundary between adjacent two ideal vertices
p_{i,j},p_{i,j+1}
. Let
\ell_{j}
denote the length of the geodesic segment.
Then, the alternate sum

L_{i}\coloneqq\pm\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}\ell_{j}

is well-defined up to the multiplication by
\pm 1
(see
[
Gup21
, Lemma 2.10]
).
If two adjacent horocycles have an intersection, we define the length
\ell_{j}
is negative.

Definition 2.2
.

The number
L_{i}
up to
\pm 1
is called the
metric residue
of
C
with respect to
\partial_{i}
.

In the case that the number of cusps of
\partial_{i}
is odd, we define
L_{i}=0
.
For closed boundary
\partial_{i}
, we define
L_{i}
as the length of
\partial_{i}
in the metric of
C
.

2.6.
Compatibility

We introduce the compatibility between the principal part data
P=(P_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and the length data
L=(L_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The length data
(L_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of
C\in\mathcal{T}(S)
is
compatible
with
P
if
|L_{i}|=4\pi|\Im\Res(P,p_{i})|
holds for each
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Remark 2.4
.

The compatibility condition is introduced by Gupta
[
Gup21
]
for hyperbolic surfaces with crown ends and by Sagman
[
Sag23
]
for those with closed boundaries, and Allegretti
[
All24
]
summarized up the both cases.
The condition in
Definition

2.3
is apparently different from one in
[
All24
, p.3855 (1),(2)]
, however we find these conditions are the same by a direct computation. (Note that the definitions of “analytic residue” and “metric residue” in papers
[
Gup21
,
Sag23
,
All24
]
differ slightly from each other.)

2.7.
Parametrization

The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{L}(S)
can be parametrized by the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
X
via harmonic maps. In the case that the differentials have a double pole at each puncture, Wolf proved the existence of harmonic maps
[
Wol91b
]
and Sagman gave the parametrization result
[
Sag23
]
.
In the case that the differentials have a higher order pole at each puncture, the existence and parametrization results were given by Gupta
[
Gup21
]
.
Allegretti
[
All24
]
also considered the mixed cases, which had not been treated in the former papers.

Theorem 2.5

(Existence,
[
Wol91b
,
Gup21
,
All24
]
)
.

For each marked hyperbolic surface
[C,f]\in\mathcal{T}_{L}(S)
, there exists a unique harmonic map
h_{[C,f]}=h\colon X\to C\setminus\partial C
such that the Hopf differential of
h
is in
\mathrm{QD}(X,P)
and
h
is homotopic to
f
relative to the direction data
\theta(P)
.

Theorem 2.6

(Parametrization,
[
Gup21
,
Sag23
,
All24
]
)
.

We define the map

\Psi\colon\mathcal{T}_{L}(S)\to\mathrm{QD}(X,P)

by assigning each
[C,f]\in\mathcal{T}_{L}(S)
to the Hopf differential of the harmonic map
h_{[C,f]}
.
Then, the map
\Psi
is a homeomorphism.

Let
\mathrm{QD}(X)
be the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
X
with poles at punctures.
By
Theorem

2.6
, given
\varphi\in\mathrm{QD}(X)
, we have the hyperbolic surface
C_{\varphi}
and the harmonic diffeomorphism
h_{\varphi}\colon X\to C_{\varphi}
such that
\Psi([C_{\varphi},h_{\varphi}])=\varphi
.
Therefore, we obtain the well-defined map

\rho\colon\mathrm{QD}(X)\to\operatorname{Met}(X)\text{ given by }\rho(\varphi)={h_{\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{\varphi},

where
\operatorname{Met}(X)
denotes the set of Riemannian metrics on
X
.
Note that
\rho(\varphi)
can be incomplete, if a puncture is a regular point, zero or simple pole of
\varphi
.

Let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
be the universal covering of
X
.
The universal covering
\widetilde{X}
is identified with the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
in the case that
X
is the Riemann sphere and the number of punctures is
1
or
2
, and with the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
in the other case.
Ler
\Gamma
be the discrete free subgroup of
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\widetilde{X})
such that
X=\widetilde{X}/\Gamma
.
The lift metric of
{h_{\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{\varphi}
to
\widetilde{X}
is denoted by
g_{\varphi}
.

2.8.
Measured foliation and dual R-tree

Let
\varphi
be a holomorphic quadratic differential on a Riemann surface
X
.
We introduce the measured foliations and the
\mathbb{R}
-tree obtained from
\varphi
(for more details, see
[
Wol95
]
).
In a
natural coordinates

\zeta=\xi+i\eta
which is defined on a sufficiently small neighborhood of a nonzero point of
\varphi
,
the holomorphic quadratic differential
\varphi
is represented by
d\zeta^{2}
.
A natural coordinate is uniquely determined up to a multiplication
\pm 1
and additive constant.
Therefore, vertical lines
\{\xi=\text{constant}\}
(resp. horizontal lines
\{\eta=\text{constant}\}
) determine a singular foliation on
\widetilde{X}
which is pronged at each zero of
\varphi
.
The number of prongs at a zero is equal to
(\text{the order of the zero})+2
.
The density
|d\xi|
(resp.
|d\eta|
) defines a transverse measure on the singular foliation.
The measured foliation obtained from
\varphi
is called the
vertical measured foliation
(resp. horizontal measured foliation) of
\varphi
and denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{hori}}
).

Taking the lift
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
of the vertical measured foliation of
\varphi
to the universal covering
\widetilde{X}
, we obtain the
leaf space

T(\varphi)
which is given by collapsing each leaf of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
to a point.
The
dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree
to
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
is the metric space
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
, where
d_{T(\varphi)}
is the distance function on
T(\varphi)
induced by the transverse measure
|d\xi|
.
The projection
\pr_{\varphi}\colon\widetilde{X}\to T(\varphi)
is called the
collapsing map
.

2.9.
Geometry of harmonic maps

Let
X
be a Riemann surface and
Y
be a hyperbolic surface with finite area.
We suppose that
h\colon X\to Y
is a harmonic diffeomorphism and
\varphi
is the Hopf differential of
h
.
The norm
|\nu(h)|\coloneqq|h_{\overline{z}}/h_{z}|
of the Beltrami differential of
h
is a well-defined function on
X
.
We set
G(h)=\log(1/|\nu(h)|)
.
The function
G(h)
plays an important role in studying the geometry of harmonic maps.
In
[
Min92
]
, Minsky gave useful estimates for the function
G(h)
, which originated from Wolf’s work
[
Wol91a
]
.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Min92
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

We fix a point
p\in X
which is nonzero of
\varphi
.
We suppose a
|\varphi|
-metric ball
B_{|\varphi|}(p,r)
contains no zero of
\varphi
and is embedded into
X
.
Then, we have

G(h)(p)\leq\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{\operatorname{Area}(X)}{2\pi r^{2}}\right).

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Min92
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
p\in X
be a point
|\varphi|
-distant at least
d
away from zeros of
\varphi
.
If
G(h)
is bounded by
B
from above on
B_{|\varphi|}(p,d)
, then we have

G(h)(p)\leq\frac{B}{\cosh d}.

Using these propositions, we prepare some estimates we will use later.
Let
p_{i}
be a puncture of
X
.
If
p_{i}
is a double pole of
\varphi
, we can take a disk neighborhood
U_{i}
of
p_{i}
such that
(U_{i}\setminus\{p_{i}\},|\varphi|)
is isometric to a Euclidean one-sided infinite annulus.
If
p_{i}
is a pole of order higher than
2
, we can take a
sink neighborhood
of
p_{i}
, which does not contain zeros of
\varphi
(see Definition 2
[
GW19
]
).
The sink neighborhood
U_{i}
is broken into
half-planes
and
horizontal strips
by the horizontal measured foliation of
\varphi
.
Each half-plane is isometric to a Euclidean half plane
\{(x,y)\mid y>0\}
and each horizontal strip is isometric to a Euclidean strip
\{(x,y)\mid a<y<b\}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

We suppose that the length of any nontrivial loop around
p_{i}
in
U_{i}\setminus\{p_{i}\}
is at least
2C
and
\partial U_{i}
is of distance at least
C
away from zeros of
\varphi
in the
|\varphi|
-metric.
Then, for each
p\in U_{i}\setminus\{p_{i}\}
,

G(h)(p)\leq 2e^{-f(p)}\sinh^{-1}(\operatorname{Area}(Y)/2\pi C^{2}),

where
f(p)=\operatorname{dist}_{|\varphi|}(p,\varphi^{-1}(0))
.

3.
Uniform convergence of distance functions: ideal polygon case

3.1.
Main result

First, we state and prove the main theorem in the case of ideal polygons, since this is the simplest case and many of the methods of the proof are the same as in the general case.

Let
S
be a topological ideal
k
-gon (
k\geq 3
), i.e.
S
is a topological closed disk and we remove distinct
k
points on the boundary.
In this case, the Riemann surface
X
is equivalent to
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}\cong\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi
is a polynomial quadratic differential on
\mathbb{C}
.
The point at infinity is a pole of order
k+2
, and
the degree of the polynomial differential
\varphi
equals
k-2
.
The principal part of
\varphi
determines the direction data at
\infty
.

For
t>0
, let
d_{t}
denote the distance function associated with the metric
(4t)^{-1}g_{t\varphi}
.
In the ideal polygon case, the metric
g_{t\varphi}
is equal to the pullback metric
{h_{t\varphi}}^{\ast}C_{t\varphi}
(see the last paragraph in
Section

2.7
).
Let
I_{\varphi}
denote the composite function of the collapsing map and the distance function of the dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree
T(\varphi)
to
\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}}
i.e. we set
I_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})=d_{T(\varphi)}(\pr_{\varphi}x_{1},\pr_{\varphi}x_{2})
.
Note that, in the ideal polygon case, the dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree is a simplicial metric tree.

Theorem 3.1
.

The family of distance functions
d_{t}
uniformly converges to
I_{\varphi}
on
X=\mathbb{C}
.

This theorem tells us that, for each
\varepsilon>0
, the collapsing map
\pr_{\varphi}
is an
\varepsilon
-approximation
between
(X,d_{t})
and
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
by taking a sufficiently large
t
, i.e. there exists
t_{1}>0
such that, for each
t>t_{1}
and each two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

(3.1)

|d_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})-d_{T(\varphi)}(\pr_{\varphi}x_{1},\pr_{\varphi}x_{2})|<\varepsilon.

Following
[
BH99
]
, we recall the definition of the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(X,d)
and
(Y,d^{\prime})
be metric spaces.
For
\varepsilon>0
, the relation
R\subset X\times Y
is called
\varepsilon
-
relation
between
(X,d)
and
(Y,d^{\prime})
if
R
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

the relation
R
is surjective i.e.
\pr_{1}(R)=X,\pr_{2}(R)=Y
,

(2)

if
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in R
, then
|d(x_{1},x_{2})-d(y_{1},y_{2})|<\varepsilon
.

A sequence of metric spaces
\{(X_{t},d_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
converges to a metric space
(Y,d)

in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff
as
t\to\infty
, if it satisfies the following: for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
t>t_{1}
, there exists an
\varepsilon
-relation
R_{t}
between
(X_{t},d_{t})
and
(Y,d^{\prime})
.

Setting
R=\{(x,y)\in X\times T(\varphi)\mid y=\pr_{\varphi}x\}
, we find that, for each
\varepsilon>0
,
R
is an
\varepsilon
-relation between
(X,d_{t})
and
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
for every
t>t_{1}
from
Equation

3.1
. Therefore we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

The metric space
(X,d_{t})
converges to
(T(\varphi),d_{T(\varphi)})
in the sense of Gromov-Hausdorff.

3.2.
One side of the inequality

In this section, we prepare some lemmas to prove
Theorem

3.1
.
For simplicity,
we denote the harmonic map
h_{t\varphi}\colon X\to C_{t\varphi}
by
h_{t}
, the function
G(h_{t})
by
G(t)
(see
Section

2.9
), and the metric
(4t)^{-1}g_{t\varphi}
by
g_{t}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each
t>0
, the inequality
I_{\varphi}<d_{t}
holds on
X
.

Proof.

We take any two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
.
By the definition of the distance on the dual
\mathbb{R}
-tree, we have

I_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})=\inf\left\{\int_{\gamma}|d\xi|\,\middle|\,\gamma\text{ is a curve connecting }x_{1}\text{ and }x_{2}\right\},

where
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
is natural coordinates of
\varphi
.
In the coordinates
\zeta
, the metric
g_{t}
is represented by

(3.2)

ds_{g_{t}}^{2}=\frac{\cosh G(t)+1}{2}\,d\xi^{2}+\frac{\cosh G(t)-1}{2}\,d\eta^{2}

(see
[
Min92
, Section 3]
).
From this representation, since we find
ds_{g_{t}}^{2}\geq d\xi^{2}
.
Let
\gamma_{t}\colon[0,1]\to X
be a
g_{t}
-geodesic segment from
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
.
Since
\int|d\xi|
defines the transverse measure of the vertical measured foliation of
\varphi
, for each
t>0
, we have

d_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})=\ell_{g_{t}}(\gamma_{t})=\int_{\gamma}ds_{g_{t}}\geq\int_{\gamma}|d\xi|\geq I_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Thus, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Since the above proof works similarly, we find the following: Let
(S,\rho)
be a surface with a convex metric,
h\colon X\to(S,\rho)
be a harmonic diffeomorphism and
\varphi
be its Hopf differential.
Then, the inequality
d_{h^{\ast}\rho}>I_{\varphi}
holds on
X
, where
d_{h^{\ast}\rho}
denotes the distance function associated with the pullback metric
h^{\ast}\rho
.

Estimate for vertical and horizontal lines

We next estimate the hyperbolic length of a vertical or horizontal line of

.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

be a sufficiently small sink neighborhood (see

) of
\infty
with respect to
\varphi
so that the length of any nontrivial loop around

(1)

Let

\varphi

is parametrized by the

\ell_{g_{t}}(H|_{[0,a]})-i(\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},H|_{[0,a]})<De^{-C\sqrt{t}}

\varphi

We suppose that

is parametrized by the

\ell_{g_{t}}(V)<De^{-C\sqrt{t}}.

Proof.

We may assume
\Gamma(s)
is a maximal horizontal (or vertical) line that is contained in

.
If
\Gamma(s)
is contained in a half-plane domain, it is a bi-infinite line, and if
\Gamma(s)
is contained in a strip domain, it is a one-sided infinite line.

We first consider the case that
\Gamma(s)
is a horizontal line.
Let

be maximal half-plane domains bounded by vertical lines contained in

.
Then, there exist adjacent

into a finite arc and at most two one-sided infinite lines.
The

-length of the finite arc is at most

of the width of strip domains which lies between

and
P_{i+1}
.
Let
\Gamma_{1}\colon[0,\infty)\to U

Then, the function

C+s
.
In the case that

is a vertical line as well, the same thing holds true.

Figure 3.

Here we introduce an inequality used later: for any constant

and

(3.5)

\sqrt{\cosh(Be^{-x})-1}\leq Ae^{-x}\ \ \ \ (x>x_{0}).

Since this inequality is equivalent to

and the right hand side converges to

as

3.5

We here consider the case where
\Gamma
is an infinite vertical line

, and for simplicity we suppose that
\Gamma

Then, setting
B=2\sinh^{-1}((k-2)\pi/2\pi C^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{g_{t}}(V)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{V}\sqrt{\cosh G(t)-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{V}\sqrt{\cosh(Be^{-f\sqrt{t}})-1}\,d\eta

\displaystyle(\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{proposition:estimateFromMinsky'sOne}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{V}e^{-f\sqrt{t}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{eq:BasicInequality}},

where the final inequality holds if

, since

.
We now have

\displaystyle\int_{V}e^{-\sqrt{t}f}\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{-W}^{W}e^{-f\circ V(s)\sqrt{t}}\,ds+2\int_{W}^{\infty}e^{-f\circ V(s)\sqrt{t}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-W}^{W}e^{-C\sqrt{t}}\,ds+2\int_{W}^{\infty}e^{-(s+C)\sqrt{t}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq e^{-C\sqrt{t}}\left(2W+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right).

We thus obtain
Equation

3.4
.

In the case where
\Gamma

\displaystyle\ell_{g_{t}}(H|_{[0,a]})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{H|_{[0,a]}}\sqrt{\cosh G(t)+1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{H|_{[0,a]}}\sqrt{\cosh G(t)-1}\,d\xi+\int_{H|_{[0,a]}}\,d\xi,

since
\sqrt{\cosh G(t)+1}<\sqrt{\cosh G(t)-1}+\sqrt{2}
.
Therefore we have

\ell_{g_{t}}(H|_{[0,a]})-i(\mathcal{F}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},H|_{[0,a]})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{H|_{[0,a]}}\sqrt{\cosh G(t)-1}\,d\xi.

Since we can evaluate the right hand side in a similar manner as the case of a vertical line,

3.3

∎

|I_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})-d_{t}(x_{1},x_{2})|<\varepsilon\ \ \ \ (\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X).

4.
Attaching the Cayley graph ends

In this section, to state the main theorem for a Riemann surface with a nontrivial fundamental group, we explain how to attach the ends of the Cayley graph to the universal covering of the Riemann surface.
Let
X
be a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
with
n>0
punctures.
If
(g,n)=(0,1)
or
(0,2)
, the Riemann surface
X
admits a Euclidean structure; otherwise, it admits a hyperbolic structure.
In the following, we regard
X
as the complete hyperbolic (or Euclidean) surface.
We fix the universal covering
\pi\colon\mathbb{H}\ (\text{or }\mathbb{E})\to X
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
denote the set of bi-infinite geodesics on
\mathbb{H}
(or
\mathbb{E}
) which are lifts of
geodesic ideal arcs
on
X
, where a geodesic ideal arc is an infinite hyperbolic (or Euclidean) geodesic connecting (possibly the same) two punctures of
X
(if
X
is a Euclidean cylinder, we suppose that geodesic ideal arcs on
X
are orthogonal to the core geodesic of
X
).

Let
\mathbf{e}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2g+n-1}\}
be a family of geodesic ideal arcs on
X
such that the complement of the union
\bigcup_{i}e_{i}
is a simply connected domain.
Let
\Gamma<\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H})
(or
\operatorname{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{E})
) be a discrete free subgroup with
X=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma
(or
\mathbb{E}/\Gamma
).
We fix a base point
p\in X\setminus\bigcup_{i}e_{i}
.
For each edge
e_{i}
, there exists a unique
\gamma_{i}\in\pi_{1}(X,p)
such that the algebraic intersection number of
\gamma_{i}
and
e_{j}
is
+1
if
i=j
or
0
if
i\neq j
.
The loops
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g+n-1}
generate the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X,p)
.
Let
G_{\mathbf{e}}
denote the Cayley graph of the group
\pi_{1}(X,p)
with respect to the generating set
\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{2g+n-1}\}
and we equip the graph
G_{\mathbf{e}}
with the word metric.
We define the word distance on
G_{\mathbf{e}}
.
The
Gromov boundary

\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}
of
G_{\mathbf{e}}
is the set of ends of the Cayley graph, i.e.

\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}=\{r\colon[0,\infty)\to G_{\mathbf{e}}\mid r\text{ is a geodesic ray in }G_{\mathbf{e}}\}/\sim,

where the equivalence relation
r_{1}\sim r_{2}
is defined by that
d(r_{1}(t),r_{2}(t))
is bounded from above for any
t\in[0,\infty)
.

The Gromov boundary
\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}
usually has the
cone topology
which is generated by the system
\{U(r,t,k)\}
of subsets.
Here, for each ray
r\colon[0,\infty)\to G_{\mathbf{e}}
, and for
t,k>0
, we define

U(r,t,k)\coloneqq\{[r_{1}]\in\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}\mid r_{1}(0)=e,d(r_{1}(t),r(t))<k\}.

It is known that the Gromov boundary
\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}
is homeomorphic to a Cantor set (e.g. see
[
BH99
, p.266]
).

However we define the same topology in another way.
Let
\iota\colon G_{\mathbf{e}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}
(or
\mathbb{E}
) be a
\pi_{1}(X,p)
-equivariant embedding.
We define a set
\mathcal{P}
of half-planes in
\mathbb{H}
(or
\mathbb{E}
) by

\mathcal{P}=\{P\subset\mathbb{H}\ (\text{or }\mathbb{E})\mid P=(\text{connected component of }\mathbb{H}\setminus\ell\ (\text{or }\mathbb{E}\setminus\ell)),\ell\in\mathcal{L}\}

Here we consider the set
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}=\mathbb{H}\sqcup\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}
(or
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\sqcup\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}
), which is topologized in the following way: for
P\in\mathcal{P}
, we define the set
U_{P}\subset\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
(or
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
) as

U_{P}=P\cup\{[r]\in\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}}\mid\exists T>0\text{ s.t. }\forall t>T,\iota\circ r(t)\in P\},

and let the union of
\{U_{P}\}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}
and all open sets of
\mathbb{H}
(or
\mathbb{E}
) be an open basis.

Next, we will show that the topology on
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
is independent of the choice of the family
\mathbf{e}
of geodesic ideal arcs, the basepoint
p
, and the embedding
\iota
.
In a similar way, we can check the same fact holds for
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
.
Let
\mathbf{e}_{j}=\{e_{1,j},\ldots,e_{2g+n-1,j}\}\ (j=1,2)
be a family of geodesic ideal arcs such that the complement of the union
\bigcup_{i}e_{i,j}
is a single simply connected domain.
We choose
p_{j}
as the basepoint of the fundamental group of
X
.
Let
\iota_{j}\colon G_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}\ (\text{or }\mathbb{E})
be a
\pi_{1}(X,p_{j})
-equivariant embedding.
Let
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{j}
denote the space
\mathbb{H}\sqcup\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}
which has a topology determined from
\mathbf{e}_{j},p_{j},
and
\iota_{j}
.
We construct a homeomorphism
\mathcal{I}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{1}\to\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{2}
.
Let
c
be a path from
p_{1}
to
p_{2}
and
\beta_{c}\colon\pi_{1}(X,p_{1})\to\pi(X,p_{2})
be the change-of-basepoint map defined by
[\gamma]\mapsto[c\cdot\gamma\cdot c^{-1}]
.
Let
f\colon G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\to G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}
be the extension map of
\beta_{c}
.
Since
f
is a quasi-isometry, it induces the homeomorphism
\partial f\colon\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\to\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}
.
We define the map
\mathcal{I}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{1}\to\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{2}
by

\mathcal{I}(x)=\begin{cases}x&(x\in\mathbb{H})\\
\partial f(x)&(x\in\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}).\end{cases}

Proposition 4.1
.

The map
\mathcal{I}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{1}\to\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{2}
is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show the continuity of
\mathcal{I}
at each point
x\in\partial_{\infty}G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}
.
Let
r_{1}\colon[0,\infty)\to G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}
be a representative of
x
.
We need to show that if
U_{P}
is a neighborhood of
[f\circ r_{1}]
, then it is also a neighborhood of
[r_{1}]
.
This assumption means that there exists
T>0
such that, for any
t>T
,
\iota_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t)\in U_{P}
.
We set
\widetilde{p}_{1}=\iota_{1}(e)\in\pi^{-1}(p_{1})
.
The embedding
\iota_{1}
maps vertices of
G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}
to the orbit
\Gamma\cdot\widetilde{p}_{1}
.
We define another embedding
\tau_{2}
of
G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}
by
\tau_{2}=\iota_{1}\circ\beta_{c}^{-1}
.
In other words, the graph
\tau_{2}(G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}})
is obtained by changing the edges of the graph
\iota_{1}(G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}})
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}
denote the sets of generators determined from edge systems
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2}
, respectively.
Then, the lipschitz constants of
\iota_{1},\tau_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}(\iota_{1})

\displaystyle=\max_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{1}}d_{\mathbb{H}}(\widetilde{p}_{1},\gamma\widetilde{p}_{1})

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}(\tau_{2})

\displaystyle=\max_{\gamma\in\mathcal{G}_{2}}d_{\mathbb{H}}(\widetilde{p}_{1},\gamma\widetilde{p}_{1}).

Since the images
\tau_{2}(G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}),\iota_{2}(G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}})
of Cayley graphs embedded into
\mathbb{H}
are mapped to a
(2g+n-1)
-bouquet embedded into
X
by
\pi
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

for any point
x\in G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}
.
Since
f\colon G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}\to G_{\mathbf{e}_{2}}
is an extension of
\beta_{c}
, for each vertex
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(X,p_{1})\subset G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}
, we see

.
Here, we prove that there exists a
K>0
such that

(4.1)

d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))<K\ \ \ \ (\forall t\geq 0).

First, for
t=i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the point
\gamma_{i}\coloneqq r_{1}(i)
is a vertex of the Cayley graph
G_{\mathbf{e}_{1}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(i),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(i))

\displaystyle=d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{2}\circ\beta_{c}(\gamma_{i}),\tau_{2}\circ\beta_{c}(\gamma_{i}))<C.

In the case of
t\in(i,i+1)\ (i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t))+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(i))+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(i),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(t),\tau_{2}\circ f\circ r_{1}(i))+d_{\mathbb{H}}(\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(i),\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C+(\operatorname{Lip}(\tau_{2})\operatorname{Lip}(f)+\operatorname{Lip}(\iota_{1}))|t-i|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C+\operatorname{Lip}(\tau_{2})\operatorname{Lip}(f)+\operatorname{Lip}(\iota_{1})\eqqcolon K.

This implies
\iota_{1}\circ r_{1}(t)\in U_{P}
for sufficiently large
t>0
.
Therefore, we obtain the conclusion.
∎

of ideal arcs, the basepoint

Thus, we can write

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

be a fundamental domain of
\Gamma

Let

Lemma

5.5

we find that the
g_{t}
-lengths of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}}\cap\bigcup\pi^{-1}(U_{i})
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{vert}}\cap\bigcup\pi^{-1}(U_{i})
is uniformly estimated.
Therefore we have,

\displaystyle\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}})

\displaystyle=\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}}\cap(X\setminus\bigcup_{i}\pi^{-1}(U_{i})))+\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}}\cap\bigcup\pi^{-1}(U_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq i(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}}\cap(X\setminus\bigcup_{i}\pi^{-1}(U_{i})))(1+De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}})

\displaystyle\quad+i(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}}\cap\bigcup_{i}\pi^{-1}(U_{i}))+De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle\leq i(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{hori}})+(K+1)De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=i(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\varphi,\mathrm{vert}},\Gamma_{1})+(K+1)De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=I_{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2})+(K+1)De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}.

We also have

\displaystyle\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{vert}})

\displaystyle=\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{vert}}\cap(X\setminus\bigcup\pi^{-1}(U_{i})))+\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{vert}}\cap\bigcup\pi^{-1}(U_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}(K+4N\varepsilon)+De^{-C\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle\leq De^{-\varepsilon\sqrt{t}}(K+4N\varepsilon+1).

And, we have

\ell_{g_{t}}(\Gamma_{t})<NM\varepsilon.

Therefore, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

In

,

6.
Convergence to the point in the Thurston boundary

6.1.
The Thurston compactification

We take
n
open disks
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
in an oriented, closed surface of genus
g
. We suppose that the closures
\overline{D_{1}},\ldots,\overline{D_{n}}
are pairwise disjoint. We remove
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
and
m_{i}\geq 0
points on the boundary of each
D_{i}
from the surface.
Let
S
be the resulting surface.
In this section, we introduce the Thurston boundary of the Teichmüller space of the surface
S
with closed boundaries or crown ends in a similar way to the paper
[
ALPS16
]
, which defines the Thurston compactifications of the Teichmüller spaces for surfaces with only closed boundary.

An
arc
on
S
is the image of a closed interval by an embedding which maps the boundary of the interval to
\partial S
and also the interior of the interval to
S\setminus\partial S
.
All homotopies of arcs are relative to
\partial S
.
An arc on
S
is
essential
if it is not homotopic to
\partial S
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves on
S
, and
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of homotopy classes of arcs on
S
.
We define the
length function

\ell_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}
as follows: for each
[C,f]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
,

\ell_{\ast}([C,f])=(\ell_{[C,f]}([\gamma]))_{[\gamma]\in\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}},

where
\ell_{[C,f]}([\gamma])=\inf_{\gamma\in[\gamma]}\ell_{C}(f(\gamma))
.

We set
P(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}})=(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}\setminus\{0\}\to P(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}})
be the projection.
We endow
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}
with the product topology and
P(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}})
with the quotient topology.

Proposition 6.1
.

\ell_{\ast}\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathcal{C}\cup\mathcal{A}}
is injective.

Proof.

A

is an annulus with finite points removed from one of its boundaries.
If we cut a hyperbolic surface

along disjoint

essential simple closed curves, then

is decomposed into pairs of pants and crowns, where

and

is the number of crown ends of

.
Let
\mathcal{K}
be the system of curves giving the decomposition.
As well known, the hyperbolic structure of the pair of pants is determined by the boundary lengths.
For the crown with

boundary cusps, its hyperbolic structure is determined by the lengths of

arcs contained in the crown (see

.

Definition 6.2
.

The closure of the image
\pi\circ\ell_{\ast}(\mathcal{T}(S))
is called the

of the Teichmüller space of
S
.
We denote it by
\overline{\mathcal{T}(S)}
.

Let

. Then,

The Teichmüller space

of

Since the Thurston compactification

\overline{\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{sym}}(S^{d})}
is a closed subset of
\overline{\mathcal{T}(S^{d})}

\overline{\mathcal{T}(S)}
is a compact set.

.

The boundary of

coincides with
\mathcal{PMF}(S)
.
The Thurston compactification
\overline{\mathcal{T}(S)}
is homeomorphic to a closed ball of dimension

6.2.
The limit along a ray in the Thurston boundary

In this section, we describe the convergence point of the family
\{C_{t\varphi}\}
in terms of a length function of the isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves and properly embedded, essential arcs.

Let

be the same surface as in

Let

As in the previous section, we suppose that

, we say that

.
Then, the

(see

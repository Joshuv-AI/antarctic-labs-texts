Embedding dimensions of matrices whose entries are indefinite distances in the pseudo-Euclidean space
Hiroshi Nozaki, Masashi Shinohara, Sho Suda

Abstract
A finite set of the Euclidean space is called an s s -distance set provided the number of Euclidean distances in the set is s s . Determining the largest possible s s -distance set for the Euclidean space of a given dimension is challenging. This problem was solved only when dealing with small values of s s and dimensions. Lisoněk (1997) achieved the classification of the largest 2-distance sets for dimensions up to 7 7 , using computer assistance and graph representation theory. In this study, we consider a theory analogous to these results of Lisoněk for the pseudo-Euclidean space ℝ p , q \mathbb{R}^{p,q} . We consider an s s -indefinite-distance set in a pseudo-Euclidean space that uses the value ‖ 𝒙 − 𝒚 ‖ = ( x 1 − y 1 ) 2 + ⋯ + ( x p − y p ) 2 − ( x p + 1 − y p + 1 ) 2 − ⋯ − ( x p + q − y p + q ) 2 ||\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}||=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{p}-y_{p})^{2}-(x_{p+1}-y_{p+1})^{2}-\cdots-(x_{p+q}-y_{p+q})^{2} instead of the Euclidean distance. We develop a representation theory for symmetric matrices in the context of s s -indefinite-distance sets, which includes or improves the results of Euclidean s s -distance sets with large s s values. Moreover, we classify the largest possible 2 2 -indefinite-distance sets for small dimensions.

The pair
(r,s)

Theorem 2.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field

, and
X
be a finite set in

. Let
s
be a nonnegative integer. Let
F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
be a

-variate polynomial with coefficients in

and a degree no greater than

.
Let the matrix
\boldsymbol{M}=(F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X}

(1)

{\rm rank}(\boldsymbol{M})\leq 2\dim_{s}(X)
.

(2)

If

\max\{r_{+},r_{-}\}\leq\dim_{s}(X)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

be an
s
-indefinite-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.
If

holds, then

|X|\leq\binom{p+q+s}{s}.

Proof.

Define the polynomial
F

F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\prod_{\alpha\in A(X)}(\alpha-||\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}||)/\alpha

for
0\not\in A(X)
.
This polynomial satisfies
F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})=1
and
F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=0
for distinct
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X
.
Here,
\boldsymbol{M}=(F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}))_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X}
is the identity matrix. Therefore, Theorem

implies that

|X|=r_{+}\leq\dim_{s}(X)\leq\dim_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{p+q})=\binom{p+q+s}{s}.\qed

If
0\in A(X)
and
(p,q)\neq(1,1)
, then
there exists a proper
s
-indefinite-distance set

in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
with

. This can be expressed as follows.
We can assume that

and

Let

be a Euclidean

-distance set in

Let

It is noteworthy that

. Let

Therefore, for any

||\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}||=||\boldsymbol{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}||+||\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}||\in A(L_{s-1})\cup\{0\},

-indefinite-distance set in

with distance

The set

is an infinite

\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
with

.
For

-indefinite-distance set in

.

For

If

is the union of two lines

If

is hyperbolic and its asymptotes are

.
It should be noted that

Suppose any
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in X

Then

is finite since

Therefore, an

Embedding dimensions of dissimilarity matrices

Let
V
be a finite set, with
|V|=n
.
We call
\mathscr{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{s}\}

relations
on
V
if
\mathscr{R}
is a partition of
V\times V
,
R_{0}=\{(u,u)\mid u\in V\}
, and

In this paper, we call

s
-relation graph

A matrix

(0,1)

For

\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})=\boldsymbol{D}(X)

is called a

\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})
.
An
s
-relation graph

is

\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}

\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})=\boldsymbol{D}(X)

.
Moreover, the set
X
is called a
representation of
(V,\mathscr{R})

\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{X}
be an

.
The

\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})

\boldsymbol{j}

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{A}_{s}
be the relation matrices of an
s
-relation graph
(V,\mathscr{R})
.
Suppose
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{1}\boldsymbol{P},\ldots,\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{s}\boldsymbol{P}
are mutually commutative. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{r}
be the common eigenspaces of
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\boldsymbol{P}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in E_{i}
and
m_{i}
the dimension of
E_{i}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
and define the two sets

\mathfrak{V}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{+}=\{\mathfrak{v}\in\mathfrak{V}_{\mathscr{R}}\mid\mathfrak{a}^{\top}\mathfrak{v}>0\},\qquad\mathfrak{V}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-}=\{\mathfrak{v}\in\mathfrak{V}_{\mathscr{R}}\mid\mathfrak{a}^{\top}\mathfrak{v}<0\}.

Then
\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})
is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
, where

p=\sum_{i:\,\mathfrak{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})\in\mathfrak{V}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{+}}m_{i},\qquad q=\sum_{i:\,\mathfrak{v}(\boldsymbol{x}_{i})\in\mathfrak{V}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{-}}m_{i}.

\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})
is
p+q
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{ij}
be the eigenvalue of
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\boldsymbol{P}
on
E_{j}
and
\boldsymbol{E}_{j}
be the orthogonal projection matrix onto
E_{j}
.
Then, we have

\boldsymbol{F}_{\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})}(\hat{\boldsymbol{j}})=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\boldsymbol{P}=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{ij}\boldsymbol{E}_{j}=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\lambda_{ij}\right)\boldsymbol{E}_{j}

Note that
\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{F}_{\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})}(\hat{\boldsymbol{j}})\boldsymbol{x}=\mathfrak{a}^{\top}\mathfrak{v}(\boldsymbol{x})
for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
.
Since
\mathfrak{v}(\boldsymbol{x})=(\lambda_{1j},\ldots,\lambda_{sj})
for each
\boldsymbol{x}\in E_{j}
with
\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}=1
, the eigenvalue of

\boldsymbol{F}_{\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})}(\hat{\boldsymbol{j}})
on
E_{j}

is expressed as
\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\lambda_{ij}=\mathfrak{a}^{\top}\mathfrak{v}(\boldsymbol{x})
.
This implies that signature
(p,q)
of
\boldsymbol{F}_{\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathscr{R}}(\mathfrak{a})}(\hat{\boldsymbol{j}})

∎

4
Algorithm for the classification of largest
2
-indefinite-distance sets

The algorithm for
classifying the largest proper
2
-indefinite-distance sets in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
is based on the algorithm of Lisoněk for classifying the largest
2
-distance sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To find the largest 2-indefinite-distance sets, we suppose that distances
a,b
are not 0.
We prove several lemmas to describe the proposed algorithm.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{M}
be a real symmetric matrix of signature
(r,s)
.
Then there exists a principal
(r+s)\times(r+s)
submatrix of signature
(r,s)
.

Proof.

Applying suitable permutations to the rows and columns of
\boldsymbol{M}
, an orthogonal matrix
\boldsymbol{P}
can be generated such that
\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{P}
is a diagonal matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{D}&\boldsymbol{O}\\
\boldsymbol{O}&\boldsymbol{O}\end{pmatrix},

where
\boldsymbol{D}
is an
(r+s)\times(r+s)
diagonal matrix with
r
positive and
s
negative entries.
Then

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{P}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{D}&\boldsymbol{O}\\
\boldsymbol{O}&\boldsymbol{O}\end{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{P}^{\top}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}&\boldsymbol{P}_{12}\\
\boldsymbol{P}_{21}&\boldsymbol{P}_{22}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{D}&\boldsymbol{O}\\
\boldsymbol{O}&\boldsymbol{O}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}_{21}^{\top}\\
\boldsymbol{P}_{12}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}_{22}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}_{11}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}_{21}^{\top}\\
\boldsymbol{P}_{21}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}^{\top}&\boldsymbol{P}_{21}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}_{21}^{\top}\end{pmatrix},

and
\boldsymbol{P}_{11}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}_{11}^{\top}
is an
(r+s)\times(r+s)
submatrix with signature
(r,s)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph
(V,\{R_{0},R_{1},R_{2}\})
of order
n
with relation matrices
\boldsymbol{A}_{i}
. Let
p,q
be non-negative integers such that
n\geq p+q+3
.
Let
\boldsymbol{P}=\boldsymbol{I}-(1/n)\boldsymbol{J}
.
Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A dissimilarity matrix
\boldsymbol{D}=\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
(resp.
-\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
) is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
for some
b\in\mathbb{R}
with
|b|<1
.

(2)

There exists a unique eigenvalue
\lambda>-1/2
of
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{1}\boldsymbol{P}
on
\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
such that the sum of the multiplicities of the eigenvalues on
\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
less than
\lambda
is at most
p
(resp.
q
), and the sum of the multiplicities of the eigenvalues greater than
\lambda
is at most
q
(resp.
p
).

Moreover, if
(1)
holds, then
b=\lambda/(1+\lambda)
(resp.
b=-\lambda/(1+\lambda)
) with eigenvalue
\lambda
obtained in
(2)
.

Proof.

We only prove this lemma for
\boldsymbol{D}=\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
.
The proof of
-\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
is similar.
First, we prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.
Let
(r,s)
be the embedding dimension of
\boldsymbol{D}
.
Then,
(r,s)={\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P})
. It follows that
p\geq r
and
q\geq s
.
From
n\geq p+q+3\geq r+s+3
, there exists a zero eigenvalue of
-\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}
with an eigenvector
\boldsymbol{x}
perpendicular to
\boldsymbol{j}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{1}\boldsymbol{P}
and
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{2}\boldsymbol{P}
are commutative,
the eigenspaces of
-\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{P}
and
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\boldsymbol{P}
are identical. From
\boldsymbol{A}_{2}=\boldsymbol{J}-\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{A}_{1}
,
we establish the following equation:

-\boldsymbol{P}(\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2})\boldsymbol{P}=(b-1)\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{1}\boldsymbol{P}+b\boldsymbol{P}.

Moreover,
the eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}_{1}\boldsymbol{P}
, derived from the eigenvector
\boldsymbol{x}
, satisfies

, which simplifies to

.
It is noteworthy that

if and only if

.
From Theorem
3.2
and Remark

, the sum of the multiplicities of the eigenvalues on

less (
resp.

is equal to
r(\leq p)
(
resp.

s(\leq q)
). Moreover, the eigenvalue

is unique because we can remove only one eigenspace by choosing

.

Considering

for a given

in
(2)
, we can clearly show

.
∎

Now, we determine an algorithm to classify representable graphs as 2-indefinite-distance sets in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
. All calculations were performed using Magma
[
6
]
.
The algorithm is applied to two cases:
\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
and
-\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
(In the case when
p=q
, we may apply it to
\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
); however, we will outline its implementation only for
\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
.
For Euclidean representations, a distance matrix (or a graph) is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if any
(d+3)
-point principal submatrix (or subgraph) of the matrix
is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This is a key argument in the Lisoněk algorithm.
This type of result is not known for
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
; however, if a subgraph of order
p+q+3
is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
, then the distances are uniquely determined by Theorem
3.2
or Lemma
4.2
.
Moreover, any indefinite-distance matrix of
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
has a principal submatrix of size
p+q+3
whose signature is
(p,q)
by Lemma
4.1
.
In our algorithm, we collect all graphs whose
(p+q+3)
-vertex subgraphs are representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
in a manner similar to Lisoněk, and we check whether
their largest graph is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.
A graph is said to be
quasi-representable
in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
if any
(p+q+3)
-vertex subgraph is representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}

First, we fixed the dimensions
(p,q)
and collected all graphs with
p+q+3
vertices by using Macky’s algorithm
[
16
]
.
Magma has a database of all graphs with a maximum of 10 vertices.
For each graph with
p+q+3
vertices,
we calculate the characteristic polynomial
f(x)
of
\boldsymbol{P}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{P}
in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
.
We obtain the prime factorization of
f(x)=g_{1}(x)^{m_{1}}\cdots g_{r}(x)^{m_{r}}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
A root of
g_{i}(x)
can be expressed as a sufficiently small segment
[s,t]
with
s,t\in\mathbb{Q}
containing the root.
Since the roots of the irreducible polynomial
g_{i}(x)
are simple, the exponent
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of the root of
f(x)
.
We collect all graphs that have an eigenvalue
\lambda
satisfying Lemma
4.2
(2).
Let
L[n,\lambda]
be the set of all graphs of order
n
that are quasi-representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
and have an eigenvalue
\lambda\neq 0
satisfying the condition of Lemma
4.2
(2).
Let
L^{\prime}[n,\lambda]
be the subset of
L[n,\lambda]
consisting of graphs that are quasi-representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
but not in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q-1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{p-1,q}
.
We can construct
L[p+q+3,\lambda]
and
L^{\prime}[p+q+3,\lambda]
by checking for the existence of
\lambda
that satisfies the condition of Lemma
4.2
(2) for each graph of order
p+q+3
.
Here, the corresponding distance
b
is
\lambda/(\lambda+1)
for
\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
and
-\lambda/(\lambda+1)
for
-\boldsymbol{A}_{1}+b\boldsymbol{A}_{2}
.
If
L^{\prime}[p+q+3,\lambda]
is empty for any
\lambda
,
then there does not exist a 2-indefinite-distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
of size at least
p+q+3
by Lemma
4.1
.

For each graph
G
in
L[p+q+3,\lambda]
, we add a new vertex such that the new graph is quasi-representable in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
. The number of options for adding a new vertex is
2^{p+q+3}
and
is determined by the vectors in
\{0,1\}^{p+q+3}
, where 0 in position
i
means that the new vertex has no edge to the current vertex
i
in the graph and 1 means that there is such an edge.
We added a new vector to the last row and column of
\boldsymbol{A}_{1}
.
For the adjacency matrix
\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}
of the new graph after adding a new vertex, let
\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{\prime}

5
Largest spherical 2-indefinite-distance sets

We define a sphere
S_{p,q}(r)
of radius
r>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
as follows:

S_{p,q}(r)=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p,q}\mid\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle\rangle=r\}.

The sphere
S_{p,q}(1)=S_{p,q}
is called the
unit sphere
.
For
X\subset S_{p,q}
,
X
is called a
proper spherical set
if
X
is not included in
S_{p-1,q}
or
S_{p,q-1}
.
If an
s
-indefinite-distance set
X
in
S_{p,q}
satisfies
\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle\neq 1
for any distinct
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X
, then we can prove an upper bound

|X|\leq\binom{p+q+s-1}{s}+\binom{p+q+s-2}{s-1}

in the same manner as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
using a polynomial

F(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=\prod_{\alpha\in B(X)}\frac{\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle-\alpha}{1-\alpha},

where
B(X)=\{\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle\mid\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X,\boldsymbol{x},\neq\boldsymbol{y}\}
.
Improved upper bounds are known for
q=1

[
5
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be an
s
-indefinite-distance set in
S_{p,1}
.
If
\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle\neq 1
for any two distinct points
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X
,
then

|X|\leq\binom{p+s}{s}.

For each
p\geq 10
,
there exists a spherical 2-indefinite-distance set attaining the upper bound from Theorem
5.1

[
15
]
.
The construction is based on Lisoněk
[
15
]
, and this particular construction is a representation of a graph in the switching class of the Johnson graph.

A dissimilarity matrix
\boldsymbol{D}
of embedding dimension
(p,q)
is said to be
Type (i)
if

-\boldsymbol{D}
satisfies the condition in Theorem
3.2
(i).

Remark 5.2
.

We can determine the type of a dissimilarity matrix
\boldsymbol{D}
of embedding dimension
{\rm sign}(\boldsymbol{F}_{\boldsymbol{D}}(\boldsymbol{\ell}))=(p,q)

using the signature of
-\boldsymbol{D}
. Thus, we obtain the following:

(1)

{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D})=(p+1,q+1)
if and only if
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (1).

(2)

{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D})=(p,q+1)
if and only if
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (2).

(3)

{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D})=(p+1,q)
if and only if
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (3).

(4)

{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D})=(p,q)
if and only if
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (4).

We can determine whether an indefinite-distance matrix has a spherical representation in its embedding dimension as follows:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{D}
be a dissimilarity matrix of embedding dimension
(p,q)
.
Then,
\boldsymbol{D}
is the indefinite-distance matrix of some set in
S_{p,q}(r)
if and only if
\boldsymbol{D}
is Type (2).

Proof.

Since the embedding dimension of
\boldsymbol{D}
is
(p,q)
, there is
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
such that
\boldsymbol{D}=(||\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}||)_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X}
.
From the equality
||\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}||=||\boldsymbol{x}||+||\boldsymbol{y}||-2\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle
,
X
is in
S_{p,q}(r)
for some
r>0

if and only if the signature of
2(\langle\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle\rangle)_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in X}=-\boldsymbol{D}+2r\boldsymbol{J}
is
(p,q)
for some
r
. Therefore we consider the signature
-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J}
with
a>0
for each Type (i).

An eigenvalue of
-\boldsymbol{D}
that is not main is an eigenvalue of
-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J}
. Thus we consider the change of main eigenvalues from
-\boldsymbol{D}
to
-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J}
. Let
\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{k}
be the main eigenvalues of
-\boldsymbol{D}
, and
\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{l}
be those of
-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J}
.
From Theorem 2.2 in
[
21
]
, it follows that

k=l
and

f(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\mu_{i}-x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-x)\left(1+a\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{n\beta_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{j}-x}\right),

where
n
is the order of
\boldsymbol{D}
. Moreover,

\lambda_{1}<\mu_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{k}<\mu_{k}
holds.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (1). Then, the signature of
-\boldsymbol{D}
is
(p+1,q+1)
and
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for each
i
since
E_{0}\subset\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
.
Under the condition
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j}=0
, we have
f(0)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
, which does not depend on
a
.
For this case,
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J})=(p+1,q+1)\neq(p,q)
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{D}
does not have a spherical representation in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (2). Then, the signature of
-\boldsymbol{D}
is
(p,q+1)
and
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for each
i
since
E_{0}\subset\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
.
Thus,
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J})=(p,q)
if and only if
f(0)=0
, namely
a=-1/(\sum_{j=1}^{k}n\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j})
. From the definition of Type (2),
\sum_{j=1}^{k}n\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j}<0
and we can take
a>0
. Therefore
\boldsymbol{D}
has a spherical embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.
Moreover, we have
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J})=(p+1,q)
for
a>-1/(\sum_{j=1}^{k}n\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j})
, and
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J})=(p,q+1)
for
a<-1/(\sum_{j=1}^{k}n\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j})
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (3). Then, the signature of
-\boldsymbol{D}
is
(p+1,q)
, and
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for each
i
since
E_{0}\subset\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
. From the definition of Type (3),
\sum_{j=1}^{k}n\beta_{j}^{2}/\lambda_{j}>0
and we cannot take
a>0
such that
f(0)=0
. In this case,
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+aJ)=(p+1,q)\neq(p,q)
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{D}
does not have a spherical representation in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{D}
is of Type (4). Then the signature of
-\boldsymbol{D}
is
(p,q)
, and
\lambda_{i}=0
for some
i
since
E_{0}\not\subset\boldsymbol{j}^{\perp}
.
In this case,
{\rm sign}(-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J})=(p+1,q)
. Therefore,
\boldsymbol{D}
does not have a spherical representation in
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The dissimilarity matrix
\boldsymbol{D}
does not have a zero entry except for diagonals if and only if the corresponding Gram matrix
-\boldsymbol{D}+a\boldsymbol{J}
has entries
a
only at diagonals.
This is consistent with the conditions
0\not\in A(X)
and
1\not\in B(X)
of the two upper bounds for
\mathbb{R}^{p,q}
and
S_{p,q}
.

Remark 5.5
.

The diagonal entries of an indefinite-distance matrix
\boldsymbol{D}
are 0. This implies
-\boldsymbol{D}
has a negative eigenvalue. Thus, a Euclidean distance matrix is of Type (1) or Type (2).
Nozaki and Shinohara
[
19
]
determined when a Euclidean 2-distance set is spherical based on the properties of its distance matrix (see Theorems 2.4 and 3.7 in
[
19
]
).
Theorem
5.3
generalizes this result.

Any dissimilarity matrix can have a spherical representation with higher dimensionality.

.

Let
\boldsymbol{D}
be a dissimilarity matrix of embedding dimension

Remark 6.1
.

From the observation of examples obtained by PC calculation, we construct an infinite series of 2-indefinite-distance sets in
\mathbb{R}^{p,1}
that have large cardinalities.
We consider graph
G=(V,E)
, where

,

, and

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\{v_{i,1}:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{v_{i,2}:1\leq i\leq n\},

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\{w_{i,j}:1\leq i<j\leq n\},

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\{v_{i,1},v_{j,2}\}:i\neq j\},

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\{w_{i,j},w_{k,l}\}:|\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}|=\emptyset\},

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle=\{\{v_{i,j},w_{k,l}\}:i\in\{k,l\}\}.

The induced subgraph of

on

on

The representation of

with distances

is in

for

Remark 6.2
.

Remark 6.5
.

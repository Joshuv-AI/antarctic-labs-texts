Lifts of supersingular abelian varieties with small Mumford–Tate groups
Yeuk Hay Joshua Lam, Abhishek Oswal

Abstract
. We investigate to what extent an abelian variety over a finite field can be lifted to one in characteristic zero with small Mumford-Tate group. We prove that supersingular abelian surfaces, respectively threefolds, can be lifted to ones isogenous to a square, respectively product, of elliptic curves. On the other hand, we show that supersingular abelian threefolds cannot be lifted to one isogenous to the cube of an elliptic curve over the Witt vectors.



1.
Introduction

Given an abelian variety
A_{0}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, the question of what kind of lifts to characteristic zero
A_{0}
admits has been studied for a long time. For example, the famous result of Honda shows that any such
A_{0}
admits a lift to a complex multiplication (CM) abelian variety,
up to isogeny
; more precisely, there exists an abelian variety
A_{0}^{\prime}/\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
isogenous to
A_{0}
which has a CM lift. A vast generalization of this result to a large class of Shimura varieties was achieved by Kisin
[
Kis17
]
. On the other hand, the question of CM liftings on the nose, i.e. not replacing
A_{0}
by something isogenous to it, was considered by Oort
[
Oor92
]
, who gave examples of
A_{0}
not admitting any CM lifts; throughout this paper, by a lift we will mean this stronger sense of lifting without allowing first an isogeny. In a recent work
[
Kis+21
]
of the first author with Kisin, Shankar, and Srinivasan, it was proven that, in fact, for a fixed
g
, only finitely many supersingular abelian varieties of dimension
g
admit CM lifts. A similar result for the other Newton polygons, i.e. intermediate between ordinary and supersingular, as well as for K3 surfaces, was also obtained
1
1

1

the precise formulation is that the points admitting CM lifts lie on finitely many central leaves; we refer the reader to
[
Kis+21
]
[Theorem 1.3]
. We note that ordinary abelian varieties always admit CM lifts, namely the
canonical lifts
over
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. For almost ordinary abelian varieties over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, in work of the second author with Shankar
[
OS20
]
, there is a similar construction of CM lifts of such abelian varieties to ‘slightly ramified’ extensions of the ring of Witt vectors
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
: we refer the reader to §2 of loc.cit. for the precise statement.

Therefore it is, in a vague sense, supersingularity as well as the ramification of the lifting base that prevents an abelian variety from having CM lifts. We then have the following natural

Question 1.1
.

What is the smallest possible Mumford-Tate group of a lift of
A_{0}
? What about for lifts over
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
?

As stated, Question
1.1
is a little imprecise; as a first approximation, one can ask whether such an
A_{0}
can be lifted so that some of its endomorphisms also lift, with the CM case being the best one can do and not always possible. In this work we begin to investigate this question, and indicate that the answer may be rather subtle.

Theorem 1.2
.

(1)

Any supersingular abelian surface
A_{0}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
admits a lift which is isogenous to the square of an elliptic curve.

(2)

Any supersingular abelian threefold
A_{0}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
admits a lift which is isogenous to a product of three elliptic curves.

(3)

Not every supersingular abelian threefold admits a lift over
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
isogenous to the cube of an elliptic curve.

In the language of Question
1.1
, this means that abelian surfaces can always be lifted to ones with Mumford-Tate group contained in
{\rm GL}_{2}
, and for threefolds
({\rm GL}_{2})^{3}
. It would also be interesting to investigate other kinds of reduction in Mumford-Tate groups: for example, for the case of surfaces the other possibilities are that the lift
\mathcal{A}
is isogenous to a product
E_{1}\times E_{2}
where
E_{1}
has CM, or that
\mathcal{A}
has quaternionic multiplication.

Remark 1.3
.

Our results concern only liftings to the Witt vectors
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. It would of course be interesting to understand whether the answer changes, in the cases we study, if we allow liftings to arbitrary discrete valuation rings.

Remark 1.4
.

In light of Theorem
1.2
, it seems reasonable to conjecture that, at least over
W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, every supersingular abelian variety admits a lift isogenous to a product of elliptic curves, and that most supersingular abelian varieties do not admit lifts isogenous to the self power of an elliptic curve.

We now give an idea of the proofs. By Grothendieck-Messing and Serre-Tate theory, to exhibit a lift of an abelian variety it suffices to give a filtration on the Dieudonné module lifting the kernel of Frobenius modulo
p
. Therefore, for parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to find such filtrations such that the resulting filtered Dieudonné module is isogenous to that of a product of elliptic curves. We achieve this by a direct computation. For the third part of Theorem
1.2
, we show that if an abelian threefold does admit a lift isogenous to a product of elliptic curves, then there exist non-trivial algebraic relations between the parameters of its Dieudonné module, which allows us to conclude. Crucial to our computations are explicit parametrizations of the supersingular loci for abelian surfaces and threefolds, which may be of independent interest.

Reduction to a computation with Dieudonné modules

Let

be a supersingular abelian variety over

.

We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists an automorphism
\alpha
of the
p
-divisible group

and an isogeny
\gamma:A_{0}\rightarrow E_{0}^{g}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

Proof of Lemma.

Consider the dual isogeny
\varphi^{\vee}:E_{0}^{g}[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow A_{0}[p^{\infty}]^{\vee}

is defined over a finite field. Indeed, since

is supersingular, we may pick some isogeny

. Then

is a self isogeny of

2

2

here

∎

The automorphism

\mathbb{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
with respect to which the Frobenius
F^{\prime}
is as in
Equation 3.6.1
of Proposition
3.6
(with
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
in place of
x,y,z
).

The Dieudonné modules
\mathbb{D}(x,y,z)
and
\mathbb{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if there exists an
M\in{\rm GL}_{6}{(W)}
such that
M^{-1}F\sigma(M)=F^{\prime}.

Suppose that the first, third and fifth columns of
M
are respectively:

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}\\
A_{2}\\
A_{3}\\
A_{4}\\
A_{5}\\
A_{6}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}\\
B_{2}\\
B_{3}\\
B_{4}\\
B_{5}\\
B_{6}\end{pmatrix},\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}\\
C_{2}\\
C_{3}\\
C_{4}\\
C_{5}\\
C_{6}\end{pmatrix}.

It is a straightforward computation that the equation
F\sigma(M)=MF^{\prime}
holds if and only if the following set of equations hold.

(I.i)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(A_{1})-A_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sigma(x)\sigma^{2}(A_{4})

(I.ii)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(A_{2})-A_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=-xA_{3}-zA_{5}

(I.iii)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(A_{3})-A_{3}

\displaystyle=-\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(A_{6})

(I.iv)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(A_{4})-A_{4}\right]

\displaystyle=-y\sigma^{2}(A_{5})

(I.v)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(A_{5})

\displaystyle=A_{5}

(I.vi)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(A_{6})

\displaystyle=A_{6}

(II.i)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(B_{1})-B_{1}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{2})-\sigma(x)\sigma^{2}(B_{4})-\sigma(z)\sigma^{2}(B_{6})

(II.ii)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{2})-B_{2}\right]=\,

\displaystyle x^{\prime}\sigma^{-1}(A_{1})-x\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{3})+\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(B_{6})\right]

\displaystyle+x^{\prime}z\sigma(A_{6})-z\sigma^{2}(B_{5})

(II.iii)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(B_{3})-B_{3}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{4})-\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(B_{6})

(II.iv)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{4})-B_{4}\right]

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\left[\sigma(A_{3})+y\sigma(A_{6})\right]-y\sigma^{2}(B_{5})

(II.v)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(B_{5})-B_{5}

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{6})

(II.vi)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{6})-B_{6}\right]

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{5})

(III.i)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(C_{1})-C_{1}

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}\sigma(B_{2})-\sigma(x)\sigma^{2}(C_{4})+z^{\prime}\sigma(A_{2})-\sigma(z)\sigma^{2}(C_{6})

(III.ii)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(C_{2})-C_{2}\right]=\,

\displaystyle y^{\prime}\left[\sigma(B_{1})+x\sigma(B_{4})+z\sigma(B_{6})\right]

\displaystyle+z^{\prime}\left[\sigma(A_{1})+x\sigma(A_{4})+z\sigma(A_{6})\right]

\displaystyle-x\left[\sigma^{2}(C_{3})+\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(C_{6})\right]-z\sigma^{2}(C_{5})

(III.iii)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(C_{3})-C_{3}

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}\sigma(B_{4})+z^{\prime}\sigma(A_{4})-\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(C_{6})

(III.iv)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(C_{4})-C_{4}\right]=\,

\displaystyle y^{\prime}\left[\sigma(B_{3})+y\sigma(B_{6})\right]

\displaystyle+z^{\prime}\left[\sigma(A_{3})+y\sigma(A_{6})\right]-y\sigma^{2}(C_{5})

(III.v)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(C_{5})-C_{5}

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}\sigma(B_{6})+z^{\prime}\sigma(A_{6})

(III.vi)

\displaystyle p\left[\sigma^{2}(C_{6})-C_{6}\right]

\displaystyle=y^{\prime}\sigma(B_{5})+z^{\prime}\sigma(A_{5})

The equation (I.iv) implies that
p|A_{5}
, and thus from (I.ii) we get that
p|A_{3}
, and then from (I.iii) that
p|A_{6}
. This and equation (II.iv) then imply that
p|B_{5}.

We note that
M\in{\rm GL}_{6}(W)
if and only if its mod
p
reduction
\overline{M}
is in
{\rm GL}_{6}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}).
It is easy to see from a computation of the determinant of
\overline{M}
, thus that
M\in{\rm GL}_{6}(W)
if and only if
\left[A_{1}\cdot(\sigma(B_{3})+y\sigma(B_{6}))\cdot C_{5}\cdot B_{3}\right]\in W^{\times}.

From (II.ii) and (III.iv) now we see that
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in W^{\times}
. This proves the first part of the proposition.

Furthermore, (II.ii) implies that

x^{\prime}\sigma^{-1}(A_{1})\equiv x\left[\sigma^{2}(B_{3})+\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(B_{6})\right]\pmod{p}

The above equation, with (I.i) and (II.iii), implies that
x^{\prime}\left[\sigma(A_{1})+x\sigma(A_{4})\right]\equiv x\left[x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{4})+B_{3}\right]\pmod{p}.
Thus,

(
\dagger
)

x^{\prime}\sigma(A_{1})\equiv xB_{3}\pmod{p}.

In particular,
\overline{x^{\prime}}=({\overline{x}\overline{B_{3}}})/({\overline{A_{1}}})^{p}.

For ease of notation, let us set
A_{5}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=A_{5}/p\in W(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}),A_{6}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=A_{6}/p\in W(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
and
A_{3}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=A_{3}/p\in W.
It is easy to see that for fixed
x,y,z
, there are at most
p^{12}
possibilities for
(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{A_{2}},\overline{A_{3}^{\prime}},\overline{A_{4}},\overline{A_{5}^{\prime}},\overline{A_{6}^{\prime}})
in
(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{6}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:
Suppose that
A_{4}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Then (I.iv) implies that
p^{2}|A_{5}
and by (II.vi) we get that
\overline{B_{6}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}.
(II.iii) implies then that there are at most
p^{4}
possibilities (for fixed
x,y,z
) in
(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{2}
for the tuple
(\overline{B_{3}},\overline{B_{6}})
. In Case 1, we have by (I.i) that
\overline{A_{1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
and thus recalling that
\overline{x^{\prime}}=({\overline{x}\overline{B_{3}}})/({\overline{A_{1}}})^{p}
, we see that there are at most
p^{6}
many possibilities in Case 1, for
(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{B_{3}},\overline{B_{6}},\overline{x^{\prime}})\in(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{4}.

Case 2:
Suppose that
A_{4}\in W^{\times}.

From
(\dagger)
, we recall that
x^{\prime}-\frac{xB_{3}}{\sigma(A_{1})}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.
Using this along with (II.iii) we get that

(
\ddagger
)

\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(B_{6})\equiv\left(1+x\sigma\left(\frac{A_{4}}{A_{1}}\right)\right)B_{3}-\sigma^{2}(B_{3})\pmod{p}

From (II.vi) and (II.iii) we get that

\left[\sigma(y)\sigma(A_{5}^{\prime})-\sigma(A_{4})\right]\sigma^{2}(B_{6})+\sigma(A_{4})B_{6}+\sigma(A_{5}^{\prime})\sigma^{2}(B_{3})-\sigma(A_{5}^{\prime})B_{3}=0.

Using
(\ddagger)
now to eliminate
B_{6}
from the above equation we get that

\displaystyle 0\equiv\,

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(B_{3})\left(\frac{\sigma(A_{4})}{\sigma(y)}\right)

\displaystyle+B_{3}\left[\frac{\left(\sigma(y)\sigma(A_{5}^{\prime})-\sigma(A_{4})\right)\cdot(1+x\sigma(A_{4}/A_{1}))}{\sigma(y)}-\frac{\sigma(A_{4})}{\sigma^{-1}(y)}\right]

\displaystyle+\sigma^{-2}(B_{3})\left[\frac{\sigma(A_{4})\sigma^{-2}(1+x\sigma(A_{4}/A_{1}))}{\sigma^{-1}(y)}\right]\pmod{p}.

We note again that for fixed
(\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z})\in(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{3}
, one sees from the first set of equations (I.i)-(I.vi) that
(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{A_{4}},\overline{A_{5}^{\prime}})\in(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{3}
has at most
p^{6}
possibilities and depends algebraically on
(\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z})
. For each such choice of
(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{A_{4}},\overline{A_{5}^{\prime}})
, we have at most
p^{4}
choices for
\overline{B_{3}}
which again depends algebraically on
(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{A_{4}},\overline{A_{5}^{\prime}})
and we note that by
(\ddagger)
,
\overline{B_{6}}
is then determined by
\overline{B_{3}}
and since
\overline{x^{\prime}}=\frac{\overline{x}\overline{B_{3}}}{(\overline{A_{1}})^{p}}
, we see that this in turns determines
\overline{x^{\prime}}
as well.

Therefore, in all, combining Case 1 and Case 2, we see that the mod
p
reduction of

\{(x,y,z,A_{1},A_{4},A_{5}^{\prime},B_{3},B_{6},x^{\prime})\in W^{9}:x,y\in W^{\times}\text{ and (I.i)-(III.vi) satisfied}\}

is contained in a Zariski constructible subset of
(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{9}
whose projection to the first three coordinates is quasi-finite with fibers of size at most
p^{11}.

Similarly, from equation (III.iv), we have that

y^{\prime}\left[\sigma(B_{3})+y\sigma(B_{6})\right]\equiv y\sigma^{2}(C_{5})\pmod{p}.

We recall that
\sigma(B_{3})+y\sigma(B_{6})\in W^{\times}
. If
B_{6}\equiv 0\pmod{p},
then (III.v) implies that
\overline{C_{5}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}},
in which case we have at most
p^{13}
choices for

(\overline{A_{1}},\overline{A_{4}},\overline{A_{5}^{\prime}},\overline{B_{3}},\overline{B_{6}},\overline{C_{5}},\overline{x^{\prime}},\overline{y^{\prime}})\in(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{8}.

If on the other hand,
B_{6}\in W^{\times}
, then from (III.v) we have

\frac{\sigma^{2}(C_{5})-C_{5}}{\sigma(B_{6})\sigma^{2}(C_{5})}\equiv\frac{y}{\sigma(B_{3})+y\sigma(B_{6})}\pmod{p}.

This implies that for each fixed choice of
(\overline{B_{3}},\overline{B_{6}},\overline{y})
there are at most
p^{2}
possibilities for
\overline{C_{5}},
which in turn determines
\overline{y^{\prime}}.
Therefore, putting everything together we see that there is a Zariski constructible subset of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
containing the mod
p
reduction of
\{(x,y,z,A_{1},A_{4},A_{5}^{\prime},B_{3},B_{6},x^{\prime},C_{5},y^{\prime}):x,y\in W^{\times}\text{ and (I.i)-(III.vi) hold}\}
and such that the projection to the first three coordinates has fibers of size at most
p^{14}.

(III.ii) implies that

\displaystyle z^{\prime}\left[\sigma^{-1}(A_{1})\right]\equiv\,

\displaystyle y^{\prime}\left[\sigma(B_{1})+x\sigma(B_{4})+z\sigma(B_{6})\right]

\displaystyle+x\left[\sigma^{2}(C_{3})+\sigma(y)\sigma^{2}(C_{6})\right]+z\sigma^{2}(C_{5})\pmod{p}.

From (III.iii), (III.v), and (III.vi), we see that the reduction of the set

\displaystyle\{(x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},A_{1},A_{4},A_{5}^{\prime},A_{6}^{\prime},B_{1},B_{3},

\displaystyle B_{4},B_{5}^{\prime},B_{6},C_{3},C_{5},C_{6})\in W^{18}:

\displaystyle x,y\in W^{\times}\text{ and (I.i)-(III.vi) hold}\}

is contained in a Zariski constructible subset of
(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{18}
whose projection to the first three coordinates has fibers of size at most
p^{26}.
This completes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

4.1.
Lifting abelian surfaces

Let
A_{0}
be a supersingular abelian surface over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and we write its Dieudonné module in the form given by Proposition
3.3
for some particular
x\in W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
. If
x
is divisible by
p

A_{0}
is itself the square of an elliptic curve, and the result is trivial in this case. We assume from now on that
x
is a unit, so that we can find
\beta\in W^{\times}
satisfying

\sigma^{2}(\beta)-\beta=x.

Let
\zeta\in W^{\times}
be a primitive
(p^{2}-1)
-th root of unity, and write
u\vcentcolon=\sigma(\zeta)-\zeta
.

The following is straightforward by an application of Hensel’s lemma.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists
b\in W^{\times}
such that

p\beta b^{2}+ub-\sigma^{-1}(\beta)=0.

Let
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
denote the Dieudonné module of a product of supersingular elliptic curves. We will work with a basis
e_{1}^{\prime},f_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}
such that the actions of Frobenius and Verschiebung are given by

F^{\prime}=V^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&p&&\\
1&0&&\\
&&0&p\\
&&1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Moroever, consider the following filtration, where
b
is as in Proposition
4.2

{\rm Fil}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\langle f_{1}^{\prime}+pbe_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}+pbe_{2}^{\prime}\rangle

on
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
, which corresponds to a square of an elliptic curve over
W
, which we denote by
\tilde{E}\times\tilde{E}
.
The following now finishes the proof of the first part of Theorem
1.2
.

Claim 4.3
.

The filtration on
\mathbb{D}
given by

{\rm Fil}\vcentcolon=\langle f_{1}+pbe_{1},f_{2}+pbe_{2}-\sigma^{-1}(x)e_{1}\rangle

gives rise to a lift of
A
which is isogenous to
\tilde{E}\times\tilde{E}
.

Proof of Claim.

By Proposition
2.1
, it suffices to show that the filtered Dieudonné modules
(\mathbb{D},{\rm Fil})
and
(\mathbb{D}^{\prime},{\rm Fil}^{\prime})
are isogenous.

Consider the map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
given by

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto pe_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto pf_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta e_{1}^{\prime}+\beta f_{1}^{\prime}+e_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle f_{2}

{\rm Fil}
to

4.4.
Lifting abelian threefolds

In this subsection, we prove that every supersingular abelian threefold over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, admits a lift to an abelian scheme over
W
such that the lift is isogenous to a product of three elliptic curves.

Let
A_{0}
be a supersingular abelian threefold over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}.
Let
(\mathbb{D},F,V)
denote the Dieudonné module of the
p
-divisible group
A_{0}[p^{\infty}]
associated to
A_{0}
. By Corollary
3.7
, we may choose a
W
-basis
\{e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2},e_{3},f_{3}\}
of
\mathbb{D}
with respect to which the Frobenius and Verschiebung operators are as described in
Equation 3.6.1
of Proposition
3.6
for some
x,y,z\in W
.

Pick an element
\beta^{\prime}\in W^{\times}
such that

\sigma^{2}(\beta^{\prime})-\beta^{\prime}=x.

Pick an element
Q\in W^{\times}
such that

\sigma^{2}(Q)-Q=py\sigma(\beta^{\prime}).

Note that if
Q\in W^{\times}
satisfies the equation
\sigma^{2}(Q)-Q=py\sigma(\beta^{\prime})
then so does
Q+u
for any
u\in W(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}).
Thus, we may also insist that
1-Q\in W^{\times}
and
Q-\sigma(Q)\in W^{\times}.

Pick an element
R
in
W^{\times}
such that

\sigma^{2}(R)-R=y+pz

and such that
R-\beta^{\prime}Q\in W^{\times}.

Pick an element
T\in W^{\times}
such that

\sigma^{2}(T)-T=y.

Again, if
T\in W^{\times}
satisfies the equation
\sigma^{2}(T)-T=y
then so does
T+u
for every
u\in W(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
. Thus, we may insist additionally that
(T-\beta^{\prime}Q)\in W^{\times}
and
(-Q(T-1)+R-\beta^{\prime}Q)\in W^{\times}
and also that
(\sigma^{-1}(Q)(T-1)-(R-\beta^{\prime}Q))\in W^{\times}.

To simplify the notations in the computations that follow, we introduce certain auxiliary elements
\lambda_{1},\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\Delta_{1},\nu_{2},\Delta_{2},\Phi,\psi,\theta,S\in W
defined as follows:

\begin{aligned} \lambda_{1}&:=pR\sigma^{-1}(\beta^{\prime})-Q,\\
\mu_{1}&:=\sigma^{-1}(Q)-p\beta^{\prime}\sigma^{-1}(R),\\
\nu_{1}&:=p(R-\beta^{\prime}Q),\\
\Delta_{2}&:=\sigma^{-1}(T)-\sigma^{-1}(S),\\
\Phi&:=\Delta_{1}\nu_{2}-\Delta_{2}\nu_{1},\\
\end{aligned}\begin{aligned} \Delta_{1}&:=\sigma^{-1}(R)-\sigma^{-1}(Q)\sigma^{-1}(\beta^{\prime})\\
S&:=1+pT,\\
\nu_{2}&:=p(T-S),\\
\psi&:=\mu_{1}\nu_{2}-\nu_{1},\\
\theta&:=\lambda_{1}\nu_{2}+\nu_{1}.\end{aligned}

Note that
\psi=p[(\sigma^{-1}(Q)-p\beta^{\prime}\sigma^{-1}(R))(T-S)-(R-\beta^{\prime}Q)]
and
\theta=p[(pR\sigma^{-1}(\beta^{\prime})-Q)(T-S)+(R-\beta^{\prime}Q)]
. We have that
1=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\psi)=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\nu_{1})=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(-\lambda_{1}\psi+\mu_{1}\theta)=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\theta)
and we note that
\Phi\in pW.

By an application of Hensel’s Lemma, we may find a
c\in W
, such that

c^{2}[-\nu_{1}\psi]+c[-\lambda_{1}\psi+\mu_{1}\theta+\nu_{1}\Phi]+[\lambda_{1}\Phi-\Delta_{1}\theta]=0.

Set
i:=\frac{\Phi-\psi c}{\theta}.
Note that
i\in W
since
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(\theta)=1.

Set
a:=\frac{\sigma^{-1}(R)+iQ}{\sigma^{-1}(Q)+piR}.
We note that
a\in W
since
\sigma^{-1}(Q)+piR\in W^{\times}.

One may also check that
a=\frac{\sigma^{-1}(\beta^{\prime})+c}{1+pc\beta^{\prime}}.

We let
b^{\prime}:=\frac{\sigma^{-1}(T)+iS}{\sigma^{-1}(S)+piT}.
Note that
b^{\prime}\in W
since
\sigma^{-1}(S)+piT\in W^{\times}.
We may also check that
b^{\prime}=\frac{1+c}{1+pc}.

Let
{\rm Fil}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
be the following admissible filtration

{\rm Fil}\vcentcolon=\langle f_{1}+pae_{1},f_{2}-\sigma^{-1}(x)e_{1}+pce_{2},f_{3}-\sigma^{-1}(y)e_{2}+pie_{3}\rangle.

Let
E_{0}
be a supersingular elliptic curve over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and let
(\mathbb{D}_{0},F_{0},V_{0})
denote the Dieudonné module of
E_{0}[p^{\infty}]
. There is a
W
-basis
\{e,f\}
of
\mathbb{D}_{0}
such that
F_{0}(e)=f=V_{0}(e)
and
F_{0}(f)=pe=V_{0}(f).

As in §
3.5
, we set
(\mathbb{D}^{\prime},F^{\prime},V^{\prime}):=(\mathbb{D}_{0},F_{0},V_{0})^{\oplus 3}
and let
e_{1}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(e,0,0),f_{1}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(f,0,0),e_{2}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(0,e,0),f_{2}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(0,f,0),e_{3}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(0,0,e)
and
f_{3}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=(0,0,f)
.

Let
{\rm Fil}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
be the filtration

{\rm Fil}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\langle f_{1}^{\prime}+pae_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}+pb^{\prime}e_{2}^{\prime},f_{3}^{\prime}+pie_{3}^{\prime}\rangle.

Consider the
W
-linear map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto p^{2}e_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto p^{2}f_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto pe_{1}^{\prime}+p\beta^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}+pe_{2}^{\prime}+pf_{2}^{\prime},

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto pf_{1}^{\prime}+p^{2}\sigma(\beta^{\prime})e_{1}^{\prime}+pf_{2}^{\prime}+p^{2}e_{2}^{\prime},

\displaystyle e_{3}

\displaystyle\mapsto Qe_{1}^{\prime}+Rf_{1}^{\prime}+Se_{2}^{\prime}+Tf_{2}^{\prime}+e_{3}^{\prime},

\displaystyle f_{3}

It is a straightforward computation to verify that

defines an isogeny of filtered Dieudonné modules

. We conclude by Proposition
2.1
.

4.5.
Impossibility of lifting general supersingular threefolds to varieties isogenous to cubes

Let
A_{0}
denote a supersinguar abelian threefold over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
(\mathbb{D},F,V)
the Dieudonné module of
A_{0}[p^{\infty}]
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose there is an abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}
over
{\rm Spec}(W)
that lifts
A_{0}
, and an elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}
over
{\rm Spec}(W)
with an isogeny
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{3}
over

. Then there is a
W
-basis
\{e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2},e_{3},f_{3}\}
of
\mathbb{D}
with respect to which the action of the Frobenius is given by

F=\begin{pmatrix}0&p&&&&\\
1&0&&x&&z\\
&&0&p&&\\
&&1&0&&y\\
&&&&0&p\\
&&&&1&0\\

for some

such that the reductions
\overline{x},\overline{y}
in

.

Proof.

Let
{\rm Fil}\subset\mathbb{D}
denote the filtration on
\mathbb{D}
corresponding to the lift
\mathcal{A}[p^{\infty}]
of
A_{0}[p^{\infty}]
and let
(\mathbb{D}^{\prime},{\rm Fil}^{\prime},F^{\prime},V^{\prime})
be the filtered Dieudonné module corresponding to
\mathcal{E}^{3}[p^{\infty}].
We choose a
W
-basis
\{e_{1}^{\prime},f_{1}^{\prime}.e_{2}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime},e_{3}^{\prime},f_{3}^{\prime}\}
of
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
such that
{\rm Fil}^{\prime}=\langle f_{1}^{\prime}+pae_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}+pae_{2}^{\prime},f_{3}^{\prime}+pae_{3}^{\prime}\rangle
for some
a\in W
and such that
F^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}=f_{i}^{\prime}
and

for

We denote by
\varphi:(\mathbb{D},{\rm Fil},F,V)\rightarrow(\mathbb{D}^{\prime},{\rm Fil}^{\prime},F^{\prime},V^{\prime})
the isogeny of filtered Dieudonné modules corresponding to the isogeny
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}^{3}.

By Proposition
3.6
, we may find a basis
\{e_{1},f_{1},e_{2},f_{2},e_{3},f_{3}\}
of
\mathbb{D}
with respect to which

Theorem 4.8
.

There exist infinitely many supersingular abelian threefolds
A_{0}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that there does not exist an abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}
over
{\rm Spec}(W)
lifting
A_{0}
and such that
\mathcal{A}
is isogenous over
W
to the cube of an elliptic curve.

Proof.

Let
Z\subset(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{3}\times(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})^{3}
be the constructible set given by Proposition
3.8
, and
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
the projections as in the statement of Proposition
3.8
.

Let us denote by
C\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\times\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
the plane curve
C:=\{(a,b)\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\times\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}:a^{p^{2}-1}=b^{p^{2}-1}\}.

Let
A_{0}
be a supersingular abelian threefold such that the Dieudonné module of
A_{0}[p^{\infty}]
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
for some
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
with
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in W^{\times}.

By Proposition
4.6
, if
A_{0}
admits a lift to
W
that is isogenous to a cube of an elliptic curve, then there exist
x,y,z\in W,
such that
\mathbb{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\cong\mathbb{D}(x,y,z)
and such that
\overline{x},\overline{y}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}},
i.e.
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in C.

This in particular implies that
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in\pi_{2}\left(\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(C\times\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\right)\right).
Since
C
is one-dimensional and since the maps
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:Z\rightarrow\left(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\right)^{3}
have finite fibers, the subset
\pi_{2}\left(\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(C\times\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\right)\right)
of
\left(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\right)^{3}
has dimension at most 2.
This completes the proof of the theorem once we note that for every
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in W^{3}
, there is a supersingular abelian threefold over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that the Dieudonné module associated to its

\mathbb{D}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
.
∎

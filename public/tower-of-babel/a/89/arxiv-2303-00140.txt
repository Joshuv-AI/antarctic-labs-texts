The limiting behavior of solutions to p p -Laplacian problems with convection and exponential terms
Anderson L. A. de Araujo

Abstract
We consider, for a , l ≥ 1 , a,l\geq 1, b , s , α > 0 , b,s,\alpha>0, and p > q ≥ 1 , p>q\geq 1, the homogeneous Dirichlet problem for the equation − Δ p ​ u = λ ​ u q − 1 + β ​ u a − 1 ​ | ∇ u | b + m ​ u l − 1 ​ e α ​ u s -\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u^{q-1}+\beta u^{a-1}\left|\nabla u\right|^{b}+mu^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}} in a smooth bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ N . \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}. We prove that under certain setting of the parameters λ , \lambda, β \beta and m m the problem admits at least one positive solution. Using this result we prove that if λ , β > 0 \lambda,\beta>0 are arbitrarily fixed and m m is sufficiently small, then the problem has a positive solution u p , u_{p}, for all p p sufficiently large. In addition, we show that u p u_{p} converges uniformly to the distance function to the boundary of Ω , \Omega, as p → ∞ . p\rightarrow\infty. This convergence result is new for nonlinearities involving a convection term. 2020 MSC: 35B40, 35J92. keywords : Convection term, distance function, exponential term, gradient estimate.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u^{q-1}+\beta u^{a-1}\left|\nabla u\right|^{b}+mu^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}}&\text{in}&\Omega\\
u>0&\text{in}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(P)

where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain
\mathbb{R}^{N},

N\geq 2,
and

\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the
p
-Laplacian operator,
p>1
. The parameters
\lambda,

\beta

and
m
are nonnegative and the constants
q,a,b,l,\alpha
and

a,l\geq 1,\text{ \ }b>0,\text{ \ }s,\alpha\geq 0,\text{ \ and }p>q\geq 1.

Our main result in this paper is stated as follows, where
d_{\Omega}
denotes
the distance function to the boundary:

d_{\Omega}(x):=\min_{y\in\partial\Omega}\left|x-y\right|,\text{
\ }x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Theorem 1.1

Assume that
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}.
Let
\lambda
and
\beta

be arbitrary, but fixed positive real numbers, and let

0<m<m_{\infty}:=(\lambda\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{q-1}+\beta\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{a-1})\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{l-1}e^{-\alpha\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{s}}.

(1.1)

There exists
p_{0}>\max\left\{q,a+b,l\right\}
such that if
p>p_{0}

then the Dirichlet problem (
P
) admits a weak solution
u_{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).
Moreover,

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p}=d_{\Omega}\text{ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

(1.2)

In the last decades, this kind of limiting behavior of solutions to Dirichlet
problems of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\operatorname{div}(\phi_{p}(\left|\nabla u\right|)\nabla u)&=&f(x,u)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u&>&0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

has been obtained by many authors. We refer to
[
5
]
,
[
20
]
,

[

]
for the

-Laplacian, i.e.
\phi_{p}(t)=t^{p-2}
, and

[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]

However, up to our acknowledge, this is the first work dealing with the
limiting behavior of solutions

to a
p
-family of Dirichlet problems

The solution

of Theorem

1.2

The standard norm of Lebesgue space

We will denote by
\phi_{p}
the
p
-torsion function associated with

that is, the weak solution in the Sobolev space

to the

-torsional creep problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}v&=&1&\text{in }\Omega\\

The first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet

-Laplacian will be denoted by

-normalized eigenfunction corresponding to

\lambda_{p}:=\inf\left\{\frac{\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|v\right\|_{p}^{p}}:v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}=\frac{\left\|\nabla e_{p}\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|e_{p}\right\|_{p}^{p}}

and also that

is a weak solution to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}v&=&\lambda_{p}\left|v\right|^{p-2}v&\text{in
}\Omega\\

Let us define

k_{p}:=\sup_{w\in\mathcal{S}_{p}}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{\infty}

(1.3)

where

}\Omega,\text{ for some }g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\text{ }\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}=1\right\}.

(1.4)

(Note that

For each

(1.5)

Existence and applications

In this section we assume that
\partial\Omega
is at least of class

C^{1,\tau}
for some
\tau\in(0,1).

We recall that in the particular case where
\Omega
is a ball centered at

with radius

the function
\phi_{p}
is radially

\phi_{p}(x)=\frac{p-1}{p}N^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right),\text{ }\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq R.

It follows from this formula that

\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}=\frac{p-1}{p}N^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}R^{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

(2.1)

As for a general bounded domain
\Omega,
one can combine Schwarz

2.1
) to derive the upper bound

\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}\leq M_{0}:=\frac{p-1}{p}N^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{\omega_{N}}\right)^{\frac{p}{N(p-1)}},

\Omega

In sequel

The set

Proposition 2.1

If

\left|u(x)\right|\leq\phi_{p}(x)\quad\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega}.

\left|u\right|_{1,\eta_{p}}\leq K_{p}.

is in terms of

(2.4)

Corollary 2.3

If

(2.5)

\left|u\right|_{1,\eta_{p}}\leq K_{p}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}^{1/(p-1)},

(2.6)

and

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\leq k_{p}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}^{1/(p-1)}.

(2.7)

Proof.
It suffices to consider

Let

v:=\frac{u}{\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}^{1/(p-1)}}\text{ \ and
\ }\widetilde{g}:=\frac{g}{\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}}.

As

(note that
\left\|\widetilde{g}\right\|_{\infty}=1
) the estimates (

) and (

) follow directly

Lemma 2.4

One has

1\leq\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-1)}\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}.

(2.8)

Proof.
As

-\Delta_{p}e_{p}=\lambda_{p}e_{p}^{p-1}\leq\lambda_{p}=-\Delta_{p}(\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-1)}\phi_{p})\text{ in }\Omega

and
e_{p}=\phi_{p}=0
on

it follows from the comparison

Hence,
1=\left\|e_{p}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-1)}\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}.

Now, we present our main existence result.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We recall, from the definition of
\mathcal{E}(M)
that

\lambda\frac{A_{p}}{M^{p-q}}+\beta\frac{B_{p}}{M^{p-(a+b)}}+m\frac{A_{p}}{M^{p-l}e^{-\alpha M^{s}}}\leq 1.

(2.9)

Let us consider the closed, convex and bounded subset
F\subset C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
defined by

F:=\left\{u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}):\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{1/(p-q)}e_{p}\leq u\leq\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\phi_{p}\text{ and \ }\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{k_{p}M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right\}.

(2.10)

Let
T:F\subset C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\rightarrow C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

be the operator that assigns to each
u\in F
the only function
T(u)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}(T(u))&=&\lambda(T(u))^{q-1}+\beta u^{a-1}\left|\nabla u\right|^{b}+mu^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}}&\text{in }\Omega\\
T(u)&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

Thus, for each
u\in F
the function
U=T(u)
is the only weak solution in

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}v&=&g(x,v)&\text{in }\Omega\\
v&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.12)

where the nonlinearity

is defined from

by the expression

g(x,t):=\lambda t^{q-1}+\beta u(x)^{a-1}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{b}+mu(x)^{l-1}e^{\alpha u(x)^{s}},\text{ }x\in\overline{\Omega}\text{ \ and
}t\geq 0.

The uniqueness of
U
follows from
[
11
]
as

is sublinear in the
variable

(recall that
p>q
).

Let us define

\overline{u}:=\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\phi_{p}\text{
\ and \ }\underline{u}:=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{1/(p-q)}e_{p}.

We are going to prove the existence of
U
from the sub-super solution method
by showing that:
\overline{u}
is a supersolution to
2.12
,
\underline{u}

is a subsolution to the same problem, and
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in

\Omega.

As

we have that

0\leq u\leq\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\phi_{p}\leq M\text{ \ in }\Omega

and

0\leq\left|\nabla u\right|\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{k_{p}M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\text{
\ in }\Omega.

Hence,

0\leq u^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}}\leq M^{l-1}e^{\alpha M^{s}}\text{ \ in }\Omega.

Therefore, as

0\leq\overline{u}:=\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\phi_{p}\leq M\text{ \ in }\Omega

the above estimates and (
2.9
) imply that

g(x,\overline{u}(x))\leq\left(\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{p-1}\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega

(2.13)

since

\displaystyle g(x,\overline{u}(x))

\displaystyle=\lambda\overline{u}(x)^{q-1}+\beta u(x)^{a-1}\left|\nabla u(x)\right|^{b}+mu(x)^{l-1}e^{\alpha u(x)^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda M^{q-1}+\beta M^{a-1}\left(\frac{k_{p}M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{b}+mM^{l-1}e^{\alpha M^{s}}\leq\left(\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{p-1}.

As

}\Omega.

Thus, recalling that

we conclude that

\overline{u}
is a supersolution to (

Using that

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(p-1)/(p-q)}\left(-\Delta_{p}e_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(p-1)/(p-q)}\lambda_{p}e_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(q-1)/(p-q)}\lambda_{p}e_{p}^{p-q}e_{p}^{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(q-1)/(p-q)}e_{p}^{q-1}=\lambda\underline{u}^{q-1}\leq g(x,\underline{u})\text{ \ in
}\Omega.

Hence, as
\underline{u}=0
on
\partial\Omega
we conclude that

\underline{u}
is a subsolution to (
2.12
).

In order to prove that
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
in
\Omega
we first
observe from (

) and (

) that

\lambda\frac{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}}{M^{p-q}}\leq 1,

Hence, by using (
2.8
) we obtain

\left(\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{p-1}\geq\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(p-1)/(p-q)}\lambda_{p}

\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-q)}\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}^{(q-1)/(p-q)}\geq\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-q)}\left(\lambda_{p}^{-1/(p-1)}\right)^{(q-1)/(p-q)}=\lambda_{p}^{1/(p-1)}.

It follows from (

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}\underline{u}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{(p-1)/(p-q)}\lambda_{p}e_{p}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{p-1}e_{p}^{p-1}\leq\left(\frac{M}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{p-1}=-\Delta_{p}\overline{u}

and this implies that

in

Therefore, we can apply the sub-super solution method to guarantee the

3
A nonexistence result

In
[
17
]
Garcia Azorero and Peral Alonso proved in Theorem 2.1 that the
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}u&=&me^{u}&\text{in }\Omega\\
u&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

does not have a solution if

m>\max\left\{\lambda_{p},\lambda_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{e}\right)^{p-1}\right\}.

(3.2)

In this section, we extends the nonexistence result by Garcia Azorero and
Peral Alonso for the more general equation

-\Delta_{p}u=mu^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}}+g(x)\text{ in }\Omega.

The following lemma was proved by Allegretto and Huang (see
[
1
, Theorem
2.4]
) as a consequence of Picone’s identity.

Lemma 3.1

Let
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative function. The
Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda_{p}\left|u\right|^{p-2}u+h(x)&\text{in}&\Omega\\
u\geq 0&\text{on}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

has a weak solution if and only if
h\equiv 0
in
\Omega
and
u=0
on

\partial\Omega.
In this case, the solution is a multiple of
e_{p}.

Proposition 3.2

Suppose that
u_{m}\in W_{0}^{1.p}(\Omega)
is a positive weak
solution to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}u&=&mu^{l-1}e^{\alpha u^{s}}+g(x)&\text{in }\Omega\\
u&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

where
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),

g\geq 0,

1\leq l<p
and
\alpha,s>0.
Then

m<\lambda_{p}\left(\frac{p-l}{\alpha se}\right)^{\frac{p-l}{s}}.

(3.4)

Proof.
Let us consider the strictly positive function

Q(t):=m\frac{e^{\alpha t^{s}}}{t^{p-l}},\text{ \ }t>0.

A simple calculation shows that the only critical point of
Q
is

t_{m}=\left(\frac{p-l}{\alpha s}\right)^{1/s}.

As

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}Q(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}Q(t)=+\infty,

we have that
t_{m}
is the only global minimum point. Thus,

Q(t)>Q(t_{m})=mC_{p}\quad\forall\,t\not=t_{m},

where

C_{p}:=\left(\frac{\alpha se}{p-l}\right)^{\frac{p-l}{s}}.

It follows that

mu_{m}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{s}}=Q(u_{m})u_{m}^{p-1}\geq Q(t_{m})u_{m}^{p-1}=mC_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}\text{ in }\Omega

(3.5)

with the equality occurring only if either
u_{m}=t_{m}
or
u_{m}=0.

Now, we observe that

-\Delta_{p}u_{m}=\lambda_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}+h\text{ in }\Omega

(3.6)

where

h:=mu_{m}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{s}}+g-\lambda_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}\geq mu_{m}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{s}}-\lambda_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}\geq(mC_{p}-\lambda_{p})u_{m}^{p-1}.

We are going to show that

mC_{p}-\lambda_{p}<0

which is (
3.4
). Let us suppose, by contradiction, that

mC_{p}\geq\lambda_{p}.

Owing to (
3.6
) and Lemma
3.1
this implies that
h\equiv 0
a.e.
in
\Omega.
Thus,

0\leq(mC_{p}-\lambda_{p})u_{m}^{p-1}\leq mu_{m}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{s}}+g-\lambda_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}=0.

(3.7)

Hence, if
mC_{p}>\lambda_{p}
then (
3.7
) leads to the absurd

u_{m}=0\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,

and if
mC_{p}=\lambda_{p}
, then (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) yield

g=\lambda_{p}u_{m}^{p-1}-mu_{m}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{m}^{s}}\leq 0.

This implies that
g=0
and leads to the equality in (
3.5
) which is
absurd, for it means that

u_{m}=t_{m}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

We remark that (
3.4
) improves the estimate (
3.2
) when
1<p\leq 1+e.

In fact, in this case, (
3.1
) also has no solution if

\lambda_{p}\left(\frac{p-1}{e}\right)^{p-1}\leq m\leq\lambda_{p}.

4

In this section we assume a stronger assumption on the regularity of
\Omega
:
either
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
or
\Omega
convex and
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\tau}
(
\tau
as before).

Our goal is to prove the uniform convergence of
u_{p}
to
d_{\Omega},
as

p\rightarrow\infty,
where
u_{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is the solution to
(
P
) given by Corollary
2.6
. Thus, we consider:
p>\max\left\{q,a+b,l\right\},
the positive parameters
\lambda
and
\beta
arbitrary,
and the parameter
m
restricted to the interval
(0,m_{p}].
We recall that

m_{p}:=\frac{M_{p}^{p-l}}{2A_{p}e^{\alpha M_{p}^{s}}},

M_{p}
is defined by (
2.17
), and

\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{p}}\right)^{1/(p-q)}e_{p}\leq u_{p}\leq\frac{M_{p}}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\phi_{p}\text{ \ and
\ }\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|\leq\frac{k_{p}M_{p}}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}.

(4.1)

To achieve our goal we will make use of the explicit gradient estimates
derived by Ercole in
[
12
]
, They are based on the results by Cianchi and
Maz’ya in
[
9
]
for a class of operators that includes the
p
-Laplacian
as a very particular case.

We recall that the Lorentz space
L^{\theta,1}(\Omega)
consists of all
measurable functions
v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}s^{-1/\theta^{\prime}}\left|v^{\ast}(s)\right|\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

Here,
\theta^{\prime}=\frac{\theta}{\theta-1}
and
v^{\ast}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
stands for the decreasing rearrangement of
v
,
which is defined as

v^{\ast}(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\sup\left\{t\geq 0:\mu_{v}(t)>s\right\}&\text{if}&0\leq s\leq\left|\Omega\right|\\
0&\text{if}&s>\left|\Omega\right|,\end{array}\right.

where

\mu_{v}(t):=\left|\left\{x\in\Omega:v(x)>t\right\}\right|,\quad t\geq 0,

is the distribution function of
v.

As it is well known,
L^{\theta,1}(\Omega)
is a Banach space endowed with the
norm

\left\|v\right\|_{\theta,1}:=\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}\left|v^{\ast\ast}(s)\right|s^{-1/\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}s

where
v^{\ast\ast}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is defined as

v^{\ast\ast}(s):=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}v^{\ast}(r)\mathrm{d}r,\quad s>0.

Thus, if
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),
then

\left\|g\right\|_{N,1}\leq\left\|g^{\ast}\right\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\left|\Omega\right|}s^{-1/N^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}s=N\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{N}}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}

(4.2)

as
\left\|g^{\ast}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}.

Lemma 4.1

Suppose that
p\geq 2
and either
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}
or

\Omega
convex. Let
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be the solution of the
Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}v&=&g&\text{in }\Omega\\
v&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).
Then, there exist positive constants
c
and

\gamma,
that are uniform with respect to
p
and
g,
such that

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq cp^{\gamma}\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}.

(4.3)

Proof.
According to Theorem 1.2 of
[
12
]
,

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq Cp^{(\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p})+\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}}\left\|g\right\|_{N,1},

(4.4)

where
C
is a positive constant that depends at most on
N
and
\Omega.

This estimate holds under the following assumptions:
N\geq 3,

\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{\theta,1},
for some
\theta>N-1,
and
f\in L^{N,1}(\Omega).
Moreover, if the assumption
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{\theta,1}

is replaced with
\Omega
convex, then the estimate (
4.4
) writes as

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq Cp^{(\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p})}\left\|g\right\|_{N,1}.

Since
C^{1,1}\subset W^{2}L^{\theta,1},
(
4.2
) and (
4.4
) lead to
(
4.3
) with
c:=CN\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{N}}
and

\gamma=\frac{5}{2}+\frac{\theta N}{\theta-(N-1)}.
If
\Omega
is convex we
can take
\gamma=\frac{5}{2}.

As for
N=2,
by assuming that
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{\theta,1},
for some

\theta>1,
and
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, for some
q>2,
Theorem 1.3 of
[
12
]

yields the estimate

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq Cp^{(\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p})+\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}}\left\|g\right\|_{q}

(4.5)

where
C
depends at most on
\Omega
and
q.
Hence, as
\left\|g\right\|_{q}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\left|\Omega\right|^{1/p}
and
C^{1,1}\subset W^{2}L^{\theta,1}
the estimate
(
4.5
) holds with
c:=C\left|\Omega\right|^{1/p}
and

\gamma:=\frac{5}{2}+\frac{2\theta}{\theta-1}.
If
\Omega
is convex, then
(
4.5
) writes as

\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\leq Cp^{(\frac{5}{2}-\frac{2}{p})}\left\|g\right\|_{q}

in which case we can take
\gamma=\frac{5}{2}.

Remark 4.2

Following Cianchi and Maz’ya in
[
9
]
, the assumption
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{\theta,1}
means that the boundary of
\Omega
is locally the subgraph
of a function of

lie on the Lorentz space

The regularity hypothesis

\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{N-1,1}
is the weakest possible integrability
assumption on second-order derivatives for the first order derivatives to be
continuous, and hence for
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,0}

[
8
]
.

Remark 4.4

The strict positiveness of
e_{\infty}
follows from the Harnack
inequality proved in
[

(see also

).

The following result is crucial in our analysis.

Proposition 4.5

One has

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}k_{p}=1.

(4.6)

Proof.
We observe from (
2.4
) and item
1
of Lemma
4.3
that

1=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}{\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}}\leq\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}k_{p}.

According to Lemma
4.1

\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{\infty}\leq(cp^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{\ \ for all }w\in\mathcal{S}_{p},\text{ }p\geq 2.

Consequently,

k_{p}\leq(cp^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{\ \ for all }p\geq 2,

so that

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}k_{p}\leq\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}(cp^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=1.

Lemma 4.6

One has

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}M_{p}=\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty},

(4.7)

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}{M_{p}}\leq\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(2\lambda\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}^{q-1})^{1/(p-q)}}=1.

(4.11)

(4.12)

(according to item 1 from Lemma
4.3

Without loss of generality we analyze the case

Proposition 4.8

Assume that
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,1}.
Let
q,

a,

b,

l,

\lambda
and
\beta
be fixed, with
q>1,

a,l\geq 1,
and
b,s,\alpha,\lambda,\beta>0.
For each
p>\max\left\{q,a+b,l\right\}
let
M_{p},

m_{p}
and
u_{p}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be as in Corollary
2.6
. Then,

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}u_{p}=d_{\Omega}\text{ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}.

Proof.
It follows from Lemma
4.7
that, up to subsequence,
u_{p}
converges
uniformly in
\overline{\Omega}
to a function
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying (
4.15
).

We recall that
u_{p}
is also the only weak solution to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rrll}-\Delta_{p}u&=&\lambda\left|u\right|^{q-2}u+h_{p}&\text{in
}\Omega\\
u&>&0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(4.16)

where

h_{p}:=\beta u_{p}^{a-1}\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|^{b}+mu_{p}^{l-1}e^{\alpha u_{p}^{s}}.

As

0\leq h_{p}\leq M_{p}^{a-1}\left(\frac{k_{p}M_{p}}{\left\|\phi_{p}\right\|_{\infty}}\right)^{b}+mM_{p}^{l-1}e^{\alpha M_{p}^{s}}

we note that

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left\|h_{p}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\beta\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{a-1}+m\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{l-1}e^{\alpha\left\|d_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}^{s}}<\infty.

(4.17)

We also know that the solution to (
4.16
) is the only positive minimizer
of the functional

I_{p}(v)=\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}\left|v\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}h_{p}v\mathrm{d}x,\text{ \ }v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

Hence, recalling that
d_{\Omega}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and that
\left|\nabla d_{\Omega}\right|=1
a.e. in
\Omega,
we obtain from the
inequality
I_{p}(u_{p})\leq I_{p}(d_{\Omega})
that

\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{p}^{q})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{\left|\Omega\right|}{p}.

(4.18)

It follows from (
4.18
) that

\limsup_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{p}^{q})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\right]\leq 0.

(4.19)

As
d_{\Omega}-u_{\infty}\geq 0
, the uniform convergence from
u_{p}
to

u_{\infty}
implies that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{p}^{q})\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{\infty}^{q})\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

(4.20)

Using again that
d_{\Omega}-u_{\infty}\geq 0
we have

\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{\infty})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(u_{\infty}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(u_{\infty}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x.

(4.21)

The uniform convergence from
u_{p}
to
u_{\infty}
combined with and
(
4.17
) yields

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(u_{\infty}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x=0

since

\left|\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(u_{\infty}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left\|u_{\infty}-u_{p}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|h_{p}\right\|_{\infty}\left|\Omega\right|.

Thus, it follows from (
4.21
) that

\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

(4.22)

Hence,

\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{p}^{q})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\right]\geq 0.

(4.23)

Combining (
4.19
) and (
4.23
) we obtain

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left[\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{p}^{q})\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}h_{p}(d_{\Omega}-u_{p})\mathrm{d}x\right]=0.

In view of (
4.20
) and (
4.22
) we arrive at

\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}(d_{\Omega}^{q}-u_{\infty}^{q})\mathrm{d}x=0.

Therefore, using once more that
u_{\infty}\leq d_{\Omega}
we conclude that

u_{\infty}=d_{\Omega}.

Observing that the limit function is always
d_{\Omega}
we conclude that

u_{p}
converges uniformly to
d_{\Omega}
in
\overline{\Omega}

(independently of subsequences).

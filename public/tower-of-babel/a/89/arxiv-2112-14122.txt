A connection between symmetry breaking for Sobolev minimizers and stationary Navier-Stokes flows past a circular obstacle
Filippo GAZZOLA – Gianmarco SPERONE – Tobias WETH

Abstract
Fluid flows around a symmetric obstacle generate vortices which may lead to symmetry breaking of the streamlines. We study this phenomenon for planar viscous flows governed by the stationary Navier-Stokes equations with constant inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary data in a rectangular channel containing a circular obstacle. In such (symmetric) framework, symmetry breaking is strictly related to the appearance of multiple solutions. Symmetry breaking properties of some Sobolev minimizers are studied and explicit bounds on the boundary velocity (in terms of the length and height of the channel) ensuring uniqueness are obtained after estimating some Sobolev embedding constants and constructing a suitable solenoidal extension of the boundary data. We show that, regardless of the solenoidal extension employed, such bounds converge to zero at an optimal rate as the length of the channel tends to infinity. Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q30, 35G60, 76D03, 46E35, 35J91. Keywords: symmetry breaking, viscous incompressible fluids, bounds for optimal Sobolev constants.

Introduction

We are interested in showing a connection between some purely theoretical tools in functional analysis, such as Sobolev minimizers, and some problems in fluid
mechanics, such as the appearance of vortices in flows around an obstacle. It is well-known that, if a quickly moving fluid hits an obstacle, a vortex shedding appears behind
the body. This happens also for flows which are laminar before hitting the obstacle.

In literature, the most documented fluid-structure interaction experiment is that of a circular pendulum (cylinder) in a water flow, where
the interaction consists both in the generation of vortices (structure modifying the fluid) and in the appearance of lift forces
(exerted by the fluid on the structure), namely forces transversal to the flow; we refer to

of the lift force, see e.g.

One can then vary the value of the speed

2
Bounds for some Sobolev embedding constants

We consider the Sobolev space
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})
(for both scalar functions and vector fields) and the space of scalar
H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
-functions that are constant on
\partial Q_{R}
and vanish on
\partial B_{1}
, which is a proper connected part of

\partial\Omega_{R}
:

H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\doteq\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})\ |\ v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \partial B_{1}\,,\ v\ \text{is constant on}\ \partial Q_{R}\}\,.

Since
|\partial B_{1}|_{1}=2\pi>0
(the 1D-Hausdorff measure),
the Poincaré inequality holds in
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
, which means that
v\mapsto\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}
is a norm on
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
. This space may be rigorously characterized by using the relative capacity potential
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
of
B_{1}
with respect to
Q_{R}
, defined as the function
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
such that

\displaystyle\Delta\psi=0\quad\text{in}\ \ \Omega_{R},\qquad\psi=0\quad\text{on}\ \ \partial Q_{R},\qquad\psi=1\quad\text{in}\ \ B_{1}\,,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}=\min_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(Q_{R})}\left\{\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla v|^{2}\ \Big|\ v=1\ \text{ in }\ \overline{B_{1}}\,\right\}\,.

Therefore,
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
has the characterization

H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})\oplus\mathbb{R}(\psi-1)\quad\text{with}\quad H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})\perp\mathbb{R}(\psi-1)\,,

so that
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
has codimension 1 within
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
and the “missing dimension” is spanned by the function
\psi-1
. To see this,
determine the orthogonal complement of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
within
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
as follows:

\displaystyle v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})^{\perp}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad v\in H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\quad\text{and}\quad(\nabla v,\nabla w)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}=0\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad v\in H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\Delta v,w\rangle_{\Omega_{R}}=0\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})\,,

so that
v
is weakly harmonic and, since
v\in H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
, it is necessarily a real multiple of
\psi-1
. In (
2.1
),
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Omega_{R}}
denotes the duality product between
H^{-1}(\Omega_{R})
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
.

We then define the Sobolev constant of the embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})\subset L^{4}(\Omega_{R})
as

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\min_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})\setminus\{0\}}\ \frac{\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}\,,

(2.2)

so that
\mathcal{S}_{R}>0
and

\mathcal{S}_{R}\,\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\qquad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})\,.

(2.3)

The main goal of this section is to provide explicit lower and upper bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{R}
, in terms of
R
and
h
. For later use we introduce

\mu_{0}=\mbox{the first zero of the Bessel function of first kind of order zero}\approx 2.40483\,.

(2.4)

Theorem 2.1
.

For
\mathcal{S}_{R}
as in (
2.2
) we have

\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}\,\max\left\{\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\dfrac{\sqrt{R^{2}+h^{2}}}{Rh},\frac{\mu_{0}}{\sqrt{4Rh-\pi}}\right\}\leq\mathcal{S}_{R}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2\kappa}}(2h^{2}\log(h)^{2}-2h^{2}\log(h)+h^{2}-1)\,,

(2.5)

where

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle\doteq-\dfrac{1}{324}+\dfrac{96h}{3125}-\dfrac{9h^{2}}{64}+\dfrac{32h^{3}}{81}-\dfrac{3h^{4}}{4}+\dfrac{7580461h^{6}}{16200000}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{66801h^{6}\log(h)-46690h^{6}\log(h)^{2}+17400h^{6}\log(h)^{3}-3000h^{6}\log(h)^{4}}{90000}\,.

In particular, for any scalar or vector function
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
one has

\|w\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\leq\dfrac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{3}}\,\min\left\{\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\dfrac{Rh}{\sqrt{R^{2}+h^{2}}},\frac{\sqrt{4Rh-\pi}}{\mu_{0}}\right\}\,\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\,.

(2.6)

Proof.

For a scalar function
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
, after combining the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by del Pino-Dolbeault
[
10
, Theorem 1]
with some Hölder inequality, the following inequality was obtained in
[
16
, Theorem 2.3]
:

\|w\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\pi}}\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\qquad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})\,.

(2.7)

In fact, (
2.7
) improves previous results by Ladyzhenskaya
[
18
]
(see also
[
19
, Lemma 1, p.8]
) and Galdi
[
14
, (II.3.9)]
.

We then notice that

-\Delta\left(\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2R}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2h}\right)\right)=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}\dfrac{R^{2}+h^{2}}{R^{2}h^{2}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi x}{2R}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2h}\right)\qquad\forall(x,y)\in Q_{R}\,;

such eigenfunction for the Dirichlet-Laplace operator is positive in
Q_{R}
and vanishes on
\partial Q_{R}
. Therefore, by applying the Poincaré inequality in
Q_{R}
to
w\in H^{1}_{0}(Q_{R})
, we obtain

\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\dfrac{2}{\pi}\dfrac{Rh}{\sqrt{R^{2}+h^{2}}}\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\,.

(2.8)

In order to finish the proof for scalar functions we apply a symmetrization argument. Let
\Omega^{*}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a disk having the same measure as
\Omega_{R}
, and thus its radius is

R_{*}=\sqrt{\dfrac{4Rh-\pi}{\pi}}\,.

Since the Poincaré constant of
B_{1}
is given by
\mu_{0}^{2}
(see (
2.4
)), by rescaling, the Poincaré constant of
\Omega^{*}
is
\mu_{0}^{2}/R_{*}^{2}
. In view of the
Faber-Krahn inequality
[
11
,
17
]
this implies that

\min_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})}\ \frac{\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}\,\geq\,\min_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{*})}\ \frac{\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{*})}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega^{*})}}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{R_{*}}\,,

and therefore

\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\frac{R_{*}}{\mu_{0}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\sqrt{\dfrac{4Rh-\pi}{\pi}}\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\,.

(2.9)

The lower bound in (
2.5
) is reached after inserting (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) into (
2.7
), which then yields (
2.6
).

The upper bound in (
2.5
) is obtained by testing the quotient (
2.2
) with

X_{0}(x,y)\doteq\dfrac{1}{2}\left(h-\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}\right)\log(x^{2}+y^{2})\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega_{R}\,,

(2.10)

which, if extended by zero for
x^{2}+y^{2}\geq h^{2}
, becomes an element of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})
.

Figure 2.1
:
Graph of the function
X_{0}
in (

) for

To conclude we notice that inequality (
2.3
) is also valid for vector functions (with the same constant): if

v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})
is a vector field, by the Minkowski inequality we get

\displaystyle\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\,|v_{1}|^{2}+|v_{2}|^{2}\,\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\left(\|v_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}+\|v_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{S}_{R}^{-2}\left(\|\nabla v_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\|\nabla v_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{R}^{-2}\|\nabla v\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\,.

Therefore, (
2.3
) also holds for vector functions.

3
Symmetry breaking of some Sobolev minimizers

In this section we consider the subspace
H_{0,e}^{1}(\Omega_{R})
of functions
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})
which are even in
x
, that is,
u(x,y)=u(-x,y)
for every
(x,y)\in\Omega_{R}
, and we set

\mathcal{S}_{R,e}=\min_{v\in H_{0,e}^{1}(\Omega_{R})\setminus\{0\}}\ \frac{\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}\,.

(3.1)

We prove here the following symmetry breaking result.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists
R_{0}>0
with the property that
\mathcal{S}_{R,e}>\mathcal{S}_{R}
for
R>R_{0}
. Hence every minimizer of the Sobolev quotient (
2.2
) is a non-even function in the
x
-variable for
R>R_{0}
.

Proof.
Consider the infinite strip
\Omega_{\infty}\doteq\mathbb{R}\times(-h,h)
and put

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}=\inf_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\infty})\setminus\{0\}}\ \frac{\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}}.

(3.2)

Since for
R^{\prime}>R>1
we have the inclusions
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R})\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R^{\prime}})\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\infty})
induced by trivial extension, it follows that

\mathcal{S}_{R}\geq\mathcal{S}_{R^{\prime}}\geq\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\qquad\text{for $R^{\prime}>R>1$}\,.

(3.3)

We claim that

the infimum in (
3.2
) is attained at a function
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
with
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
.

(3.4)

In order to prove (
3.4
) we consider a minimizing sequence for (
3.2
), namely
a sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}\to\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
. It then follows from the Poincaré inequality that
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
. By Lions’ Lemma
[
22
]
, after passing to a subsequence, there exist points

\tau_{n}=(x_{n},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with

v_{n}\doteq\tau_{n}*u_{n}\rightharpoonup v\qquad\text{weakly in $H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ for some function $v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}$},

in which we use the notation
\tau*u\doteq u(\,\cdot\,-\tau)
to denote the translation of a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
with respect
to
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
\Omega_{\infty}
is bounded in the
y
-direction and
v\not=0
, also the sequence
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded
and we may therefore assume that
y_{n}=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently,
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
and
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
. By the Brezis-Lieb Lemma
[
8
]
, we then have

1=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}+c^{4}\qquad\text{with}\quad c\doteq\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}\,.

Consequently,
\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}+c^{2}\geq 1
and therefore

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\left(\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}+c^{2}\right)\leq\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla(v_{n}-v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}.

(3.5)

From this we deduce that

\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}+c^{2}=1=\|v\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}+c^{4}\,.

Since
v\neq 0
, we infer that
c=0
and then
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
. Therefore,

\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}\geq\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
by definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
in
(
3.2
).
Then it follows from (
3.5
) that
\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla(v_{n}-v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}=0
, and consequently
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}=0
by the Poincaré inequality. Hence
v_{n}\to v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
, and
v
is a minimizer of the of the Sobolev quotient (
3.2
). This proves the claimed statement (
3.4
).

We next claim that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\,.

(3.6)

To show this, let
\rho\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a nonnegative function with
\rho\equiv 1
on
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
and

\rho\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{1}
. Moreover, let

\rho_{n}(x)\doteq\rho\left(\frac{x}{n}\right)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,,\ n\geq 1,

so that
\rho_{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(B_{n})
. By (
3.4
), there exists a function
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
with
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
and
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
.
It is then standard to see that the sequence
v_{n}\doteq\rho_{n}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies
v_{n}\to v
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
and, hence,

\|v_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}\to 1\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{2}\to\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\qquad\text{as}\ \ n\to\infty\,.

(3.7)

Since
v_{n}=0
on
\Omega_{\infty}\setminus B_{n}
we have

u_{n}\doteq x_{n}*v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R_{n}})\quad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$ with $x_{n}\doteq(n+1,0)$ and $R_{n}\doteq 2n+1$.}

It thus follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.7
) that

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{R}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{R_{n}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R_{n}})}^{2}}{\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R_{n}})}^{2}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{2}}{\|v_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}^{2}}=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}

which yields the equality in (
3.6
).

By (
3.6
), the proof of the theorem will be finished after showing that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{S}_{R,e}>\mathcal{S}_{\infty}.

(3.8)

To prove (
3.8
), we argue by contradiction, assuming that there exists an increasing divergent sequence
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,\infty)
and, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a function
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0,e}(\Omega_{R_{n}})
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R_{n}})}=1
and

\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R_{n}})}=\|\nabla u_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\infty})}\to\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u_{n}\geq 0
for every
n
, otherwise we replace
u_{n}
by
|u_{n}|
. From the proof of (
3.4
), we know that there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}
and a minimizer
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
of the Sobolev quotient (
3.2
) with
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
and the property that

(t_{n},0)*u_{n}\to v\qquad\text{in $H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})$.}

Since
u_{n}
is symmetric with respect to the
x
-variable, we have

[(-t_{n},0)*u_{n}](x,y)=u_{n}(x+t_{n},y)=u_{n}(-x-t_{n},y)=[(t_{n},0)*u_{n}](-x,y)\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega_{R_{n}}\,,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\,,

which implies that

(-t_{n},0)*u_{n}\to\tilde{v}\qquad\text{in $H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})$,}

where
\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})
is defined by
\tilde{v}(x,y)\doteq v(-x,y)
, for every
(x,y)\in\Omega_{R}
. Then, we
note the following elementary fact:
if, for a sequence
(w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and some sequences
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have that both
(\alpha_{n}*w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\beta_{n}*w_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are convergent sequences in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then the sequence
(\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
From this fact, it follows that
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Passing to a subsequence we may then assume, without loss of generality, that
t_{n}=0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence,

u_{n}\to v\qquad\text{in $H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\infty})$ as $n\to\infty$.}

Since
v
is a minimizer of the Sobolev quotient (
3.2
) with
\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\infty})}=1
, it follows that
v
is a nontrivial weak solution of the semilinear elliptic equation (see, in particular,
[
3
]
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v=\mathcal{S}_{\infty}v^{3}\ &&\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\infty}\,,\\[5.0pt]
&v=0&&\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{\infty}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

By standard elliptic regularity we have that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})
. Moreover,
v\geq 0
in
\Omega_{\infty}
and
v=0
in
B_{1}
since
u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R_{n}})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
v
is superharmonic in
\Omega_{\infty}
by (
3.9
), this contradicts the strong maximum principle. The proof of (
3.8
) is thus finished, which completes the proof of the theorem.
\Box

4
Symmetry breaking in the Navier-Stokes equations

4.1
Non-uniqueness as a necessary condition for symmetry breaking

In this subsection we start by quickly recalling some well-known functional spaces and inequalities, adapting them to our context.
Let us introduce the two functional spaces of vector fields

\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R})=\{v\in H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})^{2}\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega_{R}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{R})=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{R})\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega_{R}\},

which are Hilbert spaces if endowed with the scalar product
(u,v)\mapsto(\nabla u,\nabla v)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}
. We also introduce the continuous trilinear form

\beta(u,v,w)=\int_{\Omega_{R}}(u\cdot\nabla)v\cdot w\qquad\forall u,v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R}),

which satisfies, by Hölder’s inequality,

|\beta(u,v,w)|\leq\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|w\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\quad\qquad\forall u,v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})\,.

(4.1)

Moreover,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta(u,v,w)=-\beta(u,w,v)&\text{for any}\ \ \ u\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R}),\ v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R}),\ w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R}),\\[3.0pt]
\beta(u,v,v)=0&\text{for any}\ \ \ u\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R}),\ v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{R}).\end{array}

(4.2)

We are now in position to define weak solutions for problem (
1.2
).

Definition 4.1
.

Given
U>0
, we say that a vector field
u\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) if
u
verifies (
1.2
)
2
in the trace sense and

\eta(\nabla u,\nabla\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\beta(u,u,\varphi)=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{R}).

(4.3)

The existence of weak solutions of (
1.2
) relies on the construction of a solenoidal extension of the boundary velocity, see
[
20
]
. Namely, one needs to find a vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
such that

\nabla\cdot\Psi=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega_{R}\,,\qquad\Psi=(U,0)\quad\text{on }\partial Q_{R}\,,\qquad\Psi=(0,0)\quad\text{on }\partial B_{1}\,.

(4.4)

Then we prove a result whose qualitative statement is essentially known, see for example
[
14
, Section IX.4]
. We give here a slightly different statement and a full proof because explicit constants are needed to show how uniqueness depends on the magnitude of the solenoidal extension through the Sobolev embedding constant (
2.2
).

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
U>0
there exists a weak solution
(u,p)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
of (
1.2
). If, moreover, there exists a vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
verifying (
4.4
) and the inequality

2\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}\,\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}<\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}\,,

(4.5)

then the weak solution of (
1.2
) is unique, satisfies the symmetry property

u_{1}(x,-y)=u_{1}(x,y),\quad u_{2}(x,-y)=-u_{2}(x,y),\quad p(x,-y)=p(x,y)\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega_{R}\,.

(4.6)

and the estimate

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}<3\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}<\dfrac{3}{2}\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}\,.

(4.7)

Proof.

Existence of a weak solution
(u,p)\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
of (
1.2
) follows from
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
, recalling that the compatibility condition (
1.3
) is fulfilled.

In order to prove the uniqueness statement, suppose there exists a vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
verifying (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) and, by contradiction, that there exist two weak solutions
u,v\in\mathcal{V}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
of (
1.2
). Set
\xi\doteq v-\Psi
, so that
\xi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{R})
can be used as a test function in the weak formulation (
4.3
) satisfied by
v
, thus yielding

\eta\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\eta(\nabla\Psi,\nabla\xi)+\beta(\xi+\Psi,\xi+\Psi,\xi)=0

and, in turn,

\eta\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\eta\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}-\beta(\xi+\Psi,\xi+\Psi,\xi).

(4.8)

In view of (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) we have
\beta(\xi+\Psi,\xi+\Psi,\xi)=\beta(\xi+\Psi,\Psi,\xi)
and the
estimate

\displaystyle|\beta(\xi+\Psi,\Psi,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\left(\|\xi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\left(\dfrac{\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)\,,

where we used (
2.3
). After inserting (
4.9
) into (
4.8
) and applying (
4.5
) so that

\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}<\mathcal{S}_{R}\eta
, we infer the bound

\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\dfrac{\dfrac{\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}+\eta\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\eta-\dfrac{\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}.

(4.10)

Let
w\doteq u-v\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{R})
and
subtract the equations (
4.3
) corresponding to
u
and
v
in order to obtain

\eta(\nabla w,\nabla\varphi)_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\beta(u,w,\varphi)+\beta(w,v,\varphi)=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{R}).

By taking
\varphi=w
and using (
4.1
) and (
4.10
), we derive

\displaystyle\eta\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle=-\beta(w,v,w)=\beta(w,w,v)\leq\|w\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}\,\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\leq\dfrac{\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}\left(\|\xi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}\left(\dfrac{\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}}+\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\,\dfrac{\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}\,\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}{\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}-\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}},

which shows that
w=0
(and, therefore, unique weak solvability for (
1.2
)) provided that (
4.5
) holds. The estimate (
4.7
) follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.10
), noticing that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}=\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}\,\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}+2\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}-\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}-\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}

\displaystyle<\dfrac{\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\left(\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}-2\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\right)+2\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}-\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}-\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}

\displaystyle=3\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}<\dfrac{3}{2}\eta\mathcal{S}_{R}\,.

In case of uniqueness, the symmetry property (
4.6
) is established by noticing that the pair
(v_{1},v_{2},q)\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{R})
defined as

v_{1}(x,y)=u_{1}(x,-y),\quad v_{2}(x,y)=-u_{2}(x,-y),\quad q(x,y)=p(x,-y)\qquad\mbox{for a.e. }(x,y)\in\Omega_{R}\,,

also solves (
1.2
).
\Box

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, we infer that symmetry breaking for the flow, see Figure
1.1
, may occur only if
(
4.5
) is violated, that is, if
for any
vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
verifying (
4.4
) one has

2\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{R}}\,\|\Psi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\geq\mathcal{S}_{R}\eta\,.

In the next subsection we give a sufficient condition for the existence of a vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
satisfying (
4.4
)-(
4.5
).

4.2
A small solenoidal extension of the boundary velocity

The purpose of this subsection is to build an explicit solenoidal extension of the boundary velocity, see
[
20
]
. We will build a vector field
\Psi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
satisfying (

For every

(4.11)

Proof.

(4.12)

Finally, we show that the family of solenoidal extensions built in Theorem

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
R>1
, let
\Psi_{R}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
be a solution of (
4.4
). Then

\liminf_{R\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla\Psi_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\|\Psi_{R}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}{\sqrt[4]{R}}>0\,.

Proof.

Let
\gamma>0
and consider the convex subset of
H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})
given by

\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega_{R})=\{v\in H^{1}_{c}(\Omega_{R})\ |\ v=\gamma\ \text{ on }\ \partial Q_{R}\}\,.

Define

I_{\gamma}(R)\doteq\inf\limits_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}(\Omega_{R})}\left(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\right)\qquad\forall R>1\,,

and let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(h)>0
be the unique positive solution of the algebraic equation

\dfrac{2h}{3\pi^{3}}\,\varepsilon^{4}+\dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{h^{1/4}}\,\varepsilon=1\,.

(4.13)

It suffices to prove that

\liminf\limits_{R\to\infty}\dfrac{\,I_{\gamma}(R)\,}{\sqrt[4]{R}}\geq\varepsilon\gamma\,.

(4.14)

By homogeneity we can take
\gamma=1
, and (
4.14
) will be proved once we show that, for any possible choice of
V_{R}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Omega_{R})
, it holds

\liminf\limits_{R\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla V_{R}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}+\|V_{R}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}}{R}\geq\varepsilon^{4}\,.

(4.15)

So, for any
R>1
, choose a function
V_{R}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Omega_{R})
: two cases may occur. If

\liminf\limits_{R\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla V_{R}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{R}\geq\varepsilon^{4}\,,

then the claimed limit (
4.15
) certainly holds. Otherwise, we have that

\liminf\limits_{R\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla V_{R}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}}{R}=\delta\in\left[0,\varepsilon^{4}\right)\,.

Then there exists a sequence of functions
V_{R_{k}}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}(\Omega_{R_{k}})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
(R_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,\infty)
is
an increasing and divergent sequence, such that

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla V_{R_{k}}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R_{k}})}}{R_{k}}=\delta\,.

(4.16)

By contradiction, assume that, possibly on a further subsequence
(R_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(R_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, it holds

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{\|V_{R_{j}}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}}{R_{j}}<\varepsilon^{4}-\delta\,.

(4.17)

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
we then define a.e. in the rectangle
Q_{j}\doteq(-R_{j},R_{j})\times(-h,h)
the function

W_{j}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-V_{R_{j}}(x,y)&\text{if}\ \ (x,y)\in\Omega_{R_{j}}\,,\\[5.0pt]
1&\text{if}\ \ (x,y)\in\overline{B_{1}}\,,\end{array}\right.

so that
W_{j}\in H^{1}_{0}(Q_{j})
. In view of (
2.11
) we then have, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|\nabla V_{R_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}^{4}=\|\nabla W_{j}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{j})}^{4}\geq\dfrac{3\pi^{3}}{8}\dfrac{R^{2}_{j}+h^{2}}{R^{2}_{j}h^{2}}\|W_{j}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(Q_{j})}=\dfrac{3\pi^{3}}{8}\dfrac{R^{2}_{j}+h^{2}}{R^{2}_{j}h^{2}}\left[\pi+\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}\!\!|1-V_{R_{j}}|^{4}\right].

(4.18)

Then we notice that an application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}|1-V_{R_{j}}|^{4}

\displaystyle=|\Omega_{R_{j}}|-4\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}V_{R_{j}}+\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}V_{R_{j}}^{2}\left(6-4V_{R_{j}}+V_{R_{j}}^{2}\right)\geq|\Omega_{R_{j}}|-4\|V_{R_{j}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\geq|\Omega_{R_{j}}|-4|\Omega_{R_{j}}|^{3/4}\|V_{R_{j}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}=(4hR_{j}-\pi)-4(4hR_{j}-\pi)^{3/4}\|V_{R_{j}}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}\,.

Since we assumed (
4.17
), this shows that

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{R_{j}}\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}|1-V_{R_{j}}|^{4}

\displaystyle\geq 4\left[h-(4h)^{3/4}\left(\varepsilon^{4}-\delta\right)^{1/4}\right]\geq 4\left[h-(4h)^{3/4}\varepsilon\right].

Combining (
4.13
) with (
4.18
), this implies that

\liminf\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{\|\nabla V_{R_{j}}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R_{j}})}}{R_{j}}\geq\dfrac{3\pi^{3}}{2h^{2}}\left[h-(4h)^{3/4}\varepsilon\right]=\varepsilon^{4}>\delta\,,

which contradicts (
4.16
).

\Box

Remark 4.1
.

The lower bound in (
4.14
) can be improved by taking a larger
\varepsilon
, both by noticing that (
4.15
) is merely a sufficient condition for (
4.14
) and by observing that in (
4.19
) one may write

\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}V_{R_{j}}^{2}\left(6-4V_{R_{j}}+V_{R_{j}}^{2}\right)\geq\max\left\{2\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}V_{R_{j}}^{2}\ \ ,\ \ \dfrac{1}{3}\int_{\Omega_{R_{j}}}V_{R_{j}}^{4}\right\}\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{N}\,.

However, we will not pursue this further because we are mainly interested in the growth rate (
4.14
).

If we maintain both the radius of the obstacle
B_{1}
and the
height
2h
of the outer rectangle fixed, the result of Corollary
4.1
yields an explicit
upper bound for the Reynolds number that ensures the existence of a unique weak solution of problem (
1.2
) which, moreover, displays the symmetry (
4.6
). In Figure
4.3
we plot the quantity

\overline{\text{Re}}\doteq\dfrac{\dfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{3\pi}{2}}\dfrac{\sqrt{R^{2}+h^{2}}}{Rh}}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{B}_{1}}+\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{2Rh}}\,\sqrt[4]{\dfrac{3\pi\mathcal{B}_{2}}{2}(R^{2}+h^{2})}}\,,

(4.20)

as a function of
R>h
, for some fixed value of
h>1
.

4.20
) as a function of

, for

Remark 4.2
.

By letting
R\to\infty
, we notice that (
4.20
) implies both

\lim\limits_{h\to 1}\overline{\text{Re}}=0\quad\forall R>1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\overline{\text{Re}}\asymp\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{R}}\quad\text{as}\quad R\to\infty\,.

In fact, this asymptotic behavior as
R\to\infty
cannot be improved. Indeed, the family of solenoidal extensions
\Psi_{R}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{R})
built in Theorem
4.2
satisfies

\|\nabla\Psi_{R}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{R})}\ \ \text{does not depend on}\ R\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\Psi_{R}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{R})}\sim\sqrt[4]{4hR}\quad\text{as}\quad R\to\infty\,.

In view of Theorem
4.3
we deduce that the growth of these norms is “minimal”.

The results of the present section give a second connection between symmetry breaking in the stationary Navier-Stokes equations and Sobolev minimizers.

Remark 4.3
.

Theorem
4.1
states that, as long as uniqueness is guaranteed, symmetry of solutions of (
1.2
) is observed. In turn, uniqueness is ensured provided that (
4.5
), which strongly depends on the Sobolev constant
\mathcal{S}_{R}
, holds. As we saw in Theorem
3.1
, the minimizers for
\mathcal{S}_{R}
lose symmetry as
R\to\infty
.

Efficient Control of High Harmonic Terahertz Generation in Carbon Nanotubes using the Aharonov-Bohm Effect
Yuta Murakami, Kohei Nagai, Akihisa Koga

Abstract
We show that high-harmonic generation (HHG) in carbon nanotubes (CNTs) can be efficiently controlled using the Aharonov-Bohm (AB) effect. When a static magnetic field ( B B ) is applied along the tube, electronic wave functions acquire complex phases along the circumferential direction (the AB effect), which modifies the band structure. When the magnetic field is applied to metallic CNTs, which can be regarded as one-dimensional massless Dirac systems, realistic values of B B lead to a nonzero gap in the THz regime. We demonstrate that such change from gapless to gapped Dirac systems drastically increases the HHG intensity in the THz regime. In the gapless Dirac system, the velocity of each electron never changes under the electric field, and thus there is no HHG. On the other hand, the gap opening activates both the interband and itraband currents, which strongly contribute to HHG. Our work demonstrates a unique way to manipulate HHG in nanotubes by tuning electronic wave functions using the magnetic field and the tube structure.

J_{\rm intra}
as
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm inter}
and
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm intra}
,
the leading terms in the limit of
B\rightarrow 0
read

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm intra}\simeq-F\frac{1}{A_{B}^{2}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{3}},\;\;\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm inter}\simeq F\frac{1}{A_{B}^{2}}\frac{1}{\xi_{3}},

\displaystyle F\equiv i\sigma_{0}t_{\rm hop}\hbar^{2}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\xi_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2\zeta_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\Bigl)

(5)

at
T=0

Here
\zeta_{m}=m\hbar\Omega+i\hbar/T_{1}
and
\xi_{m}=m\hbar\Omega+i\hbar/T_{2}
and
\lambda_{m}=\int dx\frac{x^{m}}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}^{7}}
.
The expression tells that
\sigma^{(3)}
diverges for
B\rightarrow 0
when
T_{1}\neq T_{2}
, while the divergence is absent when
T_{1}=T_{2}
due to the cancelation between
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm inter}
and
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm intra}
.
Our numerical simulation suggests that the same happens also for higher harmonics with

Namely, for
T_{1}=T_{2}
, the contributions from the interband and intraband currents cancel each other efficiently, while for
T_{1}\neq T_{2}
, the cancelation becomes weaker, see SM
Note1
.
Although we used
T_{1}=98.8
fs and
T_{2}=19.8
fs to be consistent with previous studies,
to fully determine the realistic choice of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
for the present energy scale, microscopic evaluation of relaxation and dephasing processes is required
Malic et al. 2011
;
Kemper et al. 2013
;
Stefanucci and Leeuwen 2013
;
Schüler et al. 2020
;
Ridley et al. 2022
;
Rostami and Cappelluti 2021
.

To summarize, we showed that HHG in metallic CNTs can be drastically manipulated in the THz regime using the AB effect.
Tuning the strength of the magnetic field, the HHG intensity can be maximized, where HHG per atom is more efficient than in graphene.
Such controllability of HHG may be useful to develop an efficient HHG device in the THz regime.
In addition, with realistic parameters of CNTs and external fields, a metallic CNT under magnetic fields can be regarded as a tunable 1D Dirac system for THz excitations.
Therefore, it serves as a useful platform to systematically study HHG in Dirac systems.
Furthermore, the unique idea of manipulating HHG via the AB effect can be applied for all kinds of nanotubes including semiconducting CNTs, boron nitride nanotubes
Rubio et al. 1994
;
Chopra et al. 1995
and transition-metal dichalcogenide nanotubes
Tenne et al. 1992
.

Tight Binding Model

As explained in the main text, the tight-binding (TB) model for the armchair CNT with the static magnetic field and the AC electric field along the tube can be constructed on a honeycomb (graphite) sheet;

\displaystyle\hat{H}(t)=-t_{\rm hop}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}e^{i\frac{q}{\hbar}{\bm{A}}(t)\cdot{\bm{r}}_{ij}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{j}-\mu\sum_{i}\hat{n}_{i}~,

I.3
Semiconductor Bloch Equation

In the dipolar gauge
Li et al. 2020
;
Schüler et al. 2021
;
Murakami and Schüler 2022
, the semiconductor Bloch equation (SBE, i.e. the von Neumann equation for the single particle density matrix) is expressed as

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=i[\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t),{\bf h}({\bm{k}}(t))]+\hbar[\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)]_{\rm corr},

(21)

where
[\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)]_{\rm corr}
indicates the collision integral.
The collision integral expresses the scattering, relaxation and dephasing processes originating from electron-electron interactions, electron-phonon couplings and/or impurity scatterings.
In principle, these terms can be evaluated microscopically
Stefanucci and Leeuwen 2013
;
Schüler et al. 2020
;
Ridley et al. 2022
.
However, the direct microscopic evaluation is computationally expensive.
Therefore, in this paper, we employ the relaxation time approximation as

\displaystyle[\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)]_{\rm corr}=-\frac{\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)-\bm{\rho}_{{\rm eq},{\bm{k}}(t)}}{T_{1}}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;+\Bigl(\frac{1}{T_{1}}-\frac{1}{T_{2}}\Bigl){\bf U}({\bm{k}}(t))\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\rm off},{\bm{k}}}(t){\bf U}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}}(t)).

(22)

Here
T_{1}
corresponds to the relaxation of the band population, while
T_{2}
represents the interband dephasing time.
We assume that initially (
t=0
) the system is in equilibrium at temperature
T
and there is no electric field
{\bm{A}}_{E}(0)=0
.

\bm{\rho}_{{\rm eq},{\bm{k}}}
indicates the equilibrium single particle density matrix for
{\bm{k}}
at this initial temperature.
The meaning of these terms becomes clear if one expresses the SBE using the basis that diagonalizes the Hamiltonian at each time (the Houston basis).
For the expression, we introduce a unitary matrix
{\bf U}({\bm{k}})
that diagonalizes
{\bf h}({\bm{k}})
as
{\bf U}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}}){\bf h}({\bm{k}}){\bf U}({\bm{k}})={\bm{\epsilon}}({\bm{k}})

with
{\bm{\epsilon}}({\bm{k}})={\rm diag}[\epsilon_{0}({\bm{k}}),\epsilon_{1}({\bm{k}})]
. The single-particle density matrix in the new basis is
\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)\equiv{\bf U}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}}(t))\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t){\bf U}({\bm{k}}(t))
.
The diagonal component of
\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}
indicates the occupation of the bands, and the off-diagonal component means the interband hybridization (coherence).
In equilibrium,
\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\rm eq},{\bm{k}}}
only hosts the diagonal terms.
In this basis, the SBE equivalent to Eq. (
22
) is

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)

\displaystyle=i[\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\bm{k}}}(t),\bm{\epsilon}({\bm{k}}(t))-q\sum_{a=x,y}E_{a}(t){\bf d}_{a}({\bm{k}}(t))]

\displaystyle+\hbar[\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}^{\rm H}(t)]_{\rm corr},

(23)

with

\displaystyle[\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}^{\rm H}(t)]_{\rm corr}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\rm diag},{\bm{k}}}(t)-\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\rm eq},{\bm{k}}(t)}}{T_{1}}-\frac{\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{{\rm off},{\bm{k}}}(t)}{T_{2}}.

(24)

Here,
\bm{\rho}_{{\rm diag}}
indicates a matrix consisting of diagonal components of
\bm{\rho}
, while
\bm{\rho}_{{\rm off}}
indicates a matrix consisting of off-diagonal components of
\bm{\rho}
.

{\bf d}_{a}({\bm{k}})=i{\bf U}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}})[\partial_{a}{\bf U}({\bm{k}})]
is the (non-abelian) Berry connection for the direction
a
, which plays the role of dipole matrix elements.

\partial_{a}=\partial/\partial k_{a}
is the momentum derivate for the direction
a
.
Note that Eqs. (
21
) and (
23
) are equivalent to the conventional expression of SBE in the length gauge
Wilhelm et al. 2021
;
Yue and Gaarde 2022
;
Murakami and Schüler 2022
.

As explain in the main text, the origin of the radiation is the time-dependent change in the electric polarization
P\;(\equiv q\sum_{i}{\bm{e}}_{\parallel}\cdot{\bm{r}}_{i}\langle\hat{n}_{i}\rangle)
, or the corresponding current
Huttner et al. 2017
;
Yue and Gaarde 2022
.
As in previous works
Ishikawa 2010
;
Dimitrovski et al. 2017
, we introduce the intraband and interband currents based on the Houston basis as

\displaystyle J_{\parallel,\rm intra}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{\hbar N}\sum_{\bm{k}}{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}(t)\partial_{k_{\parallel}}{\bm{\epsilon}}({\bm{k}}(t))\Bigl],

(25)

\displaystyle J_{\parallel,\rm inter}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{q}{\hbar N}\sum_{{\bm{k}},a}e_{\parallel,a}{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}(t)\;[{\bf d}_{a}({\bm{k}}(t)),\bm{\epsilon}({\bm{k}}(t))]\Bigl].

(26)

The intraband current is related to the diagonal components of
\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}(t)
. Hence this current originates from the change in the band occupation.
On the other hand, the interband current originates from the off-diagonal components of
\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}(t)
(the interband components).
Given that the current is the time derivative of the polarization, the interband current is closely related the change in the interband polarization
P_{\rm inter}
.
It is the polarization originating from the interband components as

\displaystyle P_{\rm inter}=\frac{q}{\hbar N}\sum_{{\bm{k}},a}e_{\parallel,a}{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bm{\rho}^{\rm H}_{\bm{k}}(t){\bf d}^{\rm off}_{a}({\bm{k}}(t))\Bigl],

(27)

\displaystyle{\bf d}^{\rm off}_{a}({\bm{k}})=\begin{bmatrix}0&d_{01,a}({\bm{k}})\\
d_{10,a}({\bm{k}})&0\end{bmatrix}.

In the actual implementation, we evaluate currents (
J_{\parallel},J_{\parallel,\rm intra},J_{\parallel,\rm inter}
) using the difference of the single-particle density matrix from equilibrium
\delta{\bm{\rho}}_{\bm{k}}(t)=\bm{\rho}_{\bm{k}}(t)-\bm{\rho}_{{\rm eq},{\bm{k}}(t)}
.
Note that these currents are zero in equilibrium.
This strategy has two benefits. Firstly, one can save the calculation cost since away from the Dirac points
\delta{\bm{\rho}}_{\bm{k}}(t)
is essentially zero and we do not need to evaluate them.
Secondly, this can be directly applied for the Dirac model.
Namely, the currents for CNTs and graphene can be evaluated from the Dirac models using
\delta\bm{\rho}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)
at
{\bm{K}}
and
{\bm{K}}^{\prime}
and summing up these contributions.
If we evaluate the currents from the Dirac models using
\bm{\rho}_{{\bm{k}}}(t)
the results depend on the momentum cutoff.

I.4
Comments on previous studies

Although the manipulation of HHG in a single-wall CNT using the Aharanov-Bohm effect is our main point, there have been some studies on the effects of the static magnetic field on HHG in CNTs
Khosravi et al. 2009
;
Dvuzhilova et al. 2021
. In Ref.
Khosravi et al. 2009
, they consider the mangetic field applied to the perpendicular direction of the tube and numerically show the sensitivity of the HHG spectrum
for the excitation with the light of the wave length 800nm. Thus, the Aharanov-Bohm effect and the Dirac physics are not relevant here.
In Ref.
Dvuzhilova et al. 2021
, they study the propagation of the Airy beam with 1 fs duration in the photonic crystal consisting of many CNTs under magnetic field.
We note that the setup and the timescale involved are very different from our case.

and
\rho^{X}_{\rm eq}(k(t),T(t))
represents the equilibrium single-particle density matrix at the momentum
k(t)
and the temperature
T(t)
.
Note that this expression is slightly extended from Eq. (
22
).
Namely, we consider the time-dependent temperature for the equilibrium state that the system is relaxed to at each time.
Furthermore, the SBE tells that no interband component in the density matrix is induced by the electric field, which already indicates the absence of the interband current.
Using the integrated form of the SBE, the deviation of the single-particle density matrix from equilibrium
\delta\rho_{\bm{k}}^{X}(t)=\rho_{\bm{k}}^{X}(t)-\rho^{X}_{\rm eq}(k(t),T(t))
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{k}^{X}(t)

\displaystyle=\rho^{X}_{\rm eq}(k,T(0))-\rho^{X}_{\rm eq}(k(t),T(t))+\int^{t}_{0}dt[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{X}(t)]_{\rm corr}.

(34)

Note that, at
t=0
, the system is in equilibrium at the temperature
T(0)
and there is no electric field.

Now, if we focus on the contributions to the current from the line passing
K
and
K^{\prime}
points, we have

\displaystyle J_{\parallel}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}J_{{\bm{k}}\parallel}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\frac{\sqrt{3}a^{2}qt_{\rm hop}}{4\pi\hbar}\int dk[\delta\rho_{k}^{R}(t)-\delta\rho_{k}^{L}(t)],

(35)

where

\displaystyle\int dk[\delta\rho_{\bm{k}}^{R}(t)-\delta\rho_{\bm{k}}^{L}(t)]=

\displaystyle-\int dk[\rho^{R}_{\rm eq}(k(t),T(t))-\rho^{R}_{\rm eq}(k,T(0))]

\displaystyle+\int dk[\rho^{L}_{\rm eq}(k(t),T(t))-\rho^{L}_{\rm eq}(k,T(0))]

\displaystyle+\int dk\int^{t}_{0}dt\{[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{R}(t)]_{\rm corr}-[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{L}(t)]_{\rm corr}\}

(36)

\displaystyle=-2\frac{q}{\hbar}A_{E}(t)+\int dk\int^{t}_{0}dt\{[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{R}(t)]_{\rm corr}-[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{L}(t)]_{\rm corr}\}.

Note that the temperature effect vanishes in the integral of the first two terms in the second line.
Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\parallel}(t)=-\frac{1}{n}\frac{\sqrt{3}a^{2}q^{2}t_{\rm hop}}{2\pi\hbar}A_{E}(t)+\int^{t}_{0}dt[\partial_{t}J_{\parallel}(t)]_{\rm corr}

(37)

with
[\partial_{t}J_{\parallel}(t)]_{\rm corr}\equiv\frac{1}{n}\frac{\sqrt{3}a^{2}qt_{\rm hop}}{4\pi\hbar}\int dk\{[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{R}(t)]_{\rm corr}-[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{L}(t)]_{\rm corr}\}
.
Taking the derivative and substituting Eq. (
33
) to
[\partial_{t}\rho_{k}^{X}(t)]_{\rm corr}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}J_{\parallel}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\frac{\sqrt{3}a^{2}q^{2}t_{\rm hop}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}}E(t)-\frac{J_{\parallel}(t)}{T_{1}}.

(38)

This is Eq. (4) in the main text.
Our derivation tells that, even if we improve the relaxation time approximation considering the time-dependent temperature, HHG is absent in the gapless 1d Dirac system.

III
Derivation of
\sigma^{(3)}

We derive the third-order optical conductivity by perturbatively solving the SBE for the Dirac model as in Ref.
Cheng et al. 2015
.
To this end, we change the gauge from the dipolar gauge, which is mainly used in this paper, to the length gauge expressed with the band basis
Murakami and Schüler 2022
.
From the Dirac Hamiltonian Eq. (3) in the main text, we reach the SBE in the length gauge

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{\hbar}E(t)\partial_{k}\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{k}(t)+\frac{i}{\hbar}[\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{k}(t),{\bm{\epsilon}}(k)-qE(t){\bf d}(k)]

\displaystyle-\frac{\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{{\rm diag},k}(t)-\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{{\rm eq},k}}{T_{1}}-\frac{\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{{\rm off},k}(t)}{T_{2}},

(39)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{\epsilon}}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\sqrt{k^{2}+K_{B}^{2}}-\mu&0\\
0&-\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\sqrt{k^{2}+K_{B}^{2}}-\mu\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle{\bf d}(k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{K_{B}}{2(k^{2}+K_{B}^{2})^{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{bmatrix}.

(40)

Here,
k\in[-\infty,\infty]
represents the momentum along
{\bf k}_{\parallel}
,
\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{k}
is the
2\times 2
single-particle density matrix in the length gauge with the band basis,
\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{3}at_{\rm hop}}{2}
and
K_{B}\equiv\frac{q}{\hbar}A_{B}
. In the following, we set
\kappa\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+K_{B}^{2}}
,
r_{k}\equiv\frac{K_{B}}{2\kappa^{2}}
, denote the band index of the matrix as
s=0,1
, and omit ”LB” from
\bm{\rho}^{\rm LB}_{k}
.

To solve the SBE perturbatively, we expand
\bm{\rho}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho_{s_{1}s_{2},k}^{(l)}(t)

(41)

with
\rho^{(l)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t)\propto E^{l}
. Note that we have
\rho_{s_{1}s_{2},k}^{(0)}(t)=\delta_{s_{1},s_{2}}n_{s_{1},k}
, where
n_{s,k}=[e^{\epsilon_{s,k}/{k_{B}T}}+1]^{-1}
and
\epsilon_{s,k}=(-)^{s}\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa-\mu
.

By substituting Eq. (
41
) into Eq. (
39
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hbar\partial_{t}\rho^{(l)}_{ss,k}(t)&=-(-)^{s}qE(t)r_{k}(\rho^{(l-1)}_{\bar{s}s,k}+\rho^{(l-1)}_{s\bar{s},k})\\
&-qE(t)\partial_{k}\rho^{(n-1)}_{ss,k}-\Gamma_{1}\rho^{(l)}_{ss,k},\\
\hbar\partial_{t}\rho^{(l)}_{s\bar{s},k}(t)&=-i(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}\rho_{s\bar{s},k}^{(l)}(t)-(-)^{s}qE(t)r_{k}(\rho^{(n-1)}_{\bar{s}\bar{s},k}-\rho^{(l-1)}_{ss,k})\\
&-qE(t)\partial_{k}\rho^{(l-1)}_{s\bar{s},k}-\Gamma_{2}\rho^{(l)}_{s\bar{s},k},\end{split}

(42)

where
\Gamma_{1}\equiv\hbar/T_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}\equiv\hbar/T_{2}
and
\epsilon_{g,k}\equiv\epsilon_{0,k}-\epsilon_{1,k}
.
We express the SPDM as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho^{(1)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t)&=\int\frac{d\omega_{3}}{2\pi}(-q)E_{\omega_{3}}e^{-i\omega_{3}t}\mathcal{P}^{(1)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(\omega_{3}),\\
\rho^{(2)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t)&=\int\frac{d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{2}}(-q)^{2}E_{\omega_{2}}E_{\omega_{3}}e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\mathcal{P}^{(2)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(\omega_{2},\omega_{3}),\\
\rho^{(3)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t)&=\int\frac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}(-q)^{3}E_{\omega_{1}}E_{\omega_{2}}E_{\omega_{3}}\times\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;e^{-i\omega t}\mathcal{P}^{(3)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}),\end{split}

(43)

where
\omega_{0}\equiv\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}
and
\omega\equiv\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}
.
Using Eq. (
42
), one can evaluate
\mathcal{P}^{(l)}
sequentially from
l=1
.
For
l=1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{(1)}_{ss,k}(\omega_{3})&=\frac{i}{\nu_{3}}\partial_{k}n_{s,k},\\
\mathcal{P}^{(1)}_{s\bar{s},k}(\omega_{3})&=\frac{-ir_{k}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta_{3}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}},\end{split}

(44)

where
\nu_{3}\equiv\hbar\omega_{3}+i\Gamma_{1}
,
\vartheta_{3}\equiv\hbar\omega_{3}+i\Gamma_{2}
,
\Delta n_{k}\equiv n_{0,k}-n_{1,k}
.
For
l=2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{P}^{(2)}_{ss,k}(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&=\frac{i}{\nu_{0}}\Bigl[\frac{i}{\nu_{3}}\frac{\partial^{2}n_{s,k}}{\partial k^{2}}\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-i(-)^{s}r_{k}^{2}\Delta n_{k}\bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}+\epsilon_{g,k}}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\bigl)\Bigl],\\
\mathcal{P}^{(2)}_{s\bar{s},k}(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&=\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl[\frac{r_{k}}{\nu_{3}}\frac{\partial\Delta n_{k}}{\partial k}+\partial_{k}\Bigl(\frac{r_{k}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta_{3}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl)\Bigl],\end{split}

(45)

where
\nu_{0}\equiv\hbar\omega_{0}+i\Gamma_{1}
and
\vartheta_{0}\equiv\hbar\omega_{0}+i\Gamma_{2}
.

For
l=3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(3)}_{ss,k}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{0}\nu_{3}}P_{1;s,k}+\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{0}}P_{2;s,k}(\vartheta_{3})

(46)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{3}}P_{3;s,k}(\vartheta_{0})+\frac{1}{\nu}P_{4;s,k}(\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{1;s,k}&=-i\frac{\partial^{3}n_{s,k}}{\partial k^{3}},\\
P_{2;s,k}(\vartheta_{3})&=i(-)^{s}\partial_{k}\Bigl[r_{k}^{2}\Delta n_{k}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}+\epsilon_{g,k}}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl)\Bigl],\\
P_{3;s,k}(\vartheta_{0})&=i(-)^{s}r_{k}^{2}\frac{\partial\Delta n_{k}}{\partial k}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}+\epsilon_{g,k}}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl),\\
P_{4;s,k}(\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})&=i(-)^{s}r_{k}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}+\epsilon_{g,k}}\partial_{k}\Bigl(\frac{r_{k}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta_{3}+\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl)\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\partial_{k}\Bigl(\frac{r_{k}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta_{3}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl)\Bigl],\end{split}

(47)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{(3)}_{s\bar{s},k}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu_{0}\nu_{3}}P_{5;s,k}(\vartheta)+\frac{1}{\nu_{0}}P_{6;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{3})

(48)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\nu_{3}}P_{7;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0})+P_{8;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{5;s,k}(\vartheta)&=\frac{ir_{k}}{\vartheta-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta n_{k}}{\partial k^{2}},\\
P_{6;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{3})&=-2i\frac{r_{k}^{3}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}+\epsilon_{g,k}}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}-\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl),\\
P_{7;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0})&=\frac{i}{\vartheta-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\partial_{k}\Bigl[\frac{r_{k}}{\vartheta_{0}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\frac{\partial\Delta n_{k}}{\partial k}\Bigl],\\
P_{8;s,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})&=\frac{i}{\vartheta-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\times\\
&\partial_{k}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl\{\partial_{k}\Bigl(\frac{r_{k}\Delta n_{k}}{\vartheta_{3}-(-)^{s}\epsilon_{g,k}}\Bigl)\Bigl\}\Bigl].\end{split}

(49)

Here
\nu\equiv\hbar\omega+i\Gamma_{1}
and
\vartheta\equiv\hbar\omega+i\Gamma_{2}
.

Now we evaluate the third order conductivity. By taking into account the contribution from the two Dirac points (
K
and
K^{\prime}
), the third order current is expressed as

\displaystyle J^{(3)}_{\parallel}(t)=\frac{qa}{n\hbar}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{s_{2}s_{1},k}\rho^{(3)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(t),

(50)

where
v_{s_{2}s_{1},k}
denotes an element of the velocity operator

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{k}\equiv\frac{\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{\kappa}\begin{bmatrix}k&-K_{B}\\
-K_{B}&-k\end{bmatrix}.

(51)

We define the unsymmetrized third order conductivity as

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\equiv-\frac{q^{4}a}{n\hbar}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{s_{2}s_{1},k}\mathcal{P}^{(3)}_{s_{1}s_{2},k}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}),

(52)

where we have

\displaystyle J^{(3)}_{\parallel}(t)=\int\frac{d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}d\omega_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}E_{\omega_{1}}E_{\omega_{2}}E_{\omega_{3}}e^{-i\omega t}\sigma^{(3)}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}).

(53)

Note that in Eq. (
52
) the contribution from
s_{1}\neq s_{2}
corresponds to the interband current (
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm er}
), while that from
s_{1}=s_{2}
corresponds to the intraband current (
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm ra}
).

In the following, we focus on
T=0
and half filling (
\mu=0
). In this case, we have
\Delta n_{k}=-1
and
P_{1;s,k}=P_{3;s,k}=P_{5;s,k}=P_{7;s,k}=0
.
Then, we have
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm ra}=S_{2}+S_{4}
and
\sigma^{(3)}_{\rm er}=S_{6}+S_{8}
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&\equiv-\frac{q^{4}a}{n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{0}}2\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{00,k}P_{2;0,k}(\vartheta_{3}),\\
S_{4}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&\equiv-\frac{q^{4}a}{n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu}2\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{00,k}P_{4;0,k}(\vartheta_{3}),\\
S_{6}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&\equiv-\frac{q^{4}a}{n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu_{0}}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{01,k}[P_{6;0,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{3})+P_{6;1,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{3})],\\
S_{8}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})&\equiv-\frac{q^{4}a}{n\hbar}\int\frac{dk}{2\pi}v_{01,k}[P_{8;0,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})+P_{8;1,k}(\vartheta,\vartheta_{0},\vartheta_{3})].\end{split}

(54)

The concrete expressions for these terms are

\displaystyle S_{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=-\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{4\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{0}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{4}}{\kappa^{7}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}\Bigl),

(55a)

\displaystyle S_{4}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=-\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{2\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{2}k^{2}}{\kappa^{7}}\Bigl[\frac{(\vartheta_{3}+3\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)}{(\vartheta_{0}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}}+\frac{(\vartheta_{3}-3\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)}{(\vartheta_{0}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}}\Bigl],

(55b)

\displaystyle S_{6}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{8\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu_{0}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{4}}{\kappa^{7}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}+\frac{1}{\vartheta-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}\Bigl)\Bigl(\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{3}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa}\Bigl),

(55c)

\displaystyle S_{8}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{2\pi n\hbar}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{2}k^{2}}{\kappa^{7}}\times

(55d)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\Bigl[\frac{(\vartheta+4\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}+3\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)}{(\vartheta+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}(\vartheta_{0}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}+2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}}+\frac{(\vartheta-4\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}-3\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)}{(\vartheta-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}(\vartheta_{0}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)(\vartheta_{3}-2\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}\kappa)^{2}}\Bigl].

The third harmonic generation corresponds to the case of
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}=\Omega
.
In the main text, we denote
\sigma^{(3)}(\Omega,\Omega,\Omega)
simply as
\sigma^{(3)}
.

Now we consider the limit of
B\rightarrow 0
(
K_{B}\rightarrow 0
). One can show that the leading terms are

\displaystyle S_{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=-\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{2\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu\nu_{0}\vartheta_{3}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{4}}{\kappa^{7}},

(56a)

\displaystyle S_{4}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=-\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu\vartheta_{0}\vartheta_{3}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{2}k^{2}}{\kappa^{7}},

(56b)

\displaystyle S_{6}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{2\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\nu_{0}\vartheta\vartheta_{3}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{4}}{\kappa^{7}},

(56c)

\displaystyle S_{8}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})=\frac{iq^{4}a\tilde{t}_{\rm hop}}{\pi n\hbar}\frac{1}{\vartheta_{0}\vartheta\vartheta_{3}}\int dk\frac{K_{B}^{2}k^{2}}{\kappa^{7}}.

(56d)

These terms diverge as
B\rightarrow 0
, and lead to the expression of
\sigma^{(3)}
shown in the main text.
The rest contributions do not diverge (regular).
The expression of
\sigma^{(3)}
tells that the intraband component strongly depends on
T_{1}
, while the interband component strongly depends on
T_{2}
.
This can be understood by the following points.
The intraband component is related to the occupation of electrons in the conduction and valence bands, and
T_{1}
is the time scale
of the occupation returning to its equilibrium value.
On the other hand, the interband component is related to the modification of the interband polarization, i.e. the off-diagonal (interband) component of the density matrix in the Houston basis.
These off-diagonal components are zero in equilibrium, and
T_{2}
is the time scale that the components vanish.

IV.4
Effects of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}

In Fig.
9
, we show the intensity at the HHG peaks
I_{\rm peak}(l)
as a function of the electric-field strength
E_{0}
for
(T_{1},T_{2})=(98.8{\rm fs},98.8{\rm fs})
.
This figure can be directly compared with Fig. 4 in the main text, which shows the results for
(T_{1},T_{2})=(98.8{\rm fs},19.8{\rm fs})
.
The generic behavior is similar to Fig. 4 in the main text.
Namely, the higher order corrections lead to the saturation of the HHG intensity.
(The additional increase of
I_{\rm peak}(3)
around
E_{0}\simeq 100
kV/cm for
B=2
T originates from the deviation from the linear band dispersion.)
The deviation from the perturbative regime happens at smaller
E_{0}
for smaller
B
, i.e. for smaller gap.
The optimal value of
B
that maximizes
I_{\rm peak}
shifts to larger values with increasing
E_{0}
.
On the other hand, unlike the results for
(T_{1},T_{2})=(98.8{\rm fs},19.8{\rm fs})
, the optimal value of
B
does not decrease continuously with decreasing
E_{0}
.
For small
E_{0}
, the HHG intensity is largest around
B=20
T, where the gap is almost twice of
\Omega
.

In order to understand the origin of the different behavior in the weak-electric-field regime between the cases of
(T_{1},T_{2})=(98.8{\rm fs},98.8{\rm fs})
and
(T_{1},T_{2})=(98.8{\rm fs},19.8{\rm fs})
, we compare the contributions to HHG from the interaband and intraband currents, i.e.
I_{\rm inter}
and
I_{\rm intra}
, for a weak magnetic field, see Fig.
10
.
When
T_{1}=T_{2}
,
J_{\rm inter}
and
J_{\rm intra}
cancel each other very efficiently, and thus the total intensity
I_{\rm HHG}
becomes much smaller than
I_{\rm inter}
and
I_{\rm intra}
, see Fig.
10
(a).
On the other hand, when
T_{1}>T_{2}
, the cancelation between
J_{\rm inter}
and
J_{\rm intra}
is less efficient and
I_{\rm HHG}
becomes closer to
I_{\rm inter}
and
I_{\rm intra}
, see Fig.
10
(b).
Thus, the total HHG intensity becomes much larger in the latter case, although both of
I_{\rm inter}
and
I_{\rm intra}
in the former case can be larger than those in the latter case.
This result is consistent with the expression of
\sigma^{(3)}
.
Namely, for
T_{1}\neq T_{2}
it diverges with
B\rightarrow 0
due to the absence of the cancelation between
J_{\rm inter}
and
J_{\rm intra}
.

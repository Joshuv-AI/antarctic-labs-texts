Closed-Form Hybrid Beamforming Solution for Spectral Efficiency Upper Bound Maximization in mmWave MIMO-OFDM Systems Thanks: This work has been supported in part by Academy of Finland under 6Genesis Flagship (grant 318927) and EERA Project (grant 332362).
Mengyuan Ma, Nhan Thanh Nguyen and Markku Juntti

Abstract
Hybrid beamforming is considered a key enabler to realize millimeter wave (mmWave) multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) communications due to its capability of considerably reducing the number of costly and power-hungry radio frequency chains in the transceiver. However, in mmWave MIMO orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (MIMO-OFDM) systems, hybrid beamforming design is challenging because the analog precoder and combiner are required to be shared across the whole employed bandwidth. In this paper, we propose closed-form solutions to the problem of designing the analog precoder/combiner in a mmWave MIMO-OFDM system by maximizing the upper bound of the spectral efficiency. The closed-form solutions facilitate the design of analog beamformers while guaranteeing state-of-art performance. Numerical results show that the proposed algorithm attains a slightly improved performance with much lower computational complexity compared to the considered benchmarks.



I
Introduction

Millimeter wave (mmWave) communication system, with its large utilizable spectrum, is promising to meet the increasing demand for the data rate of current and future wireless networks
[
1
,
2
]
. Hybrid beamforming (HBF) for mmWave multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) communications has attracted great interest in recent years since it guarantees the spatial multiplexing gain with the requirement of reduced power-hungry radio frequency (RF) chains
[
3
]
.

However, the design of HBF in orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) is considerably challenging due to the analog beamformer is required to be shared among the whole bandwidth. This motivates various HBF designs for mmWave MIMO-OFDM systems
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Specifically, Yu
et al.
in
[
4
]
propose a beamforming design based on minimizing the total norm distances between the HBF and optimal unconstrained beamforming matrices for all the subcarriers. To solve the problem, they propose an iterative algorithm by an alternating optimization approach. In
[
5
]
, the analog beamformers are designed by maximizing the upper bound of spectral efficiency (SE). The solution is obtained by an iterative optimization method. In
[
6
]
, Tsai
et al.
propose a low-complexity procedure in which the analog precoder and combiner are designed by respectively extracting the phases of the averaged channel covariance matrix and the averaged conjugate-transposed channel covariance matrix. In
[
7
]
, Zilli
et al.
transform the analog beamformer design into a Tucker2 tensor decomposition problem, allowing to find the solution by the projected alternate least-square-based algorithm. In
[
8
]
, Ku
et al.
propose a reduced-complexity algorithm in which the sub-optimization problems of
[
5
]
are iteratively solved to bypass the matrix-inversion operation.

In most of the aforementioned works, the analog beamformers are obtained by iterative optimization approaches. Such designs entail excessively high computational burdens of the transceiver, making HBF less efficient, especially in massive MIMO systems
[
9
]
. To overcome this challenge, in this work, we propose a novel scheme for HBF design and optimization. Specifically, we focus on the problem of maximizing the upper bound of the system SE. The closed-form solutions to the analog beamformers are found efficiently by partial singular value decomposition (SVD). The proposed scheme is computationally efficient and feasible for practical implementation because it does not perform any iterative procedure. Nevertheless, it still achieves slight performance improvement compared with state-of-art HBF algorithms.

II
MmWave MIMO-OFDM System

II-A

Signal Model

Fig. 1:
HBF transceiver structure in a mmWave MIMO-OFDM system.

We consider a MIMO-OFDM system where the transmitter and receiver are equipped with
N_{t}
and
N_{r}
antennas, respectively. Without loss of generality, we assume that transmitter and receiver are deployed with the same number of RF chains, denoted by
N_{RF}
(
N_{RF}\leq\min(N_{t},N_{r})
). Let
K
be the number of subcarriers, and
{\bm{s}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{s}\times 1}

(N_{s}\leq N_{RF})
be the transmitted symbol vector at the
k
th subcarrier,
{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\bm{s}}_{k}{\bm{s}}^{H}_{k}\right]={\bm{I}}_{N_{s}},\;k=1,\cdots,K
, where
{\bm{I}}_{N_{s}}
denotes the
N_{s}\times N_{s}
identity matrix. As illustrated in Fig.
1
, the transmitted vector
{\bm{s}}_{k}
is first precoded by the low-dimensional baseband precoder
{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{RF}\times N_{s}}
and then transformed into time domain by
N_{RF}

K
-point inverse fast Fourier transforms (IFFTs). The signal vector in time domain is then added the cyclic prefix (CP) and precoded by the common high-dimensional analog precoder, denoted by
{\bm{F}}_{RF}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{t}\times N_{RF}}
satisfying
|{\bm{F}}_{RF}(i,j)|=1/\sqrt{N_{t}},\forall i,j
, for all subcarriers. Here,
{\bm{A}}(i,j)
denotes the entry on the
i
th row and
j
th colum of matrix
{\bm{A}}
, and
|x|
represents the modulus of the complex number
x
. The final transmitted signal for each subcarrier is given as

{\bm{x}}_{k}={\bm{F}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}

(1)

where
{\bm{F}}_{k}={\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]
and
\|{\bm{F}}_{k}\|^{2}_{F}\leq P_{b}
, with
P_{b}
being the power budget for each subcarrier.

At the receiver, the received signal vector is first combined by the common analog combiner, represented by
{\bm{W}}_{RF}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{r}\times N_{RF}}
,
|{\bm{W}}_{RF}(i,j)|=1/\sqrt{N_{r}},\forall i,j
. After discarding the CP and performing
N_{RF}

K
-point fast Fourier transforms (FFTs), the combined signal is further processed at frequency domain by low-dimensional baseband combiner
{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{RF}\times N_{s}}
for each subcarrier. Finally, the received signal at the
k
th subcarrier through channel
{\bm{H}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{r}\times N_{t}}
is given as

{\bm{y}}_{k}={\bm{W}}^{H}_{k}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{k}{\bm{s}}_{k}+{\bm{W}}^{H}_{k}{\bf n}_{k}

(2)

where
{\bm{W}}_{k}={\bm{W}}_{RF}{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]
, and
{\bf n}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\sigma_{n}^{2}{\bm{I}}_{N_{r}})
is the additive white Gaussian noise vector at the
k
th subcarrier with
\sigma_{n}^{2}
being the noise variance.

II-B

Channel Model

The mmWave channel can be characterized by the Saleh-Valenzuela model
[
10
]
. For OFDM systems, the frequency-selective channel is divided into
K
frequency-flat sub-channels with non-interfering subcarriers. In frequency domain, the channel matrix at the
k
th subcarrier can be given by
[
5
,
7
]

{\bm{H}}_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{N_{r}N_{t}}{N_{cl}N_{ray}}}\sum_{c=0}^{N_{cl}-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{ray}}\alpha_{c,\ell}{\bm{a}}_{r}(\theta^{r}_{c,\ell}){\bm{a}}_{t}^{H}(\theta^{t}_{c,\ell})e^{\frac{-j2\pi c(k-1)}{K}},

(3)

where
\alpha_{c,\ell}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,
\theta^{r}_{c,\ell}
, and
\theta^{t}_{c,\ell}
are the path gain, angles of arrival and departure of the
\ell
th path in the
c
th cluster, respectively. Furthermore,
\theta^{r}_{c,\ell}
and
\theta^{t}_{c,\ell}
follow a Laplacian distribution with mean angles
\theta^{r}_{c}
,
\theta^{t}_{c}
uniformly distributed over
[0,2\pi)
, and angular spread of
\sigma_{r}
and
\sigma_{t}

[
11
]
.
{\bm{a}}_{r}(\cdot)
and
{\bm{a}}_{t}(\cdot)
are array response vectors at the receiver and transmitter, respectively. For simplicity, we assume that the numbers of scattering clusters and propagation paths, and the angular spreads are the same for all subcarriers. Furthermore, we assume the uniform linear array (ULA) is employed. The steering vector of ULA with
N
antennas and spacing distance
d
of adjacent antenna is modeled as
[
12
]

{\bm{a}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[1,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}d\sin(\theta)},\cdots,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(N-1)d\sin(\theta)}\right]^{T},

(4)

where
\lambda
is the signal wavelength. Note that in this representation, the operating frequency is assume to be much larger than the total bandwidth of the OFDM system such that signal wavelength in all subcarriers is approximately the same.

II-C

Problem Formulation

In this work, we focus on the problem of HBF design with the assumption that the full channel state information (CSI) is available at both the transmitter and receiver
[
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
. We aim at designing transmit and receive HBF matrices that maximize the overall SE of the system under power constraints for subcarriers and constant-modulus constraints for analog beamformers. The problem is expressed as

\displaystyle\underset{{\bm{F}}_{RF},{\bm{W}}_{RF},\atop\left\{{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k],{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]\right\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\max}\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}R_{k}

(5a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\|{\bm{F}}[k]\|^{2}_{F}\leq P_{b},

(5b)

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{F}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{t}},\quad\forall i,j,

(5c)

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{W}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{r}},\quad\forall i,j,

(5d)

where
R_{k}
denotes the SE of the
k
th subcarrier. Considering the Gaussian signalling,
R_{k}
is expressed as

R_{k}=\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}_{N_{s}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{W}}_{k}^{\dagger}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{k}{\bm{F}}^{H}_{k}{\bm{H}}^{H}_{k}{\bm{W}}_{k}\right|,

(6)

where
\dagger
denotes the Moore-Penrose pseudo inversion,
{\bm{F}}_{k}={\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]
and
{\bm{W}}_{k}={\bm{W}}_{RF}{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]
.

III
Hybrid beamforming design

The SE maximization problem in (
5
) requires a joint optimization over both the transmitter and receiver beamformers, i.e.,
\left\{{\bm{F}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
and
\left\{{\bm{W}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
. It is non-convex due to the unit-modulus constraints (
5c
) and (
5d
) of the analog beamformers. Therefore, it is challenging to obtain the optimal solution
[
5
]
. To tackle this challenge, we decouple the problem into two subproblems. Specifically, for the design of transmitter beamformers
\left\{{\bm{F}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
, we assume that the optimal receiver is used. Then, the receiver beamformers
\left\{{\bm{W}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
are obtained given the transmitter beamformers
\left\{{\bm{F}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
. The solutions to these subproblems are presented in the following subsections.

III-A

Transmitter HBF Design

Given the receive beamforming matrices
\left\{{\bm{W}}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
, the hybrid beamforming design problem at the transmitter is expressed as

\displaystyle\max\limits_{{\bm{F}}_{RF},\left\{{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]\right\}_{k=1}^{K}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{R}_{k}

(7a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\|{\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]\|^{2}_{F}\leq P_{b},

(7b)

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{F}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{t}},\quad\forall i,j,

(7c)

where
\tilde{R}_{k}
is given as

\tilde{R}_{k}=\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]{\bm{F}}_{BB}^{H}[k]{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{H}}^{H}_{k}\right|.

(8)

When
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
is fixed, the optimal digital precoder is given as

{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]=({\bm{F}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{F}}_{RF})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{V}}_{k}{\bm{\Gamma}}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k},

(9)

where
{\bm{V}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{RF}\times N_{s}}
is the set of right singular vectors corresponding to the
N_{s}
largest singular values of
{\bm{Q}}_{k}\triangleq{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{RF}({\bm{F}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{F}}_{RF})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and
{\bm{\Gamma}}_{k}
is the diagonal matrix with its diagonal elements being the allocated power to each symbol of the subcarrier
k
.

Since it is challenging to design the analog beamforming matrix
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
that is shared among all the subcarriers, we alternatively tend to find the solution to the upper bound of the objective function in problem (
7
). For moderate and high signal-to-noise-ratio (SNR) regime, it is asymptotically optimal to adopt the equal power allocation for all streams in each subcarrier
[
13
]
. In this regime, we have
{\bm{\Gamma}}_{k}=\gamma{\bm{I}}_{N_{s}}
and
\gamma=P_{b}/N_{s}
. Substituting (
9
) into (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{k}\overset{a}{=}

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}\tilde{{\bm{I}}}_{N_{RF}}\right|

\displaystyle\overset{b}{\leq}

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}{\bm{I}}_{N_{RF}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}\right|,

(10)

where the equality in (a) is obtained based on

{\bm{V}}_{k}{\bm{V}}_{k}^{H}=\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}\tilde{{\bm{I}}}_{N_{RF}}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}^{H}

(11)

with
\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{RF}\times N_{RF}}
being a unitary matrix and
\tilde{{\bm{I}}}_{N_{RF}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}{\bm{I}}_{N_{s}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{array}\right]
being a
N_{RF}\times N_{RF}
matrix. Furthermore, the equation in (b) is because
\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}\tilde{{\bm{V}}}_{k}
is a semi-definite positive matrix. Note that the relaxation of the inequality (b) is tight when
N_{s}=N_{RF}

[
5
]
.

Since
{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{F}}_{RF}\rightarrow{\bm{I}}_{N_{RF}}
when the array size is very large
[
11
]
, it is a reasonable assumption that the analog precoder
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
is a matrix of full-column rank. In this case, the independence between different analog beamformers for different RF chains endows the largest optimization space for finding the optimal digital beamformer. Based on the QR decomposition, we can express
{\bm{F}}_{RF}={\bm{V}}_{RF}{\bm{B}}
, where
{\bm{V}}_{RF}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{t}\times N_{RF}}
has orthogonal column vectors, i.e.,
{\bm{V}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{V}}_{RF}={\bm{I}}_{N_{RF}}
, and
{\bm{B}}
is an invertible matrix. The upper bound of the objective function in (
7
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tilde{R}_{k}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{Q}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{Q}}_{k}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{V}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{V}}_{RF}\right|

\displaystyle\overset{c}{\leq}

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{V}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{e}{\bm{V}}_{RF}\right|,

(12)

where
{\bm{H}}_{e}\triangleq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{H}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{k}
, and the equation in (c) is based on Jensen’s inequality. In summary, the analog precoder
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
can be obtained by solving the following problem

\displaystyle\max\limits_{{\bm{F}}_{RF}}

\displaystyle\quad\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{\gamma}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{V}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{e}{\bm{V}}_{RF}\right|

(13)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{F}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{t}},\quad\forall i,j.

The unconstrained solution to (
13
), denoted by
{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{*}
, is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Let
{\bm{H}}_{e}={\bm{V}}_{e}{\bm{\Lambda}}_{e}{\bm{V}}_{e}^{H}
, where
{\bm{V}}_{e}=[{\bm{v}}_{e,1},\cdots,{\bm{v}}_{e,N_{t}}]
is a unitary matrix, and
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{e}
is a diagonal matrix with diagonal elements being the eigenvalues of
{\bm{H}}_{e}
in a decreasing order. The unconstrained solution to (
13
) is given as

{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{*}={\bm{V}}_{RF}^{*}{\bm{C}},

(14)

where
{\bm{V}}_{RF}^{*}=[{\bm{v}}_{e,1},\cdots,{\bm{v}}_{e,N_{RF}}]
and
{\bm{C}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{RF}\times N_{RF}}
is an arbitrary invertible matrix.

Proof

See in Appendix
A
.
\blacksquare

As a result, the constrained solution is obtained by projecting
{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{*}
into the feasible space
[
14
]
, i.e.,

{\bm{F}}_{RF}(i,j)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{t}}}e^{j\angle{\bm{F}}_{RF}^{*}(i,j)},\;\forall i,j,

(15)

where
\angle x
denotes the phase of the complex number
x
.

III-B

Receiver HBF Design

For a fixed analog combiner
{\bm{W}}_{RF}
, the optimal digital combiner of each subcarrier is the MMSE solution, i.e.,

{\bm{W}}_{BB}^{*}[k]=\left({\bm{J}}_{k}{\bm{J}}^{H}_{k}+\sigma_{n}^{2}{\bm{W}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{W}}_{RF}\right)^{-1}{\bm{J}}_{k},

(16)

where
{\bm{J}}_{k}\triangleq{\bm{W}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{k}
. Then, the problem of analog combiner design can be formulated as

\displaystyle\max\limits_{{\bm{W}}_{RF}}

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{W}}_{RF}^{\dagger}{\bm{T}}_{k}{\bm{W}}_{RF}\right|

(17)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{W}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{r}},\quad\forall i,j,

where
{\bm{T}}_{k}\triangleq{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{k}{\bm{F}}^{H}_{k}{\bm{H}}^{H}_{k}
. Based on the QR decomposition, we can express
{\bm{W}}_{RF}={\bm{U}}_{RF}{\bm{D}}
, where
{\bm{U}}_{RF}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N_{r}\times N_{RF}}
has orthogonal column vectors, i.e.,
{\bm{U}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{U}}_{RF}={\bm{I}}_{N_{RF}}
, and
{\bm{D}}
is an invertible matrix. By applying the Jensen’s inequality, we can consider maximizing the upper bound of (
17
) for designing
{\bm{W}}_{RF}
as,

\displaystyle\max\limits_{{\bm{W}}_{RF}}

\displaystyle\quad\log_{2}\left|{\bm{I}}+\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\bm{U}}_{RF}^{H}{\bm{T}}_{e}{\bm{U}}_{RF}\right|

(18)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{W}}_{RF}(i,j)\right|=1/\sqrt{N_{r}},\quad\forall i,j,

where
{\bm{T}}_{e}\triangleq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{T}}_{k}
. The optimal solution to problem (
18
) can be obtained similarly as that to (
13
).

Finally, based on (
9
), (
14
),(
15
), (
16
), and (
18
), the proposed HBF scheme is summarized in Algorithm 1.

Algorithm 1
: The proposed HBF scheme for mmWave

MIMO-OFDM systems

1. Input:
{\bm{H}}_{k},\gamma,\sigma^{2}_{n}

2.
{\bm{H}}_{e}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{H}}_{k}^{H}{\bm{H}}_{k}
.

3.  Find
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
according to (
15
).

4.  Calculate
{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]
according to (
9
).

5.
{\bm{T}}_{e}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]({\bm{H}}_{k}{\bm{F}}_{RF}{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k])^{H}
.

6.  Find
{\bm{W}}_{RF}
by solving problem (
18
).

7.  Calculate
{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]
according to (
16
).

8. Output:
{\bm{F}}_{RF}
,
{\bm{W}}_{RF}
,
{\bm{F}}_{BB}[k]
,
{\bm{W}}_{BB}[k]
.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a low-complexity algorithm for HBF design in a mmWave MIMO-OFDM system. We obtain closed-form solutions to the analog and digital beamformers that maximize the upper bound of the SE. The numerical results show that the proposed method achieves state-of-art performance while requiring very low complexity. Therefore, the proposed scheme is feasible and practical for the implementation of mmWave massive MIMO systems. For future researches, the low-complexity HBF design in multi-cell and multi-user scenarios with imperfect CSI can be considered.

Well-posedness and Continuity Properties of the Degasperis-Procesi equation in critical Besov space
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Weipeng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we obtain the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of solution to the Degasperis-Procesi equation in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, we prove that the data-to-solution of this equation is continuous but not uniformly continuous in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
.

Keywords:
Degasperis-Procesi equation; Local well-posedness; non-uniform dependence

MSC (2010):
35Q53, 37K10.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the Degasperis-Procesi (DP) equation
[
6
]

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+uu_{x}=-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}(u^{2}),&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

We should also mention the following famous Camassa-Holm (CH) equation
[
3
]

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=-\partial_{x}\big(1-\partial^{2}_{x}\big)^{-1}\big(u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\big),\;&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\;&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The DP equation can be regarded as a model for nonlinear shallow water dynamics and its asymptotic
accuracy is the same as for the CH shallow water equation
[
4
]
. The DP equation is integrable and has a
bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
5
]
. An inverse scattering approach for computing
n
-peakon solutions to the DP
equation was presented in
[
15
]
. Its traveling wave solutions were investigated in
[
17
,
24
]
.
Regarding well-posedness (existence, uniqueness, and stability of solutions) of the Cauchy problem for the
DP equation (
1.1
), we refer to see
[
9
,
14
,
18
,
23
,
27
]
. Similar to the CH equation, the DP equation has also global strong solutions

[
21
,
25
,
26
]
and finite time blow-up solutions
[
8
,
7
,
21
,
22
,
27
]
.
Although the DP equation is similar to the CH equation in several aspects, these two equations are truly
different. One of the novel features of the DP different from the CH equation is that it has not only peakon solutions
[
5
]
and periodic peakon solutions
[
28
]
, but also shock peakons
[
16
]
and the periodic shock waves
[
7
]
.

In our recent papers
[
19
,
20
]
, we proved that the DP equation (
1.1
) is not uniformly continuous in
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
s>1+1/p,\ p\in[1,\infty],\ r\in[1,\infty)
or
s=1+1/p,\ p\in[1,\infty),\ r=1.
Guo, Liu, Molinet and Yin
[
11
]
established the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) in the critical Sobolev space
H^{3/2}
and even in the Besov space
B_{p,r}^{1+1/p}
with
p\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty]
by proving the norm inflation. For more ill-posedness results of the Cauchy problem for the DP equation (
1.1
), we refer to see
[
12
,
13
]
and the references therein. However, to our best knowledge, for the Cauchy problem of the DP equation (
1.1
) with
initial data in
B^{1}_{\infty,1}
is locally well-posed in the sense of Hadamard has not been solved yet. Before stating our main result precisely, we recall the notion of well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard:

Definition 1.1
(Local Well-posedness)

We say that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed
in a Banach space
X
if the following three conditions hold

1.

(Local existence)  For any initial data
u_{0}\in X
, there exists a short time
T=T(u_{0})>0
and a solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
);

2.

(Uniqueness)  This solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is unique in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
;

3.

(Continuous Dependence)  The data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is continuous in the following
sense: for any
T_{1}<T
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that if
\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{X}\leq\delta
,
then
\mathbf{S}_{t}(\tilde{u}_{0})
exists up to
T_{1}
and

\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\tilde{u}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)}\leq\varepsilon.

Now, we can formulate the main result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
u_{0}\in B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, there exists some time
T>0
such that

1.

system (
1.1
) has a solution
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],B^{0}_{\infty,1})
;

2.

the solutions of (
1.1
) are unique;

3.

if the initial data have some additional regularity
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{\infty,1}
with
s>1
, then the solution exists on
[0,T]
and is in
the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{s}_{\infty,1})\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],B^{s-1}_{\infty,1})
;

4.

the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
is continuous from any bounded
subset of
u_{0}\in B^{1}_{\infty,1}
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})
;

5.

the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto u(t)
is not uniformly continuous from any bounded subset of
u_{0}\in B^{1}_{\infty,1}
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})
.

Remark 1.1

Theorem
1.1
tells us that there exists a positive time
T
such that System (
1.1
) has a unique solution and the solution is continuously dependent on the initial data in the sense of Hadmard.

Preliminaries

Notation
In the following, we denote by
*
the convolution. Throughout this paper, we shall use

\big(1-\partial^{2}_{x}\big)^{-1}f=p*f
with
p(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|}.

Given a Banach space
X
, we denote its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. For
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I;X)
the set of continuous functions on
I
with values in
X
. Sometimes we will denote
L^{p}(0,T;X)
by
L_{T}^{p}X
.
For all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}f
(also denoted by
\widehat{f}
) is defined by

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any}\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, we will recall some facts about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the nonhomogeneous Besov spaces and their some useful properties.

Definition 2.1
(See
[
1
]
)

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq 4/3\}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:3/4\leq|\xi|\leq 8/3\}.

There exist two radial functions
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
both taking values in
[0,1]
such that

\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\quad\forall\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

For every
u\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
, the Littlewood-Paley dyadic blocks
{\Delta}_{j}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle 0,

if
j\leq-2
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

if
j=-1
;

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}u=

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u\big),

if
j\geq 0
.

The inhomogeneous low-frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
is defined by

S_{j}u=\sum_{q=-1}^{j-1}{\Delta}_{q}u.

Definition 2.2
(See
[
1
]
)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

\displaystyle B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}):\;\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty\Big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq-1}2^{sjr}\|\Delta_{j}f\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

if
1\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq-1}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

if
r=\infty
.

Lemma 2.1
(See
[
1
]
)

For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s>0
,
B^{s}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is an algebra. Moreover,
B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and for any
u,v\in B^{s}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}\leq C(\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}).

We also have the following interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Lemma 2.2
(See
[
1
,
18
]
)

Let
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
and
\sigma\geq-\min\big\{\frac{1}{p},1-\frac{1}{p}\big\}
. Assume that
f_{0}\in B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})
,
g\in L^{1}([0,T];B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}))
and
\partial_{x}\mathbf{u}\in L^{1}([0,T];B^{\sigma-1}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}))
if
\sigma>1+\frac{1}{p}
or
\sigma=1+\frac{1}{p},r=1
.
If
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}))
solves the following linear transport equation:

\quad\partial_{t}f+\mathbf{u}\partial_{x}f=g,\quad\;f|_{t=0}=f_{0}.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,r,\sigma)
such that the following statement holds

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}}\leq e^{CV(t)}\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-CV(\tau)}\|g(\tau)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big),

where

V(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{u}(\tau)\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\text{if}\quad\sigma>1+{\frac{1}{p}}\quad\text{or}\quad\{\sigma=1+\frac{1}{p},\;r=1\}.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we divide Proof of Theorem
1.1
into three steps.

3.1
Existence and Uniqueness

Applying Lemma
2.2
to Eq.(
1.1
), we have for
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{V(t)}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-V(\tau)}\|\mathbf{P}(u)(\tau)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq e^{V(t)}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-V(\tau)}\|u(\tau)\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big),

(3.3)

where we denote

V(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

and have used

\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Then we obtain form (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle F(t):=e^{-V(t)}\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\int_{0}^{t}F(\tau)\|u(\tau)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

which follows from the Gronwall inequality that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big).

By the continuity argument, we can obtain the uniform bounds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

(3.4)

Applying Lemma
2.2
to Eq.(
1.1
) once again, we have for all
t\in[0,T]
and
s>1

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{\infty,1}}.

(3.5)

The existence follows the standard procedure, we omit the details. The uniqueness is the direct result of the following lemma. In fact, suppose that
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})
are two solutions of (
1.1
) with the same initial data
u_{0}
, then
we have

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)\|_{L^{\infty}}=0,

which implies the uniqueness.

Lemma 3.1

Let
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})
be two solutions of (
1.1
) associated with
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively. Then we have the estimate for the difference
w=u-v

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\Big(\|w_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big),

(3.7)

where the constants
C
depends on
T
and initial norm
\|u_{0},v_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}
.

Proof.
Obviously, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+uw_{x}=-wv_{x}-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big),\\
w(0,x)=u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(3.8)

Taking the inner product of Eq. (
3.8
) with
|w|^{p-2}w
with
p\geq 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|w\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{x}|w|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{x}|w|^{p}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big)|w|^{p-2}w\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{x},v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|^{p}_{L^{p}}+\frac{3}{2}\big\|\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}\|w\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{x},v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|^{p}_{L^{p}}+\frac{3}{2}\|w(u+v)\|_{L^{p}}\|w\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u,v,u_{x},v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|^{p}_{L^{p}},

(3.9)

where we have used that

\big\|\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}=\big\|\partial_{x}p\ast\big(w(u+v)\big)\big\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|w(u+v)\|_{L^{p}}.

Integrating the above (
3.1
) with respect to time
t\in[0,T]
yields

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{T}\|u,v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}s\Big).

Letting
p=\infty
implies (
3.6
).

Applying Lemma
2.2
to Eq (
3.8
) yields

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-C\|w_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|wv_{x},\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|wv_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|\partial_{x}(1-\partial^{2}_{x})^{-1}\big(w(u+v)\big)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|wv_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|w(u+v)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|w\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u+v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|w\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u,v\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\|v_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Gronwall’s inequality gives us the desired (
3.7
).

3.2
Continuous Dependence

Now we will prove that the solution is continuously dependent on the initial data by
[
2
,
10
]
. The main difficulty lies in that the DP equation is of hyperbolic type. Precisely speaking, if
u,v\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],B^{1}_{\infty,1})
are two solutions of (
1.1
) associated with
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, in view of (
3.7
), we have to tackle with the term
\|v_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}
. To bypass this, we can take
v=\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})
as the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
S_{N}u_{0}
.

Letting
u=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
and
v=\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})
, using Lemma
3.1
and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{N}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-N}\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

Then, using the triangle inequality, we have for
u_{0},\widetilde{u}_{0}\in B^{1}_{\infty,1}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle~~+\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\|S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle~~+C\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}+\mathbf{I}_{3}.

By the interpolation inequality, one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{2}_{\infty,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|S_{N}u_{0}-S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\frac{N}{2}}\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}},

which clearly implies

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|S_{N}\widetilde{u}_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+2^{\frac{N}{2}}\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{N}u_{0}-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+2^{\frac{N}{2}}\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}.

This completes the proof of continuous dependence.

3.3
Non-uniform continuous dependence

Let
\hat{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be an even, real-valued and non-negative function on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)=

\displaystyle 1,

if
|x|\leq\frac{1}{4}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)=

\displaystyle 0,

if
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Lemma 3.2

We define the high frequency function
f_{n}
and the low frequency functions
g_{n}
as follows

\displaystyle f_{n}=2^{-n}\phi(x)\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big),

\displaystyle g_{n}=\frac{12}{17}2^{-n}\phi(x),\quad n\gg 1.

Then for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n}\phi(0)\quad\text{and}\quad\|g_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n}\phi(0),

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{(\sigma-1)n}\phi(0)\quad\text{and}\quad\|g_{n}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{-(n+\sigma)}\phi(0),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq M_{1},

(3.12)

for some positive constants
C,M_{1}
.

Proof.
We refer to see Lemma 3.1 in
[
20
]
for the proof with minor modifications.

Proposition 3.1

Assume that
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim 1
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}-t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq Ct^{2}\mathbf{E}(u_{0}),

(3.13)

where we denote
\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0}):=\mathbf{P}(u_{0})-u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(u_{0})

\displaystyle:=1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\big).

Proof.
For simplicity, we denote
u(t)=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
.
By the Mean Value Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int^{t}_{0}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{C^{0,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq Ct\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(3.14)

where we have used the estimate

\|\mathbf{P}\big(u\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, (
3.5
) and (
3.3
) yield

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq Ct\big(\|u\|^{2}_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{x}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq Ct\big(1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\big).

(3.15)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|u\partial_{x}u\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq Ct\big(\|u\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\|u\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq Ct\big(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{3}_{\infty,1}}\big).

(3.16)

Using the Mean Value Theorem and Lemma
2.1
once again, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}-t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u-\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathbf{P}(u)-\mathbf{P}(u_{0})\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|u\partial_{x}u-u_{0}\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u(\tau)\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\ +\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\|u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad\ +\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int^{t}_{0}\|u(\tau)-u_{0}\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.17)

Plugging (
3.3
)–(
3.3
) into (
3.3
) yields the desired result (
3.13
). Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Now we prove the non-uniform continuous dependence.

We set
u^{n}_{0}=f_{n}+g_{n}
and compare the solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n})
. We obviously have

\displaystyle\|u^{n}_{0}-f_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}=\|g_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{-n},

which means that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{n}_{0}-f_{n}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}=0.

From Lemma
2.1
, one has

\displaystyle\|u^{n}_{0},f_{n}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{(\sigma-1)n}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|u^{n}_{0},f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-n},

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(u^{n}_{0})+\mathbf{E}(f_{n})\leq C.

Using the facts

\displaystyle\big\|u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}g_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\big\|u^{n}_{0}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\big\|g_{n}\big\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{-n},

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{P}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{P}(f_{n})\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\big\|g_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\big\|u^{n}_{0}+f_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\leq C2^{-n}.

we deduce that (for more details, see
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n})\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\geq

\displaystyle~t\big\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-t\big\|u^{n}_{0}\partial_{x}g_{n},\;\mathbf{P}(u^{n}_{0})-\mathbf{P}(f_{n})\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-Ct^{2}-C2^{-n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t\big\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}-Ct2^{-n}-Ct^{2}-C2^{-n},

(3.18)

Notice that (
3.12
)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big\|g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\gtrsim M_{1},

then we deduce from (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n}+g_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n})\big\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}\gtrsim t\quad\text{for}\ t\ \text{small enough}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

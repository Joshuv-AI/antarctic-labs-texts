Coulomb-Nuclear Interference in Polarized pA Scattering
Boris Kopeliovich *, Michal Krelina, Irina Potashnikova

Abstract
We made the first attempt to understand the observed unusual t dependence of single-spin asymmetry observed in the HJET experiment at RHIC. Usually, the interaction of hadrons is presented as a long-range Coulomb interaction and a short-range strong interaction with Coulomb corrections. Such a division gives rise to a Coulomb phase of the hadronic term. Conversely, here we consider short-range hadronic interaction as a correction to the long-range electromagnetic term, i.e., we treat it as an absorptive correction. This significantly affects the Coulomb-nuclear interference, which is a source of single-spin azimuthal asymmetry at small angles.

Introduction

Elastic scattering is usually characterized by spin non-flip
f_{nf}
and spin-flip

f_{sf}
amplitudes, which determine
the differential elastic cross-sections and single-spin azimuthal asymmetry
A_{N}(t)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma_{el}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\left(|f_{nf}|^{2}+|f_{sf}|^{2}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle A_{N}\frac{d\sigma_{el}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi\,\textrm{Im}\left(f_{nf}f_{sf}^{*}\right),

(2)

where
t
is 4-momentum transfer squared. The latter contains interference of the helicity amplitudes, which makes polarization effects sensitive to the hadron interaction
dynamics
Bilenky:1964pm
;
buttimore
.

According to Equation (
2
), an important condition for azimuthal asymmetry is the existence of a phase shift between the spin amplitudes. However, to the best of our knowledge, the phases
of
f_{nf}
and
f_{sf}
at high energies are similar. For example, in the Regge pole model,
f^{h}_{sf}
and
f^{h}_{nf}

have the same phase given by the signature factor. To maximize
A_{N}(t)
, one should combine hadronic and electromagnetic amplitudes
kl
. While the former is predominantly imaginary, the latter is nearly real. Apparently, a sizable effect is expected at small momentum transfer squared
t
, where the Coulomb and hadronic amplitudes are of the same order. The
t
dependence of asymmetry in
pp
scattering is described in a simplified approximation
kl
by

A_{N}^{pp}(t)=A_{N}^{pp}(t_{p})\,\frac{4y^{3/2}}{3y^{2}+1},

(3)

where
y=-t/t_{p}^{pp}
and

\displaystyle t^{pp}_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{8\sqrt{3}\pi\alpha_{em}}{\sigma^{pp}_{tot}};

(4)

\displaystyle A_{N}^{pp}(t_{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{3t_{p}}}{4m_{p}}\,(\mu_{p}-1).

(5)

Here,
\alpha_{em}=1/137
is the fine structure constant;
\mu_{p}=2.79
is the proton magnetic moment. The asymmetry
A_{N}^{pp}(t)
reaches a maximum (
5
) of about 4–5%, at
t=t_{p}\approx 2\times 10^{-3}\,\mbox{GeV}^{2}
. For the sake of simplicity, we assume here, like in
kl
, a pure non-flip and imaginary hadronic amplitude, no Coulomb phase, etc. In what follows, we present most of these simplifications.

The CNI asymmetry Equation (
3
) predicted in
kl
was confirmed by measurements in
704
;
poblaguev
.
It is worth mentioning at least two important practical applications of the CNI effect.
First, it is a unique opportunity to measure the spin-flip component of the hadronic amplitude at high energies
kz89
;
kkp
.

Second, an important application of CNI-generated azimuthal asymmetry is the possibility to measure the polarization of the beam, in particular at RHIC. The usage of CNI as a polarimeter has been intensively studied in
Poblaguev:2022hqh
;
Poblaguev:2022xoa
;
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
;
Poblaguev:2019saw
;
Poblaguev:2019qwk
;
Buttimore:2013rez
;
Buttimore:2012bha
;
Buttimore:2011zz
;
Akchurin:2011zzb
;
Buttimore:2008zz
;
Buttimore:2007cj
;
Buttimore:2007zz
;
Buttimore:2001df
;
Bates:2000uw
.

Similar relations can be applied to proton–nucleus elastic scattering,

t^{pA}_{p}=K_{A}\,t^{pp}_{p},

(6)

with

K_{A}=\frac{Z\sigma_{tot}^{pp}}{\sigma_{tot}^{pA}}.

(7)

Correspondingly, the maximal value of
A_{N}
at
t=t^{pA}_{p}
reads,

A_{N}^{pA}(t^{pA}_{p})=\sqrt{K_{A}}\,A_{N}^{pp}(t_{p}).

(8)

A simple estimate
\sigma_{tot}^{pA}
\sim
A^{2/3}\sigma_{tot}^{pp}
leads to non-dramatic modification of the CNI asymmetry, with about a 20% increase in
A_{N}^{pA}(t^{pA}_{p})

even for heavy nuclei, e.g., gold.

The energy of
100\,\mbox{GeV}
in the Lab frame is not sufficiently high to suppress iso-vector Reggeons, which have quite a large (dominating) spin-flip component. This is why it is difficult to disentangle large Reggeon and small Pomeron spin-flip terms. On iso-scalar nuclei, e.g., carbon, copper, etc., iso-vector Reggeons are completely excluded, otherwise they are suppressed by a small factor
(A-2Z)/A
.

2
Born Approximation

The elastic
pA
amplitude is fully described by two helicity amplitudes
f_{nf,sf}
defined in
buttimore
, each having hadronic and electromagnetic parts,

f_{i}(q_{T})=f^{h}_{i}(q_{T})+f_{i}^{em}(q_{T})
, where for small-angle elastic scattering
t\equiv-q^{2}\approx-q_{T}^{2}
, they are comparable.

Hadronic Born amplitudes can be represented as

\left.f_{nf}^{pA(h)}(q_{T})\right|_{B}=\frac{\sigma_{tot}^{pA}}{4\pi}F_{A}^{h}(q_{T}^{2}),

(9)

\left.f_{sf}^{pA(h)}(q_{T})\right|_{B}=r_{5}^{pA}\frac{q_{T}}{m_{N}}\frac{\sigma_{tot}^{pA}}{4\pi}\textrm{Im}F_{A}^{h}(q_{T}^{2}),

(10)

where
r_{5}^{pA}
is a nuclear analog of
r_{5}^{pp}
defined in
buttimore

r_{5}^{pp}=\frac{m_{N}\,f_{sf}(q_{T})}{q_{T}\,{\rm Im}\,f_{nf}(q_{T})}.

(11)

For
pp
elastic scattering,
r_{5}^{pp}
was fitted to data in
kkp
;
poblaguev
.

The hadronic nuclear form factor

F_{A}^{h}(q_{T}^{2})
in Equations (
9
)–(
10
) can be evaluated within the Glauber approximation
Kopeliovich:2003tz
,

F_{A}^{h}(q_{T})=\frac{2i}{\sigma_{tot}^{pA}}\int d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2A}\sigma_{tot}^{pp}(1-i\rho_{pp}(s))T_{A}^{h}(b)\right)^{A}\right],

(12)

as well as the total nuclear cross-section,

\sigma_{tot}^{pA}=2\textrm{Im}\int d^{2}b\,i\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{2A}\sigma_{tot}^{pp}(s)(1-i\rho_{pp}(s))T_{A}^{h}(b)\right)^{A}\right].

(13)

Correspondingly, the Born electromagnetic amplitudes read

\left.f_{nf}^{pA(em)}(q_{T})\right|_{B}=-2Z\alpha_{em}\frac{1}{q_{T}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}F_{A}^{em}(q_{T}),

(14)

\left.f_{sf}^{pA(em)}(q_{T})\right|_{B}=-Z\alpha_{em}\frac{1}{m_{N}q_{T}}(\mu_{p}-1)F_{A}^{em}(q_{T}),

(15)

where the small fictitious photon mass
\lambda
is introduced to avoid infrared divergence.
The final results are checked for stability at
\lambda\to 0
.

The nuclear electromagnetic form factor in Equations (
14
) and (
15
) has the form

F_{A}^{em}(q_{T})=\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{2}be^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}T_{Z}(b),

(16)

The nuclear thickness functions are defined as

T_{A}(b)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,\rho(b,z),

(17)

where
\rho(b,z)
is the nuclear density distribution function, and
T_{Z}(b)=T_{A}(b)\,Z/A
.

Following
Kopeliovich:2003tz
;
Kopeliovich:2005us
, next we replace the nuclear thickness function with a more accurate effective thickness function convoluted with
NN
elastic amplitude

T_{A}(b)\Rightarrow T_{A}^{h}(b),

(18)

where

T_{A}^{h}(b)=\frac{2}{\sigma_{tot}^{hN}}\int d^{2}s\,\textrm{Re}\Gamma^{hN}(s)T_{A}(\vec{b}-\vec{s});

(19)

\textrm{Re}\Gamma^{hN}(s)=\frac{\sigma_{tot}^{hN}}{4\pi B_{hN}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2B_{hN}}\right),

(20)

where
B_{hN}
is the slope of the differential
hN
elastic cross-section.
This effective nuclear thickness function can be simplified to

\displaystyle T_{A}^{h}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sigma_{tot}^{hN}}\int d^{2}s\,\frac{\sigma_{tot}^{hN}}{4\pi B_{hN}}\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2B_{hN}}\right)T_{A}(\vec{b}-\vec{s})

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi B_{hN}}\int dsd\phi\,s\exp\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{2B_{hN}}\right)T_{A}(\sqrt{b^{2}+s^{2}-2bs\cos\phi}).

3
Hadronic vs. Electromagnetic Amplitudes

The long-range Coulomb forces affect the strong-interaction amplitude by generating a phase shift, known as the Coulomb phase
bethe
;
cahn
;
Kopeliovich:2000ez
. The interplay of Coulomb and hadronic interaction mechanisms is illustrated in Figure
1
, following the consideration of this problem in
Kopeliovich:2000ez
.

Figure 1:
Three types of interaction: pure electromagnetic (
a
),
pure strong interaction (
b
), and combined strong and electromagnetic interactions (
c
).

In
Kopeliovich:2000ez
the last two graphs, (N) and (CN) were combined and treated as a Coulomb-modified strong-interaction amplitude. The modification was approximated by giving an extra phase factor to the hadronic amplitude. In the literature, this factor is called the Coulomb phase
bethe
;
cahn
;
Kopeliovich:2000ez
.
Alternatively, one can combine (C) and (CN) and obtain a hadronic correction to the Coulomb amplitude. In what follows, we call this absorptive correction

Kopeliovich:1997bp
;
trueman
.

As usual, multiple interaction amplitudes, depicted in Figure
1
, are easily calculated in impact parameter representation, where the result is just a product of multiple amplitudes. Thus, we switch from
q_{T}
to
b
dependent amplitudes, and simultaneously, from the Born approximation to the eikonal optical model.

\displaystyle\gamma_{nf}^{pA(h)}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\int d^{2}q_{T}\,e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\,f_{nf}^{pA(h)}(q_{T})

(22)

\displaystyle\gamma_{sf}^{pA(h)}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\int d^{2}q_{T}\,e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\,f_{sf}^{pA(h)}(q_{T})

(23)

These hadronic eikonal phases were used in the Glauber model expressions (
12
) and (
13
).

Adding higher order terms to the Born Coulomb amplitudes (
22
) and (
23
), as is explained in
Kopeliovich:1997bp
, we arrive at the eikonal form for the electromagnetic amplitudes

f_{nf}^{C}(q_{T})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\left(1-e^{i\chi^{C}_{nf}(b)}\right),

(24)

f_{sf}^{C}(q_{T})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\chi_{C}^{sf}(b)e^{i\chi^{C}_{nf}(b)},

(25)

with Coulomb eikonal phases, given by the Born amplitudes in impact
parameter representation.

\displaystyle\chi^{C}_{nf}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2Z\alpha_{em}}{2\pi}\int d^{2}q_{T}\,e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\,\frac{F_{A}^{em}(q_{T})}{q_{T}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}

(26)

\displaystyle\chi^{C}_{sf}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-Z\alpha_{em}(\mu_{p}-1)}{2\pi m_{N}}\int d^{2}q_{T}\,e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\,\frac{F_{A}^{em}(q_{T})}{q_{T}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\frac{\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}{b}.

(27)

Combining the Coulomb (
C
) with Coulomb-nuclear (
CN
) mechanisms depicted in Figure
1
, one obtains the Coulomb terms, Equations (
24
) or (
25
), which acquire an absorption factor, given by the standard Glauber eikonal approximation
glauber
,

\displaystyle S(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\gamma_{nf}^{pA(h)}(b)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2A}\sigma_{tot}^{pp}(s)(1-i\rho_{pp}(s))T_{A}^{h}(b)\right)^{A}

(28)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\exp\left[-{1\over 2}\sigma_{tot}^{pp}(s)T_{A}^{h}(b)\right].

As usual, the complicated multi-loop integrations in momentum representation are essentially simplified to a multiplicative combination in impact parameters. This is why the correction
S(b)
of Equation (
28
) enters the final expressions as a factor.
Thus, the absorption-corrected amplitudes
\tilde{f}
take the form

\tilde{f}^{C}_{nf}(q_{T})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\left(1-e^{i\chi^{C}_{nf}(b)}\right)S(b),

(29)

\tilde{f}^{C}_{sf}(q_{T})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}}\chi_{C}^{sf}(b)e^{i\chi^{C}_{nf}(b)}S(b),

(30)

Eventually, we arrive at the full amplitudes.

f_{nf,sf}(q_{T})=\tilde{f}^{C}_{nf,sf}(q_{T})+f^{N}_{nf,sf}(q_{T}).

(31)

5
Conclusions

Concluding, we performed the first calculation of single-spin asymmetry in polarized proton–nucleus elastic scattering in the CNI region.
We achieved a reasonable agreement with the data, in spite of the rather complicated theoretical construction. The remarkable feature of the nuclear form factor (
12
) is a change in the sign of the imaginary part of the elastic hadronic non-flip amplitude corresponding to the first zero of the Bessel function
J_{1}(t)
. Since the non-flip hadronic amplitude significantly exceeds the spin-flip part, they become of the same order right before and after the former changes sign. The asymmetry
A_{N}(t)
Equation (
2
) reaches maximum at
f_{nf}=f_{sf}
, so
A_{N}(t)
should abruptly vary between positive and negative maxima in the vicinity of the Bessel zero.
Comparison with the data in Figures
4
and
5
confirms such behavior; indeed, the measured
A_{N}(t)
exposes two maxima with opposite signs with positions close to predicted.

However, the magnitude of these maxima is exaggerated in our calculations, so there is still room for improvements. Here, nuclear effects were evaluated within the Glauber approximation, which is subject to Gribov inelastic shadowing corrections
gribov
;
gribov-my
. Their calculation requires substantial modeling, including knowledge of the proton wave function, interaction mechanism, etc. This needs a detailed study, as was performed in
Kopeliovich:2005us
. We leave this issue for future development.

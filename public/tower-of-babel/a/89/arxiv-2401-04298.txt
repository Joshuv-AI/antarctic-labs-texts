Fibered Calabi-Yau threefolds with relative automorphisms of positive entropy and c 2 c_{2} -contractions Thanks: The author is partially supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid (S) 15H05738, JSPS Grant-in-Aid (B) 15H03611 and NCTS scholar program.
Keiji Oguiso

Abstract
. We show that an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold with a positive entropy automorphism preserving the fibration is unique up to isomorphisms as fibered varieties. We also give a fairly explicit structure theorem of an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold with a birational automorphism of ”positive entropy” preserving the fibration.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14J32, 14J50

1.
Introduction

There are a lot of interesting projective surface automorphisms of positive entropy (see e.g.
[
Mc16
]
,
[
Ue16
]
,
[
OY20
]
and references therein). Target surfaces are necessarily birational to either
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, K3 surfaces, abelian surfaces or Enriques surfaces by a result of Cantat (
[
Ca99
]
). In
[
Og14
]
, we discussed some difficulty to construct ”interesting” biregular automorphisms of positive entropy of a smooth projective vaeriety of dimension greater than or equal to three. There we considered that primitive automorphisms of positive entropy introduced by Zhang
[
Zh09
]
are interesting ones and gave such a Calabi-Yau threefold example and a rational threefold example found first by Truong and me
[
OT15
]
(see also
[
Og19
]
for further progress).

In this paper, we continue to discuss the same kind of problems but from a completely different view point. We study fibered Calabi-Yau threefolds with equivariant automorphisms of positive entropy, hence, non primitive ones. The aim of this paper is to point out that the existence of such an automorphism gives rather strong constraint of the target Calabi-Yau threefold; the existence of such an automorphism uniquely determines the fibered Calabi-Yau threefold when it is an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold (Theorem
1.2
) and gives fairly explict concrete structure of the fibered Calabi-Yau threefolds when they are elliptically fibered threefolds (Theorem
1.5
), with various examples related (Examples
1.3
,
2.11
). This is not the case for pluricanocical fibrations and maximally rational connected fibrations (Examples
1.3
and
1.7
).

Unless stated otherwise, we work over
{\mathbb{C}}
. In order to state our main results, we fix a few terminologies first.

We denote by
N^{1}(V)
the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank generated by the numerically equivalence classes of Cartier divisors on a normal projective variety
V
. Then
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
acts naturally on
N^{1}(V)
. We denote
N^{1}(V)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
(resp.
N^{1}(V)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
) by
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
(resp.
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
).

We call a surjective morphism
f:V\to B
from a normal projective variety
V
to a normal projective variety
B
with connected fibers a
fiber space
, a fibration, or a contraction. Two fiber spaces
f:V\to B
and
f^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
are isomorphic (resp. birationally isomorphic) if there are isomorphisms
\tau_{V}:V\to V^{\prime}
and
\tau_{B}:B\to B^{\prime}
(resp. birational maps
\tau_{V}:V\dasharrow V^{\prime}
and
\tau_{B}:B\dasharrow B^{\prime}
) such that
\tau_{B}\circ f=f^{\prime}\circ\tau_{V}
.

Let
f:V\to B
be a fiber space. We denote by
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
the group of biregular automorphisms of
V
and define its subgroups
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
and
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V/B)
by

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f):=\{g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)\,|\,f\circ g=g_{B}\circ f\,\,\exists g_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)\},

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V/B):=\{g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)\,|\,f\circ g=f\}\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f).

We define
{\rm Bir}\,(f)
and
{\rm Bir}\,(V/B)
by replacing
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
and
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
above by
{\rm Bir}\,(V)
and
{\rm Bir}\,(B)
, the groups of birational automorphisms of
V
and
B
. We call a group
G
virtually abelian if
G
has an abelian subgroup of finite index.

An automorphism
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
of a smooth projective variety
V
is
of positive entropy
if and only if the first dynamical degree
d_{1}(g)
of
g
, which is equal to the spectral radius of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)}
when
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
, is strictly great than
1
. Otherwise, we have
d_{1}(g)=1
and we say that
g
is of zero entropy. See the original paper
[
DS05
]
and a survey
[
Og14
]
and references therein, for the precise definition of entropy, dynamical degrees and their basic properties that we need in this paper.

We call a smooth projective threefold
X
a
Calabi-Yau threefold
if
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{X})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and
\pi_{1}(X^{{\rm an}})=\{1\}
, where
X^{{\rm an}}
is the underlying complex manifold of
X
. Let
f:X\to B
be a fibration from a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
. Then smooth fibers of
f
are either K3 surfaces or abelian surfaces and
B={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
if
\dim B=1
, while smooth fibers are elliptic curves and
B
is a rational surface with at most klt as its singularities if
\dim B=2
(
[
Og93
]
). We call
f
an abelian fibration if general fibers of
f
are abelian surfaces and an elliptic fibration if general fibers of
f
are elliptic curves. We do not assume the existence of rational section.

There are many Calabi-Yau threefolds (See e.g.
[
GHJ03
, Part II]
). Let us next recall a very special Calabi-Yau threefold
X_{3}
with an abelian fibration, which will play a curcial role in this paper:

Example 1.1
.

Let
E_{\omega}
be the elliptic curve of period
\omega:=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
and

\pi_{3}:X_{3}\to\overline{X}_{3}

be the blow up at the maximal ideals of the singular points of
\overline{X}_{3}
of the quotient variety

\overline{X}_{3}:=E_{\omega}^{3}/\langle\omega\cdot{\rm id}_{E_{\omega}^{3}}\rangle

of the product threefold
E_{\omega}^{3}
. Then
X_{3}
is a Calabi-Yau threefold (
[
Be83
]
). We regard
X_{3}
as an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold by the morphism

\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\xrightarrow{\pi_{3}}\overline{X}_{3}\xrightarrow{\overline{{\rm pr}}_{3}}E_{\omega}/\langle\omega\cdot{\rm id}_{E_{\omega}}\rangle={\mathbb{P}}^{1}

induced from the third projection
{\rm pr}_{3}:E_{\omega}^{3}\to E_{\omega}
and
\pi_{3}
. Note that
\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
has an automorphism
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\varphi_{3})
of positive entropy. For instance, the automorphism
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\varphi_{3})
induced from
g_{2}\times{\rm id}_{E_{\omega}}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}({\rm pr}_{3})
, where
g_{2}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E_{\omega}^{2})
is given by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\,\,,\,\,{\rm i.e.,}\,\,g_{2}(P,Q)=(P+Q,P)

is such an automorphism. Indeed, one has

d_{1}(g)=d_{1}(g_{2}\times{\rm id}_{E_{\omega}})=d_{1}(g_{2})=(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2})^{2}>1.

Moreover,
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E_{\omega}^{2})
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\varphi_{3})
via

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E_{\omega}^{2})\ni h\mapsto h\times{\rm id}_{E_{\omega}}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\pi_{3}),

and we have

{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(E_{\omega}^{2})\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\pi_{3}).

In particular,
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\varphi_{3})
is not a virtaully abelian group.

Our first main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to B
be an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold. Assume that there is
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
of positive entropy. Then
f:X\to B
is isomorphic to
\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
in Example
1.1
as fiber spaces. In particular, any
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
is of zero entropy unless
X\simeq X_{3}
.

It is surprizing that only the existence of a relative automorphism of positive entropy determines the target fibered Calabi-Yau threefold uniquely among all abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefolds. The following example may be interesting if one compares with Theorem
1.2
.

Example 1.3
.

(1)

Let us consider two relatively minimal rational elliptic surface
\varphi_{i}:S_{i}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(
i=1
,
2
) defined by the Weierstrass equations

y^{2}=x^{3}-(t^{6}-1)\,\,,\,\,y^{2}=x^{3}-(t^{6}-a)\,\,(a\not=0,1).

Then the fiber product

f:V:=S_{1}\times_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}S_{2}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

over the base space
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a Calabi-Yau threefold with topological Euler number
0
(
[
Sc88
]
,
[
GLW22
]
). By construction,
f
is an abelian fibration whose smooth fibers
V_{t}
are isomorphic to
E_{\omega}^{2}
as
\varphi_{3}
in Example
1.1
. Thus
g_{2}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V_{t})
in Example
1.1
for all smooth
V_{t}
might seem to be gathered together to be an element
\tilde{g}\in{\rm Bir}\,(V/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
.
However, this is not true!
Note that, since singular fibers
V_{s}
(
s^{6}=1
and
a
) of
f
contain no rigid rational curves,
V
has no flops over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(
[
Ka08
]
). Then if we would obtain
\tilde{g}\in{\rm Bir}\,(V/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
, then
\tilde{g}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\,(V/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
and
d_{1}(\tilde{g})>1
. Thus
V\simeq X_{3}
by Theorem
1.2
. However,
V
is of topological Euler number
0
, while
X_{3}
is of topological Euler number
72
so that they are not isomorphic.

(2)

\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
has 27
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of normal bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}
in the singular fibers and we can perform Atiyah flop of these 27
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
within the category of projective varieties and get a new abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold
\varphi_{3}^{\prime}:X_{3}^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(
[
Og96
, Proposition 2.4]
). Then
X_{3}^{\prime}
is birational to
X_{3}
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, but there is no element of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\varphi_{3}^{\prime})
of positive entropy. Indeed,
X_{3}^{\prime}
is not even homeomorphic to
X_{3}
by
[
LO09
, Theorem 3.1]
so that the result follows from Theorem
1.2
.

(3)

There are many explicit examples of abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefolds
f:V\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with
|{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})|=\infty
, which are not isomorphic to
\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. See e.g.
[
Bor91
]
,
[
Sc88
]
,
[
GLW22
]
. For such
f
, we have
d_{1}(h)=1
for every
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
again by Theorem
1.2
.

(4)

Let
f:V\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration of a projective hyperkähler manifold
X
. Then general fibers of
f
are abelian varieties of dimension
\dim X/2
and
\rho(B)=1
(
[
Ma99
]
). Then
g^{*}L=L
for every
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
. Here
L
is the pullback of the ample generator of
N^{1}(B)
. We have
q_{V}(L)=0
with respect to the Beauvill-Bogomolov form
q_{V}
and therefore
d_{1}(g)=1
and
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
is a vertually abelian group by
[
Og07
, Proposition 2.7]
. More strongly, by Amerik and Verbitsky
[
AV23
, Proposition 3.8 and its proof]
, the translation automorphisms of fibers in
{\rm Aut}\,(f)
form a finite index subgroup of
{\rm Aut}\,(f)
(cf. Example
1.1
).

(5)

Let
E
be an elliptic curve and
C
a smooth projective curve of genus
\geq 2
. Then
V:=E\times E\times C
is of Kodaira dimension
1
and the third projection
{\rm pr}_{3}:V\to C
is the pluri-canonical morphism of
V
, which is also an abelian fibration on
V
. The automorphism
g_{2}\times{\rm id}_{C}
of the same shape as in Example
1.1
is an element of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}({\rm pr}_{3})
and it is of positive entropy. However,
V=E\times E\times C
form
3g-2
dimensional family, and
V
is far from being unique.

(6)

There are infinitely many smooth projective rational surfaces
S
with automprphism
g
of positive entropy (
[
Mc07
]
,
[
BK09
]
for interesting explicit examples). Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
\geq 1
. Then the projection
{\rm pr}_{2}:S\times C\to C
is a maximal rationally connected fibration of
S\times C
with automorphism
(g,{\rm id}_{C})\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}({{\rm pr}_{2}})
of positive entropy. Again, such
S\times C
is far from being unique.

Question 1.4

Let

be an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold which is not isomorphic to

in Example

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)

We call an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold
f:X\to W
is
of Type
II_{0}
if
f
is a
c_{2}
-contraction
, that is,
(c_{2}(X).f^{*}H)_{X}=0
for an ample class
H\in N^{1}(W)

Our second main result is the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f:X\to B
be an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold. Assume that there is
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(f)
with
d_{1}(g)>1
and denote by
g_{B}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B)
the birational automorphism of
B
induced from
g
. Then:

(1)

There is an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
of Type
II_{0}
, which is birationally isomorphic to
f:X\to B
, such that
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B^{\prime})
with
d_{1}(g_{B^{\prime}})>1
for
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B^{\prime})
induced from
g_{B}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B)
.

(2)

Asuume further that
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
(and thus
g_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
). Then there are a birational morphism
\tau:B\to B^{\prime}
and
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B^{\prime})
with
d_{1}(g_{B^{\prime}})>1
for
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B^{\prime})
induced from
g_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
such that

\tau\circ f:X\to B\to B^{\prime}

is an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold of Type
II_{0}
with
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\tau\circ f)
.

In particular, in both cases,
f:X\to B
is isotrivial in the sense that any general fibers of
f
are isomorphic.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some facts used in the proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
.

2.1.
First dynamical degrees

Let
V
be a smooth projective variety and
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(V)
be a birational automorphism of
V
such that
g
is isomorphic in codimension one. Then we have
(f^{n})^{*}=(f^{*})^{n}
on
N^{1}(V)
and
f^{*}
preserves the movable cone
\overline{{\rm Mov}}\,(V)\subset N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. If in addition
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt},(V)
, then
f^{*}
also preserves the intersection product and the nef cone
{\rm Nef}\,(V)\subset N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. We frequently use the following well-known consequence of Perron-Frobenius-Birkhoff theorem (
[
Bi67
]
, see also
[
NZ09
, Appendix A]
,
[
Og14
]
), which claims that
the spectral radius of a linear invertible map
F
of a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
L
preserving a strictly convex closed cone
C\subset L
with non-empty interior, is a real eigenvalue of
F
with real eigenvector in
C
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V
be a smooth projective variety and
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(V)
be a birational automorphism of
V
such that
g
is isomorphic in codimension one. Then the first dynamical degree
d_{1}(g)
of
g
coincides with the spectral radius of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
and it is a real eigenvalue of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
. Moreover:

(1)

There is
v\in\overline{{\rm Mov}}\,(V)\setminus\{0\}
such that
g^{*}v=d_{1}(g)v
, that is, there is a real nef eigenvector
v\in\overline{{\rm Mov}}\,(V)
of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
with eigenvalue
d_{1}(g)
.

(2)

Assume further that
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
. Then there is
v\in{\rm Nef}\,(V)\setminus\{0\}
such that
g^{*}v=d_{1}(g)v
, that is, there is a real nef eigenvector
v\in{\rm Nef}\,(V)
of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
with eigenvalue
d_{1}(g)
.

We define the first dynamical degree
d_{1}(g)
of an automorphism
g
of a normal projective variety
V
as the spectral radius of
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.5
. The following comparison is well known and will be also used:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
W
be a normal projective variety of dimension
d
,
g\in{\rm Aut}\,(W)
,
\nu:\tilde{W}\to W
an equivariant projective resolution with respect to
g
and
\tilde{g}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\tilde{W})
the induced automorphism of
\tilde{W}
.
Then
d_{1}(\tilde{g})=d_{1}(g)
. Moreover, they coincide with the spectral radii of
\tilde{g}^{*}|_{N^{1}(\tilde{W})_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
and
g^{*}|_{N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{R}}}}
with an eigenvector in
{\rm Nef}\,(\tilde{W})
and in
{\rm Nef}\,(W)
respectively.

Proof.

Let
H
be a very ample Cartier class on
W
and
B=\nu^{*}H
, which is a nef and big class on
\tilde{W}
. Let
\tilde{H}
be a very ample divisor on
\tilde{W}
. Then there are positive integers
m
and
m^{\prime}
and effective classes
E
and
E^{\prime}
on
\tilde{W}
such that

\tilde{H}+E=mB\,\,,\,\,B+E^{\prime}=m^{\prime}\tilde{H}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,N^{1}(\tilde{W}).

Combining this with
((\tilde{g}^{n})^{*}B.B^{d-1})_{\tilde{W}}=((g^{n})^{*}H.H^{d-1})_{W}
, it readily follows that
d_{1}(\tilde{g})=d_{1}(g)
. The second statement follows from
[
NZ09
, Proposition A.2]
, as
(g^{n})^{*}=(g^{*})^{n}
for
g\in{\rm Aut}\,(W)
and therefore we may apply it for the action of
g^{*}
on
{\rm Nef}\,(W)\subset N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
with ample class
H
which is in the interior of
{\rm Nef}\,(W)
, and similarly for
\tilde{g}
.

The following proposition is also well known for the experts. Since this proposition is crucial in our proof of Theorem

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
V
and
W
be smooth projective
varieties such that
\dim V-\dim W=1
and
f:V\dasharrow W
be a dominant rational map. Let
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(V)
and
g_{W}\in{\rm Bir}\,(W)
such that
f\circ g=g_{W}\circ f
. Then
d_{1}(g)=d_{1}(g_{W})
.

Proof.

Let
d=\dim V
and
e=\dim W
. Then
d-e=1
. Let
p_{n}:V_{n}\rightarrow V
be a resolution of indeterminacy of
g^{n}
and
q_{n}:V_{n}\to V
be the induced morphism such that
g^{n}\circ p_{n}=q_{n}
. We define

((g^{n})^{*}D.E^{d-1})_{V}:=(q_{n}^{*}D.(p_{n}^{*}E)^{d-1})_{V_{n}}=(D.(q_{n})_{*}((p_{n}^{*}E)^{d-1}))_{V},

where

and

are line bundles on

. Then by
[

]
, we have by definition

d_{0}(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H^{d})_{V}^{\frac{1}{n}}=1\,\,,\,\,d_{1}(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}((g^{n})^{*}H.H^{d-1})_{V}^{\frac{1}{n}},

where

is a very ample line bundle on
V
.
In
[
DS05
]
, Dinh and Sibony show that the limit

exists, hence
d_{1}(g)\geq 1
, and
d_{1}(g)
does not depend on the choices of

and
p_{n}:V_{n}\to V
. Moreover,

is a birational invariant in the sense that if
\nu:V^{\prime}\dasharrow V
is a birational map from a smooth projective variety

to

, then

for

(
[
DS05
, Corollaire 7]
).
d_{1}(g_{W})
(

and

) is defined in the same way as

.

Let
\nu:V^{\prime}\to V
be a resolution of indeterminacy of

. Set
f^{\prime}:=f\circ\nu
and

. Then

is a surjective morphism and

satisfies

and

as remarked above.

So, in what follows, we may and will assume that

is a surjective morphism
(by replacing

and
g
by
f^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to W
and
g^{\prime}
).

Let
H_{W}
be a very ample line bundle on

. Then by
[
DN11
]
in the special case where
\dim X-\dim W=1
, we have the relative
k
-th dynamical degree (

and

) for

:

d_{0}(g|f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}(H.(f^{*}H_{W})^{d-1})_{V}^{\frac{1}{n}}=1\,\,,\,\,d_{1}(g|f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}((g^{n})^{*}H.(f^{*}H_{W})^{d-1})_{V}^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Then by the main result of
[
DN11
]
applied for

with

, we have

d_{1}(f)={\rm Max}\,(d_{0}(g_{W})d_{1}(g|f),d_{1}(g_{W})d_{0}(g|f)).

Here
d_{0}(g_{W})=1
and
d_{0}(g|f)=1
. We are going to show that

. Note that

((g^{n})^{*}H.(f^{*}H_{W})^{d-1})_{V}=(q_{n}^{*}H.(p_{n}^{*}f^{*}H_{W})^{d-1})_{V_{n}}=(H.(q_{n})_{*}((f\circ p_{n})^{*}H_{W})^{d-1}))_{V}.

Here, as an element of
N_{1}(V_{n})
(which is the abelian group consisting of the numerical equivalence classes of one cycles on

), the one cycle
((f\circ p_{n})^{*}H_{W})^{d-1}
is in the same class as
N\tilde{F}_{n}
, that is,

((f\circ p_{n})^{*}H_{W})^{d-1}=N\tilde{F}_{n}

in
N_{1}(V_{n})
, where

and
\tilde{F}_{n}
is a
general
fiber of
f\circ q_{n}
.

Here the precise definition of a general fiber
\tilde{F}_{n}
of

is as follows. We choose Zariski open dense subsets
U_{n}\subset W
and
U_{n}^{\prime}\subset W
such that
g_{W}^{n}|_{U_{n}}:U_{n}\to U_{n}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism,

(resp.

) is smooth over
U_{n}
(resp.
U_{n}^{\prime}
) and
f
is smooth over
U_{n}^{\prime}
. Such

and

exist, as we may first choose
U_{n}
and
U_{n}^{\prime}
so that

is an isomorphism. Then we may just shrink

suitably to get desired

and
U_{n}^{\prime}=g_{W}^{n}(U_{n})
by the generic smoothness for

and
f
. Then

\tilde{F}_{n}:=(f\circ p_{n})^{-1}(P_{n})=(f\circ q_{n})^{-1}(g_{W}^{n}(P_{n}))=q_{n}^{-1}\circ f^{-1}(g_{W}^{n}(P_{n}))

for

. Note that the class
\tilde{F}_{n}\in N_{1}(V_{n})
is independent of the choice of the point
P_{n}
in
U_{n}
as
f\circ p_{n}
is smooth (hence flat) over
U_{n}
. Since
q_{n}
is a birational morphism and
f
is smooth over
U_{n}
, it follows that

(q_{n})_{*}(((f\circ p_{n})^{*}H_{W})^{d-1})=(q_{n})_{*}(N\tilde{F}_{n})=(q_{n})_{*}(Nq_{n}^{-1}f^{-1}(g_{W}^{n}(P_{n})))=Nf^{-1}(g_{W}^{n}(P_{n}))=NF_{n}

in

for

. Note that, for each

, the number
(H.F_{n})_{V}
does not depend on the choice of

as
f
is smooth (hence flat) over

. On the other hand, for any positive integer

is Zariski dense in

. For this choice, we have

and thus

. Hence

does not depend on the choices of

and

. We denote this number by

as

Hence, by

, we obtain

d_{1}(f)={\rm Max}\,(d_{0}(g_{W})d_{1}(g|f),d_{1}(g_{W})d_{0}(g|f))={\rm Max}\,(1,d_{1}(g_{W}))=d_{1}(g_{W}),

as claimed.
∎

2.2.
The second Chern class of a Calabi-Yau threefold.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold. We regard the second Chern class
c_{2}(X)
as a linear form on
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}:=N^{1}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{\mathbb{R}}}
via intersection form
(c_{2}(X),*)_{X}
. The hyperplane
(c_{2}(X),*)_{X}=0
in
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is a rational hyperplane with respect to the integral structure
N^{1}(X)
. By a famous theorem due to Miyaoka-Yau (
[
Mi87
]
), together with the fact that
X
is not an étale quotient of an abelian threefold by
\pi_{1}(X^{{\rm an}})=\{1\}
, we have

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold. Then, the linear form

(c_{2}(X).*)_{X}:N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\,\,;\,\,x\mapsto(c_{2}(X).x)_{X}

is strictly positive on the ample cone
{\rm Amp}\,(X)
and non-negative on the nef cone
{\rm Nef}\,(X)
, which is the closure of
{\rm Amp}\,(X)
in
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

The following proposition is due Wilson
[
Wi97
]
. This proposition indicates some crucial roles of
c_{2}(X)
in the study of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
(See also
[
OP98
]
for other roles of
c_{2}(X)
):

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold such that
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
is an infinite group. Then there is a non-zero real nef class
x\in\partial{\rm Nef}\,(X)\setminus\{0\}
such that
(c_{2}(X).x)_{X}=0
.

Though we will not use this proposition in our proof of main theorems, this proposition indicates some reason why
c_{2}
-contractions appear in our study.

2.3.
c_{2}
-contraction on a Calabi-Yau threefold.

Let
f:X\to W
be a fibration on a Calabi-Yau threefold

.
We call

a
c_{2}
-contraction
if the linear form
(c_{2}(X).*)_{X}
is identically zero on
f^{*}N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. This condition is equivalent to the condition that
(c_{2}(X).f^{*}H)_{X}=0
for an ample divisor
H
on
W
by Theorem
2.4
. We call a
c_{2}
-contraction
f_{0}:X\to W_{0}

maximal
if for any
c_{2}
-contraction
f:X\to W
, there is
\nu_{0}:W_{0}\to W
such that
f=\nu_{0}\circ f_{0}
. For any
X
, we have a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction
f_{0}:X\to W_{0}
, possibly
\dim W_{0}=0
, and a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction is unique in the sense that if
f_{0}^{\prime}:X\to W_{0}^{\prime}
is also a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction, then there is an isomorphism
\tau_{0}:W_{0}\to W_{0}^{\prime}
such that
f_{0}^{\prime}=\tau_{0}\circ f_{0}
(
[
OS01
, Lemma-Definition 4.1]
).

The next proposition will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor

on

is a klt pair such that

is linearly equivalent to

has at most quotient singularities and

-factorial. Moreover,

of Type
II_{0}
if and only if

as a Weil divisor if and only if

is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
0
if and only if

is numerically equivalent to

.

Proof.

See
[
Og93
, Lemma 3.5]
and its proof. The last equivalence follows from the proof of
[
Og93
, Lemma 3.5]
. It also follows from the log abundance theorem for surfaces with klt singularities or a general result due to Gongyo
[

, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Let
f:X\to B={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be an abelian fibered Calabi-Yau threefold and assume that there is
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
such that

.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is
g_{1}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
such that
d_{1}(g_{1})>1
.

Proof.

Since
X
is of non-negative Kodaira dimension,
f
has at least three singular fibers by Viehweg-Zuo
[
VZ01
]
. Let
N\subset B={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be the set of critical values of

. Then the automorphism
g_{B}

of

induced from
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
acts on
N
. Then, for

, the induced automorphism
g^{n}|_{B}=g_{B}^{n}
acts on the set

as identity. Since
|N|\geq 3
and

, it follows that
g^{n}|_{B}={\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt}_{B}
. Hence
g^{n}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
. Moreover
d_{1}(g^{n})=d_{1}(g)^{n}>1
. Hence we may take
g_{1}=g^{n}
.
∎

By Lemma
3.1
, we may and will assume that there is
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
such that
d_{1}(g)>1
. Let
H
be a very ample smooth divisor on
X
and let
A
be a general smooth fiber of
f
. Then
A
is an abelian surface and we have
g|_{A}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A)
as
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X/{\mathbb{P}}^{1})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is a non-zero nef real divisor
D_{1}
on
X
such that
g^{*}D_{1}=d_{1}(g)D_{1}
in

. This

satisfies the following intersection properties:

(c_{2}(X).D_{1})_{X}=0\,\,,\,\,(D_{1}.A.H)_{X}>0.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, there is a non-zero nef real divisor
D_{1}
on
X
such that
g^{*}D_{1}=d_{1}(g)D_{1}
in
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then

(c_{2}(X).D_{1})_{X}=(g_{*}c_{2}(X).D_{1})_{X}=(c_{2}(X).g^{*}D_{1})_{X}=d_{1}(g)(D_{1}.c_{2}(X))_{X}.

Thus
(c_{2}(X).D_{1})_{X}=0

.

Consider the nef real divisor classes

and

on a smooth projective surface

, then
(D_{1}|_{H}.A|_{H})_{H}=0

. Then

are proportional in

and

are proportional in

and smooth). However, this is impossible as

and

are eigenvectors of

of different eigenvalues

. Thus

. Hence

as it is the intersection numbers of nef divisors.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

d_{1}(g^{-1})>1
and there is a non-zero nef real divisor

on

such that

in

. Since

, we obtain a non-zero real nef divisor

Set

N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. We call
x\in N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
big, if it is in the big cone
{\rm Big}\,(X)
, which is an open cone in
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
generated by the classes of big divisors (
[
Ka88
, Lemma 1.7]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

M
is a real nef and big class with
(c_{2}(X).M)_{X}=0
.

Proof.

Since
A
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are nef, so is
M
. Since

(c_{2}(X).A)_{X}=c_{2}(A)=0\,\,,\,\,(c_{2}(X).D_{1})_{X}=(c_{2}(X).D_{2})_{X}=0

we have
(c_{2}(X).M)_{X}=0
as well. Again, since
A
,
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are nef,

(M^{3})_{X}=((A+D_{1}+D_{2})^{3})_{X}\geq(A.D_{1}.D_{2})_{X}=(D_{1}|_{A}.D_{2}|_{A})_{A}.

Note that
D_{1}|_{A}
and
D_{2}|_{A}
are non-zero nef eigenvectors of
g^{*}|_{A}
on
N^{1}(A)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
of different eigenvalues
d_{1}(g|_{A})>1
and
d_{1}(g|_{A})^{-1}<1
by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
. Thus, the nef vectors
D_{1}|_{A}
and
D_{2}|_{A}
are not proportional and therefore
(D_{1}|_{A}.D_{2}|_{A})_{A}>0
by the Hodge index theorem on
A
. Hence
(M^{3})_{X}>0
. Thus
M
is big by
[
FM23
, Lemma 2.3]
, even if
M
is not rational.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

There is a nef and big Cartier divisor
D
on
X
such that
(c_{2}(X).D)_{X}=0
.

Proof.

Since
K_{X}=0
, by a result of Kawamata
[
Ka88
, Theorem 5.7]
,
{\rm Nef}\,(X)\cap{\rm Big}\,(X)
is a locally rational polyhedral cone in
{\rm Big}\,(X)
. Therefore, there are an open ball
U
centered at
M
in
{\rm Big}\,(X)
and a unique minimal dimensional face
F
of
{\rm Nef}\,(X)\cap{\rm Big}\,(X)
, containing
M
as its interior, such that the face
F
is the intersection of rational hyperplanes in
U
. In particular,
F\cap U\cap N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, where
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
, is dense in
F\cap U
. Since
(c_{2}(X).*)_{X}=0
is a rational hyperplane such that
(c_{2}(X).M)_{X}=0
and
(c_{2}(X).*)_{X}\geq 0
on
{\rm Nef}\,(X)
by Theorem
2.4
, it follows that
F\cap U
is contained in the hyperplane
(c_{2}(X).*)_{X}=0
by the minimality. Therefore, there is a rational divisor
D^{\prime}
whose class is in
F\cap U\cap N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Then
D^{\prime}
is a rational nef and big divisor with
(c_{2}(X).D^{\prime})_{X}=0
. Multiplying
D^{\prime}
by some positive integer, we obtain a desired divisor
D
.
∎

The next lemma completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 3.6
.

f:X\to B
is isomorphic to
\pi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as fiber spaces.

Proof.

Since
D
in Lemma
3.5
is semi-ample as
D=K_{X}+D
, the linear system
|mD|
with some large divisible
m
defines a birational contraction
\pi:X\to\overline{X}
with
mD=\nu^{*}H
for some ample divisor on
\overline{X}
. Then, we have

(c_{2}(X).\nu^{*}H)_{X}=(c_{2}(X).mD)_{X}=0.

Therefore
\nu:X\to\overline{X}
is a birational
c_{2}
-contraction. Thus by Theorem
2.9
,
\pi:X\to\overline{X}
is isomorphic to either
\pi_{3}:X_{3}\to\overline{X}_{3}
or
\pi_{7}:X_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}
under the notation in Theorem
2.9
. By the maximality in Theorem
2.9
(4), the morphism
\nu:X\to\overline{X}
factors through
f:X\to B
, as an abelian fibration is a
c_{2}
-contraction. Since
\overline{X}_{7}
admits no non-trivial fibration
[
OS01
, Lemma 4.2]
, it follows that
X
is isomorphic to
X_{3}
and
f:X\to B
is isomorphic to an abelian fibration on
X_{3}
. Again by
[
OS01
, Proposition 3.2 (2), see also Page 73]
and by the fact that any abelian fibration of
X_{3}
factors through
\pi_{3}:X_{3}\to\overline{X}_{3}
by the maximality, any abelian fibration on
X_{3}
is isomorphic to
\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as fibered varieties. Hence
f:X\to B
is isomorphic to
\varphi_{3}:X_{3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
as fibered varieties.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.5
. Let
f:X\to B
be an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold with
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(f)
such that
d_{1}(g)>1
. Since
X
is a smooth projective variety with nef canonical divisor (hence is minimal),
g
is isomorphic in codimension one (
[
Ka08
]
). Let
g_{B}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B)
be a birational automorphism of
B
induced from
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(f)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
birationally isomorphic to
f:X\to B
such that

g_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)

for
g_{W}\in{\rm Bir}\,(W)
induced from
g_{B}\in{\rm Bir}\,(W)
.
Moreover,
d_{1}(g_{W})>1
. If
g_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
, then
d_{1}(g_{B})>1
.

Proof.

Let
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
be a standard model of
f:X\to B
in the sense of Nakayama
[
Na02
, Definition A.2]
. Since
X
is smooth projective,
K_{X}
is trivial (hence nef) and
B
is also projective, we may perform the same construction as in
[
Na02
, Appendix A]
(in which everything is formulated in the category of analytic varieties with dimension
\leq 3
) just by running a usual minimal model program in the category of complex projective varieties of dimension
\leq 3
. Thus, in the definition of a standard model in
[
Na02
, Definition A.2]
, all locally projective morphisms are chosen to be projective morphisms, a standard model
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
exists in the category of projective varieties,
X^{\prime}
is a minimal projective variety birational to
X
and
W
is a normal projective surface, for our
f:X\to B
. Since
X^{\prime}
and
X
are both minimal and they are birational, any birational map
X\dasharrow X^{\prime}
is isomorphic in codimension one and is decomposed into finitely many flops (
[
Ka08
]
). Since
X
is a smooth Calabi-Yau threefold,
X^{\prime}
is then a smooth Calabi-Yau threefold, as flops of threefolds preserves the smoothness
[
Ko89
, Theorem 2.4]
, as well as triviality of the canonical divisor and the fundamental group (as a flop is a birational map which is isomorphic in codimension one).

Let
g^{\prime}\in{\rm Bir}\,(X^{\prime})
be the birational automorphism of
X^{\prime}
induced from
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(X)
. Since
X^{\prime}
is a Calabi-Yau threefold, for the same reason as above, birational maps

g^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\dasharrow X^{\prime}\,\,{\rm and}\,\,(g^{\prime})^{-1}:X^{\prime}\dasharrow X^{\prime}

are isomorphic in codimension one, that is, none of
g^{\prime}
and
(g^{\prime})^{-1}
contracts a divisor to a curve or a point. Since
f^{\prime}
is equidimensional, it follows that none of
g_{W}
and
g_{W}^{-1}
, the birational automorphisms of
W
induced from
g^{\prime}
and
(g^{\prime})^{-1}
, contracts a prime divisor to a point, either. Thus
g_{W}:W\dasharrow W
is also isomorphic in codimension one. Since
W
is a normal projective surface, it follows that
g_{W}:W\dasharrow W
is then an isomorphism by the Zariski main theorem. Hence
g_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
as claimed.

Let us show that
d_{1}(g_{W})>1
. Let
\nu:\tilde{W}\to W
be an equivariant resolution
with respect to
g_{W}
and
\tilde{g}_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\tilde{W})
the induced automorphism. Let
\tilde{f}:X\dasharrow\tilde{W}
be the dominant rational map induced from
f
and
\nu
. Then

d_{1}(g_{W})=d_{1}(\tilde{g}_{W})

by Proposition
2.2
. Since
\dim X-\dim W=1
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

d_{1}(\tilde{g}_{W})=d_{1}(g)>1.

Hence
d_{1}(g_{W})=d_{1}(g)>1
as claimed.

If
g_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B)
, then we may apply the same argument for an equivariant resolution
\tilde{B}\to B

with respect to
g_{B}
and
\tilde{g}_{B}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\tilde{B})
to obtain
d_{1}(g_{B})=d_{1}(g_{\tilde{B}})=d_{1}(g)>1
.

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

In what follows, let
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
be as in Proposition
4.1
when
g\in{\rm Bir}\,(f)\setminus{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
and let
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
be
f:X\to B
when
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
. By Proposition
4.1
, in both cases,
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
is an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold with
g^{\prime}\in{\rm Bir}\,(f^{\prime})
and
g_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
with
d_{1}(g_{W})>1
for
g_{W}\in{\rm Bir}\,(W)
induced from
g^{\prime}
.

By Proposition
2.6
applied for
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to W
, we have an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
-K_{W}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
\Delta
. We fix one of such
\Delta
. Let

-K_{W}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\Delta=P+N

be the Zariski decomposition of an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Delta
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
such that
P
is a nef
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor class and
N
is an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor class (See
[
Sa84
, Theorem 7.4, Corollary 7.5]
for the definition and basic properties of the Zariski decomposition. See also
[
Ba09
]
). Note that the Zariski decomposition for an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Delta
exists and it is also unique in the sense that if
\Delta=P^{\prime}+D^{\prime}
is another Zariski decomposition of
\Delta
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, then
P^{\prime}=P
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
D^{\prime}=D
not only as elements of
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
but also
as
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors
(See
[
Sa84
, proof of Corollary 7.5]
, which is based on the uniqueness in
[
Sa84
, Theorem 7.4]
). For this reason, we may and will identify the numerically equivalent class of
N
with the unique effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor representing the class
N
, and we may thus speak of
the irreducible decomposition of

N
:

N=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}N_{i},

where
N_{i}
are mutually different irreducible curves and
a_{i}
are positive rational numbers. Note that the intersection matrix
((N_{i}.N_{j})_{W})_{i,j}
is of negative definite unless
N=0
(
[
Sa84
, Corollary 7.5]
).

Following a request from the referee, we remark the next fact with proof:

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the notation above, if
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
, then
h^{*}N=N
as effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors (not only as elements of
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
).

Proof.

Note that in general
h^{*}\Delta\not=\Delta
as
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors. However, since the Weil divisor
h^{*}(-K_{W})
is linearly equivalent to
-K_{W}
and since
\Delta
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
-K_{W}
, it follows that
h^{*}\Delta
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
\Delta
. Thus
\Delta-h^{*}\Delta=0
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Note also that
h^{*}N
is effective as
N
is effective and
h^{*}|_{N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
preserves the intersection form as
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
. Therefore

\Delta=(h^{*}P+\Delta-h^{*}\Delta)+h^{*}N\,\,{\rm in}\,\,N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}

is also the Zariski decomposition of
\Delta
. Thus
h^{*}N=N
as
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors by the uniquness of the Zariski decomposition of an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Delta
as explained above. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the notation above,
P=0
and
-K_{W}=N
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

Proof.

Since
g_{W}^{*}(-K_{W})=-K_{W}
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, it follows that
g_{W}^{*}P=P
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
by Lemma
4.2
. On the other hand, by Theorem
2.1
, there is a non-zero nef
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor class
D_{W}
such that
g_{W}^{*}D_{W}=d_{1}(g_{W})D_{W}
.
Then

(P.D_{W})_{W}=(g_{W}^{*}P.g_{W}^{*}D_{W})_{W}=d_{1}(g_{W})(P.D_{W})_{W}.

Since
d_{1}(g_{W})>1
by Proposition
4.2
, it follows that
(P.D_{W})_{W}=0
. Then nef classes
P
and
D_{W}
has to be proportional by the Hodge index theorem. On the other hand, since

d_{1}(g_{W})\not=1\,\,,\,\,g_{W}^{*}D_{W}=d_{1}(g_{W})D_{W}\,\,,\,\,g_{W}^{*}P=P\,\,,\,\,D_{W}\not=0

in
N_{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, it follows that
P=0
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and therefore
-K_{W}=N
in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
∎

Since klt is an open condition with respect to the coefficients of the boundary divisor, the pair
(W,E:=(1+\epsilon)N)
with sufficiently small
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
is also klt and

K_{W}+E=\epsilon N=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon a_{i}N_{i}

in
N^{1}(W)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Let us run the minimal model program with respect to
K_{W}+E
(See eg.
[
KM98
, Theorem 3.47, Corollary 3.43 (1)]
for the minimal model program for normal projective surfaces with klt as singularities). Since the intersection matrix
((N_{i}.N_{j})_{W})_{i,j}
is of negative definite, the output is not a Mori fiber space but the birational contraction
\tau:W\to B^{\prime}
such that

\tau_{*}(\epsilon N)=0

and under
\tau
,

W\setminus{\rm Supp}\,N\simeq B^{\prime}\setminus T,

where
T:=\tau({\rm Supp}\,N)
. Note that
T
is a finite set of points by
\tau_{*}(\epsilon N)=0
.
In particular,
\tau_{*}(N)=0
and therefore

K_{B^{\prime}}=\tau_{*}K_{W}=\tau_{*}N=0

in
N^{1}(B^{\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, as
K_{B^{\prime}}=\tau_{*}K_{W}
as Weil divisors. The composition
\tau\circ f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is then an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold with numerically trivial
K_{B^{\prime}}
. Hence this is an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau threefold of Type
II_{0}
by Proposition
2.6
.

Here we note that
\tau\circ f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is nothing but
\tau\circ f:X\to B\to B^{\prime}
when
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(f)
.

The next lemma will then complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Lemma 4.4
.

g_{B^{\prime}}:=\tau\circ g_{W}\circ\tau^{-1}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B^{\prime})
and
d_{1}(g_{B^{\prime}})>1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,
(g_{W}^{\pm 1})^{*}(N)=N
as
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors. Thus
g_{W}^{\pm 1}({\rm Supp}N)={\rm Supp}N
. Hence
g_{B^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}
are isomorphisms on
B^{\prime}\setminus T
for
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Bir}\,(B^{\prime})
induced from
g_{W}\in{\rm Bir}\,(W)
. In particular,
g_{B^{\prime}}:B^{\prime}\dasharrow B^{\prime}
is isomorphic in codimension one. Since
B^{\prime}
is a normal projective surface, it follows that
g_{B^{\prime}}B^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
is an isomorphism, that is,
g_{B^{\prime}}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(B^{\prime})
, by the Zariski main theorem. We also have

d_{1}(g_{B^{\prime}})=d_{1}(g_{W})=d_{1}(g_{\tilde{W}})>1

by Propositions
2.2
and
4.1
. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

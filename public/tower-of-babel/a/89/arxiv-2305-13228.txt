On the descent conjecture for rational points and zero-cycles
Nguy e ^ ~ \tilde{\hat{\text{e}}} n M a ⋅ \d{\text{a}} nh Linh

Abstract
The descent method is one of the strategies allowing one to study the Brauer–Manin obstruction to the local–global principle and to weak approximation on varieties over number fields, by reducing the problem to “descent varieties”. Very recently, in his Park City lecture notes, Wittenberg formulated a “descent conjecture” for torsors under linear algebraic groups. The present article gives a proof of this conjecture in the case of connected groups, generalizing the toric case from the previous work of Harpaz–Wittenberg. As an application, we deduce directly from Sansuc’s work the theorem of Borovoi for homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups with connected stabilizers. We are also able to reduce the general case to the case of finite (étale) torsors. When the set of rational points is replaced by the Chow group of zero-cycles, an analogue of the above conjecture for arbitrary linear algebraic groups is proved.



Keywords.
Brauer–Manin obstruction, Galois cohomology, zero-cycles, descent theory.

11R34, 14G05, 14G12.

1.1
Brauer–Manin obstruction and the descent conjecture

Let

of the field

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{Br}(X)

[

]

\left<-,-\right>_{\operatorname{BM}}:X(k_{\Omega})\times\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\quad((x_{v})_{v},\alpha)\mapsto\sum_{v}\alpha(x_{v}),

)

where

We are also interested in the question of weak approximation, namely, the density of

Definition 1.1
(Campana–Kollár–Miyaoka–Mori).

A smooth variety

if, for every algebraically closed field

, two general points
x_{0}
,
x_{1}\in X(K)
can be connected by a
K

\mathbb{A}^{1}_{K}\dashrightarrow X\times_{k}K

Rational connectedness is a stable birational invariant. Examples of rationally connected varieties include (geometrically) unirational varieties. For example, in characteristic
0
, this is the case for homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups (indeed, the celebrated theorem of Chevalley asserts that every such groups are geometrically rational, and even unirational over their field of definition
[
Che54
]
). An important conjecture concerning arithmetics of rationally connected varieties over number fields is the following.

Conjecture A
(Colliot-Thélène).

Every smooth rationally connected variety over a number field satisfies the properties
\mathbf{(BMHP)}
and
\mathbf{(BMWA)}
.

One of the reasons why Conjecture
A
is so strong is that its truth would imply that any finite group is a Galois group over
k
. This is the inverse Galois problem, long known to be open. The reader is invited to take a look at the recent notes
[
Wit23
]
of Wittenberg. One of the techniques to attack Conjecture

is the
descent theory
. Namely, let
f:Y\to X
be a
torsor
under a linear algebraic group
G
defined over a number field

is an fppf morphism between smooth

Y\times_{k}G\to Y
of

is

\sigma:\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)\to G(\bar{k})

[

, p. 22]

X(k)=\bigsqcup_{[\sigma]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G)}f^{\sigma}(Y^{\sigma}(k)).

The idea of the descent theory is to establish an equality of the form

X(k_{\Omega})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)}=\overline{\bigcup_{[\sigma]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G)}f^{\sigma}(Y^{\sigma}(k_{\Omega})^{{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y^{\sigma})}})}

in the topological space

\mathbf{(BMHP)}
(resp.
\mathbf{(BMWA)}

Conjecture B

With the above notations and assumptions, suppose in addition that

\mathbf{(BMHP)}
(resp.
\mathbf{(BMWA)}

\mathbf{(BMHP)}

Descent theory was originally developed in the foundational work

]
of Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc, and Swinnerton-Dyer. As noted by Skorbogatov (see
[
Sko01
, §6.1]
), the torus

. The torsors under consideration (
i.e.
, the twist of a given torsor) are the torsors of the same “type”. The notion of extended type, introduced later by Harari and Skorobogatov
[

]
, enabled descent theory (under a group of multiplicative type) for varieties with non-constant invertible functions. Other generalizations in this direction are found in the various works of Stoll
[

]
, Demarche
[
Dem09
]
, Skorobogatov
[
Sko09
]
, Poonen
[

]
, Wei
[

]
, and Cao–Demarche–Xu

]
…, where there are notably the notion of
étale Brauer–Manin obstruction

]
or a finite supersolvable group (scheme)

Theorem A
(Theorem

).

Let

. In particular, if the varieties

satisfy
\mathbf{(BMHP)}
(resp.
\mathbf{(BMWA)}
) for all
[\sigma]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(k,G)

The proof of Theorem

]
and Borovoi’s general machinery of

(Theorem

).

Let

What we shall show is in fact slightly stronger. Theorems

As an application, we give a new proof for Borovoi’s theorem, which states that the property

1.2
Analogue for zero-cycles

In §
4
, we consider the Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle and to weak approximation for
zero-cycles
. Let
X
be a smooth
proper
geometrically integral variety over a number field
k
. Let
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)
be the free abelian groups of zero-cycles on
X
, whose elements are (finite) formal sum
x=\sum_{P}n_{P}[P]
, where
P
runs through the closed points of
X
, and
n_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
degree
of such a cocycle is
\deg_{k}(x):=\sum_{P}n_{P}[k(P):k]
. For any
\alpha\in\operatorname{Br}(X)
and place
v
of
k
, we may evaluate
\alpha
at the closed points
P_{v}\in X_{k_{v}}
to obtain
\alpha(P_{v})\in\operatorname{Br}(k_{v}(P_{v}))\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. By linearity, we obtain a local pairing

\left<-,-\right>_{v}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{k_{v}})\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)
denote the quotient of
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)
by rational equivalence (see
e.g.

[
Ful84
, Chapter 1]
). Since
X
is proper, the above pairing factors through a pairing
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{k_{v}})\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. If
v
is an Archimedean place, this latter also factors through
\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{k_{v}}):=\operatorname{Coker}(\mathrm{N}_{\bar{k}_{v}/k_{v}})
, where
\mathrm{N}_{\bar{k}_{v}/k_{v}}:\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{\bar{k}_{v}})\to\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{k_{v}})

\left<-,-\right>_{\operatorname{BM}}:\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

where
\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X):=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{v})\times\prod_{v|\infty}\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v})

\left<-,-\right>_{\operatorname{BM}}
vanishes on the image of the localization (or restriction) map
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)\to\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)

\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)\to\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Br}(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

Since the group
\operatorname{Br}(X)
is torsion, we have a complex

\varprojlim_{n}(\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)/n)\to\varprojlim_{n}(\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)/n)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Br}(X),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}).

(Conjecture (E)).

The complex (

(Conjecture (E

Let

Let

The proof of Theorem

2

C\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}\widehat{C}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}[1]

(2.4)

In particular, taking hypercohomology yields

. The proof in the general case is similar; we shall deploy Sansuc’s argument, using the addivity theorem for

. More precisely, the projections

. By

,

Our aim is to relate the class in

Proposition 2.7
.

With the above notations, the isomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,C)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\widehat{C},\mathbb{G}_{m})

(i).

(for example,

(ii).

The torsor

Proof.

(i).

When

. By functoriality, it is enough to prove the claim when

is a torus. In this case,

provided by the relative version of Rosenlicht’s lemma, whose class in

turns out to be precisely
-[Y]
(see
[

(ii).

We follow Kottwitz’s argument in
[

, p. 369]
. Suppose that

is a surjective finite étale morphism trivializing

of

, and consider the sheaf

Lemma 3.1

If

Proof.

\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(V)/\operatorname{Br}(K)

\Sha^{1}_{\omega}(K,\widehat{C})

consisting of elements that become trivial after restriction to every procyclic subgroup of

\widehat{T}\cong\widehat{S}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}

0\to\widehat{S}\to\mathbb{Q}^{r}\to(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{r}\to 0

\Gamma_{K}

. It follows that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(K,\widehat{S})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{r})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\Gamma_{K},(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{r})

\Gamma_{K}

\Sha^{2}_{\omega}(K,\widehat{S})=\Sha^{1}_{\omega}(K,(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{r})=0

Viewing that

\operatorname{Br}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{Br}(V)\subseteq\operatorname{Br}(k(Y))
. Let

. From what has been established above,

). Since

\mathrm{H}^{1}(k(\xi),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(k(\eta),\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

, Theorem 3.7.5]

, which concludes the proof.

Following Harpaz and Wittenberg

Next, let us show that Conjecture

The “finite case” of Conjecture

is hence the most difficult one. The inductive argument used in the proofs of Theorems

[

]

[

. If we were able to prove Conjecture

(for torsors) in the finite abelian case, then we would obtain the same result for descent types thanks to

, Lemma 3.7]
. Using the same sort of dévissage argument, the descent conjecture for the descent types whose underlying

and

in the finite abelian case is currently out of reach. It should be noted that if

is a connected linear algebraic group over a number field

such that

and

satisfies
\mathbf{(BMHP)}
and
\mathbf{(BMWA)}
(see
[

).

Just like in the cyclic case, where Harpaz and Wittenberg applied the sophisticated theorem of Graber–Harris–Starr, a proof of the descent conjecture for
G
finite abelian should use the rational connected assumption in an essential way. As a matter of fact, this assumption is

, even when

. Indeed, in
[

, Theorem 2.10, Proposition 2.11]
, Harari and Skorobogatov constructed a double cover

is an Enriques surfaces

, and

is a Kummer surface, such that

). Worse, the work of Balestrieri–Berg–Manes–Park–Viray
[

gave an Enriques surface

over

4
The descent conjecture for zero-cycles

In this §, we prove Theorem
C
. For convenience, all of the subsequent results shall be formulated in the language of descent types. They can be easily converted into ones for torsors (dropping the quasi-projective assumption) using the same trick deployed in the proof of Theorem
3.15
.

4.1
Brauer–Manin obstruction for zero-cycles

When
X
is a variety over a field
k
, we may consider its group
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)
of zero-cycles, which is functorial contravariantly with respect to flat morphisms of relative dimension
0
, and covariantly with respect to any morphism. Its quotient
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)
by rational equivalence is the zero-dimensional Chow group, which is functorial covariantly with respect to proper morphisms, and contravariantly with respect to generically finite morphisms. The standard reference for the Chow groups is
[
Ful84
, Chapter 1]
. For any field extension (not necessarily finite)
K/k
, we have a restriction homomorphism
\operatorname{res}_{K/k}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{K})
, which preserves degree. If
[K:k]
is finite, we also have a corestriction homomorphism
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{K})\to\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)
, also called the
(K/k)
-norm
, which multiplies the degree by
[K:k]
. Both of these maps induce homomorphisms on Chow groups, and the composite
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}\circ\operatorname{res}_{K/k}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)
is the multiplication by
[K:k]
.

If
k
is a number field, put
\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X):=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})
. For
v\in\Omega_{k}
, denote by
\operatorname{loc}_{v}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})
the restriction map
\operatorname{res}_{k_{v}/k}
, and let
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}=((\operatorname{loc}_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}):\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)
. The
local norm

\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}:\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{K})\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)

(4.1)

is defined as follows. For each family
x_{\Omega}=(x_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{K}}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{K})
, we define
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}(x_{\Omega})\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)
to be the family whose
v
-component, for each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, is
(\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega}))_{v}:=\sum_{w\in\Omega_{K},\,w|v}\mathrm{N}_{K_{w}/k_{v}}(x_{w})
. One checks with ease that the construction (
4.1
) is functorial and compatible with the global norm
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}
. Similarly, we define the
local restriction

\operatorname{res}_{K/k}^{\Omega}:\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{K})

(4.2)

as follows. For each family
x_{\Omega}=(x_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in Z_{0,\Omega}(X)
and each place
w\in\Omega_{K}
lying over a place
v\in\Omega_{k}
, let
(\operatorname{res}_{K/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega}))_{w}:=\operatorname{res}_{K_{w}/k_{v}}(x_{v})
. Then, (
4.2
) is functorial and compatible with the global restriction
\operatorname{res}_{K/k}
. Furthermore,
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}\circ\operatorname{res}_{K/k}^{\Omega}
is the multiplication by
[K:k]
on
\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)
.

A complete exposition on the Brauer–Manin obstruction in the context of zero-cycles can be found in
[
CTS21
, §15.1]
. Let
X
be a smooth geometrically integral variety over a number field
k
. We have the local pairings

\left<-,-\right>_{v}:\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

defined for all
v\in\Omega_{k}
, which factors through a pairing
\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{v})\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
whenever
X
is proper. If
v\in\Omega_{k}^{\infty}
, this pairing vanishes on the image of the norm
\mathrm{N}_{\bar{k}_{v}/k_{v}}:\operatorname{CH}_{0}(\bar{X}_{v})\to\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{v})
. By restricting to the unramified elements of
\operatorname{Br}(X)
, we obtain from (
4.1
) a pairing

\left<-,-\right>_{\operatorname{BM}}:\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)\times\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\quad((x_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}},\alpha)\mapsto\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\left<x_{v},\alpha\right>_{v}.

(BM
{}_{\text{cyc}}
)

The pairing (
BM

cyc

) vanishes on the image of
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}
and on
\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Br}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}(X))
, thanks to the global reciprocity law. When
X
is proper, we let

\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v}):=\begin{cases}\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X_{v})&\text{if }v\in\Omega_{k}^{\mathrm{f}},\\
\operatorname{Coker}(\mathrm{N}_{\bar{k}_{v}/k_{v}})&\text{if }v\in\Omega_{k}^{\infty}.\end{cases}

Put
\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}:=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v})
. Then, the pairing (
BM

cyc

) induces a pairing

\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)\times\operatorname{Br}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},

which vanishes on the image of
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}
, hence a map
\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)\to\operatorname{Br}(X)^{D}
fitting in a complex

\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}}\operatorname{CH}_{0,\Omega}(X)\to\operatorname{Br}(X)^{D}.

(E
′
)

Applying the functor
A\mapsto\varprojlim_{n}(A/n)
to (

\operatorname{Br}(X)
is torsion) yields the complex (

) predicts that (

\operatorname{CH}_{0}(X)
and
\operatorname{Br}(X)
are so (see
[

[

[

Following Liang
[
Lia13
, §1.2]
, we consider the problem of weak approximation for zero-cycles. Let
X
be a (not necessarily proper) smooth geometrically integral variety defined over a number field
k
. Let
X^{c}
be a smooth compactification of
X
. For each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, we equip the group
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})
with the initial topology defined by the composites

\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})\subseteq\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v}^{c})\to\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v}^{c})\to\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v}^{c})/n,

for

, where
\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X_{v}^{c})/n
is equipped with the discrete topology. By birational invariance of the group
\operatorname{CH}_{0}
, this topology is independent of the choice of the smooth compactification

. If

is any finite extension and

are two points which are close to each other, then
[P]

are close in
\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})

[

\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X_{v})
with the product topology. Hence, two families
x_{\Omega}=(x_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}
and

are close to each other if for a large (but finite) subset

and a large integer

, the local zero-cycles
x_{v}
and
x^{\prime}_{v}
have the same image in
\operatorname{CH}^{\prime}_{0}(X^{c}_{k_{v}})/n
for every

.

In
[

]
, Balestrieri and Berg developed a formalism allowing one to study descent theory in the context of zero-cycles. In short, given a torsor

under an algebraic group
G
over
k
, we consider all finite extension
K/k
and all the twisted torsors

, where
[\sigma]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,G)
. Since the notion of descent type (introduced in paragraph

) classifies torsors up to twisting by a Galois cocycle, it is more convenient to translate the formalism of Balestrieri–Berg into one in this new language. Let
\lambda
be a descent type on

k
-kernel

. We fix an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of

, and let
\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}
be the set of pairs
(K,[Y])
, where

is a finite extension of

, and
[Y]
is the class of a torsor

of descent type
\mathbf{dtype}(Y)=\lambda_{K}:=\operatorname{res}_{K/k}(\lambda)\in\operatorname{DType}(X_{K},L_{K})
. Following
[
BB22
, Definition 2.2]
, we define the “recombining” map associated with

\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda}=\sum_{(K,[f:Y\to X_{K}])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{N}_{K/k}\circ f_{\ast}:\bigoplus_{(K,[Y])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{Z}_{0}(Y)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X).

(4.3)

On the local level, one defines

\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda,\Omega}=\sum_{(K,[f:Y\to X_{K}])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}\circ f_{\ast}:\bigoplus_{(K,[Y])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(Y)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X),

(4.4)

where
\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}:\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{K})\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)
is the local norm map (

\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)}
the subgroup of
\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)
consisting of the families orthogonal to
\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)
relative to the pairing (

) restricts to a homomorphism

\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda,\Omega}^{\operatorname{Br}}:\bigoplus_{(K,[Y])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(Y)^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y)}\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)}.

(4.5)

Here is the main result of this §.

Theorem 4.1
(Theorem

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety over a number field
k
. Let
\lambda=[\bar{Y},E]

, resp.
\Gamma_{k^{\prime}}
) is a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
\bar{G}
(resp.
\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{Y}/X)
, resp.
\Gamma_{k}
) in the profinite sense (see
[
Ser94
, Chapitre I, §1.4]
). The subgroup
E
is of index
n_{p}^{\prime}:=[\bar{G}:\bar{H}]
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{Y}/X_{k^{\prime}})
, hence corresponds to an étale cover
Z^{\prime}\to X_{k^{\prime}}
of degree
n_{p}^{\prime}
, over which
\bar{Y}
is Galois. The cover
Z^{\prime}\to X_{k^{\prime}}
is the base change to
k^{\prime}
of an étale cover
Z\to X_{\ell}
, for some finite extension
\ell
of
k
contained in
k^{\prime}
(hence of degree
n_{p}^{\prime\prime}:=[\ell:k]
prime to
p
). Recall from (
4.2
) that we have a local restriction map
\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}:\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X)\to\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{\ell})
. By functoriality of the pairing (
BM

cyc

), one has
\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega})\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{\ell})^{\operatorname{Br}(X^{c}_{\ell})}
.

By Nagata’s Theorem
[
Nag63
]
, we may extend the
Z\to X_{\ell}
to a morphism
\pi:Z^{c}\to X^{c}_{\ell}
, which is generically free of degree
n_{p}^{\prime}
, where
Z^{c}
is a smooth compactification of
Z
. Applying the refined Gysin pullback
\pi^{!}

[
Ful84
, Definition 8.1.2]
to the rational equivalence class of
\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega})
yields a family
z_{\Omega}=(z_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{\ell}}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(Z^{c})
such that
\pi_{\ast}z_{w}
is rationally equivalent to
n_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega}))_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega_{\ell}
. Since
\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega})\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(X_{\ell})^{\operatorname{Br}(X^{c}_{\ell})}
, we have
z_{\Omega}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(Z^{c})^{\operatorname{Br}(Z^{c})}
by (covariant) functoriality of the pairing (
BM

cyc

). We may assume that
z_{\Omega}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0,\Omega}(Z)^{\operatorname{Br}(Z^{c})}
by continuity. Now, since
\bar{Y}
is Galois over
Z
, it defines a finite descent type
\lambda_{Z}
on
Z
, with
\operatorname{Aut}(\bar{Y}/\bar{Z})=\bar{H}
. Furthermore, any
Z
-torsor of descent type
\lambda_{Z}
is an
X_{\ell}
-torsor of descent type
\lambda_{\ell}
and an
X
-torsor of descent type
\lambda
. Since
\bar{H}
is a
p
-group (
a fortiori
solvable), we have

z_{\Omega}\in\overline{\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(\mathrm{Z}_{0}(Z^{c}))+\bigcup_{(K,[g:Y\to Z_{K}])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda_{Z}}}\mathrm{N}_{K/\ell}^{\Omega}(g_{\ast}(Y(K_{\Omega})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y)}))}

by Lemma
4.6
. Applying
\pi_{\ast}
to both side, we obtain

n_{p}^{\prime}\operatorname{res}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}(x_{\Omega})\in\overline{\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c}_{\ell}))+\bigcup_{(K,[f:Y\to X_{K}])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda_{\ell}}}\mathrm{N}_{K/\ell}^{\Omega}(f_{\ast}(Y(K_{\Omega})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y)}))}.

Finally, applying the local norm
\mathrm{N}_{\ell/k}^{\Omega}
to both side, one gets

n_{p}x_{\Omega}\in\overline{\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c}))+\bigcup_{(K,[f:Y\to X_{K}])\in\mathscr{T}_{\lambda}}\mathrm{N}_{K/k}^{\Omega}(f_{\ast}(Y(K_{\Omega})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y)}))},

where
n_{p}:=n_{p}^{\prime}n_{p}^{\prime\prime}
is an integer prime to
p
.

Let us now show that
x_{\Omega}
lies in the closure of
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c}))+\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda,\Omega}^{\operatorname{Br}})
. Fix an integer
n\geq 1
and a finite set
S\subseteq\Omega_{k}
of places. For each prime
p|n
, we have seen that there are an integer
n_{p}
prime to
p
, a global zero-cycle
x^{p}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c})
, and a family
z_{\Omega}^{p}=(z_{v}^{p})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda,\Omega}^{\operatorname{Br}})
such that
n_{p}x_{v}
and
\operatorname{loc}_{v}(x^{p})+z^{p}_{v}
have the same image in
\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X^{c}_{v})/n
for all
v\in S
. The integers
n_{p}
, together with
n
, are mutually coprime, hence there are integers
d,d_{p}
such that
1=dn+\sum_{p|n}d_{p}n_{p}
. Let
x:=\sum_{p|n}d_{p}x^{p}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c})
and
z_{\Omega}:=(z_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}=\sum_{p|n}d_{p}z^{p}_{\Omega}\in\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{rec}_{\lambda,\Omega}^{\operatorname{Br}})
. Then

x_{v}=dnx_{v}^{p}+\sum_{p|n}d_{p}n_{p}x_{v}^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{loc}_{v}(x)+z_{v}

have the same image in
\operatorname{CH}_{0}^{\prime}(X^{c}_{v})/n
for all
v\in S
. This proves the proposition.

We can now combine Propositions
4.2
and
4.7
to obtain Theorem
4.1
(
i.e.
Theorem
C
).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
L^{\mathrm{f}}:=(\bar{G}^{\mathrm{f}},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}})
be the natural
k
-kernel (recall that
\bar{G}^{\mathrm{f}}=\pi_{0}(\bar{G})=\bar{G}/\bar{G}^{\circ}
), and let
\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}:=[\bar{Z}]\in\operatorname{DType}(X,L^{\mathrm{f}})
be the pushforward of
\lambda
along the morphism
L\to L^{\mathrm{f}}
induced by the projection
\bar{G}\to\bar{G}^{\mathrm{f}}
(hence
\bar{Z}=\bar{Y}/\bar{G}^{\circ}
,
cf.
Lemma
2.2

(i)
). For each
x_{\Omega}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X)^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(X)}
, applying Proposition
4.7
to
\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}
yields a global zero-cycle
x\in\mathrm{Z}_{0}(X^{c})
, a finite set
\{(k_{i},[g_{i}:Z_{i}\to X_{k_{i}}])\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\subseteq\mathscr{T}_{\lambda^{\mathrm{f}}}
, and a family
z_{\Omega}^{i}\in\mathrm{Z}_{0}(Z_{i})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Z_{i})}
for each
i\in\mathcal{I}
, such that
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(x)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathrm{N}_{k_{i}/k}^{\Omega}((g_{i})_{\ast}z_{\Omega}^{i})
is sufficiently close to
x_{\Omega}
.

For
i\in\mathcal{I}
, by Lemma
2.2

(ii)
, the torsor
Z_{i}\to X_{k_{i}}
gives rise to a
k_{i}
-kernel
L_{i}=(\bar{G}^{\circ},\kappa_{i})
and a descent type
\lambda_{i}=[\bar{Y},E_{i}]\in\operatorname{DType}(Z_{i},L_{i})
. By Proposition
4.2
, there exist a finite extension
K_{i}/k_{i}
, a torsor
h_{i}:Y_{i}\to(Z_{i})_{K_{i}}
of descent type
\operatorname{res}_{K_{i}/k_{i}}(\lambda_{i})
, and a family
P^{i}_{\Omega}\in Y_{i}((K_{i})_{\Omega})^{\operatorname{Br}_{\operatorname{nr}}(Y_{i})}
such that
\mathrm{N}_{K_{i}/k_{i}}^{\Omega}((h_{i})_{\ast}([P^{i}_{\Omega}])
is sufficiently close to
z_{\Omega}^{i}
. The composite
f_{i}=(g_{i})_{K_{i}}\circ h_{i}:Y_{i}\to X_{K_{i}}
is a torsor of descent type
\mathbf{dtype}(Y_{i})=\operatorname{res}_{K_{i}/k}(\lambda)
by Lemma
2.2

(iii)
, and
\operatorname{loc}_{\Omega}(x)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathrm{N}_{K_{i}/k}^{\Omega}((f_{i})_{\ast}[P^{i}_{\Omega}])
is sufficiently close to
x_{\Omega}
. The theorem is proved.

Theorem
4.1
is a generalization of the following result, proved in
[
HW20
, Théorème A]
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Conjecture
C
holds for the smooth compactifications of homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups over number fields.

Proof.

Let
X
a homogeneous space of a connected linear algebraic group
G
over a number field
k
. As in the proof of Corollary
3.13
, we have a descent type
\lambda=[\bar{G},E_{X}]
on
X
. For any finite extension
K/k
, a torsor
Y\to X_{K}
of type
\lambda_{K}
is a left
K
-torsor under
G_{K}
, hence satisfy
\mathbf{(BMHP)}
and
\mathbf{(BMWA)}
by
[
San81
, Corollaires 8.7 and 8.13]
and
[
Che89
]
. It follows from Liang’s theorems
[
Lia13
, Theorems A and B]
that Conjecture
C
holds for their smooth compactifications. By virtue of Theorem
4.1
, Conjecture
C
also holds for the smooth compactifications of
X
.

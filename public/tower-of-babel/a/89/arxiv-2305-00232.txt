New results for variational regularization with oversmoothing penalty term in Banach spaces
Bernd Hofmann, Chantal Klinkhammer, Robert Plato

Abstract
In this article on variational regularization for ill-posed nonlinear problems, we are once again discussing the consequences of an oversmoothing penalty term. This means in our model that the searched-for solution of the considered nonlinear operator equation does not belong to the domain of definition of the penalty functional. In the past years, such variational regularization has been investigated comprehensively in Hilbert scales, but rarely in a Banach space setting. Our present results try to establish a theoretical justification of oversmoothing regularization in Banach scales. This new study includes convergence rates results for a priori and a posteriori choices of the regularization parameter, both for Hölder-type smoothness and low order-type smoothness. An illustrative example is intended to indicate the specificity of occurring non-reflexive Banach spaces.



Keywords:

Nonlinear ill-posed problem, variational regularization, oversmoothing penalty, convergence rates results,
a priori parameter choice strategy, discrepancy principle, logarithmic source conditions

AMS subject classifications:
47J06, 65J20, 47A52

Introduction

The goal of this paper is the theoretical justification of variational regularization with oversmoothing penalties for nonlinear ill-posed problems in Banach scales. Precisely, we consider operator equations of the form

F(u)=f^{\dagger}\,,

(1)

where
F:\mathcal{X}\supset\mathcal{D}(F)\to\mathcal{Y}
is a nonlinear operator
between infinite-dimensional Banach spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
with norms
\|\cdot\|
.
We suppose that the right-hand side
f^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{Y}
is approximately given as
f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{Y}
satisfying the deterministic noise model

\|\hskip 1.13809ptf^{\delta}-f^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\delta,

(2)

with the noise level
\delta\geq 0
.
Throughout the paper, it is assumed that the considered equation (
1
) has a solution
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(F)
and is, at least at
u^{\dagger}
, locally ill-posed in the sense of
[
15
, Def. 1.1]
and
[
13
, Def. 3]
.
Consequently, a variant of regularization is required for finding stable approximations to the solution
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(F)
of equation (
1
), and we exploit in this context a variant of variational regularization with regularization parameter
\alpha>0
, where the regularized solutions
{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}
are minimizers of the extremal problem

T_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u):=\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(u)-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|^{r}+\alpha\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-\overline{u}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}^{r}\to\min\,,\quad\textup{subject to}\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(F),

(3)

with some exponent
r>0
being fixed. Here,
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}
is a norm on a densely defined subspace
\mathcal{X}_{1}
of
\mathcal{X}
, which is stronger than the original norm
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|
in
\mathcal{X}
.
Precisely, we define the stronger norm
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}
by

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}=\|\hskip 1.13809ptG^{-1}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|,u\in\mathcal{R}(G)
, where the generator

G:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
with range
\mathcal{R}(G)
is a bounded linear operator, which is one-to-one and has an unbounded inverse
G^{-1}
. Further conditions on the operator
G
are given in Section
2
below.
Moreover, the element
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}(F)
, occurring in the penalty term of the Tikhonov functional
T_{\alpha}^{\delta}
, denotes an initial guess.
Note that we restrict our consideration in this study to identical exponents
r
for the misfit term and the penalty functional in order to avoid unnecessary technical complications.

In the present work, we discuss the nonlinear Tikhonov-type regularization (
3
) with focus on an oversmoothing penalty term. This means in our model that we have
u^{\dagger}\not\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, or in other words
\|u^{\dagger}\|_{1}=+\infty,
which is an expression of
‘non-smoothness’ of the solution
u^{\dagger}
with respect to the reference Banach space
\mathcal{X}_{1}
. Variational regularization of the form (
3
) with
r=2
and oversmoothing penalty for nonlinear ill-posed operator equations (
1
) has been investigated comprehensively in the past four years in Hilbert scales, and we refer to
[
11
,
14
]
as well as further to the papers
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
17
]
. For related results on linear problems, see, e.g.,

[
21
]
.
Our present study continues and extends, along the lines of
[
14
]
, the investigations on nonlinear problems to Banach scales. This new study includes fundamental error estimates
yielding convergence and convergence rates results for a priori and a posteriori choices of the regularization parameter,
both for Hölder-type smoothness and low order smoothness.
The necessary tools for low order smoothness in the Banach space setting are provided.
In addition, a relaxed nonlinearity and smoothing condition on the operator
F

is considered that turns out to be useful for maximum norms.

Banach space results for the discrepancy principle in a pure equation form have already been proven for the oversmoothing case in the recent paper
[
3
]
. In parallel, such results have been developed for oversmoothing subcases to variants of
\ell^{1}
-regularization and sparsity promoting wavelet regularization in
[
19
, Sec. 5]
and
[
20
]
.

The outline of the remainder is as follows: in Section
2
we summarize prerequisites and assumptions for the main results in the sense of error estimates and convergence rates for the regularized solutions. On the one hand, error estimates for a priori choices of the regularization parameter are presented in Section
3
. On the other hand, Section
4
presents results and consequences for using a discrepancy principle. An illustrative example in Section
5
is intended to indicate the specificity of occurring non-reflexive Banach spaces. A numerical case study will be presented in Section
6
that illustrates the theoretical results.
Technical details, constructions and verifications for proving the main results of the paper are given in the concluding Section
7
.

2
Prerequisites and assumptions

In this section, we introduce a scale of Banach spaces generated by an operator of positive type. Moreover, we define the logarithm of a positive operator and formulate the basic assumptions for our study.
The concluding subsection is devoted to well-posedness and stability assertions for the variant of variational regularization under consideration in this paper.

2.1
Non-negative type operators, fractional powers, and regularization operators

Let
\mathcal{X}
with norm
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|
be a Banach space and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
with norm
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})}

\mathcal{X}
. Furthermore, let the injective operator
G\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
with range
\mathcal{R}(G)
and unbounded inverse

be of non-negative type, i.e.,

\displaystyle G+\beta I:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\ \textup{one-to-one and onto},\quad\|\hskip 1.13809pt(G+\beta I)^{-1}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\frac{\kappa_{*}}{\beta},\quad\beta>0,

(4)

for some finite constant
\kappa_{*}>0
.
Fractional powers of non-negative type operators may be defined as follows

[
1

2
]

(a)

For

G^{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by

\displaystyle G^{p}u:=\dfrac{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{-1.42262pt}{$\sin\pi p$}}}{\mbox{\footnotesize\raisebox{2.27621pt}{$\pi$}}}\mathop{\raisebox{-0.28453pt}{\mbox{\Large$\textstyle\int$}}}\nolimits_{\hskip-2.84526pt0}^{\infty}\hskip-0.56905pts^{p-1}(G+sI)^{-1}Gu\,ds\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in\mathcal{X}.

(5)

This defines a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{X}
.

(b)

For arbitrary values
p\geq 1
,
the bounded linear operator
G^{p}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by

G^{p}:=G^{p-\lfloor p\rfloor}G^{\lfloor p\rfloor}.

We moreover use the notation
G^{0}=I
.

In what follows, we shall need
the interpolation inequality for fractional powers of operators, see, e.g.,

[
18
]
or

[
7
, Proposition 6.6.4]

for each pair of real numbers
0<p<q
,
there exists some finite constant

such that

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptG^{p}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c\|\hskip 1.13809ptG^{q}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|^{p/q}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu\hskip 1.13809pt\|^{1-p/q}\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in\mathcal{X}.

(6)

For
0<p<1=q
, the value of the constant can be chosen as
c=2(\kappa_{*}+1)
, cf., e.g.,
[
24
, Corollary 1.1.19]
.
Under the stated assumptions on
G
, for each
p>0
, the fractional power

G^{p}
is one-to-one, and we use the notation

G^{-p}=(G^{p})^{-1}
.
We do not need that the operator
G
has dense range in
\mathcal{X}
.

The scale of normed spaces
\{\mathcal{X}_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}
,
generated by
G
, is given by the formulas

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\tau}=\mathcal{R}(G^{\tau})\ \textup{ for }\,\tau>0,\qquad\mathcal{X}_{\tau}=\mathcal{X}\ \textup{ for }\,\tau\leq 0,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\tau}:=\|G^{-\tau}u\|\ \textup{ for }\,\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ u\in\mathcal{X}_{\tau}.

(7)

For
\tau<0
, topological completion of the spaces

\mathcal{X}_{\tau}=\mathcal{X}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
is not needed in our setting.
We note that
(G^{p})_{p\geq 0}
defines a
C_{0}
-semigroup on

\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
,
which in particular means that
G^{p}u\to u
for
p\downarrow 0
is valid for any

u\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
(cf.
[
7
, Proposition 3.1.15]
). Finally, we note that

\mathcal{R}(G^{\tau_{2}})\subset\mathcal{R}(G^{\tau_{1}})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\infty.

(8)

2.2
The logarithm
\mathbf{\log G}

For the consideration of low order smoothness, we need to introduce the logarithm of

G
. For selfadjoint operators in Hilbert spaces this can be done by spectral analysis, and we refer in this context for example to
[
16
]
.
In Banach spaces,
\log G
may be defined as the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(G^{p})_{p\geq 0}
considered on
\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
:

\displaystyle(\log G)u

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{p}(G^{p}u-u),\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(\log G),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\log G)

\displaystyle=\{\,u\in\mathcal{X}:\lim_{p\downarrow 0}\tfrac{1}{p}(G^{p}u-u)\ \textup{exists}\,\},

cf., e.g.,
[
22
]
or
[
7
, Proposition 3.5.3]
.
Low order smoothness of an element
u\in\mathcal{X}
by definition then means

u\in\mathcal{D}(\log G)
. Note that
we obviously have
\mathcal{D}(\log G)\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
. In addition,
\mathcal{R}(G^{p})\subset\mathcal{D}(\log G)
is valid for arbitrarily small
p>0
,
which follows from
[
22
, Satz 1]
. Summarizing the above notes, we have a chain of subsets of
\mathcal{X}
as

\mathcal{R}(G^{p})\subset\mathcal{D}(\log G)\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}\quad\mbox{for all}\;p>0.

(9)

This means that also in the Banach space setting, any Hölder-type smoothness is stronger than low order smoothness.

2.3
Main assumptions

In the following assumption, we briefly summarize the structural properties of the space
\mathcal{X}
, of the operator
F
and of its domain
\mathcal{D}(F)
, in particular with respect to the solution
u^{\dagger}
of the operator equation. Moreover, we make one more assumption concerning
G
in addition to
the requirements on the operator
G
stated above.

Assumption 1
.

(a)

The infinite-dimensional Banach space
\mathcal{X}
has a separable pre-dual space
\mathcal{Z}
with
\mathcal{Z}^{*}=\mathcal{X}
such that a weak
∗
-convergence denoted as
\rightharpoonup^{*}
takes place in
\mathcal{X}
.

(b)

The operator
F:\mathcal{X}\supset\mathcal{D}(F)\to\mathcal{Y}
is weak
∗
-to-weak sequentially continuous, i.e., for elements
u_{n},u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(F)
, weak
∗
-convergence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}u_{0}
in
\mathcal{X}
implies weak convergence
F(u_{n})\rightharpoonup F(u_{0})
in
\mathcal{Y}
.

(c)

The domain of definition
\mathcal{D}(F)\subset\mathcal{X}
is a sequentially weak
∗
-closed subset of
\mathcal{X}
.

(d)

Let
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}(F)\cap\mathcal{X}_{1}\neq\varnothing
.

(e)

Let the solution
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(F)
to equation (
1
) with right-hand side
f^{\dagger}
be
an interior point of the domain
\mathcal{D}(F)
.

(f)

Let the data
f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{Y}
satisfy the noise model (
2
), and let the
initial guess
\overline{u}
satisfy
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}(F)
.

(g)

Let
a>0
, and let
0<c_{a}\leq C_{a}
and
c_{0},c_{1}>0

be finite constants such that the following holds:

\bullet

For each
u\in\mathcal{D}
satisfying
\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}\leq c_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(u)-f^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq C_{a}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}.

(10)

\bullet

For each
u\in\mathcal{D}
satisfying
\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(u)-f^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle c_{a}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(u)-f^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|.

(11)

(h)

The operator
G:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
defined above possesses a pre-adjoint operator
\tilde{G}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
such that
\tilde{G}^{*}=G
holds true.

Remark 2
.

From the inequality (
11
) of item
(g)
in Assumption
1
, we have for
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
that
u^{\dagger}
is the uniquely determined solution to equation (
1
) in the set
\mathcal{D}
. For
u^{\dagger}\notin\mathcal{X}_{1}
, there is no solution at all to (
1
) in
\mathcal{D}
. But in both cases, alternative solutions
u^{*}\notin\mathcal{X}_{1}
with
u^{*}\in\mathcal{D}(F)
and

cannot be excluded in general. Note that the concept of penalty-minimizing solutions, which is usual in theory of Tikhonov regularization, does not make sense in the
case of oversmoothing penalties.

2.4
Existence and stability of regularized solutions

The following two propositions on existence and stability can be immediately taken from
[
29
, § 4.1.1]
, and we refer in this context also to
[
10
]
and
[
28
, § 3.2]
.

Proposition 3

(well-posedness, cf. Prop. 4.1 of
[
29
]
)
.

For all
\alpha>0
and
f^{\delta}\in\mathcal{Y}
, there exists a regularized solution
{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in\mathcal{D}
, minimizing the Tikhonov functional
T_{\alpha}^{\delta}(u)
in (

) over all

.

Proposition 4

(stability, cf. Prop. 4.2 of
[
29
]
)
.

For all
\alpha>0
, the minimizers
{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in\mathcal{D}
of the extremal problem (
3
) are stable with respect to the data
f^{\delta}
. More precisely, for a data sequence
\{f_{n}\}
converging to

with respect to the norm topology in
\mathcal{Y}
,
i.e.
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f^{\delta}\|=0
, every associated sequence
\{u_{n}\}
of minimizers to the extremal problem

\|F(u)-f_{n}\|^{r}+\alpha\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-\overline{u}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}^{r}\to\min\,,\quad\textup{subject to}\quad u\in\mathcal{D}(F),

has a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
, which converges in the weak
∗
-topology of
\mathcal{X}
, and the weak
∗
-limit
\tilde{u}
of each such subsequence is a minimizer
{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}
of (
3
).

In order to prove the applicability of both propositions to our situation, we have to state that the relevant items of Assumptions 3.11 and 3.22 in
[
29
]
can be met as a consequence of our Assumption
1
by taking into account Remark 4.9 in
[
29
]
, where the transfer from the weak-situation to the weak
∗
-situation is explained. In particular, since

is bounded, we have
\|u\|_{1}\geq\tilde{c}\|u\|
with some constant
\tilde{c}>0
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
Then the penalty functional
\Omega:\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty]
of the Tikhonov functional

, defined as

\Omega(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|u-\overline{u}\|_{1}^{r}=\|G^{-1}(u-\overline{u})\|^{r}&\mbox{for}&u\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\\
+\infty&\mbox{for}&u\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{X}_{1}\end{array}\right.\,,

possesses the required stabilizing property (cf. item (c) of Assumption 3.22 in
[

]
) as a consequence of the sequential Banach–Alaoglu theorem, which implies that the sublevel sets

\{u\in\mathcal{X}:\Omega(u)\leq c\}\subset\{u\in\mathcal{X}:\|u-\overline{u}\|^{r}\leq\frac{c}{\tilde{c}^{r}}\}

are weak

sequentially compact in
\mathcal{X}
for all

.
Moreover,
\Omega
is sequentially weak
∗
lower semi-continuous (cf. item (b) of Assumption 3.22 in
[
29
]
), because the existence of a pre-adjoint operator
\tilde{G}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{Z}
to

in the sense of item

of Assumption

ensures that

is weak
∗
-to-weak
∗
sequentially continuous. This together with the weak
∗

yields the sequentially weak

.

3
Error estimate and a priori parameter choices

We start with an error estimate result that provides the basis for the analysis of the regularizing properties, including convergence rates under a priori parameter choices. In what follows, we use the notation

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{1}{r(a+1)}.

(12)

Theorem 5
.

Let Assumption
1
be satisfied. Then there exist finite positive constants
K_{1},\alpha_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
such that for
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
and
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
an error estimate for the regularized solutions as

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pt{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq f_{1}(\alpha)+K_{1}\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{\kappa a}}

(13)

holds, where
f_{1}(\alpha)
for
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
is some bounded function satisfying:

\bullet

(No explicit smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
, then
f_{1}(\alpha)\to 0
as
\alpha\to 0
.

\bullet

(Hölder smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X}_{p}
for some
0<p\leq 1
, then

f_{1}(\alpha)=\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\kappa p})
as
\alpha\to 0
.

\bullet

(Low order smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(\log G)
, then

f_{1}(\alpha)=\mathcal{O}((\log\frac{1}{\alpha})^{-1})
as
\alpha\to 0
.

Theorem

, the proof of which can be found in
[

]

of variational regularization with oversmoothing penalty and to obtain convergence and rates results for appropriate a priori parameter choices that
culminate in Theorem

.
For evaluating the strength of smoothness for the three different occurring situations in Theorem

(no explicit smoothness, Hölder smoothness and low order smoothness) we recall the chain (

The following theorem is a direct consequence of Theorem

, because its proof is immediately based on the error estimate (

Theorem 6
.

Let Assumption

be satisfied.

\bullet

(No explicit smoothness)
Let

4
Results for a discrepancy principle and consequences

For the specification of a suitable discrepancy principle,
the behaviour of the misfit functional
\alpha\mapsto\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|
needs to be understood, for
\delta>0
fixed.
The basic properties are summarized in the following proposition.
As a preparation, we introduce the following parameter:

\displaystyle e_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\textup{if }r\geq 1,\\
2^{-1+1/r}&\textup{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 7
.

Let Assumption
1
be satisfied. Then for
\delta>0
fixed, the function
\alpha\mapsto\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|
is non-decreasing, with

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq e_{r}\delta,\quad\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(\overline{u})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|.

In addition, we have
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\|\hskip 1.13809pt{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\overline{u}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=0
.

Proof.

This follows along the lines of the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 4.5]
.
Details are thus omitted here.
∎

Algorithm 8

(Discrepancy principle)
.

Let
b>e_{r}
and
c>1
be finite constants.

(a)

If
\|\hskip 1.13809ptF(\overline{u})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq b\delta
holds,
then choose
\alpha_{*}=\infty
, i.e.,
{u}_{\infty}^{\delta}:=\overline{u}\in\mathcal{D}
.

(b)

Otherwise, choose a finite parameter
\alpha=:\alpha_{*}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq b\delta\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\gamma_{\delta}}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\quad\textup{for some }\alpha_{*}\leq\gamma_{\delta}\leq c\alpha_{*},

(16)

where
c>1
denotes some finite constant.

Remark 9
.

(a)

Practically, a parameter
\alpha_{*}
satisfying condition (
16
) can be determined, e.g., by a sequential discrepancy principle.
For more details, see, e.g.,
Section
6
and
[
14
]
.

(b)

It follows from Proposition
7
that Algorithm

8
is feasible. We note that for
\delta>0
fixed, the function
\alpha\mapsto\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|
may be discontinuous. For this reason, we do not consider other versions of the discrepancy principle, e.g.,

\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=b\delta
or

b_{1}\delta\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptF({u}_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta})-f^{\delta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq b_{2}\delta
.

We next present the main result of this paper.

Theorem 10
.

Let Assumption
1
be satisfied, and let the regularization parameter
\alpha_{*}=\alpha(\delta,f^{\delta})
be chosen according to the discrepancy principle.

\bullet

(No explicit smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
, then
we have

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\to 0\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

\bullet

(Hölder smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X}_{p}
for some
0<p\leq 1
,
then
we have

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pt{u}_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{p/(p+a)})\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

\bullet

(Low order smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(\log G)
,
then

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pt{u}_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}((\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-1})\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

The proof of Theorem
10
is given in Section

7.5
.

5
An illustrative example

The following example with specific Banach spaces and nonlinear forward operator is intended to illustrate the assumptions stated above and to indicate the specificity of occurring non-reflexive Banach spaces.
This example should show that the general mathematical framework developed in this paper is applicable. The considered basis space is the non-reflexive and non-separable space
\mathcal{X}=L^{\infty}(0,1)

with the essential supremum norm
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}
possessing a separable pre-dual space
\mathcal{Z}=L^{1}(0,1)
. The generator
G
of the scale of Banach spaces is given by

\displaystyle[Gu](x)=\int_{0}^{x}u(\xi)\,d\xi\qquad(0\leq x\leq 1,\quad u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)).

(17)

Below we give some properties of
G
:

\bullet

The operator
G:L^{\infty}(0,1)\to L^{\infty}(0,1)
is of non-negative type with constant
\kappa_{*}=2
,
see, e.g.,
[
24
]
.

\bullet

G
is a compact operator, which possesses a compact pre-adjoint operator
\tilde{G}:L^{1}(0,1)\to L^{1}(0,1)
, which is characterized by

[\tilde{G}v](x)=\int_{x}^{1}v(\xi)\,d\xi\qquad(0\leq x\leq 1,\quad v\in L^{1}(0,1)).

\bullet

G
has a trivial nullspace and a non-dense range

\mathcal{R}(G)=W^{1,\infty}_{0}(0,1):=\{\,u\in W^{1,\infty}(0,1):u(0)=0\,\},

with

\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}=C_{0}[0,1]:=\{\,u\in C[0,1]:u(0)=0\,\}.

As a consequence of the last item we have that
\mathcal{X}_{1}=W^{1,\infty}_{0}(0,1)
with
\|u\|_{1}:=\|u^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
.

With
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{Y}=L^{\infty}(0,1)
, the nonlinear forward operator of this example is

F:L^{\infty}(0,1)\to L^{\infty}(0,1)
given by

\displaystyle[F(u)](x)=\exp((Gu)(x))\qquad(0\leq x\leq 1,\quad u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)).

(18)

This operator
F
is weak
∗
-to-weak sequentially continuous, because
F
is a composition
of the continuous outer nonlinear exponential operator and the inner linear integration operator
G
, both mapping in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
. The operator
G
transforms weak
∗
-convergent sequences in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
to norm-convergent sequences in this space, because
G
is compact and has a pre-adjoint operator (cf.
[
4
, Lemma 2.5]
).

Moreover, the operator
F
is Fréchet differentiable on its domain of definition
\mathcal{D}(F)=L^{\infty}(0,1)
, with

[F^{\prime}(u)]h=[F(u)]\cdot Gh
.
Now consider some function
u^{\dagger}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
which is assumed to be fixed throughout this section. We then have

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq Fu^{\dagger}\leq c_{2}\ \textup{ on }[0,1],\;\mbox{with}\;c_{1}:=\exp(-\|\hskip 1.13809ptGu^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty})>0,\ c_{2}:=\exp(\|\hskip 1.13809ptGu^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}),

so that

\displaystyle c_{1}|Gh|\leq|F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})h|\leq c_{2}|Gh|\quad\textup{on }[0,1]\qquad(h\in L^{\infty}(0,1)).

(19)

For any
u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
, we denote by
\Delta=\Delta(u)
and

\theta=\theta(u)
the following functions:

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle:=Fu-Fu^{\dagger}\in L^{\infty}(0,1),\qquad\theta:=G(u-u^{\dagger})\in L^{\infty}(0,1).

Thus,

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-1}=\|\hskip 1.13809pt\theta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}
, and we refer to (
7
) for the definition of

\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-1}
.

Below we show that the basic estimates (
10
) and
(
11
) are satisfied for that example with
a=1
. As a preparation, we note that

\displaystyle|\Delta-F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})(u-u^{\dagger})|\leq|\theta|\,|\Delta|\quad\textup{on }[0,1],

(20)

and refer in this context to
[
9
, Sect. 4.4]
. In this reference,
the same
F
is analyzed as an operator mapping in
L^{2}(0,1)
, where moreover
its relation to a parameter estimation problem
for an initial value problem of a first order ordinary differential equation
is outlined.

(a)

We first show that (
10
) holds. Even more general we show that it holds for any
u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
sufficiently close to
u^{\dagger}
, not only for
u\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
From (
19
) we have that

\displaystyle|\Delta-F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})(u-u^{\dagger})|\geq|\Delta|-|F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})(u-u^{\dagger})|\geq|\Delta|-c_{2}|\theta|\quad\textup{on }[0,1],

and (
20
) then implies the estimate

\displaystyle|\Delta|-c_{2}|\theta|\leq|\theta|\ |\Delta|\quad\textup{on }[0,1].

For any
u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
satisfying
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\theta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq\tau<1
, we thus have

|\Delta|\leq\tau|\Delta|+c_{2}|\theta|

and therefore

(1-\tau)|\Delta|\leq c_{2}|\theta|

on
[0,1]
.
This finally yields

\tfrac{1-\tau}{c_{2}}\|\hskip 1.13809pt\Delta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq\|\hskip 1.13809pt\theta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\quad\textup{for }\|\hskip 1.13809pt\theta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq\tau\qquad(0<\tau<1),

from which the first required nonlinearity condition (
10
) follows immediately.

(b)

We next show that (
11
) holds, in fact for any
u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
sufficiently close to
u^{\dagger}
.
From (
19
) we have

\displaystyle|\Delta-F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})(u-u^{\dagger})|\geq|F^{\prime}(u^{\dagger})(u-u^{\dagger})|-|\Delta|\geq c_{1}|\theta|-|\Delta|\quad\textup{on }[0,1],

and (
20
) then implies that

\displaystyle c_{1}|\theta|\leq|\Delta|+|\theta|\,|\Delta|\quad\textup{on }[0,1].

For any
0<\varepsilon<c_{1}
and
u\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
satisfying
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\Delta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}-\varepsilon
, we thus have

c_{1}|\theta|\leq|\Delta|+(c_{1}-\varepsilon)|\theta|

and therefore
\varepsilon|\theta|\leq|\Delta|

on
[0,1]
.
This provides us with the estimate
\varepsilon\|\hskip 1.13809pt\theta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq\|\hskip 1.13809pt\Delta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty},
which is valid for

\|\hskip 1.13809pt\Delta\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}-\varepsilon\;(0<\varepsilon<c_{1}).

This, however, yields directly the second required nonlinearity condition (
11
) and completes the list of requirements imposed by Assumption
1
.

In this section, we verify the main result of the paper. For this purpose, we return to the general setting considered in Section
2

G:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
denotes a bounded linear operator which is of non-negative type, one-to-one and has an unbounded inverse, where

7.1
Introduction of auxiliary elements

For the auxiliary elements introduced below, we consider linear bounded regularization operators associated with

,

\displaystyle R_{\beta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}\quad\text{for}\ \ \beta>0

(21)

\displaystyle S_{\beta}:=I-R_{\beta}G\quad\text{for}\ \ \beta>0.

(22)

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptR_{\beta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{c_{*}}{\beta}\quad\text{for}\ \ \beta>0,

(23)

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\beta}G^{p}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})}

(24)

\displaystyle R_{\beta}G

\displaystyle=GR_{\beta}\quad\text{for}\ \ \beta>0,

(25)

and

Example 11
.

Lemma 12
.

There exists some finite constant

7.2
Auxiliary results for
\log G

Lemma 13
.

For each
u\in\mathcal{D}(\log G)
and each
0\leq p<p_{0}
, we have

\|\hskip 1.13809ptS_{\beta}G^{p}u\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}(\beta^{p}(\log\tfrac{1}{\beta})^{-1})\quad\text{as}\ \ \beta\to 0.

Proof.

By
(G^{q})_{q\geq 0}
a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
is defined, and thus

\|\hskip 1.13809ptG^{q}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)})}\leq Ce^{\omega q}
for
q\geq 0
,
where
\omega>0
and
C>0
denote suitable constants, and
\|\hskip 1.13809pt\cdot\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{L}(\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)})}
denotes the norm of operators on
\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
.
Therefore, each real
\lambda>\omega
belongs to the resolvent set of the operator
\log G:\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}\supset\mathcal{D}(\log G)\to\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
, i.e.,

(\lambda I-\log G)^{-1}:\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}\to\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
exists and defines a bounded operator,
cf.
[
23
, Theorem 5.3, Chapter 1]
.
Since

\mathcal{R}((\lambda I-\log G)^{-1})=\mathcal{D}(\lambda I-\log G)=\mathcal{D}(\log G),

we can represent
u
as

u=(\lambda I-\log G)^{-1}w

with some
w\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
. From (cf.
[
23
, proof of Theorem 5.3, Chapter 1]
)

u=(\lambda I-\log G)^{-1}w=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda q}G^{q}w\,dq,

we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\beta}G^{p}u=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda q}S_{\beta}G^{p+q}w\,dq=y_{1}+y_{2},

with

\displaystyle y_{1}=\int_{0}^{p_{0}-p}e^{-\lambda q}S_{\beta}G^{p+q}w\,dq,\qquad y_{2}=\int_{p_{0}-p}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda q}S_{\beta}G^{p+q}w\,dq.

Below we provide suitable estimates for
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
.
The former term can be estimated as follows for
\beta<1
:

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pty_{1}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\int_{0}^{p_{0}-p}\beta^{p+q}\,dq=c_{1}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\beta^{p}\frac{1}{\log\beta}\beta^{q}\big|_{q=0}^{q=p_{0}-p}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\beta^{p}\frac{1}{|\log\beta|}(1-\beta^{p_{0}-p})\leq c_{1}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\beta^{p}\frac{1}{|\log\beta|},

where

denotes a finite constant.

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809pty_{2}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\|\hskip 1.13809ptw\hskip 1.13809pt\|\int_{p_{0}-p}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda q}\beta^{p_{0}}e^{\omega(q-(p_{0}-p))}\,dq

7.3
Some preparations for low order rates

In the analysis of low order rates, the functions

\displaystyle\varphi(t)

\displaystyle=(\log\tfrac{1}{t})^{-1},\quad 0<t<1,

(27)

\displaystyle\chi_{\pm 1,q}(t)

\displaystyle=t^{q}(\log\tfrac{1}{t})^{\mp 1},\quad 0<t<1\qquad(q>0),

(28)

will be needed. Below we state some elementary properties of those functions.
Note that

holds, so
(

) is a special case of (

).

(a)

For

the function

is monotonically increasing on the interval

, with

as

.

(b)

For

, the function
\chi_{-1,q}
is monotonically increasing on the interval
(0,t_{0}]
,
with

We have
\chi_{-1,q}(t)\to 0
as

.

(c)

For

(e)

, we have

,

\displaystyle\varphi(c_{2}t)\asymp\varphi(t),\quad 0<t\leq t_{2},

Here, for two positive, real-valued functions

Without loss of generality, in the following we may assume that
\alpha_{0}<1
considered in the beginning of the proof is chosen so small such that
the function

(\chi_{-1,\kappa}(\alpha))^{-1}=(\log\tfrac{1}{\alpha})^{-1}\alpha^{-\kappa}

is monotonically decreasing for
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
, cf. item
(b)
in Section
7.3
.
From (
42
), (
43
) and items
(b)
in Section
7.3
, we then obtain

\displaystyle(\log\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{*}})^{-1}\alpha_{*}^{-\kappa}\leq c_{3}(\delta\log{\tfrac{1}{\delta}})^{-1/a}\sigma,\quad\sigma:=(-\log({c_{2}(\delta\log{\delta})^{1/(\kappa a)}}))^{-1},

for some constant
c_{3}
.
From items
(d)
and
(e)
in Section

7.3
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma=\varphi(c_{2}\chi_{-1,1}(\delta)^{1/(\kappa a)})\leq c_{4}\varphi(\delta)=c_{4}(\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-1},

for some constant
c_{4}
.
The statement in the third item of the corollary now follows.
∎

We are now in a position to present a proof of the main result of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
10
.

We start with an elementary error estimate
\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

utilizing the auxiliaries,

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|+\|\hskip 1.13809pt\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|,

(44)

where
\beta_{\delta}
is given by
Lemma
16
.
The error of the auxiliaries
on the right-hand side of (
44
)
can be properly estimated using Lemma
16
.
Below we consider the term
\|\hskip 1.13809pt{u}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta}\hskip 1.13809pt\|
in more detail.
From the interpolation inequality (
6
),
it follows

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}^{1/(a+1)}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}^{a/(a+1)},

(45)

where
c_{1}
denotes some finite constant not depending on
\delta
.
The first term on the right-hand side of estimate (
45
)
can be estimated by using
Lemmas
16
and

17
.
Precisely, we find
the estimates

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}+\|\hskip 1.13809pt\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{-a}=\mathcal{O}(\delta)\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0,

so that estimate (
45
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\delta^{1/(a+1)}\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}^{a/(a+1)},

(46)

where
c_{2}
denotes some finite constant independent of
\delta
.
The last factor on the right-hand side of (
46
) is estimated next, and for this purpose, we make use of the following elementary estimate,

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}\leq\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\overline{u}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}+\|\hskip 1.13809pt\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}-\overline{u}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}.

(47)

We now proceed with the estimation of the right-hand side of (
47
)
by distinguishing our different smoothness assumptions.

(a)

For
u^{\dagger}\in\overline{\mathcal{R}(G)}
(no explicit smoothness),
from
estimate (
47
), Lemma
16
and Corollary
20

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}\leq o(\delta^{-1/a})+o(\delta^{-1/a})=o(\delta^{-1/a}),

and estimate (
46
)
then gives

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{2}\delta^{\tfrac{1}{a+1}}o(\delta^{-\tfrac{1}{a+1}})\to 0\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

This result in combination with estimate (
44
)
and Lemma
16

yields

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. This is the first statement of
Theorem
10
.

(b)

(Hölder smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{X}_{p}
for some
0<p\leq 1
, then
from
estimate (
47
), Lemma
16
and Corollary
20

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}\leq\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1-p}{p+a}})+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1-p}{p+a}})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1-p}{p+a}}),

and estimate (
46
)
then gives

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1-p}{p+a}\frac{a}{a+1}})=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{p}{p+a}})\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

This estimate combined with estimate (
44
)
and Lemma
16

yields

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{p}{p+a}})
as
\delta\to 0
. This is the second statement of
Theorem
10
.

(c)

(Low order smoothness)
If
u^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{D}(\log G)
, then
from
estimate (
47
), Lemma
16
and Corollary
20

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|_{1}=\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1}{a}}(\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-(1+\frac{1}{a})}),

and estimate (
46
)
then gives

\displaystyle\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-\widehat{u}_{\beta_{\delta}}\hskip 1.13809pt\|\leq c_{2}\delta^{\frac{1}{a+1}}\mathcal{O}(\delta^{-\frac{1}{a+1}}(\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-1})=\mathcal{O}((\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-1})\quad\text{as}\ \ \delta\to 0.

This estimate in combination with (
44
)
and Lemma
16

yields

\|\hskip 1.13809ptu_{\alpha_{*}}^{\delta}-u^{\dagger}\hskip 1.13809pt\|=\mathcal{O}((\log\tfrac{1}{\delta})^{-1})
as
\delta\to 0
. This is the third and final statement of
Theorem
10
.

∎

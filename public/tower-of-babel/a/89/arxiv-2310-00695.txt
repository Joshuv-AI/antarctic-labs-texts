New monotonicity and infinite divisibility properties for the Mittag-Leffler function and for the stable distributions
Nuha Altaymani, Wissem Jedidi

Abstract
​: Hyperbolic complete monotonicity property ( HCM \mathrm{HCM} ) is a way to check if a distribution is a generalized gamma ( GGC \mathrm{GGC} ), hence is infinitely divisible. In this work, we illustrate to which extent the Mittag-Leffler functions E α , α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ] E_{\alpha},\;\alpha\in(0,2] , enjoy the HCM \mathrm{HCM} property, and then intervene deeply in the probabilistic context. We prove that, for suitable α \alpha and complex numbers z z , the real and imaginary part of the functions x ↦ E α ​ ( z ​ x ) x\mapsto E_{\alpha}\big(zx\big) , are tightly linked to the stable distributions and to the generalized Cauchy kernel.

Introduction and first results

The Mittag-Leffler function

E_{a}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(1+ka)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\,a>0\,,

(1)

is a widely studied special function, see
main
and the references therein. The essence of this work is to unveil several probabilistic interpretation for the functions
E_{\alpha},\;\alpha\in(0,2]
. To this end we need some setting.

1.1
Bernstein functions and infinite divisibility

A function
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is
completely monotone
(we denote
f\in\mathcal{CM}
) if it is infinitely differentiable and it satisfies
(-1)^{n}f^{(n)}(x)\geq 0,\;\;\mbox{for all}\;n\in\mathds{N},\;x>0.

By Bernstein characterisation,
f\in\mathcal{CM}
if, and only if, it is the Laplace transform of some measure

f(\lambda):=\mathfrak{L}_{\tau}(\lambda)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda x}\,\tau(dx),\quad\lambda>0.

A subset of
\mathcal{CM}
is the class
\mathcal{S}_{a}
of
generalized Stieltjes transforms
of order
a>0
, viz. of functions
f
represented by

f(\lambda)=\mathrm{d}+\frac{\mathrm{q}}{\lambda^{a}}+\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{1}{(\lambda+x)^{a}}U(dx),\quad\lambda>0,

(2)

where
\mathrm{q},\mathrm{d}\geq 0
and
U
is a Radon measure on
(0,\infty)
such that
\int_{(0,\infty)}(1+u)^{-a}\;U(du)<\infty.
Stieltjes transforms of order 1, are usually called
Stieltjes functions
. The class
\mathcal{BF}
of
Bernstein functions
consists of functions of the form

\phi(\lambda)=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{d}\lambda+\int_{(0,\infty)}(1-e^{-\lambda x})\,\Pi(dx)=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{d}\lambda+\lambda\int_{(0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda x}\,\Pi(x,\infty)\;dx,\quad\lambda\geq 0,

(3)

where
\mathrm{q}\geq 0
is the so-called
killing rate
,
\mathrm{d}\in\mathds{R}
is the
drift
and
\Pi
is the
Lévy measure
of
\phi
, i.e. a positive measure on
(0,\infty)
which satisfies
\int_{(0,\infty)}\left(x\wedge 1\right)\Pi(dx)<\infty
. Two important subclasses of
\mathcal{BF}
are the ones of
Thorin Bernstein functions
and of
complete Bernstein functions
, denoted by
\mathcal{TBF}
and
\mathcal{CBF}
, respectively. A function
\phi
belongs to
\mathcal{TBF}
(respectively
\mathcal{CBF}
) if it is represented by

\phi(\lambda)=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{d}\,\lambda+\,\int_{(0,\infty)}\log\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{u}\right)\;U(du),\quad\big(\mbox{respectively}\;\;\phi(\lambda)=\mathrm{q}+\mathrm{d}\,\lambda+\,\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+u}\;V(du)\big),\quad\lambda\geq 0,

(4)

where the positive measures
U
and
V
satisfy

\int_{(0,1)}|\log u|\,U(du)+\int_{[1,\infty)}u^{-1}\;U(du)<\infty,\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\frac{V(du)}{u}\;\mbox{is a L\'{e}vy measure.}

(5)

Note the equivalences

\phi\in\mathcal{BF}\;\mbox{(respectively $\mathcal{TBF},\;\mathcal{CBF}$)}\Longleftrightarrow\phi\geq 0\;\mbox{and $\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{CM}$ (respectively $\mathcal{S}_{1},\;\mathcal{S}_{2}$)}.

(6)

The book of Hirschman & Widder
HW
is a good reference for the classes
\mathcal{S}_{a}
and, especially for
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we refer to
(
SSV
, Chapter 2)
. The books of Schilling, Song & Vondraçek
SSV
and the one of Steutel & van Harn
steutel
are our main references for the class
\mathcal{BF}
and its subclasses, and for
infinitely divisible measures
as well. The distribution of a nonnegative random variable
X
is said to be infinitely divisible, and we denote
X\sim\mathrm{I\,D}
, if there exists an i.i.d. sequence
(X_{i}^{n})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, such that
X\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}X_{1}^{n}+\ldots+X_{n}^{n}
. The celebrated
Lévy-Khintchine formula
gives the following characterization through the cumulant function of
X\geq 0
:

X\sim\mathrm{I\,D}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda X}]^{t}=\left(\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda x}\,\mathds{P}(X\in dx)\right)^{t}\in\mathcal{CM},\;\;\forall t>0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\phi_{X}(\lambda):=-\log\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda X}]\in\mathcal{BF}.

(7)

1.2
The
\mathrm{HCM}
property and
\mathrm{GGC}
distributions

The class of infinitely divisible distributions behind
\mathcal{TBF}
(respectively
\mathcal{CBF}
) is known as the
generalized gamma convolutions

\mathrm{GGC}
(respectively the
Bondesson class

\mathrm{BO}
), and it corresponds to the smallest class of sub-probability measures on
[0,\infty)
which contains mixtures of gamma (respectively exponential) distributions and which is closed under convolutions and vague limits. See
(
SSV
, Theorem 9.7 and Proposition 9.11)
. These classes were introduced by Olaf Thorin, and were widely developed by Lennart Bondesson in
B
, see also
SSV
;
steutel
. We will now introduce an important subclass of
\mathrm{GGC}
. A function
f:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
is said to be
hyperbolically completely monotone
, and we denote
f\in\mathcal{HCM}
if, for every
u>0
, the function

H_{u}(w):=f(uv)f(u/v),\quad\mbox{is completely monotone in the variable}\;w=v+1/v\geq 2,

(it is easy to see that
H_{u}
is always a function of
w
). In
(
B
, Theorem 5.3.1)
, it is shown that the class
\mathcal{HCM}
corresponds to pointwise limits of functions of the form

x\;\mapsto\;C\;x^{a-1}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(x+c_{i})^{-t_{i}},\quad N\in\mathds{N},\;C,\;\;c_{i},\;\;t_{i}>0,\;\;0<a<\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}.

(8)

Property
(
B
, iv) pp. 68)
asserts that

f\in\mathcal{HCM}\Longleftrightarrow x^{a}f(x^{b})\in\mathcal{HCM},\;\;\mbox{for all $\;a\in\mathds{R}$, and $|b|\leq 1$}.

(9)

By Bondessons’s definition
(
SSV
, Definition 9.10)
, and by
(
SSV
, Theorem 6.1.1)
, we have

X\sim\mathrm{GGC}\Longleftrightarrow\phi_{X}\in\mathcal{TBF}\Longleftrightarrow e^{-\phi_{X}}\in\mathcal{HCM}.

(10)

The class of
\mathcal{HCM}
functions being stable under multiplicative convolution (
(
B
, property vii) p.68)
), then the Laplace transform of an
\mathcal{HCM}
function is itself
\mathcal{HCM}
. If the p.d.f. of a continuous and positive r.v.
X
is
\mathcal{HCM}
, we denote
X\sim\mathrm{HCM}
. Clearly, we have the classification

\mathrm{HCM}\subset\mathrm{GGC}\subset\mathrm{BO},\;\;\mathrm{GGC}\subset\mathrm{SD}\subset\mathrm{I\,D},

where
\mathrm{SD}
denotes the well-known class of
self-decomposable distributions
, see
steutel
for this class.
Note that all previous classes of distributions are stable by additive convolution.
The importance of the classes
\mathrm{HCM}
and
\mathrm{GGC}
is due to their specific stability properties, one can find in
B1
: if
X
and
Y
are independent and
X,\;Y\sim\mathrm{GGC}
(respectively
\;\mathrm{HCM}
), then

X+Y,\;\;X\times Y\sim\mathrm{GGC}\;\;\mbox{(resp. $\;X^{q},\;q>1,\;\;\;X\times Y,\;\;X/Y\sim\mathrm{HCM}$).}

(11)

Note that the above stability properties are not shared by general distribution of the half real line. Besides, by (
7
), the
\mathrm{I\,D}
property reads only on the level of the cumulant function definition, whereas the
\mathrm{HCM}
one reads on the level of the probability density function, cf. the examples of next subsection.

1.3
Gamma and positive stable distributions

In what follows,
\mathbb{G}_{t}
denotes a standard gamma distributed random variable with shape with parameter
t>0
, i.e. with Laplace transform, Mellin transform and p.d.f.

\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda\mathbb{G}_{t}}]=\Bigl(\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\Bigr)^{t},\;\;\lambda\geq 0,\quad\mathds{E}[\mathbb{G}_{t}^{s}]=\frac{\Gamma(t+s)}{\Gamma(t)}\;\;s>-t,\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathbb{G}_{t}}(x)=\frac{x^{t-1}}{\Gamma(t)}e^{-x},\;\;x>0.

(12)

The last p.d.f. form and (
8
) ensure that all gamma distributions are
\mathrm{HCM}
. Another example of a
\mathrm{GGC}
distribution is given by the
positive stable
r.v.
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha},\;\alpha\in(0,1)
, which is associated to the Thorin Bernstein function

\phi_{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}(\lambda)=-\log\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}]=-\log\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}\;f_{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}(x)\;dx=\lambda^{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{x}\right)\;\frac{dx}{x^{1-\alpha}},\quad\lambda\geq 0.

(13)

Note that the p.d.f.
f_{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}
is not explicit except for
\alpha=1/2
;

\mathbb{S}_{1/2}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{1}{4\;\mathbb{G}_{1/2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi\,x^{3}}}e^{-1/4x},\;\;x>0,

(14)

nevertheless, the Mellin transform of
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}
is explicitly given by

\mathds{E}\left[\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{-s}\right]=\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{s}{\alpha})}{\Gamma(1+s)},\quad s>-\alpha,

(15)

which gives the convergence in distribution

\frac{1}{(\mathbb{S}_{\alpha})^{\alpha}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}1,\;\;\mbox{as}\;\alpha\to 1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{(\mathbb{S}_{\alpha})^{\alpha}}\mathrel{{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits}}{\mathbb{G}}_{1},\;\;\mbox{as}\;\alpha\to 0.

(16)

It is then natural to adopt the conventions
\mathbb{S}_{0}=0,\;\;\;\mathbb{S}_{1}
. Related literature for the stable distribution is referenced on Nolan’s website
Nolan
. The monograph of Zolotarev
zolo
is one of the major references for general stable distributions. By representation (
13
), and by the main result of
BS
, we know that

\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}\sim\mathrm{GGC},\;\;\mbox{for all $\alpha\in(0,1),\quad$ and}\quad\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}\sim\mathrm{HCM}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha\leq 1/2.

(17)

Additionally, in
JS
, it was shown that if
t>0
, then

\mathbb{S}^{-\frac{\alpha}{t}}\sim\mathrm{I\,D}\Longleftrightarrow t\leq 1-\alpha.

(18)

Using (
16
), and taking
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\prime}
an independent version of
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}
, let us define the quotient,

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\right)^{\alpha},\;\;\alpha\in(0,1),\quad\mathbb{T}_{1}=1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{T}_{0}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{{\mathbb{G}}_{1}}{{\mathbb{G}}_{1}^{\prime}},

(19)

where
{\mathbb{G}}_{1},\,{\mathbb{G}}_{1}^{\prime}
are i.i.d. and standard exponentially distributed. The p.d.f. of
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
is explicitly given by the generalized Cauchy form

f_{\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}}(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}\frac{1}{1+2\cos(\pi\alpha)x+x^{2}},\quad x>0,

(20)

see (
78
) below for instance. Bosch
bosch
completed (
18
) by showing

\alpha\in(0,1/2]\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;|t|\leq 1-\alpha\Longleftrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{1/t}=\left(\frac{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha/t}\sim\mathrm{HCM}.

(21)

With the above information, Bosch and Simon found it natural to raise the following open question in
BS
:

Is it true that
\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\alpha/t}\sim\mathrm{HCM}\;\;(\Longleftrightarrow\;\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{-\alpha/t}\sim\mathrm{HCM})
if, and only if,
\alpha\leq 1/2
and
|t|\leq 1-\alpha
?

(22)

This open question extends Bondesson’s conjecture
B
, which was stated in 1977. This conjecture, which is identical to (
22
) with
t=\alpha
, was investigated in
JS
and solved in
BS
.

1.4
First results on the Mittag-Leffler functions

From (
15
), one gets a first link of the Mittag-Leffler function with the positive stable distribution:

\mathds{E}\Big[e^{z/(\mathbb{S}_{\alpha})^{\alpha}}\Big]=E_{\alpha}(z),\quad\alpha\in(0,1),\;z\in\mathbb{C}.

(23)

By (
13
), it is clear that

\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{1/\alpha}}]=\mathds{E}[e^{-\lambda\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\prime}/\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}]=\mathds{E}[e^{-(\lambda/\mathbb{S}_{\alpha})^{\alpha}}]=E_{\alpha}(-\lambda^{\alpha}),\quad\lambda\geq 0\,.

(24)

In
JS
, if was shown that

0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\Longleftrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{1/\alpha}\sim\mathrm{HCM}\Longrightarrow E_{\alpha}(-\lambda^{\alpha})\in\mathcal{HCM}.

Note that the r.v.

\mathbb{R}_{\alpha}:=1+\mathbb{T}_{|\alpha-1|}^{1/\alpha},\;\;\alpha\in[0,2],

(25)

appeared in
(
lry
, (184) and (223))
, in a
\mathrm{GGC}
context without noticing its
\mathrm{GGC}
property: by (
21
), we have

\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1\Longleftrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{|\alpha-1|}^{1/\alpha}=\mathbb{R}_{\alpha}-1\sim\mathrm{HCM}\Longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\alpha}\sim\mathrm{GGC}.

(26)

Another monotonicity property of the Mittag-Leffler functions can be found in
BJH

0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}\Longleftrightarrow\lambda E_{\alpha}(-\lambda)\in\mathcal{BF}\Longleftrightarrow 1-\,\Gamma(1-\alpha)\;\lambda\;E_{\alpha}(-\lambda)\in\mathcal{CM},

and results in case
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
are also shown there. Note that the function
E_{\alpha}(-\lambda)
is completely monotone, at the contrary of
E_{\alpha}(\lambda)
. In
(
SIM3
, Theorem 1.2, (a) and (c))
, Simon showed that

\lambda\mapsto F_{\alpha}(\lambda):=\frac{e^{\lambda}-\alpha E_{\alpha}(\lambda^{\alpha})}{1-\alpha}\in\mathcal{CM},\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2].

(27)

The choice of the function
F_{\alpha}
is intuitive because
e^{\lambda}=E_{1}(\lambda)=\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\alpha E_{\alpha}(\lambda^{\alpha})
.

The main objective of this work is to exhibit new monotonicity properties of the
\mathrm{GGC}
and
\mathrm{HCM}
type for the Mittag-Leffler functions. To give a taste of our results, we start by improving Simon’s results (

2.1

properties, and we provided their explicit intervention in the distributional properties for the first passage time of the spectrally positive stable process. We also introduced new classes of
\mathrm{HCM}
distributions, and gave a possible direction to solve the open question (
22
) on the power of the positive stable r.v.’s. Indeed, (
68
) gives

\left(\frac{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}}{\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{\mathbb{G}_{t}}{(\mathbb{X}_{c,t})^{[-t]}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{\mathbb{G}_{t}}{\mathbb{G}_{t}^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}_{c,t}^{\;[t]}\sim\mathrm{HCM},\;\;\mbox{if}\;\alpha\in(0,1/2],\;c=\cos(\pi\alpha)\;\mbox{and }\;t=1-\alpha.

The latter indicates that two independent factorizations are feasible, namely,

\left(\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{t}}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathbb{G}_{t}\;\mathbb{Z}_{c,t},\quad\mathbb{D}_{c,t}^{\;[t]}\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{c,t}}{\mathbb{Z}_{c,t}^{\prime}},\;\;\;\mathbb{Z}_{c,t},\;\mathbb{Z}_{c,t}^{\prime}\;i.i.d.

In other terms the distribution of
\log\mathbb{D}_{c,t}^{\;[t]}
is symmetric. More investigation of the distribution of

\mathbb{D}_{c,t}^{\;[t]}
is then necessary to solve the open question (
22
).

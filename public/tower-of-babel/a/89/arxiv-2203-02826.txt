Introduction

In this paper, a (hyper)graph is maximum with respect to a property if it has the maximum number of (hyper)edges among the (hyper)graphs satisfying the given property. Throughout the paper, all logarithms are in base
e
.

One of the first results in extremal graph theory is Mantel’s Theorem
[
9
]
, which states that every triangle-free graph on
n
vertices has at most
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
edges. Additionally, the complete bipartite graph whose part sizes differ by at most one is the unique maximum triangle-free graph. Later, Turán
[
13
]
generalized Mantel’s Theorem for all complete graphs. Denote by
K_{s}
the complete graph on
s
vertices and by
T_{s}(n)
the complete
s
-partite graph on
n
vertices where the sizes of the parts differ by at most
1
. Turán’s Theorem states that
T_{s-1}(n)
is the unique maximum
K_{s}
-free graph on
n
vertices. Turán’s Theorem can also be understood as a property of
K_{n}
. Namely, it claims that every maximum
K_{s}
-free subgraph of
K_{n}
is
(s-1)
-partite.

Let
G(n,p)
be the standard binomial model of random graphs, where each edge in
K_{n}
is chosen independently with probability
p
. We say that an event occurs
with high probability (w.h.p.)
if its probability goes to
1
as
n
goes to infinity. A question related to Turán’s Theorem arises when
G(n,p)
replaces the role of
K_{n}
, that is, for what
p=p(n)
we have that w.h.p. every maximum
K_{s}
-free subgraph of
G(n,p)
is
(s-1)
-partite.

This question was first raised by Babai, Simonovits, and Spencer
[
1
]
, who gave an affirmative answer when
p=\frac{1}{2}
and
s=3
. Later, DeMarco and Kahn
[
6
]
determined the correct order of
p
for
s=3
. They
[
6
]
showed that if
p>K\sqrt{\log n/n}
for some large constant
K
, then w.h.p. every maximum triangle-free subgraph of
G(n,p)
is bipartite, while if
p=0.1\sqrt{\log n/n}
, then this does not hold w.h.p. Finally, DeMarco and Kahn
[
7
]
answered this question up to a constant factor for every
s\geqslant 3
.

-uniform hypergraph on

The random version of this theorem was first studied by Balogh, Butterfield, Hu, and Lenz
[
2
]
. Let
G^{3}(n,p)
be the random
3
-uniform hypergraph on vertex set
[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where each triple is included with probability

-free w.h.p. Therefore, the interesting case is when

, it was proved that if

for some large constant

F_{5}
-free subhypergraph of

is tripartite, and it was conjectured that it suffices to require only

for some large constant

. In this paper, we verify this conjecture. This is best possible up to the constant factor, as it was also shown in
[
2
]
that when

-free subhypergraph of

Theorem 1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that if
p>C\sqrt{\log n}/n
, then w.h.p. every maximum
F_{5}
-free subhypergraph of
G^{3}(n,p)
is tripartite.

Our approach will follow the general structure of the proof of the main result of
[
2
]
. Several key lemmas are improved and adapted for this smaller
p
.
In particular, in
[
2
]
, an easier version of codegree concentration was proved using Chernoff’s bound with the larger
p
. Here, for the smaller
p
, we need a stronger statement, not only using Chernoff’s bound (cf. Lemmas
6
and
8
).
As typical with the probabilistic method, one must fight to avoid applying the union bound when the concentration is not strong enough. This is the most challenging and technical part of the proof, see Remarks
7
and
19
for more details on that.
We trust that the new ideas used in the proof could be useful for other problems when one has to beat the union bound.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2

3
, we give the proof of Theorem

Appendix: Final computations

Recall that
g(p,s,r,i)=n\binom{n}{s}\binom{n^{2}}{r}\left(p\binom{s}{i}p^{i}\right)^{r}
and
O
is the set of
(s,r,i)
such that
g(p,s,r,i)=o(n^{-5})
given
p>C\sqrt{\log n}/n
. In this appendix, we give the proof for the claims made in the proof of Theorem
1
that certain
(s,r,i)
is in
O
.

Claim 23
.

For every positive integer
s\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{1}n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
,
r=\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s
, and
i=\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}
, we have
(s,r,i)\in O
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle g(p,s,r,i)

\displaystyle=n\binom{n}{s}\binom{n^{2}}{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}\left(p\binom{s}{\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}}p^{\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}

\displaystyle\leqslant n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{epn^{2}\sqrt{\log n}}{pns}\left(\frac{500eps\sqrt{\log n}}{pn\log\log n}\right)^{\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}

\displaystyle\leqslant n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{en\sqrt{\log n}}{s}\left(\frac{500es\sqrt{\log n}}{n\log\log n}\right)^{\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}.

(9)

There are three cases depending on
s
.

Case 1:

1\leqslant s\leqslant\sqrt{n}
.
In this case, (
9
) is at most

n(en)^{s}\left(en\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{\frac{C\log\log n}{500}}\right)^{Cs}\leqslant n(en)^{s}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{Cs}=o(n^{-5}).

Case 2:

\sqrt{n}<s<\frac{n}{\log n}
.
In this case, we have
\frac{n}{s}\geqslant\log n>\left(\frac{500e\sqrt{\log n}}{\log\log n}\right)^{2}
and then

\left(\frac{500es\sqrt{\log n}}{n\log\log n}\right)^{\frac{pn\log\log n}{500\sqrt{\log n}}}\leqslant\left(\frac{s}{n}\cdot\frac{500e\sqrt{\log n}}{\log\log n}\right)^{\log\log n}\leqslant\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\log\log n}\leqslant\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{100}.

Therefore, (
9
) is at most

\displaystyle n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{en\sqrt{\log n}}{s}\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{100}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}\leqslant n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}

\displaystyle\leqslant n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{Cs}\leqslant n\left(\frac{s}{n}\right)^{s}\leqslant n\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{\sqrt{n}}=o(n^{-5}).

Case 3:

\frac{n}{\log n}\leqslant s\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{1}n}{\sqrt{\log n}}
.
In this case, (
9
) is at most

\displaystyle n(e\log n)^{s}\left(e\log^{3/2}n\left(\frac{500e\varepsilon_{1}}{\log\log n}\right)^{\frac{C\log\log n}{500}}\right)^{\frac{pn}{\sqrt{\log n}}s}\leqslant

\displaystyle n(e\log n)^{s}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\log\log n}\right)^{\log\log n}\right)^{Cs}

\displaystyle\leqslant n\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leqslant n\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{\frac{n}{\log n}}=o(n^{-5}).\qed

Claim 24
.

For every positive integer
s\in\big(\frac{\varepsilon_{1}n}{\sqrt{\log n}},\,\varepsilon_{1}n\big]
,
r=\frac{pn}{500}s

\displaystyle=n\left(\frac{en}{s}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{500n}{s}\left(\frac{es}{3\varepsilon_{1}n}\right)^{3\varepsilon_{1}pn}\right)^{\frac{pn}{500}s}\leqslant n\left(\frac{e\sqrt{\log n}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)^{s}\left(\frac{500\sqrt{\log n}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\left(\frac{e}{3}\right)^{3\varepsilon_{1}C\sqrt{\log n}}\right)^{Cs\sqrt{\log n}}

A description of the integral depth- r r Bernstein center
Sarbartha Bhattacharya, Tsao-Hsien Chen

Abstract
In this paper we give a description of the depth- r r Bernstein center 𝒵 r ​ ( G ) \mathcal{Z}^{r}(G) for non-negative integers r r of a reductive simply connected group G G over a non-archimedean local field 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} as a limit of depth- r r standard parahoric Hecke algebras. Using the description, we construct maps from the algebra of stable functions on the r r -th Moy-Prasad filtration quotient of hyperspecial parahorics to 𝒵 r ​ ( G ) \mathcal{Z}^{r}(G) and use them to attach to each depth- r r irreducible representation π \pi of G ⁡ ( 𝕜 ) G(\mathbbm{k}) an invariant θ ⁡ ( π ) \theta(\pi) , called the depth- r r Deligne-Lusztig parameters of π \pi . We show that θ ⁡ ( π ) \theta(\pi) is equal to the semi-simple part of minimal K K -types of π \pi .

1.1
Main results

Let
G

\mathbbm{k}

Let

In this paper, we consider the case of

For any

\displaystyle\longmapsto h*\delta_{P_{r}^{+}}

1.2
Rational depths

In our later work
[
5
]
, we generalize the main results of the paper to general rational depths
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
.
In particular, we obtain a decomposition of the
category of smooth representations into a product of full subcategories indexed by restricted
depth-

A new ingredient in the rational depths case is the study of
invariant functions on finite graded Lie algebras (in the integral depths case we only need adjoint invariant functions on finite reductive groups or Lie algebras).

1.3
A conjecture on stability

We conclude the introduction with the following conjecture on stability.
Recall that
an element
z\in\mathcal{Z}(G)
in the Bernstein center is called stable if the associated
invariant distribution
\nu_{z}

on
G(\mathbbm{k})
is stable.
Let
\mathcal{Z}^{st}(G)\subset\mathcal{Z}(G)
be the subspace of stable elements
and
\mathcal{Z}^{st,r}(G)=\mathcal{Z}^{r}(G)\cap\mathcal{Z}^{st}(G)
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

We have
\mathrm{Im}(\xi^{r})\subset\mathcal{Z}^{st,r}(G)
.

Let

\mathrm{1}_{e}\in C^{st}(P_{r}/P_{r}^{+})
be the multiplicative unit.
The image
z^{r}=\xi^{r}(\mathrm{1}_{e})\subset\mathcal{Z}^{r}(G)

is the so-called depth-
r
Bernstein projector
and its stability was proved in

[
4
, Theorem 1.23]
.
This example provides an evidence of
Conjecture
1.1
.
It is work in progress to prove
Conjecture
1.1
in the
r=0
case using the geometric approach to the depth-zero stable center conjecture in
[
8
,
3
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Conjecture
1.1
is suggested by the conjectural theory of
L
-packets
and its relation to endoscopy of invariant distributions. Namely, it is expected that
an element
z\in\mathcal{Z}(G)
is stable if and only if the corresponding function
on the set of equivalence classes of irreducible representations of
G(\mathbbm{k})
is constant on
L
-packets and
the set of irreducible representations of the same depth-
r
Deligne-Lusztig parameter is a union of
L
-packets.

We give a brief description of some equivalent notions of the Bernstein center, mainly following
[
1
,
3
]
. Given

, each

Each

defines an endomorphism

of the

(gf)(x)=f(g^{-1}xg))

gives an isomorphism of

onto the algebra of essentially compact

-invariant distributions

.

Each smooth

representation is equivalently a non-degenerate
\mathcal{H}(G)
-module. Let
(l,\mathcal{H}(G))
and
(r,\mathcal{H}(G))
denote the

\mathcal{H}(G)
. The action on
G(\mathbbm{k})
by
G(\mathbbm{k})^{2}
, defined by

gives a

\mathcal{H}(G)

\mathcal{H}(G)^{2}

\mathcal{H}(G)

, where
\hat{\beta}(x)=\beta(x^{-1})
. Each
z\in\mathcal{Z}(G)
defines an endomorphism

of the smooth representation
(l,\mathcal{H}(G))
, and since the actions
l
and
r
commute, the endomorphism

of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G)

defines an algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{Z}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{End}_{\mathcal{H}(G)^{2}}(\mathcal{H}(G))
. In this paper, we have used this description of the Bernstein center to produce algebra isomorphisms onto the depth-
r
parts for each non-negative integer

, and finally a limit description of the entire center.

\displaystyle Av^{I_{w}w}(\delta_{P_{J}^{+}}\>*\>\delta_{w^{-1}Q_{n}^{+}w})

\delta_{P_{J^{\prime}}^{+}}\>*\>\delta_{w^{-1}Q_{n}^{+}w}=\delta_{P_{J}^{+}}\>*\>\delta_{w^{-1}Q_{n}^{+}w}

Let

Theorem 2.8

We have an algebra map

such that

. For

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{r}_{P}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto z_{\mathcal{H}}(\delta_{P_{r}^{+}})

is normal in

. So, there exists a map

.

is an algebra map. Given

Claim 3.11.2
.

\Psi^{r}
is injective.

Assume

. This is because

H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}^{F}}}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})_{\theta}=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}^{F}}}R^{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{T}}(\theta)

and hence we have

e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})=\bigoplus_{\theta\in S_{\mathrm{T},s}}R^{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{T}}(\theta)=H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}=e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}.

Now if
\mathrm{T}\subset\mathrm{B}
is
F
-stable, we know that

H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}})\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}},

(33)

where
e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=|\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}|^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}u
. Thus, as
\mathrm{G}^{F}
-module-
\mathrm{T}^{F}
, we get that

\left[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}\right]=\left[\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}\right]=\left[\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}\right]

(34)

since
e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}
is a central idempotent in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]
. Note that from the structure of
e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}
, we know that
e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}=e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=e^{\mathrm{B}}_{s}
(say), since
\mathrm{T}^{F}\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}=\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}T^{F}
. Further,
e^{\mathrm{B}}_{s}=\sum_{b\in\mathrm{B}^{F}}c_{b}b
for some
c_{b}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
.

Claim 4.2.1
.

e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}=e^{\mathrm{B}}_{s}
as operators from the right on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]
.

\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]=\bigoplus_{\theta\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}^{F}}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]_{\theta}.

For
\phi\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]
,
\phi e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=\sum_{\theta\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}^{F}}}\phi_{\theta}
, where
\phi_{\theta}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]_{\theta}
. Now,
e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}
acts as identity on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]_{\theta}
for
\theta\in S_{\mathrm{T},s}
and zero otherwise. So,
\phi e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}=\sum_{\theta\in S_{\mathrm{T},s}}\phi_{\theta}
. On the other hand,
\phi e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}\phi e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}
, and
e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}
from the left acts as identity on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}]_{\theta}
for
\theta\in S_{\mathrm{T},s}
and zero otherwise. Hence,
\phi e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}\phi e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}=\sum_{\theta\in S_{\mathrm{T},s}}\phi_{\theta}
, and we have proved our claim.

Note that, for
g\in\mathrm{G}^{F}

Tr\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]\Big|e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}g^{-1}\right)=\sum_{u\in\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}\sum_{\pi\in\varepsilon(\mathrm{G}^{F},(s))}\pi(1)\pi(gu)=\sum_{u\in\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}f_{s}(gu)

(35)

Thus, to prove our proposition, it is enough to show that the above is zero for
g\not\in\mathrm{B}^{F}
.Using claim
4.2.1
, we see that for
g\not\in\mathrm{B}^{F}

\displaystyle Tr\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]\Big|e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}g^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=Tr\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]\Big|e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}g^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=Tr\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]\Big|e^{\mathrm{B}}_{s}g^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathrm{B}^{F}}c_{b}\chi_{reg}(gb^{-1})=0

where
\chi_{reg}
is the character of the regular representation of
\mathrm{G}^{F}
. Hence, we are done in the case of Borel subgroups.

Observe that the main step was the fact that

\left[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}\right]=\left[\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}\right]=\left[\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{B}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}\right]

Let
(\mathrm{T},\theta)
corresponds to
s\in{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}^{F}
and
t\in{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}^{F}
. If
t\in(s)
, then we have
e^{\mathrm{T}}_{t}=e^{\mathrm{T}}_{s}
and

[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{U})e^{\mathrm{T}}_{t}]=[H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{U})e^{\mathrm{T}}_{t}]=[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{U})].

On the other hand, if
t\not\in(s)
,then we have

[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{U})e^{\mathrm{T}}_{t}]=0.

Note further that
S_{\mathrm{T},s}
is the set of all
\theta^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}^{F}}
such that
(\mathrm{T},\theta^{\prime})\leftrightarrow t\in{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}^{F}
for some
t\in(s)
.

Now, we move onto the case of general
\mathrm{P}\in par
. Let
\mathrm{P}\subset\mathrm{G}
be a parabolic subgroup (not necessarily
F
stable),
\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}\rtimes\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}
for
F
stable Levi subgroup
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}
containing an
F
-stable torus
\mathrm{T}
and unipotent radical
\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}=R_{u}(\mathrm{P})
. Note that the Deligne Lusztig varieties and induction is defined for
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}
, and we have a similar inuction functor
R^{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{L}}
(cf for example chapter 9 in
[
11
]
). Let
\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}}
denote the Deligne Lusztig variety for
\mathrm{P}
and
H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})
is now a
\mathrm{G}^{F}
-module-
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}
. Let
\hat{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{P}}
denote the dual of
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}
. Any semisimple class
(t)
in
\hat{\mathrm{L}}_{\mathrm{P}}
gives rise to a semisimple conjugacy class
(s)
in
\hat{\mathrm{G}}
, and the map
(t)\mapsto(s)
is finite to one. Let
e^{\mathrm{L}}_{t}
be defined similarly to
e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}
, since
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{P}}\supset\mathrm{T}
is a reductive group. Using the ideas in
[
9
]
section 2.1.4 and the fact that the Deligne-Lusztig induction is transitive we see that

[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})e^{\mathrm{L}}_{t}]=[H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})e^{\mathrm{L}}_{t}]

(36)

We also have
[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})e^{\mathrm{L}}_{t^{\prime}}]=0
if
t^{\prime}\not\in(s)
. So, if we define
e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}:=\sum_{(t)\mapsto(s)}e^{\mathrm{L}}_{t}
, we see that

[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}]=[H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}]=[e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})]

(37)

Coming back to our particular case when
\mathrm{P}
is
F
-stable, we again have

H^{*}_{c}(\tilde{X}_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}})\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}/\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}]=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}[\mathrm{G}^{F}]e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}

where everything is defined similarly to the Borel case. Defining
e^{\mathrm{P}}_{s}:=e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}=e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}
in the same way, and using ideas in claim
4.2.1
, we get

e^{\mathrm{G}}_{s}e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}=e_{\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}e^{\mathrm{L}}_{s}=e^{\mathrm{P}}_{s}

(38)

which was the main step in the proof of the Borel case. Hence, following the same steps as in that proof, we get that
\sum_{u\in\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{P}}^{F}}f_{s}(gu)=0
if
g\not\in\mathrm{P}^{F}
which finishes the proof.

\theta(\chi)\in(\hat{\mathrm{T}}//\mathrm{W})^{F}
to be the image of
\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}_{P}^{F})

along the map

\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{G}_{P}^{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}_{P}}}{{\to}}(\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{P}//\mathrm{W}_{P})^{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{P}}}{{\to}}(\hat{\mathrm{T}}//\mathrm{W})^{F},

here
\mathcal{L}_{P}
is the Deligne-Lusztig map for
\mathrm{G}_{P}^{F}
in (
29
).

(2) if
r>0
we define

\theta(\chi)\in(\mathfrak{t}_{r}//\mathrm{W})^{F,\circ}

to be the image of
\chi\in(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{*})^{F}

along the map

(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{*})^{F}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}\to(\mathfrak{t}_{P_{r}}//\mathrm{W}_{P})^{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{P_{r}}}}{{\to}}(\mathfrak{t}_{r}//\mathrm{W})^{F},

\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}

in (

Remark 5.10
.

The semi-simple part of minimal
K
-type defined here is same as the Deligne-Lusztig parameter attached to Moy-Prasad types in
[
6
]
(Check Section 4.1 for positive depths and 7.1 for depth zero), where it is treated in more generality.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
(\pi,V)
be a smooth irreducible representation of depth
r
and let
(P_{r},\chi)
be a depth-
r
minimal
K
-type contained in
V^{P^{+}_{r}}
.
We have
\theta(\pi)=\theta(\chi)
.
In particular,
we have
\theta(\pi)\in(\mathfrak{t}_{r}//\mathrm{W})^{F,\circ}
for
r>0
, and
the semi-simple part
\theta(\chi)
is independent of the choice of the minimal
K
-type contained in
V
.

Proof.

Pick
0\neq v\in V^{P^{+}_{r}}
such that
\pi^{P_{r}^{+}}(g)v=\chi(g)v
for
g\in P_{r}/P_{r}^{+}
.
Assume
r=0
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}[(\hat{\mathrm{T}}//\mathrm{W})^{F}]
, we have

z(\theta(\pi))v=\xi^{0}(f_{z})(v)=\mu(P^{+})\sum_{g\in\mathrm{G}_{P}^{F}}j_{P}(f_{z})(g)\chi(g)v

where

f_{z}\in C^{st}(\mathrm{G}^{F})
is the image of
z
under the isomorphism
C[(\hat{\mathrm{T}}//\mathrm{W})^{F}]\cong C^{st}(\mathrm{G}^{F})
in
Theorem
4.1
.
On the other hand, it follows from the defintion of
j_{P}
in (
45
) that

\sum_{g\in\mathrm{G}_{P}^{F}}j_{P}(f_{z})(g)\chi(g)v=\mu(P^{+})^{-1}t_{P}^{*}(z)(\mathcal{L}_{P}(\chi))v=\mu(P^{+})^{-1}z(\theta(\chi))v.

Thus we have

z(\theta(\pi))v=\mu(P^{+})\mu(P^{+})^{-1}z(\theta(\chi))v
for any
z\in C[(\hat{\mathrm{T}}//\mathrm{W})^{F}]
and it implies
\theta(\pi)=\theta(\chi)
.

Assume
r>0
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{t}_{r}//\mathrm{W})^{F}]
, we have

z(\theta(\pi))v=\xi^{r}(f_{z})(v)=(\mu(P_{r}^{+})\sum_{g\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}}j_{P_{r}}(f_{z})(g)\chi(g))v

where

f_{z}\in C^{st}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F})
is the image of
z
under the isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{t}_{r}//\mathrm{W})^{F}]\cong C^{st}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F})
in
Theorem
4.5
.
Note that we have

\mu(P_{r}^{+})\sum_{g\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}}j_{P_{r}}(f_{z})(g)\chi(g)=\mu(P_{r}^{+})\sum_{g\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}}\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(j_{P_{r}}(f_{z}))(g)\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(\chi^{-1})(g).

A direct computation show that

\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(\chi^{-1})=|\mathfrak{g}_{P}^{F}|^{1/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\chi}

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\chi}

is the characteristic function at
\chi\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}\cong(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{*})^{F}
.
On the other hand,
it follows from the definition of
j_{P_{r}}
in (
50
) that

\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(j_{P_{r}}(f_{z}))(g)=c_{\mu,r}^{-1}\mathrm{FT}^{2}_{P_{r}}((\chi_{P_{r}}^{*}t_{P_{r}}^{*}(z))^{-})(g)=c_{\mu,r}^{-1}((\chi_{P_{r}}^{*}t_{P_{r}}^{*}(z))^{-})(-g)=c_{\mu,r}^{-1}(\chi_{P_{r}}^{*}t_{P_{r}}^{*}(z))(g).

All together, we obtain

z(\theta(\pi))=\mu(P_{r}^{+})\sum_{g\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}}\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(j_{P_{r}}(f_{z}))(g)\mathrm{FT}_{P_{r}}(\chi^{-1})(g)=

=(\mu(P_{r}^{+})|\mathfrak{g}_{P}^{F}|^{1/2}c_{\mu,r}^{-1})\sum_{g\in\mathfrak{g}_{P_{r}}^{F}}\chi_{P_{r}}^{*}t_{P_{r}}^{*}(z)(g)\mathrm{1}_{\chi}(g)=\chi_{P_{r}}^{*}t_{P_{r}}^{*}(z)(\chi)=z(\theta(\chi)).

This implies
\theta(\pi)=\theta(\chi)
.

Abstract
. It is known that every graph with n n vertices embeds stochastically into trees with distortion O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) . In this paper, we show that this upper bound is sharp for a large class of graphs. As this class of graphs contains diamond graphs, this result extends known examples that obtain this largest possible stochastic distortion.

1.
Introduction

The continued development of the theory of low distortion embeddings of general finite metric spaces into simple metric spaces has been important for understanding how complex metric structures can be efficiently represented. Furthermore, the development of such theory often results in fruitful applications for computer science approximation algorithms (see
[
BhattLeighton1984
,
LeightonRao1999
,
KleinRaoAgrawalRavi1995
]
for classic applications involving the sparsest cut problem and how it relates to embeddings into
\ell_{1}
metrics). Due to this algorithmic connection, a particularly desirable family of simple metric spaces is weighted trees. However, there are simple graphs that cannot be well approximated by trees. For example, any embedding of the
n
-cycle into a tree must incur a distortion of at least order
n

[
RabinovichRaz1998
]

(see also Lemma
3.1
below). Nevertheless, there has been progress when the problem is generalized to stochastic embeddings.

Stochastic embeddings (also called random or probabilistic embeddings) were originally defined by Bartal
[
Bartal1996
]
. Since finite, weighted trees are isometrically isomorphic to finite dimensional
\ell_{1}
spaces, the existence of a stochastic embedding of a metric space into a family of trees implies the existence of a bi-Lipshchitz embedding into
\ell_{1}
without an increase of the distortion. More recently, it has been shown that a stochastic embedding of a finite metric space into a family of trees can be used to obtain bi-Lipshitz embeddings of lamplighter metric spaces and transportation cost spaces into
L_{1}
(see
[
BMSZ2022
,
DilworthKutzarovaOstovskii2021
,
Mathey-PrevotValette2021
]
).

Also in
[
Bartal1996
]
Bartal shows that any finite metric space on
n
elements can be stochastically embedded into trees with stochastic distortion
O(\log^{2}(n))
. This upper bound was lowered to
O(\log(n))
by Fakcharoenphol et al
[
FakcharoenpholRaoTalwar2004
]
which was known to be optimal by the observation of Bartal that all expander graphs must exhibit
\Omega(\log(n))
stochastic distortion
[
Bartal1996
]
. More basic families of metric spaces that achieve this bound have been since found such as the 2-dimensional grid
[
AlonKarpPelegWest1995
]
and a family of graphs, called the diamond graphs
[
GuptaNewmanRabinovichSinclair1999
]
. Our main result is a generalization of the latter result.

Main Theorem
.

Suppose
(G,d_{G})
is a normalized geodesic
s
-
t
graph. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
(G^{\oslash k},d_{G^{\oslash k}})
be its
k
-th slash power, and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{T}}(G^{\oslash k},d_{G^{\oslash k}})\geq 1
be the stochastic distortion
of
(G^{\oslash k},d_{G^{\oslash k}})
into the family of geodesic trees
\mathcal{T}
.
Then

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{T}}(G^{\oslash k},d_{G^{\oslash k}})=\begin{cases}\Theta(k)&G\ \text{contains a cycle,}\\
1&G\ \text{is a path.}\end{cases}

Thus, slash powers provide a large family of graphs with the largest possible stochastic distortion.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we state the necessary notation and relevant definitions, including the construction of slash powers of graphs and
expected distortion
. In addition, we show that given a probability measure on the edges of a graph the corresponding expected distortion of a map into an expanding tree is a lower bound for the stochastic distortion of the graph into a family of trees. In Section 3, we state a mild strengthening of a result of Gupta
[
Gupta2001
]
on the lower bounds for distortion of a weighted cycle into a tree. Then in Section 4, we prove the desired result for slash powers of a vital family of weighted graphs that we call
balanced generalized Laakso graphs
. A proof of the Main Theorem is then given in Section 5.

(i.e.

). We shall often denote a (directed or undirected) path by

or
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})

and call it a

to

For two paths

A subgraph

A graph

A

2.2.
Geodesic Metrics

If

is a metric on

d_{G}(u,v)=\min\{\text{\rm length}_{d_{G}}(P):P\text{ is a path from $u$ to $v$}\},\text{ for $u,v\in V$,}

where for a path

in

and sometimes refer to this value as the

a
geodesic graph
.
For

.

Assume that

is a function.
Define for

d_{G}(u,v):=\min\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}w(\{x_{j-1},x_{j}\}):(x_{j})_{j=0}^{n}\text{ is a path from $u$ to $v$}\Big\}.

Then

is a geodesic metric on

, and we call it the
metric generated by the weight function

.
Note that if

is an arbitrary function and

the geodesic metric generated by

, since it might be possible that there is a path of shorter metric length between the two endpoints of

For the special weight function, defined by

for all
e\in E(G)
, and for a path
P
in
G
we call

the
graph length of
P
.

Conversely, any geodesic metric on
G
is generated by the weight function

w:E\to\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad\{u,v\}\mapsto d_{G}(u,v).

If
(G,d_{G})
is a geodesic graph and

is a connected subgraph
we call
(H,d_{H})
, where
d_{H}
is the geodesic distance on
V(H)
generated by the weight

w:E(H)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
e\mapsto d_{G}(e)
, an
induced geodesic subgraph
and
d_{H}
the
induced geodesic metric of

on

. Note that

is not necessarily the restriction of

on

(for example, if

is a cycle and

is obtained
by taking away one edge). In the scenario when

for all

we say that

is an
isometric geodesic subgraph
of

or, when the context is clear, simply an

.

2.6.

Let

be a class of metric spaces, and let

be a metric space.

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,y)\leq d_{i}(f_{i}(x),f_{i}(y)),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}\big(d_{i}(f_{i}(x),f_{i}(y)\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}d_{i}\big(f_{i}(x),f_{i}(y)\big)\leq Dd_{X}(x,y).

is

. We denote

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,d_{X}):=\inf\{D\geq 1:\ (X,d_{X})\ \text{is}\ D-\text{stochastically embeddable into}\ \mathcal{M}\}

and call the value
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,d_{X})

the stochastic distortion of

into
\mathcal{M}
.

By following the same ideas as in
[
AlonKarpPelegWest1995
]
one could deduce from the Min-Max Theorem
that

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,d_{X})=\sup_{\nu}\inf_{(f,M)}\sum_{x,y\in X,x\not=y}\nu(\{x,y\})\frac{d_{M}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{X}(x,y)},

where the
\sup

with finite support,
and the

is take over all pairs

with
(M,d_{M})
being in the class
\mathcal{M}
and

being expansive.
We will only need that the left side is not smaller than the right side, and this only for geodesic graphs, a result
which is much more elementary and will be observed in the next Proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(G,d_{G})
, be a geodesic graph, and let

be a probability on

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\geq\max_{x,y\in V(G),x\not=y}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}d_{H_{i}}(f_{i}(x),f_{i}(y))}{d_{G}(x,y)}

Corollary 4.3

\displaystyle\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}^{(n)}_{\phi{(C)}}|}\frac{\nu_{n}(\phi(C))}{d_{n}(\phi(C))}=\frac{1}{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}^{(n)}_{\phi(C)}|}\frac{\nu_{n}(\phi(C))}{d_{n}(\phi(C))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}^{(n-1)}_{\phi(C)|[1,n-1]}|}\frac{\nu_{n-1}(\phi(C)|_{[1,n-1]})}{d_{n-1}(\phi(C)|_{[1,n-1])})}2^{-2l(k+l+m)^{n-2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}^{(n-2)}_{\phi(C)|[1,n-2]}|}\frac{\nu_{n-2}(\phi(C)|_{[1,n-2]})}{d_{n-2}(\phi(C)|_{[1,n-2])})}2^{-2l(k+l+m)^{n-3}-2l(k+l+m)^{n-2}}.

Proposition 4.4
.

For each

\Phi_{e_{1}}:V(L_{n-1})\to V(L_{n}),\quad e_{2}\oslash e_{3}\oslash\ldots\oslash e_{n-1}\oslash v\mapsto e_{1}\oslash e_{2}\oslash e_{3}\oslash\ldots\oslash e_{n-1}\oslash v

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(T,d_{T})
a geodesic tree and
\Psi:V(L_{n})\to V(T)
be an expansive map.

Then

\mathbb{D}_{\nu_{n}}(\Psi)=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{n}}\Big(\frac{d_{T}(\Psi(e))}{d_{n}(e)}\Big)=\sum_{e=\{x,y\}\in E(L_{n})}\frac{d_{T}(\Psi(e))}{d_{n}(e)}\nu_{n}(e)\geq\frac{3}{128}c_{0}n.

Proof.

We consider the map

F_{n}:E(L_{n})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad e\mapsto\min\Big(\frac{d_{T}(\Psi(e))}{d_{n}(e)},\frac{3}{32}\frac{c_{0}}{d_{n}(e)}\Big).

We will show by induction for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{n}}(F_{n})\geq\frac{3}{128}c_{0}n.

For
n=1
, (
7
) is true since it follows from (
5
) that
\frac{3}{32}\frac{c_{0}}{d_{1}(e)}\geq\frac{6}{32}c_{0}
. Assume that our claim is true for
n-1
, where
n\geq 2
.

Considering for each
e\in E(L_{n})
the cases
d_{T}(\Psi(e))\geq\frac{3}{16}c_{0}
and
d_{T}(\Psi(e))<\frac{3}{16}c_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{n}}(F_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E(L_{n})\\
d_{T}(\Psi(e))\geq\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\nu_{n}(e)}{d_{n}(e)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E(L_{n}),\\
d_{T}(\Psi(e))<\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\end{subarray}}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e)),\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n}(e)}{d_{n}(e)}

\displaystyle\geq\underbrace{c_{0}\frac{1}{2}\frac{3}{32}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E(L_{n}),\\
d_{T}(\Psi(e))\geq\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\nu_{n}(e)}{d_{n}(e)}}_{=A}+\underbrace{\sum_{e\in E(L_{n})}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e)),\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n}(e)}{d_{n}(e)}}_{=B}.

For each
C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}
choose, according to Corollary
4.1
, an edge
e^{C}=e^{C}_{1}\oslash e^{C}_{2}\oslash\ldots\oslash e^{C}_{n}\in E(C)
for which
d_{T}(\Psi(e_{C}))\geq\frac{3}{32}c_{0}
. The value of
A
can be estimated as follows:
Since for each
C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}
, the edge
e_{C}
is an element of at most
|C^{(n)}_{e_{C}}|
circles of
\mathcal{C}^{(n)}
it follows from Proposition
4.2
(c) and Corollary
4.3
, that

(8)

\displaystyle A\geq c_{0}\frac{3}{64}\sum_{e\in\{e_{C}:C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}\}}\frac{\nu_{n}(e)}{d_{n}(e)}\geq c_{0}\frac{3}{64}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{(n)}}\frac{\nu_{n}(e_{C})}{d_{n}(e_{C})}\frac{1}{|C^{(n)}_{e_{C}}|}=\frac{3}{128}c_{0}.

In order to estimate
B
we define
P_{1}=(x_{i})_{i=0}^{k}\smile(y^{(1)}_{i})_{i=0}^{l}\smile(z_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
and
P_{2}=(x_{i})_{i=0}^{k}\smile(y^{(2)}_{i})_{i=0}^{l}\smile(z_{i})_{i=0}^{m}

(
i.e.,
the two paths from
s(L)
to
t(L)
in
L
)
and compute

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{1}\in E(L)}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime})),\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}\frac{\nu_{1}(e_{1})}{d_{1}(e_{1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{1})}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime})),\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{2})}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime})),\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}.

The above equality is true since
\frac{\nu_{1}(e_{1})}{d_{1}(e_{1})}=1\iff e_{1}\in E((x_{i})_{i=0}^{k})
or
e_{1}\in E((z_{i})_{i=0}^{m})

\iff e_{1}\in E(P_{1})\cap E(P_{2})
and

\frac{\nu_{1}(e_{1})}{d_{1}(e_{1})}=\frac{1}{2}\iff e_{1}\in E((y^{(1)}_{i})_{i=0}^{l})
or
e_{1}\in E((y^{(2)}_{i})_{i=0}^{l})
.
For
\sigma=1,2
we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})}

\displaystyle\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime})),\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})}d_{1}(e_{1})\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(\frac{d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime})}{d_{1}(e_{1})},\frac{1}{d_{1}(e_{1})}\frac{3}{64}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})}d_{1}(e_{1})\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(\frac{d_{T}(\Psi(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime}))}{d_{1}(e_{1})},\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}\quad\text{ (by \eqref{E:4.1.1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})}d_{1}(e_{1})\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}\min\Big(d^{\prime}_{T}(\Psi_{e_{1}}(e^{\prime})),\frac{3}{32}c_{0}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})},

where for
e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})
we define
\Psi_{e_{1}}:V(L_{n-1})\to V(T)
,
v\mapsto\Psi(e_{1}\oslash v)
, which by Proposition
4.4
is an expansive map
for the metric
d^{\prime}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)=\frac{d_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)}{d_{L}(e_{1})}
on
V(T)
.

It follows, therefore, from the induction hypothesis that for each
\sigma=1,2

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{1}\in E(P_{\sigma})}\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E(L_{n-1})}

\displaystyle\min\Big(d_{T}(e_{1}\oslash e^{\prime}),c_{0}\frac{3}{64}\Big)\frac{\nu_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}{d_{n-1}(e^{\prime})}\geq\frac{3}{128}c_{0}(n-1).

and, thus, that
B\geq\frac{3}{128}c_{0}(n-1)
which yields

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{n}}(F_{n})\geq A+B\geq\frac{3}{128}c_{0}n,

and finishes the induction step, and, thus, the proof of the claim.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
(G,d_{G})
be a normalized, geodesic
s
-
t
graph such that
G
contains a cycle, and let

c_{0}=\max\big\{\text{\rm length}_{d_{G}}(C)\ :\ C\text{ is cycle in }G\big\}.

Suppose
N\in\mathbb{N}
is such that

contains a balanced generalized Laakso
s
-
t
subgraph, say
L
, whose cycle has length
c_{0}

for all

and
\nu
is defined as in Example
2.4
(c) on

and vanishes on

,

is a tree and an

-
t

C_{0}

5.1
and the observation that for any

is of the order

,

contains a balanced generalized Laakso
s
-
t

L
, where

. However, this follows from the following observations that are consequences of the previous results of this paper:

(1)

We can find a generalized Laakso
s
-
t

-

-

Time analyticity for the heat equation under Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature condition
Ling Wu

Abstract
. Inspired by Hongjie Dong and Qi S. Zhang’s article [ 14 ] , we find that the analyticity in time for a smooth solution of the heat equation with exponential quadratic growth in the space variable can be extended to any complete noncompact Riemannian manifolds with Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below and the potential function being of at most quadratic growth. Therefore, our result holds on all gradient Ricci solitons. As a corollary, we give a necessary and sufficient condition on the solvability of the backward heat equation in a class of functions with the similar growth condition. In addition, we also consider the solution in certain L p L^{p} spaces with p ∈ [ 2 , + ∞ ) p\in[2,+\infty) and prove its analyticity with respect to time.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional Riemmanian manifold. The Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature tensor of
M
(
[
1
]
) is defined as

\Ric_{f}:=\Ric+\Hess f,

(1.1)

where
f
is a smooth function on
M
(called the potential function), and
\Ric
and
\Hess f
denote the Ricci curvature tensor and the hessian of
f
, respectively. It is clear that when
f
is a constant,
\Ric_{f}
reduces to the Ricci curvature tensor. A gradient Ricci soliton is a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with constant Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature, namely,

\Ric+\Hess f=\lambda g

(1.2)

for some constant
\lambda
. It is called a shrinking, steady, or expanding Ricci soliton when
\lambda>0
,
=0
, or
<0
, respectively. Also, manifolds with lower Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bound are closely related to the singularity analysis of the Ricci flow and Ricci limit spaces (see e.g.,
[
4
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
5
]
). Therefore, many efforts have been made in extending the results under Ricci curvature condition to Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature condition.

The study of the analyticity of the heat equation has a rich history. For generic solutions, as expected, the space analyticity is valid. However, the time analyticity is more delicate and is indeed invalid, since in the Euclidean space, it is easy to construct a non-time-analytic solution of the heat equation in a finite space-time cylinder. Therefore, it is meaningful to study the time analyticity of the heat equation.

Recently, Qi S. Zhang
[
15
]
discovered for any complete noncompact Riemannian whose Ricci curvature is bounded from below, any solution to the heat equation with exponential growth in the space variable is analytic in time. This result was improved to any solution with exponential quadratic growth by Hongjie Dong and Qi S. Zhang
[
14
]
. In particular, as a corollary to this result, they gave a sufficient and necessary condition on the solvability of the backward heat equation. In
[
12
]
, Jiayong Wu obtained a similar result on the time analyticity of the heat equation on the complete noncompact gradient shrinking Ricci solitons. For more results, see
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
and references therein.

In
[
14
]
, for Riemannian manifolds with Ricci curvature bounded below the key estimate for the proof of time analyticity of the heat equation is the parabolic mean value inequality, which can also be found in
[
11
]
under Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature condition. Here we emphasize our result generalizes Hongjie Dong and Qi S. Zhang’s result
[
14
]
and can be extend to all gradient Ricci solitons.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with
\Ric_{f}\geq-Kg
for some constant
K\geq 0
. For a fixed point
o\in M
, assume that there exist non-negative constants
a
and
b
such that

|f(x)|\leq ad^{2}(x,o)+b\ for\ all\ x\in M,

(1.3)

where
d(x,o)
is the distance function from
x
to
o
. Let
u(x,t)
be a smooth solution of the heat equation
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
on
M\times[-2,0]
and satisfy exponential quadratic growth in the space variable, i.e.,

|u(x,t)|\leq A_{1}e^{A_{2}d^{2}(x,o)}\ for\ all\ (x,t)\in M\times[-2,0],

(1.4)

where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are some positive constants. Then
u(x,t)
is analytic in time
t\in[-1,0]
with radius
\delta>0
depending only on
n,K,a,b
and
A_{2}
. Besides, we have

u(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

(1.5)

with
\Delta a_{j}(x)=a_{j+1}(x)
and

|a_{j}(x)|\leq A_{1}A_{3}^{j+1}(j+1)^{j}e^{A_{4}d^{2}(x,o)},\ j=0,1,2,...,

(1.6)

where
A_{3}
and
A_{4}
are two positive constants depending on
K,n,a,b,A_{2}
and
n,a,A_{2}
, respectively.

Remark 1.2
.

If the potential function is
0
, i.e.,
a=b=0
in (
1.13
), after careful calculation, then we get
A_{4}=2A_{2}
in (
1.6
). Theorem
1.1
reduces Honejie Dong and Qi S. Zhang’s result
[
14
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The growth condition (
1.4
) is sharp due to the Tychonov’s solution of the heat equation in
R^{n}\times(-\infty,+\infty)
(see Remark 2.3 in
[
14
]
).

The conditions in the above theorem are especially satisfied on gradient Ricci solitons.
For gradient Ricci solitons, it is well known that

S+|\nabla f|^{2}=2\lambda f+C,

(1.7)

where
S
is the scalar curvature of
M
,
\nabla f
is the gradient of
f
and
C
is a constant.

For gradient shrinking solitons, it is showed in
[
2
]
that
S\geq 0
, then setting
\tilde{f}=f+\frac{C}{2\lambda}
, (
1.7
) implies

|\nabla\tilde{f}|^{2}\leq 2\lambda\tilde{f},

so

|\tilde{f}(x)|\leq\lambda d^{2}(x,o)+2|\tilde{f}(o)|.

For gradient expanding solitons, it is showed in
[
8
,
16
]
that
S\geq n\lambda
, then (
1.7
) implies that

\bigg|\nabla\sqrt{-f-\frac{C-n\lambda}{2\lambda}}\bigg|\leq\sqrt{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}.

Hence

\sqrt{-f(x)-\frac{C-n\lambda}{2\lambda}}\leq\sqrt{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}d(x,o)+\sqrt{-f(o)-\frac{C-n\lambda}{2\lambda}},

setting
\tilde{f}=f+\frac{C-n\lambda}{2\lambda}
, then

|\tilde{f}(x)|\leq-\lambda d^{2}(x,o)+2|\tilde{f}(o)|.

For gradient steady solitons, we know
S\geq 0
in
[
2
]
.

If
C=0
in (
1.7
), then
f
is a constant.

If
C\neq 0
in (
1.7
), by scaling the metric
g
, we can get

S+|\nabla f|^{2}=1,

which implies

|f(x)|\leq d(x,o)+|f(o)|.

To sum up, for gradient Ricci solitons, we can always adjust
f
or
g
to make

|f(x)|\leq ad^{2}(x,o)+b,

(1.8)

where
a
and
b
are two positive constants depending on
\lambda
and
f(o)
, respectively.

Therefore, Theorem
1.1
implies the analyticity in time for smooth solutions of the heat equation on complete noncompact gradient Ricci solitons.

(1.10)

and

|a_{j}(x)|\leq A_{1}A_{3}^{j+1}(j+1)^{j}e^{A_{4}d^{2}(x,o)},\ j=0,1,2,...,

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
12
]
, Jiayong Wu obtained a similar result on the time analyticity of the heat equation on the complete noncompact gradient shrinking Ricci solitons. More precisely, he showed that the bound of

where

is a constant depending on

and

denotes Perelman’s entropy functional.

Comparing (

) with (

2.
Preliminaries

For a fixed point
o\in M
and
R>0
, we define

L(R)=\sup\limits_{B_{o}(3R)}|f|,

where
B_{o}(3R)
is the geodesic ball centered at
o\in M
with radius
3R
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
11
]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\Ric_{f}\geq-Kg
for some constant
K\geq 0
. Then the following conclusions are true.

(a)(Laplacian comparison) Let
r=d(y,p)
be the distance from any point
y
to some fixed point
p\in B_{o}(R)
with
0<r<R
. Then for
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we have

\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}(\Delta r-\frac{n-1}{r})dr\leq\frac{K}{6}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2})+6L(R).

(2.1)

(b)(Volume element comparison)Take any point
p\in B_{o}(R)
and denote the volume form in geodesic polar coordinates centered at
p
with
J(r,\theta,p)drd\theta
, where
r>0
and
\theta\in S_{p}(M)
, a unit tangent vector at
p
. Then for
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we have

\frac{J(r_{2},\theta,p)}{J(r_{1},\theta,p)}\leq\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{n-1}e^{\frac{K}{6}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2})+6L(R)}.

(2.2)

(c)(Volume comparison)For any
p\in B_{o}(R),\ 0<r_{1}<r_{2}<R
, we have

\frac{\vol(B_{p}(r_{2}))}{\vol(B_{p}(r_{1}))}\leq\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{K}{6}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2})+6L(R)},

(2.3)

where
\vol(.)
denotes the volume of a region.

From
[
11
]
, we have the following parabolic mean value inequality, which is crucial to prove the analyticity of time.

Proposition 2.2
.

(Mean value inequality
[
11
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\Ric_{f}\geq-Kg
for some constant
K\geq 0
. For any real number
s
and any
0<\tilde{\delta}<\delta^{\prime}\leq 1
, let
u
be a smooth non-negative subsolution of the heat equation in the cylinder
Q=B_{o}(r)\times(s-r^{2},s)
,
0<r<R
.

For
2\leq p<\infty
, there exist constants
\tilde{c}_{1}(n)
and
\tilde{c}_{2}(n)
such that

\sup\limits_{Q_{\tilde{\delta}}}u^{p}\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}(n)e^{\tilde{c}_{2}(n)(Kr^{2}+L(R))}}{(\delta^{\prime}-\tilde{\delta})^{4n}r^{2}\vol(B_{o}(r))}\cdot\int_{Q_{\delta^{\prime}}}u^{p}dvdt.

(2.4)

For
0<p<2
, there exist constants
\tilde{c}_{3}(n,p)
and
\tilde{c}_{4}(n)
such that

\sup\limits_{Q_{\tilde{\delta}}}u^{p}\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{3}(n,p)e^{\tilde{c}_{4}(n)(Kr^{2}+L(R))}}{(\delta^{\prime}-\tilde{\delta})^{4n}r^{2}\vol(B_{o}(r))}\cdot\int_{Q_{\delta^{\prime}}}u^{p}dvdt.

(2.5)

Here
Q_{\tilde{\delta}}=B_{o}(\tilde{\delta}r)\times(s-\tilde{\delta}r^{2},s)
,
Q_{\delta^{\prime}}=B_{o}(\delta^{\prime}r)\times(s-\delta^{\prime}r^{2},s)
.

3.
proof of the main results

In this section, we apply the volume comparison theorem and the parabolic mean value inequality in section 2 to prove the results of this article. We first prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Since the heat equation is linear, we can assume that
A_{1}=1
. Indeed, we just need to prove the time analyticity result at
(x,0)
for any
x\in M
.

Given
R\geq 1
. For any point
x\in B_{o}(R)
and a positive integer
j
, since the solution
u(x,t)
is smooth, we choose
t\in[-\delta,0]
for
0<\delta<1
, by Taylor’s theorem,

u(x,t)-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\partial_{t}^{i}u(x,0)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}=\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\partial_{s}^{j}u(x,s),

(3.1)

where
s=s(x,t,j)\in[t,0]
. It suffices to prove that the right hand side of (
3.1
) tends to zero when
j
tends to infinity for any
x\in B_{o}(R)
and
t\in[-\delta,0]
with
\delta>0
sufficiently small.

Since
u^{2}
is a non-negative subsolution to the heat equation, we apply Proposition
2.2
with
p=1
. Given a point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in M\times[-1,0]
and a positive integer
k
, by letting
s=t_{0}
,
r=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
,
\tilde{\delta}=\frac{1}{2}
,
\delta^{\prime}=1
in (
2.5
), we have

u^{2}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq\frac{c_{1}(n)e^{c_{2}(n)(K\frac{1}{k}+\sup\limits_{B_{x_{0}}(\frac{3}{\sqrt{k}})}|f|)}}{(\frac{1}{2})^{4n}\frac{1}{k}\vol(B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}))}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})\times\left[t_{0}-\frac{1}{k},t_{0}\right]}u^{2}dvdt.

We observe that

\sup\limits_{B_{x_{0}}(\frac{3}{\sqrt{k}})}|f|\leq\sup\limits_{B_{o}(d(x_{0},o)+\frac{3}{\sqrt{k}})}|f|\leq a\left(d(x_{0},o)+\frac{3}{\sqrt{k}}\right)^{2}+b\leq 2ad^{2}(x_{0},o)+\frac{18a}{k}+b,

then we have

u^{2}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq\frac{c_{3}(n)e^{c_{4}(n)(K+ad^{2}(x_{0},o)+a+b)}k}{\vol(B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}))}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})\times\left[t_{0}-\frac{1}{k},t_{0}\right]}u^{2}dvdt.

(3.2)

Since
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\partial_{t}^{k-1}u=0
, from (
3.2
), we obtain

(\partial_{t}^{k-1}u)^{2}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq\frac{c_{3}(n)e^{c_{4}(n)(K+ad^{2}(x_{0},o)+a+b)}k}{\vol(B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}))}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})\times\left[t_{0}-\frac{1}{k},t_{0}\right]}(\partial_{t}^{k-1}u)^{2}dvdt

(3.3)

for a positive integer
k
.

Next, we will bound the right-hand side of (
3.3
).

For positive integers
j=1,2,...,k
, we define the following domains:

\Omega_{j}^{1}=B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\times\left[t_{0}-\frac{j}{k},t_{0}\right],

\Omega_{j}^{2}=B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j+0.5}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\times\left[t_{0}-\frac{j+0.5}{k},t_{0}\right].

It is easy to see that

\Omega_{j}^{1}\subset\Omega_{j}^{2}\subset\Omega_{j+1}^{1}.

(3.4)

Let
\psi_{j}^{(1)}
be a standard Lipschitz cut-off function supported in

B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j+0.5}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\times\left(t_{0}-\frac{j+0.5}{k},t_{0}+\frac{j+0.5}{k}\right)

satisfying

\psi_{j}^{(1)}=1\ in\ \Omega_{j}^{1}\ and\ |\nabla\psi_{j}^{(1)}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\psi_{j}^{(1)}|\leq Ck,

(3.5)

where
C
is a universal constant that may be changed line by line.

For the above cut-off function
\psi=\psi_{j}^{(1)}
, since
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
, using integration by parts, we compute that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}(u_{t})^{2}\psi^{2}dvdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}u_{t}\Delta u\psi^{2}dvdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}\left<(\nabla u)_{t},\nabla u\right>\psi^{2}dvdt-\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}u_{t}\left<\nabla u,\nabla\psi^{2}\right>dvdt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}(|\nabla u|^{2})_{t}\psi^{2}dvdt-2\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}u_{t}\psi\left<\nabla u,\nabla\psi\right>dvdt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j+0.5}{\sqrt{k}}\right)}(|\nabla u|^{2}\psi^{2})(x,t_{0})dv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\psi^{2})_{t}dvdt-2\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}u_{t}\psi\left<\nabla u,\nabla\psi\right>dvdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}(\psi^{2})_{t}dvdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}(u_{t})^{2}\psi^{2}dvdt+2\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\psi|^{2}dvdt.

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have that

\int_{\Omega_{j}^{1}}(u_{t})^{2}dvdt\leq Ck\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dvdt.

(3.6)

Let
\psi_{j}^{(2)}
also be a standard Lipschitz cut-off function supported in

B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j+1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)\times\left(t_{0}-\frac{j+1}{k},t_{0}+\frac{j+1}{k}\right)

satisfying

\psi_{j}^{(2)}=1\ in\ \Omega_{j}^{2}\ and\ |\nabla\psi_{j}^{(2)}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\psi_{j}^{(2)}|\leq Ck.

(3.7)

Then we can obtain

\int_{\Omega_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dvdt\leq Ck\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u^{2}dvdt.

(3.8)

To achieve (
3.8
), considering the cut-off function
\varphi=\psi_{j}^{(2)}
, by
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
, using integration by parts, we are continue to calculate that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\partial_{t}(u^{2}\varphi^{2})dvdt-\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\varphi\varphi_{t}u^{2}dvdt=\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}uu_{t}\varphi^{2}dvdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u\Delta u\varphi^{2}dvdt=-\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi^{2}dvdt-2\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u\varphi\left<\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\right>dvdt.

Noticing that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\partial_{t}(u^{2}\varphi^{2})dvdt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{x_{0}}\left(\frac{j+1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)}(u^{2}\varphi^{2})(x,t_{0})dv\geq 0,

we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi^{2}dvdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\varphi\varphi_{t}u^{2}dvdt-2\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u\varphi\left<\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\right>dvdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\varphi\varphi_{t}u^{2}dvdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi^{2}dvdt+2\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dvdt.

This implies that

\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi^{2}dvdt\leq 2\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}\varphi\varphi_{t}u^{2}dvdt+4\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dvdt.

Then (
3.8
) follows by (
3.4
) and (
3.7
).

Combining (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), we achieve that

\int_{\Omega_{j}^{1}}(u_{t})^{2}dvdt\leq Ck^{2}\int_{\Omega_{j+1}^{1}}u^{2}dvdt.

Since above inequality holds for all solutions of the heat equation, then we can replace
u
by
\partial_{t}^{j}u
. By induction, we conclude that

\int_{\Omega_{1}^{1}}(\partial_{t}^{k-1}u)^{2}dvdt\leq C^{k-1}k^{2(k-1)}\int_{\Omega_{k}^{1}}u^{2}dvdt.

By the selection of

, we substitute the above inequality into (

(3.9)

Using exponential quadratic growth condition (

) and the triangle inequality, for some point

we deduce

), we have

(3.12)

for all integers

Abstract
. We determine the irreducible components of Igusa varieties for Shimura varieties of Hodge type under a mild condition and use that to compute the irreducible components of central leaves. In particular, we show that a strong version of the discrete Hecke orbit conjecture is false in general. Our method combines recent work of D’Addezio on monodromy groups of compatible local systems with a generalisation of a method of Hida, using the Honda–Tate theory for Shimura varieties of Hodge type developed by Kisin–Madapusi Pera–Shin. We also determine the irreducible components of Newton strata in Shimura varieties of Hodge type by combining our methods with recent work of Zhou–Zhu.



1.
Introduction

Let
N\geq 4
, let
p
be a prime number coprime to
N
, let
Y_{1}(N)
be the modular curve of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and let
Y_{1}(N)^{\text{ord}}
be the ordinary locus. There is a tower of finite étale covers (see
[
26
]
)
\operatorname{Ig}_{m}\to Y_{1}(N)^{\text{ord}}
with Galois groups
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and we let
\operatorname{Ig}_{\infty}\to Y_{1}(N)^{\text{ord}}
be their inverse limit. It is a classical result due to Igusa that
\operatorname{Ig}_{\infty}
is irreducible.

Igusa varieties exist more generally as profinite étale covers of central leaves in the special fibers of Shimura varieties of Hodge type (cf.
[
19
]
*Section 5; see
[
37
]
for the PEL case). Understanding the irreducible components of these Igusa varieties has important consequences for the theory of
p
-adic automorphic forms. For example, in the work of Eischen–Mantovan
[
13
]
on
p
-adic automorphic forms for unitary Shimura varieties, the irreducibility of Igusa varieties is assumed throughout.

1.1.
Main results

Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum of Hodge type with reflex field
E
. Let
p>2
be a prime number, let
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
be a sufficiently small compact open subgroup and let
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a hyperspecial subgroup. For a prime
v|p
of
E
, we let
\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
be the base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of the canonical integral model over
\mathcal{O}_{E,v}
of the Shimura variety of level
K^{p}K_{p}
, see
[
29
, main theorem]
.

Let
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}\subset\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
be a non-basic Newton stratum and let
C\subset\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}
be a central leaf (see
[
19
]
or Section
2.1.3
). Then the Igusa variety
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}\to C
is a profinite étale cover with Galois group a compact open subgroup
H_{C}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the twisted centraliser in
G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
of some
b\in[b]
(see Section
2.1.5
). Let
G^{\mathrm{der}}
denote the derived group of
G
. Write
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
for the maximal abelian quotient of
G
and
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-points of the connected Néron model of
G^{\mathrm{ab}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Under the natural surjective map
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(see Section
2.1.5
) the subgroup
H_{C}
maps to
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, which defines an action of
H_{C}
on
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected and
\mathbb{Q}
-simple. If
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors, then the natural map (induced by
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}\to C\to\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
)

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G}),

is surjective with fibers in bijection with
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, equivariant for the action of
H_{C}
.

The natural map in Theorem
1
is equivariant for the prime-to-
p
Hecke operators, but it should not be true that these operators act trivially on the fibers, see Conjectures
6.1.1
and
6.2.2
.

Remark 1.1.1
.

In the case of the modular curve, the ordinary Igusa variety
\operatorname{Ig}_{[\mathrm{ord}]}
is a
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
-torsor over the ordinary locus, and our theorem tells us that its connected components are in bijection with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
; here
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
via the product map. This recovers the result of Igusa from the first paragraph of the introduction, because the Igusa tower
\operatorname{Ig}_{\infty}
introduced there is the inverse image of
1
under
\operatorname{Ig}_{[\mathrm{ord}]}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

The irreducibility of Igusa varieties was proved for Siegel modular varieties by Chai-Oort
[
7
]
, and their proof works more generally for Shimura varieties of PEL type when hypersymmetric points exist (cf.
[
23
,
13
]
). We would like to point out that even in the
\mu
-ordinary locus, hypersymmetric points often do not exist (see
[
53
]
*Corollary 7.5.). Hida, see
[
22
]
, proved the irreducibility of the ordinary Igusa tower over Shimura varieties of PEL type A and C without using hypersymmetric points. Our results are the first to treat Hodge type Shimura varieties and Igusa varieties over general central leaves (but see Remark
1.1.5
); they are even new for the
\mu
-ordinary locus in many PEL type cases.

When
[b]
is basic, the Igusa variety
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
is zero-dimensional and highly reducible. In particular, the theorem is false for products of Shimura varieties with
[b]
basic in one factor and non-basic in the other; this is where the assumption that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple comes from. This assumption is equivalent to asking that the adjoint group
G^{\mathrm{ad}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple, since we also assume that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected. It can be replaced with the assumption that
[b]
is
\mathbb{Q}
-non-basic, see Section
3
.

Remark 1.1.2
.

The assumption that
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors is relatively mild; For Siegel modular varieties, it comes down to the assumption that the
F
-isocrystal corresponding to
[b]
does not have slope
1/2
with multiplicity one. It is automatic for the
\mu
-ordinary locus or more generally for Newton strata corresponding to
[b]
with
J_{b}
quasi-split.

We can use Theorem
1
to determine the irreducible (equivalently, connected) components of the central leaf
C
.

Corollary 1.1.3
.

Assume that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected and
\mathbb{Q}
-simple. If
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors, then the natural map

(1.1.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(C)\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})

is surjective with finite fibers given by the quotient
H_{C}\backslash G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

A strong version of the discrete Hecke orbit conjecture, see
[
34
]
*Question 8.2.1.
\operatorname{HO}^{+}_{\mathrm{disc}}
,
predicts that the natural map
\pi_{0}(C)\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})
is an isomorphism. Using Corollary
1.1.3
, the conjecture comes down to the surjectivity of
H_{C}\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. We will show that if
H_{C}
is a parahoric subgroup, then
H_{C}
surjects onto
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
; this is also used in our proof of Theorem
1
. In Section
6.3
, we give an example, due to Rong Zhou, which shows that this equality does not always hold. In particular,
[
34
]
*Question 8.2.1.
\operatorname{HO}^{+}_{\mathrm{disc}}
has a negative answer in general.

Our second main result is about irreducible components of Newton strata.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected and
\mathbb{Q}
-simple. If
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors, then the natural map

(1.1.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})

is a bijection. Moreover, the number of irreducible components in each connected component of
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}
is given by the representation-theoretic constant

(1.1.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dim}V_{\mu}(\lambda_{b})_{\mathrm{rel}},

introduced in
[
55
, Section 2.6]
. In particular, if
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is split, then the connected components of
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}
are irreducible.

Theorems
1
and
2
were proved for Siegel modular varieties by Chai and Oort in their seminal paper
[
7
]
. Amusingly, they prove irreducibility of Newton strata first, irreducibility of central leaves second and irreducibility of Igusa varieties last.

Remark 1.1.4
.

In Section
6
we prove versions of our main theorems beyond the case that
K_{p}
is hyperspecial. To be precise, we work with the Igusa varieties constructed by Hamacher–Kim, see
[
19
]
, over the Kisin–Pappas integral models of Shimura varieties of Hodge type, see
[
31
]
, when
K_{p}
is a connected very special parahoric. See Theorems
6.0.4
and
6.0.7
for the more general versions of Theorems
1
and
2
and Corollary
6.0.6
for the general version of Corollary
1.1.3
.

Remark 1.1.5
.

In recent work
[
34
]
, Kret and Shin also determine the irreducible components of Igusa varieties when
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, and they moreover prove the discrete Hecke orbit conjecture (Conjecture
6.2.2
). Their proof uses harmonic analysis and automorphic forms and is completely different from ours. They compute the
0
-th étale cohomology of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
as a representation of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
using the Langlands–Kottwitz method (
[
34
]
*Theorem A), and then determine the irreducible components of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
using that computation. It might be possible to recover their computation of the
0
-th étale cohomology from Theorem
1
, see Conjecture
6.1.1
and Question
6.1.3
.

1.2.
Strategy

1.2.1.
Setup

Recall that the Igusa variety is a profinite étale cover
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}\to C
of a central leaf
C
inside the Newton stratum
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}
. To be precise, it is a torsor for a compact open subgroup
H_{C}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
J_{b}
is the twisted centraliser in
G(\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
attached to some
b\in[b]
(see Section
2
). There are many different central leaves
C
inside the Newton stratum
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b]}
, all giving rise to isomorphic Igusa varieties, but the group
H_{C}

does
depend on
C
. For the purposes of our proof we will always choose
C
to be a
distinguished
central leaf, that is, a central leaf that is also an Ekedahl–Oort stratum; these always exist by
[
49
, Theorem D]
, see Section
2.5
. Informally, distinguished central leaves are the ‘smallest possible’ central leaves
C
and correspondingly
H_{C}
is ‘as large as possible’ when
C
is distinguished. For the rest of this section, we assume that
C
is distinguished and write
H
for
H_{C}
.

The algebraic group
J_{b}
is an inner form of a Levi subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, and therefore has a surjective map
J_{b}\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}
. Let
J_{b}^{\prime}
be the kernel of this map and let
H^{\prime}=H\cap J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In Section
2.6
, we will show that
H/H^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. When
C
is not distinguished, the quotient
H_{C}/H_{C}^{\prime}
is in general strictly contained in
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, see Section
6.3
.

1.2.2.
Outline of the proof

Theorem
2
is a consequence of Theorem
1
together with a careful analysis of the Mantovan product-formula, due to Hamacher–Kim in this generality, and results of Chen–Kisin–Viehmann, Zhou–Zhu and He–Zhou, see
[
37
,
19
,
8
,
55
,
21
]
. Below, we will outline the proof of Theorem
1
.

Under the assumptions of our main theorem, distinguished central leaves are irreducible. To be precise,
[
24
]
*Theorem 4 tells us that for a distinguished
C
the natural map

(1.2.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(C)\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})

is a bijection. Therefore, the main theorem would follow if we could show that the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(C)
are in bijection with
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, equivariant for the action of
H
via
H\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. This comes down to showing that the stabiliser in
H
of a connected component of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}
is given by
H^{\prime}
.

Fix a connected component
C^{\circ}
of
C
. The
H
-torsor
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}^{\circ}\to C^{\circ}
corresponds to a continuous morphism

(1.2.2)

\displaystyle\rho_{\operatorname{Ig}}:\pi_{1}^{\text{\'{e}t}}(C^{\circ})\to H,

with image
M
. If we could show that
M=H^{\prime}
, then the fibers of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}^{\circ}\to C^{\circ}
are given by
H/H^{\prime}=G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Let
\mathbb{M}
be the Zariski closure of
M
inside
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, which is an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Let
A\to C^{\circ}
be the ‘universal’ abelian variety coming from a choice of Hodge embedding. It is a consequence of
[
24
]
*Theorem 4 that the
\ell
-adic monodromy of
A
over
C^{\circ}
is maximal. This can be combined with two results of D’Addezio from
[
9
,
10
]
to show that a certain (overconvergent)
p
-adic monodromy group over
C^{\circ}
is maximal. We deduce from this that
\mathbb{M}=J_{b}^{\prime}
using Proposition
3.3.1
, which
compares
\mathbb{M}
with the monodromy group of the
F
-isocrystal associated to
A
over
C^{\circ}
; this proposition might be of independent interest. As a corollary, we find that
M
contains a compact open subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and that
M
is contained in
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In order to show that
M=H^{\prime}
, we will make use of the fact that the action of
H
on
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
extends to an action of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The goal is to show that the action of
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})
is trivial, which then implies that
M=H\cap J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=H^{\prime}
.

1.2.3.

We will show in Section
3
via a group-theoretic argument that
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})
. The main ingredients are the equality
\mathbb{M}=J_{b}^{\prime}
mentioned above and the fact that the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-points of semisimple and simply connected groups with no compact factors have no finite index proper subgroups. We then show in Section
4
that for each connected component
Z
of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
, there is a maximal torus
T^{\prime}\subset J_{b}^{\prime}
such that
T^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
stabilises
Z
. In fact, we can show that this is true for all maximal tori
T^{\prime}
in
J_{b}^{\prime}
, up to isomorphism of tori. This is done by generalising an argument of Hida
[
22
]
using the Honda–Tate theory for Shimura varieties of
[
30
]
.

In Section
5
, we will deduce from this that
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})
. Indeed, we will show that
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is (topologically) generated by
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
T_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\cdots,T_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
T_{i}
are maximal tori of
J_{b}^{\prime}
, which may be specified up to isomorphism, see Proposition
5.0.1
. When
J_{b}^{\prime}
is quasi-split one only needs a single torus
T_{1}
, namely the centraliser of a maximal split torus. When
J_{b}^{\prime}
is not quasi-split, one needs
2^{m}
tori for some explicit
m
depending on
[b]
, see the statement of Proposition
5.0.1
.

In Section
6
we state and prove the general versions of our main results.

2.
Shimura varieties and Igusa varieties

The goal of this section is to recall the integral models of Shimura varieties of Hodge type constructed in
[
30
,
31
]
, and the constructions of central leaves and Igusa varieties from
[
19
]
.

2.0.1.
Hodge cocharacters

If
(G,X)
is a Shimura datum, then for each
x\in X
there is a cocharacter
\mu_{x}:\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{C}}\to G_{\mathbb{C}}
, see
[
30
, Section 1.2.3]
for the precise definition. The
G(\mathbb{C})
-conjugacy class of
\mu_{x}
does not depend on the choice of
x
and we will denote it by
\{\mu\}
. This conjugacy class of cocharacters is defined over a number field
E\subset\mathbb{C}
, called the reflex field. Given a place
v
of
E
above a rational prime
p
and a choice of algebraic closure
E_{v}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, there is an induced
G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
-conjugacy class of cocharacters of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, which we will also denote by
\{\mu\}
.

2.1.
The construction of integral models

For a symplectic space
(V,\psi)
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we write
\mathcal{G}_{V}:=\operatorname{GSp}(V,\psi)
for the group of symplectic similitudes of
V
over
\mathbb{Q}
. It admits a Shimura datum
(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{V}
is the union of the Siegel upper and lower half-spaces. Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum of Hodge type with reflex field
E
and let
(G,X)\to(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
be a Hodge embedding. Fix a prime
p>2
such that
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is tamely ramified and such that the order of
\pi_{1}(G^{\mathrm{der}})
is coprime to
p
.

Choose a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-lattice
V_{(p)}\subset V
on which
\psi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-valued, and write
V_{p}=V_{(p)}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Write
\mathcal{K}_{p}\subset\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for the stabiliser of
V_{p}
in
\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and similarly write
K_{p}
for the stabiliser of
V_{p}
in
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We will assume that
K_{p}
is a parahoric subgroup and let
\mathcal{G}
be the parahoric group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=K_{p}
.

Because
K_{p}
is the inverse image in
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
of the stabiliser of a lattice, it is automatically a
connected parahoric subgroup
in the sense of
[
54
, start of Section 2]
. Conversely, given a connected parahoric subgroup
K_{p}
of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
we can always find a Hodge embedding
(G,X)\to(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-lattice
V_{(p)}\subset V
on which
\psi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-valued, such that
K_{p}
is the stabiliser of
V_{p}
in
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
; this is explained in
[
30
, Section 1.3.2]
.

2.1.1.

For every sufficiently small compact open subgroup
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
, we can find
\mathcal{K}^{p}\subset\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
such that the Hodge embedding induces a closed immersion (see
[
29
, Lemma 2.1.2]
)

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Sh}_{K}(G,X)\to\mathbf{Sh}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}E

of Shimura varieties of levels
K=K^{p}K_{p}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{p}\mathcal{K}_{p}
, respectively.
We let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
be the moduli-theoretic integral model of
\mathbf{Sh}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
; it is a moduli space of (weakly) polarised abelian schemes
(A,\lambda)
up to prime-to-
p
isogeny with level
\mathcal{K}^{p}
-structure. Fix a prime
v|p
of
E
and let

(2.1.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{K}:=\mathscr{S}_{K}(G,X)\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\mathcal{O}_{E,(v)}

be the normalisation of the Zariski closure of
\mathbf{Sh}_{K}(G,X)
in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\mathcal{O}_{E,(v)}
. This construction is compatible with changing the level away from
p
and we define

(2.1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}:=\varprojlim_{\mathcal{K}^{p}\subset\mathcal{G}_{V}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}^{p}\mathcal{K}_{p}};\qquad\mathscr{S}_{K_{p}}:=\varprojlim_{K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})}\mathscr{S}_{K^{p}K_{p}}.

Then, as discussed in
[
30
, Section 2.1]
, the transition maps in both inverse systems are finite étale and moreover
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
acts on
\mathscr{S}_{K_{p}}
. Choose a map
\mathcal{O}_{E,(v)}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and write
\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
for the base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of
\mathscr{S}_{K_{p}}
and
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}
for the base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of
\mathscr{S}_{K^{p}K_{p}}
; these are both schemes over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
acts on
\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
. We will write
\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{K}^{p}}
for the base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}^{p}\mathcal{K}_{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\mathcal{O}_{E,(v)}
and
\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathcal{G}_{V}}
the base change to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\mathcal{O}_{E,(v)}
. In particular, we are omitting
K_{p}
from the notation.

Let
\pi:A\to\operatorname{Sh}_{\mathcal{G}_{V}}
be the universal abelian variety and let
\mathcal{V}^{p}
be the prime-to-
p
adelic Tate module of
A
; it is a smooth
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}
-sheaf on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
. As explained in
[
30
, Section 2.1.1]
, there is a universal isomorphism

(2.1.4)

\displaystyle\epsilon:V\otimes\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{f}^{p}\simeq\mathcal{V}^{p},

sending the symplectic form
\psi
to an
\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{f}^{p,\times}
-multiple of the Weil pairing. Here
\underline{\mathbb{A}}_{f}^{p}
denotes the pro-étale sheaf associated to the topological group
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}
.

2.1.2.
Tensors

Write
V^{\otimes}
for the direct sum of
V^{\otimes n}\otimes(V^{\ast})^{\otimes m}
for all pairs of integers
m\geq 0,n\geq 0
. We will also use this notation later for modules over commutative rings and modules over sheaves of rings. Write
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}
for the Zariski closure of
G
in
GL(V_{p})
, then
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\mathcal{G}
.

By
[
29
, Lemma 1.3.2]
, there are tensors
\{s_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\subset V_{p}^{\otimes}
such that
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times X_{\{\nu\}}(b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}.}

Proof.

We start by pointing out that both

and

have a dense set of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points. This is true for

because it is an inverse limit of perfections of finite type

-schemes along finite étale transition maps, and for

since it has an open cover by perfections of finite type
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-schemes. Then, since perfect schemes are reduced, it suffices to prove the first claim on the level of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points. In this case, what we want to prove is that for

we have an equality
\llbracket b_{w}\rrbracket=\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket
.

By
[
19
, Lemma 5.2.5]
, the restriction of
\pi_{\infty}
to
\{z\}\times X_{\{\nu\}}(b)
, for
z\in\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, can be identified with the so-called Rapoport–Zink uniformisation map
\Theta_{z}:X_{\{\nu\}}(b)\to\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}
; see
[
19
, Section 4.3]
. The relevant result for the Rapoport–Zink uniformisation map is
[
54
, Proposition 6.5]
, see also the discussion in
[
54
, Section 8. Axiom 4]
.

It follows from this discussion that the diagram is Cartesian on the level of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points, since
\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket=\llbracket b_{y^{\prime}}\rrbracket
if and only if
y
and
y^{\prime}
are in the same

-orbit in
X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
; this is Lemma
2.3.1
. We claim that this implies that the diagram is Cartesian on the level of perfect schemes. Indeed, both the fiber product and the image of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\operatorname{Orb}(y)
are reduced closed subschemes of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times X_{\{\nu\}}(b)
, see Lemma

\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points.
∎

Corollary 2.3.5
.

For
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, the natural map

(2.3.6)

\displaystyle\pi_{\infty}(-,y):\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\}\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}

is a pro-étale
H_{y}
-torsor.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
2.3.4
that

(2.3.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\operatorname{Orb}(y)\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}

is a pro-étale
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-torsor, and therefore the induced map

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\}\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}

is a pro-étale
H_{y}=\operatorname{Stab}_{J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}(y)
-torsor.
∎

Since the product formula is
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
-equivariant it follows that the maps
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\}\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}
are compatible with changing the level away from

. Therefore there is an induced
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
-equivariant map

(2.3.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket}^{\mathrm{perf}},

which is again a pro-étale
H_{y}
-torsor.

Lemma 2.3.6
.

The locally profinite group
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts continuously on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
.

Proof.

The Igusa variety
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\to C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}
is a pro-étale

-torsor by Corollary
2.3.5
and therefore

acts continuously on

∎

2.6.
Some results on parahoric group schemes

Let
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a parahoric subgroup and let
J_{p}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a parahoric subgroup. Let
\mathcal{Z}
denote the connected Néron model of

over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The goal of this section is to prove the following results, the second of which is well-known.

Proposition 2.6.1
.

The image of
J_{p}\to J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is equal to
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proposition 2.6.2
.

The image of
K_{p}\to G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is equal to
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

We will refer to
[
27
, Chapter 4]
for conventions regarding Bruhat–Tits buildings, parahoric subgroups and parahoric group schemes. In particular, we do not work with the extended Bruhat–Tits buildings in this paper. Thus, for a parahoric subgroup
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, there is a point
x
of the Bruhat–Tits building
\mathcal{B}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
such that

. Here the Bruhat–Tits parahoric group scheme

is a smooth affine group scheme which is the relative identity component of the Bruhat–Tits stabiliser group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{x}^{1}
. We will similarly write
J_{p}=\mathcal{J}_{x_{J}}^{0}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for a point
x_{J}
of the Bruhat–Tits building of
\mathcal{B}(J_{b})
of
J_{b}
.

Recall that
J_{b}^{\prime}
is a connected reductive group of
J_{b}
whose derived group is isomorphic to
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
, see Lemma
2.1.6
. Since the buildings of
G^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
respectively of
J_{b}^{\prime}
and
J_{b}
are equal, see
[
27
, bottom of page 343]
, the inclusions
G^{\mathrm{der}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and

induce morphisms

(2.6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{der},1}_{x}\to\mathcal{G}_{x}^{1}

(2.6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{{}^{\prime},1}_{x_{J}}\to\mathcal{J}_{x_{J}}^{1}.

By
[
33
, Proposition 2.4.8]
, these maps are closed immersions since
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
splits over a tamely ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(and thus the same holds for

). Note that the superscript

in our notation corresponds to the tilde in the notation of
[

, Proposition 2.4.9]

3.4.
Consequences of Theorem
3.2.1

In this section we will deduce some consequences of Theorem
3.2.1
that are relevant to us.

Corollary 3.4.1
.

If Assumption
3.1.2
holds, then the group
M
contains a compact open subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and is contained in
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Proof.

The group
M
is a
p
-adic Lie group by
[
16
, Prop. 2.3]
and the morphism
M\to H\to J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a morphism of
p
-adic Lie groups by
[
16
, Prop. 2.2]
. This implies that there is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra
\operatorname{Lie}M
and a morphism of Lie algebras
\operatorname{Lie}M\to\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}
. By Theorem
3.2.1
, the group
M
has Zariski closure equal to
J_{b}^{\prime}
and is thus contained in
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

This means that
\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\prime}
is the smallest Lie algebra of an algebraic subgroup of
J_{b}
containing
\operatorname{Lie}M
. In the notation of
[
2
, Section 7.1]
, this is expressed as
\mathfrak{a}(\operatorname{Lie}M)=\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\prime}
. By
[
2
, Corollary 7.9]
we have the following equality of Lie subalgebras of
\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}
:

(3.4.1)

\displaystyle[\operatorname{Lie}M,\operatorname{Lie}M]

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{a}(\operatorname{Lie}M),\mathfrak{a}(\operatorname{Lie}M)]

(3.4.2)

\displaystyle=[\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\prime},\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\prime}]

(3.4.3)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}.

In particular, we see that
\operatorname{Lie}J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}\subset\operatorname{Lie}M
. By the theory of
p
-adic Lie groups and their exponential maps, see
[
16
, Section 2]
, this implies that
M
contains a compact open subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
∎

Let
Z
be the center of the algebraic group
J_{b}
.

Lemma 3.4.2
.

If Assumption
3.1.2
holds, then the quotient
Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\backslash\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
is finite.

Proof.

The quotient
H\backslash\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
can be identified with
\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}})
and is therefore finite. Moreover, the stabiliser of a connected component of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
can be identified with
M\subset H
. Since
M
contains a compact open subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by Corollary
3.4.1
, we see that
M\cap J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is of finite index in
H^{\mathrm{der}}:=H\cap J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Thus the map
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to H^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
is finite-to-one, and
H
acts with finitely many orbits on the target and its action moreover factors through
H/H^{\mathrm{der}}
.

The quotient
H/H^{\mathrm{der}}
can be identified with a compact open subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In particular, a compact open subgroup of
Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
will act on it with finitely many orbits, and the lemma follows.
∎

Corollary 3.4.3
.

If Assumption
3.1.2
holds, then the group
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts with finite orbits on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
and write the
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-orbit of
a
as
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/P_{a}
, where
P_{a}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the stabiliser of
a
. We want to show that the group
P_{a}^{\mathrm{der}}:=P_{a}\cap J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has finite index in
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Equivalently, by the fact that
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cdot Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has finite index in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and the fact that
Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is finite, we need to show that
P_{a}^{\mathrm{der}}\cdot Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has finite index in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Lemma
3.4.2
implies that
P_{a}\cdot Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has finite index in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and so it suffices to show that

(3.4.4)

\displaystyle P_{a}^{\mathrm{der}}\cdot Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset P_{a}\cdot Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

has finite index. This is true because the cokernel of
P_{a}^{\mathrm{der}}\to P_{a}
is naturally a subgroup of
J_{b}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and because
Z(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to J_{b}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has finite cokernel.
∎

The group
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected because
J_{b}
is an inner form of a Levi subgroup of
G
, and
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected (see
[
36
]
*Proposition 12.14). Therefore we can write
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
as a product of restrictions of scalars of semi-simple and simply connected groups whose adjoint groups are absolutely simple. In particular, we can write
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}=J_{b,\mathrm{an}}^{\mathrm{der}}\times J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}
with the first factor anisotropic and the second factor totally isotropic. In other words, the group
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors.

Proposition 3.4.4
.

If Assumption
3.1.2
holds, then
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
.

It follows from Corollary
3.4.3
that

acts with finite orbits on

. Therefore the subgroup
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})

is totally isotropic, it follows that
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
has no finite index proper subgroups, see
[

. We conclude that the action of

on

This argument will
not
work for the anisotropic part of

, because it is not true that

has no non-trivial finite quotients. This is why we have to assume that

\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]}
, and thus

and

are compact Hausdorff topological spaces.

Since the action of

on

is continuous by Lemma

∎

Hypersymmetric points

We start with the following definition (compare with

Definition 4.2.1
.

[

*Corollary 7.5.. See also

4.3.
Honda–Tate theory

Recall that a special point datum
(T,h,i)
for
(G,X)
consists of a Shimura datum
(T,h)
, where
T
is a maximal torus of
G
, and an embedding of Shimura data

. The
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-points of
\mathbf{Sh}_{K^{p}}(G,X)
that lie in the Shimura variety for
(T,h)
are called special points; they all have good reduction and so give rise to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-points

of
\operatorname{Sh}_{G}
called special points. These points

come equipped with morphisms

such that
T_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\to I_{z,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}\to G_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
is conjugate to
i_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
for all

. (When
K_{p}
is hyperspecial, this is explained in the proof of
[
32
, Proposition 5.7.6.(ii)]
, and the same proof works in general.) In particular, the natural morphism
T\to I_{z}\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}
is equal to
T\to G\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}
. The main theorem of
[
30
]
shows that every Newton stratum in
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}
contains many special points. For a maximal torus
\mathscr{T}\subset I_{z}
, we will write
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
for

.

Proposition 4.3.1
.

If Assumption
3.1.2
holds, then for each maximal torus
T\subset J_{b}
and every connected component
W
of
C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket}
, there is a point

in

contains a torus
\mathscr{T}
, with

isomorphic to

Proof.

Since

is quasi-split, the group

transfers to

extends uniquely to

and its transfer to

]
*Proposition 1.2.5 tells us that we can find a maximal torus

such that: There exists

that factors through

making

into a special point datum, and this special point datum induces an isogeny class

with automorphism group

containing
\mathscr{T}
, such that
\mathscr{T}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is

-conjugate to

in

. In particular,

.

The construction of

in the proof of
[

]
*Proposition 1.2.5 is quite flexible. They start by choosing a maximal torus

such that there is an

factoring through

-conjugate to

, this cocycle can be chosen such that its cohomology class is trivial in

disjoint from

. Then

arises as

as above with fixed

of primes

The proposition now follows from the fact that the isogeny class

5.
Group theory

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and assume that
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected. The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
5.0.1
below, which we will apply to
\mathbf{G}=J_{b}^{\prime}
in Section
6
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\subset\mathbf{G}
be the centraliser of a maximal split torus of
\mathbf{G}
, and let
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{der}}
be its derived subgroup. Then
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected because
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
is, see
[
36
]
*Proposition 12.14, and moreover
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{der}}
is anisotropic by construction. Therefore, it follows from
[
45
]
*Theorem 6.5 that there are finite extensions
K_{1},\cdots,K_{m}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and central division algebras
D_{i}
over
K_{i}
such that

(5.0.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{der}}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Res}_{K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}D_{i}^{1}.

Here
D_{i}^{1}
is the algebraic group over
K_{i}
which is the kernel of the reduced norm map
\operatorname{Nm}_{D_{i}/K_{i}}:D_{i}^{\times}\to\mathbb{G}_{m,K_{i}}
. We will write
\breve{K}_{i}
for the completion of a maximal unramified extension of
K_{i}
. We will write
\mathcal{P}([m])
for the set of all subsets
Y\subset\{1,\cdots,m\}=:[m].

Proposition 5.0.1
.

For each
Y\in\mathcal{P}([m])
let
T_{Y}
be a maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
such that: The torus
T_{Y}
contains a maximal
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-split torus, has maximal
\breve{K}_{i}
-split rank among maximal tori containing a maximal
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-split torus for
i\in Y
, and has minimal
\breve{K}_{i}
-split rank among maximal tori containing a maximal
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-split torus for
i\not\in Y
. Then the group
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is topologically generated by
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\bigcup_{Y\in\mathcal{P}([m])}T_{Y}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Maximal tori as in the statement of Proposition
5.0.1
exist, this will be clear from its proof.

5.1.
Semisimple anisotropic groups

Choose a finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and a central division algebra
D
over
K
of rank
n^{2}
. Maximal tori of
D^{\times}
correspond to subfields
F
of degree
n
of
D
, and each degree
n
field extension of
K
is such a subfield (see
[
41
]
*Remark IV.4.4.(c)). For a subfield
F
of degree
n
we will write
R^{1}_{F/K}\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\operatorname{Res}_{F/K}\mathbb{G}_{m}
for the kernel of the norm map
\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}:\operatorname{Res}_{F/K}\to\mathbb{G}_{m,K}
, which gives rise to a maximal torus of
D^{1}
.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Let
L\subset D
be a degree
n
subfield that is unramified over
K
and let
F\subset D
be a subfield of degree
n
that is totally ramified over
K
. Let
Z
be the center of
D^{1}
. Then any cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{1}(K,Z)
maps to zero in either
H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{L/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})
or
H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{F/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

Proof.

We know that
Z\simeq\mu_{n,K}
, and therefore
H^{1}(K,Z)=K^{\times}/K^{\times,n}
. A standard long exact sequence argument shows that
H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{L/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})=K^{\times}/\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}L^{\times}
and that
H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{F/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})=K^{\times}/\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}F^{\times}
. Moreover, under these identifications the natural maps
H^{1}(K,Z)\to H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{L/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
H^{1}(K,Z)\to H^{1}(K,R^{1}_{F/K}\mathbb{G}_{m})
correspond to the natural maps

(5.1.1)

\displaystyle K^{\times}/K^{\times,n}\to K^{\times}/\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}L^{\times},K^{\times}/\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}F^{\times}

corresponding to the inclusions

(5.1.2)

\displaystyle K^{\times,n}\subset\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}L^{\times},\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}F^{\times}.

The result follows because the group generated by
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}L^{\times}
and
\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}F^{\times}
is equal to
K^{\times}
. Indeed, by local class field theory the group generated by
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}L^{\times}
and
\operatorname{Nm}_{F/K}F^{\times}
is itself equal to
\operatorname{Nm}_{K^{\prime}/K}(K^{\prime})^{\times}
for a finite abelian extension
K^{\prime}
of
K
. Moreover, this extension
K^{\prime}
is equal to the intersection of
L
and
F
inside a maximal abelian extension
K^{\mathrm{ab}}
of
K
. Since
L
is unramified over
K
and
F
is totally ramified, this intersection is equal to
K
. Therefore we have an equality
\operatorname{Nm}_{K^{\prime}/K}(K^{\prime})^{\times}=\operatorname{Nm}_{K/K}(K)^{\times}=K^{\times}
and we are done.
∎

6.
Main theorems

In this section we will state the main theorems in full generality, giving Theorems
1
and
2
as special cases. We first recall our running assumptions and some notation.

6.0.1.

Let
(G,X)
be a Shimura datum of Hodge type with reflex field
E
and assume that
G^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected. Let
p>2
be a prime number such that
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is quasi-split and splits over a tamely ramified extension. Let
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
be a sufficiently small compact open subgroup and let
K_{p}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a connected very special parahoric subgroup, where “connected” is used in the sense of
[
54
, start of Section 2]
. (Note that hyperspecial parahoric subgroups are automatically connected, see
[
31
, Remark 4.2.14.b)]
. In particular, we don’t have to worry about this assumption when deducing the main theorems of the introduction.) Choose a Hodge embedding
(G,X)\to(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
and
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-lattice
V_{(p)}\subset V
on which
\psi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-valued, such that
K_{p}
is the stabiliser of
V_{p}
in
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
; this is always possible by the discussion in
[
30
, Section 1.3.2]
. Let
v|p
be a prime of
E
and let
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}
be the geometric special fiber of the integral model of the Shimura variety of level
K^{p}K_{p}
, see Section
2.1
.

6.0.2.

Let
[b]\in B(G,\{\mu^{-1}\})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-non-basic element as defined in Section
3
. Let
x\in\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
be an element contained in a distinguished central leaf (such
x
exist by Lemma
2.5.3
) and let
b=b_{x}
be as in the first paragraph of Section
3
. Let
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\to C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}
be the Igusa variety associated to
x
as constructed in Section
2.2
. For
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, we will also consider the Igusa variety as a pro-étale
H_{y}
-torsor over
C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}
using Corollary
2.3.5
. In this notation, the Igusa variety
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\to C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}
corresponds to
y=1\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, and we will write
H=H_{1}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
for its stabiliser.

We write
J_{b}
for the
\sigma
-centraliser of
b
. Note that
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected since
J_{b}
is an inner form of a Levi subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, see
[
36
]
*Proposition 12.14.

Assumption 6.0.3
.

The group
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
has no compact factors.

This assumption means that if we write
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
as a product of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-almost-simple groups
\mathbf{G}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{G}_{n}
, then
\mathbf{G}_{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is not compact in the
p
-adic topology for any
i
. Since
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply connected, this is equivalent to asking the same for
J_{b}^{\mathrm{ad}}
.

Recall the subgroup
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\supset J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, which is the inverse image of
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
under
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Since
H_{y}
is contained in a parahoric subgroup of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, for example by the proof of Lemma
2.5.4
, it is contained in
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by Proposition
2.6.1
. We can now state our main theorem.

Theorem 6.0.4
.

Let
(G,X)
and
[b]
be as above. If Assumptions
3.1.2
and
6.0.3
hold, then for
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
the natural map

(6.0.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})

is surjective with fibers in bijection with
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, equivariant for the action of
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(which stabilises the fibers by Lemma
3.1.4
). In particular, the identification is
H_{y}
-equivariant for the natural action of
H_{y}
on the fibers.

Assumption
3.1.2
holds true if
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
splits over an unramified extension by
[
24
]
*Theorem 4.5.2; the assumption in
[
24
]
*Theorem 4.5.2 that
[
24
, Conjecture 4.3.1]
holds is satisfied when
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, see
[
24
, Remark 4.3.2]
. In particular, Theorem
6.0.4
implies Theorem
1
. Moreover, Assumption
3.1.2
also holds when
\mathbf{Sh}_{K^{p}K_{p}}(G,X)
is proper, by
[
24
, Theorem 4.5.2]
.

Remark 6.0.5
.

When
[b]
is the
\mu
-ordinary element (see Section
2.1.3
for the definition), then
J_{b}
is quasi-split, which implies that Assumption
6.0.3
holds. Moreover, Assumption
3.1.2
holds because in this case
C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}=\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}
and
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}\subset\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}
is dense by
[
30
, Theorem 3]
. Indeed, the assumption that
\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}
is locally integral in the statement of
[
30
, Theorem 3]
holds because
K_{p}
is very special, see
[
31
, Corollary 4.6.26]
.

Proof of Theorem
6.0.4
.

Step 1: The group
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
V
.
Fix a connected component
V
of
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}
. By Proposition
3.4.4
and Assumption
3.1.2
, the group
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
. By Assumption
6.0.3
we have
J_{b,\mathrm{iso}}^{\mathrm{der}}=J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}
and so
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
. By Corollary
4.3.2
we can find, for every isomorphism class of maximal tori of
J_{b}^{\prime}
, a representative
T^{\prime}\subset J_{b}^{\prime}
such that
T^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
stabilises
V
.

Recall that
J_{b}^{\prime}
is connected reductive by Lemma
2.1.6
. It thus follows from Proposition
5.0.1
that we can find maximal tori
T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}
of
J_{b}^{\prime}
, which can be specified up to isomorphism, such that the group topologically generated by
T_{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\cdots,T_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
J_{b}^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is equal to
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Since the stabiliser of
V
in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is closed, see the proof of Lemma
4.1.3
, it follows that
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
V
. Since
V
was chosen arbitrarily, this implies that
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
.

Step 2: The theorem for
y=1
.
Assumption
3.1.2
tells us that

(6.0.2)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})

is a bijection, and therefore the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
are in bijection with the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}})
. Now
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\to C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}
is an
H
-torsor and the image of monodromy is contained in
H^{\prime}=J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap H
by Proposition
3.4.4
. Recall that the natural map
C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}\to C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}
is a homeomorphism. The fact that
H^{\prime}
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
then implies that the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}})
are in bijection with
H/H^{\prime}
. Since
C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}
is a distinguished central leaf, it follows from Lemma
2.5.4
that there are parahoric subgroups
J,J^{\prime}
of
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that
J\subset H\subset J^{\prime}
. In particular, it follows from Proposition
2.6.1
that
H/H^{\prime}\simeq G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Now
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
stabilises the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
by Lemma
3.1.4
and since
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})
this action factors through an action of
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Since
H
surjects onto
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, we see that our identification of the fibers with
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is also
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant.

Step 3: The theorem for arbitrary y.
For
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
we consider the morphism

(6.0.3)

\displaystyle\pi_{\infty}(-,y):\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\})\to\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}).

We note that this morphism only depends on the connected component of
X_{\{\nu\}}(b)
containing
\{y\}
. For
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
lying in the same component as
1\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, the theorem therefore follows from the discussion above. Since
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts transitively on
\pi_{0}(X_{\{\nu\}}(b))
by
[
24
, Theorem A.1.3]
, it suffices to prove the result for
y\in\operatorname{Orb}(1)
, where
\operatorname{Orb}(1)\subset X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is the
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-orbit of
1
.

But for
j\in J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
we have
\pi_{\infty}(z,j\cdot 1)=\pi_{\infty}(j^{-1}z,1)
and so the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(-,j\cdot 1)
can be identified with the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(j^{-1}z,1)
under the isomorphism
j:\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\to\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}
. This identification of the fibers is
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant for the precomposition of the natural
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-action on the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(-,j\cdot 1)
with conjugation by
j
(considered as an automorphism of
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
).

Since the action of
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(-,1)
identifies these fibers with principal homogeneous spaces for
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, the same is true for the
j
-twisted action of
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
on the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(-,j\cdot 1)
. Since
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is abelian, it follows that the untwisted
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-action on the fibers of
\pi_{\infty}(-,j\cdot 1)
also identifies these fibers with principal homogeneous spaces for
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
; the theorem is proved.
∎

We now state the general version of Corollary
1.1.3
.

Corollary 6.0.6
.

Let
(G,X)
and
[b]
be as above. If Assumptions
3.1.2
and
6.0.3
hold, then for
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, the natural map

(6.0.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(C_{y,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})

is surjective with finite fibers. Moreover, the fibers are in bijection with
H_{y}\backslash G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(C_{y,K^{p}})
be the map induced from
\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\}\to C_{y,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}
. Then the fibers of this map are a subset of the fibers of the composition
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
of our map with
\pi_{0}(C_{y,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
. To determine this subset, we observe that

\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\{y\}\to C_{y,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}

is a
H_{y}
-torsor by Corollary
2.3.5
. Moreover,
H_{y}\subset J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts on the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
with stabiliser
H_{y}^{\prime}:=H_{y}\cap J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, by the
J_{b}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariance of Theorem
6.0.4
.

Therefore the fibers of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(C_{y,K^{p}})
can be identified with
H_{y}/H_{y}^{\prime}\subset G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and the fibers of
\pi_{0}(C_{y,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
can be identified with
H_{y}\backslash G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.
∎

Now we state the generalisation of Theorem
2
. If

is hyperspecial, then the representation-theoretic constant

.

Theorem 6.0.7
.

Let
(G,X)
and
[b]
be as above. If Assumptions
3.1.2
and
6.0.3
hold, then for
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
the natural map

(6.0.5)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})

(6.0.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dim}V^{\hat{H}}_{\underline{\mu}}(\lambda_{b})_{\text{rel}},

introduced in
[

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3.4
there is a commutative diagram

(6.0.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times\operatorname{Orb}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ig}_{[b],K^{p}}\times X_{\{\nu\}}(b)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sh}_{G,[b],K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}^{\mathrm{perf}}.}

Assumption
3.1.2
tells us that the composite map

induces a bijection

(6.0.8)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b\rrbracket,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}}).

To prove the first part of the theorem, it thus suffices to prove that

6.1.
A conjectural description of the connected components of Igusa varieties

By Corollary
3.1.5
, the group
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts transitively on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})
. Moreover, under Assumptions
3.1.2
and
6.0.3
the group
J_{b}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts trivially by the proof of Theorem
6.0.4
, and the group
G^{\mathrm{der}}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\Sigma})
acts trivially by Lemma
3.1.6
. We have the following conjectural description of
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})
.

Conjecture 6.1.1
.

There is a
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-equivariant isomorphism of topological spaces

(6.1.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]})=G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q})^{{\dagger}}\setminus G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{A}_{f}),

where
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
acts via
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and where
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q})^{{\dagger}}
is as in Section
2.4
.

Remark 6.1.2
.

Assuming the conjecture, we get an automorphic description of
H^{0}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\operatorname{Ig}_{[b]},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
as in
[
34
, Theorem A]
. Indeed, there is a
G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-equivariant bijection

(6.1.2)

\displaystyle G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{Q})^{{\dagger}}\setminus G^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})=\varprojlim_{K}\pi_{0}(\mathbf{Sh}_{K,\mathbb{C}}(G,X)),

and the zeroth étale cohomology of the right-hand side
has

6.2.
The discrete Hecke orbit conjecture

For the benefit of the reader, we recall the statement of the discrete Hecke orbit conjecture and its stronger version from
[
34
]
. Let the notation be as in Section
2
, then the following two conjectures are
[
34
, Question 8.2.1, last two bullet points]
.

Conjecture 6.2.1

(The strong discrete Hecke orbit conjecture)
.

The natural map
\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
is a bijection for all
y
in
X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and all
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
.

Conjecture 6.2.2

(The discrete Hecke orbit conjecture)
.

If
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, then
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
acts transitively on
\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket})
for all
y
in
X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
.

If
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is not ramified, then
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
does not necessarily act transitively on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})
, see
[
42
]
for explicit counterexamples. Therefore the assumption that
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified is necessary in Conjecture
6.2.2
. Conjecture
6.2.2
follows from Conjecture
6.1.1
using weak approximation for the torus
G^{\mathrm{ab}}
, which holds since
G^{\mathrm{ab}}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
splits over an unramified extension by the assumptions of
6.2.2
. Note that Conjecture
6.2.2
is proved by Kret–Shin as
[
34
, Theorem 8.2.6]
.

Conjecture
6.2.1
implies Conjecture
6.2.2
because
G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
acts transitively on
\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G})
when
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is unramified, see
[
29
, Lemma 2.2.5]
and
[
35
, Corollary 4.1.11]
.

6.3.
A counterexample to the strong version of the discrete Hecke orbit conjecture

The purpose of this section is to show that Conjecture
6.2.1
is false; we will present below a counterexample communicated to us by Rong Zhou. As noted in Remark
2.1.4
, it would be interesting to find a counterexample with
(G,X)=(\mathcal{G}_{V},\mathcal{H}_{V})
.

Our counterexample involves a unitary Shimura variety of PEL type. Let
F=F^{+}E
be a CM field where
F^{+}
is totally real of degree
4
and
E
is an imaginary quadratic field. Let
V
be a Hermitian
F
-vector space of rank
2
with signature
(1,1)
at all infinite places of
F^{+}
and let
(G,X)=(\operatorname{GU}_{V},X_{V})
be the corresponding Shimura datum of PEL type.

Let
p>2
be a prime which splits in
E
and which splits as
p=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
in
F^{+}
, such that both
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
have residual degree
2
. Then

(6.3.1)

\displaystyle G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\simeq\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}\times\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}\times\mathbb{G}_{m},

where
K
is the unique unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
[b]\in B(G,\{\mu^{-1}\})
be the unique element which is
\mu_{1}
-ordinary in the first factor and which is basic in the second factor and third factor (see the third paragraph of Section
2.1.3
for a definition of these terms). Here
\{\mu\}=(\{\mu_{1}\},\{\mu_{2}\},\{\mu_{3}\})
according to the product decomposition of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Let
b\in[b]
and write
b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
using the product decomposition of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. There is an induced product decomposition

(6.3.2)

\displaystyle X_{\{\nu\}}(b)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}X_{\{\nu_{i}\}}(b_{i}),

where
\{\nu_{i}\}=\{\sigma(\mu_{i}^{-1})\}
. Thus an element
y\in X_{\{\nu\}}(b)(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
has the form
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
and so its stabiliser
H_{y}\subset J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=J_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times J_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times J_{b_{3}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
can be written as a product

(6.3.3)

\displaystyle H_{y}=H_{y_{1}}\times H_{y_{2}}\times H_{y_{3}}.

By Corollary
6.0.6
, if we can find some
y_{2}\in X_{\{\nu_{2}\}}(b_{2})
such that
H_{y_{2}}
does not surject onto the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-points of the maximal abelian quotient of
\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}
, then
\pi_{0}(C_{\llbracket b_{y}\rrbracket,K^{p}})\to\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Sh}_{G,K^{p}})
is not a bijection and thus Conjecture
6.2.1
will be false.

In this case, there is an isomorphism
J_{b_{2}}\simeq\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}
and
X_{\{\nu_{2}\}}(b_{2})
is equidimensional of dimension
1
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

The irreducible components of
X_{\{\nu_{2}\}}(b_{2})
are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The stabiliser of an irreducible component is a hyperspecial subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(K)
, conjugate to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, which acts on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
via the natural map
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
.

Proof.

It follows from the main result of
[
17
]
that
X_{\{\nu_{2}\}}(b_{2})
is a union of two one-dimensional Ekedahl–Oort strata and one zero-dimensional Ekedahl–Oort stratum. The irreducible components are the closures of the one-dimensional Ekedahl–Oort strata. It follows from
[
17
, Section 5.10]
that the irreducible components are unions of (closures of) classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties for the group
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}
. Therefore, their irreducible components are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
factors through the natural action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
.
∎

A direct computation shows that we can find a point
a\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
such that its stabiliser in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
is given by the group of scalar matrices. Note that this stabiliser does not surject onto
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}
via the determinant map. This implies that the stabiliser of
a
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, which is the stabiliser of
a\in X_{\{\nu_{2}\}}(b_{2})(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
in
J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, does not surject onto
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
via the determinant map.

A rate-independent model for geomaterials under compression coupling strain gradient plasticity and damage
Maicol Caponi, Vito Crismale

Abstract
. We study a strain gradient-enhanced version of a model for geomaterials under compression by Marigo and Kazymyrenko (2019) coupling damage and small-strain associative plasticity. We prove that the jumps in time of the plastic variable may happen only along jumps of the damage variable. Moreover, we perform a vanishing-viscosity analysis showing existence of Balanced Viscosity quasistatic solutions à la Mielke-Rossi-Savaré.



Keywords
: variational models, quasistatic evolution, energetic solutions, vanishing-viscosity,
Drucker-Prager elasto-plasticity, gradient damage models, complete damage, strain gradient plasticity.

74C05, 74R05, 74G65, 35Q74, 49J45.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study a strain gradient-enhanced version of a model for geomaterials under compression by Marigo and Kazymyrenko
[
25
]
coupling damage and small-strain associative plasticity, proving a better regularity in time for the corresponding evolutions.

The model of
[
25
]
is based on a micromechanical analysis, accounting for microcracks inside the material as the main responsible of the progressive loss of rigidity and then for the macroscopic elasto-plastic coupled behavior.

Given a
reference domain

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denoting by
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
the
displacement field
, in the general framework of small-strain elasto-plasticity the total strain
\mathrm{E}u=(\nabla u)^{\mathrm{sym}}\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
is additively decomposed into
elastic strain
and
plastic strain
:

\mathrm{E}u=e+p

and the
stress tensor

\sigma\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
depends linearly on
e
according to Hooke’s law

\sigma=\mathbb{C}e,

where
\mathbb{C}
is the fourth order positive definite
Hooke’s tensor
, with
\sigma
lying in a fixed closed and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
(the
constraint set
) and satisfying equilibrium conditions involving the external loads.

In order to describe the model in
[
25
]
, consider the reference case of a triaxial test - a shear compression plus a compression normal to the shear plane - and the observed phenomenon of
dilatance
, i.e., the irreversible increase of volume in compression. A micromechanical justification is that in the shear compression the volume may increase with that of the internal voids between the granuli, interpreted as microcracks, due to a less efficient particle organization; conversely, in the normal compression a part of these microcracks may be closed, preventing a free sliding and thus the free relaxation to the initial configuration in unloading.

Then a
damage variable

\alpha\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to[0,1]
is considered in
[
25
]
, related to the density
of closed microcracks and reflecting into an internal blocked energy depending on plastic strain,
being related
to irreversible deformations.
Assuming Coulomb law for the sliding with friction between the lips of closed microcracks, plasticity follows a standard Drucker-Prager criterion with an associative flow rule, that is the plastic flow is normal to the constraint set
K
.
Moreover, damage is assumed to be an irreversible process (consequently
\alpha
is nonincreasing in time) and the growth of the microcracks is modelled by the terms usually present in gradient damage models

D(\alpha(t))+l_{\alpha}\|\nabla\alpha(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

l_{\alpha}>0
, cf.
[
30
]
,
[
4
, Section 4]
,
[
25
, Remark 3.1]
.

In
[
10
]
the existence of quasistatic evolutions for such a model is proven, fulfilling the notion of
energetic solutions
by Mielke and Theil
[
29
]
: this is based on a
global stability condition
, which prescribes at each time the minimization, among the admissible configurations, of the total internal energy plus the dissipation potential, and on an
energy-dissipation balance
between the total variation in time of the internal energy, the total dissipated energy, and the work of the external loadings.

In
[
35
]
the model has been enriched by including, still employing an irreversible degradation function
\alpha
, also the tensile response. This accounts for the fact that the evolution of opening microcracks in tension leads to brittle behavior and mode I fracture. Conversely, the response in compression shares many features with
[
25
]
, but plasticity is required to satisfy a non-associative flow rule.

The present contribution focuses on a different modification of the model in
[
25
]
, obtained by formally including in the internal energy the
L^{2}
norm of the plastic strain gradient, times a dimensional (length) constant. Terms involving higher order derivatives are considered on the one hand by their capability to capture the correct scale size of shear bands, usually observed in failure in soils induced by shearing, and on the other hand to avoid mesh dependence in numerical simulations.
Inspired by the work of Aifantis
[
2
]
, several plastic strain gradient models have been proposed, for both crystalline and granular materials (e.g.
[
19
,
18
,
31
,
20
,
32
,
6
,
26
,
36
]
).

The framework of our strain gradient enhancement is that of the
explicit gradient theories
, in which the gradient terms typically evolve according to ordinary differential equations determined by constitutive relations; in particular we follow the lines of
[
19
,
20
,
32
]
.

Therefore, the stored energy at time
t
is the sum of the elastic energy of the sound material, of the kinematical hardening term (depending on
\alpha
), and of the strain gradient term

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}e(t,x):e(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{B}(\alpha(t,x))p(t,x)\colon p(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x+l_{p}\|\nabla p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for
\mathbb{B}
a fourth-order tensor positive semidefinite (usually positive definite except for
\alpha=0
). The gradient term, which enforces the plasticity to be in
H^{1}
(in space), introduces a characteristic internal length related to the scale of the observed shear bands. In this setting, the plastic dissipation from a plastic strain
q
is the functional

\int_{\Omega}H(p(t,x)-q(x))\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad H(\xi)=\sup_{\sigma\in K}\sigma\colon\xi.

We observe that in the original model
p(t)
is in general not in
L^{2}
in the set
\{\alpha=0\}
, so shear bands with infinitesimal thickness may therein display. Therefore, in
[
10
]
the dissipation from a plastic strain
q
is the relaxation of the functional above for
p(t)
a bounded Radon measure.

In the present setting, the additional regularity of the plastic strain with respect to
[
10
]
makes the existence of energetic solutions much easier to prove; the core of the paper is the study of the regularity in time of the evolutions.
In fact, with general nonconvex total energies, evolutions may display jumps in time. Basing on standard techniques we show in Lemma
3.9
that energetic solutions, whose existence is proven in Theorems
3.8
, are strongly continuous outside an at most countable set of time instants.

Further, we develop a more precise analysis exploiting the particular structure of the model: we prove that the variations in time of the whole set of variables with respect to the target norms are controlled in terms of the variation in time of the damage in the
L^{1}(\Omega)
-norm, plus that of the external loading (cf. Theorem
3.15
for the precise statement). This ensures that, as soon as damage does not jump, the whole evolution, and in particular the plastic strain, has no time discontinuities.
Our analysis is in the spirit of
[
13
]
, which proves that small strain perfect plasticity is absolutely continuous in time.

The interplay between damage and plastic strain is a key point in the analysis of coupled damage-plasticity models. We consider, for instance, the uniaxial responses in
[
4
]
, a reference work for the variational analysis of such coupling, and in
[
25
]
: in
[
4
]
it is shown that strain concentrates into ‘cohesive crack points’ where the space derivative of the damage is discontinuous; in
[
25
]
, assuming solutions smooth in time and a linear
D
, it is observed that damage evolves only with plasticity.

By inspecting our proof it is apparent that the strain gradient regularization is crucial for the improved regularity. This could suggest, in analogous cases with gradient plasticity, to carefully verify if jumps in time of plasticity but not of damage are analytically ruled out and if this is in accordance with the expected experimental results.

In the final part of the paper we study the existence of quasistatic evolution obtained through a vanishing viscosity approximation, satisfying the notion of Balanced Viscosity (BV) solutions, see Definition
5.1
.

BV solutions, which have been described in an ‘abstract’ context in
[
17
]
and the subsequent
[
27
,
28
]
, are obtained trough the approximation of a rate-independent system by viscously perturbed system and provide a selection criterion for solutions with a mechanically feasible behavior at jumps, as well as a description of trajectories joining states before and after jumps. In fact,
the notion of energetic solutions is not satisfactory from the physical point of view, whenever jumps develop in time: these jumps may appear ‘too long and too early’, being forced to overtake energy barriers between energy wells (see the characterization in
[
33
]
and the references therein).

The vanishing-viscosity technique has also been adopted in various concrete applications, ranging from plasticity (cf., e.g.,
[
14
,
16
,
15
,
7
,
21
,
34
]
), to damage, fracture, and fatigue (see for instance
[
22
,
24
,
23
,
11
,
5
,
3
,
12
]
).

We show existence of BV solutions (cf. Theorem
5.4
), mostly taking advantage of the analysis carried out for the regularity result. Arguing similarly, we show that as soon as the damage variable is regularized in time with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm (in space), then all the evolution gains the same regularity in time, in the energy space.
Moreover, using an argument from
[
23
,
11
]
, we get that the evolution is absolutely continuous in time uniformly respect to the viscosity parameter.
Eventually, such regularity permits follow the lines of
[
11
]
to conclude existence of Balanced Viscosity solutions.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
we fix the notation used throughout the paper and we introduced the mathematical framework of our model. In Section
3
we first prove in Theorem
3.8
the existence of global quasistatic evolutions, and then in Theorem
3.15
we investigate their regularity in time. We continue with Section
4
, where we introduced a
\varepsilon
-regularization in time with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm in space of the damage variable, and in Theorem
4.14
we prove the existence of
\varepsilon
-approximate viscous evolution. Finally, in Section
5
we prove the existence of BV solutions (see Theorem
5.4
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

The space of
n\times n
symmetric matrices with real entries is denoted by
\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
. Given two matrices
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
, their scalar product is denoted by
A_{1}\colon A_{2}
and the induced norm of
A\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
by
|A|
. The identity matrix is denoted by
I\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
.

For a measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we use
\mathcal{L}^{n}(B)
to denote the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of
B
. By
\chi_{B}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\{0,1\}
we denote the corresponding characteristic function. Given an open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set of all distributions on
\Omega
, namely the continuous dual space of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, endowed with the strong dual topology, is denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. We adopt standard notation for Lebesgue spaces on measurable subsets
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and Sobolev spaces on open subsets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. According to the context, we use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(B)}
to denote the norm in
L^{p}(B)
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. A similar convention is also used to denote the norms in Sobolev spaces. The boundary values of a Sobolev function are always intended in the sense of traces.

The partial derivatives with respect to the variable
x_{i}
are denoted by
\partial_{i}
. Given an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote its Jacobian matrix by
\nabla u
, whose components are
(\nabla u)_{ij}\coloneqq\partial_{j}u_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
and
j=1,\dots,n
. For a function
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
we use
\mathrm{E}u\coloneqq(\nabla u)^{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T})\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
to denote the symmetric part of the gradient. Given a tensor field
F\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
, by
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits F
we mean its divergence with respect to lines, namely
(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits F)_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}F_{ij}
for
i=1,\dots,n
.

2.2.
Mathematical framework

We fix the mathematical framework of the model of complete damage and strain gradient plasticity presented in the introduction. For simplicity, all the physical constants are fixed to 1.

Throughout the paper the
reference configuration

\Omega
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n=2,3
. The
displacement field
is represented by a function
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we decompose its
total strain
as
\mathrm{E}u=e+p
, where
e
denotes the
elastic strain
and
p
the
plastic strain
. To simplify the notation, we set

\mathcal{A}\coloneqq H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}).

Given a function
w\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the set of admissible displacements and strains for the boundary datum
w
on
\partial\Omega
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(w)\coloneqq\{(u,e,p)\in\mathcal{A}\,:\,\text{$\mathrm{E}u=e+p$ in $\Omega$, $u=w$ on $\partial\Omega$}\}.

Remark 2.1
.

In this model we require a higher regularity in the plastic strain
p
, which usually is only a Radon measure. This property is ensured by the addition in the total energy (
2.8
) of the
L^{2}
norm of the plastic strain gradient term.

We fix
T>0
. Let us consider a time-dependent boundary displacement
w
satisfying

(2.1)

w\in H^{1}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})).

The damage variable is a function
\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
. Given
\alpha^{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
we set

\mathcal{D}(\alpha^{0})\coloneqq\{\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\,:\,\alpha\leq\alpha^{0}\},

so that

\mathcal{D}(\alpha^{2})\subset\mathcal{D}(\alpha^{1})\quad\text{for all $\alpha^{2}\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha^{1})$}.

The
energy dissipated in damage growth
is the functional
D\colon H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\to[0,\infty)
given by

D(\alpha)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}d(\alpha(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])$},

where
d\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle d\in C^{1,1}([0,1];[0,\infty)),\qquad\dot{d}(0)\leq 0

(notice that the assumption
\dot{d}(0)\leq 0
is compatible with the ones proposed in
[
25
]
).
The
elastic energy
is the functional
\mathcal{Q}\colon L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty)
given by

\mathcal{Q}(e)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}e(x):e(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all $e\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

where
\mathbb{C}\in\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits(\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym};\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{ijkl}=\mathbb{C}_{klij}=\mathbb{C}_{jikl}\quad\text{for all $i,j,k,l=1,\dots,n$},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathbb{C}\xi:\xi\leq\gamma_{2}|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$},

with constants
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
. In particular, it holds

|\mathbb{C}\xi|\leq\gamma_{2}|\xi|\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$}.

The
kinematical hardening term
is the functional
\tilde{Q}\colon H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty)
given by

\tilde{Q}(\alpha,p)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{B}(\alpha(x))p(x):p(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])$ and $p\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

where
\mathbb{B}\colon[0,1]\to\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits(\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym};\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
satisfies

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}\in C^{1,1}([0,1];\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits(\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym};\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})),\qquad\dot{\mathbb{B}}(0)\xi:\xi\leq 0\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{ijkl}(\alpha)=\mathbb{B}_{klij}(\alpha)=\mathbb{B}_{jikl}(\alpha)\quad\text{for all $i,j,k,l=1,\dots,n$ and $\alpha\in[0,1]$},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(\alpha)\xi:\xi\geq 0\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$}.

Remark 2.2
.

Thanks to (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we derive the Cauchy-Schwartz Inequality

|\mathbb{B}(\alpha)\xi_{1}:\xi_{2}|^{2}\leq(\mathbb{B}(\alpha)\xi_{1}:\xi_{1})(\mathbb{B}(\alpha)\xi_{2}:\xi_{2})\quad\text{for all $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$}.

Define the
total energy

\mathcal{E}\colon H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty)
as

(2.8)

\mathcal{E}(\alpha,e,p)\coloneqq\mathcal{Q}(e)+D(\alpha)+\|\nabla\alpha\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{Q}(\alpha,p)+\|\nabla p\|_{2}^{2}.

Remark 2.3
.

By the Sobolev Embedding Theorem, for
n=2,3
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
we have that the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous for all
\theta\in[1,6]
and compact for all
\theta\in[1,6)
. In particular, by (
2.5
) for all
\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
,
\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, and
p,q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
we have:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{B}(\alpha(x))p(x):q(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\mathbb{B}\|_{\infty}\|p\|_{2}\|q\|_{2},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha(x))\beta(x)p(x):q(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\dot{\mathbb{B}}\|_{\infty}\|\beta\|_{2}\|p\|_{4}\|q\|_{4},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}[\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{1}(x))-\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{2}(x))]p(x):q(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\dot{\mathbb{B}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|_{2}\|p\|_{4}\|q\|_{4},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}[\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{1}(x))-\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{2}(x))]\beta(x)p(x):q(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\ddot{\mathbb{B}}\|_{\infty}\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|_{4}\|\beta\|_{4}\|p\|_{4}\|q\|_{4}.

The
constraint set
for the stress tensor
\sigma
is identified by a closed and convex set
K\subset\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
satisfying

(2.9)

\{\sigma\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}\,:\,|\sigma|\leq r_{H}\}\subset K

for a radius
r_{H}>0
.

Remark 2.4
.

Notice that
K
can be unbounded. In particular, as in
[
10
]
, we can consider constraint set of the form

K\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}\,:\,\tau\sigma_{m}+|\sigma_{D}|-\kappa\geq 0\}

for
\tau,\kappa>0
, where
\sigma_{m}\coloneqq\frac{\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\sigma}{n}\in\mathbb{R}
is the
mean stress
and
\sigma_{D}\coloneqq\sigma-\sigma_{m}I
is the
deviatoric part
of
\sigma
.

Let us consider the support function
H\colon\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}\to[0,\infty]
of
K
, which is defined as

H(\xi)\coloneqq\sup_{\sigma\in K}\sigma:\xi\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$}.

Clearly
H
is convex, lower semicontinuous, and positively 1-homogeneous. Moreover

(2.10)

r_{H}|\xi|\leq H(\xi)\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$}.

The
plastic potential

\mathcal{H}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty]
is defined as

\mathcal{H}(p)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}H(p(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all $p\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Lemma 2.5
.

The functional
\mathcal{H}
is lower semicontinuous in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
.

Proof.

Let
(p_{k})_{k}\subset L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and
p\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
be such that
p_{k}\to p
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. We consider a subsequence
(p_{k_{j}})_{j}\subset(p_{k})_{k}
satisfying

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(p_{k_{j}})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}),\qquad p_{k_{j}}(x)\to p(x)\quad\text{for a.e.\ $x\in\Omega$}.

Since
H\colon\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous, by Fatou Lemma we have

\mathcal{H}(p)=\int_{\Omega}H(p(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\liminf_{j\to\infty}H(p_{k_{j}}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}H(p_{k_{j}}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(p_{k_{j}})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}),

which implies the lower semicontinuous of
\mathcal{H}
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Since
\mathcal{H}\colon L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty]
is convex, from Lemma
2.5
we derive that
\mathcal{H}
weakly (sequentially) lower semicontinuous in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. By arguing in the same way, we derive that also the functional
p\mapsto\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{H}(p(t))\,\mathrm{d}t
is lower semicontinuous in
L^{1}([t_{1},t_{2}];L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}))
for all
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,T]
.

Given
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,T]
and
p\colon[t_{1},t_{2}]\to L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, the
\mathcal{H}
-dissipation of
p
on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
is defined as

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;t_{1},t_{2})\coloneqq\sup\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}(p(s_{j})-p(s_{j-1}))\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,t_{1}=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{N-1}<s_{N}=t_{2}\right\}.

We recall that this notion has been introduced in
[
13
, Appendix]
. By (
2.10
) we have

(2.11)

r_{H}\mathcal{V}(p;t_{1},t_{2})\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;t_{1},t_{2}),

where
\mathcal{V}(p;t_{1},t_{2})
is the total variation of
p\colon[t_{1},t_{2}]\to L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, which is defined as

\mathcal{V}(p;t_{1},t_{2})\coloneqq\sup\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{\|}p(s_{j})-p(s_{j-1})\|_{1}\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,t_{1}=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{N-1}<s_{N}=t_{2}\right\}.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
[t_{1},t_{1}]\subset[0,T]
and
p_{k}\colon[t_{1},t_{2}]\to L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy

p_{k}(r)\rightharpoonup p(r)\quad\text{in $L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$ for all $r\in[t_{1},t_{2}]$}.

Then

(2.12)

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;t_{1},t_{2})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p_{k};t_{1},t_{2}).

Proof.

We fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a collection of points
(s_{j})_{j=0}^{N}
such that

(2.13)

t_{1}=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{N-1}<s_{N}=t_{2}.

For all
j=1,\dots,n
we have

p_{k}(s_{j})-p_{k}(s_{j-1})\rightharpoonup p(s_{j})-p(s_{j-1})\quad\text{in $L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$}.

Therefore, by Remark
2.6

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}(p(s_{j})-p(s_{j-1}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}(s_{j})-p_{k}(s_{j-1}))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}(s_{j})-p_{k}(s_{j-1}))\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p_{k};t_{1},t_{2}).

By taking the supremum over
N\in\mathbb{N}
and all collections of points
(s_{j})_{j=0}^{N}
satisfying (
2.13
) we get (
2.12
).
∎

We recall the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

For all
\theta\in[2,6)
and
\delta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\theta,\delta}>0
such that

\|u\|_{\theta}^{2}\leq C_{\theta,\delta}\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\delta\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all $u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Proof.

We fix
\theta\in[2,6)
and
\delta>0
and we argue by contradiction. We assume that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
u_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\|u_{k}\|_{\theta}^{2}>k\|u_{k}\|_{1}^{2}+\delta\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

We set
\hat{u}_{k}\coloneqq\frac{u_{k}}{\|u_{k}\|_{\theta}}
and we have

\|\hat{u}_{k}\|_{\theta}=1,\quad 1>k\|\hat{u}_{k}\|_{1}^{2}+\delta\|\hat{\nabla}u_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

In particular, by the continuity of the embedding
L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
we deduce the existence of a constant
C_{\theta}>0
such that

\|\hat{u}_{k}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\|\hat{u}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\hat{u}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\delta}\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

By the Sobolev Embedding Theorem there exists a function
\hat{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|\hat{u}\|_{\theta}=1
and a subsequence
(\hat{u}_{k_{j}})_{j}\subset(\hat{u}_{k})_{k}
such that

\hat{u}_{k_{j}}\to\hat{u}\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$ as $j\to\infty$}.

On the other hand,

\|\hat{u}_{k}\|_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{k}\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$},

which gives

\hat{u}_{k}\to 0\quad\text{in $L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$ as $k\to\infty$}.

This implies that
\hat{u}=0
, which contradicts
\|\hat{u}\|_{\theta}=1
.
∎

The following is an easy consequence of Lemma
2.8
.

Corollary 2.9
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.14)

\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)\quad\text{for all $u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
with
\theta=2
and
\delta=1
there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{1}\|u\|_{1}^{2}+2\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\max\{2,C_{1}\}\left(\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right),

which gives (
2.14
)
∎

3.
Global quasistatic evolutions

In this section we prove Theorem
3.8
, i.e., the existence of global quasistatic evolutions (also referred to as energetic solutions) for the model of geomaterials coupling damage and small-strain associative plasticity introduced before. Moreover, in Theorem
3.15
we perform a detailed analysis of the regularity in time of these evolutions.

3.1.
Definition and preliminary results

We start by introducing the mathematical formulation of our model of geomaterials. Following
[
10
]
and the definition of energetic solution of
[
29
]
, we give the following notion of global quasistatic evolutions.

Definition 3.1
.

Assume (
2.1
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). A quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
is a
global quasistatic evolution
with datum
w
if:

(qs0)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
the map

t\mapsto\alpha(t,x)\quad\text{is non increasing on $[0,T]$};

(qs1)

(u(t),e(t),p(t))\in\mathcal{A}(w(t))
for all
t\in[0,T]
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p(t))\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha(t))\times\mathcal{A}(w(t))$;}

(qs2)

p\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
has bounded
\mathcal{H}
-variation and for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,t)=\mathcal{E}(\alpha(0),e(0),p(0))+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}e(s),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s.

Remark 3.2
.

The first condition (qs0) models the
irreversibility
of the damage process, (qs1) is the
global stability condition
, and (qs2) is the
energy-dissipation balance
.

Let us check that the integral in (qs2) is well defined. To this aim, we first prove the following stability result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
). Let
w_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
(\alpha_{k},u_{k},e_{k},p_{k})\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that as
k\to\infty

\displaystyle u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\rightharpoonup\alpha_{\infty}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle e_{k}\rightharpoonup e_{\infty}\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle p_{k}\rightharpoonup p_{\infty}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle w_{k}\rightharpoonup w_{\infty}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Then
(\alpha_{\infty},e_{\infty},p_{\infty})\in\mathcal{A}(w_{\infty})
. If in addition

(3.1)

\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k},e_{k},p_{k})\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{k})\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k})$},

then

(3.2)

\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{\infty},e_{\infty},p_{\infty})\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{\infty})\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{\infty})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{\infty})$}.

Proof.

We argue as in
[
9
, Theorem 3.6]
. Since

\mathrm{E}u_{k}=e_{k}+p_{k}\quad\text{in $\Omega$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$},\qquad u_{k}=w_{k}\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$ for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$},

by passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
and using the continuity of the trace operator, we derive that

\mathrm{E}u_{\infty}=e_{\infty}+p_{\infty}\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\qquad u_{\infty}=w_{\infty}\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.

Therefore,
(u_{\infty},e_{\infty},p_{\infty})\in\mathcal{A}(w_{\infty})
.

We fix
(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{\infty})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{\infty})
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we set

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\coloneqq\beta\wedge\alpha_{k},\qquad v_{k}\coloneqq v-u_{\infty}+u_{k},\qquad\eta_{k}\coloneqq\eta-e_{\infty}+e_{k},\qquad q_{k}\coloneqq q-p_{\infty}+p_{k}.

By construction
(\beta_{k},v_{k},\eta_{k},q_{k})\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and as
k\to\infty

(3.3)

\displaystyle v_{k}\rightharpoonup v\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\rightharpoonup\beta\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\rightharpoonup\eta\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle q_{k}\rightharpoonup q\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

We use
(\beta_{k},v_{k},\eta_{k},q_{k})
as test function in (
3.1
), and from the identity

\|\nabla\beta\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\alpha_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\nabla(\beta\wedge\alpha_{k})\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla(\beta\vee\alpha_{k})\|_{2}^{2},

we derive

\displaystyle D(\alpha_{k})+\|\nabla(\beta\vee\alpha_{k})\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla\beta\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{Q}(\alpha_{k},p_{k})

\displaystyle=D(\alpha_{k})+\|\nabla\alpha_{k}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla\beta_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{Q}(\alpha_{k},p_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{Q}(\eta_{k})-\mathcal{Q}(e_{k})+D(\beta_{k})+\|\nabla q_{k}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla p_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{Q}(\beta_{k},q_{k})+\mathcal{H}(q_{k}-p_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{C}(\eta-e_{\infty}+2e_{k}),\eta-e_{\infty})_{2}+D(\beta_{k})+\tilde{Q}(\beta_{k},q_{k})

(3.5)

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla q-\nabla p_{\infty}+2\nabla p_{k},\nabla q-\nabla p_{\infty})_{2}+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{\infty}).

By (
3.3
)–(
3.4
) and Remark
2.3
, for all
\theta\in[1,6)
we have as
k\to\infty

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}\to\alpha_{\infty}\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle\beta_{k}\to\beta\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle p_{k}\to p_{\infty}\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle q_{k}\to q\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

In particular, by using (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) and arguing as in Remark
2.3
we deduce

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}D(\alpha_{k})=D(\alpha_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}D(\beta_{k})=D(\beta),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde{Q}(\alpha_{k},p_{k})=\tilde{Q}(\alpha_{\infty},p_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde{Q}(\beta_{k},q_{k})=\tilde{Q}(\beta,q).

Moreover,
\beta\vee\alpha_{k}\rightharpoonup\alpha_{\infty}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
, which implies

\|\nabla\alpha_{\infty}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla(\beta\vee\alpha_{k})\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, by passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
3.5
) we get

\displaystyle D(\alpha_{\infty})+\|\nabla\alpha_{\infty}\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla\beta\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{Q}(\alpha_{\infty},p_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{C}(\eta+e_{\infty}),\eta-e_{\infty})_{2}+D(\beta)+\tilde{Q}(\beta,q)+(\nabla q+\nabla p_{\infty},\nabla q-\nabla p_{\infty})_{2}+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}(\eta)-\mathcal{Q}(e_{\infty})+D(\beta)+\tilde{Q}(\beta,q)+\|\nabla q\|_{2}^{2}-\|\nabla p_{\infty}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{\infty}),

which gives (
3.2
).
∎

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
we deduce the following regularity result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume (
2.1
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\alpha,u,e,p)
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
be a quadruple satisfying (qs0) and (qs1), and such that
p\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
has bounded
\mathcal{H}
-variation. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent on
t\in[0,T]
, such that

(3.6)

\|\alpha(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|e(t)\|_{2}+\|p(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

Moreover, there exists a countable set
N\subset[0,T]
such that the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
. In particular, the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is strongly measurable from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Since
p\colon[0,T]\to L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
has bounded
\mathcal{H}
-variation, by (
2.11
) we deduce that
p
has bounded total variation. In particular,
\|p(t)\|_{1}
is uniformly bounded in
[0,T]
, being

(3.7)

\|p(t)\|_{1}\leq\|p(t)-p(0)\|_{1}+\|p(0)\|_{1}\leq\mathcal{V}(p;0,T)+\|p(0)\|_{1}\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

Since
(0,u(T)-w(T)+w(t),e(T)-\mathrm{E}w(T)+\mathrm{E}w(t),p(T))\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha(t))\times\mathcal{A}(w(t))
, by the global stability condition (qs1) we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{Q}(e(T))+Q(\mathrm{E}w(T)-\mathrm{E}w(t))+(\mathbb{C}e(T),\mathrm{E}w(T)-\mathrm{E}w(t))_{2}+\tilde{Q}(0,p(T))+\|\nabla p(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{H}(p(T)-p(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\|e(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\|\mathrm{E}w(T)-\mathrm{E}w(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{2}\|e(T)\|_{2}\|\mathrm{E}w(T)-\mathrm{E}w(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathbb{B}\|_{\infty}\|p(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla p(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p,t,T)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{2}\|e(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\gamma_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}+\|\mathbb{B}\|_{\infty}\|p(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla p(T)\|_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,T).

Notice that the quantity on the right-hand side is independent on
t\in[0,T]
. Since

\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\|e(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\alpha(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla p(t)\|_{2}^{2},

we deduce that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, independent on
t\in[0,T]
, such that

\|\nabla\alpha(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|e(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla p(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{1}\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

By using (
3.7
), Corollary
2.9
, and the fact that
0\leq\alpha(t)\leq 1
a.e. in
\Omega
for all
t\in[0,T]
we obtain a constant
C_{2}>0
, independent on
t\in[0,T]
, such that

(3.8)

\|\alpha(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|e(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\|p(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{2}\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

Finally, since
u(t)-w(t)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Korn Inequality there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
, independent on
t\in[0,T]
, such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)-w(t)\|_{2}+\|w(t)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathrm{E}u(t)-\mathrm{E}w(t)\|_{2}+\|w(t)\|_{2}\leq C\|e(t)\|_{2}+C\|p(t)\|_{2}+C\|\mathrm{E}w(t)\|_{2}+\|w(t)\|_{2}.

Therefore, by (
3.8
) and Korn Inequality there exists a constant
C_{5}>0
, independent on
t\in[0,T]
, such that

\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C.

This implies (
3.6
).

It remains to prove that there exists a countable set
N\subset[0,T]
such that the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
. Indeed, if this holds, then the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is weakly measurable from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
, and therefore strongly measurable, since
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
is a separable Banach space.

We start by observing that
\alpha\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
satisfies

\|\alpha(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 1\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$},\qquad\alpha(t_{2})\leq\alpha(t_{1})\quad\text{a.e.\ in $\Omega$ for all $0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T$}.

By
[
9
, Lemma A.2]
there exists a countable set
N_{1}\subset[0,T]
such that the function
\alpha\colon[0,T]\setminus N_{1}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is (strongly) continuous. In particular, thanks to the uniform estimate (
3.6
) we derive that
\alpha\colon[0,T]\setminus N_{1}\to H^{1}(\Omega)
is weakly continuous. Similarly, since
p
has bounded total variation in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, there exists a countable set
N_{2}\subset[0,T]
such that the function
p\colon[0,T]\setminus N_{2}\to L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
is strongly continuous.
Hence, the uniform estimate (
3.6
) yields that
p\colon[0,T]\setminus N_{2}\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
is weakly continuous.

Let us define
N\coloneqq N_{1}\cup N_{2}
. We claim that the pair
(u,e)
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Indeed, let us fix
t_{\infty}\in[0,T]\setminus N
and let us consider a sequence
(t_{k})_{k}\subset[0,T]\setminus N
such that
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
as
k\to\infty
. By the uniform estimate (
3.6
) there exists a constant
C>0
independent on
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|u(t_{k})\|_{H^{1}}+\|e(t_{k})\|_{2}\leq C\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Therefore, there exists a subsequence
(t_{k_{j}})_{j}\subset(t_{k})_{k}
and a pair
(u_{\infty},e_{\infty})\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that as
j\to\infty

\displaystyle u(t_{k_{j}})\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},\qquad e(t_{k_{j}})\rightharpoonup e_{\infty}\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Moreover, as
j\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\alpha(t_{k_{j}})\rightharpoonup\alpha(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$},\qquad p(t_{k_{j}})\rightharpoonup p(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},\qquad w(t_{k_{j}})\to w(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

Thus, we can apply Lemma
3.3
to deduce that
(\alpha(t_{\infty}),u_{\infty},e_{\infty},p(t_{\infty}))\in\mathcal{A}(w(t_{\infty}))
and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t_{\infty}),e_{\infty},p(t_{\infty}))\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p(t_{\infty}))\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha(t_{\infty}))\times\mathcal{A}(w(t_{\infty}))$.}

In particular, by choosing
\beta=\alpha(t_{\infty})
and
q=p(t_{\infty})
, we derive that the pair
(u_{\infty},e_{\infty})
minimizes the functional
F\colon H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\to[0,\infty)
, defined as

F(v,\eta)\coloneqq\mathcal{Q}(\eta)\quad\text{for all $(v,\eta)\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

over the convex set

G\coloneqq\{(v,\eta)\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})\,:\,(v,\eta,p(t_{\infty}))\in\mathcal{A}(w(t_{\infty}))\}.

This implies that
(u_{\infty},e_{\infty})
is uniquely determined for all
t\in[0,T]
by the strict convexity of
\mathcal{Q}
. Therefore,
u(t_{\infty})=u_{\infty}
and
e(t_{\infty})=e_{\infty}
. In particular, we get that as
k\to\infty

\displaystyle u(t_{k})\rightharpoonup u(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},\qquad e(t_{k})\rightharpoonup e(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

i.e., the pair
(u,e)
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
.
∎

3.2.
Existence of a global quasistatic evoltion

In this subsection we prove the existence of a global quasistatic evolution satisfying a fixed initial configuration. As in
[
10
]
, the proof relies on the De Giorgi’s Minimizing Movement approach to quasistatic evolutions, where time-continuous evolutions are approximated by discrete-time ones, constructed by solving incremental minimization problems.

We consider an initial configuration

(\alpha^{0},u^{0},e^{0},p^{0})\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}(w(0)),

which satisfies

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p^{0})\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha^{0})\times\mathcal{A}(w(0))$}.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\tau_{k}\coloneqq\frac{T}{k},\quad t_{k}^{i}\coloneqq i\tau_{k},\quad w_{k}^{i}\coloneqq w(t_{k}^{i})\quad\text{for $i=0,\dots,k$}.

Starting from

(\alpha_{k}^{0},u_{k}^{0},e_{k}^{0},p_{k}^{0})\coloneqq(\alpha^{0},u^{0},e^{0},p^{0})\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}(w(0)),

for all
i=1,\dots,k
we define

(\alpha_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})

as the solution of

(3.10)

\min_{(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})}\left\{\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{k}^{i-1})\right\}.

Notice that

(\alpha_{k}^{i-1},w_{k}^{i},\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{i},0)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})\neq\emptyset\quad\text{for all $i=1,\dots,k$}.

Moreover, for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
(\alpha,u,e,p)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha,e,p)+\mathcal{H}(p-p_{k}^{i})\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\|e\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\alpha\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla p\|_{2}^{2}+r_{H}\|p\|_{1}-r_{H}\|p_{k}^{i}\|_{1}

and the functional

(\alpha,u,e,p)\mapsto\mathcal{E}(\alpha,e,p)+\mathcal{H}(p-p_{k}^{i})

is sequentially lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak convergences in the spaces
H^{1}(\Omega)
,
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, and
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Hence, by the Direct Method of Calculus of Variations, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1\dots,k
there exists a solution
(\alpha_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})
to the minimum problem (
3.10
). In particular, since

\mathcal{H}(q-p_{k}^{i-1})-\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{i}-p_{k}^{i-1})\leq\mathcal{H}(q-p_{k}^{i}),

the quadruple
(\alpha_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})
satisfies

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p_{k}^{i})\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{i-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{i})$.}

In the following lemma, we derive an energy estimates for the discrete evolution
\{(\alpha_{k}^{i},u_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})\}_{i=1}^{k}
. First, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\dot{w}_{k}^{i}\coloneqq\frac{w_{k}^{i}-w_{k}^{i-1}}{\tau_{k}}\quad\text{for $i=1,\dots,k$}.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
), (
2.9
), and (
3.9
). For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,\dots,k
we have

(3.12)

\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}^{i},e_{k}^{i},p_{k}^{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tau_{k}(\mathbb{C}e_{k}^{j-1},\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{k}^{j})_{2}+\delta_{k},

where

(3.13)

\delta_{k}\coloneqq\tau_{k}\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}.

In particular, there exists a constant
C>0
independent on
k
and
i
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|e_{k}^{i}\|_{2}+\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|p_{k}^{i}\|_{H^{1}}+\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|\alpha_{k}^{i}\|_{H^{1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})\leq C.

Proof.

We fix
i=1,\dots,k
and for
j=1,\dots,i
we consider

(\alpha_{k}^{j-1},u_{k}^{j-1}-w_{k}^{j-1}+w_{k}^{j},e_{k}^{j-1}-\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j-1}+\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j},p_{k}^{j-1})\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha_{k}^{j-1})\times\mathcal{A}(w_{k}^{j}).

By the minimality condition (
3.10
)
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}^{j},e_{k}^{j},p_{k}^{j})+\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}^{j-1},e_{k}^{j-1}-\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j-1}+\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j},p_{k}^{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}^{j-1},e_{k}^{j-1},p_{k}^{j-1})+\tau_{k}(\mathbb{C}e_{k}^{j-1},\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{k}^{j})_{2}+\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\|\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j}-\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j-1}\|_{2}^{2}.

We sum over
j=1,\dots,i
and we use the inequality

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j}-\mathrm{E}w_{k}^{j-1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tau_{k}\int_{0}^{T}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

to obtain (
3.12
).

By the discrete energy inequality (
3.12
) we get

\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}\|e_{k}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})+\gamma_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tau_{k}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{k}^{j}\|_{2}\|e_{k}^{j-1}\|_{2}+\delta_{k}.

Since
\delta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, independent on
k
and
i
, such that

\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|e_{k}^{i}\|_{2}\leq C_{1}.

Therefore, by (
3.12
) we can find another constant
C_{2}>0
, independent on
k
and
i
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|\nabla\alpha_{k}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|\nabla p_{k}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})\leq C_{2}.

By Corollary
2.9
and the inequality

\displaystyle\|p_{k}^{i}\|_{1}\leq\|p^{0}\|_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1}\|_{1}\leq\|p^{0}\|_{1}+\frac{1}{r_{H}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1}),

we deduce the existence a constant
C_{3}>0
, independent on
k
and
i
, such that

\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|p_{k}^{i}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C_{3}.

Since
0\leq\alpha_{k}^{i}\leq 1
in
\Omega
, there exists a constant
C_{4}>0
independent on
k
and
i
such that

\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|\alpha_{k}^{i}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\sqrt{|\Omega|}+\max_{i=0,\dots,k}\|\nabla\alpha_{k}^{i}\|_{2}\leq C_{4}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We introduce the
piecewise constant interpolant

\overline{\alpha}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)\coloneqq\alpha_{k}^{i}\quad\text{for $t\in(t_{k}^{i-1},t_{k}^{i}]$, $i=1,\dots,k$},\qquad\overline{\alpha}_{k}(0)=\alpha_{k}^{0},

and similarly we consider
\overline{u}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\overline{e}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
,
\overline{p}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, and
\overline{w}_{k}\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, we define
\overline{\tau}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to[0,T]
as

(3.15)

\overline{\tau}_{k}(t)\coloneqq t_{k}^{i}\quad\text{for $t\in(t_{k}^{i-1},t_{k}^{i}]$, $i=1,\dots,k$},\qquad\overline{\tau}_{k}(0)=0.

By definition, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})=\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{p}_{k};0,T),

and thanks to Lemma
3.7
we deduce that the piecewise constant interpolants satisfy the following estimate: there is a constant
C>0
independent on
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,T]
such that

(3.16)

\|\overline{e}_{k}(t)\|_{2}+\|\overline{p}_{k}(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{p}_{k};0,T)\leq C\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

We can now prove our existence result for global quasistatic evolution.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume (
2.1
)–(
2.7
), (
2.9
), and (
3.9
). Then there exists a global quasistatic evolution
(\alpha,u,e,p)
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
satisfying the initial condition

(3.17)

(\alpha(0),u(0),e(0),p(0))=(\alpha^{0},u^{0},e^{0},p^{0}).

Proof.

Step 1
. Starting from
(\alpha^{0},u^{0},e^{0},p^{0})
we consider the incremental problem (
3.10
) for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,\dots,k
, and we obtain a sequence of approximate solutions
(\overline{\alpha}_{k},\overline{u}_{k},\overline{e}_{k},\overline{p}_{k})
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
. Since all functions
\overline{\alpha}_{k}
are non increasing in time, we can apply a generalized version of the classical Helly Theorem to conclude that there
exist a subsequence, still denoted by
\overline{\alpha}_{k}
, and a function
\alpha\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])
non increasing in time, such that for all
t\in[0,T]
as
k\to\infty

\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)\rightharpoonup\alpha(t)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$}.

In particular, thanks to Remark
2.3
, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\theta\in[1,6)
we have as
k\to\infty

\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)\to\alpha(t)\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega)$}.

Moreover, by (
3.16
) there exists a constant
C>0
, independent on
k
, such that

r_{H}\mathcal{V}(\overline{p}_{k};0,T)\leq\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{p}_{k};0,T)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}(p_{k}^{j}-p_{k}^{j-1})\leq C,\qquad\|\overline{p}_{k}(0)\|_{H^{1}}=\|p^{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C.

Hence, there exist a further subsequence, still denoted by
\overline{p}_{k}
, and a function
p\colon[0,T]\to H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
with bounded variation in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
as
k\to\infty

\overline{p}_{k}(t)\rightharpoonup p(t)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

In particular, by (
2.12
) we get

\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,T)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{p}_{k};0,T)\leq C,

which yields that
p
has bounded
\mathcal{H}
-variation. Furthermore, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\theta\in[1,6)
we have as
k\to\infty

\overline{p}_{k}(t)\to p(t)\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Step 2
. For all fixed
t\in[0,T]
there exist a subsequence
k_{j}
and functions
e^{*}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and
u^{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, depending on
t\in[0,T]
, such that as
j\to\infty

\overline{e}_{k_{j}}(t)\rightharpoonup e^{*}\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},\qquad\overline{u}_{k_{j}}(t)\to u^{*}\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

By construction
(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t),\overline{u}_{k}(t),\overline{e}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}(\overline{w}_{k}(t))
and in view of (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t),\overline{e}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-\overline{p}_{k}(t))\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t))\times\mathcal{A}(\overline{w}_{k}(t))$}.

By writing the inequality for
k_{j}
and using Lemma
3.3
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e^{*},p(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(\beta,\eta,q)+\mathcal{H}(q-p(t))\quad\text{for all $(\beta,v,\eta,q)\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha(t))\times\mathcal{A}(w(t))$}.

Therefore, we can argue as in Lemma
3.4
to derive that
(u^{*},e^{*})
is uniquely determined for all
t\in[0,T]
. In particular, by defining
u(t)\coloneqq u^{*}
and
e(t)\coloneqq e^{*}
we deduce that as
k\to\infty

\overline{e}_{k}(t)\rightharpoonup e(t)\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},\qquad\overline{u}_{k}(t)\to u(t)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

and the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
satisfies (qs0), (qs1), and (
3.17
).

Step 3
. By Lemma
3.4
, we deduce that
(\alpha,u,e,p)
are strongly measurable from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
. We define
\underline{e}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
as

\displaystyle\underline{e}_{k}(t)\coloneqq e_{k}^{i-1}\quad\text{for $t\in[t_{k}^{i-1},t_{k}^{i})$, $i=1,\dots,k$},\qquad\underline{e}_{k}(T)=e_{k}^{k}.

By the discrete energy inequality (
3.12
), for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in(t_{k}^{i-1},t_{i}]
, and
i=1,\dots,k
we have

(3.18)

\mathcal{E}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t),\overline{e}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(\overline{p}_{k};0,t_{k}^{i})\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})+\int_{0}^{\overline{\tau}_{k}(t)}(\mathbb{C}\underline{e}_{k}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+\delta_{k}.

Since
\underline{e}_{k}(t)=\overline{e}_{k}(t-\tau_{k})
for a.e.
t\in[\tau_{k},T]
, we derive that as
k\to\infty

\underline{e}_{k}\rightharpoonup e\quad\text{in $L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}))$}.

Hence, we can use the convergences of Steps 1 and 2 to pass to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
3.18
), and to obtain that the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
satisfies for all
t\in[0,T]

\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,t)\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}e(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

being all the terms on the left-hand side are sequentially lower semicontinuous with respect to the convergences of
\overline{\alpha}_{k},\overline{e}_{k},\overline{p}_{k}
. It remains to prove the opposite inequality.

We fix
t\in(0,T]
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we consider a collection of points
\{s_{k}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}
in
[0,t]
satisfying

0=s_{k}^{0}<s_{k}^{1}<\cdots<s_{k}^{k-1}<s_{k}^{k}=t,\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\max_{i=1,\dots,k}|s_{k}^{i}-s_{k}^{i-1}|\to 0.

For all
i=1,\dots,k
we have

(\alpha(s_{k}^{i}),u(s_{k}^{i})-w(s_{k}^{i})+w(s_{k}^{i-1}),e(s_{k}^{i})-\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i})+\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i-1}),p(s_{k}^{i}))\in\mathcal{D}(\alpha(s_{k}^{i-1}))\times\mathcal{A}(w(s_{k}^{i-1})).

By using (qs1) in
t=s_{k}^{i-1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,\dots,k
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(s_{k}^{i-1}),e(s_{k}^{i-1}),p(s_{k}^{i-1}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha(s_{k}^{i}),e(s_{k}^{i})-\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i})+\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i-1}),p(s_{k}^{i}))+\mathcal{H}(p(s_{k}^{i})-p(s_{k}^{i-1}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(\alpha(s_{k}^{i}),e(s_{k}^{i}),p(s_{k}^{i}))+\mathcal{H}(p(s_{k}^{i})-p(s_{k}^{i-1}))

\displaystyle\quad-(\mathbb{C}e(s_{k}^{i}),\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i})-\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i-1}))_{2}+\mathcal{Q}(\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i})-\mathrm{E}w(s_{k}^{i-i}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha(s_{k}^{i}),e(s_{k}^{i}),p(s_{k}^{i}))+\mathcal{H}(p(s_{k}^{i})-p(s_{k}^{i-1}))-\int_{s_{k}^{i-1}}^{s_{k}^{i}}(\mathbb{C}e(s_{k}^{i}),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{i=1,\dots,k}|s_{k}^{i}-s_{k}^{i-1}|\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

By summing over
i=1,\dots,k
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))+\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,t)-\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}\tilde{e}_{k}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

(3.19)

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{i=1,\dots,k}|s_{k}^{i}-s_{k}^{i-1}|\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\tilde{e}_{k}\colon[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
is defined as

\tilde{e}_{k}(t)\coloneqq e(s_{k}^{i})\quad\text{for all $t\in[s_{k}^{i-1},s_{k}^{i})$},\qquad\tilde{e}_{k}(T)\coloneqq e(T).

Since
\tilde{e}_{k}(r)\to e(r)
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
for a.e.
r\in[0,t]
as
k\to\infty
, we can use the Dominated Convergence Theorem to derive

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}\tilde{e}_{k}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}e(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

By passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
3.19
) we get the other opposite energy inequality. Hence,
(\alpha,u,e,p)
satisfies (qs2) and it is a global quasistatic evolution from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
.
∎

3.3.
Continuity in time

In this subsection we investigate the regularity in time of global quasistatic evolutions
(\alpha,u,e,p)
in the sense of Definition
3.1
. More precisely, we show that every evolution is continuous at every continuity point
t
of
\alpha\colon[0,T]\to L^{1}(\Omega)
. We point out that such a property improves the continuity result of Lemma
3.4
.

We start by proving several preliminary results. In the first one we show that every global quasistatic evolutions is strongly continuous up to a countable set.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\alpha,u,e,p)
be a global quasistatic evolution from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
. There exists a countable set
N\subset[0,T]
such that the quadruple
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is strongly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.4
we know that there exists a countable set
N_{1}\subset[0,T]
such that every global quasistatic evolution
(\alpha,u,e,p)
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, since the map
t\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,t)
from
[0,T]
into
\mathbb{R}
is increasing, there exists a countable set
N_{2}\subset[0,T]
such that
t\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{H}}(p;0,t)
is continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N_{2}
into
\mathbb{R}
. In particular, the energy equality (qs2) yields that the map
t\mapsto\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t),e(t),p(t))
is continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N_{2}
into
\mathbb{R}
.

Let us define
N\coloneqq N_{1}\cup N_{2}
. We fix
t_{\infty}\in[0,T]\setminus N
and a sequence
(t_{k})_{k}\subset[0,T]\setminus N
such that
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
as
k\to\infty
. Thanks to Lemma
3.4
we have

\displaystyle\alpha(t_{k})\rightharpoonup\alpha(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle u(t_{k})\rightharpoonup u(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle e(t_{k})\rightharpoonup e(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle p(t_{k})\rightharpoonup p(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$},

and by the Sobolev Embedding Theorem (see also Remark
2.3
), for all
\theta\in[1,6)
we get

\displaystyle\alpha(t_{k})\to\alpha(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega)$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle u(t_{k})\to u(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle p(t_{k})\to p(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$}

\displaystyle w(t_{k})\to w(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as $k\to\infty$}.

In particular, we derive that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t_{\infty}),e(t_{\infty}),p(t_{\infty}))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}(e(t_{k}))+\lim_{k\to\infty}D(\alpha(t_{k}))+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla\alpha(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}+\lim_{k\to\infty}\tilde{Q}(\alpha(t_{k}),p(t_{k}))+\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla p(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t_{k}),e(t_{k}),p(t_{k}))=\mathcal{E}(\alpha(t_{\infty}),e(t_{\infty}),p(t_{\infty})).

This implies that

\displaystyle Q(e(t_{\infty}))=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}(e(t_{k})),\qquad\|\nabla\alpha(t_{\infty})\|_{2}^{2}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla\alpha(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\|\nabla p(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla p(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}.

If we use also that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}(a_{k}+b_{k})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}a_{k}+\liminf_{k\to\infty}b_{k}\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}(a_{k}+b_{k})\quad\text{for all two sequences $(a_{k})_{k},(b_{k})_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}$},

we conclude that

\displaystyle Q(e(t_{\infty}))=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{Q}(e(t_{k})),\qquad\|\nabla\alpha(t_{\infty})\|_{2}^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla\alpha(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\|\nabla p(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla p(t_{k})\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence, thanks to (
2.4
) we obtain the following strong convergences:

\displaystyle\alpha(t_{k})\to\alpha(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle e(t_{k})\to e(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$},

\displaystyle p(t_{k})\to p(t_{\infty})\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$ as $k\to\infty$}.

Finally, by Korn Inequality, we derive

\|\nabla u(t_{k})-\nabla u(t_{\infty})\|_{2}\leq C\left(\|u(t_{k})-u(t_{\infty})\|_{2}+\|e(t_{k})-e(t_{\infty})\|_{2}+\|p(t_{k})-p(t_{\infty})\|_{2}\right)\to 0\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$}.

Therefore, the quadruple

is strongly continuous from
[0,T]\setminus N
into
H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{A}
.
∎

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{2}^{2}\left(\|e(t_{2})-e(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{4}^{2}+\|\nabla\alpha(t_{2})-\nabla\alpha(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+3C_{2}^{2}\left(\tilde{Q}(\alpha(t_{1}),p(t_{2})-p(t_{1}))+\|\nabla p(t_{2})-\nabla p(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{4}^{2}+C_{3}\|\alpha(t_{1})-\alpha(t_{2})\|_{4}\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{2}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|e(s)-e(t_{1})\|_{2}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{3}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}.

By summing (
3.36
) and (
3.37
) and using also Corollary
2.9
, we can find a constant
C_{4}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|e(t_{2})-e(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{4}^{2}+C_{4}\|\alpha(t_{1})-\alpha(t_{2})\|_{4}\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{4}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|e(s)-e(t_{1})\|_{2}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{4}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}.

Firstly, we use Young Inequality

\|\alpha(t_{1})-\alpha(t_{2})\|_{4}\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{2}\leq C_{\epsilon}\|\alpha(t_{1})-\alpha(t_{2})\|_{4}^{2}+\epsilon\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\quad\text{for all $\epsilon>0$},

and then Lemma
2.8
with
\theta=4
to derive that

\displaystyle\|e(t_{2})-e(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{1}^{2}+C_{5}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|e(s)-e(t_{1})\|_{2}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C_{5}\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}

for a constant
C_{5}>0
. In particular, there exist a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\|e(t_{2})-e(t_{1})\|_{2}+\|p(t_{2})-p(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}\right)^{2}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\|\alpha(t_{2})-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{1}^{2}+C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|e(s)-e(t_{1})\|_{2}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\left(C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(s)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}.

Let us define the functions

\displaystyle f(t)\coloneqq\|e(t)-e(t_{1})\|_{2}+\|p(t)-p(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha(t)-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{H^{1}}\quad\text{for all $t\in[t_{1},T]$},

\displaystyle g(t)\coloneqq C\|\alpha(t)-\alpha(t_{1})\|_{1}\quad\text{for all $t\in[t_{1},T]$},\qquad h(t)\coloneqq C\|\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(t)\|_{2}\quad\text{for a.e.\ $t\in[t_{1},T]$}.

Therefore, by (
3.38
) we deduce

\displaystyle f(t)^{2}\leq g(t)^{2}+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}f(s)h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t}h(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\quad\text{for all $t\in[t_{1},T]$}.

We can apply Lemma
3.14

3.32

∎

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})),

and the following conditions are satisfied:

(ev0)_{\varepsilon}

irreversibility
: for a.e.
x\in\Omega
the map

t\mapsto\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\quad\text{is non increasing on $[0,T]$};

(ev1)_{\varepsilon}

kinematic condition and equilibrium
: for all
t\in[0,T]

(u_{\varepsilon}(t),e_{\varepsilon}(t),p_{\varepsilon}(t))\in\mathcal{A}(w(t))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathbb{C}e_{\varepsilon}(t)=0\quad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$};

(ev2)_{\varepsilon}

stress constraint
: for all
t\in[0,T]

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e_{\varepsilon}(t),q)_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))p_{\varepsilon}(t),q)_{2}-2(\nabla p_{\varepsilon}(t),\nabla q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$};

(ev3)_{\varepsilon}

Kuhn–Tucker Inequality

t\in[0,T]

(ev4)_{\varepsilon}

: for all
t\in[0,T]

\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t),e_{\varepsilon}(t),p_{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}(\dot{p}_{\varepsilon}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(0),e_{\varepsilon}(0),p_{\varepsilon}(0))+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}e_{\varepsilon}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Condition
(ev2)_{\epsilon}
is a generalization of the standard stress constraint in plasticity. Indeed, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\alpha,u,e,p)\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
be satisfying

(4.29)

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e,q)_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha)p,q)_{2}-2(\nabla p,\nabla q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

If
p\in H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
and
K
is compact, then
(\alpha,u,e,p)\in H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}e(x)-2\mathbb{B}(\alpha(x))p(x)+2\Delta p(x)\in K\quad\text{for a.e.\ $x\in\Omega$}.

Proof.

Let
q\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. By integrating by parts in (
4.29
) we get

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e-2\mathbb{B}(\alpha)p+2\Delta p,q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Since
K
is bounded, there exists
R>0
such that
K\subset B_{R}(0)
. Then

\mathcal{H}(q)\leq R\|q\|_{1}\quad\text{for all $q\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

which implies that
\mathcal{H}
is continuous on
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Therefore, by a density argument we derive

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e-2\mathbb{B}(\alpha)p+2\Delta p,q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Let us fix
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}
. Since for all measurable set
B\subset\Omega
we can take
q\coloneqq\chi_{B}\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
, we derive

H(\xi)\geq[\mathbb{C}e(x)-2\mathbb{B}(\alpha(x))p(x)+2\Delta p(x)]:\xi\quad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}$ and for a.e.\ $x\in\Omega$}.

Therefore,
\mathbb{C}e(x)-2\mathbb{B}(\alpha(x))p(x)+2\Delta p(x)\in\partial H(0)=K
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
∎

.

)–(

) and (

(ev4)_{\varepsilon}\Longleftrightarrow(ev4^{\prime})_{\varepsilon}
.

[

]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{k}(t),e_{k}(t),p_{k}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{H}(\dot{p}_{k}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{\alpha}_{k}(r)\|_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha^{0},e^{0},p^{0})+\int_{0}^{t}(\mathbb{C}\overline{e}_{k}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{k}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+C\tau_{k}.

By sending
k\to\infty
and using the weak convergences (

)–(
4.42
) together with the lower semicontuinty of the left-hand side (see Remark

), we get
(ev_{4}^{\prime\prime})_{\varepsilon}
.

Step 3
. By (
4.4
), for all
\beta\in L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))
with
\beta\leq 0
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)),\beta(t))_{2}+2(\nabla\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t),\nabla\beta(t))_{2}+(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))\beta(t)\overline{p}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))_{2}+\varepsilon(\dot{\alpha}_{k}(t),\beta(t))_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(\dot{d}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t))-\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)),\beta(t))_{2}+([\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t))-\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))]\beta(t)\overline{p}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))_{2}.

By Remark
2.3
, as
k\to\infty

\displaystyle|(\dot{d}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t))-\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)),\beta(t))_{2}|\leq\|\ddot{d}\|_{\infty}\|\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)-\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{2}\|\beta(t)\|_{2}\to 0,

\displaystyle|([\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t))-\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))]\beta(t)\overline{p}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))_{2}|\leq\|\ddot{\mathbb{B}}\|_{\infty}\|\overline{\alpha}_{k}(t)-\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{4}\|\beta(t)\|_{4}\|\overline{p}_{k}(t)\|_{4}^{2}\to 0

in view of the strong convergences in (

), see also (

) we get

(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))\beta(t)p_{\varepsilon}(t),p_{\varepsilon}(t))_{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))\beta(t)\overline{p}_{k}(t),\overline{p}_{k}(t))_{2}.

Hence, we can apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem to derive that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}[(\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(r)),\beta(r))_{2}+2(\nabla\overline{\alpha}_{k}(r),\nabla\beta(r))_{2}+(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(r))\beta(r)\overline{p}_{k}(r),\overline{p}_{k}(r))_{2}+\varepsilon(\dot{\alpha}_{k}(r),\beta(r))_{2}]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}[(\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(r)),\beta(r))_{2}+2(\nabla\alpha_{\varepsilon}(r),\nabla\beta(r))_{2}+(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(r))\beta(r)p_{\varepsilon}(r),p_{\varepsilon}(r))_{2}+\varepsilon(\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(r),\beta(r))_{2}]\,\mathrm{d}r.

If we fix
\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\beta\leq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, by choosing

with

arbitrary set, we get

\displaystyle(\dot{d}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)),\beta)_{2}+(\dot{\mathbb{B}}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))\beta p_{\varepsilon}(t),p_{\varepsilon}(t))_{2}+2(\nabla\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t),\nabla\beta)_{2}+\varepsilon(\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(t),\beta)_{2}\geq 0\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]\setminus E_{\beta}$},

where

. Since

∎

5.
Balanced viscosity (BV) solutions

In the previous section for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we found an
\varepsilon
-approximate viscous evolution
(\alpha_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon},e_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
from
[0,T]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
which satisfies (
4.37
). We now introduce a “slow” time scale
s
and we pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, to obtain a Balanced Viscosity quasistatic evolution in the spirit of
[
11
]
, see also
[
15
,
17
,
27
]
. We first introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). A quintuplet of Lipschitz functions
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
from
[0,S]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}\times[0,T]
is
Balanced Viscosity quasistatic evolution
in the time interval
[0,S]
with datum
w
, if setting for all
s\in[0,S]

\displaystyle w_{0}(s)\coloneqq w(t_{0}(s))\quad\text{and}\quad U_{0}\coloneqq\{s\in[0,S]\,:\,\text{$t_{0}$ is constant in a neighborhood of $s$}\},

the following conditions are satisfied:

(ev0)

irreversibility
:
t_{0}
is non decreasing and surjective, and for a.e.
x\in\Omega
the map

s\mapsto\alpha_{0}(s,x)\quad\text{is non increasing on $[0,S]$};

(ev1)

kinematic condition and equilibrium
: for all
s\in[0,S]

(u_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))\in\mathcal{A}(w_{0}(s))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathbb{C}e_{0}(s)=0\quad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$};

(ev2)

stress constraint
: for all
s\in[0,S]

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e_{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{0}(s))p_{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\nabla p_{0}(s),\nabla q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$;}

(ev3)

Kuhn–Tucker Inequality in
[0,S]\setminus U_{0}
: for all
s\in[0,S]\setminus U_{0}

\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))[\beta]\geq 0\quad\text{for all $\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)$ with $\beta\leq 0$;}

(ev4)

energy balance
: for all
s\in[0,S]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))+\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{H}(\dot{p}_{0}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\|\dot{\alpha}_{0}(r)\|_{2}\Psi(\alpha_{0}(r),e_{0}(r),p_{0}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(0),e_{0}(0),p_{0}(0))+\int_{0}^{s}(\mathbb{C}e_{0}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{0}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\Psi
is the function defined in (
4.34
), with the convention
0\cdot\infty=0
.

Remark 5.2
.

A similar definition for a different model with incomplete damage can be found in
[
11
]
, where it is called rescaled quasistatic viscosity evolution. By Lemma
4.13
and
(ev3)
, for every
s\in[0,S]\setminus U_{0}
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq d(\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s)),G)

\displaystyle=\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))=\sup_{\beta\in F}\langle-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s)),\beta\rangle\leq 0,

which gives

\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))=0\quad\text{for all $s\in[0,S]\setminus U_{0}$}.

Similarly to Proposition
4.12
, the following is a technical proposition instrumental to prove existence of Balanced Viscosity quasistatic solutions.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
) and (
2.9
). Let
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
be a quintuplet of Lipschitz functions from
[0,S]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}\times[0,T]
satisfying
(ev0)
,
(ev1)
,
(ev2)
, and
(ev3)
. Then
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
is a Balanced Viscosity quasistatic evolution, i.e., it satisfies the energy balance
(ev4)
, if and only if any of the conditions
below holds true:

(ev4^{\prime})

for a.e.
s\in[0,S]
the following hold:

generalized Kuhn–Tucker equality:

-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))[\dot{\alpha}_{0}(s)]=\|\dot{\alpha}_{0}(s)\|_{2}\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s));

Hill’s maximum plastic work principle:

\mathcal{H}(\dot{p}_{0}(s))=(\mathbb{C}e_{0}(t),\dot{p}_{0}(s))_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{0}(s))p_{0}(s),\dot{p}_{0}(s))_{2}-2(\nabla p_{0}(s),\nabla\dot{p}_{0}(s))_{2};

(ev4^{\prime\prime})

energy inequality: for all
s\in[0,S]

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))+\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{H}(\dot{p}_{0}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\|\dot{\alpha}_{0}(r)\|_{2}\Psi(\alpha_{0}(r),e_{0}(r),p_{0}(r))\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(0),e_{0}(0),p_{0}(0))+\int_{0}^{s}(\mathbb{C}e_{0}(r),\mathrm{E}\dot{w}_{0}(r))_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one of Proposition
4.12
.
∎

We eventually prove existence of Balanced Viscosity quasistatic solutions.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume (
2.2
)–(
2.7
), (
2.9
), and (
3.9
). Then there exist
S>0
and a Balanced Viscosity quasistatic solution
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
in the time interval
[0,S]
satisfying the initial condition

(5.1)

(\alpha_{0}(0),u_{0}(0),e_{0}(0),p_{0}(0),t_{0}(0))=(\alpha^{0},u^{0},e^{0},p^{0},0).

Proof.

Step 1
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be fixed and let
(\alpha_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon},e_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
be the
\varepsilon
-approximate viscous evolution given by Theorem
4.14
. For all
t\in[0,T]
we define the function

s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t)\coloneqq t+\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(r)\|_{H^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{e}_{\varepsilon}(r)\|_{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\|\dot{p}_{\varepsilon}(r)\|_{H^{1}}\,\mathrm{d}r.

The function
s_{\varepsilon}^{0}
is absolutely continuous, increasing, and bijective on
[0,T]
and we have

(5.2)

s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t_{2})-s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t_{1})\geq t_{2}-t_{1}\quad\text{for all $0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T$}.

Let
S_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(T)\geq T
and let
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}\colon[0,S_{\varepsilon}]\to[0,T]
be the inverse of
s_{\varepsilon}^{0}
. By (
4.37
) we can find a constant
C>0
independent on
\varepsilon
such that
S_{\varepsilon}\leq C
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Hence, up to a subsequence,
S_{\varepsilon}\to S
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, with
S\geq T
.

We set
\overline{S}_{=}\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}S_{\varepsilon}\geq S
and we extend every function
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}
to the interval
[0,\overline{S}]
by defining
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq T
for all
s\in[S_{\varepsilon},\overline{S}]
. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we define the following rescaled functions on
[0,\overline{S}]
: for all
s\in[0,\overline{S}]

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq w(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq e_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\coloneqq p_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)).

Notice that the functions
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}\colon[0,\overline{S}]\to[0,T]
are uniformly Lipschitz in
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
by (

), since

0\leq t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{2})-t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{1})\leq s_{2}-s_{1}\quad\text{for all $0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\overline{S}$}.

Hence, there exists a subsequence and a Lipschitz map
t_{0}\colon[0,\overline{S}]\to[0,T]
such that
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}\to t_{0}
uniformly on
[0,\overline{S}]
and weakly* in
W^{1,\infty}((0,\overline{S});(0,T))
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In particular
t_{0}(0)=0
,
t_{0}(s)=T
for all
s\in[S,\overline{S}]
, and

0\leq t_{0}(s_{2})-t_{0}(s_{1})\leq s_{2}-s_{1}\quad\text{for all $0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\overline{S}$}.

In particular, the map
t_{0}\colon[0,S]\to[0,T]
is non decreasing and surjective. Moreover, as
\varepsilon\to 0
we derive

w_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\to w_{0}(s)\coloneqq w(t_{0}(s))\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$ for all $s\in[0,S]$}.

By using the definitions of
s_{\varepsilon}^{0}
and
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}
we obtain

(5.3)

\|\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{2})-\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{1})\|_{H^{1}}+\|e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{2})-e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{1})\|_{2}+\|p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{2})-p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s_{1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq s_{2}-s_{1}\quad\text{for all $0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq S$}.

By Ascoli-Arzela Theorem there exists a quadruple
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0})
of Lipschitz functions from
[0,S]
into
H^{1}(\Omega;[0,1])\times\mathcal{A}
satifying: for all
s\in[0,S]
as
\varepsilon\to 0

(5.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\rightharpoonup\alpha_{0}(s)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\rightharpoonup u_{0}(s)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

(5.5)

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\rightharpoonup e_{0}(s)\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\rightharpoonup p_{0}(s)\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

In particular, by Remark
2.3
, for all
s\in[0,S]
as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}\to\alpha_{0}\quad\text{in $C^{0}([0,S];L^{\theta}(\Omega))$},\qquad p_{\varepsilon}^{0}\to p_{0}\quad\text{in $C^{0}([0,S];L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym}))$}

for all
\theta\in[1,6)
. Furthermore, if we combine the uniform Lipschitz estimate (
5.3
) with the weak convergences (
5.4
)–(
5.5
), we get

\|\alpha_{0}(s_{2})-\alpha_{0}(s_{1})\|_{H^{1}}+\|e_{0}(s_{2})-e_{0}(s_{1})\|_{2}+\|p_{0}(s_{2})-p_{0}(s_{1})\|_{H^{1}}\leq s_{2}-s_{1}\quad\text{for all $0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq S$},

which yields

\|\dot{\alpha}_{0}(s)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\dot{e}_{0}(s)\|_{2}+\|\dot{p}_{0}(s)\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1\quad\text{for a.e.\ $s\in[0,S]$}.

Moreover, the initial conditions (
5.1
) hold by construction.

Step 2.
Let us prove that
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
is a Balanced Viscosity quasistatic solution on
[0,S]
. By (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) we derive that
(u_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))\in\mathcal{A}(w_{0}(s))
and
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathbb{C}e_{0}(s)=0
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
s\in[0,S]
, and that for a.e.
x\in\Omega
the map
s\mapsto\alpha_{0}(s,x)
in non increasing on
[0,S]
. Hence
(\alpha_{0},u_{0},e_{0},p_{0},t_{0})
satisfies
(ev0)
and
(ev1)
.
Let us fix
s\in[0,S]
and
q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})
. Then
t_{\varepsilon}(s)\in[0,T]
for all
s\in[0,S]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Hence, thanks to
(ev2)_{\varepsilon}
, for all
s\in[0,S]
we get

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s))p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\nabla p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),\nabla q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

By sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and exploiting the convergences (
5.4
)–(

), for all
s\in[0,S]
we derive

\mathcal{H}(q)\geq(\mathbb{C}e_{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\mathbb{B}(\alpha_{0}(s))p_{0}(s),q)_{2}-2(\nabla p_{0}(s),\nabla q)_{2}\quad\text{for all $q\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}.

Therefore, also condition
(ev2)
is satisfied.

Step 3.
We introduce the functions
s_{0}^{+},s_{0}^{-}\colon[0,T]\to[0,S]
, defined by

\displaystyle s_{0}^{+}(t)\coloneqq\inf\{s\in[0,S]:t_{0}(s)>t\}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T)$},

\displaystyle s_{0}^{+}(T)=S,

\displaystyle s_{0}^{-}(t)\coloneqq\sup\{s\in[0,S]:t_{0}(s)<t\}\quad\text{for $t\in(0,T]$},

\displaystyle s_{0}^{+}(0)=0.

Then

(5.7)

s_{0}^{-}(t)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t)\leq s_{0}^{+}(t)\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$},

and

t_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))=t=t_{0}(s_{0}^{+}(t))\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$},\qquad s_{0}^{-}(t_{0}(s))\leq s\leq s_{0}^{+}(t_{0}(s))\quad\text{for all $s\in[0,S]$}.

In particular, the set

S_{0}\coloneqq\{t\in[0,T]\,:\,s_{0}^{-}(t)<s_{0}^{+}(t)\}

is at most countable and

U_{0}\coloneqq\{s\in[0,S]\,:\,\text{$t_{0}$ is constant in a neighborhood of $s$}\}=\bigcup_{t\in S_{0}}(s_{0}^{-}(t),s_{0}^{+}(t)).

Thanks to (
5.7
), for all
t\in[0,T]\setminus S_{0}
we have that
s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(t)\to s_{0}^{-}(t)=s_{0}^{+}(t)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence, by exploting (
5.3
)–(

) we deduce that for all
t\in[0,T]\setminus S_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0

(5.8)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup\alpha_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup u_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

(5.9)

\displaystyle e_{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup e_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup p_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$},

and by Remark
2.3
for all
t\in[0,T]\setminus S_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0

\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t)\to\alpha_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega)$},

\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon}(t)\to p_{0}(s_{0}^{-}(t))\quad\text{in $L^{\theta}(\Omega;\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\rm sym})$}

for all
\theta\in[1,6)
.

We want to show
(ev3)
and we define

A_{0}\coloneqq\{s\in[0,S]\,:\,\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))>0\}.

Therefore, in order to prove
(ev3)

A_{0}\subset U_{0}
. Since for all
\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
the map
s\mapsto\langle-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s)),\beta\rangle
is continuous on
[0,S]
, we derive that the map
s\mapsto\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))
is lower semicontinous on
[0,T]
. In particular, the set
A_{0}
is open on
[0,T]
. Moreover, for all
\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\beta\leq 0

\langle-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s)),\beta\rangle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle-\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),\beta\rangle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Psi(\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)),

which gives

(5.10)

\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Psi(\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)).

Let us define

D_{0}\coloneqq\{s\in(0,S)\,:\,\dot{t}_{0}(s)=0\},

and let us show that

(5.11)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\dot{t}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)>0\quad\text{for a.e.\ $s\in[0,S]\setminus D_{0}$}.

On the contrary, since
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}
is non decreasing, we could find a measurable set

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\dot{t}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)=0\quad\text{for all $s\in A$}.

Since every function
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}
is 1-Lipschitz on

, by applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem we conclude that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{A}\dot{t}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=0.

On the other hand, we have that
t_{\varepsilon}^{0}\rightharpoonup t_{0}
weakly* in
W^{1,\infty}((0,S);(0,T))
, which gives

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{A}\dot{t}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{A}\dot{t}_{0}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r>0,

since
\dot{t}_{0}(s)>0
for a.e.
s\in[0,S]\setminus D_{0}
. This leads to a contradiction and proves (
5.11
).

For a.e.
s\in[0,S]\setminus D_{0}
we derive

0\leq\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Psi(\alpha_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),e_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s),p_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s))=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\|\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s))\|_{2}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\frac{\|\dot{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)\|_{2}}{\dot{t}_{\varepsilon}^{0}(s)}=0

by (
4.35
), (
5.3
), and (
5.11
). Therefore, we have

\Psi(\alpha_{0}(s),e_{0}(s),p_{0}(s))=0\quad\text{for a.e.\ $s\in[0,S]\setminus D_{0}$},

which gives that
A_{0}\subset D_{0}
, i.e.,

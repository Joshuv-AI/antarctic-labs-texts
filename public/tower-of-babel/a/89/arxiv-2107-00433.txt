Two phase flows of compressible viscous fluids
Eduard Feireisl, Antonín Novotný

Abstract
We introduce a new concept of dissipative varifold solution to models of two phase compressible viscous fluids. In contrast with the existing approach based on the Young measure description, the new formulation is variational combining the energy and momentum balance in a single inequality. We show the existence of dissipative varifold solutions for a large class of general viscous fluids with non–linear dependence of the viscous stress on the symmetric velocity gradient.

1
Introduction

We consider a simple model of a two–phase flow, where the interface between the two fluids is described via a phase variable
\chi\in\{0,1\}
that coincides with the characteristic function of the domain occupied by one of the fluid
components. Accordingly, the time evolution of
\chi
is governed by the transport equation

\partial_{t}\chi+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi=0,

(1.1)

where
{\bf u}
is the fluid velocity.
We denote

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{1}(t)&=\left\{x\in\Omega\ \Big|\ \chi(t,x)=1\right\}-\mbox{the part of the physical space}\ \Omega\subset R^{d}\ \mbox{occupied by fluid 1},\\
\mathcal{O}_{2}(t)&=\left\{x\in\Omega\ \Big|\ \chi(t,x)=0\right\}-\mbox{the part of the physical space}\ \Omega\subset R^{d}\ \mbox{occupied by fluid 2},\\
\Gamma(t)&=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{1}(t)\cap\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{2}(t)-\mbox{the interface.}\end{split}

As the mass of the fluid is conserved, its density
\varrho
satisfies the standard equation of continuity

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho{\bf u})=0.

(1.2)

The material properties of the two fluid components are characterized by a general barotropic equation of state

p_{i}=p_{i}(\varrho),\ i=1,2,\ p(\chi,\varrho)=\chi p_{1}(\varrho)+(1-\chi)p_{2}(\varrho).

(1.3)

The viscous stress
\mathbb{S}
is related to the symmetric velocity gradient
\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}
through an “implicit” rheological law

\mathbb{S}_{i}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}=F_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})+F^{*}_{i}(\mathbb{S}_{i}),\ i=1,2

(1.4)

where the dissipative potentials
F_{i}
,
i=1,2
are convex functions defined on the space
R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}
of
symmetric
d-
dimensional real matrices, and where
F^{*}_{i}
denotes the convex conjugate.
More specifically, we suppose

F_{i}:R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,\infty]\ \mbox{is l.s.c. convex},\ F_{i}(0)=0,\ i=1,2.

(1.5)

Note that (
1.4
) is equivalent
to

\mathbb{S}_{i}\in\partial F_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\in\partial F^{*}_{i}(\mathbb{S}_{i}).

It is easy to check that the choice

F_{i}=\frac{\mu_{i}}{2}\left|\mathbb{D}-\frac{1}{d}{\rm tr}[\mathbb{D}]\right|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{2}\left|{\rm tr}\mathbb{[}\mathbb{D}]\right|^{2},\ i=1,2

gives rise to the standard Newtonian viscous stress used in the Navier–Stokes system.
Similarly to (
1.3
), we set

\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\chi\mathbb{S}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})+(1-\chi)\mathbb{S}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}).

We refer to Bulíček et al

[
6
]
for more details about ”implicitly” constituted fluids, where the viscous stress is related to the velocity gradient in a way similar to (
1.4
). Similar abstract approach has been used in
[
1
]
.

The balance of momentum is satisfied for each individual fluid. The velocity is continuous, while the Cauchy stress

\mathbb{T}_{i}=\mathbb{S}_{i}-p_{i}\mathbb{I},\ i=1,2

experiences a jump on the interface,
specifically,

[\mathbb{T}\cdot{\bf n}]=\kappa H{\bf n}\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma,\ \kappa\geq 0,

where
H{\bf n}
is the mean curvature vector, and
\kappa
the
coefficient of surface tension.
Consequently, it is convenient to formulate the momentum balance in the weak form:

\begin{split}\left[\int_{\Omega}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{S}(\chi,\nabla_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Gamma(t)}H{\bf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(1.6)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T]\times\Omega;R^{d})
,
where we have omitted the effect of external volume forces for simplicity.

1.1
Varifold solutions

If the initial interface is a smooth surface (curve if
d=2
), the problem admits a local in time regular solution as long as the data are regular, see the survey of Denisova, Solonnikov
[
7
]
and the references cited therein. Global existence is not
expected because of possible singularities and self–intersections of
\Gamma(t)
.

Plotnikov
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
adapted the concept of
varifold
to study the problem for large times in the context of
incompressible non-Newtonian fluids for
d=2
. Later Abels
[
2
,
3
]
introduced the class of
measure–valued

varifold solutions to attack the problem for
d=2,3
still in the incompressible setting but for a larger family of
viscous stresses including the standard Newtonian fluids. He also showed the existence of weak varifold solutions in the absence of surface tension. Ambrose at al.
[
4
]
extended the existence proof to the case with surface tension for incompressible Newtonian fluids and
d=2
. To the best of our knowledge,
with the only exception of the work of Plotnikov
[
12
]
dealing with a simplified Stokes model, similar problems are completely open in the context of
compressible
fluids.

We introduce a new weak formulation of the problem based on the principles of the calculus of variations. The main idea is to rewrite the momentum equation with the associated total energy balance as a single inequality in terms of the dissipation potentials
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
. The resulting problem still uses the concept of varifold, however, the description of oscillations via a Young measure is no longer necessary. The new formulation is versatile and can be used for both compressible and incompressible fluids. Finally, we show that the problem admits global in time solutions as long as
{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}
is penalized in a way similar to
[
9
]
.

2
Weak formulation

To avoid problems with physical boundary, we consider the space–periodic boundary conditions. In other words,
the spatial domain
\Omega
is identified with a flat torus,

\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d},\ d=2,3.

(2.1)

2.1
Varifolds

Following Abels
[
2
]
, Plotnikov
[
10
]
, we introduce the concept of
varifold

V
,

V\in L^{\infty}_{\rm weak-(*)}(0,T;\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1})),

which, after disintegration, takes the form

V=|V|\otimes V_{x},\ |V|\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ \{V_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\subset\mathfrak{P}(S^{d-1}).

The
first variation of

V
reads

\left<\delta V(t);\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\otimes S^{d-1}}(\mathbb{I}-z\otimes z):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}(x)\ {\rm d}V(t)\ \mbox{for any}\ \boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

Similarly to
[
2
]
, we rewrite the momentum equation (
1.6
) as

\begin{split}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(2.2)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
.

2.2
Total energy balance

Introducing the pressure potentials

P_{i}(\varrho),\ P^{\prime}_{i}(\varrho)\varrho-P_{i}(\varrho)=p_{i}(\varrho),\ i=1,2,\ P(\chi,\varrho)=\chi P_{1}(\varrho)+(1-\chi)P_{2}(\varrho),

(2.3)

we deduce the
total energy balance

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0,

(2.4)

cf. Abels
[
2
]
.
In the weak formulation, the energy balance (
2.4
) is replaced by an inequality

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0.

(2.5)

Next, subtracting (
2.2
) from (
2.5
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}-\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0.\end{split}

(2.6)

Finally, in view of (
1.4
),

\begin{split}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})&\equiv\chi\Big(F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\Big)+(1-\chi)\Big(F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\Big)\\
&\geq\mathbb{S}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\end{split}

Consequently, we rewrite (
2.6
) in the final form

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(2.7)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
.

2.3
Dissipative varifold solutions

We are ready to introduce the concept of
dissipative varifold solution
.

Definition 2.1

(
dissipative varifold solution).

We say that
[\chi,\varrho,{\bf u},V]
is a
dissipative varifold solution
of the problem
(
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.6
), (
2.1
) if the following holds:

•

Regularity class.

\chi\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \chi\in\{0,1\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t,x;

(2.8)

\varrho\in C([0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})]),\ 0\leq\underline{\varrho}\leq\varrho(t,x)\leq\overline{\varrho}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t,x;

(2.9)

{\bf u}\in L^{\alpha}(0,T;W^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{for some}\ \alpha>1,\varrho{\bf u}\in C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})),

(2.10)

V\in L^{\infty}_{\rm weak-(*)}(0,T;\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1})).

(2.11)

•

Transport.

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\chi\partial_{t}\varphi+\chi{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\chi{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.12)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T\times\mathbb{T}^{d}])
.

•

Mass conservation.

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(2.13)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T\times\mathbb{T}^{d}])
.

•

Momentum–energy balance

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(2.14)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
, and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
satisfying

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})+F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty.

(2.15)

•

Varifold compatibility.

If, in addition,

\kappa>0
, we require
\chi\in L^{\infty}_{{\rm weak-(*)}}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, and

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot z\ {\rm d}V(\tau,\cdot)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot{\rm d}\nabla_{x}\chi(\tau)=0

(2.16)

for a.a.
0\leq\tau\leq T
, and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

First observe that dissipative varifold solutions coincide with weak varifold solutions in the sense of Plotnikov
[
10
]
and Abels
[
2
]
, if they satisfy the energy balance

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0,

(2.17)

where

\mathbb{S}(t,x)\in\chi\partial F_{1}((\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})(t,x))+(1-\chi)\partial F_{2}((\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})(t,x))\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ t,x.

(2.18)

Indeed subtracting (
2.17
) from (
2.14
) we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}:(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq-\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t,\end{split}

where, by virtue of (
2.18
),

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}:(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})-F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})-\mathbb{S}:(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0.\end{split}

Consequently, we deduce the standard weak varifold formulation of the momentum equation

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\mathbb{S}:\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times S^{d-1}}\left<\delta V;\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right>\ {\rm d}V\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
, and any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))
.

Note that Definition
2.1
can be easily adapted to incompressible fluids just by
dropping the pressure term and considering solenoidal test functions in (
2.14
).

3
Hypotheses and main results

Our goal is to handle the largest possible class of the pressure–density equations of state as well as the dissipative potentials.
For technical reasons, however, certain singularity of
F_{i}
will be required to keep the system out of the vacuum.
Our principal technical hypotheses concerning the dissipative potentials read:

F_{i}:R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}\to[0,\infty]\ \mbox{convex l.s.c},\ F_{i}(0)=0,\ 0\in{\rm int}({\rm Dom}[F_{1}]\cap{\rm Dom}[F_{2}]);

(3.1)

F_{i}(\mathbb{D})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\Big|\mathbb{D}-\frac{1}{d}{\rm tr}[\mathbb{D}]\Big|^{\alpha}-1\ \mbox{for some}\ \alpha>\frac{2d}{d+2};

(3.2)

F_{i}(\mathbb{D})=\infty\ \mbox{whenever}\ {\rm tr}[\mathbb{D}]>\overline{d}\geq 0;

(3.3)

i=1,2
. Hypothesis (
3.2
) seems natural to guarantee integrability of the convective term by means of the energy bounds, hypothesis (
3.3
) yields a uniform bound

|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}|\leq\overline{d}\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\Omega.

(3.4)

We refer the reader to
[
9
]
for the modelling aspects of (
3.3
).

In the context of incompressible fluids,
the hypothesis (
3.4
) becomes irrelevant, while (
3.1
), (
3.2
) can be rewritten as

F_{i}:R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym,0}\to[0,\infty]\ \mbox{convex l.s.c},\ F_{i}(0)=0,\ 0\in{\rm int}({\rm Dom}[F_{1}]\cap{\rm Dom}[F_{2}]);

(3.5)

F_{i}(\mathbb{D})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}|\mathbb{D}|^{\alpha}-1\ \mbox{for some}\ \alpha>\frac{2d}{d+2},

(3.6)

i=1,2
. As
F_{i}
may become infinite, the above class is broad enough to accommodate the rheology of
thick fluids
,
see Barnes
[
5
]
and Rodrigues
[
13
]
.

As the velocity
{\bf u}
enjoys the Sobolev regularity (
2.10
) and (
3.4
) holds, the theory of DiPerna–Lions
[
8
]
applies to (
2.12
), (
2.13
). In particular, for the initial data

0<\underline{\varrho}\leq\varrho_{0}\leq\overline{\varrho},

(3.7)

the equation of continuity (
2.13
) admits a unique renormalized solution
\varrho
belonging to the class

\varrho\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \underline{\varrho}\exp\left(-t\overline{d}\right)\leq\varrho(t,x)\leq\overline{\varrho}\exp\left(t\overline{d}\right)\ \mbox{for any}\ t,x.

By the same token, the phase variables
\chi
,

\chi\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \mbox{ ranges in the set}\ \{0,1\}

provided the same is true for the initial data.
In addition,
\chi
and
\varrho
satisfy the renormalized equation

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\Omega}b(\chi,\varrho)\varphi(0,\cdot)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[b(\chi,\varrho)\partial_{t}\varphi+b(\chi,\varrho){\bf u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\left(b(\chi,\varrho)-\frac{\partial b(\chi,\varrho)}{\partial\varrho}\varrho\right){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\varphi\right]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(3.8)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
, any
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})
, and any
b\in C^{1}_{\rm loc}([0,1]\times(0,\infty))
.

We are ready to state our main results. We start with a mixture of two compressible fluids without surface tension.

Theorem 3.1

(
Compressible fluids without surface tension).

Let
d=2,3
. Suppose that
\kappa=0
and
p_{i}\in C^{1}_{c}(0,\infty)
are increasing functions of
\varrho
for
i=1,2
. Let
F_{i}
,
i=1,2
, satisfy (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) with
\alpha\geq\frac{11}{5}
if
d=3
and
\alpha\geq 2
if
d=2
. In addition, suppose there is a constant
k>0
such that

\frac{1}{k}F_{1}(\mathbb{D})-k\leq F_{2}(\mathbb{D})\leq k\Big(F_{1}(\mathbb{D})+1\Big)\ \mbox{for any}\ \mathbb{D}\in R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}.

(3.9)

Let the initial data satisfy

\chi(0,\cdot)=\chi_{0}=1_{\Omega},\ \Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}\ \mbox{a Lipschitz domain},

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\ 0<\underline{\varrho}\leq\varrho_{0}\leq\overline{\varrho},\ \varrho{\bf u}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}).

Then the problem
(
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.6
), (
2.1
) admits a dissipative varifold solution
(\varrho,{\bf u},\chi)
in
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

The hypothesis (
3.9
) requires the dissipative potentials to share the growth of the same order for large
\mathbb{D}
.

To handle the compressible case with surface tension, we restrict ourselves to the pressure–density rheological law pertinent to isothermal gases, namely

p_{i}(\varrho)=a_{i}\varrho,\ a_{i}>0,\ i=1,2,\ p(\varrho,\chi)=\chi a_{1}\varrho+(1-\chi)a_{2}\varrho.

(3.10)

Theorem 3.2

(
Compressible fluids with surface tension).

Let
d=2,3
, and
\kappa\geq 0
. Suppose that the pressure is given by (

). Let

,

, satisfy (

)–(

).
Let the initial data satisfy

Then the problem
(

), (

), (

), (
2.1
) admits a dissipative varifold solution
(\varrho,{\bf u},\chi,V)
in
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
in the sense of Definition

.

Finally, we reformulate the result in terms of incompressible fluids.

(

Let

, and

. Suppose that

), (

in

4
Basic approximation scheme

To construct the varifold solution, we adapt the approximation scheme introduced in
[
1
]
. First, we replace
the dissipative potentials
F_{i}
by their Moreau–Yosida approximation,

F^{\varepsilon}_{i}(\mathbb{D})=\inf_{\mathbb{M}\in R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|\mathbb{M}-\mathbb{D}|^{2}+F^{i}(\mathbb{D})\right\},\ i=1,2,\ \delta>0

Next, we consider an orthogonal basis
\{{\bf e}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of the space
L^{2}(\Omega;R^{d})
consisting of, say,
trigonometric polynomials. We look for approximate velocity field belonging to the space

{\bf u}\in C([0,T];X_{N}),\ X_{N}={\rm span}\{{\bf e}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}.

Given
{\bf u}\in C([0,T];X_{N})
, the transport equation as well as the equation of continuity may be solved by the method of characteristics,

\chi(t,x)=\chi_{0}({\bf X}^{-1}(t,x)),\ \varrho(t,x)=\varrho_{0}({\bf X}^{-1}(t,x))\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}(s,{\bf X}^{-1}(s,x)){\rm d}s\right),

(4.1)

where
{\bf X}
is the associated Lagrangian flow

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}{\bf X}(t,x)={\bf u}(t,{\bf X}(t,x)),\ {\bf X}(0,x)=x.

At this stage, we approximate the initial data
\chi_{0}
to be a characteristic function of a
C^{1}-
domain
\Omega_{0}
.
Accordingly, if
{\bf u}
is smooth with respect to the
x-
variable, the image of
\Omega_{0}
under the flow remains
C^{1}
at any time,

\chi_{0}=1_{\Omega_{0}}\ \mbox{of class}\ C^{1}\ \Rightarrow\ \chi(t,\cdot)=1_{\Omega_{t}},\ \Omega_{t}\ \mbox{of class}\ C^{1}.

The velocity field will be identified via a Faedo–Galerkin approximation:

\begin{split}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \varrho{\bf u}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0},\ \varepsilon>0,\ m>2d,\end{split}

(4.2)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T];X_{N})
,
where we have set

\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})=\chi\partial F^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})+(1-\chi)\partial F^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}).

The reader may consult Abels
[
2
]
, where the specific form of the “varifold term” in (
4.2
) is discussed.
Identity (
4.2
) contains also an elliptic regularization necessary for performing the passage from smooth to
rough dissipation potentials.

The approximation scheme depends on three parameters:
N
,
\varepsilon
, and
\delta
. These being fixed, the existence of
approximate solutions can be shown by the standard fixed–point argument, see
[
1
, Section 3]
and Abels
[
2
, Section 4]
. Our goal is to perform consecutively the limits
N\to\infty
,
\varepsilon\to 0
, and
\delta\to 0
.

4.1
Energy estimates

As all quantities are smooth at the basic approximation level, we may consider
{\bf u}
as a test function in (
4.2
) obtaining the energy balance

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right]\\
+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\delta\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=0,\end{split}

(4.3)

where we have used the identity (see Abels
[
2
, Section 2.4, formula (2.8)]
)

\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\varphi\ {\rm d}S_{x}=\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}):\nabla_{x}(\varphi{\bf u})\ {\rm d}S_{x}+\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}{\bf n}\cdot\ \nabla_{x}\varphi{\bf n}\cdot{\bf u}\ {\rm d}S_{x},\ \varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),

(4.4)

together with the renormalized version (
3.8
) of the equations (
2.12
), (
2.13
), specifically

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}P(\chi,\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
.

4.2
Limit
N\to\infty

The limit
N\to\infty
in the family of Faedo–Galerkin approximations is straightforward.
Let
(\chi_{N},\varrho_{N},{\bf u}_{N})_{N\geq 1}
be the corresponding family of solutions. Keeping

\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
fixed we deduce from the energy balance and (
4.1
) the following uniform bounds:

\begin{split}\varrho_{N}&\approx 1,\\
({\bf u}_{N})_{N\geq 1}\ &\mbox{bounded in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{2m,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})),\\
(\chi_{N})_{N\geq 1}\ &\mbox{bounded in}\ L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ (\kappa\chi_{N})_{N\geq 1}\ \mbox{bounded in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{T}^{d})).\end{split}

(4.5)

First, we have

{\bf u}_{N}\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{and weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;W^{2m,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))

(4.6)

passing to a suitable subsequence as the case may be. In particular, the velocity
field remains regular at least in the spatial variable, and we get

\varrho_{N}\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ \chi_{N}\to\chi\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}_{{\rm weak-(*)}}(0,T;\mathcal{M}(R^{d})),

(4.7)

and

\chi_{N}\to\chi\ \mbox{in}\ C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

where the limit functions are given by formula (
4.1
).

Denoting

\Pi_{N}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})\to X_{N}

the orthogonal projection, we deduce from (
4.2
) that

(\partial_{t}\Pi_{N}(\varrho_{N}{\bf u}_{N}))_{N\geq 1}\ \mbox{bounded in}\ L^{p}(0,T;W^{-k,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{for some}\ p>1,\ k>0.

Consequently, by virtue of Aubin–Lions Lemma,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{N}|{\bf u}_{N}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

yielding

{\bf u}_{N}\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})).

Thus, finally,

{\bf u}_{N}\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}(0,T;C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{for some}\ q>2.

As the regularization
\partial F^{\varepsilon}_{i}
are globally Lipschitz in
\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}
, we are allowed to perform the limit in the momentum equation (
4.2
) obtaining

\begin{split}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}&=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\ \varrho{\bf u}(0,\cdot)={\bf m}_{0},\ \varepsilon>0,\ m>2d,\end{split}

(4.8)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
.

Finally, we pass to the limit in the energy balance (
4.3
):

\begin{split}\left[\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
-\int_{0}^{\tau}\partial_{t}\psi\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)\,{\rm d}t\\
+\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}):\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0,\end{split}

(4.9)

for a.a.
0\leq\tau\leq T
and any
\psi\in C^{1}[0,T]
,
\psi\geq 0
.

Remark 4.1
.

As a matter of fact, a more refined argument would yield equality in (
4.9
). This is, however, not needed for the remaining part of the proof.

5
Compressible case without surface tension

Being given a family of approximate solutions identified in Section
4.2
, our goal is to perform the limits
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\delta\to 0
. Fixing
\delta>0

we denote
(\varrho_{\varepsilon},\chi_{\varepsilon},{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
the associated approximate solution.

5.1
Limit
\varepsilon\to 0

Since the energy balance (
4.9
) holds, we may repeat step by step the arguments of Section
4.2
to obtain

\begin{split}\varrho_{\varepsilon}\approx 1,\ \varrho_{\varepsilon}&\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega),\\
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}&\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{and weakly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;W^{2m,2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})),\\
\chi_{\varepsilon}&\to\chi\ \mbox{in}\ C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\ \mbox{and weakly-(*) in}\ L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\end{split}

(5.1)

passing to a suitable subsequence as the case may be. Similarly to the preceding section, the characteristic curves are still well defined and the limit functions
\chi
,
\varrho
are given by formula (
4.1
).

Next, we take
\psi=1
in the energy inequality (
4.9
) and subtract the resulting expression from the momentum balance obtaining

\begin{split}&\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+P(\chi_{\varepsilon},\varrho_{\varepsilon})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi_{\varepsilon},\varrho_{\varepsilon}){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq 0,\end{split}

(5.2)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
,
where

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}):(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})-F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}

Now, consider
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
such that

|\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi|<M
,
and
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(0,T)
,

0\leq\psi\leq 1
.
In view if hypothesis (
3.1
),

\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi\in{\rm int}({\rm Dom}[F_{1}]\cap{\rm Dom}[F_{2}])

as long as
M>0
is small enough.
Using
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(t,x)=\psi(t)\phi(x)
as a test function in (
5.2
) we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla_{x}\phi+p(\chi_{\varepsilon},\varrho_{\varepsilon}){\rm div}_{x}\phi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon,t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\phi\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t-\delta\int_{0}^{T}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+P(\chi_{0},\varrho_{0})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\psi\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi)\ \,{\rm d}{x},\end{split}

where, furthermore,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\psi\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F_{1}(\psi\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F_{2}(\psi\mathbb{D}_{x}\phi)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq C(M).

Thus we may infer that

\begin{split}t&\in[0,T]\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\varrho_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\cdot\phi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\in BV[0,T]\\
&\mbox{whenever}\ \phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}),\end{split}

with the norm bounded uniformly for
\varepsilon\to 0
. Consequently, similarly to Section
4.2
, we conclude

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\varepsilon}|{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x},

yielding, finally,

{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{q}(0,T;C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{for some}\ q>2.

(5.3)

The ultimate goal of this step is to perform the limit in (
5.2
). Summing up the previous observations, we get

\begin{split}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(5.4)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
. Consider a test function

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}),\ \int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})+F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty,

(5.5)

and write

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\varepsilon}F^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})F^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

We already know that

\chi_{\varepsilon}\to\chi\in\{0;1\}\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\ F^{\varepsilon}_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\nearrow F_{i}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \mbox{in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\ i=1,2.

Consequently, in accordance with (
5.5
),

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\varepsilon}F^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})F^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\varepsilon}F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\to\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi)F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(5.6)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\varepsilon}F^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})F^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\varepsilon}F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\to\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi)F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\ \mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to,\ \overline{\varepsilon}\ \mbox{fixed}.\end{split}

Consequently,

\begin{split}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi)F^{\overline{\varepsilon}}_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

for any
\overline{\varepsilon}
. Thus we may infer that

\begin{split}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}&\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F^{\varepsilon}(\chi_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi F_{1}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-\chi)F_{2}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(5.7)

Combining (
5.4
), (
5.6
), and (
5.7
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+P(\chi,\varrho)\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}\chi\|_{\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\kappa\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}\left(\mathbb{I}-{\bf n}\otimes{\bf n}\right):\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ {\rm d}S_{x}\,{\rm d}t-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(5.8)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
satisfying (
5.5
).

5.2
Limit
\delta\to 0

Let
(\chi_{\delta},\varrho_{\delta},{\bf u}_{\delta})_{\delta>0}
be the family of approximate solutions obtained in the previous section.
Our ultimate goal is to perform the limit
\delta\to 0
. Up to this moment, the proof has been identical for
Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
, with the obvious modifications for Theorem
3.3
. Now, we focus on the
case of compressible fluids without surface tension.

If
\kappa=0
, the approximate solutions satisfy the following relations:

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{\delta}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\chi_{\delta}\partial_{t}\varphi+\chi_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi+\chi_{\delta}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(5.9)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T\times\mathbb{T}^{d}])
;

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\delta}\varphi\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho_{\delta}\partial_{t}\varphi+\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

(5.10)

for any
0\leq\tau\leq T
,
\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
;

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi_{\delta},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi_{\delta},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\geq\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{\delta}|{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}-\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}+P(\chi_{\delta},\varrho_{\delta})\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf u}_{\delta}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi_{\delta},\varrho_{\delta}){\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(5.11)

for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d})
satisfying (
5.5
). Setting

in (

\begin{split}{\rm ess}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|\varrho_{\delta}|{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\\
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi_{\delta},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta})\ \,{\rm d}{x}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1,\\
\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\ \,{\rm d}{x}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle<}}{{\sim}}1.\end{split}

Consequently, by virtue of hypothesis (
3.3
),

|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta}(t,x)|\leq\overline{d}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x).

(5.12)

In addition,

\chi_{\delta}\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \chi_{\delta}(t,x)\in\{0,1\}\ \mbox{for a.a.}\ (t,x)

and

\varrho_{\delta}\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ \underline{\varrho}\exp\left(-t\overline{d}\right)\leq\varrho_{\delta}(t,x)\leq\overline{\varrho}\exp\left(t\overline{d}\right)\ \mbox{for a.a}\ (t,x),

represent the unique renormalized solution of (

), (

Similarly to Section

\varrho_{\delta}\to\varrho\ \mbox{in}\ C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\ 1<q<\infty,

\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\to\varrho{\bf u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{p}_{\rm weak}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}))\ \mbox{for any}\ 1<p<\infty,

and

\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf u}_{\delta}\to\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}\ \mbox{weakly in}\ L^{q}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d\times d}_{\rm sym})\ \mbox{for some}\ q>1.

(5.13)

In particular,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varrho_{\delta}|{\bf u}_{\delta}-{\bf u}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\varrho_{\delta}|{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}-2\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot{\bf u}+\varrho_{\delta}|{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0.

As
\varrho_{\delta}
is bounded below away from zero, the above relation yields

{\bf u}_{\delta}\to{\bf u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d};R^{d}).

(5.14)

With the estimates (
5.12
), (
5.14
) at hand, we may apply

8
, Theorem II.5]

to obtain

\chi_{\delta}\to\chi\ \mbox{in}\ C([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\ \mbox{for any}\ 1\leq q<\infty,

(5.15)

where
\chi
is a renormalized solution of the transport equation (
2.12
),

\chi(t,x)\in\{0,1\}
for a.a.
(t,x)
. Moreover, it is easy to check that
\varrho
is a renormalized solution of the equation of continuity (
2.13
).

Finally, we use the fact that
\chi_{\delta}
,

) in the form

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi_{\delta},\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi_{\delta},\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\

&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf u}_{\delta}:\nabla_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+p(\chi_{\delta},\varrho_{\delta})({\rm div}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi}-{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}_{\delta})\Big)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\Delta_{x}^{m}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\Delta_{x}^{m}{\bf u}_{\delta}|^{2}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(5.16)

Repeating the arguments of Section

in (

) obtaining

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}\boldsymbol{\varphi})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\

5.2.1
Strong convergence of the density

Our ultimate goal in the proof of Theorem
3.1
is to “remove” the bars in (
5.17
) which amounts to showing strong a.a. pointwise convergence

\varrho_{\delta}\to\varrho\ \mbox{a.a. in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}.

(5.18)

To see (
5.18
), it is enough to justify the choice
\boldsymbol{\varphi}={\bf u}
as a test function
in (
5.17
). Indeed, as shown in
[
9
]
, this would yield

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{p(\chi,\varrho)}{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

On the other hand, from the renormalization,

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big[\overline{P(\chi,\varrho)}-P(\chi,\varrho)\Big]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}-\overline{p(\chi,\varrho){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(p(\chi,\varrho)-\overline{p(\chi,\varrho)}\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(5.19)

As
P_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are strictly convex on the range of
\varrho
and
\chi_{\delta}
converges strongly, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{P(\chi,\varrho)}-P(\chi,\varrho)\ \,{\rm d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\chi\Big[\overline{P_{1}(\varrho)}-P_{1}(\varrho)\Big]+(1-\chi)\Big[\overline{P_{2}(\varrho)}-P_{2}(\varrho)\Big]\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle>}}{{\sim}}\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\|\varrho_{\delta}-\varrho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.\end{split}

By the same token,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(p(\chi,\varrho)-\overline{p(\chi,\varrho)}\Big){\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\ \,{\rm d}{x}\leq\|{\rm div}_{x}{\bf u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\|\varrho_{\delta}-\varrho\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Thus we may apply Gronwall’s lemma to (
5.19
) to obtain, up to a suitable subsequence,
(
5.18
).

In order to make the preceding step rigorous, we have to justify the choice
\boldsymbol{\varphi}={\bf u}
in (
5.17
). Similarly to
[
9
]
, we consider the regularization in time via the Steklov averages. Specifically, let

\begin{split}\eta_{h}(t)&=\frac{1}{h}1_{[-h,0]}(t),\ \eta_{-h}(t)=\frac{1}{h}1_{[0,h]}(t),\ h>0,\\
\xi_{\varepsilon}&\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\tau),\ 0\leq\xi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1,\ \xi_{\varepsilon}(t)=1\ \mbox{if}\ \varepsilon\leq t\leq\tau-\varepsilon.\end{split}

A suitable approximation of
{\bf u}
reads

[{\bf u}]_{h,\varepsilon}=\xi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*(\xi_{\varepsilon}{\bf u}),\ \varepsilon>0,\ h>0,

where
*
stands for the convolution in the time variable
t
. The function
[{\bf u}]_{h,\varepsilon}
can be used as a test function
in (
5.17
). As shown in
[
9
, Section 4.3]
, we have

\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|{\bf u}|^{2}-\varrho{\bf u}\cdot[{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}]\right)\ \,{\rm d}{x}\right]_{t=0}^{t=\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(\varrho{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}[{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}]+\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla_{x}[{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}]\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\to 0

as
\varepsilon,h\to 0
. Note that it is only at this point, where the hypothesis
\alpha\geq\frac{11}{5}
is needed. Consequently, it is enough to show

\limsup_{\varepsilon,h\to 0}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}[{\bf u}]_{h,\varepsilon})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\chi,\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

(5.20)

To see (
5.20
) we first use convexity of
F_{i}
and the hypothesis
F_{i}(0)=0
to observe

F_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}[{\bf u}]_{h,\varepsilon}))=F_{i}(\xi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}))\leq\xi_{\varepsilon}F_{i}(\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})),\ i=1,2.

Next, by Jensen’ inequality,

F_{i}(\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}))\leq\eta_{-h}*F_{i}(\eta_{h}*(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u}))\leq\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*F_{i}(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})),\ i=1,2.

Consequently,

F_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}[{\bf u}]_{h,\varepsilon}))\leq\xi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{-h}*\eta_{h}*F_{i}(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})).

By virtue of hypothesis (
3.9
),

F_{i}(\xi_{\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\to F_{i}(\mathbb{D}_{x}{\bf u})\ \mbox{in}\ L^{1}((0,\tau)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ i=1,2,\ \mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0;

whence (
5.20
) follows. Note that it is only at this moment, where the hypothesis (
3.9
) was needed.

We have proved Theorem
3.1
.

On the elliptic harmonic mappings and sense-preserving harmonic mappings
Ming-Sheng Liu ∗ {}^{~\mathbf{*}}, Hao XU

Abstract
. In this paper, we first establish two versions of Landau-Bloch type theorem for ( K , K ′ ) (K,K^{\prime}) -elliptic harmonic mappings with a bounded minimum distortion. Next, we provide several coefficient estimates and a conjecture for ( K , K ′ ) (K,K^{\prime}) -elliptic harmonic mappings. Then, we establish three new versions of Landau-Bloch type theorem for sense-preserving harmonic mappings. Finally, we establish two sharp versions of Landau-Bloch type theorem for certain harmonic mappings. These results are sharp in some given cases and improve the related results of different authors.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Suppose that
f(z)=u(z)+iv(z)
is a twice continuously differentiable function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. Then
f
is a harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if
f
satisfies
\Delta f=4f_{z\bar{z}}=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial y^{2}}=0
for all

z=x+iy\in\mathbb{D}
(cf.
[
13
]
), where we use the common notations for its formal derivatives:

f_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}-if_{y}\right),\quad f_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}+if_{y}\right).

For such function
f
, we define the maximum and minimum distortions
[
3
]
as follows:

\Lambda_{f}=\max_{0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 2\pi}\left|f_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}f_{\bar{z}}\right|=\left|f_{z}\right|+\left|f_{\bar{z}}\right|,

and

\lambda_{f}=\min_{0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 2\pi}\left|f_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}f_{\bar{z}}\right|=\|f_{z}|-|f_{\bar{z}}||.

Since
\mathbb{D}
is simply connected,
f(z)
can be written as
f=h+\bar{g}
with
f(0)=h(0)
,
g(z)
and
h(z)
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
(cf.
[
13
]
).
Thus the Jacobian of
f
is given by

J_{f}=\left|f_{z}\right|^{2}-\left|f_{z}\right|^{2}=\left|h^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\left|g^{\prime}\right|^{2}.

Notice that
\left|J_{f}\right|=\lambda_{f}\cdot\Lambda_{f}
. It is known
[
18
]
that a harmonic mapping is locally univalent if and only if
J_{f}\neq 0
. A harmonic mappings
f
is said to be sense-preserving if
J_{f}>0
. Therefore, a sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f
is said to be
K
-quasiregular harmonic
(K\geq 1)
on
\mathbb{D}
if
\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq K\lambda_{f}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. There is currently great interest in harmonic mappings due to their relevance for fluid flows (please refer to the discussion in
[
1
,
10
]
).

Definition 1.1.
(
[
24
]
) Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a domain. A mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be absolutely continuous on lines,
ACL
in brief, in a domain
\Omega
if for every closed rectangle
R\subset\Omega
with sides parallel to the axes
x
and
y,f
is absolutely continuous on almost every horizontal line and almost every vertical line in
R
. Such a mapping
f
has partial derivatives
f_{x}
and
f_{y}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Moreover, we say
f\in ACL^{2}
if
f\in ACL
and its partial derivatives are locally
L^{2}
integrable in
\Omega
.

Definition 1.2.
(
[
14
]
) A sense-preserving and continuous mapping
f
of
\mathbb{D}
onto
\mathbb{C}
is said to be
\left(K,K^{\prime}\right)
-elliptic mapping if

(1)
f
is
ACL^{2}
in
\mathbb{D},J_{f}\neq 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{D}
;

(2)
\exists K\geq 1
and
K^{\prime}\geq 0
such that
\left\|D_{f}\right\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}+K^{\prime}\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{D}
.

It is easy to see that
\left\|D_{f}(z)\right\|=\Lambda_{f}(z)
. Thus, a
\left(K,0\right)
-elliptic mapping becomes
K
-quasiregular mapping. For more details on elliptic mappings, please refer to (
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
23
]
).

The classical Landau theorem asserts that if
f
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f^{\prime}(0)=1
and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<\rho_{0}\}
, and
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}})
contains a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
, where

(1.1)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}=\frac{1}{M+\sqrt{M^{2}-1}}=M-\sqrt{M^{2}-1}~\mbox{ and }~R_{0}=M\rho_{0}^{2}.

This result is sharp, with the extremal function

(1.2)

\displaystyle f_{0}(z)=Mz\left(\frac{1-Mz}{M-z}\right).

The Bloch theorem asserts the existence of a positive constant number b such that if
f
is an analytic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, then
f(\mathbb{D})
contains a disk of radius
b
, that is, a disk of radius
b
which is the univalent image of some region in
\mathbb{D}
. Such a disk is called “schlicht disk” for
f
. The supremum of all such constants
b
is called the Bloch constant (see
[
3
,
15
]
).

In 2000, H.H. Chen
et al.

[
3
]
obtained two versions of Laudau-type theorems for bounded harmonic mappings in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
However, their results are not sharp. Dorff and Nowak
[

]
, Grigoryan
[

]
,
S.L. Chen
et al.

[

,

,

]
, Huang
et al.

[

,
25
]
, Liu
et al.

[

22
]
and H.H. Chen
et al.

[

]
improved their results respectively. In particular, the following sharp version of Landau-type theorem for harmonic mappings with bounded maximum distortion was obtained in
[
16
,
20
,
22
]
.

Theorem A.
(
[
16
,
20
,
22
]
) Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a harmonic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
and
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
.

(1)

If
\varLambda_{f}(z)\leq 1
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
and
f(\mathbb{D})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}
,
and the result is sharp.

(2)

If
\varLambda_{f}(z)<\varLambda
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then
\varLambda>1
and
f
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho}
with
\rho=\frac{1}{\Lambda}
, and
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R}
, where

R=\varLambda+(\varLambda^{3}-\varLambda)\ln\Big(1-\frac{1}{\varLambda^{2}}\Big).

The result is sharp.

The sharp form of Landau-type theorem for harmonic mappings with
f(0)=0,\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
|f(z)|\leq 1
in the unit disk was also obtained in
[
19
]
. In 2020, Liu
et al.
proved the sharp result of Landau-Bloch type theorem for strongly-bounded harmonic mappings when
M>1
in
[
21
]
, and obtained several new sharp versions of Landau-Bloch type theorems of harmonic mappings. We recall one of these results as follows.

Theorem B.
(
[
21
, Theorem 3.5]
) Suppose that
M>1
. Let
f(z)
be a harmonic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=\lambda_{f}(0)-1=0
, and

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{b_{n}}\overline{z^{n}}

satisfying the following inequality

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}n(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n-1}\leq\frac{(M^{2}-1)(2Mr-r^{2})}{(M-r)^{2}},\quad 0\leq r\leq\rho_{0}=M-\sqrt{M^{2}-1}.

Then
f(z)
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
and
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
, where
R_{0}=M\rho_{0}^{2}
. This result is sharp, with
f_{0}(z)=Mz\frac{1-Mz}{M-z}
being an extremal mapping.

Recently, Allu and Kumar
[

]
established two new versions of Laudau-Bloch type theorem for
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})
-elliptic harmonic mappings and K-quasiregular mappings as follows.

Theorem C.
(
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
) Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})
-elliptic harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}
and
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{1}}
, where

\rho_{1}=\frac{1}{1+K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}},\quad R_{1}=1+(K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}})\ln\Big(1-\frac{1}{1+K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}\Big).

Theorem D.
(
[
2
, Theorem 2.7]
)
Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a K-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,J_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{2}}
and
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{2}})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{2}}
, where

\rho_{2}=\frac{1}{1+K^{3/2}\Lambda},\quad R_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}+K\Lambda\ln\Big(1-\frac{1}{1+K^{3/2}\Lambda}\Big).

However, Theorems C and D are not sharp. It is then natural to raise the following problems:

Problem 1
.

Can we improve the results in Theorems C and D? Can we establish several sharp results?

-quasiregular mappings are also provided. In particular, these results are sharp in some given cases. In Section

Theorem 1
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})
-elliptic harmonic mapping defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}

Next, we provide several results and a conjecture of coefficient estimates for normalized
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})

Theorem 3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})
-elliptic harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,\,J_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and

(2.1)

\displaystyle h(z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{a_{n}z^{n}},\quad g(z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{b_{n}z^{n}}.

Then, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{K+K^{\prime}}}\leq|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|\leq\sqrt{K+K^{\prime}},

and

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{(K+K^{\prime})(K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{2}\Lambda^{2}+4K^{\prime}})^{2}-4}{2n(K+K^{\prime})(K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{2}\Lambda^{2}+4K^{\prime}})},\,n=2,3,\ldots.

When
K=1,K^{\prime}=0
, the above estimates are sharp for all
n=2,3,\ldots
, with the extremal functions

(2.2)

\displaystyle f_{n}(z)=\Lambda^{2}z-\int_{0}^{z}{\frac{\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda}{\Lambda+z^{n-1}}dz}.

Applying a similar method as for that used in Theorem
3
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a
(K,K^{{}^{\prime}})
-elliptic harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,\,\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and let
h,g
be given by (
2.1
). Then, we have that

\displaystyle 1\leq|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|\leq\frac{K+\sqrt{K^{2}+4K^{\prime}}}{2},

and

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{(K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{2}\Lambda^{2}+4K^{\prime}})^{2}-4}{2n(K\Lambda+\sqrt{K^{2}\Lambda^{2}+4K^{\prime}})},\,n=2,3,\ldots.

When
K=1,K^{\prime}=0
, the above estimates are sharp for all
n=2,3,\ldots
, with the extremal functions given by (
2.2
).

Setting
K^{\prime}=0
in Theorem
3
, we get the following result.

Corollary 4
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a K-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,J_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and let
h,g
be given by (
2.1
). Then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\leq|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|\leq\sqrt{K},\quad\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{K^{3}\Lambda^{2}-1}{n\Lambda K^{2}},\,n=2,3,\ldots.

When
K=1
, the above estimates are sharp for all
n=2,3,\ldots
, with the extremal functions given by (
2.2
).

Setting
K^{\prime}=0
in Corollary
3
, we get the following result.

Corollary 5
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a K-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all

, and let

be given by (

, with the extremal functions

given by (

Note that

\frac{K^{2}\Lambda^{2}-1}{n\Lambda K}=K\frac{\Lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{K^{2}}}{n\Lambda},

we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
F=h+\overline{g}
be a
K
-quasiregular mapping in unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
F(0)=0,\lambda_{F}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{F}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all

be given by (

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq K\frac{\Lambda^{2}-1}{n\Lambda},n=2,3,\ldots,

and the above estimates are sharp for all

F_{n}(z)=\frac{K+1}{2}\bigg(\Lambda^{2}z-\int_{0}^{z}{\frac{\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda}{\Lambda+z^{n-1}}}dz\bigg)+\overline{\frac{K-1}{2}\bigg(\Lambda^{2}z-\int_{0}^{z}{\frac{\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda}{\Lambda+z^{n-1}}dz}\bigg)}.

Now, we establish three versions of Landau-Bloch type theorem for sense-preserving harmonic mappings and a new version of Landau-Bloch type theorem for K-quasiregular mapping as follows:

Theorem 4
.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,h^{\prime}(0)-1=g^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
for all

\lambda\geq 1
,
f
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{4}}
with
r_{4}=r_{4}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2(\sqrt{2}+1)\lambda}
, and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{4}})
contains a schlicht disk with radius

\sigma_{4}=\frac{(\sqrt{2}+1)\lambda}{2}\bigg\{1+\Big((\sqrt{2}+1)^{2}\lambda^{2}-1\Big)\ln\Big(1-\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)\bigg\}.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
M>1
. Let
f=h+\overline{g}
be a sense-preserving harmonic mapping in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=f_{z}(0)-1=f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0
and
|h(z)|\leq M
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

(see, for example, p.297 in

]
)

Next, we establish a new lemma, which play a key role in the proof of our main results.

Lemma H.
Suppose that
x\geq 1
. Then
1+x\ln\frac{x}{1+x}<\frac{1}{2(x+0.5)}
.

Proof:
Let
f(x)=1+x\ln\frac{x}{1+x}-\frac{1}{2x+1}
. Then, a direct computation yields

f^{\prime}(x)=\ln\frac{x}{1+x}+\frac{1}{1+x}+\frac{1}{2(x+0.5)^{2}},

f^{\prime}(1)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{9}-\ln 2>0
and

\lim_{x\to+\infty}f^{\prime}(x)=0.

Also, for
x\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x(1+x)}-\frac{1}{(1+x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x+0.5)^{3}}=\frac{1}{x(1+x)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x+0.5)^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{8}(2x-1)}{x(1+x)^{2}(x+0.5)^{3}}<0,

showing that

is strictly decreasing on
[1,\infty)
, and thus, we have that
f^{\prime}(x)>0
in
[1,\infty)
. This implies that
f(x)
is strictly increasing in
[1,\infty)
. We note that
f(1)=1-\ln 2-\frac{1}{3}<0
and

\lim_{x\to+\infty}f(x)=0,

and therefore, we obtain that
f(x)<0
in
[1,\infty)
, and the proof is complete.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
9

Let
z=re^{i\theta},\,r\in[0,1)
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
, then

f(re^{i\theta})=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{a_{n}r^{n}e^{in\theta}}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\overline{b_{n}}r^{n}e^{-in\theta}}.

Since
|zf_{z}(z)+\overline{z}f_{\overline{z}}(z)|<\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, by Parseval’s identity, we have that

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{n^{2}(|a_{n}|^{2}+|b_{n}|^{2})r^{2n}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{|re^{i\theta}f_{z}(re^{i\theta})+\overline{re^{i\theta}}f_{\overline{z}}(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta}\leq\Lambda^{2}.

Notice that
\lambda_{f}(0)=||a_{1}|-|b_{1}||=1
, let
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
, we have that
\Lambda\geq 1
, and

\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}{n^{2}(|a_{n}|^{2}+|b_{n}|^{2})}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{n^{2}(|a_{n}|^{2}+|b_{n}|^{2})}-(|a_{1}|^{2}+|b_{1}|^{2})\leq\Lambda^{2}-1.

Since
a_{n}b_{n}=0
for
n=2,3,\ldots
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}{n^{2}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)^{2}}=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}{n^{2}(|a_{n}|^{2}+|b_{n}|^{2})}\leq\Lambda^{2}-1.

For any complex number
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=1
, let

with

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}{(a_{1}+\lambda b_{1})dz}\bigg|-\bigg|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}{\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}{n(a_{n}+\lambda b_{n})z^{n-1}}dz}\bigg|\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

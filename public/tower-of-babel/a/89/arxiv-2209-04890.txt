An analogue of Kida’s formula in graph theory
Anwesh Ray, Daniel Vallières

Abstract
. Let ℓ \ell be a rational prime and let p : Y → X p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X be a Galois cover of finite graphs whose Galois group is a finite ℓ \ell -group. Consider a ℤ ℓ \mathbb{Z}_{\ell} -tower above X X and its pullback along p p . Assuming that all the graphs in the pullback are connected, one obtains a ℤ ℓ \mathbb{Z}_{\ell} -tower above Y Y . Under the assumption that the Iwasawa μ \mu -invariant of the tower above X X vanishes, we prove a formula relating the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariant of the ℤ ℓ \mathbb{Z}_{\ell} -tower above X X to the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariant of the pullback. This formula is analogous to Kida’s formula in classical Iwasawa theory. We present an application to the study of structural properties of certain noncommutative pro- ℓ \ell towers of graphs, based on an analogy with classical results of Cuoco on the growth of Iwasawa invariants in ℤ ℓ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2} -extensions of number fields. Our investigations are illustrated by explicit examples.

1.
Introduction

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}_{2}
be a branched cover of compact Riemann surfaces of degree
d
. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula stipulates that

(1.1)

\chi(\mathcal{S}_{1})=d\chi(\mathcal{S}_{2})-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}(e_{P}-1),

where
\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic of a compact Riemann surface, and
e_{P}
is the ramification index at the point
P
. Note that the above sum is finite since
e_{P}=1
for all but finitely many points
P
. If the cover is unramified, then (
1.1
) simply becomes

(1.2)

\chi(\mathcal{S}_{1})=d\chi(\mathcal{S}_{2}).

Using the relation
\chi(\mathcal{S})=2-2g(\mathcal{S})
, where
g(\mathcal{S})
denotes the genus of
\mathcal{S}
, (
1.1
) can also be written as

(1.3)

2g(\mathcal{S}_{1})-2=d(2g(\mathcal{S}_{2})-2)+\sum_{P\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}(e_{P}-1).

Kida proved an analogous formula in the context of Iwasawa theory, which we now explain. Throughout,
\ell
will be a rational prime, and let
\mu_{\ell^{\infty}}
be the
\ell
-power roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Let
K
be a
CM
number field. The cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-extension of
K
is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-extension contained in
K(\mu_{\ell^{\infty}})
and is denoted by
K_{\infty}
. Associated to
K_{\infty}
is a tower of
CM
-fields

K=K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq K_{n}\subseteq\ldots

for which
K_{n}/K
is Galois with Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
K_{n}^{+}
be the maximal totally real subfield of
K_{n}
and
h_{n}^{+}
be the class number of
K_{n}^{+}
. Denote by
h_{n}
, the class number of
K_{n}
and let
h_{n}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=h_{n}/h_{n}^{+}
denote the relative class number which is known to be an integer. Iwasawa showed (see
[
Iwa59
]
and
[
Iwa73
]
) that there exist nonnegative integers
\mu^{-}=\mu_{K}^{-},\lambda^{-}=\lambda_{K}^{-}
and an integer
\nu^{-}=\nu_{K}^{-}
such that for
n
large, one has

(1.4)

{\rm ord}_{\ell}(h_{n}^{-})=\mu^{-}\ell^{n}+\lambda^{-}n+\nu^{-},

where
{\rm ord}_{\ell}
is the usual
\ell
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
. Let now
E/F
be a Galois extension of
CM
-fields for which
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
is an
\ell
-group, and assume for simplicity that
\ell>2
and that
F
contains the
\ell
-th roots of unity. Kida showed in
[
Kid80
]
that if
\mu_{F}^{-}=0
, then
\mu_{E}^{-}=0
, and

(1.5)

2\lambda_{E}^{-}-2=[E_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F_{\infty}](2\lambda_{F}^{-}-2)+\sum_{w}(e_{w}-1),

where the sum is over all nonarchimedean places of
E_{\infty}
that split completely in
E_{\infty}/E_{\infty}^{+}
and that do not lie above
\ell
. Several authors pointed out the striking similarity between Kida’s formula (
1.5
) and the Riemann-Hurwitz formula (
1.3
).

In this paper, we study analogies between Galois theoretic objects in arithmetic and their topological counterparts in graph theory. The properties of the Dedekind zeta function of a number field correlate to the arithmetic distribution properties of its primes. On the other hand, the Ihara zeta function in graph theory behaves much like the Dedekind zeta function, and has many interesting combinatorial applications, cf.
[
Ter11
]
. There are also many analogies between graphs and algebraic curves. For instance,
[
BN09
]
establishes an analogue of the Abel-Jacobi map and the Riemann-Roch theorem in graph theory. There is a natural notion of the class group in graph theory (known in the literature under various names such as the critical group, the sandpile group, the Jacobian group, the Picard group, etc), which exhibits properties similar to its number theoretic and geometric counterparts. From an Iwasawa theoretic viewpoint, it is of natural interest to study the behavior of class numbers in various towers of graphs. Recently, a result similar to Iwasawa’s theorem (
1.4
) above has been observed in graph theory. Let
X
be a connected graph (with loops and parallel edges allowed), and consider a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower above
X
. This means that we are given a sequence of covers of connected graphs

(1.6)

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\ldots

for which the cover
X_{n}/X
obtained by composing the covers
X_{n}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X
is Galois with group of covering transformations isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Then,
[
MV22b
, Theorem 6.1]
shows that there exist nonnegative integers
\mu,\lambda
and an integer
\nu
such that for
n
large, one has

{\rm ord}_{\ell}(\kappa_{n})=\mu\ell^{n}+\lambda n+\nu,

where
\kappa_{n}
is the class number (i.e., number of spanning trees) of the graph
X_{n}
. The approach is based on studying the properties of a suitable analogue of the
\ell
-adic
L
-function in graph theory, obtained from the
\ell
-adic interpolation of the special values of the Artin-Ihara
L
-functions. For a more algebraic approach to this result, one is referred to
[
Gon21
,
Gon22
]
. We shall refer to
\mu
and
\lambda
as the Iwasawa invariants of the tower (
1.6
). Furthermore, it is known (see for instance
[
Sun13
, page 55]
) that if
Y\rightarrow X
is a cover of connected graphs of degree
d
, then one has

(1.7)

\chi(Y)=d\chi(X),

where
\chi
here denotes the Euler characteristic of a graph. It becomes then natural to study whether an analogue of Kida’s formula holds true in graph theory as well. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
X
be a connected graph for which
\chi(X)\neq 0
and consider a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower

(1.8)

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\ldots

Let
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
be a Galois cover of graphs whose Galois group is an
\ell
-group and pull back the tower (
1.8
) along
p
to obtain another
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower

(1.9)

Y\leftarrow Y\times_{X}X_{1}\leftarrow Y\times_{X}X_{2}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow Y\times_{X}X_{n}\leftarrow\ldots

Assume that all the graphs in both towers are connected. Further, let us denote the Iwasawa invariants of the tower (
1.8
) by
\mu_{X}
and
\lambda_{X}
and the Iwasawa invariants of the tower (
1.9
) by
\mu_{Y}
and
\lambda_{Y}
. Then
\mu_{X}=0
if and only if
\mu_{Y}=0
in which case

(1.10)

\lambda_{Y}+1=[Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X](\lambda_{X}+1),

where
[Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X]
is the degree of the cover
Y/X
.

Theorem
A
can be viewed as an analogue of Kida’s formula (
1.5
), but in graph theory. We are aware of three different proofs of Kida’s formula: the original one by Kida in
[
Kid80
]
using genus theory, the one by Iwasawa in
[
Iwa81
]
where he proves a result analogous to the Chevalley-Weil formula for a branched Galois cover of compact Riemann surfaces, and the one by Sinnott in
[
Sin84
]
using
\ell
-adic
L
-functions. Our approach here to proving Theorem
A
is inspired by
[
Sin84
]
.

In §
5
, we present an application of Theorem
4.1
to the study of certain growth questions in noncommutative pro-
\ell
towers of graphs. Our investigations are inspired by a result of Cuoco
[
Cuo80
]
, who studied the growth of Iwasawa invariants in a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
-tower over a number field
K
. This provides us insight into the structural properties of certain non-commutative pro-
\ell
examples.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
BN09
]
, Baker and Norine prove that if
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
is a branched cover of graphs of degree
d
in the sense of
[
Sun13
, Notes 5.6]
, then

(1.11)

\chi(Y)=d\chi(X)-\sum_{w\in V_{Y}}(e_{w}-1),

where
e_{w}
is the ramification index of the vertex
w
. In the present paper (as well as in
[
Val21
,
MV22a
,
MV22b
]
,
[
LV22
]
,
[

]
,
[
DRLV22
]
, and
[
Gon21
,

]

), and this might explain the lack of ramification indices in (

).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Graphs and covers of graphs

We think of a graph as presented in
[
Ser77
]
and
[
Sun13
]
. Thus, a graph
X
consists of a set of vertices
V_{X}
, a set of directed edges
\mathbf{E}_{X}
, an incidence map
{\rm inc}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{E}_{X}\rightarrow V_{X}\times V_{X}
given by
e\mapsto{\rm inc}(e)=(o(e),t(e))
, and an inversion map
\mathbf{E}_{X}\rightarrow\mathbf{E}_{X}
given by
e\mapsto\bar{e}
satisfying

(1)

\bar{e}\neq e
,

(2)

\bar{\bar{e}}=e
,

(3)

o(\bar{e})=t(e)
and
t(\bar{e})=o(e)
,

for all
e\in\mathbf{E}_{X}
. For
v\in V_{X}
, we let

\mathbf{E}_{X,v}=\{e\in\mathbf{E}_{X}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,o(e)=v\}.

The set of undirected edges is obtained by identifying
e
with
\bar{e}
and will be denoted by
E_{X}
. Note that we allow loops and parallel edges. Such graphs are sometimes called multigraphs, but we will refer to them as graphs for simplicity.
We assume throughout this paper that all of our graphs are finite unless otherwise stated. This means that both
V_{X}
and
\mathbf{E}_{X}
are finite sets.
Setting
b_{i}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\rm rank}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,H_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the Euler characteristic
\chi(X)
is defined as follows

\chi(X)=b_{0}(X)-b_{1}(X).

We note that
b_{i}(X)=0
for all
i\geq 2
.
If
X
is connected, then

\chi(X)=|V_{X}|-|E_{X}|

as explained for instance in
[
Sun13
, Section 4.4]
. In this case, we also note that
\chi(X)=1-b_{1}(X)
. The valency of a vertex
v\in V_{X}
is defined as follows

{\rm val}_{X}(v)=|\mathbf{E}_{X,v}|.

Henceforth, we choose a labeling of the vertices of
X
, say
V_{X}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{g}\}
. The degree (or valency) matrix associated to
X
is the diagonal matrix
D=(d_{ij})
satisfying

(2.1)

d_{ii}={\rm val}_{X}(v_{i}),

for all
i=1,\ldots,g
. The adjacency matrix
A=(a_{ij})
of
X
is the symmetric matrix whose entries are specified as follows

(2.2)

a_{ij}=\begin{cases}\text{twice the number of undirected loops at }v_{i},&\text{ if }i=j;\\
\text{the number of undirected edges between }v_{i}\text{ and }v_{j},&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The matrix
Q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=D-A
is called the Laplacian matrix and is known to be singular. In fact, it is easy to see that any vector whose entries are all the same is in the null-space of
Q
.

In order to introduce the Galois theory of graphs, we first recall the notion of a morphism in the category of graphs. Let
X
and
Y
be two graphs. A morphism of graphs
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
consists of two functions
f_{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{Y}\rightarrow V_{X}
and
f_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{E}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathbf{E}_{X}
satisfying

(1)

f_{V}(o(e))=o(f_{E}(e))
,

(2)

f_{V}(t(e))=t(f_{E}(e))
,

(3)

\overline{f_{E}(e)}=f_{E}(\bar{e})
,

for all
e\in\mathbf{E}_{X}
. We will often write
f
for both
f_{V}
and
f_{E}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two graphs and let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
be a morphism of graphs. If
f
satisfies the following conditions

(1)

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{Y}\rightarrow V_{X}
is surjective,

(2)

for all
w\in V_{Y}
, the restriction
f|_{\mathbf{E}_{Y,w}}
induces a bijection

f|_{\mathbf{E}_{Y,w}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{E}_{Y,w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\approx}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbf{E}_{X,f(w)}},

then
f
is said to be a cover. We shall often say that
Y/X
is a cover when the projection map
f
is understood from the context.

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
Sun13
, Section 5.1]
, Sunada requires both
X
and
Y
to be connected in the definition of a cover, but we do not make this requirement here. If
Y
is assumed to be connected, then so is
X
. Indeed, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{X}
, then let
w_{1},w_{2}\in V_{Y}
be such that
f(w_{i})=v_{i}
for
i=1,2
. If
Y
is connected, there exists a path
c
going from
w_{i}
to
w_{j}
. The image
f(c)
is a path in
X
going from
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
showing that
X
is connected as well. On the other hand, if
X
is assumed to be connected, then
Y
is not necessarily connected.

If
Y/X
is a cover and
Y
is connected, then there exists a positive integer
d
such that
|f^{-1}(v)|=d
for all
v\in V_{X}
. The integer
d
is called the degree of
Y/X
and will be denoted by
[Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X]
. If
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
is a cover, then we let

{\rm Aut}_{f}(Y/X)=\{\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(Y)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,f\circ\sigma=f\}.

Definition 2.3
.

The cover
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
is called Galois if the following two conditions are satisfied.

(1)

The graph
Y
is connected (and hence also
X
by Remark
2.2
).

(2)

The group
{\rm Aut}_{f}(Y/X)
acts transitively on the fiber
f^{-1}(v)
for all
v\in V_{X}
.

If
Y/X
is a Galois cover, then
[Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X]=|{\rm Aut}_{f}(Y/X)|
, and we write
{\rm Gal}_{f}(Y/X)
, or
{\rm Gal}(Y/X)
if
f
is understood, instead of
{\rm Aut}_{f}(Y/X)
. One has the usual Galois correspondence between subgroups of
{\rm Gal}(Y/X)
and equivalence classes of intermediate covers of
Y/X
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\ell
be a rational prime, and let
X
be a connected graph. A
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower above
X
consists of a sequence of covers of connected graphs

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\ldots

such that for all positive integer
n
, the cover
X_{n}/X
obtained from composing the covers
X_{n}\rightarrow X_{n-1}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X
is Galois with Galois group
{\rm Gal}(X_{n}/X)
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

2.2.
Artin-Ihara
L
-functions

Let
Y/X
be an abelian cover of graphs, meaning that
Y/X
is Galois with abelian group of covering transformations. The theory can be extended to a non-abelian setting, but we restrict ourselves to the abelian setting for simplicity, see
[
Ter11
]
for further details. Set
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(Y/X)
and denote by
\widehat{G}={\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
the group of complex valued characters of
G
. If
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, then the corresponding Artin-Ihara
L
-function is defined as follows

L_{Y/X}(u,\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{c}}\left(1-\psi\left(\frac{Y/X}{\mathfrak{c}}\right)u^{l(\mathfrak{c})}\right)^{-1}.

The above product runs over all primes
\mathfrak{c}
of
X
;
l(\mathfrak{c})
is the length of
\mathfrak{c}
, and
\left(\frac{Y/X}{\mathfrak{c}}\right)\in G
denotes the corresponding Frobenius automorphism.

Remark 2.5
.

For the definition of a prime in a graph, see
[
Ter11
, Section 2.2]
, and for the definition of the Frobenius automorphism, see
[
Ter11
, Section 16]
.

Recall that we labeled the vertices of
X
as follows
V_{X}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{g}\}
. For each
i=1,\ldots,g
, choose a vertex
w_{i}
of
Y
in the fiber of
v_{i}
. For
\sigma\in G
, define the matrix
A(\sigma)=(a_{ij}(\sigma))
as follows

a_{ij}(\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\text{twice the number of undirected loops at the vertex }w_{i},&\text{if }i=j\text{ and }\sigma=1;\\
\text{the number of undirected edges connecting }w_{i}\text{ to }w_{j}^{\sigma},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, one defines the
twisted adjacency matrix
as follows

(2.3)

A_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\sigma\in G}\psi(\sigma)\cdot A(\sigma).

Then the three-term determinant formula
[
Ter11
, Theorem 18.15]
asserts that

(2.4)

L_{Y/X}(u,\psi)^{-1}=\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-\chi(X)}\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{I}-A_{\psi}u+(D-\operatorname{I})u^{2}\right).

In the above formula, we recall that
\chi(X)
is the Euler characteristic of
X
,
D
is the degree matrix of
X
(cf. (
2.1
)),
A_{\psi}
the
twisted adjacency matrix
associated to
\psi
and the cover
Y/X
(cf. (
2.3
)), and
\operatorname{I}
is the identity matrix. For ease of notation, set

(2.5)

h_{Y/X}(u,\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{I}-A_{\psi}u+(D-\operatorname{I})u^{2}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}[\psi][u],

where
\mathbb{Z}[\psi]
denotes the ring of integers in the cyclotomic number field

\mathbb{Q}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\{\psi(\sigma)\,|\,\sigma\in G\}).

One can check that
h_{Y/X}(u,\psi)
does not depend on the choice of the vertices
w_{i}
. When
\psi=\psi_{0}
is the trivial character, then we write
h_{X}(u)
instead of
h_{Y/X}(u,\psi_{0})
, and
Z_{X}(u)
instead of
L_{Y/X}(u,\psi_{0})
. The function
Z_{X}(u)
is the Ihara zeta function of the graph
X
introduced in
[
Iha66
]
and reinterpreted in the context of graph theory by Sunada in
[
Sun86
]
following a suggestion of Serre. Note that
A_{\psi_{0}}=A
, the usual adjacency matrix of
X
(cf. (
2.2
)). Note that when the cover
Y/X
is understood, it is suppressed in our notation, and we shall simply write
h_{X}(u,\psi)
instead of
h_{Y/X}(u,\psi)
.

It was shown by Stark and Terras (see
[
ST00
, Proposition 3]
) that the Artin-Ihara
L
-functions satisfy the usual Artin formalism. It follows that

Z_{Y}(u)=Z_{X}(u)\cdot\prod_{\psi\neq\psi_{0}}L_{Y/X}(u,\psi).

Furthermore, (
2.4
) and (
1.7
) imply that

(2.6)

h_{Y}(u)=h_{X}(u)\prod_{\psi\neq\psi_{0}}h_{Y/X}(u,\psi).

If
Z
is an arbitrary connected graph, note that
h_{Z}(1)=0
since the Laplacian matrix
Q=D-A
is singular, and it follows from (
2.5
) that
h_{Z}(1)=\operatorname{det}(Q)
. In
[
Has90
]
, Hashimoto showed that for a connected graph
Z
, one has

(2.7)

h_{Z}^{\prime}(1)=-2\chi(Z)\kappa_{Z},

where
\kappa_{Z}
is the number of spanning trees of
Z
(see also
[
Nor98
]
).

Coming back to our abelian cover
Y/X
, and assuming that
\chi(X)\neq 0
, (
1.7
), (
2.6
), and (
2.7
) together imply that the following identity holds

(2.8)

|G|\cdot\kappa_{Y}=\kappa_{X}\cdot\prod_{\psi\neq\psi_{0}}h_{Y/X}(1,\psi),

(cf.
[
Val21
, Equation (7)]
). Very often, we shall make the following assumption on our graphs.

Assumption 2.6
.

Let
X
be a connected graph. We assume that
\chi(X)\neq 0
.

Remark 2.7
.

The special case when
\chi(X)=0
can be treated separately. If
X
is a connected graph such that
\chi(X)=0
, note that removing all vertices of degree one and their adjacent edge does not change the primes, the Ihara zeta function, and the number of spanning trees. Thus we can assume that
{\rm val}_{X}(v)\geq 2
for all
v\in V_{X}
. In this case,
X
is necessarily the cycle graph on
g
vertices, which we denote by
C_{g}
. Now,
\pi_{1}(C_{g},v_{0})
is a free group on one generator; hence, it is abelian, and

\pi_{1}(C_{g},v_{0})\simeq H_{1}(C_{g},\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}.

It follows that up to isomorphism, there is a unique Galois cover
Y/C_{g}
of degree
n
, it is abelian, with
{\rm Gal}(Y/C_{g})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. By (
1.7
), we have
Y\simeq C_{gn}
. Also note that the number of spanning trees of
C_{g}
is
g
, and thus, the number of spanning trees of
Y
is
gn
. Thus, the growth patterns in the number of spanning trees in any abelian tower is well understood, due to this explicit description.

2.3.
Constructions of Galois covers via voltage assignments

Our main reference for this section is
[

]
. If

is a graph, then we say that a subset

In other words, an orientation

Remark 2.8

In

Given a finite group

2.3.1.
Connectedness of graphs obtained from voltage assignments

Let
X
be a connected graph,
S
an orientation for
X
,
G
a finite group, and let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow G
be a function which we extend to all of

as usual by setting

(2.13)

\alpha(\bar{s})=\alpha(s)^{-1}.

If

is a path in

, then we set

\alpha(c)=\alpha(e_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha(e_{n})\in G.

The condition (

) guarantees that if

and

are homotopically equivalent, then
\alpha(c_{1})=\alpha(c_{2})
. Choosing an arbitrary vertex

, it follows that

induces a group morphism

(2.14)

\rho_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{1}(X,v_{0})\rightarrow G,

defined via
\rho_{\alpha}([\gamma])=\alpha(\gamma)
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
X
be a connected graph and let
S,G,\alpha
be as above. Then the graph
X(G,S,\alpha)
is connected if and only if the group morphism
\rho_{\alpha}
defined above in (
2.14
) is surjective.

Proof.

Assume first that
\rho_{\alpha}
is surjective. Since
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X(G,S,\alpha)\rightarrow X
is a cover, every directed edge
e
of
X
can be uniquely lifted to a directed edge of
X(G,S,\alpha)
once a vertex in the fiber of
o(e)
has been chosen. The connectedness of
X
implies that given any vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V_{X}
and a choice of
w_{1}\in p^{-1}(v_{1})
, there exists a path in
X(G,S,\alpha)
starting at
w_{1}
and ending at a vertex in
p^{-1}(v_{2})
. To show that
X(G,S,\alpha)
is connected, it suffices then to show that every vertex in
p^{-1}(v_{0})
can be connected by a path in
X(G,S,\alpha)
. Let thus
(v_{0},\sigma),(v_{0},\tau)\in p^{-1}(v_{0})
be arbitrary. Since
\rho_{\alpha}
is assumed to be surjective, there exists a loop
\gamma
in
X
based at
v_{0}
satisfying
\rho_{\alpha}(v_{0})=\sigma^{-1}\cdot\tau
. One has

\gamma=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots e_{n},

for some
e_{i}\in\mathbf{E}_{X}
. Then, the path

(e_{1},\sigma)(e_{2},\sigma\cdot\alpha(e_{1}))\ldots(e_{n},\sigma\cdot\alpha(e_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha(e_{n-1}))

in
X(G,S,\alpha)
connects
(v_{0},\sigma)
to
(v_{0},\tau)
showing the claim.

Conversely, if
X(G,S,\alpha)
is connected, then there is a path
c
in
X(G,S,\alpha)
going from
(v_{0},1_{G})
to
(v_{0},\sigma)
. The path
c
is of the form

(e_{1},1_{G})(e_{2},\alpha(e_{1}))\ldots(e_{n},\alpha(e_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha(e_{n-1})),

for some directed edges
e_{i}\in\mathbf{E}_{X}
satisfying

(1)

o(e_{1})=v_{0}=t(e_{n})
,

(2)

t(e_{i})=o(e_{i+1})
for

,

(3)

\sigma=\alpha(e_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha(e_{n})
.

Then, the loop
\gamma=p(c)
satisfies
\rho_{\alpha}([\gamma])=\sigma
and since
\sigma\in G
was chosen arbitrarily, this shows the surjectivity of
\rho_{\alpha}
.
∎

can be used to check if

3.
Pullbacks

In the category of graphs, there is a well-defined notion of pullback as was pointed out for instance in
[
Sta83
]
. If one starts with a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{2},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{2}}

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are morphisms of graphs, one defines the pullback
Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}
by defining the set of vertices to be

V=\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in V_{Y_{1}}\times V_{Y_{2}}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p_{1}(w_{1})=p_{2}(w_{2})\},

and the set of directed edges to be

\mathbf{E}=\{(e_{1},e_{2})\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1}}\times\mathbf{E}_{Y_{2}}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,p_{1}(e_{1})=p_{2}(e_{2})\}.

Further, define

o(e_{1},e_{2})=(o(e_{1}),o(e_{2})),t(e_{1},e_{2})=(t(e_{1}),t(e_{2}))\text{ and }\overline{(e_{1},e_{2})}=(\bar{e}_{1},\bar{e}_{2}).

We leave it to the reader to check that
Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}
is a graph. For
i=1,2
, the maps
\pi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{i}
given by

\pi_{i}(w_{1},w_{2})=w_{i}\text{ and }\pi_{i}(e_{1},e_{2})=e_{i}

are morphisms of graphs, and the triple
(Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
satisfies the usual universal property satisfied by a pullback. We leave the details to the reader.

Remark 3.1
.

Even if
X
,
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are connected graphs, then the pullback
Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}
is not necessarily connected. For example, take
X=B_{1}
, the bouquet with one undirected loop, and
Y
the graph consisting of two vertices
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
and two parallel undirected edges between the two vertices. Say
\mathbf{E}_{X}=\{s,\bar{s}\}
and
\mathbf{E}_{Y}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\bar{e}_{1},\bar{e}_{2}\}
, where
e_{1}
goes from
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
and
e_{2}
from
v_{2}
to
v_{1}
. Consider also the morphism of graphs
p\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\rightarrow X
given by
e_{i}\mapsto s
and by mapping the two vertices to the unique one of
X
. If we take
Y_{1}=Y_{2}=Y
and
p_{1}=p_{2}=p
, then the graph
Y\times_{X}Y
is disconnected; it is two disjoint copies of
Y
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{2},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{2}}

in the category of graphs. If
p_{2}
is a cover, then so is
\pi_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}\rightarrow Y_{1}
.

Proof.

For the first property, let
w_{1}\in V_{Y_{1}}
. Since
p_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{Y_{2}}\rightarrow V_{X}
is assumed to be surjective, there exists
w_{2}\in V_{Y_{2}}
such that
p_{2}(w_{2})=p_{1}(w_{1})
. Then
(w_{1},w_{2})\in V_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}}
, and
\pi_{1}(w_{1},w_{2})=w_{1}
.

For the second property, let
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in V_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}}
. We show first that

(3.1)

\pi_{1}|_{\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2}},w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2},w}\rightarrow\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1},w_{1}}

is injective. Let
(e_{1},e_{2}),(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2},w}
, then in particular
o(e_{2})=o(\varepsilon_{2})=w_{2}
. If
\pi_{1}(e_{1},e_{2})=\pi_{1}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
, then
e_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}
. It follows that
p_{2}(e_{2})=p_{1}(e_{1})=p_{1}(\varepsilon_{1})=p_{2}(\varepsilon_{2})
and thus
p_{2}(e_{2})=p_{2}(\varepsilon_{2})
. Since
e_{2},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{2},w_{2}}
, the assumption on
p_{2}
implies that
e_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}
and this shows that (
3.1
) is injective. In order to show that (

) is surjective, let
e_{1}\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1},w_{1}}
. Then
p_{1}(e_{1})\in\mathbf{E}_{X,p_{1}(w_{1})}=\mathbf{E}_{X,p_{2}(w_{2})}
. The assumption on
p_{2}
implies that there exists
e_{2}\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{2},w_{2}}
such that
p_{2}(e_{2})=p_{1}(e_{1})
. But then
(e_{1},e_{2})\in\mathbf{E}_{Y_{1}\times_{X}Y_{2},w}
and
\pi_{1}(e_{1},e_{2})=e_{1}
. This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.4

\mathfrak{o}
be the ring of integers of the local field
K=\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\mu_{\ell})
. If
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, then we define

A_{\psi,\rho}=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in S\\
{\rm inc}(s)=(v_{i},v_{j})\end{subarray}}\psi(\beta(s))\rho(\alpha(s))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in S\\
{\rm inc}(s)=(v_{j},v_{i})\end{subarray}}\psi(-\beta(s))\rho(-\alpha(s))\right)\in M_{g\times g}(\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket),

and set

f_{\psi}(T)=\operatorname{det}(D-A_{\psi,\rho})\in\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket,

where
D
is as usual the degree matrix of
X
. Note that

f_{\psi_{0}}(T)=f_{X,\alpha}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\llbracket T\rrbracket.

The characters of
G\times\Gamma_{n}
are of the form
\psi\otimes\phi
, where
\psi\in\widehat{G}
and
\phi\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{n}
. Now, view
\phi
as a character on
1\times\Gamma_{n}\leq G\times\Gamma_{n}
. We claim that

(4.2)

{\rm Ind}_{1\times\Gamma_{n}}^{G\times\Gamma_{n}}(\phi)\simeq\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}\psi\otimes\phi.

To prove the claim, let
W
be the
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{n}]
-module corresponding to the character
\phi
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[G\times\Gamma_{n}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{n}]}W

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{n}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{n}]}W

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{C}[G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W

\displaystyle\simeq\Big(\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}\mathbb{C}e_{\psi}\Big)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}\mathbb{C}e_{\psi}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W,

and this shows the claim (
4.2
) above. Using the Artin formalism satisfied by the Artin-Ihara
L
-functions
[
ST00
, Proposition 3]
and (
1.7
), we get

(4.3)

h_{Y}(u,\phi)=\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}h_{Y/X}(u,\psi\otimes\phi),

for all characters
\phi\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{n}
and for all
n\geq 0
.

Combining (
2.5
), (
2.15
), and (
2.16
) give

(4.4)

f_{\psi}(t_{\phi})=h_{X}(1,\psi\otimes\phi),

for all characters
\phi\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{n}
, for all
\psi\in\widehat{G}
, and for all
n\geq 0
. Combining further with (
4.3
), we obtain

f_{Y,\alpha\circ p}(t_{\phi})=\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}f_{\psi}(t_{\phi})

for all characters
\phi\in\widehat{\Gamma}_{n}
and for all
n\geq 0
. Since a power series in
\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket
has finitely many zeros in the unit disk
D=\{t\in\mathbb{C}_{\ell}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,|t|_{\ell}<1\}
, the equalities (
4.4
) above imply

(4.5)

f_{Y,\alpha\circ p}(T)=\prod_{\psi\in\widehat{G}}f_{\psi}(T).

Let
{\rm ord}_{\ell}
be the valuation on
\mathbb{C}_{\ell}
normalized so that
{\rm ord}_{\ell}(\ell)=1
. One defines the Iwasawa invariant of a non-zero power series

Q(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\ldots\in\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket

similarly as before, namely

\mu(Q(T))={\rm min}\{{\rm ord}_{\ell}(a_{i})\,|\,i\geq 0\},

and

\lambda(Q(T))={\rm min}\{i\geq 0\,|\,{\rm ord}_{\ell}(a_{i})=\mu(Q(T))\}.

Note that
\mu(Q(T))
is a nonnegative rational number and
\lambda(Q(T))
is an integer. If
Q_{1}(T),Q_{2}(T)\in\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket
, then

(4.6)

\mu(Q_{1}\cdot Q_{2})=\mu(Q_{1})+\mu(Q_{2})\text{ and }\lambda(Q_{1}\cdot Q_{2})=\lambda(Q_{1})+\lambda(Q_{2}).

If
\psi
is a non-trivial character of
G
, and
\mathcal{L}
is the unique maximal ideal of
\mathfrak{o}
, then

(4.7)

A_{\psi,\rho}\equiv A_{\rho}\pmod{M_{g\times g}(\mathcal{L}\llbracket T\rrbracket)}.

Let us denote the reduction map
\mathfrak{o}\llbracket T\rrbracket\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\llbracket T\rrbracket
by
f(T)\mapsto\bar{f}(T)
. Equation (
4.7
) implies

\bar{A}_{\psi,\rho}=\bar{A}_{\rho}\text{ in }M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\llbracket T\rrbracket),

and it follows that

(4.8)

\bar{f}_{\psi}(T)=\bar{f}_{X,\alpha}(T)\text{ in }\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\llbracket T\rrbracket,

for all non-trivial characters
\psi
of
G
. We deduce that if
\mu(f_{X,\alpha}(T))=0
, then
\mu(f_{\psi}(T))=0
for all non-trivial characters
\psi
of
G
. Combined with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) above, one gets that if
\mu(f_{X,\alpha}(T))=0
, then
\mu(f_{Y,\alpha\circ p}(T))=0
. Since the
\mu
invariant is nonnegative, the converse is clear. Under the assumption that
\mu(X,\alpha)=0
, it follows from (
4.8
) that

\lambda(f_{\psi}(T))=\lambda(f_{X,\alpha}(T)),

for all non-trivial characters
\psi
of
G
. Therefore, (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) again imply the desired result.

∎

\ell
towers

The Iwasawa theory of graphs was motivated by the study of growth properties of the number of spanning trees in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-towers of graphs. These
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-towers are described by voltage assignments, as described in §
2.3
. Of natural interest is to study similar questions for more general pro-
\ell
towers of graphs, subject to suitable conditions. As a first step, it seems of natural interest to consider
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{d}
-towers of connected graphs as is done for instance in
[
DV22
]
. We study a related theme, and our results are motivated by the results of Cuoco
[
Cuo80
]
, who studied the growth of Iwasawa invariants in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{2}
-extensions of number fields. We do however work in a more general setting, which we now explain.

Let
G
be an infinite pro-
\ell
group. The lower central
\ell
-series is defined recursively as follows
G_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G
, and
G_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(G_{n}\right)^{\ell}[G_{n},G]
. Set
G^{(n)}
to denote
G/G_{n}
. A set of elements
A_{1},\dots,A_{r}\in G
is a set of topological generators if the closure of the subgroup generated by them is
G
. The group
G
is said to be finitely generated if there is a finite set of topological generators. We recall the notion of a uniform pro-
\ell
group (cf.
[
DDSMS03
, Definition 4.1]
).

Definition 5.1
.

The group
G
is
uniform
if the following conditions are all satisfied

(1)

G
is finitely generated,

(2)

[G,G]\subseteq G^{\ell}
if
\ell
is odd and
[G,G]\subseteq G^{4}
if
\ell=2
,

(3)

[G_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{n+1}]=[G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{1}]
for all
n\geq 1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be so that
[G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{1}]=\ell^{d}
, then, we find that
[G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G_{n}]=\ell^{nd}
. A
G
-tower over a graph
X
is a sequence of connected graphs related by covering maps

(5.1)

X=Y_{0}\leftarrow Y_{1}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow Y_{n}\leftarrow\ldots,

such that the map
Y_{n}\rightarrow X
obtained by composing the maps
Y_{n}\rightarrow Y_{n-1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X
is a Galois cover with Galois group
G^{(n)}
. In §
5.2
, we shall construct an example for which
G
is the pro-
\ell
group
\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right)
. Such covers can be constructed from voltage assignments. In greater detail, let
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
be a set of topological generators for
G
, and choose an enumeration
s_{1},\dots,s_{t}
of
S
. Assume that
t\geq k
, and let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow G
be the voltage assignment that sends
s_{i}\mapsto g_{i}
for
i\leq k
, and to any arbitrary elements of
G
for
i>k
. Let
\alpha_{/n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow G^{(n)}
be the map obtained upon composing
\alpha
with the quotient map
G\rightarrow G^{(n)}
. Let
Y_{n}
be the graph given by
X(G^{(n)},S,\alpha_{/n})
, and note that there are maps
Y_{n}\rightarrow Y_{n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
. The map
Y_{n}\rightarrow X
obtained upon composition is Galois provided
Y_{n}
is connected. In our next discussion, let us assume that this is indeed the case. Suppose that we are given a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower

(5.2)

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\dots

over
X
. We set
\Gamma^{(n)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(X_{n}/X)
and
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\Gamma^{(n)}
. Note that there are isomorphisms
\Gamma^{(n)}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
and
\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. Set
\mathfrak{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G\times\Gamma
and
\mathfrak{G}^{(n,m)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G^{(n)}\times\Gamma^{(m)}
. Furthermore, there is a voltage assignment
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow\Gamma
such that
X_{n}=X(\Gamma^{(n)},G,\beta_{/n})
. Let
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}
be the voltage assignment given by
\gamma(s)=(\alpha(s),\beta(s))
. Set
\gamma_{n,m}
to denote the composite of
\gamma
with the quotient map to
\mathfrak{G}^{(n,m)}
, and set also
Y_{n,m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X(\mathfrak{G}^{(n,m)},S,\gamma_{n,m})
. Denote by
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
the tower over
Y_{n}
given by

Y_{n}=Y_{n,0}\leftarrow Y_{n,1}\leftarrow Y_{n,2}\leftarrow Y_{n,3}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow Y_{n,m}\leftarrow\dots,

and let
\mu_{n}
(resp.
\lambda_{n}
) denote the
\mu
-invariant (resp.
\lambda
-invariant) for the tower
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
. Set
\mu\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mu_{0}
and
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda_{0}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

With respect to the notation above, assume that the following conditions hold

(1)

the group
G
is uniform of dimension
d
,

(2)

the graph
Y_{n,m}
is connected for all
m
and
n
,

(3)

the
\mu
-invariant
\mu=\mu_{0}
is equal to
0
.

Then, for all values of
n
,
\mu_{n}=0
, and furthermore, as a function of
n
,

(5.3)

\lambda_{n}=\ell^{dn}(\lambda+1)-1.

Proof.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-tower
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
is the pullback of (
5.2
) under the map
Y_{n}\rightarrow X
. It thus follows from Theorem
A
that
\lambda_{n}+1=[Y_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X](\lambda+1)=|G^{(n)}|(\lambda+1)=\ell^{nd}(\lambda+1)
, and the result follows.
∎

5.2.
An Example

We illustrate the above result through an example for which the assumptions are shown to be satisfied. Assume that
\ell
is an odd prime. Let
G
denote the kernel of the mod-
\ell
reduction map

G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right).

We find that for all
n
,

G_{n}=\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z})\right)

and
G^{(n)}=G/G_{n}=\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right)
.

Proposition
5.3
below shows that the group
G
is uniform pro-
\ell
with dimension
d=3
(in the sense of Definition
5.1
). Furthermore, we shall obtain an explicit set of generators for
G
. First, we set up some preliminary notation. Let
\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
denote the
3
-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
-vector space of
2\times 2
square matrices with trace zero. Given a matrix
A\in\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
, let
\widetilde{A}
be a lift of
A
to a matrix with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. The element
\ell^{n}A\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z})
is taken to be
\ell^{n}\widetilde{A}
, and is clearly independent of the choice of lift of
A
. Identify
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
with the kernel of the mod-
\ell^{n}
reduction map
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
. The map

\operatorname{exp}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow G_{n}/G_{n+1}

sending
A\mapsto\operatorname{exp}_{n}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{I}+\ell^{n}A
is an isomorphism.

Proposition 5.3
.

With respect to notation above, the group
G
is a uniform pro-
\ell
group of dimension
d=3
and is topologically generated by the matrices

A_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ell\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right),A_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\ell&0\\
0&(1+\ell)^{-1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\text{ and }A_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\ell&1\\
\end{array}}\right).

Proof.

Let us first verify the condition (
1
) of Definition
5.1
, and show that
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
is a set of generators. Let
H
be the closure of the subgroup of
G
generated by
A_{1},A_{2}
and
A_{3}
. Denote by
\pi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow G^{(n)}
the natural quotient map. It suffices to show that
\pi_{n}(H)=G^{(n)}
for all
n
. We prove this by induction on
n
.

Note that
G^{(1)}=G/G_{1}
is generated by

\operatorname{exp}_{1}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}}\right)\right),\operatorname{exp}_{1}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)\text{ and }\operatorname{exp}_{1}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
1&0\\
\end{array}}\right)\right).

The above matrices are
\pi_{1}(A_{1}),\pi_{1}(A_{2})
and
\pi_{1}(A_{3})
respectively. Therefore,
\pi_{1}(H)=G^{(1)}
.

For the inductive step, assume that
\pi_{n}(A_{1}),\pi_{n}(A_{2})
and
\pi_{n}(A_{3})
generate
G^{(n)}
. We show that
\pi_{n+1}(A_{1}),\pi_{n+1}(A_{2})
and
\pi_{n+1}(A_{3})
generate
G^{(n+1)}
. Identifying
G_{n-1}/G_{n}
with
\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
, we find that
G_{n-1}/G_{n}
is generated by the matrices

\begin{split}&B_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}_{n}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ell^{n}\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
&C_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}_{n}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\ell^{n}&0\\
0&1-\ell^{n}\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
&D_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}_{n}\left(\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ell^{n}\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\\
\end{split}

which are considered modulo
G_{n}
. Note that since
\pi_{n}(H)=G^{(n)}
, the matrices
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
are contained in
\pi_{n}(H)
. Therefore, we may choose lifts
\tilde{B}_{n},\tilde{C}_{n},\tilde{D}_{n}
of
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
respectively to
\pi_{n+1}(H)
. The following relations hold in
G^{(n+1)}

\begin{split}&A_{2}\tilde{B}_{n}A_{2}^{-1}\tilde{B}_{n}^{-1}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&2\ell^{n+1}\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\\
&A_{2}\tilde{D}_{n}A_{2}^{-1}\tilde{D}_{n}^{-1}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-2\ell^{n+1}&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\\
&A_{1}\tilde{D}_{n}A_{1}^{-1}\tilde{D}_{n}^{-1}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\ell^{n+1}&0\\
0&1-\ell^{n+1}\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
\end{split}

see the proof of
[
Ray21
, Lemma 2.8]
. The above matrices are therefore contained in
\pi_{n+1}(H)
. Since
\ell
is assumed to be odd, we find that they generate
G_{n}/G_{n+1}=\operatorname{exp}_{n}\left(\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right)
. It follows that
\pi_{n+1}(H)
contains
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
. Since
\pi_{n}(H)=G/G_{n}
by assumption, and
\pi_{n+1}(H)
contains
G_{n}/G_{n+1}
it follows that
\pi_{n+1}(H)=G^{(n+1)}
. This completes the inductive step. We conclude that
H=G
, and therefore,
G
is topologically generated by
A_{1},A_{2}
and
A_{3}
. In particular, (
1
) of Definition
5.1
is satisfied.

The same arguments as above show that the matrices

\begin{split}&A_{1}^{\ell}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ell^{2}\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
&A_{2}^{\ell}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}(1+\ell^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ell^{2}\dots)&0\\
0&(1-\ell^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\ell^{2}\dots)\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
&A_{3}^{\ell}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\ell^{2}&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\end{split}

generate
G_{2}
. Thus the property (
2
) of Definition
5.1
is satisfied. Finally, since the exponential map

\operatorname{exp}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow G_{n}/G_{n+1}

is seen to be an isomorphism for all
n
, it follows that the property (
3
) is satisfied as well.
∎

Let
X
be the bouquet with
4
undirected edges. Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{4}
denote the edges in
S
, and
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow G
denote the map taking
s_{i}\mapsto A_{i}
for
i\leq 3
and
s_{4}\mapsto 1
. Let
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow\Gamma
denote the map taking
s_{i}\mapsto 0
for
i\leq 3
and
s_{4}\mapsto 1
. Then, the map
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow\mathfrak{G}=G\times\Gamma
maps
s_{1},\dots,s_{4}
to a set of generators, moreover, all graphs
Y_{n,m}
are connected since
\gamma_{n,m}(S)
generates
G^{(n)}\times\Gamma^{(m)}
(cf. Theorem
2.11
). Consider the tower

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\dots,

where
X_{n}
is taken to be
X(\Gamma^{(n)},S,\beta_{/n})
. We see that the characteristic series is

f_{X,\beta}(T)=-T^{2}+T^{3}-T^{4}+\dots,

and hence,
\mu=0
and
\lambda=1
. Recall that
\mu_{n}
and
\lambda_{n}
are the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of the tower
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
. According to the formula (
5.3
) of Theorem
5.2
, we find that
\mu_{n}=0
for all
n
, and that
\lambda_{n}=2\ell^{3n}-1
for all
n
.

Firstly, we review some vocabulary, notations, and common characteristics of the theory of Modulars and modular spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary vector space. A functional
\rho:Y\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called a modular if for arbitrary
u,v\in Y
;

(1)

\rho(u)=0
if and only if
u=0
.

(2)

\rho(\alpha u)=\rho(u)
for every scalar
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
.

(3)

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\rho(u)+\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

If (
1
) is replaced by:

(4)

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\alpha\rho(u)+\beta\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, then we say that
\rho
is a convex modular.

(5)

It is said that the modular
\rho
has the Fatou property if and only if
\rho(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho(x_{n})
whenever
x=\rho-\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}
.

A modular
\rho
defines a corresponding modular space, i.e., the vector space
Y_{\rho}
given by:

Y_{\rho}=\{u\in Y:\rho(\lambda u)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0\}.

A function modular is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition if there exist
\tau>0
such that
\rho(2u)\leq\tau\rho(u)
for all
u\in Y_{\rho}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
and
u
be in
Y_{\rho}
. Then:

(1)

The sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
, with
u_{n}\in Y_{\rho}
, is
\rho
-convergent to
u
and write:
u_{n}\to u
if
\rho\left(u_{n}-u\right)\to 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(2)

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}
, with
u_{n}\in Y_{\rho}
, is called
\rho
-Cauchy if
\rho\left(u_{n}-u_{m}\right)\to 0
as
n,m\to\infty
.

(3)

Y_{\rho}
is called
\rho
-complete if every
\rho
-Cauchy sequence in
Y_{\rho}
is
\rho
-convergent.

Proposition 1.3
.

In modular space,

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u
and a is a constant vector, then
u_{n}+a\overset{\rho}{\to}u+a
.

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u
and
v_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}v
then
\alpha u_{n}+\beta v_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}\alpha u+\beta v
, where
\alpha+\beta\leq 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that
\rho(u)
is an increasing function, for all
u\in X
.
Suppose
0<a<b
, then property
(4)
of Definition
1.1
with
v=0
shows that
\rho(au)=\rho\left(\dfrac{a}{b}bu\right)\leq\rho(bu)
for all
u\in Y
.
Morever, if
\rho
is a convexe modular on
Y
and
|\alpha|\leq 1
, then
\rho(\alpha u)\leq\alpha\rho(u)
.

In general, if
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,\dots,n
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n}\leq 1
then
\rho(\lambda_{1}u_{1}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}u_{n})\leq\lambda_{1}\rho(u_{1})+\lambda_{2}\rho(u_{2})+\dots+\lambda_{n}\rho(u_{n})
.

If
\{u_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
u
, then
\{cu_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
cu
, where
|c|\leq 1
.
But the
\rho
-convergent of a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
to

does not imply that
\{\alpha u_{n}\}
is
\rho
-convergent to
\alpha u_{n}
for scalars
\alpha
with
|\alpha|>1

If
\rho

\rho(u)\leq\tau\rho(\dfrac{1}{2}u)\leq\dfrac{\tau}{2}\rho(u)

Hence

Lemma 2.1

(

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a vector space, and
Y_{\rho}
be a
\rho
-complete convex modular space satisfying the Fatou property.
Let
\alpha:X^{2}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
be a function such that

\varphi(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}y\right)<\infty

(2.1)

and

\psi(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}y\right)<\infty

(2.2)

for all
x,y\in X
.
Let
\phi:X^{2}\rightarrow Y_{\rho}
be a mapping that satisfies

\phi(x,0)=\phi(0,z)=0,

and

\rho(\phi(x+y,z+w)+\phi(x-y,z-w)-2\phi(x,z)-2\phi(x,w))\leq\alpha(x,y)\alpha(z,w)

(2.3)

for all

.
Then there exists a unique additive quadratic mapping

\rho(\phi(x,z)-H(x,z))\leq\min\{\varphi(x,x)\alpha(z,0),\alpha(x,0)\psi(z,z)\}\text{ for all }x,z\in X.

(2.4)

Proof.

Letting
y=x
and

in (
2.3
), we get

\rho\left(\phi(2x,z)-2\phi(x,z)\right)\leq\alpha(x,x)\alpha(z,0)

Then by convexity of
\rho
, we have

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi(2x,z)-\phi(x,z)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x,x)\alpha(z,0)

(2.5)

and by induction, we have

\rho\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}}\phi\left(2^{k}x,z\right)-\phi(x,z)\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}x\right)\alpha(z,0)

(2.6)

for all
x,z\in X
and all positive integer
k
.
For
k=1
, we obtain (
2.5
).
Suppose that (
2.6
) holds for a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We have

\displaystyle\rho\left(\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\phi\left(2^{k+1}x,z\right)-\phi(x,z)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2^{k}}\phi\left(2^{k}\cdot 2x,z\right)-\phi(2x,z)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\phi(2x,z)-\phi(x,z)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{j}x,2^{j}x\right)\alpha(z,0)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha(x,x)\alpha(z,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}x\right)\alpha(z,0)

Hence, (
2.6
) holds for every

.

Let
m,n
be positive integers such that

\displaystyle\rho\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\phi\left(2^{n}x,z\right)-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\phi\left(2^{m}x,z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{1}{2^{m}}\left(\frac{\phi\left(2^{n-m}\cdot 2^{m}x,z\right)}{2^{n-m}}-\phi\left(2^{m}x,z\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\alpha\left(2^{m+j-1}x,2^{m+j-1}x\right)\alpha(z,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\alpha\left(2^{k-1}x,2^{k-1}x\right)\alpha(z,0)

Now, by application the Fatou property, we have

\displaystyle\rho(A(x,z)-\phi(x,z))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\phi(2^{n}x,z)-\phi(x,z)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\alpha\left(2^{k-1}x,2^{k-1}x\right)\alpha(z,0)

(2.9)

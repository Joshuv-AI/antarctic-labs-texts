1.
Motivations and the Main Results

Throughout the paper
K
stands for a field of characteristic zero and
{\mathcal{A}}
for a
K
-algebra (not necessarily unital or commutative). We denote by
1_{\mathcal{A}}
or simply
1
the identity element of
{\mathcal{A}}
, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is unital, and

{\operatorname{I}}_{\mathcal{A}}
or simply
{\operatorname{I}}
the identity map of
{\mathcal{A}}
, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is clear in the context.

A
K
-linear endomorphism
\eta
of
{\mathcal{A}}

(LN)

there exists

such that
\eta^{m}(a)=0
,
and

(LF) if for each

the
K
-subspace spanned
by
\eta^{i}(a)

(i\geq 0)
is finite dimensional over
K
.

By a
K
-derivation

of
{\mathcal{A}}
we mean a
K
-linear map

that satisfies
D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b)

.
By a

-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation

{\mathcal{A}}

K
-linear map
\delta:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
such that for all

a,b\in{\mathcal{A}}
the following equation holds:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+a\delta(b)-\delta(a)\delta(b).

It is easy to verify that
\delta
is an
R
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
if and only if
\delta={\operatorname{I}}-\phi
for some
R
-algebra endomorphism

\phi
of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore an

-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation is
a special so-called
(s_{1},s_{2})
-derivation introduced
by N. Jacobson
[

]
and also a special
semi-derivation introduced
by J. Bergen in
[
B
]
.
R
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivations have also been
studied by many others under some different names such as

-derivations in
[

,
E2
]
and

\phi
-derivations in
[

,

]
, etc..

We denote by
{{\mathcal{E}}nd}_{K}({\mathcal{A}})
the set of all

K
-algebra endomorphisms of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{K}({\mathcal{A}})
the set of all

K
-derivations of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
{{\mathcal{E}}der}_{K}({\mathcal{A}})
the set of all

K
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivations of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Furthermore, for each
K
-linear endomorphism

of
{\mathcal{A}}
we denote by

the

of

, i.e.,

, and

the

of

.
When

is an
R
-derivation or
R
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation, we also denote

It is conjectured in

]

{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation of

]

[

]

[

For some other studies on the algebraic structure of

-derivations or

{\mathcal{E}}

-derivations and

Conjecture 1.1

Let

-derivation or a LF

-derivation of

of

Our first main result is the following theorem, which gives a partial

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero and
{\mathcal{A}}
a
K
-algebra
(not necessarily unital). Then the following statements hold:

1)

for every locally finite
D\in{{\mathcal{D}}er}_{K}({\mathcal{A}})
and an idempotent

(e)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}D

2)

for every locally finite
\delta\in{{\mathcal{E}}der}_{K}({\mathcal{A}})
and an idempotent

e{\mathcal{A}},\,{\mathcal{A}}e\subseteq\operatorname{Im}\delta
. Furthermore,

\delta

.

Note that for every

lie in

Assume that

For a different proof of the corollary above for commutative

[

Our second main result of this paper is the following

2.
Van den Essen’s One-to-One Correspondence between Locally Nilpotent Derivations
and Locally Nilpotent
{\mathcal{E}}
-Derivations

Throughout this section,
K
stands for a field of characteristic zero,

{\mathcal{A}}
for a
K
-algebra (not necessarily unital or commutative)
and
{\operatorname{I}}
for the identity map of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{D}}
the set of all LN (locally nilpotent)
K
-derivations
of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
the set of all LN
K
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivations of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
We define the following map:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Xi:{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\to{\operatorname{I}}-e^{D},

where
e^{D}\!:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{D^{k}}{k!}
.

With the setting as above we have the following remarkable one-to-one correspondence
between
{\mathcal{D}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
, which was first proved by A. van den Essen in
[
E1
]
.
See also
[
E2
, Proposition 2.1.3]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The map
\Xi:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{E}}
is an one-to-one correspondence between the sets
{\mathcal{D}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}

with the inverse map
\Xi^{-1}

given by the following map:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda:{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\to\ln({\operatorname{I}}-\delta),

where
\ln({\operatorname{I}}-\delta)\!:=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta^{k}}{k}
.

For the sake of completeness, we here give a proof for the theorem above,
which is some shorter than the one given in
[
E2
, Proposition 2.1.3]
.

First, the following lemma can be easily verified by induction,
as noticed in
[
E1
,
E2
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a ring and
\delta
an
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation of
{\mathcal{B}}
. Then for all

a,b\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
n\geq 1
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\delta^{n}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\delta^{i}(a)\,\delta^{n-i}({\operatorname{I}}-\delta)^{i}(b).

Now we can show Theorem
2.1
as follows.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
:

First, since
D
is LN,
e^{D}
is well-defined.
It is well-known (and also easy to check directly)
that
e^{D}
is a
K
-algebra automorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Hence
\Xi(D)
is a
K
-
{\mathcal{E}}
-derivation of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Consider

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Xi(D)={\operatorname{I}}-e^{D}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{D^{n}}{n!}=Dh(D)=h(D)D,

where

(2.6)

\displaystyle h(D)=-{\operatorname{I}}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{D^{n-1}}{n!}=-{\operatorname{I}}-D\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{D^{n-2}}{n!}.

Since
D
is LN, and
D
and
h(D)
commute, by Eq. (
2.5
)

\Xi(D)
is also LN. Therefore,
\Xi
is indeed a map from
{\mathcal{D}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Next, we show that
\Lambda(\delta)\in{\mathcal{D}}
for all

\delta\in{\mathcal{E}}
.
Set
D_{\delta}\!:=\Lambda(\delta)
. Then

(2.7)

\displaystyle D_{\delta}\!=\ln({\operatorname{I}}-\delta)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta^{n}}{n}=\delta g(\delta)=g(\delta)\delta,

where

(2.8)

\displaystyle g(\delta)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta^{n-1}}{n}=-{\operatorname{I}}-\delta\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\delta^{n-2}}{n}.

Since
\delta
is LN, and
\delta
and
g(\delta)
commute,
by Eq. (
2.7
)
D_{\delta}
is also LN.

Now, let
x,y\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then by Lemma
2.2
we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle D_{\delta}(xy)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\delta^{n}(xy)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\delta^{i}(x)\delta^{n-i}(1-\delta)^{i}(y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\delta^{i}(x)S_{i}(y),

where for each
i\geq 0
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle S_{i}(y)=\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{i}\delta^{n-i}(1-\delta)^{i}(y)=(1-\delta)^{i}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{i}\delta^{n-i}(y).

In particular, by Eq. (
2.7
) and the equation above we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle S_{0}(y)=-D_{\delta}(y).

Claim:
S_{i}(y)=\frac{1}{i}y
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof of Claim
: For each
i\geq 1

we introduce the formal power series

(2.12)

\displaystyle f_{i}(t)\!:

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{i}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{i}t^{n-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-t)^{i}}{i!}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-i+1)t^{n-i}.

Then
S_{i}(y)=f_{i}(\delta)(y)
. On the other hand,
we have the following identity of formal power series:

\frac{(i-1)!}{(1-t)^{i}}=-\frac{d^{i}}{dt^{i}}\ln(1-t)=\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-i+1)t^{n-i}

By Eq. (
2.10
) and the identity above
we have
f_{i}(t)=1/i
. Hence

S_{i}(y)=f_{i}(\delta)(y)=\frac{1}{i}y
and
the claim follows.

Now by Eqs. (
2.7
), (
2.9
), (
2.11
) and the claim
above we have

\displaystyle D_{\delta}(xy)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\delta^{i}(x)S_{i}(y)=-xS_{0}(y)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta^{i}(x)S_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=xD_{\delta}(y)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i}\delta^{i}(x)y=xD_{\delta}(y)+D_{\delta}(x)y.

Therefore
\Lambda(\delta)=D_{\delta}
is a LN

K
-derivation of
{\mathcal{A}}
, i.e.,
\Lambda
is indeed
a map from
{\mathcal{E}}
to
{\mathcal{D}}
. Since
\Xi
and

\Lambda
are obviously inverse to each other,
we see that
\Xi
gives an one-to-one
correspondence between
{\mathcal{D}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}
,
i.e., the theorem follows.

\Box

Next, we derive some consequences of Theorem
2.1
.
But, we first need to show the following lemma. Although it is
almost trivial, it will be frequently used throughout

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
R
be a ring and
{\mathcal{B}}
an
R
-algebra.
Let
F
and
G
be two commuting
R
-linear endomorphisms
of
{\mathcal{B}}
such that
F
is invertible and

is LN (locally nilpotent).
Then
F-G
is an
R
-linear automorphism
with the inverse map given by

(F-G)^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}G^{k}F^{-k-1}.

Proof:  Note that
F-G=({\operatorname{I}}-GF^{-1})F
.
Since
F
commutes with
G
, so does
F^{-1}
.
Hence
U\!:=GF^{-1}
is LN,
for
G
is LN. Therefore the formal power series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}U^{k}
is a well-defined
R
-linear
endomorphism of
{\mathcal{A}}
, which gives
the inverse map of
{\operatorname{I}}-U
. Hence,
F^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}U^{k}
gives
the inverse map of
F-G
, from which the
lemma follows.

\Box

, and

.
Second, by Lemma

with

and

,

-linear map

in Eq. (

)
is a

-linear automorphism of

.
Therefore, we have

by Eq. (

).
Furthermore, we also have

),
and

2.7
),

\Box

The Derivation Case of Theorem

In this section we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
for
LF (locally finite)
K
-derivations.
Throughout this section we let
K
and
{\mathcal{A}}
be as in
Theorem
1.2
,
D
a LF
K
-derivation of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
and
e
an idempotent in
{\mathcal{A}}^{D}\cap\operatorname{Im}D
.

Let
s\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
Ds=e
. Since
De=0
,
we have
D(ese)=e(Ds)e=e
. So replacing
s
by

ese
we assume
s\in e{\mathcal{A}}e
.
Furthermore, for convenience we set
\mathbf{s^{0}=e}
.
Then with the setting above it is easy
to see that for all
i,k\geq 0
, we have

es^{i}=s^{i}e=s^{i}
and

(3.13)

\displaystyle D^{i}(s^{k})=\begin{cases}k(k-1)\cdots(k-i+1)s^{k-i}&\text{ if }i\leq k\\
0&\text{ if }i>k.\end{cases}

We first consider the case that
D
is LN (locally nilpotent).

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
D
is LN. For all
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
set

(3.14)

\displaystyle\phi_{-s}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}D^{i}(a)s^{i}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\psi_{-s}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}s^{i}D^{i}(a).

Then

\phi_{-s}(a),\psi_{-s}(a)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{D}
and

(3.16)

\displaystyle ae=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}\phi_{-s}\big(D^{j}(a)\big)\,s^{j}

(3.17)

\displaystyle ea=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}s^{j}\psi_{-s}\big(D^{j}(a)\big).

Note that the case when
{\mathcal{A}}
is commutative and
e=1
the lemma has been proven
in
[
GN
,
W
]
. See also
[
E2
]
. The main idea of the proof given below is to modify the proof in
[
GN
,
W
,
E2
]
to the more general case in the lemma.

Proof:  First, by Eqs. (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we have

\displaystyle D\phi_{-s}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}D\big(D^{i}(a)s^{i}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}\big(D^{i+1}(a)s^{i}+iD^{i}(a)s^{i-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}+(-1)^{j+1}}{j!}D^{j+1}(a)s^{j}=0.

Hence
\phi_{-s}(a)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{D}
.
The proof of
\psi_{-s}(a)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{D}

is similar.

Next we show Eq. (
3.16
).
The proof of Eq. (

Minkowski dimension of the boundaries of the lakes of Wada
Zhangchi Chen

Abstract




Abstract

The lakes of Wada are three disjoint simply connected domains in
S^{2}
with the counterintuitive property that they all have the same boundary. The common boundary is a indecomposable continuum. In this article we calculated the Minkowski dimension of such boundaries. The lakes constructed in the standard Cantor way has
\ln(6)/\ln(3)\approx 1.6309
-dimensional boundary, while in general, for any number in
[1,2]
we can construct lakes with such dimensional boundaries.

Keywords:

Minkowski dimension,
Lakes of Wada.

U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}

The common boundary
W_{\sf cantor}
is a nowhere dense closed continuous fractal set of infinite length and of 0 area. The Minkowski-Bouligand (also called ‘box-counting’) dimension and Hausdorff-Besicovitch dimension describe how ‘rough’ this fractal set is. We abbreviate them as Minkowski dimension and Hausdorff dimension.

Definition 1.1

(Minkowski dimension)
.

For any bounded set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
\epsilon>0
, let
N(\epsilon)
be the number of boxes (squares) required to cover
S
. We define the Minkowski dimension of
S

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S):=\lim\limits_{r\to 0+}\inf\big\{\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}^{d}:\text{there is a countable cover of $S$ by balls with radius $r_{i}\in(0,r)$}\big\},

and we define the
upper Minkowski dimension of
S
as

\overline{\dim_{\sf box}}(S):=\limsup\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(1/\epsilon)}

and the
lower Minkowski dimension of
S
as

\underline{\dim_{\sf box}}(S):=\liminf\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(1/\epsilon)}.

When the two numbers agree, we define the
Minkowski dimension of
S
as

\dim_{\sf box}(S):=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(1/\epsilon)}.

Definition 1.2

(Hausdorff dimension)
.

For any bounded set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
d>0
we define the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff outer measure of
S

\mathcal{H}^{d}(S):=\lim\limits_{r\to 0+}\inf\big\{\sum\limits_{i}r_{i}^{d}:\text{there is a countable cover of $S$ by balls with radius $r_{i}\in(0,r)$}\big\},

and we define the
Hausdorff dimension of
S

\dim_{H}(S):=\inf\{d\geqslant 0~|~\mathcal{H}^{d}(S)=0\}.

The Minkowski dimension is relative easy to calculate, while the Hausdorff dimension deals with countable unions better. There are several basic properties of these dimensions. Assuming
S,S^{\prime}
have Minkowski dimensions.

1.

If
S
is a dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}
manifold, then
\dim_{H}(S)=\dim_{\sf box}(S)=d
;

2.

If
S^{\prime}\subset S
then

\dim_{H}(S^{\prime})\leqslant\dim_{H}(S)
and
\dim_{\sf box}(S^{\prime})\leqslant\dim_{\sf box}(S)
;

3.

If
S=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}
then

\dim_{\sf box}(S)=\max\dim_{\sf box}(S_{i})
;

4.

If
S=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}S_{i}
then

\dim_{H}(S)=\sup\dim_{H}(S_{i})
;

5.

In general
\dim_{H}(S)\leqslant\dim_{\sf box}(S)

[
2
]
. The equality holds when
S
is strictly self-similar.

From the properties we have
1\leqslant\dim_{H}(W_{\sf cantor})\leqslant\dim_{\sf box}(W_{\sf cantor})\leqslant 2
. In Section
2
we prove

.

has

\dim_{\sf box}(W_{\sf cantor})=\frac{\ln(6)}{\ln(3)}\approx 1.6309.

2
Minkowski dimension of the boundary in the standard Cantor case

From the construction

W_{\sf cantor}=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\partial B_{3k+1}=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\partial R_{3k+2}=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\partial G_{3k+3}.

To simplify notations, we write
W_{3k+1},

W_{3k+2}
and
W_{3k+3}
for
\partial B_{3k+1}
,
\partial R_{3k+2}
and
\partial G_{3k+3}
respectively. Thus

W_{\sf cantor}=\bigcup\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}W_{n}.

First we calculate its dimension by box-counting.

Day 1: we cover the blue boundary
\partial B_{1}
by exactly 1 square of length 1. So
N(1)=1
. This square also covers
W_{\sf cantor}
.

Day 2: we cover the red boundary
\partial R_{2}
by 7 squares of length
3^{-1}
. So
N(3^{-1})=7
. These squares also covers
W_{\sf cantor}
.

Day 3: we cover the green boundary
\partial G_{3}
by 43 squares of length
3^{-2}
. So
N(3^{-2})=43
. These squares also covers
W_{\sf cantor}
.

Figure 7:
Day 2: 7 squares of length
3^{-1}

On day
n
, all the
N(3^{1-n})
squares covering
W_{n}
hence covering
W_{\sf cantor}
, up to a rotation, are classified into 4 cases:

•

Case 1 (Terminal): The new canal ends in this square, like the square 1 on Day 2.

•

Case 2 (Straight): The new canal passes this square straightly, like squares 2, 4, 6, 7 on Day 2.

•

Case 3 (Turning): The new canal passes this square with a left or right turn, like squares 3, 5 on Day 2.

•

Case 4 (Separation): The new canal enters this square from the left and separates into two branches up and down. It appears exactly once everyday from Day 4.

Figure 8:
Terminal, Straight and Turning

Figure 9:
Separation comes from extending a Terminal, and will split into two Terminals

In the first three days, there is exactly one Terminal and no Separation. From Day 4, there will be exactly two Terminals and one Separation.

On day
n+1
, all the
N(3^{-n})
squares required to cover
W_{n+1}
are sub-squares of the Terminal, the Straights and the Turnings used one day before. In fact

•

each Terminal is covered by and requires 7 sub-squares;

•

each Straight and each Turning is covered by and requires 6 sub-squares.

•

each Separation is covered by and requires 5 sub-squares.

thus

\left\{\begin{aligned} N(1)&=1,\\
N(3^{-n})&=6N(3^{1-n})+1,\ \ \ n=1,2,3,\dots\end{aligned}\right.

We concludes that
N(3^{-n})=O(6^{n})
so

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\ln\big(N(3^{-n})\big)}{\ln(3^{n})}=\frac{\ln(6)}{\ln(3)}.

For any
\epsilon\in[3^{-n-1},3^{-n}]
clearly we have

N(3^{-n})\leqslant N(\epsilon)\leqslant N(3^{-n-1}).

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\big(N(3^{-n})\big)}{\ln(3^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(1/\epsilon)}\leqslant\frac{\ln\big(N(3^{-n-1})\big)}{\ln(3^{n})}

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\big(N(3^{-n})\big)/\ln(3^{n})}{1+\ln(3)/\ln(3^{n})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(1/\epsilon)}\leqslant\frac{\ln\big(N(3^{-n-1})\big)/\ln(3^{n+1})}{1-\ln(3)/\ln(3^{n+1})}

We concludes that the Minkowski dimension exists and

\dim_{\sf box}(W_{\sf cantor})=\frac{\ln(6)}{\ln(3)}\approx 1.6309.

Minkowski dimension of the boundary in the modified Cantor case

First we treat the case
\mathcal{A}:=\{a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 3}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of integer series.

W_{4}

4,4,4,\dots

Recall the notations

w_{n}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}^{-1}(1-\tfrac{2}{a_{n}}),\ \ \ t_{n}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1},\ \ \ (n\geqslant 2)

for the width of canals and
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\times
(the maximal distances between points on the islands and the extended canals on Day
n
).

On Day
n
we use
N(t_{n})
squares of length
t_{n}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{-1}
. They are also classified into one Terminal (two Terminals and one Separation from Day 4) and finitely many Straights and Turnings.

On Day
n+1
those squares splits into sub-squares of length
t_{n+1}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}a_{i}^{-1}
, while

•

each Terminal is covered by and requires
3a_{n+1}-2
sub-squares;

•

each Straight and each Turning is covered by and requires
2a_{n+1}
sub-squares;

•

each Separation is covered by and requires
a_{n+1}+2
sub-squares.

Thus we get recurrence formulas

\left\{\begin{aligned} N(t_{0})&=N(1)=1,\\
N(t_{n+1})&=2a_{n+1}N({t_{n}})+a_{n+1}-2,\ \ \ n=1,2,3,\dots\end{aligned}\right.

We conclude that
N(t_{n+1})=O\big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}2a_{i}\big)
.

If the sequence
\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\ln(a_{n+1})}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\ln(a_{i})}=0

(1)

for example, if
\mathcal{A}
is bounded or
a_{n}=O(n^{k})
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then by the same argument as before, we conclude that

\dim_{\sf box}(W_{\mathcal{A}})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\ln\big(N(t_{n})\big)}{\ln(t_{n})}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\ln\big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}2a_{i}\big)}{\ln\big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\big)}=1+\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n\ln(2)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}.

But if
\mathcal{A}
does not satisfy the condition (
1
), for example if
a_{n}=2^{2^{n}}
, then we need to analyse more delicately.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
there is a unique
n
such that
t_{n+1}\leqslant\epsilon<t_{n}
, i.e.

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}<1/\epsilon\leqslant a_{n+1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

Recall that
a_{n+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 3}
. Thus there is a unique integer
p\in\{1,2,\dots,a_{n+1}-1\}
such that

p\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}<1/\epsilon\leqslant(p+1)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

On Day
n+1
, for any
q\in\{1,2,\dots,a_{n+1}\}
, if we use sub-squares of length
q^{-1}t_{n}
instead of
a_{n+1}^{-1}t_{n}
, then

•

each Terminal is covered by and requires
3q-2
sub-squares;

•

each Straight and each Turning is covered by and requires
2q
sub-squares;

•

each Separation is covered by and requires
q+2
sub-squares.

Thus

N(q^{-1}\,t_{n+1})=2qN({t_{n}})+q-2,\ \ \ q=1,2,\dots,a_{n+1}.

In particular

\displaystyle N(p^{-1}t_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant N(\epsilon)\leqslant N\big((p+1)^{-1}t_{n}\big)

\displaystyle 2pN(t_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant N(\epsilon)\leqslant 2(p+1)N\big(t_{n}\big)+p-2<(2p+3)N\big(t_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\frac{\ln(2p)+\ln\big(N(t_{n})\big)}{\ln(p+1)+\ln\big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\big)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\ln\big(N(\epsilon)\big)}{\ln(\epsilon)}\leqslant\frac{\ln(2p+3)+\ln\big(N(t_{n})\big)}{\ln(p)+\ln\big(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\big)}

Both sides converges to
1+\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n\ln(2)}{\ln(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})}
The Minkowski dimension is then

\dim_{\sf box}(W_{\mathcal{A}})=1+\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{n\ln(2)}{\ln(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})}

In particular, when
\mathcal{A}
is a constant sequence
c,c,c,\dots
we get

\dim_{\sf box}(W_{c})=1+\frac{\ln(2)}{\ln(c)}

which tends to 1 when
c\rightarrow+\infty
.

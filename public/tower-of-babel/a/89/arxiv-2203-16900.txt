Transducing paths in graph classes with unbounded shrubdepth Journal: arXiv
Patrice Ossona de Mendez, Michał Pilipczuk, Sebastian Siebertz

Abstract
Transductions are a general formalism for expressing transformations of graphs (and more generally, of relational structures) in logic. We prove that a graph class 𝒞 \mathscr{C} can be 𝖥𝖮 {\mathsf{FO}} -transduced from a class of bounded-height trees (that is, has bounded shrubdepth ) if, and only if, from 𝒞 \mathscr{C} one cannot 𝖥𝖮 {\mathsf{FO}} -transduce the class of all paths. This establishes one of the three remaining open questions posed by Blumensath and Courcelle about the 𝖬𝖲𝖮 {\mathsf{MSO}} -transduction quasi-order, even in the stronger form that concerns 𝖥𝖮 {\mathsf{FO}} -transductions instead of 𝖬𝖲𝖮 {\mathsf{MSO}} -transductions. The backbone of our proof is a graph-theoretic statement that says the following: If a graph G G excludes a path, the bipartite complement of a path, and a half-graph as semi-induced subgraphs, then the vertex set of G G can be partitioned into a bounded number of parts so that every part induces a cograph of bounded height, and every pair of parts semi-induce a bi-cograph of bounded height. This statement may be of independent interest; for instance, it implies that the graphs in question form a class that is linearly χ \chi -bounded.



1
Introduction

1.1
Background and the main result

Transductions

Transductions provide a model theoretical framework for transformations of relational structures definable in logic. In this work we consider only graphs; these will be sometimes
colored
, by which we mean that there can be some unary predicates distinguishing subsets of vertices (not necessarily disjoint).

To define transductions in this setting, we first introduce interpretations. For a logic
\mathcal{L}
, an
\mathcal{L}
-interpretation

\mathsf{I}
consists of two
\mathcal{L}
-formulas
\varphi(x,y)
and
\psi(x)
. Applying
\mathsf{I}
to a colored graph
G
yields a new graph
\mathsf{I}(G)
, whose vertex set consists of all vertices of
G
satisfying
\psi
and whose edge set consists of all pairs of vertices of
G
satisfying
\varphi
. An
\mathcal{L}
-transduction

\mathsf{T}
is a mechanism of transforming graphs that consists of an
\mathcal{L}
-interpretation
\mathsf{I}
. The semantic is as follows: for a (colored) graph
G
,
\mathsf{T}(G)
consists of all graphs that can be obtained from
G
by first
coloring

G
— extending
G
by the unary predicates used by
\mathsf{I}
and not present in
G
— in an arbitrary way and then applying
\mathsf{I}
. Note that due to the coloring step,
\mathsf{T}(G)
is a set of graphs rather than a single graph: it contains one output per each possible coloring of the input. (See
section

2
for a formal definition. In particular, one usually allows also the operation of copying vertices, which is immaterial here.)

For a graph class
\mathscr{C}
, we define
\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})=\bigcup_{G\in\mathscr{C}}\mathsf{T}(G)
.
We say that a class
\mathscr{C}
is
\mathcal{L}
-transducible
from
\mathscr{D}
if there is an
\mathcal{L}
-transduction
\mathsf{T}
such that
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{D})
; we denote this by
\mathscr{C}\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{L}}\mathscr{D}
. For the standard logics,
\mathcal{L}
-transductions are closed under composition. Hence in this case
\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{L}}
is a quasi-order on graph classes; we call it the
\mathcal{L}
-transduction quasi-order
. If classes
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are such that
\mathscr{C}\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{L}}\mathscr{D}
and
\mathscr{D}\sqsubseteq_{\mathcal{L}}\mathscr{C}
, then we say that
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are
\mathcal{L}
-equivalent
.

Depending on the expressive power of
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}
-transductions provide containment notions of varying strength for graphs and for graph classes. In this work we consider
\mathcal{L}\in\{{\mathsf{FO}},{\mathsf{MSO}},{\mathsf{CMSO}}\}
, where
{\mathsf{FO}}
is the standard first-order logic on graphs,
{\mathsf{MSO}}
is the
monadic second order logic
that extends
{\mathsf{FO}}
by the possibility of quantifying over vertex subsets, and
{\mathsf{CMSO}}
is an extension of
{\mathsf{MSO}}
by allowing counting modular predicates that can be applied to the cardinalities of sets.

Shrubdepth

To make the notion of a transduction more concrete, let us consider the following example. A
tree model
of a graph
G
is a labelled rooted tree
T
such that:

1.

the vertices of
T
are labelled with a finite set of labels;

2.

the leaf set of
T
is equal to the vertex set of
G
; and

3.

for every pair of vertices
u,v
of
G
, whether
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G
depends only on the triple of labels: of
u
, of
v
, and of the lowest common ancestor of
u
and
v
in
T
.

(See
section

2
for a formal definition.) Observe that if
T
is a tree model of
G
of height
h
and using a label set of size
k
, then
G
can be transduced from
T
using a fixed
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduction (that depends on
h
and
k
). The transduction first introduces a coloring of
T
that distinguishes vertices with different labels, as well as the leaves and the root. Then, the edge relation of
G
can be recovered by an
{\mathsf{FO}}
formula that for given two leaves
u,v
, finds the lowest common ancestor of
u
and
v
and compares the relevant triple of labels. Finally, the vertex set is restricted to the leaves of
T
.

Tree models were introduced by Ganian et al.
[
11
,
10
]
to define a class parameter called
shrubdepth
. Precisely, the
shrubdepth
of a graph class
\mathscr{C}
is the least
h\in\mathbb{N}
for which the following holds: there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that every graph of
\mathscr{C}
admits a tree model of height at most
h
that uses a set of at most
k
labels. The argument from the previous paragraph shows that if
\mathscr{C}
has bounded shrubdepth, then
\mathscr{C}
can be
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduced from a class of trees of bounded height. As proved in
[
10
]
, the converse is also true in a very strong sense: every class
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transducible from a class of bounded-height trees in fact has bounded shrubdepth, and this holds even for the stronger notions of
{\mathsf{MSO}}
- and
{\mathsf{CMSO}}
-transductions. Thus, the tree models presented above provide a “canonical form” for
\mathsf{FO}
-,
\mathsf{MSO}
-, and even
\mathsf{CMSO}
-transductions from classes of bounded height trees.

Classes of bounded shrubdepth appear to be an interesting concept on its own. As argued in
[
10
]
, shrubdepth is an analogue of treedepth suited for the treatment of dense graphs.
Classes of bounded treedepth have bounded shrubdepth, classes of bounded shrubdepth that are additionally sparse (say, exclude some fixed biclique as a subgraph) have bounded treedepth and, just as graphs with bounded treedepth are building blocks of graphs in bounded expansion classes
[
19
]
, graphs with bounded shrubdepth are building blocks of graphs in
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transductions of bounded expansion classes
[
8
]
.
Also, shrubdepth is functionally equivalent to several other parameters such as
rankdepth
and
SC-depth
, in the sense that the boundedness of one is equivalent to the boundedness of the others. It is noteworthy that any hereditary class with bounded shrubdepth can be defined by the exclusion of a finite number of induced subgraphs
[
10
]
.

{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transductions

The
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transduction quasi-order has been studied by Blumensath and Courcelle in
[
1
]
, who described the following structure. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathscr{F}_{n}
be the class of rooted forests of height
n
; here, the height of a forest is the length of the longest root-to-leaf path, hence
\mathscr{F}_{0}
is the class of edgeless graphs. Further, let
\mathscr{P}
be the class of all paths,
\mathscr{T}
be the class of
all trees, and
\mathscr{G}
be the class of all graphs. Clearly, we have

\emptyset\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{F}_{0}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{F}_{1}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\ldots\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{F}_{n}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\ldots\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{P}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{T}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{G}.

Up to
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transduction equivalence, these classes correspond to the classes with bounded shrubdepth (transducible from
\mathscr{F}_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
), classes with bounded linear cliquewidth (transducible from
\mathscr{P}
), and classes with bounded cliquewidth (transducible from
\mathscr{T}
).
In
[
1
]
, Blumensath and Courcelle proved that this hierarchy is strict. They conjectured that it is also complete in the following sense: every graph class is
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-equivalent to one of the classes in the hierarchy; see
[
1
, Open Problem 9.3]
.

This conjecture was confirmed for the initial prefix of the hierarchy by Ganian et al.
[
10
]
in the following sense: if
\mathscr{C}\sqsubseteq_{\mathsf{MSO}}\mathscr{F}_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
(equivalently,
\mathscr{C}
has bounded shrubdepth), then in fact
\mathscr{C}
is
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-equivalent to
\mathscr{F}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore, to resolve the open problem of Blumensath and Courcelle it remains to prove the following three conjectures.

Conjecture 1
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded shrubdepth if, and only if,
the class of all paths is not
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

Conjecture 2
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded linear cliquewidth if, and only if, the class of all trees is not
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

Conjecture 3
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded cliquewidth if, and only if, the class of all grids is not
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

We note that a positive resolution of
3
would establish another long-standing open problem,
Seese’s Conjecture
.

1
and
3
have been established in weaker forms, where one replaces
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transductions with
{\mathsf{CMSO}}
-transductions. More precisely, Kwon et al.
[
17
]
proved that every graph class of unbounded shrubdepth contains all paths as vertex-minors, and Courcelle and Oum
[
6
]
proved that every graph class of unbounded cliquewidth contains all grids as vertex-minors. Since the vertex-minor relation can be expressed by a
{\mathsf{CMSO}}
-transduction
[
6
]
, this yields right-to-left implications of the
{\mathsf{CMSO}}
-variants of
1
and
3
. The left-to-right implications are well-known to hold.

{\mathsf{FO}}
-transductions

Compared to the above, much less is known about
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transductions and the picture appears to be much more complicated; see
[
22
]
. However, in recent years there has been an attempt of constructing a sound structural theory for well-structured dense graphs based on this notion; see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
20
,
21
]
. In particular, the following conjectures have been formulated about characterizations of shrubdepth, linear cliquewidth, and cliquewidth in terms of
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transductions. These are strengthenings of
1
,
2
and
3
, respectively.

Conjecture 4

(
[
22
]
, repeated in
[
9
]
)
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded shrubdepth if, and only if,
the class of all paths is not
\mathsf{FO}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

Conjecture 5

(
[
9
]
)
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded linear cliquewidth if, and only if,
no class containing some subdivision of every binary tree is

\mathsf{FO}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

Conjecture 6

(
[
9
]
)
.

A class
\mathscr{C}
of graphs has bounded cliquewidth if, and only if,
no class containing some subdivision of every wall is
\mathsf{FO}
-transducible from
\mathscr{C}
.

In this work we prove
4
. As shown in
[
10
]
, the class of all paths does not have bounded shrubdepth, hence it is not
\mathsf{FO}
-transducible (equivalently,
\mathsf{MSO}
- or
\mathsf{CMSO}
-transducible) from any class with bounded shrubdepth (equivalently, from any class of bounded height trees). Therefore,
4
follows from the next theorem, which is the main result of our paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

If the class of all paths is not
\mathsf{FO}
-transducible from a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
, then
\mathscr{C}
has bounded shrubdepth.

Since every
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduction is also an
{\mathsf{MSO}}
-transduction, this also confirms
1
. More generally, together with the results of
[
10
]
,
theorem

1.1
proves that the property of having bounded shrubdepth is the largest property of graph classes that is closed under taking
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transductions (is a so-called
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduction ideal
) and whose restriction to classes that exclude a fixed biclique as a subgraph is the property of having bounded treedepth.

1.2
Graph-theoretic statements

The main idea behind the proof of
theorem

1.1
is to analyze graphs that exclude some simple substructures from which long paths can easily be transduced. These are: paths, complements of paths, and half-graphs. We prove two purely graph-theoretic statements which explain that graphs excluding the substructures mentioned above can be nicely decomposed into cographs. We need a few definitions to state the decomposition theorems formally.

For a graph
G
and disjoint subsets of vertices
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, by
G[A]
we denote the subgraph induced by
A
in
G
, and by
G[A,B]
we denote the bipartite graph
semi-induced
by
A
and
B
in
G
. The latter is the graph with vertex set
A\cup B
and edge set comprising of all edges of
G
with one endpoint in
A
and second in
B
. By
P_{t}
,
\overline{P}_{t}
, and
\widetilde{P}_{t}
we respectively denote the path on
t
vertices, its complement, and its
bipartite complement
: the graph obtained from
P_{t}
by taking its unique bipartition
A,B
and complementing the edge relation on the set
A\times B
. We also use the order-
k

half-graph

H_{k}
, and the order-
k

universal threshold graph

R_{k}
; see
fig.

1
.

Figure 1:
Left: the half-graph
H_{k}
. Right: the universal threshold graph
R_{k}
.

For decompositions we rely on the well-known class of
cographs
(that is,
P_{4}
-free graphs) and on their bipartite counterparts,
bi-cographs
(see
section

2
for a definition).
For a graph
G
, a
cosplit
of
G
is a partition
\mathscr{P}
of the vertex set of
G
such that for every part
A\in\mathscr{P}
, the induced subgraph
G[A]
is a cograph. A cosplit
\mathscr{P}
is moreover a
2
-cosplit
if for every pair of distinct parts
A,B\in\mathscr{P}
, the semi-induced bipartite graph
G[A,B]
is a bi-cograph. The
height
of a cosplit
\mathscr{P}
is the maximum height of the cographs in
\{G[A]\colon A\in\mathscr{P}\}
, where the height of a cograph is the height of its cotree. We define the height of a
2
-cosplit analogously, but we also take into account the heights of bi-cographs in
\{G[A,B]\colon A,B\in\mathscr{P},\ A\neq B\}
. Note that the height of a
2
-cosplit
\mathscr{P}
may be larger than the height of
\mathscr{P}
treated as a (standard) cosplit; this distinction will be always clear from the context. The
size
of a cosplit
\mathscr{P}
is simply
|\mathscr{P}|
.
The minimum size of a cosplit of a graph
G
is its

c
-chromatic number
(also called
P_{4}
-chromatic number
)
[
13
]
.

Our main decomposition results can be now stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every pair of integers
t,k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds: Every graph that excludes
P_{t}
,
\overline{P}_{t}
, and
R_{k}
as induced subgraphs admits a cosplit of size at most
N
and height at most
4k
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every pair of integers
t,k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds: Every graph that excludes
P_{t}
,
\widetilde{P}_{t}
, and
H_{k}
as semi-induced subgraphs admits a
2
-cosplit of size at most
N
and height at most
4k
.

Thus,
theorem

1.3
provides a much stronger form of a decomposition — a
2
-cosplit instead of a cosplit — at the expense of a stronger assumption about the excluded substructures. We note that our proofs yield bounds
N\leq 4\cdot(2t-5)^{k-1}
in case of
theorem

1.2
(for
t\geq 4
) and
N\leq 3^{2^{k}\cdot t^{3k-2}}
in case of
theorem

1.3
(for
t\geq 5
). In the proof of
theorem

1.1
we use only
theorem

1.3
, but
theorem

1.2
is actually a stepping stone in the proof of
theorem

1.3
and provides some interesting corollaries on its own; we will discuss these later.

The proofs of
theorems

1.2
and
1.3
rely on the influential Gyárfás’ path method
[
15
]
. Through a suitable understanding, this method provides a convenient decomposition notion for
P_{t}
-free graphs, which we call a
Gyárfás decomposition
. We sometimes consider a Gyárfás decomposition of the graph itself and sometimes of its complement; this is why we assume that the graph excludes both
P_{t}
and its (bipartite) complement. The main idea is to apply induction where the measure of progress is (roughly) the largest
k
such that
R_{k}
or
H_{k}
can be found as a (semi-)induced subgraph. This choice was inspired by the work of Gajarský et al.
[
9
]
.

The proof of
theorem

1.1
proceeds roughly as follows. The left-to-right implication is known
[
10
]
, hence it remains to show that if from a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
one cannot transduce the class of all paths, then
\mathscr{C}
has bounded shrubdepth. The assumption implies that there are some
t,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that all graphs in
\mathscr{C}
exclude
P_{t}
,
\widetilde{P}_{t}
, and
H_{k}
as semi-induced subgraphs. By
theorem

1.3
, every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
admits a
2
-cosplit of bounded size and height. This allows us to
sparsify

G
, that is, find a sparse graph
G^{\prime}
that encodes
G
in the following sense: both
G^{\prime}
can be transduced from
G
and
G
can be transduced back from
G^{\prime}
. Letting
\mathscr{D}
be the class comprised of all graphs
G^{\prime}
as above, we still have that from
\mathscr{D}
one cannot transduce the class of all paths, but now moreover we have that
\mathscr{D}
is sparse (formally, it is degenerate). Using known connections between treedepth and the existence of long paths one can now argue that
\mathscr{D}
actually has bounded treedepth. So
\mathscr{C}
, being transducible from
\mathscr{D}
, has bounded shrubdepth.

1.3
Other corollaries

Finally, we discuss several other statements that can be inferred from
theorems

1.2
and
1.3
. The first one concerns
\chi
-boundedness of the considered graph classes. Recall that a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
is
\chi
-bounded
if there is a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for every graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
, where
\chi(G)
and
\omega(G)
respectively denote the chromatic number and the clique number of
G
. If
f
can be additionally chosen to be a linear function, then
\mathscr{C}
is
linearly
\chi
-bounded
. It is known that for every
t\in\mathbb{N}
, graphs excluding
P_{t}
as an induced subgraph are
\chi
-bounded
[
15
]
. We show that if one additionally excludes the complement of a path and a universal threshold graph, then the resulting class is even linearly
\chi
-bounded.

Corollary 1.1
.

For every pair of integers
t,k\in\mathbb{N}
, the class of graphs that exclude
P_{t}
,
\overline{P}_{t}
, and
R_{k}
as induced subgraphs is linearly
\chi
-bounded.

Proof.

By
theorem

1.2
, there exists an integer
N
such that every graph
G
that excludes
P_{t}
,
\overline{P}_{t}
, and
R_{k}
as induced subgraphs has a cosplit
\mathscr{P}
of size at most
N
. As cographs are perfect we have

\chi(G)\leq\sum_{X\in\mathscr{P}}\chi(G[X])=\sum_{X\in\mathscr{P}}\omega(G[X])\leq N\omega(G).\qed

We remark that in
corollary

1.1
, excluding a path and an antipath is not sufficient to guarantee linear
\chi
-boundedness, for the following reason.
The lexicographic product of two
P_{t}
-free graphs is
P_{t}
-free and, as the complement of the lexicographic product of two graphs is (isomorphic to) the product of their complements, the same holds for
\overline{P}_{t}
-free graphs.
So, consider a triangle-free graph
G_{t}
with
t
vertices and fractional chromatic number
\chi_{f}(G_{t})\geq\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{t/\log t}
(see
[
16
]
).
Obviously,
G_{t}
is
P_{t+1}
-free. The lexicographic powers of
G_{t}
are
P_{t+1}
and
\overline{P}_{t+1}
-free and have chromatic number
\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)^{\log_{2}\chi_{f}(G_{t})}
(see, for example,
[
20
]
). Hence, the exponent of
\omega(G)
has to grow at least as
(1-o(1))\,\log_{2}t
.
For instance, the lexicographic powers of
C_{5}
exclude both
P_{5}
and
\overline{P}_{5}
as induced subgraph and satisfy
\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)^{c}
, where
c=\log_{2}\chi_{f}(C_{5})=\log_{2}5-1\approx 1.32
.

The next corollary concerns the Erdős-Hajnal property of graphs excluding a universal threshold graph.
Recall that a graph class
\mathscr{C}
has the
Erdős-Hajnal property
if there exists
\delta>0
such that every
n
-vertex graph
G\in\mathscr{C}
contains a
homogeneous set
— a clique or an independent set — of size at least
n^{\delta}
. It can be derived from the stable regularity lemma of Malliaris and Shelah
[
18
]
that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, graphs excluding
R_{k}
as an induced subgraph have the Erdős-Hajnal property. See also
[
4
]
, and
[
23
, Theorem 2.8 in Chapter 5]
for a streamlined presentation

†
†\dagger

†
†\dagger

\dagger

[

]

yielding
\delta=\frac{1}{2k+2}
. We now show that from
theorem

1.2
one can infer essentially the same result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Every graph
G
on
n
vertices that does not contain
R_{k}
as an induced subgraph contains a homogeneous set of size at least
n^{1/2k}/4
.

Proof.

As shown in the proof, for
t\geq 4
, the value of
N
in
theorem

1.2
can be upper bounded by
4\cdot(2t-5)^{2k-2}
.
Let
h
be the maximum size of a homogeneous subset in
G
; we may assume that
h\geq 2
. Obviously,
G
excludes
P_{2h+1}
and
\overline{P}_{2h+1}
as induced subgraphs, for taking every second vertex of such an induced subgraph would yield a homogeneous set of size
h+1
.
Thus, by
theorem

1.2
,
G
admits a cosplit of size at most
p=4\cdot(4h-3)^{2k-2}
. Hence,
G
contains an induced cograph with at least
n/p
vertices. It is well-known that every cograph on
m
vertices contains a homogeneous set of size at least
\sqrt{m}
, hence
G
contains a homogeneous set of size at least
\sqrt{n/p}
. It follows that

n/p\leq h^{2}
, that is,

A

We start by recalling some general definitions on relational structures. Then, we will focus on (uncolored or colored) graphs.

Recall that a

Let

Let
\sigma,\tau

For a

\varphi(\mathbf{A})=\{(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in A^{k}\colon\mathbf{A}\models\varphi(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\}.

A

A
(non copying) transduction

A more general notion of a transduction allows the so-called
copying
operation: blowing up every vertex into a bounded size clique. In this paper we prove that the class of all paths can be obtained from any class with unbounded shrubdepth using a non-copying transduction, hence there is no need for this extension.

\mathsf{T}
is defined by an interpretation
\mathsf{I}
in colored graphs. The semantic is as follows: for an uncolored graph
G

\mathsf{T}(G)=\{\mathsf{I}\circ\Lambda(G)\colon\Lambda\text{ is a coloring of }G\}.

In other words, one can think of a transduction as of a non-deterministic mechanism that inputs a graph, colors it arbitrarily, and applies a fixed interpretation at the end. Then
\mathsf{T}(G)
is the set of all graphs that can be obtained in this manner. Note that even though there exist infinitely many colorings of
G
, only a finite numbers of predicates appear in the formulas defining the interpretation

. Hence, for a graph
G

\mathsf{T}(G)
is always finite.

For a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
we define
\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})=\bigcup_{G\in\mathscr{C}}\mathsf{T}(G)
. The terminology above can be lifted to colored graphs (and in fact, to relational structures) in the expected manner; we assume here that all unary predicates introduced in the coloring step are distinct from all unary predicates already used in the graph.

We note that the composition of two transductions is a transduction.
A class of graphs
\mathscr{D}
can be

from a class of graphs
\mathscr{C}
if there exists a transduction
\mathsf{T}
such that
\mathscr{D}\subseteq\mathsf{T}(\mathscr{C})
.
Two classes
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are called

if
\mathscr{D}
can be transduced from
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{C}
can be transduced from
\mathscr{D}
.

We shall consider a very restricted type of interpretations.

1.

A formula
\eta(x,y)
in the signature of (colored) graphs naturally defines a simple interpretation
\mathsf{I}_{\eta}
of (colored) graphs in (colored) graphs, where
\rho_{0}
is a tautology (i.e. a formula that is always satisfied),
\rho_{E}(x,y)\coloneqq(x\neq y)\wedge(\eta(x,y)\vee\eta(y,x))

\rho_{P_{i}}(x)\coloneqq P_{i}(x)
.
(Note that the formula

if the source graphs are colored.)

2.

A

of two classes

\mathscr{D}

Note that the existence of a simple equivalence between two classes

Gyárfás decomposition

As explained in
section

1
, our main tool will be the Gyárfás’ path argument

]
. For convenience, we encapsulate it in an abstract notion of a
Gyárfás decomposition
.
We shall see that this decomposition is very close
to the one used to define treedepth and, more generally, to the
property of Trémaux trees (a.k.a. Depth-First Search trees).

Definition 3.1
.

A
Gyárfás decomposition
of a connected graph
G
is a
rooted tree
Y
satisfying the following properties:

1.

The nodes of
Y
are pairwise disjoint and non-empty subsets of
V(G)
, called

, whose union is equal to

. In other words, the node set of

is a partition of
V(G)
.

2.

The root bag of
Y
consists of a single
vertex, called the
initial vertex
of the Gyárfás decomposition
Y
.

3.

If vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V(G)
are adjacent in
G
and
B,B^{\prime}
are bags of
Y
such that
u\in B
and
u^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, then one of the bags
B,B^{\prime}
is an ancestor of the other in
Y
. (Note that possibly
B=B^{\prime}
, as every node is an ancestor of itself.)

4.

For every bag
B
of
Y
, the subgraph of
G
induced by the union of
B
and all its descendants in
Y
is connected.

5.

For every non-root bag
B
of
Y
, there exists a
vertex
h(B)
, called the
hook
of
B
, which belongs to the parent of
B
in
Y
and satisfies the following property:
h(B)

is adjacent to all the vertices of
B
and non-adjacent to all the vertices contained in strict descendants of
B
.

A Gyárfás decomposition of a disconnected graph
G
is the union of Gyárfás decompositions of the connected components of
G
.

We first note that every graph has a Gyárfás decomposition and that such a decomposition can be efficiently computed. The proof closely follows the construction of Gyárfás’ presented in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every connected graph
G
and vertex
r
of
G
, there exists a Gyárfás decomposition of
G
whose initial vertex is
r
. Moreover, given
G
and
r
, such a Gyárfás decomposition can be computed in polynomial time.

Proof.

Consider the following recursive procedure that given a connected graph
G
and a non-empty subset of vertices
A
,
constructs a decomposition of
G
into a tree of bags with
A
being the root bag as follows. Consider the connected components
C_{1},\dots,C_{\ell}
of

G-A
.
For each
1\leq i\leq\ell
choose an arbitrary vertex
v_{i}\in A

with a neighbor in
C_{i}
. Such a vertex must exist as
G
is connected.
For each
1\leq i\leq\ell
, apply the procedure recursively to
C_{i}
(which is connected by definition) and the subset

A_{i}=N(v_{i})\cap V(C_{i})\subseteq V(C_{i})
. Finally, combine the obtained decompositions by attaching their root bags

as children of a new root bag

.
It is easily checked that if we apply the procedure to
G
and

, then the obtained
decomposition is a Gyárfás decompositionof
G

v_{i}
serve as hooks. Also, the procedure clearly runs in polynomial time.

Let

be a Gyárfás decomposition of a graph

.
The

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a Gyárfás decomposition of a

-free graph

is at most

.

Proof.

Let

be the longest root-to-leaf path in

, where

is a root bag. Let
v_{\ell}
be any vertex of

, and for

, let

Gyárfás decompositions of bipartite graphs will be of prime importance in this paper. In this setting, it is worth noticing the next property (see

).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a connected bipartite graph, and let

Therefore, we are left with proving

. As we deal with bipartite graphs, it will be convenient to introduce a variant of the strong index invariant that is suited for this setting.

Fact 5.2

For every bipartite graph

.

Next, let us characterize connected bipartite graphs of bipartite index

Fact 5.3
.

If

, then

is a complete bipartite graph.

Let

be the sides of

. By contradiction, suppose there exist

and

. So there is no edge with one endpoint in

and second in

is connected.
∎

Also, we have the following counterpart of

6
Sparsification: proof of
theorem

1.1

In this section we use the results gathered so far to prove our main result,
theorem

1.1
. As mentioned, the strategy is to show that for fixed
t,k\in\mathbb{N}
, graphs excluding semi-induced
P_{t}
,
\widetilde{P}_{t}
, and
H_{k}
can be sparsified using an
{\mathsf{FO}}
transduction. Then we exploit the known connections between treedepth and the existence of long paths as subgraphs.

We first show that cographs with bounded height can be efficiently sparsified. Here, by a
coloring procedure
we mean an algorithm that inputs a graph and outputs some coloring of it

†
†\dagger

†
†\dagger

\dagger

Formally, we defined colorings to use an infinite number of colors, but in what follows, coloring procedures always output colorings using a finite number of colors. One may imagine that the remaining colors are set to be empty sets and are thus omitted in the description of the output. They are also never used by any formula that we are about to construct.
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every positive integer
h
there exist a polynomial-time coloring procedure
\mathsf{A}
, a class
\mathscr{D}_{1}
of colored graphs with treedepth at most
h
, and two first-order formulas
\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}
, such that

(\varphi_{1},\psi_{1})
is a simple equivalence of the classes
\mathsf{A}(\mathscr{C})
and
\mathscr{D}_{1}
, where
\mathscr{C}
is the class of all cographs with height at most
h
.

Proof.

We first define a sequence of partitions
\mathscr{P}_{0},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{h}
of the vertex set of
G
as follows:

1.

\mathscr{P}_{0}=\{V(G)\}
.

2.

For odd
i\geq 1
,
\mathscr{P}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathscr{P}_{i-1}
by splitting every part
A\in\mathscr{P}_{i-1}
into the vertex sets of the connected components of
G[A]
.

3.

For even
i\geq 1
,
\mathscr{P}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathscr{P}_{i-1}
by splitting every part
A\in\mathscr{P}_{i-1}
into the vertex sets of the connected components of
\overline{G}[A]
.

It is straightforward to see that provided
G
is a cograph of height at most
h
,
\mathscr{P}_{h}
will be the discrete partition with every vertex in its own part. Clearly, partitions
\mathscr{P}_{0},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{h}
can be computed in polynomial time.

Next, for
0\leq i\leq h
we inductively define formulas
\varpi_{i}(x,y)
expressing that
x
and
y
are in the same part of
\mathscr{P}_{i}
. For
i=0
this is trivial. For odd
i\geq 1
,
\varpi_{i}(x,y)
can be obtained from
\varpi_{i-1}(x,y)
by checking that
\varpi_{i-1}(x,y)
holds and
x
and
y
are in the same connected component of the subgraph of
G
induced by the part of
\mathscr{P}_{i-1}
to which
x
and
y
belong. Note here that in the setting of of cographs, belonging to the same connected component is equivalent to being at distance at most
3
, hence it can be expressed in
{\mathsf{FO}}
. The construction for even
i\geq 1
is the same, except we complement the subgraph induced by the part of
x
and
y
.

The coloring procedure
\mathsf{A}
first computes the partitions
\mathscr{P}_{0},\ldots,\mathscr{P}_{h}
. Then, for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,h\}
, it marks one arbitrary vertex in each part of
\mathscr{P}_{i}
with predicate
P_{i}
. The coloring of
G
obtained in this way is the output of
\mathsf{A}
on
G
.

The formula
\varphi_{1}(x,y)
expresses that for some
0\leq i\leq h
,
\varpi_{i}(x,y)
holds and one of
x
and
y
is marked with
P_{i}
. It is straightforward to see that then
\mathsf{I}_{\varphi_{1}}(\mathsf{A}(G))
is a colored graph with treedepth at most
h
.

Denote by
N[v]
the closed neighborhood of a vertex
v
(i.e. the set formed by
v
and all its neighbors).
It is easily checked that two vertices
u
and
v
of
G
are adjacent if and only if, in
\mathsf{I}_{\varphi_{1}}(\mathsf{A}(G))
, the maximum integer
i

such that
N[u]\cap N[v]
contains a vertex marked with
P_{i}
is odd. As
0\leq i\leq h
, this property is first-order definable by a formula
\psi_{1}
.
∎

We now give an analogous result for bi-cographs with bounded height.
As the proof is very similar (considering bi-complement instead of complements), we omit it.

Lemma 6.2
.

For every positive integer
h
there exist a polynomial time coloring procedure
\mathsf{B}
, a class
\mathscr{D}_{2}
of colored graphs with treedepth at most
h
, and two first-order formulas
\varphi,\psi
, such that

(\varphi_{2},\psi_{2})
is a simple equivalence of the classes
\mathsf{B}(\mathscr{B})
and
\mathscr{D}_{2}
, where
\mathscr{B}
is the class of all bi-cographs with height at most

.

The next theorem is an easy consequence of the two preceding lemmas.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N,k
be integers, and let
\mathscr{C}
be the class of all graphs that admit a
2
-cosplit of size at most
N
and height at most

. Then there exist a coloring procedure
\mathsf{C}
, a class
\mathscr{D}
of
dN
-degenerate graphs, and two first-order formulas

, such that the following hold:

1.

Given a graph

2
-cosplit of

of size at most
N
and height at most

, the procedure
\mathsf{C}

2.

The pair
(\varphi,\psi)
is a simple equivalence of the classes
\mathsf{C}(\mathscr{C})
and

.

Proof.

The coloring procedure
\mathsf{C}
first colors the vertices according to the
2
-cosplit.
Then, it calls the procedure
\mathsf{A}
of

6.1
on the graph induced by each part, and the procedure

of

on the bipartite subgraph induced by each pair of distinct parts. Here, all unary predicates used by all invocation of procedures

and

are kept distinct. Then, one can easily construct the formulas

and
\psi
using a disjunction on the possible parts of the
2
-cosplit to which
x
and
y
belong.
∎

By combining

with

we derive the following statement.

Corollary 6.1
.

Every class of graphs that excludes a path, the bipartite complement of a path, and a half-graph as semi-induced subgraphs is transduction equivalent to a class of degenerate graphs.

We are now ready to prove our main result.

Proof of
theorem

1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a class of graphs such that the class of all paths cannot be

{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduced from
\mathscr{C}
. Then there are integers

such that no graph in
\mathscr{C}
contains

,
\widetilde{P}_{t}
, or

as a semi-induced subgraph.
Therefore, by
corollary

6.1
,
\mathscr{C}
is transduction equivalent to a class
\mathscr{D}
of degenerate graphs. Clearly, it is still the case that the class of all paths cannot be
{\mathsf{FO}}
-transduced from
\mathscr{D}
, hence graphs in
\mathscr{D}
do not contain all paths as induced subgraphs. Since
\mathscr{D}
is in addition degenerate,
by
[
19
, Proposition 6.4]

it follows that
\mathscr{D}
has bounded treedepth, hence also bounded shrubdepth. So by the results of
[
10
]
,
\mathscr{C}
has bounded shrubdepth as well.
∎

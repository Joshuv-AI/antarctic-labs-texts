A bound on the Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of semigroup rings
Havi Ellers

Abstract
In this paper we prove an explicit, computable upper bound on the Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of the local cohomology of a pointed, affine semigroup ring over a perfect field of positive characteristic. This bound depends only on the characteristic of the ring and properties of the semigroup.



1
Introduction

The Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number (HSL number) is a numerical invariant of modules with a Frobenius action. Roughly speaking, it is a degree of nilpotency for the Frobenius action on the module. One important example of a class of modules with a Frobenius action is the class of local cohomology modules of a ring of positive characteristic. If the ring is local, we can define the Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number (HSL number) of the ring to be the largest HSL number of any of its local cohomology modules with support at the maximal ideal. Then the Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number can be seen as a singularity invariant of the ring, and can be connected to various types of
F
-singularities. See
[
HQ19
,
Mad19
,
Quy19
,
PQ19
,
HQ22
,
KMPS23
,
MP23
,
ST17
]
for several of these connections. In particular, a local ring of positive characteristic is
F
-injective if and only if it has zero HSL number.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring of characteristic
p>0
. A
Frobenius action
on an
R
-module
M
is an additive map
\rho:M\to M
such that
\rho(rm)=r^{p}\rho(m)
for all
r\in R,m\in M
. Given any
R
-module
M
with Frobenius action
\rho:M\to M
, we can define the
nilpotent submodule
of
M
to be

\displaystyle 0^{\rho}_{M}

\displaystyle=\{m\in M\,|\,\rho^{e}(m)=0\text{ for some }e\in\mathbb{N}\}.

The Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number of
M
is then defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module with Frobenius action
\rho:M\to M
. Then the
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of
M
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{HSL}(M)

\displaystyle=\inf\{e\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,\rho^{e}(m)=0\text{ for all }m\in 0^{\rho}_{M}\}.

If
M
is Noetherian it is straightforward to see that
\mathrm{HSL}(M)
is finite. Surprisingly, the HSL number is also finite for Artinian modules:

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
HS77
, Prop 1.11]
,
[
Lyu97
, Prop 4.4]
,
[
Sha06
, Cor 1.8]
) If
M
is an Artinian
R
-module with Frobenius action
\rho:M\to M
, then
\mathrm{HSL}(M)<\infty
. That is, there exists an
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho^{e}(m)=0
for all
m\in 0^{\rho}_{M}
.

One important class of Artinian
R
-modules with a Frobenius action is the class of local cohomology modules of a local ring
R
with support at the maximal ideal, and with Frobenius action induced by the natural Frobenius action on
R
. In this paper, we focus on affine pointed semigroup rings
R
over a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
with maximal monomial ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. We find an explicit upper bound on
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Q
be an affine pointed semigroup of dimension
n
. There exists
N_{Q}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending only on
Q
, such that the following holds. Let
k
be any perfect field of any characteristic
p>N_{Q}
and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal monomial ideal of
k[Q]
. For
m_{\mathcal{H}}
as described in Remark
4.2
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))\leq\max_{\mathcal{H}}\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil,

where the maximum ranges over faces
\mathcal{H}
of
Q
with
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell
.

Remark 1.4
.

The number
m_{\mathcal{H}}
depends only on
\mathcal{H}
, and roughly speaking it depends on how far
\mathcal{H}
is from its saturation.

Remark 1.5
.

See Definition
4.1
and Remark
4.4
for an explicit description of
N_{Q}
.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.3
can be refined so that
N_{Q}
is optimized for each
\ell
. See Theorem
4.3
.

Theorem
1.3
has the following consequence:

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
Q
be an affine pointed semigroup of dimension
n
. Then for any
p\gg 0
, if
k
is any perfect field of characteristic
p
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
then
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))
is either 0 or 1. That is, either Frobenius is injective on
H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
or the nilpotent submodule of
H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
is exactly the kernel of Frobenius.

Note that if the semigroup
Q
is saturated then
k[Q]
is normal (see for example
[
MS05
, Prop 7.25]
) and strongly
F
-regular (see
[
SS
, Example 4.20]
) and therefore Frobenius acts injectively on local cohomology and
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))=0
for all indices
\ell
. However, the converse is false: there are non-saturated semigroups
Q
such that
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))=0
for all indices
\ell
and all
p\gg 0
. For example, using Theorem
1.3
the reader can easily verify the following example.

Example 1.8
.

Let
R=k[x^{2},x^{2}y,xy,xy^{2},y^{2}]
and
Q
be the associated semigroup in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then
x=\frac{x^{2}y}{xy}
is in the saturation of
Q
but not in
Q
, hence
Q
is not saturated. However, if
p>2
then
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))=\mathrm{HSL}(H^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))=0
.

In Section
5
we additionally see how the results of this paper can be used to obtain bounds on the Frobenius test exponent of any weakly
F
-nilpotent or Cohen-Macaulay affine pointed semigroup ring. See Corollary
5.1
and Corollary
5.2
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we give background on semigroup rings, local cohomology, and HSL numbers. In Section
3
we give some preliminary results and in Section
4
we give the proof of the main theorem. In Section
5
we describe applications to bounds for Frobenius test exponents.

2
Background

2.1
Semigroup Rings

A
semigroup
is a set
Q
together with a binary operation
+
that satisfies the associative property: for all
u,v,w\in Q
we have
(u+v)+w=u+(v+w)
. A semigroup is
affine
if it is finitely generated and is isomorphic to a sub-semigroup of the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
BG09
, page 50]
. We always assume that our semigroups are affine.

Given a semigroup
Q
, for each field
k
we can form the corresponding
semigroup ring

k[Q]
, which is the
k
-algebra with
k
-basis
\{\underline{x}^{v}\,|\,v\in Q\}
and multiplication defined by
\underline{x}^{v}\cdot\underline{x}^{w}=\underline{x}^{v+w}
. Note that
k[Q]
has a natural
Q
-grading. If
Q
is affine then
k[Q]
is finitely generated as a
k
-algebra.

The
maximal monomial ideal
of
k[Q]
is the ideal generated by all non-unit monomials. A semigroup is
pointed
if it has no non-identity units. The maximal monomial ideal of
k[Q]
is a maximal ideal if and only if
Q
is pointed (see
[
ILL+07
, Exercise 20.15]
). When
Q
is pointed we will also refer to
k[Q]
as a
pointed
semigroup ring.

Let
M
be a lattice, i.e. a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group. Let
M^{\vee}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
denote the dual lattice of
M
, which is itself a lattice. Let
M_{\mathbb{R}}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
M_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
the usual vector space dual. A
rational convex polyhedral cone
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a set

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\{r_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+r_{s}v_{s}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,r_{i}\geq 0\}

generated by a finite set of vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\in M
. The
dual
of
\sigma
is

\displaystyle\sigma^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\,|\,u(v)\geq 0\text{ for all }v\in\sigma\}.

Note that the dual of a rational convex polyhedral cone in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a rational convex polyhedral cone in
M_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
. The
dimension
of a cone is the dimension of the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
\mathbb{R}\cdot\sigma=\sigma+(-\sigma)
.

Given a lattice
M
and a rational convex polyhedral cone
\sigma
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, a
face

\tau
of
\sigma
is
\{v\in\sigma\,|\,u(v)=0\}
for some
u\in\sigma^{\vee}
. A face is itself a rational convex polyhedral cone, and the
dimension
of a face is its dimension as a cone. A
facet
is a face of codimension 1.

If
\sigma
spans
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tau
is a facet of
\sigma
then there is a
u\in\sigma^{\vee}
, unique up to scalar multiplication, such that
\tau=\{v\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,u(v)=0\}
. We denote such a vector by
u_{\tau}
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

(See
[
Ful93
, page 11]
.)
Let
\sigma
be a rational convex polyhedral cone in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
\sigma
spans
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\sigma\not=M_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\sigma
is the intersection of the half-spaces
H_{\tau}=\{v\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,u_{\tau}(v)\geq 0\}
as
\tau
ranges over the facets of
\sigma
.

Given a semigroup
Q
, let
\mathrm{gr}\left(Q\right)
denote the group generated by
Q
(see
[
BG09
, page 50]
for a construction of
\mathrm{gr}\left(Q\right)
; if
Q
is a sub-semigroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
then
\mathrm{gr}\left(Q\right)
can be identified with
\mathbb{Z}Q\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
). Then
\mathrm{gr}\left(Q\right)
is a lattice. If
M=\mathrm{gr}\left(Q\right)
and
\sigma
is the cone generated in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
by
Q
, we can, similarly to above, define a
face
of
Q
to be the intersection of
Q
with a face of
\sigma
, and a
facet
of
Q
to be the intersection of
Q
with a facet of
\sigma
. The
dimension
of a face of
Q
is the dimension of the corresponding face of
\sigma
. We will also refer to a face of
Q
of dimension
i
as an
i
-face. A
ray
is a 1-face. We further define the
saturation
of
Q
to be

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{sat}}=\sigma\cap M.

Note that
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
is also a semigroup, and that
k[Q_{\mathrm{sat}}]
is the normalization of
k[Q]
(see
[
MS05
, Prop 7.25]
).

2.2
Local Cohomology and HSL Numbers

For a commutative ring
R
with ideal
I\subset R
, the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
local cohomolgy module
of
R
at
I
is the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
right derived functor of

\displaystyle H^{0}_{I}(R)=\{x\in R\,|\,I^{N}x=0\text{ for some }N\in\mathbb{N}\}

in the category of
R
-modules. We consider
R=k[Q]
a pointed semigroup ring and
I=\mathfrak{m}
the maximal monomial ideal of
R
. In this case, the local cohomology of
R
at
\mathfrak{m}
can be computed using a combinatorial complex called the Ishida complex (see
[
Ish88
]
).

We use the following notation from
[
ILL+07
]
: for a face
\mathcal{F}
of
Q
, we write
k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
for the localization of
k[Q]
at the set of monomials
\underline{x}^{v}
for
v\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that
k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
is equivalently the semigroup ring generated by the semigroup

Q-\mathcal{F}=\{u-v\,|\,u\in Q,v\in\mathcal{F}\}.

The
Ishida complex

\mho^{\bullet}_{Q}
of the semigroup
Q
is the complex

\displaystyle 0\to k[Q]\to\bigoplus_{\text{rays }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{\delta^{i}}\cdots\to\bigoplus_{\text{facets }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to k[M]\to 0,

(1)

where
M
is the group generated by
Q
.

The differential
\delta
is described via componentwise maps
\delta_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}:k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
, for
\mathcal{F}
an
i
-face and
\mathcal{G}
an
(i+1)
-face. If
\mathcal{F}\not\subset\mathcal{G}
then
\delta_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
is the zero map. Otherwise
\delta_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}}
is, up to a sign, the natural localization
k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
. The signs will not be relevant in this paper, but are derived as in the algebraic cochain complex for the polytope
\mathcal{P}
obtained as a transverse hyperplane section of
\sigma
. See
[
ILL+07
, page 208]
,
[
MS05
, Def 13.21]
,
[
MOY22
, Section 2]
and
[
Ish88
]
for more extensive descriptions. Note that the
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
are all
M
-graded, since the
k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
are
M
-graded and the differential
\delta
preserves that grading.

There is a natural Frobenius action on the complex (
1
), given by

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}\frac{f_{\mathcal{F}}}{x^{w_{\mathcal{F}}}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}\frac{f_{\mathcal{F}}^{p}}{x^{pw_{\mathcal{F}}}}

and this induces a corresponding Frobenius action on local cohomology. We will use this description of the Frobenius action on
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
to study their HSL numbers.

4
Main Result

In this section we prove the main result, Theorem
1.3
. Throughout we use notation as in Notation
3.1
. First we must define a certain invariant.

Definition 4.1
.

For
Q
an affine pointed semigroup and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
N_{Q}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{Z}
to be the maximum annihilator of any of the groups

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)}{\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)}

where
\mathcal{H}
is a face of
Q
with
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell-1
, or zero if all these groups are trivial.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall from Section
3
that for a face
\mathcal{H}
of
Q
the number
m_{\mathcal{H}}
is calculated as follows. Write
\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
as an intersection of half-spaces:

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r_{\mathcal{H}}}\{v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\,|\,u_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}(v)\geq 0\}

and let
\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathcal{H}
be such that
\gamma_{\mathcal{H}}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{sat}}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Then
m_{\mathcal{H}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq r_{\mathcal{H}}}\{u_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}(\gamma_{\mathcal{H}})\}
.

We will prove the following refinement of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
Q
be an affine pointed semigroup of dimesion
n
. Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
N_{Q}^{(\ell)}
be as in Definition
4.1

be the maximal monomial ideal of

\max_{\mathcal{H}}\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil

\mathcal{H}
of

\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell

Remark 4.4
.

We have
N_{Q}^{(1)}\geq\cdots\geq N_{Q}^{(n)}

Before we prove Theorem

starts on

.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the semigroup

from Example

. Here we calculate

and the upper bound from Theorem
4.3
for

\frac{\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)}{\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)}

of

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1\\

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
4.3
. It turns out that the proof will come down to the following fact about semigroups:

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
Q
be an affine pointed semigroup of dimension
n
. Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and let
N_{Q}^{(\ell)}
be as in Definition
4.1
. Fix a prime

, let
\mathcal{F}
be an
(\ell-1)
-face of

be such that

, where the maximum ranges over faces
\mathcal{H}
of

and where

is as in Definition

.

Before proving Theorem
4.6
we first prove two additional lemmas. The proof of Theorem
4.6
starts on
section

4
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
(\ell-1)
-face of
Q
and let
v\in M,e\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{e}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
. Write
p^{e}v+w\in Q
for some
w\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
v+w\in\sigma
.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq j\leq r
. We wish to show that
u_{j}(v+w)\geq 0
. First suppose
u_{j}(v)\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle u_{j}(v+w)=u_{j}(v)+u_{j}(w)\geq 0

since
w\in\mathcal{F}\subset Q\subset\sigma
. Now suppose
u_{j}(v)<0
. Then

\displaystyle u_{j}(v+w)=u_{j}(v)+u_{j}(w)\geq p^{e}u_{j}(v)+u_{j}(w)=u_{j}(p^{e}v+w)\geq 0

since
p^{e}v+w\in Q\subset Q
. In either case we get
v+w\in\sigma
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
(\ell-1)
-face of
Q
and let
v\in M,e\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{e}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
. Then there is a face
\mathcal{H}
of
Q
and
w\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H},p^{e}v+w\in Q
and
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{int}}
.

Proof.

To prove Lemma
4.8
it suffices to show the following statement:

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
(\ell-1)
-face of
Q
and let
v\in M,e\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{e}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
. Let
w\in\mathcal{F}
be such that
p^{e}v+w\in Q
. Then there is a face
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
of
Q
and
\widetilde{w}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
and
v+w+\widetilde{w}\in\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}}^{\mathrm{int}}
.

To prove this, first recall that
v+w\in\sigma
by Lemma
4.7
. We proceed by induction on the number,
d
, of facets of
\sigma
containing
v+w
.

For the base case, suppose
d=0
. Then
v+w
is not contained in any facets of
\sigma
, i.e.,
v+w\in\sigma^{\mathrm{int}}=\sigma_{Q}^{\mathrm{int}}
. Since
\mathcal{F}\subset Q
we can set
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=Q
and
\widetilde{w}=0\in\mathcal{F}
and we’re done.

Now suppose the claim holds for natural numbers strictly less than
d
. Suppose
v+w
is contained in
d
facets of
\sigma
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the minimal face of
Q
such that
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcap_{v+w\in\sigma_{j}}\sigma_{j},\qquad\mathcal{H}=\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}
we’re done, since
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{int}}
by minimality of
\mathcal{H}
. So suppose
\mathcal{F}\not\subset\mathcal{H}
. We claim there is
w_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
v+w+w_{1}
is contained in strictly fewer facets than
v+w
. We can then apply the induction hypothesis.

Indeed, since
\mathcal{F}\not\subset\mathcal{H}
, there is
w_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
w_{1}\not\in\mathcal{H}
. Further,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q=\left(\bigcap_{v+w\in\sigma_{j}}\sigma_{j}\right)\cap Q

so there is a facet
\sigma_{j_{1}}
such that
v+w\in\sigma_{j_{1}}
and
w_{1}\not\in\sigma_{j_{1}}
. We claim that
v+w+w_{1}
is contained in strictly fewer facets than
v+w
. Indeed, recall that for an arbitrary cone
\tau
, a face
\widetilde{\tau}
of
\tau
, and
a,b\in\tau
, we have
a+b\in\widetilde{\tau}
if and only if
a\in\widetilde{\tau}
and
b\in\widetilde{\tau}
. Hence
v+w+w_{1}
is not contained in
more
facets than
v+w
, since any facet not containing
v+w
cannot contain
v+w+w_{1}
. Similarly, since
w_{1}\not\in\sigma_{j_{1}}
we have
v+w+w_{1}\not\in\sigma_{j_{1}}
. Hence
v+w+w_{1}
is contained in strictly fewer facets than
v+w
, as desired.

By the induction hypothesis there is a face
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
of
Q
and
\widetilde{w}_{1}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
v+w+w_{1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}\in\sigma_{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}}^{\mathrm{int}}
. Taking
\widetilde{w}=w_{1}+\widetilde{w}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
we get the desired result.
∎

We now prove Theorem
4.6
.

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
N_{Q}^{(\ell)}
be as in Definition
4.1
and let

\displaystyle e_{0}:=\max_{\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell}\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil.

Fix
p>N_{Q}^{(\ell)}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
(\ell-1)
-face of
Q
, and let
v\in M,e\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
p^{e}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
. We wish to show that
p^{\widetilde{e}}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
for
\widetilde{e}\geq e_{0}
.

To this end, let
w\in\mathcal{F}
be such that
p^{e}v+w\in Q
. By Lemma
4.1
, we may assume there is a face
\mathcal{H}
of
Q
such that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}
and
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{int}}
. We claim that
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, and we will show this using Lemma
3.5
.

To apply Lemma
3.5
, we must show that
p^{e}v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)
and
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)
. We certainly have
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)
, since
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M
and
w\in\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M
. So let’s show
p^{e}v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)
.

First, since
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)
we also have
p^{e}v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)
. But
p^{e}v+w\in Q
, so
p^{e}v+w\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M\right)\cap Q=(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap M)\cap Q=\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q
. Since
w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q
, we get
p^{e}v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)
as desired.

We can now apply Lemma
3.5
to get that
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\cap Q\right)=\mathrm{gr}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

To prove the theorem, we now have two cases to consider. First suppose
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}
. Then
v\in\mathrm{gr}\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\subset Q-\mathcal{F}
, which immediately gives the desired result. On the other hand, suppose
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{H}
. Then
v+w\in\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{int}}\cap\mathrm{gr}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, so
m(v+w)\in\mathcal{H}
for
m\geq m_{\mathcal{H}}
by Lemma
3.4
. Since
mw\in\mathcal{F}
we get
mv\in Q-\mathcal{F}
for
m\geq m_{\mathcal{H}}
. Now let
\widetilde{e}\geq e_{0}
. Note that
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell
because
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{H}
. Hence
\widetilde{e}\geq\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil
, and so
p^{\widetilde{e}}\geq m_{\mathcal{H}}
. Thus
p^{\widetilde{e}}v\in Q-\mathcal{F}
as desired.
∎

Finally, we can prove Theorem
4.3
.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\ell=0
or
\ell>n
then
H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])=0
so the claim holds trivially. Thus we may assume
1\leq\ell\leq n
. Let

\displaystyle e_{0}:=\max_{\dim\mathcal{H}\geq\ell}\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil.

We wish to show that
\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q]))\leq e_{0}
.

First recall that
H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])=\ker(\delta^{\ell})/\mathrm{im}(\delta^{\ell-1})
where
\delta
is the differential in the Ishida complex:

\displaystyle 0\to k[Q]\to\bigoplus_{\text{rays }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{i\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\xrightarrow{\delta^{i}}\cdots\to\bigoplus_{\text{facets }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}\to k[M]\to 0.

(3)

Hence every cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
is represented by some
\alpha\in\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
. The natural Frobenius action on the complex (
3
), given by

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}\frac{f_{\mathcal{F}}}{x^{w_{\mathcal{F}}}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}\frac{f_{\mathcal{F}}^{p}}{x^{pw_{\mathcal{F}}}},

induces the Frobenius action on local cohomology. For an element
\alpha\in\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
we denote the images of
\alpha
and
[\alpha]
under these Frobenius actions by
\alpha^{p}
and
[\alpha]^{p}
, respectively.

The natural
Q
(and therefore
M
) grading on
k[Q]
induces an
M
-grading on all of the objects in the Ishida complex; furthermore the maps in the Ishida complex respect this grading.

We must show that for a local cohomology class
[\alpha]\in H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])
, if
[\alpha]\in 0^{F}_{H^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{m}}(k[Q])}
then
[\alpha]^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=0
for
\widetilde{e}\geq e_{0}
. Considering the
M
-grading on the Ishida complex, we may assume that
\alpha
is homogeneous, i.e.,

\alpha=\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}a_{\mathcal{F}}x^{v}

for some
v\in M
and
a_{\mathcal{F}}\in k
.

Since
[\alpha]^{p^{e}}=0
we have
\alpha^{p^{e}}\in\mathrm{im}\delta^{\ell-1}
, i.e.,
\alpha^{p^{e}}=\delta^{\ell-1}(\beta)
for some
\beta\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
. Write

\beta=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}b_{\mathcal{G}}x^{p^{e}v}

for some
b_{\mathcal{G}}\in k
.

Fix
\widetilde{e}\geq e_{0}
. If
\widetilde{e}\geq e
then

\displaystyle\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=\left(\alpha^{p^{e}}\right)^{p^{\widetilde{e}-e}}=\left(\delta^{\ell-1}(\beta)\right)^{p^{\widetilde{e}-e}}=\delta^{\ell-1}(\beta^{p^{\widetilde{e}-e}}).

Hence
\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}\in\mathrm{im}\delta^{\ell-1}
, i.e.,
[\alpha]^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=0
.

On the other hand, suppose
\widetilde{e}<e
. Let
\widetilde{\beta}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}b_{\mathcal{G}}^{p^{\widetilde{e}-e}}x^{p^{\widetilde{e}}v}
. We claim that
\widetilde{\beta}\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
and that
\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=\delta^{\ell-1}(\widetilde{\beta})
.

First we show that
\widetilde{\beta}\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
. Since
k
contains all
p^{e-\widetilde{e}}
roots, it suffices to show that
p^{\widetilde{e}}v\in Q-\mathcal{G}
for all
(\ell-1)
-faces
\mathcal{G}
with
b_{\mathcal{G}}\not=0
. Indeed, since
\beta\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
we have
p^{e}v\in Q-\mathcal{G}
for all
(\ell-1)
-faces
\mathcal{G}
such that
b_{\mathcal{G}}\not=0
. Since
p>N_{Q}^{(\ell)}
, by Theorem
4.6
we then have
p^{\widetilde{e}}v\in Q-\mathcal{G}
for all such
(\ell-1)
-faces
\mathcal{G}
, as desired. Hence
\widetilde{\beta}\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Now let’s show that
\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=\delta^{\ell-1}(\widetilde{\beta})
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left(\delta^{\ell-1}(\widetilde{\beta})\right)^{p^{e}}

\displaystyle=\left(\delta^{\ell-1}\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}b_{\mathcal{G}}^{p^{\widetilde{e}-e}}x^{p^{\widetilde{e}}v}\right)\right)^{p^{e}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{\ell-1}\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}b_{\mathcal{G}}^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}x^{p^{\widetilde{e}+e}v}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\delta^{\ell-1}\left(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell-1)\\
\text{faces }\mathcal{G}\end{subarray}}b_{\mathcal{G}}x^{p^{e}v}\right)\right)^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha^{p^{e}}\right)^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}\right)^{p^{e}}.

Since Frobenius acts injectively on
\bigoplus_{\ell\text{-faces }\mathcal{F}}k[Q]_{\mathcal{F}}
we have
\alpha^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=\delta^{\ell-1}(\widetilde{\beta})
as desired. Hence
[\alpha]^{p^{\widetilde{e}}}=0
and the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

5
Bounds for Frobenius Test Exponents

One area of interest is the connection between HSL numbers of local cohomology modules and Frobenius test exponents. In this section we see how the results of this paper can be used to find bounds on Frobenius test exponents in some cases.

For a Noetherian ring
R
of characteristic
p>0
and an ideal
I\subset R
, the
Frobenius closure
of
I
is

\displaystyle I^{F}

\displaystyle=\{r\in R\,|\,r^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for some }e\in\mathbb{N}\},

where
I^{[p^{e}]}
denotes the ideal generated by all
\left(p^{e}\right)^{th}
powers of elements in
I
. The
Frobenius test exponent
of
I
, denoted
\mathrm{Fte}\left(I\right)
, is the minimal
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left(I^{F}\right)^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. Since
R
is Noetherian, each ideal
I\subset R
has a finite Frobenius test exponent. However, it may not be the case that there is a single
e\in\mathbb{N}
that bounds
\mathrm{Fte}\left(I\right)
for
all
ideals
I\subset R
. The question of the existence of such an
e
was posed by Katzman-Sharp in
[
KS06
]
, and a counterexample was found by Brenner
[
Bre06
]
for a two-dimensional domain which is standard graded over a field. However, the behavior is slightly nicer if we restrict to the set of parameter ideals of
R
. We thus define the
Frobenius test exponent
of
R
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fte}\left(R\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\mathrm{Fte}\left(\mathfrak{q}\right)\,|\,\mathfrak{q}\subset R\text{ a parameter ideal}\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

(4)

It has been shown that
\mathrm{Fte}\left(R\right)<\infty
for Cohen-Macaulay
[
KS06
]
, generalized Cohen-Macaulay
[
HKSY06
]
, weakly
F
-nilpotent
[
Quy19
]
, and generalized weakly
F
-nilpotent
[
Mad19
]
rings.

In several cases, bounds on the Frobenius test exponent of a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
are given in terms of HSL numbers of the local cohomology of
R
at
\mathfrak{m}
(see
[
KS06
, Thm 2.4]
,
[
Quy19
, Main Thm]
,
[
Mad19
, Thm 3.6]
). Theorem
1.3
together with these known bounds may be used to give an explicit upper bound for
\mathrm{Fte}\left(R\right)
in the case that
R
is any weakly
F
-nilpotent or Cohen-Macaulay affine semigroup ring.

In particular, Katzman-Sharp
[
KS06
, Thm 2.4]
showed that if a local ring
(S,\mathfrak{n})
is Cohen-Macaulay then
\mathrm{Fte}\left(S\right)=\mathrm{HSL}(H^{\dim S}_{\mathfrak{n}}(S))
. Hence Theorem
1.3
has the following corollary:

Corollary 5.1
.

With setup as in Theorem
1.3
, if
k[Q]
is Cohen-Macaulay then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fte}\left(k[Q]\right)\leq\max_{\mathcal{H}}\lceil\log_{p}m_{\mathcal{H}}\rceil,

where the maximum ranges over all faces
\mathcal{H}
of
Q
.

Furthermore, Quy
[
Quy19
, Main Thm]
showed that if a local ring
(S,\mathfrak{n})
of dimension
n
is weakly
F
-nilpotent then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fte}\left(S\right)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\mathrm{HSL}(H^{j}_{\mathfrak{n}}(S)).

Hence Theorem
1.3
has the following corollary as well:

Corollary 5.2
.

With setup as in Theorem
1.3
, if
k[Q]
is weakly

-nilpotent then

1.
Introduction

On a polarized variety
(X,H)
the moduli spaces
M_{X,H}(\mathbf{v})
of
H
-semistable sheaves of numerical type
\mathbf{v}
carry
Brill-Noether loci

B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M_{X,H}(\mathbf{v})
whose members
E
satisfy
h^{0}(X,E)\geq k
. In this paper we develop the foundational properties of Brill-Noether loci on
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Set
r=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
,
\mu=\mu(\mathbf{v})=\frac{\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})}
and
\Delta=\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{2}\mu(\mathbf{v})^{2}-\frac{\text{ch}_{2}(\mathbf{v})}{\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})}
to be the rank, slope, and discriminant of
\mathbf{v}
. The Brill-Noether loci have natural determinantal scheme structures and expected dimensions
\text{expdim}B^{k}(\mathbf{v})=\dim M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})-k(k-\chi(E))
. When
\mu>0
we show that
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty. When
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})=1
, we show each Brill-Noether locus
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
is irreducible and of the expected dimension as a determinantal variety,
\dim B^{k}(\mathbf{v})=\dim M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})-k(k-\chi(\mathbf{v}))
. We prove that when
r,\text{ch}_{1}>0
and
\mu>1/2
is not an integer with
\Delta\gg 0
, then
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is reducible, and describe distinct irreducible components.

When
X=C
is a smooth curve and the rank is 1,
M_{X}(\mathbf{v})=\text{Pic}^{d}(C)
is the space of line bundles of a given degree
d
, and the Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})=W^{k}_{d}(C)
have been studied for over a century
[
ACGH85
]
. Much is known about their geometry when
C
is general: when the expected dimension of
W^{k}_{d}(C)
as a determinantal variety is positive, it is nonempty of that dimension and irreducible. A general
L\in\text{Pic}^{d}(C)
has
h^{0}(L)=\chi(L)=d-g+1
when this is non-negative. In higher rank the general bundle still has
h^{0}(E)=\chi(E)
when this is non-negative, and the Brill-Noether loci have been studied in detail (for a survey see, e.g.,
[
New21
]
and its bibliography).

On algebraic surfaces, however, much less is known about Brill-Noether theory. The basic theory has begun to be worked out on Hirzebruch surfaces (see
[
CMR10
]
) and in rank 2 on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(see
[
RLTLZ21
]
). On
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the moduli spaces
M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})
of semistable bundles of any rank are irreducible, and a well-known theorem of Göttsche-Hirschowitz describes the global sections of a general bundle
E\in M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})
of rank at least two: if the slope
\mu(E)
is positive, then
h^{0}(E)=\max\{0,\chi(E)\}
, which is determined by
\mathbf{v}
. More generally, a general bundle
E
of any slope has at most one nonzero cohomology group, and by semicontinuity there is an open dense subset of
M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})
of bundles with this cohomology. The Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
with
k>\chi(\mathbf{v})
form its complement. These foundational results make the study of the Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
approachable.

1.1.
Geometry of Brill-Noether loci

The moduli spaces
M(\mathbf{v})=M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})
of semistable sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with Chern character
\mathbf{v}
are irreducible projective algebraic varieties. We call a Chern character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
stable if it is the Chern character of a stable sheaf, so that
M(\mathbf{v})\neq\emptyset
. We are interested in the Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M(\mathbf{v})
. As in the case of line bundles on curves, the Brill-Noether loci are constructed as determinantal varieties, so each has an
expected dimension
, which is a lower bound for the dimension of an irreducible component of
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
; it is

\text{expdim}B^{k}(\mathbf{v})=\dim M_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathbf{v})-k(k-\chi(\mathbf{v})).

Our main results are summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be a stable Chern character and
M(\mathbf{v})
the associated moduli space of semistable sheaves. Suppose that
\mu(\mathbf{v})>0
and set
r=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
.

(1)

(Emptiness) For any
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
,

(1)

h^{0}(E)\leq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(E)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}c_{1}(E)+1,r(E)\right\}.

Equivalently, if
k\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(E)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}c_{1}(E)+1,r(E)\right\}
, then
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
is empty.

When
r(E)\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(E)^{2}+\frac{3}{2}c_{1}(E)+1
, this bound is sharp, and
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
contains a component of the expected dimension. See Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.6
.

(2)

(Nonemptiness)
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty. See Theorem
4.7
.

(3)

(Irreducibility) If
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})=1
, all of the nonempty Brill-Noether loci on
M(\mathbf{v})
are irreducible and of the expected dimension. See Theorem
5.1
.

(4)

(Reducibility) Suppose
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})>1
. If
\mu(\mathbf{v})>1/2
is not an integer and
\Delta(\mathbf{v})\gg 0
, then
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is reducible and contains components of both expected and unexpected dimensions. See Theorem
5.8
.

Since the Brill-Noether loci are nested, Theorem
1.1
(
2
) implies the Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
are nonempty for all
0\leq k\leq r
. The bound (
1
) is also sharp for the line bundles
\mathcal{O}(d)
and the Lazarsfeld-Mukai-type bundles
M_{d}=\text{coker}(\mathcal{O}(-d)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(d))})
, including
M_{1}=T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)
.

Our main technical tools for studying Brill-Noether loci are parametrizations of certain moduli spaces
M(\mathbf{v})
and Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
by projective bundles whose fibers are (projectivizations of) extension spaces. The applications to Brill-Noether loci are often of the following form. A sheaf
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has an
evaluation map
on global sections

\text{ev}_{E}:H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\rightarrow E.

Assume that the general
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
has
m
global sections, i.e.,
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)=m
, and
m\leq r(E)
. When
\text{ev}_{E}
has full rank
E
sits as an extension class

[0\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{ev}_{E}}}{{\rightarrow}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}\rightarrow 0]\in\text{Ext}^{1}(E^{\prime},H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}).

If
E^{\prime}
is semistable with Chern character
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, we may form the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}
over
M(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
whose fiber over
E^{\prime}
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}\text{Ext}^{1}(E^{\prime},H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},E^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})
. If the general such extension is stable, we obtain a classifying rational map
\phi:\mathbb{P}\dashrightarrow M(\mathbf{v})
, which we call the
extension parametrization
of
M(\mathbf{v})
.

The critical technical challenge in constructing such parametrizations is in proving the stability of extension sheaves. When the rank of
E^{\prime}
is 0, so
\chi(E)=r(E)
, we accomplish this by studying smooth curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
realized as the support of
E^{\prime}
and the moduli spaces
\text{Pic}^{d}_{\mathcal{C}/U}
of these sheaves (Sections
4
and
5
). The central inputs to our results are the classification of stable Chern characters on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by Drézet-Le Potier (
[
DLP85
, Théorème C]
) and the computation of the cohomology of the general stable bundle in
M(\mathbf{v})
by Göttsche-Hirschowitz (Theorem
2.1
). Our methods likely extend to other surfaces for which these two notions are understood, e.g., Hirzebruch surfaces and K3 surfaces of Picard rank 1 (see
[
CH21
]
and
[
CNY21
]
respectively).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Stability on
\mathbb{P}^{2}

In this section we collect basic facts about stability for coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We refer the reader to the books by Le Potier
[
LP97
]
and Huybrechts-Lehn
[
HL10
]
for further details.

2.1.1.
Numerical invariants & stability

All sheaves in this paper will be coherent, but not necessarily torsion-free. Let
E
be a coherent sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The Hilbert polynomial
P_{E}(m)=\chi(E(m))
is of the form

P_{E}(m)=\alpha_{d}\frac{m^{d}}{d!}+O(m^{d-1}),

and we define the reduced Hilbert polynomial
p_{E}(m)
to be

p_{E}(m)=P_{E}(m)/\alpha_{d}.

A pure-dimensional coherent sheaf
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is (semi)stable if for all nontrivial subsheaves
F\hookrightarrow E
we have
p_{F}<(\leq)p_{E}
, where polynomials are compared for sufficiently large
m
. We will assume throughout this paper that a semistable sheaf has positive rank.

Given a character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, we define, respectively, the slope and the discriminant of
\mathbf{v}
by

\mu(\mathbf{v})=\frac{\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})}{\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})},\quad\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\frac{1}{2}\mu(\mathbf{v})^{2}-\frac{\text{ch}_{2}(\mathbf{v})}{\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})}.

On
\mathbb{P}^{2}
these classes may be considered as rational numbers. When
\mathbf{v}=\text{ch}(E)
for a coherent sheaf
E
we set
\mu(E)=\mu(\mathbf{v})
and
\Delta(E)=\Delta(\mathbf{v})
. We have

\mu(E\otimes F)=\mu(E)+\mu(F),\quad\Delta(E\otimes F)=\Delta(E)+\Delta(F)

for coherent sheaves
E,F
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

In terms of these invariants, the Riemann-Roch theorem takes the form

\chi(E)=r(E)(p(\mu(E))-\Delta(E)),

where

p(x)=p_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+3x+2).

More generally for sheaves
E,F
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we set
\text{ext}^{i}(E,F)=\dim\text{Ext}^{i}(E,F)
. The relative Riemann-Roch theorem states

\chi(E,F)=\sum(-1)^{i}\text{ext}^{i}(E,F)=r(E)r(F)(p(\mu(E)-\mu(F))-\Delta(E)-\Delta(F)),

and similarly for Chern characters.

Additionally, we may define slope-stability for sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
:
E
is slope-(semi)stable if for all subsheaves
F\hookrightarrow E
of smaller rank, we have
\mu(F)<(\leq)\mu(E)
. Slope-stability implies stability, and is often easier to check.

2.1.2.
Classification of stable bundles

The Chern characters of stable bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
have been classified (see, e.g.,
[
DLP85
]
,
[
LP97
]
). The classification is important for our results, and we sketch it in this subsection.

Recall that we say a character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is stable if there is a stable sheaf of Chern character
\mathbf{v}
, and that we assume stable characters have
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})>0
. Stability imposes strong conditions on the numerical data of
\mathbf{v}
. These conditions are determined by exceptional bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. A sheaf
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is
exceptional
if
\text{Ext}^{i}(E,E)=0
for
i>0
.

By a theorem of Drézet
[
Dré86
]
, every exceptional sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a stable vector bundle. A stable bundle
E
is exceptional if and only if
\Delta(E)<1/2
, by Riemann-Roch
[
LP97
, Proposition 16.1.1]
. By definition, exceptional bundles are rigid and their moduli spaces consist of a single reduced point
[
LP97
, Corollary 16.1.5]
. Exceptional bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
include the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(k)
and the tangent bundle
T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
, and all others can be formed from these by a process called mutation
[
Dré86
]
.

The slopes of exceptional bundles can be completely described
[
LP97
, §16.3]
; set
\mathcal{E}
to be the set of slopes of exceptional bundles. For an exceptional bundle
E
of slope
\alpha
we write
E=E_{\alpha}
and
\Delta(E)=\Delta_{\alpha}
. We define the Drézet-Le Potier curve to be the following piecewise-polynomial curve in the
(\mu,\Delta)
-plane:

\delta(\mu)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{E}:|\mu-\alpha|<3}(p(-|\mu-\alpha|)-\Delta_{\alpha}).

A plot of an approximation of the Drézet-Le Potier curve between 0 and 1 is shown in Figure
1
. The precise relationship of exceptional bundles to stable characters is due to Drézet and Le Potier (
[
DLP85
, Théorème C]
,
[
LP97
]
): a character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is stable if and only if
c_{1}:=r\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\chi:=r(P(\mu)-\Delta)\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\Delta(\mathbf{v})\geq\delta(\mu(\mathbf{v}))
, or
\mathbf{v}
is exceptional. A character satisfying the first two conditions in the theorem is called integral. Each exceptional bundle
E_{\alpha}
of slope
\alpha
determines two “branches” of the Drézet-Le Potier curve, on the left and right sides of the vertical line
\mu=\alpha
. The characters
\mathbf{v}
on the branch to the right of
\mu=\alpha
satisfy
\chi(\mathbf{v},E_{\alpha})=0
, and we call this branch the
{}^{\perp}E_{\alpha}
-branch of the curve (or left orthogonal branch), and characters
\mathbf{v}
on the branch to the left of
\mu=\alpha
satisfy
\chi(E_{\alpha},\mathbf{v})=0
and we call this the
E_{\alpha}^{\perp}
-branch (or right orthogonal branch).

Let
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be a character and set
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})=r
,
\mu(\mathbf{v})=\mu
, and
\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\Delta
. When
\mathbf{v}
is stable with
r>0
, Drézet and Le Potier showed the moduli space of stable sheaves with Chern character
\mathbf{v}
is a normal, irreducible, factorial projective variety of dimension

\dim M(\mathbf{v})=r^{2}(2\Delta-1)+1

(
[
LP97
, Theorem 17.0.1]
). When
\mathbf{v}
is a non-exceptional stable character of rank
>1
, the general bundle
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
is slope-stable (
[
DLP85
, Corollaire 4.12]
). Slope-stability allows us to use elementary modifications:

2.2.
Elementary modifications

If
\Delta_{0}
is the discriminant of a stable non-exceptional sheaf
E
, then by integrality of the Euler characteristic one can see that the discriminant of another stable sheaf of slope
\mu(E)
and rank
r(E)
differs from
\Delta_{0}
by an integral multiple of
\frac{1}{r(E)}
. One can in fact obtain any such discriminant larger than or equal to
\delta(\mu(E))
by taking elementary modifications: choosing a point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
and a surjection
E\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{p}
one forms the exact sequence

0\rightarrow E^{\prime}\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{p}\rightarrow 0.

The sheaf
E^{\prime}
is not locally free, but it has
r(E^{\prime})=r(E)
,
\mu(E^{\prime})=\mu(E)
, and
\Delta(E^{\prime})=\Delta(E)+1/r(E)
. One can check that when
E
is slope-stable, so is
E^{\prime}
(but this is not true for stability). In this way one can construct slope-stable bundles of discriminant
\Delta_{0}+k/r(E)
for any non-negative integer
k
. See
[
CH20
, Lemma 2.7]
for details.

2.3.
Brill-Noether loci

We now define the Brill-Noether loci and endow them with a natural determinantal structure, which leads to a lower bound on the dimension of their components.

The expected value of
h^{0}(E)
when
E
is stable is provided by the following well-known theorem due to Göttsche-Hirschowitz.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
GH98
]
)
.

When
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\geq 2
, the general sheaf
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
has at most one nonzero cohomology group:

(1)

if
\chi(E)\geq 0
and
\mu(E)>-3
, then
h^{1}(E)=h^{2}(E)=0
;

(2)

if
\chi(E)\geq 0
and
\mu(E)<-3
, then
h^{0}(E)=h^{1}(E)=0
;

(3)

if
\chi(E)<0
,
h^{0}(E)=h^{2}(E)=0
.

When a sheaf
F\in M(\mathbf{v})
has cohomology groups as prescribed in Theorem
2.1
, we will say that it is cohomologically general or has general cohomology. By semicontinuity, when
r\geq 2
the locus of sheaves with general cohomology forms an open subset of the moduli space; the Brill-Noether loci
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
with
k>\chi(\mathbf{v})
make up the complement of this open set. Let
M^{s}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M(\mathbf{v})
denote the locus of strictly stable sheaves.

Definition 2.2
.

For a stable Chern character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, the
k
th Brill-Noether locus

B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M(\mathbf{v})
is defined as a set to be the closure

B^{k}(\mathbf{v})=\overline{\{E\in M^{s}(\mathbf{v}):h^{0}(E)\geq k\}}\subseteq M(\mathbf{v}).

B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
is the locus where
h^{0}
jumps by at least
k-\chi(\mathbf{v})
.

The Brill-Noether loci are clearly nested:
B_{k}(\mathbf{v})\supseteq B_{k+1}(\mathbf{v})
for all
k
.

Remark 2.3
.

We restrict to stable sheaves in the definition because the number of global sections is not constant in
S
-equivalence classes, so
h^{0}([E])
is not defined for strictly semistable points
[E]\in M(\mathbf{v})
.

Note that for a stable sheaf
F
with
\mu(F)<0
, any map
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow F
corresponding to
0\neq s\in H^{0}(F)
would be destabilizing, so
H^{0}(F)=0
and there are no Brill-Noether loci in the associated moduli space. Thus in what follows we assume the slope is nonnegative. In these cases, we expect
h^{0}(F)=\max\{0,\chi(F)\}
. When
\mu<-3
, we have
h^{0}(E)=0
but
h^{2}(E)=h^{0}(E^{\vee}(-3))
by Serre-duality, where
\mu(E^{\vee}(-3))>0
. Thus
h^{2}
-jumping loci are Serre-dual to
h^{0}
-jumping loci when the slope is sufficiently negative. When
-3<\mu(E)<0
, Serre duality implies that
h^{0}(E)=h^{2}(E)=0
, so there are no Brill-Noether loci for any cohomology.

We endow
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
with a determinantal scheme structure, as follows (
cf.

[
CHW17
, Proposition 2.6]
and
[
CMR10
, §2]
). Let
\mathscr{E}/S
be a proper flat family of stable sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of Chern character
\mathbf{v}
. We will examine the relative Brill-Noether locus
B^{k}_{S}(\mathbf{v})
; when
M^{s}(\mathbf{v})
admits a universal family
\mathscr{U}
, this will give the appropriate scheme structure on
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
. In general, one can work on the stack
\mathcal{M}^{s}(\mathbf{v})
and take the image in the coarse moduli space
M^{s}(\mathbf{v})
(see
[
Alp13
, Theorem 4.16 and Example 8.7]
).

Choose
s\in S
and let
d\gg 0
be sufficiently large and let
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a general-enough curve of degree
d
, chosen so that the singularities of
\mathscr{E}_{s}
do not meet
C
. After replacing
S
by an open subset, we can assume
C
does not meet the singularities of any member of
\mathscr{E}
. Let
p:S\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\rightarrow S
and
q:S\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the projections. Form the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\otimes q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}\otimes(q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d)|_{C})\rightarrow 0

of sheaves on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Choosing
d
large enough so that each of the cohomology groups
H^{1}(\mathscr{E}_{s}\otimes q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d))
vanish, we conclude
R^{1}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d))=0
and we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow p_{*}\mathscr{E}\rightarrow p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\rightarrow}}p_{*}(\mathscr{E}\otimes(q^{*}\mathcal{O}(d)|_{C}))\rightarrow R^{1}p_{*}(\mathscr{E})\rightarrow 0

of sheaves on
S
. When
d
is sufficiently large the map
\phi
is a map between bundles of the same rank, and the
k
th determinantal variety associated to
\phi
is supported on those
s\in S
such that
h^{0}(\mathscr{E}_{s})\geq k
, or equivalently
h^{1}(\mathscr{E}_{s})\geq k-\chi(\mathbf{v})
. Thus scheme-theoretically we define
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
to be this determinantal variety, and observe that its support agrees with the set described above.

The construction above does not depend on the choice of
d
or
C
; precisely, over an open subset
U
of
S
over which the source and target of
\phi
are trivial, the Fitting ideal
\text{Fitt}_{0}(R^{1}p_{*}\mathcal{E})
is generated by
\det\phi
, viewed as a section of
\mathcal{O}_{U}
. See
[
Eis13
, Chapter 20]
. The Fitting ideal is the ideal sheaf of
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
, and gives the desired scheme structure.

Being a determinantal variety, the codimension of a component
Z
of
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
is bounded:

\text{codim}_{M(\mathbf{v})}(Z)\leq k(k-\chi(\mathbf{v})).

Thus since, for a general bundle
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
, we have

\dim(M(\mathbf{v}))=\text{ext}^{1}(E,E)=r(\mathbf{v})^{2}(2\Delta(\mathbf{v})-1)+1

the dimension of a component
Z
of
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})
is bounded below:

\dim(Z)\geq r(\mathbf{v})^{2}(2\Delta(\mathbf{v})-1)+1-k(k-\chi(\mathbf{v})).

Definition 2.4
.

The
expected codimension
of the
k
th Brill-Noether locus
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M(\mathbf{v})
is

\text{expcodim}^{k}(\mathbf{v})=k(k-\chi(\mathbf{v})).

3.
Extension parametrizations of moduli spaces

In this section we study the stability of extension sheaves. When extension sheaves are semistable, we obtain families of sheaves over loci in the associated moduli space.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
are stable characters with
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
. Let
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}/S^{\prime\prime}
be families of semistable sheaves of Chern characters
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
, respectively. If the general extension
E

0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow 0

with
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\prime\prime}
is semi-stable, then an induced rational map
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\mathbf{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}\times S^{\prime\prime}}

defined on a locus where
\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
has constant rank, is called an extension parametrization associated to
M(\mathbf{v})
.

Stability of these extensions is the central technical challenge of this paper. However, there is a class called the
extremal
extensions whose stability is not hard to show, which we now recall.

3.1.
Extremal extensions

Stability of extension sheaves is known when the slope of the subsheaf is
extremal
with respect to the extension sheaf. The following definition is derived from
[
CH16
, Definition 4.1]
, but we make some slight modifications for our purposes.

Definition 3.2
.

A Chern character
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is called
extremal
for
\mathbf{v}
if
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
satisfies:

(D1)

\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})<\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
;

(D2)

\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq\mu(\mathbf{v})
;

(D3)

\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
is stable;

and furthermore, for any
\mathbf{w}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
satisfying (
D1
)-(
D3
):

(E1)

\mu(\mathbf{w})\leq\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
;

(E2)

If
\mu(\mathbf{w})=\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
, then
\Delta(\mathbf{w})\geq\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
;

(E3)

If
\mu(\mathbf{w})=\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
and
\Delta(\mathbf{w})=\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
, then
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{w})
.

A triple
\Xi=(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
of Chern characters on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is called
extremal
if
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{v}
, where
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
is the extremal character for
\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
is stable.

It is not obvious
a priori
that extremal decompositions exist for any
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
since
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
may not be stable, but at least it is clear when
\mathbf{v}
has large discriminant, see e.g.
[
CH16
, Lemma 4.3]
, or simply observe that
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})\gg 0
when
\Delta(\mathbf{v})\gg 0
.

Our main use of Definition
3.2
will be to construct extension parametrizations associated to moduli spaces. The following simple observation is crucial.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
\Xi=(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
is an extremal triple, and we have a nonsplit short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E^{\prime}\rightarrow E\rightarrow E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0

with
E^{\prime}\in M(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
and
E^{\prime\prime}\in M(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
. Then
E
is semistable.

Proof.

Suppose that
E\rightarrow Q
is a destabilizing quotient of
E
, so that
\mu(Q)\leq\mu(E)
with
r(Q)<r(E)
. After perhaps passing to a further quotient of
Q
, we may assume
Q
is stable. Slope-closeness (
E1
) implies that
\mu(Q)\leq\mu(E^{\prime})
. If
\mu(Q)<\mu(E^{\prime})
, the composition
E^{\prime}\rightarrow E\rightarrow Q
is zero by stability. So there is an induced map
E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Q
. However
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})\geq\mu(\mathbf{v})>\mu(Q)
, hence the induced map is zero. This implies that
E\rightarrow Q
vanishes.

We now have
\mu(Q)=\mu(E^{\prime})
. Discriminant-minimality (
E2
) implies that
\Delta(Q)\geq\Delta(E^{\prime})
. If
\Delta(Q)>\Delta(E^{\prime})
, we get a contradiction as in the preceding paragraph.

Hence
\mu(Q)=\mu(E^{\prime})
and
\Delta(Q)=\Delta(E^{\prime})
. By (
E3
),
E^{\prime},Q
are stable. Since
E
is a non-split extension,
E^{\prime}\rightarrow Q
is zero, inducing a map
E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow Q
. Now by (
D1
), (
D2
) this map can be non-zero only if
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})=\mu(\mathbf{v})=\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
and
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})=\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
. Hence
E
is semi-stable.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be a stable character which admits an extremal decomposition
(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
, and let
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}/S^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}/S^{\prime\prime}
be families of semistable sheaves of characters
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
respectively. Then the induced extension parametrization
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(\mathbf{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}\times S^{\prime\prime}}

defined over the locus where
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
has minimal rank, exists, where
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, for any
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}\in S^{\prime\prime}
, a nonsplit extension

0\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow 0

is semistable of Chern character
\mathbf{v}
. So on any locus in
S^{\prime}\times S^{\prime\prime}
where
\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
is of constant rank, there is an induced map
\mathbb{P}\dashrightarrow M(\mathbf{v})
, where
\mathbb{P}
is the projective bundle over
S^{\prime}\times S^{\prime\prime}
with fiber
\mathbb{P}\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}_{s^{\prime\prime}},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
over
(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})\in S^{\prime}\times S^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

When the subsheaf is not extremal for the extension sheaf, in general additional conditions are required to prove generic stability, see for instance Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
5.9
.

4.
Emptiness & non-emptiness of Brill-Noether loci

For a stable Chern character
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
there are only finitely many values of
k\geq 0
such that
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\subseteq M(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty. That is, there are only finitely many values of
h^{0}(E)
for a stable sheaf
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
. We first give a bound on this quantity. Later on in this section we will analyze which such values are achieved.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
E
is a slope-semistable sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with Chern character
\mathbf{v}
such that
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})\geq 0
, then

h^{0}(E)\leq\max\{p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})),\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\},

where
p(x)=p_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}(x)=\frac{x^{2}+3x+2}{2}
.

In the proof we will consider partial evaluation maps
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
for a subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
. We fix
E
and
V
for the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F\subseteq E
be the image sheaf of the evaluation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
, and let
0=F_{0}\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{m}=F
be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
F
, with graded pieces
\text{gr}_{i}=F_{i}/F_{i-1}
.

If
r(F)<r(E)
, then
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})<c_{1}(E)
for each
i
.

Proof.

If not, then since
r(\text{gr}_{i})\leq r(F)<r(E)
,

\mu(E)=\frac{c_{1}(E)}{r(E)}\leq\frac{c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}<\frac{c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(\text{gr}_{i})}=\mu(\text{gr}_{i}).

(Note that we need
c_{1}(E)\geq 0
for
V
to be nonempty.) The inclusions
\text{gr}_{1}=F_{1}\hookrightarrow F\hookrightarrow E
and semistability of
E
imply

\mu(\text{gr}_{1})\leq\mu(E)<\mu(\text{gr}_{i})

contradicting that the slopes of the
\text{gr}_{i}
are non-increasing. We conclude that
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})<c_{1}(E)
for each
i
.
∎

We observe for use in the cases below that the function
p(x)/x
is increasing for integers
x\geq 2
, with
p(1)=p(2)/2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

First, since we have
E\subseteq E^{\vee\vee}
, we have
h^{0}(E)\leq h^{0}(E^{\vee\vee})
. Because
E^{\vee\vee}
is slope-semistable with
r(E^{\vee\vee})=r(E)
and
c_{1}(E^{\vee\vee})=c_{1}(E)
, it suffices to prove the theorem when
E
is locally free.

Case I.
In this case we assume
r(E)=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\geq p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}))
, and proceed by induction on
r(E)
. In this case when
r(E)=1
we have
c_{1}(E)\leq 0
, and when
c_{1}(E)<0
slope-semistability implies
h^{0}(E)=0
, as desired. When
c_{1}(E)=0
, it follows that
E
is the ideal sheaf of a finite length subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Thus
h^{0}(E)\leq 1
, and equality holds if and only if
Z
is empty.

We first show that any
r(E)
-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
spans a full rank subsheaf of
E
. That is, the evaluation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
is full rank. Suppose not, so that
r(F)<r(E)
. To show this we first claim that each
\text{gr}_{i}
has
r(\text{gr}_{i})\geq p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))
, so by induction on the rank we have
h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq r(\text{gr}_{i})
. It follows that

h^{0}(F)\leq\sum h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq\sum r(\text{gr}_{i})=r(F)<r(E).

Since by definition
h^{0}(F)\geq r(E)
, we conclude from this contradiction that any partial evaluation map of any
r(E)
independent sections in this case is of full rank.

To check the claim, assume otherwise that
r(\text{gr}_{i})<p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))
. It is enough to produce a contradiction for those
\text{gr}_{i}
with
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})>0
, since when
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq 0
we have
h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq r(\text{gr}_{i})
. Then

\mu(E)=\frac{c_{1}(E)}{r(E)}\leq\frac{c_{1}(E)}{p(c_{1}(E))}\leq\frac{c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})}{p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))}<\frac{c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(\text{gr}_{i})}=\mu(\text{gr}_{i}),

where we have used Lemma
4.2
and that
x/p(x)
is non-increasing when
x\geq 1
. The slopes
\mu(\text{gr}_{i})
in the Harder-Narasimhan filtration are decreasing and
E
is semistable, so we have

\mu(\text{gr}_{i})\leq\mu(\text{gr}_{1})\leq\mu(E)

so this contradiction proves the claim. We conclude that any
r(E)
sections span a full rank subsheaf of
E
.

Therefore an
r(E)
-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
determines a nonzero section of
\mathcal{O}(c_{1}(E))
via the natural map

\wedge^{r(E)}H^{0}(E)\rightarrow H^{0}(\wedge^{r(E)}E)=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(c_{1}(E))),

where we have
\wedge^{r}E=\mathcal{O}(c_{1}(E))
because
E
is locally free. In this way we obtain a morphism

f:G(r(E),H^{0}(E))\rightarrow\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(c_{1}(E))).

When
h^{0}(E)\geq r(E)+1
, it is easy to see that
f
is nonconsant. Indeed, any
r(E)
-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
sits in a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0

because the evaluation map is of full rank. Choose
s\in H^{0}(E)\smallsetminus V
, and consider a general point
p\in\text{Supp}(Q)
. Then
0\neq s_{p}\in H^{0}(Q|_{p})
does not lie in
V|_{p}\subseteq E|_{p}
. Choosing a generating set
s_{1},...,s_{r(E)}
for the span
\langle s_{p},V|_{p}\rangle\subseteq E_{p}
; then the
s_{i}
span a rank
r(E)
subspace
V^{\prime}\subseteq H^{0}(E)
such that the induced section
\wedge^{r(E)}V^{\prime}\in H^{0}(\wedge^{r(E)}E)
is independent from
\wedge^{r(E)}V
. For instance, the multiplicity of
\wedge^{r(E)}V^{\prime}
at
p
is lower than that of
\wedge^{r(E)}V
.

However if
h^{0}(E)\geq r(E)+1
, we have

\dim G(r(E),H^{0}(E))\geq r(E)\geq p(c_{1}(E))

since
E
has
r(E)\geq p(c_{1}(E))
, and
\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(c_{1}(E)))=p(c_{1}(E))-1
. Since the Picard rank of a Grassmannian is 1, there is no non-constant map from a Grassmannian to a lower-dimensional projective variety. We conclude that
h^{0}(E)\leq r(E)
, as desired.

Case II.
We are now in the situation that
r(E)<p(c_{1}(E))=\frac{c_{1}(E)^{2}+3c_{1}(E)+2}{2}
, and we want to prove
h^{0}(E)\leq p(c_{1}(E))
. We will use induction on
c_{1}(E)
. If
c_{1}(E)=0
, then
p(c_{1}(E))=1
and there is nothing to prove.

We first show that any
p(c_{1}(E))
-dimensional subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
spans a full rank subsheaf of
E
. We proceed by contradiction, so we let
F
be the image of the evaluation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
and assume
r(F)<r(E)
. Since
p(x)/x
is non-decreasing, when
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})\geq 1
we have

p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))=c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})\frac{p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))}{c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})}\leq c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})\frac{p(c_{1}(E))}{c_{1}(E)}\leq p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}

by Lemma
4.2
. The
\text{gr}_{i}
may fall into either Case I or Case II. When
\text{gr}_{i}
is in Case II, we obtain

h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))\leq p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}

by induction on
c_{1}
. When
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})<1
, we have

h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq r(\text{gr}_{i})=r(E)\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}\leq p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}

by the assumption that
E
is in Case II. For those
\text{gr}_{i}
in Case I with
r(\text{gr}_{i})\geq p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))
, we also have

h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq r(\text{gr}_{i})=r(E)\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}<p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}.

We now have

(2)

h^{0}(F)\leq\sum h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})\leq\sum p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}=p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(F)}{r(E)}<p(c_{1}(E)).

By definition we have
h^{0}(F)\geq p(c_{1}(E))
, and this contradiction proves that any
p(c_{1}(E))
-dimensional subspace
V
spans a full rank subsheaf of
E
.

We can additionally assume that the dependency locus of
V
is zero-dimensional. If not, consider the image
F\subseteq E
of the evaluation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
and its Harder-Narasimhan filtration. Then
c_{1}(F)<c_{1}(E)
. By (
2
), we know that
h^{0}(F)\leq p(c_{1}(E))
(only the final inequality in (
2
) is no longer strict), and equality holds only if for every
i
,
h^{0}(\text{gr}_{i})=p(c_{1}(E))\frac{r(\text{gr}_{i})}{r(E)}
for every graded piece. This is true only if
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})>0
;
r(\text{gr}_{i})<p(c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i}))
;
c_{1}(\text{gr}_{i})=1
; and
c_{1}(E)=2
. In this case,
c_{1}(F)<c_{1}(E)=2
,
c_{1}(F)\leq 1
. If
F
is unstable, then there is at least one Harder-Narasimhan factor
\text{gr}_{i}
whose degree is non-positive, which is not the case. If
F
is stable, it satisfies
r(F)<p(c_{1}(F))
, then by induction,
h^{0}(F)\leq p(c_{1}(F))<p(c_{1}(E))
. In any case, we have
h^{0}(F)<p(c_{1}(E))
, a contradiction.

We now return to the bound on
h^{0}(E)
. We first prove
h^{0}(E)\leq p(c_{1}(E))
when
0\leq\mu(E)<1
. We have just shown that any
p(c_{1}(E))
sections of
E
span a full rank subsheaf, and their dependency locus is zero-dimensional. Let
L\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a general line. Consider

0\rightarrow E(-1)\rightarrow E\rightarrow E|_{L}\rightarrow 0

and write
E|_{L}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r(E)}\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i})
. Since
\mu(E)<1
, there is at least one
d_{i}\leq 0
, say
d_{1}
. Taking
H^{0}
we obtain
\pi:H^{0}(E)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r(E)}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i}))
. Let
W\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r(E)}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i}))
be the image of
\pi
. If
W\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r(E)}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i}))
, then sections of
E
are dependent along
L
, hence
h^{0}(E)<p(c_{1})
. If
W\not\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r(E)}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i}))
, then
d_{1}=0
, and since
\pi^{-1}(W\cap\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r(E)}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i})))
has codimension 1, there are
h^{0}(E)-1
sections dependent along
L
, hence
h^{0}(E)-1<p(c_{1})
. That is,
h^{0}(E)\leq p(c_{1}(E))
, as desired.

Now if
n\leq\mu<n+1
, we induct on
n
. We have just shown the statement is true for
n=0
; now assume
n\geq 1
. Again we use that any
p(c_{1}(E))
sections span a full rank subsheaf, and the dependency locus
is supported in dimension zero. Consider again the restriction to a line:

0\rightarrow E(-1)\rightarrow E\rightarrow E|_{L}\rightarrow 0.

Taking
H^{0}
we get
h^{0}(E)\leq h^{0}(E(-1))+h^{0}(E|_{L})
. By induction,
h^{0}(E(-1))\leq p(c_{1}(E)-r(E))
. Consider
E|_{L}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r(E)}\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i})
. If some
d_{i}<0
, say
d_{1}
, then the span of sections in
W
is contained in
\bigoplus_{i\geq 2}\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i})
, and it follows that
h^{0}(E)<p(c_{1}(E))
. Hence we assume
d_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Then
h^{0}(E|_{L})=\sum_{i=1}^{r(E)}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}(d_{i}+1))=c_{1}(E)+r(E)
. Now since
n\geq 1
,
c_{1}(E)\geq r(E)\geq 1
and we obtain

\displaystyle r(E)(2c_{1}(E)-r(E)+1)-2c_{1}(E)

\displaystyle=(r(E)-1)2c_{1}(E)-r(E)^{2}+r(E)

\displaystyle\geq(r(E)-1)2r(E)-r(E)^{2}+r(E)

\displaystyle=r(E)(r(E)-1)\geq 0

hence
r(E)(2c_{1}(E)-r(E)+1)\geq 2c_{1}(E)
, and

\displaystyle p(c_{1}(E))-p(c_{1}(E)-r(E))

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{1}(E)r(E)-r(E)^{2}+3r(E)}{2}

\displaystyle=r(E)+\frac{r(E)(2c_{1}(E)-r(E)+1)}{2}

\displaystyle\geq r(E)+c_{1}(E).

In particular we have

h^{0}(E)\leq h^{0}(E(-1))+h^{0}(E|_{L})\leq p(c_{1}(E)-r(E))+(r(E)+c_{1}(E))\leq p(c_{1}(E)).

This finishes the proof.
∎

We will later need the following corollary, which is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
E
is a semistable sheaf on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
c_{1}(E)=1
and
r(E)\geq 3
, then
h^{0}(E)\leq r(E)
.

Remark 4.4
.

We can describe the behavior of
E
with
c_{1}(E)=1
also when the rank is small. If the rank is 1, then
E
is a twist of an ideal sheaf
E\simeq I_{Z}(1)
. Then
h^{0}(E)\neq 0
if and only if
Z
is colinear, in which case

•

h^{0}(E)=1
if
\ell(Z)\geq 2
,

•

h^{0}(E)=2
if
\ell(Z)=1
, and

•

h^{0}(E)=3
if
\ell(Z)=0
.

If the rank is 2, suppose
h^{0}(E)\geq 3
and consider the partial evaluation map
V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow E
for
V\subseteq H^{0}(E)
a rank 3 subspace. Let
F
be the image of
V\otimes\mathcal{O}
in
E
. If the rank of
F
is one, then it falls into one of the above cases. Since
r(V)=3
, we have
h^{0}(F)\geq 3
, so as above
F=\mathcal{O}
, but in this case the kernel
K=\ker(V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow F)
has
h^{0}(K)\neq 0
, which contradicts the definition of
F
. It follows that
r(F)=2
, and in fact is semistable of slope
1/2
.

This produces a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow V\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow F\rightarrow 0

with
r(K)=1
,
c_{1}(K)=-1
. It follows that
K\simeq I_{Z}(-1)
for some codimension two subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In particular,
\text{ch}_{2}(K)=\frac{1}{2}-\ell(Z)
. Thus

\Delta(F)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\ell(Z)-1/2}{2}=\frac{3}{8}-\frac{\ell(Z)}{2}.

Semistability additionally implies that
\Delta(F)=\frac{3}{8}+\frac{k}{2}
for some
k\geq 0
(see Section
2.2
), from which we find
k=-\ell(Z)\leq 0
, so
k=0
and
F\simeq E=T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)
. In all other cases, we again have
h^{0}(E)\leq r(E)
.

4.1.
The Brill-Noether loci
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})

In this section we show that
B^{k}(\mathbf{v})\neq\emptyset
whenever
k\leq\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
. We can say a great deal about the loci of sheaves
E
with
h^{0}(E)\geq r=r(E)
, i.e., the Brill-Noether loci
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
. We will need the following statements to study the Brill-Noether loci when
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\geq p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}))
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be a stable Chern character and write
r=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
and
c_{1}=\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})
. Assume that
r\geq p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}))
.

(1)

For every
E\in B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
, evaluation map on global sections
\text{ev}_{E}
sits in an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E\rightarrow Q_{E}\rightarrow 0,

where
Q_{E}
is a pure sheaf supported on a 1-dimensional subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

(2)

Let
E\in B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
be a general sheaf. If
Q_{E}
is supported on a smooth curve
C
of degree
c_{1}(E)
, then
Q_{E}
is the pushforward of a line bundle on
C
. Writing
Q_{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
, we have

\deg_{C}D\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}+3c_{1}}{2}-r\leq-1.

(3)

Conversely, let
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
be the pushforward of a line bundle on a smooth curve
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
, where
\deg_{C}D\leq\frac{1}{2}(\deg(C)^{2}+3\deg(C))-r\leq-1
. Then a general extension sheaf

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\rightarrow 0

is stable.

Proof.

(
1
) First, the proof of Theorem
4.1
, Case I, implies that the evaluation map has full rank. It follows that
Q_{E}
is a torsion sheaf. Consider a negative twist

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(-m)^{r}\rightarrow E(-m)\rightarrow Q_{E}(-m)\rightarrow 0

of the above exact sequence, where
m\gg 0
. If
Q_{E}
is has torsion
T\subseteq Q_{E}
supported in codimension two, then
T(-m)\simeq T
, so in particular
T(-m)
and
Q_{E}(-m)
have global sections. The associated cohomology exact sequence

H^{0}(E(-m))\rightarrow H^{0}(Q_{E}(-m))\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}(-m)^{r})

has outer terms which vanish and an inner term which does not. This contradiction proves that
Q_{E}
is supported in pure codimension one.

(
2
) There is a filtration
0=F_{k}Q_{E}\subseteq F_{k-1}Q_{E}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{1}Q_{E}\subseteq Q_{E}
, where each graded piece is the pushforward of a sheaf on
C

[
Dré08
]
. If
F_{i}Q_{E}\neq 0
then its support is
C
, otherwise it would be a torsion subsheaf of
Q_{E}
supported on zero dimensional scheme, contradicting the purity of
Q_{E}
from (
1
). Now if
F_{1}Q_{E}\neq 0
, then
c_{1}(F_{1}Q_{E})\geq c_{1}
, but
Q_{E}/F_{1}Q_{E}
has support
C
, whose first Chern class is
\geq c_{1}
, hence
c_{1}(Q_{E})\geq 2c_{1}
, a contradiction.

For the degree, note that since
r\geq p(c_{1})
, we have

\mu(E)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{p(c_{1})}\leq 1/3

since
x/p(x)\leq 1/3
for integers
x
. Thus
E
lies directly above the
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}
-branch of the Drézet-Le Potier curve, which gives
\chi(E,\mathcal{O})\leq 0
. Thus

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})=\chi(E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\leq-r.

On the other hand,

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C},\mathcal{O}_{C}(D-3H))=\deg_{C}(D-3H)+1-g(C)

so

\deg_{C}D-3c_{1}+1-\frac{c_{1}^{2}-3c_{1}+2}{2}\leq-r

i.e.,
\deg_{C}D\leq\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}^{2}+3c_{1})-r
. The last inequality follows from the assumption
r\geq p(c_{1})
.

(
3
) Let
F
be a possible destabilizing semistable quotient sheaf of
E
. Since
F
is torsion-free, there is no nonzero map
from
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\rightarrow F
, hence the composition
\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F
is nonzero. Since
\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E
is of full rank, the composition
\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E\rightarrow F
is of full rank as well. The sheaves
\mathcal{O}^{r}
and
F
are both semistable, which gives
\mu(F)\geq 0
. On the other hand, since
F
has smaller rank than
E
,
c_{1}(F)<c_{1}(E)
, hence the assumption on
\deg(D)
implies
F
also has
r(F)\geq p(c_{1}(F))
. Therefore
h^{0}(F)\leq r(F)
, hence
\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow F
gives only
r(F)
sections, i.e., it factors through
\mathcal{O}^{r(F)}\rightarrow F
. By part (
1
), we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{r(F)}\rightarrow F\rightarrow Q_{F}\rightarrow 0

where
Q_{F}
is supported on a curve
C^{\prime}
, and
\deg C^{\prime}\leq c_{1}(F)<c_{1}=\deg C
. Now we have a diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}^{r(F)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where the vertical maps are surjective. The map
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\rightarrow Q_{F}
induces an inclusion
\text{Supp }Q_{F}\subseteq C
.
\text{Supp }Q_{F}
has strictly smaller degree than
C
. Since
C
is irreducible, this is impossible. We conclude
Q_{F}=0
, so
F
is a slope 0 semistable sheaf with
r(F)
sections. Hence
F=\mathcal{O}^{r(F)}
, and the exact sequence defining
E
is partially split.

We count dimensions to show such extensions form a closed locus in the associated extension space. If
\deg D\leq\frac{1}{2}(\deg(C)^{2}+3\deg(C))-r
, then
\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\geq r
. If
E=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{q}\oplus E^{\prime}
where
E^{\prime}\in\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r-q})
, then the dimension of the locus of such

is no larger than

\dim G(q,r)+\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}^{r-q})=q(r-q)+(r-q)e,

and
\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r})=re
. Now
q(r-q)+(r-q)e-re=(r-e-q)q
, so we see that when

and

,

\dim G(q,r)+\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r-q})<\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r}).

The lemma follows.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is stable and
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\geq p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}))
,
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})>0
. Then
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty and contains a component of the expected dimension.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
(
1
), we get a morphism
\psi:B^{r}(\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})))=\mathbb{P}V
, by sending
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
with
r
global sections
s_{1},...,s_{r}\in H^{0}(E)
to
s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{r}\in\mathbb{P}V
, and by Lemma
4.5
(
3
),
\psi
is surjective. Denoting by
U=\mathbb{P}V\smallsetminus\Gamma
the locus of smooth curves, we see that
\psi
factors through the relative Picard scheme
\text{Pic}^{d}_{\mathcal{C}/U}
over the universal curve
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow U
, where
d=\deg(D)
. The induced map
\phi:B^{r}(\mathbf{v})\rightarrow\text{Pic}^{d}_{\mathcal{C}/U}
is surjective, and since
E
uniquely determines
Q_{E}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
its fibers are quotients of open subsets of the extension spaces
\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{r})
. It follows in particular that
\psi^{-1}(U)\subseteq B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is irreducible and open, so its closure forms a nonempty irreducible component.

We now compute its dimension. Write
r=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
,
c_{1}=\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})
,
\mu=c_{1}/r
, and
\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\Delta_{0}+k/r
where
\Delta_{0}
is the minimal discriminant of a stable sheaf with
\mu=\mu(\mathbf{v})
and
r=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
. Note in particular that
\mathbf{v}
lies directly above the
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}
-branch of the Drézet-Le Potier curve, which is given by

\Delta=p(-\mu)=\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\mu+1.

In particular, the characters
(\mu,\delta(\mu))
are integral, so
\delta(\mu)=\Delta_{0}
for these slopes. Then
M(\mathbf{v})
is irreducible of dimension

\dim M(\mathbf{v})=r^{2}(2(\Delta_{0}+k/r)-1)+1=1+c_{1}^{2}-3c_{1}r+r^{2}+2kr.

The expected codimension of the Brill-Noether locus
B^{m}(\mathbf{v})
is
m(m-\chi(\mathbf{v}))
. Now

\chi(\mathbf{v})=r\left(p\left(\mu\right)-\Delta_{0}-k/r\right)=3c_{1}-k,

so the expected codimension of
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is
r(r-3c_{1}+k)
, and its expected dimension is

\text{expdim}B^{r}(\mathbf{v})=(1+c_{1}^{2}-3c_{1}r+r^{2}+2kr)-r(r-3c_{1}+k)=1+c_{1}^{2}+kr.

The dimension of the locus of interest is equal to
\dim\phi^{-1}\text{Pic}^{d}(\mathcal{C}/U)
. The general
E\in\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}^{r})
admits precisely one map
\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E
, and automorphisms of
\mathcal{O}^{r}
determine distinct extension classes but isomorphic extension sheaves. Thus

\dim\phi^{-1}\text{Pic}^{d}_{\mathcal{C}/U}=\dim U+g(C)+\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}^{r})-r^{2}+1.

We have
\text{ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O})=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D-3H))=0
,
and clearly
\hom(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O})=0
, so
\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O})=-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O})
.
From the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{r}\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\rightarrow 0

we obtain

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O})=\chi(E,\mathcal{O})-\chi(\mathcal{O}^{r},\mathcal{O})=-k-r.

Hence

\dim\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))=r(k+r)-r^{2}=kr.

As
\dim U=\dim\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(c_{1}))=\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}^{2}+3c_{1})
, we have

\dim\text{Pic}^{d}_{\mathcal{C}/U}=\dim U+g(C)=(c_{1}^{2}+3c_{1})/2+(c_{1}^{2}-3c_{1}+2)/2=c_{1}^{2}+1,

hence

\dim\psi^{-1}(U)=c_{1}^{2}+1+kr,

which equals the expected dimension.
∎

When we have instead
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})<p(\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}))
, we prove a weaker statement: that the Brill-Noether locus
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty.

Note that the condition
\chi(\mathbf{v})=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
can be expressed via Riemann-Roch as the polynomial condition

\Delta(\mathbf{v})=p(\mu(\mathbf{v}))-1=\frac{1}{2}\mu(\mathbf{v})^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\mu(\mathbf{v}).

This defines a parabola
\xi_{r}
in the
(\mu,\Delta)
-plane. (
\xi_{r}
does not depend on the rank.)

In the region of the
(\mu,\Delta)
-plane with
\mu>0
, the parabola
\xi_{r}
meets the Drézet-Le Potier curve only at the
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}
branch, at the point
(\mu,\Delta)=(1/3,5/9)
. The Chern characters
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
lying on
\xi_{r}
satisfy
\chi(\mathbf{v})=\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
, and a general sheaf
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
has
h^{0}(E)=r(E)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be a stable Chern character with
\mu(\mathbf{v})>0
. The Brill-Noether locus
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})
is nonempty.

Proof.

When
r=1
,
c_{1}\geq 1
, we may take
I_{Z}(c_{1})
where
Z
lies on a curve of degree
c_{1}
. When
c_{1}=1
, by Theorem
4.6
the theorem is true for
r\geq 3
. Hence in the following we assume
r>1
,
c_{1}>1
or
c_{1}=1,r=2
. We separate into two cases.

Case I.
In the first case we assume
0<\mu(\mathbf{v})\leq 1/3
; in particular,
\mathbf{v}
lies above the
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}
-branch of the Drézet-Le Potier curve. We induct on the rank for a stronger statement: there exists
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
such that
h^{0}(E)=\text{ch}_{0}(E)
. When the rank is 1 or 2 the statement is vacuous. When
r=3
,
c_{1}=1
, by Theorem
4.6
the theorem is true.

Let
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
be the associated extremal character, and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
the quotient character. Then
0\leq\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq\mu(\mathbf{v})\leq 1/3
. We have
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=0
if and only if
c_{1}=1
, where the only possiblity for the theorem to be false is
r=2
, which is not considered in this case, hence
0<\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq 1/3
.

We also have
0<\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})\leq 1/3
. To see this, first consider the case where
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=\mu(\mathbf{v})
, which occurs if and only if
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
and
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})
are not coprime. In this case,
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})=\mu(\mathbf{v})\leq 1/3
. Otherwise,
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})
and
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})
are coprime. If
\mu(\mathbf{v})=1/3
, then
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})=3
and
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})=1
, which has already been addressed. In the remaining cases,
\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v})\geq 4
. In this case
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
is the right Farey neighbor of
\mu(\mathbf{v})
, which is no greater than
1/3
.

An immediate consequence is that the extremal decomposition exists in this case. Denote the Drézet-Le Potier curve by
\Delta=\delta(\mu)
. If
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})<\mu(\mathbf{v})
, then
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq\delta(\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}))
since
(r(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}),\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}),\delta(\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})))
is integral (
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
is either on the Drézet-Le Potier curve or semi-exceptional). Then
\chi(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathcal{O})\geq 0
(with equality only if
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=\text{ch}(\mathcal{O}^{k})
), and
\chi(\mathbf{v},\mathcal{O})\leq 0
, hence
\chi(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{O})=\chi(\mathbf{v},\mathcal{O})-\chi(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\mathcal{O})\leq 0
, so
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
lies on or above the Drézet-Le Potier curve since
0<\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})\leq 1/3
. If
\mu(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=\mu(\mathbf{v})
, we may write
\mathbf{v}=(mr_{0},\mu,\delta(\mu)+\frac{k}{mr})
where
\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v})=md_{0}
,
\mu=d/r_{0}
, and
r_{0},d_{0}
are coprime. Let
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{0}:=(r_{0},\mu,\delta(\mu)+\frac{k}{r})
,
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}_{0}:=((m-1)r_{0},\mu,\delta(\mu))
, one may check that
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{0}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
are indeed integral. Then
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{0}+\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}_{0}
. Now clearly
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{0})\geq\Delta(\mathbf{v})
, hence by definition of extremal character,
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\leq\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime}_{0})
, hence
\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})\geq\Delta(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}_{0})=\delta(\mu)
, so
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
also lies on or above the Drézet-Le Potier curve.

By assumption
r\geq 2
, both
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
have smaller rank than
\mathbf{v}
, thus by induction we can assume that there are
E^{\prime}\in M(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
and
E^{\prime\prime}\in M(\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime})
satisfying
h^{0}(E^{\prime})=r(E^{\prime})
and
h^{0}(E^{\prime\prime})=r(E^{\prime\prime})
, and by Proposition
3.3
any nontrivial extension sheaf

0\rightarrow E^{\prime}\rightarrow E\rightarrow E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0

is semistable. To show
h^{0}(E)=r(E)=r(E^{\prime})+r(E^{\prime\prime})
, it is enough to show that we can find such an extension such that the associated connecting homomorphism
H^{0}(E^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow H^{1}(E^{\prime})
vanishes.

Indeed, the association
e\mapsto\delta_{e}
determines a linear map

\text{Ext}^{1}(E^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime})\rightarrow\text{Hom}(H^{0}(E^{\prime\prime}),H^{1}(E^{\prime})).

We need to show that it has nonzero kernel; in fact, the dimension of the source exceeds the dimension of the target. By stability and Serre duality,
\text{ext}^{2}(E^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime})=\hom(E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}(-3))=0
, so

\text{ext}^{1}(E^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime})\geq-\chi(E^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime})=-r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime})-\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta^{\prime\prime}),

and we know already that
h^{0}(E^{\prime\prime})=r^{\prime\prime}
, and

h^{1}(E^{\prime})=h^{0}(E^{\prime})-\chi(E^{\prime})=r^{\prime}-r^{\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime})-\Delta^{\prime}).

Since
\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}
lies above the
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{O}
-branch of the Drézet-Le Potier curve,

\chi(E^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{O})\leq 0
. It follows in particular from Riemann-Roch that
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\geq p(-\mu^{\prime\prime})
. We then have:

\displaystyle\text{ext}^{1}(E^{\prime\prime},E)-\hom(H^{0}(E^{\prime\prime}),H^{1}(E^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\geq-r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime})-\Delta^{\prime}-\Delta^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad-r^{\prime\prime}(r^{\prime}-r^{\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime})-\Delta^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=-r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime})-p(\mu^{\prime})-\Delta^{\prime\prime}+1)

\displaystyle\geq-r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}(p(\mu^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime\prime})-p(\mu^{\prime})-p(-\mu^{\prime\prime})+1)

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}r^{\prime\prime}\mu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}=c_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}^{\prime\prime}>0

as desired. We conclude there is
E\in M(\mathbf{v})
with

h^{0}(E)=h^{0}(E^{\prime})+h^{0}(E^{\prime\prime})=r(E^{\prime})+r(E^{\prime\prime})=r(E),

i.e.,
B^{r}(\mathbf{v})\neq\emptyset
.

5.
Irreducibility & reducibility of Brill-Noether loci

In this section we address the reducibility of the Brill-Noether loci

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose

Proof.

Example 5.11
.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{k}\in K(\mathbb{P}^{2})
be the Chern character

\text{ch}(\mathbf{v})=(\text{ch}_{0}(\mathbf{v}),\text{ch}_{1}(\mathbf{v}),\text{ch}_{2}(\mathbf{v}))=(3,2,-1-k)

so that

\mu(\mathbf{v})=\frac{2}{3},\quad\Delta(\mathbf{v})=\frac{5}{9}+\frac{k}{3}.

Then
\mathbf{v}_{k}
is stable for all
k\geq 0
. As in Theorem
5.8
, there are two components in the Brill-Noether loci

, whose general members are given as extensions

with

a smooth conic and
D
of degree
1-k
, and

0\rightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)\rightarrow E\rightarrow I_{Z}(1)\rightarrow 0

where
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a codimension two subscheme of length
k+1
. For both extension types the general extension bundle
E
is stable, as in Theorem
5.8
, and the locus
Z_{1}
whose points correspond to the first extension type form an irreducible component of

. Its dimension is

\dim Z_{1}=\dim\text{Pic}^{1-k}_{\mathcal{C}/U}+\text{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D),\mathcal{O}^{3})-1+\dim GL_{3}=3k+8.

The locus

Thus

, and we conclude the Brill-Noether locus

.

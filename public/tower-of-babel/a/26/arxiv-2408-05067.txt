Quasilinear parabolic equations with superlinear nonlinearities in critical spaces
Bogdan–Vasile Matioc, Luigi Roberti, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. Well-posedness in time-weighted spaces for quasilinear (and semilinear) parabolic evolution equations u ′ = A ⁡ ( u ) ​ u + f ⁡ ( u ) u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u) is established in a certain critical case of strict inclusion dom ⁡ ( f ) ⊊ dom ⁡ ( A ) \mathrm{dom}(f)\subsetneq\mathrm{dom}(A) for the domains of the (superlinear) function u ↦ f ⁡ ( u ) u\mapsto f(u) and the quasilinear part u ↦ A ⁡ ( u ) u\mapsto A(u) . Based upon regularizing effects of parabolic equations, it is proven that the solution map generates a semiflow in a critical intermediate space. The applicability of the abstract results is demonstrated by several examples including a model for atmospheric flows and semilinear and quasilinear evolution equations with scaling invariance for which well-posedness in the critical scaling invariant intermediate spaces is shown.

Introduction

Solvability of abstract quasilinear parabolic problems

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t>0\,,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\,,

(1.1)

where
A(u)
is the generator of an analytic semigroup on an appropriate Banach space, has a long history and is well-established by means of
semigroup or maximal regularity theory, see e.g.
[

,
4
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
(and e.g.
[

,
17
,
15
,
16
,
32
,
33
]
for even fully nonlinear problems).
In most of the considered cases, the involved nonlinearities
u\mapsto f(u)
and
{u\mapsto A(u)}
have the same domain of definition.
In this article we focus on the case of strict inclusions
\mathrm{dom}(f)\subsetneq\mathrm{dom}(A)
,
which yields more flexibility when applying the theory to concrete problems.
To handle the regularity gap induced by such a strict inclusion we shall use time-weighted spaces.

To set the stage, let
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
be Banach spaces over
\mathbb{K}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},\mathbb{C}\}
with continuous and dense embedding

E_{1}\hookrightarrow E_{0}
.
For each
\theta\in(0,1)
, we denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
an arbitrary admissible interpolation functor of
exponent
\theta
(see
[
6
, I. Section 2.11]
) and define
{E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}}
as the corresponding interpolation space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
.

We fix exponents

\quad 0<\gamma<\beta<\alpha<\xi<1\qquad\text{ with }\qquad q:=\frac{1+\gamma-\alpha}{\xi-\alpha}>1\,.

(1.2a)

Moreover, we assume that there are interpolation functors
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\alpha/\xi}
and
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\gamma/\eta}
of exponents
\alpha/\xi
and
\gamma/\eta
for
\eta\in\{\alpha,\beta,\xi\}
, respectively, such that

E_{\alpha}\doteq\{E_{0},E_{\xi}\}_{\alpha/\xi}\,,\qquad E_{\gamma}\doteq\{E_{0},E_{\eta}\}_{\gamma/\eta}\,,\quad\eta\in\{\beta,\alpha,\xi\}\,.

(1.2b)

For the quasilinear part we assume for some open subset
O_{\beta}
of
E_{\beta}
that

A\in C^{1-}\big(O_{\beta},\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,,

(1.2c)

where

refers to local Lipschitz continuity and
\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
is the open subset of the bounded linear operators
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})

consisting of generators

(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
on
E_{0}
.

Moreover, denoting by
O_{\xi}
the open subset of
E_{\xi}
defined by
O_{\xi}:=O_{\beta}\cap E_{\xi}
, we assume that the semilinear part
f:O_{\xi}\to E_{\gamma}

is locally Lipschitz continuous in the sense that, for each
R>0
, there is a constant
{N(R)>0}
such that

\|f(w)-f(v)\|_{\gamma}\leq N(R)\big[1+\|w\|_{\xi}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q-1}\big]\big[\big(1+\|w\|_{\xi}+\|v\|_{\xi}\big)\|w-v\|_{\beta}+\|w-v\|_{\xi}\big]

(1.2d)

for
w,\,v\in O_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(0,R)
,
where
q>1
is defined in (
1.2a
) and
\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(0,R)
is the closed ball in
E_{\beta}
centered at
0
with radius
R
. Of course, (
1.2d
) includes the case that

\|f(w)-f(v)\|_{\gamma}\leq N(R)\big[1+\|w\|_{\xi}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q-1}\big]\|w-v\|_{\xi}

for
w,\,v\in O_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(0,R)
.

Under assumptions (
1.2
) we shall prove that problem (
1.1
) is well-posed in
O_{\alpha}:=O_{\beta}\cap E_{\alpha}
.
We emphasize that the semilinear part
f
needs not be defined on
O_{\alpha}
and possibly requires more regularity
than the quasilinear part
A
since
E_{\xi}\hookrightarrow E_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow E_{\beta}
by (
1.2a
). Before stating the main result precisely, let us comment on the imposed assumptions. If the nonlinearities
A
and
f

are defined and Lipschitz continuous on the same set
O_{\beta}
, a general and powerful result on the well-posedness of (
1.1
) is
[
5
, Theorem 12.1]

(for proofs see
[
4
]
and
[
36
]
).
It states that if

(A,f)\in C^{1-}\big(O_{\beta},\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\times E_{\gamma}\big)\,,\qquad u_{0}\in O_{\alpha}\,,\quad 0<\gamma\leq\beta<\alpha<1\,,

then problem (
1.1
) admits a unique maximal strong solution and the solution map induces a semiflow on
O_{\alpha}
.
Our purpose herein is to prove a similar result under the assumptions stated above.
In fact, we extend our previous work
[
37
]
, where we proved the semiflow property when the second part of assumption (
1.2a
) is replaced by the assumption that the strict inequality

1\leq q<\frac{1+\gamma-\alpha}{\xi-\alpha}

(1.3)

holds, to the critical case
q(\xi-\alpha)=1+\gamma-\alpha
.
The latter covers important applications and yields a semiflow in spaces that are optimal in a certain sense. As pointed out
in
[
39
,
42
,
23
]
, for
evolution equations which possess a scaling invariance, the space
E_{\alpha}
corresponding to the critical case
q(\xi-\alpha)=1+\gamma-\alpha

may be invariant under this scaling and is therefore in this sense a critical space (see Example
4.3
and Example
4.4

4

In
[
39
,
42
]
the quasilinear problem (
1.1
) is thoroughly investigated in the context of maximal

L_{p}
-regularity for the critical case (
1.2a
), with
an additional structural condition
on the Banach spaces

and

(i.e.

needs to be a UMD space and
{H_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{+},E_{0})\cap L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{+},E_{1})\hookrightarrow H_{p}^{1-\theta}({\mathbb{R}}^{+},E_{\theta})}
for each
\theta\in[0,1]

Recently it has been shown that these structural conditions are in fact not needed for the critical case in the maximal

L_{p}
-regularity setting, see
[
23
, Section 18.2]
for details and also
[
23
, Section 18.5]
for a discussion of the history of the critical case.

It is worth emphasizing that our approach covers situations for which maximal regularity does not seem to be available (see Example
4.1
).

In our setting, we impose only the interpolation property (
1.2b
) as an additional assumption to the subcritical case (
1.3
). Regarding this property, we point out:

Assumption (
1.2b
) is automatically satisfied if

is the complex interpolation functor for every
\theta\in\{\gamma,\beta,\alpha,\xi\}
,
or if
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,p}
is the real interpolation functor with parameter
p\in[1,\infty]
for every
\theta\in\{\gamma,\beta,\alpha,\xi\}

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,\infty}^{0}

This is explicitly stated in

and a consequence of the reiteration theorems for the complex and the real methods (see

, I.Section 2.8]

For the subcritical regime (
1.3
), well-posedness of (

]
in time-weighted

-spaces in the framework of maximal

)
in real interpolation spaces
with

with strict inequality (

that extends our previous research

Suppose (

(i)

(Existence)
Given any

(1.5)

2.
The Quasilinear Problem: Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on Proposition
2.1
below.
Before stating this result, we recall that given a Banach space
E
,
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
T>0
, the time weighted space

C_{\mu}\big((0,T],E\big):=\big\{u\in C((0,T],E)\,:\,\text{$t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{E}\to 0$ for $t\to 0$}\big\}

is a Banach space with the norm

\|u\|_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E)}:=\sup_{t\in(0,T]}t^{\mu}\|u(t)\|_{E}\,.

For a suitable choice
of

plays a crucial role in the subsequent analysis.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume (
1.2
) and let
\mu:=\xi-\alpha

Then, given
\overline{u}\in O_{\alpha},

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],O_{\alpha}\big)

in

(ii) The fixed point mapping.

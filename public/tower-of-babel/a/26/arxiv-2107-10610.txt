Generalized Turán problems for K 2 , t K_{2,t}
Dániel Gerbner Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics

Abstract
We study the generalized Turán function ex ⁡ ( n , H , F ) \mathrm{ex}(n,H,F) , when H H or F F is K 2 , t K_{2,t} . We determine the order of magnitude of ex ⁡ ( n , H , K 2 , t ) \mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{2,t}) when H H is a tree, and determine its asymptotics for a large class of trees. We also determine the asymptotics of ex ⁡ ( n , K 2 , t , H ) \mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},H) in most cases.

Introduction

One of the most fundamental problems of extremal graph theory deals with determining the largest number of edges in
n
-vertex graphs that do not have a given subgraph
F
. This quantity is called the Turán number of
F
and is denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
. Here we mention one particular result due to Füredi
[
2
]
: for
t\geq 2
we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t})=(1+o(1))\sqrt{t-1}n^{3/2}/2
.

In this paper we deal with a generalization of the Turán number. Given two graphs
H
and
G
, we denote by
{\mathcal{N}}(H,G)
the number of (unlabeled) copies of
H
in
G
. We let
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,F):=\max\{{\mathcal{N}}(H,G):\,G\text{ is an $n$-vertex $F$-free graph}\}
, i.e. the largest number of copies of
H
in
n
-vertex
F
-free graphs. In particular,
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2},F)=\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
.
We will consider the cases when either
H
or
F
is
K_{2,t}
for some
t\geq 2
.

Let us describe the above mentioned
K_{2,t}
-free construction of Füredi
[
2
]
in more detail, as we will use it later. Let
q_{t}(n)
be the largest prime power such that
t-1
divides
q_{t}(n)-1
and
(q_{t}(n)^{2}-1)/(t-1)\leq n
. When
t
and
n
are clear from the context, we will sometimes omit them and denote
q_{t}(n)
by
q
. It is well-known that for sufficiently large
n
there exists such a
q_{t}(n)
with
\sqrt{nt}-n^{1/3}<q_{t}(n)

[
10
]
. Therefore, we can use graphs on
(q_{t}(n)^{2}-1)/(t-1)
vertices instead of
n
-vertex graphs, and this does not change the asymptotics of the lower bounds obtained.

Let us consider a
q_{t}(n)
-element field and let
h
be an element of order
t-1
, thus the elements
\{h,h^{2},h^{3},\dots,h^{t-1}\}
form a multiplicative subgroup. Two pairs
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
of elements are considered equivalent if
a=h^{p}a^{\prime}
and
b=h^{p}b^{\prime}
for some
p
. Then we have
(q_{t}(n)^{2}-1)/(t-1)
equivalence classes, they form the vertex set of our graph. The equivalence class of
(a,b)
and the equivalence class of
(c,d)
are joined by an edge if
ac+bd=h^{p}
for some
p
. Observe that by this definition some vertices could be connected to themselves, creating a loop; we will call such vertices
special
and ignore the loops to obtain a simple graph that we call the Füredi graph and denote by
F(n,t)
.

Füredi
[
2
]
showed that any special vertex of
F(n,t)
is connected to exactly
q-1
vertices and other vertices are connected to exactly
q
vertices. Two vertices
u
and
v
of
F(n,t)
have exactly
t-1
common neighbors unless
u
and
v
are adjacent and at least one of them is a special vertex, in which case they have at most
t-2
common neighbors.

Now we are ready to introduce the main definition of this paper.
A graph
H
is called
t
-Füredi-good
if

\mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{2,t})=(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(H,F(n,t)).

This definition is motivated by the similar notion of
k
-Turán-good graphs
[
7
]
. Those are the graphs
H
with
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{k})={\mathcal{N}}(H,T_{k-1}(n)
, where
T_{k-1}(n)
is the
(k-1)
-partite Turán graph, which is the unique
n
-vertex
K_{k}
-free with
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{k})
edges. Similarly, here we examine when we have the same extremal graph as in the ordinary Turán problem, but only in the asymptotical sense.

Now, we can quickly summarize most of the results concerning
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{2,t})
for
t\geq 2
: Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
showed that
K_{3}
is
t
-Füredi-good, Gerbner and Palmer
[
6
]
showed that
P_{k}
and
C_{k}
are
t
-Füredi-good, Gerbner and Patkós
[
8
]
showed that
K_{2,s}
is
t
-Füredi-good.

There are some other results. Zhang and Ge
[
13
]
showed that for
t\geq 2m-3\geq 3
we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{m},K_{2,t})=\Theta(n^{3/2})
.
Gerbner and Patkós
[
8
]
determined
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{1,p},K_{2,t})
exactly if
p>t+1
and
n
is large enough.
The case of forbidden
K_{2,2}
was studied in
[
3
,
4
]
. In particular, Gerbner
[
3
]
showed that
P_{3}
is 2-Füredi-good, but also presented an exact result
\mathrm{ex}(n,P_{3},K_{2,2})=\binom{n}{2}
for
n
even and
\mathrm{ex}(n,P_{3},K_{2,2})=\binom{n}{2}-1
(with the construction being a matching added to the star
S_{n}
). Moreover, for other stars we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,S_{r},K_{2,2})=\binom{n-1}{r-1}
.

In our main result, we characterize the
t
-Füredi-good trees. Furthermore, we determine the order of magnitude of
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})
for other trees as well. Before stating our results, we need to introduce further definitions.

Let
T
be a tree. We will partition its vertex set into two parts
A
and
B
applying a greedy process. Originally
A
consists of vertices of degree 2, and
B
contains the rest of the vertices. Then, if there is a non-leaf vertex
v
in
B
that is connected to at most two vertices in
B
, then we add
v
to
A
. We repeat this as long as we can find a vertex satisfying the above property. This gives an ordering
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
of vertices in
A
with degree more than 2 (the order of adding them to
A
). This ordering is not unique, but each ordering gives the same
A
and
B
. When we refer to this ordering later, we mean that we fix one of the possible orderings.

We say that
T
is
nice
if every vertex of
B
is a leaf, and
T
has at least 3 vertices.

Theorem 1.1
.

Nice trees are
t
-Füredi-good for every
t\geq 2
.

Let us assume now that
T
is not nice and introduce some notation. Let
L
be the set of leaves of
T
.
Let
Q
be the subgraph induced on
B
(thus
Q
contains
L
). Let
Q^{\prime}
be the subgraph we obtain by deleting
L
from
Q
.

Q^{\prime}
is a forest, let
Q_{i}
denote its connected components.
Removing
Q^{\prime}
cuts
T
into several subtrees
T_{1},\dots,T_{s}
. Each
T_{j}
is connected to one or more
Q_{i}
with an edge.
Observe that if
Q^{\prime}
has no vertices, then
T
is nice.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
T
be a tree on at least three vertices that is not nice. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})=\Theta\left(n^{|L|+\frac{|A|+s}{2}}\right)
.

We will show later that the Füredi graph contains
\Theta\left(n^{\frac{|V(T)|+1}{2}}\right)=\Theta\left(n^{\frac{|A|+|L|+1}{2}}\right)
copies of
T
, thus for non-nice trees
{\mathcal{N}}(T,F(n,t))
does not even have the same order of magnitude as
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})
.

Let us consider now the problem of counting copies of
K_{2,t}
.
Several results of Gerbner and Patkós contain this as a subcase; in particular they determined the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{p,q})
if
2<p,q\leq t
and
n
is large enough.

Győri, Pach and Simonovits
[
9
]
showed that if
H
is complete multipartite, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{k})={\mathcal{N}}(H,G)
for some complete
(k-1)
-partite graph
G
on
n
vertices.
G
does not have to be balanced, but they showed that if
H
is
K_{2,2}
or
K_{2,3}
, then
G
is balanced, i.e. the Turán graph. It is a straightforward optimization to find the optimal graph for given values of
t
and
k
, but we are unable to deal with this problem in this generality. Ma and Qiu
[
11
]
showed that for
k=3
, in the case of
t=4
the Turán graph is not optimal, but is asymptotically optimal. In other words,
\mathrm{ex}(K_{2,4},K_{3})\neq{\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil})
but
\mathrm{ex}(K_{2,4},K_{3})=(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil})
. For larger
t
,
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
does not even give the correct asymptotics.

Gerbner and Palmer
[
6
]
proved that if
F
has chromatic number
k
, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,H,F)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,H,K_{k})+o(n^{k})
for any
H
. In our case it shows that the complete
(k-1)
-partite
n
-vertex graph
G
with the most copies of
K_{2,t}
gives the correct asymptotics for
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},F)
.

Gerbner, Z. Nagy and Vizer
[
5
]
determined the asymptotics of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},C_{2k})
, generalizing a result of Gerbner, Győri, Methuku and Vizer
[
4
]
. There are too many results on
\mathrm{ex}(n,C_{4},F)
to list them here.

Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
determined the graphs
F
with
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{3},F)=O(n)
. Their results also implied a similar characterization where
K_{3}
is replaced with any forest. Gerbner and Palmer
[
6
]
obtained such a characterization for every cycle.

In this paper we obtain such a characterization for
K_{2,t}
. Moreover, we characterize the possible orders of magnitude below
n^{t}
. Let
K_{2,r}^{p,q}
denote the graph we obtain from
K_{2,r}
if we connect
p
new vertices to
u
and
q
new vertices to
v
, where
u
and
v
are the vertices of the partite set of size 2 of
K_{2,r}
. We note that
p,q,r
can be 0. For integers
k,t\geq 2
, we let
M(k,t)
denote the complete
k
-partite graph with the most copies of
K_{2,t}
. Clearly,
M(k,t)
contains
\Theta(n^{t+2})
copies of
K_{2,t}
. For a graph
H
, we denote by
kH
the graph consisting of
k
vertex-disjoint copies of
H
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any integer
t\geq 2
and any graph
F
we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},F)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}0&\textrm{if\/ $K_{2,t}$ contains $F$},\\
(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},\lfloor\frac{n}{p+q+r+1}\rfloor K_{p+q+r+1})&\textrm{if\/ $F=K_{2,r}^{p,q}$ with $r\leq t$ and $p+q+r>t$},\\
(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},F(q(n),r))&\textrm{if\/ $F=K_{2,r}^{p,q}$ with $r>t$},\\
\Omega(n^{t})&\textrm{if\/ $F$ is any other bipartite graph},\\
(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},M(k-1,t))&\textrm{if\/ $F$ has chromatic number at least three}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Let us discuss the missing case.
For a bipartite graph
F
, we denote by
\beta(F)
the smallest
p
such that
F
is a subgraph of
K_{p,q}
for some
q
. In the above theorem, the
\Omega(n^{t})
bound belongs to the case
\beta(F)\geq 3
. If
\beta(F)\leq t
, we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},F)=\Theta(n^{t})
, where the upper bound follows from a result of Gerbner and Patkós
[
8
]
on
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{p,q})
.
If
\beta(F)<t
, we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},F)=(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},K_{\beta(F)-1,n-\beta(F)+1})
, where the upper bound follows from another result of Gerbner and Patkós
[
8
]
. This also implies that for any given
F
, for
t\geq|V(F)|-2
we have the asymptotics of
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},F)
(assuming in the case
\chi(F)\geq 3
that we can solve the optimization problem mentioned earlier).

If

\beta(F)>t
, then by a result of Alon and Shikhelman
[
1
]
we have the upper bound
O(n^{t+2-2t/\beta})
. This is also sharp in some cases, see
[
8
]
for a collection of results on
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{s,t},K_{p,q})
.

In Section 2, we deal with the case where

is forbidden, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, and a corollary. In Section 3 we deal with the case of counting copies of

and prove Theorem

2
Forbidding
K_{2,t}

In this section instead of copies of
T
, we will often talk about embeddings of
T
, that create ordered copies of
T
. As it only depends on
T
how many ordered copies of itself
T
contains, it will not change anything when we compare the number of copies of
T
in
G
and in
F(n,t)
. More precisely, an embedding
f:V(T)\rightarrow V(G)
is an injective function such that if
uv
is an edge of
T
, then
f(u)f(v)
is an edge of
G
. Let
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}(T,G)
denote the number of embeddings of
T
into
G
. Then
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}(T,G)=c(T){\mathcal{N}}(T,G)
for some
c(T)
depending only on
T
(the number of automorphisms). We will also talk about embeddings with some fixed vertices. That means that
f(v_{1}),\dots,f(v_{\ell})
are already given. More precisely, if
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}\in V(T)
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in V(G)
, then let
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}(T,v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell},G,x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
denote the number of embeddings
f
of
T
into
G
such that
f(v_{i})=x_{i}
for every
i\leq\ell
.

Let us define embeddings
f
of
T
into
F(n,t)
,
greedily. First we let
f(v)=x
for some
v\in V(T)
and
x\in V(F(n,t))
, and then in each step, we pick a vertex of
T
adjacent to exactly one of the vertices already embedded. It is well-known that we can build any tree this way. When we pick a vertex
y
that is adjacent to an already embedded vertex
z
, we need to pick a neighbor
f(z)
in
G
. We pick a neighbor that we have not picked as an image.
Clearly, there are
(1+o(1))\sqrt{(t-1)n}
ways to pick such an image. The number of embeddings we found is
(1+o(1))(t-1)^{(|V(T)|-1)/2}n^{(|V(T)|+1)/2}
.

We state the following strengthening of Theorem
1.1
. This will help us with the induction. Note that
K_{2}
is not a nice tree, but it is
t
-Füredi-good. However, the strengthening below does not hold for
K_{2}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T
be a nice tree. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})=(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(T,F(n,t))
. Moreover, let
\ell\geq 1
be an integer and
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
be leaves of
T
such that any pair of them has distance more than 2. Then for any
K_{2,t}
-free
n
-vertex graph
G
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in V(G)
, we have

{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}(T,v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell},G,x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})\leq(1+o(1))(t-1)^{(|V(T)|-1)/2}n^{(|V(T)|-2\ell+1)/2}
.

Proof.

We use induction on
|V(T)|
.
We deal first with the case
T
has a vertex
w
such that each leaf is at distance two from
w
(i.e.
T
is a spider with legs of length two). This proves the base case
|V(T)|=3
. First we embed the leaves that are not fixed and
w
, arbitrarily, this can be done at most
n^{\frac{|V(T)|+1}{2}-\ell}
ways. Then each of the other
(|V(T)|-1)/2
vertices is the common neighbor of
w
and a leaf, thus can be embedded to at most
t-1
vertices.

Let us assume that
|V(T)|\geq 4
.
We will define an embedding
g
of
T
into
G
recursively such that
g(v_{i})=x_{i}
for every
i\leq\ell
. It is easy to see that each such embedding of
T
into
G
can be found with the recursive embeddings defined below, thus an upper bound on the number of such embeddings is an upper bound on
{\mathcal{N}}^{\prime}(T,v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell},G,x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})
.

Let us consider a subtree
T^{\prime}
with a vertex
v\in A
such that a leaf neighbor
v^{\prime}
of
v
is already embedded. If
v
has another leaf neighbor, we denote that neighbor by
v^{\prime\prime}
. By deleting
v
from
T^{\prime}
we obtain several subtrees
T_{i}^{\prime}
. We label a subtree with the most vertices to be
T_{1}^{\prime}
. Each subtree
T_{i}^{\prime}
has a vertex
w_{i}
adjacent to
v
. If
v^{\prime\prime}
does not exist, we can assume that
T_{1}^{\prime}
has more than 2 vertices (otherwise
T
is a spider with legs of length two and we are done).

Now we are ready to define the recursive embedding
g
of
T^{\prime}
.
We first embed
v^{\prime\prime}

arbitrarily. If there is no
v^{\prime\prime}
, we will embed
T_{1}^{\prime}
first. There are two possibilities. If there is a fixed vertex
u^{\prime}
in
T_{1}^{\prime}
, we use that as the first embedded vertex. Then
u^{\prime}
is a leaf and has a neighbor
u
. Then

is not a leaf, thus

, hence we can apply the recursion with

playing the role of

and

playing the role of
v^{\prime}
, and embed

.

If
T_{1}^{\prime}
does not have a fixed vertex, then we pick a vertex
w_{1}^{\prime}
of

in

with a leaf neighbor

. Such vertices exist as

has leaves and also has at least one vertex (

) in

. We embed

has an already embedded leaf neighbor, and at most one other neighbor in

, thus we can apply the recursion and embed

.

After embedding
v^{\prime\prime}
or
T_{1}^{\prime}
, we pick
g(v)
as a common neighbor of
g(v^{\prime})
and
g(v^{\prime\prime})
, or if

does not exist, we pick
g(v)
as a common neighbor of
g(v^{\prime})
and
g(w_{1})
. Consider a tree

not yet embedded. We have

, and
w_{i}
has a neighbor

already embedded. In the tree that we obtain by adding

to
T_{i}^{\prime}
,
v
is a leaf, thus we can embed

recursively.

Let us count the embeddings.
By induction, we know the situation in each

. Assume that
T_{i}^{\prime}
contains
\ell_{i}
fixed vertices. For

, we embed

together with

, thus we have
|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|+1

(1+o(1))(t-1)^{|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|/2}n^{(|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|-2\ell_{i})/2}
embeddings of
T_{i}^{\prime}
.

If
v^{\prime\prime}
exists, we have the same bound for

, and a factor of at most

for choosing

as a common neighbor of

and

. Furthermore, if

is not fixed, we have an additional factor of at most

for choosing

.
The
\ell_{i}
’s add up to

if

’s add up to

, thus we obtain the desired bound.

If

does not exist, then the

’s add up to

. We can embed

at most
(1+o(1))(t-1)^{(|V(T_{1}^{\prime})|-1)/2}n^{(|V(T_{1}^{\prime})|-2\ell_{1}+1)/2}

∎

The above theorem easily implies that we can attach nice trees to

-Füredi-good graphs to obtain other

-Füredi-good graphs.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
H
be a
t
-Füredi-good graph,

be a nice tree and

be obtained from

and

by identifying an arbitrary vertex of

. Then

is

First we embed

, and then embed

asymptotically maximizes the number of embeddings.
∎

Let us turn to other trees. Recall that Theorem
1.2
states that
\mathrm{ex}(n,T,K_{2,t})=\Theta(n^{|L|+\frac{|A|+s}{2}})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us start with the upper bound and let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{2,t}
-free graph. We will take a copy of

the following way. First we pick the leaves adjacent to

, we have at most linearly many choices for each. If a vertex of

. Recall that
Q^{\prime}
is a forest. After we have picked some of its vertices, the remaining vertices form a forest
Q^{\prime\prime}
. If
Q^{\prime\prime}
is not empty, then there is a leaf
w
of
Q^{\prime\prime}
. Observe that
w
is a non-leaf vertex of
T
and

. This shows that
w
is connected to at least 3 vertices of

, and at most one of those is in
Q^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
w
has at least two neighbors already picked, thus we also pick
w
. This shows that we picked every vertex of
B
, and we had
O(n^{\ell})
choices.

Let
T_{i}
be a subtree with neighbors
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
, then let
T_{i}^{\prime}
be
T_{i}
together with
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
. The number of ways to pick
T_{i}^{\prime}
with
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
fixed is
\Theta(n^{(|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|-2\ell+1)/2})
by Theorem
2.1
. The
|V(T_{i}^{\prime})|
’s add up to
|A|
, and we have an extra
n^{1/2}
factor for every
i
, which completes the proof of the upper bound.

To prove the lower bound, we define a
K_{2,t}
-free graph
G_{0}
. We let
n^{\prime}=(n-|V(Q^{\prime})|)/|V(T)
. We take a copy of
Q^{\prime}
, and one copy of each
T_{j}
. Consider a vertex
v
of
Q^{\prime}
. If
v
has a leaf neighbor, we add
n^{\prime}
new vertices and connect them to
v
.
Let us consider the subtrees
T_{j}
now and let
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
be the neighbors of vertices of
T_{j}
in
Q^{\prime}
. We take a copy of
F(n^{\prime},t)
, and we will pick
\ell
independent vertices in it. We identify those
\ell
vertices with
v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}
. We pick the
\ell
independent vertices of
F^{\prime}(n,t)
such a way that it maximizes the number of embeddings of
T_{j}
with the vertices
v_{i}
connected to the appropriate vertices of the embedded copy of
T_{j}
. We repeat this for every
j
(with different copies of
F(n^{\prime},t)
).

The resulting graph
G_{0}
has at most
n
vertices as we replace at most
|V(T)|
vertices with
n^{\prime}
new vertices.
It is also easy to see that
G_{0}
is
K_{2,t}
-free. Indeed, the only cycles in
G_{0}
are inside the vertex-disjoint copies of
F(n^{\prime},t)
, which are
K_{2,t}
-free.

Let us count the number of embeddings of
T
into
G_{0}
. For each leaf adjacent to a vertex of
Q^{\prime}
, we have linearly many choices.

We will show that the number of ways to pick
T_{j}^{\prime}
in
F(n^{\prime},t)
is
\Omega(n^{(|V(T)|-2\ell+1)/2})
, completing the proof. Indeed, without fixing the
\ell
leaves there are
\Theta(n^{(|V(T)|+1)/2})
ways to pick
T_{j}^{\prime}
by Theorem
2.1
, thus there must be a way to fix the leaves such that we can pick the remaining part of
T^{\prime}_{j}
(i.e.
T_{j})
in
\Omega(n^{(|V(T)|-2\ell+1)/2})
ways.
∎

3
Counting
K_{2,t}

In the section we prove Theorem
1.3
.
First we deal with the linear case, where we also obtain an exact result for infinitely many values of
n
. Recall that
K_{2,r}^{p,q}
is obtained by connecting
p
and
q
leaves to the two vertices in the partite set of size two of
K_{2,r}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
r\leq t
and
p+q+r>t
. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{2,r}^{p,q})=\lfloor\frac{n}{p+q+r+1}\rfloor{\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},K_{p+q+r+1})+O(1)
. Moreover, if
p+q+r+1
divides
n
, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{2,r}^{p,q})=\frac{n}{p+q+r+1}{\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},K_{p+q+r+1})
.

Proof.

The lower bound is obtained by
\lfloor\frac{n}{p+q+r+1}\rfloor
vertex disjoint copies of
K_{p+q+r+1}
.

For the upper bound, consider an
n
-vertex
K_{2,r}^{p,q}
-free graph
G
. We will define an auxiliary multigraph
G^{\prime}
on the same vertex set. Two vertices
u
and
v
are connected by
m
edges if there are
m
copies of
K_{2,t}
in
G
where
u
and
v
form the partite set of size 2. Clearly, the number of edges in
G^{\prime}
is equal to the number of copies of
K_{2,t}
in
G
. Therefore, it is enough to bound the average degree in
G^{\prime}
.

We claim that it is enough to
show that the average degree in
G^{\prime}
is at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)
.
Indeed, if
G
consists of
\lfloor\frac{n}{p+q+r+1}\rfloor
vertex disjoint copies of
K_{p+q+r+1}
, then the average degree in
G^{\prime}
is at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)
, with equality if
p+q+r+1
divides
n
.

Observe that for a vertex
v
with degree
d
in
G
, its degree is at most
\binom{d}{t}(p+q+r-2)
in
G^{\prime}
, as we pick
t
neighbors of
v
, and then those
t
neighbors have at most
p+q+r-1
common neighbors, we pick one of them that is different from
v
.

Claim 3.2
.

If
d_{G}(v)\geq p+q+r
, then in
G^{\prime}
at most
(p+q+r)\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}
edges go from
v
to vertices
w
with
d_{G}(w)\geq p+r
.

Proof of Claim.

If
v
is connected to a vertex
w
in
G^{\prime}
, this means that
v
and
w
have
t
common neighbors in
G
. Assume first that
vw\not\in E(G)
. If
d_{G}(w)\geq p+r
, then we pick
p
common neighbors of
v
and
w
,
r
other neighbors of
w
, and
v
still has
q
neighbors left. This way we obtain a
K_{2,t}^{p,q}
, a contradiction. Thus
d_{G}(w)\leq p+r-1
.

Assume now that
vw\in E(G)
. If
d_{G}(v)\geq p+q+r+1
, the above reasoning still works and we obtain that
d_{G}(w)\leq p+r-1
. Finally, let us assume that
d_{G}(v)=p+q+r
and
w
is one of the
p+q+r
neighbors of
v
in
G
. Then there are at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
where
v
and
w
are in the partite set of size 2, i.e. there are at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}
edges between
v
and
w
in
G^{\prime}
.
∎

Let us return to the proof of the proposition. Let
A
denote the set of vertices with degree at least
p+q+r
in
G
, and
B
denote the set of vertices with degree at most
p+r-1
. Then in
G^{\prime}
, the vertices of
B
have degree at most
\binom{p+r-1}{t}(p+q+r-2)
. For every vertex
v
of
A
, in
G^{\prime}
all but
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)
edges incident to
v
go to a vertex of
B
. This implies that in
G^{\prime}
all but
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)|A|
edges incident to
A
go to
B
, thus there are at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)|A|+\binom{p+r-1}{t}(p+q+r-2)|B|
edges incident to
A\cup B
. This shows that the average degree in
G^{\prime}
is at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r)
on
A\cup B
. The other vertices have degree at most
p+q+r-1
in
G
, thus degree at most
\binom{p+q+r-1}{t}(p+q+r-2)
in
G^{\prime}
, finishing the proof.
∎

We remark that it is easy to extend this proof to show that in a
K_{2,r}^{p,q}
-free graph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{2,r}^{p,q})
copies of
K_{2,t}
, all but
O(1)
of the vertices form vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{p+q+r+1}
. Indeed, all but
O(1)
vertices must be in
A
and have
p+q+r-1
common neighbors (as other vertices have smaller degree in
G^{\prime}
). In other words, we take almost
n/(p+q+r+1)
copies of
K_{p+q+r+1}
, and then the graph with the most copies
K_{2,t}
on the remaining
O(1)
vertices. What is left is to determine
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{2,r}^{p,q})
for small values of
n
.

A natural idea is to take
\lfloor n/(p+q+r+1)\rfloor
copies of
K_{p+q+r+1}
and a smaller clique on the remaining vertices, but this construction is not always optimal. Consider e.g.
\mathrm{ex}(14,K_{2,7},K_{2,2}^{3,3})
. The above construction contains 36 copies of
K_{2,7}
, while another
K_{2,2}^{3,3}
-free graph
K_{7,7}
contains 42 copies.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
r>t>1
. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t},K_{2,r}^{p,q})=(1+o(1)){\mathcal{N}}(K_{2,t},F(q(n),r))=(1+o(1))\binom{n}{2}\binom{r-1}{t}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
K_{2,r}^{p,q}
-free graph.
Assume first that two vertices
u
and
v
have degree at least
r+p+q
. Then
u
and
v
have at most
r-1
common neighbors, thus they are in the smaller partite set of at most
\binom{r-1}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
.

Assume now that the degree of a vertex
u
is less than
r+p+q
. Then for every
v
such that
u
and
v
form the smaller partite set of a
K_{2,t}
, there are at least
t
neighbors of
u
connected to
v
. There are at most
\binom{p+q+r}{t}
ways to pick
t
neighbors of
u
. These
t
neighbors have at most
p+q+r
common neighbors. Therefore, there are at most
(p+q+r)\binom{p+q+r}{t}
vertices that can be connected to
t
neighbors of
u
, thus
u
is in the smaller partite set of at most
(p+q+r)\binom{p+q+r}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
.

We count the copies of
K_{2,t}
by picking the two vertices in the smaller partite set. Let us count first the copies where the two vertices in the smaller partite set
have degree at least
r+p+q
. They are in the smaller partite set of at most
\binom{r-1}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
, thus there are at most
\binom{n}{2}\binom{r-1}{t}
such copies of
K_{2,t}
. Finally, we count the copies of
K_{2,t}
where one of the vertices in the smaller partite set has degree less than
p+q+r
. There are at most
n
such vertices, and each of them is contained in the smaller partite set of at most
(p+q+r)\binom{p+q+r}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
. Therefore, there are at most
\binom{n}{2}\binom{r-1}{t}+n(p+q+r)\binom{p+q+r}{t}
copies of
K_{2,t}
in
G
, finishing the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The first line of the inequality is obvious, the second and third lines are dealt with in Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
. The fourth line is shown by the construction
K_{2,n-2}
.

For non-bipartite graphs
F
, the lower bound holds by definition and the upper bound follows from the results mentioned in the introduction: the result of Győri, Pach and Simonovits
[
9
]
stating that a complete
(k-1)
-partite graph contains the most copies of any complete multipartite
H
among
K_{k}
-free graphs, and a result of Gerbner and Palmer
[
6
]
stating that changing
K_{k}
to any
k
-chromatic
F
results in an additive term
o(n^{|V(H)|})
.
∎

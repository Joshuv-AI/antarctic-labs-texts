On the strong convergence of the trajectories of a Tikhonov regularized second order dynamical system with asymptotically vanishing damping
Szilárd Csaba László

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper deals with a second order dynamical system with vanishing damping that contains a Tikhonov regularization term, in connection to the minimization problem of a convex Fréchet differentiable function
g
.
We show that for appropriate Tikhonov regularization parameters the value of the objective function in a generated trajectory converges fast to the global minimum of the objective function and a trajectory generated by the dynamical system converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function. We also obtain the fast convergence of the velocities towards zero and some integral estimates. Nevertheless, our main goal is to extend and improve some recent results obtained in
[
6
]
and
[
12
]
concerning the strong convergence of the generated trajectories to an element of minimal norm from the
\argmin
set of the objective function
g
. Our analysis also reveals that the damping coefficient and the Tikhonov regularization coefficient are strongly correlated.

Key Words.
convex optimization, continuous second order dynamical system, Tikhonov regularization, strong convergence, convergence rate

AMS subject classification.
34G20, 47J25, 90C25, 90C30, 65K10

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space endowed with the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
and let

g:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex continuously differentiable function whose solution set
\argmin g
is nonempty. Assume further, that
\nabla g
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets. Consider the minimization problem

(P)\,\,\,\inf_{x\in\mathcal{H}}g(x)

in connection to the second order dynamical system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)\right)+\frac{a}{t^{p}}x(t)=0\\
x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\end{cases}

(1)

where
t_{0}>0
,
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
and
\alpha,\,q,\,a,\,p>0.

First of all, note that the term
\frac{a}{t^{p}}x(t)
is a Tikhonov regularization term, which may assure the strong convergence of a generated trajectory to the minimizer of minimal norm of the objective function
g.

For further insight into the Tikhonov regularization techniques we refer to
[
8
,
11
,
10
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
.

The case
q=1
was intensively studied in the literature. Indeed, in
[
8
]
Attouch, Chbani and Riahi showed that for
q=1,

p>2,

a=1
and
\alpha>3
the generated trajectories of (
1
) converge weakly to a minimizer of
g
. Further, one has
g(x(t)-\min g=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right).
On the other hand, if
p<2
, then the strong convergence result
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|=0
holds, where
x^{*}
is the element of minimum norm from
\argmin g.
The previous result is also true if
p=2
and
a>\frac{4}{9}\alpha(\alpha-3).
Similar results have been obtained in
[
17
]
and
[
3
]
for some second order dynamical systems with (implicit) Hessian driven damping and Tikhonov regularization term (see also
[
15
,
4
]
). The case
p=2
seems to be critical in the sense that separates the case when one obtains fast convergence of the function values and weak convergence of the trajectories to a minimizer, and the case when the strong convergence of the trajectories to a minimizer of minimum norm is ensured. However, recently Attouch and László in
[
12
]
succeeded to obtain both rapid convergence towards the infimal value of
g
, and the strong convergence of the trajectories towards the element of minimum norm of the set of minimizers of
g.
More precisely, in
[
12
]
it is shown that if
q=1,

p=2,

a>0
and
\alpha>3
then

\displaystyle g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.
Further, the trajectory
x
is bounded,

\displaystyle\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
, and
there is strong convergence to the minimum norm solution
x^{*}
, i.e.

\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.
A similar result has been obtained in
[
3
]
for a second order dynamical systems with implicit Hessian driven damping.

We emphasize that for
q=1
the dynamical system (
1
) is a Tikhonov regularized version of the second order dynamical system with vanishing damping, studied by Su-Boyd-Candès
[
25
]
in connection to the optimization problem
(P)
, that is,

\rm{(HBS)}\,\,\,\,\,\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)\right)=0,\,x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,\dot{x}(t_{0})=v_{0},\,u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}.

It is obvious that the latter system can be obtained from (
1
) by taking
q=1
and
a=0
.

According to
[
25
]
, the trajectories generated by (HBS) assure fast minimization property of order
\mathcal{O}\left(1/t^{2}\right)
for the decay
g(x(t))-\min g,
provided
\alpha\geq 3
. For
\alpha>3
, it has been shown by Attouch-Chbani-Peypouquet-Redont
[
7
]
that each trajectory generated by (HBS) converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function
g
. Further, it is shown in
[
13
]
and
[
23
]
that the asymptotic convergence rate of the values is actually
o(1/t^{2})
.

The case
\alpha=3
corresponds to Nesterov’s historical algorithm
[
24
]
as it was emphasized in
[
25
]
, more precisely for
\alpha=3
, (HBS) can be seen as a continuous version of the accelerated gradient method of Nesterov, (see also
[
22
]
).

However, the case
\alpha=3
is critical, that is, the convergence of the trajectories generated by (HBS) remains an open problem.
The subcritical case
\alpha\leq 3
has been examined by Apidopoulos-Aujol-Dossal
[
5
]
and Attouch-Chbani-Riahi
[
9
]
, with the convergence rate of the objective values
\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}\Big(t^{-\frac{2\alpha}{3}}}\Big)
.

When the objective function
g
is not convex, the convergence of the trajectories generated by (HBS) is a largely open question. Recent progress has been made in
[
16
]
, where the convergence of the trajectories of a system, which can be considered as a perturbation of (HBS) has been obtained in a non-convex setting.
For other results concerning the dynamical system (HBS) and its extensions we refer to
[
9
,
19
,
21
]
.

Let us mention that for the particular case
q=\frac{p}{2},
(
1
) becomes (TRIGS) the dynamical system introduced recently in
[
12
]
and studied further in
[
6
]
.

Note that in
[
12
]
it is shown that for
\frac{2}{3}<p<2,

q=\frac{p}{2}
,
\alpha>0
and
a=1
a trajectory of the dynamical system (
1
) satisfies the following:

g(x(t))-\min g=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3p}{2}-1}}\right)
and
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.

The previous result has been improved in
[
6
]
, where the authors showed that for
0<q<1,\,p=2q,

\alpha>0
and
a=1
one has:

\displaystyle g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

\displaystyle\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{q+1}{2}}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty

and

\lim_{t\to\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.

More precisely, by denoting
x_{t}
the unique minimizer of the strongly convex function
g(x)+\frac{1}{2t^{p}}\|x\|^{2}
, it is shown in
[
6
]
that
\|x(t)-x_{t}\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{1-q}{2}}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
and this implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}{\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|}=0.

The main goal of this paper is to extend and improve the results obtained in
[
12
]
and
[
6
]
for the general case
0<q<1
and
p,\alpha,a>0.

Since our analysis shows that the parameters
q,\,p
and
a
are strongly related in order to give a better perspective of
the results obtained in this paper, we emphasize the following.

1.

If
0<q<1,\,0<p<q+1
and
x^{*}=\proj_{\argmin g}0
is the minimum norm element from
\argmin g
, then the following results hold.

(i)

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|=0.

(ii)

If
\frac{3q+1}{2}\leq p<q+1
then
\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q-p+1}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

Further, if
\frac{3q+1}{2}\leq p\leq\frac{4q+2}{3}
, then

g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
and for
\frac{4q+2}{3}<p<q+1
one has

g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{4q-2p+2}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

(iii)

If
0<p<\frac{3q+1}{2}
, then

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p+1-\max(q,p-q)}{2}}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty

and
g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

2.

If
0<q<1,\,q+1<p<2,
or
p=2
and
a\geq q(1-q)
then the trajectory
x(t)
is bounded and following results hold.

(i)

\displaystyle{\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty\mbox{ and }\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g\right)dt<+\infty}.

(ii)

\displaystyle{\|\dot{x}(t)\|=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty\mbox{ and }g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=o\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.}

(iii)

For every
x^{*}\in\argmin g
, there exists the limit
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|.
Even more, the trajectory
x(t)
converges weakly, as
t\to+\infty
, to an element of
\argmin g
.

3.

The case
p=q+1
,
0<q<1
is critical in the sense that separates the cases of weak and strong convergence of the trajectories. In this case we could not obtain any convergence result for the generated trajectories, however we show that some pointwise estimates hold.

Observe that our results considerably extend the results obtained in
[
6
]
, where only the case
q=\frac{p}{2}
was considered. Of course for this instance we reobtain the results from
[
6
]
. Further, our analysis reveals that the choice
p=2q
in the dynamical system (
1
) is not necessarily optimal. Indeed, for a fixed
q
we may obtain the convergence rate of order

O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{4q+2}{3}}}\right)
for the decay
g(x(t))-\min g
, meanwhile in
[
6
]
only the rate
O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)
has been obtained. On the other hand, if we fix
p\in]0,2[
, then for every
q\in\left]\max\left(0,\frac{2p-1}{3}\right),1\right[
one has
g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right),
hence we can choose infinitely many damping coefficients in order to obtain the same convergence rate as in case
q=\frac{p}{2}.
These features of our dynamical system (
1
) will also be underlined via some numerical experiments.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we show the weak convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
1
) to a minimizer of the objective function
g.
Pointwise and integral estimates for the velocity and decay
g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g
are also obtained. In section 3 we give sufficient conditions that assure the strong convergence of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
1
) to the minimum norm element from
\argmin g.
Pointwise estimates are also obtained under the same assumptions. In section 4 we present some numerical experiment in order to give a better insight on the behaviour of a trajectory generated by the dynamical system (
1
). Finally we conclude our paper by emphasizing some perspectives.

2
Asymptotic analysis of the trajectories generated by the dynamical system (
1
)

Existence and uniqueness of a
C^{2}([t_{0},+\infty),\mathcal{H})
global solution of the dynamical system
(
1
) can be shown via the classical Cauchy-Lipschitz-Picard theorem by rewriting
(
1
) as a first order system in the product space
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
, see Theorem
10
from Appendix. In this
section we carry out the asymptotic analysis concerning the trajectories
x(t)
generated by
the dynamical system (
1
). A new feature of our analysis is that we provide the integral estimates
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}dt<+\infty
and
\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}t^{q}\left(g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g\right)dt<+\infty.

The convergence rates

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
and
g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty
are also obtained and it is shown that the trajectory is bounded. Based on these results we are able to show for every
x^{*}\in\argmin g
the existence of the limit
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|.
Finally, we obtain ’
o
’ estimates for the decay
g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g
and the velocity
\|\dot{x}(t)\|
and we show that a trajectory converges weakly to a minimizer of the objective function
g.

In the next result we show that pointwise estimates can be obtained in the following general cases.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
0<q<1,

\alpha>0
,
0<p\leq 2
and for
p=2
one has
a\geq q(1-q)
.
Let
t_{0}>0
and for some starting points
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
let
x:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
be the unique global solution of (
1
).
Then, the following results hold.

(i)

If
0<q<\frac{p}{2}\leq 1
then
g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty\mbox{ and }\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

(ii)

If
\frac{p}{2}\leq q<1
then
g\left(x(t)\right)-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty\mbox{ and }\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p}{2}}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

Proof
.

First, let
x^{\ast}\in\argmin g
and consider
b>0
that will be adjusted later.
We denote
g^{\ast}:=g(x^{\ast})=\min g
and we introduce the energy functional
\mathcal{E}:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)=

\displaystyle t^{2q}\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+\frac{a}{2t^{p-2q}}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{b(\alpha-b-qt^{q-1})}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(2)

Then,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=2qt^{2q-1}\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+t^{2q}\langle{\nabla}g(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle

(3)

\displaystyle+\frac{a(2q-p)t^{2q-p-1}}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}+at^{2q-p}\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)\rangle+\langle(b+qt^{q-1})\dot{x}(t)+t^{q}\ddot{x}(t),b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle+b(\alpha-b-qt^{q-1})\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{bq(1-q)t^{q-2}}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

From the dynamical system (
1
), we have that

\displaystyle\ddot{x}(t)=-\frac{a}{t^{p}}x(t)-\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t)-\nabla g(x(t)),

(4)

hence

\displaystyle\langle(b+qt^{q-1})\dot{x}(t)+t^{q}\ddot{x}(t),b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\rangle=

(5)

\displaystyle\langle(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha)\dot{x}(t)-at^{q-p}x(t)-t^{q}\nabla g(x(t)),b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle b(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha)\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha)t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle-abt^{q-p}\langle x(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle-at^{2q-p}\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle-bt^{q}\langle{\nabla}g(x(t)),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle-t^{2q}\langle\nabla g(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

Combining (
3
) and (
5
) we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=2qt^{2q-1}\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha)t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{a(2q-p)t^{2q-p-1}}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle-bt^{q}\langle{\nabla}g(x(t))+at^{-p}x(t),x(t)-x^{\ast}\rangle+\frac{bq(1-q)t^{q-2}}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Consider now the strongly convex function

g_{t}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\,g_{t}(x)=g(x)+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x\|^{2}.

From the gradient inequality we have

g_{t}(y)-g_{t}(x)\geq\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x-y\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Take now
y=x^{*}
and
x=x(t).
We get

\displaystyle g(x^{*})+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-g(x(t))-\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x(t)\|^{2}\geq

\displaystyle-\langle{\nabla}g(x(t))+at^{-p}x(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle-bt^{q}\langle{\nabla}g(x(t))+at^{-p}x(t),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq bt^{q}(g(x^{*})-g(x(t)))+\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle-\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

By inserting (
7
) in (
6
) we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=(2qt^{2q-1}-bt^{q})\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha)t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{a(2q-p)t^{2q-p-1}-abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle+\frac{bq(1-q)t^{q-2}-abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

Since

\frac{1}{2}\|b(x(t)-x^{\ast})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\leq b^{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+t^{2q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)\leq

\displaystyle t^{2q}\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+\frac{a}{2t^{p-2q}}\|x(t)\|^{2}+t^{2q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{b(\alpha+b-qt^{q-1})}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.

(9)

Let
r=\max(q,p-q)
and consider
K>0
that will be defined in what follows.
Now, by multiplying (
9
) with
\frac{K}{t^{r}}
and adding to (
8
) we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)+\frac{K}{t^{r}}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq(2qt^{2q-1}-bt^{q}+Kt^{2q-r})\left(g(x(t))-g^{\ast}\right)+(b+qt^{q-1}-\alpha+Kt^{q-r})t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle+\frac{a(2q-p)t^{2q-p-1}-abt^{q-p}+aKt^{2q-p-r}}{2}\|x(t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{bq(1-q)t^{q-2}-abt^{q-p}+Kb(\alpha+b-qt^{q-1})t^{-r}}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}.

Now, take
0<b<\alpha
. If
p<2
consider
0<K<\min\left(b,\alpha-b,\frac{a}{\alpha+b}\right).
If
p=2
then by hypotheses we have
a>q(1-q)
, so take
0<K<\min\left(b,\alpha-b,\frac{a-q(1-q)}{\alpha+b}\right).

Then, easily can be observed that there exists
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)+\frac{K}{t^{r}}\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{abt^{q-p}}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1}.

(11)

Now, since
r=\max(q,p-q)
and
0<q<1
we can take
r=1
provided
p=q+1.
In this case, by multiplying (
11
) with

t^{K}
we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{K}\mathcal{E}(t)\right)\leq\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2}t^{K-1}\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1}.

By integrating the latter relation on an interval
[t_{1},T],\,T>t_{1}
we conclude that there exists
C>0
such that

T^{K}\mathcal{E}(T)\leq\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2K}T^{K}+C.

Consequently,

\mathcal{E}(T)\leq\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2K}+\frac{C}{T^{K}}\leq C_{1}\mbox{ for some }C_{1}>0,

hence for all
t\geq t_{1}
one has

g(x(t))-g^{*}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t^{2q}}\mbox{ and }\|\dot{x}(t)\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{C_{1}}}{t^{q}}.

In other words

g(x(t))-g^{*}=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q}}\right)\mbox{ and }\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{q}}\right),\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

(12)

In the case
r\neq 1
, by multiplying (
11
) with

e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}
we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\mathcal{E}(t)\right)\leq\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2}t^{q-p}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1}.

(13)

Observe further, that for all
t\geq t_{1}
one has

\frac{1}{K}\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{q-p+r}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\right)=\frac{1}{K}\left((q-p+r)t^{q-p+r-1}+Kt^{q-p}\right)e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\geq t^{q-p}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}.

Combining the latter relation with (
13
) we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\mathcal{E}(t)\right)\leq\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2K}\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{q-p+r}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t^{1-r}}\right)\mbox{ for all }t\geq t_{1}.

(14)

By integrating (
14
) on an interval
[t_{1},T],\,T>t_{1}
we conclude that

e^{\frac{K}{1-r}T^{1-r}}\mathcal{E}(T)\leq C_{1}T^{q-p+r}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}T^{1-r}}+C_{2},

where
C_{1}=\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2K}
and
C_{2}=e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t_{1}^{1-r}}\mathcal{E}(t_{1})-\frac{ab\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2K}t_{1}^{q-p+r}e^{\frac{K}{1-r}t_{1}^{1-r}}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{E}(T)\leq C_{1}T^{q-p+r}+\frac{C_{2}}{e^{\frac{K}{1-r}T^{1-r}}}\leq C_{3}T^{q-p+r}\mbox{ for some }C_{3}>0,

hence for all
t\geq t_{1}
one has

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
q+1<p\leq 2
and for
p=2
one has
a\geq q(1-q).

For some starting points
u_{0},v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
let
x:[t_{0},\infty)\to\mathcal{H}
be the unique global solution of (
1
). Let

Then, there exists the limit
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|.

Proof
.

The proof is based on Lemma
13
. Indeed, for
x^{*}\in\argmin g
consider the function
w(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}.
Then, by using (
1

\ddot{w}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{w}(t)=\left\langle\ddot{x}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}=\left\langle-{\nabla}g({x}(t))-\frac{a}{t^{p}}{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}

Now, from the monotonicity of
{\nabla}g
we have
\langle-{\nabla}g({x}(t)),x(t)-x^{*}\rangle\leq 0
and

\left\langle-\frac{a}{t^{p}}{x}(t),x(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle=\frac{a}{2t^{p}}(\|x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x(t)\|^{2}-\|x(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}).

Consequently,

\ddot{w}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\dot{w}(t)\leq\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

(20)

We show that Lemma
13
can be applied with
p(t)=1,\,q(t)=\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}
and
k(t)=\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}.

Note that since
p>q+1
and according to Theorem
4
it holds that
t^{q}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[,
we have

t^{q}k(t)\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[.

(21)

Now, if
0<q<1
then
\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds=\frac{\alpha}{1-q}(t^{1-q}-t_{0}^{1-q}),
hence

\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)=C_{1}e^{C_{2}t^{1-q}},
where
C_{1}=e^{-\frac{\alpha}{1-q}t_{0}^{1-q}}
and
C_{2}=\frac{\alpha}{1-q}.

Consequently,

\frac{1}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[.

(22)

Further

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{dT}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\right)\frac{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}{p(t)}k(t)=\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-C_{2}T^{1-q}}dTe^{C_{2}t^{1-q}}k(t).

(23)

Consider the integral

I_{t}=\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-C_{2}T^{1-q}}dT.
By substituting
C_{2}T^{1-q}=\theta
we obtain
T=C_{2}^{-\frac{1}{1-q}}\theta^{\frac{1}{1-q}},
hence

dT=C_{3}\theta^{\frac{q}{1-q}}d\theta,
where
C_{3}=\frac{C_{2}^{-\frac{1}{1-q}}}{1-q}.
Consequently,

I_{t}=C_{3}\int_{C_{2}t^{1-q}}^{+\infty}\theta^{\frac{q}{1-q}}e^{-\theta}d\theta=C_{3}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{1-q},C_{2}t^{1-q}\right),

where
\Gamma(s,x)=\int_{x}^{+\infty}\theta^{s-1}e^{-\theta}d\theta
is the upper incomplete gamma function. It is well known (see
[
2
]
) that

\frac{\Gamma(s,x)}{x^{s-1}e^{-x}}\to 1\mbox{ as }x\to+\infty.

Consequently,

\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{1-q},C_{2}t^{1-q}\right)}{(C_{2}t^{1-q})^{\frac{q}{1-q}}e^{-C_{2}t^{1-q}}}\to 1,\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty,

which shows that there exists
C_{4}>0
such that

\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-C_{2}T^{1-q}}dTe^{C_{2}t^{1-q}}\leq C_{4}t^{q}.

Now, combining the previous relation with (
21
) and (

\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{dT}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\right)\frac{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}{p(t)}k(t)\leq C_{4}t^{q}k(t)\in L^{1}[t_{0},+\infty[.

(24)

Hence, according to Lemma
13
, there exists the limit
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|x(t)-x^{*}\|.

3
Strong convergence

We continue the present section by emphasizing the main idea behind the Tikhonov regularization, which will generate strong convergence results for our dynamical system (
1
) to a minimizer of the objective function of minimal norm. By
x_{t}
we denote the unique solution of the strongly convex minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left(g(x)+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x\|^{2}\right).

We know, (see for instance
[
10
]
), that the Tikhonov approximation curve
t\to x_{t}
satisfies
x^{\ast}=\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}x_{t}
, where
x^{\ast}=\argmin\limits_{x\in\argmin g}\|x\|
is the minimal norm element from the set
\argmin g.
Obviously,
\{x^{*}\}=\proj_{\argmin g}0
and we have the inequality
\|x_{t}\|\leq\|x^{\ast}\|
(see
[
17
]
).

Since
x_{t}
is the unique minimum of the strongly convex function
g_{t}(x)=g(x)+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x\|^{2},
obviously one has

{\nabla}g_{t}(x_{t})={\nabla}g(x_{t})+\frac{a}{t^{p}}x_{t}=0.

(31)

Further, according to Lemma 2 from
[
6
]
the function
t\longrightarrow x_{t}
is derivable almost everywhere and one has

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}x_{t}\right\|\leq\frac{p}{t}\|x_{t}\|\mbox{ for almost every}t\geq t_{0}.

(32)

Note that since
g_{t}
is strongly convex, from the gradient inequality we have

g_{t}(y)-g_{t}(x)\geq\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x-y\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(33)

In particular

g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t})\geq\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x-x_{t}\|^{2},\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}.

(34)

Let
y:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H},s\longrightarrow y(s)
derivable at a point
t\in]t_{0},+\infty[.
Then it is obvious that

\frac{d}{dt}g_{t}(y(t))=\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(y(t)),\dot{y}(t)\rangle-\frac{ap}{2t^{p+1}}\|y(t)\|^{2}.

(35)

Finally, for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
, one has

g(x)-g(y)=(g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t}))+(g_{t}(x_{t})-g_{t}(y))+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}(\|y\|^{2}-\|x\|^{2})\leq g_{t}(x)-g_{t}(x_{t})+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|y\|^{2}.

(36)

Now, in order to show the strong convergence of the dynamical system (
1
) to an element of minimum norm of the nonempty, convex and closed set
\argmin g
, we state our main result of the present section.

Theorem 8
.

Assume that
0<q<1,\,0<p<q+1
and let
x
be the unique global solution of (
1
). Let
x^{*}=\proj_{\argmin g}0.

Then,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-x^{\ast}\|=0

(i)

\frac{3q+1}{2}\leq p<q+1

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2q-p+1}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty

If
0<p<\frac{3q+1}{2}

\|\dot{x}(t)\|=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{p+1-\max(q,p-q)}{2}}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

Further,
g(x(t))-\min g=O\left(\frac{1}{t^{p}}\right)\mbox{ as }t\to+\infty.

.

Consider
\alpha>b>0
and define, for every
t\geq t_{0}
, the following energy functional

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=t^{2q}(g_{t}(x(t))-g_{t}(x_{t}))+\frac{1}{2}\|b(x(t)-x_{t})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{b(\alpha-b-qt^{q-1})}{2}\|x(t)-x_{t}\|^{2}.

(37)

Obviously, there exists

for all
t\geq t_{1}

for all
t\geq t_{1}.

Now, by using (
35

{\nabla}g_{t}(x_{t})=0

\displaystyle\dot{E}(t)

\displaystyle=2qt^{2q-1}(g_{t}(x(t))-g_{t}(x_{t}))+t^{2q}\left(\langle{\nabla}g_{t}(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle-\frac{ap}{2t^{p+1}}\|x(t)\|^{2}+\frac{ap}{2t^{p+1}}\|x_{t}\|^{2}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle+\left\langle b\left(\dot{x}(t)-\frac{d}{dt}x_{t}\right)+qt^{q-1}\dot{x}(t)+t^{q}\ddot{x}(t),b(x(t)-x_{t})+t^{q}\dot{x}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{b(q(1-q)t^{q-2})}{2}\|x(t)-x_{t}\|^{2}+b(\alpha-b-qt^{q-1})\left\langle\dot{x}(t)-\frac{d}{dt}x_{t},x(t)-x_{t}\right\rangle.

Proceeding as in (

A.1
Existence and uniqueness for the Cauchy problem

Let us first show that the solution for (
1
) is well posed.

Theorem 10
.

Given
(x_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique global classical solution
x:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathcal{H}
of the dynamical system (
1
).

Proof
.

The proof relies on the combination of the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem with energy estimates.
First consider the Hamitonian formulation of (
1
) as the first order system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)-y(t)=0\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\dot{y}(t)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}y(t)+\nabla g\left(x(t)\right)+\frac{a}{t^{p}}x(t)=0\vskip 2.84526pt\\
x(t_{0})=u_{0},\,y(t_{0})=v_{0}.\end{cases}

(57)

Taking into account the hypotheses and by applying the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem in the locally Lipschitz case, we obtain the existence and uniqueness of a local solution.
Then, in order to pass from a local solution to a global solution, we rely on the energy estimate obtained by taking the scalar product of (
1
) with
\dot{x}(t)
. It gives

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+g(x(t))+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x(t)\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{t^{q}}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{ap}{2t^{p+1}}\|x(t)\|^{2}=0.

Obviously, the energy function
t\mapsto W(t)
is decreasing where

W(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+g(x(t))+\frac{a}{2t^{p}}\|x(t)\|^{2}.

The end of the proof follows a standard argument. Take a maximal solution defined on an interval
[t_{0},T_{\max}[
. If
T_{\max}
is infinite, the
proof is over. Otherwise, if
T_{\max}
is finite, according to the above energy estimate, we have that
\|\dot{x}(t)\|
remains bounded.
Let
\|\dot{x}_{\infty}\|=\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T_{\max})}\|\dot{x}(t)\|.
Since
\|x(t)-x(t^{\prime})\|\leq\|\dot{x}_{\infty}\||t-t^{\prime}|
, we get that
\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}x(t):=x_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}
. By (
1
) the map
\ddot{x}
is also bounded on the interval
[t_{0},T_{\max})
and under the same argument as before

\lim_{t\to T_{\max}}\dot{x}(t):=x_{\infty}
exists. Applying the local existence theorem with initial data
(x_{\infty},\dot{x}_{\infty})
, we can extend the maximal solution to a strictly larger interval, a clear contradiction. Hence
T_{\max}=+\infty,
which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

Appendix B
Auxiliary results

In this appendix, we collect some lemmas and technical results which we will use in the analysis of the dynamical system (
1

The following statement is the continuous counterpart of a convergence result
of quasi-Fejér monotone sequences. For its proofs we refer to
[
1

.

Lemma 11
.

Suppose that
F:[t_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally absolutely continuous and bounded from below and that
there exists
G\in L^{1}([t_{0},+\infty))
such that

\frac{d}{dt}F(t)\leq G(t)

for almost every
t\in[t_{0},+\infty)
. Then there exists
\lim_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}F(t)\in\mathbb{R}
.

The continuous version of the Opial Lemma (see
[
7
]
) is the main tool for proving weak convergence for the generated trajectory.

Lemma 12
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be a nonempty set and
x:[t_{0},+\infty)\to H
a given map such that:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\mbox{for every }z\in S\ \mbox{the limit}\ \lim\limits_{t\longrightarrow+\infty}\|x(t)-z\|\ \mbox{exists};

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\text{every weak sequential limit point of }x(t)\text{ belongs to the set }S.

Then the trajectory
x(t)
converges weakly to an element in
S
as
t\to+\infty
.

Inspired from
[
14
]
Lemma A.6, we have the following result.

Lemma 13
.

Let
t_{0}>0
, and let
w:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuously differentiable function which
is bounded from below. Consider
p,q,k:[t_{0},+\infty[\to\mathbb{R}
nonnegative functions and assume that the function
\frac{1}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\in L^{1}(t_{0},+\infty[
and

\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{dT}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\right)\frac{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}{p(t)}k(t)\in L^{1}(t_{0},+\infty[.

Assume further, that

p(t)\ddot{w}(t)+q(t)\dot{w}(t)\leq k(t)

(58)

for some
\alpha>0
, almost every
t>t_{0}
. Then,
the positive part
[\dot{w}]_{+}
of
\dot{w}
belongs to
L^{1}(t_{0},+\infty[
, and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}w(t)
exists.

Proof
.

By multiplying (
58
) with
\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)
we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)\dot{w}(t)\right]\leq\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)k(t).

By integrating the above relation on an interval
[t_{0},T]
we obtain

\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)\dot{w}(T)\leq\dot{w}(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)k(t)dt.

(59)

Consequently,

[\dot{w}]_{+}(T)\leq\frac{|\dot{w}(t_{0})|}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}+\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)k(t)dt}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)},

(60)

hence

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}[\dot{w}]_{+}(T)dT\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{|\dot{w}(t_{0})|}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}dT+\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)k(t)dt}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}dT.

(61)

Now, from the hypotheses we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{|\dot{w}(t_{0})|}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}dT<+\infty.

According to Fubini’s theorem

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{1}{p(t)}\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)k(t)dt}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}dT=\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{dT}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\right)\frac{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}{p(t)}k(t)dt.

But, according to the hypotheses

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}\frac{dT}{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{T}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}\right)\frac{\exp\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{q(s)}{p(s)}ds\right)}{p(t)}k(t)dt<+\infty,

consequently

\int_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}[\dot{w}]_{+}(T)dT<+\infty.

This implies that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}w(t)
exists.

\blacksquare

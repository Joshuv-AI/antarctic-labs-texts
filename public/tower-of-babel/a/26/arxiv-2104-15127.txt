Spiked Singular Values and Vectors Under Extreme Aspect Ratios
Michael J. Feldman

Abstract
The behavior of the leading singular values and vectors of noisy low-rank matrices is fundamental to many statistical and scientific problems. Theoretical understanding currently derives from asymptotic analysis under one of two regimes: (1) the classical regime, with a fixed number of rows and large number of columns, or vice versa, and (2) the proportional regime, with large numbers of rows and columns, proportional to one another. This paper is concerned with the disproportional regime, where the matrix is either “tall and narrow” or “short and wide”: we study sequences of matrices of size n × m n n\times m_{n} with aspect ratio n / m n → 0 n/m_{n}\rightarrow 0 or n / m n → ∞ n/m_{n}\rightarrow\infty as n → ∞ n\rightarrow\infty . This regime has important “big data” applications. Theory derived here shows that the displacement of the empirical singular values and vectors from their noise-free counterparts and the associated phase transitions—well-known under proportional growth asymptotics—still occur in the disproportionate setting. They must be quantified, however, on a novel scale of measurement that adjusts with the changing aspect ratio as the matrix size increases. In this setting, the top singular vectors corresponding to the longer of the two matrix dimensions are asymptotically uncorrelated with the noise-free signal.



1
Introduction

The low-rank signal-plus-noise model is a simple statistical model of data with latent low-rank structure. Data
\widetilde{X}_{n}
is the sum of a low-rank matrix and white noise:

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{m_n}}$}}\widetilde{X}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\theta_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{m_n}}$}}X_{n}\,,

(1.1)

where
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are signal strengths,
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
are the left and right signal vectors, and the noise matrix
X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m_{n}}
contains independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) entries with mean zero and variance one. The noise matrix is normalized so that rows are asymptotically unit norm.

Proportionate growth asymptotic

Recent work studies this model in the high-dimensional setting where

and

are of comparable magnitude. Such analyses derive limiting behavior of sequences

as

\phantom{\,,}n,m_{n}\rightarrow\infty\,,\,\,\,\,\frac{n}{m_{n}}\rightarrow\beta>0\,.

Here, the parameter
\beta>0
is the limiting aspect ratio of the data; thus for
\beta=1

[
7
]
, Baik and Silverstein
[
8
]

[
22

study eigenvalues of the spiked covariance model, which is closely related to model (

Benaych-Georges and Nadakuditi
[
12

derive asymptotic properties of the singular value decomposition of

(details of model (
1.1

asymptotics:

Leading eigenvalue displacement
. Let
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
denote the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{S}_{n}=\frac{1}{m_{n}}\widetilde{X}_{n}\widetilde{X}_{n}^{\top}
, the sample covariance matrix. We assume signal strengths
\theta_{1}>\cdots>\theta_{r}
are constant and distinct. The leading eigenvalues of
\widetilde{S}_{n}
are inconsistent estimators of the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{E}\widetilde{S}_{n}
: for fixed

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{a.s.}\begin{cases}\frac{(1+\theta_{i}^{2})(\beta+\theta_{i}^{2})}{\theta_{i}^{2}}\,,&i\leq r,\,\theta_{i}>\beta^{1/4}\\
(1+\sqrt{\beta})^{2}\,,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,.

The singular values of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{n}}}\widetilde{X}_{n}
are of course determined by the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{S}_{n}
, and vice versa. We will occasionally switch between the two without comment.

Leading singular vector inconsistency
. Let
\tilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{u}_{n}
and
\tilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{m}
denote the left and right singular vectors of
\widetilde{X}_{n}
. The leading singular vectors
\tilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{u}_{r}
and
\tilde{v}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{r}
are inconsistent estimators of the left and right signal vectors. For
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq r
,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1-\frac{\beta(1+\theta_{i}^{2})}{\theta_{i}^{2}(\theta_{i}^{2}+\beta)}\,,\quad|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{j}\rangle|^{2}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1-\frac{\beta+\theta_{i}^{2}}{\theta_{i}^{2}(\theta_{i}^{2}+1)}\,,

\displaystyle i=j,\,\theta_{i}>\beta^{1/4}\,,

and
|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|,|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{j}\rangle|\xrightarrow{a.s.}0
otherwise.

1.2
Disproportionate growth asymptotic

Contrary to the proportionate growth asymptotic, the dimensions of many large data matrices are not comparable. For example, Novembre et al.
[
21
]
demonstrate a genetic dataset with 3,000 rows (people) and 250,000 columns (genetic measurements) has low-rank structure. Here the aspect ratio is
\beta\approx 3/250
.

This paper considers the signal-plus-noise model under disproportional growth:

\phantom{\,.}n,m_{n}\rightarrow\infty\,,\,\,\,\,\beta_{n}=n/m_{n}\rightarrow 0\,.

Transposing
\widetilde{X}_{n}
if necessary, our results also apply to
\beta_{n}\rightarrow\infty
.
Substitution of
\beta=0
into the above proportional-limit formulas heuristically suggests
\lambda_{i}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1+\theta_{i}^{2}
and
|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1
(right signal vectors are partially recovered). Indeed, these formulas are corollaries of Theorems 2.9 and 2.10 of
[
12
]
. Thus, under
\beta_{n}\rightarrow 0
and constant signal strengths, leading eigenvalues of
\widetilde{S}_{n}
consistently estimate those of
\mathbf{E}\widetilde{S}_{n}
, and left singular vectors
\tilde{u}_{i}
, corresponding to the shorter matrix dimension, consistently estimate left signal vectors
u_{i}
. In particular, leading eigenvalue displacement and (left) singular vector inconsistency no longer occur, and seemingly no phase transition exists.

Our main contribution is the discovery of a vanishingly small phase transition located at
\beta_{n}^{1/4}
. Signal strengths
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i,n}
, hitherto fixed, now vanish. For signal strengths above this “microscopic” threshold, left singular vectors partially recover their signal counterparts—enabling signal estimation at signal-to-noise ratios previously thought insufficiently strong. Right singular vectors, corresponding to the longer matrix dimension, are asymptotically uncorrelated with the signal.

We introduce a particular calibration
{\theta_{n}=\tau\cdot\beta_{n}^{1/4}}
between signal strength
\theta_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
depending on a new (constant) formal parameter
\tau
. Under this calibration, we rigorously establish a new set of formulas for the limiting displacement of singular values and inconsistency of singular vectors, in explicit terms of the parameter
\tau
. These results may be heuristically derived by substitution of
\theta_{n}=\tau\cdot\beta_{n}^{1/4}
into proportional formulas and taking limits. Many important applications previously made under proportional growth may in parallel fashion now be rigorously made under disproportional growth. For example, in the high-dimensional Gaussian mixture model with many more parameters
m
than samples
n
, our theory gives a phase transition for recovery of length-
n
signal vectors (encoding cluster membership). The optimal singular value hard-thresholding level for low-rank matrix recovery as
\beta_{n}\rightarrow 0
may be calculated, analogous to proportional results of Donoho and Gavish
[
17
]
. Furthermore, there are new potential uses. Spiked tensors closely relate to the disproportionate asymptotic: Montanari and Richard propose a tensor unfolding algorithm
[
20
]
that produces noisy low-rank matrices of size
n\times n^{k}
,
k\geq 2
(equivalently,
\beta_{n}=n^{-(k-1)})
. Precise asymptotic analysis of tensor unfolding is made possible by our results.

The related spiked covariance model is studied in the disproportionate asymptotic by Bloemendal, Knowles, Yau, and Yin
[
13
]
. An advantage of this work is that
\beta_{n}
is permitted to vanish or diverge at any rate, while
[
13
]
requires the existence of a constant
k>0
such that
n^{1/k}\leq m_{n}\leq n^{k}
.

(B2. Signal vectors) The signal rank

Results

.

Let

2&i>i_{0}\end{cases}\,.

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}(z)
are degenerate, as all noise eigenvalues converge to one. The upper edge of the support of

lies approximately at

. Theorem

tells us the leading eigenvalues of

may still emerge from the bulk, but only by a multiple of

. Phenomena of interest are no longer made visible by taking limits, but must be captured by detailed finite-

to

, developed in Section

and
1.2
are refinements of the proofs of Theorems 2.9 and 2.10 of
[

]
and Lemma 6.1 of
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.4

Let

, respectively.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma

(2.10)

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\eta>0
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}=\{z:\Re(z)\geq 1+(2+\eta)\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\,|\bar{s}_{n}(z)|\lesssim\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\,,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\,\Big|\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(z)\Big|\lesssim\beta_{n}^{-1}\,.

Furthermore, almost surely,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\,|s_{n}(z)|\lesssim\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\,,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\,\Big|\frac{d}{dz}s_{n}(z)\Big|\lesssim\beta_{n}^{-1}\,.

The implied coefficients depend on
\eta
only.

Proof.

As the upper edge of the support of the Marchenko-Pasteur law lies at
(1+\sqrt{\beta_{n}})^{2}
,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\inf_{\lambda\in\text{supp}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}}|\lambda-z|\gtrsim\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\,,

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\inf_{\lambda\in\text{supp}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}}|\lambda-z|^{2}\gtrsim\beta_{n}\,.

(2.11)

The first claims of the lemma now follow from the integral representations

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\bar{s}_{n}(z)=\int\frac{1}{\lambda-z}d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}(\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(z)=-\int\frac{1}{(\lambda-z)^{2}}d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}(\lambda)\,.

By (
2.10
), almost surely eventually,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq n}|\lambda_{\alpha}-z|\geq\frac{\eta\sqrt{\beta_{n}}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\eta,n}}\min_{1\leq\alpha\leq n}|\lambda_{\alpha}-z|^{2}\geq\frac{\eta^{2}\beta_{n}}{4}\,.

(2.12)

Together with

\displaystyle s_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\alpha}-z}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}s_{n}(z)=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{\alpha}-z)^{2}}\,,

we obtain the second group of claims.
∎

Proof.

The argument is presented under assumptions (A1, A2, A3) for the diagonal of

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}(z)-M_{n}(z)

Relation (
2.13
): the upper left block
. For

Hence, almost surely,

\displaystyle\Big|\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{z}}$}}\widetilde{M}_{n}(z)-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{z'}}$}}\widetilde{M}_{n}(z^{\prime})\Big)_{i,i}\Big|

(2.18)

A.4

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\mathbf{E}|u_{i}^{\top}(S_{n}-z^{\prime}I_{n})^{-1}u_{i}-s_{n}(z^{\prime})|^{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{n^{4}}\mathbf{E}|\mathrm{tr}(S_{n}-z^{\prime}I_{n})^{-1}(S_{n}-\bar{z}^{\prime}I_{n})^{-1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n^{4}}\mathbf{E}\mathrm{tr}\big((S_{n}-z^{\prime}I_{n})^{-1}(S_{n}-\bar{z}^{\prime}I_{n})^{-1}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\,\frac{2}{n^{4}}\mathbf{E}\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{\alpha}-z^{\prime}|^{2}}\bigg)^{2}\,.

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\Big|\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{z'}}$}}\big(\widetilde{M}_{n}(z^{\prime})-M_{n}(z^{\prime})\big)_{i,i}\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{tr}\big((S_{n}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}I_{n})^{-1}+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}(S_{n}-\tilde{\lambda}_{i}I_{n})^{-2}\big)=\beta_{n}\Big(s_{n}(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})+\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}s_{n}(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\,,

(
2.25
), (
2.27
), and (

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{r}\theta_{j}\theta_{k}\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{k}\rangle\Big(\delta_{jk}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)+O(n^{-\ell})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\theta_{j}^{2}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)+O(n^{-\ell}\sqrt{\beta_{n}})\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}|c_{n}|

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\ell}\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{r}\theta_{j}\theta_{k}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{k}\rangle|\lesssim n^{-\ell}\,.

(4.10)

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}\theta_{j}^{2}|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{j}\rangle|^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\theta_{j}^{2}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)=1+O\big(n^{-\ell}(1+\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\|V_{n}^{\top}\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{1}^{2})\big)\,.

(4.11)

Terms on the left-hand side with
j\neq i
are negligible: by (

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{j\neq i}\theta_{j}^{2}|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{j}\rangle|^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\bigg|\lesssim n^{-4\ell}(\beta_{n}^{-1/2}|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}+\sqrt{\beta_{n}})\,,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{j\neq i}\theta_{j}^{2}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{j}\rangle|^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\bigg|\lesssim\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\,,

implying

\displaystyle\theta_{i}^{2}|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\theta_{i}^{2}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{i}\rangle|^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)=1+O\big(n^{-\ell}(1+\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\|V_{n}^{\top}\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{1}^{2})+\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\big)\,.

(4.12)

Substitution of (

) into (

\displaystyle|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}\Big(\theta_{i}^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\theta_{i}^{4}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}\bigg(\theta_{i}^{2}\int\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{i}}{(t-\bar{\lambda}_{i})^{2}}d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}(t)+\theta_{i}^{4}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})^{2}\beta_{n}\int\frac{t}{(t-\bar{\lambda}_{i})^{2}}d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n}(t)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle 1+O\big(n^{-\ell}(1+\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\|V_{n}^{\top}\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{1}^{2})+\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\big)\,.

(4.13)

[

]
. In the regime where

and
\theta_{i}>1
is constant, the term multiplying

converges to a positive limit, and
|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\phantom{}\Big|\theta_{i}^{2}|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{i}\rangle|^{2}\beta_{n}\Big(\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})+\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Big)\Big|\lesssim\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\theta_{i}^{2}|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})=1+O\big(n^{-\ell}(1+\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\|V_{n}^{\top}\tilde{v}_{i}\|_{1}^{2})+\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\big)\,.

\displaystyle(1-Cn^{-\ell})|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|^{2}\lesssim\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\,,

\displaystyle|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle|\lesssim\beta_{n}^{1/4}\,,

\displaystyle|\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{j}\rangle|\lesssim n^{-2\ell}\beta_{n}^{1/4}\,.

Now, by (

\displaystyle|\langle\tilde{u}_{i},u_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\bar{\sigma}_{i}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})\theta_{i}\langle\tilde{v}_{i},v_{i}\rangle)^{2}+O(n^{-4\ell})=\frac{\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})^{2}}{\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})}+O(n^{-\ell}+\sqrt{\beta_{n}})\,.

Direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\bar{s}_{n}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})=-\frac{1}{2\beta_{n}\bar{\lambda}_{i}}\bigg(1-\frac{\tau_{i}^{2}+\tau_{i}^{-2}}{\sqrt{(\tau_{i}^{2}+\tau_{i}^{-2})^{2}-4}}\bigg)+O(\beta_{n}^{-1/2})\,,

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\eta>0
and

be a bounded sequence. For any
\ell<1/4
,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\sup_{|z-u_{n}|\leq n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\beta_{n}}}\,|s_{n}(z)-\bar{s}_{n}(z)|\lesssim n^{-\ell}\beta_{n}^{-1/2}\,.

(5.1)

.

Let
\eta>0
and

be a bounded sequence. For any

,

These bounds are novel in that (1) dependence on

is more carefully tracked and (2) the bound applies to

(rather than to

. For example,

\phantom{\,,}\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrm{tr}(S_{n}-zI_{n})^{-1}(S_{n}-\bar{z}I_{n})^{-1}\leq\Im(z)^{-2}\,,

we will argue using Lemma

that

, the entries of

are assumed truncated and normalized as in Lemma

Let
x_{k}^{\top}
denote the
k

,

n-1
rows, and
\mathbf{E}_{k}(\cdot)
denote the conditional expectation given
\{x_{ij},i\leq k,j\leq m\}
. Additionally, we define

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}s_{kk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x_{kj}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle S_{nk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}X_{nk}X_{nk}^{\top}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=X_{nk}x_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(S_{n}-zI_{n})^{-1}-\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})\sigma_{k}\,,

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z-\beta_{n}-\frac{z}{m}\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\tilde{b}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s_{kk}-z-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\alpha_{k}}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z-\beta_{n}-\beta_{n}z\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\epsilon_{k}

\displaystyle=s_{kk}-1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\alpha_{k}+\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}S_{nk}+\frac{1}{m}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}

\displaystyle=s_{kk}-1+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n}z\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\alpha_{k}\,.

(5.3)

Standard properties of these definitions are stated below (see Theorem A.5, Lemma 8.18, and (8.4.19) of
[
4
]
).

Lemma 5.2
.

For

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}}{s_{kk}-z-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\alpha_{k}}\,,

\displaystyle|\sigma_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Im(z)}\,,

\displaystyle|\tilde{b}_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Im(z)}\,,

\displaystyle|\bar{b}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta_{n}|z|}}\,.

Furthermore, for
\Im(z)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
,
|b_{k}|\lesssim(\beta_{n}|z|)^{-1/2}
.

In the following lemmas, we will use
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
to refer to the real and imaginary parts of a complex sequence
z_{n}
. The subscripts will be suppressed for notational lightness. Lemmas
5.3
and
5.4
respectively bound
|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}\bar{s}_{n}(z)|
and
|\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\bar{s}_{n}(z)|
for

with imaginary part at least

. The proof of Theorem

extends the bound to the real axis.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
z=u+iv
be a bounded sequence where
u\geq 1+(2+\eta)\sqrt{\beta_{n}}
and
v\geq 1/\sqrt{m}
. Then,

\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}+\frac{1}{v^{2}}\Big)\,.

Proof.

Begin with a standard decomposition of
s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)
:

\displaystyle s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})\big(\mathrm{tr}(S_{n}-zI_{n})^{-1}-\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,.

As
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
forms a martingale difference sequence, the Burkholder inequality (Lemma 2.12 of
[
4
]
) yields

\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}|\gamma_{k}|^{2}\,.

This martingale decomposition, together with the bound
|\gamma_{k}|\leq 2/v
, appear in proofs of convergence to the Marchenko-Pastur distribution as
\beta_{n}\rightarrow\beta>0

[
1
,
6
]
. Such proofs consider
z
fixed. Here and in
[
2
,
4
]
, as
v
may decay rapidly, a tighter bound on the moments of
\gamma_{k}
is needed. Using the identity

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}S_{nk}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}=(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}S_{nk}=I_{n-1}+z(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\,,

(5.4)

we obtain

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\tilde{b}_{k}^{-1}-b_{k}^{-1}=\epsilon_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{k}=\frac{b_{k}}{1+b_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{k}}{1+b_{k}\epsilon_{k}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}\Big)=-\sigma_{k}b_{k}\epsilon_{k}+b_{k}\Big(1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=-(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})\sigma_{k}b_{k}\epsilon_{k}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})b_{k}\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=-(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})\sigma_{k}b_{k}\epsilon_{k}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\mathbf{E}_{k}b_{k}\big[\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}-\mathrm{tr}(X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}X_{nk})\big]\,.

(5.5)

Here, we have used that
b_{k}
does not depend on
x_{k}
, so
(\mathbf{E}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{k-1})b_{k}=0
. By Lemmas
5.2
and
A.4
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m^{4}}\mathbf{E}b_{k}^{2}\big|\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-\,zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}-\,\mathrm{tr}(X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}X_{nk})\big|^{2}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{m^{4}\beta_{n}}\,\mathbf{E}\mathrm{tr}\,X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}X_{nk}(X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-\bar{z}I_{n-1})^{-2}X_{nk})^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{nm}\mathbf{E}\mathrm{tr}\,S_{nk}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}S_{nk}(S_{nk}-\bar{z}I_{n-1})^{-2}=\frac{1}{m}\mathbf{E}\bigg(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda_{k,i}^{2}}{|\lambda_{k,i}-z|^{4}}\bigg)\,,

(5.7)

where
\lambda_{k,1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{k,n-1}
denote the eigenvalues of
S_{nk}
. By (
2.10
) and a union bound,

\phantom{\,,}\Pr\big(\lambda_{k,1}\geq 1+(2+\eta/2)\sqrt{\beta_{n}}\text{ for some }k\big)\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\,.

On the complement of this event, the integrand of (
5.7

16|z|/(\eta\sqrt{\beta_{n}})^{4}
. In addition,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{k,i}^{2}}{|\lambda_{k,i}-z|^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{u^{2}}{v^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{k,i}^{2}}{|\lambda_{k,i}-z|^{4}}\leq\frac{|z|^{2}}{v^{4}}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\frac{1}{m^{4}}\mathbf{E}b_{k}^{2}\big|\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}\alpha_{k}-\mathrm{tr}(X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-2}X_{nk})\big|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{m}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}+\frac{1}{nv^{4}}\Big)\,.

(5.8)

Note that
[
4
]
bounds terms such as
(\ref{957})
deterministically.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|\epsilon_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbf{E}|s_{kk}-1|^{2}+\frac{1}{m^{2}}+\frac{1}{m^{4}}\mathbf{E}|\alpha_{k}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\alpha_{k}-\mathrm{tr}(X_{nk}^{\top}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}X_{nk})|^{2}

(5.9)

By Lemma

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|\sigma_{k}b_{k}\epsilon_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{v^{2}\beta_{n}}\mathbf{E}|\epsilon_{k}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{nv^{2}}\,.

(5.10)

Thus, using (

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)|^{2}

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
z=u+iv
be a bounded sequence where
u\geq 1+(2+\eta)\sqrt{\beta_{n}}
and
v\geq 1/\sqrt{m}
. Then,

\phantom{\,.}|\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\bar{s}_{n}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{nv}\,.

Proof.

Recall definitions (
5.3
). We have

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\tilde{b}_{k}^{-1}-\bar{b}_{n}^{-1}=\bar{\epsilon}_{k}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{k}-\bar{b}_{n}=-\bar{b}_{n}^{2}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}+\bar{b}_{n}^{2}\tilde{b}_{k}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}^{2}\,.

Together with Theorem A.4 of
[
4
]
, this leads to the following decomposition of

:

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}s_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{b}_{k}=\bar{b}_{n}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-\bar{b}_{n}^{2}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}+\bar{b}_{n}^{2}\tilde{b}_{k}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}^{2})\,.

(5.11)

(8.3.12) of
[
4
]
proves the moment bound
|\mathbf{E}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|\leq 1/(mv)
. The second moment of
\bar{\epsilon}_{k}
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}|\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbf{E}|\bar{\epsilon}_{k}-\mathbf{E}(\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|X_{nk})|^{2}+\mathbf{E}|\mathbf{E}(\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|X_{nk})-\mathbf{E}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}+|\mathbf{E}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}\,,

(5.12)

A bound on

is given by (

) and Lemma

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}|\mathbf{E}(\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|X_{nk})-\mathbf{E}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\mathbf{E}\big|\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}S_{nk}-\mathbf{E}\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}S_{nk}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|z|^{2}}{m^{2}}\mathbf{E}\big|\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}-\mathbf{E}\mathrm{tr}(S_{nk}-zI_{n-1})^{-1}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}\mathbf{E}|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)|^{2}+\frac{2|z|^{2}}{m^{2}v^{2}}\,.

(5.13)

By (
5.11

5.13

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}|\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\bar{b}_{n}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n\beta_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\mathbf{E}\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|+\frac{1}{nv\beta_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}|\bar{\epsilon}_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{nv}+\frac{1}{v\beta_{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m^{2}v^{2}}+\beta_{n}^{2}\mathbf{E}|s_{n}(z)-\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)|^{2}\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{nv}\,.

(5.14)

The above bound describes an equation that is quadratic in

\displaystyle\phantom{\,,}\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\frac{1}{1-z-\beta_{n}-\beta_{n}z\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)}=\delta_{n}\,,

(5.15)

where

obeys

(note that

\phantom{\,.}\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{2\beta_{n}z}\Big(1-z-\beta_{n}+\beta_{n}z\delta_{n}+\sqrt{(z+\beta_{n}-1+\beta_{n}z\delta_{n})^{2}-4\beta_{n}z}\,\Big)\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\phantom{\,.}|\mathbf{E}s_{n}(z)-\bar{s}_{n}(z)|

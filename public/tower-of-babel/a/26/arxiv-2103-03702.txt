Some Properties and Applications of Burr III-Weibull Distribution
Deepthy G S, Nicy Sebastian and Reshma Rison

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we introduce a new distribution called Burr III-Weibull(BW) distribution using the concept of competing risk. We derive moments, conditional moments, mean deviation and quantiles of the proposed distribution. Also the Renyi’s entropy and order statistics of the distribution are obtained. Estimation of parameters
of the distribution is performed via maximum likelihood method. A simulation study is performed to validate the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE). A real practical data set
is analyzed for illustration.

Key words
:Burr III distribution, Weibull distribution, Maximum Likelihood Estimation.

1.  Introduction

Burr type III distribution with two parameters was first introduced in the literature of Burr
[
1
]
for modelling lifetime data or survival data. It is more flexible and includes a variety of distributions with varying degrees of skewness and kurtosis. This distribution has a wide application in areas of statistical modelling such as forestry Gove et al.
[
3
]
, meteorology Mielke
[
2
]
, and reliability Mokhlis
[
7
]
. Burr type XII distribution can be derived from Burr type III distribution by replacing
X
with
\frac{1}{X}
. The usefulness and properties of Burr distribution are discussed by Burr and Cislak
[
8
]
and Johnson et al.
[
9
]
.

The hazard function of distributions may include one or more of the following behavioural patterns; increasing, decreasing or constant
shapes. Thus, they cannot be used to model lifetime data with a bathtub shaped hazard function, such as human mortality and machine life cycles. For last few decades, statisticians have been developing various extensions and modified forms of the Weibull distribution and other related models.
The two-parameter, flexible Weibull extension of Bebbington et al.
[
4
]
has a hazard function that can be increasing, decreasing or bathtub shaped. Zhang and Xie
[
5
]
studied the characteristics
and application of the truncated Weibull distribution, which has a bathtub shaped hazard function. A new modified Weibull distribution by Saad and Jingsong
[
6
]
considered an increasing and a bathtub shaped hazard function.

The cumulative distribution function(cdf) and probability density function(pdf) of the weibull distribution are given by,

\displaystyle F_{W}(x;\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}},x\geq 0,\lambda>0,\beta>0

(1)

\displaystyle f_{W}(x;\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta-1}e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}

(2)

where
\lambda
and
\beta
are the scale and shape parameters. The cumulative distribution(cdf) and probability density function(pdf) of the BurrIII distribution is given by,

\displaystyle G_{B}(x;c,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+x^{-c})^{-k},x\geq 0,k>0,c>0

(3)

\displaystyle g_{B}(x;c,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ckx^{-c-1}\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}

(4)

where c and k are shape parameters.

2.  Burr III-Weibull Distribution

The reliability function of the new distribution, say Burr III Weibull(BW) distribution, can be constructed by combining the corresponding reliability functions of Burr III and Weibull distributions. The resulting reliability function, the cumulative distribution function and the probability density function are given by,

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{BW}(x;c,k,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right)\left(e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}\right)\ \ ;c,k,\lambda,\beta>0

(5)

\displaystyle F_{BW}(x;c,k,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\left(1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k}\right)\left(e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}\right)

(6)

for
c,k,
\lambda
,
\beta>0
.

\displaystyle f_{BW}(x;c,k,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}\left[ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}\left(1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right)\right]

(7)

for
c,k,\lambda,\beta>0
.
The hazard rate
h(x)
and reverse hazard rate
r(x)
are given respectively as,

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ck(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}(1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k})}{1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k}}

(8)

\displaystyle T(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}[ck(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}(1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k})]}{1-[1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k}e^{-\left(x/\lambda\right)^{\beta}}}

(9)

3.2  Moments

Moments can be used to study the most important features and characteristics of a distribution such as central tendency, dispersion, skewness, kurtusis etc.
The
r^{th}
moment of BW distribution is given by,

\displaystyle E(X^{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r}e^{-(x/\lambda)^{\beta}}\left[ck(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}(1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k})\right]dx

\displaystyle ck\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx.

Let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ck\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx\text{and}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx.

(12)

Consider,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle ck\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{r+m\beta-c-1}dx,\text{put $u=(1+x^{-c})^{-1}$ ,}

(13)

\displaystyle ck\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{(m\beta)}m!}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c}-1}(1-u)^{1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}-1}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ck\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{(m\beta)}m!}B\left(k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}e^{-({x/\lambda})^{\beta}}dx

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\frac{\lambda^{r+\beta}}{\beta}\Gamma\left(\frac{r+\beta}{\beta}\right),\text{using generalised gamma distribution}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t-1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-ct-1}e^{-(x/\lambda)^{\beta}}dx

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t-1}{t}\lambda^{r-ct}\Gamma\left(\frac{r+\beta-ct}{\beta}\right)

Substituting (
13

14
) and (
15

12

\displaystyle E(X^{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ck\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{(m\beta)}m!}B\left(k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)+\lambda^{r}\Gamma\left(\frac{r}{\beta}+1\right)

(16)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{t}\binom{k+t-1}{t}\Gamma\left(\frac{r+\beta-ct}{\beta}\right)\lambda^{r-ct}\ \text{where}\ \ r<c,m\beta<c.

Where
B\left(a,b\right)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt
is the beta function and
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{d-1}e^{-(x/a)^{p}}dx=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)}{\frac{p}{a^{d}}}
is generalised gamma function. The moment generating function of the BW distribution is given by,
E\left(e^{tY}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}E\left(Y^{i}\right)
where
E\left(Y^{i}\right)
is given above.

3.3  Conditional Moments

The
r^{th}
conditional moment is defined as
E(X^{r}/X>t)
. The
r^{th}
conditional moment of the BW distribution is given by,

\displaystyle E(X^{r}/X>t)

\displaystyle\quad~~~~=\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{r}f(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad~~~~=\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{r}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\left(ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\left(\beta-1\right)}\left(1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right)\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad~~~~=\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{r-c-1}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}dx+\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}dx

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{r-c-1}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}dx,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-1}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}dx,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}x^{r+\beta-1}dx.

Then

E(X^{r}/X>t)=\frac{1}{\bar{F}(t)}\left(A+B-C\right).

(17)

Consider

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ck\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{r+m\beta-c-1}dx,\text{put}~~u=\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-1},

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}\int_{(1+t^{-c})^{-1}}^{1}u^{k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c}-1}\left(1-u\right)^{1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}-1}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}B_{(1+t^{-c})^{-1}}\left(k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{r}\int_{(\frac{t}{\lambda})^{\beta}}^{\infty}u^{1+\frac{r}{\beta}-1}e^{-u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{r}\Gamma\left(\left(\frac{r}{\beta}+1\right),\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{r+\beta-cp-1}e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}dx

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{r-cp}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\int_{\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}^{\infty}u^{1+\frac{r}{\beta}-\frac{cp}{\beta}-1}e^{-u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{r-cp}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\Gamma\left(\frac{r-cp}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

Substituting (
18

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F(t)}}\left(k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}B_{(1+t^{-c})^{-1}}\left(k+\frac{r}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{r}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)\right)

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F(t)}}\left(\lambda^{r}\Gamma\left(\left(\frac{r}{\beta}+1\right),\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{F(t)}}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\lambda^{r-cp}\Gamma\left(\frac{r-cp}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{t}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)\right)

3.4  Mean Deviation

The amount of scatter in a population is measured to some extent by the totality of deviations from the mean and median. These are known as mean deviation about mean and as mean deviation about median and are defined as,

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mid{x-\mu}\mid f_{BW}(x)dx\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta_{2}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mid{x-M}\mid f_{BW}(x)dx

respectively where
\mu=E(X)
and
M
=
Median(X)
denote the median.
The measures
\delta_{1}(x)
and
\delta_{2}(x)
can be calculated using the relationships,

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mu F_{BW}(\mu)-2\mu+2\int_{\mu}^{\infty}xf_{BW}(x)dx

(22)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu+2\int_{M}^{\infty}xf_{BW}(x)dx

(23)

respectively. When
r=1
we get the mean
\mu=E(X)
. Note that
T(\mu)=\int_{\mu}^{\infty}xf_{BW}(x)dx
and
T(M)=\int_{M}^{\infty}xf_{BW}(x)dx
, where

\displaystyle T(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mu}^{\infty}xf(x)dx

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}B_{(1+\mu^{-c})^{-1}}\left(k+\frac{1}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{1}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)+\lambda\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1-cp}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-cp}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

similarly,

\displaystyle T(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{\infty}xf(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}B_{(1+M^{-c})^{-1}}\left(k+\frac{1}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{1}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{M}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)-\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1-cp}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-cp}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{M}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

Consequently, the mean deviation about the mean and the mean deviation about the median reduces to

\displaystyle\delta_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mu F_{BW}(\mu)-2\mu+2T(\mu)\ \ \ and\ \ \delta_{2}(x)=-\mu+2T(M)

respectively.

3.5  Bonferroni and Lorenz curves

Bonferroni and Lorenz curves have applications not only in economics for the study of income and poverty, but also in other fields such as reliability, demography, insurance and medicine. Bonferroni and Lorenz curves for the BW distribution are given by,

\displaystyle B(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p\mu}\int_{0}^{q}xf_{BW}(x)dx=\frac{1}{p\mu}[\mu-T(q)],\ \ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle L(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{q}xf_{BW}(x)dx=\frac{1}{\mu}[\mu-T(q)],

respectively, where

\displaystyle T(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{q}^{\infty}xf_{BW}(x)dx

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\lambda^{m\beta}m!}B_{(1+q^{-c})^{-1}}\left(k+\frac{1}{c}+\frac{m\beta}{c},1-\frac{1}{c}-\frac{m\beta}{c}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{q}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)-\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1-cp}(-1)^{p}\binom{k+p-1}{p}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-cp}{\beta}+1,\left(\frac{q}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}\right)

and
q=F^{-1}(p),0\leq p\leq 1
.

3.6  Order Statistics

The density function
f_{i:m}(x)
of the
i^{th}
order statistic for i=1,2,3,…,m from independently and identically distributed random variables

X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{m}
following BW distribution is given by,

\displaystyle f_{i:m}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m!f_{BW}(x)}{(i-1)!(m-i)!}[F_{BW}(x)]^{i-1}[1-F_{BW}(x)]^{m-i}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m!f_{BW}(x)}{(i-1)!(m-i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}(-1)^{j}\binom{m-i}{j}[F_{BW}(x)]^{j+i-1}

Using the binomial expansion
[1-F(x)]^{m-i}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}\binom{m-i}{j}(-1)^{j}[F(x)]^{j}

and the pdf and cdf of BW distribution in (
27
) we have,

\displaystyle f_{i:m}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m!f_{BW}(x)}{(i-1)!(m-i)!}\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}(-1)^{j}\binom{m-i}{j}\left(1-\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]\left[e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\right]\right)^{j+i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-i}(-1)^{j}\frac{m!}{(i-1)!(m-i-j)!(j)!}\left(1-\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]\left[e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\right]\right)^{j+i-1}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\left(ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\left(\beta-1\right)}\left(1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right)\right)

The pdf of the
1^{st}
and
n^{th}
order statistic is given by,

\displaystyle f_{1:m}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}\frac{m!}{(m-1-j)!(j)!}\left(1-\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]\left[e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\right]\right)^{j}

(28)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\left(ck\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\left(\beta-1\right)}\left(1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle f_{n:m}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-n}(-1)^{j}\frac{m!}{(n-1)!(m-n-j)!(j)!}\left(1-\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]\left[e^{-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}}\right]\right)^{j+n-1}

(29)

5.  Method of Maximum Likelihood Estimation

The most useful parametric estimation method is the maximum likelihood method. Let
x_{1},x_{2},....x_{n}
be a random sample of size n from BW distribution. Then the log likelihood function is given by,

\displaystyle l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln[f(x_{i},c,k,\lambda,\beta)]

(31)

The log likelihood for a single observation is given by,

\displaystyle l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta}+\ln(ck(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}x^{-c-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}[1-(1+x^{-c})^{-k}])

(32)

The maximum likelihood estimates can be obtained by solving the following the equations simultaneously,

\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial c}=0,\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial k}=0,\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial\lambda}=0,\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial\beta}=0
where

\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial c}=\frac{k\lambda^{\beta}\left(-cx^{-c-1}\ln x\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k-1}-cx^{-2c-1}(-k-1)(1+x^{-c})^{-k-2}\ln x+x^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}\right)-k\beta x^{-c+\beta-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}\ln x}{\lambda^{\beta}ckx^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]}

\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial k}=\frac{ckx^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}-cx^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}\ln(1+x^{-c})+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}\ln(1+x^{-c})(1+x^{-c})^{-k}}{ckx^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]}

\frac{\partial l(c,k,\lambda,\beta)}{\partial\lambda}=\frac{\beta x}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)^{\beta-1}-\frac{\beta^{2}x^{\beta-1}\lambda^{-\beta-1}\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]}{ckx^{-c-1}(1+x^{-c})^{-k-1}+\frac{\beta}{\lambda^{\beta}}x^{\beta-1}\left[1-\left(1+x^{-c}\right)^{-k}\right]}

8.  Conclusion

A new distribution called Burr III-Weibull(BW)distribution is proposed and its properties are
studied. The BW distribution possesses increasing, decreasing and upside-down bathtub shaped failure rate function. We derived the moments, conditional moments, mean deviation, quantiles, Bonferroni and Lorentz curve etc. of the proposed distribution. Order statistics and Renyi’s entropy of the proposed distribution are also obtained. Estimation of the parameters of the distribution is performed via maximum likelihood method. A simulation study is performed to validate the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE). Finally, the BW distribution
is fitted to real data sets in order to illustrate its applicability and usefulness.

3.1.
Bernoulli numbers

We derive a convenient generating function for Bernoulli numbers.

Lemma 3.1
.

If

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\text{\rm sinc}(X)}&=\exp\left(-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-4)^{k}B_{2k}}{(2k)(2k)!}\cdot X^{2r}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

We begin with Euler’s product formula for the sinc function

\text{sinc}(X)=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{X^{2}}{\pi^{2}n^{2}}\right).

Recalling that the series expansion for the products of logs

-\log\prod\limits_{n\geq 1}\left(1-za_{n}\right)=\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{z^{k}}{k}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{k},

and by letting
a_{n}:=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}n^{2}},z=X^{2}
, we obtain

-\log(\text{sinc}(X))=\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}^{\infty}\frac{X^{2k}}{k}\cdot\frac{\zeta(2k)}{\pi^{2k}}.

By Euler’s formula for

at the positive even integers (for example, see Th. 12.17 of
[

\zeta(2k)=\frac{(-1)^{k+1}B_{2k}(2\pi)^{2k}}{2(2k)!},

we obtain the claimed formula.

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}(-1)^{t}V_{2t}(q)\cdot\frac{X^{2t}}{(2t)!}=\cos X\prod_{n\geq 1}(1+2\cos(2X)q^{n}+q^{2n})(1-2\cos(4X)q^{2n-1}+q^{4n-2})

\displaystyle\ \hskip 36.135pt=\cos X\prod_{k\geq 1}(1+q^{k})^{2}(1-q^{2k-1})^{2}\prod_{j\geq 1}\left[1-\frac{4(\sin^{2}X)q^{j}}{(1+q^{j})^{2}}\right]\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}2X)q^{2j-1}}{(1-q^{2j-1})^{2}}\right].

The proof now follows from the simple identity

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{k})=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2k-1}}.

∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For each positive odd integer
j
, we consider the Lambert series

(3.3)

\mathbf{S}_{j}(q):=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{m^{j}q^{m}}{1-q^{m}}=\frac{B_{j+1}}{2(j+1)}-\frac{B_{j+1}}{2(j+1)}E_{j+1}(q).

This expression in terms of the
E_{j+1}(q)
follows from (
1.4
).
The proof of Theorem
1.2
boils down to deriving expressions for the power series in Theorem
3.4
in terms of the
\mathbf{S}_{j}(q).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first prove claim (1) regarding Ramanujan’s
U_{2t}
series. The key fact underlying the proof is the following power series identity.

(3.4)

\sum_{t\geq 0}(-1)^{t}U_{2t}(q)\cdot\frac{X^{2t+1}}{(2t+1)!}=\sin x\cdot\exp\left(-2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)}{(2r)!}(-4X^{2})^{r}\right).

Thanks to Lemma
3.4
(1), this identity will follow from

(3.5)

\exp\left(-2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)}{(2r)!}(-4X^{2})^{r}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 1}\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}X)q^{j}}{(1-q^{j})^{2}}\right].

To establish (
3.5
), we compute the following double-sum in two different ways. First, we use the Taylor expansion of
\cos(y)
and then interchange the order of summation to get

\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{q^{kj}\cos(2kX)}{k}=\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(-4X^{2})^{r}}{(2r)!}\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{2r-1}\sum_{j\geq 1}q^{kj}.

By combining the geometric series and the Taylor series for
\log(1-Y)
with (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.6)

\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{q^{kj}\cos(2kX)}{k}=\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{(-4X^{2})^{r}}{(2r)!}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{2r-1}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}=-\log(q)_{\infty}+\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)}{(2r)!}(-4X^{2})^{r},

where
(q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})
is the
q
-Pochhammer symbol.

On the other hand, using
2\cos\theta=e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}
and Taylor expansion of
\log(1-Y)
, we find that

\begin{split}\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{q^{kj}\cos(2kX)}{k}&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(e^{2iX}q^{j})^{k}}{k}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(e^{-2iX}q^{j})^{k}}{k}\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\log\left[1-2(\cos 2X)q^{j}+q^{2j}\right].\end{split}

After straightforward algebraic manipulation, we get

(3.7)

\sum_{j,k\geq 1}\frac{q^{kj}\cos(2kX)}{k}=-\log(q)_{\infty}-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\prod_{j\geq 1}\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}X)q^{j}}{(1-q^{j})^{2}}\right]\right).

Identity (
3.5
) follows by comparing (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), and in turn confirms (
3.4
).

We now investigate the exponential series in (
3.4
). Thanks to
(
3.3
), followed by an application of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-2\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-4)^{k}\mathbf{S}_{2k-1}(q)}{(2k)!}\,X^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.90755pt=\exp\left(-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-4)^{k}B_{2k}}{(2k)(2k)!}\,X^{2k}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{(2k)(2k)!}\,(-4X^{2})^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.90755pt=\frac{X}{\sin X}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{2\,(2k)!}\,\frac{(-4X^{2})^{k}}{k}\right).

We recognize this last expression in the context of Pólya’s cycle index polynomials. Namely,
Lemma
3.2
gives the identity (here
\lambda=(1^{m_{1}}\dots t^{m_{t}}\vdash t)
)

\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}Y_{k}\,\frac{w^{k}}{k}\right)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left(\frac{Y_{k}}{k}\right)^{m_{k}}\right)w^{t},

which we apply with
Y_{k}=\frac{B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{2(2k)!}
and
w=-4X^{2}.
This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}(-1)^{t}U_{2t}(q)\frac{X^{2t+1}}{(2t+1)!}=\sin X\cdot\exp\left(-2\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2k-1}(q)}{(2k)!}\,(-4X^{2})^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 50.58878pt=\sin X\cdot\frac{X}{\sin X}\cdot\sum_{t\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left(\frac{B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{(2k)(2k)!}\right)^{m_{k}}\right)(-4X^{2})^{t}.

By comparing the coefficients of
X^{2t+1}
, we find that

(-1)^{t}\frac{U_{2t}(q)}{(2t+1)!}=(-4)^{t}\sum_{\lambda\vdash t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left(\frac{B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{{(2\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k)}(2k)!}\right)^{m_{k}},

which in turn, thanks to
(
1.7
), proves Theorem
1.2
(1).

We now turn to claim (2) regarding Ramanujan’s
V_{2t}
whose proof is analogous to the proof of (1). The main difference follows from the need for the generalized Lambert series

(3.8)

\mathbf{A}_{2r-1}(q):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}k^{2r-1}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}=\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)-4^{r}\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q^{2}).

The expression in
\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}
is straightforward.
For the sake of brevity, we note that calculations analogous to the proof of (
3.5
) gives the identity

\prod_{n\geq 1}\left[1-\frac{4(\sin^{2}X)q^{n}}{(1+q^{n})^{2}}\right]=\exp\left(2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{A}_{2r-1}(q)(-4X^{2})^{r}}{(2r)!}\right),

as well as

\begin{split}&\prod_{n\geq 1}\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}2X)q^{2n-1}}{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}\right]=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}2X)q^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\right]\cdot\prod_{n\geq 1}\left[1+\frac{4(\sin^{2}2X)q^{2n}}{(1-q^{2n})^{2}}\right]^{-1}\\
&\hskip 50.58878pt=\exp\left(-2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)(-16X^{2})^{r}}{(2r)!}\right)\exp\left(2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q^{2})(-16X^{2})^{r}}{(2r)!}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, combining these two expressions with (
3.8
), Theorem
3.4
(2) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}(-1)^{t}V_{2t}(q)\cdot\frac{X^{2t}}{(2t)!}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.58878pt=\cos X\cdot\exp\left(-2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)}{(2r)!}(-4(2X)^{2})^{r}\right)\exp\left(2\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{2r-1}(q)}{(2r)!}(-4X^{2})^{r}\right).

As in the proof of (1), we recognize generating functions for Pólya’s cycle index polynomials. Namely, by applying Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{t}V_{2t}(q)X^{2t}}{(2t)!}

\displaystyle=\cos X\cdot\frac{2X}{\sin(2X)}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{4^{k}B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{2\,(2k)!}\frac{(-4X^{2})^{k}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\sin X}{X}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{-B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{2\,(2k)!}\frac{(-4X^{2})^{k}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(4^{k}-1)B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{2\,(2k)!}\frac{(-4X^{2})^{k}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\lambda\vdash t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left(\frac{(4^{k}-1)B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{(2{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k})(2k)!}\right)^{m_{k}}\right)(-4X^{2})^{t}.

By comparing the coefficients of
X^{2t}
, we deduce that

V_{2t}(q)=4^{t}\,(2t)!\sum_{\lambda\vdash t}\prod_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{m_{k}!}\left(\frac{(4^{k}-1)B_{2k}\cdot E_{2k}(q)}{(2{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}k})(2k)!}\right)^{m_{k}},

which thanks to (

) completes the proof of Theorem

∎

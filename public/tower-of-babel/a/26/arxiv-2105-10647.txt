Ground and Excited state masses of Ω c 0 \Omega_{c}^{0} , Ω c ​ c + \Omega_{cc}^{+} and Ω c ​ c ​ c + ⁣ + \Omega_{ccc}^{++} baryons Preprint: APS/123-QED
Juhi Oudichhya, Keval Gandhi, Ajay Kumar Rai

Abstract
The masses of ground and excited states of Ω c 0 \Omega_{c}^{0} , Ω c ​ c + \Omega_{cc}^{+} and Ω c ​ c ​ c + ⁣ + \Omega_{ccc}^{++} baryons are calculated within the framework of Regge phenomenology. Here our attempt is to assign a possible spin-parity to these obtained masses. Our calculated masses in ( J , M 2 ) (J,M^{2}) plane with both natural ( J P J^{P} = 1 / 2 + 1/2^{+} , 3 / 2 − 3/2^{-} , 5 / 2 + 5/2^{+} , ….) and unnatural ( J P J^{P} = 3 / 2 + 3/2^{+} , 5 / 2 − 5/2^{-} , 7 / 2 + 7/2^{+} , ….) spin-parities are in agreement with the experimental observations where available and reasonably close to other theoretical predictions. Further we fix the slope and intercept of each Regge line in ( n , M 2 ) (n,M^{2}) plane and estimate the masses of higher excited states of these baryons. The obtained mass relations and the mass value predictions could provide an useful information in future experimental searches and the spin-parity assignment of these states.

II
Theoretical Framework

Regge theory is one of the simplest and effective phenomenological approach to study the hadron spectroscopy. Various theories were developed to understand the regge trejectory. Among them, one of the straightforward explaination for linear regge trejectories were proposed by Nambu
Nambu1974
;
Nambu1979
. He assumed that the uniform interaction of quark and antiquark pair form a strong flux tube and at the end of the tube the light quarks rotating with the speed of light at radius
R
. The mass originating in this flux tube is estimated as
chen2017
,

M=2\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}(r)}}dr=\pi\sigma R,

(1)

where
\sigma
represent the string tension, i.e the mass density per unit length. Likewise, the angular momentum of this flux tube is evaluated as,

J=2\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{\sigma r\nu(r)}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}(r)}}dr=\dfrac{\pi\sigma R^{2}}{2}+c^{{}^{\prime}},

(2)

one can also write,

J=\dfrac{M^{2}}{2\pi\sigma}+c^{{}^{\prime\prime}},

(3)

where
c^{{}^{\prime}}
and
c^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
are the constants of integration. Hence we can say that,
J
and
M^{2}
are linearly related to each other.
The plots of Regge trejectories of hadrons in the
(J,M^{2})
plane are usually called Chew-frautschi plots
Chew1961
. Also hadrons lying on the same Regge line possess the same internal quantum numbers. From Eq. (
3
), the most general form of linear regge trejectories can be expressed as
Wei2008
,

J=\alpha(M)=a(0)+\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}M^{2},

(4)

where
a(0)
and
\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}
represents the intercept and slope of the trejectory respectively. These parameters for different quark constituents of a baryon multiplet can be related by two relations
Wei2008
;
Add1
;
Add2
;
Add3
;
Add4

a_{iiq}(0)+a_{jjq}(0)=2a_{ijq}(0),

(5)

\dfrac{1}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{iiq}}+\dfrac{1}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{jjq}}=\dfrac{2}{{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}_{ijq}},

(6)

where
i,j,q
represent quark flavors,
m_{i}<m_{j}
and
q
denotes an arbitrary light or heavy quark. Using Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) we obtain,

\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{iiq}M^{2}_{iiq}+\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{jjq}M^{2}_{jjq}=2\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{ijq}M^{2}_{ijq}.

(7)

Combining the relations (
6
) and (
7
) and after solving the quadratic equation, we obtain a pair of solutions as,

\dfrac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{jjq}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{iiq}}=\dfrac{1}{2M^{2}_{jjq}}\times[(4M^{2}_{ijq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{jjq})\pm\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{ijq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{jjq}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{iiq}M^{2}_{jjq}}].

(8)

This is the important relation we obtained between slope ratios and baryon masses. Eq. (
8
) can also be expressed in terms of baryon masses by introducing

can be any quark flavor,

\dfrac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{jjq}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{iiq}}=\dfrac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{kkq}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{iiq}}\times\dfrac{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{jjq}}{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}_{kkq}},

(9)

we have,

\begin{split}\dfrac{[(4M^{2}_{ijq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{jjq})+\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{ijq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{jjq}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{iiq}M^{2}_{jjq}}]}{2M^{2}_{jjq}}\\
=\dfrac{[(4M^{2}_{ikq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{kkq})+\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{ikq}-M^{2}_{iiq}-M^{2}_{kkq}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{iiq}M^{2}_{kkq}}]/2M^{2}_{kkq}}{[(4M^{2}_{jkq}-M^{2}_{jjq}-M^{2}_{kkq})+\sqrt{{{(4M^{2}_{jkq}-M^{2}_{jjq}-M^{2}_{kkq}})^{2}}-4M^{2}_{jjq}M^{2}_{kkq}}]/2M^{2}_{kkq}}.\end{split}

(10)

This is the general relation we have derived in terms of baryon masses which can be used to predict the mass of any baryon state by knowing all other masses.

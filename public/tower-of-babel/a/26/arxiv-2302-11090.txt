Abstract
. For 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty and α > 0 \alpha>0 , Besov spaces B α p B^{p}_{\alpha} play a key role in the theory of α \alpha -Möbius invariant function spaces. In some sense, B α 1 B^{1}_{\alpha} is the minimal α \alpha -Möbius invariant function space, B α 2 B^{2}_{\alpha} is the unique α \alpha -Möbius invariant Hilbert space, and B α ∞ B^{\infty}_{\alpha} is the maximal α \alpha -Möbius invariant function space. In this paper, under the α \alpha -Möbius invariant pairing and by the space B α ∞ B^{\infty}_{\alpha} , we identify the predual and dual spaces of B α 1 B^{1}_{\alpha} . In particular, the corresponding identifications are isometric isomorphisms. The duality theorem via the α \alpha -Möbius invariant pairing for B α p B^{p}_{\alpha} with p > 1 p>1 is also given.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the open unit disk in the complex plane. Denote by

{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
the Möbius group of one-to-one analytic functions that maps
\mathbb{D}
onto itself. For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, let

\sigma_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

be a Möbius map of
\mathbb{D}
interchanging the points
0
and
a
. It is well known that

\text{Aut}(\mathbb{D})=\{e^{i\theta}\sigma_{a}:\ \ a\in\mathbb{D}\ \text{and}\ \theta\ \ \text{is a real number}\}.

A space
X
contained in
H(\mathbb{D})
, the set of functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, is said to be Möbius invariant if it is equipped with a semi-norm
\rho
such that
f\circ\varphi\in X
and

\rho(f\circ\varphi)\lesssim\rho(f)
for all
f\in X
and all
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
.
The study of the theory of Möbius invariant function spaces is a classical topic in complex analysis (cf.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
). Roughly speaking, it is known from
[
3
,
4
,
12
]
that the
Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
, the Dirichlet space
\mathcal{D}
, and the Besov space
B^{1}
give us the maximal Möbius invariant function space, the unique Möbius invariant Hilbert space, and the minimal Möbius invariant function space, respectively.

For
\alpha>0
, let
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
be the Bloch type space consisting of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty.

|||\cdot|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}
is a semi-norm on
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
. Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,0}
the closure of polynomials in
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
.
If
\alpha=1
, then
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
is the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
. Because of the maximal property of
\mathcal{B}
among Möbius invariant function spaces, K. Zhu
[
19
]
posed the question of whether
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
is maximal among some family of analytic function spaces. To answer K. Zhu’s question and understand the theory of a general family of analytic function spaces
F(p,q,s)
, in 1996 R. Zhao
[
16
]
introduced a notion of weighted composition of
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
with
\varphi
in
{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. For
\alpha>0
,
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, let

f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f^{\prime}(\varphi(w))(\varphi^{\prime}(w))^{\alpha}\,dw+f(\varphi(0))(\varphi^{\prime}(0))^{\alpha-1}

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Clearly, if
\alpha=1
, then
f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi=f\circ\varphi
, the usual composition of
f
and
\varphi
. Suppose
X
is a subspace of
H(\mathbb{D})
equipped with a semi-norm
\rho
. Following Section 4 in
[
16
]
, we say that
X
is

\alpha
-Möbius invariant if
f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi\in X
and

\rho(f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi)\leq C\rho(f)
for all
f\in X
and all
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, where
C
is a positive constant independent of
f
and
\varphi
. In fact,
if the condition above holds for a semi-norm
\rho
, then there is another equivalent semi-norm
\rho^{\prime}
on
X

satisfying that
\rho^{\prime}(f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi)=\rho^{\prime}(f)
for all
f\in X
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. Also, when
\alpha=1
, an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function space is a Möbius invariant function space. Indeed, among
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces, R. Zhao
[
16
]
proved that
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
is maximal in some sense. In
[
16
, p. 54]
, R. Zhao posed the following two questions: which space is the minimal
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function space? Is there one
\alpha
-Möbius invariant Hilbert space only? The two questions are answered in
[
6
]
recently. We refer to
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
for some recent study related to Möbius invariant function spaces.

For
\beta>-1
, we write
dA_{\beta}(z)=(\beta+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}dA(z)
, where
dA(z)=1/\pi dxdy
,
z=x+iy
, is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
Suppose
1\leq p<\infty
,
\alpha>0
, and
n
is a positive integer with
p(\alpha-1+n)-1>0
. Recall that the Besov space

is the set of those functions

in

satisfying that the function

is in

is the Möbius invariant Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Also, for
\alpha>0
and a positive integer

,
B^{\infty}_{\alpha}
consists of functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
such that
f^{(n)}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-1+n}
is bounded on
\mathbb{D}
. It is known from
[
17
]
that
B^{p}_{\alpha}
is independent of the choice of the integer
n
. Clearly,
B^{\infty}_{\alpha}
is the Bloch type space
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
. By
[
16
]
,
|||f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}
for every
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. From Theorem 6.2 in
[
6

, for any
p\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
, the space
B^{p}_{\alpha}
is also
\alpha
-Möbius invariant.

For
\alpha>0
, denote by
M_{\alpha}
the space of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
that can be
represented as

f(z)=c_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\int_{0}^{z}\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(w)^{\alpha}\,dw,\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

(1.1)

where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{1}
, and every
\varphi_{n}\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. A semi-norm on
M_{\alpha}
is given by

|||f|||_{M_{\alpha}}=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|c_{n}|:\ \ (\ref{eq4})\ \text{holds}\right\}.

It is known from
[
6
]
that
|||f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi|||_{M_{\alpha}}=|||f|||_{M_{\alpha}}
for every

and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, and
M_{\alpha}
is minimal among all non-trivial
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces.
From Theorem 3.2 in
[
6
]
,

|||f|||_{M_{\alpha}}\thickapprox|f^{\prime}(0)|+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\,dA(z).

(1.2)

for all
f\in M_{\alpha}
. A norm of
f
in
M_{\alpha}
is
\|f\|_{M_{\alpha}}=|f(0)|+|||f|||_{M_{\alpha}}
. Because of (
1.2
),
M_{\alpha}
is the Besov space
B^{1}_{\alpha}
.

Given
\alpha>0
and a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, we say that
f\in H_{\alpha}
if

|||f|||_{H_{\alpha}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}n|a_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

From
[
6
]
,
|||f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi|||_{H_{\alpha}}=|||f|||_{H_{\alpha}}
for each
f\in H_{\alpha}
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, and
H_{\alpha}
is the unique
non-trivial
\alpha
-Möbius invariant Hilbert space. It is also known from
[
6
]
that
H_{\alpha}
is the Besov space
B^{2}_{\alpha}
.

Fix
\alpha>0
, related to the space
H_{\alpha}
, a paring on
\mathbb{D}
is given by

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,na_{n}\overline{b}_{n}r^{2n},

(1.3)

if the limit exists, where
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
are functions analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. In fact, it is known from
[
6
]
that

\langle f\circ_{\alpha}\varphi,g\circ_{\alpha}\varphi\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}

for all
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus we say that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
is an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing.

In this paper, under the
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing, we give the dual relation between the minimal and the maximal
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces.
The corresponding identifications are isometric isomorphisms. In particular, we prove that there exists
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
B_{\alpha,0}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
in
M_{\alpha}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,n\overline{a}_{n}b_{n}

is divergent, which means that the definition of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
is reasonable via a limit. We also investigate the duality theorem for
B^{p}_{\alpha}
when
p>1
.

Throughout this paper, we write

. If

, then we write

2.
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing and a general duality result

In this section, we give some equalities of the
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing and a general duality theorem for
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces. These results will be useful in next sections.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose both
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
belong to
H(\mathbb{D})
, and the limit
\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,na_{n}\overline{b}_{n}r^{2n}
exists. Then the following statements hold.

(a)

For
\alpha>1/2
,

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}=(2\alpha-1)\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(rz)\overline{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}g^{\prime}(rz)}\,d\lambda(z).

(2.1)

(b)

For
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(rz)\overline{g^{\prime}(rz)}dA_{2\alpha-1}(z)

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime\prime}(rz)\overline{g^{\prime\prime}(rz)}dA_{2\alpha}(z)\Big].

Proof.

For
r\in(0,1)
, replacing
f
and
g
in
[
6
, Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3]
by
f_{r}
and
g_{r}
respectively, we get the desired results. Here
f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
and
g_{r}(z)=g(rz)
.
∎

Next, by the
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing, we give a description of the dual space of an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function space.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
and
X
is a function space equipped with an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant semi-norm
|||\cdot|||_{X}
. Assume that polynomials are dense in
X
.
Then the dual space
(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}
of
X/\mathbb{C}
can be identified with the space of functions

f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

|||f|||_{(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}}=\sup\{|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}|:g\in X,|||g|||_{X}\leq 1\}<\infty.

Moreover,
(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}
is an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function space.

Proof.

Let
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with
|||f|||_{(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}}<\infty
. Clearly,
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
gives a bounded linear functional on
X/\mathbb{C}
.

Now let
F
be a bounded linear functional on
X
with
F(1)=0
.
Write

a_{n}=\overline{F(z^{n})}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n},\ \ n=1,2,3,\cdots,

(2.3)

and set
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

|a_{n}|\leq\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n}\|z^{n}\|_{X}\|F\|,

where
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is a norm of
X
, and
\|F\|
is the norm of the bounded linear functional
F
.
Note that
M_{\alpha}
is the minimal non-trivial
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function space. By Theorem 3.3 in
[
6
]
,

\|z^{n}\|_{X}\lesssim\|z^{n}\|_{M_{\alpha}}

for all positive integers
n
. Clearly,
\|z\|_{M_{\alpha}}
is a positive constant.
For
n\geq 2
, a direct computation yields

\|z^{n}\|_{M_{\alpha}}\approx n(n-1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{n-2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\,dA(z)\approx n^{2-\alpha},

where the comparable positive constants depend only on
\alpha
. Consequently,

|a_{n}|\lesssim\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n}n^{2-\alpha}\|F\|

for all positive integers
n
. Note that the function
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n}n^{2-\alpha}z^{n}
belongs to
H(\mathbb{D})
. Thus
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.
For any polynomial
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}b_{n}z^{n}
, where
N
is a positive integer, it follows from (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle F(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{0}F(1)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}F(z^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}\overline{a_{n}}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}.

Since polynomials are dense in
X
, we see that
F(h)=\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
h\in X
. Thus
(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}
can be identified with the space of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with
|||f|||_{(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}}<\infty
.

The
\alpha
-Möbius invariance of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\alpha}
and
|||\cdot|||_{X}
yields that
|||\cdot|||_{(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}}
is an
\alpha
-Möbius invariant semi-norm of
(X/\mathbb{C})^{*}
. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem
2.2
generalizes the classical duality result in
[
4
]
from Möbius invariant function spaces to
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces for each
\alpha>0
.

3.
Duality theorems associated with the minimal and maximal
\alpha
-Möbius invariant function spaces

In this section, for every
\alpha>0
, we show that the dual space of
M_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
, and the predual space of
M_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha,0}/\mathbb{C}
. The corresponding identifications are isometric isomorphisms. For the clear presentation, we will state these results by the notion of bounded linear functional.

We first consider the existence of the limit in (
1.3
) as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
.
For
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
in
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
in
M_{\alpha}
, let

h(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,na_{n}\overline{b_{n}}r^{2n}.

Then the limit
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}h(r)
exists.

Proof.

For
r\in(1/2,1)
, it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle h(r)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(rz)\overline{g^{\prime}(rz)}dA_{2\alpha-1}(z)+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime\prime}(rz)\overline{g^{\prime\prime}(rz)}dA_{2\alpha}(z).

Combining this with the characterization of weighted Bergman spaces and Bloch type spaces via higher order derivatives (cf.
[
18
,
19
]
), we see that

\displaystyle|h(r)|\lesssim

\displaystyle|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(rz)|dA_{\alpha-1}(z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|dA_{\alpha-1}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime\prime}(rz)|dA_{\alpha}(z)+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}|||g_{r}|||_{M_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}|||g_{r}|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq C|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}|||g|||_{M_{\alpha}},

(3.1)

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
\alpha
.

Let
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
be any real numbers in
(1/2,1)
. Bear in mind (
3
) and

g_{r_{1}}(z)-g_{r_{2}}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(r_{1}^{n}-r_{2}^{n})z^{n}.

We deduce

\displaystyle|h(r_{1})-h(r_{2})|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,na_{n}\overline{b_{n}}(r_{1}^{2n}-r_{2}^{2n})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,na_{n}\overline{b_{n}}(r_{1}^{2n}-r_{2}^{2n})s^{2n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}|||g_{r_{1}}-g_{r_{2}}|||_{M_{\alpha}}.

Note that
|||g_{r_{1}}-g_{r_{2}}|||_{M_{\alpha}}
tends to
0
uniformly as
|r_{1}-r_{2}|\to 0
. Then
|h(r_{1})-h(r_{2})|\to 0
uniformly as
|r_{1}-r_{2}|\to 0
. Thus the limit
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}h(r)
exists.
∎

The following result means that the dual space of
M_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
by isometric isomorphisms.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
\alpha>0
, the following statements are true.

(a)

For any
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
,
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
defines a bounded linear functional on
M_{\alpha}
. Moreover,

|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup\{|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}|:g\in M_{\alpha},|||g|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1\}.

(3.2)

(b)

If
F
is a bounded linear functional on
M_{\alpha}
with
F(1)=0
, then there exits a function
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
such that

F(g)=\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
g\in M_{\alpha}
. Also,

|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup\{|F(g)|:g\in M_{\alpha},|||g|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1\}.

(3.3)

Proof.

(a) Let
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
. Because of Lemma
3
,
\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
is well defined for any
g\in M_{\alpha}
. It is clear that
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
defines a linear functional on
M_{\alpha}
.

For
\alpha>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
,

\left\langle I_{\alpha,\zeta},f\right\rangle_{\alpha}=(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\overline{f^{\prime}(\zeta)},

(3.4)

where

I_{\alpha,\zeta}(z)=:\int_{0}^{z}\sigma^{\prime}_{\zeta}(w)^{\alpha}\,dw,\ \ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Consider the case of
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
first. From (
2.1
) and the well-known reproducing formula (cf.
[
18
, Proposition 4.23]
), we get that

\displaystyle\left\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\right\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\alpha-1)(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\prime}(rz)(1-|z|^{2})^{2\alpha-2}}{(1-r\zeta\overline{z})^{2\alpha}}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}f^{\prime}(r^{2}\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(\zeta),

which yields formula (
3.4
). Next we consider the case of all
\alpha>0
. By (

),

\displaystyle\left\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\right\rangle_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\prime}(rz)}{(1-r\zeta\overline{z})^{2\alpha}}dA_{2\alpha-1}(z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}2\alpha\zeta r^{2}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(rz)}{(1-r\zeta\overline{z})^{2\alpha+1}}dA_{2\alpha}(z)\Big].

Note that
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. Lebesgue’s Dominated
Convergence Theorem yields

\displaystyle\left\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\right\rangle_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta\overline{z})^{2\alpha}}dA_{2\alpha-1}(z)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(2\alpha+2)}2\alpha\zeta\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{(1-\zeta\overline{z})^{2\alpha+1}}dA_{2\alpha}(z)\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle G(\alpha).

Denote by
\Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re(z)>0\}
the right-half plane. It is well known that the Gamma function is analytic on
\Omega
. Hence
G
extends to an analytic function on
\Omega
. For fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, we write
G_{1}(\beta)=(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\beta}f^{\prime}(\zeta)
. Clearly,
G_{1}
is also an analytic function on
\Omega
. We have shown that
G(\beta)=G_{1}(\beta)
when
\beta
is any real number in
(\frac{1}{2},+\infty)
. From the uniqueness property of analytic functions, we get
G_{1}(\beta)=G(\beta)
for all
\beta\in\Omega
. Hence our claim holds when
\alpha>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.

Suppose
g\in M_{\alpha}
. Then there exists
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
\{c_{n}\}\in\ell^{1}
and
\{\zeta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
such that

g(z)=c_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}I_{\alpha,\zeta_{n}}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|c_{n}||\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta_{n}}\rangle_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|c_{n}|\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\rangle_{\alpha}|.

Taking the infimum over all representations for
g
, we get

|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}|\leq|||g|||_{M_{\alpha}}\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\rangle_{\alpha}|.

(3.5)

It follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) that

|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}|\leq|||g|||_{M_{\alpha}}||||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}.

(3.6)

Thus
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
defines a bounded linear functional on
M_{\alpha}
.

Clearly, (
3.6
) implies

\sup\{|\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}|:h\in M_{\alpha},|||h|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1\}\leq||||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}.

Note that
|||I_{\alpha,\zeta}|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1
for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

||||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|\langle f,I_{\alpha,\zeta}\rangle_{\alpha}|\leq\sup\{|\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}|:h\in M_{\alpha},|||h|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1\}.

Hence

|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup\{|\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}|:h\in M_{\alpha},|||h|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq 1\}.

(b) Suppose
F
is a bounded linear functional on
M_{\alpha}
with
F(1)=0
.
Set

a_{n}=\overline{F(z^{n})}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n},\ \ n=1,2,3,\cdots,

and let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Note that polynomials are dense in
M_{\alpha}
. By the proof of Proposition
2.2
, we see that
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
F(h)=\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
h\in M_{\alpha}
.

For any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha,\zeta}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\overline{\zeta}w)^{2\alpha}}dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}\overline{\zeta}^{n-1}z^{n}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle F(I_{\alpha,\zeta}(z))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}\overline{\zeta}^{n-1}F(z^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}n\overline{a_{n}}\overline{\zeta}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\zeta|^{2}-1)^{\alpha}\overline{f^{\prime}(\zeta)}.

Thus,

|||f|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}=\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|F(I_{\alpha,\zeta})|\leq\|F\|\sup_{\zeta\in\mathbb{D}}|||I_{\alpha,\zeta}|||_{M_{\alpha}}\leq||F||.

Hence
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
. Since
F(g)=\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all

, it follows from (
3.2
) that (
3.3
) also holds. We finish the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For

(a)

For any

,

(3.7)

(b)

(3.8)

Proof.

Combining this with Theorem

|||f|||_{M_{\alpha}}=\sup_{h\not=0}\frac{|\langle h,f\rangle_{\alpha}|}{|||h|||_{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}}

(3.9)

Thus,

(3.11)

(b) Write

4.
Duality for
\alpha
-Möbius invariant
B^{p}_{\alpha}
spaces with
p>1

For
p>1
and
\alpha>0
, this section is devoted to show that under the
\alpha
-Möbius invariant pairing, the dual space of
B^{p}_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
B^{q}_{\alpha}/\mathbb{C}
, where
q
satisfies
1/p+1/q=1
and the corresponding identification is with equivalent norms.

For the investigation of the Besov space
B^{p}_{\alpha}
with
p>1
and
\alpha>0
, the easier case is when
p\alpha>1
. For such case,
B^{p}_{\alpha}
is the space of those functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|^{p}d\lambda(z)<\infty.

To cover the case of
p\alpha<1
, we can describe
B^{p}_{\alpha}
as the space of those functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
such that

|||f|||_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}|^{p}d\lambda(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

(4.1)

A norm of
f
in
B^{p}_{\alpha}
is

\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}=|f(0)|+|f^{\prime}(0)|+|||f|||_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}.

For
q>0
and
\beta>-1
, recall that the Bergman space
A^{q}_{\beta}
consists of functions
f
in
H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{A^{q}_{\beta}}^{q}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}dA(z)<\infty.

By (
4.1
), for
p>1
and
\alpha>0
,
f\in B^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if
f^{\prime\prime}\in A^{p}_{p(\alpha+1)-2}
.

In this section, our proof of the main result involves the property of coefficient multipliers for Besov spaces.
Given two spaces
X
and
Y
of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, a complex sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is said to be a coefficient multiplier from
X
to
Y
if the function
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}a_{n}z^{n}
belongs to
Y
whenever
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in X
. Denote by
(X,Y)
the set of coefficient multipliers from
X
to
Y
. We refer to a recent book
[
9
]
for the theory of coefficient multiplier between spaces of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
BV
be the classical space of complex sequences of bounded variation; that is,

BV=\left\{\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}:\ \ |\lambda_{0}|+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}|<\infty\right\}.

The following result is due to S. Buckley, P. Koskela, and D. Vukotić
[
7
]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a Banach space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
such that for every
f\in X
the sequence
\{S_{n}f\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of Taylor polynomials converges to
f
in the norm of
X
. Then
BV\subseteq(X,X)
, and the inclusion is strict if the involution
Tf(z)=f(-z)
is bounded on
X
.

We also need the following well-known result (cf. Corollary 3.13 in
[
18
]
).

Theorem B
.

Suppose
a
,
b
,
\alpha
are real parameters,
1\leq p<\infty
, and

Tf(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{a}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{b}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2+a+b}}f(w)dA(w).

Then the operator
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},dA_{\alpha})
if and only if
-pa<\alpha+1<p(b+1)
.

For
g\in B_{\alpha}^{p}
and
f\in B_{\alpha}^{q}
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, we give a new formula of
\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
p>1
,
\alpha>0
, and
q
is the real number with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
be in
B_{\alpha}^{q}
and let
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
be in

Note that

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)\overline{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}(1-|z|^{2})^{2\alpha}dA(z).

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}m_{g_{r},\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)\overline{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}(1-|z|^{2})^{2\alpha}dA(z)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,nb_{n}\overline{a}_{n}r^{2n}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)}{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}\,nb_{n}\overline{a}_{n}.

(4.8)

By (
4
) and (
4
), the limit in (
4.2
) exists. By (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we get (
4.3
). The proof is complete.

f\in B_{\alpha}^{q}
and
g\in B_{\alpha}^{p}
. By Lemma
4.1
, the limit in the definition of
\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
exists. Clearly,
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
defines a linear functional on
B_{\alpha}^{p}
.
From (
4.3
) and (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}\right|\lesssim|f^{\prime}(0)||g^{\prime}(0)|+|||f|||_{B^{q}_{\alpha}}\|g\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}.

(4.10)

Thus the linear functional
\langle\cdot,f\rangle_{\alpha}
is also bounded on
B_{\alpha}^{p}
.

On the other hand, suppose
F
is a bounded linear functional on
B_{\alpha}^{p}
with
F(1)=0
. Let

a_{n}=\overline{F(z^{n})}\frac{\Gamma(n+2\alpha-1)}{n!\,\Gamma(2\alpha)n},\ \ n=1,2,3,\cdots,

and let
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
It is known that polynomials are dense in
B_{\alpha}^{p}
. From the proof of Proposition
2.2
,
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
F(g)=\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
g\in B_{\alpha}^{p}
.
Next we show that
f
belongs to
B_{\alpha}^{q}
.

For
g\in\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}
, set

G(g)(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z).

From the proof of Lemma
4.1
,
|||m_{g,\alpha}|||_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}\lesssim|||g|||_{B^{p}_{\alpha}}
for all
g\in B^{p}_{\alpha}
. Thus
G
is a bounded mapping from
\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
.
Write
G(\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p})
the image of
G
. Then
G:\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}\rightarrow G(\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p})
is bijective. As usual, denote by
G^{-1}
the inverse mapping of
G
.
Then
F\circ G^{-1}
is a bounded linear functional on
G(\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p})
. By the Hahn-Banach extension theorem, there is a function
\varphi
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)
such that

\displaystyle F\circ G^{-1}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}k(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}d\lambda(z)

for all
k
in
G(\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p})
, and

\|F\circ G^{-1}\|=\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{D},d\lambda)}.

(4.11)

Thus, for
g\in\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}
, we get

\displaystyle F(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\circ G^{-1}(G(g))

(4.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}G(g)(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}d\lambda(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)\overline{\varphi(z)}d\lambda(z).

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha-1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{-1}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}d\lambda(z)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|^{q}d\lambda(z)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

Then we can define an analytic function
f_{1}
in
\mathbb{D}
by

f_{1}(z)=(2\alpha+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\varphi(w)(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha-1}}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2+2\alpha}}dA(w).

Let
f_{2}
be the function with
f_{2}^{\prime\prime}=f_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}(2\alpha+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\varphi(w)}{(1-z\overline{w})^{2+2\alpha}}dA(w).

Since
-q(\alpha+1)<-1<q\alpha
, it follows from Theorem
B
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)\right|^{q}d\lambda(z)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|^{q}d\lambda(z).

Joining this with (
4.11
), we get

|||f_{2}|||_{B^{q}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|F\circ G^{-1}\|\lesssim\|G^{-1}\|\ \|F\|.

(4.14)

By (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), for
g\in\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}
, we see

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)\overline{f_{2}^{\prime\prime}(z)}(1-|z|^{2})^{2\alpha}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\overline{\varphi(w)}dA(w)(2\alpha+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{2\alpha}}{(1-\overline{z}w)^{2+2\alpha}}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}m_{g,\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(w)(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\overline{\varphi(w)}dA(w)=F(g).

Combining this with Lemma
4.1
, we get
F(g)=\langle g,f_{2}\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
g\in\widetilde{B}_{\alpha}^{p}
.
We also have proven that
F(g)=\langle g,f\rangle_{\alpha}
for all
g\in B_{\alpha}^{p}
. Bear in mind (
4.3
). Then
f(z)-f(0)-f^{\prime}(0)z=f_{2}(z)-f_{2}(0)-f_{2}^{\prime}(0)z
. Due to
(
4.14
),
f\in B^{q}_{\alpha}
and
|||f|||_{B^{q}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|F\|
. By this and (
4.10
), we obtain (
4.9
). The proof is complete.

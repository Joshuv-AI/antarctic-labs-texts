On Monotonicities of Interval Valued Functions
Ana Shirley Monteiro, Regivan Santiago, Martin Papco, Radko Mesiar, Humberto Bustince

Abstract




1
Introduction

In this paper, we develop the notions of weak/directional monotonicity (developed by Sesma-Sara et al.
[
1
]
in terms of the
Kulisch-Miranker order
) and the notion of
G
-monotonicity (introduced by Santiago et al.
[
2
]
for
[0,\!1]
)
for intervals endowed with admissible orders.

2
Preliminaries

The following notation will be used:
\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the set of all closed intervals with real numbers, uppercase letters denote its elements,
\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R}_{-})
denotes the subset consisting of all closed intervals with negative real numbers,
\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denotes the subset of closed intervals with positive real numbers. The set
\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
contains all closed subintervals of
[0,\!1]
. Further, we use uppercase letters to denote any interval variable
X
and use bold fonts to denote degenerated intervals;
\vec{0}
and
\vec{1}
to denote the vectors
(0,\ldots,0)
and
(1,\ldots,1)
, respectively;
\vec{\boldsymbol{0}}
and
\vec{\boldsymbol{1}}
, in bold, to denote the vectors
(\boldsymbol{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{0})
and
(\boldsymbol{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1})
, respectively, where
\boldsymbol{0}=[0,0]
and
\boldsymbol{1}=[1,1]
. The prefix “
IV
” will mean “
interval-valued
” in general.

Definition 2.1

(Operations over intervals)
.

Let
X,Y\in\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

(
\mathbf{OI}
-1)

The
sum of
X
and
Y
is defined by
X+Y=[\underline{X}+\underline{Y},\overline{X}+\overline{Y}]
.

(
\mathbf{OI}
-2)

The
opposite of
X
is defined by
-X=[-\overline{X},-\underline{X}]
.

(
\mathbf{OI}
-3)

The
difference of
X
and
Y
is defined by
X-Y=[\underline{X}-\overline{Y},\overline{X}-\underline{Y}]
.

(
\mathbf{OI}
-4)

The
product of
X,Y\in\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is defined by
X\cdot Y=[\underline{X}\cdot\underline{Y},\overline{X}\cdot\overline{Y}]
.

(
\mathbf{OI}
-5)

The
\alpha
-multiple of
X
is defined by
\alpha\cdot X=[\alpha\cdot\underline{X},\alpha\cdot\overline{X}]
if
\alpha\geq 0
and
\alpha\cdot X=[\alpha\cdot\overline{X},\alpha\cdot\underline{X}]
if
\alpha<0
.

3
Monotonicities of IV-Functions

In the case of intervals, considering the
Kulisch-Miranker order
(
{\preceq}_{KM}
) on
\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
, Sesma-Sara et al.
[
1
]
provided the definitions of standard monotonicity, weak monotonicity, directional monotonicity and pre-aggregation functions to the interval context as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

A function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be increasing (resp. decreasing) if for all
X,Y\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
such that
X\ {\preceq}_{KM^{n}}\ Y
it holds that
F(X)\ {\preceq}_{KM}\ F(Y)
(resp.
F(X)\ {\succeq}_{KM}\ F(Y)
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\overrightarrow{V}=\langle(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n},b_{n})\rangle\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{n}
such that
(a_{i},b_{i})\not=\vec{0}
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be
\overrightarrow{V}
-increasing (resp.
\overrightarrow{V}
-decreasing) if for all
X\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
and
c>0
such that
X+c\overrightarrow{V}\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
, it holds that
F(X)\ {\preceq}_{KM}\ F(X+c\overrightarrow{V})
(resp.
F(X)\ {\succeq}_{KM}\ F(X+c\overrightarrow{V})
). In the case that
F
is simultaneously
\overrightarrow{V}
-increasing and
\overrightarrow{V}
-decreasing,
F
is said to be
\overrightarrow{V}
-constant.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
(a,b)\not=\vec{0}
and
\overrightarrow{V}=\langle(a,b),\ldots,(a,b)\rangle
. A function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])

is said to be
(a,b)
-weakly increasing (resp.
(a,b)
-weakly decreasing) if for all
x\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
and
c>0
such that
x+c\overrightarrow{V}\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
, it holds that
F(X)\ {\preceq}_{KM}\ F(X+c\overrightarrow{V})
(resp.
F(X)\ {\succeq}_{KM}\ F(X+c\overrightarrow{V})
).

The previous definitions use vectors of
(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{n}
and the Kulisch-Miranker order on
\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
. In what follows we provide definitions which are based on admissible orders.

Definition 3.4
.

An IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be
weakly increasing
if

F(X_{1}+C,\ldots,X_{n}+C)\succeq F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
,

for all
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n},C\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
such that
(X_{1}+C,\ldots,X_{n}+C)\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
. Dually we define a
weakly decreasing
IV-function.

In what follows we introduce the notions of
\overrightarrow{V}
​-increasing and
\overrightarrow{V}
​-decreasing IV-functions.

Definition 3.5
.

Given
\overrightarrow{V}=(\boldsymbol{V_{1}},\ldots,\boldsymbol{V_{n}})\in\mathbb{I}(\mathbb{R})^{n}\setminus\{\vec{\boldsymbol{0}}\}
such that each
\boldsymbol{V_{i}}
is a degenerated interval for
i=1,\ldots,n
, an IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be
\overrightarrow{V}
​-increasing
, if

F(X_{1}+k\cdot\boldsymbol{V_{1}},\ldots,X_{n}+k\cdot\boldsymbol{V_{n}})\succeq F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
,

for all
0<k\in\mathbb{R}
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
such that
(X_{1}+k\cdot\boldsymbol{V_{1}},\ldots,X_{n}+k\cdot\boldsymbol{V_{n}})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
. Dually we define
\overrightarrow{V}
​-increasing
IV-function. In the case that
F
is simultaneously
\overrightarrow{V}
​-increasing and
\overrightarrow{V}
​-decreasing,
F
is said to be a
\overrightarrow{V}
​-constant IV-function.

Remark 3.6
.

The weak increase (resp. the weak decrease) implies the
\vec{\boldsymbol{1}}
-increase (resp. the
\vec{\boldsymbol{1}}
-decrease) of IV-functions.

Proposition 3.7
.

Every IV-implication
I:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is a
(\boldsymbol{-1},\boldsymbol{1})
-increasing IV-function.

Proof.

Indeed, if
I:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is an IV-implication, then for every
0<k\in\mathbb{R}
and
(X,Y)\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}
such that
(X+k\cdot(\boldsymbol{-1}),\boldsymbol{y}+k\cdot\boldsymbol{1})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}
we have:

I(X+k\cdot(\boldsymbol{-1}),Y+k\cdot\boldsymbol{1})=I(X-[k,k],Y+[k,k])\succeq I(X,Y+[k,k])\succeq I(X,Y)
,

since
I
is an IV-function decreasing in the first component and increasing in the second component.
∎

4
G\text{-}
weak monotonicity

Let us recall the notion of
g
-weakly increasing function on
U
provided by Santiago et al. in
[
2
]
:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
g:[0,\!1]^{2}\to[0,\!1]
be a function such that
g(x,y)\geq y
, for all
x,y\in[0,\!1]
. A partial function
f:[0,\!1]^{n}\to[0,\!1]
is said to be
g
-weakly increasing if
f(g(\lambda,x_{1}),\ldots,g(\lambda,x_{n}))\geq f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, for all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in[0,\!1]^{n}
and
\lambda\in(0,\!1]
. Dually we define a
g
-weakly decreasing function.

Next we provide the notion of
G
-weak monotonicity to the interval context considering on
\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
admissible orders.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be an IV-function such that
G(X,Y)\succeq Y
for all
X,Y\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
. An IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be
G
-weakly increasing
, if

F\left(G(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,G(\Lambda,X_{n})\right)\succeq F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
,

for all
\Lambda\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
and
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}
. Dually we define a
G
-weakly decreasing
IV-function.

Proposition 4.3
.

Every increasing (resp. decreasing) IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is a
G
-weakly increasing (resp.
G
-weakly decreasing) IV-function for every IV-function
G:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
such that
G(X,Y)\succeq Y
.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

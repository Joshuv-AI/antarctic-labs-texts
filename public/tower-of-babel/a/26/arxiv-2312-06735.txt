On the feasibility and usefulness of applying the ‘Schrödinger c.q. Liouville-von Neumann equation’ to quantum measurement
Willem M. de Muynck TU/e, Group SMB, FLUX 5.105, Postbus 513, 5600 MB Eindhoven, The Netherlands E-mail: W.M.d.Muynck@tue.nl

Abstract
The present paper is a sequel to papers [ 1 , 2 , 8 , 4 ] dealing with recent developments on the issue of ‘quantum measurement’. In this paper ‘measurement within the domain of application of quantum mechanics’ is treated as a quantum mechanical interaction of a ‘(sub)microscopic object ( o ) (o) ’ and an ‘equally (sub)microscopic part of the measuring instrument ( a ) (a) being sensitive to the (sub)microscopic information’, that interaction to be described by a Schrödinger equation. The Stern-Gerlach experiment is used as a paradigmatic example. An alternative to the Heisenberg inequality is found, exhibiting the independent contributions of ‘preparation of the initial state of object ( o ) (o) ’ and ‘interaction of object ( o ) (o) and measuring instrument/probe ( a ) (a) ’. Applicability of the Liouville-von Neumann equation is stressed.

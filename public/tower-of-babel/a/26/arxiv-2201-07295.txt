A detailed look at the Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson spaces
Jørgen Olsen Lye

Abstract
. This article takes a detailed look at the Ricci-flat metrics introduced by Eguchi-Hanson and Calabi on the canonical line bundle of complex projective space. We give a description of these spaces as resolutions of certain orbifold singularities. We then compute the curvature explicitly and show that all compact, minimal submanifolds are contained in the zero section. This extends a result by Tsai and Wang.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 32Q25; Secondary 53C42, 53C25

Key words and phrases: Calabi-Yau, Kähler-Einstein ALE, Minimal submanifolds.

1.
Introduction

Finding Einstein (pseudo-) Riemannian metrics has been an interesting project for more than a century. All the early successes rely on assuming a high degree of symmetry, reducing the Einstein equations to an ODE. This was successfully carried out by Eguchi and Hanson in
[
8
]
, allowing them to find a Ricci-flat, nowhere flat metric locally defined on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
, which in fact is a complete hyperkähler metric defined on the total space of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2)
of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The fact that the a priori real metric is Kähler and defined on a certain line bundle was quickly seen by Calabi
[
7
]
to not be a coincidence. Calabi
[
7
]
then proceeded to generalize the Eguchi-Hanson metrics to two families of spaces, giving explicit examples of complete hyperkähler metrics on
T^{*}\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
and complete Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-n)
. We call the latter family Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson spaces in this article, even though the first family would also deserve this name. The Calabi construction was taken up again by R. Bryant and S. Salamon
[
6
]
and M. Stenzel
[
27
]
, and they introduced Ricci-flat metrics on certain spin bundles and on
T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{n}
respectively. In the Stenzel case
T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the metrics are Kähler metrics.

A common feature of all these special holonomy spaces is that they are the total spaces of certain vector bundles over compact manifolds (
\mathbb{S}^{n}
or
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
, to be precise). These constructions were taken up again by B. Feix
[
9
]
and D. Kaledin
[
16
]
for arbitrary complex spaces
X
. They both independently showed that no matter which analytic metric on puts on
X
, one can extend it to hyperkähler metric defined on a neighbourhood of the zero section of
T^{*}X
, but one cannot hope for completeness. In
[
4
]
R. Bielawski proved that for any real analytic Kähler manifold
X
, one can extend the metric to be a Ricci-flat Kähler metric in a neighbourhood of the zero section in the canonical line bundle
\mathcal{K}_{X}
. This extends Calabi’s second family of examples,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-n)=\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}
, but one cannot in general hope for completeness.

Apart from being explicit examples of non-flat, Ricci-flat spaces, the Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson spaces provide simple but interesting examples of instantons and asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) spaces. For
n=2
they enter as crucial building blocks of Kummer K3 surfaces
[
26
,
11
,
18
]
, which has a generalization to
n=3

[
22
]
. In both cases, one glues in copies of Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson spaces into a singular quotient of a torus. One equips the Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson spaces with the Ricci-flat metrics we will discuss in this article, which one glues together with a flat metric on the torus. If the parameter in the Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson metric is small enough, this procedure produces a compact, almost Ricci-flat Calabi-Yau manifold. The Ricci-flat metric of Yau
[
29
]
can be shown
[
18
,
22
]
to be close to the given almost Ricci-flat metric.

The aims of this article are twofold. The first goal is to present an interpretation of the spaces
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-k)
as smooth resolutions of
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mu_{k}
, where
\mu_{k}
denotes the
k
’th roots of unity acting diagonally. The second goal is to give a more detailed analytic description of the Ricci-flat Calabi-Eguchi-Hanson metric. We also include an addition to
[
28
, Theorem 5.3]
. Much of the material is known in some form to the experts, but seems to be lacking a clear and explicit exposition. We hope the present article can serve as a gentle introduction to these fascinating spaces.

Calabi’s deduction of the Ricci-flat metric in
[
7
]
is a bit different. He instead works directly on the bundle
p\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-n)\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. On the trivializing sets
U_{i}
, he makes an ansatz for a Kähler potential on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-n)_{|U_{i}}
of the form

\Phi_{i}\coloneqq p^{*}\phi_{FS}+v(t),

where
\phi_{FS}
is the Fubini-Study Kähler potential on
U_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
,
t=|y|^{2}_{g_{FS}}
is the norm squared of an element in the fibre, and
v
is an unknown scalar function. Imposing Ricci-flatness, Calabi finds an ODE for
v
which he explicitly solves. This leads to the metric expression (
22
).

Calabi’s approach makes the completeness of the metric and the role of the Fubini-Study metric much more transparent. What we do in this paper is to start looking for rotationally symmetric Kähler potentials on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. These of course descend to quotients of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
by
\mu_{k}
for any
k
, and extend to Ricci-flat metrics on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-k)
if and only if
k=n
. These Ricci-flat metrics are exactly the same as the ones found by Calabi. We feel that the deduction presented in this paper offers a fruitful complementary access to these metrics.

Lemma 12
.

The quotient spaces
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mu_{k}

(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\})/\mu_{k}

Proof.

To see that
\mathbb{C}^{n}/\mu_{k}
is a variety, one can argue as follows.
For any finite group
G

means the

is linearly reductive, which all finite groups are. A reference for these facts is for instance
[

To see that

is not smooth near the origin, notice that it is not even a topological manifold there. A way to see this is as follows. Assume for contradiction that there is some neighbourhood

When the origin is removed from

\mu_{k}

∎

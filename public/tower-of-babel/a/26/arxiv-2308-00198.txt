Syntactically and semantically regular languages of λ \lambda -terms coincide through logical relations
Vincent Moreau, Lê Thành Dũng (Tito) Nguyễn

Abstract
A fundamental theme in automata theory is regular languages of words and trees, and their many equivalent definitions. Salvati has proposed a generalization to regular languages of simply typed λ \lambda -terms, defined using denotational semantics in finite sets. We provide here some evidence for its robustness. First, we give an equivalent syntactic characterization that naturally extends the seminal work of Hillebrand and Kanellakis connecting regular languages of words and syntactic λ \lambda -definability. Second, we show that any finitary extensional model of the simply typed λ \lambda -calculus, when used in Salvati’s definition, recognizes exactly the same class of languages of λ \lambda -terms as the category of finite sets does. The proofs of these two results rely on logical relations and can be seen as instances of a more general construction of a categorical nature, inspired by previous categorical accounts of logical relations using the gluing construction.

\{0,1,\infty\}\times\{\top,\bot\}
, where
\{0,1,\infty\}
is seen as the additive monoid

truncated to

. We consider the two set-theoretic functions
f_{\textup{{app}}}:Q\to(Q\Rightarrow Q)
and
f_{\textup{{abs}}}:(Q\Rightarrow Q)\to Q
defined as

f_{\textup{{app}}}(k,b)(k^{\prime},b^{\prime})\ :=\ (k+k^{\prime},\;b\wedge b^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\textup{{abs}}}(g)\ :=\ (g_{1}(0,\top),\;g_{2}(0,\top)\wedge(g_{1}(1,\top)\leq 1))

where
g_{1}:Q\to\{0,1,\infty\}
and
g_{2}:Q\to\{\top,\bot\}
are the compositions of
g:Q\to Q
with the two projections. We verify that, for any closed untyped term
t
, we have

\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket_{Q}(f_{\textup{{abs}}})(f_{\textup{{app}}})\quad=\quad(0,b)\qquad\text{where $b$ is $\top$ if and only if $t$ is affine.}

Therefore, if
F
is the subset of
\llbracket{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{UntypedTerms}}}{}{}\rrbracket_{Q}
defined as

F\ :=\ \{s\in((Q\Rightarrow Q)\Rightarrow Q)\Rightarrow(Q\Rightarrow Q\Rightarrow Q)\Rightarrow Q\mid s(f_{\textup{{abs}}})(f_{\textup{{app}}})=(0,\top)\}

then the
\mathbf{FinSet}
-
recognizable
language
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}L}_{F}{}{}
of terms of type
UntypedTerms
is the language of affine terms.

To the best of our knowledge, this regularity result is original. It also strongly suggests that the notion of
\mathbf{FinSet}
-
recognizable
language, when applied to the type
UntypedTerms
of syntax trees with binders, differs from the recognizability of these syntax trees by the nominal tree automata of
[

. Indeed, nominal automata cannot recognize the language of data words whose letters are all different
[
5
, Proof of Lemma 5.4]
.

4
Logical relations and the squeezing construction

Sconing in a nutshell

In this paragraph, we recall the construction of a
CCC of logical relations
from one CCC to another. We first recall the construction of logical predicates, also called sconing, which will be general enough to give logical relations as a special case. This method is well-known, see for instance
[
20
]
for an introductory account.

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a CCC. The category of logical predicates over
\mathbf{C}
, that we denote by
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
, is defined as follows:

•

its objects are the pairs
(c,S)
of an object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
together with a subset
S\subseteq\mathbf{C}(1,c)
,

•

its morphisms from
(c,S)
to
(c^{\prime},S^{\prime})
are the morphisms
f:c\to c^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{C}
such that
f\circ(-)
restricts to a set-theoretic function
S\to S^{\prime}
.

Claim 16
.

This category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
is a CCC, with exponentiation given by

(c,S)\Rightarrow(c^{\prime},S^{\prime})\quad=\quad(c\Rightarrow c^{\prime},\{f\in\mathbf{C}(1,c\Rightarrow c^{\prime})\mid\forall s\in S,\ev_{c,c^{\prime}}\circ\langle f,s\rangle\in S^{\prime}\})

The forgetful functor
(c,S)\mapsto c
is a CCC functor.

{claimproof}

See
[
20
, p. 5]
, where the notation
\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
is used for the category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
.

Definition 17
.

Let
\mathbf{C}_{1}
and
\mathbf{C}_{2}
be two CCCs. The
CCC of logical relations
from
\mathbf{C}_{1}
to
\mathbf{C}_{2}
is the CCC
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{C}_{1}\times\mathbf{C}_{2}){}{}
, which admits a CCC functor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{C}_{1}\times\mathbf{C}_{2}){}{}\to\mathbf{C}_{1}\times\mathbf{C}_{2}
.

Remark 18
.

We have defined the
CCC of logical relations
in terms of the logical predicate construction
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{P}}(-){}{}
of
Definition

15
. More concretely, this construction gives a category that can be described in the following way:

•

its objects are triples
(c_{1},c_{2},\Vdash)
where
c_{i}
is an object of
\mathbf{C}_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
\Vdash
is a subset of
\mathbf{C}_{1}(1,c_{1})\times\mathbf{C}_{2}(1,c_{2})
, and is thus a relation between the points of
c_{1}
and of
c_{2}
,

•

its morphisms from
(c_{1},c_{2},\Vdash)
to
(c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},\Vdash^{\prime})

(f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{C}_{1}(c_{1},c_{1}^{\prime})\times\mathbf{C}_{2}(c_{2},c_{2}^{\prime})

\text{if}\ x_{1}\Vdash x_{2}\ ,\quad\text{then}\ f_{1}\circ x_{1}\Vdash^{\prime}f_{2}\circ x_{2}\ .

For the proof that this category is a CCC, see
[
20
, Proposition 4.3]

Remark 19
.

The

from
\mathbf{C}_{1}
to

and to

\llbracket A\rrbracket_{(c_{1},c_{2},\Vdash)}\quad=\quad(\llbracket A\rrbracket_{c_{1}},\llbracket A\rrbracket_{c_{2}},\Vdash^{A})\quad\text{for some $\Vdash^{A}\ \subseteq\ \mathbf{C}_{1}(1,\llbracket A\rrbracket_{c_{1}})\times\mathbf{C}_{2}(1,\llbracket A\rrbracket_{c_{2}})$.}

The interpretation of a

-term

Remark 20
.

At this stage, the categories

The squeezing construction

We describe here a construction, which we call squeezing, which produces a CCC
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
from a CCC
\mathbf{C}
equipped with an additional structure that we call a
squeezing structure
. Intuitively, the objects of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
are objects of
\mathbf{C}
coming with bounds induced by the structure, inspired by the squeeze theorem of calculus. This construction can be seen as the proof-irrelevant counterpart to the twisted gluing construction described in
[
1
, Definition 5]
. The notion of
squeezing structure
that is used is related to the hypotheses of
[
8
, Lemma 6]
; this pattern also occurs in the older proof theory tradition, see for instance
[
9
, §8.A]
.

Throughout this paragraph, we fix any CCC
\mathbf{C}
. We recall that a wide subcategory is a subcategory containing all objects, and can hence be seen as a predicate on morphisms, closed under finite compositions.

Definition 21
.

A
squeezing structure
on
\mathbf{C}
is the data of

•

two wide subcategories
{\mathbf{C}}_{\operatorname{left}}
and
{\mathbf{C}}_{\operatorname{right}}
of
\mathbf{C}
with associated notations
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{l}}
and
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{r}}
for morphisms, which are stable under finite cartesian products and such that for all
u:c_{l}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{l}}c_{l}^{\prime}
and
v:c_{r}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{r}}c_{r}^{\prime}
,

v\Rightarrow u\ :\ c_{r}^{\prime}\Rightarrow c_{l}\overset{\operatorname{l}}{\longrightarrow}c_{r}\Rightarrow c_{l}^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u\Rightarrow v\ :\ c_{l}^{\prime}\Rightarrow c_{r}\overset{\operatorname{r}}{\longrightarrow}c_{l}\Rightarrow c_{r}^{\prime}\ .

•

for every object
c
of
\mathbf{C}
, two objects
L_{c}
and
R_{c}
of
\mathbf{C}
such that there exists morphisms:

\immediate\immediate\begin{matrix}L_{1}\overset{\operatorname{l}}{\longrightarrow}1\quad&\quad L_{c\times c^{\prime}}\overset{\operatorname{l}}{\longrightarrow}L_{c}\times L_{c^{\prime}}\quad&\quad L_{c\Rightarrow c^{\prime}}\overset{\operatorname{l}}{\longrightarrow}R_{c}\Rightarrow L_{c^{\prime}}\\
1\overset{\operatorname{r}}{\longrightarrow}R_{1}\quad&\quad R_{c}\times R_{c^{\prime}}\overset{\operatorname{r}}{\longrightarrow}R_{c\times c^{\prime}}\quad&\quad L_{c}\Rightarrow R_{c^{\prime}}\overset{\operatorname{r}}{\longrightarrow}R_{c\Rightarrow c^{\prime}}\ .\end{matrix}

(2)

Remark 22
.

As we work in a proof-irrelevant setting, we are merely interested in the existence of these morphisms. Nonetheless, knowing that they belong to
{\mathbf{C}}_{\operatorname{left}}
or
{\mathbf{C}}_{\operatorname{right}}
gets us back some precious information, as we will see in
Lemma

25
and
Section

6
.

Definition 23
.

For a
squeezing structure
on
\mathbf{C}
, the category
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
is the full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}
whose objects are the objects
c
of
\mathbf{C}
for which there exist both a left morphism
L_{c}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{l}}c
and a right morphism
c\xrightarrow{\operatorname{r}}R_{c}
.

Notice that we write
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
even though this construction depends both on the CCC
\mathbf{C}
and on a
squeezing structure
on
\mathbf{C}
.

Theorem 24
.

For a
squeezing structure
on
\mathbf{C}
, the category
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{C}){}{}
is a sub-CCC of
\mathbf{C}
.

The proof is in
Appendix

B
.

Logical relations which are partial surjections, i.e. both functional and surjective relations, can be a useful tool to obtain partial equivalence relations and to prove semantic results, see

[

Lemma 25
.

Let
\mathbf{C}_{1}
and
\mathbf{C}_{2}
be two CCCs and
\mathbf{R}
be the

from
\mathbf{C}_{1}
to
\mathbf{C}_{2}
, whose objects are triples containing relations. Suppose that we are given a

on
\mathbf{R}
such that

•

the relations in the objects

are surjective,

•

the relations in the objects

are functional,

•

the morphisms
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
{\mathbf{R}}_{\operatorname{left}}
are such that
\mathbf{C}_{2}(1,u_{2})
is a surjective function,

•

the morphisms
(v_{1},v_{2})
in
{\mathbf{R}}_{\operatorname{right}}
are such that
\mathbf{C}_{2}(1,v_{2})
is an injective function,

where, for

is the composition

.

Then, the relation of any object belonging to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{R}){}{}
is a partial surjection.

The proof is in
Appendix

C
. We end this section by giving a definition which will appear in

in

and in the proof of

.

Let

We now recall the definition of the class of CCCs

We start by introducing the following constructions, which will help us to use partial surjections.

be a category with a terminal object. We define the following full subcategories of

of simple types and
\lambda
-terms, the notion of

If
\mathbf{C}

.

.

. Moreover, the category

The proof is in
Appendix

B
. We now prove the interesting fact that

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{E}
be a

. By

Example 33

•

The category

Encoding recognizability by finite sets

We have shown in
Proposition

40
that we have a
squeezing structure
on the
CCC of logical relations

\mathbf{R}
from
\mathbf{Lam}
to
\mathbf{FinOrd}
. We now show how to use this structure, culminating in the link established in
Theorem

41
between
\mathbf{FinOrd}
-
recognizable
and
syntactically regular
languages.

Theorem 41
.

If a language is
\mathbf{FinSet}
-
recognizable
, then it is
syntactically regular
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a simple type and
L\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
be any
\mathbf{FinSet}
-
recognizable
language. There exists a finite set
Q
and a subset
F\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\langle}\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\rangle}{}{}
such that

L\ =\ \{t\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}\mid\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket_{n}\in F\}\ .

We take
n
to be the cardinality of
Q
and note
\chi:\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n}\to 2
the characteristic function associated to the subset
F
. By applying the functor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Bij}}_{(-)}{}{}
, we get a morphism of relations

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Bij}}_{\chi}{}{}:=({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(\chi)}{}{}\,,\,\chi)\quad:\quad\left({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n})}{}{},\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}_{\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n}}{}{}\right)\ \longrightarrow\ \left({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(2)}{}{},2,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}_{2}{}{}\right)\ .

The interpretation
\llbracket A\rrbracket_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Bij}}_{n}{}{}}
is of the form
(A[{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(n)}{}{}],\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n},\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}^{A}_{n}{}{}})
as explained in
Remark

5
. As it is an object of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\mathbf{Sqz}}(\mathbf{R}){}{}
, it has a
target-identity
into
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Bij}}_{\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n}}{}{}
. By composing this morphism with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Bij}}_{\chi}{}{}
, we obtain a morphism

(r\,,\,\chi)\quad:\quad(A[{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(n)}{}{}],\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n},\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}^{A}_{n}{}{}})\ \longrightarrow\ \left({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(2)}{}{},2,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}_{2}{}{}\right)\ .

By the
fundamental lemma of logical relations
, we get that, for every
\lambda
-term
t
of type
A
,

t[{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(n)}{}{}]\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}^{A}_{n}{}{}}\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket_{n}\qquad\text{on which we apply $(r,\chi)$ to get}\qquad r\ t[{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(n)}{}{}]\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Vdash}_{2}{}{}}\chi(\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket_{n})

which states that
r\ t[{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Fin}}(n)}{}{}]=_{\beta\eta}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{true}}}{}{}
if and only if
\chi(\left\llbracket t\right\rrbracket_{n})
is
1
. This proves that
r
recognizes the language
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}L}_{F}{}{}
given by
F\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\langle}\llbracket A\rrbracket_{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\rangle}{}{}
, and so that
L
is
syntactically regular
.
∎

7
Regular languages

In this section, we want to point out a few consequences of the equivalence previously proved through
Theorem

14
,
Theorem

36
and
Theorem

41
. Using these theorems, the following definition of
regular languages
is well-defined.

Definition 42
.

Let
A
be a simple type. A
regular language
of
\lambda
-terms of type
A
is a subset
L\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\Lambda}(A){}{}
such that one of the following equivalent propositions holds:

•

L
is
syntactically regular
;

•

L
is
\mathbf{C}
-
recognizable
, for some
non-degenerate
,
well-pointed
and
locally finite
CCC
\mathbf{C}
;

•

L
is
\mathbf{FinSet}
-
recognizable
.

We denote by
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.7,0,0}\operatorname{Reg}}(A){}{}
the set of
regular languages
of
\lambda
-terms of type
A
.

Remark 43
.

Note that
\mathbf{FinSet}
recognizes all the
regular languages
of
\lambda
-terms. In that sense, it plays the same role as the monoid

M\ :=\ \{f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\mid\exists N\in\mathbb{N},\forall n\geq N,f(n)=n\}\quad\text{with the composition of functions}

which recognizes all the regular languages of finite words as all finite monoids can be embedded into
M
. Such a monoid cannot be finite; however,
M
is a
locally finite monoid
, i.e. all its finitely generated submonoids are finite (this is a standard notion, see e.g.
[
11
, §V.5]
).

In the case of finite words, recognizability by finite monoids and locally finite monoids are equivalent when the alphabet is finite. In the case of
\lambda
-terms however, finite CCCs are all
degenerate
whereas the
locally finite
case yields
regular languages
of
\lambda
-terms, with some additional conditions.

Proposition 44
.

The set
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Reg}}(A){}{}
of
regular languages
of
\lambda
-terms of some simple type
A
is a Boolean algebra.

This fact boils down to stability by union or intersection. It is proved in
[
27
, Theorem 8]
using intersection types and in
[
34
, Proposition 2.5]
using logical relations. We provide another proof, showing that it is a direct consequence of our results.

Proof.

Using any of the three conditions of
Definition

42
, it is clear that
regular languages
are closed under complement.

The product CCC
\mathbf{FinSet}\times\mathbf{FinSet}
is
non-degenerate
,
well-pointed
and
locally finite
. It comes with two projections which are both CCC functors
\mathbf{FinSet}\times\mathbf{FinSet}\to\mathbf{FinSet}
. Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be two finite sets; we consider the object
(c,c^{\prime})
of
\mathbf{FinSet}\times\mathbf{FinSet}
. As explained in
Remark

5
, for any simple type
A
, we get that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{Q}(A){}{}\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{(Q,Q^{\prime})}(A){}{}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{Q^{\prime}}(A){}{}\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{(Q,Q^{\prime})}(A){}{}
.

Moreover,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{(Q,Q^{\prime})}(A){}{}
is a Boolean algebra. This shows that the intersection of a language in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{Q}(A){}{}
with another in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{Q^{\prime}}(A){}{}
can be taken in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Rec}}_{(Q,Q^{\prime})}(A){}{}
, so it is still a
regular language
. Therefore,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\operatorname{Reg}}(A){}{}
is a Boolean algebra.
∎

We now point out two other consequences of the equivalence in
Definition

42
.

•

As stated in the introduction, Statman’s finite completeness theorem tells us that singleton languages of
\lambda
-terms, taken modulo
\beta\eta
-conversion, are
regular languages
. It has multiple proofs, see
[
32
]
for proof directly in the finite standard model,
[
27
]
using intersection types,
[
17
]
in the model of complete lattices and
[
29
]
using Böhm trees.

These results are usually proved in one CCC. Using
Theorem

14
,
Theorem

36
and
Theorem

41
, we get that the singleton languages are recognized by any
non-degenerate
,
well-pointed
and
locally finite
CCC and are also
syntactically regular
.

•

Some CCCs satisfying these three conditions are the coKleisli categories of a model of linear logic, see
[
18
]
. In
[
19
]
, a notion of higher-order automaton is presented, which recognizes a language of
\lambda
-terms of a given simple type. The run-trees for these non-deterministic automata are defined using an intersection type system, which is an equivalent way of presenting the semantic interpretation
\left\llbracket-\right\rrbracket
of the simply typed
\lambda
-calculus in the coKleisli category
\mathbf{ScottL}_{!}
of the Scott model of linear logic. Using the equivalence proved in the present article, a language is recognized by a higher-order automaton, i.e.
\mathbf{ScottL}_{!}
-
recognizable
, if and only if it satisfies one of the conditions of
Definition

42
.

8
Conclusion and future perspectives

In this article, we have shown that every
non-degenerate
,
well-pointed
and

locally finite
CCCs recognizes exactly Salvati’s
regular languages
of

\lambda
-terms
[
27
]
, and that those also coincide
with
syntactically regular
languages. This is evidence for the robustness of this notion, and
therefore of the dual notion of profinite
\lambda
-term introduced in

[
34
]
.

Among the aforementioned conditions, non-degeneracy is needed to recognize
non-trivial languages, and local finiteness is clearly crucial: in the case of
finite words and trees, regularity is closely related to recognition by finitary
structures. What about well-pointedness? In other words, one question that
remains open is the following: is there a locally finite CCC that recognizes
languages of
\lambda
-terms that are not
regular
, i.e. not recognizable by

\mathbf{FinSet}
? For example, sequential algorithms famously form a locally finite CCC
which is
not
well-pointed,
cf.
[
2
, Chapter 14]
; we would like to
understand its recognition power.

As explained in
Example

2
, the
regular languages
of
\lambda
-terms of type
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0.7}\textsf{{Church}}}}_{n}{}{}
for some natural number
n
are exactly the usual regular languages of the finite words associated by the
Church encoding
. It is possible to encode words in other calculi, like the non-commutative affine
\lambda
-calculus in which case a syntactic approach analogous to
Definition

10
yields the star-free languages, see
[
22
]
. Moreover, gluing techniques have been studied for other calculi, see
[
12
]
for the linear case. One can therefore wonder whether it is possible to develop a semantic approach
à la
Salvati, analogous to
Definition

1
, for other calculi.

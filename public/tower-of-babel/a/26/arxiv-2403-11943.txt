Introduction

Consider a random polynomial

f=x^{n}+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],\quad a_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,H\},

(1.1)

with each
a_{i}
drawn uniformly and independently from
\{1,\ldots,H\}
. The question of how often such a polynomial is reducible or has a Galois group
G_{f}
smaller than
S_{n}
has been widely studied (along with variations where the coefficients are taken from
\{-H,\ldots,H\}
or other distributions). The two main regimes considered in the literature are when we fix
n
and take
H\to\infty
, called the
large box model
(or the fixed degree model), and when we fix
H
and take
n\to\infty
, called the
restricted coefficients model
.

In the large box model it was shown by van der Waerden
[
vdW36
]
that
\lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}\neq S_{n})=0
, and he conjectured that in fact
{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}\neq S_{n})=O(H^{-1})
as
H\to\infty
. After a series of incremental results due to Knobloch
[
Kno55
]
, Gallagher
[
Gal73
]
, Dietmann
[
Die12a
,
Die12b
]
, Chow-Dietmann
[
CD23b
]
and Anderson et al
[
AGLO+23
]
the conjecture was finally established by Bhargava
[
Bha21
]
, who was able to treat the remaining most difficult case of
G_{f}=A_{n}
(the alternating group). Van der Waerden also made the more precise conjecture
{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}\neq S_{n},S_{n-1})=o(H^{-1})
, which remains open. Here once again the remaining case is to show that
{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=A_{n})=o(H^{-1})
(except for
n=7,8
where additional cases remain). For
{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}\neq S_{n},A_{n})
an improved bound was obtained by Lemke-Oliver
[
LO23
, Corollary 1.3]
. For recent results on the related problem of counting number fields of fixed degree and Galois group ordered by discriminant see
[
CD23a
,
LO23
,
LO24
]
.

The restricted coefficients model received considerable attention over the last three decades, with important breakthroughs made in recent years. A folklore conjecture is that for fixed
H\geq 2
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n})=1
.
The weaker result
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n}\mbox{ or }A_{n})=1
was first established
by Bary-Soroker and Kozma
[
BSK20
]
for

H
divisible by at least four
distinct primes (e.g.

H=210
) and later by
Bary-Soroker, Kozma and
Koukoulopoulos

[
BSKK23
]
for all
H\geq 35
. Conditional on the Extended Riemann Hypothesis (for Dedekind zeta functions) Breuillard and Varjú
[
BV19
]
established
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n}\mbox{ or }A_{n})=1
for any
H\geq 2
. However showing that

A_{n}
occurs with
negligible probability
in the restricted coefficients model
proved to be a
formidable challenge. A similar difficulty in eliminating
A_{n}
occured in the work of Eberhard
[
Ebe22
]
on the Galois group of characteristic polynomials of random matrices.

The reason that the group
A_{n}
poses a unique challenge (compared to other transitive subgroups of
S_{n}
) is that it is highly transitive (the method of Breuillard-Varjú works by showing that
G_{f}
is highly transitive with probability close to 1) and the cycle structure of its elements looks quite “random” except for the parity condition (the methods of Bary-Soroker–Kozma–Koukoulopoulos use the fact that the elements of a transitive subgroup
A_{n}\neq G<S_{n}
have very special cycle structures). The natural approach to estimating
{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=A_{n})
in the restricted coefficients model is to apply the discriminant criterion and estimate
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathrm{disc}}(f)=\square)
. However the discriminant is a complicated expression in the coefficients of
f
(especially in the large degree limit) and it is typically very hard to determine how often it is a square.

In some variations of the problem this challenge can be overcome. In the case of Littlewood
polynomials (random polynomials with
coefficients
\pm 1
)
of degree
n\equiv 2,4\pmod{8}

one always has
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)\equiv 5\pmod{8}
(see
[
BSKK23
, §4]
), so
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)
can never be a square and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=A_{n})=0
in this model.
In the case of palindromic Littlewood polynomials

[
Hok23
]
one has
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)=(-1)^{n}f(1)f(-1)\cdot\square
and using this one can show
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}\subset A_{n})=0

[
Hok23
, Corollary 1.2]
(the precise typical Galois group has not been established in this model, though this might be accessible to existing methods under the ERH). In these and similar variations it is easy to tell when

{\mathrm{disc}}(f)
is not a square, which is not the case for the more basic model defined by (
1.1
).

The weaker statement
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(f\mbox{ is irreducible})=1
(first conjectured by Odlyzko and Poonen
[
OP93
]
for a variation with
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}\,(1\leq i\leq n-1),\,a_{0}=1
) is also only known for
H\geq 35
unconditionally and for all
H\geq 2
under the ERH, by the same machinery. Recently this has been established unconditionally for the Littlewood model
a_{i}=\pm 1
for a sparse sequence of values of
n

[
BSHKP24
]
. A variation with characteristic polynomials of random symmetric matrices was established in
[
FJSS23
]
under ERH.

In
[
EP23b
]
the author and Popov studied the function field analog of the restricted coefficients model, which we now describe (a different function field model involving additive polynomials was studied by the author, Bary-Soroker and McKemmie
[
BSEM24
]
). Let
q
be a fixed prime power and
d
a fixed natural number. Denote

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}=\{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]:\,\deg h\leq d\}

and consider the random polynomial

f=x^{n}+a_{n-1}(t)x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{1}(t)x+a_{0}(t)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t][x],\quad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d},

(1.2)

with

a_{i}
drawn uniformly and independently from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}
. For a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)[x]
we denote by
G_{f}
the Galois group of its splitting field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)
, provided that the irreducible factors of
f
are separable (otherwise we leave
G_{f}
undefined). We are interested in the distribution of
G_{f}
where
f
is a random polynomial as in (
1.2
) and
n
is large.

An important feature of the function field analog is that with positive probability
f
has a nontrivial factor lying in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
and thus having a cyclic Galois group, preventing
G_{f}=S_{n}
. This phenomenon likely persists in any reasonable variation of the model (e.g. if we require each
a_{i}
to lie in some subset
S_{i}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}
with
|S_{i}|\geq 2
). Indeed we may rewrite
f=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}(x)t^{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x][t]
and denote

c(x)={\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=\gcd(b_{0},\ldots,b_{d})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]

(the content of
f
in the variable
t
), so that
f(t,x)=c(x)g(t,x)
where
{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(g)=1
.
It is reasonable to expect that the GCD
of a fixed number of
random polynomials
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is

\neq 1
with
positive
probability, and
indeed it is shown
in
[
EP23b
, Proposition 1.9]
that
for a random polynomial
f
as in (
1.2
)
and any monic

c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x],\deg c=k

we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left({\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=c\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{d}}\right)\frac{1}{q^{k(d+1)}}

(1.3)

whenever
n>k
.

If
{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=c
and
g(t,x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)[x]
is separable (the latter condition holds with limit probability 1 by
[
EP23b
, Proposition 7.3]
) then the splitting field of
c(x)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)
, which has the form
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}(t)
and thus has a cyclic Galois group
C
, while the Galois group of the splitting field of
g
can be viewed as a subgroup of
S_{n-k}
via its action on the roots of
g
. Hence
G_{f}
can be viewed as a subgroup of
S_{n-k}\times C
.

With this notation, it was shown in
[
EP23b
, Theorem 1.7]
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(G_{f}\in\{S_{n-k}\times C,A_{n-k}\times C,S_{n-k}\times_{C_{2}}C\}\right)=1

(1.4)

(the third possibility
S_{n-k}\times_{C_{2}}C
occurs only if
|C|
is even and denotes the fiber product with respect to the unique nontrivial homomorphisms to the group
C_{2}
of order 2) and that assuming a uniform variant of the polynomial Chowla conjecture over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
one has
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n-k}\times C)=1
. In the present paper we show this unconditionally, thus resolving the ambiguity in (
1.4
), which is analogous to the
S_{n}
versus
A_{n}
ambiguity in the restricted coefficients model over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
q
be a fixed odd prime power,
d
a fixed natural number. Let
f=x^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(t)x^{i}
be a random polynomial with each
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq d}
drawn uniformly and independently. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n-k}\times C)=1,

where
k=\deg_{x}{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)
and
C={\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t))={\mathrm{Gal}}({\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is a cyclic group of order

\mathrm{lcm}(\deg P_{1},\ldots,\deg P_{l})
, with
{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{l}P_{i}
being the prime factorization of
{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
q
be a fixed odd prime power,
d
a fixed natural number. Let
f=x^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(t)x^{i}
be a random polynomial with each
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq d}
drawn uniformly and independently. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n}\,|\,f\mbox{ is irreducible})=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(G_{f}=S_{n}\,|\,{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=1)=1.

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
1
and the fact that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(f\mbox{ is irreducible})=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}({\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f)=1)=1-\frac{1}{q^{d}}>0
, which follows from (
1.4
) and (
1.3
).
∎

Remark 1.2
.

One may argue that a more natural analog of the model (
1.1
) over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
would be to take the coefficients
a_{i}(t)
to be monic of degree
d
or monic of degree up to
d
. Our methods, as well as the methods of
[
EP23b
]
, can be adapted with minimal changes to prove the assertion of Theorem
1
for the variation with
a_{i}
monic of degree
d
, chosen uniformly and independently. The distribution of the values of
k
and
|C|
is different in this model, but it can also be easily computed. We chose the model (
1.2
) for convenience and because it is the one studied in
[
EP23b
]
. The model with
a_{i}
monic of degree up to
d
, as well as other non-uniform distributions on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}
, would require a substantial enhancement of our methods.

Remark 1.3
.

It is possible to obtain a more explicit estimate in Theorem
1
of the form
P(G_{f}=S_{n-k}\times C)=1+O(n^{-\delta})
for some
\delta=\delta(q,d)>0
. We chose not to give the details here, as it would require reworking the results of
[
EP23b
]
(as well as
[
BSK20
, Lemma 9]
, a key result used therein) with more explicit error terms, which would significantly lengthen the exposition. Theorem
2
below, which is the main new ingredient in the present work, will be stated with an explicit error term of this form.

Remark 1.4
.

Our method crucially depends on the assumption that
q
is odd, as it uses the discriminant criterion for when the Galois group of a polynomial is contained in the alternating group, which does not apply in characteristic 2. The alternative criteria available in characteristic 2 lack the multiplicative structure we exploit in the present work, so this case would require entirely new methods.

2.1
Notation and conventions

Throughout the rest of the paper
p>2
is a fixed prime,
q
is a fixed power of
p
and
d
is a fixed natural number. All asymptotic notation will have implicit constants or rate of convergence which may depend on
q,d
, while dependence on other parameters will be stated explicitly. We will occasionally reiterate these assumptions for emphasis.

For a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t,x]
we will always denote by
{\mathrm{lc}}(f)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
its leading coefficient as a polynomial in
x
, by
f^{\prime}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t,x]
its derivative in the variable
x
and by
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
its discriminant in the variable
x
. For any

we denote

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq e}=\{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]:\deg h\leq e\},

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{\mathrm{mon}}_{e}=\{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]\mbox{ monic}:\deg h=e\}.

The cardinality of a finite set
S
is denoted by
|S|
. If
P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
is a prime and
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
then
P^{k}\|Q
means that
P
divides
Q
exactly
k
times. The algebraic closure of a field
K
is denoted by
\overline{K}
. By an absolutely irreducible polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t,x]
we mean a polynomial which is irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. If
A
is a ring and

we denote by
A/a=A/(a)
the quotient of
A
by the ideal generated by
a
.

2.2
Outline of the proof of Theorem
2

Our basic strategy is to subdivide the family of polynomials (
1.2
) into subfamilies with a fixed value of
f^{\prime}
, along which
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)
behaves more regularly. A similar strategy underlies the work of Sawin and Shusterman on the Hardy-Littlewood, Chowla and Bateman-Horn conjectures over function fields
[
SS22b
,
SS22a
]
as well as the work on squarefree values of polynomials over function fields by Poonen
[
Poo03
]
, Lando
[
Lan15
]
and Carmon
[
Car21
]
.

First we recall (see e.g.
[
BSEF23
, Equation (2.4)]
) that for a monic
f\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t][x]
with
f^{\prime}\neq 0
we have

{\mathrm{disc}}(f)=\pm{\mathrm{lc}}(f^{\prime})^{\deg f}\prod_{f^{\prime}(\alpha)=0}f(\alpha),

(2.1)

where the product is over the roots of
f^{\prime}
in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)}
counted with multiplicity (if
f^{\prime}=0
then
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)=0
).

The main idea of our proof is to subdivide the family of polynomials

\mathcal{F}=\Biggl\{x^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{i}:\,a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}\Biggr\}

(2.2)

into subfamilies of the form

\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}=\bigl\{f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p}:\,h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}\bigr\}

(2.3)

with
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that for a given
f_{0}
the derivative of any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}
is
f^{\prime}=f_{0}^{\prime}+(h(x)^{p})^{\prime}=f_{0}^{\prime}
and therefore if
f_{0}^{\prime}\neq 0
we have (from (
2.1
))

{\mathrm{disc}}(f)=\pm{\mathrm{lc}}(f_{0}^{\prime})^{n}\prod_{f_{0}^{\prime}(\alpha)=0}(f_{0}(\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}),

(2.4)

with the product being over the roots of
f_{0}^{\prime}
.

Let us now assume for simplicity that
f_{0}
is such that
f_{0}^{\prime}(t,x)
is absolutely irreducible (we will see later that with probability close to 1 as
f_{0}
ranges uniformly over
\mathcal{F}
,
f_{0}^{\prime}
is close to being absolutely irreducible. We will also see how to overcome the discrepancy when present). Then by (
2.4
) we have

{\mathrm{disc}}(f)=\pm{\mathrm{lc}}(f_{0}^{\prime})^{n}N_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t,\alpha)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)}(f_{0}(\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}),

(2.5)

where
\alpha
is a root of
f
in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)}
.
The set of elements
f_{0}(\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p},\,h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
is sufficiently regular for us to extract useful information about the prime (divisor) factorization of its typical element, using an elementary but delicate sieve argument. Using (
2.5
) we will be able to deduce that with probability close to 1, the discriminant
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)
has a prime divisor
P
of multiplicity 1 and therefore
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)\neq{\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square
.

For technical reasons we impose two additional conditions on
f_{0}
, which we will show are satisfied with probability close to 1. Namely, we require that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(f_{0}^{\prime}(t,x)/\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
and that
f_{0}
satisfies the following

Condition (
\bigstar
).
Write
f_{0}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}(x)t^{i}
. Then either
d=1
, or
b_{1}\neq 0
and

\frac{b_{1}}{(b_{1},b_{2})}\not\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p}]
.

We now state precisely the two main steps outlined above.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
q
is odd. Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
be uniformly random. If
f_{0}^{\prime}\neq 0
write
f_{0}^{\prime}=c(x)g(t,x)
with
c={\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}^{\prime})
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{0}^{\prime}\neq 0,\,g\mbox{ is absolutely irreducible and separable in }t,\,{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d},\,(\bigstar)\mbox{ holds},\,\deg c<n^{1/2}\right)\\
=1+O\left(q^{-n^{1/3}}\right).

(2.6)

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
q
is odd. Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
be such that
f_{0}^{\prime}=c(x)g(t,x),\,c={\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}),\,\deg c<n^{1/2}
,
g
is absolutely irreducible and separable in
t
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t))=S_{d}
and (
\bigstar
) holds. Let
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
be uniformly random and denote
f(t,x)=f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p}
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\mbox{ prime such that }n^{2/3}\leq\deg P\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n},P\|{\mathrm{disc}}(f)\right)=1+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right).

Consequently

{\mathbb{P}}({\mathrm{disc}}(f)\neq{\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square)=1+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right).

We now show how propositions
2.1
and
2.2

2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}

By Proposition

|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{E}|=O\left(q^{-n^{1/3}}|\mathcal{F}|\right).

(2.7)

For any

2.8
))

q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\left|\{f\in\mathcal{F}:{\mathrm{disc}}(f)={\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square\}\right|=\sum_{f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}}\left|\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}:{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0})={\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square\}\right|\\
=\sum_{f_{0}\in\mathcal{E}}\left|\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}:{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0})={\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square\}\right|+\sum_{f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{E}}\left|\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}:{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0})={\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square\}\right|\\
\leq O\left(|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right)+|\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{E}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\leq O\left(|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right)+O\left(q^{-n^{1/3}}|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\right)\\
=O\left(|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}+q^{-n^{1/3}}\right)\right)=|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}\cdot O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right).

Dividing by
|\mathcal{F}|q^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1}
gives
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathrm{disc}}(f)={\mathrm{const}}\cdot\square)=O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right)
, which is the assertion of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Our arguments can be refined to show that for any sequence
\omega(n)\to\infty
,
{\mathrm{disc}}(f)
has
\geq\frac{\log n}{\omega(n)}
prime factors of multiplicity one with limit probability 1. Heuristically the precise number of such factors should be
\sim\log n
with limit probability 1. We omit the details because a single such prime factor is sufficient for our purposes and we obtain a better error term for the weaker statement.

Remark 2.4
.

Proposition
2.1
essentially says that for a random

and

. Thus the first step in our argument is a large box result. A similar situation occurs in the proof of (

) in
[
EP23b
]
.

It remains to prove propositions
2.1
and
2.2
. Proposition
2.1
will be proved in §
3
. The proof of Proposition
2.2
, which is the technical core of the present work, occupies §
4
-
6
.

3
Irreducibility of the primitive part of
f^{\prime}

In the present section we prove Proposition
2.1
. The main tool is an explicit Hilbert irreducibility theorem for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)
, which was proved in
[
BSE21
]
. Before recalling its statement we introduce some notation and recall some basic facts about the Galois group of a specialization of a polynomial.
Let
p
be a prime (in the present section we do not have to assume
p>2
except in Lemma
3.5
and the proof of Proposition
2.1
),
q
a power of
p
,
m,d
natural numbers,
\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
a set of
m
independent variables and
x,t
additional independent variables. Throughout the present section by a separable polynomial we always mean separable in the variable
t
. Let
F\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,\boldsymbol{y},t]
be separable with
\deg_{t}F=d
and denote
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,\boldsymbol{y}))
. Let
\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{m}
be such that
F(x,\boldsymbol{\eta},t)
is separable with
\deg_{t}F(x,\boldsymbol{\eta},t)=d
and denote
G_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}={\mathrm{Gal}}(F(x,\boldsymbol{\eta},t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))
. Then one may view
G_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
as a subgroup of
G
defined up to conjugation (this well-known fact can be deduced as a special case of
[
Lan02
, Theorem VII.2.9]
). Denote

\mathcal{H}_{F}=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{m}:\,F(x,\boldsymbol{\eta},t)\mbox{ separable of degree }d,\,G_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=G\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
C>0
be a constant,
F\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,\boldsymbol{y},t]
be separable with Galois group
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,\boldsymbol{y}))
and
\deg_{x}F\leq n^{C}
. With notation as above we have

\left|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{n}^{\mathrm{mon}}\right)^{m}\right|=q^{nm}(1+O_{q,m,C,\deg_{\boldsymbol{y},t}F}(nq^{-n/2})).

Here the implicit constant may depend on
q,m,C,\deg_{\boldsymbol{y},t}F
(the total degree of
F
in the variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m},t
).

Proof.

Follows immediately from
[
BSE21
, Corollary 3.5]
.∎

We need a slight refinement of the above proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

In the setup of Proposition
3.1
let
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{n}^{\mathrm{mon}},\,1\leq i\leq m
be subsets with
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\geq nq^{n/2}
. Then

|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{m})|=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\right)\cdot\left(1+O_{q,m,C,\deg_{\boldsymbol{y},t}F}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{i}|}\right)\right).

The same is true if we require
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}
instead.

Proof.

The last assertion follows by applying the main assertion to
F(x,y_{1}+t^{n},\ldots,y_{m}+t^{n},t)
instead of
F
. Hence we only need to deal with the case
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{n}^{\mathrm{mon}}
.

For the present proof we assume that
q,m,C,\deg_{\boldsymbol{y},t}F
are fixed and hence will be omitted from asymptotic notation. We will prove the assertion by induction on
m
. If
m=1
we have (using Proposition
3.1
)

|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\geq|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}|\geq|\mathcal{A}_{1}|-\left|({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{\mathrm{mon}}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{f})\right|=|\mathcal{A}_{1}|-O(nq^{n/2})=|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\left(1+O\left(\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{1}|}\right)\right)

and the assertion follows.

Assume
m\geq 2
and that the assertion holds for
m-1
. We denote

\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}=(y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}),\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=(\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{m}),\quad\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{m}.

Denote also
d=\deg_{t}F
.
First we claim that

\bigl|\{\eta_{1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{n}^{\mathrm{mon}}:\,F(x,\eta_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{y}},t)\mbox{ not separable of degree $d$ or }{\mathrm{Gal}}(F(x,\eta_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{y}},T)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}))\neq G\}\bigr|=O(nq^{n/2}).

(3.1)

Indeed for any
\eta_{1}
from the set
E
appearing on the LHS of (
3.1
) and any
\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{\mathrm{mon}}_{n})^{m-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}})
, either
F(x,\boldsymbol{\eta},t)
is not separable of degree
d
or the group
G_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\leqslant{\mathrm{Gal}}(F(x,\eta_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{y}},t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}))<G
is smaller than
G
and therefore
\boldsymbol{\eta}\not\in\mathcal{H}_{F}
.
Consequently
(E\times({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]^{\mathrm{mon}}_{n})^{m-1})\cap\mathcal{H}_{F}=\emptyset
and therefore by Proposition
3.1
we must have
E=O(nq^{n/2})
as claimed.

Now for any
\eta_{1}\not\in E
we may apply the induction hypothesis to the polynomial
F(x,\eta_{1},\hat{\boldsymbol{y}},t)
, which is separable of degree
d
and has Galois group
G
, and conclude that

\Bigl|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap\left(\{\eta_{1}\}\times\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\right)\Bigr|=|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}|\left(1+O\left(\max_{2\leq i\leq m}\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{i}|}\right)\right).

Summing over
\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\setminus E
, noting that
|\mathcal{A}_{1}\setminus E|=1+O\left(\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{1}|}\right)
(by (
3.1
)) and using the assumption
|\mathcal{A}_{1}|\geq nq^{n/2}
we obtain

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{m})|&\geq|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}|\left(1+O\left(\max_{2\leq i\leq m}\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{i}|}\right)\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{1}|}\right)\right)\\
&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\right)\cdot\left(1+O\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{i}|}\right)\right).\end{split}

The assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
be quadratic and irreducible,
H\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x,t]
separable with
\deg_{t}H=d
. Assume that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(H(x,t)Q(t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))\cong S_{d}\times C_{2}
(
C_{2}
is the cyclic group of order 2). Then
H(x,t)=c(x)g(x,t)
where
c={\mathrm{con}}_{t}(H)
and
g
is absolutely irreducible and separable with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
.

Proof.

Let
K,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
be the respective splitting fields of
H,Q
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
. Since
{\mathrm{Gal}}(Q/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=C_{2},

{\mathrm{Gal}}(H/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))\leqslant S_{d}
, our assumption implies that
{\mathrm{Gal}}(K/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
and
K,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
are linearly disjoint over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
. Since the only cyclic quotient of
S_{d}
is
C_{2}
, the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
in
K
is contained in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
and by the above linear disjointness it must equal
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Consequently
K
is linearly disjoint from
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and therefore
{\mathrm{Gal}}(H/\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}(x))={\mathrm{Gal}}(H/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
and
H
is irreducible over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}(x)
. By Gauss’s lemma we have
H(x,t)=c(x)g(t,x)
with
c={\mathrm{con}}_{t}(H)
and
g
irreducible in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}[x,t]
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))={\mathrm{Gal}}(H/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
, as required.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
be uniformly random, where
\mathcal{F}
is given by (
2.2
). Then
{\mathbb{P}}((\bigstar)\mbox{ holds})=1+O(q^{-n/2})
(Condition (
\bigstar
) was defined in §
2.2
).

Proof.

If
d=1
Condition (
\bigstar
) is vacuous, so we assume
d>1
.
Write
f_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}(x)t^{j}
. Then
b_{1},b_{2}
are independent and uniform in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}
. If (
\bigstar
) fails then we may write
b_{1}=bu,\,b_{2}=bv
for some
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p}],v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
and
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
monic. For a given
b
with
\deg b=k
, there are at most
q^{\lfloor\frac{n-k-1}{p}\rfloor+1}
values of
u
and at most
q^{n-k}
possible values
v
such that
bu,bv\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}
. Given
\deg b=k
there are
q^{k}-1
possible values of
b
. Hence (using
{\mathbb{P}}(b_{2}=0)=q^{-n}
)

{\mathbb{P}}((\bigstar)\mbox{ fails})=\frac{1}{q^{n}}+\frac{1}{q^{2n}}\left|\left\{(b_{1},b_{2})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}\times({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}\setminus\{0\}):\frac{b_{1}}{\gcd(b_{1},b_{2})}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p}]\right\}\right|\\
\leq q^{-n}+q^{-2n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{k}q^{\lfloor\frac{n-k-1}{p}\rfloor+1}q^{n-k}\ll q^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}q^{\frac{n-k}{p}}\ll q^{-n}q^{n/p}\ll q^{-n/2}.

The assertion follows.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
q
is odd. Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
be uniformly random, where
\mathcal{F}
is given by (
2.2
). Then

{\mathbb{P}}(\deg{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}^{\prime})\geq k)=O\left(q^{-\min(k,n/2)}\right).

Proof.

The main tool for the proof of the lemma is
[
EP23a
, Lemma 6.2]
, which gives the estimate

\left|\{h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n}:\,Q|h^{\prime}\}\right|\ll\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{n-\deg Q}&0\leq\deg Q\leq\frac{n}{2},\\
q^{n-\deg Q+\frac{1}{p}(2\deg Q-n)},&\frac{n}{2}<\deg Q<n.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

for any
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}
, where
p=\mathrm{char}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})>2
. This estimate is derived using the theory of lattices over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
.

For
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
write
f_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}(x)t^{j},\,b_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n}
. Then
f_{0}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}^{\prime}t^{j}
and
{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}^{\prime})|b_{j}^{\prime}
for
0\leq j\leq d
. Therefore for
k<n
(which we assume WLOG because
\deg{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}^{\prime})<n
) we have (using (
3.2
))

\left|\{f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}:\,\deg{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0})\geq k\}\right|\leq\sum_{l=k}^{n-1}\sum_{c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{l}^{\mathrm{mon}}}\left|\{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n}:\,c|b^{\prime}\}\right|^{d+1}\\
\leq\sum_{l=k}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}q^{l}q^{(n-l)(d+1)}+\sum_{l=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}^{n-1}q^{l}q^{\left(n-l+\frac{2l-n}{p}\right)(d+1)}\ll q^{n(d+1)-k}+q^{n(d+1)-n/2}\ll q^{n(d+1)}q^{-\min(k,n/2)}.

Dividing by
|\mathcal{F}|=q^{n(d+1)}
we obtain the assertion of the lemma.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Set
p=\mathrm{char}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})>2
and denote

\mathcal{D}=\left\{h^{\prime}:\,h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]\right\}=\left\{\sum_{i\geq 0}e_{i}x^{i}:a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\,a_{i}=0\mbox{ if }i\equiv-1\pmod{p}\right\}.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
be a polynomial
(with
\mathcal{F}
defined by (
2.2
)). Then
f_{0}
can be written uniquely as

f_{0}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}(x)t^{j},\quad b_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{n}^{\mathrm{mon}},\,b_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-1}\,(1\leq j\leq d).

(3.3)

We have

f_{0}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}^{\prime}(x)t^{j},\quad b_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{i},

where

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\left\{nx^{n-1}+h:\,h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-2}\right\}\cap\mathcal{D},\,\quad\mathcal{A}_{i}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq n-2}\cap\mathcal{D}.

It is easy to see that the map

f\mapsto(b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{d}^{\prime}):\,\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{A}_{0}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{d}

is
q^{(d+1)(\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor+1)}
-to-1.

Let
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
be any irreducible quadratic polynomial. We will now apply Proposition
3.2
to the polynomial

F=(y_{d}t^{d}+y_{d-1}t^{d-1}+\ldots+y_{0})\cdot Q(t),

which has Galois group
G={\mathrm{Gal}}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x,y_{0},\ldots,y_{d}))=S_{d}\times C_{2}
. If
\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{F}
then
F(\boldsymbol{\eta},t)
is separable of degree
d
in
t
and
{\mathrm{Gal}}(F(\boldsymbol{\eta},t)/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}\times C_{2}
, so by Lemma
3.3
we can write
F(\boldsymbol{\eta},t)=c(t)g(x,t)
with
g
absolutely irreducible and separable,
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
. Therefore if
f_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
is uniformly random and
n
is large enough, by Proposition
3.2
(note that since
p>2
we have
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\gg q^{2n/3}
and the condition
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\geq nq^{n/2}
of Proposition
3.2
is satisfied for large enough
n
) we have

{\mathbb{P}}(f_{0}^{\prime}\neq 0,\,g=f_{0}^{\prime}/{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f_{0}^{\prime})\mbox{ is absolutely irreducible and separable in }t,\,{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d})\\
\geq\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{F}\cap(\mathcal{A}_{0}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{d})|}{|A_{0}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{d}|}=1+O\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq d}\frac{nq^{n/2}}{|\mathcal{A}_{i}|}\right)=1+O(nq^{-n/6}),

(3.4)

where in the last equality we used once again that
p>2
and therefore
|\mathcal{A}_{i}|\gg q^{2n/3}
.
By Lemma
3.4
we also have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(f_{0}\mbox{ satisfies }(\bigstar)\right)=1+O(q^{-n/2}),

(3.5)

and by Lemma
3.5
we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\deg{\mathrm{con}}_{t}(f^{\prime})\geq n^{1/2}\right)=O\left(q^{-\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor}\right)=O\left(q^{-n^{1/3}}\right).

(3.6)

Combining (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.5
) we obtain the assertion of Proposition
2.1
.

∎

4
Discriminants in
\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}
: preliminaries

In the remainder of the paper we prove Proposition
2.2
. In the present section we develop the necessary preliminaries before carrying out the main sieve argument in §
5
. The proof will be concluded in §
6
.

Throughout this section
q
is a fixed power of a prime
p>2
,
d
a fixed natural number and
n
is a parameter eventually taken to infinity. All asymptotic notation has implicit constants or rate of convergence which may depend on
q,d
.

We are assuming the reader’s familiarity with the basic theory of univariate function fields. Good sources on the subject are
[
Ros02
]
and
[
Sti09
]
. Henceforth by a function field we will always mean a univariate function field with field of constants
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For a function field

we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{M}
its set of prime divisors. If

is a finite extension of function fields and
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
lies over
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
, we will denote this by
\mathfrak{P}|\mathfrak{p}
. We will also denote the relative degree of
\mathfrak{P}
in

by
\deg_{N}\mathfrak{P}=\frac{\deg\mathfrak{P}}{\deg\mathfrak{p}}

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{M},f\in M

the corresponding valuation. The height
\mathrm{ht}_{M}(f)
is the degree of the polar divisor of

.

We now recall the setup of Proposition
2.2
. Let

f_{0}=x^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}(t)x^{i},\quad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq d}

(4.1)

be such that
f_{0}^{\prime}=c(x)g(t,x)\neq 0
with
g
absolutely irreducible and separable in
t
and
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
. We are also assuming that
f_{0}
satisfies Condition (
\bigstar
) of §
2.2
:

f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}(x)t^{i},\quad d=1\quad\mbox{or}\quad b_{1}\neq 0\mbox{ and }\frac{b_{1}}{(b_{1},b_{2})}\not\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x^{p}].

(4.2)

We will see below (Lemma
4.1
(i)) that this assumption guarantees that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t,\alpha)={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t,f_{0}(t,\alpha))
and that
f_{0}(t,\alpha)
is not a
p

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t,\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is a root of
g\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)[x]
in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)}
. This will be important in the sequel.

Our goal is to show that for a uniformly random
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
, if we make the additional assumption
\deg c<n^{1/2}
then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists P\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]\mbox{ prime}:\,n^{2/3}\leq\deg P\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n},\,P\|{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p})\right)\\
=1+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right).

(4.3)

From now on
f_{0}
will be of the form (
4.1
) with
f_{0}^{\prime}=c(x)g(t,x)
,
g
absolutely irreducible and separable in
t
with
{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
and we also assume (
4.2
). We do not yet require
\deg c<n^{1/2}
, this condition will come into play in §
6
.
In what follows
h
will always be a random polynomial drawn uniformly from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
.

Let
\alpha\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)}
be a root of
g
,

L={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t,\alpha),\quad F={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t),\quad K={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\alpha).

Note that since
g
is absolutely irreducible, the field of constants of
L
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the integral closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t,\alpha]
in
L
. We will identify prime divisors
P
of
F={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(t)
satisfying
v_{P}(t)\geq 0
with (monic) prime polynomials in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
in the usual way, and do the same for
K={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\alpha)
. We also identify the prime divisors
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
satisfying

with the nonzero prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}
via
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\{f\in\mathcal{O}:\,v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f)\geq 1\}
.

Let
H\in K[T]={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\alpha)[T]
be the monic minimal polynomial of
f_{0}(t,\alpha)
over
K
and denote

D={\mathrm{disc}}(H)={\mathrm{res}}_{T}\left(H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)\in K,\quad R={\mathrm{res}}_{T}\left(H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}\right)\in K

(resultant in the variable
T
). Since
g(T,\alpha)\in K[T]
is a minimal polynomial of
t
over
K
and is irreducible and separable in
t
, the extension
L/K
is separable and therefore
H
is separable and
D\neq 0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(i)

L=K(f_{0}(t,\alpha))
.

(ii)

f_{0}(t,\alpha)\not\in L^{p}=\{a^{p}:\,a\in L\}.

(iii)

R\neq 0
.

(iv)

Let
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
be a prime and denote
A={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]_{Q}
(localization at
Q
). Assume that
H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}\in A[T]
and
v_{Q}(D),v_{Q}(R)\in A^{\times}
. Let

\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{L},\,\mathfrak{q}|Q

and assume that

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+u^{p})\geq 1

for some
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
. Then
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{q}=1
and
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+u^{p})=1
.

Proof.

Denote
\gamma=f_{0}(t,\alpha)
.

(i).
As noted above the extension
L/K
is separable. We have
L=K(t)
, a (not necessarily monic) minimal polynomial of
t
over
K
is
U(T)=g(T,\alpha)\in K[T]
and hence
{\mathrm{Gal}}(U/K)\cong{\mathrm{Gal}}(g/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x))=S_{d}
(the latter equality was another assumption we made on
g
). The group
{\mathrm{Gal}}(U/K)=S_{d}
acts primitively on the
d
roots of
U
and therefore there are no intermediate fields between
K
and
L
. Thus either
L=K(\gamma)
and we are done, or
\gamma\in K
. If
d=1
we have
L=K
and the assertion is trivial. It remains to show that if
d>1
then
\gamma\not\in K
.

Assume by way of contradiction that
d>1
and
\gamma\in K
. We will show that this contradicts the assumption (
4.2
). Write
\gamma=w(\alpha)
for some
w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
. Since
g(T,\alpha)
and equivalently
f_{0}^{\prime}(T,\alpha)=c(\alpha)g(T,\alpha)\in K[T]
, is a minimal polynomial of
t
over
K
, we have

f_{0}(T,\alpha)+v(\alpha)f_{0}^{\prime}(T,\alpha)=w(\alpha)
for some
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
(here we used
\deg_{t}f_{0}=\deg_{t}f_{0}^{\prime}=\deg_{t}g=d
, so the coefficient
v(\alpha)
does not depend on
T
), equivalently

f_{0}(t,x)+v(x)f_{0}^{\prime}(t,x)=w(x),\quad v,w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x).

Plugging in
f_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}t^{i},\,f_{0}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}^{\prime}t^{i}
and recalling
d>1
, we obtain
b_{1}=-vb_{1}^{\prime},\,b_{2}=-vb_{2}^{\prime}
. Since by (
4.2
) we have
b_{1},b_{2}\neq 0
, it follows that

\left(\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{b_{1}^{\prime}b_{2}-b_{1}b_{2}^{\prime}}{b_{2}^{2}}=0,

which implies
\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x^{p})
. But this contradicts (
4.2
).

(ii)
Assume by way of contradiction that
f_{0}(t,\alpha)\in L^{p}
. Then we may write
f_{0}(t,\alpha)=\frac{w(t^{p},\alpha^{p})}{z(\alpha^{p})}
for some
w\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t,x],0\neq z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x)
(because
K[t,\alpha]=L
). Arguing as in part (i) we may write

z(x^{p})f_{0}(t,x)+z(x^{p})v(x)f_{0}^{\prime}(t,x)=w(t^{p},x^{p}),\quad u,v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}(x).

Comparing coefficients at
t,t^{2}
and using
p>2
we once again deduce
\left(\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\right)^{\prime}=0
, contradicting (
4.2
).

(iii)
Since
H\in K[T]
is monic and irreducible, we have
\deg_{T}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}<\deg_{T}H
(
\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}
kills the leading term) and therefore
R={\mathrm{res}}_{T}\left(H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}\right)\neq 0
iff
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}\neq 0
. Assume by way of contradiction that
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}=0
, so each coefficient of
H
is a
p
-th power. By (i) we have
\deg H=d
and we may write
H=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}^{p}T^{i},\,a_{i}\in K
. Then
\gamma^{1/p}
is a root of
\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}T^{i}
and therefore
[K(\gamma^{1/p}):K]\leq d=[K(\gamma):K]
and
\gamma^{1/p}\in K(\gamma)=L
, contradicting (ii).

(iv).
Since
H\in A[T]
is the (monic) minimal polynomial of
\gamma
over
K
, the element
\gamma
is integral over
A
. Since by assumption
D={\mathrm{disc}}(H)\in A^{\times}
and
L=K(\gamma)
it follows (e.g. by
[
Ser79
, Corollary III.6.2]
) that
A[\gamma]
is the integral closure of
A
in
L
.
Consequently
A[\gamma]=(A\smallsetminus QA)^{-1}\mathcal{O}
and the inclusion
\mathcal{O}\subset A[\gamma]
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q}{\xrightarrow{\sim}}A[\gamma]/\mathfrak{q}A[\gamma]
(since
\mathfrak{q}\cap A=QA
and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q}
is a field).

The assumption
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma+u^{p})\geq 1,\,u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
implies
\gamma\equiv-u^{p}\pmod{\mathfrak{q}}
for some
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]\subset A
and hence
A[\gamma]=A+\mathfrak{q}A[\gamma]
and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q}\cong A[\gamma]/\mathfrak{q}A[\gamma]\cong A/Q\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]/Q
(using
QA=A\cap\mathfrak{q}A[\gamma]
for the second isomorphism). Therefore
\deg\mathfrak{q}=\log_{q}|\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q}|=\log_{q}|{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]/Q|=\deg Q
and
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{q}=1
. Equivalently,
N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{q})=Q
.

It remains to show
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma+u^{p})=1
. Assume by way of contradiction that
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\gamma+u^{p})>1
, equivalently
\mathfrak{q}^{2}\,|\,\gamma+u^{p}
. Taking norms we obtain
Q^{2}\,|\,H(-u^{p})
. Write
H(-u^{p})=Q^{2}W,\,W\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
and take the derivative with respect to
\alpha
:

Q\left(2\frac{\partial Q}{\partial\alpha}W+Q\frac{\partial W}{\partial\alpha}\right)=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}(-u^{p}).

We see that
-u^{p}
is a common root of
H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}
modulo
Q
in
A
, hence
R={\mathrm{res}}_{T}(H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha})\in QA
, contradicting the assumption
R\in A^{\times}
.

∎

Next we define a set of “good” primes of
L
that will be used to carry out the sieve in §
5
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{P}_{L}
be the set of prime divisors
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(a)

\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}=\deg_{K}\mathfrak{p}=1
.

(b)

Any
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
lying over the same prime divisor
P\in\mathcal{P}_{F}
as
\mathfrak{p}
satisfies
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha),v_{\mathfrak{q}}(t)\geq 0
and if
Q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
is the prime of
K
lying under
\mathfrak{q}
then
H,\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}\in\left({\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]_{Q}\right)[T],\,v_{Q}(D)=v_{Q}(R)=1
(i.e.
Q
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
(iv)).

We also denote

\mathcal{S}_{m}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}:\,\deg\mathfrak{p}=m\}.

By condition (a) in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in S
we have
P=N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{P}_{F},\,Q=N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{P}_{K},\,\deg P=\deg Q=\deg\mathfrak{p}
and (since condition (b) implies
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(t),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)\geq 0
)
P,Q
can be identified with monic prime polynomials in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t],{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
respectively. In particular condition (b) is well-defined. Since
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(t),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)\geq 0
the prime divisor
\mathfrak{p}
can be identified with a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}
. In a suitable sense most primes in
\mathcal{P}_{L}
are in
\mathcal{S}
(see Lemma
4.4
below) and we aim to show the following:

(i)

With probability
1+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right)
there exists
n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}
such that
f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
is divisible by a unique
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
.

(ii)

If the above event occurs then with probability
1+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{1/3}}\right)
we have
P\,\|\,{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p})
, where
P=N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})
.

Some useful properties of the primes in
\mathcal{S}
are summarized in the following

Lemma 4.3
.

(i)

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
be distinct with
e=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\deg\mathfrak{p}_{i}\leq\frac{n-1}{p}
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathfrak{p}_{i}\mbox{ divides }\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\mbox{ for each }1\leq i\leq k\right)=q^{-e}.

(ii)

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathfrak{p}^{2}\mbox{ divides }f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)=0.

(iii)

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S},\,P=N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})
(recall
P\in\mathcal{P}_{F}
) and
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
a prime divisor lying over
P
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathfrak{q}\mbox{ divides }\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)\leq q^{-{\min\left(\deg\mathfrak{q},\frac{n-1}{p}\right)}}.

Proof.

(i).
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem the assertion is equivalent to

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\mbox{ divides }\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)=q^{-e}.

(4.4)

Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{p}_{i}=1
and
Q_{i}=N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]
is prime with
\deg Q_{i}=\deg\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. There is an isomorphism
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]/Q_{i}{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
induced by the inclusion
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]\subset\mathcal{O}
. Composing with the Frobenius if necessary and using the Chinese Remainder Theorem we see that
h(\alpha)\bmod\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}\mapsto h(\alpha)^{p}\bmod\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is an isomorphism
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]/\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{O}/\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.
Since we assumed
e=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\deg\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\deg Q_{i}\leq\frac{n-1}{p}
and
h
is picked uniformly from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
, the residue
h\bmod\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}
is uniform and therefore so is
h(\alpha)^{p}\bmod\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. The claim (
4.4
) follows.

(ii).

By condition (b) in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
(iv) are satisfied for
Q=N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{p})
. Therefore by Lemma
4.1
(iv) we cannot have
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})>1
, equivalently we cannot have
\mathfrak{p}^{2}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
.

(iii).
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S},\,P=N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})
,
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
a prime divisor lying over
P
. By condition (b) in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
, the assumptions of Lemma
4.1
(iv) are satisfied and therefore by Lemma
4.1
(iv) we have
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{q}=1
if
\mathfrak{q}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
for any
h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
. Hence we may assume
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{q}=1
(otherwise the asserted probability is 0).

Now if
\deg\mathfrak{q}\leq\frac{n-1}{p}
we may argue as in part (i) to obtain
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathfrak{q}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})=q^{-\deg\mathfrak{q}}
. If on the other hand
\deg\mathfrak{q}>\frac{n-1}{p}
then denoting
Q=N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{q})\in\mathcal{P}_{K}
we have (as in part (i)) an isomorphism
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[\alpha]/Q{\xrightarrow{\sim}}\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{q}
given by
h(\alpha)\bmod Q\mapsto h(\alpha)^{p}\bmod\mathfrak{q}
. Since
\deg Q=\deg\mathfrak{q}>\frac{n-1}{p}
, the map
h\mapsto\gamma+h(\alpha)^{p}\bmod\mathfrak{q}
is injective on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}
and we obtain

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathfrak{q}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)\leq\left|{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]_{\leq(n-1)/p}\right|^{-1}=q^{-\lfloor\frac{n-1}{p}\rfloor-1}\leq q^{-\frac{n-1}{p}},

as required.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

|\mathcal{S}_{m}|=\frac{q^{m}}{m}+O({n^{2}}q^{m/2})
whenever
m\geq 2\log_{q}n
.

Proof.

First denote
\mathcal{P}_{L,m}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}:\,\deg\mathfrak{p}=m\}
. Since we assumed that
g(t,x)
is absolutely irreducible, there exists a smooth projective curve
C_{L}
defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with function field
L
. The curve
C_{L}
has an affine plane model given by the equation
g(t,x)=0
. Since
\deg g=O(n)
, the genus of
C_{L}
is
O(n^{2})
. Therefore by the Weil bound

|\mathcal{P}_{L,m}|=\frac{q^{m}}{m}+O\left(\frac{n^{2}}{m}q^{m/2}\right)=\frac{q^{m}}{m}+O(n^{2}q^{m/2}).

It remains to check that for each of the conditions appearing in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
, the number of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
violating the condition is
O(n^{2}q^{m/2})
.

First we bound the number of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
violating condition (a), i.e. satisfying
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}>1
or
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{p}>1
.
If
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
and say
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}>1
, denote by
P
the prime of
\mathcal{P}_{F}
lying under
\mathfrak{p}
. Then

\deg P=\deg\mathfrak{p}/\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}\leq m/2
and there are
O(q^{m/2})
possible values of
P
, over
each of which lie at most
n
primes of
L
(since
[L:F]\leq n
). Overall we
have
O(nq^{m/2})
values of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
such
that
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}>1
and
similarly (using
[L:K]\leq d=O(1)
) there are at most

O(q^{m/2})
primes

\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
with
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{p}>1
. In total the number of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L,m}
violating condition (b) is
O(nq^{m/2})
, which is negligible.

Next we bound the number of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
violating condition (b).
Since
[L:F]\leq\deg_{x}g=O(n),\,[L:K]=\deg_{t}g=d=O(1)
, we have

\mathrm{ht}_{L}(t)=[L:F]\cdot\mathrm{ht}_{F}(t)=[L:F]=O(n),

\mathrm{ht}_{L}(\alpha)=[L:K]\cdot\mathrm{ht}_{K}(\alpha)=[L:K]=O(1),

\mathrm{ht}_{L}(f_{0}(t,\alpha))\leq(\deg f_{0})\cdot(\mathrm{ht}_{L}(t)+\mathrm{ht}_{L}(\alpha))=O(n^{2})

and since
H
is the monic minimal polynomial of
f_{0}(t,\alpha)
over
K
(and
[L:K]=O(1)
) its coefficients have height
O(n^{2})
. Consequently (using
\deg H=O(1)
) we have
\mathrm{ht}_{L}D,\mathrm{ht}_{L}(R)=O(n^{2})
.
Hence the number of primes of
L
at which one of
\alpha,t,D,R
or a coefficient of
H
or
\frac{\partial H}{\partial\alpha}
has a zero or a pole is
O(n^{2})
. If
\mathfrak{q}
is such a prime, the number of
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
lying over the same
P\in\mathcal{P}_{F}
as
\mathfrak{q}
is
\leq[L:F]=O(n)
. In total we have
O(n^{3})
primes
\mathfrak{p}
violating condition (b), which is
O(n^{2}q^{m/2})
provided
m\geq 2\log_{q}n
.

∎

5
Discriminants in
\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}
: the sieve

In the present section we maintain the setup and notation of §
4
.

Notation.
For any natural number
m
denote by
E_{m}
the event that
f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
has a unique prime divisor
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
. Denote by
E_{m}^{\prime}
the event that
{\mathrm{disc}}_{x}(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]
has a prime divisor of degree
m
with multiplicity
1
.

In the present section we establish by an elementary sieve argument that with probability close to 1, one of the events
E_{m}
with
n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}
occurs. In the next section we will see how this implies the same for
E_{m}^{\prime}
by showing that
\sum_{n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}}{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\setminus E_{m}^{\prime})
is small. This will give us (
4.3
), completing the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k,M,N
be natural numbers such that
8\log_{q}n\leq M\leq N\leq\frac{n-1}{2pk}
and
k\leq M
. For any
M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N
we have

{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m_{k}})=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k}{M}\right)\right).

Proof.

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
denote by
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
the event that
\mathfrak{p}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
. Using the shorthand

\underline{\mathfrak{p}}=(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{k}),\quad A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}=A_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap A_{\mathfrak{p}_{k}},\quad\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}=\mathcal{S}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times S_{m_{k}}

we have

E_{m_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m_{k}}=\bigcup_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\setminus\bigcup_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\underline{\mathfrak{q}}}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}}).

(5.1)

and by inclusion-exclusion

\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\underline{\mathfrak{q}}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}})\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)\leq\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}).

Combined with (
5.1
) this gives

\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}})-\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\underline{\mathfrak{q}}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}})\leq{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap E_{m_{k}})\leq\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}).

(5.2)

By Lemma
4.3
(i) and the assumption
N\leq\frac{n-1}{2pk}
, which implies
m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}\leq Nk\leq\frac{n-1}{p}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}})={\mathbb{P}}(A_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap A_{\mathfrak{p}_{k}})=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}

(5.3)

whenever
\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}
(i.e.
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{m_{i}},\,1\leq i\leq k
). Similarly, if for
\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}
we denote

I(\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}})=\{1\leq i\leq k:\,\mathfrak{p}_{i}\neq\mathfrak{q}_{i}\}

then

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}})={\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}A_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\cap\bigcap_{j\in I(\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}})}A_{\mathfrak{q}_{j}}\right)=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}-\sum_{j\in I(\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}})}m_{j}}

(5.4)

(Lemma
4.3
(i) applies because
2\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\leq 2Nk\leq\frac{n-1}{p}
).

Using (
5.3
) and Lemma
4.4
we obtain

\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}})=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}|\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}|=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\mathcal{S}_{m_{i}}|=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\frac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+O(n^{2}q^{-{m_{i}/2}}))\\
=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}(1+O(n^{2}q^{-M/2}))^{n}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)

(5.5)

(in the last equality we used the condition
M\geq 8\log_{q}n
). Similarly, using (
5.4
) we obtain

\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\underline{\mathfrak{q}}}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{I(\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}})=I}}{\mathbb{P}}(A_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap A_{\underline{\mathfrak{q}}})=\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\sum_{\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\underline{m}}\atop{I(\underline{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{\mathfrak{q}})=I}}q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}-\sum_{j\in I}m_{j}}\\
=\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}-\sum_{j\in I}m_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}|\mathcal{S}_{m_{i}}|\prod_{j\in I}|\mathcal{S}_{m_{j}}|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}\prod_{j\in I}m_{j}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\right)\\
\ll\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}\frac{1}{M^{|I|}}\ll\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}k\\
l\end{array}\right)\frac{1}{M^{l}}\leq\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\left(1+\frac{1}{M}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\cdot O\left(\frac{k}{M}\right).

(5.6)

Combining (
5.2
),(
5.5
),(
5.6
) we obtain the assertion of the lemma.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\log^{2}n\leq M\leq N\leq\frac{n-1}{2p\log^{2}n}
be integers. Then

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{M\leq m\leq N}E_{m}\right)=1+O\left(\frac{M}{N}+\frac{N\log^{2}N}{M^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Let
l\leq\log^{2}n
be an even natural number. By inclusion-exclusion and using Lemma
5.1
(note that for large enough
n
and any
k\leq l
the conditions of Lemma
5.1
hold) we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{M\leq m\leq N}E_{m}\right)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}{\mathbb{P}}(\cap_{i=1}^{k}E_{m_{i}})\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{l}{M}\right)\right)\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}+O\left(\frac{l}{M}\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\right).

(5.7)

We have

\sum_{k=1}^{l}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}\leq\prod_{M\leq m\leq N}\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)\leq e^{\sum_{m=M}^{N}\frac{1}{m}}=e^{\log N-\log M+O(1)}=O\left(\frac{N}{M}\right)

and hence the error term in (
5.7
) is
O\left(\frac{lN}{M^{2}}\right)
.
For any natural number
l
denote

U(l)=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{k}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}}

Then for even
l
we have

U(l)\leq 1-\prod_{M\leq m\leq N}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\leq U(l-1)

(e.g. apply inclusion-exclusion to a sequence of independent events
F_{m},\,M\leq m\leq N
with
{\mathbb{P}}(F_{m})=\frac{1}{m}
).
Now

\prod_{M\leq m\leq N}\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)\leq e^{-\sum_{m=M}^{N}\frac{1}{m}}=e^{\log M-\log N+O(1)}=O\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)

and

|U(l)-U(l-1)|\leq\sum_{M\leq m_{1}<m_{2}<\ldots<m_{l}\leq N}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}m_{i}}=\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{M\leq m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}\leq N\atop{\mathrm{distinct}}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}m_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{l!}\left(\sum_{M\leq m\leq N}\frac{1}{m}\right)^{l}\\
=O\left(\frac{(\log N-\log M+1)^{l}}{l!}\right).

Therefore

U(l)=1+O\left(\frac{M}{N}+\frac{(\log N-\log M+1)^{l}}{l!}\right)

and we conclude that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{M\leq m\leq N}E_{m}\right)=U(l)+O\left(\frac{lN}{M^{2}}\right)=1+O\left(\frac{(\log N-\log M+1)^{l}}{l!}+\frac{M}{N}+\frac{lN}{M^{2}}\right).

Taking
l=2\big\lfloor\frac{\log^{2}N}{2}\big\rfloor
we obtain the desired result.
∎

in Proposition

6
Discriminants in
\mathcal{F}_{f_{0}}
: bounding
\sum{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\setminus E_{m}^{\prime})

In the present section we conclude the proof of Proposition
2.2
by showing that the discrepancy between
E_{m}
and
E_{m}^{\prime}
is small when
m
lies in a suitable range. We maintain the setup and notation of §
4
-
5
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Denote
k=\deg c
. Then
{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\setminus E_{m}^{\prime})=O(nq^{-m})
whenever
\max(kd,8\log_{q}n)<m\leq\frac{n}{2p}
.

Proof.

Assume that
E_{m}
occurs. This means that
f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
has a unique prime divisor
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
. Denote
P=N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{P}_{F}
. We have
\deg P=m
(since
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}=1
). It is enough to show that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(E_{m}\mbox{ and not }P\,\|\,{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p})\right)=O(nq^{-m}),

(6.1)

since this event contains
E_{m}\setminus E_{m}^{\prime}
.

Recall
f_{0}^{\prime}(t,x)=c(x)g(t,x)
and write
g(t,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}g_{i}(t)x^{i},\,g_{s}\neq 0
. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}\in\overline{L}
be the roots of
g
(counted with multiplicity),
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}\subset L,\,k=\deg c
the roots of
c(x)
. Using (
2.4
) and the irreducibility of
g
, we have

\begin{split}{\mathrm{disc}}\left(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p}\right)&={\mathrm{const}}\cdot g_{s}(t)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{s}(f_{0}(t,\alpha_{i})+h(\alpha_{i})^{p})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(f_{0}(t,\beta_{j})+h(\beta_{j})^{p})\\
&={\mathrm{const}}\cdot g_{s}(t)^{n}N_{L/F}\left(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(f_{0}(t,\beta_{j})+h(\beta_{j})^{p}\right).\end{split}

Here
{\mathrm{const}}
denotes an arbitrary constant in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
.

First note that
\prod_{j=1}^{k}(f_{0}(t,\beta_{j})+h(\beta_{j})^{p})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[t]_{\leq kd}
because
\deg_{t}(f_{0})\leq d
and
\beta_{j}\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, so by the assumption
m>kd
this term is not divisible by
P
. The term
g_{s}(t)^{n}
is also not divisible by
P
since
\deg g_{s}\leq\deg_{t}g=d<m
. Consequently we have

v_{P}\left({\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0}(t,x)+h(x)^{p})\right)=v_{P}\left(N_{L/F}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})\right).

Therefore conditional on
E_{m}
, the negation of the event in (
6.1
) is equivalent to
v_{P}\left(N_{L/F}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})\right)=1
, which in turn (since
\mathfrak{p}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
,
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{p}=1
and any
\mathfrak{q}|P
satisfies
v_{\mathfrak{q}}(t),v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\alpha)\geq 0
by condition (b) in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
) is equivalent to the conjunction of the following two conditions:

(i)

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})=1
,

(ii)

v_{\mathfrak{q}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p})=0
for any
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
lying over
P
.

Since
\mathfrak{p}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
, (i) holds by Lemma
4.3
(ii).
If condition (ii) fails, there exists
\mathfrak{q}|P,\,\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathfrak{q}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}
. By condition (b) in the definition of
\mathcal{S}
and Lemma
4.1
(iv) we have
\deg_{K}\mathfrak{q}=1
. If in addition
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q}=1
, then
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{S}
and
\deg\mathfrak{q}=\deg P=m
, so
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
. This contradicts the uniqueness assumption in the definition of
E_{m}
. Therefore we must have
\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q}>1
. Hence using Lemma
4.3
(iii), Lemma
4.4
,
[L:F]=O(n)
and the assumption
8\log_{q}n<m\leq\frac{n}{2p}
,

{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\mbox{ and (ii) fails})\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}}\sum_{\mathfrak{q}|N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})\atop{\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q}>1}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathfrak{q}\,|\,f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(\alpha)^{p}\right)\ll\frac{q^{m}}{m}\cdot n\cdot\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}\atop{\mathfrak{q}|N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})\atop{\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q}>1}}}\left(q^{-\min(\deg\mathfrak{q},n/p)}\right)\\
=\frac{nq^{m}}{m}\max_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}\atop{\mathfrak{q}|N_{L/F}(\mathfrak{p})\atop{\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q}>1}}}\left(q^{-\min((\deg_{F}\mathfrak{q})m,n/p)}\right)\leq\frac{nq^{m}}{m}q^{-2m}\ll nq^{-m}.

Thus

{\mathbb{P}}\left(E_{m}\mbox{ and not }P\,\|\,{\mathrm{disc}}(f_{0}(t,\alpha)+h(x)^{p}))\right)={\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\mbox{ and (ii) fails})=O(nq^{-m}),

establishing (
6.1
) and completing the proof.
∎

6.1

Proof of Proposition
2.2

By Corollary
5.3

6.2

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}}E_{m}^{\prime}\right)\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigcup_{n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}}E_{m}\right)-\sum_{n^{2/3}\leq m\leq\frac{n}{3p\log^{2}n}}{\mathbb{P}}(E_{m}\setminus E_{m}^{\prime})\\

4.3

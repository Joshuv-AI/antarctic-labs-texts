Non-relativistic spin- 𝟑 \mathbf{3} symmetries in 𝟐 + 𝟏 \mathbf{2+1} dimensions from expanded/extended Nappi-Witten algebras
Ricardo Caroca, Diego M. Peñafiel, Patricio Salgado-Rebolledo

Abstract
We show that infinite families of Galilean spin- 3 3 symmetries in 2 + 1 2+1 dimensions, which include higher-spin extensions of the Bargmann, Newton-Hooke, non-relativistic Maxwell, and non-relativistic AdS-Lorentz algebras, can be obtained as Lie algebra expansions of two different spin- 3 3 extensions of the Nappi-Witten symmetry. These higher-spin Nappi-Witten algebras, in turn, are obtained by means of Inönü-Wigner contractions applied to suitable direct product extensions of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 , ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R}) . Conversely, we show that the same result can be obtained by considering contractions of expanded 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 3 , ℝ ) \mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R}) algebras. The method can be used to define non-relativistic higher-spin Chern-Simon gravity theories in 2 + 1 2+1 dimensions in a systematic way.

3.1
Expansions of higher-spin Nappi-Witten algebras

In order to define expansions of the spin-
3

\lambda_{i}\cdot\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{j}\cdot\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i\diamond j}\,,

(3.2)

[\boldsymbol{k}^{(i)},\boldsymbol{k}^{(j)}_{a}]=\epsilon_{a}^{\;\;b}\boldsymbol{k}^{(i\diamond j)}_{b},\quad[\boldsymbol{k}^{(i)}_{a},\boldsymbol{k}^{(j)}_{b}]=-\epsilon_{ab}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i\diamond j)}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{a}=\mathrm{d}A^{a}+\epsilon^{ab}A^{b}\,\alpha,

whereas the components of the curvature

3.4

It is natural to expect that the NR spin-

3

[

and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{X}^{(i)}=\dfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{k}-c^{2}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i)},

3.5
Flat limit

In the following section, we will use two particular semigroups to construct higher-spin NR symmetries, namely, the
S_{E}^{(n)}
and
S_{M}^{(n)}
semigroups
[
35
]
, defined by the following multiplication rules (
3.2
).

\hskip-65.44142ptS_{E}^{(n)}=\left\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n+1}\right\},\qquad i\diamond j=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}i+j&\mathrm{if}\quad i+j\leq n\,,&\\
n+1&\mathrm{if}\quad i+j>n\,.&\end{array}\right.

(3.54)

S_{M}^{(n)}=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\},\qquad\hskip 14.22636pti\diamond j=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}i+j&\mathrm{if}\,\,\,\,\ i+j\leq n\,,&\\
i+j-2\left[\frac{n+1}{2}\right]&\mathrm{if}\,\,\,\,i+j>n\,.&\end{array}\right.

(3.55)

Note that
S_{E}^{(n)}
has an extra
zeroth
element
\lambda_{n+1}
(
\lambda_{i}\cdot\lambda_{n+1}=\lambda_{n+1}\;\forall\;\lambda_{i}\in S_{E}^{(n)}
), not present in
S_{M}^{(n)}
. When defining an expanded Lie algebra, the fact that the semigroup
S_{E}^{(n)}
has a zero requires to impose the
reduction condition

T^{(n+1)}_{\alpha}\equiv 0
on the expanded generators, together with
\mu_{n+1}\equiv 0
and
\nu_{n+1}\equiv 0
(and additionally
\rho_{n+1}\equiv 0
for
S\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}
) on the invariant tensor constants introduced in (
3.25
)-(
3.27
). This condition is not necessary when defining expanded algebras by means of the semigroup
S_{M}^{(n)}
.

In
[
41
]
, it was shown that the expanded Nappi-Witten algebras
S_{E}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{nw}
define
Generalized Bargmann algebras
, i.e. an infinite family of extensions of the Bargmann algebra in
2+
1 dimensions. On the other hand, the expanded algebras
S_{M}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{nw}
were named
Generalized Newton-Hooke algebras
since they provide generalizations of the
S_{E}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{nw}
algebras that include a cosmological constant. By using the same semigroups in the definition of the expanded higher-spin Nappi-Witten algebras
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1}
and
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}
, we can define the following families of spin-
3
symmetries

\displaystyle\textnormal{Generalized spin-}3\;\textnormal{Bargmann algebras}:

\displaystyle S_{E}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1},\;S_{E}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2},

(3.56)

\displaystyle\textnormal{Generalized spin-}3\;\textnormal{Newton-Hooke algebras}:

\displaystyle S_{M}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1},\;S_{M}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}.

(3.57)

These symmetries are related by an Inöü-Wigner contraction, which can be interpreted as a vanishing cosmological constant limit or, equivalently, as the limit when the AdS radius, say
\ell
, becomes infinite. The constant
\ell
can be made explicit in the symmetry algebras
S^{(n)}_{M}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1}
and
S^{(n)}_{M}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}
previously presented by rescaling the expanded generators (
3.4
), (

), (
3.10
) in the form

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}_{a}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{k}_{a}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{k}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i)},

(3.58)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{ab}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{ab}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{a}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{a}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{a}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{a}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{a}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{a}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{ab}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{ab}^{(i)},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ab}^{(i)}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{ab}^{(i)},

and similarly for the invariant tensor constants given in (
3.25
)-(

\mu_{i}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\mu_{i},\quad\nu_{i}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\rho_{i},\quad\rho_{i}\rightarrow\ell^{i}\rho_{i}.

5
Concluding Remarks

We have constructed NR higher-spin theories of Galilean type in
2+1
space-time dimensions as expansions of particular higher-spin extensions of the Nappi-Witten algebras, namely,
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1}
and
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}
. These extended Nappi-Witten symmetries were obtained after applying Inönü-Wigner contractions to suitable extensions of
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)\;

\displaystyle\underset{c\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)\;

\displaystyle\underset{c\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}.

The expansion method applied to these algebras gave rise to two infinite families of NR spin-
3
symmetries, which are labeled by the coefficient
n
in the semigroups
S^{(n)}_{E}
and
S^{(n)}_{M}
used in the expansion mechanism. These are

\displaystyle\textnormal{Generalized spin-}3\;\textnormal{Bargmann algebras}:

\displaystyle S_{E}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\textnormal{Generalized spin-}3\;\textnormal{Newton-Hooke algebras}:

\displaystyle S_{M}^{(n)}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}.\;

(5.3)

The first level of these families of algebras reproduces the higher-spin extensions of the Extended Bargmann and Newton-Hooke algebras in three space-time dimensions previously found in
[
13
]
, whereas the
n=2
case yields novel spin-
3
NR symmetries in
2+1
-dimensions, which come to extend the (exotic) NR Maxwell algebra
[
62
]
and the NR AdS-Lorentz algebra
[
41
]
. Greater values of
n
correspond to spin-
3
extensions of Generalized Galilean symmetries algebras
[
48
,
41
]
. We have shown that these two families of NR spin-
3
algebras are related by a vanishing cosmological constant limit, i.e.

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{M}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}\simeq\mathfrak{hs_{3}nh1,2_{2+1}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\ell\rightarrow\infty]{\,}

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{E}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}\simeq\mathfrak{hs_{3}barg1,2_{2+1}}

(5.4)

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{M}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}\simeq\mathfrak{hs_{3}nr\textnormal{-}adslor1,2_{2+1}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\ell\rightarrow\infty]{\,}

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{E}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}\simeq\mathfrak{hs_{3}nr\textnormal{-}max1,2_{2+1}}

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{M}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\ell\rightarrow\infty]{\,}

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{E}\times\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1,2}.

Moreover, we have shown that all the algebras of this form define a NR version of an expanded
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
symmetry. The relation between relativistic and non-relativistic symmetries is however non-trivial. Indeed, the relativistic algebras needed to define a
c\rightarrow\infty
contraction leading to a non-degenerate invariant tensor are semidirect sum extensions of the expanded
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})
algebras considered in
[
49
]
. Unlike the known contractions giving rise to double and triple central extension in the spin-
2
case (see for example
[
63
,
62
,
90
]
), these extensions do not add only Abelian generators to the relativistic symmetries under consideration, but include Euclidean and Lorentzian symmetries in three dimensions (see (
4.56
), (
4.57
) and (
4.73
), (
4.74
)), which are expansions of the
\mathfrak{so}(3)
or the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)
algebras. The addition of such non-Abelian generators to the relativistic spin-
3
algebras constructed in
[
49
]
was not to be expected and provides a non-trivial generalization of the known spin-
2
constructions. In this way, the expansion mechanism produces a non-degenerate invariant bilinear form for every NR expanded algebra, together with a CS higher-spin gravity action. The particular cases
n=1,2
were explicitly worked out.

An interesting question for future research is the generalization of our results to the case of NR spin-
N
symmetries for
N>3
. Indeed the fact that CS forms based on the
\mathfrak{sl}(N)\times\mathfrak{sl}(N)
algebra define more general higher-spin gravity theories in
2+1
dimensions
[
10
]
admitting a flat limit
[
71
]
, together with the structure of the algebras
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(3)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2)

suggest that spin-
N
extensions of the Nappi-Witten algebra
\mathfrak{nw}
could be obtained as contractions of suitable extensions of
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
. A direct generalization of the structure (
5.1
) signals the symmetries

\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(N),\qquad\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(N-1,\mathbb{R})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(2),

(5.5)

as potential candidates to be expanded. One could as well explore extensions of
\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})
that look like

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(N-1,\mathbb{R})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(N-\mathfrak{m},\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{so}(N-\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle\underset{c\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle\quad\mathfrak{hs}_{N}\mathfrak{nw}(\mathfrak{1+m}),

(5.6)

and thus interpolate between the ones given in (
5.5
), which could lead to several possible higher-spin extensions of the Nappi-Witten algebra. We hope to explore this problem elsewhere.

Due to the recently discovered relations between NR geometry and condensed matter systems
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
, and in particular, the recent application of CS theories invariant under extended Nappi-Witten algebras to construct effective descriptions of the fractional quantum Hall effect
[
91
,
92
]
, it would be interesting to consider the NR spin-
3
CS theories here obtained in the description of collective excitations in quantum Hall systems along the lines of
[
93
]
, where NR higher-spin fields in three space-time dimensions appear in the multipole expansion of the low-energy excitations of a quantum Hall fluid. Moreover, in
[
92
]
, a supersymmetric extension of the Nappi-Witten algebra was considered in the description of the Moore-Read fractional quantum Hall state. Thus, supersymmetric extensions of the
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw1}
and
\mathfrak{hs_{3}nw2}
algebras defined along the same lines can be of interest in the exploration of collective excitations of the fractional quantum Hall effect in terms of superpartners
[

. Furthermore, a natural generalization of our results is the inclusion of a space-time boundary and the construction of (Hamiltonian reductions of) higher-spin WZW theories (along the lines of, for example,
[
95
]
), which could lead to interesting edge dynamics and could be useful both in the context of the holographic duality in 3D gravity as well as as in condensed matter physics. We expect to consider this possibility in a future work.

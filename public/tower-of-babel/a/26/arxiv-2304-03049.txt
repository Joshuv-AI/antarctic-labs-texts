Introduction

In 2004, A. A. Karatsuba in his seminar ‘‘Analytic number theory and applications’’ suggested the following problem: find the asymptotic formula for the sum

\Phi_{a}(x)=\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{\tau(p+a)},\qquad x\to+\infty,

where
\tau(n)
denotes divisor function,
a
is a fixed integer, and the summation is taken over primes not exceeding
x
.

This problem is a result of discussions between A. A. Karatsuba and V. I. Arnold. Note that it contains features of the following two problems from analytic number theory. The first one is Titchmarsh divisor problem. It is asking about the asymptotic behavior of the sum

F_{a}(x)=\sum_{p\leqslant x}\tau(p+a).

In 1930, Titchmarsh
[
3
]
proved the estimate

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\tau(p-1)=O(x).

He also proved under Generalized Riemann Hypothesis that

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\tau(p-1)\sim cx,\qquad c=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)}.

The first unconditional result was obtained by Yu. V. Linnik
[
4
]
. He proved with the dispersion method that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leqslant x}\tau(p-1)=cx+R(x),\qquad c=\frac{\zeta(2)\zeta(3)}{\zeta(6)},

\displaystyle R(x)\ll\frac{x}{(\ln x)^{\alpha}},\qquad 0<\alpha<1.

Subsequently, this result was refined by many authors (see
[
5
]
–
[
9
]
).
The second problem is to find the asymptotic formula for the sum

T(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{\tau(n)}.

In 1916, S. Ramanujan
[
10
]
proved that

T(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\biggl(a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{\ln x}+\dots+\frac{a_{n}}{(\ln x)^{n}}+O_{n}\biggl(\frac{1}{(\ln x)^{n+1}}\biggr)\biggr),

where
a_{n}
are some constants; in particular,

a_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\prod_{p}\sqrt{p^{2}-p}\,\ln{\frac{p}{p-1}}.

The aim of this paper is to find an upper bound for the sum
\Phi_{a}(x)
.

Since

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{p\leqslant x}1\asymp\frac{1}{\ln x},

and

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}\frac{1}{\tau(n+a)}\asymp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}},

then it is natural to expect that

\frac{1}{x}\Phi_{a}(x)\asymp\frac{1}{(\ln x)\sqrt{\ln x}}=\frac{1}{(\ln x)^{3/2}}.

In this paper, we obtain the upper bound

\Phi_{a}(x)\ll_{a}\frac{x}{(\ln x)^{3/2}}.

More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Let
a\geqslant 1
be a fixed integer. Then we have

\sum_{p\leqslant x}\frac{1}{\tau(p+a)}\leqslant{4K(a)}\frac{x}{(\ln x)^{3/2}}+O\biggl(\frac{x\ln\ln x}{(\ln x)^{5/2}}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle K(a)=K\beta(a),\qquad K=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\prod_{p}\sqrt{\frac{p}{p-1}}\,\biggl(p\ln\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{1}{p-1}\biggr),

\displaystyle\beta(a)=\prod_{p\,|\,a}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{p(p-1)\ln(p/(p-1))-1}\biggr).

Thus, the upper bound coincides with the conjectural order of magnitude of
\Phi_{a}(x)
.

In this paper we rely on Selberg’s sieve method. The essence of this method is the following. We need to estimate the sum

S(\mathcal{A},z)=\sum_{(n,P(z))=1}a_{n},

where
\mathcal{A}=(a_{n})
is a given sequence of non-negative real numbers and
P(z)=\prod_{p\leqslant z}p
is the product of all primes up to
z
. Since

\sum_{d\,|\,n}\mu(d)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }n=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

it follows that

S(\mathcal{A},z)=\sum_{n}a_{n}\sum_{d\,|\,(n,P(z))}\mu(d).

Let us introduce arbitrary numbers
\rho_{d}
(here
d\leqslant z
,
d\,|\,P(z)
), for which
\rho_{1}=1
. Then
\sum_{d\,|\,n}\mu(d)\,{\leqslant}\bigl(\sum_{d\,|\,n}\rho_{d}\bigr)^{2}
for all
n\geqslant 1
. Thus, we obtain

S(\mathcal{A},z)\leqslant\sum_{n}a_{n}\biggl(\sum_{d\,|\,(n,P(z))}\rho_{d}\biggr)^{2}=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\,|\,P(z)}\rho_{d_{1}}\rho_{d_{2}}A_{[d_{1},d_{2}]},

where

A_{d}=\sum_{n\equiv 0\,(\operatorname{mod}d)}a_{n}.

Further, assume that for considered
d
we have
A_{d}=Xg(d)+r_{d}
, where
g(d)
is multiplicative function,
X
is independent of
d
, and
r_{d}
is small in average. The coefficients
\rho_{d}
are chosen to minimize the quadratic form.

B=\sum_{d_{1},d_{2}\,|\,P_{a}(z)}\rho_{d_{1}}\rho_{d_{2}}g([d_{1},d_{2}]).

(1)

In our case,
A_{d}
has a more complicated form

A_{d}=X_{0}g_{0}(d)+X_{1}g_{1}(d)+\dots+X_{m}g_{m}(d),

where
g_{k}(d)
are some functions (generally speaking,
g_{k}(d)
are not multiplicative for
k\geqslant 1
), and the values
X_{k}
are independent of
d
. The coefficients
\rho_{d}
are constructed only from the first quadratic form (
1
) corresponding to the function
g\,{=}\,g_{0}
. In this case we observe the following effect. These coefficients simultaneously minimize the remaining quadratic forms corresponding to the functions
g_{k}
, at least in terms of the order of magnitude.

Note that the solution of Titchmarsh divisor problem is based on Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem.
In our paper we essentially use the analogue of Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem,
obtained by M. A. Korolev (see
[
11
, Lemma 13]
).
We observe that the methods of this paper can also be applied for other sums. For instance, one can show that if

T_{a}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant x\\
p,\,p+2\text{ primes}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\tau{(p+a)}},

where the summation is over twin primes, then

T_{a}(x)\leqslant\frac{c(a)x}{(\ln x)^{5/2}}(1+o(1)).

The proof of the lemma is complete.

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mathbf{i}|=m\end{subarray}}a_{\mathbf{i}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\leqslant z\\
p_{1}\nmid\,a\end{subarray}}f_{1}(p_{1})\cdots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}\leqslant z\\
p_{m}\nmid\,a\end{subarray}}f_{m}(p_{m})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\,|\,P_{a}(z)\\
d_{1},d_{2}\leqslant z\\
[d_{1},d_{2}]\equiv 0\,(\operatorname{mod}P)\end{subarray}}\frac{\rho_{d_{1}}\rho_{d_{2}}}{\varphi([d_{1},d_{2}])}J_{[d_{1},d_{2}]}(1),

where
P

p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{m}
. Applying (

S_{l}(z;a)\ll_{m_{0}}\frac{1}{H_{a}(z)}.

(38)

r_{4}\ll_{m_{0}}\frac{x}{(\ln x)^{3/2}H_{a}(z)}.

Let us find the asymptotic formula for
1/H_{a}(z)
. For this purpose we apply equality (
32
). For prime
p
we set

g_{0}(p)=\begin{cases}g(p),&\text{if }p\nmid a,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad h_{0}(p)=\frac{g_{0}(p)}{1-g_{0}(p)},

and define
g_{0}(d)
,
h_{0}(d)
for square-free
d
by multiplicativity. From the definition of the function
h_{0}

H_{a}(z)=\sum_{k\leqslant z}\mu^{2}(k)h_{0}(k).

Now we find the parameters

,
A_{2}
,

,
\kappa
corresponding to the function
g_{0}

\kappa=1

A_{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{H_{a}(z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{C\beta(a)}{\ln z}\biggl(1+O\biggl(\frac{1}{\ln z}\biggr)\biggr),

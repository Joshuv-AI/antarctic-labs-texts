1.
Introduction

We study non-negative solutions of the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u)\geq f(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1.1)

where
n\geq 2
and
A
is a Caratheodory function such that

C_{1}|\xi|^{p}\leq\xi A(x,\xi)\quad\mbox{and}\quad|A(x,\xi)|\leq C_{2}|\xi|^{p-1}

with some constants
C_{1}>0
,
C_{2}>0
, and
p>1
for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
It is also assumed that the function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
does not decrease on the interval
[0,\varepsilon]
for some real number
\varepsilon>0
.

We say that
u\in W_{p,loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a solution of (
1.1
) if

f(u)\in L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}A(x,\nabla u)\nabla\varphi\,dx\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(u)\varphi\,dx

for any non-negative function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

A partial case of (
1.1
) is the inequality

-\Delta_{p}u\geq f(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1.2)

where

\Delta_{p}u=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)

is the
p
-Laplace operator.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that solutions of (
1.1
) satisfy the relation

\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u=0;

(1.3)

otherwise we replace
u
by
u-\alpha
, where

\alpha=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u.

(1.4)

In so doing, the left-hand side of (
1.1
) obviously does not change and the right-hand side transforms to
f(u+\alpha)
.

The absence of nontrivial global solutions of differential equations and inequalities or, in other words, the blow-up phenomenon, traditionally attracts the attention of mathematicians [1–11].
We obtain exact conditions on the function
f
guaranteeing that any non-negative solution of (
1.1
), (
1.3
) is identically zero.
Let us note that the only relevant case is
n>p
. Really, in the case of
n\leq p
, any non-negative solution of the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u)\geq 0\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

(1.5)

is a constant
[
9
]
.
For
n>p
, in papers
[
3
,
4
]
, it was shown that (
1.2
) has no positive solutions if

\operatorname*{lim\,inf}\limits_{\zeta\to+0}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}}>0.

We manage to strengthen this
statement
(see Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
).

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n>p
and

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(\zeta)\,d\zeta}{\zeta^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}=\infty.

(2.1)

Then any non-negative solution of (
1.1
), (
1.3
) is identically zero.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n>p
and

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(\zeta)\,d\zeta}{\zeta^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}<\infty.

(2.2)

Then problem (
1.2
), (
1.3
) has a positive solution.

Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
are proved in Section
3
.
Now we demonstrate their applications.

Example 2.1
.

Consider the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u)\geq u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(2.3)

where
n>p
and
\lambda
is a real number.
Replacing if necessary the function
u
with
u-\alpha
, where
\alpha
is defined by (
1.4
) and applying Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that, for

\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p},

(2.4)

any non-negative solution of (
2.3
) is identically zero.
Condition (
2.4
) is exact. Really, by Theorem
2.2
, in the case of

\lambda>\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p},

the inequality

-\Delta_{p}u\geq u^{\lambda}\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

has a positive solution.

We note that (
2.4
) coincides with the analogous condition obtained in
[
3
,
4
]
.
Earlier in
[
9
]
it was shown that the blow-up of solutions of (
2.3
) is occur if

p-1<\lambda\leq\frac{n(p-1)}{n-p}.

Example 2.2
.

Let us examine the critical exponent

\lambda=n(p-1)/(n-p)

in (
2.4
). Consider the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u)\geq u^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}\log^{\mu}\left(e+\frac{1}{u}\right)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(2.5)

where
n>p
. For
u=0
, we assume that the right-hand side of (
2.5
) equals to zero.

According to Theorem
2.1
, if

\mu\geq-1,

(2.6)

then any non-negative solution of (
2.5
) is identically zero.
At the same time, Theorem
2.2
implies that, in the case of

\mu<-1,

the inequality

-\Delta_{p}u\geq u^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}\log^{\mu}\left(e+\frac{1}{u}\right)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

has a positive solution.
Thus, condition (
2.6
) is exact.

3.
Proof of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2

In this section, by
C
and
\sigma
we mean various positive constants that can depend only on
p
,
n
,
\varepsilon
,
\lambda
, and the ellipticity constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Also let
B_{r}
and
S_{r}
be the open ball and the sphere of radius
r>0
centered at zero and
A_{r}=B_{2r}\setminus B_{r}
.
As is customary, by
\chi_{\Omega}
we mean the characteristic function of a set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e.

\chi_{\Omega}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&x\in\Omega,\\
0,&x\not\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

We need a few preliminary statements.

Lemma 3.1

(Generalized Kato’s inequality)
.

Let
v\in W_{p,loc}^{1}(\Omega)
be a solution of the inequality

\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla v)\geq a(x)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a non-empty open set and
a\in L_{1,loc}(\Omega)
.
Then the function
v_{+}=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}v
is a solution of the inequality

\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla v_{+})\geq\chi_{\Omega_{+}}(x)a(x)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

where
\Omega_{+}=\{x\in\Omega:v(x)>0\}
.

Lemma
3.1
is proved in
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
.
Applying Lemma
3.1
with
v=\varepsilon-u
, we arrive at Corollary
3.1
given below.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
u
be a solution of (
1.1
). Then

u_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u+(1-\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\varepsilon

is a solution of the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\geq\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(x)f(u)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}
.

Lemma 3.2

(Weak Harnack inequality)
.

Let
n>p
and
u\geq 0
be a solution of (
1.5
).
Then

\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{2r}}\int_{B_{2r}}u^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{1/\lambda}\leq C\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r}}u

for all
\lambda\in(0,n(p-1)/(n-p))
and
r\in(0,\infty)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\geq 0
be a solution of the inequality

-\operatorname{div}A(x,\nabla u)\geq a(x)\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

where
a\in L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a non-negative function.
If
u^{\lambda}\in L_{1,loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for some
\lambda\in(p-1,\infty)
, then

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}a(x)\,dx\leq Cr^{-p}\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits A_{r}}\int_{A_{r}}u^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{(p-1)/\lambda}

for all
r\in(0,\infty)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n>p
and
u\geq 0
be a solution of (
1.5
), (
1.3
). Then

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r}}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{r}}=1,

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}
.

Proof of Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
can be found in
[

,
13
,
14
]
and
[
1
]
, respectively.
Lemma

is given in
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. Proof of this lemma follows immediately from Lemma

.
Indeed, by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\varepsilon\left(\frac{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{2r}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{2r}}\right)^{1/\lambda}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{2r}}\int_{B_{2r}}u^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{1/\lambda}\leq C\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r}}u

with some
\lambda\in(0,n(p-1)/(n-p))
for all
r\in(0,\infty)
.
In the limit as

, this yields

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{2r}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{r^{n}}=0.

Corollary

and Lemmas
3.2
and

lead to the following assertion.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
n>p
and
u\geq 0
be a solution of (
1.1
).
Then

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{r}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r}}f(u)\,dx\leq Cr^{-p}\left(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}

for all

,

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}

u_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}u+(1-\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})\varepsilon.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
u\geq 0
is a non-zero solution of (
1.1
), (
1.3
).
In view of Lemma
3.2
, this solution is positive almost everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
By Lemma
3.4
, there exists a real number
r_{0}>0
such that

\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r}\geq Cr^{n}

and

\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap A_{r}\geq Cr^{n}

for all
r\geq r_{0}
, where

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:u(x)<\varepsilon\}
.

We denote
r_{i}=2^{i}r_{0}
,
i=1,2,\ldots
.
Also let

E(r)=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r}}f(u)\,dx,\quad r>0.

Corollary
3.2
implies that

\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm mes}\nolimits B_{r_{i}}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{i}}}f(u)\,dx\leq Cr^{-p}\left(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r_{i}}}u_{\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,

whence we immediately obtain

\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{i}}}u=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{B_{r_{i}}}u_{\varepsilon}\geq\sigma\left(\frac{E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}\right)^{1/(p-1)},\quad i=1,2,\ldots.

(3.1)

By (
1.3
), this allows us to assert that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}=0.

(3.2)

Since
f
is a non-decreasing function on the interval
[0,\varepsilon]
,
formula (
3.1
) yields

f\left(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{i}}}u\right)\geq f\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right),\quad i=1,2,\ldots.

In view of the evident inequality

\frac{1}{\operatorname{mes}\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap A_{r_{i-1}}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap A_{r_{i-1}}}f(u)\,dx\geq f\left(\operatorname*{ess\,inf}\limits_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{i}}}u\right)

and the choice of the real number
r_{0}
, this implies that

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap A_{r_{i-1}}}f(u)\,dx\geq Cr_{i}^{n}f\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right)

or, in other words,

E(r_{i})-E(r_{i-1})\geq Cr_{i}^{n}f\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right),\quad i=1,2,\ldots.

The last estimate, in turn, yields

\frac{E(r_{i})-E(r_{i-1})}{E^{n/(n-p)}(r_{i})}\geq Ch\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,

(3.3)

where

h(\zeta)=\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}},\quad\zeta>0.

There obviously are sequences of integers
0<s_{j}<l_{j}\leq s_{j+1}
,
j=1,2,\ldots
, such that

\frac{E(r_{i-1})}{r_{i-1}^{n-p}}>\frac{E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}

if
i\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},l_{j}]
and

\frac{E(r_{i-1})}{r_{i-1}^{n-p}}\leq\frac{E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}

otherwise.
Multiplying (
3.3
) by the inequality

1\geq\frac{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})-r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})}{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})},

one can conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{E(r_{i})-E(r_{i-1})}{E^{n/(n-p)}(r_{i})}\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{Ch\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right)}{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})}

\displaystyle{}\times\left(r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})-r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right)

(3.4)

for all
i\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},l_{j}]
.
Since
E
is a non-decreasing function, we have

\frac{2^{n-p}E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}\geq\frac{E(r_{i-1})}{r_{i-1}^{n-p}}>\frac{E(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-p}}

for all
i\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},l_{j}]
. Hence,

\int_{E(r_{i-1})}^{E(r_{i})}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}\geq\frac{E(r_{i})-E(r_{i-1})}{E^{n/(n-p)}(r_{i})}

and

\displaystyle\frac{h\left(\sigma r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right)}{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})}\left(r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})-r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}\geq 2^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\int_{r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})}^{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta

for all
i\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},l_{j}]
, where

\tilde{h}(\zeta)=\inf_{(2^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\zeta,\,\zeta)}h,\quad\zeta>0.

Combining this with (
3.4
), we obtain

\int_{E(r_{i-1})}^{E(r_{i})}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}\geq C\int_{r_{i}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i})}^{r_{i-1}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{i-1})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta

for all
i\in\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},l_{j}]
, whence it follows that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{E(r_{s_{j}})}^{E(r_{l_{j}})}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}\geq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{r_{l_{j}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{l_{j}})}^{r_{s_{j}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{j}})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta.

(3.5)

Taking into account the inequalities

E(r_{s_{j+1}})\geq E(r_{l_{j}}),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,

we have

\int_{E(r_{s_{1}})}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{E(r_{s_{j}})}^{E(r_{l_{j}})}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}.

At the same time, (
3.2
) and the inequalities

r_{s_{j+1}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{j+1}})\geq r_{l_{j}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{l_{j}}),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,

allows us to assert that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{r_{l_{j}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{l_{j}})}^{r_{s_{j}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{j}})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta\geq\int_{0}^{r_{s_{1}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{1}})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta.

Hence, (
3.5
) implies the estimate

\int_{E(r_{s_{1}})}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}\geq C\int_{0}^{r_{s_{1}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{1}})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta.

(3.6)

It is easy to see that

\int_{E(r_{s_{1}})}^{\infty}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{n/(n-p)}}=\frac{n-p}{p}E^{-p/(n-p)}(r_{s_{1}})<\infty.

In so doing,

\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)\geq\frac{Cf(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta^{n(p-1)/(n-p)}}

for all
\zeta>0
in a neighborhood of zero, because
f
is a non-decreasing function on the interval
[0,\varepsilon]
. Thus, in accordance with (
2.1
) we obtain

\int_{0}^{r_{s_{1}}^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}E^{1/(p-1)}(r_{s_{1}})}\frac{\tilde{h}(\sigma\zeta)}{\zeta}\,d\zeta=\infty.

This contradicts (
3.6
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Without loss of generality, it can be assumed that
f(\zeta)>0
for all
\zeta\in(0,\varepsilon)
; otherwise we replace the function

by

\max\{f(\zeta),\zeta^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}\}.

Let us find the required solution of (

), (

) in the form

Here,

We note that the right-hand side of the last equality is well defined for all

and, moreover,

(3.7)

and

w_{\delta}(r)\leq C\delta^{n/(p-1)}r^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(\zeta)\,d\zeta}{\zeta^{1+n(p-1)/(n-p)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}

Really, by change of variables

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{\xi}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle w_{\delta}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{n-1}f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{\xi}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right)d\xi\right)^{1/(p-1)}d\zeta

(3.9)

\displaystyle{}\leq w_{\delta}(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\xi^{n-1}f\left(\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{\xi}{\delta}\right)^{-(n-p)/(p-1)}\right)d\xi\right)^{1/(p-1)}d\zeta

By direct differentiation, it can be verified that

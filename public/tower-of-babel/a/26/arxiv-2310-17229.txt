Geometry of exactness of moment-SOS relaxations for polynomial optimization
Didier Henrion

Abstract
The moment-SOS (sum of squares) hierarchy is a powerful approach for solving globally non-convex polynomial optimization problems (POPs) at the price of solving a family of convex semidefinite optimization problems (called moment-SOS relaxations) of increasing size, controlled by an integer, the relaxation order. We say that a relaxation of a given order is exact if solving the relaxation actually solves the POP globally. In this note, we study the geometry of the exactness cone, defined as the set of polynomial objective functions for which the relaxation is exact. Generalizing previous foundational work on quadratic optimization on real varieties, we prove by elementary arguments that the exactness cones are unions of semidefinite representable cones monotonically embedded for increasing relaxation order.

1
Solving POPs with the moment-SOS hierarchy

Given a compact semialgebraic set
{\mathscr{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a polynomial
f
in the vector space
\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}
of polynomials of
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of degree up to
d
, consider the
polynomial optimization problem
(POP)

v(f):=\min_{{\bf x}\in{\mathscr{X}}}f({\bf x}).

(1)

The notation emphasizes that the optimal value depends parametrically on the objective function.

The key observation behind the moment-SOS (sum of squares) hierarchy
[
7
,
5
,
4
,
8
]
is that the POP is equivalent to the primal-dual problems

\begin{array}[]{rcllcll}v(f)&=&\min_{{\bf y}}&\ell_{{\bf y}}(f)&=&\max_{v}&v\\
&&\mathrm{s.t.}&{\bf y}\in{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}&&\mathrm{s.t.}&f-v\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}\\
&&&\ell_{{\bf y}}(1)=1\end{array}

where the dual maximization problem consists of finding the largest lower bound
v\in\mathbb{R}
on
f
on
{\mathscr{X}}
, formalized as a linear conic problem in the convex cone
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
of polynomials of degree up to
d
which are positive on
{\mathscr{X}}
. The primal minimization problem is over vectors
{\bf y}
in the convex cone
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
of moments of degree up to
d
of positive measures on
{\mathscr{X}}
, which is the convex dual of
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
, defined as the set of linear functionals positive on
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
, cf. e.g.
[
8
, Thm. 8.1.2]
. The linear objective function in the primal conic problem is
\ell_{{\bf y}}(f):=\int_{{\mathscr{X}}}f({\bf x})d\mu({\bf x})
where
\mu
is a positive measure with moment vector
{\bf y}
, and the linear constraint
\ell_{{\bf y}}(1)=\int_{{\mathscr{X}}}d\mu({\bf x})=1
enforces that
\mu
is a probability measure.

If
{\mathscr{X}}:=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:g_{i}({\bf x})\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,m\}
is basic semialgebraic, defined by a given polynomial vector
{\bf g}=(g_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,m}
, then
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
can be approximated with other convex cones, the truncated quadratic modules

{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}:=\{p\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}:p=\sum_{i=0}^{m}s_{i}g_{i},\>s_{i}\in\Sigma[{\bf x}],\>s_{i}g_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{2r}\}

where
\Sigma[{\bf x}]\subset\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]
is the convex cone of sums of squares (SOS) of polynomials of
{\bf x}
and
g_{0}({\bf x}):=1
.

Remark 1
.

Note that if a polynomial equation enters the definition of
{\mathscr{X}}
, i.e.
g_{i}({\bf x})=0
instead of
g_{i}({\bf x})\geq 0
for some
i=1,\ldots,m
, then the corresponding weight
s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]
in the quadratic module
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
is not constrained in sign, while satisfying
s_{i}g_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{2r}
. This is consistent with the fact that two inequalities of opposite signs are equivalent to an equation. Without loss of generality, and for notational conciseness, in this note we use only inequalities.

Note that by construction the quadratic modules are monotonically embedded for decreasing relaxation order:

{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\subset{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r+1}_{d}\subset{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}.

(2)

Contrary to
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
, the truncated quadratic module
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
is
semidefinite representable
, i.e. it is the linear projection of a spectrahedron, itself defined as a linear section of the cone of positive semidefinite quadratic forms. Practically, this means that linear optimization in
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
can be done efficiently with powerful interior-point algorithms.

Let us denote by
{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
the convex cone dual to the truncated quadratic module
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
.
By convex duality, for the primal problem we have the reversed monotone embedding

{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\supset{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r+1}_{d}\supset{\mathscr{M}}({\bf g})_{d}
meaning that the moment cone is approximated from outside, or relaxed. This motivates the terminology
moment relaxation
to refer to
{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
.

Now we have all the ingredients to define the
moment-SOS hierarchy
also known as Lasserre’s hierarchy: a family of primal-dual convex semidefinite optimization problems whose size is controlled by the relaxation order
r\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{array}[]{rcllcrcll}\text{mom}(f)^{r}&:=&\inf_{{\bf y}}&\ell_{{\bf y}}(f)&\geq&\text{sos}(f)^{r}&:=&\sup_{v}&v\\
&&\mathrm{s.t.}&{\bf y}\in{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}&&&&\mathrm{s.t.}&f-v\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\\
&&&\ell_{{\bf y}}(1)=1.\end{array}

(3)

This primal-dual pair of semidefinite optimization problems is called the
moment-SOS relaxation
of order
r
. Note that by construction

\text{sos}(f)^{r}\leq\text{mom}(f)^{r}\leq v(f).

(4)

Assumption 1
.

For large enough
R\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
it holds
R^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{2}_{i}\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{2}
.

Since
{\mathscr{X}}
is bounded, it is always possible to add a redundant quadratic constraint
R^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{2}_{i}\geq 0
to the description of
{\mathscr{X}}
. So Assumption
1
is without loss of generality.
Then it follows from
[
6
]
that in (
3
) the primal is attained (i.e. the infimum is a minimum), there is no duality gap (i.e. the infimum equals the supremum) and the relaxed values are monotonically converging lower bounds on the value:

\text{sos}(f)^{r}=\text{mom}(f)^{r}\leq\text{sos}(f)^{r+1}=\text{mom}(f)^{r+1}\leq\text{sos}(f)^{\infty}=\text{mom}(f)^{\infty}=v(f).

Moreover if
{\mathscr{X}}
has an interior point, then the dual is attained (i.e. the supremum is a maximum). If
{\mathscr{X}}
does not have an interior point, e.g. if it is a low-dimensional algebraic variety, then additional algebraic or geometric conditions are required for the dual to be attained, cf.
[
2
,
1
,
8
]
.

2
Exactness cone

Beyond asymptotic convergence guarantees, it is important to know whether the moment-SOS relaxation of a given order
r
is
exact
, i.e. whether
\text{sos}(f)^{r}=v(f)
. If this is the case, there is no need to increase
r
and solve larger semidefinite optimization problems.

In this note, we are interested in the geometry of the
exactness cone
, defined as the set of objective functions which are such that the moment-SOS relaxation (
3
) is exact.

Definition 1
.

The exactness cone of degree
d
at relaxation order
r
is defined by

{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}:\ell_{\hat{{\bf y}}}(f)=\hat{v}=v(f),\>\hat{{\bf y}}\in{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d},\>f-\hat{v}\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\}.

Note that this set is a cone since
v(af)=av(f)
for all
a\geq 0
.

Our main result states that the exactness cone is a (generally uncountable and non-convex) union of semidefinite representable cones. We also describe the convex geometry of the exactness cones, their monotone embedding, and how they are related to normal cones of the moment relaxations.

Our analysis is elementary. It is inspired by the foundational work
[
3
]
which focused on the particular case of the Shor relaxation (
r=1
) with
f
linear (
d=1
) or quadratic (
d=2
), and
{\mathscr{X}}
a real algebraic variety defined by quadratic equations. We believe that our contribution consists of considerably simplifying and extending this analysis to higher order relaxations of general semialgebraic sets.

3
Main result

Theorem 1
.

The exactness cone at relaxation order
r
is given by

{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}=\bigcup_{\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}}{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}

(5)

where each cone

{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}:=\{f\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}:f-f(\hat{{\bf x}})\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\}

is semidefinite representable.

Proof:

If
f\in{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
then
\text{sos}(f)^{r}=v(f)=f(\hat{{\bf x}})
where
\hat{{\bf x}}
is a global minimizer of
f
on
{\mathscr{X}}
. As
\text{sos}(f)^{r}
is attained, we have
f-\text{sos}(f)^{r}=f-f(\hat{{\bf x}})\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
and thus
f
belongs to
{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
for some
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
.

Conversely, assume
f\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
for some
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
. Then from (
4
) we have
f(\hat{{\bf x}})\leq\text{sos}(f)^{r}\leq\text{mom}(f)^{r}\leq v(f)\leq f(\hat{{\bf x}})
, which implies that
\text{sos}(f)^{r}=\text{mom}(f)^{r}=v(f)
and hence
f\in{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
.

Cone
{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
is semidefinite representable since it is the projection of linear sections of the SOS cone
\Sigma[{\bf x}]
, which is itself semidefinite representable.

\Box

Remark 2
.

The truncated quadratic module
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
can be replaced by any other semidefinite representable approximation of
{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})
, e.g. the preordering of
{\bf g}
, or any other Positivstellensatz
[
7
,
8
]
.

Remark 3
.

The exactness cone is semialgebraic since
f
belongs to
{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
whenever 1)
f
belongs to
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
, a semidefinite representable hence semialgebraic cone, and 2) there exists
\hat{{\bf x}}
in
{\mathscr{X}}
, a semialgebraic set, such that
f
vanishes at
\hat{{\bf x}}
.

Remark 4
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
1
that it is enough to restrict the union (
5
) to points
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
which are optimal for some objective function
f
. For example if
d=1
, the union can be restricted to the set of extreme points of the convex hull of
{\mathscr{X}}
. In general however it is not easy to describe explicitly these subsets of
{\mathscr{X}}
.

Remark 5
.

In our definition of the exactness cone, we require that both primal and dual values are attained in (
3
). Since what matters is whether
\text{sos}(f)^{r}=v(f)
, we may relax our attainment requirements. The corresponding exactness cones would be slightly larger, at the price of more technicalities.

4
Geometry of exactness cones

Lemma 1
.

The exactness cones are monotonically embedded for increasing relaxation order:

\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{0}\subset{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\subset{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r+1}_{d}\subset\overline{{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{\infty}_{d}}=\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}.

Proof
:
The inclusion
R[{\bf x}]_{0}\subset{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
follows from the translation invariance of the value:
v(f+a)=v(f)+a
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
. The inclusion
{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}\subset{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r+1}_{d}
follows from the inclusion relations (
2
). Finally, the identity
\overline{{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{\infty}_{d}}=\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}
follows from Putinar’s Positivstellensatz – see e.g.
[
7
, Thm. 2.15]
– which states that under Assumption
1
every strictly positive polynomial of degree
d
on
{\mathscr{X}}
belongs to
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
for sufficiently large
r\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.
\overline{{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{\infty}_{d}}={\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
.

\Box

Remark 6
.

Note that in Lemma
1
the closure is required for asymptotic exactness of all degree
d
polynomial objective functions, i.e.
\overline{{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{\infty}_{d}}=\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}
. A classical example is
n=m=1
,
g_{1}(x)=-x^{2}
for which
{\mathscr{X}}=\{0\}
and Assumption
1
is readily satisfied. Whereras
f(x)=\pm x\notin{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{1}
for finite
r\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that
f(x)+\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(1\pm\frac{x}{\varepsilon})^{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{2\varepsilon}+\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{1}_{1}
for every
\varepsilon>0
, see
[
1
, Ex. 1.3.4]
or
[
8
, §2.5.2]
. For this example
{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{r}_{1}=\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{0}
for all finite
r
, and
\overline{{\mathscr{F}}({\bf g})^{\infty}_{1}}=\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{1}
.

Given
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
, let
{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}
be the Dirac vector at
\hat{{\bf x}}
, i.e. such that
\ell_{{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}}(f)=f(\hat{{\bf x}})
for every
f\in\mathbb{R}[{\bf x}]_{d}
.
Given a convex set
{\mathscr{Y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, let
{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{Y}}}({{\bf y}})
denote the normal cone to
{\mathscr{Y}}
at a point
{{\bf y}}\in{\mathscr{Y}}
.
Recall that
f\in{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{Y}}}({{\bf y}})
if and only if
{{\bf y}}\in\mathrm{argmax}_{{\bf y}\in{\mathscr{Y}}}\ell_{{\bf y}}(f)
, where
\ell_{{\bf y}}(f)
is a linear functional on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. With these notations, we have the following geometric counterpart to Theorem
1
.

Lemma 2
.

Up to the sign, the spectrahedral cones of Theorem
1
are normal cones to the moment relaxation:

{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}=-{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}({\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}})

for all
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
.

Proof:
First observe that extreme points of the moment cone
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
are Dirac vectors of
{\mathscr{X}}
, cf. e.g.
[
8
, Thm. 8.1.1]
. Dirac vectors which are also extreme points of the moment relaxation
{{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}
are of the form
{{\bf y}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}
for some optimal
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
for POP (
1
).

Therefore, if
\hat{{\bf x}}
is optimal then
{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}\in\mathrm{argmin}_{{\bf y}\in{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}\ell_{{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}}(f^{0})
with
f^{0}:=f-v(f)\in-{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}({\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}})
,

\ell_{{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}}(f^{0})=f^{0}(\hat{{\bf x}})=0
. Since
{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
is dual to
{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
, it follows that

f^{0}\in{\mathscr{Q}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}

and hence
f^{0}\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
.

If
\hat{{\bf x}}\in{\mathscr{X}}
is not optimal, then the Dirac vector
{\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}
cannot be on the boundary of the moment relaxation
{{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}
, so the normal cone
{\mathscr{N}}_{{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}}({\bf y}_{\hat{{\bf x}}})
at this point is zero, which trivially belongs to
{\mathscr{S}}_{\hat{{\bf x}}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}
.

\Box

Lemma
2
is a generalization of
[
3
, Prop. 4.7]
which considers only the case
d\leq 2
and the Shor relaxation (i.e.
r=1
) for real varieties
{\mathscr{X}}
generated by quadratic polynomials.

It is of interest to know whether we have exactness at relaxation order
r
for all objective functions. Obviously, a necessary and sufficient condition is that
{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{d}={\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{X}})_{d}
, i.e. all vectors of the moment relaxation are convex combinations of Dirac vectors of
{\mathscr{X}}
.
If
d=1
the condition becomes
{\mathscr{R}}({\bf g})^{r}_{1}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathrm{conv}\>{\mathscr{X}}
,
the Cartesian product of the positive real line with the convex hull of
{\mathscr{X}}
.

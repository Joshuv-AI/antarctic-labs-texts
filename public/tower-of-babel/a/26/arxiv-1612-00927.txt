Simplified Expressions of the Multi-Indexed Laguerre and Jacobi Polynomials
Satoru ODAKE and Ryu SASAKI

Abstract
The multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials form a complete set of orthogonal polynomials. They satisfy second-order differential equations but not three term recurrence relations, because of the ‘holes’ in their degrees. The multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials have Wronskian expressions originating from multiple Darboux transformations. For the ease of applications, two different forms of simplified expressions of the multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials are derived based on various identities. The parity transformation property of the multi-indexed Jacobi polynomials is derived based on that of the Jacobi polynomial.

2
Multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials

The foundation of the theory of multi-indexed orthogonal polynomials is the exactly solvable one-dimensional quantum mechanical system
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n}\phi_{n}(x),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,\qquad\mathcal{H}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+U(x),

(1)

and its iso-spectrally deformed system

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}\phi_{\mathcal{D}\,n}(x)=\mathcal{E}_{n}\phi_{\mathcal{D}\,n}(x),\quad n=0,1,\ldots,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+U_{\mathcal{D}}(x),

(2)

in terms of multiple application of Darboux transformations
[
4
,
17
]
. In this note we discuss two systems which have the Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials as the main parts of the eigenfunctions. We follow the notation of
[
17
]
with slight modifications for simplification sake.

2.1
Original Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials

2.1.1
Radial oscillator potential

The Hamiltonian with the radial oscillator potential

\displaystyle U(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}x^{2}+\frac{g(g-1)}{x^{2}}-2g-1,\qquad 0<x<\infty,\qquad g>\frac{1}{2},

(3)

has the Laguerre polynomials
L^{(\alpha)}_{n}(\eta)
as the main part of the eigenfunctions:

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(x;g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\phi_{0}(x;g)L^{(g-\frac{1}{2})}_{n}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}4n,\qquad\eta(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}x^{2},

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(x;g)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}x^{g},\qquad L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(\eta)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}}{k!}(\alpha+k+1)_{n-k}\eta^{k}.

Here are some identities of the Laguerre polynomials
[
24
]
to be used in Sections
2.4
and
2.5
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(\eta)=-L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(\eta),

(4)

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(\eta)-L_{n}^{(\alpha-1)}(\eta)=L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(\eta),

(5)

\displaystyle\eta L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(\eta)-\alpha L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(\eta)=-nL_{n}^{(\alpha-1)}(\eta).

(6)

As is clear from (
3
), the lower boundary,
x=0
, is the regular singular point with the characteristic exponents
g
and
1-g
. The upper boundary point,
x=\infty
, is an irregular singular point. Here
(a)_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(a+j-1)
is the shifted factorial or the so-called Pochhammer’s symbol.

2.1.2
Pöschl–Teller potential

The Hamiltonian with the Pöschl–Teller potential

\displaystyle U(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{g(g-1)}{\sin^{2}x}+\frac{h(h-1)}{\cos^{2}x}-(g+h)^{2},\qquad 0<x<\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad g>\frac{1}{2},\qquad h>\frac{1}{2},

has the Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\eta)
as the main part of the eigenfunctions:

\displaystyle\phi_{n}\bigl(x;(g,h)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\phi_{0}\bigl(x;(g,h)\bigr)P_{n}^{(g-\frac{1}{2},h-\frac{1}{2})}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\eta(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\cos 2x,

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\bigl(x;(g,h)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}(\sin x)^{g}(\cos x)^{h},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{n}(g,h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}4n(n+g+h),

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\eta)=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{n}}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{k}}{(\alpha+1)_{k}}\left(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\right)^{k}.

Here are some identities of the Jacobi polynomials
[
24
]
to be used in in Sections
2.4
and
2.5
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\eta)=\tfrac{1}{2}(n+\alpha+\beta+1)P_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(\eta),

(7)

\displaystyle(n+\beta)P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-1)}(\eta)-\beta P_{n}^{(\alpha-1,\beta)}(\eta)=(n+\alpha+\beta)\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta+1)}(\eta),

(8)

\displaystyle(n+\alpha)P_{n}^{(\alpha-1,\beta)}(\eta)-\alpha P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-1)}(\eta)=-(n+\alpha+\beta)\tfrac{1-\eta}{2}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta)}(\eta).

(9)

The two boundary points,
x=0,\frac{\pi}{2}
are the regular singular points with the characteristic exponents
g
,
1-g
and
h
,
1-h
, respectively.

2.2
Discrete symmetry transformations and virtual state wavefunctions

The seed solutions (virtual state wavefunctions) for Darboux transformations can be constructed by applying discrete symmetry transformations of the
Hamiltonian to the eigenfunctions. They have negative energies and have no node and they are not square integrable, see
[
17
]
for more detail.

2.2.1
Laguerre (L) system

Type
I
transformation.
It is obvious that the transformation
x\to ix
is the symmetry of the radial oscillator system. The seed solutions are

\displaystyle\text{L1}\colon\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{0}(x)\xi^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}e^{\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}x^{g},

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}L^{(g-\frac{1}{2})}_{\text{v}}(-\eta),\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-4(g+\text{v}+\tfrac{1}{2}),\qquad\text{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Hereafter we use
v
for the degree of the seed polynomial
\xi_{\text{v}}
in order to distinguish it with the degree
n
of eigenpolynomial
P_{n}
.

Type
II
transformation.
The exchange of the characteristic exponents of the regular singular point
x=0
,
g\to 1-g
, is another discrete symmetry transformation. It generates the seed solutions,

\displaystyle\text{L2}\colon\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{0}(x)\xi^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}x^{1-g},

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}L^{(\frac{1}{2}-g)}_{\text{v}}(\eta),\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-4\big(g-\text{v}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big),\qquad\text{v}=0,1,\ldots,\big[g-\tfrac{1}{2}\big]^{\prime},

in which
[a]^{\prime}
denotes the greatest integer less than
a
.

2.2.2
Jacobi (J) system

Type
I
transformation.
The exchange of the characteristic exponents of the regular singular point
x=\frac{\pi}{2}
,
h\to 1-h
, is a discrete symmetry transformation:

\displaystyle\text{J1}\colon\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{0}(x)\xi^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{I}}_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}(\sin x)^{g}(\cos x)^{1-h},

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}P^{(g-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-h)}_{\text{v}}(\eta),\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{I}}_{\text{v}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-4\big(g+\text{v}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\big(h-\text{v}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

\displaystyle\text{v}=0,1,\ldots,\big[h-\tfrac{1}{2}\big]^{\prime}.

Type
II
transformation.
Likewise the exchange of the characteristic exponents of the regular singular point
x=0
,
g\to 1-g
, is a discrete symmetry transformation:

\displaystyle\text{J2}\colon\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{0}(x)\xi^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad\tilde{\phi}^{\text{II}}_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}(\sin x)^{1-g}(\cos x)^{h},

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}P^{(\frac{1}{2}-g,h-\frac{1}{2})}_{\text{v}}(\eta),\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\text{II}}_{\text{v}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-4\big(g-\text{v}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\big(h+\text{v}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

\displaystyle\text{v}=0,1,\ldots,\big[g-\tfrac{1}{2}\big]^{\prime}.

2.3
Wronskian forms of the multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials

We deform the original Hamiltonian system (
1
) by applying multiple Darboux transformations
[
4
,
17
]
in terms of
M_{\text{I}}
Type
I
seed solutions and
M_{\text{II}}
Type

\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{M}\}

M_{\text{I}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,d_{j}\in\mathcal{D},\,\text{Type $\text{I}$}\}
,
M_{\text{II}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,d_{j}\in\mathcal{D},\,\text{Type $\text{II}$}\}
. The multi-indexed orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)\}
are the main parts of the eigenfunctions
\{\phi_{\mathcal{D}\,n}(x)\}
of the deformed Hamiltonian system
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{D}}
(

\displaystyle U_{\mathcal{D}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}U(x)-2\partial_{x}^{2}\log\bigl|\text{W}\big[\tilde{\phi}_{d_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{\phi}_{d_{M}}\big](x)\bigr|,

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{D}\,n}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\text{W}\big[\tilde{\phi}_{d_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{\phi}_{d_{M}},\phi_{n}\big](x)}{\text{W}\big[\tilde{\phi}_{d_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{\phi}_{d_{M}}\big](x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}c_{\text{\tiny$\mathcal{F}$}}^{M}\psi_{\mathcal{D}}(x)P_{\mathcal{D},n}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathcal{D}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\hat{\phi}_{0}(x)}{\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(\eta(x)\bigr)},

in which
\hat{\phi}_{0}(x)
and
c_{\text{\tiny$\mathcal{F}$}}
are defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\phi_{0}(x;g+M_{\text{I}}-M_{\text{II}}),&\text{L},\\
\phi_{0}\bigl(x;(g+M_{\text{I}}-M_{\text{II}},h-M_{\text{I}}+M_{\text{II}})\bigr),&\text{J},\end{cases}\qquad c_{\text{\tiny$\mathcal{F}$}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}2,&\text{L},\\
-4,&\text{J}.\end{cases}

The superscript
I
and
II
of the seed solutions are suppressed for simplicity of notation. The Wronskian of
n

is defined by formula

\displaystyle\text{W}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}](x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\det\left(\frac{d^{j-1}f_{k}(x)}{dx^{j-1}}\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}.

There are two different but equivalent Wronskian definitions of the denominator polynomial
\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)
and the multi-indexed orthogonal polynomial
P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)
. The first is based on the Wronskians of the ‘polynomials’
[
17
]
:

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\text{W}[\mu_{d_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{d_{M}}](\eta)\times\begin{cases}\eta^{(M_{\text{I}}+g-\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}e^{-M_{\text{I}}\eta},&\text{L},\\
\bigl(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\bigr)^{(M_{\text{I}}+g-\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}\bigl(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\bigr)^{(M_{\text{II}}+h-\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{I}}},&\text{J},\end{cases}

(10)

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\text{W}[\mu_{d_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{d_{M}},P_{n}](\eta)\times\begin{cases}\eta^{(M_{\text{I}}+g+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}e^{-M_{\text{I}}\eta},&\text{L},\\
\bigl(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\bigr)^{(M_{\text{I}}+g+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}\bigl(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\bigr)^{(M_{\text{II}}+h+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{I}}},&\text{J},\end{cases}

(11)

\displaystyle\mu_{\text{v}}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}e^{\eta}L^{(g-\frac{1}{2})}_{\text{v}}(-\eta),&\text{L, $\text{v}$ Type $\text{I}$},\\
\eta^{\frac{1}{2}-g}L^{(\frac{1}{2}-g)}_{\text{v}}(\eta),&\text{L, $\text{v}$ Type $\text{II}$},\\
\bigl(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}-h}P^{(g-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}-h)}_{\text{v}}(\eta),&\text{J, $\text{v}$ Type $\text{I}$},\\
\bigl(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}-g}P^{(\frac{1}{2}-g,h-\frac{1}{2})}_{\text{v}}(\eta),&\text{J, $\text{v}$ Type~$\text{II}$},\end{cases}

(12)

\displaystyle P_{n}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}L^{(g-\frac{1}{2})}_{n}(\eta),&\text{L},\\
P^{(g-\frac{1}{2},h-\frac{1}{2})}_{n}(\eta),&\text{J}.\end{cases}

(13)

The second is based on the Wronskians of the virtual state wavefunctions
\tilde{\phi}_{\text{v}}(x)
and the eigenfunction
\phi_{n}(x)

[
14
]
:

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)=c_{\text{\tiny$\mathcal{F}$}}^{-\frac{1}{2}M(M-1)}\text{W}\big[\tilde{\phi}_{d_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{\phi}_{d_{M}}\big](x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)=}{}\times\begin{cases}\eta^{-M^{\prime}(M^{\prime}+g-\frac{1}{2})}e^{-M^{\prime}\eta},&\text{L},\\
\bigl(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\bigr)^{-M^{\prime}(M^{\prime}+g-\frac{1}{2})}\bigl(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\bigr)^{-M^{\prime}(M^{\prime}-h+\frac{1}{2})},&\text{J},\end{cases}

(14)

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)=c_{\text{\tiny$\mathcal{F}$}}^{-\frac{1}{2}M(M+1)}\text{W}\big[\tilde{\phi}_{d_{1}},\ldots,\tilde{\phi}_{d_{M}},\phi_{n}\big](x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)=}{}\times\begin{cases}\eta^{-(M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})(M^{\prime}+g)}e^{-(M^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2})\eta},&\text{L},\\
\bigl(\frac{1-\eta}{2}\bigr)^{-(M^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})(M^{\prime}+g)}\bigl(\frac{1+\eta}{2}\bigr)^{-(M^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2})(M^{\prime}-h)},&\text{J},\end{cases}

(15)

in which

and

Simplified forms of the multi-indexed Laguerre

2.6
Parity transformation of the multi-indexed Jacobi polynomials

The Jacobi polynomial has the parity transformation property
[
24
]

\displaystyle P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n}P^{(\beta,\alpha)}_{n}(x).

(22)

We will show that this property is inherited by the multi-indexed Jacobi polynomials. It is based on the property of the Wronskian

\displaystyle\text{W}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}](-\eta)=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)}\text{W}[g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}](\eta),\qquad g_{k}(\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}f_{k}(-\eta).

In this subsection, we indicate the types of the virtual states explicitly by
(\text{v},\text{t})
, in which
t
stands for Type
I
or
II
. Based on (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{(\text{v},\text{t})}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)=(-1)^{\text{v}}\mu_{(\text{v},\bar{\text{t}})}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr),\qquad\bar{\text{t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\text{II},&\text{t}=\text{I},\\
\text{I},&\text{t}=\text{II},\end{cases}

\displaystyle P_{n}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr).

For the multi-index set
\mathcal{D}=\{(d_{1},\text{t}_{1}),\ldots,(d_{M},\text{t}_{M})\}
of the virtual state wavefunctions, let us define the ‘mirror reflected’ multi-index set
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{(d_{1},\bar{\text{t}}_{1}),\ldots,(d_{M},\bar{\text{t}}_{M})\}
. Corresponding to
M_{\text{I}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{I})\in\mathcal{D}\}
,
M_{\text{II}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{II})\in\mathcal{D}\}
, we have
M^{\prime}_{\text{I}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{I})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\}=M_{\text{II}}
,

M^{\prime}_{\text{II}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\#\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{II})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\}=M_{\text{I}}
. By parity transformation
\eta\to-\eta
, the multi-indexed Jacobi polynomial
P_{\mathcal{D},n}\bigl(\eta;(g,h)\bigr)
is mapped to
P_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},n}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr)
with the ‘mirror reflected’ multi-index set
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{D},n}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\big(\tfrac{1-\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{II}}+h+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{I}}}\big(\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{I}}+g+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}\text{W}\big[\mu_{(d_{1},\text{t}_{1})},\ldots,\mu_{(d_{M},\text{t}_{M})},P_{n}\big]\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\big(\tfrac{1-\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{II}}+h+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{I}}}\big(\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{I}}+g+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}M(M+1)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\text{W}\big[(-1)^{d_{1}}\mu_{(d_{1},\bar{\text{t}}_{1})},\ldots,(-1)^{d_{M}}\mu_{(d_{M},\bar{\text{t}}_{M})},(-1)^{n}P_{n}\big]\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\big(\tfrac{1-\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{II}}+h+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{I}}}\big(\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}\big)^{(M_{\text{I}}+g+\frac{1}{2})M_{\text{II}}}(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}M(M+1)}(-1)^{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{M}+n}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\text{W}\big[\mu_{(d_{1},\bar{\text{t}}_{1})},\ldots,\mu_{(d_{M},\bar{\text{t}}_{M})},P_{n}\big]\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(-1)^{n+\frac{1}{2}M(M+1)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{M}d_{k}}P_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},n}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr).

Similarly, the denominator polynomial
\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(\eta;(g,h)\bigr)
is mapped to
\Xi_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr)
with a sign factor:

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}M(M-1)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{M}d_{k}}\Xi_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr).

For the special case of ‘mirror symmetric’ multi-index set
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}
(as a set), i.e.,
\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{I})\in\mathcal{D}\}=\{d_{j}\,|\,(d_{j},\text{II})\in\mathcal{D}\}
(as a set), we have
P_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},n}(\eta)=\pm P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta)
. In fact, this formula turns out to be

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime},n}(\eta)=(-1)^{(\frac{M}{2})^{2}}P_{\mathcal{D},n}(\eta).

For this special case the parity transformation gives

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{D},n}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)=(-1)^{n}P_{\mathcal{D},n}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr),\qquad\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl({-}\eta;(g,h)\bigr)=\Xi_{\mathcal{D}}\bigl(\eta;(h,g)\bigr).

3
Summary and comments

The multi-indexed Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials are defined by the Wronskian expressions originating from multiple Darboux transformations. Two simplified determinant expressions of them, (
16
), (
17
) and (
20
), (
21
), which do not contain derivatives, are derived based on the properties of the Wronskian and identities of the Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials. For (
20
), (
21
), the Schrödinger equation is used. For (
16
), (
17
), various identities of the Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials are used, which are essentially forward shift relations. Although the calculation in Section
2.4
is performed for polynomials, it can be done for wavefunctions just like
[
20
]
, in which simplified determinant expressions are presented for the multi-indexed polynomials obtained by multiple Darboux transformations with pseudo virtual states wavefunctions as seed solutions. The parity transformation property of the multi-indexed Jacobi polynomials is also derived.

Multi-indexed orthogonal polynomials have been constructed for the classical orthogonal polynomials in the Askey scheme
[
1
,
12
]
, i.e., the Wilson, Askey–Wilson, Meixner, little
q
-Jacobi and (
q
-)Racah polynomials, etc.
[
18
,
19
,
21
]
. These polynomials belong to ‘discrete’ quantum mechanics
[
16
]
, in which the Schrödinger equations are second-order difference equations. The Casoratian expressions of these multi-indexed polynomials can also be simplified by using various identities as demonstrated here. These simplifications will be published elsewhere
[
13
]

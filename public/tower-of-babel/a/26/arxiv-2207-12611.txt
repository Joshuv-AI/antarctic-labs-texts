Disforming to Conformal Symmetry
and, Masahide Yamaguchi

Abstract
We analyse the dynamical properties of disformally transformed theories of gravity. We show that disformal transformation typically introduces novel degrees of freedom, equivalent to the mimetic dark matter, which possesses a Weyl-invariant formulation. We demonstrate that this phenomenon occurs in a wider variety of disformal transformations than previously thought.

Introduction

Ongoing observational advances in physics of cosmological inflation, dark energy and dark matter resulted in a revival of interest in theories where gravity is modified on astrophysical and cosmological scales. Arguably the simplest theories of this type should involve only one additional dynamical degree of freedom on top of the usual gravitons. This minimalistic restriction on the number of the extra degrees of freedom is manifestly realized in the Horndeski theories
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
where all equations of motion are second order. In this way the additional dynamical degree of freedom appears explicitly in the action as a scalar field variable
\phi
. However, the true degrees of freedom can sometimes be hidden and can reveal themselves only after a tedious Hamiltonian/Dirac analysis of a constrained system. In particular, a degenerate system with higher-derivative equations of motion can still have only one extra degree of freedom. This idea is realized in the "beyond Horndeski" or (GLPV)
[
4
,
5
]
theories in and degenerate higher-order scalar-tensor
theories (DHOST) theories
[
6
]
, for recent reviews see e.g.
[
7
,
8
]
. It turned out, some of these degenerate theories can be obtained from the Horndeski construction by introducing a non-minimal coupling of the scalar field to matter, see
[
9
]
. Namely, this non-minimal coupling appears through an
invertible
disformal transformation
[
10
]
of the metric

g_{\mu\nu}=C\left(Y,\phi\right)h_{\mu\nu}+D\left(Y,\phi\right)\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi\,,

(1.1)

where we denoted

Y=h^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial_{\nu}\phi\,.

(1.2)

Consequently, the Horndeski part of the action only depends on
h_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi
, while matter lives in the spacetime given by the disformally transformed metric
g_{\mu\nu}
defined by (
1.1
). For instance, a dust-like matter moves along the timelike geodesics of
g_{\mu\nu}
instead of those of
h_{\mu\nu}
. If the transformation (
1.1
) is invertible, one can express
h_{\mu\nu}
in terms of
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi
and then substitute this expression into the Horndeski action. In this way, matter becomes minimally coupled, while the resulting theory has higher order equations of motion due to the presence of the derivatives in (
1.1
).

Crucially, the dynamical content of the minimally coupled theory is equivalent to that of the original non-minimally coupled Horndeski model. It follows then that the number of degrees of freedom remains unchanged despite the presence of higher derivatives in the action. This way we can easily generate higher order scalar-tensor theories beyond Horndeski (more precisely of the class DHOST Ia) without running into the risk of introducing additional degrees of freedom to the model,
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
, for exceptions from this rule see
[
17
]
.
In particular, this construction of the new theories through the disformal transformations provides a convenient method to investigate compact objects in these theories, for recent studies see e.g.
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
The caveat here is that the stated equivalence and our ability to switch between the two descriptions (frames) relies heavily on the invertibility of the transformation, which is not guaranteed for a generic choice of functions
C
and
D
. Correspondingly, non-invertible transformations have been shown to introduce additional degrees of freedom to the theory and thus they change the dynamical content. A notable class of such non-invertible transformations has been studied in
[
26
]
. This class is defined by the following relation between the functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)
and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)

D(Y,\phi)=-\frac{C(Y,\phi)}{Y}+c(\phi)\ .

(1.3)

It has been demonstrated in
[
26
]
that applying these transformations to General Relativity (GR) with usual minimally coupled matter results in an emergence of a novel dynamical sector equivalent to the mimetic gravity
[
27
]
, for review see e.g.
[
28
]
. In the latter construction, the appearance of the novel hidden degree of freedom is connected to gauge degeneracy in form of the Weyl symmetry.

Clearly, it is crucial to understand which disformal transformations are "safe" - are just changes of description, and which secretly introduce novel dynamical degrees of freedom. In this paper, we address this issue. We consider a general
seed
theory consisting of usual matter and a general metric theory of gravity including GR. We show that transformation with a typical choice of functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)

and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)
violating relation (
1.3
) still does introduce novel degrees of freedom when applied to such a seed theory. Namely, this happens on solutions
\phi_{\star}(x)
of the first order nonlinear partial differential equation (PDE)

C=C_{Y}Y+D_{Y}Y^{2}\ ,

(1.4)

were the subscript denotes a partial derivative (e.g.
C_{Y}\equiv\partial C/\partial Y
). Note that functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)

and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)
related by (
1.3
) automatically satisfy equation (
1.4
), so that generic configurations
\phi(x)
satisfy this PDE. Clearly, there are many more functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)

and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)
which allow for existence of a solution of (
1.4
) then those related through (
1.3
). The additional matter sector emerging on these configurations
\phi_{\star}(x)
is either equivalent to the original mimetic dark matter (DM)
[
27
]
or to its analytical or tachyonic continuation to spacelike gradients of
\phi
. We will refer to both cases under the name
mimetic gravity
. In turn, mimetic DM is classically equivalent to irrotational dust
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
. Mimetic DM can well describe cosmology on large/linear scales. Our analysis shows that mimetic gravity is much more common in disformally transformed theories than previously thought.

This paper is organized as follows. In section

[

h_{\mu\nu}
. In section
4
we vary the disformed action with respect to field
\phi
. We show that this equation of motion takes the form of covariant continuity or conservation equation written again in terms of disformed

. In section
5
we illustrate our findings with simple examples. In particular we point out that one can construct such functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)
and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)
that it is impossible to satisfy condition (
1.4
). However, most choices of these functions will result in some solutions of (

) subclass of transformations (
1.1

Finally, we discuss our findings and propose future direction of research in section

The Jacobian: Singularity and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

Before we inspect the dynamical consequences of a general disformal transformation
g_{\mu\nu}\left(h_{\sigma\rho},\phi\right)
given by (
1.1
) let us first revisit the properties of its Jacobian matrix. This matrix can be easily calculated since the transformation does not depend on the derivatives of the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
. Hence, we get

\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho\sigma}=\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial h_{\rho\sigma}}=\frac{1}{2}C\left(\delta_{\mu}^{\sigma}\delta_{\nu}^{\rho}+\delta_{\nu}^{\sigma}\delta_{\mu}^{\rho}\right)-\left(C_{Y}h_{\mu\nu}+D_{Y}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\right)\phi^{,\sigma}\phi^{,\rho},

(2.1)

where the upper indexes of the partial derivatives (
\phi_{,\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\phi
) on the right hand side have been raised using the contravariant metric
h^{\mu\nu}
, so that

\phi^{,\mu}\equiv h^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\nu}\ .

(2.2)

On the other hand, we use notation

\nabla^{\mu}\phi\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\nu}\ ,

(2.3)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the Levi-Civita connection preserving the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. We consider
\mathcal{J}^{\sigma\rho}_{\mu\nu}
as a
10
by
10
matrix whose "indexes" are parameterized by a symmetric pair of spacetime indexes. In the above equation the upper "index" is the pair
\sigma\rho
while the lower "index" is the pair
\mu\nu
. Much of the properties of this matrix can be glanced from its eigenvalues and their associated eigenvectors. These are obtained by solving the following equation

\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma\rho}\,\xi^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{a}\,\xi^{a}_{\sigma\rho}\ ,

(2.4)

where
\xi^{a}_{\mu\nu}
is the eigenvector (symmetric eigentensor) associated to
\lambda_{a}
. The index
a
labels all possible solutions of this equation and it is not summed over here. In
[
9
]
it was showed that there are only two distinct eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}=

\displaystyle C\ ,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\star}=

\displaystyle C-C_{Y}Y-D_{Y}Y^{2}\ ,

(2.5)

for general disformal transformation (
1.1
). The determinant of
\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma\rho}
is the product of all the eigenvalues raised to the power of their respective multiplicities. Thus, as long as
all
eigenvalues,
\lambda_{a}
, are non-vanishing one has
\text{det}\mathcal{J}_{\mu\nu}^{\rho\sigma}\neq 0
. Consequently, the mapping
g_{\mu\nu}\left(h_{\sigma\rho},\phi\right)
is locally invertible with respect to
h_{\mu\nu}
by virtue of the Inverse Function Theorem. We will refer to transformations with vanishing determinant of the Jacobian as
singular disformal transformations
.

If we further require that
g_{\mu\nu}
constitutes a valid metric tensor we find that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
cannot vanish. Indeed, the calculation of the tensor inverse to

straightforwardly gives

g^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{C}\left(h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{D}{C+DY}\phi^{,\mu}\phi^{,\nu}\right)\ ,

(2.6)

where again
\phi^{,\mu}\equiv h^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\nu}
. This inverse tensor is clearly ill defined when
\lambda_{0}=C=0
. The non-existence of

would be clearly incompatible with

being a metric tensor. Therefore, the vanishing of the determinant is necessarily tied to

\lambda_{\star}\left(\phi,Y\right)=C-C_{Y}Y-D_{Y}Y^{2}=0,

(2.7)

in any physically viable scenarios with singular disformal transformation

Metric Dynamics due to Singular Disformal Transformation

Dynamical consequences of disformal transformations (
1.1

26

1.4

[

1.3

S_{\mathrm{seed}}[g,\Psi_{M}]

where
\Psi_{M}

\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{dis}}}{\delta h_{\mu\nu}}=\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{seed}}}{\delta g_{\sigma\rho}}\,\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma\rho}=0\ .

(3.2)

The properties of solutions of this equation significantly depend on whether the Jacobian

In the regular case, the Jacobian can be inverted and the resulting equations of motion match those of the original seed theory when they are interpreted as equations for

\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}=0\ .

In the singular case, the Jacobian matrix

3.5
).

The existence of a non-trivial kernel of the Jacobian matrix further manifests itself as an extra symmetry of the equations of motion. By inspecting the original set of equations (
3.2
) we can see that there is an invariance under the shift

\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\rightarrow\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}+\bar{\gamma}\,\zeta^{\mu\nu}_{\star}\ .

(3.13)

The
\bar{\alpha}
here is an arbitrary spacetime density. For the Einstein-Hilbert seed action this can be understood
[
40
]
as a shift of either the energy momentum-tensor of
\Psi_{M}
or of the Einstein tensor itself

G^{\mu\nu}\rightarrow G^{\mu\nu}+\gamma_{1}\zeta^{\mu\nu}_{\star}\ ,\qquad\mathrm{or}\qquad T^{\mu\nu}\rightarrow T^{\mu\nu}+\gamma_{2}\zeta^{\mu\nu}_{\star}\ .

(3.14)

Here
\gamma_{1,2}
are arbitrary scalar functions. The symmetry can be found even in the equation (
3.4
), where the above transformation can be compensated by a corresponding shift of the scalar
\rho
.

It is worthy to note that the appearance of the new term on the right hand side of the equation (
3.4
) is reminiscent of the appearance of novel terms due to restricted variation. Restricted variation is often used as a possible way to obtain unimodular gravity
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
. In the context of the unimodular gravity the Einstein-Hilbert action is varied while enforcing an additional condition on the variation of the metric

g^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\delta}g_{\mu\nu}=0\ .

(3.15)

This introduces a novel term
\varepsilon\,g^{\mu\nu}
in the Einstein equation and results in the dynamics of unimodular gravity, see e.g.
[
45
,
42
,
46
]
. Similarly, the entire term
\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma\rho}\delta h_{\mu\nu}
can be understood as a restricted variation

\tilde{\delta}g_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{\sigma\rho}\delta h_{\mu\nu}\ ,

(3.16)

which, in contrast to the standard variation
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
, satisfies the condition

\zeta^{\mu\nu}_{\star}\tilde{\delta}g_{\mu\nu}=0\ ,

(3.17)

in the singular case. This introduces a novel term
\bar{\rho}\,\zeta^{\mu\nu}_{\star}
in the tensor equations of motion. Similar type of restricted variation can be used to obtain global dynamics of the Newton or even of the Planck constants
[
47
]
. This raises an interesting question whether all types of such restricted variations of the metric can be realized through some metric transformation. For instance, unimodular restriction on variation of the metric (
3.15
) has been realized through different mimetic transformations involving vector fields
[
48
,
49
]
.

4
Scalar Dynamics due to Singular Disformal Transformation

Let us now inspect the role of the field
\phi
in the two regimes of the theory, that is, when the Jacobian is either regular or singular.
By assumption, the action
S_{\text{dis}}
defined through (
3.1
) depends on
\phi
through the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
only. The chain rule applied to variation with respect to
\phi
yields

\frac{\delta S_{\text{dis}}}{\delta\phi\left(x\right)}=\int d^{4}y\,\frac{\delta S_{\text{seed}}}{\delta g_{\sigma\rho}\left(y\right)}\,\frac{\delta g_{\sigma\rho}\left(y\right)}{\delta\phi\left(x\right)}\ .

(4.1)

Hence, equation of motion for
\phi
is trivially satisfied as a consequence of tensor equation of motion (
3.2
), which in the regular case results in (
3.3
).
In this regime
\phi
is an unphysical gauge field, which we are free to chose. However, as we have discussed earlier, there are requirements on the configurations of
\phi
, which ensure that both
h_{\mu\nu}
and
g_{\mu\nu}
are proper metric tensors. So we subject our choice of
\phi
to these conditions.

In the singular regime instead of equation (
3.3
) one has
(
3.4
), so that (
4.1
) provides a nontrivial differential equation.
The key observation of this paper is that when (
1.3
) is not satisfied, the factor
\lambda_{\star}
appears as an overall factor of one of the equations of motion. Thus, it is natural to interpret singularity condition (
2.7
) as a differential equation for
\phi
determining its dynamics. We can see this by contracting tensor equation of motion (
3.2
) with the eigenvector
\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}
, which gives

\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}\,\,\lambda_{\star}=0\ .

(4.2)

The left hand side is clearly a product of two factors and the equation is satisfied if either of them is zero. Hence, we see that any
\phi
satisfying
\lambda_{\star}=0
is a solution of (
4.2
).

Since
\phi
is obtained from (
2.8
) the variation of the disformed action with respect to
\phi
allows us to determine
\rho
defined in equation (
3.7
) for a general seed theory. The variation gives

\frac{\delta S_{\text{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\phi}=2\partial_{\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{\delta S_{\text{seed}}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial Y}\,h^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{\delta S_{\text{seed}}}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}D\right)\phi_{,\beta}\right]\ ,

(4.3)

which using equations (
3.7
) and (
3.4
) or (
3.8
), after straight calculations, results in

\nabla_{\mu}\left(\rho\,\left[C_{Y}+D_{Y}Y+D\right]\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\right)=\frac{1}{2}\,\rho\,\left[\frac{C_{\phi}+D_{\phi}Y}{C+DY}\right]\ .

(4.4)

Using singularity condition (
2.7
) and its solution
{X}_{\star}\left(\phi\right)
one can rewrite above equation as

\nabla_{\mu}\left[\frac{\rho}{X_{\star}}\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\,\rho\,\left[\frac{X_{\phi}}{X}\right]_{\star}\ .

(4.5)

Utilizing relation (
2.10
) and definition (
3.11
) this equation can be further simplified as

\nabla_{\mu}\left[\rho\,\nabla^{\mu}\Phi\right]=0\ ,

(4.6)

valid for timelike and spacelike gradients. For timelike gradients,
one can utilize the definition of four-velocity (
3.9
) and write this equation in form of the conservation equation

\nabla_{\mu}\left(\rho\,u^{\mu}\right)=0\ .

(4.7)

Notably, the above equations always admit the solution
\rho=0
upon which the theory reverts to its original seed dynamics. In this sense the entire physical content of the disformed theory including the regular branch is represented in the singular branch of solutions.

Crucially, equation of motion for
\rho
given by (
4.6
), the Einstein equation (
3.8
) and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
2.11
) can be expressed purely using the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, which we will call the
physical metric
.

5
Examples

Let us illustrate our finding from previous sections on some simple examples. We chose these examples so that they do not satisfy the condition (
1.3
). Hence, as it was believed before this work, the corresponding disformed theories would not have any new degrees of freedom.

Simple Conformal Transformation

We first consider a simple, purely conformal transformation

g_{\mu\nu}=e^{Y-1}h_{\mu\nu}\ ,

(5.1)

applied to the Einstein-Hilbert action. The
h_{\mu\nu}
– equation of motion (
3.2
) is

e^{Y-1}\left(G^{\mu\nu}-G^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}\phi^{,\mu}\phi^{,\nu}\right)=0\ ,

(5.2)

where the Einstein tensor as well as its trace is calculated purely through the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. The eigenvector
\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}
given by (
2.13
) for the above conformal transformation is just

\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}\ .

(5.3)

Hence, contracting equation (
5.2
) with this eigenvector gives

G\,e^{Y-1}\left(1-Y\right)=0\ .

(5.4)

This equation clearly has two branches: either
G
is equal to zero, which, after plugging it back in the above equation, clearly corresponds to GR in a vacuum. Or there is the second branch, which is characterized by the vanishing of the second factor:

e^{Y-1}\left(1-Y\right)=0\ .

(5.5)

This is exactly the singularity condition (
2.7
), whose vanishing is necessary for the appearance of the novel dynamics. In this particular case the above equation has a single solution
Y_{\star}=1
, which can be plugged back into our equations to give

G_{\mu\nu}-G\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}=0\ ,

(5.6)

which can be directly obtained from general equation (
3.8
). Here every term is evaluated with respect to the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. These equations are clearly equivalent to those of mimetic dark matter. Furthermore, the solution
Y_{\star}=1
implies that
X_{\star}=1
, which can be calculated from equation (
2.9
). Due to this constraint on
X
, the trace of (
5.6
) becomes an identity. Thus, there is one less equation in comparison to the standard GR. The missing information is instead carried by equation of motion (
4.7
) for
\phi
which reads

\nabla_{\mu}\left(G\nabla^{\mu}\phi\right)=0\ .

(5.7)

This equation is also evaluated purely using the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. The above set of equations clearly provides us with a solution for
g_{\mu\nu}
rather then a solution for
h_{\mu\nu}
. However, the corresponding solution for
h_{\mu\nu}
can be determined due to the constraint
Y_{\star}=1
. Indeed, plugging this into the original transformation (
1.1
) we get

g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}\ .

(5.8)

Note that this is a significant difference in comparison to the original formulation of mimetic dark matter where
Y
is a pure gauge quantity and thus the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
cannot be determined uniquely.

Simple Disformal Transformation

Let us now examine an example where we get solutions
\phi_{\star}(x)
with both spacelike as well as timelike gradients. We consider the following disformal transformation

g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}+Y\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\ ,

(5.9)

and the Einstein-Hilbert action as the seed theory. The
h_{\mu\nu}
– equation of motion (
3.2
) is

G^{\mu\nu}-G^{\sigma\rho}\,\phi_{,\sigma}\phi_{,\rho}\,\phi^{,\mu}\phi^{,\nu}=0\ .

(5.10)

The eigenvector
\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}
given by (
2.13
) is

\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}=\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\ .

(5.11)

Contracting equation (
5.10
) with this eigenvector
\xi^{\star}_{\mu\nu}
we obtain

G^{\mu\nu}\,\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\left(1-Y^{2}\right)=0\ .

(5.12)

This again leads to two branches of solutions: the GR branch (or regular branch) characterized by
G^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi=0
upon which we can retrieve the Einstein vacuum equations from (
5.10
), and the new branch characterized by

1-Y^{2}=0\ .

(5.13)

This is again singularity condition (
2.7
), which is satisfied by
Y^{\pm}_{\star}=\pm 1
. Via (
2.9
) these solutions correspond to
X^{\pm}_{\star}=\pm 1/2
. Using these solutions one can lower the indices with metric
g_{\mu\nu}
to rewrite (
5.10
) as

G_{\mu\nu}-4G^{\sigma\rho}\,\phi_{,\sigma}\phi_{,\rho}\,\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}=0\ .

(5.14)

It is not too hard to see that this equation is equivalent to the mimetic dark matter. Indeed, by taking the trace we find:

G=\pm 2G^{\sigma\rho}\,\phi_{,\sigma}\phi_{,\rho}\ ,

(5.15)

which can be plugged back into (
5.14
) to obtain

G_{\mu\nu}=\pm 2G\,\,\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}\ ,

(5.16)

which can be directly obtained from general equation (
3.8
). Here the sign corresponds to the chosen branch
Y^{+}_{\star}
or
Y^{-}_{\star}
. Equation of motion (
4.6
) reads

\nabla_{\mu}\left[\rho\,\nabla^{\mu}\phi\right]=0\ .

(5.17)

This example illustrates that in general there are multiple solutions of the equation
\lambda_{\star}=0
. In this example one is timelike while the other is spacelike. Note that the above equations only describe irrotational dust for the timelike solution,
Y^{+}_{\star}
. The spacelike branch does not have such simple intuitive description, though some discussions have already been done, see e.g.
[
26
,
50
]
. Interestingly, physics described by the first purely conformal example and the branch
Y^{+}_{\star}
of the current disformal example is identical. Thus, different disformal transformations can result in the same physics, at least for particular branches.

To Solve or not to Solve

To argue that most functions
C\left(Y,\phi\right)
and
D\left(Y,\phi\right)
would allow for solution of equation (
2.7
) let us consider these functions to be analytical around
Y=0
. Up to second order we have

\displaystyle C\left(Y,\phi\right)

\displaystyle=c_{0}\left(\phi\right)+c_{1}\left(\phi\right)Y+c_{2}\left(\phi\right)Y^{2}\ ,

\displaystyle D\left(Y,\phi\right)

\displaystyle=d_{0}\left(\phi\right)+d_{1}\left(\phi\right)Y+d_{2}\left(\phi\right)Y^{2}\ .

(5.18)

Taking into account that
Y\neq 0
, existence of solutions for (
2.7
) requires to reach equality between

\frac{c_{0}\left(\phi\right)}{Y^{2}}-c_{2}\left(\phi\right)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}\left(\phi\right)+2d_{2}\left(\phi\right)Y\ .

(5.19)

Clearly, for non-vanishing
c_{0}\left(\phi\right)
and
d_{2}\left(\phi\right)
there is always at least one solution
Y_{\star}(\phi)
. The maximal possible number of branches is three, with two branches which are of the same sign. Note that
c_{1}\left(\phi\right)
and
d_{0}\left(\phi\right)
are irrelevant for the existence of solutions
Y_{\star}(\phi)
. Thus, only particularly constructed coefficient functions
c_{i}\left(\phi\right)
and
d_{i}\left(\phi\right)
would allow for the absence of the additional degree of freedom in the disformed theory. Thus, emergence of mimetic DM is a typical result of general disformal transformations.

6
Mimetic with and without the Weyl Invariance

Following the discussion in
[
31
]
we may introduce a new set of variables, which conveniently isolates the novel degrees of freedom, which are responsible for the appearance of mimetic DM dynamics in the disformed theory. For simplicity, let us first discuss a simplified case where the disformal transformation is purely conformal, so that

g_{\mu\nu}=C(Y,\phi)h_{\mu\nu}\ .

(6.1)

We will revisit the full disformal case in the next section. For our seed theory we consider a general action

S_{\text{seed}}[g,\Psi_{M}]=\int d^{4}x\,\mathcal{L}[g,\Psi_{M}]\ .

(6.2)

In the following we will not indicate any explicit dependence on matter fields
\Psi_{M}
as they do not play any role in the discussion. This dependence can be easily recovered if needed. Moreover, we included the measure
\sqrt{-g}
into Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}(g)
. We first introduce a Lagrange constraint and a novel scalar field
\chi

S_{\text{dis}}[h,\phi,\Lambda,\chi]=\int d^{4}x\left[\,\mathcal{L}(g(h,\phi))+\sqrt{-h}\,\Lambda\left(C(Y,\phi)-\chi\right)\right]\ .

(6.3)

The addition of such constraint has no effect on the theory because any changes to the equations of motion are proportional to
\Lambda
, which is constrained to be zero by the
\chi
equation of motion. However, the introduction of this constraint allows us to substitute
C(Y,\phi)=\chi
anywhere in the action outside of the constraint itself
[
51
]
without changing the theory. In this way we can use it to eliminate the dependence on
C(Y,\phi)
in the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
:

g_{\mu\nu}=Ch_{\mu\nu}\quad\rightarrow\quad g_{\mu\nu}=\chi h_{\mu\nu}\ .

(6.4)

By doing so the action becomes

S_{\text{dis}}[h,\phi,\Lambda,\chi]=\int d^{4}x\left[\,\mathcal{L}(\chi h_{\mu\nu})+\sqrt{-h}\,\Lambda\left(C(Y,\phi)-\chi\right)\right]\ .

(6.5)

Note that after these steps the
\chi
equation of motion no longer forces
\Lambda
to vanish. Instead, the non-trivial value of
\Lambda
ends up compensating for the changes to the equations of motion which appear due to substitution (
6.4
). Crucially, we may now introduce the novel variables
5
5

5

Effects of field redefinition (
6.7
) on the dynamics of the theory are determined by the associated Jacobian matrix. Comparing the Jacobian for a conformal transformation (
6.1
) with the Jacobian of field redefinition (
6.7
) it becomes very clear why the two operations are so different. Indeed for the latter the Jacobian is purely diagonal

\mathcal{J}^{\sigma\rho}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\delta\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}}{\delta h_{\mu\nu}}\chi\delta^{\rho}_{\mu}\delta^{\sigma}_{\nu}\ .

(6.6)

Consequently, the matrix has but a single eigenvalue
\chi
, which corresponds to
\lambda_{0}
from previous sections.

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\chi\,h_{\mu\nu}\ ,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\chi^{-1}\Lambda\ ,

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\chi\ ,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\phi\ .{}

(6.7)

Note that an additional condition
\chi\neq 0
must be imposed by hand, as the redefinition would be ill-defined for such cases. In the new variables the disformed action reduces to

S_{\text{dis}}[\tilde{g},\phi,\tilde{\Lambda},\chi]=\int d^{4}x\left[\,\mathcal{L}(\tilde{g})+\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\,\tilde{\Lambda}\,\left(\chi^{-1}C(\chi\tilde{X},\phi)-1\right)\right]\ ,

(6.8)

where
\tilde{X}=\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi
. We can see that the above procedure allowed us to isolate the effect of the conformal transformation (
6.1
), which effectively boiled down to the introduction of an extra Lagrange constraint with a very specific dependence on the scalar field
\chi
.

The key difference between the general setup (
6.1
) and the specific mimetic dark matter case with
C(Y,\phi)=b(\phi)Y
, where
b(\phi)
is an arbitrary function of
\phi
, is that in the latter case
\chi
drops out of the action entirely. This reflects the underlying Weyl invariance of the mimetic DM setup. For any theory disformed via (
6.1
), the Weyl invariance is broken and the field dependence on
\chi
remains in the action. Crucially, this dependence is purely algebraic, there are no derivatives of
\chi
in action (
6.8
). This means that
\chi
is an auxiliary field which can be integrated out of the action.
The equation of motion for
\chi
reads

\tilde{\Lambda}\Big[\chi^{-1}C_{Y}(\chi\tilde{X},\phi)\tilde{X}-\chi^{-2}C(\chi\tilde{X},\phi)\Big]=0\ .

(6.9)

This is satisfied when

C_{Y}(\chi\tilde{X},\phi)\tilde{X}\chi=C(\chi\tilde{X},\phi)\ .

(6.10)

It is easy to recognise in the above relation the singularity condition given by (
2.7
). Note that in general the
\chi
equation of motion also admits the solution
\tilde{\Lambda}=0
, which corresponds to the solutions of the original seed theory (
6.2
). A key feature of equation (
6.10
) above is that
\chi
enters only in combination with
\tilde{X}
as
\chi\tilde{X}
. Thus, (
6.10
) effectively depends only on two variables
\chi\tilde{X}
and
\phi
.
Therefore, any solution of this algebraic equation with respect to
\chi\tilde{X}
(if such solution exists) has the form

\chi_{\star}\tilde{X}=Y_{\star}(\phi)\ ,

(6.11)

where we used the same notation for the roots of singularity equation as in section
2
.
Plugging this particular branch
\chi_{\star}
back into action (
6.8
) yields

S^{\star}_{\text{dis}}[\tilde{g},\phi,\tilde{\Lambda}]=\int d^{4}x\left[\,\mathcal{L}(\tilde{g})+\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\,\tilde{\Lambda}\,\left(\frac{\tilde{X}}{X_{\star}(\phi)}-1\right)\right]\ ,

(6.12)

where we substituted
Y_{\star}(\phi)
into equation (
2.9
).
This is clearly a reduced form of the action for mimetic DM given by the mimetic ansatz

g_{\mu\nu}=\left[\frac{h^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{,\alpha}\phi_{\beta}}{X_{\star}\left(\phi\right)}\right]\,h_{\mu\nu}\,.

(6.13)

and thus corresponds to a manifestly Weyl invariant theory. This expression is identical to the original mimetic ansatz from
[
27
]
if one uses redefined scalar field
\Phi
given by equation (
3.11
). Contrary to action (
6.12
), which is reduced to just one solution
Y_{\star}(\phi)
of the singularity equation, action (
6.8
) preserves information about all branches of solutions for the singularity equation.

7
Equivalent Action for Theory with Singular Disformal Transformation

In the case of the fully-fledged disformal transformation (
1.1
)
we need to isolate two free functions
C
and
D
. We use the same strategy as before, which now requires to introduce two Lagrange constraints instead of one:

S_{\text{dis}}[h,\phi,\Lambda,\alpha,\beta]=\int d^{4}x\,\Bigg[\,\mathcal{L}(g(h,\phi))+\Lambda_{\alpha}\sqrt{-h}\big(C(Y,\phi)-\alpha\big)+\Lambda_{\beta}\sqrt{-h}\big(D(Y,\phi)-\beta\big)\Bigg]\ .

(7.1)

This allows us to eliminate the dependence on
C(Y,\phi)
and
D(Y,\phi)
in the action outside of the constraints. Furthermore, using the scalars
\alpha
and
\beta
, we are able to propose a new metric variable analogical to (
6.7
) from the conformal case

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\alpha\,h_{\mu\nu}+\beta\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\ .

(7.2)

Again, a field redefinition like this is, in principle, able to produce novel dynamics; however, this does not happen here because
\alpha
and
\beta
here are free fields that do not depend on the metric
h_{\mu\nu}
in any way. Consequently the Jacobian of the above mapping has a very simple form (
6.6
). In order to rewrite the action (
7.1
) in terms of the new variable
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
it is useful to derive the relation between various quantities defined with respect to the two metrics
h_{\mu\nu}
and
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
. Assuming
\alpha\neq 0
the mapping (
7.2
) can be inverted to give

h_{\mu\nu}=\alpha^{-1}\Big[\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}-\beta\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\Big]\ .

(7.3)

The inverse metric
h^{\mu\nu}
is then

h^{\mu\nu}=\alpha\Big[\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\beta}{1-\beta\bar{X}}\,\bar{\partial}^{\mu}\phi\bar{\partial}^{\nu}\phi\Big]\,.

(7.4)

Here
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}
is inverse of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
, and the bar above the partial derivatives denotes that their index has been raised using the contravariant metric
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}
. We also define the associated kinetic term
\bar{X}=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi
. Contracting both sides with
\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi
we find the relation between
\bar{X}
and
Y
to be

Y=\frac{\alpha\bar{X}}{1-\beta\bar{X}}\ .

(7.5)

Finally, the determinant of
h_{\mu\nu}
is related (see e.g.
[
52
]
) to the determinant of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
as

\det h_{\mu\nu}=\alpha^{-4}\det\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\big(1-\beta\bar{X})\ .

(7.6)

We can see that in order for the above relations to be well defined we need to pose additional requirements on the scalar fields
\alpha
and
\beta
. This stems from the fact that even when
h_{\mu\nu}
is a proper metric tensor and the mapping (
7.2
) is regular and invertible, it is not guaranteed that
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
has all the characteristics of a metric tensor and vice versa. These properties need to be ensured by hand, as discussed for the disformal transformations in
[
10
,
11
,
32
]
. This translates into
\alpha\neq 0
and
\beta\bar{X}<1
, which enable the invertibility of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
and also ensure that
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
has a proper Lorentzian signature. Substituting the above relations into action (
7.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\text{dis}}[\bar{g},\phi,\Lambda,\alpha,\beta]=\int d^{4}x\,\Bigg[\,\mathcal{L}(\bar{g})+\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\alpha^{-2}\sqrt{1-\beta\bar{X}}\left(\Lambda_{\alpha}\,\psi_{\alpha}+\Lambda_{\beta}\,\psi_{\beta}\right)\Bigg]\ ,

(7.7)

where we have introduced

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=C\Big(\frac{\alpha\bar{X}}{1-\beta\bar{X}},\phi\Big)-\alpha\ ,

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=D\Big(\frac{\alpha\bar{X}}{1-\beta\bar{X}},\phi\Big)-\beta\ .

Due to our assumption
\beta\bar{X}\neq 1
the factors following the Lagrange multipliers
\Lambda_{\alpha}
and
\Lambda_{\beta}
are strictly non-vanishing and therefore we are allowed to absorb them by the following redefinition

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\alpha}\,\alpha^{-2}\sqrt{1-\beta\bar{X}}\ ,

\displaystyle\bar{\Lambda}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\beta}\,\alpha^{-2}\sqrt{1-\beta\bar{X}}\ .

(7.8)

Hence, the action simplifies to

S_{\text{dis}}[\bar{g},\phi,\bar{\Lambda},\alpha,\beta]=\int d^{4}x\,\Bigg[\mathcal{L}(\bar{g})+\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\bar{\Lambda}_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}+\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\bar{\Lambda}_{\beta}\psi_{\beta}\Bigg]\ .

(7.9)

Due to the redefinition
h_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
the Lagrange multipliers
\alpha
and
\beta
became auxiliary fields. However, their equations of motion are not as simple as in the conformal case from the previous section. The main difficulty stems from the presence of two Lagrange multipliers in their equations of motion
\bar{\Lambda}_{\alpha}
and
\bar{\Lambda}_{\beta}
. Indeed the solution for either
\alpha
or
\beta
from their own equations of motion will in general depend on
\bar{\Lambda}_{\alpha}
or
\bar{\Lambda}_{\beta}
, which is qualitatively very different from what we encountered in the conformal case. Interestingly, we have found that these difficulties can be avoided by introducing an additional auxiliary variable
\theta
, which allows us to reduce the number of constraints in the action to just one. The variable
\theta
is defined as

\theta=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta\bar{X}}\ ,

(7.10)

which we enforce through yet another Lagrange constraint

\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\Lambda_{\theta}\Bigg(\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta\bar{X}}-\theta\Bigg)\ .

(7.11)

This term is meant to be added to action (
7.9
). We can now substitute this constraints to the two original constraints
\psi_{\alpha,\beta}
to obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle C\Big(\theta\bar{X},\phi\Big)-\alpha\ ,

(7.12)

\displaystyle\psi_{\beta}=

\displaystyle D\Big(\theta\bar{X},\phi\Big)-\beta\ .

(7.13)

Then we can use these constraints to eliminate
\alpha
and
\beta
from the
\theta
-constraint, which becomes

\Lambda_{\theta}\Bigg(\frac{C(\theta\bar{X},\phi)}{1-D(\theta\bar{X},\phi)\bar{X}}-\theta\Bigg)\ .

(7.14)

This way
\alpha
and
\beta
only appear linearly in their respective constraints and therefore they are simple Lagrange multipliers. Hence, they can be integrated out of the action. This means that we may remove the constraints
\psi_{\alpha,\beta}
without changing the dynamical content of the theory, so that the action becomes

S_{\text{dis}}[\bar{g},\phi,\Lambda_{\theta},\theta]=\int d^{4}x\,\Bigg[\mathcal{L}(\bar{g})+\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\Lambda_{\theta}\Bigg(\frac{C(\theta\bar{X},\phi)}{1-D(\theta\bar{X},\phi)\bar{X}}-\theta\Bigg)\Bigg]\ .

(7.15)

\theta
obtained from this action does not have the same properties as equation (
6.10

\theta
does not appear in the combination
\bar{X}\theta
everywhere as
\chi
did. However, the terms where this property fails are proportional to the above constraint itself. Consequently, this shortcoming can be remedied by a field redefinition of the Lagrange multiplier
\Lambda_{\theta}
itself

\bar{\Lambda}_{\theta}=\frac{\theta\Lambda_{\theta}}{1-D(\theta\bar{X},\phi)\bar{X}}\ .

(7.16)

This finally leaves us with the action

S_{\text{dis}}[\bar{g},\phi,\bar{\Lambda}_{\theta},\theta]=\int d^{4}x\,\Bigg[\mathcal{L}(\bar{g})+\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\bar{\Lambda}_{\theta}\Bigg(\theta^{-1}C(\theta\bar{X},\phi)+D(\theta\bar{X},\phi)\bar{X}-1\Bigg)\Bigg]\ .

(7.17)

It is important to stress that by deriving this action we have effectively isolated the entire effect of the disformal transformation into a single constraint. Indeed, the change in the action of the seed theory is reduced to mere replacement
g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
. The new constraint depends on an extra auxiliary field
\theta
, which is in general non-trivial and yields its own equation of motion

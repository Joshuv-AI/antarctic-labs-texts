Capacities and density conditions in metric spaces
Javier Canto, Lizaveta Ihnatsyeva, Juha Lehrbäck, Antti V. Vähäkangas

Abstract
. We examine the relations between different capacities in the setting of a metric measure space. First, we prove a comparability result for the Riesz ( β , p ) (\beta,p) -capacity and the relative Hajłasz ( β , p ) (\beta,p) -capacity, for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty and 0 < β ≤ 1 0<\beta\leq 1 , under a suitable kernel estimate related to the Riesz potential. Then we show that in geodesic spaces the corresponding capacity density conditions are equivalent even without assuming the kernel estimate. In the last part of the paper, we compare the relative Hajłasz ( 1 , p ) (1,p) -capacity to the relative variational p p -capacity.

1.
Introduction

It is well known
[
24
,
30
]
that the classical Newtonian capacity of a
subset of the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
can be characterized as the minimum of an energy functional
in terms of the weak gradient on the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This characterization has led to extensions of the notion of capacity in several directions and, in particular, to the development of a theory of capacities related to Riesz and Bessel potentials; see
[
3
]
.
In many cases the
different definitions lead to comparable
capacities, see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
34
]
.
For instance, the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity
R_{\beta,p}
is comparable to the classical Newtonian capacity when
\beta=1
and
p=2
.

In this paper, we consider various capacities
in the setting of a metric measure space
X
,
and our main goal is to clarify the connections between these different notions.
Analogues of Riesz potentials in metric measure spaces
have been studied for instance in
[
13
,
15
,
16
,
28
,
29
]
,
and with these it is possible to consider the corresponding Riesz capacities, see e.g.
[
32
]

and Section
3
.
On the other hand, generalizations of the (weak) gradients to the metric setting
lead to variants of capacities
defined in terms of Sobolev functions, such as the Newtonian capacity.
In particular, we will use a metric space
version
of the relative variational
p
-capacity

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)
defined via

p
-weak upper gradients as in
[
35
,
5
]
; see Section
7
.
Here
\Omega\subset X
is a bounded open set and
F\subset\Omega
is a closed set.
Another possibility is to use
the so-called Hajłasz gradients. These
were first defined in
[
11
]
, and
like Riesz potentials, they are of non-local nature,
whereas
p
-weak upper gradients are local.
The Hajłasz gradient approach to capacities in metric spaces has been used for instance
in
[
22
,
19
,
23
]
. Following
[
8
]
, we consider the relative
Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,\Omega)
,
where
F
and
\Omega
are as above; see Section
3
.

After preliminary definitions and results in Sections
2
and
3
,
we begin the comparisons of capacities in Section
4
,
where we study the Riesz and Hajłasz capacities of sets relative to balls.
In the special case of an Ahlfors regular metric space, the main result of that section
(Corollary
4.5
) reads as follows; see Remark
4.6
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\beta<1
, and
0<Q<\infty
be such that
Q>\beta p
.
Assume that
X
is a complete and connected metric space
equipped with an Ahlfors
Q
-regular measure.
Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

(1)

C^{-1}\>R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\leq\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\leq C\>R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,,

whenever
E\subset X
is a closed set,
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

More generally,
the lower bound in (
1
) holds under much weaker assumptions,
while in the upper bound the Ahlfors regularity assumption can be replaced with
a reverse doubling condition and an explicit “kernel estimate”. See
Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
, respectively,
for details.

From the point of view of analysis on general metric spaces, the assumptions for the upper
bound in (
1
) are still rather restrictive.
In Section
6
, we take another approach to the comparison of capacities
under different assumptions. Namely, instead of a direct comparison between the Hajłasz and Riesz
capacities, we study the equivalence of the corresponding capacity density conditions.
For example, a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Riesz
({\beta},p)
-capacity
density condition, if there is a constant
c>0
such that

R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c\>R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
. The definitions
of density conditions for other capacities are similar,
see Definitions
6.1
and
7.3
.

The origins of the
(Riesz) capacity density conditions come
from
[
27
]
, where the Riesz
({\beta},p)
-capacity
R_{\beta,p}
was used in Euclidean spaces
to define a fatness condition. The main result of
[
27
]
states that in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the Riesz capacity density condition is open ended on
\beta
and on
p
, that is, if a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition, then
E
also satisfies the condition for some
q<p
and
\alpha<\beta
. This result has found numerous applications in potential theory , in the study of Hardy inequalities and in partial differential equations, see for instance
[
20
,
21
,
31
]
.

Capacity density conditions have been generalized also to metric spaces, and
often these conditions turn out to be open-ended (self-improving) as well.
For instance,
the
p
-capacity density condition, defined in terms of the relative variational
p
-capacity for
1<p<\infty
,
has been shown to be open-ended in
[
7
,
26
]
under the “standard assumptions”
on analysis on metric spaces (see Section
7
).
On the other hand,
for the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition, the open-endedness
on both
\beta
and
p

was proven recently in
[
8
]
in complete geodesic spaces.

In Section
6
, we show the equivalence of
Riesz and Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density conditions
in a complete geodesic space,
see Theorem
6.4
. As a tool we use another density condition,
given in terms of Hausdorff content of codimension
q
, where
0<q<\beta p
.
Under a suitable reverse doubling condition for the space
X
, such a
Hausdorff content density condition for a set
E\subset X
implies that

E
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition;
this follows from the results in Section
5
.
On the other hand, in
[
8
]
it was shown that in complete geodesic spaces
the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition is equivalent to
the Hausdorff content density condition of codimension
q
, for some
0<q<\beta p
.
Taking also into account the lower bound in (
1
),
which is valid under minimal assumptions, this leads to the equivalence
of Riesz and Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density conditions;
see Section
6
for details.

Since the Hajłasz capacity density conditions are known to be open-ended by
[
8
]
,
we obtain as a particular consequence of the above equivalence result that
also the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition
is open-ended in complete geodesic spaces,
see Corollary
6.5
.
This is an extension of the main result of
[
27
]
to metric spaces.

Finally, in Section
7
we show that the relative variational
p
-capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)
and
the Hajłasz
(1,p)
-capacity
\operatorname{cap}_{1,p}(F,\Omega)

are comparable, for
1<p<\infty
, assuming that the metric space
X
supports a
q
-Poincaré inequality
for some
1\leq q<p
. Therefore we conclude that under suitable assumptions also the
density conditions for the variational
p
-capacity, Hajłasz
(1,p)
-capacity, and
Riesz
(1,p)
-capacity are equivalent; see Theorem
7.8
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Metric spaces

Throughout the paper we assume that
X=(X,d,\mu)
is a metric measure space equipped with a metric
d
and a
positive complete Borel
measure
\mu
such that
0<\mu(B)<\infty

for all balls
B\subset X
, each of which is an open set of the form

B=B(x,r)=\{y\in X\,:\,d(y,x)<r\}

with
x\in X
and
r>0
. Under these assumptions
the space
X
is separable,
see
[
5
, Proposition 1.6]
.
We also assume that
\#X\geq 2
and
that the measure
\mu
is
doubling
, that is,
there is a constant
c_{\mu}>1
, called
the
doubling constant of
\mu
, such that

(2)

\mu(2B)\leq c_{\mu}\,\mu(B)

for all balls
B=B(x,r)
in
X
.
Here we use for
0<t<\infty
the notation
tB=B(x,tr)
.

If
X
is connected,
then the doubling measure
\mu
satisfies also
the reverse doubling condition, that is,
there is a constant
0<c_{R}=C(c_{\mu})<1
such that

(3)

\mu(B(x,r/2))\leq c_{R}\,\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(X)/2
;
see for instance
[
5
, Lemma 3.7]
.
Iteration of (
3
) shows that
there exist
an exponent
\sigma>0
and a constant
c_{\sigma}>0
,
both depending on
c_{\mu}
only, such that the quantitative
reverse doubling condition

(4)

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\leq c_{\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{\sigma}

holds in a connected space
X

for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<R\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

The space
X
is called Ahlfors
Q
-regular, for
Q>0
, if there is a constant
c_{Q}\geq 1
such that

(5)

c_{Q}^{-1}r^{Q}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq c_{Q}r^{Q}

for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
Note that (
4
)
holds in an Ahlfors
Q
-regular space with
\sigma=Q
.

By a
curve
we mean a nonconstant, rectifiable, continuous
mapping from a compact interval of
{\mathbb{R}}
to
X
; we tacitly assume
that all curves are parametrized by their arc-length.
We say that
X
is a
geodesic space
, if
every pair of points in
X

can be joined by a curve whose length is equal to the distance between the two points.

2.2.
Hölder functions and Hajłasz gradients

Let
A\subset X
.
We say that

u\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function,
with
an exponent
0<\beta\leq 1
and a constant

0\leq\kappa<\infty
, if

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq\kappa\,d(x,y)^{\beta}\,,\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in A\,.

The set of all
\beta
-Hölder functions
u\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}

is denoted by
\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(A)
.
The
1
-Hölder functions are also called
Lipschitz functions
,
and we write
\operatorname{Lip}(A)=\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(A)
.

The definition of the Hajłasz capacities
will be based on the
following Hajłasz
\beta
-gradients (see Definition
3.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

For each function
u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we let

\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)

be the (possibly empty) family of all measurable functions
g\colon X\to[0,\infty]
such that

(6)

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)^{\beta}\big(g(x)+g(y)\big)

almost everywhere, that is, there exists an exceptional set
N=N(g)\subset X
for which
\mu(N)=0
and
inequality (
6
) holds for every
x,y\in X\setminus N
.
A function
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
is called a Hajłasz
\beta
-gradient of the function
u
.

The following nonlocal generalization of the Leibniz rule
is taken from
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
, see also
[
12
]
.
The nonlocality is reflected
by the appearance of the two global terms
\lVert\psi\lVert_{\infty}
and
\kappa
in the statement below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
.
Assume that

u\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded
\beta
-Hölder function and
\psi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded
\beta
-Hölder
function with a constant
\kappa\geq 0
. Then
u\psi\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function and

(g_{u}\lVert\psi\lVert_{\infty}+\kappa\lvert u\rvert){\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\{\psi\not=0\}$}}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u\psi)

for all
g_{u}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Here
\{\psi\not=0\}=\{y\in X:\psi(y)\not=0\}
.

The following
(\beta,p,p)
-Poincaré inequality can be proved in a similar way as Theorem 5.15 in
[
15
]
.
This inequality relates the
\beta
-Hajłasz gradient to the given measure. We want to emphasize that no additional assumptions on the measure are needed here.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
,
and assume that

u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Then

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u(x)-u_{B}\rvert^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq 2^{p}\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta p}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

whenever
B\subset X
is a ball.

Here we use the notation

(7)

u_{B}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}u(y)\,d\mu(y)=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}u(y)\,d\mu(y)

for the integral average of
u\in L^{1}(B)
over a ball
B\subset X
.

2.3.
Riesz potentials

Riesz potentials appear
frequently in potential analysis,
see for instance
[
3
,
24
]
.
For a nonnegative measurable function
f
on a metric space
X
, the

Riesz potential of order

\beta>0
in
X
can be defined by the expression

I_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{X}\frac{f(y)d(x,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(x,d(x,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X\,;

see, for instance,
[
13
,
15
,
16
]
.

We prove two auxiliary lemmata on relations between the Riesz potential
I_{\beta}f
and
the non-centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal function
Mf
.
If
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a measurable function, then

(8)

Mf(x)=\sup_{B}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert f(y)\rvert\,d\mu(y)\,,\qquad x\in X\,,

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subset X
such that
x\in B
. The sublinear operator
M
is bounded on
L^{s}(X)
for
1<s\leq\infty
, see
[
5
, Theorem 3.13]
.

The first lemma
is a variant of a well known Euclidean estimate,
see
[
3
, Lemma 3.1.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\beta>0
and let
f
be a nonnegative
measurable function on
X
. Then

\int_{B(z,r)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta)r^{\beta}Mf(z)

for all
z\in X
and all
r>0
.

Proof.

Define
r_{i}=2^{-i}r
,
i=0,1,2,\dots
.
Using the doubling property of
\mu

and the definition of the maximal function,
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,r_{i})\setminus B(z,r_{i+1})}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,r_{i})\setminus B(z,r_{i+1})}\frac{f(y)r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,r_{i+1}))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,r_{i+1}))}\int_{B(z,r_{i})}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}^{\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}r^{\beta}Mf(z)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-i\beta}\ =\ C(c_{\mu},\beta)r^{\beta}Mf(z).\qed

The second lemma shows that the maximal function
Mf

can be (essentially) used as a Hajłasz
\beta
-gradient of
I_{\beta}f
.
The method of proof
is standard, we refer to

[
10
,
14
]
,
and it requires the validity of
the rather technical
kernel estimate
(
9
).
However, such estimate (or a suitable variant) holds,
for instance, if the measure
is
Q
-uniform or Ahlfors
Q
-regular,
see Example
2.6
and Remark
2.7
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0<\beta<\eta
and let
f
be
a nonnegative measurable function
on
X
such that
I_{\beta}f
is finite everywhere in
X
.
Assume that there exists
c_{K}>0
such that
for all
w,y\in X
,
w\not=y
, we have

(9)

\left\lvert\frac{d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}-\frac{d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\right\rvert\leq c_{K}\>\frac{d(w,y)^{\eta}}{d(w,z)^{\eta-\beta}\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}

for all
z\in X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))
. Then there is a constant
C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},\beta,\eta,c_{K})>0
such that

(10)

\lvert I_{\beta}f(w)-I_{\beta}f(y)\rvert\leq C_{1}d(w,y)^{\beta}\left(Mf(w)+Mf(y)\right)\,

for every
w,y\in X
.
In particular, it follows that
C_{1}Mf
is a Hajłasz
\beta
-gradient of
I_{\beta}f
.

Proof.

Let
w,y\in X
with
w\not=y
. Then,

\displaystyle\lvert I_{\beta}f(w)-I_{\beta}f(y)\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{X}\frac{f(z)d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,d\mu(z)-\int_{X}\frac{f(z)d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,d\mu(z)+\int_{B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))}f(z)\left\lvert\frac{d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}-\frac{d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\right\rvert\,d\mu(z)\,.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have

\int_{B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta)d(w,y)^{\beta}Mf(w)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(y,3d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta)d(w,y)^{\beta}Mf(y)\,.

On the other hand, the assumed kernel estimate
(
9
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))}

\displaystyle f(z)\left\lvert\frac{d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}-\frac{d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\right\rvert\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq c_{K}d(w,y)^{\eta}\int_{X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(w,z)^{\beta-\eta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,d\mu(z)\,.

Write
r_{j}=2^{j+1}d(w,y)
for every
j=0,1,\ldots
. Since
\eta>\beta
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))}\frac{f(z)d(w,z)^{\beta-\eta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(r_{j})^{\beta-\eta}\int_{B(w,r_{j+1})\setminus B(w,r_{j})}\frac{f(z)}{\mu(B(w,r_{j}))}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(r_{j})^{\beta-\eta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(w,r_{j+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,r_{j+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,r_{j+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(w,r_{j+1})}}f(z)\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,\eta)d(w,y)^{\beta-\eta}Mf(w)\,.

By combining the estimates above, we obtain

\lvert I_{\beta}f(w)-I_{\beta}f(y)\rvert\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,\eta,c_{K})d(w,y)^{\beta}\left(Mf(w)+Mf(y)\right)\,

for every
w,y\in X
.
That is, we have
C_{1}Mf\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(I_{\beta}f)
with

C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},\beta,\eta,c_{K})
.
∎

Example 2.6
.

Assume that
\mu
is a
Q
-uniform measure in
X
for some
Q>0
, that is, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\mu(B(x,r))=C_{1}r^{Q},\text{ for all }x\in X\text{ and all }r>0\,.

If
0<\beta<\min\{Q,1\}
, then the kernel estimate (
9
) holds with
\beta<\eta=1
.
To show this, we proceed as in
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
. By the mean-value theorem,
for every
s,t>0

there exists
0<\theta<1
such that

(11)

\lvert s^{\beta-Q}-t^{\beta-Q}\rvert\leq(Q-\beta)\lvert(1-\theta)s+\theta t\rvert^{\beta-Q-1}\lvert s-t\rvert\,.

Fix
w,y\in X
,
w\not=y
, and
z\in X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))
. By
Q
-uniformity of
\mu
and inequality (
11
),
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}-\frac{d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C_{1}}\left\lvert d(w,z)^{\beta-Q}-d(y,z)^{\beta-Q}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{1},Q,\beta)\frac{\lvert d(w,z)-d(y,z)\rvert}{d(w,z)^{Q-\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{1},Q,\beta)\frac{d(w,y)}{d(w,z)^{1-\beta}\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}\,.

As an example, the
n
-dimensional
Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is
n
-uniform. Uniform measures were first studied in
[
33
]
.

Remark 2.7
.

In an Ahlfors
Q
-regular space
X
, with
Q>0
,
the following version of the Riesz potential is often used:

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)=\int_{X}\frac{f(y)}{d(x,y)^{Q-\beta}}\,d\mu(y)\,,\quad x\in X\,,

see
[
9
,
10
]
and references therein. By the
Q
-regularity,

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
is pointwise comparable with the potential
I_{\beta}f
, that is,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(12)

C^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)\leq I_{\beta}f(x)\leq C\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f(x)\,,

for every
x\in X
.

If
X
is
Q
-regular and
0<\beta<\min\{Q,1\}
, then one can obtain results analogous
to Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
2.5
for
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
.
Observe that the
analogue of (
9
) reads as

\left\lvert\frac{1}{d(w,z)^{Q-\beta}}-\frac{1}{d(y,z)^{Q-\beta}}\right\rvert\leq c_{K}\frac{d(w,y)^{\eta}}{d(w,z)^{Q-\beta+\eta}}\,,\quad w,\,y\in X,\ \ z\in X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))\,.

This automatically holds for
0<\beta<1=\eta
, see the reasoning in Example
2.6
for a proof.

3.
Hajłasz and Riesz capacities

Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded open set and let
F\subset\Omega
.
One of the main objects in this paper is
a capacity of
F
relative to
\Omega
defined via

\beta
-Hajłasz gradients.
The following definition is from
[
8
]
. In the case
\beta=1
, such capacities were earlier considered
in
[
23
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, and let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded open set.
The variational Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity of a closed subset
F\subset\Omega
is

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,\Omega)=\inf_{u}\inf_{g}\int_{X}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

where the infimums are taken over all
u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
, with
u\geq 1
in
F
,
u=0
in
X\setminus\Omega
, and all
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
. If there are no such functions
u
, we set
\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,\Omega)=\infty
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
and
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)
, then
v=\max\{0,\min\{u,1\}\}
is a
\beta
-Hölder function and
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(v)
. Hence, we may also assume
0\leq u\leq 1
in Definition
3.1
.

The following lemma shows that in connected spaces
capacities of balls are comparable to suitably scaled measures of balls.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that the metric space
X
is connected, and let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
.
Then there is a constant
C=C(c_{\mu},p)>0
such that

(13)

C^{-1}r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\leq Cr^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.
Moreover, the second inequality in (
13
) holds even if
X
is not connected.

Proof.

The first inequality in (
13
) is a consequence of

[
8
, Example 4.5]
, where connectivity of
X
is used.
In order to show the second inequality in (
13
), we
let
x\in X
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
. Define

u(y)=\max\left\{0,1-r^{-\beta}\>{\operatorname{dist}(y,B(x,r))^{\beta}}\right\}\ ,\qquad y\in X\,.

Then
u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
,
with a constant
r^{-\beta}
, and therefore
g=r^{-\beta}{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2r)$}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{H}(u)

by
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
. Since

u=1
in
\overline{B(x,r)}
and

u=0
in
X\setminus B(x,2r)
,
we conclude that

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\leq\int_{X}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leq r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,2r))\leq c_{\mu}\,r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.\qed

Another capacity that we use in this paper is the Riesz

(\beta,p)
-capacity.
Such capacities are well known in Euclidean spaces;
we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
27
]
, and references therein. In metric spaces, variants of Riesz
capacities have been studied for instance in
[
32
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
F\subset X
,
\beta>0
and
p\geq 1
. The Riesz

(\beta,p)
-capacity of
F
is

R_{\beta,p}(F)=\inf\bigl\{\lVert f\rVert_{p}^{p}\colon f\geq 0\text{ and }I_{\beta}f\geq 1\text{ on }F\bigr\}\,,

where
\lVert f\rVert_{p}=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(X)}
is the Lebesgue
p
-norm of
f
on
X
.

See Lemma
5.5
for an analogue of Lemma
3.3
for the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacities.

4.
Comparability of Riesz and Hajłasz capacities

In this section we show that, under certain geometric hypotheses on the metric and measure,
the capacities defined in terms of Riesz potentials and Hajłasz gradients are comparable.
One direction of the comparison, given in Theorem
4.2
,
holds under much weaker assumptions than the other one in Theorem
4.4
.
In particular, in the latter the kernel estimate (
9
) is assumed, together with
a reverse doubling condition. Recall also that we assume throughout the paper that the measure
\mu

is doubling, with a constant
c_{\mu}
.

The following chaining lemma,
which will be applied in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, is a
straightforward modification of a result in
[
13
, p. 30]
;
hence we omit the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that the metric space
X
is connected. Then
there exists a constant
M
such that for all
y\in X
and all
0<\rho<R<(3/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
, there exists
k=k(c_{\mu},y,\rho,R)\in{\mathbb{N}}
and balls
B_{0},...,B_{k}

satisfying the following properties:

(i)

B_{0}\subset X\setminus B(y,R)
and
B_{k}\subset B(y,\rho)
,

(ii)

M^{-1}\operatorname{diam}(B_{i})\leq d(y,B_{i})\leq M\operatorname{diam}(B_{i})
for all

i=0,1,2,\ldots,k
,

(iii)

there is a ball
R_{i}\subset B_{i}\cap B_{i+1}
such
that
B_{i}\cup B_{i+1}\subset MR_{i}
for
all
i=0,1,2,\ldots,k-1
,

(iv)

No point of
X
belongs to more than

M
balls
B_{i}
.

The first main result of this section
gives an upper bound for the Riesz
capacity in terms of the Hajłasz capacity in connected metric spaces.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
X
is connected and
let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
.
Moreover, let

E\subset X
be a closed set,
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
. Then

(14)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\geq C(c_{\mu},p)R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Proof.

Let
u\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)
be such that
0\leq u\leq 1
,
u=1
in
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
and
u=0
outside of
B(x,2r)
.
Denote by
\kappa
the
\beta
-Hölder constant of
u
in
X
and let
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
.
Theorem
2.3
implies that the
Hajłasz
(\beta,1,1)
-Poincaré inequality

(15)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u-u_{B}\rvert\,d\mu\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(B)^{\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}g\,d\mu

holds for all balls
B\subset X
.
We claim that (
15
), together with the facts that
u=0
outside of
B(x,2r)
and
r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
, give the following inequality

(16)

\lvert u(y)\rvert\leq C(c_{\mu})I_{\beta}g(y)\,,

for every
y\in\overline{B(x,r)}
.
We postpone the proof of (
16
)
and finish the proof of the theorem while assuming (
16
).
It follows from the properties of
u
and (
16
) that

I_{\beta}(C(c_{\mu})g)\geq 1
in
E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
. Thus

C(c_{\mu},p)\int_{X}g^{p}\,d\mu=\lVert C(c_{\mu})g\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

Taking infimum over all
u
and
g\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
, we get

(17)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\geq C(c_{\mu},p)R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,.

For convenience of the reader, we
give the proof of (
16
), which is contained in
[
28
]
.
Let
y\in\overline{B(x,r)}
,
R=3r<(3/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
and
0<\rho<R
,
and let
B_{0},B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
be the corresponding family of balls given by Lemma
4.1
.

Notice that since
B_{0}\subset X\setminus B(y,3r)\subset X\setminus B(x,2r)
, we have
u=0
on
B_{0}
.
Then, we can write

\displaystyle\lvert u(y)\rvert=\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{0}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lvert u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{B_{i}}\rvert+\lvert u(y)-u_{B_{k}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\lvert u_{B_{i+1}}-u_{R_{i}}\rvert+\lvert u_{B_{i}}-u_{R_{i}}\rvert\right)+\kappa\rho^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},M)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}\lvert u-u_{B_{i}}\rvert\,d\mu+\kappa\rho^{\beta}\,.

Applying the Poincaré inequality (
15
), we get

\displaystyle\lvert u(y)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},M)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\operatorname{diam}(B_{i})^{\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{i}}}g\,d\mu+\kappa\rho^{\beta}

\displaystyle=C(c_{\mu},M)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{g(z)\operatorname{diam}(B_{i})^{\beta}}{\mu(B_{i})}\,d\mu(z)+\kappa\rho^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},M)\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{g(z)d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\,d\mu(z)+\kappa\rho^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},M)I_{\beta}g(y)+\kappa\rho^{\beta}\,,

and inequality (
16
) follows
by letting
\rho\to 0
.
∎

Under additional assumptions on
X
inequality (
14
) can be reversed,
see Theorem
4.4
. Before a precise formulation of this reverse estimate,
we prove an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, and
assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Let
x\in X
,
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
and let
f\in L^{p}(X)
be a nonnegative function such that

(18)

\int_{X\setminus B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}

for some
z\in\overline{B(x,r)}
. Then
there exists a constant
C=C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma,p)
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(X)}\geq Cr^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we assume
that
X
is bounded. The case when
X
is unbounded
is treated in a similar way,
but the essential difference is that then the sums below will have infinitely many terms.
Write
r_{k}=2^{k}r/5

for every
k=0,1,2,\ldots,K+1
, where
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
is
chosen such that
r_{K}\leq\operatorname{diam}(X)<2r_{K}
. Note that
X=B(z,r_{K+1})

and recall that
z\in\overline{B(x,r)}
. Hence,
by the doubling condition for the measure
\mu
and by (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu(B(x,r))^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{\mu}^{4}}{2}\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{4}\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{X\setminus B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=c_{\mu}^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{B(z,r_{k+1})\setminus B(z,r_{k})}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{4}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta}\int_{B(z,r_{k+1})\setminus B(z,r_{k})}\frac{f(y)}{\mu(B(z,r_{k}))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{5}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,r_{k+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{k+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{k+1})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{k+1})}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{5}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\mu(B(z,r_{k+1}))^{\frac{1}{p}}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta}\biggl(\int_{B(z,r_{k+1})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

By the reverse doubling condition (
4
), for every

k=0,1,2\ldots,K
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\mu(B(z,r_{k+1}))^{\frac{1}{p}}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta}\ =\frac{\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\mu(B(z,r_{k+1}))^{\frac{1}{p}}}(r_{k+1})^{\frac{\sigma}{p}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\leq c_{\sigma}(r/5)^{\frac{\sigma}{p}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\,,

since
r_{k+1}\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
. Hence, we can continue to estimate as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu(B(x,r))^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{5}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{\mu(B(z,r/5))^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\mu(B(z,r_{k+1}))^{\frac{1}{p}}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta}\biggl(\int_{B(z,r_{k+1})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{5}c_{\sigma}\sum_{k=0}^{K}(r/5)^{\frac{\sigma}{p}}(r_{k+1})^{\beta-\frac{\sigma}{p}}\biggl(\int_{B(z,r_{k+1})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mu}^{5}c_{\sigma}(r/5)^{\beta}\biggl(\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{(\beta-\frac{\sigma}{p})(k+1)}\,.

Since
\sigma>\beta p
, the geometric series converges. By combining the above estimates, we get

\mu(B(x,r))^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma,p)r^{\beta}\biggl(\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\biggl)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

The estimate

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(X)}\geq Cr^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

follows after simplication.
∎

The second main result of this section is a converse of Theorem
4.2
.
Observe that assumption (
19
) is the same as the kernel estimate (
9
)
that appears in Lemma
2.5
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that the metric space
X
is complete. Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
,
and assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
. In addition,
assume that there exist
\eta>\beta
and
c_{K}>0
such that
for all
w,y\in X
,
w\not=y
, we have

(19)

\left\lvert\frac{d(w,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}-\frac{d(y,z)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(y,d(y,z)))}\right\rvert\leq c_{K}\>\frac{d(w,y)^{\eta}}{d(w,z)^{\eta-\beta}\mu(B(w,d(w,z)))}

for all
z\in X\setminus B(w,2d(w,y))
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(c_{K},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\eta,\sigma,p)
such that

(20)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\leq C\,R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})

whenever
E\subset X
is a closed set,
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

Proof.

We may assume that the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity on
the right-hand side of (
20
) is finite.
Fix a nonnegative function
f\in L^{p}(X)
such that
I_{\beta}f\geq 1
on
F=E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
.
It suffices to show that

(21)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,B(x,2r))\leq C(c_{K},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\eta,\sigma,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)

since then the claim (
20
) follows
by taking infimum over all
f
as above. In order to prove inequality (
21
), we consider several cases.

First we assume that

(22)

\int_{X\setminus B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,

for some
z\in F\subset\overline{B(x,r)}
. Then, by Lemma
4.3
, we have

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y).

By monotonicity and Lemma
3.3
, we get

(23)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,B(x,2r))\leq\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,,

and so (
21
) holds in this case.

Next we consider the case when (
22
)
does not hold for any
z\in F
. Since
I_{\beta}f\geq 1
on
F
, we have

(24)

\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)=I_{\beta}f(z)-\int_{X\setminus B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\,,

for every
z\in F
.

Assume first that
f
is bounded in
X
. Let

\psi(z)=\max\bigl\{0,1-r^{-\beta}d(z,B(x,r))^{\beta}\bigr\}\,

for every
z\in X
.
Then
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,
\psi=0
in
X\setminus B(x,2r)
,
\psi=1
in
\overline{B(x,r)}
,
and
\psi
is a
\beta
-Hölder function in
X
with a constant
r^{-\beta}
.
Define
h=2f{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2r)$}}
and

(25)

v(z)=I_{\beta}h(z)\psi(z)\,,\qquad z\in X\,.

By inequality (
24
), for every
z\in F=E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
we have

1\leq\int_{X}\frac{2f(y){\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2r)$}}(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)=I_{\beta}\bigl(2f{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2r)$}}\bigr)(z)=I_{\beta}h(z).

Thus

v\geq 1
in
F=E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
and
v=0
in
X\setminus B(x,2r)
.
By using Lemma
2.4
and properties of
h
, we see that
I_{\beta}h
is finite everywhere in
X
.
Since
Mh
is bounded in
X
, Lemma
2.5
implies
that
I_{\beta}h\in\operatorname{Lip}_{\beta}(X)

and
C_{1}Mh\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(I_{\beta}h)
,
where
C_{1}=C(c_{\mu},\beta,\eta,c_{K})>0
.

Let
M=\sup_{z\in B(x,2r)}I_{\beta}h(z)
and
u=\min\{I_{\beta}h,M\}
. Then
u
is a bounded
\beta
-Hölder function that coincides with
I_{\beta}h
on
B(x,2r)
; thus
v=u\psi
in
X
.
We also have that
C_{1}Mh\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(u)
. Lemma
2.2
implies that the function
v
is
\beta
-Hölder in
X
and

g_{v}=\left(C_{1}Mh\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}+r^{-\beta}I_{\beta}h\right){\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\{\psi\not=0\}$}}=\left(C_{1}Mh\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}+r^{-\beta}u\right){\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\{\psi\not=0\}$}}\in\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\beta}(v)\,.

Thus the pair
v
and
g_{v}
is admissible for testing the Hajlasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity of
F
relative to
B(x,2r)
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,B(x,2r))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}g_{v}(z)^{p}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}C_{1}^{p}\int_{B(x,2r)}(Mh(z))^{p}\,d\mu(z)+2^{p}r^{-\beta p}\int_{B(x,2r)}(I_{\beta}h(z))^{p}\,d\mu(z).

Since
h\leq 2f
, we have
Mh\leq 2Mf
. By Lemma
2.4
, we also have

\displaystyle I_{\beta}h(z)

\displaystyle=2\int_{B(x,2r)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B(z,4r)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta)r^{\beta}Mf(z)

for every
z\in B(x,2r)
.
Hence,
we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(F,B(x,2r))\leq C(C_{1},p,c_{\mu},\beta)\int_{X}(Mf(z))^{p}\,d\mu(z)\leq C(C_{1},p,c_{\mu},\beta)\int_{X}f(z)^{p}\,d\mu(z)\,.

The last step follows
from the Hardy–Littlewood maximal theorem since
p>1
, see

[
5
, Theorem 3.13]
. We have shown
that inequality (
21
) holds.

In case
f
is not bounded in
X
and (
24
) holds for all
z\in F
, we write
f_{k}(y)=\min\{k,f(y)\}
for every
y\in X
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Observe that
f_{k}\to f
almost everywhere in
X
, as
k\to\infty
.
Hence, by Fatou’s lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f_{k}(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

for every
z\in F
. We claim that there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(26)

\frac{1}{3c_{\mu}}\leq\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f_{k}(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

for all
z\in F
.
Assume that such a number
k
does not exist. Then for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists

z_{k}\in F
such that

\frac{1}{3c_{\mu}}>\int_{B(z_{k},r/5)}\frac{f_{k}(y)d(z_{k},y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z_{k},d(z_{k},y)))}\,d\mu(y)\,.

Since
F\subset X
is a closed and bounded set and
X
is a complete doubling space,
by
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]

we find that
F
is compact. In particular, there exists
a subsequence
(z_{k_{m}})_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
(z_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}z_{k_{m}}=z_{0}\in F
. By Fatou’s lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3c_{\mu}}\geq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{B(z_{k_{m}},r/5)}\frac{f_{k_{m}}(y)d(z_{k_{m}},y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z_{k_{m}},d(z_{k_{m}},y)))}\,d\mu(y)\geq\frac{1}{c_{\mu}}\int_{B(z_{0},r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z_{0},y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z_{0},d(z_{0},y)))}\,d\mu(y)\,.

This is a contradiction with (
24
), since
z_{0}\in F
.
We have shown that there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that (
26
) holds for all
z\in F
.
Using a test function
v
as in (
25
) but with

h=3c_{\mu}f_{k}{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle B(x,2r)$}}
, we repeat the reasoning above and finish the proof.
∎

Combining Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
, we obtain the following result
on the comparability of the Riesz and Hajłasz capacities.

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume that the metric space
X
is complete and connected. Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
,
and assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
and that the kernel
estimate (
19
) holds for some
\eta>\beta
and
c_{K}>0
.
Then there is a constant

C=C(c_{K},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\eta,\sigma,p)
such that

(27)

C^{-1}\>R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\leq\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\leq C\>R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\,,

whenever
E\subset X
is a closed set,
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

Remark 4.6
.

In an Ahlfors
Q
-regular space
X
it is natural to consider capacities defined in terms of Riesz potentials
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f
, see Remark
2.7
. For these capacities, one can obtain the results analogous to Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
. The proofs are similar and use the accordingly modified statements of Lemma
2.4
, Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
4.3
.
In this setting with
0<\beta<1
, the kernel estimate (
19
) is not required anymore, see Remark
2.7
,
and the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
) holds with
\sigma=Q
.
Hence, it follows that the corresponding Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity
\mathcal{I}_{\beta}f

(and by (
12
) also
I_{\beta}f
)
is comparable to the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity in any
complete and connected Ahlfors
Q
-regular space with
0<\beta<1
and
Q>\beta p
.
This proves Theorem
1.1
.

5.
Hausdorff content density condition

In this section, we introduce a version of the Hausdorff content density condition,
slightly different from the one that was used in
[
25
]
.
This is an auxiliary condition that
will be used in Section
6
to connect
Riesz and Hajłasz capacity density conditions.

Definition 5.1
.

The (
\rho
-restricted) Hausdorff content of codimension
q\geq 0

of a set
F\subset X
is defined by

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{\rho}(F)=\inf\Biggl\{\sum_{k}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))\,r_{k}^{-q}:F\subset\bigcup_{k}B(x_{k},r_{k})\text{ and }0<r_{k}\leq\rho\Biggr\}.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition of codimension
q\geq 0
if there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(28)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{0}\>{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
q\geq 0
.
Then it is easy to show that

r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))={\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(B(x,r))
and that

r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(\overline{B(x,r)})\leq C(c_{\mu})\,r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
and all
r>0
.
This can be seen as an analogue of Lemma
3.3

for the Hausdorff content.
In particular, it follows that
a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition of codimension
q\geq 0
if, and only if
there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{1}r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))

for every
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
This equivalent condition was taken as the definition
of the Hausdorff content density condition
of codimension
q
in
[
8
]
.

By definition, we have
{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(F)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)

if
0<r\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
and
F\subset X
. Theorem
5.3
gives a condition under which this inequality can be reversed.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
q>0
.
The following conditions are
equivalent:

(i)

There is a constant
c_{q}>0
such that the quantitative
reverse doubling condition

(29)

\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\leq c_{q}\Bigl(\frac{r}{R}\Bigr)^{q}

holds
for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<R\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

(ii)

There is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)\leq C_{2}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(F)

whenever

F\subset\overline{B(x,r)}
, with
x\in X
and
r>0
.

(iii)

There is a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{3}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
and all
r>0
.

Proof.

We assume that condition (i) holds and we prove condition (ii). For this purpose, we fix
F\subset\overline{B(x,r)}
with
x\in X
and
r>0
.
Let
\{B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k\in I}
be a finite or countable cover of
F
such that

B(x_{k},r_{k})\cap F\not=\emptyset
and
0<r_{k}\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
for all
k\in I
.
If
r_{k}\leq r
for all
k\in I
, then

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)\leq\sum_{k\in I}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))r_{k}^{-q}\,.

Next we assume that
at least one of the radii
r_{k}
,
k\in I
, is not bounded by
r
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

r_{1}>r
.
By the
5r
-covering lemma
[
5
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain
N

pairwise disjoint balls
B(y_{i},r/5)
such that

F\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B(y_{i},r)
and
y_{i}\in F
for every
i=1,\ldots,N
.
The doubling condition implies that
N\leq C(c_{\mu})
.
By the assumed reverse doubling condition, followed by the doubling condition, we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu(B(y_{i},r))r^{-q}\leq c_{q}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu(B(y_{i},r_{1}))r_{1}^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq Nc_{\mu}^{2}c_{q}\mu(B(x_{1},r_{1}))r_{1}^{-q}\leq C(c_{q},c_{\mu})\sum_{k\in I}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))r_{k}^{-q}\,.

In any case, we see that

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)\leq C(c_{q},c_{\mu})\sum_{k\in I}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k}))r_{k}^{-q}\,,

and condition (ii) with a constant
C_{2}=C(c_{q},c_{\mu})>0
follows by taking infimum over all covers

\{B(x_{k},r_{k})\}_{k\in I}
of
F
as above.

Condition (ii) implies condition (iii) by choosing
F=B(x,r)
and using Remark
5.2
.
Finally, assume that
condition (iii) holds. Let
x\in X
and
0<r<R\leq 2\operatorname{diam}(X)
. Then
Remark
5.2
implies that

\displaystyle r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(B(x,r))\leq C_{3}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(B(x,R))

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{R}(B(x,R))=C_{3}R^{-q}\mu(B(x,R))\,.

Thus, condition (i) holds with a constant
c_{q}=C_{3}
.
∎

Assuming (
29
) for some
q>0
, then a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition of codimension
q\geq 0
if and only if

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq C\>{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{2\operatorname{diam}(X)}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
This is a direct consequence of Theorem
5.3
.

The next lemma is an extension of
[
17
, Proposition 3.12]

to metric spaces, with a new proof; see
also
[
3
, Corollary 5.1.14]
.

It
gives a Riesz capacity estimate for sets satisfying the Hausdorff content density condition.
This result and also its consequence in Lemma
5.5

will be applied in the proof of Theorem
6.4
,
which relates the density conditions corresponding to the Riesz capacity,
the Hajłasz capacity and the Hausdorff content.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
,
and assume that
\mu

satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Moreover, let
E\subset X
be a closed set
that
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
28
) for some
0<q<\beta p
.
Then there is a constant
C=C(c_{1},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,q,p)>0
such that

R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq Cr^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
Here the constant
c_{1}
is defined in (
30
).

Proof.

By Remark
5.2
, there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(30)

{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{1}r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))\,,

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

Fix
x\in E
and
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
, and write
F=E\cap\overline{B(x,r)}
.
We may assume that
R_{\beta,p}(F)<\infty
.
Let
f\in L^{p}(X)
be a nonnegative test function for the capacity
R_{\beta,p}(F)
. Then, in particular,

(31)

1\leq I_{\beta}f(z)=\int_{X}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

for every
z\in F
.
We consider two cases.

First, assume that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

for every
z\in F
. In this case,
we proceed as in the proof
of
[
32
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Namely, we fix
z\in F
and
define
r_{i}=2^{-i}r/5
,
i=0,1,2,\dots
. Estimating as in Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\leq 2c_{\mu}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}^{\beta}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B(z,r_{i})}}f(y)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{\mu}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,r_{i}))^{1/p}}\left(\int_{B(z,r_{i})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p}.

For every
\delta>0
, there is a constant
C(\delta)>0
such that

1=C(\delta)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{-i\delta}=C(\delta)5^{\delta}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(r_{i}/r\right)^{\delta}=C(\delta)(r/5)^{-\delta}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}^{\delta}.

Choose
\delta=\beta-q/p>0
, and
combine two previous expressions to obtain the inequality

(32)

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}r_{i}^{\beta-q/p}\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)r^{\beta-q/p}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{r_{i}^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,r_{i}))^{1/p}}\left(\int_{B(z,r_{i})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p}\,,

which is valid for every
z\in F
.

It follows from (
32
)
that, for each
z\in F
, there is a ball
B(z,r_{i_{z}})
such that
r_{i_{z}}\leq r/5
and

\mu(B(z,r_{i_{z}}))r_{i_{z}}^{-q}\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)r^{\beta p-q}\int_{B(z,r_{i_{z}})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

Using the
5r
-covering lemma
[
5
, Lemma 1.7]
, we obtain countably many points
z_{k}\in F
,
k\in I
,
such that the corresponding balls
B(z_{k},r_{k})=B(z_{k},r_{i_{z_{k}}})
are pairwise disjoint and
F\subset\bigcup_{k\in I}B(z_{k},5r_{k})
.
Recalling definition (
28
) of the Hausdorff content of codimension
q
, and using the doubling property of measure
\mu
and the fact that
\{B(z_{k},r_{k})\}_{k\in I}
is a family of pairwise disjoint balls, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in I}\mu(B(z_{k},5r_{k}))(5r_{k})^{-q}\leq c_{\mu}^{3}\sum_{k\in I}\mu(B(z_{k},r_{k}))r_{k}^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)r^{\beta p-q}\sum_{k\in I}\int_{B(z_{k},r_{k})}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\leq C(c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)r^{\beta p-q}\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

Applying (
30
), we get

r^{-q}\mu(B(x,r))\leq c_{1}^{-1}{\mathcal{H}}^{\mu,q}_{r}(F)\leq C(c_{1},c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)r^{\beta p-q}\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

Simplifying the exponents yields

(33)

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{1},c_{\mu},\beta,q,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

In the second case, we assume that there exists
z\in F
such that

\int_{B(z,r/5)}\frac{f(y)d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)<\frac{1}{2}\,.

By inequality (
31
) and Lemma
4.3
we have

(34)

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\beta,\sigma,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\,.

From (
33
) and (
34
) it follows that

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{1},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,q,p)\int_{X}f(y)^{p}\,d\mu(y)

for all nonnegative test functions
f\in L^{p}(X)
for the capacity
R_{\beta,p}(F)
.
By taking infimum over all such functions, we obtain

r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq C(c_{1},c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,q,p)R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<\operatorname{diam}(E)/8
, as desired.
∎

Lemma
5.4
leads to an analogue of Lemma
3.3
for the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacities.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that
X
is connected. Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
,
and assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
Then there is a constant
C=C(c_{\mu},c_{\sigma},\sigma,\beta,p)>0

such that

C^{-1}r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\leq R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)})\leq Cr^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in X
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
.

Proof.

Observe that
X
itself is a closed set
that
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
28
) for every
q\geq 0
.
Hence, the first inequality follows from Lemma

5.4
.
On the other hand, by Theorem
4.2

and inequality (
13
), we obtain

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)})\leq C\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}\big(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r)\big)\leq C\,r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))\,.

The second inequality follows.
∎

We finish this section with an example showing that the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
) for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
cannot be removed from Lemma
5.5
.

Example 5.6
.

Consider
X={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
equipped with the Euclidean
distance
d
and the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure which we denote by
\mu
. Fix
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
such that

\beta p>n
.
Observe that
the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
does not hold in
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},d,\mu)
for any
\sigma>\beta p
, since
\beta p>n
.
Define
A_{j}=B(0,3j)\setminus B(0,2j)
for all
j=1,2,\ldots
.
Let
z\in\overline{B(0,1)}
. Then

\displaystyle I_{\beta}\left(j^{-\beta}{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle A_{j}$}}\right)(z)

\displaystyle=j^{-\beta}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{d(z,y)^{\beta}}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)\geq j^{-\beta}j^{\beta}\int_{A_{j}}\frac{1}{\mu(B(z,d(z,y)))}\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{A_{j}}\frac{1}{\mu(B(0,5j))}\,d\mu(y)=\frac{\mu(A_{j})}{\mu(B(0,5j))}=C(n)>0\,.

It follows that functions
f_{j}=C(n)^{-1}j^{-\beta}{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle A_{j}$}}
are admissible
test functions for the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity of
\overline{B(0,1)}
for every
j=1,2,\ldots
, and therefore

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(0,1)})\leq\lVert f_{j}\rVert_{p}^{p}=C(n,p)j^{-\beta p}\mu(A_{j})\leq C(n,p)j^{n-\beta p}

for all
j=1,2,\ldots
. By taking
j\to\infty
, we see that

R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(0,1)})=0
.

6.
Equivalence of density conditions

In Corollary
4.5
we showed that the
Riesz and Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacities
are comparable in a complete and connected space
X

if
\mu
satisfies a suitable reverse doubling condition and the kernel estimate (
19
) holds.
Next, we consider the corresponding capacity density conditions
and show their equivalence in Theorem
6.4
.
The kernel estimate is no longer needed, but instead we need to assume that
the space
X
is geodesic since that is required in Theorem
6.2
.

The Hajłasz capacity density condition is known to be
self-improving (open-ended) by the results in
[
8
]
,
and hence the same also holds for the Riesz capacity density condition.
This is stated explicitly in Corollary
6.5
.

Definition 6.1
.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, if there
is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(35)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\geq c_{0}\>\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
that a closed set
E
in a connected metric space
X
satisfies
the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, if and only if
there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(36)

\operatorname{cap}_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\geq c_{1}\>r^{-\beta p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
This condition was taken as the definition
of the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition
in
[
8
]
.

The following result from
[
8
, Theorem 9.5]
shows that in complete geodesic metric spaces
the Hajłasz capacity density condition is equivalent to a Hausdorff content density condition.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
X
is a complete geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, and let
E\subset X
be a closed
set. Then the following conditions are equivalent

(i)

E
satisfies the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
35
).

(ii)

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
28
) for some
0<q<\beta p
.

Theorem
6.2
and Lemma
5.4
are the main
ingredients in the proof of Theorem
6.4
, which
is one of our main results and adds the following Riesz capacity density condition
to the list of conditions in Theorem
6.2
.
See also
[
8
, Theorem 9.5]
for many more equivalent conditions,
and Theorem
7.8
for the case
\beta=1
.

Definition 6.3
.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
\beta>0
, if there
is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(37)

R_{\beta,p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)})\geq c_{0}\>R_{\beta,p}(\overline{B(x,r)})

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume that
X
is a complete geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, and
assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
. Then the following
conditions are equivalent for a closed set
E\subset X
:

(i)

E
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
37
).

(ii)

E
satisfies the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
35
).

(iii)

E
satisfies the Hausdorff content density condition (
28
) for some
0<q<\beta p
.

Proof.

Since
X
is geodesic, it is in particular connected.
To show the implication from (i) to (ii) we assume that
E
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition (
37
). By using this condition, Theorem
4.2

and Lemma
5.5
, we find that inequality (
36
) holds
for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.
By Lemma
3.3
, this is equivalent to the Hajłasz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition.

The implication from (ii) to (iii) is a part of Theorem
6.2
, and
the implication from (iii) to (i) follows by Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.5
.
∎

From Theorem
6.4

we obtain as a corollary
that the Riesz capacity density condition is self-improving.
This can be regarded as an extension of Lewis’ result
[
27
]

to the setting of complete geodesic spaces.
For the proof of the corollary, it suffices
to notice that condition (iii) in Theorem
6.4

remains valid even if we modify both
\beta
and
p
slightly.
In the Euclidean case, this approach to the self-improvement
of Riesz capacity density condition, which is largely
based on the results in
[
8
]
, is
completely different from the original argument of Lewis.

Corollary 6.5
.

Assume that
X
is a complete geodesic space. Let

1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta\leq 1
, and
assume that
\mu
satisfies the quantitative reverse doubling condition (
4
)
for some exponent
\sigma>\beta p
.
If a closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the Riesz
(\beta,p)
-capacity density condition,
then there exists
0<\delta<\min\{\beta,p-1\}
such that

E
satisfies the Riesz
(\gamma,s)
-capacity density condition
whenever

\beta-\delta<\gamma\leq 1
,
p-\delta<s<\infty
and
\sigma>\gamma s
.

7.
Comparability of Hajłasz
(1,p)
-capacity and
p
-capacity

In this last section of the paper, we add into the considerations
one more notion of capacity, the variational
p
-capacity.
The definition of this capacity is given in terms of

p
-weak upper gradients, and we begin by recalling relevant preliminaries.
The main result of this section is Theorem
7.5
,
which shows the comparability of the variational
p
-capacity and the
(1,p)
-Hajłasz capacity
under suitable assumptions. The comparability of the capacities implies
also the equivalence of the corresponding density conditions, stated
at the end of the section in Corollary
7.7
,
which in turn can be combined with the results in Section
6

to give further lists of equivalent conditions in the case
\beta=1
;
see Theorems
7.8
and
7.9
.

Let
u
be a real valued function on
X
.
A Borel function
g\geq 0
on
X
is
an
upper gradient
of
u
if for all curves
\gamma

(see Section
2.1
) joining
any two points
x,y\in X
we have

(38)

\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds\,,

whenever both
u(x)
and
u(y)
are finite, and
\int_{\gamma}g\,ds=\infty

otherwise.
In addition, a measurable function
g\geq 0
on
X
is a
p
-weak upper gradient

of
u
, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
if inequality (
38
) holds for
p
-almost every curve
\gamma
joining arbitrary points
x
and
y
in
X
. That is, there exists a nonnegative Borel function
\rho\in L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(X)
such that

\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\infty
whenever (
38
) does not hold for the curve
\gamma
.
Here
\rho\in L^{p}_{\textup{loc}}(X)
means that
for each
x\in X
there exists
r_{x}>0
such that
\rho\in L^{p}(B(x,r_{x}))
.
We refer to
[
5
]
for more information on
p
-weak upper gradients.

We say that the space
X
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality
, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
if there exist constants
C_{P}>0
and
\lambda\geq 1

such that for all balls
B\subset X
, all measurable
functions
u
on
X
, and all
p
-weak upper gradients
g
of
u
we have

(39)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B}}\lvert u-u_{B}\rvert\,d\mu\leq C_{P}\,\operatorname{diam}(B)\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773pt\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{\lambda B}}g^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{1/p}\,.

Here
u_{B}
is the integral average of
u
over
B
as in (
7
), and the left-hand side of (
39
) is interpreted as
\infty
whenever
u_{B}
is not defined.
We refer to
[
5
, Chapter 4]
for further details.
The doubling condition (
2
) and
the Poincaré inequality are the standard assumptions on analysis on
metric spaces based on (weak) upper gradients;
recall that we assume throughout that
\mu

is doubling with a constant
c_{\mu}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded open set.
The variational
p
-capacity of a closed subset
F\subset\Omega
is

(40)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)=\inf_{u}\inf_{g}\int_{\Omega}g(x)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,,

where the infimums are taken over all
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
, with
u\geq 1
in
F
,
u=0
in
X\setminus\Omega
,
and all
p
-weak upper gradients
g
of
u
. If there are no such functions
u
, we set
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)=\infty
.

Remark 7.2
.

If
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)<\infty
,
then the infimum in (
40
) can be restricted to
functions
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
satisfying
{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle F$}}\leq u\leq{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega$}}

and to
p
-weak upper gradients
g
of
u
such that
g=g{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega$}}\in L^{p}(X)
.
For a proof we refer to
[
26
, Remark 2.1]
.

Definition 7.3
.

A closed set
E\subset X
satisfies the
p
-capacity density condition,
for
1\leq p<\infty
, if there
is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(41)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\geq c_{0}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

Remark 7.4
.

If
X
supports a
p
-Poincaré inequality for some
1\leq p<\infty
,
then there is a close connection between the
p
-capacity and the measure
of balls, similar to Lemma
3.3
.
Namely, there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
balls
B=B(x,r)
, with
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
, and for
all closed sets
F\subset\overline{B}
we have

(42)

\frac{\mu(F)}{C\,r^{p}}\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,2B)\leq\frac{c_{\mu}\,\mu(B)}{r^{p}}\,.

For a proof of this fact see, for instance,
[
5
, Proposition 6.16]
.
In particular, it holds
for all balls
B=B(x,r)
with
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(X)
that

(43)

\frac{\mu(B)}{C\>r^{p}}\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}\big(\overline{B},2B\big)\leq\frac{c_{\mu}\>\mu(B)}{r^{p}}\,.

As a consequence, if
E\subset X
is a closed set and
X
supports
a
p
-Poincaré inequality, then
E
satisfies the
p
-capacity density condition (
41
) if and only if
there is a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(E\cap\overline{B(x,r)},B(x,2r))\geq c_{1}r^{-p}\mu(B(x,r))

for all
x\in E
and all
0<r<(1/8)\operatorname{diam}(E)
.

The following Theorem
7.5
says that for
1<p<\infty
the variational

p
-capacity and
(1,p)
-Hajłasz capacity are comparable in the appropriate geometrical setting.
In the proof we use the noncentered maximal function
Mf
, which is defined by (
8
).

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Assume that
\Omega\subset X
is a bounded open set and

F\subset\Omega
is a closed set. Then

(44)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)\leq 4^{p}\operatorname{cap}_{1,p}(F,\Omega)\,.

Moreover, if
X
supports a
q
-Poincaré inequality for some
1\leq q<p
, with constants
C_{P}
and
\lambda\geq 1
, then there is a constant
C=C(C_{P},c_{\mu},p,q)
such that

(45)

\operatorname{cap}_{1,p}(F,\Omega)\leq C\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)\,.

Proof.

There are test functions for
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)
if and only if there are test functions for

\operatorname{cap}_{1,p}(F,\Omega)
. Namely, the existence
of test functions is in both cases characterized by the inequality
\operatorname{dist}(F,X\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that this inequality holds since otherwise
both capacities are equal to
\infty
.

We begin with the proof of inequality (
44
). Let
u
be a test function for
\operatorname{cap}_{1,p}(F,\Omega)
, that is,

u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
with
u\geq 1
in
F
and
u=0
in
X\setminus\Omega
.
Let
\kappa\geq 0
be a Lipschitz constant of
u
and let

g
be a Hajłasz
1
-gradient of
u
.
By redefining
g=\kappa
in the exceptional set
N=N(g)
of measure zero, we may assume that
inequality (
6
) holds for all
x,y\in X
, with
\beta=1
. Then arguing as in

[
35
, Lemma 4.7]
with the aid of
[
5
, p. 20]
, we see that
4g
is actually a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}(4g)^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 4^{p}\int_{X}g^{p}\,d\mu\,.

Since this holds for all
u
and all Hajłasz
1
-gradients
g
of
u
, we conclude that (
44
) holds.

Next we prove estimate (
45
)
under the assumption that
X
supports a
q
-Poincaré inequality for
some
1\leq q<p
, with constants
C_{P}
and
\lambda\geq 1
.
Let
u\in\operatorname{Lip}(X)
be a test function for
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(F,\Omega)
and let
g
be a
p
-weak upper gradient of
u
.
Observe that
g
is also a
q
-weak upper gradient of
u
, since
q<p
.
By Remark
7.2
, we may assume that
g=g{\mbox{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{\large$\chi$}}}_{\lower 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Omega$}}
.

We claim that a constant multiple of
(Mg^{q})^{1/q}
is a
1
-Hajłasz gradient of
u
, that is,

(46)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C(C_{P},c_{\mu})\>d(x,y)\Bigl(\big(Mg^{q}(x)\big)^{1/q}+\big(Mg^{q}(y)\big)^{1/q}\Bigr)

for all
x,y\in X
.
To prove (
46
),
we follow a chaining argument from
[
13
, p. 13–14]
.
For this purpose, we let
x,y\in X
,
x\not=y
, and define
B_{0}^{x}=B_{0}^{y}=B\big(x,2d(x,y)\big)
and

B_{j}^{x}=B\big(x,2^{-j}d(x,y)\big),\qquad B_{j}^{y}=B\big(y,2^{-j}d(x,y)\big),\qquad j\geq 1\,.

Notice that the definition of
B_{0}^{x}=B_{0}^{y}
is different
from the other ones.
Nevertheless, the balls are nested in the following way:
B_{0}^{x}\supset B_{1}^{x}\supset B_{2}^{x}\supset\dotsb
and
B_{0}^{y}\supset B_{1}^{y}\supset B_{2}^{y}\supset\dotsb
. Moreover, we have
\mu(B_{j}^{x})\leq c_{\mu}^{2}\mu(B_{j+1}^{x})
and
\mu(B_{j}^{y})\leq c_{\mu}^{3}\mu(B_{j+1}^{y})
for all
j=0,1,\ldots
by the doubling property (
2
). Using continuity of
u
and the above properties, we have

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|u(x)-u_{B_{0}^{x}}|+|u(y)-u_{B_{0}^{y}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|u_{B_{j+1}^{x}}-u_{B_{j}^{x}}|+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|u_{B_{j+1}^{y}}-u_{B_{j}^{y}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j+1}^{x}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{x}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{x}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{x}}}|u-u_{B_{j}^{x}}|\,d\mu\>+\>\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.27773ptB_{j+1}^{y}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{y}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{y}}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{j+1}^{y}}}|u-u_{B_{j}^{y}}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

Next we give an example which shows that

q
-Poincaré inequality assumption cannot be omitted
from the second part of Theorem

This example also shows that the

-Poincaré inequality assumption cannot be omitted

Example 7.6
.

Fix
1<p<q<\infty
and let

for all

.
We consider the metric measure

equipped with the Euclidean distance

and
the weighted measure

such that

.
By

[

we see that

belongs to
the Muckenhoupt class

for all
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, the

\mu
is doubling.
Fix
0<\rho<1

and consider the Lipschitz test

Then
\lvert u^{\prime}\rvert
is a
p
-weak upper gradient of

,
by
[

and so

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,2)}\lvert u^{\prime}(x)\rvert^{p}w(x)\,dx\leq 2\rho^{-p}\int_{0}^{\rho}t^{q-1}\,dt=\frac{2}{q}\rho^{q-p}\,.

.

The equivalence of the variational

(1,p)
-Hajłasz capacity
in Theorem

Corollary 7.7

Let

-capacity density condition if and only if

Let

and

(ii)

-capacity density condition.

(iii)

(1,p)
-capacity density condition.

(iv)

Since

The conditions (i) and (iii) are then equivalent by Corollary

On the other hand, the equivalence of the conditions (ii), (iii) and (iv) follows from Theorem

.
∎

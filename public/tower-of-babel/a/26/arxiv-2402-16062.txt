Introduction and statement of main results

In this paper
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk and
\mathbb{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}
is its boundary.
For
\alpha>-1

T[f]=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}(1-|z|^{2})^{-\alpha-1}+\frac{\alpha}{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{-\alpha-1}\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\bar{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\right)+(1-|z|^{2})^{-\alpha}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\bar{z}}

be the second order elliptic partial differential operator.
Of particular interest to our analysis is the homogeneous partial differential equation

(1.1)

T[f]=0\text{ in }\ \ \ \mathbb{D},

and its associated the Dirichlet boundary value problem as follows

(1.2)

\begin{split}T[f]&=0\text{ in }\ \ \ \mathbb{D}\\
f|_{\mathbb{T}}&=f^{\ast}.\end{split}

Here the boundary data
f^{\ast}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
is a distribution on
\mathbb{T}
, and the boundary condition on (

f_{r}\rightarrow f^{\ast}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
as

, where

,

.
Then
f
is a

solution to the equation (

) with the boundary condition

, if and only if

(1.3)

f(z)=P_{\alpha}[F]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}K_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})f^{\ast}(e^{it})dt

where

K_{\alpha}(z)=c_{\alpha}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{|1-z|^{\alpha+2}},

c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha/2)^{2}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}.

The constant

is chosen in order to ensure that

\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}K_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})dt=1.

Theorem 1.3

(Schwarz lemma for
\alpha-

.

Assume that

-harmonic function with

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We start with the formula

f(z)=P_{\alpha}[f^{\ast}]=\int_{0}^{2\pi}K_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})f^{\ast}(e^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}.

Then by Hölder inequality we have

|f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\|f^{\ast}\|_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|K_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})|^{q}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)^{1/q}.

Let
z=re^{ix}
. After the change of variables

e^{i(t-x)}=\frac{r+e^{is}}{1+re^{is}},

dt=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1+r^{2}+2r\cos s}ds,

we get

|f(z)|\leq c_{\alpha}\|f^{\ast}\|_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}A(q,s)\frac{ds}{2\pi}\right)^{1/q}.

Where

A(q,s)=\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1-q}\left(1+r^{2}+2r\cos s\right)^{-1+q+\frac{\alpha q}{2}}.

Now

B_{\alpha,p}(r)=c_{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(1+r^{2}+2r\cos s\right)^{-1+q+\frac{\alpha q}{2}}\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)^{1/q}.

Then for
m=q(1+\alpha/2)-1,
by using the Taylor development

\left(1+r^{2}+2r\cos s\right)^{m}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{n}r^{n}\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{m-n}\binom{m}{n}\cos^{n}s

and the formula

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{n}sds=\frac{\left(1+(-1)^{n}\right)\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1+n}{2}\right]}{\Gamma\left[\frac{2+n}{2}\right]}

we obtain

where

is the Gaussian hypergoemtric function and

(2.2)

The last fact follows from Lemma
2.2

2.1

We have

\begin{split}H^{\prime}(y)&=2mr\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\cos x|^{p}\left(1+r^{2}+2r\cos(x-y)\right)^{m-1}\sin(x-y)dx\\

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{-}}\frac{(1+u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{+}}\frac{(1-u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt.

Since
u(0)=0
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{-}}u(e^{it})dt=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{+}}u(e^{it})dt

Thus

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{-}}\frac{(1+u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{-}}\frac{(1+u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt

=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{+}}\frac{(1-u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{+}}\frac{(1-u(e^{it}))}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt.

Thus we proved
u(z)\leq U_{\alpha}(z)
. From (
3.1
) we obtain

(3.2)

\begin{split}U(ir)&=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{2\pi}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt\\
&-\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}dt\bigg).\end{split}

Now

\frac{(1-r^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{\left(1+r^{2}-2r\sin t\right)^{\alpha/2+1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\sin^{k}t

where

a_{k}=\frac{(-2k)^{k}\left(1-r^{2}\right)^{1+\alpha}}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}+k}}\binom{-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}{k}.

Further

\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{k}tdt=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1+k}{2}\right]}{\Gamma\left[1+\frac{k}{2}\right]}

and

\int_{\pi}^{2\pi}\sin^{k}tdt=\frac{(-1)^{k}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1+k}{2}\right]}{\Gamma\left[1+\frac{k}{2}\right]}.

By summing we get

U(ir)=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{2\pi}\frac{4(2+\alpha)(1-r^{2})^{1+\alpha}r}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{2+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}F\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1\ \ \ 1+\frac{\alpha}{4}\ \ \ \frac{3}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\\
\frac{3}{2}\ \ \ \frac{3}{2}\end{array};\frac{4r^{2}}{\left(1+r^{2}\right)^{2}}\right].

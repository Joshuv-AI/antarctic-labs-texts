Accelerated Minimax Algorithms Flock Together Published in SIAM Journal on Optimization.
TaeHo Yoon tyoon7@jhu.edu, Ernest K. Ryu eryu@math.ucla.edu

Abstract
Several new accelerated methods in minimax optimization and fixed-point iterations have recently been discovered, and, interestingly, they rely on a mechanism distinct from Nesterov’s momentum-based acceleration. In this work, we show that these accelerated algorithms exhibit what we call the merging path (MP) property; the trajectories of these algorithms merge quickly. Using this novel MP property, we establish point convergence of existing accelerated minimax algorithms and derive new state-of-the-art algorithms for the strongly-convex-strongly-concave setup and for the prox-grad setup.

1.2
The MP property

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a deterministic algorithm.
Write
\mathfrak{A}(x_{0};{\mathcal{P}})=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
to denote that
\mathfrak{A}
applied to problem
{\mathcal{P}}
with starting point
x_{0}
produces iterates
x_{1},x_{2},\dots
.
We say two algorithms
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
have
\mathcal{O}(r(k))
-
merging paths
if for any problem
{\mathcal{P}}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the iterates
\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}(x_{0};{\mathcal{P}})=\big(x_{0},x_{1}^{(\ell)},x_{2}^{(\ell)},\dots\big)
for
\ell=1,2
satisfy
\big\|x_{k}^{(1)}-x_{k}^{(2)}\big\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(r(k))
.
More concisely, we say
\mathfrak{A}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{A}_{2}
are
\mathcal{O}(r(k))
-MP if they have
\mathcal{O}(r(k))
-merging paths.

The MP property precisely formalizes a notion of near-equivalence of algorithms. In particular, it is a stronger notion of approximation compared to the conceptual ones of prior work
[
66
,
1
]
.
Classical first-order algorithms for smooth convex minimization are not
\mathcal{O}(r(k))
-MP with
r(k)\rightarrow 0
.

In this paper, we show that the accelerated minimax algorithms for smooth convex-concave minimax optimization are
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
-MP (which is faster than the
o(1)
-point convergence).

Fig.

shows that the algorithms’ paths indeed merge far before they converge to the limit.

Background and preliminaries

A function
\mathbf{L}(\cdot,\cdot):{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to\mathbb{R}
is
convex-concave
if
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
are convex sets,
\mathbf{L}(x,y)
is convex as a function of
x
for all
y
, and
\mathbf{L}(x,y)
is concave as a function of
y
for all
x
.
A point
(x_{\star},y_{\star})\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
is a
saddle point
, or
(minimax) solution
of (
1
) if
\mathbf{L}(x_{\star},y)\leq\mathbf{L}(x_{\star},y_{\star})\leq\mathbf{L}(x,y_{\star})
for all
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
.

An operator
{\mathbb{A}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted
{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is a set-valued function, i.e.,
{\mathbb{A}}(z)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For simplicity, we often write
{\mathbb{A}}z={\mathbb{A}}(z)
.
If
{\mathbb{A}}z
contains exactly one element for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we say
{\mathbb{A}}
is
single-valued
and view it as a function.
An operator
{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
monotone
if

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{A}}z-{\mathbb{A}}z^{\prime},z-z^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall\,z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where the notation means that
\langle u-v,z-z^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0
for any
u\in{\mathbb{A}}z
and
v\in{\mathbb{A}}z^{\prime}
.
For
\mu>0
, an operator
{\mathbb{A}}
is
\mu
-
strongly monotone
if

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{A}}z-{\mathbb{A}}z^{\prime},z-z^{\prime}\rangle\geq\mu\|z-z^{\prime}\|^{2},\qquad\forall\,z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The graph of
{\mathbb{A}}
is denoted
\mathrm{Gra}\,{\mathbb{A}}=\{(z,u)\,|\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}z\}
.
A monotone operator
{\mathbb{A}}
is
maximally monotone
if there is no monotone operator
{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{Gra}\,{\mathbb{A}}\subset\mathrm{Gra}\,{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
strictly.
In particular, if
{\mathbb{A}}
is monotone, single-valued, and continuous as a function, then it is maximally monotone
[
8
, Corollary 20.28]
.
Define

\displaystyle({\mathbb{A}}+{\mathbb{B}})(z)=\{u+v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}z,v\in{\mathbb{B}}z\},\quad(\alpha{\mathbb{A}})(z)=\{\alpha u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}z\}

for operators
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
and scalar
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
The inverse of an operator
{\mathbb{A}}
is the operator
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}(u)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,u\in{\mathbb{A}}z\}
.

In a
monotone inclusion problem
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\textrm{find}}&0\in{\mathbb{A}}(z),\end{array}

where
{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is monotone, one finds zeros of an operator
{\mathbb{A}}
.
For any
\alpha>0
, we have
0\in{\mathbb{A}}(z)\iff z\in({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{A}})(z)\iff z\in({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}(z)
.
Therefore, (
2
) is equivalent to the
fixed-point problem
(
1
) with
{\mathbb{T}}=({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{A}})^{-1}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}
.
We call
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}
the
resolvent
of
\alpha{\mathbb{A}}
.

We say
{\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
nonexpansive
if it is
1
-Lipschitz continuous and
contractive
if it is
\rho
-Lipschitz with
\rho<1
.
If
{\mathbb{A}}
is maximally monotone, then
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}
has full domain
[
65
]
and is nonexpansive
[
88
]
for any
\alpha>0
.
Denoting
\mathrm{Zer}\,{\mathbb{A}}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,0\in{\mathbb{A}}(z)\}
and
\mathrm{Fix}\,{\mathbb{T}}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,z={\mathbb{T}}(z)\}
, we have
\mathrm{Zer}\,{\mathbb{A}}=\mathrm{Fix}\,{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}
.
We call the algorithm
z_{k+1}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(z_{k})
the
proximal point method (PPM)
for
{\mathbb{A}}
.

For differentiable and convex-concave
\mathbf{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define

\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(x,y)=(\nabla_{x}\mathbf{L}(x,y),-\nabla_{y}\mathbf{L}(x,y))

and call it the
saddle operator
of
\mathbf{L}
.
Then
\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}
is monotone
[
87
]
, and
z_{\star}=(x_{\star},y_{\star})
is a solution of (
1
) if and only if
\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{\star})=0
.
Therefore, a convex-concave minimax optimization problem (
1
) can be reformulated as a monotone inclusion problem (
2
) with
{\mathbb{A}}=\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}
and as a fixed-point problem (
1
) with
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{J}}_{\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}}
.

The
fixed-point residual norm

\|z-{\mathbb{T}}z\|
quantifies the rate of convergence of an algorithm for finding fixed points.
When
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
, we have
z-{\mathbb{T}}z=z-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(z)\in\alpha{\mathbb{B}}({\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(z))
, because

\displaystyle u={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(z)=({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}})^{-1}(z)\iff z\in({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}})(u)=u+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}u.

Thus, when
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
is single-valued, a convergence rate on
\|z-{\mathbb{T}}z\|
is equivalent to a convergence rate on
\|{\mathbb{B}}(\cdot)\|
.

3
Accelerated smooth minimax algorithms are
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
-MP

In this section, we prove the
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
-MP property among accelerated minimax algorithms and the optimized Halpern iteration.
The problem setup is unconstrained minimax problems (so
{\mathcal{X}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}=\mathbb{R}^{m}
) with convex-concave
\mathbf{L}
such that
\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}
is
L
-Lipschitz.
For notational convenience, write
{\mathbb{B}}=\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d=n+m
.
Our MP result provides new insight into these acceleration mechanisms and allows us to establish their point convergence
z_{k}\to z_{\star}\in\mathrm{Zer}\,{\mathbb{B}}
.

3.1
Preliminaries: Accelerated minimax and proximal algorithms

Recently, several accelerated minimax algorithms with rate
\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
have been proposed.
The list includes the
extra anchored gradient (EAG)
algorithm
[

\displaystyle z_{k+1/2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2},

the
fast extragradient (FEG)

[

\displaystyle z_{k+1/2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})\left(z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}\right)

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2},

and the

[

]

\displaystyle v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}v_{k}

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}.

Prior to the development of these minimax algorithms, the
optimized Halpern’s method (OHM)

[

,

]
was presented for solving the fixed-point problem (
1
) with nonexpansive operator

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{k+1/2}&=\beta_{k}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})w_{k}\\
w_{k+1}&={\mathbb{T}}(w_{k+1/2}),\end{aligned}

(4)

where
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}

OHM converges with rate
\|w_{k+1/2}-{\mathbb{T}}w_{k+1/2}\|^{2}\leq 4\|w_{0}-w_{\star}\|^{2}/(k+1)^{2}

exists.

To clarify, the prior works
[

]
study further general forms of EAG and APS with iteration-dependent step-sizes.
For the sake of simplicity, we consider the case of constant step-sizes in this paper.

3.2
MP property among accelerated algorithms

The accelerated forward algorithms, EAG, FEG, and APS, do not merely resemble OHM in their form. The algorithms are, in fact, near-equivalent in the sense that they have quickly merging paths.

3.1
EAG
\approx
FEG
\approx
APS
\approx
OHM.

Let
{\mathbb{B}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be monotone and
L
-Lipschitz and assume
z_{\star}\in\mathrm{Zer}\,{\mathbb{B}}=\mathrm{Fix}\,{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
exists.
Let
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
and
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}
.
Then FEG and OHM are
\mathcal{O}(\|z_{0}-z_{\star}\|^{2}/k^{2})
-MP for
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
,
and there exists
\eta>0
(not depending on
L
) such that EAG, APS, and OHM are
\mathcal{O}(\|z_{0}-z_{\star}\|^{2}/k^{2})
-MP for
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{\eta}{L}\right)
.

Proof.

Here we provide the proof for the FEG case and defer the proofs for EAG and APS to Appendix
B.2
.
Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\dots}
be the OHM iterates and
\{z_{k}\}_{k=0,1,\dots}
the FEG iterates, and assume
z_{0}=w_{0}
.
Then

\displaystyle z_{k+1}-w_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right)-\left(\beta_{k}w_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})w_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\beta_{k})(z_{k}-w_{k})+\alpha\left({\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right)

where we used
({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}})(w_{k+1})=w_{k+1/2}
in the first equality and
z_{0}=w_{0}
in the second.
Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|z_{k+1}-w_{k+1}\right\|^{2}&=(1-\beta_{k})^{2}\left\|z_{k}-w_{k}\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle\alpha(1-\beta_{k})(z_{k}-w_{k}),{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\rangle\\
&\quad\quad+\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(5)

Now use the similar identity

\displaystyle z_{k+1/2}-w_{k+1}=(1-\beta_{k})(z_{k}-w_{k})-\alpha(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{B}}z_{k}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}

to rewrite the inner product term in (
5
) as

\displaystyle 2\left\langle\alpha(1-\beta_{k})(z_{k}-w_{k}),{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\alpha(z_{k+1/2}-w_{k+1})+\alpha^{2}(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{B}}z_{k}-\alpha^{2}{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha^{2}\left\langle(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{B}}z_{k}-{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\rangle,

where the last inequality follows from monotonicity of
{\mathbb{B}}
.
Combining this with (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|z_{k+1}-w_{k+1}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\beta_{k})^{2}\left\|z_{k}-w_{k}\right\|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\left\langle(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{B}}z_{k}-{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbb{B}}w_{k+1}-{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1/2}\right\|^{2}

be the iterates of OC-Halpern with respect to the resolvent operator
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
with
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{\sum_{j=0}^{k}(1+2\alpha\mu)^{j}}
(viewing
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}
as a
(1+2\alpha\mu)^{-1/2}
-contractive operator) and
w_{0}=z_{0}
.
Then

\displaystyle\|z_{k}-z_{\star}\|^{2}=\|(w_{k}-z_{\star})+(z_{k}-w_{k})\|^{2}\leq 2\|w_{k}-z_{\star}\|^{2}+2\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}.

The first term and the second term can be respectively bounded using
Lemma

4.5
and
Theorem

4.1
.
Applying
Lemma

4.5
with
\gamma=(1+2\alpha\mu)^{1/2}
yields the bound

\displaystyle\|w_{k}-z_{\star}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left(\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{j}}{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{2j}}\right)^{2}\|z_{0}-z_{\star}\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1+\gamma)^{2}}{\gamma^{2k+2}}\|z_{0}-z_{\star}\|^{2}.

Substituting
\gamma=(1+2\alpha\mu)^{1/2}
into the above bound gives the first term of (
6
).
Applying the bound on
\|z_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2}
from
Theorem

4.1
gives the second term of (
6
).
Combining these, the proof is complete.

5
Near-optimal prox-grad-type accelerated minimax algorithm

Consider the setup with minimax objective of the form
\mathbf{L}(x,y)=\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y)+\mathbf{L}_{s}(x,y)
, where
\mathbf{L}_{s}
is
L
-smooth.
Informally assume that
\mathbf{L}_{p}
is nonsmooth and proximable (we clarify this notion below).
We reformulate the minimax optimization problem into the monotone inclusion problem

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\textrm{find}}&0\in(\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}+\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{s})(z),\end{array}

where
\partial_{\pm}
is the analog of
\nabla_{\pm}
for nonsmooth convex-concave functions (precisely defined in
Section

5.1
).
We design the
A
nchored
P
roximal
G
radient
*
(APG
*
)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z_{k}&=\textbf{SM-EAG{+}}\left({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}-\xi_{k},\xi_{k},\epsilon_{k}\right)\\
\xi_{k+1}&=\beta_{k}z_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})\left({\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(z_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k})+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}\right),\end{aligned}

(APG
*
)

where
{\mathbb{A}}=\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}
,
{\mathbb{B}}=\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{s}
,
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the initial point, and
\textbf{SM-EAG{+}}\left({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}-\xi_{k},\xi_{k},\epsilon_{k}\right)
denotes the execution of
SM-EAG+
with the
1
-strongly monotone
(1+\alpha L)
-smooth operator

with
\xi_{k}
as the initial point using the step-size
\frac{\sqrt{(1+\alpha L)^{2}+1}+1}{(1+\alpha L)^{2}}
until reaching the tolerance

APG*
is a proximal-gradient-type algorithm; each iteration,

uses a single proximal operation
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}}
and
multiple (logarithmically many) gradient operations
\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{s}
.
(The superscript * in the name

indicates the multiple gradient evaluations per iteration.)
Thus,

is useful when
\mathbf{L}_{p}
is

in the sense that

can be computed efficiently.

APG*
is designed to approximate the accelerated proximal algorithm

5.1
Preliminaries: Subdifferential operators and forward-backward residual

If
\mathbf{L}_{p}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a finite-valued, not necessarily differentiable convex-concave function, then its
(saddle) subdifferential
operator
\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y)=\{(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,v\in\partial_{x}\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y),w\in\partial_{y}(-\mathbf{L}_{p})(x,y)\}

is maximal monotone
[
86
, Corollary 37.5.2]
, where
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
respectively denote the convex subdifferential with respect to the
x
- and
y
-variables.
However, in order to encompass a broader range of problems, such as constrained minimax optimization problems, it is necessary to consider
\mathbf{L}_{p}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.
In this case,
\partial\mathbf{L}_{p}
is maximal monotone if
\mathbf{L}_{p}
is closed and proper
[
86
, Corollary 37.5.2]
, and if that holds,
z_{\star}
is a solution of (
1
) if and only if
0\in\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{\star})
.
(We refer the readers to Appendix
F
for a more detailed discussion on properness.)
In our setup with
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}_{p}+\mathbf{L}_{s}
, we assume closedness and properness of
\mathbf{L}_{p}
and rewrite (
1
) into (
5
), using the fact that
\partial_{\pm}=\nabla_{\pm}
for differentiable convex-concave functions.

Of particular interest are problems with
\mathbf{L}(x,y)=f(x)+\langle Ax,y\rangle-g(y)
, with some
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
or, more generally,
\mathbf{L}(x,y)=f(x)+\mathbf{L}_{s}(x,y)-g(y)
, with some
L
-smooth convex-concave
\mathbf{L}_{s}
.
In this case,
\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y)=(\partial f(x),\partial g(y))
, and
{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha\partial_{\pm}\mathbf{L}_{p}}(x,y)=(\mathrm{Prox}_{\alpha f}(x),\mathrm{Prox}_{\alpha g}(y))
, where

\displaystyle\mathrm{Prox}_{\alpha f}(x)=\underset{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\argmin}\left\{\alpha f(x^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}\right\},\,\,\,\mathrm{Prox}_{\alpha g}(y)=\underset{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}{\argmin}\left\{\alpha g(y^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\|y^{\prime}-y\|^{2}\right\}

are the proximal operators.
When
f(x)=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&x\in{\mathcal{X}}\\
+\infty&x\notin{\mathcal{X}}\end{cases}
, the proximal operator becomes the projection operator:
\mathrm{Prox}_{\alpha\delta_{\mathcal{X}}}(x)=\Pi_{{\mathcal{X}}}(x)
for any
\alpha>0
.
Therefore, the constrained problem (
1
) with
{\mathcal{X}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is recovered with
\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y)=\delta_{\mathcal{X}}(x)-\delta_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)
.

Note that
z_{\star}
is a solution of (
5
) if and only if
z_{\star}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(z_{\star}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z_{\star})
.
Therefore, the norm of the
forward-backward residual

{\mathbb{G}}_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}(z-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(z-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z))
serves as a measure for the convergence of algorithms solving (
5
), and it generalizes the operator norm and the fixed-point residual norm.

5.2
Preliminaries: Accelerated proximal splitting algorithm

Consider

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{k}&={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(u_{k})\\
u_{k+1}&=\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})({\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(w_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k})+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k})\end{aligned}

(OHM-DRS)

with
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
.
As we explain below, this is the application of OHM to the Douglas–Rachford splitting (DRS) operator
[
28
,
56
]
defined by
{\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}={\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}+{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}\circ(2{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}-{\mathbb{I}})
.
Here we use the alternative representation of OHM
u_{k+1}=\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{T}}u_{k}
with
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
, which is equivalent to (
4

u_{k}=w_{k+1/2}
.
Given
u_{k}
, letting
w_{k}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(u_{k})
and
v_{k}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}\circ(2{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}-{\mathbb{I}})(u_{k})={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(2w_{k}-u_{k})
, we have
{\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}(u_{k})=u_{k}-w_{k}+v_{k}
.
Therefore, OHM with
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}
is written as

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{B}}}(u_{k})

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(2w_{k}-u_{k})

(14)

\displaystyle u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}u_{0}+(1-\beta_{k})(u_{k}+v_{k}-w_{k})

where
\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}
.
But
2w_{k}-u_{k}=2w_{k}-(w_{k}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k})=w_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k}
, so
v_{k}={\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(w_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k})

By eliminating the second line and replacing

Since
{\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}
is nonexpansive

]

exhibits the accelerated rate

\|u_{k}-{\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}(u_{k})\|^{2}\leq 4\|u_{0}-u_{\star}\|^{2}/(k+1)^{2}

Additionally, this rate directly translates into the accelerated rate with respect to

\displaystyle u_{k}-{\mathbb{T}}_{\textrm{DRS}}(u_{k})=w_{k}-v_{k}=w_{k}-{\mathbb{J}}_{\alpha{\mathbb{A}}}(w_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{B}}w_{k})=\alpha{\mathbb{G}}_{\alpha}(w_{k}).

OHM-DRS
will serve as a conceptual algorithm (not implemented) that our
APG*
(implementable) is designed to
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{2})
-MP approximate, so that
APG*
inherits the accelerated rate of
OHM-DRS
.
(
OHM-DRS
is not prox-grad-type, while
APG*
is.)

5.3
MP property and convergence of APG

We now describe the design of
APG*
.
The forms of
APG*
and

bear a clear resemblance. The second lines are identical.
In the first lines, the resolvent computation

of

Note,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\textrm{find}}&0=z+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}z-\xi_{k}=({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}-\xi_{k})(z),\end{array}

-strongly monotone,

-Lipschitz operator.

Therefore the

approximates the

Throughout the following theorems, we assume that

As

5.1
APG*

With

With the conditions and notations of

5.3
Oracle complexity of
APG*
.

With the conditions and notations of
Theorem

5.1
, the
k
-th inner loop of

in

requires
\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(k\left(\|\xi_{0}-\xi_{\star}\|+1\right)\right)\right)
oracle calls of
{\mathbb{B}}
for

.
This implies that the total computational cost needed to achieve

is

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{C(\xi_{0})}{\epsilon}\left(C_{\mathbb{A}}+C_{\mathbb{B}}\log\frac{C(\xi_{0})}{\epsilon}\right)\right),

where
C_{\mathbb{A}},C_{\mathbb{B}}
are upper bounds on the respective costs of evaluating

and
{\mathbb{B}}
.

We clarify some key differences between
APG*
and the prior work of Diakonikolas
[
25
, Algorithm 3]

First,
APG*
is a more general proximal-gradient-type algorithm, while the prior algorithm of Diakonikolas is a projected-gradient-type algorithm.
Second,
APG*
is an anytime algorithm, as it requires no a priori specification of the terminal accuracy or the total iteration count, while the prior algorithm is not an anytime algorithm, as it requires the terminal accuracy

Third,

\xi_{0},\xi_{\star}

Therefore,
APG*
offers a speedup for constrained minimax problems when the projection represents the dominant cost, i.e., when
C_{\mathbb{A}}\gg C_{\mathbb{B}}
.

The initial point
\xi_{k}
for the inner loop
\textbf{SM-EAG{+}}\left({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}}-\xi_{k},\xi_{k},\epsilon_{k}\right)
of

is a crude choice. However, while a more careful warm starting may improve the iteration complexity by a constant factor, we do not believe it alone can eliminate the

-factor. In our subsequent analysis of

, we induce the MP property by solving the

-th inner loop up to accuracy

. Therefore, unless a mechanism for finding a warm-starting point

\|({\mathbb{I}}+\alpha{\mathbb{B}})(z)-\xi_{k}\|={\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon_{k})=o(1/k)
is proposed (which seems nontrivial), the
\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon))
-factor will persist. Eliminating the logarithmic factor probably requires a new additional insight, and we leave it as a future work.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we identified the novel merging path (MP) property among accelerated minimax algorithms, EAG
[
108
]
, FEG
[
51
]
, and APS
[
98
]
, and the accelerated proximal algorithm OHM
[
36
,
53
,
44
]
. We then used this MP property to design new state-of-the-art algorithms.

We quickly mention that our results straightforwardly extend to the setup where the underlying space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is replaced with an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, since our proofs do not rely on the finite-dimensionality of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular, the point convergence results of
Corollary

3.3
and
Corollary

5.2
hold as strong convergence.

The MP property provides the insight that the four different accelerated algorithms, EAG, FEG, APS, and OHM, actually reduce to one single acceleration mechanism. However, elucidating the fundamental principle behind this single anchoring-based acceleration mechanism still remains an open problem, and further investigating the phenomenon from multiple different viewpoints, as was done for Nesterov’s acceleration, would be an interesting direction of future work.

The presence or applicability of the MP property in other setups is another interesting direction of future work. One question is whether other prior optimization algorithms exhibit the MP property. In our analysis, we found that anchoring induces the MP property, so algorithms using similar regularizations, such as the recursive regularization
[
4
]
or the Katyusha momentum
[
3
]
, may be approximating some other conceptual algorithm. Another question is whether the MP property can be used to design new algorithms in other setups. The strategy of designing efficient algorithms by approximating a conceptual algorithm, in the MP sense, may be widely applicable in setups beyond non-stochastic minimax optimization.

A.1
Classical minimax optimization algorithms

Simultaneous gradient descent-ascent
for smooth minimax optimization is defined by

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x}\mathbf{L}(x_{k},y_{k})

\displaystyle y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\alpha\nabla_{y}\mathbf{L}(x_{k},y_{k})

where
\alpha>0

Writing

\displaystyle z_{k+1}=z_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k}).

The

\displaystyle z_{k+1/2}

\displaystyle=z_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k}),

\displaystyle z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=z_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k+1/2}).

The

\displaystyle z_{k+1}=z_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k})-\alpha\left(\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k})-\nabla_{\pm}\mathbf{L}(z_{k-1})\right),

where

&\quad\quad+\left(\alpha(k+1)^{2}-2\tau_{2}\right)\langle{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\rangle+(\tau_{2}-p_{k+1})\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(46)

We take

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=\frac{\alpha(k+1)\left((1+2\alpha^{2}L^{2})k-\alpha L(1-2\alpha L)\right)}{3},\quad\tau_{2}=\frac{\tau_{1}}{4\alpha^{2}L^{2}},

which are positive for
\alpha L
sufficiently small (by Lemma
E.1
), and plug in
\epsilon=\frac{\alpha^{2}L\left(1-\alpha L-\alpha^{2}L^{2}-\alpha^{3}L^{3}\right)}{2(1+\alpha L)}
and

\displaystyle p_{k}=\frac{\alpha(k+1)(k+\alpha L(k-1))}{2(1+\alpha L)},\quad p_{k+1}=\frac{\alpha(k+2)(k+1+\alpha Lk)}{2(1+\alpha L)}

to obtain

\displaystyle p_{k}-\tau_{1}=\frac{\alpha(k+1)\left((1+r-4r^{2}-4r^{3})k-r(1+2r+4r^{2})\right)}{6(1+r)}

(47)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{1}}{2}-\alpha^{2}L^{2}\tau_{2}-\alpha^{2}L^{2}p_{k+1}-\epsilon(k+1)^{2}=\frac{\alpha\left(T^{(2)}(r)k^{2}+T^{(1)}(r)k+T^{(0)}(r)\right)}{12(1+r)}

where
r=\alpha L
and
T^{(\ell)}(r)
,
\ell=0,1,2
, are polynomials in
r
defined by

\displaystyle T^{(0)}(r)

\displaystyle=-r(7+5r-8r^{2}-6r^{3})

\displaystyle T^{(1)}(r)

\displaystyle=1-12r-3r^{2}+4r^{3}+12r^{4}

\displaystyle T^{(2)}(r)

\displaystyle=1-5r+2r^{2}+2r^{3}+6r^{4}.

Therefore, by Lemma
E.1
,
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and the coefficients of the first two norm sqaure terms in (
46
) are positive provided that
r
is sufficiently small, and thus we obtain

\displaystyle V_{k}-V_{k+1}-\epsilon(k+1)^{2}\|{\mathbb{B}}v_{k}-{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq(p_{k}-\tau_{1})\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}\|^{2}+\left(2\tau_{1}-\alpha k(k+1)\right)\langle{\mathbb{B}}z_{k},{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\rangle+(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})\|{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(\alpha(k+1)^{2}-2\tau_{2}\right)\langle{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\rangle+(\tau_{2}-p_{k+1})\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{M}_{k}\mathbf{S}_{k}\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\intercal}\right),

where
\mathbf{M}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}&{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}&{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}p_{k}-\tau_{1}&\tau_{1}-\frac{\alpha}{2}k(k+1)&0\\
\tau_{1}-\frac{\alpha}{2}k(k+1)&\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}&\frac{\alpha}{2}(k+1)^{2}-\tau_{2}\\
0&\frac{\alpha}{2}(k+1)^{2}-\tau_{2}&\tau_{2}-p_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}s_{11}&s_{12}&0\\
s_{12}&s_{22}&s_{23}\\
0&s_{23}&s_{33}\end{bmatrix}.

We have seen in (
47
) that
s_{11}=p_{k}-\tau_{1}>0
if
r
is small.
Next, we have

\displaystyle t_{22}

\displaystyle=s_{22}-\frac{s_{12}^{2}}{s_{11}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-2r)(k+1)\left((1+3r+2r^{2})k-r(1-2r)\right)\left((1-4r^{2})k-r(1+4r)\right)}{12Lr\left((1+r-4r^{2}-4r^{3})k-r(1+2r+4r^{2})\right)}

and

\displaystyle t_{33}

\displaystyle=s_{33}-\frac{s_{23}^{2}}{t_{22}}=\frac{\alpha r\left((1-8r-4r^{2}-4r^{3})k-2r(5-2r+2r^{2})\right)}{2(1+r)(1-2r)\left((1+3r+2r^{2})k-r(1-2r)\right)},

and as Lemma
E.1
shows that these quantities are positive for small
r
, we conclude that

\displaystyle V_{k}-V_{k+1}-\epsilon(k+1)^{2}\|{\mathbb{B}}v_{k}-{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathbf{M}_{k}\mathbf{S}_{k}\mathbf{M}_{k}^{\intercal}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(s_{11}\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k}\|^{2}+2s_{12}\langle{\mathbb{B}}z_{k},{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{s_{12}^{2}}{s_{11}}\|{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(t_{22}\|{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1}\|^{2}+2s_{23}\langle{\mathbb{B}}v_{k+1},{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{s_{23}^{2}}{t_{22}}\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right)+t_{33}\|{\mathbb{B}}z_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq 0.

∎

Appendix F
Regularity of convex and convex-concave functions

Consider an extended real-valued convex function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.
We say
f
is
proper
if
f(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f(x)<+\infty
for at least one
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The
closure

f
, written as
\mathrm{cl}\,f
, is defined as the constant function
\mathrm{cl}\,f\equiv-\infty
if
f(x)=-\infty
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and otherwise

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}\,f(x):=\liminf_{x^{\prime}\to x}f(x^{\prime}).

We say
f
is
closed
if
\mathrm{cl}\,f=f
(when
f
is proper, this is equivalent to lower semicontinuity).
If
f
is
closed, convex, and proper (CCP)
, its convex subdifferential operator
\partial f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\displaystyle\partial f(x)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,f(x^{\prime})\geq f(x)+\langle v,x^{\prime}-x\rangle,~\forall\,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

is maximal monotone
[
86
, Corollary 31.5.2]
.
For an extended real-valued concave function
g
, we define
\mathrm{cl}\,g=-\mathrm{cl}\,(-g)
and say it is proper (
resp.
closed) if
-g
is proper (
resp.
closed) as a convex function.

Let
{\mathbf{K}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
be an extended real-valued convex-concave function.
Define

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)<+\infty,~\forall\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)>-\infty,~\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

\displaystyle\,\,\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbf{K}}=\mathrm{dom}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}\times\mathrm{dom}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}.

We say
{\mathbf{K}}
is
proper
if
\mathrm{dom}\,{\mathbf{K}}\neq\emptyset
.
Define
\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
as the function obtained by taking the convex closure of
{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
with respect to
x
for each fixed
y
, and define
\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
analogously.
Then both
\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}
and
\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}
are convex-concave
[
86
, Corollary 33.1.1]
.
Respectively define the
lower
and
upper closures
of
{\mathbf{K}}
by

\displaystyle\underline{{\mathbf{K}}}=\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}},\quad\overline{{\mathbf{K}}}=\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}},

which satisfy
\underline{{\mathbf{K}}}\leq\overline{{\mathbf{K}}}
.
We say
{\mathbf{K}}
is
closed
if
\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}=\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}
and
\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}=\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}
.

The
saddle subdifferential operator

\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)=\{(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,v\in\partial_{x}{\mathbf{K}}(x,y),w\in\partial_{y}(-{\mathbf{K}})(x,y)\},

where
\partial_{x}{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
denotes the convex subdifferential of
{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
as a convex function of
x
(for each fixed
y
), and
\partial_{y}(-{\mathbf{K}})(x,y)
denotes the convex subdifferential of
-{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)
as a convex function of
y
(for each fixed
x
).
If
{\mathbf{K}}
is
closed, convex-concave, and proper (CCCP)
, then
\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}
is maximal monotone
[
86
, Corollary 37.5.2]
and
(x_{\star},y_{\star})
is a minimax solution to

\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\textrm{minimize}}\,\,\underset{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}{\textrm{maximize}}\,\,\,\,{\mathbf{K}}(x,y)

if and only if
0\in\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}(x_{\star},y_{\star})
.
Given
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
{\mathbf{K}}
is differentiable at
z
if and only if
\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}(z)
is singleton, and if that is the case,
\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}(z)=\nabla_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}(z)

[
86
, Theorem 35.8]
.

We say two convex-concave functions
{\mathbf{K}},{\mathbf{L}}
are
equivalent
if
\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{K}}=\mathrm{cl}_{x}\,{\mathbf{L}}
and
\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{K}}=\mathrm{cl}_{y}\,{\mathbf{L}}
.
If
{\mathbf{K}},{\mathbf{L}}
are equivalent, then they share the set of minimax solutions,
the function values at the solutions (if any) are equal, and
\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{K}}=\partial_{\pm}{\mathbf{L}}

[
86
, Theorem 36.4, Corollary 37.4.1]
.
If
{\mathbf{K}}
is a closed convex-concave function, then the equivalence class of closed convex-concave functions containing
{\mathbf{K}}
is precisely characterized
[
86
, Theorem 34.2]
by

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathbf{L}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}\,|\,{\mathbf{L}}\text{ is closed, convex-concave, and }\underline{{\mathbf{K}}}\leq{\mathbf{L}}\leq\overline{{\mathbf{K}}}\right\}.

When
f
and
g
are respectively CCP functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define

\displaystyle f(x)-g(y)=\begin{cases}f(x)-g(y)&x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,g\\
+\infty&x\notin\mathrm{dom}\,f,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,g\\
-\infty&x\in\mathrm{dom}\,f,y\notin\mathrm{dom}\,g\end{cases}

(48)

but when
x\notin\mathrm{dom}\,f,y\notin\mathrm{dom}\,g
, it seems unclear how to define
f(x)-g(y)=+\infty-\infty
.
However, it turns out that one can extend (
48
) to a CCCP function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and any such extensions are equivalent to one another
[
86
, Theorem 34.4]
.
In particular, using the convention
+\infty-\infty=-\infty
, one gets the lower closure (minimal element) of the equivalence class containing the equivalent CCCP extensions of (
48
).
Alternatively, using the convention
+\infty-\infty=+\infty
, one gets the upper closure (maximal element) of the equivalence class.
In this regard, we can safely deal with the case
\mathbf{L}_{p}(x,y)=f(x)-g(y)
(where the equality in fact means
\mathbf{L}_{p}
belongs to the equivalence class of CCCP extensions of (
48
)) without pathology or ambiguities.

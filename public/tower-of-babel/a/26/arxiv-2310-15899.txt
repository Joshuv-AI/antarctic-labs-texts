Abstract
. A vertex coloring of a graph G G is called a 2-distance coloring if any two vertices at a distance at most 2 2 from each other receive different colors. Suppose that G G is a planar graph with a maximum degree at most 5 5 . We prove that G G admits a 2 2 -distance 16 16 coloring, which improves the result given by Hou et al. (Graphs and Combinatorics 39:20, 2023).



1.
Introduction

We consider only finite simple graphs throughout this paper, and refer to
[
10
]
for terminology and notation not defined here. Let
G
be a graph, we use
V(G),E(G),F(G),
and
\Delta(G)
to denote the vertex, edge and face set, and the maximum degree of
G
, respectively. If there is no confusion in the context, we abbreviate
\Delta(G)
to
\Delta
.
A 2-distance coloring is a proper vertex coloring where two vertices that are adjacent or have a common neighbour receive different colors, and the smallest number of colors for which
G
admits a 2-distance coloring is known as the 2-distance chromatic number
\chi_{2}(G)
of
G
.
In
[
4
]
, Cranston has provided a comprehensive survey on 2-distance coloring and related coloring concepts.

In 1977, Wegner
[
9
]
posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For every planar graph
G
,
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 7
if
\Delta=3
,
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+5
if
4\leq\Delta\leq 7
, and
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\big\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\big\rfloor+1
if
\Delta\geq 8
,

Thomassen
[
8
]
(independently by Hartke et al.
[
5
]
) proved the conjecture for planar graphs with
\Delta=3
, by showing that every cubic planar graph is 2-distance colorable with
7
colors. For general planar graphs, van den Heuvel and McGuinness
[
6
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 2\Delta+25
, while the bound
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\big\lceil\frac{5\Delta}{3}\big\rceil+78
was proved by Molloy and Salavatipour
[
7
]
.

The conjecture remains open, even for small
\Delta
. Zhu
[
11
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 13
for planar graph with
\Delta=4
. On the other hand, Zhu and Bu
[
12
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 20
for planar graph with maximum degree
\Delta\leq 5
. This bound was later reduced to
19
by Chen et al.
[
3
]
, and to 18 by Hou et al.
[
2
]
. Recently, Aoki
[
1
]
and Zou et al.
[
13
]
(independently) in unpublished papers have proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 17
. In this paper, we further improve the current bound by reducing it by one.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
G
is a planar graph with maximum degree at most
5
, then
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 16
.

Given a planar graph
G
, we denote by
\ell(f)
the length of a face
f
and by
d(v)
the degree of a vertex
v
.
A
k
-vertex is a vertex of degree
k
. A
k^{-}
-vertex is a vertex of degree at most
k
while a
k^{+}
-vertex is a vertex of degree at least
k
. A
k
(
k^{-}
or
k^{+}
)-face is defined similarly. A vertex
u\in N(v)
is called
k
-neighbour (resp.
k^{-}
-neighbour,
k^{+}
-neighbour) of
v
if
d(u)=k
(resp.
d(u)\leq k
,
d(u)\geq k
).
For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we use
n_{i}(v)
to denote the number of
i
-vertices adjacent to
v
. We denote by
d(u,v)
the distance between
u
and
v
for any pair
u,v\in V(G)
. Also, we set
N_{i}(v)=\{u\in V(G)\ |\ 1\leq d(u,v)\leq i\}
for
i\geq 1
, so
N_{1}(v)=N(v)
and let
d_{2}(v);=|N_{2}(v)|
.

For
v\in V(G)
, we use
m_{k}(v)
to denote the number of
k
-faces incident with
v
.
An
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
-face is a
k
-face with vertices of degrees
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}
.
Two faces
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are called adjacent if they share a common edge.

1.2

2.1.
The Structure of Minimum Counterexample

Let
G
be a minimal counterexample to Theorem
1.2
such that
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
is minimum. So,
G
does not admit any
2
-distance
16
-coloring, but
any planar graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from
G
with smaller
|V(G^{\prime})|+|E(G^{\prime})|
admits a 2-distance 16-coloring.
Obviously,
G
is a connected graph.

We call a graph
H

proper
with respect to
G
if
H
is obtained from
G
by deleting some edges or vertices and adding some edges, ensuring that for every pair of vertices
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
V(G)\cap V(H)
having distance at most 2 in
G
, they also have distance at most
2
in
H
.
If
f
is a 2-distance coloring of such a graph
H
, then
f
can be extended to the whole graph
G
, provided that each of the remaining uncolored vertices has an available color.

First, we present some structural results and forbidden configurations for the graph
G
. The proofs of the following lemmas are quite similar: Given a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we construct a graph
H
consisting of the vertices
V(G-v)
such that
H
is proper with respect to
G
. When we have a
2
-distance
16
-coloring of
H
, we show that such a coloring can always be extended to the whole graph
G
by assigning a color to the removed vertex
v
when
d_{2}(v)\leq 15
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For the graph
G
, we have
\delta(G)\geq 3
.

Proof.

Assume that
v
is a
1
-vertex in
G
. By minimality, the graph
G-v
has a 2-distance coloring
f
with
16
colors. Since
v
has an available color, the coloring
f
can be extended to the whole graph
G
, a contradiction.

We next assume that
v
is a
2
-vertex in
G
. Denote by
x
and
y
the neighbours of
v
, and let
G^{\prime}=G-v+xy
. By minimality, the graph
G^{\prime}
has a 2-distance coloring
f
with
16
colors. Notice that every vertex in
V(G)
, except
v
, is colored by the same color appears on the same vertex in
G^{\prime}
. Thus, it suffices to color only
v
. Since
d_{2}(v)\leq 10
, i.e.,
v
has at most
10
forbidden colors, we can color
v
with an available color, a contradiction. Hence,
G
has no
1
-vertex and
2
-vertex.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

A
3
-vertex cannot be adjacent to any
4^{-}
-vertex.

Proof.

Let

-vertex with

A note on the convergence of the Bayesian entropy estimator for exchangeable partitions Thanks: Corresponding author: Servet Martinez, smartine@dim.uchile.cl
Servet Martinez 1

Abstract
. We show that when the proportions of a countable set of species are organized as an exchangeable partition of the unit interval and we take a sample on it, then the Bayesian posterior entropy converges a.s. and in L 1 L^{1} to the entropy of the species when the sample size diverges to infinity.



Running title
: Posterior entropy estimator for exchangeable partitions.

AMS Classification Number:
62B10, 94A17

Keywords:
Entropy, Bayesian posterior distribution,
exchangeable partitions, Poisson-Dirichlet Process.

ANID Basal PIA program FB210005 Center for Mathematical Modeling.

1.
Introduction

The study of the diversity of abundance of species when the number of species and
their abundance are unknown has been approached by using several models, many of
which take the Poisson Dirichlet process (PDP)
as the prior distribution of the abundance of species. For instance this was done
in
[
4
]
, and in
[
7
]
and
[
18
]
,
in ecology and machine learning respectively.
For this prior, the study of the diversity of abundance of species was studied in

[
6
]
by using the Shannon entropy as an index of diversity.
The estimation of the entropy when data on the observation of
species is collected was studied in
[
3
]
, by considering
the posterior Bayesian entropy and the plug-in estimator of the entropy.
Here we study these estimators in the more general framework of exchangeable partitions
and we sharpen the convergence description of the posterior Bayesian entropy by
using a martingale characterization.

To be more precise let
H(\cdot)
be the Shannon entropy of the
random masses of an exchangeable partition of the unit interval. Take
a sample of size
n
by using i.i.d. random variables in
[0,1]
and denote
the number of classes containing the first
n
observations by
k_{n}
and
the total number of individuals in the
i-
th class by
\pi^{n}(i)
.
Let
{\widehat{H}}_{n}
be the plug-in entropy estimator

{\widehat{H}}_{n}(\cdot)=-\sum_{i=1}^{k^{n}}(\pi^{n}(i)/n)\log(\pi^{n}(i)/n)
and let

{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\cdot)=-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}q^{n}_{i}\log q^{n}_{i}

be the posterior Bayesian entropy which is the entropy of the classes
distributed as
q^{n}=(q^{n}(j):j\in{\mathbb{N}})\sim P(\cdot|\pi^{n}(1),..,\pi^{n}(k^{n}))
,
the posterior distribution of the exchangeable partition given the sample.

In
[
2
]
, the entropy and its estimators were studied when the prior is a PDP
distribution. The mean of the entropy was given in formula (12),
and an explicit formula for the posterior Bayesian entropy
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
was found
in terms of the digamma function, based upon the characterization of the posterior distribution

P(\cdot|\pi^{n}(1),..,\pi^{n}(k^{n}))
given in Corollary
20
in
[
14
]
.
Moreover, in Theorem
3
in
[
3
]
,
it was shown that
|{\widehat{H}}_{n}-{\mathcal{H}}_{n}|\to 0
in probability as
n\to\infty
.
This uses the behavior of the digamma function close to the log for big values
and that
k^{n}/n\to 0
in probability (the latter following from Section
3
and
Proposition
2
in
[
8
]
). In formula
(13)
in
[
3
]
,
is was also shown that the entropy has finite second moment.

In this work we enlarge the study of the entropy to the case
when the prior is an exchangeable random partition
and show the consistency of the entropy estimators.
In Proposition
1
, we show that when the exchangeable partition has finite mean entropy,
one has

(1)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\cdot)=H(\cdot)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\widehat{H}}_{n}(\cdot)\hbox{ a.s. and in }L^{1}.

Moreover, if the second moment of the entropy is finite, the above convergence holds in
L^{2}
and
if the entropy has finite
p-
th moment for some
p>1
, the first equality holds in
L^{p}
.
To prove the first equality in (
1
), we show and use that the posterior
Bayesian entropy is an integrable martingale when the sample size varies in
{\mathbb{N}}
. The convergence
of the plug-in estimator follows from Corollary
1
in
[
1
]
.
When the second moment is finite, the martingale property gives an increasing process
with the sample size, see Remark
3
.

The relation (
1
) also holds when one replaces the entropy
H
by some other function

G:{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}\to{\mathbb{R}}
which is symmetric in its arguments and satisfies the moment conditions.
While the result holds for the Bayesian posterior without any other requirements, for
the plug-in estimator, the result needs
G
to be an additive function that satisfies additional conditions.

In Section
3
we state and show Proposition
1
, which is our main result.
In Section
2
we introduce some of the main notions for exchangeable partitions.
We closely follow the presentation in
[
5
]
, but with some minor variation in the way
the concepts are introduced which is more convenient for presenting and showing the result.
Finally, we discuss this result in the context of the PDP process in Section
4
.

2.
Exchangeable partitions

A random partition
\Xi=\{\xi(i)\}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
is called exchangeable if its law is invariant under
the class of permutations
\{\varphi\}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
that are the identity after some arbitrary finite
integer. Then,
\varphi(\Xi)\sim\Xi

\;\forall\sigma
, where

\varphi(\Xi)=\{\varphi^{-1}(\Xi(i))\}
. In this section we
give some of the main notions allowing to describe this class of partitions, and we follow
closely Section 2.3 in
[
5
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}=\{s=(s(i):i\in{\mathbb{N}}):s(i\!+\!1)\geq s(i)\!\geq\!0\;\forall i\in{\mathbb{N}},\,\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)=1\}
.
As done in
[
5
]
Section 2.1.2,
it is useful to associate to each
s\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
a collection of disjoint open intervals

{\mathcal{J}}^{s}=(J^{s}(i):i\in{\mathbb{N}})
of
[0,1]
such that the sequence
(|J^{s}(i)|^{\downarrow})
of the
interval lengths ranked in a decreasing way, is equal to
s
. Let
P
be a probability law on
{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
.
The mean expected value with respect to
P
is denoted by
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}=(X_{n}:n\geq 1)
be a sequence of i.i.d. Uniform r.v’s in
[0,1]
independent of
s\sim P
.
The law of
{\mathcal{X}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{R}}
, it is invariant under
the set of permutations
\{\varphi\}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
that satisfy
\varphi(i)=i
for some
i>n(\varphi)
.
So, when writing
\varphi({\mathcal{X}})=(X_{\varphi(n)}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
, one has

\varphi(X)\sim{\mathcal{R}}
. We will consider the probability law
{\mathbb{P}}=P\otimes{\mathcal{R}}
of the pair
(s,{\mathcal{X}})
,
and the conditional distribution given
s
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)
.

Define a random sequence
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}={\mathcal{X}}^{*}(s,{\mathcal{X}})=(X^{*}_{n}:n\geq 1)
taking values in
{\mathbb{N}}
and
obeying the recursion:

X^{*}_{1}=1
and if, up to
n
, the sequence
X_{1},...,X_{n}
have visited
k^{n}

different intervals in
{\mathcal{J}}^{s}
, then

\displaystyle X^{*}_{n+1}\!=\!j,k^{n+1}\!=\!k^{n}\hbox{ if for some }j\!\in\!\{1,..,k^{n}\},X_{n+1}\hbox{ is in the same interval as }X^{*}_{j};

\displaystyle X^{*}_{n+1}\!=\!k^{n}\!\!+\!\!1,k^{n+1}\!=\!k^{n}\!\!+\!\!1\hbox{ if }X_{n+1}\hbox{ is in an interval not being visited before }n.

Most of the notions will only depend on the probability distribution of the pair
(s,{\mathcal{X}}^{*})
.

Let
\xi^{n}=(\xi^{n}(1),..,\xi^{n}(k^{n}))
be the partition of
\{1,..,n\}

given by
\xi^{n}(i)=\{j\in\{1,..,n\}:X^{*}_{j}=i\}
. The sequence of partition
(\xi^{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})

is compatible (that is
\xi^{n+1}|_{\{1,..,n\}}=\xi_{n}\;\forall n
)
and it defines an exchangeable random partition
\Xi=(\xi(i):i\in{\mathbb{N}})
of
{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
\pi^{n}(j)=\#\xi^{n}(j)
be the number
of elements of
\xi^{n}(j)
. Define the vector
\pi^{n}=(\pi^{n}(1),..,\pi^{n}(k^{n}))

ranked by the order of the indexes that firstly visit the new intervals. One has
k_{1}=1
,

\pi^{1}=(1)
and
\pi^{n+1}\in\{\pi^{n}\!+\delta_{k^{n}}(j):j\in\{1,...,k^{n}\}\}\cup\{(\pi^{n},1)\}

where
\delta_{k}(i)
is the vector of dimension
k
with all
0^{\prime}
s except by a
1
in position
j
.
Define
\Pi=(\pi^{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
, we have
\pi=\Pi(s,{\mathcal{X}})
. A recurrence argument
shows that the passage from
\pi^{n}
to
\pi^{n+1}

determines the value of
X^{*}_{n}
, then the random elements
\Pi
,
{\mathcal{X}}^{*}
and

\Xi
, mutually determine each other.

There exists the asymptotic frequencies

\widehat{\pi}(i)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\#(\xi^{n}(i)\cap\{1,..,n\})/n

\,\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)
a.s.
and
\widehat{\pi}=(\widehat{\pi}(i):i\in{\mathbb{N}})
is a size-biased reordering of
s
,
see Proposition
2.8
in
[
5
]
. In a reciprocal way, in Kingman theory
it is shown that every random exchangeable
partition
\Xi
posseses asymptotic frequencies a.s. given by a vector of frequencies
\widehat{\pi}
,
see
[
10
]
and also
[
13
]
. Then, the
decreasing ranked sequence
\widehat{\pi}^{\downarrow}
is distributed with
some law
P
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
. Hence, since
\Xi
and
\Pi
determine one another,
the law of an exchangeable random partition
can be set in the form
\mathcal{P}(\Pi|s)dP(s)
, see Theorem
2.1
in
[
5
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=(X_{1},..,X_{n})
and
\Pi_{n}=(\pi^{1},..,\pi^{n})
. We have
\Pi_{n}=\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})
.
Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
be the joint probability of
(s,\Pi_{n})
, then

d{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(\Pi_{n}|s)dP(s)
.
Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be the marginal distribution of
\Pi
, so

d{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(ds|\Pi_{n}){\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})
, where

\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\Pi_{n})
is the conditional distribution given
\Pi_{n}
.
We claim that,

(2)

\mathcal{P}(ds,X^{*}_{n+1}|\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(ds,X^{*}_{n+1}|\pi^{n}).

Let us show it. The set of values taking by the variable
\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})
is denoted
by
{\mathfrak{N}}_{n}
. For a value
\underline{\pi}^{n}
taken by
\pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})
, we set

\mathfrak{N}_{n}(\underline{\pi}^{n})=\{\Pi_{n}=(\pi_{n},..,\pi^{n})\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}:\pi_{n}=\underline{\pi}_{n}\}
and so

\{\pi^{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\pi}^{n}\}=\bigcup_{\underline{\Pi}_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}(\underline{\pi}^{n})}\{\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\Pi}_{n}\}
.
Fix some value
{\Pi}^{*}_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}(\underline{\pi}^{n})
.
For every
\underline{\Pi}_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}(\underline{\pi}^{n})
one can find a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,..,n\}

that satisfies
\{\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\Pi}_{n}\}=\{\Pi_{n}(s,\sigma({\mathcal{X}}_{n}))={\Pi}^{*}_{n}\}
.
The equality

{\mathbb{P}}(ds,\!X^{*}_{n+1},\!\Pi_{n}(\!s,\!{\mathcal{X}}_{n})\!\!=\!\underline{\Pi}_{n})\!\!=\!{\mathbb{P}}(ds,\!X^{*}_{n+1},\!\Pi_{n}(\!s,\!\sigma(\!{\mathcal{X}}_{n}))\!\!=\!{\Pi}^{*}_{n})\!\!=\!{\mathbb{P}}(ds,\!X^{*}_{n+1},\!\Pi_{n}(\!s,\!{\mathcal{X}}_{n})\!\!=\!{\Pi}^{*}_{n}),

gives

{\mathbb{P}}(ds,X^{*}_{n+1}|\pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\pi}_{n})={\mathbb{P}}(ds,X^{*}_{n+1}|\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})={\Pi}^{*}_{n})
,
so (
2
) follows.

Let us use (
2
) to show that
the marginal distribution
{\mathcal{Q}}
has the following Markovian structure,

(3)

{\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}Q(\pi^{k};\pi^{k+1})\hbox{ with }Q(\pi^{k};\pi^{k+1})=\mathcal{P}(\pi^{k+1}|\pi^{k}).

Since
\pi^{n+1}=\pi^{n+1}(\pi^{n},X^{*}_{n+1})
, we use (
2
) to get

{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n+1})\!=\!{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(\Pi^{n+1}|\Pi_{n})\!=\!{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(\pi^{n+1}|\Pi_{n})={\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(\pi^{n+1}|\pi^{n}).

Then,
{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n+1}|\Pi_{n})=Q(\pi^{n};\pi^{n+1})
. Since
{\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})={\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n})\;\forall n
and

{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{1}=(1))=1
, we get (
3
).

From (
2
) we get

d{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(ds|\Pi_{n}){\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(ds|\pi^{n}){\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})
.
Then, every measurable nonnegative
g_{n}:{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}\times{\mathfrak{N}}_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfies

(4)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}\!\sum\limits_{\Pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}}\!\!\!g_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(\Pi_{n}|s)dP(s)=\!\int\!\!g_{n}d{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\Pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}}\int\limits_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}\!\!g_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(ds|\Pi_{n}){\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum\limits_{\Pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}}\int\limits_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}\!\!g_{n}(s,\Pi_{n})\mathcal{P}(ds|\pi^{n}){\mathcal{Q}}(\Pi_{n}).

The first two equalities are found in Lemma
1
in
[
9
]
.

3.
Main Result

The Shannon entropy of a distribution

\rho=(\rho_{i}:i\in I)
on a countable set
I
is given by

H(\rho)=-\sum_{i\in I}\rho_{i}\log(\rho_{i})
. So, the entropy of
s\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
is

H(s)=-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)\log(s(i))
and the mean entropy is

{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)={\mathbf{E}}_{P}(-\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)\log(s(i)))
. We will assume that

{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)
is finite.

The structural distribution of the random partition
is the law of
\widehat{\pi}(1)
, we call it
F
.
In relation (
25
) in
[
15
]
it is shown that every Borel function
g:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
satisfies
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}g(s(i))\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{g(x)}{x}dx
.
So,
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)=-\int_{0}^{1}\log(x)dF(x)
and

{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)\!<\!\infty
is equivalent to
\int_{0}^{1}{\log}(x)dF(x)\!>\!-\infty
.

The Bayes posterior mean and the plug-in estimator of the entropy at step
n
are, respectively,
given by
{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(H)
and
H(\pi^{n}/n)
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}=\{s=(s(i):i\!\in\!{\mathbb{N}}):s(i)\!\geq\!0\;\forall i\!\in\!{\mathbb{N}},\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}s(i)=1\}
.
A function
G:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
s\to{\mathbb{R}}
, is symmetric (in its arguments) if

G(s)=G(s^{\prime})
where
s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
is the sequence of the components
(s(i))
ranked in a decreasing way.
So
G:{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and its integrability properties refer to the probability

P
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}
.

We state our main result for both, the mean posterior and the plug-in
entropy estimators. But the proof for the plug-in estimator follows directly from
[
1
]
.

Proposition 1
.

Assume
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)<\infty
. Then,

(5)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(H)=H=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}H(\pi^{n}/n)\;\;{\mathbb{P}}\!-\!\hbox{a.s. and in }L^{1}({\mathbb{P}}).

If
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H^{p})<\infty
, then the first limit holds in
L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})
and if

{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H^{2})<\infty
then the second limit holds in
L^{2}
.

Moreover, when
G:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is symmetric and belongs to
L^{1}(P)
,
then

(6)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(G)=G\;\;{\mathbb{P}}\!-\!\hbox{a.s. and in }L^{1}({\mathbb{P}}),

and if
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(G^{p})<\infty
for some
p>1
, then the limit holds in
L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})
.

Proof.

Let us prove (
6
). From hypothesis,

\infty>{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(|G|)={\mathbf{E}}_{\mathbb{P}}(|G|)
.
Fix some value
\underline{\Pi}_{n}=(\underline{\pi}^{j}:j=1,..,n)\in\mathfrak{N}_{n}
and
take
g_{n}(s,\underline{\Pi}_{n})=G(s){\mathbf{1}}(\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\Pi}_{n})
in formula (
4
). Then,

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}G(s)\mathcal{P}(\underline{\Pi}_{n}|s)P(ds)={\mathcal{Q}}(\underline{\Pi}_{n})\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}G(s)\mathcal{P}(ds|\underline{\pi}^{n})={\mathcal{Q}}(\underline{\Pi}_{n}){\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(G).

Since

\int_{{\mathcal{S}}^{\downarrow}}G(s)\mathcal{P}(\underline{\Pi}_{n}|s)P(ds)=\int_{\{\Pi_{n}=\underline{\Pi}_{n}\}}\!G\,d{\mathbb{P}}\;\hbox{ and }\;{\mathcal{Q}}(\underline{\Pi}_{n})={\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=\underline{\Pi}_{n}),

we get

(7)

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n})}(G)=\frac{1}{{\mathbb{P}}(\Pi_{n}=\underline{\Pi}_{n})}\int_{\{\Pi_{n}=\underline{\Pi}_{n}\}}Gd{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{E}}(G|\sigma(\Pi_{n}))(\underline{\Pi}).

Here
\sigma(\Pi_{n})
is the
\sigma-
field generated by
\Pi
.
Then,
\left({\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi_{n})}(G):n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right)
is an
integrable
{\mathbb{P}}-
martingale with respect to the filtration of
\sigma-
fields
(\sigma(\Pi_{n}):n\!\in\!{\mathbb{N}})
.
The limit
\sigma-
field is
\sigma(\Pi)
(
{\mathbb{P}}-
completed).
We have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi_{n})}(G)=G

\,{\mathbb{P}}-
a.s.
because the
\sigma(\Pi)-
measurable sequence
\widehat{\pi}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\pi^{n}/n

is a size-biased reordering of
s

\;\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)
a.s.
and the symmetry of
G
gives
G(\widehat{\pi})=G(s)

\;\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)
a.s. Then, the
{\mathbb{P}}-
a.s.
convergence in (
6
) is satisfied.

The martingale theorem for integrable martingales
(for instance Proposition
II\!-\!2\!-\!11
in
[
12
]
) gives the

L^{1}({\mathbb{P}})
convergence in (
6
) and when
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(G^{p})<\infty
for some
p>1
, the

L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})
convergence in (
6
) follows from Proposition
II\!-\!2\!-\!11
in
[
12
]
.
So, the first equality in (
5
) is satisfied and under
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H^{p})<\infty
the assertion
on the
L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})
convergence holds.

Since
{\mathbf{E}}_{P}(H)<\infty
one has
H(s)<\infty

\,P
-a.s. So, from Corollary
1

in
[
1
]
we get that
P
-a.s.,
H(\pi^{n}/n)\to H(s)

\;\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)
-a.s. Then

H(\pi^{n}/n)\to H(s)

\;{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s.
From Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
in
[
1
]
we get that
P
-a.s. for all

\epsilon\in(0,1)
there is a constant
K(\epsilon)
such that

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s)}((H(\pi^{n}/n)-H(s))^{2})\leq 2K(\epsilon)n^{-\epsilon}
, so the bound does not depends on
s
.
(For a sharper bound see Remark
(iv)
in page
[
1
]
p.168).
Then,
{\mathbf{E}}_{{\mathbb{P}}}((H(\pi^{n}/n)-H(s))^{2})\leq 2Kn^{-\epsilon}
and in the second equality of (
5
),
the
L^{1}({\mathbb{P}})
and the
L^{2}({\mathbb{P}})
convergence follow.

Remark 1
.

Property (
6
) expresses that the posterior Bayesian property holds for
an integrable symmetric function
G:{\mathcal{S}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. A similar generalization
can be stated for the plug-in estimators, but for a particular class of symmetric integrable functions
G
.
It is required that they have the additive form
G(s)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}f(s(i))
where
f
satisfies
the conditions given in Theorem
1
in
[
1
]

(this Theorem is stated for the class of functions that satisfy
G(s)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{i}(s(i))
,
but together with symmetry this reduces to
f_{i}=f
).
Finally, let us mention that other consistent estimators of the entropy for finite entropy partitions
having an infinite number of classes, can be found in Sections
4
and
5
in
[
17
]
.

Remark 2
.

Let
\underline{\pi}^{n}
be a value taken by
\pi^{n}(s,{\mathcal{X}}_{n})
. Then,

C(\underline{\pi}^{n})=\{\underline{\pi}^{n}+\delta_{k_{n}}(j):j\!=\!1,..,k_{n}\}\cup\{(\underline{\pi}^{n},1)\}

is the set of the values that can take
\pi^{n+1}
when
\pi^{n}=\underline{\pi}^{n}
. Since

E({\mathbf{1}}_{\underline{\pi}^{n+1}}|\Pi_{n})=\mathcal{P}(\underline{\pi}^{n+1}|\pi^{n})=Q(\pi^{n};\underline{\pi}^{n+1}),

the martingale property

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(G)=E\left({\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n+1})}(G)|\Pi_{n}\right)
gives

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(G)=\sum_{\underline{\pi}^{n+1}\in C(\pi^{n})}{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\underline{\pi}^{n+1})}(G)Q(\pi^{n};\underline{\pi}^{n+1}).

Remark 3
.

Assume
H^{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{P}})
. Then the sequence
(A_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
given by

A_{n+1}-A_{n}=E\left(({\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n+1})}(H))^{2}|\pi^{n}\right)-\left({\mathbf{E}}_{\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})}(H)\right)^{2},

is increasing in the sample size, where
E(\cdot|\pi^{n})
is the mean expected value conditioned to
\pi^{n}
.
See Section
VII\!-\!2
in
[
12
]
.
Other monotone functions on the sample size that are based upon a
weighted difference of entropies in successive steps, is found in
[
11
]
.
There, it is studied in detail their relation to the sample sizes when new species appear for
the first time.

4.
Poisson Dirichlet Distribution

An important class of exchangeable partitions is given by the two parameter Poisson Dirichlet
Process introduced in
[
16
]
, with parameters
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\theta>-\alpha
.
It is denoted
PDP(\alpha,\theta)
.
Its size-biased distribution
\pi=(\pi(i):i\in{\mathbb{N}})
can be described by
a sequence of independent random variables

\beta_{j}\sim\,\text{Beta}(1-\alpha,\theta+\alpha j)
. It is

\pi(1)=\beta_{1}
and
\pi(j)=\beta_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}(1-\beta_{i})
for
j\geq 2
.

The marginal distribution
{\mathcal{Q}}
satisfies formula (
3
). In

[
14
]
formula
(42)
(also see relation
(33)
),
and in
[
5
]
Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.6,
it is shown that the transition kernel
Q(\Pi_{n};\Pi_{n+1})=Q(\pi^{n};\pi^{n+1})

is given by the Pitman formula,

(8)

Q(\pi^{n};\pi^{n}\!+\!\delta_{k_{n}}(j))\!=\!\frac{\pi^{n}(j)\!-\!\alpha}{\theta\!+\!n},\,j=1,..,k^{n}\hbox{ and }Q(\pi^{n};(\pi^{n},1))\!=\!\frac{\theta\!+\!\alpha k^{n}}{\theta\!+\!n}.

In Corollary
20
in
[
14
]

it was shown that the posterior distribution

\mathcal{P}(\cdot|\pi^{n})
chooses a random partition
\pi^{\prime}
with a law

(p_{1},\dots,p_{k^{n}},(1-\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}p_{j})\pi^{\prime\prime})
, where

\displaystyle(p_{1},\dots,p_{k^{n}},1\!-\!\sum_{j=1}^{k^{n}}p_{j})

\displaystyle\!\sim\!

\displaystyle\text{Dirichlet}(\pi^{n}(1)\!-\!\alpha,\dots,\pi^{n}(k^{n})\!-\!\alpha,\theta\!+\!\alpha k^{n}),

(9)

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime\prime}\!=\!(\pi^{\prime\prime}(1),\pi^{\prime\prime}(2),\dots)

\displaystyle\!\sim\!

\displaystyle PDP(\alpha,\theta\!+\!\alpha k^{n}),

and they are independent.

In
[
2
]
formula
(10)
and
[
3
]
formula
(12)

it is shown that

{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathbb{P}}(H)=\psi(\theta+1)-\psi(1-\alpha)
,
where
\psi(x)=\Gamma^{\prime}(x)/\Gamma(x)
is the digamma function.
Based upon (
9
), in
[
2
]
formula (
15
) it is shown
that the posterior entropy
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
satisfies,

(\theta+n){\mathcal{H}}_{n}=(\theta+n)\psi(\theta+n+1)-(A_{n}+B_{n})
with

A_{n}=(\theta+\alpha k^{n})\psi(1-\alpha)
and

B_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k^{n}}(\pi^{n}_{i}-\alpha)\psi(\pi^{n}_{i}-\alpha+1)
.

Now, when using (
8
) and the property,
x\psi(x+1)=x\psi(x)+1
for
x>0
,
one proves that the mean expected values of
A_{n+1}
and
B_{n+1}
conditioned to
{\mathcal{X}}_{n}
, satisfy:

\displaystyle E(A_{n+1}|{\mathcal{X}}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta+n+1}{\theta+n}A_{n}-(1-\alpha)\psi(1-\alpha)\frac{\theta+\alpha k^{n}}{\theta+n}\hbox{ and }

\displaystyle E(B_{n+1}|{\mathcal{X}}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta+n+1}{\theta+n}B_{n}+1-\frac{\theta+\alpha k^{n}}{\theta+n}+((1-\alpha)\psi(1-\alpha)+1)\frac{\theta+\alpha k^{n}}{\theta+n}.

Then
(\theta+n+1)E({\mathcal{H}}_{n+1}|{\mathcal{X}}_{n})=(\theta+n+1)\psi(\theta+n+1)-(\theta+n+1)\frac{A_{n}+B_{n}}{\theta+n}
,
and so the martingale property
E({\mathcal{H}}_{n+1}|{\mathcal{X}}_{n})={\mathcal{H}}_{n}
is satisfied. But this does
not imply that the martingale converges in
L^{1}
(see the paragraph
after Proposition II-2-11 in
[
12
]
). The a.s. and the
L^{1}
convergence
to
H
, follow from Proposition
1
.

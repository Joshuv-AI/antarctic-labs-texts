Some extensions of Krasnoselskii’s fixed point result for real functions
Hassan Khandani

Abstract
We extend Krasnoselskii’s fixed point result to non-self-real functions. We find a new and simple proof for Hillam’s result. In our approach, we don’t assume the image of the related mapping to be compact or bounded. In this way, we extend Hillam’s result to self-mappings on ℝ \mathbb{R} . Finally, we present a new proof for the global convergence of the Newton-Raphson method.

Introduction

We start our discussion by presenting the definition of L-Lipschitz functions, Krasnoselskii’s theorem, and some generalizations of this result. In these results, the related function is self-mapping. In this manuscript, we extend Theorem
1.2

Definition 1.1
.

Let

\lvert h(x)-h(y)\rvert\leq L\lvert x-y\rvert

.

(Krasnoselskii’s sequence

Let

x_{n+1}=tx_{n}+(1-t)h(x_{n})\text{ for each }n\geq 0.

(1)

Theorem 1.1

(Krasnoselskii

Suppose

Moreover, Edelstein generalized this result to Banach spaces with a strictly convex norm

Apart from all these generalizations, Bailey gave proof of Krasnoselskii’s result for nonexpansive real-valued functions on a closed interval

,
bailey1974krasnoselski

borwein1991fixed
. Hillam B. P.

extended Bailey’s result to Lipschitzian functions, which is the main subject of our discussion.

Theorem 1.2

(Hillam

)
.

Let
L>0
, and

be a
L
-Lipschitz mapping. Then, the sequence of iterations defined by Equation
1
converges monotonically to a fixed point of

if
0<t\leq\frac{1}{1+L}

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L>0
,
h:[a,c]\to\mathbb{R}
be a
L
-Lipschitz mapping,
h(x)>x
for each
x\in[a,c)
, and
c
be the unique fixed point of
h
in
[a,c]
. Let
x_{0}\in[a,c]
and for each
n\geq 0
define:

x_{n+1}=(1-t)x_{n}+th(x_{n}),

(4)

then for each
0<t\leq\frac{1}{1+L}
the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
c
.

Proof.

If
x_{m}=c
for some non-negative integer
m
, then we have
x_{n}=c
for each
n\geq m
and the proof is complete. Therefore, we suppose
x_{n}\not=c
for each
n\geq 0
. Let
0<t\leq\frac{1}{1+L}
and
x\in[a,c)
. Since
h
is
L
-Lipschitz function we have
\frac{h(x)-c}{x-c}\geq-L\geq 1-\frac{1}{t}
. This follows that
\frac{h(x)-x}{x-c}\geq\frac{-1}{t}
. Rearranging this inequality, we get:

(1-t)(x)+th(x)\leq c

(5)

Assume
\{x_{n}\}
be defined by Equation
6
. From equation
5
and argument by induction we see that
x_{n}<c
for each
n\geq 0
. We show that
\{x_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence. Since
h(x_{0})>x_{0}
, we have
c>x_{1}=(1-t)x_{0}+th(x_{0})>x_{0}
. Suppose
x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{m}<c
. Since
a\leq x_{m}<c
, by our assumption
h(x_{m})>x_{m}
and
x_{m+1}=(1-t)x_{m}+th(x_{m})>x_{m}
. Therefore, by induction,
\{x_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence. Now,
x_{n}<c
for each
n\geq 0
and
\{x_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence in
[a,c]
. Assume
x_{n}
converges to
b\in[a,c]
.
b
is also a fixed point of
h
. Since f has a unique fixed point in

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
L>0
,
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
L
-Lipschitz mapping. Let
e\in\mathbb{R}
, define
x_{0}=e
and

x_{n+1}=(1-t)x_{n}+th(x_{n}),n\geq 1

(7)

Then,
\{x_{n}\}
is a monotone sequence that either converges to a fixed point of
h
or diverges to infinity.

Proof.

Assume
x_{0}=e\in[a,b]
. If
h(x_{0})=x_{0}
, then
\{x_{n}\}
is a constant sequence and converges to the fixed point
e=x_{0}
. If
h(x_{0})>x_{0}
, then we have the following two different cases:

•

(a) There exists a fixed point
d
such that
x_{0}<d
. In this case, suppose that
c
be the smallest fixed point of
h
such that
x_{0}<c
. Then,
h:[x_{0},c]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies all the conditions of
2.1
and therefore
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
c
.

•

(b) There is no fixed point
d
such that
x_{0}<d
. Suppose
f(y)<y
for some
y>x_{0}
. Then, using intermediate value theorem for the continuous function
h(x)-x
on the interval
[x_{0},y]
follows that there exists a point
z
with
x_{0}<z<y
such that
h(z)=z
. This is a contradiction, therefore, we deduce that
h(x)>x
each
x>x_{0}
. We have
x_{1}=(1-t)x_{0}+th(x_{0})>(1-t)x_{0}+tx_{0}=x_{0}
. By induction, we deduce that
x_{n+1}=(1-t)x_{n}+th(x_{n})>x_{n}
for each
n\geq 0
. Therefore,
\{x_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence. If
\{x_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence, then it converges to some point
c>x_{0}
. Since
h
is a continuous map, taking limit of both sides of Equation
7
follows that
h(c)=c
that is a contradiction. Therefore,
\{x_{n}\}
is not bounded and diverges to infinity.

Therefore, the proof is complete when
f(x_{0})>x_{0}
. A similar proof holds when
f(x_{0})<x_{0}
. The only difference is that the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is decreasing when
f(x_{0})<x_{0}
.
∎

Remark 2.1

If

A new proof of global convergence condition for the Newton-Raphson method

In this section, we show that the Newton-Raphson sequence is a special case of Krasnoselskii’s sequence, and we provide new proof for its global convergence as follows. It is worth mentioning that these results have been presented already as a pre-print paper

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
a,c\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<c
,
h:[a,c]\to\mathbb{R}
is a real-valued function,
c
is the unique root of
h
in
[a,c]
. Also assume that
h^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x),h^{{}^{\prime}}(x)
exist for each
x\in(a,c)

h(x)h^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)\geq 0
for each

h(x)h^{{}^{\prime}}(x)<0
for each
x\in[a,c)
,
h^{{}^{\prime}}(x)\not=0
for each

,
h^{{}^{\prime}}(a+)\not=0
,
h^{{}^{\prime}}(c-)\not=0
. For each

define:

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\frac{h(x_{n})}{h^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n})}\text{ for all }n\geq 0.

(9)

Then,
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
c
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

For each
x\in[a,c]
, define
g(x)=x-\frac{2h(x)}{h^{{}^{\prime}}(x)}

h(x)>x
for each
x\in[a,c)

g^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=-1+2\frac{h^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(x)h(x)}{(h^{{}^{\prime}}(x))^{2}}\geq-1\text{ for each }x\in(a,c).

(10)

We have
\frac{g(x_{n})+x_{n}}{2}=x_{n}-\frac{h(x_{n})}{h^{{}^{\prime}}(x_{n})}=x_{n+1}
. Now,
g
satisfies all conditions of Lemma
1.3
, so
\{x_{n}\}
converges to
c
as
n\to\infty
where
c
is the fixed point of
h
. We have
c=c-\frac{h(c)}{h^{{}^{\prime}}(c-)}
which follows that
h(c)=0.

∎

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced a new method for finding the fixed points of Lipschitz mappings. Using this method, we extended Krasnoselskii’s fixed point theorem to non-self mappings. It is worth mentioning that generalizing a result to non-self mapping is more useful in practice and can be applied to more functions. We provided a new simple proof of Hillam’s result. Our results are valuable for two reasons. First, using them, we can obtain an algorithm to estimate the fixed point or real root of a Lipschitz map. On the other hand, using this method, it is possible to give new proofs of Krasnoselski’s result for self or non-self maps on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and to present new fixed point results.
We also investigated the convergence of the Newton-Raphson method and presented the global convergence condition for this method. This global convergence condition is not a new result, but we have obtained it more easily. Moreover, we can apply our method to more iterative sequences and provide convergence conditions for these iterations.

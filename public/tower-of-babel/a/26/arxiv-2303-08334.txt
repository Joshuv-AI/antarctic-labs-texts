Improvement of selection formulas of mesh size and truncation numbers for the DE-Sinc approximation and its theoretical error bound
Tomoaki Okayama, Shota Ogawa

Abstract
The Sinc approximation applied to double-exponentially decaying functions is referred to as the DE-Sinc approximation. Because of its high efficiency, this method has been used in various applications. In the Sinc approximation, the mesh size and truncation numbers should be optimally selected to achieve its best performance. However, the standard selection formula has only been “near-optimally” selected because the optimal formula of the mesh size cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions of truncation numbers. In this study, we propose two improved selection formulas. The first one is based on the concept by an earlier research that resulted in a better selection formula for the double-exponential formula. The formula performs slightly better than the standard one, but is still not optimal. As a second selection formula, we introduce a new parameter to propose truly optimal selection formula. We provide explicit error bounds for both selection formulas. Numerical comparisons show that the first formula gives a better error bound than the standard formula, and the second formula gives a much better error bound than the standard and first formulas.

Introduction and summary

The Sinc approximation is based on Shannon’s sampling formula

F(x)\approx\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right),

(1.1)

where
\sinc x=\sin(\uppi x)/(\uppi x)
, and
h
represents a mesh size.
If
F
is analytic and absolutely integrable
on a strip complex region

\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert\operatorname{Im}\zeta\rvert<d\}\quad(d>0),

(1.2)

this approximation is capable of achieving exponential convergence,
denoted as
\OO(\ee^{-\uppi d/h})
.
Furthermore, if
\lvert F(x)\rvert
decays exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
,
i.e.,
\lvert F(x)\rvert=\OO(\ee^{-\mu\lvert x\rvert})

(\mu>0)
,
the infinite sum in (
1.1
) may
be truncated at some truncation numbers
M
and
N
as

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right).

(1.3)

Its error rate is estimated as
\OO(\ee^{-\mu nh}/h)
,
where
n=\max\{M,N\}
.
The overall error is estimated by
the sum of the error in (
1.1
)
(termed the discretization error) and
that in (
1.3
) (termed the truncation error).
Thus,
by setting the exponential rates of the two errors equal to each other,
we can derive the optimal formula for
the mesh size
h
with respect to
n

as

\ee^{-\uppi d/h}=\ee^{-\mu nh}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad h=\sqrt{\frac{\uppi d}{\mu n}},

resulting in
\OO(\sqrt{n}\ee^{-\sqrt{\uppi d\mu n}})

as the final (overall) error rate.
The Sinc approximation for
exponentially decaying functions is called
the SE-Sinc approximation,
which has been extensively developed
and analyzed by Stenger
Stenger
;
stenger00:_summar
.

Recently, it has been reported
SugiMatsu
;
TanaSugiMuro
that
if
\lvert F(x)\rvert
decays double exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
,
i.e.,
\lvert F(x)\rvert=\OO(\ee^{-(\uppi/2)\mu\exp(\lvert x\rvert)})
,
the approximation error in (
1.3
)
is
\OO(\ee^{-(\uppi/2)\mu\exp(nh)})
,
which is considerably smaller than that
in the case where
\lvert F(x)\rvert
decays single exponentially.
The Sinc approximation for
double-exponentially decaying functions is called
the DE-Sinc approximation.
In this case, the optimal formula of
the mesh size
h
with respect to
n

is given by the equation

\ee^{-\uppi d/h}=\ee^{-(\uppi/2)\mu\exp(nh)},

(1.4)

but a closed form for
h

in terms of elementary functions of
n

cannot be obtained.
Instead, several authors have employed the following near-optimal formula

h=\frac{\log(2dn/\mu)}{n}.

(1.5)

By the formula, the left-hand side in (
1.4
)
can be written as
\ee^{-\uppi dn/\log(2dn/\mu)}
,
and the right-hand side in (
1.4
)
can be written as
\ee^{-\uppi dn}
.
Although these two rates are approximately equal,
the former is slightly lower.
Consequently, the final error rate of the DE-Sinc approximation
is
\OO(\ee^{-\uppi dn/\log(2dn/\mu)})
.
We note that not only its error rate
but also its computable error bound has been provided
OkaMatsuSugi
;
Oka1
,
which is useful for a computation with guaranteed accuracy.

Considering the above background, this study improves the formula
that relates
h
and
n
in the case of the DE-Sinc approximation.
We propose two improved formulas.
The first one is based on the concept by an earlier research
OkaKuro
,
which focused on the double-exponential formula.
Their improved formula between
h
and
n

reduced the gap between the discretization and truncation errors.
As a result,
a sharper error bound for the double-exponential formula
was obtained than the existing one.
Thus, utilizing the same idea,
we provide a sharper error bound for the DE-Sinc approximation
than the existing one
OkaMatsuSugi
;
Oka1
.

Our second formula is based on a distinct approach.
Thus far,
n
has denoted the maximum number of truncation numbers
M
and
N
,
i.e.,
n=\max\{M,N\}
.
This condition has been a barrier to the construction
of the optimal selection formula that
eliminates the gap between the discretization and truncation errors.
In the new formula, we regard
n
as a free parameter
that does not have to be equal to
M
or
N
.
Then, we can derive formulas for
h
,
M
, and
N

with respect to
n
, such that
the discretization and truncation errors
have exactly the same rate.
In consequence, we give a much better error bound
for the DE-Sinc approximation.
Numerical experiments also support our theoretical results.

Theorem 2.1

(Okayama
(

, Theorem 4.8)
)

Assume that

In the same form,

.

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{\arsinh(x/\arsinh x)},

\displaystyle q(x)

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathrm{DE}}_{L,R,\alpha,\beta}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
h
be defined as

Theorem 2.3

4.1
Sketch of the proofs

In this section, we provide the proofs
for Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
.
In both cases,
as outlined in Section
1
,
we divide the approximation error into
the sum of the
discretization and truncation errors.
More precisely, we estimate the error as

\displaystyle\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lvert\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert.

As for the first term (the discretization error),
we use the following bound.

Theorem 4.1

(Okayama
(
Oka1
, Part of Theorem 4.8)
)
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathrm{DE}}_{L,R,\alpha,\beta}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu(1-\ee^{-2\uppi d/h})\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\ee^{-\uppi d/h},

where
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
.

As for the second term (the truncation error),
we give the following bound.
The proof is provided in Section
4.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathrm{DE}}_{L,R,\alpha,\beta}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
and let
M
and
N
be defined as in (

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2R}{\uppi\mu h\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

We then specify the relation between
h
and

,
considering the exponential rates of the two errors:

\ee^{-\uppi d/h}
and
\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}
.
In Theorem

,
we set the mesh size

as in (

4.2
Proof of Lemma
4.2

We show Lemma
4.2
as follows.

Proof.

First, using
\lvert\sinc x\rvert\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\lvert F(kh)\rvert+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\lvert F(kh)\rvert.

Using (
2.1
), we estimate the second term as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\lvert F(kh)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq R\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\alpha}(1+\ee^{\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\beta}}

\displaystyle=R\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(kh)}}{(1+\ee^{-\uppi\sinh(kh)})^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq R\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(kh)}}{(1+0)^{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{h}\int_{Nh}^{\infty}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{h\uppi\beta\cosh(Nh)}\int_{Nh}^{\infty}\uppi\beta\cosh(x)\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh x}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{h\uppi\beta\cosh(Nh)}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(Nh)}.

Furthermore, using (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{R}{h\uppi\beta\cosh(Nh)}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(Nh)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{h\uppi\beta\cosh(\arsinh((\mu/\beta)q(dn/\mu)))}\ee^{-\uppi\beta\sinh(\arsinh((\mu/\beta)q(dn/\mu)))}

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{h\uppi\beta\sqrt{1+\{(\mu/\beta)q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{h\uppi\sqrt{\beta^{2}+\mu^{2}\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{h\uppi\sqrt{\mu^{2}+\mu^{2}\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{h\uppi\mu\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

In the same way,
using (
2.1
) and (
2.8
),
we estimate the first term as

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\lvert F(kh)\rvert\leq\frac{R}{h\uppi\mu\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

Thus, we obtain the desired inequality.

For all

Proof.

Using

\displaystyle=\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)\cosh\left(\left(1-\frac{2}{\uppi}\right)t\right)+\left\{\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)\right\}\sinh\left(\left(1-\frac{2}{\uppi}\right)t\right)

we have

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=g(t)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+h(t),

where

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\left(1-\frac{2}{\uppi}\right)t\right)-t,

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)\sinh\left(\left(1-\frac{2}{\uppi}\right)t\right).

Here, we set
\gamma
and
\delta
as

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\uppi}{\uppi-2}\log\left(\frac{\uppi}{\uppi-2}\right),

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{\uppi}{2(\uppi-2)}\log\left(\frac{\uppi}{4-\uppi}\right),

which satisfy
g^{\prime}(\gamma)=0
and
h^{\prime}(\delta)=0
.
In what follows, we consider three cases:
(i)
0\leq t<\uppi\log 2
,
(ii)
\uppi\log 2\leq t<\uppi\log 3
, and
(iii)
\uppi\log 3\leq t
, and we show
f(t)\geq 0
in each case.

(i) Consider the case
0\leq t<\uppi\log 2
.
Because
g(t)
is monotonically decreasing
for
t\in(0,\gamma)
,
noting
\uppi\log 2<\gamma
,
we have

f(t)\geq g(\uppi\log 2)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+h(t).

Furthermore, from
g(\uppi\log 2)>0
,

\sinh((2/\uppi)t)\geq 0
and
h(t)\geq 0
, we have
f(t)\geq 0
.

(ii) Consider the case
\uppi\log 2\leq t<\uppi\log 3
.
Because
g(t)
has its minimum at
t=\gamma
,
noting
\gamma\in(\uppi\log 2,\uppi\log 3)
,
we have

f(t)\geq g(\gamma)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+h(t).

Because
g(\gamma)<0
,

g(\gamma)\sinh((2/\uppi)t)
is a monotonically decreasing function.
Furthermore, because
h(t)
is monotonically decreasing for
t\geq\delta
,
noting
\delta<\uppi\log 2
, we have

g(\gamma)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+h(t)\geq g(\gamma)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}\cdot\uppi\log 3\right)+h(\uppi\log 3)>0.

(iii) Consider the case
\uppi\log 3\leq t
.
Because
g(t)
is monotonically increasing
for
t\geq\gamma
,
noting
\gamma<\uppi\log 3
, we have

f(t)\geq g(\uppi\log 3)\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)+h(t).

Furthermore, because
g(\uppi\log 3)>0
,

\sinh((2/\uppi)t)>0
, and
h(t)>0
,
we have
f(t)>0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Using these propositions,
we prove Theorem
2.2
as follows.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
substituting (
2.5
),
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\cdot\frac{\ee^{-\uppi d/h}}{(1-\ee^{-2\uppi d/h})}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2R}{\uppi\mu h\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\cdot\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\mu p(dn/\mu)}}{(1-\ee^{-2\uppi\mu p(dn/\mu)})}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2R(dn/\mu)}{\uppi d\arsinh(q(dn/\mu))\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

(4.2)

Here, for
t>0
, the inequality (
4.1
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh t}{t}

\displaystyle\geq\sinh\left(\frac{2}{\uppi}t\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\arsinh\left(\frac{\sinh t}{t}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\uppi}t

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{t}{\arsinh\left(\frac{\sinh t}{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\uppi}{2}.

Putting
t=\arsinh(dn/\mu)
in the last inequality, we have

\frac{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}{\arsinh\left(\frac{dn/\mu}{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}\right)}=\frac{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}{\arsinh(q(dn/\mu))}\leq\frac{\uppi}{2}.

Therefore, the second term in (
4.2
)
is bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{2R(dn/\mu)}{\uppi d\arsinh(q(dn/\mu))\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2R}{\uppi d}\cdot\frac{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}{\arsinh(q(dn/\mu))}\cdot\frac{q(dn/\mu)}{\sqrt{1+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2R}{\uppi d}\cdot\frac{\uppi}{2}\cdot\frac{q(dn/\mu)}{\sqrt{0+\{q(dn/\mu)\}^{2}}}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{d}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

Furthermore, from Proposition
4.3
,
the first term in (
4.2
)
is bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\cdot\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\mu p(dn/\mu)}}{(1-\ee^{-2\uppi\mu p(dn/\mu)})}

\displaystyle=\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\cdot\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\mu\{p(dn/\mu)-q(dn/\mu)\}}}{(1-\ee^{-2\uppi\mu p(dn/\mu)})}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4L}{\uppi^{2}d\mu\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}\cdot\frac{\ee^{-\uppi\mu\{p(d/\mu)-q(d/\mu)\}}}{(1-\ee^{-2\uppi\mu p(d/\mu)})}\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.5
Remark on the function
q(x)

So far, we have defined the function
q(x)
by (
2.4
).
However, notice that
in the proof of Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
we do not use the explicit form of
q(x)
.
Therefore, even if we choose another function for
q(x)
,
we can establish the following theorem.
The proof is omitted here because it can be shown in exactly the same way
as Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathrm{DE}}_{L,R,\alpha,\beta}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Here, let
q(x)
be a function that satisfies

q(x)\geq 0
for
x\geq 0
and
q^{\prime}(x)\geq 0

for
x\geq 0
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
h
be defined as

h=\frac{d}{\mu q(dn/\mu)},

and let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.8
).
Then, it holds that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert\leq C\ee^{-\uppi\mu q(dn/\mu)},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
,
expressed as (
2.9
).

According to this theorem,
if we define
q(x)
as
q(x)=x
,
we can derive the following result instead of Theorem
2.3
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Assume that
F\in\mathbf{L}^{\mathrm{DE}}_{L,R,\alpha,\beta}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
h
be defined as

h=\frac{1}{n},

(4.4)

and let
M
and
N
be defined as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} M&=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\arsinh\left(\frac{dn}{\alpha}\right)\right\rceil,&N&=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\arsinh\left(\frac{dn}{\beta}\right)\right\rceil.\end{aligned}

(4.5)

Then, it holds that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)\sinc\left(\frac{x-kh}{h}\right)\right\rvert\leq C\ee^{-\uppi dn},

where
C
is a constant independent of
n
, expressed as

C=\frac{2}{\uppi d}\left[\frac{2L}{\uppi\mu(1-\ee^{-2\uppi d})\cos^{\alpha+\beta}((\uppi/2)\sin d)\cos d}+R\right].

The convergence rate given by this result is

\OO(\ee^{-\uppi dn})
, which appears to be faster than
that by Theorem
2.3
:
\OO(\ee^{-\uppi dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)})
.
However, we emphasize that these rates are given
with respect to
n
,
not with respect to the number of function evaluations (
M+N+1
).
According to (
4.4
)
and (
4.5
),
we derive
M=N=\OO(n\log n)
,
which grows more rapidly than
n
.

In this way, by choosing a higher order function for
q(x)
,
we can obtain a higher convergence rate with respect to
n
.
However, in exchange for the higher convergence rate,
we obtain higher growth of
M+N+1
.
As a consequence, we cannot improve the convergence rate
with respect to
M+N+1
.
Although Corollary
4.7
is meaningful
as an error bound with simpler formulas of
h
,
M
, and
N
,
we should note that the convergence rate is given
not with respect to
M+N+1
but with respect to
n
.

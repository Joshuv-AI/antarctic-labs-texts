On sums of Fourier coefficients of cusp forms twisted with additive characters
Zihao Liu

Abstract
. When a n a_{n} is the n n ’th coefficient of some holomorphic cusp form, we prove a variety of Omega results for the twisted sum ∑ n ≤ x a n ​ e 2 ​ π ​ i ​ n ​ α \sum_{n\leq x}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\alpha} and discuss their applications to the Ramanujan τ \tau -function and sums of a n a_{n} over arithmetic progressions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma=SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
f
is said to be a holomorphic cusp form of weight
k>0
on
\Gamma
if it is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
f(\infty)=0
and for any
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
with
c>0
, there exists some number
A(\gamma)
of modulus unity satisfying

(1.1)

f\left(a\tau+b\over c\tau+d\right)=A(\gamma)[-i(c\tau+d)]^{k}f(\tau).

Assume also that
f
can be expanded at
\infty
into

(1.2)

f(\tau)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\tau}

such that not all the
a_{n}
’s are zero, so
f(\tau+1)=f(\tau)
, meaning that
A(\gamma)
is unchanged if
\gamma
is replaced with
\gamma^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&m\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\gamma\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
for any integral
m,n
. As a result, knowing either two of
a,b,c,d
in
\gamma
is sufficient to determine the value of
A(\gamma)
.

In this paper, we derive Omega results for a twisted partial sum over
a_{n}
:

(1.3)

S(x,\alpha)=\sum_{n\leq x}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\alpha}.

Specifically, we show that

Theorem 1.1
.

When
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
, there is
S(x,\alpha)=\Omega(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
.

If we impose more conditions on
a_{n}
, we can strengthen this to an
\Omega_{\pm}
theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
, if there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Re[A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}]\neq 0
, then when
\alpha=a/c
, we have
\Re[S(x,\alpha)]=\Omega_{\pm}(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
.

Theorem 1.1
and
Theorem 1.2
are proved based on the asymptotic behavior of the following generating series

(1.4)

F(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\alpha}e^{-s\sqrt{n}}

when
s
approaches the imaginary axis from the right half plane.

Theorem 1.3
.

When
\alpha=a/c
for some
(a,c)=1
,
\sigma>0
, and
t_{n}=4\pi\sqrt{n}/c
, there exists nonzero constants
B^{+},B^{-}
only depending on
k
such that as
\sigma\to 0^{+}
,
F(\sigma,\alpha)=O(\sigma)
and

F(\sigma\pm it_{n})=\begin{cases}[1+o(1)]B^{\pm}A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}c^{-k}\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}},&a_{n}\neq 0,\\
O(1),&a_{n}=0,\end{cases}

in which
b
and
d
are any integers satisfying
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
.

Omega results concerning the Fourier coefficients of modular forms are usually proved by studying the second moments. For instance, the theory of Rankin–Selberg integrals
[
5
]
produces

(1.5)

\sum_{n\leq x}|a_{n}|^{2}\asymp x^{k},

immediately producing
a_{n}=\Omega(n^{k-1\over 2})
, which is essentially optimal in view of the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture. Walker
[
7
]
applied this method to study the second moments of
S(x,0)
, thereby proving the
\alpha=0
special case of
Theorem 1.1
.

Our method is motivated by G. H. Hardy’s investigations of the classical lattice-point problems
[
3
]
[
4
]
, in which it was proved that as
x\to+\infty

\sum_{n\leq x}d(n)-[x\log x+(2\gamma-1)\log x]=\Omega_{\pm}(x^{\frac{1}{4}}),

\sum_{n\leq x}r_{2}(n)-\pi x=\Omega_{\pm}(x^{\frac{1}{4}}),

where
d(n)
is the divisor function and
r_{2}(n)
is the number of ways to express
n
as a sum of two squares. It should be noted that another means to yield Omega results is by appealing to the general theorems of Chandrasekharan and Narasimhan
[
1
]
[
2
]
, but due to the generality of their arguments, more stringent assumptions have to be imposed on
a_{n}
hein order to apply their theorems.

The paper is organized in the following manner: In
Section 2
, we study the asymptotic behavior of
F(s,\alpha)
and deduce
Theorem 1.3
. In
Section 3
, we applied the properties of
F(s)
to prove
Theorem 1.1
. In
Section 4
, we perform a more refined analysis and derive
Section 4
. Finally, in
Section 5
, we apply our results to Ramanujan
\tau
-function and sums of Fourier coefficients over arithmetic progressions.

2.
The generating series
F(s)

From now on,
\alpha=a/c
for some integers
(a,c)=1
. Choose integers
b,d
such that
ad-bc=1
so
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
. Write

\tau^{\prime}=\frac{a}{c}+{iu^{-1}\over c},\quad\tau=-\frac{d}{c}+{iu\over c},

so that (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{2\pi ina/c}e^{-{2\pi n\over c}\cdot u^{-1}}

\displaystyle=A(\gamma)u^{k}f(\tau)=A(\gamma)\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}u^{k}e^{-{2\pi n\over c}\cdot u}.

When
s>0
, the integral identity

(2.2)

e^{-s\sqrt{n}}={s\over\sqrt{\pi}}\left(c\over 8\pi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\left(c\over 8\pi\right)s^{2}u}e^{-{2\pi n\over c}\cdot u^{-1}}\mathrm{d}u,

transforms (
1.4
) into

F(s,\alpha)={s\over\sqrt{\pi}}\left(c\over 8\pi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\left(c\over 8\pi\right)s^{2}u}f\left(\frac{a}{c}+{iu^{-1}\over c}\right)\mathrm{d}u.

Plugging in (
2.1
) gives

F(s,\alpha)={A(\gamma)s\over\sqrt{\pi}}\left(c\over 8\pi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}\int_{0}^{+\infty}u^{k-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\left[\left(c\over 8\pi\right)s^{2}+{2\pi n\over c}\right]u}\mathrm{d}u.

Computing the remaining integral and simplifying yields the following identity:

(2.3)

F(s,\alpha)=A(\gamma)s\left(8\pi\over c\right)^{k}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})\over\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{n\geq 1}{a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}\over(s^{2}+t_{n}^{2})^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Due to Hecke’s bound
a_{n}=O(n^{\frac{k}{2}})
and
t_{n}^{2}\asymp n
, the right-hand side converges in any compact subset
\Re(s)>0
, so (
2.3
) is valid throughout the half plane
\Re(s)>0
by analytic continuation.

Proof of
Theorem 1.3
.

When
s\to 0
, it follows from (
2.3
) that
F(s,\alpha)/s
is bounded, so
F(\sigma,\alpha)=O(\sigma)
for
\sigma>0
. When
s=\sigma\pm it_{n}
, notice that

s^{2}+t_{n}^{2}=(s+it_{n})(s-it_{n})\sim\pm 2it_{n}\sigma=2e^{\pm i\pi/2}\sigma,

so we have

\displaystyle F(s,\alpha)

\displaystyle=A(\gamma)(\pm it_{n})\left(8\pi\over c\right)^{k}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})\over 2^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}e^{\mp(k+\frac{1}{2}){\pi i\over 2}}\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}+o(\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{e^{\pm(\frac{1}{2}-k){\pi i\over 2}}(4\pi)^{k}{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})\over\sqrt{2\pi}}}_{B^{\pm}}\cdot A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}c^{-k}\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}+o(\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}).

∎

Remark 2.1
.

G. H. Hardy
[
3
]
proved a corresponding identity for
r_{2}(n)
and obtained a similar asymptotic result as
\Re(s)\to 0^{+}
, but there are no analogs of (
2.3
) in divisor problems
[
4
]
.

3.
Omega results for the magnitude of partial sums

By partial summation, we can express
F(s,\alpha)
as an integral of
S(x,\alpha)
:

F(s,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-sx^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathrm{d}S(x,\alpha)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-sy}\mathrm{d}S(y)=s\int_{0}^{+\infty}S(y)e^{-sy}\mathrm{d}y.

If
|S(x,\alpha)|<\varepsilon x^{\theta}
when
x>x_{0}
, then whenever
0<\Re(s)=\sigma\leq 1
and
\Im(s)
is fixed, there is

|F(s,\alpha)|<\varepsilon|s|\int_{x_{0}}^{+\infty}y^{2\theta}e^{-\sigma y}\mathrm{d}y+O_{x_{0}}(1)=\varepsilon|s|\Gamma(2\theta+1)\sigma^{-2\theta-1}+O_{x_{0}}(1),

which indicates that

Lemma 3.1
.

If
S(x,\alpha)=o(x^{\theta})
as
x\to+\infty
, then
F(\sigma+it,\alpha)=o(\sigma^{-2\theta-1})
when
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
is fixed and
\sigma\to 0^{+}
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

By our assumption on
f
in
Section 1
, we can choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{n}\neq 0
, so setting
\theta=\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
and
t=t_{n}
in
Lemma 3.1
contradicts
Theorem 1.3
. Thus,
S(x,\alpha)=\Omega(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
(i.e. the negation of
o(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
).
∎

4.
Oscillations of real components of partial sums

In this section, we only give a detailed proof of
\Re[S(x,\alpha)]=\Omega_{+}(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
, the
\Omega_{-}
theorem can be proved by formulating a symmetrical reasoning.

If
\Re[S(x,\alpha)]<\varepsilon x^{\theta}
when
x>x_{0}
and define

(4.1)

G(s)=s\int_{0}^{+\infty}\{\varepsilon y^{2\theta}-\Re[S(y^{2})]\}e^{-sy}\mathrm{d}y,

then whenever
0<\Re(s)=\sigma\leq 1
and
\Im(s)
is fixed, there is

\displaystyle|G(s)|

\displaystyle<|s|\int_{x_{0}}^{+\infty}[\varepsilon y^{2\theta}-\Re[S(y^{2})]]e^{-\sigma y}\mathrm{d}y+O_{x_{0}}(1)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon|s|\int_{0}^{+\infty}y^{2\theta}e^{-\sigma y}\mathrm{d}y-|s|\Re\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}S(y^{2})e^{-\sigma y}\mathrm{d}y\right]+O_{x_{0}}(1).

\displaystyle=\varepsilon|s|\Gamma(2\theta+1)\sigma^{-2\theta-1}-|s|\sigma^{-1}\Re[F(\sigma,\alpha)]+O_{x_{0}}(1).

By
Theorem 1.3
,
F(\sigma,\alpha)=O(\sigma)
, so the second term is bounded as
\sigma\to 0^{+}
, which indicates that

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\Re[S(x,\alpha)]x^{-\theta}\leq 0
, then
G(\sigma+it)=o(\sigma^{-2\theta-1})
whenever
t\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed and
\sigma\to 0^{+}
.

Proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}[F(s,\alpha)+\overline{F(\overline{s},\alpha)}]
, so it follows from (
1.4
) and partial summation that when
\alpha=a/c
for some
(a,c)=1
, there is

(4.2)

F_{1}(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\Re(a_{n}e^{2\pi ina/c})e^{-s\sqrt{n}}=s\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Re[S(y^{2})]e^{-sy}\mathrm{d}y.

When
a_{n}\neq 0
for some
n
,
s=\sigma+it_{n}
, and
\sigma\to 0^{+}
, it follows from
Theorem 1.3
that (
4.2
) becomes

F_{1}(s)=\frac{1}{2}[B^{+}A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}+\overline{B^{-}A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}}]c^{-k}\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}+o(\sigma^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}})

According to the proof of
Theorem 1.3
,
\overline{B^{-}}=B^{+}
, so

(4.3)

F_{1}(s)=B^{+}\Re[A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}]c^{-k}\sigma^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}+o(\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}).

Plugging (
4.2
) into (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\Gamma(2\theta+1)s^{-2\theta}-F_{1}(s)

\displaystyle=B^{+}\Re[A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}]c^{-k}\sigma^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}+o(\sigma^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}).

Due to our assumption, we can choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Re[A(\gamma)a_{n}e^{-2\pi ind/c}]\neq 0
. Plugging
\theta=\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
into
Lemma 4.1
leads to a contradiction, so we must have
\Re[S(x,\alpha)]=\Omega_{+}(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
.
∎

5.
Examples and Applications

5.1.
Ramanujan
\tau
-function

If
f(\tau)=\Delta(\tau)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\tau(n)e^{2\pi in\tau}
, then
k=12
and
A(\gamma)=1
, so it follows from
Theorem 1.1
that

Corollary 5.1
.

For any integer
(a,c)=1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}\tau(n)e^{2\pi ina/c}=\Omega(x^{6-\frac{1}{4}}).

As for the
\Omega_{\pm}
result, observe that

\Re[A(\gamma)\tau(n)e^{-2\pi ind/c}]=\tau(n)\cos\left(2\pi nd\over c\right),

so according to
Theorem 1.2
, we have

Corollary 5.2
.

For any integer
(a,c)=1
, if
c
is odd or there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tau(n)\neq 0
and
na\not\equiv\pm\frac{c}{2}\pmod{c}
, then

\sum_{n\leq x}\tau(n)\cos\left(2\pi na\over c\right)=\Omega_{\pm}(x^{6-\frac{1}{4}}).

5.2.
Sums over arithmetic progressions

Define

S(x;q,h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv h\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}a_{n}.

Then from the properties of additive characters, we have

Corollary 5.3
.

For every
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
1\leq h\leq q
such that
(h,q)=1
and
S(x;q,h)=\Omega(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
.

Proof.

For all integers
a,q\geq 1
, there is

\displaystyle S(x,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq x}a_{n}e^{2\pi ina/q}=\sum_{1\leq r\leq q}e^{2\pi ir/q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
rn\equiv a\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}a_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq r\leq q}e^{2\pi ir/q}S(x;q,h_{r})\quad(rh_{r}\equiv 1\pmod{q}).

If
S(x;q,h)=o(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
for all
1\leq h\leq q
, then
S(x,\alpha)=o(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})
, which will contradict
Theorem 1.1
.
∎

By partial summation,
Corollary 5.3
can also be phrased using normalized Fourier coefficients:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\hat{a}_{n}=a_{n}/n^{k-1\over 2}
be the normalized
n
’th Fourier coefficient of
f
. Then for every
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
1\leq h\leq q
such that
(h,q)=1
and

\hat{S}(x;q,h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv h\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\hat{a}_{n}=\Omega(x^{\frac{1}{4}}).

In the
O
-direction, Lü
[
6
]
proved that for all
q\geq 1
, there is

\sum_{1\leq h\leq q}|\hat{S}(x;q,h)|^{2}\ll x\log x,

where
\ll
only depend on
f
. Combining this with
Corollary 5.4
, we obtain an upper bound for
\tilde{S}(x;q,h)
and
S(x;q,h)
valid “almost everywhere.”

Corollary 5.5
.

If
x^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\leq q\leq x
, then

\tilde{S}(x;q,h)=O(x^{\frac{1}{4}}),\quad S(x;q,h)=O(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})

for almost all
1\leq h\leq q
.

This demonstrates that the exponents of our Omega bounds in
Corollary 5.3
and
Corollary 5.4
are optimal.

5.3.
Further discussions on
S(x;q,h)

Although
Corollary 5.3
demonstrates that we are unable to reduce the exponent
\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
in the bound of
S(x;q,h)
, it is not an effective result that allows us to determine which
h
fulfills the Omega bound.

From the orthogonality of additive characters, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle S(x;q,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{1\leq m\leq q}e^{-2\pi ihm/q}S\left(x,\frac{m}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{c|q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq c\\
(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}e^{-2\pi iha/c}S\left(x,\frac{a}{c}\right).

Similar to how we prove
Theorem 1.1
, define

F(s;q,h)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
n\equiv h\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}a_{n}e^{-s\sqrt{n}}=s\int_{0}^{+\infty}S(y^{2};q,h)e^{-sy}\mathrm{d}y,

so it follows from (
5.1
) that

F(s;q,h)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{c|q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq c\\
(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}e^{-2\pi iha/c}F\left(x,\frac{a}{c}\right).

Let
b
and
d
be chosen such that
ad-bc=1
and
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
. Then it follows from
Theorem 1.3
that when
s=\sigma+it_{n}
such that
a_{n}\neq 0
, there is

F(s;q,h)={B^{+}a_{n}\over q\sigma^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{c|q}{K_{f}(-h,-n;c)\over c^{k}}+o(\sigma^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}),

where
K_{f}(h,n;c)
is the Kloosterman sum associated with
f
:

K_{f}(m,n;c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq c\\
(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}A(\gamma)e^{2\pi ima/c+2\pi ind/c}.

By adapting the arguments in
Section 3
, we deduce

Corollary 5.6
.

For every
q\in\mathbb{N}
, if there exist
1\leq h\leq q
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a_{n}\sum_{c|q}{K_{f}(-h,-n;c)\over c^{k}}\neq 0,

then as
x\to+\infty
, we have

S(x;q,h)=\Omega(x^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}),\quad\tilde{S}(x;q,h)=\Omega(x^{\frac{1}{4}}).

Although this result requires more conditions on
a_{n}
, it is considerably more effective than
Corollary 5.3
and
Corollary 5.4
.

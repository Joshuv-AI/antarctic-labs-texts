Monopoles And Landau-Ginzburg Models III: A Gluing Theorem
Donghao Wang

Abstract
. This is the third paper of this series. In [ Wan20b ] , we defined the monopole Floer homology for any pair ( Y , ω ) (Y,\omega) , where Y Y is a compact oriented 3-manifold with toroidal boundary and ω \omega is a suitable closed 2-form viewed as a decoration. In this paper, we establish a gluing theorem for this Floer homology when two such 3-manifolds are glued suitably along their common boundary, assuming that ∂ Y \partial Y is disconnected, and ω \omega is small and yet non-vanishing on ∂ Y \partial Y . As applications, we construct a monopole Floer 2-functor and the generalized cobordism maps. Using results of Kronheimer-Mrowka and Ni, it is shown that for any such 3-manifold Y Y that is irreducible, this Floer homology detects the Thurston norm on H 2 ​ ( Y , ∂ Y , ℝ ) H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{R}) and the fiberness of Y Y . Finally, we show that our construction recovers the monopole link Floer homology for any link inside a closed 3-manifold.

Introduction

1.1.
An Overview

The Seiberg-Witten Floer homology of a closed oriented 3-manifold as introduced by Kronheimer-Mrowka
[
KM07
]
has greatly influenced the study of 3-manifold
topology since its inception. The underlying idea is an infinite dimensional Morse theory: the monopole equations on
\mathbb{R}_{t}
times a closed 3-manifold is interpreted as the downward gradient flow of the Chern-Simons-Dirac functional.

This idea was further explored by the author in
[
Wan20b
]
in order to define the monopole Floer homology for any pair
(Y,\omega)
, where
Y
is a compact oriented 3-manifold with toroidal boundary and
\omega
is a suitable closed 2-form on
Y
. This Floer homology categorifies the Milnor-Turaev torsion invariant of
Y
(this follows from the work of Meng-Taubes
[
MT96
]
and Turaev
[
Tur98
]
) and can be cast into a functor from a suitable cobordism category; see
[
Wan20b
, Theorem 1.5]
. However, the second paper
[
Wan20b
]
was devoted to the analytic foundation of this Floer theory; very little was explored about its topological properties.

The goal of the present paper is to understand the properties of this Floer homology in the special case that

(
\star
)

\partial Y
is disconnected, and
\omega
has non-zero small pairing with each component of
\partial Y
.

Under this assumption, we will prove that the monopole Floer homology of
(Y,\omega)
is a topological invariant: it depends only on the 3-manifold
Y
, the cohomology class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(Y;i\mathbb{R})
and an additional class in
H^{1}(\partial Y;i\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, when
Y
is irreducible, this invariant detects the Thurston norm on
H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{R})
and the fiberness of
Y
, generalizing the classical results
[
KM97
,
KM07
,
Ni08
]
for closed 3-manifolds.

In the context of Heegaard Floer homology, the knot Floer homology
\widehat{\HFK}_{*}
and
\HFK_{*}^{-}
were introduced by Oszváth-Szábo
[
OS04
]
and independently Rasmussen
[

]

[

,
Juh08
]

[

]
defined the monopole knot Floer homology

as the analogue of the hat-version

One motivation of this project is to give an alternative definition of

so that a (3+1) TQFT property can be verified easily. This goal is accomplished in this paper: for any knot

is isomorphic to

, where

[

]
that the knot Floer homology is related to the monopole equations on

Most topological implications of this paper follow immediately from a gluing theorem that computes the monopole Floer homology when two such 3-manifolds

1.2.
Summary of Results

For the benefit of the readers, we give a more detailed account of results that come out of the Gluing Theorem

. Let

•

•

Such a quadruple

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem

)
.

Under above assumptions, the monopole Floer homology group

Y

[\omega]\in H^{2}(Y;i\mathbb{R})
and
[*_{\Sigma}\lambda]\in\im(H^{1}(Y;i\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(\Sigma;i\mathbb{R}))

For any closed irreducible 3-manifold

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem

)

Consider the set of monopole classes

\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}):\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\neq\{0\}\}\subset H^{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z}),

and define
\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max_{a\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})}\langle a,\kappa\rangle

1.3.
Connected Sum Formulae

Two simple connected sum formulas can be derived from the Gluing Theorem
2.7
for reducible 3-manifolds. For any
\mathbb{Y}_{1}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{2}
, take their connected sum to be

\mathbb{Y}_{1}\#\mathbb{Y}_{2}=(Y_{1}\#Y_{2},\omega_{1}\#\omega_{2},\cdots).

The class
[\omega_{1}\#\omega_{2}]\in H^{2}(Y_{1}\#Y_{2};i\mathbb{R})
is canonically determined by
\omega_{j}\in\Omega^{2}(Y_{j};i\mathbb{R}),\ j=1,2
and by the relation
\langle[\omega_{1}\#\omega_{2}],S^{2}\rangle=0
, where
S^{2}\subset Y_{1}\#Y_{2}
is the 2-sphere separating
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
.

Proposition 1.6

(Proposition
10.1
)
.

Under above assumptions, we have

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1}\#\mathbb{Y}_{2})=\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}V,

where
V
is a two dimensional vector space over
\mathcal{R}
.

Proposition 1.7

(Proposition
10.3
)
.

For any closed 3-manifold
Z
, one can form the connected sum
\mathbb{Y}\#Z
in a similar way. Then

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}\#Z)\cong\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\widetilde{\HM}_{*}(Z)

where
\widetilde{\HM}_{*}(Z)
is the sutured monopole Floer homology of
(Z(1),\delta)
, which is the balanced sutured manifold obtained from
Z
by removing a 3-ball; the unique suture
\delta
is the equator of
\partial Z(1)
; see Definition
10.2
.

Proposition
1.6
and
1.7
are consistent with the connected sum formulas in
[
Juh06
, Proposition 9.15]
for sutured Heegaard Floer homology; the analogue for sutured monopole Floer homology was obtained in
[

, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition

should be compared with a vanishing result, which is concerned with the case that the 2-form
\omega
on

has non-zero pairing with the separating 2-sphere

.

Proposition 1.8
.

Suppose

is any 3-manifold with toroidal boundary that contains an embedded 2-sphere

\langle c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}),[S^{2}]\rangle=0

1.4.
Gerig’s program in contact topology

This paper is motivated in part by Gerig’s broader program to attack the two-or-infinite conjecture (see for instance
[
HWZ98
,
HWZ03
,
HT09
,
CGH16
]
) by mimicking Taubes’ solution
[
Tau07
]
to the Weinstein conjecture in dimension 3. What he needed from the Seiberg-Witten theory is a much stronger result, which we state now as a conjecture.

Conjecture 1.9

(Gerig)
.

For any connected 3-manifold
Y
with toroidal boundary, one can choose a closed 2-form
\omega
and a geometric datum
\mathfrak{d}=(g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
on
\partial Y
such that
\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})\geq 1
. If
\rank=1
, then
Y
is the connected sum of
[-1,1]_{s}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
with some monopole
L
-space.

In light of Proposition
1.5
,
1.6
and
1.7
, this conjecture holds if
Y
is the connected sum
Y_{1}\#\cdots\#Y_{m}
of prime 3-manifolds, with each
Y_{j}
either closed or boundary-disconnected.

Gerig’s approach towards the two-or-infinite conjecture can be described as follows. Suppose that
Z_{0}
is any compact oriented 3-manifold, equipped with a contact 1-form
\alpha_{0}
with only finitely many Reeb orbits. Denote by
Y_{0}
the complement of these orbits. If one can define an embedded contact homology for the pair
(Y_{0},\alpha_{0})
and prove an ECH=SW type theorem, then
\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})=\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\text{ECH}(Y_{0},\alpha_{0})=1
. Conjecture
1.9
then implies that
\partial Y_{0}
has only two components.

An appropriate candidate for this ECH theory has already appeared in
[
CGH10
]
and
[
HS05
]
assuming that all Reeb orbits of
\alpha_{0}
are non-degenerate and elliptic. In this case, the Reeb flow of
\alpha_{0}
foliates the boundary of
Y_{0}
with irrational slopes, which is a technical condition needed for their construction. The main difficulty in Gerig’s program is to find the correct 2-form
\omega_{0}
on
Y_{0}
and to establish this ECH=SW type theorem using the deformation
\omega_{0}+td\alpha_{0}
as
t\to+\infty
. The 2-form
\omega_{0}
must be adapted to
\alpha_{0}
in a certain sense so that Taubes’ compactness argument goes through as usual. (Of course, Taubes’ solution to the Weinstein conjecture in dimension 3 requires only a weaker version of “ECH=SW”, but it is conceptually easier to think of Gerig’s strategy this way).

1.5.
Relations with Link Floer Homology

Our monopole Floer theory can be also used to recover the monopole link Floer homology
\LHM_{*}
. The next theorem will be proved using the Decomposition Theorem

Theorem 1.10

(Corollary

)
.

For any link

in a closed oriented 3-manifold
Z
, we pick a meridian

for each component

, and consider the link complement

Then for a suitable 2-form

By the work of Osváth-Szábo
[

]
and Ni
[
Ni09b
]
, the link Floer homology detects the Thurston norm of the link complement for any links in rational homology spheres. The assumption on homology is later removed by Juhász
[

, Remark 8.5]
. The same detection result using monopole link Floer homology

was obtained by Ghosh-Li
[

, Theorem 1.17]
. By Theorem

, Theorem

recovers the previous detection results in the case that the link complement is irreducible. This constraint can be further removed by our connected sum formulae.

On the other hand, any 3-manifold

with toroidal boundary can be viewed as the link complement of some link

detects the Thurston norm of

must rely on the choice of

.

1.7.
A Remark on the Gluing Theorem

Most results of this paper are based on the monopole Floer 2-functor in the Gluing Theorem
2.7
, and we give a short conceptual reason here to see why it has a particularly simple form and how this gluing result fits into the broader context of Landau-Ginzburg models.

In the first paper
[
Wan20a
]
, the author introduced an infinite dimensional gauged Landau-Ginzburg model for any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
:

(M(\Sigma),W_{\lambda},\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)).

It is more appropriate to think of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
as an invariant of
Y
relative to this Landau-Ginzburg model. To develop a gluing theory in general, one has to assign an
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
to
(M(\Sigma),W_{\lambda},\mathcal{G}(\Sigma))
and upgrade the Floer homology of
\mathbb{Y}
as an
A_{\infty}
-module over
\mathcal{A}
. By the work of Seidel
[
Sei08
, Corollary 18.27]
, we wish to construct a spectral sequence abutting to
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1}\circ_{h}\mathbb{Y}_{2})
with
E_{1}
page equal to

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2}).

In a finite dimensional framework, this spectral sequence has been reproved using the complex gradient flow equation in
[
Wan22
]
, and a generalization to suitable infinite dimensional situations may come soon afterwards. However, in the special case
(

⋆

)
‣
1.1
that we have discussed so far, the
A_{\infty}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is expected to be trivial: the thimbles of different critical points of
W_{\lambda}
are not supposed to intersect at all. This suggests that the spectral sequence collapses at
E_{1}
-page and the gluing formula is simply a tensor product. On the technical level, this allows us to take a shortcut in this paper by using Floer’s excision argument, which bypasses the complicated general framework of
[

]
.

The next interesting case that we hope to investigate in the future is when
\Sigma
is connected and
\mu=0
in the geometric datum
\mathfrak{d}=(g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
, which is also important for Gerig’s program in contact topology. This may lead eventually to an analytic construction of the knot Floer homology
\HFK_{*}^{-}
from our story. In this case, the gluing map (

) constructed in this paper is identically zero, and the general framework of
[
Wan22
]
will be indispensable. Readers are referred to
[
Wan20b
, Section 1.6]
for more heuristics on this direction.

Remark 1.13
.

We also note that the Gluing Theorem

is indeed subject to certain limitations. When

and
(Y_{2},\omega_{2})
are glued along their common boundaries, the 2-forms

and

must match within a neighborhood of the boundary. This imposes a non-trivial homological constraint on the way they are glued.
∎

The category

consists of closed 3-manifolds equipped with imaginary valued closed 2-forms that are never balanced or negatively monotone on each component.

3.
Proof of the Gluing Theorem

In this section, we present the proof of the Gluing Theorem
2.7
. The construction of the gluing map
\alpha
in Theorem
2.7

(G2)
is based upon Floer’s Excision Theorem
[
BD95
]
, which has been adapted to the monopole Floer homology by Kronheimer-Mrowka
[
KM10
]
. We will follow the setup of
[
KM10
, Theorem 3.1 & 3.2]
closely.

This section starts with an overview of the monopole Floer homology defined in
[
Wan20b
]
, which yields the monopole Floer functor
\HM_{*}
in Theorem
2.7

(G1)
. The gluing map
\alpha
is then constructed in Subsection
3.6
.

3.1.
Review

Recall that a
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}_{X}
on an oriented Riemannian 4-manifold
X
is a pair
(S_{X},\rho_{4})
where
S_{X}=S^{+}\oplus S^{-}
is the spin bundle, and the bundle map
\rho_{4}:T^{*}X\to\End(S_{X})
defines the Clifford multiplication. A configuration
\gamma=(A,\Phi)\in\mathcal{C}(X,\mathfrak{s})
consists of a smooth
spin^{c}\
connection
A
and a smooth section
\Phi
of
S^{+}
. Use
A^{t}
to denote the induced connection of
A
on
\bigwedge^{2}S^{+}
. Let
\omega_{X}\in\Omega^{2}(X,i\mathbb{R})
be a closed 2-form on
X
and
\omega^{+}_{X}
denote its self-dual part. The Seiberg-Witten equations perturbed by
\omega_{X}
are defined on the configuration space
\mathcal{C}(X,\mathfrak{s})
by the formula:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{4}(F_{A^{t}}^{+})-(\Phi\Phi^{*})_{0}&=\rho_{4}(\omega^{+}_{X}),\\
D_{A}^{+}\Phi&=0,\end{array}\right.

where
D_{A}^{+}:\Gamma(S^{+})\to\Gamma(S^{-})
is the Dirac operator and
(\Phi\Phi^{*})_{0}=\Phi\Phi^{*}-\frac{1}{2}|\Phi|^{2}\otimes\Id_{S^{+}}
is the traceless part of the endomorphism
\Phi\Phi^{*}:S^{+}\to S^{+}
.

When it comes to an oriented Riemannian 3-manifold
Y
, the dimensional reduction of (
3.1
) is obtained by looking at (
3.1
) on the product manifold
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times Y
and by asking the configuration
(A,\Phi)
to be
\mathbb{R}_{t}
-invariant. A
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
Y
is again a pair
(S,\rho_{3})
where the spin bundle
S=S^{+}
has complex rank 2 and
\rho_{3}:T^{*}Y\to\End(S)
defines the Clifford multiplication. The 3-dimensional Seiberg-Witten equations now read:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{3}(F_{B^{t}})-(\Psi\Psi^{*})_{0}&=\rho_{3}(\omega),\\
D_{B}\Psi&=0.\end{array}\right.

where
B
is a
spin^{c}\
connection and
\Psi\in\Gamma(Y,S)
. Here
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(Y,i\mathbb{R})
is a closed 2-form and
D_{B}:\Gamma(Y,S)\to\Gamma(Y,S)
denotes the Dirac operator on
Y
.

3.2.
Results from the Second Paper

In this subsection, we review the construction of the monopole Floer homology from
[
Wan20b
]
, which defines the functor in Theorem
2.7

(G1)
. For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}
, define
-\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},-\lambda,\mu)
to be the orientation reversal of
\mathbf{\Sigma}
. Since the category
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
is more or less equivalent to
\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,(-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})\cup\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
(only the property
(P2)
may be different), we focus on the case when
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}=\emptyset
.

Given any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}=(Y,\psi,g_{Y},\omega,\{\mathfrak{q}\})\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
, we first attach a cylindrical end
[1,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma
to
Y
to obtain a complete Riemannian manifold:

\widehat{Y}=Y\cup_{\psi}[1,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma.

The metric on the end is given by
d^{2}s+g_{\Sigma}
. The closed 2-form
\omega
is extended constantly as
\mu+ds\wedge\lambda
on
[1,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma
, denoted also by
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\widehat{Y},i\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\mathfrak{s}_{std}=(S_{std},\rho_{std,3})
be the standard
spin^{c}\
structure on
\mathbb{R}_{s}\times\Sigma
with
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}_{std})=0\in H^{2}(\Sigma,i\mathbb{R})
. The spin bundle
S_{std}
can be constructed explicitly as

S_{std}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\Lambda^{0,1}\Sigma.

See
[
Wan20b
, Section 2]
for more details. A relative
spin^{c}\
structure
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}
on
Y
is a pair
(\mathfrak{s},\varphi)
where
\mathfrak{s}=(S,\rho_{3})
is a
spin^{c}\
structure on
Y
and

\varphi:(S,\rho_{3})|_{\partial Y}\to\psi^{*}\mathfrak{s}_{std}|_{\partial Y}

is an isomorphism of
spin^{c}\
structures near the boundary, compatible with
\psi:\partial Y\to\Sigma
. The set of isomorphism classes of relative
spin^{c}\
structures on
Y

\Spincr(Y)

is a torsor over
H^{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z})
. There is a natural forgetful map from
\Spincr(Y)
to the set of isomorphism classes of
spin^{c}\
structures:

\Spincr(Y)\to\Spinc(Y),\ \widehat{\mathfrak{s}}=(\mathfrak{s},\varphi)\mapsto\mathfrak{s},

whose fiber is acted on freely and transitively by
H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})/\im(H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z}))
reflecting the change of boundary trivializations. Any
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\in\Spincr(Y)
extends to a relative
spin^{c}\
structure on
\widehat{Y}
, denoted also by
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}
.

The key observation is that on
\mathbb{R}_{s}\times\Sigma
, the 3-dimensional Seiberg-Witten equations (
3.2
) perturbed by
\omega=\mu+ds\wedge\lambda
have a canonical
\mathbb{R}_{s}
-invariant solution, denoted by

(B_{*},\Psi_{*}),

which is also the unique finite energy solution on
\mathbb{R}_{s}\times\Sigma
, up to gauge, by our assumptions on
(\lambda,\mu)
. This result is due to Taubes
[
Tau01
, Proposition 4.4 & 4.7]
. See
[
Wan20b
, Theorem 2.6]
for the precise statement that we use here.

When it comes to
\widehat{Y}
, each configuration is required to approximate this special solution
(B_{*},\Psi_{*})
as
s\to\infty
. Take
(B_{0},\Psi_{0})
to be a smooth configuration on
\widehat{Y}
which agrees with
(B_{*},\Phi_{*})
on the cylindrical end
[1,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma
and consider the configuration space for any
k>\frac{1}{2}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})=\{(B,\Psi):(b,\psi)=(B,\Psi)-(B_{0},\Psi_{0})\in L^{2}_{k}(\widehat{Y},iT^{*}\widehat{Y}\oplus S)\}.

which is acted on freely by the gauge group

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(\widehat{Y})=\{u:\widehat{Y}\to S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}:u-1\in L^{2}_{k+1}(\widehat{Y},\mathbb{C})\},

using the formula:

u(B,\Psi)=(B-u^{-1}du,u\Psi).

The perturbed Chern-Simons-Dirac functional on
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
is then defined as

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}_{\omega}(B,\Psi)=-\frac{1}{8}\int_{\widehat{Y}}(B^{t}-B_{0}^{t})\wedge(F_{B^{t}}+F_{B_{0}^{t}})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widehat{Y}}\langle D_{B}\Psi,\Psi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\widehat{Y}}(B^{t}-B_{0}^{t})\wedge\omega.

For any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{T})
and any relative
spin^{c}\
structure
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\in\Spincr(Y)
, the monopole Floer homology
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
is defined as the Morse homology of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
on the quotient configuration space
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})/\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(\widehat{Y})
. However, it is not guaranteed that
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
descends to a Morse function on
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\widehat{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})/\mathcal{G}_{k+1}(\widehat{Y})
, so an admissible perturbation
\mathfrak{q}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
, which is encoded already in
(P6)
, is needed to make critical points Morse and moduli spaces of flowlines regular. The main result of
[
Wan20b
]
says that the monopole Floer homology

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})

is well-defined as a finitely generated module over the mod
2
Novikov field
\mathcal{R}
. Since we have assumed in
(T3)
that for any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}
, both
\lambda
and
\mu
are non-vanishing on any component
\Sigma^{(i)}\subset\Sigma
, we have a stronger statement:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Wan20b
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

For any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
, the direct sum

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\in\Spincr(Y)}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}),

is finitely generated over
\mathcal{R}
. In particular, the group
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
is non-trivial for only finitely many relative
spin^{c}\
structures.

As explained in
[
Wan20b
, Section 17]
, this Floer homology is further enhanced into a functor:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Wan20b
, Theorem 1.6 & Remark 1.7]
)
.

For any
T
-surfaces
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}
, there is a functor from
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
to the category of finitely generated
\mathcal{R}
-modules
\mathbf{R}(\star)
:

\HM_{*}:\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})\to\mathbf{R}(\star),

which assigns to each 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}
to its monopole Floer homology group
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
.

Remark 3.3
.

In the second paper
[
Wan20b
]
, we focused on connected 3-manifolds with toroidal boundary, but the results generalize to the disconnected case with no difficulty.
∎

For any 2-cell morphism
\mathbb{X}:\mathbb{Y}_{1}\to\mathbb{Y}_{2}
, the cobordism map

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{X}):\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1})\to\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2})

is constructed as follows. We focus on the case when
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}=\emptyset
. For the underlying strict cobordism
X:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
, pick a diffeomorphism
\psi_{X}:W_{X}\to[-1,1]_{t}\times\Sigma
and a closed 2-form
\omega_{X}\in\Omega^{2}(X,i\mathbb{R})
belonging to the class
[\psi_{X}]
and
[\omega_{X}]_{cpt}
respectively, as in
(Q2)
(Q3)
. Choose a metric
g_{X}
of
X
compatible with its corner structures. We attach an end in the spatial direction to obtain a cobordism from
\widehat{Y}_{1}
to
\widehat{Y}_{2}
:

\widehat{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\ \bigcup_{\psi_{X}}\ [-1,1]_{t}\times[0,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma,

and attach cylindrical ends in the time direction to form a complete Riemannian manifold:

\mathcal{X}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(-\infty,-1]_{t}\times\widehat{Y}_{1}\ \bigcup\ \widehat{X}\ \bigcup\ [1,+\infty)_{t}\times\widehat{Y}_{2}.

The closed 2-form
\omega_{X}
extends to a 2-form on
\mathcal{X}
by setting

\omega_{X}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega_{1}&\text{ on }(-\infty,-1]_{t}\times\widehat{Y}_{1},\\
\omega_{2}&\text{ on }[1,+\infty)_{t}\times\widehat{Y}_{2},\\
\mu+ds\wedge\lambda&\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{t}\times[0,\infty)_{s}\times\Sigma.\end{array}\right.

The cobordism map
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{X})
is then defined by counting 0-dimensional solutions (modulo gauge) to the Seiberg-Witten equations (
3.1
) with
\omega_{X}
defined above. Some additional perturbations are required here to make moduli spaces regular; see
[
Wan20b
, Section 16]
for more details. The cobordism map
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{X})
depends only on the isotopy class of
\psi_{X}
and the relative cohomology class of
\omega_{X}
, and is independent of the planar metric
g_{X}
of
X
.

3.3.
The Canonical Grading

By
[
KM07
, Lemma 28.1.1]
, the standard spinor
\Psi_{*}
on
\mathbb{R}_{s}\times\Sigma
determines a canonical oriented 2-plane field
\xi_{*}
that is
\mathbb{R}_{s}
-invariant.

For any 3-manifold
Y
with
\partial Y\cong\Sigma
, an oriented 2-plane field
\xi
is called relative if
\xi=\xi_{*}
near the boundary. Any homotopy of oriented relative 2-plane fields is supposed to preserve
\xi_{*}
near the boundary
\partial Y
.

Inspired by the construction in
[
KM07
, Section 28]
, the author introduced a canonical grading on the group
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
in
[
Wan20b
, Section 18]
. The grading set

\Xi^{\pi}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})

is identified with the homotopy classes of oriented relative 2-plane fields that belongs to the relative
spin^{c}\
structure
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}
.

3.4.
Euler Characteristics

The 3-manifold
Y
is homology oriented, if we pick an orientation of
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{3}H_{i}(Y;\mathbb{R})
. Any homology orientation of
Y
induces a canonical mod 2 grading on
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
(cf.
[
MT96
]
[
Wan20b
, Subsection 18.2]
). Then the graded Euler Characteristics of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
is well-defined and recovers a classical algebraic invariant for 3-manifolds with toroidal boundary. For future reference, we record the statement below:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
MT96
,
Tur98
,
Tau01
]
)
.

The graded Euler Characteristic of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
:

\displaystyle\SW(Y):\Spincr(Y)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi(\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})),

is independent of the auxiliary data
(g_{Y},g_{\Sigma};\omega,\lambda,\mu)
and is equal to the Minor-Turaev torsion
T(Y)
up to an overall sign ambiguity. Moreover, the function
\SW(Y)
is invariant under the conjugacy symmetry:
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\leftrightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}^{*}
.

3.5.
Identity 1-cells

Before we proceed to the construction of the gluing map, let us first define the canonical isomorphism

\iota_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}:\HM_{*}(e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}})\to\mathcal{R}

in Theorem
2.7

(G4)
. By the definition of the identity 1-cell
e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
,
\HM_{*}(e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}})
is computed using the product manifold
\mathbb{R}_{s}\times\Sigma
with
\omega=\mu+ds\wedge\lambda
. As noted earlier, the 3-dimensional Seiberg-Witten equations (
3.2
) have a unique finite energy solution
(B_{*},\Psi_{*})
up to gauge for the standard relative
spin^{c}\
structure
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std}
. Moreover,
(B_{*},\Psi_{*})
is irreducible and non-degenerate as the critical point of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
in the quotient configuration space; see the proof of
[
Wan20b
, Proposition 12.1]
. By
[
Wan20b
, Theorem 2.4]
, any downward gradient flowline of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
connecting
(B_{*},\Psi_{*})
to itself is necessary a constant path. As a result, the monopole Floer chain complex of
(e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std})
is generated by this special solution with trivial differential. The canonical isomorphism
\iota_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
is then defined by sending this generator to
1\in\mathcal{R}
. When
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\neq\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std}
, the 3-dimensional equations (
3.2
) have no solutions at all, so
\HM_{*}(e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})=\{0\}
.

3.6.
The Gluing Map

Having defined the functor
\HM_{*}
in Theorem
2.7

(G1)
and the canonical isomorphism
\iota_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
in
(G4)
, let us construct the gluing map
\alpha
in
(G2)
in this subsection. The idea is borrowed from the proof of Floer’s excision theorem
[
BD95
]
and
[
KM10
, Theorem 3.2]
. We focus on the special case when
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{3}=\emptyset
and construct the map

\alpha:\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2})\to\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1}\circ_{h}\mathbb{Y}_{2}),

for any 1-cells
\mathbb{Y}_{1}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
and
\mathbb{Y}_{2}\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma},\emptyset)

Let

be the underlying 3-manifold of
\mathbb{Y}_{i}
and

\alpha
is induced from an explicit strict cobordism between

\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
are given by strict cobordisms: the induced cobordisms between boundaries are necessarily standard products, as required by Property
(Q2)

For any 1-cells
\mathbb{Y}_{1}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})
and
\mathbb{Y}_{2}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})

(X,W):(Y_{1},\Sigma_{1})\to(Y_{2},\Sigma_{2})

\mathbf{T}_{1}

\mathbf{T}_{1}

.

.

5.
Invariance of Boundary Metrics

For any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
, we defined its monopole Floer homology group in
[
Wan20b
]
; but its invariance is only proved in a weak sense:

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Wan20b
, Remark 1.8 & Corollary 19.10]
)
.

For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}
and any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
, the monopole Floer functor from Theorem
3.2
implies the invariance of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
when

•

we change the cylindrical metric
g_{Y}
in
(P3)
;

•

we replace
\omega
by
\omega+d_{\widehat{Y}}b
for a compactly supported 1-form
b\in\Omega^{1}_{c}(Y,i\mathbb{R})
in
(P4)
;

•

we apply an isotopy to the diffeomorphism
\psi:\partial Y\to\Sigma
.

In this section, we strengthen this invariance result by showing that
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
is independent of the flat metric of
\Sigma
and depends only on the class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(Y;i\mathbb{R})
and
[*_{\Sigma}\lambda]\in H^{1}(\Sigma,i\mathbb{R})
. In particular, the Floer homology
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
is a topological invariant for the triple
(Y,[\omega],[*_{\Sigma}\lambda])
. The main result is as follows:

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}=(\Sigma,g_{i},\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}),i=1,2
have the same underlying oriented surface
\Sigma
and

\displaystyle[*_{1}\lambda_{1}]

\displaystyle=[*_{2}\lambda_{2}]\in H^{1}(\Sigma,i\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle[\mu_{1}]

\displaystyle=[\mu_{2}]\in H^{2}(\Sigma,i\mathbb{R}).

If 1-cells
\mathbb{Y}_{i}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}),i=1,2
have the same underlying 3-manifold
Y_{1}\cong Y_{2}
(this diffeomorphism is compatible with the identification maps
\psi_{i}:\partial Y_{i}\to\Sigma,i=1,2
) and

[\omega_{1}]=[\omega_{2}]\in H^{2}(Y,i\mathbb{R}),

then
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\cong\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
for any relative
spin^{c}\
structure
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\in\Spincr(Y)
.

The proof of Theorem
5.2
is based on the Gluing Theorem
2.7
and a special property concerning the product manifold
[-3,3]_{s}\times\Sigma
:

Lemma 5.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.2
, consider the 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}_{0}\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
with
Y_{0}=[-3,3]_{s}\times\Sigma
and
\psi=\Id:\partial Y_{0}\to(-\Sigma)\cup\Sigma
. Then for any cylindrical metric of
Y_{0}
and any compatible 2-form
\omega_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(Y_{0},i\mathbb{R})
, we have

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{R}&\text{ if }\ \widehat{\mathfrak{s}}=\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std},\\
\{0\}&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

By applying the gluing functor from Theorem
2.7

\alpha:\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})\times\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})\to\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}).

to the pair
(\mathbb{Y}_{1},\mathbb{Y}_{0})
, we deduce from Theorem
3.7
and Lemma
5.3
that

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\cong\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1}\circ_{h}\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}).

for any
\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}\in\Spincr(Y)
. Now
\mathbb{Y}_{1}\circ_{h}\mathbb{Y}_{0}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{2}
are 1-cells in the same strict cobordism category
\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2})
. The difference
\omega_{1}\circ_{h}\omega_{0}-\omega_{2}
determines a class
\beta\in H^{2}(Y,\partial Y;i\mathbb{R})
. Our goal here is to choose
\omega_{0}
properly so that
\beta=0
. This can be always done, because
[\omega_{2}]=[\omega_{1}]=[\omega_{1}\circ_{h}\omega_{0}]
; so
\beta
lies in the image of
H^{1}(\partial Y;i\mathbb{R})
:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\cdots\to H^{1}(\partial Y;i\mathbb{R})\to&H^{2}(Y,\partial Y;i\mathbb{R})&\to H^{2}(Y;i\mathbb{R})\to\cdots\\
&\beta&\mapsto 0.\end{array}

When
\beta=0
, we can then identify
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{1}\circ_{h}\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
with
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{2},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
using Theorem
5.1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
5.3
relies on a computation for the 3-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, which generalizes Lemma
3.8
.

Lemma 5.4
.

For any closed 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},i\mathbb{R})
, suppose that the period class
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-2\pi i[\omega]
is neither negative monotone nor balanced for any
spin^{c}\
structures on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, then

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathfrak{s},c;\mathcal{R}_{\omega})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{R}&\text{ if }c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})=0,\\
\{0\}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

The meaning of Lemma
5.4
will become more transparent when we review the theory of closed 3-manifolds in Section
6
. When
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})\neq 0
, the statement can be verified as in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, by working with a product metric and a harmonic 2-form
\omega
. When
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})=0
, the statement follows from
[
Tau01
, Lemma 3.1]
. Alternatively, we may apply Proposition
6.2
to reduce the problem to the case of exact perturbations.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

With loss of generality, we assume that
\Sigma
is connected. Since the roles of
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}
and
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}
are symmetric, consider a similar 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2},\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1})
. Now regard
\mathbb{Y}_{0}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{0}^{\prime}
as 1-cells in
\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}\cup(-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}))
and
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{2}\cup(-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}),\emptyset)
respectively. We apply Theorem
3.6
to obtain that

(5.1)

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0}^{\prime})\cong\HM_{*}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathfrak{s},c;\mathcal{R}_{\omega})

where
\omega=\omega_{0}\circ_{h}\omega_{0}^{\prime}
is a closed 2-form on the 3-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=S^{1}\times\Sigma
. The condition of Lemma
5.4
can be verified, since

|\langle\omega,[\Sigma]\rangle|<2\pi\text{ and }\neq 0\text{ by properties }\ref{T3}\text{ and }\ref{T1}.

We conclude from (
5.1
) and Lemma
5.4
that

\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0})=\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0}^{\prime})=1.

In particular, the group
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})
vanishes except for one particular relative
spin^{c}\
structure. By Theorem
3.4
, the Euler characteristic
\chi(\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}))
is independent of the metric and the 2-form
\omega_{0}
. We conclude from the computation of
\HM_{*}(e_{\mathbf{\Sigma}_{1}},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std})
that

\chi(\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std}))=1.

Thus
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}_{0},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}_{std})\neq\{0\}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Part III
Monopoles and Thurston Norms

Let
F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}
be a compact oriented surface with
F_{i}
connected. Recall that the norm of
F
is defined to be

x(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\sum_{i}\min\{\chi(F_{i}),0\}.

For any 3-manifold
Y
with toroidal boundary, Thurston
[
Thu86
]
introduced a semi-norm
x(\cdot)
on
H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{R})
such that for any integral class
\kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z})
, we have

x(\kappa)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\{x(F):(F,\partial F)\subset(Y,\partial Y)\text{ properly embedded and }[F]=\kappa\}.

In this part, we show that the monopole Floer homology
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
defined in
[
Wan20b
]
detects the Thurston norm and fiberness of
Y
, if the underlying 3-manifold
Y
is connected and irreducible, generalizing the previous results for closed 3-manifolds
[
KM07
,
Ni08
,
Ni09a
]
. The same detection theorems for link Floer homology have been obtained in
[
OS08
,
Ni09b
,
Juh08
,
GL19
]
and
[
Ni07
]
. The main results are Theorem
7.2
and
7.3
.

For the proof of Theorem
7.2
, we use the Gluing Theorem
2.7
to reduce the problem to the double of
Y
, and apply the non-vanishing result
[
KM07
, Corollary 41.4.3]
for closed 3-manifold. However, we have to adapt this corollary first to the case of non-exact perturbations, which is done in Section
6
.

The proof of Theorem
7.3
is accomplished in Section
9
, which exploits the relation of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
with sutured Floer homology, as discussed in Section
8
. Any 3-manifold with toroidal boundary is a sutured manifold in the sense of Gabai
[
Gab83
]
, but it is not an example of balanced sutured manifolds in the sense of
[
Juh06
, Definition 2.2]
. One may think of our construction as a natural extension of the sutured Floer homology. Although the author was not able to prove a general sutured manifold decomposition theorem, a preliminary result, Theorem
8.1
, will be supplied in Section
8
to justify this heuristic.

6.
Closed 3-Manifolds Revisited

Throughout this section, we will take
Y
to be a closed connected oriented 3-manifold. In
[
KM07
]
, Kronheimer and Mrowka introduced three flavors of monopole Floer homology groups for any
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
Y
, which fit into a long exact sequence:

(6.1)

\cdots\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\widecheck{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)\xrightarrow{j_{*}}\widehat{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)\xrightarrow{p_{*}}\overline{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\cdots

Here
\Gamma
is any local coefficient system on the blown-up configuration space
\mathcal{B}^{\sigma}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
. For instance, one may take
\Gamma
to be the trivial system with
\mathbb{Z}
coefficient. For any real 1-cycle
\xi
, we can also define the local coefficient system
\Gamma_{\xi}
as in
[
KM07
, Section 3.7]
, whose fiber is always
\mathbb{R}
. We write
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\im j_{*}
for the reduced Floer homology. The third group
\overline{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)
in (
6.1
) is trivial if

•

c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})\in H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
is non-torsion, or

•

\Gamma=\Gamma_{\xi}
and
[\xi]\neq 0\in H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{R})
; see
[
KM07
, Proposition 3.9.1]
.

In either case, the map
j_{*}
is an isomorphism, so
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)\cong\widecheck{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)
.

Monopole Floer homology detects the Thurston norm
x(\cdot)
on
H_{2}(Y;\mathbb{R})
. The next theorem expand on what lies behind this slogan:

Theorem 6.1

(
[
KM07
, Corollary 40.1.2 & 41.4.3]
)
.

Let
Y
be a closed oriented 3-manifold with
b_{1}(Y)>0
and
\kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
be any integral class.

(1)

(
The adjunction inequalities
)
For any local coefficient system
\Gamma
and any
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
with
|\langle c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}),\kappa\rangle|>\|\kappa\|_{Th}
, the monopole Floer homology group
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma)
is trivial.

(2)

If in addition
Y
is irreducible, then there is a
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
Y
such that

\langle c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}),\kappa\rangle=\|\kappa\|_{Th}\text{ and }\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma_{\xi})\neq\{0\}

for any 1-cycle
\xi
with
[\xi]\neq 0\in H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{R})
.

In particular, when
[\xi]\neq 0\in H_{1}(Y,\mathbb{R})
, the subset

\{c_{1}(\mathfrak{s}):\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma_{\xi})\neq\{0\}\}\subset H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{R})

determines the Thurston norm on
H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{R})
in the same way that the Newton polytope of the Alexander polynomial determines the Alexander norm; see
[
McM02
]
.

However, Theorem
6.1
is stated only for the monopole Floer homology defined using exact perturbations. In
[
KM07
, Section 30]
, these groups are extended for non-exact perturbations:

(6.2)

\cdots\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\widecheck{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)\xrightarrow{j_{*}}\widehat{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)\xrightarrow{p_{*}}\overline{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\cdots

where
c\in H^{2}(Y;\mathbb{R})
is the period class and
\Gamma
is any
c
-complete local coefficient system in the sense of
[
KM07
, Definition 30.2.2]
. These groups are defined using the Seiberg-Witten equations (
3.2
) on
Y
with the closed 2-form
\omega
belonging to the class
[\omega]=\frac{i}{2\pi}\cdot c\in H^{2}(Y,i\mathbb{R})
.

The purpose of this section is to understand the extent to which Theorem
6.1
generalizes to these groups defined using non-exact perturbations. With that said, the results of this section follow almost trivially from the general theory in
[
KM07
]
; no originality is claimed here.

6.1.
Statements

Fix a
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
Y
. Recall from
[
KM07
, Definition 29.1.1]
that the non-exact perturbation associated to a closed 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(Y,i\mathbb{R})
is called monotone if

2\pi^{2}c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})+c=2\pi^{2}c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})\cdot t

for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-2\pi i[\omega]
is the period class of this perturbation. Furthermore, it is called balanced, positively or negatively monotone if
t=0
,
t>0
or
<0
respectively. We write

\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\im j_{*}\subset\widehat{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)

for the reduced monopole Floer homology. The third group
\overline{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)
in (
6.2
) is always trivial unless the period class
c=c_{b}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-2\pi^{2}c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})
is balanced. Thus
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)=\widehat{\HM}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)
if
c\neq c_{b}
.

We focus on the local coefficient systems
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{B}^{\sigma}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
, whose fiber at every point is always the mod 2 Novikov ring
\mathcal{R}
. The fundamental group of
\mathcal{B}^{\sigma}(Y,\mathfrak{s})
is
H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})=\pi_{0}(\mathcal{G}(Y))
. The monodromy of
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
is then defined by sending
z\in H^{1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
to
q^{f(z)}
with

(6.3)

\displaystyle f(z)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle(2\pi^{2}c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})+c)\cup z,[Y]\rangle\in\mathbb{R}.

For the adjunction inequality, it suffices to consider
spin^{c}\
structures with non-torsion
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})
. The problem already occurs for the 3-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times S^{1}
in Lemma
3.8
. Let
d\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathbb{Z})
be the Poincaré dual of
\{pt\}\times S^{1}
. Suppose
\omega=i\delta\cdot d
and consider the
spin^{c}\
structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with
c_{1}(\mathfrak{s})=2\cdot d
. If
\delta<-2\pi
, then this perturbation is negatively monotone. In this case, the moduli space of the Seiberg-Witten equations (
3.2
) is non-empty, and is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{pt\}
. As one may verify, for either
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
, the monopole Floer homology group
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{T}^{3},\mathfrak{s},c;\Gamma)
is non-trivial, and so the adjunction inequality in Theorem
6.1
is violated in this case.

However, by Proposition
6.2

.

Let

be any closed oriented 3-manifold with

The non-vanishing result is more robust: it suffices to rule out exact perturbations:

Proposition 6.3

Let

be any irreducible closed oriented 3-manifold with

When

If

6.2.
Completions of Chain Complexes

The proof of Proposition
6.2
is achieved in two steps: the first step is to relate the group
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c;\mathcal{R}_{\omega})
with the Floer homology associated to a balanced class
c_{b}=-2\pi^{2}c_{1}(s)
. For monotone perturbations, this is already done in
[
KM07
, Theorem 31.1.1 & 31.5.1]
. We shall describe a slightly different setup that allows generalization for any non-balanced perturbations.

Consider the monopole Floer chain complexes of
(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b})
associated to the local system
\mathcal{R}_{\omega}
:

\widecheck{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega}),\ \widehat{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega}),\ \overline{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega}).

As modules, they are free over the Novikov ring
\mathcal{R}
, but none of them is finitely generated. Since the period class
c_{b}
is balanced, we have to blow up the configuration space in order to define the Floer homology. Suppose an admissible perturbation of the Chern-Simons-Dirac functional
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
is chosen, then each reducible solution
[\alpha]
of the perturbed 3-dimensional Seiberg-Witten equations will contribute infinitely many generators to each of
\widecheck{C}_{*},\widehat{C}_{*},\overline{C}_{*}
, corresponding to the eigenvectors of the Dirac operator at
[\alpha]

As noted in
[
KM07

\mathcal{B}(Y,\mathfrak{s})

[\alpha^{1}],\cdots,[\alpha^{p}]
and label the corresponding critical points in the blown-up space as

with

and
1\leq r\leq p
, so that
[\alpha^{r}_{i}]
corresponds to the eigenvalue
\lambda^{r}_{i}
of the perturbed Dirac operator at
[\alpha^{r}]
and

\cdots<\lambda^{r}_{i}<\lambda^{r}_{i+1}<\cdots,

with
\lambda^{r}_{0}
being the first positive one. For any
m\geq 1

(6.4)

\widehat{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega})_{m}\subset\widehat{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega}),

\overline{C}_{\bullet}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega})\supset\overline{C}_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s},c_{b};\mathcal{R}_{\omega}).

at different critical points using a collection of paths in the blown-down space

Hat02
, Theorem 3A.3]
then provides an injection:

0\to H(\widehat{C}_{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{2}\to H(\widehat{C}_{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{2},\hat{\partial}),

which contradicts the assumption that we start with.

Now consider the tensor product of
(
6.10
)
with
\mathbb{R}
. The Fitting ideal
I_{\mathbb{R}}
of
H(\hat{C}_{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is then the extension of
I
in
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}]
. Since

,
I_{\mathbb{R}}\neq\{0\}
. Using the universal coefficient theorem, we conclude that

H(\hat{C}_{*})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong H(\hat{C}_{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R})

is a torsion
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}]
-module and is therefore annihilated by some polynomial
u(t)\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}]
.

On the one hand, one may pick

such that
g_{\alpha}(u(t))=u(e^{\alpha})\neq 0\in\mathbb{R}
; as a result, the group
H(\widehat{C}_{*}\otimes_{g_{\alpha}}\mathbb{R},\hat{\partial})
is trivial for this particular ring homomorphism
g_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{Z}]\to\mathbb{R}
.

On the other hand, the local coefficient system
\Pi_{\omega}\otimes_{g_{\alpha}}\mathbb{R}
agrees with
\Gamma_{\xi}
for some 1-cycle
\xi
with
[\xi]\neq 0\in H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
, since
[\omega]
is non-balanced. Theorem
6.1
(2) then asserts that
\HM_{*}(Y,\mathfrak{s};\Gamma_{\xi})
is non-trivial. A contradiction.∎

7.
Thurston Norm Detection

From now on, we will always take
Y
to be an oriented 3-manifold with toroidal boundary.

Definition 7.1
.

For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
and any 1-cell
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
, consider the set of monopole classes:

\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}):\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\neq\{0\}\}\subset H^{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z}),

and for any
\kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z})
, define

\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max_{c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}})\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})}\langle c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}),\kappa\rangle,\ \kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z}).

Our convention here is that
\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}\equiv-\infty
, if
\mathfrak{M}(\widehat{Y})=\emptyset
.
∎

When
Y
is connected and irreducible, it is tempting to generalize Theorem
6.1
and relate
\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})
with the Thurston norm on
H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{R})
. However, the author was not able to show that
\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})
is symmetric about the origin; so only a weaker statement is obtained in this paper.

Theorem 7.2
.

For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
, let
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
be any 1-cell with
Y
connected and irreducible. Then the set of monopole classes
\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})
is non-empty and determines the Thurston norm on
H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{R})
in the following sense:

x(\kappa)=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)+\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(-\kappa)),\ \forall\kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z}).

In general, we only have an inequality
\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)+\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(-\kappa)\leq 2x(\kappa)
.

One may approach the more desirable statement

x(\kappa)=\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)=\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(-\kappa).

by either proving the symmetry of
\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{Y})
or the adjunction inequality:

\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)\leq x(\kappa).

But the author was unable to verify either of them directly. This Thurston norm detection result is accompanied with a fiberness detection result:

Theorem 7.3
.

For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
, let
\mathbb{Y}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma})
be any 1-cell with
Y
connected and irreducible, and
\kappa\in H_{2}(Y,\partial Y;\mathbb{Z})
be any integral class. Consider the subgroup

\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}|\kappa)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigoplus_{\langle c_{1}(\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}),\kappa\rangle=\varphi_{\mathbb{Y}}(\kappa)}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y},\widehat{\mathfrak{s}}).

If
\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}|\kappa)=\rank_{\mathcal{R}}\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y}|-\kappa)=1
, then
\kappa
can be represented by a Thurston norm minimizing surface
F
and
Y
fibers over
S^{1}
with
F
as fiber.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
7.2
, while the proof of Theorem
7.3
is deferred to Section
9
, after a digression into sutured monopole Floer homology in Section
8
.

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

We focus on the case when

and reduce the problem to the double of

For any 1-cell

The problem here is that the

This problem is circumvented by changing the 2-form

on

is independent of the metric

if

and

We shall work instead with the orientation reversal

with that of

.

For any
T
-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}

(Reversing the sign of

We verify that the closed 2-form

8.
Relations with Sutured Floer Homology

Before we prove the fiberness detection result, Theorem
7.3
, we add a digression to explain the relations of
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
with the sutured Floer homology
\SHM

[
KM10
]
and
\SFH

[
Juh06
,
Juh08
]
. In the original definition of Gabai
[
Gab83
, Definition 2.6]
, any 3-manifolds with toroidal boundary are sutured manifolds with only toral components. However, they are not examples of balanced sutured manifolds in the sense of Juhász
[
Juh06
, Definition 2.2]
; thus the sutured Floer homology, either
\SHM
or
\SFH
, is never defined for this class of sutured manifolds.

One may regard our construction as a natural extension of the existing sutured Floer theory, and ask if the suture manifold decomposition theorem, e.g.
[
Juh08
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
KM10
, Proposition 6.9]
, continue to hold in our case. In this section, we will only prove a preliminary result towards this direction. Since our Floer homology
\HM_{*}(\mathbb{Y})
also relies on the closed 2-form
\omega
, it is not clear to the author whether a general result is available.

It is worth mentioning that the sutured monopole Floer homology
\SHM
introduced by Kronheimer-Mrowka
[
KM10
]
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
, but the construction extends naturally to the mod 2 Novikov ring
\mathcal{R}
, as explained in
[
Siv12
, Section 2.2]
. We shall work with the latter case.

8.1.

For any

-surface
\mathbf{\Sigma}=(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\lambda,\mu)
and any 1-cell

, we listed a few cohomological conditions on

in
(P4)

(P5)
. In this section, we shall think of them geometrically and work with a more restrictive setup:

Y=[-1,1]_{s}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a product.
∎

10.
Connected Sum Formulae

Having discussed irreducible 3-manifolds with toroidal boundary, we focus on reducible 3-manifolds in this section and derive the connected sum formulae.

10.1.
Connected Sums with 3-Manifolds with Toroidal Boundary

For
i=1,2

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i}
be any

\mathbb{Y}_{i}\in\mathbf{T}(\emptyset,\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i})

Proposition 10.1
.

The monopole Floer homology of

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
, we can compute the group

using a convenient metric on

and a 2-form
\omega_{3}
. Take a component

Let

[\omega_{1}^{\prime}]\#[\omega_{2}^{\prime}]

and
[\omega_{2}^{\prime}]

∎

Scattering and Blow-up for threshold even solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with repulsive delta potential at low frequencies
Stephen Gustafson, Takahisa Inui

Abstract
. We consider the L 2 L^{2} -supercritical nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a repulsive Dirac delta potential in one dimensional space. In a previous work, we clarified the global dynamics of even solutions with the same action as the high-frequency ground state standing wave solutions. In that case, there are obvious non-scattering global solutions, i.e., the standing waves. In this paper, we show a scattering and blow-up dichotomy for threshold even solutions in the low-frequency case. We emphasize that this dichotomy still holds at the critical frequency between high and low.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

We consider the Schrödinger equation with a repulsive Dirac delta potential and a focusing power type nonlinearity in one dimension:

(
\delta
NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+\gamma\delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where
\gamma<0
,
\delta
is the Dirac delta with the mass at the origin, and
p>5
. Though the equation is not scaling invariant, the condition
p>5
means that the nonlinearity is
L^{2}
-supercritical. The Schrödinger operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}-\gamma\delta_{0}
is defined by

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\gamma}f

\displaystyle:=-f^{\prime\prime}\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}):f^{\prime}(0+)-f^{\prime}(0-)=-\gamma f(0)\},

where
f^{\prime}
denotes the spatial derivative of
f
.
The condition
\gamma<0
means that the potential is repulsive. In the repulsive case, it is known that the operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}
is non-negative and self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
2
, Chapter I.3]
) and thus the linear Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}
is unitary on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by the Stone theorem. We may also define the operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}
through the quadratic form

\displaystyle q(f,g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x)\overline{g^{\prime}(x)}dx-\gamma f(0)\overline{g(0)}

for
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
32
]
and
[
44
, Example 10.7]
). It is well-known that the equation (

δ

NLS
) is locally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and the mass and the energy, defined respectively by

\displaystyle M(f)

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}(f)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}|f(0)|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

are conserved.
See
[
15
, Section 3]
,
[
6
, Theorem 3.7.1]
, and
[
14
, Proposition 1]
for the local well-posedness.

Global dynamics of nonlinear Schrödinger equations has attracted a lot of interest after the pioneering work by Kenig and Merle
[
33
]

More recently, dynamics of nonlinear Schrödinger equations with potential have been considered: see

]

The authors have considered the global dynamics of the equation (

δ

NLS
). To explain the known results, we first give some notations and definitions.
For a frequency
\omega>0
, we define the action
S_{\omega,\gamma}
by

\displaystyle S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):=E_{\gamma}(f)+\frac{\omega}{2}M(f)

and the virial functional
K_{\gamma}
by

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}(f):=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}|f(0)|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

The scaling critical regularity index is

s_{c}:=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}.

Definition 1.1

(scattering)
.

We say that a solution
u
scatters in the positive (resp. negative) time direction if the solution exists at least on
[0,\infty)
(resp.
(-\infty,0]
) and there exists
u_{+}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(resp.
u_{-}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
) such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}u_{+(-)}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty\text{ (resp. $-\infty$)}.

We only say that a solution
u
scatters if the solution scatters in both time directions.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

Mizutani
[
39
]
shows that, if a solution scatters in the positive time direction, then there exists
\widetilde{u}_{+}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\widetilde{u}_{+}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty.

This means the solution of the nonlinear equation behaves freely at time infinity.

(2)

For the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation with repulsive Dirac equation, Banica and Visciglia
[
4
]
show that all solutions scatter.

Definition 1.2

(blow-up, grow-up)
.

(1)

We say that a solution
u
blows up in positive time if the solution exists until finite
T>0
and does not extend beyond
T
. By the blow-up alternative, if
u
blows up in positive time, then
\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty
as

goes to maximal positive existence time.

(2)

We say that a solution
u
grows up in positive time if it exists on
[0,\infty)
and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
.

We define blow-up (resp. grow-up) in negative time in a similar way.
We say that a solution
u
blows up if it blows up in both time directions.
We say a solution blows up or grows up if the solution blows or grows up in positive time and blows or grows up in negative time.

1.2.
Main result

We show the following scattering and blow-up dichotomy result at the threshold in the low frequency case, for even solutions:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
0<\omega\leq\gamma^{2}/4
. Assume that
u_{0}\in H_{\mathrm{even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

and

(1)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
, the solution
u
scatters.

(2)

Assume in addition that
xu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
, the solution
u
blows up.

Remark 1.6
.

(1)

The mass-energy conditions

(2)

1.3.
Idea of proof

The basic idea to show the scattering result relies on the work by Duyckaerts, Landoulsi, and Roudenko
[
9
]
(see also
[
38
,
3
,
20
]
).
Their argument is by contradiction, based on concentration compactness and modulation. First of all,
it is enough to consider the case
\omega=1
by a scaling argument.
We suppose there exists a threshold, even, non-scattering solution to (

δ

NLS
) satisfying the mass and energy condition
M(u)=2M(Q)
and
E_{\gamma}(u)=2E(Q)
and
K_{\gamma}(u)>0
.
This solution has a compactness property:
there exists
x:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R=R_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x-x(t)|>R\}\cap\{|x+x(t)|>R\}}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}+|u(t)|^{2}dx<\varepsilon

for any
t>0
. If
\mu_{\gamma}(u):=2\|Q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}>c
for some
c>0
, then we get a contradiction in a similar way to the case below the ground state. Indeed, when we calculate the localized virial identity, the error term can be estimated using the compactness, and can be absorbed by the virial functional
K_{\gamma}
, which does not go to zero by
\mu_{\gamma}(u)>c
.
On the other hand, if
\mu_{\gamma}(u)\to 0
,
we need a more careful modulation argument. In particular, we need to exploit the effect of the repulsive potential. Indeed, if
\gamma=0
, we have a non-scattering solution converging to the ground state (see
[
5
]
). The repulsive effect appears through an estimate like

(1.3)

0\leq\left(1-\frac{2}{|\gamma|}\right)e^{-2y(t)}\lesssim|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|,

where
y(t)
is the translation parameter of the modulation (for
\gamma<-2
; see below for
\gamma=-2
). This shows that if
\mu_{\gamma}(u(t))\to 0
, then
y(t)\to\infty
. On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle|\dot{y}(t)|\lesssim|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|

by the modulation analysis
(this estimate holds in general for NLS and is not influenced by the repulsive potential), implying that
y
is bounded, giving a contradiction.

One difficulty of our problem is that we cannot remove the translation parameter by even symmetry. In the higher dimensional case, radial symmetry removes the translation parameter since the radial Sobolev embedding
H_{\mathrm{rad}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
2<q<2d/(d-2)
and
d\geq 3
, is compact. In one dimension,
however, the embedding is not compact.

A second difficulty is that the scaling of
\dot{H}^{1}
and of the delta interaction are different. Indeed, letting
f_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\alpha}f(\lambda^{\beta}x)
, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{\lambda}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\lambda^{2\alpha+\beta}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},~|f_{\lambda}(0)|^{2}=\lambda^{2\alpha}|f(0)|^{2}.

This means that the virial functional
K_{\gamma}
differs from the functional
\mu_{\gamma}
, which corresponds to the Nehari functional under the mass-energy condition
(while they are equivalent for NLS without potential).
However, we can show that there exists
c>0
such that
|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|\leq c|K_{\gamma}(u)|
under the assumption, by using a variational argument based on more general functionals than the Nehari and virial ones.

A third difficulty is to obtain the repulsive effect from the delta interaction. In the odd case (see
[
20
]
), we use the odd function
Q(x-y)-Q(-x-y)
as the imaginary ground state and we could show an estimate like
e^{-2y}\lesssim|\mu(u)|
. In our problem, the key point to use
Q(|x|-y)
. While it is not smooth, it is more natural than
Q(x-y)+Q(-x-y)
, since the ground state of (

δ

NLS
) is given by
Q(|x|-\xi)
for some
\xi
when
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
(see Remark
1.2
).
In this way, we obtain an estimate like (
1.3
). The
e^{-2y}
here comes from the decay order
e^{-x}
of the ground state
Q(x)
. When
\gamma=-2
,
however, the coefficient of
e^{-2y}
vanishes. In this degenerate case, we need a more careful calculation, using the next order in the decay of
Q
, which is
e^{-px}
. In this way, we get

\displaystyle e^{-(p+1)y}\lesssim|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|,

which allows for a contradiction in the same way as for
\gamma<-2
.

The proof of the blow-up result is based on
the contradiction argument of Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
10
]
. Suppose that
u
is global in the positive time direction. Then, by the virial identity and a Cauchy–Schwarz type inequality, which can be shown by using a Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality related to the delta potential, we know that
u
blows up in finite negative time and satisfies
\int_{t}^{\infty}|\mu_{\gamma}(u(s))|ds\leq Ce^{-ct}
. The estimate implies that
y(t)
converges to a positive constant as
t\to\infty
. This contradicts the divergence of
y
, which follows from
e^{-2y}\lesssim|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|
(or
e^{-(p+1)y}\lesssim|\mu_{\gamma}(u)|
).

1.4.
Notation

For a function
f
, we denote the spatial derivative by
f^{\prime}
and the time derivative by
\dot{f}
.

We set norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}:=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\gamma||f(0)|^{2}+\omega\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

2.1.
Variational structure

We revisit the variational argument not only for the

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\lambda}S_{\omega,\gamma}(e^{\alpha\lambda}f(e^{\beta\lambda}\cdot))|_{\lambda=0}

2.2.
The Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

By the characterization of the ground state
Q_{\omega,0}
, we have the following Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}\leq C_{\omega,0}\|f\|_{H_{\omega,0}^{1}}^{2}

for
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, where

\displaystyle C_{\omega,0}^{-1}=\frac{\|Q_{\omega,0}\|_{H_{\omega,0}^{1}}^{2}}{\|Q_{\omega,0}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}}=\left\{\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}S_{\omega,0}(Q_{\omega,0})\right\}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}=\|Q_{\omega,0}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p-1}

is the best constant and it is attained by
Q_{\omega,0}
.
For the repulsive Dirac delta potential, we have the following inequality.

Lemma 2.4

(The Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality w.r.t. delta potential)
.

Let
0<\omega\leq\gamma^{2}/4
.
For any

, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}\leq 2^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}C_{\omega,0}\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}.

The constant is optimal, but it is not attained.

Proof.

This follows from the minimizing problem:

\displaystyle 2S_{\omega,0}(Q_{\omega,0})=\inf\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):f\in H_{\mathrm{even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},I_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=0\}.

We omit the proof.

∎

When

, we have a different best constant. See
[

]
.

2.5.
Virial identity

Let
u
be a solution to (

δ

NLS
). We define

\displaystyle J(u(t))=J_{\infty}(u(t)):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

Direct calculations give the virial identity:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J(u(t))

\displaystyle=4\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}xu^{\prime}(t,x)\overline{u(t,x)}dx,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J(u(t))

\displaystyle=8K_{\gamma}(u(t)).

Let
\varphi
be an even function in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2}&(|x|<1),\\
0&(|x|>2).\end{cases}

For
R>0
, we define

\displaystyle J_{R}(u(t)):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

Then we have the localized virial identity:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J_{R}(u(t))=2R\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)u^{\prime}(t,x)\overline{u(t,x)}dx

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J_{R}(u(t)):=8K_{\gamma}(u(t))+A_{R}(u(t)),

where

\displaystyle A_{R}(u(t))

\displaystyle:=-4\int_{|x|>R}\left\{2-\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\right\}\left\{|u^{\prime}(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\right\}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{R<|x|<2R}\varphi^{(4)}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

We set

\displaystyle F_{R}(f):=8K_{\gamma}(f)+A_{R}(f)

for
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We set
F_{\infty}(f):=8K_{\gamma}(f)
.

Lemma 2.10
.

For any
y>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{R}(e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y))=0

and so

\displaystyle F_{R}(e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y))=8K_{\gamma}(e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y))=F_{\infty}(e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y)).

Proof.

Let
v(t):=e^{it}e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y)
. Then the function
v
satisfies

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{2}v+\gamma_{y}\delta v+|v|^{p-1}v=0,

where
\gamma_{y}:=-2Q^{\prime}(-y)/Q(-y)
(in fact
v
is the ground state soliton of (

δ

NLS
) with
\gamma=\gamma_{y}\in(-2,0)
).
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}J_{R}(v(t))=2R\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)Q^{\prime}(|x|-y)Q(|x|-y)dx=0.

This shows that

\displaystyle F_{R}^{y}(v):=A_{R}(v)+8K_{\gamma_{y}}(v)=0.

By the Pohozaev identity for
Q(|\cdot|-y)
, we see that
K_{\gamma_{y}}(v)=0
, which implies that
A_{R}(v)=0
(noting that
A_{R}
is independent of the delta potential),
and the lemma follows.
∎

3.
Modulation argument

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\mu_{0}>0
and a function
\varepsilon:(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that the following holds. For any
\mu<\mu_{0}
and for all
f\in H_{\mathrm{even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E_{\gamma}(f)=2E(Q)
,
M(f)=2M(Q)
and
\mu_{\gamma}(f)<\mu
, there exist
(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|f-e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y)\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\mu).

Proof.

We use a contradiction argument. We suppose that the statement fails.
Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
\mu_{n}
with
\mu_{n}\to 0
and
f_{n}\in H_{\mathrm{even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E_{\gamma}(f_{n})=2E(Q)
,
M(f_{n})=2M(Q)
and
\mu(f_{n})<\mu_{n}
such that
\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\inf_{y\geq 0}\|f_{n}-e^{i\theta}Q(|\cdot|-y)\|_{H^{1}}>\varepsilon_{0}
.
Since
\mu_{\gamma}(f_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
E_{\gamma}(f_{n})=2E(Q)
, we have
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\to 2\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}
and thus

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}(f_{n})\to 2S(Q)\text{ and }I_{\gamma}(f_{n})\to 2I(Q)=0.

By Fukuizumi and Jeanjean
[
13
, Lemma 20]
, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-e^{i\theta_{n}}(Q(\cdot-y_{n})+Q(-\cdot-y_{n}))\|_{H^{1}}\to 0,

where
y_{n}\to\infty
.
Now

\displaystyle\|(Q(\cdot-y_{n})+Q(-\cdot-y_{n}))-Q(|\cdot|-y_{n})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\|Q(-\cdot-y_{n})\|_{H^{1}(0,\infty)}+\|Q(\cdot-y_{n})\|_{H^{1}(-\infty,0)}

\displaystyle\to 0.

This is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Modulation)
.

Let
R>0
be sufficiently large.
There exist
\mu_{0}>0
and a function
\varepsilon:(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that the following holds. For any
\mu<\mu_{0}
and for all
f\in H_{\mathrm{even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E(f)=2E(Q)
,
M(f)=2M(Q)
and
\mu(f)<\mu
, there exist
(\tilde{\theta},y)\in\mathbb{R}\times(2R,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}f-Q(|\cdot|-y)\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon(\mu)

and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\chi_{R}^{+}(x)Q(x-y)dx=0,\quad\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}(x)Q(x-y))dx=0,

where
g=e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}f-Q(|\cdot|-y)
.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle J(\tilde{\theta},y,v)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}J_{1}(\tilde{\theta},y,v)\\
J_{2}(\tilde{\theta},y,v)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}v-Q(|\cdot|-y))\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx\\
\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}v-Q(|\cdot|-y))\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx\end{pmatrix}

for
\tilde{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
,

,

. Then
J(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))=0
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J_{1}}{\partial\tilde{\theta}}(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(|x|-y)\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{2}dx+O(e^{-2y})

\displaystyle=-\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(e^{-4R})

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J_{2}}{\partial y}(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{\prime}(|x|-y)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(R^{-1}+e^{-4R}).

We also have
\frac{\partial J_{1}}{\partial y}(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))=\frac{\partial J_{2}}{\partial\tilde{\theta}}(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))=0
.
These imply that the Jacobian of
J
near
(0,y,Q(|\cdot|-y))

4.
Modulation parameters

4.1.
Properties of the ground state

To give estimates for the modulation parameters
\rho,y
and the remainder terms
g,h
, the function
\mathcal{Q}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(y)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q^{\prime}(x-y)|^{2}dx-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q(x-y)|^{2}dx+\frac{|\gamma|}{2}Q(-y)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2|\gamma|}\{|\gamma|Q(-y)-2Q^{\prime}(-y)\}^{2}

plays an important role. In this subsection, we compute its function large-
y
asymptotics.
The ground state
Q
is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle Q(x)=c_{p}\left\{2\cosh\left(\frac{p-1}{2}x\right)\right\}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}=c_{p}\{e^{\alpha x}+e^{-\alpha x}\}^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where
c_{p}:=\left(2(p+1)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
and
\alpha=(p-1)/2
.
By Taylor expansion, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle Q(x)=c_{p}e^{-|x|}-\frac{c_{p}}{\alpha}e^{-p|x|}+o(e^{-p|x|}),

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(x)=-c_{p}\frac{x}{|x|}e^{-|x|}+\frac{pc_{p}}{\alpha}\frac{x}{|x|}e^{-p|x|}+o(e^{-p|x|}).

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q^{\prime}(x-y)|^{2}dx=-\frac{c_{p}^{2}}{2}e^{-2y}+\frac{2pc_{p}^{2}}{\alpha(p+1)}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Proof.

By the Taylor expansion (
4.3
) of
Q^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q^{\prime}(x-y)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|-c_{p}e^{x-y}+\frac{pc_{p}}{\alpha}e^{p(x-y)}|^{2}dx+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-c_{p}e^{x-y})^{2}+2(-c_{p}e^{x-y})\frac{pc_{p}}{\alpha}e^{p(x-y)}dx+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=-c_{p}^{2}e^{-2y}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{2x}dx+2\frac{pc_{p}^{2}}{\alpha}e^{-(p+1)y}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(p+1)x}dx+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{p}^{2}}{2}e^{-2y}+\frac{2pc_{p}^{2}}{\alpha(p+1)}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q(x-y)|^{2}dx=-\frac{c_{p}^{2}}{2}e^{-2y}+\frac{2c_{p}^{2}}{\alpha(p+1)}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Proof.

Using the Taylor expansion (
4.2
) of
Q
, this is a similar calculation to Lemma
4.1
.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{|\gamma|}{2}Q(-y)^{2}=\frac{|\gamma|}{2}c_{p}^{2}e^{-2y}-\frac{|\gamma|c_{p}^{2}}{\alpha}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of (
4.2
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2|\gamma|}\{|\gamma|Q(-y)-2Q^{\prime}(-y)\}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2|\gamma|}(|\gamma|-2)^{2}c_{p}^{2}e^{-2y}-\frac{|\gamma|-2}{|\gamma|}\frac{2p-|\gamma|}{\alpha}c_{p}^{2}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Proof.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.3
),
we have

\displaystyle\{|\gamma|Q(-y)-2Q^{\prime}(-y)\}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{|\gamma|\left(c_{p}e^{-y}-\frac{c_{p}}{\alpha}e^{-py}\right)-2\left(c_{p}e^{-y}-\frac{pc_{p}}{\alpha}e^{-py}\right)\right\}^{2}+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=\left\{(|\gamma|-2)c_{p}e^{-y}+\frac{2p-|\gamma|}{\alpha}c_{p}e^{-py}\right\}^{2}+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=(|\gamma|-2)^{2}c_{p}^{2}e^{-2y}+2(|\gamma|-2)\frac{2p-|\gamma|}{\alpha}c_{p}^{2}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

and ther result follows.
∎

Combining all the above estimates, we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(y)=\left(1-\frac{2}{|\gamma|}\right)c_{p}^{2}e^{-2y}-\frac{4p(|\gamma|-2)}{|\gamma|(p-1)}c_{p}^{2}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Remark 4.1
.

So when
\gamma<-2
, the leading term of
\mathcal{Q}
is

with positive coefficient. When
\gamma=-2
, we will simply regard
\mathcal{Q}
as an error term with
\mathcal{Q}=o(e^{-(p+1)y})
.

For
\gamma=-2
, the following estimate provides our main term:

Lemma 4.6
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q(x-y)|^{p+1}dx=\frac{4c_{p}^{2}}{(p+1)}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|Q(x-y)|^{p+1}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}|c_{p}e^{x-y}|^{p+1}dx+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{p}^{p+1}}{p+1}e^{-(p+1)y}\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{(p+1)x}dx+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{p}^{p+1}}{(p+1)^{2}}e^{-(p+1)y}+o(e^{-(p+1)y})

Since
c_{p}=\{2(p+1)\}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}
we have

\displaystyle c_{p}^{p+1}=c_{p}^{2}c_{p}^{p-1}=2(p+1)c_{p}^{2}.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle\frac{2c_{p}^{p+1}}{(p+1)^{2}}=\frac{4(p+1)c_{p}^{2}}{(p+1)^{2}}=\frac{4c_{p}^{2}}{(p+1)}.

∎

\displaystyle=\langle S_{(0,\infty)}^{\prime}(Q(\cdot-y),g\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle S_{(0,\infty)}^{\prime\prime}(Q(\cdot-y))g,g\rangle+o(\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Lemma 4.13

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\infty}Q(x-y)^{p-1}(p\chi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}^{c}(x)|h_{1}(x)|^{2}+\chi_{R}(x)\chi_{R}^{c}|h_{2}(x)|^{2})dx

Abstract
. Let G G be a Lie group acting properly on a smooth manifold M M .If M / G M/G is connected, then we exhibit some simple and basic constructions for proper actions. In particular, we prove that the reduction principle in compact transformation groups holds for proper actions. As an application, we prove that a reduction principle holds for polar actions and for the integral invariant for isometric actions of Lie groups, called copolarity, which measures how far from being polar the action is. We also investigate symplectic actions. Hence we assume that ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) is a symplectic manifold and the G G action on M M preserves ω \omega . The main result is the Equivalence Theorem for coisotropic actions, generalizing [ 25 , Theorem 3 p.267] . Finally, we completely characterize asystatic actions generalizing results proved in [ 17 ] .

Let

G

For any

G\cdot x:=\{gx:\,g\in G\}
and the stabilizer or the isotropy subgroup of the
G

which is clearly a closed subgroup, and so a Lie group
[
13

If

, respectively

The relation

Let

It is easy to check that

2.
Global
G
manifold Reduction

Let
G
be a Lie group acting properly on
M
. We always assume that
M
is a paracompact, second countable Hausdorff space and
M/G
is connected. Hence
M

has different many connected components and
G
acts transitively on the set of the connected components of
M
. In particular, different connected components have the same dimension. In this section, we always refer to
[
8
,
13
]
.

If
G
acts properly on
M
then the stabilizer subgroups are compact and the
G
orbits are embedded closed submanifolds. The two foundational results for proper actions are the Slice Theorem
[
8
, Slice Theorem 3.49]
and the Tubular Neighborhood Theorem
[
8
, Tubular Neighborhood Theorem 3.5]
.

Let
x\in M
. Since
G_{x}
is compact, there exists a
G_{x}
invariant Riemannian metric
\mathtt{l}
on
M
. This metric for instance is defined by averaging the
G_{x}
action, that is,

\mathtt{l}(X,Y)(p):=\int_{G_{x}}\langle(\mathrm{d}g)_{p}(X),(\mathrm{d}g)_{p}(Y)\rangle(gp)\,\tilde{\omega},

where
\tilde{\omega}
is a right invariant volume form on
G_{x}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is an arbitrary Riemannian metric on
M
.
Let
(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal of
T_{x}G\cdot x
with respect to
\mathtt{l}
. Then

T_{x}M=T_{x}G\cdot x\oplus(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}

is a
G_{x}
orthogonal splitting. The Slice Theorem proves that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\mathcal{V}_{x}=\exp_{x}(B_{\epsilon}\cap(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp})
is a submanifold of
M
satisfying the following conditions:

•

\mathcal{V}_{x}
is
G_{x}
invariant;

•

G\mathcal{V}_{x}
is an open subset of
M
;

•

if
g\in G
and
y\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
are such that
gy\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
, then
g\in G_{x}
.

A
G_{x}
invariant submanifold of
M
satisfying the above conditions is called a slice at
x
for the
G
action on
M
.

Let
x\in M
and let
G_{x}
be the stabilizer of
x
. The isotropy representation is the natural orthogonal representation of
G_{x}
on the tangent space
T_{x}M
given by

G_{x}\longrightarrow\mathrm{O}(T_{x}M),\qquad g\mapsto\mathrm{(}dg)_{x}.

If
M
is connected, then the kernel of the isotropy representation coincides with the kernel of the homomorphism
A:G\longrightarrow\mathrm{Diff}(M)
.
The isotropy representation preserves the orthogonal splitting
T_{x}M=T_{x}G\cdot x\oplus(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}
.
The restriction of the isotropy representation to
(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}
, i.e.,

\rho:G_{x}\longrightarrow\mathrm{O}((T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}),\,g\mapsto(\mathrm{d}g_{x})_{|_{(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}}},

is called the
slice representation
.

The compact Lie group
G_{x}
acts on
G\cdot x\times(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}
as follows

h(g,v):=(gh^{-1},hv).

This action is free and proper. Hence its orbit space denoted by
E=G\times(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}/G_{x}=G\times_{G_{x}}(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}

is an homogenous vector bundle associated to the principal bundle
G\longrightarrow G/G_{x}

[
13
]
. The group
G
acts on
E
as follows:

h[g,v]:=[hg,v]
. Since
G\cdot[e,v]\cap(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}=G_{x}\cdot v
the orbit space

E/G
is homeomorphic to the orbit space
(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}/G_{x}
. The Tubular Neighborhood Theorem proves the existence of a
G
-invariant neighborhood of
G\cdot x
which is
G
equivariant diffeomorphic to
E
.
Therefore, the slice representation determines a
G
equivariant neighborhood of the orbit
G\cdot x
. Moreover, the Slice Theorem and the Tubular Neighborhood Theorem imply that the orbit space
M/G
is Hausdorff, locally compact, paracompact
and second countable.

Let

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be an open covering of
M
of
G
invariant subset. The Tubular Neighborhood Theorem, in combination with the paracompactness of
M/G
, proves there exists a
G
-invariant partition of unity

\{f_{j}:M\longrightarrow\text{$\mathbb{R}$}\}_{j\in J}
subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
. This means each
f_{j}
is a smooth
G
-invariant function,
f_{j}\geq 0
and the support of
f_{j}
is contained in some
U_{\alpha(j)}\in\mathcal{U}
. Moreover,
\{\mathrm{supp}f_{j}/G\}_{j\in J}
form a locally finite family of compact subsets of
M/G
, and
\sum_{j\in J}f_{j}=1
on
M/G
. As an application we give the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acting properly on
M
. If

\mathtt{g}

G\subset\mathrm{Iso}(M,\mathtt{g})

Proof.

The existence of a
G

[
8
]
. A Theorem due to Nomizu and Ozeki
[

is complete. Hence, given any Riemannian metric

there exists a complete Riemannian metric
\mathtt{g}
which is conformal to
\mathtt{h}

In any connected component of
M
, the Riemannian metric
\mathtt{h}
defines a distance function
d
. Since
M
could have many different connected components, we define

whenever
x

We define

r(x)
the supremum of the positive real number
r
such that the open ball
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in M:\,d(x,y)<r\}
is relative compact. If
r(x)=+\infty
for some
x\in M
, then any open ball in
M^{\prime}
, the connected component of
x
, is relative compact. By the Hopf Rinow Theorem
(M^{\prime},\mathtt{h})
is a complete Riemannian manifold
[
16
]
. Since
G
acts transitively on the set of the connected components of
M
, it follows that
(M,\mathtt{h})
is a complete Riemannian manifold. Hence, we may assume
r(x)<+\infty
for any
x\in M
.

Let
x,y\in M
be such that
y\in B_{r(x)}(x)
. Then
r(y)\geq r(x)+d(x,y)
and so

|r(x)-r(y)|\leq d(x,y).

Nomizu and Ozeki proved that the Riemannian metric
\mathtt{g}=f^{2}\mathtt{h}
is complete if the smooth function
f:M\longrightarrow\text{$\mathbb{R}$}
satisfies
f(x)\geq\frac{1}{r(x)}
for any
x\in M
. Indeed, if we denote by
d^{\prime}
the distance induced from
\mathtt{g}
, then

\{y\in M:\,d^{\prime}(x,y)<1/3\}\subset\{y\in M:\,d(x,y)\leq\frac{r(x)}{2}\}.

for any
x\in M
.
This implies that any open ball of radius
1/3
with respect to
\mathtt{g}
is relative compact. As a consequence, any Cauchy sequence with respect to
d^{\prime}
admits a convergent subsequence and so
(M,d^{\prime})
is a complete metric space. By a classical result due to Hopf-Rinow,
(M,f^{2}\mathtt{g})
is a complete Riemannian manifold
[
16
]
.

Since
\mathtt{h}
is
G
invariant it follows that the function
r
is a
G
invariant continuous function. Our result follows form the existence of a
G
-invariant function
f:M\longrightarrow\text{$\mathbb{R}$}
such that
f(x)\geq\frac{1}{r(x)}
for any
x\in M
.

Let
x\in M
. By the Tubular Neighborhood Theorem, we may think a

G
-invariant neighborhood of
G\cdot x
as the homogeneous vector bundle
G\times_{G_{x}}\mathcal{N}_{x}
.
Let
B_{n}=\{v\in\mathcal{N}_{x}:\,\parallel v\parallel<n\}
.
The open covering
\mathcal{U}=\{GB_{n}\}_{n\in\text{$\mathbb{N}$}}
of
G\times_{G_{x}}\mathcal{N}_{x}
is
G
-invariant. Let
\{h_{i}:G\times_{G_{x}}\mathcal{N}_{x}\longrightarrow[0,1]\}_{i\in I}
be a

G
-invariant partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
. The function

\tilde{f}=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{h_{n(i)}}{r_{n(i)}},

where
n(i)\in\text{$\mathbb{N}$}
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,h_{i}\subset GB_{i(n)}
and
r_{n}=\mathrm{inf}_{z\in GB_{n}}r
,
satisfies
\tilde{f}\geq\frac{1}{r}
. Now, using the
G
-invariant partition of unity subordinated to
\{G\times_{G_{x}}\mathcal{N}_{x}\}_{x\in M}
,
one may construct a
G
invariant smooth function
f
satisfying

f(y)\geq\frac{1}{r(y)},

for any
y\in M
, concluding the proof.
∎

From now on we always assume that

is a complete Riemannian manifold and

is a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Iso}(M,\mathtt{g})
. We denote by

[
16
]
, keeping in mind that

whenever

and

Let

denote the connected component of the identity of
G
. It is well known that
G^{o}
is a closed normal subgroup of
G

[
13
]
. Hence
G^{o}
acts properly on
M
.
For any
g\in G
, the diffeomorphism

maps any connected component
C
of

diffeomorphically onto a connected component
C^{\prime}
of
M
. Furthermore, if
g\in G^{o}
then

. Hence we get an action of

on the discrete set of the connected components of
M
.

Let
C
be a connected component of
M
. Since
\pi
is an open map, it follows that
\pi(C)
is a connected component of
M/G
. Viceversa, if
\overline{C}
is a connected component of
M/G
, then there exists a connected component
C
of
M
such that
\pi(C)=\overline{C}
and
\pi^{-1}(\overline{C})=G/G^{o}\cdot C
. From now on, we assume that
M/G
is connected. In particular, the group
G/G^{o}
acts transitively on the set of connected components of
M
.

Let
\pi:M\longrightarrow M/G
be the natural projection. If
M
is a connected compact manifold, and so
G
is a compact Lie group, then the orbit space
M/G
is endowed by
the so called orbital metric
[
20
]
. We point out that this fact holds in our setting.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p,q\in M
. There exists a minimizing geodesic
\gamma:[0,r]\longrightarrow M
such that
\gamma(0)\in G\cdot p
,
\gamma(r)\in G\cdot q
and satisfying

L(\gamma)=d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)=\mathrm{inf}\left\{d(gp,g^{\prime}q):\,g,g^{\prime}\in G\right\}.

Moreover,
G_{\gamma(t)}=G_{\gamma}
for any
t\in(0,r)
and
G_{\gamma(0)}\supseteq G_{\gamma}\subseteq G_{\gamma(r)}
.

Proof.

Since
G
acts transitively on the set of connected components of
M
, it follows that
d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)<+\infty
. Let
G^{\prime}
be a connected component of
G
. Then
G^{\prime}=hG^{o}
for some
h\in G
. Therefore, keeping in mind that
G^{o}
is a normal subgroup of
G
, we have

G^{\prime}\cdot p=hG^{o}\cdot p=G^{o}\cdot hp.

This means that there exists a subset
\mathcal{W}\subset G
such that

G\cdot p=\bigsqcup_{h\in\mathcal{W}}G^{o}\cdot hp.

We point out that
\mathcal{W}
depends only to the connected component of
M
containing
p
. Hence, if
p,q
belong to the same connected component, then

d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)=d(G^{o}\cdot p,G^{o}\cdot p)=d(G^{o}\cdot hp,G^{o}\cdot hq),

for any
h\in\mathcal{W}
.

Let
x_{n}\in G\cdot x
and
y_{n}\in G\cdot q
be such that

\lim_{n\mapsto+\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)=r.

Since
G
acts isometrically, we may assume that
x_{n}=p
. By the above discussion
y_{n}
lies in the same connected component of
p
. By the Hopf-Rinow Theorem there exists a minimizing geodesic
\gamma_{n}
, parameterizing with the arc length, joining
p
and
y_{n}
.
The geodesic
\gamma_{n}:[0,t_{n}]\longrightarrow M
is given by
\gamma_{n}(t)=\exp_{p}(t\xi_{n})
, where
\parallel\xi_{n}\parallel=1
and
\gamma(t_{n})=y_{n}
. In particular,
L(\gamma_{n})=t_{n}
and

\lim_{n\mapsto+\infty}t_{n}=d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)=r
. Up to pass to a subsequence, we may assume that the sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\text{$\mathbb{N}$}}
converges to some unit vector
v\in T_{p}M
. Now, keeping in mind that
G\cdot q
is closed, it is easy to check that the geodesic
\gamma(t):=\exp_{p}(tv)
is a minimizing geodesic joining

p
and
\gamma(r)\in G\cdot q
and satisfying
L(\gamma)=d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)
.

The last part is a simply but useful fact observed by Kleiner in his PhD thesis
[
27
]
, see also
[
8
]
. For sake of completeness we give the proof.

Let
t\in(0,r)
and let
g\in G_{\gamma(t)}
. The curve

\tilde{\gamma}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}g\gamma(s)&0\leq s\leq t\\
\gamma(s)&t<s\leq r\end{array}\right.

joins
g(\gamma(0))
and
\gamma(r)
. Since
L(\tilde{\gamma})=L(\gamma)=d(G\cdot p,G\cdot q)
it follows that
\tilde{\gamma}
is a minimizing geodesic and so smooth. This implies
(\mathrm{d}g)_{\gamma(t)}(\dot{\gamma}(t))=\dot{\gamma}(t)
. Since
g(\gamma(t))=\gamma(t)
, it follows
g(\gamma(s))=\gamma(s)
for any
s\in[0,r]
concluding the proof.
∎

Let

Proposition 2.4

The function

By Lemma

Let

, we may assume that

∎

Theorem 2.5

The metric space

Proof.

Let
[p]\in M/G

Let

Let

Proposition 2.6

Let

be Lie group acting properly and isometrically on

M/G
is connected. The

is called

if there exists a connected immersed submanifold
\Sigma
of

which intersects all the orbits and is orthogonal to the

[

,

there exists a

(M,\mathtt{g})
is polar.

Since

is connected, the
G

. The following Lemma is easy to check.

Lemma 3.1
.

The
G
action on

is polar if and only if the
G^{o}
action on
M
is polar and so if and only if the
G^{o}
action on a connected component, and so on any, is polar.

Let
H
be a principal isotropy. Let
L=N(H)/H
. We are ready to prove a reduction principle for polar action. We begin with the following Lemma. A proof is given in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x\in M
and let
\mathcal{V}_{x}=\exp_{x}((T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp})
. Then

•

\mathcal{V}_{x}
meets all the orbits of
G
;

•

if the

action is polar and

, then
\Sigma=\mathcal{V}_{x}
is the unique section through the point

.

acts polarly on

if and only if

acts polarly on
\small{c}M
.

Proof.

Assume that the

, there exists a unique section through

and so the

Vice-versa, assume that the

is polar. Let

\mathcal{V}_{x}
is the unique section through

which meets any

. Let

4.
Symplectic Actions

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold. Hence
\omega
is a nondegenerate closed two form. We do not assume that
M
is connected but it possibly
has many different connected components of the same dimension. Symplectic manifolds are even dimensional. If
\dim M=2n
and
M
is compact and connected, then
\underbrace{\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega}_{n}
is a volume form. A symplectomorphism of
M
is a diffeomorphism
h:M\longrightarrow M
such that
h^{*}\omega=\omega
. The set of all symplectomorphisms of
M
is denoted by

5.
Hamiltonian Actions

Let
G
be a Lie group acting properly on
(M,\omega)
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that the
G
action is Hamiltonian if there exists a map
\mu:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the dual of
\mathfrak{g}
, called
momentum map
and satisfying:

•

for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, let

\mu^{\xi}:M\longrightarrow\text{$\mathbb{R}$},\,p\mapsto\mu(p)(\xi)
.
Then
\it{i}_{\xi_{M}}\omega=\mathrm{d}\mu^{\xi}
;

•

\mu(gx)=\mathrm{Ad}^{*}(g)(\mu(x))
, for any
x\in M
and any
g\in G
, where
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}(g):=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})^{*}
. Hence, the momentum map interchanges the
G
action on
M
and the coadjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
x\in M
. Then the following facts hold true:

(i)

\mathrm{Ker}\,{\rm d}\mu_{x}=(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\omega}
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{x}=\{\phi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\,:\,\phi(v)=0,\,\mbox{ for all }v\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}\}
. Thus,
\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}
is surjective if and only if
\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\{0\}
;

(iii)

\omega{|_{G\cdot x}}=\bigl(\mu^{*}\omega_{(G\cdot\mu(x))}\bigr){|_{G\cdot x}}
. Hence the restriction of the symplectic form
\omega
to
G\cdot x
coincides with the pull-back by the momentum map
\mu
of the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau symplectic form defined on the coadjoint orbit through
\mu(x)
.

(iv)

T_{x}(G\cdot x)\cap(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\omega}=T_{x}G_{\mu(x)}\cdot x
;

(v)

\dim G_{\mu(x)}-\dim G_{x}=\dim\bigl(T_{x}{G}\cdot x\,\cap\,(T_{x}{G}\cdot x)^{\omega}\bigr);

Proof.

v\in\mathrm{Ker}\,{\rm d}\mu_{x}
if and only if for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have
\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}^{\xi}=0
. Since
\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}^{\xi}=i_{\xi_{M}(x)}\omega
, it follows that
v\in{Ker}\,{\rm d}\mu_{x}
if and only if
v\in(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\omega}
.

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}
and let
v\in T_{x}M
. Then
\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}(v)(\xi)=i_{\xi_{M}(x)}\omega(v)=0
. Hence
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{o}_{x}
. On the other hand

\dim\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=\dim M-\dim\mathrm{Ker}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}=\dim M-(\dim M-\dim G+\dim G_{x})=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{o},

and so
[(ii)]
holds by dimensional reason.

Let
\xi,\nu\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\mu(\exp(t\xi)x)(\nu)=\mathrm{Ad}^{*}(\exp(t\xi))(\mu(x))(\nu),

and so

\mathrm{d}\mu_{x}^{\nu}(\xi_{M}(x))=\omega(\nu_{M}(x),\xi_{M}(x))=\mu(x)([\nu,\xi])=\omega_{G\cdot\mu(x)}(\nu_{G\cdot\mu(x)}(x),\xi_{G\cdot\mu(x)}(x)).

Therefore the symplectic form
\omega
to
G\cdot x
coincides with the pull-back by the momentum map
\mu
of the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau symplectic form defined on the coadjoint orbit through
\mu(x)

[
22
]
. In particular

T_{x}(G\cdot x)\cap(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\omega}=T_{x}G_{\mu(x)}\cdot x,

and so
\dim G_{\mu(x)}-\dim G_{x}=\dim\bigl(T_{x}{G}\cdot x\,\cap\,(T_{x}{G}\cdot x)^{\omega}\bigr)
, concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acting in a Hamiltonian fashion on
(M,\omega)
. If
G^{o}
is Abelian, then any

The momentum map gives a criterion for a

Proposition 5.4
.

Let

. Then

(1)

is symplectic if and only if
(G_{x})^{o}=G_{\mu(x)}^{o}
and so if and only if the restricted momentum map

\mu:G\cdot x\longrightarrow G\cdot\mu(x)

(2)

then

is coisotropic
if and only if

\dim G_{\mu(x)}-\dim G_{x}=
cohom
(G,M)
;

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2

(iv)
, keeping in mind that
\mu
is
G
-equivariant, it follows that

is symplectic if and only if
\mathfrak{g}_{x}=\mathfrak{g}_{\mu(x)}
and so
if and only if

and hence if and only if

Let
x\in M
be such that

is a principal orbit. By Lemma

is an orthogonal splitting. Hence it is enough to prove that

X\mathtt{h}(\xi_{M},\xi_{M})=0.

Since
\Theta
is a local diffeomorphism, we can extend
X
in a neighborhood
U
of
z
in
M
satisfying the following condition: if
Z
is a vector field defined on
U
with

Z(y)\in T_{y}G\cdot y
, then
[X,Z]=0
.

Let
Y=-J(\xi_{M})
. Then

\begin{split}0=\mathrm{d}\omega(X,Y,\xi_{M})&=X\omega(Y,\xi_{M})+Y\omega(\xi_{M},X)+\xi_{M}\omega(X,Y)\\
&-\omega([X,Y],\xi_{M})-\omega([Y,\xi_{M}],X)-\omega([\xi_{M},X],Z).\end{split}

Therefore, keeping in mind that any
G
orbit is almost complex,
(T_{z}G\cdot z)^{\omega}=T_{z}F
for any
z\in F
and
[X,\xi_{M}]=[X,Y]=0
, it follows that

0=X\omega(-J\xi_{M},\xi_{M})=X\mathtt{h}(\xi_{M},\xi_{M}).

Let
G_{F}=\{g\in G:\,gF=F\}
. Since
F
is a connected component of
M^{H}
, keeping in mind
H
is the principal isotropy, it follows that
G_{F}\subset N(H)
and
G_{F}^{o}=N(H)^{o}
. This implies that
G_{F}/H
is a finite group.

Let
L=G_{F}/H
.
The map

Hn(gH,x):=(gn^{-1}H,nx),

defines an action of
L
on
G/H\times F
. Here we think
L
as the right cosets. It is easy to check that
L
action on
G/H\times F
is free. Therefore
G/H\times_{L}F:=G/H\times F/L
is a manifold. Note that

\Theta
descends to a smooth map on
G/H\times_{L}F
. We claim that
\Theta:G/H\times_{L}F\longrightarrow M
is a diffeomorphism onto. Since
\Theta:G/H\times F\longrightarrow M
is a local diffeomorphism onto, It is enough to prove that
\Theta
is injective.

Assume

\Theta(gH,x)=\Theta(g^{\prime}H,y)
. Then
gx=g^{\prime}y
and so

g^{\prime-1}gx=y
. Let
s=g^{\prime-1}g
. Since
\mu(x)=\mu(y)=\beta
, it follows that
s\in G_{\beta}
. Since
G_{\beta}
preserves
F
and
sx=y
, it follows
sF=F
. This implies that
s\in G_{F}
and so

[gH,x]=[g^{\prime}sH,x]=[g^{\prime}H,s^{-1}x]=[g^{\prime}H,y],

proving that
\Theta:G/H\times_{L}F\longrightarrow M
is injective. Note that we have also proved that
\Theta:G/H\times F\longrightarrow M
is a
G
equivariant finite covering map. Moreover,
(G/H)^{H}
is finite and
G
acts trivially on
F
.

Vice-versa, let
G/H
be an homogeneous almost Kähler manifold with
(G/H)^{H}
is finite and let
F
be an almost Kähler manifold acted on trivially by
G
. Assume that there exists a
G
equivariant finite covering map
\Theta:G/H\times F\longrightarrow M
. It is easy to check that the
G
action on
M
is asystatic and so the result follows.
∎

As a consequence we get the following

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group acting properly, effectively and in a Hamiltonian fashion on a simply connected symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
. Then the
G

M
is asystatic if and only if
M
is
G
equivariantly isometrically symplectomorphic to the product of an homogeneous almost Kähler manifold
G/H
with
(G/H)^{H}
finite and an almost Kähler manifold which is acted on trivially by
G
. Moreover, if
G
is reductive then
G
is semisimple.

Definition 6.4
.

The
G

(M,\omega)
is called

, if
(N(H)/H)^{o}
is Abelian, where

Symplectic asystatic actions are studied in
[

]
under the assumption that

.

Let

(T_{x}M)^{H}\cap T_{x}G\cdot x=T_{x}L\cdot x

\mathfrak{n}=T_{x}G\cdot x\cap(T_{x}L\cdot x)^{\perp}

it follows that

(3)

T_{x}M=(T_{x}G\cdot x)^{\perp}\oplus T_{x}L\cdot x\oplus\mathfrak{n},

and so

T_{x}M
.
By
[

\small{c}M

J(T_{x}L\cdot x)\subseteq(T_{x}G\cdot x).

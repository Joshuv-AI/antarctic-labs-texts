On the Success Probability of Three Detectors for the Box-Constrained Integer Linear Model
Jinming Wen, Xiao-Wen Chang

Abstract
This paper is concerned with detecting an integer parameter vector inside a box from a linear model that is corrupted with a noise vector following the Gaussian distribution. One of the commonly used detectors is the maximum likelihood detector, which is obtained by solving a box-constrained integer least squares problem, that is NP-hard. Two other popular detectors are the box-constrained rounding and Babai detectors due to their high efficiency of implementation. In this paper, we first present formulas for the success probabilities (the probabilities of correct detection) of these three detectors for two different situations: the integer parameter vector is deterministic and is uniformly distributed over the constraint box. Then, we give two simple examples to respectively show that the success probability of the box-constrained rounding detector can be larger than that of the box-constrained Babai detector and the latter can be larger than the success probability of the maximum likelihood detector when the parameter vector is deterministic, and prove that the success probability of the box-constrained rounding detector is always not larger than that of the box-constrained Babai detector when the parameter vector is uniformly distributed over the constraint box. Some relations between the results for the box constrained and ordinary cases are presented, and two bounds on the success probability of the maximum likelihood detector, which can easily be computed, are developed. Finally, simulation results are provided to illustrate our main theoretical findings.

Introduction

Suppose that we have the following box-constrained linear model:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{y}}=\boldsymbol{A}{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}+\boldsymbol{v},\quad\boldsymbol{v}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I}),

(1)

\displaystyle{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\in\mathcal{B}\equiv\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\boldsymbol{\ell}\leq{\boldsymbol{x}}\leq\boldsymbol{u},\;\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\boldsymbol{\ell}<\boldsymbol{u}\},

(2)

where
{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is an observation vector,

\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with
m\geq n
is a deterministic full column rank model matrix,

\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an integer parameter vector and

\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a noise vector following the Gaussian distribution

\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I})
with given
\sigma
.

This paper studies the detection of
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
, which can be deterministic or random in the box
\mathcal{B}
, from (
1
).
This problem arises from many applications.
For example, in some wireless communications systems (see e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
),

{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is a random vector which is uniformly distributed over the box
\mathcal{B}
;
in transportation science (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) and image processing (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
),

{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is a deterministic vector in a finite box
\mathcal{B}

in power electronics (see, e.g.,
[
6
]

[
7
]
),

{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is a deterministic vector in a infinite box, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

One of the most commonly used methods to detect
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is to solve the following
Box-constrained Integer Least Squares (BILS) problem:

\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{B}}\|{\boldsymbol{y}}-\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{2}^{2},

(3)

whose solution, denoted by
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
, is the maximum likelihood detector of
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
due to the fact that
\boldsymbol{v}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{I})
.

If
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
in (
2
), then
(
1

and the maximum likelihood estimator of
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
, denoted by

the Ordinary Integer Least Squares (OILS) problem:

\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|{\boldsymbol{y}}-\boldsymbol{A}{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(4)

One of the widely used approaches to solving (
3
) or (
4
) is sphere decoding,
which consists of two steps: reduction and discrete search.
Reduction is the process of using some lattice reduction strategies to preprocess
(
3
) or (
4

Discrete search is the process of finding the solution of the preprocessed problem
with certain search algorithm.
The most popular reduction method for preprocessing the OILS problem (
4
) is the Lenstra-Lenstra-Lovász (LLL)
reduction
[
8
]

[
9
]
. It consists of two kinds of strategies:
size reductions and column permutations, which reduce the magnitudes of the off-diagonal entries
and reorder the columns of the triangular factor of the QR factorization of
\boldsymbol{A}
, respectively.
For the BILS problem (
3

to be handled, thus instead of using the LLL reduction, some column reordering strategies
are frequently utilized to preprocess (

The commonly used column reordering strategies includes
Vertical-Bell Laboratories Layered Space Time (V-BLAST)
[
10
]
and
Sorted QR Decomposition (SQRD)
[
11

which use the information of
\boldsymbol{A}

[

13

\boldsymbol{A}

{\boldsymbol{y}}
and
\mathcal{B}

The most widely used discrete search strategy for (
4

Schnorr-Euchner search algorithm
[
14
]
, which is an improvement of

[
15
]

By taking the box-constraint into account, the Schnorr-Euchner search algorithm

Theorem 1

Let

and

We mentioned before that

Suppose that in (

\displaystyle P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}=\frac{1}{4}[1+\phi_{\sigma}(2)]^{2}=0.7079.

By (

\boldsymbol{\xi}\in S

satisfies all of the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\xi_{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}-\frac{5}{4}\ \ (\mbox{take}\;{\boldsymbol{x}}=[1,1]^{T}),

\displaystyle\Big\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\mid\xi_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}-\frac{5}{4},\;\xi_{1}\geq 0\Big\}

\displaystyle\bigcup

\displaystyle\Big\{\boldsymbol{\xi}\mid\xi_{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}-\frac{5}{4},\;-1\leq\xi_{1}\leq 0\Big\}.

.

Theorem 10

Suppose that

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the success probabilities of the box-constrained
rounding detectors
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}
, the box-constrained Babai detectors
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}

and the BILS detectors
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
for
detecting an integer parameter vector
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\in\mathcal{B}
in the linear model (
1
),
and studied their relationships for two cases:

{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is deterministic and
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
is uniformly distributed over
\mathcal{B}
.
We first developed formulas for the success probabilities
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}
and
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}

for
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}
, the success probability
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
for
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
,
and the success probabilities
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
and
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
for
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
.
Since it is time consuming to compute
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
and
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
,
we also developed upper bounds, which can easily be calculated, on them.
Then, we gave two examples to show that
both
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}>P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
and
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}>P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
are possible,
and rigorously proved that
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}\leq P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
always holds.

In MIMO applications, often the entries of
\boldsymbol{A}
are assumed
to independently and identically follow the standard Gaussian distribution (see, e.g.,
[
34
]
).
A closed-form expression of
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}
has been developed in
[
35
]
and
[
32
]

for this class of random matrices
\boldsymbol{A}
.
Although we do not have a formula for
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}
or
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
for random
\boldsymbol{A}
,
we can see that
P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BR}}\leq P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BB}}\leq P_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{BL}}
still hold
if the entries of
\boldsymbol{A}
,
\boldsymbol{v}
and
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}
are independent random variables,
since it holds for any realization of
\boldsymbol{A}
which implies it also holds for random matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
.

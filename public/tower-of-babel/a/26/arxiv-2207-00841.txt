Local Max-Entropy and Free Energy Principles
Olivier Peltre

Abstract
A statistical system is classically defined on a set of microstates E E by a global energy function H : E → ℝ H:E\to\mathbb{R} , yielding Gibbs probability measures (softmins) ρ β ​ ( H ) \rho^{\beta}(H) for every inverse temperature β = T − 1 \beta=T^{-1} . Gibbs states are simultaneously characterised by free energy principles and the max-entropy principle, with dual constraints on inverse temperature β \beta and mean energy 𝒰 ​ ( β ) = 𝔼 ρ β ​ [ H ] \mathcal{U}(\beta)=\mathbb{E}_{\rho^{\beta}}[H] respectively. The Legendre transform relates these diverse variational principles [ 1 , 2 ] which are unfortunately not tractable in high dimension. We here consider a finite free sheaf E : 𝒫 ​ ( Ω ) o ​ p → 𝐒𝐞𝐭 E:\mathcal{P}(\Omega)^{op}\to\mathbf{Set} of microstates, defined by E a = ∏ i ∈ a E i E_{\mathrm{a}}=\prod_{i\in\mathrm{a}}E_{i} for every region a ⊆ Ω \mathrm{a}\subseteq\Omega , whose size grows exponentially with Ω \Omega . The global energy function H : E → ℝ H:E\to\mathbb{R} is assumed given as a sum H ( x ) = ∑ a ⊆ Ω h a ( x | a ) H(x)=\sum_{\mathrm{a}\subseteq\Omega}h_{\mathrm{a}}(x_{|\mathrm{a}}) of local short-range interactions h a : E a → ℝ h_{\mathrm{a}}:E_{\mathrm{a}}\to\mathbb{R} , indexed by bounded subregions. The cluster variational method (CVM) [ 3 , 4 , 5 ] can then be used to estimate the global free energy F ( β ) = − 1 β ln ∑ e − β ​ H F(\beta)=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\sum\e^{-\beta H} , by searching for critical points of a localised approximation of a variational free energy. These critical points can be found by generalised belief propagation (GBP) algorithms, according to a correspondence theorem initially conjectured by Yedidia et al [ 6 , 7 ] . The proof of this theorem [ 8 , 9 ] involved combinatorial and topological structures acting on dual complexes of local observables and local measures, on which continuous-time diffusion equations gave new message-passing algorithms regularising GBP [ 9 , 10 ] . We now show that stationary states of GBP algorithms solve a collection of equivalent variational principles on local Bethe-Kikuchi functionals, which approximate the free energy F ⁡ ( β ) F(\beta) , the Shannon entropy S ⁡ ( 𝒰 ) S(\mathcal{U}) , and the variational free energy ℱ ⁡ ( β ) = 𝒰 − β − 1 ​ S ​ ( 𝒰 ) \mathcal{F}(\beta)=\mathcal{U}-\beta^{-1}S(\mathcal{U}) respectively. This local form of Legendre duality yields a possibly degenerate relationship between the constraints 𝒰 \mathcal{U} and β \beta , exhibiting singularities on loopy hypergraphs where GBP converges to multiple equilibria.

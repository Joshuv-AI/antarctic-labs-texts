Photon-Odderon interference in exclusive χ c \chi_{c} charmonium production at the Electron-Ion Collider Preprint: ZTF-EP-24-02
Sanjin Benić, Adrian Dumitru, Abhiram Kaushik, Leszek Motyka, Tomasz Stebel

Abstract
Exclusive C = + 1 C=+1 scalar, axial-vector, and tensor quarkonium production in high-energy electron-proton scattering requires a C C -odd t t -channel exchange of a photon or a three gluon ladder. We derive the expressions for the corresponding amplitudes. The relative phase of the photon vs. three gluon exchange amplitudes is determined by the sign of the light-front matrix element of the eikonal color current operator d a ​ b ​ c ​ J + a ​ J + b ​ J + c d^{abc}J^{+a}J^{+b}J^{+c} at moderate x x , and is not affected by small- x x QCD evolution. Model calculations predict constructive interference, which is particularly strong for momentum transfer | t | ∼ 1 |t|\sim 1 GeV 2 where the cross section for χ c ​ J \chi_{cJ} production exceeds that for pure photon exchange by up to a factor of 4. Exclusive χ c ​ J \chi_{cJ} electroproduction at the high luminosity Electron-Ion Collider occurs with well measurable rates and measurements of these processes should find evidence for the perturbative Odderon exchange. We also compute the total electroproduction cross section as a function of energy and provide first estimates of the number of χ c ​ J \chi_{cJ} events per month at the Electron-Ion Collider design luminosity.

Introduction

A basic prediction of QCD, related to the non-zero cubic Casimir invariant, is that there exists a contribution to high-energy
scattering amplitudes from a
C
-conjugation odd exchange driven mostly by gluons, with a weakly energy dependent cross section.
The possible existence of such an exchange, called the Odderon, was predicted 50 years ago from general principles of quantum field theory
Lukaszuk and Nicolescu 1973
.
If the scattering involves hadrons of size much smaller than the QCD confinement scale, or high momentum transfer, then the composition of the exchanged “state” can be understood from perturbative QCD. At leading order in perturbation theory, it corresponds to an exchange of three gluons which is symmetric under permutations of their colors. At high energies the three gluon exchange is dressed by higher order QCD corrections enhanced by logarithms of energy that have to be resummed into a gluon ladder. It accounts for interactions between the exchanged
t
-channel gluons, and for virtual corrections. The resulting “reggeized” three gluon exchange is the hard Odderon. In the weak field limit the corresponding Bartels-Jaroszewicz-Kwieciński-Praszałowicz (BJKP) linear QCD evolution equation has been obtained long ago
Bartels 1980
;
Kwiecinski and Praszalowicz 1980
;
Jaroszewicz 1980
,
and it amounts to the reggeization of the exchanged gluons by iteration of the Balitsky-Fadin-Kuraev-Lipatov (BFKL) interaction kernel
Lipatov 1976
;
Kuraev et al. 1977
;
Balitsky and Lipatov 1978
for each pair of gluons in the ladder. The NLO corrections to the BJKP equation were found in Ref.
Bartels et al. 2013
.
At asymptotically high energies the leading solution to the BJKP equation corresponds to a configuration of gluons in which two reggeized gluons are combined together
Bartels et al. 2000
– the Bartels–Lipatov–Vacca (BLV) solution. The BLV solution couples to color dipoles so it is particularly important in
C
-even quarkonia (or meson) photo- and electroproduction
Bartels et al. 2000
. The intercept of this solution equals one, in the leading logarithmic approximation
Bartels et al. 2000
, and it was argued that it remains at this value to all orders in the perturbative expansion
Stasto 2009

E^{\mu}(\bar{\lambda}=\pm 1,\Delta_{0})=\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\perp}^{\bar{\lambda}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}}{\Delta^{-}},0,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\perp}^{\bar{\lambda}}\right)\,,\quad E^{\mu}(\bar{\lambda}=0,\Delta_{0})=\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}-M_{0}^{2}}{2{M_{0}}\Delta^{-}},\frac{\Delta^{-}}{{M_{0}}},\frac{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}}{{M_{0}}}\right)=\frac{1}{{M_{0}}}\Delta_{0}^{\mu}-\frac{{M_{0}}}{\Delta^{-}}n^{\mu}\,.

(12)

(13)

We have checked
that the projector
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\not{E}(\bar{\lambda}=0,\Delta)
(instead of
\mathrm{i}\gamma_{5}\not{E}(\bar{\lambda}=0,\Delta_{0})

This happens because in the light-cone formulation the rest frames of the quarkonia and of the

\Delta_{0}\cdot E(\bar{\lambda},\Delta)=0

{M_{0}}=M_{\mathcal{A}}
.
In other words, we will assume that the amplitude for the transition from

Martin et al. 2000

The tensor quarkonia polarizations
E^{\mu\nu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})
can be obtained in terms of
E^{\mu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})
, using Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. We have
Berger et al. 2000

\begin{split}&E^{\mu\nu}(\pm 2,\Delta_{0})=E^{\mu}(\pm 1,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(\pm 1,\Delta_{0})\,,\\
&E^{\mu\nu}(\pm 1,\Delta_{0})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(E^{\mu}(\pm 1,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(0,\Delta_{0})+E^{\mu}(0,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(\pm 1,\Delta_{0})\big)\,,\\
&E^{\mu\nu}(0,\Delta_{0})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\big(E^{\mu}(+1,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(-1,\Delta_{0})+E^{\mu}(-1,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(+1,\Delta_{0})+2E^{\mu}(0,\Delta_{0})E^{\nu}(0,\Delta_{0})\big)\,.\\
\end{split}

(14)

These polarization tensors are traceless,

E^{\mu\nu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})=E^{\nu\mu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})

\Delta_{0}^{\mu}E_{\mu\nu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})=0=\Delta_{0}^{\nu}E_{\mu\nu}(\bar{\lambda},\Delta_{0})

(22)

\begin{split}\left\langle\mathcal{M}_{\lambda=0}\right\rangle&\equiv q^{-}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda=0}\equiv q^{-}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{L}\,,\\

\end{split}

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{L}=8\pi N_{c}eq_{c}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\int_{z}\int_{0}^{\infty}r_{\perp}\mathrm{d}r_{\perp}\mathcal{O}_{2k+1}(r_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})\mathcal{A}_{L}(r_{\perp})\,{\rm sgn}(z-\bar{z})J_{2k+1}(r_{\perp}\delta_{\perp})\,,\\

Appendix A
Computation of the light-cone wave functions

In order to compute the light-cone wave functions, the key element is a vertex contraction between spinors, e. g.
\bar{u}_{h}(k)\Gamma v_{\bar{h}}(k^{\prime})
(
\Gamma

\begin{split}&u_{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}\left(\sqrt{2}k^{-}+\gamma^{0}m+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\right)\chi_{h}=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}\left(\sqrt{2}k^{-}+\gamma^{0}m+\not{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\perp}\gamma^{0}\right)\chi_{h}\,,\\
&v_{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}\left(\sqrt{2}k^{-}-\gamma^{0}m+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\right)\chi_{-h}=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}\left(\sqrt{2}k^{-}-\gamma^{0}m+\not{\boldsymbol{k}}_{\perp}\gamma^{0}\right)\chi_{-h}\,,\end{split}

(68)

where
\alpha^{i}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}
. In accordance with the conventions used in this work, we have switched plus and minus light-cone coordinates in the above expression as compared to the original LB convention
Lepage and Brodsky 1980
. Thus the spinors
\chi_{h}
are eigenstates of
\gamma^{+}\gamma^{-}

\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{+}\gamma^{-}\chi_{h}=\chi_{h}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{-}\gamma^{+}\chi_{h}=0\,.

(69)

Explicitly, we have

\begin{split}&u_{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{0}\chi_{h}\,,\\
&v_{h}(k)=\frac{1}{2^{1/4}\sqrt{k^{-}}}(\not{k}-m)\gamma^{0}\chi_{-h}\,.\end{split}

(70)

By defining a projection matrix

\chi_{h\bar{h}}\equiv\gamma^{0}\chi_{-\bar{h}}\bar{\chi}_{h}\gamma^{0}\,,

one obtains

\bar{u}_{h}(k)\Gamma v_{\bar{h}}(k^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k^{-}k^{\prime-}}}{\rm tr}\left[(\not{k}+m)\Gamma(\not{k}^{\prime}-m)\chi_{h\bar{h}}\right]\,,

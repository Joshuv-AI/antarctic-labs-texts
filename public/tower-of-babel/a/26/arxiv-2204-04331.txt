The boundedness of Fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on variable ℓ p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( ℤ ) \ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z}) spaces using Calderon-Zygmund decomposition
A.Sri Sakti Swarup, A. Michael Alphonse

Abstract
In this paper, we prove strong type, weak type inequalities of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on variable sequence spaces ℓ p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( ℤ ) \ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z}) . This is achieved using Calderon-Zygmund decomposition for sequences, properties of modular functional and Log Holder continuity.

Introduction

In this paper, we prove strong type, weak type inequalities of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and fractional Hardy Littlewood maximal operator on variable
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
sequence spaces. Inequalities for Hardy Littlewood maximal operator for variable sequence spaces
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
, follows from corresponding inequalities for
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces and Log Holder continuity. To prove, the inequalities for fractional Hardy Littlewood maximal operator, we use Calderon Zygmund decomposition for sequences and Log Holder continuity.

Several authors have proved strong type and weak type inequalities for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator using various methods. In this paper, we provide a method that uses Calderon-Zygmund decomposition for sequences
to prove strong and weak type inequalities for these maximal operators. Similar proof for the real line can be found in
[
3
]
where boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is proved using Calderon-Zygmund decomposition.
Even though, we follow same method of proof given in
[
3
]
, there are some remarkable differences
between the proofs on integers and proofs on real line.

For detailed history of variable Lebesgue spaces, please refer
\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{fior_var_book}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{lars1}{}{}]}}
. Similar conclusions in broader scope utilizing framework of homogeneous spaces may be found in
[
2
]
. For detailed references on work in spaces of homogeneous type under variable exponent setting, please refer
\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alme1}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{mizuta1}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{goro1}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{hast2}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{kok1}{}{}]}},\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{kok2}{}{}]}}
.

In this paper, we use the method of Calderon Zygmund decomposition for sequences and Log Holder continuity. We assume only Log Holder continuity at infinity. The local Log Holder continuity follows automatically in case of integers.

2
Definitions and Notation

In this paper, the following notation are used. Given a bounded sequence
\left\{p(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
which takes values in
[1,\infty)
, define

\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
to be set of all sequences
\left\{a(k)\right\}
on
\mathbb{Z}
such that for some
\lambda>0
,
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\frac{\lvert a(k)\rvert}{\lambda})^{p(k)}<\infty
.

Throughout this paper,
\left\{p(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
denotes a bounded sequence which takes values in
[1,\infty)
. Define
{p_{-}}=\inf\left\{p(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},{p_{+}}=\sup\left\{p(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the set of all bounded sequences which take values in
[1,\infty)
such that
{p_{+}}<\infty
.

For any set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
\lvert A\rvert
denotes cardinality of A.

Let

\Omega=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:1\leq p(n)<\infty\right\}

\Omega_{\infty}=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:p(n)=\infty\right\}

Then define modular functional associated with
{p(\cdot)}
as

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\Omega_{\infty}}\lvert a(k)\rvert^{p(k)}+\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}(\Omega_{\infty})}

Note, when
{p_{+}}<\infty
, modular functional becomes

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert a(k)\rvert^{p(k)}

Throughout this paper, we assume
{p_{+}}<\infty
. Further for a given sequence
\left\{a(k):k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
, define norm in
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
as

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\lambda})\leq 1\right\}

\left\lVert\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
is a norm
[
13
]
. A similar norm on
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined in
[
4
]
and there it is proved it is a norm.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a non-negative sequence of real numbers,
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},0\leq\alpha<1
, define fractional Hardy-Littlewood Maximal operator as follows:

M_{\alpha}a(n)=\sup_{n\in I}\lvert I\rvert^{\alpha-1}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert a(k)\rvert

where supremum is taken over all intervals of integers which contain
n
. When
\alpha=0
, fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator becomes Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a sequence
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
, we say that
{p(\cdot)}
is log-Holder continuous at infinity, if there exists positive real constants
C_{\infty},p_{\infty}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\lvert p(n)-p_{\infty}\rvert\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)},n\in\mathbb{Z}

and
e
is exponential number. In this case, we write
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

We will also require Young’s inequality in this paper. For any real
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, and any two real numbers
p,q
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, Young’s inequality
[
6
]
is as follows:

ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{q}}{q}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\left\{q(n)\right\}
be the sequence which satisfies
\frac{1}{p(n)}+\frac{1}{q(n)}=1

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
1\leq{p_{-}}\leq p(n)\leq{p_{+}}<\infty
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The following are equivalent:

1.

{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

2.

\frac{1}{{p(\cdot)}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

3.

\frac{1}{{q(\cdot)}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

4.

{q(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

(a) We shall prove (1)
\implies(2).\\

Let
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note, that when
{p_{+}}<\infty
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\frac{1}{p(n)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\rvert=\lvert\frac{p(n)-p_{\infty}}{p(n)p_{\infty}}\rvert\leq\frac{\lvert p(n)-p_{\infty}\rvert}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}\leq\frac{\frac{C_{\infty}}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

for some
LH_{\infty}
constant, which is
k_{\infty}=\frac{C_{\infty}}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}
.

(b) We shall prove (2)
\implies
(1).

Let
\frac{1}{{p(\cdot)}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then,
\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\lvert p(n)-p_{\infty}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert p(n)p_{\infty}\biggl(\frac{1}{p(n)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\biggr)\rvert\leq\lvert p(n)p_{\infty}\rvert\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

\displaystyle\leq{p_{+}}p_{\infty}\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k_{\infty}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

for some
LH_{\infty}
constant which is
k_{\infty}={p_{+}}p_{\infty}C_{\infty}
.

(c) We shall prove that (1)
\implies
(3).

Let
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note, that when
{p_{+}}<\infty
, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\frac{1}{q(n)}-\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\frac{1}{p(n)}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\rvert=\lvert\frac{p(n)-p_{\infty}}{p(n)p_{\infty}}\rvert\leq\frac{\lvert p(n)-p(\infty)\rvert}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{C_{\infty}}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

for some
LH_{\infty}
constant, which is
k_{\infty}=\frac{C_{\infty}}{{p_{-}}p_{\infty}}
.

(d) We shall prove (3)
\implies
(4) and (4)
\implies
(1)

Let
\frac{1}{{q(\cdot)}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\frac{1}{\frac{1}{{q(\cdot)}}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, which implies that
{q(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
. This shows (3)
\implies
(4).

(4)
\implies
(1) follows same argument as (c) .

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\left\{q(n)\right\}
be the sequence which satisfies
\frac{1}{p(n)}+\frac{1}{q(n)}=1

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
1\leq{p_{-}}\leq p(n)\leq{p_{+}}<\infty
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
implies
p_{\infty}\geq{p_{-}}
.

Proof.

Given that
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
implies
\frac{1}{{p(\cdot)}}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
using Lemma[
2.3
], it follows that

\displaystyle\lvert\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\frac{1}{p(n)}+\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{p(n)}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\frac{1}{p(n)}\rvert+\lvert\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{p(n)}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\frac{1}{{p_{-}}}\rvert+\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\lvert n\rvert)}

Since this is true for every
n
,
p_{\infty}\geq{p_{-}}
.
∎

Some Results on Variable Lebesgue spaces

In this section, we prove certain results that are used in proving boundedness of maximal operators.The proofs of these results in discrete case are same as corresponding results on real line
[
4
]
. For completeness sake, some of the proofs are included.

The modular functional defined in Section 2 has the following properties defined below
[
4
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\left\{u(n)\right\}
be a non-negative sequence of real numbers. Let
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
, then:

1.

For all u,
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)\geq 0
and
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\lvert u\rvert)=\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)

2.

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)=0
if and only if
u(k)=0
for all k
\in\mathbb{Z}

3.

If
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)<\infty
, then
u(k)<\infty
for all k
\in\mathbb{Z}

4.

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}
is convex: Given
\alpha,\beta\geq 0,\alpha+\beta=1,\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\alpha\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)+\beta\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(v)

5.

If
\lvert u(k)\rvert\geq\lvert v(k)\rvert
, then
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)\geq\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(v)
.

6.

If for some
\delta>0,\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\delta})<\infty
, then the function
\lambda\to\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})
is continuous
and decreasing on
[\delta,\infty)
. Further
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

The following lemma gives the connection between modular functional and
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
norm.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\left\{u(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
be a non-negative sequence of real numbers. Let
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
.
Then
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
if and only if

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\lvert u(k)\rvert}^{p(k)}<\infty

Proof.

If
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)<\infty
, then by definition of norm
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Conversely since
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
by definiton of norm,
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})<\infty
for some
\lambda>0
. Further by (6) of modular functional properties, we have
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})<\infty
for some
\lambda>1
. So, it follows that

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(u)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{\lvert u(k)\rvert\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{p(k)}\leq\lambda^{{p_{+}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})<\infty

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\left\{a(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
be a non-negative sequence of real numbers such that
a\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
and let
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}
.

1.

For all
\lambda\geq 1
,

\lambda^{{p_{-}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)\leq\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\lambda a)\leq\lambda^{{p_{+}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)

2.

When
0<\lambda<1
the reverse inequalities are true, i.e

\lambda^{{p_{+}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)\leq\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\lambda a)\leq\lambda^{{p_{-}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)

Proof.

For
\lambda\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\lambda a)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\biggl(\lambda a(n)\biggr)^{p(n)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{p(n)}{a(n)}^{p(n)}\leq\lambda^{{p_{+}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)

Further,

\displaystyle{\lambda}^{{p_{-}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{{p_{-}}}{a(n)}^{p(n)}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\biggl(\lambda a(n)\biggr)^{p(n)}=\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\lambda a)

Similar proof can be used for the case
0<\lambda<1
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

[Fatou Property of the Norm].
Let
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
be sequence of non-negative real numbers and
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
.
Further, let
\left\{u_{k}\right\}\subset\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
be non-negative sequences of real numbers such that
u_{k}
increases to the sequence
u
pointwise.
Then
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\to\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

Proof.

Since for every
n
,
\frac{u_{k}(n)}{\lambda}\leq\frac{u_{k+1}(n)}{\lambda}
, by property (5) of modular functional,

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u_{k}}{\lambda})\leq\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u_{k+1}}{\lambda})
and hence
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\left\lVert u_{k+1}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
.
Therefore,
\left\{u_{k}\right\}
is an increasing sequence, so is
\left\{\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\right\}
and so this increasing sequence either converges to a finite limit or diverges to
\infty
.

It is required to prove
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
. Note that

\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
is increasing and
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
. Hence

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
.

Take
\lambda>0,\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}>\lambda
. We shall prove that if
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}>\lambda
then for sufficiently large values of
k
,
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}>\lambda
.
Since
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})>1
and using Monotone convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(\frac{{u(m)}}{\lambda}\biggr)^{p(m)}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(\frac{\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}(m)}{\lambda}\biggr)^{p(m)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\biggl(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(\frac{{u_{k}(m)}}{\lambda}\biggr)^{p(m)}\biggr)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u_{k}}{\lambda})

So,
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u_{k}}{\lambda})>1
for sufficiently large values of
k
.
Let
A_{k}=\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u_{k}}{\lambda})\leq 1\right\}
and
B=\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{u}{\lambda})\leq 1\right\}
.
From above discussion,
B^{\complement}\subseteq{A^{\complement}_{k}}
for sufficiently large values of
k
.
Therefore
A_{k}\subseteq B
for sufficiently large values of
k
. Hence
\inf A_{k}\geq\inf B
for sufficiently large values of
k
.
Therefore
\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\geq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
for sufficiently large values of
k
.
and hence
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\geq\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

[Fatou’s lemma for sequences].
Let
\left\{u_{k}\right\}
be a non-negative sequence of real numbers such that
\left\{u_{k}\right\}\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
, suppose the sequence
\left\{u_{k}\right\}\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
u_{k}(n)\to u(n)
for every
n
. If

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty

then
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
and

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

Proof.

Define,

v_{k}(i)=\inf_{m\geq k}{u_{m}(i)}

Then for all
m\geq k,v_{k}(i)\leq{u_{m}(i)}
and this shows that
v_{k}\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
. Since
\left\{v_{k}\right\}
is an increasing sequence and

\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{k}(i)=\liminf_{m\to\infty}{u_{m}(i)}={u(i)},\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}

Also,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\left\lVert\lim_{k\to\infty}v_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert v_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert v_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

Therefore, by Fatou’s norm property[
3.4
] for sequences

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert v_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}(\inf_{m\geq k}\left\lVert u_{m}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})})=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

So, if
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty
, then
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty
, which implies
u\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

The following lemma shows normalizing a sequence gives
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})\leq 1
. Additionally if
{p_{+}}<\infty
,
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})=1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\left\{a(k)\right\}
be a non-negative sequence of real numbers and
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}

1.

If
a\in\ell^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}>0
, then
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})\leq 1

2.

If
{p_{+}}<\infty
, then
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})=1
for all nontrivial
a\in\ell^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{Z}})

Proof.

(1) By definition
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}
,
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}+\frac{1}{n}
is not an infimum. Therefore, there exists a
\lambda_{n}
such that

\lambda_{n}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}+\frac{1}{n}
and
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\lambda_{n}})\leq 1
.
Fix such a decreasing sequence
\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}
such that
\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}\to\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
. Then by Fatou’s lemma and the definition of modular functional,

\displaystyle\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert a(k)\rvert}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\biggr)^{p(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\biggl(\frac{\lvert a(k)\rvert}{\lambda_{n}}\biggr)^{p(k)}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\biggl(\frac{\lvert a(k)\rvert}{\lambda_{n}}\biggr)^{p(k)}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\lambda_{n}})\leq 1

(2). Assume
{p_{+}}<\infty
but
\rho(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})<1
. Then
\forall\lambda
such that
0<\lambda<\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
, by Lemma[
3.3
]

\rho_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}(a/\lambda)=\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}{\lambda}\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})\leq\biggl(\frac{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}{\lambda}\biggr)^{{p_{+}}}\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(\frac{a}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}})

So,a
\lambda
can be found, sufficiently close to
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
such that
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a/\lambda)<1
.
However norm definition requires that
\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(a/\lambda)\geq 1
.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S}
and

,

\rho_{{q(\cdot)}}(\alpha_{2}t\chi_{\Omega})=\sum_{k\in\Omega}[\alpha_{2}t]^{q(m)}\leq 1

We will show that each term on the right is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}
for suitable choice of
\alpha_{2}
.
To estimate

\sum_{k\in\Omega}[\alpha_{2}t]^{q(m)}
, we note from results in previous section,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\Omega}[\alpha_{2}t]^{q(m)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{E_{j}}\delta_{2}\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert 2I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{k\in 2I_{j}}{g_{2}(k)}^{\frac{1}{p(k)}}\biggr)^{p_{\infty}}+\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{E_{j}}\delta_{2}\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert 2I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{k\in 2I_{j}}{g_{2}(k)}^{\frac{p_{\infty}}{p(k)}}\biggr)+\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\Omega}\delta_{2}{g_{2}(k)}^{\frac{p_{\infty}}{p(k)}}+\frac{1}{6}

Now , choose
\delta_{2}>0
such that right hand side is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

6
Hardy-Littlewood Maximal operator

In this section, we prove boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator for
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces where
{p_{-}}>1
. The proof is based on boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
, where
p
is a fixed number,
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 6.1
.

Note that when
\alpha=0
the fractional Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator is nothing but Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.
However we can prove strong type, weak type inequalities for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})
directly from the corresponding results for fixed
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces,
1<p<\infty
.
The key point of the proofs is Lemma[
6.2
]. The proof of continuous version of Lemma[
6.2
] can be found in
[
4
]
. Same line of proof works here.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{Z}\to[0,\infty)
be such that
p(\cdot)\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
and
0<p_{\infty}<\infty
. Let
R(k)=(e+\lvert k\rvert)^{-N},N>\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}
. Then there exists a real constant C depending on N and
LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
constant of
p(\cdot)
such that given any set E and any function F with
0\leq F(y)\leq 1
for
y\in E

(CD5)

\displaystyle\sum_{E}F(m)^{p(m)}\leq\sum_{E}F(m)^{p_{\infty}}+C\sum_{E}R(m)^{p_{\infty}}

(CD6)

\displaystyle\sum_{E}F(m)^{p_{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{E}F(m)^{p(m)}+C\sum_{E}R(m)^{p_{\infty}}

6.1
Strong
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
Inequality

Theorem 6.3

(Strong
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
Inequality)
.

Given a non-negative sequence
\left\{a(i)\right\}\in\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z}),{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S},{p_{+}}<\infty,{p_{-}}>1
, then

\left\lVert Ma\right\rVert_{\ell^{p(.)}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p(.)}(\mathbb{Z})}

Proof.

By homogenity, it is enough to prove the above result with the assumption
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{{\ell^{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=1
.
By Lemma
[\ref{sak_corollary_2}]
,
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert a(i)\rvert^{p(i)}\leq 1
. So, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert Ma(i)\rvert^{p(i)}\leq C

Given that
0\leq a(k)\leq 1
, it follows that
0\leq Ma(k)\leq 1
. To prove boundedness of
\left\{Ma\right\}
,
we start with Lemma[
6.2
] as follows:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}Ma(k)^{p(k)}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}Ma(k)^{p_{\infty}}+C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}R(k)^{p_{\infty}}

Since
N>\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}
,
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}R(k)^{Np_{\infty}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\frac{1}{e+\lvert k\rvert})^{Np_{\infty}}
converges and can be bounded as
\leq 1
. So, the second integral is a constant depending only on
p_{\infty}
by taking sufficiently large
N>\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}
.

To bound the first integral, note that
1<{p_{-}}\leq p_{\infty}
. Since
p_{\infty}>1
, M is bounded on
\ell^{p_{\infty}}(\mathbb{Z})
and by using strong
(p,p)
inequality valid for classical Lebesgue spaces with index
p_{\infty}
, we get using Lemma[
6.2
] and [
(
CD5
)
],

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}Ma(k)^{p_{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a(k)^{p_{\infty}}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a(k)^{p(k)}+C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}R(k)^{p_{\infty}}\leq C

Like previous case, the term involving summation of
R(k)
is bounded by a constant depending only on
p_{\infty}
by taking sufficiently large
N>\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}
.

Therefore, using above results,

\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(Ma)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}Ma(k)^{p(k)}\leq C.

∎

6.2
Weak
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
Inequality for Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator

Theorem 6.4

(Weak
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
Inequality for Hardy-Littlewood Maximal Operator)
.

Given
{p(\cdot)}\in\mathcal{S},{p(\cdot)}\geq 1
, if
{p(\cdot)}\in LH_{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
, then

\sup_{t>0}\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{Ma(n)>t\right\}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq c\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

where constant depends on the Log-Holder constants of
{p(\cdot)},{p_{-}}
and
p_{\infty}
(if this value is finite) .

Proof.

1.

Case:
{p_{-}}>1
and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=1
.

Let
A=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:Ma(n)>t\right\}
. Then, by the use of strong
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
inequality for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator from previous section, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)>t\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)>t\right\}}(k)\rvert^{p(k)}\leq\sum_{k\in A}\lvert t\rvert^{p(k)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in A}{Ma}^{p(k)}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{Ma(k)}^{p(k)}=\rho_{{p(\cdot)}}(Ma)\leq C=C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

2.

Case:
{p_{-}}>1
and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\neq 1

Denote,
b(n)=\frac{a(n)}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}
, so that
\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=1
. Let
A=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:Ma(n)>t\right\}

By homogenity of the norm, it follows that,

\displaystyle\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)>t\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{n:Mb(n)>\frac{t}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{t}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\chi_{\left\{n:Mb(n)>\frac{t}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\left\lVert\frac{t}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\chi_{\left\{n:Mb(n)>\frac{t}{\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}}\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

3.

Case:
{p_{-}}=1
and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}=1

Let
A=\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:Ma(n)>t\right\}
. Then, by the use of strong
({p(\cdot)},{p(\cdot)})
inequality for Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator from previous section, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)>t\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lvert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)<t\right\}}(k)\rvert^{p(k)}=\sum_{k\in A}\lvert t\rvert^{p(k)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}t^{{p_{+}}}\lvert A\rvert&t\geq 1\\
t^{{p_{-}}}\lvert A\rvert&t\leq 1\end{cases}

Now, by use of weak
({p_{+}},{p_{+}})
inequality applicable to
\ell^{{p_{+}}}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces when
t\geq 1
and weak
({p_{+}},{p_{+}})
inequality applicable to
\ell^{{p_{-}}}(\mathbb{Z})
spaces when
t\leq 1
respectively, it follows that

\lvert A\rvert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{C}{t^{{p_{+}}}}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p_{+}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\geq 1\\
\\
\frac{C}{t^{{p_{-}}}}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p_{-}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\leq 1\end{cases}

Note, by Lemma
[\ref{sak_corollary_2}]
,

(\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{{p_{+}}})^{{p_{+}}}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\lvert a(k)\rvert}^{{p_{+}}}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\lvert a(k)\rvert}^{p(k)}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{{p_{-}}}=\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{1}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}
as
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence

\displaystyle\lvert A\rvert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{C}{t^{{p_{+}}}}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\geq 1\\
\frac{C}{t}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\leq 1\end{cases}

and therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lVert t\chi_{\left\{n:Ma(n)>t\right\}}(k)\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\geq 1\\
\leq C\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}&t\leq 1\end{cases}

4.

Case:
{p_{-}}=1
and
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{{p(\cdot)}}(\mathbb{Z})}\neq 1
. The conclusion follows similar to case(2) and case(3).

∎

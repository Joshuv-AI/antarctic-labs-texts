Gluck twists on concordant or homotopic spheres
Daniel Kasprowski, Mark Powell, Arunima Ray

Abstract
. Let M M be a compact 4-manifold and let S S and T T be embedded 2 2 -spheres in M M , both with trivial normal bundle. We write M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} for the 4-manifolds obtained by the Gluck twist operation on M M along S S and T T respectively. We show that if S S and T T are concordant, then M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are s s -cobordant, and so if π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is good, then M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are homeomorphic. Similarly, if S S and T T are homotopic then we show that M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are simple homotopy equivalent. Under some further assumptions, we deduce that M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are homeomorphic. We show that additional assumptions are necessary by giving an example where S S and T T are homotopic but M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are not homeomorphic. We also give an example where S S and T T are homotopic and M S M_{S} and M T M_{T} are homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic.

1.1.
Effect on diffeomorphism type

The following proposition is well-known, but important to observe. It shows that the classical idea of comparing
M_{S}
and
M
is a special case of comparing
M_{S}
and
M_{T}
, by taking
T
to be an unknotted 2-sphere.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
M
be a compact, smooth
4
-manifold. If
T\subseteq M
is an unknotted smooth
2
-knot, then
M
and
M_{T}
are diffeomorphic.

Proof.

First let
M=S^{4}
. Then
M_{T}\cong M
because the Gluck twist
G
extends to a diffeomorphism of
S^{1}\times D^{3}
, the complement in
S^{4}
of the trivial
2
-knot
S
. Since every orientation-preserving diffeomorphism can be isotoped to fix a ball, this implies that for an unknotted
2
-sphere
T
we have a rel. boundary diffeomorphism
D^{4}_{T}\cong D^{4}
. Now for a general
M
, every trivial
2
-knot
T
is contained in a
4
-ball, and the result follows.
∎

Gluck twisting along nontrivial, smooth
2
-knots in
S^{4}
is a well-known method to produce smooth homotopy 4-spheres, which could potentially be counterexamples to the smooth
4
-dimensional Poincaré conjecture. Progress thus far has been in the opposite direction: there are several families of nontrivial 2-knots in
S^{4}
for which it has been proven that Gluck twisting along them yields
S^{4}
again
[
17
,
19
,
36
,
31
,
33
,
34
]
.

The topological
4
-dimensional Poincaré conjecture or the homeomorphism classification of simply connected
4
-manifolds is usually cited to prove that Gluck twisting on
S^{4}
preserves homeomorphism type. Since every
2
-knot in
S^{4}
is concordant to the trivial
2
-knot
[
26
]
, the combination of
Theorem

1.1
and
Proposition

1.5
gives a different route. This method, and also that of
Corollary

1.3
, both have the advantage that they can be applied to the study of Gluck twisting on 2-knots in more general non-simply connected
4
-manifolds. We hope that readers will be motivated to investigate this.

For non-homotopic 2-spheres, Gluck twisting can change the smooth structure of a fixed 4-manifold. Akbulut
[
3
]
constructed an example of a homotopically essential
2
-knot

in
P:=(S^{1}\widetilde{\times}S^{3})\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
such that Gluck twisting changes the smooth structure. That is

is homeomorphic
[

]
but not diffeomorphic to

[

]
extended this construction to other nonorientable

-manifolds.

The following proposition sharpens this result by exhibiting two homotopic spheres
S
and
T
in a closed, nonorientable
4
-manifold

such that Gluck twisting gives rise to an exotic pair
M_{S}
and
M_{T}
. Our construction makes use of the Cappell–Shaneson exotic
\mathbb{RP}^{4}

[
8

9
]
, which we denote by

. Recall that two

and

are said to be

if there exist

such that
M\#n(S^{2}\times S^{2})\cong N\#n^{\prime}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
.

Proposition 1.6
.

There exists a pair of homotopic smooth
2
-knots
S
and
T
in
M:=\mathbb{RP}^{4}\#S^{2}\widetilde{\times}S^{2}
, both with trivial normal bundle, such that
M_{S}\cong\mathbb{RP}^{4}\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})

M_{T}\cong R\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})

and

The proof of this proposition uses neither
Theorem

1.1
nor

1.3
. We also remark that we do not produce a new example of an exotic pair of
4
-manifolds, but rather the proof uses the fact that
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
and

are known to not be stably diffeomorphic.

At present, there is no known example of the Gluck twisting operation changing the smooth structure of an orientable
4
-manifold.

Question 1.7

Does there exist a smooth,

4
-manifold

Akbulut and Yasui give a simple criterion when a Gluck twist on a smooth

Theorems

In this section we prove

We start with a straightforward lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a compact
4
-manifold, and let
S\subseteq M
be a locally flat embedded
2
-sphere with trivial normal bundle. Then
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(M_{S})
.

Proof.

The operation of attaching

to a 4-manifold along

can be decomposed into a

are obtained from

by gluing in a copy of
D^{2}\times S^{2}
, and in both cases the

in

. In the case of
M_{S}
, we can use the meridian at a fixed point of the rotation
R\colon S^{1}\to SO(3)
.
Then both
\pi_{1}(M)
and
\pi_{1}(M_{S})
are isomorphic to the quotient of
\pi_{1}(M\setminus\nu S)
by the normal subgroup generated by a meridian of

, and so in particular are isomorphic to each other.
∎

(see also

]

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a compact 4-manifold.
Let

be locally flat, embedded, and homotopic

-spheres, both with normal bundle having euler number

Let

and

Proof.

The upcoming proof is inspired by an argument due to Stong
[

]
.
For an arbitrary
4
-manifold

and

, let
\capp(W,\beta)
denote the result

of adding a 3-cell to

along
\beta
.
By the definition of the blow down operation, there are canonical homeomorphisms
N^{A}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2}\cong N
and
N^{B}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2}\cong N
sending
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
to
A
and
B
respectively.
Let

denote a generator, which we consider as an element in both
\pi_{2}(N^{A}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2})
and
\pi_{2}(N^{B}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2})
, via the inclusion induced maps. Note that

(3.1)

\capp(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)\simeq_{s}S^{4},

where
\simeq_{s}
denotes simple homotopy equivalence. To see this extend the standard degree one map
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\to S^{4}
over
\capp(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)
and observe that both spaces are simply connected and that the map induces an isomorphism on all homology groups. Therefore
\capp(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)\simeq S^{4}
by the Whitehead theorem. Since the Whitehead group of the trivial group is trivial, we deduce that (
3.1
) holds. Now we make the following computation:

\displaystyle N^{B}

\displaystyle\cong N^{B}\#S^{4}\simeq_{s}N^{B}\#\capp(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)\cong\capp(N^{B}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)

\displaystyle\cong\capp(N,B)\simeq_{s}\capp(N,A)\cong\capp(N^{A}\#\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)\cong N^{A}\#\capp(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\sigma)

\displaystyle\simeq_{s}N^{A}\#S^{4}\cong N^{A}.

Since the computation is symmetrical in

and

it suffices to justify this as far as the

3.1
). The third equivalence uses that we may perform the connected sum away from

. The fourth equivalence uses the canonical homeomorphism

discussed above.
The fifth, central simple homotopy equivalence uses that

and

are homotopic. The remaining equivalences can be justified by switching the rôles of

and

, and applying the previous justifications in reverse order.
This gives the desired simple homotopy equivalence

. The boundaries of

,
N^{A}
, and
N^{B}
are naturally identified, and by inspection our simple homotopy equivalence restricts to the identity on the boundary. This completes the proof of

.
∎

1.2
.

By

,

and

Bounds on Maximum Weight Directed Cut
Jiangdong Ai Stefanie Gerke Gregory Gutin Anders Yeo Yacong Zhou

Abstract
We obtain lower and upper bounds for the maximum weight of a directed cut in the classes of weighted digraphs and weighted acyclic digraphs as well as in some of their subclasses. We compare our results with those obtained for the maximum size of a directed cut in unweighted digraphs. In particular, we show that a lower bound obtained by Alon, Bollobás, Gyáfás, Lehel and Scott (J Graph Th 55(1) (2007)) for unweighted acyclic digraphs can be extended to weighted digraphs with the maximum length of a cycle being bounded by a constant and the weight of every arc being at least one. We state a number of open problems.

2.1
Basic Bounds

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
D=(V(D),A(D),w_{D})
be a weighted digraph and let
G=UG(D).

(a)

If the minimum weight of a dicut of
D
is
k
, then
r^{+}(D)+k\leq{\rm mac}(D)
.

(b)

\frac{{\rm mac}(G)}{2}\leq{\rm mac}(D)\leq\frac{{\rm mac}(G)+r^{+}(D)}{2}
.

(c)

\frac{{\rm mac}(G)/2+r^{+}(D)}{2}\leq{\rm mac}(D)\leq\frac{{\rm mac}(G)+r^{+}(D)}{2}
.

(d)

Let the chromatic number of
G
be
\chi
. If
\chi
is even, then

\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4(\chi-1)}\right)w(D)\leq{\rm mac}(D);

and if
\chi
is odd, then

\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4\chi}\right)w(D)\leq{\rm mac}(D).

Proof.

For (a), let
X
contain all vertices,
x
, with
r(x)\geq 0
. Then by (
1
)

r^{+}(D)=w_{D}(X,Y)-w_{D}(Y,X)\leq w_{D}(X,Y)-k\leq{\rm mac}(D)-k.

For (b), we have by definition that for any cut
(X,Y)

w_{G}(X,Y)=w_{D}(X,Y)+w_{D}(Y,X).

In particular this equality is true for the maximum cut in
G
and hence in this case
w_{D}(X,Y)
or
w_{D}(Y,X)
has to be at least
{\rm mac}(G)/2
which shows the lower bound.
For the upper bound, adding this equality to (
1
), we get

w_{G}(X,Y)+\sum_{x\in X}r(x)=2w_{D}(X,Y).

As
\sum_{x\in X}r(x)\leq r^{+}(D)
and
w_{G}(X,Y)\leq{\rm mac}(G)
, this
implies that

2w_{D}(X,Y)\leq{\rm mac}(G)+r^{+}(D)

for any cut and in particular when
(X,Y)
is a cut of
D
of the maximum weight.

The lower bound of (c) follows by adding (
2
) and (b) together and dividing by two, while the upper bound is the same as in (b).

For (d) consider a partition of
V(D)
into
\chi
independent sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\chi}
. We construct an auxilary weighted undirected complete graph
G^{\prime}
with
\chi

and for

the edge

has weight

and that every cut

in

corresponds to two cuts

and

in

such that

. If
\chi=2t
is even then we partition

randomly and uniformly into two parts
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
of size
t
.
For an edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
, let the
I(e)=w^{\prime}(e)
if one endpoint of

is in
X^{\prime}
and the other in
Y^{\prime}
, and
I(e)=0
otherwise. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(w^{\prime}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}I(e)\right)=\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}(I(e))

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}w^{\prime}(e)\frac{2{{2t-2}\choose{t-1}}}{{{2t}\choose t}}=\frac{t}{2t-1}w^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\chi-2}\right)w(D).

If
\chi=2t+1
is odd then we partition
V(G^{\prime})
randomly and uniformly into two parts

and
Y^{\prime}
of size

and

For an edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
, let

if one endpoint of

X^{\prime}
and the other in

, and

\mathbb{E}(w^{\prime}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))=\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}w^{\prime}(e)\frac{2{{2t-1}\choose{t-1}}}{{{2t+1}\choose t}}=\frac{t+1}{2t+1}w^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\chi}\right)w(D).

In either case there exists a cut
(X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime})
in

with
w^{\prime}(X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime})\geq\mathbb{E}(w(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))
, and the result now follows since there exist corresponding cuts
(X,Y)
and
(Y,X)
such that

. ∎

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 2.84544pt{\rm mac}(D_{\nu})&\leq&|A(D_{\nu})|\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\nu^{{3/2}}}{3\nu(\sqrt{\nu}-1)^{2}-3\nu^{{3/2}}-\nu^{{3/2}}+\sqrt{\nu}-1}\right)\\
&\leq&|A(D_{\nu})|\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\nu^{{3/2}}}{3\nu^{2}-10\nu^{{3/2}}}\right)\\
&=&|A(D_{\nu})|\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{\nu}-10}\right).\end{array}

Therefore,
c_{\nu}\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{3\sqrt{\nu}-10}
, which completes the proof.
∎

The main goal of this section is to prove a lower bound for
c_{\nu}
. The main idea of how we obtain the lower bound for all
\nu
is roughly the following: we first prove (in Theorem
4.3
) that
c_{\nu}=\frac{1}{4}+\Omega(\nu^{-2/3})
for every
\nu\in N^{*}
,
where
N^{*}=\{n_{i}^{*}:\ i\in\mathbb{N},i\geq 2\}
(the elements of

will be defined later).
Thus, for any
\nu\geq n_{2}^{*}
, we may assume
n_{k-1}^{*}\leq\nu<n_{k}^{*}
for some

. And we will show that the gap between these two numbers is not too large compared to
\nu
(namely,
n_{k}^{*}-n_{k-1}^{*}=O(\nu^{2/3})
). As
c_{\nu}
is non-increasing, we conclude that
c_{\nu}\geq c_{n^{*}_{k}}=\frac{1}{4}+\Omega({n^{*}_{k}}^{-2/3})=\frac{1}{4}+\Omega({(\nu+O(\nu^{2/3}))}^{-2/3})=\frac{1}{4}+\Omega({\nu}^{-2/3})
. For each integer
k\geq 2
, let

\displaystyle n_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{2k-2i^{2}-i}{2}\right\rfloor+i\right)

\displaystyle=2k+4k\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor-2\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{k/2}+1}{2}\right\rfloor-4\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}i^{2}

\displaystyle=2k+4k\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor-2\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{k/2}+1}{2}\right\rfloor-\frac{2}{3}\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor+1)(2\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor+1).

Theorem 4.3
.

For any integer
k\geq 7
, we have
c_{n_{k}^{*}}\geq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8{n_{k}^{*}}^{2/3}-4}
.

Proof.

For any positive integer
k\geq 7
and
0\leq q\leq z=\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor
,
let
f(q)=\left\lfloor\frac{2k-2q^{2}-q}{2}\right\rfloor
. Let
S_{q}
be any set of size
2f(q)+q
.

Claim A:
Let
(X,Y)
be a random partition of
S_{q}
, with
|X|=f(q)+q
and
|Y|=f(q)
.
For any distinct
s_{1},s_{2}\in S_{q}
, the probability that
s_{1}\in X
and
s_{2}\in Y
is at least
\frac{k}{4k-2}
.

Proof of Claim A:
The probability that
s_{1}\in X
is
\frac{f(q)+q}{2f(q)+q}
.
And given that
s_{1}\in X
, the probability that
s_{2}\in Y
is
\frac{f(q)}{2f(q)+q-1}
.
So the probability that
s_{1}\in X
and
s_{2}\in Y
is the following:

\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)=\frac{f(q)+q}{2f(q)+q}\times\frac{f(q)}{2f(q)+q-1}.

We first consider the case when
q
is even in which case
f(q)=k-q^{2}-\frac{q}{2}
.
In this case,
\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)\geq\frac{k}{4k-2}
is equivalent to the
following inequality.

\frac{(k-q^{2}+q/2)(k-q^{2}-q/2)}{(2k-2q^{2})(2k-2q^{2}-1)}\geq\frac{k}{4k-2}.

This is equivalent to the following:

(4k-2)\left[(k-q^{2})^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}\right]\geq 4k(k-q^{2})\left(k-q^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right).

Subtracting
4k(k-q^{2})^{2}
from both sides gives us the following equivalent inequality.

(-2)\cdot\left[(k-q^{2})^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}\right]-kq^{2}\geq-2k(k-q^{2}).

Thus,

0\geq 2k^{2}-4kq^{2}+2q^{4}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}+kq^{2}-2k(k-q^{2}).

We note that this is equivalent to
0\geq 2q^{4}-kq^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
. We recall that
q\leq\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor
, which implies that

kq^{2}\geq 2q^{4}
, which in turn implies that
0\geq 2q^{4}-kq^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
holds. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)\geq\frac{k}{4k-2}
also holds.

We now consider the case when
q
is odd, in which case
f(q)=k-q^{2}-\frac{q}{2}-0.5
.
Let
k^{*}=k-1/2
and note that the following holds.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(q)+q}{2f(q)+q}\times\frac{f(q)}{2f(q)+q-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-q^{2}+q/2-0.5)(2k-q^{2}-q/2-0.5)}{(2k-2q^{2}-1)(2k-2q^{2}-1-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k^{*}-q^{2}+q/2)(k^{*}-q^{2}-q/2)}{(2k^{*}-2q^{2})(2k^{*}-2q^{2}-1)}.

Using the same computations as above (but with
k^{*}
instead of
k
) we note that
\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)\geq\frac{k^{*}}{4k^{*}-2}
is equivalent with
0\geq 2q^{4}-k^{*}q^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
.
As
kq^{2}\geq 2q^{4}
we have
2q^{4}\leq kq^{2}=k^{*}q^{2}+q^{2}/2
, which implies that
0\geq 2q^{4}-k^{*}q^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{2}
holds.
Therefore the following holds.

\mathbb{P}(s_{1}\in X,\;s_{2}\in Y)\geq\frac{k^{*}}{4k^{*}-2}=\frac{k-1/2}{4k-4}\geq\frac{k}{4k-2}.

This completes the proof of Claim A.
(
\Box
)

Let
n_{q}=2f(q)+q
and
let
D
be any acyclic digraph whose longest path has order
n_{k}^{*}
. Recall that
n^{*}_{k}
is

n_{k}^{*}=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}(2f(i)+i)=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}n_{i}.

Claim B:

n_{k}^{*}\geq k^{3/2}
.

Proof of Claim B:
Let
z=\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor
. As
\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}-3k^{-1/2}-\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{6}k^{-1}-k^{-3/2}\geq 1
for all
k\geq 7
the following holds.

\displaystyle n_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{z}(2f(i)+i)

\displaystyle=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{z}\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{2k-2i^{2}-i}{2}\right\rfloor+i\right)

\displaystyle=2k-2\left\lfloor\frac{z+1}{2}\right\rfloor+2\sum_{i=1}^{z}\left(2\left(\frac{2k-2i^{2}-i}{2}\right)+i\right)

\displaystyle=2k-2\left\lfloor\frac{z+1}{2}\right\rfloor+4kz-4\sum_{i=1}^{z}i^{2}

\displaystyle=2k-2\left\lfloor\frac{z+1}{2}\right\rfloor+4kz-4\times\frac{2z^{3}+3z^{2}+z}{6}

\displaystyle=2k-2\left\lfloor\frac{z+1}{2}\right\rfloor+4kz-\frac{4z^{3}+6z^{2}+2z}{3}.

By using
\sqrt{x}-1\leq\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor\leq\sqrt{x}
, we have

\displaystyle n_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq 2k-2\left(\frac{(k/2)^{1/2}+1}{2}\right)+4k((k/2)^{1/2}-1)-\frac{\sqrt{2}k^{3/2}+3k+\sqrt{2}k^{1/2}}{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}k^{3/2}-3k-((k/2)^{1/2}+1)-\frac{\sqrt{2}k^{1/2}}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}k^{3/2}-3k-\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{6}k^{1/2}-1

\displaystyle=k^{3/2}\left(\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}-3k^{-1/2}-\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{6}k^{-1}-k^{-3/2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq k^{3/2}.

This completes the proof of Claim B.
(
\Box
)

Let
T_{1}
contain all sources in
D
and let

T_{2}
contain all sources in

.
Continuing in this way we obtain the sets

).

in

.

Let

Let

We now produce a ”random” solution as follows. Pick a partition

of

at random such that

and

Claim C:

\mathbb{E}(w(X^{*},Y^{*}))\geq w(D^{\prime})\times\frac{k}{4k-2}

Proof of Claim C:
Let

be an arbitrary arc.
If

for some

then

belongs to the dicut
(X^{*},Y^{*})

by Claim A. So assume that

and

where

.

Let

be arbitrary and note that the probability that

is the following.

\mathbb{P}(w_{i}\in X^{*})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\vskip 3.41418pt\frac{f(|i|)+|i|}{2f(|i|)+|i|}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4f(|i|)/|i|+2}&&\mbox{if $i<0$},\\
\frac{f(i)}{2f(i)+i}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4f(i)/i+2}&&\mbox{if $i\geq 0$}.\\

When

Analogously,

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(u\in X^{*})

And then using Claim A to show that

\Box

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
k_{2}=\frac{1}{8\times 3^{2/3}}
. Then for any
\nu\geq 1

.

Proof.

Note that when
\nu<36

and Theorem

. So we assume
\nu\geq n^{*}_{7}=36
.
Suppose without loss of generality that
n^{*}_{k-1}\leq\nu\leq n^{*}_{k}
. Recall the definition of
n_{k}^{*}
:

\displaystyle n_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=2k+2\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}\left(2\left\lfloor\frac{2k-2i^{2}-i}{2}\right\rfloor+i\right)

\displaystyle=2k+4k\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor-2\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{k/2}+1}{2}\right\rfloor-4\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor}i^{2}

For
k\geq 7
, we have

\displaystyle n_{k}^{*}-n_{k-1}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2+4k(\lfloor\sqrt{k/2}\rfloor-\lfloor\sqrt{(k-1)/2}\rfloor)+4\lfloor\sqrt{(k-1)/2}\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq 2+4k+2\sqrt{2(k-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 6k.

The last inequalty follows from the fact that

. By Claim B in Theorem

,

k(l)>0
such that the following holds.
For all arc-weighted digraphs
D
where each arc has weight at least one and
\mathrm{circ}(D)\leq l
we have
{\rm mac}(D)\geq\frac{w(D)}{4}+k(l)\cdot w(D)^{0.6}
.

The following holds for small
\nu
.

Theorem A

c_{2}=1
,
c_{3}=c_{4}=\frac{1}{2}
,
c_{5}=c_{6}=\frac{2}{5}
,
c_{7}=\frac{3}{8}
and
c_{8}=\frac{4}{11}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be an acyclic digraph whose longest path has order
\nu
, where
\nu\in\{2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}
.
Let
S_{1}
denote all sources in
D
. For all
i=2,3,\ldots,\nu
let
D_{i}=D_{i-1}-S_{i-1}
and let
S_{i}
be all sources in
D_{i}
.

Note that
V(D)=S_{1}\cup\cdots\cup S_{\nu}
and every arc in
D
is a
(S_{i},S_{j})
-arc for some
i<j
.
Let
D^{\prime}
be obtained from
D
by contracting each
S_{i}
into a vertex
s_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,\nu
.
Let the weight of an arc
s_{i}s_{j}
in
D^{\prime}
be
w(s_{i}s_{j})=w(S_{i},S_{j})
(i.e. the sum of the weights
of all
(S_{i},S_{j})
-arcs).

Note that
{\rm mac}(D)\geq{\rm mac}(D^{\prime})
as any dicut
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
in
D^{\prime}
can be made into a dicut of the same weight in
D
by expanding each
s_{i}
to
S_{i}
.
Let
l_{2}=1
,
l_{3}=l_{4}=\frac{1}{2}
,
l_{5}=l_{6}=\frac{2}{5}
,
l_{7}=\frac{3}{8}
and
l_{8}=\frac{4}{11}
. Clearly
c_{2}=1
, as dicut
(\{s_{1}\},\{s_{2}\})
contains all arcs in
D^{\prime}
(as
A(D^{\prime})=\{s_{1}s_{2}\}
), and therefore
{\rm mac}(D^{\prime})=w(D^{\prime})
.

\nu

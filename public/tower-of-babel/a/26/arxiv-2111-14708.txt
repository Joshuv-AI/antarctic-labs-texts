Ergodic aspects of trading with threshold strategies Thanks: Both authors benefitted from the support of the “Lendület” grant LP 2015-6 of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.
Attila Lovas, Miklós Rásonyi

Abstract
To profit from price oscillations, investors frequently use threshold-type strategies where changes in the portfolio position are triggered by some indicators reaching prescribed levels. In this paper we investigate threshold-type strategies in the context of ergodic control. We make the first steps towards their optimization by proving ergodic properties of related functionals. Assuming Markovian price increments satisfying a minorization condition and (one-sided) boundedness we show, in particular, that for given thresholds, the distribution of the gains converges in the long run. We also extend recent results on the stability of overshoots of random walks from the i.i.d. increment case to Markovian increments, under suitable conditions.



Keywords:
Minorization, random walk, stochastic stability, threshold-type strategies,
optimal investment.

3
Trading with threshold strategies

Let the observed price of an asset be denoted by
A_{t}
at time
t\in\mdmathbb{N}
.
We may think of the price of a futures contract, for instance. Positive
prices can also be handled, see Remark
3.4
below.
We assume a simple dynamics:

A_{t}=\mu t+S_{t},

(7)

where
\mu\in\mdmathbb{R}
,
S_{0}\in\mdmathbb{R}
are constants and
S_{t}:=S_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}X_{j}
for a Markov process
X

with values in
[-M,M]
for some
M>0
and satisfying Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
.

In this price model,
\mu t
represents the drift (or “trend”) and
S_{t}

performs fluctuations around the trend (the martingale part).
The minorization condition (
2
) is easy to interpret: whatever the current increment

x\in[-M,M]
of the fluctuations

S
is, with a positive probability the next increment will be small (that is, the price change
will be close to

0
) and the movements of
S
are diffuse, more precisely, they have a
diffuse component.

The practical situation we have in mind is an algorithm that tries to “buy low and sell high”
an asset at high (but not ultra-high) frequencies, revising the portfolio, say, once every second or every minute.
Such an algorithm is run continuously during the
trading day which can be considered a “stationary” environment as economic fundamentals do
not change significantly on such timescales.

It seems that ergodic stochastic control is the right settings for such investment
problems: the algorithms does the same thing “forever” and its average perfomance should
be optimized. We remark that
\mu
in such a setting is negligible and can safely be assumed
0
, as
often done in papers on high-frequency trading. Our results work nevertheless for arbitrary
\mu
which
is of interest for trading on different timescales (e.g. daily revision of a portfolio for several
months).

Now we set up the elements of our trading mechanism. Let the thresholds
\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}\in\mdmathbb{R}
be
fixed, satisfying
\underline{\theta}<0<\overline{\theta}
. We interpret
\underline{\theta}
as a level for
S_{t}
under
which it is advisable to buy the asset. Analogously, it is recommended to
sell
the asset if
S_{t}

exceeds
\overline{\theta}
. Thus, to realize a “buying low, selling high”-type strategy,
the asset should be bought at the times
T_{n}
,
n\geq 1
and sold at the times
L_{n}
,

n\geq 1
, realizing the profit

A_{L_{n}}-A_{T_{n}}=S_{L_{n}}-S_{T_{n}}+\mu(L_{n}-T_{n}).

(8)

We now explain the significance of Theorem
2.6
in studying optimal trading with threshold
strategies. An investor aims to maximize in
\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}

the long-term average utility from wealth, that is,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[u(S_{L_{k}(\theta)}-S_{T_{k}(\theta)}+\mu(L_{k}(\theta)-T_{k}(\theta)))-p(L_{k}(\theta)-T_{k}(\theta))\right]}{n},

(9)

where
u:\mdmathbb{R}\to\mdmathbb{R}
is a utility function and
L_{k}(\theta),T_{k}(\theta)
refer to
the respective stopping times defined in terms of the parameter

\theta=(\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta})
. The function
p:\mdmathbb{R}_{+}\to\mdmathbb{R}_{+}

serves to penalize long waiting times.

Remark 3.1
.

If the price is modelled by processes with continuous trajectories, as in

[
6
,
8
,
20
,
22
]
then the thresholds are hit precisely and the profit realized
between
T_{n}
and
L_{n}
is exactly
\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta}
. In the present
setting (just like in the case of continuous-time processes with jumps), the profit
realized may be significantly different due to the overshoot (resp. undershoot) of the level
\overline{\theta}

(resp.
\underline{\theta}
). From the point of view of ergodic control, it is crucial to establish
that these overshoots/undershoots tend to a limiting law, which is the central concern of our present paper.

According to Theorem
2.6
, the limsup in the above expression is, in fact, a limit, for a large
class of
u
,
p
. One could easily incorporate various types of transaction costs in the model.

Example 3.2
.

Let
u,p
be non-decreasing functions that are bounded from above (e.g.

can be the exponential utility, expressing
high risk-aversion),
and assume
\mu\geq 0
. Since

is necessarily bounded

, (

)
holds with the choice
\phi(U_{n}):=u(S_{L_{k}}-S_{T_{k}}+\mu(L_{k}-T_{k}))-p(L_{k}-T_{k})
and the limsup is a limit in (
11
) above.

Remark 3.3
.

In the alternative setting of Theorem
2.7
above (with
\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}
),
the trader sells one unit of the financial asset at
\tilde{L}_{n}
(shortselling) and then closes the position
at
\tilde{T}_{n}

thus realizing a profit

-(A_{\tilde{T}_{n}}-A_{\tilde{L}_{n}})=S_{\tilde{L}_{n}}-S_{\tilde{T}_{n}}-\mu(\tilde{L}_{n}-\tilde{T}_{n}).

(10)

This is the analogue (with short positions) of the long-position strategy realizing (
8
).
Theorem
2.7
implies that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[u(S_{\tilde{L}_{k}(\theta)}-S_{\tilde{T}_{k}(\theta)}+\mu(\tilde{L}_{k}(\theta)-\tilde{T}_{k}(\theta)))-{}p(\tilde{L}_{k}(\theta)-\tilde{T}_{k}(\theta))\right]}{n}

is a limit in this case, too.

In future work, we intend to optimize

by means of adaptive control, using recursive schemes

algorithm, see

]
and Section 6 of
[

{S_{L_{k}}-S_{T_{k}}+\mu(L_{k}-T_{k})}

This is precisely the content of

Remark 3.4
.

In an alternative setting,

may model the

of an asset. In that case
investing one dollar between

and

yields
\exp\left(S_{L_{n}}-S_{T_{n}}+\mu(L_{n}-T_{n})\right)

Let

,
p:\mdmathbb{R}_{+}\to\mdmathbb{R}_{+}
be non-decreasing functions,

(such as a negative power utility function).
In this setting the optimization

\max_{\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[u(\exp\left(S_{L_{k}(\theta)}-S_{T_{k}(\theta)}+\mu(L_{k}(\theta)-T_{k}(\theta))\right))-p(L_{k}(\theta)-T_{k}(\theta))\right]}{n}

corresponds to maximizing the utility of the long-term investment of one dollar (minus an impatience

strategies controlled by

. When

, the limsup is a limit, again by Theorem

.

[

, only some of the directly related papers.
Formulations as optimal stopping problems with discounting appear e.g. in

In our setting of intraday

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

Iterated random function representation of Markov chains on standard Borel spaces is a commonly used construction, see e.g.

[
5
,
4
]

is shown in Lemma

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
and
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
be i.i.d. sequences, independent of each other, and
also independent of
\sigma(X_{0},S_{0})
, moreover let
\xi_{0},\eta_{0}
be uniform on
[0,1]
. Then there exists a
map
\Phi:\mathcal{X}\times[0,1]\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{X}
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and
u\in[0,1]
,
we have

\forall v\in[0,\alpha)\quad\Phi(x,u,v)=h(2u-1),

(13)

where
h,\alpha>0
are as in Assumption
2.1
.
Furthermore, the process
(X_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
given by the recursion
X_{0}^{\prime}=X_{0}
,

X_{n+1}^{\prime}=\Phi(X_{n}^{\prime},\xi_{n+1},\eta_{n+1})
,
n\in\mdmathbb{N}

is a version of
(X_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
, let us consider the decomposition

P(x,A)=\alpha\ell(A)+(1-\alpha)q(x,A),

where by Assumption
2.1
,

q(x,A)=\frac{P(x,A)-\alpha\ell(A)}{1-\alpha}

is a probability kernel. For
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
u,v\in[0,1]
, we define

\Phi(x,u,v)=\mathds{1}_{\{v<\alpha\}}\,h(2u-1)+\mathds{1}_{\{v\geq\alpha\}}\,q^{-1}(x,u),

where

q^{-1}(x,u):=\inf\{r\in\mdmathbb{Q}\mid q(x,(-\infty,r])\geq u\}
,
u\in[0,1]

is the pseudoinverse of the cumulative distribution function
r\mapsto q(x,(-\infty,r))
,
x\in\mathcal{X}=(-\infty,M]
.

Obviously, (
13
) holds true, and thus for any fixed

, the random map
x\mapsto\Phi(x,u,\eta_{n+1})
is constant on
\mathcal{X}
with probability
\alpha
showing that

forgets its previous state with positive probability. This observation will play a central role later.

On the other hand, by the definition of the pseudoinverse,
\mathrm{Law}(q^{-1}(x,\xi_{0}))=q(x,\cdot)
,
and thus we can write

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{P}(\Phi(x,\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in A)

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{P}(\Phi(x,\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in A;\eta_{0}<\alpha)+\mdmathbb{P}(\Phi(x,\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in A;\eta_{0}\geq\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mdmathbb{P}(h(2\xi_{0}-1)\in A)+(1-\alpha)\mdmathbb{P}(q^{-1}(x,\xi_{0})\in A)

\displaystyle=\alpha\ell(A)+(1-\alpha)q(x,A)=P(x,A).

To sum up, the chains
(X_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
and
(X_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
have the same transition kernel, and their initial states also coincide showing that these processes are versions of each other.
∎

,

,

,

In what follows, we are going to prove that the minorization property of

is
inherited by

\tilde{\kappa}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U})\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
y\in\mathcal{U}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U})
,

Q(y,B)\geq\tilde{\kappa}(B).

(14)

For
n\in\mdmathbb{N}_{+}
, we define
\tau_{n}=\sup\{t\in\mdmathbb{N}\mid\eta_{L_{n}+k}<\alpha,\,k=1,\ldots,t\}
. Clearly,

\tau_{n}
is independent of
U_{n}
, moreover it follows a
\mathrm{Geo}(1-\alpha)
distribution counting the number of
failures until the first success i.e.
\mdmathbb{P}(\tau_{n}=j)=\alpha^{j}(1-\alpha)
,
j\in\mdmathbb{N}
.

Now, let
y=(\underline{x},\underline{s},\overline{x},\overline{s},r)\in\mathcal{U}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U})
be arbitrary and fixed. By the tower rule, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q(y,B)&=\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B\mid U_{n}=y\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B\mid U_{n}=y,\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\mdmathbb{P}(\tau_{n}=k\mid U_{n}=y)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B\mid U_{n}=y,\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)\\
&\geq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,L_{n+1}\leq L_{n}+k\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha),\end{split}

(15)

where we used that the sigma algebras
\sigma(\xi_{L_{n}+k},\,\eta_{L_{n}+k},\,k\geq 1)
and
\sigma(U_{n})
are independent, moreover on sets
\{S_{L_{n}}=\bar{s},\tau_{n}=k\}
, we have

(X_{L_{n}+j},S_{L_{n}+j})=\left(h(2\xi_{L_{n}+j}-1),\bar{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}h(2\xi_{L_{n}+j}-1)\right),\,\,1\leq j\leq k

(16)

implying that
U_{n+1}
and
(X_{T_{n}},S_{T_{n}},X_{L_{n}},L_{n}-T_{n})
are
conditionally independent given
\{S_{L_{n}}=\bar{s},\tau_{n}=k\}
whenever
k\geq 1
.

Furthermore, we can write

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,L_{n+1}\leq L_{n}+k\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)=\\
&=\sum_{l=2}^{k}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,L_{n+1}=L_{n}+l\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\\
&=\sum_{l=2}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{l-1}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,T_{n+1}=L_{n}+j,\,L_{n+1}=L_{n}+l\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\end{split}

(17)

Let us introduce the auxiliary random walk
W_{n}=\overline{s}+h\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2\xi_{i}-1)
, and we introduce the associated quantities
L_{0}^{W}:=0
and for
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
,

T_{n+1}^{W}:=\min\{k>L_{n}^{W}:\ W_{k}<\underline{\theta}\},\quad L_{n+1}^{W}:=\min\{k>T_{n+1}^{W}:\ W_{k}>\overline{\theta}\}.

Similarly, we define
U_{n}^{W}=\left(h(2\xi_{T_{n}^{W}}-1),W_{T_{n}^{W}},h(2\xi_{L_{n}^{W}}-1),W_{L_{n}^{W}},L_{n}^{W}-T_{n}^{W}\right)
,
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
.

Obviously, for fixed
1\leq j<l\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mdmathbb{P}&\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,T_{n+1}=L_{n}+j,\,L_{n+1}=L_{n}+l\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)=\\
&=\mdmathbb{P}(U_{1}^{W}\in B,\,T_{1}^{W}=j,\,L_{1}^{W}=l).\end{split}

(18)

We estimate this probability from below by taking into account only trajectories
that consist of just one decreasing and one increasing segment (see Figure
1
for
an illustration).

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mdmathbb{P}(U_{1}^{W}\in B,\,T_{1}^{W}=j,\,L_{1}^{W}=l)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2^{l}}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{1}^{W}\in B,\,T_{1}^{W}=j,\,L_{1}^{W}=l\middle|\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\},\,\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{l}\{\xi_{i}\geq 1/2\}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{l}}\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{1}^{W}\in B,\,W_{j}<\underline{\theta}\leq W_{j-1},\,W_{l-1}\leq\overline{\theta}<W_{l}\middle|\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\},\,\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{l}\{\xi_{i}\geq 1/2\}\right)\end{split}

(19)

Trajectories of
(W_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
with one local minimum at
n=5
(
\overline{s}=1
,
h=1
).

Note that the conditional distribution of
(W_{1},\ldots,W_{l})
given
\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\}
and
\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{l}\{\xi_{i}\geq 1/2\}
coincides with the distribution of
(W^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,W^{\prime}_{l})
, where

W^{\prime}_{m}:=\overline{s}-h\sum_{i=1}^{\min(j,m)}\xi_{i}+h\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}\xi_{i},\,\,1\leq m\leq l

with the convention that empty sums are defined to be zero.

Using this, and that
B\subset\mathcal{U}=(-\infty,0)\times(-\infty,\underline{\theta})\times(0,M]\times(\overline{\theta},\overline{\theta}+M)\times(\mdmathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})
, we can write

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left(U_{1}^{W}\in B,\,W_{j}<\underline{\theta}\leq W_{j-1},\,W_{l-1}\leq\overline{\theta}<W_{l}\middle|\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\},\,\bigcap_{i=j+1}^{l}\{\xi_{i}\geq 1/2\}\right)=

\displaystyle=\mdmathbb{P}\left((-h\xi_{j},W^{\prime}_{j},h\xi_{l},W^{\prime}_{l},l-j)\in B,\,\,\underline{\theta}\leq W^{\prime}_{j-1},\,W^{\prime}_{l-1}\leq\overline{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{(-hw,\overline{s}-hu-hw,hz,\overline{s}-hu-hw+hv+hz,l-j)\in B}\times

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}_{\underline{\theta}\leq\overline{s}-hu}\times\mathds{1}_{\overline{s}-hu-hw+hv\leq\overline{\theta}}\times f_{j-1}(u)\times f_{l-1-j}(v)\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}z

where for
m\in\mdmathbb{N}_{+}
,
f_{m}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
stands for the probability density function of the sum
of
m
independent random variables each having a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
.
Now, we can evaluate the quadruple integral using the substitution

x_{1}=-hw
,
x_{2}=\overline{s}-hu-hw
,
x_{3}=hz
,
x_{4}=\overline{s}-hu-hw+hv+hz
,
and thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{(-hw,\overline{s}-hu-hw,hz,\overline{s}-hu-hw+hv+hz,l-j)\in B}\times

\displaystyle\times\mathds{1}_{\underline{\theta}\leq\overline{s}-hu}\times\mathds{1}_{\overline{s}-hu-hw+hv\leq\overline{\theta}}\times f_{j-1}(u)f_{l-1-j}(v)\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}w\mathrm{d}z=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{4}}\int_{\overline{\theta}}^{\overline{\theta}+M}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{-\infty}^{\underline{\theta}}\int_{-h}^{0}\mathds{1}_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},l-j)\in B}\times\mathds{1}_{\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}}\times\mathds{1}_{x_{4}-x_{3}\leq\overline{\theta}}\times

\displaystyle\times f_{j-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\times f_{l-1-j}\left(\frac{x_{4}-x_{3}-x_{2}}{h}\right)\,\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\mathrm{d}x_{3}\mathrm{d}x_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},l-j)\in B}\times g_{\overline{s},j,l-j}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}),

where
\mathbf{x}
is a shorthand notation for
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
,
\lambda
is the
Lebesgue measure on
\mdmathbb{R}^{4}
, and
\mathcal{V}
is used for

(-\infty,0)\times(-\infty,\underline{\theta})\times(0,M]\times(\overline{\theta},\overline{\theta}+M)
, moreover for
j,m>1

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})&=\frac{1}{h^{4}}\mathds{1}_{\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}}\times\mathds{1}_{x_{4}-x_{3}\leq\overline{\theta}}\times\mathds{1}_{x_{1}\in[-h,0]}\times\mathds{1}_{x_{3}\in[0,h]}\\
&\times f_{j-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\times f_{m-1}\left(\frac{x_{4}-x_{3}-x_{2}}{h}\right).\end{split}

(20)

Substituting this back into (
17
) and reindexing by
m=l-j
yields

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{P}\left(U_{n+1}\in B,\,L_{n+1}\leq L_{n}+k\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{l=2}^{k}\sum_{j=2}^{l-2}\frac{1}{2^{l}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},l-j)\in B}\times g_{\overline{s},j,l-j}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{m=2}^{k-2}\sum_{j=2}^{k-m}\frac{1}{2^{j+m}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},m)\in B}\times g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

and thus by (
15
), we arrive at

\displaystyle Q(y,B)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{V}}\sum_{k=4}^{\infty}\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)\sum_{m=2}^{k-2}\sum_{j=2}^{k-m}\frac{1}{2^{j+m}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},m)\in B}\times g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},m)\in B}\times\mathds{1}_{m\geq 2}\times\sum_{k=m+2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{k-m}\frac{\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)}{2^{j+m}}g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{V}}\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{x},m)\in B}\times\mathds{1}_{m\geq 2}\times\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\sum_{k=j+m}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)}{2^{j+m}}g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\mathds{1}_{m\geq 2}\times\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j+m}}{2^{j+m}}g_{\overline{s},j,m}(\mathbf{x})\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})\otimes\delta(\mathrm{d}m),

where
\delta
is the usual counting measure on
\mdmathbb{N}
.

Let us observe that on
\mathrm{Supp}(g)\subseteq\mathcal{U}
,
\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}\leq\underline{\theta}+h
.
Now, we fix
0<\tilde{\gamma}<\min\left((\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta})/h,1\right)
and consider
only
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
satisfying
\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}\leq\underline{\theta}+\tilde{\gamma}h
. Furthermore, since the jumps of
(S_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
are bounded from above by
M
, we have
\overline{\theta}<\overline{s}<\overline{\theta}+M
hence for the argument of
f_{j-1}
in (
20
), we get

0<\frac{\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta}}{h}-\tilde{\gamma}\leq\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\leq\frac{\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta}}{h}+\frac{M}{h}.

(21)

provided that
\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}\leq\underline{\theta}+\tilde{\gamma}h
.

Introducing
\omega_{j}=\min\{f_{j-1}(t)\mid(\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta})/h-\tilde{\gamma}\leq t\leq(\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta})/h+M/h\}
, we arrive at the estimate

f_{j-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\geq\omega_{j}\times\mathds{1}_{\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}\leq\underline{\theta}+\tilde{\gamma}h}

which is uniform in
\overline{s}
, and
\omega_{j}>0
whenever
j>(\overline{\theta}-\underline{\theta})/h+M/h+1
.

If we put all together, for
C_{h}=\{(\mathbf{x},m)\in\mathcal{U}\mid x_{1}\in[-h,0];\,\underline{\theta}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}\leq\underline{\theta}+\tilde{\gamma}h;\,x_{3}\in[0,h];\,x_{4}-x_{3}\leq\overline{\theta};\,m\geq 2\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q(y,B)\geq\int_{B\cap C_{h}}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{h^{4}2^{m}}f_{m-1}\left(\frac{x_{4}-x_{3}-x_{2}}{h}\right)\times\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}\omega_{j}}{2^{j}}\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})\otimes\delta(\mathrm{d}m),

where the right hand-side depends only on
\alpha,M,h,\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}
, but not on
\overline{s}
hence (
14
) holds with

\tilde{\kappa}(B)=\int_{B\cap C_{h}}\frac{\alpha^{m}}{h^{4}2^{m}}f_{m-1}\left(\frac{x_{4}-x_{3}-x_{2}}{h}\right)\times\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}\omega_{j}}{2^{j}}\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})\otimes\delta(\mathrm{d}m)

(22)

which is obviously a non-zero Borel measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U})
.

To sum up, we showed that the chain
(U_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies the uniform minorization condition (
14
), and
thus by Theorem 16.2.2 in
[
12
]
, there exist a probability measure, independent of
(S_{0},X_{0})
, such that

\mathrm{Law}(U_{n})\to\upsilon
in total variation. Moreover, by Theorem 17.0.1 in
[
12
]
, for bounded measurable
functionals of
U_{n}
the law of large numbers holds as it is stated in Theorem
2.6
.
(Actually, even a central limit theorem could be established.) This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

The idea of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.6
, but the details
are somewhat simpler. We only sketch the main steps.

We consider the process
Z_{n}=(X_{L_{n}},S_{L_{n}})
,
n>1
which is obviously a time-homogeneous Markov chain
on the state space
\triangle:=\{(x,s)\in(0,M]^{2}\mid x\geq s\}
. In what follows, we prove that chain
(Z_{n})_{n>1}
satisfies a minorization condition similar to (
14
) in the proof of Theorem

2.6
. More precisely, we aim to show that there exist a non-zero Borel measure
\beta:\mathcal{B}(\triangle)\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
z\in\triangle
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\triangle)
,

Q(z,A)\geq\beta(A),

(23)

where
Q:\triangle\times\mathcal{B}(\triangle)\to[0,1]
is the transition kernel of the chain
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
.

Let
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
,
(\eta_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
, and
(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
be as in the the proof of Theorem

2.6
. For
z=(\overline{x},\overline{s})\in\triangle
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\triangle)
arbitrary and fixed,
by the tower rule, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q(z,A)&=\mdmathbb{P}\left(Z_{n+1}\in A\mid Z_{n}=z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mdmathbb{P}\left(Z_{n+1}\in A\mid Z_{n}=z,\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)\\
&\geq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{l=2}^{k}\mdmathbb{P}\left(Z_{n+1}\in A,\,L_{n+1}=L_{n}+l\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha),\end{split}

(24)

where we applied the same principles as in the derivation of (
15
).

Again by introducing the auxiliary random walk
W_{n}=\overline{s}+h\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2\xi_{j}-1)
, and the associated quantities
L_{n}^{W}
,
Z_{n}^{W}=(h(2\xi_{L_{n}^{W}}-1),W_{L_{n}^{W}})
,
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
, where
L_{0}^{W}:=0
and for
n\in\mdmathbb{N}
,

L_{n+1}^{W}:=\inf\{k>L_{n}^{W}\mid W_{k-1}\leq 0<W_{k}\}
, for fixed
2\leq l\leq k
, we can write

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mdmathbb{P}\left(Z_{n+1}\in A,\,L_{n+1}=L_{n}+l\mid S_{L_{n}}=\overline{s},\,\tau_{n}=k\right)=\mdmathbb{P}(Z_{1}^{W}\in A,\,L_{1}^{W}=l)\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{l}}\mdmathbb{P}\left((h(2\xi_{l}-1),W_{l})\in A,\,W_{l-1}\leq 0<W_{l}\middle|\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{l-1}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\},\,\{\xi_{l}\geq 1/2\}\right)\end{split}

(25)

where similarly to (
19
), we have taken into account trajectories decreasing in
l-1>0
steps
and increasing only in the
l
-th step. For the conditional probability, we have

\displaystyle\mdmathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left((h(2\xi_{l}-1),W_{l})\in A,\,W_{l-1}\leq 0<W_{l}\middle|\,\bigcap_{i=1}^{l-1}\{\xi_{i}<1/2\},\,\{\xi_{l}\geq 1/2\}\right)=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{(hv,\overline{s}-hu+hv)\in A}\times\mathds{1}_{\overline{s}-hu\leq 0}\times f_{l-1}(u)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\mathds{1}_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in A}\times f_{l-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,h]^{2}\cap A}f_{l-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

where
f_{m}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is the probability density function of the sum
of
m\geq 1
independent random variables each having a uniform distribution on
[0,1]
,

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
, and
\lambda
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mdmathbb{R}^{2}
.

Notice that if
(x_{1},x_{2})\in(0,h]\cap\triangle
then
0\leq x_{1}-x_{2}\leq\min(M,h)
, moreover
0<\overline{s}\leq M

hence we have
0\leq(\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1}))/h\leq M/h+\min(M/h,1)
, and thus we obtain

f_{l-1}\left(\frac{\overline{s}-(x_{2}-x_{1})}{h}\right)\geq\omega^{\prime}_{l}\times\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\min(M,h)\leq x_{1}-x_{2}},

(26)

where
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}
can be any fixed number in
(0,1)
, and
\omega_{l}^{\prime}=\inf\{f_{l-1}(t)\mid\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\min(M/h,1)\leq t\leq M/h+\min(M/h,1)\}
that is a positive number not depending on
\overline{s}
whenever
l>M/h+1
.

If we put all together, we obtain the following lower estimate

\displaystyle Q(z,A)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{(0,h]^{2}\cap A}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{l=2}^{k}\frac{\alpha^{k}(1-\alpha)}{2^{l}}\omega^{\prime}_{l}\times\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\min(M,h)\leq x_{1}-x_{2}}\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,h]^{2}\cap A}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\min(M,h)\leq x_{1}-x_{2}}\times\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{l}\omega^{\prime}_{l}}{2^{l}}\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}),

where the right hand-side depends only on
\alpha,M,h
and the choice of
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, but not depends on
z
, moreover

\mathcal{B}(\triangle)\ni A\mapsto\beta(A):=\int_{(0,h]^{2}\cap A}\mathds{1}_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}\min(M,h)\leq x_{1}-x_{2}}\times\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{l}\omega^{\prime}_{l}}{2^{l}}\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

defines a non-zeros Borel measure on
\mathcal{B}(\triangle)
, and thus (
23
) holds with this
\beta
.

To sum up, we proved that the chain
(Z_{n})_{n\in\mdmathbb{N}}
satisfies the uniform minorization condition, and thus
it admits a unique invariant probability measure
\pi
such that
\mathrm{Law}(Z_{n})\to\pi
at a
geometric rate in total variation as
n\to\infty
(See for example
Lemma 18.2.7 and Theorem 18.2.4 in
[
9
]
)
which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let Assumption

,

)

,

.

Remark 5.3
.

One could treat certain stochastic volatility-type models where
X_{t}=\sigma_{t}\varepsilon_{t}

with
\varepsilon_{t}
i.i.d. and
\sigma_{t}
a Markov process. In this case
X_{t}
is not Markovian
but the pair
(X_{t},\sigma_{t})
is. An extension to even more general non-Markovian
X_{t}
also seems possible.
We do not pursue these generalizations here.

6
Conclusions and future work

It would be desirable to remove the (one-sided) boundedness assumption on the state space
of
X_{t}
and relax the minorization condition (
2
) to some kind of local minorization. Due to the rather
complicated dynamics of
U_{t}
such extensions do not appear to be straightforward at all.

Removing the boundedness hypothesis on
u,p
in Section
3
would also be desirable but looks challenging.

Replacing the constant drift
\mu
by a functional of
X_{t}
would also significantly extend the
family of models in consideration.

An adaptive optimization of the thresholds
\underline{\theta},\overline{\theta}
could be performed
using the Kiefer-Wolfowitz algorithm, as proposed in Section 6 of
[
17
]
. There are a number of technical
conditions (e.g. mixing properties, smoothness of the laws) that need to be checked for applying

[
17
]
but the ergodic
properties established in this article strongly suggest that this programme indeed can be carried out.

Extensions to non-Markovian stochastic volatility models (see
[
7
,
10
]
) seem feasible but require
further technicalities.

The free Eggert-operad and an operadic description of groups
Roman Haak

Abstract




An Eggert-operad is a variant of Mac Lane’s notion of a PROP, for which not only bijective maps, but all maps between standard finite sets, are part of the structure. We construct the free Eggert-operad and prove the universal property it satisfies. Further we use the free Eggert-operad to define the Eggert-operad
\mathrm{GROUP}_{0}^{\mathrm{fp}}
via generators and relations and show that a
\mathrm{GROUP}_{0}^{\mathrm{fp}}
-algebra is a group and vice versa. At the end we prove operadic versions of some elementary facts about groups.

Introduction

In the sixties, Mac Lane introduced PROPs to describe algebraic structures such as
\operatorname{e}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-2.15277pt\operatorname{g}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-0.86108pt
monoids and algebras from a categorical standpoint
[
ML
,
p. 97]
,
[
ML2
]
. A PROP is given by a strict monoidal category
\mathcal{R}_{0}
with
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},+)
as monoid of objects such that the symmetric group
\operatorname{S}_{m}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{R}_{0}(m,m)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Roughly speaking, an element in
\mathcal{R}_{0}(m,n)
, where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, gives rise to an operation with
m
“inputs” and
n
“outputs”. Boardman and Vogt used PROPs to study infinite loop spaces
[
BV
,
p. 1119]
.

In the seventies May introduced operads
\mathcal{Q}=\big(\mathcal{Q}(m)\big)_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
as a restricted version of PROPs by putting the number of “outputs” to
1
. Roughly speaking, an element in
\mathcal{Q}(m)
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, gives rise to an operation with
m
“inputs” and
1
“output”. He used them to study iterated loop spaces
[
May
]
,
[
May2
]
. As to the scope of operads, they suffice to describe
\operatorname{e}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-2.15277pt\operatorname{g}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-0.86108pt
algebras, but not bialgebras
[
MSS
,
p. 7]
.

Eggert considers a variant of Mac Lane’s PROPs
[
Egg
]
. First, a preoperad is defined as a strict monoidal category
\mathcal{P}_{0}
with
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},+)
as monoid of objects. For example,
\mathrm{Map}_{0}
, given by

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\mathrm{Map}_{0}(m,n)&:=&\{f:\{1,{\dots},m\}\to\{1,{\dots},n\}\,|\,f\text{ is a map}\}\end{array}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, is a preoperad. The opposite category of
\mathrm{Map}_{0}
, denoted as
\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}
, is also a preoperad.

Then, an Eggert-operad is a tuple
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})
consisting of a preoperad
\mathcal{P}_{0}
and a morphism of preoperads
\mathfrak{p}_{0}:\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{P}_{0}
satisfying a Braiding Property and a Branching Property; cf. definition
1.9
.

So in contrast to Mac Lans’s PROPs, where to each bijective map
\{1,{\dots},m\}\to\{1,{\dots},m\}
an element of
\mathcal{R}_{0}(m,m)
is attached, in an Eggert-operad to each arbitrary map
\{1,{\dots},m\}\to\{1,{\dots},n\}
an element of
\mathcal{P}_{0}(m,n)
is attached, where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

For example, given a set
M
, we can define the Eggert-operad
\mathrm{END}_{0}(M)=(\mathrm{End}_{0}(M),\mathfrak{e}_{0})
with the underlying preoperad
\mathrm{End}_{0}(M)
given by

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\mathrm{End}_{0}(M)(m,n)&:=&\{f:M^{\times m}\to M^{\times n}\,|\,f\text{ is a map}\}\end{array}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a certain morphism
\mathfrak{e}_{0}:\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{End}_{0}(M)
of preoperads.

Given an Eggert-operad
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})
, a
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})
-algebra is then defined as a tuple
(M,\varphi_{0})
consisting of a set
M
and a morphism
\varphi:(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})\to\mathrm{END}_{0}(M)
.

A biindexed set is just a tuple of sets
X=\big(X(m,n)\big)_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
. We construct the free Eggert-operad
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)
and show that it satisfies a universal property. We point out how to construct Eggert-operads with generators and relations using the free Eggert-operad.

To illustrate the concept, we construct the Eggert-operad
\mathrm{GROUP}_{0}^{\mathrm{fp}}
and prove that every group is a
\mathrm{GROUP}_{0}^{\mathrm{fp}}
-algebra and vice versa.

We prove operadic versions of some elementary facts about groups such as the uniqueness of the inverse element.

It is a well-known fact that to define a group it suffices to require the associativity of the multiplication, left-neutrality and the existence of left-inverse elements. We provide an operadic version of this fact.

1.1
Basic notations

Notation 1.1
.

•

We write
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,m\geq 0\}

[m,n]:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,|\,m\leq i\leq n\}

•

Let

•

We denote the composition of morphisms from left to right;
\operatorname{i}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-2.15277pt\operatorname{e}\hskip-2.15277pt.\hskip-0.86108pt

•

In accordance with the notation of Eggert, we add an index

(

We define a

, as a strict monoidal category

Example 1.4

(
[
Egg
,
Def. 2.57]
)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We define

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\mathrm{Map}_{0}(m,n)&:=&\{f:[1,m]\to[1,n]\,|\,f\text{ is a map}\}.\end{array}

In this way we obtain a category
\mathrm{Map}_{0}
using identities
\operatorname{id}_{m}:=\operatorname{id}_{m,\mathrm{Map}_{0}}:=\operatorname{id}_{[1,m]}:[1,m]\to[1,m]
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and composition of maps.

Let
m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
f:[1,m]\to[1,n]
,
f^{\prime}:[1,m^{\prime}]\to[1,n^{\prime}]
be maps. We define the map

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}f\boxtimes f^{\prime}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{short}}}{{=}}&f\boxtimes_{\mathrm{Map}_{0}}f^{\prime}&:&[1,m+m^{\prime}]&\to&[1,n+n^{\prime}]\\
&&&&i&\mapsto&\begin{cases}if&\text{ if }i\in[1,m]\\
n+(i-m)f^{\prime}&\text{ if }i\in[m+1,m+m^{\prime}].\end{cases}\end{array}

This yields a functor

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}(\boxtimes)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{short}}}{{=}}&(\boxtimes_{\mathrm{Map}_{0}})&:&\mathrm{Map}_{0}\times\mathrm{Map}_{0}&\to&\mathrm{Map}_{0}\\
&&&&\big((m,m^{\prime})\xrightarrow{(f,f^{\prime})}(n,n^{\prime})\big)&\mapsto&(m+m^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f\boxtimes f^{\prime}}n+n^{\prime}).\end{array}

Endowed with the multiplication
(\boxtimes)
, the category
\mathrm{Map}_{0}
becomes a preoperad, again denoted as
\mathrm{Map}_{0}
.

We consider the opposite category

Let

(
[

We define an

2
The free Eggert-operad
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)

Throughout this section, let
X=(X,\mathrm{s}_{X},\mathrm{t}_{X})
be a biindexed set.

2.1
Words over the biindexed set
X

Definition 2.1
.

We define a
word
(over
X
) as a
(2k+1)
-tuple
w=(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1})
, where

\begin{array}[]{r c l l }\vskip 4.30554ptk&\in&\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
\vskip 4.30554pta_{2i+1}&=:&f_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}&\text{ for }i\in[0,k]\\
\vskip 4.30554pta_{2i}&=:&\big(l_{i},x_{i},r_{i}\big)\in\{\big(l,x,r\big)\,|\,l,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},x\in X\}&\text{ for }i\in[1,k]\end{array}

such that

\begin{array}[]{r c c c l l }&&f_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{t}_{\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}}&=&l_{i+1}+x_{i+1}\mathrm{s}_{X}+r_{i+1}&\text{ for }i\in[0,k-1]\\
\vskip 4.30554ptl_{i}+x_{i}\mathrm{t}_{X}+r_{i}&=&f_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{s}_{\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}}&&&\text{ for }i\in[1,k].\end{array}

Further we call
k
the
length
of the word
w
and denote it as
|w|:=k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We denote the set of words over
X
as
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
.

Let
w=(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1})\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word. We define its source
w\mathrm{s}:=a_{1}\mathrm{s}
and its target
w\mathrm{t}:=a_{2k+1}\mathrm{t}
.

Endowed with the source and target defined above,
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
becomes a biindexed set, again denoted as
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
.

We endow
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
with the composition

\begin{array}[]{r c l}(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1})\cdot(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},{\dots},a^{\prime}_{2k^{\prime}+1})&:=&(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k},a_{2k+1}\cdot a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},{\dots},a^{\prime}_{2k^{\prime}+1}),\end{array}

where
(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1}),(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},{\dots},a^{\prime}_{2k^{\prime}+1})\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
are words such that
(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1})\mathrm{t}=(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},{\dots},a^{\prime}_{2k^{\prime}+1})\mathrm{s}
. Note that
a_{2k+1}\cdot a^{\prime}_{1}
is the composite formed in
\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

With the identities
\operatorname{id}_{m,\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}=(\operatorname{id}_{m}^{\mathrm{op}})\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)(m,m)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and the composition defined above, the biindexed set
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
becomes a category, again denoted as
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
.

Note that
|w\cdot w^{\prime}|=|w|+|w^{\prime}|
for words
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
such that
w\mathrm{t}=w^{\prime}\mathrm{s}
.

We will often denote a word
\big(f^{\mathrm{op}}\big)\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length 0 as

\begin{array}[]{r c l}f^{\mathrm{op}}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{short}}}{{=}}&(f^{\mathrm{op}}).\end{array}

Let
w=(a_{1},{\dots},a_{2k+1})\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word. Note that there exist unique
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}
such that
a_{2i+1}=f_{i}^{\mathrm{op}}
for
i\in[0,k]
. Note that there exist unique
l_{i},r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
x_{i}\in X
such that
a_{2i}=\big(l_{i},x_{i},r_{i}\big)
for
i\in[1,k]
. We define the words

\begin{array}[]{r c c c l}w_{i}&:=&(\operatorname{id}_{l_{i}+x_{i}\mathrm{s}_{X}+r_{i}}^{\mathrm{op}},\big(l_{i},x_{i},r_{i}\big),\operatorname{id}_{l_{i}+x_{i}\mathrm{t}_{X}+r_{i}}^{\mathrm{op}})&\in&\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)\end{array}

of length 1 for
i\in[1,k]
. Further we define the word

\begin{array}[]{r c l}f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{j}\cdot f_{j}^{\mathrm{op}}&:=&\begin{cases}f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}&\text{ if }j=0\\
(f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{j-1}\cdot f_{j-1}^{\mathrm{op}})\cdot w_{j}\cdot f_{j}^{\mathrm{op}}&\text{ if }1\leq j\leq k\end{cases}\end{array}

for
j\in[0,k]
. Note that

\begin{array}[]{r c c c l}f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}&=&w&\in&\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X).\end{array}

We call
f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}
the
standard decomposition
of
w
.

Let

,

and

. We define

\begin{array}[]{r c l}f^{\mathrm{op}}\triangleright p&:=&f^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}^{\mathrm{op}}\\

\big(l,x,r\big)\triangleright p&:=&\big(l,x,r+p\big)\\
q\triangleleft\big(l,x,r\big)&:=&\big(q+l,x,r\big)\\
q\triangleleft\big(l,x,r\big)\triangleright p&:=&\big(q+l,x,r+p\big).\end{array}

Let
q,p,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

\begin{array}[]{r c l c l}w\triangleright p&:=&\big(a_{1}\triangleright p,{\dots},a_{2k+1}\triangleright p\big)&\in&\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)(m+p,n+p)\\

We will often denote a word of the form

as

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\big(l,x,r\big)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{short}}}{{=}}&(\operatorname{id}_{l+x\mathrm{s}_{X}+r}^{\mathrm{op}},\big(l,x,r\big),\operatorname{id}_{l+x\mathrm{t}_{X}+r}^{\mathrm{op}}).\end{array}

Remark 2.2
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

be words such that

. The following equations hold.

\begin{array}[]{r c l c r c l}0\triangleleft w&=&w&&w\triangleright 0&=&w\\

Lemma 2.5
.

1.

Let
d,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words such that
w\sim w^{\prime}
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}d\triangleleft w\triangleright b&\sim&d\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright b.\end{array}

2.

Let
d,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words such that
w\approx w^{\prime}
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}d\triangleleft w\triangleright b&\approx&d\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright b.\end{array}

3.

Let
u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words such that
u\approx u^{\prime}
and
v\approx v^{\prime}
. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}(u\triangleright v\mathrm{s})\cdot(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v)&\approx&(u^{\prime}\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime}).\end{array}

Proof.

Ad 1: In all four cases
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.}}{{\sim}}w^{\prime}
of definition
2.4
, where
i\in[1,4]
, this follows from remark
2.2
.

Ad 2: We may choose words
z_{0},{\dots},z_{k}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
such that
w=z_{0}
,
z_{k}=w^{\prime}
and
(z_{i},z_{i+1})\in M_{0,X}^{\prime}
for
i\in[0,k-1]
. By 1. and remark
2.2
we conclude that
(d\triangleleft z_{i}\triangleright b,d\triangleleft z_{i+1}\triangleright b)\in M_{0,X}^{\prime}
for
i\in[0,k-1]
, hence
d\triangleleft w\triangleright b\approx d\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright b
.

Ad 3: By 2. and
v\mathrm{s}=v^{\prime}\mathrm{s}
we have
(u\triangleright v\mathrm{s})\approx(u^{\prime}\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})
. By 2. and
u\mathrm{t}=u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}
we have
(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v)\approx(u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})
. Since
N_{0,X}
is a congruence relation, we conclude that
(u\triangleright v\mathrm{s})\cdot(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v)\approx(u^{\prime}\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})&\approx&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}).\end{array}

Proof.

We consider particular cases:

Case 1: We have
|w|,|w^{\prime}|\leq 1
.

Then we have
(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1.}}{{\sim}}(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})
, hence
(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\approx(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})
; cf. definition
2.4
.

Case 2: We have
|w^{\prime}|\leq 1
.

Let
k:=|w|
. If
k\leq 1
, Case 1 finishes the proof. Otherwise let
w=f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}
be the standard decomposition. We define the word
u:=f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k-1}\cdot f_{k-1}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
k-1
and the word
v:=w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
1
. Note that
w=u\cdot v
,
w\mathrm{s}=u\mathrm{s}
,
u\mathrm{t}=v\mathrm{s}
,
v\mathrm{t}=w\mathrm{t}
. Using induction over
k
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})&=&\big((u\cdot v)\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s}\big)\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}{{=}}&(u\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Case 1}}}{{\approx}}&(u\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(v\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\\
=&(u\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(v\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IH}}}{{\approx}}&(u\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(u\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\cdot(v\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}{{=}}&(u\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot\big((u\cdot v)\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\big)&=&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}).\end{array}

Case 3: We have
|w^{\prime}|\geq 2
.

Let
k^{\prime}:=|w^{\prime}|
. Let
w^{\prime}={f_{0}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}^{\prime}\cdot{f_{1}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\cdot{f_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{op}}
be the standard decomposition. We define the word
u^{\prime}:={f_{0}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w^{\prime}_{1}\cdot{f_{1}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}-1}\cdot{f^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}-1}}\hskip-12.91663pt^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
k^{\prime}-1
and the word
v^{\prime}:=w^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\cdot{f^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
1
. Note that
w^{\prime}=u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}
,
w^{\prime}\mathrm{s}=u^{\prime}\mathrm{s}
,
u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}=v^{\prime}\mathrm{s}
,
v^{\prime}\mathrm{t}=w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}
. Using induction over
k^{\prime}
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})&=&(w\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot\big(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft(u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})\big)\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}{{=}}&(w\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IH}}}{{\approx}}&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})\\
=&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Case 2}}}{{\approx}}&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft v^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}{{=}}&\big(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft(u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})\big)\cdot(w\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{t})&=&(w\mathrm{s}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\cdot(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}).\end{array}

∎

2.2

\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)

Definition 2.7
.

We define the factor category of
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
modulo
N_{0,X}

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)&:=&\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)/N_{0,X}.\end{array}

This construction is also known as quotient category.

We shall now endow the category
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
with a multiplication in order to obtain a preoperad, again denoted as
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
.
Let
m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)(m,n)
,
w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
be words. We define

\begin{array}[]{r c c c l}[w]\boxtimes[w^{\prime}]&:=&[(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})]&\in&\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)(m+m^{\prime},n+n^{\prime}),\end{array}

which is well-defined by lemma
2.5
.3. We shall verify in lemma
2.9
that
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
is in fact a preoperad. The upper index “pre” indicates that
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
will be the underlying preoperad of an operad
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)
, which we will introduce in definition
2.15
.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
m,n,o\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and

and
g\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}(n,o)
. Note that in
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
we have

.

Let
m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
f\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}(m,n)
and
f^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})
. Note that in

we have

.

Lemma 2.9
.

The category
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
together with the multiplication
(\boxtimes)
given by definition
2.7
is a preoperad.

Proof.

In the following proof we will make repeated use of remark
2.2
.

Functoriality of
(\boxtimes)
on identities: Let
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then in
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
we have

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&\vskip 4.30554pt\operatorname{id}_{m,\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{m^{\prime},\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}&=&[\operatorname{id}_{m,\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}]\boxtimes[\operatorname{id}_{m^{\prime},\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}]\\
=&[\operatorname{id}_{[1,m]}^{\mathrm{op}}]\boxtimes[\operatorname{id}_{[1,m^{\prime}]}^{\mathrm{op}}]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_BEM_RECHENREGELN_F\"{U}R_W\"{O}RTER_DER_L\"{A}NGE_NULL}}}{{=}}&[\operatorname{id}_{[1,m]}^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{[1,m^{\prime}]}^{\mathrm{op}}]\\
=&[\operatorname{id}_{[1,m+m^{\prime}]}^{\mathrm{op}}]&=&[\operatorname{id}_{m+m^{\prime},\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}]\\
=&\operatorname{id}_{m+m^{\prime},\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}.\end{array}

Functoriality of
(\boxtimes)
on composition: Let
u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words such that
u\mathrm{t}=v\mathrm{s}
and
u^{\prime}\mathrm{t}=v^{\prime}\mathrm{s}
. Then in
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
we have

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&([u]\boxtimes[u^{\prime}])\cdot([v]\boxtimes[v^{\prime}])&=&[(u\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft u^{\prime})]\cdot[(v\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})]\\
=&[(u\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})]\cdot[(u\mathrm{t}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(v\triangleright v^{\prime}\mathrm{s})]\cdot[(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})]&=&[(u\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})]\cdot[(v\mathrm{s}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(v\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{t})]\cdot[(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})]\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{VER1_LEM_FREEOPREX_INTERCHANGING_RULE}}}{{=}}&[(u\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})]\cdot[(v\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft u^{\prime})]\cdot[(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})]&=&[(u\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft u^{\prime})\cdot(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft v^{\prime})]\\
=&[\big((u\cdot v)\triangleright u^{\prime}\mathrm{s}\big)\cdot\big(v\mathrm{t}\triangleleft(u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})\big)]&=&[\big((u\cdot v)\triangleright(u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})\mathrm{s}\big)\cdot\big((u\cdot v)\mathrm{t}\triangleleft(u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime})\big)]\\
=&[u\cdot v]\boxtimes[u^{\prime}\cdot v^{\prime}].\end{array}

Associativity of
(\boxtimes)
: Let
w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words. Then in
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}([w]\boxtimes[w^{\prime}])\boxtimes[w^{\prime\prime}]&=&[(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})]\boxtimes[w^{\prime\prime}]\\
&=&[\Big(\big((w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\big)\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s}\Big)\cdot\Big(\big((w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\big)\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime}\Big)]\\
&=&[\big((w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s}\big)\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot\big((w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime}\big)]\\
&=&[\big((w\triangleright(w^{\prime}\mathrm{s}+w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\big)\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot\big((w\mathrm{t}+w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime}\big)]\\
&=&[\big(w\triangleright(w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\mathrm{s}\big)\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot\big(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft(w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime})\big)]\\
&=&[\Big(w\triangleright\big((w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime})\big)\mathrm{s}\Big)\cdot\Big(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft\big((w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime})\big)\Big)]\\
&=&[w]\boxtimes[(w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w^{\prime}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime})]\\
&=&[w]\boxtimes([w^{\prime}]\boxtimes[w^{\prime\prime}]).\end{array}

Left and right identity for
(\boxtimes)
: Let
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word. Then in
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)
we have

\begin{array}[]{c l c c l}&\operatorname{id}_{0,\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}\boxtimes\,[w]&\quad\quad\text{and}&&[w]\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{0,\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}\\
=&[\operatorname{id}_{0,\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}]\boxtimes[w]&&=&[w]\boxtimes[\operatorname{id}_{0,\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)}]\\
=&[\operatorname{id}^{\mathrm{op}}_{[1,0]}]\boxtimes[w]&&=&[w]\boxtimes[\operatorname{id}^{\mathrm{op}}_{[1,0]}]\\
=&[(\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}}\triangleright\,w\mathrm{s})\cdot(\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w)]&&=&[(w\triangleright\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}})]\\
=&[(\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{[1,w\mathrm{s}]}^{\mathrm{op}})\cdot(0\triangleleft w)]&&=&[(w\triangleright 0)\cdot(\operatorname{id}_{[1,w\mathrm{t}]}^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{[1,0]}^{\mathrm{op}})]\\
=&[\operatorname{id}_{[1,w\mathrm{s}]}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\,w]&&=&[w\cdot\operatorname{id}_{[1,w\mathrm{t}]}^{\mathrm{op}}]\\
=&[w]&&=&[w].\end{array}

∎

Remark 2.10
.

Let
w,w^{\prime},w^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be words. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}[w]\boxtimes[w^{\prime}]\boxtimes[w^{\prime\prime}]&=&[\big(w\triangleright(w^{\prime}\mathrm{s}+w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\big)\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime}\triangleright w^{\prime\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot\big((w\mathrm{t}+w^{\prime}\mathrm{t})\triangleleft w^{\prime\prime}\big)].\end{array}

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r c l}[\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{s},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(w\triangleright p)]&=&[(p\triangleleft w)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{t},p}^{\mathrm{op}}]\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{r c l}[\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft w)]&=&[(w\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}].\end{array}

Proof.

If
k:=|w|\leq 1
, this follows from
\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{s},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(w\triangleright p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2.}}{{\sim}}(p\triangleleft w)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{t},p}^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft w)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 3.}}{{\sim}}(w\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}
; cf. definition
2.4
.

Otherwise let
w=f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}
be the standard decomposition. We define the word
u:=f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k-1}\cdot f_{k-1}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
k-1
and the word
v:=w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
of length
1
. Note that
w=u\cdot v
,
w\mathrm{s}=u\mathrm{s}
,
u\mathrm{t}=v\mathrm{s}
,
v\mathrm{t}=w\mathrm{t}
. Using induction over
k
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}[\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{s},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(w\triangleright p)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}}{{=}}&[\mathsf{s}_{u\mathrm{s},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(u\triangleright p)\cdot(v\triangleright p)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IH}}}{{=}}&[(p\triangleleft u)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{u\mathrm{t},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(v\triangleright p)]&&\\
&=&[(p\triangleleft u)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{v\mathrm{s},p}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(v\triangleright p)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|v|=1}}{{=}}&[(p\triangleleft u)\cdot(p\triangleleft v)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{v\mathrm{t},p}^{\mathrm{op}}]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}}{{=}}&[(p\triangleleft w)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{w\mathrm{t},p}^{\mathrm{op}}]\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}[\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft w)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}}{{=}}&[\mathsf{s}_{p,u\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft u)\cdot(p\triangleleft v)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{IH}}}{{=}}&[(u\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,u\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft v)]&&\\
&=&[(u\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,v\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot(p\triangleleft v)]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|v|=1}}{{=}}&[(u\triangleright p)\cdot(v\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,v\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{VER1_BEM_FORMELN_F\"{U}R_TRIANGLE}}}}{{=}}&[(w\triangleright p)\cdot\mathsf{s}_{p,w\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}].\end{array}

∎

Theorem 2.17

(Universal property of
\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})
be an operad and
\mathsf{p}_{0}:X\to\mathcal{P}_{0}
a morphism of biindexed sets; cf. definition
1.2
. Then there exists a unique morphism of operads
\chi:\mathrm{FREE}_{0}(X)\to(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathfrak{p}_{0})
such that
\mathsf{fr}_{0,X}\cdot\chi=\mathsf{p}_{0}
. In particular the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Map}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{p}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{fr}_{0,X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{f}_{0,X}}

Proof.

Existence: First we construct a functor
\hat{\chi}:\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)\to\mathcal{P}_{0}
that acts identically on the objects.

Let
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word of length
k
and
w=f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot w_{1}\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot w_{k}\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}
the standard decomposition. Note that there exist unique
l_{i},r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
x_{i}\in X
for
i\in[1,k]
such that
w_{i}=\big(l_{i},x_{i},r_{i}\big)
. We define

\begin{array}[]{r c l}w\hat{\chi}&=&(f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot\big(l_{1},x_{1},r_{1}\big)\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot\big(l_{k},x_{k},r_{k}\big)\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}})\hat{\chi}\\
&:=&f_{0}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot\Big(\operatorname{id}_{l_{1}}\boxtimes\,x_{1}\mathsf{p}_{0}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{r_{1}}\Big)\cdot f_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot{\dots}\cdot\Big(\operatorname{id}_{l_{k}}\boxtimes\,x_{k}\mathsf{p}_{0}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{r_{k}}\Big)\cdot f_{k}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}.\end{array}

So in particular we have
f^{\mathrm{op}}\hat{\chi}=f^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}
for
f\in\mathrm{Map}_{0}
.

Note that
\hat{\chi}
respects source and target since
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
acts identically on the objects. Note that
\hat{\chi}
respects identities and composition since
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
is a functor; cf. the composition in
\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
given in definition
2.1
.

Claim 1: We have
(q\triangleleft w\triangleright p)\hat{\chi}=\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,w\hat{\chi}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}
for
q,p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a word
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
.

This follows from the definition of
q\triangleleft w\triangleright p
, the definition of
\hat{\chi}
and the fact that
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is a preoperad and that
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
is a morphism of preoperads. This proves
Claim 1
.

Claim 2: We have
w^{\boxtimes a}\hat{\chi}=(w\hat{\chi})^{\boxtimes a}
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a word
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
w\in\mathrm{WORDS}_{0}(X)
be a word. If
a=0
, this follows from definition
2.3
and definition
1.8
. Otherwise using induction over
a
, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&w^{\boxtimes a}\hat{\chi}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\ref{VER1_DEFI_POTENZ_IN_WORDS}}}}{{=}}&\big((w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\triangleright w\mathrm{s})\cdot(w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w)\big)\hat{\chi}\\
=&(w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\triangleright w\mathrm{s})\hat{\chi}\cdot(w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w)\hat{\chi}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&(w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\hat{\chi}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{w\mathrm{s}})\cdot(\operatorname{id}_{w^{\boxtimes(a-1)}\mathrm{t}}\boxtimes\,w\hat{\chi})\\

.

Claim 3: We have
w\hat{\chi}=w^{\prime}\hat{\chi}
for
w\sim w^{\prime}
.

Let

.

(of length
\leq 1

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}w\hat{\chi}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554pt\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,\big(\mathsf{s}_{v\mathrm{s},a}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot(v\hat{\chi}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{a})\big)\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Braiding}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554pt\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,\big((\operatorname{id}_{a}\boxtimes\,v\hat{\chi})\cdot\mathsf{s}_{v\mathrm{t},a}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\big)\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554ptw^{\prime}\hat{\chi}.\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{r c c c c c l}w\hat{\chi}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554pt\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,\big(\mathsf{s}_{a,v\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot(\operatorname{id}_{a}\boxtimes\,v\hat{\chi})\big)\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Braiding}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554pt\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,\big((v\hat{\chi}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{a})\cdot\mathsf{s}_{a,v\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\big)\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}&\hskip-4.30554pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&\hskip-4.30554ptw^{\prime}\hat{\chi}.\end{array}

If
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 4.}}{{\sim}}w^{\prime}
, we may choose
a,q,p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a word

(of length
\leq 1
) such that

and

. Using the functoriality of
\hat{\chi}
we have

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&w\hat{\chi}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,(\mathsf{h}_{a,v\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot v^{\boxtimes a}\hat{\chi})\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 2}}}{{=}}&\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,\big(\mathsf{h}_{a,v\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cdot(v\hat{\chi})^{\boxtimes a}\big)\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Branching}}}{{=}}&\operatorname{id}_{q}\boxtimes\,(v\hat{\chi}\cdot\mathsf{h}_{a,v\mathrm{t}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathfrak{p}_{0})\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{p}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim 1}}}{{=}}&w^{\prime}\hat{\chi}.\end{array}

This proves

.

Claim 3 yields the functor

\begin{array}[]{r c c c l}\chi&:&\mathrm{FREE}_{0}^{\mathrm{pre}}(X)&\to&\mathcal{P}_{0}\\
&&[w]&\mapsto&w\hat{\chi}\end{array}

by the universal property of the factor category. Note that

acts identically on the objects.

Claim 4: The functor
\chi
is a morphism of preoperads.

Let

be words. Then we have

\begin{array}[]{c l c l}&([w]\boxtimes[w^{\prime}])\chi&=&[(w\triangleright w^{\prime}\mathrm{s})\cdot(w\mathrm{t}\triangleleft w^{\prime})]\chi\\

This proves
Claim 4
.

Claim 5: We have
\mathfrak{f}_{0,X}\cdot\chi=\mathfrak{p}_{0}

Let

Claim 5

\mathsf{fr}_{0,X}\cdot\chi=\mathsf{p}_{0}

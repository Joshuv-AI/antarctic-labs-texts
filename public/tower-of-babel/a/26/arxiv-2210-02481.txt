Conjugacy classes in PSL 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
Christopher-Lloyd Simon

Abstract
. We first describe, over a field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} of characteristic different from 2 2 , the orbits for the adjoint actions of the Lie groups PGL 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K}) and PSL 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K}) on their Lie algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K}) . While the former are well known, the latter lead to the resolution of generalised Pell-Fermat equations which characterise the corresponding orbit. The synthetic approach enables to change the base field, and we illustrate this picture over the fields with three and five elements, in relation with the geometry of the tetrahedral and icosahedral groups. While the results may appear familiar, they do not seem to be covered in such generality or detail by the existing literature. We apply this discussion to partition the set of PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) -classes of integral binary quadratic forms into groups of PSL 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K}) -classes. When 𝕂 = ℂ \mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C} we obtain the class groups of a given discriminant. Then we provide a complete description of their partition into PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℚ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}) -classes in terms of Hilbert symbols, and relate this to the partition into genera. The results are classical, but our geometrical approach is of independent interest as it may yield new insights into the geometry of Gauss composition, and unify the picture over function fields. Finally we provide a geometric interpretation in the modular orbifold PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \ ℍ \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H} for when two points or two closed geodesics correspond to PSL 2 ⁡ ( 𝕂 ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K}) -equivalent quadratic forms, in terms of hyperbolic distances and angles between those modular cycles. These geometric quantities are related to linking numbers of modular knots. Their distribution properties could be studied using the geometry of the quadratic lattice ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ( ℤ ) , det ) (\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\det) but such investigations are not pursued here.

Introduction

Adjoint action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
on
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})

Let us work over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic different from
2
.
The automorphism group
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
of the projective line and its largest simple subgroup
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
play a fundamental role in various areas of mathematics.
They appear for instance in algebraic geometry when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
and in hyperbolic geometry when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
; in arithmetics when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
and in Galois theory when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}/p
.

The first step to understand (the representation theory of) those linear algebraic groups is to describe their conjugacy classes, and more precisely the adjoint actions on their Lie algebra
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
These actions preserve the Killing form, which is a multiple of the non-degenerate quadratic form
\det\colon\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})\to\mathbb{K}
.
It is well known that
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
acts transitively on every level set of
\det
. After introducing the cross-ratio
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\in\mathbb{K}
of two elements
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
with non-zero determinant, we will precise this statement.

Proposition 0.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
have determinant
-\delta\neq 0
and
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\notin\{1,\infty\}
.

The matrices
M\in\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
conjugating
\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{b}
have a well defined determinant in the quotient
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}]^{\times})
, and its is equal to the class of
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
.

In contrast, we will index the
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-orbits inside
\{\det=-\delta\}
by the classes
[\chi]
in the quotient group
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}])
, and parametrize each orbit
(\delta,\chi)
by the solutions in
\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K}
to the generalised Pell-Fermat equation
x^{2}-\delta y^{2}=\chi
.
In homological terms, the conjugacy problem in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
has obstructions measured by the group
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}]^{\times})
, and when they vanish the conjugacies form a torsor under the group of units
\{\gamma\in\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}]\mid\Norm(\gamma)=1\}
.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
have determinant
-\delta\neq 0
and cross-ratio
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=4\chi\notin\{1,\infty\}
.
The elements
C\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
such that
C\mathfrak{a}C^{-1}=\mathfrak{b}
are parametrized by the Pell-Fermat conic:

(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K}\>\colon\>\quad x^{2}-\delta y^{2}=\chi

C(x,y)=x(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})+y(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})

In particular,
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are conjugate by an element
C(x,y)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
if and only if
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
belongs to the subgroup of norms
\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}]\subset\mathbb{K}^{\times}
of the quadratic extension, and by an element
C(x,0)\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{K})\cap\mathbb{K}[\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}]
if and only if
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
belongs to the subgroup of squares
(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}\subset\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.

The proofs of these statements can be reduced to elementary linear algebra once we thoroughly understand the geometry of the Lie algebra
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
inside the quaternion algebra
\Gl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
In short, commutativity rhymes with colinearity whereas anti-commutativity rhymes with orthogonality.

We recall this background material in the first two sections
, and provide an amusing application in the third to prove an analogue of Ptolemy’s identity for quadrilaterals inscribed in the isotropic cone
\mathbb{X}
of
(\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K}),\det)
, relying on a natural quadratic desingularization
\psi\colon\mathbb{K}^{2}\to\mathbb{X}
.

Classes of binary quadratic forms

By polarizing a binary quadratic form on
\mathbb{K}^{2}
with respect to the canonical symplectic form
\det
, we obtain an isomorphism:

Q=lx^{2}+mxy+ry^{2}\in\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})\qquad\xleftrightarrow{Q(v)=\det(v,\mathfrak{q}v)}\qquad\mathfrak{q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-m&-2r\\
2l&m\end{pmatrix}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})

between the Poisson algebra
(\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2}),\disc)
and the Lie algebra
(\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K}),-4\det)
, conjugating the actions of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
by change of variables and by conjugacy.
After interpreting the values of quadratic forms as the scalar products with elements in the isotropic cone
Q(v)=\langle\mathfrak{q},\psi(v)\rangle
, and computing that the products
Q_{a}(u)Q_{b}(v)=\langle\mathfrak{a},\psi(u)\rangle\langle\mathfrak{b},\psi(v)\rangle
for primitive vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{K}^{2}
are equivalent to the cross-ratio
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
in
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\Norm(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times})
, we will deduce the following.

Proposition 0.3
.

The set
\Cl_{\mathbb{K}}(\Delta)
of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-orbits in
\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})
with non-square discriminant
\Delta
embeds into the group
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}})
of exponent two, by sending the class of the norm
x^{2}-\tfrac{\Delta}{4}y^{2}
of the
\mathbb{K}
-extension
\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]
to the identity, and using the multiplication of values for composition.

The initial motivation was to understand the space
\Qfb(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
of integral binary quadratic forms
Q(x,y)=lx^{2}+mxy+ry^{2}
up to change of variables by
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and the class groups
\Cl(\Delta)
of primitive classes with non-square discriminant
\Delta=m^{2}-4lr\in\mathbb{Z}
introduced by Gauss in
[
Gau07
]
. We refer to
[
Cas78
,
Cox97
]
and
[
Wei84
]
for the relevant background and history.

For a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
\neq 2
, the extension of scalars
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{K}
induces a map
\Qfb(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\to\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})
, yielding a group morphism
\Cl(\Delta)\to\Cl_{\mathbb{K}}(\Delta)
.
We say that
Q_{a},Q_{b}\in\Qfb(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
are
\mathbb{K}
-equivalent when they are conjugate by
C\in\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
When
\mathbb{K}\supset\mathbb{Q}
this implies that they have the same discriminant (not just modulo

), and Theorem
0.2
& Proposition

show that their

-equivalence is measured by
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\equiv Q_{a}(1,0)Q_{b}(1,0)=l_{a}l_{b}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}/\Norm(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times})
.

Thus, when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
this groups the
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-classes of
\Qfb(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
according to their discriminant
\Delta
, and as
\mathbb{K}
decreases we obtain finer partitions of the class groups
\Cl(\Delta)
into
\mathbb{K}
-classes.
In section
5
we provide a computable characterisation of
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence in terms of the Hilbert symbols
(\delta,\chi)_{p}
at all primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, which measures the obstruction to solving the equation
x^{2}-\delta y^{2}=\chi
in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

We also deduce from Theorem
0.2
a relation between the partition of
\Cl(\Delta)
into
\mathbb{Q}
-classes and its partition into genera, which are given by the cosets
\Cl(\Delta)/\Cl(\Delta)^{2}
modulo the subgroup of squares.

Theorem 0.4
.

For all non-square discriminant
\Delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, genus equivalence implies
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence.

For all fundamental discriminants
\Delta
, genus equivalence is also implied by
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence.

Arithmetic equivalence of singular moduli & modular geodesics

We conclude with a geometric interpretation of
\mathbb{K}
-equivalence, which one may compare with
[
Pen96
]
.
The modular group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}
by linear fractional transformations, and the quotient is the modular orbifold
\mathbb{M}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.

Consider primitive integral binary quadratic forms
Q_{a},Q_{b}
with non-square discriminant
\Delta
,
and denote
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)
,
(\beta^{\prime},\beta)
their roots (which one may order up to simultaneous inversion).

If
\Delta>0
, then
Q_{a}
and
Q_{b}
are uniquely determined by
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
, and their
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-classes correspond to points
[\alpha],[\beta]\in\mathbb{M}
, called singular moduli.

Corollary 0.5
.

Two complex irrationals
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})
are
\mathbb{K}
-equivalent if and only if there exists a hyperbolic geodesic arc in
\mathbb{M}
from
[\alpha]
to
[\beta]
whose length
\lambda
is of the form
\left(\cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{(2x)^{2}-\Delta y^{2}}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
, in which case all geodesic arcs from
[\alpha]
to
[\beta]
have this property.

If
\Delta<0
then
Q_{a}
and
Q_{b}
correspond to the oriented geodesics
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha),(\beta^{\prime},\beta)
in
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
and their
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-classes correspond to primitive closed oriented geodesics in
\mathbb{M}
, called modular geodesics.

Corollary 0.6
.

Two modular geodesics of the same length
2\sinh^{-1}(\sqrt{\Delta}/2)
are
\mathbb{K}
-equivalent if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions hold:

\theta

There exists one intersection point with angle
\theta\in\,]0,\pi[
such that

\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{(2x)^{2}-\Delta y^{2}}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
,
in which case all intersections have this property.

\lambda

There exists one co-oriented ortho-geodesic of length
\lambda
such that
\left(\cosh\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}=\tfrac{1}{(2x)^{2}-\Delta y^{2}}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
,
in which case all such ortho-geodesics have this property.

In conclusion, the
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence is measured by the geometric quantities
(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2})^{2}
or
\left(\cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}
as elements in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\bmod\Norm_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})
, and their multiplication implies a geometric interpretation for the multiplication of genera.

1.
Geometric algebra of
\Gl_{2}(\mathbb{K})

Consider a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic different from
2
and a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
\mathbb{V}
of dimension
2
.

Involutive algebra

The
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
of linear endomorphisms of
\mathbb{V}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}^{*}
with product defined by
(u\otimes\mu)\cdot(v\otimes\nu)=u\otimes\mu(v)\nu
.
It is endowed with the canonical linear form
\Tr\colon\Gl(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{K}
defined by
\Tr(u\otimes\mu)=\mu(u)
.
Let us find a canonical
involution

M\mapsto M^{\#}
on
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
, that is an anti-commutative linear endomorphism of order two, to deduce a canonical non-degenerate bilinear form
(M,N)\mapsto\Tr(MN^{\#})
isomorphic to the pairing
\Gl(\mathbb{V})\times\Gl(\mathbb{V})^{*}\to\mathbb{K}
.

A non-degenerate bilinear form
\omega\colon\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{K}
is equivalent to an isomorphism
\omega^{*}\colon\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{V}^{*}
. Its associated adjoint involution
\#\colon\Gl(\mathbb{V})\to\Gl(\mathbb{V})
is the composition of
(\omega^{*})\otimes(\omega^{*})^{-1}\colon\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}^{*}\to\mathbb{V}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{V}
with the canonical map switching factors, thus
\#\colon u\otimes\mu\mapsto(\omega^{*})^{-1}(\mu)\otimes\omega^{*}(u)
.
Observe that
\#
only depends on
\omega
up to scaling.
The plane
\mathbb{V}
admits a unique non-degenerate anti-symmetric bilinear form
\omega
up to scaling as
\Lambda^{2}\mathbb{V}^{*}\simeq\mathbb{K}
, and this defines our canonical involution
\#
.

Only after choosing a basis of
\mathbb{V}
do we have the identifications
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{K}^{2}
and
\Gl(\mathbb{V})=\Gl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
Then
\omega(u,v)=\det(u,v)
and the associated adjoint involution on
\Gl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
is the transpose-comatrix:

M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto M^{\#}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{pmatrix}.

The fixed subalgebra of
M\mapsto M^{\#}
is reduced to the center
\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}
of
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
.
Composing
(M,M^{\#})
with addition or multiplication yields the central elements:

\Tr(M)\mathfrak{1}:=M+M^{\#}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\det(M)\mathfrak{1}:=M\times M^{\#}

which recovers the linear
trace
map
\Tr\colon\Gl(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{K}
, and defines the multiplicative
determinant
map
\det\colon\Gl(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{K}
.
The involution
\#
preserves the group
\GL(\mathbb{V})
of invertible elements, which consists in those
A\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
such that
\det(A)\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}
, in which case
A^{-1}=\det(A)^{-1}A^{\#}
.

For
A\in\GL(\mathbb{V})
and
M\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
we have
(AMA^{-1})^{\#}=AM^{\#}A^{-1}
, so the left adjoint linear action of
\GL(\mathbb{V})
on
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
preserves the involution, whence all the structures which will follow.

The kernel
\SL(\mathbb{V})
of the determinant morphism
\det\colon\GL(\mathbb{V})\to\mathbb{K}^{\times}
is called the subgroup of units, thus
A\in\SL(\mathbb{V})\iff\det(A)=1\iff A^{\#}=A^{-1}
.
The kernel
\Sl(\mathbb{V})
of the trace form is the anti-symmetric part for the involution, thus
a\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})\iff\Tr(a)=0\iff a^{\#}=-a
.

Quadratic space

On the vector space
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
the determinant is a non degenerate quadratic form, and as
\det(M+N)\mathfrak{1}=(M+N)(M+N)^{\#}=\left(\det(M)+\Tr(MN^{\#})+\det(N)\right)\mathfrak{1}
, its polar symmetric bilinear form is:

\langle M,N\rangle=\tr(MN^{\#})\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\tr(P):=\tfrac{1}{2}\Tr(P)

The involution
\#
has eigenvalues
\pm 1
and its eigenspaces provide a decomposition

\Gl(\mathbb{V})=\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}\oplus\Sl(\mathbb{V})

which is orthogonal with respect to the determinant form.
Thus every element
M\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
splits as the sum of its symmetric and anti-symmetric parts with respect to the involution:

M=\tr(M)\mathfrak{1}+\pr(M)\qquad\mathrm{where}\quad\tr(M)\mathfrak{1}=\tfrac{M+M^{\#}}{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\pr(M):=\tfrac{M-M^{\#}}{2}.

In particular
\det(M)\mathfrak{1}=\tr(M)^{2}\mathfrak{1}-\pr(M)^{2}
which we may write
\det=\tr^{2}-\pr^{2}
.

The
4
-dimensional space
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
, which contains the isotropic cone
\Gl(\mathbb{V})\setminus\GL(\mathbb{V})
defined by
\det(M)=\langle M,M\rangle=0
, decomposes as the direct sum of the anistropic line
\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}
and its orthogonal hyperplane
\Sl(\mathbb{V})
defined by
\tr(M)=\langle\mathfrak{1},M\rangle=0
.
Denote by
\mathbb{X}
the isotropic cone for the determinant restricted to the kernel
\Sl(\mathbb{V})
of the trace, in formulae:

\mathbb{X}=\{M\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})\mid\langle\mathfrak{1},M\rangle=0=\langle M,M\rangle\}=\{\mathfrak{a}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})\mid\det(\mathfrak{a})=0\}

Discriminant

The relation
M^{2}-(M+M^{\#})M+(MM^{\#})=0
yields the Cayley-Hamilton identity
\chi_{M}(M)=0
for
X^{2}-\Tr(M)X+\det(M)\in\mathbb{K}[X]
the
characteristic polynomial
of
M
.
Hence a non-central element
M\in\Gl_{2}(\mathbb{K})\setminus\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}
generates a commutative subalgebra
\mathbb{K}[M]=\Span(\mathfrak{1},M)
of dimension
2
, which is isomorphic to the quadratic extension
\mathbb{K}[X]/(\chi_{M})
of
\mathbb{K}
with Galois involution given by the restriction of the involution
\#
.

The discriminant of
M\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
is defined as that of its characteristic polynomial, equal to

\disc(M)=\Tr(M)^{2}-4\det(M).

In particular
\disc(A)=\Tr(A)^{2}-4
for
A\in\SL(\mathbb{V})
and
\disc(\mathfrak{a})=-4\det(\mathfrak{a})
for
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
.

We call
M\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})

semi-simple
when
\disc(M)\neq 0
, that is when
\chi_{M}
has simple roots in its splitting field.
If these roots belong to
\mathbb{K}
then
\mathbb{K}[M]
is isomorphic to the direct product
\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K}
, otherwise
\mathbb{K}[M]
is a simple
\mathbb{K}
-algebra (no proper ideals).
In both cases
\mathbb{K}[M]
is a semi-simple
\mathbb{K}
-algebra (a product of simple algebras).
When
\disc(M)=0
we have
\chi_{M}(X)=(X-\lambda)^{2}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
so the algebra
\mathbb{K}[M]
is not integral (it has zero divisors) as

is nilpotent.

The discriminant is preserved under the projection
\disc(M)=\disc(\pr M)

\Sl(\mathbb{V})
lies outside the cone
\mathbb{X}

The isomorphism
\PGL(\mathbb{V})\to\SO(\Sl(\mathbb{V}),\det)

The left adjoint linear action of
\GL(\mathbb{V})
on
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
preserves the involution whence every structure which derives from it, such as the determinant form and the orthogonal decomposition
\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}\oplus\Sl(\mathbb{V})
. It also preserves the orientations of
\Sl(\mathbb{V})
defined for a basis as the class of its determinant in
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}
.
Only the scalar matrices act trivially, and the maximal subspace on which the action is trivial equals
\mathbb{K}\mathfrak{1}
. Therefore no information is lost after quotienting by these centers, and this yields a faithful representation
\PGL(\mathbb{V})\to\SO(\Sl(\mathbb{V}),\det)
into the group of orientation preserving isometries of
(\Sl(\mathbb{V}),\det)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The adjoint action yields an isomorphism
\PGL(\mathbb{V})\simeq\SO(\Sl(\mathbb{V}),\det)
.

Proof.

To prove surjectivity, we use a theorem of Cartan-Dieudonné
[
Die71
]
stating that every isometry of a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form over a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space is a product of at most
d
reflections.
In particular, an element of
\SO(\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K}),\det)
is a product of at most
3
reflections, but since it has determinant
1
it is in fact a product of exactly two reflections. Thus we must express all products of two reflections as the conjugacy by some element.

If
q\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
is not isotropic, that is
\det(q)\neq 0
, then the orthogonal reflection
\sigma_{q}\in\End(\Gl(\mathbb{V}))
of vector
q
across
q^{\perp}
is given by:

\sigma_{q}(m)=m-2\tfrac{\langle q,m\rangle}{\langle q,q\rangle}\cdot q

Notice that the orthogonal reflection of vector
\mathfrak{1}
across
\Sl(\mathbb{V})
equals
\sigma_{\mathfrak{1}}\colon m\mapsto-m^{\#}
.
The endomorphism
\mu_{q}\in\End(\Gl(\mathbb{V}))
corresponding to left multiplication by
q
, left conjugates
\sigma_{\mathfrak{1}}
to
\sigma_{q}
.
In formulae, we have
\mu_{q}\colon m\mapsto qm
and
\sigma_{q}=\mu_{q}\circ\sigma_{\mathfrak{1}}\circ\mu_{q^{-1}}
.
Thus

\sigma_{q}(m)=-q(q^{-1}m)^{\#}=-\tfrac{qm^{\#}q}{\det(q)}.

Now restricting the attention to
\End(\Sl(\mathbb{V}))
, we notice that for
q,m\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
this formula becomes
\sigma_{q}(m)=-qmq^{-1}
.
Hence for
p,q\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})\setminus\mathbb{X}
the reflection
\sigma_{p}\circ\sigma_{q}\in\SO(\Sl(\mathbb{V}),\det)
coincides with the left adjoint action of

.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
have determinant
-\delta\neq 0
and cross-ratio
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=4\chi\notin\{1,\infty\}
.
The elements
C\in\SL(\mathbb{V})
such that
C\mathfrak{a}C^{-1}=\mathfrak{b}
are parametrized by the Pell-Fermat conic:

(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}\times\mathbb{K}\>\colon\>\quad x^{2}-\delta y^{2}=\chi

C(x,y)=x(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})+y(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})

In particular,
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are conjugate by an element
C(x,y)\in\SL(\mathbb{V})
if and only if
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
belongs to the subgroup of norms
\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\delta}]\subset\mathbb{K}^{\times}
of the quadratic extension, and by an element
C(x,0)\in\SL(\mathbb{V})\cap\mathbb{K}[\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}]
if and only if
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
belongs to the subgroup of squares
(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}\subset\mathbb{K}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{H}
.
Since
\det\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}\neq 0
the elements
\mathfrak{1},\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b},\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}
form a basis of
\Gl(\mathbb{V})
.
Let
C\in\Gl(\mathbb{V})
be decomposed as
C=t\mathfrak{1}+x\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}+y\mathfrak{a}+z\mathfrak{b}
for
t,x,y,z\in\mathbb{K}
.
The condition
C\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}C
can be rewritten using
\mathfrak{a}^{2}=-\mathfrak{1}=\mathfrak{b}^{2}
as well as
\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}-\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}=\mathfrak{a}-\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle\mathfrak{b}
.
After grouping terms we find
C\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}C\iff\left(t-x(1+\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle)\right)\cdot(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{b})+(z-y)\cdot(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a})=0
.
But
\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{b}\in\Span(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}=(1-\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle)\mathfrak{1}-\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}\in\mathbb{K}[\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}]\setminus\{0\}
so by orthogonality of the planes
\mathbb{K}[\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}]
and
\Span(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
we have
C\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}C\iff t=x(1+\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle)\>\&\>y=z
.

Now for
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
the determinant of
C=x(\mathfrak{1}-\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a})+y(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})
can be computed using the orthogonality of
\Span(\mathfrak{1},\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})
and
\Span(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
and the hypothesis
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{H}
:

\det(C)=(x^{2}+y^{2})\cdot(2+2\langle\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\rangle)=\tfrac{4(x^{2}+y^{2})}{\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})}

so
C\in\SL(\mathbb{V})\iff\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=(2x)^{2}+(2y)^{2}
.
This proves the Lemma for
\delta=-1
.

Finally, let us reduce the general case
\delta\neq 0
to the previous one.
The points
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}/\sqrt{d}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{b}/\sqrt{d}
satisfy
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=\bir(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})
and the endomorphisms
C\in\SL(\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{K}^{\prime})
with
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{d}]
conjugating
\mathfrak{a}
to
\mathfrak{b}
are the same as those conjugating
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
.
We just showed that those elements
C
correspond to the pairs
(x,y)\in\mathbb{K}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
such that
\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})=4(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})
by the formula
C=x^{\prime}(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime-1})+y^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}+\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})
.
Setting
x=x^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
which satisfy
4(x^{2}+dy^{2})=\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
, we may rewrite
C=x(\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1})+y(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})
. But recall that
a
and
b
have coefficients
\mathbb{K}
and that
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}\neq 0
is orthogonal to
\mathfrak{1}+\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}^{-1}\neq 0
. Hence
C
is has coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
if and only if
x,y\in\mathbb{K}
.

5.
Applications to binary quadratic forms

Let
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
be the space of quadratic forms
Q\colon\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{K}
. After choosing a basis of
\mathbb{V}
, those amount to homogeneous polynomials in two ordered variables with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
.

Isomorphism
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})\simeq\Sl(\mathbb{V})

One may polarise
Q\in\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
with respect to any non degenerate bilinear form on the plane
\mathbb{V}
, and one usually learns this for some euclidean scalar product, but we may also use a symplectic form: there exists a unique
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
such that
Q(v)=\omega(v,\mathfrak{q}v)
.
If we fix a basis
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{K}^{2}
and
\omega=\det
we have the formula:

Q=lx^{2}+mxy+ry^{2}\in\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})\qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathfrak{q}=\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-m&-2r\\
2l&m\end{pmatrix}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})

This defines a bijective correspondence between
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
and
\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})
through which the adjoint action of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
corresponds to the action by change of variables.
It matches the discriminants
m^{2}-4lr
and sends the Lie bracket
\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a})
of
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
to the Poisson bracket of functions on
\mathbb{K}^{2}
, under which quadratic forms are closed
\{Q_{a},Q_{b}\}=\tfrac{1}{4}\left[(\partial_{x}Q_{a})(\partial_{y}Q_{b})-(\partial_{x}Q_{b})(\partial_{y}Q_{a})\right]=\{\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\}
.

Consequently, all the notions defined for an element
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
or a pair of elements
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
, can be translated in terms of the corresponding binary quadratic forms
Q,Q_{a},Q_{b}\in\Qfb(\mathbb{K}^{2})
.
For instance, after choosing a root
\sqrt{\disc(Q_{a})\disc(Q_{b})}=-4\sqrt{\det(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b})}
, we may define the cosine:

\cos(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\tfrac{\disc(Q_{a}+Q_{b})-(\disc(Q_{a})+\disc(Q_{b}))}{2\sqrt{\disc(Q_{a})\disc(Q_{b})}}=\cos(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})

and the cross-ratio of their roots
\{\alpha^{\prime},\alpha\}\&\{\beta^{\prime},\beta\}
, which are ordered up to simultaneous inversion:

\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\bir(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha;\beta^{\prime},\beta)=\tfrac{(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)(\beta^{\prime}-\beta)}{(\alpha-\beta^{\prime})(\beta-\alpha^{\prime})}=\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}).

For a common choice of root these are related by

\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})^{-1}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\cos(Q_{a},Q_{b}))
.
In particular, if
Q_{a},Q_{b}
have the same discriminant
\Delta
, which is to be chosen as the root
\sqrt{\disc(Q_{a})\disc(Q_{b})}
, then
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})^{-1}=\disc(Q_{a}+Q_{b})/(4\Delta)
, and we may compute
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\equiv l_{a}l_{b}\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]}
.

The actions by change of variable of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
and
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
on
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
preserve the discriminant as an element of
\mathbb{K}/(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{2}
and
\mathbb{K}
respectively, as well as the cross ratio.
Note that the condition
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\notin\{1,\infty\}\iff Q_{a}\neq\pm Q_{b}
can always be achieved after conjugating
Q_{b}
by
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
Consequently, Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
4.6
describe the orbits of
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
under the action of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
and
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
in terms of
\disc
and
\bir
, the latter being empty or
\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}
-torsors.

The variables live in the cone.

Conversely one may try to recover some notions defined for binary quadratic form in terms of the corresponding matrices.
This was the motivation leading to Lemma
3.1
, namely to recover the values that a form
Q\in\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
takes on
\mathbb{V}
in terms of the geometry of
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})
with respect to
\mathbb{X}
.
Indeed, Lemma
3.1
implies that if
\psi\colon v\in\mathbb{V}\mapsto p\in\mathbb{X}
then:

Q(v)=\det(v,\mathfrak{q}v)=\langle\mathfrak{q},p\rangle.

So the elements
p\in\mathbb{X}
in the cone play the role of the vector of variables
v\in\mathbb{V}
, whereas the other elements
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sl(\mathbb{V})\setminus\mathbb{X}
are the non-degenerate binary quadratic forms
Q\in\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
.

The values of
Q
on
\mathbb{V}
may thus be interpreted in terms of the distances between
\mathfrak{q}^{\perp}
and
\mathbb{X}
.
In particular, if
\disc Q_{a}=\Delta=\disc Q_{b}
then for any
v_{a},v_{b}\in\mathbb{V}
which belong to
\mathbb{K}
-bases, we have:

\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\equiv Q_{a}(v_{a})Q_{b}(v_{b})\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}}\>\mathrm{whence}\>\bir(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\equiv\langle\mathfrak{a},p_{a}\rangle\langle\mathfrak{b},p_{b}\rangle\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}}.

Proposition 5.1
.

The set
\Cl_{\mathbb{K}}(\Delta)
of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
-orbits in
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
with non-square discriminant
\Delta
embeds into the group
\mathbb{K}^{\times}/{\Norm_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}})
of exponent two, by sending the class of the norm
x^{2}-\tfrac{\Delta}{4}y^{2}
of the
\mathbb{K}
-extension
\mathbb{K}[\sqrt{\Delta}]
to the identity, and using the multiplication of values for composition.

Describing
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence with Hilbert symbols

In this paragraph we fix
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{Q}^{2}
and a non-square discriminant
\Delta
. Consider two forms
Q_{a},Q_{b}
representing variable
\mathbb{Q}
-classes in
\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)
, and let us provide a method for computing
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}}
.

\mathcal{P}=\{-1,2\}\cup\{3,5,7\dots\}
denotes the set of rational primes and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the
p
-adic completion of
\mathbb{Q}
. The prime
-1
refers (following Conway
[
CF97
]
) to the place at which the completion of
\mathbb{Q}
is the Archimedian field
\mathbb{Q}_{-1}=\mathbb{R}
.
For
\delta,\chi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
the
Hilbert symbol

\left(\delta,\chi\right)_{p}
equals
1
or
-1
according to whether the homogenised Pell-Fermat equation
x^{2}-\delta y^{2}=\chi z^{2}
admits a solution in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\mathbb{P}^{2}
or not.
Thus we have
(\delta,\chi)_{p}=1
if and only if
\chi
is the norm of an element in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{\delta})
.

We define the set of prime obstructions to solving the equation
(2x)^{2}-\Delta y^{2}=\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})
by
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid(\Delta,\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b}))_{p}=-1\}
, which only depends on the
\mathbb{Q}
-classes of
Q_{a}\&Q_{b}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The forms
Q_{a}
and
Q_{b}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalent if and only if
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Apply the Hasse-Minkowski theorem
[
Ser70
, Chapitre IV, Théorème 8]
to the ternary quadratic form
(2x)^{2}-\Delta y^{2}-\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})z^{2}
: it represents
0
over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if it represents
0
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

The following Lemma and Remark enable us to turn the previous Theorem into a finite method for computing
\mathbb{Q}
-classes.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
p\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{2\}
divides
\delta
and
\chi
to even powers, then
(\delta,\chi)_{p}=1
.

In other terms
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})\setminus\{2\}
is contained in the set of primes appearing with odd valuations in the factorisation of
\Delta
or
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})
. In particular it is finite.

Proof.

A pedestrian method is to reduce the equation
\bmod{p}
, argue that there exists a solution by a counting procedure, and lift it to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
using Hensel’s lemma.

Alternatively, one may use the explicit formulae
[
Ser70
, Theorem III.1]
for the Hilbert symbol at
p\neq 2
in terms of the Legendre symbols of
\delta,\chi\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
at
-1
and
p
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Hilbert proved a global relation among the local symbols:
\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\delta,\chi)_{p}=1
, which is a reformulation of the quadratic reciprocity law.
Consequently
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})\setminus\{2\}
determines
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})
.

Our final Proposition implies that
Q_{a}\&Q_{b}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalent if and only if
\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q_{a})=\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q_{b})
.
This simplifies the determination of all sets
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})
to those involving a fixed element
Q_{0}
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For
Q_{a},Q_{b},Q_{c}\in\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)
the set
\mathcal{P}(Q_{a},Q_{b})
is equal to the symmetric difference of
\mathcal{P}(Q_{c},Q_{a})
and
\mathcal{P}(Q_{b},Q_{c})
.
In other terms for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
we have:

(\Delta,\bir(Q_{c},Q_{a})\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\bir(Q_{b},Q_{c}))_{p}=1.

Proof.

According to
[
Ser70
, Theorem III.2]
, the Hilbert symbol of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
defines a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space
(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})/(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
.
The Lemma can thus be reformulated as
(\Delta,\chi_{a,b,c})_{p}=1
where
\chi_{a,b,c}=\bir(Q_{c},Q_{a})\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\bir(Q_{b},Q_{c})
.

We must therefore compare
\chi_{a,b,c}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with the subgroup generated by the norms of elements in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})^{\times}
.
Using the explicit formula for the cross-ratio we find that:

\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})=\tfrac{-\Delta/(l_{a}l_{b})}{\Norm_{\Delta}(\alpha^{\prime}-\beta)}\quad\mathrm{hence}\quad\chi_{a,b,c}=\tfrac{-\Delta^{3}/(l_{a}l_{b}l_{c})^{2}}{\Norm_{\Delta}((\gamma^{\prime}-\alpha)(\alpha^{\prime}-\beta)(\beta^{\prime}-\gamma))}

Consequently
(\Delta,\chi_{a,b,c})=(\Delta,-\Delta)_{p}=1
as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Denoting
Q_{0}
a representative for the norm of the
\mathbb{Q}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})
, the map
Q\mapsto\mathcal{P}(Q_{0},Q)
yields an isomorphism
\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\Delta)
.

Integral binary quadratic forms

Notice that under the 1:1 correspondence
\Qfb(\mathbb{Q}^{2})\leftrightarrow\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, the lattice
\Qfb(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
of integral binary quadratic forms gets mapped to the dual lattice
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}
of
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
with respect to the quadratic form
\det
.
We will concentrate on the primitive points of the lattices
\Qfb(\mathbb{V})
or
\Sl(\mathbb{V})^{\vee}
, namely those which are visible from the origin, thus not multiples of another lattice point by a non-invertible integer.

Now for any field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic different from
2
, we may consider the extension of scalars
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/2])\to\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
, and its restriction to
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\in\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\vee}
are
\mathbb{K}
-equivalent when their images in
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
belong to the same orbit under the adjoint action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.
We may thus group the conjugacy classes of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
into
\mathbb{K}
-classes, and observe how this varies with
\mathbb{K}
.

When
\mathbb{K}\supset\mathbb{Q}
has characteristic zero, the extension of scalars
\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\to\Sl_{2}(\mathbb{K})
is injective so the
\mathbb{K}
-equivalence implies the equality of discriminants.
When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, this groups the integral binary quadratic forms according to their discriminant, and we find the finite class groups
\Cl(\Delta)
.
When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
, this defines for each discriminant
\Delta
a partition of the class group
\Cl(\Delta)
into
\mathbb{Q}
-classes.

Class groups and genera

Fix a non-square discriminant
\Delta
and consider the set
\Cl(\Delta)
of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivalence classes of primitive integral binary quadratic forms with that discriminant.
This is a finite set by the classical reduction theory of binary quadratic forms (see
[
Cox97
,
CF97
]
).

In
[
Gau07
]
, C.-F. Gauss endowed
\Cl(\Delta)
with the structure of a finite abelian group
which was later reformulated by Dirichlet as follows
[
Wei84
]
. One may represent two classes in
\Cl(\Delta)
by forms
Q_{a}
and
Q_{b}
whose first coefficients
l_{a}
and
l_{b}
are coprime, and with the same middle coefficient
m
: their composition
Q_{c}
of the same discriminant is determined by its first coefficient
l_{c}=l_{a}l_{b}
and middle coefficient
m
.
(Beware that this corresponds to the
narrow
class group of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
.)
The neutral element of
\Cl(\Delta)
is represented by any form which takes the value
1
, for instance the principal form
x^{2}+\epsilon xy+\tfrac{1}{4}(\epsilon-\Delta)y^{2}
where
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
satisfies
\Delta=\epsilon\bmod{4}
, obtained from the
\mathbb{Q}
-extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})
by restricting the norm to its unique order
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
of discriminant
\Delta
.

Two classes in
\Cl(\Delta)

belong to the same genus
when for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
they are conjugate by
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, with
\mathbb{Z}_{-1}=\mathbb{R}
(see
[
Cas78
, Chapter 14]
).
One may consult
[
Cox97
, Theorem 3.21]
for several other characterisations, such as representing the same values in
(\mathbb{Z}/\Delta)^{\times}
.
The equivalence classes for this relation form a group
\operatorname{Gen}(\Delta)
given by the multiplication of their sets of values in
(\mathbb{Z}/\Delta)^{\times}
.
Gauss identified it with the quotient of his class group by the subgroup of squares. Moreover the kernel of the squaring map consists in the subgroup
\operatorname{Sym}(\Delta)
of classes invariant by the Galois involution.
In other terms we have a short exact sequence of abelian groups:

1\to\operatorname{Sym}(\Delta)\to\Cl(\Delta)\xrightarrow{square}\Cl(\Delta)\to\operatorname{Gen}(\Delta)\to 1.

For two classes in
\Cl(\Delta)
represented by
Q_{a},Q_{b}
we saw that
\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})\equiv l_{a}l_{b}\bmod{\Norm_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{\Delta}]^{\times}}
.
We used this multiplication of values to define the composition law on
\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)
in proposition
5.1
.
Hence the extension of scalars
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}
yields a group morphism
\Cl(\Delta)\to\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)
whose kernel consists in the
\mathbb{Z}
-classes which are
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalent to the principal form.
This kernel contains the subgroup of squares so we find a morphism of groups with exponent two
\Cl(\Delta)/\Cl(\Delta)^{2}\to\Cl_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta)
.

A fundamental discriminant
\Delta
is that of (the ring of integers in) a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, which means that
\Delta=1\bmod{4}
is a square-free integer, or that
\Delta/4\neq 1\bmod{4}
is a square-free integer.
For such
\Delta
, the genus equivalence amounts to being conjugate by an element in
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
(
[
Cox97
, Exercise 3.17]
), which is implied by
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence.
This discussion implies the following.

Proposition 5.7
.

In
\Cl(\Delta)
, the principal principal genus
\Cl(\Delta)^{2}
is contained in the subgroup of classes which are
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalent to the principal form, thus genus equivalence implies
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence.
If
\Delta
is fundamental then
\mathbb{Q}
-equivalence implies genus equivalence (but otherwise it may not).

Arithmetic equivalence of singular moduli and modular geodesics

The modular group
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}
by linear fractional transformations, and the quotient is the modular orbifold
\mathbb{M}=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.

Consider primitive integral binary quadratic forms
Q_{a},Q_{b}
with non-square discriminant
\Delta
. Fix a root
\sqrt{\Delta}
which is to be positive if
\Delta>0
, and define the first roots of
Q_{a}(x,1)
and
Q_{b}(x,1)
by:

\alpha=\tfrac{-m_{a}+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2l_{a}}\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\beta=\tfrac{-m_{b}+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2l_{b}}.

Arithmetic equivalence of singular moduli

If
\Delta>0
, then
Q_{a}
and
Q_{b}
are uniquely determined by their roots
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
.
Their
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

often called singular moduli in the study of elliptic curves.
The geodesic arc from
\alpha
to
\beta

\mathbb{H}\mathbb{P}
has length
\lambda
given in terms of the cross-ratio
\bir(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha;\beta^{\prime},\beta)
by the formula:

\left(\cosh\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1+\cosh(\lambda)}{2}=\frac{1}{\bir(Q_{a},Q_{b})}

(to Theorem

.

Two singular moduli

in which case all geodesic arcs from
[\alpha]

Let us briefly relate the arithmetic-geometric intersections of modular geodesics to the topological linking numbers of modular knots, referring to

The unit tangent bundle of the modular orbifold

(Linking and intersection from boundary evaluations)
.

For primitive hyperbolic

\Link_{q}(A,B)
and
\Cos_{q}(A,B)

\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

recover their linking and intersection numbers:

\displaystyle\Link_{q}(A,B)\xrightarrow[q\to\infty]{}\lk(A,B)

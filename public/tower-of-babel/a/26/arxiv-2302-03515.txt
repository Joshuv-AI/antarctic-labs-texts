Comment on WKB Series Of All Orders
C. V. Sukumar

Abstract
The Dunham expansion for the one-dimensional two-turning-point eigenvalue problem for all orders in the WKB approximation is examined. An explicit form for all the odd order terms in the expansion which are are total derivatives is given.



1
Introduction

The following discussion closely follows the work of Bender
et al

[
1
]
.
We consider the two-turning-point eigenvalue problem for the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation

\Big[-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)-E\Big]\psi(x)=0,\quad\quad\psi(\pm\infty)=0

(1)

To construct the WKB approximation for all orders we introduce a small parameter
\epsilon
and consider the eigenvalue problem

\epsilon^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\psi(x)=Q(x)\psi(x),\quad Q(x)=V(x)-E

(2)

The parameter
\epsilon
helps to organize the WKB series and we can set
\epsilon=1
at the end of the calculation. The WKB approximation to the wave function is

\psi(x)=\exp\Big[\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\epsilon^{n}S_{n}(x)\Big]

(3)

Substitution of this expression for
\psi
in to the differential equation and comparison of like powers of
\epsilon
gives

\displaystyle[S_{0}^{\prime}(x)]^{2}=Q(x)\ \longrightarrow\ S_{0}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{Q(x)}

(4)

\displaystyle 2S_{0}^{\prime}S_{n}^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}S_{j}^{\prime}S_{n-j}^{\prime}+S_{n-1}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=0\ ,\quad n\geq 1

(5)

where the prime symbol refers to derivatives with respect to x. The recursion relation in (
5
) may be used to find
S_{n}^{\prime}
from
S_{j}^{\prime}
for
j<n
. The exact quantization formula for the eigenvalues valid for all orders of the WKB approximation, obtained by setting
\epsilon=1
, has been shown to be

\frac{1}{2i}\oint_{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}^{\prime}(z)dz=K\pi,\quad K=0,1,2,...

(6)

where the integral is a complex contour integral which encircles the two turning points on the real axis. This beautiful formula was first written in this form by J.L.Dunham
[
2
]
.

By direct calculation it may be shown that the first three terms of the
S_{n}^{\prime}
are given by

\displaystyle S_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q^{\prime}}{4Q}=-\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dx}[\log Q]

(7)

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{5[Q^{\prime}]^{2}}{32[Q]^{\frac{5}{2}}}\ -\ \frac{Q^{\prime\prime}}{8[Q]^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(8)

\displaystyle S_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{-15[Q^{\prime}]^{3}}{64Q^{4}}+\frac{9Q^{\prime}Q{\prime\prime}}{32Q^{3}}-\frac{Q^{\prime\prime\prime}}{16Q^{2}}\quad=\ \frac{d}{dx}\Big[\frac{5[Q^{\prime}]^{2}}{64Q^{3}}-\frac{Q^{\prime\prime}}{16Q^{2}}\Big]=\ -\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\frac{S_{2}^{\prime}}{S_{0}^{\prime}}\Big]

(9)

The lowest order WKB approximation arises from the use of the
n=0
and
n=1
terms given in (
4
) and (
7
) in (
6
). The contribution of the
n=0
term to the integral in (
6
) may be found by the choosing a single-valued
Q(z)
by the joining of the turning points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
by a branch cut which gives

\frac{1}{2i}\oint-\sqrt{[V(z)-E]}dz=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\sqrt{[E-V(x)]}dx

(10)

The contribution of the
n=1
term to the integral in (
6
) is

\frac{1}{2i}\oint-\frac{Q^{\prime}(z)}{Q(z}dz=-\frac{1}{8i}[\ln Q(z)]|_{z}^{z}\ \Longrightarrow-\frac{1}{8i}4\pi i=-\frac{\pi}{2}

(11)

Evaluating
\ln Q(z)
once around the contour gives
4i\pi
because the contour encloses two simple zeros of
Q(z)
at
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. Thus the first two terms in the expression given in (
6
) leads to the approximate WKB quantization rule

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\sqrt{[E-V(x)]}=\Big(K+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\pi,\quad K=0,1,2,...

(12)

an approximation that increases in accuracy as
K
increases to high values. The exact quantization formula given in (
6
) is a generalization of the well known WKB quantization formula.

It has been suggested (Bender
et al
) that only the even terms in the series expansion in the Dunham formula need to be considered since
S_{2n+1}^{\prime},\ n\geq 1
, is a total derivative of a function and hence gives a vanishing contribution to the contour integral. To understand this we note that the quantization condition (
6
) is a constraint on the phase of
\psi(x)
in (
3
).
S_{2n+1}^{\prime},\ n\geq 1
, is always real because it contains no fractional powers of
(V-E)
and therefore cannot contribute to the phase of
\psi(x)
. It is
S_{2n}^{\prime}
which becomes imaginary as x crosses into a classically allowed region and causes the wave function to become oscillatory. It is then no surprise that
S_{2n+1}^{\prime},\ n\geq 1
, drops out of the quantization condition.
Even though this reasoning by Bender
et al
is valid no direct construction showing that
S_{2n+1}^{\prime}
is a total derivative has been given. It is my purpose to exhibit the explicit structure of
S_{2n+1}
.

2
Odd members of the WKB series as exact derivatives

In terms of the functions defined by
T_{n}\equiv S_{n}^{\prime}
the recursion relation arising from the WKB series may be written in the form

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{Q}\ ,\quad T_{1}=-\frac{T_{0}^{\prime}}{2T_{0}}\ ,\quad T_{2}=-\frac{T_{1}^{\prime}+T_{1}^{2}}{2T_{0}}

(13)

\displaystyle T_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2T_{0}}\Big[T_{n-1}^{\prime}\ +\ \sum_{m=1}^{n-1}T_{m}T_{n-m}\Big]\ ,n\geq 3

(14)

Using the expression for
T_{1}
this recursion relation may be be brought to the form

T_{n}=-\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{d}{dx}\frac{T_{n-1}}{T_{0}}+\frac{1}{T_{0}}\sum_{m=2}^{n-2}T_{m}T_{n-m}\Big]\,\quad n\geq 3

(15)

Now the even and odd members of the series can be scaled differently using

F_{j}\equiv 2T_{2j+1},\quad G_{j}\equiv-\frac{T_{2j}}{T_{0}}

(16)

so that the recursion relation for the odd members of the WKB series becomes

F_{n}=G_{n}^{\prime}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}F_{n-m}

(17)

Our aim is to prove that
F_{n}
are exact differentials for all values of
n
.

The first few expressions given by

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=G_{1}^{\prime}

(18)

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=G_{2}^{\prime}+G_{1}F_{1}=G_{2}^{\prime}+G_{1}G_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[G_{2}+\frac{1}{2}G_{1}^{2}\Big]

(19)

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=G_{3}^{\prime}+G_{1}F_{2}+G_{2}F_{1}=G_{3}^{\prime}+G_{1}G_{2}^{\prime}+G_{2}G_{1}^{\prime}+G_{1}^{2}G_{1}^{\prime}

(20)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[G_{3}+G_{1}G_{2}+\frac{1}{3}G_{1}^{3}\Big]

(21)

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle=G_{4}^{\prime}+G_{1}F_{3}+G_{2}F_{2}+G_{3}F1

\displaystyle=G_{4}^{\prime}+G_{1}G_{3}^{\prime}+G_{2}G_{2}^{\prime}+G_{3}G_{1}^{\prime}+G_{1}^{2}G_{2}^{\prime}+G_{1}G_{2}G_{1}^{\prime}+G_{2}G_{1}G_{1}^{\prime}+G_{1}^{3}G_{1}^{\prime}

(22)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[G_{4}+G_{1}G_{3}+\frac{1}{2}G_{2}^{2}+G_{1}^{2}G_{2}+\frac{1}{4}G_{1}^{4}\Big]

(23)

suggest a general structure. This structure may be extracted by iteration of (
17
). The first few iterations

\displaystyle F_{n}=G_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}\Big[G_{n-m}^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{j}F_{n-m-j}\Big]

\displaystyle=G_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}^{\prime}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}\Big[G_{n-m-j}^{\prime}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-m-j-1}G_{k}F_{n-m-j-k}\Big]

\displaystyle=G_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}^{\prime}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{n-m-j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n-3}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-m-j-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{k}F_{n-m-j-k}

(24)

show that the solution for
F_{n}
may be given in the form

\displaystyle F_{n}=G_{n}^{\prime}\

\displaystyle+\ \sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}^{\prime}\ +\ \sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{n-m-j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\ \sum_{m=1}^{n-3}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-j-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{k}G_{n-m-j-k}^{\prime}\ +....+\ G_{1}^{n-1}G_{1}^{\prime}

(25)

It can be shown that this expression for
F_{n}
is an exact differential. This can be accomplished by noting that each of the terms in the groupings of term in the expression for
F_{n}
is an exact differential because of the indexes in the summations. For example

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}^{\prime}G_{n-m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}\Big]

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{n-j-m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dx}\Big[\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{n-j-m}\Big]

(27)

Hence

\displaystyle F_{n}=\frac{d}{dx}\Big[G_{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}G_{m}G_{n-m}+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{n-m-j}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{m=1}^{n-3}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-j-m-1}G_{m}G_{j}G_{k}G_{n-m-j-k}+....+\frac{1}{n}G_{1}^{n}\Big]

(28)

which explicitly demonstrates the exact differential character of
F_{n}
.

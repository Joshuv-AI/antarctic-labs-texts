Hydrodynamics formalism with Spin dynamics Thanks: Presented at the on-line meeting Criticality in QCD and the Hadron Resonance Gas, Wrocław, Poland, July 29–31, 2020
Rajeev Singh

Abstract
We review the key steps of the relativistic fluid dynamics formalism with spin degrees of freedom initiated recently. We obtain equations of motion of the expansion of the system from the underlying definitions of quantum kinetic theory for the equilibrium phase space distribution functions. We investigate the dynamics of spin polarization of the system in the Bjorken hydrodynamical background.

2
Distribution functions in equilibrium

If we know phase space distribution function for the system’s equilibrium state, then it is possible to derive relativistic hydrodynamics from the underlying kinetic theory definitions
[
58
]
. Following ideas developed by Becattini
et al
.
[
6
]
, we take into consideration the following distribution functions for the relativistic systems of spin
{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
massive particles (and antiparticles) in the local equilibrium state.

\displaystyle f^{+}_{rs}(x,p)={\bar{u}}_{r}(p)X^{+}u_{s}(p),\qquad f^{-}_{rs}(x,p)=-{\bar{v}}_{s}(p)X^{-}v_{r}(p),

(1)

where
x
and
p
is the space-time position coordinate and the four-vector momentum, respectively, with
u_{r}(p)
and
v_{r}(p)
being the Dirac bispinors (
r,s=1,2
). Dirac bispinors follow the normalization conditions as
{\bar{u}}_{r}(p)u_{s}(p)=\,\delta_{rs}
and
{\bar{v}}_{r}(p)v_{s}(p)=-\,\delta_{rs}
, here
\delta_{rs}
is the Dirac delta function and,
X^{\pm}
have the following form in terms of relativistic Boltzmann distributions

\displaystyle X^{\pm}=\exp\left[\pm\xi(x)-\beta_{\mu}(x)p^{\mu}\pm\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu\nu}(x){\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}\right],

where
\beta^{\mu}\equiv U^{\mu}/T
and
\xi\equiv\mu/T
, here
T
,
\mu
and
U^{\mu}
is the temperature, baryon chemical potential and four-vector velocity, respectively, and
\omega_{\mu\nu}
is the second rank asymmetric tensor known as spin polarization tensor with
{\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{i}{4}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]
being the spin operator.

With the help of the expressions from Ref.
[
59
]
and Eqs. (
1
) we can obtain the Wigner functions in equilibrium as follows

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\pm\xi}}{4m}\int dP\,e^{-\beta\cdot p}\,\,\delta^{(4)}(k\mp p)

(2)

\displaystyle\times\left[2m(m\pm\not{p})\cosh(\zeta)\pm\frac{\sinh(\zeta)}{2\zeta}\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\,(\not{p}\pm m){\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}(\not{p}\pm m)\right],

where
k
being the off mass-shell particle four-momentum,

dP=d^{3}p/((2\pi)^{3}E_{p})
is the invariant measure with
E_{p}=\sqrt{m^{2}+{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}
denoting the on-shell energy of the particle, and
\zeta=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}}
in terms of spin polarization tensor.

Wigner function can also be expanded using Clifford-algebra expansion (
2
)

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[{\cal F}^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,k)+i\gamma_{5}{\cal P}^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,k)+\gamma^{\mu}{\cal V}^{\pm}_{{\rm eq},\mu}(x,k)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 28.45274pt+\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}{\cal A}^{\pm}_{{\rm eq},\mu}(x,k)+{\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}{\cal S}^{\pm}_{{\rm eq},\mu\nu}(x,k)\right],

where
\mathcal{X}\in\left\{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{V}_{\mu},\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}\right\}
are the coefficient functions of the Wigner function, which can obtained from the trace of
{\cal W}^{\pm}_{\rm eq}(x,k)
multiplying first by:
\left\{\mathbf{1},-i\gamma_{5},\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5},2\Sigma_{\mu\nu}\right\}

3
Kinetic and hydrodynamical equations

The kinetic equation to be followed by Wigner function is

\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{\mu}K^{\mu}-m\right){\cal W}(x,k)=C[{\cal W}(x,k)],

(3)

with
K^{\mu}=k^{\mu}+\frac{i\hbar}{2}\,\partial^{\mu}
.
For global equilibrium state, the Wigner function follows exactly Eq. (
3
) with the collision term
C[{\cal W}(x,k)]=0
. The widely used method of treating Eq. (
3

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{(0)}+\hbar\mathcal{X}^{(1)}+\hbar^{2}\mathcal{X}^{(2)}+\cdots.

Up to the first order (i.e. next-to-leading order) in
\hbar

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{eq}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\nu}

\displaystyle k^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{eq}}(x,k)=0,\quad k^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\nu}(x,k)=0,\quad k_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\nu}(x,k)=0.

(4)

In the case of global equilibrium Eqs. (
4
) are satisfied exactly which in-turn yields the conditions that
\beta_{\mu}
is a Killing vector, whereas,

But in the case of local equilibrium Eqs. (
4

[

and by permitting

[

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{\mu}N^{\mu}=0,

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm GLW}=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}S_{\rm GLW}^{\lambda,\alpha\beta}=0,

(7)

here the baryon current, the energy-momentum and the spin tensors are based on the forms by the de Groot - van Leeuwen - van Weert (GLW)
[
59

\displaystyle N^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nU^{\alpha},

(8)

\displaystyle T^{\alpha\beta}_{\rm GLW}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon+P)U^{\alpha}U^{\beta}-Pg^{\alpha\beta},

(9)

\displaystyle S^{\alpha,\beta\gamma}_{\rm GLW}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh(\xi)\left[n_{(0)}U^{\alpha}\omega^{\beta\gamma}+{\cal A}_{(0)}\,U^{\alpha}U^{\delta}U^{[\beta}\omega^{\gamma]}_{\hphantom{\alpha}\delta}\right.

(11)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\sinh(\xi)\,n_{(0)}(T),

(12)

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\cosh(\xi)\,\varepsilon_{(0)}(T),

Bjorken expansion set-up

Since the spin polarization tensor
\omega_{\mu\nu}
is a 2nd rank asymmetric tensor, so in analogy to the Faraday electromagnetic field strength tensor, it can be written into electric-like (
\kappa
) and magnetic-like (
\omega

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mu}U_{\nu}-\kappa_{\nu}U_{\mu}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}U^{\alpha}\omega^{\beta},

(19)

where
\kappa
and
\omega

U_{\mu}

For longitudinal boost-invariant and transversely homogeneous systems

\displaystyle U^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(t,0,0,z\right)=\left(\cosh(\eta),0,0,\sinh(\eta)\right),

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(0,1,0,0\right),

\displaystyle Y^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(0,0,1,0\right),

\displaystyle Z^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(z,0,0,t\right)=\left(\sinh(\eta),0,0,\cosh(\eta)\right),

(20)

where longitudinal proper time is defined as
\tau=\sqrt{t^{2}-z^{2}}
and, the space-time rapidity is defined as
\eta=\half\ln((t+z)/(t-z))

\displaystyle U\cdot U=1,

\displaystyle X\cdot U\,\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y\cdot U\,\,\,\,=\,\,Z\cdot U\,\,=\,\,0,

\displaystyle X\cdot Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y\cdot Z\,\,=\,\,Z\cdot X\,\,=\,\,0.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\omega X}(\tau)X^{\alpha}+C_{\omega Y}(\tau)Y^{\alpha}+C_{\omega Z}(\tau)Z^{\alpha},

Particle spin polarization at freeze-out

To calculate the mean spin polarization per particle, the following formula is used

56

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\mu}\rangle=E_{p}\frac{d\Pi_{\mu}(p)}{d^{3}p}/E_{p}\frac{d{\cal{N}}(p)}{d^{3}p},

(26)

with
E_{p}\frac{d\Pi_{\mu}(p)}{d^{3}p}
being the total value of the Pauli-Lubański vector (after integrating over the freeze-out hypersuface,
\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}
),

\displaystyle E_{p}\frac{d\Pi_{\mu}(p)}{d^{3}p}=-\frac{\cosh(\xi)}{(2\pi)^{3}m}\int\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}\,e^{-\beta\cdot p}\,\tilde{\omega}_{\mu\beta}p^{\beta},

and

\displaystyle E_{p}\frac{d{\cal{N}}(p)}{d^{3}p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\cosh(\xi)}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\Delta\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}\,e^{-\beta\cdot p},

is the total momentum density of both particles and antiparticles with four-momentum given as
p^{\lambda}=\left(m_{T}\cosh y_{p},p_{x},p_{y},m_{T}\sinh y_{p}\right)
.

After performing the the canonical boost
[
64
]
of (
26
), we obtain the polarization vector
\langle\pi^{\star}_{\mu}\rangle
in the local rest frame of the particle as

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\star}_{\mu}\rangle=-\frac{1}{8m}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\\
\left(\frac{p_{x}\sinh y_{p}}{b}\right)a_{i}+\left(\frac{\chi\,p_{x}\cosh y_{p}}{b}\right)a_{j}\!+\!2C_{\kappa Z}p_{y}\!-\!\chi C_{\omega X}{m}_{T}\\
\\
\left(\frac{p_{y}\sinh y_{p}}{b}\right)a_{i}+\left(\frac{\chi\,p_{y}\cosh y_{p}}{b}\right)a_{j}\!-\!2C_{\kappa Z}p_{x}\!-\!\chi C_{\omega Y}{m}_{T}\\
\\
-\left(\frac{m\cosh y_{p}+m_{T}}{b}\right)a_{i}-\left(\frac{\chi\,m\,\sinh y_{p}}{b}\right)a_{j}\\
\end{array}\right],

(34)

with
a_{i}=\chi\left(C_{\kappa X}p_{y}-C_{\kappa Y}p_{x}\right)+2C_{\omega Z}m_{T}
,
a_{j}=C_{\omega X}p_{x}+C_{\omega Y}p_{y}

b=m_{T}\cosh y_{p}+m

\chi=\left(K_{0}\left(\hat{m}_{T}\right)+K_{2}\left(\hat{m}_{T}\right)\right)/K_{1}\left(\hat{m}_{T}\right)
and
\hat{m}_{T}=m_{T}/T

7
Summary

We briefly presented the key ingredients of relativistic perfect-fluid hydrodynamics with spin framework initiated recently. From the definitions of kinetic theory for the equilibrium phase space distribution functions in the local equilibrium we obtained the equations of motions for the expansion of the the system. For the case of Bjorken type of flow we investigated the system’s spin polarization dynamics, which in turn show that the scalar functions describing the dynamics of the spin polarization evolve independently of each other. These results are used to obtain the particle spin polarization at the freeze-out hypersurface. We however note that within the current simple set-up of Bjorken symmetry experimental measurements cannot be addressed properly.

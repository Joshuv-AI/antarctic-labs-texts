Quasi-exactly solvable extensions of the Kepler-Coulomb potential on the sphere
C. Quesne

Abstract
We consider a family of extensions of the Kepler-Coulomb potential on a d d -dimensional sphere and analyze it in a deformed supersymmetric framework, wherein the starting potential is known to exhibit a deformed shape invariance property. We show that the members of the extended family are also endowed with such a property, provided some constraint conditions relating the potential parameters are satisfied, in other words they are conditionally deformed shape invariant. Since, in the second step of the construction of a partner potential hierarchy, the constraint conditions change, we impose compatibility conditions between the two sets to build quasi-exactly solvable potentials with known ground and first-excited states. Some explicit results are obtained for the first three members of the family. We then use a generating function method, wherein the first two superpotentials, the first two partner potentials, and the first two eigenstates of the starting potential are built from some generating function W + ​ ( r ) W_{+}(r) [and its accompanying function W − ​ ( r ) W_{-}(r) ]. From the results obtained for the latter for the first three family members, we propose some formulas for W ± ​ ( r ) W_{\pm}(r) valid for the m m th family member, depending on m + 1 m+1 constants a 0 a_{0} , a 1 a_{1} , …, a m a_{m} . Such constants satisfy a system of m + 1 m+1 linear equations. Solving the latter allows us to extend the results up to the seventh family member and then to formulate a conjecture giving the general structure of the a i a_{i} constants in terms of the parameters of the problem.



Keywords: Schrödinger equation, Kepler-Coulomb potential, quasi-exactly solvable potentials, supersymmetry

2
The Kepler-Coulomb problem on the sphere and deformed supersymmetry

In Ref.
[
33
]
, it was shown that the Lagrangian of the classical nonlinear oscillator introduced by Mathews and Lakshmanan
[
1
]
can be generalized to a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space, where it is defined by

L=\frac{1}{2}\frac{m}{1+\lambda r^{2}}\left(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}+\lambda\sum_{i<j}(x_{i}v_{j}-x_{j}v_{i})^{2}\right)-{\cal V}(r),\qquad{\cal V}(r)=\frac{1}{2}m\alpha^{2}\frac{r^{2}}{1+\lambda r^{2}}.

(2.1)

Here all summations run over
i,j=1,2,\ldots,d
, and
r^{2}\equiv\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}
, with
r
running on
(0,+\infty)
or
(0,1/\sqrt{|\lambda|})
according to whether the nonlinearity parameter
\lambda
is positive or negative. It was also shown that to

corresponds a Hamiltonian

H=\frac{1}{2m}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}+\lambda\left(\sum_{i}x_{i}p_{i}\right)^{2}\right)+{\cal V}(r)=\frac{1}{2m}\left((1+\lambda r^{2})\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}-\lambda\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}^{2}\right)+{\cal V}(r),

(2.2)

where
J_{ij}=x_{i}p_{j}-x_{j}p_{i}
. Such a Hamiltonian in Euclidean space was then proved to be interpretable as describing the Hamiltonian of an oscillator in a constant-curvature space with the curvature
\kappa
related to the nonlinearity parameter
\lambda
through the relation
\kappa=-\lambda
.

A similarr approach was later on applied
[
27

to the case where the nonlinear oscillator considered in (
2.1

2.2

{\cal V}(r)=-\frac{Q}{r}\sqrt{1+\lambda r^{2}},\qquad Q>0.

(2.3)

The latter is also amenable to an interpretation as a KC potential in a space of constant curvature
\kappa=-\lambda

[

To quantize (

\begin{split}{\hat{H}}&=-\left((1+\lambda r^{2})\hat{\Delta}+\lambda r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\lambda{\hat{J}}^{2}\right)+{\cal V}(r)\\

(2.4)

\hbar=2m=1

2.3

\kappa=-\lambda>0

\kappa=-\lambda<0

The Schrödinger equation corresponding to (
2.4

\left(-(1-\kappa r^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-(d-1-d\kappa r^{2})\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+\frac{l(l+d-2)}{r^{2}}-\frac{Q}{r}\sqrt{1-\kappa r^{2}}-{\cal E}\right)R(r)=0,

(2.5)

where
l=0
, 1, 2, … is the angular momentum quantum number,
Q>0
, and the radial variable
r
runs over
(0,1/\sqrt{\kappa})
. The differential operator in (
2.5

(1-\kappa r^{2})^{-1/2}r^{d-1}dr

R(r)

(1-\kappa r^{2})^{-1/2}r^{d-1}dr

Equation (

\left(-\sqrt{f(r)}\frac{d}{dr}f(r)\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{f(r)}+V(r)-E\right)\psi(r)=0,

(2.6)

where

\begin{split}&f(r)=\sqrt{1-\kappa r^{2}},\\
&V(r)=V(r;l,Q)=\frac{L(L+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{Q}{r}f(r),\quad L=l+\frac{d-3}{2},\\
&E={\cal E}+\frac{1}{4}\kappa(d-1)^{2},\\
&\psi(r)=r^{(d-1)/2}f^{-1/2}(r)R(r),\end{split}.

(2.7)

and can then be interpreted as a deformed Schrödinger equation, written in terms of a deformed radial momentum
\hat{\pi}_{r}=\sqrt{f(r)}(-{\rm i}d/dr)\sqrt{f(r)}
, or as a position-dependent mass (PDM) Schrödinger equation

\left(-m^{-1/4}(r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{-1/2}(r)\frac{d}{dr}m^{-1/4}(r)+V(r)-E\right)\psi(r)=0

(2.8)

with

[
34

and the differential operator

[
35

normalizable with respect to

on the interval
(0,1/\sqrt{\kappa})

It turns out

A deformed Schrödinger equation, such as (

V_{1}(r)=V(r)-E_{0},

(2.14)

Here

\hat{H}_{1}=\hat{A}^{+}\hat{A}^{-}+E_{0},\qquad\hat{H}_{2}=\hat{A}^{-}\hat{A}^{+}+E_{0},

(2.16)

\hat{A}^{\pm}=\mp\sqrt{f(r)}\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{f(r)}+W(r).

(2.17)

\hat{A}^{+}
and

\hat{A}^{-}\hat{H}_{1}=\hat{H}_{2}\hat{A}^{-},\qquad\hat{A}^{+}\hat{H}_{2}=\hat{H}_{1}\hat{A}^{+}.

(2.18)

The operator
\hat{A}^{-}
annihilates the ground-state wavefunction (

, whereas

\hat{H}_{1}

On considering
\hat{H}_{2}

\hat{H}^{\prime}_{1,2}=\hat{\pi}_{r}^{2}+V^{\prime}_{1,2}(r)+E^{\prime}_{0},\qquad V^{\prime}_{1,2}(r)=W^{\prime 2}(r)\mp f(r)\frac{dW^{\prime}}{dr},

V^{\prime}_{1}(r)+E^{\prime}_{0}=V_{2}(r)+E_{0}.

The first-excited state wavefunction

\psi_{1}(r)\propto\hat{A}^{+}\psi^{\prime}_{0}(r).

(2.22)

In the case of the KC potential on the sphere of Eq. (

(2.25)

3.1
First extension

For
m=1
, the potential (
2.26

V(r)=\frac{L(L+1)}{r^{2}}-\frac{Q}{r}f+\kappa B_{1}\frac{r}{f}+\kappa B_{2}\frac{1}{f^{2}},\qquad B_{2}>0,

(3.1)

and depends on the four parameters
L
,
Q
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
. Let us assume a superpotential of the type

W(r)=\frac{\xi}{r}f+\eta+\zeta\frac{r}{f},\qquad\xi\leq 0,\qquad\zeta>0,

(3.2)

depending on three parameters
\xi
,
\eta
, and
\zeta
. As shown in (
2.13
), in DSUSY the rescaled potential (
2.12
) is represented by
V_{1}(r)=W^{2}-fdW/dr
. With the extended potential (
3.1
), this Riccati equation gives rise to the system of equations

\begin{split}&\xi(\xi+1)=L(L+1),\\
&-\kappa\xi^{2}+2\xi\zeta+\eta^{2}-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\kappa}=-E_{0},\\
&2\xi\eta=-Q,\\
&2\eta\zeta=\kappa B_{1},\\
&\frac{\zeta}{\kappa}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\kappa}-1\right)=B_{2}\end{split}.

(3.3)

for the three unknowns
\xi
,
\eta
,
\zeta
. The first, third, and fourth of these equations lead to the values of the latter

\xi=-L-1,\qquad\eta=\frac{Q}{2(L+1)},\qquad\zeta=\frac{\kappa(L+1)B_{1}}{Q}.

(3.4)

Since
\zeta>0
, we must have
B_{1}>0
. From the second equation of (
3.3
), the ground-state energy
E_{0}
is obtained in the form

E_{0}=\kappa(L+1)^{2}-\frac{Q^{2}}{4(L+1)^{2}}+\frac{\kappa(L+1)B_{1}}{Q}\left(\frac{(L+1)B_{1}}{Q}+2L+2\right).

(3.5)

The last equation of (
3.3
) then provides a constraint

B_{2}=\frac{(L+1)B_{1}}{Q}\left(\frac{(L+1)B_{1}}{Q}-1\right),

(3.6)

connecting the potential parameters. The condition
B_{2}>0
imposes that
B_{1}>Q/(L+1)
. To
E_{0}
corresponds the wavefunction (
2.15
), which can be written as

\psi_{0}(r)\propto r^{-\xi}\exp\left(-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\arcsin(\sqrt{\kappa}r)\right)f^{\frac{\zeta}{\kappa}-\frac{1}{2}},

with
\xi
,
\eta

\zeta

3.4

The partner
V_{2}(r)=W^{2}+fdW/dr

in terms of some new parameters
L^{\prime}
,
Q^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}_{1}
,
B^{\prime}_{2}

\begin{split}&L^{\prime}(L^{\prime}+1)=\xi(\xi-1),\\
&R=-\kappa\xi^{2}+2\xi\zeta+\eta^{2}-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\kappa},\\

&B^{\prime}_{2}=\frac{\zeta}{\kappa}\left(\frac{\zeta}{\kappa}+1\right).\end{split}

On using some results obtained above, we get

L^{\prime}=L+1,\qquad Q^{\prime}=Q,\qquad B^{\prime}_{1}=B_{1},\qquad B^{\prime}_{2}=B_{2}+2\frac{(L+1)B_{1}}{Q},

V_{2}(r)

V^{\prime}(r)=V_{2}(r)

For higher

4
Generating function method

Let us start from two pairs of DSUSY partner Hamiltonians
(\hat{H}_{1},\hat{H}_{2})
and
(\hat{H}^{\prime}_{1},\hat{H}^{\prime}_{2})
, defined as in (
2.13
) and (
2.19
) in terms of two superpotentials
W(r)
and
W^{\prime}(r)
. The latter are related by the equation

W^{2}(r)+f(r)\frac{dW(r)}{dr}=W^{\prime 2}(r)-f(r)\frac{dW^{\prime}(r)}{dr}+E_{1}-E_{0},

(4.1)

which directly follows from (
2.20
), where we set
E_{1}=E^{\prime}_{0}
. From
W(r)
and
W^{\prime}(r)
, we may define the two functions

W_{+}(r)=W^{\prime}(r)+W(r),\qquad W_{-}(r)=W^{\prime}(r)-W(r),

(4.2)

which allow us to rewrite Eq. (
4.1
) as

f(r)\frac{dW_{+}(r)}{dr}=W_{+}(r)W_{-}(r)+E_{1}-E_{0}.

(4.3)

Hence,
W_{-}(r)
can be expressed in terms of
W_{+}(r)
and the energy difference
E_{1}-E_{0}
as

W_{-}(r)=\frac{f(r)dW_{+}(r)/dr+E_{0}-E_{1}}{W_{+}(r)}.

(4.4)

The generating function method starts from two functions
W_{+}(r)
and
W_{-}(r)
that are compatible, i.e., such that Eq. (
4.3
) is satisfied for some positive constant
E_{1}-E_{0}
. The two superpotentials are then determined from Eq. (
4.2
). The starting potential

[as well as its partner
V_{2}(r)
] follows from Eq. (
2.13
) and its ground-state wavefunction from Eq. (
2.15
). Its first-excited state wavefunction, given in Eq. (
2.22
) with

and

defined in (
2.17
) and (
2.21
), respectively, can be rewritten as

\psi_{1}(r)\propto W_{+}(r)f^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\int^{r}\frac{W^{\prime}(r^{\prime})}{f(r^{\prime})}dr^{\prime}\right),

(4.5)

where use is made of Eq. (
4.2
). It then remains to check that the functions
\psi_{0}(r)
and
\psi_{1}(r)
are normalizable functions on the definition interval of
r
.

The difficulty of the generating function method is to guess an appropriate function
W_{+}(r)
[and its accompanying function
W_{-}(r)
], such that Eq. (
4.3
) is satisfied for some positive constant
E_{1}-E_{0}
and that the potential and wavefunctions are well behaved.

5
Conclusion

In the present paper, we have shown that the combination of methods previously used to build families of extended oscillator potentials in a curved space with known ground and first-excited states can be applied to extended KC potentials on the sphere containing
2m
additional terms, where
m
may take any value in
\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

We have first extended the DSI symmetry, known to be valid for the KC potential alone, by completing it with
m
constraints relating the parameters of the extended potential, which therefore turns out to be CDSI. The second step in the construction of an extended potential hierarchy has then provided us with a second set of
m
constraints among the extended potential parameters. Compatibility conditions between the two sets of
m
constraints have given rise to QES extensions of the KC potential with two known eigenstates. In this way, some explicit results have been obtained for the first three members of the extended KC potential family.

To generalize those outcomes to other members of the extended KC potential family, we have then turned ourselves to the generating function method, wherein the first two DSUSY superpotentials (and hence the potentials and their first two eigenstates) are expressed in terms of a function
W_{+}(r)
[and its accompanying function
W_{-}(r)
]. From the results obtained for
W_{\pm}(r)
for the first three members of the family, we have proposed some general formulas for
W_{\pm}(r)
, depending on
m+1
constants
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
, …,
a_{m}
, and we have shown that such constants must satisfy a system of
m+1
linear equations. On solving such a system, we have obtained some explicit results for
m=4
, 5, 6, and 7. We have then formulated a conjecture giving the general structure of the
a_{i}
constants in terms of the parameters of the problem. All the aspects of this conjecture and the consequences drawn from it for the first two eigenstates of
V(r)
have been confirmed for
m\leq 7
.

The problem that remains open is to prove our conjecture by finding the explicit values of the constants appearing there. Solving it would be an interesting topic for future investigation. Applying the methods presented here to extensions of the KC potential in a hyperbolic space would also be an open question for future work.

Taking into account the large number of physical problems wherein the KC potential (either in curved space or in a PDM background) provides a first crude approximation and some additional terms are needed to reproduce experimental data, it is obvious that the exact results presented here for potentials including some additional terms may find some interesting applications to practical problems. The search for such applications would be another interesting topic for future investigation.

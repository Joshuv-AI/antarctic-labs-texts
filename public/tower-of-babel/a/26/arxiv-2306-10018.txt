A Unified Immersed Finite Element Error Analysis for One-Dimensional Interface Problems
Slimane Adjerid, Tao Lin, Haroun Meghaichi

Abstract
It has been noted that the traditional scaling argument cannot be directly applied to the error analysis of immersed finite elements (IFE) because, in general, the spaces on the reference element associated with the IFE spaces on different interface elements via the standard affine mapping are not the same. By analyzing a mapping from the involved Sobolev space to the IFE space, this article is able to extend the scaling argument framework to the error estimation for the approximation capability of a class of IFE spaces in one spatial dimension. As demonstrations of the versatility of this unified error analysis framework, the manuscript applies the proposed scaling argument to obtain optimal IFE error estimates for a typical first-order linear hyperbolic interface problem, a second-order elliptic interface problem, and the fourth-order Euler-Bernoulli beam interface problem, respectively.



keywords:

Immersed finite elements, error analysis, Bramble Hilbert lemma, scaling argument.

Introduction

Partial differential equations with discontinuous coefficients arise in many areas of sciences and engineering such as heat transfer, acoustics, structural mechanics, and electromagnetism. The discontinuity of the coefficients results in multiple challenges in the design and the analysis of numerical methods and it is an active area of research in the communities of finite element, finite volume, as well as finite difference method.

One challenge in error estimation for IFE methods is that the scaling argument commonly used in error estimation for traditional finite element methods cannot be directly applied. In the standard scaling argument, local finite element spaces on elements in a sequence of meshes are mapped to the same finite element space on the reference element via an affine transformation. However, the same affine transformation will map the local IFE spaces on interface elements in a sequence of meshes to different IFE spaces on the reference element because of the variation of interface location in the reference element, see the illustration in
Figure 1
. A straightforward application of the scaling argument to the analysis of the approximation capability of an IFE space will result in error bounds of a form
C(\check{\alpha})h^{r}
, i.e., the constant factor
C(\check{\alpha})
in the derived
error bounds depend on the location of the interface in the reference element, and this kind of error bounds cannot be used to show the convergence of the related IFE method unless one can show that the constant factor
C(\check{\alpha})
is bounded for all
\check{\alpha}
in the reference element, which, to our best knowledge, is difficult to establish. Alternative analysis techniques such as multi-point Taylor expansions are used
[
5
]
which becomes awkward for higher degree IFE spaces, particularly so for higher degree IFE spaces in higher dimension. To circumvent this predicament of the classical scaling argument, we introduce a mapping between the related Sobolev space and the IFE space by using weighted averages
of the derivatives in terms of the coefficients in the jump conditions. We show that the Sobolev norm of the error of this mapping can be bounded by the related Sobolev semi-norm. This essential property enables us to establish a Bramble-Hilbert type lemma for the IFE spaces, and, to our best knowledge, this is the first result that makes the scaling argument applicable in the error analysis of a class of IFE methods. For demonstrating the versatility of this unified error analysis framework, we apply it to establish, for the first time, the optimal approximation capability of the IFE space designed for the acoustic interface problem (
1
). Similarly, we apply this immersed scaling argument to the IFE space designed for an elliptic interface problem considered in
[
5
]
as well as the IFE space for the Euler-Bernoulli Beam interface problem considered in
[
24
,
26
,
35
]
leading to much simpler and elegant proofs.

Preliminaries

Throughout the article, we will consider a bounded open interval
I=(a,b)
with
\left|a\right|,\left|b\right|<\infty
, and let
\alpha\in I
be the interface point dividing
I
into two open intervals
I^{-}=(a,\alpha),I^{+}=(\alpha,b)
. This convention extends to any other open interval
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
B^{-}=B\cap(-\infty,\alpha)
and
B^{+}=B\cap(\alpha,\infty)
. For every bounded open interval
B
not containing
\alpha
, let
W^{m,p}(B)
be the usual Sobolev space on
B
equipped with the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,p,B}
and the seminorm
|\cdot|_{m,p,B}
. We are particularly interested in the case of
p=2
corresponding to the Hilbert space
H^{m}(B)=W^{m,2}(B)
, and we will use
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,B}
and
|\cdot|_{m,B}
to denote
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m,2,B},|\cdot|_{m,2,B}
respectively for convenience. We will use
(\cdot,\cdot)_{B}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{w,B}
to denote the classical and the weighted
L^{2}
inner product defined as

(f,g)_{B}=\int_{B}f(x)g(x)\ dx,\qquad(f,g)_{w,B}=\int_{B}w(x)f(x)g(x)\ dx,\qquad w(x)>0,\ \forall\ x\in B.

Given a positive finite sequence
r=(r_{i})_{i=0}^{\tilde{m}},~{\tilde{m}}\geq 0

and an open interval
B
containing
\alpha
, we introduce the following piecewise Sobolev space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r}(B)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\mid u_{\mid B^{\pm}}\in H^{m{+1}}(B^{\pm}),u^{(k)}(\alpha^{+})=r_{k}u^{(k)}(\alpha^{-}),\ \forall k=0,1,\dots,m\right\},~0\leq m\leq{\tilde{m}}.

(3)

The norms, semi-norms and the inner product that we will use on
\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r}(B)
are

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m+1,B}=\sqrt{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m+1,B^{-}}^{2}+\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m+1,B^{+}}^{2}},~~\left|\cdot\right|_{m,B}=\sqrt{\left|\cdot\right|_{m,B^{-}}^{2}+\left|\cdot\right|_{m,B^{+}}^{2}},~~\left(f,g\right)_{w,B}=\left(f,g\right)_{w,B^{-}}+\left(f,g\right)_{w,B^{+}}.

We note, by the Sobolev embedding theory, that
\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r}(B)
is a subspace of

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(\overline{B})

\displaystyle=\left\{u\mid u_{\mid B^{\pm}}\in C^{m}(\overline{B^{\pm}}),u^{(k)}(\alpha^{+})=r_{k}u^{(k)}(\alpha^{-}),\ \forall k=0,1,\dots,m\right\},~0\leq m\leq{\tilde{m}}.\

(4)

By dividing
I
into
N
sub-intervals, we obtain the following partition of
I
:

I_{k}=(x_{k-1},x_{k}),\quad\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{I_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N},\quad a=x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}=b,\qquad h=\max_{1\leq k\leq N}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}).

We will assume that there is
k_{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
such that
x_{k_{0}-1}<\alpha<x_{k_{0}}
, which is equivalent to
\alpha\in\overset{\circ}{I}_{k_{0}}
. We define the discontinuous immersed finite element space
W^{m}_{\alpha,r}
on the interval
I
as

{\cal W}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\left\{\varphi\mid\varphi_{\mid{I_{k}}}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(I_{k})\text{ for }k\in\{1,\dots,N\}\backslash\{k_{0}\}\text{ and }\varphi_{\mid I_{k_{0}}}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})\right\},

(5)

where
\mathcal{P}^{m}(I_{k})
is the space of polynomials of degree at most
m
on
I_{k}
and
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})
is the local immersed finite element (
LIFE
) space defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})=\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})\mid\varphi_{\mid I_{k_{0}}^{s}}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}\left(I_{k_{0}}^{s}\right),\ s=+,-\right\},~0\leq m\leq{\tilde{m}}.

(6)

In discussions from now on, given a function
v
in
\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r}
(or
\mathcal{C}^{m}_{\alpha,r}
or
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}
), its derivative is understood in the piecewise sense unless specified otherwise. By definition, we can readily verify that
\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})\subset\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})
for
a given finite sequence
r=(r_{i})_{i=0}^{\tilde{m}},~{\tilde{m}}\geq 1
.
In order to study the LIFE space
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})
, we will investigate the properties of the
corresponding reference IFE (
RIFE
) space
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
on the reference interval
\check{I}=[0,1]
with an interface point
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. Our goal is to extend the scaling argument to
such IFE spaces and use the IFE scaling argument to show IFE spaces such as
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})

have the optimal approximation capability, i.e., every function in
\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})
can be approximated by functions from the IFE space
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r}(I_{k_{0}})
at the optimal convergence rate.

Following the convention in the error analysis literature for finite element methods, we will often use a generic constant
C
in estimates whose value varies depending on the context, but this generic constant is independent of
h
and the interface
\alpha\in I_{k_{0}}
or
\check{\alpha}\in\check{I}
unless otherwise declared.

3
Properties of the RIFE space
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})

For a given function
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
, we will write
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})
where
\check{\varphi}_{s}=\check{\varphi}_{\mid\check{I}^{s}}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s})
for
s=+,-
. Additionally, we will use
\check{\varphi}_{s}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha})
to denote
\lim_{x\to\check{\alpha}^{s}}\check{\varphi}_{s}^{(k)}(x),~s=\pm
for a given integer
k\geq 0
. For clarity, we will use
s^{\prime}
to denote the dual of
s
, i.e., if
s=\pm
, then
s^{\prime}=\mp
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
s\in\{+,-\}
. The following statements hold

1.

For every
\check{\varphi}_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s})
there is a unique
\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s^{\prime}})
such that
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
.

2.

The dimension of
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is
m+1
.

3.

The set
\{\mathcal{N}^{k}_{\check{\alpha},r}\}_{k=0}^{m}
, where

\mathcal{N}^{k}_{\check{\alpha},r}(x)=\begin{cases}(x-\check{\alpha})^{k},&x\in\check{I}^{-},\\
r_{k}(x-\check{\alpha})^{k},&x\in\check{I}^{+},\end{cases}

(7)

forms a basis of
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
and will be referred to as the canonical basis.

Proof.

We will prove the statements in order:

1.

Let
\check{\varphi}_{\pm}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{\pm})
, then
(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
if and only if

\check{\varphi}_{\mp}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha})=\left(r_{k}\right)^{\mp 1}\check{\varphi}_{\pm}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha}),\qquad k=0,1,\dots,m,

which uniquely defines a polynomial
\check{\varphi}_{\mp}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{\mp})
:

\check{\varphi}_{\mp}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{\left(r_{k}\right)^{\mp 1}\check{\varphi}_{\pm}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha})}{k!}(x-\check{\alpha})^{k}.

2.

We have shown that the maps
\check{\varphi}_{-}\mapsto\check{\varphi}
is well defined and injective which implies that the map
\check{\varphi}\mapsto\check{\varphi}_{-}
is surjective since every
\check{\varphi}_{-}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{-})
can be extended to
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
. Hence,
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{-})
implying that the dimension of
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is
m+1
.

3.

We only need to show that
\{\mathcal{N}^{k}_{\check{\alpha},r}\}_{k=0}^{m}
is linearly independent: Assume that
\check{\varphi}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_{k}\mathcal{N}^{k}_{\check{\alpha},r}\equiv 0
, then
\check{\varphi}_{-}\equiv 0
which implies that
c_{k}=0
for all
k=0,1,\dots,m
.

∎

The results in
Lemma 1
allows us to introduce
an extension operator that maps
\check{\varphi}_{s}
to
\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
s\in\{+,-\}
. We define the extension operator
\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}:\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s})\to\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s^{\prime}})
that maps every
\check{\varphi}_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s})
to
\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{\varphi}_{s})=\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}
such that
(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
.

By
Lemma 1
, the extension operator
\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}
is well-defined and is linear. Furthermore, by
Lemma 1
again, this extension operator is also invertible. Consequently, the dimension of the RIFE space is the same as the dimension of the traditional polynomial space of the same degree.

Next, we will estimate the operator norm of
\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}
. Let
\check{h}_{-}=\check{\alpha},\ \check{h}_{+}=1-\check{\alpha}
be the lengths of the sub-intervals
\check{I}^{\pm}
formed by
\check{\alpha}
. First, let us consider the following example
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})=\mathcal{N}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}
defined by (
7
), we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{\varphi}_{-})\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}=\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}=|r_{m}|\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{m}\sqrt{\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}}\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}},\qquad\text{where }\check{h}_{-}=\check{\alpha},\ \check{h}_{+}=1-\check{\alpha}.

Hence, if
h_{-}>h_{+}
, we get
\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}\leq|r_{m}|\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}
. In the following lemma, we will show that a similar result holds for all
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
. Consequently, for every interface position
\check{\alpha}\in I
, one of the two extension operators
\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r},~s^{\prime}=-,+
will be bounded independently of
\check{\alpha}
.

Lemma 2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
that depends on
m
such that for every
(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
, we have

\left\lVert\mathcal{E}^{m,s^{\prime}}_{\check{\alpha},r}\check{\varphi}_{s}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{s^{\prime}}}=\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{s^{\prime}}}\leq C\sqrt{\frac{\check{h}_{s^{\prime}}}{\check{h}_{s}}}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}r_{i}^{{\tilde{s}}}\right)\max\left(1,\left(\frac{\check{h}_{s^{\prime}}}{\check{h}_{s}}\right)^{m}\right)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{s}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{s}},\qquad s=+,-,

(8)

where
{\tilde{s}}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mp}1
for
s=\pm
.

In particular, if
\check{h}_{s}\geq\check{h}_{s^{\prime}}
, we have

\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{s^{\prime}}}\leq C\sqrt{\check{h}_{s^{\prime}}}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}r_{i}^{{\tilde{s}}}\right)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{s}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{s}}.

(9)

Proof.

First, we note that (
9
) is a straightforward consequence of (
8
). Here, we only need prove (
8
) for
s=-
since the case
s=+
can be proven similarly. For every
(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
, we first define
\hat{\varphi}_{-}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}([0,1])
as
\hat{\varphi}_{-}(\xi)=\check{\varphi}_{-}(\check{h}_{-}\xi)
which yields

\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)=\check{h}_{-}^{i}\check{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha}),\quad i=0,1,\dots,m.

(10)

Now, let us write
\check{\varphi}_{+}
as a finite Taylor sum around
\check{\alpha}
and use
\check{\varphi}_{+}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha})=r_{i}\check{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha})
to obtain:

\check{\varphi}_{+}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\check{\varphi}_{+}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha})\frac{(x-\check{\alpha})^{i}}{i!}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}r_{i}\check{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha})\frac{(x-\check{\alpha})^{i}}{i!}.

Using (
10
), we can replace
\check{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(\check{\alpha})
by
\check{h}_{-}^{-i}\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)
:

\check{\varphi}_{+}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}r_{i}\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)\check{h}_{-}^{-i}\frac{(x-\check{\alpha})^{i}}{i!}.

(11)

We square and integrate (
11
), then we apply the change of variables

to get

\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}^{2}=\int_{\check{\alpha}}^{1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}r_{i}\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)\check{h}_{-}^{-i}\frac{(x-\check{\alpha})^{i}}{i!}\right)^{2}\ dx=\int_{0}^{\check{h}_{+}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m}r_{i}\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)\left(\frac{z}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{i}\frac{1}{i!}\right)^{2}\ dz.

We can bound

and
|\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)|

\left(\frac{z}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{i}

\left(\frac{z}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{i}\leq\max\left(1,\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{m}\right).

We also have
\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{1}{i!}\leq e
. Using these bounds, we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}r_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}|\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)|\right)^{2}\max\left(1,\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{m}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\check{h}_{+}}e^{2}\ dz

\displaystyle=\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}r_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}|\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)|\right)^{2}\max\left(1,\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{m}\right)^{2}\check{h}_{+}e^{2}.

(12)

Since
\mathcal{P}^{m}([0,1])
is a finite dimensional space, all norms are equivalent. In particular, there is a constant
C(m)
such that

\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}|p^{(i)}(1)|\right)\leq C(m)\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{0,[0,1]},~\forall p\in\mathcal{P}^{m}([0,1]),

which leads to

\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}|\hat{\varphi}_{-}^{(i)}(1)|\right)^{2}\leq C(m)\left\lVert\hat{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,[0,1]}^{2}.

(13)

By using a change of variables, we can show that

\left\lVert\hat{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,[0,1]}^{2}=\frac{1}{\check{h}_{-}}\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}^{2}.

(14)

Finally, we combine (
12
), (
13
) and (
14
) to get

\left\lVert\mathcal{E}_{\check{\alpha},r}^{m,+}\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}^{2}\leq C(m)\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq m}r_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)\max\left(1,\left(\frac{\check{h}_{+}}{\check{h}_{-}}\right)^{m}\right)^{2}\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}^{2}

which is (
8
) for
s=-
.

Next, we will use the bounds on the extension operator
\mathcal{E}^{m,s}_{\check{\alpha},r}
to establish inverse inequalities which are independent of

Lemma 3
.

Let
\tilde{m}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. Then there exists
C(m,r)>0
independent of
\check{\alpha}
such that for every
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
we have

\displaystyle|\check{\varphi}|_{i,\check{I}}\leq C(m,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}},~0\leq i\leq m+1.

(15)

Proof.

The estimate given in (
15
) obviously holds for
i=0
and
i=m+1
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\check{h}_{-}\geq\check{h}_{+}
, this implies that
\check{h}_{-}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Then, using the classical inverse inequality
[
11
]
we have

\left\lVert\check{\varphi}^{\prime}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}\leq C\check{h}_{-}^{-1}\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}\leq 2C\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}.

(16)

By the Taylor expansion of
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}_{+}(x)
at
x=\check{\alpha}
, we have

\check{\varphi}^{\prime}_{+}=\mathcal{E}^{m-1,+}_{\check{\alpha},\tau(r)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\check{\varphi}_{-}\right),

(17)

where
\tau:(r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{m})\mapsto(r_{1},\dots,r_{m})
is the shift operator. By (
9
) and the inverse inequality given in (
16
), we have

\left\lVert\check{\varphi}^{\prime}_{+}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{+}}\leq C(m)\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}r_{i}\right)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}\leq C(m,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}.

(18)

Therefore, we have

\left|\check{\varphi}\right|_{1,\check{I}}=\left\lVert\check{\varphi}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq C(m,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}_{-}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}^{-}}\leq C(m,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}

which proves (
15
) for
i=1
.
Applying similar arguments, we can prove (
15
) for other values of
i
.

∎

Since
\check{\varphi}_{+}=\mathcal{E}^{m,+}_{\check{\alpha},r}\left(\check{\varphi}_{-}\right)
, the formula in (
17
) leads to the following identity about the permutation of the classical differential operator and the extension operator:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\mathcal{E}^{m,+}_{\check{\alpha},r}\left(\check{\varphi}_{-}\right)=\check{\varphi}^{\prime}_{+}=\mathcal{E}^{m-1,+}_{\check{\alpha},\tau(r)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\check{\varphi}_{-}\right),~\forall m\geq 1.

(19)

As a piecewise function
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
, the value of
\check{\varphi}
at
\check{\alpha}
is not defined in general since the two sided limits
\check{\varphi}_{-}(\check{\alpha})
and
\check{\varphi}_{+}(\check{\alpha})
could be different if
r_{0}\neq 1
. However, if
\check{\varphi}_{s}(\check{\alpha})=0
then
\check{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}(\check{\alpha})=0
for
s=+,-
. Furthermore, the multiplicity of
\check{\alpha}
as a root of
\check{\varphi}_{-}
is the same as its multiplicity as a root of
\check{\varphi}_{+}
. This observation motivates
to define
\check{\alpha}
as a root of
\check{\varphi}
of multiplicity
d
if
\check{\alpha}
is a root of
\check{\varphi}_{-}
of multiplicity
d
. The following theorem shows that the number of roots of a non-zero function
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
counting multiplicities cannot exceed
m
(similar to a polynomial of degree
m
), this theorem will be crucial to establish the existence of a Lagrange-type basis in
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
and constructing an immersed Radau projection later in
Section 5
.

In the discussions below, we will omit the phrase “counting multiplicities” for the sake of conciseness. For example, we say that
(x-2)^{2}
has two roots in
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1
.

For
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
, every non-zero
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
has at most
m
roots.

Proof.

We start from the base case
m=0
and then proceed by induction. Let
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{0}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
. if
\check{\varphi}\not\equiv 0
then
\check{\varphi}=c\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\check{\alpha},r}
for some
c\neq 0
. In this case,
\check{\varphi}
has no roots since
\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\check{\alpha},r}
has no roots.
Now, assume that for every positive sequence
q
, the number of roots of any non-zero
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},q}(\check{I})
is at most
m-1
. Next, we will show that for a given positive sequence
r
, every function
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
has at most
m
roots by contradiction:

Assume that
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is a non-zero function that has
j
disctinct roots
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{j}
of multiplicities
\{d_{i}\}_{i=1}^{j}
such that
D=d_{1}+d_{2}+\dots+d_{j}>m
. Therefore,
\xi_{j}>\check{\alpha}
and
\xi_{1}\leq\check{\alpha}
because
\check{\varphi}_{\pm}\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(\check{I}^{s})
. let
\xi_{i_{0}}
be the largest root
that is not larger than
\check{\alpha}
, i.e.,

0\leq\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}<\dots<\xi_{i_{0}}\leq\check{\alpha}<\xi_{i_{0}+1}<\dots<\xi_{j}\leq 1,

By the definition of
\check{\varphi}=(\check{\varphi}_{-},\check{\varphi}_{+})
,
\check{\varphi}_{-}
has
D_{1}=d_{1}+d_{2}+\dots+d_{i_{0}}
roots in
[\xi_{1},\xi_{i_{0}}]
and
\check{\varphi}_{+}
has
D_{2}=d_{i_{0}+1}+d_{i_{0}+2}+\dots+d_{j}
roots in
[\xi_{i_{0}+1},\xi_{j}]
. Therefore,
\check{\varphi}_{-}^{\prime}
has
D_{1}-1
roots in
[\xi_{1},\xi_{i_{0}}]
and
\check{\varphi}_{+}^{\prime}
has
D_{2}-1
roots in
[\xi_{i_{0}+1},\xi_{j}]
. It remains to show that
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}
has an additional root in
(\xi_{i_{0}},\xi_{i_{0}+1})
. To show that,we consider two cases:

•

\xi_{i_{0}}=\check{\alpha}
: In this case,
\check{\varphi}
is continuous and
\check{\varphi}_{+}(\check{\alpha})=\check{\varphi}_{+}(\xi_{i_{0}+1})=0
. By the mean value theorem, we conclude that
\check{\varphi}_{+}^{\prime}
has a root in
(\xi_{i_{0}},\xi_{i_{0}+1})
.

•

\xi_{i_{0}}<\check{\alpha}
: Assume that
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in(\xi_{i_{0}},\xi_{i_{0}+1})\backslash\{\check{\alpha}\}
, then
\check{\varphi}_{-}(\check{\alpha})>0
. Since
r_{0}>0
, we have
\check{\varphi}_{+}(\check{\alpha})>0
. By integrating
\check{\varphi}_{+}^{\prime}
from
\check{\alpha}
to
\xi_{i_{0}+1}
, we get
0=\check{\varphi}_{+}(\xi_{i_{0}+1})>0
, a contradiction. A similar conclusion follows if we assume that
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)<0
for all
x\in(\xi_{i_{0}},\xi_{i_{0}+1})\backslash\{\check{\alpha}\}
. Therefore,
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}
changes sign at some
x_{0}\in(\xi_{i_{0}},\xi_{i_{0}+1})
. Since,
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}
does not change sign at
\check{\alpha}
, then
x_{0}\neq\check{\alpha}
and
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
(because
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}
is continuous at
x_{0}
).

In either case above,
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}
has
(D_{1}-1)+(D_{2}-1)+1=D-1>m-1
roots in
\check{I}
which contradicts the induction hypothesis since
\check{\varphi}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},\tau(r)}(\check{I})
. Therefore,
\check{\varphi}
has at most
m
roots.

∎

The previous theorem allows us to establish the existence of a Lagrange basis on
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
for every choice of nodes and for every degree
m
which was proved by Moon in
[
32
]
for a few specific cases
m=1,2,3,4
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. Assume
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}
are
m+1
distinct points in
\check{I}
, then there is a Lagrange basis
\{L_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
that satisfies

\displaystyle L_{i}(\xi_{j})=\delta_{i,j},\qquad\ 0\leq i,j\leq m.

(20)

Proof.

For each
0\leq i\leq m
, we construct
\tilde{L}_{i}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
such that
\tilde{L}_{i}(\xi_{j})=0
for all
j\neq i
by writing
\tilde{L}_{i}
as

\tilde{L}_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\mathcal{N}^{i}_{\check{\alpha},r},

for some
\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
chosen such that

\tilde{L}_{i}(\xi_{j})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\mathcal{N}^{i}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\xi_{j})=0,\quad\forall j\in\{0,1,\dots,i-1,i+1,\dots,m\}.

(21)

The equations (
21
) form a homogeneous system of
m
equations with
m+1
unknowns. Therefore, it has a non-zero solution.
From
Theorem 1
, we know that
\tilde{L}_{i}(\xi_{i})\neq 0
; otherwise,
\tilde{L}_{i}
would have
m+1
roots. This allows us to define
L_{i}
as

L_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{\tilde{L}_{i}(\xi_{i})}\tilde{L}(x).

By (
20
),
L_{i},0\leq i\leq m
are linearly independent. Consequently,
\{L_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
is a basis for
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
since its dimension is
m+1
from
Lemma 1
.

∎

In addition to having a Lagrange basis, the RIFE space has an orthogonal basis with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{w,\check{I}}
as stated in the following theorem in which we also show that if a function
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{w,\check{I}}
, then
\check{\varphi}
has exactly
m
distinct interior roots similar to the classical orthogonal polynomials. Although the theorem holds for a general weight
w
, we will restrict our attention to a piecewise constant function
w
:

w(x)=\begin{cases}w_{-},&x\in\check{I}^{-},\\
w_{+},&x\in\check{I}^{+},\end{cases}

(22)

where
w_{\pm}
are positive constants. The result of this theorem can also be considered as a generalization for the theorem about the orthogonal IFE basis described in
[
12
]
for elliptic interface problems.

Theorem 3
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 1,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
, and let
w:\check{I}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be defined as in (
22
), then there is a non-zero
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
such that

\displaystyle(\check{\varphi},\check{\psi})_{w,\check{I}}=\int_{\check{I}}w(x)\check{\varphi}(x)\check{\psi}(x)\ dx=0,\quad\forall\check{\psi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I}).

(23)

Furthermore,
\check{\varphi}
has exactly
m
distinct roots in the interior of
\check{I}
.

Proof.

Existence is a classical result of linear algebra.
The proof of the second claim follows the same steps used for orthogonal polynomials: Note that
\check{\varphi}
has at least one root of odd multiplicity in the interior of
\check{I}
since
(\check{\varphi},\mathcal{N}^{0}_{\check{\alpha},r})_{w,\check{I}}=0
.

Assume that
\check{\varphi}
has
j<m
distinct roots
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{j}
of odd multiplicity in the interior of
\check{I}
. Following the ideas in the proof of
Theorem 2
, we can show that there is
\check{\psi}_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{j}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
such that
\check{\psi}_{0}(\xi_{i})=0
for
1\leq i\leq j
.

Furthermore, all roots of
\check{\psi}_{0}
are simple according to
Theorem 1
since the sum of multiplicities cannot exceed
j
, and
\check{\psi}_{0}
changes sign at these roots. This means that
w\check{\varphi}\check{\psi}_{0}
does not change sign on
\check{I}
. As a consequence,
(\check{\varphi},\check{\psi}_{0})_{w,\check{I}}\neq 0
which contradicts the assumption
(\check{\varphi},\check{\psi})_{w,\check{I}}=0
for all
\check{\psi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
since
\mathcal{V}^{j}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
.
∎

For every integer
m
with
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 1
, we use
\mathcal{Q}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},w,r}(\check{I}),~m\geq 1
to denote the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
in
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
with respect to the weight
w
, that is

\mathcal{Q}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},w,r}(\check{I})=\left\{\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\mid(\check{\varphi},\check{\psi})_{w,\check{I}}=0,\ \forall\check{\psi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\right\}.

According to
Theorem 3
, one can see that
\check{\varphi}\mapsto\sqrt{\check{\varphi}(0)^{2}+\check{\varphi}(1)^{2}}
defines a norm on
\mathcal{Q}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},w,r}(\check{I})
which is one-dimensional. Thus, it is is equivalent to the
L^{2}
norm and the quantity
\frac{\sqrt{\check{\varphi}(0)^{2}+\check{\varphi}(1)^{2}}}{\left\lVert\check{\varphi}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}}
depends only on
\check{\alpha},w
and
r
(and not on the choice of
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{Q}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},w,r}(\check{I})
). Furthermore, The following lemma shows that the equivalence constant is independent of the interface location. This result will be crucial later in the analysis of Radau projections.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 1,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
w
be defined as in (
22
) then, there exist
C(m,w,r)
and
\tilde{C}(m,w,r)>0
independent of
\check{\alpha}
such that for every
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{Q}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},w,r}(\check{I})
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\check{\varphi}(0)^{2}+\check{\varphi}(1)^{2}}\geq C(m,w,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\geq\tilde{C}(m,w,r)\left\lVert\check{\varphi}\right\rVert_{1,\check{I}}.

(24)

Proof.

The inequality on the right follows from the inverse inequality (
15
) for the IFE funcdtions. For a proof of the inequality on the left, see
Appendix A
.

4
An immersed Bramble-Hilbert lemma and the approximation capabilities of the LIFE space

In this section, we will develop a new version of Bramble-Hilbert lemma that applies to functions in
\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
and its IFE counterpart. This lemma will serve as a fundamental tool for investigating the approximation capability of IFE spaces. In the discussions below, we will use
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
for the indicator function of a set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
and we define
w_{i}=r_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\check{I}^{-}}+\mathbbm{1}_{\check{I}^{+}}
for
i=0,1,\dots,m
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
, and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
.
Assume
(w_{i},v^{(i)})_{\check{I}}=0
for
i=0,1,\dots,m
. Then, there exists
C(i,r)>0
independent of
\check{\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}\leq C(i,r)|v|_{i,\check{I}},~i=0,1,\cdots,m+1.

(25)

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
and assume
(w_{i},v^{(i)})_{\check{I}}=0
for
i=0,1,\dots,m
.
Because
w_{0}
is such that
w_{0}v
is continuous and since
v_{\mid\check{I}^{\pm}}\in H^{1}(\check{I}^{\pm})
, we have
w_{0}v\in H^{1}(\check{I})
. Therefore, for any given
x,y\in\check{I}
, we have

w_{0}(x)v(x)-w_{0}(y)u(y)=\int_{y}^{x}w_{0}(z)v^{\prime}(z)\ dz

We integrate this identity on
\check{I}
with respect to
x
and use
(w_{0},v^{(0)})_{\check{I}}=0
to get

-w_{0}(y)v(y)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{y}^{x}w_{0}(z)v^{\prime}(z)\ dz\ dx,\quad\forall y\in\check{I}.

Taking the absolute value and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

|v(y)|\leq\frac{1}{\min(1,r_{0})}|w_{0}(y)v(y)|\leq\frac{\max(1,r_{0})}{\min(1,r_{0})}|v|_{1,\check{I}}=\max\left(r_{0},r_{0}^{-1}\right)|v|_{1,\check{I}},

which implies
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq\max\left(r_{0},r_{0}^{-1}\right)|v|_{1,\check{I}}
. Since
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},\tau(r)}(\check{I})
, where
\tau
is the shift operator described in (
17
), we can use the same reasoning to show that

|v|_{1,\check{I}}\leq\max\left(r_{1},r_{1}^{-1}\right)|v|_{2,\check{I}}.

Repeating the same arguments, we can obtain

\displaystyle|v|_{i,\check{I}}\leq\max\left(r_{i},r_{i}^{-1}\right)|v|_{i+1,\check{I}},~~i=0,1,\cdots,m

(26)

which leads to (
25
) with

C(i,r)=\sqrt{1+\sum_{k=0}^{i}\prod_{j=k}^{i}\max\left(r_{j}^{2},r_{j}^{-2}\right)}.

∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
. Then, there is a unique
\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
that satisfies

\int_{\check{I}}w_{i}(x)\frac{d^{i}}{dx^{i}}\left(v(x)-\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v(x)\right)\ dx=0,\qquad\forall i=0,1,\dots,m.

(27)

Proof.

For
v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
, to see that
\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v
exists and is unique, we consider the problem of finding
\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
such that

(w_{i},\check{\varphi}^{(i)})_{\check{I}}=(w_{i},v^{(i)})_{\check{I}},\qquad\text{for }i=0,1,\dots,m.

(28)

By
Lemma 1
, we can express
\check{\varphi}
in terms of the canonical basis

\check{\varphi}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{j}\mathcal{N}^{j}_{\check{\alpha},r}.

Then, by (
28
), the coefficients
\mathbf{c}
of
\check{\varphi}
are determined by the linear system
A\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{b}
, where
A=(A_{i,j})
is a triangular matrix with
A_{i,j}=(w_{i},(\mathcal{N}^{j}_{\check{\alpha},r})^{(i)})_{\check{I}},~0\leq i,j\leq m
and diagonal entries

A_{i,i}=(w_{i},(\mathcal{N}^{i}_{\check{\alpha},r})^{(i)})_{\check{I}}=i!(h_{-}+r_{i}h_{+})\neq 0,~0\leq i\leq m.

Therefore,
A
is invertible and
\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v=\check{\varphi}
is uniquely determined by (
27
).
∎

We note that the mapping
\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}:v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\mapsto\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is linear because
of the linearity of
integration.

We now present an immersed version of the Bramble-Hilbert lemma
[
10
]
which can be considered a generalization of the one-dimensional Bramble-Hilbert lemma in the sense that if
r\equiv 1
, then, this immersed Bramble-Hilbert lemma recovers the classical Bramble-Hilbert lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Let
{\tilde{m}}\geq m\geq i\geq 0,\{r_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\tilde{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\check{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. Assume
\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}:\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\to\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
is a linear map that satisfies the following two conditions

1.

\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}
is a projection on
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
in the sense that

\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\check{\varphi}=\check{\varphi},\quad\forall\check{\varphi}\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I}).

(29)

2.

There exists an integer
j
,
0\leq j\leq m+1
such that
\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}
is bounded with respect to the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{j,\check{I}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}\leq C\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{j,\check{I}},\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I}).

(30)

Then, there exists
C(m,r)>0
independent of
\check{\alpha}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}\leq C(m,r)\left(1+\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\right\rVert_{i,j,\check{I}}\right)|v|_{m+1,\check{I}},\quad v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I}),

(31)

where

\displaystyle\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\right\rVert_{i,j,\check{I}}=\sup\left\{\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}\mid v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})\text{ and }\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{j,\check{I}}=1\right\}.

Proof.

Since
\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}
is a projection in the sense of (
29
), we have
\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v=\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v
. Using the triangle inequality and (
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert v-\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v-\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}}+\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\left(v-\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right)\right\rVert_{i,\check{I}},

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\right\rVert_{i,j,\check{I}}\right)\left\lVert v-\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{j,\check{I}}\leq\left(1+\left\lVert\check{P}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\right\rVert_{i,j,\check{I}}\right)\left\lVert v-\check{\pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}v\right\rVert_{m+1,\check{I}},\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I}).

Then, applying
Lemma 5
and
Theorem 4

to the right hand side of the above estimate leads to (
31
).

∎

Next, we extend the results of
Lemma 6
to the physical interface element
I_{k_{0}}=[x_{k_{0}-1},x_{k_{0}-1}+h]
. Following the tradition in finite element analysis, for every function

\varphi
defined on the interface element
I_{k_{0}}
, we can map it to a function
\mathcal{M}\varphi=\check{\varphi}
defined on
the reference interval

5
Analysis of an IFE-DG method for a class of hyperbolic systems with discontinuous coefficients

In this section, we employ the results of
Section 3
and
Section 4
to analyse an IFE-DG method for the acoustic interface problem (
1
). To our knowledge, the analysis of such method for hyperbolic systems has so far not been considered in the literature unlike the IFE methods for elliptic problems. The main challenge that time-dependent problems present is the plethora of possible jump coefficients. For instance, the jump coefficients
r^{p}_{k},r^{v}_{k}
described in (
2
) for the acoustic interface problem are given by:

r^{p}_{2k}=r^{v}_{2k}=\left(\frac{c_{-}}{c_{+}}\right)^{2k},\qquad r^{p}_{2k+1}=\frac{\rho_{+}}{\rho_{-}}\left(\frac{c_{-}}{c_{+}}\right)^{2k},\qquad r^{v}_{2k+1}=\frac{\rho_{-}}{\rho_{+}}\left(\frac{c_{-}}{c_{+}}\right)^{2k+2},\quad k=0,1,\dots

(41)

The nature of the jump coefficients in (
41
) makes the study of this particular IFE space extremely tedious as observed in
[
32
]
. Fortunately, the theory developed in
Section 3
and
Section 4
is general and applies to any choice of positive jump coefficients.

5.1
Problem statement and preliminary results

Let
I=(a,b)
be a bounded interval containing
\alpha
, and let
\rho_{\pm},c_{\pm}
be positive constants describing the density and the sound speed in
I^{\pm}
, respectively. Now, we consider the acoustic interface problem on
I

\mathbf{u}_{t}(x,t)+A(x)\mathbf{u}_{x}(x,t)=0,\qquad x\in I\backslash\{\alpha\},\qquad t>0,

(42a)

where
\mathbf{u}=(p,u)^{T}
is the pressure-velocity couple and

A_{\mid I^{\pm}}=A_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\rho_{\pm}c_{\pm}^{2}\\
\rho_{\pm}^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(42b)

The matrices
A_{\pm}
can be decomposed as
A_{\pm}=P_{\pm}\Lambda_{\pm}P_{\pm}^{-1}
, where
\Lambda_{\pm}=\mathrm{diag}(-c_{\pm},c_{\pm})
. Using this eigen-decomposition, we define
A_{\pm}^{+}=P_{\pm}\mathrm{diag}(0,c_{\pm})P_{\pm}^{-1}
,
A_{\pm}^{-}=P_{\pm}\mathrm{diag}(-c_{\pm},0)P_{\pm}^{-1}
, and
|A_{\pm}|=P_{\pm}\mathrm{diag}(c_{\pm},c_{\pm})P_{\pm}^{-1}
to be the positive part,the negative part, and the absolute value of
A_{\pm}
, respectively.
The acoustic interface problem that we are considering here is subject to the following homogeneous inflow boundary conditions

A_{-}^{+}\mathbf{u}(a,t)=A_{+}^{-}\mathbf{u}(b,t)=0,\qquad t\geq 0,

(42c)

initial conditions

\mathbf{u}(x,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(x),x\in I,

(42d)

and interface condition

\mathbf{u}(\alpha^{-},t)=\mathbf{u}(\alpha^{+},t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(42e)

In the remainder of this section, let
S_{\pm}=\mathrm{diag}(\rho_{\pm}^{-1}c_{\pm}^{-2},\rho_{\pm})
and
S(x)=S_{\pm}
if
x\in I^{\pm}
, then

S_{\pm}A_{\pm}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=\tilde{A}.

(43)

Now, we can multiply (
42a
) by
S
and write the acoustic interface problem as

S(x)\mathbf{u}_{t}(x,t)+\tilde{A}\mathbf{u}_{x}(x,t)=0,\qquad x\in I\backslash\{\alpha\},\qquad t>0.

(44)

Lombard and Piraux
[
30
]
, and Moon
[
32

have shown that by successively differentiating
\left\llbracket\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)\right\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0
, where
\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket
is the jump, we obtain

\left\llbracket A(\cdot)^{k}\mathbf{u}(\cdot,t)\right\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0\Longleftrightarrow\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\mathbf{u}(\alpha^{+},t)=R_{k}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\mathbf{u}(\alpha^{-},t),\quad R_{k}=A_{+}^{-k}A_{-}^{k},\ k=0,1,\dots,m.

Since
R_{k}
is diagonal (see part (a) of

\mathbf{u}^{(k)}(\alpha^{+},t)=R_{k}\mathbf{u}^{(k)}(\alpha^{-},t)
is equivalent to

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}p(\alpha^{+},t)=r^{p}_{k}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}p(\alpha^{-},t),\qquad\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}u(\alpha^{+},t)=r^{u}_{k}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}u(\alpha^{-},t),

(45)

where
r^{p}_{k}
and
r^{u}_{k}
are defined in (
41
). These decoupled interface conditions make the results obtained previously about the approximation capabilities of the LIFE space directly applicable to vector functions in the product spaces

\mathbb{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(I_{k_{0}})=\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r^{p}}(I_{k_{0}})\times\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\alpha,r^{u}}(I_{k_{0}}),\quad\mathbb{V}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(I_{k_{0}})=\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r^{p}}(I_{k_{0}})\times\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\alpha,r^{u}}(I_{k_{0}}),\quad\mathbb{W}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=W^{m}_{\alpha,r^{p}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times W^{m}_{\alpha,r^{u}}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

where
\mathbf{r}=(r^{p},r^{u})
. Now, we define the following bilinear forms

M(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(S\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right)_{I_{k}}

(46a)

B(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{v}^{\prime},\tilde{A}\mathbf{w}\right)_{I_{k}}+\sum_{k=0}^{N}\left\llbracket\mathbf{v}\right\rrbracket_{x_{k}}^{T}S(x_{k})\hat{\mathbf{w}}(x_{k}),

where the numerical flux
\hat{\mathbf{w}}(x_{k})=A(x_{k})^{+}\mathbf{w}(x_{k}^{-})+A(x_{k})^{-}\mathbf{w}(x_{k}^{+})

,
\hat{\mathbf{w}}(x_{0})=A(x_{0})^{-}\mathbf{w}(x_{0}^{+})

\left\llbracket\mathbf{w}\right\rrbracket_{x_{N}}=\mathbf{w}(x_{N}^{-})
and
\left\llbracket\mathbf{w}\right\rrbracket_{x_{0}}=-\mathbf{w}(x_{0}^{+})
.

Now we define the immersed DG formulation as: Find
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in C^{1}\left([0,T],\mathbb{W}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\right)
such that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}_{h}(\cdot,0),\mathbf{v}_{h})_{I}=(\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{I},\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{W}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

(47a)

\displaystyle M(\mathbf{u}_{h,t}(\cdot,t),\mathbf{v}_{h})=B(\mathbf{u}_{h}(\cdot,t),\mathbf{v}_{h}),\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{W}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{r}}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

(47b)

Lemma 8

Let

(a)

For any integer

(b)

(d)

\displaystyle[left={\empheqlbrace}]A^{-}_{-}\check{\Pi}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},\mathbf{r}}\check{\mathbf{u}}(0)

Lemma 10

Let

\displaystyle G:\mathbb{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},\mathbf{r}}(\check{I})

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha}^{+})

\displaystyle=A_{+}\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)}(\check{\alpha}^{+})

\displaystyle=A_{+}A_{+}^{-k-1}A_{-}^{k+1}\mathbf{p}^{(k+1)}(\check{\alpha}^{-})

\displaystyle=A_{+}^{-k}A_{-}^{k}\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(k)}(\check{\alpha}^{-}).

Now, we show that

Theorem 9

\displaystyle\left(S\mathbf{z}_{t}(\cdot,t),\mathbf{g}(\cdot,t)\right)_{I}

The

\begin{cases}\mathscr{L}^{m}_{\alpha,r}u(x_{k_{0}-1})=u(x_{k_{0}-1}),\\

1,&x\in I_{k_{0}}^{+},\end{cases}

\check{u}=\mathcal{M}v
.

For simplicity, let

for a given

and note that the system (

m+1

Therefore, we only need to show that if
\check{u}\equiv 0

to prove that
\check{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}

Lemma 13
.

The reference immersed Lobatto projection
\check{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}
is well defined.

Proof.

Let
\check{u}\equiv 0
, we will show that
\tilde{u}=\check{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}\check{u}\equiv 0
. We have

\tilde{u}(0)=\tilde{u}(1)=0,\qquad\left(\tilde{u},v_{h}\right)_{\check{w},\check{I}}=0,\ \forall v\in\mathcal{V}^{m-2}_{\check{\alpha},\tau^{2}(r)}(\check{I}),

where
\check{w}=\mathcal{M}w
.
Using (
17
),
\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-2}_{\check{\alpha},\tau^{2}(r)}(\check{I})
, then

\displaystyle 0=\int_{0}^{1}w(x)\tilde{u}(x)\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}(x)\ dx

\displaystyle=r_{1}\int_{0}^{\check{\alpha}}\tilde{u}(x)\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}(x)\ dx+\int_{\check{\alpha}}^{1}\tilde{u}(x)\tilde{u}^{\prime\prime}(x)\ dx

\displaystyle=\tilde{u}(\check{\alpha})\left[r_{1}\tilde{u}^{\prime}(\check{\alpha}^{-})-\tilde{u}^{\prime}(\check{\alpha}^{+})\right]-\int_{0}^{1}w(x)[\tilde{u}^{\prime}(x)]^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}w(x)[\tilde{u}^{\prime}(x)]^{2}\ dx,

which implies that
\tilde{u}
is zero since

.
∎

Next, we will show that

Lemma 14
.

There is a constant
C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-},m)>0
independent of
\check{\alpha}
such that the following estimate holds for
every
\check{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{m+1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})

\left\lVert\tilde{u}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-},m)\left\lVert\check{u}\right\rVert_{1,\check{I}}.

Proof.

We write
\tilde{u}
as
\tilde{u}=q_{1}+q_{2}
, where
q_{1}\in\mathcal{V}^{1}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
such that

q_{1}(0)=\check{u}(0),\quad q_{1}(1)=\check{u}(1),

and
q_{2}=\check{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{u}-q_{1})\in\mathcal{V}^{m}_{\check{\alpha},r}(\check{I})
. The construction of

is straightforward (see
[
21
]
) and we have
\left\lVert q_{1}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-})\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{1,\check{I}}

satisfies

q_{2}(0)=q_{2}(1)=0,\qquad\left(q_{2},v_{h}\right)_{\check{w},\check{I}}=\left(\check{u}-q_{1},v_{h}\right)_{\check{w},\check{I}},\ \forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{m-2}_{\check{\alpha},\tau^{2}(r)}(\check{I}),

where
\check{w}=\mathcal{M}w
.
Following the proof of
Lemma 13
, we can choose

and integrate by parts to get

-\left\lVert wq_{2}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}^{2}=\left(\check{u}-q_{1},q_{2}^{\prime\prime}\right)_{\check{w},\check{I}}.

\left\lVert q_{2}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}^{2}\leq C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-},m)\left\lVert\tilde{u}-q_{1}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\left\lVert q_{2}^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}.

\left\lVert q_{2}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-},m)\left\lVert\check{u}-q_{1}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}\leq C(\beta^{+},\beta^{-},m)\left\lVert\check{u}\right\rVert_{1,\check{I}}.

(88)

q_{2}(0)=q_{2}(1)=0
, we can apply Poincaré’s inequality to obtain
\left\lVert q_{2}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}<C\left\lVert q_{2}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{0,\check{I}}

∎

Theorem 6
to derive an error bound for the Lobatto projection

\mathscr{L}^{m}_{\alpha,r}u
in the following theorem which confirms the optimal approximation capability of the

This follows immediately from

7
Conclusion

In this manuscript we developed a framework for analyzing the approximation properties of one-dimensional IFE spaces using the scaling argument. We have applied this IFE scaling argument to establish the optimal convergence of IFE spaces constructed for solving the acoustic interface problem, the elliptic interface problem and the Euler-Bernoulli beam interface problem, respectively. We are currently working on extending these results to IFE spaces and methods for solving interface problems in two and three dimensions.

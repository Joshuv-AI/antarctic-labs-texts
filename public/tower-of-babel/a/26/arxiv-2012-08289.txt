Quartic L p L^{p} -convergence of cubic Riemannian splines
Hanne Hardering, Benedikt Wirth

Abstract
We prove quartic convergence of cubic spline interpolation for curves into Riemannian manifolds as the grid size of the interpolation grid tends to zero. In contrast to cubic spline interpolation in Euclidean space, where this result is classical, the interpolation operator is no longer linear. Still, concepts from the linear setting may be generalized to the Riemannian case, where we try to use intrinsic Riemannian formulations and avoid charts as much as possible.



1
Introduction

Let a smooth function
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be given as well as a grid on
[0,1]
of knots
t_{i}=ih
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
where
h=\frac{1}{N}
denotes the knot distance or grid size.
A standard approximation of
\gamma
is obtained by evaluating
\gamma
at the knots
and then computing the cubic spline interpolant
\gamma_{h}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
of the resulting data
(t_{i},\gamma(t_{i}))
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
for instance with Hermite boundary conditions
\dot{\gamma}_{h}(0)=\dot{\gamma}(0)
,
\dot{\gamma}_{h}(1)=\dot{\gamma}(1)
.
It is a classic result that this approximation is of quartic order,
\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{4}
,
proved for instance by de Boor
[
2
]
(analogous convergence results hold for spline interpolation of different order).

The well-known minimum curvature property of cubic splines states
that cubic spline interpolants uniquely minimize the accumulated squared acceleration
\int_{0}^{1}|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
among all functions interpolating
(t_{i},\gamma(t_{i}))
,
i=0,\ldots,N
,
which allows to generalize the notion of a cubic spline interpolant to functions or curves
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
with values in a smooth Riemannian manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Definition 1

(Riemannian cubic spline interpolation)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a continuous curve and set
t_{i}=ih
for
i=0,\ldots,N
and
h=\frac{1}{N}
.
A corresponding
cubic spline interpolation
of
\gamma
is defined as

\gamma_{h}\in{\mathrm{argmin}}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\middle|\,\gamma_{h}(t_{i})=\gamma(t_{i})\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,N\text{ and }\dot{\gamma}_{h}(t)=\dot{\gamma}(t)\text{ for }t=0,1\right\}.

Above,
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
denotes the intrinsic second derivative, the covariant derivative of
\dot{\gamma}_{h}
along the curve
\gamma_{h}
,
and
|\cdot|
represents the Riemannian norm on the tangent space bundle
T{\mathcal{M}}
.
A natural question is whether the approximation properties of cubic splines transfer from the setting of real-valued functions to the manifold-valued generalization.
Our main result is that they do.

Theorem 2

(Quartic convergence of Riemannian cubic spline interpolation)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be complete with bounded Riemann curvature and its derivatives.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be four times differentiable.
For
h
small enough (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
)

1.

the cubic spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
exists and is unique,

2.

and it satisfies

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{4},

where the constant
c>0
only depends on
{\mathcal{M}}
,
\gamma
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Above,
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Riemannian distance and
\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}
the
L^{p}
-norm of the function
t\mapsto d(\gamma_{h}(t),\gamma(t))
for
t\in[0,1]
.
Note that the conditions for existence and uniqueness can be a little relaxed, see
proposition

9
.
One could envision two strategies to arrive at this approximation result.
One strategy would be to reduce the problem to the real-valued case
by reformulating the Riemannian cubic spline interpolation as an interpolation problem in a linear space (using charts or a tangent space to the manifold)
which can be viewed as a perturbation of Euclidean or real-valued cubic spline interpolation.
The complementary strategy consists in the generalization of all ingredients to the Euclidean analysis to the Riemannian setting,
using a mostly coordinate-free intrinsic Riemannian formulation.
We will follow the latter strategy (but also discuss the former along the way).
In particular, the Euclidean proof in
[
2
]
makes use of the fact
that the second derivative of the cubic spline interpolant
\gamma_{h}
is the
L^{2}
-best approximation to the the second derivative of
\gamma
,
and for our proof in the nonlinear manifold setting we will quantify the deviation from a Riemannian generalization of that property.
This will result in an
L^{\infty}
-bound for the second derivative
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
, which is crucial for the convergence estimate.

For the sake of completeness, along the way we will deal with linear spline interpolation
since it is a widespread alternative for applications in Riemannian manifolds
and since its convergence properties follow without additional effort.

Definition 3

(Riemannian linear spline interpolation)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a continuous curve and set
t_{i}=ih
for
i=0,\ldots,N
and
h=\frac{1}{N}
.
A corresponding
linear spline interpolation
of
\gamma
is defined as

\gamma_{h}\in{\mathrm{argmin}}\left\{\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\middle|\,\gamma_{h}(t_{i})=\gamma(t_{i})\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,N\right\}.

Theorem 4

(Quadratic convergence of Riemannian linear spline interpolation)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be complete with bounded Riemann curvature and its derivatives.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be twice differentiable.
For
h
small enough (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
)

1.

the linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
exists and is unique,

2.

and it satisfies

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{2},

where the constant
c>0
only depends on
{\mathcal{M}}
,
\gamma
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Higher order spline interpolation of odd degree could in principle be tackled in a similar way as cubic spline interpolation, but of course requires more technical estimates.

In
section

2
we will summarize the required basic Riemannian notions and our notation
after which we prove the well-posedness of (linear and) cubic spline interpolation in
section

3
and derive the corresponding optimality conditions.
We then present two convergence proofs for the classical Euclidean setting in
section

4

before adapting these to the Riemannian setting in
section

5
.

2
Required Riemannian notions and preliminaries

Throughout we will assume
{\mathcal{M}}
to be a smooth, complete, connected, finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold with Riemannian metric
g

such that the Riemann curvature tensor and all its derivatives are bounded.
The tangent space at a point
p\in{\mathcal{M}}
will be denoted
T_{p}{\mathcal{M}}
, and for
v,w\in T_{p}{\mathcal{M}}
we will write

(v,w)=g_{p}(v,w)\qquad\text{as well as}\qquad|v|=\sqrt{(v,v)},

where the explicit dependence on the base point
p
is suppressed for better readability.
The induced Riemannian distance is denoted by
d:{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{M}}\to[0,\infty)
,
the Levi-Civita connection on
{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted by
\nabla
,
and the Riemann curvature tensor is denoted by
{\mathrm{R}}
.
For an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
we will denote the parameterization variable by
t

and its velocity by
\partial_{t}\gamma(t)=\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}{\mathcal{M}}

(time derivatives of functions into a vector space will also be denoted with powers of
\partial_{t}
or dots).
A lift of the curve
\gamma
is a mapping
v
from
[0,1]
into the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{M}}
such that
v(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}{\mathcal{M}}
for all
t\in[0,1]
;
the vector space of all liftings is abbreviated
\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
.
For a vector field
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
along the curve
\gamma
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we will write

D_{t}^{k}v=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{k}v,

where with a slight misuse of notation the covariant derivative of
v
along
\gamma
shall be

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}v(t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}(\pi_{\gamma}v(t+\epsilon)-v(t))

with
\pi_{\gamma}
being the parallel transport along
\gamma
from
\gamma(t+\epsilon)
to
\gamma(t)

(start and end point of the parallel transport along a curve will usually be clear from the context).
We will also write

D_{t}^{k}\gamma=\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{k-1}\dot{\gamma}

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
A family of curves
\gamma(s,\cdot):[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
will be parameterized by the variable
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
and analogously to before, for a family of vector fields
v(s,\cdot)
along the curves
\gamma(s,\cdot)
we introduce the notation

D_{s}v(s,t)=\nabla_{\partial_{s}\gamma}v(s,t)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}(\pi_{\gamma(\cdot,t)}v(s+\epsilon,t)-v(s,t)),

where
\pi_{\gamma(\cdot,t)}
is parallel transport along the curve
s\mapsto\gamma(s,t)
.
Below we collect a few classical Riemannian calculus rules to be used in the sequel.

Lemma 5

(Differentiation rules)
.

1.

Parallel transport along a curve commutes with differentiation along the curve in the following sense.
Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be differentiable and denote by
\pi_{\gamma}:T_{\gamma(t)}{\mathcal{M}}\to T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
the parallel transport along
\gamma
,
then for any differentiable
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
we have

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\pi_{\gamma}v=\pi_{\gamma}D_{t}v.

2.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a differentiable family of differentiable curves, then

D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma=D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma
.

3.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a differentiable family of differentiable curves and
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
differentiable, then

D_{s}D_{t}v=D_{t}D_{s}v+{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma,\partial_{t}\gamma)v
.

Proof.

1.

For all
w\in T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
we have

(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\pi_{\gamma}v,w)=\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\pi_{\gamma}v,w)=\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(v,\pi_{\gamma}^{-1}w)=(D_{t}v,\pi_{\gamma}^{-1}w)+(v,D_{t}\pi_{\gamma}^{-1}w)=(D_{t}v,\pi_{\gamma}^{-1}w)=(\pi_{\gamma}D_{t}v,w).

2.

Since the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free, we have
D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma=D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma+[\partial_{s}\gamma,\partial_{t}\gamma]
for the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
of vector fields.
Now for any smooth function
f:{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathbb{R}
we have

[\partial_{s}\gamma,\partial_{t}\gamma]f=\partial_{s}\gamma(\partial_{t}\gamma(f))-\partial_{t}\gamma(\partial_{s}\gamma(f))=\partial_{s}\partial_{t}f\circ\gamma-\partial_{t}\partial_{s}f\circ\gamma=0.

3.

We have

{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma,\partial_{t}\gamma)v=D_{s}D_{t}v-D_{t}D_{s}v-\nabla_{[\partial_{s}\gamma,\partial_{t}\gamma]}v,

where the last term is zero as shown in the previous point.
∎

For a Lebesgue measurable function
v:I\to T{\mathcal{M}}
on some measurable domain
I
we denote its
L^{p}
-norm for
p\in[1,\infty]
by

\|v\|_{L^{p}}=\|\,|v|\,\|_{L^{p}(I)}.

If the domain is not clear from the context, we will write
\|v\|_{L^{p}(I)}
instead.
Similarly, for a function
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
we introduce its Hölder norm of exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]
by

\|v\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}=\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{r,t\in[0,1]}\frac{|\pi_{\gamma}v(t)-\pi_{\gamma}v(r)|}{|t-r|^{\alpha}},

where
\pi_{\gamma}
is parallel transport to
\gamma(0)
along
\gamma
.

Finally, the Riemannian exponential with base point
p\in{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted by
\exp_{p}:T_{p}{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
.
Letting
\rho
be the injectivity radius of
{\mathcal{M}}
, the inverse of
\exp_{p}
can be defined on the geodesic ball
B_{\rho}(p)\subset{\mathcal{M}}
of radius
\rho

and is called the Riemannian logarithm
\log_{p}:B_{\rho}(p)\to T_{p}{\mathcal{M}}
.
We close by providing some bounds on the Riemannian logarithm and exponential.

Lemma 6

(Bounds on Riemannian logarithm and exponential)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a
C^{k+1}
-manifold,
k\geq 2
, with injectivity radius
\rho
, sectional curvature bounded from above by
K
, and bounds
\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
on
{\mathrm{R}}
and
\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
on
\nabla{\mathrm{R}}
. Then the bivariate mapping
\log(p,q)=\log_{p}q
is in
C^{k}
for
d(p,q)<\rho
. If
K>0
, assume additionally
d(p,q)\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{K}}
. The operator norms of the first derivatives of
\log
satisfy

\displaystyle\|\partial_{1}\log_{p}q+\mathrm{Id}_{T_{p}M}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{2}d^{2}(p,q),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{2}\log_{p}q-\pi_{q\to p}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{2}d^{2}(p,q),

where
\pi_{q\to p}
denotes parallel transport along the geodesic from
q
to
p
. The covariant second order derivatives, denoted by
D^{2}
, satisfy

\displaystyle\|D^{2}\log_{p}q\|\leq C(\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty},\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty})d(p,q)

for a constant
C(\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty},\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty})
depending only on
\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
and
\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
.
Similarly, the bivariate mapping
\exp(p,v)=\exp_{p}v
is in
C^{k}
, and for
|v|
small enough, depending only on
\rho
,
K
, and
\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
,
\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty}
, it satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial_{2}\exp_{p}v-\pi_{p\to q}\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty},\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty})|v|^{2},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{p}v\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty},\|\nabla{\mathrm{R}}\|_{\infty})|v|.

The proof for the derivatives of the logarithm follows from Jacobi field estimates and can be found in
[
5
, Prop. A.1-A.2]
(the estimates for the first order derivatives can also be found in
[
7
]
).
The estimates for the derivatives of the exponential are then straightforward applications of the inverse function theorem.

3
Well-posedness and Euler–Lagrange equations of Riemannian spline interpolations

All throughout the article,
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
will be a given curve,

k
times differentiable (
k=2
for linear and
k=4
for cubic spline interpolation)
in the sense that
\gamma
is continuous and
D_{t}^{l}\gamma
is well-defined and bounded for
l=1,\ldots,k
.
Furthermore,
\gamma_{h}:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
will denote the spline interpolation according to
definition

1
or
definition

3
at points
t_{i}=ih
,
i=0,\ldots,N
, where
h=\frac{1}{N}
.

Remark 7

(Boundary conditions for Riemannian cubic spline interpolation)
.

In
definition

1
we chose to impose the so-called Hermite boundary conditions
\dot{\gamma}_{h}(t)=\dot{\gamma}(t)\text{ for }t=0,1
.
An alternative would be to solely require
\gamma_{h}=\gamma
at all interpolation points.
This is known to result in so-called natural boundary conditions, essentially a vanishing acceleration of
\gamma_{h}
at
t=0
and
t=1
.
Unfortunately, though, existence of cubic spline interpolations with natural boundary conditions cannot be guaranteed due to lacking control of the curve velocity
(a simple example is provided in
[
6
]
).

We begin with straightforward a priori bounds that follow from the coercivity of the spline interpolation energy,
which is nothing else but
\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in the case of linear splines and
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in case of cubic splines.
Boundedness of higher derivatives for the cubic spline interpolation will be proven later.

Proposition 8

(A priori bounds)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be an absolutely continuous curve.

1.

If
\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded, then
\gamma
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
, and for any
r\in[0,1]
we have

\|d(\gamma,\gamma(r))\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}\leq 2\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}.

2.

If
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded, then
\gamma
is even Hölder continuously differentiable with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle d(\gamma(t),\gamma(r))

\displaystyle\leq|t-r|\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\text{for all }r,t\in[0,1].

Proof.

1.

For any
t,r\in[0,1]
, by Hölder’s inequality we have

d(\gamma(t),\gamma(r))\leq\int_{r}^{t}|\dot{\gamma}(\tau)|\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\||\dot{\gamma}|\|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{|t-r|}=\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{|t-r|}

so that
\gamma
is indeed Hölder continuous with exponent
2
.
Furthermore, this implies

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}d(\gamma(t),\gamma(r))+\sup_{t,\tilde{t}\in[0,1]}\frac{|d(\gamma(t),\gamma(r))-d(\gamma(\tilde{t}),\gamma(r))|}{\sqrt{|t-\tilde{t}|}}\leq\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}+\sup_{t,\tilde{t}\in[0,1]}\frac{d(\gamma(t),\gamma(\tilde{t}))}{\sqrt{|t-\tilde{t}|}}\leq 2\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}},

from which the estimate follows.

2.

Letting
\pi_{\gamma}
denote parallel transport along
\gamma
to
T_{\gamma(r)}{\mathcal{M}}
we can analogously estimate

|\pi_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}(t)-\dot{\gamma}(r)|=\left|\int_{r}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\pi_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|=\left|\int_{r}^{t}\pi_{\gamma}D_{\tau}\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq\sqrt{|t-r|}\|\pi_{\gamma}D_{t}\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{|t-r|}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}

by
lemma

5
(
1
) and Hölder’s inequality. Thus

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|+\sup_{t,r\in[0,1]}\frac{|\pi_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}(t)-\dot{\gamma}(r)|}{\sqrt{|t-r|}}\leq|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+\sqrt{t}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}+\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}},

which is the first norm bound.
The second bound trivially follows from
d(\gamma(t),\gamma(r))\leq\int_{r}^{t}|\dot{\gamma}(\tau)|\,\mathrm{d}\tau
.
∎

Obviously, an immediate consequence is that the linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
of
\gamma
, if it exists, is Hölder continuous with

d(\gamma_{h}(t),\gamma_{h}(t_{i}))\leq 2\sqrt{|t-t_{i}|}\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1],i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}

(1)

and that the cubic spline interpolation, if it exists, is Hölder continuously differentiable with

\displaystyle\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}},

(2)

\displaystyle d(\gamma_{h}(t),\gamma(t_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq|t-t_{i}|\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1],i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

(3)

We proceed by proving the well-posedness of spline interpolation,
theorem

4
(
1
) and
theorem

2
(
1
).

Proposition 9

(Well-posedness of Riemannian spline interpolation)
.

Linear and cubic spline interpolations
\gamma_{h}
of
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
exist.
Furthermore, they are unique if
\gamma
is continuous (for linear spline interpolation) or has finite
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}
(for cubic spline interpolation)
and
h
is small enough (depending on
\gamma
and
{\mathcal{M}}
).

Proof.

Obviously, a linear spline interpolation is obtained by minimizing for each
i=1,\ldots,N
the energy

\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

among all curve segments
\gamma_{h}:[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\to{\mathcal{M}}
that satisfy
\gamma_{h}(t_{i-1})=\gamma(t_{i-1})
and
\gamma_{h}(t_{i})=\gamma(t_{i})
.
Those curve segments are known to be exactly the constant speed-parameterized geodesics connecting
\gamma(t_{i-1})
and
\gamma(t_{i})
,
which always exist on a complete Riemannian manifold and which are unique if the end points are close enough to each other (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
).
By uniform continuity of
\gamma
the latter condition will be fulfilled for all
i=1,\ldots,N
as soon as
h
is small enough.

As for the well-posedness of the cubic spline interpolation,
consider a minimizing sequence
\gamma_{h}^{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, of curves satisfying the interpolation conditions
such that the cubic spline energy
E[\gamma_{h}^{n}]=\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
converges monotonically to the infimum,

E[\gamma_{h}^{n}]\to\inf\{E[\gamma_{h}]\,|\,\gamma_{h}(t_{i})=\gamma(t_{i})\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,N\text{ and }\dot{\gamma}_{h}(t)=\dot{\gamma}(t)\text{ for }t=0,1\}
.
Letting
\pi_{\tilde{\gamma}}
denote parallel transport to
T_{\tilde{\gamma}(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
along a curve
\tilde{\gamma}
, we have

\|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=E[\gamma_{h}^{n}]

so that the functions
\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}:[0,1]\to T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
have uniformly bounded
H^{1}
-seminorm.
Together with
\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}(0)=\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}(0)=\dot{\gamma}(0)
it follows by Poincaré’s inequality that the functions even have uniformly bounded
H^{1}
-norm
so that a subsequence converges weakly in
H^{1}
(and thus strongly in
C^{0}([0,1])
) to some function
\beta:[0,1]\to T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
.
Now define
\gamma_{h}:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
as the solution to the ordinary differential equation

\dot{\gamma}_{h}(t)=\pi_{\gamma_{h}}^{-1}\beta(t)

with initial value
\gamma_{h}(0)=\gamma(0)
, where we write
\pi_{\gamma_{h}}^{-1}\beta(t)
for the parallel transport of
\beta(t)
to
\gamma_{h}(t)
along
\gamma_{h}
.
The solution
\gamma_{h}:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
exists and is unique by the theorem of Picard and Lindelöf, and it satisfies the interpolation conditions by Gronwall’s lemma.
Indeed, let us express all quantities in local coordinates
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and indicate this by a hat.
Further, let
\hat{\Pi}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denote the matrix representation in local coordinates of
\pi_{\gamma_{h}}^{-1}
.
Then
\hat{\gamma}_{h}
solves the initial value problem

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\hat{\gamma}_{h}\choose\hat{\Pi}}(t)=F(t,\hat{\gamma}_{h}(t),\hat{\Pi}(t)),\;{\hat{\gamma}_{h}\choose\hat{\Pi}}(0)={\hat{\gamma}_{h}(0)\choose\mathrm{Id}}\\
\quad\text{for }F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\;F(t,\hat{\gamma}_{h},\hat{\Pi})={\hat{\Pi}\hat{\beta}(t)\choose-\Gamma_{\hat{\gamma}_{h}}(\hat{\Pi}\hat{\beta}(t),\hat{\Pi})},

where
\Gamma_{\hat{\gamma}_{h}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the Christoffel operator at coordinates
\hat{\gamma}_{h}

and the second entry of
F(t,\hat{\gamma}_{h},\hat{\Pi})
refers to the matrix defined by
\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\xi\mapsto\Gamma_{\hat{\gamma}_{h}}(\hat{\Pi}\hat{\beta}(t),\hat{\Pi}\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The function
F
is obviously Lipschitz so that the Picard–Lindelöf theorem can be applied
(it is straightforward to show that
\hat{\gamma}_{h}
and
\hat{\Pi}
stay bounded for
t\in[0,1]
).
Since
\hat{\gamma}_{h}^{n}
satisfies the same initial value problem, only with
\hat{\beta}
replaced by
\widehat{\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}}
,
the curves
\hat{\gamma}_{h}^{n}
converge uniformly on
[0,1]
to
\hat{\gamma}_{h}
by Gronwall’s lemma, as do the corresponding
\hat{\alpha}
.
Consequently,
\hat{\gamma}_{h}
satisfies the interpolation conditions.
Finally note that

E[\gamma_{h}]=\|\pi_{\gamma_{h}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\pi_{\gamma_{h}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\dot{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\pi_{\gamma_{h}^{n}}\dot{\gamma}_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}E[\gamma_{h}^{n}]

due to the sequential weak lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm so that
\gamma_{h}
minimizes
E
under the interpolation constraints.

It remains to prove uniqueness of
\gamma_{h}
.
To this end we will show local convexity of the problem for
h
small enough.
Indeed, for
h
small enough the interpolation problem can equivalently be formulated in terms of functions

v_{i}:[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\to T_{\gamma_{i}}{\mathcal{M}},\quad v_{i}(t)=\log_{\gamma_{i}}\gamma_{h}(t),\;i=1,\ldots,N,

where we abbreviated
\gamma_{i}=\gamma(t_{i-1})

(equivalently,
\gamma_{h}(t)=\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}(t)
for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
).
This is possible since
E[\gamma_{h}]\leq E[\gamma]
and thus by
proposition

8
(
2
),

d(\gamma_{h}(t),\gamma_{i})\leq h\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq h\left(|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}\right)\quad\text{for }t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]

so that for
h
small enough the Riemannian logarithm in the definition of
v_{i}
is uniquely defined and smooth.
Furthermore, again by
proposition

8
(
2
), the
v_{i}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}]\dot{v}_{i}\|_{C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\quad\text{for }C=|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch,

for
i=1,\ldots,N
, which by
lemma

6
implies
\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2C
for
h
small enough.
Now introduce

J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\left|[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}(t)]\ddot{v}_{i}(t)+[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}(t)](\dot{v}_{i}(t),\dot{v}_{i}(t))\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

which equals
E[\gamma_{h}]
.
Due to the above bounds on
\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}
we can apply
lemma

6

to obtain
E[\gamma]\geq E[\gamma_{h}]=J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}]\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-1
for
h
small enough.
Thus, the cubic spline interpolation problem in terms of the
v_{i}
can thus be written as

\displaystyle\!\min\left\{J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}]\,\middle|\,(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}\right\}\quad\text{with }

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}=\big\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1}))\times\ldots\times L^{\infty}((t_{N-1},t_{N}))\,\big|\,\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch,\,\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2C,\,\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2E[\gamma]+2,

\displaystyle\hskip 245.4159ptv_{i}(t_{i-1})=0,\,v_{i}(t_{i})=\log_{\gamma_{i}}\gamma(t_{i})\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,N

\displaystyle\hskip 223.88818pt\text{and }\dot{v}_{1}(0)=\dot{\gamma}(0),\,[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma(t_{N-1})}v_{N}(1)]\dot{v}_{N}(1)=\dot{\gamma}(1)\big\}.

It is obvious that
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is convex, so it remains to show convexity of
J
on
\mathcal{A}_{h}
.
Abbreviating
\gamma_{v}(t)=\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}(t)
for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
,
the Gâteaux derivatives of
J
in a direction
\phi\equiv(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N})
with
\varphi_{i}(t_{i-1})=\varphi(t_{i})=0
and
\dot{\varphi}_{1}(0)=\dot{\varphi}_{N}(1)=0
are

\displaystyle\partial J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}](\phi)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{v},\underbrace{[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}]\ddot{\varphi}_{i}+[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\ddot{v}_{i},\varphi_{i})+[\partial_{2}^{3}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{v}_{i},\dot{v}_{i},\varphi_{i})+2[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{v}_{i},\dot{\varphi}_{i})}_{U_{i}})\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\partial^{2}J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}](\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(U_{i},U_{i})+(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{v},\underbrace{2[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\varphi_{i},\ddot{\varphi}_{i})+[\partial_{2}^{3}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\varphi_{i},\varphi_{i},\ddot{v}_{i})+4[\partial_{2}^{3}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{\varphi}_{i},\dot{v}_{i},\varphi_{i})}_{V_{i}^{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 236.80481pt+\underbrace{[\partial_{2}^{4}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\varphi_{i},\varphi_{i},\dot{v}_{i},\dot{v}_{i})+2[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{\varphi}_{i},\dot{\varphi}_{i})}_{V_{i}^{2}})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Now by
lemma

6
,

\displaystyle\|U_{i}-\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)+K\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|V_{i}^{1}+V_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+K\left(\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

for a constant
K>0
depending only on
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Now recall that for
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\in\mathcal{A}_{h}
we have

\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch,\quad\|\dot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2C,\quad\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2.

Furthermore, due to the homogeneous boundary conditions on each
\varphi_{i}
there exists
c>0
independent of
h
or
\varphi_{i}
such that

\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{3/2}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{1/2}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\dot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

(as follows from straightforward scaling arguments).
Summarizing, there is a constant
\kappa>0
depending only on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
such that

\|U_{i}-\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\kappa h\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|V_{i}^{1}+V_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\kappa h^{3/2}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore, for
h
small enough we have

\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{v}\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}=\|[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}]\ddot{v}_{i}+[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{v}_{i},\dot{v}_{i})\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}\\
\leq 2\|[\partial_{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}]\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}+2\|[\partial_{2}^{2}\exp_{\gamma_{i}}v_{i}](\dot{v}_{i},\dot{v}_{i})\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}\leq 4\|\ddot{v}_{i}\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}+1\leq 8\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+9

by
lemma

6
and the previous estimates on
v_{i}
so that

\partial^{2}J[v_{1},\ldots,v_{N}](\phi,\phi)\geq 2\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\kappa^{2}h^{2}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{v}\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}\kappa h^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\\
\geq(1-2\kappa^{2}h^{2}-\kappa h^{\frac{3}{2}}(8\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}+9))\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\ddot{\varphi}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}>0

for
h
small enough (depending just on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).
Therefore,
J
is strictly convex on
\mathcal{A}_{h}
.
∎

We close the section by deriving the Euler–Lagrange equations satisfied by the linear and cubic spline interpolations.

Proposition 10

(Optimality conditions)
.

1.

A linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies the optimality conditions

\displaystyle 0=D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\text{ on }(t_{k-1},t_{k}),\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle\gamma_{h}=\gamma\text{ at }t_{0},\ldots,t_{N}.

2.

A cubic spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies the optimality conditions

\displaystyle 0=D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\dot{\gamma}_{h})\dot{\gamma}_{h}\text{ on }(t_{k-1},t_{k}),\,k=1,\ldots,N,

\displaystyle D_{t}\gamma_{h}\text{ and }D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\text{ exist and }\gamma_{h}=\gamma\text{ at }t_{0},\ldots,t_{N},

\displaystyle D_{t}\gamma_{h}=D_{t}\gamma\text{ at }t_{0}\text{ and }t_{N}.

Proof.

1.

Extend
\gamma_{h}
to a family
\gamma_{h}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
of competing curves with
\gamma(0,\cdot)
being the minimizer.
Then at
s=0
we necessarily have

0=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{t}\gamma_{h},D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{t}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[(\partial_{t}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\right]_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}-\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

where we used
lemma

5
, performed integration by parts using
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(v,w)=(D_{t}v,w)+(v,D_{t}w)

and where we exploited
\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}(t_{k})=0
due to the admissibility of the curve family
\gamma_{h}
.
Since
\gamma_{h}
was extended arbitrarily, the result now follows from the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations.

2.

Extend
\gamma_{h}
to a family
\gamma_{h}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
of competing curves with
\gamma(0,\cdot)
being the minimizer.
Then at
s=0
we necessarily have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{0}^{1}|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{s}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{t}D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\right]_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})-(D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h},D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\right]_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(\partial_{s}\gamma_{h},{\mathrm{R}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})-(D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\right]_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}-\left[(D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\right]_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}((D_{t}^{-})^{2}\gamma_{h}(t_{k})-(D_{t}^{+})^{2}\gamma_{h}(t_{k}),D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}(t_{k}))+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h},\partial_{s}\gamma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
D_{t}^{+}
and
D_{t}^{-}
denote the left and right derivative.
Above, we again repeatedly exploited
lemma

5
and performed integration by parts.
Furthermore we exploited
\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}(t_{k})=0
and
D_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}(t)=D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}(t)=0
at
t=t_{0},t_{N}
due to the admissibility of the curve family
\gamma_{h}
.
Since
\gamma_{h}
was extended arbitrarily, the result now follows from the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations.
∎

4
Two short convergence proofs in the Euclidean case

Linear and cubic spline interpolation of curves in Euclidean space converge quadratically and quartically, repectively.
As our aim is to transfer this result to the Riemannian setting, we briefly provide the corresponding Euclidean proof here.
We first give a simple proof exploiting the piecewise polynomial structure of splines,
in particular that the second derivative of the cubic spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
is the
L^{2}
-best piecewise linear approximation of
\ddot{\gamma}

(in the remainder of the article we will try to mimick this idea).
Afterwards we give a second, more direct but less elegant proof for cubic spline interpolation
which essentially just checks the stability of interpolation as well as its consistency (by comparing the Taylor expansions of spline interpolant and original curve).
In principle, an analogous approach should also be possible on a manifold,
but the corresponding stability and Taylor estimates would best be obtained in a chart and promise to be quite cumbersome.
Below we will denote the Sobolev space of integrable functions with essentially bounded derivatives up to fourth order by
W^{4,\infty}
.

Theorem 11

(Spline convergence)
.

Let
\gamma\in W^{4,\infty}((0,1))
. Its linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies

\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{2}

for some
c>0
depending only on
\gamma
and
p
.
The corresponding cubic spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies

\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{4}

for some
c>0
depending only on
\gamma
and
p
.

Proof.

For a function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with zeros at
t_{0},\ldots,t_{N}
one has
\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\ddot{f}\|_{L^{p}}
for some
C>0
depending on
p
.
Picking
f=\gamma_{h}-\gamma
yields for the linear spline interpolation (which satisfies
(\ddot{\gamma}_{h}=0
)

\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}.

For the cubic spline interpolation one obtains

\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}.

Now it turns out that
\ddot{\gamma}_{h}
is the piecewise linear
L^{2}
-best approximation of
\ddot{\gamma}
.
Indeed, for any piecewise linear
w:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
one obtains the Galerkin orthogonality

\int_{0}^{1}(w,\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma})\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[(w,\dot{\gamma}_{h}-\dot{\gamma})\right]_{t=t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}-\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(\dot{w},\dot{\gamma}_{h}-\dot{\gamma})\,\mathrm{d}t=\left[(w,\dot{\gamma}_{h}-\dot{\gamma})-(\dot{w},\gamma_{h}-\gamma)\right]_{t=t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}=0

from two integrations by parts, exploiting that
\gamma_{h}-\gamma=0
at the interpolation points, that
\dot{\gamma}_{h}
exists by
proposition

10
, and that
\dot{\gamma}_{h}-\dot{\gamma}=0
at
t=0
and
t=1
.
This immediately implies (e. g. via Céa’s lemma) that
\ddot{\gamma}_{h}
is the
L^{2}
-projection or -best approximation of
\ddot{\gamma}
.
Thus, by
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
(or by the
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of the
L^{2}
-projection onto splines
[
3
]
and the resulting best approximation argument as in
[
1
, Thm. 3.3.7]
) we have

\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\gamma\|_{W^{4,\infty}}

for some
C>0
.
∎

The alternative, more direct proof for cubic spline interpolation just uses stability and fourth order consistency of cubic spline interpolation via Taylor expansion.

Proof.

The piecewise cubic spline interpolation can be written as a linear combination of shifted and scaled versions of the cubic B-spline basis function

B(t)=\begin{cases}(3|t|^{3}-6t^{2}+4)/6&\text{if }|t|\leq 1,\\
(2-|t|)^{3}/6&\text{if }|t|\in(1,2],\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Writing
\gamma_{h}
as a linear combination

\gamma_{h}(t)=\sum_{i=-1}^{N+1}x_{i}B(\tfrac{t}{h}-i)

of these basis functions, the interpolation constraints translate into the linear system

Ax=b\quad\text{with }A=\frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&1\\
1&4&1\\
&1&4&1\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&1&4&1\\
&&&&1&\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix},\,x=\begin{pmatrix}x_{-1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{N+1}\end{pmatrix},\,b=\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{\gamma(t_{0})}{4}-\tfrac{\dot{\gamma}(0)h}{12}\\
\gamma(t_{0})\\
\vdots\\
\gamma(t_{N})\\
\tfrac{\gamma(t_{N})}{4}+\tfrac{\dot{\gamma}(1)h}{12}\end{pmatrix}.

Now for a point
t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
we abbreviate
a_{i}=B(\tfrac{t}{h}-i)
(thus all
a_{i}
are zero except for
a_{i-1},\ldots,a_{i+2}
) so that

\gamma_{h}(t)-\gamma(t)=a^{T}x-\gamma(t)=a^{T}A^{-1}b-\gamma(t)\quad\text{with }a=(a_{-1},\ldots,a_{N+1})^{T}.

Now define
p(r)=\gamma(t)+\dot{\gamma}(t)(r-t)+\frac{1}{2}\ddot{\gamma}(t)(r-t)^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\dddot{\gamma}(t)(r-t)^{3}
to be the third order Taylor expansion of
\gamma
and notice

b=b_{1}+b_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}p(t_{0})/4-\dot{p}(0)h/12\\
p(t_{0})\\
\vdots\\
p(t_{N})\\
p(t_{N})/4+\dot{p}(1)h/12\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}O(|0-t|^{4})\\
O(|t_{0}-t|^{4})\\
\vdots\\
O(|t_{N}-t|^{4})\\
O(|1-t|^{4})\end{pmatrix}.

Since
a^{T}A^{-1}b_{1}=p(t)=\gamma(t)
, we arrive at

|\gamma_{h}(t)-\gamma(t)|=|a^{T}A^{-1}b_{2}|=\left|\sum_{j=i-1}^{i+2}a_{j}A^{-1}_{j,:}b_{2}\right|\leq\sum_{j=i-1}^{i+2}|A^{-1}_{j,:}b_{2}|,

where
A^{-1}_{j,:}=(q_{-1},q_{0},\ldots,q_{N+1})
denotes the
j
th row of
A^{-1}
and thus necessarily satisfies

\displaystyle q_{-1}=-2q_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{k}=-(q_{k-2}+4q_{k-1}),\,k=1,\ldots,j,

\displaystyle q_{N+1}=-2q_{N}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{k}=-(q_{k+2}+4q_{k+1}),\,k=N-1,\ldots,j.

By induction this immediately implies

q_{k}q_{k-1}<0,\,k=0,\ldots,N+1,\qquad|q_{k}|\begin{cases}\geq|q_{k-1}|,\,k=1,\ldots,j,\\
\leq|q_{k-1}|,\,k=j+1,\ldots,N,\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }|q_{k}|\leq 3^{-|k-j|}|q_{j}|,\,k=0,\ldots,N.

Furthermore, the equation
1=q_{j-1}+4q_{j}+q_{j+1}
together with the fact that
q_{j-1},q_{j+1}
have a different sign than
q_{j}
and are at least by the factor
3
smaller in absolute value implies

|q_{j}|\leq 1\quad\text{and thus}\quad|q_{k}|\leq 3^{-|k-j|},\,k=0,\ldots,N.

Summarizing, we obtain

|\gamma_{h}(t)-\gamma(t)|\leq\sum_{j=i-1}^{i+2}|A^{-1}_{j,:}b_{2}|\leq\sum_{j=i-1}^{i+2}\left|\sum_{k=-1}^{N+1}3^{-|k-j|}O(|t_{k}-t|^{4})\right|\leq 4h^{4}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}3^{-|k-j|}(|k-j|+1)^{4}\leq Ch^{4}

for a constant depending only on the maximum fourth derivative of
\gamma
.
∎

Corollary 12

(Perturbed spline)
.

Let
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be twice differentiable with
g(t_{i})=\dot{g}(0)=\dot{g}(1)=0
for
i=0,\ldots,N

and
g\in W^{4,\infty}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))
with
\|\partial_{t}^{4}g\|_{L^{\infty}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}\leq C
for
i=1,\ldots,N
.
Then
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{4}
with a constant
c>0
depending only on
C
.

Proof.

Define
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
to be the curve in
W^{4,\infty}((0,1))
with
\gamma(0)=\dot{\gamma}(0)=\gamma(1)=\dot{\gamma}(1)=0

whose fourth weak derivative is
f\in L^{\infty}((0,1))
with
f=\partial_{t}^{4}g
in each
(t_{i-1},t_{i})
,
that is, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{q}f(p)\,\mathrm{d}p\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\gamma}(t)+(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(1)-3\tilde{\gamma}(1))t^{2}+(2\tilde{\gamma}(1)-\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(1))t^{3}.

Letting now
\gamma_{h}
be the cubic spline interpolation of
\gamma
, we obviously have
\gamma-\gamma_{h}=g

and thus
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{4}
, where the constant only depends on the maximum fourth derivative of
\gamma
(which is bounded by
C
).
∎

5
The approximation properties of Riemannian cubic splines

In this section we transfer the proof of
theorem

11
for the Euclidean case to the Riemannian setting.
Essentially, we will proceed in three steps:
we will first derive a generalization of the estimate
\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}
,
then we will introduce a linear interpolation operator
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
of vector fields
as a preparation for estimating
\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}
(which we think of as some kind of best approximation error) by
\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}
,
and finally we will derive boundedness of
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}
using the
L^{2}
-best approximation idea from
theorem

11

and bootstrap this
L^{2}
-best approximation argument with the help of the bound on
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}

to obtain a generalization of the Euclidean estimate
\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\gamma\|_{W^{4,\infty}}
.

5.1
Quadratic convergence rate exploiting local interpolation

Just like in the proof of
theorem

11
for the Euclidean case we first derive a quadratic dependence of the spline interpolation error on
h
.

Lemma 13

(Approximation lemma)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a curve and
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
a vector field along the curve with
v(t_{k})=v(t_{k+1})=\ldots=v(t_{k+l})=0
,
then for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
I=(t_{k},t_{k+l})
we have

\|v\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq ch^{l+1}\|D_{t}^{l+1}v\|_{L^{p}(I)}

for a constant
c
depending only on
l
and
p
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{\gamma}:T_{\gamma(t)}{\mathcal{M}}\to T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
denote the parallel transport along the curve
\gamma
.
Then
\pi_{\gamma}v:[0,1]\to T_{\gamma(0)}{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
\pi_{\gamma}v(t_{k})=\ldots=\pi_{\gamma}v(t_{k+l})=0

so that the standard approximation result for curves in Euclidean space
[
1
, Thm. 3.1.5]
or a straightforward scaling argument can be applied, giving

\|v\|_{L^{p}(I)}=\|\pi_{\gamma}v\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq ch^{l+1}\|\partial_{t}^{l+1}(\pi_{\gamma}v)\|_{L^{p}(I)}=ch^{l+1}\|D_{t}^{l+1}v\|_{L^{p}(I)}

by
lemma

5
(
1
).
∎

This immediately implies the generalization of the Euclidean estimate
\|\gamma_{h}-\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\|\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}}
.

Proposition 14

(Quadratic convergence)
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
the linear and cubic spline interpolation of an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq ch^{2}\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{p}(I)}

on each interval
I=(t_{i-1},t_{i})

for a constant
c>0
independent of
\gamma
,
\gamma_{h}
, or
h
, for
all
h
small enough (both depending only on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).

Proof.

Simply apply
lemma

13
with
l=1
to
v=\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma
and note
d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)=|\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma|_{\gamma_{h}}
as long as
h
is small enough for
v
to be well-defined (follows from
proposition

8
and continuity of
\gamma
).
∎

The full convergence result of
theorems

2
and
4
for linear and cubic spline interpolation will follow from estimating the right-hand side of the previous inequality.
To this end we expand

D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma=\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)+\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h})+\partial_{2}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})+\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}_{h})\\
+\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma})+\partial_{1}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\,(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma}_{h}).

Note that at this point,
proposition

14
can already be turned into a quadratic convergence result (which for linear splines already is the optimal rate),
which is briefly done in the remainder of this paragraph.
Indeed, with
lemma

6
and the boundedness of
\gamma
and
\gamma_{h}
from
proposition

8

or more specifically from (
1
)-(
3
) we can estimate

\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{p}(I)}\leq C\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{p}(I)}+\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{p}(I)}+\|(|\dot{\gamma}|+|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|)^{2}\|_{L^{p}(I))}\right)

for some
C>0
if
h
is small enough (depending on
\gamma
and
{\mathcal{M}}
), which leads to the following result.

Corollary 15

(Quadratic
L^{1}
- and
L^{2}
-convergence)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be twice differentiable, then its linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{1}}\leq ch^{2}

for some constant
c>0
and all
h
small enough (both depending only on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).
If
\gamma
is four times differentiable, then its cubic spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
satisfies

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch^{2}

for some constant
c>0
and all
h
small enough (again both depending only on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).

Proof.

By
proposition

14
, for the linear spline we have

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{1}}\leq ch^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{1}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}\leq ch^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}C\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}+\||\dot{\gamma}|+|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|\|_{L^{2}((t_{i-1},t_{i}))}^{2}\right)\\
=cCh^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\||\dot{\gamma}|+|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq cCh^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+2\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq cCh^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+4\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

where we exploited the optimality condition
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}=0
as well as
\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Similarly, for the cubic spline we have

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch^{2}\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{2}}\leq cCh^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}+\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(|\dot{\gamma}|+|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|)^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
\leq cCh^{2}\left(2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}+(\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{2}\right)\leq cCh^{2}\left(2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}+(\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\dot{\gamma}(0)|+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\right),

where we exploited
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}
as well as (
2
).
∎

To improve the previous convergence estimate for linear spline interpolation to the final estimate of
theorem

4
we require the following uniform bound on
\dot{\gamma}_{h}
,
which immediately follows from properties of unit speed parameterized geodesics.

Proposition 16

(Boundedness of first derivative)
.

The linear spline interpolation
\gamma_{h}
of a curve
\gamma
satisfies

\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

Recall that between
t_{i-1}
and
t_{i}
the curve
\gamma_{h}
is a shortest unit speed parameterized geodesic between
\gamma(t_{i-1})
and
\gamma(t_{i})
,
thus on
(t_{i-1},t_{i})
we have

|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|=d(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i}))/h\leq\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}|\dot{\gamma}|\,\mathrm{d}t/h\leq\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}.\qed

Together with
proposition

16
for linear spline interpolation and
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}+Ch
for cubic spline interpolation, derived later in
corollary

24
, which by a bootstrapping argument makes use of
corollary

15
,
one finally arrives at the following quadratic convergence estimate.

Corollary 17

(Quadratic
L^{\infty}
-convergence)
.

The linear and cubic spline interpolation satisfy

\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq ch^{2}

for some constant
c>0
depending only on
\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and
{\mathcal{M}}
, for
h
small enough depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Abbreviating
I_{i}=(t_{i-1},t_{i})
, by
proposition

14
and
proposition

16
or
corollary

24
we have

\displaystyle\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2}\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2}\max_{i=1,\ldots,N}C\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i})}+\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i})}+\||\dot{\gamma}|+|\dot{\gamma}_{h}|\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{i})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}h^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}+Ch+\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right),

for some
\tilde{C}>0
, where in the case of linear splines we exploited that
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}=0
.
∎

5.2
A linear interpolation operator of vector fields

As mentioned before, the guiding idea of our approach is to interpret
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
as an approximation to
D_{t}^{2}\gamma

which is (maybe up to a constant factor) as good as the
L^{2}
-best approximation or a piecewise linear interpolation.
To formalize this, below we introduce a piecewise linear interpolation operator of vector fields along curves
and analyse its well-posedness and approximation properties.

Definition 18

(Linear interpolation operator)
.

Let
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\mathcal{M}}
be a piecewise differentiable and globally continuous curve and
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
a continuous vector field along
\gamma
.
We define the piecewise linear interpolation
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v
of
v
as solution
w
to the linear boundary value problem

\displaystyle D_{t}^{2}w+{\mathrm{R}}(w,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}=0\text{ on }(t_{k-1},t_{k}),\,k=1,\ldots,N,

(4)

\displaystyle w(t_{k})=v(t_{k}),\,k=0,\ldots,N.

Lemma 19

(Well-posedness and boundedness of linear interpolation)
.

For
h
small enough depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
is well-defined, that is, the boundary value problem (
4
) has a unique solution.
Furthermore, for every continuous vector field
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{k=0,\ldots,N}|v(t_{k})|\leq 2\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

As the boundary value problem (
4
) is a linear system of second order ordinary differential equations, we obtain existence of solutions by standard ordinary differential equation theory.
Uniqueness and the estimate on the norm follow similarly to Rauch comparison estimates for classical Jacobi fields on each interval
(t_{k-1},t_{k})
.
In detail, assume that the scalar curvature of
{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded from above by
\mu\geq 0
, and let
h
be small enough such that
h\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{2\mu}
.
Let
w
be defined on
(t_{k-1},t_{k})
by (
4
) with
w(t_{k-1})=0
and
w(t_{k})=\hat{v}\in T_{\gamma(t_{k})}{\mathcal{M}}
.
Set

\displaystyle f(t)=\partial_{t}|w|(t_{k-1})\;\tfrac{2h}{\pi}\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi(t-t_{k-1})}{2h}\right)\quad\text{for }t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],

then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}f+\tfrac{\pi}{2h}f=0\text{ on }(t_{k-1},t_{k})\quad\text{with}

\displaystyle f(t_{k-1})=0,\qquad\partial_{t}f(t_{k-1})=\partial_{t}|w|(t_{k-1}),\quad\text{and }0<f(t)<f(t_{k})\quad\text{for }t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}).

As long as
w(t)\neq 0
, abbreviating by
K(w,\dot{\gamma})=(w,{\mathrm{R}}(w,\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma})/\left(|w|^{2}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}-(w,\dot{\gamma})^{2}\right)
the sectional curvature, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}|w|

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|w|}K(w,\dot{\gamma})\left(|w|^{2}|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}-(w,\dot{\gamma})^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{|w|^{3}}\left(|w|^{2}|D_{t}w|^{2}-(w,D_{t}w)^{2}\right)\geq-\mu|\dot{\gamma}|^{2}|w|

and thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(f\partial_{t}|w|-|w|\partial_{t}f\right)=f\partial_{t}^{2}|w|-|w|\partial_{t}^{2}f\geq 0.

As
(f\partial_{t}|w|-|w|\partial_{t}f)(t_{k-1})=0
, this implies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\tfrac{|w|}{f}\right)=\frac{1}{f^{2}}\left(f\partial_{t}|w|-|w|\partial_{t}f\right)\geq 0

as long as
w(t)\neq 0
, and thus for
s>t>t_{k-1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{|w|(s)}{f(s)}\geq\frac{|w|(t)}{f(t)}\geq\lim_{r\searrow t_{k-1}}\frac{|w|(r)}{f(r)}=1,

where the limit follows by de l’Hôpital’s rule.
This shows in particular, that
|w|
cannot have a zero before
f
does, and thus the estimate

\displaystyle|w|(t)\leq\frac{f(t)}{f(t_{k})}|w|(t_{k})\leq|\hat{v}|

holds for all
t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Together with the linearity of (
4
), this stability result implies uniqueness of the solution to the boundary value problem as well as (using that the same estimate is obtained when the roles of
t_{k-1}
and
t_{k}
are swapped) the estimate

\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\max_{k=0,\ldots,N}|v(t_{k})|\leq 2\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}.\qed

Lemma 20

(Approximation properties of linear interpolation)
.

Let
h
be small enough such that
lemma

19
applies, and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. There exists a constant
c>0
depending on
p
and
{\mathcal{M}}
such that for any vector field
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
the estimate

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{2}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}(I_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{2}\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}(I_{k})}

holds on each interval
I_{k}=(t_{k},t_{k+1})
.
For
p>q\geq 1
and
\alpha=(q^{-1}-p^{-1})
there exist constants
c>0
depending on
p
,
q
and
{\mathcal{M}}
such that the inverse estimates

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|\dot{\gamma}\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\infty}}+h^{-\alpha}\right)\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}},

\displaystyle\|D_{t}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\alpha}+h^{-1-\alpha}\right)\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}}

hold. If
\|D_{t}^{l}v\|_{L^{p}}
is bounded for
l=0,1,2
, we further have the interpolation error estimate

\displaystyle\|v-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}}\leq c\;h^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}v\|_{L^{p}}+\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Proof.

The estimate for
D_{t}^{2}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v
follows on each interval
I_{k}
directly from the definition of
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
with
c=\|{\mathrm{R}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
The inverse estimates follow from the scaled Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality and the estimates on the second derivatives,

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{j}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{p}(I_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|D_{t}^{2}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}(I_{k})}^{\beta}\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}(I_{k})}^{1-\beta}+ch^{-j+p^{-1}-q^{-1}}\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}(I_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2\beta}+h^{-j+p^{-1}-q^{-1}}\right)\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\|_{L^{q}(I_{k})}

with
p^{-1}=j-2\beta+q^{-1}
and
j=0,1
.
The global estimates are now obtained by taking the
p
th power, summing over all subintervals
I_{k}
, and exploiting that
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{p/q}\leq(\sum_{i}a_{i})^{p/q}
for any vector of nonnegative values
a_{i}
.
The interpolation error follows from
lemma

13
with
l=1
, the estimate for
D_{t}^{2}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v
on each
I_{k}
, and
lemma

19
.
∎

Remark 21

(Vector field interpolation along splines)
.

We will always apply the vector field interpolation
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
along the cubic spline interpolations
\gamma_{h}
of some continuous curve
\gamma
. The dependence on
\gamma_{h}
in the estimates of
lemma

19
and
lemma

20
only comprises bounds on
\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}
that can be estimated in terms of
\gamma
using
proposition

8
. Thus, by slight abuse of notation, also for
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
we will take
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}v\in\gamma_{h}^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
.

Remark 22

(Alternative interpolation operators)
.

Note that the linear vector field interpolation defined by the boundary value problem (
4
) is natural along both linear and cubic splines in the following sense:

1.

Let
\gamma_{h}(s,t)
be a family of linear splines, then its variation is linear in the sense
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}=\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}
.
Indeed, the optimality conditions in
proposition

10
imply

0=D_{s}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h})=D_{t}D_{s}\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}=D_{t}^{2}\partial_{s}\gamma_{h}+{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{s}\gamma_{h},\partial_{t}\gamma_{h})\partial_{t}\gamma_{h}
(where we used
lemma

5
), the well-known equation of Jacobi fields.

2.

For cubic splines the second derivatives
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
are linear in the sense that
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}=D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
.

It is obvious that there are multiple possible alternatives to generalize piecewise linear interpolation from Euclidean space to vector fields on Riemannian manifolds.
In fact replacing (
4
) by
D_{t}^{2}w=0
would also work in the following convergence analysis with just slight modifications to the proofs, however, it seems a little less natural.
Indeed, the motivation for
definition

18
is to view a (piecewise) linear vector field as a second derivative of a cubic spline (which in Euclidean space is a piecewise cubic polynomial).
Would one consider higher order spline interpolation, that is, minimization of
\int_{0}^{1}|D_{t}^{k}\gamma_{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
under interpolation constraints,
then again a corresponding natural definition of
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
would have been the
(2k-2)
th derivative of local minimizers of that energy
(and the special case
k=2
leads to
definition

18
).

5.3
Quartic convergence of cubic splines

We aim to derive a quartic convergence rate by applying
corollary

12
to
g=\pi_{\gamma_{h}}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma
.
To this end we need to show boundedness of
\partial_{t}^{4}g
, which can be reduced to the boundedness of
D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}
and thus, via
proposition

10
(
2
), of
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
.
The derivation of such a bound occupies the major part of this paragraph, at the end of which the quartic convergence is deduced.
Exploiting
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}=D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
,
we could bound
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
by
lemma

19
if we knew its values at the interpolation times
t_{0},\ldots,t_{N}
,
however, those are not available.
This is in contrast to the piecewise linear interpolation
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma
of
D_{t}^{2}\gamma
(along
\gamma_{h}
), which thus can readily be bounded in
L^{\infty}((0,1))
.
Therefore we will show boundedness of
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
by bounding its difference to
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma
.
The proof, in turn, approximates this difference by
D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma
,
the generalization of
\ddot{\gamma}_{h}-\ddot{\gamma}
from the Euclidean case, for which there is a Galerkin orthogonality and consequently a best-approximation result.

Proposition 23

(Approximation error for
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
)
.

For
\gamma
four times differentiable and
h
small enough (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
) the cubic spline interpolation satisfies

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma-(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(h^{2}+\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\right)\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right),

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(h^{2}+\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right)

for a constant
c
only depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

We will estimate the difference by adding a zero and using the triangle inequality to split into easier terms.
In particular, we will add

0=-D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma+D_{t}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}))+D_{t}(\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h}))

to obtain

\displaystyle D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma

\displaystyle=-D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma+\left[D_{t}(\dot{\gamma}_{h}+\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h}))\right]+\left[D_{t}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}))-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma))\right]

\displaystyle=-D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma+\left[D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}+\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h})\right]+\left[\partial_{1}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma}_{h})+\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}_{h})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\left[\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma))\right]+\left[\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma})+\partial_{2}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\right],

where we additionally exploited
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma={\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma))
.
We now estimate the bracketed terms.
By
lemma

20
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma))\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\;h^{2}\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

We have

D_{t}^{2}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)=\partial_{1}^{2}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},D_{t}^{2}\gamma)+r,

where
r
only contains derivatives up to third order of the logarithm evaluated in directions of derivatives of
\gamma
up to fourth order and of
\gamma_{h}
up to first order.
Thus,
r
can be bounded by a constant (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).
Together we obtain

\|\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma)-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma))\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{2}(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1).

By
lemma

6
we furthermore have

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\partial_{1}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma}_{h})+\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}_{h})\right]+\left[\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h},\dot{\gamma})+\partial_{2}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\right]\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\hskip 172.5pt\leq c\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}(\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{2}\leq\tilde{c}\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}},

\displaystyle\left\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}+\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h})\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq c\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\tilde{c}\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}

so that in summary

\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma-(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\leq c\left(h^{2}+\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\right)\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right).

Next let
w\in\gamma_{h}^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}
be piecewise linear in the sense of
definition

18
, then applying integration by parts twice we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}(-D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma,w)\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma,{\mathrm{R}}(w,\dot{\gamma}_{h})\dot{\gamma}_{h})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq c\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\gamma}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq\tilde{c}\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

By the above we have

\int_{0}^{1}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma,w)\,\mathrm{d}t\leq c\|w\|_{L^{2}}\left(h^{2}+\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right).

The choice
w=D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma
now finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 24

(Uniform boundedness of
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
)
.

For
\gamma
four times differentiable and
h
small enough (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
) the cubic spline interpolation satisfies

\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 3\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}+ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{2}},\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch^{2}

for a constant
c
only depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Using
lemma

19
we have

\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}.

By
lemma

20
and
D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}={\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}
we have

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+h^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}.

Using
proposition

23
and
corollary

15
this turns into

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(h^{2}+h^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)(\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)\;,

and thus by
lemma

19

\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2ch^{\frac{3}{2}}(1+\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}})+2\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Taking
h
small enough such that
ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{6}
, we arrive at the desired bound for
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
This bound in turn can be applied in
proposition

23
together with
corollary

15
to yield the desired bound on
\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}D_{t}^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

Remark 25

(Boundedness of
\dot{\gamma}_{h}
for linear splines)
.

Note that a slightly weaker boundedness result than
proposition

16
for linear splines could have been obtained
following exactly the same argument as the one above for cubic splines.
In that case, one would have used a piecewise constant interpolation operator
{\mathcal{I}^{0}_{0}}
on vector fields
v\in\gamma^{\#}T{\mathcal{M}}

which would simply parallel transport
v(t_{k-1})
along
\gamma((t_{k-1},t_{k}))

and which would satisfy analogous properties to those of
{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{0}}
in
lemma

20
.
In the subsequent estimate of

\dot{\gamma}_{h}-{\mathcal{I}^{0}_{0}}\dot{\gamma}=-D_{t}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma+\left[\dot{\gamma}_{h}+\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}_{h})\right]+\left[\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma})-{\mathcal{I}^{0}_{0}}(\partial_{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(\dot{\gamma}))\right]

one would this time exploit the orthogonality property

\int_{0}^{1}(D_{t}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma,w)\,\mathrm{d}t=0
for any piecewise constant vector field along
\gamma_{h}
.

With the above preparations we can now turn to the final convergence result.

Corollary

24
and
proposition

14
already prove the desired result
\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{2}}\leq ch^{4}
in the
L^{2}
-norm;
for the
L^{\infty}
-norm (or any other
L^{p}
-norm) we will proceed by bounding
\|D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Corollary 26

(Quartic convergence)
.

For
\gamma
four times differentiable,
p\in[1,\infty]
, and
h
small enough (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
) the cubic spline interpolation satisfies

\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{2},\qquad\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{p}}\leq ch^{4},

where the constant
c>0
only depends on
{\mathcal{M}}
,
\gamma
, and
p
.

Proof.

We just show the
L^{\infty}
estimate, which is the strongest among all
p
,
and apply
corollary

12
to
g=\pi_{\gamma_{h}}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma
to obtain the desired result.
To this end it remains to show uniform boundedness of
|\partial_{t}^{4}g|=|D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma|
on each interval
I_{k}=(t_{k-1},t_{k})
. Now

D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma=\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h})+c_{1}\partial_{1}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\gamma)+c_{2}\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma(D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h},D_{t}\gamma_{h})+r

with some integers
c_{1},c_{2}
, where
r
is a term containing derivatives of the logarithm up to fourth order
evaluated in directions of derivatives of
\gamma
up to fourth order and of
\gamma_{h}
up to second order.
Thus,
|r|
can be bounded by a constant (depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
).
Consequently, exploiting
lemma

6
it remains to show uniform boundedness of
|D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}|
and
d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)|D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h}|
,
where by
corollary

17
the latter is no larger than
h^{2}|D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h}|
up to a constant factor
(in fact, below we will even obtain boundedness of
h|D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h}|
).
By
proposition

10
(
2
) as well as
proposition

8
(
2
) and
corollary

24
we have

|D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}|=|{\mathrm{R}}(D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h},\dot{\gamma}_{h})\dot{\gamma}_{h}|\leq C

for a constant
C>0
depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
.
Using the scaled Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality or an inverse inequality, we then obtain

\|D_{t}^{3}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}\leq c\|D_{t}^{4}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{1/2}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{1/2}+ch^{-1}\|D_{t}^{2}\gamma_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}\leq ch^{-1},

which finally proves boundedness
|\partial_{t}^{4}g|<C
for a constant
C
only depending on
{\mathcal{M}}
and
\gamma
.

The estimate of
\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}}
now follows again from the the scaled Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality.
Indeed, there exists
c>0
such that on each interval
I_{k}=(t_{k-1},t_{k})

\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}\leq c\|D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{1/2}\|\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{1/2}+ch^{-2}\|\log_{\gamma_{h}}\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}.

Using
\|d(\gamma_{h},\gamma)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{4}
as well as the uniform boundedness of
D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma
, the estimate turns into

\|D_{t}^{2}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}\leq c(\sqrt{C}\|D_{t}^{4}\log_{\gamma_{h}}\!\gamma\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{k})}^{1/2}+C)h^{2}.\qed

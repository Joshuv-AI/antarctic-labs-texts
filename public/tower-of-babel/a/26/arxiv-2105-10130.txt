Convergence of a spatial semi-discretization for a backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equation Thanks: This work was supported in part by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (11901410, 12171340) and the Fundamental Research Funds for the Central Universities in China (2020SCU12063).
Binjie Li, Xiaoping Xie

Abstract
This paper studies the convergence of a spatial semi-discretization for a backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equation. The filtration is general, and the spatial semi-discretization uses the standard continuous piecewise linear finite element method. Firstly, higher regularity of the solution to the continuous equation is derived. Secondly, the first-order spatial accuracy is derived for the spatial semi-discretization. Thirdly, an application of the theoretical results to a stochastic linear quadratic control problem is presented.



Keywords
. backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equation,
Brownian motion, semi-discretization, stochastic linear quadratic control.

Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a given complete probability space
with a normal filtration
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
(i.e.,

\mathbb{F}
is right continuous and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathcal{F}
). Assume that
W(\cdot)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted one-dimensional real Brownian motion. Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) be a bounded convex polygonal domain, and let
\Delta
be
the realization of the Laplace operator with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary
condition in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
. We consider the following backward semilinear
stochastic parabolic equation:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}p(t)=-(\Delta p(t)+f(t,p(t),z(t)))\,\mathrm{d}t+z(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
p(T)=p_{T},\end{cases}

(1)

where
p_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathbb{P};\dot{H}^{1})
and
f

satisfies the following conditions:

•

f(\cdot,p,z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
for each
p,z\in H
;

•

there exists a positive constant
\mathcal{M}
such that,
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely for almost every
t\in[0,T]
,

\lVert{f(t,p_{1},z_{1})-f(t,p_{2},z_{2})}\rVert_{H}\leqslant\mathcal{M}(\lVert{p_{1}-p_{2}}\rVert_{H}+\lVert{z_{1}-z_{2}}\rVert_{H})

(2)

for all
p_{1},p_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\in H
.

The above notations are defined later in
Section

2
.

Bismut
[
4
,
5
]
proposed the finite-dimensional linear
backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs for short) and used them to
form the stochastic maximum principle for finite-dimensional stochastic optimal
control problems. Bensoussan
[
3
]
used the infinite-dimensional
linear BSDEs to form the maximum stochastic principle of stochastic distributed
parameter systems. Later, Pardoux and Peng
[
44
]
made a
significant breakthrough by establishing the well-posedness of a class of
finite-dimensional nonlinear BSDEs, and soon Hu and Peng
[
28
]

proposed a highly non-trivial extension to the infinite-dimensional BSDEs.
Since then the theory of BSDEs began to develop quickly, mainly motivated by
applications to stochastic optimal control, partial differential equations and
mathematical finance; see
[
30
,
43
,
45
,
46
,
55
]
and the references therein. We particularly refer the reader to

[
21
, Chapter 6]
and
[
41
]
and the references
therein for the applications of the infinite-dimensional BSDEs to the stochastic
optimal control problems.

The above mentioned works on the BSDEs all require that the filtration is
generated by the underlying Wiener process. Motivated by the transposition
method for non-homogeneous boundary value problems for partial differential
equations (see
[
36
]
), Lü and Zhang
[
37
,
38
]

proposed a new notion of solution, the transposition solution, to BSDEs with
general filtration. The transposition solution coincides with the usual strong
solution when the filtration is natural. The transposition solution has been
successfully used to investigate the stochastic maximal principle for the
infinite-dimensional distributed parameter systems; see

[
39
,
40
]
and the references therein.

By now, the numerical solutions of the finite-dimensional BSDEs have been
extensively studied; see
[
6
,
13
,
14
,
20
,
27
,
29
,
42
,
56
]
and the references therein. In particular, we note that, based on the
definition of the transposition solution, Wang and Zhang
[
51
]

proposed a numerical method for solving finite-dimensional BSDEs. In view of the
fact that the numerical analysis of the infinite-dimensional stochastic
differential equations (SDEs for short) differs considerably from that of the
finite-dimensional SDEs, generally it is difficult to extend the numerical analysis of
finite-dimensional BSDEs to the infinite-dimensional BSDEs directly. We
note that there is a huge list of papers in the literature on the numerical
analysis of the infinite-dimensional SDEs; see

[
1
,
2
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
31
,
32
,
54
,
57
]

and the references
therein. Despite this fact, the numerical analysis of the infinite-dimensional
nonlinear BSDEs is expected to be a challenging problem, since the SDEs and the
BSDEs are essentially different.

So far, the numerical analysis of the infinite-dimensional BSDEs is very
limited. Wang
[
52
]
analyzed a semi-discrete Galerkin scheme for a
backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equation. Since this scheme uses the
eigenvectors of the Laplace operator, its application appears to be limited. Li
and Tang
[
35
]
developed a splitting-up method for solving
backward stochastic partial differential equations.
To our best knowledge, no convergence rate is available for the spatial
semi-discretization with finite element method of backward semilinear stochastic
parabolic equations with general filtration.

In this paper, we use the notion of transposition solution introduced by Lü
and Zhang
[
37
,
38
]
, and make the following threefold main
contributions.

•

Firstly, higher regularity of the transposition solution is derived,
which is essential for deriving convergence rate of the spatial
semi-discretization. We note that
[
38
]
gives the basic regularity result

(p,z)\in D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H))\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H).

Using the regularity estimates of the deterministic backward parabolic equations,
we prove that

(p,z)\in\big(L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})\cap D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{1}))\big)\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}).

•

Secondly, for a spatial semi-discretization of
Eq.

1
, we derive the
error estimate

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(p-p_{h})(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{H}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{p-p_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{z-z_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant ch,

which is optimal with respect to the regularity of the transposition solution.
This spatial semi-discretization adopts the standard continuous piecewise linear
finite element method. The case that
\mathbb{F}
is the natural filtration of

W(\cdot)
is also covered by our numerical analysis, since in this case the
transposition solution coincides with the usual strong solution.

•

Thirdly, with the derived higher order regularity of the transposition
solution and the convergence estimate, the first-order accuracy is derived for
a spatially semi-discrete stochastic linear quadratic control problem, where
the filtration is general and the diffusion term of the state equation
contains the control variable. Here we note that the stochastic optimal
control problems governed by stochastic partial differential equations have
been extensively studied in the past four decades; however, these problems
have rarely been numerically studied. To our best knowledge, this paper
provides the first convergence rate for a spatial semi-discretization of a
general stochastic linear quadratic control problem with general filtration.

We believe that the obtained theoretical results in this paper are useful for
further numerical analysis of backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equations
and stochastic linear quadratic control problems.

. In

. Finally, using the derived higher regularity result and the

stochastic linear quadratic control problem, and provide some numerical results in
Section

.

2
Preliminaries

Assume that
X
is a separable Hilbert space with norm
\lVert{\cdot}\rVert_{X}
. We
simply write the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathbb{P};X)
as
L^{2}(\Omega;X)
, and denote by
\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\cdot}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{X}
its norm. For any
v\in L^{2}(\Omega;X)
, we use
\mathbb{E}v
and
\mathbb{E}_{t}v
to denote
respectively the expectation of
v
and the conditional expectation of

v
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
for each
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
.
Let
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;X)
be the space of all
\mathbb{F}
-progressively
measurable processes
\varphi
such that

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\varphi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;X)}:=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\varphi(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{X}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}<\infty.

For any
0<t<T
, the space
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,t;X)
is defined analogously
to
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;X)
. Let
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];X))
be the
space of all
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
\varphi
with
continuous paths in
X
such that

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\varphi}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{C([0,T];X)}:=\Big(\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert{\varphi(t)}\rVert_{X}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}<\infty.

Let
D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;X))
be the
space of all
X
-valued and
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes that are right continuous with left limits in
L^{2}(\Omega;X)
with respect to the time variable. This is a Banach space with
the norm

\lVert{\varphi}\rVert_{D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;X))}:=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\varphi(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{X}\quad\forall\varphi\in D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;X)).

Denote
H:=L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
. For each
\gamma\geqslant 0
, define

\dot{H}^{\gamma}:=\{(-\Delta)^{-\gamma/2}v\mid v\in H\}

and endow this space with the norm

\lVert{v}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}:=\lVert{(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}v}\rVert_{H}\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}^{\gamma}.

We use
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
to denote the dual space of
\dot{H}^{\gamma}
. The
operator
\Delta
can be extended as a bounded linear operator from
H
to
\dot{H}^{-2}
by

\langle{\Delta v,\varphi}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{2}}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}v\Delta\varphi\quad\text{for all }v\in H\text{ and }\varphi\in\dot{H}^{2},

where
\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{2}}
denotes the duality pairing between
\dot{H}^{-2}
and
\dot{H}^{2}
.

For any
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{\gamma})
with
-2\leqslant\gamma<\infty
, let
S_{0}g
be the mild solution of the stochastic parabolic equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t)=\Delta y(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)\quad\forall t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=0.\end{cases}

(3)

It is standard that (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Chapter 3]
) for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

(S_{0}g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4)

Moreover, a routine argument with Itô’s formula gives

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(S_{0}g)(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}^{2}+2\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{S_{0}g}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}^{\gamma+1})}^{2}=\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}^{\gamma})}^{2}\quad\forall 0<t\leqslant T.

(5)

Finally, we introduce the mild solutions to a forward parabolic equation and a
backward parabolic equation, respectively. For any
g\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
, let
S_{1}g
and
S_{2}g
be the mild solutions of the equations

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)=\Delta y(t)+g(t)\quad\forall t\in[0,T],\\
y(0)=0\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}z^{\prime}(t)=-\Delta z(t)-g(t)\quad\forall t\in[0,T],\\
z(T)=0,\end{cases}

respectively. We have (see, e.g.,
[
53
, Chapter 3]
)

\displaystyle(S_{1}g)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

(6)

\displaystyle(S_{2}g)(t)=\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(7)

It is standard that, for any
v,w\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
,

(S_{1}v,w)_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}=(v,S_{2}w)_{L^{2}(0,T;H)},

(8)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}
denotes the inner product of the Hilbert
space
L^{2}(0,T;H)
.

3
Regularity

Following
[
38
]
, we call

(p,z)\in D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H))\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)

a transposition solution to
Eq.

1
if

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\big[p(s),g(s)\big]+\big[z(s),\sigma(s)\big]\,\mathrm{d}s+\big[p(t),v\big]

(9)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}\big[f(s,p(s),z(s)),(S_{0}\sigma+S_{1}g)(s)+e^{(s-t)\Delta}v\big]\,\mathrm{d}s{}

\displaystyle+\big[(S_{0}\sigma+S_{1}g)(T)+e^{(T-t)\Delta}v,p_{T}\big]

for all
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,
(g,\sigma)\in\big(L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)\big)^{2}
and
v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P};H)
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega;H)
. For the unique existence of the transposition solution to
Eq.

1
,
we refer the reader to
[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
. Moreover, the proof of

[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
contains that, for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

p(t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)\Delta}f(s,p(s),z(s))\,\mathrm{d}s+e^{(T-t)\Delta}\,p_{T}\Big)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(10)

In particular,

p(T)=p_{T}\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(11)

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

The transposition solution
(p,z)
of
Eq.

1
possesses the
following properties:

(i)

p\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})
;

(ii)

p
admits a modification in
D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{1}))
;

(iii)

z\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})
.

Remark 3.1
.

When
\mathbb{F}
is the natural filtration of
W(\cdot)
, by the theory in

[
25
,
28
]
we easily obtain

(p,z)\in\big(L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})\cap L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1}))\big)\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}).

The rest of this section is devoted to proving the above theorem. We first
introduce three technical lemmas, i.e.
Lemmas

3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
0\leqslant a<b\leqslant T
and
v\in L^{2}(\Omega;X)
,
with
X
being a separable Hilbert space. Then there exists a unique
y\in D_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega;X))
such that

\mathbb{P}(y(t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}v)=1\quad\forall t\in[a,b],

(12)

where
D_{\mathbb{F}}([a,b];L^{2}(\Omega;X))
is defined analogously to
D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;X))
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
w\in L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];X))
, where
X
is a separable
Hilbert space. Then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant r<s\leqslant T\\
s-r\leqslant 1/m\end{subarray}}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w(r)-w(s)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{X}=0.

(13)

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
v\in\dot{H}^{1}
and
g\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
. Define

w(t):=e^{(T-t)\Delta}v+\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Then

\lVert{w}\rVert_{C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1})}+\lVert{w}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})}\leqslant C\big(\lVert{v}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\lVert{g}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\big),

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
v
,
g
,
T
and
\mathcal{O}
.

The proofs of
Lemmas

3.1
and
3.2
are straightforward, and

Lemma

3.3
is standard; see, e.g.,
[
10
, Theorem 10.11]
.

Based on the three lemmas above, we are in a position to prove
Theorem

3.1
as follows.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
. Let us first prove
(i)
. Inserting
t=0
,
\sigma=0
and
v=0
into
Eq.

9
, by

8
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}[p(s),g(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{T}[\eta(s),g(s)]\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\forall g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H),

where

\eta(s):=\int_{s}^{T}e^{(r-s)\Delta}f(r,p(r),z(r))\,\mathrm{d}r+e^{(T-s)\Delta}p_{T}\quad\forall s\in[0,T].

It follows that

p=\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}}\eta\quad\text{ in }L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H),

(14)

where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}}
is the
L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H))
-orthogonal projection onto
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
. For each
n>0
, define

\eta_{n}(t):=\begin{cases}p_{T}&\text{ if }t=T,\\
\frac{n}{T}\int_{\frac{jT}{n}}^{\frac{(j+1)T}{n}}\eta(t)\,\mathrm{d}t&\text{ if }t\in\big[\frac{jT}{n},\frac{(j+1)T}{n}\big)\text{ with }0\leqslant j<n.\end{cases}

(15)

By

3.3
we have

\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1})),

(16)

and a routine density argument yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{n}=\eta\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})).

(17)

By

3.1
we conclude that there exists a unique

satisfying that

\mathbb{P}\big(p_{n}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}\eta_{n}(t)\big)=1\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Hence, by the inequality

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(p_{m}-p_{n})(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{2}}=\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\mathbb{E}_{t}(\eta_{m}-\eta_{n})(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{2}}\leqslant\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(\eta_{m}-\eta_{n})(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{2}}

for any

and

, we obtain

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to\infty\\
n\to\infty\end{subarray}}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{p_{m}-p_{n}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})}^{2}\leqslant\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\to\infty\\
n\to\infty\end{subarray}}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{m}-\eta_{n}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:426})}.

is a Cauchy sequence in

, and so there exists a unique

\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=\widetilde{p}\quad\text{ in }L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2}).

(19)

By

, it is easy to verify that, for each

so that by

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}}\eta_{n}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}}\eta=p\quad\text{ in }L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H).

(20)

In view of

19
and

, we readily obtain

.

Secondly, let us prove

. For any

\displaystyle\lVert{p_{m}-p_{n}}\rVert_{D_{\mathbb{F}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{1}))}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\max_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\mathbb{E}_{t}(\eta_{m}(t)-\eta_{n}(t))}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:pn-etan})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\max_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{m}(t)-\eta_{n}(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant r<s\leqslant T\\

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{1}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{2}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{3}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}.

(52)

For
\eta_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{1}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\lVert{\int_{t}^{T}\big(e^{(s-t)\Delta}-e^{(s-t)\Delta_{h}}Q_{h}\big)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}\Big\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\Big(\mathbb{E}\lVert{F}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:foo-2})}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq:f(p,z)-esti})}.

For
\eta_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{2}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\lVert{\int_{t}^{T}e^{(s-t)\Delta_{h}}(Q_{h}F-F_{h})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}\Big\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\mathbb{E}\lVert{F_{h}-Q_{h}F}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:foo-1})}

\displaystyle=\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{F_{h}-Q_{h}F}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:Fh-QhF}).}

For
\eta_{3}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\eta_{3}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\big\lVert{(e^{(T-t)\Delta}-e^{(T-t)\Delta_{h}}Q_{h})p_{T}}\big\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\Big(\mathbb{E}\lVert{p_{T}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:foo-3})}

\displaystyle=ch\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{p_{T}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leqslant ch.

Combining
Eq.

52
and the above estimates of
\eta_{1}
,
\eta_{2}
and
\eta_{3}
yields
Eq.

50
. Finally, summing up

Eqs.

49
and
50
proves
Eq.

30
and thus concludes the proof
of
Theorem

4.1
.

5
Application to a stochastic linear quadratic control problem

5.1
Continuous problem

We consider the following stochastic linear quadratic control problem:

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)\\
y\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{y-y_{d}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{u}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2},

(53)

subject to the state equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t)=(\Delta y+\alpha_{0}y+\alpha_{1}u)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+(\alpha_{2}y+\alpha_{3}u)(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),&0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y(0)=0,\end{cases}

(54)

where
0<\nu,T<\infty
,
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
, and
y_{d}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
. It is standard that
(see
[
38
, Theorem 8.1]
) problem
Eq.

53
admits a unique
solution
(\bar{u},\bar{y})
, and

\bar{u}=-\nu^{-1}(\alpha_{1}\bar{p}+\alpha_{3}\bar{z}),

(55)

where
(\bar{p},\bar{z})
is the transposition solution of the backward
stochastic parabolic equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{p}(t)=-(\Delta\bar{p}+\alpha_{0}\bar{p}+\bar{y}-y_{d}+\alpha_{2}\bar{z})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{z}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{p}(T)=0.\end{cases}

(56)

Since
Theorem

3.1
implies

\alpha_{1}\bar{p}+\alpha_{3}\bar{z}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}),

we then obtain

\bar{u}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1}).

(57)

Moreover, we have

\bar{y}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1}))\cap L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{2}).

(58)

Remark 5.1
.

For the theoretical treatment of the stochastic linear quadratic
control problems, we refer the reader to
[
41
]
and
the references therein.

Remark 5.2
.

The regularity result
Eq.

58
is standard. It can be easily proved
by the standard Galerkin method and the theory of the finite-dimensional linear
SDEs (see
[
45
, Chapter 3]
).

Remark 5.3
.

We note that Zhou and Li
[
58
]
established the convergence of a
full discretization for a Neumann boundary control problem governed by a
stochastic parabolic equation with additive boundary noise and general
filtration. When
\mathbb{F}
is the natural filtration of the Brownian
motion, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
,
[
33
]
and

[
48
]
for some related numerical analysis.

5.2
Spatially semi-discrete problem

The spatial semi-discretization of problem
Eq.

53
reads as follows:

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})\\
y_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-0.96873pt\left|\kern-0.96873pt\left|{y_{h}-y_{d}}\right|\kern-0.96873pt\right|\kern-0.96873pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\left|\kern-0.96873pt\left|\kern-0.96873pt\left|{u_{h}}\right|\kern-0.96873pt\right|\kern-0.96873pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2},

(59)

subject to the state equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)=(\Delta_{h}y_{h}+\alpha_{0}y_{h}+\alpha_{1}u_{h})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+(\alpha_{2}y_{h}+\alpha_{3}u_{h})(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),&0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y_{h}(0)=0.\end{cases}

(60)

Similarly to problem
Eq.

53
, problem
Eq.

59
admits a unique
solution
(\bar{u}_{h},\bar{y}_{h})
, and

\bar{u}_{h}=-\nu^{-1}(\alpha_{1}\bar{p}_{h}+\alpha_{3}\bar{z}_{h}),

(61)

where
(\bar{p}_{h},\bar{z}_{h})
is the transposition solution of the spatially
semi-discrete backward stochastic parabolic equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{p}_{h}(t)=-(\Delta_{h}\bar{p}_{h}+\alpha_{0}p_{h}+\bar{y}_{h}-Q_{h}y_{d}+\alpha_{2}\bar{z}_{h})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\bar{z}_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{p}_{h}(T)=0.\end{cases}

(62)

The main result of this section is the following error estimate.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\bar{u},\bar{y})
and
(\bar{u}_{h},\bar{y}_{h})
be the solutions of
problems
Eqs.

53
and
59
, respectively. Then

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant ch.

(63)

To prove this theorem, we first introduce three lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
y_{h}
be the solution of the stochastic equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)=(\Delta_{h}y_{h}+\alpha_{0}y_{h}+g_{h})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{2}(t)y_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y_{h}(0)=0,\end{cases}

(64)

where
g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})
. Then

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{y_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}.

(65)

Proof.

Letting
w_{h}:=(-\Delta_{h})^{-1/2}y_{h}
, by
Eq.

64
we have

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}w_{h}(t)=\big(\Delta_{h}w_{h}+\alpha_{0}w_{h}+(-\Delta_{h})^{-1/2}g_{h}\big)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{2}(t)w_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\hskip 9.24994pt0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
w_{h}(0)=0.\end{cases}

A routine argument with the Itô’s formula then yields, for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{H}^{2}+2\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{t}\big[w_{h}(s),\alpha_{0}(s)w_{h}(s)+(-\Delta_{h})^{-1/2}g_{h}(s)\big]\,\mathrm{d}s+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\alpha_{2}w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}^{2}+2\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(-\Delta_{h})^{-1/2}g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(-\Delta_{h})^{-1/2}g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}^{2}.

(66)

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{H}^{2}\leqslant c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;H)}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,t;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}^{2}\quad\forall 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

and so using the Gronwall’s inequality yields

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{H}\leqslant c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}.

(67)

In addition, inserting
t=T
into
Eq.

66
gives

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})}^{2}\leqslant c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{g_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}^{2}.

(68)

Finally, combining
Eq.

67
,
Eq.

68
and the fact

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{y_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}=\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{w_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{1})},

we readily obtain
Eq.

65
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that
(\bar{u},\bar{y})
is the solution of problem
Eq.

53
.
Let
y_{h}
be the mild solution of the stochastic equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)\!=\!(\Delta_{h}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{0}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{1}Q_{h}\bar{u})(t)\mathrm{d}t\!+\!(\alpha_{2}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{3}Q_{h}\bar{u})(t)\mathrm{d}W(t),\,0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y_{h}(0)=0.\end{cases}

(69)

Then

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-y_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant ch^{2}.

(70)

Proof.

By definition we have

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}\bar{y}(t)=(\Delta\bar{y}+\alpha_{0}\bar{y}+\alpha_{1}\bar{u})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+(\alpha_{2}\bar{y}+\alpha_{3}\bar{u})(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\hskip 9.24994pt0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{y}(0)=0,\end{cases}

so that

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Q_{h}\bar{y}(t)=Q_{h}(\Delta\bar{y}+\alpha_{0}\bar{y}+\alpha_{1}\bar{u})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+Q_{h}(\alpha_{2}\bar{y}+\alpha_{3}\bar{u})(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\hskip 9.24994pt0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
Q_{h}\bar{y}(0)=0.\end{cases}

Hence, by
Eq.

69
we get

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}e_{h}(t)=(\Delta_{h}e_{h}+\alpha_{0}e_{h}+\Delta_{h}Q_{h}\bar{y}-Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\alpha_{2}(t)e_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\hskip 9.24994pt0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
e_{h}(0)=0,\end{cases}

where
e_{h}:=y_{h}-Q_{h}\bar{y}
. By
Lemma

5.1
we then obtain

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{e_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\Delta_{h}Q_{h}\bar{y}-Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}

\displaystyle=c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\Delta_{h}(Q_{h}\bar{y}-\Delta_{h}^{-1}Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y})}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{-2})}

\displaystyle=c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{Q_{h}\bar{y}-\Delta_{h}^{-1}Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-y_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-Q_{h}\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{e_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-Q_{h}\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{Q_{h}\bar{y}-\Delta_{h}^{-1}Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-Q_{h}\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+c\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\Delta_{h}^{-1}Q_{h}\Delta\bar{y}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}.

Hence, the desired estimate
Eq.

70
follows from
Eq.

58

and the standard estimate

\lVert{v-Q_{h}v}\rVert_{H}+\lVert{v-\Delta_{h}^{-1}Q_{h}\Delta v}\rVert_{H}\leqslant ch^{2}\lVert{v}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{2}}\quad\forall v\in\dot{H}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
g_{h},v_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})
.
Let
(p_{h},z_{h})
be the transposition solution of the equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}p_{h}(t)=-\big(\Delta_{h}p_{h}+\alpha_{0}p_{h}+g_{h}+\alpha_{2}z_{h}\big)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+z_{h}(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\,0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
p_{h}(T)=0,\end{cases}

and let
y_{h}
be the mild solution of the equation

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)\!=\!(\Delta_{h}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{0}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{1}v_{h})(t)\mathrm{d}t\!+\!(\alpha_{2}y_{h}\!+\!\alpha_{3}v_{h})(t)\mathrm{d}W(t),\,0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y_{h}(0)=0.\end{cases}

Then

\int_{0}^{T}\big[(\alpha_{1}p_{h}+\alpha_{3}z_{h})(t),\,v_{h}(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\big[g_{h}(t),\,y_{h}(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t.

(71)

Proof.

Note that

y_{h}=S_{0}^{h}(\alpha_{2}y_{h}+\alpha_{3}v_{h})+S_{1}^{h}(\alpha_{0}y_{h}+\alpha_{1}v_{h}).

By definition (see
Eq.

28
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big[p_{h}(t),\,(\alpha_{0}y_{h}+\alpha_{1}v_{h})(t)\big]+\big[z_{h}(t),\,(\alpha_{2}y_{h}+\alpha_{3}v_{h})(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big[(\alpha_{0}p_{h}+g_{h}+\alpha_{2}z_{h})(t),\,y_{h}(t)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t,

which implies the desired equality
Eq.

71
.
This completes the proof.
∎

. Firstly, we present some

y_{h}

, let
(\bar{p},\bar{z})
and

4.1
we have

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{(\bar{p}-p_{h})(t)}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{H}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{p}-p_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{1})}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{z}-z_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant ch.

(74)

Secondly, we use the standard argument in the numerical analysis of
optimization with PDE constraints (see
[
26
, Theorem 3.4]
) to prove

Eq.

63
. By
Eq.

55
we obtain

\nu\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{u},\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}]\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}[\alpha_{1}\bar{p}+\alpha_{3}\bar{z},\bar{u}_{h}-\bar{u}]\,\mathrm{d}t,

(75)

and by
Eq.

61
we get

-\nu\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{u}_{h},\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}]\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}[\alpha_{1}\bar{p}_{h}+\alpha_{3}\bar{z}_{h},\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}]\,\mathrm{d}t.

(76)

Summing up the above two equalities yields

\displaystyle\nu\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\big[\alpha_{1}(\bar{p}-\bar{p}_{h})+\alpha_{3}(\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{h}),\,\bar{u}_{h}-\bar{u}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{1}+\mathbb{I}_{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\big[\alpha_{1}(\bar{p}-p_{h})+\alpha_{3}(\bar{z}-z_{h}),\,\bar{u}_{h}-\bar{u}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\big[\alpha_{1}(p_{h}-\bar{p}_{h})+\alpha_{3}(z_{h}-\bar{z}_{h}),\,\bar{u}_{h}-\bar{u}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t.

For
\mathbb{I}_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\big(\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{p}-p_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{z}-z_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\big)\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:barp-ph}).}

For
\mathbb{I}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h},\bar{y}_{h}-y_{h}]\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:barph-barph-ph-yh})}

\displaystyle=-\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}[\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h},\bar{y}-y_{h}]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-y_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+ch^{4}\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:bary-yh}).}

Combining the above estimates of
\mathbb{I}_{1}
and
\mathbb{I}_{2}
yields

\displaystyle\nu\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle ch\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+ch^{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle ch^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2},

which implies
Eq.

63
. This completes the proof.

5.3
Numerical results

Let
J\geqslant 2
be a positive integer. Set
t_{j}:=j\tau
for each
0\leqslant j\leqslant J
, where
\tau:=T/J
. Define

L_{\mathbb{F},\tau}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h}):=\big\{U\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})\mid\text{$U$ is constant on $[t_{j},t_{j+1})$, $\forall 0\leqslant j<J$}\big\}.

For each
0\leqslant j<J
, define
\delta W_{j}:=W(t_{j+1})-W(t_{j})
, and let
\mathrm{Net}_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{j}\to\mathbb{R}
be a neural network. Define the control
U\in L_{\mathbb{F},\tau}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})
as follows:

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad U(0,x):=\mathrm{Net}_{0}(x)\quad\text{for each interior node $x$ of $\mathcal{K}_{h}$};

\displaystyle\text{for any $1\leqslant j<J$},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad U(t_{j},x):=\mathrm{Net}_{j}(x,\delta W_{0},\ldots,\delta W_{j-1})\quad\text{for each interior node $x$ of $\mathcal{K}_{h}$}.

The numerical optimal control
\bar{U}
is obtained by training these neural
networks
\mathrm{Net}_{j}
with the loss function

\mathcal{L}_{h,\tau}(U):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{Y-y_{d}(t_{j})}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(t_{j},t_{j+1};H)}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{U}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2},

where the numerical state
Y\in L_{\mathbb{F},\tau}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V}_{h})
is
calculated as follows:

\begin{cases}Y(t_{j+1})-Y(t_{j})=\tau\Delta_{h}Y(t_{j+1})+\tau(\alpha_{0}Y+\alpha_{1}U)(t_{j}){}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(\alpha_{2}Y+\alpha_{3}U)(t_{j})\delta W_{j},\qquad 0\leqslant j<J,\\
Y(0)=0.\end{cases}

Our numerical experiment adopts the following settings:
\mathcal{O}=(0,1)
,

T=$2\text{\times}{10}^{-1}$
,
\nu=$1\text{\times}{10}^{-2}$
,
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=1
,
\alpha_{3}=$1\text{\times}{10}^{-1}$
; each
\mathrm{Net}_{j}
,
0\leqslant j<J
, is a
fully connected feedforward neural network with four hidden layers,
where each hidden layer possesses
200
neurons with the ReLU activation
function; in the computation of each numerical optimal control, these neural
networks
\mathrm{Net}_{j}
are trained by the Adam optimization algorithm with

5000
iterations, where each iteration uses
128
paths. The numerical
experiment is performed by means of PyTorch with single precision.

In
Tables

1
and
2
, the reference control
U^{*}
is the numerical
control with
h=1/64
,
\bar{Y}
and
Y^{*}
are the numerical states of
the controls
\bar{U}
and
U^{*}
, respectively, and
\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\cdot}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|
denotes
the norm
\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\cdot}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}
. In addition, the norm
\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\cdot}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}
is calculated by
2.56\text{\times}{10}^{6}
paths. The numerical results
in

and

demonstrate that

\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{U}-U^{*}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}+\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|{\bar{Y}-Y^{*}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}

O(h)

Theorem

5.1
.

6
Conclusion

For the backward semilinear stochastic parabolic equation with general
filtration, we have derived the higher regularity of the solution to the
continuous problem, and obtained the first-order accuracy of the spatial
semi-discretization with the linear finite element method. The derived
theoretical results have been applied to a general stochastic linear quadratic
control problem, and the first-order spatial accuracy has been derived for a
spatially semi-discrete stochastic linear quadratic control problem.

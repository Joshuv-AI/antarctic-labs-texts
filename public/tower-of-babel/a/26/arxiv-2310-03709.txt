.

(

,

.

On the other hand, there is an elliptic curve

(namely

.

We now give an informal description of the algorithm below.

Input:
d=-1
or
-3
and an elliptic curve
E/K
where
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}).

Output: List of all quadratic extensions where torsion grows and the torsion structure in each case.

(1)

Let
S:=\{2,3,5,7\}
be the set of all primes
p
for which there is an elliptic curve
E/K

with
p\ |\ E(K)_{\text{tor}}
.

(2)

For each prime
p
in
S
, determine the division polynomial
f_{p}
of smallest degree
necessary to detect growth of the
p
-part of
E(K)_{\text{tor}}
upon quadratic base change:

(a)

Find the primary decomposition of
E(K)_{\text{tor}}.

(b)

For each prime
p
in
S
, count the number
S_{p}
of
p
-summands in the
p
-part of
E(K)_{\text{tor}}.

(i)

For
p=2
:

•

If
S_{p}=0
then
E(K)[2^{\infty}]
does not grow in a quadratic extension by Theorem
2.4
.

•

If
S_{p}\neq 0
and
E(K)[2^{\infty}]\simeq[2^{a},2^{b}]
with
a<b
then
f_{p}=\psi_{2^{b}}.

(ii)

For
p=3
:

•

If
S_{p}=0
or
1
then if any growth occurs, by Lemma
2.3
,
E(K)[p]
must grow by Theorem
2.4
, so we let
f_{p}:=\psi_{p}.

•

If
S_{p}=2
then the
p
-part cannot grow in a quadratic extension by Lemma
2.3
, so let
f_{p}=1.

(iii)

For
p>3
:

•

If
S_{p}=0
then if any growth occurs, by Lemma
2.3
,
E(K)[p]
must grow by Theorem
2.4
, so we let
f_{p}:=\psi_{p}.

•

If
S_{p}=1
then the
p
-part cannot grow in a quadratic extension by Lemma
5.3
, so let
f_{p}=1.

•

If
S_{p}=2
then the
p
-part cannot grow in a quadratic extension by Lemma
2.3
, so we ignore it.

(3)

For each prime
p
in
S
:

(a)

Factor
f_{p}
over
K.

(b)

For each factor
g
of
f_{p}
over
K
:

(i)

If deg
(g)=1
:

•

We may write
g=x-c.
Compute
(c,d)
on
E.
If
d\not\in K,
then the torsion grows in
L=K(d)
and Magma can compute
E(L)_{\text{tor}}.

(ii)

If deg
(g)=2
:

•

Construct the splitting field
L
of
g
over
K,
and let
c
be a root of
g
in
L.
Compute
(c,d)
on
E
. If
d\in L,
then the torsion grows in
L
and Magma can compute
E(K)_{\text{tor}}.

Characterization of k k -positive maps
Tomasz Młynik, Hiroyuki Osaka, Marcin Marciniak

Abstract
. We present a general characterization of k-positivity for a positive map in terms of the estimation of the Ky Fan norm of the matrix constructed from the Kraus operators of the associated completely positive map. Combining this with the result given by Takasaki and Tomiyama we construct a family of positive maps between matrix algebras of different dimensions depending on a parameter. The estimate bounds on the parameter to obtain the k k -positivity are better than those derived from the spectral conditions considered by Chruściński and Kossakowski. We further look with special attention at the case where we give the precise bound for the regions of decomposability.



1.
Introduction

Examining the mixed states of composite quantum systems to determine the presence of quantum correlation, or whether the state is entangled or separable
[
12
,
10
]
, is a fundamental issue in quantum information theory. This is particularly important for private and quantum communication problems
[
2
]
. Most communication-related experiments are conducted based on the entanglement between qubits. For low-dimensional complex systems, such as qubit-qubit and qubit-qutrit, the Peres-Horodecki criterion
[
9
]
fully characterizes separable states, stating that a state is separable if and only if its partial transpose is positive. However, technological advancements now allow for the experimental control of composite systems with a larger number of degrees of freedom
[
5
,
8
,
7
,
1
]
. For high-dimensional systems, there is no exhaustive separability condition.

The aim of our paper is to explore the potential for constructing indecomposable positive
k
maps for higher
k
values.
We investigate a family of linear maps
\Phi_{a}
acting from
\mathbb{M}_{m}
to
\mathbb{M}_{n}
(
m\leq n
), indexed by a positive parameter
a
. Our construction is inspired by
[
13
]
and represents a specific instance of the general construction outlined in
[
4
]
. Using different approaches from those in
[
4
]
, we estimate the bounds of the parameter
a
that ensure the
k
-positivity of
\Phi_{a}
. These estimates prove to be significantly better than those derived from the spectral condition considered by Chruściński and Kossakowski. We further focus on the scenario where
n
and
m
differ by one, providing an explicit analytical formula for the constraints on the parameter
a
that guarantees
k
-positivity.
We also pay special attention to cases where we establish the precise bounds for the regions of decomposability. Furthermore, we demonstrate that for sufficiently large differences
n-m
, some maps are neither completely positive nor completely copositive within the region of
k
-positivity for higher
k
.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

By
\mathbb{M}_{n}
we denote the algebra of all square
n\times n
-matrices with complex coefficients and by
\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
the cone of positive semidefinite matrices from
\mathbb{M}_{n}
.
Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, a linear map
\phi:\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{n}
is called a
positive map
if
\phi(\mathbb{M}_{m}^{+})\subset\mathbb{M}_{n}^{+}
.
The identity map and the transpose map on
\mathbb{M}_{n}
are denoted by
\mathrm{id}_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
, respectively. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a map
\phi
is called
k
-positive
(respectively,
k
-copositive
) if the ampliation map
\mathrm{id}_{k}\otimes\phi:\mathbb{M}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
(respectively,
\tau_{k}\otimes\phi:\mathbb{M}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{k}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
) is positive.
We say that a map
\phi
is
completely positive
(respectively
completely copositive
) if it is
k
-positive (respectively
k
-copositive) for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A positive map
\phi
is said to be
decomposable
if it is a sum of a completely positive map and a completely copositive one. The sets of all linear, positive,
k
-positive,
k
-copositive, completely positive, completely copositive and decomposable maps from
{\mathbb{M}}_{m}
into
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}
will be denoted by
L({\mathbb{M}}_{m},{\mathbb{M}}_{n})
,
\mathbb{P}(m,n)
,
\mathbb{P}_{k}(m,n)
,
\mathbb{P}^{k}(m,n)
,
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}(m,n)
,
\mathbb{P}^{\infty}(m,n)
and
\mathbb{D}(m,n)
, respectively. We will omit the dimensions
m,n
in case it does not lead to confusion.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be finite dimensional Hilbert spaces with
d_{i}=\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and let
A:{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
be a linear operator.
For
k=1,2,\ldots,d
, where
d=\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
, we define Ky Fan
k
-norm of
A

[
6
]

(2.1)

\|A\|_{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(A),

where
s_{1}(A)\geq\ldots\geq s_{d}(A)
are the singular values of
A
. Clearly, for

k=1
one recovers the operator norm
\|A\|_{(1)}=\|A\|
and if
d_{1}=d_{2}=d
, then for
k=d

one reproduces the trace norm
\|A\|_{(d)}=\|A\|_{\mathrm{tr}}:=\Tr((A^\dagger A)^{1/2})
.
Note that a Ky Fan
k
-norm may be equivalently introduced as follows. By
\mathrm{Proj}_{k}({\mathcal{H}})
we denote the set of all orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with trace (i.e. dimension of the image) equal to
k
.
It is easy to show that

\displaystyle\|A\|_{(k)}=\max\left\{\Tr(Q(AA^\dagger)^\frac{1}{2}):\,Q\in\mathrm{Proj}_{k}({\mathcal{H}}_{2})\right\}.

3.
Characterisation of
k
-positivity

Let
\mathbf{Tr}:\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a map defined by
\mathbf{Tr}(X)=\Tr(X)\mathds{1}_{n}
for
X\in\mathbb{M}_{m}
.
Each positive map is a scalar multiple of a map of the form
\mathbf{Tr}-\psi
with
\psi
being a uniquely determined completely positive map. Since every completely positive map has its Kraus representation, we arrive at the following general form of a positive map
\phi:\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{n}

(3.1)

\phi(X)=a\Tr(X)\mathds{1}_{n}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}K_{\alpha}XK_{\alpha}^{\dagger},\qquad X\in\mathbb{M}_{m},

where

K_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
\alpha=1,2,\ldots,N
, are linear operators and
a
is a positive number.
Assume that the operators
K_{\alpha}
are fixed. Our aim is to characterize such a range of numbers
a
that the map
\phi
given by (
3.1
) is
k
-positive for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us observe that the Choi matrix
C_{a}
of
\phi
has the form
C_{a}=a\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-C
, where

C=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e_{ij}\otimes\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}K_{\alpha}e_{ij}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}

We define

(3.2)

a_{k}=\sup\{\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)}:\,Q\in\mathrm{Proj}_{k}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
\phi
defined in (
3.1
) is
k
-positive if and only if
a\geq a_{k}
.

Proof.

Assume
a\geq a_{k}
. According to
[
14
, Prop. 1.1]
it is enough to show that
(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C_{a}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)
is positive for every
Q\in\mathrm{Proj}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Let
\ket{\xi}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
be such that
\norm{\xi}=1
.
We can assume that
\ket{\xi}\in(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)({\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\bra{\xi}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C_{a}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)\ket{\xi}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)\xi}^{2}-\bra{\xi}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)\ket{\xi}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle a_{k}-\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)C (\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)})\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows from (
3.2
).

Now, assume that
a<a_{k}
. It follows from the definition of supremum that there exists a projection
Q\in\mathrm{Proj}_{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and a unit vector
\ket{\xi}\in(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)({\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
such that
a<\bra{\xi}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)\ket{\xi}
. Hence

\displaystyle\bra{\xi}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C_{a}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)\ket{\xi}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a-\bra{\xi}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes Q)\ket{\xi}<0

what shows that
\phi
is not
k
-positive.
∎

We are now ready to formulate our main result specifying the lower bound
a_{k}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\phi
is of the form (
3.1
). It is
k
-positive if and only if

(3.3)

a\geq\sup_{\zeta}\norm{\left( \sum_{\alpha=1}^N{\zeta_\alpha}K_\alpha\right)\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^N\zeta_\beta K_\beta\right)^\dagger}_{(k)},

where the supremum is taken over all vectors
\ket{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\dots,\zeta_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, such that
\norm{ \zeta}=1
and
\norm{\cdot}_{(k)}
denotes the
k
-th Ky Fan norm.

Proof.

We will show that
a_{k}
defined in (
3.2
) is equal to the expression on the right-hand side of (
3.3
). Let us note that
C=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e_{ij}\otimes\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}K_{\alpha}e_{ij}K^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}\subset\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}\simeq\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
where the embedding is of the form

(3.4)

X\otimes Y\mapsto X\otimes\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}Y&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

This embedding is isometric. Let us define matrices
\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{M}_{n,m})
,
\mathbf{e}_{ij}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{M}_{m})
and
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{M}_{N}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
by

\mathbf{K}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}K_{0}&\cdots&K_{r}\\
0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0\end{array}\right),\quad\mathbf{e}_{ij}=\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes e_{ij}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}e_{ij}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&e_{ij}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&e_{ij}\end{array}\right),

\mathbf{Q}=\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}Q&0&\cdots&0\\
0&Q&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&Q\end{array}\right).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\sum_{\alpha=1}^N K_\alpha e_{ij}K_\alpha^\dagger\right)(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\sum_{\alpha=1}^N QK_\alpha e_{ij}K_\alpha^\dagger Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\left(\begin{array}{cccc}\sum_{\alpha=1}^N QK_\alpha e_{ij}K_\alpha^\dagger Q & 0 & \cdots& 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots& 0 \\ \vdots& \vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\0&0& \cdots& 0 \end{array}\right)
}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{e}_{ij}\mathbf{K}^\dagger\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\norm{\left(\mathds{1}_m\otimes\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}\right) \left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{ij}\right)\left(\mathds{1}_m\otimes\mathbf{K}^\dagger\mathbf{Q}\right)}.

The middle term above is a projection, so one obtains

\displaystyle\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\norm{ \left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{ij}\right) \left(\mathds{1}_m\otimes\mathbf{K}^\dagger\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}\right) \left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{ij}\right) }.

Let
\{h_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N}
be a system of matrix units in
\mathbb{M}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. Note that
\mathbf{e}_{ij}=\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes e_{ij}
, hence

(3.6)

\displaystyle\norm{(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)C(\mathds{1}_m\otimes Q)}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left\|\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e_{ij}\otimes\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes{e}_{ij}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N}\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes h_{\alpha\beta}\otimes K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}QK_{\beta}\right)\right.\times

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e_{ij}\otimes\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes{e}_{ij}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\norm{
\frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{i,j=1}^m\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^N e_{ij}\otimes h_{\alpha\beta}\otimes\Tr\left(K_\alpha^\dagger Q K_\beta\right)e_{ij}
}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{
\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^m e_{ij}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{ij}\right) \left(\mathds{1}_m \otimes\Big(\Tr\left(K_\alpha^\dagger Q K_\beta\right)\Big)_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\mathds{1}_m\right)
}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\norm{\Big(\Tr(K_\alpha^\dagger QK_\beta)\Big)_{\alpha,\beta}}.

The above matrix
(\Tr(K_\alpha^\dagger QK_\beta))_{\alpha,\beta=1,\ldots,N}
is a selfadjoint element of
\mathbb{M}_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
. The last equality between lines (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) is due to the fact that the first and the second factors in (
3.7
) lie in
\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{N}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{m}
and
\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{N}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m}
respectively and the operator norm on tensor product is a cross norm. Observe that
\Tr(K_\alpha^\dagger Q K_\beta)=\Tr(K_\alpha^\dagger QQ^\dagger K_\beta)=\Tr(QK_\beta K_\alpha^\dagger Q)
for every
\alpha,\beta
.
We have

\displaystyle\norm{\Big(\Tr(QK_\beta K_\alpha^\dagger Q)\Big)_{\alpha, \beta}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{N}\overline{\zeta_{\alpha}}\zeta_{\beta}\Tr(QK_\beta K_\alpha^\dagger Q)\right|:\,\ket{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{N})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\,\|\zeta\|\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\zeta}\left|\Tr\left(Q\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^{N}\zeta_{\beta}K_{\beta}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\zeta_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}\right)^{\dagger}Q\right)\right|.

Now according to (
3.2
) we get

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\in\mathrm{Proj_{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\;\sup_{\zeta}\left|\Tr\left(Q\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^{N}\zeta_{\beta}K_{\beta}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\zeta_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}\right)^{\dagger}Q\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\zeta}\;\sup_{Q}\left|\Tr\left(Q\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^{N}\zeta_{\beta}K_{\beta}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\zeta_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}\right)^{\dagger}Q\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\zeta}\norm{\left(\sum_{\beta=1}^N \zeta_\beta K_\beta\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^N\zeta_\alpha K_\alpha\right)^\dagger}_{(k)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Applications

In this section, we present an explicit construction of a family of
k
-positive maps.

The main motivation for our consideration are results of
[
13
]
. This paper describes a linear map acting on
\mathbb{M}_{n}
into itself whose Choi matrix is of the form
A-\lambda\ket{\psi_n^+}\!\bra{\psi_n^+}
where
\lambda\geq 0
,
and
A
is a positive invertible matrix in
\mathbb{M}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
acting on the orthogonal complement of
\ket{\psi_n^+}
in
({\mathbb{C}}^{n})^{\otimes 2}
. Their result states that for
k=1,2,\ldots,n-1
, this map is
k
-positive when
A\geq\dfrac{k\lambda}{n-k}\left(\mathds{1}_{n}-\ket{\psi_n^+}\!\bra{\psi_n^+}\right)
and is not
k
-positive when
\|A\|<\dfrac{k\lambda}{n-k}
. When applying this result to the case
A=a(\mathds{1}_{n}-\ket{\psi_n^+}\!\bra{\psi_n^+})
,
a>0
, one can deduce that the map

(4.1)

\Phi_{a}(X)=a\Tr(X)\mathds{1}_{n}-X

is
k
-positive and is not
(k+1)
-positive if and only if
k\leq a<k+1
.

Remark 4.1
.

For
a=1
the map (
4.1
) is known as a reduction map, while for
a=n-1
it is nothing but the map considered by Choi in
[
3
]
.

4.1.
One-parameter family of maps

Motivated by
[
13
]
and
[
4
]
we propose the following construction.
Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\leq m\leq n
.
Let
\{\ket{e_p}\}_{p=1}^{m}
and
\{\ket{f_s}\}_{s=1}^{n}
be standard orthonormal bases in
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
respectively.
Consider linear isometries
V_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\alpha=0,1,\ldots,r
, where
r=n-m
, given by

(4.2)

V_{\alpha}\ket{e_p}=\ket{f_{p+\alpha}},\qquad p=1,2,\ldots,m.

Given
a>0
, we define a map
\Phi_{a;m,n}:\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{n}
,

(4.3)

\Phi_{a;m,n}(X)=a\Tr(X)\mathds{1}_{n}-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}V_{\alpha}XV_{\alpha}^{\dagger},

for
X\in\mathbb{M}_{m}
. When dimensions are fixed we will write
\Phi_{a}
instead of
\Phi_{a;m,n}
.

To describe the Choi matrix of
\Phi_{a}

consider projections
P_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{M}_{m}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{n}
, of the form

(4.4)

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ket{e_i}\!\bra{e_j}\otimes V_{\alpha}\ket{e_i}\!\bra{e_j}V_{\alpha}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ket{e_i}\!\bra{e_j}\otimes\ket{f_{i+\alpha}}\!\bra{f_{j+\alpha}}.

Observe that
P_{\alpha}P_{\beta}=P_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
, hence
P=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}P_{\alpha}
is a projection too. Therefore the Choi matrix
C_{\Phi_{a}}
of the map (
3.1
) has the form

(4.5)

C_{a}:=C_{\Phi_{a}}=m(\mu\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-P),

where
\mu=a/m
.

Remark 4.2
.

Observe that for
m=n
the map
\Phi_{a;n,n}
is precisely the map given by (
4.1
).

Proposition 4.3
.

If
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
are such that
2\leq m\leq n
then
a_{k}<a_{k+1}
for
k=1,\ldots,m-1
.

Proof.

For
\ket{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r+1}
define
V(\zeta)=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}\zeta_{\alpha}V_{\alpha}
.
Firstly, we will show that
\|V(\zeta)V(\zeta)^{\dagger}\|_{(k)}<\|V(\zeta)V(\zeta)^{\dagger}\|_{(k+1)}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m-1
, when
\|\zeta\|=1
. Notice that

V(\zeta)=(v_{pq}(\zeta))_{1\leq p\leq n;\,1\leq q\leq m}
where

v_{pq}(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\zeta_{p-q},&\text{if $0\leq p-q\leq m$}\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\tilde{\alpha}=\min\{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,r\}:\,\zeta_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
. Then the square
m\times m
submatrix
(v_{p,q}(\zeta))_{\tilde{\alpha}+1\leq p\leq\tilde{\alpha}+m;\,1\leq q\leq m}
has the following triangular form

(4.6)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\zeta_{\tilde{\alpha}}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
&\zeta_{\tilde{\alpha}}&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\zeta_{\tilde{\alpha}}&0\\
&*&\cdots&*&\zeta_{\tilde{\alpha}}\end{array}\right).

It follows that
K(\zeta)
has rank
m
, and consequently the matrix
K(\zeta)K(\zeta)^{\dagger}
has rank
m
as well. This in turn implies that the matrix
K(\zeta)K(\zeta)^{\dagger}
has
m
strictly positive eigenvalues, which provides sharp inequalities between its successive Ky Fan norms.

Now, assume that
a_{k}=a_{k+1}
for some
k=1,\ldots,m-1
. Let
\ket{\tilde{\zeta} }\in{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
be a unit vector such that
a_{k}=\|K(\tilde{\zeta})K(\tilde{\zeta})^{\dagger}\|_{(k)}
. The existence of such a vector follows from the compactness of the set of all unit vectors in
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
. Then we have

a_{k+1}=a_{k}=\|K(\tilde{\zeta})K(\tilde{\zeta})^{\dagger}\|_{(k)}<\|K(\tilde{\zeta})K(\tilde{\zeta})^{\dagger}\|_{(k+1)}
, which leads to a contradiction because
a_{k+1}
is a supremum of the
k+1
-th Ky Fan norms over all unit vectors
\ket{\zeta}
.
∎

4.2.
Comment on complete copositivity

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 3m-2
. Then complete copositivity of
\Phi_{a}
(
4.3
) implies
a\geq m
.

Proof.

Let
\ket{\xi}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\ket{e_k}\otimes\ket{f_{2m-k}}
. Then

\displaystyle C_{a}^{\Gamma}\ket{\xi}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\ket{e_i}\!\bra{e_j}\otimes\left(a\delta_{ij}\mathds{1}_{n}-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}\ket{f_{j+\alpha}}\!\bra{f_{i+\alpha}}\right)\right)\ket{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ket{e_i}\otimes\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(a\delta_{ij}\ket{f_{2m-j}}-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{n-m}\delta_{i+\alpha,2m-j}\ket{f_{j+\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(a-\#S_{i}\right)\ket{e_i}\otimes\ket{f_{2m-i}},

where

S_{i}=\{(j,\alpha)\colon 1\leq j\leq m,\,0\leq\alpha\leq n-m,\,j+\alpha=2m-i\}.

If the assumption
n\geq 3m-2
is satisfied, then
r=n-m\geq 2m-2
, so for every pair
i,j
(
1\leq i,j\leq m
) there is precisely one number
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots,r\}
such that
j+\alpha=2m-i
. Hence,
\#S_{i}=m
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. Consequently,
C_{a}^{\Gamma}\ket{\xi}=(a-m)\ket{\xi}
. Thus
a-m
is an eigenvalue of
C_{a}^{\Gamma}
. If
\Phi_{a}
is completely copositive then
C_{a}^{\Gamma}
is positive semidefinite, therefore
a-m
must be non-negative.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The map
\Phi_{a}
is k-positive and decomposable if
a\geq ma_{k}\geq r+1
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1

\Phi_{a}
is
k
-positive if and only if
a\geq ma_{k}
.

On the contrary, since

\displaystyle C_{a}

\displaystyle=m(\mu\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-P)

\displaystyle=a\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP

\displaystyle\geq ma_{k}\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP

\displaystyle\geq(r+1)\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-m\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}P_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP_{\alpha}).

and
(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP_{\alpha})^{\tau}
is positive for all
\alpha

(\tau
is the partial transpose
)
, we know that
\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\mathds{1}_{m}-mP_{\alpha})
is completely copositive.

Hence, since
C_{\alpha}=\{m(a\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-P)-\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP_{\alpha})\}+\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}(\mathds{1}_{m}\otimes\mathds{1}_{n}-mP_{\alpha})
,
\Phi_{a}
is decomposable.
∎

4.3.
Special case
r=1

In this section, we consider the case when dimensions
m
and
n
differ precisely by
1
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Phi_{a}:\mathbb{M}_{m}\to\mathbb{M}_{m+1}
be of the form (
4.3
) and let
1\leq k\leq m
.
\Phi_{a}
is
k
-positive if and only if

(4.7)

a\geq k+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{m+1}\right).

Proof.

Let
M=(\sum_{\beta=0}^{1}\overline{\zeta_{\beta}}V_{\beta})(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{1}\zeta_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}_{\alpha})
be the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
matrix which appeared in Theorem
3.2
, where
\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
|\zeta_{0}|^{2}+|\zeta_{1}|^{2}=1
.
It turns out that
M
is a tridiagonal matrix of the form

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}|\zeta_{0}|^{2}&\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\zeta_{0}\overline{\zeta_{1}}&1&\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\zeta_{0}\overline{\zeta_{1}}&1&\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}&&0&0\\
0&0&\zeta_{0}\overline{\zeta_{1}}&1&\ddots&&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}&\vdots\\
0&0&0&&\zeta_{0}\overline{\zeta_{1}}&1&\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&\zeta_{0}\overline{\zeta_{1}}&|\zeta_{1}|^{2}\end{array}\right)

Let us note that

M=M_{m+1,1}(1,-|\zeta_{1}|^{2},-|\zeta_{0}|^{2},z,\overline{z})
, (see
[
11
]
) and
z=\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}
.
In order to apply Theorem
3.2
one should calculate
\|M\|_{(k)}
.
If
z=0
then
M
is a projection with
\Tr(M)=m
, so
\|M\|_{(k)}=k
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Assume
z\neq 0
. Observe that

(4.9)

M-\lambda\mathds{1}=M_{m+1,1}(1-\lambda,-|\zeta_{1}|^{2},-|\zeta_{0}|^{2},z,\overline{z}).

Therefore, to find eigenvalues of
M
one needs to solve the equation

(4.10)

|z|^{n}\left(U_{m+1}\left(x\right)-\frac{1}{|z|}U_{m}\left(x\right)+U_{m-1}\left(x\right)\right)=0,

where

U_{j}
are Chebychev polynomials of the second kind, and

(4.11)

x=\frac{1-\lambda}{2|z|}.

Chebyshev polynomials satisfy the following recurrence relations

(4.12)

\begin{split}&U_{-1}(x)=0,\qquad U_{0}(x)=1\\
&U_{m+1}(x)+U_{m-1}(x)=2xU_{m}(x),\quad m=0,1,\ldots.\end{split}

Thus the equation (
4.10
) is equivalent to

(4.13)

-\lambda|z|^{m}U_{m}\left(\frac{1-\lambda}{2|z|}\right)=0.

It follows that roots of
U_{m}
are precisely the numbers
\cos\left({j\pi}/(m+1)\right)
, for
j=1,2,\ldots,m
. Thus, the decreasing sequence
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\ldots>\lambda_{m}
of non-zero eigenvalues of
M
is given by

\lambda_{j}=1+2|z|\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{m+1}\right),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

so,

(4.14)

\|M\|_{(k)}=k+2|z|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\cos\left(\frac{j\pi}{m+1}\right),\quad k=1,2\ldots,m.

Taking into account that
\sup\{\left|\overline{\zeta_{0}}\zeta_{1}\right|\colon\|\zeta\|=1\}=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain (
4.7
).
∎

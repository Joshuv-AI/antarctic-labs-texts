The Branched Deformations of the Special Lagrangian Submanifolds
Siqi He

Abstract
. The branched deformations of immersed compact special Lagrangian submanifolds are studied in this paper. If there exists a nondegenerate ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} harmonic 1-form over a special Lagrangian submanifold L L , we construct a family of immersed special Lagrangian submanifolds L ~ t \tilde{L}_{t} , that are diffeomorphic to a branched covering of L L and L ~ t \tilde{L}_{t} convergence to 2 ​ L 2L as current. This answers a question suggested by Donaldson [ 8 ] . Combining with the work of [ 1 ] , we obtain constraints for the existence of nondegenerate ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} harmonic 1-forms.



1.
Introduction

Calabi-Yau n-folds
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
are compact complex manifold
(X,J)
of complex dimension
n
, with a Kähler metric
g
, a compatible symplectic form
\omega
, and a parallel holomorphic volume form
\Omega
with specific constant norm. Harvey and Lawson
[
12
]
introduced the concepts of calibrated submanifolds, with special Lagrangian submanifolds being an important class.

Let
L
be a closed special Lagrangian submanifold, then the normal bundle
N_{L}
could be identified with the cotangent bundle
T^{\star}L
using the complex structure. The exponential map could identify small 1-forms
\alpha
with submanifolds
L_{\alpha}
close to
L
and we could regard this as a deformation. According to McLean’s deformation theorem
[
36
]
, the
\mathcal{C}^{1}
deformations of the special Lagrangian submanifold
L
could be parameterized by harmonic 1-forms on
L
with the induced metric. For generalizations in different circumstances, see
[
27
,
33
,
41
]
.

In this work, we’ll look at a generalization of McLean’s theorem to branched covering deformations using the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-forms, which could be thought of as a multivalued 1-forms satisfy extra constrains. The study of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-forms started from the work of Taubes
[
45
]
on characterized the non-compactness behavior of flat
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
connections, see also
[
16
,
17
,
49
]
for different generalizations.

Let
(L,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma
a codimension 2 oriented embedded submanifold of
L
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a flat
\mathbb{R}
bundle over
L\setminus\Sigma
, such that every small loop locally linking
\Sigma
has monodromy
-1
. The monodromy of
\mathcal{I}
will define a branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
, that branches along
\Sigma
. A section
\alpha\in T^{\star}L\otimes\mathcal{I}
is called a multivalued harmonic 1-form if
\alpha\in L^{2}
satisfies the harmonic equations
d\alpha=d\star\alpha=0,

where
\star
the metric Hodge star operator on
L
and the derivative is taken using the flat structure on
\mathcal{I}
.

According to the work of Donaldson
[
8
]
, the multivalued harmonic 1-forms have an asymptotic description near
\Sigma
. Fixed
p\in\Sigma,
we could find suitable complex coordinates
z
on a slice through
p
transverse to
\Sigma
with the leading asymptotic

\alpha=\Re d(Az^{\frac{1}{2}})+\Re d(Bz^{\frac{3}{2}})+o(|z|^{\frac{3}{2}-\gamma}),

where
A
and
B
are sections of suitable bundles,
d
is taking the derivative using the flat structure and
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.
\alpha
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form, named in
[
46
]
, if
|\alpha|
is bounded with
\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}=1
, and nondegenerate if
A=0
and
B
is nowhere vanishing along
\Sigma
. For a more detailed explanation, see Section
4
.

The deformation theory for nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-forms have been studied by Donaldson
[
8
]
, see also
[
35
,
44
]
for different settings and generalizations. Donaldson observed that the nondegenerate multivalued harmonic 1-forms could be suitable candidates for the branched deformations of the special Lagrangian submanifolds. To be more explicit, Donaldson asked the following question.

Question 1.1
.

[
8
, Page 3]

Let
\iota_{0}:L\to X
be the inclusion map of an immersed special Lagrangian submanifold, suppose over
\iota_{0}(L)
there exists a nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form with branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
. Could we construct a family of immersed special Lagrangian submanifolds
\tilde{\iota}_{t}:\tilde{L}\to X
such that
\tilde{\iota}_{t}
convergences to the double branched covering
\iota_{0}\circ p
?

We could give a positive answer to Donaldson’s question, in particular, we proved:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold and
\iota_{0}:L\to X
be an immersed special Lagrangian submanifold with induced metric
g_{L}
. Suppose there exists a nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form
\alpha
on
L
with induced branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
, then there exists a positive constant
T
and a family of special Lagrangian submanifold
\tilde{\iota}_{t}:\tilde{L}\to X
for
|t|\leq T
such that

(i)

\tilde{\iota}_{t}(\tilde{L})
convergence to 2
\iota_{0}(L)
as current,

(ii)

\lim_{t\to 0}\|\tilde{\iota}_{t}-\iota_{0}\circ p\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}=0
,

(iii)

in a Weinstein neighborhood
U_{L}
, there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}:L\to L
such that suppose we write
\tilde{\iota}_{t\phi_{t}^{\star}(\alpha)}:\tilde{L}\to U_{L}
be the inclusion map induces by the graph of
t\phi_{t}^{\star}(\alpha)
, then there exists a t independent constant
C
such that

\|\tilde{\iota}_{t\phi_{t}^{\star}(\alpha)}-\tilde{\iota}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq Ct^{2},

where
\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}
is the Hölder norm taken with respect to the metric
p^{\star}g_{L}
over
\tilde{L}
and
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.

When
L
is Riemannian surface, nondegenerate harmonic 1-forms could be identified with quadratic differentials with simple zeros. Given a quadratic differential, the family of spectral curves will be an example of branched deformations for a suitable Calabi-Yau structure in a neighborhood of the zero section in
T^{\star}L
, which we refer Section
5
for more details of this construction. However, when
\dim(L)\geq 3
, as the graph of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form might not be an embedded submanifold in a Weinstein neighborhood of
L
, we don’t expect the branched deformation family we constructed above are embedded submanifolds.

By
[
18
]
, there exist examples of real analytic rational homology 3-spheres that admits nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form, which could be realized as a special Lagrangian submanifolds in a neighborhood of the zero section in the cotangent bundle by the Calabi-Yau neighborhood theorem
[
4
]
. Then for these special Lagrangian submanifolds, as the first Betti number vanishes, the McLean’s deformations will not exist. We obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

There exist special Lagrangian submanifolds which are rigid in classical sense but could have branched deformations.

There are two major challenges in answering this question. The unbounded geometry of the branched deformation family presented the first challenge. Let
t
be the parameter for the family, we may expect the injective radius, as well as the curvatures of the branched submanifolds, to grow to infinity when
t\to 0
. This phenomenon also occurred in previous desingularization problems of calibrated submanifolds with singularities. For example, the isolated conical singularity desingularization
[
24
]
and the Lawlor neck problem, see
[
5
,
21
,
29
,
40
]
.

The key observation for the branched deformation problem is that, despite the family’s unbounded geometry, the first eigenvalues of the linearization operators over the family are bounded by a uniform positive number. In addition, we have a good understanding of the blow-up order of the geometry of the family, including the second fundamental form, the injective radius and the size of the Weinstein tubular neighborhood.

The second challenge comes from the singular set moving. The multivalued forms branched along submanifold could be considered as a free boundary problem, while the branch locus itself needs to be determined. For additional information on the linearized problem, we refer
[
8
,
44
]
. The linearization of the special Lagrangian equation is the harmonic equation for multivalued 1-forms. Even we start with a multivalued harmonic 1-form which solves the linearization equations with branch locus
\Sigma
, it doesn’t guarantee that we will be able to solve the non-linear equation using the same branch locus. In actuality, for each
t
, the branched deformation family may branch along a different codimension 2 submanifold
\Sigma_{t}
which will be close to the initial branch locus of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form.

The above observation contributes to the construction of approximate special Lagrangian submanifolds, where the method we used is very similar to Donaldson in
[
8
,
9
]
. Given an initial nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form
\alpha
, we proved that after moving the singular set in a specified direction, we could make good enough approximate special Lagrangian submanifolds
\tilde{L}_{t}^{\mathrm{app}}
, which is close to
t\phi_{t}^{\star}\alpha
, for some diffeomorphisms
\phi_{t}
of
L
. Then we could apply a version of Joyce’s nearby special Lagrangian theorem
[
26
]
to obtain the branched deformation family.

According to the celebrated work of Taubes
[
46
,
45
]
, the bounded
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form plays an important role in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
gauge theory, which characterized the non-compactness behavior of the moduli space of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
flat connections. It would be fascinating to learn if there are any topological constraints on the existence of nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-forms. Combing our main theorem with the work of nearby special Lagrangian theorem by Abouzaid and Imagi
[
1
]
, we obtain a criterion for non-existence of nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(L,g_{L})
be a real analytic Riemannian manifold and
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
be a multivalued harmonic 1-form with associate branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
, suppose the following holds:

(i)

\pi_{1}(L)
is finite or contains no nonabelian free subgroup,

(ii)

let
R
be the canonical involution on
T^{\star}L
, there exists a Calabi-Yau neighborhood
(U_{L},J,\omega,\Omega)
of the zero section in
T^{\star}L
with
R
an anti-holomorphic involution,

(iii)

all
R
-invariant immersed special Lagrangian submanifolds that are diffeomorphic to
\tilde{L}
is unobstructed,

then
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
is not a nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form.

According to the work of Bryant
[
4
]
and Doice
[
7
]
, the condition (ii) holds when
\chi(L)=0
. For the condition (iii), in general, it is very hard to check whether a Lagrangian is unobstructed or not. We do, however, have an additional
R
-symmetry given by the anti-holomorphic involution, which allows us to check the assumption (iii) for specific situations. When
\dim(L)=2
, according to
[
42
]
, the condition (i) and (iii) holds for
L=S^{2}
and
T^{2}
. For a single special Lagrangian submanifold, we could introduce topological constraint to avoid obstruction, which give us a Betti number constraint for the branched covering.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
be a nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form on
L
with branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
, suppose
\pi_{1}(L)
is either finite or contains no nonabelian free subgroups and the Euler characteristic
\chi(L)=0
, then for the second Betti number, we have the strict inequality
b_{2}(\tilde{L})>b_{2}(L)
.

The first Betti number inequality
b_{1}(\tilde{L})>b_{1}(L)
is already implied by the presence of nondegenerate
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-forms, hence the preceding theorem is trivial when
\dim(L)=3
by Poincare duality. However, when
\dim(L)\geq 4
, we found examples that the above theorem could be applied.

2.1.
Calabi-Yau manifold and the special Lagrangian submanifolds

In this subsection, we will define the immersed special Lagrangian submanifolds in a Calabi-Yau manifold, follows from Harvey and Lawson
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Calabi-Yau manifold is a quadruple
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
such that
(X,J,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold with a Kähler metric
g_{X}
and Kähler class
\omega
. Let
n
be the complex dimension of
X
,
\Omega
is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(n,0)
-form on
X
satisfies

(1)

\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{(\sqrt{-1})}^{n}\Omega\wedge\bar{\Omega}.

An immersed submanifold is a pair
[L]=(L,\iota)
, where
L
is a closed oriented manifold and
\iota:L\to X
is an immersion.
[L]
is called a Lagrangian submanifold if
\iota^{\star}\omega=0
. For any immersed Lagrangian submanifold
[L]
, for the holomorphic volume form, we have
|\iota^{\star}\Omega|=1
, where the norm is taken using the pull-back metric
\iota^{\star}g_{X}.
Therefore, we could write

(2)

\iota^{\star}\Omega=e^{i\theta}\mathrm{Vol}_{\iota^{\star}g_{X}},

where
e^{i\theta}:L\to S^{1}
is called a phase function.

Harvey and Lawson
[
12
]
introduced the concepts of special Lagrangian submanifolds.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold, an immersed oriented submanifold
[L]=(L,\iota)
is called a special Lagrangian submanifold if
e^{i\theta}=1
.

By (
2
),
e^{i\theta}=1
is equivalent to
\iota^{\star}\Im\Omega=0
or
\iota^{\star}\Re\Omega=\mathrm{Vol}_{\iota^{\star}g_{X}}
. For a general Lagrangian submanifold, the
S^{1}
valued function
e^{i\theta}
doesn’t necessarily have a lift to a real valued function
\theta
, while
d\theta
is well-defined, which will satisfy

d\theta=\iota_{H}\omega,

where
H
is the mean curvature vector field.
Suppose the lifting
\theta:L\to\mathbb{R}
exists, then we call
\theta
the Lagrangian angle. In particular, any Lagrangian that sufficiently close to a special Lagrangian will have a global well-defined Lagrangian angle, which are the cases that considered in this paper.

Special Lagrangian submanifolds are an important class of calibrated submanifold. A Lagrangian submanifold
[L]=(L,\iota:L\to X)
is called calibrated by
\Re\Omega
if
\iota^{\star}\Re\Omega=d\mathrm{Vol}_{\iota^{\star}g_{X}}
, where
d\mathrm{Vol}_{\iota^{\star}g_{X}}
is the volume form for the pullback metric
\iota^{\star}g_{X}
. The special Lagrangian submanifold is calibrated by
\Re\Omega
based on the definition. The following is a fundamental property of calibrated submanifold.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
[L]=(L,\iota)
be a special Lagrangian submanifold equipped with the pull metric
g_{L}:=\iota^{\star}g
, then
\iota^{\star}\Im\Omega=d\mathrm{Vol}_{L}
. In addition, in the homology class
\iota_{\star}[L]
,
L
is volume-minimizing.

Proof.

Let
[L^{\prime}]
is another special Lagrangian submanifold such that
[L^{\prime}]
lies in the same homology class of
[L]
. Then we compute

\mathrm{Vol}(L)=\int_{L}d\mathrm{Vol}(L)=\int_{L}\iota^{\star}\Im\Omega=\int_{L^{\prime}}\iota^{\star}\Im\Omega\leq\int d\mathrm{Vol}_{L^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Vol}(L^{\prime}),

which implies the proposition.
∎

There are many interesting examples of special Lagrangian submanifolds have been constructed, which we refer to
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
22
,
23
]
. In addition, with extra assumptions of symmetry, we could also obtain examples of special Lagrangian submanifolds. We will list some of them which will be used later.

Example 2.4
.

[
20
]

Suppose over a Calabi-Yau manifold
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
with Calabi-Yau metric
g
, there exists an anti-holomorphic involution
R:X\to X
such that
R^{\star}\omega=-\omega,\;R_{\star}J=-JR_{\star},\;R^{\star}g=g
and
R^{\star}\Omega=\bar{\Omega}
, then the fix real locus
\{x\in X|R(x)=x\}
is a special Lagrangian submanifold.

The following example could be found in
[
33
, Example 2.25]
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(X,g)
be a hyperKähler manifold of dimension
4k
, with complex strictures
I,J,K
satisfies
I\circ J\circ K=\mathrm{Id}
and symplectic structure
\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K}
. Take
\Omega:=(\omega_{I}+i\omega_{J})^{k}
, then
(X,K,\omega_{K},\Omega)
and
(X,K,\omega_{K},i\Omega)
are both Calabi-Yau structures.
\iota:L\to X
is called a complex Lagrangian submanifold if it is a complex submanifold for
J
and symplectic for
\omega_{K}
. Then
\iota^{\star}\Im\Omega=0
if
k
is even and
\iota^{\star}\Im(i\Omega)=0
if
k
is odd. Therefore, every complex Lagrangian submanifold is a special Lagrangian submanifold for some Calabi-Yau structure.

2.2.
Tubular neighborhood for immersed Lagrangian submanifolds

In this subsection, we will discuss the Weinstein tubular neighborhood for immersed Lagrangian submanifolds, which identified a neighborhood of an immersed submanifold with a neighborhood of the zero sections in the cotangent bundle.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
[L]=(L,\iota)
be an immersed submanifold, we define the normal bundle
N_{L}:=\iota^{\star}TX/TL\cong TL^{\perp}\subset\iota^{\star}TY
, where
TL^{\perp}
is the complement bundle defined by the pullback metric
\iota^{\star}g_{X}
on
\iota^{\star}TX
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
[L]=(L.\iota)
be an immersed Lagrangian submanifold of the Calabi-Yau
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
, then there exists a canonical isomorphism between the normal bundle
N_{\Sigma}
and cotangent bundle
T^{\star}L
.

Proof.

Let
g
be the Riemannian metric on
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
, using the pull-back metric
\iota^{\star}g
, we could identify
T^{\star}L
with
TL
. In addition, we have
\omega(v_{1},v_{2})=g(v_{1},Jv_{2})
for any vector fields
v_{1},v_{2}
. The Lagrangian condition
\iota^{\star}\omega=0
implies that
J
maps between
TL
and
N_{L}
, which is an isomorphism by dimension reason.
∎

Under the identification of
T^{\star}L
and
N_{L}
, we can define the tubular neighborhood as follows.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
[L]=(L,\iota)
be an immersed Lagrangian submanifold, a tubular neighborhood of
[L]
is an open set
U_{L}\subset T^{\star}L
which containing the zero section. We call
U_{L}(s)
is a tubular neighborhood of
[L]
with size
s
if

(3)

U_{L}(s)=\{\alpha\in T^{\star}L|\|\alpha\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq s\}.

We write
\varphi:T^{\star}L\to N_{L}
be the isomorphism in Proposition
2.7
, then we could define

(4)

\Phi:U_{L}\to X\;\mathrm{as}\;\Phi(\alpha)=\exp_{\iota\circ\pi(\alpha)}\circ\varphi(\alpha),

where
\exp
is the exponential map on
X
,
\alpha\in T^{\star}L
and
\pi:T^{\star}L\to L
is the projection map of the bundle. Composing
\Phi
with self diffeomorphism of
U_{L}
, we have the Weinstein tubular neighborhood theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
6
]

Let
(X,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and
[L]=(L,\iota)
is a compact Lagrangian submanifold, then there exists an open tubular neighborhood
U_{L}
with
\Phi:U_{L}\to X
such that
\Phi^{\star}\omega=\omega_{0}
, where
\omega_{0}
is the canonical symplectic structure on
T^{\star}L
.

Suppose
[L]
is a Lagrangian submanifold in a Calabi-Yau manifold, then over the Weinstein neighborhood
U_{L}
, we could have a pull-back Calabi-Yau strcture
(U_{L},J,\omega,\Omega)
with
\omega
the canonial symplectic form, which is the case that mostly considered in this article.

2.3.
McLean’s deformation for the special Lagrangian submanifolds

The deformation theory of compact special Lagrangian submanifold is first studied by McLean
[
36
]
, see also
[
27
,
33
,
41
]
in different settings.

Let
[L_{0}]
be an immersed special Lagrangian submanifold,
U_{L}\subset T^{\star}L_{0}
be a Weinstein neighborhood of
[L_{0}]
and
\iota_{0}:L_{0}\to U_{L}
be the inclusion map of the zero section. Then any Lagrangian immersion that is sufficiently
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-close to
\iota_{0}
will be given by the graph of a 1-form on
L
. Suppose we have a family of special Lagrangian submanifolds
[L_{t}]=(L,\iota_{t})
on
U_{L}
such that
\iota_{t}
are given by the graph of 1-forms on
L
and
\lim_{t\to 0}\|\iota_{t}-\iota_{0}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1}}=0
, then we call
[L_{t}]
a graphic deformations of
[L_{0}]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
36
]

Let
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold with metric
g_{X}
,
[L_{0}]=(L,\iota_{0})
be an immersed special Lagrangian submanifold on
X
, then the graphic deformations of
[L_{0}]
are parameterized by harmonic 1-forms on
L
for the metric
\iota_{0}^{\star}g_{X}
.

Proof.

We will give a sketch of proof of McLean’s theorem by an implicit functional argument. Let
\Phi:U_{L}\to X
be a Weinstein neighborhood of
[L_{0}]
, let
\alpha
be a closed 1-form on
L
, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
a real parameter, we define
[L_{t}]:=(L,\iota_{t})
, with
\iota_{t}(x)=\Phi(t\alpha|_{x})
, which is the graph of
t\alpha
.

We construct a map

(5)

\begin{split}F_{t}&:\Omega^{1}(L)\to\Omega^{2}(L)\oplus\Omega^{n}(L),\\
F_{t}&(v):=(\iota^{\star}_{t}\omega,\iota^{\star}_{t}\Im\Omega).\end{split}

By
[
36
, Page 722]
, we obtain

(6)

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\iota^{\star}_{t}\omega=d\alpha,\;\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\iota^{\star}_{t}\Im\Omega=d\star\alpha,

where
\star
is the Hodge star operator on
[L_{0}]
w.r.t. the pull-back metric
\iota^{\star}g_{X}
. In addition, by the special Lagrangian condition
\iota_{0}^{\star}\omega=0
and
\iota_{0}^{\star}\Im\Omega=0
,
\iota_{t}^{\star}\omega
and
\iota^{\star}_{t}\Omega
are trivial in the cohomology group
H^{2}(L;\mathbb{R})
and
H^{n}(L;\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Im}F_{t}\subset d\Omega^{1}(L)\oplus d\star\Omega^{1}(L)
. In a suitable norm
dF_{t}
will be surjective and
F_{t}^{-1}(0,0)
will be a smooth manifold model by the harmonic 1-forms.
∎

The McLean’s deformation theorem works straight forward if we consider unbranched smooth deformation. Let
p:\tilde{L}\to L
be any unbranched k-fold covering of
L
, then
[\tilde{L}_{0}]=(\tilde{L},\iota\circ p)
is also an immersed submanifold. Applying Theorem
2.10
for
[\tilde{L}_{0}]
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.11
.

Under the previous assumptions, given an harmonic 1-form
v
on
\tilde{L}
w.r.t. the pull-back smooth metric
(\iota\circ p)^{\star}g_{X}
, then there exists a family of special Lagrangian submanifolds
[\tilde{L}_{t}]=(\tilde{L},\tilde{\iota}_{t})
such that
\lim_{t\to 0}\tilde{\iota}_{t}=\iota\circ p
.

3.
The Special Lagrangian Equation over the Cotangent Bundle

In a Weinstein neighborhood, the special Lagrangian equation is an equation for 1-forms
\alpha
on
T^{\star}L
which characterized whether the graph of
\alpha
is a special Lagrangian submanifold. The structure of the special Lagrangian equation up to quadratic terms was studied in
[
25
,
36
]
. In this section, we will study the higher order expansion of the special Lagrangian equation using pseudo holomorphic volume form on
T^{\star}L
, which is an approximation of the real holomorphic volume form.

3.1.
Special Lagrangian equations in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a graph

We first consider the special Lagrangian equation in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for a graph manifold, introduced by Harvey and Lawson
[
12
]
. Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the n-dimensional complex plane with complex coordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
. We write
z_{i}=x_{i}+\sqrt{-1}y_{i}
. Then
\mathbb{C}^{n}
has a natural Calabi-Yau structure

g_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|dz_{i}|^{2},\;\omega_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{i},\;\mathrm{and}\;\Omega_{0}=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the n-plane span by
(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
and we identified
T^{\star}\mathbb{R}^{n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
f
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the graph manifold of
df
will be

\Gamma_{f}=\{(x_{1}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{x_{1}}f,\;\cdots,\;x_{n}+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{x_{n}}f_{n})\},

which is a smooth Lagrangian on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We write
\iota_{f}:\Gamma_{f}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the natural embedded map, then we have

(7)

\begin{split}\iota_{f}^{\star}\Im\Omega=\Im\det(I+i\mathrm{Hess}f)dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n},\\
\iota_{f}^{\star}\Re\Omega=\Re\det(I+i\mathrm{Hess}f)dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}.\end{split}

The special Lagrangian equation
\iota_{f}^{\star}\Im\Omega=0
for
\Gamma_{f}
can be written as

(8)

\Im\det(I+i\mathrm{Hess}f)=0.

In particular, when
n=3
, the above equation obtain the following beautiful form

-\Delta f+\det(\mathrm{Hess}f)=0.

3.2.
The geometry of the cotangent bundle

In this subsection, we will introduce the Sasaki metric on the cotangent bundle of a Riemannian manifold and the readers are referred to
[
51
]
for further details. Moreover, using the Sasaki metric, we will define an approximation to the holomorphic volume form.

Let
(L,g)
be a Riemannian manifold with Riemannian metric
g
and cotangent bundle
T^{\star}L
. Let
p\in T^{\star}L
with
\pi(p)=x
, using the Levi-Civita connection, we have the following splitting of the tangent bundle of
T^{\star}L
, which is
T_{p}(T^{\star}L)=H\oplus V,

where the horizontal part
H|_{p}\cong T_{x}L
and the vertical part
V|_{p}\cong T^{\star}_{x}L
, and we write the isomorphism induced by the Levi-Civita connection as

(9)

\begin{split}\Gamma:T_{p}(T^{\star}L)\to T_{x}L\oplus T_{x}^{\star}L.\end{split}

Let
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be a local coordinate system of
L
, and
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
by the fiber coordinate of
T_{x}^{\star}L
, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}dx_{i}
is a section of
T^{\star}_{x}L
. Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection with
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\partial_{x_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{x_{k}}
, then for
\partial_{x_{i}}
and
d_{x_{i}}
, under the identification in (
9
), we write the horizontal and vertical lifting in local coordinates
(x,y)
as

(10)

\partial_{x_{i}}^{H}=\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}y_{j}\Gamma^{j}_{ik}\partial_{y_{k}},\;dx_{i}^{V}=\partial_{y_{i}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a 1-form on
L
, we write
\iota_{\alpha}:L\to T^{\star}L
be the inclusion induced by the graph of
\alpha
, then for any vector field
v
on
L
, we have
\Gamma\circ(\iota_{\alpha})_{\star}v=(v,\nabla_{v}\alpha)\in TL\oplus T^{\star}L
.

Proof.

Without losing generality, we take
v=\partial_{x_{i}}
and write
\alpha=\alpha_{i}dx_{i}
. We compute

(\iota_{\alpha})_{\star}\partial_{x_{i}}=\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}\alpha_{j}\partial_{y_{j}}=\partial_{x_{i}}^{H}+(\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\alpha)^{V},

thus
\Gamma((\iota_{\alpha})_{\star}\partial_{x_{i}})=(\partial_{x_{i}},\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\alpha).

∎

Let
X,Y
be vectors on
L
with horizontal lifting
X^{H},Y^{H}
and
\alpha,\beta
be 1-forms on
L
with vertical lifting
\alpha^{V},\beta^{V}
, then the Sasaki metric
g_{0}
on
T^{\star}L
is defined as

g_{0}(X^{H},Y^{H})=g(X,Y)\circ\pi,\;g_{0}(\alpha^{V},\beta^{V})=g^{-1}(\alpha,\beta)\circ\pi,\;g_{0}(X^{H},\alpha^{V})=0.

In addition, there exists a natural almost complex structure
J_{0}
on
T^{\star}L
. Let
\varphi_{g}:TL\to T^{\star}L
be the induced isomorphism of the vector bundle, then we define the almost complex structure as

J_{0}(X^{H}):=(\varphi_{g}(X))^{V},\;J_{0}(\varphi_{g}(X)^{V})=-X^{H},

where
X
is a vector field on
L
. Let
\omega_{0}
is the canonical symplectic structure on
T^{\star}L
, then by a straight forward computation, these three structures
g_{0},\omega_{0},J_{0}
are compatible:

\omega_{0}(X,Y)=g_{0}(X,J_{0}Y).

Using the Sasaki metric, we could give an isomorphism of the tangent and cotangent bundle of
T^{\star}L
, which we write as
\Phi_{g_{0}}:T(T^{\star}L)\to T^{\star}(T^{\star}L)
. As the decomposition
T(T^{\star}L)\cong H\oplus V
is orthogonal under the Sasaki metric and
T^{\star}(T^{\star}L)\cong H^{\star}\oplus V^{\star}
,
\Phi_{g_{0}}
preserve the decomposition and gives the identification
\Phi_{g_{0}}|_{H}:H\to H^{\star}
and
\Phi_{g_{0}}|_{V}:V\to V^{\star}
.

We define the dual basis to
\partial_{x_{i}}^{H}
,
dy_{i}
and compute

(11)

\begin{split}dx_{i}^{H}:&=g_{0}(\partial_{i}^{H},\;)=\sum_{j}g_{ij}dx_{j},\;\\
dy_{i}^{V}:&=g_{0}(dy_{i},\;)=\sum_{j}g^{ij}dy_{j}-\sum_{j,k}y_{k}\Gamma^{k}_{sj}g^{is}dx_{j}.\end{split}

3.3.
The pseudo holomorphic volume form

Now we will define a canonical n-form over
T^{\star}L
.

3.3.1.
The pseudo holomorphic volume form

We write
\xi:V\to T^{\star}L
be the identification and
\xi_{k}:=\Lambda^{k}\xi:\Lambda^{k}V\to\Lambda^{k}T^{\star}L
. We could also identify
T^{\star}L
and
H^{\star}
by the following map

(12)

\eta:T^{\star}L\rightarrow TL\rightarrow H\rightarrow H^{\star},

where the first map above is given by the Riemannian metric on
L
and the third map above is given by the Sasaki metric. A straight forward computation gives
\eta(dx_{i})=\pi^{\star}dx_{i}
, with projection
\pi:T^{\star}L\to L
. To avoid notation, we also write
\pi^{\star}dx_{i}
as
dx_{i}
. In addition, we define
\eta_{k}:=\Lambda^{k}\eta:\Lambda^{k}T^{\star}L\to\Lambda^{k}H^{\star}
.

Using
\xi_{k}
and
\eta_{n-k}
, we defines an endormorphism
\kappa_{k}
by

(13)

\kappa_{k}:\Lambda^{k}V\xrightarrow{\xi_{k}}\Lambda^{k}T^{\star}L\xrightarrow{\star}\Lambda^{n-k}T^{\star}L\xrightarrow{\eta_{n-k}}\Lambda^{n-k}H^{\star},

where
\star
is Hodge star operator induced by the Riemannian metric on
L
and we write
K_{k}
be the corresponding section of
\Lambda^{k}V^{\star}\otimes\Lambda^{n-k}H^{\star}
.

In local coordinates,
K_{k}
could be written as

(14)

K_{k}:=\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}}\frac{\sqrt{\det(g)}}{n!}\epsilon_{j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}dy^{V}_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy^{V}_{j_{p}}\wedge dx_{j_{p+1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{j_{n}},

where
\epsilon_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}}
is the Levi-Civita symbol. Moreover, as
\Lambda^{n}(T^{\star}(T^{\star}L))=\oplus_{k=1}^{n}\Lambda^{k}V^{\star}\otimes\Lambda^{n-k}H^{\star},

K_{k}
is a n-form on
T^{\star}L
.

Definition 3.2
.

The pseudo holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{0}\in\Lambda^{n}(T^{\star}(TL)\otimes\mathbb{C})
is defined by

(15)

\Im\Omega_{0}:=\sum_{k\;\mathrm{odd}}(-1)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}K_{k},\;\Re\Omega_{0}:=\sum_{k\;\mathrm{even}}(-1)^{\frac{k}{2}}K_{k},\;\Omega_{0}:=\Re\Omega_{0}+i\Im\Omega_{0}.

Proposition 3.3
.

The pseudo holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{0}
has the following properties:

(i)

Let
\iota_{0}:L\to T^{\star}L
be the inclusion of the zero section, then
\iota_{0}^{\star}\Im\Omega_{0}=0,\;\iota_{0}^{\star}\Re\Omega_{0}=\mathrm{Vol}_{L}.

(ii)

Suppose at
m\in L
,
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
are normal coordinates w.r.t. the Riemannian metric on
L
, then at
q=(m,0)\in T^{\star}L
,
\Omega_{0}|_{q}=\Lambda_{i=1}^{n}(dx_{i}+\sqrt{-1}dy_{i}).

(iii)

\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\frac{i^{n}}{2^{n}}\Omega_{0}\wedge\bar{\Omega}_{0}
on the zero section.

(iv)

d\Omega_{0}|_{\iota_{0}(L)}=0
.

Proof.

For (i), as
\iota_{0}^{\star}dy_{i}^{V}=0
and every term of
\Im\Omega
consists of
dy_{i}^{V}
, we obtain
\iota_{0}^{\star}\Im\Omega=0
. In addition, we have
\iota_{0}^{\star}\Im\Omega=\iota_{0}^{\star}K_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\det(g)}}{n!}\epsilon_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}dx_{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{k_{n}}
, which is the volume form on
L
. As in the normal coordinates
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
, we have
dy_{i}^{V}|_{q}=dy_{i}
, which implies (ii). (iii) follows directly from (ii). For (iv), in the normal coordinates above, we obtain
d(dy_{i}^{V})|_{q}=dg^{ij}\wedge dy_{j}|_{q}-d(y_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}g^{is})\wedge dx_{j}|_{q}=0
. Therefore,
d\Omega_{0}|_{\iota_{0}(L)}=d(\mathrm{Vol}_{X})|_{\iota_{0}(L)}=0.

∎

3.3.2.
The pseudo special Lagrangian equation

Given a closed 1-form
\alpha
, we write
[L_{\alpha}]=(L,\iota_{\alpha})
be the graph of
\alpha
. Using the pseudo holomorphic volume form, we could define the pseudo special Lagrangian equation.

Definition 3.4
.

[L_{\alpha}]
is called a pseudo special Lagrangian submanifold if
[L_{\alpha}]
is symplectic and
i_{\alpha}^{\star}(\Im\Omega)=0
.
Moreover, we define the pseudo special Lagrangian equation for 1-form
\alpha
as
\mathrm{pSL}(\alpha):=\star\iota_{0}^{\star}(\Im\Omega),

where
\star
is the Hodge star operator w.r.t the induced Riemannian metric on the zero section

We could explicitly compute pseudo special Lagrangian equation. Let
\alpha
be a closed 1-form on
L
, then
\nabla\alpha
is a symmetric
(0,2)
tensor, we could use the Riemannian metric to define a
(1,1)
tensor
A_{\alpha}:TL\to TL
. We define the
k
-th symmetric power of
A_{\alpha}
as
P_{k}(\nabla\alpha):=\mathrm{Tr}(\Lambda^{k}A_{\alpha})
. If we choose a orthonormal base of
T^{\star}L
, we could regard
\nabla\alpha
as a
n\times n
matrix, then we have

\det(\mathrm{Id}+t\nabla\alpha)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}(\nabla\alpha)t^{k}.

In addition, we define

(16)

P(\nabla\alpha):=\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\rceil}(-1)^{k}P_{2k+1}(\nabla\alpha).

Proposition 3.5
.

The pseudo special Lagrangian equation of
[L_{\alpha}]
on
T^{\star}L
can be written as

(17)

\begin{split}\mathrm{pSL}(\alpha)=-d^{\star}\alpha+P(\nabla\alpha),\end{split}

where
P
is a smooth function defined in (
16
).

Proof.

We will compute in local coordinates
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
under previous conventions. The linearization of
\mathrm{pSL}(\alpha)
would be
\star(\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}K_{1})
, where

K_{1}=\sum_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{\det g}}{n!}\epsilon_{k_{1}\cdots k_{n}}dy_{k_{1}}^{V}\wedge dx_{k_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{k_{n}}.

We write
\alpha=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}dx_{k}
, then by a straight computation, we found

\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}dy_{i}^{V}=\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}g^{il}(\partial_{x_{j}}\alpha_{l}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\Gamma^{k}_{lj})dx_{j}=\sum_{j,l=1}^{n}g^{il}\nabla\alpha(\partial_{x_{j}},\partial_{x_{l}})dx_{j}.

Therefore,
\star(\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}K_{1})=-d^{\star}\alpha
.

For the non-linear terms, we define
H^{i}_{j}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}g^{ik}\nabla\alpha(\partial_{x_{j}},\partial_{x_{k}})
, then we compute

\star(\iota_{f}^{\star}K_{2l+1})=\sum\epsilon_{k_{1}\cdots k_{2l+1}}\epsilon_{j_{1}\cdots j_{2l+1}}H^{k_{1}}_{j_{1}}H^{k_{2}}_{j_{2}}\cdots H^{k_{2l+1}}_{j_{2l+1}}=P_{2l+1}(\nabla\alpha),

which is the desire term.
∎

When
L=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T^{\star}L\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
. When we associating
T^{\star}L
with the canonical Calabi-Yau structure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the pseudo special Lagrangian equation will coincident with (
8
).

3.3.3.
The relationship between the holomorphic volume forms.

Let
[L]
be a Lagrangian submanifold of a Calabi-Yau
X
, we could choose
U_{L}\subset T^{\star}L
be the Weinstein neighborhood of
[L]
with the pull-back Calabi-Yau structure
(U_{L},J,\omega,\Omega)
with Calabi-Yau metric
g
. We also write the zero section as
L_{0}
. Using the induced metric
\iota_{0}^{\star}g
on the zero section, we could define a Sasaki metric
g_{0}
on
U_{L}
together with almost complex structure
J_{0}
and the pseudo holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{0}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

(i)

Over the zero section
L_{0}\subset U_{L}
, we have
g_{0}|_{L_{0}}=g|_{L_{0}}
.

(ii)

Let
m\in L_{0}
, we write
T_{m}^{\perp}L
be the orthogonal complement of
T_{m}L
in
T_{x}U
w.r.t
g
, then
T_{m}^{\perp}L
is also a Lagrangian subspace of
\omega
.

(iii)

Let
\theta
be the Lagrangian angle of
[L]
, then
\Omega|_{L_{0}}=e^{i\theta}\Omega_{0}|_{L_{0}}
.

Proof.

Let
m\in L_{0}
, we choose normal coordinate
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
of an open set of
L
centered at
m
with fiber coordinates
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
, we will check the statement in this coordinate system. As
\iota_{0}^{\star}g=\iota_{0}^{\star}g_{0}
,
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
is a normal coordinate for both metrics. For (i), based on the definition of the Sasaki metric,
\{\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}},\partial_{y_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{y_{n}}\}
is a orthonormal frame on
T|_{m}(T^{\star}L)
for
g_{0}
and
\{\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}},J\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,J\partial_{x_{n}}\}
is an orthonormal frame for
g
. As
L_{0}
is a Lagrangian submanifold, we have
g(J\partial_{x_{i}},\partial_{x_{j}})=0
. Thus,
J\partial_{x_{i}}
lies in the plane span by
\{\partial_{y_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{y_{n}}\}

\omega
is the canonical symplectic structure, we have
g(\partial_{x_{i}},J\partial_{y_{j}})=-\omega(\partial_{x_{i}},\partial_{y_{j}})=-\delta_{ij}
, which implies

. Therefore,
\{\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{x_{n}},\partial_{y_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{y_{n}}\}

, we obtain (ii). As

∎

3.4.
The special Lagrangian equation in a neighborhood of a Lagrangian

Let
[L]
be a Lagrangian submanifold in Calabi-Yau,
(U_{L},J,\omega,\Omega)
be the pull-back Calabi-Yau structure in the Weinstein neighborhood of
[L]
with
g
the Calabi-Yau metric. Let
\alpha
be a 1-form on
U_{L}
with
\iota_{\alpha}:L\to U_{L}
be the graph manifold defined by
\alpha
and we write
L_{0}
be the inclusion of the zero section in
U_{L}\subset T^{\star}L
with the inlusion
\iota_{0}
.

The graph of
\alpha
is a Lagrangian submanifold if and only if
\alpha
is closed. In addition, the special Lagrangian equation for
\alpha
is defined as

(18)

\begin{split}\mathrm{SL}(\alpha):=\star\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega,\end{split}

where
\star
is the Hodge star operator with the pull-back metric
\iota_{0}^{\star}g
on
L
. Taking the Hodge star operator for the metric
\iota^{\star}g
instead of
\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}g
is a more natural metric to consider, which makes the coefficients of the equations independent of
\alpha
.

Proposition 3.7
.

[
25
, Prop 2.21]

The special Lagrangian equation could be written as

(19)

\begin{split}\mathrm{SL}(\alpha)=\sin\theta-d^{\star}(\cos\theta\alpha)+Q(\alpha,\nabla\alpha),\end{split}

where
Q(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)
is a smooth function with
|Q(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)|=\mathcal{O}(|\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\alpha|^{2})
for small
\alpha

Proof.

Let
\theta
be the Lagrangian degree defined in (
2
), then we have

\mathrm{SL}(0)=\star\iota_{0}^{\star}\Im\Omega=\star\Im(e^{i\theta}\mathrm{Vol}_{\iota_{0}^{\star}g})=\sin\theta.

By a similar computation as in (
6
), we obtain
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathrm{SL}(t\alpha)=-d^{\star}(\cos\theta\alpha)
and the proposition follows straightforward.
∎

Now, we will focus on the case when
[L]
is a special Lagrangian submanifold, while the following observations are due to Joyce
[
26
, Page 15]
. The value of
\mathrm{SL}(\alpha)
at
x\in L
depends on the
T^{\star}_{p}(\iota_{\alpha}(L))
, where
p=\iota_{\alpha}(x)
, and the cotangent bundle depends on
\alpha|_{x}
and
\nabla\alpha|_{x}
. Therefore,
\mathrm{SL}(\alpha)|_{x}
depends pointwisely on
\alpha|_{x}
and
\nabla\alpha|_{x}
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
be the pseudo holomorphic volume form, we define a n-form over
U_{L}
as

(20)

T:=\Im\Omega-\Im\Omega_{0},

then by Proposition
3.6
,
T|_{L_{0}}=0
. The error term
S
of the quadratic terms of the special Lagrangian equation could be expressed as

S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha):=\star\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}T,

as
S
depends on the tangent space of the graph manifold of
\alpha
,
S
is a smooth function of both
\alpha
and
\nabla\alpha
.

After fixing
x\in L
, define variable
y\in T_{x}^{\star}L
and
z\in\otimes^{2}T_{x}^{\star}L
. Under the identification
\Gamma:T_{(x,y)}U_{L}\to T_{x}L\oplus T_{x}^{\star}L
, for any
z\in\otimes^{2}T_{x}^{\star}L
, we define the map

I_{z}:T_{x}L\to T_{x}L\oplus T_{x}^{\star}L,\;I_{z}(y):=(y,\iota_{y}z).

We define

(21)

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}:\{(x,y,z):x\in L,\;y\in T_{x}^{\star}L,\;z\in\otimes^{2}T_{x}^{\star}L\}\to\mathbb{R},\;\\
&\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}(x,y,z):=\star I_{z}^{\star}(\Gamma^{-1})^{\star}\Im\Omega|_{(x,y)}.\end{split}

Similarly, we define

(22)

\begin{split}&\tilde{S}:\{(x,y,z):x\in L,\;y\in T^{\star}_{x}L,\;z\in\otimes^{2}T^{\star}_{x}L\}\to\mathbb{R},\\
&\tilde{S}(x,y,z):=\star I_{z}^{\star}(\Gamma^{-1})^{\star}T|_{(x,y)}.\end{split}

After fixing
x\in L
, the variable
y,z
lies in the vector spaces
T_{x}^{\star}L
and
\otimes^{2}T_{x}^{\star}L
. So we could take partial derivative in
y
and
z
direction without using a connection. Then we have

\partial_{y}^{k_{1}}\partial_{z}^{k_{2}}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}|_{x},\;\partial_{y}^{k_{1}}\partial_{z}^{k_{2}}\tilde{S}|_{x}\in S^{k_{1}}T_{x}L\otimes S^{k_{2}}(\otimes^{2}T_{x}L).

We have the following direct descriptions for
\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}
and
\tilde{S}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}(x,\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=\mathrm{SL}(\alpha)|_{x},\;\tilde{S}(x,\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)|_{x}
. Suppose
[L]
is a special Lagrangian submanifold, then
\tilde{\mathrm{SL}},\tilde{S}
are real analytic functions.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, we compute
I_{\nabla\alpha}(y)=(y,\nabla_{y}\alpha)=\Gamma\circ(\iota_{\alpha})_{\star}y.
Therefore, we compute

\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}(x,\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=\star I_{\nabla\alpha}^{\star}(\Gamma^{-1})^{\star}\Im\Omega=\star(\Gamma^{-1}\circ I_{\nabla\alpha})^{\star}\Im\Omega=\star\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega,

while a similar computation holds for
\tilde{S}
.

As
\Omega
is holomorphic, over
U_{L}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}
is real analytic. When
[L]=(L,\iota)
is a special Lagrangian submanifold, by
[
38
]
,
\iota^{\star}g_{X}
is a real analytic metric on
L
. Thus, the pseudo holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{0}
and
T=\Omega-\Omega_{0}
are real analytic forms. Therefore,
\tilde{S}
is also a real analytic function.
∎

The following proposition explains the structure of the special Lagrangian equation.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
[L]
be a special Lagrangian submanifold, over the Weinstein neighborhood
(U_{L},J,\omega,\Omega)
with Calabi-Yau metric

, then the following holds:

(i)

the special Lagrangian equation for a closed 1-form
\alpha
could be written as

(23)

\begin{split}\mathrm{SL}(\alpha):=\star\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega=-d^{\star}(\alpha)+P(\nabla\alpha)+S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha),\end{split}

where
\star
is the Hodge star operator on the zero section,
P
is a real analytic function of
\nabla\alpha
defined in (
16
) and
S
is a real analytic function of
\alpha,\nabla\alpha
.

(ii)

Let
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be a coordinate on a neighborhood center at
m\in L_{0}
with fiber coordinates
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
. We define a function
A_{ij}=\langle\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\alpha,\partial_{x_{j}}\rangle
with
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
g
, then we could write

(24)

S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}\neq i_{2}\cdots\neq i_{k}\leq n\\
1\leq j_{1}\neq j_{2}\cdots\neq j_{k}\leq n\end{subarray}}F_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}(\alpha)A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{k}j_{k}},

where
F_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}(\alpha)
are real analytic functions of
\alpha
. In addition,
F_{0}(0)=F_{0}^{\prime}(0)=0
and
F_{i_{1}j_{1}}(0)=0
for any
1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq n
.

Proof.

For (i), as
\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega=\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega_{0}+\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}\Im T
, and
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\iota_{t\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\iota_{t\alpha}^{\star}\Im\Omega_{0}
, we could write

Q(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=P(\nabla\alpha)+S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha).

By Proposition
3.8
,
P,S
are real analytic functions of
\alpha
and
\nabla\alpha
.

For (ii), as
\alpha
is closed, we have
A_{ij}=A_{ji}
. Over a neighborhood with coordinate
(x,y)
, we could write

T=\sum T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{n-k}}(x,y)dy_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_{i_{k}}\wedge dx_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{j_{n-k}},

where
T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{n-k}}
is real analytic function with variable
(x,y)
. Then for the pull-back, we compute

\begin{split}&\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}dy_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}\alpha_{i}dx_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{x_{j}}\alpha_{i})dx_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(A_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\Gamma_{ij}^{k})dx_{j},\\
&\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}dx_{i}=dx_{i},\;(\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{n-k}})|_{x}=T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{n-k}}(x,\alpha|_{x}).\end{split}

In addition, as
S(0,0)=0
and
S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=\star\iota_{\alpha}^{\star}T
,
S
could be written as the form

S(\alpha,\nabla\alpha)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}\neq i_{2}\cdots\neq i_{k}\leq n\\
1\leq j_{1}\neq j_{2}\cdots\neq j_{k}\leq n\end{subarray}}F_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}(\alpha)A_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{k}j_{k}}.

In addition, as
\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}S(t\alpha,t\nabla\alpha)=0
. For the
k=0
term
F_{0}
in the above expression, we have
F_{0}(0)=F_{0}^{\prime}(0)=0
and for any
k=1
terms, we have
F_{i_{1}j_{1}}(0)=0
for any
1\leq i_{1},j_{1}\leq n
.

∎

4.
Multivalued harmonic function and the analytic theory

In this section, we will introduce the analytic theory for the multivalued harmonic function developed in
[
8
]
. The analytic aspects of the multivalued harmonic functions have been widely studied, we also refer
[
47
]
for different settings.

4.1.
Multivalued harmonic function and multivalued harmonic 1-form

Let
(L,g)
be a compact oriented Riemannian manifold,
\Sigma\subset L
be a smooth embedded oriented codimensional two submanifold. Let
\Xi
be the group of isomorphism of the real line, which satisfies the exact sequence

0\to(\mathbb{R},+)\to\Xi\to\{\pm 1\}\to 0.

Let
\chi:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\Xi
be a representation such that for any small loop linking
\Sigma
,
\chi
maps to a reflection. We write
V^{-}
be the associate affine
\mathbb{R}
bundle defined on
L\setminus\Sigma
and
\mathcal{I}
be the vertical line bundle of
V^{-}
. Therefore, a section
V^{-}
could locally be regarded as a two-valued function.

The composition of
\chi
with the quotient map
\Xi\to\{\pm 1\}
defines a representation
\chi^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\{\pm\}
with associate flat vector bundle
\mathcal{I}
which is vertical tangent bundle of
V^{-}
. Using the flat structure on
V^{-}
and the Riemannian metric on
L
, the Laplacian operator make sense as

\Delta_{g}:\Gamma(V^{-})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{I}).

Definition 4.1
.

A multivalued harmonic function consists of
(\Sigma,\chi,f)
, where

(i)

\Sigma
is an oriented smooth embedded codimension 2 submanifold of
L
,

(ii)

\chi
is a representation
\chi:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\Xi
such that for any small loop linking
\Sigma
,
\chi
maps to a reflection,

(iii)

if we write
V^{-}
be the associative affine bundle of
\chi
with induced flat structure, then
f
is a section of
V^{-}
such that
\Delta_{g}f=0
, where
\Delta_{g}
is defined w.r.t. the flat structure on
V^{-}
,

(iv)

as a section of
\mathcal{I}
,
df\in L^{2}
.

As
df
might be considered as a
\mathcal{I}
valued 1-form, which leads to the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

A multivalued harmonic 1-form
\alpha\in L^{2}
is a triple
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
consisting of

(i)

a flat
\mathbb{R}
bundle
\mathcal{I}
with holonomy
-1
on small loop linking
\Sigma
,

(ii)

a section
\alpha\in\Gamma(L\setminus\Sigma,T^{\star}L\otimes\mathcal{I})
such that
d\alpha=d\star\alpha=0
, where
d
and
d\star
are taken w.r.t. the flat structure of
\mathcal{I}
.

In addition,
\alpha
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
harmonic 1-form if
|\alpha|
is bounded near
\Sigma
and
\|\alpha\|_{L^{2}}=1.

A model example of multivalued function would be
L=\mathbb{C}
,
\Sigma=\{0\}
,
f=\Re(z^{k+\frac{1}{2}})
with
k\geq 1
and
v=df=\frac{2k+1}{2}\Re(z^{k-\frac{1}{2}}dz)
. We could either think of
v
as a two valued 1-from over
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
or a section of a
\mathbb{R}
vector bundle which has monodromy
-1
along loops linking
\{0\}
.

In general, the multivalued harmonic 1-form are purely topological and we will briefly review the construction in
[
8
]
.

The kernel of
\chi^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\{\pm 1\}
is a index two normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)
, which gives us a covering
p:L^{\prime}\to L\setminus\Sigma
. In addition, the deck transformation defines an involution on
L^{\prime}
. We choose a Riemannian metric on
L
and write
N_{\Sigma}
be the normal bundle. As
\Sigma
is oriented, using the co-orientation,
N_{\Sigma}
is a complex vector bundle and the flat
\mathcal{I}
bundle defines a square root
N_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Using the normal exponential map, a neighborhood of
\Sigma
in
L
could be identified with a neighborhood of the zero section in
N_{\Sigma}
.

We write
\tilde{L}:=L^{\prime}\cup\Sigma
and we could associate a smooth structure on
\tilde{L}
. Let
U
be an open set of
m\in\Sigma
with trivialization
N_{\Sigma}|_{U}\cong U\times\mathbb{C}
. Let
\tilde{x}=(\tilde{x}_{3},\cdots,\tilde{x}_{n})
be the coordinate on
U
,
\tilde{z}
be the coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
, then for
\tilde{z}\neq 0
, the covering map is locally given by

p:U\times(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})\to U\times(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}),\;(\tilde{z},\tilde{x})\to(\tilde{z}^{2},\tilde{x}),

which naturally extends to the zero section and gives
\tilde{L}
a smooth structure. In addition, the involution on
L^{\prime}
extends naturally to an involution
\sigma
on
\tilde{L}
with fixed point the branch locus. To save notation, we also write
\Sigma
be the branch locus on
\tilde{L}
.

A neighborhood of
\Sigma\subset\tilde{L}
could be identified with a neighborhood of the zero section of
N_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Without loss of generality, we could assume that
\sigma
acts on each fibre of
N_{\Sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}
by changing the sign and
\Sigma
is the fixed point of the involution.

Let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
be a multivalued harmonic 1-form and with
p:\tilde{L}\to L
the double branched covering constructed above. Based on our assumptions in Definition
4.2
,
p^{\star}\mathcal{I}
is trivial on
\tilde{L}\setminus\Sigma
and
p^{\star}\alpha
is a 1-form over
\tilde{L}
. As
p
is only Lipschitz along
\Sigma
,
p^{\star}\alpha
will not be a smooth 1-from and the pull-back Riemannian metric
p^{\star}g
will be a Lipschitz metric in a neighborhood of
\Sigma\subset\tilde{L}
. As
\alpha
is a harmonic section,
p^{\star}\alpha
will satisfy the harmonic equations for the pull-back metric
p^{\star}g
.

By
[
50
, Lemma 1.5]
, the space of
L^{2}
cohomology

\{\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})|\alpha\in L^{2},\;d\alpha=d\star_{p^{\star}g}\alpha=0\;\mathrm{over}\;\tilde{L}\setminus Z\}

is naturally isomorphism to the singular cohomology
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
. From another side, the involution
\sigma:\tilde{L}\to\tilde{L}
induces a decomposition
H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})=H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{+}\oplus H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
, while
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{\pm}
is the
\pm 1
eigenvalue of
\sigma^{\star}
. Therefore,
p^{\star}\alpha
represents an element in
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
with
\sigma^{\star}p^{\star}\alpha=-p^{\star}\alpha
.

Given an element
[\delta]\in H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
, one could find a harmonic representative
\alpha_{\delta}
and there exists a harmonic affine bundle section with
\alpha_{\delta}=df
. Moreover, the representation
\chi:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\Xi
is up to conjugate determined by
\chi^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\{\pm 1\}
and
[\delta]\in H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
.

We have the following proposition proved by Donaldson
[
8
]
, which is a version of the Hodge theorem for
p^{\star}g
.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
8
, Section 4]

Given
\chi:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\Xi
satisfies (ii) of Definition
4.1
, then there exists an unique harmonic section
f
of
V^{-}
. In addition,
\sigma^{\star}\pi^{\star}df=-\pi^{\star}df
.

We synthesize all of the above discussions to arrive at the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

[
8
, Page 2]

Let
\Sigma
be an oriented embedded codimensional 2 smooth submanifold, the following four concepts are equivalent :

(i)

a representation
\chi:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\Xi
such that for any small loop linking
\Sigma
,
\chi
maps to a reflection,

(ii)

a multivalued harmonic function
(\Sigma,V^{-},f)
,

(iii)

a multivalued harmonic 1-form
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
,

(iv)

a double branched covering
\tilde{L}\to L
branched along a codimension 2 submanifold
\Sigma
together with
[\delta]\in H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
.

4.2.
Topological constraints for multivalued 1-form

In this subsection, we will discuss the topology for the branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
and the constraint for the existence of multivalued 1-form.

Let
\sigma
be the involution on
\tilde{L}
,
C_{\star}(L),C_{\star}(\tilde{L})
be the singular chains on
L
and
\tilde{L}
, then the transformation morphism for the branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
is a map of singular chain

T:C_{\star}(L)\to C_{\star}(\tilde{L}),\;T(\gamma)=\tilde{\gamma}+\sigma^{\star}\tilde{\gamma},

where
\gamma
is a small enough chain and
\tilde{\gamma}
is any lifting of
\gamma
. In other word, the transformation morphism map a singular chain to the sum of two distinct lifts.

Lemma 4.5
.

\mathrm{Im}\;p^{\star}\subset H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{+}
and
p^{\star}:H^{i}(L;\mathbb{R})\to H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{+}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

As
p\circ\sigma=p
, for
x\in H^{i}(L;\mathbb{R})
, we have
{p}^{\star}x\in H^{i}(L;\mathbb{R})^{+}
. Let
T_{\star}:H_{i}(L;\mathbb{R})\to H_{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
be the induce map of the transformation map, then we have
p_{\star}\circ T_{\star}=2
and
T_{\star}\circ p_{\star}|_{H_{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{+}}=2.
Therefore,
p^{\star}
is both injective and surjective thus induces an isomorphism.
∎

Let
b_{1}(\tilde{L})^{\pm}:=\dim H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{\pm}
, then we have
b_{1}(\tilde{L})=b_{1}(\tilde{L})^{+}+b_{1}(\tilde{L})^{-}
with
b_{1}(\tilde{L})^{+}=b_{1}(L)
. Combing with the Hodge theorem for the pull-back
L^{2}
metric, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose there exists a multivalued harmonic function
(\Sigma,\chi,f)
over
L
, then for the double branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
, we have
b_{1}(\tilde{L})>b_{1}(L)
.

We could construct multivalued harmonic 1-form in the following way.

Example 4.7
.

Let
(L,g)
be a Riemannian 3-dimensional rational homology sphere and
\Sigma
be an oriented link of
L
, there exists a canonical morphism
\mu:\pi_{1}(L\setminus\Sigma)\to\{\pm 1\}
, which defines a double branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
branched along
\Sigma
. We refer to
[
31
, Chapter 7]
for details of this construction. By
[
31
, Corollary 9.2]
, suppose the determinant of the link is zero, then
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})\neq 0
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}=H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
. By
L^{2}
Hodge theorem, for the Riemannian metric
p^{\star}g
, for any
[\delta]\in H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
, we could find a harmonic representative
\alpha_{[\delta]}
that gives a multivalued harmonic 1-form on
L
.

As the determinant of a link is zero implies the link has more than one component, Haydys
[
15
]
showed that the branch locus
\Sigma
in this case is disconnected. In general, let
(\Sigma,\mathcal{I},\alpha)
be a multivalued harmonic 1-form, then by Proposition
4.4
,
H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}>0
, which give constraints of the topological type of
\Sigma
.

Lemma 4.8
.

[
30
]

There exists a long exact sequence

(25)

\begin{split}\cdots\rightarrow H_{i}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{T}H_{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{p_{\star}}H_{i}(L;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{\partial_{\star}}H_{i-1}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow\cdots,\end{split}

where
T
is the transformation map.

Proof.

We consider the chain with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients. As
T_{\star}|_{C_{\star}(\Sigma)}=0
, we have short exact sequence

0\to C_{\star}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{T_{\star}}C_{\star}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{p_{\star}}C_{\star}(L;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to 0,

which implies the long exact sequence.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose there exists a multivalued harmonic 1-form on
L
with
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})=0
, then
\Sigma
has to be disconnected.

Proof.

Let
k
be the number of connected components, by the long exact sequence of relative homology of
(L,\Sigma)
, we obtain

\to H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{0}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{0}(L;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{0}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

As
H_{1}(L;\mathbb{Z})=0
, we obtain
H_{1}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}.
By (
25
), we obtain

\to H_{1}(L,\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\xrightarrow{T_{\star}}H_{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to 0.

Suppose
\Sigma
is connected,
k=1
, then
H_{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{Z}_{2})=0
which implies
b_{1}(\tilde{L})=0
. However, this violates the existence of multivalued harmonic 1-form.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
L
be a rational homology 3-sphere, then for
i=1,2
and
\alpha\in H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
, we have
\sigma^{\star}\alpha=-\alpha
. In particular, the rational cohomology ring structure of
\tilde{L}
is trivial.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
, for
i=1,2
, we have
H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})\cong H^{i}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})^{-}
. For
\alpha,\beta\in H^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
, we compute
-\alpha\cup\beta=\sigma^{\star}(\alpha\cup\beta)=\sigma^{\star}\alpha\cup\sigma^{\star}\beta=\alpha\cup\beta,
thus
\alpha\cup\beta=0
.
∎

4.3.
The differential forms on the branched covering

Let
p:\tilde{L}\to L
be the branched covering map and
\sigma
be the involution map defined on
\tilde{L}
. The involution induces a decomposition of
k
-forms

\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})=\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})^{-}\oplus\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})^{+},

where
\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})^{+}\cong\Omega^{k}(L),\;\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})^{-}\cong\Omega^{k}(L,\mathcal{I})
, and
\Omega^{k}(L,\mathcal{I})
is
\mathcal{I}
valued k-form on
L\setminus\Sigma
. Therefore, given any
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Omega^{k}(\tilde{L})
, we could write
\tilde{\alpha}=p^{\star}\alpha^{+}+p^{\star}\alpha^{-}
where
\alpha^{+}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})
and
\alpha^{-}\in\Omega^{k}(L,\mathcal{I})
. We define
\mathfrak{a}:=\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}
and
p^{\star}\mathfrak{a}=\tilde{\alpha}.
Moreover, as
\alpha^{-}
could be understood as a two valued 1-form over
L
, we could also regard
\mathfrak{a}
be a two valued 1-form on
L
. We usually call
\mathfrak{a}
as a pair as
\mathfrak{a}
consists of two parts.

Near the branch locus, let
m\in\Sigma\subset L
with
U\subset L
a neighborhood of
m
, we choose
x=(z=x_{1}+\sqrt{-1}x_{2},x_{3},\cdots,x_{n})
such that
\Sigma\cap U=\{z=0\}
. Let
\tilde{m}=p^{-1}(m)
and we could choose a coordinate system
(\tilde{z}=\tilde{x}_{1}+\sqrt{-1}\tilde{x}_{2},\tilde{x}_{3},\cdots,\tilde{x}_{n})
on
\tilde{U}=p^{-1}(U)
such that
p
is given by

\begin{split}p(\tilde{z},\tilde{x}_{3},\cdots,\tilde{x}_{n})=(\tilde{z}^{2},\tilde{x}_{3},\cdots,\tilde{x}_{n}).\end{split}

We compute

p^{\star}dz=2\tilde{z}d\tilde{z},\;p^{\star}(z^{k-\frac{1}{2}}dz)=2\tilde{z}^{2k}d\tilde{z},\;p^{\star}dx_{i}=d\tilde{x}_{i}.

We have an unfavorable situation when
k=0
, where the pullback would be
p^{\star}(z^{-\frac{1}{2}}dz)=2d\tilde{z}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})^{-}
.

For
\tilde{\alpha}=p^{\star}\mathfrak{a}=p^{\star}\alpha^{+}+p^{\star}\alpha^{-}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L})
, suppose
\alpha^{-}
is bounded, then by the assumption on the holonomy of the bundle, we have
|\alpha^{-}||_{\Sigma}=0
and
\tilde{\alpha}|_{m}=p^{\star}\alpha^{+}|_{m}
. Recall that
p^{\star}
is an isomorphism over
L\setminus\Sigma
, for
\tilde{\alpha}
will corresponding
\alpha^{-}
bounded, we could define the following inclusion

(26)

\iota_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\iota_{\mathfrak{a}}:\tilde{L}\to T^{\star}L,\;\iota_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\tilde{x})=\iota_{\mathfrak{a}}:=(p(\tilde{x}),(p^{\star})^{-1}\alpha|_{\tilde{x}}).

Geometrically,
\iota_{\tilde{\alpha}}
will be just the graph of the two-valued 1-forms
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha^{+}+\alpha^{-}
and we defines as follows.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\iota:\tilde{L}\to T^{\star}L
be an inclusion with image
\Gamma_{\iota}:=\iota(\tilde{L})\subset T^{\star}L
. We call
\Gamma_{\iota}
a 2-valued graph if

(i)

for all
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{L}
,we have
\pi\circ\iota(\tilde{x})=\pi\circ\iota(\sigma(\tilde{x}))
,

(ii)

\iota(\tilde{x})=\iota\circ\sigma(\tilde{x})
if and only if
\tilde{x}\in\Sigma
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Given a 2-valued graph
\Gamma_{\iota}
, there exists
\tilde{\alpha}\in\Omega^{1}(\tilde{L};\mathbb{R})
such that
\Gamma_{\iota}
is the graph of
\tilde{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in L
, then we could write
p^{-1}(x)=\{\tilde{x},\sigma(\tilde{x})\}
. We define
\alpha^{+}|_{x}=\frac{\iota(\tilde{x})+\iota(\sigma(\tilde{x}))}{2}
, then
\alpha^{+}
is a well-defined 1-form on
L
. Using the linear structure on each fiber of
T^{\star}L
, we could define a shift of graph
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma-\alpha^{+}
. To be more explicit, we define
\iota^{\prime}
as

\iota^{\prime}:\tilde{L}\to T^{\star}L,\;\iota^{\prime}(\tilde{x}):=\iota(\tilde{x})-\alpha^{+}|_{p(\tilde{x})},

which satisfies
\iota^{\prime}(\tilde{x})=-\iota^{\prime}(\sigma(\tilde{x}))
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
will be the image of
\iota^{\prime}
The condition
\iota^{\prime}(\tilde{x})=-\iota^{\prime}(\sigma(\tilde{x}))
implies that
\iota^{\prime}=\iota_{p^{\star}\alpha^{-}}
for some
\alpha^{-}\in\Omega^{1}(L,\mathcal{I})
. In addition, we have
\iota=\iota_{\tilde{\alpha}}
, with
\tilde{\alpha}:=p^{\star}\alpha^{+}+p^{\star}\alpha^{-}
.
∎

4.4.
Norms of multivalued forms.

In this subsection, we will discuss functional spaces that will be used in our paper, for more details, we refer to Donaldson
[
8
]
. Let
(L,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
\Sigma
be an embedded codimension 2 submanifold,
\mathcal{I}
be a flat
\mathbb{R}
bundle statisfies Definition
4.2
with branched covering
p:\tilde{L}\to L
.

4.4.1.
Norms on
\mathcal{I}
valued forms.

Let
U
be a open neighborhood of a point
m\in\Sigma
in
L
and
U^{\prime}
be an open neighborhood of the zero section of
N_{\Sigma}
, then for a suitable choice of
U
and
U^{\prime}
, there exists a diffeomorphism
z:U\to U^{\prime}
which is the inverse of the normal exponential map. The co-orientation of
\Sigma
and the real line bundle
\mathcal{I}
makes
N_{\Sigma}
a complex vector bundle with square root. If
p
is a half integer, for
\sigma_{p}\in\Gamma(N_{\Sigma}^{-p})
,
\sigma_{p}z^{p}
is a section of the complexfied bundle
N_{\Sigma}
over
U\setminus\Sigma
. Let
t=(t_{3},\cdots,t_{n})

5.1.
Examples of branched deformations

To warm up, we introduce some toy examples of the branched deformation of special Lagrangian submanifolds when the background Calabi-Yau is hyperKähler, where we could use hyperKähler rotation to produce examples.

5.1.1.
Local model for the branched deformation

We start with a toy model for Question
1.1
of the branched deformation. Let
\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the two dimensional complex plane with complex coordinates

\begin{split}&g=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dy_{1}^{2}+dy_{2}^{2},\\

The defining equations of the graph manifold of

\iota_{t\tilde{\alpha}}:\tilde{L}\to U_{L}(s)
be the family of submanifolds defined by the graph of

In addition, by (
34

\begin{split}\int_{\tilde{L}}\mathrm{SL}(\tilde{\alpha})d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}=\int_{\tilde{L}}p^{\star}(\mathrm{SL}(\mathfrak{a})^{+})d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}+\int_{\tilde{L}}p^{\star}(\mathrm{SL}(\mathfrak{a})^{-})d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}.\end{split}

As
\sigma^{\star}d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}=d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}
and
\sigma^{\star}p^{\star}\mathrm{SL}(\mathfrak{a})^{-}=-p^{\star}\mathrm{SL}(\mathfrak{a})^{-}

, which implies
\int_{\tilde{L}}p^{\star}\mathrm{SL}(\alpha)^{+}d\mathrm{Vol}_{p^{\star}g}=0
. By definition, we obtain

∎

5.3.

In Example

\lambda^{\star}\mathrm{SL}_{g}(\mathfrak{a})=\lambda^{\star}(\star_{g}\iota_{\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}\Im\Omega)=\star_{\lambda^{\star}g}(\iota_{\mathfrak{a}}\circ\lambda)^{\star}\Im\Omega=\star_{\lambda^{\star}g}(\mu\circ\iota_{\lambda^{\star}\mathfrak{a}})^{\star}\Im\Omega=\mathrm{SL}_{\lambda^{\star}g}(\lambda^{\star}\mathfrak{a}).

In addition, we compute

\begin{split}\mathrm{SL}_{\lambda^{\star}g}(\mathfrak{a})=\lambda^{\star}\mathrm{SL}_{g}((\lambda^{-1})^{\star}\mathfrak{a})&=-\lambda^{\star}d^{\star_{g}}(\lambda^{-1})^{\star}\mathfrak{a}+\lambda^{\star}Q_{g}((\lambda^{-1})^{\star}\mathfrak{a})=-d^{\star_{\lambda^{\star}g}}\mathfrak{a}+Q_{\lambda^{\star}g}(\mathfrak{a}).\end{split}

∎

Now, we will estimate the differences between terms in the special Lagrangian equation under the variation.

Lemma 6.6
.

(i)

We write
g_{s}=\lambda_{s}^{\star}g
, then the following estimates holds,

\|\frac{d}{ds}g_{s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\gamma}},\;\|\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}g_{s}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}},\;\|\frac{d}{ds}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}},

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
is the Christoffel symbols w.r.t metric
g_{s}
and any smooth coordinates.

(ii)

Let
\alpha
be a n-form on
L
, then

(41)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\star_{g_{s}}\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{s}g_{s})\star_{s}\alpha.\end{split}

Proof.

(i) follows directly from the definition of Lie derivative on tensors. For (ii), let
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a coordinates on
L
,
\star_{s}
be the Hodge star operator for the metric
g_{s}
, then we write
\alpha=fdx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}
and
\star_{s}\alpha=|g_{s}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f
, where
|g_{s}|
is the determinant of the metric.

We compute

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\star_{s}\alpha=\frac{d}{ds}f|g_{s}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}|g_{s}|^{-\frac{3}{2}}\partial_{s}|g_{s}|f=-\frac{1}{2}|g_{s}|^{-1}\partial_{s}|g_{s}|\star_{s}\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{s}g_{s})\star_{s}\alpha,\end{split}

where we use
\partial_{s}|g_{s}|=|g_{s}|\mathrm{Tr}(g_{s}^{-1}\partial_{s}g_{s})
for the last identity.
∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Over the Riemannian manifold
(L,g)
, for
f
is either a function or a section of
\mathcal{I}
, we have the following expressions:

(42)

\Delta_{\lambda^{\star}g}f=\Delta_{g}f+\nabla_{v}(\Delta_{g}f)-\Delta_{g}(\nabla_{v}f)+e_{\Delta}(f,\lambda,g),

with

\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{\Delta}(f,\lambda,g)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\nabla f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}.

Proof.

For
x\in L
, we define
F(s,x):=\Delta_{\lambda_{s}^{\star}g}f
, then the Taylor expansion in terms of
s
would be

\Delta_{\lambda_{1}^{\star}g}f=\Delta_{g}f+\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\Delta_{\lambda_{s}^{\star}g}fs+e_{\Delta},

where
e_{\Delta}
is the integral reminder term which could be written as

e_{\Delta}=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{s}^{2}F(s,x)(1-s)ds=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{s}^{2}\Delta_{\lambda^{\star}g}fds.

As
\lambda_{s}^{\star}(\Delta_{g}f)=\Delta_{\lambda_{s}^{\star}g}\lambda_{s}^{\star}f
, we take derivative of
s
in both side at
s=0
, we obtain

(\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\Delta_{\lambda_{s}^{\star}g})f=\nabla_{v}(\Delta f)-\Delta(\nabla_{v}f).

Let
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
be local coordinate on
L
and now we compute
\partial_{s}^{2}F(s,x)
in this coordinate. We write
g=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j}
, then the Laplacian operator in this coordinate could be written as

\Delta_{g}f=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}-|g|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{i}(|g|^{\frac{1}{2}}g^{ij}\partial_{j}f)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}-\partial_{i}(g^{ij}\partial_{j}f)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\log|g|g^{ij}\partial_{j}f,

where
|g|=\det(g_{ij})
. We compute

(43)

\begin{split}\partial_{s}^{2}F(s,x)=&\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\Delta_{\lambda_{s}^{\star}g}f\\
=&\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}-\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(\lambda_{s}^{\star}g)^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f-\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\partial_{i}(\lambda_{s}^{\star}g^{ij})\partial_{j}f-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(\log|\lambda_{s}^{\star}g|(\lambda_{s}^{\star}g)^{ij})\partial_{j}f.\end{split}

A straight forward computation shows that

\|\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{s}^{\star}g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}=\|\lambda_{s}^{\star}(L_{v}g)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\gamma}},\;\|\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}(\lambda_{s}^{\star}g)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}=\|\lambda_{s}^{\star}L_{v}^{2}g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}.

As for each
s
,
\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{s}^{2}F(s,x)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim\|r^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\nabla f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}
, we obtain the desire estimate for
r^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{\Delta}
.
∎

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a polyhomogeneous nondegenerate pair, then the following estimates hold

(i)

\|r(P_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\mathfrak{a})-P_{g}(t\mathfrak{a}))\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq Ct^{3}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}},

(ii)

\|r(S_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\mathfrak{a})-S_{g}(t\mathfrak{a}))\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq Ct^{2}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}},

(iii)

\|r(Q_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\mathfrak{a})-Q_{g}(t\mathfrak{a}))\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq Ct^{2}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}},

where

is a constant depends on
\mathfrak{a}
but independents of

and

Proof.

For (i), we write
g_{s}=\lambda_{s}^{\star}g
, then
P_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\mathfrak{a})-P_{g}(t\mathfrak{a})=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{s}P_{g_{s}}(t\mathfrak{a})ds
. By Lemma

, we have

\|\partial_{s}g^{ik}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\gamma}},\;\;\|\partial_{s}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}.

By the expression of

and the same argument of Lemma
6.3
, near
\Sigma
, we obtain

which implies
\|r\partial_{s}P_{g_{s}}(t\mathfrak{a})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}t^{3}.

For (ii), as
\iota_{t\mathfrak{a}}\circ\lambda_{s}=\mu_{s}\circ\iota_{t\lambda_{s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}

(44)

\begin{split}S_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\alpha)-S_{g}(t\alpha)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\star_{g_{s}}\iota_{t\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}(\mu_{s}^{\star}T)ds=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{s}^{\star}\star_{g}(\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}T)ds.\end{split}

As
\star_{g}(\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}T)=S_{g}(t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a})

6.2

As
\lambda_{s}^{\star}\lambda_{-s}^{\star}r=r
, which is independent of the order of
r
direction. Therefore, the singular terms of
\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{s}^{\star}\star_{g}(\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}T)

or

near
\Sigma
, which implies

is bounded near

.

On the other hand, we compute

(45)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\lambda_{s}^{\star}\star_{g}(\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}T)=&\lambda_{s}^{\star}(\nabla_{v}\star_{g}(\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}\mathfrak{a}}^{\star}T))+\lambda_{s}^{\star}(\star_{g}\iota_{t\lambda_{-s}^{\star}(-L_{v}\mathfrak{a})}^{\star}T)\\

Near

\|S_{\lambda^{\star}g}(t\mathfrak{a})-S_{g}(t\mathfrak{a})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq Ct^{2}\|v\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}}.

(iii) follows by (i) and (ii).

7.2.
First eigenvalue estimate

Let
[\tilde{L}_{t}]=(\tilde{L},\tilde{\iota}_{t})
be the immersed submanifold constructed in Theorem
6.1
with
\theta_{t}
be the Lagrangian angle and let
\tilde{g}_{t}:=\iota_{t}g
be the pull-back metric, which by Proposition
7.3
is
\mathcal{C}^{1,\gamma}
. Let
f
be a function on
\tilde{L}
, we write
\mathcal{D}_{t}f:=d^{\star_{t}}(\cos\theta_{t}df)
be the linear term of the special Lagrangian equation, where
d^{\star_{t}}
is taken for the Riemannian metric
\tilde{g}_{t}
. It is straight forward to check that
\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
is a self-adjoint operator. If we write
\hat{g}_{t}={(\cos\theta_{t})}^{\frac{2}{n-2}}g_{t}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}f=(\cos\theta_{t})^{\frac{2}{n-2}}\Delta_{\hat{g}_{t}}f
, where
\Delta_{\tilde{g}_{t}}
is the Laplace operator for
\hat{g}_{t}
.

To begin with, we will understand the eigenvalue for the cone metric. Let
g_{0}:=p^{\star}g_{L}
be the pullback cone metric and
\Delta_{g_{0}}
be the corresponding cone Laplace operator. We define

\lambda_{0}:=\inf_{f}\{\frac{\int|df|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}}{\int|f|^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}}|f\in W^{1,2},\int fd\mathrm{Vol}_{0}=0\},

where
d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}
is the volume form w.r.t. the metric
g_{0}
. As the cone metric is quasi-isometric to the smooth metric, the
\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}
norms and
W^{1,2}
norms using the cone metric will be same as using any smooth metric.

Theorem 7.6
.

\lambda_{0}
is the first positive eigenvalue for the cone metric Laplace operator
\Delta_{g_{0}}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\lambda_{0}
is positive and could be achieved. Suppose
\lambda_{0}=0
, then there exists a sequence of functions
f_{k}
such that
\int|df_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{k}
and
\int|f_{k}|^{2}=1
, which is a bounded sequence in
W^{1,2}
. Thus after passing to a subsequence, there exists
f_{\infty}
such that
f_{k}
convergence to
f_{\infty}
weakly in
W^{1,2}
and strongly in
L^{2}
. Therefore,
\int|f_{\infty}|^{2}=1,\int f_{\infty}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}=0
. In addition,
\|df_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|df_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=0,
thus
f_{\infty}
is a constant, which contradicts to
\int f_{\infty}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}=0
. Therefore,
\lambda_{0}>0
and we still denote
f_{\infty}
be the weak limit for a sequence of minimizers.

By Lagrange multiplier theorem, we have

\int\langle df_{\infty},df^{\prime}\rangle d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}=\lambda_{0}\int f_{\infty}f^{\prime}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0},

for any
f^{\prime}\in W^{1,2}
. Therefore,
f_{\infty}
is a weak solution to
\Delta_{g_{0}}-\lambda_{0}
. In addition, as the cone metric is quasi-isomorphism to the Euclidean metric, the coefficients of the Laplace operator will be
L^{\infty}
, then by the elliptic theory for
L^{\infty}
coefficients elliptic operator,
f_{\infty}
is a eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
.
∎

We write
\lambda_{t}
be the first eigenvalue of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
, then
\lambda_{t}
could be realized by the following variation

\lambda_{t}=\inf_{f}\{\frac{\int\cos\theta_{t}|df|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}}{\int|f|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}}|f\in W^{1,2},\int fd\mathrm{Vol}_{t}=0\},

where
d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}
is the volume form for the metric
\tilde{g}_{t}
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\lambda_{t}
be the first eigenvalue of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}
, then
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda_{t}=\lambda_{0}>0
. In particular, there exists a constant
c_{0}
independent of
t
such that
\lambda_{t}\geq c_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{Vol}_{t}
be the volume form of
\tilde{g}_{t}
over
\tilde{L}
and
\mathrm{Vol}_{0}
be the volume form of
g_{0}
over
\tilde{L}
. In local coordinates
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
, we could write

\cos\theta_{t}|df|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\cos\theta_{t}(\tilde{g}_{t})^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}f\partial_{x_{j}}f\det(\tilde{g}_{t})dx_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{n}.

As
\lim_{t\to 0}\tilde{g}_{t}=g_{0}
and
\lim_{t\to 0}\cos\theta_{t}=1
, we obtain

(66)

(1-\epsilon)\int|df|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}\leq\int\cos\theta_{t}|df|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{t}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}\leq(1+\epsilon)\int|df|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}.

Let
f_{t}
be the eigenfunction
\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{t}}f_{t}=\lambda_{t}f_{t}
, then we compute

\begin{split}&\int\cos\theta_{t}|df_{t}|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}=\lambda_{t}\int|f_{t}|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}\mathrm{Vol}_{t}\leq\lambda_{t}(1+\epsilon)\int|f_{t}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0},\\
&\int\cos\theta_{t}|df_{t}|_{\tilde{g}_{t}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}\geq(1-\epsilon)\int|df_{t}|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda_{0}\int|f_{t}|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}.\end{split}

Therefore,
\lambda_{t}\geq\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\lambda_{0}
.

On the other hand, by the definition of
\lambda_{0}
, for every
\epsilon
, we can find a function

such that

\int|df|_{g_{0}}^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}\leq(\lambda_{0}+\epsilon)\int|f|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}.

In addition, we have

\begin{split}\int|df|^{2}_{g_{0}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\geq(1-\epsilon)\int|df|^{2}_{g_{t}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}\geq(1-\epsilon)\lambda_{t}\int|f|^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{t}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{2}\lambda_{t}\int|f|^{2}d\mathrm{Vol}_{0}\end{split}

Therefore, we conclude that
\lambda_{t}\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}+\epsilon}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}
, which implies the claim.
∎

7.5.2.
Quadratic term estimate

Let

Proposition 7.18
.

[
26

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be 1-forms on

\|\alpha_{1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}+\|\alpha_{1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}\leq C\tau^{-1}
and

(75)

\begin{split}\|Q(\alpha_{1})-Q(\alpha_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim&\tau^{\gamma}(\tau\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla\alpha_{1}-\nabla\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}})\cdot\\

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two 1-forms, for each
x\in L
, we write
P(r,s)=Q(r(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})+s\alpha_{2})

Then we compute

Q(\alpha_{1})-Q(\alpha_{2})=P(1,1)-P(0,1)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}(r,1)dr.

As

\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}(u,1)=\int_{0}^{1}(\frac{d}{ds}(\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}(us,s)))ds=\int_{0}^{1}u\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r^{2}}(us,s)+\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r\partial s}(us,s)ds.

By a change of variable, we obtain

(76)

\begin{split}Q(\alpha_{1})-Q(\alpha_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}[\frac{r}{s^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r^{2}}(r,s)+\frac{1}{s}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r\partial s}(r,s)]drds.\end{split}

By (
21

\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}:\{(x,y,z):x\in L,\;y\in T_{x}^{\star}L,\;z\in\otimes^{2}T_{x}^{\star}L\}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\partial_{1}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}
be taking the derivative for

\partial_{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}
be taking the derivative for

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r^{2}}(r,s)=&\otimes^{2}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\cdot\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}+2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\otimes(\nabla\alpha_{1}-\nabla\alpha_{2})\cdot\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}\\
&+\otimes^{2}(\nabla\alpha_{1}-\nabla\alpha_{2})\cdot\partial_{2}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}},\;\mathrm{and}\\
\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial r\partial s}(r,s)=&(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\otimes\alpha_{2}\cdot\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}+((\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\otimes\nabla\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\otimes(\nabla\alpha_{1}-\nabla\alpha_{2}))\cdot\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}\\
&+(\nabla\alpha_{1}-\nabla\alpha_{2})\otimes\nabla\alpha_{2}\cdot\partial_{2}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}},\end{split}

where we are evaluating
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}

We define

(77)

\begin{split}E_{1}:=&rs^{-2}\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}^{2}+s^{-1}\|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\|\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}},\\

&+s^{-1}\|\alpha_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\|\nabla(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}.\end{split}

Then (
76

(78)

\begin{split}&\|Q(\alpha_{1})-Q(\alpha_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\\
\leq&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}(E_{1}\|\partial_{1}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}^{2}+E_{2}\|\partial_{2}^{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+E_{3}\|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\tilde{\mathrm{SL}}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}})drds.\end{split}

As

8.1.

Let
(X,J,\omega,\Omega)
be a Calabi-Yau manifold,
[L]=(L,\iota:L\to X)
be an immersed Lagrangian submanfiold with
\mathcal{C}^{2,\gamma}

\theta
be the Lagrangian angle. By Theorem

where

is a

Let
f
be a function on
L
, such that

, then by Proposition
3.7
, we could write the special Lagrangian equation for

as

\sin\theta-\mathcal{D}_{\theta}f+Q(df)=0,

where
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}f:=d^{\star}(\cos\theta df).

We start with a lemma follows from Aubin
[
3
, Thorem 4.7]
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For each
h\in\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}(L)
with
\int_{L}hd\mathrm{Vol}=0
, there exists

such that
\int_{L}fd\mathrm{Vol}=0
and
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}f=-h
.

We take
f:=\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h
, then we are looking for suitable
h
with
\int_{L}hd\mathrm{Vol}=0
to solve

h+Q(d\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h)=-\sin\theta.

We define the operator

T:\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}\to\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma},\;Th:=-Q(d\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h)-\sin\theta,

where
\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}
is the Hölder norm for functions on
L
, then we have:

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\lambda
be the first eigenvalue of
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for
N:=C\tau^{\gamma}(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})^{2}
and
B_{N^{-1}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}|\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq N^{-1}\}
, suppose
\|\sin\theta\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq\frac{1}{4N}

is a contraction mapping.

Proof.

For
h_{1},h_{2}\in B_{N}
, we compute

\begin{split}\|Th_{1}-Th_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}=&\|Q_{g}(d\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h_{1})-Q_{g}(d\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\\

&(\tau\|d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\tau\|d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla d{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla d{\mathcal{D}}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}),\\
\end{split}

where for the second inequality we use Proposition
7.18
.
By Corollary
7.17
, we have

\begin{split}&\tau\|d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{i}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}h_{i}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})\|h_{i}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}},\;\mathrm{and}\\
&\tau\|d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}(h_{1}-h_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla d\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{\theta}(h_{1}-h_{2})\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}.\end{split}

Therefore, there exists a constant
C_{0}
such that

\begin{split}\|Th_{1}-Th_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq&C_{0}\tau^{\gamma}(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})^{2}\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}(\|h_{1}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}})\\
\leq&\frac{C_{0}}{C}\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}.\end{split}

Thus, for any

, we have
\|Th_{1}-Th_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|h_{1}-h_{2}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}
. Moreover, we still need to check

\begin{split}\|Th\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}&\leq\|Q(d\mathcal{D}_{\theta}^{-1}h)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\sin\theta\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\\
&\leq C_{0}^{\prime}(\tau^{\gamma}(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})^{2}\|h\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\sin\theta\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}})\\
&\leq(C_{0}^{\prime}C^{-1}+\frac{1}{4})N^{-1}.\end{split}

We take

, then

is a well-defined contraction map.
∎

Corollary 8.3
.

There exists an unique solution to (
81
)
which satisfies

Proof.

Let

\tau\|df\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}+\|\nabla df\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}})\|h\|_{\mathcal{C}^{,\gamma}}\lesssim N^{-1}(1+\lambda^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(L)\tau^{2+\gamma+\frac{n}{2}}).

∎

For

The moduli space of special Lagrangian submanifold have been studied in

Let

A.1.
Mellin transformation

Mellin transformation is a tool to study the expansion of functions along a singular set, which gives a nice correspondence between meromorphic functions and functions with expansions. We refer to
[
34
, Section 2]
and
[
37
]
for more details.

Let
u(r)
be a compact supported function defined on the positive real axis
0<r<\infty
, the Mellin transformation
Mu:=u_{M}(\zeta)
of
u
is a function defined on the complex plane defined as

Mu(\zeta)=u_{M}(\zeta):=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(r)r^{i\zeta-1}dr,

where
\zeta:=\xi+i\eta
is a complex variable.

The Mellin transformation is related to the Fourier transformation. Let
r=e^{s}
, then

u_{M}(\zeta)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u(e^{s})e^{i\zeta s}ds,

which is the Fourier transform for the function
u(e^{s})
. Therefore, there is a Plancherel relationship extended the definition of Mellin transform to
L^{2}
function, or more generally, weighted
L^{2}
space
r^{\delta}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that the Mellin transform induces an isomorphism

(83)

r^{\delta}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\cong L^{2}(\eta=\delta-\frac{1}{2}),

where
r^{\delta}L^{2}:=\{r^{\delta}f|f\in L^{2}\}
.

As for
\delta^{\prime}\leq\delta
, we have
r^{\delta}L^{2}\subset r^{\delta^{\prime}}L^{2}
, the domain that Mellin transform exists will be
\{\Im\zeta<\beta\}
. Analogy to the Fourier transform, the Mellin transform also have an inverse formula.

M^{-1}(u_{M}(\zeta))=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Im\zeta=C}u_{M}(\zeta)x^{-i\zeta}d\xi.

In particular, if
u\in r^{\delta}L^{2}
and
C=\delta-\frac{1}{2}
, we have
M^{-1}(u_{M}(\zeta))=u(x)
.

Now, we will focus on the expansion at
r=0
. Let
\phi(r)
be a smooth function over
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\phi
equals
1
near
r=0
and
\phi
vanishes for large
t
, we consider
u(r):=r^{s}(\log r)^{p}\phi(r)
. In addition, as
r^{s}(\log r)^{p}\phi(r)\in x^{\delta}L^{2}
, for any
\delta<\Re s+\frac{1}{2}
,
u_{M}(\zeta)
is well defined on
\{\Im\zeta<\beta\}
.

In addition, as

\int_{0}^{1}r^{s}(\log r)^{p}r^{i\zeta-1}dr=(-1)^{p+1}\frac{p!}{(i\zeta+s)^{p+1}},

an integration by parts shows that
u_{M}
can be extended to a meromorphic function in the whole complex plane, with poles of order
p+1
at
\zeta=is
.

In our situation, let
\Sigma
be an embedded submanifold on a Riemannian manifold, Let
U
be a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
and we choose local coordinates
(z=re^{i\theta},t)
along
U
such that
\Sigma=\{z=0\}
. Let
u(r,\theta,t)
be a polyhomogeneous function near
\Sigma
with index set
E
, then the above arguments could be applied to
u(r,\theta,t)
. The Mellin transform of
r
coordinate and
(\phi(r)u)_{M}(\zeta,\theta,t)
could be understood as a function on
(\zeta,\theta,t)\in\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}\times\Sigma
. In addition, for
s\in E
,
(\phi(r)u)_{M}
has a meromorphic extension at
\zeta=is
.

We have the following fundamental correspondence of Mellin transformation:

Theorem A.1
.

[
37
, Proposition 5.27]

The Mellin transformation gives an isomorphism between the polyhomogeneous function with index set

and meromorphic functions with values in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(Y)

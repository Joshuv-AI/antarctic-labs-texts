Introduction

In this paper, we are mainly concerned with the nonexistence of positive
supersolution to the following semilinear elliptic equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=h(x,u)&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u\geq 0&{\rm on}\ \partial{\Omega},\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\Omega
is a punctured or an exterior domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 3
;
that is,
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{O}
, here
\mathcal{O}
is
a bounded, closed smooth subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
h:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function, here
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)
.
We assume, unless otherwise specified, that
N\geq 3
throughout the paper.
Without loss of generality, we also assume that
\mathcal{O}\subset\overline{B}_{R}(0)
,
where
\overline{B}_{R}(0)
represents the ball with the radius
R
, centered at the origin.
A typical punctured domain is
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and a typical exterior domain is

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{R}(0)
with
\mathcal{O}=\overline{B}_{R}(0)
.

A function
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is said to be a positive supersolution of (
2
) if
u(x)>0
and

-\Delta u(x)\geq h(x,u)
for all
x\in\Omega
.

The existence and nonexistence of solution or supersolution to problem (
2
) have attracted great attentions for many years; see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
19
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
and the references therein. In the special case that
h
only depends on

u
, given
R_{0}>0
, Alarcon, Melian and Quaas
[
2
]
proved that problem (
2
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R_{0}}(0)
admits a positive solution
if and only if

h
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\sigma_{0}}h(t)t^{-\frac{2N-2}{N-2}}dt<+\infty

for some
\sigma_{0}>0
, by appealing to ODE techniques. Nevertheless, such an approach fails to apply to (
2
)
if the nonlinear term
h
is not radially symmetric.

In particular, when
h(x,u)=V(x)u^{p}
, (
2
) becomes the following

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=V(x)u^{p}&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u\geq 0&{\rm on}\ \partial{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

(3)

The traditional method to establish
nonexistence results for solution or supersolution to (
3
) is to
make use of the fundamental solution and Hadamard property (
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
13
]
).
It is worth mentioning that if
p=-2
, problem (
3
) in a bounded domain
is used to describe the MEMS model (
[
21
,
24
]
),
and if
p=-1
, problem (
3
) is related to the study of singular minimal
hypersurfaces with symmetry (
[
29
,
34
]
).

When
V(x)=(1+|x|)^{\beta}
and
\Omega
is a punctured or an exterior domain, Bidaut-Véron
[
7
]

and Bidaut-Véron and Pohozaev
[
8
]
showed that
problem (
3
) has no solution
if

p\leq\frac{N+\beta}{N-2}:=p_{\beta}^{*}\ \ \ \ \mbox{with}\ \beta\in(-2,2).

On the other hand, when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
and

V(x)=|x|^{a_{0}}(1+|x|)^{\beta-a_{0}}
with
a_{0}\in(-N,+\infty)
and
\beta\in(-\infty,a_{0})
,
Chen, Felmer and Yang
[
15
]
derived infinitely many positive solutions of (
3
)
if

p\in\left(p_{\beta}^{*},\frac{N+a_{0}}{N-2}\right)\cap(0,\,+\infty).

Armstrong and Sirakov
[
5
]
and Chen and Felmer
[
13
]
dealt with the more general potential

V(x)\geq|x|^{\beta}(\ln|x|)^{\tau}\quad{\rm for}\ |x|>e,

where
\beta>-2
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Especially,
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]

imply the following result:

\bullet

Let
\beta\in(-2,2)
. Problem (
3
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0)

has no positive supersolution provided that either

p<p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
or
p=p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau\geq 0
.

In the current paper, we will provide a sharp improvement of the above result;
indeed, we can conclude the following:

\bullet

Let
\beta>-2
. Problem (
3
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0)

has no positive supersolution provided that either

1\leq p<p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
or
p=p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau>-1
(see Proposition
4.1
);

\bullet

Let
\beta=-2
and
p=p_{\beta}^{*}=1
. Problem (
3
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0)

has no positive supersolution provided that

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}V(x)|x|^{2}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{2}
(see Theorem
2.1
);

\bullet

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Problem (
3
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\ell}(0)
has positive supersolution for properly large
\ell
provided that either

p>p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
or
p=p_{\beta}^{*},\,\tau<-1
(see Proposition
4.2
).

As a consequence, the above results show that both
p=p_{\beta}^{*}
and
\tau=-1
are the critical values for the existence
of positive supersolution to (
3
).

For the more general nonlinear problem (
2
), let us assume that

h(x,u)\geq\tilde{h}(x,u)\geq 0\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0),\,u\geq 0,

and
\tilde{h}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a function satisfying the following

\rm(H)

(a) for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0)
,

\frac{\tilde{h}(x,s_{1})}{s_{1}}\geq\frac{\tilde{h}(x,s_{2})}{s_{2}}\quad{\rm if}\ s_{1}\geq s_{2}>0;

(b) for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{h}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N})|x|^{N}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4};

if (b) fails, we assume that

(b1) there exists
\sigma_{1}\in(0,1)
such that for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{h}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N})|x|^{N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{1}}>0;

and

(b2) there exists
\sigma_{2}>0
such that for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}(x,t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{2}})}{t|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{2}}}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the assumption
(H)
, problem (
2
) has no positive supersolution.

We would like to mention that (H)-(a) means that
h
is linear or superlinear,
(H)-(b) is related to the subcritical case while (H)-(b1)(b2) deal with the critical case. As one will see below,
Theorem
1.1
allows us to obtain some optimal nonexistence results of positive supersolution to problem (
3
).

To prove Theorem
1.1
, it turns out that a nonexistence result of positive supersolution of
the linear Hardy elliptic problem (
1
)
(see Section 2) is vital in our analysis. Such a nonexistence result can be established by Agmon-Allegretto-Piepenbrink theory
[
1
]
; in this paper, we shall provide a different proof which seems simpler.

Another focus of our paper is on the following semilinear elliptic system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=h_{1}(x,u,v)&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\displaystyle-\Delta v=h_{2}(x,u,v)&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u,v\geq 0&{\rm on}\ \partial{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

(4)

It is known that Liouville-type theorems for system (
4
) have been established (see
[
16
,
22
,
32
,
33
,
35
]
) primarily on the whole space
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In particular, when
h_{1}(x,u,v)=v^{p},\ h_{2}(x,u,v)=u^{q}
, the nonexistence of positive solution to (
4
) in
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
has been investigated by
[
16
,
33
,
35
]

in the subcritical case that

\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>1-\frac{2}{N}.

It seems that there is little research work devoted to the nonexistence/existence of solution/supersolution to (
4
) when
\Omega
is an exterior domain.
We say a function pair
(u,v)\in C^{2}(\Omega)\times C^{2}(\Omega)
is a positive supersolution of (
4
) if
u(x),\,v(x)>0
,
-\Delta u(x)\geq h_{1}(x,u,v)
and

-\Delta v\geq h_{2}(x,u,v)
for all
x\in\Omega
.

For system (
4
), two functions
\tilde{h}_{1}
,
\tilde{h}_{2}
are involved to control functions
h_{1},\ h_{2}
respectively.

\rm(SH)

Suppose that the nonnegative function
\tilde{h}_{i}\,(i=1,2)
defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0))\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
satisfies that

(a) the maps
t\to\tilde{h}_{1}(x,s,t)
,
s\to\tilde{h}_{2}(x,s,t)
are nondecreasing and
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0)
,

\frac{\tilde{h}_{1}(x,s_{1},t_{1})}{s_{1}}\geq\frac{\tilde{h}_{1}(x,s_{2},t_{2})}{s_{2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\frac{\tilde{h}_{2}(x,s_{1},t_{1})}{t_{1}}\geq\frac{\tilde{h}_{2}(x,s_{2},t_{2})}{t_{2}}\quad{\rm if}\ s_{1}\geq s_{2}>0,\ t_{1}\geq t_{2}>0;

(b) for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{h}_{1}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N},\,t|x|^{2-N})|x|^{N}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},

or

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{h}_{2}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N},\,t|x|^{2-N})|x|^{N}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4};

if (b) fails, we assume that

(b1) there exist positive constants
\sigma_{3},\sigma_{4}
with either
\sigma_{3}<1
or
\sigma_{4}<1
,
such that for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\tilde{h}_{i}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N},\,t|x|^{2-N})|x|^{N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{i+2}}>0,\quad i=1,2;

and

(b2) if
\sigma_{3}<1
,
\sigma_{4}\geq 1

there exists
\sigma_{5}>0
such that for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}_{1}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{5}},t|x|^{2-N})}{t|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{5}}}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4};

if
\sigma_{3}\geq 1
,
\sigma_{4}<1
, there exists
\sigma_{6}>0
such that for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}_{2}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N},t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{6}}))}{t|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{6}}}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4};

(b3) if
\sigma_{3},\,\sigma_{4}<1
, there exist
\sigma_{5},\sigma_{6}>0
such that
for any
t>0
,

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}_{1}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{5}},t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{6}})}{t|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{5}}}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}

or

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{h}_{2}(x,\,t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{5}},t|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{6}}))}{t|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma_{6}}}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.

Then we can state

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that for all
(x,u,v)\in(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{e}(0))\times[0,+\infty)\times[0,+\infty)
,
the functions
h_{1},\,h_{2}
satisfy

h_{1}(x,u,v)\geq\tilde{h}_{1}(x,u,v),\ \quad h_{2}(x,u,v)\geq\tilde{h}_{2}(x,u,v),

with
\tilde{h}_{1},\tilde{h}_{2}
fulfilling
(SH)
. Then system (
4
) has no positive supersolution.

Assumption (SH)-(b) is related to the subcritical case, (SH)-(b2) says that one of the nonlinearities is critical, and (SH)-(b3) represents that both nonlinear terms are critical. In particular, when the nonlinearities
h_{1},\ h_{2}
take the form
h_{1}(x,u,v)=|x|^{\beta_{1}}(\ln|x|)^{\tau_{1}}u^{p_{1}}v^{q_{1}}
and
h_{2}(x,u,v)=|x|^{\beta_{2}}(\ln|x|)^{\tau_{2}}u^{p_{2}}v^{q_{2}}
, we are able to clarify the nonexistence and existence of positive supersolution in terms of the parameters
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2},\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
; see Proposition
4.3
and Proposition
4.4
for the precise details.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we show the nonexistence of supersolution of a linear Hardy problem. In Section 3, we prove our main results Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In Section 4, we apply Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to
two concrete examples to obtain sharp nonexistence results.

2
Nonexistence of positive supersolution of a linear Hardy problem

In this section, we shall investigate the linear elliptic problem with Hardy potential:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=V(x)u&{\rm in}\ \Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u\geq 0&{\rm on}\ \partial{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

(1)

The nonexistence result of positive supersolution to (
1
) reads as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a punctured or an exterior domain, and

V
is a nonnegative function satisfying

\liminf_{|x|\to+\infty}V(x)|x|^{2}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.

(2)

Then problem (
1
) has no positive supersolution.

We remark that
{(N-2)^{2}}/{4}
is the best constant in the Hardy-Sobolev inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\geq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx.

Related Hardy problems have been studied extensively; one may refer to, for instance,
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
20
]
. In particular,
the authors
[
14
]
considered the Hardy problem

-\Delta u=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u+f(x)
in
\mathcal{D}\setminus\{0\}
, subject to the zero Dirchlet boundary condition,
where
f\geq 0
and
\mathcal{D}
is a bounded domain containing the origin, and proved that this problem has no positive solution once
\mu>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
. To this end, they used new distributional identities
to classify the isolated singular solution of
-\Delta u=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u+f
in
\mathcal{D}\setminus\{0\}

and find fundamental solutions of
-\Delta u=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

It is worth noting that
[
18
,
30
,
31
]
indicate that the nonexistence of positive supersolution to (
1
) can be obtained by using Agmon-Allegretto-Piepenbrink theory
[
1
]
. We will provide a different and elementary proof. Our strategy is to employ the Kelvin transform to transfer the unbounded domain
\Omega
into a bounded one containing the origin.

For the linear elliptic equation involving the general homogeneous potential in the punctured domain
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
:

\displaystyle-\Delta u=\mu|x|^{-\alpha}u\quad\ {\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

(3)

we obtain that

Theorem 2.2
.

Problem (
3
) has no positive supersolution provided that one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\alpha\not=2,\ \mu>0;

(ii)

\alpha=2,\ \mu>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}.

Theorem
2.2
is optimal in a certain sense, and it also reveals essential differences between problem (
2
) with a
punctured domain and problem (
2
) with an exterior domain; see the following remark.

Remark 2.3
.

Concerning Theorem
2.2
, we would like to make some comments as follows.

(i)

When
\alpha<2,\ \mu>0
, one can easily see from the proof of Theorem
2.2
that
the linear problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\mu|x|^{-\alpha}u&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\ell}(0),\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u\geq 0&{\rm on}\ \,\partial{B_{\ell}(0)}\end{array}\right.

(4)

has no positive supersolution for any
\ell>0;

(ii)

When
\alpha>2,\ \mu>0
, problem (
4
) has positive supersolution for properly large
\ell
; this can be seen from Proposition
4.2
(i) below (by taking
p=1,\,\beta<-2,\,\tau=0
there). Such a result is in sharp contrast with the above (i) and Theorem
2.2
(i)
;

(iii)

When
\alpha=2,\ \mu\leq{(N-2)^{2}}/{4}
, problem (
3
) (and so problem (
4
)) has
positive supersolution by considering the fundamental solutions of Hardy operators; one can refer to, for example,
[
14
]
. Hence, for
\alpha=2
,
\mu={(N-2)^{2}}/{4}
is the critical value of existence of positive supersolution of problems (
3
) and (
4
).

2.1
Proof of Theorem
2.1

In the case that
\beta=-2
and so the critical exponent
p^{*}_{\beta}=1
, problem (
1
)
is related to the Hardy-Leray potentials. The following result plays an essential role
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
14
, Proposition 5.2]

Assume that
\mu>(N-2)^{2}/4
,
\mathcal{D}
is a bounded smooth domain containing the origin and

f\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\overline{\mathcal{D}\setminus\{0\}})
is a nonnegative function. Then the Hardy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta u=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u+f&{\rm in}\ \mathcal{D}\setminus\{0\},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle u=0&{\rm on}\ \partial\mathcal{D}\end{array}\right.

(5)

has no positive solution.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
We argue indirectly and suppose that

u
is a positive supersolution of (
1
). The main idea below is to reflect

\Omega
to a bounded punctured domain through the Kelvin transform and then to obtain a contradiction
by Lemma
2.4
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Omega
is a connected exterior domain satisfying
0\not\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Denote by

\Omega^{\sharp}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ \frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\in\Omega\right\}\quad{\rm and}\quad v(x)=u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\quad{\rm for}\ x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

Clearly,
\Omega^{\sharp}
is a bounded punctured domain. By direct computation, for
x\in\Omega^{\sharp}
, we have

\nabla v(x)=\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-2\left(\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\cdot x\right)\frac{x}{|x|^{4}}

and

\Delta v(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}\Delta u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)+\frac{2(2-N)}{|x|^{4}}\left(\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\cdot x\right).

Let

u^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{2-N}v(x),\ \ \ V^{\sharp}(x)=|x|^{-4}V\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right).

Then for
x\in\Omega^{\sharp}
, using the fact that
\Delta(|x|^{2-N})=0
, we observe that

\displaystyle-\Delta u^{\sharp}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta v(x)\,|x|^{2-N}-2\nabla v(x)\cdot(\nabla|x|^{2-N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}\Delta u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)+\frac{2(2-N)}{|x|^{4}}\left(\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\cdot x\right)\right]|x|^{2-N}

\displaystyle-\frac{2(2-N)x}{|x|^{2}}\left[\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-2\left(\nabla u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\cdot x\right)\frac{x}{|x|^{4}}\right]|x|^{2-N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x|^{-2-N}-\Delta u\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle V^{\sharp}(x)u^{\sharp}(x).

Set
V^{*}(x)=\frac{-\Delta u^{\sharp}(x)}{u^{\sharp}(x)}
. Thus, we notice that

V^{*}
is continuous in
\Omega^{\sharp}
and
V^{*}\geq V^{\sharp}
in
\Omega^{\sharp}
. Therefore,

V^{*}(x)\geq|x|^{-4}V(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

Because of (
2
), it follows that

\liminf_{|x|\to 0^{+}}V^{*}(x)|x|^{2}>\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},

which in turn implies that there exist
\mu_{1}>(N-2)^{2}/4
and
r_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle V^{*}(x)\geq\mu_{1}|x|^{-2},\quad\forall\,x\in B_{r_{1}}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

Denote
u_{0}=u^{\sharp}-\varphi_{0}
, where
\varphi_{0}
is the unique positive solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-\Delta\varphi_{0}=0&{\rm in}\ B_{r_{1}}(0),\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}\displaystyle\varphi_{0}=u^{\sharp}&{\rm on}\ \partial B_{r_{1}}(0).\end{array}\right.

Then
u_{0}
satisfies
u_{0}=0
on
\partial B_{r_{1}}(0)
and

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{0}(x)-\frac{\mu_{1}}{|x|^{2}}u_{0}=V^{*}(x)u^{\sharp}(x)-\frac{\mu_{1}}{|x|^{2}}u^{\sharp}(x)+\frac{\mu_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\varphi_{0}(x):=f^{*}(x)\geq 0,\quad\forall\,x\in B_{r_{1}}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

As a consequence,
u_{0}
is a positive solution of (
5
) with
\mathcal{D}=B_{r_{1}}(0)
,

\mu=\mu_{1}
and
f=f^{*}
. This contradicts
Lemma
2.4
. Thus, (
1
) admits no positive supersolution, and the proof is complete.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
h(x,u)=|x|^{\beta}(\ln|x|)^{\tau}u^{p},\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\ell}(0)
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then for some large
\ell>e
, problem (
2
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\ell}(0)
has a positive bounded supersolution if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

\rm(i)

p>p^{*}_{\beta}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
;

\rm(ii)

p=p^{*}_{\beta}
and
\tau<-1
.

Proof.
Recall that
w_{0}(x)=|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma}
with
\sigma>0
.
If

|x|>\max\Big\{1,\ e^{\frac{2(\sigma-1)}{N-2}}\Big\},

it immediately follows from (
1
) that

\displaystyle-\Delta w_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(N-2)|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma-1}-t\sigma(\sigma-1)|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma-2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma(N-2)|x|^{-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma-1}

and

\displaystyle h(x,w_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x|^{\beta}(\ln|x|)^{\tau}(|x|^{2-N}(\ln|x|)^{\sigma})^{p}=|x|^{\beta+(2-N)p}(\ln|x|)^{\tau+\sigma p}.

When
p>p^{*}_{\beta}
, then
\beta+(2-N)p<-N
. One can easily see that there exists a large constant
\ell>e
such that

-\Delta w_{0}(x)\geq h(x,w_{0}(x)),\quad\forall\,|x|>\ell.

Hence,
w_{0}
is a desired supersolution.

When
p=p^{*}_{\beta}
, we have
\beta+(2-N)p=-N
. Similarly as above, for some large
\ell>e
,
problem (
2
) has a positive supersolution
w_{0}
if
\sigma-1>\tau+\sigma p^{*}_{\beta}
, that is,

\sigma(p^{*}_{\beta}-1)<-\tau-1,

(2)

where
-\tau-1>0
by our assumption
\tau<-1
. So when
\beta>-2
, (
2
) holds if
we take
\sigma>\frac{-\tau-1}{p^{*}_{\beta}-1}
. When
\beta\leq-2
, then
p^{*}_{\beta}-1\leq 0
and
(
2
) is satisfied if we just take
\sigma=1
.

In each case, the supersolution
w_{0}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\ell}(0)
.

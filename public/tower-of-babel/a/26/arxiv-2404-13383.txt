Quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras and Pre-Novikov bialgebras
Yue Li, Yanyong Hong

Abstract
. Pre-Novikov algebras and quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras naturally appear in the theory of Novikov bialgebras. In this paper, we show that there is a natural pre-Novikov algebra structure associated to a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra. Then we introduce the definition of double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to two pre-Novikov algebras and show that it is characterized by a pre-Novikov bialgebra. We also introduce the notion of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation, whose symmetric solutions can produce pre-Novikov bialgebras. Moreover, the operator forms of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation are also investigated.

1.
Introduction

Novikov algebras appeared in the study of Hamiltonian
operators in the formal variational calculus
[
7
,
8
]
and
Poisson brackets of hydrodynamic type
[
5
]
. It was also shown in
[
19
]
that Novikov algebras correspond to a class of Lie conformal algebras, which describe the singular part of operator product expansion of chiral fields in conformal field
theory
[
13
]
. Note that Novikov algebras are also an important subclass of pre-Lie algebras (also called left-symmetric algebras), which are closely related to many fields in mathematics and physics such as affine manifolds and affine
structures on Lie groups
[
15
]
, convex homogeneous cones
[
18
]
, deformation of associative algebras
[
9
]
and vertex algebras
[
1
,
4
]
.

The definitions of pre-Novikov algebras and quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras naturally appeared in the study of Novikov bialgebras
[
11
]
. It was shown in
[
11
]
that there is a Novikov algebra associated to a pre-Novikov algebra and pre-Novikov algebras can produce skewsymmetric solutions of Novikov Yang-Baxter equation and hence Novikov bialgebras. Moreover, by
[
20
]
, pre-Novikov algebras correspond to a class of left-symmetric conformal algebras
[
12
]
and there are close relationships between pre-Novikov algebras and Zinbiel algebras with a derivation (see
[
11
,
14
]
). Quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras are a class of Novikov algebras with a special bilinear form, which are closely related with the skewsymmetric solutions of Novikov Yang-Baxter equation (see
[
11
]
). Moreover, it was shown in
[
11
]
that quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras also correspond to a class of quasi-Frobenius infinite-dimensional Lie algebras.

As we know, in the case of associative algebras, there is a natural construction of Frobenius algebras called a double construction of Frobenius algebras (see
[
2
]
). It was shown in
[
2
]
that a double construction of Frobenius algebras is characterized by an antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra. Motivated by this, it is natural to consider double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras and the relationships with the bialgebra theory of pre-Novikov algebras. Note that pre-Novikov algebras are a class of L-dendriform algebras
[
3
]
and the theory of L-dendriform bialgebras was developed in
[
3
,
16
]
. Therefore, it is also natural to investigate the bialgebra theory of pre-Novikov algebras.

In this paper, we introduce the definitions of pre-Novikov bialgebras and double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to two pre-Novikov algebras and show that these two definitions are equivalent. We also investigate a special class of pre-Novikov bialgebras, which resembles the coboundary Lie bialgebras
[
6
]
. The notion of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation is also introduced, whose symmetric solutions give pre-Novikov bialgebras. We introduce the
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a pre-Novikov algebra as an operator form of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation. It gives a symmetric solution of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation in a semi-direct product pre-Novikov algebra. It should be pointed out that although pre-Novikov algebras are L-dendriform algebras, their bialgebra theories are totally different, for example, skewsymmetric solutions of LD-equation give L-dendriform bialgebras, whereas symmetric solutions of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation give pre-Novikov bialgebras.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic facts about Novikov algebras, pre-Novikov algebras and quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras. Moreover, we show that there is a pre-Novikov algebra associated to a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra. In Section 3, the definition of double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to two pre-Novikov algebras is introduced and we show that it is equivalent to a special matched pair of Novikov algebras. Moreover, we introduce the definition of pre-Novikov bialgebras and prove that it can also characterize the double construction of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to two pre-Novikov algebras. In Section 4, we introduce the definition of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation and show that symmetric solutions of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation can produce pre-Novikov bialgebras. Moreover, we investigate the operator forms of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation. The definition of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on pre-Novikov algebras is introduced and we show that it produces a symmetric solution of pre-Novikov Yang-Baxter equation in a semi-direct product pre-Novikov algebra.

Notations.

Throughout this paper, we fix a base field
{\bf k}
of characteristic
0
. All vector spaces and algebras are over
{\bf k}
. Unless otherwise stated, they are assumed to be finite-dimensional even though many results still hold in the infinite-dimensional cases. The identity map is denoted by
{\rm id}
. Let
A
be a vector space with a binary operation
\circ
.
Define linear maps

L_{\circ},R_{\circ}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\bf k}(A)
by

\displaystyle L_{\circ}(a)b:=a\circ b,\;\;R_{\circ}(a)b:=b\circ a,\;\;\;a,b\in A.

Let

\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes A,\quad a\otimes b\mapsto b\otimes a,\;\;\;\;a,b\in A,

be the flip operator.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some known facts about Novikov algebras and pre-Novikov algebras and show that there is a natural pre-Novikov algebra structure associated to a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra.

Recall that a
Novikov algebra
is a vector space
A
with a binary operation
\circ:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c-a\circ(b\circ c)

\displaystyle=(b\circ a)\circ c-b\circ(a\circ c),

(2)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c

\displaystyle=(a\circ c)\circ b,\quad a,b,c\in A.

Denote it by
(A,\circ)
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
17
]

A
representation
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is a triple
(V,l,r)
, where
V
is a vector space and
l,r:A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\bf k}(V)
are linear maps satisfying

(3)

\displaystyle l(a\circ b-b\circ a)v

\displaystyle=l(a)l(b)v-l(b)l(a)v,

(4)

\displaystyle l(a)r(b)v-r(b)l(a)v

\displaystyle=r(a\circ b)v-r(b)r(a)v,

(5)

\displaystyle l(a\circ b)v

\displaystyle=r(b)l(a)v,

(6)

\displaystyle r(a)r(b)v

\displaystyle=r(b)r(a)v,\quad a,b\in A,\;\;v\in V.

Remark 2.2
.

Obviously,
(A,L_{\circ},R_{\circ})
is a representation of
(A,\circ)
, which is called the
adjoint representation
of
(A,\circ)
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a vector space with a binary operation
\circ
and
V
be a vector space. For a linear map
\rho:A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\bf k}(V)
,
define a linear map
\rho^{*}:A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\bf k}(V^{*})
by

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\rho^{*}(a)f,v\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle f,\rho(a)v\rangle,\quad a\in A,v\in V,f\in V^{*},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
11
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
(V,l,r)
be a representation of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
. Then
(V^{*},l^{*}+r^{*},-r^{*})
is also a representation of
(A,\circ).

Remark 2.4
.

The adjoint representation
(A,L_{\circ},R_{\circ})
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
gives the representation
(A^{*},L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*},-R_{\circ}^{*})
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
11
]

Let
A
be a vector space. A
pre-Novikov algebra
is a triple
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
, where
\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
are binary operations satisfying

(8)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleright c)

\displaystyle=(a\circ b)\vartriangleright c+b\vartriangleright(a\vartriangleright c)-(b\circ a)\vartriangleright c,

(9)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright(b\vartriangleleft c)

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleright b)\vartriangleleft c+b\vartriangleleft(a\circ c)-(b\vartriangleleft a)\vartriangleleft c,

(10)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\vartriangleright c

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleright c)\vartriangleleft b,

(11)

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleleft b)\vartriangleleft c

\displaystyle=(a\vartriangleleft c)\vartriangleleft b,\quad a,b,c\in A,

where
a\circ b=a\vartriangleleft b+a\vartriangleright b
.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
11
, Proposition 3.33]

Let
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
be a pre-Novikov algebra. The binary operation

(12)

\displaystyle a\circ b:=a\vartriangleleft b+a\vartriangleright b,\quad a,b\in A,

defines a Novikov algebra, which is called the
associated Novikov algebra
of
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
. Furthermore,
(A,L_{\vartriangleright},R_{\vartriangleleft})
is a representation of
(A,\circ)
. Conversely, let
A
be a vector space equipped with binary operations
\vartriangleleft
and
\vartriangleright
. If
(A,\circ)
defined by Eq. (
12
) is a Novikov algebra and
(A,L_{\vartriangleright},R_{\vartriangleleft})
is a representation of
(A,\circ)
, then
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
is a pre-Novikov algebra.

Definition 2.7
.

[
11
]

Let
(V,l,r)
be a representation of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
. A linear map
T:V\rightarrow A
is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(V,l,r)
if
T
satisfies

(13)

\displaystyle T(u)\circ T(v)=T(l(T(u))v)+T(r(T(v))u),\quad u,v\in V.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
11
, Proposition 3.34]

Let
(V,l,r)
be a representation of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
. If
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(V,l,r)
, then there is a pre-Novikov algebra structure on
V
defined by

(14)

\displaystyle u\vartriangleright v:=l(T(u))v,\quad u\vartriangleleft v:=r(T(v))u,\quad u,v\in V.

Definition 2.9
.

[
11
]

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra. If there is a skewsymmetric nondegenerate bilinear form
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
on
A
satisfying

(15)

\displaystyle\omega(a\circ b,c)-\omega(a\circ c+c\circ a,b)+\omega(c\circ b,a)=0,\quad a,b,c\in A,

then
(A,\circ,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
is called a
quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra
.

Next, we show that there is a natural pre-Novikov algebra structure on
A
, when
(A,\circ,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
is a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(A,\circ,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
be a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra. Then there is a compatible pre-Novikov algebra structure on
A
given by

(16)

\displaystyle\omega(a\vartriangleright b,c)

\displaystyle=\omega(a\circ c+c\circ a,b),

(17)

\displaystyle\omega(a\vartriangleleft b,c)

\displaystyle=\omega(a,c\circ b),\quad a,b,c\in A,

such that
(A,\circ)
is the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
. This pre-Novikov algebra is called the
associated pre-Novikov algebra
of
(A,\circ,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
.

Proof.

Obviously, the nondegenerate bilinear form
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
on
A
can induce an invertible linear map
T:A^{*}\rightarrow A
given by

(18)

\displaystyle\omega(T(f),a)=\langle f,a\rangle,\quad f\in A^{*},a\in A.

Therefore, for all
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(a\vartriangleright b,c)

\displaystyle=\omega(a\circ c+c\circ a,b)=-\omega(b,a\circ c+c\circ a)

\displaystyle=-\langle T^{-1}(b),a\circ c+c\circ a\rangle=-\langle T^{-1}(b),(L_{\circ}+R_{\circ})(a)c\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(a)T^{-1}(b),c\rangle=\omega(T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(a)T^{-1}(b)),c),

and

\displaystyle\omega(a\vartriangleleft b,c)

\displaystyle=\omega(a,c\circ b)=\langle T^{-1}(a),c\circ b\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T^{-1}(a),R_{\circ}(b)c\rangle=-\langle R_{\circ}^{*}(b)T^{-1}(a),c\rangle

\displaystyle=\omega(T((-R_{\circ}^{*})(b)T^{-1}(a)),c).

By the nondegenerate property of
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain

(19)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright b=T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(a)T^{-1}(b)),\;\;a\vartriangleleft b=T((-R_{\circ}^{*})(b)T^{-1}(a)),\;\;a,b\in A.

Set
a=T(f),\ b=T(g)
. Define

\displaystyle f\vartriangleright_{*}g

\displaystyle:=(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g,

\displaystyle f\vartriangleleft_{*}g

\displaystyle:=(-R_{\circ}^{*})(T(g))f,\quad f,g\in A^{*}.

Then we have

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright b

\displaystyle=T(f)\vartriangleright T(g)=T(f\vartriangleright_{*}g),

\displaystyle a\vartriangleleft b

\displaystyle=T(f)\vartriangleleft T(g)=T(f\vartriangleleft_{*}g),\;\;a,b\in A.

If we prove that
(A^{\ast},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
is a pre-Novikov algebra, then
(A,\lhd,\rhd)
is a pre-Novikov algebra and
T
is an isomorphism of pre-Novikov algebras. Therefore, we only need to show that
(A^{\ast},\vartriangleleft_{*},

\vartriangleright_{*})
is a pre-Novikov algebra.

For all
f
,
g
,
h\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle h,T(f)\circ T(g)-T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(g))f)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\omega(T(h),T(f)\circ T(g))-\omega(T(h),T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(g))f))

\displaystyle\quad=\omega(T(h),T(f)\circ T(g))+\omega(T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(g))f),T(h))

\displaystyle\quad=\omega(T(h),T(f)\circ T(g))+\langle(L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(g))f,T(h)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\omega(T(h),T(f)\circ T(g))-\langle g,T(f)\circ T(h)+T(h)\circ T(f)\rangle+\langle f,T(h)\circ T(g)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\omega(T(h),T(f)\circ T(g))-\omega(T(g),T(f)\circ T(h)+T(h)\circ T(f))+\omega(T(f),T(h)\circ T(g))

\displaystyle\quad=0.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle T(f)\circ T(g)-T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(T(f))g-R_{\circ}^{*}(T(g))f)=0.

Hence
T:A^{*}\rightarrow A
defined by Eq. (
18
) is an invertible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(A^{*},L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*},-R_{\circ}^{*})
.
By Proposition
2.8
,
(A^{\ast},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
is a pre-Novikov algebra.

Moreover,

\displaystyle a\lhd b+a\rhd b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T((L_{\circ}^{*}+R_{\circ}^{*})(a)T^{-1}(b))+T((-R_{\circ}^{*})(b)T^{-1}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(f)\circ T(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ b.

Therefore,
(A,\circ)
is the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\lhd,\rhd)
.

The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras and pre-Novikov bialgebras

In this section, we introduce the definitions of double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras and pre-Novikov bialgebras, and show that the double construction of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras is equivalent to a pre-Novikov bialgebra, which is also characterized by some matched pair of Novikov algebras.

First, we recall matched pairs of Novikov algebras.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
10
]

Let
(A,\circ)
and
(B,\bullet)
be Novikov algebras. If
(B,l_{A},r_{A})
is a representation of
(A,\circ)
,
(A,l_{B},r_{B})
is a representation of
(B,\bullet)
and the following conditions are satisfied:

(20)

\displaystyle l_{B}(x)(a\circ b)=-l_{B}(l_{A}(a)x-r_{A}(a)x)b+(l_{B}(x)a-r_{B}(x)a)\circ b+r_{B}(r_{A}(b)x)a+a\circ(l_{B}(x)b),

(21)

\displaystyle r_{B}(x)(a\circ b-b\circ a)=r_{B}(l_{A}(b)x)a-r_{B}(l_{A}(a)x)b+a\circ(r_{B}(x)b)-b\circ(r_{B}(x)a),

(22)

\displaystyle l_{A}(a)(x\bullet y)=-l_{A}(l_{B}(x)a-r_{B}(x)a)y+(l_{A}(a)x-r_{A}(a)x)\bullet y+r_{A}(r_{B}(y)a)x+x\bullet(l_{A}(a)y),

(23)

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)(x\bullet y-y\bullet x)=r_{A}(l_{B}(y)a)x-r_{A}(l_{B}(x)a)y+x\bullet(r_{A}(a)y)-y\bullet(r_{A}(a)x),

(24)

\displaystyle(l_{B}(x)a)\circ b+l_{B}(r_{A}(a)x)b=(l_{B}(x)b)\circ a+l_{B}(r_{A}(b)x)a,

(25)

\displaystyle(r_{B}(x)a)\circ b+l_{B}(l_{A}(a)x)b=r_{B}(x)(a\circ b),

(26)

\displaystyle l_{A}(r_{B}(x)a)y+(l_{A}(a)x)\bullet y=l_{A}(r_{B}(y)a)x+(l_{A}(a)y)\bullet x,

(27)

\displaystyle l_{A}(l_{B}(x)a)y+(r_{A}(a)x)\bullet y=r_{A}(a)(x\bullet y),\quad a,b\in A,x,y\in B,

then there is a Novikov algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus B
of the underlying vector spaces of
A
and
B
given by

(28)

\displaystyle(a+x)\cdot(b+y):=(a\circ b+l_{B}(x)b+r_{B}(y)a)+(x\bullet y+l_{A}(a)y+r_{A}(b)x),\;\;a,b\in A,\;\;x,y\in B.

(A,B,l_{A},r_{A},l_{B},r_{B})
satisfying the above conditions is called a
matched pair of Novikov algebras.
Conversely, any Novikov algebra that can be decomposed into a linear direct sum of two Novikov subalgebras is obtained from a matched pair of Novikov algebras.

Next, we introduce the definition of double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to pre-Novikov algebras.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
,
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})
be pre-Novikov algebras and
(A,\circ)
,
(A^{\ast},\circ_{\ast})
be their associated Novikov algebras respectively. If a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra
(B,\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

B
is the direct sum of
A
and
A^{*}
as vector spaces,

(b)

(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})
are pre-Novikov subalgebras of
(B,\trianglelefteq,\trianglerighteq)
, which is the associated pre-Novikov algebra of
(B,\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
,

(c)

the bilinear form
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
on
B=A\oplus A^{*}
is given by

(29)

\displaystyle\omega(a+f,b+g)=\langle f,b\rangle-\langle g,a\rangle,\quad a,b\in A,\;\;f,g\in A^{*},

then
(B,\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
is called a
double construction of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras
associated to
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})
.

We give a characterization of double constructions of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to pre-Novikov algebras by matched pairs of Novikov algebras.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
be a pre-Novikov algebra and
(A,\circ)
be the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
. Suppose that there exists a pre-Novikov algebra structure
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
on the dual vector space
A^{*}
and
(A^{*},\circ_{*})
is the associated Novikov algebra of
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
. Then there is a double construction of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})
if and only if
(A,A^{*},L_{\vartriangleright}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*},-R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*},L_{\vartriangleright_{*}}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*},-R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})
is a matched pair of Novikov algebras.

Proof.

Suppose that
(A,A^{*},L_{\vartriangleright}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*},-R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*},L_{\vartriangleright_{*}}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*},-R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})
is a matched pair of Novikov algebras. Then by Proposition
3.1
, there is a Novikov algebra structure
(A\oplus A^{\ast},\cdot)
on the vector space
A\oplus A^{*}
given by Eq. (
28
). By Eq. (
29
), for all
a,b,c\in A
, and
f,g,h\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega((a+f)\cdot(b+g),c+h)

\displaystyle\;=\omega((a\circ b+(L_{\vartriangleright_{*}}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})(f)b+(-R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})(g)a)+(f\circ_{*}g+(L_{\vartriangleright}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*})(a)g+(-R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*})(b)f),c+h)

\displaystyle\;=\langle f\circ_{*}g+(L_{\vartriangleright}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*})(a)g+(-R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*})(b)f,c\rangle-\langle h,a\circ b+(L_{\vartriangleright_{*}}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})(f)b+(-R_{\vartriangleleft_{*}}^{*})(g)a\rangle

\displaystyle\;=\langle f,c\vartriangleleft b\rangle-\langle g,a\vartriangleright c+c\vartriangleleft a\rangle-\langle h,a\circ b\rangle-\langle h\vartriangleleft_{*}g,a\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\;+\langle f\vartriangleright_{*}h+h\vartriangleleft_{*}f,b\rangle+\langle f\circ_{*}g,c\rangle.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega((a+f)\cdot(c+h),b+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,b\vartriangleleft c\rangle-\langle g,a\circ c\rangle-\langle h,a\vartriangleright b+b\vartriangleleft a\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle g\vartriangleleft_{*}h,a\rangle+\langle f\circ_{*}h,b\rangle+\langle f\vartriangleright_{*}g+g\vartriangleleft_{*}f,c\rangle,

\displaystyle\omega((c+h)\cdot(a+f),b+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle f,c\vartriangleright b+b\vartriangleleft c\rangle-\langle g,c\circ a\rangle+\langle h,b\vartriangleleft a\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle h\vartriangleright_{*}g+g\vartriangleleft_{*}h,a\rangle+\langle h\circ_{*}f,b\rangle-\langle g\vartriangleleft_{*}f,c\rangle,

\displaystyle\omega((c+h)\cdot(b+g),a+f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle f,c\circ b\rangle-\langle g,c\vartriangleright a+a\vartriangleleft c\rangle+\langle h,a\vartriangleleft b\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle h\circ_{*}g,a\rangle+\langle h\vartriangleright_{*}f+f\vartriangleleft_{*}h,b\rangle-\langle f\vartriangleleft_{*}g,c\rangle.

Hence we get

\displaystyle\omega((a+f)\cdot(b+g),c+h)-\omega((a+f)\cdot(c+h)+(c+h)\cdot(a+f),b+g)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\omega((c+h)\cdot(b+g),a+f)

\displaystyle\;\;=\langle f,c\vartriangleleft b-b\vartriangleleft c+(c\vartriangleright b+b\vartriangleleft c)-c\circ b\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\langle g,-(a\vartriangleright c+c\vartriangleleft a)+a\circ c+c\circ a-(c\vartriangleright a+a\vartriangleleft c)\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\langle h,-a\circ b+(a\vartriangleright b+b\vartriangleleft a)-b\vartriangleleft a+a\vartriangleleft b\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\langle a,-h\vartriangleleft_{*}g+g\vartriangleleft_{*}h-(h\vartriangleright_{*}g+g\vartriangleleft_{*}h)+h\circ_{*}g\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\langle b,(f\vartriangleright_{*}h+h\vartriangleleft_{*}f)-f\circ_{*}h-h\circ_{*}f+(h\vartriangleright_{*}f+f\vartriangleleft_{*}h)\rangle

\displaystyle\;\;=0.

(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
is a quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebra. Then by Theorem
2.10
, there is a compatible pre-Novikov algebra structure on

. Denote it by
(A\oplus A^{*},\trianglelefteq,\trianglerighteq)
. It suffices to check that
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
are pre-Novikov subalgebras of
(A\oplus A^{*},\trianglelefteq,\trianglerighteq)
. For all
a,b,c\in A,h\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(a\trianglerighteq b,c+h)-\omega(a\vartriangleright b,c+h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(a\cdot(c+h),b)+\omega((c+h)\cdot a,b)-\omega(a\vartriangleright b,c+h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle h,a\vartriangleright b+b\vartriangleleft a\rangle+\langle h,b\vartriangleleft a\rangle+\langle h,a\vartriangleright b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

and

\displaystyle\omega(a\trianglelefteq b,c+h)-\omega(a\vartriangleleft b,c+h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(a,(c+h)\cdot b)-\omega(a\vartriangleleft b,c+h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle h,a\vartriangleleft b\rangle+\langle h,a\vartriangleleft b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

By the nondegenerate property of
\omega(\cdot,\cdot)
, we obtain
a\trianglerighteq b=a\vartriangleright b
and
a\trianglelefteq b=a\vartriangleleft b
for all

. Hence
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
is a pre-Novikov subalgebra of
(A\oplus A^{*},\trianglelefteq,\trianglerighteq)
. Similarly, we can prove that
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{*},\vartriangleright_{*})
is a pre-Novikov subalgebra of
(A\oplus A^{*},\trianglelefteq,\trianglerighteq)
.
Hence
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))
is a double construction of quasi-Frobenius Novikov algebras associated to
(A,\vartriangleleft,\vartriangleright)
and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})
.

Conversely, suppose that
(A\oplus A^{*},\cdot,\omega(\cdot,\cdot))

and
(A^{*},\vartriangleleft_{\ast},\vartriangleright_{\ast})

3.1
, the Novikov algebra

is obtained from a matched pair of Novikov algebras
(A,A^{\ast},l_{A},r_{A},l_{A^{\ast}},r_{A^{\ast}})

\cdot
is given by Eq. (

).
By Theorem
2.10
, we have

\displaystyle\langle l_{A}(a)f,b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a\cdot f,b\rangle=\omega(b,a\cdot f)=\omega(b,a\cdot f+f\cdot a)-\omega(b,f\cdot a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(f,a\vartriangleright b)+\omega(f,b\vartriangleleft a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(L_{\vartriangleright}^{*}+R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*})(a)f,b\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle r_{A}(a)f,b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f\cdot a,b\rangle=\omega(b,f\cdot a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(b\vartriangleleft a,f)=\langle R_{\vartriangleleft}(a)b,f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle R_{\vartriangleleft}^{*}(a)f,b\rangle,\quad\quad\quad a,b\in A,f\in A^{*}.

Therefore, we obtain

. Similarly, we can prove

The proof is completed.

Next, we introduce the definition of pre-Novikov coalgebras.

Definition 3.4
.

A
pre-Novikov coalgebra
is a vector space
A
with linear maps
\alpha,\beta:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
satisfying

(30)

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(a)+(\tau\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes\alpha)\beta(a)-({\rm id}\otimes(\alpha+\beta))\alpha(a)-(\tau\otimes{\rm id})(\beta\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(a)=0,

(31)

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\beta)\beta(a)+(\tau\otimes{\rm id})((\alpha+\beta)\otimes{\rm id})\beta(a)-((\alpha+\beta)\otimes{\rm id})\beta(a)-(\tau\otimes{\rm id})({\rm id}\otimes\beta)\beta(a)=0,

(32)

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\beta\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(a)-((\alpha+\beta)\otimes{\rm id})\beta(a)=0,

(33)

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\tau)(\alpha\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(a)-(\alpha\otimes{\rm id})\alpha(a)=0,\quad a\in A.

Denote it by
(A,\alpha,\beta)
.

Let
V
and
W
be vector spaces. Suppose that
\phi:V\rightarrow W
is a linear map. Then there is an induced dual linear map
\phi^{*}:W^{*}\rightarrow V^{*}
defined by

(34)

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{*}(f),v\rangle=\langle f,\phi(v)\rangle,\quad v\in V,f\in W^{*}.

It is easy to see that

(A,\alpha,\beta)
is a pre-Novikov coalgebra if and only if
(A^{*},\alpha^{*},\beta^{*})
is a pre-Novikov algebra.

A KAM theorem of symplectic algorithms for nearly integrabel Hamiltonian systems
Zaijiu Shang, Yang Xu

Abstract
In this paper we prove a KAM-like theorem of symplectic algorithms for nearly integrable Hamiltonian systems which generalises the result of [ 1 ] and [ 5 ] for the case of integrable systems.

1
Introduction

In
[
1
]
the author established a KAM theorem of symplectic algorithms for integrable Hamiltonian systems and left the question of generalization to nearly integrable systems open. In this paper we will solve it. The notations of
[
1
]
will be still used.

Consider the nearly integrable Hamiltonian system :

\dot{p}=-\frac{\partial H^{\epsilon}}{\partial q}(p,q),\ \dot{q}=\frac{\partial H^{\epsilon}}{\partial p}(p,q),\ (p,q)\in D,

(1.1)

where
H^{\epsilon}(p,q)=H^{0}(p)+\epsilon H(p,q,\epsilon)
is the Hamiltonian function,

D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a bounded connected open domain, and the dot is the derivative with respect to time
t
.
For such nearly integrable Hamiltonian system, if the perturbation is small enough and the Hamiltonian function is sufficiently smooth, then there exist invariant tori, which is what we know.
In this paper, we will use the method of discretization of symplectic algorithm to show that the system after the discretization of symplectic algorithm also has invariant tori.
Compared with the invariant tori of the nearly integrable Hamiltonian system, there is only slight deformation between them.
Without loss of generality, we can assume
\lvert H\rvert_{D}\leq M_{1}
, where
\lvert\cdot\lvert_{D}
denotes the supremum norm on
D
,
M_{1}
is a constant, and we can ignore the parameter expression of
\epsilon
.

Denote
\omega
as the derivative of
H^{0}
,
\omega(\xi)=\frac{\partial H^{0}}{\partial\xi}(\xi)
, which is defined on
V_{\kappa}
, where
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded connected domain,
V_{\kappa}=V+\kappa:=\bigcup\limits_{b\in V}\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\lvert\xi-b\rvert_{2}<\kappa\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\kappa
is a constant.

Use symplectic algorithm to discretize the Hamiltonian system (
1.1
). By Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.3 in
[
1
]
, there is a function
P
, which depends on time step
t
, and it is well defined and real analytic on
D_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
, such that if time step
t
is small enough,
0<t<\delta
, where
\delta
is a small enough constant, then the symplectic differential form of the system (
1.1
) after discretization by symplectic algorithm can be expressed as
G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}:(p,q)\to(\hat{p},\hat{q})

G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}:\begin{cases}\hat{p}=p-t\epsilon\frac{\partial S}{\partial q}(\hat{p},q)-t^{\alpha+1}\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial q}(\hat{p},q),\\
\hat{q}=q+t\omega+t\epsilon\frac{\partial S}{\partial\hat{p}}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial\hat{p}}(\hat{p},q),\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
(\hat{p},q)\in D_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
, and
\alpha
is a positive constant.
Here
S
is a generating function,
S(p,q,t)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(S)_{k}(p,q)t^{k}=H^{1}(p,q)+(S)_{1}(p,q)t+(S)_{2}(p,q)t^{2}+\dots
, and
D_{r,s}
refers to

D_{r,s}=\{p:\inf\limits_{p^{*}\in V}\lvert p-p^{*}\rvert<r\}\times\{q:\Re q\in\mathbb{T}^{n},\lvert\Im q\rvert<s\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}.

By Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.3 in
[
1
]
,

\big\lvert\frac{\partial P}{\partial{\hat{p}}}\big\rvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}\leq M_{2}
,
\big\lvert\frac{\partial P}{\partial q}\big\rvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}\leq M_{2}
, where
\lvert\cdot\lvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
denotes the supremum norm on
D_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
, and
M_{2}
is a constant.
Similar to
[
1
]
, fixed
(\hat{p_{0}},q_{0})\in D
, let
P(\hat{p_{0}},q_{0})=0
, for

(\hat{p},q)\in D_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
, then
we have
\lvert P\rvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}\leq 2nM_{2}l_{*}
, where
l_{*}
is an upper bound of the length of the shortest curves from
(\hat{p_{0}},q_{0})
to
(\hat{p},q)
in
D_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}
.
And,
\lvert G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}(p,q)-g_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}(p,q)\rvert\leq M_{3}t^{\alpha+1}
, where
g_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}
is the phase flow of the nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.1
), and
M_{3}
is a constant that does not depend on
t
.

For the nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.1
), there exists a generating function
\widetilde{S}
such that its invariant tori can be found by the following system:

\hat{p}=p-t\epsilon\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial q}(\hat{p},q),\ \hat{q}=q+t\omega+t\epsilon\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial\hat{p}}(\hat{p},q),

(1.3)

and for the time step
t
in the common area, we have
S(p,q,t)-\widetilde{S}(p,q,t)\sim o(t^{\alpha})
, let us denote it as
\lvert S-\widetilde{S}\rvert\leq M_{4}t^{\alpha}
, where
M_{4}
is a constant that does not depend on
t
.

In this paper, we assume that
\omega
satisfies the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition.

Remark 1.1
.

\omega
satisfies the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition means that
\omega\in\{\omega(\xi):\xi\in V\}
does not lie in any hyperplane that passes through the origin, i.e.
\langle\omega,y\rangle\not\equiv 0
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
Xu, You and Qiu gave an expression of the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition for analytic cases(see Remark 3.1 in
[
2
]
),
that is, if
\omega
satisfies the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition, then there exists an integer
\bar{n}>0
, such that

rank\big\{\partial^{i}_{\xi}\omega(\xi):|i|\leq\bar{n}\big\}=n,\ \forall\xi\in V.

(1.4)

And, by Rüssmann (Lemma 18.2 in
[
4
]
), there are numbers
\bar{n}(V)\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta_{0}(V)>0
such that

\min_{\xi\in V}\max_{0\leq v\leq\bar{n}}|D^{v}\langle k,\omega(\xi)\rangle|\geq\beta_{0},

(1.5)

where
\langle k,\omega\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\omega_{j}
,
k\in\{c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lvert c_{j}\rvert=1,j=1,\dots,n\}
. We can take the smallest of such integer
\bar{n}
.
Moreover, by Rüssmann (Theorem 18.4 in
[
4
]
), we have

\min_{\xi\in V}\max_{0\leq v\leq\bar{n}}\big\lvert D^{v}{\lvert k\rvert_{2}}^{-2}\lvert\langle k,\omega(\xi)\rangle\rvert^{2}\big\rvert\geq\beta_{0},

(1.6)

for
\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
As
\lvert\cdot\rvert
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{2}
are equivalent, where
\lvert k\rvert=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\lvert k_{j}\rvert
,
\lvert k\rvert_{2}=(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\lvert k_{j}\rvert^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then there exist
\bar{n}
and
\beta
such that

\min_{\xi\in V}\max_{0\leq v\leq\bar{n}}|D^{v}\langle k,\omega(\xi)\rangle|\geq\beta\lvert k\rvert,

(1.7)

for
\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, where
\bar{n}=\bar{n}(\omega,V)\in\mathbb{N}
is called the index of non-degeneracy of
\omega
with respect to
V
, and
\beta=\beta(\omega,V)>0
is called the amount of non-degeneracy of
\omega
with respect to
V
.

Fixed
\tau\geq(n+2)(\bar{n}+1)
,
\gamma>0
and
0<t<1
, introduce the concept of Diophantine condition.
The Diophantine condition refers to that
\omega(\xi)
satisfies

\lvert e^{i\langle k,t\omega\rangle}-1\rvert\geq\frac{t\gamma}{{\lvert k\rvert}^{\tau}},\ \forall\ k=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

(1.8)

where
\xi\in V_{\kappa}
,
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n})
,
\langle k,\omega\rangle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\omega_{j}
,
\lvert k\rvert=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\lvert k_{j}\rvert
.
\lvert\cdot\lvert_{r,s}
denotes the supremum norm on
D_{r,s}
.

2
Main theorem

Theorem 2.1
.

For the nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.1
) ,
H
is real analytic on
D_{r,s}\times V_{\kappa}
, and
H^{0}
is analytic on
V_{\kappa}
.
If time step
t
and disturbance parameter
\epsilon
are small enough,
\omega
satisfies the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition,
then the generating function representation of symplectic algorithm (
1.2
) has invariant tori on a set of large measures.
The generating function representation of phase flow in
nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.3
) also has invariant tori on a set of large measures, and there is only a slight deformation in the common area. That is to say, for system (
1.2
), there is a non-empty Cantor set
V_{\epsilon,t}\subseteq V
and a Whitney smooth symplectic mapping

\Phi_{\epsilon,t}:V_{\epsilon,t}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
that makes the following true.

(i)

\Phi_{\epsilon,t}
is a symplectic conjugation between
G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}
and
R_{\epsilon,t}
, i.e.,

\Phi_{\epsilon,t}^{-1}\circ G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}\circ\Phi_{\epsilon,t}=R_{\epsilon,t}
, where
R_{\epsilon,t}
is a rotation on
V_{\epsilon,t}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
with frequency
t\omega_{\epsilon,t}
, i.e.,
R_{\epsilon,t}(p,q)=(p,q+t\omega_{\epsilon,t})
.

(ii)

V_{\epsilon,t}
is a set of positive measure if
\gamma
is small enough. And for
\gamma\to 0
, we have
\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\to 0
.
To be specific,

\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\leq c_{7}\gamma d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d),

(2.1)

where
c_{7}=12\bar{c}(2\pi e)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(\bar{n}+2)^{\bar{n}+3}[(\bar{n}+2)!]^{-1}\hat{K}\beta^{-\frac{\bar{n}+2}{\bar{n}+1}}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\hat{K}=\lvert\omega\rvert_{V}+c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}(M_{1}+2nM_{2}l_{*})+1
,
\mathcal{B}=(V\times(0,1)+\kappa)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
d
is the diameter of
V\times(0,1)
,
\bar{c}
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\bar{n}
and
\tau
, and
c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}
is a constant depending only on
\tau,n,\bar{n},\gamma,\rho_{0},\sigma_{0}
and
K_{0}
.

(iii)

Let the time step be
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
respectively, and comppare the two systems, then for
\xi\in V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}}
we have

\lvert\Phi_{\epsilon,t_{1}}-\Phi_{\epsilon,t_{2}}\rvert\leq 2c_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}nM_{2}l_{*}(t_{1}^{\alpha}-t_{2}^{\alpha}),\ \lvert\omega_{\epsilon,t_{1}}-\omega_{\epsilon,t_{2}}\rvert\leq 2c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}nM_{2}l_{*}(t_{1}^{\alpha}-t_{2}^{\alpha}),

where
M_{2}
and
l_{*}
are constants, and
c_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}
are constants depending only on
\tau,n,\bar{n},\rho_{0},\sigma_{0},\gamma_{0}
and
K_{0}
.

V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}}
is also a set of positive measure if
\gamma
is small enough. And for
\gamma\to 0
, we also have
\lvert V\setminus(V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}})\rvert\to 0
.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.1

3.1
One-step Iterative Analysis

Firstly, we focus on one-step iteration.
Set

V_{\gamma,t,v}=\big\{\xi\in V:\lvert e^{i\langle k,t\omega_{v}(\xi)\rangle}-1\rvert\geq\frac{t\gamma_{v}}{{\lvert k\rvert}^{\tau}},\ \forall\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\big\},

that is, for
\xi\in V_{\gamma,t,v}
, we have
\omega_{v}(\xi)
satisfies the Diophantine condition (
1.8
).
It is a Cantor set, about which measure estimates are given later. Whitney analytical extension was carried out for
\omega_{v}(\xi)
on
V_{\gamma,t,v}
and denoted as
V_{\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}}=V_{\gamma,t,v}+\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}
.

Denote the one-step transforming of the symplectic difference scheme as

G_{v}:(p,q)\to(\hat{p},\hat{q})
with

G_{v}:\begin{cases}\hat{p}=p-\partial_{2}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big),\\
\hat{q}=q+t\omega_{v}+\partial_{1}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big),\end{cases}

(3.1)

here the subscript
v
represents the
v
-th step.
Assume the one-step symplectic mapping can be expressed through the generating function
\psi_{v}
as
\Psi_{v}:(I,\theta)\to(p,q)
with

\Psi_{v}:\begin{cases}I=p-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q),\\
\theta=q+\partial_{1}\psi_{v},(I,q),\end{cases}

(3.2)

so we have
(I,\theta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi_{v}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(p,q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle G_{v}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\hat{p},\hat{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi_{v}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\hat{I},\hat{\theta})
, and
\Psi_{v}^{-1}\circ G_{v}\circ\Psi_{v}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{I}

\displaystyle=\hat{p}-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})

\displaystyle=p-\partial_{2}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big)-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})

\displaystyle=I-\partial_{2}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big)+\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q)-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q}),

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}=

\displaystyle\ \hat{q}+\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ q+t\omega_{v}+\partial_{1}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big)+\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \theta+t\omega_{v}+\partial_{1}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(\hat{p},q)\big)-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)+\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q}).

Note that for
0<t<\delta
, we have

\big\lvert\partial_{1}\big(t\epsilon S_{0}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{0}(\hat{p},q)\big)\big\rvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}\leq\frac{M_{1}t\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha+1}}{r/4},

\big\lvert\partial_{2}\big(t\epsilon S_{0}(\hat{p},q)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{0}(\hat{p},q)]\big)\big\rvert_{\frac{r}{4},\frac{s}{4}}\leq\frac{M_{1}t\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha+1}}{s/4}.

Thus, for
\epsilon
and
t
small enough such that
\frac{M_{1}\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha}}{r/4},\frac{M_{1}\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha}}{s/4}\leq E_{0}
, then, if
r_{0}\leq\frac{r}{4}
,
s_{0}\leq\frac{s}{4}
, we can denote
\big\lvert\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{0}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{0})\big\rvert_{r_{0},s_{0}}\leq tE_{0}
,

\big\lvert\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{0}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{0})\big\lvert_{r_{0},s_{0}}\leq tE_{0}
, here
E_{0}
is also small enough as
\epsilon
and
t
are small enough.

Similarly, we can assume that

\big\lvert\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})\big\rvert_{r_{v},s_{v}}\leq tE_{v}
as well as

\big\lvert\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})\big\lvert_{r_{v},s_{v}}\leq tE_{v}
, where
E_{v}
is also a small quantity.
Then, as
\psi_{v}
and
(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})
are small quantities, we can get the following analysis:

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)=\

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)=\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,\theta)+o(E_{v}),

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})=\

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi_{v}\big(I-\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)+\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q)-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q}),

\displaystyle\theta+t\omega_{v}+\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta+t\omega_{v}(\xi)\big)+o(E_{v}),

\displaystyle\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle P_{v})(\hat{p},q)=\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})\big(I-\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)

\displaystyle\ +\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q),\theta-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(I,\theta)+o(E_{v}).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q)=\

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)=\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,\theta)+o(E_{v}),

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q})=\

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\psi_{v}\big(I-\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)+\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q)-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(\hat{I},\hat{q}),

\displaystyle\theta+t\omega_{v}+\partial_{1}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta+t\omega_{v}(\xi)\big)+o(E_{v}),

\displaystyle\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle P_{v})(\hat{p},q)=\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})\big(I-\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(\hat{p},q)

\displaystyle\ +\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,q),\theta-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,q)\big)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\partial_{2}(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})(I,\theta)+o(E_{v}).

So, we have
\Psi_{v}^{-1}\circ G_{v}\circ\Psi_{v}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{I}=

\displaystyle\ I-\partial_{2}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)+\partial_{2}\psi_{v}(I,\theta)

\displaystyle-\partial_{2}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta+t\omega_{v}\big)+o(E_{v}),

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}=

\displaystyle\ \theta+t\omega_{v}(\xi)+\partial_{1}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)-\partial_{1}\psi_{v}(I,\theta)

\displaystyle+\partial_{1}\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta+t\omega_{v}\big)+o(E_{v}).

Looking at this expression, we can truncate
t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}
by zero degree term and one degree term with respect to the action variables to get
(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}
.
For general function
f(I,\theta)
, entry notation
[f]
and Fourier coefficients
f_{k}(I)
,

\displaystyle f(I,\theta)=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f_{k}(I)e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}=\widetilde{f(I,\theta)}+[f],

\displaystyle\widetilde{f(I,\theta)}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f_{k}(I)e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}.

To get
\psi_{v}
, we consider the following equation:

\psi_{v}\big(I,\theta+t\omega_{v}\big)-\psi_{v}(I,\theta)+\widetilde{\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}}=0,

where

\displaystyle\ \widetilde{\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}-\big[\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\big((t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}\big)_{k}(I)e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}.

Here
\big[(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}\big]
is the mean values of
(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}
with respect to the angle variables on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\big[(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}d\theta.

Define
\omega_{v+1}=\omega_{v}+\big[\partial_{1}(\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha}P_{v})^{*}\big(\xi)]
. By the way,
[\partial_{2}(\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha}P_{v})^{*}\big]=0
.
Moreover, we truncate the Fourier series expansion of
(t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}
with respect to angle variables by order
K_{v}
.
And then we get the final homological equation:

\psi_{v}(I,\theta+t\omega_{v})-\psi_{v}(I,\theta)+T_{K_{v}}\widetilde{\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}}=0,

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle T_{K_{v}}\widetilde{\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}}=\sum\limits_{0<\lvert k\rvert\leq K_{v}}\big((t\epsilon S_{v}+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}\big)_{k}e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}.

To solve
\psi_{v}
in the homological equation (
3.3
), we can expand
\psi_{v}(I,\theta+t\omega_{v})
,
\psi_{v}(I,\theta)
and
\big(t\epsilon S_{v}(I,\theta)+t^{\alpha+1}P_{v}(I,\theta)\big)^{*}
by Fourier series, and then compare their coefficients. Their Fourier series expansion are shown below:

\displaystyle\psi_{v}(I,\theta+t\omega_{v})=

\displaystyle\leq c_{6}d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d)(\frac{\gamma_{v}}{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\bar{n}+1}}\frac{1}{\beta}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}\lvert k\rvert^{\frac{\bar{n}+1-\tau}{\bar{n}+1}},

where
c_{6}=6(2\pi e)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(\bar{n}+2)^{\bar{n}+3}[(\bar{n}+2)!]^{-1}\hat{K}
.

By
V_{\epsilon,t}=\bigcap\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}V_{\gamma,t,v}
,
we have the following inequality.

\displaystyle\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert R_{v}\rvert\ \leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\lvert R_{v}^{k}\rvert\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}\lvert R_{v}^{r}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}c_{6}d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d)(\frac{\gamma_{v}}{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\bar{n}+1}}\frac{1}{\beta}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}r^{\frac{\bar{n}+1-\tau}{\bar{n}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}r^{\frac{\bar{n}+1-\tau}{\bar{n}+1}}c_{6}d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d)

\displaystyle\ \cdot\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{v}^{\frac{1}{\bar{n}+1}}\beta^{-\frac{\bar{n}+2}{\bar{n}+1}}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}.

And for
\tau\geq(n+2)(\bar{n}+1)
,

\bar{c}:=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}r^{\frac{\bar{n}+1-\tau}{\bar{n}+1}}
is convergent.
Besides,
\gamma_{v}=\frac{\gamma^{\bar{n}+1}}{2^{(\bar{n}+1)v}}
,
we have
\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{v}^{\frac{1}{\bar{n}+1}}=\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma}{2^{v}}=2\gamma
, and
\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}<\infty
by analysis, therefore,

\displaystyle\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bar{c}\cdot 2\gamma\cdot c_{6}d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d)\beta^{-\frac{\bar{n}+2}{\bar{n}+1}}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{7}\gamma d^{n}((n+1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+2d+\kappa^{-1}d),

where
c_{7}=12\bar{c}(2\pi e)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(\bar{n}+2)^{\bar{n}+3}[(\bar{n}+2)!]^{-1}\hat{K}\beta^{-\frac{\bar{n}+2}{\bar{n}+1}}\lvert\omega\rvert^{\bar{n}+2}_{\mathcal{B}}
,

\bar{c}
depends only on
n
,
\bar{n}
,
\tau
.
So for
\gamma
small enough ,
V_{\epsilon,t}
can be a set of positive measures. And for
\gamma\to 0
, we have
\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\to 0
. Then

(ii)
in theorem
2.1
proves.

Similarly, we know that
V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}}
is also a set of positive measure if
\gamma
is small enough. Since
\lvert V\setminus(V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}})\rvert\leq\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\rvert+\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t_{2}}\rvert
, we also have
\lvert V\setminus(V_{\epsilon,t_{1}}\cap V_{\epsilon,t_{2}})\rvert\to 0
as
\gamma\to 0
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.
\square

4
Kolmogorov’s non-degeneracy condition

Remark 4.1
.

Without the Rüssmann’s non-degeneracy condition, Theorem
2.1
also holds for
\omega
satisfying the Kolmogorov’s non-degeneracy condition
\Theta_{1}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert\leq\lvert\omega(\xi_{1})-\omega(\xi_{2})\rvert\leq\Theta_{2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert
, where
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
are constants.
Moreover, when time step
t
and disturbance parameter
\epsilon
are small enough,
both the generating function representation of symplectic algorithm (
1.2
) and
the generating function representation of phase flow in
nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.3
) have invariant tori on corresponding sets of large measures, and there is only a slight deformation in the common area. The Hausdorff’s distance between the invariant tori in the phase spaces of the two systems is about
o(t^{\alpha})
.

We explain this remark in three steps.

Step1:

To be specific, if
\tau>n+2
,
\gamma_{v}
is changed to
\frac{\gamma}{2^{v}}
,
c_{3}
and
c_{4}
are changed to
2^{\tau+n+2}
and
\frac{1}{3}\cdot 2^{-(\tau+n+4)}F_{3}^{\frac{1}{3}}
respectively, and the other parameters reduce the dependence on
\bar{n}
, then we can construct a similar iterative procedure.
That is to say, there is a non-empty Cantor set
V_{\epsilon,t}\subseteq V
and a Whitney smooth symplectic mapping

\Phi_{\epsilon,t}
such that

\Phi_{\epsilon,t}^{-1}\circ G_{H^{\epsilon}}^{t}\circ\Phi_{\epsilon,t}=R_{\epsilon,t}
, and

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi_{\epsilon,t}-id\rvert\leq c_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(M_{1}\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha}),

\displaystyle\lvert\omega_{\epsilon,t}-\omega\rvert\leq c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}(M_{1}\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha}),

where
c_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}
and
c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}
depend only on
\tau,n,\rho_{0},\sigma_{0},\gamma_{0}
and
K_{0}
.

As for the corresponding measure estimation, let
\frac{t\gamma}{{\lvert k\rvert}^{\tau}}<1
, then we have

|R^{k,l}_{v}|=|\big\{\xi\in V:\lvert\langle k,t\omega_{v}\rangle-2\pi l\rvert<t\gamma_{v}\lvert k\rvert^{-\tau}\big\}|\leq\frac{2\gamma_{v}}{|k|^{\tau+1}\Theta_{1}}.

So,

\lvert R^{k}_{v}\rvert\leq 2\lvert k\rvert\hat{K}\lvert R^{k,l}_{v}\rvert\leq\frac{4\gamma_{v}\hat{K}}{|k|^{\tau}\Theta_{1}},

here
\hat{K}
stays the same, then, we get

\displaystyle\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert R_{v}\rvert\ \leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\lvert R_{v}^{k}\rvert\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}\lvert R_{v}^{r}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}\frac{4\gamma_{v}\hat{K}}{r^{\tau}\Theta_{1}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{r=1}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}\frac{8\gamma_{0}\hat{K}}{r^{\tau}\Theta_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{c}\frac{8\gamma_{0}\hat{K}}{\Theta_{1}},

where
\bar{c}:=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{\infty}2n(2r+1)^{n-1}\frac{1}{r^{\tau}}
is convergent.
That is to say, we also get that
V_{\epsilon,t}
can be a set of positive measures for
\gamma
small enough, and
\lvert V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\to 0
as
\gamma\to 0
.

And, more importantly, in the Kolmogorov’s non-degeneracy condition, the frequency mapping is a local differential homeomorphism. For all
w_{*}=\omega(\xi),\xi\in V\setminus V_{\epsilon,t}
, there exist
x_{v}\in V
, such that
\omega_{v}(x_{v})=w_{*}
,
v=0,1,2,\dots
. Let
v\to\infty
, then there exists
x_{0}\in V
, such that
\omega_{0}(x_{0})=w_{*}=\omega_{\infty}(x_{\infty})
, and we have

\lvert x_{\infty}-x_{0}\rvert\leq\frac{c_{4}^{{}^{\prime}}(M_{1}\epsilon+2nM_{2}l_{*}t^{\alpha})}{\Theta_{1}}.

Step 2:

We can also do the same for the generating function representation of phase flow in
nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.3
).
For
\xi\in\widetilde{V}_{\epsilon,t}
, where

\widetilde{V}_{\epsilon,t}=\bigcap\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{V}_{\gamma,t,v}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}-id\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert\widetilde{\Phi}_{v+1}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}\rvert\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{2}\lvert\widetilde{\Phi}_{v+1}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}\rvert+\sum\limits_{v=3}^{\infty}\frac{4c_{1}\widetilde{E}_{v}}{\gamma_{v}\sigma_{v}^{\tau+n}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{c}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon+8c_{1}\widetilde{F}_{0}\leq\widetilde{c}_{2}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon,

\displaystyle\lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{v}-\omega\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{v+1}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v}\rvert\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{2}\lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{v+1}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v}\rvert+\sum\limits_{v=3}^{\infty}2\widetilde{E}_{v}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{c}_{3}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon+4\widetilde{F}_{0}\leq\widetilde{c}_{4}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon,

where
\widetilde{c}_{2}
and
\widetilde{c}_{4}
depend only on
\tau,n,\rho_{0},\sigma_{0},\gamma_{0}
and
K_{0}
.
Let
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\epsilon,t}=\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}
,

\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon,t}=\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\widetilde{\omega}_{v}
, then we have

\lvert\widetilde{\Phi}_{\epsilon,t}-id\rvert\leq\widetilde{c}_{2}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon,\ \lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon,t}-\omega\rvert\leq\widetilde{c}_{4}\cdot M_{1}\epsilon.

Step 3:

What is more, since the frequency mapping is a local differential homeomorphism, we can compare the generating function representation of symplectic algorithm (
1.2
) and the generating function representation of phase flow in
nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.3
).

For the time step
t
in the common area, we have

\psi_{v}-\widetilde{\psi}_{v}=-\sum\limits_{0<\lvert k\rvert\leq K_{v}}\frac{\big((t\epsilon S_{v}-t\epsilon\widetilde{S}_{v})^{*}\big)_{k}e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}+\big((t^{\alpha+1}P_{v})^{*}\big)_{k}e^{i\langle k,\theta\rangle}}{e^{i\langle k,t\omega_{v}\rangle}-1},

and

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{1}\psi_{v}-\partial_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{v}\rvert_{s_{v}-\sigma_{v}}\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma_{v}\sigma_{v}^{\tau+n}}\lvert\partial_{1}(\epsilon S_{v}-\epsilon\widetilde{S}_{v}+t^{\alpha}P_{v})\rvert_{s_{v}},

\displaystyle\lvert\partial_{2}\psi_{v}-\partial_{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{v}\rvert_{s_{v}-\sigma_{v}}\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma_{v}\sigma_{v}^{\tau+n}}\lvert\partial_{2}(\epsilon S_{v}-\epsilon\widetilde{S}_{v}+t^{\alpha}P_{v})\rvert_{s_{v}}.

(4.1)

Let
F_{v}^{*}=\frac{E_{v}^{*}}{\gamma_{v}\sigma_{v}^{\tau+n+1}\rho_{v}},F_{v+1}^{*}=(F_{v}^{*})^{\frac{v+1}{v}}
,

E_{0}^{*}=E_{0}-\widetilde{E}_{0}
, where
E_{0}^{*}=o(t^{\alpha})
, then for the time step
t
in the common area, combined with (
), we have

\displaystyle\lvert(\Phi_{v}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v})-(\Phi_{v-1}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v-1})\rvert\leq\frac{4c_{1}E_{v-1}^{*}}{\gamma_{v-1}\sigma_{v-1}^{\tau+n}},

\displaystyle\lvert(\omega_{v}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v})-(\omega_{v-1}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v-1})\rvert\leq 2E_{v}^{*}.

Then, for the common area
\xi\in\bigcap\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}V_{\gamma,t,v}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\gamma,t,v}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\Phi_{v}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert(\Phi_{v+1}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v+1})-(\Phi_{v}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{2}\lvert(\Phi_{v+1}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v+1})-(\Phi_{v}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v})\rvert+\sum\limits_{v=3}^{\infty}\frac{4c_{1}E_{v}^{*}}{\gamma_{v}\sigma_{v}^{\tau+n}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}^{*}\cdot t^{\alpha}+8c_{1}F_{0}^{*}\leq c_{2}^{*}\cdot t^{\alpha},

\displaystyle\lvert\omega_{v}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{\infty}\lvert(\omega_{v+1}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v+1})-(\omega_{v}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{v=0}^{2}\lvert(\omega_{v+1}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v+1})-(\omega_{v}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v})\rvert+\sum\limits_{v=3}^{\infty}2E_{v}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}^{*}\cdot t^{\alpha}+4F_{0}^{*}\leq c_{4}^{*}\cdot t^{\alpha},

where
c_{2}^{*}
and
c_{4}^{*}
depend only on
\tau,n,\rho_{0},\sigma_{0},\gamma_{0}
and
K_{0}
. And by
\Phi_{\epsilon,t}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{\epsilon,t}=\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\Phi_{v}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}
,
\omega_{\epsilon,t}-\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon,t}=\lim\limits_{v\to\infty}\omega_{v}-\widetilde{\omega}_{v}
, we have

\lvert\Phi_{\epsilon,t}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\leq c_{2}^{*}t^{\alpha},\ \lvert\omega_{\epsilon,t}-\widetilde{\omega}_{\epsilon,t}\rvert\leq c_{4}^{*}t^{\alpha}.

And, for all
w_{*}=\omega(\xi),\xi\in V_{\epsilon,t}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\epsilon,t}
, there exist
x_{0},\widetilde{x}_{0}\in V
, such that
\omega_{0}(x_{0})=w_{*}=\widetilde{\omega}_{0}(\widetilde{x}_{0})
, and we have

\lvert x_{0}-\widetilde{x}_{0}\rvert\leq\frac{4c_{4}^{*}t^{\alpha}}{\Theta_{1}},

here
\lvert V\setminus(V_{\epsilon,t}\cap\widetilde{V}_{\epsilon,t})\rvert\to 0
as
\gamma\to 0
.

Thus, if time step
t
and disturbance parameter
\epsilon
are small enough,
\omega
satisfies the Kolmogorov’s non-degeneracy condition,
the generating function representation of symplectic algorithm (
1.2
) has invariant tori on a set of large measures.
The generating function representation of phase flow in
nearly integrable Hamiltonian system (
1.3
) also has invariant tori on a set of large measures, and there is only a slight deformation in the common area. The Hausdorff’s distance between the invariant tori in the phase spaces of the two systems is about
o(t^{\alpha})
.

Abstract
. We study the weighted Fock spaces in one and several complex variables. We evaluate the dimension of these spaces in terms of the weight function extending and completing earlier results by Rozenblum–Shirokov and Shigekawa.



1.
Introduction

Let
\psi
be a plurisubharmonic function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
. The weighted Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
is the space of entire functions
f
such that

\lVert f\rVert_{\psi}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}e^{-\psi(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty,

where
dv
is the volume measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Note that
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},e^{-\psi}\,dv)
and hence is a Hilbert space endowed with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\psi}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}e^{-\psi(z)}\,dv(z),\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}.

In this paper we study when the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
is of finite dimension depending on the weight
\psi
.
This problem (at least for the case
n=1
) is motivated by some quantum mechanics questions, especially by the study of zero modes, eigenfunctions with zero eigenvalues.

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, Rozenblum and Shirokov proposed a sufficient condition for the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
to be of infinite dimension, when
\psi
is a subharmonic function.

More precisely, they claimed that if
\psi
is a finite subharmonic function on the complex plane such that the measure
\mu=\Delta\psi
is of infinite mass:

(1.1)

\mu(\mathbb{C})=\int_{\mathbb{C}}d\mu(z)=\infty,

then the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
has infinite dimension.

(For the fact that if
\mu=\Delta\psi
a non-trivial doubling measure, then
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
has infinite dimension see
[
4
, Theorem 11.45]
).

We improve and extend somewhat the statement of Rozenblum–Shirokov in our paper, give a necessary and sufficient condition on
\psi
for the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
to be of finite dimension,
and calculate this dimension.

The situation is much more complicated in
\mathbb{C}^{n},n\geq 2
. Shigekawa established in
[
10
]
(see also
[
4
, Theorem 11.20]
in a book by Haslinger), the following interesting result.

Theorem A
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
smooth function and let
\lambda_{0}(z)
be the smallest eigenvalue of the Levi matrix

L_{\psi}(z)=i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(z)=\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\psi(z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}\right)_{j,k=1}^{n}.

Suppose that

(1.2)

\lim_{|z|\to\infty}|z|^{2}\lambda_{0}(z)=\infty.

Then
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.

Note that the condition (
1.2
) is not necessary. A corresponding example is given in
[
4
, Section 11.5]
(
\psi(z,w)=|z|^{2}|w|^{2}+|w|^{4}
). In this paper, we improve Theorem A by presenting a weaker condition for the dimension of the Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
to be infinite. Furthermore, we give several examples that show how far is our condition from being necessary. Finally, we consider several examples (classes of examples) of weight
functions
\psi
of special form and evaluate the dimension of
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. The case of dimension one is considered in Section 2, and the case of higher dimension is considered in Section 3.

2.
The case of
\mathbb{C}

Given a subharmonic function
\psi:\mathbb{C}\to[-\infty,\infty)
denote by
\mu_{\psi}
the corresponding Riesz measure,
\mu_{\psi}=\Delta\psi
.
Next, consider the class
\mathcal{M}^{d}
of the positive
\sigma
-finite atomic measures with masses which are integer multiples of
4\pi
.
Given a
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
, consider the corresponding atomic measure
\mu^{d}
,

\mu^{d}=\max\Bigl\{\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{d}:\mu_{1}\leq\mu\Bigr\}.

In fact, for every atom
a\delta_{x}
of
\mu
,
\mu^{d}
has at the point
x
an atom of size
4\pi
times the integer part of
a/(4\pi)
.
Denote
\mu^{c}=\mu-\mu^{d}
,
\mu^{d}=\sum_{k}4\pi\delta_{x_{k,\mu}}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{M}^{c}
the class of the positive
\sigma
-finite measures
\mu
such that
\mu^{d}=0
. Note that if

\psi
is finite on the complex plane, then
\mu_{\psi}
has no point masses and
\mu_{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}^{c}
.
Furthermore, if
\mu_{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}^{c}
, then
e^{-\psi}\in L^{1}_{loc}(v)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\psi,\psi_{1}
be two subharmonic functions such that
(\mu_{\psi})^{c}=(\mu_{\psi_{1}})^{c}
.
Then
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
F,F_{1}
be two entire functions with the zero sets, correspondingly,
\{x_{k,\mu_{\psi}}\}
and
\{x_{k,\mu_{\psi_{1}}}\}

(taking into account the multiplicities). Then

\Delta\log|F|^{2}=(\mu_{\psi})^{d}
,
\Delta\log|F_{1}|^{2}=(\mu_{\psi_{1}})^{d}
, and the functions
h=\psi-\log|F^{2}|-\psi^{c}
,
h_{1}=\psi_{1}-\log|F_{1}^{2}|-\psi_{1}^{c}
are harmonic. Let
h=\Re H
,
h_{1}=\Re H_{1}

for some entire functions
H,H_{1}
.

Given an entire function
f
we have

f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\iff\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi^{c}(z)-h(z)-\log|F(z)|^{2}}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-H(z)/2}/F(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi^{c}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-H(z)/2}/F(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi_{1}^{c}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-H(z)/2}/F(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi_{1}(z)+h_{1}(z)+\log|F_{1}(z)|^{2}}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-H(z)/2+H_{1}(z)/2}|F_{1}(z)/F(z)|^{2}e^{-\psi_{1}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty\iff\\
f\cdot\frac{F_{1}}{F}e^{-H/2+H_{1}/2}\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}.

Thus,
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\psi
be a subharmonic function such that
\mu_{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}^{c}
.
If
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}<\infty
, then
\mu_{\psi}(\mathbb{C})<\infty
.

See the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\psi
be a subharmonic function.
Then

\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\leq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu_{\psi}(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil.

Here and later on, given a real number
x
,
\lceil x\rceil
is the maximal integer smaller than
x
.

Proof.

Set
\mu=\mu_{\psi}
and consider
a modified logarithmic potential
G
of the measure
\mu
:

G(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D(0,2)}\log\lvert z-w\rvert\,d\mu(w)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,2)}\log\Bigl\lvert\dfrac{z-w}{w}\Bigr\rvert\,d\mu(w)\\
=G_{1}(z)+G_{2}(z).

Here and later on,
D(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w-z|<r\}
.
Since
\Delta G=\mu=\Delta\psi
, by Lemma
2.1
we have
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{G}
.

Next,

(2.1)

\Bigl|G_{1}(z)-\frac{\mu(D(0,2))}{2\pi}\log|z|\Bigr|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D(0,2)}\log\Bigl|1-\frac{w}{z}\Bigr|\,d\mu(w)\\
\leq\frac{C}{|z|},\qquad|z|\geq 4,

and

G_{2}(z)-\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,2))}{2\pi}\log|z|\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,2)}\log\Bigl|\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{w}\Bigr|\,d\mu(w)\leq 0,\qquad|z|\geq 4.

Thus,

G(z)\leq\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C})}{2\pi}\log(1+|z|)+\frac{C}{1+|z|},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Now, given an entire function
f
, we have

f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\implies\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)|^{2}(1+|z|)^{-\mu(\mathbb{C})/(2\pi)}\,dv(z)<\infty.

By a Liouville type theorem,
f
is a polynomial of degree
N
such that

\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2N}r^{-\mu(\mathbb{C})/(2\pi)}\,rdr<\infty.

Therefore,
N<-1+\mu(\mathbb{C})/(4\pi)
. Thus,
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\leq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\psi
be a subharmonic function and suppose that
\mu_{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}^{c}
.
Then

\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\geq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu_{\psi}(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil.

Proof.

Set
\mu=\mu_{\psi}
and choose
\varepsilon>0
,
R>1
such that

\frac{\mu(D(0,R))}{4\pi}>\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Next, increasing
R
, we can guarantee that

\mu(D(0,R))>\mu(\mathbb{C})-\frac{1}{2}.

Consider
a modified logarithmic potential
U
of measure
\mu
:

U(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D(0,R)}\log\lvert z-w\rvert\,d\mu(w)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,R)}\log\Bigl\lvert\dfrac{z-w}{w}\Bigr\rvert\,d\mu(w)\\
=U_{1}(z)+U_{2}(z).

Since
\Delta U=\mu=\Delta\psi
, by Lemma
2.1
we have
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{U}
.
Arguing as in (
2.1
), we get

U_{1}(z)\geq\frac{\mu(D(0,R))}{2\pi}\log|z|-\frac{C}{|z|},\qquad|z|\geq 2R.

Next, let
|z|\geq 2R
. Then

U_{2}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus(D(0,R)\cup D(z,|z|/2))}\log\Bigl\lvert\dfrac{z-w}{w}\Bigr\rvert\,d\mu(w)\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D(z,|z|/2)}\log\Bigl\lvert\dfrac{z-w}{w}\Bigr\rvert\,d\mu(w)\\
\geq C-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D(z,|z|/2)}\log\Bigl\lvert\dfrac{z/2}{z-w}\Bigr\rvert\,d\mu(w)=C-U_{3}(z).

Now, we apply a result by Hayman
[
5
, Lemma 4]
. The following notation is used there.
Let
\nu
be a finite positive measure. Given
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
h>0
, set
n(z,h)=\nu(D(z,h))
,
N(z,h)=\int_{D(z,h)}\log\Bigl|\frac{h}{w-z}\Bigr|\,d\nu(w)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
0<d<h/2
. There exists a set
S
of area at most
\pi d^{2}
such that

N(z,h/2)\leq n(z_{0},h)\log\frac{16h}{d},\qquad z\in D(z_{0},h/2)\setminus S.

Given
m\geq 1
, denote
A_{m}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:2^{m}R\leq|z|<2^{m+1}R\}
.
Fix
m\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
and apply Lemma
2.5
with
\nu=\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,R)}\mu
,
2^{m}R\leq|z_{0}|<2^{m+1}R
,
h=2^{m-1}R
,

n(z_{0},h)\leq 1/2
, and
d=2^{m-k-1}R
to get for some
C,C_{1}>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
:

m_{2}\bigl\{z\in A_{m}:U_{3}(z)>C_{1}+\delta k\bigr\}\leq C\cdot 2^{2m}R^{2}2^{-2k},\quad k\geq 1.

Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{C}}(1+|z|)^{-2-\varepsilon}e^{U_{3}(z)}\,dv(z)\leq C+C\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-(2+\varepsilon)m}e^{\delta k}\\
\times m_{2}\bigl\{z\in A_{m}:C_{1}+\delta k\leq U_{3}(z)<C_{1}+\delta(k+1)\bigr\}\\
\leq C+C\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}2^{-(2+\varepsilon)m}e^{\delta k}2^{2m}R^{2}2^{-2k}<\infty.

Next, for every
0\leq N\leq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil-1
we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2N}e^{-U(z)}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2N}(1+|z|)^{-\mu(D(0,R))/(2\pi)}e^{U_{3}(z)}\,dv(z)\\
\leq C\int_{\mathbb{C}}(1+|z|)^{-2-\varepsilon}e^{U_{3}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty

Here we use that
\mu_{\psi}\in\mathcal{M}^{c}
and, hence,
e^{-U}
is locally integrable.

Finally, we have

\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}\geq\Bigl\lceil\frac{\mu(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil.

∎

Summing up Lemmata
2.1
,
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
, we obtain the following result, extending and slightly correcting
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\psi
be a subharmonic function on the complex plane.
Then the Fock space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
is finite-dimensional if and only if

(2.2)

(\mu_{\psi})^{c}(\mathbb{C})<\infty.

If
\psi
is finite on
\mathbb{C}
, then we can write condition (
2.2
) as

\mu_{\psi}(\mathbb{C})<\infty
.
Finally, if
(\mu_{\psi})^{c}(\mathbb{C})<\infty
, then

\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\Bigl\lceil\frac{(\mu_{\psi})^{c}(\mathbb{C})}{4\pi}\Bigr\rceil.

Remark 2.7
.

It is an interesting open question to characterize non subharmonic functions
\psi
such that the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
is of finite dimension.
For some results in this direction
and some physical interpretations see
[
9
]
.

3.
The case of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n>1

Let
\mathbb{C}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional complex Euclidean space. Given
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we set

\lvert z\rvert=\sqrt{\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2}+\cdots+\lvert z_{n}\rvert^{2}}.

Denote
\mathbb{B}_{n}(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|w-z|<r\}
. Then
\mathbb{B}_{n}=\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,1)
is the unit ball and
\mathbb{S}_{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
d\sigma
be the normalized surface measure on
\mathbb{S}_{n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
smooth function. Given
M>0
, consider
\psi_{M}(z)=M\log(\lvert z\rvert^{2})
.
Suppose that for every
M>0
, the function
\psi-\psi_{M}
is plurisubharmonic outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.

Proof.

We use the fundamental result of Bedford–Taylor
[
1
]
on the solutions of the Dirichlet problem for the complex Monge–Ampère equation. Given
M>0
, choose
r_{M}>1

such that
\psi-\psi_{M}
is plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}
. Solving the Dirichlet problem for the complex Monge–Ampère equation on
\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})
with
the boundary conditions
(\psi-\psi_{M})|_{\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}
, we obtain a function
u
. Set

\widetilde{\psi}_{M}(z)=\begin{cases}(\psi-\psi_{M})(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M}),\\
u(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M}).\end{cases}

Then
\widetilde{\psi}_{M}
is a continuous plurisubharmonic function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(see also
[
3
, Section 7]
).

Now, by the Hörmander theorem (
[
6
, Theorem 4.4.4]
, see also
[
2
, Section IV]
),
there exists an entire function
f\not\equiv 0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{-3n}e^{-\widetilde{\psi}_{M}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty.

Hence, for every
0\leq k\leq M-\frac{3}{2}n
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}|z|^{2k}e^{-\psi(z)}\,dv(z)\leq C+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}|z|^{2k}e^{-\psi(z)}\,dv(z)\\
=C+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}|z|^{2k}e^{-\psi_{M}(z)}e^{-(\psi(z)-\psi_{M}(z))}\,dv(z)\\
\leq C+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}\lvert f(z)\rvert^{2}|z|^{-3n}e^{-\widetilde{\psi}_{M}(z)}\,dv(z)<\infty.

Since
M
is arbitrary, we have
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Theorem A is an immediate corollary of Theorem
3.1
.

Indeed, an easy computation shows that if
\psi(z)=\varphi(\lvert z\rvert^{2})
,
\varphi\in C^{2}((0,+\infty))
, then

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z_{k}}}(z)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})\bar{z_{j}}z_{k}+\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})\delta_{jk},

where
\delta_{jk}
is the Kronecker delta symbol. This implies that

i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(z)=\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})I+\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})z^{*}z,

where
z^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{z}_{1}\\
\dots\\
\bar{z}_{n}\end{pmatrix}
,
z^{*}z=\bigl[\bar{z}_{j}z_{k}\bigr]_{j,k=1}^{n}
.
Note also that the spectrum of the matrix
i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(z)
is

(3.1)

\sigma(i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(z))=\left\{\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2}),\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})+\lvert z\rvert^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})\right\}.

The first eigenvalue has multiplicity
n-1
and the second one has multiplicity
1
.

Furthermore,

L_{\psi}(z)=i\partial\bar{\partial}\psi(z)=i\partial\bar{\partial}(\psi-\psi_{M})(z)+\frac{M}{|z|^{2}}I-\frac{M}{|z|^{4}}z^{*}z\\
=L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)+\frac{M}{|z|^{2}}I-\frac{M}{|z|^{4}}z^{*}z.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let

V=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}\\
\dots\\
V_{n}\end{pmatrix}

be a normalized eigenvector corresponding to an eigenvalue
\nu
of
L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)
. By the hypothesis of Theorem A, for
|z|>r_{M}
we have
\lambda_{0}(z)|z|^{2}\geq M
, where

\lambda_{0}(z)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
L_{\psi}(z)
. Thus,

\nu=\langle L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)V,V\rangle=\langle L_{\psi}(z)V,V\rangle-\frac{M}{|z|^{2}}+\frac{M}{|z|^{4}}\langle z^{*}zV,V\rangle\\
\geq\lambda_{0}(z)-\frac{M}{|z|^{2}}+\frac{M}{|z|^{4}}|zV|^{2}\geq 0.

Therefore,
\psi-\psi_{M}
is plurisubharmonic on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}(0,r_{M})}
, and we are in the conditions of Theorem
3.1
. ∎

Now we give an easy example when Theorem
3.1
applies while Theorem A does not work.

Example 3.3
.

Set

\psi(z)=\varphi(\lvert z\rvert^{2})=\bigl(\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})\bigr)^{3/2},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Then
\varphi(t)=\bigl(\log(1+t)\bigr)^{3/2}
,
t>0
.

Evidently,
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
. We will show that condition (
1.2
) fails for
\psi
while the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied.

We have

\varphi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{1+t}\bigl(\log(1+t)\bigr)^{1/2},

and

\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\bigl(\log(1+t)\bigr)^{1/2}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{3}{4(1+t)^{2}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{1/2}}.

By (
3.1
), the eigenvalues of the matrix
L_{\psi}(z)
are

\lambda_{1}(z)=\dfrac{3\big(\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})\big)^{1/2}}{2(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})},

and

\lambda_{2}(z)=\dfrac{3\big(\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})\big)^{1/2}}{2(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}}+\dfrac{3\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{4(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\big(\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})\big)^{1/2}}\\
=\dfrac{3}{4}\dfrac{2\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})+\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\big(\log(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})\big)^{1/2}}.

For
|z|\geq 2
, the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix
L_{\psi}(z)
is
\lambda_{2}(z)
and

\lim_{\lvert z\rvert\to\infty}\lvert z\rvert^{2}\lambda_{2}(z)=0.

Thus, condition (
1.2
) does not hold.

On the other hand, for
M>0
, the eigenvalues of matrix
L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)
are

\alpha_{1}(z)=\lambda_{1}(z)-\dfrac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}},

and

\alpha_{2}(z)=\lambda_{2}(z).

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{\lvert z\rvert\to\infty}\lvert z\rvert^{2}\lambda_{1}(z)=\infty
and
\alpha_{2}(z)>0
,
z\not=0
, the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied. ∎

In the rest of the paper we
show that in different situations the sufficient condition of Theorem
3.1
is not necessary for
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.

Example 3.4
.

Set

\psi(z,w)=\lvert z\rvert^{2}+2\log(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2}),\qquad w,z\in\mathbb{C}.

It is clear that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
. Let us verify that for
M>2
the function
\psi-\psi_{M}
is not plurisubharmonic
at the points
(1,w)
,
w\in\mathbb{C}
.

We start with some easy computations:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}=\overline{z},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}=1,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z\partial\overline{w}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial w}=\frac{2\overline{w}}{1+\lvert w\rvert^{2}},\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial w\partial\overline{z}}=0,\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial w\partial\overline{w}}=\frac{2}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}.

Now, given
M>0
, we have

L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z,w)\\
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\frac{2}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix}+\frac{M}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\lvert z\rvert^{2}&\overline{z}w\\
z\overline{w}&\lvert w\rvert^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2}}I\\
=\begin{pmatrix}1-\frac{M\lvert w\rvert^{2}}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}&\frac{M\overline{z}w}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}\\
\frac{Mz\overline{w}}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}&\frac{2}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}-\frac{M\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

and, hence,

\det(L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z,w))\\
=\frac{2}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}-\frac{M\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}-\frac{2M\lvert w\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}\\
=\frac{2(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}-M(2\lvert w\rvert^{2}+\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2})}{(1+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}(\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\lvert w\rvert^{2})^{2}}<0

for
M>2
,
z=1
and arbitrary
w
. Therefore, the conditions of Theorem
3.1
do not hold. ∎

3.1.
Weight functions
\psi
of special form

In this subsection we evaluate the dimension of
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
and the applicability of our criterion in Theorem
3.1
, for some concrete weight functions
\psi

and for
\psi
in some special classes.

Example 3.5
.

Let
k\geq 3
. Set
\psi(z)=\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}
,
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Given
M>0
, we have

L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}k^{2}\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2(k-1)}-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2}&k^{2}(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})^{k-1}+\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\overline{z_{1}}z_{2}\\
k^{2}(\overline{z_{1}}{z_{2}})^{k-1}+\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}&k^{2}\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2(k-1)}-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2}\end{pmatrix},

and, hence,

\det(L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z))\\
=\left(k^{2}\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2(k-1)}-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2}\right)\left(k^{2}\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2(k-1)}-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2}\right)\\
\qquad-\left(k^{2}(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})^{k-1}+\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\overline{z_{1}}z_{2}\right)\left(k^{2}(\overline{z_{1}}{z_{2}})^{k-1}+\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}\right)\\
=-\frac{k^{2}M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\left(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2k}+\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2k}+(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})^{k}+(\overline{z_{1}}z_{2})^{k}\right)\\
=-\frac{k^{2}M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\;\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}<0

when
z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\not=0
. Thus, for
M>0
, the function
\psi-\psi_{M}
is not plurisubharmonic outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Next we are going to verify that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.

We have

X:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}e^{-\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)\asymp\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{2}}r^{3}e^{-r^{2k}\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,d\sigma(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\,dr\\
\asymp\int_{\mathbb{S}_{2}}\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert^{-4/k}\,d\sigma(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}).

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the set

T_{\varepsilon}=\left\{(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}_{2}:\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert<\varepsilon\right\}.

Given
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}_{2}
such that
|\zeta_{1}|\geq|\zeta_{2}|
, set
\zeta_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}+r}\cdot e^{i\theta}
and
\zeta_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}-r}\cdot e^{i\varphi}
,
r\geq 0
.
If
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in T_{\varepsilon}
, then
\lvert\zeta_{1}\rvert^{2}-\lvert\zeta_{2}\rvert^{2}<C\varepsilon
for some constant
C=C(k)>0
. Hence,
r\lesssim\varepsilon
.
Next, since
\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert<\varepsilon
, we obtain that
|e^{ik\theta}-e^{ik\varphi}|\lesssim\varepsilon
. As a result, we obtain that

\sigma(T_{\varepsilon})\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}.

Set

U_{s}=\left\{(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}_{2}:2^{-s}<\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert\leq 2^{-s+1}\right\}.

Then

X\asymp\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\int_{U_{s}}\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\zeta_{2}^{k}\rvert^{-4/k}\,d\sigma(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\\
\lesssim\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{-2s}\,2^{4s/k}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}2^{-2s(1-(2/k))}<\infty,

since
k\geq 3
. Thus,
1\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
.

In the same way, for every
\alpha>0
we get

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}e^{-\alpha\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)<\infty.

Consider the entire functions
f(z)=e^{\beta(z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k})^{2}}
,
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}
. Since

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\bigl|e^{\beta(z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k})^{2}}\bigr|^{2}e^{-\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}e^{2\beta\Real((z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k})^{2})-\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}e^{-(1-2\beta)\lvert z_{1}^{k}+z_{2}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)<\infty,

we conclude that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.
∎

Interestingly,
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=0
if
k=2
. Indeed, let
\psi((z_{1},z_{2}))=|z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}|^{2}
,
f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
,
f(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})^{s}g(z_{1},z_{2})
for some

s\geq 0
, where
g(z_{1},z_{2})
is not a multiple of
z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}
. By the mean value property, for every
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus D(0,10)

we have

|g(z_{1},iz_{1})|^{2}\\
\lesssim(1+|z_{1}|)^{2}\int_{D(iz_{1},2/(1+|z_{1}|)\setminus D(iz_{1},1/(1+|z_{1}|))}|g(z_{1},z_{2})|^{2}e^{-|z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}|^{2}}\,dv(z_{2})\\
\lesssim(1+|z_{1}|)^{2}\int_{D(iz_{1},2/(1+|z_{1}|)\setminus D(iz_{1},1/(1+|z_{1}|))}|f(z_{1},z_{2})|^{2}e^{-|z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}|^{2}}\,dv(z_{2}).

Hence,

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|g(z_{1},iz_{1})|^{2}(1+|z_{1}|)^{-2}\,dv(z_{1})\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{\psi},

and by a Liouville type theorem,

g(z,iz)\equiv 0
. Analogously,
g(z,-iz)\equiv 0
. Set
h(z,w)=g(z-iw,z+iw)
. Then
h
is an entire function and
h(0,w)=h(w,0)\equiv 0
.
Hence,
h(z,w)=zwh_{1}(z,w)
for another entire function
h_{1}
and
g(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})g_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})
for some entire function
g_{1}
. This contradiction shows that
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=0
.

Extending the previous example to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
requires a bit more work.

Example 3.6
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
k\geq n+1
.
Set

\psi(z)=\lvert z_{1}^{k}+\cdots+z_{n}^{k}\rvert^{2},\qquad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Let us verify that for
M>0
, the function
\psi-\psi_{M}
is not plurisubharmonic outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

We have

L_{\psi}(z)=k^{2}\begin{pmatrix}\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2(k-1)}&(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})^{k-1}&\ldots&(z_{1}\overline{z_{n}})^{k-1}\\
(\overline{z_{1}}z_{2})^{k-1}&\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2(k-1)}&\ldots&(z_{2}\overline{z_{n}})^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots&\vdots\\
(\overline{z_{1}}z_{n})^{k-1}&(\overline{z_{2}}z_{n})^{k-1}&\ldots&\lvert z_{n}\rvert^{2(k-1)}\end{pmatrix}\\
=k^{2}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{k-1}\\
z_{2}^{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
z_{n}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{z_{1}}^{k-1}&\overline{z_{2}}^{k-1}&\ldots&\overline{z_{n}}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Set

A(z)=\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{z_{1}}\\
\overline{z_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\overline{z_{n}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}&z_{2}&\ldots&z_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)=L_{\psi}(z)+A(z)-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}I.

The spectra of the matrices
L_{\psi}(z)
and
A(z)
are

\displaystyle\sigma_{L_{\psi}(z)}=\left\{k^{2}\bigl(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2(k-1)}+\lvert z_{2}\rvert^{2(k-1)}+\cdots+\lvert z_{n}\rvert^{2(k-1)}\bigr),0\right\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{A(z)}=\left\{\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}},0\right\}.

Let
V
be the a unit vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
orthogonal to

\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}^{k-1}\\
z_{2}^{k-1}\\
\vdots\\
z_{n}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}
and to
\begin{pmatrix}\overline{z_{1}}\\
\overline{z_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\overline{z_{n}}\end{pmatrix}
.
Then

\langle L_{\psi-\psi_{M}}(z)V,V\rangle=\langle L_{\psi}(z)V+A(z)V-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}V,V\rangle=-\frac{M}{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}<0.

Thus, for
M>0
, the function
\psi-\psi_{M}
is plurisubharmonic at no points of
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Finally, let us verify that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
. Set

X:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\lvert z_{1}^{k}+\ldots+z_{n}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,dv(z)\\
\asymp\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}r^{2n-1}e^{-r^{2k}\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\cdots+\zeta_{n}^{k}\rvert^{2}}\,d\sigma(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\,dr\\
\asymp\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\ldots+\zeta_{n}^{k}\rvert^{-2n/k}\,d\sigma(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}).

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the set

T_{\varepsilon}=\left\{(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{S}_{n}:\lvert\zeta_{1}^{k}+\ldots+\zeta_{n}^{k}\rvert<\varepsilon\right\}.

Set

P(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{k},\qquad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Then the function
f=\log|P|
is plurisubharmonic. Following
[
7
]
, we consider the Lelong number of
f
at
a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\nu_{f}(a)=\lim_{r\to 0}\dfrac{\sup_{\lvert z\rvert\leq r}f(a+z)}{\log r}\in[0,\infty].

If
f(a)\neq 0
, then
\nu_{f}(a)=0
. Otherwise, let
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\neq 0
and
f(a)=0
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a_{1}\neq 0
. If
0<r<\frac{\lvert a_{1}\rvert}{2}
, then

f\bigl(a+(r,0,\ldots,0)\bigr)=\log\lvert(a_{1}+r)^{k}-a_{1}^{k}\rvert=\log\lvert ka_{1}^{k-1}r+O(r^{2})\rvert,\quad r\to 0,

and hence,
\nu_{f}(a)=1
. By Theorem 3.1 in
[
7
]
, applied to
\Omega=2\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
K=\overline{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\setminus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
1<\alpha<2
, we obtain

v\bigl(\{z\in K:\lvert P(z)\rvert\leq e^{-u}\}\bigr)=v\bigl(\{z\in K:f(z)\leq{-u}\}\bigr)\\
\leq C_{\alpha}e^{-\alpha u},\qquad u\geq 0.

By homogeneity of
P
,

\sigma(T_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha},\qquad\varepsilon>0,

for some constant
C>0
.

Arguing as in Example
3.5
, we obtain first that
1\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
and then that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
for
k\geq n+1
. ∎

At the end of the paper, we consider two special classes of weight functions
\psi
: radial weight functions and the functions of the form
\psi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}(z_{j})
.

Suppose that
\psi(z)=\varphi(|z|^{2})
is a radial plurisubharmonic function of class
C^{2}
. By the computations in Remark
3.2
,

(3.2)

\dfrac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z_{k}}}(z)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})\bar{z_{j}}z_{k}+\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})\delta_{jk}.

The action of the Monge–Ampère operator on
\psi
is

(dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=4n!\det\Bigl(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z_{j}\partial\bar{z_{k}}}\Bigr)\,dv\\
=4n!(\varphi^{\prime}(|z|^{2}))^{n-1}(\varphi^{\prime}(|z|^{2})+|z|^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|z|^{2}))\,dv.

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that
\psi(z)=\varphi(|z|^{2})
is a radial plurisubharmonic function of class
C^{2}
. Then
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
if and only if

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=\infty.

Proof.

Since the spectrum of the matrix (
3.2
) consists of the eigenvalues

\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})
and
\varphi^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})+\lvert z\rvert^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\lvert z\rvert^{2})
,
the first eigenvalue has multiplicity
n-1
and the second one has multiplicity
1
, we have
\varphi^{\prime}\geq 0
,

(r\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Furthermore, we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=C\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi^{\prime}(r^{2}))^{n-1}(\varphi^{\prime}(r^{2})+r^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r^{2}))\,dr^{2n}\\
=C\int_{0}^{\infty}d\bigl((r\varphi^{\prime}(r))^{n}\bigr).

Thus, (
3.3
) is equivalent to the relation
\lim_{r\to\infty}r\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\infty
. Now, if
r\varphi^{\prime}(r)
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then

\psi(z)=O(\log|z|)
,
|z|\to\infty
, and a version of the Liouville theorem shows that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}<\infty
. On the other hand, if

\lim_{r\to\infty}r\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\infty
, then
\log|z|=o(\psi(z))
,
|z|\to\infty
, and the polynomials belong to
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
. Hence,
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
.
∎

For general
C^{2}
plurisubharmonic functions, the radial case suggests the following question.
Is it true that
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=\infty
if and only if (
3.3
) holds? Our last example gives a negative answer to this question.

Example 3.8
.

Given subharmonic functions
\psi_{j}
on the complex plane,
1\leq j\leq n
, set

(3.4)

\psi(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}(z_{j}).

Claim:
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}<\infty
if and only if either
\max_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}<\infty
or
\min_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}=0
.

In one direction, by the Fubini theorem, if
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}<\infty
, then
\max_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}<\infty
or
\min_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}=0
.
In the opposite direction, it is clear that if
\min_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}=0
, then
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=0
. It remains to verify that
if
\max_{j}\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{j}}<\infty
, then
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}<\infty
.

First, suppose that
n=2
,
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}<\infty
,
N=\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{2}}<\infty
. Fix
a basis
(g_{k})
,
1\leq k\leq N
, in the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{2}}
and choose a family of points
(w_{m})
,
1\leq m\leq N
, such that
\det Q\not=0
, where

Q=\bigl(g_{k}(w_{m})\bigr)_{k,m=1}^{N}
.

Next, choose
f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
. By the mean value property,

|f(z,w)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{D(z,1)}|f(\zeta,w|^{2}\,dv(\zeta),\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{C}.

Therefore, for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the function
f(z,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{2}}
, and, hence, we have

f(z,\cdot)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}(z)g_{k}.

In the same way, the functions
f(\cdot,w_{j})
,
1\leq j\leq N
, belong to
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}
.

Next,

Q^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}f(z,w_{1})\\
\vdots\\
f(z,w_{N})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}(z)\\
\vdots\\
a_{N}(z)\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, every
a_{j}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}
. Since
\dim\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi_{1}}<\infty
,
we conclude that the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}
has finite dimension.
For
n\geq 2
we can just use an inductive argument. This completes the proof of Claim.

Let us return to general
\psi
satisfying (
3.4
). We have

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Delta\psi_{j}(z_{j})\,dv(z)=C\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Delta\psi_{j}(z_{j})\,dv(z_{j}).

Now, if
n=2
,
\psi_{1}(z)=|z|^{2}
,
\Delta\psi_{2}(z)=\max(1-|z|,0)
, then

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}(dd^{c}\psi)^{n}=\infty,

but
\mathcal{F}^{2}_{\psi}=0
. Thus, Proposition
3.7
does not extend to general
C^{2}
-smooth plurisubharmonic functions.

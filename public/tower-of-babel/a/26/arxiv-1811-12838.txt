Phenomenological consequences of Higgs inflation in the NMSSM at the electroweak scale
Wolfgang Gregor Hollik, Stefan Liebler, Gudrid Moortgat-Pick, Georg Weiglein

Abstract
The Next-to-Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model (NMSSM) can incorporate inflation, where a combination of the Higgs-doublet fields plays the role of the inflaton. At the high scale, the Higgs doublets are non-minimally coupled to supergravity; this coupling appears as an additional contribution to the μ \mu term in the low-energy effective superpotential and potentially changes physics at the electroweak scale. In a recent publication, we investigate the extended parameter space of this model with respect to collider phenomenology at the electroweak scale, and discuss scenarios which are potentially different from the pure NMSSM. We analyse the stability of the electroweak vacuum, the masses of neutralinos/charginos and Higgs bosons as well as the mixing and decays of Higgs bosons. Some important aspects of this study are described in the following.

Analysis

The superpotential of the
\mu
NMSSM
with the non-minimal coupling to
supergravity reads
\mathcal{W}=\lambda\,\hat{S}\,\hat{H}_{u}\cdot\hat{H}_{d}+\frac{1}{3}\,\kappa\,\hat{S}^{3}+\frac{3}{2}\,m_{3/2}\,\chi\,\hat{H}_{u}\cdot\hat{H}_{d}

gravitino mass
m_{3/2}

expectation value (vev)

\mu_{\text{eff}}=\lambda\,v_{s}

\chi

10^{5}\,\lambda

[
9

6

thus that the value of
\mu=\frac{3}{2}\,m_{3/2}\,\chi

mainly steered by the gravitino mass: values of
\mu\gtrsim 1
TeV

\lambda\gtrsim 0.1
require light gravitinos

m_{3/2}\sim 100
MeV
(typically LSP), while
for
\lambda\lesssim 10^{-5}
the gravitino mass is similar
to
\mu

problem
[
38

overcloses the universe
[
39
,
40

[
41

42

-\mathcal{L}_{\text{s}}=\left[A_{\lambda}\,\lambda\,S\,H_{u}\cdot H_{d}+\frac{1}{3}\,A_{\kappa}\,\kappa\,S^{3}+B_{\mu}\,\mu\,H_{u}\cdot H_{d}+\text{h.\,c.}\right]+m_{H_{d}}^{2}\,\lvert H_{d}\rvert^{2}+m_{H_{u}}^{2}\,\lvert H_{u}\rvert^{2}+m_{s}^{2}\,\lvert S\rvert^{2}

softly breaks SUSY and the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-symmetry, is used. Further

\mathbb{Z}_{3}

radiatively and renormalised in the
\overline{\text{DR}}
scheme at the scale of
the top-mass

8

Conclusions

The extended parameter space of the
\mu
NMSSM
compared to
the
NMSSM
is investigated with respect to vacuum stability, Higgs
masses and decays, and neutralino phenomenology. Scenarios with the
potential to distinguish both models are proposed; differences
typically occur in the singlet sector. Some excluded scenarios in
the
NMSSM
may still be viable in the
\mu
NMSSM
.

High-dimensional Berry–Esseen bound for m m -dependent random samples
Heejong Bong, Arun Kumar Kuchibhotla, Alessandro Rinaldo abel=e3, mark]{hbong, arunku, arinaldo}@cmu.edu

Abstract
In this work, we provide a ( n / m ) − 1 / 2 (n/m)^{-1/2} -rate finite sample Berry–Esseen bound for m m -dependent high-dimensional random vectors over the class of hyper-rectangles. This bound imposes minimal assumptions on the random vectors such as nondegenerate covariances and finite third moments. The proof uses inductive relationships between anti-concentration inequalities and Berry–Esseen bounds, which are inspired by the telescoping method of Chen and Shao 2004 and the recursion method of Kuchibhotla and Rinaldo 2020 . Performing a dual induction based on the relationships, we obtain tight Berry-Esseen bounds for dependent samples.

1
Introduction

Recent advances in technology have led to the unprecedented availability of large-scale spatiotemporal data. An important challenge in the analyses of such data is to provide a theoretical guarantee of statistical inferences under temporal dependence. Many existing theoretical studies, such as
Liu 2020
, relied on parametric or distributional assumptions to give a valid confidence interval, but the validity of the assumptions remains questionable in real-world applications.

In this paper, we focus on high-dimensional Central Limit Theorems (CLTs) over the class of hyper-rectangles. That is, our objective is to bound the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance between a summation of samples
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and its Gaussian approximation
Y
, denoted by

\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},Y\right):=\sup_{r\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\lvert\mathbb{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\preceq r]-\mathbb{P}[Y\preceq r]\rvert,

where for two vectors
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
a\preceq b
means that
a_{k}\leq b_{k}
for every
k\in[p]
. For independent samples, there has been a flurry of novel results since the seminal work of
Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and
Kato 2013
. A popular approach has been the Lindeberg interpolation, leading to an
n^{-1/6}
rate
(
Bentkus et al. 2000
;
Lopes 2022
)
. Recently,
Kuchibhotla and
Rinaldo 2020
used a recursion method over the Lindeberg interpolation to establish a high-dimensional Berry–Esseen bound with the rate at most
(\log n)^{3/2}/\sqrt{n}
under minimal assumptions: nondegenerate covariances and finite third moments of the samples. Here in this discussion, we are focusing on the dependence of the rate on the number
n
of observations.
Our work extends their arguments to
m
-dependent cases in which
X_{i}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp X_{j}
if
\lvert i-j\rvert>m
.

For
m
-dependent random samples, the optimal
(n/m)^{-1/2}
-rate has been only obtained in cases for
p=1
by
Shergin 1980
. Later, the result was generalized to broad dependence structures by
Chen and Shao 2004
. For high-dimensional cases,
Zhang and Wu 2017
studied the Gaussian approximation for
m
-dependent sequences (see Appendix A.2 therein), but the rate of their Berry–Esseen bound and its dependency to sample assumptions were presented implicitly. More recently,
Chang, Chen and Wu 2021
provided a bound with the rate
n^{-1/6}
for sub-exponential random vectors, using a decomposition of samples into “large” blocks and “small” blocks as done by
Romano and Wolf 2000
for
1
-dimensional cases.

We obtain a
(n/m)^{-1/2}
-rate bound under minimal assumptions that the samples have nondegenerate covariances and finite third moments. The proof uses inductive relationships between anti-concentration inequalities and Berry–Esseen bounds, which are results of the telescoping method by
Chen and Shao 2004
and the recursion method by
Kuchibhotla and
Rinaldo 2020
. Performing a dual induction based on the relationships, we obtain tight Berry-Esseen bounds for dependent samples.

2
Summation of
m
-dependent random vectors

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
be
m
-dependent centered random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. That is,
X_{i}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp X_{j}
if
\lvert i-j\rvert>m
, and
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=0
for
i\in[n]
. We use the superscript
X_{i}^{(k)}
to notate each
k
-th element of
p
dimensional random vector
X_{i}
.
Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{n}
be Gaussian random vectors with mean zero and the same second moment as
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. i.e.,

\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}Y_{j}^{\top}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}^{\top}],\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i,j\in[n].

For the brevity of the following arguments, let

X_{[i,j)}:=\{X_{k}:i\leq k<j\}\enskip\text{and}\enskip Y_{(i,j]}:=\{Y_{k}:i<k\leq j\}

and
S
be the summation operator such that

SX_{[i,j)}:=\sum_{k:i\leq k<j}X_{k}\enskip\text{and}\enskip SY_{(i,j]}:=\sum_{k:i<k\leq j}Y_{k}.

For
i,j:1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, let
{\sigma}_{\min,[i,j]}
,
\underline{\sigma}_{[i,j]}
and
\overline{\sigma}_{[i,j]}
be

\displaystyle{\sigma}^{2}_{\min,[i,j]}:=

\displaystyle\min_{k\in[p]}\operatorname{Var}[SX^{(k)}_{[i,j]}],

\displaystyle\underline{\sigma}^{2}_{[i,j]}:=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\operatorname{Var}[SX_{[i,j]}]),

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}^{2}_{[i,j]}:=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}(\operatorname{Var}[SX_{[i,j]}]).

Also, let
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{3}
be

\nu_{1}:=\mathrm{max}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}[\lVert X_{i}\rVert_{\infty}],\mathbb{E}[\lVert Y_{i}\rVert_{\infty}]\end{aligned}:i\in[n]\right\},

\nu_{3}:=\mathrm{max}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}[\lVert X_{i}\rVert_{\infty}^{3}],\mathbb{E}[\lVert Y_{i}\rVert_{\infty}^{3}]\end{aligned}:i\in[n]\right\}.

Notation.

In the following argument,
C(\dots)
is a constant with implicit dependency on the parameters in the parentheses, whose value is changing across lines. For absolute constants with no dependency, we omit the parentheses and denote them by
C
.
\mathbf{1}
stands for the vector with elements
1
in the appropriate dimension at each line.
\mathbb{I}\{\cdot\}
denotes the indicator function.

3
High-dimensional Berry–Esseen bound for
m
-dependent random samples with nondegenerate covariance matrices

In this section, we state a high-dimensional Berry–Esseen bound for
m
-dependent random samples with nondegenerate covariance matrices. That is, the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of sums of consecutive samples are bounded away from
0
and
\infty
, respectively. We assume

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{\min,[i,j]}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma^{2}_{\min}\cdot(i-j+1)\cdot\min\{m,i-j+1\},

(MIN-VAR)

\displaystyle\underline{\sigma}^{2}_{[i,j]}

\displaystyle\geq\underline{\sigma}^{2}\cdot(i-j+1)\cdot\min\{m,i-j+1\},

(MIN-EV)

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}^{2}_{[i,j]}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\sigma}^{2}\cdot(i-j+1)\cdot\min\{m,i-j+1\},

(MAX-EV)

for arbitrary constants
\sigma_{\min},\underline{\sigma},\overline{\sigma}>0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
m=1
, suppose that Assumptions (
MIN-VAR
), (
MIN-EV
) and (
MAX-EV
) hold. Then,

\displaystyle\mu\left(SX_{[1,n]},SY_{[1,n]}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\frac{\nu_{3}}{\underline{\sigma}^{3}}\log(en)(\log(ep))^{2}\sqrt{\log(epn)}+\frac{\nu_{3}^{1/3}\overline{\sigma}^{1/3}}{\sigma_{\min}\underline{\sigma}^{1/3}}\log(ep)\sqrt{\log(epn)}\right),

for some constant
C>0
.

The Berry-Esseen bounds for
m>1
come as corollaries of
Theorem

3.1
, using a similar argument with Theorem 2 in
Shergin 1980
and Theorem 2.6 in
Chen and Shao 2004
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the same assumptions as
Theorem

3.1
except that
m=1
is replaced with
m\geq 1
, with
n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n/m
, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu\left(SX_{[1,n]},SY_{[1,n]}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}}\left(\frac{\nu_{3}}{\underline{\sigma}^{3}}\log(en_{\mathrm{eff}})(\log(ep))^{2}\sqrt{\log(epn_{\mathrm{eff}})}+\frac{\nu_{3}^{1/3}\overline{\sigma}^{1/3}}{\sigma_{\min}\underline{\sigma}^{1/3}}\log(ep)\sqrt{\log(epn_{\mathrm{eff}})}\right).

4
Sketch of Proof

Here we provide a sketch of proof for the cases of
m=1
. We use inductive relationships between anti-concentration inequalities and Berry-Esseen bounds to prove
Theorem

3.1
. For a random variable
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, anti-concentration inequalities give upperbounds for the probability of
X
to be contained in a specific type of subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
For example,
Nazarov 2003
and
Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and
Kato 2017
provided an anti-concentration inequality for Gaussian random variables to be contained in
A_{r,\delta}
, where for
r\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\delta\in[0,\infty)
,

A_{r,\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:x\preceq r+\delta\mathbf{1}\}\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:x\preceq r-\delta\mathbf{1}\}.

(1)

Lemma 4.1
(Gaussian anti-concentration inequality;
Nazarov 2003
;
Chernozhukov, Chetverikov and
Kato 2017
).

For a random vector
Y\sim N(0,\Sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and
\delta\in[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}[Y\in A_{r,\delta}]\leq C\delta\sqrt{\frac{\log(ep)}{\min_{i=1,\dots,p}\Sigma_{ii}}}

(2)

for an absolute constant
C>0
.

We note that the anti-concentration inequality for
SX_{[1,n]}
to be contained in
A_{r,\delta}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}[SX_{[1,n]}\in A_{r,\delta}]\leq f(n,\delta),

(3)

is a corollary of
Lemma

4.1
and
Theorem

3.1
. In cases of independent samples,
Kuchibhotla and
Rinaldo 2020
used the anti-concentration inequalities for
SX_{[1,i]}
at
i<n
in the Lindeberg swapping to prove the Berry-Esseen bounds for
SX_{[1,n]}
. In their proof, the dual induction between anti-concentration inequalities and Berry-Esseen bounds implied the desired result. However, the anti-concentration inequality for the marginal probability in
Eq.

3
is not sufficient to prove the Berry–Esseen bound for dependent random vectors. Rather, we need a generalized anti-concentration inequality spanning the conditional probability,

\mathbb{P}[SX_{[1,i]}\in A_{r,\delta}|X_{(i,n]}],~~\forall i\in[1,n],

(4)

where
r,\delta
are Borel-measurable functions with respect to
X_{(i,n]}
. Let

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(\delta):=\sup_{r\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\mathbb{P}[SX_{[1,i]}\in A_{r,\delta}|X_{(i,n]}]\enskip\text{and}\enskip\mu_{i}:=\mu(SX_{[1,i]},SY_{[1,i]}).

(5)

The Lindeberg swapping method as in
Kuchibhotla and
Rinaldo 2020
derives the inductive relationship from
\kappa_{i}(\delta)
to
\mu_{i}
: for
i\in[n]
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\sqrt{i}\mu_{i}\leq C(\nu_{3},\sigma_{\min},\underline{\sigma})\varepsilon\log(ep)+C(\nu_{3},\overline{\sigma},\underline{\sigma})\frac{\log(en)(\log(ep))^{3/2}}{\varepsilon}\sup_{j:\frac{i}{2}<j<i}\sqrt{j}\kappa_{j}\left(\varepsilon\right).

On the other hand, the telescoping scheme in
Chen and Shao 2004
derives the inductive relationship from
\mu_{i}
to
\kappa_{i}(\delta)
: for
i\in[n]
,

\sqrt{i}\kappa_{i}(\delta)\leq C\left(\frac{\delta}{\sigma_{\min}}\sqrt{\log(ep)}+\max_{j:j\leq i-2}\sqrt{j}\mu_{j}\right).

The full statements of the inductive relationships are in
Lemmas

A.1
and
A.2
of
Section

A.1
. Summing the two equations and taking an appropriate
\varepsilon
, we obtain the following inductive inequality among
\mu_{i}
’s: for
i\geq 4
,

\sqrt{i}\mu_{i}\leq C(\nu_{3},\sigma_{\min},\overline{\sigma},\underline{\sigma})\log(en)(\log(ep))^{2}\sqrt{\log(epn)},+\frac{1}{2}\max_{j:j\leq i-3}\sqrt{j}\mu_{j}.

Based on mathematical induction, we obtain for all
i\in[n]
,

\sqrt{i}\mu_{i}\leq 2C(\nu_{3},\sigma_{\min},\overline{\sigma},\underline{\sigma})\log(en)(\log(ep))^{2}\sqrt{\log(epn)},

which proves the desired theorem.

5
Discussion

We derived a
n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}
scaling of Berry–Esseen bound for high-dimensional
m
-dependent random vectors over hyper-rectangles, where
n_{\mathrm{eff}}=n/m
indicated the effective sample size. This result only required nondegenerate covariances and finite third moments of the random vectors. The exact rate with respect to
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
was
(\log n_{\mathrm{eff}})^{-3/2}/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}
, where the dependency on the dimension
p
was logarithmic. The rate had the same scale up to logarithm as the result of
Shergin 1980
on
1
-dimensional
m
-dependent samples, which is also known to be unimprovable based on
Berk 1973
. Our result supports the high-dimensional CLT and use of bootstrap over hyper-rectangles under
m
-dependency between samples.

Our advancement in the Gaussian approximation rate of
m
-dependent samples could benefit the theoretical analyses under physical dependence frameworks.
Zhang and Cheng 2018
introduced the
m
-approximation technique to study the Gaussian approximation of weakly dependent time series under physical dependence. The technique extends the Berry–Esseen bounds for
m
-dependent samples to weaker temporal dependencies (see Theorem 2.1 and the end of Section 2.2 therein). Similarly,
Chang, Chen and Wu 2021
extended the
n^{-1/6}
rate under
m
-dependence to samples with physical dependence. The resulting rate in Theorem 3 was better than the best rate of
Zhang and Wu 2017
at that time.

Another important future direction is extending our technique to samples with generalized graph dependency. Random vectors
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are said to have dependency structure defined by graph
G=([n],E)
if
X_{i}\perp\!\!\!\!\perp X_{j}
if
(i,j)\in E
. Graph dependency generalizes
m
-dependence as a special case by taking
E=\{(i,j):\lvert i-j\rvert\leq m\}
. The only CLT result up to our best knowledge has been
Chen and Shao 2004
for
1
-dimensional samples with graph dependency. Extending their result to high-dimensional samples has a huge potential to advance statistical analyses on network data, which is another data type with increasing availability.

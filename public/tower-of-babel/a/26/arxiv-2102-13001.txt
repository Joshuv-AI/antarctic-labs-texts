Lorentzian distance functions in contact geometry
Jakob Hedicke

Abstract
. An important tool to analyse the causal structure of a Lorentzian manifold is given by the Lorentzian distance function. We define a class of Lorentzian distance functions on the group of contactomorphisms of a closed contact manifold depending on the choice of a contact form. These distance functions are continuous with respect to the Hofer norm for contactomorphisms defined by Shelukhin ( [ 21 ] ) and finite if and only if the group of contactomorphisms is orderable. To prove this we show that intervals defined by the positivity relation are open with respect to the topology induced by the Hofer norm. For orderable Legendrian isotopy classes we show that the Chekanov-type metric defined in [ 20 ] is non-degenerate. In this case similar results hold for a Lorentzian distance functions on Legendrian isotopy classes. This leads to a natural class of metrics associated to a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian manifold such that its Cauchy hypersurface has a unit co-tangent bundle with orderable isotopy class of the fibres.

1.
Introduction

Consider a closed co-oriented contact manifold
(M,\xi)
, i.e. an
(2n+1)
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
with a hyperplane distribution
\xi\subset TM
that is the kernel of a
1
-form
\alpha
such that
\alpha\wedge d\alpha^{n}
is nowhere vanishing.
Denote by
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
the identity component of the group of contactomorphisms, i.e. the group of diffeomorphisms preserving
\xi
that are isotopic to
id_{M}
through contactomorphisms.
Fore details see e.g.
[
15
]
.

In
[
13
]
Eliashberg and Polterovich introduced the concept of positivity on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
(see also
[
3
]
).
An isotopy
\varphi_{t}
of contactomorphisms is called
positive
if the contact vector field

X_{t}^{\varphi}\circ\varphi_{t}:=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=t}\varphi_{s}

satisfies
\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})>0
for all
t
.
Note that this definition only depends on the co-orientation defined by the contact form
\alpha
.
Similarly
\varphi_{t}
is called
non-negative
if
\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})\geq 0
.
This induces two relations on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
by

\varphi\llcurly\psi:\Leftrightarrow\text{ there exists a positive isotopy from $\varphi$ to $\psi$}

and

\varphi\preccurlyeq\psi:\Leftrightarrow\text{ there exists a non-negative isotopy from $\varphi$ to $\psi$}.

The relations
\llcurly
and
\preccurlyeq
turn
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
into a causal space (see
[
17
]
).

The properties of the relations on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
resemble properties of the chronological and causal relation in Lorentzian geometry.
Moreover as we will show, one can define an analogue of the Lorentzian distance function on the group
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
.

Let
(N,g)
be a smooth time-oriented Lorentzian manifold, that is a manifold
N
with a smooth pseudo-Riemannian metric of signature
(1,n)
and the choice of a vector field
X
such that
g(X,X)<0
.
A tangent vector
v\in TN
is called
future pointing timelike
if
g(v,v)<0
and
g(v,X)<0
and future pointing causal if
g(v,v)\leq 0
and
g(v,X)<0
.
A smooth curve
\gamma(t)
is called future pointing timelike (causal) if
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
is future pointing timelike (causal) for all
t
.
This induces two relations on
N
.
The chronological relation

p\ll q:\Leftrightarrow\text{ there exists a future pointing timelike curve from $p$ to $q$}

and the causal relation

p\leq q:\Leftrightarrow\text{ there exists a future pointing causal curve from $p$ to $q$}.

Most causal properties of
(N,g)
can be phrased in terms of the chronological (causal) future/past of the points in
N
:

\displaystyle I^{+}(p):=\{q\in N|p\ll q\}

\displaystyle,I^{-}(p):=\{q\in N|q\ll p\}

\displaystyle J^{+}(p):=\{q\in N|p\leq q\}

\displaystyle,J^{-}(p):=\{q\in N|q\leq p\}.

A natural topology on
N
related to
g
is the interval (Alexandrov) topology whose basis is given by the open ’intervals’
I^{+}(p)\cap I^{-}(q)
of the chronological relation.
The interval topology coincides with the manifold topology of
N
iff the Lorentzian manifold is
strongly causal
, i.e. iff for every open
U\subset N
there exists a causally convex open
V\subset U
(
[
19
]
).

Analogously to the length of a curve
\gamma
in a Riemannian manifold, one can define a
Lorentzian length (eigentime)
for causal curves by

L_{g}(\gamma):=\int\limits_{a}^{b}\sqrt{-g(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))}\,dt.

The
Lorentzian distance function
between two points is then defined by

\displaystyle\tau_{g}\colon N\times N\rightarrow[0,\infty]

\displaystyle(p,q)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sup\{L_{g}(\gamma)\},&\text{if }p\leq q\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Here the supremum is taken over all future pointing causal curves connecting
p
and
q
.

The Lorentzian distance function satisfies (see
[
2
]
)

(i)

\tau_{g}(p,q)>0
if and only if
p\ll q
.

(ii)

\tau_{g}
is lower semi-continuous.

(iii)

\tau_{g}(p,q)\geq\tau_{g}(p,r)+\tau_{g}(r,q)
for
p\leq r\leq q
.

If
(N,g)
is strongly causal the metric
g
can be recovered from
\tau_{g}
and the Lorentzian distance reflects many causal properties of
(N,g)
.

A strongly causal time oriented Lorentzian manifold
(N,g)
is called
globally hyperbolic
if
J^{+}(p)\cap J^{-}(q)
is compact for all
p,q\in N
.
In this case
N
contains a smooth Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
, i.e. a surface that is intersected in a unique point by every inextendible causal curve.
In particular
N
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
(
[
19
]
).

Theorem

(
[
2
, Corollary 4.7.]
)
.

If
(N,g)
is globally hyperbolic, then
\tau_{g}
is finite and continuous.

Theorem

(
[
2
, Theorem 4.30.]
)
.

A strongly causal manifold
(N,g)
is globally hyperbolic if and only if
\tau_{\tilde{g}}
is finite for all
\tilde{g}
in the conformal class of
g
, i.e. for all
\tilde{g}
with
\tilde{g}=e^{f}g
for some smooth function
f
.

As pointed out for instance in
[
17
]
Lorentzian distance functions can be used to explore the causal properties of more general causal spaces.
There the authors consider causal spaces and functions satisfying the properties (i)-(iii) (here lower semi-continuity is with respect to some background metric).

For closed
(M,\xi)
we will define a class of Lorentzian distance functions on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
for the relations
\llcurly
and
\preccurlyeq
inspired by Shelukhins definition of the Hofer norm (
[
21
]
).
The Lorentzian distance functions depend on the choice of a contact form.
They are not conjugation invariant but continuous with respect to the Hofer-Shelukhin norm and share some of its properties.
To prove this we show that the interval topology on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
introduced in
[
11
]
is coarser than the topology induced by the Hofer-Shelukhin norm.
The same method that proves continuity of the Lorentzian distance function can be used to answer
[
21
, Question 18]
if
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is orderable in the sense of
[
9
]
.

In the case of certain unit co-tangent bundles we use spectral invariants for Legendrians to give an estimate of the Lorentzian distance function in terms of the Hofer-Shelukhin norm.

Analogously to the relations on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
, positivity defines two relations on the Legendrian isotopy classes of
(M,\xi)
(see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
for details).
In Section 5 we will define Lorentzian distance functions for isotopy classes of closed Legendrians.
For orderable Legendrian isotopy classes we give a simple proof of
[
20
, Conjecture 1.10]
proving that the Chekanov-type metric defined by Rosen and Zhang is non-degenerate.
In this case the same results like for contactomorphisms hold, i.e. the Lorentzian distance functions are finite and continuous with respect to the Rosen-Zhang metric.

The results about the isotopy class of the fibre of unit co-tangent bundles are of particular interest to Lorentzian geometry.
Unlike the Riemannian case on Lorentzian manifolds there is no canonical way to define a metric.
As observed in
[
18
]
a unit co-tangent bundle
ST^{\ast}\Sigma
is contactomorphic to the space of null geodesics of globally hyperbolic manifolds with Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
.
If the isotopy class of the fibre of the unit co-tangent bundle of the Cauchy surface is orderable, the points in the Lorentzian manifold can be identified with Legendrians isotopic to the fibres such that the identification respects the relations on the manifold and the Legendrian isotopy class (see
[
9
,
11
]
).
In this case the Lorentzian distance and the Rosen-Zhang metric on the Legendrian isotopy class naturally induce a Lorentzian distance and a metric on the globally hyperbolic manifold.

2.
Main Results

Let
(M,\xi)
be a closed co-oriented contact manifold.
Given a contact form
\alpha
Shelukhin
[
21
]
defined a non-conjugation invariant norm on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
by

|\varphi|_{\alpha}:=\inf\left\{\left.\int\limits_{0}^{1}\max\limits_{M}|\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})|\,dt\right|\varphi_{t}\text{ isotopy with }\varphi_{0}=id_{M},\varphi_{1}=\varphi\right\}.

The norm
|\cdot|_{\alpha}
has the following properties

Theorem

(
[
21
]
)
.

The norm
|\cdot|_{\alpha}
satisfies

(i)

|\varphi|_{\alpha}=0\Leftrightarrow\varphi=id_{M}
.

(ii)

|\varphi\psi|_{\alpha}\leq|\varphi|_{\alpha}+|\psi|_{\alpha}
.

(iii)

|\varphi^{-1}|_{\alpha}=|\varphi|_{\alpha}
.

(iv)

|\psi\varphi\psi^{-1}|_{\alpha}=|\varphi|_{\psi^{\ast}\alpha}
.

Remark
.

In the following we will work with the metric
d_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi):=|\psi^{-1}\varphi|_{\alpha}
.
Note that the metrics induced by two different contact forms are equivalent (
[
21
]
).
Therefore they induce the same topology on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
.

For a contactomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
define the sets
I^{+}(\varphi),I^{-}(\varphi),J^{+}(\varphi)
and
J^{-}(\varphi)
with respect to the relations
\llcurly
and
\preccurlyeq
analogously to the Lorentzian case.
A natural topology on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
introduced in
[
11
]
is then given by the interval topology, i.e. the topology induced by sets of the form
I^{+}(\varphi)\cap I^{-}(\psi)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The interval topology is coarser than the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
, in particular the sets
I^{\pm}(\varphi)
are open with respect to
d_{\alpha}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a contact form
\alpha
on a closed contact manifold
(M,\xi)
define
\tau_{\alpha}\colon\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)\times\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sup\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt\right\},&\text{if }\varphi\preccurlyeq\psi\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Here the supremum is taken over all non-negative paths
\varphi_{t}
with
\varphi_{0}=\varphi
and
\varphi_{1}=\psi
.

Like the metric
d_{\alpha}
the function
\tau_{\alpha}
fails to be left invariant.
In
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
it is shown that any conjugation invariant norm on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is discrete in the sense that any contactomorphism that is not the identity has norm greater than some positive constant.
It is a natural question to ask weather a similar result holds for Lorentzian distance functions.

Conjecture
.

Let
\tau\colon\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)\times\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a map satisfying
\tau(\varphi,\psi)>0
iff
\varphi\llcurly\psi
and
\tau(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\geq\tau(\varphi_{1},\psi)+\tau(\psi,\varphi_{2})
for
\varphi_{1}\preccurlyeq\psi\preccurlyeq\varphi_{2}
.
Assume that
\tau
is lower semi-continuous with respect to the interval topology and bi-invariant.
Then

\displaystyle\tau(\varphi,\psi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\infty,&\text{if }\varphi\llcurly\psi\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Remark
.

The reverse triangle inequality immediately implies that there are no finite discrete Lorentzian distance functions since such a function has to be strictly increasing along positive paths of contactomorphisms.

In section
5
we will prove a version of this conjecture for Legendrian isotopy classes.

Theorem 2.3
.

The map
\tau_{\alpha}
satisfies

(i)

\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)>0\Leftrightarrow\varphi\llcurly\psi
.

(ii)

\tau_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to the interval topology and the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
.

(iii)

\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\geq\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi_{1},\psi)+\tau_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi_{2})
for
\varphi_{1}\preccurlyeq\psi\preccurlyeq\varphi_{2}
.

(iv)

\tau_{\alpha}(\psi\varphi_{1},\psi\varphi_{2})=\tau_{\psi^{\ast}\alpha}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
.

Question 1
.

Theorem
2.3
shows that
{(\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M),d_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha})}
is a Lorentzian pre-length space in the sense of
[
17
]
.
Are there contact manifolds such that

{(\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M),d_{\alpha},\tau_{\alpha})}
has the structure of a Lorentzian length space?

To answer the question one would need to show the local existence of non-negative paths maximizing the integral in Definition
2.2
, i.e. the existence of paths connecting two
d_{\alpha}
-close contactomorphisms
\varphi\preccurlyeq\psi
so that their ’Lorentzian length’ coincides with
\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)
.

Call
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)

orderable
if
\preccurlyeq
defines a partial order on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
, i.e. if there are no non-negative loops.
By
[
13
, Proposition 2.1.B]
this is equivalent to the non existence of a positive loop connecting
id_{M}
to itself.
Note that the proof in
[
13
]
is given for contractible non-negative loops but works analogously for non contractible loops.

The following Corollary of Lemma
3.1
answers
[
21
, Question 18]
if
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is orderable.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
(M,\xi)
is closed and
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
orderable.
Denote by
\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}
the Reeb flow of a contact form
\alpha
.
Then
|\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}|_{\alpha}=|t|
, in particular

\sup\limits_{\varphi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)}|\varphi|_{\alpha}=\infty.

Similarly one can prove

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be a closed contact manifold.
The group
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is orderable if and only if
\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)<\infty
for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
.
In this case, if
\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}
denotes the Reeb-flow with respect to
\alpha
, then

\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi)=t

for all
t\geq 0
and all
\varphi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
.

Remark
.

The fact that orderability is equivalent to the non existence of positive loops and to the finiteness of
\tau_{\alpha}
is a huge difference to Lorentzian geometry.
In
[
19
]
there are given examples of manifolds that contain lightlike loops but no timelike loops.
Moreover there are many examples of strongly causal spacetimes such that the Lorentzian distance is not finite (see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 4.30.]
).

For
0<\epsilon<\infty
consider the sets

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}^{+}(\varphi,\epsilon):=\{\psi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)|0<\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)<\epsilon\},

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}^{-}(\varphi,\epsilon):=\{\psi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)|0<\tau_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi)<\epsilon\}.

Corollary 2.6
.

The sets
B_{\alpha}^{+}(\varphi,\epsilon_{1})\cap B_{\alpha}^{-}(\psi,\epsilon_{2})
form a basis of the interval topology.

Proof.

Theorem
2.3
implies that
\tau_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to the interval topology.
Then the proof works analogous to the case of a strongly causal Lorentzian manifold
(M,g)
such that
\tau_{g}
is continuous (see
[
2
, Proposition 4.31.]
).
∎

Now consider the case when
M
is a spherical co-tangent bundle, i.e.
M
is the quotient bundle
T^{\ast}N\setminus\{0\}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for some smooth manifold
N
.
Here
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
acts on
T^{\ast}N\setminus\{0\}
by positive fibrewise homotheties.
The spherical co-tangent bundle naturally carries a contact structure
\xi_{st}
:
Consider the canonical Liouville form
\lambda
on
T^{\ast}N
.
The
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action induces a Liouville vector field
Y
on
T^{\ast}N\setminus\{0\}
, i.e. a vector field that satisfies
d\lambda(Y,\cdot)=\lambda
.
Since
Y
is tangent to the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action, the kernel of
\lambda
projects to a contact structure
\xi_{st}
on the quotient (see
[
15
]
).

Using estimates for spectral invariants of Legendrians on jet-spaces (
[
26
]
) we will in section
4
show the following estimate for
\tau_{\alpha}
:

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that
(M,\xi)\cong(ST^{\ast}N,\xi_{st})
, where
N
is closed and smoothly covered by an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
depending on
\alpha
such that for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)

\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)\leq C_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi).

In general the interval topology is strictly coarser than the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
.
If
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is not orderable it is not even Hausdorff.
With the assumptions of Theorem
2.7
(except for
N
being closed)
[
11
]
used similar methods to show that the interval topology on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is Hausdorff.

Question 2
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is orderable.
Is
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
then strongly causal in the sense that the interval topology coincides with the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
?

Question 3
.

As pointed out above in Lorentzian geometry the strongly causal manifolds with a Lorentzian distance finite and continuous on the conformal class are globally hyperbolic.
Do Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.5
imply that if
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
is orderable other properties of globally hyperbolic manifolds like the existence of time functions or Cauchy surfaces transfer to
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
?

3.
Proofs

The following result is the key Lemma to prove Theorem
2.3
and many other results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be a path of contactomorphisms with

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt=\epsilon.

Then for any
\delta>0
there exists a path
\psi_{t}
with
\psi_{0}=\varphi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}=\varphi_{1}
such that for all
t\in[0,1]

\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})\in(\epsilon-\delta,\epsilon+\delta).

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let
\tau\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by
\tau(0)=0
and

\tau^{\prime}(t)=-\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})+\epsilon.

Note that
\tau^{\prime}
is continuous since
\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})
is smooth, i.e.
\tau(t)
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
.
Denote by
\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}
the Reeb-flow of the contact form
\alpha
.
Define
\tilde{\psi}_{t}:=\varphi^{\alpha}_{\tau(t)}\circ\varphi_{t}
.
Then
\tilde{\psi}_{t}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
path of contactomorphisms with
\tilde{\psi}_{0}=\varphi_{0}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{1}=\varphi_{1}
since
\tau(0)=0
and

\tau(1)=-\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt+\epsilon=0.

Moreover

\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\tilde{\psi}})=\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})+\tau^{\prime}(t)=\epsilon.

Here we used that
\varphi_{\tau(t)}^{\alpha}
is a strict contactomorphism for all
t
, i.e.
\varphi_{\tau(t)}^{\alpha}{}^{\ast}\alpha=\alpha
.
Approximating
\tau
with smooth functions with fixed endpoints we get
\psi_{t}
with the desired properties.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

We show that
I^{+}(\varphi)
is open with respect to
d_{\alpha}
.
The proof works analogously for
I^{-}(\varphi)
.
Let
\psi\in I^{+}(\varphi)
and
\varphi_{t}
be a positive path with
\varphi_{0}=\varphi
and
\varphi_{1}=\psi
.
Choose
\epsilon>0
such that
\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})>\epsilon
.
There exists a smooth family of positive functions
\rho_{t}
such that
(\varphi_{t}\circ\psi^{-1})^{\ast}\alpha=\rho_{t}\alpha
.
Define

\alpha_{0}:=\max\limits_{[0,1]\times M}\rho_{t}\alpha.

Take
\tilde{\psi}
with
d_{\alpha_{0}}(\psi,\tilde{\psi})<\epsilon
.
By Lemma
3.1
we can choose a path
\psi_{t}
with
\psi_{0}=\psi
and
\psi_{1}=\tilde{\psi}
such that
\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})>-\epsilon
.
Define

\tilde{\varphi}_{t}:=\varphi_{t}\circ\psi^{-1}\circ\psi_{t}.

Then
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\varphi
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{1}=\tilde{\psi}
.
Moreover

\displaystyle\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\tilde{\varphi}})

\displaystyle\geq\min\limits_{M}(\varphi_{t}\psi^{-1})^{\ast}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})+\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})

\displaystyle\geq\min(0,\min\limits_{M}\alpha_{0}(X_{t}^{\psi}))+\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})>0.

Hence
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}
is a positive path from
\varphi
to
\tilde{\psi}
, i.e.
\tilde{\psi}\in I^{+}(\varphi)
.
It follows that
I^{+}(\varphi)
is open with respect to
d_{\alpha_{0}}
and thus with respect to any
d_{\alpha}
since these metrics are equivalent.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

(i)

By definition
\varphi\llcurly\psi\Rightarrow\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)>0
.
If
\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)>0
there exists a path
\varphi_{t}
between
\varphi
and
\psi
with
\epsilon:=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt>0
.
Lemma
3.1
implies the existence of a positive path between
\varphi
and
\psi
by setting e.g.
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

(ii)

We show that
\tau_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to the interval topology.
Then the claim follows by Proposition
2.1
.
Let
(\varphi,\psi)\in\tau_{\alpha}^{-1}((a,b))
, where
(a,b)\subset[0,\infty]
is an open interval or
(a,b)=[0,b)
or
(a,b)=(a,\infty]
.
Denote by
\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}
the Reeb flow of
\alpha
.

Claim 1:
There exists
r>0
such that
\tau_{\alpha}(\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}\varphi,\varphi_{s}^{\alpha}\psi)\in(a,b)
for all
s,t\in[-r,r]
.

Suppose not.

1. Case:
Assume
b<\infty
and for arbitrarily small

there exist
t_{0},s_{0}
and a path
\psi_{t}
with
\psi_{0}=\varphi_{t_{0}}^{\alpha}\varphi
and
\psi_{1}=\varphi_{s_{0}}^{\alpha}\psi
such that
\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})\,dt\geq b
.

Due to Lemma
3.1
one can assume that
\min\limits_{M}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})>b-\delta
for
\delta
arbitrarily small.
Let
\tau(t):=(t-1)t_{0}-ts_{0}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{t}:=\varphi_{\tau(t)}^{\alpha}\psi_{t}
.
Then
\tilde{\psi}_{0}=\varphi
,
\tilde{\psi}_{1}=\psi
and since

4.
Spherical co-tangent bundles and spectral invariants

To prove Theorem
2.7
we will use spectral invariants on jet-spaces as defined for instance in
[
12
]
,
[
7
]
or
[
11
]
.
We will follow the conventions in
[
26
]
.
Let
N
be a closed manifold.
Its
1
-jet space is given by

J^{1}N:=T^{\ast}N\times\mathbb{R}.

The space
J^{1}N
canonically carries the contact structure defined by the
1
-form
\alpha_{0}:=\lambda+dt
, where
\lambda
denotes the canonical Liouville-form on
T^{\ast}M
and
t
the projection to the
\mathbb{R}
-component.
An important example of Legendrian submanifolds are given by
1
-jets of functions.
For
f\colon N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
define its
1
-jet by

j^{1}f:=\{(p,-df_{p},f(p))\in J^{1}N|p\in N\}.

In particular the
0
-section of
J^{1}N
as the
1
-jet of the
0
-function is a Legendrian submanifold.
Note that all
1
-jets are Legendrian isotopic to the
0
-section.
On the other hand not all Legendrians in this isotopy class are
1
-jets of some function.
An easy way to describe them is given by generating functions:

For some
m\in\mathbb{N}
consider a smooth function
S\colon N\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Look at the set
\Sigma_{S}:=\{(p,e)\in N\times\mathbb{R}^{m}|d_{e}S=0\}
, where
d_{e}S
denotes the fibre differential in the
\mathbb{R}^{m}
direction.
If
0
is a regular value of
d_{e}S
,
\Sigma_{S}
is a submanifold of
N\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Moreover the map
i_{S}(p,e):=(p,-d_{p}S(p,e),S(p,e))
immerses
\Sigma_{S}
into
J^{1}(N)
as a Legendrian submanifold.
Here
d_{p}S
denotes the differential in the
N
direction which is well defined (independent of the choice of a horizontal distribution) on
\Sigma_{S}
.
The function
S
is called a
generating function
for the Legendrian
i_{S}(\Sigma_{S})
.
We say that
S
is
quadratic at infinity
if
S
is of the form

S(p,e)=f(p,e)+Q(e),

where
f
is compactly supported and
Q
is a non-degenerate quadratic form.
For Legendrians isotopic to the
0
-section one can show

Theorem

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
L\subset J^{1}N
be a Legendrian submanifold Legendrian isotopic to the
0
-section.
Then there exists a quadratic at infinity generating function
S
generating
L
.
Moreover if
L_{t}
is a smooth isotopy of Legendrians isotopic to
0
, there exists a smooth family of quadratic at infinity generating functions
S_{t}
such that
S_{t}
generates
L_{t}
.

Generating functions can be used to define spectral invariants for Legendrian submanifolds.
Let
S
be a quadratic at infinity generating function for a Legendrian submanifold
L
.
Since
S=f+Q
and the function
f
has compact support, there exist
a_{0},b_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
H_{\ast}(\{S<b\},\{S<a\},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong H_{\ast}(\{S<b_{0}\},\{S<a_{0}\},\mathbb{Z}_{2})
for all
a\leq a_{0}<b_{0}\leq b
.
Let
q
be the (negative) index of
Q
.
There exists a natural graded isomorphism

i\colon H_{\ast}(\{S<b\},\{S<a\},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow H_{\ast-q}(N,\mathbb{Z}_{2})

that is independent of
a\leq a_{0}
and
b\geq b_{0}
(see
[
26
]
).
Thus for
a\leq a_{0}
and any
b>a
there is a natural inclusion
i^{b}\colon H_{\ast}(\{S<b\},\{S<a\},\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow H_{\ast-q}(N,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
For
A\in H_{\ast}(N,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
define

l(L,A):=\inf\{b\in\mathbb{R}|A\in\mathrm{im}(i^{b})\}.

Due to the Viterbo-Theret uniqueness theorem
[
24
]
,
[
22
]
the spectral invariant
l(L,A)
is independent of the choice of a generating function.
We will use the following Lemma from Zapolsky, for further properties of the spectral invariants see e.g.
[
26
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
L\subset J^{1}N
be a Legendrian submanifold isotopic to
0
and
\varphi_{t}
a path of contactomorphisms such that
\varphi_{0}=id_{M}
and
\varphi_{1}(0)=L
.
Then for any
A\in H_{\ast}(N)

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{\varphi_{t}(0)}\alpha_{0}(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt\leq l(L,A)\leq\int\limits_{0}^{1}\max\limits_{\varphi_{t}(0)}\alpha_{0}(X_{t}^{\varphi})\,dt.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Assume that
M=ST^{\ast}N
, where
N
is smoothly covered by an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
W.l.o.g this open subset contains the origin.
Consider a positive path of contactomorphisms
\varphi_{t}
in
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
.
Note that
M
is covered by
ST^{\ast}\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the projection map is a local contactomorphism.
Hence
\varphi_{t}
lifts to a positive path
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}
on

.
Since

and

are right invariant we can assume that

.
As in
[
7
]
we use the hodograph transform to get a contactomorphism from

to

such that the fibre over the origin is mapped to the
0

J^{1}(S^{n-1})
.
In particular the spectral invariants can be defined for Legendrians in

isotopic to the fibres.
Denote by
\tilde{\alpha}
the contact form on

induced by the standard contact form on

and by

a Legendrian that lifts to the fibre over the origin.
Then using Lemma

Note that the spectral invariant

is independent of the choice of path between
id_{M}
and
\varphi_{1}
.
Taking the supremum over all paths from

to
\varphi_{1}
on the left hand side and the infimum over all paths on the right hand side shows

\tau_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\leq d_{\tilde{\alpha}}(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}).

For

one gets

5.
A Lorentzian distance function for Legendrian isotopy classes

Let
(M,\xi)
be any co-orientable contact manifold (not necessarily closed).
For a closed Legendrian denote by
\mathcal{L}
its Legendrian isotopy class.
Given a parametrisation
l_{t}\colon L\rightarrow L_{t}
of a Legendrian isotopy denote by
X_{t}^{l}
the section of
TM|_{L_{t}}
defined by

X_{t}^{l}(l_{t}(p)):=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=t}l_{s}(p).

A Legendrian isotopy
L_{t}
is called
positive (non-negative)
if there is a parametrisation
l_{t}\colon L\rightarrow L_{t}
such that

\alpha(X_{t}^{l})>(\geq)0.

Given a positive (non-negative) isotopy
L_{t}
of closed Legendrians, there exists a compactly supported non-negative path of contactomorphisms
\varphi_{t}
such that
L_{t}=\varphi_{f(t)}(L)
for a non-decreasing function
f
(see
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
).
Analogous to the relations on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
positivity defines relations
\llcurly
and
\preccurlyeq
on
\mathcal{L}
.
The isotopy class
\mathcal{L}
is called
orderable
if
\preccurlyeq
is a partial order.

There also holds a version of Lemma
3.1
for Legendrian isotopy classes.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
l_{t}\colon L\rightarrow L_{t}
be a Legendrian isotopy with

\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{L_{t}}\alpha(X_{t}^{l})\,dt=\epsilon.

Assume that
l_{t}=\varphi_{t}|_{L_{0}}
for some compactly supported path of contactomorphisms
\varphi_{t}
.
Then for all
\delta>0
there exists
\tilde{l}_{t}\colon L_{0}\rightarrow\tilde{L}_{t}
with
\tilde{L}_{1}=L_{1}
such that

\min\limits_{L_{t}}\alpha(X_{t}^{\tilde{l}})\in(\epsilon-\delta,\epsilon+\delta).

Proof.

Take a compactly supported path of contactomorphisms such that
\varphi_{t}|_{L_{0}}=l_{t}
.
Let
\tau\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\tau(0)=0
and

\tau^{\prime}(t):=-\min\limits_{L_{t}}\alpha(X_{t}^{l})+\epsilon.

Then
\psi_{t}:=\varphi_{\tau(t)}^{\alpha}\varphi_{t}
satisfies
\psi_{0}(L_{0})=L_{0}
and
\psi_{1}(L_{0})=L_{1}

Moreover since
\varphi_{\tau(t)}^{\alpha}
is a strict contactomorphism

\min\limits_{\psi_{t}(L_{0})}\alpha(X_{t}^{\psi})=\min\limits_{L_{t}}\alpha(X_{t}^{l})+\tau^{\prime}(t)=\epsilon.

Approximating
\tau
by smooth functions with fixed endpoints finally gives
\tilde{l_{t}}
with the desired properties.

.
Then

\displaystyle\tau(L_{0},L_{1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\infty,&\text{if }L_{0}\llcurly L_{1}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Proof.

Let
L
be a closed Legendrian and assume there exists such a function
\tau
on its Legendrian isotopy class
\mathcal{L}
.
Let
L_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
with
L\llcurly L_{2}
.
Choose a positive path
\varphi_{t}
with
\varphi_{0}=L
and
\varphi_{1}=L_{2}
.
Then for
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<1
small enough and
L_{0}:=\varphi_{t_{0}}(L),L_{1}:=\varphi_{t_{1}}(L)
one can assume that
L_{0}\cap L=L_{1}\cap L=L_{0}\cap L_{1}=\emptyset
.

Claim:
There exists
\psi\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
with
\psi(L_{0})=L_{1}
and
\psi(L)=L
.

Take an open neighbourhood
U
of
L
such that
\varphi_{t}(L)\cap U=\emptyset
for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.
Let
V
be an open neighbourhood of
L
with
\overline{V}\subset U
.
Let
F\colon[t_{0},t_{1}]\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with
F(t,p)=\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi\varphi_{t_{0}}^{-1}})(p)
for
p\in M\setminus U
and
F(t,p)=0
for
p\in V
.
Define
\psi
to be the flow of the time-dependent contact Hamiltonian vector field of
F
at time
t_{1}
.
Then
\psi=id_{M}
on
V
and since for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
we have
\varphi_{t}(L)\cap U=\emptyset
,
\psi
coincides with
\varphi_{t_{1}}\varphi_{t_{0}}^{-1}
around
L_{0}
.

It follows

\tau(L,L_{0})=\tau(\psi(L),\psi(L_{0}))=\tau(L,L_{1})\geq\tau(L,L_{0})+\tau(L_{0},L_{1}).

Since
\tau(L_{0},L_{1})>0
this implies
\tau(L,L_{0})=\infty
.
Due to the reverse triangle inequality
\tau(L,\cdot)
is strictly increasing along positive paths.
Hence
\tau(L,L_{2})=\infty
.
∎

We define a Lorentzian distance function on
\mathcal{L}
that is not
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
-invariant by

Definition 5.5
.

Define

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sup\left\{\int\limits_{0}^{1}\min\limits_{L_{t}}\alpha(X_{t}^{l})\,dt\right\},&\text{if }L_{0}\preccurlyeq L_{1}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

Here the supremum is taken over all non-negative Legendrian isotopies
l_{t}
with
l_{0}(L_{0})=L_{0}
and
l_{1}(L_{0})=L_{1}
such that
l_{t}=\varphi_{t}|_{L_{0}}
for some compactly supported non-negative path of contactomorphisms
\varphi_{t}
.

Using Lemma
5.1
one can analogously to the case of the Lorentzian distance on
\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(M)
prove the following.

Theorem 5.6
.

The map
\tau_{\alpha}
satisfies

(i)

\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1})>0\Leftrightarrow L_{0}\llcurly L_{1}
.

(ii)

\tau_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to the interval topology.

(iii)

\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{2})\geq\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1})+\tau_{\alpha}(L_{1},L_{2})
for
L_{0}\preccurlyeq L_{1}\preccurlyeq L_{2}
.

If
M
is closed and
\mathcal{L}
orderable then

(iv)

\tau_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 5.7
.

The Legendrian isotopy class
\mathcal{L}
is orderable if and only if
\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1})<\infty
for all
L_{0},L_{1}\in\mathcal{L}
.
In this case for
t\geq 0

\tau_{\alpha}(L,\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}(L))=t.

Since Lemma
4.1
is formulated in terms of Legendrians, one also has

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume that
(M,\xi)\cong(ST^{\ast}N,\xi_{st})
, where
N
is smoothly covered by an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
depending on
\alpha
such that for all
L_{0},L_{1}
isotopic to the fibres one has

\tau_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1})\leq C_{\alpha}d_{\alpha}(L_{0},L_{1}).

6.
A metric for globally hyperbolic spacetimes

In
[
18
]
Low constructed the
space of null geodesics

\mathcal{N}_{g}
of a Lorentzian manifold
(N,g)
.
He observed that for globally hyperbolic
(N,g)
the space of null geodesics naturally carries the structure of a smooth contact manifold.
Moreover given a Cauchy hypersurface
\Sigma
he constructed a contactomorphism
\rho_{\Sigma}
from
\mathcal{N}_{g}
to
ST^{\ast}\Sigma
equipped with its standard contact structure.
Given a point
p\in N
its
sky

S(p)
is the set of null geodesics through the point
p
.
Denote by
S(N)
the set of all skies.
The set
S(p)
is always a Legendrian submanifold of
\mathcal{N}_{g}
and is mapped by
\rho_{N}
to the isotopy class of the fibres in
ST^{\ast}N
(
[
18
]
).

Chernov and Nemirovski
[
11
, Proposition 4.5]
proved that if the isotopy class of the fibre is orderable, then the map
p\mapsto S(p)
bijectively maps
N
to
S(N)
so that the natural orders on both sets coincide.
Hence the maps
\tau_{\alpha}
and
d_{\alpha}
restrict to
S(N)
and induce a Lorentzian distance and a metric on
N
.

Theorem 6.1
.

In the case described above the metric
d_{\alpha}^{N}:=d_{\alpha}|_{S(N)\times S(N)}
induces the manifold topology on
N
.

Proof.

Since the isotopy class of the fibres in
ST^{\ast}\Sigma
is orderable Corollary
5.3
implies that the interval topology on
S(N)
is open with respect to the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}
.
Thus
[
11
, Corollary 4.6]
implies that the manifold topology on
N
is coarser than the topology induced by
d_{\alpha}^{N}
.

Due to the Bernal-Sánchez theorem (see e.g.
[
19
]
) one can assume that
N=\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
, where
\{t\}\times\Sigma
is a Cauchy hypersurface for every
t
.
Let
(t_{0},p)\in N
and
\epsilon>0
.
W.l.o.g. assume
t_{0}=0
.
Since
(N,g)
is strongly causal it suffices to show that there exists
\delta>0
such that
d_{\alpha}(S((0,p)),S((t,q)))<\epsilon
for any
(t,q)\in I^{+}((-\delta,p))\cap I^{-}((\delta,p))
.
Let
\rho_{t}\colon\mathcal{N}_{g}\rightarrow ST^{\ast}(\{t\}\times\Sigma)
be the natural contactomorphism described in
[
18
]
.
Define
\varphi_{t}:=\rho_{0}\circ\rho_{t}^{-1}
.
Using the natural identification one has
\varphi_{t}\in\mathrm{Cont}_{0}(ST^{\ast}(\{0\}\times\Sigma))
.
Denote by
F_{q}
the fibre over the point
q\in\Sigma
.
Then by definition
S(t,q)=\varphi_{t}(F_{q})
.
In particular for a curve of the form
(f(t),\gamma(t))
one has
S(f(t),\gamma(t))=\varphi_{f(t)}(F_{\gamma(t)})
.
Choose a parametrisation
l_{t}\colon S^{n}\rightarrow F_{\gamma(t)}
.
Then for
w\in S((f(t),\gamma(t))
and
u\in S^{n}
with
w=\varphi_{f(t)}(l_{t}(u))

\displaystyle\alpha_{w}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{f(t)}(l_{t}(u))\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{w}\left(d\varphi_{f(t)}\left(\frac{d}{dt}l_{t}(u)\right)+f^{\prime}(t)X^{\varphi}_{f(t)}(w)\right)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{f(t)}^{\ast}\alpha)_{l_{t}(u)}(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(t))+f^{\prime}(t)\alpha_{w}(X^{\varphi}_{f(t)}(w)).

Here
\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)
denotes any vector
v\in T_{l_{t}(u)}ST^{\ast}\Sigma
with
d\pi v=\gamma^{\prime}(t)
.

Define
C:=\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\max\limits_{\varphi_{t}(F_{p})}|\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})|
.
It follows that for
0<\delta<1

d_{\alpha}(S((0,p)),S((\delta,p)))\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\delta}\max\limits_{\varphi_{t}(F_{p})}|\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})|\,dt\leq C\delta

and

d_{\alpha}(S((0,p)),S((-\delta,p)))\leq\int\limits_{-\delta}^{0}\max\limits_{\varphi_{t}(F_{p})}|\alpha(X_{t}^{\varphi})|\,dt\leq C\delta.

Let
(s,q)\in I^{+}((-\delta,p))\cap I^{-}((\delta,p))
.
Since the metrics induced by different contact forms are equivalent one can assume that
\alpha
is induced by a Riemannian metric
h
, i.e.
\alpha_{[v]}(w)=v(d\pi(w))
, where
v\in[v]\in ST^{\ast}\Sigma
with
h^{\ast}(v,v)=1
and
\pi\colon ST^{\ast}\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
denotes the projection.
There exist smooth positive functions
\rho_{t}
with
\varphi_{t}^{\ast}\alpha=\rho_{t}\alpha
.
Define
\tilde{C}:=\max\limits_{[0,1]\times ST^{\ast}\Sigma}\rho_{t}
.
Then for
0<\delta<1
one has
\varphi_{t}^{\ast}\alpha\leq\tilde{C}\alpha
for all
t\in[-\delta,\delta]
.
Moreover since
N
is globally hyperbolic one can choose
\hat{C}
such that

I^{+}((-\delta,p))\cap I^{-}((\delta,p))\subset[-\delta,\delta]\times B_{\hat{C}\delta}(p).

Here
B_{\hat{C}\delta}(p)
denotes the ball of radius
\hat{C}\delta
around
p
with respect to
h
.
Let
(f(t),\gamma(t))
be a causal curve from
(-\delta,p)
to
(s,q)
.
Then

\displaystyle d_{\alpha}(S((0,p)),S((s,q)))

\displaystyle\leq d_{\alpha}(S((0,p)),S((-\delta,p)))+d_{\alpha}(S((-\delta,p)),S((s,q)))

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{1}\max\limits_{\varphi_{t}(F_{p})}|\varphi_{t}^{\ast}\alpha(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(t))|\,dt+C\delta\leq(\tilde{C}\hat{C}+C)\delta.

∎

Contrary to the Riemannian case there is no canonical way to associate a metric to a Lorentzian manifold.
So it is not surprising that our construction depends on the choice of a contact form
\alpha
on
\mathcal{N}_{g}
.

Remark
.

An ’intrinsic’ way to define a metric and a Lorentzian distance function on

in the definition of

Example 6.2
.

Consider the manifold
N=\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
with Lorentzian metric
g=-dt^{2}+k
, where

is a complete Riemannian metric on
\Sigma

In this case
(N,g)
is globally hyperbolic (
[
19
]
).
Moreover assume that the isotopy class of the fibres in
ST^{\ast}\Sigma
is orderable.
All null geodesics are up to parametrisation of the form
(t,\beta(t))
, where
\beta(t)
is a geodesic of
k
in
\Sigma
.
Thus the path
\varphi_{t}=\rho_{0}\rho_{t}^{-1}
used in the proof of Theorem
6.1
is given by

\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{t}^{\alpha}.

Here
-\alpha
is the contact form induced by
k
on
ST^{\ast}\Sigma
, i.e.
\varphi_{t}
is the inverse of the co-geodesic flow of
k
and is positive with respect to the co-orientation defined by
\alpha
.
Denoting by
F_{p}
the fibre over a point
p\in\Sigma

S(t,p)=\varphi_{t}(F_{p})
.
Let
(f(t),\gamma(t))
be a curve in
N
.
Choose a parametrisation
l_{t}\colon S^{n}\rightarrow F_{\gamma(t)}
.
Then for
w\in S((f(t),\gamma(t))
and
u\in S^{n}
with

\displaystyle\alpha_{w}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{f(t)}(l_{t}(u))\right)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{f(t)}^{\ast}\alpha)_{l_{t}(u)}(\hat{\gamma}^{\prime}(t))+f^{\prime}(t)\alpha_{w}(X^{\varphi}_{f(t)}(w))

\displaystyle=k(l_{t}(u),\gamma^{\prime}(t))+f^{\prime}(t).

Hence

\displaystyle\max\limits_{v\in S(f(t),\gamma(t))}\alpha_{v}(X_{t}^{l}(v))=|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{k}+f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\min\limits_{v\in S(f(t),\gamma(t))}\alpha_{v}(X_{t}^{l}(v))=-|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{k}+f^{\prime}(t).

It follows that
\hat{d}_{\alpha}
is the metric induced by the continuous Finsler-metric

and

the Lorentzian distance function of the continuous Lorentz-Finsler metric (
[
16
]
)
-|\cdot|_{k}+dt
.

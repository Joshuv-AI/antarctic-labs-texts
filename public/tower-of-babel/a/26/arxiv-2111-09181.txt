If

contains a

In the present article we tackle the foremost obstacle that stands in the way of applying the theory we sketched: namely, the notoriously difficult task of deciding whether, for specific
choices of
\Lambda

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

In both directions we make headway, next to retrieving known results in a simpler, more uniform format.

In more detail: In the first of our main results we address an Artin algebra
\Lambda
for which

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting
if there exists an infinite sequence of basic algebras

2.1.
Contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})

Following Miyashita
[

we call a left
\Lambda

(1)

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
,
(2)

\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}_{\Lambda}(T,T)=0
for

(3)
there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow{{}_{\Lambda}\Lambda}\rightarrow T_{0}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow T_{m}\rightarrow 0

rank
K_{0}(\Lambda)

gives rise to a left-right symmetric situation as follows: If

\widetilde{\Lambda}=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(T)^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}

then the right
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-module

is in turn a tilting module and

is canonically isomorphic to
\Lambda
. This justifies the reference to a

{{}_{\Lambda}T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}}
. Strong tilting modules were first considered by Auslander and Reiten in
[
1
]
. We introduce them via a characterization equivalent to the original definition (see
[
1
]
):
Namely, we call a tilting module
T

strong
if it satisfies the following relative injectivity condition in
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
:
(4)

\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}(M,T)=0
for all

and

. It was shown by Auslander
and Reiten [loc.cit.] that
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
has a strong tilting module if and only if the category
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, a property which we will recall next. Moreover, according to [loc.cit], in case of existence, there is precisely one basic strong tilting module in

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, up to isomorphism.

The concept of contravariant finiteness of subcategories of
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
has its roots in
work of Auslander and Smalø
[
2
]

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})

M\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

the functor

is finitely generated in the category of additive contravariant functors
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})\rightarrow\mathbf{Ab}

This condition translates into the following requirement for arbitrary

: There is an object

\phi\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(A,M)

such that each map in
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}),M)

\phi
.
Any such pair
(A,\phi)
is called a (

)
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
M

A
of

as a
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
M
. Suppose
M
has a

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation, say
\phi:A\rightarrow M
. As was shown by Auslander-Smalø in [loc.cit.], up to isomorphism, there is only one approximation

\phi:\mathcal{A}(M)\rightarrow M
such that
\mathcal{A}(M)
has minimal length.
It is alternatively characterized by the condition that any endomorphism
u
of
\mathcal{A}(M)
which
satisfies
\phi\circ u=\phi
is an automorphism; we say that the map
\phi
is
(
right
)
minimal
if this implication holds. Since it is unlikely to cause misunderstandings, we will also refer to
\mathcal{A}(M)
as “the” minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of

3.
Iteration of strong tilting

When can the process of strongly tilting
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
to a category of right modules,

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
, be iterated? In the positive case, how do the resulting sequences of strongly tilted module
categories behave? The main purpose of this section is to answer these questions. More specifically, we will show
that, whenever
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
can be strongly tilted twice, the process can be iterated arbitrarily and turns
periodic after the initial step. Roughly speaking: In case
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
is in turn contravariantly finite in

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
, the initial transition from
\Lambda
to
\widetilde{\Lambda}
increases the homological symmetry by increasing
the number of simple modules of finite projective dimension.
This symmetrization makes the subsequent sequence

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}\rightsquigarrow\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}\mbox{\rm-mod}\rightsquigarrow\cdots

periodic with period
2
.

The statement of the following theorem is based on part (1) of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a basic Artin algebra such that
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is
contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Moreover, let
{{}_{\Lambda}T}
be
the corresponding basic strong tilting module, and
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(T)^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
is in turn contravariantly finite in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
,
thus giving rise to a basic tilting bimodule
{{}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}}\widetilde{T}}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
which is strong in

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
. Then
\widetilde{T}
is strong on both sides.

In particular, the process of strongly tilting
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
then allows for unlimited iteration, yielding a sequence of basic Artin algebras
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3},\dots
with the property that
\Lambda_{i}
and
\Lambda_{j}
are isomorphic whenever
i
and
j
are positive integers with the same parity.

Moreover, the algebras
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are isomorphic precisely when the tilting bimodule

{}_{\Lambda}T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
is strong on both sides, i.e., when
T=\widetilde{T}
.

Comment 3.2
.

If, in the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, the qualifiers “basic” are dropped, one
obtains Morita equivalent pairs of algebras
(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i+2})
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

By a result of Auslander and
Green (see
[
1
, Proposition 6.5]
and
[
7
, Proposition 7.1]
for a short argument), a tilting
bimodule
{}_{A}U_{B}
over Artin algebras
A
and
B
is strong in
A\text{-mod}
if and only if all simple right
B
-modules embed into
U_{B}
. In light of the hypothesis that
{}_{\Lambda}T
is
a strong tilting module, we thus find that all simple right
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-modules are contained in

\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
, up to isomorphism.
Moreover, we deduce that
strongness of
{}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}}\widetilde{T}
as
a tilting object in

\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}\mbox{\rm-mod}
will follow if we can show that all simple
right
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-modules embed into
\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
.

To realize such an embedding, let
(\widetilde{\Lambda}/\widetilde{J})_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}=\widetilde{S}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\widetilde{S}_{n}
, where the
\widetilde{S}_{i}
are simple.
Further denote by
E(\widetilde{S}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\widetilde{S}_{n})
an
injective envelope, and let
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
be a minimal

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
-approximation of the latter.
In light of our hypothesis that
\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
is a
strong tilting object in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
, the
categories
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}})
and
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}})

coincide; see Theorem
2.1
(2). Embed
T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
into an
injective right
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-module, say

\iota:T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n}E(\widetilde{S}_{i})^{m_{i}}
.
For a suitable exponent
m
, we obtain a
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
-approximation

\phi:\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{m}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n}E(\widetilde{S}_{i})^{m_{i}}
. Since
T_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
,
the embedding
\iota
factors through
\phi
, say
\iota=\phi\circ f
for some

.
Then

is injective, and we deduce that all simple right
\widetilde{\Lambda}
-modules embed into
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}^{m}
.
Clearly, this implies that all simples in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
embed into
\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
.
Invoking the fact that
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}})=\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}})
, we conclude that all

\widetilde{S}_{i}
occur in
\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
as well,
which proves two-sided strongness of
{{}_{\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}}\widetilde{T}}_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
.

The final assertion follows from the preceding argument.
∎

The result is sharp in the sense that the existence of a strong tilting module in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

does not, by itself, provide sufficient homological symmetry to allow for iteration of strong tilting.

Example 3.3
.

This example is due to Igusa-Smalø-Todorov

. Let

, where

(see
[

]
).

For a first application, we combine Theorem

We conclude the section with a variant of the criterion of Auslander and Green for a strong tilting module

Observation 3.5
.

Suppose
\Lambda
is an Artin algebra such that

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)
The basic strong tilting module

is strong also in

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
, where
\widetilde{\Lambda}=\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(T)^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
.

(2)
Every simple left
\Lambda
-module of infinite projective dimension embeds into a
\Lambda
-module of finite projective dimension.

(3)
There exists a duality

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
.

If
(1)-(3)
are satisfied, then
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(-,T)
and
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}(-,T)
induce
(
unique up to isomorphism
)
quasi-inverse dualities

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})\ \longleftrightarrow\ \mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
.

Proof.

“(1)
\implies
(2)” is immediate from the criterion of Auslander and Green quoted in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. “(2)
\implies
(1)”: By (2), every simple left
\Lambda
-module
S
embeds into a module
M=M(S)
of finite projective dimension. Consider an embedding
\iota:M\hookrightarrow E
of
M
into an injective module
E
, and let
\phi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow E
be a minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation. Then
\iota
factors through
\phi
, which shows
M
to be contained in
\mathcal{A}
up to isomorphism. Hence so is
S
. Since
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(T)
by hypothesis (cf. the proof of Theorem
3.1
), we conclude that
S
embeds into
T
. Thus an application of
[
1
, Proposition 6.5]
yields (1).

The equivalence “(1)
\iff
(3)” follows from
[
13
, Reference Theorem III and Theorem 1]
or
[

, Theorems 7,8]
. For the supplementary statement, see
[

, Corollary 9]
.

4.
Testing for contravariant finiteness of the category
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})

Our main objective in this section is to show that, in exploring whether the subcategory
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, a comparatively mild hypothesis (
4.1
(ii) below) permits us to eliminate primitive idempotents which correspond to simple left
\Lambda
-modules of finite projective dimension. This reduces the problem to a more manageable corner
e\Lambda e
of
\Lambda
.

4.1.
The setting

Setting 4.1

(Blanket hypotheses)
.

Throughout this section, we will assume that the idempotent
e\in\Lambda
satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)
The semisimple left
\Lambda
-module
\Lambda(1-e)/J(1-e)
has finite projective dimension.

(ii)

e\Lambda(1-e)
has finite projective dimension as a left
e\Lambda e
-module.

Remarks 4.2
.

\bullet
Suppose that
e=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{m}
and
1-e=e_{m+1}+\cdots+e_{n}
, where the
e_{i}
are orthogonal primitive idempotents of
\Lambda
. Then the conditions under
4.1
amount to finiteness of the projective dimensions of the simple
\Lambda
-modules
S_{i}=\Lambda e_{i}/Je_{i}
for
i\geq m+1
and those of the

e\Lambda e
-modules
e\Lambda e_{i}
for
i\geq m+1
. In particular,
4.1
(i) implies that,
for all
M\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
,

\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}M<\infty\iff\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{core}(M)<\infty\iff\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}M_{\sigma}<\infty
.

With regard to
4.1
(ii), we mention that, in case
\Lambda
is a path algebra modulo relations, the quiver and relations of
e\Lambda e
are available from those of
\Lambda
, making this condition computationally accessible.

\bullet
Suppose that
S_{1},\dots,S_{m}
are precisely the simple left
\Lambda
-modules of infinite projective dimension; in particular, this means that
4.1
(i) is satisfied. Even over monomial algebras, condition
4.1
(ii) is not automatic in this situation, as is witnessed by an example of Fuller and Saorín
[
8
, Example 4.2]
. However, if all of the supplementary simples
S_{m+1},\dots,S_{n}
have projective dimension at most
1
, then
4.1
(ii) does always hold in this scenario [loc.cit.].

In the present setting, the adjoint pair
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}-,\ {\bf{e}})
is particularly
useful towards the transfer of homological information between the categories
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
and
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Namely:

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\Lambda
satisfies the conditions under
4.1
. Then the
functors
{\bf{e}}:\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}\rightarrow e\Lambda e\text{-mod}
and its left adjoint

\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}-:e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}\rightarrow\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
preserve and reflect finite projective dimension.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Lemma 1.2(b)]
, the functor
{\bf{e}}:\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}\rightarrow e\Lambda e\text{-mod}
preserves finite projective dimension. In light of the canonical isomorphism

{\bf{e}}\circ(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}-)\cong\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}}

this shows that
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}-
reflects finite projective dimension.

To see that
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}-
preserves finite projective dimension, let
X\in\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
, and let

{\mathbb{X}}:\ \ 0\rightarrow\,Q_{m}\,{\overset{f_{m}}{\longrightarrow}}\,\cdots\,{\overset{f_{2}}{\longrightarrow}}\;Q_{1}\,{\overset{f_{1}}{\longrightarrow}}\,Q_{0}\,{\overset{f_{0}}{\longrightarrow}}\,X\,\rightarrow 0

be a
projective resolution in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Then all terms
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}Q_{j}
of the complex
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}{\mathbb{X}}
are projective over
\Lambda
; indeed, the
Q_{j}
belong to
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda e)
, whence the
modules
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}Q_{j}
belong to the category
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda e)=\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits({{}_{\Lambda}\Lambda e})
.
Moreover, all homology modules of
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}{\mathbb{X}}
have finite projective dimension in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
; indeed, due to the natural isomorphism
{\bf{e}}(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}{\mathbb{X}})\,\cong\,{\mathbb{X}}
, they are all
annihilated by
{\bf{e}}
, whence their simple composition factors are direct summands of
\Lambda(1-e)/J(1-e)
. Writing
(F_{i})
for the differential
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}f_{i})
of
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}{\mathbb{X}}
, we
find that
\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits(F_{m})
has finite projective dimension. In view of projectivity of
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}Q_{m}
, so does
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(F_{m})
, whence we obtain finiteness of
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits(F_{m-1})
from that of

\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits(F_{m-1})/\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(F_{m})
. An obvious induction thus shows that
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(F_{0})\cong\Lambda\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X
has finite projective dimension over
\Lambda
.

That the functor
{\bf{e}}
reflects finite projective dimension is now seen as follows: Given
M\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
such that
{\bf{e}}(M)
has finite projective dimension over
e\Lambda e
, we apply the conclusion of the preceding paragraph and Lemma
2.3
to deduce that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{core}(M)<\infty
. Once more invoking
4.1
(i), we conclude that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}M<\infty
.
∎

The following consequences of Lemma
4.3

will facilitate applications of the main result of this section.

First we combine Lemma
4.3

with the equivalences
e\Lambda e\text{-Mod}\approx\mathcal{G}\approx{\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}}
of
Lemma
2.4
, to find that finiteness of projective dimensions transfers smoothly among the categories singled out in Section
2.4
. Namely: If
M_{\sigma}\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-Mod}
is an object of the Giraud subcategory
\mathcal{G}
of
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-Mod}
,
then
M_{\sigma}
has finite projective dimension in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if

\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}eM_{\sigma}<\infty
,
if and only if
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}M_{\sigma}<\infty
. Analogously: If
M=\mathfrak{core}(M)
is an object of

{\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F}}
, then
M
has finite projective dimension in this subcategory if and only
if
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}M<\infty
. This leads to

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
M\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, suppose that
eM
has a
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation,
and let
q:X\rightarrow eM
be a minimal such approximation.
Then
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}:(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X)_{\sigma}\rightarrow(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eM)_{\sigma}\cong M_{\sigma}
is a minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
M_{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Referring to the explicit category equivalences of Lemma
2.4
, we
see that
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}
is a minimal approximation
of
M_{\sigma}
with respect to the subcategory
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}\bigl(\mathcal{G}\cap(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})\bigr)

of
\mathcal{G}\cap(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
.
In light of the preceding remarks, the domain of this map has finite projective dimension also in

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Right minimality of
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}
in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

is clear. To see that
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}
is actually a

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation
of
M_{\sigma}
, let
U\in\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
and
f\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(U,M_{\sigma})
. Since
M_{\sigma}
is torsionfree and
U/\Delta(U)
again belongs to
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
, we do not
lose generality in assuming that
U
is torsionfree. Consequently,
\mu_{U}:U\rightarrow U_{\sigma}
is
an embedding with the property that
U_{\sigma}/U\in\mathcal{T}
. Now
the restricted injectivity property of
M_{\sigma}
yields an
extension of
f
to
f^{*}:U_{\sigma}\rightarrow M_{\sigma}
, i.e.,
f=f^{*}\circ\mu_{U}
. Since
U_{\sigma}
has finite projective dimension in
\mathcal{G}\cap(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
, we further obtain a
factorization
f^{*}=(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}\circ g
for some

g\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(U_{\sigma},(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X)_{\sigma})
. This clearly yields a factorization
of
f
through
(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}q)_{\sigma}
.
∎

The next observation depends only on Condition
4.1
(i).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M\in\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, and let
p:\mathcal{A}(M)\rightarrow M
be a
minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation. Suppose
p
factors in the
form
p=\tau\circ\rho

with
\rho\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{A}(M),N)
and
\tau\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(N,M)
.

If
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits N<\infty
and
\rho
is an epimorphism, then
\rho
is an isomorphism.

In particular: If
M\in\mathcal{F}
, then also
\mathcal{A}(M)\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

For the first implication it suffices to observe that, under the given premise,
\tau:N\rightarrow M
is in turn a
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
M
. Minimality of
p
thus shows that
\text{length}(\mathcal{A}(M))\leq\text{length}(N)
. Therefore the epimorphism
\rho
is an isomorphism.

To derive the final implication, assume that
M
is torsionfree, and apply the preceding implication to the canonical map
\rho:\mathcal{A}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(M)/\Delta\bigl(\mathcal{A}(M)\bigr)
.
∎

4.2.
The main theorem

The upcoming theorem shows that, in the situation of
4.1
, contravariant finiteness of

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
is equivalent to
contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
. In fact, we obtain sharper information, relating minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
-approximations in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
to minimal

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
-approximations in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
.

As before,
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=({\mathcal{T}}_{e},{\mathcal{F}}_{e})
is the torsion pair of subsection 2.2. Recall
that the class
\mathcal{T}
consists of the
\Lambda
-modules all of whose composition factors
belong to
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits\bigl(\Lambda(1-e)/J(1-e)\bigr)
, while
\mathcal{F}
consists of the modules
F

with
\mathop{\rm soc}\nolimits F\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\Lambda e/Je)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a basic Artin algebra, and let
e\in\Lambda
be an idempotent such that
the left
\Lambda
-module
\Lambda(1-e)/J(1-e)
has finite
projective dimension and
e\Lambda(1-e)
has finite
projective dimension as a left
e\Lambda e
-module.

Then
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
if and only if
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
e\Lambda e\text{-}{\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits}
.

In more detail: The following two implications hold for every finitely generated left
\Lambda
-module
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

(1)

If
p:M\longrightarrow F
is a minimal right
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
F
,
then
p_{|eM}:eM\longrightarrow eF
is a minimal right
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation of
eF
.

(2)

Suppose that
q:X\longrightarrow eF
is a minimal right
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\text{-}\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits)
-approximation of
eF
, and let
f
be the composition

\frac{\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X}{\Delta(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X)}\ \ \ \overset{\overline{1\otimes q}}{\longrightarrow}\ \ \ \frac{\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eF}{\Delta(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eF)}\ \ \overset{\overline{\epsilon}_{F}}{\longrightarrow}\ \ F,

where
\overline{\epsilon}_{F}
is induced by the natural map
\epsilon_{F}:\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eF\rightarrow F
.
Then any representative of the unique maximal element of the poset
\mathcal{E}_{f}
of
essential
\Delta
-extensions of
f
(cf. Proposition
2.7
for existence) is a minimal right
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\text{-}\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits)
-approximation of
F
.

Remark 4.7
.

By Lemma
2.3
, the map
\overline{1\otimes q}
in statement (2) of the theorem is naturally
isomorphic to the map

\mathfrak{core}(1\otimes q):\mathfrak{core}(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}X)\rightarrow\mathfrak{core}(F)
and, modulo
the canonical isomorphism
\mathfrak{core}(F)\cong\frac{\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eF}{\Delta(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}eF)}
, the map
\overline{\epsilon}_{F}:\mathfrak{core}(F)=\nabla(F)\rightarrow F
is the embedding of
\mathfrak{core}(F)
into
F
.

Proof.

We start by showing that the first assertion follows from (1) and (2). Indeed, the correspondence
S\leftrightarrow eS
is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of simple
\Lambda
-modules in
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\Lambda e/Je)
and those of the simple left
e\Lambda e
-modules. In light of
[
1
, Proposition 3.7]
and the fact that the simples in
\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits\bigl(\Lambda(1-e)/J(1-e)\bigr)

by the proposition, it suffices to show
that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Gamma}\,\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{i},T^{\prime})\linebreak<\infty
for
i\geq m+1
.
Fix
i\geq m+1
in the following. Towards another reduction
step, we apply Proposition
4.8
to a minimal
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
-approximation

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
of
E(S_{i})_{\sigma}
. Part (c) of

4.8
provides us with
a map
\rho:\mathcal{A}_{i}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
such that
both
\mathop{\rm Ker}\nolimits(\rho)
and
\mathop{\rm Coker}\nolimits(\rho)

belong to
\mathcal{T}
.
Since the restriction of the functor
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(-,T)
to
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is exact, so
is
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(-,T^{\prime})_{|_{\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})}}
, and consequently torsionfreeness of
T^{\prime}

shows
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\rho,T^{\prime}):\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i},T^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{i},T^{\prime})

to be an isomorphism of right
\Gamma
-modules. Lemma
4.3
moreover ensures
that
e\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
has finite
projective dimension in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Returning to the category
equivalence
\mathcal{G}\cap(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})\approx e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}

which
sends
M
to
eM
(cf. Lemma
2.4
),
we thus find finiteness of
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Gamma}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i},T^{\prime})

to be equivalent to finiteness of the
projective dimension of

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}(e\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{i},eT^{\prime})
over
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}(eT^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
alias
\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e}
. Given that
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}(-,eT^{\prime})
takes objects in
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
to objects in
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e})
[loc.cit., applied to
e\Lambda e
in place of
\Lambda
], condition (ii) follows.
∎

Theorem
5.4
ensures that, with a “duplicate” in
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of the original idempotent
e\in\Lambda
, the test provided by Theorem
4.6
is again available towards deciding whether
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
is contravariantly finite in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Adopt the hypotheses and notation of Theorem
5.4
. Then contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
is equivalent to
contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e})
in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e}
.

In light of the fact that the basic strong tilt
\widetilde{e\Lambda e}
of
e\Lambda e
coincides with

\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e}
,
we thus obtain: Not only is existence of a strong tilting object
in

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

equivalent to existence of a strong tilting object in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
under conditions
4.1
,
but the same hypothesis implies that
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

allows for arbitrary repetitions of strong tilting precisely when this is true for
e\Lambda e
-mod. This conclusion compiles information from Theorems
3.1
,
4.6
and Corollary
5.5
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a basic Artin algebra and
e\in\Lambda
an idempotent
satisfying the hypotheses
4.1

(
i.e., all simple left
\Lambda
-modules of infinite projective dimension belong
to

\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\Lambda e/Je)
,
and
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda(1-e)<\infty
)
.
Then the category
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows
for unlimited iteration of strong tilting if and only if the same is true for the category
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
.

A first straightforward application of our techniques shows that, in testing for contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
or iterability of strong tilting, we may automatically discard the idempotents corresponding to the simple left
\Lambda
-modules of projective dimension at most
1
. Namely:

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\Lambda
be a basic Artin algebra on which we fix the complete set of primitive idempotents
\{e_{1},...,e_{r},e_{r+1},...,e_{n}\}
, ordered in such a way that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits(\Lambda e_{i}/Je_{i})\leq 1
for
i>r
. For
e=\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}

(
resp.,
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting
)
;

(2)

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}

(
resp.,
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting
)
.

Proof.

From the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
, we know that
e\Lambda(1-e)
is projective as a left
e\Lambda e
-module. Thus the pair
(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod},e)
satisfies the blanket hypotheses of Setting
4.1
. Assertions 1 and 2 are thus consequences of Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
5.6
, respectively.
∎

6.
Applications and examples

By way of the equivalences established in the previous sections, we can now easily secure contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
and

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda})
in cases in which this originally required a considerable effort.
Moreover, the unifying reasons behind these results become more transparent through the reduction and permit us to expand the settings to which they apply.

6.1.
Precyclic/postcyclic vertices and normed Loewy lengths

An instance in which the simplification gained by reduction to corner algebras stands out is that of truncated path algebras and their strong tilts (see
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
); without a reduction technique, it is challenging to confirm that
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting in this case.

In light of Theorem
4.6
, the first step, namely to confirm contravariant finiteness of

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
for truncated
\Lambda
, has now been trivialized. To generalize it, recall that, given any path algebra modulo relations,
\Lambda=KQ/I
, a vertex
e_{i}
of
Q
(systematically identified with a primitive idempotent of
\Lambda
) is called
precyclic
if there exists a path of length
\geq 0
which starts in
e_{i}
and ends on an oriented cycle; the attribute
postcyclic
is dual, and
e_{i}
is called
critical
if it is both pre- and postcyclic. Clearly, all vertices which give rise to simple modules of infinite projective dimension are among the precyclic ones; moreover
e_{i}\Lambda e_{j}=0
whenever
e_{i}
is precyclic, but
e_{j}
is not. Theorem 4.6 thus yields

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Lambda=KQ/I
be an arbitrary path algebra modulo relations,and let
e
be the sum of the primitive idempotents corresponding to the precyclic vertices of
Q
.
If
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
, e.g., if

\mathop{\rm l.findim}\nolimits e\Lambda e=0
, then
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
.

For truncated
\Lambda
, the final condition concerning the left finitistic dimension of
e\Lambda e
is clearly satisfied since all indecomposable projective left
e\Lambda e
-modules
e\Lambda e_{i}
for precyclic
e_{i}
have the same Loewy length; this is not necessarily true for the indecomposable injective left
e\Lambda e
-modules, but it is for those whose socles correspond to critical vertices; namely, if
e^{\prime}
is the sum of the critical vertices of
Q
, then the indecomposable injective left
e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime}
-modules also have coinciding Loewy lengths in the truncated case. The combination of these two conditions which norm the Loewy lengths of certain projective or injective modules is, in fact, all that is needed to guarantee that
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for iterated strong tilting.

Proposition 6.2
.

Again, let
\Lambda=KQ/I
be a path algebra modulo relations and
e
the sum of the primitive idempotents corresponding to the precyclic vertices of
Q
. Moreover, let
e^{\prime}
be the sum of those idempotents which correspond to the critical vertices.

Suppose that all indecomposable projective left
e\Lambda e
-modules have the same Loewy length, and that the analogous equality holds for the Loewy lengths of the indecomposable injective left
e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime}
-modules. Then
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting, thus giving rise to a sequence of related module categories

\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}\,\rightsquigarrow\,\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}\,\rightsquigarrow\,\widetilde{\widetilde{\Lambda}}\mbox{\rm-mod}\,\rightsquigarrow\cdots
.
Starting with the first strong tilt,

\mbox{\rm mod-}\widetilde{\Lambda}
,
the Morita equivalence classes of these categories repeat periodically with period
2
.

Proof.

Suppose that
e=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{m}
and
e^{\prime}=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{r}
for some
r\leq m
. Since

\mathop{\rm l.findim}\nolimits e\Lambda e=0
due to the first condition on Loewy lengths,
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
. Proposition
6.1
thus guarantees that
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
.

As we already pointed out above, the conditions
(i) and (ii) of Setting 4.1 are satisfied for the pair
(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod},e)
, whence, by Theorem
5.4
, we only
need to show that
e\Lambda e\text{-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting. Set
\Lambda^{\prime}=e\Lambda e
, and let

Q^{\prime}
, resp.
J^{\prime}
, be the quiver and Jacobson radical of
\Lambda^{\prime}
, respectively. We already know that the category

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda^{\prime}\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda^{\prime}\mbox{\rm-mod}
, due to the vanishing of the left finitistic
dimension of
\Lambda^{\prime}
. The latter in fact entails that the basic strong left
\Lambda^{\prime}
-module
T^{\prime}
is a copy of the left
regular module
{}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\Lambda^{\prime}
. Consequently, the strongly tilted algebra
\widetilde{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\widetilde{e\Lambda e}
, i.e.,
the opposite of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(T^{\prime})
, coincides with
\Lambda^{\prime}
. By Theorem
4.6
, it therefore suffices to check that
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda^{\prime})
is contravariantly finite in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda^{\prime}
. To confirm this, we observe that the pair
(\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda^{\prime},e^{\prime})
in turn satisfies the hypotheses 4.1: Indeed, the precyclic vertices of
(Q^{\prime})^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
are
precisely
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
, whence the simple right
\Lambda^{\prime}
-modules of infinite projective dimension are among
the quotients
e_{i}\Lambda^{\prime}/e_{i}J^{\prime}
for
i\leq r
, and
(e-e^{\prime})\Lambda^{\prime}e^{\prime}=0
. The right finitistic dimension of
e^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}e^{\prime}=e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime}
is in turn zero, because all indecomposable projective right
e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime}
-modules have the same Loewy
length; indeed, this follows by duality from the second of our two hypotheses. Consequently, another
application of Proposition
6.1
yields contravariant finiteness of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda^{\prime})
as required.

The concluding statements are part of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

We deduce Theorem D of
[
13
]
as a special case.

Corollary 6.3
.

Suppose that
\Lambda=KQ/I
is a truncated path algebra.
Then
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting.

The proof of the following generalization of Proposition
6.2
is immediate from that of the latter.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\Lambda=KQ/I
, and let
e
and
e^{\prime}
be as in the statement of
6.2
.
If
\,\mathop{\rm l.findim}\nolimits e\Lambda e=0

and
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime})
is contravariantly finite in
\mbox{\rm mod-}e^{\prime}\Lambda e^{\prime}
, then
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting.

Recall that an Artin algebra
\Lambda
is said to be
left serial
if all indecomposable projective left
\Lambda
-modules are uniserial. The algebras which are left and right serial are also called
Nakayama algebras
. In
[
6
]
it was shown that, for any split left serial algebra
\Lambda
, the category
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
. The conclusion actually carries over to the category
\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda
of right
\Lambda
-modules. Namely:

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a path algebra modulo relations. If
\Lambda
is left serial, then

\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
and
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda)
is contravariantly finite in
\mbox{\rm mod-}\Lambda
.

Proof.

The assertion for left modules was proved in
[
6
]
. We only address right
\Lambda
-modules.

By hypothesis,
\Lambda\cong KQ/I
is left serial. In particular, this means that
Q
is free of double arrows. Without loss of generality, we assume that
Q
is a connected quiver; we may further assume that it is not a tree, since otherwise
\Lambda
has finite global dimension, which renders the contravariant finiteness claim trivial. By left seriality,
Q
then contains a single oriented cycle such that all off-cycle vertices are pre- but not postcyclic. Consequently, any vertex of
Q^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
either belongs to said cycle or else is post- but not precyclic. On letting
e
be the sum of the precyclic vertices of
Q^{\mathop{\rm op}\nolimits}
, i.e., the vertices located on the oriented cycle in the present situation, we thus obtain a Nakayama algebra
e\Lambda e
. Hence the
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
-categories in both
e\Lambda e\mbox{\rm-mod}
and
\mbox{\rm mod-}e\Lambda e
are contravariantly finite in the corresponding ambient module categories by
[
6
, Theorem 5.2]
. (The latter may alternatively be deduced from the fact that Nakayama algebras have finite representation type.) On combining this with Proposition
6.1
, we obtain the claim.
∎

6.2.
Applications to Morita contexts

In this subsection we shall see that Morita contexts provide a tool to construct examples of
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
-contravariant finiteness and iteration of strong tilting. Recall that a
Morita context
(over a ground commutative ring
K
) consists of a sextuple
(A,B,M,N,\varphi,\psi)
, where
A
and
B
are
K
-algebras,
M
and
N
are an
A
-
B
- and a
B
-
A
-bimodule, which we always assume with the same action of
K
on the left and on the right, and
\varphi:M\otimes_{B}N\longrightarrow A
and
\psi:N\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow B
are morphisms of
A
-
A
- and
B
-
B
-bimodules, respectively, satisfying certain compatibiliity conditions (see
[
19
]
) which are exactly the ones that make
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}A&M\\
N&B\end{pmatrix}
into a
K
-algebra with the obvious multiplication. Recall that
\tau_{A}=\text{Im}(\varphi)
and
\tau_{B}=\text{Im}(\psi)
are two-sided ideals of
A
and
B
, respectively, called the
trace ideals
of the Morita context.

The Morita contexts in which we are interested have some additional properties. We assume that
K
is artinian and
A
,
B
,
M
and
N
are finitely generated as
K
-modules. Such a Morita context will be called a
basic Morita context of Artin algebras
if
\Lambda
is basic or, equivalently, if
A
and
B
are basic,
\tau_{A}\subseteq J(A)
and
\tau_{B}\subseteq J(B)
, where
J(-)
denotes the Jacobson radical.

The following is the main result of the subsection.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(A,B,M,N,\varphi,\psi)
be a basic Morita context of Artin algebras, where
M
is projective as a left
A
-module. Suppose also that
\psi:N\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow B
is a monomorphism and the algebra
B/\tau_{B}
has finite global dimension.

Set
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}A&M\\
N&B\end{pmatrix}
. Then
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod})
is contravariantly finite in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
(resp.,
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting) if and only if this is true for
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A\mbox{\rm-mod})
(resp.,
A\mbox{\rm-mod}
).

In particular,
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting when
A
is a Gorenstein algebra or a truncated path algebra or when
\mathop{\rm l.findim}\nolimits A=0=\mathop{\rm r.findim}\nolimits A
.

Proof.

Take
e=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
. The goal is to check that the pair
(\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod},e)
satisfies the blanket hypotheses of Setting
4.1
. Bearing in mind that the algebra
e\Lambda e
is isomorphic to
A
, the result will then be a direct consequence of Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
5.6
. Note that
B
and
(1-e)\Lambda(1-e)
are also isomorphic algebras and hence the
e\Lambda e
-
(1-e)\Lambda(1-e)
-bimodule
e\Lambda(1-e)
is isomorphic to the
A
-
B
-bimodule
M
, with the obvious meaning. Hence
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{e\Lambda e}(e\Lambda(1-e))=0
and we need to check that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\frac{\Lambda(1-e)}{J(\Lambda))(1-e)})<\infty
.

We first prove that
\Lambda e\Lambda(1-e)=\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\
0&\tau_{B}\end{pmatrix}\cong\begin{pmatrix}M\\
\tau_{B}\end{pmatrix}
is a projective left
\Lambda
-module. Indeed the adjunction map
\epsilon_{\Lambda(1-e)}:\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda(1-e)\longrightarrow\Lambda(1-e)
gets identified, in the obvious way, with the map
\begin{pmatrix}A\\
N\end{pmatrix}\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}M\\
B\end{pmatrix}
, that can be expressed matricially as
\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0\\
0&\psi\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}A\otimes_{A}M\\
N\otimes_{A}M\end{pmatrix}\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}M\\
B\end{pmatrix}
. Here
\mu:A\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow M
is the canonical isomorphism given by multiplication and
\psi:N\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow B
is the map in the Morita context. It follows that
\epsilon_{\Lambda(1-e)}
is a monomorphism, and so

\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda(1-e)\cong\Lambda e\Lambda(1-e)=\text{Im}(\epsilon_{\Lambda(1-e)}).

But
\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda(1-e)\in\text{add}(\Lambda e\otimes_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda e)=\text{add}(\Lambda e)
since
e\Lambda(1-e)\in e\Lambda e\text{-proj}=\text{add}(_{e\Lambda e}e\Lambda e)
. Therefore
\Lambda e\Lambda(1-e)
is a projective left
\Lambda
-module.

Next we should notice that there is an algebra isomorphism
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda}\cong\frac{B}{\tau_{B}}
, so that these isomorphic algebras have finite global dimension. Moreover, we have an isomorphism
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda}\cong\frac{\Lambda(1-e)}{\Lambda e\Lambda(1-e)}
in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, which, by the previous paragraph, implies that
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda}
has projective dimension
\leq 1
as a left
\Lambda
-module. Note also that
\frac{\Lambda(1-e)}{J(\Lambda)(1-e)}
is canonically a left
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda}
-module.
Fix a minimal projective resolution
0\rightarrow Q_{t}\rightarrow...\rightarrow Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{0}\rightarrow\frac{\Lambda(1-e)}{J(\Lambda)(1-e)}\rightarrow 0
in
\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda}\text{-mod}
. This is also an exact sequence in
\Lambda\mbox{\rm-mod}
, and we have that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(Q_{k})\leq 1
since
Q_{k}\in\text{add}(\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda e\Lambda})
, for all
k=0,1,...,t
. It then follows that
\mathop{\rm pdim}\nolimits_{\Lambda}(\frac{\Lambda(1-e)}{J(\Lambda)(1-e)})<\infty
, as desired.

The final statement clearly follows from assertion 3 since in those examples
A\text{-mod}
allows for unlimited iteration of strong tilting.
∎

The last theorem and its proof have the following consequence for triangular matrix algebras.

Corollary 6.7

Let
K
be a commutative Artinian ring, let

B

K

\mathcal{P}^{\infty}(A\mbox{\rm-mod})

(1)

If

Proof.

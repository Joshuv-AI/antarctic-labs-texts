Introduction

All graphs considered here are simple, undirected and connected. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with vertex set
V(G)=\left\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\right\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The adjacency matrix of
G
is defined as
A(G)=(a_{ij})_{n\times n}
, where
a_{ij}=1
if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise. Let
D(G)
be the diagonal matrix of the degrees of
G
. In
[
14
]
, Nikiforov defined the
A_{\alpha}
-matrix of
G
as

A_{\alpha}(G)=\alpha D(G)+(1-\alpha)A(G),

where
\alpha\in[0,1]
is an arbitrary real number. It is clear that
A_{0}(G)=A(G)
is the adjacency matrix of
G
, and
2A_{\frac{1}{2}}(G)=D(G)+A(G)=Q(G)
is the signless Laplacian matrix of
G
. The eigenvalues of
A_{\alpha}(G)
are called the
A_{\alpha}
-eigenvalues of
G
, and the largest of them, denoted by
\rho_{\alpha}(G)
, is called the
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of
G
. By Perron-Frobenius Theorem, the
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius
\rho_{\alpha}(G)
of a connected graph
G
is simple and has an unit positive eigenvector. For some latest interesting results of the
A_{\alpha}
-matrix, the reader is invited to read the following recent papers
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
and references therein.

A matching
M
of
G
is a set of disjoint edges of
G
, and the maximum size of a matching in
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
in here, is called the matching number of
G
. If each vertex of
G
is incident with an edge in
M
, we call
M
perfect. Obviously, a graph containing a perfect matching has an even number of vertices and
\mu(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
. Studies on connections between eigenvalues and the matching number of a graph emerged in the 1990s, and there have been abundant publications up to now, see
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
]
. Recently, O
[
17
]
provided a lower bound for the adjacency spectral radius which guarantees the existence of a perfect matching in a connected graph
G
. Later, Liu et al.
[
13
]
and Zhao et al.
[
19
]
considered this problem with respect to the signless Laplacian spectral radius and
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius, respectively.

The matching number of a graph is closely related to its (edge-) connectivity. The (edge-) connectivity of
G
is the minimum number of vertices (edges) of which the deletion induces a non-connected graph or a single vertex. If the (edge-) connectivity of
G
is at least
t

, we say that

is
t
-connected (
t
-edge-connected). In 2010, Cioabǎ and O
[
4

gave a sharp upper bound for the third largest eigenvalue of a

-edge-connected regular graph

to guarantee that

has a perfect matching, generalizing their results in
[
5
]
. Very Recently, Zhang
[
20
]
has studied the extremal graphs with maximum spectral radius among all
t
-connected graphs on
n
vertices with matching number at most
\frac{n-k}{2}
, which generalized the results in
[
17
]
.

Along this line, we intend to investigate the maximum
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius of
t
-connected graphs with bounded matching number. For a vertex subset

, the subgraphs induced by the vertex set
V(G)-S
and

are denoted by
G-S
and

, respectively. The complement of

is denoted by

. For any two graphs

and
G_{2}
, the joint of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted by

2
Preliminaries

First, we present some essential lemmas in matrix theory. For a matrix
B
, we denote the largest eigenvalue of
B
by
\lambda(B)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Rayleigh-Ritz theorem)
.

If
B
is an
n\times n
real symmetric matrix, then
\lambda(B)=\max\limits_{\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\textbf{x}^{t}B\textbf{x}}{\textbf{x}^{t}\textbf{x}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
H
be a connected subgraph of
G
. For
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(H)\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G)
with equality holding if and only if
H\cong G
.

Proof.

Let
n_{0}=|V(H)|
and let
\boldsymbol{x}_{H}=(x^{H}_{i})^{t}_{i=1,\dots,n_{0}}
be an unit positive eigenvector of
H
corresponding to
\rho_{\alpha}(H)
. Then we construct the square matrix

B=\begin{bmatrix}A_{\alpha}(H)&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0}&\boldsymbol{0}\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n},

and the vector
\boldsymbol{x}=(x^{H}_{1},\dots,x^{H}_{n_{0}},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-n_{0}})^{t}
. Obviously,
\lambda(B)=\lambda(A_{\alpha}(H))=\rho_{\alpha}(H)
, and
\boldsymbol{x}
is the eigenvector of
B
corresponding to
\lambda(B)
. It can be checked that

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}(G)-\rho_{\alpha}(H)

\displaystyle=\lambda(A_{\alpha}(G))-\lambda(B)

\displaystyle\geq\boldsymbol{x}^{t}\left(A_{\alpha}(G)-B\right)\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\geq 0

with equality holding if and only if
H\cong G
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we introduce the concepts of equitable matrices and equitable partitions.

Definition 2.3
.

[
2
]

Let
B
be a symmetric real matrix of order
n
whose rows and columns are indexed by
P=\left\{1,2,\cdots,n\right\}
, where
\left\{P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{r}\right\}
is a partition of
P
with
n_{i}=|P_{i}|
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r}
. Let
B
be a matrix with the partition
\left\{P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{r}\right\}
, i.e.

M=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1,1}&B_{1,2}&\cdots&B_{1,r}\\
B_{2,1}&B_{2,2}&\cdots&B_{2,r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
B_{r,1}&B_{r,2}&\cdots&B_{r,r}\end{pmatrix}_{n\times n},

where the blocks
B_{i,j}
denotes the submatrix of
B
formed by rows in
P_{i}
and the
P_{j}
columns. Let
m_{i,j}
denote the average row sum of
B_{i,j}
. Then the matrix
M=(m_{i,j})
is called the quotient matrix of
B
under the given partition. Particularly, if the row sum of each submatrix
B_{i,j}
is constant, then the partition is called equitable.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
18
]

Let
M
be an equitable quotient matrix of
B
as defined in Definition
2.3
. If
B
is a nonnegative matrix, then
\lambda(M)=\lambda(B)
.

Theorem 2.5

(Berge-Tutte Formula)
.

Let
G
be a graph with matching number
\mu(G)
. Then

\mu(G)=\frac{n-\max_{S\subseteq V(G)}(o(G-S)-|S|)}{2},

where
S
is a subset of
V(G)
, and
o(G-S)
is the number of odd components of
G-S
.

3
Main results

From Fact 1 in
[
20
]
, one see that if
G
is an
n
-vertex connected graph with connectivity
t
and matching number
\mu(G)<\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, then
t\leq\mu(G)
. Moreover, for a subset
S
of
V(G)
, we have
t\leq|S|\leq\mu(G)
. First, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
,
t
,
s
,
k
be four positive integers, where
2\leq k\leq n-2
,
1\leq t\leq s\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, and
n\equiv k
(mod
2
). For a
t
-connected graph
G
on
n
vertices with matching number
\mu(G)\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}(K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}}))
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Equality holds if and only if
G\cong K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha\in[0,1)
and let
G
be a
t
-connected graph with the maximum
A_{\alpha}
-spectral radius and matching number
\mu(G)\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
.
From Theorem
2.5
, we see that
\max_{S\subseteq V(G)}(o(G-S)-|S|)=n-2\mu(G)
. Suppose that
S
is the subset of
V(G)
with the maximum number of vertices, such that
o(G-S)-|S|=n-2\mu(G)
. It can be checked that all components of
G-S
are odd components (otherwise, we can randomly remove one vertex from each even component of
G-S
and add them to the set
S
until all components of
G-S
are odd components, and the equality
o(G-S)-|S|=n-2\mu(G)
always holds in the process).

For convenience, we denote
s=|S|
,
q=o(G-S)
. Note that
o(G-S)-|S|=n-2\mu(G)\geq k
, implying
q\geq s+k
. Let
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{q}
be the odd components of
G-S
with
n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{q}
vertices, respectively. Without loss of generality, we assume that
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\dots\geq n_{q}\geq 1
. Then we present the following three claims.

Claim 1.
Let
G^{\prime}\cong K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n_{1}}\cup K_{n_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{q}}\right)
. Then
\mu(G^{\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
and
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime}\right)
with equality holding if and only if
G\cong G^{\prime}
.

Proof of Claim 1.

Since
o(G^{\prime}-S)=o(G-S)\geq s+k
and
n-2\mu(G^{\prime})\geq o(G^{\prime}-S)-|S|
, we get
\mu(G^{\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
, it’s easy to check that
\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}(G^{\prime})
and equality holds if and only if
G\cong G^{\prime}
. This proves Claim 1.
∎

Claim 2.
Let
G^{\prime\prime}\cong K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{s+k}}\right)
, where
n^{\prime}_{1}=n_{1}+\sum_{i=s+k+1}^{q}n_{i}
, and
n^{\prime}_{i}=n_{i}
for
i=2,\dots,s+k
. Then
\mu(G^{\prime\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, and
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime}\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)
with equality holding if and only if
G^{\prime}\cong G^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof of Claim 2.

Since
n_{i}
(
i=1,2,\dots,n_{q}
) are all odd and
s+\sum_{i=1}^{q}n_{i}=n
, we see that
q+s\equiv n\equiv k
(mod
2
),
q-s-k
is even, and
n^{\prime}_{1}=n_{1}+\sum_{i=s+k+1}^{q}n_{i}
is odd. Note that
o(G^{\prime\prime}-S)=o(G-S)-(q-s-k)=s+k
and
n-2\mu(G^{\prime\prime})\geq o(G^{\prime\prime}-S)-|S|
, we obtain
\mu(G^{\prime\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime}\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)
with equality holding if and only if
G^{\prime}\cong G^{\prime\prime}
. This proves Claim 2.
∎

Claim 3.
Let
G^{\prime\prime\prime}\cong K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}}\right)
. Then
\mu(G^{\prime\prime\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
and
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)
with equality holding if and only if
G^{\prime\prime}\cong G^{\prime\prime\prime}
.

Proof of Claim 3.

Note that
o(G^{\prime\prime\prime}-S)=o(G^{\prime\prime}-S)=s+k
and
n-2\mu(G^{\prime\prime\prime})\geq o(G^{\prime\prime\prime}-S)-|S|
. It’s easy to see that
\mu(G^{\prime\prime\prime})\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. Now, we prove
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)
for
[0,1)
. It suffices to show that
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{s+k}}\right)\right)<\rho_{\alpha}(K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n^{\prime}_{1}+2}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{j}-2}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{s+k}}\right)
whenever
n_{j}\geq 3
for
j=2,\dots,s+k
. Without loss of generality, we take
j=s+k
. By the proof of Claim 1 of Theorem 3 in
[
19
]
, we see that
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{s+k}}\right)\right)<\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{s}\vee\left(K_{n^{\prime}_{1}+2}\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{2}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n^{\prime}_{s+k-2}}\right)\right)
. This inequality is strict. Hence,
\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime}\right)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(G^{\prime\prime\prime}\right)
with equality holding if and only if
G^{\prime\prime}\cong G^{\prime\prime\prime}
. This proves Claim 3.
∎

Given the above, we conclude that for a
t
-connected graph
G
on
n
vertices with matching number
\mu(G)\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, it follows that
\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})\right)
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
, with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
,
t
,
k
be three positive integers, where
2\leq k\leq n-2
,
1\leq t\leq\frac{n-k-4}{2}
, and
n\equiv k
(mod
2
). For
t+1\leq s\leq\frac{n-k-2}{2}
and
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
, it follows that
\rho_{\alpha}(K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}}))<\max\left\{\rho_{\alpha}(K_{t}\vee(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}})),\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)\right\}
.

(Note that
K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})\cong K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}
for

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
t\leq s\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. For convenience, we denote
\rho_{\alpha}(s)=\rho_{\alpha}(K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}}))
. Then
\rho_{\alpha}(t)=\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}})\right)

\rho_{\alpha}(\frac{n-k}{2})=\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)
. The equitable quotient matrix of
A_{\alpha}\left(K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})\right)
with partition
\left\{V(K_{s}),V(K_{n+1-2s-k}),\right.\\
\left.V(\overline{K_{s+k-1}})\right\}
has the form

\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(n-1)+(1-\alpha)(s-1)&(1-\alpha)(n-2s-k+1)&(1-\alpha)(s+k-1)\\
(1-\alpha)s&\alpha(n-s-k)+(1-\alpha)(n-2s-k)&0\\
(1-\alpha)s&0&\alpha s\end{bmatrix}.

By a simple calculation, we obtain the characteristic polynomial is

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)

\displaystyle=x^{3}+[k-n+s+1-\alpha(n+s)]x^{2}+[ns\alpha^{2}+(n^{2}-kn-2s)\alpha+k-n+2s-ks-s^{2}]x

\displaystyle+(-k^{2}s+2kns-4ks^{2}+ks-n^{2}s+2ns^{2}-ns-3s^{3}+3s^{2})\alpha^{2}+(3ns-3ks+7ks^{2}

\displaystyle+2k^{2}s-2ns^{2}-6s^{2}+5s^{3}-2kns)\alpha+ks-ns-3ks^{2}-k^{2}s+ns^{2}+2s^{2}-2s^{3}+kns.

Referring to Lemma
2.4
, we see that
f_{1}(\rho_{\alpha}(s),n,s,k,\alpha)=0
. Note that
K_{n-s-k+1}
is a subgraph of
K_{s}\vee(K_{n+1-2s-k}\cup\overline{K_{s+k-1}})
, then by Lemma
2.2
, we have
\rho_{\alpha}(s)>\rho_{\alpha}(K_{n-s-k+1})=n-s-k
.

The equitable quotient matrix of
A_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)
with partition
\left\{V\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}},V(K_{\frac{n+k}{2}})\right)\right\}
equals

\begin{bmatrix}\alpha(n-1)+(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{n+k}{2}-1\right)&(1-\alpha)\frac{n+k}{2}\\

and the corresponding characteristic polynomial is

f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)=x^{2}-\left(\frac{n-k}{2}-1+\alpha n\right)x+\left(-\frac{k^{2}}{4}-\frac{kn}{2}+\frac{k}{2}+\frac{3n^{2}}{4}-\frac{n}{2}\right)\alpha+\frac{k^{2}-n^{2}}{4}.

From Lemma
2.4
, we have
f_{2}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right),n,k,\alpha\right)=0

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-k-2+2\alpha n}{4}+\frac{\sqrt{(n-k-2+2\alpha n)^{2}+4(n^{2}-k^{2})-4\alpha(3n+k-2)(n-k)}}{4}

\displaystyle>\frac{n-k-2}{2}.

By a calculation, we obtain

\begin{split}&f_{1}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)-\left(x-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)s}{2}\right)f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)\\
&=\frac{n-2s-k}{8}\cdot([4k+4s-4-2\alpha(n+k-2)]x-(12\alpha-8)(1-\alpha)s^{2}\\

&-(n-k)[(1-\alpha)(n+k)-2\alpha(n-1)]).\end{split}

Let

\begin{split}h(x,n,s,k,\alpha)&=[4k+4s-4-2\alpha(n+k-2)]x-(12\alpha-8)(1-\alpha)s^{2}-[(2n-10k+12)\alpha^{2}\\

(mod

\displaystyle 4k+4s-4-2\alpha(n+k-2)>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow n<\left(\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right)k+\frac{2}{\alpha}s-2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}-1\right).

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)\geq n-k

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{n-k-2+2\alpha n}{4}+\frac{\sqrt{(n-k-2+2\alpha n)^{2}+4(n^{2}-k^{2})-4\alpha(3n+k-2)(n-k)}}{4}\geq n-k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow n\leq 3k-4+\alpha(n-k+2)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow n\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}(3k-4)-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}(k-2).

\displaystyle=[4k+4s-4-2\alpha(n+k-2)](n-k)-(12\alpha-8)(1-\alpha)s^{2}-[(2n-10k+12)\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle+(18k+2n-24)\alpha-8k+8]s-(n-k)[(1-\alpha)(n+k)-2\alpha(n-1)]

\displaystyle=4(3\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)s^{2}+[-2(n-5k+6)\alpha^{2}-2(n+9k-12)\alpha+4n+4k-8]s

\displaystyle+(n-k)[3k-4-n+\alpha(n-k+2)].

\varphi_{2}(\alpha)=-2(n-5k+6)\alpha^{2}-2(n+9k-12)\alpha+4n+4k-8

\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
n\leq 3k-4
, we have
\varphi_{1}(\alpha)>0
and
-2(n-5k+6)>0
. Notice that

one can see

. Thus, for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
n\leq 3k-4
,

, and

, we get

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)-\left(x-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)s}{2}\right)f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)\\
&=\frac{n-2s-k}{8}h(x,n,s,k,\alpha)\\
&\geq\frac{n-2s-k}{8}\left\{\varphi_{1}(\alpha)s^{2}+\varphi_{2}(\alpha)s+(n-k)[3k-4-n+\alpha(n-k+2)]\right\}\\
&>0,\end{split}

which implies that

Case 2.

Case 2.1.

(or

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)

\displaystyle=x^{3}+[k-n+s+1-\alpha(n+s)]x^{2}+[ns\alpha^{2}+(n^{2}-kn-2s)\alpha+k-n+2s-ks-s^{2}]x

\displaystyle+(-k^{2}s+2kns-4ks^{2}+ks-n^{2}s+2ns^{2}-ns-3s^{3}+3s^{2})\alpha^{2}+(3ns-3ks+7ks^{2}

\displaystyle+2k^{2}s-2ns^{2}-6s^{2}+5s^{3}-2kns)\alpha+ks-ns-3ks^{2}-k^{2}s+ns^{2}+2s^{2}-2s^{3}+kns,

. We calculate that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)&=3x^{2}+2[k-n+s+1-(n+s)\alpha]x-s^{2}+(n\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-k+2)s\\
&+k-n+\alpha n^{2}-\alpha kn.\end{split}

Observe the following inequality

\displaystyle-\frac{k-n+s+1-(n+s)\alpha}{3}<n-s-k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(2-\alpha)n-(2+\alpha)s-2k+1>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow n>\frac{(2+\alpha)}{2-\alpha}s+\frac{2}{2-\alpha}k-\frac{1}{2-\alpha}.

Since

,

,

, and

) holds. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)

\displaystyle>\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x}(n-s-k,n,s,k,\alpha)

\displaystyle=ns\alpha^{2}+[(k-n)n+2s(s+k-1)]\alpha

,

, and

Notice that

\displaystyle-\frac{4-7s-6k}{4}<4s+3k-3\Leftrightarrow 9s+6k>8.

For

, and

for

and

in the domain

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial s}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)&=(1-\alpha)x^{2}+(n\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-k-2s+2)x-3(3\alpha-2)(\alpha-1)s^{2}\\

It is routine to show

\displaystyle-\frac{n\alpha^{2}-2\alpha-k-2s+2}{2(1-\alpha)}\leq n-s-k

,

, and

for

and

. Then we consider the following inequality

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-k-n)(n-k)+2s(2s+k-n)+10s(s+k-1)}{2[(n-k-7s)(s+k-1)+2s(n-s-\frac{1}{2})]}>1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(4s+k-n)(n-s-k)-s(n-k-2)-(n-k)<0.

,

. Let

\tau_{5}(n,k,\alpha)=[-3(n+k)+4]\alpha+\left[6(n+k)-(n-k)-16\right]-2g_{2}(n,k,\alpha)

and

\tau_{6}(n,k,\alpha)=g_{2}(n,k,\alpha)-\left[2(n+k)-(n-k)-6\right].

It’s obvious that
\psi_{1}(n,k,\alpha)=\alpha\tau_{5}(n,k,\alpha)+\tau_{6}(n,k,\alpha)
. Then we calculate that

\begin{split}\tau_{5}(n,2,\alpha)&=[(5n-2)-(3n+2)\alpha]-2\sqrt{(n+2\alpha n-4)^{2}+4n^{2}-12\alpha(n-2)n-16}\\
&=\frac{(-7n^{2}+12n+4)\alpha^{2}+(2n^{2}-40n+8)\alpha+5n^{2}+12n+4}{[(5n-2)-(3n+2)\alpha]+2\sqrt{(n+2\alpha n-4)^{2}+4n^{2}-12\alpha(n-2)n-16}}.\end{split}

Denote
\tilde{\tau}_{5}(n,2,\alpha)=(-7n^{2}+12n+4)\alpha^{2}+(2n^{2}-40n+8)\alpha+5n^{2}+12n+4
.
Since
-7n^{2}+12n+4\leq-60<0
for
n\geq 4
, we check that
\tilde{\tau}_{5}(n,2,0)=5n^{2}+12n+4\geq 132>0
for
n\geq 4
and
\tilde{\tau}_{5}(n,2,\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{4}(17n^{2}-20n+36)\geq 57>0
for
n\geq 4
. Hence,
\tau_{5}(n,2,\alpha)=\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{5}(n,2,\alpha)}{[(5n-2)-(3n+2)\alpha]+2\sqrt{(n+2\alpha n-4)^{2}+4n^{2}-12\alpha(n-2)n-16}}>0
for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
k=2
, and
n\geq 4
. Then we consider the function

\begin{split}\tau_{6}(n,2,\alpha)&=\sqrt{(n+2\alpha n-4)^{2}+4n^{2}-12\alpha n(n-2)-16}-n\\
&=\frac{4(1-\alpha)n[(1-\alpha)n-2]}{\sqrt{(n+2\alpha n-4)^{2}+4n^{2}-12\alpha n(n-2)-16}+n}.\end{split}

It’s easy to see that
4(1-\alpha)n[(1-\alpha)n-2]\geq 0
for any
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
n\geq 4
. Therefore, we have
\tau_{6}(n,2,\alpha)\geq 0
. By the above discussion, we deduce that

\psi_{1}(n,2,\alpha)=\alpha\tau_{5}(n,2,\alpha)+\tau_{6}(n,2,\alpha)>0,

for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
k=2
, and
n\geq 4
.

As can be seen,
\psi_{1}(n,k,\alpha)>0
for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
k\geq 2
, and
n\geq 3k-2
. Consequently,
for any
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\frac{n+4-3k}{4}\leq s\leq\frac{n-k-2}{2}
,
k\geq 2
, and
x\geq\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)
, we have

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}(x,n,s,k,\alpha)-\left(x-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)s}{2}\right)f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)\\
&\geq\frac{n-2s-k}{8}h\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right),n,s,k,\alpha\right)\\
&\geq\frac{n-2s-k}{8}\left[\frac{n+k}{4}\psi_{1}(n,k,\alpha)+\alpha\psi_{2}(n,k,\alpha)\right]\\
&>0,\end{split}

which implies that
\rho_{\alpha}\left(s\right)<\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)
.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
n
,
t
,
k
be three positive integers, where
2\leq k\leq n-2
,
1\leq t\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
and
n\equiv k
(mod
2
), and let
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
be an arbitrary real number. The functions
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)
and
\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)
are defined as in Definition
3.3
. For a
t
-connected graph
G
on
n
vertices with matching number
\mu(G)\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, we obtain the following three conclusions.

(i)

If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)<0
, then

\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\right)

with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)
.

(ii)

If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)=0
and
\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)\geq 0
, then

\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\right)=\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)

with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)
or
G\cong K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}
.

(iii)

If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)>0
and
\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)\geq 0
, then

\rho_{\alpha}(G)\leq\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)

with equality holding if and only if
G\cong K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}
.

Proof.

Note that
K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\cong K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}
if
t=\frac{n-k}{2}
, then we consider the case
t\leq\frac{n-k-2}{2}
, i.e.,
n\geq 2t+k+2
. Similarly, it follows that

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}(x,n,t,k,\alpha)-\left(x-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)t}{2}\right)f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)\\
&=\frac{n-2t-k}{8}\cdot([4k+4t-4-2\alpha(n+k-2)]x-(12\alpha-8)(1-\alpha)t^{2}\\
&-[(2n-10k+12)\alpha^{2}+(18k+2n-24)\alpha-8k+8]t\\
&-(n-k)[(1-\alpha)(n+k)-2\alpha(n-1)])\\
&=\frac{n-2t-k}{8}h(x,n,t,k,\alpha).\end{split}

Plugging
\rho_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)
into
h(x,n,t,k,\alpha)
yields

\begin{split}&~~~~h\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)\\
&=\left(k+t-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}(n+k)+\alpha\right)\sqrt{(n-k-2+2\alpha n)^{2}+4(n^{2}-k^{2})-4\alpha(3n+k-2)(n-k)}\\
&+\left[12t^{2}+(10k-2n-12)t+2n-kn-n^{2}\right]\alpha^{2}+\left[(24-18k)t-20t^{2}-\frac{k^{2}}{2}+2k+\frac{5}{2}n^{2}\right.\\
&\left.-2n-2\right]\alpha+k^{2}-n^{2}+(k+t-1)(n+8t-k-2)\\
&\triangleq\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha).\end{split}

By Cauchy’s Interlace Theorem
[
8
]
, one can see that the second largest root of
f_{1}(x,n,t,k,\alpha)
is no bigger than
n-(2-\alpha)t-k
. By a calculation, we have

\begin{split}&\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)-[n-(2-\alpha)t-k]\geq 0\\
&\Leftrightarrow\sqrt{(n-k-2+2\alpha n)^{2}+4(n^{2}-k^{2})-4\alpha(3n+k-2)(n-k)}\geq 4[n-(2-\alpha)t-k]-(n-k-2+2\alpha n)\\
&\Leftrightarrow\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)\geq 0.\end{split}

(i) If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)<0
, we have

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)-\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)t}{2}\right)\\
&\times f_{2}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,k,\alpha\right)\\
&=\frac{n-2t-k}{8}h\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)\\
&<0,\end{split}

implying
f_{1}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)<0
. Hence,
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\right)>\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)
.

(ii) If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)=0
and
\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)\geq 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)-\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)t}{2}\right)\\
&\times f_{2}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,k,\alpha\right)\\
&=\frac{n-2t-k}{8}h\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)\\
&=0,\end{split}

which implies that
f_{1}(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha)=0
and
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)\geq n-(2-\alpha)t-k
. Thus, we have
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\right)=\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)
.

(iii) If
\Delta_{1}(n,t,k,\alpha)>0
and
\Delta_{2}(n,t,k,\alpha)\geq 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}&~~~~f_{1}(x,n,t,k,\alpha)-\left(x-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)t}{2}\right)f_{2}(x,n,k,\alpha)\\
&\geq f_{1}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)-\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)-\frac{n-k-2(1-\alpha)t}{2}\right)\\
&\times f_{2}\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,k,\alpha\right)\\
&=\frac{n-2t-k}{8}h\left(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha\right)\\
&>0,\end{split}

which implies that
f_{1}(\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right),n,t,k,\alpha)>0
and
\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)\geq n-(2-\alpha)t-k
. We deduce that

\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{t}\vee\left(K_{n+1-2t-k}\cup\overline{K_{t+k-1}}\right)\right)<\rho_{\alpha}\left(K_{\frac{n-k}{2}}\vee\overline{K_{\frac{n+k}{2}}}\right)
.

This completes the proof.
∎

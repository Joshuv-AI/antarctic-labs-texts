Thurston obstructions and tropical geometry
Rohini Ramadas

Abstract
. We describe an application of tropical moduli spaces to complex dynamics. A post-critically finite branched covering φ \varphi of S 2 S^{2} induces a pullback map on the Teichmüller space of complex structures of S 2 S^{2} ; this descends to an algebraic correspondence on the moduli space of point-configurations of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . We make a case for studying the action of the tropical moduli space correspondence by making explicit the connections between objects that have come up in one guise in tropical geometry and in another guise in complex dynamics. For example, a Thurston obstruction for φ \varphi corresponds to a ray that is fixed by the tropical moduli space correspondence, and scaled by a factor ≥ 1 \geq 1 . This article is intended to be accessible to algebraic and tropical geometers as well as to complex dynamicists.

Introduction

Topologically speaking, every rational function
f:\CP\to\CP

is an identification

under which
\varphi
is isotopic, relative to
\mathbf{P}
, to a PCF rational function on
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Furthermore,
\TP_{\varphi}
is distance-nonincreasing with respect to the Teichmüller metric on
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})

2.
The moduli space of point-configurations

We introduce the moduli space of point-configurations, its compactification, and its tropicalization. Throughout this article, we will assume that
\mathbf{P}
is a finite set of cardinality at least
3
. The moduli
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
is a smooth quasiprojective
(\left\lvert\mathbf{P}\right\rvert-3)
-dimensional variety parameterizing injective maps
\iota:\mathbf{P}\to\CP
, up to post-composition by automorphisms of
\CP
. If
\mathbf{P}_{1}\subset\mathbf{P}_{2}\subset S^{2}
, there is a forgetful map
\mu:\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{2}}\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{1}}
.

2.1.
Stable curves and the Deligne-Mumford compactification.

A
prestable

\mathbf{P}
-marked genus-
0
curve is a pair
(C,\iota)
, where
C
is an at worst nodal curve of arithmetic genus
0
(i.e. a tree of
\CP
s attached at simple nodes), and
\iota
is an injective map from
\mathbf{P}
into the smooth locus of
C
. In this article, all prestable curves are assumed to be genus-
0
. A prestable curve is called stable if each irreducible component has at least
3
points that are either nodes or marked. The dual graph of a nodal curve is the graph which has a vertex for each irreducible component of the curve, and an edge between two vertices if the corresponding irreducible components of the curve meet at a node. Given a prestable
\mathbf{P}
-marked curve, its marked dual tree
\tree
is the pair
(\underline{\tree},\widehat{\iota})
, where
\underline{\tree}
is the dual graph of
C
(which is necessarily a tree), and
\widehat{\iota}
is the map from
\mathbf{P}
to the set of vertices of
\underline{\tree}
sending
p\in\mathbf{P}
to the vertex of
\underline{\tree}
corresponding to the irreducible component of
C
that is marked by
p
. We define the valence of a vertex
v
of
\tree
to be

\val(v):=(\mbox{number of edges adjacent to $v$})+\left\lvert\widehat{\iota}^{-1}(v)\right\rvert.

A
\mathbf{P}
-marked tree
\tree
is called stable if every vertex has valence at least
3
. A prestable
\mathbf{P}
-marked curve is stable if and only if its marked dual tree is stable. The Deligne-Mumford compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
parameterizes, up to isomorphism, stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked curves
(C,\iota)
.

Given a stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked tree
\tree
, the locus in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
of curves with marked dual tree isomorphic to
\tree
is a locally closed set
\stratum_{\tree}^{\circ}
whose closure is an irreducible smooth subvariety
\stratum_{\tree}
called a
boundary stratum
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
. The codimension of
\stratum_{\tree}
is equal to the number of edges of
\tree
. The containment
\stratum_{\tree_{1}}\subset\stratum_{\tree_{2}}
holds if and only if
\tree_{2}
can be obtained from
\tree_{1}
by contracting a subset of edges of
\tree_{1}
.

A prestable
\mathbf{P}
-marked curve
(C,\iota)
admits a unique
stabilization
, which is a nodal curve
C^{\prime}
together with a surjective degree-
1
map
\stab:C\to C^{\prime}
with the property that
(C^{\prime},\stab\circ\iota)
is a stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked curve. The map
\stab
may contract some irreducible components of
C
to points. Given
\mathbf{P}_{1}\subset\mathbf{P}_{2}
, the forgetful map
\mu
extends to a map
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{2}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{1}}
, sending
(C,\iota:\mathbf{P}_{2}\hookrightarrow C)
to the stabilization of
(C,\iota|_{\mathbf{P}_{1}})
. The
\mathbf{P}_{1}
-marked dual tree of
\mu((C,\iota))
is determined by the
\mathbf{P}_{2}
-marked dual tree of
(C,\iota)
— we will denote by
\mu_{*}
the induced map from the set of (isomorphism classes of)
\mathbf{P}_{2}
-marked stable trees to the set of (isomorphism classes of)
\mathbf{P}_{1}
-marked stable trees.

2.2.
Tropical curves and the tropical moduli space

The topological space of a
\mathbf{P}
-marked tree
\tree
is the topological space obtained from the usual underlying topological space of
\underline{\tree}
by attaching, for every
p\in\mathbf{P}
, a ray
\cong[0,+\infty)
to the vertex
\widehat{\iota}(p)
. The ray corresponding to
p\in\mathbf{P}
is often referred to as the
leg
of
p
. A
stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked genus-
0
tropical curve
is a metrization of the topological space of a stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked tree
\tree
, obtained by assigning a positive edge length to every edge of
\tree
, and assigning the legs infinite length. All tropical curves in this article are genus-
0
.

The space
\cone^{\circ}(\tree)
of
\mathbf{P}
-marked tropical curves with underlying marked tree
\tree
is a cone canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\edges(\tree)}
. Here, the coordinates in terms of the natural basis are exactly the edge-lengths. We denote by
\cone(\tree)
the space
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\edges(\tree)}
of possibly degenerate metrizations of
\tree
. If
\tree_{2}
is obtained from
\tree_{1}
by contracting a subset
E
of edges of
\tree_{1}
, then a degenerate metrization of
\tree_{1}
that assigns length
0
to every
e\in E
also yields a metrization of
\tree_{2}
. This realizes the cone
\cone(\tree_{2})
as a face of
\cone(\tree_{1})
. Note that the direction of inclusion of cones is the reverse of the direction of inclusion of boundary strata outlined in Section
2.1
. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
is the space of all stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked tropical curves; it is a cone complex obtained by gluing together the cones
\cone(\tree)
, where
\tree
ranges over all stable
\mathbf{P}
-marked trees, along the inclusions
\cone(\tree_{2})\hookrightarrow\cone(\tree_{1})
mentioned above. As we explain in Section
3.4
,
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
can be identified with the cone over the boundary complex of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{P}_{1}\subset\mathbf{P}_{2}
and that
\Sigma
is a
\mathbf{P}_{2}
-marked tropical curve. Set
\mu^{\trop}(\Sigma)
to be the convex hull, within
\Sigma
, of the legs
corresponding to
p\in\mathbf{P}_{1}
; this is a
\mathbf{P}_{1}
-marked tropical curve. The map
\mu^{\trop}:\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{2}}^{\trop}\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{1}}^{\trop}
is piecewise linear, and is the tropicalization of the forgetful map
\mu
.

3.
Teichmüller space and its tropicalization

We introduce some objects from surface topology and propose a candidate for the role of tropical Teichmüller space. Given
\mathbf{P}\subset S^{2}
, the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
is a non-algebraic complex manifold of dimension
\left\lvert\mathbf{P}\right\rvert-3
, parameterizing complex structures on
(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
. More precisely, a point of
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
is an equivalence class of homeomorphisms
\psi:S^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are equivalent if there is a Möbius transformation
\alpha
such that
\psi_{1}
and
\alpha\circ\psi_{2}
are isotopic relative to
\mathbf{P}
.

The pure mapping class group
\Mod(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
is the group of self-homeomorphisms of
S^{2}
that fix every element of
\mathbf{P}
, up to isotopy relative to
\mathbf{P}
.
\Mod(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
acts freely and properly on
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
by precomposition
[
FM11
]
. There is a forgetful map
\rho:\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
sending
[\psi:S^{2}\to\CP]
to
[\psi|_{\mathbf{P}}]
; this map is the quotient by the action of
\Mod(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, and realizes
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
as the universal cover of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
.

If
\mathbf{P}_{1}\subset\mathbf{P}_{2}\subset S^{2}
, there is a holomorphic forgetful map
\widetilde{\mu}:\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{2})\to\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{1})
. The map
\widetilde{\mu}
is a lift of the forgetful map
\mu:\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{2}}\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{1}}
.

3.1.
Multicurves and the curve complex

A simple closed curve on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
is called
essential
if it does not bound a disc, and is called
non-peripheral
if it does not bound a punctured disc. A multicurve on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
is a (possibly empty) set of homotopy classes of pairwise disjoint, essential and non-peripheral simple closed curves on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
.

The curve complex
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
of
(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
is the simplicial complex defined as follows: vertices of
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
are in bijection with homotopy classes of simple closed curves on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
that are essential and non peripheral. The vertices corresponding to
[\gamma_{1}],\ldots,[\gamma_{k+1}]
span a
k
-simplex if and only if there exist pairwise disjoint representatives for
[\gamma_{1}],\ldots,[\gamma_{k+1}]
, i.e. if and only if
\{[\gamma_{1}],\ldots,[\gamma_{k+1}]\}
is a multicurve on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
. The curve complex is locally infinite, and Gromov hyperbolic
[
MM99
]
. The mapping class group
\Mod(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
acts on
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, but this action can have infinite stabilizers.

3.2.
Augmented Teichmüller space

In order to tell a more complete story we introduce augmented Teichmüller space
\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, a partial compactification of
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
parameterizing isotopy classes of tuples
(C,\iota,\psi)
, where
(C,\iota)
us a
\mathbf{P}
-marked stable curve and
\psi:S^{2}\to C
is a surjective continuous map satisfying:

•

\psi
is a homeomorphism away from nodes of
C
, and the preimage of every node of
C
is a simple closed curve of
S^{2}
, and

•

\psi|_{\mathbf{P}}=\iota
.

The action of the mapping class group
\Mod(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
on
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
extends continuously to an action on
\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
; the quotient is
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
. The quotient map
\rho:\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
sends
[C,\iota,\psi]
to the stable curve
[C,\iota]
. Note that given
[C,\iota,\psi]\in\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, the set
\Gamma
of homotopy classes of simple closed curves of
S^{2}
contracted to nodes by
\psi
is a multicurve on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
. The boundary
\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\setminus\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
is stratified, with strata indexed by multicurves. The boundary complex of
\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
(defined in Section
3.4
) is exactly the curve complex
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
.

3.3.
“Tropical Teichmüller space”: the space of weighted multicurves

Given a multicurve
\Gamma
, we denote by
\cone(\Gamma)
the cone
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}_{\geq 0}
. If there is a containment
\Gamma_{2}\subset\Gamma_{1}
of multicurves on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
, then there is a natural inclusion, as a face, of
\cone(\Gamma_{2})
into
\cone(\Gamma_{1})
. We set
\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
to be the union of the cones
\cone(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
ranges over all multicurves on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}

\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P})

\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})

is the correct candidate for the “tropical Teichmüller space”
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})^{\trop}

3.4

Suppose that
\Gamma

. We pick pairwise disjoint representative curves

\text{on }S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}^{1}\end{subarray}}a_{[\gamma^{\prime}]}\right)[\gamma]

The above maps
\cone(\Gamma^{\prime})\to\cone(\widetilde{\mu}_{*}(\Gamma^{\prime}))
glue together to determine a continuous piecewise linear map
\widetilde{\mu}^{\trop}:\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{2})\to\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{1})
. We suggest that this map is a good candidate for the tropicalization of the forgetful map
\widetilde{\mu}
. Note that there is no well-defined forgetful map from
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{2})
to
\CCom(S^{2},\mathbf{P}_{1})
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mu}^{\trop}
as defined above is a natural lift of
\mu^{\trop}:\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{2}}^{\trop}\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}_{1}}^{\trop}
.

3.4.
Tropicalization, within and without algebraic geometry

Here, we attempt to provide more justification for the proposal (Section
3.3
) that
the space of weighted multicurves be considered a good candidate for “tropical Teichmüller space”. In the context of algebraic geometry, the term “tropicalization” has a few different, but related, meanings. We discuss some of them below (not in their most general senses).

(1)

Tropicalizations from embeddings in tori.
The most traditional and unambiguous meaning arises in the context of toric geometry. Suppose that
X
is a closed subvariety of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
. Then its tropicalization
X^{\trop}
is a polyhedral complex in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, see the textbook
[
MS15
]
for a definition.

(2)

Tropicalizations from compactifications.
Suppose
X
is a smooth, non-compact variety admitting a smooth compactification
\overline{X}
with the property that the boundary
\overline{X}\setminus X
is a simple normal crossings hypersurface. Then the
boundary complex of
\overline{X}
is a simplicial complex that has a vertex for every irreducible component of the boundary, and
k
vertices span a simplex if and only if the corresponding
k
-fold intersection of hypersurfaces is non-empty. The homotopy type of the boundary complex depends only on
X
, not on the choice of compactification
\overline{X}

[
Thu07
,
Pay13
]
. The cone
X^{\trop}
over the boundary complex of
\overline{X}
is sometimes referred to as a tropicalization of
X
, see for example
[
ACP15
]
(including for a connection to the
Berkovich analytification
of
X
). The cone complex encodes the asymptotics of
1
-parameter families of points in
X
in the following way: A point
x(t)
of
X
defined over the field
\mathbb{C}((t))
of formal Laurent series determines a point
x^{\trop}
of
X^{\trop}
. The point
x^{\trop}
lies in the relative interior of the cone of
X^{\trop}
that corresponds to the boundary stratum of
\overline{X}
whose relative interior contains the “
t=0
limit” of
x(t)
in
\overline{X}
; the coordinates of
x^{\trop}
inside that cone are the
t
-adic valuations of local equations for the boundary hypersurfaces.

(3)

Tropicalizations from degenerations.
Suppose
(C(t),p_{1}(t),\ldots,p_{n}(t))
is a
1
-parameter family of smooth algebraic curves defined over
\mathbb{C}((t))
. There is a tropical curve (metric graph)
C^{\trop}
associated to
C(t)
; its underlying combinatorial graph is the dual graph of the stable curve obtained as the “
t=0
limit”
C(0)
of
C(t)
, and edge-lengths are determined by the exponential rates of formation of nodes as
C(t)
degenerates to
C(0)
.

We briefly summarize how the above notions apply to the case of moduli spaces of curves and their tropical counterparts. We include in our discussion topological and Riemann surfaces (and algebraic curves) of genus greater than
1
, their moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, and Teichmüller and augmented Teichmüller spaces
\mathcal{T}(g,n)
and
\Tbar_{g,n}
, all defined analogously to the
g=0
case discussed elsewhere in this article. We also need to include genus-
g
,
n
-marked tropical curves, which are obtained by assigning edge-lengths to the dual graphs of genus-
g
,
n
-marked stable curves. The moduli space of genus-
g
,
n
-marked tropical curves is denoted
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}

[
ACP15
]
. (Here, for uniformity, we use the notation
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
rather than
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
.) The relationship between the algebraic and tropical moduli spaces is particularly satisfying in genus
0
because all three notions of tropicalization apply, and interact seamlessly with one another. In the case of genus
g>0
, the first notion — tropicalization from toric embedding — does not apply, but the other two notions do apply with caveats, as we explain below.

•

There is a natural embedding of
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
into a torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
with respect to which the tropicalization — in the sense of Item
1
above — is
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}^{\trop}
. There is no such embedding of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
into a torus for
g>0
.

•

As mentioned earlier,
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
is the cone over the boundary complex of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
. In positive genus, there are two complications: firstly,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is an orbifold, not a complex manifold, and secondly, its boundary is normal crossings but not simple normal crossings. Both technicalities can be addressed, and there is a precise sense in which
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
is the cone over the boundary complex of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

[
CCUW20
,
CGP21
]
.

•

A point
x(t)
of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
defined over
\mathbb{C}((t))
determines a point
x^{\trop}
of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
, as described in Item
2
above — in this interpretation,
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
plays its role as boundary complex. The same point
x(t)
also determines a smooth genus
g
curve
X(t)
defined over
\mathbb{C}((t))
(or possibly over
\mathbb{C}((t^{\frac{1}{r}}))
), which admits a tropicalization
C^{\trop}
in the sense of Item
3
above. Since
C^{\trop}
is a tropical curve, it corresponds to a point of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
— in this interpretation,
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
plays its role as the space of tropical curves. One of the main results of
[
ACP15
]
is that the points of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
determined by
x(t)
with respect to these two different interpretations agree with each other.

There is also a fourth connection between the algebro-geometric and the tropical moduli spaces of curves due to Chan-Galatius-Payne
[
CGP21
]
; this one departs from the realm of algebraic geometry:

•

There is a proper surjective continuous map
\lambda:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
, natural up to homotopy. Conceptually, this is a non-algebraic version of the tropicalization map defined on “dynamic”
\mathbb{C}((t))
-points as in Item
2
. Points of
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
— defined over
\mathbb{C}
and therefore “static” — that are “close” to some boundary stratum of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
map to the cone of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
corresponding to that stratum. The map
\lambda
is described in Section 7.1 of
[
CGP21
]
; we give a brief summary here. Fix
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small so that on any genus-
g
,
n
-punctured hyperbolic surface, any two closed geodesics of length
\leq\epsilon
must be disjoint. Given
x\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, we let
C_{x}
be the genus-
g
,
n
-punctured Riemann surface representing
x
, together with its canonical hyperbolic metric. The collection
\Gamma
of simple closed curves on
C_{x}
with length strictly less than
\epsilon
is a multicurve. We obtain a genus-
g
,
n
-marked tropical curve from the marked dual graph
G
of
\Gamma
by assigning length
-\log(\frac{\len(\gamma)}{\epsilon})
to the edge of
G
corresponding to
\gamma\in\Gamma
. The tropical curve thus obtained is the point
\lambda(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
.

Moduli spaces of tropical curves were studied in other guises before the birth of tropical geometry, most notably in mathematical biology and geometric group theory. For example,
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}^{\trop}
was studied in
[
BHV01
]
as the space of phylogenetic trees, and (a certain open subset of)
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
was used
[
SV87
]
as a tool to compute the Euler characteristic of the outer automorphism group of the free group. There has increasingly been communication between tropical geometry and geometry outside algebraic geometry. It is in this context that we believe that the term “tropicalization” might have even wider applications. None of the existing definitions of “tropicalization” seem to apply to Teichmüller space. It would be very interesting to have precise definitions that might apply, but we do not attempt to come up with any here. Instead, we make an argument that the relationship between Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
and the cone
\ConeCC(g,n)
over the curve complex (for a genus-
g
,
n
-marked surface) is analogous to the relationship between
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
.

•

The pure mapping class group
\Mod(g,n)
acts on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
and
\ConeCC(g,n)
; the quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
by
\Mod(g,n)
is
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and the quotient of
\ConeCC(g,n)
by
\Mod(g,n)
is
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
.

•

As in Item
2
just above,
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
is the cone over the boundary complex of the normal-crossings compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. In contrast,
\Tbar_{g,n}
is not a compactification of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
, and the complex structure of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
does not extend to the boundary, and so the boundary of
\Tbar_{g,n}
is in no way normal crossings. However, in a combinatorial sense, the boundary is as good as a simple normal crossings boundary — the boundary is (complex) codimension-
1
and stratified, and any non-empty
k
-fold intersection of codimension-
1
strata has codimension exactly
k
. As a result, there is a well-defined notion of boundary complex that applies — the boundary complex of
\Tbar_{g,n}
is the simplicial complex that has a vertex for every closed codimension-
1
stratum, and
k
vertices span a simplex if and only if the corresponding codimension-
1
strata have non-empty intersection. It is straightforward from the definition of
\Tbar_{g,n}
(Section
3.2
) that the boundary complex of
\Tbar_{g,n}
can be canonically identified with the curve complex
\CCom(g,n)
, and so
the cone over the boundary complex is exactly
\ConeCC(g,n)
.

•

The non-algebro-geometric continuous proper map
\lambda:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\trop}
of Chan-Galatius Payne (discussed above) lifts to a
\Mod(g,n)
-equivariant continuous (but non-proper) map
\tilde{\lambda}:\mathcal{T}_{g,n}\to\ConeCC(g,n)
; this is transparently clear from the description of
\lambda
given above.

Remark 3.2
.

There is a compelling related story about
Schottky space
, a non-algebraic complex manifold that can be realized as the quotient of Teichmüller space by a certain subgroup of the mapping class group. It is established in
[
GH88
,
PT22
,
Uli21
,
CMV13
]
that the tropicalization of Schottky space is the simplicial completion of
Culler-Vogtmann Outer Space

[
CV86
]
. For example, there is a partial compactification of Schottky space whose boundary complex is the completion of Outer Space. There seems to be at least one key difference between the cases of Schottky space and Teichmüller space — the partial compactification of Schottky space extends its complex structure, whereas, as noted above, the complex structure of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
does not extend to
\Tbar_{g,n}
.

4.
Thurston’s topological characterization of PCF rational functions

In this section, we summarize the relevant parts of Thurston’s results (written in
[
DH93
]
) giving a topological characterization of PCF rational functions.

4.1.
PCF branched coverings and PCF rational functions

A map
\varphi:S^{2}\to S^{2}
is called a
branched cover
if there is a finite set
\mathbf{B}\subset S^{2}
such that
\varphi:S^{2}\setminus\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{B})\to S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{B}
is a covering map. Here, we will only deal with orientation-preserving branched covers. The restriction of
\varphi
to a small punctured neighbourhood of any point
x\in S^{2}
is a covering map of some degree; this is the
local degree
of
\varphi
at that point. A
critical point
of
\varphi
is a point at which the local degree is greater than
1
. Every rational function
f:\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an orientation-preserving branched cover, and its critical points are exactly the points at which the derivative vanishes. The local degree of
f
at
x
is the unique
r>0
such that the first
r-1
derivatives of
f
vanish at
f
but the
r
th does not.

The
post-critical set
of a branched cover is the set
\{\varphi^{n}(x)\thickspace|\thickspace n>0,x\mbox{ critical point of }\varphi\}
. A branched covering
\varphi:S^{2}\to S^{2}
is called PCF (post-critically finite) if its post-critical set is finite. Two PCF branched covers
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
with post-critical sets
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
respectively are called
combinatorially equivalent
(also referred to as
Thurston equivalent
) if there are orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:S^{2}\to S^{2}
such that

•

\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are isotopic relative to
\mathbf{P}
, and both map
\mathbf{P}
onto
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
, and

•

\varphi^{\prime}=\psi_{2}\circ\varphi\circ\psi_{1}^{-1}
.

Thurston’s influential result answers the question: given a PCF branched cover
\varphi
, when is it combinatorially equivalent to a PCF rational function? In fact, if
\varphi
has hyperbolic orbifold (as in footnote
1
), then either (A)
\varphi
is combinatorially equivalent to a rational map
f
, which is unique up to Möbius conjugacy, or (B)
\varphi
has a specific type of topological obstruction in the form of an
\varphi
-stable multicurve, defined in Section
4.3
.

Fixing a branched cover

We now fix
\varphi
, a PCF branched cover with hyperbolic orbifold, of degree at least
2
, and whose post-critical set
\mathbf{P}
has cardinality at least
4
. Note that
\mathbf{P}\subset\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})
. We denote by
\widetilde{\mu}
the forgetful map
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}))\to\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, denote by
\mu
the forgetful map from
\mathcal{M}_{0,\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}})
to
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
, denote by
\rho
the map
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}
, and denote by
\widetilde{\rho}
the map
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}))\to\mathcal{M}_{0,\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})}
.

4.2.
The pullback map on Teichmüller space

A homeomorphism
\psi:S^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
determines a complex structure on
S^{2}
. One can pull this complex structure back on charts along the branched cover
\varphi
to obtain a new complex structure on
S^{2}
, and thus a global homeomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:S^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
; by construction,
\psi^{\prime}\circ\varphi\circ\psi:\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is holomorphic. Although there are choices involved, the class of
\psi^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}))
is well-defined and only depends on the class of
\psi
in
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
. The map
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}:\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}))
that sends
[\psi]
to
[\psi^{\prime}]
is holomorphic. Composing with the forgetful map
\widetilde{\mu}
, we obtain a holomorphic selfmap
\TP_{\varphi}:=\widetilde{\mu}\circ\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}
of
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, called
Thurston’s pullback map
.

4.3.
\varphi
-stable multicurves and Thurston obstructions

If
\Gamma
is a multicurve on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
, then its pullback
\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}(\Gamma):=\{[\varphi^{-1}(\gamma)]\thickspace|\thickspace[\gamma]\in\Gamma\}
is a multicurve on
S^{2}\setminus\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})
. Some (or all) of the components components of
\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}(\Gamma)
may be peripheral or non-essential on
S^{2}\setminus\mathbf{P}
. We set
\varphi^{*}(\Gamma)
to be the possibly empty multicurve
\widetilde{\mu}_{*}(\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}(\Gamma))
.

There is a natural
Thurston linear transformation

\TLTtilde_{\varphi,\Gamma}
from
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
to
\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}(\Gamma)}
, defined as follows in terms on its action on the natural basis:

(1)

\displaystyle\TLTtilde_{\varphi,\Gamma}([\gamma])=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\text{simple closed curve}\\
\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})=\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg(\varphi:\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma)}[\gamma^{\prime}]

The composition
\TLT_{\varphi,\Gamma}:=\widetilde{\mu}^{\trop}\circ\TLTtilde_{\varphi,\Gamma}
is also called the Thurston linear transformation; this is a map from
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
to
\mathbb{R}^{\varphi^{*}(\Gamma)}
acting on the natural basis as follows:

(2)

\displaystyle\TLT_{\varphi,\Gamma}([\gamma])=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\gamma^{\prime\prime}]\text{ essential and}\\
\text{non-peripheral rel. }\mathbf{P}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\text{simple closed}\\
\varphi(\gamma^{\prime})=\gamma\\
\gamma^{\prime}\text{ homotopic to }\gamma^{\prime\prime}\text{ rel. }\mathbf{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\deg(\varphi:\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma)}[\gamma^{\prime\prime}]

Both linear transformations (
1
) and (
2
) are commonly referred to as
Thurston linear transformations
associated to the multicurve
\Gamma
.

A multicurve
\Gamma
is called
\varphi
-stable
if
\varphi^{*}(\Gamma)\subset\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is
\varphi
-stable then the Thurston linear transformation associated to
\Gamma
can be considered to be a linear self-map of
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
. Given that the Thurston linear transformation has non-negative coefficients with respect to the natural basis, its dominant eigenvalue
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
is non-negative and has an eigenvector in
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}_{\geq 0}
. A
\varphi
-stable multicurve
\Gamma
is called a
Thurston obstruction
if
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\Gamma)\geq 1
.

4.4.
Thurston’s theorem

The pullback map
\TP_{\varphi}
admits a fixed point if and only if
\varphi
does not admit a Thurston obstruction. Also,
\TP_{\varphi}
has a fixed point if and only if
\varphi
is combinatorially equivalent to a rational function: if
[\psi]
is a fixed point of
\TP_{\varphi}
, then there is a homeomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:S^{2}\to\CP
that is isotopic to
\psi
rel.
\mathbf{P}
, and such that
\psi^{\prime}\circ\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}
is a PCF rational function. In fact,
\TP_{\varphi}
is distance-nonincreasing and
\TP_{\varphi}^{2}
is strictly distance-decreasing on
\mathcal{T}(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
, and so a fixed point, if it exists, is unique. We conclude that either (A)
\varphi
is combinatorially equivalent to a rational map, which is unique up to Möbius conjugacy, or (B)
\varphi
admits a Thurston obstruction
\Gamma
.

4.5.
The augmented pullback map

Selinger
[
Sel12
]
showed that Thurston’s pullback map extends continuously to augmented Teichmüller space
\Tbar(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
. We describe this extension in more detail in the proof of Lemma
7.3
.

4.6.
“The tropical pullback map”

Observe that since the Thurston linear transformation (
1
) has non-negative coefficients with respect to the natural bases, it restricts to a map
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}^{\trop}(\Gamma):\cone(\Gamma)\to\cone(\widetilde{\varphi}^{*}(\Gamma)).

It is straightforward to check that the maps
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}^{\trop}(\Gamma)
glue together along faces to yield a piece-wise-linear map
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}^{\trop}:\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\ConeCC(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})).
Similarly, the linear transformation (
2
) restricts to a map
\TP_{\trop}^{\trop}(\Gamma):\cone(\Gamma)\to\cone(\varphi^{*}(\Gamma)),

and the maps
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}(\Gamma)
glue together along faces to yield a piece-wise-linear map
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}:\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P})\to\ConeCC(S^{2},\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})).
We use the notation
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}^{\trop}
and
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}
in order to propose that these piecewise linear maps ought to be considered to be tropicalizations of
\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}
and
\TP_{\varphi}
respectively. Note that
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}=\widetilde{\mu}^{\trop}\circ\widetilde{\TP}_{\varphi}^{\trop}.

Under this interpretation, a
\varphi
-stable multicurve
\Gamma
is exactly a multicurve such that
\cone(\Gamma)
is invariant under the action of
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}
, in which case there is a ray in
\cone(\Gamma)
that is fixed and scaled by the dominant eigenvalue
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
. A Thurston obstruction therefore is the same as a ray in
\ConeCC(S^{2},\mathbf{P})
that is fixed by
\TP_{\varphi}^{\trop}
, and scaled by a factor
\geq 1
.

5.
Hurwitz spaces and the moduli space correspondence

Here, we summarize the results of
[
Koc13
]
as they apply to this article. There is
Hurwitz space
— a non-compact smooth variety
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prof}}(\varphi)
parameterizing marked rational functions on
\CP
that have the same branching profile as
\varphi
. More precisely,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prof}}(\varphi)
parameterizes tuples
[f,\iota_{1},\iota_{2}]
, where

•

\iota_{1}:\mathbf{P}\hookrightarrow\CP
and
\iota_{2}:\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})\hookrightarrow\CP
are injections,

•

f:(\CP,\iota_{2}(\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})))\to(\CP,\iota_{1}(\mathbf{P}))
is a rational function that has the same degree as
\varphi
, and that maps
\iota_{2}(\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P}))
to
\iota_{1}(\mathbf{P})
exactly as
\varphi
maps
\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})
to
\mathbf{P}
(that is,
f\circ\iota_{2}=\iota_{1}\circ\varphi
), including with the same local degrees. (Note that this forces
f
to have no critical values away from
\iota_{1}(\mathbf{P})
.)

Two tuples
[f,\iota_{1},\iota_{2}]
and
[f^{\prime},\iota_{1}^{\prime},\iota_{2}^{\prime}]
are equal in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prof}}(\varphi)
if they differ by changes of coordinates on
\CP
, i.e. if there are automorphisms
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
of
\CP
such that
\iota_{1}^{\prime}=\alpha_{1}\circ\iota_{1}
,
\iota_{2}^{\prime}=\alpha_{2}\circ\iota_{2}
, and
f^{\prime}=\alpha_{1}\circ f\circ(\alpha_{2})^{-1}
. In general,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prof}}(\varphi)
is disconnected, with connected components corresponding to a type of non-dynamical topological equivalence known as
Hurwitz equivalence
, defined below.

Definition 5.1
.

Given
\iota_{1}^{\prime},\iota_{1}^{\prime\prime}:\mathbf{P}\hookrightarrow S^{2}
and
\iota_{2}^{\prime},\iota_{2}^{\prime\prime}:\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})\hookrightarrow S^{2}
injections, and
\varphi^{\prime}:(S^{2},\iota_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})))\to(S^{2},\iota_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{P}))
and
\varphi^{\prime\prime}:(S^{2},\iota_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})))\to(S^{2},\iota_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{P}))
branched covers, we say that
\varphi^{\prime}
and
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
are
Hurwitz equivalent
if there exist homeomorphisms
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:S^{2}\to\CP
such that
(\psi_{1})\circ\iota_{1}^{\prime}=\iota_{1}^{\prime\prime}
on
\mathbf{P}
,
\psi_{2}\circ\iota_{2}^{\prime}=\iota_{2}^{\prime\prime}
on
\varphi^{-1}(\mathbf{P})
, and
\varphi^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}\circ(\psi_{2})^{-1}
. Note that
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are
not
required to be isotopic rel.
\mathbf{P}
.

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{prof}}(\varphi)
has a unique connected component
\mathcal{H}(\varphi)

.

7.3.
Weakly fixed cones

A

\MSC_{\varphi}^{\trop}

\cone(\tree_{1})

•

if
\MSC_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}
has an eigenvector in
\cone^{\circ}(\tree_{1})

Corollary 7.13
.

(1)

If
\Gamma
is a Thurston obstruction for
\varphi
then
\mathrm{FixedRays}_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
is non-empty, and for all
R\in\mathrm{FixedRays}_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
,
\lambdascal(R)\geq 1.

(2)

If
R
is a weakly fixed ray of the moduli space correspondence with
\lambdascal(R)\geq 1
then there exist
\varphi^{\prime}
Hurwitz equivalent to
\varphi
and a Thurston obstruction
\Gamma
for

Remark 7.14
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(\Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2},\mathbf{F})
is a weakly-fixed ray of
\MSC^{\trop}_{\varphi}
with scaling factor
\lambdascal(R)>0
. The
\mathbf{P}
-marked tropical curves
\mu^{\trop}(\Sigma_{2})
and
\Sigma_{1}
have the same underlying tree, and the length of an edge on
\mu^{\trop}(\Sigma_{2})
is
\lambdascal(R)
times the length of the corresponding edge on
\Sigma_{1}
. There is a “scale by
\lambdascal(R)
” map
h:\Sigma_{1}\to\mu^{\trop}(\Sigma_{2})
. The composite
\mathbf{F}\circ h:\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}
is a piecewise linear self-map whose dynamics could be of interest. This idea has been mentioned by Shishikura
[
Shi22
]
in the context of
\varphi
-stable multicurves.

Remark 7.15
.

The link
\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
(see Remark
1.2
) has the structure of a compact polyhedral complex. Given that
\MSC_{\varphi}^{\trop}
is a piecewise linear multivalued self-map of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
, we might hope that it descends to a piecewise affine multivalued self-map of
\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
. Unfortunately, this is not the case. If we try to define such an induced multivalued self-map
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi}^{\trop}
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
, we hit a snag as follows. Given a ray
R\subset\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
and a local single-valued branch
\MSC_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}
of
\MSC_{\varphi}^{\trop}
whose domain includes
R
, we would like to be able to specify the image of
R\in\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
under a corresponding single-valued branch
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}
of the desired multivalued self-map
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi}^{\trop}
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
. The image, under
\MSC_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}
, of
R
is either another ray
R^{\prime}
, or the cone point of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
. In the former case, we could set
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}(R)=R^{\prime}\in\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
. However, in the latter case, there is no point of
\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
corresponding to the cone point of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
, so there is no good value for
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi,\Lambda}^{\trop}(R)
. One could possibly think of
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi}^{\trop}
as a multivalued “map with indeterminacy”, analogous to a meromorphic map. Note that if
R\subset\mathcal{M}_{0,\mathbf{P}}^{\trop}
is a weakly fixed ray of
\MSC_{\varphi}^{\trop}
with strictly positive scaling factor, then
R\in\Delta_{\mathbf{P}}
is a weakly fixed point of
\overline{\MSC}_{\varphi}^{\trop}
. Making this strategy precise, and studying the global dynamical properties of an induced action on the link are currently the subject of investigation by the author and C. Favre.

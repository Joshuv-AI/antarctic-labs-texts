A topological proof of Wolpert’s formula for the Weil-Petersson symplectic form in terms of the Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 32G15; Secondary 57M05. Keywords: Teichmüller space, Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates, Weil-Petersson symplectic form, groupoid cocycle
Nariya Kawazumi

Abstract
We introduce a natural cell decomposition of a closed oriented surface associated with a pants decomposition, and an explicit groupoid cocycle on the cell decomposition which represents each point of the Teichmüller space 𝒯 g \mathcal{T}_{g} . We call it the standard cocycle of the point of 𝒯 g \mathcal{T}_{g} . As an application of the explicit description of the standard cocycle, we obtain a topological proof of Wolpert’s formula for the Weil-Petersson symplectic form in terms of the Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates associated with the pants decomposition.

The fundamental groupoid and holonomy homomorphisms
: In this paper, the concatenation of two paths
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
on a space
X
means that one traverses
\gamma_{1}
first, transfers at
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(0)
and traverses
\gamma_{2}
. In other words, we consider the dual category
\Pi X^{\mathrm{op}}
of the fundamental groupoid of
X
. The set of objects in
\Pi X^{\mathrm{op}}
is
X
, and
\Pi X^{\mathrm{op}}(x_{1},x_{0}):=[([0,1],0,1),(X,x_{0},x_{1})]
,
the homotopy set of paths running from
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
. The composite is induced by the concatenation stated above.
As usual, the group
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts on the upper half plane
\mathbb{H}
from the left.
In this paper, we define the holonomy
\rho:\pi_{1}(X,*)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\widetilde{X}/X)
of the universal covering
\pi:(\widetilde{X},\tilde{*})\to(X,*)
by
\tilde{\ell}(1)=\rho([\ell])(\tilde{*})
for any loop
\ell:([0,1],\{0,1\})\to(X,*)
and its lift
\tilde{\ell}:([0,1],0)\to(X,\tilde{*})
. Then our concatenation convention makes the holonomy
\rho

a group homomorphism
.

Lemma 1.1
.

For
{\lambda}>1
and
A,B\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have the following.

(1)
If
A{\overline{D}}({\lambda})={\overline{D}}({\lambda})A
, then we have
A={\overline{D}}(a)
for some
a>0

(2)
If
AB=BA={\overline{D}}({\lambda})
, then we have
A={\overline{D}}(a)
and

B={\overline{D}}({\lambda}/a)
for some
a>0
.

(3)
If
A{\overline{D}}({\lambda})B^{-1}=B{\overline{D}}({\lambda})A^{-1}={\overline{D}}({\lambda})
, then we have
A=B={\overline{D}}(a)
for some
a>0
.

Proof.

(1) Denote
A=\pm\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{pmatrix}
. Then

\begin{pmatrix}{{\lambda}a}&{{\lambda}^{-1}b}\\
{{\lambda}c}&{{\lambda}^{-1}d}\end{pmatrix}=\pm\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{{\lambda}}&{0}\\
{0}&{{\lambda}^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}=A{\overline{D}}({\lambda})={\overline{D}}({\lambda})A=\pm\begin{pmatrix}{{\lambda}}&{0}\\
{0}&{{\lambda}^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{pmatrix}=\pm\begin{pmatrix}{{\lambda}a}&{{\lambda}b}\\
{{\lambda}^{-1}c}&{{\lambda}^{-1}d}\end{pmatrix}
,
so that
{\lambda}^{2}|b|=|b|
and
{\lambda}^{2}|c|=|c|
.
Since
{\lambda}^{2}\neq 1
, we have

.
From

, we have

. We may choose

by changing the sign of

if necessary.

(2) Since

, we have

, while

. Hence, by (1), we have

for some

.
We conclude

from

.

(3) We have

Hence we obtain

Finally we discuss lifts of the standard cocycle for a hyperbolic surface to

. The first two subsections are devoted to a review on spin hyperbolic surfaces, because the author cannot find an expository reference about relations between the spin and hyperbolic structures which fits to our construction.
In this section we denote

D({\lambda}):=\begin{pmatrix}{{\lambda}}&{0}\\
{0}&{{\lambda}^{-1}}\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

for any
{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

5.1
Spin structures on an oriented surface

Now we recall some basic facts on spin structures on a compact oriented surface together with their proofs.

Let
\Sigma
be a compact oriented surface, and
denote by
U\Sigma
the unit tangent bundle of the surface
\Sigma
. It is a principal
S^{1}
bundle
defined by the subset
\{e\in T\Sigma;\,\,\|e\|=1\}
of the tangent bundle
T\Sigma
with respect to a Riemannian metric
\|\cdot\|
, or equivalently the quotient of
T\Sigma\setminus(\text{zero section})
by scalar multiplication by the positive real numbers
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
The projection
\varpi:U\Sigma\to\Sigma
induces the Gysin exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\varpi^{*}}{\to}H^{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\iota^{*}}{\to}(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\pi_{0}(\Sigma)},

(28)

where the map
\iota^{*}
is induced by the inclusion of the fiber on each fiber into the total space
U\Sigma
.
A spin structure on
\Sigma
is a fiberwise double covering of
U\Sigma
. It can be regarded as an element
s\in H^{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
such that
\iota^{*}s=(1,1,\dots,1)\in(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{\pi_{0}(\Sigma)}
, and so defines the
\bmod\,2
rotation number
\mathrm{rot}_{s}
of any immersed loop
\ell:S^{1}\to\Sigma
by

\mathrm{rot}_{s}(\ell):=\langle s,[\overset{\cdot}{\ell}]\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}/2,

(29)

where
\overset{\cdot}{\ell}:S^{1}\to U\Sigma
is the normalized velocity vector
of
\ell
. It is independent of the orientation of
\ell
, since it is defined over
\mathbb{Z}/2
. Two spin structures
s
and
s^{\prime}
coincide with each other if
\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(\ell)=\mathrm{rot}_{s}(\ell)
for any immersed loop
\ell
. In fact, the difference
s^{\prime}-s
can be regarded as an element
of
H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
, and we have
\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(\ell)-\mathrm{rot}_{s}(\ell)=\langle s^{\prime}-s,[\ell]\rangle
.
As was proved by Johnson
[
7
, Theorem 1A]
,
if
\ell
is a simple closed curve, then the homology class

[\overset{\cdot}{\ell}]\in H_{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
depends only on the homology class
[\ell]\in H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Proposition 5.1

(Poincaré-Hopf theorem)
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is connected, and its boundary
\partial\Sigma
is non-empty. Fix a labeling of the boundary components, and denote each of them by
\partial_{k}\Sigma
,
1\leq k\leq n
.
Then there exists a spin structure on
\Sigma
, and we have

\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathrm{rot}_{s}(\partial_{k}\Sigma)\equiv\chi(\Sigma)\pmod{2}

for any spin structure
s
on
\Sigma
.
Here
\chi(\Sigma)
is the Euler characteristic of the surface
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is connected and
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, the bundle
U\Sigma
is trivial. Hence there exists a fiberwise double covering of
U\Sigma
. Moreover, for any spin structure
s\in H^{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
,
we have some
\tilde{s}\in H^{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
whose
\bmod\,2
reduction equals
s\in H^{1}(U\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
and whose restriction to the fiber equals the positive generator in
H^{1}(S^{1};\mathbb{Z})
. The cohomology class
\tilde{s}
can be regarded as a framing of
U\Sigma
, and the
\bmod\,2
reduction of its rotation number
\mathrm{rot}_{\tilde{s}}
equals
\mathrm{rot}_{s}
for any immersed loop. Hence,
by the Poincaré-Hopf theorem, we have

\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathrm{rot}_{\tilde{s}}(\partial_{k}\Sigma)=\chi(\Sigma)\in\mathbb{Z},

where the orientation on
\partial_{k}\Sigma
is induced by that of
\Sigma
. (See, for example,
[
8
, Lemma 3.3]
.) This proves the proposition.
∎

Let
C=\coprod^{m}_{l=1}C_{l}
be a disjoint union of simple closed curves in the surface
\Sigma
. Take an open tubular neighborhood

\nu:C\times\mathopen{]}-2,2\mathclose{[}\hookrightarrow\Sigma
with
\nu(\cdot,0)=1_{C}
. We denote
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\setminus\nu(C\times\mathopen{]}-1,1\mathclose{[})
and

C_{l}^{\pm}:={\nu}(C_{l}\times\{\pm 1\})\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
.
By gluing all
C_{l}^{+}
and
C_{l}^{-}

through
\nu(\cdot,1)
and
\nu(\cdot,-1)
,
we have a surjective
C^{\infty}
map
q:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
.
We denote
C^{\prime}:=q^{-1}(C)=\coprod^{m}_{l=1}(C_{l}^{+}\amalg C_{l}^{-})\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s^{\prime}
be a spin structure on
\Sigma^{\prime}
. Then
s^{\prime}=q^{*}s
for some spin structure
s
on
\Sigma
,
if and only if
\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{+}_{l})=\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{-}_{l})
for every
1\leq l\leq m
.

Proof.

In this proof, the coefficients of all the cohomology groups are
\mathbb{Z}/2
. By excision we have an isomorphism
q^{*}:H^{*}(U\Sigma,U\Sigma|_{C})\cong H^{*}(U\Sigma^{\prime},U\Sigma^{\prime}|_{C^{\prime}})
.
Consider the morphism of cohomology exact sequences of pairs

\begin{CD}H^{1}(U\Sigma,U\Sigma|_{C})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(U\Sigma)@>{\iota^{*}}>{}>H^{1}(U\Sigma|_{C})@>{}>{}>H^{2}(U\Sigma,U\Sigma^{\prime}|_{C})\\
\Big\|@V{q^{*}}V{}V@V{q^{*}}V{}V\Big\|\\
H^{1}(U\Sigma^{\prime},U\Sigma^{\prime}|_{C^{\prime}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(U\Sigma^{\prime})@>{\iota^{*}}>{}>H^{1}(U\Sigma^{\prime}|_{C^{\prime}})@>{}>{}>H^{2}(U\Sigma^{\prime},U\Sigma^{\prime}|_{C^{\prime}}).\end{CD}

(30)

The condition on the
\bmod\,2
rotation numbers is equivalent to

\iota^{*}s^{\prime}\in q^{*}(H^{1}(U\Sigma|_{C}))
. Clearly it follows from
s^{\prime}\in q^{*}(H^{1}(U\Sigma))
. Conversely,
\iota^{*}s^{\prime}\in q^{*}(H^{1}(U\Sigma|_{C}))
implies
s^{\prime}\in q^{*}(H^{1}(U\Sigma))
by some diagram chasing on (
30
). This proves the lemma.
∎

As is known, there exists a spin structure also when

Now let
s^{\prime}
be a spin structure on
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{+}_{l})=\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{-}_{l})

1\leq l\leq m
. By Lemma
5.2
, the set
\mathcal{S}(s^{\prime})
of all spin structures

\Sigma

Since the spin structure is defined to be a fiberwise double covering of the unit tangent bundle of the surface, the restriction

for each

\tau_{l}
whose restricts to the antipode map of the circle on each fiber. The difference of two

transitively
through the
\tau_{l}

Now fix some
1\leq l_{0}\leq m
.

Lemma 5.3
.

If we obtain
s_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(s^{\prime})
by re-glueing
s_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(s^{\prime})
along
C_{l_{0}}
using
\tau:=\tau_{l_{0}}
, then the difference
s_{2}-s_{1}\in H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
equals the Poincaré dual of
[C_{l_{0}}]\in H_{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Proof.

The lemma is equivalent to the equation

\mathrm{rot}_{s_{2}}(\ell)-\mathrm{rot}_{s_{1}}(\ell)=[C_{l_{0}}]\cdot[\ell]\in\mathbb{Z}/2

(31)

for any immersed loop
\ell:S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\Sigma
. Here
the RHS means the
\bmod\,2
intersection number of the homology classes
[C_{l_{0}}]
and
[\ell]
. We may assume
\ell
is transverse to
C_{l_{0}}
. It is clear if the intersection
\ell\cap C_{l_{0}}
is empty. Hence we can assume
\ell\cap C_{l_{0}}
is not empty. Then there exist some real numbers
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n-1}<t_{n}=t_{0}+1
with
n\geq 1
such that
\ell^{-1}(C_{l_{0}})=\{t_{i}\bmod{\mathbb{Z}}\in S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z};\,\,1\leq i\leq n\}
. Then we have
n\equiv[C_{l_{0}}]\cdot[\ell]\pmod{2}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we denote
I_{i}:=[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\subset\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and take a lift
\ell_{i}
of
\overset{\cdot}{\ell}|_{I_{i}}:I_{i}\to U\Sigma
to the spin structure
s_{1}
. We have
\ell_{i+1}(t_{i})=\tau^{\epsilon_{i}}(\ell_{i}(t_{i}))
for some
\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}
.
Then we have
\mathrm{rot}_{s_{1}}(\ell)\equiv\sum^{n}_{i=1}\epsilon_{i}\bmod{2}
. If we consider the spin structure
s_{2}
similarly, we have

\mathrm{rot}_{s_{2}}(\ell)\equiv\sum^{n}_{i=1}(\epsilon_{i}+1)\equiv\mathrm{rot}_{s_{1}}(\ell)+n\equiv\mathrm{rot}_{s_{1}}(\ell)+[C_{l_{0}}]\cdot[\ell]\bmod{2},

which proves the equation (

We denote the composite of the homomorphism induced by the inclusion

and the Poincaré duality map by
\imath_{*}:H_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H_{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)=H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
. By the excision isomorphisms
H_{*}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)=H_{*}(C\cup\partial\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)
and

, we have a long exact sequence

0\to H_{2}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{j_{*}}{\to}H_{2}(\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime};\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\partial_{*}}{\to}H_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)\overset{\imath_{*}}{\to}H^{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}/2).

(32)

One can deduce the following theorem from what we have proved.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
s^{\prime}
be a spin structure on
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{+}_{l})=\mathrm{rot}_{s^{\prime}}(C^{-}_{l})
for each

. Then the action of
H_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)
on the set
\mathcal{S}(s^{\prime})
is transitive, and its isotropy subgroup at each point equals
\partial_{*}(H_{2}(\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime};\mathbb{Z}/2))
.

Here the fundamental classes of the connected components of
\Sigma^{\prime}
constitute a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-basis of the homology group
H_{2}(\Sigma^{\prime},\partial\Sigma^{\prime};\mathbb{Z}/2)
. For any connected component

\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}
,
\partial_{*}[\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}]\in H_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)
is the fundamental class of the boundary of the image
q(\Sigma^{\prime}_{0})
. It should be remarked that two boundary components of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{0}
which have the same image in
C
cancel each other in
H_{1}(C;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.

Now let
\Sigma_{g,b}
denote a compact connected oriented surface of genus
g
with
b
boundary components. If
\chi(\Sigma_{g,b})=2-2g-b
is negative, it admits a pants decomposition
\{\delta_{i}\}^{3g-3+b}_{i=1}
, where
\delta_{i}
is a simple closed curve in
\Sigma_{g,b}
. It yields
-\chi(\Sigma_{g,b})=2g-2+b
pairs of pants

\{P_{j}\}^{2g-2+b}_{j=1}
. Suppose a spin structure is given on each pair of pants such that the
\bmod\,2
rotation numbers of each
\delta_{i}
for the spin structures in its both sides coincide. Then the set of all the spin structures on
\Sigma_{g,b}
which restrict to the given one at each pair of pants is bijective to the quotient of
H_{1}(\bigcup^{3g-3+b}_{i=1}\delta_{i};\mathbb{Z}/2)
by the action of the group generated by the covering transformations for the given spin structures on the pairs of pants. More explicitly, by changing the label of
\{\delta_{i}\}
if necessary,
we glue all the pair of pants along
\{\delta_{i}\}^{3g-3+b}_{i=g+1}
to obtain a connected surface
\hat{\Sigma}
of genus
0
,
which is diffeomorphic to
\Sigma_{0,2g+b}
.
We can construct the surface
\hat{\Sigma}
by adding a pair of pants one by one.
By Theorem
5.4
we have a unique spin structure
\hat{s}
on
\hat{\Sigma}
which restricts to the given spin structure on each pair of pants.
We have exactly
2^{g}
spin structures on
\Sigma_{g,b}
extending the structure
\hat{s}
again by Theorem
5.4
. In fact,
the inclusion homomorphism
H_{1}(\bigcup^{g}_{i=1}\delta_{i};\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,b},\partial\Sigma_{g,b};\mathbb{Z}/2)
is injective.

5.2
Spin structures on a hyperbolic surface

\Sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
.

(i)
The restriction of
\rho
to the pair
P_{j}
of pants equals the standard cocycle on
P_{j}
for any
1\leq j\leq 2g-2
.

(ii)
For any
g+1\leq i\leq 3g+3
, we have
\rho(e^{1}_{((i))0})=\epsilon_{i}\rho(e^{1}_{((i))1})=\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{-{T_{i}}^{-1}}\\
{T_{i}}&{0}\end{pmatrix}
for some
T_{i}>0
.

In particular, for
1\leq i\leq g
, we have
\rho(e^{1}_{((i))0})=\rho(e^{1}_{((i))1})=\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{-{T_{i}}^{-1}}\\
{T_{i}}&{0}\end{pmatrix}
for some
T_{i}\neq 0
.
By Theorem
2.1
,
\tau_{i}:=-2\log|T_{i}|
is the twisting parameter along
\delta_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq 3g-3
.
The set of signs
\{T_{i}/|T_{i}|\}^{g}_{i=1}
indicates a choice among the
2^{g}
spin structures on
{\Sigma_{g}}
, as was stated in the end of §4.1.

6
Appendix: The Shimura isomorphism and symplectic forms on the Teichmüller space

Up to some multiplicative constant, the Weil-Petersson symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
in Wolpert
[
19
]
equals the Atiyah-Bott-Goldman symplectic form defined as a cup product on the twisted cohomology group in Goldman
[
2
]
. On the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}
, we can take the Killing form
B
and the trace form
B^{\text{trace}}
of the standard representation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as invariant non-degenerate symmetric forms. We denote by
\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}
and
\omega^{\text{\rm trace}}_{\mathrm{ABG}}
the Atiyah-Bott-Goldman symplectic form using
B
and
B^{\text{trace}}
, respectively. Since
B=4B^{\text{trace}}
, we have
\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}=4\omega^{\text{\rm trace}}_{\mathrm{ABG}}
. It is
\omega^{\text{\rm trace}}_{\mathrm{ABG}}
that we compute in this paper. In this section, by reading the paper
[
2
]
in detail, and comparing it with Wolpert’s works
[
18
,
19
]
,
we prove

Proposition 6.1
.

2\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}=4\omega^{\text{\rm trace}}_{\mathrm{ABG}}.

The key ingredient of the proof is Wolpert’s argument
[
18
, §4]

based on Hejhal’s result
[
5
]
.

Let
z
be the standard coordinate of the complex line
\mathbb{C}
,
which is divided into three parts:
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C};\,\,\Im z>0\}
,

\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{L}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C};\,\,\Im z<0\}
. Here we denote
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
y=\Im z=(z-\overline{z})/2i
and
\mathbb{C}P^{1}=\hat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
in order to refer some fundamental facts on quasi-conformal mappings
[
1
]
.
Let
\Gamma\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be a Fuchsian group such that
M=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma
is a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
, and

\mathcal{F}\subset\mathbb{H}
a fundamental domain of the action of
\Gamma
. The tangent space of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
at a point over the isomorphism class of
M
is regarded as the cohomology group
H^{1}(M;K^{\otimes(-1)})
.
Here
K=K_{M}
is the holomorphic cotangent bundle of the Riemann surface
M
.
Its Serre dual is the space
H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})
of holomorphic quadratic differentials on
M
, which is identified with the space of
\Gamma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differentials on
\mathbb{H}
.

6.1
The Weil-Petersson metric and the Shimura isomorphism

The Weil-Petersson Hermitian metric on the Teichüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is induced by a Hermitian inner product on the space
H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})
coming from the hyperbolic metric
dA=(\Im z)^{-2}dxdy=-2i(z-\overline{z})^{-2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}
. For two
\Gamma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differrentials
\Xi=\xi(z)dz^{\otimes 2}
and
\Psi=\psi(z)dz^{\otimes 2}
on
\mathbb{H}
, Goldman
[
2
, p.212]
and Wolpert
[
19
, p.210]
define the same Hermitian product

h(\Xi,\Psi)=\langle\Xi,\Psi\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{F}}\xi(z)\overline{\psi}(z)(\Im z)^{2}dxdy=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\Xi\cdot(dA)^{-1}\cdot\overline{\Psi}.

Here we mean by the symbol
\langle\Xi,\Psi\rangle
the Hermitian product in
[
2
]
, though it means
\frac{1}{2}\Re h(\Xi,\Psi)
in
[
19
]
. In this section we denote

\hat{h}(\Xi):=(\Im z)^{2}\overline{\xi(z)}d\overline{z}\otimes({\partial}/{\partial z})

for any
\Gamma
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differrential
\Xi=\xi(z)dz^{\otimes 2}\in H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})
. The image
\hat{h}(H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2}))
is called the space of harmonic Beltrami differentials. Taking their Dolbeault cohomomology classes, we get an anti-linear isomorphism
H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})\to H^{1}(M;K^{\otimes(-1)})
,
\Xi\mapsto[\hat{h}(\Xi)]
. In
[
19
, p.228]
the Weil-Petersson Hermitian product on the tangent space is given by

h([\hat{h}(\Xi)],[\hat{h}(\Psi)])=\int_{\mathcal{F}}\overline{\xi(z)}\psi(z)(\Im z)^{-2}dxdy=\overline{h(\Xi,\Psi)}

for any
\Xi
and
\Psi\in H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})
.
Hence we adopt the anti-linear isomorphism
\hat{h}
as in
[
19
]
, which is different from that in
[
2
, p.212]
.
The Weil-Petersson symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
is given by

\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}([\hat{h}(\Xi)],[\hat{h}(\Psi)])=-2\Im h([\hat{h}(\Xi)],[\hat{h}(\Psi)])=2\Im h(\Xi,\Psi)

(38)

in
[
19
, p.228]
.

Next we consider the column vector field
Z=\begin{pmatrix}z^{2}\partial/\partial z\\
z\partial/\partial z\\
\partial/\partial z\end{pmatrix}
in
[
2
, §2.3]
in detail. In order to make the situation clear, we use the classical basis

E=\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{1}\\
{0}&{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad H=\begin{pmatrix}{1}&{0}\\
{0}&{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad F=\begin{pmatrix}{0}&{0}\\
{1}&{0}\end{pmatrix}

of
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
. The Killing form
B
of
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
is represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&4\\
0&8&0\\
4&0&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the basis
\{E,H,F\}
.
It induces an isomorphism
\tilde{B}:{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
. We denote the dual basis of
\{E,H,F\}
by

\{E^{*},H^{*},F^{*}\}
. Then we have
\tilde{B}(E^{*})=\frac{1}{4}F
,

\tilde{B}(H^{*})=\frac{1}{8}F
and
\tilde{B}(F^{*})=\frac{1}{4}E
.
The Killing form
B
naturally defines a symmetric pairing
B^{*}

by
B^{*}(-,-)=B(\tilde{B}(-),\tilde{B}(-))
, which is represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1/4\\
0&1/8&0\\
1/4&0&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the basis
\{E^{*},H^{*},F^{*}\}
.
The action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
defines a Lie algebra isomorphism
\imath:{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}\to H^{0}(\hat{\mathbb{C}};T\hat{\mathbb{C}})
. One can compute

\imath(pE+qH+rF)=(-rz^{2}+2qz+p)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z},

which means
Z=(-z^{2}F^{*}+2zH^{*}+E^{*})(\partial/\partial z)

from the original definition of the column vector
Z
in the third paragraph of
[
2
, 2.3]
. Hence we obtain an
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant

{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathrm{Ad}}
-valued holomorphic vector field

\displaystyle\tilde{B}(Z)

\displaystyle=(-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}E+\frac{1}{4}zH+\frac{1}{4}F)(\partial/\partial z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}{-z}&{z^{2}}\\
{-1}&{z}\end{pmatrix}\otimes\left(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\in H^{0}(\hat{\mathbb{C}};{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathrm{Ad}}\otimes T\hat{\mathbb{C}}).

Since the
1
-form
Z

has values in
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}^{*}
, so we compute

\displaystyle B^{*}(Z\wedge\overline{Z})

\displaystyle=B((-z^{2}F^{*}+2zH^{*}+E^{*}),(-z^{2}F^{*}+2zH^{*}+E^{*}))(\partial/\partial z)\wedge(\partial/\partial\overline{z})

\displaystyle=(-\frac{1}{4}z^{2}+\frac{2}{4}z\overline{z}-\frac{1}{4}\overline{z}^{2})(\partial/\partial z)\wedge(\partial/\partial\overline{z})=-\frac{1}{4}(z-\overline{z})^{2}(\partial/\partial z)\wedge(\partial/\partial\overline{z})

Hence we have

B^{*}((Z\cdot\Xi)\wedge(\overline{Z}\cdot\overline{\Psi}))=-2i\xi(z)\overline{\psi(z)}y^{2}dx\wedge dy,

and so

\displaystyle\langle\Xi,\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}B^{*}((Z\cdot\Xi)\wedge(\overline{Z}\cdot\overline{\Psi}))

(39)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int_{M}B((\tilde{B}(Z)\cdot\Xi)\wedge(\overline{\tilde{B}(Z)}\cdot\overline{\Psi}))

\displaystyle=2i\int_{M}\mathrm{trace}((\tilde{B}(Z)\cdot\Xi)\wedge(\overline{\tilde{B}(Z)}\cdot\overline{\Psi})),

since
B^{*}(-,-)=B(\tilde{B}(-),\tilde{B}(-))=4\mathrm{trace}(\tilde{B}(-)\tilde{B}(-))
.

The Shimura isomorphism
H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})\to H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{Ad}})
maps
\Xi=\xi(z)dz^{\otimes 2}\in H^{0}(M;K^{2})
to the cohomology class of the group cocycle

u^{\prime}_{\Xi}:A\in\Gamma\mapsto-\frac{1}{4}\int^{Az_{0}}_{z_{0}}\xi(z)\begin{pmatrix}{-z}&{z^{2}}\\
{-1}&{z}\end{pmatrix}dz=\int^{Az_{0}}_{z_{0}}\tilde{B}(Z)\cdot\Xi\in{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}.

(40)

From Proposition in
[
2
, 2.5]
we obtain

h(\Xi,\Psi)=\langle\Xi,\Psi\rangle=\frac{i}{2}B_{*}\left([u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\cup\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]}\right)\cdot[M].

By (
38
) this implies

\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}(\hat{h}(\Xi),\hat{h}(\Psi))=\Re B_{*}\left([u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\cup\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]}\right)\cdot[M].

(41)

6.2
Quasi-conformal mappings

Next we recall some fundamental facts on quasi-conformal mappings stated in
[
1
]
to understand the last sentence of
[
2
, 2.4]
‘It has been shown by various authors…’ in detail.
We denote by
\mu(f):=f_{\overline{z}}/f_{z}
the complex dilatation of a quasi-conformal mapping
f:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
g
and
h:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
be quasi-conformal mappings.
Then the chain rule implies

\mu(h)\circ g=\frac{\mu(h\circ g)-\mu(g)}{1-\overline{\mu(g)}\mu(h\circ g)}\,\frac{g_{z}}{\overline{g}_{\overline{z}}}

(42)

[
1
, p.9, (6)]
. If we define

A^{*}\mu:=(\mu\circ A)\frac{\overline{A^{\prime}}}{A^{\prime}}

for
A\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mu\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{C}})
,
we have
\mu(f\circ A)=A^{*}(\mu(f))
and

\mu(f\circ A\circ f^{-1})\circ f=\frac{A^{*}(\mu(f))-\mu(f)}{1-\overline{\mu(f)}A^{*}(\mu(f))}\,\frac{f_{z}}{\overline{f}_{\overline{z}}}

by (
42
).
Hence
f\circ A\circ f^{-1}\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is equivalent to
A^{*}(\mu(f))=\mu(f)
.

Suppose
\nu\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\mathbb{C}})
satisfies
A^{*}\nu=\nu
.
For
|t|\ll 1
, there is a unique quasi-conformal mapping
f^{t\nu}:\hat{\mathbb{C}}\to\hat{\mathbb{C}}
which fixes
0
,
1
and
\infty
pointwise, and satisfies
\mu(f^{t\nu})=t\nu
. We have
A^{t\nu}:=f^{t\nu}\circ A\circ(f^{t\nu})^{-1}\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Similarly to
[
1
, p.77]
, we denote

\overset{\cdot}{F}[\nu]:=\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{t=0}F(t\nu)

for a function
F(t\nu)
of
t\nu
.
We have
\overset{\cdot}{A}[\nu]A^{-1}\in{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
.
Some straight-forward computation shows that

\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{t=0}(f^{t\nu})^{-1}(z)=-\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu](z),

and so that

\overset{\cdot}{A}[\nu](z)=\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu](Az)-(A^{\prime}(z))\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu](z).

Since
A^{\prime}\circ A^{-1}=((A^{-1})^{\prime})^{-1}
by the chain rule,
we obtain

\overset{\cdot}{A}[\nu](A^{-1}z)=\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu](z)-\left(((A^{-1})^{\prime})^{-1}\cdot(\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu]\circ A^{-1})\right)(z),

or equivalently

\imath\left(\overset{\cdot}{A}[\nu]\circ A^{-1}\right)=\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu]({\partial}/{\partial z})-(A^{-1})^{*}\left(\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu]({\partial}/{\partial z})\right).

(43)

On the other hand, as was shown in
[
1
, p.60]
, we have

\overset{\cdot}{f}[\nu](\zeta)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int\hskip-7.0pt\int_{\mathbb{C}}\nu(z)R(z,\zeta)dxdy,

(44)

where

R(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{z-\zeta}-\frac{\zeta}{z-1}+\frac{\zeta-1}{z}=\frac{\zeta(\zeta-1)}{z(z-1)(z-\zeta)}.

Now let
{\lambda}(z)({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
, be a
\Gamma
-invariant Beltrami differential, whose Dolbealt class
[{\lambda}(z)({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}]
is a tangent vector of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
at a point corresponding to
\Gamma
.
We define

\hat{\lambda}(z):=\begin{cases}{\lambda}(z),&\text{if $z\in\mathbb{H}$},\\
0,&\text{if $\Im z=0$},\\
\overline{{\lambda}(\overline{z})},&\text{if $z\in\mathbb{L}$},\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\lambda}(z):=\begin{cases}{\lambda}(z),&\text{if $z\in\mathbb{H}$},\\
0,&\text{if $\Im z\leq 0$}.\end{cases}

Since
f^{t\hat{\lambda}}(\mathbb{H})=\mathbb{H}
for any
|t|\ll 1
, we have
f^{t\hat{\lambda}}\Gamma(f^{t\hat{\lambda}})^{-1}\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, which equals the deformation of the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
along the path
t{\lambda}
.
Hence the map

\hat{c}({\lambda}):A\in\Gamma\mapsto\overset{\cdot}{A}[\hat{\lambda}]A^{-1}\in{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}

is a well-defined cocycle, whose cohomology class
[\hat{c}({\lambda})]\in H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{Ad}})
is identified with the tangent vector
[{\lambda}]
. Hence the Atiyah-Bott-Goldmann symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}
is given by

\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}([{\lambda}_{1}(z)({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}],[{\lambda}_{2}(z)({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}])=B_{*}([\hat{c}({\lambda}_{1})]\cup[\hat{c}({\lambda}_{2})])\cdot[M]

(45)

for any
\Gamma
-invariant Beltrami differentials
{\lambda}_{1}({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}
and
{\lambda}_{2}({\partial}/{\partial z})\otimes d\overline{z}
.

The Shimura isomorphism is directly related to
the quasi-Fuchsian group
f^{t\tilde{\lambda}}\Gamma(f^{t\tilde{\lambda}})^{-1}\subset PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The cohomology class
[\tilde{c}({\lambda})]\in H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathrm{Ad}})=H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{Ad}})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
defined by the cocycle

\tilde{c}({\lambda}):A\in\Gamma\mapsto\overset{\cdot}{A}[\tilde{\lambda}]A^{-1}\in{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}

satisfies

[\hat{c}({\lambda})]=2\Re[\tilde{c}({\lambda})]\in H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{Ad}}).

(46)

In fact, we have

\overset{\cdot}{f}[\hat{\lambda}](\zeta)=\overset{\cdot}{f}[\tilde{\lambda}](\zeta)+\overline{\overset{\cdot}{f}[\tilde{\lambda}](\overline{\zeta})}

from (
44
), and so

\hat{c}({\lambda})=\tilde{c}({\lambda})+\overline{\tilde{c}({\lambda})}

from (
43
).

Based on Hejhal’s result
[
5
]
, Wolpert
[
18
]
relates the cohomology class
[\tilde{c}({\lambda})]\in H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}_{\mathrm{Ad}})
to the Shimura isomorphism.
It should be remarked that
\overline{\xi(\overline{z})}dz^{\otimes 2}
,
z\in\mathbb{L}
, is a
\Gamma
-holomorphic quadratic differential for any
\Xi=\xi(z)dz^{\otimes 2}\in H^{0}(M;K^{\otimes 2})
.
We set
{\lambda}(z)={\lambda}_{\Xi}(z)=(\Im z)^{2}\overline{\xi(z)}
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
, i.e.,

{\lambda}_{\Xi}(z)d\overline{z}\otimes({\partial}/{\partial z})=\hat{h}(\Xi)
.
Then we have
[\hat{h}(\Xi)]=\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Xi})
as tangent vectors of the Teichmüller space.
For
|t|\ll 1
,
f^{t\tilde{\lambda}}
equals
w^{t}
for
\varphi=0
and
\psi(z)=-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\xi(\overline{z})}
in
[
18
]
. Fix a point

As was deduced by Wolpert
[
18
, §4]
from Hejhal’s result
[
5
]
,
there is a path
N^{t}\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for

such that

((N^{0})^{-1}N^{t})w^{t}A(w^{t})^{-1}((N^{0})^{-1}N^{t})^{-1}=A-\frac{t}{4}\int^{A\overline{z_{0}}}_{\overline{z_{0}}}\overline{\xi(\overline{z})}\begin{pmatrix}{-z}&{z^{2}}\\
{-1}&{z}\end{pmatrix}Adz+O(t^{2})

In other words, the complex conjugate of the cocycle
u^{\prime}_{\Xi}

(
40

\tilde{c}({\lambda})
for
{\lambda}(z)=(\Im z)^{2}\overline{\xi(z)}

Hence, from (

[\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Xi})]=2\Re[u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\in H^{1}(\Gamma;{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{Ad}}).

(47)

We have
[u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\cup[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]=0

[\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Xi})]\cup[\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Psi})]=([u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]+\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]})\cup([u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]+\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]})=2\Re([u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\cup\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]}).

From (

\omega_{\mathrm{ABG}}([\hat{h}(\Xi)],[\hat{h}(\Psi)])=B_{*}\left([\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Xi})]\cup[\hat{c}({\lambda}_{\Psi})]\right)\cdot[M]=2\Re B_{*}([u^{\prime}_{\Xi}]\cup\overline{[u^{\prime}_{\Psi}]})=2\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}([\hat{h}(\Xi)],[\hat{h}(\Psi)]).

\Xi\mapsto[\hat{h}(\Xi)]

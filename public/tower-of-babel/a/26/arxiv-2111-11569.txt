Fourier Transformable Measures with Meyer set support and their lift to the cut and project scheme
Nicolae Strungaru

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that given a cut-and-project scheme ( G , H , ℒ ) (G,H,\mathcal{L}) and a compact window W ⊆ H W\subseteq H , the natural projection gives a bijection between the Fourier transformable measures on G × H G\times H supported inside the strip ℒ ∩ ( G × W ) {\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times W) and the Fourier transformable measures supported inside ⋏ ( W ) \mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W) , and relate their Fourier transforms. We use this formula to relate the Fourier transforms of the measures, and explain how one can use this relation to re-derive some known results about Fourier analysis of measures with Meyer set support.

1.
Introduction

After the discovery of quasi-crystals
[
39
]
, it has become clear that we need to better understand the process of diffraction. Mathematically, the diffraction pattern of a solid can be viewed as the Fourier transform
\widehat{\gamma}
of the autocorrelation measure
\gamma
of the structure (see
[
14
]
for the setup and the monographs
[
3
,
4
]
for a general review of the theory). The measure
\gamma
is positive definite and therefore it is Fourier transformable as a measure
[
1
,
8
,
31
]
with positive Fourier transform
\widehat{\gamma}
. It is this measure
\widehat{\gamma}
which models the diffraction of our solid.

Structures with pure point diffraction, that is structures for which
\widehat{\gamma}
is a pure point measure are now very well understood. Building on the earlier work of Gil deLamadrid–Argabright
[
10
]
, Solomyak
[
40
,
41
]
, Lee–Moody–Solomyak
[
20
]
, Baake–Moody
[
7
]
, Baake–Lenz
[
6
]
, Gouere
[
12
,
11
]
, Moody–Strungaru
[
30
]
, Meyer
[
27
]
, pure point diffraction was characterized in
[
22
,
23
]
. The focus now shifted towards models with mixed diffraction spectrum, and especially those with a large pure point part.

The best mathematical models for Delone sets with a large pure point spectrum and (generic) positive entropy are Meyer sets. They have been introduced in the pioneering work of Meyer
[
26
]
, and popularized in the area of Aperiodic Order by Moody
[
28
,
29
]
and Lagarias
[
18
,
19
]
. They are usually constructed via a cut and project scheme (or simply CPS) and can be characterised via harmonic analysis, discrete geometry, algebra and almost periodicity
[
26
,
29
,
46
]
. The basic idea behind a CPS is to project points from a higher dimensional lattice which lie within a bounded strip of the real space(see Def.
2.8
below for the exact definition). If the width of the strip (called the window) is regular, then the resulting model set is pure point diffractive
[
13
,
38
,
7
,
35
]
. Recent work shown that this holds for a larger class of weak model sets
[
5
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
49
]
.

As subsets of regular model sets, Meyer sets still exhibit a large pure point spectrum
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
48
]
and a highly ordered continuous spectrum
[
43
,
45
,
47
,
48
]
. The long range order of the spectrum of Meyer sets is inherited from a covering regular model set
[
47
,
48
]
, which in turn is a residue of the Poisson summation formula for the lattice in the CPS
[
3
,
25
,
34
,
35
]
.

One would expect to be possible to related the diffraction of a Meyer set (or more generally a measure with Meyer set support) directly to the lattice
{\mathcal{L}}
in the CPS. It is the goal of this paper to establish this connection. Let us briefly explain our approach.

Fix a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
and a compact set
W\subseteq H
. It is easy to see that

\gamma=\sum_{x\in\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)}c_{x}\delta_{x}\qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad\eta=\sum_{x\in\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)}c_{x}\delta_{(x,x^{\star})}

establishes a bijection between translation bounded measures supported inside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
and translation bounded measures supported inside
{\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times W)
.
We first show in Prop.
3.5
that
\gamma
is positive definite if and only if
\eta
is positive definite. Since each Fourier transformable measure supported inside a Meyer set can be written as a linear combination of positive definite measures supported inside a common model set, we use this to establish in Thm.
4.1
that
\gamma
is Fourier transformable if and only if
\eta
is Fourier transformable, and relate their Fourier transform (see (
4.1
)).

We complete the paper by discussing in Section
5
how these results can be used to re-derive the known properties of diffraction for measures with Meyer
set support, and potentially used to prove new results.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Throughout the paper,
G
denotes a second countable locally compact Abelian group (LCAG). By
C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
we denote the space of uniformly continuous and bounded functions on
G
. This is a Banach space with respect to the sup norm
\|.\|_{\infty}
. As usual, we denote by
C_{0}(G)
the subspace of
C_{\mathsf{u}}(G)
consisting of functions vanishing at infinity, and by
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
the subspace of compactly supported continuous functions. Note that
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
is not complete in
(C_{\mathsf{u}}(G),\|.\|_{\infty})
.

In the spirit of
[
10
]
we denote by

K_{2}(G):=\mbox{Span}\{\varphi*\psi:\varphi,\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\}\,.

Given two LCAG’s
G
and
H
and two functions
g:G\to{\mathbb{C}},h:H\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we denote by
g\odot h:G\times H\to{\mathbb{C}}
their tensor product

(g\odot h)(x,y)=g(x)h(y)\,.

It is obvious that whenever
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G),\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(H)
we have
\varphi\odot\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G\times H)
. Moreover, if
\varphi\in K_{2}(G)
and
\psi\in K_{2}(H)
, we have
\varphi\odot\psi\in K_{2}(G\times H)
.

[
3
,

.

2.1.
Measures

In the spirit of Bourbacki
[
9
]
, by a measure we understand a linear functional on
C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
which is continuous with respect to the inductive topology. This notion corresponds to the classical concept of a Radon measure (see
[
35
, Appendix]
). For the case
G={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a clear exposition of this is given in
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A linear functional
\mu:C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is called a
Radon measure
if for each compact set
K\subseteq G
there exists a constant
C_{K}
such that, for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
\mbox{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subseteq K
we have

\left|\mu(\varphi)\right|\leq C_{K}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\,.

We will often write
\int_{G}\varphi(t)d\mu(t)
instead of
\mu(\varphi)
.

\mu
is called
positive
if for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
\varphi\geq 0
we have
\mu(\varphi)\geq 0
.

By the Riesz representation Theorem
[
37
]
, a positive Radon measure is simply a positive regular Borel measure. Moreover, each Radon measure is a linear combination of (at most four) positive Radon measures
[
35
, Appendix]
.

Next, we review the total variation of a measure.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a measure
\mu
, we can define
[
32
,
33
,
35
]
a positive measure
\left|\mu\right|
, called the
total variation
of
\mu
, such that for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
with
\varphi\geq 0
we have

\left|\mu\right|(\varphi)=\sup\{\left|\mu(\psi)\right|:\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G),\mbox{ with }|\psi|\leq\varphi\}\,.

We are now ready to introduce the concept of translation boundedness for measures and norm almost periodicity.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A\subseteq G
be a fixed pre-compact set with non-empty interior. We define the

A
-norm
of
\mu
via

\|\mu\|_{A}:=\sup_{x\in G}\left|\mu\right|(x+A)\,.

A measure
\mu
is called
translation bounded
if
\|\mu\|_{A}<\infty
.

Remark 2.4
.

[
7
,
42
]
Different pre-compact sets
A_{1},A_{2}
with non-empty interior define equivalent norms. Therefore, the definition of translation boundedness does not depend on the choice of
A
.

This allows us to define

{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G):=\{\mu:\mu\mbox{ is a translation bounded measure }\}\,.

Then
({\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G),\|.\|_{A})
is a normed space. It is in fact a Banach space
[
36
]
.

Next we review the definition of norm almost periodicity as introduced in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A\subseteq G
be a fixed pre-compact set with non-empty interior. A measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G)
is called
norm almost periodic
if for each
\varepsilon>0
the set

P_{\varepsilon}^{A}(\mu):=\{t\in G:\|T^{t}\mu-\mu\|_{A}<\varepsilon\}\,,

of
\varepsilon
-norm almost periods
of
\mu
is relatively dense.

As discussed above, different pre-compact sets define equivalent norms. This means that while the set of
\varepsilon
-norm almost periods on
\mu
depends on the choice of
A
, the almost periodicity of
\mu
is not dependent of this choice.

Any norm almost periodic measure is strongly almost periodic
[
7
]
, and the two concepts are equivalent for measures with Meyer set support
[
7
]
. In general, norm almost periodicity is an uniform version of strong almost periodicity
[
42
, Thm. 4.7]
. The class of norm almost periodic pure point measure was studied in detail and characterized in
[
46
]
.

Let us next recall positive definiteness for functions and measures. For more details, we recommend
[
8
,
31
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

A function
f:G\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is called
positive definite
if, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in G
, the
matrix
\left(f(x_{k}-x_{l})\right)_{k,l=1,\ldots,n}
is positive Hermitian. This is equivalent to

\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\overline{c_{l}}f(x_{k}-x_{l})c_{k}\geq 0\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in G,c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}

A measure
\mu
is called
positive definite
if for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)
we have

\mu(\varphi*\widetilde{\varphi})\rangle\geq 0\,.

This is equivalent to
\mu*\varphi*\tilde{\varphi}
being a positive definite function for all
\varphi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(G)

[
8
,
31
]
.

We complete the subsection by reviewing the notion of Fourier transformability for measures. For a more detailed review of the subject, we recommend
[
31
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

A measure
\mu
on
G
is called
Fourier transformable
if there exists a measure
\widehat{\mu}
on
\widehat{G}
such that, for all
\varphi\in K_{2}(G)
we have
|\check{\varphi}|\in L^{1}(|\widehat{\mu}|)
and

\int_{G}\varphi(t)\mbox{\rm d}\mu(t)=\int_{\widehat{G}}\widecheck{\varphi}(\chi)\mbox{\rm d}\widehat{\mu}(\chi)\,.

2.2.
Cut and Project Schemes and Meyer sets

In this part, we review some notions related to the cut and project formalism. For more details, we recommend
[
3
,
28
,
29
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

By a
cut and project scheme
(or simply
CPS
) we understand a triple
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
consisting of a
\sigma
-compact LCAG
G
, a LCAG
H
, and a lattice
{\mathcal{L}}\subset G\times H
such that

(i)

\pi_{H}({\mathcal{L}})
is dense in
H
.

(ii)

the restriction
\pi_{G}|_{\mathcal{L}}
of the first projection
\pi_{G}
to
{\mathcal{L}}
is one to one.

Given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
we will denote by
L:=\pi_{G}({\mathcal{L}})
. Then,
\pi_{G}
induces a group isomorphism between
{\mathcal{L}}
and
L
. Composing the inverse of this with the second projection
\pi_{H}
we get a mapping

\star:L\to H

which we will call the
\star
-mapping
. We then have

{\mathcal{L}}=\{(x,x^{\star}):x\in L\}\,.

Given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
and a subset
W\subseteq H
we can define

\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W):=\{x\in L:x^{\star}\in W\}\,.

When
W
is compact, we will call
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
a
weak model set
. If furthermore
W
has non-empty interior
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
is called a
model set
.

Next, let us review the concept of a Meyer set, which plays a fundamental role in this paper.

Definition 2.9
.

A set
\Lambda\subset G
is called a
Meyer set
if
\Lambda
is relatively dense and
\Lambda-\Lambda-\Lambda
is uniformly discrete.

For equivalent characterisations of Meyer sets see
[
18
,
19
,
26
,
28
,
46
]
. Of importance to us will be the following result.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
46
]
Let
\Lambda\subseteq G
be relatively dense. Then
\Lambda
is Meyer if and only if it is a subset of a (weak) model set.

Moreover, if
\Lambda
is Meyer, it is a subset of a weak model set in a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
with metrisable and compactly generated
H
.

Given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
, the map

(2.1)

L\ni x\to(x,x^{\star})\in{\mathcal{L}}\,.

is a group isomorphism, and hence it induces an isomorphism between the spaces of (bounded) functions on
L
and
{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively. Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is a discrete group, the space of (translation) bounded measures on
{\mathcal{L}}
can be identified with the space of (bounded) functions on
{\mathcal{L}}
. On another hand
L
is typically dense in
G
, and many functions on
L
do not induce pure point measures.

For us, of interest will be measures supported inside weak model sets
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
. Since
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
is uniformly discrete
[
28
]
, the space of (translation) bounded measures on
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
can be identified with the space of (bounded) functions on, and corresponds via the above isomorphism with the spaces of (translation) bounded measures or (bounded) functions on
{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively, that are supported inside
G\times W
.

Our focus in this paper is on these two spaces. We will study them as spaces of measures, and we will be interested in the relation between the Fourier theory of these two spaces, and the behaviour of the Fourier transform
with respect to the isomorphism induced by (
2.1
). For this reason, let us introduce the following notations.

Given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
and a compact set
W
we denote by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W))

\displaystyle:=\{\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G):\mbox{\rm supp}(\mu)\subseteq\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}({\mathcal{L}};W)

\displaystyle:=\{\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(G\times H):\mbox{\rm supp}(\nu)\subseteq\left({\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times W)\right)\}\,.

The isomorphism (
2.1
) induces a bijection
\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W))\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}({\mathcal{L}};W)

\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\mu)=\sum_{(x,x^{\star})\in{\mathcal{L}}}\mu(\{x\})\delta_{(x,x^{\star})}\,,

with inverse
\mathcal{DE}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}:{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}({\mathcal{L}};W)\to{\mathcal{M}}^{\infty}(\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W))

\mathcal{DE}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\nu)=\sum_{(x,x^{\star})\in{\mathcal{L}}}\nu(\{(x,x^{\star})\})\delta_{x}\,.

We will refer to these mappings as the
lift operator
and the
descent operator
, respectively.

The main results in this paper are that these operators are bijections between the subspaces of Fourier transformable (or cones of positive definite) measures, and relate their Fourier transform.

To understand the connection between the Fourier transforms, let us recall the notion of dual CPS. Given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
, we can define

{\mathcal{L}}^{0}:=\{(\chi,\psi)\in\widehat{G}\times\widehat{H}:\chi(x)\psi(x^{\star})=1\,\forall x\in L\}\,.

Then,
(\widehat{G},\widehat{H},{\mathcal{L}}^{0})
is a CPS
[
5
,
28
,
29
,
46
]
. We will refer to this as the CPS
dual to
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
.

3.
Positive definite measures with Meyer set support

In this section, we show that
\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}
and
\mathcal{DE}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}
take positive definite measures to positive definite measures.

Let us start with the following obvious Lemma, which follows immediately from Def.
2.6
and the fact that the function from (
2.1
) is a group isomorphism.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a CPS and let
f:L\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a function. Define
g:{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
via

g(x,x^{\star}):=f(x)\,.

Then
f
is positive definite on
L
if and only if
g
is positive definite on
{\mathcal{L}}
. ∎

Next, we prove a slight generalisation of
[
24
, Lemma 2.10]
and
[
3
, Lemma 8.4]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma
be a positive definite pure point measure on
G
, and let
L
be any subgroup of
G
. Then, the function
g:L\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined via

g(x):=\gamma(\{x\})\,,

is a positive definite function on
L
.

Proof.

Define
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
via

f(x):=\gamma(\{x\})\,.

Then
f
is a positive definite function on
G

[
24
, Prop 2.4]
. Then, Def.
2.6
immediately gives that the restriction
g=f|_{L}
to the subgroup
L
is a positive definite function on
L
.
∎

We will also need the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be any group and let
H\leq G
be a subgroup. Let
f:H\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a positive definite functions. Then, the function
g:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined via

g(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c}f(x)&\mbox{ if }x\in H\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

is positive definite on
G
.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in G
and
c_{1},..,c_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that
g(x_{k}-x_{l})=0
whenever
x_{k}-x_{l}\notin H
.

On
G
define the standard equivalence
\pmod{H}
as

x\equiv y\pmod{H}\Leftrightarrow x-y\in H\,.

This induces an equivalence relation on the set
\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
, and hence we can partition this set in equivalence classes
F_{1},...,F_{m}
.

To make the computation clearer, define
c:G\to{\mathbb{C}}

c(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{j}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{j}\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.\,.

Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}g(x_{k}-x_{l})c_{k}\overline{c_{l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}g(x_{k}-x_{l})c(x_{k})\overline{c(x_{l})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{x\in F_{i}}\sum_{y\in F_{j}}g(x-y)c(x)\overline{c(y)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{x,y\in F_{i}}g(x-y)c(x)\overline{c(y)}\right)\,.

Now, fix some
1\leq i\leq m
, and let
F_{i}:=\{z_{1},..,z_{q}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in F_{i}}g(x-y)c(x)\overline{c(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s=0}^{q}g(z_{r}-z_{s})c(z_{r})\overline{c(z_{s})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s=1}^{q}g(z_{r}-z_{s})c(z_{r})\overline{c(z_{s})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s=1}^{q}f\left((z_{r}-z_{1})-(z_{s}-z_{1})\right)c(z_{r})\overline{c(z_{s})}\geq 0

by the positive definiteness of
f
applied to
m;y_{1}:=z_{1}-z_{1};y_{2}:=z_{2}-z_{1};...;y_{q}:=z_{q}-z_{1}\in H
and
c^{\prime}_{1}=c(z_{1}),...,c^{\prime}_{q}=c(z_{q})
.

Therefore, for each
i
we have
\sum_{x,y\in C_{i}}g(x-y)c(x)\overline{c(y)}\geq 0
, and hence

\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}g(x_{k}-x_{l})c_{k}\overline{c_{l}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{x,y\in C_{i}}g(x-y)c(x)\overline{c(y)}\right)\geq 0\,.

∎

.

One can also prove Lemma

We are now ready to prove the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a CPS,
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
be a weak model set, and
f:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a function which vanishes outside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
.
Let

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)}f(x)\delta_{x}

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x,x^{\star})\in{\mathcal{L}}}f(x)\delta_{(x,x^{\star})}=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,L,W}(\gamma)\,.

Then
\gamma
is a positive definite measure on
G
if and only if
\eta=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\gamma)
is a positive definite measure on
G\times H
.

Proof.

\Rightarrow
: Denote as usual
L:=\pi_{G}({\mathcal{L}})
. Define
g:L\to{\mathbb{C}}
via

g(x):=\gamma(\{x\})\,,

that is
g=f|_{L}
.

Then, by Lemma
3.2
,
g
is a positive definite function on
L
and hence, by Lemma
3.1
, the function
h:{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

h(x,x^{\star})=g(x)

is a positive definite function on
{\mathcal{L}}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.3
, the function
j:G\times H\to{\mathbb{C}}

j(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c}h(z)&\mbox{ if }z\in{\mathcal{L}}\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

is positive definite on
G\times H
. The claim follows from
[
24
, Prop. 2.4]
.

\Leftarrow:
Since
\eta
is positive definite, by Lemma
3.2
the function
h:{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

h(x,x^{\star}):=\eta(\{(x,x^{\star})\})=f(x)\,,

is positive definite on
{\mathcal{L}}
and hence, by Lemma
3.1
, the restriction
g=f|_{L}
is positive definite on
L
. As
f
is zero outside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)\subseteq L
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
f
is a positive definite function on
G
. The claim follows now from
[
24
, Prop. 2.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

(a)

In Proposition
3.5
, the positive definiteness of
\eta
and
\gamma
is equivalent to the positive definiteness of the function

.

(b)

Denoting by

\displaystyle\mathcal{PD}({\mathcal{L}};W)

\displaystyle\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\mu)(\mathcal{PD}(\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)))

4.
The lift of Fourier transformable measures

We can now prove that, given a CPS
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
and a compact set
K
, the lifting operator induces a bijection between the space of Fourier transformable measures supported inside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
and the space of Fourier transformable measures supported inside
{\mathcal{L}}\cap(G\times W)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(G,H,{\mathcal{L}})
be a CPS and let
W\subseteq H
be compact. Let
\gamma
be a translation bounded measure supported inside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(W)
and let

\eta:=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\gamma)\,.

Then
\gamma
is Fourier transformable if and only if
\eta
is Fourier transformable.

Moreover, if
\varphi\in K_{2}(H)
is any function so that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
W
, then for all
\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
we have
\psi\otimes\hat{\varphi}\in L^{1}(\eta)
and

(4.1)

\widehat{\gamma}(\psi)=\widehat{\eta}(\psi\otimes\check{\varphi})=:(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\psi)\,.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
By
[
47
, Lemma 8.3]
, there exists a compact set
W\subseteq K
and four positive definite measures
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\omega_{4}
supported inside
\mbox{\LARGE$\curlywedge$}(K)
such that

\gamma=\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+i\omega_{3}-i\omega_{4}\,.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},W}(\gamma)=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\gamma)=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+i\omega_{3}-i\omega_{4})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{1})-\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{2})+i\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{3})-i\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{4})\,.

Now, by Prop.
3.5
, for all
1\leq j\leq 4
the measure
\mathcal{LI}_{G,H,{\mathcal{L}},K}(\omega_{j})
is positive definite. Therefore, as a linear combination
of positive definite measures,
\eta
is Fourier transformable.

\Longrightarrow
. Our argument is similar to
[
34
]
. We split the argument into two steps.

Step 1:
We show that
(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}
is a measure.

Let us first note that for all
\psi\in K_{2}(G)
we have
\psi\otimes\varphi\in K_{2}(G\times H)
. Therefore, since
\eta
is Fourier transformable, we have

(4.2)

\left|\check{\psi}\otimes\check{\varphi}\right|\in L^{1}(|\widehat{\eta}|)\,.

We now show that for all
\phi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
we have
\left|\phi\otimes\check{\varphi}\right|\in L^{1}(|\widehat{\eta}|)
and that

(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\phi):=\widehat{\eta}(\phi\otimes\check{\varphi})\,.

defines a measure.

Let
K\subseteq\widehat{G}
be a fixed compact set. Then, there exists some
\psi\in K_{2}(G)
such that
\check{\psi}\geq 1_{K}

[
8
,
31
]
.

Now, for all
\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
with
\mbox{\rm supp}(\psi)\subseteq K
we have

(4.3)

|\widehat{\eta}|\left(|\phi\otimes\check{\varphi}|\right)=\int_{G\times H}\left|\phi(s)\right|\cdot\left|\check{\varphi}(t)\right|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}|(s,t)\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\int_{G\times H}\left|\check{\psi}(s)\right|\cdot\left|\widehat{\varphi}(t)\right|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}|(s,t)<\infty

and hence
(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}
is well defined.

Moreover, for all
\psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
with
\mbox{\rm supp}(\psi)\subseteq K
, it follows from (
4.3
) that

\left|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\psi)\right|\leq C_{K}\|\psi\|_{\infty}

where

C_{K}:=\int_{G\times H}\left|\widehat{\psi}(s)\right|\cdot\left|\widehat{\varphi}(t)\right|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}|(s,t)<\infty\,.

This shows that
(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}
is a measure.

Step 2:
We show that for all
\psi\in K_{2}(G)
we have
|\widecheck{\psi}|\in L^{1}(|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|)
and

(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\widecheck{\psi})=\gamma(\psi)\,.

Let
\psi\in K_{2}(G)
be arbitrary.

Since
G
is second countable, so is
\widehat{G}

[
33
]
. In particular
\widehat{G}
is
\sigma
-compact
[
33
]
. Therefore, there exists a sequence
K_{n}
of compact sets with

K_{n}\subseteq(K_{n+1})^{\circ}
such that

\widehat{G}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}\,.

Let
\phi_{n}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
be so that
1_{K_{n}}\leq\phi_{n}\leq 1_{K_{n+1}}
.

Then,
\phi_{n}\widehat{\psi}\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\widehat{G})
and by the definition of
(\widecheck{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}
we have

(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi})=\widehat{\eta}\left((\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi})\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right)\,.

Now, for all
n
we have by (
4.2
)

\left|(\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi})\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right|\leq\left|\widecheck{\psi}\right|\otimes\left|\widecheck{\varphi}\right|\in L^{1}(|\widehat{\eta}|)

Therefore, by the Dominated convergence theorem (
[
33
, Thm. 3.2.51]
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}\left((\widecheck{\psi})\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\widehat{\eta}\left((\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi})\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right)=\lim_{n}(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}\left(\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}\right)\,.

Next, by the monotone convergence theorem
[
33
]
we have

|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|\left(|\widecheck{\psi}|\right)=\lim_{n}|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|\left(|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\right)\,.

Note that for each
n
we have

\displaystyle|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|\left(|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\left|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}(\Psi)\right|:\Psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\hat{G}),|\Psi|\leq|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\left|\widehat{\eta}\left(\Psi\otimes\check{\varphi}\right)\right|:\Psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\hat{G}),|\Psi|\leq|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\left|\int_{\hat{G}\times\hat{H}}\Psi(x)\check{\varphi}(y)\mbox{\rm d}\widehat{\eta}(x,y)\ \right|:\Psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\hat{G}),|\Psi|\leq|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\int_{\hat{G}\times\hat{H}}|\Psi(x)\check{\varphi}(y)|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}(x,y)|:\Psi\in C_{\mathsf{c}}(\hat{G}),|\Psi|\leq|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}|\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{G}\times\hat{H}}|\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}(x)\check{\varphi}(y)|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}(x,y)|\leq\int_{\hat{G}\times\hat{H}}|\widecheck{\psi}(x)\check{\varphi}(y)|\mbox{\rm d}|\widehat{\eta}(x,y)|

\displaystyle=|\widehat{\eta}|\left(|\widecheck{\psi}\otimes\check{\varphi}|\right)\,.

Since
\widecheck{\psi}\otimes\check{\varphi}\in L^{1}(|\widehat{\eta}|)
we get

|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|\left(|\widecheck{\psi}|\right)\leq|\widehat{\eta}|\left(|\widecheck{\psi}\otimes\check{\varphi}|\right)<\infty\,.

This shows that
|\widecheck{\psi}|\in L^{1}(|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|)
. Therefore,
\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}
is dominated by
|\widecheck{\psi}|\in L^{1}(|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|)
and converges pointwise to
\widecheck{\psi}
. Thus, by (
4.4
) and the dominated convergence theorem we get

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}\left((\widecheck{\psi})\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}\left(\phi_{n}\widecheck{\psi}\right)=(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}\left(\widecheck{\psi}\right)\,.

Finally, by the Fourier transformability of
\eta
we have

\widehat{\eta}\left(\widecheck{\psi}\otimes\widecheck{\varphi}\right)=\eta\left(\psi\otimes\varphi\right)=\gamma(\psi)\,.

Therefore, we proved that for all
\psi\in K_{2}(G)
we have
\widecheck{\psi}\in L^{1}(|(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}|)
and

(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}\left(\widecheck{\psi}\right)=\gamma(\psi)\,.

This proves that
\gamma
is Fourier transformable and

\widehat{\gamma}=(\widehat{\eta})_{\check{\varphi}}\,,

completing the proof.

∎

5.1.

5.3.

In this subsection we show a pseudo-compatibility of the mapping

of (

First, it is easy to see that the map satisfies

•

\rho

•

if

can have all three spectral components;

can be uniquely extended to a measure

We have seen in this section that the Fourier transform of a measure

we know much more about fully periodic measures in LCAG (see for example

is likely to transfer many properties from

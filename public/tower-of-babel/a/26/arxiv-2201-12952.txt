A

We will refer to a poset

Given a poset

This notion of poset containment allows us to relate a poset to

Given posets

If

Preliminaries

A
linear extension
of a poset
P
is a total (linear) order on the elements of
P
that agrees with all relations in
P
. Given a set
\mathcal{L}
of linear extensions of
P
, we say that
\mathcal{L}
is a
realiser
if for each pair
2
2

2

We will refer to ordered pairs as pairs when the order is clear.
of incomparable elements (
x,y
), there exists an extension
L\in\mathcal{L}
such that
x\geq_{L}y
. Then
\dim(P)
is also the minimum size of a realiser of
P
. One way to see this is by embedding
P
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and constructing a linear extension from each of the
d
coordinates.

We now discuss some basic properties of dimension. Given two posets
P
and
Q
, if
P
embeds into
Q
, then
\dim(P)\leq\dim(Q)
.

An important concept in the study of poset dimensions is the notion of critical pairs. A
critical pair
in a poset
P
is a pair of incomparable elements
(x,y)
such that
x
is minimal among elements incomparable to
y
and
y
is maximal among elements incomparable to
x
. We say that a linear extension
L
of
P

reverses
a critical pair
(x,y)
in
P
if
x\geq_{L}y
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a set of linear extensions of a (finite) poset
P
. Suppose that each critical pair in
P
is reversed by some extension in
\mathcal{L}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is a realiser of
P
.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)
be any pair of incomparable elements in
P
. As long as
(x,y)
is not a critical pair, we can decrease
x
or increase
y
while keeping the pair incomparable. Since
P
is finite, this means we can find a critical pair
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
such that
x\geq_{P}x^{\prime}
and
y\leq_{P}y^{\prime}
. Now there exists some
L\in\mathcal{L}
such that
x^{\prime}\geq_{L}y^{\prime}
, so we also have
x\geq_{L}y
as desired.
∎

Using the concept of a critical pair, we can see why we only care about the lowest and highest layers of a suborder of
\mathcal{Q}^{n}
. Let
0<k<\ell<n
and consider the poset
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]}
. In this poset the critical pairs are precisely the incomparable pairs of subsets
(X,Y)
where
\left|X\right|=k
and
\left|Y\right|=\ell
. So to construct a realiser for
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]}
it suffices to construct a realiser for the suborder
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{\{k,\ell\}}
. Thus we have that
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]})\leq\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{\{k,\ell\}})
. Additionally, since
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{\{k,\ell\}}
is a suborder of
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]}
, we have that
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{\{k,\ell\}})\leq\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]})
. Thus
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{\{k,\ell\}})=\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{[k,\ell]})
.

Critical pairs also explain why
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n})=n
. The critical pairs in
Q^{n}
are precisely the pairs
(\{x\},[n]\setminus\{x\})
for
x\in[n]
. There are
n
such critical pairs and a linear extension of
\mathcal{Q}^{n}
can reverse at most one of them. Thus
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\geq n
. We already know that
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq n
, so
\dim(\mathcal{Q}^{n})=n
.

Next we consider some basic poset constructions. Given posets
P
and
Q
, recall the product poset
P\times Q
, where
(p_{1},q_{1})\leq(p_{2},q_{2})
if and only if
p_{1}\leq_{P}p_{2}
and
q_{1}\leq_{Q}q_{2}
. Then we have that
\dim(P\times Q)\leq\dim(P)+\dim(Q)
by combining embeddings of
P
and
Q
into an embedding of
P\times Q
.

Another poset construction we will use is the disjoint union of two posets. Given posets
P
and
Q
, let
P\sqcup Q
be their disjoint union. Here we take the union of a copy of
P
and a copy of
Q
with all elements of
P
being incomparable with all elements of
Q
. As long as
P
and
Q
are nonempty,
P\sqcup Q
cannot be a chain, so
\dim(P\sqcup Q)\geq 2
. Next, let

and note that both

and

can be embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By appropriately translating these embeddings, we can construct an embedding of
P\sqcup Q
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So
\dim(P\sqcup Q)=\max(\dim(P),\dim(Q),2)
.

Next, to illustrate some of the techniques used in the proof of
Theorem

1.7
, we will prove the following easier result, which is already a small improvement upon the previous best known bound.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\kappa>1
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\pi(\kappa)
denote the number of primes less than or equal to

. Then we have

\dim(\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]})\leq\max(\pi(\kappa),2)=(1+o(1))\kappa(\log\kappa)^{-1}.

Proof.

The key idea is to write
\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]}
as a disjoint union of several posets.

Call a prime
p

small
if
p\leq\kappa
and
large
if
p>\kappa
. Let
K
be the set of all positive integers
M\leq N
with no small primes dividing

. For each
M\in K
, let

be the set of all positive integers of the form
Mq
, where

has only small prime divisors. Now, note that we have a set partition of
[N/\kappa,N]
given by

[N/\kappa,N]=\bigsqcup_{M\in K}g(M)\cap[N/\kappa,N].

Furthermore, elements of different sets in this partition are incomparable in
\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]}
, because if they
were comparable, their ratio would be divisible by a large prime. Thus
\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]}
is a disjoint union of posets of the form
\mathcal{D}_{g(M)\cap[N/\kappa,N]}
, and
it suffices to show that
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{g(M)\cap[N/\kappa,N]})\leq\pi(\kappa)
for any

For any given
M\in K
, we can embed
\mathcal{D}_{g(M)\cap[N/\kappa,N]}
into
\mathbb{R}^{\pi(\kappa)}
by mapping an integer

to its tuple of exponents of small primes. Applying the prime number theorem completes the proof.
∎

The main result of this paper is to provide a nontrivial bound on
\dim(\mathcal{D}_{g(M)\cap[N/\kappa,N]})
using new results on multisets. We first review some notation for multisets.

When referring to multisets, we always count size/cardinality with multiplicity. We denote the set of distinct elements of a multiset

\left|\supp\left(S\right)\right|
is the number of distinct elements of

S

is the set of elements which have greater multiplicity in
S
than in
T
. Also, we write
S\subseteq T
if the multiplicity of each element in
S
is at most its multiplicity in
T

\dim(\mathcal{M}_{[k_{M},\ell_{M}]}^{n,\vec{v}})\leq 2
. Otherwise, it can be checked (see
Appendix

A
) that with
r=4\frac{\log\kappa}{\log\log\kappa}
, we have
m(\vec{v},r)\geq 2\log(\kappa)
. So by
Theorem

1.8
,
\dim(\mathcal{M}_{[k_{M},\ell_{M}]}^{n,\vec{v}})\leq 43r^{2}\log n
. This holds for all choices of
M
, so we have that

\dim(\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]})\leq 43r^{2}\log n\leq 688\frac{(\log\kappa)^{3}}{(\log\log\kappa)^{2}},

which proves
Theorem

1.7
.

6.
Other Divisibility Orders

In this section we consider other divisibility orders. In some sense the poset
\mathcal{M}_{[k,\ell]}^{n,\vec{v}}
captures all possible normed divisibility orders subject to reasonable conditions. Specifically, given a (multiplicative) monoid with a norm and some notion of primes and unique factorization
4
4

4

We just need commutativity, cancellation, and finitely many elements with norm at most
N
for each
N
.
, we can construct a divisibility order on the elements with norms lying in an interval
[N/\kappa,N]
. Letting
\vec{v}
be the vector of the norms of primes with norm at most
\kappa
, we can then apply
Theorem

1.8
. If we have an understanding of the primes and the norm we can obtain a concrete bound, as in
Theorem

1.7
.

As another example of this principle, we analyze the divisibility order on monic polynomials over a finite field. As in the proof of
Proposition

2.2
,
P(\mathbb{F}_{q})_{[d_{0}-\delta,d_{0}]}
is isomorphic to a disjoint union of posets of the form
\mathcal{M}^{n,\vec{v}}_{k,\ell},
where
\ell-k=\delta
,
n
is the number of monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with degree at most
\delta
, and
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector of degrees of these
n
polynomials.

It can be checked (see
Appendix

B
) that
n\leq q^{\delta}
and that with
r=4.6\frac{\delta\log q}{\log\delta}
, we have
m(\vec{v},r)\geq 2\delta
. Additionally, since each entry of
\vec{v}
is at least
1
, we have
m(v,r)\geq 2\delta
with
r=2\delta
. These two choices of
r
handle the regimes when
q<\delta
and
q\geq\delta
, respectively. By applying
Theorem

1.8
, we obtain

\dim(P(\mathbb{F}_{q})_{[d_{0}-\delta,d_{0}]})\leq 43\left(\min\left(4.6\frac{\delta\log q}{\log\delta},2\delta\right)\right)^{2}\log(q^{\delta})\leq\min\left(910\frac{(\delta\log q)^{3}}{(\log\delta)^{2}},172\delta^{3}\log q\right).

This proves
Theorem

1.9
.

7.
Further Directions

Considering multiset posets was initially motivated by working on divisibility posets. However, multiset posets are a natural extension of subset posets and interesting on their own. It would be nice to see
Theorem

1.8
applied to problems not directly obtained from divisibility orders.

Although
Theorem

1.7
is a substantial improvement over previously known bounds, it is not quite tight with the lower bound given by Lewis and Souza. This lower bound cannot be improved without new ideas, since it is obtained by embedding the optimal subset poset of the form
\mathcal{Q}_{[1,\ell]}^{n}
into
\mathcal{D}_{[N/\kappa,N]}

[
7
]
. On the other hand, there are also reasons to believe that
Theorem

1.7
might be close to tight. The limiting factor in the upper bound is the dimension of the subset poset
\mathcal{Q}_{[1,3r]}^{n}
. In the regime when
r
is close to
\log n
, Kierstead showed that
\dim(\mathcal{Q}_{[1,3r]}^{n})
is close to
r^{3}

[
5
]
. Unfortunately the proof technique does not extend easily to the divisibility problem, but perhaps it could be modified with new ideas.

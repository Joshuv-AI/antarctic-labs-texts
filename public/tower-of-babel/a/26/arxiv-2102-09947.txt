Singular quasilinear convective elliptic systems in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N}
Umberto Guarnotta, Salvatore Angelo Marano

Abstract
The existence of a positive entire weak solution to a singular quasi-linear elliptic system with convection terms is established, chiefly through perturbation techniques, fixed point arguments, and a priori estimates. Some regularity results are then employed to show that the obtained solution is actually strong.



Keywords:
quasilinear elliptic system, gradient dependence, singular term, entire solution, strong solution.

AMS Subject Classification:
35J47, 35J75, 35B08, 35D35.

Introduction and main result

In this paper, we deal with the problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}u&=f(x,u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)\;\;&&\mbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta_{q}v&=g(x,u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)\;\;&&\mbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u,v&>0\;\;&&\mbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}\right.

(
{\rm P}
)

where

•

However, they give rise to some nontrivial difficulties, such as the loss of variational structure and the lack of compactness for Sobolev embedding. This work continues the study started in

Primarily, we need an appropriate functional framework where to treat the problem, mainly because the integrability properties of solutions and their gradients may differ at infinity, as the example in

Let

\mathcal{D}^{1,r}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{r^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Consequently, if

Hereafter,

To avoid cumbersome expressions, define

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

, and let

Proof.

Pick any

and

. Via
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]

, or
[

Lemma 4.2
.

We evidently have, for any

Due to the nature of

Using (

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\psi_{k,n}{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi_{n}{\rm d}x,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

If, to shorten notation,

Observe next that

\supp\psi_{k,n}\subseteq\overline{\supp\rho_{k}+\supp\varphi_{n}}\subseteq\overline{B_{1}+B_{2n}}\subseteq B_{2n+2}\quad\forall\,n,k\in\mathbb{N};

see

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\hat{f}\psi_{k,n}{\rm d}x&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{2n+2}}\hat{f}\psi_{k,n}{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{B_{2n+2}}\hat{f}\varphi_{n}{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\hat{f}\varphi_{n}{\rm d}x.\end{split}

On the other hand, the hypothesis
(u,v)\in X_{+}
distributional solution to (

Claim.

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi^{+}

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}&|\nabla\varphi_{n}-\nabla\varphi^{+}|^{p}{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi^{+}\nabla\theta_{n}+\theta_{n}\nabla\varphi^{+}-\nabla\varphi^{+}|^{p}{\rm d}x\\

Remark 4.5
.

Unfortunately, we were not able to find in the literature a definition of strong solution for elliptic equations driven by
non-linear
operators in divergence form. The one adopted here represents a quite natural extension of the semi-linear case
p=2
, where it is asked that the solution
u\in W^{2,2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and satisfies the differential equation a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
; cf.
[
13
, p. 219]
and
[
27
, pp. 7–8]
. We cannot expect
u\in W^{2,q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
q>1
, as the example of
[
5
, Remark 2.7]
shows. Nevertheless, if
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{+}
is a distributional solution to (

P

) then

once

; see
[
26
, p. 2]
. On the other hand, each strong solution turns out distributional. So, our notion of strong solution should be read as a distributional solution with an extra differentiability property on the fields
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u
and
|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v
.

Abstract
. Building on work of Holy, Lücke and Njegomir [ 30 ] on small embedding characterizations of large cardinals, we use some classical results of Baumgartner (see [ 7 ] and [ 9 ] ), to give characterizations of several well-known large cardinal ideals, including the Ramsey ideal, in terms of generic elementary embeddings; we also point out some seemingly inherent differences between small embedding and generic embedding characterizations of subtle cardinals. Additionally, we present a simple and uniform proof which shows that, when κ \kappa is weakly compact, many large cardinal ideals on κ \kappa are nowhere κ \kappa -saturated. Lastly, we survey some recent consistency results concerning the weakly compact ideal as well as some recent results on the subtle, ineffable and Π 1 1 \Pi^{1}_{1} -indescribable ideals on P κ ​ λ P_{\kappa}\lambda , and we close with a list of open questions.

((V_{\kappa},\in,A)\models\varphi)^{V}\implies((V_{\kappa},\in,A)\models\varphi)^{M}.

(3)

Proof.

3.2.
The subtle ideal

Recall that for
S\subseteq\kappa
we say that a sequence
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
is an
S
-list
if
S_{\alpha}\subseteq\alpha
for all
\alpha\in S
. Jensen and Kunen defined a set
S\subseteq\kappa
to be
subtle
if for every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
and for every club
C\subseteq\kappa
there exist
\alpha,\beta\in S\cap C
with
\alpha<\beta
such that
S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
. Although the least subtle cardinal is not weakly compact, every subtle cardinal is a stationary limit (and more) of cardinals which are
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable for every
n<\omega
(this follows from Lemma
3.12
below).

To show that the collection of non-subtle subsets of a subtle cardinal forms a normal ideal let us fix some notation. We let
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}:\mathop{{\rm ORD}}\times\mathop{{\rm ORD}}\to\mathop{{\rm ORD}}
denote the standard definable pairing function. If
\alpha
is a closure point of
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}
, meaning
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}[\alpha\times\alpha]\subseteq\alpha
, and if
\vec{A}=\langle A_{\xi}\mid\xi<\alpha\rangle
is a sequence of subsets of
\alpha
, then we can use
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}
to code the sequence into a single subset of
\alpha
:
A=[[\vec{A}]]=\{\mathop{\rm\Gamma}(\eta,\xi)\mid\eta\in A_{\xi}\}
. The following lemma was used in
[
7
]
and its proof is straightforward.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\mathop{{\rm ORD}}
are closure points of
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}
with
\alpha<\beta
, and further suppose that
A\subseteq\alpha
codes the sequence
\vec{A}=\langle A_{\xi}\mid\xi<\alpha\rangle
of subsets of
\alpha
and
B\subseteq\beta
codes the sequence
\vec{B}=\langle B_{\xi}\mid\xi<\beta\rangle
of subsets of
\beta
. In other words,
A=[[\vec{A}]]
and
B=[[\vec{B}]]
. Then it follows that if
A=B\cap\alpha
then
A_{\xi}=B_{\xi}\cap\alpha
for all
\xi<\alpha
.

Proposition 3.9

(Baumgartner
[
7
]
)
.

If
\kappa
is subtle then the collection

{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}=\{X\subseteq\kappa\mid\text{$X$ is not subtle}\}

is a normal ideal.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq\kappa
be subtle and suppose
f:S\to\kappa
is regressive. We must show that
f
is constant on a subtle subset of
S
, that is, we must show that for some
\eta<\kappa
we have
f^{-1}(\eta)\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}^{+}
. Suppose not. Let

D=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{$\alpha$ is closed under the pairing function $\mathop{\rm\Gamma}$}\}.

Then
D
is club and hence
S\cap D
is subtle. By assumption, for each
\eta<\kappa
, we may let
\vec{S}^{\eta}=\langle S^{\eta}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in f^{-1}(\eta)\rangle
be an
f^{-1}(\eta)
-list and let
C_{\eta}
be a club such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in f^{-1}(\eta)\cap C_{\eta}
with
\alpha<\beta
we have
S^{\eta}_{\alpha}\neq S^{\eta}_{\beta}\cap\alpha
.

Define an
S\cap D
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\cap D\rangle
by letting
S_{\alpha}=[[\langle S^{f(\alpha)}_{\alpha},\{f(\alpha)\}\rangle]]
. Let
C=\bigtriangleup_{\eta<\kappa}C_{\eta}
. Since
S\cap D
is subtle there exists
\alpha,\beta\in S\cap D\cap C
with
\alpha<\beta
and
S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
. Thus
[[\langle S^{f(\alpha)}_{\alpha},\{f(\alpha)\}\rangle]]=[[\langle S^{f(\beta)}_{\beta},\{f(\beta)\}\rangle]]\cap\alpha
and since
\alpha
and
\beta
are both closed under Gödel-pairing, it follows from Lemma
3.8
that
S^{f(\alpha)}_{\alpha}=S^{f(\beta)}_{\beta}\cap\alpha
and
f(\alpha)=f(\beta)
. If we let
\eta=f(\alpha)=f(\beta)
then we have
S^{\eta}_{\alpha}=S^{\eta}_{\beta}\cap\alpha
where
\alpha,\beta\in f^{-1}(\eta)\cap C_{\eta}
, a contradiction.
∎

In the following result we give a characterization of subtle sets in terms of elementary embeddings. The fact that (1) and (3) are equivalent in the following is due to Holy, Lücke and Njegomir
[
30
, Lemma 5.2]
; we provide a proof here since it will be referenced below in order to emphasize a difference in character between generic embedding and small embedding characterizations of large cardinal ideals.

Proposition 3.10
.

For all cardinals
\kappa
and all
S\subseteq\kappa
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

S\subseteq\kappa
is subtle

(2)

There is a generic elementary embedding
j:V\to M\subseteq V[G]
with critical point
\kappa
and
\kappa\in j(S)
such that for every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
and every club
C\subseteq\kappa
in
V
, we have
S_{\alpha}=j(\vec{S})(\kappa)\cap\alpha
for some
\alpha\in S\cap C
.

(3)

For every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
and for every club
C\subseteq\kappa
, there is a small embedding
j:M\to H(\kappa^{+})
for
\kappa
such that
S,C,\vec{S}\in\mathop{\rm ran}(j)
,
\mathop{\rm crit}(j)\in S
and
S_{\alpha}=S_{\mathop{\rm crit}(j)}\cap\alpha
for some
\alpha\in C\cap\mathop{\rm crit}(j)
.

Proof.

For (1) implies (3), suppose
S
is subtle,
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
is an
S
-list and
C\subseteq\kappa
is a club. Let
\langle X_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\rangle
be a continuous increasing sequence of elementary substructures of
H(\kappa^{+})
of cardinality less than
\kappa
with
\vec{S},C\in X_{0}
and
\alpha\subseteq X_{\alpha}\cap\kappa\in\kappa
for all
\alpha<\kappa
. Then
D=\{\alpha\in C\mid\alpha=X_{\alpha}\cap\alpha\}
is club in
\kappa
and since
S\cap D
is subtle there are
\alpha,\beta\in S\cap D
with
\alpha<\beta
and
S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
. Let
\pi:X_{\beta}\to M
be the transitive collapse of
X_{\beta}
. Then
j=\pi^{-1}:M\to H(\kappa^{+})
is a small embedding for
\kappa
with
\mathop{\rm crit}(j)=\beta
,
\vec{S}
,
C\in\mathop{\rm ran}(j)
and
S_{\alpha}=S_{\mathop{\rm crit}(j)}\cap\alpha
. The converse, namely (3) implies (1), is quite straight forward.

To see that (1) implies (2), suppose
S\subseteq\kappa
is subtle. Clearly
S
is stationary in
\kappa
. Let
G\subseteq P(\kappa)/({\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\upharpoonright S)
be generic. Let

be the
V
-normal

-ultrafilter obtained from

and let

be the generic ultrapower by
U_{G}
. Then the critical point of

is
\kappa
and

since
S\in U_{G}
. Fix an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
and a club

. Let us show that the set

X=\{\xi\in S\mid(\exists\zeta\in S\cap C\cap\xi)\ S_{\zeta}=S_{\xi}\cap\zeta\}

is in
({\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\upharpoonright S)^{*}
; in other words, we will show that
\kappa\setminus X\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\upharpoonright S
, i.e.

. Since

(\kappa\setminus X)\cap S=\{\xi\in S\mid(\forall\zeta\in S\cap C\cap\xi)\ S_{\zeta}\neq S_{\xi}\cap\zeta\}

we have that
S_{\alpha}\neq S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
for all

with

. Hence
(\kappa\setminus X)\cap S
is not subtle. Thus

\kappa\in j(X)
and hence there is an
\alpha\in S\cap C
such that
S_{\alpha}=j(\vec{S})(\kappa)\cap\alpha
.

Conversely, for (2) implies (1), fix an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
and a club
C\subseteq\kappa
and suppose

is a generic elementary embedding with critical point
\kappa
such that
\kappa\in j(S)
and

for some

. Since
\alpha,\kappa\in j(S\cap C)

.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Let us point out that there is a substantial difference between the generic embedding characterization and the small embedding characterization of subtlety given in the previous proposition, which is not manifested in the embedding characterizations of stationarity. Namely, in the generic embedding characterization of the subtlety of
S\subseteq\kappa
, Proposition
3.10
(2), a single embedding
j
works for all
S
-lists and all clubs
C
. Whereas, in the small embedding characterization, Proposition
3.10
(3), for each
S
-list there is a small embedding with the desired property. This seems to be an inherent difference between these two characterizations because one cannot place all club subsets of
\kappa
into an elementary substructure
X_{0}\prec H(\theta)
while still guaranteeing that
X_{0}\cap\kappa\in\kappa
. Indeed, it is easy to see that for regular cardinals
\omega<\kappa<\theta
, if
X\prec H(\theta)
and
X\cap\kappa\in\kappa
then
X
does not contain all club subsets of
\kappa
. Suppose not. Let
X\prec H(\theta)
contain all club subsets of
\kappa
with
X\cap\kappa\in\kappa
. Then
\mathop{\rm crit}(j)=X\cap\kappa\in C
for all clubs
C\subseteq\kappa
, which is a contradiction.

As it will be needed below for a characterization of ineffability, let us present a result of Baumgartner, which shows that if
\kappa
is a subtle cardinal then there are many cardinals less than
\kappa
which are
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable for all
n<\omega
. Recall that for a cardinal
\kappa
,
S\subseteq\kappa
and an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
, a set
X\subseteq\kappa
is
homogeneous
for
\vec{S}
if and only if whenever
\alpha,\beta\in X
with
\alpha<\beta
we have
S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
.

Lemma 3.12

(Baumgartner
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\kappa
be a cardinal and
S\subseteq\kappa
. Assume
S
is subtle and
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
is an
S
-list. Let

\displaystyle A=\{\alpha\in S\mid\exists X\subseteq S\cap\alpha

such that
X
is
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable in
\alpha

\displaystyle\text{for all $n<\omega$ and $X$ is homogeneous for $\vec{S}$}\}

Then
S\setminus A
is not subtle.

Proof.

Suppose
S\setminus A
is a subtle subset of
\kappa
. Fix a bijection
b:V_{\kappa}\to\kappa
and let
C
be a club subset of
\kappa
such that for all
\alpha\in C
we have
b\upharpoonright V_{\alpha}:V_{\alpha}\to\alpha
is a bijection and
\alpha
is closed under the standard definable pairing function
\mathop{\rm\Gamma}:\kappa\times\kappa\to\kappa
. By normality of the subtle ideal, it follows that
E:=(S\setminus A)\cap C
is a subtle subset of
\kappa
. We define an
E
-list
\vec{E}
as follows. Fix
\beta\in E
and let
B_{\beta}=\{\alpha\in S\cap\beta\mid S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha\}
. Notice that
B_{\beta}\cup\{\beta\}
is homogeneous for
\vec{S}
. Since
\beta\in E
, it follows that for some
n<\omega
the set
B_{\beta}
is not
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable in
\beta
. Let
\varphi_{\beta}
be a
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-sentence and let
A_{\beta}\subseteq V_{\beta}
be such that
(V_{\beta},\in,\mathop{\rm\Gamma},A_{\beta})\models\varphi_{\beta}
while for all
\alpha\in B_{\beta}
we have
(V_{\alpha},\in,\mathop{\rm\Gamma}\upharpoonright\alpha,A_{\beta}\cap\alpha)\models\lnot\varphi_{\beta}
. Let
E_{\beta}=[[(S_{\beta},\{\varphi_{\beta}\},b[A_{\beta}])]]
. This defines an
E
-list
\vec{E}=\langle E_{\beta}\mid\beta\in E\rangle
.

Since
E
is subtle there exist
\alpha<\beta
in
E
such that
E_{\alpha}=E_{\beta}\cap\alpha
. It follows that
S_{\alpha}=S_{\beta}\cap\alpha
and thus
\alpha\in B_{\beta}
. Furthermore,
b[A_{\alpha}]=b[A_{\beta}]\cap\alpha
and hence
A_{\alpha}=A_{\beta}\cap V_{\alpha}
. Also we have
\varphi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\beta}=\varphi
. By definition of
\varphi_{\alpha}
we see that
(V_{\alpha},\in,\mathop{\rm\Gamma},A_{\alpha})\models\varphi
, and since
\alpha\in B_{\beta}
we have
(V_{\alpha},\in,\mathop{\rm\Gamma}\upharpoonright\alpha,A_{\beta}\cap V_{\alpha})\models\lnot\varphi
, a contradiction.
∎

3.3.
The almost ineffable and ineffable ideals

Given a regular cardinal
\kappa
and an ideal
I\supseteq[\kappa]^{<\kappa}
we define another ideal
{\mathcal{I}}(I)
by letting
S\notin{\mathcal{I}}(I)
if and only if for every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
there is a set
H\in P(S)\cap I^{+}
which is homogeneous for
\vec{S}
. We say that a set
S\subseteq\kappa
is
almost ineffable
if
S\in{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})^{+}
, that is, every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
has a homogeneous set
H\subseteq S
of size
\kappa
. Similarly,
S
is
ineffable
if
S\in{\mathcal{I}}({\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa})^{+}
, that is, every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
has a homogeneous set
H\subseteq S
which is stationary in
\kappa
. Baumgartner
[
7
]
showed that if
\kappa
is ineffable then the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa})
is nontrivial and normal, and similarly, if
\kappa
is almost ineffable then
{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})
is nontrivial and normal. The collection
{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})
is called the
almost ineffable ideal
and
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa})
is called the
ineffable ideal
. It is easy to see that for any ideal
I\supseteq[\kappa]^{<\kappa}
we have
S\in{\mathcal{I}}(I)^{+}
if and only if for every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
there is a set
D\subseteq\kappa
which is anticipated by
S
on a set in
I^{+}
, meaning that the set
\{\alpha\in S\mid S_{\alpha}=D\cap\alpha\}
is in
I^{+}
. This easily leads to the following characterization of the ineffability of a set
S\subseteq\kappa
; note that below we give another characterization of ineffability (see Proposition
3.25
).

Proposition 3.15
.

For all cardinals
\kappa
and
S\subseteq\kappa
the following are equivalent.

(1)

S
is ineffable.

(2)

For all
S
-lists
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
there is a generic elementary embedding
j:V\to M\subseteq M[G]
with critical point
\kappa
and
\kappa\in j(S)
such that
j(\vec{S})(\kappa)\in V
.

(3)

For all
S
-lists
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
there is a small embedding
j:M\to H(\kappa^{+})
for
\kappa
with
S,\vec{S}\in\mathop{\rm ran}(j)
and
\mathop{\rm crit}(j)\in S
such that
S_{\mathop{\rm crit}(j)}\in M
.

Proof.

The proof that (1) and (3) are equivalent is very similar to that of
[
30
, Lemma 5.5]
. For (1) implies (2), suppose
S
is ineffable, let
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
be an
S
-list and let

be such that the set
A=\{\alpha\in S\mid S_{\alpha}=D\cap\alpha\}
is stationary. Let

and let

be the corresponding generic ultrapower. Then,
\mathop{\rm crit}(j)=\kappa
and
\kappa\in j(S)
. Furthermore, since

\kappa\in j(A)
and hence

.

For (2) implies (1), fix an
S
-list

. We must show that
\vec{S}
has a homogeneous set that is stationary in

with critical point

and

such that

. Let

and

∎

By combining the arguments of Proposition

Proposition 3.16
.

For all
n<\omega
, all cardinals
\kappa
and all

, the following are equivalent.

(1)

S\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))^{+}

(2)

For all
S
-lists
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
there is a generic elementary embedding
j:V\to M\subseteq V[G]
with critical point
\kappa
and
\kappa\in j(S)
such that the following properties hold.

(a)

j(\vec{S})(\kappa)\in V

(b)

For all
R\in V_{\kappa+1}^{V}
and all
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-sentences
\varphi
we have

((V_{\kappa},\in,R)\models\varphi)^{V}\implies((V_{\kappa},\in,R)\models\varphi)^{M}.

Proof.

For (1) implies (2), suppose

and fix an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
. Let
D\subseteq\kappa
be such that the set
A=\{\alpha\in S\mid S_{\alpha}=D\cap\alpha\}

\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable. Let

\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa)\upharpoonright S

is a

\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa)^{*}\subseteq(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa)\upharpoonright A)^{*}\subseteq U_{G}
, the same argument as that of Proposition
3.5

Conversely, suppose (2) holds. Fix an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle

.

Notice that the case

Lemma 3.18

(Baumgartner
[
7
]
)
.

Suppose
\kappa
is a cardinal and
S\subseteq\kappa
is such that every
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
has a homogeneous set which is
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable where

. Then
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable.

Proof.

Let us first handle the case in which
n=-1
. Suppose every
S
-list has a homogeneous set
H
in
(\Pi^{1}_{-1}(\kappa))^{+}=([\kappa]^{<\kappa})^{+}
. In other words,
S
is almost ineffable. Let us show that
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-indescribable. Since the almost ineffability of
S
implies that
\kappa
is inaccessible, it suffices to show that
S
is weakly
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-indescribable (although showing that
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable in
\kappa
is not much harder using a bijection
b:V_{\kappa}\to\kappa
and a club
C\subseteq\kappa
of closure points of

). Suppose not. Then there is an
A\subseteq\kappa
and a
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-sentence
\forall X\psi(X)
such that
(\kappa,\in,A)\models\forall X\psi(X)
and for all
\alpha\in S
there is an
S_{\alpha}\subseteq\alpha
such that
(\alpha,\in,A\cap\alpha,S_{\alpha})\models\lnot\psi(S_{\alpha})
. Let
H\subseteq S
be homogeneous for
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
with
|H|=\kappa
and let
X=\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}S_{\alpha}
. Then
(\kappa,\in,A,X)\models\psi(X)
where
\psi(X)
is a
\Pi^{1}_{0}
sentence. Since
\kappa
is inaccessible and
S
is stationary, it follows that
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{0}
-indescribable. Thus, there is an
\alpha\in S
such that
(\alpha,\in,A\cap\alpha,X\cap\alpha)\models\psi(X\cap\alpha)
, but since

, this is a contradiction.

Now suppose
n\geq 0
and every
S
-list
\vec{S}
has a
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable homogeneous set

. Then
\kappa
is inaccessible, so to show that
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable, it suffices to show that
S
is weakly
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable. Suppose
S
is not weakly
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indscribable. Then there is an

and a
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}

\forall X\exists Y\psi(X,Y)
, where
\psi(X,Y)
is a
\Pi^{1}_{n}

(\kappa,\in,A)\models\forall X\exists Y\psi(X,Y)
and for all
\alpha\in S
there is an

such that
(\alpha,\in,A\cap\alpha,S_{\alpha})\models\forall Y\lnot\psi(S_{\alpha},Y)
. Then
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
is an
S
-list and we may let

be a
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable homogeneous set for

. Since
(\kappa,\in,A)\models\forall X\exists Y\psi(X,Y)

then there is a

(\kappa,\in,A,X,Y)\models\psi(X,Y)
. Since

is weakly
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable, and the set

C=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid(\alpha,\in,A\cap\alpha,X\cap\alpha,Y\cap\alpha)\models\psi(X,Y)\}

is in the weakly
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable filter, it follows that

is weakly
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable. Thus, there is some

(\alpha,\in,A\cap\alpha,S_{\alpha})\models\exists Y\psi(S_{\alpha},Y),

a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

Baumgartner showed that a cardinal
\kappa
is almost ineffable if and only if it is subtle,
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-indescribable, and the ideal generated by the subtle ideal together with the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-indescribable ideal is nontrivial and equals the almost ineffable ideal; moreover, the statement about the ideals cannot be removed from this characterization since the least cardinal which is subtle and
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-indescribable is not almost ineffable (this follows from Theorem
3.23
). Similarly,
\kappa
is ineffable if and only if it is subtle,
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-indescribable and the ideal generated by the subtle ideal and the
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-indescribable ideal is nontrivial and equals the ineffable ideal; as above, the statement about ideals cannot be removed from the characterization because the least subtle cardinal which is
\Pi^{1}_{2}
-indescribable is not ineffable (this follows from Theorem
3.23
). In fact, Baumgartner proved a more general result, which appears as Theorem
3.23
below.

Let us consider the ideals
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))
where
n\in\{-1\}\cup\omega
. Recall, using the notation introduced above,
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{-1}(\kappa))={\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})
is the almost ineffable ideal and
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{0}(\kappa))={\mathcal{I}}({\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa})
is the ineffable ideal. By definition, a set
S\subseteq\kappa
is in
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))^{+}
if and only if every
S
-list has a homogeneous set
H\subseteq S
which is
\Pi^{1}_{n}
-indescribable in
\kappa
. Since ineffability implies almost ineffability, which in turn implies subtlety, it is easy to see that

{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}({\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa})\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{1}(\kappa))\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{2}(\kappa))\subseteq\cdots.

In fact, by applying Lemma
3.18
, one can show that the above containments are proper when the ideals are nontrivial. Let us show that
{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\subsetneq{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa})
when
\kappa
is almost ineffable (see Corollary
3.22
below). Similar arguments, which are left to the reader, establish the remaining proper containments (for a more general result see
[
29
, Theorem 1.5]
).

Recall that if
S
is stationary in
\kappa
and for each
\alpha\in S
we have a set
S_{\alpha}
which is stationary in
\alpha
, then
\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}S_{\alpha}
is stationary in
\kappa
. The analog of this result also holds for large cardinal ideals. For example, the stationary limit of subtle cardinals is subtle by the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.20
.

Suppose
S\in{\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa}^{+}
and that for each
\alpha\in S
we have a set
S_{\alpha}\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\alpha}^{+}
. Then
\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}S_{\alpha}\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}^{+}
.

To show that
T=\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}S_{\alpha}
is subtle in
\kappa
, fix a
T
-list
\vec{T}=\langle T_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in T\rangle
and a club
C\subseteq\kappa
. Since
S
is stationary in
\kappa
, we may choose
\alpha\in S\cap C^{\prime}
. Since
S_{\alpha}
is subtle in
\alpha
and
C\cap\alpha
is a club subset of
\alpha
, there exist
\xi,\zeta\in S_{\alpha}\cap C\subseteq T\cap C
with
\xi<\zeta
such that
T_{\xi}=T_{\zeta}\cap\xi
. Thus,
T
is subtle in
\kappa
.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

If
\kappa
is subtle then the set

T=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{$\alpha$ is not subtle}\}

is subtle.

Proof.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose
\kappa
is the least counterexample. That is,
\kappa
is the least cardinal such that
\kappa
is subtle and
\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{$\alpha$ is not subtle}\}
is not subtle. Then
S:=\kappa\setminus T\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}^{*}
and hence
S\in{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}^{+}
. For each
\alpha\in S
, by the minimality of
\kappa
, the set
S_{\alpha}:=T\cap\alpha
is subtle in
\alpha
. Thus, by Lemma

T=\bigcup_{\alpha\in S}S_{\alpha}
is subtle in

∎

Corollary 3.22
.

Suppose
\kappa
is almost ineffable. Then

{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\subsetneq{\mathcal{I}}([\kappa]^{<\kappa}).

Proof.

Lemma
3.21
implies that
T=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{$\alpha$ is not subtle}\}\notin{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}
. Furthermore, by definition of subtlety, there is a
\Pi^{1}_{1}

\kappa

,

∎

Theorem 3.23

(Baumgartner
[
7
]
)
.

For all
n\in\{-1\}\cup\omega
, we have

(1)

\kappa
is subtle and
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable.

(2)

The ideal generated by the subtle ideal and the
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable ideal is a nontrivial normal ideal; in this case,

{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))=\overline{{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\cup\Pi^{1}_{n+2}(\kappa)}.

Furthermore, (2) cannot be removed from this characterization because the least cardinal
\kappa
which is subtle and
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable is in
{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))
.

Let
I=\overline{{\mathop{\rm NSub}}_{\kappa}\cup\Pi^{1}_{n+2}(\kappa)}
. We will show that
S\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))^{+}
if and only if
S\in I^{+}
.

Suppose
S\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))^{+}
. To show
S\in I^{+}
, it suffices to show
S
is subtle and
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable by Remark
2.2
. Clearly,
S
is subtle, and by Lemma
3.18
,
S
is
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-indescribable. Thus
S\in I^{+}
. Conversely, suppose
S\in I^{+}
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose
S\in{\mathcal{I}}(\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa))
. Then there is an
S
-list
\vec{S}=\langle S_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in S\rangle
such that every homogeneous set for
\vec{S}
is in the ideal
\Pi^{1}_{n}(\kappa)
. This is expressible by a
\Pi^{1}_{n+2}
-sentence
\varphi
over
(V_{\kappa},\in,\vec{S})
. Thus, it follows that the set

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid(V_{\alpha},\in,\vec{S}\cap V_{\alpha})\models\varphi\}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{every hom. set for $\vec{S}\upharpoonright\alpha$ is in $\Pi^{1}_{n}(\alpha)$}\}

\Pi^{1}_{n+2}(\kappa)^{*}

Remark 3.24

5.
Consistency results

Recently progress has been made in generalizing forcing constructions from the nonstationary ideal to the weakly compact ideal. Such generalizations often require a substantial amount of effort because there are some significant differences between stationarity and weak compactness: for example, one cannot force ground model nonstationary sets to become stationary in an extension, whereas, in some settings, ground model non–weakly compact sets can become weakly compact in after certain kinds of forcing
[
36
]
. The main reasons for the success in this direction are twofold. First, weak compactness has a simple characterization in terms of elementary embeddings which allows one to use standard techniques to argue that weak compactness is preserved after certain Easton-support forcing iterations (see
[

-indescribability and state a few open questions.

-indescribable

{\mathop{\rm Tr}}_{-1}(\kappa)=\{\alpha<\kappa\mid\text{$\alpha$ is a limit ordinal and $\sup(X\cap\alpha)=\alpha$}\}.

In this section we survey some results on two-cardinal versions of subtlety, ineffability and indescribability. Recall that, for cardinals

that, when it is nontrivial, the ideal

equals the minimal

, and which consists of all non–

(see Section

for more details regarding this result). This leads to a definition of

is

-indescribable if and only if

(see Section

In Section

Finally, in Section

6.1.

P_{\kappa}\lambda

Throughout this section we assume
\kappa\leq\lambda
are cardinals and
\kappa
is a regular cardinal. Recall that an ideal
I
on
P_{\kappa}\lambda
is
normal
if for every
X\in I^{+}
and every function
f:P_{\kappa}\lambda\to\lambda
with
\{x\in X\mid f(x)\in x\}\in I^{+}
there is a
Y\in P(X)\cap I^{+}
such that
f\upharpoonright Y
is constant. Equivalently, an ideal
I
on
P_{\kappa}\lambda
is normal if and only if for every
\{X_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq I
the set
\bigtriangledown_{\alpha<\kappa}X_{\alpha}=_{\mathop{\rm def}}\{x\mid\text{$x\in X_{\alpha}$ for some $\alpha\in x$}\}
is in

I
on
P_{\kappa}\lambda

if and only if
\{x\in P_{\kappa}\lambda\mid\alpha\in x\}\in I^{*}
for every
\alpha<\lambda

[

generalized the notion of closed unbounded and stationary subsets of cardinals to subsets
P_{\kappa}\lambda
and proved that the nonstationary ideal
{\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa\lambda}
is a normal fine
\kappa
-complete ideal on
P_{\kappa}\lambda

[
12
]
proved that, when
\kappa
is a regular cardinal, the nonstationary ideal
{\mathop{\rm NS}}_{\kappa,\lambda}
is the minimal normal fine
\kappa
-complete ideal on
P_{\kappa}\lambda
.

When considering ideals on
P_{\kappa}\lambda
for
\kappa

(P_{\kappa}\lambda,\subseteq)
with a different one. For
x\in P_{\kappa}\lambda
we define
\kappa_{x}=|x\cap\kappa|
and we define an ordering
(P_{\kappa}\lambda,<)
by letting

\text{$x<y$ if and only if $x\in P_{\kappa_{y}}y$}.

Given a function
f:P_{\kappa}\lambda\to P_{\kappa}\lambda

C_{f}=_{\mathop{\rm def}}\{x\in P_{\kappa}\lambda\mid x\cap\kappa\neq\varnothing\land f[P_{\kappa_{x}}x]\subseteq P_{\kappa_{x}}x\}.

We say that a set

is

if there is an

such that

. Moreover,

is called

if for every

. An ideal

on
P_{\kappa}\lambda
is

if for any

and function

such that

is constant. It follows easily that an ideal

P_{\kappa}\lambda
is strongly normal if and only if for any

6.3.
Subtle, strongly subtle and ineffable subsets of
P_{\kappa}\lambda

Menas
[
41
]
defined a set

to be

if for every club
C\subseteq P_{\kappa}\lambda
and every function
\vec{S}=\langle S_{x}\mid x\in S\rangle
where

for all

and

. Menas proved that no matter how large
\lambda

Theorem 6.10

(Menas
[
41
]
)
.

If
\kappa
is subtle then
P_{\kappa}\lambda
is subtle for all
\lambda\geq\kappa
.

Menas also showed that the subtlety of
P_{\kappa}\lambda

\diamondsuit_{\kappa,\lambda}
for all
\lambda\geq\kappa

P_{\kappa}\lambda
, where

\kappa
.

Theorem 6.11

(Usuba
[
47
]
)
.

Suppose
\lambda
is a measurable cardinal. Then there is a regular uncountable
\kappa<\lambda
such that
P_{\kappa}\lambda
is subtle but
\kappa
is not subtle.

As noted above, when studying
P_{\kappa}\lambda
combinatorics where

is inaccessible, it can be fruitful to shift attention from the structure

to the structure

where

if and only if

(see Section

above). Abe
[

]
defined a set

to be

if for every function

where

for

, and for every weak club

there exist

with

(meaning

) such that

. Abe proved
[

]
that the collection

\text{NSSub}_{\kappa,\lambda}=\{X\subseteq P_{\kappa}\lambda\mid\text{$X$ is not strongly subtle}\}

of non–strongly subtle subsets of

is a strongly normal ideal on

when it is nontrivial.
Although Abe’s definition of strong subtlety of

Theorem 6.12

(Abe
[
2
]
)

If
\kappa

is subtle for all

to characterize supercompactness. Carr
[

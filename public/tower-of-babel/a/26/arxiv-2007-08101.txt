The Sparse Hausdorff Moment Problem, with Application to Topic Models
Spencer L. Gordon, Bijan Mazaheri, Yuval Rabani, Leonard J. Schulman

Abstract
We consider the problem of identifying, from its first m m noisy moments, a probability distribution on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] of support k < ∞ k<\infty . This is equivalent to the problem of learning a distribution on m m observable binary random variables X 1 , X 2 , … , X m X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m} that are iid conditional on a hidden random variable U U taking values in { 1 , 2 , … , k } \{1,2,\dots,k\} . Our focus is on accomplishing this with m = 2 ​ k m=2k , which is the minimum m m for which verifying that the source is a k k -mixture is possible (even with exact statistics). This problem, so simply stated, is quite useful: e.g., by a known reduction, any algorithm for it lifts to an algorithm for learning pure topic models. We give an algorithm for identifying a k k -mixture using samples of m = 2 ​ k m=2k iid binary random variables using a sample of size ( 1 / w min ) 2 ⋅ ( 1 / ζ ) O ⁡ ( k ) \left(1/w_{\min}\right)^{2}\cdot\left(1/\zeta\right)^{O(k)} and post-sampling runtime of only O ⁡ ( k 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) O(k^{2+o(1)}) arithmetic operations. Here w min w_{\min} is the minimum probability of an outcome of U U , and ζ \zeta is the minimum separation between the distinct success probabilities of the X i X_{i} s. Stated in terms of the moment problem, it suffices to know the moments to additive accuracy w min ⋅ ζ O ⁡ ( k ) w_{\min}\cdot\zeta^{O(k)} . It is known that the sample complexity of any solution to the identification problem must be at least exponential in k k . Previous results demonstrated either worse sample complexity and worse O ⁡ ( k c ) O(k^{c}) runtime for some c c substantially larger than 2 2 , or similar sample complexity and much worse k O ⁡ ( k 2 ) k^{O(k^{2})} runtime.

The Hausdorff moment problem is that of determining what moment sequences

(1)

Associated with this is the computational problem of determining

In this paper we are concerned with the sparse version of the computational problem, namely, the task of computing

The problem of reconstructing

(i) By a known reduction, any algorithm for this problem lifts to an algorithm for learning topic models. This will be discussed in Sec.

(ii) This problem is a special case of the problem of identifying mixture models of

(iii) Algorithms for identifying mixtures of product distributions are the simplest case of the yet-more-general problem of identifying distributions on “structural causal models”

The method.

The algorithm we analyze is essentially that of Prony, 1795
[
9
]

Mixture Models and other Definitions

Definition 1

(The
k
-coin model)
.

A
k
-coin model
\mathcal{M}=(\alpha,w)
is a mixture of
k
Bernoulli variables with success probabilities
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
with non-negative mixing weights
w_{1},\dotsc,w_{k}
, respectively.

Definition 2

(
m
-snapshots of a
k
-coin model)
.

Given a
k
-coin model

\mathcal{M}=(\alpha,w)
, an
m
-snapshot
is a sample from the
mixture of binomial distributions
w_{1}\Binomial(m,\alpha_{1})+\dotsc+w_{k}\Binomial(m,\alpha_{k})
. (The binomial is a sufficient statistic for
m
rv’s
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
because they are iid given the selected coin.)

For a
k
-coin model, the moments defined in equation (
1
) can be written as follows where
\delta_{\alpha}
being the Dirac measure at
\alpha
,

\mathcal{P}=w_{1}\delta_{\alpha_{1}}+\dotsb+w_{k}\delta_{\alpha_{k}},\quad\mu_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{i}w_{j}.

Definition 3

(Separation for polynomials and mixtures)
.

For a
k
-coin probability model
\mathcal{M}=(\alpha,w)
, define the separation by
\zeta(\mathcal{M})=\min_{i\neq j}\left|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\right|
.
For a degree
k
polynomial with roots
\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
, define the root separation by
\min_{i\neq j}\left|\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\right|
.

Definition 4
.

The rectangular Vandermonde matrix
V^{(m)}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m+1)\times k}
associated with a vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
is given by

V_{\alpha}^{(m)}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1&\dotsm&1\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{3}&\dotsm&\alpha_{k}\\
\alpha_{1}^{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\alpha_{3}^{2}&\dotsm&\alpha_{k}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{1}^{m}&\alpha_{2}^{m}&\alpha_{3}^{m}&\dotsm&\alpha_{k}^{m}\end{bmatrix}

We’ll denote the square Vandermonde matrix with
V_{\alpha}\coloneqq V_{\alpha}^{(k-1)}
.

Definition 5

(Hankel Matrix)
.

The
(k+1)\times(k+1)
Hankel matrix
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}=\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(\mathcal{P})
is defined as:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{H}_{k+1}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{k}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\mu_{3}&\cdots&\mu_{k+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\mu_{k}&\mu_{k+1}&\mu_{k+2}&\cdots&\mu_{2k}\end{bmatrix}.\end{array}

(2)

Note that if
\mathcal{P}
is supported on a
set of cardinality
k
(a.k.a. a
k
-coin
distribution), then

\mathcal{H}_{k+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}=V_{\alpha}^{(k)}\diag(w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}){V_{\alpha}^{(k)}}\mathsf{T}

(3)

where
\alpha_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}=(1,\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}^{2},\alpha_{j}^{3},\dots,\alpha_{j}^{k})
. This also shows that the Hankel matrix is positive semi-definite.

Definition 6

(Polynomial associated with a vector)
.

We associate to each vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
a degree
k-1
polynomial

\hat{q}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q_{j}x^{j}
. (For this reason we use zero indexing for the vector.)

Definition 7
.

For a matrix
M
, let
\left\|M\right\|_{2}
denote the
2\to 2
operator norm of
M
. Thus,
\left\|M\right\|_{2}=\sigma_{\max}(M)
, the largest singular value of
M
.

Definition 8
.

For a Hermitian matrix
M
, let
\lambda_{i}(M)
denote the
i
th smallest eigenvalue of
M
.
In particular
\lambda_{1}(M)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
M
.

Definition 9

(Euclidean projection onto a closed convex set)
.

For a closed convex set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
and any point
x\notin S
, the Euclidean projection of
x
onto
S
is
\Proj_{S}(x)\coloneqq{\mathrm{argmin}}_{y\in S}\left\|y-x\right\|_{2}.
This projection is unique.

Properties of Hankel Matrices

We begin with some properties of Hankel matrices corresponding to finitely supported distributions that follow from results in Chihara
[
7
]
. (See Schmudgen
[
29
, Ch. 10]
for a complete characterization.) For completeness, a proof is provided in the appendix.

Lemma 10
.

Let
{\cal P}
be a probability measure on
[0,1]
. Then,

1.

\mathcal{P}
is supported on a set of cardinality at most
k
iff
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}
is singular.

2.

If the support of
\mathcal{P}
is a set
\left\{\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{k}\right\}\subset[0,1]
then the kernel of
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}
is spanned by the vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
where
\hat{q}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-\alpha_{i})
is the unique monic polynomial with roots at the support of
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof in Appendix A.

We prove a quantitative version of the above lemma.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=(\alpha,w)
be a
k
-coin distribution with separation
\zeta
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{k}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathcal{P})
. For every monic degree
k^{\prime}\leq k-1
polynomial represented by
q\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

q^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{H}_{k}q\geq\frac{w_{\min}}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{16}\right)^{2k-2}\cdot\left\|q\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k^{\prime}}
be the roots (possibly complex) of the polynomial
\hat{q}
, ordered so that
\left|\beta_{1}\right|\geq\left|\beta_{2}\right|\geq\dotsb\geq\left|\beta_{k^{\prime}}\right|
. Since
\hat{q}
is monic, we can write
\hat{q}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}(x-\beta_{j})
.
As the balls

B(\alpha_{i},\zeta/2)
,
i=1,2,\dots,k
, are disjoint, by the pigeonhole
principle, there exists an
i\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}

such that
B(\alpha_{i},\zeta/2)\cap\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k^{\prime}}\}=\emptyset
. The value of
\hat{q}
at
\alpha_{i}
is

\hat{q}(\alpha_{i})=\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}(\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}).

There must be some

\ell\in\{0,1,2,\dots,k^{\prime}\}
such that
\left|q_{\ell}\right|^{2}\geq\frac{\left\|q\right\|_{2}^{2}}{k^{\prime}+1}
. Notice that
\left|q_{\ell}\right|=\left|e_{k^{\prime}-\ell}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k^{\prime}})\right|
, where
e_{r}
is the
r
-th elementary
symmetric polynomial over
k^{\prime}
variables. (
e_{0}=1,e_{1}=\sum\beta_{i},e_{2}=\sum_{i<j}\beta_{i}\beta_{j}
etc.)
So,
e_{k^{\prime}-\ell}
is the sum over

{k^{\prime}\choose k^{\prime}-\ell}\leq 2^{k^{\prime}}
monomials, hence

\left|\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{k^{\prime}-\ell}\right|\geq\frac{\left\|q\right\|_{2}}{(\sqrt{k^{\prime}+1})2^{k^{\prime}}}
. Eliminating from the product all the factors whose absolute value is below
2
, we get that for some
r\leq k^{\prime}-\ell
,

\left|\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{r}\right|\geq\frac{\left\|q\right\|_{2}}{(\sqrt{k^{\prime}+1})4^{k^{\prime}}}
.
For
j\in\left\{1,2,\dots,r\right\}
, since
\left|\beta_{j}\right|\geq 2
and
\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]
, it follows that
\left|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}\right|\geq\frac{\left|\beta_{j}\right|}{2}
.
Also, by the definition of
i
we have that
\left|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}\right|>\zeta/2
for all
j\in\left\{1,2,\dots,k^{\prime}\right\}
. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\hat{q}(\alpha_{i})\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}\right|\right)\left(\prod_{j=r+1}^{k^{\prime}}\left|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{j}\right|\right)\geq\frac{\left|\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{r}\right|}{2^{r}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{2}\right)^{k^{\prime}-r}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\|q\right\|_{2}}{(\sqrt{k^{\prime}+1})8^{k^{\prime}}}\zeta^{k^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{8}\right)^{k-1}\left\|q\right\|_{2}.

Therefore,

q^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{H}_{k}q=\sum_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}\cdot\left(\hat{q}(\alpha_{j})\right)^{2}\geq w_{\min}\cdot\left(\hat{q}(\alpha_{i})\right)^{2}>w_{\min}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{8}\right)^{2k-2}\cdot\left\|q\right\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

Corollary 12
.

For a
k
-coin model
(\alpha,w)
,

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{k+1})>w_{\min}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{16}\right)^{2k-2}
.

Proof.

By the Courant-Fischer-Weyl min-max principle, the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is given
by minimizing the Rayleigh-Ritz quotient. Let
q\neq 0
be a minimizer of
\frac{q^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{H}_{k}q}{q^{\mathsf{T}}q}
.

Let
k^{\prime}
be greatest such that
q_{k^{\prime}}\neq 0
, and w.l.o.g. set
q_{k^{\prime}}=1
. Then by Lemma
11
,

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{k})=\min_{q\neq 0}\frac{q^{\mathsf{T}}\mathcal{H}_{k}q}{q^{\mathsf{T}}q}\geq\frac{w_{\min}}{k}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{8}\right)^{2k-2}\geq w_{\min}\cdot\left(\frac{\zeta}{16}\right)^{2k-2},

where the last inequality follows from observing that
1/k\geq 1/2^{2k-1}
for
k\geq 2
.
Notice that
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is a principal submatrix of
\mathcal{H}_{k+1}
.
Therefore, by the Cauchy interlacing theorem (Theorem
36
),
\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{H}_{k+1})\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{H}_{k})
.
∎

Lemma 19

\mathcal{H}_{k+1}

Proof.

By Weyl’s inequality, we have that
\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})\leq\varepsilon

By Corollary

Lemma 20

For every

0<\varepsilon\ll\min\{\lambda_{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})-\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1}),1\}

Proof sketch.

We follow the outline in the papers by Pan, Chen, and Zheng
[
20
,
21
]
. As
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1}

is a Hankel matrix, a similarity transformation
A=T\ \tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1}\ T^{-1}
, where

A
is tridiagonal, can be computed in time
O(k^{2}\log k)
. The characteristic polynomial
c_{A}(x)

of

can then be computed in time
O(k)
. Then, a root
\tilde{\lambda}
that satisfies

|\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})|<\varepsilon^{2}

O\left((k\log^{2}k)(\log\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log^{2}k)\right)
(see Theorem

\|A\|_{2}=\|\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1}\|_{2}
, thus it is trivially upper bounded by
(k+1)^{2}
). Next, proceed to
compute

as follows. Pick an initial guess

uniformly at random on the unit sphere (i.e.,
from the unit Haar measure on the sphere). We need

which happens with constant probability. To boost the success probability to

, we can repeat
the entire process
O(\log(1/\delta))
times. For constant
\delta

asymptotic bound. We compute

using the
inverse power iteration (see, for instance, Chapter 4 in

\left(\tilde{\lambda}I-\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1}\right)\tilde{v}^{(t)}=v^{(t-1)}
,
then set

. As

is a Hankel

arithmetic operations.
How many iterations are needed?—It is known that
if
\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})

Notice that in our case

\rho=\frac{|\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda_{1}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})|}{\min_{i>1}|\tilde{\lambda}-\lambda_{i}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k+1})|}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2}}<2\varepsilon

Lemma 21

Let

be any

We’re going to use the roots of the polynomial
\hat{r}

Lemma 22
.

Let

be the vector representing a degree-

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k}
contained in
[0,1]
. Let
\zeta
be the root separation for
\hat{q}

Let

represent another degree-

\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{(\zeta/2)^{k}}{4k}\right)

If
r
satisfies
\left\|q-r\right\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon

then the (possibly complex) roots
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{k}
of
\hat{r}

satisfy

d(\alpha,\beta)\leq\frac{4k\varepsilon}{(\zeta/2)^{k-1}}

where

is the optimal matching distance defined by

d(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq\min_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{k}}\max_{i}\left|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{\sigma(i)}\right|.

Proof.

Fix any root
\alpha_{i}
of
\hat{q}
, and consider the ball

B_{i}=B\left(\alpha_{i},\frac{4k\varepsilon}{(\zeta/2)^{k-1}}\right)

in the complex plane. By assumption,
\frac{4k\varepsilon}{(\zeta/2)^{k-1}}<\frac{\zeta}{2}
, so there are no other roots of
\hat{q}
, aside from
\alpha_{i}
, in
B_{i}
.
Moreover, for any
x\in B_{i}
, and for any

, we have that
\left|x-\alpha_{j}\right|\geq\frac{\zeta}{2}
.
Thus for every
x\in\partial B_{i}
, we have

\left|\hat{q}(x)\right|=\left|(x-\alpha_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}(x-\alpha_{j})\right|>\frac{4k\varepsilon}{(\zeta/2)^{k-1}}\left(\frac{\zeta}{2}\right)^{k-1}=4k\varepsilon.

On the other hand, we also have that
B_{i}\subset B(0,(2k-1)/(2k-2))
,
as
\alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{k}\in[0,1]
and
\zeta\leq\frac{1}{k-1}
. Therefore,
\left|x\right|\leq\frac{2k-1}{2k-2}
,
and thus

\displaystyle\left|\hat{q}(x)-\hat{r}(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{k}(q_{j}-r_{j})x^{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left|q_{j}-r_{j}\right|\cdot\left|x\right|^{j}

\displaystyle\leq(k+1)\cdot\left(\frac{2k-1}{2k-2}\right)^{k}\cdot\left\|q-r\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 4k\varepsilon.

By Rouché’s theorem (Theorem

∎

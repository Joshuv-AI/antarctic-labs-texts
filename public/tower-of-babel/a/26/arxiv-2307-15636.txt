Wells type exact sequence and crossed extensions of algebras with bracket
José Manuel Casas 1, Emzar Khmaladze 2, Manuel Ladra 3

Abstract
. We study the extensibility problem of a pair of derivations associated with an abelian extension of algebras with bracket, and derive an exact sequence of the Wells type. We introduce crossed modules for algebras with bracket and prove their equivalence with internal categories in the category of algebras with bracket. We interpret the set of equivalence classes of crossed extensions as the second cohomology. Finally, we construct an eight-term exact sequence in the cohomology of algebras with bracket.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
16E40, 16E99, 16W99

1.
Introduction

The concept of a new algebraic structure called algebra with bracket was introduced in
[
8
]
as a generalization of a (non-commutative) Poisson algebra.
It is an associative algebra equipped with a bracket operation
[-,-]
related to the multiplication by the following fundamental relation:

[ab,c]=[a,c]b+a[b,c].

The origins of such generalization of Poisson algebras can be found in the physics literature (see, e.g.
[
15
]
).

Based on the careful analyses of the free objects in the category of algebras with bracket and using the Hochschild complex for associative algebras, an explicit cochain complex computing Quillen cohomology of algebras with bracket is constructed in
[
8
]
. In the same paper, zero and first cohomologies are characterized by means of derivations and abelian extensions of algebras with bracket, respectively. Later, in
[
6
]
, a homology with trivial coefficients of algebras with bracket is developed with applications to universal central extensions, and among other results, the five-term exact homology and cohomology sequences are obtained.

In this paper, we aim to investigate the following three tasks in the context of algebras with bracket:

1
.
To study the extensibility problem for derivations associated with an abelian extension.

In the case of groups, such an extensibility problem of automorphisms goes back to Baer
[
1
]
.
Important progress was archived by Wells in extensions of abstract groups
[
22
]
, where a map, later called a Wells map, is defined and an exact sequence, also called a Wells exact sequence, connecting various automorphism groups is constructed.
The Wells map and the extensibility of a pair of automorphisms associated with an (abelian) extension were studied in the context
of various algebraic structures
[
3
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
19
]
.
In parallel, the extensibility of derivations associated with an abelian extension of Lie algebras and associative algebras
have been studied in
[
2
]
and
[
20
]
, respectively.

2
.
To introduce crossed modules and to describe the second cohomology via crossed extensions.

Crossed modules of groups introduced in
[
23
]
are algebraic models of path-connected CW-spaces, whose homotopy groups are trivial in all dimensions greater than
2
. Crossed modules also play an essential role in algebra because any crossed module determines an element in the third cohomology of groups. This was firstly observed for group cohomology by Mac Lane-Whitehead
[
17
]
.
The similar fact that a cohomology class can be considered as an equivalence class of crossed modules is known in Lie algebras, associative algebras, Leibniz algebras, Leibniz
n
-algebras and so on
[
4
,
7
,
10
,
21
]
.

3
.
To construct an eight-term exact cohomology sequence
.

The five-term exact sequence in low-dimensional cohomology of algebras with bracket obtained in
[
6
]
and our description of the second cohomology via crossed
extensions leads us to extend this five-term sequence in three new terms, involving the second cohomology vector spaces, similarly to the case of cohomology sequences in groups, Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras and so on
[
5
,
9
,
18
]
.

Organization

After the introductory Section 1, the paper is organized into five more sections. Section 2 recalls basic definitions on algebras with bracket, construction and some results of their cohomology. In section 2, the extensibility problem for derivations of algebras with bracket associated with an abelian extension is stated and investigated, the Wells map is constructed
(Definition
3.5
), which is used to determine the necessary and sufficient condition for the extensibility of a pair of derivations associated to a given abelian extension (Theorem
3.7
); the Wells exact sequence connecting various vector spaces of derivations is also obtained (Theorem
3.9
, Corollary
3.10
). In Section 4, we introduce the notion of crossed module of algebras with bracket (Definition
4.1
) and show the equivalence with internal categories (Theorem
4.6
). In Section 5, the crossed modules are used to define crossed extensions of algebras with bracket. We establish a bijection between the second cohomology and the set of equivalence classes of crossed extensions (Theorem
5.1
). The last Section 6 is devoted to the construction of an eight-term exact sequence in the cohomology of algebras with bracket (Theorem
6.5
).

2.
Algebras With Bracket

2.1.
Basic definitions

Definition 2.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

An algebra with bracket, or an
AWB
for short, is an associative (not necessarily commutative) algebra
{\A}

equipped with a bilinear map (bracket operation)
[-,-]\colon{\A}\times{\A}\to{\A}
,
(a,b)\mapsto[a,b]
satisfying the following identity:

[ab,c]=[a,c]b+a[b,c]

(2.1)

for all
a,b,c\in{\sf A}
.

A homomorphism
of AWB’s is a homomorphism of associative algebras preserving the bracket operation.
We denote by
{\sf AWB}
the respective category of AWB’s.

Example 2.2
.

(i)

Any vector space
\A
with the trivial multiplication and bracket, i.e.
ab=0
and
[a,b]=0
for all
a,b\in\A
, is an
AWB
, called
an abelian AWB
.

(ii)

Any Poisson algebra is an
AWB
. In fact, the category
{\sf Poiss}
of commutative Poisson algebras is a subcategory of
{\sf AWB}
. The inclusion functor
\sf Poiss\hookrightarrow AWB
has as left adjoint the functor given by

{\A}\mapsto{\A}_{\sf Poiss}
, where
{\A}_{\sf Poiss}
is the maximal quotient of
{\A}
, such that the following relations hold:
ab-ba\sim 0
,
[a,a]\sim 0
and
[a,[b,c]]+[b,[c,a]]+[c,[a,b]]\sim 0
.

(iii)

Let
{\A}
be an associative algebra equipped with a linear
map
D\colon{\A}\to{\A}
. Then
{\A}
is an
AWB
concerning the bracket given by

[a,b]\coloneqq aD(b)-D(b)a.

For
D=0
, one obtains an
AWB
, which has the trivial bracket. The more interesting is the case
when
D=\operatorname{id}
, then
{\A}
is an algebra with bracket concerning the usual bracket

[a,b]\coloneqq ab-ba.

This particular algebra with bracket is called the tautological algebra with bracket associated to an associative algebra
{\A}
.
Let us recall that this last bracket also defines a Lie algebra structure on the underlying vector space of
\A
.

(iv)

If
({\A},\prec,\succ)
is a dendriform algebra (see
[
16
]
), then
{\A}
with the product and bracket defined respectively by

ab=a\prec b+a\succ b
and
[a,b]=a\prec b+a\succ b-b\prec a-b\succ a
is an
AWB
.

(v)

Let
{\A}
be an
AWB
, then the vector space
{\A}\otimes{\A}
can be endowed with the structure of
AWB
in the following two ways:

1.

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=(a_{1}b_{1})\otimes(a_{2}b_{2}),

\displaystyle[a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}]

\displaystyle=[a_{1},[b_{1},b_{2}]]\otimes a_{2}+a_{1}\otimes[a_{2},[b_{1},b_{2}]].

2.

\displaystyle(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})(b_{1}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\otimes(a_{2}(b_{1}b_{2})),

\displaystyle[a_{1}\otimes a_{2},b_{1}\otimes b_{2}]

\displaystyle=a_{1}\otimes[a_{2},b_{1}b_{2}]+[a_{1},b_{1}b_{2}]\otimes a_{2}.

for all
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in{\A}
.

Let us recall the following notions from
[
6
]
.

A subalgebra

{\B}
of an AWB
{\A}
is a vector subspace which is closed under the product and the bracket operation, that is,
{\B}~{\B}\subseteq{\B}
and
[{\B},{\B}]\subseteq{\B}
.
A subalgebra
{\B}
is said to be a
right (respectively, left) ideal
if
{\A}~{\B}\subseteq{\B}
,
[{\A},{\B}]\subseteq{\B}
(respectively,

{\B}~{\A}\subseteq{\B}
,
[{\B},{\A}]\subseteq{\B}
).
If
{\B}
is both left and right ideal, then it is said to be a
two-sided ideal
. In this case, the quotient

{\A}/{\B}
is endowed with an AWB structure naturally induced from the operations on
{\A}
.

Let
\A
be an AWB and
{\B},{\C}
be two-sided ideals of
{\A}
. The
commutator ideal
of
{\B}
and
{\C}
is the two-sided ideal of

{\B}
and
{\C}

[[{\B},{\C}]]=\langle\{bc,cb,[b,c],[c,b]\mid b\in{\B},c\in{\C}\}\rangle.

Obviously
[[{\B},{\C}]]\subseteq{\B}\bigcap{\C}
. Observe that
[[{\B},{\C}]]
is not a two-sided ideal of

{\A}
in general, except when
{\B}={\A}
or
{\C}={\A}
. In the particular case
{\B}={\C}={\A}
, one obtains the definition of derived algebra of
{\A}
,

[[{\A},{\A}]]=\langle\{aa^{\prime},[a,a^{\prime}]\mid a,a^{\prime}\in{\A}\}\rangle.

Note that the quotient
{\A}/[[{\A},{\A}]]
is an abelian AWB and will be denoted by
\A_{\ab}
.

The
center
of an AWB
{\A}
is the two-sided ideal

{\Z}({\A})=\{a\in{\A}\mid ab=0=ba,[a,b]=0=[b,a],{\rm for\ all}\ b\in{\sf A}\}.

Note that an algebra with bracket
{\A}
is abelian if and only if
{\A}={\Z}({\A})
.

.

Let

Example 2.4
.

(i)

If

(ii)

Let

2.3.
Cohomology of AWB’s

The Quillen cohomology of an AWB
\A
with coefficients in a
representation
{\M}
of
\A
is computed as the
cohomology of an explicit cochain complex
K^{*}(\A,\M)
in
[
8
]
. Let us recall the main constructions.

Let
\A
be an AWB and
\M
a representation of
\A
. Since
\M
is a bimodule over the associative algebra
\A
, we can consider the Hochschild cochain complex
C^{*}(\A,\M)
of
\A
with coefficients in
\M
. Let us recall that
C^{n}(\A,\M)=\Hom(\A^{\otimes n},\M)
and the coboundary map
b^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle b^{n}(f)(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})=

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot f(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})+\underset{\scriptsize{0\leq i\leq n-1}}{\sum}(-1)^{i+1}f(a_{0},\dots,a_{i}a_{i+1},\dots,a_{n})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})\cdot a_{n}\;.

Now we let
\overline{C}^{*}(\A,\M)
be the cochain complex defined by

\overline{C}^{n}(\A,\M)={C}^{n+1}(\A,\M),\quad n\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{C}^{n}(\A,\M)=0,\quad n\leq 0.

At the same time, the vector space
\M^{e}=\Hom(\A,\M)
has a bimodule structure over
\A
given by

(a_{0}\cdot f)(a_{1})=a_{0}\cdot f(a_{1}),\qquad(f\cdot a_{0})(a_{1})=f(a_{1})\cdot a_{0},

for all
a_{0},a_{1}\in\A
and
f\in\M^{e}
.

There is a cochain map

\alpha^{n}\colon\overline{C}^{n}(\A,\M)\to\overline{C}^{n}(\A,\M^{e}),\quad n\geq 0

defined by

\left(\alpha^{0}(f)(a_{0})\right)(a_{1})=\{a_{0},f(a_{1})\}-f[a_{0},a_{1}]+\{f(a_{0}),a_{1}\}

provided

and for

\big(\alpha^{n}(f)(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})\big)(a_{n+1})=\{f(a_{0},\dots,a_{n}),a_{n+1}\}-\underset{\scriptsize{0\leq i\leq n}}{\sum}f\left(a_{0},\dots,[a_{i},a_{i+1}],\dots,a_{n+1}\right).

Then the complex
K^{*}(\A,\M)
is defined
to be the cone of the cochain map

. By definition, the

-th AWB cohomology

of

\M
is the

, i.e.
H^{n}_{\sf AWB}(\A,\M)=H^{n-1}K^{*}(\A,\M)

For future references, we specify below the cochains and coboundary

2.4.
H^{0}_{\AWB}
and
H^{1}_{\AWB}

Given an AWB
\A
and its representation
\M
,

H^{0}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)
and
H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)
are described in
[
8
]
via AWB-derivations and abelian extensions, respectively.
Let us briefly recall the relevant concepts related to these results, which will be useful later.

Let
\A
be an AWB and
\M
a representation of
\A
.
An AWB-
derivation
is a linear map
d\colon\A\to\M
such that

d(a_{0}a_{1})=a_{0}d(a_{1})+d(a_{0})a_{1}
(i.e.
d
is a derivation in the sense of associative algebras)
and
d[a_{0},a_{1}]=[a_{0},d(a_{1})]+[d(a_{0}),a_{1}]
for all
a_{0},a_{1}\in\A
.
Let
\Der_{\AWB}(\A,\M)
denote the vector space of all AWB-derivations. By
[
8
, Lemma 4.4]
there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

H^{0}_{\sf AWB}(\A,\M)\cong\Der_{\AWB}(\A,\M).

(2.9)

In the particular case when
\M=\A
, we denote
\Der_{\AWB}(\A,\A)
by
\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
.
It is easy to check that
d_{1}d_{2}-d_{2}d_{1}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
if
d_{1},d_{2}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
,
showing that
\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
is a Lie subalgebra of the Lie algebra of all derivations
\Der(\A)
of the associative algebra
\A
.

Any abelian extension of an AWB
\A
by a representation
\M
of
\A
, that is, a short exact sequence of AWB’s

E\colon 0\longrightarrow\M\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\E\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\A\longrightarrow 0,

which induces the given representation structure on
\M
, gives rise to a
0
-cocycle
(f,g)\in K^{0}(\A,\M)
by choosing a linear section

s\colon\A\to\M
of
p
, and by defining
f\colon\A^{\otimes 2}\to\M
and
g\colon\A\to\Hom(\A,\M)
as follows

\displaystyle f(a_{0},a_{1})

\displaystyle=i^{-1}\big(s(a_{0})s(a_{1})-s(a_{0}a_{1})\big),

(2.10)

\displaystyle g(a_{0})(a_{1})

\displaystyle=i^{-1}\big([s(a_{0}),s(a_{1})]-s[a_{0},a_{1}]\big)

for all
a_{0},a_{1}\in\A
. This gives a well-defined bijection between the set of equivalence classes
\Ext_{\AWB}(\A,\M)

of such abelian extensions of
\A
by
\M
and the first cohomology of
\A
with coefficients in
\M

[
8
, Lemma 4.6]
, i.e.

H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)\cong\Ext_{\AWB}(\A,\M).

(2.11)

Let us note that this bijection allows us to endow the set
{\Ext}(\A,\M)
with a vector space structure induced from the one of
H_{\AWB}^{1}(\A,\M)
.
The respective addition in
{\Ext}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)
is defined by the well-known “Baer sum” of extensions and the scalar multiplication
is defined for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
by
\lambda\operatorname{cl}(E)=\operatorname{cl}{(\lambda E)}
,
where
\lambda E\colon 0\to\M\xrightarrow{\lambda\cdot i}\E\xrightarrow{\;p\;}\A\to 0
.

3.
AWB-derivations of abelian extensions

3.1.
Extensibility of AWB-derivations

In this section, we consider the extensibility problem of AWB-derivations associated with an abelian extension.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
0\longrightarrow\M\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\E\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\A\longrightarrow 0
be an abelian extension of an
AWB

\A
by its representation
\M
.
A pair
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
is called extensible if there is a derivation
d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E)

such that
d_{\E}\circ i=i\circ d_{\M}
and
p\circ d_{\E}=d_{A}\circ p
.

Let us remark that, here
\M
is considered as an abelian AWB and hence,
a derivation
d_{\M}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)
is just a linear map, and
\Der_{\AWB}(\M)=\End_{\mathbb{K}}(\M)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\M
be a representation of an
AWB

\A
. Consider
\M
as an abelian
AWB
.
Let
d_{\A}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
and
d_{\M}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)
. Then
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M\rtimes\A)

if and only if the following equalities hold for all
a\in\A
and
m\in\M

\displaystyle d_{\M}(a\cdot m)

\displaystyle=a\cdot d_{\M}(m)+d_{\A}(a)\cdot m,

(3.1)

\displaystyle d_{\M}(m\cdot a)

\displaystyle=m\cdot d_{\A}(a)+d_{\M}(m)\cdot a,

(3.2)

\displaystyle d_{\M}\{a,m\}

\displaystyle=\{a,d_{\M}(m)\}+\{d_{\A}(a),m\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle d_{\M}\{m,a\}

\displaystyle=\{m,d_{\A}(a)\}+\{d_{\M}(m),a\}.

(3.4)

Proof.

The proof requires only direct calculations using the definition of AWB-derivations.
∎

For any AWB
\A
and a representation
\M
of
\A
, let us denote by

\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)=\big\{(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A)\mid d_{\M}\ \text{and}\ d_{\A}\ \text{satisfy equations}\ \eqref{eq_d1}-\eqref{eq_d4}\big\}.

One readily checks that

(d_{\M}d^{\prime}_{\M}-d^{\prime}_{\M}d_{\M},d_{\A}d^{\prime}_{\A}-d^{\prime}_{\A}d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{{\AWB}}({\M},{\A})\ \text{if}\ (d_{{\M}},d_{{\A}}),(d^{\prime}_{\M},d^{\prime}_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A),

which implies that
\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
is a Lie subalgebra of
\Der_{\AWB}(\M\rtimes\A)
.

Now we define a map

\theta\colon{\operatorname{D}}_{{\AWB}}({\M},{\A})\times K^{0}({\A},{\M})\longrightarrow K^{0}({\A},{\M})

(3.5)

as follows. For any
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
and any
0
-cochain
(f,g)\in K^{0}(\A,\M)
we set

\theta\big((d_{\M},d_{\A}),(f,g)\big)=(f_{\theta},g_{\theta}),

where
f_{\theta}\colon\A^{\otimes 2}\to\M
and
g_{\theta}\colon\A\to\Hom(\A,\M)
are given by

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(f(a_{0},a_{1}))-f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1})-f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1})),

(3.6)

\displaystyle g_{\theta}(a_{0})(a_{1})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(g(a_{0})(a_{1}))-g(d_{\A}(a_{0}))(a_{1})-g(a_{0})(d_{\A}(a_{1})).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let us fix
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
. Then we have:

(i)

If
(f,g)\in K^{0}(\A,\M)
is a
0
-cocycle, then
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})
is a
0
-cocycle as well.

(ii)

If
(f,g)
and
(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
are two cohomologous cocycles, then
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})
and
(f^{\prime}_{\theta},g^{\prime}_{\theta})
are cohomologous as well.

Proof.

(i) We need to check that
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})
satisfies

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot f_{\theta}(a_{1},a_{2})-f_{\theta}(a_{0}a_{1},a_{2})+f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1}a_{2})-f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1})\cdot a_{2}=0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot\big(g_{\theta}(a_{1})(a_{2})\big)+\big(g_{\theta}(a_{0})(a_{2})\big)\cdot a_{1}-g_{\theta}(a_{0}a_{1})(a_{2})

(3.8)

\displaystyle\qquad=\{f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1}),a_{2}\}-f_{\theta}\big([a_{0},a_{2}],a_{1}\big)-f_{\theta}\big(a_{0},[a_{1},a_{2}]\big),

for all
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in\A
, whenever the same conditions hold for
(f,g)
.
In effect, to check (
3.7
) we just use equations (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and get

\displaystyle a_{0}\cdot f_{\theta}(a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(a_{0}\cdot f(a_{1},a_{2}))-d_{\A}(a_{0})\cdot f(a_{1},a_{2})-a_{0}\cdot f(d_{\A}(a_{1}),a_{2})-a_{0}\cdot f(a_{1},d_{\A}(a_{2})),

\displaystyle-f_{\theta}(a_{0}a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=-d_{\M}(f(a_{0}a_{1},a_{2}))+f(a_{0}d_{A}(a_{1}),a_{2})+f(d_{A}(a_{0})a_{1},a_{2})+f(a_{0}a_{1},d_{A}(a_{2})),

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1}a_{2})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(f(a_{0},a_{1}a_{2}))-f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1}a_{2})-f(a_{0},a_{1}d_{\A}(a_{2}))-\ \ f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1})a_{2}),

\displaystyle-f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1})\cdot a_{2}

\displaystyle=-d_{M}(f(a_{0},a_{1})\cdot a_{2})+f(a_{0},a_{1})\cdot d_{\A}(a_{2})+f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1})\cdot a_{2}+f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{2}))\cdot a_{2},

and obviously the sum of all these expressions is equal to
0
. In the same way, by using equations (
3.1
)–(
3.4
) one checks immediately that (
3.8
) also holds.

(ii) If
(f,g)
and
(f^{\prime},g^{\prime})
are cohomologous cocycles, then there is a linear map
h:\A\to\M
such that
(f-f^{\prime},g-g^{\prime})=\partial^{-1}(h)
, by (
2.4
) we get

\displaystyle(f-f^{\prime})(a_{0},a_{1})

\displaystyle=-a_{0}\cdot h(a_{1})+h(a_{0}a_{1})-h(a_{0})\cdot a_{1},

\displaystyle(g-g^{\prime})(a_{0})(a_{1})

\displaystyle=-\{a_{0},h(a_{1})\}+h[a_{0},a_{1}]-\{h(a_{0}),a_{1}\}.

Then using only the definition of
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})
in (
3.6
), straightforward computations show that the linear map

d_{\M}h-hd_{\A}\colon\A\to\M
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle(f_{\theta}-f^{\prime}_{\theta},g_{\theta}-g^{\prime}_{\theta})=\partial^{-1}(d_{\M}h-hd_{\A}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

As a consequence of Lemma
3.3
, note that the map
\theta
in (
3.5
) induces a bilinear map (denoted again by
\theta
)

\theta\colon{\operatorname{D}}_{{\AWB}}({\M},{\A})\times H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M).

Moreover, it is routine to check that
\theta
defines a module structure on
H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M)
over the Lie algebra
{\operatorname{D}}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
.

Using Lemma
3.3
, we state the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
E\colon 0\longrightarrow\M\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\E\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\A\longrightarrow 0
be an abelian extension of an
AWB

\A
by its representation
\M
and
(f,g)\in K^{0}(\A,\M)
the corresponding
0
-cocycle as in (
2.10
). The map

\omega\colon{\operatorname{D}}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\AWB}(\A,\M),\quad\omega(d_{\M},d_{\A})=\theta\big((d_{\M},d_{\A}),\operatorname{cl}(f,g)\big)=\operatorname{cl}(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})

is called the Wells map associated to the given abelian extension
{E}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that if the abelian extension
E
is split, that is, there is a homomorphism of
AWB
’s
s\colon\A\to\E
such that
p\circ s=\operatorname{id}_{\A}
, then
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})=(f,g)=(0,0)
and so
\omega
is the trivial map.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
E\colon 0\longrightarrow\M\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\E\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\A\longrightarrow 0
be an abelian extension of an
AWB

\A
by its representation
\M
. A pair

(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
is extensible if and only if
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
and
w(d_{\M},d_{\A})=0
.

Proof.

Let
s\colon\A\to\E
be a linear section of
p
. Then any element of
\E
has the form
i(m)+s(a)
, where
m\in\M
and
a\in\A
. We write
m
instead of
i(m)\in\E
to simplify notations.

If
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
and
w(d_{\M},d_{\A})=\theta\big((d_{\A},d_{\M}),\operatorname{cl}(f,g)\big)=\operatorname{cl}(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})=0
, there exists a linear map
h\colon\A\to\M
such that
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})=\partial^{-1}(h)
.
By using (
2.4
) and (
3.6
) we get immediately:

\displaystyle d_{\M}(f(a_{0},a_{1}))-f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1})-f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1}))

\displaystyle=-a_{0}\cdot h(a_{1})+h(a_{0}a_{1})-h(a_{0})\cdot a_{1},

(3.9)

\displaystyle d_{\M}(g(a_{0})(a_{1}))-g(d_{\A}(a_{0}))(a_{1})-g(a_{0})(d_{\A}(a_{1}))

\displaystyle=-\{a_{0},h(a_{1})\}+h[a_{0},a_{1}]-\{h(a_{0}),a_{1}\}.

(3.10)

We define a map
d_{\E}\colon\E\to\E
by

d_{\E}(m+s(a))=d_{\M}(m)-h(a)+s(d_{\A}(a)).

(3.11)

Obviously
d_{\E}
is a linear map and

\displaystyle d_{\E}(i(m))

\displaystyle=i(d_{\M}(m)),

\displaystyle p(d_{\E}(m+s(a)))

\displaystyle=p(d_{\M}(m))-p(h(a))+p(s(d_{\A}(a)))=d_{\A}(a)=d_{\A}(p(m+s(a))).

To show that
d_{\E}
is an AWB-derivation, it suffices to check the following identities

\displaystyle d_{\E}(s(a)m)

\displaystyle=d_{\E}(s(a))m+s(a)d_{\E}(m),

(3.12)

\displaystyle d_{\E}(ms(a))

\displaystyle=d_{\E}(m)s(a)+md_{\E}(s(a)),

(3.13)

\displaystyle d_{\E}(s(a_{0})s(a_{1}))

\displaystyle=d_{\E}(s(a_{0}))s(a_{1})+s(a_{0})d_{\E}(s(a_{1})),

(3.14)

\displaystyle d_{\E}[s(a),m]

\displaystyle=[d_{\E}(s(a)),m]+[s(a),d_{\E}(m)],

(3.15)

\displaystyle d_{\E}[m,s(a)]

\displaystyle=[d_{\E}(m),s(a)]+[m,d_{\E}(s(a))],

(3.16)

\displaystyle d_{\E}[s(a_{0}),s(a_{1})]

\displaystyle=[d_{\E}(s(a_{0})),s(a_{1})]+[s(a_{0}),d_{\E}(s(a_{1}))].

(3.17)

The identities (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) easily follow from (
3.1
), (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively.
Justifications of (
3.14
) and (
3.17
) are very similar and use (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). We present only the calculations confirming (
3.17
):

\displaystyle d_{\E}[s(a_{0}),s(a_{1})]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_1cocycle(f,g)}}{=}d_{\E}\big(g(a_{0})(a_{1})+s[a_{0},a_{1}]\big)\overset{\eqref{def_d_E}}{=}d_{\M}\big(g(a_{0})(a_{1})\big)-h[a_{0},a_{1}]+sd_{\A}[a_{0},a_{1}]

\displaystyle\ \,=d_{\M}\big(g(a_{0})(a_{1})\big)-h[a_{0},a_{1}]+s[d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1}]+s[a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1})]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_1cocycle(f,g)}}{=}d_{\M}\big(g(a_{0})(a_{1})\big)-h[a_{0},a_{1}]+[sd_{\A}(a_{0}),s(a_{1})]-g(d_{\A}(a_{0}))(a_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ +[s(a_{0}),sd_{\A}(a_{1})]-g(a_{0})(d_{\A}(a_{1}))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{def_d_E}}{=}d_{\M}\big(g(a_{0})(a_{1})\big)-g(d_{\A}(a_{0}))(a_{1})-g(a_{0})(d_{\A}(a_{1}))-h[a_{0},a_{1}]

\displaystyle\qquad+[d_{\E}(s(a_{0})),s(a_{1})]+[h(a_{0}),s(a_{1})]+[s(a_{0}),d_{\E}(s(a_{1}))]+[s(a_{0}),h(a_{1})]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_g and h}}{=}-[a_{0},h(a_{1})]+h[a_{0},a_{1}]-[h(a_{0}),a_{1}]-h[a_{0},a_{1}]+[d_{\E}(s(a_{0})),s(a_{1})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ +[h(a_{0}),a_{1}]+[s(a_{0}),d_{\E}(s(a_{1}))]+[a_{0},h(a_{1})]

\displaystyle\ =[d_{\E}(s(a_{0})),s(a_{1})]+[s(a_{0}),d_{\E}(s(a_{1}))].

Conversely, suppose that
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
is extensible, i.e. there exists
d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E)
such that
d_{\E}\circ i=i\circ d_{\M}
and
p\circ d_{\E}=d_{\A}\circ p
, then
d_{\M}
is the restriction of
d_{\E}
on
\M
,
d_{\M}={d_{\E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu{\M}}
. Fix again a linear section
s\colon\A\to\E
of
p
. Since
s(d_{\A}(a))-d_{\E}(s(a))\in\operatorname{Ker}p
for all
a\in\A
, we have a linear map
h\colon\A\to\M
defined by

h(a)=s(d_{\A}(a))-d_{\E}(s(a)).

(3.18)

Now we claim that the pair
(d_{\M},d_{\A})
satisfies the conditions (
3.1
)–(
3.4
). For instance, we only present the proof for (
3.3
):

\displaystyle d_{\M}\{a,m\}

\displaystyle=d_{\E}[s(a),m]=[d_{\E}(s(a)),m]+[s(a),d_{\E}(m)]

\displaystyle\!\!\!\overset{\eqref{coboundary h}}{=}[s(d_{\A}(a))-h(a),m]+[a,d_{\M}(m)]

\displaystyle=[s(d_{\A}(a)),m]+\{a,d_{\M}(m)\}=\{d_{\A}(a),m\}+\{a,d_{\M}(m)\}.

The justifications of (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) are very similar. Hence,
(d_{\M},d_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
.

Finally, we show that

(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})=\partial^{-1}(h)
, where

is defined in (
3.18
) and
w(d_{\M},d_{\A})=\theta\big((d_{\A},d_{\M}),\operatorname{cl}(f,g)\big)=\operatorname{cl}(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})
. In effect, for any
a_{0},a_{1}\in\A
we have

\displaystyle d_{\E}\big(s(a_{0})s(a_{1})\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_1cocycle(f,g)}}{=}d_{\E}\big(s(a_{0}a_{1})+f(a_{0},a_{1})\big)\overset{\eqref{coboundary h}}{=}s(d_{\A}(a_{0}a_{1}))-h(a_{0}a_{1})+d_{\M}\big(f(a_{0},a_{1})\big)

(3.19)

\displaystyle\ =s\big(d_{\A}(a_{0})a_{1}\big)+s\big(a_{0}d_{\A}(a_{1})\big)-h(a_{0}a_{1})+d_{\M}\big(f(a_{0},a_{1})\big).

On the other hand, since
d_{\E}
is an AWB-derivation, we get

\displaystyle d_{\E}\big(s(a_{0})s(a_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=d_{\E}\big(s(a_{0})\big)s(a_{1})+s(a_{0})d_{\E}\big(s(a_{1}\big)\big)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\overset{\eqref{coboundary h}}{=}\big(s(d_{\A}(a_{0}))-h(a_{0})\big)s(a_{1})+s(a_{0})\big(s(d_{\A}(a_{1}))-h(a_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=s\big(d_{\A}(a_{0})\big)s(a_{1})-h(a_{0})s(a_{1})+s(a_{0})s\big(d_{\A}(a_{1})\big)-s(a_{0})h(a_{1})

\displaystyle=s\big(d_{\A}(a_{0})\big)s(a_{1})-h(a_{0})\cdot a_{1}+s(a_{0})s\big(d_{\A}(a_{1})\big)-a_{0}\cdot h(a_{1}).

Comparing the last lines in (

) and (

\displaystyle s\big(d_{\A}(a_{0})\big)s(a_{1})-s\big(d_{\A}(a_{0})a_{1}\big)

\displaystyle=f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1}),

\displaystyle s(a_{0})s\big(d_{\A}(a_{1})\big)-s\big(a_{0}d_{\A}(a_{1})\big)

\displaystyle=f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1})),

it is readily seen that

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(f(a_{0},a_{1}))-f(d_{\A}(a_{0}),a_{1})-f(a_{0},d_{\A}(a_{1}))

\displaystyle=-a_{0}\cdot h(a_{1})+h(a_{0}a_{1})-h(a_{0})\cdot a_{1},

One can repeat similar computations to show that

\displaystyle g_{\theta}(a_{0},a_{1})

\displaystyle=d_{\M}(g(a_{0})(a_{1}))-g(d_{\A}(a_{0}))(a_{1})-g(a_{0})(d_{\A}(a_{1}))

\displaystyle=-\{a_{0},h(a_{1})\}+h[a_{0},a_{1}]-\{h(a_{0}),a_{1}\}.

Hence
(f_{\theta},g_{\theta})=\partial^{-1}(h)
and the proof is completed.
∎

3.2.
The Wells sequence for AWB-derivations

We continue using the previous subsection’s notation. Given an abelian extension
E\colon 0\longrightarrow\M\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\E\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}\A\longrightarrow 0
of an AWB
\A
by its representation
\M
, we fix a linear section
s
of
p
and denote

\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\M)=\{d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E)\mid d_{\E}(\M)\subseteq\M\}.

It is obviously a Lie subalgebra of
\Der_{\AWB}(\E)
. One readily checks that any element
d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\A)
defines two AWB-derivations

d_{\E\mid\M}={d_{\E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu{\M}}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\quad\text{and}\quad d^{\E}_{\A}=p\circ d_{\E}\circ s\in\Der_{\AWB}(\A).

Moreover, since
p(d_{\E}(m))=0
for any
m\in\M
, it follows that
d^{\E}_{\A}
does not depend on the choice of the section
s
. Thus, we get a linear map

\kappa\colon\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\M)\longrightarrow\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A),\quad\kappa(d_{\E})=(d_{\E\mid\M},d^{\E}_{\A}).

It is easy to see that
i\circ d_{\E\mid\M}=d_{\E}\circ i
and
d^{\E}_{\A}\circ p=p\circ d_{\E}
, that is,
(d_{\E\mid\M},d^{\E}_{\A})
is an extensible pair in
\Der_{\AWB}(\M)\times\Der_{\AWB}(\A)
. Then by Theorem
3.7
we have that

(d_{\E\mid\M},d^{\E}_{\A})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
and
w(d_{\E\mid\M},d^{\E}_{\A})=0
. Moreover, we have the following result

Lemma 3.8
.

With the above notations, the following assertions hold:

(i)

\operatorname{Im}(\kappa)\subseteq\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
;

(ii)

\kappa\colon\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\M)\to\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras;

(iii)

\operatorname{Im}(\kappa)=\operatorname{Ker}(\omega)
.

Proof.

(i) There is nothing to prove because, as we have shown above,
\kappa(d_{\E})\in\operatorname{D}_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
for all
d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\M)
.

(ii) We need to show that
\kappa\big(d_{\E}d^{\prime}_{\E}-d^{\prime}_{\E}d_{\E}\big)=\kappa(d_{\E})\kappa(d^{\prime}_{\E})-\kappa(d^{\prime}_{\E})\kappa(d_{\E})
for all
d_{\E},d^{\prime}_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\E|\M)
. For this, it suffices to repeat the proof given in the case of associative algebras in
[
20
, Lemma 5.4]
.

(iii) Since
w(\kappa(d_{\E}))=w(d_{\E\mid\M},d^{\E}_{\A})=0
for all
d_{\E}\in\Der_{\AWB}(\M,\A)
, we have
\operatorname{Im}(\kappa)\subseteq\operatorname{Ker}(\omega)
.
So we must show that
\operatorname{Ker}(\omega)\subseteq\operatorname{Im}(\kappa)
. For that, take

4.
Crossed modules of AWB’s

4.1.
Crossed modules

Definition 4.1
.

A crossed module of
AWB
’s is a homomorphism of
AWB
’s
\mu\colon{\M}\to{\A}
together with an action of
\A
on
\M
such that the following identities hold:

(CM1)

\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}\mu(m{\cdot a})&=&\mu(m)a,&\mu({{a\cdot}}m)&=&a\mu(m),\\
\mu\{m,a\}&=&[\mu(m),a],&\mu\{a,m\}&=&[a,\mu(m)];\end{array}

(CM2)

\begin{array}[]{lcccl}{{\mu(m)\cdot}}m^{\prime}&=&mm^{\prime}&=&m{\cdot{\mu(m^{\prime})}},\\
\{\mu(m),m^{\prime}\}&=&[m,m^{\prime}]&=&\{m,\mu(m^{\prime})\}\end{array}

for all
m,m^{\prime}\in{\M}
,
a\in{\A}
.

Definition 4.2
.

A morphism of crossed modules
\left({\M}\overset{\mu}{\longrightarrow}{\A}\right)\to\left({\M}^{\prime}\overset{\mu^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}{\A}^{\prime}\right)

is a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, where
\alpha\colon{\M}\to{\M}^{\prime}
and
\beta\colon{\A}\to{\A}^{\prime}
are homomorphisms of

’s satisfying:

(a)

\beta\circ\mu=\mu^{\prime}\circ\alpha

(b)

\begin{array}[]{lclclcl}\alpha({{a\cdot}m})&=&{{\beta(a)\cdot}}\alpha(m),&&\alpha(m{\cdot a})&=&\alpha(m){\cdot{\beta(a)}},\\
\alpha\{a,m\}&=&\{\beta(a),\alpha(m)\},&&\alpha\{m,a\}&=&\{\alpha(m),\beta(a)\}\end{array}

for all
a\in{\A}
,
m\in{\M}
.

It is clear that crossed modules of AWB’s constitute a category, denoted by
XAWB
.

The following lemma is an easy consequence of Definition
4.1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu\colon{\M}\to{\A}
be a crossed module of algebras with bracket. Then the following statements are satisfied:

(i)

\operatorname{Ker}(\mu)\subseteq{\Z}(\M)
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Im}(\mu)

{\A}
.

(iii)

\operatorname{Im}(\mu)

\operatorname{Ker}(\mu)
inherits an action of
{\A}/\operatorname{Im}(\mu)

a representation of the algebra with bracket
{\A}/\operatorname{Im}(\mu)
.

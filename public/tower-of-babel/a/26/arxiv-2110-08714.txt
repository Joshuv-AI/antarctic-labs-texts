Statistics for p p -ranks of Artin–Schreier Covers
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Given a prime p p and q q a power of p p , we study the statistics of p p -ranks of Artin–Schreier covers of given genus defined over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , in the large q q -limit. We refer to this problem as the geometric problem . We also study an arithmetic variation of this problem, and consider Artin–Schreier covers defined over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} , letting p p go to infinity. Distribution of p p -ranks has been previously studied for Artin–Schreier covers over a fixed finite field as the genus is allowed to go to infinity. The method requires that we count isomorphism classes of covers that are unramified at ∞ \infty .

Introduction

The study of curves over finite fields leads to many interesting problems in arithmetic statistics. Recently, statistical questions have been framed and studied for curves varying in certain naturally occurring ensembles, see for instance,
[
12
,
9
,
17
,
3
,
6
,
16
,
5
,
2
,
4
,
13
]
. Throughout,
p
will denote a prime number and
q
will be a power of
p
. The field with
q
elements is denoted
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Given a curve
\mathcal{C}
of positive genus
g
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, its arithmetic is better understood by studying the structure of its Jacobian
\operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal{C})
, which is a
g
-dimensional abelian variety. An important invariant associated to the curve is the
p
-rank of
\operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal{C})(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, which we denote by
\tau
. This is the number such that
\operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal{C})(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\tau}
. The
p
-rank lies in the range
0\leq\tau\leq g
and when
\tau=g
, the curve is
ordinary
. In
[
4
,
13
]
, statistical questions are studied for
p
-ranks of Artin–Schreier covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over a fixed finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, as the genus goes to infinity. It is shown that when
p
is odd, the proportion of Artin–Schreier covers that are ordinary is zero as
g\rightarrow\infty
. When
p=2
, this proportion is shown to be non-zero, see
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
.

We study similar questions about the distributions of
p
-ranks, however, the curves will no longer be defined over a fixed finite field. There are two independent questions we study in this paper. For the former, we fix the prime
p
and let
q\rightarrow\infty
. This is referred to as the
geometric problem
.

We make certain simplifications to the problem in question:

•

the curves are not classified up to isomorphism, however, up to
isomorphism of covers
, see Definition
2.1
.

•

We restrict to isomorphism classes of covers that are unramified at
\infty
. This is not a significant assumption since it can be guaranteed unless the cover is branched at all
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points.

In studying the arithmetic variant, the prime
p
itself is allowed to vary. By a standard application of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the genus
g
of an Artin–Schreier cover is always divisible by
\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
. Since we are interested in the case when the genus is positive it follows that
g\rightarrow\infty
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. In this setting, we fix the
speed
at which the genus grows. Hence, we fix an integer
d>0
throughout, and let
g(d,p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
. It follows from the Deuring-Shafarevich formula
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
that the
p
-rank is of the form
\tau=r(p-1)
, where
r\geq 0
is an integer. We fix
r
, thus the
p
-rank will also grow at a linear rate.

Thus, the two main questions studied are as follows.

(1)

Geometric problem:
Given a prime
p
, what are the statistics for
p
-ranks of Artin–Schreier covers with fixed genus over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, as
q\rightarrow\infty
?

(2)

Arithmetic problem:
Suppose we are given an integer
d>0
. Then, what are the statistics for
p
-ranks of Artin–Schreier covers over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with genus
g(d,p)
, as
p\rightarrow\infty
.

In section
3
, we prove the main results of the paper. Theorem
3.10
gives a solution to the geometric problem. The results in the geometric context are compatible with results of Pries and Zhu
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
and Maugeais
[
10
, Corollary 3.16]
which analyze the stratification of the moduli stack of Artin–Schreier curves over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. The
p
-rank induces a stratification on the space of Artin–Schreier curves of genus
g
. For the stratum of genus
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
and
p
-rank
\tau=r(p-1)
, the irreducible components are given in terms of combinatorial data and are in bijection with partitions of
(d+2)
into
(r+1)
numbers satisfying further constraints. For further details, see Remark
3.2
.

Theorem
3.10
provides an answer to the above problem and is also expressed in terms of partition data. This result applies to all odd primes
p
, and when
p=2
, it is required that
d
is even. The result can be interpreted in terms of the distribution of points on the irreducible components of maximal dimension in the moduli of Artin–Schreier curves of fixed genus. We refer to Remark
3.9
for further details.

The distribution of arithmetic data over the set of prime numbers is theme of central interest in number theory. Often the arithmetic data is associated with a
global object
such as a variety over a number field or Galois representation. Famous examples of problems of this flavor include the
Sato–Tate conjecture
for abelian varieties and
Lehmer’s conjecture
for elliptic curves. Although the
arithmetic problem
above is not intrinsically associated with a global object such as a motive defined over a number field, it has certain similarities since it concerns arithmetic objects and the limit is taken as
p
goes to infinity. Our results fit into a broader theme since the
p
-rank is the number of roots of the
L-polynomial
that are
p
-adic units. There is significant interest in the study of the arithmetic of such polynomials and the properties of their associated Newton polygons, see for instance
[
1
,
8
]
. Theorem
3.16
provides a solution to the above mentioned arithmetic problem.

We arrive at our results via combinatorial methods that are independent of the results and techniques in the above mentioned works. The contents of the paper are thus comprehensible to a wide audience. In section
4
, the results of section
3
are illustrated through an explicit example.

2.
Preliminaries

We fix a prime number
p
and set
q=p^{n}
. Let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
be a non-constant rational function such that
f(x)\neq z^{p}-z
for any
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Associate to
f
the Artin–Schreier cover
\mathcal{C}_{f}
of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, taken to be the projective closure of the affine curve defined by the equation
y^{p}-y=f(x)
. The map
\mathcal{C}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
sends
(x,y)\mapsto x
. Note that the Galois group of the cover is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and generated by
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+1)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Two covers
\mathcal{C}_{f}
and
\mathcal{C}_{g}
associated to
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
are isomorphic if there is an isomorphism
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{C}_{f}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{C}_{g}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which fits into a commuting diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{P}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{P}^{1}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}}

We express this relation by simply writing
\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
; note that it is stronger than requiring that
\mathcal{C}_{f}
and
\mathcal{C}_{g}
are isomorphic as curves.

Let
B\subset\mathbf{P}^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
be the set of poles of
f(x)
. By adjusting
f
by a function of the form
z^{p}-z
, we may assume that
p
does not divide the order of any pole of
f
. Then, the cover is ramified at precisely the points in
B
. We assume that the cover is unramified at
\infty
. Letting
d_{\alpha}
be the order of the pole of
f(x)
at
\alpha
, we set
\mathcal{D}_{f}=\sum_{\alpha\in B}d_{\alpha}\alpha
to be the ramification divisor. We shall explicitly work with partial fractions. In setting up notation, for
\alpha\in B
, define

x_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(x-\alpha)}&\text{ if }\alpha\neq\infty,\\
x&\text{ if }\alpha=\infty.\end{cases}

Then, using a partial fraction decomposition, we write

f(x)=\sum_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha}),

where
f_{\alpha}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
d_{\alpha}
. The order of the pole of
f
at
\alpha
is
d_{\alpha}
.

We wish to reduce the question of counting equivalence classes of Artin–Schreier covers to the simpler question of counting rational functions with prescribed properties. Following
[
13
]
, we introduce a class of rational functions that are suitable for counting, which we shall refer to as
admissible
.

Definition 2.2
.

A rational function
f(x)
is
admissible
if its partial fraction decomposition satisfies the following conditions

(1)

At
\infty
, the cover
\mathcal{C}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
is unramified, in other words, the polynomial
f_{\infty}(x)
is a constant.

(2)

For each pole
\alpha
of
f(x)
such that
\alpha\neq\infty
,
f_{\alpha}
has no constant term.

(3)

If
p|j
, then the coefficient of
x^{j}
in
f_{\alpha}
is
0
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of admissible functions, and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
the subset with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Note that (
1
) implies that
f(x)
can be expressed as a quotient
f(x)=g(x)/h(x)
, where
\operatorname{deg}g(x)\leq\operatorname{deg}h(x)
. Suppose that
f(x)
is admissible. Let
d
denote the following sum

(2.1)

d\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-2+\sum_{\alpha}(d_{\alpha}+1),

where
\alpha
runs over all poles of
f(x)
. In the formula above,
d_{\alpha}
is set to be equal to
-1
if there is no pole at
\alpha
. In particular,
d_{\infty}=-1
since the cover is not ramified at
\infty
. By an application of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the genus of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is given by
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
, see
[
14
, Proposition 3.7.8]
for further details. Letting
r+1
be the number of poles of
f(x)
, it follows from the Deuring-Shafarevich formula
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
that the
p
-rank is given by
\tau=r(p-1)
.

Given rational functions
f
and
g
, the following gives an explicit criterion for there to be an isomorphism
\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Then, there is an isomorphism of curves
\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{g}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
f(x)=ug(\gamma x)+\delta^{p}-\delta
for
u\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\times}
,
\gamma\in\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. There is an isomorphism of covers
\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
if and only if
f(x)=ug(x)+\delta^{p}-\delta
for
u\in\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\times}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Furthermore, if
f
and
g
are both admissible and
\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\mathcal{C}_{g}
, then,
g(x)=uf(x)
for
u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
. The remaining assertions are left as an exercise (see
[
11
, Remark 3.9]
).
∎

Henceforth, the word "isomorphism" shall refer to isomorphisms of covers in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Any Artin–Schreier cover
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
that is unramified at
\infty
is isomorphic to one that is of the form
\mathcal{C}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
, with
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then,
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

Let
g
be such that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{g}
. Let
\delta
be such that for
f(x)=g(x)+\delta^{p}-\delta
condition (
3
) is satisfied. In greater detail, all monomials involving
x^{j}
where
p|j
in
f_{\alpha}(x)
can be removed by adding
(\delta^{p}-\delta)
. It is easy to see that all other conditions are satisfied.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
that are conjugate over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then,
d_{\alpha}=d_{\alpha^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}/\mathbb{F}_{q})
be such that
\alpha^{\prime}=\sigma(\alpha)
. Note that since
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
we have that
(\sigma f)(x)=f(x)
. Here,
(\sigma f)(x)
is the rational function obtained by applying
\sigma
to the coefficients of
f(x)
. This implies that

\sigma\left(f_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\right)=f_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x_{\alpha^{\prime}}).

On the other hand,

\sigma\left(f_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\right)=(\sigma f_{\alpha})\left(\sigma(x_{\alpha})\right)=(\sigma f_{\alpha})(x_{\alpha^{\prime}}).

Setting
y=x_{\alpha^{\prime}}
, we find that
f_{\alpha^{\prime}}(y)=\sigma(f_{\alpha})(y)
, and hence,
d_{\alpha^{\prime}}=d_{\alpha}
.
∎

We introduce some further notation:

•

Let
\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the set of all isomorphism classes of Artin–Schreier covers
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}^{1}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that are of genus
g
and unramified at
\infty
.

•

Set
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
to be the set of all admissible rational functions

, such that
\mathcal{C}_{f}\in\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. The map
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rightarrow\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is surjective by Corollary

.

•

Set
\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(resp.
\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
) to be the subset of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
(resp.
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
) for which the
p
-rank (resp.
p
-rank of the associated Artin–Schreier cover) is
\tau
.

We shall compute the large
q
-limit:

(2.2)

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}{\#\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\right),

where
g>0
and
q
ranges over all powers of
p
. From this, it is easy to calculate

(2.3)

\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}{\#\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\right).

3.
Main Results

We prove the main results of this paper. First, we treat the geometric problem, after which we study the arithmetic variant, and see that the densities match up. In the next section, we provide an example to illustrate some of the results.

3.1.
Geometric problem: fixed prime, large
q
-limit

Fix a prime number
p
and let
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
. Assume throughout that
d>0
. In this section we study the large
q
-limits
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)
and
\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)
defined in the previous section (cf. (
2.2
), (
2.3
)). We introduce some further notation. Let
f(x)\in\mathcal{S}
, note that this means that each polynomial
f_{\alpha}(x)
satisfies the conditions of Definition
2.2
. For
\alpha\neq\infty
, we write
f_{\alpha}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\alpha}}c_{i,\alpha}x^{i}
, where the terms
c_{i,\alpha}=0
for all terms
i
that are divisible by
p
. Thus, it follows from condition (
3
) that
d_{\alpha}\not\equiv 0\mod{p}
. Note that it is assumed that
f_{\infty}(x)
is a constant. For
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
with
j\not\equiv 0\mod{p}
, we set
g_{j}(x)
to be the product

g_{j}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\prod_{d_{\alpha}=j}(x-\alpha)\right),

and set
g(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{j}g_{j}(x)^{j}
. Note that if
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are conjugate, then, by Lemma
2.5
,
d_{\alpha}=d_{\alpha^{\prime}}
. It follows that
g_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.

We write
f(x)=\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}
, where
\operatorname{deg}h(x)\leq\operatorname{deg}g(x)
. Note that the polynomials
g_{j}(x)
are squarefree polynomials. Set
\kappa_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{deg}g_{j}(x)
, note that

d+2=\sum_{j}(j+1)\kappa_{j},

hence, this gives rise to a partition of
d+2
. Here,
\kappa_{j}
is the multiplicity of
j+1
as a term in the partition, and
j\not\equiv 0\mod{p}
. We have set
r+1
to be the total number of poles of
f(x)
, and this is given by

r+1=\sum_{j}\operatorname{deg}g_{j}(x)=\sum_{j}\kappa_{j}.

Recall from the previous section that the
p
-rank of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is given by
\tau=r(p-1)
. For
i=1,2,\dots
, set

(3.1)

\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\{j|\kappa_{j}\geq i\},

note that
r+1
is the total number of terms in the partition of
(d+2)
and
\lambda_{i}
the number of distinct terms with multiplicity
\geq i
. In the case when
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j>1
, we have that
d
is even and
\kappa_{1}=\frac{d+2}{2}
. In this case,
r=\frac{d}{2}
and the
p
-rank is equal to the genus. Thus, the curve is ordinary precisely when the degree at each pole is
1
. There is only one partition that corresponds to this case when
d
is even, and no partitions when
d
is odd.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)>0
and
r\geq 0
. Denote by
\Omega_{p}(r+1,d+2)
the set of partitions of

into
r+1

\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
, and set

.

Represent a partition in
\Omega_{p}(r+1,d+2)
by the

, i.e., the tuple of integers
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{t})
, where (by (

))

Thus for a partition of
(d+2)
into
(r+1)

\kappa_{j}
is the multiplicity of
(j+1)
. Note that
\kappa_{j}=0
for all

. The number

. The number of terms in the associated partition is

The multiplicity vector of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is denoted

. Given a partition in

2(r+1)=\sum_{j}2\kappa_{j}\leq\sum_{j}(j+1)\kappa_{j}=d+2.

.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
11
]
, Pries and Zhu consider
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
, the moduli space of genus

Artin–Schreier curves and study the stratification of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
according to
p

, let
\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}
be the stratum consisting of Artin–Screier curves of

shows that the set of irreducible components of

is in bijection with the set of partitions

Thus in particular, the dimension is maximal when

for all

. In terms of the multiplicity vector, this means that

for all

. We note here that the dimension of

is

\mathcal{AS}_{g}
.

Lemma 3.3

Let

\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)q^{N}

\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{1}+1}q^{d+3}\leq N(\kappa)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+3},

Proof.

Note that the degree of

i=1,\dots,t
, we set

\beta_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\kappa_{i}\leq 0,\\
1&\text{ if }\kappa_{i}>0.\end{cases}\text{ and }\xi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\kappa_{i}\leq 1,\\
1&\text{ if }\kappa_{i}>1\end{cases}

We observe that
\lambda_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_{i}
and
\lambda_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\xi_{i}
. By Lemma
3.3
, the number of squarefree monic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
\kappa_{1}
is
\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\xi_{1}}q^{\kappa_{1}}
, and this is the number of choices for
g_{1}(x)
. For a given choice of
g_{1}(x)
, the number of choices for
g_{2}(x)
that are coprime to
g_{1}(x)
is
\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\xi_{2}}q^{\kappa_{2}}
. Assume that
\kappa_{2}>0
. Since there are at most
(d+1-r)
factors that divide
g_{1}(x)
, the total number of choices for
g_{2}(x)
is

\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)q^{\kappa_{2}}-(d+1-r)q^{\kappa_{2}-1}=\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)q^{\kappa_{2}}.

If
\kappa_{2}=0
, we find that the number of choices of
g_{2}(x)
is
q^{\kappa_{2}}=1
. Combining the above statements, we find that the total number of choices for
g_{2}(x)
is
\geq\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)^{\beta_{2}}q^{\kappa_{2}}
. Suppose we have made choices for
g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{j}(x)
, and would like to choose
g_{j+1}(x)
to be coprime to
g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{j}(x)
. The same argument tells us that the number of choices for
g_{j+1}(x)
lies between
\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)^{\beta_{j+1}}q^{\kappa_{j+1}}
and
\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\xi_{j+1}}q^{\kappa_{j+1}}
. Therefore, we find that the number of choices for
g(x)
lies in between

\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{1}}q^{r+1}

and
\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{r+1}
. Note that
\operatorname{deg}g(x)=d+1-r
and
h(x)
has degree
\leq\operatorname{deg}g(x)
. We find that for a given choice of
g(x)
, the number of choices for
h(x)
lies in between

q^{d+2-r}-(d+1-r)q^{d+1-r}=\left(1-\frac{d+1-r}{q}\right)q^{d+2-r}\geq\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)q^{d+2-r}

and
q^{d+2-r}
. Therefore, we obtain the bounds

\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{1}+1}q^{d+3}\leq N(\kappa)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+3}.

∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the subset of
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
consisting of all
f(x)
for which
\kappa(f)=\kappa
.

We review some standard notation. Given two functions
F(q)
and
G(q)
, we recall that
F(q)\sim G(q)
if
\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}F(q)/G(q)=1
. In the limit above
q=p^{n}
, where
n\rightarrow\infty
. We write
F(q)=O\left(G(q)\right)
if there is a constant
C>0
such that
F(q)\leq CG(q)
for
all
values of
q
. We write
F(q)=o\left(G(q)\right)
if
\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}F(q)/G(q)=0
.

Next, we prove estimates for the size of
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Note that if
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j\geq p
, then the associated Artin–Schreier curve lies on an irreducible component of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
of maximal dimension (cf. Remark
3.2
). The result below shows that
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim q^{d+3}
for partitions such that
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j\geq p
, and
\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=o\left(q^{d+3}\right)
if
\kappa_{j}>0
for some value of
j
which is larger than
p
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\kappa\in\Omega_{p}(r+1,d+2)
. Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be the quantities defined in the previous section, cf. (
3.1
). We have the following assertions:

(1)

Suppose that
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j\geq p
. Then, we have that

\left(1-\frac{d+2}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{1}+1}q^{d+3}\leq\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+3},

in particular, as
q\rightarrow\infty
,

\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim q^{d+3}.

(2)

Suppose that
\kappa_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\geq p
. Then, we have that

\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=o(q^{d+3}).

Proof.

Recall that we have expressed
f(x)=h(x)/g(x)
, where
g(x)=\prod_{j}g_{j}(x)^{j}
and
\kappa_{j}=\operatorname{deg}g_{j}(x)
, where
g_{j}(x)
is a squarefree polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Assume without loss of generality that
g_{j}(x)
is a monic polynomial for all
j
. Note that the degree of
g(x)
is

\sum_{j}j\kappa_{j}=d+2-(r+1)=d-r+1.

Since
f_{\infty}(x)
is a constant, we find that
\operatorname{deg}h(x)\leq\operatorname{deg}g(x)
. Furthermore, note that by construction, the polynomials
g_{i}(x)
and
g_{j}(x)
are coprime for
i\neq j
.

First, we consider the case when
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j\geq p
. In this case, for every pole
\alpha
of
f(x)
, we have that
d_{\alpha}<p
. Thus, the condition (
3
), which requires that the coefficient of
x^{j}
be zero in
f_{\alpha}(x)
whenever
p|j
, is automatically satisfied. Furthermore,
h(x)
is coprime to
g(x)
. Therefore, the result in this case follows from Lemma
3.4
. In greater detail, the lower bound proceeds from the following observation

\left(1-\frac{d+2}{q}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{d+2-r}{q}\right).

Next, assume that for some
j>p
, we have that
\kappa_{j}\neq 0
. In this case, the same calculation goes through with the one difference being that there is an additional constraint requiring that the coefficient of
x^{j}
in
f_{\alpha}(x)
is
0
for all
j>0
such that
p|j
. This condition was automatically satisfied in the previous case. In this case, this gives an additional condition on
h(x)
, which forces the number of choices to be

\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+2}=o(q^{d+3}).

Let us explain this in greater detail.

Since
\kappa_{j}\neq 0
for some
j>p
, there exists a pole
\alpha
of
f(x)
such that
d_{\alpha}=j>p
. The coefficient of
x^{p}
in
f_{\alpha}(x)
is required to be
0
. Set
\eta(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
to denote the function given by

\eta(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(x-\alpha^{\prime})},

where
\alpha^{\prime}
ranges over the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-conjugates of
\alpha
. Note that
\eta(x)^{p}=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(x-\alpha^{\prime})^{p}}
. This function
\eta(x)^{p}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and is expressed as
\eta(x)^{p}=\frac{\eta_{1}(x)}{\eta_{2}(x)}
, with

\operatorname{deg}\eta_{1}(x)\leq\operatorname{deg}\eta_{2}(x)\leq\kappa_{j}.

Moreover, note that
\eta_{2}(x)
divides
\prod_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x-\alpha^{\prime})^{p}
, and hence divides
\prod_{\alpha^{\prime}}(x-\alpha^{\prime})^{j}
. Since this latter product divides
g(x)
, it follows that
\eta_{2}(x)
divides
g(x)
. For any choice of
0\neq c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the function
f_{c}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f(x)+c\eta(x)^{p}
does not satisfy condition (
3
) since now the coefficient of
x^{p}
in
f_{c,\alpha}(x)
is nonzero, and
p<j=d_{\alpha}
. We may express
f_{c}(x)=h_{c}(x)/g(x)
, where

\operatorname{deg}h_{c}(x)\leq\operatorname{deg}g(x).

The number of functions
f(x)=h(x)/g(x)
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.4
is
\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+3}
.

On the other hand, from an admissible function
f(x)=\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}
, we have constructed
q
new functions
f_{c}(x)
, as
c
ranges over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Only one of which is admissible (i.e., when
c=0
). It is easy to see that if
f(x)
and
F(x)
are distinct admissible functions, then
f_{c}(x)\neq F_{c^{\prime}}(x)
for all
c,c^{\prime}
. Hence, the total number of admissible functions for the partition vector
\kappa
is

\leq\frac{1}{q}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}q^{d+3}\right)=O(q^{d+2})=o(q^{d+3}).

∎

Definition 3.7
.

Let
M_{p}(r+1,d+2)
be the number of partitions
\kappa\in\Omega_{p}(r+1,d+2)
such that
\kappa_{j}=0
unless
1\leq j<p
. Set

M_{p}(d+2)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}M_{p}(r+1,d+2).

Lemma 3.8
.

With respect to notation above, we have that
M_{p}(d+2)=0
if and only if
p=2
and
d
is odd.

Proof.

The proof of the result is rather straightforward and not particularly interesting, hence it is omitted.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We note here that by the discussion in Remark
3.2
,
M_{p}(d+2)
(resp.
M_{p}(r+1,d+2)
) is equal to the number of irreducible components of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
(resp.
\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}
) of dimension
d-1
. These are the components of maximal dimension. The fraction
\frac{M_{p}(d+2)}{M_{p}(r+1,d+2)}
plays a role in the next result. It follows from the aforementioned assertions that this fraction is the proportion of irreducible components of maximal dimension in
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
parametrizing Artin–Schreier covers with
p
-rank
\tau
. Thus, the Theorem below has a suitable geometric interpretation.

Theorem 3.10
.

Fix a prime number
p
and let
r,d\geq 0
such that
r\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. Assume that
d
is even when
p=2
. Set
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
and
\tau=r(p-1)
, and consider the limits
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)
(cf. (
2.2
)) and
\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)
(cf. (
2.3
)). With respect to notation above, we have that

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)=\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)=\frac{M_{p}(r+1,d+2)}{M_{p}(d+2)}.

Proof.

Since it is assumed that
d
is even for
p=2
, it follows from Lemma
3.8
that
M_{p}(d+2)
is not equal to
0
. Note that

\#\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\sum_{\kappa\in\Omega_{p}(r+1,d+2)}\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

where we recall that
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
and
\tau=r(p-1)
. It follows from Lemma
3.6
that
\#\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim M_{p}(r+1,d+2)q^{d+3}
. Likewise, we have that
\#\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim M_{p}(d+2)q^{d+3}
, and it thus follows that

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)=\frac{M_{p}(r+1,d+2)}{M_{p}(d+2)}.

On the other hand, if
f
and
g
are both admissible, then, by Proposition
2.3
,

\mathcal{C}_{f}\simeq_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}\mathcal{C}_{g}\Leftrightarrow f=ug\text{ for }u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

Hence, each isomorphism class of covers (unramified at
\infty
) consists of exactly
(p-1)
admissible functions. As a result,

\#\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim\frac{M_{p}(r+1,d+2)}{p-1}q^{d+3}\text{ and }\#\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\sim\frac{M_{p}(d+2)}{p-1}q^{d+3},

and consequently,
\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)=\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(p,g,\tau)
.
∎

Note that
\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)
depends on
p
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Fix a prime number
p
and let
r,d\geq 0
such that
r\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. Assume that
d
is even when
p=2
. Suppose that
(r+1)p<d+2
, then,
\mathfrak{d}(p,g,\tau)=0
. In particular, if
p<d+2
, then, the proportion of isomorphism classes of Artin–Schreier covers of genus
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
and
p
-rank
\tau=0
is zero in the large
q
-limit.

Proof.

Note that when
(r+1)p<d+2
, it is not possible to partition
d+2
into
r+1
numbers, all of which are
\leq p
. In this case,
M_{p}(r+1,d+2)=0
, and the result follows from Theorem
3.10
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

It follows from Remark
3.2
that since
M_{p}(1,d+2)=0
, the irreducible components of the stratum of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
for which
\tau=0
all have dimension
<(d-1)
. These components contain fewer points than other components of
\mathcal{AS}_{g}
with dimension
(d-1)
.

3.2.
Arithmetic problem: large
p
-limit

We now study the
arithmetic
variant of the problem considered in the previous section. Many of the calculations are similar in spirit. Fix
d>0
,
r\geq 0
, and let
g=g(d,p)=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
,
\tau=\tau(r,p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r(p-1)
. Note that

and
\tau
depend on
p
and are increasing at fixed speeds as

. This is suppressed in the notation.

Set
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(d,r)
to be the limit

(3.2)

\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(d,r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}{\#\mathcal{S}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{p})},

and
set

(3.3)

\mathfrak{p}(d,r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{AS}_{g}^{\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}{\#\mathcal{AS}_{g}(\mathbb{F}_{p})},

with

and
\tau=\tau(r,p)
.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\Theta(r+1,d+2)
be the set of all partitions of
d+2
into
r+1
numbers that are all
\geq 2
. Denote by
\Theta(d+2)
the set of all partitions of
d+2
into numbers
\geq 2
. We set

and

.

It is clear that

. A partition in

is represented by a partition vector

, where

in the partition. There is no dependence on
p
in the definition of

. Note that

(3.4)

T(d+2)=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}T(r+1,d+2).

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\kappa\in\Theta(r+1,d+2)
and suppose that
\kappa_{j}=0
for all

. Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
be the quantities defined in (

). Then, we have that

\left(1-\frac{d+2}{p}\right)^{\lambda_{1}+1}p^{d+3}\leq\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}p^{d+3}.

Proof.

The result follows from the first part of Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
\kappa\in\Theta(r+1,d+2)
, and
g=g(d,p)
,
\tau=\tau(r,p)
. We have that

\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\sim p^{d+3},

as
p\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

The condition requiring
\kappa_{j}=0
for all

is satisfied when

is large, and the result thus follows from Lemma

.
∎

In the result below, there is no constraint on

when

. This is because

is always non-zero, even when

and

is odd. Recall that this was not the case for

in the statement of Theorem

, which is why the case when

and

Theorem 3.16
.

Fix a prime number
p
and let
r,d\geq 0
such that
r\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. Recall that
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(d,r)
are
\mathfrak{p}(d,r)
the limits defined by (

) and (

) respectively. Then, we have that

\mathfrak{p}(d,r)=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(d,r)=\frac{T(r+1,d+2)}{T(d+2)}.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
3.10
. Assume that
p
is large enough so that
\kappa_{j}=0
for all
j\geq p
. Recall that by abuse of notation,
g\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g(d,p)
,
\tau=\tau(r,p)
. Since

and

and

(but this is suppressed in our notation). We have that

\#\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\sum_{\kappa\in\Theta(r+1,d+2)}\#\mathcal{S}_{g}^{\kappa}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

It follows from Lemma

follows from the equalities:

\#\mathcal{AS}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\sim\frac{1}{(p-1)}\#\mathcal{S}_{g,\tau}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\text{ and }\#\mathcal{AS}_{g,}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\sim\frac{1}{(p-1)}\#\mathcal{S}_{g,}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

see the proof of Theorem

.
∎

4.
Some examples

We illustrate results proved in the previous section through examples.

4.1.
The geometric case

First, let us pick the prime
p=5
, and let
d=8
. The genus is given by
g=d\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)=8\times 2=16
. The
p
-rank
\tau=r(p-1)=4r
. The values of
r
range from
r=0
to
r=4
. Note that when
r=4
,
\tau=16=g
, and this is the case when the curve is ordinary.

We compute the proportion of isomorphism classes of Artin–Schreier covers with genus
g=16
and
5
-rank
\tau=4r
, in the large
q
-limit. There are a total of
42
partitions of
10
, however, there are constraints on the partitions we consider. Recall that
M_{p}(r+1,d+2)=M(r+1,10)
is the number of partitions

10=\sum_{j}(j+1)\kappa_{j}

for which
\kappa_{j}=0
for
j
outside the range
1\leq j<5
. Thus, we are to use only numbers
2,3,4,5
in partitioning
10
into
r+1
numbers. Let’s list the partitions for each choice of
r=0,\dots,4
.

(1)

r=0:
There is no partition, since
10=10
is not allowed. Hence,
M(1,10)=0
. Thus, the proportion of curves with
5
-rank
0
is
0
in the
q
-limit.

(2)

r=1:
We write
10=a+b
, where
a,b\in\{2,\dots,5\}
. The only partition is

10=5+5,

hence,
M(2,10)=1
.

(3)

r=2:
We write
10=a+b+c
, where
a,b,c\in\{2,\dots,5\}
. The partitions are

10=5+3+2=4+4+2=4+3+3,

hence,
M(3,10)=3
.

(4)

r=3:

We write
10=a+b+c+d
this time. Since they are all
\geq 2
, there are only two choices:

10=2+2+2+4=2+2+3+3,

hence,
M(4,10)=2
.

(5)

r=4:
We come to single ordinary case. The only partition is

10=2+2+2+2+2,

and
M(5,10)=1
.

Putting it all together, we find that

M(10)=\sum_{r=1}^{4}M(r+1,10)=0+1+3+2+1=7.

Therefore, according to Theorem
3.10
, the proportion of isomorphism classes of covers of genus
g=16
and
p
-rank
\tau=4r
is given by the following proportions:

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{d}(5,16,0)=\frac{0}{7},\\
&\mathfrak{d}(5,16,4)=\frac{1}{7},\\
&\mathfrak{d}(5,16,8)=\frac{3}{7},\\
&\mathfrak{d}(5,16,12)=\frac{2}{7},\\
&\mathfrak{d}(5,16,16)=\frac{1}{7}.\end{split}

4.2.
The arithmetic case

Let’s forget the prime
p
, and study the arithmetic problem for

. The value of

ranges from
0
to
4
and specifies a given speed at which

increases with

. In this setting, we no longer have the constraint that the partitions should involve terms from
\{2,\dots,5\}
, but only that the terms be
\geq 2

.

(1)

r=0:
This time,

is
allowed. Hence,

.

(2)

r=1:
We write

, where

. There are
4

hence,

.

(3)

r=2:
We write

. The partitions are

hence,

.

(4)

r=3:

We write

this time. Since they are all

(5)

r=4:

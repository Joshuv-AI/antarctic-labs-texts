Well-posedness and scattering for a 2D inhomogeneous NLS with Aharonov-Bohm magnetic potential
Mohamed Majdoub and Tarek Saanouni

Abstract
. We consider the magnetic nonlinear inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation i ∂ t u − ( − i ∇ + α | x | 2 ( − x 2 , x 1 ) ) 2 u = ± | x | − ϱ | u | p − 1 u , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ 2 , i\partial_{t}u-\left(-i\nabla+\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}(-x_{2},x_{1})\right)^{2}u=\pm|x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}, where α ∈ ℝ ∖ ℤ , ϱ > 0 , p > 1 \alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z},\,\varrho>0,\,p>1 . We prove a dichotomy of global existence and scattering versus blow-up of energy solutions under the ground state threshold in the inter-critical regime. The scattering is obtained by using the new approach of Dodson-Murphy (A new proof of scattering below the ground state for the 3D radial focusing cubic NLS, Proc. Am. Math. Soc. (2017)). This method is based on Tao’s scattering criteria and Morawetz estimates. The novelty here is twice: we investigate the case ϱ ​ α ≠ 0 \varrho\alpha\neq 0 and we consider general energy initial data (not necessarily radially symmetric). The particular case α = 0 \alpha=0 , known as INLS, was widely investigated in the few recent years. Moreover, the particular case ϱ = 0 \varrho=0 , which gives the homogeneous regime, was considered recently by X. Gao and C. Xu (Scattering theory for NLS with inverse-square potential in 2D, J. Math. Anal. Appl. (2020)), where the scattering is proved for spherically symmetric datum. In the radial framework, the above problem translate to the INLS with inverse square potential, which was widely investigated in space dimensions higher than three. The Hardy inequality ‖ | x | − 1 ​ f ‖ L 2 ​ ( ℝ N ) ≤ 2 N − 2 ​ ‖ ∇ f ‖ L 2 ​ ( ℝ N ) \||x|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{2}{N-2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})} , which gives the norm equivalence ‖ f ‖ H 1 ≃ ‖ f ‖ H 1 + ‖ | x | − 1 ​ f ‖ L 2 \|f\|_{H^{1}}\simeq\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\||x|^{-1}f\|_{L^{2}} , fails in two space dimensions. Thus, it is not clear how to treat the NLS with inverse square potential in H 1 H^{1} for two space dimensions. This article seems to be the first one dealing with the NLS with Aharonov-Bohm magnetic potential in the inhomogeneous regime, namely ϱ ≠ 0 \varrho\neq 0 .

1.
Introduction and main results

We consider the initial value problem for the nonlinear inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation with Aharonov-Bohm potential

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u-\left(-i\nabla+\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}(-x_{2},x_{1})\right)^{2}u=\kappa|x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}u,\\
u(t=0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the wave function is
u:=u(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the time variable,
x:=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the space variable,
p>1
is the exponent of the source term,
\varrho>0
gives a singular inhomogeneous term in the non-linearity,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
gives an Aharonov-Bohm potential and
\kappa=\pm 1
. Moreover,
\kappa=1
stands for the defocusing case while
\kappa=-1
corresponds to the focusing regime.

The linear counterpart of (
1.1
) is the so-called electromagnetic Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}u=\Big(-i\nabla+\frac{{\bf A}(\frac{x}{|x|})}{|x|}\Big)^{2}u+\frac{{\bf a}(\frac{x}{|x|})}{|x|^{2}}u,

where
{\bf a}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbf{S}^{1},\mathbb{R})
,
\mathbf{S}^{1}
denotes the unit
circle, and
{\bf A}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbf{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a transversal vector field, namely

{\bf A}(\omega)\cdot\omega=0,\quad\forall\,\,\omega\in\mathbf{S}^{1}.

The magnetic potential (1-
form)
\frac{{\bf A}(\frac{x}{|x|})}{|x|}
is connected with the associated magnetic tensor (2-form)
{\bf B}
by the
exterior derivative
{\bf B}=d\frac{{\bf A}(\frac{x}{|x|})}{|x|}
. The magnetic tensor is compatible since the Maxwell equation

d{\bf B}=0
means that
{\bf B}
is a closed form. The Aharonov–Bohm potential gives a magnetic field
associated to thin solenoids: if the radius of the solenoid tends to zero while the flux through
it remains constant, then the particle is subject to a
\delta
-type magnetic field, that is so-called
Aharonov–Bohm field. The Aharonov–Bohm (AB) effect
[
2
]
lies at the interface of
gauge theories and quantum mechanics. In its best known form,
the AB effect predicts a shift in the interference pattern of the
quantum mechanical double-slit experiment which has a magnetic
flux carrying solenoid placed between the slits. If a solenoid with
a magnetic field
{\bf B}=\nabla\times{\bf A}
(where
{\bf A}
is the electromagnetic vector
potential) is placed between the two slits of a double-slit experiment the phase of the wave-function of the electrons going
through the slits and following some path to the screen
[
29
,
24
]
. We refer to
[
5
]
for a rigorous study of the magnetic field in quantum mechanics and to
[
15
,
31
,
32
]
for other physical aspects with many references therein.

The vector potential

\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}(x):=\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}x^{\perp},\quad x^{\perp}:=(-x_{2},x_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

(1.2)

generates the Aharonov–Bohm magnetic field. We know from
[
1
]
that the operator

\left(-i\nabla+\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}\right)^{2}\quad\mbox{on}\quad C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})

(1.3)

is not essentially self-adjoint and admits infinitely many self-adjoint extensions. Here we choose to work with the Friedrichs extension of (
1.3
) denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
. To be more precise, we introduce the space
\dot{H}_{\alpha}^{1}
as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
with respect to the norm

\big\|\left(\nabla+i\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}\right)u\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

We also define the associated inhomogeneous space
H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\dot{H}_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The quadratic form on
H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

u\longmapsto\big\|\left(\nabla+i\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}\right)u\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}

is closed and generates a unique non-negative self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with domain

\mathcal{D}=\left\{f\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2});\;\;\;\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}.

Moreover, the unitary group
{\rm e}^{it\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}
extends to a group of isometries on the dual
\mathcal{D}^{*}
of
\mathcal{D}
. Therefore for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the unique solution to (
2.1
) reads

u(t,x):={\rm e}^{it\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}\,u_{0}(x)\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{D}^{*}).

Note that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
acts on functions as follows

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}u:=-\Delta u+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{|x|^{2}}u-2i\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla u=-\Delta u+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{|x|^{2}}u,

where the later equality is valid in the spherically symmetric framework.

From
[
22
, Remark 2.1, p. 3891]
and
[
22
, (4.1), p. 3895]
, we know that
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha+m}
are unitary equivalent for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that
0<|\alpha|\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

The following Hardy inequality was obtained in
[
27
, Theorem 3]
:

\left(d(\alpha,\mathbb{Z})\right)^{2}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\frac{f(x)}{|x|^{2}}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,|\nabla_{\alpha}f(x)|^{2}\,dx,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z},

(1.4)

where

\nabla_{\alpha}:=\nabla+i\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}=\nabla+i\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}x^{\perp}.

(1.5)

From (
1.5
) and (
1.4
), we obtain the following Sobolev embedding

H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\;2\leq r<\infty,\;\;\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}.

(1.6)

The dispersive and Strichartz estimates are fundamental tools in studying the linear and nonlinear dynamics for dispersive equations. In our setting, we refer to
[
17
,
33
]
. See also
[
18
, Theorem 2.3, p. 91]
for a precise statement of the dispersive estimate for the Aharonov-Bohm potential. It is now quite classical that the dispersive estimate implies Strichartz one by means of the
TT^{*}
argument of Keel-Tao
[
25
]
. See, among many,
[
33
,
22
]
and the references therein. For the case of Schrödinger operator with inverse-square potential, we refer to
[
28
]
. The Strichartz estimates for the Aharonov-Bohm potential was successfully used to obtain well-posedness and scattering for the homogeneous case, that is (
1.1
) with
\varrho=0
. In
[
34
]
, the authors prove the scattering in the defocusing regime for radially symmetric initial data under the supplementary conditions
p>3
and
2\alpha^{2}>1
. The Scattering in the focusing regime was obtained in
[
21
]
for radially symmetric initial data under the ground state threshold.

Now, we turn back to (
1.1
). The main purpose of this work is to extend and somehow improve the results in
[
21
,
9
]
to the case of singular weight in the nonlinearity, that is
\varrho>0
.

Since the inverse-square potential
|x|^{-2}
conserves the same scaling of the Laplace operator, the non-linear Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) satisfies the scaling invariance

0<\mu\longmapsto u_{\mu}(t,x):=\mu^{\frac{2-\varrho}{p-1}}u(\mu^{2}t,\mu x).

The identity
\|u_{\mu}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\mu^{s-(1-\frac{2-\varrho}{p-1})}\|u(\mu^{2}t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
gives the only one homogeneous Sobolev norm stable under the above dilatation. It is
s_{c}:=1-\frac{2-\varrho}{p-1}
called the critical Sobolev index. The energy-critical case corresponds to
s_{c}=1
, or
p=\infty
. This case is related to the energy conservation law

\displaystyle E[u(t))]

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{\alpha}u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\kappa\frac{2}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{-\varrho}|u(t)|^{p+1}\,dx=E[u_{0}].

(Energy)

The mass-critical one corresponds to
s_{c}=0
, or
p=p_{c}:=3-\varrho
which is related to mass conservation law

\displaystyle M[u(t)]

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx=M[u_{0}].

(Mass)

It is worth to mention that
s_{c}<1
provided that
\varrho<2
. This means that (
1.1
) is energy sub-critical for any
p>1
.

In the sequel we will focus on the inter-critical regime
0<s_{c}<1
, that is
3-\varrho<p<\infty
, and define the positive real number

\lambda_{c}:=\frac{1}{s_{c}}-1=\frac{p_{c}-1}{p-p_{c}}=\frac{2-\varrho}{p+\varrho-3}.

(1.7)

Here and hereafter, one denotes for simplicity the Lebesgue norms

\|\cdot\|_{r}:=\|\cdot\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{2}.

Define also the quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[u]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p+1}\,dx,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}[u]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\,u\|^{2}-\frac{B}{p+1}\mathcal{P}[u]:=\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\,u\|^{2}-\frac{p-1+\varrho}{p+1}\mathcal{P}[u].

(1.9)

Denote the scale invariant quantities

\displaystyle\mathcal{EM}[u]

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{E[u]}{E[\phi]}\Big)\Big(\frac{M[u]}{M[\phi]}\Big)^{\lambda_{c}},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{GM}[u]

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{\|\nabla\,\phi\|}\Big)\Big(\frac{M[u]}{M[\phi]}\Big)^{\frac{\lambda_{c}}{2}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{PM}[u]

\displaystyle:=\Big(\frac{\mathcal{P}[u]}{\mathcal{P}[\phi]}\Big)\Big(\frac{M[u]}{M[\phi]}\Big)^{\lambda_{c}},

(1.12)

where
\phi
is a ground state solution to (
2.12
).

Useful tools and auxiliary results

For future convenience, we recall some known and useful tools which will play an important role in the proof of our main results.

First, let us collect some standard estimates related to the linear electromagnetic Schrödinger equation:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}u=F(t,x),\\
u(t=0,x)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(2.1)

The following dispersive estimate can be found in
[
16
,
17
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
2\leq r\leq\infty
. Then,

\left\|e^{it\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}\to L^{r}}\lesssim|t|^{\frac{2}{r}-1}.

(2.2)

To state the Strichartz estimates, we need the following definition of admissible pairs.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair
(q,r)
is said to be admissible if

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2},\quad q,r\geq 2,\quad(q,r)\neq(2,\infty).

(2.3)

Let
\Gamma
be the set of all admissible pairs. For
T>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
a measurable set, define

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S(\Omega,T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{(q,r)\in\Gamma}\|u\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{r}(\Omega))},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S^{\prime}(\Omega,T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{(q,r)\in\Gamma}\|u\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(0,T;L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega))}.

For
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we simply write

\|u\|_{S(T)}=\|u\|_{S(\mathbb{R}^{2},T)},\quad\|u\|_{S^{\prime}(T)}=\|u\|_{S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2},T)}.

Thanks to the above disperive estimate (
2.2
) and an argument of Keel-Tao
[
25
]
, one obtains some Strichartz estimates as stated below.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
33
,
22
]
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,
T>0
and
u
the solution of (
2.1
). Then

\|u\|_{S(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{S^{\prime}(T)}.

(2.4)

Employing
\left[\nabla_{\alpha},\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\right]=0
together with (
2.4
), we infer that

\|\nabla_{\alpha}u\|_{S(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}+\|\nabla_{\alpha}F\|_{S^{\prime}(T)},

(2.5)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(H^{1}_{\alpha})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}}+\|\langle\nabla_{\alpha}\rangle F\|_{S^{\prime}(T)},

(2.6)

where

\langle\nabla_{\alpha}\rangle=\sqrt{1+|\nabla_{\alpha}|^{2}}.

(2.7)

We also recall the following local-in-time Strichatz estimate (see, for instance,
[

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}\,{\rm e}^{i(t-\tau)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}g(\cdot,\tau)d\tau\right\|_{S(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|g\|_{S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.8)

The following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality will be of interest in the proofs of our main results.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,
1<p<\infty
,
0<\varrho<2
.
Then the following sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{-\varrho}|f(x)|^{p+1}\,dx\leq\mathtt{K}_{opt}\|f\|^{A}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B},\quad f\in H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(2.9)

where
A
and
B
are given by

B:=p-1+\varrho\quad\text{and}\quad A:=1+p-B.

(2.10)

Moreover, the sharp constant
K_{opt}
is given by

\mathtt{K}_{opt}=\frac{p+1}{A}\Big(\frac{A}{B}\Big)^{\frac{B}{2}}\|\phi\|^{-(p-1)},

(2.11)

where
\phi
is a ground state solution to

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\phi+\phi=|x|^{-\varrho}|\phi|^{p-1}\phi,\quad 0\neq\phi\in H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(2.12)

Before getting to the proof of Proposition
2.4
, we prove the following compact Sobolev embedding.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p>1
,
0<\varrho<2
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have the compact Sobolev embedding

H_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(|x|^{-\varrho}\,dx).

(2.13)

Proof.

Suppose that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H_{\alpha}^{1}
and let
R>\frac{1}{2-\varrho}
. It follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{-\varrho}|f_{n}|^{p+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-\varrho}|f_{n}|^{p+1}\,dx+\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{-\varrho}|f_{n}|^{p+1}\,dx,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\varrho}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{\varrho+\frac{1}{R}}}(|x|<R)}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{2(1+p)}{2-\varrho-\frac{1}{R}}}(|x|<R)}^{p+1}+|R|^{-\varrho}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{R}\|f_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{2(1+p)}{2-\varrho-\frac{1}{R}}}(|x|<R)}^{p+1}+C|R|^{-\varrho}.

Owing to
\frac{2(1+p)}{2-\varrho-\frac{1}{R}}>2
and using a Rellich-Kondrachov compactness Theorem, we easily conclude the proof of Lemma
2.5

Proof of Proposition

J(u):=\frac{\|u\|^{A}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B}}{\mathcal{P}[u]},

(2.15)

(2.16)

\psi_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}v_{n}(\mu_{n}x).

(2.17)

\|\psi_{n}\|=\|\psi_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{\mathtt{k}_{opt}}=\lim_{n}\,J(\psi_{n}).

(2.18)

Then, up to a sub-sequence extraction and owing to (

Using lower semi-continuity of the

\|\psi\|=\|\psi\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}=1.

It follows that
\psi_{n}\rightarrow\psi\quad\mbox{in}\quad H^{1}_{\alpha}
and

\frac{1}{\mathtt{k}_{opt}}=J(\psi)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}[\psi]}.

Note that the minimizer
\psi
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\partial_{\varepsilon}J(\psi+\varepsilon\eta)_{|\varepsilon=0}=0,\quad\forall\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Using
\text{div}\left(\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}}\right)=0
, one can see that
\psi
solves

(p-1+\varrho)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\psi+(2-\varrho)\psi-\beta(1+p)|x|^{-\varrho}|\psi|^{p-1}\psi=0.

(2.19)

Let us pick

\lambda:=\Big((\frac{A}{B})^{-\frac{\varrho}{2}}\frac{A}{\beta(1+p)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},\quad\mu:=\Big(\frac{A}{B}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and re-scale the function
\psi
as
\psi(x)=\lambda\phi(\mu x)
. A straightforward computation yields

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\phi+\phi-|x|^{-\varrho}|\phi|^{p-1}\phi=0.

Moreover, since
\|\psi\|=1=\lambda\mu^{-1}\|\phi\|
, one gets

\frac{1}{\mathtt{k}_{opt}}=\frac{A}{p+1}\Big(\frac{A}{B}\Big)^{-\frac{B}{2}}\|\phi\|^{p-1}.

This ends the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<\varrho<1
and
1<p<\infty
. There exist
2<r<\infty
,

and
\frac{2}{p-1}<\nu<\infty

(3.2)

Proof.

Since

there exists

small enough such that

\frac{1-\varrho}{2}-\frac{p-1}{2}\varepsilon>0.

Choose

\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1-\varrho}{2}-\frac{p-1}{2}\varepsilon,

and define

It follows that
\frac{2}{p-1}<\nu<\infty
and

\frac{1}{\gamma}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}-\frac{1-\varrho}{2}+\frac{1}{\nu}=\frac{\varrho}{2}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<\varrho<1
and
1<p<\infty
. There exist
2<r<\infty
,
0<\gamma<\frac{2}{\varrho+1}

{2p}<\nu<\infty

Proof.

Since

\frac{1-\varrho}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0.

Choose

\frac{1}{r}<\frac{1-\varrho}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

It follows that

\frac{1}{\gamma}>1-\frac{1-\varrho}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=\frac{1+\varrho}{2}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
(q_{0},r_{0})=\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1},p+1\right)

\displaystyle\||u|^{p-1}v\|_{L_{T}^{q_{0}^{\prime}}(L^{r_{0}^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}(L^{r_{0}})}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L_{T}^{q_{0}}(L^{r_{0}})},

\displaystyle\|\langle\nabla_{\alpha}\rangle u\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{2}}(L^{2})}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L_{T}^{q_{0}}(L^{r_{0}})},

\displaystyle T^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\,\|\langle\nabla_{\alpha}\rangle u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{p-1}\,\|v\|_{L_{T}^{q_{0}}(L^{r_{0}})}.

This gives (
3.4

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
0<\varrho<1
,
1<p<\infty
,
T>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exist

depending only on

Proof.

We will making use of the elementary observation:

(3.7)

where

\mathbb{R}^{2}

First, let us prove the estimate (
3.5

\||x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}v\|_{S^{\prime}(T)}\leq\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}v\|_{S^{\prime}(\mathbf{B},T)},

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}v\|_{S^{\prime}(\mathbf{B}^{c},T)}.

be as in Lemma

and
q
such that
(q,r)\in\Gamma

The estimate of

We turn now to (

\|\nabla_{\alpha}\left(|x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p-1}u\right)\|_{S^{\prime}(T)}\leq\mathbf{J}_{1}+\mathbf{J}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{1}

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle:=e^{it\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}[|x|^{-\varrho}|u(s)|^{p-1}u(s)]\,ds.

4.1.
Variational Analysis

Recall that
\phi
stands for a radially symmetric decreasing solution to (
2.12
). The following inequality will be useful in obtaining a coercivity result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then,

\mathcal{P}[u]\leq\frac{p+1}{B}\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[u]}{M[\phi]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[\phi]}\bigg)^{\frac{B-2}{B}}\;\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{2},

(4.3)

where
B
is given by (
2.10
) and
\lambda_{c}
is given by (
1.7
).

Proof.

Thanks to Pohozaev identities, one has

\mathcal{P}[\phi]=\frac{p+1}{A}\,M[\phi]=\frac{p+1}{B}\Big(\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\||x|^{-1}\phi\|^{2}\Big).

(4.4)

Indeed, multiplying (
2.12
) with
\bar{\phi}
and integrating, it follows that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(\phi)\bar{\phi}\,dx+\|\phi\|^{2}=\mathcal{P}[\phi].

Moreover, since
\text{div}\left(\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}}\right)=0
, an integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(\phi)\bar{\phi}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(-\Delta\phi-2i\frac{\alpha}{|x|^{2}}x^{\perp}\cdot\nabla\phi+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\Big)\bar{\phi}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}-2i\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla\phi\,\bar{\phi}\,dx+\alpha^{2}\left\|\frac{\phi}{|x|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}+2\alpha\Re\left(i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\overline{\nabla\phi}\phi\,dx\right)+\alpha^{2}\left\|\frac{\phi}{|x|}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi\|^{2}.

Thus,

\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi\|^{2}+\|\phi\|^{2}=\mathcal{P}[\phi].

Define the action

S(\phi):=\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi\|^{2}+\|\phi\|^{2}-\frac{2}{p+1}\mathcal{P}[\phi].

Since
S^{\prime}(\phi)=0
, one gets
\partial_{\lambda}\Big(S(\phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda})\Big)_{\lambda=1}=0
, where
\phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda}(x):=\lambda^{\alpha}(\phi(\lambda^{\beta}x)

\displaystyle\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi^{\lambda}_{\alpha,\beta}\|^{2}=\lambda^{2\alpha}\Big(\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\||x|^{-1}u\|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\|\phi^{\lambda}_{\alpha,\beta}\|=\lambda^{\alpha-\beta}\|\phi\|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[\phi^{\lambda}_{\alpha,\beta}]=\lambda^{\alpha(1+p)+\beta(-2+\varrho)}\mathcal{P}[\phi].

Therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}\Big(S(\phi_{\alpha,\beta}^{\lambda})\Big)_{|\lambda=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\|{\nabla_{\alpha}}\phi\|^{2}+2(\alpha-\beta)\|u\|^{2}-2\frac{\alpha(1+p)+\beta(-2+\varrho)}{p+1}\mathcal{P}[\phi].

By taking

, we obtain that

This leads to

\|\phi\|^{2}=(1-\frac{B}{p+1})\mathcal{P}[\phi]=\frac{A}{p+1}\mathcal{P}[\phi].

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (

\displaystyle[\mathcal{P}[u]]^{\frac{B}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\tt K}_{opt}\left(\|u\|^{2\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[u]\right)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\,\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{p+1}{A}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{B}{2}}\|\phi\|^{-(p-1)}\left(M[u]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[u]\right)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B}

\displaystyle\frac{p+1}{A}\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\frac{B}{2}}M[\phi]^{\frac{A-(p-1)}{2}}[\mathcal{P}[\phi]]^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[u]}{M[\phi]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[\phi]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{A}{B}\frac{\mathcal{P}[\phi]}{M[\phi]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}}\,\bigg(\frac{M[u]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[u]}{M[\phi]^{\lambda_{c}}\mathcal{P}[\phi]}\bigg)^{\frac{B}{2}-1}\,\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\alpha}}^{B}

\displaystyle 0.

(4.16)

4.3.

In this section we give a scattering criterion as stated below.

Proposition 4.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\varrho}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{B})}^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\||x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p+1}\|_{1}^{\frac{p}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\||x|^{-\varrho}|u|^{p+1}\|_{1}^{\frac{p}{p+1}}.

Hence, using (

) and (

\displaystyle E^{p}\,T\,\varepsilon^{\beta}.

\displaystyle\|F_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{8(p-1)}{3(2-\varrho)}}((T,\infty),L^{\frac{8(p-1)}{2-\varrho}})}

\displaystyle\|F_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{8}{3}}((T,\infty),L^{8})}^{\frac{2-\varrho}{p-1}}\|F_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}((T,\infty),L^{\infty})}^{1-\frac{2-\varrho}{p-1}}

where

.
The proof of Proposition
4.7
is ended if one picks

.

∎

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{\alpha}u|^{2}\frac{b_{R}^{\prime}}{r}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\frac{|x\cdot\nabla_{\alpha}u|}{r})^{2}(1-\frac{b_{R}^{\prime}}{r})\,dx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{\alpha}u|^{2}\frac{b_{R}^{\prime}}{r}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{\alpha}u|^{2}(1-\frac{b_{R}^{\prime}}{r})\,dx

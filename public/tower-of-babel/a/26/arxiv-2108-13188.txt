Perturbation theory for fractional evolution equations in a Banach space
Arzu Ahmadova, Ismail T. Huseynov, Nazim I. Mahmudov

Abstract
A strong inspiration for studying perturbation theory for fractional evolution equations comes from the fact that they have proven to be useful tools in modeling many physical processes. In this paper, we study fractional evolution equations of order α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] \alpha\in(1,2] associated with the infinitesimal generator of an operator fractional cosine function generated by bounded time-dependent perturbations in a Banach space. We show that the abstract fractional Cauchy problem associated with the infinitesimal generator A A of a strongly continuous fractional cosine function remains uniformly well-posed under bounded time-dependent perturbation of A A . We also provide some necessary special cases.

The following lemma plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorem 3.2.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be infinitesimal generator of the strongly continuous fractional cosine family
\left\{C_{\alpha}(t;A),t\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\right\}
and

be the corresponding fractional Riemann-Liouville family on
\mathbb{X}
. For

strongly continuous on
\mathbb{R_{+}}
to
\mathbb{X}

\mathbb{R_{+}}
to
\mathbb{X}
.
If
f(t)
is strongly continuously differentiable on
\mathbb{R_{+}}
to
\mathbb{X}
, then

is strongly continuously differentiable for any

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{1}g(t)

\displaystyle=T_{\alpha}(t;A)f(0)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(t-s;A)f^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{t}T^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t-s;A)f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in\mathbb{R_{+}},

(3.24)

where

is defined as in (

).

Since
\left\lVert T_{\alpha}(t;A)\right\rVert\leq Me^{\omega t}g_{\alpha}(t)
for any

, it is obvious that

is strongly continuous in

whenever the same is true of

. In this case,
g(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(t-s;A)f(s)\mathrm{d}s
will exist in the strong topology and be equal to
\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(s;A)f(t-s)\mathrm{d}s
. Moreover, since

is strongly continuously differentiable for any

, by using the well-known integration by parts formula for the operator-valued functions, we obtain the desired result:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(t-s;A)f^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s=-T_{\alpha}(t;A)f(0)+\int\limits_{0}^{t}T^{\prime}_{\alpha}(t-s;A)f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in\mathbb{R_{+}}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\left\{C_{\alpha}(t;A):t\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\right\}
be a strongly continuous fractional cosine family with infinitesimal generator

\left\{T_{\alpha}(t;A):t\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\right\}

\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{X})
and
\mathbb{X}

2

)

It follows since

is likewise for any

. In fact, by Lemma

\displaystyle\|w_{0}^{\prime\prime}(t)x\|\leq MN_{t}e^{\omega t}\Big[g_{2\alpha-1}(t)+g_{2\alpha}(t)\Big]\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert+N_{t}\Big[g_{\alpha-1}(t)+g_{\alpha}(t)\Big]\left\lVert x\right\rVert,

\displaystyle\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}w(t)

\displaystyle=\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(t-s;A+B)f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)=\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}\Big(T_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)\Big)=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}\Big(T_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{2-\alpha}_{t}}\Big(\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\Big)\ast f(t)=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{2-\alpha}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}\Big(C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{t}}\Big(C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)\Big)\ =\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{1}_{t}}\Big(C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{1}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\ast f(t)+C_{\alpha}(0;A+B)f(t).

In a similar to (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=(A+B)C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)(A+B).

(3.31)

Since
C_{\alpha}(0;A+B)=I
and using (
3.31
), we attain

\displaystyle\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{1}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{1}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{2-\alpha}_{t}}\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}\left(A+B\right)C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=\prescript{}{}{\mathcal{I}^{\alpha-1}_{t}}C_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\left(A+B\right)

\displaystyle=(A+B)T_{\alpha}(t;A+B)=T_{\alpha}(t;A+B)(A+B).

So, we derive a desired result:

\displaystyle\prescript{C}{}{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{t}}w(t)

\displaystyle=\Big((A+B)T_{\alpha}(t;A+B)\Big)\ast f(t)+f(t)=\left(A+B\right)\int\limits_{0}^{t}T_{\alpha}(t-s;A+B)f(s)\mathrm{d}s+f(t)

\displaystyle=\Big(A+B\Big)w(t)+f(t).

The proof is complete.
∎

Slow rates of approximation of U-statistics and V-statistics by quadratic forms of Gaussians
Kevin Han Huang, Peter Orbanz

Abstract
We construct examples of degree-two U- and V-statistics of n n i.i.d. heavy-tailed random vectors in ℝ d ⁡ ( n ) \mathbb{R}^{d(n)} , whose ν \nu -th moments exist for ν > 2 {\nu>2} , and provide tight bounds on the error of approximating both statistics by a quadratic form of Gaussians. In the case ν = 3 {\nu=3} , the error of approximation is Θ ( n − 1 / 12 ) \Theta(n^{-1/12}) . The proof adapts a result of Huang, Austern and Orbanz [ 12 ] to U- and V-statistics. The lower bound for U-statistics is a simple example of the concept of variance domination used in [ 12 ] .

1 Introduction

We consider distributional approximations of degree-two U- and V-statistics that are of the form

\displaystyle u_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\;\coloneqq

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\sum_{1 \leq i \neq j \leq n} k_u(x_i, x_j)}{n(n-1)}$}}

and

\displaystyle v_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\;\coloneqq

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\sum_{1 \leq i,j \leq n} k_v(x_i, x_j)}{n^2}$}}\;.

Here,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}
are elements of a high-dimensional Euclidean space, i.e. the dimension
d(n)
may depend on
n
, and
k_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\times\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
k_{v}:\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\times\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are symmetric functions. These statistics arise frequently in the estimation of two-way interactions. Applications include gene-set testing
[
6
]
, change-point detection
[
21
]
and kernel-based tests in machine learning
[
10
]
.

Classically, with
d(n)=d
fixed, it is well-known that the distributions of both statistics admit either a Gaussian limit or a sum-of-chi-squares limit (up to reweighting and centering of the sum), depending on a degeneracy condition
[
17
]
. Recently, the high-dimensional case, where
d(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, has gained attention due to its relevance in machine learning applications. Recent work investigates (variants of) U-statistics that admit a Gaussian limit despite degeneracy
[
6
,
22
,
7
,
13
,
18
]
, or a sum-of-chi-squares limit despite non-degeneracy
[
11
,
3
]
. In all cases, the error of Gaussian approximation for both U- and V-statistics is known to be
O(n^{-1/2})

[
5
]
, and is not improvable
[
2
]
. The behavior of non-Gaussian approximation is more nuanced: The known upper bounds depend on the number of non-zero eigenvalues of the Hilbert-Schmidt operator associated with the kernel
u
of the U-statistic, ranging from
O(n^{-1})
for five non-zero eigenvalues
[
9
]
,
O(n^{-1/12})
for one non-zero eigenvalue
[
23
]
(and control of an
18/5
th moment), to
O(n^{-1/14})
without eigenvalue assumptions
[
11
]
. This note constructs a set of i.i.d. random vectors
X=(X_{i})_{i\leq n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}
, whose
\nu
-th moments exist for
\nu\in(2,3]
, and functions
k_{u}
and
k_{v}
, such that the following error bounds hold for the approximation by a quadratic form of Gaussians.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
\nu\in(2,3]
. Let
\chi^{2}_{1}
be a chi-squared random variable with
1
degree of freedom,
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
be independent of
\chi^{2}_{1}
and
\overline{\chi^{2}_{1}}=\chi^{2}_{1}-1
. There exist some constants
c,C>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(\sigma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\sigma_{n}\rightarrow 0
, where
X
,
k_{u}
and
k_{v}
depend on
\sigma_{n}
, such that for all
n>N
and
d(n)\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\sup\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,u_{n}(X)\leq t\big)-\mathbb{P}\Big(\,\sigma_{n}\xi+\overline{\chi_{1}^{2}}\,\leq\,t\,\Big)\Big|\;\leq\;Cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;,

\displaystyle cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\sup\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathbb{P}\big(n\,v_{n}(X)\leq t\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(\,\sigma_{n}\xi+\chi^{2}_{1}\leq t\,\big)\Big|\;\leq\;Cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;.

Remark 1
.

(i) When
\nu=3
,
Theorem

1
says that the approximation error is
\Theta(n^{-\frac{1}{12}})
. (ii) In the construction, we choose
\sigma_{n}
to decay as
\Theta\big(n^{-\frac{\nu-2}{2\nu}}\big)
. The approximation becomes a chi-squared approximation in the limit

, but at a very slow rate. (iii) Since

can be obtained as the limiting distribution of a partial sum of weighted and centred chi-squared variables,

can be read as a result on the approximation of

and

by infinite sums of weighted and shifted chi-squares. For further discussions on when a Gaussian component may emerge from an infinite sum of weighted chi-squares, see
[

]
.

Theorem

is an instance of the broader theme of Gaussian universality: Consider a set of i.i.d. random vectors
Y=(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{b}
and the collection of Gaussian vectors

\displaystyle Z\;=

\displaystyle\;(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})

\displaystyle Z_{i}\;\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}],\text{\rm Var}[Y_{1}])\;.

The term Gaussian universality refers to the fact that, for a large class of functions

is close to

in distribution as

,

for some intermediate functions
\tilde{u}_{n}
and
\tilde{v}_{n}
, and approximate them by
\tilde{u}_{n}(Z)
and
\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)
respectively. The lower bound of

1
adapts a degree-

constructed in our recent work

—inspired by Senatov

. The upper bound of

improves upon our generic universality result in

]
by using an argument specific to

, instead of applying Lindeberg’s technique. To extend these results to

2 Construction of
k_{u}
,
k_{v}
and
X

We first recall the lower bound construction from
[
12
]
in the case
m=2
. The construction involves a polynomial
p^{*}_{n}:(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle p^{*}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;\coloneqq

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i1}+\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i2}\Big)^{2}\qquad\text{ for }y_{i}=(y_{i1},y_{i2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;.

The collection of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
random vectors
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
are generated as follows. For a fixed
\sigma_{0}>0
and
\nu\in(2,3]
, write
\sigma_{n}=\min\{\sigma_{0}\,n^{-(\nu-2)/2\nu}\,,\,1\}
. Note that this is the sequence
\sigma_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
Theorem

1
. Let
U_{\sigma_{n}}
be the discrete random variable supported at three points with

\displaystyle U_{\sigma_{n}}\;=\;\begin{cases}-6^{-1/2}\sigma_{n}^{-2/(\nu-2)}\qquad&\text{ with probability }2\sigma_{n}^{2\nu/(\nu-2)}\;,\\
0\qquad&\text{ with probability }1-3\sigma_{n}^{2\nu/(\nu-2)}\;,\\
2\times 6^{-1/2}\sigma_{n}^{-2/(\nu-2)}\qquad&\text{ with probability }\sigma_{n}^{2\nu/(\nu-2)}\;.\end{cases}

U_{\sigma_{n}}
is constructed such that it becomes increasingly heavy-tailed as
\sigma_{n}\rightarrow 0
. Now let
U_{1;\sigma_{n}},\ldots,U_{n;\sigma_{n}}
be i.i.d. copies of
U_{\sigma_{n}}
. The i.i.d. random vectors
Y=(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}
are generated by

\displaystyle Y_{i;\sigma_{n}}\;\coloneqq\;\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{2}}$}}U_{i;\sigma_{n}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\sigma_n}{\sqrt{2}}$}}\xi_{i1}\,,\,\xi_{i2}\Big)\hskip 30.00005pt\;\text{ where }\;\;\xi_{11},\xi_{12},\ldots,\xi_{n1},\xi_{n2}\;\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\;\mathcal{N}(0,1)\;.

To adapt
p^{*}_{n}(Y)
to our setup, we first observe that
p^{*}_{n}
can be rewritten as a V-statistic:

\displaystyle p^{*}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i,j=1}^{n}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{y_{i1}}{2\sqrt{n}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{y_{j1}}{2\sqrt{n}}$}}+y_{i2}\,y_{j2}\Big)\;=\;n\,\tilde{v}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;,

where we have defined, for
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;\coloneqq\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n^2}$}}\msum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{k}_{v}(y_{i},y_{j})\;,\hskip 15.00002pt\tilde{k}_{v}((a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2}))\;\coloneqq\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{a_1}{2\sqrt{n}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{b_1}{2\sqrt{n}}$}}+a_{2}b_{2}\;.

Moreover, since we have no restrictions on how
d(n)
depends on
n
, there are non-unique choices of a function
\phi_{d(n)}:\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a probability measure
\mu_{d(n)}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d(n)}
such that

\displaystyle X_{1}\;\sim

\displaystyle\;\mu_{d(n)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\phi_{d(n)}(X_{1})\;\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\;Y_{1;\sigma_{n}}\;.

Our construction of the V-statistic is thus given by taking
X_{i}\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\mu_{d(n)}
and
k_{v}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq\tilde{k}_{v}(\phi_{d(n)}(x_{1}),\phi_{d(n)}(x_{2}))
, which gives

\displaystyle v_{n}(X)\;=\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n^2}$}}\msum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}k_{v}(X_{i},X_{j})\;\overset{d}{=}\tilde{v}_{n}(Y)\;=\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\,p^{*}_{n}(Y)\;,

where
\overset{d}{=}
denotes equality in distribution. This makes the lower bound from
[
12
]
immediately applicable. For the U-statistics construction, we observe that

\displaystyle p^{*}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}$}}\msum_{i\neq j}^{n}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{y_{i1}}{2\sqrt{n-1}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{y_{i2}}{2\sqrt{n-1}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\sqrt{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\,y_{i2}y_{j2}\Big)+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i2}^{2}

\displaystyle\;=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})+R_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;,

where we have defined, for
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;\coloneqq\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n(n-1)}$}}\msum_{i\neq j}\tilde{k}_{u}(y_{i},y_{j})\;,\hskip 15.00002ptR_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;\coloneqq\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i2}^{2}\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{u}((a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2}))\;\coloneqq\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{a_1}{2\sqrt{n-1}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{b_1}{2\sqrt{n-1}}$}}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\sqrt{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\,a_{2}b_{2}\;.

Therefore, our construction of the U-statistic is to take
k_{u}(x_{1},x_{2})\coloneqq\tilde{k}_{u}(\phi_{d(n)}(x_{1}),\phi_{d(n)}(x_{2}))
, which gives

\displaystyle u_{n}(X)\;=\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n(n-1)}$}}\msum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}k_{u}(X_{i},X_{j})\;\overset{d}{=}\tilde{u}_{n}(Y)\;=\;p^{*}_{n}(Y)-R_{n}(Y)\;.

The main technical task is thus to show that
p^{*}_{n}(Y)
approximates a chi-squared distribution and that
R_{n}(Y)
has negligible effect other than centering the chi-squared distribution.

3 Variance domination

One main technique used in the proofs is the idea of variance domination: When computing the limit of a sum of two dependent, real-valued random variables
X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}
, it suffices to ignore
Y^{\prime}
in the limit provided that the variance of
Y^{\prime}
is negligible compared to that of
X^{\prime}
. The same idea is used in
[
12
]
for extending their universality results to non-polynomial functions. The next result summarises the technique.

Proposition 2
.

Let
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{R}
-valued, possibly dependent random variables with
\mathbb{E}[Y^{\prime}]=0
. Then for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq t)-

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t)\big|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\inf_{\epsilon>0}\Big(\max\big\{\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\in(t-\epsilon,t])\,,\,\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\in(t,t+\epsilon])\big\}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{ \epsilon^2}$}}\Big)\;.

If we further have
\sigma^{2}_{X^{\prime}}\coloneqq\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}]>0
, then

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}

\displaystyle(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime})\leq t\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\leq t\big)\big|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\inf_{\epsilon>0}\Big(\max\big\{\;\mathbb{P}(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\in(t-\epsilon,t])\,,\,\mathbb{P}(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\in(t,t+\epsilon])\big\}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{\Var[X'] \, \epsilon^2}$}}\Big)\;.

Remark 2
.

Several adaptations are useful: (i) To swap the roles of
X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}
, one may replace
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
above by
X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}
and
-Y^{\prime}
respectively; (ii) To replace
\sigma_{X^{\prime}}
by another normalisation, e.g.
\sqrt{\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}]}
, one may rescale
t
and
\epsilon
simultaneously.

Proposition

2
formalises the variance domination effect: If
\text{\rm Var}[Y^{\prime}]/\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}]=o(1)
, by choosing
\epsilon=(\text{\rm Var}[Y^{\prime}]/\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}])^{1/3}
,
Proposition

2
implies that the c.d.f. difference
\big|\mathbb{P}(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime})\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\leq t)\big|=o(1)
. Meanwhile, to get a finite-sample control, one needs an anti-concentration bound on
\sigma_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}X
. By a triangle inequality, it also suffices if
\sigma_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}X
is well-approximated in distribution by some random variable
Z^{\prime}
with an anti-concentration bound. For polynomials of a random vector following a log-concave probability measure, a celebrated anti-concentration result is available due to
[
4
]
. This in particular applies to polynomials of Gaussian random vectors.

Fact 3

(Carbery-Wright inequality, Theorem 8 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
q_{m}(\mathbf{x})
be a degree-
m
polynomial of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
, and
\eta
be an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random vector following a log-concave probability measure. Then there exists a constant
C
independent of
q_{m}
,
d
,
m
or
\eta
such that, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(|q_{m}(\eta)|\leq\epsilon\big)\;\leq\;Cm\,\epsilon^{1/m}(\mathbb{E}[|q_{m}(\eta)|^{2}])^{-1/{2m}}\;.

This immediately implies the following corollary of
Proposition

2
:

Corollary 4
.

Let
X^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime}
and
\sigma_{X^{\prime}}>0
be defined as in
Proposition

2
, and
q_{m}(\eta)
be given as in
Fact

3
. Suppose
\text{\rm Var}[q_{m}(\eta)]=\text{\rm Var}\big[\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\big]=1
. Then there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\sup\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}\big(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq t\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(X^{\prime}\leq t\big)\big|

\displaystyle\;\leq\;Cm\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{\Var[X']}$}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2m+1}}+2\sup\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}\big(\sigma^{-1}_{X^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\leq t\big)-\mathbb{P}\big(q_{m}(\eta)\leq t\big)\big|\;.

Proof of
Corollary

4
.

The result follows from combining the second statement of
Proposition

2
and
Fact

3
, noting that
\mathbb{E}[|q_{m}(\eta)|^{2}]\geq\text{\rm Var}[q_{m}(\eta)]=1
, choosing
\epsilon=(\text{\rm Var}[Y^{\prime}]/\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}])^{m/(2m+1)}
and taking a supremum over
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

The generality of
Proposition

2
comes at a cost: It does not provide the tightest control on the c.d.f. difference in general, as the variance ratio term comes from the Markov’s inequality. When one has more information about the tail behaviour of
Y^{\prime}/\sqrt{\text{\rm Var}[X^{\prime}]}
, the bound can usually be improved.

4 Proofs

Throughout this section, we denote the collection of Gaussian vectors

\displaystyle Z\;=

\displaystyle\;(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})

where

\displaystyle Z_{i}\;\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}(\mathbb{E}[Y_{1;\sigma_{n}}],\text{\rm Var}[Y_{1;\sigma_{n}}])\;=\;\mathcal{N}\Big(\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{2}_{n}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\Big)\;.

We state three intermediate results used in the proof of
Theorem

1
. The first two results concerns the Gaussian universality approximations of
\tilde{v}_{n}(Y)
and
\tilde{u}_{n}(Y)
. We improve the upper bound of
[
12
]
,
n^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu+2}}
, by using an argument specific to the construction instead of the generic Lindeberg’s technique used in
[
12
]
. Note that the
\sigma_{0}
-dependence below arises because
Y
and
Z
are implicitly parameterised by
\sigma_{0}
.

Lemma 5
.

Fix
\nu\in(2,3]
. Then there exist some absolute constants
{c,C,\sigma_{0}>0}
and
{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that for all
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\sup\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbb{P}(n\,\tilde{v}_{n}(Y)\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(n\,\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)\leq t)\big|\;\leq\;Cn^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;.

Lemma 6
.

Fix
\nu\in(2,3]
and let
\sigma_{0}
be given as in
Lemma

5
. Then there exist some absolute constants
{c^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0}
and
{N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that for all
n\geq N^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle c^{\prime}n^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;\leq

\displaystyle\;\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(Y)\leq t\Big)-\mathbb{P}\Big(\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(Z)\leq t\Big)\Big|\;\leq\;C^{\prime}n^{-\frac{\nu-2}{4\nu}}\;.

Now observe that the universality approximations can be expressed as

\displaystyle n\,\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)\;=

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i1}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i,j=1}^{n}Z_{i2}Z_{j2}\;\overset{d}{=}\;\sigma_{n}\xi+\chi^{2}_{1}\;,

\displaystyle\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(Z)\;=

\displaystyle\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{\sqrt{n}}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i1}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i\neq j}Z_{i2}Z_{j2}\;\overset{d}{=}\;\sigma_{n}\xi+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i\neq j}Z_{i2}Z_{j2}\;.

By using the triangle inequality to combine the U-statistic bounds from

4.2. Proof of
Proposition

2

The proof relies on the following result:

Lemma 8

(Lemma 25 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
be two real-valued random variables. For any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a\leq X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq b)\;\leq\;\mathbb{P}(a-\epsilon\leq X^{\prime}\leq b+\epsilon)+\mathbb{P}(|Y^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon)\;,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a\leq X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq b)\;\geq\;\mathbb{P}(a+\epsilon\leq X^{\prime}\leq b-\epsilon)-\mathbb{P}(|Y^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon)\;.

Proof of
Proposition

2
.

By
Lemma

8
followed by the Markov’s inequality, we get that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq t)\;\leq\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t+\epsilon)+\mathbb{P}(|Y^{\prime}|\geq\epsilon)\;\leq\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t+\epsilon)+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{\epsilon^2}$}}\;,

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq t)\;\geq\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t-\epsilon)-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{\epsilon^2}$}}\;.

Subtracting
\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t)
from both sides, we get that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}\leq t)-\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\leq t)\big|\;\leq\;\max\big\{\;\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\in(t-\epsilon,t])\,,\,\mathbb{P}(X^{\prime}\in(t,t+\epsilon])\big\}+\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[Y']}{\epsilon^2}$}}\;,

and taking an infimum over
\epsilon>0
gives the first bound. The second bound follows by rescaling
t
and
\epsilon
at the same time by
\sigma_{X}^{\prime}>0
.
∎

\text{\rm Var}[R_{n}(Z)]=\frac{2}{n}
and that
\text{\rm Var}[n\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)]=\text{\rm Var}[p^{*}_{n}(Z)]\geq 2
. Since

\displaystyle\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(Z)\;=\;n\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i2}^{2}\;=\;\big(n\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)-1\big)-\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{n}$}}\msum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i2}^{2}-1\Big)\;,

we can again apply variance domination (
Corollary

4
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\Big|\mathbb{P}\Big(\sqrt{n(n-1)}\,\tilde{u}_{n}(Z)\leq t\Big)-\mathbb{P}\Big(n\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)-1\leq t\Big)\Big|\;\leq

\displaystyle\;C\bigg(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{\Var[ R_n(Z) ]}{ \Var[ n \tilde v_n(Z) - 1] }$}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{5}}\;=\;Cn^{-\frac{1}{5}}

for some absolute constant
C>0
. Noting that
n\tilde{v}_{n}(Z)-1\overset{d}{=}\sigma_{n}\xi+\overline{\chi^{2}_{1}}
finishes the proof.
∎

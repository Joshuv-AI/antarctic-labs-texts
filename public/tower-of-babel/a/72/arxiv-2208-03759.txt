Implication in weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattices
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
Weakly orthomodular and dually weakly orthomodular lattices were introduced by the authors in a recent paper. Similarly as for orthomodular lattices we try to introduce an implication in these lattices which can be easily axiomatized and which yields a nice lattice structure. As shown in the paper, this can be realized in several different ways. Moreover, we reveal the connection of weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattices to residuated structures. Moreover, we provide a characterization of these lattices by means of certain generalized measures.

Orthomodular lattices were introduced independently by K. Husimi (

that the orthomodular law may be satisfied also in complemented lattices which are not orthomodular because their complementation need not be an orthocomplementation. This fact was the starting point for the study

by the authors where the concepts of a weakly orthomodular respectively dually weakly orthomodular lattice were introduced and elaborated. It was shown that these lattices have a number of features similar to those of orthomodular lattices despite the fact that de Morgan laws do not hold there. The classes of such lattices form varieties and it was shown that these varieties are congruence permutable and congruence regular and, of course, congruence distributive. It is a natural question if weakly orthomodular and/or dually weakly orthomodular lattices can serve as an algebraic semantics for a reasonable propositional logic. As usually, when a non-classical logic is investigated, one is focused on the logical connective implication because this connective is preferably used in logical deduction and derivations. For orthomodular lattices, there exist several ways how to introduce implication. The most popular is the so-called Sasaki implication but also other possible modifications were treated by several authors. Due to the lack of de Morgan laws for weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattices, we have here more possibilities how to define an implication. Hence, our problem is to choose those which can be characterized within the theory of weakly and/or dually weakly orthomodular lattices by simple identities and, moreover, have a structure with a nice interpretation. This is the aim of the paper.

In the following all algebras considered are assumed to have non-empty universe.

x\rightarrow 1\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge 1)\approx x^{\prime}\vee x\approx 1
,

(iii)

x\vee(x\rightarrow y)\approx x\vee x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\approx 1
,

(iv)

x\rightarrow(x\wedge y)\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge x\wedge y)\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\approx x\rightarrow y
,

(v)

x\leq y
implies
x\rightarrow y=x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)=x^{\prime}\vee x=1
,

(vi)

if
\mathbf{L}
has a
0
then
x\rightarrow 0\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge 0)\approx x^{\prime}\vee 0\approx x^{\prime}
.

∎

Now, we want to investigate the question how to characterize weakly orthomodular or dually weakly orthomodular lattices via the systems of their principal filters or principal ideals, respectively, which are determined by special mappings of the lattice into the interval
[0,1]
of the real numbers. Hence, such mappings can be considered as generalized measures.

Definition 12
.

For a lattice
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
with a unary operation we define

\displaystyle S_{1}(\mathbf{L}):=\{s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]\mid s(x\vee(y\wedge x^{\prime}))=s(y)\text{ for all }x,y\in L\text{ with }x\leq y\},

\displaystyle S_{2}(\mathbf{L}):=\{s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]\mid s(y\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime}))=s(x)\text{ for all }x,y\in L\text{ with }x\leq y\}.

Let us mention that the sets
S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
and
S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
are non-empty since they contain every constant function from
L
to
[0,1]
, in particular the constant functions with value
0
respectively
1
.

Theorem 13
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a lattice with a unary operation. Then the following hold:

(i)

\mathbf{L}
is weakly orthomodular if and only if for every
x\in L
which is not the smallest element of
\mathbf{L}
there exists some
s\in S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{1\})=[x)
.

(ii)

\mathbf{L}
is dually weakly orthomodular if and only if for every
x\in L
which is not the greatest element of
\mathbf{L}
there exists some
s\in S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{0\})=(x]
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in L
and assume
a\leq b
.

(i)

If
\mathbf{L}
is weakly orthomodular then for every
x\in L
every
s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfying
s(y)=1
if
y\geq x
and
s(y)<1
otherwise is an element of
S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{1\})=[x)
. Conversely, assume that for every
x\in L
which is not the smallest element of
\mathbf{L}
there exists some
s\in S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{1\})=[x)
. If
b
is the smallest element of
\mathbf{L}
then
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})=b
since
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\leq b
. Now assume
b
not to be the smallest element of
\mathbf{L}
. Then there exists some
s\in S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
with
s^{-1}(\{1\})=[b)
. Now
s(a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime}))=s(b)=1
and hence
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\geq b
. Together with
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\leq b
we obtain
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})=b
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{L}
is dually weakly orthomodular then for every
x\in L
every
s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfying
s(y)=0
if
y\leq x
and
s(y)>0
otherwise is an element of
S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{0\})=(x]
. Conversely, assume that for every
x\in L
which is not the greatest element of
\mathbf{L}
there exists some
s\in S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
satisfying
s^{-1}(\{0\})=(x]
. If
a
is the greatest element of
\mathbf{L}
then
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})=a
since
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})\geq a
. Now assume
a
not to be the greatest element of
\mathbf{L}
. Then there exists some
s\in S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
with
s^{-1}(\{0\})=(a]
. Now
s(b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime}))=s(a)=0
and hence
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})\leq a
. Together with
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})\geq a
we obtain
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})=a
.

∎

One can ask why the function
s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]
was introduced in this way. The right answer is that it was mentioned as a generalized measure on a lattice with complementation. The problem is that we cannot assume de Morgan laws in our case. Thus relatively strong assumptions are necessary as stated in Definition
12
. However, we are able to get a more detailed way how to introduce such functions.

For a lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
with a unary operation
′
and a mapping
s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]
we define the following conditions:

(i)

s(1)=1
,

(ii)

if
x,y\in L
and
x\leq y
then
s(x\vee y^{\prime})=s(x)+s(y^{\prime})
,

(iii)

if
x,y\in L
and
x\leq y
then
s((x\vee y^{\prime})\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y))=1
,

(iv)

if
x,y\in L
and
x\leq y
then
s(x\vee(y\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime}))^{\prime})=1
.

It should be mentioned that conditions (iii) and (iv) are not so strong as it looks at the first glance. Namely, if
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
satisfies de Morgan laws then

\displaystyle(x\vee y^{\prime})\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)

\displaystyle\approx(x^{\prime}\wedge y)^{\prime}\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\approx 1,

\displaystyle x\vee(y\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime}))^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx x\vee(y^{\prime}\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime})\approx(x\vee y^{\prime})\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx 1.

Thus (iii) and (iv) follow directly by the simple condition (i). In other words, this generalized measure
s
does not distinguish certain terms that coincide whenever de Morgan laws hold.

Now we can state

Proposition 14
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a lattice with a complementation and
s\colon L\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfy
(i)
and
(ii)
. Then
s\in S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
if
s
satisfies
(iii)
and
s\in S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
if
s
satisfies
(iv)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in L
. Since
a\leq a
we have

s(a)+s(a^{\prime})=s(a\vee a^{\prime})=s(1)=1

according to (i) and (ii) whence
s(a^{\prime})=1-s(a)
. Now assume
a\leq b
. Then
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\leq b
and

s(a\vee(a^{\prime}\wedge b))+s(b^{\prime})=s((a\vee(a^{\prime}\wedge b))\vee b^{\prime})=s((a\vee b^{\prime})\vee(a^{\prime}\wedge b))=1

according to (ii) and (iii) and hence

s(a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime}))=1-s(b^{\prime})=1-(1-s(b))=s(b),

i.e.
s\in S_{1}(\mathbf{L})
. Similarly, we have
a\leq b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})
and

s(a)+s((b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime}))^{\prime})=s(a\vee(b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime}))^{\prime})=1

according to (ii) and (iv) whence

s(b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})=1-(1-s(b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})))=1-s((b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime}))^{\prime})=s(a)

proving
s\in S_{2}(\mathbf{L})
.
∎

A connection between orthomodular lattices and residuated structures was investigated by the authors in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
. Despite the fact that the complementation in lattices being both weakly and dually weakly orthomodular need not be antitone, we are still able to find a residuated structure which can be assigned to every such lattice. This is important from the point of view of algebraic semantics because residuated structures are applied in algebraic semantics of several fuzzy logics. Thus, although we started with a generalization of the semantics of the logic of quantum mechanics, we can provide a bridge between these rather different approaches. For this reason, the W-implication introduced before Theorem
8
turns out to be more appropriate.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a dually weakly orthomodular lattice and
a,b,c\in L
and define

\displaystyle x\odot y

\displaystyle:=(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y,

\displaystyle x\rightarrow y

\displaystyle:=x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)

for all
x,y\in L
. If
a\leq b\rightarrow c
then
a\odot b\leq c
.

Proof.

Using dual weak orthomodularity, if
a\leq b\rightarrow c
we compute

\displaystyle a\odot b

\displaystyle=(a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b\leq((b\rightarrow c)\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b=(b^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge c)\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b=b\wedge((b\wedge c)\vee b^{\prime})=

\displaystyle=b\wedge c\leq c.

∎

Consider the lattice
{\rm M}_{3}
depicted in Fig. 1 where the unary operation
′
is defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccc}x&0&a&b&c&1\\
\hline\cr x^{\prime}&1&b&c&b&0\end{array}

It is easy to check that
(M_{3},\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is both weakly and dually weakly orthomodular, but not orthomodular since
(a^{\prime})^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=c\neq a
. Hence it does not satisfy the double negation law. On the other hand,
((x^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}
. This is e.g. the condition for negation in intuitionistic logic. For such lattices we can state the following theorem.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattice satisfying
((x^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}
and
a,b,c\in L
and define

\displaystyle x\odot y

\displaystyle:=(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y,

\displaystyle x\rightarrow y

\displaystyle:=x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)

for all
x,y\in L
. Then
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x\rightarrow y)\odot x\approx x\wedge y
. Moreover,

a\odot(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\leq c\text{ if and only if }a\leq(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\rightarrow c.

Proof.

We have

(x\rightarrow y)\odot x\approx(x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime})\wedge x\approx x\wedge((x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime})\approx x\wedge y.

If
a\leq(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\rightarrow c
then
a\odot(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\leq c
according to Lemma
16
. Conversely, assume
a\odot(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\leq c
. Using weak orthomodularity and the identity
((x^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}
we compute

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq a\vee b^{\prime}=b^{\prime}\vee((a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge(b^{\prime})^{\prime})=b^{\prime}\vee((b^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge(a\vee((b^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime})\wedge(b^{\prime})^{\prime})=

\displaystyle=b^{\prime}\vee((b^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge(a\odot(b^{\prime})^{\prime}))\leq b^{\prime}\vee((b^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge c)=((b^{\prime})^{\prime})^{\prime}\vee((b^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge c)=(b^{\prime})^{\prime}\rightarrow c.

∎

For the reader’s convenience, we repeat the following concept which is frequently used in the theory of residuated structures.

Definition 18
.

A left residuated
l
-groupoid is an algebra
{\mathbf{L}}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
of type
(2,2,2,2,0,0)
such that
(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is a bounded lattice,
x\odot 1\approx 1\odot x\approx x
and for all
x,y,z\in L
,
x\odot y\leq z
is equivalent to
x\leq y\rightarrow z
.

For lattices being both weakly and dually weakly orthomodular and satisfying the double negation law we can prove a result which is stronger than that of Theorem
17
, see the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattice satisfying the double negation law and
a,b\in L
and define

\displaystyle x\odot y

\displaystyle:=(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y,

\displaystyle x\rightarrow y

\displaystyle:=x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)

for all
x,y\in L
. Then
\mathbf{L}
is bounded and
(L,\vee,\wedge,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
a left residuated
l
-groupoid satisfying the identity

(x\rightarrow y)\odot x\approx x\wedge y.

Moreover,

\displaystyle a\odot b=a

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }a\leq b,

\displaystyle a\rightarrow b=b

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }a^{\prime}\leq b.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2
,
\mathbf{L}
is bounded and
1^{\prime}\approx 0
. Let
c\in L
. If
a\odot b\leq c
then, due to weak orthomodularity of
\mathbf{L}
and to the double negation law,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leq a\vee b^{\prime}=b^{\prime}\vee((a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge(b^{\prime})^{\prime})=b^{\prime}\vee((a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b)=b^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b)=

\displaystyle=b^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge(a\odot b))\leq b^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge c)=b\rightarrow c.

Conversely, if
a\leq b\rightarrow c
then
a\odot b\leq c
according to Lemma
16
. Moreover,

\displaystyle x\odot 1

\displaystyle\approx(x\vee 1^{\prime})\wedge 1\approx(x\vee 0)\wedge 1\approx x\wedge 1\approx x,

\displaystyle 1\odot x

\displaystyle\approx(1\vee x^{\prime})\wedge x\approx 1\wedge x\approx x,

\displaystyle(x\rightarrow y)\odot x

\displaystyle\approx(x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime})\wedge x\approx x\wedge((x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime})\approx x\wedge y.

Now the following are equivalent:
a\odot b=a
;
(a\vee b^{\prime})\wedge b=a
;
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})=a
;
a\leq b
. Finally, the following are equivalent:
a\rightarrow b=b
;
a^{\prime}\vee(a\wedge b)=b
;
a^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge(a^{\prime})^{\prime})=b
;
a^{\prime}\leq b
.
∎

The lattice whose Hasse diagram is depicted in Fig. 2

We can prove also the converse of Theorem
19
.

Theorem 21
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\odot,\rightarrow,0,1)
be a left residuated
l
-groupoid satisfying the identities

\displaystyle(x\rightarrow 0)\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\approx x,

\displaystyle x\odot y

\displaystyle\approx(x\vee(y\rightarrow 0))\wedge y,

\displaystyle x\rightarrow y

\displaystyle\approx(x\rightarrow 0)\vee(x\wedge y)

and define
x^{\prime}:=x\rightarrow 0
for all
x\in L
. Then
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is a weakly and dually weakly orthomodular lattice satisfying the double negation law.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in L
and
a\leq b
. Then
a\leq b^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge a)=b\rightarrow a
and hence
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})=a\odot b\leq a
according to left-adjointness. This shows
b\wedge(a\vee b^{\prime})\leq a
. Since the converse inequality is obvious,
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is dually weakly orthomodular. Now
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\leq b
. To prove the converse inequality, we must use the double negation law. We have

b\odot a^{\prime}=(b\vee(a^{\prime})^{\prime})\wedge a=(b\vee a)\wedge a^{\prime}=b\wedge a^{\prime}\leq b.

Thus, using left-adjointness, we obtain that
b\odot a^{\prime}\leq b
is equivalent to

b\leq a^{\prime}\rightarrow b=(a^{\prime})^{\prime}\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})=a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})

proving
a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})\geq b
. Together we have
b=a\vee(b\wedge a^{\prime})
. Thus
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
is also weakly orthomodular and, obviously, satisfies the double negation law.
∎

Abstract
. We propose indifference pricing to estimate the value of the weak information. Our framework allows for tractability, quantifying the amount of additional information, and permits the description of the smallness and the stability with respect to small perturbations of the weak information. We provide sharp conditions for the stability with counterexamples. The results rely on a theorem of independent interest on the stability of the optimal investment problem with respect to small changes in the physical probability measure. We also investigate contingent claims that are indifference price invariant with respect to changes in weak information. We show that, in incomplete models, the class of information-invariant claims includes the replicable claims, and it can be strictly bigger. In particular, in complete models, all contingent claims are information invariant. We augment the results with examples and counterexamples.

2.1.
Weak anticipation

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a Polish space, e.g.,
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{R}^{n}

be an
\mathcal{F}

T

Let us consider an insider who is weakly informed of
\sf Y

\left(\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
and of the

of
\sf Y

on
\left(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})\right)

\nu
is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
and note that in
[
Bau03
]
, more conditions are imposed. Following
[
Bau03

, one can set a probability measure

on

\mathbb{P}^{\nu}(A)=\int_{\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{P}[A|\sf Y=y]\nu(dy),\quad A\in\mathcal{F},

which is called, in
[

]
, the minimal probability measure associated with the weak information
(\sf Y,\nu)
.

Multiple results for the initial enlargement of filtration can be recovered from the weak anticipation approach. These include the semimartingale decomposition in the Jacod theorem - the key component for quantifying the value of information and results on stochastic differential equations, among others. We refer to
[
Bau02
]
and
[
Bau03
]
for more details, where additionally dynamic conditioning, modeling weak information flow, and even connections to the theory of Schrödinger processes are developed.

2.2.
Our formulation

The weak anticipation approach allows us to consider small perturbations of information by supposing that
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
is close in some sense to
\mathbb{P}
. In turn, this leads to the concepts of continuity and stability of various problems with respect to small changes in information that can be modeled via alterations of the physical probability measure.

Therefore, we can consider a sequence of equivalent probability measures on one fixed probability space, converging to a physical probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, and see how various problems related to indifference pricing respond. For the notion of convergence of probability measures on a fixed space, we propose the total variation norm. These considerations are represented by the following assumption.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let us consider a sequence of probability measures
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
, and let
Z^{n}_{T}:=\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{n}}{d\mathbb{P}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be the sequence of the corresponding Radon-Nikodym derivatives.
We suppose that

\mathbb{P}\text{-}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Z^{n}_{T}=1=:Z^{\infty}_{T}.

Remark 2.3
.

Assumption
2.2
by means of Scheffe’s lemma implies that

\mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})\text{-}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Z^{n}_{T}=1.

We refer to
[
JS03
, Section V.4]
for characterizations of such a convergence.

Let us denote
\mathbb{N}^{*}:=\mathbb{N}\cup{\{\infty\}}
,
\mathbb{P}^{\infty}:=\mathbb{P}
, and consider the following problems.

(2.2)

u_{n}(x):=\sup\limits_{X\in\mathcal{X}(x)}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\left[U(X_{T})\right],\quad(x,n)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Here, we use the convention

(2.3)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\left[U(X_{T})\right]:=-\infty\quad{\rm if}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\left[U^{-}(X_{T})\right]=\infty.

Let us further set

(2.4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Y}^{n}(y):=\left\{\right.&Y\geq 0:~Y_{0}\leq y~~and~~XY=(X_{t}Y_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}~~is~a\\
&\left.\mathbb{P}^{n}\text{-}supermartingale~for~every~X\in\mathcal{X}(1)\right\},\quad(y,n)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}^{*},\end{split}

where, for every

V(\omega,y):=\sup\limits_{x>0}\left(U(\omega,x)-xy\right),\quad(\omega,y)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty),

Proof of Proposition

4.
Indifference price invariance

In this section, we take a closer look at the contingent claims whose indifference price does not depend on the choice of the probability measure.

4.1.
Complete Models

Let work under
\mathbb{P}
, and denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of equivalent to
\mathbb{P}
separating measures for
S
, and suppose that

(4.1)

\mathcal{M}\neq\emptyset,

which is equivalent to assuming (

) and additionally requiring that

contains a strictly positive martingale.
Here we show that in complete models, the indifference price of every bounded contingent claim is unique and coincides with the arbitrage-free price. In fact, the converse is also true. This is the subject of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let us suppose that
S
satisfies (
4.1
) and consider a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

(ii)

,

,
\mathbb{Q}

,

,
\mathbb{P}

(4.2)

\Pi(f,x,U,\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[f\right]=\Pi(f,\tilde{x},\tilde{U},\mathbb{P}),

where
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
is the unique separating measure for
S
and
\Pi

2.10

Proof.

Let us introduce

\mathcal{D}^{\mathbb{Q}}:=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{L}^{0}_{+}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}):~\eta\leq\frac{d\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}}{d\mathbb{Q}},~for~some~\tilde{\mathbb{Q}}\in\mathcal{M}\right\}.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii).

One can see that
\mathcal{D}^{\mathbb{Q}}

\mathcal{M}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
. It follows from the argument in
[

]
; see the proofs of Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, that

. We observe that the completeness of the model implies that

has a unique
{\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}}

\widehat{\eta}
. As

,
\mathbb{Q}\text{-}
a.s.. Therefore, we can define a probability measure

via its Radon-Nykodim derivative that satisfies

. One can see that
\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}
is a separating measure for

that is unique by the completeness assumption.

Now, let us fix

, where

is defined in Definition
2.11

in

y\widehat{\eta}
is the dual minimizer at

. As
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\widehat{\eta}\right]=1
, we deduce that the indifference price of
f
at
x>0
associated with

and
\mathbb{Q}
is given by

\Pi(f,x,U,\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}}\left[f\right],\quad x>0,\quad U\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q}).

As a similar argument can be carried out under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, we conclude that
(ii)
holds.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i).
Let
(ii)
holds. Assume by contradiction that the model is incomplete. Let
\tilde{V}
be a bounded from the above deterministic function on
[0,\infty)
, such that
-\tilde{V}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
, and let
\tilde{U}
its convex conjugate. As
\tilde{U}
is non-random and
\tilde{V}
is bounded from above, one can show that
\tilde{U}\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{P})
. Let us work under the probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
and consider the utility maximization problem and its dual under
\mathbb{Q}
, that is

(4.3)

\sup\limits_{X\in\mathcal{X}(x)}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\tilde{U}(X_{T})\right],\quad x>0,

and

(4.4)

\inf\limits_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{Q}}(y)}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\tilde{V}(Y_{T})\right],\quad y>0.

As
\tilde{U}\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{P})
, from
[
Mos15
, Theorem 3.2]
, we deduce that there exists a unique solution to (

) at
y=1
,
\widehat{Y}^{\mathbb{Q}}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
. By the incompleteness of the model assumption, there exists an element

4.3.
Incomplete models

We begin with the definition of replicability.

Definition 4.3
.

A bounded random variable
f
is
replicable
if there exists an admissible wealth process
X
, such that
-X
is also admissible and
X_{T}=f
.

Next, we show that in incomplete markets, bounded

contingent claims are indifference price invariant.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let us suppose that a separating measure exists for
S
. Then, for every
\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}
,
x>0
, and
U\in\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{Q})
, the indifference price for every bounded replicable contingent claim

satisfies

\Pi(f,x,U,\mathbb{Q})=x_{0},

where
x_{0}
is the initial value of the replicating strategy for

Proof.

Let us fix a bounded and replicable at the initial price
x_{0}
contingent claim

. Recalling the definition of sets
\mathcal{X}(x,q)

2.10
), let us set

u(x,q):=\sup\limits_{X\in\mathcal{X}(x,q)}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[U(X_{T}+qf)\right],\quad(x,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where we use the convention as in (
2.3
), and here, if for some

,

, we set
u(x,q):=-\infty
.

Next, for every
\tilde{x}\neq x_{0}
, let us consider
q=sign(x_{0}-\tilde{x})
, and we have

(4.6)

u(x-q\tilde{x},q)=u(x-q\tilde{x}+qx_{0},0)>u(x,0),\quad x>0,

as

is
strictly
increasing by
[
Mos15
, Theorem 3.2]
. We deduce from Definition

and (

) that

is not an indifference price.
In turn, for

, we have

and so, by Definition

is an indifference price for

.
∎

The following example gives an incomplete model, where the set of contingent claims, whose indifference price does not depend on the reference probability measure, is exactly the set of replicable contingent claims.

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{i})=1,\quad\mathbb{Q}(\omega_{j})\geq 0,j=1,2,3,\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[S_{1}\right]=S_{0}=1.\end{split}

(s_{3}-s_{2})f(\omega_{1})+(s_{1}-s_{3})f(\omega_{2})+(s_{2}-s_{1})f(\omega_{3})=0,

\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}
(under the associated physical measure) is precisely the class of replicable claims.

In the previous example, indifference price invariant claims are exactly the replicable ones. In general, the class of indifference price invariant (with respect to changes in the physical probability measure) claims can be strictly bigger than the class of replicable ones.

The next example gives an incomplete model, where
every
bounded contingent claim is indifference price invariant if we restrict ourselves to
deterministic
utilities, with respect to a particular class of changes of the physical probability measure
\mathbb{P}
.

Example 4.6
.

Let us suppose that
B
and W are independent Brownian motions on the complete stochastic basis
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P}\right)
, where the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is generated by
B
and
W
and made right-continuous and complete,
T\in(0,\infty)
is a time horizon. Let us suppose that there is a riskless asset with a price process equal to
1
at all times, and there is one risky traded asset whose price process is given by

\frac{dS_{t}}{S_{t}}=\mu dt+\sigma dW,\quad t\in(0,T],\quad S_{0}=1,

where
\mu
and
\sigma>0
are constants. Let us introduce the minimal martingale measure

\mathbb{Q}
, whose Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to
\mathbb{P}
is given by

(4.13)

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\mathcal{E}\left(-\frac{\mu}{\sigma}W\right)_{T}.

Let us consider a deterministic Inada utility function (that is, the one satisfying Assumption
2.1
and which does not depend on
\omega
), whose convex conjugate
V
satisfies

(4.14)

\mathbb{E}\left[V\left(y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\right)\right]<\infty,\quad y>0.

Following
[
MSZ24
, Example 4.4]
, one can show that the density of
\mathbb{Q}
is the dual minimizer for every
y>0
.

Next, let us change the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
in a way that the dynamics of
S
under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
is given by

\frac{dS_{t}}{S_{t}}=\tilde{\mu}dt+\sigma dW^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}},\quad t\in(0,T],\quad S_{0}=1,

where
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
and
W^{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}_{t}=W_{t}+\frac{\mu-\tilde{\mu}}{\sigma}t
,
t\in[0,T]
, is a Brownian motion under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
. Then, for every
deterministic
Inada utility function
\tilde{U}
, whose convex conjugate
\tilde{V}
satisfies

(4.15)

\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\tilde{V}\left(y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}\right)\right]<\infty,\quad y>0,

one can show that the density of
\mathbb{Q}
(defined via (
4.13
)) with respect to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
is the dual minimizer.

Now, for every
f\in\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, replicable or not, where an example of a bounded nonreplicable contingent claim is
f=1_{\{B_{T}>0\}}
, we have

(4.16)

\Pi\left(f,x,U,\mathbb{P}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}\left[f\right]=\Pi(f,\tilde{x},\tilde{U},\tilde{\mathbb{P}}),

where
U
and
\tilde{U}
are arbitrary deterministic Inada utility functions, whose convex conjugates
V
and
\tilde{V}
satisfy (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), respectively,
x
and
\tilde{x}
are positive constants,
\mathbb{Q}
defined via (
4.13
), and
\mathbb{P}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
are the probability measures specified in this example above. We note that (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) are adaptations of condition (
2.7
) to the present setting of two probability measures in the case of deterministic utilities
U
and
\tilde{U}
.

We conclude that every
f\in\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is indifference price invariant, in the sense (
4.16
), with respect to a change of probability measure from
\mathbb{P}
to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
that is specified in this example above.

5.
Counterexamples

Example 5.1
.

This example shows that without Assumption
2.4
, stability may fail, in particular, the assertions of Proposition
2.8
might not necessarily hold.

Let us fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, where the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
is the usual augmentation of the filtration generated by a Brownian motion
W
and
1
is the time horizon. Let us suppose that there are two traded assets one riskless with price process equal to
1
at all times and one risky, whose dynamics is given by

\frac{dS_{t}}{S_{t}}=\mu dt+\sigma dW_{t},\quad t\in(0,T],\quad S_{0}=1,

where
\mu
and
\sigma>0
are constants. Let us set

(5.1)

\phi:=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\exp\left({\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n}\right)W^{2}_{1}}\right).

Then, one can see that
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})
. Now, let us consider a utility stochastic field

U(\omega,x)=\tilde{U}(x)-\phi(\omega),\quad(\omega,x)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty),

where
\tilde{U}
is an Inada deterministic utility function. We suppose that
\tilde{U}
is negative-valued, e.g.,
\tilde{U}(x)=\frac{x^{p}}{p}
,
p<0
. One can see that the base model satisfies (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), as the convex conjugate of
U
,
V
, is non-positive-valued.

Next, let us consider perturbations of the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, such that the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of the new probability measures with respect to
\mathbb{P}
are given by

Z^{n}_{1}=\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{n+2}}\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{n+2}W^{2}_{1}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, one can see that
\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}_{1}]=1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{P}{\text{-}}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Z^{n}_{1}=1
, and so Assumption
2.2
holds.

On the other hand, by direct computations, we have

\mathbb{E}[Z^{n}_{1}\phi]=\infty,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

and so, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every wealth process
X\in\bigcup\limits_{x\geq 0}\mathcal{X}(x)
, we have

(5.2)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{T}U^{-}(X_{T})\right]=-\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{T}U(X_{T})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{T}\left(\tilde{U}(X_{T})+\phi\right)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{T}\phi\right]=\infty.\end{split}

Therefore,
Assumption
2.4
does not hold

and, in view of the convention (
2.3
), from (
5.2
), we have

(5.3)

u_{n}(x)=-\infty,\quad x>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

On the other hand, as
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})
, we have that
u_{\infty}(x)\in(-\infty,0)
,
x>0
, which together with (
5.3
) implies that the convergence of the primal value functions in the sense of Proposition
2.8
, item
(2)
, fails. Moreover, (
5.3
) shows that the finiteness of
u_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as in Proposition
2.8
, item
(1)
, fails too.

As pointed out in Remark
2.5
, Assumption
2.4
holds under Assumption
2.2
if
U
is deterministic. The next example shows that without Assumption
2.6
, the assertions of Proposition
2.8
may fail.

Example 5.2
.

Let us consider the probability space and the financial model consisting of two assets, as in the previous example. Let us suppose that the preferences of an economic agent are modeled by a deterministic utility function
U(x)=\frac{x^{p}}{p}
,
x>0
, where
p\in\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right)
. Then, the convex conjugate of
U
,
V
, is given by

V(y)=\frac{y^{-q}}{q}
,
y>0
, where
q=\frac{p}{1-p}>1.
Under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, the unique minimizer to the dual problem, for every
y>0
, is given by
y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}
, where

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}=\mathcal{E}\left(-\frac{\mu}{\sigma}W\right)_{1}.

In particular, the dual value function for the base model is finite-valued, as
\exp\left(q\frac{\mu}{\sigma}W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{1}(\mathbb{P})
, that is

(5.4)

v_{\infty}(y)<\infty,\quad y>0.

Now, with

c_{n}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{n}W^{3}_{1}1_{\{W_{1}\geq 0\}}\right)\right],\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

let us consider

Z^{n}_{1}=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{n}W^{3}_{1}1_{\{W_{1}\geq 0\}}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, one can see that Assumption
2.2
holds.
However, we get

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{1}V^{+}\left(\frac{y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}}{Z^{n}_{1}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{1}V\left(\frac{y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}}{Z^{n}_{1}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{n}_{1}\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{y\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}}{Z^{n}_{1}}\right)^{-q}\right]\\
&=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{\mu^{2}q}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)y^{-q}}{q}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z^{n}_{1}\right)^{1-q}\exp\left(q\frac{\mu}{\sigma}W_{1}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{\mu^{2}q}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)y^{-q}}{qc^{1-q}_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\frac{q-1}{n}W^{3}_{1}1_{\{W_{1}\geq 0\}}+q\frac{\mu}{\sigma}W_{1}\right)\right]=\infty.\end{split}

This implies that Assumption
2.6
does not hold.
Therefore, in view of the convention (
2.6
), using (
5.5
), we deduce that

(5.6)

v_{n}(y)=\infty,\quad y>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

which implies that the finiteness of the dual value functions, as in Proposition
2.8
, item
(1)
, does not hold. Furthermore, from (
5.4
) and (
5.6
), we conclude that the convergence of the dual value functions in Proposition
2.8
, item
(2)
, also does not hold.

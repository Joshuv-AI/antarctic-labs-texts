Local Stochastic Factored Gradient Descent for Distributed Quantum State Tomography
Junhyung Lyle Kim, Mohammad Taha Toghani, César A. Uribe, Anastasios Kyrillidis

Abstract
We propose a distributed Quantum State Tomography (QST) protocol, named Local Stochastic Factored Gradient Descent (Local SFGD), to learn the low-rank factor of a density matrix over a set of local machines. QST is the canonical procedure to characterize the state of a quantum system, which we formulate as a stochastic nonconvex smooth optimization problem. Physically, the estimation of a low-rank density matrix helps characterizing the amount of noise introduced by quantum computation. Theoretically, we prove the local convergence of Local SFGD for a general class of restricted strongly convex/smooth loss functions. Local SFGD converges locally to a small neighborhood of the global optimum at a linear rate with a constant step size, while it locally converges exactly at a sub-linear rate with diminishing step sizes. With a proper initialization, local convergence results imply global convergence. We validate our theoretical findings with numerical simulations of QST on the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state.

\displaystyle U_{t+1}

\diamond

(16)

For the third term, we decompose further to have:

\displaystyle\big\langle\hat{U}_{t}{-}U^{\star}R^{\star},\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big\langle\hat{U}_{t}{-}U_{t}^{i},\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-51.21504pt+\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big\langle U_{t}^{i}-U^{\star}R^{\star},\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big\rangle.

(17)

We again bound the two terms in (
17
) separately. Using
\langle A,B\rangle\geqslant-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\|A\|_{F}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\delta}\|B\|_{F}^{2},
The first term admits:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big\langle\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i},\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big\rangle

\displaystyle{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\geqslant}}\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\delta}\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}\big)}

By Lemma
1
, the second term in (
17
) admits:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big\langle U_{t}^{i}-U^{\star}R^{\star},\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tfrac{2\eta_{t}}{3}\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tfrac{3\mu\cdot\sigma_{r}(X^{\star})}{20}D^{2}(U_{t}^{i},U^{\star})

\displaystyle\geqslant\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tfrac{2\eta_{t}}{3}\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{3\mu\cdot\sigma_{r}(X^{\star})}{20}D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star}),

where we used convexity of
D^{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
in the second inequality.

Combining above two bounds into (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{g}_{t},\hat{U}_{t}-U^{\star}R^{\star}\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(-\tfrac{\delta}{2}\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\delta}\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tfrac{2\eta_{t}}{3}\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{3\mu\cdot\sigma_{r}(X^{\star})}{20}D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star}).

(18)

Substituting (
16
) and (
18
) into (
15
), we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{U}_{t}-U^{\star}R^{\star}-\eta_{t}\bar{g}_{t}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\left(1-\eta_{t}\cdot\tfrac{3\mu}{10}\sigma_{r}(X^{\star})\right)\|\hat{U}_{t}-U^{\star}R^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big[\big(\tfrac{\eta_{t}}{\delta}-\tfrac{\eta_{t}^{2}}{3}\big)\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}+\eta_{t}\delta\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta=4/\eta_{t}}}{{=}}\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)\|\hat{U}_{t}{-}U^{\star}R^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad{+\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big[\eta_{t}\big(\tfrac{\eta_{t}}{4}-\tfrac{\eta_{t}}{3}\big)\|\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}+4\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2}\big]}

\displaystyle\quad{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\leqslant}}\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)\|\hat{U}_{t}{-}U^{\star}R^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}+\tfrac{4}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2},}

(19)

where in the equality we defined
\alpha:=\tfrac{3\mu}{10}\sigma_{r}(X^{\star}),
and in the last inequality we used that
\tfrac{\eta_{t}}{4}-\tfrac{\eta_{t}}{3}<0
.

Substituting (
19
) into (
14
) and taking expectations conditional on previous iterates,
and using
\mathbb{E}\left[g_{t}\right]=\bar{g}_{t}
,
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t+1},U^{\star})]=\|\hat{U}_{t}{-}U^{\star}R^{\star}{-}\eta_{t}\bar{g}_{t}\|_{F}^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{g}_{t}{-}g_{t}\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle{\hskip 0.0pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:main-rec-first-term-bound}}}{{\leqslant}}\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)\|\hat{U}_{t}-U^{\star}R^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle{\quad\quad+\tfrac{4}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\hat{U}_{t}-U_{t}^{i}\|_{F}^{2}\big]+\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{g}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{F}^{2}]}

\displaystyle{\hskip 0.0pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:avg-local-diff}}}{{\leqslant}}\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)\|\hat{U}_{t}-U^{\star}R^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle{\quad\quad+4\eta_{t}^{2}(h-1)^{2}G^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{g}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{F}^{2}].}

where
the last inequality is by Lemma
3
.
We further have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{g}_{t}-g_{t}\|_{F}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big\|\tfrac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\nabla g_{i}^{j_{t}}(U_{t}^{i})-\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\big)\Big\|_{F}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip-48.36958pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\leqslant}}\tfrac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla g_{i}^{j_{t}}(U_{t}^{i})-\nabla g_{i}(U_{t}^{i})\|_{F}^{2}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{A2:eq:stoc-grad-var-bound}}}{{\leqslant}}\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{M},

where we used
\text{Var}(\sum_{m=1}^{M}X_{m})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\text{Var}(X_{m})
for independent random variables.

We now arrive at the iteration invariant bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t+1},U^{\star})]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\leqslant\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star})+\eta_{t}^{2}\big(4(h-1)^{2}G^{2}+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{M}\big).

(20)

Lastly, we unfold (
20
) for
T
iterations, and using

\sum_{t=0}^{T}(1-\eta_{t}\alpha)^{t}\leqslant\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(1-\eta_{t}\alpha)^{t}=\frac{1}{\eta_{t}\alpha},
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{T+1},U^{\star})]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)^{T+1}D^{2}(\hat{U}_{0},U^{\star})

\displaystyle\quad+\eta_{t}\left(\tfrac{4(h-1)^{2}G^{2}}{\alpha}+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{M\alpha}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4

(Local sub-linear convergence with diminishing step size)
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
,
and the initialization condition of Lemma
1

hold. Moreover, let
\eta_{t}=\frac{2}{\alpha(t+2)}
for
t\in[0:T]
and
\max_{p}|t_{p}-t_{p+1}|\leqslant h
. Then, the output of Algorithm
1
has the following property:

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{T+1},U^{\star})\big]&\leqslant\tfrac{4C}{\alpha(T+3)},\end{split}

(21)

where
X^{\star}
is the optimum such that
\text{rank}(X^{\star})=r
,
U^{\star}
is such that
X^{\star}=U^{\star}U^{\star\top}
, and
\alpha=\tfrac{3\mu}{10}\sigma_{r}(X^{\star})
and
C=4(h-1)^{2}G^{2}+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{M}
are global constants.

Proof.

We claim the following, and prove by induction:

\displaystyle D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star})\leqslant\tfrac{4C}{\alpha^{2}(t+2)},\quad\text{with}\quad\eta_{t}=\tfrac{2}{\alpha(t+2)}.

(22)

We start from the iteration invariant bound in (
20
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t+1},U^{\star})\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star})+\eta_{t}^{2}\cdot C.

For the base case
t=0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{1},U^{\star})\big]\leqslant(1-\eta_{0}\alpha)D^{2}(\hat{U}_{0},U^{\star})+\eta_{0}^{2}\cdot C

\displaystyle=\left(1-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\alpha\right)D^{2}(\hat{U}_{0},U^{\star})+\tfrac{C}{\alpha^{2}}=\tfrac{C}{\alpha^{2}}\leqslant\tfrac{4C}{3\alpha^{2}}.

Now, we proceed to the inductive step. Assuming (
22
) holds for time step
t
, we want to prove the same holds for the time step
t+1
.
Starting from (
20
) again, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t+1},U^{\star})\big]\leqslant\left(1-\eta_{t}\alpha\right)D^{2}(\hat{U}_{t},U^{\star})+\eta_{t}^{2}\cdot C

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:induction-hypothesis}}}{{\leqslant}}\left(1-\tfrac{2}{t+2}\right)\cdot\tfrac{4C}{\alpha^{2}(t+2)}+\tfrac{4C}{\alpha^{2}(t+2)^{2}}=4C\cdot\tfrac{t+1}{\alpha^{2}(t+2)}\cdot\tfrac{1}{t+2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4C\cdot\tfrac{t+2}{\alpha^{2}(t+3)}\cdot\tfrac{1}{t+2}=\tfrac{4C}{\alpha^{2}(t+3)},

where
in the last inequality we used the fact that
\frac{t+1}{t+2}\leqslant\frac{t+2}{t+3}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Numerical Results

We use Local SFGD to reconstruct the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state, using simulated measurement data from Qiskit.
GHZ state is known as
maximally entangled
quantum state
[
6
]
, meaning it exhibits the maximal inter-particle correlation, which does not exist in classical mechanics.
We are interested in:
(i)
how the number of local steps affect the accuracy defined as
\varepsilon=\|\hat{U}_{t}\hat{U}_{t}^{\top}-\rho^{\star}_{\text{ghz}}\|_{F}^{2}
, where
\rho^{\star}_{\text{ghz}}=U^{\star}U^{\star\dagger}
is the true density matrix for the GHZ state;
and
(ii)
the scalability of the distributed setup for various number of classical machines
M
.

In Fig.
1
(Top), we first fix the number of machines
M=10
and the number of total synchronization steps to be
100
, and vary the number of local iterations between two synchronization steps, i.e.,
h\in\{1,10,25,50,100,200\}.
We use constant step size
\eta=1
for all
h
.
Increasing
h
, i.e., each distributed machine performing more local iterations, leads to faster convergence in terms of the synchronization steps.
Notably, the speed up gets marginal: e.g., there is not much difference between
h=100
and
h=200
, indicating there is an “optimal”
h
that leads to the biggest reduction in the number of synchronization steps. Further, one can notice that higher
h
leads to slightly worse final accuracy—this is consistent with (
12
) in Theorem
2
, where the first variance term that depends on
G^{2}
disappears with
h=1
. Finally, note that
\varepsilon
does not decrease below certain level due to the inherent finite sampling error of quantum measurements
[
29
]
.

In Fig.
1
(Bottom), we plot the number of synchronization steps to reach
\varepsilon\leqslant 0.05,
while fixing

h=20
. We vary the number of workers
M\in\{5,10,15,20\}
, where each machine gets
200
measurements. There is a significant speed up from
M=5
to
M=15
, while for
M=20,
it took one more syncrhonization step compared to
M=15,
which is likely due to the stochasticity of SFGD within each machine.

. The batch size

is used for all cases.

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we introduced a distributed problem set up for QST as an instance of a general distributed optimization problem with PSD/low-rank constraints. We proposed the Local SFGD, a distributed non-convex algorithm that utilizes local
steps at each distributed worker to estimate the low-rank factor of a density matrix. We proved the local convergence of Local SFGD for restricted strongly convex/smooth objectives, which can be of independent interest. For future work, extension to the heterogeneous data case as well as the decentralized case with various topologies can be investigated.

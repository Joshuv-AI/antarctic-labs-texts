Divide bounded sets into sets having smaller diameters
Yanlu Lian, Senlin Wu

Abstract
. For each positive integer m m and each real finite dimensional Banach space X X , we set β ⁡ ( X , m ) \beta(X,m) to be the infimum of δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \delta\in(0,1] such that each set A ⊂ X A\subset X having diameter 1 1 can be represented as the union of m m subsets of A A whose diameters are at most δ \delta . Elementary properties of β ⁡ ( X , m ) \beta(X,m) , including its stability with respect to X X in the sense of Banach-Mazur metric, are presented. Two methods for estimating β ⁡ ( X , m ) \beta(X,m) are introduced. The first one estimates β ⁡ ( X , m ) \beta(X,m) using the knowledge of β ⁡ ( Y , m ) \beta(Y,m) , where Y Y is a Banach space sufficiently close to X X . The second estimation uses the information about β X ​ ( K , m ) \beta_{X}(K,m) , the infimum of δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \delta\in(0,1] such that K ⊂ X K\subset X is the union of m m subsets having diameters not greater than δ \delta times the diameter of K K , for certain classes of convex bodies K K in X X . In particular, we show that β ⁡ ( l p 3 , 8 ) ≤ 0.925 \beta(l_{p}^{3},8)\leq 0.925 holds for each p ∈ [ 1 , + ∞ ] p\in[1,+\infty] by applying the first method, and we proved that β ⁡ ( X , 8 ) < 1 \beta(X,8)<1 whenever X X is a three-dimensional Banach space satisfying β X ​ ( B X , 8 ) < 221 328 \beta_{X}(B_{X},8)<\frac{221}{328} , where B X B_{X} is the unit ball of X X , by applying the second method. These results and methods are closely related to the extension of Borsuk’s problem in finite dimensional Banach spaces and to C. Zong’s computer program for Borsuk’s conjecture.

2.
Elementary properties of
\beta(X,m)

Proposition 1
.

For each finite dimensional Banach space
X=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|)
and each positive integer
m
, we
have

\beta(X,m)=\sup\quantity{\beta_X(A,m)\mid A\in\mathcal{C}_X}.

Proof.

Put
\beta=\sup\quantity{\beta_X(A,m)\mid A\in\mathcal{C}_X}
. We only need to show
that
\beta(X,m)\leq\beta
. Let
A
be an arbitrary set in
\mathcal{B}^{n}
,
and
A^{c}
be a completion of
A
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
collection
\quantity{B_i\mid i\in[m]}
of subsets of
A^{c}
such that

A^{c}=\bigcup\limits_{i\in[m]}B_{i},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{\delta\left(A\right)}\max\quantity{\delta_X(B_k)\mid k\in[m]}\leq\beta_{X}(A^{c},m)+\varepsilon.

It follows that

A=A\cap A^{c}=\bigcup\limits_{i\in[m]}\quantity(A\cap B_i).

Thus

\beta_{X}(A,m)\leq\frac{1}{\delta\left(A\right)}\max\quantity{\DIAM{A\cap B_k}\mid k\in[m]}\leq\beta_{X}(A^{c},m)+\varepsilon,

which implies that
\beta_{X}(A,m)\leq\beta_{X}(A^{c},m)
. Thus
\beta(X,m)\leq\beta
as claimed.
∎

Let
\mathcal{T}^{n}
be the set of all non-singular linear transformations on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The (multiplicative) Banach-Mazur metric
d_{BM}^{M}:~\mathcal{K}^{n}\times\mathcal{K}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is defined by

d_{BM}^{M}(K_{1},K_{2})=\inf\quantity{\gamma\geq 1\mid\exists T\in\mathcal{T}^n, v\in\mathbb{R}^n \text{ s.t. } T(K_2)\subset K_1 \subset\gamma T(K_2)+v},~\forall K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}.

The infimum is clearly attained. When both
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are symmetric with
respect to
o
, we have

d_{BM}^{M}(K_{1},K_{2})=\inf\quantity{\gamma\geq 1\mid\exists T\in\mathcal{T}^n\text{
s.t. } T(K_2)\subset K_1 \subset\gamma T(K_2)}.

In this situation,
d_{BM}^{M}(K_{1},K_{2})
equals to the Banach-Mazur distance
between the Banach spaces
X
and
Y
having
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
as unit balls,
respectively. I.e.,

d_{BM}^{M}(K_{1},K_{2})=d_{BM}^{M}(X,Y):=\inf\quantity{\norm{T}\cdot\norm{T^{-1}}\mid T\text{ is an
isomorphism from $X$ onto $Y$}}.

We have the following result showing the stability of
\beta(X,m)
with respect
to
X
in the sense of Banach-Mazur metric.

Theorem 2
.

If
X=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
and
Y=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y})
are two Banach
space satisfying
d_{BM}^{M}(X,Y)\leq\gamma
for some
\gamma\geq 1
, then

\beta(X,m)\leq\gamma\beta(Y,m),~\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

Proof.

By applying a suitable linear transformation if necessary, we may assume that

B_{Y}\subseteq B_{X}\subseteq\gamma B_{Y}.

In this situation we have, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{X}=\inf\quantity{\lambda>0\mid x\in\lambda B_X}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\quantity{\lambda>0\mid x\in\lambda B_Y}

\displaystyle=\left\|x\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\inf\quantity{\lambda\gamma>0\mid x\in\lambda\gamma B_Y}

\displaystyle=\gamma\inf\quantity{\lambda>0\mid x\in\lambda\gamma B_Y}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\inf\quantity{\lambda>0\mid x\in\lambda B_X}=\gamma\left\|x\right\|_{X}.

Hence,

(3)

\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{Y}\leq\gamma\left\|x\right\|_{X}.

In the rest of this proof, we denote by
\delta_{X}(A)
and
\delta_{Y}(A)
the
diameter of a bounded subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}

and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}
, respectively. By (
3
), we have

(4)

\delta_{X}(A)\leq\delta_{Y}(A)\leq\gamma\delta_{X}(A),~\forall A\in\mathcal{B}^{n}.

Let
A
be a bounded subset of
X
. Then
A
is also bounded in
Y
. Let
A^{c}

be a completion of
A
in
Y
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
collection
\quantity{B_i\mid i\in[m]}
of subsets of
A^{c}
such that
A^{c}
is the
union of this collection and that

\frac{1}{\delta_{Y}(A^{c})}\max\quantity{\delta_Y(B_i)\mid i\in[m]}\leq\beta_{Y}(A^{c},m)+\varepsilon.

Then

\displaystyle A=A\cap A^{c}=\bigcup\limits_{i\in[m]}(B_{i}\cap A),

and, by (
4
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{X}(A)}\max\quantity{\delta_X(B_i\cap A)\mid i\in[m]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{\delta_{Y}(A)}\max\quantity{\delta_Y(B_i)\mid i\in[m]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\delta_{Y}\quantity(A^c)}\max\quantity{\delta_Y(B_i)\mid i\in[m]}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(\beta_{Y}(A^{c},m)+\varepsilon).

Therefore,
\beta_{X}(A,m)\leq\gamma\beta_{Y}(A^{c},m)
. It follows that
\beta(X,m)\leq\gamma\beta(Y,m)
.
∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
l_{\infty}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
. Then
\beta(l_{\infty}^{n},2^{n})=\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Put
X=l_{\infty}^{n}
. Then every complete set in
X
is a homothetic copy of

, see
[
11

and
[

. Therefore,

\beta_{X}(A,2^{n})=\beta_{X}(B_{X},2^{n}),~\forall A\in\mathcal{C}_{X}.

Thus it sufficies to show
\beta_{X}(B_{X},2^{n})=\frac{1}{2}
.

On the one hand,
B_{X}=\frac{1}{2}B_{X}+\frac{1}{2}V
, where

is the set of vertices of
B_{X}
. Since the cardinality of

is
2^{n}
, we have
\beta_{X}(B_{X},2^{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

On the other hand, suppose that

is the union of

of its subsets
B_{1},\cdots,B_{2^{n}}
. For each

, let
B_{i}^{c}
be a completion of

. Then

B_{X}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{i\in[2^{n}]}B_{i}^{c}.

It follows that

\VOL{B_{X}}\leq\sum\limits_{i\in[2^{n}]}\VOL{B_{i}^{c}}\leq\sum\limits_{i\in[2^{n}]}\quantity(\frac{1}{2^n})\max\quantity{\delta_X(B_i^c)\mid i\in[2^n]}\VOL{B_{X}},

which implies that
\max\quantity{\delta_X(B_i^c)\mid i\in[2^n]}\geq 1
. Thus

\beta_{X}(B_{X},2^{n})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Let

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{7}\sum_{i\in[4]}\lambda_{i}v_{i}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i\in[4]}v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[4]}\quantity(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{4}{7}\lambda_i)v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\quantity(\sum_{j\in[4]}\qty(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{4}{7}\lambda_j))\sum_{i\in[4]}\frac{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{4}{7}\lambda_{i}}{\sum_{j\in[4]}(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{4}{7}\lambda_{j})}v_{i}

Remark 10
.

The estimations in Proposition
8
and Proposition

9
are independent of the choice of norm on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

For a convex body
K
,
the Minkowski measure of symmetry
, denoted by

s(K)
, is defined as

s(K)=\min\quantity{\lambda>0\mid\exists x\in X\text{ s.t. } K+x\subset-\lambda K}.

It is known that

1\leq s(K)\leq n,~\forall K\in\mathcal{K}^{n};

the equality on the left holds if and only if
K
is centrally symmetric, and
the equality on the right holds if and only if
K
is a simplex (cf.
[
29
]
).

The following lemma shows the stability of
\beta_{X}(K,m)
with respect to
K
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
X=(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\|\cdot\right\|)
, and
K
and
L
be two convex bodies in
X
. If
there exist a number
\gamma\geq 1
and a point
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

K\subseteq L\subseteq\gamma K+c,

then, for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

\beta_{X}(L,m)\leq\gamma\beta_{X}(K,m).

Proof.

For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a collection
\quantity{K_i\mid i\in[m]}
of
subsets of
\gamma K+c
such that

\gamma K+c=\bigcup_{i\in[m]}K_{i}

and

\delta\left(K_{i}\right)\leq\gamma\delta\left(K\right)\beta_{X}(K,m)+\varepsilon\leq\gamma\delta\left(L\right)\beta_{X}(K,m)+\varepsilon,~\forall i\in[m].

Since

L=L\cap(\gamma K+c)=\bigcup_{i\in[m]}\quantity(L\cap K_i),

we have

\beta_{X}(L,m)\leq\gamma\beta_{X}(K,m)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta\left(L\right)}.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary,
\beta_{X}(L,m)\leq\gamma\beta_{X}(K,m)
as claimed.
∎

Theorem 12
.

Let
X=(\mathbb{R}^{3},\left\|\cdot\right\|)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and

\eta=\sup\quantity{\beta_X(T,m)\mid T\text{ is a simplex in $\R^3$}}.

We have

\beta(X,m)\leq\min\limits_{\varepsilon\in(0,1/3)}\max\quantity{\qty(1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon})\eta,\frac{2(3-\varepsilon)}{4-\varepsilon}\beta_X(B_X,m)}.

Proof.

Let
K
be a complete set in
X
,
\varepsilon
be a number in
\quantity(0,\frac{1}{3})
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1
. The Banach-Mazur distance from
K
to three-dimensional simplices is bounded from the above by

1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}.

Then there exist a tetrahedron
T
and a point
c\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

T\subset K\subset\quantity(1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon})T+c.

By Lemma
11
, we have

\beta_{X}(K,m)\leq\quantity(1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon})\eta.

Case 2
. The Banach-Mazur distance from
K
to three-dimensional
simplex is at least

1+\frac{4\varepsilon}{1-3\varepsilon}.

From Theorem 2.1 in
[
25
]
, it follows that

s(K)\leq 3-\varepsilon.

Denote by
R(K)
the circumradius of
K
. Theorem 1.1 in

[
7
]
shows that

s(K)=\frac{R(K)/\delta\left(K\right)}{1-R(K)/\delta\left(K\right)}.

It follows that

\frac{R(K)}{\delta\left(K\right)}\leq\frac{3-\varepsilon}{4-\varepsilon}.

By a suitable translation if necessary, we may assume that

K\subseteq\frac{3-\varepsilon}{4-\varepsilon}\delta\left(K\right)B_{X}.

For each
\gamma>0
, there exists a collection
\quantity{B_i\mid i\in[8]}
such that

B_{X}=\bigcup_{i\in[8]}B_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\delta\left(B_{i}\right)\leq 2\beta_{X}(B_{X},m)+\gamma,~\forall i\in[m].

It follows that

\beta_{X}(K,m)\leq\frac{2(3-\varepsilon)}{4-\varepsilon}\beta_{X}(B_{X},m)+\frac{3-\varepsilon}{4-\varepsilon}\gamma.

Hence

\beta_{X}(K,m)\leq\frac{2(3-\varepsilon)}{4-\varepsilon}\beta_{X}(B_{X},m).

This completes the proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
. Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
that may contain isolated vertices. Via the Stanley-Reisner correspondence, simplicial complexes on
[n]
are in one-to-one correspondence with squarefree monomial ideals in
S
. For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
. The Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by

I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta).

By
[
CHT
,
K
]
, the function
s\mapsto\reg I_{\Delta}^{s}
is asymptotically linear. Nonetheless, for a given simplicial complex
\Delta
, studying the sequence
\{\reg I_{\Delta}^{s}|s\geq 1\}
is a challenging problem. When
\dim\Delta=1
, Hoa and Trung
[
HTr
]
computed
\reg I_{\Delta}^{(s)}
for all
s
, while Lu
[
L
]
computed the geometric regularity of
I_{\Delta}^{s}
. Note that
a_{0}(I_{\Delta}^{(s)})=-\infty
, while Lu did not compute
a_{0}(I_{\Delta}^{s})
(see Subsection
2.2
for the definition of the
a_{i}
-invariants). In this paper, we compute the regularity of all intermediate ideals lying between
I_{\Delta}^{s}
and
I_{\Delta}^{(s)}
extending work of Hoa and Trung
[
HTr
]
and Lu
[
L
]
. More precisely, for monomial ideals
I\subseteq J
, we define
\Inter(I,J)
the set of monomial ideals
L
such that
L=I+(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
where
f_{i}
are among minimal monomial generators of
J
. Our main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a one-dimensional simplicial complex. Let
I=I_{\Delta}
be the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
. Then for all
s\geq 2
and all intermediate ideal
J\in\Inter(I_{\Delta}^{s},I_{\Delta}^{(s)})
, we have

\reg(J)=\begin{cases}3s&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=3,\\
2s+1&\text{ if }\girth\Delta=4,\\
2s&\text{ if }\girth\Delta\geq 5.\end{cases}

Example 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a one-dimensional simplicial complex whose facets are

\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\},\{1,4\},\{4,5\},\{5,6\},\{2,6\}.

Let
I=I_{\Gamma}
. Note that
f_{1}=x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}
,
f_{2}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{6}
,
f_{3}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
are minimal generators of
I^{(3)}
. Then
I^{3},I^{3}+(f_{1}),I^{3}+(f_{1},f_{2}),I^{3}+(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
all have regularity
7
.

We noticed the rigidity of regularity for intermediate ideals
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
for
s=2,3
(see
[
MV
]
for more detail) from the proofs in our previous work with Nam, Phong, and Thuy
[
MNPTV
]
. Prior to this, we did not aware of any rigidity result for the regularity of ideals in non-trivial sequences. We conjecture that this rigidity property holds for edge ideals of graphs.

Conjecture A
.

Let
I
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. For all
s\geq 1
, let
J
be an intermediate ideal in
\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
. Then

\reg(J)=\reg(I^{s})=\reg(I^{(s)}).

Theorem
1.1
settles Conjecture
A
for edge ideals of graphs with independence number
2
. This paper is a continuation of
[
MNPTV
]
where we analyse the degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
via the radical ideal
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
.

We now describe the idea of proof of Theorem
1.1
. An exponent
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called an extremal exponent of
J
if
\reg S/J=|\mathbf{a}|+i
and
\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}F
has a non-vanishing homology in degree
i-1
for some face
F
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
such that
F\cap\supp a=\emptyset
. When
\Delta
contains a triangle, we prove that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in J
if
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 3s
and
a_{i}\leq s
for all
i
. We then deduce that
\reg J=3s
. The main work is in the case where
\Delta
contains no triangles. Then
I_{\Delta}=I(G)
is the edge ideal of
G
, the complement of
\Delta
. By Lemma
2.14
, it suffices to prove that

(1.1)

|\mathbf{a}|+i\leq\begin{cases}2s&\text{ when }\girth\Delta=4,\\
2s-1&\text{ when }\girth\Delta\geq 5.\end{cases}

The main tools to analyse
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, hence
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
are Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.16
. These lemmas provide a new approach toward understanding the behaviour of the sequences
\{\reg I^{s}\mid s\geq 1\}
and
\{\reg I^{(s)}\mid s\geq 1\}
for general edge ideals. While the description of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{(s)})
is known, to our knowledge Lemma
2.16
gives a first description of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
for edge ideals of graphs. For a monomial
f\in S
, the
I
-order of
f
, denoted
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(f)
is defined by
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(f)=\max(t\mid f\in I^{t})
.

Lemma
2.16
.
Let
G
be a simple graph. Let
I=I(G)
. Let
F
be an independent set of
G
, and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
an exponent. Assume that

(1.2)

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\operatorname{ord}_{I}(\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}})\geq s,

then
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Conversely, if
x_{F}
is a minimal generator of
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
then (
1.2
) holds.

We now outline the steps, where Lemma
2.5
and Lemma
2.16
play a crucial role to establish the upper bound (
1.1
). Assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(x^{\mathbf{a}})=r
. A decomposition
x^{\mathbf{a}}=MN
is called a good decomposition if
M
is a minimal monomial generator of
I^{r}
and
N\notin I
. An index
i
where
x_{i}
is of highest exponent in
N
is called a critical index of
\mathbf{a}
with respect to this decomposition. The steps are:

(1)

We prove that if
i=2
, then
|\mathbf{a}|\leq(\girth\Delta)/(\girth\Delta-2)(s-1)
, reducing the problem to consider extremal exponents
(\mathbf{a},i)
where
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
and
i\leq 1
.

(2)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
, we prove that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
.

(3)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
, let
x^{\mathbf{a}}=MN
be a good decomposition of
x^{\mathbf{a}}
. Let
i
be a critical index of
N
with respect to this decomposition. We prove that if
x_{i}^{2}|N
, then
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
is a cone over
i
. Consequently, this implies that
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2s
, and if
|\mathbf{a}|=2s
, then
N
is squarefree and
\deg N=2
.

(4)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|=2s
, we prove that
\girth\Delta=4
and
i=0
. This completes the case
\girth\Delta=4
.

(5)

Assuming
\girth\Delta\geq 5
and
|\mathbf{a}|=2s-1
, we prove that
i=0
.

One of the main obstructions to carry out this procedure for computing the regularity of intermediate ideals of edge ideals of other classes of graphs is the computation of the order of monomials of the form in Lemma
2.16
. In subsequent work, we can overcome this difficulty and compute the regularity of intermediate ideals of edge ideals for other classes of graphs.

Now we explain the organization of the paper. In Section
2
, we recall some notation and basic facts about the symbolic powers of a squarefree monomial ideal, the degree complexes, and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we provide an example where we can verify the rigidity property of the regularity of intermediate ideals for graphs with
\alpha(G)>2
to further illustrate our procedure.

2.
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, symbolic powers and degree complexes

In this section, we recall some definitions and properties concerning Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, the symbolic powers of a squarefree monomial ideal, and the degree complexes of a monomial ideal. The interested reader is referred to (
[
BH
,
E
,
S
]
) for more details. The material in this section follows closely
[
MNPTV
, Section 2]
.

2.1.
Simplicial complexes and Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
that is a collection of subsets of
[n]
closed under taking subsets. We put
\dim F=|F|-1
, where
|F|
is the cardinality of
F
. The dimension of
\Delta
is
\dim\Delta=\max\{\dim F\mid F\in\Delta\}
. The set of its maximal elements under inclusion, called by facets, is denoted by
\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called a cone over
x\in[n]
if
x\in B
for any
B\in\mathfrak{F}(\Delta)
. If
\Delta
is a cone, it is acyclic (i.e., has vanishing reduced homology).

For a face
F\in\Delta
, the link of
F
in
\Delta
is the subsimplicial complex of
\Delta
defined by

\lk_{\Delta}F=\{G\in\Delta\mid F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\emptyset\}.

For each subset
F
of
[n]
, let
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
be a squarefree monomial in
S
. We now recall the Stanley-Reisner correspondence

Definition 2.1
.

For a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, the Stanley-Reisner complex of
I
is defined by

\Delta(I)=\{F\subset[n]\mid x_{F}\notin I\}.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
, the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
is defined by

I_{\Delta}=(x_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta).

The Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Delta
is the quotient by the Stanley-Reisner ideal,
K[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}.

From the definition, it is easy to see the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I,J
be squarefree monomial ideals of
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then

(1)

\Delta(I)
is a cone over
t\in[n]
if and only if
x_{t}
is not divided by any minimal generator of
I
.

(2)

I\subseteq J
if and only if
\Delta(I)\supseteq\Delta(J)
.

2.2.
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a polynomial ring over a field
K
. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, let

a_{i}(L)=\begin{cases}\max\{j\in\ZZ\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)_{j}\neq 0\}&\text{ if $H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)\neq 0$}\\
-\infty&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. Then, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity (or regularity for short) of
L
is defined to be

\reg(L)=\max\{a_{i}(L)+i\mid i=0,\ldots,\dim L\}.

For a non-zero and proper homogeneous ideal
J
of
S
we have
\reg(J)=\reg(S/J)+1
.

2.3.
Graphs and their edge ideals

Let
G
denote a finite simple graph over the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
x\in V(G)
, let the neighbour of
x
be the subset
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)~|~\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
, and set
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
. For a subset
U
of the vertices set
V(G)
,
N_{G}(U)
and
N_{G}[U]
are defined by
N_{G}(U)=\cup_{u\in U}N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}[U]=\cup_{u\in U}N_{G}[u]
. If
G
is fixed, we shall use
N(U)
or
N[U]
for short.

An independent set in
G
is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices. A maximal independent set is an independent set that is maximal under inclusion. The independence number of
G
, denoted by
\alpha(G)
, is the cardinality of a maximal independent set of maximum size.

A subgraph
H
is called an induced subgraph of
G
if for any vertices
u,v\in V(H)\subseteq V(G)
then
\{u,v\}\in E(H)
if and only if
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
.

An induced matching is a subset of the edges that do not share any vertices and it is an induced subgraph. The induced matching number of
G
, denoted by
\mu(G)
, is the largest size of an induced matching in
G
.

A
m
-cycle in
G
is a sequence of
m
distinct vertices
1,\ldots,m\in V(G)
such that
\{1,2\},\ldots,\{m-1,m\},\{m,1\}
are edges of
G
. The girth of
G
, denoted
\girth(G)
is the size of a smallest induced cycle in
G
.

The edge ideal of
G
is defined to be

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}~|~\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subseteq S.

For simplicity, we often write
i\in G
(resp.
ij\in G
) instead of
i\in V(G)
(resp.
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
).

A clique in
G
is a complete subgraph of
G
. We also call a clique of size
3
a triangle.

2.4.
Symbolic powers

Let
I
be a non-zero and proper homogeneous ideal of
S
. Let
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}
be the set of the minimal prime ideals of
I
. Given a positive integer
s
, the
s
-th symbolic power of
I
is defined by

I^{(s)}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}I^{s}S_{P_{i}}\cap S.

For a monomial
f
in
S
, we denote
\frac{\partial^{*}(f)}{\partial^{*}(x^{\mathbf{a}})}
the
*
-partial derivative of
f
with respect to
x^{\mathbf{a}}
, which is derivative without coefficients. In general,
\partial f/\partial x^{\mathbf{a}}=c\partial^{*}(f)/\partial^{*}(x^{\mathbf{a}})
for some constant
c
. We define

I^{[s]}=(f\in S~|~\frac{\partial^{*}f}{\partial^{*}x^{\mathbf{a}}}\in I,\text{ for all }x^{\mathbf{a}}\text{ with }|\mathbf{a}|=s-1),

the
s
-th
*
-differential power of
I
. When
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, the symbolic powers of
I
is equal to the
*
-differential powers of
I
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal. Then
I^{(s)}=I^{[s]}.

For a monomial
f
in
S
, and
i\in[n]
,
\deg_{i}(f)=\max(t\mid x_{i}^{t}\text{ divides }f)
denotes the degree of
x_{i}
in
f
. The support of
f
, denoted
\supp(f)
, is the set of all indices
i\in[n]
such that
x_{i}|f
. The radical of
f
is defined by
\sqrt{f}=\prod_{i\in\supp f}x_{i}
. As a consequence, we deduce the following property of generators of
I^{(s)}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal. Let
f
be a minimal generator of
I^{(s)}
. Then
\deg_{i}(f)\leq s
for all
i=1,...,n
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
f=x_{1}^{t}g
is a minimal generator of
I^{(s)}
where
t>s
and
x_{1}\not|g
. Since
f
is minimal,
f_{1}=x_{1}^{t-1}g
does not belong to
I^{(s)}
. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists an exponent
\mathbf{a}
such that
|\mathbf{a}|=s-1
and

h_{1}=\partial^{*}(f_{1})/\partial^{*}(x^{\mathbf{a}})\notin I.

Since
|\mathbf{a}|=s-1
and
t-1\geq s
,
x_{1}|h_{1}
. This implies that

h=\partial^{*}(f)/\partial^{*}(x^{\mathbf{a}})=x_{1}h_{1}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
h\in I
, but this is a contradiction, as
I
is squarefree and
\sqrt{h}=\sqrt{h_{1}}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

As another consequence, we give a necessary condition for a squarefree monomial to be in
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
I
be a squarefree monomial ideal. Let
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
be an intermediate ideal lying between
I^{s}
and
I^{(s)}
. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin J
. Assume that
f\in\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
and that
f\notin I
. Let
F
be a facet of
\Delta(I)
that contains
\supp(f)
. Then
\sum_{i\notin F}a_{i}\geq s
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\sum_{i\notin F}a_{i}\leq s-1
. Let
\mathbf{b}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
b_{i}=0
for all
i\in F
,
b_{i}=a_{i}
for all
i\notin F
. Then
|\mathbf{b}|\leq s-1
. Furthermore,

\supp\big(\dfrac{\partial^{*}(f^{u}x^{\mathbf{a}})}{\partial^{*}(x^{\mathbf{b}})}\big)\subseteq F

implying that
f^{u}x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I^{(s)}
for all
u\geq 1
by Lemma
2.3
, which is a contradiction to the fact that
f\in\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\subseteq\sqrt{I^{(s)}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}.

∎

2.5.
Degree complexes

For a monomial ideal
I
in
S
, Takayama in
[
T
]
found a combinatorial formula for
\dim_{K}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(S/I)_{\mathbf{a}}
for all
\mathbf{a}\in\ZZ^{n}
in terms of certain simplicial complexes which are called degree complexes. For every
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\ZZ^{n}
we set
G_{\mathbf{a}}=\{i\mid\ a_{i}<0\}
and write
x^{\mathbf{a}}=\Pi_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{a_{j}}
. Thus,
G_{\mathbf{a}}=\emptyset
whenever
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. The degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is the simplicial complex whose faces are
F\setminus G_{\mathbf{a}}
, where
G_{\mathbf{a}}\subseteq F\subseteq[n]
, so that for every minimal generator
x^{\mathbf{b}}
of
I
there exists an index
i\not\in F
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
. It is noted that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
may be either the empty complex or
\{\emptyset\}
and its vertex set may be a proper subset of
[n]
. The next lemma is useful to compute the regularity of a monomial ideal in terms of its degree complexes.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. Then

\reg(S/I)=\max\{|\mathbf{a}|+i~|~\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n},i\geq 0,\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}F;K)\neq 0\\
\text{ for some $F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)$ with $F\cap\supp\mathbf{a}=\emptyset$}\}.

In particular, if
I=I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex
\Delta
then

\reg(K[\Delta])=\max\{i~|~i\geq 0,\widetilde{H}_{i-1}(\lk_{\Delta}F;K)\neq 0\text{ for some }F\in\Delta\}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
and a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. In the proof of
[
T
, Theorem 1]
, the author showed that if there exists
j\in[n]
such that
a_{j}\geq\rho_{j}=\max\{\deg_{j}(u)~|~u\text{ is a minimal monomial generator of }I\}
then
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is either a cone over
\{j\}
or the void complex.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. A pair
(\mathbf{a},i)\in\NN^{n}\times\NN
is called
an extremal exponent of the ideal
I
, if
\reg(S/I)=|\mathbf{a}|+i
as in Lemma
2.6
.

Remark 2.9
.

We sometime call
\mathbf{a}
instead of
(\mathbf{a},i)
an extremal exponent of
I
. Let
\mathbf{a}
be an extremal exponent of
I
. Then
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin I
and
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is not a cone over
t
with
t\in\supp\mathbf{a}
. In particular, by Remark
2.7
,
\mathbf{a}
belongs to the finite set

\Gamma(I)=\{\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}~|~a_{j}<\rho_{j}\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,n\}.

Furthermore, we have the following interpretation of the degree complex
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. Then

I_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}=\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be an extremal exponent of
I
. By Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
2.10
, and Remark
2.9
, for each
t\in\supp\mathbf{a}
, there exists a minimal generator
f
of
\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
such that
x_{t}|f
.

We first deduce the following inequality on the regularity of restriction of a monomial ideal.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
x_{j}
is a variable. Then

\reg(I,x_{j})\leq\reg I.

For an exponent
\mathbf{a}\in\ZZ^{n}
, we denote
\supp(\mathbf{a})=\{i\in[n]\mid\ a_{i}\neq 0\}
, the support of
\mathbf{a}
. For any subset
V\subset[n]
, we denote

I_{V}=(f~|~f\text{ is a monomial which belongs to }I\text{ and }\supp(f)\subseteq V)

be the restriction of
I
on
V
. We have

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
J
be a monomial ideal in
S
. Let
V\subseteq[n]
. We have

\reg(J_{V})\leq\reg(J).

Proof.

Let
\{t,\ldots,n\}=[n]\setminus V
. Then,
J_{V}+(x_{t},...,x_{n})=J+(x_{t},...,x_{n}).
The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.12
and the fact that
x_{t},\ldots,x_{n}
is a regular sequence with respect to
S/J_{V}
.
∎

Consequently, we deduce a lower bound on the regularity of intermediate ideals of edge ideals.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of a simple graph
G
. Let
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
be an intermediate ideal. Then
\reg J\geq 2s+\mu(G)-1
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a maximum induced matching of
G
. Then
I_{H}^{s}\subseteq J_{H}\subseteq I_{H}^{(s)}
. Since
I_{H}
is a complete intersection of
\mu(G)
quadrics,
I_{H}^{(s)}=I_{H}^{s}
and
\reg I_{H}^{s}=2s+\mu(G)-1
. The Corollary follows from Corollary
2.13
and
[
MNPTV
, Corollary 2.4]
.
∎

2.6.
Radicals of colon ideals

We start with a simple observation.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
generated by the monomials
f_{1},...,f_{r}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
. Then
\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
is generated by
\sqrt{f_{1}/\gcd(f_{1},x^{\mathbf{a}})},...,\sqrt{f_{r}/\gcd(f_{r},x^{\mathbf{a}})}
.

Assume now that
I=I(G)
is the edge ideal of a graph, and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
an exponent. We will give a sufficient condition for a squarefree monomial to belong to the radical
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. It is also necessary for
f
to be minimal. First, we introduce some notation. Let
f
be a monomial. The
I
-order of
f
is defined by

\operatorname{ord}_{I}(f)=\max(t\mid f\in I^{t}).

From the definition, it is clear that if
g|f
, then
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(g)\leq\operatorname{ord}_{I}(f)
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
F
be an independent set of
G
, and
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
an exponent. Assume that

(2.1)

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\operatorname{ord}_{I}(\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}})\geq s,

then
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Conversely, if
x_{F}
is a minimal generator of
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
then (
2.1
) holds.

Proof.

Let

g=\prod_{j\in N(F)}x_{j}^{a_{j}}\text{ and }h=\prod_{u\notin N[F]}x_{u}^{a_{u}}.

We have
x_{F}^{\deg g}g\in I^{\deg g}
. Therefore, if
\deg g+\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)\geq s
,
x_{F}^{\deg g}gh\in I^{s}
.

Conversely, assume that
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
is a minimal generator. By Lemma
2.15
, there exist edges
e_{1},...,e_{s}
of
G
such that
x_{F}=\sqrt{P/\gcd(P,x^{\mathbf{a}})}
where
P=e_{1}\cdots e_{s}
. Replacing
x^{\mathbf{a}}
by
\gcd(P,x^{\mathbf{a}})
, we may assume that there exists exponents
b_{i}>0
,
i\in F
such that

P=(\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}^{b_{i}})\cdot x^{\mathbf{a}}.

Assume that among
e_{1},...,e_{s}
there are
s_{1}
edges of the form
x_{i}x_{j}
,
s_{2}
edges of the form
x_{j}x_{k}
,
s_{3}
edges of the form
x_{k}x_{u}
, and
s_{4}
edges of the form
x_{u}x_{v}
where
i\in F
,
j,k\in N(F)
and
u,v\notin N[F]
. Then
s=s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}
and

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}=s_{1}+2s_{2}+s_{3}.

Furthermore,
h
is divisible by the product of
s_{4}
edges of the form
x_{u}x_{v}
. In particular,
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)\geq s_{4}
. Thus

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)\geq s_{1}+2s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}\geq s,

as required.
∎

As a consequence, we have

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
F
be a maximal independent set of
G
. Let
J\in(I^{s},I^{(s)})
be an intermediate ideal. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin J
. Then
x_{F}\in\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
if and only if
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
.

Proof.

Since
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\subseteq\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, it suffices to prove that if
x_{F}\in\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
then
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Since
F
is a maximal independent set
F\cap N(F)=\emptyset
and
N[F]=F\cup N(F)=[n]
. By Lemma
2.5
,

\sum_{j\notin F}a_{j}\geq s.

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.16
.
∎

From Lemma
2.16
, we see that the computation of order is of particular importance. The order of a clique term, which plays a crucial role in the study of
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
when
\alpha(G)=2
, can be computed as follows.

Lemma 2.18
.

Assume that
\{1,...,t\}
is a clique of
G
. Let
f=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{t}^{a_{t}}
. Assume that
a_{1}=\max(a_{1},...,a_{t})
. Then

\operatorname{ord}_{I}(f)=\min(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t},\lfloor(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{t})/2\rfloor).

Proof.

We may assume that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
. Let
r=\min(a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t},\lfloor(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{t})/2\rfloor).
We prove by induction on
r
that
f\in I^{r}
. The base case
r=0
is obvious.

Assume that
r\geq 1
, then
a_{2}\geq 1
. Let
v
be the largest index such that
a_{v}>0
, and
u
be the largest index such that
a_{u}=a_{1}
. In other words, we have
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{u}>a_{u+1}\geq\cdots a_{v}>a_{v+1}=0
. If
u=v
, i.e.
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{v}
, then let
\mathbf{b}=(a_{1},...,a_{v-1}-1,a_{v}-1)
, then
f=x^{\mathbf{b}}(x_{v-1}x_{v})
and by induction
x^{\mathbf{b}}\in I^{r-1}
. If
u>v
, let
\mathbf{b}=(a_{1},...,a_{u-1},a_{u}-1,a_{u+1},...,a_{v}-1)
. Then
f=x^{\mathbf{b}}(x_{u}x_{v})
and
x^{\mathbf{b}}\in I^{r-1}
by induction.

We will now show that
f\notin I^{r+1}
. Indeed, if
a_{1}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{t}a_{i}
, then
\deg f\leq 2r+1
, and thus
f\notin I^{r+1}
. Assume that
a_{1}>a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}
. Let
\mathbf{b}=(0,a_{2},...,a_{t})
. Then
|\mathbf{b}|=r=a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}
. We have
\partial^{*}f/\partial^{*}x^{\mathbf{b}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\notin I
. Thus
f\notin I^{(r+1)}
. In particular
f\notin I^{r+1}
as required.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Throughout the section,
\Delta
denotes a one-dimensional simplicial complex,
I=I_{\Delta}
the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
. Since
\dim\Delta=1
,
\reg I_{\Delta}\leq 3
by Lemma
2.6
. Thus,

is generated by squarefree monomials of degree at most three. Furthermore,

has a minimal generator of degree three if and only if
\Delta
contains a triangle. We first give a simple proof of Theorem
1.1
when
\girth\Delta=3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a one-dimensional simplex such that
\girth\Delta=3
. Let
I=I_{\Delta}
. Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
a_{i}\leq s
for all
i=1,...,n
. Assume that
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 3s
, then
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{s}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
s
. The base case when
s=1
follows from the fact that
\dim\Delta=1
.

Assume that
s\geq 2
. We may assume that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
. If
a_{3}=s
, then
a_{1}=a_{2}=s
by assumption. Thus
x^{\mathbf{a}}
is divisible by
(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})^{s}
. Thus
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{s}
. Thus we may assume that
a_{i}\leq s-1
for all
i\geq 3
.

Furthermore, since
a_{1}+a_{2}\leq 2s
and
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 3s
, we deduce that
a_{3}\geq 1
. Let
\mathbf{b}=(a_{1}-1,a_{2}-1,a_{3}-1,a_{4},...,a_{n})
. Then
b_{i}\leq s-1
for all
i
and
|\mathbf{b}|=|\mathbf{a}|-3\geq 3(s-1)
. By induction
x^{\mathbf{b}}\in I^{s-1}
. Therefore,
x^{\mathbf{a}}=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})x^{\mathbf{b}}\in I^{s}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a one-dimensional simplex such that
\girth\Delta=3
. Let
I=I_{\Delta}
. For
s\geq 1
, let
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
be an intermediate ideal lying between
I^{s}
and
I^{(s)}
. Then
\reg J=3s
.

Proof.

Since
\girth\Delta=3
,
I
has a generator of degree three. By degree reason,
\reg J\geq 3s
. Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
J
. By Remark
2.9
and Lemma
2.4
,
a_{i}\leq s-1
for all
i=1,...,n
and
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin J
. By Lemma
3.1
,
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 3s-1
. It suffices to show that
|\mathbf{a}|+i\leq 3s-1
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
is a subsimplicial complex of
\Delta
, thus
i\leq 2
. It remains to consider the cases where
|\mathbf{a}|=3s-1
and
|\mathbf{a}|=3s-2
.

If
|\mathbf{a}|=3s-1
. By Lemma
3.1
,
x_{j}x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{s}
, for all
j\in[n]
, i.e.
I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}=(x_{1},...,x_{n})
. Thus
i=0
and
\reg J=3s
.

If
|\mathbf{a}|=3s-2
. By Lemma
3.1
,
x_{u}x_{v}x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{s}
for all
u\neq v\in[n]
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
is a subsimplicial complex of the zero-dimensional simplicial complex whose facets are
\{1\},...,\{n\}
. Therefore,
i\leq 1
, and
|\mathbf{a}|+i\leq 3s-1
, as required.
∎

Now assume that
\girth\Delta\geq 4
. In this case,
I_{\Delta}
is the edge ideal of the complement of
\Delta
. Till the rest of the section, we denote
G
the complement of
\Delta
. Then
G
has no isolated vertices,
\alpha(G)=2
and
I=I(G)
is the edge ideal of
G
. Before proving Theorem
1.1
, we recall the steps from Introduction for convenient of readers. Fix
s\geq 2
and
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
an intermediate ideal. By Corollary
2.14
,

\reg J\geq 2s+\mu(G)-1=\begin{cases}2s+1&\text{ when }\girth\Delta=4\\
2s&\text{ when }\girth\Delta\geq 5.\end{cases}

Let
(\mathbf{a},i)
be an extremal exponent of
J
. Assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(x^{\mathbf{a}})=r
. A decomposition
x^{\mathbf{a}}=MN
is called a good decomposition if
M
is a minimal monomial generator of
I^{r}
, and
N\notin I
. An index
i
where
x_{i}
is of highest exponent in
N
is called a critical index of
\mathbf{a}
with respect to this decomposition. The steps to prove the required upper bounds for
|\mathbf{a}|+i
are:

(1)

We prove that if
i=2

|\mathbf{a}|\leq(\girth\Delta)/(\girth\Delta-2)(s-1)
, reducing the problem to consider extremal exponents
(\mathbf{a},i)
where
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
and
i\leq 1
.

(2)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
, we prove that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
.

(3)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
, let
x^{\mathbf{a}}=MN
be a good decomposition of
x^{\mathbf{a}}
. Let
i
be a critical index of
N
with respect to this decomposition. We prove that if
x_{i}^{2}|N
, then
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
is a cone over
i
. Consequently, this implies that
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2s
, and if
|\mathbf{a}|=2s
, then

is squarefree and
\deg N=2
.

(4)

Assuming
|\mathbf{a}|=2s

\girth\Delta=4
and
i=0
. This completes the case
\girth\Delta=4
.

(5)

Assuming
\girth\Delta\geq 5
and

, we prove that
i=0
.

We fix the following notation throughout the rest of the section. Let
\Delta
be a one-dimensional simplicial complex with
\girth\Delta\geq 4
. Let
G
be the complement of
\Delta
. Let
I=I_{\Delta}=I(G)
, and
J\in\Inter(I^{s},I^{(s)})
an intermediate ideal.

We now proceed to the first step to bound the degree of extremal exponents
(\mathbf{a},i)
of
J
when
i=2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(\mathbf{a},2)
is an extremal exponent of
J
. Let
\ell
be the length of a smallest cycle in
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
. Then

|\mathbf{a}|\leq\ell(s-1)/(\ell-2).

In particular, if
\girth\Delta=4
, then
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2s-2
, and if
\girth\Delta\geq 5
, then
|\mathbf{a}|\leq 2s-3
.

Proof.

Let
1\cdots\ell
be a cycle in
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
. Then we have
x_{i}x_{i+1}\notin\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. In particular
x_{i}x_{i+1}\notin I
. But then for every
t\notin\{i,i+1\}
,
t
is adjacent to either
i
or
i+1
as
\Delta
is of dimension
1
. By Lemma
2.16
, we have

|\mathbf{a}|-a_{i}-a_{i+1}\leq s-1.

Summing over all
\ell
edges we deduce that

(\ell-2)|\mathbf{a}|\leq\ell(s-1).

In particular,

|\mathbf{a}|\leq\frac{\ell(s-1)}{\ell-2}.

By Lemma
2.2
, and the fact that
I\subseteq\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
is a subsimplicial complex of
\Delta
. Thus
\ell\geq\girth\Delta
, and the last past follows immediately.
∎

To accomplish step 2, we also need the following property of minimal generators of symbolic powers.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a simple graph such that
\alpha(G)=2
. Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of
G
. Let
f=x^{\mathbf{a}}
be a minimal monomial generator of
I^{(s)}
. Then

a_{i}\leq\sum_{j\in N(i)}a_{j}.

Proof.

We may assume that
i=1
and
N(1)=\{2,...,t\}
. Assume by contrary that
a_{1}>a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}
. Let
g=f/x_{1}
. Then
g\notin I^{(s)}
and
x_{1}\in\sqrt{I^{(s)}:g}
. Let
h=x_{t+1}^{a_{t+1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
and we may assume that
a_{t+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
. Since
\{t+1,...,n\}
forms a clique in
G
,
h\in I^{(s_{2})}
, where
s_{2}=a_{t+2}+\cdots+a_{n}
. By Lemma
2.5
,
\sum_{i\neq 1,t+1}a_{i}\geq s
. Now
x_{1}^{a_{1}-1}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{t}^{a_{t}}\in I^{s_{1}}
where
s_{1}=a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}
as
a_{1}-1\geq a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}
. Therefore,
g\in I^{(s_{1}+s_{2})}\subseteq I^{(s)}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

We are now ready for step 2.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be an exponent such that
x^{\mathbf{a}}\notin J
. Assume that
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
, then
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)=\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\neq\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Let
g
be a minimal generator of
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
such that
g\notin\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
. Therefore,
g\notin I
. By Corollary
2.17
and the fact that
\dim\Delta=1
, we deduce that
\deg g=1
. We may assume that
g=x_{1}
. Let
\{2,3,...,t\}
be the set of neighbors of
1
. Assume that
a_{t+1}\geq a_{t+2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
. By Lemma
2.5
,

a_{2}+\cdots+a_{t}+a_{t+2}+\cdots+a_{n}\geq s.

Since
x_{1}\notin\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, by Lemma
2.16
and Lemma
2.18
, we deduce that

a_{2}+a_{3}+\cdots+a_{t}+\lfloor(a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{n})/2\rfloor\leq s-1.

Therefore,

2(a_{2}+a_{3}+\cdots+a_{t})+(a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{n})\leq 2s-1.

By Lemma
2.15
, there exists a minimal generator
P=x^{\mathbf{b}}
of
I^{(s)}
such that
x_{1}=\sqrt{P/\gcd(P,x^{\mathbf{a}})}
. Thus,
b_{1}>a_{1}
and
b_{i}\leq a_{i}
for all

. By Lemma
3.4
,

a_{1}<b_{1}\leq\sum_{i\in N(1)}b_{i}\leq\sum_{i\in N(1)}a_{i}.

Therefore,

|\mathbf{a}|<2(a_{2}+a_{3}+\cdots+a_{t})+(a_{t+1}+\cdots+a_{n})\leq 2s-1,

a contradiction.
∎

.

In general,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)\neq\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
when

Steps 3, 4, 5 are done in the next three lemmas.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{n}
be an exponent such that
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(x^{\mathbf{a}})=r<s
and
|\mathbf{a}|\geq 2s-1
. Let
x^{\mathbf{a}}=MN
be a good representation of

. Let

be a critical index of

such that

\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
is a cone over

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, assume that
i=1
is the critical index of
N
such that
x_{1}^{2}|N
. We will prove by induction on
s
that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
is a cone over
1
. For the base case
s\leq 2
, since
a_{1}\geq 2
, by Remark
2.9
,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)
is a cone over
1
.

Assume that
s\geq 3
. Since
\deg N\geq 2(s-r)-1
,
1
is a critical index of
N
, and
|\supp N|\leq 2
, we deduce that
b_{1}\geq(s-r)
where
b_{1}
is the exponent of
1
in
N
. Since
\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}\supseteq\sqrt{I^{s-r}:N}
, we deduce that
x_{j}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
for all
j\in N(1)
.

Assume that
N(1)=\{2,...,t\}
. Since
\alpha(G)=2
,
\{t+1,...,n\}
forms a clique in
G
. Since
a_{1}\leq s-1
,
r\geq 1
. Write
M=e_{1}\cdots e_{r}
where
e_{i}
are edges in
G
. Note that none of the edges
e_{i}
is of the form
x_{i}x_{j}
where

, as otherwise
x_{1}^{2}x_{i}x_{j}\in I^{2}
, and thus
x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{r+1}
, a contradiction.

Assume by contradiction that
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I^{s})
is not a cone over
1
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a minimal generator
f\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
such that
x_{1}|f
. Since
\alpha(G)=2
,
\deg f\leq 2
. There are two cases:

Case 1.

\deg f=2
. Since
x_{j}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, for all
j\in N(1)
, we may assume that
f=x_{1}x_{t+1}
. By Lemma
2.16
,
\sum_{i\neq 1,t+1}a_{i}\geq s
.

If one of the edges
e_{i}
is of the form
x_{1}x_{i}
or
x_{t+1}x_{j}
, say
e_{1}
. Let
x^{\mathbf{b}}=e_{2}\cdots e_{r}N
. Then
\sum_{i\neq 1,t+1}b_{i}\geq s-1
. By Lemma

,
f\in\sqrt{I^{s-1}:x^{\mathbf{b}}}
. By induction

is a cone over

, thus
x_{t+1}\in\sqrt{I^{s-1}:x^{\mathbf{b}}}\subseteq\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, which is a contradiction.

If
\supp M\subseteq\{t+2,...,n\}
, then
\sum_{j\in N(t+1)}a_{j}\geq a_{t+2}+\cdots+a_{n}=\sum_{j\neq 1,t+1}a_{j}\geq s
. By Lemma
2.16
,
x_{t+1}\in\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbf{a}}}
, which is a contradiction.

Thus we may assume that one of the edges

is of the form

where

,

, say

. This implies that

, as otherwise, say

(x_{1}x_{u})e_{1}=(x_{1}x_{i})(x_{u}x_{t+2})\in I^{2}\implies x^{\mathbf{a}}\in I^{r+1},\text{ a contradiction}.

Thus
a_{t+1}=0
. Let
A=N(t+1)\cap\supp\mathbf{a}
and

. Denote

and

. Then we have

Since

,
a\geq 1
. Denote

If
a_{1}\geq b
, by Lemma
2.18
,
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)=b
. Thus,

\sum_{i\in N(t+1)}a_{i}+\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)=a+b\geq s.

If
a_{1}<b
. By Lemma
2.18
,
\operatorname{ord}_{I}(h)=\lfloor(b+a_{1})/2\rfloor
. We have
|\mathbf{a}|=a+b+a_{1}\geq 2s-1

Lemma 3.8

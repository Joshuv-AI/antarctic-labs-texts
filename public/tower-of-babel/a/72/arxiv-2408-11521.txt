Dickman type stochastic processes with short- and long- range dependence
Danijel Grahovac, Anastasiia Kovtun, Nikolai N. Leonenko and Andrey Pepelyshev

Abstract
We study properties of the (generalized) Dickman distribution with two parameters and the stationary solution of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck stochastic differential equation driven by a Poisson process. In particular, we show that the marginal distribution of this solution is the Dickman distribution. Additionally, we investigate superpositions of Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes which may have short- or long-range dependencies and marginal distribution of the form of the Dickman distribution. The numerical algorithm for simulation of these processes is presented.

Introduction

In
[
46
]
a (generalised) Dickman distribution is defined as a distribution of a random variable
D_{\theta}
satisfying the distributional fixed-point equation

D_{\theta}\overset{d}{=}U^{1/\theta}(1+D_{\theta}),

(1)

where
\theta>0
,
U
is independent of
D_{\theta}
and has the uniform distribution on
(0,1]
. The density of
D_{\theta}
is given by

f_{\theta}(x)=\frac{e^{-\gamma\theta}}{\Gamma(\theta)}\rho_{\theta}(x)\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x),~x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function,
\gamma=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)\approx 0.5772
is Euler’s constant and the function
\rho_{\theta}(x)
satisfies the difference-differential equation:

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}(x)=0,\quad x\leq 0,

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}(x)=x^{\theta-1},\quad 0<x\leq 1,

\displaystyle x\rho^{\prime}_{\theta}(x)+(1-\theta)\rho_{\theta}(x)+\theta\rho_{\theta}(x-1)=0,\quad x>1.

It is also known that the random variable
D_{\theta}
with the Dickman distribution satisfies

D_{\theta}\overset{d}{=}U_{1}^{1/\theta}+(U_{1}U_{2})^{1/\theta}+(U_{1}U_{2}U_{3})^{1/\theta}+(U_{1}U_{2}U_{3}U_{4})^{1/\theta}+\cdots,

where
\{U_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are independent identically distributed random variables with the uniform distribution on
(0,1]
, see e.g.
[
46
,
47
,
26
]
.

The function

occurs, among others, in context of number theory and combinatorics, see
[
57
,
22
,
23
]
and references therein. For
\theta=1
the function

is the celebrated Dickman function
[

]
, which appears even earlier in Ramanujan’s unpublished paper
[
49
]
. The Dickman distribution is closely related to the so-called Goncharov distribution and the Poisson-Dirichlet family of distributions, see
[
48
,
36
,
46
,
45
,
35
]
and the references therein. Recently, a number of new applications of the Dickman distribution have appeared in random graph theory
[
46
]
, biology
[
33
]
, physics
[
17
]
and other fields; see
[
45
]
for more references and details. The Dickman distribution also appears in various limiting schemes
[

,
46
,
47
,
13
,
10
,

]
. It also appears as a special case of Vervaat perpetuities

For the historical account of the Dickman distribution see

The term

In this paper we first propose a new generalisation of the Dickman distribution by introducing another parameter. The new parameter is the scale parameter of the distribution. We study the properties of the Dickman distribution with the two parameters.

We then consider the Lévy driven Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) type processes with Dickman type marginals. Non-Gaussian OU processes and their superpositions were introduced and studied in

2

7

2
Generalised Dickman distribution

We start by introducing a generalised Dickman distribution with two parameters.

Definition 2.1

A random variable
D_{\theta,a}
has the (generalised) Dickman distribution with parameters
\theta>0
and
a>0
, shortly
D_{\theta,a}\sim GD(\theta,a)
, if
D_{\theta,a}
satisfies the distributional fixed-point equation

D_{\theta,a}\overset{d}{=}U^{1/\theta}(a+D_{\theta,a}),

(3)

where
"\overset{d}{=}"
denotes the equality in distribution,
U
is independent of
D_{\theta,a}
and has the uniform distribution on
(0,1]
.

For
a=1
, the
GD(\theta,1)
distribution is equivalent to the generalised Dickman distribution defined by (
1
). Since
aD_{\theta,1}\overset{d}{=}U^{1/\theta}(a+aD_{\theta,1})
,
aD_{\theta,1}
satisfies (
3
). Hence,

aD_{\theta,1}\overset{d}{=}D_{\theta,a},

(4)

the parameter
a
is the scale parameter of the
GD(\theta,a)
distribution. The next proposition shows that there are many different characterizations of the
GD(\theta,a)
distribution.

Proposition 2.2

Let
\theta>0
and
a>0
.

1.

Let a random variable
D
satisfy the distributional fixed-point equation

D\overset{d}{=}U_{a}^{1/\theta}(1+a^{-1}D),

(5)

where
U_{a}
is independent of
D
and has the uniform distribution on
(0,a^{\theta}]
. Then
D\sim GD(\theta,a)
.

2.

A random variable given by

D=aU_{1}^{1/\theta}+a(U_{1}U_{2})^{1/\theta}+a(U_{1}U_{2}U_{3})^{1/\theta}+\cdots,

(6)

where
U_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{N}
, are mutually independent with the uniform distribution on
(0,1]
, has the
GD(\theta,a)
distribution.

3.

A random variable given by

D=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}ae^{-T_{n}}

where
T_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{N}
, are arrival times a Poisson process with parameter
\theta
, has the
GD(\theta,a)
distribution.

Proof 1

1.

Let

be uniformly distributed on
(0,1]

D

D\overset{d}{=}(a^{\theta}U)^{1/\theta}(1+a^{-1}D)\overset{d}{=}U^{1/\theta}(a+D)

satisfies (

2.

Note first that the almost sure convergence of the series (

[

]

D=U_{1}^{1/\theta}(a+aU_{2}^{1/\theta}+a(U_{2}U_{3})^{1/\theta}+\cdots)\overset{d}{=}U_{1}^{1/\theta}(a+D),

hence,
D
satisfies (
3

3.

This statement follows from Proposition 2 in
[
46
]
and (

3
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type processes with Dickman marginals and beyond

In this section we introduce several stationary processes related to the Dickman distribution. For a stochastic process
Y(t),\;t\geq 0
, we will denote the cumulant function of a random vector
(Y(t_{1}),\ldots,Y(t_{m}))
by

\kappa_{(Y(t_{1}),\ldots,Y(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})=\log\mathbb{E}e^{i(z_{1}Y(t_{1})+\ldots+z_{m}Y(t_{m}))},\quad z_{j}\in\mathbb{R},~j=1,\ldots,m,

and, in particular,

\kappa_{Y}(z)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{izY(1)},\;z\in\mathbb{R},

is the cumulant function of
Y(1)
.

3.1
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process driven by a Poisson process

Non-Gaussian Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type processes have been studied in
[
6
,
7
]
, see also references therein.

A strictly stationary stochastic process
X(t),\;t\geq 0,
is said to be an
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process driven by the Lévy process
(OU type process) if it is the strong solution of the following stochastic differential equation

dX(t)=-\lambda X(t)dt+dZ(\lambda t),\quad t\geq 0,

(8)

where
\lambda>0
and
Z(t)
is a Lévy process such that

\mathbb{E}\log(1+|Z(1)|)<\infty,

see
[
4
]
for details. The process
Z(t),\;t\geq 0,
is commonly referred to as the
backward driving Lévy process
(BDLP), see
[
6
]
.

The strictly stationary solution of (
8
) is given by

X(t)=e^{-\lambda t}X(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}dZ(\lambda\tau).

If we extend
Z(t),\;t\geq 0,
to a two-sided Lévy process
Z(t),\;t\in\mathbb{R}
, meaning that we put for
t<0
,
Z(t)=-\widetilde{Z}(-t-)
, where
\widetilde{Z}(t),\;t\geq 0,
is an independent copy of the Lévy process
Z(t),\;t\geq 0,
modified to be càdlàg, then an OU type process can be written in the form

X(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}dZ(\lambda\tau),\quad t\geq 0.

(9)

From
[
6
]
and
[
50
]
, it follows that for any self-decomposable distribution
D
there exists a BDLP
Z(t),\;t\geq 0
, such that the stationary distribution of an OU type process is
D
.
Moreover, for the cumulant function of
Z
and
X
it holds that

\kappa_{(X(t_{1}),\dots,X(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{Z}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}e^{-\lambda(t_{j}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{j}-s)\right)ds,

see
[
38
]
.
In particular, using the change of variables we get

\kappa_{X}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{Z}(e^{-s}z)ds,\quad\kappa_{Z}(z)=z\frac{d}{dz}\kappa_{X}(z).

(10)

The correlation function of an OU type process (if it exists) is of the form

r(\tau)=\mathrm{Corr}(X(t),X(t+\tau))=e^{-\lambda\tau},\quad\tau\geq 0,

see e.g.
[
51
,
6
,
50
]
for more details on OU type processes.

For an OU type processes where the stationary distribution is
GD(\theta,a)
the cumulant function of the BDLP
Z(t)
is of the form

\kappa_{Z}(z)=z\frac{d}{dz}\int_{0}^{a}(e^{izu}-1)\frac{\theta}{u}du=z\int_{0}^{a}e^{izu}(iu)\frac{\theta}{u}du=\theta(e^{iaz}-1)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{izN_{\theta,a}(1)},

(11)

where
N_{\theta,a}(t),\;t\geq 0,
is a homogeneous Poisson process with rate parameter
\theta>0
and jumps of size
a>0
, that is
\mathbb{P}(N_{\theta,a}(t)=k)=\mathbb{P}(aN_{\theta,1}(t)=k),\;k\geq 0
. Thus, we arrived at the following statement.

Theorem 3.1

Let
N_{\theta,a}(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a homogeneous Poisson process with rate parameter
\theta
and jumps of size
a>0
. The stationary solution
X(t)
of the stochastic differential equation

dX(t)=-\lambda X(t)dt+dN_{\theta,a}(\lambda t),\quad t\geq 0,

(12)

has the marginal distribution
GD(\theta,a)
and

\mathrm{Cov}(X(t),X(t+\tau))=e^{-\lambda\tau}\mathrm{Var}X(t)=a^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}e^{-\lambda\tau},\quad\tau\geq 0.

(13)

We will refer to a process
X(t)
from Theorem
3.1
as
Dickman OU (DOU) process
. From (
13
) we get that the second-order spectral density of the DOU process is

f(\omega)=\frac{a^{2}\theta}{2\pi}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+\omega^{2}},\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}.

3.2
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process driven by a Poisson process of order
k

The Poisson process of order
k
was introduced in
[
41
]
, see also
[
40
,
39
]
and the references therein.
The
Poisson process of order
k
,
k\geq 1
, is given by

N_{\theta}^{(k)}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k\theta}(t)}Y_{i},\quad t\geq 0,

where
Y_{i},\;i=1,2,\dots
is a sequence of independent identically distributed random variables with the discrete uniform distribution on the set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, which is independent of the homogeneous Poisson process
N_{k\theta}(t)
with parameter
k\theta
.
If we take the Poisson process of order
k
as the BDLP in (
8
), we get an OU type process which is closely related to the Dickman OU process.

Theorem 3.5

Let
X^{(k)}(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a stationary solution of the stochastic differential equation (
8

\{X^{(k)}(t),\;t\geq 0\}\overset{\text{fdd}}{=}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}X_{\theta,j}(t),\;t\geq 0\right\},

(18)

where
X_{\theta,j}(t),\;j=1,\ldots,k
, are independent DOU processes with parameters
\theta
and
a_{j}=j
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}X^{(k)}(t)=\theta\frac{k(k+1)}{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X^{(k)}(t),X^{(k)}(t+\tau))=\frac{\theta}{2}\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}e^{-\lambda\tau},\;\tau\geq 0.

Proof 3

For
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}
, the cumulant function of the random vector
(X^{(k)}(t_{1}),\dots,X^{(k)}(t_{m}))
is

\displaystyle\kappa_{(X^{(k)}(t_{1}),\dots,X^{(k)}(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\log\mathbb{E}e^{i(z_{1}X^{(k)}(t_{1})+\cdots+z_{m}X^{(k)}(t_{m}))}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{N_{\theta}^{(k)}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds

,

see e.g. Eq. (3.7) in
[

]
.
The cumulant function of

is

\kappa_{N_{\theta}^{(k)}}(z)=k\theta\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{k}e^{ijz}-1\right)=\theta\sum_{j=1}^{k}(e^{ijz}-1).

Hence, we get that

\displaystyle\kappa_{(X^{(k)}(t_{1}),\dots,X^{(k)}(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\theta\left(e^{ij\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)}-1\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{N_{\theta,j}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{i(z_{1}X_{\theta,j}(t_{1})+\cdots+z_{m}X_{\theta,j}(t_{m}))},

where for each
j=1,\dots,k
,
X_{\theta,j}(t)
is the DOU processes with parameters
\theta
and
j
,
N_{\theta,j}(t)
is its driving Poisson process with rate parameter
\theta
and jumps of size
j
. This proves (
18
).

3.3
OU processes driven by Bell-Touchard processes

Following
[
24
]
, a Bell-Touchard process with parameter
\alpha>0
and
\nu>0
is a compound Poisson process

B_{\alpha,\nu}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\alpha,\nu}(t)}Y_{i},\quad t\geq 0,

(19)

where
Y_{i},\;i=1,2,\dots
, is the sequence of i.i.d. random variables with the probability mass function

\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}=n)=\frac{1}{(e^{\nu}-1)}\frac{\nu^{n}}{n!},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

which are independent of the Poisson process
N_{\alpha,\nu}(t)
with parameter
\alpha(e^{\nu}-1)
.

Remark 5

Note that the distribution of the jumps
Y_{i},i=1,2,\ldots
, is the zero-truncated Poisson distribution.

Similarly as in the previous subsection, we consider the OU process (
8
) with the BDLP
Z(t)=B_{\alpha,\nu}(t),\;t\geq 0
, given by (
19
). It turns out that this process corresponds to an infinite superposition of independent DOU processes.

Theorem 3.6

Let
X^{BT}(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a stationary solution of the stochastic differential equation (
8
) with the BDLP
Z(t)=B_{\alpha,\nu}(t),\;t\geq 0
, the Bell-Touchard process. Then

\left\{X^{BT}(t),\;t\geq 0\right\}\overset{\text{fdd}}{=}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{\theta_{j},j}(t),\;t\geq 0\right\},

where
X_{\theta_{j},j}(t),\;j\geq 1
, are independent DOU processes with parameters
\theta_{j}=\frac{\alpha\nu^{j}}{j!}
and
a_{j}=j
. Mean and covariance functions of
X_{\alpha,\nu}(t),\;t\geq 0
, are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}X^{BT}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j\frac{\alpha\nu^{j}}{j!}=\alpha\nu e^{\nu},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X^{BT}(t),X^{BT}(t+\tau))=\frac{\alpha e^{\nu}}{2}\nu(\nu+1)e^{-\lambda\tau}.

Proof 4

Since

\kappa_{B_{\alpha,\nu}}(z)=\alpha(e^{\nu}-1)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{ijz}\frac{1}{(e^{\nu}-1)}\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!}-1\right)=\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!}\left(e^{ijz}-1\right),

similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
we have that

\displaystyle\kappa_{(X^{BT}(t_{1}),\dots,X^{BT}(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{B_{\alpha,\nu}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\alpha\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!}\left(e^{i\sum_{l=1}^{m}jz_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)}-1\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{N_{\alpha\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!},j}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}z_{l}e^{-\lambda(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\log\mathbb{E}e^{i(z_{1}X_{\theta_{j},j}(t_{1})+\cdots+z_{n}jX_{\theta_{j},j}(t_{n}))},

where for each
j=1,\dots,k
,
X_{\theta_{j},j}(t),\;t\geq 0
, is the DOU process with parameters
\theta_{j}=\frac{\alpha\nu^{j}}{j!}
and
a_{j}=j
, driven by the Poisson process
N_{\alpha\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!},j}(t)
with rate parameter
\alpha\frac{\nu^{j}}{j!}
and jumps of size

.

4
Superpositions of DOU processes

While OU type processes provide stationary models with flexible choice of marginal distributions, the correlation function of these processes always decays exponentially. However, by considering superpositions of such processes, different correlation structures may be obtained.
Superpositions of OU type processes have been introduced in
[
2
]
, see also
[
8
,
3
]
. The basic idea of the construction is to randomize the parameter
\lambda
in (
9
). Instead of following
[
2
]
, we shall introduce supOU processes using the parametrization from
[
20
]
which is more suitable for simulation and corresponds better to the definition (
9
).

Let
Z(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a Lévy process such that
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|Z(1)|)<\infty
and having the Lévy-Khintchine characteristic triplet
(b,\sigma^{2},\mu)
such that

\kappa_{Z}(z)=izb-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\sigma^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{izx}-1-izx\mathbbm{1}_{[-1,1]}(x))\mu(dx).

The second ingredient in the construction of the superposition process is a measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\eta^{-1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\xi^{-1}\pi(d\xi)<\infty.

(20)

The quadruple
(b,\sigma^{2},\mu,\pi)
is sometimes called the
generating quadruple

[
20
]
.
Suppose that Lévy basis
\Lambda
is a homogeneous infinitely divisible independently scattered random measure on
S=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\kappa_{\Lambda(A)}(z)=\log\mathbb{E}e^{iz\Lambda(A)}=(\pi\times Leb)(A)\kappa_{Z}(z),\quad A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}),

where
Leb
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. A
superposition of OU type processes
(
supOU
) is defined as the following stochastic integral with respect to
\Lambda

[
2
,
20
]

Y(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\xi(t-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t-s)\Lambda(d\xi,\eta ds).

(21)

The existence of the integral in (
21
) was proven in
[
2
]
and the equivalence of different parametrizations was shown in
[
20
]
, see also
[
8
]
. The supOU process is strictly stationary and its stationary distribution is a self-decomposable distribution determined by the BDLP
Z(t)
similarly as with the OU type processes. Moreover, for
0\leq t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}
, the cumulant function of finite dimensional distributions is given by

\kappa_{(Y(t_{1}),\dots,Y(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta\kappa_{Z}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\xi(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds\pi(d\xi).

(22)

In particular, the relation (
10
) between cumulant functions holds. The correlation function of
Y(t)
, if it exists, is given by

r(\tau)=\mathrm{Corr}(Y(t),Y(t+\tau))=\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-1}e^{-\tau\xi}\pi(d\xi),\quad\tau\geq 0.

(23)

Thus, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\infty}r(\tau)d\tau=\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-2}\pi(d\xi),

(24)

and this integral can be both finite and infinite. Hence, we will say that a supOU process exhibits long-range dependence (has a long memory) if the integral in (
24
) is infinite and we will say that it exhibits a short-range dependence (short memory) otherwise. Moreover, if
\pi
is regularly varying at zero, i.e.

\pi((0,\xi])\sim\ell(\xi^{-1})\xi^{1+\alpha},\quad\text{ as }\xi\to 0,

(25)

for some
\alpha>0
and a slowly varying function
\ell(\cdot)
at infinity, then

r(\tau)\sim\frac{\eta(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\ell(\tau)\tau^{-\alpha},\quad\text{ as }\tau\to\infty,

see
[
30
]
.
In particular, if
\alpha\in(0,1)
in (
25
), then the supOU process exhibits long-range dependence; see
[
20
]
and
[
28
]
for details.
Overall, we can see that the supOU processes form a wide class of stochastic processes and the representation (
21
) enables one to independently model marginal distributions by the choice of the BDLP and the correlation structure by the choice of the measure
\pi
.

4.1
Dickman supOU processes

Let
\pi
be any measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that (
20
) holds and let
Z(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a Poisson process with parameter
\theta
and jumps of size
a>0
such that

\kappa_{Z}(z)=\theta(e^{iaz}-1),

and the Lévy-Khintchine triplet is

b=\begin{cases}a\theta,&\text{if }a\leq 1\\
0,&\text{if }a>1\\
\end{cases},\quad\sigma^{2}=0,\quad\mu=\theta\delta_{a},

(26)

where
\delta_{a}
is the Dirac measure concentrated at
a
.

It follows from (
11
) that the supOU process
Y(t),\;t\geq 0,
with generating quadruple
(b,0,\mu,\pi)
has the
GD(\theta,a)
marginal distribution and the correlation function (
23
). We will refer to this class of supOU processes as
Dickman supOU processes
(
supDOU
). Note that for any choice of the measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
we get one example of a stationary process with Dickman marginals. We now consider some examples for specific choices of
\pi
.

Example 4.1

Suppose that
\pi
is degenerate such that
\pi\left(\{\lambda\}\right)=1
for some
\lambda>0
. Then it follows from (
22
) since
\eta=\lambda
that the finite dimensional distributions of the supDOU process are the same as for the standard OU type process (
9
), that is

\kappa_{(Y(t_{1}),\dots,Y(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda\kappa_{Z}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}z_{j}e^{-\lambda(t_{j}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{j}-s)\right)ds.

Example 4.2

Suppose
\pi
is a discrete probability measure such that
\pi\left(\{\lambda_{j}\}\right)=p_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{j}>0
and suppose that (
20
) holds, i.e.

\eta^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{-1}p_{j}<\infty.

Then we have from (
22
) that the supDOU process has the cumulant function of finite dimensional distributions given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{(Y(t_{1}),\dots,Y(t_{m}))}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta p_{j}\kappa_{Z}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda_{j}\frac{\eta p_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}\kappa_{Z}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{m}z_{l}e^{-\lambda_{j}(t_{l}-s)}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t_{l}-s)\right)ds.

Hence, a supDOU process
Y(t),\;t\geq 0,
has the same finite dimensional distributions as

\left\{Y(t),\;t\geq 0\right\}\overset{\text{fdd}}{=}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X^{(\lambda_{j})}_{\theta_{j},a}(t),\;t\geq 0\right\},

where
X^{(\lambda_{j})}_{\theta_{j},a}(t),\;t\geq 0
, are independent DOU processes with parameters
\theta_{j}=\frac{\eta p_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}\theta
,
a_{j}=a
and mean-reverting parameter
\lambda_{j}
.

Example 4.3

Let
\pi
be the gamma distribution
Gamma(1+\alpha,\beta)
for some
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
given by the density

p(\xi)=\frac{\beta^{1+\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\beta\xi},\quad\xi>0,

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the gamma function. Then (
20
) holds and a resulting supDOU process has
GD(\theta,a)
marginals and the correlation function can be explicitly computed to be

r(t)=\left(1+\frac{t}{\beta}\right)^{-\alpha},\quad t\geq 0.

In particular, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
we obtain a long-range dependent process with Dickman marginals.

More examples of possible choices of
\pi
and corresponding supOU processes are given in
[
5
]
.

4.2
Limit theorems for supDOU processes

Let
Y(t),\;t\geq 0,
be a supDOU process with parameters
\theta>0
and
a>0
. Denote
Y^{*}(t),\;t\geq 0,
the integrated supDOU process

Y^{*}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(Y(u)-\theta)du,

Limit theorems for integrated supOU processes have been proved in
[
30
]
for the finite variance case and in
[
31
]
for the infinite variance case. We apply now these results to supDOU processes.
In the following,
\overset{\text{fdd}}{\to}
denotes convergence of all finite-dimensional distributions and de Bruijn conjugate
\ell^{\sharp}(\cdot)
of some slowly varying function
\ell(\cdot)
which is a unique slowly varying function such that

\ell(x)\ell^{\sharp}(x\ell(x))\rightarrow 1
and

\ell^{\sharp}(x)\ell(x\ell^{\sharp}(x))\rightarrow 1
as
x\rightarrow\infty

see
[
12
, Theorem 1.5.13]
.

Corollary 4.4

1.

If
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-2}\pi(d\xi)<\infty
, then

\left\{\frac{1}{T^{1/2}}Y^{*}(Tt)\right\}\overset{\text{fdd}}{\to}\tilde{\sigma}B(t)\quad\text{ as }T\to\infty,

where
\{B(t),t\geq 0\}
is the standard Brownian motion and

\tilde{\sigma}^{2}=\eta a^{2}\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-2}\pi(d\xi)
.

2.

If
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-2}\pi(d\xi)=\infty
, assume that
\pi
has a density
p(x)
such that for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\ell(\cdot)
is slowly varying at infinity

p(x)\sim(1+\alpha)\ell(x^{-1})x^{\alpha},\quad as\;x\rightarrow 0.

Then

\Bigg\{\frac{1}{T^{1/(1+\alpha)}\ell^{\sharp}(T)^{1/(1+\alpha)}}Y^{*}(Tt)\Bigg\}\xrightarrow{\text{fdd}}\{S_{1+\alpha}(t)\},\quad\text{ as }T\to\infty,

where
\ell^{\sharp}(\cdot)
is de Bruijn conjugate of
1/\ell(x^{1/(1+\alpha)})
and
\{S_{1+\alpha}(t)\}
is the
(1+\alpha)
-stable Lévy process such that

\displaystyle\kappa_{S_{1+\alpha}(1)}(z)=-|z|^{1+\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}{-\alpha}\theta\eta^{-1}a^{1+\alpha}\cos\left(\frac{\pi(1+\alpha)}{2}\right)\times

\displaystyle\times\left(1-i\,\mathrm{sign}(z)\tan\left(\frac{\pi(1+\alpha)}{2}\right)\right).

Proof 5

The statement follows from Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 in
[
30
]
since the supDOU process has finite variance and the Blumenthal-Getoor index of the BDLP is
0
.

The convergence in Corollary
4.4
(i) can be extended to weak convergence in the space of continuous functions
C[0,1]
with uniform topology provided that additionally the fourth moment is finite (which is always true for the Dickman distribution) and
\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-3}\pi(d\xi)<\infty
, see
[
30
]
for details.

Integrated supOU processes with long-range dependence exhibit another interesting limiting behaviour called intermittency, see
[
30
]
.

Definition 4.5

For a stochastic process
X(t),t\geq 0
, let

\tau_{X}(q)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log\mathbb{E}|X(t)|^{q}}{\log t}

denote its scaling function which measures the rate of growth of moments as time goes to infinity.
We will say that the process
X(t)
exhibits the intermittency property if there exists
q^{*}
such that the function
q\rightarrow\tau_{X}(q)/q
is strictly increasing on
[q^{*},\infty)
.

For the integrated supDOU processes
Y^{*}(t),t\geq 0
, the scaling function
\tau_{Y^{*}}(q)/q
is constant if, for example, the measure
\pi
is concentrated on a finite set; specifically, we have

\tau_{Y^{*}}(q)=\frac{1}{2}q.

However, under the assumptions of Corollary
4.4
(i), if we additionally assume that (
25
) holds with
\alpha>1
, then the scaling function has the form

\tau_{Y^{*}}(q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}q,&0<q\leq 2\alpha,\\
q-\alpha,&q\geq 2\alpha,\end{cases}

and under the assumptions of Corollary
4.4
(ii) the scaling function has the form

\tau_{Y^{*}}(q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\alpha}q,&0<q\leq 1+\alpha,\\
q-\alpha,&q\geq 1+\alpha.\end{cases}

Since in both cases
\tau_{Y^{*}}(q)/q
is strictly increasing for
q>2
and
q>1+\alpha
respectively, such integrated supDOU processes exhibit intermittency, see
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
32
]
for details.

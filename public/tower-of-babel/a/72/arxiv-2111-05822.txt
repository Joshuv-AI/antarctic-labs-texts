L p L^{p} -theory for the exterior Stokes problem with Navier’s type slip-without-friction boundary conditions
Anis Dhifaoui

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the stationary Stokes equations in an exterior domain three-dimensional under a slip boundary condition without friction. We set the problem in weighted Sobolev spaces in order to control the behavior at infinity of the solutions. In this work, we try to investigate the existence and uniqueness of the weak solutions related to this problem in L P − L^{P}- theory when p > 3 p>3 . Our proof are based on obtaining inf-sup conditions that play a fundamental role. Keywords : Fluid mechanics, Stokes equations, slip boundary condition, exterior domain, Inf-Sup conditions, weighted Sobolev spaces.

Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the Stationary Stokes equations, this problem describes the flow of a viscous and incompressible fluid past an obstacle:

\begin{split}-\Delta\textit{{u}}+\nabla\pi&=\textit{{f}}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\mathrm{div}\,\textit{{u}}&=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{split}

(1.1)

In (

\Omega

the unknowns are the velocity of the fluid

\pi

For many years, the standard boundary conditions are the Dirichlet or no-slip boundary condition, has been the most widely used, given its success in reproducing the standard velocity profiles for incompressible viscous fluid. These latter conditions prescribe that the fluid adheres to the boundary of the domain. More generally, in the case where the obstacles have an approximate limit, the standard conditions are no longer valid (see for example
[
30
]
). Therefore, another approach has been introduced, which assumes that due to the roughness of the boundary and the viscosity of the fluid, there is a stagnant fluid layer near the boundary, which allows the fluid to slip (see for example
[
23

). To overcome the complicated description of the problem, the Navier slip boundary conditions are commonly used.

These conditions, proposed by Navier
[
28

\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0,\quad\big((\mathrm{\textbf{D}}\textbf{{u}})\textbf{{n}}+\alpha\textbf{{u}}\big)_{\tau}=\boldsymbol{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma,

(1.2)

\Gamma

\Gamma

We define the semi-norm

|u|_{W_{\alpha}^{m,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|=m}\|\rho^{\alpha}\partial^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)^{1/p}.

Proof.

.

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle W^{-1+m,p}_{\alpha+m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\perp P_{[1-3/p^{\prime}+\alpha]}^{\bigtriangleup}

\displaystyle-\Delta\,u=f\quad\text{ in}\,\,\,\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial n}=g\quad\text{ on}\,\,\,\Gamma.

Our first proposition is established also in

\mathcal{N}_{p,\alpha}^{\Delta}=\left\{w\in W_{\alpha}^{1,p}(\Omega);\,\,\,\,\Delta\,w=0\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial n}=0\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma\right\}.

Proposition 2.10
.

For any integer

,
\mathcal{N}_{p,\alpha}^{\Delta}
the subspace of all functions in

of the form

, where

Here also, we set

Theorem 2.11
.

Let

For any

. Then, the problem (

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{-1}(\Omega)/\mathcal{N}^{\Delta}_{p,-1}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{W^{-1/p,p}(\Omega)}\big)

(2.22)

If in addition,

and

in

) belongs to

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{W^{1/p^{\prime},p}(\Omega)}\big)

Proof.

Let us extend

by zero in
\Omega^{\prime}
and let
\widetilde{f}
denote the extended function. Then
\widetilde{f}
belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Using Theorem

Then

belongs to

. It follows from
[
24
, Theorem 3.12]
, that the following problem:

\displaystyle\Delta\,w=0\quad\text{ in }\,\,\,\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\nabla\,w\cdot n=g-\nabla\,\widetilde{v}\cdot n\quad\text{ on }\,\,\,\Gamma,

Now, suppose that

belongs to

The aim is to prove that

\begin{split}&\varphi,\,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad 0\leq\varphi,\,\psi\leq 1,\quad\varphi+\psi=1\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&\varphi=1\quad\text{in}\quad B_{R},\quad\text{supp }\varphi\subset B_{R+1}.\end{split}

be a continuous linear mapping from

belongs to

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}=\varphi\,{\widetilde{u}}+\psi\,\widetilde{u}.

Next, one can easily observe that

\displaystyle-\Delta\,\psi\,\widetilde{u}=f_{1}\quad\text{ in }\,\,\,\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle f_{1}=\widetilde{f}{\psi}-(2\nabla{\widetilde{u}}\nabla{\psi}+\widetilde{u}\Delta\,{\psi}).

Owing to the support of
\psi

\mathbb{R}^{3}.

yields that
{\psi\,\widetilde{u}}
belongs to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\,\varphi\,u=f_{2}\quad\text{ in }\,\,\,\Omega_{R+1},\\

where

]

Potential vector and inf-sup condition

In this section, we recall a result concerning the existence a vector potential, and we give the proof of the Inf-sup condition, which plays a crucial role in the existence and uniqueness of solutions. Let us recall the abstract setting of Babu
\check{s}
ka-Brezzi’s Theorem (see Babu
\check{s}
ka
[
9
]
and Brezzi
[
14
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
and
M
be two reflexive Banach spaces and
X^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}
their dual spaces. Let
a
be the continuous bilinear form defined on
X\times M
, let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X;\ M^{\prime})
and
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(M;\ X^{\prime})
be the operators defined by

\forall v\in X,\ \forall w\in M,\ a(v,w)=\ <Av,w>\ =\ <v,A^{\prime}w>

and
V=\mathrm{Ker}\,A
.
The following statements are equivalent:

i) There exist
\beta>0
such that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in M\\
w\not=0\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in X\\
v\not=0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{a(v,w)}{\|v\|_{X}\,\|w\|_{M}}\geq\beta.

(3.1)

ii) The operator
A:\ X/V\mapsto M^{\prime}
is an isomophism and
1/\beta
is the continuity constant of
A^{-1}
.

iii) The operator
A^{\prime}:\ M\mapsto X^{\prime}\bot\,V
is an isomophism and
1/\beta
is the continuity constant of
(A^{\prime})^{-1}
.

Remark 3.2
.

As consequence, if the Inf-Sup condition (
3.1
) is satisfied, then we have the following properties:

i) If
V=\{0\}
, then for any
f\in X^{\prime}
, there exists a unique
w\in M
such that

\forall v\in X,\ a(v,w)=\ <f,v>\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\|w\|_{M}\leq\dfrac{1}{\beta}\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}.

(3.2)

ii) If
V\not=\{0\}
, then for any
f\in X^{\prime},\
satisfying the compatibility condition:

\forall v\in V,\ <f,v>\ =0
, there exists a unique
w\in M
such that (
3.2
).

iii) For any
g\in M^{\prime},\ \exists v\in X
, unique up an additive element of
V
, such that:

\forall w\in M,\ a(v,w)=\ <g,w>\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\|v\|_{X/V}\leq\dfrac{1}{\beta}\|g\|_{M^{\prime}}.

Now, we recall a result concerning the potential vector provided in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
1<p<3/2
. Let
z
in
H^{p}_{0}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
satisfying

\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{z}}=0

in

\Omega

and

\langle\textbf{{z}}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\rangle_{\Gamma}=0
.

Then there exists a unique vector potential
\boldsymbol{\psi}
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that:

\displaystyle\textbf{{z}}=\textbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\text{,}\quad\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0\quad\text{ on }\,\,\Gamma.

(3.3)

In addition we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\textbf{{z}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.4)

We introduce the following space:

\displaystyle\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega)=\Big\{\textbf{{v}}\in W^{0,p}_{-1}(\Omega):\,\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{v}}\in L^{p}(\Omega),\,\,\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{v}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,\,,\,\,\textbf{{v}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0\,\,\text{ on }\,\,\Gamma\Big\}.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1<p<3/2
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega),
we have

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(||\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+||\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big).

(3.5)

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
in
\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega)
. It follows from [
[
24
]
, Theorem 5.1], that
\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega)
is continuously imbedded in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and so
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
belongs to
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Observe that for any
\chi\in W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, the following Green formula holds:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\varphi\cdot\textbf{{n}},\chi\rangle_{\Gamma}=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\varphi\cdot\nabla\,\chi\,d\textbf{x}.

(3.6)

Because
1<p<3/2
, the constants belong to
W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then (
3.6
) is still valid for

and thus we have:

\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\varphi\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\rangle_{\Gamma}=0.

Using Theorem
3.3
, we prove that there exists a unique vector potential
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\textbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\text{,}\quad\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0\quad\text{ on }\,\,\Gamma.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, let us solve the following problem,

\displaystyle\Delta\,\textit{{w}}=\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\Omega\quad\text{ and }\,\,\dfrac{\partial\textit{{w}}}{\partial\textbf{{n}}}=0\,\,\text{ on }\,\,\Gamma.

(3.7)

Thanks to Theorem
2.11
, problem (

\displaystyle\|\nabla\,\textit{{w}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(3.8)

Setting
{\textbf{{z}}}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}-\nabla\,\textit{{w}}
. It is clear that

belongs to

, it is divergence-free,

\displaystyle\|{\textbf{{z}}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.9)

Finally, the inequality (

) and (

).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.4
, the seminorm in the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is a norm on
\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega)
equivalent to the norm
||\boldsymbol{\varphi}||_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
.

The "inf-sup" condition is given by the following lemma:

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
p>3
. The following Inf-Sup Condition holds: there exists a constant
\beta>0
, such that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{V}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)\\
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\textbf{{V}}^{\,p}_{0,T}(\Omega)\\
\boldsymbol{\psi}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}}{\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p}_{T}(\Omega)}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{T}(\Omega)}}\geq\beta.

(3.10)

Proof.

Let
\textbf{{g}}\in\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)
and let us introduce the following Dirichlet problem:

-\Delta\,\chi=\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{g}}\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega,\quad\chi=0\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma.

It is shown in Theorem
3.7
of
[
24
]
, that this problem has a solution
\chi\in\mathring{W}_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and we have

\|\nabla\,\chi\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}.

Set
\textbf{{z}}=\textbf{{g}}-\nabla\,\chi
. Then we have
\textbf{{z}}\in\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)
,
\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{z}}=0
and we have

\|\textbf{{z}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.11)

Let
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
any function of
\textbf{{V}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)
, by Theorem
3.4
we have
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{Z}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{T}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Then due to (
3.5
) we can write

\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{W^{\,1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}=C\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{{g}}\in\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)\\
\textbf{{g}}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}}.

(3.12)

Using the fact that
\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in H_{0}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
and applying (
2.11
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\,\chi\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}=0.

(3.13)

Now, let
\lambda\in W_{1}^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{1}^{2,2}(\Omega)
the solution of the following problem:

\Delta\,\lambda=0\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\lambda=1\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma.

It follows from Lemma
3.11
of
[
20
]
that

\Big\langle\dfrac{\partial\lambda}{\partial\textbf{{n}}},1\Big\rangle_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma}\dfrac{\partial\lambda}{\partial\textbf{{n}}}d\sigma=C_{1}>0.

Now, setting

\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}=\textbf{{z}}-\dfrac{1}{C_{1}}\left\langle\textbf{{z}}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\right\rangle_{\Gamma}\nabla\,\lambda.

It is clear that
\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}\in\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)
,
\mathrm{div}\,\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}=0
in
\Omega
and that
\left\langle\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=0
. Due to Theorem
3.3
, there exists a potential vector
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}=\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi},\quad\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}=0\quad\mathrm{in}\,\Omega\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0\quad\mathrm{on}\,\Gamma.

(3.14)

In addition, we have the estimate

||\boldsymbol{\psi}||_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C||\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}||_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C||\textbf{{z}}||_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}.

(3.15)

Then, we obtain that
\boldsymbol{\psi}
belongs to
\textbf{{V}}^{\,p}_{0,T}(\Omega)
. Since
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is
W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{loc}
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
, then
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
has an
W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{loc}
extension in
\Omega^{\prime}
denoted by
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
. Applying Green’s formula in
\Omega^{\prime}
, we obtain

0=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{curl}\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}})\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}=\left\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=\left\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\right\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Using the fact that
\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}
in
H_{0}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
and
\lambda
in
W_{1}^{2,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and applying (
2.11
), we obtain

0=\left\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{n}},1\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=\left\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{n}},\lambda\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\,\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}.

(3.16)

Using (
3.13
) and (
3.16
), we deduce that

\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{z}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}.

(3.17)

From (
3.11
), (
3.15
) and (
3.17
), we deduce that

\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}}\leq C\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\widetilde{\textbf{{z}}}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}}=C\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{\textbf{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}}.

Applying again (
3.5
), we obtain

\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\textbf{{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\textbf{{g}}\|_{W^{p}(\Omega)}}\leq C\dfrac{\big|\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}\big|}{\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{W_{0}^{\,1,p}(\Omega)}},

and the Inf-Sup Condition (
3.10
) follows immediately from (
3.12
).
∎

4
Existence of weak solution for
p>3

We prove in this sequel the existence and the uniqueness of weak solutions for the following Problem:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{T})\begin{cases}-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}+\nabla\,{\pi}=\textbf{{f}}\quad and\quad\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}={\chi}\quad\text{ in }\,\Omega,\\
\quad\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=g\,\,\,\,and\quad\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}}=\textbf{{h}}\quad\text{ on }\,\Gamma.\end{cases}

The following result concerns the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution of the problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
where
\chi=g=0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
p>3
, suppose that
\chi=g=0
. Then, for any
\textbf{{f}}\in[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}
and
\textbf{{h}}\in W^{-1/p,p}(\Gamma)
, the problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
has a unique solution
(\textbf{{u}},{\pi})\in(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\times{{L}}^{p}(\Omega))
. In addition, we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|{\pi}\|_{{{L}}^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C(\|\textbf{{f}}\|_{[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}}+\|\textbf{{h}}\|_{W^{-1/p,p}(\Gamma)}).

(4.1)

Proof.

On the first hand, observe that Problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
is reduced to the following variational problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Find\quad\textbf{{u}}\in\textbf{V}_{0,T}^{p}(\Omega)\quad such\quad that,\\
\quad\displaystyle{\int}_{\Omega}\textbf{curl}\textbf{{u}}.\textbf{curl}\boldsymbol{\varphi}d\textbf{x}=\langle\textbf{{f}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Omega}+\left\langle\textbf{{h}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\Gamma},\forall\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{V}}^{p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)\end{cases}

(4.2)

Let us introduce the space

\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\Big\{\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega),\,\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0\Big\}.

Indeed, every solution of
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
also solves (
4.2
). Conversely, let us take
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
as the test function in (
4.2
), then we get

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}-\textbf{{f}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{\prime}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}(\Omega)}=0.

(4.3)

By De Rham theorem, there exist a function
\pi\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}+\nabla\,\pi=\textbf{{f}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega.

Moreover, by the fact that
u
belongs to the space
\textbf{{V}}^{p}_{0,T}(\Omega)
, we have
\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{u}}=0
in
\Omega
,
\textbf{{u}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0
on
\Gamma
. The remainder boundary condition
\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}}=\textbf{{H}}
on
\Gamma
is implicitly contained in (
4.2
). Observe that since
\nabla\,\pi
is elements of
[\mathring{H}^{p^{\prime}}_{-1}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}
, it is the same for
\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}
. Since,
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathring{H}^{p^{\prime}}_{-1}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
, it is clear then that for any
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathring{H}^{p^{\prime}}_{-1}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\,\pi,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Omega}=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\pi\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,d\textbf{{x}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathring{H}^{p^{\prime}}_{-1}(\mathrm{div};\Omega).

Particularly

\langle\nabla\,\pi,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Omega}=0\quad\text{if}\quad\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in{\textbf{V}}^{p^{\prime}}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega).

Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{V}}^{p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)
, using the Green formula (
2.17
) we have

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta\,\textbf{{u}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}\times\mathring{H}_{-1}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\,.\,\mathbf{curl}\,\varphi\,d\textbf{x}+\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}},\varphi\rangle_{\Gamma}

(4.4)

Therefore, from (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) we deduce that for all
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\textbf{{V}}^{p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle\textbf{{H}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Gamma}

Let now
\boldsymbol{\mu}
any element of the space
W^{1-1/p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
. So, there exists an element
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\mathrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}=0
in
\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}
on
\Gamma
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}},\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\textbf{{H}},\boldsymbol{\mu}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\textbf{{H}},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\tau}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\textbf{{H}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Gamma}

This implies that
\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{u}}\times\textbf{{n}}=\textbf{{H}}
on
\Gamma
.

This implies that problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
and problem (
4.2
) are equivalent. Now, to solve Problem (
4.2
), we use the Inf-Sup condition (
3.10
). We consider the bilinear form
\textbf{a}:

\textbf{{V}}_{0,T}^{\,p}(\Omega)\times\textbf{{V}}_{0,T}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\textbf{a}(\textbf{{u}},\varphi)=\displaystyle{\int}_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,{\textbf{{u}}}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,\,\mathrm{d}\textbf{{x}}.

Let consider the following mapping
\boldsymbol{\ell}:\,\textbf{{V}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{R}
such that
\boldsymbol{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})=\left\langle\textbf{{f}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\textbf{{h}},\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rangle_{\Gamma}
. It is clear that
\boldsymbol{\ell}
belongs to
(\textbf{{V}}^{\,p^{\prime}}_{0,T}(\Omega))^{\prime}
and according to Remark
3.2
, there exists a unique solution
\textbf{{u}}\in\textbf{{V}}^{\,p}_{0,T}(\Omega)
of Problem (
4.2
). Due to Theorem
3.4
, we prove that this solution
u
belongs ro
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. It follows from Remark
3.2

i)
that

\displaystyle\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C(\|\textbf{{f}}\|_{[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}}+\|\textbf{{h}}\|_{W^{-1/p,p}(\Gamma)}).

(4.5)

Since
\nabla\,\pi=\textbf{{f}}+\Delta\,\textbf{{u}}
, then we have the following estimate

\displaystyle{\|\pi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\nabla\,\pi\|_{W^{-1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}}\leqslant C\big(\|\textbf{{f}}\|_{[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)]^{\prime}}+\|\textbf{{u}}\|_{W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\big)

(4.6)

The estimate (
4.1
) follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.6
).
∎

We can also solve the Stokes problem when the divergence operator does not vanish and
g\neq 0
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
p>3
. Let
f
,
\chi
,
g
,
h
such that

\textbf{{f}}\in[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\,\Omega)]^{\prime},\,\chi\in L^{\,p}(\Omega),\,\,g\in W^{\,1-1/p,p}(\Gamma)\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,\,\textbf{{h}}\in W^{\,-1/p,p}(\Gamma).

Then, the Stokes problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
has a unique solution
(\textbf{{u}}\,,\,\pi)\in W_{0}^{\,1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{\,p}(\Omega)
and we have:

\|\textbf{{u}}\,\|_{W_{0}^{\,1,p}(\Omega)}+\|\,\pi\,\|_{L^{\,p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\left(\|\,\textbf{{f}}\,\|_{[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\,\Omega)]^{\prime}}+\|\,\chi\,\|_{L^{\,p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{W^{\,1-1/p,p}(\Gamma)}+\|\textbf{{h}}\|_{W^{\,-1/p,p}(\Gamma)}\right).

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
\chi\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\Gamma)
. We solve the following Neumann problem in
\Omega
:

-\Delta\,\theta=\chi\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega,\quad\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\textbf{{n}}}=g\quad\mathrm{on}\,\,\Gamma.

(4.8)

It follows from Theorem
2.11
, that the Problem (
4.8
) has a solution
\theta
in
W_{0}^{\,2,p}(\Omega)
and we have:

||\theta||_{W_{0}^{\,2,p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\left(||\chi||_{L^{\,p}(\Omega)}+||g||_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\Gamma)}\right).

(4.9)

Setting
\textbf{{z}}=\textbf{{u}}-\nabla\,\theta
, then Problem
(\mathcal{S}_{T})
becomes: Find
(\textbf{{z}},\pi)\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{\,p}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{cases}-\Delta\,\textbf{{z}}+\nabla\,\pi=\textbf{{f}}+\nabla\,\chi\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathrm{div}\,\textbf{{z}}=0&\quad\mathrm{in}\,\Omega,\\
\textbf{{z}}\cdot\textbf{{n}}=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\mathbf{curl}\,\textbf{{z}}\times\textbf{{n}}=\textbf{{h}}&\quad\mathrm{on}\,\mathrm{\Gamma},\\
\end{cases}

(4.10)

Observe that
\textbf{{f}}+\nabla\,\chi
belongs to
[\mathring{H}_{-1}^{\,p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\,\Omega)]^{\prime}
. According to Theorem
4.1
, this problem has a unique solution
(\textbf{{z}}\,,\,\pi)\in W_{0}^{\,1,p}(\Omega)\times L^{\,p}(\Omega)
. Thus
\textbf{{u}}=\textbf{{z}}+\nabla\,\theta
belongs to
W_{0}^{\,1,p}(\Omega)
and estimate (
4.7
) follows from (
4.1
) and (
4.9
).
∎

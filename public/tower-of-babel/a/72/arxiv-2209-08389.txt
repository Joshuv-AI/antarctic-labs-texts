Parameterizing Conjugacy Classes of Unramified Tori via Bruhat-Tits theory
Stephen DeBacker

Abstract
. Suppose k k is a nonarchimedean local field, K K is a maximally unramified extension of k k , and 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} is a connected reductive k k -group. In this paper we provide parameterizations via Bruhat-Tits theory of: the rational conjugacy classes of k k -tori in 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} that split over K K ; the rational and stable conjugacy classes of the K K -split components of the centers of unramified twisted Levi subgroups of 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} ; and the rational conjugacy classes of unramified twisted generalized Levi subgroups of 𝐆 {\mathbf{G}} . We also provide parameterizations of analogous objects for finite groups of Lie type.

This paper takes up the former problem. The latter problem is studied in

A subgroup
{\mathbf{L}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}

{\mathbf{G}}

{\mathbf{L}}
is a

A

[
3

If one tries to generalize
[
3
]
by naïvely replacing the role of “maximal tori in groups over the residue field” with “twisted Levis in groups over the residue field,” it will not work. One reason for this failure is that any reductive group over the residue field is quasi-split, but unramified twisted Levis do not need to be
k
-quasi-split. To make it work, one needs to introduce a bit more data, as we now discuss.

In Theorem
5.5.4
the rational conjugacy classes of unramified tori are parameterized in terms of equivalence classes of elliptic triples
(F,\theta,w)
that arise from Bruhat-Tits theory. When
{\mathbf{G}}
is
k
-split, the triples

may be described as follows. Let
{\mathbf{A}}^{k}
denote a maximal
k
-split torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
. Then

is a facet in the apartment of
{\mathbf{A}}^{k}
in the Bruhat-Tits building of
{\mathbf{G}}
,
\theta
is a subset of a basis for
\Phi({\mathbf{G}},{\mathbf{A}}^{k})
, the set of roots of
{\mathbf{G}}
with respect to
{\mathbf{A}}^{k}
, and

is an element of the Weyl group of the reductive quotient at
F
which preserves the root subsystem in
\Phi({\mathbf{G}},{\mathbf{A}}^{k})
spanned by

(F,\theta,w)
is elliptic provided that
\dim(F)\geq\dim(F^{\prime})
for all triples
(F^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},w^{\prime})
that are equivalent to
(F,\theta,w)
. When
{\mathbf{G}}
is not
k
-split, the parameterization is modified to account for the action of the Galois group of
K
over
k
.

If we restrict our attention to the set of triples that are of the form
(F,\emptyset,w)
, then we
recover the parameterization of maximal unramified tori of
{\mathbf{G}}
in
[
3
]
. It is the addition of the datum

, that allows the parameterization of this paper to work. In analogy with
[
3
]
, the dimension of the Lie algebra of the maximal
k
-split torus that occurs in the centralizer of an unramified torus parameterized by

is equal to the dimension of

.

We now discuss the contents of this paper.
By way of motivation, in Section

In Section

In Section

Let

Lemma 2.2.1

There is a natural bijective correspondence between

Remark 2.2.2

Proof.

We begin by defining a map
\varphi\colon I_{\mathsf{G}}\rightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}
.
Suppose that we have a pair
(\theta,w)\in I_{\mathsf{G}}
. Thanks to
[
3

such that

and the image of

in

is

and

{\mathrm{Fr}}({}^{g}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta})={}^{{\mathrm{Fr}}(g)}{\mathrm{Fr}}({\mathsf{M}}_{\theta})={}^{gn_{g}^{-1}}({\mathsf{M}}_{{\mathrm{Fr}}(\theta)})={}^{g}({}^{n_{g}^{-1}}({\mathsf{M}}_{w\theta}))={}^{g}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta}.

Thus,

is a twisted Levi
{\mathfrak{f}}
-subgroup of
{\mathsf{G}}
.

The only choice in the above construction was

{\mathfrak{f}}
-subgroup that is
{\mathsf{G}}^{\mathrm{Fr}}
-conjugate to
{}^{g}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta}
. Suppose

is chosen so that

has image
w
in
W_{\mathsf{G}}
. Choose

so that

. Since
{\mathrm{Fr}}(hg^{-1})^{-1}hg^{-1}={{}^{g}a}\in{{}^{g}{\mathsf{A}}}
and

such that
{\mathrm{Fr}}(hg^{-1})^{-1}hg^{-1}={\mathrm{Fr}}(t)^{-1}t

This shows that

is rationally conjugate to

We now show that
\varphi
descends to an injective map from

to
\tilde{{\mathcal{L}}}
; we shall call this map

as well. Suppose

and

. Choose

(resp.

) so that the image of

(resp.

) in

is
w
(resp.

). If
\varphi(\theta,w)=\varphi(\theta^{\prime},w^{\prime})
, then there is

so that
{}^{g}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta}={}^{xg^{\prime}}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta^{\prime}}
. Without loss of generality, we may replace

by

. Since
{}^{g}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta}={{}^{g^{\prime}}{\mathsf{M}}_{\theta^{\prime}}}
, we have that both
{}^{g}{\mathsf{A}}

3.
Tori and Levi subgroups

In this section we introduce some notation and recall some facts about tori and Levi subgroups.

3.1.
Facts about Levi
(E,k)
-subgroups

For this subsection suppose that
k
is any field and
E
is a Galois extension of
k
.
A subgroup
{\mathbf{M}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
will be called a
Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup
provided that it is a
k
-subgroup that occurs as a Levi factor for a parabolic
E
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

If
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}
is the maximal
E
-split torus in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
, then
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}
is defined over
k
and
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E})
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbf{M}}
is defined over
k
, the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
is defined over
k
. Thus
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}
, the unique maximal
E
-split sub-torus of the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
, is also defined over
k
.

Since
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
, there is a parabolic
E
-subgroup
{\mathbf{P}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
such that
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi factor for
{\mathbf{P}}
.
Thanks to the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 16.1.1 (ii)]

{\mathbf{P}}
contains an
E
-split torus
{\mathbf{S}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
such that
C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
is a Levi factor of
{\mathbf{P}}
. Since
{\mathbf{M}}
and
C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
are
{\mathbf{P}}(E)
-conjugate
[
20
, Proposition 16.1.1 (ii)]
, we may assume, after conjugating
{\mathbf{S}}
by an element of
{\mathbf{P}}(E)
, that
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
. Since
{\mathbf{S}}
is in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
and is
E
-split, we conclude that
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}
. Thus

{\mathbf{M}}\leq C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E})\leq C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})={\mathbf{M}}
,
and we conclude that
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E})
.
∎

Corollary 3.1.2
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup with center
{\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{M}}
. If
{\mathbf{H}}
is a
k
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
that lies between
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
, then
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{H}})
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E}\leq{\mathbf{H}}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
, we have

{\mathbf{M}}\leq C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{M}})\leq C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{H}})\leq C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}}^{E})={\mathbf{M}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.1.3
.

If
{\mathbf{T}}
is a maximal
k
-torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
, then
C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{T}}^{E})
is the unique Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
that is minimal among Levi
(E,k)
-subgroups that contain
{\mathbf{T}}
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

The condition of maximality on
{\mathbf{T}}
is necessary for uniqueness.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{T}}^{E})
.

We first show that
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
.
Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}
is a torus,
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi subgroup (see
[
8
, Proposition 1.2.2]
). Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}
is the unique maximal
E
-split subtorus in
{\mathbf{T}}
, it is defined over
k
. Hence
{\mathbf{M}}
is defined over
k
.
If
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
denotes the maximal
E
-split torus in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
, then
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
. Moreover, since
{\mathbf{T}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}
, we also have
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}\leq{\mathbf{T}}
, and so by maximality of
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}
we conclude that
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}\leq{\mathbf{T}}^{E}
. Thus
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}={\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
, and so
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{T}}^{E})=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{\mathbf{M}})
.

Let
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
denote a maximal
E
-split torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
that contains
{\mathbf{T}}^{E}
. Choose
\lambda\in\X_{*}^{E}({\mathbf{T}}^{\prime})
that is non-trivial on every root of
{\mathbf{G}}
with respect to
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
; let
{\mathbf{P}}_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding parabolic
E
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
. Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}\leq{\mathbf{M}}
, the subgroup
{\mathbf{M}}{\mathbf{P}}_{\lambda}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
is a parabolic
E
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
for which
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi factor.

We now show that
{\mathbf{M}}
is the unique minimal Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
that contains
{\mathbf{T}}
.
Suppose
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
is another Levi
(E,k)
-subgroup that contains
{\mathbf{T}}
. Let
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}}
denote the maximal
E
-split torus in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
. From Lemma
3.1.1
we have
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}})
. Since
{\mathbf{T}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
, we have
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}}\leq{\mathbf{T}}
and so
{\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}}\leq{\mathbf{T}}^{E}={\mathbf{Z}}^{E}_{{\mathbf{M}}}
; hence
{\mathbf{M}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.2.
On unramified twisted Levi subgroups and unramified tori

We again assume that
k
is complete with respect to a nontrivial discrete valuation and
{\mathfrak{f}}
is perfect. When
E
is the maximal unramified extension
K
, we use the following language.

Definition 3.2.1
.

A subgroup
{\mathbf{L}}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
is called an
unramified twisted Levi subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
provided that
{\mathbf{L}}
is a Levi
(K,k)
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Definition 3.2.2
.

A
k
-torus
{\mathbf{S}}
is called an
unramified torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
provided that
{\mathbf{S}}
is the
K
-split component of the center of an unramified twisted Levi subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Remark 3.2.3
.

Lemma
3.1.1
tells us that
if
{\mathbf{L}}
is an unramified twisted Levi subgroup in
{\mathbf{G}}
and
{\mathbf{S}}
is the
K
-split component of the center of
{\mathbf{L}}
, then
{\mathbf{L}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
.

Remark 3.2.4
.

Since two Levi
(K,k)
-subgroups are
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugate if and only if the
K
-split components of their centers are
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugate, any parameterization of
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes of unramified tori is also a parameterization of
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes of Levi
(K,k)
-subgroups.

Lemma 3.2.5
.

Suppose
{\mathfrak{f}}
is finite.
A torus
{\mathbf{T}}
in
{\mathbf{G}}
is unramified in
{\mathbf{G}}
if and only if there exists
a maximal
k
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
in
{\mathbf{G}}
such that
{\mathbf{T}}
is the maximal
K
-split subtorus of
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathbf{T}}
is the
K
-split component of the center of a Levi
(K,k)
-subgroup
{\mathbf{L}}
.
From Appendix
A

there is a
K
-minisotropic maximal
k
-torus, call it
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
, in
{\mathbf{L}}
. Then
{\mathbf{T}}
is the maximal
K
-split subtorus of
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
.

Suppose
there exists a maximal
k
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
in
{\mathbf{G}}
for which
{\mathbf{T}}
is the maximal
K
-split subtorus of
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
. Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
is defined over
k
, so too is
{\mathbf{T}}
. Let
{\mathbf{L}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{T}})
. From Lemma
3.1.3
, we know
{\mathbf{L}}
is the unique minimal Levi
(K,k)
-subgroup containing
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
. By construction,
{\mathbf{T}}
is contained in
{\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}}
, the center of
{\mathbf{L}}
. Thus
{\mathbf{T}}={\mathbf{T}}^{K}\leq{\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}}^{K}
. Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
contains
{\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}}
and
{\mathbf{T}}
is the maximal
K
-split subtorus of
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
, we conclude that
{\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}}^{K}
is contained in
{\mathbf{T}}
. Hence
{\mathbf{T}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{L}}^{K}
, and so
{\mathbf{T}}
is an unramified torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
.
∎

3.3.
A question about unramified tori

Suppose
{\mathfrak{f}}
is quasi-finite and

{\mathbf{S}}
is a torus that occurs as the center of a Levi
(K,K)
-subgroup. Based on our experience with maximal
K
-split tori of
{\mathbf{G}}
, it is natural to ask:

Question 3.3.1
.

If
{\mathrm{Fr}}({\mathbf{S}})
is
G
-conjugate to
{\mathbf{S}}
, then does there exist
a
g\in G
for which
{}^{g}{\mathbf{S}}
is defined over
k
? That is, does
{}^{G}{\mathbf{S}}
contain an unramified torus?

Unfortunately, the answer is no as the following example illustrates.

Let
{\mathbf{H}}
be a connected reductive group of type
\mathbf{A}_{2}
such that
H^{{\mathrm{Fr}}}\cong{\mathrm{SL}}_{1}(D)
where
D
is an unramified division algebra of index
3
over
k
. Recall that we may identify
H={\mathbf{H}}(K)
with
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}(K)
. Let
{\mathbf{A}}
denote a maximal
K
-split
k
-torus in
{\mathbf{H}}
; it is unique up to
H^{\mathrm{Fr}}
-conjugacy. Suppose
C
is the alcove in
\mathcal{A}(A)\leq{\mathcal{B}}(H)
for which
C^{{\mathrm{Fr}}}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\{\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}
be the simple affine
K
-roots determined by
{\mathbf{H}}
,
{\mathbf{A}}
,
\nu
, and
C
. We assume that the
\psi_{i}
are labeled so that
{\mathrm{Fr}}(\psi_{i})=\psi_{i+1}
mod
3
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}=(\ker\dot{\psi}_{j})^{\circ}
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. Note that
{\mathrm{Fr}}({\mathbf{A}}_{1})={\mathbf{A}}_{2}
. Since there exists
n\in N_{H}({\mathbf{A}})
for which
n\dot{\psi}_{2}=\dot{\psi}_{1}
, we conclude that
{\mathrm{Fr}}({\mathbf{A}}_{1})
is
H
-conjugate to
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
. Suppose some conjugate, call it
{\mathbf{S}}
, of
{\mathbf{A}}_{1}
is defined over
k
. Let
{\mathbf{T}}
be a maximal
k
-torus in the Levi
k
-subgroup
C_{\mathbf{H}}({\mathbf{S}})
. Then
{\mathbf{T}}
corresponds to an extension
E\leq D
of degree
3
over
k
, and
{\mathbf{S}}
corresponds to a quadratic extension of
k
that lies in
E
. Since no such quadratic extension exists, we conclude that the answer to Question
3.3.1
is no.

of

If

-subgroup of

Recall that
{\mathbf{Z}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{\mathbf{G}}
denotes the center of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Definition 4.0.1
.

A torus in
{\mathbf{G}}
will be called a
(K,k)
-torus in

-split

-torus that contains

A

-split maximal

-torus

is a maximal
(K,k)
-torus

in

for all maximal

-tori

.

To ease the notation, if

for a

-torus

in

, then we will call

a

-torus as well.

We let
\mathcal{T}_{K}
denote the set of
(K,K)
-tori in
{\mathbf{G}}
. The set
\mathcal{T}_{K}
carries a natural action of

, and we denote the set of

-fixed points in

by

.

The goal of this section is to describe the

-conjugacy classes in

4.1.
Some indexing sets

To understand the elements of
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
, we introduce indexing sets that arise naturally from Bruhat-Tits theory.
For a facet
F
in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)
, we let
{\mathsf{Z}}_{F}
denote the group
corresponding to the image of

in

.
Consider the indexing set

J:=\{(F,{\mathsf{S}})\,|\,\text{$F$ is a facet in ${\mathcal{B}}(G)$ and ${\mathsf{S}}$ is
a torus in ${\mathsf{G}}_{F}$ that contains ${\mathsf{Z}}_{F}$}\}.

The following definition provides a link between
\mathcal{T}_{K}
and

.

Definition 4.1.1
.

A
K
-split torus
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}
is said to be a

of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J
provided that

(1)

F\subset{\mathcal{B}}(C_{G}(S))

(2)

the image of

in

is
{\mathsf{S}}
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

It follows from
[

, 4.4.2]
that if

and

, then a point

is
(S\cap G_{y})
-fixed if and only if

.

Suppose
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J
. Note that if

, then
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
is defined over
{\mathfrak{f}}
. In this situation, it makes sense to consider
\Gamma({\mathsf{S}})
.
We define

J^{\Gamma}:=\{(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J\,|\,\text{$\Gamma(F)=F$ \text{and} $\Gamma({\mathsf{S}})={\mathsf{S}}$}\}.

Definition 4.1.3
.

A pair
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J^{\Gamma}
will be called

provided that whenever a facet

is both

-stable and contains

in its closure, then

belongs to the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-parabolic subgroup

of

if and only if

.
We let

denote the subset of maximal pairs in

.

Example 4.1.4
.

Suppose

. If

is

-split, then

is a

-alcove in

.

More generally,
suppose

. As in Section

one can attach to

-conjugacy class

of Levi
(k,k)
-subgroups. From Example

, one expects that if
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
is a lift of

, then
({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})
is the minimal conjugacy class of Levi
(k,k)
-subgroups for which
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}
for some
{\mathbf{M}}\in({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})
. This is true and will be proved in Section
4.5
.

4.2.
Passing between tori over
{\mathfrak{f}}
and tori over
k

Suppose
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J^{\Gamma}
. Our next two lemmas show that there is an element of
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
that lifts
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
, and any two lifts of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
are conjugate by an element of
G_{F,0^{+}}^{\Gamma}
.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Set
{\mathsf{M}}=C_{{\mathsf{G}}_{F}}({\mathsf{S}})
and let
{\mathsf{T}}
denote
a maximally
{\mathfrak{f}}
-split
maximal
{\mathfrak{f}}
-torus in
{\mathsf{M}}
. There is a torus
{\mathbf{T}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
that lifts
(F,{\mathsf{T}})
. Moreover, for all
{\mathbf{T}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
lifting
(F,{\mathsf{T}})
there exists a unique lift
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
with the property that
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{T}}
.

Remark 4.2.2
.

If
{\mathsf{M}}
is
{\mathfrak{f}}
-quasi-split,
then
{\mathsf{T}}
is the centralizer of a maximal
{\mathfrak{f}}
-split torus in
{\mathsf{M}}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathbf{T}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{T}})
. (Such a torus
{\mathbf{T}}
exists by
[
3
, Lemma 2.3.1]
.)
Note that
\X_{*}({\mathsf{T}})=\X_{*}({\mathbf{T}})
as
\Gamma
-modules, and we can therefore choose a subtorus
{\mathbf{S}}
of
{\mathbf{T}}
corresponding to the image of
\X_{*}({\mathsf{S}})
under
\X_{*}({\mathsf{S}})\hookrightarrow\X_{*}({\mathsf{T}})=\X_{*}({\mathbf{T}})
. We have that
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
. Since
T\leq C_{G}(S)
and
F\subset{\mathcal{B}}(T)\subset{\mathcal{B}}(C_{G}(S))
, we conclude that
S
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
.

If
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
is another lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
that lies in
{\mathbf{T}}
, then
\X_{*}({{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}})=\X_{*}({\mathbf{S}})=\X_{*}({\mathsf{S}})
in
\X_{*}({\mathbf{T}})
, and so
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{S}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.2.3
.

If
{\mathbf{S}},{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
both lift
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
, then there exists an element
g\in G_{F,0^{+}}^{\Gamma}
such that
{}^{g}{\mathbf{S}}={\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We will use the notation of Lemma
4.2.1
and its proof.

Set
{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}}^{\prime})
. Note that
F\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M^{\prime})
and the image of
M^{\prime}\cap G_{F}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
is
{\mathsf{M}}=C_{{\mathsf{G}}_{F}}({\mathsf{S}})
. Let
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}\leq{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
be a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{T}})
.
Since
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}
is in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
, we have
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}\leq{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
. Since
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}
(resp.
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
) is a
K
-split torus lifting
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
(resp.
(F,{\mathsf{T}})
), from Lemma
4.2.1
we conclude that
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}
is the unique lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
in
{\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
.
By
[
3
, Lemma 2.2.2]
, there is an element
g\in G_{F,0^{+}}^{\Gamma}
such that
{}^{g}{\mathbf{T}}={\mathbf{T}}^{\prime}
. The result follows from Lemma
4.2.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2.4
.

Suppose
{\mathsf{C}}
is the maximal
{\mathfrak{f}}
-split component of the center of
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
. If
{\mathbf{C}}
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{C}}{\mathsf{Z}}_{F})
, then
C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{C}})
is
G^{\Gamma}_{F,0^{+}}
-conjugate to
{}_{F}{\mathbf{M}}
.

Thanks to Lemma
4.2.1
and Corollary
4.2.3
we can define an action of
G^{\Gamma}
on
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
. Suppose
g\in G^{\Gamma}
and
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
. Let
{\mathbf{S}}
be a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
. Let
{}^{g}{\mathsf{S}}
denote the image of
{}^{g}S\cap G_{gF}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{gF}
and set
g(F,{\mathsf{S}}):=(gF,{}^{g}{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
.

The following lemma allows us to move in the opposite direction: from tori over
k
to tori over
{\mathfrak{f}}
.

Lemma 4.2.5
.

For all
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
there exists
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
such that
{\mathbf{S}}
lifts
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
.

Proof.

Fix
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
. Let
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
. Note that
{\mathbf{M}}
is a Levi
(K,k)
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Choose a
\Gamma
-stable
M
-facet
H
in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
so that
H^{\Gamma}
is an
M^{\Gamma}
-alcove; the choice of
H
is unique up to
M^{\Gamma}
-conjugation. Since
H
can be written as the disjoint union of
G
-facets in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)
, we may choose a
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facet
F
in
H
so that
\dim(F^{\Gamma})\geq\dim(\tilde{F}^{\Gamma})
for all
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facets
\tilde{F}
in
\bar{H}
.
In fact,
\dim(F^{\Gamma})\geq\dim(\tilde{F}^{\Gamma})
for all
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facets
\tilde{F}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
: if
\tilde{F}
is a
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
, then, since
M^{\Gamma}
-alcoves are
M^{\Gamma}
-conjugate, there is some
m\in M^{\Gamma}
that carries
\tilde{F}
into
\overline{H}
; thus
\dim(\tilde{F}^{\Gamma})=\dim((m\tilde{F})^{\Gamma})\leq\dim(F^{\Gamma})
.

Let
{\mathsf{S}}
be the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-torus in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
corresponding to the image of
S\cap G_{F}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
. By construction, the torus
{\mathbf{S}}
is a lift of the pair
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
. We need to show that
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
. Suppose
F^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(G)
is a
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facet with
F\subset\overline{F}^{\prime}
and
F\neq F^{\prime}
. Note that
S\cap G_{F^{\prime}}\subset S\cap G_{F}
. If
{\mathsf{S}}
belongs to the proper parabolic
{\mathfrak{f}}
-subgroup
G_{F^{\prime}}/G_{F,0^{+}}
of
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}=G_{F}/G_{F,0^{+}}
, then
S\cap G_{F}=S\cap G_{F^{\prime}}
fixes
F^{\prime}
and
(F^{\prime},{\mathsf{S}}^{\prime})\in J^{\Gamma}
where
{\mathsf{S}}^{\prime}
is the
{\mathfrak{f}}
-torus in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F^{\prime}}
corresponding to the image of
S\cap G_{F^{\prime}}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F^{\prime}}
. From Remark
4.1.2
we conclude that
F^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)
. But the dimension of
F^{\prime\Gamma}
is strictly larger than that of
F^{\Gamma}
, contradicting that
\dim(F^{\Gamma})\geq\dim(\tilde{F}^{\Gamma})
for all
\Gamma
-stable
G
-facets
\tilde{F}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
.
∎

4.3.
An equivalence relation

Thanks to Lemma
4.2.5
and Corollary
4.2.3
, we have a surjective map
\varphi
from
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
to the set of
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
. We introduce an equivalence relation, to be denoted
\sim
, on
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
so that
\varphi
descends to a bijection between
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}/\!\sim
and the set of
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is an apartment in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{\Gamma}
. This means there is a maximal
k
-split torus
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{G}}
such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{S}},k)
. Note that every such apartment is equal to
\mathcal{A}({}^{g}A)^{\Gamma}
for some
g\in G^{\Gamma}
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathcal{A}
, then we denote the smallest affine subspace of
\mathcal{A}
that contains
\Omega
by
A(\mathcal{A},\Omega)
. When
F_{1},F_{2}
are two
G^{\Gamma}
-facets in
\mathcal{A}
for which
\emptyset\neq A(\mathcal{A},F_{1})=A(\mathcal{A},F_{2})
, then, since an affine root vanishes on
F_{1}
if and only if it vanishes on
F_{2}
, there is a natural identification of
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{1}}
with
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{2}}
as algebraic
{\mathfrak{f}}
-groups. When this happens, we write

{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{1}}\overset{i}{=}{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{2}}
.

Definition 4.3.1
.

Suppose
(F_{i},{\mathsf{S}}_{i})\in J^{\Gamma}
. We write
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})\sim(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})
provided that there exist an element
g\in G^{\Gamma}
and an apartment
\mathcal{A}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{\Gamma}
such that

(1)

\emptyset\neq A(\mathcal{A},F_{1}^{\Gamma})=A(\mathcal{A},gF_{2}^{\Gamma})

(2)

{\mathsf{S}}_{1}\overset{i}{=}{{}^{g}{\mathsf{S}}_{2}}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{1}}\overset{i}{=}{\mathsf{G}}_{gF_{2}}
.

Lemma 4.3.2
.

The relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

The proof is nearly identical to the material in
[
4
, Section 3.6]
or
[
3
, Section 3.2]
.
∎

4.4.
A bijective correspondence

Suppose
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
. Let
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
. Recall from Definition
4.1.1
that since
{\mathbf{S}}
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
, we have
F\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)
.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

Let
C
denote a
\Gamma
-stable
M
-facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
that contains
F
in its closure. The
M^{\Gamma}
-facet
C^{\Gamma}
is an alcove in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
and
F^{\Gamma}
is an open subset of
C^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

It will be enough to show that
F^{\Gamma}
is a maximal
G^{\Gamma}
-facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
. Choose a
G^{\Gamma}
-facet
D\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
such that
F^{\Gamma}\subset\overline{D}
. If
F^{\Gamma}\neq D
, then as
{\mathbf{S}}
is in the center of
{\mathbf{M}}
, the image of
S\cap G_{F}=S\cap G_{D}
in
G_{F}/G_{F,0^{+}}
belongs to the parabolic
{\mathfrak{f}}
-subgroup
G_{D}/G_{F,0^{+}}
. This contradicts that
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4.2
.

Suppose
(F_{i},{\mathsf{S}}_{i})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with lift
{\mathbf{S}}_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
. If there exists
g\in G^{\Gamma}
such that
{}^{g}{\mathbf{S}}_{1}={\mathbf{S}}_{2}
, then
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})\sim(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})
.

Proof.

After replacing
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})
with
(gF_{1},{}^{g}{\mathsf{S}}_{1})
we may and do assume that
{\mathbf{S}}_{1}={\mathbf{S}}_{2}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
{\mathbf{S}}={\mathbf{S}}_{1}={\mathbf{S}}_{2}
, and set
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
.
Since
{\mathbf{S}}
is a lift of
(F_{i},{\mathsf{S}}_{i})
, from Definition
4.1.1
we have
F_{i}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)
. Let
C_{i}
denote the
M
-facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
to which
F_{i}
belongs. By Lemma
4.4.1
,
C_{i}^{\Gamma}
is an
M^{\Gamma}
-alcove in
{\mathcal{B}}(M^{\Gamma})
. Since
M^{\Gamma}
acts transitively on the alcoves in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
, there exists an
m\in M^{\Gamma}
such that
mC_{1}=C_{2}
. We may and do replace
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})
by
(mF_{1},{}^{m}{\mathsf{S}}_{1})
. Since
F_{1}^{\Gamma}
and
F_{2}^{\Gamma}
are open in
C_{1}^{\Gamma}=C_{2}^{\Gamma}
, for any apartment
\mathcal{A}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{\Gamma}
we have
\emptyset\neq A(\mathcal{A},F_{1}^{\Gamma})=A(\mathcal{A},F_{2}^{\Gamma})
. Since
{}^{m}{\mathbf{S}}={\mathbf{S}}
, we conclude that
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})\sim(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})
.
∎

Corollary 4.4.3
.

There exists a bijection between
J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}/\sim
and the set of
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes in
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

The only thing remaining to check is that if
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1}),(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1})\sim(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})
, then they have lifts that are
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugate. Suppose
(F_{1},{\mathsf{S}}_{1}),(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with
(F,{\mathsf{S}}_{1})\sim(F_{2},{\mathsf{S}}_{2})
. Then there exist an element
g\in G^{\Gamma}
and an apartment
\mathcal{A}
in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{\Gamma}
such that

(1)

\emptyset\neq A(\mathcal{A},F_{1}^{\Gamma})=A(\mathcal{A},gF_{2}^{\Gamma})

(2)

{\mathsf{S}}_{1}\overset{i}{=}{{}^{g}{\mathsf{S}}_{2}}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{1}}\overset{i}{=}{\mathsf{G}}_{gF_{2}}

We may and do assume that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(A)^{\Gamma}
and that
g
is the identity.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}_{i}={}_{F_{i}}{\mathbf{M}}
.
Since
A(\mathcal{A},F_{1}^{\Gamma})=A(\mathcal{A},F_{2}^{\Gamma})
, we have
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}={\mathbf{M}}_{2}
. Set
{\mathbf{M}}={\mathbf{M}}_{1}
. By construction, the image of
M\cap G_{F_{i}}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{i}}
is
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{i}}
itself (that is,
{\mathsf{M}}_{F_{i}}={\mathsf{G}}_{F_{i}}
).

Since
{\mathsf{S}}_{1}\overset{i}{=}{\mathsf{S}}_{2}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{1}}\overset{i}{=}{\mathsf{G}}_{F_{2}}
, we can find
{\mathbf{T}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
so that the image of
T\cap{}_{F_{1}}M\cap{}_{F_{2}}M
in
{\mathsf{M}}_{F_{i}}={\mathsf{G}}_{F_{i}}
is a
maximally
{\mathfrak{f}}
-split
{\mathfrak{f}}
-torus containing
{\mathsf{S}}_{i}
. As in Lemma
4.2.1
there is exactly one lift
{\mathbf{S}}
of
(F_{i},{\mathsf{S}}_{i})
in
{\mathbf{T}}
. Since the image of
S\cap{}_{F_{1}}M\cap{}_{F_{2}}M
in
{\mathsf{M}}_{F_{i}}={\mathsf{G}}_{F_{i}}
is
{\mathsf{S}}_{i}
, the proof is complete.
∎

4.5.
(K,k)
-tori and Levi
(k,k)
-subgroups

If
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
is a Levi
(k,k)
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
, then we let
({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime})
denote the
G^{\Gamma}
-conjugacy class of
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
. Set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime})\colon\text{${\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}$ is a Levi $(k,k)$-subgroup of ${\mathbf{G}}$}\}
. If
({\mathbf{M}}_{1}),({\mathbf{M}}_{2})\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}

({\mathbf{M}}_{1})\leq({\mathbf{M}}_{2})
provided that there exists
{\mathbf{L}}_{i}\in({\mathbf{M}}_{i})
such that
{\mathbf{L}}_{1}\leq{\mathbf{L}}_{2}
; this defines a partial order on
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
.

Lemma 4.5.1
.

Fix
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with
F\subset\mathcal{A}(A)
, and let
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
be a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
. There exists
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}\in({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})
such that
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
.
Choose a maximally
k
-split maximal
(K,k)
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}
in
{\mathbf{M}}
. Note that
{\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}:=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{T}}^{k})
.
From Lemma
4.4.1
,
F^{\Gamma}
is a subset of an
M^{\Gamma}
-alcove; thus, we can replace
{\mathbf{T}}
with an
M^{\Gamma}
-conjugate and assume
F^{\Gamma}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(T)^{\Gamma}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
.

From Lemma
4.4.1
,
F^{\Gamma}
is a maximal
G^{\Gamma}
-facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(M)
. Thus, if
{\mathsf{C}}
denotes the image of
{\mathbf{T}}^{k}(K)\cap G_{F}
in
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
, then
{\mathsf{C}}
is the maximal
{\mathfrak{f}}
-split component in the center of
{\mathsf{G}}_{F}
. Since
{\mathbf{T}}^{k}{\mathbf{Z}}^{K}
is a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{C}}{\mathsf{Z}}_{F})
, from Remark
4.2.4
we have
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{T}}^{k})
is
G_{F,0^{+}}^{\Gamma}
-conjugate to
{}_{F}{\mathbf{M}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5.2
.

Fix
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with
F\subset\mathcal{A}(A)
, and let
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
be a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
. If
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}
is a Levi
(k,k)
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
that contains
{\mathbf{S}}
, then
({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})\leq({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}
is a Levi
(k,k)
-subgroup that contains
{\mathbf{S}}
. Recall that
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}}^{k}
denotes the
k
-split component of the center of
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}}^{k}
commutes with
{\mathbf{S}}
, we have
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}}^{k}\leq{\mathbf{M}}
. Choose a maximally
k
-split maximal
(K,k)
-torus
{\mathbf{T}}
in
{\mathbf{M}}
that contains
{\mathbf{Z}}_{{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}}^{k}
.

From Lemma
4.4.1
,
F^{\Gamma}
is a subset of an
M^{\Gamma}
-alcove; thus, we can replace
{\mathbf{T}}
and
{\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime}
with
M^{\Gamma}
-conjugates and assume
F^{\Gamma}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(T)^{\Gamma}\subset{\mathcal{B}}(M)^{\Gamma}
. Since, from Lemma
4.4.1
,

F^{\Gamma}
is a maximal
G^{\Gamma}
-facet in

, we have, as in the proof of Lemma
4.5.1
,
({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})=(C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{T}}^{k}))
.

Since

({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})\leq({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 4.5.3
.

Fix
(F,{\mathsf{S}})\in J_{\mathrm{max}}^{\Gamma}
with
F\subset\mathcal{A}(A)
, and let
{\mathbf{S}}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{\Gamma}
be a lift of
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
. We have

({}_{F}{\mathbf{M}})=\min\{({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime})\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\colon\text{ there exists ${\mathbf{L}}^{\prime}\in({\mathbf{M}}^{\prime})$ such that ${\mathbf{S}}\leq{\mathbf{L}}^{\prime}$}\}.\qed

5.
A parameterization of unramified tori

We again assume that
{\mathfrak{f}}
, the residue field of
k
, is quasi-finite.

We want to understand unramified tori, that is, those
k
-tori
{\mathbf{S}}
in
{\mathbf{G}}
for which
{\mathbf{S}}
is the
K
-split component of the center of
C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
.

Remark 5.0.1
.

Tori such as
\{\diag(t,t^{2},t^{-3})\}
in
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}
are
(K,k)
-tori but are not unramified tori in our sense.

We begin by recalling a known result (see, for example,
[
7
, Lemma 2.3.1]
).

Lemma 5.0.2
.

If
F
is a facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{\mathrm{Fr}}
, then
\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},G_{F})=1
.

Proof.

Note that
\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},G_{F,0^{+}})=1
and from Lemma
2.0.1
we have
\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},{\mathsf{G}}_{F})=1
.
Since

1\longrightarrow G_{F,0^{+}}\longrightarrow G_{F}\longrightarrow{\mathsf{G}}_{F}\longrightarrow 1

is exact, from
[
17
, I Section 5.5: Proposition 38]
we have

\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},G_{F,0^{+}})\longrightarrow\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},G_{F})\longrightarrow\cohom^{1}({\mathrm{Fr}},{\mathsf{G}}_{F})

is exact. The result follows.
∎

5.1.
Comparison with the case of maximal unramified tori

We already know (see, for example,
[
3
]
) how to describe the set of
G^{{\mathrm{Fr}}}
-conjugacy classes of maximal unramified tori of
{\mathbf{G}}
(the situation where
C_{{\mathbf{G}}}({\mathbf{S}})
is abelian) in terms of Bruhat-Tits theory. In this case, there is a bijective correspondence between the set of
G^{{\mathrm{Fr}}}
-conjugacy classes of maximal unramified tori in
{\mathbf{G}}
and the set of equivalence classes (as in Definition
4.3.1
)
of pairs
(F,{\mathsf{S}})
where
F
is a facet in
{\mathcal{B}}(G)^{{\mathrm{Fr}}}
and
{\mathsf{S}}
is a maximal
{\mathfrak{f}}
-minisotropic torus in

The natural inclusion

and

Consider the map

in

7.2.
A result about parahoric subgroups and unramified twisted generalized Levi subgroups

Suppose
{\mathbf{H}}
is an unramified twisted generalized Levi subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
, that is,
{\mathbf{H}}
is a
connected reductive
k
-subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
that contains the centralizer of a maximal unramified torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
. Since
{\mathbf{H}}
contains a maximal unramified torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
, the building of
{\mathcal{B}}(H)
embeds into the building of
{\mathcal{B}}(G)
. There is not a canonical embedding, but all such embeddings have the same image.

Lemma 7.2.1
.

If
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(H)
, then
H_{x}\leq G_{x}\cap H
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{S}}
be a maximal
K
-split torus of
{\mathbf{H}}
such that
x\in\mathcal{A}(S)\subset{\mathcal{B}}(H)
. Since the maximal
K
-split tori in
{\mathbf{H}}
form a single
H
-conjugacy class and since
{\mathbf{H}}
contains the centralizer of a maximal unramified torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
, we conclude that
{\mathbf{S}}^{\prime}=C_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}})
is a subgroup of
{\mathbf{H}}
.

Note that
S^{\prime}_{0}
, the parahoric subgroup of
S^{\prime}
, is equal to
S_{0}S^{\prime}_{0^{+}}
where
S_{0}
is the parahoric subgroup of
S
and
S^{\prime}_{0^{+}}
is the pro-unipotent radical of
S^{\prime}_{0}
.

The parahoric subgroup
H_{x}
is generated by
S^{\prime}_{0}
and the groups
U_{\psi}
for
\psi\in\Psi({\mathbf{H}},{\mathbf{S}},\nu)
with
\psi(x)\geq 0
. Suppose
\psi\in\Psi({\mathbf{H}},{\mathbf{S}},\nu)
and
\psi(x)\geq 0
. If
u\in U_{\psi}
, then
u
is unipotent in
G
and fixes
x
, hence

. Since
G_{x}
contains
S^{\prime}_{0}
, we conclude that
H_{x}\leq G_{x}\cap H
.
∎

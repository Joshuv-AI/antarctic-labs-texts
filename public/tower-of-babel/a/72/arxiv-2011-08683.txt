Fisher Information of a Family of Generalized Normal Distributions
Precious Ugo Abara, Sandra Hirche

Abstract
In this brief note we compute the Fisher information of a family of generalized normal distributions. Fisher information is usually defined for regular distributions, i.e. continuously differentiable (log) density functions whose support does not depend on the family parameter θ \theta . Although the uniform distribution in [ − θ , + θ ] [-\theta,+\theta] does not satisfy the regularity requirements, as a special case of our result, we will obtain the Fisher information for this family.



I
Introduction

A natural question is that arises in the context of parameter estimation is: how much information can a sample of data provide about the unknown parameter? This is a widely studied problem in literature
[
1
,
2
]
. One information metric is the Fisher information
[
3
]
. Given a family of density functions
f(x,\theta)
function of a parameter
\theta
, it always hold

\int f(x,\theta){\rm d}x=1.

(1)

Under certain regularity conditions, if
\theta
is the true parameter, it can be shown that of (
1
) translates into

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)\Big|\theta\right]=0.

The partial derivate
{\partial}/{\partial\theta}
of the natural logarithm of the likelihood
f(x,\theta)
is referred to as
score
. The Fischer information
\mathcal{I}(\theta)
is defined as the variance of the score i.e.

\mathcal{I}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)\right)^{2}\Big|\theta\right].

If the second derivative with respect to
\theta
of
\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)
exists for all
x
and
\theta
and the second derivative with respect to
\theta
of the left side of (
1
) can be obtained by differentiating twice under the integral sign then the Fisher information may also be written as
[
3
]

\mathcal{I}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)\Big|\theta\right].

(2)

We note that the condition of interchange of the derivative and integration excludes the uniform distribution
f(x,\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta}
,
\theta\in[a,b]
. In order words, the uniform distribution does not meet the regularity conditions.

II
Preliminaries

In this section we give results for a random variable
X
with a generalized normal distribution with zero mean i.e.

f(x,\theta)=\frac{\beta}{2\theta\Gamma(1/\beta)}\operatorname{exp}\left(-\frac{|x|^{\beta}}{\theta^{\beta}}\right)

(3)

where
\Gamma
denotes the gamma function,
\theta>0
is called the scale,
\beta>0
is the shape. For a complex variable
z
,
\Gamma
is defined as

\Gamma(z)=\begin{cases}(z-1)!,&\text{if }z\text{ is positive integer},\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}\operatorname{exp}(-x){\rm d}x,&\text{if }\mathbb{R}(z)>0.\end{cases}

Equation (
3
) is a parametric family of symmetric distributions. It includes the zero-mean Laplace distribution when
\beta=1
, the zero-mean normal distribution when
\beta=2
. Furthermore, it converges point-wise to the uniform density for
\beta\to\infty
. See Fig.
1
.

\theta=1
and different shape parameter
\beta
.

Lemma 1
(Moments
[
4
]
)

Let
X
be zero mean generalized Gaussian distribution of shape
\beta
and scaling parameter
\theta
as in (
3
). The moments of
X
exist and are finite for any
k
greater than
−1
. For any non-negative integer
k
, the plain central moments are

\mathbb{E}\left[X^{k}\right]=\begin{cases}\theta^{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{\beta}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)},&\text{if }k\text{ is even},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2
(
\Gamma
at some rational arguments)

Given integers
n>0
and
p>0
it holds that

\Gamma\left(n+\frac{1}{p}\right)=\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{(pn-(p-1))!^{(p)}}{p^{n}}

(4)

where
n!^{(p)}
denotes the
p
-th multi-factorial of
n
.

Proof:

Using the functional equation
\Gamma(\alpha+1)=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)
for
\alpha>0
, and by its successive application, it is easy to obtain

\Gamma\left(n+\frac{1}{p}\right)=\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\frac{(pn-p+1)\left(p(n-1)-p+1\right)\cdots\left(p(n-n+1)-p+1\right))}{p^{n}}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

III
Result

Theorem 1

Suppose
X
is a random variable with a zero-mean generalized normal distribution given by (
3
), and an even shape
\beta
, i.e.
\beta=2n
, with
n
a positive integer. Then

\mathcal{I}(\theta)=\frac{\beta}{\theta^{2}}.

Proof:

Since (
3
) is twice differentiable we can use (
2
) to compute the Fisher information. The log-likelihood is given by

\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)=\operatorname{log}\frac{\beta}{2}-\operatorname{log}\theta-\operatorname{log}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)-\frac{|x|^{\beta}}{\theta^{\beta}}.

Its first partial derivative with respect to the parameter
\theta
is

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)=-\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\beta\theta^{\beta-1}}{\theta^{2\beta}}|x|^{\beta}=-\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{\beta|x|^{\beta}}{\theta^{\beta+1}}.

The second derivative then follows as

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}-\frac{\beta(\beta+1)\theta^{\beta}}{\theta^{2\beta+2}}|x|^{\beta}=\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}-\frac{\beta(\beta+1)|x|^{\beta}}{\theta^{\beta+2}}.

(5)

Using (
2
) and (
5
) it then follows

\mathcal{I}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\operatorname{log}f(x,\theta)\Big|\theta\right]=-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}+\frac{\beta(\beta+1)\mathbb{E}\left[|x|^{\beta}|\theta\right]}{\theta^{\beta+2}}.

(6)

Since
\beta
is assumed to be a positive even integer it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[|x|^{\beta}|\theta\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[x^{\beta}|\theta\right]=\theta^{\beta}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}\overset{\eqref{eq: gamma rational argument}}{=}\frac{\theta^{\beta}}{\beta}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)}=\frac{\theta^{\beta}}{\beta}.

(7)

where we used (
4
) with
p=\beta
,
n=1
and noticing that
1!^{(\beta)}=1
. Substituting (
7
) in (
6
)

\mathcal{I}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}+\frac{\beta+1}{\theta^{2}}=\frac{\beta}{\theta^{2}}.

∎

Example 1

A zero-mean Gaussian distribution is obtained from (
3
) with
\beta=2
. It is easy to see that a family of Gaussian distributions with unknown standard deviation has Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(\theta)=\frac{2}{\theta^{2}}
.

Remark 1

When
\beta\to\infty
the distribution (
3
) converges to a uniform density on
(-\theta,+\theta)
. The Fisher information is thus
\mathcal{I}(\theta)=\infty
, for
\beta\to\infty
.

Moduli of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -bundles under nonconnected group schemes and nondensity of essentially finite bundles
Ludvig Olsson, Stefan Reppen, Tuomas Tajakka

Abstract
. We prove the existence of a projective good moduli space of principal 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -bundles under nonconnected reductive group schemes 𝒢 \mathcal{G} over a smooth projective curve C C . We also prove that the moduli stack of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -bundles decomposes into finitely many substacks Bun 𝒫 \bun_{\mathcal{P}} each admitting a torsor Bun 𝒢 𝒫 → Bun 𝒫 \bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}}\to\bun_{\mathcal{P}} under a finite group, for some connected reductive group schemes 𝒢 𝒫 \mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}} over C C . We use this for the second purpose of the article: for any constant connected reductive group G G , the subset of essentially finite G G -bundles in the moduli of degree 0 semistable G G -bundles over C C is not dense, unless G G is a torus or the genus of C C is smaller than 2. We do this by giving an upper bound on the dimension of the closure of the subset of essentially finite G G -bundles.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field
k
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a reductive group scheme over
C
. In most studies on
\mathcal{G}
-bundles over
C
the group is usually assumed to be connected. There are both group-theoretic and geometric complications that occur in the nonconnected case. For example, Zariski-local triviality (see
[
DS95
]
) of
\mathcal{G}
–bundles does not hold for nonconnected groups. Nonetheless, nonconnected groups occur naturally both in their own right, such as the orthogonal group corresponding to a vector bundle with a quadratic form, and in the study of connected groups, for instance as normalizers of tori. Thus, one of the purposes of this text is to study the moduli stack
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}
of principal bundles under nonconnected group schemes over
C
. In particular, we show that
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}
decomposes into finitely many substacks
\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
, each of which admits a finite torsor
\bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}}\to\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
is a connected reductive group over
C
. When
\Char(k)=0
, we use this to prove that the substack of semistable
\mathcal{G}
-bundles admits a projective good moduli space (see
Section

1.1
for some background on this topic).

We also use this decomposition for the second purpose of the article, which is to settle a density question (as discussed in
[
DM10
]
,
[
GR23
]
,
[
Wei22
]
) regarding essentially finite bundles under connected reductive groups over
k
, in the characteristic 0 case (see
Theorem

1.5
).

1.1.
Moduli of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles

The construction of moduli of
G
-bundles over
C
has its roots in the work of Ramanathan who
constructed a projective good moduli space of semistable
G
-bundles for any connected reductive group
G
over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
Ram96
,
Ram96a
]
). After decades of work addressing the existence of such moduli spaces (see e.g.
[
Fal93
]
,
[
BP03
]
,
[
BS02
]
) the theory culminated
in the seminal work
[
GLS+10
]
by Gómez-Langer-Sols-Schmitt, where moduli of
G
-bundles were constructed in arbitrary characteristic, for arbitrary reductive groups over
k
, over an arbitrary smooth projective base scheme.
1
1

1

We remark that to obtain projectivity in some of the previous works, some version of the semistable reduction theorem was needed, and this was proved by Heinloth in
[
Hei08
]
for large classes of reductive groups.

In recent years, there has been a growing interest in constructions of moduli of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles for more general groups than reductive group schemes over
k
, in particular for so-called Bruhat-Tits group schemes over
C
. These are smooth affine group schemes
\mathcal{G}
over
C
with geometrically connected fibers such that there is an open subset
U\subset C
such that
\mathcal{G}|_{U}
is connected reductive and for all
x\in C\setminus U
, the group scheme
\mathcal{G}|_{\Spec\mathcal{O}_{C,x}}
is a connected parahoric Bruhat-Tits group.
In particular,
\mathcal{G}
could be a connected reductive group scheme over
C
.
When
\Char(k)=0
, Balaji and Seshadri proved in
[
BS15
]
the existence of a projective good moduli space of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles in the case where
\mathcal{G}|_{U}
is semisimple and simply connected, and for general Bruhat-Tits group schemes Alper–Halpern-Leistner–Heinloth proved in
[
AHH18
]
the existence of a proper good moduli space for arbitrary Bruhat-Tits group schemes.

In this paper we study moduli of principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundles in the case where the group scheme
\mathcal{G}
is reductive over all of
C
, but not necessarily connected. We declare a
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
to be semistable if the adjoint bundle
\Ad_{*}\mathcal{E}
is semistable (see
Section

3.2
), and we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Char(k)=0
, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a (not necessarily connected) reductive group scheme over
C
. The stack
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathrm{ss}}
of semistable
\mathcal{G}
-bundles admits a projective good moduli space.

We prove this result by reducing to the case of connected groups via the following key statement.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a (not necessarily connected) reductive group over
C
. If
\Char(k)>0
assume furthermore that that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{0}
is finite and that
\Char(k)
does not divide
|\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{\circ}|
. Then there exists a finite decomposition
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}\cong\coprod_{\mathcal{P}}\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
by open and closed substacks such that each
\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
admits a torsor
\bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}}\to\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
under a finite group, where
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
is a connected reductive group scheme over
C
.

We believe that this decomposition could be useful in extending various results about
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}
from the case of connected groups to the case of disconnected groups, as illustrated in its use to prove
Theorem

1.1
. It also seems useful in the study of moduli of principal bundles under connected groups. This is manifested in its crucial use in our proof of nondensity of essentially finite
G
-bundles for connected reductive groups
G
over
k
(see below).

To obtain projectivity of the moduli space we use the following auxiliary result which can be deduced from
[
Beh90
, Theorem 4.4.3]
and might be worth stating explicitly since it has occurred in the literature in the case of moduli of semistable vector bundles (see
[
Wei22
, Theorem 4.2]
cf.
[
Hei06
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
Fal93
, Theorem I.4]
).

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an affine smooth group scheme of finite type over
C
and suppose that there is a notion of semistability on
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}
for which the substack of semistable bundles admits a proper good moduli space
M_{\mathcal{G},C}^{\mathrm{ss}}
. For any finite cover
f\colon D\to C
such that a
\mathcal{G}
–bundle
\mathcal{E}
is semistable if and only if
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
is semistable,
f
induces via pullback a finite morphism
f^{*}\colon M_{\mathcal{G},C}^{\mathrm{ss}}\to M_{f^{*}\mathcal{G},D}^{\mathrm{ss}}
.

1.2.
Essentially finite bundles

Building on work by Weil in the 1930’s, Nori introduced in
[
Nor76
]
the notion of an essentially finite (EF) vector bundle.
2
2

2

He studied these bundles over more general bases and over fields which are not necessarily algebraically closed.
A vector bundle is EF if it admits a reduction to a finite group. Essentially finite vector bundles form a Tannakian category and the corresponding fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{N}(C)
classifies covers of
C
by finite group schemes (omitting base points from the notation). For example, if
\Char(k)=0
then
\pi_{1}^{N}(C)(k)=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C)
. EF bundles have since then been studied extensively from various perspectives (e.g.
[
EHS08
]
,
[
BdS11
]
,
[
BV15
]
). If
G
is any algebraic group one may extend the definition to
G
-bundles by saying that a
G
-bundle is EF if it admits a reduction to a finite group. A heuristic question one may ask is the following.

Question 1.4
.

How many essentially finite bundles are there?

Nori proved in
[
Nor82
]
that EF vector bundles are semistable of degree 0 and in
[
GR23
]
the second author and Ghiasabadi proved more generally that EF
G
-bundles are semistable of torsion degree, for any connected reductive group
G
over
k
. Hence, for any such group
G
, in the moduli space
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
of semistable
G
-bundles of degree
0
, we have a subset
M_{G}^{\ef}\subset M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
consisting of EF
G
-bundles of degree 0. A first approximation of
1.4
is to ask about the size of
M_{G}^{\ef}
. For instance, if
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}
, then EF bundles correspond to torsion points on the Jacobian. Since torsion points are dense in any abelian variety, we see that
M_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\ef}\subset M_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
is dense. In
[
DM10
]
Ducrohet and Mehta showed that if
\Char(k)=p>0
, then
M_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}^{\ef}
is dense for all
n\geq 1
. However, Weissmann proved in
[
Wei22
]
that those EF bundles which are trivialized by a prime to
p
cover are not dense by giving an explicit upper bound on the dimension of their Zariski closure in
M_{\mathrm{GL}(n)}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
. In a similar direction, it is proved in
[
GR23
]
that if
\Char(k)=0
, then
M_{G}^{\ef}
is not dense for any connected reductive group
G
of semisimple rank 1. In this article we settle this question of density completely in the characteristic 0 case.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Char(k)=0
, let
g
denote the genus of
C
and assume that
g\geq 2
. For any connected, reductive group
G
we have that

(1.1)

\dim\overline{M_{G}^{\textnormal{ef}}}\leq g\rk(G).

In particular, if
G
is not a torus, then
M_{G}^{\textnormal{ef}}
is not dense in
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
.

Remark 1.6
.

If
g=0
, then
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}(k)=\{\ast\}
, and if
g=1
, then
[
GR23
, Proposition 4.9]
shows that
M_{G}^{\ef}
is dense for all connected reductive groups
G
. The same holds for all
g
if

G\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
is a torus, since then
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}M_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\mathrm{ss}}
.

A key input in proving
Theorem

1.5
is the following purely group theoretic result inspired by Jordan’s classical result in
[
Jor77
]
on finite subgroups of reductive groups, which could be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\Char(k)=0
, and let
G
be a connected, reductive group over
k
. There exist subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\subset G
such that the connected component of identity of each
H_{i}
is a torus and such that all finite subgroups of
G
, up to conjugation, are contained in some
H_{i}
.

3.
Moduli of principal bundles under nonconnected group schemes

3.1.
Decomposition of
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}

Fix a reductive group scheme
\mathcal{G}
over
C
. In this section we decompose
\bun_{\mathcal{G}}
into finitely many open and closed substacks
\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
, each admitting a finite torsor
\bun_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}}\to\bun_{\mathcal{P}}
for some connected reductive group scheme
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
over
C
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quasi-finite étale group scheme over
C
. If
\Char(k)>0
assume furthermore that
\Gamma
is finite and that
\Char(k)
and
|\Gamma|
are coprime. There is a finite decomposition

\bun_{\Gamma,C}\cong\coprod_{[\mathcal{P}]\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(C,\Gamma)}\mathrm{B}\Aut(\mathcal{P}).

Proof.

Suppose first that
\Gamma
is finite over
C
. Upon choosing a geometric base point
c
in
C
, we have that
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(C,\Gamma)=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,c),\Gamma_{c})
, where
\Gamma_{c}
is the fiber of
\Gamma
at
c
equipped with its
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,c)
-action. Since
\Gamma_{c}
is finite and
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,c)
is (pro)finitely generated, we see that

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(C,\Gamma)
is finite.

If
\Char(k)=0
and
\Gamma
is quasi-finite, then let
U\subset C
be the largest open dense subset such that
\Gamma|_{U}
is finite, and let
u
be a geometric point in
U
. Then the functor

P\mapsto(P_{u},\{P_{x}\}_{x\in C\setminus U})

determines an equivalence of categories between the category of quasi-finite étale schemes
P\to C
such that
P|_{U}
is finite, and the category consisting of tuples
(\Sigma,\{F_{x}\}_{x\in C\setminus U})
where
\Sigma
is a finite
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u)
-set and
F_{x}\subset\Sigma^{\gal(k(C)^{\text{sep}}/K_{x}^{\text{h}})}
, where
K_{x}^{\text{h}}
denotes the fraction field of the henselization of
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. This functor respects products and takes group objects to group objects. The essential image of all
\Gamma
-bundles
P\to C
thus consists of tuples
(\Sigma,\{F_{x}\}_{x\in C\setminus U})
where
\Sigma
is a
(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u),\Gamma_{u})
-set and the
F_{x}
are
\Gamma_{x}
-sets, where the action of
\Gamma_{u}
on
\Sigma
(resp.
\Gamma_{x}
on
F_{x}
) is free and transitive. To give such a
(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u),\Gamma_{u})
-set is equivalent to giving a cocycle in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u),\Gamma_{u})
. Since
\Char(k)=0
,
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u)
is (pro)finitely generated and thus
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u),\Gamma_{u})
is finite.

Hence, in either case there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of
\Gamma
-bundles on
C
.
Since a
k
-valued point of
\bun_{\Gamma,C}
is a
\Gamma
-bundle on
C
, we have a surjection

In particular,
\bun_{\Gamma,C}
is quasi-compact.
Moreover, since all stabilizer groups are finite,
\bun_{\Gamma,C}
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack (see e.g.
[

, Theorem 3.6.1]
). Thus, there exists an étale surjection

from a smooth, quasi-compact

.

3.2.
Semistability

In
[
Ram96
,
Ram96a
]
Ramanathan defined a notion of semistability for
G
-bundles under a connected reductive group
G
over
k
. When
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, this notion agrees with
\mu
-semistability when
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-bundles are viewed as vector bundles of rank
n
(see
[
HM04
, Corollary 1]
for a proof), and Ramanathan proved that a
G
-bundle
E
is semistable if and only if
\Ad_{*}E
is semistable (see
[
Ram96
, Corollary 3.18]
).

Let now
\mathcal{G}
be a connected reductive group scheme over
C
. In
[
Beh95
]
Behrend introduced the following notion of semistability for
\mathcal{G}
-bundles, which reduces to Ramanathan’s notion in the case of a constant group scheme. A
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
is
semistable
if for each parabolic subgroup
\mathcal{P}\subset\uAut(\mathcal{E})
, we have that

\deg\text{Lie}(\mathcal{P})\leq 0.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a connected reductive group over
C
, then a
\mathcal{G}
–bundle
\mathcal{E}
is semistable if and only if
\Ad_{*}\mathcal{E}
is semistable.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathcal{G}
is an inner form of a constant connected reductive group
G
. That is, there is a
G
-bundle
E
such that
\mathcal{G}\cong\uAut(E)
. The functor
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}E
is then an equivalence of categories between
\mathcal{G}
-bundles on
C
and
G
-bundles on
C
. Under this equivalence semistable
\mathcal{G}
-bundles correspond to semistable
G
-bundles. Indeed, if
\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-bundle, then we have that

\uAut(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}(E\times^{G}G)\cong(\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}E)\times^{G}G\cong\uAut(\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}E).

Furthermore, since
\Lie(\mathcal{G})\cong E\times^{G}\Lie(G)
we see that for any
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
we have that

\Ad_{*}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}\Lie(\mathcal{G})\cong\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}}E\times^{G}\Lie(G)\cong\Ad_{*}(\mathcal{E}\times^{G}E).

Since
\mathcal{E}
is semistable if and only if
\mathcal{E}\times^{G}E
is semistable, the statement follows from the corresponding statement for a constant group.

In the general case, by
[
Beh95
, Section 4.2]
, there is a finite étale cover
f\colon D\to C
such that
f^{*}\mathcal{G}
is an inner form of a constant group. By
[
Beh95
, Corollary 7.4]
any
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
is semistable if and only if
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
is semistable, and since
\Ad_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{E}\cong f^{*}\Ad_{*}\mathcal{E}
the statement thus follows.
∎

We thus make the following definition of semistability for
\mathcal{G}
–bundles to include the nonconnected case.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a reductive group scheme over
C
. A
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
is

if
\Ad_{*}\mathcal{E}
is semistable in the sense above.

From the previous discussions we immediately obtain the following statements.

\mathcal{G}

4.
Nondensity of essentially finite bundles

4.1.
Finite subgroups of reductive groups

We remind the reader that we continue with the assumption that
\Char(k)=0
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary reductive group over
k
. The main purpose of this section is to prove that there are finitely many subgroups
H_{1},...,H_{n}
of
G
such that each
H_{i}^{\circ}
is a torus and any finite subgroup of
G
is contained in some
H_{i}
, up to conjugation. To do so, recall first Jordan’s classical result in
[
Jor77
]
on finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(n)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
Jor77
]
)
For each integer
n>1
there is an integer
f_{n}
such that every finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{GL}(n)
contains an abelian normal subgroup
A
such that
[\Gamma:A]\leq f_{n}.

Inspired by this result, we prove the following generalized version.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a (not necessarily connected) reductive group. There exists a constant
f_{G}\geq 1
with the following property: for every finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
there is an abelian normal subgroup
A\subset\Gamma
contained in a torus of
G^{\circ}
such that
[\Gamma:A]\leq f_{G}
.

The proof of
Theorem

4.2
closely follows Breuillard’s exposition in
[
Bre23
]
on Jordan’s theorem. Mainly, we make sure that one can run the same arguments upon making appropriate changes from the
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-case to the general case of a reductive group (e.g. the use of the center instead of scalar matrices, the use of the projection
G\to G/[G,G]
instead of the determinant map, etc.). As such, we relegate the proof to an appendix (see
Appendix

A
).

Before proving the main result of this section, we prove the following lemma and introduce some notation.

Lemma 4.3
.

For a (not necessarily connected) reductive group
G
and a finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
, there is a finite number of elements in
\Hom(\Gamma,G)
up to conjugacy of
G
.

Proof.

We can give
\Hom(\Gamma,G)
the structure of a variety over
k
, and we can consider the categorical quotient quotient
X(\Gamma,G)\coloneqq\Hom(\Gamma,G)//G
, the
G
-character variety of
\Gamma
. It is an affine variety, and the points of
X(\Gamma,G)
are completely reducible representations of
\Gamma
up to conjugacy. If
\Gamma
is finite and
\Char(k)=0
, this is just every homomorphism up to conjugacy. If
\rho:\Gamma\to G
is a homomorphism, then

\dim T_{[\rho]}X(\Gamma,G)\leq H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g}),

where
T_{[\rho]}
is the tangent space at the class
[\rho]\in X(\Gamma,G)
and where
\Gamma
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
trough
\rho
. This follows from
[
Sik10
, Theorem 1]
. Take a cocycle
\gamma\in H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g})
, and define for
g\in G
the map
\varphi_{g}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

\varphi_{g}(X)=\rho(g)X+\gamma(g).

This is an action of
\Gamma
on
\mathfrak{g}
by affine transformations. By averaging over an orbit, we find a fixed point
X_{0}
. We therefore have

\gamma(g)=\varphi_{g}(X_{0})-\rho(g)X_{0}=X_{0}-\rho(g)X_{0}.

In other words,
\gamma
is a coboundary and
H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathfrak{g})=0
. We see that
X(\Gamma,G)
is finite.
∎

Given a subset
S\subset G
we denote by
C_{G}(S)
and
N_{G}(S)
the centralizer and the normalizer of
S
in
G
.
Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
. For any representation
V
of
G
with central kernel, let
\Phi(V)
be the set of weights of
V
relative to
T
. We say that a subgroup
H\subset T
is a
weight kernel
if it is defined by a finite number of equations of the form
\alpha(t)=\beta(t)
for
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi(V)
and
t\in T
. That is, for any
S\subset\Phi^{2}
we have a weight kernel

H_{S}=\{\;t\in T\;:\;\alpha(t)/\beta(t)=1\;\text{for all}\;(\alpha,\beta)\in S\;\}.

Conversely, any weight kernel gives a subset
S\subset\Phi^{2}
. We see that

S\subset S^{\prime}\iff H_{S}\supset H_{S^{\prime}}.

If
A\subset T
is a group, then we let
H_{A}
be the minimal weight kernel containing
A
. Since both
C_{G}(A)
and
C_{G}(H_{A})
consist exactly of the elements preserving the decomposition
V
into characters of
A
, we see that
C_{G}(A)=C_{G}(H_{A})
. Similarly, since both
N_{G}(A)
and
N_{G}(H_{A})
consists exactly of the elements permuting the characters of
V
we obtain that
N_{G}(A)=N_{G}(H_{A})
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G
be a reductive group. There is a set
S(G)=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
of closed subgroups of
G
such that
H_{i}^{\circ}
is a torus and for any finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
some conjugate is contained in one of the
H_{i}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the dimension of
G
. If
\dim G=1
, then
G^{\circ}
is a torus, so we are done. For the induction step, we assume the statement of the theorem for any group reductive group
H
such that
\dim H<\dim G
. Let
\Gamma\subset G
be a finite subgroup. By
Theorem

4.2
, there is an abelian normal subgroup
A\subset\Gamma
contained in a maximal torus
T\subset G
.

First, assume that
A
is not in the center of
G^{\circ}
. This means that
C_{G}(A)\subsetneq G
, which implies that
\dim C_{G}(A)<\dim G
. Since
C_{G}(A)
has finite index in
N_{G}(A)
, we also see that
\dim N_{G}(A)<\dim G
. Now
\Gamma\subset N_{G}(A)
. Up to conjugacy, the number of groups of the form
N_{G}(A)
as we let
\Gamma
run over all the finite subgroups of
G
will be finite. Indeed, we can choose a faithful representation
V
of
G
and a weight kernel
H_{A}
relative
T
such that
N_{G}(H_{A})=N_{G}(A)
, and there are only finitely many such weight kernels. In other words, if we add
S(N_{G}(H))
to
S(G)
where
H
is a weight kernel we get by induction that
\Gamma\subset H_{i}
for some
H_{i}\in S(G)
.

Suppose now that
A\subset Z(G^{\circ})
. By the structure theory of reductive groups,
G^{\circ}=ZG^{\prime}
where
Z\coloneqq Z(G^{\circ})^{\circ}
,
G^{\prime}\coloneqq[G^{\circ},G^{\circ}]
and where the intersection
Z\cap G^{\prime}
is finite. Consider the map
\rho:G\to G/Z
. The group
B\coloneqq Z((G/Z)^{\circ})=Z(G^{\circ})/Z
is a finite group, and
\rho(A)\subset B
. This means that

|\rho(\Gamma)|=[\rho(\Gamma):\rho(A)]|\rho(A)|\leq f_{G}|B|.

This means that
|\rho(\Gamma)|
is bounded in terms of
G
. In particular, up to isomorphism the set

\{\rho(\Gamma):\Gamma\subset G,A\subset\mathrm{Z}(G^{\circ})\}

is finite. By
Lemma

4.3
, for a given choice of
\Gamma
there is a finite number of conjugacy classes in
\Hom(\rho(\Gamma),G/Z)
. This means there is a finite set of choices for
\rho(\Gamma)
. Each choice determines a group
H=\rho^{-1}(\rho(\Gamma))
, which has connected component a torus. We add each
H
to
S(G)
.
∎

4.2.
Nondensity of essentially finite bundles

In this section,
G
is a connected reductive group over
k
. We recall that a
G
-bundle is essentially finite if it admits a reduction to a finite group. The same definition applies to nonconnected groups as well, and an important remark is that if a bundle is essentially finite, then there is a finite étale cover trivialising it (these notions are in fact equivalent but we won’t need the other direction in what follows). We denote by
M_{G}^{\ef}
the set of essentially finite
G
-bundles of degree 0 inside the moduli space of semistable
G
-bundles of degree 0, denoted
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
.

In this section we give an upper bound on the dimension of
\overline{M_{G}^{\ef}}
and thus prove that
M_{G}^{\ef}
is not dense in
M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
. The idea is as follows. Let
H_{i}
be as in
theorem

4.4
and denote by
\varphi_{i}\colon H_{i}\hookrightarrow G
the inclusion. We would like to say that
M_{G}^{\ef}\subset\cup_{i}\varphi_{i,*}M_{H_{i}}^{\mathrm{ss}}\subset M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
and then conclude by dimension reasons. However, if
E
is a semistable
H_{i}
-bundle, then
\varphi_{i,*}E
need not be a semistable
G
-bundle, so we don’t have a morphism
\varphi_{i,*}\colon M_{H_{i}}^{\mathrm{ss}}\to M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}
. Thus we prove first that there is a sublocus of semistable
H_{i}
-bundles whose pushforward is semistable. Then we show that every essentially finite
H_{i}
-bundle is contained in this sublocus. Finally we make sure that we have the expected relation between dimensions:
\dim M_{H_{i}}^{\mathrm{ss}}<\dim M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}
. After introducing some notation, we prove these claims in in a sequence of lemmas, leading up to the stated density result.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a torus over
C
. By
[
Hei10
]
we have that

\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T}})=X_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{k(C)^{\text{sep}}})_{\gal(k(C)^{\text{sep}}/k(C))}.

We denote by
\Bun_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}
the component of
\Bun_{\mathcal{T}}
corresponding to
d\in\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T}})
, and for
E\in\Bun_{\mathcal{T}}^{d}(k)
, we say that
E
is of degree
d
. We use similar notation for the semistable locus. By
[
Hei17
]
for all
d\in\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T}})
,
\bun_{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{ss},d}
is of finite type. If
H
is a non-connected reductive group such that
H^{\circ}=T
is a torus, then let

\bun_{H}^{0}\coloneqq\im\Big(\coprod_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(C,H/H^{\circ})}\bun_{H_{\mathcal{P}}}^{0}\to\bun_{H}\Big),

with the notation
H_{\mathcal{P}}
analogous to that in
Section

3.1
.
We define
\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
and
M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss},0}
similarly.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a torus over
C
, and let
f\colon C^{\prime}\to C
be a finite étale morphism. The induced map
f^{*}\colon\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T},C})\to\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T},C^{\prime}})
is given by multiplication with
\deg(f)
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{T}
is induced, i.e. that there is some finite, generically étale morphism
\pi\colon D\to C
such that
\mathcal{T}\cong\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m}\coloneqq\res_{D/C}(\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The Leray spectral sequence

\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,\mathrm{R}^{q}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})\implies\mathrm{H}^{p+q}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(D,\mathbb{G}_{m})

induces a filtration on
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(D,\mathbb{G}_{m})
whose graded pieces are
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})
. For any point
x\in C
, by proper base change (see e.g.
[
Den88
]
) and using that
\pi
is finite, we see that
(\mathrm{R}^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m})_{x}\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(D_{x},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
. Hence,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C,\mathcal{T})\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(D,\mathbb{G}_{m})
via the Leray spectral sequence and the result follows from this known case for line bundles.

In the general case, following the proof of
[
Hei10
, Lemma 16]
(cf.
[
PR08
, Section 3. and 5.]
) we can choose induced tori
\mathcal{I}_{2}\to\mathcal{I}_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{T}
such that the induced sequence

\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{I}_{2}})\to\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{I}_{1}})\to\pi_{0}(\Bun_{\mathcal{T}})\to 0

is exact. The result now follows.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
g
denote the genus of
C
and assume that
g\geq 2
. Let
G
be a connected, reductive group not equal to a torus, and let
H\subset G
be a subgroup such that
H^{\circ}=T
, for some torus
T\subset G
. Then we have that

(4.1)

\dim M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}\leq g\dim T.

In particular,
\dim M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}<\dim M_{G}^{\mathrm{ss}}
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{x}\in M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}
be a closed point, let
x\in\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}
be the unique closed point in the fiber over
\overline{x}
and let
H_{x}
denote the stabiliser of

. By the local structure theorem for good moduli spaces
[
Alp23
, Corollary 6.5.3]
we have a Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\Spec A/H_{x}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec A^{H_{x}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}}

where the horizontal maps are étale. Hence,
\dim M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\dim\Spec A^{H_{x}}
and
\dim\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\dim\Spec A-\dim H_{x}
.
To get an upper bound on

we may assume that

is connected, and hence irreducible. Since

is irreducible, it preserves the irreducible components of

so we may furthermore assume that

\displaystyle=\dim\Spec A-\dim G+d

\displaystyle=\dim[\Spec A/H_{x}]+d

\displaystyle=\dim\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}+d.

\displaystyle\dim M_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}

\displaystyle\leq\dim\bun_{H}^{\mathrm{ss}}+\dim H

Appendix A

The purpose of this appendix is to prove the following.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
G
be a (not necessarily connected) reductive group and let
\Gamma\subset G
be a finite subgroup. Then there is a constant
f_{G}\geq 1
with the following property: For every finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
there is an abelian, normal subgroup
A\subset\Gamma
contained in a torus of
G^{\circ}
such that
[\Gamma:A]\leq f_{G}
.

We follow
[
Bre23
]
, only making necessary adjustments to make the theorem work for a general reductive group.

Definition A.2
.

Let
T
be a torus. Let
\Gamma\subset G
be a finite group and
M
be a positive integer greater than 2. We say that a subgroup
F\subset\Gamma
is an
M
-fan
if it is conjugate to a subgroup
F^{\prime}
of
T
such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi
,
\alpha/\beta
either attains
M
different values on
F^{\prime}
or is reduced to 1 on
F^{\prime}
.

Remark A.3
.

To clarify, any
M
-fan is by definition also an
M^{\prime}
-fan for any
M^{\prime}\leq M
. Further, it follows from the definition that if
F
is an
M
-fan then
H_{F}
is the weight kernel defined by setting the same
\alpha/\beta=1
as those which reduce to 1 on
F
. We also have that
H_{H_{A}}=H_{A}
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
G
be a reductive group. The following are equivalent.

(1)

There is a constant
f_{G}\geq 1
with the following property: For every finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
there is an abelian, normal subgroup
A\subset\Gamma
contained in a torus of
G^{\circ}
such that
[\Gamma:A]\leq f_{G}
.

(2)

There is a positive integer
N=N(G)
such that for any positive integer
M>N
any finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset G
contains a unique maximal
M
-fan
\mathcal{F}
such that
[\Gamma:\mathcal{F}]\leq N
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\subset G
be an arbitrary finite subgroup.

If (2) holds, then
\Gamma
contains a unique maximal
M
-fan, which is normal and toral. Hence, with
f_{G}\coloneqq N
we obtain (1).

Conversely, assume that (1) holds. Let
N\coloneqq f_{G}
, and let
A\subset\Gamma
be a normal toral subgroup such that
[\Gamma:A]\leq N
. Then
\Gamma\subset N_{G}(A)=N_{G}(H_{A})
, where
H_{A}
is a weight kernel for the adjoint representation. We let
M>N
. If
F
is an
M
-fan in
\Gamma
and

, then we have that
f^{N}\in A\subset H_{A}
since
[F:F\cap A]\leq[\Gamma:A]\leq N
. Hence, for any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Phi(\mathfrak{g})
, if
\alpha_{1}/\alpha_{2}
attains
M
different values on

, then
(\alpha_{1}/\alpha_{2})(f^{N})
attains at least two different values as we go trough

. Furthermore, since

,
\alpha_{1}/\alpha_{2}
reduces to 1 on

if and only if it does so on

. This means that the weights determining

as a 2-fan in
\Gamma
are the same as the weights determining

as an
M
-fan in
\Gamma
. Hence,
H_{F\cap H_{A}}=H_{F}
, which means that

Abstract
This Letter presents the first experimental evidence of the attractive strong interaction between a proton and a ϕ \phi meson. The result is obtained from two-particle correlations of combined p \mathrm{p} – ϕ \phi ⊕ \oplus p ¯ \overline{\mathrm{p}} – ϕ \phi pairs measured in high-multiplicity pp collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}~=~13 TeV by the ALICE collaboration. The spin-averaged scattering length and effective range of the p \mathrm{p} – ϕ \phi interaction are extracted from the fully corrected correlation function employing the Lednický–Lyuboshits approach. In particular, the imaginary part of the scattering length vanishes within uncertainties, indicating that inelastic processes do not play a prominent role for the p \mathrm{p} – ϕ \phi interaction. These data demonstrate that the interaction is dominated by elastic p \mathrm{p} – ϕ \phi scattering. Furthermore, an analysis employing phenomenological Gaussian- and Yukawa-type potentials is conducted. Under the assumption of the latter, the N \mathrm{N} – ϕ \phi coupling constant is found to be g N ​ – ​ ϕ = 0.14 ± 0.03 ( stat . ) ± 0.02 ( syst . ) g_{\mathrm{N}\mbox{--}\phi}=\nolinebreak 0.14\pm\nolinebreak 0.03\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.02\,(\mathrm{syst.}) . This work provides valuable experimental input to accomplish a self-consistent description of the N \mathrm{N} – ϕ \phi interaction, which is particularly relevant for the more fundamental studies on partial restoration of chiral symmetry in nuclear medium.

Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) is the theory of the strong interaction, where the degrees of freedom are represented by colored quarks and gluons.
The observable degrees of freedom at low energies, however, are composite hadrons, and their mass is dynamically generated by the interplay between spontaneous and explicit chiral symmetry breaking. The spontaneous breaking arises from the non-zero vacuum expectation value
\langle 0|\mathrm{\overline{q}}\mathrm{q}|0\rangle
of the ground state of the QCD Lagrangian, the quark condensate
\mathrm{\overline{q}}
q. The consideration of the quark mass term leads to the explicit breaking of chiral symmetry.
A link between the hadron properties and
\langle 0|\mathrm{\overline{q}}\mathrm{q}|0\rangle
is provided by the QCD sum rule method
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, with the hadrons being interpreted as excitations of the
\mathrm{\overline{q}}
q ground state.
Chiral symmetry is expected to be partially restored in a dense and/or hot strongly interacting medium
[
5
]
.
Consequently, the modification of the
\mathrm{\overline{q}}
q condensate in the medium is reflected in the spectral shape of its hadronic excitations. This results in a potentially measurable mass shift and/or width broadening of the hadrons.
From the experimental point of view, the light vector mesons
\uprho
,
\upomega
and
\phi
represent the most suitable hadronic probes due to the short lifetime
[
6
,
7

\mathrm{e}^{+}\mathrm{e}^{-}
and
\upmu^{+}\upmu^{-}

In this context, the
\phi

\uprho
and
\upomega
mesons. The KEK-PS E325 collaboration measured the
\phi
spectral function in p–C and p–Cu reactions and reported a slight mass shift and moderate increase of the width, which was in a model-dependent manner interpreted as an in-medium modification
[
13

The collected data were, however, limited due to the small branching ratio (BR
\approx$3\text{\times}{10}^{-4}$

[
14

Larger data samples were collected by the Spring8-LEPS collaboration in order to determine the

For large values of

Finally, the data are employed to constrain the parameters of phenomenological Gaussian- and Yukawa-type potentials. As the imaginary contribution of the scattering length is consistent with zero, only real values are used for the parameters.
The fits yield a comparable degree of consistency as the fit with the Lednický–Lyuboshits approach.
The resulting values for the Gaussian-type potential are
V_{\mathrm{eff}}=\nolinebreak 2.5\pm\nolinebreak 0.9\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 1.4\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{MeV}$
and
\upmu=\nolinebreak 0.14\pm\nolinebreak 0.06\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.09\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{f}\mathrm{m}^{-2}$
, indicating a much shallower strong interaction potential than lattice QCD results for the N–
\mathrm{J}/\psi
strong interaction
[
66
]
.
For the Yukawa-type potential the fit yields
A=\nolinebreak 0.021\pm\nolinebreak 0.009\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.006\,(\mathrm{syst.})
and
\upalpha=\nolinebreak 65.9\pm\nolinebreak 38.0\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 17.5\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{MeV}$
. Predictions of possible
\mathrm{N}
–
\phi
bound states employing the same kind of potential with modified parameters (
\upalpha=$600\text{~}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}$
and
A=1.25
)
[
24
]
are therefore incompatible with this measurement. The
\mathrm{N}
–
\phi
coupling constant under the assumption of a Yukawa-type potential (
g_{\mathrm{N}\mbox{--}\phi}=\sqrt{A}
) is
g_{\mathrm{N}\mbox{--}\phi}=\nolinebreak 0.14\pm\nolinebreak 0.03\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.02\,(\mathrm{syst.})
.

In conclusion, this Letter presents the first correlation-based measurement of the
\mathrm{p}
–
\phi
interaction. The correlation function reflects the pattern of an attractive interaction. The scattering parameters, extracted with the Lednický–Lyuboshits approach, are
\Re(f_{0})=\nolinebreak 0.85\pm\nolinebreak 0.34\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.14\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{f}\mathrm{m}$
,
\Im(f_{0})=\nolinebreak 0.16\pm\nolinebreak 0.10\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.09\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{f}\mathrm{m}$
, and
d_{0}=\nolinebreak 7.85\pm\nolinebreak 1.54\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.26\,(\mathrm{syst.})~$\mathrm{f}\mathrm{m}$
. Remarkably, the imaginary contribution to the scattering length vanishes, indicating that inelastic processes do not play a prominent role.
Instead, the interaction is dominated by the elastic
\mathrm{p}
–
\phi
dynamics. Under the assumption of a Yukawa-type potential for the
\mathrm{N}
–
\phi
interaction, the value of the coupling constant is extracted as
g_{\mathrm{N}\mbox{--}\phi}=\nolinebreak 0.14\pm\nolinebreak 0.03\,(\mathrm{stat.})\pm\nolinebreak 0.02\,(\mathrm{syst.})
.

These results seem to contradict the interpretation of the
\phi
production off nuclear targets in terms of large absorption cross-sections but more data are needed to extract the precise value of the imaginary part of the scattering length and the here reported results should serve as an input for more advanced modellings in medium.
The upcoming Run 3 and Run 4 data taking at the LHC will allow to significantly improve the precision of the extracted interaction parameters.
This measurement demonstrates for the first time that the
\mathrm{p}
–
\phi
interaction in vacuum is attractive and dominated by elastic scattering. It provides valuable experimental input on the
\mathrm{N}
–
\phi
interaction, which is fundamental to reach a self-consistent description of the interaction as required for the correct interpretation of data from nuclear collisions.

Lipschitz stability of γ \gamma -FOCS and RC canonical Jordan bases of real H H -selfadjoint matrices under small perturbations Journal: TBD
S. Dogruer Akgul, A. Minenkova, V. Olshevsky

Abstract
In 2008 Bella, Olshevsky and Prasad proved that the flipped orthogonal (FO) Jordan bases of H H -selfadjoint matrices are Lipschitz stable under small perturbations. In 2022, Dogruer, Minenkova and Olshevsky considered the real case, and proved that for real H H -selfadjoint matrices there exist a more refined bases called FOCS bases. In addition to flipped orthogonality they also possess the conjugate symmetric (CS) property. In this paper we prove that these new FOCS bases are Lipschitz stable under small perturbations as well. We also establish the Lipschitz stability for the classical real canonical Jordan bases.

Introduction

1.1
H
-selfadjoint matrices and the affiliation relation

Let us start with the definition of
the indefinite inner product
. It is a relation denoted by
[\cdot,\cdot]
that satisfies all the axioms of the usual inner product except for positivity.

For example, every
n\times n
invertible Hermitian matrix
H

determines an indefinite inner product through

[x,y]_{H}=y^{*}Hx,\,\text{ for }x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(1.1)

The converse is also true: for any indefinite inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we can find such invertible Hermitian
H
that (
1.1
) holds true. If
H
is positive-definite then
[\cdot,\cdot]_{H}
turns into the classical inner product. However, in this paper we do not impose such an assumption.

H-selfadjoint matrices.
A matrix
A
is called
H-selfadjoint
if

A=H^{-1}A^{*}H\text{ (equivalently $[Ax,y]_{H}=[x,Ay]_{H}$)}.

(1.2)

There are many application for such matrices and they have been studied by many authors (e.g. see
[
4
]
and many references therein).

If follows from (
1.2
) that the eigenvalues of
H
-selfadjoint matrices are symmetric with respect to the real axis. In addition, the conjugate pairs of eigenvalues have the Jordan blocks of the same sizes.

Affiliation relation.
For any change of basis

x\mapsto u=T^{-1}x,\quad y\mapsto v=T^{-1}y,

we have

[x,y]_{H}=y^{*}Hx=(Tv)^{*}H(Tu)=v^{*}\overset{G}{\overbrace{(T^{*}HT)}}u=[u,v]_{G}.

That is,
a new congruent matrix
G=T^{*}HT
generates the same inner product in the new basis.

Two pairs
(A,H)
and
(B,G)
are called
affiliated
if

T^{-1}AT=B\text{ and }T^{*}HT=G.

and this equivalence relation is called the
affiliation relation
.

(A,H)\mapsto(B,G).

(1.3)

The affiliation relation preserves selfadjointness in the indefinite inner product, that is if
A
is
H
-selfadjoint, then
B
is
G
-selfadjoint.

For an
H_{0}
-selfadjoint matrix there exists an invertible matrix
T_{0}
(see e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
9
]
and the references therein), such that
(A_{0},H_{0})\overset{T_{0}}{\mapsto}(J,P)
, i.e.

T_{0}^{-1}A_{0}T_{0}=J\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{0}^{*}H_{0}T_{0}=P,

where
J
is the Jordan Form of
A_{0}
and
P
has a particularly sparse simple form called
the sip matrix
in
[
4
, Section 5.5]
, which is uniquely defined up-to perturbation of the Jordan blocks in
J
. This is a very special affiliation relation. It first appeared in the works by Weierstrass
[
15
,
16
]
(see also
[
4
, Chapter 5]
and
[
9
, Chapter 3]
). The columns of
T_{0}
form a Jordan basis of
A_{0}
, and this basis was called “canonical”(see
[
5
]
) and the pair
(J,P)
is sometimes called the
Weierstrass form
of
A_{0}
. Since we introduce two more canonical bases, we suggest to call this basis
flipped-orthogonal
(or just FO) basis of
(A_{0},H_{0})
in order to distinguish them.

FO, CS, and FOCS Bases

Here is an example of an
H
-selfadjoint matrix.

A=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&-16\\
1&0&0&0\\
0&1&0&-8\\
0&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}=TJT^{-1},\quad H=\frac{1}{128}\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&-12\\
1&0&-12&0\\
0&-12&0&80\\
-12&0&80&0\end{bmatrix},

where

J=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}-2i&1&0&0\\
0&-2i&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&2i&1\\
0&0&0&2i\end{array}\right],\quad P=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&0&0&1\\
0&0&1&0\\
\hline\cr 0&1&0&0\\
1&0&0&0\end{array}\right],

(1.5)

and

-4&4i&-4&-8i\\
2i&-1&-2i&1\\
-1&0&-1&-i\end{bmatrix}.

Here,
(J,P)
is the FO form of

and the columns of

is called

1
]

Note that FO bases exist in the general complex case. However, the matrices

in our example are real, which suggests the following question.

Are there any canonical bases specific for the case of real matrices?
Yes, for example a conjugate symmetric (CS) basis. Let us return to the same matrices

and

are as in (
1.4
).
and consider another Jordan basis for

, captured by the columns of the following matrix.

(1.7)

The columns

are conjugate of each other:

Therefore, we say that

captures a

of

from (

) and
(A,H)\overset{L}{\mapsto}(J,G)
, where

is not a sip matrix as was

in (

Also, if we look back at the FO basis in

from (

), it is not CS.

Now that we have seen the example of the FO and CS canonical bases we started to wonder:

Could we find a Jordan basis for real
H
-selfadjoint matrices that is flipped orthogonal and
\gamma
-conjugate symmetric at the same time?

Here is a matrix whose columns also capture a Jordan basis of the same

The positive answer to this question can be found in

]

1.3
Lipschitz Stability of FO Bases

Stability of Jordan bases were studied previously, for example, in the paper by Bella, Olshevsky and Prasad
[
1
]
. They proved the Lipschitz
stability of the so-called flipped orthogonal (FO) Jordan bases of
complex

H
-selfadjoint matrices under small perturbations.

The following fact was presented in
[
1
, Proposition 1.5]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

(Lipschitz stability of FO-bases)
Let
A_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a fixed
H_{0}
-selfadjoint matrix and let the columns of this matrix capture the FO-basis, that is,

(A_{0},H_{0})\overset{T_{0}}{\mapsto}(J,P).

There exist constants
K,\delta>0
(depending on
A_{0}
and
H_{0}
only) such that the following assertion holds for any
H
-selfadjoint matrix
A
with
A
similar to
A_{0}
and

\|A-A_{0}\|+\|H-H_{0}\|<\delta,

there exists a matrix
T
whose columns capture the FO-basis of
A
, i.e.

(A,H)\overset{T}{\mapsto}(J,P)

and such that

\|T-T_{0}\|\leq K(\|A-A_{0}\|+\|H-H_{0}\|).

(1.8)

1.4
Main results and the structure of the paper

1.4.1
Lipschitz Stability of FOCS Bases

We are going to build on what we mentioned before and show that if the pair
(A,H)
is real, and if matrix
T_{0}
captures the FO but FOCS basis of
(A,H)
, there is a matrix

which can be chosen to capture the
\gamma
-FOCS basis of
(A,H)
and still be Lipschitz stable under small perturbation preserving the Jordan structure. That is, we prove the bound (

), in which both matrices

and

enjoy the additional CS-property.

1.4.2
Lipshitz stability of RC bases

We refer the reader to
[
4
]
for an introduction to real canonical bases.

Let us come back to our example from (

). Here is the real Jordan form

of

J_{R}=R^{-1}AR=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}0&-2&1&0\\

In this paper, via FOCS bases we were able to obtain results on stability of

We prove that there exists an invertible matrix

1.4.3
Weak Affiliation

Now let us clarify what the difference is between
affiliation
and
weak affiliation
.

Definition 1.2
.

(Weak Affiliation Relation)

Two pairs
(A,H)
and
(B,G)
are called
weakly affiliated
if for some invertible matrix
T
matrices
T^{-1}AT
and
B
have the same Jordan basis, and
T^{*}HT=G
.
The relation
(A,H)\overset{T}{\mapsto_{w}}(B,G)
is called the

.

In
[
1
, Theorem 1.8]
it was showed that if a small perturbation
A
of

has the same Jordan structure as
A_{0}
,
H
is a
small perturbation of
H_{0}
, and as before
A_{0}
is
H_{0}
-selfadjoint and
A
is
H
-selfadjoint, then an affiliation
matrix
S
exists, and it can be chosen to be a small perturbation of the identity matrix, and a
Lipschitz-type bound

\|I-S\|\leq K(\|A-A_{0}\|+\|H-H_{0}\|)

holds for some positive

depending on

only. More details on the weak affiliation case are presented in Section 6.

1.4.4
The Structure of the Paper

For the sake of brevity we used examples of FO and FOCS bases to present our main result. In Section 2 we introduce these concepts rigorously.
In
[
2
]
we established the existence of FOCS bases. That construction does not seem to be useful to derive our main result, Lipschitz stability. Hence, in Section 3 we present a different proof for
the existence of FOCS bases. The new construction allows us to establish the Lipshitz stability in Section 4.
In Section 5 the Lipshitz stability of real canonical forms is established.
Finally, we slightly generalize the results of Sections 4 and 5 to the weak affiliation case in Section 6.

\gamma
-FOCS basis of

This theorem says that
\gamma
-FOCS bases are Lipshitz stable under small perturbations, preserving the Jordan structure of
A_{0}
. To prove Theorem

we need a technical result stated in Lemma

and in this proof we need to modify the notations as described next.

\bf{\Theta-Calculus}

In this paper we use and prove a large number of inequalities with the right-hand side

K(\|A-A_{0}\|+\|H-H_{0}\|)

with some constant
K>0
. Each of these inequalities hold for its own
K
implying cumbersome notations (introducing large number of
K_{i}
’s and the necessity to explain at each step that those
K_{i}
’s are different). To avoid it we introduce the notation

\Theta=K(\|A-A_{0}\|+\|H-H_{0}\|),

where we do not keep track of the actual value of
K
. Though it is not quite rigorous (e.g,
3\Theta\leq 2\Theta
is a valid inequality) but this does not lead to a confusion, and greatly simplifies the presentation. However, one has to always keep in mind that in different inequalities
\Theta
’s may invoke different values of
K
.

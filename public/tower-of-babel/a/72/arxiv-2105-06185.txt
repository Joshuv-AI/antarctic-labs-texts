Sharp gradient estimates on weighted manifolds with compact boundary
Ha Tuan Dung, Nguyen Thac Dung, Jia-Yong Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we prove sharp gradient estimates for positive solutions to the weighted heat equation on smooth metric measure spaces with compact boundary. As an application, we prove Liouville theorems for ancient solutions satisfying the Dirichlet boundary condition and some sharp growth restriction near infinity. Our results can be regarded as a refinement of recent results due to Kunikawa and Sakurai.

1
Introduction

In geometric analysis, gradient estimates play an important role in the studying
elliptic and parabolic equations on Riemannian manifolds. In
[
Yau75
]
, Yau proved
that if the Ricci curvature of manifold
(M^{n},g)
satisfies
{\rm Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K

for some constant
K\geq 0
, then any positive harmonic function
u
on
(M^{n},g)
satisfies

\lim\limits_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\sqrt{K}\right),

where
B_{R}(x_{0})
is a geodesic ball with radius
R
and center
x_{0}\in M^{n}
. As a
consequence, any positive harmonic function on manifolds with non-negative Ricci
curvature must be constant. Later, in
[
LY86
]
, Li and Yau derived gradient
estimates for parabolic equations on Riemannian manifolds with or without boundary.
Moreover, they applied parabolic gradient estimates to study upper and lower bounds
of the heat kernel, eigenvalue estimates and Betti numbers estimates on manifolds.
Motivated by Li-Yau’s gradient estimate technique, in
[
Ham93
]
, Hamilton
established a new gradient estimate for the heat equation. His result may allow one
to compare the temperature of two different points at the same time on compact manifolds.
In
[
SZ06
]
, Souplet and Zhang extended Hamilton’s estimate to a localized version.
Their result enables the comparison of temperature distribution instantaneously,
without any lag in time, even for noncompact manifolds.

On the other hand, there exist some interesting works regarding to Riemannian manifolds
with boundary. In
[
Chen90
]
, Chen generalized Li-Yau gradient estimates to compact
Riemannian manifolds with possible non-convex boundary. As an application, he gave a
lower bound of the first Neumann eigenvalue in terms of geometric invariants with the
boundary. In
[
Wang97
]
, Wang developed Li-Yau gradient estimates for the heat
equation under Neumann boundary condition. As an application, he obtained upper and
lower bounds for the heat kernel satisfying Neumann boundary conditions on compact
manifolds with non-convex boundary. In
[
Hsu14
]
, Hsu proved global Li-Yau gradient
estimates for the conjugate heat equation on compact manifolds with boundary, whose
metric evolving by the Ricci flow. In
[
Oli19
]
, Olivé proved Li-Yau gradient
estimates for the heat equation, with Neumann boundary conditions, on a compact
Riemannian submanifold with boundary, satisfying some integral Ricci curvature
assumption. For more related works, we refer to
[
DKN18
,
DLT20
,
Wu15
]
and the
references therein.

Besides the above results related to Neumann boundary conditions, Kunikawa and Sakurai

[
KS20
]
recently proved Yau type gradient estimates for harmonic functions with
some Dirichlet boundary condition. More precisely, they proved that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
for some constant
K\geq 0
and the mean curvature of

\partial M
satisfies
H_{\partial M}\geq-(n-1)\sqrt{K}
.
Let
u:B_{R}(\partial M)\to(0,\infty)
be a positive harmonic function with Dirichlet
boundary condition (i.e., it is constant on the boundary). If the derivative
u_{\nu}

in the direction of the outward unit normal vector
\nu
is non-negative over
\partial M
,
then

\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\ \sqrt{K}\right).

Here
H_{\partial M}
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial M
and the Dirichlet boundary
condition means that
u
is constant on
\partial M
.

Kunikawa and Sakurai
[
KS20
]
further proved local Souplet-Zhang gradient
estimates for the heat equations with Dirichlet boundary condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}_{M}\geq-(n-1)K
and
H_{\partial M}\geq 0
. Let
0<u<A
for
some constant
A>0
, be a solution to the heat equation on

Q_{R,T}(\partial M):=B_{R}(\partial M)\times[-T,0]
. If
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary
condition, and
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
over
\partial M\times[-T,0]
, then

\sup\limits_{Q_{R/2,T/4}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\sqrt{K}\right)\left(1+\log\frac{A}{u}\right).

Inspired by the Kunikawa-Sakurai work, in this paper we will study gradient
estimates for weighted harmonic functions and weighted heat equation with Dirichlet boundary
condition. In particular, our results improve Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

on the manifold case.

Before stating our results, we fix some notations. A smooth metric measure space (also called
weighted manifold) is a triple
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
, where
(M,g)
is a complete
n
-dimensional
Riemannian manifold,
f
is a smooth real-valued function on
M
and
dv
is the volume form
with respect to the metric
g
. Denote by
\nabla
and
\Hess
the gradient and Hessian operators.
The weighted Laplacian is defined by
\Delta_{f}:=\Delta-\nabla f\cdot\nabla
. On
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
,
a natural generalization of the Ricci curvature is called
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature, defined by

{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}:={\rm Ric}+\Hess f-\frac{{\nabla f\otimes\nabla f}}{{m-n}},\quad n\leq m\leq\infty.

When
m=n
, we regard
f
to be constant and
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}={\rm Ric}
. When
m=\infty
,
we have
\infty
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}_{f}={\rm Ric}_{f}^{\infty}
. For a complete
manifold
M
with compact boundary
\partial M
, let
H_{f}:=H-\nabla f\cdot\nu
be weighted
mean curvature on
\partial M
, where
\nu
is the outer unit normal vector to
\partial M

(see e.g.
[
CMZ14
,
LW15
]
) and the mean curvature
H
is defined with respect to
\nu
.
But if
\nu
is the inner unit normal vector to
\partial M
, then
H_{f}:=H+\nabla f\cdot\nu
.
We refer the reader to
[
Sak19
]
for further discussions on
H_{f}
and weighted manifolds
with boundary.

Let us first state gradient estimates for the
f
-harmonic function with Dirichlet boundary
condition on
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
, which is a mild generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
be an
n
-dimensional smooth metric measure space with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)K
and
H_{f}\geq-L
for some constants
K,L\geq 0
. Let

u:B_{R}(\partial M)\to(0,\infty)
be a positive
f
-harmonic function (i.e.
\Delta_{f}u=0
)
with Dirichlet boundary condition. If the derivative
u_{\nu}
in the direction of the outward
unit normal vector
\nu
is non-negative over
\partial M
, then

\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{R}+L+\sqrt{K}\right).

We do not know if there exists a gradient estimate under a lower bound of
{\rm Ric}_{f}
.
Our present proof strongly depends on a refined Kato inequality in Lemma
2.3
,
which seems to be not true on weighted manifolds (see
[
RV13
]
). When
L=(m-1)\sqrt{K}

and
f
is constant, our result returns to Theorem
1.1
.

Second, we prove elliptic gradient estimates for the
f
-heat equation with Dirichlet
boundary condition on
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
by improving the argument of
[
DD19
]
,
which seems to be new even for manifolds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete smooth metric measure space with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}_{f}\geq-(n-1)K
for some constant
K\geq 0
and
H_{f}\geq 0
.
Let
0<u\leq A
for some constant
A>0
be a solution to the
f
-heat
equation
u_{t}=\Delta_{f}u
on
Q_{R,T}(\partial M):=B_{R}(\partial M)\times[-T,0]
.
If
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition (i.e.
u(\cdot,t)\mid_{\partial M}

is constant for each fixed
t\in[-T,0]
),
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
over

\partial M\times[-T,0]
, then

\sup\limits_{Q_{R/2,T/2}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{D}+1}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\sqrt{K}\right)\sqrt{1+\log\frac{A}{u}},

where
D=1+\log A-\log(\inf\limits_{Q_{R,T}(\partial M)}u)
.

As a consequence, we obtain the following Liouville theorems.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete smooth metric measure space with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}^{m}_{f}\geq 0
and
H_{f}\geq 0
. Let

u:M\to(0,\infty)
be a positive
f
-harmonic function
with Dirichlet boundary condition. If
u_{\nu}\geq 0
over
\partial M
, then

u
is constant.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete smooth metric measure space with compact boundary.
Assume
{\rm Ric}_{f}\geq 0
and
H_{f}\geq 0
. Let
u
be an ancient solution to
the
f
-heat equation.

1.

Suppose that
u>0
. If
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
on
\partial M\times(-\infty,0]
and

u(x,t)=e^{o(\rho(x)+{|t|})}
near infinity, then

is constant.

2.

If
u_{\nu}\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}u\leq 0
on
\partial M\times(-\infty,0]
and

u(x,t)={o(\rho(x)+{|t|})}
near infinity, then

is constant.

Here,
\rho(x)=\rho_{\partial M}(x)
denotes the Riemannian distance from the boundary.

Remark 1.7
.

We would like to emphasize that Corollary
1.6
is better than those in Corollary 1.7 in

[

]
. Because gradient estimates in Theorem

(also for manifolds)

Preliminaries

We start to introduce some basic facts. For a smooth metric measure space

(M^{n},g,e^{-f})dv
with boundary
\partial M
, the distance function from
the boundary is defined by

\rho:=\rho_{\partial M}:=d(\cdot,\partial M).

Note that
\rho
is smooth outside of the cut locus for the boundary (see
[
Sak17a
]
).
The Laplacian comparison for the distance function on smooth metric measure
spaces with boundary was first proved in
[
WZZ16
]
; see also
[
Sak19
]
.
Here, we give a general statement due to
[
Sak17b
]
.
We assume that
x\in B_{R}(\partial M)
, let
B(x,d(x,\partial M))\subset M
be the largest geodesic ball centered at
x
and
z=\partial B(x,d(x,\partial M))\cap\partial M
. Suppose that
\gamma_{z,x}(t)
is the geodesic curve starting from
z
and connecting
z
and
x
. We see that
\gamma^{\prime}_{z,x}(0)
is the unit inner normal vector for
\partial M
at
z
. Since
\gamma_{z,x}(d(x,\partial M))=x
and
d(x,\partial M)\leq R
, Lemma 6.1 in
[
Sak17b
]
implies the
Laplacian comparison theorem for
\infty
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature, which will be used in our proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Sak17b
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete smooth metric measure space with compact boundary
\partial M
. Assume that
{\rm Ric}_{f}\geq-K
and
H_{f}\geq-L
for some constants
K\geq 0
and
L\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\Delta_{f}\rho(x)\leq KR+L

for all
x\in B_{R}(\partial M)
.

Next, we recall the so-called Reilly formula (see
[
MD10
,
Li12
,
CMZ14
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
,

\displaystyle(|\varphi|^{2})_{\nu}=

\displaystyle 2\varphi_{\nu}(\Delta_{f}\varphi-\Delta_{\partial M,f}(\varphi\mid_{\partial M}-\varphi_{\nu}H_{f}))+2g_{\partial M}(\nabla_{\partial M}\varphi\mid_{\partial M},\nabla_{\partial M}\varphi_{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\rm II}(\nabla_{\partial M}\varphi\mid_{\partial M},\nabla_{\partial M}\varphi_{\partial M}).

For the
f
-harmonic function, we have a useful inequality, which was
proved in Theorem 2.2 of
[
Li05
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(see
[
Li05
]
)
.

Let
(M,g,e^{-f}dv)
be a smooth metric measure space with
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)K
. If
u
is a positive
f
-harmonic function and
m\geq n
, then
\phi:=|\nabla\log u|
satisfies

\Delta_{f}\phi\geq-(m-1)K\phi-2\frac{m-2}{m-1}\frac{\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle}{u}+\frac{\phi^{3}}{n-1}.

For the
f
-heat equation, we have another useful inequality, which was established in

[
DD19
]
for the manifold case.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under the same assumption as in Theorem
1.4
, let
w={{\left|\nabla h\right|}^{2}}
,
where
h=\sqrt{1+\log(A/u)}
. For any
\left(x,t\right)\in Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
,

(2.1)

\Delta_{f}w-{w_{t}}\geq-2(n-1)Kw+2\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right)\left\langle\nabla w,\nabla h\right\rangle+2\left(2+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\right){w^{2}}.

Proof..

Let
u(x,t)
be a solution to the
f
-heat equation and
0<u\leq A
for some constant
A
in

Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
. Let
B=Ae
and
h=\sqrt{\log(B/u)}=\sqrt{1+\log(A/u)}\geq 1
.
Then
u=Be^{-h^{2}}
and
\log u=\log B-{{h}^{2}}
. By the
f
-heat equation, we directly
compute that

(2.2)

{{h}_{t}}=\Delta_{f}h+|\nabla h|^{2}\left(\frac{1}{h}-2h\right).

On the other hand, by the Bochner formula and
\Ric_{f}\geq-(n-1)K
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w-w_{t}

\displaystyle=2|\nabla^{2}h|^{2}+2\Ric_{f}(\nabla h,\nabla h)+2\langle\nabla\Delta_{f}h,\nabla h\rangle-w_{t}

\displaystyle\geq-2(n-1)Kw+2\langle\nabla\Delta_{f}h,\nabla h\rangle-w_{t}.

By the equality (
2.2
), we obtain

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}w-{{w}_{t}}

\displaystyle\geq-2(n-1)Kw+2\left\langle\nabla\left({{h}_{t}}+{{\left|\nabla h\right|}^{2}}\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right)\right),\nabla h\right\rangle-{{w}_{t}}

\displaystyle\geq-2(n-1)Kw+2\left\langle\nabla\left({{h}_{t}}\right),\nabla h\right\rangle+2\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right)\left\langle\nabla\left({{\left|\nabla h\right|}^{2}}\right),\nabla h\right\rangle

\displaystyle+2{{\left|\nabla h\right|}^{2}}\left\langle\nabla\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right),\nabla h\right\rangle-{{w}_{t}}.

Observe that
2\langle\nabla(f_{t}),\nabla h\rangle=(|\nabla h|^{2})_{t}=w_{t}
,
and

\nabla\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right)=2\nabla h+\frac{\nabla h}{{{h}^{2}}}=\left(2+\frac{1}{{{h}^{2}}}\right)\nabla h.

Hence, (
2.3
) implies the desired inequality.
∎

3
Gradient estimates for harmonic functions

In this section we will follow an argument of
[
KS20
]

to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Define
G:=(R^{2}-\rho^{2})\phi
, where
\phi
is as in Lemma
2.3
. Assume
G

obtains its maximal at
x_{1}\in B_{R}(\partial M)
.

Case 1:
If
x_{1}\in B_{R}(\partial M)\setminus\partial M
, we
may assume that
x_{1}\notin{\rm Cut}(\partial M)
by the Calabi’s argument.
At
x_{1}
, we have

\nabla G=0
and
\Delta_{f}G\leq 0
. Consequently,

\frac{\nabla\rho^{2}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}}=\frac{\nabla\phi}{\phi},\quad-\frac{\Delta_{f}\rho^{2}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}}+\frac{\Delta_{f}\phi}{\phi}-\frac{2\left\langle\nabla\rho^{2},\nabla\phi\right\rangle}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2})\phi}\leq 0.

Hence

(3.1)

\frac{\Delta_{f}\phi}{\phi}-\frac{\Delta_{f}\rho^{2}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}}-2\frac{|\nabla\rho^{2}|^{2}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2}}\leq 0.

Since
{\rm Ric}_{f}^{m}\geq-(m-1)K
(and hence
{\rm Ric}_{f}\geq-(m-1)K
),
by the Laplacian comparison of Theorem
2.1
, we have

(3.2)

\Delta_{f}\rho^{2}=2|\nabla\rho|^{2}+2\rho\Delta_{f}\rho\leq 2+2\rho((m-1)KR+L)\leq 2+2LR+2(m-1)KR^{2},

where we used
|\nabla\rho|=1
and
\rho\leq R
.
Since
|\nabla\rho^{2}|=4\rho^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}=4\rho^{2}
, Lemma
2.3

together with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) implies

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Delta_{f}\phi}{\phi}-\frac{2+2LR+2(m-1)KR^{2}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}}-\frac{8\rho^{2}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-(m-1)K-\frac{2(m-2)}{m-1}\frac{\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle}{\phi u}+\frac{\phi^{2}}{m-1}

\displaystyle-\frac{2+2LR+2(m-1)KR^{2}}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}}-\frac{8\rho^{2}}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2}}.

at
x_{1}
. By the definition of
G
and

\frac{\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle}{\phi u}=\frac{2\rho\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla u\rangle}{(R^{2}-\rho^{2})u}\leq\frac{2\rho\phi}{R^{2}-\rho^{2}},

(
3.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G^{2}}{m-1}-\frac{4(m-2)}{m-1}\rho G-[2+2LR+2(m-1)KR^{2}](R^{2}-\rho^{2})-8\rho^{2}-(m-1)K(R^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G^{2}}{m-1}-\frac{4(m-2)}{m-1}RG-[2+2LR+2(m-1)KR^{2}]R^{2}-8R^{2}-(m-1)KR^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{G^{2}}{2(m-1)}-[(c_{m}+2LR)]R^{2}-3(m-1)KR^{4}

at
x_{1}
, where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. This gives

G(x_{1})\leq c_{m}\left(\sqrt{1+LR}\,R+\sqrt{K}R^{2}\right).

By the definition of
G
, we conclude

\frac{3R^{2}}{4}\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq c_{m}\left(\sqrt{1+LR}\,R+\sqrt{K}R^{2}\right)

and the desired result follows.

Case 2:
If
x_{1}\in\partial M
, the proof follows by the argument of
[
KS20
]

which is originated in
[
LY79
]
. We include it for the completeness.
Indeed, at
x_{1}
,
G_{\nu}\geq 0
,
\phi_{\nu}=\frac{G_{\nu}}{R^{2}}\geq 0
and hence

(\phi^{2})_{\nu}\geq 0
. Since
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition and

u_{\nu}\geq 0
, then
|\nabla u|=u_{\nu}
. We note that

\phi=|\nabla u|
and
\log u
also satisfies Dirichlet boundary condition. So
Proposition
2.2
gives

\displaystyle 0\leq(\phi^{2})_{\nu}

\displaystyle=(|\nabla\log u|^{2})_{\nu}=2(\log u)_{\nu}(\Delta_{f}\log u-(\log u)_{\nu}H_{f})

\displaystyle=2\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}\left(-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}H_{f}\right)=2\frac{(u_{\nu})^{2}}{u^{2}}\left(-\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}-H_{f}\right),

which implies

\phi(x_{1})=\frac{u_{\nu}}{u}\leq-H_{f}\leq L.

Hence
G(x_{1})=R^{2}\phi(x_{1})\leq LR^{2}
. This gives

\frac{3R^{2}}{4}\sup\limits_{B_{R/2}(\partial M)}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq LR^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Gradient estimates for heat equations

To prove Theorem

, we introduce a smooth cut-off function as in

]
.

]
(see also
[

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a smooth cut-off function
\psi=\psi(x,t)
supported in

such that

(i)

\psi=\psi\left(\rho_{\partial M}(x),t\right)\equiv\psi\left(r,t\right);\psi\left(r,t\right)=1

in

(iii)

\left|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}\right|\frac{1}{{{\psi}^{{1}/{2}\;}}}\leq\frac{C}{T}
,

\left|\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}\right|\leq\frac{{{C}_{\varepsilon}}{{\psi}^{\varepsilon}}}{{{R}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\frac{{{\partial}^{2}}\psi}{{{\partial}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}}\right|\leq\frac{{{C}_{\varepsilon}}{{\psi}^{\varepsilon}}}{{{R}^{2}}},\quad 0<\varepsilon<1.

Using the cut-off function, we have

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\Phi:=\psi(\Delta_{f}w-{w_{t}})+w\Delta_{f}\psi-w{{\psi}_{t}}-2\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{\psi}w
, then

\psi{w^{2}}\leq c\left(\frac{D^{2}+1}{R^{4}}+\frac{1}{T^{2}}+K^{2}\right)+\frac{\Phi}{4},

where

. Here

denotes a constant
depending only on

whose value may change from line to line in the following.

Proof..

Plugging (
2.1
) into
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle\geq-2(n-1)K\psi w-2\left(2h-\frac{1}{h}\right)\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\psi\right\rangle w

\displaystyle\quad+2\left(2+\frac{1}{{{h}^{2}}}\right)\psi{{w}^{2}}+w{{\Delta_{f}}}\psi-w{{\psi}_{t}}-2\frac{{{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{2}}}{\psi}w.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle 4\psi{{w}^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}2(n-1)K\psi w-\frac{4h\left(1-2{{h}^{2}}\right)}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\psi\right\rangle w

\displaystyle-\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}w{{\Delta_{f}}}\psi+\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}w{{\psi}_{t}}+\frac{4{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\frac{{{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{2}}}{\psi}w+\Phi.

Since
0<\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\leq 1
and
0<\frac{2}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\leq 2
, we get

(4.1)

4\psi{w^{2}}\leq 2(n-1)K\psi w-\frac{4h\left(1-2{h^{2}}\right)}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\langle\nabla h,\nabla\psi\rangle w-\frac{2h^{2}}{1+2h^{2}}w\Delta_{f}\psi+w|{{\psi}_{t}}|+\frac{2{{|\nabla\psi|}^{2}}}{\psi}w+\Phi.

In the following we will estimate each term of the right hand side of (
4.1
).
Since
\Ric_{f}\geq-(n-1)K
and
H_{f}\geq 0
, by the Laplacian comparison in Theorem
2.1
,
\Delta_{f}\rho\leq(n-1)KR
. Using this and Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}w\Delta_{f}\psi

\displaystyle=-\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}w\left({{\psi}_{r}}\Delta_{f}\rho+{{\psi}_{rr}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2{{h}^{2}}}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}w\left(|{\psi}_{r}|(n-1)KR+|{\psi}_{rr}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq{{\psi}^{1/2\ }}w\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{rr}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{1/2\ }}}+(n-1)KR{{\psi}^{1/2}}w\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{r}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{1/2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+c\left[{{\left(\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{rr}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{1/2\ }}}\right)}^{2}}+\left((n-1)KR\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{r}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{1/2\ }}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+\frac{c}{{{R}^{4}}}+cK^{2}.

By the Young inequality, we also have

(4.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{4h\left(1-2{{h}^{2}}\right)}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\psi\right\rangle w

\displaystyle\leq 4h\frac{\left|1-2{{h}^{2}}\right|}{1+2{{h}^{2}}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|\left|\nabla h\right|w

\displaystyle\leq 4h\left|\nabla\psi\right|{{w}^{3/2}}=4h\left|\nabla\psi\right|{{\psi}^{-3/4\ }}{{\left(\psi{{w}^{2}}\right)}^{3/4\ }}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+ch^{4}\frac{{{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{4}}}{{{\psi}^{3}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+\frac{c{{D}^{2}}}{{{R}^{4}}},

where
D:=\log B-\log(\underset{Q_{R,T}(\partial M)}{\mathop{\inf}}\,u)
.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is not hard to see that the following estimates hold.
We first estimate that

(4.4)

2(n-1)K\psi w\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+{4cK^{2}};

for
w\left|{{\psi}_{t}}\right|
, we estimate that

(4.5)

w\left|{{\psi}_{t}}\right|={{\psi}^{{1}/{2}\;}}w\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{t}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{{1}/{2}\;}}}\leq\frac{3}{5}{{\left({{\psi}^{{1}/{2}\;}}w\right)}^{2}}+c{{\left(\frac{\left|{{\psi}_{t}}\right|}{{{\psi}^{{1}/{2}\;}}}\right)}^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{{w}^{2}}+\frac{c}{T^{2}};

we also estimate that

(4.6)

\frac{2|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{\psi}w=2\left({{|\nabla\psi|}^{2}}{{\psi}^{-3/2}}\right)\left({{\psi}^{1/2\;}}w\right)\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{w^{2}}+c\frac{{{|\nabla\psi|}^{4}}}{{{\psi}^{3}}}\leq\frac{3}{5}\psi{w^{2}}+\frac{c}{{R^{4}}}.

We substitute (
4.2
)-(
4.6
) into the right hand side of (
4.1
), and get that

\psi w^{2}\leq c\left(\frac{D^{2}+1}{R^{4}}+\frac{1}{T^{2}}+K^{2}\right)+\frac{\Phi}{4}.

and the result follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
by improving the argument of
[
KS20
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We may assume
u
is non-constant in
Q_{R/2,T/2}
. If
u
is constant,
it is trivial. Hence we let
\psi w
be a positive function at a point in
Q_{R/2,T/2}(\partial M)

and achieves its maximal value at
\left(x_{1},t_{1}\right)
in
Q_{R/2,T/2}(\partial M)
.
We first claim
x_{1}\not\in\partial M
. Indeed, by contradiction, assume that
x_{1}\in\partial M
.
Then at
(x_{1},t_{1})
,
(\psi w)_{\nu}\geq 0
, consequently
\psi_{\nu}w+\psi w_{\nu}=\psi w_{\nu}\geq 0
;
in particular,
w_{\nu}\geq 0
. Since
w=|\nabla\sqrt{\log(B/u)}|^{2}
, and
h=\sqrt{\log(B/u)}
also
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition, Proposition
2.2
implies

0\leq w_{\nu}=(|\nabla{h}|^{2})_{\nu}=2{h}_{\nu}(\Delta_{f}h-h_{\nu}H_{f})
.
Since
u
satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition, then
|\nabla u|=u_{\nu}
. Hence,

h_{\nu}=-\frac{u_{\nu}}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}=-\frac{|\nabla u|}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}=-w^{1/2}.

We now compute

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}h

\displaystyle={\rm div}\left(-\frac{\nabla u}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}\right)-\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta u}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla\left(\frac{1}{u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla f,\frac{\nabla u}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta_{f}u}{2u\ \sqrt{\log(B/u)}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}\sqrt{\log(B/u)}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}\left(\log(B/u)\right)^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{u_{t}}{2uh}+\left(\frac{2h^{2}-1}{h}\right)w.

Plugging these identities into the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-2w^{1/2}\left(-\frac{u_{t}}{2uh}+\left(\frac{2h^{2}-1}{h}\right)w+w^{1/2}H_{f}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-2w\left(\left(\frac{2h^{2}-1}{h}\right)w^{1/2}+H_{f}\right)

where we used
u_{t}\leq 0
in the last inequality. This implies

\left(\frac{2h^{2}-1}{h}\right)w^{1/2}\leq-H_{f}\leq 0.

Since
h\geq 1
,
w=0
at
(x_{1},t_{1})
. This means
\psi w\equiv 0
on
Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
. This is a contracdiction.

We have shown that
x_{1}\not\in\partial M
. By the standard argument of Calabi (see
[
Cal57
]
), we may assume that
x_{1}\not\in\partial M\cup{\rm Cut}(\partial M)
. Now at
\left(x_{1},t_{1}\right)
, Lemma
4.2
gives

(4.7)

w^{2}\leq c\left(\frac{D^{2}+1}{R^{4}}+\frac{1}{T^{2}}+K^{2}\right)+\frac{\Phi}{4}.

On the other hand, since
(x_{1},t_{1})
is a maximal point, we have

\nabla(\psi w)=0
,
\Delta_{f}(\psi w)\leq 0
and
(\psi w)_{t}\geq 0

at
(x_{1},t_{1})
. Thus, at
(x_{1},t_{1})
,

0\geq{{\Delta_{f}}}\left(\psi w\right)-{{\left(\psi w\right)}_{t}}=\psi\left({{\Delta_{f}}}w-{{w}_{t}}\right)+w\left({{\Delta_{f}}}\psi-{{\psi}_{t}}\right)+2\left\langle\nabla w,\nabla\psi\right\rangle,

that is,
\Phi(x_{1},t_{1})\leq 0
. Hence, (
4.7
) implies

(\psi w)(x,t)\leq(\psi w)(x_{1},t_{1})\leq c^{1/2}\left(\frac{D+1}{R^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}+K\right)

for all
(x,t)\in Q_{R,T}(\partial M)
. Since
\psi\equiv 1
in
Q_{R/2,T/2}(\partial M)
, by the definition of
w
, we get

\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}\leq 2c^{1/4}\left(\frac{\sqrt{D}+1}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}+\sqrt{K}\right)\sqrt{1+\log\frac{A}{u}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary
1.5
follows by letting
R\to\infty
. So we only give a proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Now
K=L=0
and
u_{t}=\Delta_{f}u
. Let
v=u+1
an then
v_{t}=\Delta_{f}v
. Moreover,
u
and
v
have
the same growth at infinity. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume
u\geq 1
.
Fix
(x_{0},t_{0})
. We apply Theorem
1.4
to
Q_{R,R}(\partial M)=B_{R}(\partial M)\times[t_{0}-R,t_{0}]

and get

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}(x_{0},t_{0})

\displaystyle\leq c_{m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\log A}}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}\right)\sqrt{1+\log\frac{A}{u(x_{0},t_{0})}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{o(R+|R|)}}{R}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}\right)\sqrt{o(R+|R|)-\log(u(x_{0},t_{0}))}.

Letting
R\to\infty
, we get
|\nabla u(x_{0},t_{0})|=0
and
u
is constant because
(x_{0},t_{0})
is arbitrary.
The proof of (1) is complete.

The proof of (2) is similar as in
[
SZ06
]
, we omit the details.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In Theorem 4.2 of
[
WZZ16
]
, the authors proved that
\rho
is finite
if some extra conditions on
K
and
L
are given. We point out that our gradient
estimates and Liouville theorems work well in this case. In fact, if
\rho
is finite,
then all boundary balls
B_{\partial M}(R)
are the same if
R
is large. The proof
still work without any change. Therefore, we can let
R\to\infty
even when
\rho
is finite.

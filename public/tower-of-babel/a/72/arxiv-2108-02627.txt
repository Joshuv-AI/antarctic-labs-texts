Lie theory and cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng, Chenchang Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a local Lie theory for relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 . First we recall the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 on Lie algebras and construct a cohomology theory for them. We use the second cohomology group to study infinitesimal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators and modified r r -matrices. Then we introduce a cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie group. We construct the differentiation functor from the category of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups to that on Lie algebras, and extend it to the cohomology level by proving the Van Est theorem between the two cohomology theories. We integrate a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1 on a Lie algebra to a local relative Rota-Baxter operator on the corresponding Lie group, and show that the local integration and differentiation are adjoint to each other. Finally, we give two applications of our integration of Rota-Baxter operators: one is to give an explicit formula for the factorization problem, and the other is to provide an integration for matched pairs.



1.
Introduction

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter and they are applied in the
Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization of quantum
field theory
[
7
,
15
]
. In the corresponding semi-classical world, the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators) on Lie algebras was introduced in
[
19
]
, and they are closely related to various classical Yang-Baxter equations. Moreover, from a more algebraic approach, a relative Rota-Baxter operator naturally gives rise to a pre-Lie or a post-Lie
algebra, and play important roles in mathematical
physics
[
2
,
5
]
.

A Rota-Baxter operator of weight 0 on a Lie
algebra is naturally the operator form of a classical skew-symmetric

r
-matrix
[
31
]
. Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 are in one-to-one correspondence with
solutions of the modified Yang-Baxter equation (see Remark

2.3
), and give rise to factorizations of Lie algebras. Integrating such factorizations locally to the level of Lie groups provides natural solutions of a certain Hamiltonian system, which is of great interest for the people coming from integrable
systems
[
31
]
. In a recent work
[
16
]
, the notion of a Rota-Baxter operator on a
Lie group was introduced. In particular, one can obtain a Rota-Baxter
operator of weight 1 on a Lie algebra by taking the differentiation of a
Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie group.

Rota-Baxter operators are
special cases of relative Rota-Baxter operators, where we distinguish
the source and the target of the operator (see Def.
2.1
and

3.1
). In this article, we further study the
differentiation and integration problem for relative Rota-Baxter
operators, and explore their cohomology theory. We find such a
separation of the source and the target makes the cohomology theory and Lie
theory actually much more clear. Differentiation on the level of
cohomology, namely a version of Van-Est theorem, is also given. We
show that differentiation and local integration work for relative
Rota-Baxter operators. Moreover, these two procedures are adjoint to
each other. Our results in particular imply that we may integrate a
Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra to a local Rota-Baxter operator
on a Lie group in a functorial way. This seems to provide some
global geometrical insight to the above mentioned problem in integrable
systems. Moreover, via our integration, we are able to provide an explicit formula for the factorization problem on the level of Lie groups.

Differentiation works without much obstruction, and follows
from adapted Lie theoretical calculations. Integration, even local
integration, is however, a bit tricky: the relative Rota-Baxter
operator is not a Lie algebra or a Lie group homomorphism. To integrate such a map, we
turn to its graph, which has a relative good Lie theoretical property
but not completely compatible with exponential maps. Nevertheless,
a bit luck happens, and the non-compatibleness somehow falls in the kernel
of the operator and the local integration works. To really achieve the global integration, we expect some topological obstruction. In fact, we have already faced this when we try to integrate an infinite dimensional Lie algebra
[
26
,
38
]
, where the obstruction lies in second cohomology groups; and when we try to integrate a Lie algebroid
[
9
]
to a Lie groupoid. On the other hand, we might also use Malcev’s method to extend local structures to global ones
[
24
]
. As therein, an obstruction in algebraic terms would show up. Nevertheless, it turns out that the algebraic obstruction therein is actually topological in nature
[
13
]
. Thus, the local integration and some topological invariants obtained in this article should be good ingredients for the global integration in the next step.

We notice that the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter
operators of weight 0 on Lie algebras, together with those on associative
algebras have been much studied recently
[
10
,
11
,
21
,
33
]
. Also there are some further progresses on Rota-Baxter operators on groups in recently works
[
3
,
4
,
14
]
.

A byproduct of our cohomology theory is to provide a classification of
deformation of modified
r
-matrices, which seems to be easier to
carry out from the operator point of view.

Now we describe our results in the time order: we first show that a relative Rota-Baxter operator

of weight 1 on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

gives rise to a representation of the descendent

controlling algebra of the relative Rota-Baxter operator

define the

to be the
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the corresponding descendent Lie

cohomology group to study infinitesimal deformations of relative

r
-matrices. Then parallelly, we show that

In Section

In Section

In Section

2.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and their
cohomology theory

In this section, we define the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 on Lie algebras. As applications, we classify infinitesimal deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 on Lie algebras using the second cohomology group.

2.1.
The category
\mathsf{RB_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{h}}}
of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras

In the sequel,
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
are Lie algebras. Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be a Lie algebra
homomorphism. We call
\phi
an
action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie
algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. A linear map
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect
to
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
if

(1)

[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=B\Big(\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}
and the action is the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, then
B
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the context of Lie algebras, usually one considers relative
Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda
, where
\lambda
is a
parameter in the front of
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}}
in (
1
). However, in the
context of Lie groups, one can only consider relative Rota-Baxter
operators of weight
\pm 1
due to the fact that there is no linear
structure on Lie groups in general. Thus, in this paper we
consider only relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
, and call
them simply relative Rota-Baxter operators.

Remark 2.3
.

A Rota-Baxter operator
B
on
\mathfrak{g}
one-to-one corresponds to a solution of the
modified
Yang-Baxter equation
, which in turn has important applications
in integrable systems. We now recall some facts
from
[
18
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
.
Let
R:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a solution of the modified Yang-Baxter equation:

(2)

[R(u),R(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R([R(u),v]_{\mathfrak{g}})+R([u,R(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Each such a solution (which we call a
modified

r
-matrix
) gives rise to a so-called infinitesimal
factorization of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The authors integrated this infinitesimal factorization
locally into a factorization of the Lie group
G
of
\mathfrak{g}
. The
integrated factorization in turn
gives an elegant solution of a certain hamiltonian system for small
time
t
. The factorization itself may also be viewed as some version
of Riemann-Hilbert problem.

Under the transformation

R={\rm{Id}}+2B,

the operator
R
satisfies the modified Yang-Baxter equation if and only if the operator
B
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{up}(2;\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathrm{up}(2;\mathbb{R})
is the set of upper triangular matrices and
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{R}
. Define
B:\mathbb{R}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{up}(2;\mathbb{R})
and
\phi:\mathrm{up}(2;\mathbb{R})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{R})
by

B(r)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&r\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\phi(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
0&z\\
\end{array}\right))r=xr,\quad\forall\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
0&z\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{up}(2;\mathbb{R}),r\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
B
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action
(\mathbb{R};\phi)
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Define a skewsymmetric bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
by

(3)

[x+u,y+v]_{\phi}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi(x)v-\phi(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then it is straightforward to see that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
is a Lie algebra, and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then a linear map
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
Gr(B)=\{B(u)+u|u\in\mathfrak{h}\}
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

Let
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map.
For all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},
we have

\displaystyle[B(u)+u,B(v)+v]_{\phi}=[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

which implies that the graph
Gr(B)=\{B(u)+u|u\in\mathfrak{h}\}
is a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
if and only if
B
satisfies

\displaystyle[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=B\Big(\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

which means that
B
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

Since
\mathfrak{h}
and the graph

Gr(B)
are isomorphic as vector spaces, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
. Then
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
is a Lie algebra, called
the descendent Lie algebra
of
B
, where

(4)

[u,v]_{B}=\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Moreover,
B
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
B
and
B^{\prime}
be relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
. A
homomorphism
from
B^{\prime}
to
B
consists of a Lie algebra homomorphism
\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
and a Lie algebra homomorphism
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{h}},

(6)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},v\in\mathfrak{h}.

In particular, if both
\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}
are invertible,
(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}},\psi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is called an isomorphism from
B^{\prime}
to
B
.

In fact, (
6
) is equivalent that
(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}},\psi_{\mathfrak{h}})
is an endomorphism of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\phi}\mathfrak{h}
.

It is straightforward to see that relative Rota-Baxter operators on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
together with homomorphisms between them form a category, which we denote by
\mathsf{RB_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{h}}}
.

2.2.
A cohomology theory in
\mathsf{RB_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{h}}}

We first prove some statements which are important for us to build up
our cohomology theory in a functorial way.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
. Define a linear map
\theta:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

(7)

\theta(u)x=B(\phi(x)u)+[B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then
\theta
is a representation of the descendent Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

By the fact that
\phi(x)\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator, for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle[\theta(u),\theta(v)]x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(u)\theta(v)x-\theta(v)\theta(u)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(u)(B(\phi(x)v))+\theta(u)[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\theta(v)(B(\phi(x)u))-\theta(v)[B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\phi(B(\phi(x)v))u)+[B(u),B(\phi(x)v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B(\phi([B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})u)+[B(u),[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-B(\phi(B(\phi(x)u))v)-[B(v),B(\phi(x)u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-B(\phi([B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v)-[B(v),[B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\Big(\phi(B(u))\phi(x)v-\phi(B(\phi(x)v))u+[u,\phi(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle+B(\phi(B(\phi(x)v))u)+B(\phi([B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})u)+[B(u),[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-B\Big(\phi(B(v))\phi(x)u-\phi(B(\phi(x)u))v+[v,\phi(x)u]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle-B(\phi(B(\phi(x)u))v)-B(\phi([B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v)-[B(v),[B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\phi(B(u))\phi(x)v)+B(\phi([B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})u)-B(\phi(B(v))\phi(x)u)-B(\phi([B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}})v)

\displaystyle+B([u,\phi(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}})-B([v,\phi(x)u]_{\mathfrak{h}})+[B(u),[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[B(v),[B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\Big(\phi(x)\phi(B(u))v-\phi(x)\phi(B(v))u+\phi(x)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)+[[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}},x]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(\phi(x)[u,v]_{B})+[B([u,v]_{B}),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta([u,v]_{B})x.

Thus
\theta
is a representation of the descendent Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
B:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
. Then

\phi(x)[u,v]_{B}=[\phi(x)u,v]_{B}+[u,\phi(x)v]_{B}+\phi(\theta(v)x)u-\phi(\theta(u)x)v,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

For any
u,v\in\mathfrak{h},x\in\mathfrak{g}
, by Proposition
2.8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi(x)[u,v]_{B}-([\phi(x)u,v]_{B}+[u,\phi(x)]_{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)\phi(B(u))v-\phi(x)\phi(B(v))u+\phi(x)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle-(\phi(B(\phi(x)u))v-\phi(B(v))\phi(x)u+[\phi(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle-(\phi(B(u))\phi(x)v-\phi(B(\phi(x)v))u+[u,\phi(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi(B(v)),\phi(x)]u+\phi(B(\phi(x)v))u-[\phi(B(u)),\phi(x)]v-\phi(B(\phi(x)u))v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B(\phi(x)v))u-\phi([B(u),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B(\phi(x)u))v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\theta(v)x)u-\phi(\theta(u)x)v,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Let
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{B}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+1}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
be the corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the descendent Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
with coefficients in
(\mathfrak{g};\theta)
. More precisely, for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}^{B}f(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\theta(u_{i})f(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{k+1})+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}f([u_{i},u_{j}]_{B},u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u_{i}},\cdots,\hat{u_{j}},\cdots,u_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}B(\phi(f(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{k+1}))u_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}[B(u_{i}),f(u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u_{i}},\cdots,u_{k+1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}f(\phi(B(u_{i}))u_{j}-\phi(B(u_{j}))u_{i}+[u_{i},u_{j}]_{\mathfrak{h}},u_{1},\cdots,\hat{u_{i}},\cdots,\hat{u_{j}},\cdots,u_{k+1}).

Definition 2.10
.

Let

It implies in particular that

\displaystyle B(\phi(f(u_{2}))u_{1})+[B(u_{1}),f(u_{2})]_{\mathfrak{g}}-B(\phi(f(u_{1}))u_{2})-[B(u_{2}),f(u_{1})]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle f(\phi(B(u_{1}))u_{2}-\phi(B(u_{2}))u_{1}+[u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}).

Remark 2.11
.

The controlling algebra of relative
Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie algebras was given in

d

\Theta(h)g=(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h^{{\dagger}}))^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h^{{\dagger}}),\quad\forall g\in G,h\in H.

Then
\Theta
is an action of the descendent Lie group
(H,e_{H},\star)
on the manifold

.

Proof.

For all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
and
g\in G
, by Lemma

\displaystyle\Theta(h_{1})\Theta(h_{2})g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta(h_{1})\Big(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\Phi\Big(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\Big)h_{1}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{1}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(\Phi\Big(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\Big)h_{1}^{{\dagger}})\Big)^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}}\star h_{1}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}}\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}}))\Phi\Big(\mathcal{B}(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\Big)h_{1}^{{\dagger}}\Big)^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}}\star h_{1}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(\Phi(g)h_{2}^{{\dagger}}\cdot_{H}\Phi\Big(g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}})\Big)h_{1}^{{\dagger}}\Big)^{-1}\cdot_{G}g\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(h_{2}^{{\dagger}}\star h_{1}^{{\dagger}})

\displaystyle\Theta(h_{1}\star h_{2})g.

(H,e_{H},\star)
on the manifold

.
∎

Since
\Theta(h)\in\text{Diff}(G)
for all

\Theta(h_{1}\star h_{2})_{*e_{G}}=\Theta(h_{1})_{*e_{G}}\Theta(h_{2})_{*e_{G}}
. Thus we obtain a Lie group homomorphism from the descendent Lie group
(H,e_{H},\star)
to
GL(\mathfrak{g})
, which is also denoted by
\Theta:H\,\rightarrow\,GL(\mathfrak{g})
. Consequently, we have the following result which plays important roles in our definition of the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups.

Lemma 3.9
.

With the above notations,
\Theta:H\,\rightarrow\,GL(\mathfrak{g})
is an action of the descendent Lie group
(H,e_{H},\star)
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now we are ready to define a cohomology theory for relative

\displaystyle\Pi(g_{1})\alpha_{n}(g_{2},\cdots,g_{n},g_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\alpha_{n}(g_{1},\cdots,g_{n})

In this section, we establish the differentiation functor for relative
Rota-Baxter operators and the Van Est homomorphism on the cohomology
level. The classical Van Est isomorphism
[

]
gives the
relation between the smooth cohomology of Lie groups and the
cohomology of Lie algebras. See
[
1
,
6
,

,

,

,

for various Van Est type theorems.

4.1.
The differentiation functor for relative
Rota-Baxter operators

Let
\Phi
be an action of a Lie group
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
on a manifold
M
. Define
\Delta:G\times M\rightarrow M
by
\Delta(g,m)=\Phi(g)m
. Then

\Delta(e_{G},m)=m
and
\Delta(g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2},m)=\Delta(g_{1},\Delta(g_{2},m))
, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G,m\in M
. Moreover, we have the following Leibniz rule,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Delta(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Delta(\gamma_{1}(0),\gamma_{2}(t))+\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Delta(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Phi(\gamma_{1}(t))\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Phi(\gamma_{1}(0))\gamma_{2}(t)+\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\Phi(\gamma_{1}(t))\gamma_{2}(0),

where
\gamma_{1}(t)
is a path in
G
with
\gamma_{1}(0)=g
and
\gamma_{2}(t)
is a path in
M
with
\gamma_{2}(0)=m
.

Let
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
be Lie groups and denote by
\exp_{G}
and
\exp_{H}
the exponential maps for the Lie groups
(G,e_{G},\cdot_{G})
and
(H,e_{H},\cdot_{H})
respectively.
Let
\Phi:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(H)
be an action of
G
on
H
. Since

\Phi(g)\in\mathrm{Aut}(H)
for all
g\in G
, then
\Phi(g)_{*e_{H}}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. By
\Phi(g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2})=\Phi(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\Phi(g_{2})
, we have
\Phi(g_{1}\cdot_{G}g_{2})_{*e_{H}}=\Phi(g_{1})_{*e_{H}}\Phi(g_{2})_{*e_{H}}
. Thus we obtain a Lie
group homomorphism from
G
to
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
, which we denote by

\tilde{\Phi}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
.
Then taking the differential, we obtain a Lie algebra homomorphism

\phi:=\tilde{\Phi}_{*e_{G}}
from the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
. We call

\phi
the differentiated action of
\Phi
. In fact, Lie II theorem tells us that

\mathrm{Aut}(H)\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
, if
H
is connected and simply
connected. Therefore when both
G
and
H

are connected and simply connected, given a Lie algebra action
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, there is a unique integrated action
\Phi:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(H)

whose differentiation is
\phi
. This procedure can be well explained by the following diagram:

(20)

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}(H)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
differentiation
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\Phi}}

\textstyle{\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\phi=\tilde{\Phi}_{*e_{G}}}

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\,\rightarrow\,G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
G
with respect to an action
(H;\Phi)
. Define

by

B=\mathcal{B}_{*e_{H}},

which is the tangent map of
\mathcal{B}
at the identity
e_{H}
. Then
B

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the

(\mathfrak{h};\phi)
, where
\phi
is the differentiated action
of
\Phi
defined in (

Proof.

For all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\exp_{G}(tB(u))\cdot_{G}\exp_{G}(sB(v))\cdot_{G}\exp_{G}(-tB(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv))\cdot_{G}(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv))\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))^{-1})\exp_{H}(-tu)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathcal{B}\Big(\exp_{H}(tu)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))\exp_{H}(sv)\Big)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))^{-1})\exp_{H}(-tu)\Big)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathcal{B}\Big(\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp_{H}(tu)}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))\exp_{H}(sv))\cdot_{H}(\exp_{H}(tu)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp_{H}(tu)}(\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))\exp_{H}(sv))\cdot_{H}(\exp_{H}(tu)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Big(\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp_{H}(tu)}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))\exp_{H}(sv)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\exp_{H}(tu)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\Big(\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\mathrm{Ad}_{\exp_{H}(tu)}\exp_{H}(sv)+\frac{d^{2}}{dtds}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu)))\exp_{H}(sv)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dsdt}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\exp_{H}(tu)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\Big([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}(u))v\Big)+\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dsdt}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(tu))}\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\Big([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}(u))v\Big)+\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\frac{d^{2}}{dsdt}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Phi(\mathcal{B}(\exp_{H}(sv)))\exp_{H}(-tu)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}\Big([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}(u))v\Big)-\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}(\phi(\mathcal{B}_{*e_{G}}(v))u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(B(u))v-\phi(B(v))u).

Thus

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}

with respect to an action
(H;\Phi)

We denote

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{s,t=0}\Psi_{H}(\Phi(\exp_{G}(sx))\exp_{H}(tu))=\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}\frac{d}{ds}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Phi(\exp_{G}(sx))\exp_{H}(tu)=\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi(x)u)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t,s=0}\Phi(\exp_{G}(s\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)))\exp_{H}(t\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u))

\displaystyle\phi(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

respectively,
then the above differentiation procedure gives us a functor

\mathbf{\mathfrak{D}}:\mathsf{RB_{G}^{H}}\to\mathsf{RB_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathfrak{h}}}.

.

and

and

and

\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Let

H^{1}(\mathfrak{g}/{\mathfrak{so}(n)},\mathfrak{g}^{{\mathfrak{so}(n)}})=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{R}^{n},{\mathfrak{so}(n)})\cong\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n^{2}(n-1)}{2}}.

Therefore, we have

H^{2}(\mathbf{\mathfrak{D}}(\mathcal{B}))=H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathbb{R}^{\frac{n^{2}(n-1)}{2}}.

5.
Local integration of relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a local relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie group and show that any relative Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra can be integrated to a local relative Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie group integrating the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Phi:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)

Let

Let

We consider the semi-direct product Lie group

\mathcal{B}(P_{H}(\mathrm{Exp}(B(u),u)))=\exp_{G}B(u).

2

Geometric applications

In this section, we give some geometric applications including the explicit expression of the local factorization of a Lie group given in
[

6.1.
Local descendent Lie group

As in the global case, a local Rota-Baxter operator also
determines a local descendent Lie group. This turns out to be important to our geometric application. Thus we make this construction here in this subsection.

While the definition of a Lie
group is standard, the definition of a local Lie group varies
slightly. Thus we first fix our definition which is taken mostly from the classical one in
[
29
]
, and however with a slight modification similar to the more recent paper
[
27
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

(
[
29
,
27
]
)

A
local Lie group

L
is a manifold equipped with an open set
V\subset L
together with

(i)

a multiplication map
m:V\times V\to L
, which satisfies
g_{1}(g_{2}g_{3})=(g_{1}g_{2})g_{3}
, as long as
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{1}g_{2},g_{2}g_{3}\in V
(here we write
m(g_{1},g_{2})
as
g_{1}g_{2}
for short),

(ii)

an identity element
e\in V
, which satisfies
eg=ge=g
, for all
g\in V
,

(iii)

an inverse map
i:V\to V
, such that
i(V)=V
, and
g^{-1}g=e=gg^{-1}
(here we write
i(g)
as
g^{-1}
for short).

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:U\to G
be a
local Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie group
(G,e,\cdot)
. Then it gives rise to a local Lie group structure on
G:

\bullet

the multiplication
\star
is given by

(39)

g_{1}\star g_{2}:=g_{1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g_{1})\cdot g_{2}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g_{1})^{-1},

\bullet

the identity element is
e
, which is the identity element of the Lie group
G
,

\bullet

the inverse
g^{\dagger}
of
g
is given by

(40)

g^{\dagger}:=\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g).

Proof.

Notice that the inverse is defined from
U
to
G
, thus we may take
V:=U\cap U^{\dagger}
. Clearly
V
is an open set of
G
containing
e
. Note that
\star:U\times U\to G
is well-defined, thus
\star
is well-defined also on
V\times V
.

If
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{1}\star g_{2},g_{2}\star g_{3}\in V
, then the same calculation (
16
) for proving the associativity in Proposition
3.5
still goes through because all the relevant elements are in
V
which is within the definition domain of
\mathcal{B}
.

By (
32
), for all
g\in U
, we have
\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot\mathcal{B}(g^{\dagger})=\mathcal{B}(g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot g^{\dagger}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1})=\mathcal{B}(e)=e
. This shows that
\mathcal{B}(g^{\dagger})=\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1}
. Thus

(41)

(g^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot(g^{\dagger})^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1}=g.

Therefore,
(U^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=U
, which implies that
(V)^{\dagger}=V
. Moreover, for all
g\in V
,
g^{\dagger}\star g=e=g\star g^{\dagger}
follows from the same calculation (
15
) in Proposition
3.5
because
\mathcal{B}(g^{\dagger})=\mathcal{B}(g)^{-1}
still holds for
g\in U
. Thus
(V\subset G,\star,{\dagger})
is a local Lie group.
∎

This local Lie group is called the
descendent local Lie group
of
\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 6.3
.

Similar to the case of the descendent Lie group, it is clear that

•

the Lie algebra of the descendent local Lie group
(V\subset G,\star,{\dagger})
is the descendent Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
, where

•

\mathcal{B}:(V\subset G,\star,{\dagger})\to G

6.2.
Application in the local factorization problem

As mentioned in Remark
2.3
, Rota-Baxter operators
B
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
one-to-one correspond to modified
r
-matrices
R
. The study of integration of such modified
r
-matrices gives arise to the Adler-Kostant-Symes (AKS) theory
[
31
]
. The AKS theory
[
20
]
allows one to construct an explicit integral curve
L(t)
of certain Hamiltonian function
h
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, by

(42)

L(t)=\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{g_{+}(t)}L_{0}=\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{g_{-}(t)}L_{0},

with initial value
L_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, as long as we know the solution
(g_{-}(t),g_{+}(t))
of the local factorization problem, which in turn comes from a split (i.e. an infinitesimal factorization) of
\mathfrak{g}
. However, usually, the solution
(g_{-}(t),g_{+}(t))
is not explicitly given. With the help of the local integration of Rota-Baxter operators, we can give such a local factorization explicitly.

To state this more precisely, let us first recall some facts adapted to the Rota-Baxter operators context from
[
16
,
31
]
, and we refer the readers to the references therein for the original references. Let
B
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
. Then it gives rise to an infinitesimal factorization (or a split)
[
31
, Proposition 9]
of
\mathfrak{g}
as follows. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{+}=\mathrm{Im}(B+{\rm{Id}}),~\mathfrak{g}_{-}=\mathrm{Im}(B).

We further denote by
\mathfrak{k}_{+}:=\ker(B)
and
\mathfrak{k}_{-}:=\ker(B+{\rm{Id}})
. Since both
B
and
B+{\rm{Id}}
are Lie algebra homomorphisms from the descendent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{B}
to
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{+},\mathfrak{g}_{-},\mathfrak{k}_{+}
and
\mathfrak{k}_{-}
are Lie subalgebras. Moreover,
\mathfrak{k}_{\pm}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
are Lie ideals, and
\theta:\mathfrak{g}_{+}/\mathfrak{k}_{+}\to\mathfrak{g}_{-}/\mathfrak{k}_{-}
given by
(B+{\rm{Id}})(u)\mapsto B(u)
on the representatives of equivalence classes is well-defined and is a Lie algebra isomorphism. The map
\theta
is called the
Cayley transform
of
B
. Define
\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}=\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}
by

\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}=\{(x,y)\in\mathfrak{g}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-}~\mbox{ such that }~\theta(\bar{x})=\bar{y}\}.

Then any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
can be uniquely expressed as
x=x_{+}-x_{-}
for
(x_{+},x_{-})\in\mathfrak{g}_{\theta}.

Let
G
be a Lie group of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
G_{\pm}
and
K_{\pm}
be the Lie subgroups of
G
integrating the above Lie subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}
and
\mathfrak{k}_{\pm}
correspondingly. Then the Cayley transform
\theta
integrates also to the group level into a (local) Lie group homomorphism
\Theta:G_{+}/K_{+}\to G_{-}/K_{-}
, by Lie’s II Theorem. Let us denote by
\bar{g}
the equivalence class of
g
. Then the solution
(g_{+}(t),g_{-}(t))\in G_{+}\times G_{-}
of the (local) factorization problem in
[
31
, Theorem 11]
is the solution of the following equations,

\exp 2tX_{0}=g_{+}(t)\cdot g_{-}(t)^{-1},\quad\Theta(\bar{g}_{+}(t))=\bar{g}_{-}(t),\quad\text{where}\>X_{0}=dh|_{L_{0}}\in\mathfrak{g},

with the initial value
g_{\pm}(0)=e\in G
, for sufficiently small
t
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Then the above solution
(g_{+},g_{-})
of the (local) factorization problem
has an explicit expression,

(43)

g_{-}(t)=\mathcal{B}(\exp 2tX_{0}),\quad g_{+}(t)=\exp 2tX_{0}\cdot\mathcal{B}(\exp 2tX_{0}),

for sufficiently small
t
, with
\mathcal{B}
given explicitly by (
34
) locally.

Proof.

Let
(G,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
be the integrated local Rota-Baxter Lie group of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
given in Theorem
5.3
. By Remark
6.3
,
\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{B})
and
G_{-}
are locally isomorphic. Define
\mathcal{B}_{+}:U\to G
by

\mathcal{B}_{+}(g)=g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g).

By the same discussion in
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
,
\mathcal{B}_{+}
is a local Lie group homomorphism from the descendent local Lie group to
G
. Since
\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{B}_{+})
and
G_{+}
have the same Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, so they are also locally isomorphic. Obviously, locally we have

g=g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot(\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1},\quad g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{B}_{+}),~\mathcal{B}(g)\in\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{B}).

Therefore, we have
\exp 2tX_{0}=g_{+}(t)\cdot g_{-}(t)^{-1}
, where
g_{+}(t)
and
g_{-}(t)
are given by (
43
).
∎

6.3.
Application in the integration of matched pairs

A
matched pair of Lie algebras
consists of a pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
and a representation
\mu:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

(44)

\displaystyle\rho(x)[\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x)\xi,\eta]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\xi,\rho(x)\eta]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho\big((\mu(\eta)x\big)\xi-\rho\big(\mu(\xi)x\big)\eta,

(45)

\displaystyle\mu(\xi)[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mu(\xi)x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mu(\xi)y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mu\big(\rho(y)\xi\big)x-\mu\big(\rho(x)\xi\big)y,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{h}
.

It is well known that if there is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
such that both
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
are Lie subalgebras, and
\mathfrak{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
as vector spaces, then
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a matched pair of Lie algebras.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g}_{B},\mathfrak{g}_{diag})
is a matched pair of Lie algebras, where
\mathfrak{g}_{diag}=\{(x,x)|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{B}=\{(B(x),x+B(x))|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}
.

Proof.

Consider the direct sum Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
, in which the Lie bracket is given by

[(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})]=([x,x^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[y,y^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

It is obvious that
\mathfrak{g}_{diag}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. Since
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
is a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra, we have

\displaystyle[(B(x),x+B(x)),(B(y),y+B(y))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(B([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\Big)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{B},

which implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{B}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, it is obvious that
\mathfrak{g}_{diag}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{B}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
as vector spaces. Therefore,
(\mathfrak{g}_{B},\mathfrak{g}_{diag})
is a matched pair of Lie algebras.
∎

A pair of Lie groups
(P,Q)
is called a
matched pair of Lie groups
(
[
23
]
) if there is a left action of
P
on
Q
and a right action of
Q
on
P
:

P\times Q\to Q,\qquad(p,q)\mapsto p\triangleright q;\qquad P\times Q\to P\qquad(p,q)\mapsto p\triangleleft q,

such that

(46)

\displaystyle p\triangleright(q_{1}q_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p\triangleright q_{1})\big((p\triangleleft q_{1})\triangleright q_{2}\big);

(47)

\displaystyle(p_{1}p_{2})\triangleleft q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(p_{1}\triangleleft(p_{2}\triangleright q)\big)(p_{2}\triangleleft q).

Similar to the case of Lie algebras, the following equivalent characterization of matched pairs of Lie groups is well known.

Proposition 6.6
.

A pair of Lie groups
(P,Q)
is a matched pair if there exists a Lie group
K
, and injective Lie group homomorphism
i_{P}:P\to K
and
i_{Q}:Q\to K
, such that the map
P\times Q\,\rightarrow\,K
defined by
(p,q)\to i_{P}(p)i_{Q}(q)
is a diffeomorphism.

It is not hard to see that a matched pair of Lie groups differentiates to a matched pair of Lie algebras. However, not every matched pair of Lie algebras integrates to a matched pair of Lie groups (e.g.

[
12
, Example 2.7]
gives rise to a counter example). Some partial result is available for this integration problem. For example, it is proved in
[
23
]
that a matched pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
integrates to a matched pair of Lie groups
(G,H)
, if the simply connected Lie groups
G
and
H
integrating
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
respectively are compact. In the following, we give another integration result for matched pairs coming from Rota-Baxter operators using the integration of Rota-Baxter operators.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
be a Rota-Baxter Lie algebra. Assume that
B
is integrable and
(G,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
is the integrated Rota-Baxter Lie group of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},B)
. Then
(G_{\mathcal{B}},G_{diag})
is a matched pair of Lie groups, where
G_{diag}=\{(g,g)|\forall g\in G\}
and
G_{\mathcal{B}}=\{(\mathcal{B}(g),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g))|\forall g\in G\}
. Furthermore, the differentiation of
(G_{\mathcal{B}},G_{diag})
is the matched pair
(\mathfrak{g}_{B},\mathfrak{g}_{diag})
.

Proof.

Consider the direct product Lie group
G\times G
, in which the group multiplication is given by

(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})=(g\cdot g^{\prime},h\cdot h^{\prime}).

It is obvious that
G_{diag}
is a Lie subgroup. Since
(G,\cdot,\mathcal{B})
is a Rota-Baxter Lie group, for any
g,h\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}(g),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\Big)\Big(\mathcal{B}(h),h\cdot\mathcal{B}(h)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot\mathcal{B}(h),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h\cdot\mathcal{B}(h)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}(g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h\cdot(\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h\cdot(\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h\cdot(\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1})\Big)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{B}}

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}(g),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}((\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)),(\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot\mathcal{B}((\mathcal{B}(g))^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(g))\Big)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{B}},

which implies that
G_{\mathcal{B}}
is a subgroup. Moreover, since
\mathcal{B}
is a smooth map, it follows that
G_{\mathcal{B}}
is a closed set in
G\times G
. Thus
G_{\mathcal{B}}
is a Lie subgroup.
For any
(a,b)\in G\times G
, there is

(a,b)=\Big(\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{-1}),b\cdot a^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{-1})\Big)\Big((\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{-1}))^{-1}\cdot a,(\mathcal{B}(b\cdot a^{-1}))^{-1}\cdot a\Big).

Thus the map
G_{\mathcal{B}}\times G_{diag}\,\rightarrow\,G\times G
defined by
\Big((\mathcal{B}(g),g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)),(h,h)\Big)\,\rightarrow\,\Big(\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h,g\cdot\mathcal{B}(g)\cdot h\Big)
is a diffeomorphism. Therefore, by Proposition
6.6
,
(G_{\mathcal{B}},G_{diag})
is a matched pair of Lie groups. The other conclusion is straightforward.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

Despite there are explicit counter examples of nonintegrable matched pairs, we do not know yet an explicit example of nonintegrable Rota-Baxter operators.

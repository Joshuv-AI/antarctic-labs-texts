ON PERIODIC DECOMPOSITIONS AND NONEXPANSIVE LINES
Cleber Fernando Colle

Abstract
. In his Ph.D. thesis, Michal Szabados conjectured that for a not fully periodic configuration with a minimal periodic decomposition the nonexpansive lines are exactly the lines that contain a period for some periodic configuration in such decomposition. In this paper, we study Szabados’s conjecture. First, we show that we may consider a minimal periodic decomposition where each periodic configuration is defined on a finite alphabet. Then we prove that Szabados’s conjecture holds for a wide class of configurations, which includes all not fully periodic low convex pattern complexity configurations.



1.
Introduction

In his Ph.D. thesis
[
12
]
, with an algebraic viewpoint on symbolic configurations, Michal Szabados showed that every low pattern complexity configuration can be decomposed into a finite sum of periodic configurations. Such a periodic decomposition theorem is the heart of new recent results related to Nivat’s conjecture and other topics
[
8
,
11
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
7
,
2
]
. If we consider a not fully periodic configuration with a minimal periodic decomposition, i.e., a periodic decomposition with the smallest possible number of periodic configurations, a nonexpansive line contains a period of some periodic configuration. Michal Szabados conjectured that the conversely also holds.

To make the statements precise we need to introduce some terminologies.

1.1.
Symbolic dynamics

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an alphabet with at least two elements. The elements of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, called
configurations
, have the form
\eta=(\eta_{g})_{g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, where
\eta_{g}\in\mathcal{A}
for all
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. For each
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the
shift application

T^{u}:\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is defined by
(T^{u}\eta)_{g}=\eta_{g-u}
for all
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and all
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
. A configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is said to be
periodic
if there exists a non-zero vector
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, called
period
of
\eta
, such that
T^{h}\eta=\eta
. If
\eta
has two periods linearly independents over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we say that
\eta
is
fully periodic
.

Let
Orb\,(\eta)=\{T^{u}\eta:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
denotes the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-orbit of
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
. If
\mathcal{A}
is a finite alphabet endowed with the discrete topology, then
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
equipped with the product topology is a metrizable compact space. In particular, for all
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, the set
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
, where the bar denotes the closure, is a compact
subshift
, i.e., a closed subset invariant by the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action
T^{u}
,
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is finite, we say that a configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
has
low pattern complexity
if
|\{T^{u}\eta{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{S}$}:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}|\leq|\mathcal{S}|
holds for some non-empty, finite set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where
\cdot{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{S}$}
means the restriction to the set
\mathcal{S}
. If in addition
\mathcal{S}
is
convex
, i.e., a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
whose convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, denoted by
\conv(\mathcal{S})
, is closed and
\mathcal{S}=\conv(\mathcal{S})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we say that
\eta
has
low convex pattern complexity
.

From now on, we will assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a finite alphabet endowed with the dis- crete topology.

1.2.
Nonexpansive lines

Let
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a line through the origin. Given
t>0
, the
t
-neighbourhood of
\ell
is defined as

\ell^{t}=\{g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\dist(g,\ell)\leq t\}.

Following Boyle and Lind
[
1
]
, we say that
\ell
is an
expansive
line on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
if there exists

such that

\forall\ x,y\in\overline{Orb\,(\eta)},\quad x{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\ell^{t}$}=y{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\ell^{t}$}\implies x=y.

Otherwise,
\ell
is called a
nonexpansive
line on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
.

For a configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
​, we use
\nexpl(\eta)
to denote the set formed by the nonexpansive lines on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
. A particular case of Boyle-Lind Theorem
[
1

implies that
\nexpl(\eta)
has at least one element if the subshift
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
is infinite. An immediate application of Boyle-Lind Theorem allows us to state the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
be a configuration. Then
\nexpl(\eta)=\emptyset
if and only if
\eta
is fully periodic.

When
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is a periodic configuration, but not fully periodic, it is easy to see that the unique line
\ell\in\nexpl(\eta)
, which exists due to Boyle-Lind Theorem, contains any period for
\eta
. Hence, any configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
where
\nexpl(\eta)
has at least two lines can not be periodic.

1.3.
The algebraic viewpoint

In
[
9

R

(1.1)

(\varphi\eta)_{g}=a_{1}\eta_{g-u_{1}}+\cdots+a_{n}\eta_{g-u_{n}}\quad\forall g\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Let

We remark that the operations in (

are denoted by

, where

denotes the inverse additive of

for some prime number

Szabados’s conjecture is a very recent open problem in symbolic dynamics and for the best we know there are not many works toward a proof for the conjecture.

In this paper, as a corollary of our main result we get that Szabados’s conjecture holds for any not fully periodic low convex pattern complexity configuration.

1.4.
Our contributions

We believe that the difficulty in approaching Szabados’s conjecture remains mostly on the fact that the periodic configurations in a periodic decomposition may not be defined on finite alphabets. Our first result allows us to get around this difficulty.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let us denote
[[n]]=\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
​, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-minimal periodic decomposition, then, for some prime number

, with
\mathcal{A}\subset[[p]]
,

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition, where the bar denotes the congruence modulo

.

By using Theorem
1.6
, we get the following result:

Theorem 1.7

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, be a not fully periodic configuration and suppose
\eta=\eta_{1}+\cdots+\eta_{m}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-minimal periodic decomposition. If for any configuration

\overline{Orb\,(x)}
has a periodic configuration, then, for each

contains a period for

if and only if

.

Let

\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, be a not fully periodic low convex patterncomplexity configuration and suppose

[

2.
Background

In this section, we will revisit terminologies, known results or immediate applications of known results.

2.1.
One-sided nonexpansive directions

In the sequel, we will revisit a more refined version of expansiveness called one-sided nonexpansiveness and introduced by Cyr and Kra in
[
3
]
. We recall that two vectors are parallel if they have the same direction and antiparallel if they have opposite directions. Two oriented objects in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
are said to be
(anti)parallel
if the adjacent vectors to their respective orientations are (anti)parallel, where by object we mean an oriented line, an oriented line segment or a vector. In a slight abuse of notation, we will view an oriented line also as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Given a line
\ell\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we use
\boldsymbol{\ell}
to denote the line
\ell
endowed of a given orientation. Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an oriented line. The half plane determined by
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}):=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u_{1}g_{2}-u_{2}g_{1}\geq 0\},

where
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a non-zero vector parallel to
\boldsymbol{\ell}
. Let
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
be a configuration. An oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
through the origin is called a
one-sided expansive direction on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
if

\forall\ x,y\in\overline{Orb\,(\eta)},\quad x{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})$}=y{\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})$}\ \Longrightarrow\ x=y.

Otherwise,
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is called a
one-sided nonexpansive direction on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
.

For a configuration
\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, we use
\nexpd(\eta)
to denote the set formed by the one-sided nonexpansive directions on
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
. By the compactness of
\overline{Orb\,(\eta)}
, a line
\ell\in\nexpl(\eta)
if and only if, for some orientation,
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\nexpd(\eta)
(see
[
3
]
for more details). As highlighted in
[
3
]
, lines in
\nexpl(\eta)
or oriented lines in
\nexpd(\eta)
are rational, where by
rational
we mean a line or an oriented line in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a rational slope.

2.2.
Geometric notations

Let
\boldsymbol{\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an oriented line and suppose
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a non-empty, convex set such that
\mathcal{S}+u\subset\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell})
for some

. The
support line of
\mathcal{S}
determined by
\boldsymbol{\ell}
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}

\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}

such that
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})
and

. Of course, if
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a non-empty, finite, convex set, then
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be a non-empty, convex set. If
\mathcal{S}
has at least two points and

holds for some oriented line

of
\mathcal{S}
. If
\conv(\mathcal{S})
has positive area and

holds for some oriented line
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we call
\conv(\mathcal{S}\cap\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{S}})
an
edge
of
\mathcal{S}
. We use

to denote the set of edges of
\mathcal{S}
.

If
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a convex set (possibly infinite) such that
\conv(\mathcal{S})
has positive area, our standard convention is that the boundary of
\conv(\mathcal{S})
is positively oriented. With this convention, every edge

inherits a natural orientation from the boundary of
\conv(\mathcal{S})
. Moreover, if

, then

\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}
are parallels.

L_{\ell_{k}}(l)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with

has at least one edge

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\lim_{l\to+\infty}T^{g_{n_{l}}+s_{l}u_{j}}\eta=\varTheta

and, for each
1\leq i\leq m
,

\lim_{l\to+\infty}T^{g_{n_{l}}+s_{l}u_{j}}\eta_{i}=\varTheta_{i}.

Since
T^{g_{n_{l}}+s_{l}u_{j}}\eta=T^{g_{n_{l}}+s_{l}u_{j}}\eta_{1}+\cdots+T^{g_{n_{l}}+s_{l}u_{j}}\eta_{m}
, then
\varTheta=\varTheta_{1}+\cdots+\varTheta_{m}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-periodic decomposition, where
h_{i}
is a period for
\varTheta_{i}
, with
1\leq i\leq m
. By construction,
\varTheta_{j}
is fully periodic, which implies that
\varTheta
has
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-order lower than
m
. Let
\varTheta=\varTheta^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\varTheta^{\prime}_{n}
, with
n\leq m-1
, be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-minimal periodic decomposition. Due to Lemma
3.1
, there exists a sequence
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\leq m
such that, for each
1\leq i\ \leq m
, with
i\neq i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
,
\varTheta_{i}
is fully periodic.

Claim 3.3
.

k\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
.

Indeed, if
k\neq i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
, then, by the previous discussion,
\varTheta_{k}
is fully periodic. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
as large as we want, there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
, with
l>r
, such that

(T^{g_{n_{l}}}\eta_{k}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$R_{r}-s_{l}u_{j}$}

is fully periodic. But, since
L_{\ell_{k}}(l)\cap(R_{r}-s_{l}u_{j})
can be as large as we want, by item (i) we get that

(T^{g_{n_{l}}}\eta_{k}){\mathbin{|}}\raise-1.72218pt\hbox{$L_{\ell_{k}}(l)\cup(S_{\ell_{k}}(r)-s_{l}u_{j})$}

is fully periodic of periods
u_{j}
and
h_{k}
, which is a contradiction and establishes the claim.

Note that
\varTheta
can not be fully periodic, since, otherwise,
\varTheta=\varTheta^{\prime}_{1}
and condition (ii) of Lemma
3.1
allows us to consider
i_{1}\neq k
, which due to the previous claim is a contradiction. Thus, since
\varTheta

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-order

and for any configuration
x

\in\overline{Orb\,(\varTheta)}
the subshift
\overline{Orb\,(x)}
has a periodic configuration, by induction hypothesis Szabados’s conjecture holds for
\varTheta
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the lines through the origin such that, for each
1\leq i\leq m
,
h_{i}\in\ell_{i}
.

Claim 3.4
.

\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}\in\nexpl(\eta)
.

Indeed, since
k\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\}
, it follows by condition (i) of Lemma
3.1
that some configuration
\varTheta^{\prime}_{i}
, with
1\leq i\leq n
, has a period parallel to
h_{k}
. Thus, as Szabados’s conjecture holds for
\varTheta
, then
\ell_{k}\in\nexpl(\varTheta)\subset\nexpl(\eta)
. Being
1\leq k\leq m
, with
k\neq\iota_{1}
, an arbitrary integer, we conclude that
\ell_{i}\in\nexpl(\eta)
for every
1\leq i\leq m
, with
i\neq\iota_{1}
.

If
\vartheta
is fully periodic, then by repeating the same construction for
1\leq l_{1}\leq m
, with
l_{1}\neq\iota_{1}
(see condition (ii) of Lemma
3.1
), we get that
\ell_{\iota_{1}}\in\nexpl(\eta)
.

If
\vartheta=\vartheta^{\prime}_{1}
is not fully periodic, the unique line
\ell^{\prime}\in\nexpl(\vartheta^{\prime}_{1})\subset\nexpl(\eta)
contains any period for
\vartheta^{\prime}_{1}

, we see that such a period is either parallel or antiparallel to
h_{\iota_{1}}
, which implies that
\ell^{\prime}=\ell_{\iota_{1}}
and proves the claim.

Therefore, the inductive step is done, which proves Theorem
3.2
.

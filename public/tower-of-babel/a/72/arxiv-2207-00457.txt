On τ \tau -tilting subcategories
Javad Asadollahi, Somayeh Sadeghi and Hipolito Treffinger

Abstract
. The main theme of this paper is to study τ \tau -tilting subcategories in an abelian category 𝒜 \mathscr{A} with enough projective objects. We introduce the notion of τ \tau -cotorsion torsion triples and show a bijection between the collection of τ \tau -cotorsion torsion triples in 𝒜 \mathscr{A} and the collection of τ \tau -tilting subcategories of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} , generalizing the bijection by Bauer, Botnan, Oppermann and Steen between the collection of cotorsion torsion triples and the collection of tilting subcategories of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} . General definitions and results are exemplified using persistent modules. If 𝒜 = Mod ​ - ​ R \mathscr{A}={\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R , where R R is an unitary associative ring, we characterize all support τ \tau -tilting, resp. all support τ − \tau^{-} -tilting, subcategories of Mod ​ - ​ R {\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R in term of finendo quasitilting, resp. quasicotilting, modules. As a result, it will be shown that every silting module, respectively every cosilting module, induces a support τ \tau -tilting, respectively support τ − \tau^{-} -tilting, subcategory of Mod ​ - ​ R {\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R . We also study the theory in Rep ⁡ ( Q , 𝒜 ) {\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A}) , where Q Q is a finite and acyclic quiver. In particular, we give an algorithm to construct support τ \tau -tilting subcategories in Rep ⁡ ( Q , 𝒜 ) {\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A}) from certain support τ \tau -tilting subcategories of 𝒜 \mathscr{A} and present a systematic way to construct ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -tilting subcategories in Rep ⁡ ( Q , 𝒜 ) {\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A}) from n n -tilting subcategories in 𝒜 \mathscr{A} .

1.
Introduction

Tilting theory is one of the most prominent tools in representation theory of artin algebras. The classical tilting modules were introduced by Brenner and Butler
[
BB
]
, and Happel and Ringel
[
HR
]
as an axiomatisation of the reflection functors of Bernstein, Gelfand and Ponomarev
[
BGP
]
and Auslander, Platzek and Reiten
[
APR
]
.
It has been shown by Bongartz
[
Bo
]
that every partial tilting module can be completed to a tilting module and by Happel and Unger
[
HU
]
that every almost complete tilting module can be completed in at most two ways.
However, there are examples of almost complete tilting modules that have exactly one complement.

Several years later, and inspired by the cluster algebras defined by Fomin and Zelevnisky in
[
FZ
]
, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten introduced
\tau
-tilting theory
[
AIR
]
.
This is a generalization of classical tilting theory in which every almost complete support
\tau
-tilting module has exactly two complements, allowing to introduce a notion of mutation among these objects.
The success of
\tau
-tilting theory was immediate, offering an explanation for several phenomena in the module category of artin algebras and offering new connections between representation theory and other areas of mathematics (see
[
T
]
).

Due to the effectiveness of
\tau
-tilting theory for the study of the categories of finitely presented modules, many mathematicians have introduced theories generalizing
\tau
-tilting theory, and its dual, to other contexts.
For instance, there are the works of Angeleri-Hügel, Marks and Vitoria
[
AMV
]
and Breaz and Pop
[
BP
]
for the module category of rings; Iyama, Jørgensen and Yang
[
IJY
]
for functor categories; or Liu and Zhou for Hom-finite abelian categories with enough projective objects
[
LZh
]
.

In this paper we are interested in studying
\tau
-tilting theory in arbitrary abelian categories with enough projective objects.
Since in general there is no notion of Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau
in such general categories, the definition of support
\tau
-tilting subcategories needs to be made with no mention to it. We follow
[
IJY
,
LZh
]
and define support
\tau
-tilting subcategories as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be an additive contravariantly finite full subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then
\mathscr{T}
is called a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory if

1.

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(T_{1},{\rm{Fac}}(T_{2}))=0,
for all
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{T}
.

2.

For any projective
P
in
\mathscr{A}
, there exists an exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
T^{0}
and
T^{1}
are in
\mathscr{T}
and
f
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
.

The idea of generalizing a well-behaved theory from the category of finitely presented modules of an artin algebras to more general abelian categories is not exclusive to
\tau
-tilting theory.
Indeed, Beligiannis introduced tilting theory for arbitrary abelian categories in
[
Be1
]
at the beginning of the millenium, see also
[
BR
]
.
In recent years, the work of Bauer, Botnan, Oppermann and Steen
[
BBOS
]
has found a striking application of this theory in persistence theory and topological data analysis (TDA).
One of the main results in
[
BBOS
]
is a bijection between tilting subcategories of an abelian category with enough projective objects and the collection of cotorsion torsion triples in the same category.
The motivation that started this collaboration is to find a generalization of
[
BBOS
, Theorem 2.29]
to support
\tau
-tilting subcategories, but for that we first need an adequate notion of triple.
Inspired by
[
BR
, Lemma V.3.3]
we give the definition of
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triple as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
A triple of full subcategories
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D},\mathscr{F})
of
\mathscr{A}
is called a
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triple if

1.

\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}
.

2.

For every projective object
P\in\mathscr{A}
, there exists an exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}{D}\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0,

where
D\in\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
,
C\in\mathscr{C}
and
f
is a left
\mathscr{D}
-approximation.

3.

\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

4.

(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{F})
is a torsion pair in
\mathscr{A}
.

Having the notion of
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triple we are able to show the desired bijection in an abelian category with enough projective objects.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.7
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Then there are bijections

\displaystyle\Phi:\{\mbox{support $\tau$-tilting subcategories}\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\{\mbox{$\tau$-cotorsion torsion triples}\}

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}

\displaystyle\longmapsto({}^{\perp_{1}}{(\rm{Fac}(\mathscr{T}))},\rm{Fac}(\mathscr{T}),\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}})

\displaystyle\Psi:\{\mbox{$\tau$-cotorsion torsion triples}\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\{\mbox{support $\tau$-tilting subcategories}\}

\displaystyle(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D},\mathscr{F})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}

which are mutually inverse. Moreover these bijections restrict to bijections between the collection of tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
and the collection of cotorsion torsion triples in
\mathscr{A}
.

We note that Buan and Zhou
[
BZ
]
introduced the notion of left weak cotorsion torsion triple in order to provide a version of
[
BBOS
, Theorem 2.29]
for support
\tau
-tilting modules in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is an artin algebra. In Theorem
4.10
we show that a triple
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D},\mathscr{F})
of subcategories of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
is a left weak cotorsion torsion triple if and only if it is a
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triple.

One of the main properties of support
\tau
-tilting modules is that they generate functorially finite torsion classes in module categories.
As a consequence of our results, we obtain the following corollary, see Theorem
5.7
and Corollary
4.8
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects and let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
is a functorially finite torsion class in
\mathscr{A}
.

For
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, using results of
[
AIR
]
, it is shown that a module
T
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
is a support
\tau
-tilting module if and only if
{\rm{add}}(T)
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
. On the other hand,
T
is a support
\tau^{-}
-tilting module if and only if
{\rm{add}}(T)
is a support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, see Propositions
5.9
and
6.11
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
is an abelian category with enough injective objects.
Then in this category all the dual definitions and results can be stated and proved, see Section
6
.
Using this symmetry, when
\mathscr{A}
is an abelian category with enough projective and enough injective objects, we introduce the notion of a
\tau
-
\tau^{-}
-quadruple and use it to create a map from support
\tau
-tilting to support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
.
We show in Proposition
7.4
that this map is exactly the dual of the dagger map defined in
[
AIR
, Theorem 2.15]
when
\mathscr{A}={\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.

Besides
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
, another prominent example of an abelian category with enough projective objects is
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, the category of modules over an unitary associative ring
R
.

The theory of support
\tau
-tilting modules of
[
AIR
]
is generalized to
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
by Angeleri Hügel, Marks and Vitoria
[
AMV
]
, where they introduced silting modules showing that finitely presented silting
\Lambda
-modules coincide with the support
\tau
-tilting modules of
[
AIR
]
. As the categorical dual of silting modules, Breaz and Pop introduced the notion of cosilting modules in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
and showed that finitely copresented cosilting
\Lambda
-modules coincide with the support
\tau^{-}
-tilting modules.

We study the connection between silting, resp. cosilting, modules in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
with the
\tau
-tilting, resp.
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
. Furthermore, we characterize all support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
.
In particular, we provide a bijection between the equivalence classes of all support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
and the collection of all equivalent classes of certain
R
-modules, the so-called finendo quasitilting
R
-modules, see Theorem
8.1.10
. It is known that all silting modules are finendo quasitilting.
Dually, we also characterize all support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
by providing a bijection between the equivalence classes of all support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
and the collection of all equivalent classes of the quasicotilting
R
-modules.

Towards the end of the paper, we provide applications of this theory to the category of the representations of a finite and acyclic quiver in an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
with enough projective objects. Let
Q
be such a quiver. It is known that the category
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
of representations of
Q
over
\mathscr{A}
is again an abelian category with enough projective objects. See Section
9
for more details.
We give a recipe to construct support
\tau
-tilting subcategories in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
from certain support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
. More explicitly, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
9.2.2
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects and
Q
be a finite acyclic quiver.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
such that
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
is closed with respect to the kernels of epimorphisms.
Then

\mathbb{T}={\rm{add}}\{e_{i}^{\rho}(T)|~i\in Q_{0},T\in\mathscr{T}\}

is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.

Let
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
denote the category of all projective objects in
\mathscr{A}
. In
[
BBOS
, Proposition 3.9]
the authors have shown that

\mathbb{T}={\rm{add}}\{e_{i}^{\rho}(P)|~i\in Q_{0},P\in{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})\}

is a tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then the previous result can be seen as a generalization of this result, since
\mathscr{A}={\rm{Fac}}({\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A}))
is clearly closed under kernels of epimorphisms.

There are other generalisations of tilting theory that we have not mentioned yet.
One of these generalizations consists on defining
n
-tilting subcategories, where
1
-tilting subcategories correspond to classical tilting subcategories, see Definition
2.1
.
By convention we consider
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
as the
0
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Using similar methods as in the proof of Theorem
9.2.2
we were able to show another generalization of
[
BBOS
, Proposition 3.9]
, which we believe might have interesting implications in the study of higher homological algebra, introduced by Iyama in
[
I1
,
I2
]
.

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
9.4.1
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
n
be a non-negative integer and let
Q
be a finite and acyclic quiver.
For an
n
-tilting subcategory
\mathscr{T}
of
\mathscr{A}
put

\mathscr{T}^{\prime}={\rm{add}}(\{e_{i}^{\rho}(T)|~i\in Q_{0},T\in\mathscr{T}\}).

Then
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
is an
(n+1)
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.

Throughout the paper, several examples are provided using the theory of persistent modules, which are central objects of study in topological data analysis (TDA).
We refer the reader to the survey
[
Ca
]
by Carlsson, or the book
[
O
]
by Oudot, for an introduction to topological data analysis and connections to quiver representations.

In Section

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category.
In this paper by subcategory we always mean a full subcategory.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be a subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
A morphism
\varphi:X\longrightarrow A
, where
A
is an object of
\mathscr{A}
, is called a right
\mathscr{X}
-approximation of
A
if,
X\in\mathscr{X}
and for every
X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{X}
, the induced morphism
\mathscr{A}(X^{\prime},X)\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}(X^{\prime},A)\longrightarrow 0
of abelian groups is exact.
We say that
\mathscr{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
if every object
A
of
\mathscr{A}
admits a right
\mathscr{X}
-approximation.
Dually, the notions of left
\mathscr{X}
-approximations and covariantly finite subcategories are defined.
Moreover we say that
\mathscr{X}
is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
if it is both a contravariantly finite and a covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
If
\mathscr{X}
is closed under taking finite direct sums and direct summands we say that it is additively closed.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. We define

\mathscr{X}^{\perp_{n}}:=\{A\in\mathscr{A}~|~{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{X},A)=0\},

{}^{\perp_{n}}\mathscr{X}:=\{A\in\mathscr{A}~|~{\rm{Ext}}^{n}_{\mathscr{A}}(A,\mathscr{X})=0\}.

Note that
{\rm{Ext}}^{0}
is just the usual Hom-functor.

Moreover, we set

\mathscr{X}^{\perp}:=\{A\in\mathscr{A}~|~{\rm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{X},A)=0,~~~\forall~i\geq 1\},

{}^{\perp}\mathscr{X}:=\{A\in\mathscr{A}~|~{\rm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathscr{A}}(A,\mathscr{X})=0,~~~\forall~i\geq 1\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Let
n
be a non-negative integer.
An additively closed subcategory
\mathscr{T}
of
\mathscr{A}
is called an
n
-tilting subcategory if

(i)

\mathscr{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

(ii)

{\rm{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathscr{A}}(T_{1},T_{2})=0
, for all
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{T}
and all
i\geq 1
.

(iii)

Every object
T\in\mathscr{T}
has projective dimension at most
n
.

(iv)

For every projective object
P
in
\mathscr{A}
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow T^{n}\longrightarrow 0

with
T^{i}\in\mathscr{T}
.

If
\mathscr{T}
only satisfies the conditions
(ii)-(iv)
, it is called a weak
n
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It follows, by breaking out the exact sequence of
(iv)
to short exact sequences and using the vanishing of Ext-groups in
(ii)
of the previous definition, that the map
f:P\longrightarrow T^{0}
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
. Moreover, it is easy to see that the category
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
of all projective objects in
\mathscr{A}
is the only
0
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. In this paper, when we say tilting subcategory we mean a
1
-tilting subcategory.

2.3
.

Cotorsion torsion triple.
Here we recall the notion of torsion pairs, cotorsion pairs and cotorsion torsion triples. Let us begin by the following classical definition of Dickson
[
D
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category.
A pair
(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
of full subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
is called a torsion pair if
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})=0
and for every
A\in\mathscr{A}
there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow tA\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow fA\longrightarrow 0

such that
tA\in\mathscr{T}
and
fA\in\mathscr{F}
.

It is well known that
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{F}
determine each others and the short exact sequence is functorial, see for instance
[
BR
, §I.1]
.

Definition 2.5
.

(see
[
Sa
]
)
Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
A pair
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
of full subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
is called a cotorsion pair if
\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}
,
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{C}^{\perp_{1}}
and for every object
A\in\mathscr{A}
, there are short exact sequences

0\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow 0

0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow D^{\prime}\longrightarrow C^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0

where
C
and
C^{\prime}
are in
\mathscr{C}
and
D
and
D^{\prime}
are in
\mathscr{D}
.

This notion sometimes called complete cotorsion pair in the literature.
See
[
BBOS
, Remark 2.7]
for more details.

Remark 2.6
.

It is immediate from the definition that in a cotorsion pair
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
both
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are closed under extensions.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective and enough injective objects.
In view of
[
BR
, Lemma V.3.3]
, a pair
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
of full subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
is a cotorsion pair if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}
,

2.

for every object
A\in\mathscr{A}
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}D\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0,

where
D\in\mathscr{D}
and
C\in\mathscr{C}
.

Following proposition shows that when
\mathscr{D}
is closed under factors it is enough, in the above definition, to check the Condition
2
only for projective objects.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective and enough injective objects.

(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})

\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}

Following lemma shows that if the subcategory
\mathscr{C}
of a cotorsion pair
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})

\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}

.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
be a cotorsion pair in
\mathscr{A}
such that
\mathscr{D}
is closed under factors. Then
\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
is a cotorsion pair, it follows easily that
\mathscr{D}
is closed under extensions and products. So
\mathscr{D}
is indeed a torsion class.
Now, since
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
is a cotorsion pair, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath}}{{\longrightarrow}}tA\longrightarrow 0

such that
D\in\mathscr{D}
and
C\in\mathscr{C}
. Here, since
D,tA\in\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{T}
is closed under extensions, we get
C\in\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
. We claim that the composition
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath}}{{\longrightarrow}}tA\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\jmath}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
is a right
\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
-approximation. To show the claim, let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
be a morphism with
X\in\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
. Since
X\in\mathscr{D}
and every map from
\mathscr{D}
to
A
, factors through
tA
, the morphism
\ell
factors through
\jmath
. Now since
X\in\mathscr{C}
and
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{A}(X,D)=0
, the morphism
\ell
factors through
\jmath\imath
. Hence the claim is proved.
∎

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
A triple
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
of full subcategories in
\mathscr{A}
is called a cotorsion torsion triple, if
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{T})
is a cotorsion pair and
(\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
is a torsion pair.

It has been recently shown in
[
BBOS
]
that cotorsion torsion triples and tilting subcategories are closely related.
The explicit relation between these two concepts is as follows.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
BBOS
, Theorem 2.29]

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Then there is a bijection

\{\mbox{tilting subcategories}\}~~~~\longleftrightarrow~~~~\{\mbox{cotorsion torsion triples}\}

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathscr{T}~~~~\longrightarrow~~~~~({}^{\perp_{1}}{(\rm{Fac}(\mathscr{T}))},\rm{Fac}(\mathscr{T}),\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}})

\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{T}~~\longleftarrow~~~(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})

where
\rm{Fac}(\mathscr{T})
is the full subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
consisting of factors of objects in
\mathscr{T}
.

2.11
.

Pointwise finite dimensional representations.

Let
k
be a field and
(\mathcal{X},\leq)
be a poset.
Consider
\mathcal{X}
as a category.
A persistence module is a (covariant and additive) functor
V:\mathcal{X}\to{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}k
from
\mathcal{X}
to
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}k
, the category of
k
-vector spaces.
A persistence module
V
is called pointwise finite dimensional representation of
\mathcal{X}
if it is a functor from
\mathcal{X}
to
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}k
, the category of finite dimensional vector spaces.

The abelian category of pointwise finite dimensional representations will be denoted by
{\rm Rep}_{k}^{\rm{pfd}}\mathcal{X}
.
A subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{X}
is called a convex subset if for every
x\leq y\in\mathcal{C}
,
x\leq z\leq y
implies
z\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a convex subset of
\mathcal{X}
. The constant representation
k_{\mathcal{C}}
is defined by
k_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=k
, for
x\in\mathcal{C}
,
k_{\mathcal{C}}(x)=0
, for
x\not\in\mathcal{C}
and
k_{\mathcal{C}}(x\leq y)=\rm{id}_{k}
.
Set
P_{x}=k_{\{y\in\mathcal{X}|\ y\geq x\}}.

As application of Yoneda’s Lemma one can see that, for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
P_{x}
is a projective object of
{\rm Rep}_{k}^{\rm{pfd}}\mathcal{X}
and vice versa all projective modules are of this form, see e.g.
[
BBOS
, §2]
.
A representation
V\in{\rm Rep}_{k}^{\rm{pfd}}\mathcal{X}
is called finitely generated if there exists an epimorphism of functors
\bigoplus_{i\in J}P_{x_{i}}\longrightarrow V
, where
J
is a finite indexing set.
Moreover, if the kernel of this epimorphism is again finitely generated, it is called finitely presented.
We denote the full subcategory of
{\rm Rep}_{k}^{\rm{pfd}}\mathcal{X}
consisting of finitely presented representations by
{\rm Rep}_{k}^{\rm{fd}}\mathcal{X}
.

Of particular importance is the case
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the poset of non-negative real numbers and to concentrate on the abelian subcategory
{\rm Rep}^{\rm{fp}}_{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of finitely presented representations.
It is proved in
[
C
, Theorem 1.1]
that the indecomposable objects in this category are classified by the constant representation
k_{[x,y)}
, where
x<y\leq\infty
.
Moreover, for each
x\geq 0
, the constant representations
k_{[x,\infty)}
and
k_{[0,x)}
are projective and injective objects, respectively.

3.
\tau
-tilting subcategories

In this section, we recall the definition of
\tau
-tilting subcategories and study some of their properties. Following definition is motivated by
[
IJY
, Definition 1.5]
and
[
LZh
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be an additive full subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then
\mathscr{T}
is called a weak support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
if

(i)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(T_{1},{\rm{Fac}}(T_{2}))=0,
for all
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{T}
.

(ii)

For any projective
P
in
\mathscr{A}
, there exists an exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
T^{0}
and
T^{1}
are in
\mathscr{T}
and
f
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
.

If furthermore
\mathscr{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, it is called a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
A support
\tau
-tilting subcategory is simply called
\tau
-tilting if the approximation
f:P\longrightarrow T^{0}
is non-zero for every projective object
P
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that a key difference between tilting and
\tau
-tilting subcategories is that the approximations
f:P\longrightarrow T^{0}
are always a monomorphism when
\mathscr{T}
is a tilting subcategory.
It is immediate from the definition that every (weak) tilting subcategory is a (weak)
\tau
-tilting subcategory.

In the following example we present a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory which is not a tilting subcategory. Note that for
X\in\mathscr{A}
, we let
{\rm{add}}(X)
be the category of all direct summands of finite direct sums of copies of
X
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}={\rm Rep}^{\rm{fp}}_{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and set

\mathscr{T}={\rm{add}}(\{k_{[x,\infty)}|\ x\geq 1\}\cup\{k_{[0,x)}|\ x\leq 1\}).

Then
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory. To see this, first note that,

{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})={\rm{add}}(\{k_{[x,y)}|\ 1\leq x<y\leq\infty\}\cup\{k_{[0,x)}|\ x\leq 1\}),

and obviously
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}))=0
.

Now let
k_{[a,\infty)}
be an indecomposable projective in
\mathscr{A}
.
If
a\geq 1
, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow 0,

and if
0\leq a<1
, the exact sequence

k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[0,1)}\longrightarrow k_{[1,\infty)}\longrightarrow 0,

are the desired exact sequences.

Moreover,
\mathscr{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Indeed, depending on
a
and
b
, for an indecomposable representation
k_{[a,b)}
we have the following right
\mathscr{T}
-approximations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ k_{[0,b)}\longrightarrow k_{[0,b)},&\ 0=a<b\leq 1;\\
k_{[1,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 0\leq a<1<b\leq\infty;\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ 0\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 0<a<b\leq 1;\\
k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 1\leq a<b\leq\infty.\end{array}\right.

It is obviously not a tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, because
k_{[0,\infty)}
is a projective object, for which we do not have a short exact sequence like Condition
(iv)
of Definition
[
BBOS
]
.

One of the main reasons behind the success of
\tau
-tilting theory
[
AIR
]
is the formal inclusion of the notion of
support
from the start, see Definition
5.8
.
Note that in this definition we are defining objects in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
by using a property of the object in a different category, namely
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}(\Lambda/\langle e\rangle)
.
So one needs to verify that the good properties of a support
\tau
-tilting object
M
in
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}(\Lambda/\langle e\rangle)
can be transported to
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
.
This was done in
[
AIR
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Note that
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}(\Lambda/\langle e\rangle)
is a functorially finite wide subcategory of
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}\Lambda
which is at the same time a torsion and a torsion-free class. Recall that a subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
is called a wide subcategory if it is closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions. In particular, this implies that
\mathscr{X}
itself is an abelian category.
It is known that a subcategory
\mathscr{X}
of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
is a torsion class if it is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, closed with respect to quotients and extensions. Moreover,
\mathscr{X}
is a torsion free class if it is a covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
which is furthermore closed under subobjects and extensions.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Let
\mathscr{X}
be a wide and functorially finite full subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Then
\mathscr{X}
is a torsion class of
\mathscr{A}
if and only if
\mathscr{X}
is a torsion free class of
\mathscr{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a torsion class of
\mathscr{A}
. In order to show that it is a torsion free class, we need to show that it is covariantly finite, closed under subobject and closed under extension. By the assumption
\mathscr{X}
is a covariantly finite subcategory. It is closed under extension, since
\mathscr{X}
is a torsion class. Moreover, it is closed under subobject, since it is a wide subcategory.
The other implication follows similarly.
∎

Our next result is inspired by
[
AIR
, Lemma 2.1]
and it justifies the name of support
\tau
-tilting subcategories.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Let
\mathscr{X}
be a wide and functorially finite torsion class of
\mathscr{A}
. Then every support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{X}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{X}
. First we note that, since
\mathscr{X}
is a wide subcategory, then
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})\subseteq{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{X})=\mathscr{X}
. Therefore, since
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{X}
, we have
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}(\mathscr{T},{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}))=0
. Hence
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}))=0
.

Now let
P
be a projective object in
\mathscr{A}
. By Lemma
3.4
, there is a subcategory
\mathscr{Y}
of
\mathscr{A}
such that
(\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{X})
is a torsion pair of
\mathscr{A}
. Consider the canonical short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}X\longrightarrow 0

of
P
with respect to this torsion pair. So
Y\in\mathscr{Y}
and
X\in\mathscr{X}
. Let
X^{\prime}
be an arbitrary object of
\mathscr{X}
. By applying the functor
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,X^{\prime})
to the above short exact sequence, we get
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X,X^{\prime})=0
and therefore
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{X}}(X,X^{\prime})=0
. Hence
X
is an Ext-projective object in
\mathscr{X}
. Since
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{X}
, there exists an exact sequence

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

where
T^{0},T^{1}\in\mathscr{T}
and
g
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation. Now the exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle gf}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

is the desired one. In fact, it is easy to see that
gf
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
.

Finally we show that
\mathscr{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Let
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Since
\mathscr{X}
is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, there exists a right
\mathscr{X}
-approximation
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
of
A
. Consider a right
\mathscr{T}
-approximation
T\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}X
of
X
, which exists because
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Now it is easy to see that
T\stackrel{{\scriptstyle fg}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
is a right
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
A
.
∎

We illustrate our previous result with the following example.

Example 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}={\rm Rep}^{\rm{fp}}_{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathscr{B}={\rm Rep}^{\rm{fp}}_{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
.
On one hand, since
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
are isomorphic as posets, it is clear that
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are equivalent as categories.
On the other hand, the natural inclusion of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
into
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
induces an embedding of
\mathscr{B}
into
\mathscr{A}
.
In fact, it is easy to see that
\mathscr{B}
is a functorially finite wide subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
which is both a torsion and a torsion free class.
Set

\mathscr{T}={\rm{add}}(\{k_{[1,y)}|\ 1<y\leq\infty\}).

It is easy to see that
\mathscr{T}
is a tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{B}
and hence a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{B}
.
So, Proposition
3.5
implies that
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Note that
\mathscr{T}
is not a tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
since the
\mathscr{T}
-approximation

is not a monomorphism, in fact it is a zero morphism.

By
[
R
, Proposition 3.42]
, if
\mathscr{A}
is an abelian category with enough projective objects such that
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})={\rm{add}}(P)
, then for every tilting subcategory
\mathscr{T}
of
\mathscr{A}
there exists an object
T\in\mathscr{T}
such that
\mathscr{T}={\rm{add}}(T)
. In fact
T
is a tilting object. Note that an object
T\in\mathscr{A}
is called a tilting object if

(i)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(T,T)=0
.

(ii)

Projective dimension of
T
is at most one.

(iii)

For every projective object
P\in\mathscr{A}
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
T^{0},T^{1}\in{\rm{add}}(T)
.

We prove a version of this fact for
\tau
-tilting subcategories. To do this we provide the following definition of a
\tau
-tilting object in an abelian category.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. An object
T\in\mathscr{A}
is called a support
\tau
-tilting object if

(i)

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(T,{\rm{Fac}}(T))=0
.

(ii)

For every projective object
P\in\mathscr{A}
, there exists an exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
T^{0},T^{1}\in{\rm{add}}(T)
and
f
is a left
{\rm{add}}(T)
-approximation.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Let
T

\tau
-tilting object in
\mathscr{A}
.
It follows directly from the definition that
{\rm{add}}(T)
is a weak support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

4.
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triples

In this section we introduce a triple of full additive subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
, that will be called a
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triple, or simply a
\tau
-triple, and show that there is a bijection between the collection of all
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
and the collection of all
\tau
-triples.
The Proposition
2.7
motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
A pair of full subcategories
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
of
\mathscr{A}
is called a
\tau
-cotorsion pair if

1.

\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}
.

2.

For every projective object
P\in\mathscr{A}
, there exists an exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}{D}\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0,

where
D\in\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
,
C\in\mathscr{C}
and
f
is a left
\mathscr{D}
-approximation.

3.

\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}={\rm Rep}^{\rm{fp}}_{k}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and set

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}=

\displaystyle{\rm{add}}(\{k_{[x,\infty)}|\ 0\leq x<\infty\}\cup\{k_{[x,y)}|\ 0\leq x<y<1\})

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}=

\displaystyle{\rm{add}}(\{k_{[x,y)}|\ 1\leq x<y\leq\infty\}).

Then
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
is a
\tau
-cotorsion pair.
Note that it is not a cotorsion pair, because
\mathscr{D}
does not contain the injective representations.
To see it is a
\tau
-cotorsion pair, first note that

\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}={\rm{add}}(\{k_{[x,\infty)}|x\geq 1\})

is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Indeed, every indecomposable representation
k_{[a,b)}
admits a right
\mathscr{C}\cap\mathscr{D}
-approximation as follows

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 1\leq a<b\leq\infty;\\
k_{[1,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 0<a<1<b\leq\infty;\\
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\longrightarrow k_{[a,b)},&\ 0\leq a<b\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

Now let
k_{[a,\infty)}
be an indecomposable projective in
\mathscr{A}
.
If
a\geq 1
, the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[a,\infty)}\longrightarrow 0,

and if
a<1
, the exact sequence

k_{[a,\infty)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}k_{[b,\infty)}\longrightarrow k_{[b,\infty)}\longrightarrow 0

with
b\geq 1
, are the desired exace sequences.
Finally, it is straightforward to see that
\mathscr{C}={}^{\perp_{1}}\mathscr{D}
.

It is shown in
[

, Lemma 2.12]
that if
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are two subcategories of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
such that

(1)

;

(2)

\mathscr{D}
is closed under factor objects;

(3)

For every object

, there exists a short exact sequence

where
D\in\mathscr{D}
and
C\in\mathscr{C}
,

then every object in
\mathscr{C}
is of projective dimension at most one. In particular, a cotorsion pair
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{T})
satisfies all the above conditions when it is embedded in a cotorsion torsion triple
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{T},\mathscr{F})
. So every object in
\mathscr{C}
has projective dimension at most one.

The following example shows that the injectivity of the morphism

in Condition
(3)
is essential.
We note that this is implicit in the proof of
[
BBOS
, Lemma 2.12]
.
Also note that it follows automatically from the previous definition that the map
\varphi
is a left
\mathscr{D}
-approximation.

Definition 4.4
.

A triple

-triple.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Then every cotorsion torsion triple

is a

5.
\tau
-tilting subcategories and
\tau
-triples

In this section, we show that there is a bijection between the collection of all support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
and the collection of all
\tau
-cotorsion torsion triples in
\mathscr{A}
.
In case we start with a tilting subcategory, this bijection specializes to the one introduced in
[
BBOS
, Theorem 2.29]
.
We prepare the ground with some preliminary results.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
. Then
{}^{\perp_{1}}{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})\cap{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})=\mathscr{T}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{A}
is an abelian category with enough projectives
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
, we have
\mathscr{A}\simeq{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
, where
{\rm{{mod\mbox{-}}}}{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
is the category of finitely presented functors on
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
, see
[
Be2
, Corollaries 3.9 and 3.10]
.
Now the result follows by the part
(ii)
of the proof of
[
IJY
, Proposition 5.3]
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
Then

({}^{\perp_{1}}{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}),{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}))

is a
\tau
-cotorsion pair.

Proof.

The first condition of Definition
4.1
holds trivially.
For the second condition, consider the exact sequence

P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

where
f
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
and
T^{0},T^{1}\in\mathscr{T}
, which exists for every projective object
P
.
Now by Lemma
5.1
, we observe that
T^{0},T^{1}\in{}^{\perp_{1}}{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})\cap{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
.
So it remains to show that
f
is a left
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
-approximation of
P
.
To show this, let
X\in{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
and let
g:P\longrightarrow X
be a morphism.
Consider an epimorphism
\pi:T\longrightarrow X
with
T\in\mathscr{T}
.
Since
P
is a projective object, there is a morphism
h:P\longrightarrow T
such that
\pi h=g
.
Now because
f
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation, there is a morphism
t:T^{0}\longrightarrow T
such that

.
Therefore a morphism
\pi t:T^{0}\longrightarrow X
exists such that
\pi tf=\pi h=g
.
Finally Lemma
5.1
, implies that
{}^{\perp_{1}}{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})\cap{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})=\mathscr{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.
∎

By
[
BBOS
, Proposition 2.22]
if
\mathscr{T}
is a tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, then the pair
({\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}),\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}})
is a torsion pair.
We use the same technique to prove the validity of the same result for
\tau
-tilting subcategories.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. If
\mathscr{T}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is a
\tau
-tilting subcategory, then
({\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}),\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}})
is a torsion pair.

Proof.

Let
X\in{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
. Then there exists an epimorphism
\mathscr{T}\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0
with
T\in\mathscr{T}
. The exact sequence

shows that
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(X,Y)=0
whenever
Y\in\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}}
.

Now let
A\in\mathscr{A}
be an arbitrary object. Since
\mathscr{T}
is contravariantly finite subcategory, there exists a right
\mathscr{T}
-approximation
\varphi:T\longrightarrow A
. Consider the short exact sequence

where
{\rm{Im}}\varphi\in{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
. By applying the functor
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},-)
, we have a long exact exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\rm{Im}}\varphi)|_{\mathscr{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,A)|_{\mathscr{T}}\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\rm{Coker}}\varphi)|_{\mathscr{T}}\longrightarrow{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\rm{Im}}\varphi)|_{\mathscr{T}}.

Now since
f
is a right
\mathscr{T}
-approximation,
f_{*}
is an epimorphism. Also since
\mathscr{T}
is
\tau
-tilting and
{\rm{Im}}\varphi\in{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
,
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\rm{Im}}\varphi)|_{\mathscr{T}}=0
. So
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(-,{\rm{Coker}}\varphi)|_{\mathscr{T}}=0
and hence
{\rm{Coker}}\varphi\in\mathscr{T}^{\perp_{0}}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects. Let

To this end we need the following easy lemma.

8.
Connection to silting and cosilting modules

This section is divided to two subsections and is devoted to the study the connections between support
\tau
- and support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories and silting and cosilting theories in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, where
R
is an associative unitary ring. Based on these theories we are able to characterize all support
\tau
- and support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
.

8.1.
Silting modules and support
\tau
-tilting subcategories

Our aim in this subsection is to characterize all support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
. We do this by providing a bijection between the equivalence classes of all support
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
and the collection of all equivalent classes of the certain
R
-modules, the so-called finendo quasitilting
R
-modules. It is known that all silting modules are finendo quasitilting. As a result, it will be shown that
{\rm{Add}}(S)
is a
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, where
S
is a silting
R
-module.

For a module

in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, let
{\rm{Add}}(M)
denote the class of all modules isomorphic to a direct summand of an arbitrary direct sum of copies of
M
.
We also let
{\rm{Gen}}(M)
to be the subcategory of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
consisting of all
M
-generated modules, i.e. all modules isomorphic to an epimorphic images of modules in
{\rm{Add}}(M)
.
and
{\rm Pres}(M)
to be the subcategory of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
consisting of all
M
-presented modules, i.e. all modules that admit an
{\rm{Add}}(M)
-presentation.
Recall that an
{\rm{Add}}(M)
-presentation of an
R
-module
X
is an exact sequence

M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{0}\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0,

with
M_{1}
and
M_{0}
in
{\rm{Add}}(M)
.

Let
\sigma
be a morphism in
{\rm{Prj}}(R)
. Let
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}
be the class of all modules
M
in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
such that the induced homomorphism
{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(\sigma,M)
is surjective.

Definition 8.1.1
.

(see
[
AMV
, Definition 3.7]
)
An
R
-module
S
is called a partial silting module if there exists a projective presentation
\sigma
of
S
such that
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}
contains
S
and is a torsion class in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
.
S
is called a silting module if there is a projective presentation
\sigma
of
S
such that
{\rm{Gen}}(S)=\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}.

Remark 8.1.2
.

By
[
AMV
, Remark 3.8]
, every silting module is a partial silting module, hence
{\rm{Gen}}(S)
is a torsion class. Support
\tau
-tilting modules over a finite dimensional
k
-algebra are examples of silting modules
[
AMV
, Proposition 3.15]
.

Definition 8.1.3
.

(see
[
AMV
, Lemdef 3.1]
)
An
R
-module
T
is called quasitilting if
{\rm Pres}(T)={\rm{Gen}}(T)
and
T
is Ext-projective in
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
.

Following proposition collects some of the basic properties of the quasitilting modules. Their proofs can be found in Lemdef 3.1, Lemma 3.3 and Proposition 3.2 of
[
AMV
]
. Recall that an
R
-modules
T
is called finendo if it is finitely generated over its endomorphism ring.

Proposition 8.1.4
.

Let
T
be a quasitilting
R
-module. Then the following statements hold true.

1.

If
X\in{\rm{Gen}}(T)
then, there exist a set
J
and a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Ker}}\pi\longrightarrow T^{(J)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}X\longrightarrow 0

such that
{\rm{Ker}}\pi\in{\rm{Gen}}(T)
and
T^{(J)}
is the coproduct of copies of
T
indexed by
J
.
That is,
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
is closed with respect to the kernels of epimorphisms.

2.

{\rm{Add}}(T)
is the class of
{\rm{Ext}}
-projective modules in
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
.

3.

The following are equivalent.

(i)

T
is a finendo quasitilting
R
-module.

(ii)

{\rm{Gen}}(T)
is a torsion class and

is a tilting
R/{\rm{ann}}(R)
-module.

(iii)

T
is an
{\rm{Ext}}
-projective module in
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
and there exists an exact sequence

R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{0}\longrightarrow T_{1}\longrightarrow 0

such that
T_{0},T_{1}\in{\rm{Add}}(T)
and

is a left
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
-approximation.

Proposition 8.1.5
.

Let

be a finendo quasitilting module in

is a support
\tau

{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R

Proof.

We show the validity of conditions of Definition

Note that

Then

By Statement

of Proposition
8.1.4

R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{0}\longrightarrow T_{1}\longrightarrow 0

and

is a left
{\rm{Gen}}(T)
-approximation. Since

is also a left
{\rm{Add}}(T)
-approximation. Now let

be a projective

\textstyle{R^{(J)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{(J)}}

.

Let

Definition 8.1.9

8.2.
Cosilting modules and support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories

Our aim in this subsection is to characterize all support
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
in term of quasicotilting
R
-modules. It is known that all cosilting modules are quasicotilting. As a result, we show that
{\rm{Prod}}(C)
is a
\tau^{-}
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, where

is a cosilting

-module, where
{\rm{Prod}}(M)
denote the class of all modules isomorphic to an arbitrary direct product of copies of

For a module

{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R

{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R

{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R

Recall that a

and

Let

{\rm{Inj}}(R)

{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R

Definition 8.2.1
.

(see
[

By
[

, Remark 3.2]
, every cosilting module is a partial cosilting module. In particular, for every cosilting module

{\rm{Cogen}}(C)
is a torsion-free class.

Definition 8.2.2
.

(see
[

An

9.
Applications to quiver representations

This section is devoted to produce support
\tau
-tilting and
n
-tilting subcategories of category of representation of quivers, where
n
is a non-negative integer.
We divide the section in four subsections.
In the first subsection we recall some known definitions and properties of the category
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
of representations of a finite acyclic quiver
Q
over an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
with enough projective objects.
In the second subsection we produce support
\tau
-tilting subcategories in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
from certain
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
.
In the third we construct (co)silting modules in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}RQ
from (co)silting modules in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
.
Finally, in the last subsection, we use similar techniques to produce
(n+1)
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
from
n
-tilting subcategories of
\mathscr{A}
.

9.1.
Notions on quiver representations

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
and
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite acyclic quiver with vertex set
Q_{0}
and arrow set
Q_{1}
.
An arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
of sourse
i=s(\alpha)
and target
j=t(\alpha)
is usually denoted by
\alpha:i\rightarrow j
.
We denote by
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
the category of representations of
Q
in
\mathscr{A}
. An object
X
in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
is defined by the following data:

1.

To each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
is associated an object
X_{i}
in
\mathscr{A}
.

2.

To each arrow
\alpha:i\rightarrow j
in
Q_{1}
is associated a morphism
X_{\alpha}:X_{i}\longrightarrow X_{j}
.

A morphism
\varphi:X\longrightarrow Y
in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
is a family
\{\varphi_{i}:X_{i}\longrightarrow Y_{i}\}_{i\in Q_{0}}
of morphisms in
\mathscr{A}
such that for each arrow
\alpha:i\longrightarrow j
in
Q_{1}
, the diagram

\textstyle{X_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{X_{\alpha}}
\textstyle{Y_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Y_{\alpha}}
\textstyle{X_{j}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{j}}
\textstyle{Y_{j}}

is commutative.

The category
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
is an abelian category.
Kernels, cokernels, and images in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
are computed vertex-wise in
\mathscr{A}
.
In fact, a sequence
X\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow Z
in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
is exact if and only if for every vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, the sequence
X_{i}\longrightarrow Y_{i}\longrightarrow Z_{i}
is exact in
\mathscr{A}
.

For each vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, there exists the evaluation functor

e_{i}:{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})\longrightarrow\mathscr{A}

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ X\mapsto X_{i}

which sends each representation
X\in{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
to the object
X_{i}\in\mathscr{A}
at vertex
i
.
It is clear that the evaluation functor
e_{i}
is exact and moreover it has an exact left and also an exact right adjoint, which will be denoted by
e_{i}^{\lambda}
and
e_{i}^{\rho}
, respectively.
Let us recall the constructions of
e_{i}^{\lambda},e_{i}^{\rho}:\mathscr{A}\longrightarrow{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
more explicitly,
cf.

[
HJ
]
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{A}
.
Then
e_{i}^{\lambda}(A)_{j}=\bigoplus_{Q(i,j)}A
, where
Q(i,j)
denotes the set of paths starting in
i
and ending in
j
.
The morphisms are natural inclusions, that is, for any arrow
\alpha:j\longrightarrow k
, we set
e_{i}^{\lambda}(A)_{\alpha}:\bigoplus_{Q(i,j)}A\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{Q(i,k)}A
.

The right adjoint
e_{i}^{\rho}
is defined dually.
Let
A\in\mathscr{A}
.
Then
e_{i}^{\rho}(A)_{j}=\bigoplus_{Q(j,i)}A
.
The morphisms are natural projections.
Moreover, the functor
e_{i}^{\rho}
has a right adjoint, which will be denoted by
Re_{i}^{\rho}
.

It is proved that the sets

\{e_{i}^{\lambda}(P):\ i\in Q_{0},P\in{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})\}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \{e_{i}^{\rho}(I):\ i\in Q_{0},I\in{\rm{Inj}}(\mathscr{A})\}

are sets of projective generators and injective cogenerators for the category
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
, respectively.
For details of the proofs see e.g.
[
EE
]
and
[
EER
]
.

9.2.
Constructing
\tau
-tilting subcategories of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})

Our aim in this subsection is to provide a systematic technique to construct, starting from a certain
\tau
-tilting subcategory
\mathscr{T}
of an abelian category
\mathscr{A}
, a new
\tau
-tilting subcategory in the category of representation of a finite and acyclic quiver in
\mathscr{A}
.
For the proof of the main result of this subsection, we need the following lemma.
Although it seems that it is known to the experts, we could not find a reference.
So we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 9.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects and
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite and acyclic quiver.
Then for every
X,Y\in{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
there exists the long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{l})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\ \longrightarrow\ \bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r})\ \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{l})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Ext}}^{2}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\ \longrightarrow\cdots,

where here and throughout we set
\mathscr{R}:={\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathscr{R}:={\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.
By
[
BBOS
, Lemma 3.5]
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}e_{l}^{\lambda}(X_{r})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}e_{r}^{\lambda}(X_{r})\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0

which is natural in
X
.
By applying the functor
{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(-,Y)
to this sequence, we get the following long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{r}^{\lambda}(X_{r}),Y)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{l}^{\lambda}(X_{r}),Y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\ \ \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{r}^{\lambda}(X_{r}),Y)\ \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{l}^{\lambda}(X_{r}),Y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\ {\rm{Ext}}^{2}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\ \ \longrightarrow\cdots.

Now the result follows in view of the adjoint pair
(e_{i}^{\lambda},e_{i})
and using the fact that the adjunction between
e_{i}^{\lambda}
and
e_{i}
extends to
{\rm{Ext}}^{t}
, for all
t\geq i
, see
[
HJ
, Proposition 5.2]
.
∎

Now we can state and prove the main result of this part.

Theorem 9.2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects and
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite and acyclic quiver.
Let
\mathscr{T}
be a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
such that
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
is closed with respect to the kernels of epimorphisms.
Then

\mathbb{T}={\rm{add}}\{e_{i}^{\rho}(T)|~i\in Q_{0},T\in\mathscr{T}\}

is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

We show the validity of conditions of Definition
3.1
.
For the first condition, let
i,j\in Q_{0}
and
T\in\mathscr{T}
.
We show that

{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T),{\rm{Fac}}(e_{j}^{\rho}(T))=0.

Set
X:=e_{i}^{\rho}(T)
and pick
Y\in{\rm{Fac}}(e_{j}^{\rho}(T))
.
Then for every
r\in Q_{0}
,
X_{r}
is the sum of some finite copies of
T
, maybe zero, and
Y_{r}
is in
{\rm{Fac}}(T)
.
Moreover, for every
\alpha:r\rightarrow l\in Q_{1}
,
Y_{\alpha}:Y_{r}\longrightarrow Y_{l}
is an epimorphism.
Hence since by assumption
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
is closed with respect to the kernels of epimorphisms, we deduce that
{\rm{Ker}}Y_{\alpha}\in{\rm{Fac}}(T)
.
This in particular implies that for every
T\in\mathscr{T}
, the induced morphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(T,Y_{r})\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(T,Y_{l})

is an epimorphism.

Hence, in the exact sequence

\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\alpha:r\rightarrow l}{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{l})\longrightarrow\\
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(X,Y)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r}),

of the above lemma, we deduce that
\varphi
is an epimorphism.
So to show the result, it is enough to show that

\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r})=0.

This follows from the fact
{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T}))
, because
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
and the fact that

\bigoplus_{r\in Q_{0}}{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(X_{r},Y_{r})\subseteq{\rm{Ext}}^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{T},{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})).

Now we show the validity of the second condition.
It is enough to show it only for the projective generators of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
, i.e. for representations of the form

e_{i}^{\lambda}(P)
, where
P
is a projective object in
\mathscr{A}
.
Let
\{\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{k}\}
be the set of all longest paths in
Q
starting from
i
.
Since
\mathscr{T}
is a support
\tau
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, for
P
there exists an exact sequence
P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}T^{1}\longrightarrow 0
, such that
T^{0},T^{1}\in\mathscr{T}
and
f
is a left
\mathscr{T}
-approximation of
P
.
Since
{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
is closed with respect to the kernels of epimorphisms,
{\rm{Ker}}g\in{\rm{Fac}}(\mathscr{T})
and so the induced short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\rm{Ker}}g\longrightarrow T^{0}\longrightarrow T^{1}\longrightarrow 0

splits and so
{\rm{Ker}}g\in\mathscr{T}
.
Take the exact sequence

e^{\lambda}_{i}(P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{q=1}^{k}e^{\rho}_{t(\rho_{q})}(T^{0})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{q=1}^{k}e^{\rho}_{t(\rho_{q})}(T^{1})\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}e^{\rho}_{s(\alpha)}({\rm{Ker}}g)\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathfrak{I}\subset Q_{1}
is the set of arrows
\alpha
of
Q
such that
\alpha
is not part of any path in the set
\{\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{k}\}
but there is a path in that set passing through
t(\alpha)
.
An easy verification shows that this is the desired sequence for
e^{\lambda}_{i}(P)
.
In particular,
\psi
is a left
\mathbb{T}
-approximation of
e^{\lambda}_{i}(P)
.

In order to complete the proof, we have to show that
\mathbb{T}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.
Let
X\in{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
. For each
i
, consider a right
\mathscr{T}
-approximation
\pi^{i}:T^{i}\longrightarrow Re_{i}^{\rho}(X)
, where
Re_{i}^{\rho}
is the right adjoint of
e_{i}^{\rho}
.
Following the same argument as in
[
BBOS
, Proposition 3.9]
one can show that

\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}e_{i}^{\rho}(T^{i})\longrightarrow X

is a right
\mathbb{T}
-approximation of
X
.
∎

9.3.
(Co)silting objects in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}RQ

Let
R
be an associative ring with unity and
Q
be a finite and acyclic quiver. In this subsection we construct silting, resp. cosilting, objects in the category of representations of
Q
in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
,
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
, from silting, resp. cosilting, modules in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
.
Note that
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
is equivalent to the
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}RQ
, where
RQ
denotes the path algebra of
Q
over
R
.
So by a silting, resp. cosilting, object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
we mean a silting, resp. cosilting, module in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}RQ
.

Theorem 9.3.1
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite and acyclic quiver.

(i)

Let
S
be a silting module in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
. Let
i\in Q_{0}
be an arbitrary vertex of
Q
. Then
e_{i}^{\lambda}(S)
is a silting object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
.

(ii)

Let
C
be a cosilting module in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
. Let
i\in Q_{0}
be an arbitrary vertex of
Q
. Then
e_{i}^{\rho}(C)
is a cosilting object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
.

Proof.

(i)
Let
S
be a silting
R
-module. By definition, there exists a projective presentation

P_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{0}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0

of
S
such that
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}={\rm{Gen}}(S)
. By applying the exact functor
e_{i}^{\lambda}
on the projective presentation of
S
and using the fact that
e_{i}^{\lambda}
is an exact functor that preserves projectives, we get the projective presentation

e_{i}^{\lambda}(P_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e_{i}^{\lambda}(\sigma)}}{{\longrightarrow}}e_{i}^{\lambda}(P_{0})\longrightarrow e_{i}^{\lambda}(S)\longrightarrow 0

of
e_{i}^{\lambda}(S)
. To complete the proof, we show that
\mathscr{D}_{e_{i}^{\lambda}(\sigma)}={\rm{Gen}}(e_{i}^{\lambda}(S))
.

Let
X\in\mathscr{D}_{e_{i}^{\lambda}(\sigma)}
. So there exists an epimorphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{i}^{\lambda}(P_{0}),X)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(e_{i}^{\lambda}(P_{1}),X)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{R}
means
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
. The adjoint pair
(e_{i}^{\lambda},e_{i})
induces the epimorphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(P_{0},X_{i})\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(P_{1},X_{i})\longrightarrow 0.

This, in turn, implies that
X_{i}\in\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}={\rm{Gen}}(S)
. Therefore,
e_{i}^{\lambda}(X_{i})\in e_{i}^{\lambda}({\rm{Gen}}(S))
. On the other hand, by
[
BBOS
, Lemma 3.5]
, there exists an epimorphism

The following result provides a partial converse to the above theorem. Recall that a vertex
i\in Q_{0}
is called a source, resp. a sink, of
Q
if there is no arrows
\alpha\in Q_{1}
such that
t(\alpha)=i
, resp.
s(\alpha)=i
.

Theorem 9.3.2
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a finite and acyclic quiver.

(i)

Let
X
be a silting object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
. Then
e_{i}(X)
is a silting module in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, provided
i\in Q_{0}
is a source of
Q
.

(ii)

Let
Y
be a cosilting object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
. Then
e_{i}(Y)
is a cosilting module in
{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R
, provided
i\in Q_{0}
is a sink of
Q
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
X
is a silting object in
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
, there exists a projective presentation

P^{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}P^{0}\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0

of
X
such that
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}={\rm{Gen}}(X)
. By applying the exact functor
e_{i}
on the this exact sequence we get the exact sequence

P^{1}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P^{0}_{i}\longrightarrow X_{i}\longrightarrow 0,

which is a projective presentation of
e_{i}(X)=X_{i}
.

In order to complete the proof, we have to show that
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma_{i}}={\rm{Gen}}(X_{i})
. First let
M\in\mathscr{D}_{\sigma_{i}}
. Then there exists an epimorphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(P^{0}_{i},M)\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{R}(P^{1}_{i},M)\longrightarrow 0.

By using the adjoint pair
(e_{i},e_{i}^{\rho})
, we get the epimorphism

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(P^{0},e_{i}^{\rho}(M))\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathscr{R}}(P^{1},e_{i}^{\rho}(M)\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathscr{R}
means
{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
. So
e_{i}^{\rho}(M)\in\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}={\rm{Gen}}(X)
. Therefore
M=e_{i}e_{i}^{\rho}(M)\in e_{i}({\rm{Gen}}(X))
. But
e_{i}({\rm{Gen}}(X))={\rm{Gen}}(e_{i}(X))={\rm{Gen}}(X_{i})
. Hence
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma_{i}}\subseteq{\rm{Gen}}(X_{i})
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
Y_{i}\in{\rm{Gen}}(X_{i})
. We define
Y\in{\rm Rep}(Q,{\rm{Mod\mbox{-}}}R)
by setting

at source vertex
i
, and
0
elsewhere. Since
i
is a source, it follows easily that
Y\in{\rm{Gen}}(X)
. Therefore
Y\in\mathscr{D}_{\sigma}
which implies that
Y_{i}\in\mathscr{D}_{\sigma_{i}}
. Hence we have the equality
\mathscr{D}_{\sigma_{i}}={\rm{Gen}}(X_{i})
and the proof is complete.

(ii)
The proof is just dual of the proof of part
(i)
. So we skip the proof.
∎

9.4.
Higher tilting subcategories of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
denote the subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
consisting of all projective objects.
In
[
BBOS
, Proposition 3.9]
it is shown that

\mathscr{T}^{\prime}={\rm{add}}(\{e_{i}^{\rho}(P)|~i\in Q_{0},P\in{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})\})

is a (
1
-)tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
, where
Q
is a finite and acyclic quiver.

Now if we interpret
{\rm{Prj}}(\mathscr{A})
as a
0
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
, then by the above result, starting from a
0
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
we get a
1
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.
In our next and last result we provide a higher version of this result by constructing an
(n+1)
-tilting subcategory in
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
starting from an
n
-tilting subcategory of
\mathscr{A}
.

Theorem 9.4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category with enough projective objects.
Let
n
be a non-negative integer. Let
Q
be a finite and acyclic quiver.
For an
n
-tilting subcategory
\mathscr{T}
of
\mathscr{A}
put

\mathscr{T}^{\prime}={\rm{add}}(\{e_{i}^{\rho}(T)|~i\in Q_{0},T\in\mathscr{T}\}).

Then
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
is an
(n+1)
-tilting subcategory of
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

We show the validity of the conditions of Definition
2.1
. The validity of Condition
(i)
follows by the similar argument in the proof of Theorem
3.5
.
For the validity of Condition
(ii)
, we have to show that for all
t\geq 1
and
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{T}
,

{\rm{Ext}}^{t}_{{\rm Rep}(Q,A)}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1}),e_{j}^{\rho}(T_{2}))=0.

[
HJ
]
implies that

{\rm{Ext}}^{t}_{{\rm Rep}(Q,A)}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1}),e_{j}^{\rho}(T_{2}))\cong{\rm{Ext}}^{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1})_{j},T_{2}).

e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1})_{j}
is either zero or a sum of copies of
T_{1}
and
\mathscr{T}
is an
n
-tilting subcategory, hence

{\rm{Ext}}^{t}_{\mathscr{A}}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1})_{j},T_{2})=0.

{\rm{Ext}}^{t}_{{\rm Rep}(Q,A)}(e_{i}^{\rho}(T_{1}),e_{j}^{\rho}(T_{2}))=0.

For the Condition
(iii)

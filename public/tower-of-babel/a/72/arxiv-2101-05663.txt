Twist-minimal trace formula for holomorphic cusp forms
Kieran Child University of Bristol

Abstract
. We derive an explicit formula for the trace of an arbitrary Hecke operator on spaces of twist-minimal holomorphic cusp forms with arbitrary level and character, and weight at least 2. We show that this formula provides an efficient way of computing basis elements for newform or cusp form spaces. This work was motivated by the development of a twist-minimal trace formula in the non-holomorphic case by Booker, Lee and Strömbergsson, as well as the presentation of a fully generalised trace formula for the holomorphic case by Cohen and Strömberg.



1.
Introduction

Modular forms play a central role in much of modern number theory. Their properties have been crucial in establishing the modularity theorem (in turn proving Fermat’s last theorem, see
[
8
]
), and the Fourier coefficients of modular forms provide explicit data for problems such as the congruent number problem, representations of integers by quadratic forms, and classifications of Galois representations (see
[
23
]
for an overview of several applications).

Cusp forms are of particular interest for many reasons. For example, cusp forms give rise to holomorphic
L
-functions (which in turn account for all
L
-functions arising from elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
) and are associated with families of irreducible
\ell
-adic Galois representations following work by Deligne and Serre (see
[
10
, Section 9.6]
).

While there exist simple, explicit expressions for Fourier coefficients of Eisenstein series, such as in
[
22
]
, basis elements for spaces of cusp forms are trickier to compute. In this paper we derive a formula for the trace of Hecke operators on spaces of so-called ‘twist-minimal’ forms (Theorem
2.1
), and demonstrate that this is sufficient to recover the Fourier coefficients of basis elements of cusp form spaces (Theorem
2.2
). We give a result enabling the extraction of Fourier coefficients of newforms from twist-minimal representatives (Theorem
2.3
).

The value of this paper is twofold. Firstly, the formula is given in an explicit, classical form, and so the terms are readily computable. Secondly, the sieving of the formula to twist-minimal spaces results in much simpler expressions to compute these spaces, and allows us to calculate a smaller number of twist-minimal spaces in order to recover a large number of different cusp form spaces at once. This efficiently provides explicit data for aforementioned problems on representation of integers and Galois representations. The results can also be incorporated into Schaeffer’s method for computing weight-1 spaces, given in
[
20
]
. Further work implementing the results of this paper for the efficient computation of all forms of weight 1 and level at most 10,000 is currently in progress. A full explanation and discussion of the advantages of this method is given at the end of section
2
.

The idea of using trace formulae to derive information on modular forms dates back to the 1950s. Following the construction of certain non-holomorphic cusp forms, now called Maass cusp forms, by Maass in
[
16
]
, Selberg sought to demonstrate the infinitude of such forms and provide an asymptotic expression for their count, which he achieved with a trace formula in
[
21
]
. He and Eichler continued work in this direction, leading to a trace formula for the action of Hecke operators
T_{n}
on spaces of holomorphic cusp forms for the full modular group and square-free
n
in
[
11
]
. Following work by others in
[
12
]
and
[
17
]
, this formula is now available in full generality for traces of any Hecke operator on spaces of any level and character. Although this formula can be used to find Fourier coefficients for basis elements of cusp form spaces (as done in
[
3
]
, with a newform sieve, for the generation of LMFDB data), our formula contains much simpler expressions, and is more efficient for this task in the case that the level
N
is not square-free.

On the structural side, twists of modular forms were studied in
[
2
]
and the concept of a twist-minimal form

[

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 covers the required background theory leading to the presentation of two results. The formula for the trace of Hecke operators on spaces of twist-minimal modular forms is given in Theorem

2.
Twist-minimal trace formula

2.1.
Preliminary theory

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a Dirichlet character

is a homomorphism:

\chi:\left(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\right)^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}.

(2.1)

The domain of

is extended to

by the canonical mod-

If

\chi(x)=\prod_{p\mid N}\chi_{p}(x).

(2.2)

We use this factorisation to define
\chi_{p}
for any prime

. The order of a non-trivial character is the least

such that

. This is denoted Ord

. We define twist-minimal characters as in

Definition 2.1

Fix a prime

This completes the prerequisites on Dirichlet characters, and we move onto modular forms. Let

(2.4)

For any

\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

Fix

(1)

(2)

\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}|f|^{2}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

Modular forms have Fourier expansions:

(2.6)

Such an expansion completely describes the form, and so it is these

All modular forms of weight

and character

. Let

be a modular form of weight

and character

we define the generic Hecke operator

This definition is more general than in the original literature, where for

T_{n}^{\chi}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{ad=n}\chi(a)a^{k}\sum_{b\text{ mod }d}f\left(\frac{az+b}{d}\right).

(2.7)

Here,

T_{nm}^{\chi}=T_{n}^{\chi}T_{m}^{\chi}

A cusp form is a modular form for which the slash action
f|_{k}\gamma(z)
tends to 0 as
\Im(z)\rightarrow\infty
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Cusp forms of weight

, level

and character

form a subspace of modular forms, denoted
\mathcal{S}_{k}(N,\chi)
. This subspace is stable under the action of Hecke operators, and is a Hilbert space with respect to the Petersson inner product, defined as:

\langle f,g\rangle_{N}:=\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

(2.8)

This inner product is used to decompose the space of cusp forms. Fix
M|N
and
d|\frac{N}{M}
, then it is verified that for any level
M
cusp form
f
the function
g(z)=f(dz)
is a level
N
cusp form with the same weight and character as

. Forms of level
N
which arise in this manner from any level

are called oldforms, and the orthogonal complement (with respect to the Petersson inner product) of the space spanned by oldforms is called the newform space. It is denoted
\sknew(N,\chi)
, and was studied in detail in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
. The utility of this decomposition is that one can recover data on cusp form spaces solely from data on newform spaces, and so the scope of any study into cusp forms is reduced.

A cusp form which is an eigenfunction for all Hecke operators is called a Hecke-eigenform. Many results about the corresponding Hecke-eigenvalues are given in
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
. We say that two Hecke-eigenforms in
\skfull(N,\chi)
are equivalent if they have the same eigenvalues for all but finitely many
T_{p}
. We thus normalise these forms by setting their first Fourier coefficient to 1. For a normalised Hecke-eigenform
f
, the eigenvalue of
T_{n}^{\chi}
is equal to the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of

.

By
[
1

there is a basis for
\sknew(N,\chi)
consisting of representatives from distinct equivalence classes of simultaneous eigenforms. In the other direction, by
[
1
, Lemmas 21, 22]
, every simultaneous eigenform of a given level

is equivalent to a normalised newform of some level
M\mid N
.

We define the twist of a form
f
by a Dirichlet character
\psi
by the following action on its Fourier expansion:

f_{\psi}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\psi(n)a_{n}e^{2\pi inz}.

(2.9)

We immediately get the following interaction between twists and hecke operators.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a Hecke-eigenform of weight
k
, level
N
and character
\chi
. Let
\psi
be a character of level
M
. If
(M,n)=1
then
T_{n}^{\chi\psi^{2}}(f_{\psi})=\psi(n)(T_{n}^{\chi}(f))_{\psi}
.

This relation, first stated in
[
2
]
, is checked by expanding out the definitions of the operators involved. We also verify that

is a cusp form with character
\chi\psi^{2}
. This means that the twist of any Hecke-eigenform is equivalent to a newform basis element. We can use twists to pass between Hecke-eigenforms of different levels and characters.

We define a twist-minimal form as a normalised newform which is not equivalent to any form arising as the twist of a newform from a lower level. Twist-minimal forms of weight
k
, level
N
and character
\chi
again span a Hilbert space, stable under Hecke operators, which we denote
\skmin(N,\chi)
. As in the newform decomposition, the immediate utility here is from further restriction to the scope of study.

These results give us an efficient way of computing basis elements of cusp form spaces. The most computationally expensive term in Theorem

3.2.

Definition 3.1

Let

We know from Lemma

Now, assume that

is now equivalent to the Möbius
\mu
function, we get the much simpler decomposition formula:

f^{\min}(p^{e},\chi)=f(p^{e},\chi)-\underset{s<e}{\delta}f(1,\mathbbm{1})

(4.49)

For the
A_{1}
term, if
s>0
then as
p\mid n
we necessarily have
\chi(\sqrt{n})=0
. Thus, we only have a non-zero
A_{1}
term when
e=1
and
s=0
. In this case, we get
R_{p}^{\min}=-1
and so in full:

R_{p}^{\min}(p,\chi,n)=-\underset{s=0}{\delta}

(4.50)

For the
A_{2}
term, depending on
\gamma
, we have at most two elements in
\Omega
. If
\gamma=\nu_{p}(t^{2}-4n)
is non-zero, then we must have
p\mid x
whereupon
\chi(x)=0
. On the other hand, if
\gamma=0
then we get
J=\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t+u}{2}\right)+\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t-u}{2}\right)
. We get:

R_{p}^{\min}(p^{e},\chi,t,n)=\begin{cases}-\left(p^{\nu_{p}(\ell)}+\left(1-\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)\right)\frac{p^{\nu_{p}(\ell)}-1}{p-1}\right)&\text{if }s=0,\gamma>0\\
\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t-u}{2}\right)+\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t+u}{2}\right)&\text{if }s=e,\gamma=0\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(4.51)

For the
A_{3}
term note that
\chi(x_{1})\not=0
for either
c=1
or
c=p
if and only if
p\nmid d
or
p\nmid n/d
, which is subsequently true if and only if
\nu_{p}(n/d-d)=0
. Using this to evaluate, we get:

R_{p}^{\min}(p^{e},\chi,d,n)=\chi(d)+\chi(n/d)-\underset{s=0}{\delta}

(4.52)

Finally for the
A_{4}
term we get:

R_{p}^{\min}(p,n)=\underset{s=0}{\delta}\left(\frac{p^{\nu_{p}(n)}}{\sigma(p^{\nu_{p}(n)})}-1\right)

(4.53)

4.3.
Factorising over
n

The preceding subsections allow us to produce a formula in the form of (
4.11
),
where each
B_{i}
is evaluated on the arguments
(\frac{N}{d},\chi,k,\frac{n}{d^{2}})
. We now incorporate the outer sum by swapping the sum and product in each term.

For the
B_{1}
term, swapping gives:

\begin{split}C_{1}=&\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid N\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}\begin{cases}p^{e}+p^{e-1}&\text{if }s=e\\
\frac{\phi(\lceil p^{e-2}\rceil)(p-1)}{1+\underset{2\mid e,p>2}{\delta}}(1+\underset{e>1}{\delta}p+\underset{e=2}{\delta}(2s-2))&\text{if }s<e\end{cases}\\
\cdot&\sum_{d\in\mathcal{P}}d\frac{n^{k/2-1}(k-1)\chi_{\cond}(\sqrt{n})}{12}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid\frac{N}{d}\\
p\mid\frac{n}{d^{2}}\end{subarray}}(-1)\end{split}

(4.54)

Then, using multiplicativity of
\mathcal{P}
as defined in (
4.3
), we evaluate the inner sum, giving:

C_{1}=\frac{n^{k/2-1}(k-1)\chi_{\cond}(\sqrt{n})}{12}\prod_{p\mid N}\begin{cases}p^{e}+p^{e-1}&\text{if }s=e\\
\frac{\phi(\lceil p^{e-2}\rceil)(p-1)}{1+\underset{2\mid e,p>2}{\delta}}(1+\underset{e>1}{\delta}p+\underset{e=2}{\delta}(2s-2))&\text{if }s<e\\
\end{cases}

(4.55)

For
B_{3}
swapping the order of summation and substituting
md
for
m
gives:

\begin{split}C_{3}=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\mid n\\
m\leq\sqrt{n}\end{subarray}}^{\prime}m^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{P}(N,n,\chi)\\
d\mid(m,n/m)\end{subarray}}\\
\cdot&\prod_{p\mid\frac{N}{d}}\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{2}^{e}\left(\chi_{p}(m)+\chi_{p}\left(n/m\right)\right)}{8}(1-\underset{\gamma=\frac{e}{2}-1}{\delta}2)&\text{if }p=2,2\mid e,\gamma\geq\frac{e}{2}-1\geq s,2\nmid\frac{n}{d^{2}},e>2\\
\chi_{p}(m)+\chi_{p}\left(n/m\right)-\underset{s=0}{\delta}&\text{if }s=e\text{ or }p\mid\frac{n}{d^{2}}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

(4.56)

Noting that if
p\nmid n
then the product evaluates identically for all
d
, and using multiplicativity of
\mathcal{P}
we get:

C_{3}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\leq\sqrt{n}\end{subarray}}^{\prime}d^{k-1}\prod_{p\mid N}\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{2}^{e}(\chi_{p}(d)+\chi_{p}(n/d))}{8}(1-\underset{\gamma=\frac{e}{2}-1}{\delta}2)&\text{if }p=2,2\mid e,\gamma\geq\frac{e}{2}-1\geq s,2\nmid n,e>2\\
\chi_{p}(d)+\chi_{p}(n/d)&\text{if }s=e\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases}\\

(4.57)

For the
B_{4}
contribution we get:

\begin{split}C_{4}=&\underset{k=2,\chi=\mathbbm{1}}{\delta}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid N\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}\mu(p^{e})\sum_{d\in\mathcal{P}(N,n,\chi)}d\sigma(n/d^{2})\prod_{p\mid\left(\frac{N}{d},\frac{n}{d^{2}}\right)}\left(\frac{p^{\nu_{p}(n/d^{2})}}{\sigma(p^{\nu_{p}(n/d^{2})})}-1\right)\\
=&\underset{k=2,\chi=\mathbbm{1}}{\delta}\mu(N)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\\
p\nmid N\end{subarray}}\sigma(p^{\nu_{p}(n)})\end{split}

(4.58)

For the
B_{2}
term, we follow the same procedure as above. Noting that
\gamma
is unchanged by substituting
n/d^{2}
and
t/d
for
n
and
t
respectively, we get:

R_{p}^{\min}(p^{e},\chi,t,n)=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{d}{p}\right)-1&\text{if }\gamma>s=0\\
\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t-u}{2}\right)+\chi_{p}\left(\frac{t+u}{2}\right)&\text{if }s=e,\gamma=0\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(4.59)

These
C_{i}
terms are now exactly as in Theorem
2.1
.

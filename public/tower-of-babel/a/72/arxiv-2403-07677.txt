Many-sided Poisson-Voronoi cells with only Gabriel neighbors xxx
H.J. Hilhorst

Abstract
Let p n G p_{n}^{G} be the probability for a planar Poisson-Voronoi cell to be n n -sided and have only Gabriel neighbors. Using an exact coordinate transformation followed by scaling arguments and a mean-field type calculation, we obtain the asymptotic expansion of log ⁡ p n G \log p_{n}^{\rm{G}} in the limit of large n n . We determine several statistical properties of a many-sided cell obeying this ‘Gabriel condition.’ In particular, the cell perimeter, when parametrized as a function τ ⁡ ( θ ) \tau(\theta) of the polar angle θ \theta , behaves as a Brownian bridge on the interval 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2 ​ π 0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi . We point out similarities and differences with related problems in random geometry. Keywords: random graphs, Voronoi diagrams, Gabriel neighbors, exact results

Introduction

In the literature many questions have been asked about the statistics of Poisson-Voronoi tessellations. Scientists in a wide diversity of fields have investigated these random structures because of their numerous applications
[
1
]
.
One question that has received strong interest from physicists
[
2
,
3
]
is the following.
What is, in the two-dimensional plane, the probability
p_{n}
that the cell of a randomly chosen seed have exactly
n
sides? The asymptotic large-
n
behavior of
p_{n}

was analyzed numerically and analytically more than forty years ago by Drouffe and Itzykson
[
4
]
. Much later the present author
[
5
,
6
]
returned to this question and obtained the analytic result

p_{n}=2C\,\frac{(8\pi^{2})^{n-1}}{(2n)!}[1+o(1)],\qquad n\to\infty,

(1.1)

where

C=\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}\,(1-q^{-2}+4q^{-4})^{-1}=0.344\,347...
.
A Monte Carlo algorithm based on the derivation of (
1.1
)
was developed
[
7
]
for finding
p_{n}
with high numerical precision for arbitrary
n
.
It was also shown that, in the limit of large
n
,
the perimeter of an
n
-sided cell tends to a circle, and that the constant
C
in (
1.1
) is due, in physical parlance, to the entropy of ‘elastic’ deformations of the circle of wavelengths
2\pi n/q
.

The key initial step in deriving equation (
1.1
)
is to cast the problem in terms of angular variables. This procedure is well adapted, more generally, to studying problems in two-dimensional random geometry that are statistically rotational invariant. The method was successfully used
[
8
]
to study the Crofton cell (that is, the cell containing the origin) of a class of random line tessellations. It was also instrumental
[
9
]

in establishing a link between the answer to Sylvester’s question
[
10
]
and the Random Acceleration Process
[
11
,
12
]
.
It was furthermore extended
[
13
]
to the study of the
n
-sided face between two adjacent three-dimensional Poisson-Voronoi cells and to an analysis

[
14
]
of computer simulations initiated by
Mason
et al.

[
15
]
and by Lazar
et al.

[
16
]
.
Further work along these lines
[
17
,
18
]
has dealt with heuristic approximations
to the exact derivations as well as with extensions of those approximations to higher dimensions, where exact methods are lacking.
It has, moreover, led to a refutation
[
19
]

of Aboav’s linear law
[
20
]
as applied to planar Poisson-Voronoi cells,
and to a heuristic analysis
[
21
]
of analogous laws in higher dimensions.

In this note we ask a new question that is in the same class
as the problems of references
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
13
]
.
In a two-dimensional Poisson-Voronoi tessellation we consider a randomly selected cell (the ‘central cell’) together with its neighbor cells.
Two neighboring cells are called ‘Gabriel neighbors’ of one another if the line segment connecting their seeds intersects the
perimeter segment
that the two cells have in common, as opposed to intersecting the
extension of this perimeter segment
.
If the cells represent countries and the seeds their capitals, then two countries are Gabriel neighbors if the line segment connecting the two capitals does not pass through a third country.
Figure
1
shows a six-sided Poisson-Voronoi cell that has five Gabriel neighbors and one non-Gabriel neighbor.
When a cell has only Gabriel neighbors we will say that it ‘satisfies
the Gabriel condition.’

Our question is: what is the probability, to be called
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
,
that the central cell be
n
-sided
and
satisfy the Gabriel condition?
The main results of this work are the following.
We derive the asymptotic identity

p_{n}^{\rm{G}}=\frac{(16\pi^{2})^{n-1}(n-1)!}{(15\pi n^{3})^{1/2}(3n-3)!}\,[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})],\qquad n\to\infty,

(1.2)

which with the aid of Stirling’s formula may be written alternatively as

\log p_{n}^{\rm{G}}=-2n\log n+n\log(2^{4}3^{-3}\pi^{2}\mbox{e}^{2})+{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}\log(2^{-8}3^{4}5^{-1}\pi^{-5}n)+{\cal O}(n^{-1}).

(1.3)

The ratio
\phi_{n}\equiv p_{n}^{\rm{G}}/p_{n}
is the fraction of
n
-sided Voronoi cells that have only Gabriel neighbors.
From (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) we find

\phi_{n}=\frac{3^{2}n}{2\cdot 5^{1/2}\,C}\,\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\!3n}[1+o(1)],\qquad n\to\infty.

(1.4)

We show that the
n
-sided cell satisfying the Gabriel condition
has a perimeter that for large
n
tends towards a circle of radius
(n/4\pi)^{1/2}+{\cal O}(1)
,
where the
{\cal O}(1)
term is a random contribution, and
that the cell perimeter runs mostly through a narrow annulus of width of
{\cal O}(n^{-1})
.
This means that the Gabriel condition renders the central cell more rigidly circular than it would have been without this condition. Concomitantly, contributions to (
1.2
) from elastic deformations appear only in the
{\cal O}(n^{-1})
terms of the asymptotic expansion.

In section
2
we start from the basic expression for
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
, which is a
2n
-dimensional integral, and recall concisely how to transform it to an integration over only angular variables.
In section
3

we perform a large-

This leads to a mean-field type problem which we identify and solve in section

and which yields (

).
In section

we analyze the probability distribution of the angular variables and the shape of the cell perimeter.
In the limit

In section
6
we present some further comments and conclude.

A mean-field problem

When in expression (

The calculation of

and

\delta\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}a^{+}_{m}-2\pi n\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mbox{i}}\int_{c-\mbox{\scriptsize i}\infty}^{c+\mbox{\scriptsize i}\infty}\!\mbox{d}s\,\mbox{e}^{2\pi sn-s\sum_{m=1}^{n}a^{+}_{m}}

(4.6)

where we take
c>0
.
The scaling factor
n^{-1/2}
in (
4.5
), which at this stage could have been any other power of
n
, will be essential below.
When we substitute (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) in (
), the integrals on the
a_{m}^{\pm}
decouple into
n
identical pairs and we get

p_{n}^{\rm{G}}=\frac{(n-1)!}{2n\pi^{n}}\cdot\frac{n^{5/2}}{n^{3n}}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi\mbox{i}}\int_{c-\mbox{\scriptsize i}\infty}^{c+\mbox{\scriptsize i}\infty}\!\mbox{d}s\,\mbox{e}^{2\pi sn}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\mbox{d}t\,I^{\,n}(t,s)\,\big[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\big]

(4.7)

in which

\displaystyle I(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mbox{d}a^{+}\,a^{+}\,\mbox{e}^{-sa^{+}}\int_{-a^{+}/2}^{a^{+}/2}\!\mbox{d}a^{-}\,\mbox{e}^{\mbox{\scriptsize i}tn^{-1/2}a^{+}a^{-}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mbox{d}a^{+}\,\mbox{e}^{-sa^{+}}\left[(a^{+})^{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{4!\,n}(a^{+})^{6}+{\cal O}(n^{-2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{s^{3}}\left[1-\frac{15t^{2}}{s^{4}n}+{\cal O}(n^{-2})\right].

The positivity of
c
ensures the convergence of the
a^{+}
integral in (
4.8
).
Furthermore the scaling of
t
with
n^{-1/2}
adopted in equation (
4.5
) now leads to a nontrivial
t
dependence
of
I^{\,n}(t,s)
when
n
gets large. Explicitly,

I^{\,n}(t,s)=\frac{2^{n}}{s^{3n}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{15t^{2}}{s^{4}}\right)\big[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\big],

(4.9)

whence

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\mbox{d}t\,I^{\,n}(t,s)=\frac{2^{n}}{s^{3n-2}}\left(\frac{\pi}{15}\right)^{1/2}\left[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\right].

(4.10)

We substitute (
4.10
) in (
4.7
) and perform the integration on
s
by letting its path run counterclockwise around the origin and employing the identity

\frac{1}{2\pi\mbox{i}}\oint\mbox{d}s\frac{\mbox{e}^{-2\pi sn}}{s^{3n-2}}=\frac{(2\pi n)^{3n-3}}{(3n-3)!}\,,\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.

(4.11)

This leads to

p_{n}^{\rm{G}}=\frac{(16\pi^{2})^{n-1}}{(15\pi n^{3})^{1/2}}\,\frac{(n-1)!}{(3n-3)!}\,\left[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\right],

(4.12)

which is result (
1.2
) announced in the introduction.

5
Probability distributions, cell shape, and Langevin equation

In this section we are interested in the probability distributions of individual angles
\beta_{m}
and
\gamma_{m}
.
For an arbitrary function
X(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma})

we let
P_{n}^{\rm{G}}[X]
denote the same integral as
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
but with an insertion
X
in the integrand; hence
P_{n}^{\rm{G}}[1]=p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
.
The average
\langle X\rangle
is given by

\langle X\rangle=\frac{P_{n}^{\rm{G}}[X]}{p_{n}^{\rm{G}}},

(5.1)

which we will use below.

5.1
The probability distribution
w_{2}(\beta_{m},\gamma_{m})

We now ask about the probability distribution
w_{2}(\beta_{m},\gamma_{m})
of the pair
(\beta_{m},\gamma_{m})
in an
n
-sided Poisson-Voronoi cell with only Gabriel neighbors. We have

\displaystyle w_{2}(\beta,\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\delta(\beta_{m}-\beta)\,\delta(\gamma_{m}-\gamma)\big\rangle

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}\big\langle\delta({\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}a^{+}_{m}+a^{-}_{m}-\beta n)\,\delta({\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}a^{+}_{m}-a^{-}_{m}-\gamma n)\big\rangle.

We may implement the angle brackets in the second line of (
5.2
) by directly using expression (
) and going through the same steps as in section
4.2
.
This leads to

w_{2}(\beta,\gamma)=\frac{n^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2\pi}\right)^{\!\!3}(\beta n+\gamma n)\,\exp\left(-\frac{3(\beta n+\gamma n)}{2\pi}\right)\big[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\big],

(5.3)

valid in the limit of large
n
with
\beta n
and
\gamma n
fixed, this latter condition being coherent with the scaling (
3.1
).
Integration of (
5.3
) over
\gamma
yields the probability distribution
w_{1}(\beta_{m})
of the single variable
\beta_{m}
,

w_{1}(\beta)=\frac{3n}{4\pi}\left(1+\frac{3\beta n}{2\pi}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{3\beta n}{2\pi}\right)\big[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\big],

(5.4)

valid in the limit of large
n
with
\beta n
fixed.
By symmetry (
5.4
) is also the distribution of
\gamma_{m}
.
It correctly reproduces
\langle\beta_{m}\rangle=\langle\gamma_{m}\rangle=\pi/n
.

In the limit
n\to\infty
the effect of the two global constraints
on locally defined quantities will vanish. In particular, for
n\to\infty
the pairs
(\beta_{m},\gamma_{m})
with
m=1,2,\ldots,n

become
n
independent two-dimensional random variables.
This structure of the angular statistics is distinct from that found in earlier work
[
6
]
in the absence of the Gabriel condition.
In that case there exist

2n
asymptotically independent angles of two kinds, namely

\xi_{m}\equiv\beta_{m}+\gamma_{m}
and
\eta_{m}\equiv\gamma_{m}+\beta_{m+1}
,
with distributions that also involve linear terms and exponentials, but are different from (
5.3
) and (
5.4
).

5.2
Cell shape

We will show that for large
n
the cell perimeter of the Poisson-Voronoi cell satisfying the Gabriel condition tends towards a circle and quantify the deviations from circularity. We abbreviate

\epsilon_{m}\equiv{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}(\beta_{m}^{2}-\gamma_{m}^{2}),\qquad m=1,2,\ldots,n.

(5.5)

The variance
\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle
under the distribution
w_{2}(\beta,\gamma)

is readily calculated with the result

\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle=\frac{30}{n^{4}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right)^{\!\!4}\big[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})\big].

(5.6)

We will also need the correlation

\langle\epsilon_{\ell}\epsilon_{m}\rangle
where
\ell\neq m
.
Because of the mean-field character of this problem
this correlation is independent of
\ell
and
m
.
To leading order in
n
equation (
3.3
)
implies that
\sum_{m=1}^{n}\epsilon_{m}=0
.
This leads to
\big\langle\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\epsilon_{m}\right)^{2}\big\rangle=0
,
whence

\langle\epsilon_{m}\epsilon_{m+\ell}\rangle=\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle\,\left[\frac{n}{n-1}\delta_{\ell,0}-\frac{1}{n-1}\right],

(5.7)

which we will use below.

The integration over
\bar{R}
mentioned in section (
2.2
)
reads, explicitly,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{d}\bar{R}\,

\bar{R}^{2n-1}\exp\left(-4\pi\bar{R}^{2}[1+n^{-2}U(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma})]\right)
.
For large
n
the integrand gets strongly peaked around

\bar{R}=\bar{R}_{\rm c}
with a standard deviation
\bar{\sigma}
.
Both
\bar{R}_{\rm c}
and
\bar{\sigma}
are to leading order in
n
independent of
(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma})
and are given by

\bar{R}_{\rm c}=(n/4\pi)^{1/2}[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})],\qquad\bar{\sigma}=(16\pi)^{-1/2}[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1})].

(5.8)

They describe averages taken over the
ensemble
of Poisson-Voronoi cells.
The mean radius
\bar{R}
of an individual cell will have a random value

\bar{R}=\bar{R}_{\rm c}+{\cal O}(\bar{\sigma})=\bar{R}_{\rm c}+{\cal O}(1)
.
We now ask the finer question of how close the perimeter of an

individual
cell approaches a circle when
n
gets large.
A good characterization of the degree of approach is provided by the set of
averaged square differences
d_{\ell}^{\,2}
, for
\ell=0,1,\ldots,n
,
between the radii
R_{m}
and
R_{m+\ell}
,

d_{\ell}^{\,2}=\langle(R_{m+\ell}-R_{m})^{2}\rangle.

(5.9)

Because of translational symmetry in the index
m
, the
d_{\ell}^{\,2}

cannot depend on
m
. We use that

R_{m+\ell}-R_{m}=\bar{R}(\tau_{m+\ell}-\tau_{m}).

(5.10)

In (
5.10
) we replace
\bar{R}
by its leading term, which is the constant
(n/4\pi)^{1/2}
. We then square this expression, substitute it in (
5.9
), and use (
3.4
) while employing the notation (
5.5
).
The result is

\displaystyle d_{\ell}^{\,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4\pi}\,\big\langle(\epsilon_{m+1}+\epsilon_{m+2}+\ldots+\epsilon_{m+\ell})^{2}\big\rangle

(5.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4\pi}\,\left[\ell\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle+\ell(\ell-1)\langle\epsilon_{m}\epsilon_{m+\ell}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\ell(n-\ell)}{4\pi}\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle\,[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1}],\qquad\ell=0,1,\ldots,n,

where to arrive at the last line we have used (
5.7
).
The proportionality of the RHS of (
5.11
) to
\ell(n-\ell)
is the well-known parabolic behavior of the mean square displacement of a Brownian bridge on an interval
[0,n]
.
Since
\langle\epsilon_{m}^{2}\rangle={\cal O}(n^{-4})
, equation (
5.11
)
tells us that for a typical value
\ell={\cal O}(n)
we will have that

d_{\ell}={\cal O}(n^{-1})
. This means that the perimeter of a typical
n
-sided Poisson-Voronoi cell satisfying the Gabriel condition
runs through an annulus of radius
(n/4\pi)^{1/2}[1+{\cal O}(n^{-1/2})]

and of width
{\cal O}(n^{-1})
. Without the Gabriel condition
[
6
]
the radius of an
n
-sided cell is the same but the width of the annulus is
{\cal O}(1)
.
Loosely speaking, in the large
n
limit a Poisson-Voronoi cell having only Gabriel neighbors deviates much less from a circle than a cell without this condition. This section has proven and quantified this intuitively appealing result.

5.3
Langevin equation

We define the polar angle
\theta=2\pi m/n
and set

\tau(\theta)=\tau_{m}
. In the limit of large
n
the recursion

\tau_{m}-\tau_{m-1}=\epsilon_{m}

becomes the Langevin equation

\frac{\mbox{d}\tau}{\mbox{d}\theta}=\epsilon(\theta)

(5.12)

in which
\epsilon(\theta)
is zero average Gaussian noise whose covariance should be of the form

\langle\epsilon(\theta)\epsilon(\theta^{\prime})\rangle=n^{-3}D\left[\,2\pi\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\,-\,1\,\right].

(5.13)

One readily finds the value of the constant
D
by equating the
mean square of
\tau_{m^{\prime}}-\tau_{m}
obtained from (
5.9
)-(
5.11
)
to the mean square of
\tau(\phi^{\prime})-\tau(\phi)
obtained from (
5.12
)-(
5.13
) with
\ell=m^{\prime}-m
.
The result is that
D=40\pi^{2}/27
.

The pair of equations (
5.12
)-(
5.13
) is consistent with the scaling
\tau\sim n^{-3/2}
found in equation (
3.5
).
Of course we may scale
n
out of (
5.12
)-(
5.13
) by setting
{\tau}=n^{-3/2}\tilde{\tau}
and
{\epsilon}=n^{-3/2}\tilde{\epsilon}
.

The noise
\epsilon(\theta)
would be white if it were not for the extra term
-1
in the brackets in (
5.13
), which adds a
coloring, even though an admittedly trivial one.
Equation (
5.13
) is simpler than Langevin equations found in related work: The problem dealing with Sylvester’s question
[
10
]
and the problem of the
n
-sided Poisson-Voronoi cell without the Gabriel condition
[
6
]
are both associated with second order Langevin equations, the first one having trivial, and the second one nontrivial colored noise.

6
Conclusion

We have derived an expression, valid in the limit of large
n
,
for the probability
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
that a planar Poisson-Voronoi cell be
n
-sided
while satisfying the additional condition of having only Gabriel neighbors.
We have contrasted
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
with the probability
p_{n}
, calculated in earlier work
[
6
]
, that a cell be
n
-sided but
without
this additional condition.
In both cases the initial expression has the form of a
2n
-dimensional phase space integral, similar to the partition function of
n
interacting two-dimensional particles; and in both cases the key initial step is to transform to an integration over a set of angular variables.
The expressions for
p_{n}
and
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
then differ by the limits of integration,
and from there on the calculation
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}

proceeds in a different way.
Scaling arguments led us to neglect all terms of
{\cal O}(1)
in the asymptotic expansion of
\log p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
. The
2n
angular integrations then decouple into
n
pairs, subject only to two global constraints
represented by the two delta functions in (
2.5
).
One constraint is linear and the other one is nonlinear in the angles.
The corresponding constraints reappear in expression (
),
where it becomes clear that they
can be treated by integral representations with appropriate scalings with
n
.

We have, subsequently, refined our analysis and explicitly calculated the joint probability distribution of a pair of angles
(\beta_{m},\gamma_{m})
, valid in the large
n
limit. Such a pair is a local stochastic variable and in the limit of large
n
the effect of the two constraints on its distribution vanishes.

In the problem without the Gabriel condition
[
6
]

the deviations of the cell perimeter from circularity
contributed a constant
C
to the prefactor of
p_{n}
.
Here, however, there is no such contribution to
p_{n}^{\rm{G}}
: the perimeter of an
n
-sided cell obeying the Gabriel condition is more rigidly circular than the perimeter of the unconstrained cell. This intuitively appealing effect has been quantified in the final section of this work.

We believe that this note constitutes an interesting addition to the general class of random geometry problems outlined in the introduction.

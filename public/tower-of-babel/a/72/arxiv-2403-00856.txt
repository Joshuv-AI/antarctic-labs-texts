Higher Vortexability: Zero Field Realization of Higher Landau Levels
Manato Fujimoto, Daniel E. Parker, Junkai Dong, Eslam Khalaf, Ashvin Vishwanath, Patrick Ledwith

Abstract
The rise of moiré materials has led to experimental realizations of integer and FCIs in small or vanishing magnetic fields. At the same time, a set of minimal conditions sufficient to guarantee an Abelian fractional state in a flat band were identified, namely “ideal" or “vortexable" quantum geometry. Such vortexable bands share essential features with the LLL, while excluding the need for more fine-tuned aspects such as flat Berry curvature. A natural and important generalization is to ask if such conditions can be extended to capture the quantum geometry of higher Landau levels, particularly the first (1LL), where non-Abelian states at ν = 1 / 2 , 2 / 5 \nu=1/2,2/5 are known to be competitive. The possibility of realizing these states at zero magnetic field, and perhaps even more exotic ones, could become a reality if we could identify the essential structure of the 1LL in Chern bands. In this work, we introduce a precise definition of 1LL quantum geometry, along with a figure of merit that measures how closely a given band approaches the 1LL. Periodically strained Bernal graphene is shown to realize such a 1LL structure even in zero magnetic field.

First Landau Levels Are Diverse—
To extend the success of ideal bands to the first LL, one could imagine imposing band geometric criteria to mimic a 1LL features.
For example, the first LL of a
p^{2}/2m
particle has
\tr g=3\Omega

[
22

H_{\rm BG}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&D_{1}^{\dagger}\\
D_{1}&0\end{array}\right),\quad D_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{0}&\gamma_{1}\\
0&D_{0}\end{array}\right),

(2)

(|A,1\rangle,|A,2\rangle,|B,1\rangle,|B,2\rangle)

\gamma_{1}
is the interlayer coupling. There are two bands of zero modes;

is spanned by

\Phi_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{0}\\
0\end{array}\right),\quad\Phi_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v^{-1}\ell_{B}\gamma_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\psi_{0}\\
-\sqrt{2}\psi_{0}\end{array}\right),

(3)

forming a zeroth and first LL respectively, where
\psi_{0}({\bm{r}})=f(z)e^{-\lvert z\rvert^{2}/4\ell_{B}^{2}}
in symmetric gauge with

holomorphic.

Direct computation shows that, as

varies from

of band

varies continuously from

The fact that the first

Definition of First LL Structure—

We now precisely define the structure bands must satisfy to be “first LL analogues".
A Chern band, with projector
P_{1}
, is
first vortexable
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

(
\exists
Partner Band) There exists an orthogonal vortexable band with projector
P_{0}

(i.e.
P_{0}z\Phi_{0}~=~z\Phi_{0}
for all
P_{0}\Phi_{0}=\Phi_{0}
) such that

\displaystyle(P_{1}+P_{0})z\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle=z\Phi_{1}

(4)

for all states
P_{1}\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{1}
.

2.

(Indecomposability) there is no alternative basis of wavefunctions
(\Phi_{0,{\bm{k}}}^{\prime},\Phi_{1,{\bm{k}}}^{\prime})
, where each band is vortexable, that also spans the two band subspace of
P_{0}+P_{1}
.

The additional band, described by the projector
P_{0}
, functions as an effective zeroth LL. Intuitively, acting with
z
either keeps the wavefunction within the ‘first’ band
P_{1}
or lowers it to the ‘zeroth’ band
P_{0}
— but does not generate components in any other bands (see Fig.
1
a). The second condition is in principle necessary to avoid subtle situations where the first condition can nominally be realized, but the system should be thought of as a collection of two separate, individually vortexable, bands instead. Given a single Bloch band
\Phi_{1}
, the partner band is
\Phi_{0}\propto(1-P_{1})z\Phi_{1}
(see Appendix
C
for algorithmic construction), so Eq. (
4
) can always be explicitly checked.

A
C=1
band is first-vortexable if, and only if, its wavefunctions have the form

\Phi_{1}({\bm{r}})=Q_{0}\left[\left(\overline{z}f(z)-2B_{0}^{-1}f^{\prime}(z)\right)\mathcal{N}_{0}({\bm{r}})+f(z)\mathcal{N}_{1}({\bm{r}})\right].

(5)

Here
Q_{0}=1-P_{0}
,
\mathcal{N}_{0,1}
are spinor-valued [
cf
. Eq. (
1
)], and
P_{0}
is the projector onto the “zeroth-LL" wavefunctions
f(z)\mathcal{N}_{0}({\bm{r}})
.
B_{0}=\frac{2\pi}{A_{\mathrm{UC}}}=\frac{A_{\mathrm{BZ}}}{2\pi}
, and
A_{\mathrm{UC}}
and
A_{\mathrm{BZ}}
are the areas of the unit cell and the Brillouin Zone (BZ), respectively.
Appendix
B

5

[

E

\Psi_{\nu=2}
be the many-body state where both bands are full. Now consider the vortex attached state
\Psi_{v}=\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{2s}\Psi_{\nu=p}

We also introduce a refinement of our criterion, which is specifically geared towards realizing states unique to the first LL. While we motivated our definition through the inclusion of Bernal graphene for any value of the interlayer tunneling, as a byproduct we have included bands that for all practical purposes function as LLLs; Consider the decoupled

M({\bm{k}})=\frac{\lvert\lambda_{1}({\bm{k}})-\lambda_{2}({\bm{k}})\rvert}{\lambda_{1}({\bm{k}})+\lambda_{2}({\bm{k}})},

(6)

First vortexable bands with

In this work we have proposed “first vortexability" — a precise definition of when a Chern band has the essential character of the first LL. First vortexable bands are defined by their quantum geometric data, and each requires a partner band analogous to the zeroth LL. Our examples, both analytic and physically reasonable, suggest such bands are experimentally realizable. Future work will investigate the many-body physics of partially-filled first vortexable bands, particularly at half filling where non-Abelian states are likely.

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{ab}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}

Appendix C
Finding the Partner Band and determining first-vortexability

This section explains how to algorithmically check if a band is first-vortexable for a given set of (perhaps numerical) band wavefunctions
\Phi_{1,{\bm{k}}}({\bm{r}})
.
We proceed in three steps: (i) we describe how to find a candidate partner band given a conjectured first-vortexable band; (ii) we show that if the partner band constructed in the previous step is vortexable, then property (P1) is satisfied, i.e. the two-band space is vortexable; (iii) we explain how to find the instance of (
5
) that the band corresponds to, a procedure that will necessarily fail if the two-band vortexable subspace obtained in steps (i),(ii) is decomposable.

Step (i) comes from the observation that, from (P1), the partner band associated with a first vortexable band may be computed through

Appendix D
QGT of “maximal" first vortexability

In this section, we compute the quantum geometric tensor of the combined space of a “maximal" first vortexable band and its zeroth counterpart. By maximal, we specifically mean that there is no zeroth LL portion in the wavefunction other than what is required for the orthogonal basis. For example, in the language of the holomorphic periodic states of the previous section,

\displaystyle v_{kl}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=f_{k}({\bm{r}})\mathcal{N}_{0}({\bm{r}}),

(37)

\displaystyle v_{kl}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=2i\partial_{k}f_{k}({\bm{r}})\mathcal{N}_{0}({\bm{r}})+f_{k}({\bm{r}})\mathcal{N}_{1}({\bm{r}}),

we demand
\mathcal{N}_{1}({\bm{r}})\to 0
. This corresponds to the limit
\gamma_{1}\ell_{B}/v\to\infty
in the Bernal graphene based Hamiltonians in the main text. In an orthonormal basis we then obtain

\displaystyle u_{0,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{0,{\bm{k}}}}f_{k}({\bm{r}})\mathcal{N}_{0}({\bm{r}}),

(38)

\displaystyle u_{1,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{1,{\bm{k}}}}Q_{0}({\bm{k}})\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}

where
Q_{0}({\bm{k}})=1-\ket{u_{0,k}}\bra{u_{0,k}}
orthogonalizes
u_{1,k}
relative to
u_{0,k}
and the factors

are such that the wavefunctions are normalized.

We recall property (V4), which states that vortexability of the bands is equivalent to
\hat{\eta}^{\mu\nu}_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Omega}_{ab}(\delta_{\mu\nu}-i\varepsilon_{\mu\nu})
, where the Berry curvature is

\hat{\Omega}_{ab}({\bm{k}})=\bra{\partial_{k}u_{{\bm{k}}b}}Q({\bm{k}})\ket{\partial_{k}u_{{\bm{k}}a}}

(39)

We now show that

\mathcal{N}^{(1)}\to 0

\hat{\Omega}_{11}
, as claimed in the main text.

From (
39

. This in turn follows from writing

\displaystyle\ket{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}}

\displaystyle=P_{0}\ket{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}}+Q_{0}\ket{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}},

(40)

\displaystyle=P_{0}\ket{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}}+N_{1,{\bm{k}}}\ket{u_{1,{\bm{k}}}},

t(\bm{l})=e^{i\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\nabla}}
is the ordinary,
B=0
translation operator. The phase satisfies
\bm{A}(\bm{r}+\bm{l})=\bm{A}(\bm{r})+\nabla\xi_{\bm{l}}(\bm{r})
, where
\bm{A}
is the vector potential. The magnetic translation operators satisfy

T(\bm{l}_{1})T(\bm{l}_{2})=T(\bm{l}_{2})T(\bm{l}_{1})e^{iB\hat{z}\cdot\bm{l}_{1}\times\bm{l}_{2}}

(58)

A commuting subset, generated by
T_{{\bm{R}}_{1}}
,
T_{{\bm{R}}_{2}}
with
B\hat{z}\cdot{\bm{R}}_{1}\times{\bm{R}}_{2}=2\pi
, can then be used to define Bloch states
\Phi_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{r}})
. The algebra (
58
) implies that magnetic translations at other
\ell\neq{\bm{R}}
act as “ladder operators" on the Bloch states,
T_{\bm{l}}\Phi_{{\bm{k}}}\propto\Phi_{{\bm{k}}-B\hat{z}\times\bm{l}}
(see e.g. Appendix B of Ref.
[
28
]
for a pedagogical discussion). Thus, all Bloch momenta are symmetry related. We will shortly use this fact to specialize to calculating the Berry curvature at
{\bm{k}}=0
.

The periodic states
\mathcal{U}_{m,{\bm{k}}}=e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{r}}}\Phi_{m,{\bm{k}}}({\bm{r}})
of Bernal graphene have the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}\\
0\end{array}\right),

(59)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1,{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}^{2}+2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma_{1}u_{1,{\bm{k}}}\\
\sqrt{2}v\ell_{B}^{-1}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}\end{array}\right),

where
u_{0,{\bm{k}}}
is the periodic state of the usual LLL and
u_{1,{\bm{k}}}
is that of the usual first LL, obtained from the lowest through the raising operator. Both states are normalized.
We will shortly use

u_{1,{\bm{k}}}=\sqrt{B}Q_{0}({\bm{k}})\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}

(60)

which can be computed directly or indirectly. The indirect route begins by writing
u_{1,{\bm{k}}}=\lambda Q_{0}({\bm{k}})\partial_{k}u_{0k}
. One then solves for
\lambda=\sqrt{B}
through
1=\braket{u_{1,{\bm{k}}}|u_{1,{\bm{k}}}}=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}\bra{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}}Q({\bm{k}})\ket{\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}}=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{2}\Omega_{\text{LLL}}
, where we have identified
\Omega_{\text{LLL}}=1/B
as the Berry curvature of the LLL.
We will also use

\displaystyle\langle u_{1,{\bm{k}}}|\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{B}\langle\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}|Q_{0}({\bm{k}})|\partial_{k}u_{0,{\bm{k}}}\rangle,

(61)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{B}\Omega_{\text{LLL}}=1/\sqrt{B}.

We are now ready to compute the non-Abelian Berry curvature

\hat{\Omega}_{mn}({\bm{k}})=\left\langle\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{n,{\bm{k}}}|Q({\bm{k}})|\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{m,{\bm{k}}}\right\rangle.

(62)

Let us fix
{\bm{k}}=0
without loss of generality, so that we can more easily apply rotation symmetry (recall that all momenta are related by magnetic translation symmetry, such that
\hat{\Omega}
is
{\bm{k}}
-independent). We note that
\mathcal{U}_{1,{\bm{k}}=0}
has a different angular momentum than
\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}=0}
. This implies that the off-diagonal elements of
\hat{\Omega}
must vanish.

The diagonal terms follow from

\displaystyle\hat{\Omega}_{00}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}|(1-P_{0}-P_{1})|\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}\rangle

(63)

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}|Q_{0}|\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}|P_{1}|\partial_{k}\mathcal{U}_{0,{\bm{k}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\text{LLL}}-\frac{\gamma_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{1}^{2}+2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}}\lvert\langle u_{1,k}|\partial_{k}u_{0,k}\rangle\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=B^{-1}\frac{2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}}{\gamma_{1}^{2}+2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}}.

Because the trace of the non-Abelian Berry curvature must be
2/B
, to be consistent with
C=2
for the two-band system, we must have

\hat{\Omega}_{11}=B^{-1}\frac{2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}+2\gamma_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{1}^{2}+2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}}.

(64)

We therefore obtain

M=\frac{\gamma_{1}^{2}}{\gamma_{1}^{2}+2(v\ell_{B}^{-1})^{2}},

(65)

as claimed in the main text.
At
\gamma_{1}\rightarrow 0
which corresponds to the decoupling of Bernal graphene into two identical monolayer graphene, the ingredients of non-Abelian Berry curvature are
\hat{\Omega}_{01}=\hat{\Omega}_{10}=0
and
\hat{\Omega}_{00}=\hat{\Omega}_{11}=1/B
so the maximal index is
M=0
.
On the other hand, at
\gamma_{1}\rightarrow\infty
,
\hat{\Omega}_{00}=\hat{\Omega}_{01}=\hat{\Omega}_{10}=0
and
\hat{\Omega}_{11}=2/B
, leading to
M=1
.

We numerically calculate the
\langle M(\bm{k})\rangle_{\rm BZ}
for the analytical example [Eq.(
41
)] at magic angle
\alpha=0.586
in Fig.
5
.
The index depends on
\gamma_{1}
almost as same as that of Bernal graphene
B>0
.

Self-consistent Hatree-Fock calculation

We consider realistic gate-screened Coulomb interactions:

\hat{H}_{\rm tot}=\hat{H}_{SBG}+\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\bm{q}}V_{\bm{q}}:\hat{\rho}_{\bm{q}}\hat{\rho}_{-\bm{q}}:,\quad V_{q}=\frac{2\pi\tanh(qd)}{\epsilon_{r}\epsilon_{0}q}

(66)

with density operator
\rho_{\bm{q}}
at wavevector
\bm{q}
, normal ordering relative to filling
\nu=0
, sample area
A
, gate distance
d=$250\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
, and relative permittivity
\epsilon_{r}=15
. To obtain the two isolated Chern bands, we employ self-consistent Hartree-Fock (SCHF) calculations on
24\times 24
unit cells. We assume spin and valley polarization
[
87
]
, and project to well-isolated subspace of the three highest valence bands.

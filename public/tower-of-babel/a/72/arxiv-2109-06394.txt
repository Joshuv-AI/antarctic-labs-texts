Dynamical Systems of Correspondences on the Projective Line I: Moduli Spaces and Multiplier Maps
Rin Gotou

Abstract
We consider moduli spaces of dynamical systems of correspondences over the projective line as a generalization of moduli spaces of dynamical systems of endomorphisms on the projective line. We obtain the rationality of the moduli spaces. The rationality of the moduli space of degree ( d , e ) (d,e) correspondences is obtained by constructing a projection to the one for the usual dynamical systems of degree d + e − 1 d+e-1 representation-theoretically. We also show that the multiplier maps for the fixed points and the multiplier index theorem (Woods Hole formula) are also reduced through the projection and obtain the reduced form explicitly.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we set up notation and terminology. In Section 3, we review representation theory of linear groups including Clebsch-Gordan decomposition that we use later. In Section 4, we construct moduli spaces of correspondences and rewrite the composition and conjugation of correspondences as maps and actions on the moduli spaces. In Section 5, we construct the moduli spaces of dynamical systems of correspondences, characterize the stable and semistable loci of the conjugation action and show the rationality of the moduli spaces. In Section 6, we construct multiplier maps and reformulate the multiplier index theorem representation-theoretically.

Proposition 3.1

Proof.

We first construct a map

. For a global section

\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{I}

We set

is surjective.

For

To show the naturality, we have to show

\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-actions. By Remark

-linearization is given by the natural representation over

The reductive group

Theorem 5.10

A divisorial correspondence
C

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{e}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
is
not
a stable point (resp.

\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}

\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-conjugate of the coefficient matrix

5.2
Rationality

Let
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
be a representation of a reductive group
G
. Then we have the inclusion morphism
k[V^{\prime}]\to k[V]
. This morphism is
G
-equivariant by definition, therefore leads to the morphism
k[V^{\prime}]^{G}\to k[V]^{G}
and the rational map
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})^{ss}\sslash G\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}((V^{\prime})^{*})^{ss}\sslash G
. If the action of the group
G
on
(V^{\prime})^{*}
is free for general point, then the fiber of general point of
\mathbb{P}((V^{\prime})^{*})^{ss}\sslash G
via
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})^{ss}\sslash G\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}((V^{\prime})^{*})\sslash G
is naturally isomorphic to
(V/V^{\prime})^{*}
. Therefore we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.11
.

For a representation of a reductive group
G
and a representation
V
,
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})\sslash G
is rational if there exists a subrepresentation
V^{\prime}\subset V
such that the action of
G
on
V^{\prime}
is generically free and
\mathbb{P}((V^{\prime})^{*})\sslash G
is rational.

We fix a infinity field
k
. Let
1\leq d\leq e
be positive integers. Then the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition (Proposition
3.7
) gives

\dim_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Rep}_{k}(\operatorname{SL}_{2})}(V_{d+e-1}\otimes V_{1},V_{d}\otimes V_{e})=2.

We fix an injective morphism

\rho_{1}:V_{d+e-1}\otimes V_{1}\to V_{d}\otimes V_{e}.

The morphism
\rho_{1}
gives rational maps

\rho_{1}:\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}\dashrightarrow\operatorname{Corr}_{1,d+e-1}

and

\bar{\rho_{1}}:\operatorname{Dyn}_{d,e}\dashrightarrow\operatorname{Dyn}_{1,d+e-1}.

Proposition 5.12
.

\operatorname{Dyn}_{d,e}
is rational for
d,e\geq 1
and
(d,e)\neq(1,1)
.

Proof.

For the case
d=1
, this is shown in
[
17
]
. In the same paper, it is also shown
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(k)
acts generically free on the representation
V_{D}\otimes V_{1}
for
D\geq 3
. Therefore the general case follows from Proposition
5.11
.
∎

6
Multiplier Map

6.1
Construction

Let
f
be a divisorial quasi-correspondence

f(x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1})=\sum_{0\leq i\leq d,\ 0\leq j\leq e}a_{ij}x_{0}^{d-i}x_{1}^{i}y_{0}^{e-j}y_{1}^{j}.

We fix affine coordinates
x=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}}
and
y=\frac{y_{1}}{y_{0}}
. Then the restriction of the divisorial quasi-correspondence given by
f
on the affine chart
\mathbb{A}_{x}\times\mathbb{A}_{y}=U_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{x}}^{+}(x_{0})\times U_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{y}}^{+}(y_{0})
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{x}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{y}
is given by
V(\bar{f}(x,y))
, where

\bar{f}(x,y):=\sum_{0\leq i\leq d,\ 0\leq j\leq e}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}.

Intuitively, from implicit function theorem, the derivative (we denote by)
\frac{dy}{dx}
on
V(\bar{f}(x,y))
around a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in V(\bar{f}(x,y))
is given by

\frac{dy}{dx}=-\left.\frac{\partial_{x}\bar{f}(x,y)}{\partial_{y}\bar{f}(x,y)}\right|_{(x,y)=(\bar{x},\bar{y})}.

(10)

if
\left.\partial_{y}\bar{f}(x,y)\right|_{(x,y)=(\bar{x},\bar{y})}\neq 0
. In fact, from the universality of differential sheaf, if we change affine coordinates of each components equivariantly, the value
\frac{dy}{dx}
does not changes.
We use (
10
) as the definition of the derivative
\frac{dy}{dx}
of
f
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in V(f)\setminus V(\partial_{y}f)
.
We note that here

\displaystyle x_{0}^{d}y_{0}^{e}\partial_{x}\bar{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}f(x,y)=d\cdot f(x,y)-\partial_{x_{0}}f(x,y)\ \text{and}

\displaystyle x_{0}^{d}y_{0}^{e}\partial_{y}\bar{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{y_{1}}f(x,y)=d\cdot f(x,y)-\partial_{y_{0}}f(x,y)

as rational functions of
x_{0},x_{1},y_{0}
and
y_{1}
. Therefore, at a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in V(f)\setminus V(\partial_{y}f)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})}{\partial_{y_{1}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})}=-\frac{-\partial_{x_{0}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})}{-\partial_{y_{0}}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})}.

For convenience, we define rational functions
\partial_{x}f(x,y)
and
\partial_{y}f(x,y)
as

\displaystyle x\partial_{x}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}f(x,y)-x_{0}\partial_{x_{0}}f(x,y)\text{ and }

\displaystyle y\partial_{y}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}f(x,y)-y_{0}\partial_{y_{0}}f(x,y).

(11)

We construct the symmetric form of the fixed point multipliers
\sigma_{i}(f)
by

1+\sum_{i=1}^{d+e}(-1)^{i}\sigma_{i}(f)t^{i}=\prod_{z:f(z,z)=0}\left(1+\frac{\partial_{x}f(z,z)}{\partial_{y}f(z,z)}t\right).

(12)

The map
\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{d+e}
given by
f\mapsto(\sigma_{i}(f))_{i=1,\ldots d+e}
is a morphism if
d=1
and
f
is a morphism, but is just a rational map for
d\geq 2
because there are points such that
\partial_{y}f(z)=0
. To incorporate
y
-critical points, then we prefer to consider the following homogenized form of the multiplier map:

Definition 6.1
.

The fixed point multiplier map
is the rational map

\lambda_{1,(d,e)}:\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}\ni f\mapsto[\operatorname{res}_{z}(f(z,z),\partial_{x}f(z,z)dx+\partial_{y}f(z,z)dy)]\in\mathbb{P}(D_{d+e}),

where we regard
dx
and
dy
as just a variables and
D_{n}
be the space of
n
-ary forms in them.

The
n
-th multiplier map
is the rational map
\lambda_{n,(d,e)}:=\lambda_{1,(d^{n},e^{n})}\circ\Psi_{n}
.

Remark 6.2
.

By some elementary transformation of Sylvester matrix, we have a fundamental property of resultant

\operatorname{res}_{x}(f(x),g(x)+af(x))=\operatorname{res}_{x}(f(x),g(x))

for any
f,g\in k[x]
and
a\in k
.
For any divisorial quasi-correspondence
f(x,y)
, this property of resultant and
x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}f(x,y)=df(x,y)-x_{0}\partial_{x_{0}}f(x,y)
shows the equality

[\operatorname{res}_{z}(f(z,z),\partial_{x}f(z,z)dx+\partial_{y}f(z,z)dy)]=[\operatorname{res}_{z}(f(z,z),z_{0}\partial_{x_{0}}f(z,z)dx+z_{0}\partial_{y_{0}}f(z,z)dy)]

of points of
\mathbb{P}(D_{d+e})
.

6.2
Resultant Form of Woods Hole Formula

Let
f
be a divisorial quasi-correspondence of degree
(d,e)
. Then we have

\displaystyle z(\partial_{x}f)(z,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(d-2i)a_{ij}z^{i+j}\text{ and }

\displaystyle z(\partial_{y}f)(z,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(e-2j)a_{ij}z^{i+j}

from (
11
) and

\displaystyle z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(d+e-2i-2j)a_{ij}z^{i+j}\text{ and }

\displaystyle z_{0}z_{1}(\Omega^{1}f)(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(-ei+dj)a_{ij}z^{i+j}

from the definition of Cayley operator (
4
).
This leads to

\left(\begin{matrix}z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)\\
z_{0}z_{1}(\Omega^{1}f)(z)\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
\frac{e}{2}&\frac{-d}{2}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}z(\partial_{x}f)(z,z)\\
z(\partial_{y}f)(z,z)\end{matrix}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}z(\partial_{x}f)(z,z)\\
z(\partial_{y}f)(z,z)\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{d+e}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{-d}{2}&-1\\
\frac{-e}{2}&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)\\
z_{0}z_{1}(\Omega^{1}f)(z)\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{d}{d+e}&\frac{2}{d+e}\\
\frac{e}{d+e}&-\frac{2}{d+e}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)\\
z_{0}z_{1}(\Omega^{1}f)(z)\end{matrix}\right).

This coordinate change gives the following proposition.

Proposition 6.3
.

There exists a projective linear morphism
A\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{d+e})=\operatorname{PGL}_{d+e}(k)
which makes the following diagram commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Dyn}_{d,e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}(D_{d+e})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Dyn}_{1,d+e-1}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{1,(d,e)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\rho}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{1,(1,d+e-1)}}

Proof.

By the definition of
\rho_{1}
, we have
\Omega^{i}\rho_{1}(f)=c_{i}\cdot\Omega^{i}f
for any
f\in\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}
and each

, where
c_{i}\in k^{\times}
are given. Therefore for a correspondence
f\in\operatorname{Corr}_{d,e}
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,(1,d+e-1)}\circ\rho_{1}(f)=\operatorname{res}_{z}(\Omega^{0}f(z),

\displaystyle c_{0}z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)\left(\frac{1}{d+e}dx+\frac{d+e-1}{d+e}dy\right)

\displaystyle+c_{1}z_{0}z_{1}\Omega^{1}f(z)\left(\frac{2}{d+e}dx-\frac{2}{d+e}dy\right))

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,(d,e)}(f)=\operatorname{res}_{z}(\Omega^{0}f(z),

\displaystyle z\partial_{z}(\Omega^{0}f)(z)\left(\frac{d}{d+e}dx+\frac{e}{d+e}dy\right)

\displaystyle+z_{0}z_{1}\Omega^{1}f(z)\left(\frac{2}{d+e}dx-\frac{2}{d+e}dy\right))

for any correspondence
f
. We take the basis
(dz^{0}_{(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})},dz_{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}^{1})
of
D_{1}=k\cdot dx\oplus k\cdot dy
for each pair of positive integers
d^{\prime},e^{\prime}
by

dz^{0}_{(d^{\prime},e^{\prime})}:=\frac{d^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}dx+\frac{e^{\prime}}{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}dy\text{ and }dz_{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}^{1}:=\frac{2}{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}dx-\frac{2}{d^{\prime}+e^{\prime}}dy.

(13)

The automorphism
A
on
\mathbb{P}(D_{d+e})
is induced by the linear map

\left(\begin{matrix}dz^{0}_{(d,e)}\\
dz_{d+e}^{1}\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}c_{0}dz^{0}_{(1,d+e-1)}\\
c_{1}dz_{d+e}^{1}\end{matrix}\right).

∎

Theorem 6.5
.

The image of
\lambda_{1,(d,e)}:\operatorname{Dyn}_{d,e}\to\mathbb{P}(D_{d+e})

[(dz^{0})^{d+e-1}(dz^{1})^{1}]=0,

[(dz^{0})^{d+e-1}dz^{1}]
is the coordinate function of
(dz_{(d,e)}^{0})^{d+e-1}dz_{d+e}^{1}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
, it is enough to show the assertion for
e=1
, the case of the moduli of maps. We show the index theorem (
3
) is equivalent to the hyperplane
[(dz^{0})^{d}(dz^{1})^{1}]=0

\left(\begin{matrix}dz^{0}\\
dz^{1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{d}{d+1}&\frac{2}{d+1}\\

on

\left(\begin{matrix}[dz^{0}]\\
[dz^{1}]\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{d}{d+1}&\frac{2}{d+1}\\

[dy]\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\

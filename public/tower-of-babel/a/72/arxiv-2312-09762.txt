Discontinuous Galerkin methods for the Vlasov-Stokes’ system
Harsha Hutridurga, Krishan Kumar, Amiya K. Pani

Abstract
. This paper develops and analyses semi-discrete numerical method for two dimensional Vlasov-Stokes’ system with periodic boundary condition. The method is based on coupling of semi-discrete discontinuous Galerkin method for the Vlasov equation with discontinuous Galerkin scheme for the stationary incompressible Stokes’ equation. The proposed method is both mass and momentum conservative. Since it is difficult to establish non-negativity of the discrete local density, the generalized discrete Stokes’ operator become non-coercive and indefinite and under smallness condition on the discretization parameter, optimal error estimates are established with help of a modified the Stokes’ projection to deal with Stokes’ part and with the help of a special projection to tackle the Vlasov part. Finally, numerical experiments based on the dG method combined with a splitting algorithm are performed.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study a mathematical model describing sprays and aerosols, bubbly flows or suspension and sedimentation phenomena in two dimensions. It is a coupled system of
2
D incompressible steady generalized Stokes’ equation and a Vlasov type equation:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)f\Big)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
f(0,x,v)&=f_{0}(x,v)\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{x}\bm{u}+\rho\bm{u}+\nabla_{x}p&=\rho V\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x},\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{u}&=0\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x},\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
\Omega_{x}
denotes the two dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The fluid velocity of the background medium is denoted by
\bm{u}(t,x)
, and the pressure is denoted by
p(t,x)
which are functions of space and time variables. The distribution function
f(t,x,v)
describing the particle distribution depends on the time variable
t>0
, the spatial variable
x\in\Omega_{x}
and the velocity variable
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. The local density of the particles is given by
\rho:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\;f\,{\rm d}v
. The coupling between the two systems is due to a drag force which is proportional to the relative velocity
(\bm{u}-v)
. This system is also important in biological applications, such as transport in the respiratory tract
[
GCC02
,
BBJM05
]
.

The Vlasov-Stokes’ system has been rigorously derived from the dynamics of a system of particles in a fluid by Jabin and Perthame
[
JP00
]
. The existence of solution with its large time behaviour of a similar system has been studied by Hamdache
[
Ham98
]
. In this work, authors take unsteady incompressible Stokes’ equation as the fluid equation for the background medium. The main idea of the existence proof in
[
Ham98
]
is to first regularize the main problem followed by an application of the Schauder fixed point theorem to arrive at a solution of the regularized problem. Finally, the regularized solution in the limiting case yields the existence of a weak solution to the original problem. One of the crucial steps in the proof is the use of non-negativity of
\rho
, which makes the generalized Stokes’ system into a damped Stokes’ problem leading to an application of the inf-sup condition. Based on the Banach fixed point argument and using
\rho\geq 0
in a crucial way, Höfer
[
Höf18
]
has proved the existence of a unique weak solution to the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). In
[
HKP
]
, authors proved the existence of a unique strong solution in
3
D using the Banach fixed point argument. In
[
HKP
]
, the authors consider the unsteady Stokes’ equation for the background medium. For some regularity result, see
[
GJP00
]
.

In this paper, we develop discontinuous Galerkin methods for both the equations (dG-dG). Our work is inspired by the works
[
DDCS09
]
and
[
DDCS12
]
which dealt with the Vlasov-Poisson system. Recently, in
[
HKP23
]
, a discontinuous Galerkin method for the Vlasov-viscous Burgers’ system is developed. In those three papers, the authors have analysed conservation and stability properties of the discrete system and have also derived optimal error estimates with the help of a special projection operator. The Vlasov equation is a form of transport problem which conserves total mass. Out of the several numerical methods, discontinuous Galerkin method has the property to preserve the total mass of the discrete system and hence, we propose dG for the Vlasov equation. One can also choose local discontinuous Galerkin or any conforming numerical method for the Stokes’ system.
One of the main difficulty in proving results like existence and uniqueness as well as optimal error estimates for the semi-discrete system
is the currently unavailable results demonstrating the non-negativity of the discrete local density
\rho_{h}
and hence, the corresponding generalized discrete Stokes’ problem becomes non-coercive and indefinite. This creates serious difficulties in the analysis and therefore, a more refined analysis is needed to derive results under smallness condition on the discretizing parameter
h
. In the literature, there have been some numerical works for similar type of models in
[
GJLY13
,
GJLY14
]
.

Our major contributions in this paper are as follows:

•

Conservation properties and stability of the discrete system are analysed.

•

Since the generalized discrete Stokes’ operator fails to satisfy the inf-sup condition, a more in depth analysis helps to achieve the result under a smallness assumption on the discretizing parameter
h
.

•

Based on a modified generalized Stokes’ projection and a special projection, optimal error estimates are derived for the semi-discrete system.

•

Existence of a unique pair of solutions for the discrete system is established.

•

Finally, using a splitting algorithm, we perform some numerical experiments.

The paper is structured as follows. In section
2
, we describe some properties of the continuum model. In section
3
, we introduce a semi-discrete numerical method followed by proving certain properties of the discrete solution. The error analysis for the semi-discrete method is detailed in section
4
. Finally, in section
5
, some computational results are given.

2.
Qualitative and Quantitative aspects of the model problem

Through out this paper, we use standard notation for Sobolev spaces. Denote by
W^{m,p}
the
L^{p}
-Sobolev space of order
m\geq 0
.
Set
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{x})=\left\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega_{x}):\int_{\Omega_{x}}g\,{\rm d}x=0\right\},

\bm{W^{m,p}}=\left(W^{m,p}(\Omega_{x})\right)^{2},\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L^{\infty}}}=\|u\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x})]^{2}},\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{W^{m,p}}}=\|u\|_{[W^{m,p}(\Omega_{x})]^{2}}\,\,\forall\,\,m\geq 0,\,\,1\leq p\leq\infty
. We, further, denote a special class of divergence-free (in the sense of distribution) vector fields by

\bm{J_{1}}=\left\{\bm{z\in W^{1,2}}:\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{z}=0,\bm{z}\,\,\mbox{is periodic}\right\}.

Set the local macroscopic velocity
V
as

V(t,x)=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(t,x,v)v\,{\rm d}v.

We define some physical quantities: mass and momentum, respectively as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}vf(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v.

The energy and the velocity moments
m_{k}f
, for
k\geq 0
, respectively, as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}\;f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{k}f(t,x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|v|^{k}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v.

Throughout this manuscript, any function defined on
\Omega_{x}
is assumed to be periodic in the
x
-variable.

The following properties hold for the solution
(f,\bm{u},p)
of the Vlasov-Stokes’ equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), whose proof can be found in
[
Ham98
]
.

•

(Positivity preserving)
For any given non-negative initial datum
f_{0}
, the solution
f
is also non-negative, i.e., if
f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
then
f(t,x,v)\geq 0
. Further, the local density
\rho(t,x)\geq 0
.

•

(Mass conservation)
The distribution function
f
conserves mass in the sense that

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}f_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\,\,t\in[0,T].

•

(Total momentum conservation)
The distribution function
f
conserves momentum in the sense that

(2.2)

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}v\,f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}v\,f_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\quad\forall\,\,t\in[0,T].

•

(Energy dissipation)
The energy of the system dissipates, i.e.,

(2.3)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\leq 0,\quad\mbox{for any }\quad f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0.

Multiplying equation (
1.1
) by
|v|^{2}
, and equation (
1.2
) by
2\bm{u}
followed by an integration by parts shows

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bm{u}-v|^{2}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}t=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}\,f_{0}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v.

This is referred to as the energy identity.
As
f\geq 0
, the third term on the left hand side becomes non-negative and hence

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}\,f_{0}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v.

Moreover, if
\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\,|v|^{2}f_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x<\infty
, then the above inequality yields

(2.4)

\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{J_{1}}).

A use of the Sobolev inequality yields

(2.5)

\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L^{p}}),\quad\forall\,\,2\leq p<\infty.

The following result shows integrability estimates on the local density and on the momentum. As these appear as source terms in the Stokes’ equation, these estimates are crucial in deducing the regularity of solution to the Stokes’ problem. The proof of the following result can be found in
[
Ham98
, Lemma 2.2, p.56]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Let
\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L^{p+2}}),f_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap L^{1}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and let

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\,|v|^{p}f_{0}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}v<\infty.

Then, the local density
\rho
and the momentum
\rho V
satisfy the following:

(2.6)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{p+2}{2}}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{p+2}{3}}(\Omega_{x})\right).

Remark 2.2
.

Taking
p=4
in Lemma
2.1
yields

(2.7)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{3}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{x})\right).

These integrability properties of
\rho
and
\rho V
in the Stokes’ equation (
1.2
) guarantee that
\bm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T;\bm{L^{\infty}})
which plays a crucial role in arriving at an
L^{\infty}
estimate on
\rho
.

The following Lemma gives the
L^{\infty}
estimate in time and space variable for the local density. The proof of this can be found in
[
HKMMM20
, Proposition 4.6, p.44]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\bm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T;\bm{L^{\infty}})
and let
\sup_{C^{r}_{t,v}}f_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}\left({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\right)
, where
C^{r}_{t,v}:=\Omega_{x}\times B(e^{t}v,r),\,\forall\,r>0
.
B(e^{t}v,r)
denote a ball of radius
r
with center at
e^{t}v
. Then, the following estimate holds:

\|\rho(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega_{x})}\leq e^{2T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sup_{C^{r}_{t,v}}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.

3.
Discontinuous Galerkin approximations

In this section, we design and analyze a discontinuous Galerkin scheme to approximate solutions to the continuum model (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Note that a compactly supported (in the
v
variable) initial datum results in a compactly supported (in the
v
variable) solution

to the Vlasov equation (
1.1
) provided
t\in[0,T]
where
T
depends on the size of
f_{0}
and the magnitude of the background fluid velocity
\bm{u}
. So, if we restrict ourselves to compactly supported (in the
v

L>0
and
T>0
such that
\mbox{supp}f(t,x,\cdot)\subset\Omega_{v}=:[-L,L]^{2}
for all
t\in[0,T]
and

Let
\mathcal{T}^{x}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}

\Omega_{x}
and
\Omega_{v}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h}=\left\{R=T^{x}\times T^{v}:T^{x}\in\mathcal{T}^{x}_{h},\,T^{v}\in\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}\right\}.

The mesh sizes

and

relative to these partitions are defined as follows:

h_{x}:=\max_{T^{x}\in\mathcal{T}^{x}_{h}}{\rm diam}(T^{x});\quad h_{v}:=\max_{T^{v}\in\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}}{\rm diam}(T^{v});\quad h:=\max\left(h_{x},h_{v}\right).

We denote by
\Gamma_{x}
and
\Gamma_{v}
the set of all edges of the partitions
\mathcal{T}^{x}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}
, respectively and we set

(respectively,
\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}

\Gamma^{0}_{x}
(respectively,
\Gamma^{0}_{v}
) and

(respectively,
\Gamma^{\partial}_{v}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega_{x}):\psi|_{T^{x}}\in\mathbb{P}^{k}(T^{x}),\,\,\forall\,T^{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}\right\},

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

\|g-g_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x})}\lesssim h_{x}\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{x})}+h^{k}_{x}\|g\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{x})}+h_{x}^{-1}\|g-g_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\quad\forall\quad g_{h}\in X_{h}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|g-g_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x})}

\displaystyle\leq\|g-\mathcal{P}_{x}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x})}+\|\mathcal{P}_{x}g-g_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{x}\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{x})}+h^{-1}_{x}\|\mathcal{P}_{x}g-g_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{x}\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{x})}+h^{-1}_{x}\|g-\mathcal{P}_{x}g\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}+h^{-1}_{x}\|g-g_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{x}\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{x})}+h^{k}_{x}\|g\|_{H^{k+1}(\Omega_{x})}+h^{-1}_{x}\|g-g_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}.

Here, in the second step, we have employed the projection estimate
[

]
for the first term and the norm comparison estimate (

Error estimates for Stokes’ system

The error equation for the Stokes’ problem is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}\left((\bm{u-u_{h}},p-p_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\right)+\left(\rho\bm{u}-\rho_{h}\bm{u_{h}},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)=\left(\rho V-\rho_{h}V_{h},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right).

To estimate
\bm{u-u_{h}}
, we rewrite it as

\displaystyle\bm{u}-\bm{u_{h}}

\displaystyle=\bm{u}-\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}+\bm{\tilde{u}_{h}}-\bm{u_{h}}=\bm{u}-\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u}+\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u}-\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}+\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}-\bm{u_{h}}.

Here,
\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}
is the solution of an auxiliary problem (
4.1
) given below. Projection estimate gives the estimate of
\|\bm{u}-\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}
. So, to estimate
\|\bm{u}-\bm{u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}
, it is sufficient to estimate
\|\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u}-\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}
and
\|\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}-\bm{u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}
. We will obtain these estimates in Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, respectively.

We now consider the following auxiliary discrete Stokes’ problem for the unknown
(\bm{\tilde{u}_{h}},p_{h})\in U_{h}\times P_{h}
:

(4.1)

\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}))+\left(\rho\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)=\left(\rho V,\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\hskip 2.84526pt(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\in U_{h}\times P_{h},

where,
\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}))
is defined as in (
3.14
). Note that the above auxiliary problem differs from the original discrete generalized Stokes’system (
3.14
) in that the discrete local density
\rho_{h}
and discrete local momentum
\rho_{h}V_{h}
are replaced by the local density
\rho
and the local momentum
\rho V
associated with the continuum solution
f
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The auxiliary discrete Stokes’ problem (
4.1
) satisfies the discrete inf-sup condition, i.e. there exists a constant
\alpha>0
, independent on
h
, such that

(4.2)

\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\sup_{(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\in U_{h}\times P_{h}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}))+\left(\rho\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}.

Furthermore, we have

(4.3)

|\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}))+\left(\rho\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h},\phi_{h}}\right)|\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Proof.

Let right hand side of (
4.2
) be denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})
. Note that

(4.4)

C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}\leq\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{h},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Note that
b_{h}(\bm{\phi_{h}},p_{h})
satisfies the discrete inf-sup condition (for detailed proof, see
[
DPE12
, Lemma 6.10, p. 251]
), i.e.

\|\widetilde{p}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\lesssim\sup_{\bm{\phi_{h}}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b_{h}(\bm{\phi_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}+\left(\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking
q_{h}=0
in (
3.14
), it follows that

b_{h}(\bm{\phi_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})=\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},0))-a_{h}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\bm{\phi_{h}}),

and hence, there holds

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{p}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\phi_{h}}\in U_{h}\times P_{h}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},0))}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\phi_{h}},0)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}-\frac{a_{h}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h},\phi_{h}})}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\phi_{h}}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|a_{h}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h},\phi_{h}})|}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}+\sup_{\bm{\phi_{h}}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|(\rho\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h},\phi_{h}})|}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})+\left(\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})+\left(\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Squaring on both sides yields

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{p}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}^{2}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\Gamma_{x}}h_{x}\|[\![\widetilde{p}_{h}]\!]\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}^{2}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})+\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

where we have use (
4.4
).

This along with (
4.4
) yields

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{L}^{2}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})+\mathcal{L}(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

from which (
4.2
) follows. A use of (
3.17
) with the fact that
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x})
(Lemma
2.3
) shows the boundedness result.
This completes the proof.
∎

Note that the local density
\rho\geq 0
. Thanks to the inf-sup condition and the boundedness property proved in Lemma
4.1
, an application of the Lax-Milgram lemma demonstrate the existence of a unique solution
(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\in U_{h}\times P_{h}
for the auxiliary problem (
4.1
).

To estimate
\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}
, we use the following identity:

(4.5)

\mathcal{A}_{h}\left((\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}},p-\widetilde{p}_{h}),(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h})\right)+\left(\rho\left(\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right),\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)=0.

Using
L^{2}
-projections, we rewrite

(4.6)

\displaystyle\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{u-\mathcal{P}_{x}u}\right)+\left(\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right)=\bm{\theta_{u}-\eta_{u}},

\displaystyle p-\widetilde{p}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left(p-\mathcal{P}_{x}p\right)+\left(\mathcal{P}_{x}p-\widetilde{p}_{h}\right)=\theta_{p}-\eta_{p},

where,

\displaystyle\bm{\theta_{u}}=\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u-\widetilde{u}_{h}},\quad\bm{\eta_{u}}=\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}u-u},\quad\theta_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{x}p-\widetilde{p}_{h},\quad\eta_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{x}p-p.

Using (
4.6
), we rewrite (
4.5
) as

(4.7)

\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}\right),\left(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}\right)\right)+\left(\rho\bm{\theta_{u}},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{h}\left(\left(\bm{\eta_{u}},\eta_{p}\right),\left(\bm{\phi_{h}},q_{h}\right)\right)+\left(\rho\bm{\eta_{u}},\bm{\phi_{h}}\right).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\bm{u},p)
denote the unique solution of the Stokes’ equation (
1.2
). Let
(\bm{\widetilde{u}_{h}},\widetilde{p}_{h})\in U_{h}\times P_{h}
be the solution of the auxiliary problem (
4.1
). Assume
(\bm{u},p)\in L^{\infty}([0,T];\bm{H^{k+1}})\times L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{k})
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
h
, such that

\|\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}+h_{x}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+h_{x}\|p-\widetilde{p}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\leq Ch^{k+1}_{x}.

Proof.

Note that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\theta_{u}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\eta_{u}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Projection estimate yields
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\eta_{u}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq Ch^{k}
. Hence, it suffices to estimate
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\theta_{u}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
.

Employing the discrete inf-sup condition and the boundedness property(proved in Lemma
4.1
) in the equation (
4.7
), we obtain

\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\bm{\eta_{u}},\eta_{p}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

A use of the projection estimate yields

(4.8)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\left(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}\right)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq Ch^{k}_{x}.

Similarly, as

(4.9)

\|\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}\leq\|\bm{\eta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}+\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}},

and as we have
\|\bm{\eta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}\leq Ch^{k+1}_{x}
from the projection estimate, it is enough to estimate
\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}
.

Let
(\bm{\zeta},\xi)\in[\bm{H^{1}\cap L_{0}^{2}}]\times L^{2}
denote the solution of the Stokes’ problem (
1.2
) with forcing term
\bm{\theta_{u}}\in\bm{L^{2}}
.
Then, owing to Stokes’ regularity
[
GS91
,
AG91
,
Lad69
]
, we have

(4.10)

\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{\bm{H^{2}}}+\|\xi\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}.

Moreover, since
\bm{\zeta}\in\bm{H^{2}\cap L^{2}_{0}},\nabla\cdot\bm{\zeta}=0,[\![\nabla\zeta_{i}]\!]=0,i=1,2
across all
F\in\Gamma_{x}^{0}
and
[\![\bm{\zeta}]\!]=0
across all

, we obtain

a_{h}(\bm{\theta_{u},\zeta})=a_{h}(\bm{\zeta,\theta_{u}})=-\left(\Delta_{x}\bm{\zeta},\bm{\theta_{u}}\right)_{\Omega_{x}},

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{\zeta},\theta_{p})=0.

Since
\xi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{x}),[\![\xi]\!]\cdot\bm{n}=0
across all
F\in\Gamma_{x}^{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle b_{h}(\bm{\theta_{u}},\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{\theta_{u}},\nabla_{x}\xi\right)_{\Omega_{x}},

\displaystyle s_{h}(\theta_{p},\xi)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(-\Delta_{x}\bm{\zeta}+\nabla_{x}\xi+\rho\bm{\zeta}\right),\bm{\theta_{u}}\right)_{\Omega_{x}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}),(\bm{\zeta},\xi))+\left(\rho\bm{\theta_{u},\zeta}\right).

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}),(\bm{\mathcal{P}_{x}\zeta},\mathcal{P}_{x}p))+\left(\rho\bm{\theta_{u},\mathcal{P}_{x}\zeta}\right)=0

Using the boundedness property and the projection estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{h}((\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p}),(\bm{\zeta-\mathcal{P}_{x}\zeta},\xi-\mathcal{P}_{x}\xi))+\left(\rho\bm{\theta_{u},\zeta-\mathcal{P}_{x}\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\zeta-\mathcal{P}_{x}\zeta},\xi-\mathcal{P}_{x}\xi)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{x}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{p})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\left(\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{\bm{H^{2}}}+\|\xi\|_{H^{1}}\right)

where we have used (
4.10
) in the final step.
Hence we get

\|\bm{u-\widetilde{u}_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}\leq Ch^{k+1}_{x}.

Observe that

From the projection estimate, we have

.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Error estimates for Vlasov equation

The error equation for the Vlasov equation is

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\left(f-f_{h}\right),\psi_{h}\right)+\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{h,R}(\bm{u};f,\psi_{h})-\mathcal{B}_{h,R}(\bm{u_{h}};f_{h},\psi_{h})\right)=0\quad\forall\quad\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{h}

where
\mathcal{B}_{h,R}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)
is defined in (
3.5
).

Setting
e_{f}=f-f_{h}
, we rewrite the above equation as

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}e_{f},\psi_{h}\right)+a_{h}^{0}(e_{f},\psi_{h})+\mathcal{N}(\bm{u};f,\psi_{h})-\mathcal{N}^{h}(\bm{u_{h}};f_{h},\psi_{h})=0\quad\forall\,\psi_{h}\in\mathcal{Z}_{h},

where

(4.16)

a_{h}^{0}(e_{f},\psi_{h})=-\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{R}e_{f}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{h}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{T^{v}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{v}}\int_{T^{v}}\int_{\Gamma_{x}}\{ve_{f}\}_{\alpha}\cdot[\![\psi_{h}]\!]\,{\rm d}s(x)\,{\rm d}v,

(4.17)

\mathcal{N}(\bm{u};f,\psi_{h})=-\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{R}f(\bm{u}-v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\psi_{h}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\,\,+\sum_{T^{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\int_{T^{x}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}}f(\bm{u}-v)\cdot[\![\psi_{h}]\!]\,{\rm d}s(v)\,{\rm d}x\,,

and

(4.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{h}(\bm{u_{h}};f_{h},\psi_{h})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{R}f_{h}(\bm{u_{h}}-v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\psi_{h}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\sum_{T^{x}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{x}}\int_{T^{x}}\int_{\Gamma_{v}}\{(\bm{u_{h}}-v)f_{h}\}_{\beta}\cdot[\![\psi_{h}]\!]\,{\rm d}s(v)\,{\rm d}x\,.

4.3.
Special Projection:

To define the projection operator, first we define one dimensional projection operator
\pi^{\pm}
whose definition is based on the projection defined in
[
SS00
, Section 3.1]
. Let
\pi^{+}:H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\rightarrow X_{h}
be the projection operator defined by

(4.19)

\int_{T^{x}}(\pi^{+}(w)-w)q_{h}\,{\rm d}x=0\quad\forall\,\,q_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(T^{x}),

together with the matching condition:

\pi^{+}(w(x^{+}))=w(x^{+}),

and let
\pi^{-}:H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\rightarrow X_{h}
be the projection operator defined as

(4.20)

\int_{T^{x}}(\pi^{+}(w)-w)q_{h}\,{\rm d}x=0\quad\forall\,\,q_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{k-1}(T^{x}),

together with the matching condition:

\pi^{-}(w(x^{-}))=w(x^{-}).

The projection estimate for
\pi^{\pm}
are available(refer to
[
SS00
]
)

\|w-\pi^{\pm}(w)\|_{0,T^{x}}\leq Ch^{k+1}|w|_{k+1,T^{x}},

where
C
is a constant depending only on the shape-regularity of the mesh and the polynomial degree.

Inspired by the projection operator defined in
[
DDCS09
,
DDCS12
]
, we define projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{h}
as follows: Let

be an arbitrary element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and let
w\in\mathcal{C}^{0}({R})
. The restriction of
\Pi_{h}(w)
to
R
is defined by

(4.21)

\Pi_{h}(w)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\tilde{\Pi}_{x}\otimes\tilde{\Pi}_{v})(w)\quad\mbox{if sign}\left(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)\cdot\bm{n}\right)=\mbox{constant},\\
&(\tilde{\Pi}_{x}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{v})(w)\quad\mbox{if sign}\left(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)\cdot\bm{n}\right)\neq\mbox{constant},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{\Pi}_{x}:\mathcal{C}^{0}(\Omega_{x})\rightarrow X_{h}

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let

be the solution of the Vlasov-Stokes’ problem (

)-(
1.2

. Further, let

\|f(t)-f_{h}(t)\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\leq C(K^{*})h^{k+1}\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T]

where

depends on the final time
T
, the polynomial degree
k
, the shape regularity of the partition and the continuous solution
(\bm{u},f)
.

Proof.

Note that

\|f-f_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\leq\|\eta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Since from equation (
4.23
), the estimate of

is known, it is enough to estimate

It follows from (
4.26

(4.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\theta_{f}\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\||v\cdot\bm{n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}[\![\theta_{f}]\!]\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma_{x}\times\mathcal{T}^{v}_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\||(\bm{u_{h}}-v)\cdot\bm{n}|^{\frac{1}{2}}[\![\theta_{f}]\!]\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{T}^{x}_{h}\times\Gamma_{v}}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}^{2}(\bm{u_{h}}-v,f,\theta_{f})+\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{R}\theta^{2}_{f}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=:I_{1}-I_{2}-\mathcal{K}^{1}-\mathcal{K}^{2}+I_{3},

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

to be

Observe that

(4.32)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{R}(\bm{u-u_{h}})\cdot\nabla_{v}f\theta_{f}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x|

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{u-u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}\|\nabla_{v}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

4.4
. Note that it is here in (

) that we require

to be in
W^{1,\infty}
in the

The estimate for

in Lemma

yields

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{\phi_{h}}\in U_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\rho-\rho_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{u_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\phi_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-f_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{u_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{u_{h}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq Ch^{k+1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{u_{h}},p_{h})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Choosing
h
small enough so that
\left(\alpha-Ch^{k+1}\right)>0
implies that
(\bm{u_{h}},p_{h})\equiv(0,0)
. This completes the proof of existence and uniqueness of solution
(\bm{u_{h}},p_{h})
for a given
f_{h}
.

Therefore, the solution map
f_{h}\mapsto\bm{u_{h}}=S(f_{h})
is well defined. On substituting
S(f_{h})
in place of
\bm{u_{h}}
in equation (
3.5
) leads to a system of non-linear ODE’s. Since the non-linearity is locally Lipschitz, an application of the Picard’s theorem yields an existence of unique solution
f_{h}(t)
for
t\in(0,t_{n}^{*})
for some
t_{n}^{*}
. Now, using Lemma
3.5
for boundedness of
f_{h}
, we can extend our solution to the complete interval
[0,T]
using continuation arguments. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

With appropriate modifications, it is possible to extend the present analysis to
3

\|f(t)-f_{h}(t)\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\|\bm{(u-u_{h})}(t)\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}=O(h^{k+1})\quad\mbox{for}\quad k\geq 2.

To expand it a bit in

\|w_{h}\|_{L^{p}(T^{x})}\leq Ch_{x}^{\frac{3}{p}-\frac{3}{q}}\|w_{h}\|_{L^{q}(T^{x})}.

Hence, on using (

\displaystyle\|\bm{u-u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{\infty}}}

Then, the following changes in (
4.34
) as follows

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}\|\bm{u-u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{\infty}}}\|f\|_{k+1,\Omega}\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle+Ch^{k+1}\left(\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{W^{1,\infty}}}\|f\|_{k+1,\Omega}+\left(L+\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L^{\infty}}}\right)\|f\|_{k+2,\Omega}\right)\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k}\left(h\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{W^{1,\infty}}}+h^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{H^{k+1}}}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{u-u_{h}}\|_{\bm{L^{2}}}\right)\|f\|_{k+1,\Omega}\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}

6.
Conclusion

This paper introduces the semi-discrete numerical method (
3.5
)-(
3.13
) using discontinuous Galerkin methods for the continuum problem and the conservation properties are stated in Lemma
3.4
. It is difficult to prove the non-negativity of the discrete density
\rho_{h}
. Due to this the proposed scheme is not coercive and this creates a major problem in the error estimates which is handled by a smallness condition on
h
. The optimal rate of convergence for polynomial degrees
k\geq 1
is proved in Theorem
4.10
. Finally, using splitting scheme, some numerical experiments are reported.

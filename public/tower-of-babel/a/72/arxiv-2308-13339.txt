Similitude exceptional theta correspondences
Petar Bakić, Wee Teck Gan and Gordan Savin

Abstract




2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
11S90, 17A75, 17C40

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a local field and suppose
(G,H)
is a reductive dual pair in simple linear algebraic group
\mathcal{E}
over
F
.
Hence,
G
and
H
are subgroups of
\mathcal{E}
which are mutual centralizers of each other.
We thus have a homomorphism of algebraic groups

i:G\times H\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}.

If
\Omega
is a minimal representation of
\mathcal{E}(F)
, we may pull back
\Omega
to the product group
G(F)\times H(F)
and study the resulting branching problem: this is the usual set up of theta correspondence.

However, the map
i
is frequently not injective. Typically, one has (multiplicative type) central subgroups

Z\hookrightarrow G\quad\text{and}\quad Z\hookrightarrow H

which are identified under
i
. More precisely, one has

\mathrm{Ker}(i)=Z^{\nabla}=\{(z,z^{-1}):z\in Z\},

so that one has an injecion

(G\times H)/Z^{\nabla}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}.

This already occurs in the setting of the classical theta correspondence for the symplectic-orthogonal dual pair

i:{\mathrm{O}}(V)\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Sp}}(V\otimes W)

whose kernel is the diagonally embedded
Z=\mu_{2}
.

In this short note, we explain how the non-injectivity of
i
can be exploited to extend the theta correspondence for
G(F)\times H(F)
to the setting of similitude groups associated to
G
and
H
. Indeed, as we shall see, this non-injectivity is the reason for the existence of such a theory.

Here is a brief summary of the content of the paper. In §2, we associate to a dual pair
G\times H\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
as above, and some additional data, a similitude dual pair
\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H}
. After introducing the associated similitude version of theta correspondence in §3 and demonstrating some simple properties of this similitude theta correspondence in §4, we show in §5 that the Howe duality theorem holds for
G\times H
if and only if the analogous theorem holds for
\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H}
; this is Proposition
5.1
and should be considered the main result of this paper. The rather long §6 is devoted to
highlighting a few families of examples in the context of exceptional groups. In §7, we discuss how the useful notion of seesaw duality can be extended to the similitude setting, using the families of examples discussed in §6 as illustration. Finally, §8 discusses the similitude theta correspondences in the global setting and §9 collects together some basic results in Clifford theory that are used in the paper.

2.
Similitude Groups

Let us first introduce the notion and construction of the relevant similitude groups. We will continue to work in the context of the introduction.

2.1.
Initial data

The initial data needed for the construction of similitude groups is described in the following hypotheses:

(a)

the commutative group
Z
can be embedded into an induced torus:

j:Z\hookrightarrow T:=\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}\mathbb{G}_{m}

where
K
is an étale
F
-algebra of finite rank.

(b)

One has

S:=T/j(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m}),

for some
E/F
.

We shall fix these data in what follows.

2.2.
Examples

Let us give some examples:

(1)

when
Z=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{n_{i}}
, one takes embeddings
\mu_{n_{i}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, so that one has

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>Z@>{}>{}>T=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}@>{}>{}>T=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

where the map
T\rightarrow T
is
z\mapsto z^{n_{i}}
on the
i
-th coordinate.

(2)

when

Z=\mathrm{Res}^{1}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})=\mathrm{Ker}(N_{K/F}:\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}),

one takes
T=\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and one has

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>Z@>{}>{}>T@>{N_{K/F}}>{}>{\mathbb{G}}_{m}@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

(3)

when

Z=\mathrm{Res}^{1}_{K/F}(\mu_{n})=\mathrm{Ker}(N_{K/F}:\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}(\mu_{n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}),

one takes
T=\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and one has

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>Z@>{}>{}>T@>{N_{K/F}\times[n]}>{}>{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m}).\end{CD}

Note that in this last example, the sequence above is not exact on the right, as the image of the last arrow is a codimension 1 torus.
Consider however the special case when

|n-[K:F]|=1.

In this case, it turns out that the composite

\begin{CD}T/Z@>{N_{K/F}\times[n]}>{}>{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})@>{(t,x)\mapsto tx^{-1}}>{}>\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})=T\end{CD}

is an isomorphism of algebraic tori. Indeed, if
x\in\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})
lies in the kernel of the above composite map, so that

N_{K/F}(x)=x^{n},

then on taking norms on both sides, we conclude that

N_{K/F}(x)^{[K:F]}=N_{K/F}(x)^{n},

and hence
x^{n}=N_{K/F}(x)=1
, i.e.
x\in\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}^{1}(\mu_{n})
. In the examples we shall consider later, the rather peculiar condition
|n-[K:F]|=1
will turn out to be satisfied.

2.3.
Similitude groups

Now we make the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

The similitude group
\tilde{G}
associated to
G
and the data in (a) and (b) above is:

\tilde{G}:=(G\times T)/Z^{\nabla}.

The associated similitude homomorphism is the map

{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}:\tilde{G}\twoheadrightarrow T/j(Z)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

given by the second projection and (b).

Then observe that one has:

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>G@>{}>{}>\tilde{G}@>{{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}}>{}>\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>T@>{}>{}>\tilde{G}@>{}>{}>G/Z@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

Likewise, we have the similitude group and similitude character

{\mathrm{sim}}_{H}:\tilde{H}:=(H\times T)/Z^{\nabla}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{E/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})

associated to
H
and the data in (a) and (b), as well as analogs of the two exact sequences above.

2.4.
Surjectivity of similitude factor

Recalling that
S=T/j(Z)
, we have a commutative diagram of short exact sequences of algebraic groups:

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>Z@>{j}>{}>T@>{}>{}>S@>{}>{}>1\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\Big\|\\
1@>{}>{}>G@>{}>{}>\tilde{G}@>{{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}}>{}>S@>{}>{}>1.\end{CD}

On taking
F
-points, we obtain the following commutative diagram with exact rows:

\begin{CD}T(F)@>{}>{}>S(F)@>{\alpha}>{}>H^{1}(F,Z)\\
@V{}V{}V\Big\|@V{}V{\beta}V\\
\tilde{G}(F)@>{{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}}>{}>S(F)@>{}>{}>H^{1}(F,G)\end{CD}

From this, we see that the similitude map
{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}:\tilde{G}(F)\longrightarrow S(F)
is surjective if and only if the map
S(F)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,G)
is
0
.
Since the latter map factors as:

\begin{CD}S(F)@>{\alpha}>{}>H^{1}(F,Z)@>{\beta}>{}>H^{1}(F,G),\end{CD}

we obtain:

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}:\tilde{G}(F)\longrightarrow S(F)
is surjective if one of the following conditions holds:

•

\alpha:S(F)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,Z)
is
0
;

•

\beta:H^{1}(F,Z)\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,G)
is
0
.

Observe that the first condition in the proposition depends only on the initial data (a) and (b) in §
2.1
, whereas the second condition depends only on the pair
Z\subset G
.

2.5.
The group
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}

Going back to dual pairs, let us set

\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=(\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=\{(g,h)\in\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H}:{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}(g)\cdot{\mathrm{sim}}_{H}(h)=1\}.

Note that one has exact sequences:

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>G(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{H}(F)\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>H(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{G}(F)\end{CD}

One denotes the image of the last arrow in each sequence above by
\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
and
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
respectively. Then one has the following containments with finite index:

T(F)\cdot G(F)\subset\tilde{G}(F)^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad T(F)\cdot H(F)\subset\tilde{H}(F)^{+}.

Observe moreover that

\tilde{G}(F)^{+}=\tilde{G}(F)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}(\tilde{G}(F))\subseteq{\mathrm{sim}}_{H}(\tilde{H}(F))

and

\tilde{H}(F)^{+}=\tilde{H}(F)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad{\mathrm{sim}}_{H}(\tilde{H}(F))\subseteq{\mathrm{sim}}_{G}(\tilde{G}(F)).

In particular,
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}=\tilde{G}(F)
if
{\mathrm{sim}}_{H}:\tilde{H}(F)\longrightarrow S(F)
is surjective.

Definition 2.3
.

We shall call the pair of groups
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
constructed above a similitude dual pair.

3.
Similitude Theta Correspondence

We now consider the theory of theta correspondence.
Suppose that
\Omega
is a minimal representation of
\mathcal{E}(F)

[
GS
]
.
If
\pi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
, then the big theta lift of
\pi
to
H(F)
is by definition

\Theta(\pi)=(\Omega\otimes\pi^{\vee})_{G(F)},

which is a smooth representation of
H(F)
. Likewise, one has a smooth
G(F)
-representation
\Theta(\sigma)
for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H(F))
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the dual pair
(G,H)
satisfies the Howe duality property, or the Howe duality theorem holds for
(G,H)
, if
\Theta(\pi)
is of finite length with a unique irreducible quotient (if nonzero) for any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
and likewise for
\Theta(\sigma)
for any
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H(F))
.

We would now like to extend the theta correspondence for
(G(F),H(F))
to the setting of the similitude dual pair
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
. For this,
we observe that

T^{\nabla}=\{(t,t^{-1}):t\in T\}\subset\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}

is a central subgroup.
Setting

J^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}/T^{\nabla}=\left((\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\right)/T^{\nabla},

one sees that the natural inclusion gives an isomorphism

(G\times H)/Z^{\nabla}\simeq J^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=\left((\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\right)/T^{\nabla}.

In particular, one has a natural homomorphism

\iota:\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\twoheadrightarrow J^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}.

On taking
F
-points and noting that
T
is an induced torus, so that
H^{1}(F,T)
is trivial, we see that

((G\times H)/Z^{\nabla})(F)=J^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)=(\tilde{G}(F)\times\tilde{H}(F))^{{\mathrm{sim}}}/T^{\nabla}(F),

so that one has

\iota:\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\twoheadrightarrow J^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(F).

In particular, any representation of
\mathcal{E}(F)
can be pulled back via
\iota
to

\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)=(\tilde{G}(F)\times\tilde{H}(F))^{{\mathrm{sim}}}.

Now one has the following short exact sequences:

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>G(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{H}(F)^{+}@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

and

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>H(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)@>{}>{}>\tilde{G}(F)^{+}@>{}>{}>1.\end{CD}

Given an irreducible representation
\tilde{\pi}
of
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
, we may regard
\tilde{\pi}^{\vee}
as a representation of
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)
via the natural surjection
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\rightarrow\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
given in the second short exact sequence above. Noting that
G(F)
is a normal subgroup of
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)
by the first short exact sequence above, we set

\Theta(\tilde{\pi}):=(\Omega\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\vee})_{G(F)},

so that
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
is a smooth representation of
\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
.
Likewise for
\tilde{\sigma}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
, one has

\Theta(\tilde{\sigma})=(\Omega\otimes\tilde{\sigma}^{\vee})_{H(F)}

which is a smooth representation of
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
.

One can rephrase the above definitions slightly by introducing the similitude minimal representation. Set

\tilde{\Omega}={{\mathrm{ind}}}_{\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)}^{\tilde{J}(F)^{+}}\Omega

where

\tilde{J}=\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H}

and

\tilde{J}(F)^{+}=\tilde{G}(F)^{+}\times\tilde{H}(F)^{+}.

Then
\tilde{\Omega}
is a representation of the similitude dual pair
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}\times\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
and we can defined
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
and
\Theta(\tilde{\sigma})
in the usual way.
We leave it to the reader to verify that the two definitions are the same.

As in the “isometry” case, we can define the notion of the Howe duality property holding for
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
:

Definition 3.2
.

We say that the similitude dual pair
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
satisfies the Howe duality property if

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
has finite length and unique irreducible quotient (if nonzero) for any
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F)^{+})
, and likewise for
\Theta(\tilde{\sigma})
with
\tilde{\sigma}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
.

4.
Central Characters

Let us record some simple properties of the similitude theta correspondence.

Lemma 4.1

(Central characters)
.

Suppose that
\pi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F)^{+})
has
T(F)
-central character
\chi
. Then the smooth
\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
-module
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})

has
T(F)
-central character
\chi
.

Proof.

This is because
T^{\nabla}(F)\subset\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)
acts trivially on
\Omega
and so
T(F)\subset\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
acts on

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})=(\Omega\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\vee})_{G(F)}

in the same way as
T(F)\subset\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
acts on
\tilde{\pi}
.
∎

One can also decompose the
\tilde{J}(F)^{+}
-module
\tilde{\Omega}
according to characters of the central subgroup
T(F)\times T(F)/T^{\nabla}(F)
.
Each character of
T(F)\times T(F)/T^{\nabla}(F)
is of the form
\chi\otimes\chi
, where
\chi
is a character of
T(F)
.
We can consider the
\chi
-isotypic quotient

\tilde{\Omega}_{T,\chi}
of
\tilde{\Omega}
. which can be described as follows.

One has an intermediate group

\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\subset\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\cdot(T(F)\times T(F))\subset\tilde{J}(F)^{+}.

We may extend the
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)
-module
\Omega
to the intermediate group
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\cdot(T(F)\times T(F))
by letting
T(F)\times T(F)
acts by
\chi\otimes\chi
.
This extension is well-defined because
\chi\otimes\chi
is trivial on the intersection

\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\cap(T(F)\times T(F))=T^{\nabla}(F).

We denote this extended representation by
\Omega_{\chi}

Then one has

\tilde{\Omega}_{T,\chi}\simeq{\mathrm{ind}}_{\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F)\cdot(T(F)\times T(F))}^{\tilde{J}(F)^{+}}\Omega_{\chi}.

It will sometimes be convenient to restrict to a fixed
T(F)
-central character
\chi
, in which case one will be working with
\tilde{\Omega}_{T,\chi}
.

5.
The Howe Duality Property

In this section, we shall show:

Proposition 5.1
.

The Howe duality property holds for the dual pair
(G(F),H(F))
if and only if the Howe duality property holds for the similitude dual pair
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
.

5.1.
Some lemmas

We shall show this in a series of lemmas, beginning with the following observation:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V
be a not-necessarily-smooth representation of
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
on which the central subgroup
T(F)
acts by a character
\chi
. Then:

(i) A vector
v\in V
a smooth with respect to
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
if and only if it is smooth with respect to
G(F)
.

(ii) If
V^{\infty}
denotes the subspace of smooth vectors of
V
(with respect to
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
or
G(F)
), then
V^{\infty}
has finite length as a
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
-module if and only if it has finite length as a
G(F)
-module.

(iii) If
V^{\infty}
is an irreducible smooth
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
-module, then
V^{\infty}
is semisimple as a
G(F)
-module. Indeed,

V^{\infty}|_{G(F)}\simeq m\cdot\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}

where the
V_{i}
’s are irreducible smooth
G(F)
-modules which are pairwise inequivalent. Moreover,
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
permutes the isomorphism classes of the
V_{i}
’s transitively.

(iv) Suppose that
V=V^{\infty}
is of finite length (as
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
-module or
G(F)
-module). Let
\tilde{cosoc}(V)
be the cosocle (or maximal semisimple quotient) of
V
as a
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
-module and likewise let
cosoc(V)
be the cosocle of
V
as a
G(F)
-module. Then the natural map

cosoc(V)\longrightarrow\tilde{cosoc}(V)

is an isomorphism of
G(F)
-modules.

Proof.

All these follows readily from the fact that
T(F)\cdot G(F)
is an open subgroup of finite index in
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
and the Clifford theory recounted in Appendix A below.
More precisely,

•

(i) follows from the fact that an open compact subgroup of
G(F)\cdot T(F)
is an open compact subgroup of
\tilde{G}(F)^{+}
;

•

(ii) follows from Proposition
9.1
(1);

•

(iii) follows from Proposition
9.1
(2) and Proposition
9.4
;

•

(iv) follows from Proposition
9.1
(3).

∎

Next we have:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F)^{+})
and suppose (as in Lemma
5.2
(iii)) that

\tilde{\pi}|_{G(F)}\simeq m\cdot\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{i}

for some
\pi_{i}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
. Then we have:

(5.4)

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})|_{H(F)}=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\Theta(\pi_{i}).

In particular,
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
is nonzero if and only if
\Theta(\pi_{i})\neq 0
for some (equivalently for all)
i
.

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle\Theta(\tilde{\pi})|_{H(F)}

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\vee}\right)_{G(F)}\quad\text{(by definition)}

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega\otimes(m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{\vee})\right)_{G(F)}

\displaystyle=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}(\Omega\otimes\pi_{i}^{\vee})_{G(F)}

\displaystyle=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\Theta(\pi_{i}).

Here, we have used Lemma
5.2
(i) which ensures that the contragredient of
\tilde{\pi}
as a representation of
\tilde{G}(F)
is the same as its contragredient as a representation of
G(F)
, so that

\tilde{\pi}^{\vee}|_{G(F)}\simeq m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\pi_{i}^{\vee}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

∎

By Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.2
, we deduce:

Corollary 5.5
.

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
has finite length as an
\tilde{H}(F)^{+}
-module

if and only if

\Theta(\pi_{i})
has finite length as an
H(F)
-module for all
i
.

Assuming this finiteness condition holds, one has:

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\Theta(\pi_{i})

and

(5.6)

\theta(\tilde{\pi})=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\theta(\pi_{i})

as
H(F)
-modules.

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.1

We can now prove Proposition
5.1
.
Suppose first that the Howe duality property holds for the pair
(G,H)
. By symmetry, for any
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F)^{+})
, we need to show that if
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
is nonzero, then it has finite length and
\theta(\tilde{\pi})
is irreducible.

Suppose that

\tilde{\pi}|_{G(F)}\simeq m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}

for
\pi_{i}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
. Then by Corollary
5.5
,

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})|_{H(F)}=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\Theta(\pi_{i}).

By hypothesis,
\Theta(\pi_{i})
has finite length for each
i
, and hence so does
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
as a
\tilde{H}(F)
-module by Corollary
5.5
. Moreover,
by Lemma
5.3
,
\Theta(\pi_{i})
is nonzero for each
i
and
\sigma_{i}:=\theta(\pi_{i})
is irreducible by hypothesis.

Let
\tilde{\sigma}\subset\theta(\tilde{\pi})
be an irreducible summand.
Since (by (
5.6
))

\theta(\tilde{\pi})|_{H(F)}=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\theta(\pi_{i})=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\sigma_{i},

we see that

\tilde{\sigma}|_{H(F)}\simeq n\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\sigma_{i}\quad\text{for some $0<n\leq m$.}

If
m=1
, then
\theta(\tilde{\pi})=\tilde{\sigma}
and we would have been done already. For general
m
, we argue as follows.
Switching the roles of
G
and
H
in the above argument, it follows by Corollary
5.5
that

\theta(\tilde{\sigma})|_{G(F)}=n\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\theta(\sigma_{i}).

By hypothesis, each
\theta(\sigma_{i})
is irreducible and hence
\theta(\sigma_{i})=\pi_{i}
.
But since
\tilde{\pi}\subset\theta(\tilde{\sigma})
, we deduce that
m\leq n
.
Hence, we must have
m=n
, so that
\theta(\tilde{\pi})=\tilde{\sigma}
is irreducible.

Conversely, suppose now the that Howe duality property holds for the similitude pair
(\tilde{G}(F)^{+},\tilde{H}(F)^{+})
.
By symmetry, take any
\pi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
and choose
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F)^{+})
such that
\pi\subset\tilde{\pi}
. Suppose

\tilde{\pi}|_{G(F)}\simeq m\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}

with
\pi_{i}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
for each
i
, and
\pi=\pi_{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}\in I
.

By Cor.
5.5
, we have

\Theta(\tilde{\pi})|_{H(F)}=m\cdot\bigoplus_{i}\Theta(\pi_{i}).

Since we are assuming that
\Theta(\pi)\neq 0
, it follows by Lemma
5.3
that
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
is nonzero.
By hypothesis and Cor.
5.5
,
\Theta(\tilde{\pi})
has finite length as a
H(F)
-module, and hence so does
\Theta(\pi_{i})
for each
i
.

Consider
\tilde{\sigma}:=\theta(\tilde{\pi})
, which is irreducible by hypothesis. By (
5.6
), we see that

(5.7)

\tilde{\sigma}|_{H(F)}\simeq m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\theta(\pi_{i}),

with each
\theta(\pi_{i})
semisimple of finite length. On writing each
\theta(\pi_{i})
as a sum of irreducible summands, we have:

(5.8)

\tilde{\sigma}|_{H(F)}\simeq n\bigoplus_{j\in J}\sigma_{j}

with

\text{distinct $\sigma_{j}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H(F))$},\quad\text{$|I|\leq|J|$}\quad\text{and $n\geq m$.}

Then applying (

) with the roles of
G
and
H

m\cdot\bigoplus_{i\in I}\pi_{i}=\tilde{\pi}|_{G(F)}=\theta(\tilde{\sigma})|_{G(F)}=n\cdot\bigoplus_{j\in J}\theta(\sigma_{j}),

with
\theta(\sigma_{j})\neq 0
.
This implies that

,

|I|=|J|

and
\theta(\sigma_{j})=\pi_{j}
,

after fixing some bijection of

In particular, going back to (

) and (

), we deduce that

is irreducible for each

.

This completes the proof of Proposition

Examples

We give some interesting examples of similitude dual pairs and similitude theta correspondences.

6.1.
Classical dual pairs

We begin by revisiting the case of classical dual pairs.

•

(Symplectic-orthogonal) For a quadratic space
V
and a symplectic vector space

\iota:H\times G={\mathrm{O}}(V)\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(W)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Sp}}(V\otimes W).

Hence, we have

Recall however that the Weil representation

Likewise, to obtain a similitude theta correspondence, we would need to extend

Such extensions have been constructed by B. Roberts

•

(Unitary) If

More precisely, note that

\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{U}}(V\otimes_{E}W)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mp}(V\otimes_{E}W).

Thus, there is no need to do extra work beyond that needed for splitting the isometry dual pairs.

6.5.
Jordan pairs

We refer the reader to
[
L
, Introduction and Chap. 1]
for the notion of Jordan pairs. Since this notion is perhaps less familiar to the reader than the notion of Jordan algebras, let us give some motivation and a brief introduction.

A Freuthendal-Jordan algebra or a cubic norm structure
J
comes equipped with a norm form, which is a cubic form
N_{J}:J\longrightarrow F
. The similitude group of this cubic form

{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})=\{(g,t)\in{\mathrm{GL}}(J)\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}:N_{J}\circ g=t\cdot N_{J}\}

is called the structure group of
J
, and we will call the isometry group
\mathrm{iso}(J,N_{J})
of
N_{J}
the reduced or special structure group (this consists of those pairs
(g,t)
with
t=1
). If
J
is the 27-dimensional Jordan algebra, for example,
{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})
is the group
GE_{6}
and
{\mathrm{iso}}(J,N_{J})
is the simply-connected
E_{6}
.

Now the group
{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})
acts irreducibly on
J
and its linear dual
J^{*}
, but these two representations are not isomorphic (as their central characters are different) and there is no reason to favour one of these representation over the other (a more familiar example is: a group isomorphic to
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
has two standard representations). Indeed, the trace bilinear form on
J
allows us to identify
J
with
J^{*}
, and this defines an outer automorphism of
{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})
which is the inverse map on the center of
{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})
and which interchanges the two representations. In addition, in the context of
E_{6}
, when one considers a quasi-split
E_{6}
associated to a quadratic field extension
K/F
, the two 27-dimensional representations are fused together to give a single rational representation over
F
. Hence, such a quasi-split
E_{6}
cannot be realized as the isometry group of a cubic form over
F
, unlike the split form.

The theory of Jordan pairs, introduced by Loos
[
L
]
, treats both these representations
J
and
J^{*}
on equal footing and realizes
{\mathrm{sim}}(J,N_{J})
as the automorphism group of an algebraic structure on the pair
\{J,J^{*}\}
. More formally, a Jordan pair over
F
consists of the data:

•

a pair
(J^{+},J^{-})
of
F
-vector spaces;

•

a pair of quadratic maps defined over
F
:

Q^{+}:J^{+}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F}(J^{-},J^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad Q^{-}:J^{-}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{F}(J^{+},J^{-}),

satisfying the following axioms
[
L
, Pg. 1, Def. 1.2]
for
\epsilon=\pm
and any
F
-algebra
K
:

(JP1)

\{x,y,Q^{\epsilon}(x)(z)\}^{\epsilon}=Q^{\epsilon}(x)\left(\{y,x,z\}^{-\epsilon}\right)\quad\text{for any $x\in J^{\epsilon}(K)$ and $y,z\in J^{-\epsilon}(K)$};

(JP2)

\{Q^{\epsilon}(x)(y),y,z\}^{\epsilon}=\{x,Q^{-\epsilon}(y)(x),z\}^{\epsilon}\quad\text{for any $x,z\in J^{\epsilon}(K)$ and $y\in J^{-\epsilon}(K)$};

(JP3)

Q^{\epsilon}(Q^{\epsilon}(x)(y))=Q^{\epsilon}(x)\circ Q^{-\epsilon}(y)\circ Q^{\epsilon}(x)\quad\text{for any $x\in J^{\epsilon}(K)$ and $y\in J^{-\epsilon}(K)$},

where we have set

\{x,y,z\}^{\epsilon}=Q^{\epsilon}(x+z)(y)-Q^{\epsilon}(x)(y)-Q^{\epsilon}(z)(y)\in J^{\epsilon}

for the linearization of the map
(x,y)\mapsto Q^{\epsilon}(x)(y)
. These axioms may look a bit unwieldy for the uninitiated (including ourselves), but we will not seriously make use of them in this paper.

A homomorphism from one Jordan pair
(J^{+},J^{-})
to another
(V^{+},V^{-})
is a pair of linear maps

\phi^{\epsilon}:J^{\epsilon}\longrightarrow V^{\epsilon}\quad\text{(with $\epsilon=\pm$)}

such that

\phi^{\epsilon}(Q_{J}^{\epsilon}(x)(y))=Q_{V}^{\epsilon}(\phi^{\epsilon}(x))(\phi^{-\epsilon}(y))\quad\text{ for $x\in J^{\epsilon}$ and $y\in J^{-\epsilon}$.}

Hence, one has a notion of the automorphism group of a Jordan pair
(J^{+},J^{-})
, which is a subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(J^{+})\times{\mathrm{GL}}(J^{-})
.
Via the projection onto each of the factors,
{\mathrm{Aut}}(J^{+},J^{-})
has two natural representations.

6.6.
Examples

We give two pertinent examples here:

•

For a vector space

and define:

This defines a Jordan pair whose automorphism group is the subgroup

\phi:(J^{\square},Q_{J}^{\square})\longrightarrow(V^{\square},Q_{V}^{\square})
is then a
F\times F
-module homomorphism
J^{\square}\longrightarrow V^{\square}
such that

\phi(Q_{J}^{\square}(x)(y))=Q_{V}^{\square}(\phi(x))(\phi(y))\quad\text{ for $x,y\in J^{\square}$.}

The advantage of such a repackaging is that it allows one to introduce twisted version (or
F
-rational forms) of Jordan pairs, where one replaces
F\times F
by a separable quadratic field extension
K/F
.

Moreover, in the definition of an automorphism of
(J^{\square},Q^{\square})
, we may consider those
\phi
’s which are
(F\times F,\sigma)
-linear instead of
(F\times F)
-linear. This gives rise to a larger
F
-automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}_{F}(J^{\square},Q^{\square})
containing the subgroup
{\mathrm{Aut}}_{F\times F}(J^{\square},Q^{\square})
as a normal subgroup of index
2
; these extra
F
-automorphisms are outer automorphisms of
{\mathrm{Aut}}_{F\times F}(J^{\square},Q^{\square})
and their action on
J^{\square}
exchanges the two factors
J^{+}
and
J^{-}
.

Now let
K
be a separable étale
F
-algebra with nontrivial automorphism
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(K/F)
. We shall define a notion of Jordan pair
(J,Q)
with respect to
K/F
, following a paper of de Medts
[
dM
]
where it was introduced under the guise of
Hermitian cubic norm structure

[
dM
, §4]
. Such a Jordan pair consists of:

•

a
K
-vector space
J
;

•

a
K
-quadratic map
Q:J\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K}(J^{\sigma},J)

satisfying the reformulated analog of (JP1-3) (we will not elaborate further here). Let us give two examples:

•

Suppose
(V,h)
is a
K
-vector space equipped with a Hermitian form
h:V\times V^{\sigma}\rightarrow K
. Then defining
Q
by

Q(v)(w)=h(v,w)\cdot v\quad\text{for $v,w\in V$}

gives a Jordan pair relative to
K/F
. The automorphism group of
(V,Q)
is precisely the unitary group
{\mathrm{U}}(V,h)
.

•

In
[
dM
, Thm. 4.6]
, it was explained how a cubic norm structure
J
over
K
and a “
\sigma
-linear self adjoint autotopy” of
J
give rise to a Hermitian cubic norm structure on
J
, from which one can deduce a Jordan pair
(J,Q)
over
K/F
with
Q
given by the
U
-operator for the Hermitian cubic norm structure. If
J
is the exceptional Jordan algebra, then
{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)^{{\mathrm{der}}}
is the quasi-split simply connected
E_{6}^{K}
associated to
K/F
and

{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)/{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)^{{\mathrm{der}}}\simeq{{\mathrm{Res}}}^{1}_{K/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m})

is the anisotropic 1-dimensional torus associated to
K
.

Associated to these two examples and as a twisted version of the dual
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3}\times\mathrm{iso}(J,N_{J})
introduced in the previous subsection, one has the quasi-split but non-split dual pair

{\mathrm{SU}}(V,h)\times{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)^{{\mathrm{der}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E},

where we assume for simplicity that
\mathcal{E}
is split.
The centers of these groups are isomorphic to

Z={{\mathrm{Res}}}^{1}_{K/F}(\mu_{3}).

Following Example (3) in §
2
, we choose
T=\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})
and note that there is a short exact sequence

\begin{CD}1@>{}>{}>Z@>{}>{}>T@>{}>{}>T@>{}>{}>1\end{CD}

defined by the map

x\mapsto x^{3}/N_{K/F}(x)\quad\text{ on $T$.}

The associated similitude dual pair is:

{\mathrm{GU}}(V,h)\times\mathrm{G}{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)

where

\mathrm{G}{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(V,Q)=({{\mathrm{Res}}}_{K/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\times{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)^{der})/Z^{\nabla}=(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q))/\mu_{2}^{\Delta}.

We note here that the similitude character on
{\mathrm{GU}}(V,h)
is not the usual similitude character
{\mathrm{sim}}:{\mathrm{GU}}(V,h)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. Rather, by construction, it is the homomorphism

{\mathrm{GU}}(V,h)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}({\mathbb{G}}_{m})

given by

g\mapsto\det(g)/{\mathrm{sim}}(g).

This is surjective onto
K^{\times}
, as one can see by restricting it to a maximal torus in a Borel subgroup (or by observing that
\mathrm{SU}(V,h)
is simply-connected when
F
is nonarchimedean).
Hence
\mathrm{G}{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)(F)^{+}=\mathrm{G}{\mathrm{Aut}}_{K}(J,Q)(F)
. We will leave it to the reader to work out what
{\mathrm{GU}}(V,h)(F)^{+}
is in the various cases.

Seesaw Duality

In the theory of theta correspondence, seesaw dual pairs and the associated seesaw dualities serve as useful tools. In this section, we examine how this seesaw duality is impacted by the extension to similitude dual pairs. Hence, suppose one has a seesaw diagram of dual pairs in
\mathcal{E}
:

\textstyle{G^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{\prime}}

For
\pi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G(F))
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H^{\prime}(F))
, the associated seesaw identity reads:

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\Theta(\pi),\sigma)\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G\times H^{\prime}}(\Omega,\pi\otimes\sigma)\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\Theta(\sigma),\pi).

Now let us make the following hypotheses:

•

The pair
(G,H)
gives rise to a corresponding similitude dual pair
(\tilde{G},\tilde{H})
, as we explained earlier in this paper;

•

for the other dual pair
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
,
G^{\prime}\times H^{\prime}
maps injectively into
\mathcal{E}
(so that there is no associated similitude pair).

Observe also that

G\times H^{\prime}\subset\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=(\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}(F).

Then we have:

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume the above hypotheses. Then for
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F))
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H^{\prime}(F))
, one has the similitude seesaw identity:

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\Theta(\sigma),\tilde{\pi})\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\Theta(\tilde{\pi}),\sigma).

Proof.

For
\tilde{\pi}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\tilde{G}(F))
and
\sigma\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(H^{\prime}(F))
, we consider

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G\times H^{\prime}}(\Omega,\tilde{\pi}\otimes\sigma).

On the one hand, this is isomorphic to

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G\times H^{\prime}}(\Omega\otimes\sigma^{\vee},\tilde{\pi})={\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}((\Omega\otimes\sigma^{\vee})_{H^{\prime}},\tilde{\pi})\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\Theta(\sigma),\tilde{\pi}).

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.2
(i), we also get

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G\times H^{\prime}}(\Omega\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\vee},\sigma)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}((\Omega\otimes\tilde{\pi}^{\vee})_{G},\sigma)={\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\Theta(\tilde{\pi}),\sigma).

Hence, one deduces that

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\Theta(\sigma),\tilde{\pi})\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\Theta(\tilde{\pi}),\sigma),

as desired.
∎

In other words, there is no significant problem in extending the seesaw identity to the similitude setting, at least under the hypotheses in the proposition.

Let us give some examples of the above situation, using the exceptional dual pairs we discussed above. A first example is the seesaw diagram:

\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{O})=G_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{iso}(J,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}(V^{\square},Q^{\square})^{der}={\mathrm{SL}}_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}(J)}

where

•

J
is a cubic norm structure over
F
with norm form
N
;

•

V
is a 3-dimensional vector space over
F
, giving rise to a Jordan pair
(V^{\square},Q^{\square})
;

•

\mathbb{O}
is the octonion
F
-algebra constructed as
\mathbb{O}=F^{2}\times V^{\square}
(as a structurable algebra
[
dM
, §4]
, a notion we did not introduce).

We have explained that the dual pair
{\mathrm{Aut}}(J)\times{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{O})
does not have a similitude version, whereas the other pair
{\mathrm{SL}}(V)\times\mathrm{iso}(J,N)
does.

As another example, we have:

\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}(E\oplus C^{\flat})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}_{E}(\mathbb{O}^{\flat})={\mathrm{Spin}}^{E}_{8}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}_{E}(C^{\flat})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{O})=G_{2}}

where

•

\mathbb{O}
is the octonion
F
-algebra giving rise to the twisted octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}^{\flat}=\mathbb{O}\otimes_{F}E
over
E/F
;

•

C_{1}^{\flat}
is a twisted composition algebra relative to
E/F
giving rise to a a cubic norm structure (or Freudenthal-Jordan algebra)
J=E\oplus C_{1}^{\flat}
via the Springer decomposition
[
KMRT
, §38A, Pg. 522]
.

As above, the dual pair
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{O})\times{\mathrm{Aut}}(J)
does not have a similitude version whereas the pair
{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{O}^{\flat})\times{\mathrm{Aut}}_{E}(C^{\flat})
does.

Observe that in forming both these seesaw diagrams, the initial data consists of giving two algebraic structures on the bottom row of the diagram, which then induces the algebraic structures in the top row. Moreover, observe that the two seesaw diagrams can be combined into a single one involving 3 dual pairs.

8.
Global Theta Correspondence

In this section, we consider the global theta correspondence. Hence, let
k
be a number field with adele ring
{\mathbb{A}}
. Suppose that we have a dual pair

i:G\times H\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}

as in the introduction, with
{\mathrm{Ker}}(i)=Z^{\nabla}
.

Let
\Omega
be the global minimal representation of
\mathcal{E}
and suppose one has an automorphic realization

\theta:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E}).

For an irreducible cuspidal representation
\pi\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(G)
of
G
, one has the usual notion of global theta lifting. More precisely, for
\phi\in\Omega
and
f\in\pi
, one sets

\theta(\phi,f)(h)=\int_{[G]}\theta(\phi)(gh)\cdot\overline{f(g)}\,dg

for
h\in H({\mathbb{A}})
. Then the global theta lift of
\pi
is the
H({\mathbb{A}})
-submodule

\Theta(\pi)=\langle\theta(\phi,f):\phi\in\Omega,\,f\in\pi\rangle\subset\mathcal{A}(H).

Moreover the map
(\phi,f)\mapsto\theta(\phi,f)
is a
G({\mathbb{A}})
-invariant and
H({\mathbb{A}})
-equivariant map

\theta:\Omega\otimes\overline{\pi}\longrightarrow\Theta(\pi).

Now suppose we have chosen data (a) and (b) as in §
2
and hence have the similitude groups
\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{H}
.
On taking points, we have the groups

\tilde{G}(k)^{+}\subset\tilde{G}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}=\prod_{v}\tilde{G}(k_{v})^{+}

and likewise for
\tilde{H}(k)^{+}\subset\tilde{H}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
.
Moreover, by construction, one has a group homomorphism

\iota:\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=(\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}.

Let
\tilde{\pi}
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\tilde{G}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}=\prod_{v}\tilde{G}(F_{v})^{+}
with a realization

\tilde{\pi}\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(\tilde{G}(k)^{+}\backslash\tilde{G}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}).

We would like to define its similitude global theta lifting to the space
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{H}^{+})
of automorphic forms on
\tilde{H}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
.
For
f\in\tilde{\pi}
,
\phi\in\Omega
and
y\in\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}({\mathbb{A}})
, we set

\theta(\phi,f)(y)=\int_{[G]}\theta(\phi)(g\cdot\iota(y))\cdot\overline{f(g)}\,dg.

This function of
\tilde{J}^{{\mathrm{sim}}}({\mathbb{A}})
descends to a function on
\tilde{H}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
which is left invariant under
\tilde{H}(k)^{+}
, thus giving an element of
\mathcal{A}(\tilde{H}^{+})
.

A more concrete way of defining the function
\theta(\phi,f)
as a function on
\tilde{H}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
is as follows. Given
h\in\tilde{H}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
, one can find an element
t(h)\in\tilde{G}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
such that

{\mathrm{sim}}_{\tilde{G}}(t(h))={\mathrm{sim}}_{\tilde{H}}(h).

Then one has an element

(t(h),h)\in\tilde{J}({\mathbb{A}})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}=(\tilde{G}\times\tilde{H})^{{\mathrm{sim}}}({\mathbb{A}}).

Then

\theta(\phi,f)(h)=\int_{[G]}\theta(\phi)(\iota(gt(h),h))\cdot\overline{f(g)}\,dg.

Since this is merely an explication of the more natural definition of
\theta(\phi,f)
given initially, the choice of the element
t(h)
is immaterial.

The span of the functions
\theta(\phi,f)
, as
\phi\in\Omega
and
f\in\pi
vary, is the global similitude theta lift
\Theta(\pi)
of
\pi
.
We note:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\pi\subset\mathcal{A}(\tilde{G}^{+})
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
\tilde{G}({\mathbb{A}})^{+}
. Assume that

•

\Theta(\pi)\subset\mathcal{A}_{2}(\tilde{H}^{+})
is nonzero and contained in the space of square-integrable automorphic forms (with a fixed central character);

•

the local Howe duality property holds at all places
v
of
k
for
G(k_{v})\times H(k_{v})
or equivalently
\tilde{G}(k_{v})^{+}\times\tilde{H}(k_{v})^{+}
;

Then

\Theta(\pi)\simeq\bigotimes_{v}\theta(\pi_{v}).

In particular,
\Theta(\pi)
is irreducible and one has the compatibility of the local and global similitude theta correspondences.

Appendix: Clifford theory for
p
-adic groups

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
N
be a normal open subgroup of
G
of finite index. Let
A=G/N
, and
m=|A|
.
We assume that the topology on
N
(and hence on
G
) is defined by a sequence of
open compact groups. We assume that
G/K
is countable for one, and hence all of those open compact groups.
This condition assures that Schur’s lemma holds for smooth irreducible representations of
G
and
N
. The following elementary result is
based on
[
BH
, §2.7]
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
(\pi,V)
be a smooth finite length representation of
G
. Then

(1)

V
is a finite length
N
-module.

(2)

V
is semisimple
G
-module if and only if it is a semisimple
N
-module.

(3)

The
N
-socle of
V
is equal to the
G
-socle of
V
.

Proof.

(1) We can assume that
V
is irreducible. Since
V
is finitely generated over
N
, it admits an irreducible
N
-quotient
U
. Then, by Frobenius reciprocity,

V
is a submodule of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}U
. The latter is a semi-simple
N
-module of finite length. Thus
V
is not only finite length but also a semi-simple
N
-module.
This completes (1) and gives one direction of (2). Now assume that
V
is
N
-semisimple.
Let
W\subset V
be a
G
-submodule. Since
V
is
N
-semisimple, there exists an
N
-invariant projection
P
of
V
onto
W
. Then

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{g\in G/N}\pi(g)P\pi(g)^{-1}

is a
G
-invariant projection of
V
onto
W
. Let
U\subset V
be the kernel of this projection. Then
V=W\oplus U
and
V
is
G
-semisimple. (3) The
G
-socle of
V
is
H
-semisimple by
(2) hence it is contained in the
H
-socle. On the other hand, the
H
-socle is
G
-stable, and
G
-semisimple by (2). Hence it is contained in the
G
-socle.
∎

Next we want to describe smooth irreducible representations of
G
in terms of those of
N
.
Let
V
be an irreducible representation of
G
and
U
an irreducible

N
-quotient. By Frobenius reciprocity,
V
is an irreducible submodule of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}(U)
. Thus we need to analyze the induction from
N
to
G
.
For every
g\in G
, let
U^{g}
be the
g
-conjugate of
(\pi,U)
, that is, the representation of
N
on
U
where every
n\in N
acts by
\pi(gng^{-1})
.
Let
G_{U}
be the stabilizer of
U
in
G
, that is, the subgroup of
G
consisting of all
g\in G
such that
U
is isomorphic to
U^{g}
, as
N
-modules.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
V
be an irreducible representation of
G_{U}
containing
U
. Then

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}V

is an irreducible representation of
G
.

Proof.

Observe that the restriction of
V
to
N
is a multiple of
U
. Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{l}
be all non-isomorphic
G
-conjugates of
U
. We have a decomposition, as an
N
-module,

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}V_{i}

where
V_{i}
is a multiple of
U_{i}
. Observe that
G
permutes these summands, and that each
V_{i}
is an irreducible
G_{U_{i}}
-module. Furthermore, for each
i
,

V_{i}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{j\neq i}V_{j}

is a
G_{U_{i}}
-module. Now let
W
be a
G
-submodule of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}V
. Fix an
i
. It is easy to see that any
G_{i}
-submodule of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}V
, in particular
W
,
either contains
V_{i}
or is contained in
V_{i}^{\prime}
. Since
W
is a
G
-module, it follows that
W={\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}V
or
0
.
∎

Next we need to understand irreducible representation of
G_{U}
containing
U
. To simplify notation assume that
G=G_{U}
.
Then, for every
g\in G
, there exists
A_{g}:U\rightarrow U
that intertwines
U
and
U^{g}
. By Schur’s lemma
A_{g}

is unique up to an element in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Thus
U
is naturally a module for a central extension

1\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\tilde{G}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1.

This extension splits over
N
, indeed, we have a canonical choice
A_{n}=\pi(n)
for all
n\in N
. After taking the quotient by
N
we get a central extension

1\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\tilde{A}\rightarrow A\rightarrow 1.

Next, we have

U\otimes{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}^{\tilde{A}}(\epsilon)\simeq{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}(U)

where
U
is viewed as
\tilde{G}
-module and
\epsilon:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the inverse character. This isomorphism is realized by

u\otimes f\mapsto(g\mapsto f(g)\pi(g)(u)).

Thus in order to decompose
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}(U)
it suffices to decompose
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}^{\tilde{A}}(\epsilon)
.

Claim: The extension
\tilde{A}
is defined by a co-cycle with values in
\mu_{m}
. To see this, define a section
s:A\rightarrow\tilde{A}
such that
s(a)
, for every
a\in A
, acts on

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}^{\tilde{A}}(\epsilon)
as a linear transformation of determinant 1.
Then the extension
\tilde{A}
is determined by the co-cycle
c(a,b)
defined by

s(a)s(b)=c(a,b)s(ab).

After taking determinant of both sides, we get
c(a,b)^{m}=1
, as claimed.

Using well known facts from representations of finite groups, we can now write

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}^{\tilde{A}}(\epsilon)\simeq\oplus_{E}\dim(E)\cdot E

where the sum runs over all irreducible representations
E
of
\tilde{A}
such that
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\subset\tilde{A}
acts by
\epsilon
. Call such representations genuine.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
U
be an irreducible representation of
N
such that
G_{U}=G
. With notation as above

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}U=\bigoplus_{E}\dim(E)\cdot U\otimes E

where the sum is over all irreducible genuine representations
E
of
\tilde{A}
.
Representations
U\otimes E
are irreducible and mutually non-isomorphic.

Proof.

It remains to prove the last sentence. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be any two genuine
\tilde{A}
-modules. Since
U
is irreducible
N
-module,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N}(U\otimes E_{1},U\otimes E_{2})\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{C}}(E_{1},E_{2}).

Thus

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(U\otimes E_{1},U\otimes E_{2})\simeq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\tilde{A}}(E_{1},E_{2}).

The lemma follows.
∎

We now briefly discuss the lemma when
A
is abelian. Then
\tilde{A}
is a two-step nilpotent group.
The commutator of elements in
\tilde{A}
defines a skew-linear form on
A\times A

\langle a,b\rangle=[s(a),s(b)]

Since the order of
A
is
m
, the skew form, and therefore the commutator, takes values in
\mu_{m}
.
The dimension of any genuine irreducible representation
E
is equal to
\sqrt{|\bar{A}|}
where
\bar{A}
is the quotient of

A
by the kernel of the skew form
\langle a,b\rangle
. Furthermore, any two irreducible representations of
G
containing
U
are
A
-character twists one of another. Moreover,
two character twists are isomorphic if and only if the characters coincide on the kernel of the skew form.

Finally we combine the two lemmas to obtain the general case.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
U
be an irreducible
N
-module, and
G_{U}
its stabilizer in
G
. Let
A_{U}=G_{U}/N
. Let
\tilde{A}_{U}
be the central extension of
A_{U}
arising from the projective
action of
G_{U}
on
U
. Then

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}U=\bigoplus_{E}\dim(E)\cdot{\mathrm{Ind}}_{G_{U}}^{G}(U\otimes E)

where the sum is over all irreducible genuine representations
E
of
\tilde{A}_{U}
. This is a decomposition of
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{N}^{G}U
into isotypic summands.

{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)

BMS Symmetry of Celestial OPE Preprint: SAGEX-20-01-E
Shamik Banerjee, Sudip Ghosh, Riccardo Gonzo

Abstract
In this paper we study the BMS symmetry of the celestial OPE of two positive helicity gravitons in Einstein theory in four dimensions. The celestial OPE is obtained by Mellin transforming the scattering amplitude in the (holomorphic) collinear limit. The collinear limit at leading order gives the singular term of the celestial OPE. We compute the first subleading correction to the OPE by analysing the four graviton scattering amplitude directly in Mellin space. The subleading term can be written as a linear combination of BMS descendants with the OPE coefficients determined by BMS algebra and the coefficient of the leading term in the OPE. This can be done by defining a suitable BMS primary state. We find that among the descendants, which appear at the first subleading order, there is one which is created by holomorphic supertranslation with simple pole on the celestial sphere.



1
Introduction

Operator product expansion (OPE) plays a central role in the non-perturbative formulation of conformal field theory. OPE is the statement that when two primary operators
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
come close to each other (inside a correlation function) we can replace the product
\phi_{i}\phi_{j}
by a sum over conformal families each of which contains a primary operator, say
\phi_{k}
and its descendants. In general, the OPE coefficient
C_{ij}^{k}
which multiplies the primary operator
\phi_{k}
, cannot be determined by conformal symmetry alone. But, once
C_{ij}^{k}
is specified, the coefficients of all the descendants of
\phi_{k}
are completely fixed by conformal invariance of the OPE.
C_{ij}^{k}
is known as the structure constant of the operator algebra. The goal of the present paper is to study this aspect of the OPE in case of Celestial Conformal Field Theory.

Celestial CFT is conjectured to be the holographic dual of quantum gravity in asymptotically flat space-time
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
;
Cheung:2016iub
;
deBoer:2003vf
;
Banerjee:2018gce
. The observables of the celestial CFT are related to Mellin transformations of flat space scattering amplitudes
Pasterski:2017ylz
;
Schreiber:2017jsr
;
Cardona:2017keg
;
Lam:2017ofc
;
Banerjee:2017jeg
;
Banerjee:2019prz
;
Stieberger:2018edy
;
Stieberger:2018onx
. Under Lorentz transformations, which act on the celestial sphere as global conformal group, Mellin amplitudes transform like correlation functions of a CFT. Now the correspondence between soft theorems
Weinberg:1965nx
;
Cachazo:2014fwa
and Ward identities for asymptotic symmetries
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He
;
Strominger:2014pwa
;
Barnich:2009se
;
Barnich:2011ct
;
Kapec:2014opa
;
Kapec:2016jld
;
He:2017fsb
;
Campiglia:2015kxa
;
Campiglia:2014yka
;
Banerjee:2018fgd
;
Kapec:2017gsg
show that the celestial CFT has, in fact, a much larger symmetry known as BMS
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
. The BMS group

with an abuse of notation, in this paper we call BMS group what would be appropriate to call extended BMS group
is an extension of the usual Poincaré group and consists of superrotations

, which are local conformal transformations of the celestial sphere, and supertranslations, which are local angle-dependent space-time translations at null-infinity. Due to the presence of the supertranslations, the properties of the celestial CFT are somewhat different from usual CFT. For example, BMS algebra is not a direct product of holomorphic and antiholomorphic transformations because supertranslation generators have both holomorphic and antiholomorphic weights. As a result, at least naively, we do not expect holomorphic factorisation at the level of BMS representations. This is a major difference from usual CFTs.

A useful way to study various aspects of celestial CFT and representation theory of BMS algebra is through the construction of celestial OPE. OPE of two primary operators can be obtained by Mellin transformation of the collinear limit of flat space scattering amplitudes
Pate:2019lpp
;
Fotopoulos:2019vac
;
Fan:2019emx

(n+1)

n

Bern:1998sv
;
Akhoury:2011kq
. By the Mellin transformation of the splitting function one obtains the leading term in the celestial OPE and the structure constant of the celestial operator algebra. It is conceivable that the subleading terms in the OPE can be generated by Mellin transforming the subleading terms in the collinear expansion
Nandan:2016ohb
. Now for the celestial OPE what is remarkable is that one can obtain the structure constant by imposing a constraint coming from the subleading soft theorem in case of gluons and subsubleading soft theorem in case of gravitons
Pate:2019lpp
. This suggests that owing to an unusually large amount of global symmetry, algebraic techniques
Pate:2019lpp
;
Banerjee:2019aoy
;
Banerjee:2019tam
;
Fotopoulos:2019tpe
;
Law:2019glh
may play a crucial role in determining the structure of celestial correlation functions (or flat-space
S
-matrix elements). This, in particular, will require an understanding of BMS representation theory in the context of
S
-matrix theory or celestial amplitudes.

Motivated by this, in this paper we compute the first subleading correction to the (holomorphic) collinear limit directly in the Mellin space. The subleading terms in the collinear limit give the subleading terms in the celestial OPE. We focus on the tree level four graviton scattering amplitude in Einstein theory and compute subleading OPE of two positive helicity outgoing graviton primaries. Unlike in the case of
2
-D CFT, the first correction to the leading order result contains the supertranslation descendant created by
singular
supertranslation of the form
u\rightarrow u+\epsilon/z
. We also show that the subleading OPE coefficients can be derived from the BMS algebra once we define a suitable notion of BMS primary state. This suggests the possibility that just like in the case of ordinary CFT, celestial OPE also organizes itself into representations of BMS algebra with the OPE coefficients of BMS descendants determined by BMS algebra. It will be very interesting to prove or disprove this in complete generality.

2
BMS algebra

Let us now describe BMS transformations acting on a three dimensional space with coordinates
(u,z,\bar{z})
where
u
can be thought of as the retarded or Bondi time and
(z,\bar{z})
are the stereographic coordinates of the celestial sphere. At the end, when we derive the subleading OPE coefficients from the BMS algebra, we will restrict to the celestial sphere at
u=0
.

BMS transformations consist of two parts, superrotation and supertranslation. Infinitesimal superrotation is the transformation given by,

z\rightarrow z+\epsilon z^{n+1},\quad\bar{z}\rightarrow\bar{z},\quad u\rightarrow u+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon z^{n+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

(1)

and its antiholomorphic counterpart,

z\rightarrow z,\quad\bar{z}\rightarrow\bar{z}+\bar{\epsilon}\bar{z}^{n+1},\quad u\rightarrow u+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\epsilon}\bar{z}^{n+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

(2)

The corresponding generators are denoted by
L_{n}
and
\bar{L}_{n}
, respectively. They satisfy the (centerless) Virasoro algebra
Barnich:2009se
;
Barnich:2011ct
,

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n},\quad[\bar{L}_{m},\bar{L}_{n}]=(m-n)\bar{L}_{m+n}

(3)

Among these,
\{L_{0},L_{\pm 1},\bar{L}_{0},\bar{L}_{\pm 1}\}
are the generators of Lorentz or global conformal transformations.

Supertranslations act as,

z\rightarrow z,\quad\bar{z}\rightarrow\bar{z},\quad u\rightarrow u+\epsilon z^{a+1}\bar{z}^{b+1},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}

(4)

We denote the corresponding generators by
P_{a,b}
. They satisfy the algebra
Barnich:2009se
;
Barnich:2011ct
;
Banerjee:2018fgd
,

[P_{a,b},P_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}]=0

(5)

In this case,
\{P_{-1,-1},P_{-1,0},P_{0,-1},P_{0,0}\}
are the generators of global space-time translations.

The commutator algebra between supertranslation and superrotation is given by
Barnich:2009se
;
Barnich:2011ct
,

[L_{n},P_{a,b}]=\bigg(\frac{n-1}{2}-a\bigg)P_{a+n,b}\qquad[\bar{L}_{n},P_{a,b}]=\bigg(\frac{n-1}{2}-b\bigg)P_{a,b+n}

(6)

2.1
The Transformation of Fields

Under an infinitesimal conformal transformation (
1
), the primary field
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
of weight
(h,\bar{h})
transforms as
Banerjee:2018gce
,

\delta\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})=\epsilon\left[z^{n+1}\partial+\left(n+1\right)\left(h+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u}\right)z^{n}\right]\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(7)

Similarly for antiholomorphic transformation
Banerjee:2018gce
,

\delta\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})=\epsilon\bigg[\bar{z}^{n+1}\bar{\partial}+(n+1)\left(\bar{h}+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u}\right)\bar{z}^{n}\bigg]\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(8)

For infinitesimal supertranslation given by (
4
) the transformation of the primary field is given by
Banerjee:2018gce
,

\delta\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})=\epsilon z^{a+1}\bar{z}^{b+1}\partial_{u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(9)

At this point we would like to mention one useful point. From the transformation laws (
7
), (
8
) and (
9
) it is easy to check that if
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
is a primary then so is
(\partial/\partial u)^{n}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
, with weight
(h+n/2,\bar{h}+n/2)
.

3
Superrotation and Supertranslation Ward Identities

In celestial CFT correlation functions of the two dimensional primary operator
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
are defined as Mellin transformation of flat space
S
-matrix elements
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Pasterski:2017kqt
,

\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\ \omega_{i}^{i\lambda_{i}}\mathcal{A}(\{p_{i}(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}),\sigma_{i}\})

(10)

where
\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1
for outgoing and incoming particles, respectively. The null momentum
p(\omega,z,\bar{z})
is parametrised as,

p(\omega,z,\bar{z})=\omega(1+z\bar{z},z+\bar{z},-i(z-\bar{z}),1-z\bar{z})

(11)

and
\sigma
denotes the helicity of the particle. Under (Lorentz) global conformal transformation, the L.H.S of (
10
) transforms as the correlation function of primary operators of weight
(h_{i},\bar{h}_{i})
, given by

h_{i}=\frac{1+i\lambda_{i}+\sigma_{i}}{2},\qquad\bar{h}_{i}=\frac{1+i\lambda_{i}-\sigma_{i}}{2}

(12)

The action of global space-time translation on (
10
) was studied in
Stieberger:2018onx
.

The two dimensional field
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
is the restriction of the three dimensional field
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
to the
u=0
celestial sphere. Correlation function of the three dimensional fields
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
is defined as
Banerjee:2018gce
,

\langle{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\rangle\ =\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{i}\ \omega_{i}^{i\lambda_{i}}e^{-i\epsilon_{i}\omega_{i}u_{i}}\mathcal{A}(\{p_{i}(\omega_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}),\sigma_{i}\})

(13)

Under (Lorentz) global conformal transformation, the L.H.S of (
13
) transforms as,

\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle\ =\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(cz_{j}+d)^{2h_{j}}}\frac{1}{(\bar{c}\bar{z}_{j}+\bar{d})^{2\bar{h}_{j}}}\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}\bigg(\frac{u_{i}}{|cz_{i}+d|^{2}}\ ,\frac{az_{i}+b}{cz_{i}+d}\ ,\frac{\bar{a}\bar{z}_{i}+\bar{b}}{\bar{c}\bar{z}_{i}+\bar{d}}\bigg)\rangle

(14)

Similarly, if we do a global space-time translation under which
u\rightarrow u+a+bz+\bar{b}\bar{z}+cz\bar{z}
, with
(z,\bar{z})
remaining fixed, the correlation function (
13
) is invariant. Let us now discuss the transformation law of the correlation functions under local BMS transformations which are captured by BMS Ward identities.

It is well known that Cachazo-Strominger subleading soft graviton theorem
Cachazo:2014fwa
is equivalent to the (superrotation) conformal Ward identity
Kapec:2016jld
,

\langle T(z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\frac{h_{k}+\frac{1}{2}u_{k}\partial_{u_{k}}}{(z-z_{k})^{2}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}\bigg)\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle

(15)

where the stress tensor
T(z)
can be constructed as the shadow of the subleading soft graviton. In
Kapec:2016jld
the Ward-identity was derived for the two dimensional fields
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
, but the same derivation can be easily repeated for the fields
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
and one obtains (
15
). For details please see the appendix. It is important to note that the stress tensor
T(z)
does not depend on the time coordinate
u
because it is constructed from a soft graviton and in the soft limit the time coordinate decouples.

The singular terms in the OPE between the stress tensor
T(z)
and the primary
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})
are given by
Fotopoulos:2019tpe
,

T(z)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})\sim\frac{h+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u}}{(z-w)^{2}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})+\frac{1}{z-w}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})

(16)

This is consistent with the transformation law (
7
) which one can check by using the standard
2
-D CFT method. The OPE (
16
) gives the commutation relation,

[L_{n},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})]=\bigg[z^{n+1}\partial+(n+1)\left(h+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u}\right)z^{n}\bigg]\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(17)

where the Virasoro generator
L_{n}
is defined in the usual manner as,

L_{n}=\oint_{c_{0}}dzz^{n+1}T(z)

(18)

with
c_{0}
defined as a contour around
z=0
.

Similarly, Weinberg’s soft graviton theorem
Weinberg:1965nx
is equivalent to the supertranslation Ward-identity given by
Strominger:2013jfa
;
He
;
Banerjee:2018fgd
,

\langle P(z)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{z-z_{k}}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle

(19)

Here
P(z)
is the supertranslation current and can be written as,
P(z)=-\lim_{i\lambda\rightarrow 0}i\lambda\bar{\partial}G^{+}_{\Delta=1+i\lambda}
, where
G^{+}_{\Delta}
is the positive helicity graviton primary of weight
\Delta(=h+\bar{h})
. Again,
P(z)
has no
u
dependence.

The singular term in the OPE between
P(z)
and a matter primary
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
is given by,

P(z)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})\sim\frac{1}{z-w}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})

(20)

This OPE is equivalent to the commutation relation,

[P_{a,-1},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})]=z^{a+1}i\partial_{u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(21)

where the supertranslation generator
P_{a,-1}
, defined as
Strominger:2013jfa
,

P_{a,-1}=\oint_{c_{0}}dzz^{a+1}P(z)

(22)

generates the holomorphic supertranslation
u\rightarrow u+\epsilon z^{a}
. The commutation relation (
21
) shows that,

[P_{a,-1},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)]=0,\qquad a>-1

(23)

From the BMS commutation relation (
6
) one can check that the supertranslation generator
P_{a,-1}
has weight
(-a-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. So for
a>-1
the holomorphic weight of
P_{a,-1}
is negative and it annihilates the primary operator
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)
.

BMS Descendants and Their Correlators

4.1
Supertranslation

Let us first consider the (holomorphic) supertranslation descendants.

Let us assume that the standard CFT form for the OPE between the supertranslation current
P(z)
and a matter primary
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
holds, i.e,

\begin{gathered}P(w)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})=\frac{(P_{-1,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})}{w-z}+(P_{-2,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})\\
+(w-z)(P_{-3,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})+(w-z)^{2}(P_{-4,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})+......\end{gathered}

(24)

where the leading term is given by,

(P_{-1,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})=i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(25)

From the conformal transformation laws (
7
) and (
8
) of primary fields, we know that
i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
also transforms like a primary field of weight
(h+1/2,\bar{h}+1/2)
. We denote this field by

\phi_{h+1/2,\bar{h}+1/2}(u,z,\bar{z})=i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(26)

The nonsingular terms of the OPE (
24
) define the (holomorphic) supertranslation descendants
\bigg\{(P_{-n,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u_{0},z_{0},\bar{z}_{0})\bigg\}_{n\geq 2}
which are new local fields created by singular supertranslations of the form,

u\rightarrow u+\frac{\epsilon}{(z-z_{0})^{n-1}}

(27)

The descendants can be defined by the usual contour integral formula,

(P_{-a,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})=\oint_{c_{z}}dw\frac{1}{(w-z)^{a-1}}P(w)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(28)

which follows from (
24
). Here
c_{z}
is a contour around
w=z
. Later in the paper we will explicitly verify the existence of these descendants by taking the leading conformal soft limit of tree-level four graviton scattering amplitude in Einstein theory.

We now need to find out correlation functions with the insertion of the descendants
P_{-a,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}
, i.e, correlators of the form
\langle(P_{-a,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle
. This can be computed in the standard way by using the Ward identity (
19
),

\displaystyle\langle P(w)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w-z_{k}}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{w-z}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle

(29)

and taking the limit
w\rightarrow z
. In this limit we can use the OPE (
24
) and obtain,

\begin{gathered}\hskip-20.0pt\boxed{\langle(P_{-a,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(z_{k}-z)^{a-1}}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle}\\
=\mathcal{P}_{-a,-1}(z)\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle\end{gathered}

(30)

where the differential operator
\mathcal{P}_{-a,-1}(z)
acting on correlation functions of primary operators is defined as,

\mathcal{P}_{-a,-1}(z)=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(z_{k}-z)^{a-1}}i\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}

(31)

The rest of the (holomorphic) supertranslation descendants are of the form

P_{-i_{1},-1}P_{-i_{2},-1}....P_{-i_{n},-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
where
i_{1}\geq i_{2}\geq...\geq i_{n}\geq 1
. The mixed supertranslation descendants of the form
P_{-a,-b}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
, where
a,b>1
, will not appear in the OPE to the first subleading order and so we leave their discussion to future work.

Mellin transform of graviton scattering amplitudes in string theory
Stieberger:2018edy
is well defined without the time coordinate
u
. In this case the correlation function with the insertion of a holomorphic supertranslation descendant is given by a simple change of (
30
),

\begin{gathered}\langle(P_{-a,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(z,\bar{z};\epsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i};\epsilon_{i})\rangle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\epsilon_{k}\widetilde{P}_{k}}{(z_{k}-z)^{a-1}}\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z};\epsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i};\epsilon_{i})\rangle\\
=\mathcal{P}_{-a,-1}(z)\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z};\epsilon)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i};\epsilon_{i})\rangle\\
\end{gathered}

(32)

where
\epsilon=\pm 1
for an outgoing and incoming particle, respectively and

\widetilde{P}_{i}\ \phi_{h_{j},\bar{h}_{j}}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j};\epsilon_{j})=\phi_{h_{j}+1/2,\bar{h}_{j}+1/2}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j};\epsilon_{j})\ \delta_{ij}

(33)

4.2
Superrotation or Virasoro

The discussion of superrotation or Virasoro descendants is identical to that in
2
-D CFT. The correlation function with the insertion of
(L_{-n}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})
is given by,

\langle(L_{-n}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z}))\prod_{i=1}^{p}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{-n}(z)\langle\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})\prod_{i=1}^{p}\phi_{h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(u_{i},z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\rangle

(34)

where

\mathcal{L}_{-n}(z)=-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\bigg((1-n)\frac{h_{i}+\frac{1}{2}u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}}{(z_{i}-z)^{n}}+\frac{1}{(z_{i}-z)^{n-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\bigg)

(35)

This can be obtained by assuming the following OPE between the stress tensor
T(z)
and the primary field
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
,

\begin{gathered}T(z)\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})=\frac{(h+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{u})\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,w,\bar{w})}{z-w}+(L_{-2}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,w,\bar{w})\\
+(z-w)(L_{-3}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,w,\bar{w})+......\end{gathered}

(36)

In the absence of the time coordinate
u
, superrotation transformations act on the primaries
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})
exactly in the same way as local conformal transformations act on Virasoro primaries in
2
-D CFT.

Now, symmetry requires
all
descendants to be present in the OPE and this implies that the
order of the pole
in
z
or
\bar{z}

cannot be bounded from above
because
a,b
can be arbitrarily large positive integers. But, this is practically impossible because in the present situation the
leading
pole can be obtained by Mellin transforming the splitting function in the collinear limit
Pate:2019lpp
;
Fotopoulos:2019vac
;
Fan:2019emx
. So it seems reasonable to assume that the order of the pole in
z
or
\bar{z}
will be bounded from above and therefore, we should impose the condition (
67
) on the primary
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}
.

For the Virasoro generators we have the standard conditions that,

[L_{n},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)]=0,\qquad[\bar{L}_{n},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)]=0,\qquad n>0

(68)

and

[L_{0},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)]=h\ \phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0),\qquad[\bar{L}_{0},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)]=\bar{h}\ \phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)

(69)

Therefore we can now consider the descendants generated by the raising operators only.

8.1
A Hilbert space picture

In the following discussion it will be convenient to assume a Hilbert space picture and define a vacuum
\left|0\right>
by the condition,

P_{-1,-1}\left|0\right>=0

(70)

P_{a,b}\left|0\right>=0,\qquad a>-1\ or\ b>-1\ or\ both\ a,b>-1

(71)

L_{n}\left|0\right>=\bar{L}_{n}\left|0\right>=0,\quad n\geq-1

(72)

We also define the state
\left|h,\bar{h}\right>
as,

\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(0)\left|0\right>

(73)

using the above definitions the BMS-primary state condition can be written as,

P_{a,b}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=0,\qquad a>-1\ or\ b>-1\ or\ both\ a,b>-1

(74)

L_{n}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=\bar{L}_{n}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=0,\quad n\geq 1

(75)

L_{0}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=h\left|h,\bar{h}\right>,\quad\bar{L}_{0}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=\bar{h}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>

(76)

For the restricted class of supertranslation generators
P_{a,b}
with both
(a,b)>-1
, condition (
71
) was also proposed in
Bagchi:2016bcd
.

With this definition of the primary state the BMS descendants are given by,

L_{-n_{1}}L_{-n_{2}}.......L_{-n_{p}}P_{-a_{1},-b_{1}}P_{-a_{2},-b_{2}}.......P_{-a_{q},-b_{q}}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>

(77)

where
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq.......n_{p}>0
and
a_{i}>0,b_{i}>0
. We do not put any order on the
\{a_{i}\}
and
\{b_{i}\}
because the supertranslation generators commute among themselves.

8.2
Primary descendants

Suppose
\left|h,\bar{h}\right>
is a BMS-primary state. Then it is easy to check using the commutation relations that the state
(P_{-1,-1})^{n}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>
, with
n\geq 1
, is also a BMS-primary with weight
(h+n/2,\bar{h}+n/2)
. We denote this state by,

(P_{-1,-1})^{n}\left|h,\bar{h}\right>=\left|h+n/2,\bar{h}+n/2\right>

(78)

8.3
Graviton-Graviton OPE

From now on our discussion will be confined to the OPE of two positive helicity gravitons denoted by
G^{+}_{\Delta}(u,z,\bar{z})
. In our case,
\Delta=1+i\lambda
and so,

h=\frac{3+i\lambda}{2},\quad\bar{h}=\frac{-1+i\lambda}{2},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

(79)

At this point let us note that although the Mellin amplitudes in Einstein gravity are UV divergent, the OPE (
64
) has no singularity as
u_{34}\rightarrow 0
and we can safely restrict the OPE to the celestial sphere at constant
u
, if we wish. From this point of view, the
time coordinate
u
acts as a covariant regulator in the celestial CFT
, which can be set to zero at the end of the calculation. For simplicity, we will do so.

Once we set
u=0
, modulo the singular prefactor, the OPE expansion becomes a Taylor series in
z
and
\bar{z}
around the origin
(z=0,\bar{z}=0)
and we can write the following (
64
),

G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}}(z,\bar{z})G^{+}_{\Delta_{2}}(0)=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\bigg(P_{-1,-1}+\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})\bigg)G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}(0)

(80)

where
G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}}(z,\bar{z})=G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}}(u=0,z,\bar{z})
. Let us now enumerate the possible subleading terms in the OPE at
O(z)
and
O(\bar{z})
. For this we remind ourselves that
P_{-1,-1}
is a
(1/2,1/2)
operator. Taking this into account we get,

1)
O(z)
operators :

L_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\rightarrow(3/2,1/2),\quad P_{-2,-1}\rightarrow(3/2,1/2)

(81)

2)
O(\bar{z})
operators :

\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\rightarrow(1/2,3/2),\quad P_{-1,-2}\rightarrow(1/2,3/2)

(82)

If we keep powers of
u
then there will be an additional operator at
O(u)
given by
(P_{-1,-1})^{2}
, which follows from the fact that
u
has scaling dimension
(-1/2,-1/2)
.

Now generically, at every order, there can be descendants and also
new primaries
. But here we focus on the contribution arising from a specific primary
G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}(0)
.

So let us write the first subleading order in the OPE as,

\begin{gathered}G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}}(z,\bar{z})G^{+}_{\Delta_{2}}(0)=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\bigg(P_{-1,-1}+c_{1}zL_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+c_{2}zP_{-2,-1}\\
+\bar{c}_{1}\bar{z}\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+\bar{c}_{2}\bar{z}P_{-1,-2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{2},\bar{z}^{2},z\bar{z})\bigg)G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}(0)\end{gathered}

(83)

Here
\bar{c}_{i}
is
not
the complex conjugate of
c_{i}
. Also, for simplicity of notation, we have kept the dependence of the OPE coefficients
(c_{i},\bar{c}_{i})
on the scaling dimensions of primary operators implicit.

We will now compute the coefficients
(c_{i},\bar{c}_{i})
from the fact that both sides of the OPE must transform in the same way under BMS transformation. In order to do this it is more convenient to write the OPE as,

\begin{gathered}G_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z,\bar{z})\left|h_{2},\bar{h}_{2}\right>=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\bigg(P_{-1,-1}+c_{1}zL_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+c_{2}zP_{-2,-1}\\
+\bar{c}_{1}\bar{z}\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+\bar{c}_{2}\bar{z}P_{-1,-2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{2},\bar{z}^{2},z\bar{z})\bigg)\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>\end{gathered}

(84)

where,

h_{1,2,3}=\frac{3+i\lambda_{1,2,3}}{2},\quad\bar{h}_{1,2,3}=\frac{-1+i\lambda_{1,2,3}}{2},\qquad\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

(85)

The BMS generators act on the operators as,

[L_{n},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})]=\bigg[z^{n+1}\partial+(n+1)hz^{n}\bigg]\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(86)

[\bar{L}_{n},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})]=\bigg[\bar{z}^{n+1}\bar{\partial}+(n+1)\bar{h}\bar{z}^{n}\bigg]\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})

(87)

[P_{a,b},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})]=z^{a+1}\bar{z}^{b+1}\phi_{h+1/2,\bar{h}+1/2}(z,\bar{z})=z^{a+1}\bar{z}^{b+1}(P_{-1,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(z,\bar{z})

(88)

The first two relations are obtained by setting
u=0
in commutation relations (
17
). The last commutator is obtained from,

[P_{a,b},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})]=z^{a+1}\bar{z}^{b+1}i\partial_{u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})

(89)

by setting
u=0
and recognizing that
i\partial_{u}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(u,z,\bar{z})
is the BMS - primary (descendant)
(P_{-1,-1}\phi_{h,\bar{h}})(u,z,\bar{z})
with dimension
(h+1/2,\bar{h}+1/2)
.

With this information one can readily compute the OPE coefficients, in the standard way, by applying the lowering operators to both sides of (
84
). Let us now state the equations for the OPE coefficients obtained in this way,

1) Applying
P_{0,-1}
we get,

c_{1}B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})=B(i\lambda_{1}+1,i\lambda_{2})\implies c_{1}=\frac{i\lambda_{1}}{i\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}}

(90)

2) Applying
P_{-1,0}
we get,

\bar{c}_{1}B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})=B(i\lambda_{1}+1,i\lambda_{2})\implies\bar{c}_{1}=\frac{i\lambda_{1}}{i\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}}

(91)

3) Applying
L_{1}
we get,

(2h_{3}+1)c_{1}+2c_{2}=2h_{1}-1\implies c_{2}=\frac{i\lambda_{2}-i\lambda_{1}}{i\lambda_{2}+i\lambda_{1}}

(92)

4) Applying
\bar{L}_{1}
we get,

(2\bar{h}_{3}+1)\bar{c}_{1}+2\bar{c}_{2}=2\bar{h}_{1}+1\implies\bar{c}_{2}=0

(93)

This matches exactly with the OPE coefficients (
64
), obtained from graviton scattering amplitude, with the replacement
\lambda_{3}\rightarrow\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{4}\rightarrow\lambda_{2}
. We would like to point out that since the descendants
P_{-2,-1}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
and
P_{-1,-2}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
appear in the OPE (
84
), the above equations for the OPE coefficients probe the BMS algebra beyond the Poincaré subalgebra.

Before we conclude, we would like to point out that to arrive at the equations for the OPE coefficients we need commutators of the form
[\bar{L}_{1},P_{-2,-1}]\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>=P_{-2,0}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
, which by the definition (
74
) of a primary state is equal to zero. The generator
P_{-2,0}
generates supertranslation of the mixed type,
u\rightarrow u+\epsilon\frac{\bar{z}}{z}
. So we can see that the condition that generators of the mixed type should
also
annihilate a primary state is necessary to obtain the correct OPE coefficients starting from the BMS algebra, at least to the order we are working.

8.4
Virasoro Representations

Since Virasoro algebra is a subalgebra of the BMS algebra, one should be able to decompose a BMS representation into irreducible Virasoro representations. This is useful because in this way one can use the known results from the (highest-weight) Virasoro representation theory to compute some of the OPE coefficients. At the first subleading order this can be done in the following way.

Let us write the OPE at the first subleading order as,

\begin{gathered}G_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z,\bar{z})\left|h_{2},\bar{h}_{2}\right>=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\bigg(P_{-1,-1}+c_{1}zL_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+c_{2}zP_{-2,-1}\\
+\bar{c}_{1}\bar{z}\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+\mathcal{O}(z^{2},\bar{z}^{2},z\bar{z})\bigg)\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>\end{gathered}

(94)

where we have set
\bar{c}_{2}=0
following (
93
).

Among these states, the state
\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
is the antiholomorphic Virasoro descendant of the BMS primary
P_{-1,-1}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
. We have two more states, given by
L_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
and
P_{-2,-1}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
, one of which is a Virasoro descendant and the other one is a supertranslation descendant. So let us consider the state,

\boxed{\left|\psi\right>=\bigg(P_{-2,-1}-\frac{2}{2h_{3}+1}L_{-1}P_{-1,-1}\bigg)\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>}

(95)

The corresponding field can be written as,

\psi(z,\bar{z})=(P_{-2,-1}G_{h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}})(z,\bar{z})-\frac{2}{2h_{3}+1}(L_{-1}P_{-1,-1}G_{h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}})(z,\bar{z})

(96)

where the correlation function with the insertion of
(P_{-2,-1}G_{h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}})(z,\bar{z})
is given by (
32
). Now one can check using the BMS algebra and the definition of the BMS primary state
\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>
that
\left|\psi\right>
is a
Virasoro primary
but,
not
a BMS primary. For example, one can check that

P_{0,-1}\left|\psi\right>\propto P_{-1,-1}^{2}\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>\neq 0

(97)

and so on. Now using the
Virasoro primary

\left|\psi\right>
we can rewrite the OPE (
94
) as,

\begin{gathered}G_{h_{1},\bar{h}_{1}}(z,\bar{z})\left|h_{2},\bar{h}_{2}\right>=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\Bigg(P_{-1,-1}+c_{1}^{\prime}zL_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+\bar{c}_{1}\bar{z}\bar{L}_{-1}P_{-1,-1}+...\Bigg)\left|h_{3},\bar{h}_{3}\right>\\
-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\bar{z}c_{2}\left|\psi\right>+......\\
=-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})\frac{\bar{z}}{z}\bigg(1+c_{1}^{\prime}zL_{-1}+\bar{c}_{1}\bar{z}\bar{L}_{-1}+...\bigg)\left|h_{3}+1/2,\bar{h}_{3}+1/2\right>\\
-B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})c_{2}\ \bar{z}\left|\psi\right>+......\end{gathered}

(98)

where,

c_{1}^{\prime}=c_{1}+\frac{2}{2h_{3}+1}c_{2}

(99)

The OPE (
98
) now has the familiar structure of OPE in
2
-D CFT. The first line consists of the Virasoro primary
\left|h_{3}+1/2,\bar{h}_{3}+1/2\right>
and its level
1
descendants. In the second line we have a new Virasoro primary
\left|\psi\right>
with the (Virasoro) structure constant
B(i\lambda_{1},i\lambda_{2})c_{2}
. The Virasoro structure constant
B(i\lambda_{2},i\lambda_{2})c_{2}
cannot be determined by Virasoro algebra alone but, the coefficients
c_{1}^{\prime}
and
\bar{c}_{1}
are determined by Virasoro algebra. They are given by the known formulas,

c_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{h_{3}^{\prime}+h_{1}-h_{2}}{2h_{3}^{\prime}},\qquad h_{3}^{\prime}=h_{3}+\frac{1}{2}

(100)

\bar{c}_{1}=\frac{\bar{h}_{3}^{\prime}+\bar{h}_{1}-\bar{h}_{2}}{2\bar{h}_{3}^{\prime}},\qquad\bar{h}_{3}^{\prime}=\bar{h}_{3}+\frac{1}{2}

(101)

Using the values (
85
) for the scaling dimensions we get, from (
100
) and
(
101
)
, that

c_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{2+i\lambda_{1}}{4+i(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}

(102)

and

\bar{c}_{1}=\frac{i\lambda_{1}}{i\lambda_{1}+i\lambda_{2}}

(103)

We can see that the value of
\bar{c}_{1}
obtained in this way using Virasoro algebra matches with the value (
91
) obtained using translational invariance of the OPE. Similarly, the value of
c_{1}^{\prime}
also matches with (
99
) once we substitute the values (
90
) and (
92
) of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, obtained using BMS algebra. This matching is a check of the overall consistency of the procedure.

9
Future Directions

The problem of extracting the celestial OPE from flat space scattering amplitudes consists of two parts. The first part is the (holomorphic or antiholomorphic) collinear expansion including subleading terms and the second part is the determination of the celestial correlation functions with the insertion of BMS descendants. The correlation functions involving superrotation or Virasoro descendants are well known from the work of Belavin-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov on two dimensional CFTs. The new objects are the supertranslation descendants. Among these, the simplest ones are the descendants
\{P_{-a,-1}\phi\ ,a>1\}
created by singular (anti) holomorphic supertranslations. These are captured in a straightforward manner by supertranslation Ward-identity (
19
) following from Weinberg’s soft graviton theorem. The correlation functions with the insertion of (anti) holomorphic supertranslation descendants follow from this Ward-identity and is given by (
30
) or (
32
). But, there are also descendants of the form
\{P_{-a,-b}\phi,a>1,b>1\}
created by supertranslations, which are neither purely holomorphic nor antiholomorphic. For example, if we want to go to higher order in the OPE expansion (
83
), then at
O(z\bar{z})
we encounter the descendant
P_{-2,-2}G^{+}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}(0)
. Unless we know the Mellin amplitude with the insertion of this descendant, it will not be possible to extract the OPE at higher order. Although the Ward-identity (
19
) captures all the supertranslation descendants
Strominger:2013jfa
- not just holomorphic - it may require a more involved procedure to determine the correlation function of a general supertranslation descendant.

Another important point is that the BMS algebra may have (field-dependent) central extension
Barnich:2017ubf
;
Distler:2018rwu
. In this paper the central extension does not play any role because in the first subleading order the Virasoro descendants
\{L_{-n}\phi,n>1\}
do not appear, although they appear in the higher order of the OPE. The values of the OPE coefficients should depend on the central charge and perhaps one can determine the central charge by demanding that the OPE coefficients determined from the BMS algebra match with those obtained from the subleading terms in the collinear expansion of the Mellin amplitude. We hope to return to these problems in future.

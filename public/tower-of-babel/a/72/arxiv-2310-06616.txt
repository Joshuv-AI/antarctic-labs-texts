Local index theory and the Riemann-Roch-Grothendieck theorem for complex flat vector bundles II
Man-Ho Ho

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the real part of the Riemann–Roch–Grothendieck theorem for complex flat vector bundles at the differential form level.

1.
Introduction

Let
\pi:X\to B
be a submersion with closed fibers
Z
, equipped with a Riemannian structure
\bm{\pi}=(T^{H}X,g^{T^{V}X})
. The Riemann–Roch–Grothendieck (RRG) theorem for complex flat vector bundles
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
states that for any complex flat vector bundle
(F,\nabla^{F})
over
X
,

\CCS(H(Z,F|_{Z}),\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z})})=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(T^{V}X)\cup\CCS(F,\nabla^{F})

(1.1)

in
H^{\odd}(B;\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})
, where
\CCS(F,\nabla^{F})
and
(H(Z,F|_{Z}),\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z})})
are the Cheeger–Chern–Simons class and the cohomology bundle of
(F,\nabla^{F})
, respectively.

Bismut–Lott prove a refinement of the imaginary part of (
1.1
) at the differential form level
[
4
, Theorem 3.23]
by constructing the analytic torsion form
T

[
4
, Definition 3.22]
. Bismut then proves the real part of (
1.1
), namely,

\re(\CCS(H(Z,F|_{Z}),\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z})}))=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(T^{V}X)\cup\re(\CCS(F,\nabla^{F}))

(1.2)

in
H^{\odd}(B;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q})
under the assumption that the fibers are fiberwise orientable
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
. Ma–Zhang prove (
1.1
) by giving another proof of the imaginary part of (
1.1
) and prove (
1.2
) in full generality.

The strategy of the proof of (
1.2
) by Ma–Zhang is roughly as follows. They first prove that

\begin{split}&\re(\CCS(H(Z,F|_{Z}),\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z})}))-\re(\CCS(H(Z,\mathbb{C}^{\ell}|_{Z})))\\
&=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(T^{V}X)\cup\re(\CCS(F,\nabla^{F}))\end{split}

(1.3)

in
H^{\odd}(B;\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Q})

[
11
, (3.98)]
, where
\ell=\rk(F)
. Then they show that
[
11
, p.613-614]

\re(\CCS(H(Z,\mathbb{C}^{\ell}|_{Z})))=0

(1.4)

by considering the parity of
\dim(Z)
separately. For
\dim(Z)
odd, they prove (
1.4
) by an application of Poincaré duality; for
\dim(Z)
even, they prove (
1.4
) by applying a result of Bismut
[
3
, Theorem 3.12]
, which is essentially an application of the special case of (
1.2
) proved by Bismut.

The main result of this paper is a proof of (
1.2
) at the differential form level. This gives a new proof of (
1.2
).

Theorem 1
.

(
=
Theorem
2
)
Let
\pi:X\to B
be a submersion with closed fibers
Z
, equipped with a Riemannian structure
\bm{\pi}
. For any complex flat vector bundle
(F,\nabla^{F})
of rank
\ell
over
X
, there exist sufficiently large
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
, an isometric isomorphism
\alpha:(kF,kg^{F})\to(\mathbb{C}^{k\ell},g^{k\ell})
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded isometric isomorphism

h:(kH(Z,F|_{Z})\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}^{N},kg^{H(Z,F|_{Z})}\oplus{\widehat{g}}^{N})\to(\mathbb{C}^{km}\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}^{N},g^{km}\oplus{\widehat{g}}^{N})

such that

\CS\big(k\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z}),u}\oplus{\widehat{d}}^{N},h^{*}(d^{km}\oplus{\widehat{d}}^{N})\big)\equiv\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(\nabla^{T^{V}X})\wedge\CS(k\nabla^{F,u},\alpha^{*}d^{k\ell}),

(1.5)

where
m=\rk(H(Z,F|_{Z}))
.

In the final step of the proof of Theorem
1
, we make use of a result by Bismut
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
(see also Zhang
[
13
, Proposition 2.3]
).

Although the left-hand side of (
1.5
) does not look like a differential form representative of
\re(\CCS(H(Z,F|_{Z}),\nabla^{H(Z,F|_{Z})}))
, in Theorem
3
we justify the claim that (
1.5
) is a refinement of (
1.2
) at the differential form level. On the other hand, by taking
(F,\nabla^{F})
to be the trivial bundle
(\mathbb{C}^{\ell},d^{\ell})
in Theorem
1
and noting that a differential form representative of
\CCS(\mathbb{C}^{\ell},d^{\ell})
is given by an odd Chern character form, which implies
\CCS(\mathbb{C}^{\ell},d^{\ell})=0
in
H^{\odd}(X;\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Q})
, we obtain a unified proof of (
1.4
) at the differential form level regardless of the parity of
\dim(Z)
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is similar to that of
[
8
, Theorem 1]
in spirit. However, there are two notable differences. The first difference is the use of a perturbed generalized twisted de Rham operator in the proof of Theorem
1
, in contrast to the perturbed twisted spin
c
Dirac operator used in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
. The former allows us to treat the even and odd dimensional fiber cases simultaneously, while the latter only allows us to treat the even dimensional fiber case. The second difference is a refinement of the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, namely, we take a more suitable pair of unitary connections on the “index bundle” over
B\times[0,1]
when defining a certain Chern–Simons form. This is the main reason why we can directly consider complex flat vector bundles instead of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded ones of virtual rank zero, as we did in
[
8
]
.

While Theorem
1
is a generalization of
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, we would like to emphasize that
[
8
, Theorem 1]
is much weaker than Theorem
1
. First,
[
8
, Theorem 1]
only holds for complex flat vector bundles that are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded and have virtual rank zero. Second, and more importantly, we have come to realize the difficulty of proving (
1.4
) independently when we try to obtain (
1.2
) from (
1.3
). Although (
1.4
) is now a consequence of Theorem
1
, we still regard proving (
1.4
) independently as a difficult problem, and the proof of (
1.4
) given by Ma–Zhang and Bismut is more intricate than we expect.

Background Material

2.1.
Notations and conventions

In this subsection, we fix the notations and conventions used in this paper.

In this paper
X
and
B
are closed manifolds and
I
is the closed interval
[0,1]
. Given a manifold
X
, define
{\widetilde{X}}=X\times I
. Given
t\in I
, define a map
i_{X,t}:X\to{\widetilde{X}}
by
i_{X,t}(x)=(x,t)
. Denote by
p_{X}:{\widetilde{X}}\to X
the standard projection map. For
k\geq 0
, denote by
{}^{k}{Q}(X,\mathbb{C})
the set of all complex-valued closed
k
-forms on
X
with periods in
\mathbb{Q}
, and write
{}^{k}{Q}(X)
for
{}^{k}{Q}(X,\mathbb{R})
. For any differential forms
\omega
and
\eta
, we write
\omega\equiv\eta
if
\omega-\eta\in\im(d)
.

To shorten the notations and terminologies, we will work with the category
\bun_{\nabla}(X)
whose objects
\bm{\mathrm{E}}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
, called geometric bundles, are Hermitian bundles and unitary connections and whose morphisms
\alpha:\bm{\mathrm{E}}\to\bm{\mathrm{F}}
are isometric isomorphisms
\alpha:(E,g^{E})\to(F,g^{F})
, i.e.
g^{E}=\alpha^{*}g^{F}
.

Here are some terminologies and notations regarding geometric bundles.

•

If
\alpha:\bm{\mathrm{E}}\to\bm{\mathrm{F}}
is a morphism, then
\alpha^{*}\nabla^{F}:=\alpha^{-1}\circ\nabla^{F}\circ\alpha
is a unitary connection on
(E,g^{E})
.

•

A geometric bundle
\bm{\mathrm{E}}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
is said to be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded if
E\to X
,
g^{E}
and
\nabla^{E}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded.

•

For a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle
\bm{\mathrm{E}}
, denote by
\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{\op}
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle whose
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded grading is opposite to that of
\bm{\mathrm{E}}
.

•

A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle
\bm{\mathrm{E}}
is said to be balanced if
E^{+}=E^{-}
,
g^{E,+}=g^{E,-}
and
\nabla^{E,+}=\nabla^{E,-}
.

•

For any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, write
\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell}=(\mathbb{C}^{\ell},g^{\ell},d^{\ell})
for the geometric bundle that consists of the trivial bundle of rank
\ell
with the standard Hermitian metric and the standard trivial connection.

•

For a geometric bundle
\bm{\mathrm{E}}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E})
, write
{\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{E}}}}=({\widehat{E}},g^{{\widehat{E}}},\nabla^{{\widehat{E}}})
for the balanced
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle defined by
{\widehat{E}}^{\pm}=E
, and similarly for
g^{{\widehat{E}}}
and
\nabla^{{\widehat{E}}}
. In the case that
\bm{\mathrm{E}}=\bm{\mathrm{C}}^{\ell}
, we write
{\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{C}}}}^{\ell}=({\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}^{\ell},{\widehat{g}}^{\ell},{\widehat{d}}^{\ell})
for
{\widehat{\bm{\mathrm{E}}}}
.

Let
(E,g^{E})
be a Hermitian bundle with a connection
\nabla^{E}
on
E\to X
that is not assumed to be unitary with respect to
g^{E}
. Define

\omega(E,g^{E}):=(g^{E})^{-1}(\nabla^{E}g^{E})\in{}^{1}(X,\End(E)).

(2.1)

By
[
5
, p.62]
, the connection
\nabla^{E,u}
on
E\to X
defined

\nabla^{E,u}=\nabla^{E}+\frac{1}{2}\omega(E,g^{E})

(2.2)

is unitary with respect to
g^{E}
. Write
\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{u}
for
(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E,u})
.

In this paper, we will constantly make use of Chern character form and Chern–Simons form. We refer the readers to
[
9
, §2.2]
for the details of the constructions and properties of these forms.

2.2.
Local index theory for perturbed generalized twisted de Rham operators

In this subsection, we review the local family index theorem for perturbed generalized twisted de Rham operators without the kernel bundle assumption. We refer to
[
1
, Chapter 10]
,
[
4
, §III]
and
[
6
, §7.12]
for the details.

Let
\pi:X\to B
be a submersion with closed fibers
Z
of dimension
n
. Denote by
T^{V}X\to X
the vertical tangent bundle. Recall from
[
6
, p.918]
that a Riemannian structure
\bm{\pi}=(T^{H}X,g^{T^{V}X})
on
\pi:X\to B
consists of a horizontal distribution
T^{H}X\to X
, i.e.
TX=T^{V}X\oplus T^{H}X
, and a metric
g^{T^{V}X}
on
T^{V}X\to X
. Put a Riemannian metric
g^{TB}
on
TB\to B
. Define a metric
g^{TX}
on
TX\to X
by

g^{TX}=g^{T^{V}X}\oplus\pi^{*}g^{TB}.

Denote by
\nabla^{TX}
and
\nabla^{TB}
the Levi-Civita connections on
TX\to X
and
TB\to X
associated to
g^{TX}
and
g^{TB}
, respectively. Denote by
P^{T^{V}X}:TX\to T^{V}X
the projection map. Then
\nabla^{T^{V}X}:=P^{T^{V}X}\circ\nabla^{TX}\circ P^{T^{V}X}
is a metric connection on the Riemannian bundle
(T^{V}X,g^{T^{V}X})
.

Define a connection
{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{TX}
on
TX\to X
by

{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{TX}=\nabla^{T^{V}X}\oplus\pi^{*}\nabla^{TB}.

Let
S=\nabla^{TX}-{\widetilde{\nabla}}^{TX}\in{}^{1}(X,\End(TX))
. By
[
2
, Theorem 1.9]
the
(3,0)
tensor
g^{TX}(S(\cdot)\cdot,\cdot)
depends only on the Riemannian structure
\bm{\pi}=(T^{H}X,g^{T^{V}X})
. Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame for
T^{V}X\to X
. For any
U\in\Gamma(B,TB)
, denote by
U^{H}\in\Gamma(X,T^{H}X)
its horizontal lift. Define a horizontal one-form
k
on
X
by

k(U^{H})=-\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}g^{TX}(S(e_{k})e_{k},U^{H}).

(2.3)

For any
U,V\in\Gamma(B,TB)
,

T(U,V):=-P^{T^{V}X}[U^{H},V^{H}]

(2.4)

is a horizontal two-form with values in
T^{V}X
, and is called the curvature of
\pi:X\to B
.

The complexified exterior bundle
\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\to X
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading and is a Clifford module with Clifford multiplication
c(Y)=\varepsilon(Y)-i(Y)
, where
Y\in\Gamma(X,T^{V}X)
, and
\varepsilon
and
i
are the exterior and interior multiplications, respectively. Denote by
g^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}}
and
\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}}
the extensions of
g^{T^{V}X}
and
\nabla^{T^{V}X}
to
\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\to X
, respectively.

Let
\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{u}=(E,g^{E},\nabla^{E,u})
. The generalized twisted de Rham operator
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E}
associated to
\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{u}
and
\bm{\pi}
is defined to be

\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E}=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}c(e_{k})\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E,u}_{e_{k}},

where
\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E,u}
is the tensor product of
\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}}
and
\nabla^{E,u}
. It acts on
\Gamma(X,\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E)
and is odd self-adjoint, i.e.
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E}_{-}=(\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E}_{+})^{*}
. Set
{\widehat{c}}(Y)=\varepsilon(Y)+i(Y)
. Define a map
V:\Gamma(X,\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E)\to\Gamma(X,\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E)
by

V=-\frac{1}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}{\widehat{c}}(e_{k})\omega(E,g^{E})(e_{k}).

(2.5)

Note that
V
is odd self-adjoint and anti-commutes with the
c(X)
’s. Thus

\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}:=\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E}+V

is also odd self-adjoint.

Define an infinite rank
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded complex vector bundle
\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\to B
whose fiber over
b\in B
is

(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)_{b}=\Gamma(Z_{b},(\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E)|_{Z_{b}}).

Note that
\Omega(X,E)\cong\Omega(B,\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)
. Denote by
\ast
the fiberwise Hodge star operator associated to
g^{T^{V}X}
, and extend it from
\Gamma(X,\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*})
to
\Gamma(X,\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E)\cong\Gamma(B,\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)
. Define an
L^{2}
-metric on
\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\to B
by

g^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E}(s_{1},s_{2})(b)=\intop\nolimits_{Z_{b}}g^{E}(s_{1}(b)\wedge\ast s_{2}(b)).

The connection on
\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\to B
defined by

\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E}_{U}s=\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes E}_{U^{H}}s,

where
s\in\Gamma(B,\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)
and
U\in\Gamma(B,TB)
, is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded. Then

\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u}:=\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E}+\frac{1}{2}k,

where
k
is given by (
2.3
), is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded unitary connection on the Hermitian bundle
(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,g^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E})
. Write
\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{u}=(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,g^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E},\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u})
.

Define the rescaled Bismut superconnection
\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\to B
by

\mathbb{B}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}=\sqrt{t}\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}+\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u}-\frac{c(T)}{4\sqrt{t}},

where
T
is given by (
2.4
). By
[
4
, (3.76)]
, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\ch(\mathbb{B}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t})=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(\nabla^{T^{V}X})\wedge\ch(\nabla^{E,u}).

(2.6)

Remark 1
.

For any given complex flat vector bundle
(F,\nabla^{F})
,
[
4
, (3.76)]
states that

\lim_{t\to 0}\ch(\mathbb{C}_{t})=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(\nabla^{T^{V}X})\wedge\ch(\nabla^{F,u}),

where
\mathbb{C}_{t}
is the rescaled superconnection defined by
[
4
, (3.50)]
. Under our notations,
[
4
, (3.59)]
states that

\mathbb{C}_{4t}=\bigg(\sqrt{t}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}c(e_{k})\nabla^{\Lambda(T^{V}X)^{*}\otimes F,u}_{e_{k}}+\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}F,u}-\frac{c(T)}{4\sqrt{t}}\bigg)-\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}{\widehat{c}}(e_{k})\omega(F,g^{F})(e_{k}).

(2.7)

The right-hand side of (
2.7
) is exactly
\mathbb{B}^{\Lambda,F;V}_{t}
. Thus (
2.6
) holds for complex flat vector bundles. Since the proof of
[
4
, (3.76)]
does not depend on the flatness of
\nabla^{F}
, (
2.6
) holds.

By applying
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
to
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
, Miščenko–Fomenko
[
12
, p.96-97]
(see also
[
6
, Lemma 7.13]
) prove that there exist finite rank subbundles

and complementary closed subbundles

of

such that

(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)^{+}=K^{+}\oplus L^{+},\qquad(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)^{-}=K^{-}\oplus L^{-},

(2.8)

\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}:(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)^{+}\to(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)^{-}
is block diagonal as a map with respect to (
2.8
), and
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}|_{K^{+}}:K^{+}\to K^{-}
is a smooth bundle isomorphism.

Given
L^{\pm}\to B
satisfying the above conditions, we say the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded complex vector bundle
L\to B
, defined by
L=L^{+}\oplus L^{-}
, satisfies the MF property for
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
. If
L\to B
satisfies the MF property for
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
, then the analytic index of
[E]\in K(X)
associated to
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
is defined to be

\ind^{a,\Lambda}([E])=[L^{+}]-[L^{-}]\in K(B).

It is proved in
[
12
, p.96-97]
that
\ind^{a,\Lambda}([E])
is independent of the choice of
L\to B
satisfying the MF property for
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
.

Remark 2
.

If
L_{0}\to B
and
L_{1}\to B
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded complex vector bundles satisfying the MF property for
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}
, it follows from above and
[
1
, p.289]
that there exist complex vector bundles

and

L_{0}\oplus{\widehat{V}}_{0}\cong L_{1}\oplus{\widehat{V}}_{1}.

Let
g^{L}
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hermitian metric on
L\to B
inherited from
g^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E}
. Denote by
P:\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\to L
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded projection map with respect to (
2.8
). Then

\nabla^{L}:=P\circ\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u}\circ P

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded unitary connection on the Hermitian bundle
(L,g^{L})
. Write

\bm{\mathrm{L}}=(L,g^{L},\nabla^{L}).

Henceforth, whenever
\bm{\mathrm{L}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded geometric bundle for which

satisfies the MF property for
\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}

and

are obtained as above unless otherwise specified.

Let
i_{-}:L^{-}\to(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E)^{-}
be the inclusion map. For any

, define a map
{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}(z):(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\oplus L^{\op})^{+}\to(\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E\oplus L^{\op})^{-}

{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}&zi_{-}\\

Note that
{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}(z)
is invertible for any

(cf.
[
6

). Define a map

{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}(z):=\begin{pmatrix}0&({\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}(z))^{*}\\
{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}_{+}(z)&0\end{pmatrix}.

Choose and fix

and a smooth increasing function

. Define a rescaled Bismut superconnection on

{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}=\sqrt{t}{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}(\alpha(t))+\big(\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u}\oplus\nabla^{L,\op}\big)-\frac{c(T)}{4\sqrt{t}}.

(2.9)

Since
{\widetilde{\mathsf{D}}}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}(\alpha(t))
is invertible for any

\lim_{t\to\infty}\ch({\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t})=0.

(2.10)

On the other hand, for

{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}=\bigg(\sqrt{t}\mathsf{D}^{\Lambda\otimes E;V}+\nabla^{\pi^{\Lambda}_{*}E,u}-\frac{c(T)}{4\sqrt{t}}\bigg)\oplus\nabla^{L,\op}=\mathbb{B}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}\oplus\nabla^{L,\op}.

(2.11)

By (
2.6
), we have

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 0}\ch({\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t})&=\lim_{t\to 0}\ch(\mathbb{B}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t})-\ch(\nabla^{L})\\
&=\intop\nolimits_{X/B}e(\nabla^{T^{V}X})\wedge\ch(\nabla^{E,u})-\ch(\nabla^{L}).\end{split}

(2.12)

The Bismut–Cheeger eta form associated to
{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}
is defined to be

{\widehat{\eta}}^{\Lambda}(\bm{\mathrm{E}}^{u},\bm{\pi},\bm{\mathrm{L}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\intop\nolimits^{\infty}_{0}\str\bigg(\frac{d{\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t}}{dt}e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi i}({\widehat{\mathbb{B}}}^{\Lambda,E;V}_{t})^{2}}\bigg)dt.

(2.13)

By (
2.10
) and (

), the local family index theorem for

3.
Main Results

We prove the main results in this section. We refer the readers to
[
4
, §III]
for the details of the local index theory for twisted de Rham operators, and to
[
8
, §2.2 and §2.3]
for a quick summary. In this section, we will repeatedly make use of the following facts.

•

For any given complex vector bundle
E\to X
with a connection
\nabla^{E}
satisfying
\ch(\nabla^{E})=\rk(E)
, there exists a (sufficiently large)
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
kE\cong k\mathbb{C}^{\rk(E)}
as complex vector bundles (cf.
[
8
, Remark 1]
). In particular, this applies to any complex flat vector bundle.

•

Let
\bm{\mathrm{E}}
and
\bm{\mathrm{F}}
be geometric bundles. If there exists a bundle isomorphism
\alpha:E\to F
, there always exists an isometric isomorphism, still denoted by
\alpha:(E,g^{E})\to(F,g^{F})
, that is uniquely determined by
g^{E}
and
g^{F}

[
9
, (2) of Remark 2.1]
. Thus
\alpha:\bm{\mathrm{E}}\to\bm{\mathrm{F}}
is a morphism.

3.1.

1
.

. Given a complex flat vector bundle

over

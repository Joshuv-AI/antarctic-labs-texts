Value sets of non-permutation polynomials over the residue class rings of integers
Shikui Shang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the value sets of non-permutation polynomial functions over the residue class ring ℤ / m ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}} . When m = p r m=p^{r} is a power of some prime p p , an upper bound is given for the size of the value set of a polynomial function which is not a permutation. We also show that this upper bound can be achieved by some integral polynomials. Finally, we generalize the results for any positive integer m m with known prime decomposition.



Keywords and phrases:
Polynomial functions; Value set;

1
Introduction

Assume that
R
is a unital commutative ring. Let

f=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}X^{i}\in R[X],\ a_{i}\in R

be a polynomial over
R
with
\deg f=d
.
f
induces a function

\Phi_{f}:R\rightarrow R,a\mapsto f(a)
. In general,
\Phi_{f}
is neither injective nor surjective. Set
V(f)=\text{Im}(\Phi_{f})
is the value set of
f
on
R
. Then,
\Phi_{f}
is surjective if and only if
V(f)=R
.

If
\Phi_{f}
is a permutation over
R
, i.e.,
\Phi_{f}
is both injective and surjective,
f
is a called a permutation polynomial. We see that
V(f)=R
is a necessary condition for that
f
is a permutation polynomial. When
R
is finite, it is also sufficient.

The most famous finite commutative rings are finite fields, of which the cardinalities are powers of primes. The value set problems for polynomial functions over finite fields are classic topics studied by many papers(e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
13
]
). Besides them, there are several results on the upper bounds of
|V(f)|
for a non-permutation polynomial
f
obtained by Wan and his coauthors in
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
. All
of these results relate
|V(f)|
to the degree
d
of
f
. For example, Wan proved that

|V(f)|\leq q-\lceil\frac{q-1}{d}\rceil.

in
[
18
]
.

In contrast to finite fields, the study on the value set of polynomial functions over other finite commutative rings are few. In
[
1
]
, the value sets of Dickson Polynomials over Galois rings were studied.

The residue class rings of integers are also elementary. Some topics on polynomial functions over residue class rings were studied in
[
2
]
and
[
11
]
. Specifically, the papers such as
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
17
]
focus on counting the number of polynomial functions.

In this paper, we study the upper bound of the sizes of value sets for non-permutation polynomials over the residue class rings
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
. For convenience, we always consider integral polynomials in
{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
. Take a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
. For any
m\geq 1
, the polynomial
(f\bmod m)\in({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})[X]
induces a function on
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
. Set

N(f,m)=\#V(f\bmod m)

is the size of the value set of
(f\bmod\ m)
. Then,

(f\bmod\ m)
is a permutation if and only if
N(f,m)=m
.

If
f(\bmod\ m)
is not a permutation, then
N(f,m)<m
. Define

M(m)=\max\{N(f,m)\ |\ (f\bmod\ m)\text{ is not a permutation.}\}

is the upper bound of the sizes of value sets for non-permutation polynomials over
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
.

When
m=p^{r}
is a power of a prime
p
, we can use the
p
-adic lifting method to compute the precise value of
M(p^{r})
. That is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1

For a prime
p
and
r\geq 1
,

M(p^{r})=\begin{cases}p-1,&r=1\\
p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1),&r>1\end{cases}.

This result tell us that even without the restriction on the degree of polynomials, the value sets of polynomial functions over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
are far from being arbitrary. When
r\geq 2
, the gap of the sizes of the value sets between permutation polynomials and non-permutation polynomials is
p^{r-1}-p^{r-2}
at least.

Furthermore, we give the value of
M(m)
for a general positive integer
m
with known prime decomposition using Chinese remainder theorem in the last section.

2
Preliminaries

Consider an integral polynomial
f(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
and an integer
m\geq 1
. An important property of
f
is that for any
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
a\equiv b(\bmod\ m)
implies
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ m)
.

Let
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}X^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
of degree
d
.
Set

f^{(1)}(X)=f^{\prime}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}ia_{i}X^{i-1}

is the (formal) derivation of
f(X)
.
Inductively, let

f^{(n+1)}(X)=(f^{(n)}(X))^{\prime}.

There exists the expansion formula of Taylor

f(X+Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{f^{(i)}(X)}{i!}Y^{i}.

(2.1)

Notice that for any
i\geq 0,a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\frac{f^{(i)}(a)}{i!}
is always an integer.

Applying Hensel’s Lemma in
p
-adic analysis, we have the following well-known theorem(Theorem 123 in
[
9
]
)

Theorem 2.1

(The criterion for permutation polynomial functions)For a prime
p
, an integer
r\geq 2
and a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
,
f
is a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if the following tow conditions hold:

(1)
(f\bmod\ p)
is a a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
;

(2)
f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
\Box

Corollary 2.1

When
r\geq 2
,
f
is a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if
f
is a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
.
\Box

Remark.
In
[
16
]
, R. Rivest also gave a more concrete criterion for permutation polynomial functions when
p=2
.
A polynomial
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}X^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
is a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
for
r\geq 2
if and only if the following
three linear identities over
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
hold

\displaystyle a_{1}\equiv 1(\bmod\ 2),

\displaystyle a_{2}+a_{4}+a_{6}+\cdots\equiv 0(\bmod\ 2),

\displaystyle a_{3}+a_{5}+a_{7}+\cdots\equiv 0(\bmod\ 2).

For any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
, define
\text{ord}_{p}(a)=\max\{i\geq 0\ |\ p^{i}\mid a\}
.
For convenience, we take
\text{ord}_{p}(0)=\infty
.

The following Lemma is also useful.

Lemma 2.1

(A variant of generalized Hensel’s Lemma)Assume that
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
,

a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
s=\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))
.

(1) If
r\geq 2s
, then
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s})
implies
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})
.

(2) If there exists some
r_{0}\geq 2s+1
such that

a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r_{0}-s})\Longleftrightarrow f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r_{0}}),

then for any
r\geq r_{0}
,

a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s})\Longleftrightarrow f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r}).

(2.2)

(3) If
s=0
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p)
, then for all
r\geq 1
,

a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r})\Longleftrightarrow f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r}).

(2.3)

Proof.
Since
s=\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))
,
f^{\prime}(a)\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{s})
but
f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{s+1})
.

(1) Assume that
r\geq 2s
. If
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s})
,
there exists some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
b=a+lp^{r-s}
.
When
i\geq 2
,

i(r-s)\geq 2(r-s)\geq r.

Taking
X=a,Y=lp^{r-s}
and modulo
p^{r}
, we only need the first two terms in
(2.1)
. That is,

f(a)=f(b+lp^{r-s})\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)lp^{r-s}\equiv f(a)(\bmod\ p^{r}).

(2) We only need to show that
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})
implies
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s})
for all
r\geq r_{0}
.

we induct on
r
. For
r=r_{0}
, it is from the assumption.

If it is true that

f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})\Longrightarrow a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s}),

then for
r+1
, first one has

f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r+1})\Rightarrow f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})\Rightarrow a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s}).

Set
b=a+lp^{r-s}
for some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
r\geq r_{0}\geq 2s+1
,
i(r-s)\geq 2(r-s)\geq r+1
for
i\geq 2
.
Modulo
p^{r+1}
, we also have

f(b)\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)lp^{r-s}(\bmod\ p^{r+1}).

Hence,
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r+1})
yields

f^{\prime}(a)l\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{r+1})
. But
f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{s+1})
, we obtain that
l\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
. And,

b=a+lp^{r-s}\equiv a(\bmod\ p^{r+1-s}).

Therefore, (2.2) holds for any
r\geq r_{0}
.

(3) When
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p)
,

a\equiv b(\bmod\ p)\Longleftrightarrow f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p)

holds unconditionally. Taking
s=0
and
r_{0}=1
in (2), we obtain (3).
\Box

Remark.
(1) The assumption on the existence of
r_{0}
in (2) is necessary. Otherwise,
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})
may not imply
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-s})
even when
r\geq 2s+1
holds. A counterexample is as follows. Set
f(X)=X^{2}+p(p-1)X
,
a=0
. Then,
f^{\prime}(0)=p(p-1)
and
s=\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(0))=1
. When
r=3
,
we have that
f(p)=p^{3}\equiv 0\equiv f(0)(\bmod\ p^{3})
but
p\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{2})
. Here,
r_{0}
can be seen as the base to begin
p
-adic lifting.

(2) Comparing with the original Hensel’s lemma, the generalized Hesnel’s lemma works for the polynomial functions of which the derivations have higher
p
-orders somewhere. A simple version of the generalized Hensel’s lemma can be found in Exercise 6 of Section 1.5 in
[
12
]
.

3
The proof of the main result

In this section, we prove the main theorem of this paper. First, the upper bound of
M(p^{r})
is easily to be obtained by the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1

Assume that
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
.
If
f
is not a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
, then for any
r\geq 1

N(f,p^{r})\leq p^{r-1}(p-1).

Proof.
Since
f
is not a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
, there is
b_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f(a)\not\equiv b_{0}(\bmod\ p)
for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For a fixed
r\geq 1
, set

T=\{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}\ |\ b\equiv b_{0}(\bmod\ p)\}.

Then,
|T|=p^{r-1}
and
V(f\bmod\ p^{r})\cap T=\phi
. Hence,

V(f\bmod\ p^{r})\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}})\setminus T

and
N(f,p^{r})\leq p^{r}-p^{r-1}=p^{r-1}(p-1)
.
\Box

Lemma 3.2

Assume
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
.
If there exists an integer
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
f^{\prime}(a)\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
, then for
r\geq 2
,

N(f,p^{r})\leq p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).

Proof.
Without lose of generality, we can take an integer
a_{0}
in
\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}
such that
f^{\prime}(a_{0})\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
. Then, for
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, if
a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-1})
,
i.e., for some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
b=a+lp^{r-1}
, we have that

f^{\prime}(a)\equiv f^{\prime}(a_{0})(\bmod\ p)
and

f(b)\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)p^{r-1}\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a_{0})lp^{r-1}\equiv f(a)(\bmod\ p^{r})

since
r\geq 2
.

Consider the set
R_{r}=\{0,1,\cdots,p^{r}-1\}
of all representives in
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Definte the subset
S_{r}(a_{0})
of
R_{r}
by

S_{r}(a_{0})=\{a_{0}+lp+kp^{r-1}\ |\ 0\leq l\leq p^{r-2},1\leq k\leq p-1\}.

We have seen that
f(a_{0}+lp+kp^{r-1})\equiv f(a_{0}+lp)(\bmod\ p^{r})
for
1\leq k\leq p-1
. But notice that
a_{0}+lp\notin S_{r}(a_{0})
. One has that

f(S_{r}(a_{0}))\subseteq f(R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0})).

Hence,

V(f\bmod\ p^{r})=f(R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0}))\cup f(S_{r}(a_{0}))=f(R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0})).

Consequently,

\displaystyle N(f,p^{r})=|V(f\bmod\ p^{r})|=|f(R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0}))|=|R_{r}|-|S_{r}(a_{0})|=p^{r}-p^{r-2}(p-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).\ \ \ \Box

Next, we will show the upper bound of
M(p^{r})
can be achieved by some polynomial function over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.3

For
f(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
, assume
that
f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{2})
for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
N(f,p^{3})=p^{3}-p^{2}+p
, then for any
r\geq 3
,

N(f,p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).

Proof.
First, by Lemma
3.1
,
N(f,p^{3})=p^{3}-p^{2}+p
yields that
f
is a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
. As we have seen in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
N(f,p^{3})=p^{3}-p^{2}+p
also means that there exits an
a_{0}\in\{0,\cdots,p-1\}
such that
a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)\Rightarrow f^{\prime}(a)\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
and
a\not\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)\Rightarrow f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence, associated with the assumption of
f^{\prime}(a)
,

\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))=\begin{cases}1,&a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)\\
0,&a\not\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)\end{cases}.

We use the same notions in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, one has that

|f(R_{3}\setminus S_{3}(a_{0}))|=N(f,p^{3})=p^{3}-p^{2}+p=|R_{3}\setminus S_{3}(a_{0}))|,

which show that
(f\bmod\ p^{3})
is injective restricted on the set
R_{3}\setminus S_{3}(a_{0})
.
Therefore,
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{3})
holds if and only if one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)
a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p),\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))=1
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{2})
;

(2)
a\not\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p),\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))=0
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{3})
.

When
a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)
, the assumption of Lemma
2.1
(2) is satisfied for
s=1
. And, when
a\not\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)
, the assumption of Lemma
2.1
(3) is satisfied. We obtain that for
r\geq 3
,
f(a)\equiv f(b)(\bmod\ p^{r})
holds if and only if one of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)
a\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r-1})
;

(2)
a\not\equiv a_{0}(\bmod\ p)
and
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p^{r})
.

Thus,
(f\bmod\ p^{r})
is injective on
R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0})
. By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1)=|R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0})|=|f(R_{r}\setminus S_{r}(a_{0}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|f(R_{r})|=N(f,p^{r})\leq p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1),

which implies
N(f,p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1)
.
\Box

Lemma 3.4

For a prime
p
, set
f(X)=X^{2p-1}+pX\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
. Then, for any
r\geq 2
,

N(f,p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).

Proof.
First, by Fermat’s little theorem, for any
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
a^{p}\equiv a(\bmod\ p)
and

f(a)=a^{2p-1}-pa\equiv a^{2p-1}\equiv a(\bmod\ p).

Meanwhile,
f^{\prime}(X)=(2p-1)X^{2p-2}+p
,

f^{\prime}(a)=(2p-1)a^{2p-2}+p\equiv-a^{2p-2}\equiv-a^{p-1}(\bmod\ p).

For
a\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
,
f^{\prime}(a)\equiv-1(\bmod\ p)
. And, for
a\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
,
f^{\prime}(a)=(2p-1)a^{2p-2}-p\equiv-p\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{2})
since
2p-2\geq 2
for any prime
p
.

We always have that
f^{\prime}(a)\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p^{2})
. By Lemma
3.3
, we only need to show that
N(f,p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1)
for
r=2,3
.

When
r=2
, since
f(a)\equiv a(\bmod\ p)
,
(f\bmod\ p)
is a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover, the fact that

\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a))=\begin{cases}1,&a\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)\\
0,&a\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)\end{cases}

implies
(f\bmod\ p^{2})
is injective on
R_{2}\setminus S_{2}(0)
, where
S_{2}(0)=\{lp\ |\ 1\leq l\leq p-1\}
.
Hence,
N(f,p^{2})=p^{2}-p+1
.

When
r=3
, we need to consider the third term in the Taylor expansion
(2.1)
. That is,

f(a+lp)\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)lp+\frac{f^{(2)}(a)}{2}(lp)^{2}(\bmod\ p^{3}).

Since
\frac{f^{(2)}(X)}{2}=(2p-1)(p-1)X^{2p-3}
and
2p-3\geq 1
,

\frac{f^{(2)}(a)}{2}=(2p-1)(p-1)a^{2p-3}\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
for any
a\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
. Then, for
b=a+lp
with
a\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
and
l\not\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
, since

\text{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime}(a)lp)=2
,

f(b)\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)lp+\frac{f^{(2)}(a)}{2}(lp)^{2}\equiv f(a)+f^{\prime}(a)lp\not\equiv f(a)(\bmod\ p^{3}).

Hence,
(f\bmod\ p^{3})
is injective restricted on
R_{3}\setminus S_{3}(0)
and

N(f,p^{3})=p^{3}-p^{2}+p.

The proof is finished.
\Box

Remark.
Lemma
3.4
shows that given a prime
p
, we can construct a unified non-permutation polynomial
f(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
such that the value set of
(f\bmod\ p^{r})
are maximal in all non-permutation polynomial functions for all
r\geq 2
.

Putting the above things together, we obtain

The Proof of Theorem 1.1
When
r=1
, if
f
is not a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
,
V(f,p)
has
(p-1)
elements at most. And,
M(p)=p-1
since every mapping on
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
is a polynomial function.

When
r\geq 2
, the polynomial
f(X)=X^{2p-1}+pX
in Lemma
3.4
shows that

M(p^{r})\geq N(f,p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).

On the other hand, if
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
is not a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}}
for
r\geq 2
, then
f
is not a permutation over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
or

f^{\prime}(a)\equiv 0(\bmod\ p)
for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then, by
Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
,

M(p^{r})\leq\max\{p^{r}-p^{r-1},p^{r}-p^{r-1}+p^{r-2}\}=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1).

We obtain that
M(p^{r})=p^{r-2}(p^{2}-p+1)
.
\Box

4
The value
M(m)
for
m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}

For a positive integer
m
with prime decomposition

m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
, there is an isomorphism of rings

\Phi:{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/p_{1}^{r_{1}}{\mathbb{Z}})\times\cdots\times({\mathbb{Z}}/p_{k}^{r_{k}}{\mathbb{Z}})

by Chinese remainder theorem.

One has the following two lemmas

Lemma 4.1

For an integer
m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
and a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
,

V(f\bmod\ m)\simeq V(f\bmod\ p_{1}^{r_{1}})\times\cdots\times V(f\bmod\ p_{k}^{r_{k}}),

and

N(f,m)=N(f,p_{1}^{r_{1}})\times\cdots\times N(f,p_{k}^{r_{k}}).

Proof.
Since
a\equiv b(\bmod\ m)
implies
a\equiv b(\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}})
,
1\leq i\leq k
, the morphism
\Phi
restricted on
V(f\bmod\ m)
gives us a mapping

\displaystyle\Phi:V(f\mod\ m)

\displaystyle\rightarrow V(f\bmod\ p_{1}^{r_{1}})\times\cdots\times V(f\bmod\ p_{k}^{r_{k}})

\displaystyle(f(a)\bmod m)

\displaystyle\mapsto(f(a)\bmod\ p_{1}^{r_{1}},\cdots,f(a)\bmod\ p_{k}^{r_{k}}),

which is injective by Chinese remainder theorem.

On the other hand, for any
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{k})\in V(f\bmod\ p_{1}^{r_{1}})\times\cdots\times V(f\bmod\ p_{k}^{r_{k}})
, there is
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
such that

f(a_{i})\equiv b_{i}(\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}}),\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq k.

Also by Chinese remainder theorem, there exists an integer

a
satisfying
a\equiv a_{i}(\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}})
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Then,
f(a)\equiv f(a_{i})(\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}})

and

(f(a)\bmod p_{1}^{r_{1}},\cdots,f(a)\bmod p_{k}^{r_{k}})=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{k}).

Hence,
\Phi
is also surjective.

We have that
\Phi
is a bijection on
V(f\bmod\ m)
, the size of which is the product of the sizes of
V(f\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}}),1\leq i\leq k
.
\Box

In particular,
f
is a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if
f
is a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p_{i}^{r_{i}}{\mathbb{Z}}
for any
1\leq i\leq k
.

Meanwhile,

Lemma 4.2

For an integer
m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
and integral polynomials
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X],1\leq i\leq k
, there exists a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
such that

V(f\bmod\ m)\simeq V(f_{1}\bmod\ p_{1}^{r_{1}})\times\cdots\times V(f_{k}\bmod\ p_{k}^{r_{k}}).

Proof.
Applying Chinese remainder theorem on the coefficients of
f_{i}
, we can obtain a polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
such that
f\equiv f_{i}(\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}})
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Then,

V(f\bmod\ p_{i}^{r_{i}})=V(f_{i}\bmod p_{i}^{r_{i}}),1\leq i\leq k.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have the result.
\Box

Furthermore, for a prime factor
p
of
m
, set the number

m(p)=\begin{cases}m\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right),&\text{ord}_{p}(m)=1\\
m\left(1-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)&\text{ord}_{p}(m)\geq 2\end{cases}.

Then,

Theorem 4.1

For an integer
m=p_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
, let
p
be the maximal prime factor of
m
. One has that

M(m)=m(p),

unless
m=2^{r}3
for
r\geq 2
and
M(m)=2^{r-2}3^{2}
in this case.

Proof.
If
f
is not a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
, there is some
1\leq i\leq k
such that

f
is not a permutation polynomial over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p_{i}^{r_{i}}{\mathbb{Z}}
by Lemma
4.1
.
Using Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
4.2
,
it is obvious that

M(m)=\max\{m(p_{i})\ |\ 1\leq i\leq k\}.

Observe that both
1-\frac{1}{p}
and
1-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}
are strictly ascending when
p
is increasing. On the other hand, if
p^{\prime}
is another prime less than
p
, then
1-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime 2}}>1-\frac{1}{p}
holds only if
p=3,p^{\prime}=2
. We obtain the last assertion.
\Box

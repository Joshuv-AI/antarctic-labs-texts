Cohomology groups invariant under continuous orbit equivalence
Yongle Jiang

Abstract
. By the work of Brodzki-Niblo-Nowak-Wright and Monod, topological amenability of a continuous group action can be characterized using uniformly finite homology groups or bounded cohomology groups associated to this action. We show that (certain variations of) these groups are invariants for topologically free actions under continuous orbit equivalence.



1.
Introduction

We continue our study of continuous orbit equivalence introduced by Li
[
14
]
. In this paper, we focus on certain (co)homology groups for a continuous group action. Let us first review the introduction of these (co)homology groups.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group. There are two remarkable characterizations of amenablility of
G
. One, given by Johnson-Ringrose
[
13
]
, says that
G
is amenable if and only if the first bounded cohomology group with coefficients in
\ell^{1}_{0}(G)^{**}
vanishes, i.e.
H_{b}^{1}(G,\ell^{1}_{0}(G)^{**})=0
, where
\ell^{1}_{0}(G)
denotes the augmentation ideal, i.e. kernel of the summation map from
\ell^{1}(G)
to
\mathbb{R}
. By contrast, Block and Weinberger
[
2
]
described amenability in terms of non-vanishing of the 0-dimensional uniformly finite homology of
G
, i.e.
H_{0}^{uf}(G,\mathbb{R})\neq 0
. For a short, unified proof of these two results, see
[
5
]
. In particular, it was observed there that
H_{*}^{uf}(G,\mathbb{R})=H_{*}(G,\ell^{\infty}(G))
if
G
is finitely generated.

The notion of amenable actions of groups acting on topological spaces generalizes the concept of amenability and appears in many areas of mathematics. For example, a group acts amenably on a point if and only if it is amenable, while every hyperbolic group acts amenably on its Gromov boundary. For more on amenable actions, see
[
1
,
10
,
11
,
18
]
.

Parallel with these characterizations, people also found two similar characterizations for the amenablity of group actions in the topological sense. To do this, the key step is to find appropriate coefficient modules associated to a continuous action
G\curvearrowright X
.

More precisely, Brodzki, Niblo, Nowak and Wright considered the standard module
W_{0}(G,X)
and its submodule
N_{0}(G,X):=C(X,\ell^{1}_{0}(G))
. Note that
W_{0}(G,X)^{*}
and
N_{0}(G,X)^{**}
reduce to
\ell^{\infty}(G)
and
\ell^{1}_{0}(G)^{**}
respectively when
X
is a point. Naturally, they considered the bounded cohomology groups with coefficients in
N_{0}(G,X)^{**}

[
4
]
and also the uniformly finite homology of an action,
H^{uf}_{n}(G\curvearrowright X)
as the group homology with coefficients in
W_{0}(G,X)^{*}
, i.e.
H^{uf}_{n}(G\curvearrowright X):=H_{n}(G,W_{0}(G,X)^{*})

[
3
]
. They succeeded in characterizing amenability of actions using these (co)homology groups, generalizing the above results of Johson-Ringrose and Block-Weinberger for group case. A similar approach was also taken by Monod in
[
17
]
.

In
[
2
]
(see also
[
15
]
), among other results,
H_{*}^{uf}(G,\mathbb{R})
is shown to be an invariant for groups under coarse equivalence, i.e. quasi-isometry if the groups are finitely generated. Hence it is natural to ask whether the above (co)homology groups for actions are also invariants under some “coarse equivalence” for actions.

In this paper, we show this is indeed possible if we take “coarse equivalence” to be “continuous orbit equivalence”, and actions are assumed to be topologically free.

Let us recall the definition of continuous orbit equivalence, for known results on this notion and its connection to geometric group theory, see
[
9
,
8
,
7
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be two actions by homeomorphisms, where
G
,
H
are countable discrete groups and
X
,
Y
are compact Hausdorff spaces. Following
[
14
]
, we say the two actions are
continuous orbit equivalent
(abbreviated as COE) if there are homeomorphisms
\phi:X\approx Y
,
\psi:Y\approx X
and continuous maps
c:G\times X\to H
,
c^{\prime}:H\times Y\to G
such that
\phi(gx)=c(g,x)\phi(x)
and
\psi(hy)=c^{\prime}(h,y)\psi(y)
hold for all
g\in G
,
h\in H
,
x\in X
and
y\in Y
. If these two actions are
topologically free
, i.e. points with trivial stabilizers are dense, then both
c
and
c^{\prime}
are cocycles
[
14
, Lemma 2.8]
. Recall that
c:G\times X\to H
is a
cocycle
if
c(g_{1}g_{2},x)=c(g_{1},g_{2}x)c(g_{2},x)
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and all
x\in X
.

Now, we can state our main theorems. Note that all acting groups are assumed to be countable discrete and spaces are assumed to be compact Hausdorff.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be topologically free actions. If these two actions are COE, then
H^{uf}_{0}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong H^{uf}_{0}(H\curvearrowright Y)
and
H_{n}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})\cong H_{n}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
for all
n\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be topologically free actions. If these two actions are COE, then
H^{p}_{b}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{**})\cong H^{p}_{b}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
for all
p\geq 0
.

The paper is organized as follows.

Besides the introduction, the paper contains five other sections. In Section
2
, we review the definitions of group (co)homology and certain coefficient modules. In Section
3
, we state several lemmas on basic properties of the orbit cocycles and observe that the coefficient modules are closed under restriction maps. These are the crucial ingredients for constructing bijective (co)chain maps later. The next two sections will take up the majority of our paper:
Section
4
proves Theorem
1.1
and Section
5
proves Theorem
1.2
. The proof of these two theorems uses the same idea: we construct maps between (co)chain complexes directly and then use the whole sections to verify these maps are bijective (co)chain maps. Detailed proof for lower dimensional cases will be included to illustrate the main ideas. We conclude with several remarks in Section
6
to discuss corollaries of the theorems and other related questions.

Following the convention in
[
4
,
3
,
17
]
, all Banach spaces are assumed to be real.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Group (co)homology

We briefly recall the definition of group homology and (bounded) group cohomology using bar resolutions, see
[
15
, §4.3]
,
[
6
, Chapter III, §1]
and
[
16
]
.

Let
G
be a group and
V
be a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module. Let
(C_{*}(V),\partial_{*})
be the chain complex
\dots\overset{\partial_{3}}{\rightarrow}C_{2}(V)\overset{\partial_{2}}{\rightarrow}C_{1}(V)\overset{\partial_{1}}{\rightarrow}C_{0}(V)

with
C_{0}(V)=V
,
C_{n}(V)=C_{f}(G^{n},V)
, where
C_{f}
stands for maps with finite support, and

\partial_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\partial_{n}^{(i)}
, where

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{(0)}(f)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g_{0}\in G}g_{0}^{-1}f(g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1}),

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{(i)}(f)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\in G\\
g\bar{g}=g_{i}\end{subarray}}f(g_{1},\dots,g_{i-1},g,\bar{g},g_{i+1},g_{n-1})~\text{for}~1\leq i\leq n-1,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{(n)}(f)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g_{n}\in G}f(g_{1},\dots,g_{n-1},g_{n}).

Then we define
H_{n}(G,V):=Ker(\partial_{n})/Im(\partial_{n+1})
.

Let
(C^{*}(V),\partial^{*})
be the cochain complex

C^{0}(V)\overset{\partial^{0}}{\rightarrow}C^{1}(V)\overset{\partial^{1}}{\rightarrow}C^{2}(V)\overset{\partial^{2}}{\rightarrow}\dots
with
C^{0}(V)=V
,
C^{n}(V)=C(G^{n},V)
for all
n\geq 1
, and
\partial^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\partial_{(i)}^{n}
, where

\displaystyle\partial_{(0)}^{n}(f)(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=g_{0}f(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}),

\displaystyle\partial_{(i)}^{n}(f)(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=f(g_{0},\cdots,g_{i-1}g_{i},\dots,g_{n})~\text{for}~1\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\partial_{(n+1)}^{n}(f)(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=f(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1}).

Then we define
H^{n}(G,V):=Ker(\partial^{n})/Im(\partial^{n-1})
.

If
V
is a Banach
G
-module, we may replace
C^{*}(V)
by
C^{*}_{b}(V)
, where
C^{n}_{b}(V):=C_{b}(G^{n},V)
and
C_{b}
stands for uniformly bounded maps, then the group cohomology we get is the bounded cohomology group, written as
H^{n}_{b}(G,V)
.

2.2.
Coefficient modules

The module
V
we are interested in are certain dual or double dual Banach spaces, i.e.
N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
,
W_{0}(G,X)^{*}
and
N_{0}(G,X)^{**}
associated to a continuous action
G\curvearrowright X
.
Let us recall their definition below
[
3
,
4
]
.

The space
C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
of continuous
\ell^{1}(G)
-valued functions on
X
is equipped with the sup-
\ell^{1}
norm

||\xi||=\sup_{x\in X}\sum_{g\in G}|\xi_{x}(g)|.

Here, for
\xi\in C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
, we write
\xi_{x}(g):=\xi(x)(g)
.
We remind the reader that we are using a different notation from the one in
[
3
,
4
]
, where
\xi_{g}(x)
was used.

In this notation, the Banach space
C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
is equipped with a natural action of
G
,

(g\cdot\xi)_{x}(h):=\xi_{g^{-1}x}(g^{-1}h),

for each
g,h\in G
and
x\in X
.

The summation map on
\ell^{1}(G)
induces a continuous map
\sigma:C(X,\ell^{1}(G))\to C(X)
, where
C(X)
is equipped with the
\ell^{\infty}
norm. The space
N_{0}(G,X)
is defined to be the pre-image
\sigma^{-1}(0)
which we identify as
C(X,\ell^{1}_{0}(G))
. Then we define
W_{0}(G,X):=\sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathbb{R}
is regarded as constant functions on
X
.
Note that
W_{0}(G,X)=N_{0}(G,X)\oplus\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{R}
is identified as constant
\mathbb{R}\delta_{e}
-valued functions on
X
, here
e
is the neutral element in
G
. Obviously, both
N_{0}(G,X)
and
W_{0}(G,X)
are invariant under the above
G
-action since
\sigma
is
G
-equivariant.
Also recall that if
V
is a Banach
G
-module, then the Banach dual space
V^{*}
is also equipped with a
G
-module structure by requiring
(g\phi)(\xi)=\phi(g^{-1}\xi)
for
g\in G
,
\phi\in V^{*}
and
\xi\in V
.

3.
Properties of orbit cocycles and restriction invariant property

In this section, we state several lemmas proving basic properties of orbit cocycles and observe that the module
N_{0}(G,X)
is restriction invariant. These give us the hint on how to define bijective (co)chain maps between two (co)chain complexes later.

From now on, we use the notations introduced when defining COE, i.e.
\phi:X\approx Y
and
\psi:Y\approx X
are homeomorphisms;
c:G\times X\to H
and
c^{\prime}:H\times Y\to G
are maps satisfying certain identities.
Concerning COE between topologically free actions, the following property will be used frequently.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.10]

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be topologically free actions that are COE. Then
c^{\prime}(c(g,x),\phi(x))=g
for all
g\in G
,
x\in X
. Similarly,
c(c^{\prime}(h,y),\psi(y))=h
for all
h\in H
,
y\in Y
.

Clearly, this implies that for all
x\in X
,
G\ni g\mapsto c(g,x)\in H
is a bijection.

The starting point for our proof is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be topologically free actions which are COE. Then there exists a (linear) map
\pi
from
C(Y,\ell^{1}(H))
onto
C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
which is an isometry, and which moreover has the following properties:
\pi(W_{0}(H,Y))=W_{0}(G,X)
,
\pi(N_{0}(H,Y))=N_{0}(G,X)
and
\pi(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathbb{R}
is identified as constant
\mathbb{R}\delta_{e}
-valued functions on
X
.

Proof.

We define
\pi:C(Y,\ell^{1}(H))\to C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
by setting
\pi(\xi)=\xi^{\prime}
, where

\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g):=\xi_{\phi(x)}(c(g,g^{-1}x)).

First, we check
\pi
is a well-defined isometry.

Note that for all
x\in X
,
G\ni g\mapsto c(g^{-1},x)^{-1}=c(g,g^{-1}x)\in H
is a bijection. A calculation shows that
\sum_{g}\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g)=\sum_{h}\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)
for all
x\in X
and hence
||\xi||=||\xi^{\prime}||
. So
\pi
is an isometry.

Now we check
\xi^{\prime}\in C(X,\ell^{1}(G))
. For any given
x\in X
and
\epsilon>0
, we need to find
\mathcal{U}_{x}
, a neighborhood of
x
, such that if
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
, then
\sum_{g\in G}|\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g)-\xi^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}(g)|<\epsilon
.

Since
\xi\in C(Y,\ell^{1}(H))
, there exists a small neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{\phi(x)}
containing
\phi(x)
such that if
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\phi(x)}
, then
\sum_{h\in H}|\xi_{y^{\prime}}(h)-\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)|<\epsilon/4
.

Take a large finite set
F\subseteq H
such that
\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)|<\epsilon/4
. This implies that if
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\phi(x)}
, then

\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{y^{\prime}}(h)|\leq\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{y^{\prime}}(h)-\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)|+\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)|\leq\epsilon/2.

Now, we take
\mathcal{U}_{x}
to be any sufficiently small neighborhood of
x
, which is contained in
\psi(\mathcal{V}_{\phi(x)})
and satisfies the property that for any
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
, we have
c(g^{-1},x)=c(g^{-1},x^{\prime})
for all
g\in K
, where
K:=\{g\in G:~c(g^{-1},x)^{-1}\in F\}
is a finite set.

Take any
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
, note that
g\in K
iff
c(g^{-1},x)^{-1}\in F
iff
c(g^{-1},x^{\prime})^{-1}\in F
. Moreover,
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
implies
\phi(x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}_{\phi(x)}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}|\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g)-\xi^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}(g)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in K}|\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g)-\xi^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}(g)|+\sum_{g\not\in K}|\xi^{\prime}_{x}(g)-\xi^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}(g)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h\in F}|\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)-\xi_{\phi(x^{\prime})}(h)|+\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{\phi(x)}(h)|+\sum_{h\not\in F}|\xi_{\phi(x^{\prime})}(h)|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/4+\epsilon/4+\epsilon/2=\epsilon.

Second, it is easy to check
\pi(W_{0}(H,Y))\subseteq W_{0}(G,X),\pi(N_{0}(H,Y))\subseteq N_{0}(G,X)
and
\pi({\mathbb{R}})\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
hold. To see
\subseteq
is really
=
, we need to find the inverse of
\pi
.

By symmetry, we can define another isometry
L:C(X,\ell^{1}(G))\to C(Y,\ell^{1}(H))
by setting
L(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}
, where

\eta^{\prime}_{y}(h):=\eta_{\psi(y)}({c^{\prime}(h,h^{-1}y)}).

It is straightforward to check that
L
is the inverse of
\pi
. Indeed, this boils down to check that
c(c^{\prime}(h,y),\psi(y))=h
and
c^{\prime}(c(g,x),\phi(x))=g
, which hold by Lemma
3.1
. Then using
L
, we know
\pi
maps the three subspaces onto the corresponding ones.
∎

Observation

In the proof of the main theorems, we will use the following observation, which shows that
N_{0}(G,X)
is restriction invariant.

Under the assumptions in the main theorems, for any given
g\in G
, let us list the elements in the finite set
c(g^{-1},X):=\{c(g^{-1},x)|~x\in X\}
as
h_{1}^{-1},\dots,h_{n}^{-1}
. Then define
X_{i}:=\{x\in X:c(g^{-1},x)=h_{i}^{-1}\}
. Clearly,
X=\sqcup_{i}X_{i}
and each
X_{i}
is clopen since
c
is continuous. Note that
N_{0}(G,X)=C(X,\ell^{1}_{0}(G))=\oplus_{i}C(X_{i},\ell^{1}_{0}(G))
. For each
\xi^{\prime}\in C(X,\ell^{1}_{0}(G))
, let
\xi^{\prime}|_{X_{i}}
be the
i
-th component with respect to the above decomposition, i.e.
(\xi^{\prime}|_{X_{i}})_{g}(x)=\xi^{\prime}_{g}(x)
if
x\in X_{i}
and zero otherwise. Clearly,
\xi^{\prime}|_{X_{i}}\in C(X,\ell^{1}_{0}(G))
. For each
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
, define
\tau_{i}\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
by setting
\tau_{i}(\xi^{\prime})=\tau(\xi^{\prime}|_{X_{i}})
. Then, we have that
\xi^{\prime}=\sum_{i}\xi^{\prime}|_{X_{i}}
and
\tau=\sum_{i}\tau_{i}
. Note that the above decomposition is not
G
-equivariant, i.e.
g\tau_{i}\neq(g\tau)_{i}
.
Moreover, for any clopen subset
X_{0}
of
X
,
\xi|_{X_{0}}\in N_{0}(G,X)
if
\xi\in N_{0}(G,X)
, hence
\tau|_{X_{0}}
is also well-defined. (By convention,
\xi^{\prime}|_{\emptyset}:=0
and
\tau|_{\emptyset}:=0
.)

Below we record two more lemmas that we use frequently later to simplify the proof. The first lemma tells us the map
\pi
is equivariant in a local sense, and the second one tells us how the composition of
\pi
and
L
behaves under restrictions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g\in G
,
h\in H
and
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
. If
X_{0}\subseteq\{x\in X:~c(g^{-1},x)=h^{-1}\}
is clopen, then
(\pi(h\xi))|_{X_{0}}=(g\pi(\xi))|_{X_{0}}=g(\pi(\xi)|_{g^{-1}X_{0}})
, where
\pi
is the map constructed in Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

We may assume
X_{0}\neq\emptyset
; otherwise, the equality holds trivially as both side equals zero.
Take any
x\in X
and
g^{\prime}\in G
, we get

\displaystyle[(\pi(h\xi))|_{X_{0}}]_{x}(g^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(h\xi)_{\phi(x)}(c(g^{\prime},g^{\prime-1}x)),~\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,~\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi_{h^{-1}\phi(x)}(h^{-1}c(g^{\prime},g^{\prime-1}x)),~\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,~\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi_{\phi(g^{-1}x)}(c(g^{-1}g^{\prime},g^{\prime-1}x)),~\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,~\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[\pi(\xi)]_{g^{-1}x}(g^{-1}g^{\prime}),~\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,~\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[g(\pi(\xi))]_{x}(g^{\prime}),~\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,~\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=[(g\pi(\xi))|_{X_{0}}]_{x}(g^{\prime}).

Hence,
\pi(h\xi)|_{X_{0}}=(g\pi(\xi))|_{X_{0}}
holds. It is easy to check
(g\pi(\xi))|_{X_{0}}=g(\pi(\xi)|_{g^{-1}X_{0}})
holds by definition.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
be clopen subsets of
X
and
Y
respectively. If
\eta\in N_{0}(G,X)
and
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, then
\pi(L(\eta)|_{Y_{0}})|_{X_{0}}=\eta|_{X_{0}\cap\psi(Y_{0})}
and
L(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{0}})|_{Y_{0}}=\xi|_{Y_{0}\cap\phi(X_{0})}
.

Proof.

We will check the first equality below, the second one can be checked similarly.

Let
\xi=L(\eta)|_{Y_{0}}\in N_{0}(H,Y)
. Take any
g\in G
and
x\in X
, then

\displaystyle\quad(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{0}})_{x}(g)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi_{\phi(x)}(c(g,g^{-1}x)),\quad\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}$}\\
0,\quad\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad(\mbox{def. of restriction map and $\pi$})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\eta_{x}(c^{\prime}(c(g,g^{-1}x),c(g,g^{-1}x)^{-1}\phi(x))),\quad\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}\cap\psi(Y_{0})$}\\
0,\quad\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad(\mbox{def. of $\xi$ and $L$})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\eta_{x}(g),\quad\mbox{if $x\in X_{0}\cap\psi(Y_{0})$}\\
0,\quad\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\quad(\mbox{cocycle identity and Lemma \ref{lemma: xin's lemma}})

\displaystyle=(\eta|_{X_{0}\cap\psi(Y_{0})})_{x}(g).

Hence,
(\pi(L(\eta)|_{Y_{0}}))|_{X_{0}}=\eta|_{X_{0}\cap\psi(Y_{0})}
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By
[
3
, Corollary 10]
, we know that
H_{0}^{uf}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus H_{0}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
or
H_{0}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
depending on whether the action is amenable or not. Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove the second part, i.e.
H_{n}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})\cong H_{n}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
for all
n\geq 0
.

We will construct a (linear) map
S_{n}:C_{f}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{*})\to C_{f}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
and check
\partial_{n}S_{n}=S_{n-1}\partial_{n}
for each
n
, i.e.
S_{n}
is a chain map. Moreover, we will show it is an isomorphism by finding the inverse map
T_{n}
. Clearly, this will induce an isomorphism between homology groups and hence finish the proof.

We present the detailed proof when
n=0,1
to illustrate the main ideas. The proof of the general case will be given after the proof of these two cases.

4.1.
Case
n=0

Clearly, the map
\pi:N_{0}(H,Y)\to N_{0}(G,X)
as used in Lemma
3.2
induces a map, denoted by
\pi^{*}
, on the dual spaces.

Define
S_{0}:=\pi^{*}
.

Let
f\in C_{f}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
, we define
S_{1}(f)\in C_{f}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
by setting

S_{1}(f)=f^{\prime}
, where

f^{\prime}(h_{1})(\xi):=\sum_{j}f(g_{j})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}}).

Here
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
,
c^{\prime}(h_{1},Y):=\{c^{\prime}(h_{1},y):y\in Y\}:=\{g_{j}~|~j\},X_{j}=\{x\in X:~c(g_{j}^{-1},x)=h_{1}^{-1}\}
. Strictly speaking, it is better to use notation
X_{g_{j},h_{1}}
for
X_{j}
, but we often simplify the subscripts if no confusion arises.

Clearly,
S_{0},S_{1}
are well-defined. Indeed, to see
S_{1}
is well-defined, observe that if
supp(f)=F
, then
supp(S_{1}(f))\subseteq\cup_{g\in F}c(g,X)
, which is finite once
F
is a finite set.

Step 1
: we check
\partial_{1}S_{1}=S_{0}\partial_{1}
.

Let
f\in C_{f}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
and
f^{\prime}=S_{1}(f)
. Recall
\partial_{1}(f^{\prime})=\sum_{h_{1}\in H}h_{1}^{-1}f^{\prime}(h_{1})-\sum_{h_{1}\in H}f^{\prime}(h_{1})
. We need to show
\partial_{1}S_{1}(f)=S_{0}\partial_{1}(f)
; equivalently,

\sum_{h_{1}\in H}h_{1}^{-1}f^{\prime}(h_{1})-\sum_{h_{1}\in H}f^{\prime}(h_{1})=\sum_{g\in G}\pi^{*}(g^{-1}f(g))-\sum_{g\in G}\pi^{*}(f(g)).

It suffices to check

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}\in H}h_{1}^{-1}f^{\prime}(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in G}\pi^{*}(g^{-1}f(g)),

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}\in H}f^{\prime}(h_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in G}\pi^{*}(f(g)).

To check (
2
), take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}}f^{\prime}(h_{1})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{1}}\sum_{j}f(g_{j})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})=\sum_{g}\sum_{h_{1},h_{1}\in c(g,X)}f(g)(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{g,h_{1}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}f(g)(\pi(\xi))=\sum_{g}\pi^{*}(f(g))(\xi).

The 2nd last equality holds since
X=\sqcup_{h_{1},h_{1}\in c(g,X)}X_{g,h_{1}}
for every
g
.

Similarly, let us check (
1
) holds below.

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}}(h_{1}^{-1}f^{\prime}(h_{1}))(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{1}}f^{\prime}(h_{1})(h_{1}\xi)=\sum_{h_{1}}\sum_{j}f(g_{j})(\pi(h_{1}\xi)|_{X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\sum_{h_{1},h_{1}\in c(g,X)}f(g)(\pi(h_{1}\xi)|_{X_{g,h_{1}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\sum_{h_{1},h_{1}\in c(g,X)}f(g)((g\pi(\xi))|_{X_{g,h_{1}}})~\mbox{(by Lemma \ref{lem: module structure under restriction})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}f(g)((g\pi(\xi))|_{\sqcup_{h_{1}\in c(g,X)}X_{g,h_{1}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}f(g)(g\pi(\xi))~\mbox{(as $X=\sqcup_{h_{1}\in c(g,X)}X_{g,h_{1}}$)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\pi^{**}(g^{-1}f(g))(\xi).

Step 2
:
S_{0}
is an isomorphism.

Recall in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, there exists some map
L:N_{0}(G,X)\to N_{0}(H,Y)
defined by setting
L(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}
, where
\eta^{\prime}_{h}(y):=\eta_{\psi(y)}({c^{\prime}(h,h^{-1}y)})
. By symmetry, it also induces a well-defined map
T_{0}:=L^{*}:N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}\to N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
. Clearly,
\pi^{*}L^{*}=id
and
L^{*}\pi^{*}=id
.

4.2.
Case
n=1

Write
c^{\prime}(h_{0},Y)=\{g_{i}|~i\}
,
c^{\prime}(h_{1},Y)=\{g_{j}|~j\}
,
c^{\prime}(h_{2},Y)=\{g_{k}|~k\}
,
c^{\prime}(h,Y)=\{g_{s}|~s\}
and
c^{\prime}(\bar{h},Y)=\{g_{t}|~t\}
. In this subsection, we will use the following notations.

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{x:c(g_{i}^{-1},x)=h_{0}^{-1}\},\quad X_{j}:=\{x:c(g_{j}^{-1},x)=h_{1}^{-1}\},

\displaystyle X_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{x:c(g_{k}^{-1},x)=h_{2}^{-1}\},\quad X_{s}:=\{x:c(g_{s}^{-1},x)=h^{-1}\},

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle:=\{x:c(g_{t}^{-1},x)=\bar{h}^{-1}\}.

Strictly speaking, it is better to use the notation
X_{g_{i},h_{0}},X_{g_{j},h_{1}},X_{g_{k},h_{2}},X_{g_{s},h},X_{g_{t},\bar{h}}
for
X_{i},X_{j},X_{k},X_{s},X_{t}
respectively, but we often simplify the subscripts as the variables
h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},h,\bar{h}
are usually fixed in the context.

Let
\theta\in C_{f}(G^{2},N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
, we define
S_{2}:C_{f}(G^{2},N_{0}(G,X)^{*})\to C_{f}(H^{2},N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
by setting
\theta^{\prime}=S_{2}\theta
, where for any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
,

\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(\xi):=\sum_{i,j}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{i}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}).

Note that
S_{2}
is well-defined. Indeed, observe that

supp(\theta^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{(g_{i},g_{j})\in supp(\theta)}c(g_{i},X)\times c(g_{j},X).

Therefore,
supp(\theta)
is finite implies
supp(\theta^{\prime})
is finite.

Write
f=\partial_{2}\theta
and
f^{\prime}=S_{1}f
, from the definition of
\partial_{2}
, we know

f(g_{1})=\sum_{g_{0}}g_{0}^{-1}\theta(g_{0},g_{1})-\sum_{g,\bar{g},g\bar{g}=g_{1}}\theta(g,\bar{g})+\sum_{g_{2}}\theta(g_{1},g_{2}).

Step 1
: we check
S_{1}\partial_{2}=\partial_{2}S_{2}
.

Evaluate both sides at
\theta
and use the above notations, we are left to show the following identity holds.

f^{\prime}(h_{1})=\sum_{h_{0}}h_{0}^{-1}\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\theta^{\prime}(h,\bar{h})+\sum_{h_{2}}\theta^{\prime}(h_{1},h_{2}).

Let
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, a calculation shows:

\displaystyle\quad f^{\prime}(h_{1})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}f(g_{j})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}[\sum_{g}\theta(g,g_{j})(g(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}}))-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\\
g\bar{g}=g_{j}\end{subarray}}\theta(g,\bar{g})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})+\sum_{g}\theta(g_{j},g)(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})].

\displaystyle\quad[\sum_{h_{0}}h_{0}^{-1}\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\theta^{\prime}(h,\bar{h})+\sum_{h_{2}}\theta^{\prime}(h_{1},h_{2})](\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}}\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(h_{0}\xi)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\theta^{\prime}(h,\bar{h})(\xi)+\sum_{h_{2}}\theta^{\prime}(h_{1},h_{2})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}}\sum_{i,j}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(\pi(h_{0}\xi)|_{g_{i}X_{j}\cap X_{i}})-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{s,t}\theta(g_{s},g_{t})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{s}X_{t}\cap X_{s}})

\displaystyle\quad{}+\sum_{h_{2}}\sum_{j,k}\theta(g_{j},g_{k})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{j}X_{k}\cap X_{j}}).

Comparing the two expressions above, we just need to prove the corresponding terms are equal, i.e.

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{g}\theta(g,g_{j})(g(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}}\sum_{i,j}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(\pi(h_{0}\xi)|_{g_{i}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}),

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\\
g\bar{g}=g_{j}\end{subarray}}\theta(g,\bar{g})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{s,t}\theta(g_{s},g_{t})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{s}X_{t}\cap X_{s}}),

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{g}\theta(g_{j},g)(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{2}}\sum_{j,k}\theta(g_{j},g_{k})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{j}X_{k}\cap X_{j}}).

To check (
3
), apply Lemma
3.3
to get
\pi(h_{0}\xi)|_{g_{i}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}=g_{i}(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}\cap g_{i}^{-1}X_{i}})
.

Then, RHS of (
3
) =
\sum_{j}\sum_{h_{0},i}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(g_{i}(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}\cap g_{i}^{-1}X_{i}}))
.

Now it suffices to prove for every
j
,

\sum_{h_{0},i}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(g_{i}(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}\cap g_{i}^{-1}X_{i}}))=\sum_{g}\theta(g,g_{j})(g(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})).

First, recall that
g_{i}^{-1}X_{i}=\{x:c(g_{i},x)=h_{0}\}
, which we denote by
Z_{g_{i},h_{0}}
.

Clearly, for every
g\in G
,
X=\sqcup_{h_{0}\in c(g,X)}Z_{g,h_{0}}
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{0},i}\theta(g_{i},g_{j})(g_{i}(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}\cap g_{i}^{-1}X_{i}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\sum_{h_{0}\in c(g,X)}\theta(g,g_{j})(g(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}\cap Z_{g,h_{0}}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\theta(g,g_{j})(g(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})).

To check (
4
), first, observe that we have a bijection/reordering between the two index sets:

\displaystyle\left\{(h,\bar{h},g_{s},g_{t})\begin{tabular}[]{|l}$h\bar{h}=h_{1}$\\
$g_{s}\in c^{\prime}(h,Y),g_{t}\in c^{\prime}(\bar{h},Y)$\\
$g_{s}X_{g_{t},\bar{h}}\cap X_{g_{s},h}\neq\emptyset$\end{tabular}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\left\{(g_{j},g,\bar{g},h,\bar{h})\begin{tabular}[]{|l}$h\bar{h}=h_{1},g\bar{g}=g_{j}$\\
$g_{j}\in c^{\prime}(h_{1},Y),h\in c(g,X),\bar{h}\in c(\bar{g},X)$\\
$gX_{\bar{g},\bar{h}}\cap X_{g,h}\neq\emptyset$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Indeed, one can define a bijection as follows:
g_{s}\mapsto g,g_{t}\mapsto\bar{g},h\mapsto h,\bar{h}\mapsto\bar{h}
.

Then we have the following.

RHS of (
4
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\\
h\bar{h}=h_{1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{s,t}\theta(g_{s},g_{t})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{s}X_{g_{t},\bar{h}}\cap X_{g_{s},h}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\\
g\bar{g}=g_{j}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h},h\bar{h}=h_{1}\\
h\in c(g,X),\bar{h}\in c(\bar{g},X)\end{subarray}}\theta(g,\bar{g})(\pi(\xi)|_{gX_{\bar{g},\bar{h}}\cap X_{g,h}})

\displaystyle\quad(\mbox{use the above bijection to do change of variables})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\\
g\bar{g}=g_{j}\end{subarray}}\theta(g,\bar{g})(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}})=\text{LHS of \eqref{eq7}}.

The 2nd last equality holds since for every
g,\bar{g}
with
g\bar{g}=g_{j}
,

X_{j}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h},h\bar{h}=h_{1}\\
h\in c(g,X),\bar{h}\in c(\bar{g},X)\end{subarray}}(gX_{\bar{g},\bar{h}}\cap X_{g,h}),

which can be checked easily.

To check (
5
), observe for every
j
,

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{h_{2},k}\theta(g_{j},g_{k})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{j}X_{k}\cap X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{2},k}\theta(g_{j},g_{k})(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{j}X_{g_{k},h_{2}}\cap X_{j}})~(\mbox{as $X_{k}=X_{g_{k},h_{2}}$ by convention})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h_{2}\\
h_{2}\in c(g,X)\end{subarray}}\theta(g_{j},g)(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{j}X_{g,h_{2}}\cap X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g}\theta(g_{j},g)(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{j}}).

The last equality holds since
X=\sqcup_{h_{2}~s.t.~h_{2}\in c(g,X)}g_{j}X_{g,h_{2}}
for every
g
.

Step 2
:
S_{1}
is an isomorphism.

Recall
L:N_{0}(G,X)\to N_{0}(H,Y)
is the inverse of
\pi
. Then we may define a map
T_{1}:C_{f}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})\to C_{f}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
by setting
T_{1}(f^{\prime})=f^{\prime\prime}
, where

f^{\prime\prime}(g_{1})(\eta):=\sum_{j}f^{\prime}(h_{j})(L(\eta)|_{Y_{j}}).

Here
\eta\in N_{0}(G,X)
and
Y_{j}:=\{y\in Y:~c^{\prime}(h_{j}^{-1},y)=g_{1}^{-1}\}
.

Let
f\in C_{f}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
, write
f^{\prime}=S_{1}f
,
f^{\prime\prime}=T_{1}f^{\prime}
. We check
T_{1}S_{1}=id
. Equivalently,
f^{\prime\prime}=f
.
A calculation shows

\displaystyle\quad f^{\prime\prime}(g_{1})(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}f^{\prime}(h_{j})(L(\eta)|_{Y_{j}})~(\mbox{def. of $T_{1}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{i}f(g_{i})(\pi(L(\eta)|_{Y_{j}})|_{X_{g_{i},h_{j}}})~(\mbox{def. of $S_{1}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,i}f(g_{i})(\eta|_{X_{g_{i},h_{j}}\cap\psi(Y_{j})}).~(\mbox{Lemma \ref{lem: composition of pi and L under restrictions}})

Recall by our notation,
X_{g_{i},h_{j}}:=\{x\in X:c(g_{i}^{-1},x)=h_{j}^{-1}\}
.
Now, it is easy to check that
X_{g_{i},h_{j}}\cap\psi(Y_{j})=\emptyset
unless
g_{i}=g_{1}
. And
X_{g_{1},h_{j}}\cap\psi(Y_{j})=X_{g_{1},h_{j}}
by Lemma
3.1
.

Hence,
f^{\prime\prime}(g_{1})(\eta)=\sum_{j}f(g_{1})(\eta|_{X_{g_{1},h_{j}}})=f(g_{1})(\eta)
.

So
T_{1}S_{1}=id
. By symmetry,
S_{1}T_{1}=id
.

4.3.
General case

Based on the definition of
S_{i}
for
i=0,1
, it is natural to consider the following general formula for
S_{n}
.

For all
n\geq 2
, let
\theta\in C_{f}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
, define
S_{n}(\theta)=\theta^{\prime}\in C_{f}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{*})
by setting
for every
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
,

(6)

\displaystyle{}\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},\dots,h_{n-1})(\xi):=\sum_{t_{0},\dots,t_{n-1}}\theta(g_{t_{0}},\dots,g_{t_{n-1}})(\pi(\xi)|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}).

Here,
c^{\prime}(h_{i}^{-1},Y)=\{g_{t_{i}}^{-1}:t_{i}\in T_{i}\}
for some finite set
T_{i}
for all
i=0,\cdots,n-1
,

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{t_{i}}&:=\{x:c(g_{t_{i}}^{-1},x)=h_{i}^{-1}\},\\
[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]&:=X_{t_{0}}\cap g_{t_{0}}X_{t_{1}}\cap\dots\cap(g_{t_{0}}\cdots g_{t_{n-2}})X_{t_{n-1}}.\end{split}

Strictly speaking, the index set for the above
\sum
in (
6
) should be
(g_{t_{0}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}})
, but we simplify it to
(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1})
for convenience. Again, we will switch to the notation
X_{g_{t_{i}},h_{i}}
once using
X_{t_{i}}
causes confusion. We also reserve the notation
g_{i}
for an arbitrary element in
G
.

Clearly,
S^{n}
is well-defined. Indeed, observe that

supp(\theta^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcup_{(g_{t_{0}},\cdots,g_{t_{n-1}})\in supp(\theta)}c(g_{t_{0}},X)\times\cdots\times c(g_{t_{n-1}},X).

Hence,
supp(\theta)
is finite implies
supp(\theta^{\prime})
is finite.

Our goal is to check that
\partial_{n}S_{n}=S_{n-1}\partial_{n}
holds and
S_{n}
is a bijection for each
n\geq 2
. This will finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Step 1
: We claim
\partial_{n}S_{n}=S_{n-1}\partial_{n}
.

Take any
\theta\in C_{f}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
, write
\theta^{\prime}=S_{n}(\theta)
and
f=\partial_{n}\theta
.
Fix any
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})\in H^{n-1}
and any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
.

Now, we calculate both
[\partial_{n}S_{n}(\theta)](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\xi)
and
[S_{n-1}\partial_{n}(\theta)](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\xi)
and show they are equal.

First, we have the following calculation.

\displaystyle\quad[\partial_{n}S_{n}(\theta)](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})

\displaystyle=[\partial_{n}\theta^{\prime}](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{0}\in H}h_{0}^{-1}\theta^{\prime}(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\in H\\
h\bar{h}=h_{i}\end{subarray}}\theta^{\prime}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{i-1},h,\bar{h},h_{i+1},\ldots,h_{n-1})

\displaystyle\quad{}+(-1)^{n}\sum_{h_{n}\in H}\theta^{\prime}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}).

Evaluate both sides of the above at the element
\xi
, then plug in the definition of
\theta^{\prime}
, i.e. (
6
) into the RHS of the above, we deduce that

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\quad[\partial_{n}S_{n}(\theta)](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\xi)\\
&=\sum_{h_{0}\in H}\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\big\langle\theta(g_{t_{0}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}}),\pi(h_{0}\xi)|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\big\rangle\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h,\bar{h}\in H\\
h\bar{h}=h_{i}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},\ldots,t_{i-1},s\\
k,t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n-1}\end{subarray}}\big\langle\theta(g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{i-1}},g_{s},g_{k},g_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}}),\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\pi(\xi)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{i-1},s,k,t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\big\rangle\\
&\quad+(-1)^{n}\sum_{h_{n}\in H}\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\big\langle\theta(g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n}}),\pi(\xi)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\big\rangle.\end{split}

Here, we use
\langle-,-\rangle
to denote the evaluation, i.e. evaluating the first entry (a function) at the second entry (a variable).
Let us recall some notations used here:

\displaystyle X_{s}:=\{x:c(g_{s}^{-1},x)=h^{-1}\},\quad X_{k}:=\{x:c(g_{k}^{-1},x)=\bar{h}^{-1}\}.

(9)

[t_{1},\ldots,t_{i-1},s,k,t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n-1}]:=\\
X_{t_{1}}\cap g_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap(g_{t_{1}}\cdots g_{t_{i-2}})X_{t_{i-1}}\cap(g_{t_{1}}\cdots g_{t_{i-1}})X_{s}\\
\cap(g_{t_{1}}\cdots g_{t_{i-1}}g_{s})X_{k}\cap\cdots\cap(g_{t_{1}}\cdots g_{t_{i-1}}g_{s}g_{k}g_{t_{i+1}}\cdots g_{t_{n-2}})X_{t_{n-1}}.

Second, another calculation tells us the following.

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad[S_{n-1}\partial_{n}\theta](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\xi)\\
&=[S_{n-1}f](h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\xi)\\
&=\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}}f(g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}})(\pi(\xi)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}).\quad(\mbox{def. of $S_{n-1}$})\end{split}

Now, recall that
f=\partial_{n}\theta
and the definition of
\partial_{n}
from Section
2
, we get

\displaystyle\quad f(g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{g_{0}\in G}g_{0}^{-1}\theta(g_{0},g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,\bar{g}\in G\\
g\bar{g}=g_{t_{i}}\end{subarray}}\theta(g_{t_{1}},\ldots,g_{t_{i-1}},g,\bar{g},g_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,g_{t_{n-1}})

C_{b}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
and check directly
S^{n}
is a cochain map, i.e.
\partial^{n-1}S^{n-1}=S^{n}\partial^{n-1}
holds. Then, we use symmetry to find the inverse of
S^{n}
. Since the proof of general case uses cumbersome notations, we include the proof of the initial cases (
n=1,2,3
) to illustrate the main ideas.

5.1.
Case
n=1

Recall that
C_{b}(H^{0},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})=N_{0}(H,Y)^{**}
. We define
S^{0}=\pi^{**}
and
S^{1}(f)=f^{\prime}
, where

f^{\prime}(g)(\tau):=\sum_{i}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{i})),\tau_{i}\rangle.

Here,
f\in C_{b}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
,
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
and
\tau_{i}=\tau|_{X_{i}}
. Moreover, we write
X_{i}=\{x\in X:~c(g^{-1},x)=h_{i}^{-1}\}
, where
c(g,X)=\{h_{i}|~i\}
.

Step 1: check
S^{1}
is well-defined, i.e.
\sup_{g\in G}||f^{\prime}(g)||<\infty
.

Since
\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{i})),\tau_{i}\rangle=f(h_{i})(\tau_{i}\pi)
, it suffices to show that
\sum_{i}||\tau_{i}||/||\tau||
is bounded (and independent of the choice of
g
,
\{X_{i}\}
and
n:=\#c(g,X)
). This is done by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
and
X=\sqcup_{i}X_{i}
be a finite clopen partition. Then
\sum_{i}||\tau_{i}||\leq||\tau||
.

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, take
a_{i}\in N_{0}(G,X)
such that
||a_{i}||=1
and
||\tau_{i}||\leq|\tau_{i}(a_{i})|+\epsilon/n
for all
i
.

Then
\sum_{i}||\tau_{i}||\leq\sum_{i}|\tau_{i}(a_{i})|+\epsilon=\sum_{i}\tau(a_{i}|_{X_{i}}\cdot\lambda_{i})+\epsilon=\tau(\sum_{i}(a_{i}|_{X_{i}}\cdot\lambda_{i}))+\epsilon
, where
\lambda_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
is the sign of
\tau_{i}(a_{i})
.

Observe that
\sum_{i}(a_{i}|_{X_{i}}\cdot\lambda_{i})\in N_{0}(G,X)
and
||\sum_{i}(a_{i}|_{X_{i}}\cdot\lambda_{i})||\leq 1
. Hence,
\sum_{i}||\tau_{i}||\leq||\tau||+\epsilon
for all
\epsilon>0
. So,
\sum_{i}||\tau_{i}||\leq||\tau||
.
∎

Step 2: check
\partial^{0}S^{0}=S^{1}\partial^{0}
.

Take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{**}
,
g\in G
and
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
. We do computation as follows.

\displaystyle(S^{1}\partial^{0}\xi)(g)(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle\pi^{**}((\partial^{0}\xi)(h_{i})),\tau_{i}\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $S^{1}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle(\partial^{0}\xi)(h_{i}),\tau_{i}\pi\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\pi^{**}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle h_{i}\xi,\tau_{i}\pi\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\partial^{0}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,h_{i}^{-1}(\tau_{i}\pi)\rangle.\qquad(\mbox{def. of dual action})

\displaystyle(\partial^{0}S^{0}\xi)(g)(\tau)

\displaystyle=[g(S^{0}\xi)](\tau)\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\partial^{0}$})

\displaystyle=(S^{0}\xi)(g^{-1}\tau)\qquad(\mbox{def. of dual action})

\displaystyle=\langle\pi^{**}\xi,g^{-1}\tau\rangle\qquad(\mbox{$S^{0}=\pi^{**}$})

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,(g^{-1}\tau)\pi\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\pi^{**}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle\xi,(g^{-1}\tau_{i})\pi\rangle.\qquad(\mbox{$\tau=\sum_{i}\tau_{i}$})

Therefore, to show the above two equations are equal, it suffices to check that for each
i
, we have
h_{i}^{-1}(\tau_{i}\pi)=(g^{-1}\tau_{i})\pi
. Equivalently, we need to check that
\tau(\pi(h_{i}\xi)|_{X_{i}})=\tau([g(\pi(\xi))]|_{X_{i}})
for each
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, which is clear by Lemma
3.3
.

Clearly,
S^{0}
is a bijection by Lemma
3.2
.

5.2.
Case
n=2

Now, we construct a (linear) map
S^{2}:C_{b}(H^{2},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})\to C_{b}(G^{2},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
.

Write

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{i}&=\{x:c(g_{0}^{-1},x)=h_{i}^{-1}\},\\
X_{j}&=\{x:c(g_{1}^{-1},x)=h_{j}^{-1}\},\\
X_{k}&=\{x:c((g_{0}g_{1})^{-1},x)=h_{k}^{-1}\},\\
X_{s}&=\{x:c((g_{1}g_{2})^{-1},x)=h_{s}^{-1}\},\\
X_{l}&=\{x:c(g_{2}^{-1},x)=h_{l}^{-1}\}.\end{split}

Given
f\in C_{b}(H^{2},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, we define
S^{2}(f)=f^{\prime}
, where for each
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
,

f^{\prime}(g_{0},g_{1})(\tau):=\sum_{i,j}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle.

Step 1:
S^{2}
is well-defined.

To show
\sup_{(g_{0},g_{1})\in G^{2}}||f^{\prime}(g_{0},g_{1})||<\infty
, apply Lemma
5.1
to
X=\sqcup_{i,j}g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}
.

Step 2: check
\partial^{1}S^{1}=S^{2}\partial^{1}
.

Take any
f\in C_{b}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, write
f^{\prime}=S^{1}f
.
For any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad(S^{2}\partial^{1}f)(g_{1},g_{2})(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,l}\langle(\partial^{1}f)(h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $S^{2}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,l}\langle h_{j}f(h_{l})-f(h_{j}h_{l})+f(h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\partial^{1}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,l}\langle h_{j}f(h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle-\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle+\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle.

\displaystyle\quad(\partial^{1}S^{1}f)(g_{1},g_{2})(\tau)

\displaystyle=(g_{1}f^{\prime}(g_{2})-f^{\prime}(g_{1}g_{2})+f^{\prime}(g_{1}))(\tau)\qquad(\mbox{def. of $\partial^{1}$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{l})),(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\rangle-\sum_{s}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{s})),\tau_{s}\rangle+\sum_{j}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{j})),\tau_{j}\rangle.

By comparing the two expressions above, it suffices to show each corresponding terms are equal, i.e.

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,l}\langle h_{j}f(h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{l})),(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\rangle,

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{s})),\tau_{s}\rangle,

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{j})),\tau_{j}\rangle.

To check (
21
), it suffices to show for each
l
,

\sum_{j}\langle h_{j}f(h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle=\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{l})),(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\rangle.

Equivalently, we check
\sum_{j}h_{j}^{-1}(\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi)=(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\pi
holds.

Take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}h_{j}^{-1}(\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi)(h_{j}\xi)\qquad(\mbox{def. of dual action})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\tau(\pi(h_{j}\xi)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\tau((g_{1}\pi(\xi))|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}))\qquad(\mbox{Lemma \ref{lem: module structure under restriction}})

\displaystyle=\tau((g_{1}\pi(\xi))|_{\sqcup_{j}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}))

\displaystyle=\tau((g_{1}\pi(\xi))|_{g_{1}X_{l}})\qquad(\mbox{$X=\sqcup_{j}X_{j}$})

\displaystyle=\tau(g_{1}(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{l}}))

\displaystyle=(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\pi(\xi).

Hence,
\sum_{j}h_{j}^{-1}(\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi)=(g_{1}^{-1}\tau)_{l}\pi
holds.

To check (
22
), observe that by the cocycle identity,
X_{s}=\sqcup_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}(g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j})
holds, where
I_{s}:=\{(j,l):h_{s}=h_{j}h_{l}\}
.

LHS of (
22
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\sum_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}\langle f(h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle~(\mbox{as $g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}\neq\emptyset$ only if $(j,l)\in I_{s}$ for some $s$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\sum_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}\langle f(h_{s}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\langle f(h_{s}),\tau|_{\sqcup_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\langle f(h_{s}),\tau|_{X_{s}}\rangle~(\mbox{as $X_{s}=\sqcup_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}(g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j})$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\langle f(h_{s}),\tau_{s}\pi\rangle=\mbox{RHS of (\ref{eq 2 for n=1, thm 1.2})}.

To check (
23
), it suffices to show for each
j
,
\sum_{l}\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle=\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{j})),\tau_{j}\rangle
. Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle=\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{\sqcup_{l}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle=\langle f(h_{j}),\tau|_{X_{j}}\pi\rangle=\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{j})),\tau_{j}\rangle.

Step 3: check that
S^{1}
is a bijection.

For this purpose, we define a map
T^{1}:C_{b}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{**})\to C_{b}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
by setting
T^{1}(z)=z^{\prime}
, where
z^{\prime}(h)(\nu):=\sum_{i}\langle L^{**}(z(g_{i})),\nu_{i}\rangle
. Here
\nu\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
,
\nu_{i}=\nu|_{Y_{i}},Y_{i}=\{y:c^{\prime}(h^{-1},y)=g_{i}^{-1}\}
.

Now, we check
T^{1}S^{1}=id
holds.
S^{1}T^{1}=id
can be checked similarly by symmetry.

Take any
f\in C_{b}(H,N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
,
h\in H
and
\nu\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
, write
f^{\prime}=S^{1}f
.

\displaystyle(T^{1}S^{1}f)(h)(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle L^{**}(f^{\prime}(g_{i})),\nu_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle f^{\prime}(g_{i}),\nu_{i}L\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\langle\pi^{**}(f(h_{j})),(\nu_{i}L)_{j}\rangle

(Here,
(\nu_{i}L)_{j}:=(\nu_{i}L)|_{X_{g_{i},h_{j}}}
,
X_{g_{i},h_{j}}=\{x:c(g_{i}^{-1},x)=h_{j}^{-1}\}.
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\langle f(h_{j}),(\nu_{i}L)_{j}\pi\rangle.

Now, we claim that
(\nu_{i}L)_{j}\pi=\nu|_{\phi(X_{g_{i},h_{j}})\cap Y_{i}}
.
To see this, take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, then

\displaystyle(\nu_{i}L)_{j}\pi(\xi)

\displaystyle=(\nu_{i}L)(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{g_{i},h_{j}}})=\nu([L(\pi(\xi)|_{X_{g_{i},h_{j}}})]|_{Y_{i}})

\displaystyle=\nu(\xi|_{\phi(X_{g_{i},h_{j}})\cap Y_{i}})\qquad(\mbox{Lemma \ref{lem: composition of pi and L under restrictions}})

\displaystyle=(\nu|_{\phi(X_{g_{i},h_{j}})\cap Y_{i}})(\xi).

Note that
\phi(X_{g_{i},h_{j}})\cap Y_{i}=Y_{i}
if
h_{j}=h
and
\emptyset
otherwise. Therefore,

(T^{1}S^{1}f)(h)(\nu)=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\langle f(h_{j}),(\nu_{i}L)_{j}\pi\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle f(h),\nu_{i}\rangle=\langle f(h),\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\rangle=f(h)(\nu).

So,
T^{1}S^{1}=id
. By symmetry,
S^{1}T^{1}=id
.

5.3.
Case
n=3

Now, we construct a (linear) map
S^{3}:C_{b}(H^{3},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})\to C_{b}(G^{3},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
.
For each
f\in C_{b}(H^{3},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, define
S^{3}(f)=f^{\prime}
, where

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(g_{0},g_{1},g_{2})(\tau):=\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle.

Here,
\tau\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
and we still use (
20
) for the definition of
X_{i},X_{j},X_{k},X_{l},X_{s}
.

Step 1: check
S^{3}
is well-defined.

The proof is similar to the one when showing
S^{1}
is well-defined.

Step 2: check
S^{3}\partial^{2}=\partial^{2}S^{2}
.

Take any
f\in C_{b}(H^{3},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, write
f^{\prime}=S^{2}f
, we have

\displaystyle\quad(S^{3}\partial^{2}f)(g_{0},g_{1},g_{2})(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,l}\langle(\partial^{2}f)(h_{i},h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,l}\langle h_{i}f(h_{j},h_{l})-f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l})+f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l})-f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,l}\langle h_{i}f(h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle-\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle-\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=:\textcircled{1}-\textcircled{2}+\textcircled{3}-\textcircled{4}.

Here,
\textcircled{1},\textcircled{2},\textcircled{3}
and
\textcircled{4}
denote the corresponding
terms appeared above.

\displaystyle\quad(\partial^{2}S^{2}f)(g_{0},g_{1},g_{2})(\tau)

\displaystyle=(g_{0}f^{\prime}(g_{1},g_{2})-f^{\prime}(g_{0}g_{1},g_{2})+f^{\prime}(g_{0},g_{1}g_{2})-f^{\prime}(g_{0},g_{1}))(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle-\sum_{k,l}\langle f(h_{k},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{k}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,s}\langle f(h_{i},h_{s}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{s}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle-\sum_{i,j}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=:\textcircled{5}-\textcircled{6}+\textcircled{7}-\textcircled{8}.

Now, in order to prove
\textcircled{1}-\textcircled{2}+\textcircled{3}-\textcircled{4}=\textcircled{5}-\textcircled{6}+\textcircled{7}-\textcircled{8}
, it suffices to check the corresponding terms are equal, i.e.
\textcircled{1}=\textcircled{5}
,
\textcircled{2}=\textcircled{6}
,
\textcircled{3}=\textcircled{7}
and
\textcircled{4}=\textcircled{8}
. More precisely, we want to prove the following equalities hold.

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,l}\langle h_{i}f(h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,l}\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle,

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\langle f(h_{k},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{k}}\pi\rangle,

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,s}\langle f(h_{i},h_{s}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{s}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle,

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,l}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle.

To check (
24
), it suffices to check for each
j,l
,

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\langle h_{i}f(h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle=\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle.

Observe that

(29)

\displaystyle h_{i}^{-1}(\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi)=(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}\cap g_{0}^{-1}X_{i}}\pi.

Indeed, take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
, and write
X_{0}:=(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}
, we have

(h_{i}^{-1}(\tau|_{X_{0}}\pi))\xi=\tau(\pi(h_{i}\xi)|_{X_{0}})\overset{Lem.\ref{lem: module structure under restriction}}{=}\tau(g_{0}(\pi(\xi)|_{g_{0}^{-1}X_{0}}))=[(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{0}^{-1}X_{0}}\pi](\xi)
.

Therefore,

LHS of (
28
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle h_{i}f(h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),h_{i}^{-1}(\tau|_{(g_{0}g_{1})X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi)\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{middle eq for thm 1.2, n=2})}{=}\sum_{i}\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}\cap g_{0}^{-1}X_{i}}\pi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{\sqcup_{i}(g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}\cap g_{0}^{-1}X_{i})}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f(h_{j},h_{l}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mbox{RHS of (\ref{eq9})}.

To check (
25
),
observe
X_{k}=\sqcup_{(i,j)\in I_{k}}g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}
, where
I_{k}=\{(i,j):h_{k}=h_{i}h_{j}\}
.
So,

RHS of (
25
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{k}}\langle f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\sum_{k}\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{k}}\langle f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,i,j}\langle f(h_{i}h_{j},h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mbox{LHS of (\ref{eq 2= eq 6 in thm 1.2 n=2})}.

Here, the 2nd last equality holds as
g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset
only if
(i,j)\in I_{k}
for some
k
.

To check (
26
), use
X_{s}=\sqcup_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}g_{1}X_{l}\cap X_{j}
, where
I_{s}=\{(j,l):h_{s}=h_{j}h_{l}\}
to deduce

RHS of (
26
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,s}\langle f(h_{i},h_{s}),\tau|_{g_{0}X_{s}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,s}\sum_{(j,l)\in I_{s}}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{k}}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{l,i,j}\langle f(h_{i},h_{j}h_{l}),\tau|_{g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mbox{LHS of (\ref{eq 3= eq 7 in thm 1.2 n=2})}.

The 3rd last equality holds since we have a bijection between the index sets:

\{(i,s,j,l):h_{s}=h_{j}h_{l},g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset\}

and

\{(k,l,i,j):h_{k}=h_{i}h_{j},g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset\}.

And the 2nd last equality holds as
g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}\neq\emptyset
only if
(i,j)\in I_{k}
for some
k
.

To check (
27
), just observe
g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}=\sqcup_{l}(g_{0}g_{1}X_{l}\cap g_{0}X_{j}\cap X_{i}).

Step 3:
S^{2}
is a bijection.

Define
T^{2}:C_{b}(G^{2},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})\to C_{b}(H^{2},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
by setting
T^{2}(f^{\prime})=f^{\prime\prime}
, where

f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu)=\sum_{i,j}\langle f^{\prime}(g_{i},g_{j}),\nu|_{h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}}L\rangle.

Here
\nu\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
, and

\displaystyle Y_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{y\in Y;c^{\prime}(h_{0}^{-1},y)=g_{i}^{-1}\},

\displaystyle Y_{j}

\displaystyle:=\{y\in Y;c^{\prime}(h_{1}^{-1},y)=g_{j}^{-1}\}.

Now, let us check
T^{2}S^{2}=id
.

Take any
f\in C_{b}(H^{2},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
,
\nu\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
, let
f^{\prime}=S^{2}f
and
f^{\prime\prime}=T^{2}f^{\prime}
, we aim to show
f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu)=f(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu)
.

A calculation shows

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{s,t}\langle f(h_{s},h_{t}),(\nu|_{h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}}L)|_{g_{i}X_{g_{j},h_{t}}\cap X_{g_{i},h_{s}}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\sum_{s,t}\langle f(h_{s},h_{t}),\nu|_{h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}\cap\phi(g_{i}X_{g_{j},h_{t}}\cap X_{g_{i},h_{s}})}\rangle.

Here,
X_{g_{i},h_{s}}=\{x\in X:c(g_{i}^{-1},x)=h_{s}^{-1}\}
and
X_{g_{j},h_{t}}=\{x\in X:c(g_{j}^{-1},x)=h_{t}^{-1}\}
.

One can check that
h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}\cap\phi(g_{i}X_{g_{j},h_{t}}\cap X_{g_{i},h_{s}})=\emptyset
unless
(h_{s},h_{t})=(h_{0},h_{1})
. If this condition holds, then
h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}\cap\phi(g_{i}X_{g_{j},h_{1}}\cap X_{g_{i},h_{0}})=h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}
.

Hence,

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu)=\sum_{i,j}\langle f(h_{0},h_{1}),\nu|_{h_{0}Y_{j}\cap Y_{i}}\rangle=f(h_{0},h_{1})(\nu).

5.4.
General case

We’ll define a map
S^{n}:C_{b}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})\to C_{b}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
and check it is a bijective cochain map. Hence, it induces an isomorphism between the two bounded cohomology groups.

We introduce some notations which will be used in this subsection.

Let
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
be elements in
G
. Write

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}c(g_{j}^{-1},X)&:=\{h_{t_{j}}^{-1}|~t_{j}\},\\
X_{t_{j}}&:=\{x\in X:c(g_{j}^{-1},x)=h_{t_{j}}^{-1}\},\\
c((g_{i}g_{i+1})^{-1},X)&:=\{h_{s_{i}}^{-1}|~s_{i}\},\\
X_{s_{i}}&:=\{x\in X:c((g_{i}g_{i+1})^{-1},x)=h_{s_{i}}^{-1}\}.\end{split}

Note that
X_{s_{i}}=\sqcup_{(t_{i},t_{i+1})\in\Delta_{i}}X_{t_{i}}\cap g_{i}X_{t_{i+1}}
, where
\Delta_{i}:=\{(t_{i},t_{i+1}):h_{s_{i}}=h_{t_{i}}h_{t_{i+1}}\}
.

We remind the reader once again that
X_{t_{j}}
,
X_{s_{i}}
and
\Delta_{i}
are just simplified versions for the notations
X_{g_{j},h_{t_{j}}}
,
X_{g_{i}g_{i+1},h_{s_{i}}}
and
\Delta_{s_{i}}
respectively, which we will switch to if necessary.

Let
f\in C_{b}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, we define
S^{n}(f):=f^{\prime}\in C_{b}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
, where

f^{\prime}(g_{0},\dots,g_{n-1})(\tau):=\sum_{t_{0},\dots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\dots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\circ\pi\rangle

for all
g_{i}\in G
and
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
.

Here,

(31)

\displaystyle[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]:=X_{t_{0}}\cap g_{0}X_{t_{1}}\cap g_{0}g_{1}X_{t_{2}}\cap\dots\cap(g_{0}\cdots g_{n-2})X_{t_{n-1}}.

We split the proof into several steps.

Step 1:
S^{n}
is well-defined.

Apply Lemma
5.1
to
X=\sqcup_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]
.

Step 2:
S^{n}
is a cochain map, i.e.
S^{n+1}\partial^{n}=\partial^{n}S^{n}
.

Fix
f\in C_{b}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, let
f^{\prime}=S^{n}f
. We aim to show
S^{n+1}\partial^{n}f=\partial^{n}f^{\prime}
.

Take any
g_{0},\ldots,g_{n}
in
G
, and any
\tau\in N_{0}(G,X)^{*}
. We compute as follows.

First,

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\qquad\partial^{n}f^{\prime}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})(\tau)\\
&=[g_{0}f^{\prime}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f^{\prime}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{i-1}g_{i},\ldots,g_{n})+(-1)^{n+1}f^{\prime}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n-1})](\tau)\\
&=\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},\\
s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}\end{subarray}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-2}},h_{s_{i-1}},h_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
&\quad+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi\rangle.\end{split}

Recall here,
c(g_{i-1}g_{i},X)=\{h_{s_{i-1}}|~s_{i-1}\}
,
X_{s_{i-1}}=\{x:c((g_{i-1}g_{i})^{-1},x)=h_{s_{i-1}}^{-1}\}
and

(33)

[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}]=X_{t_{0}}\cap g_{0}X_{t_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap(g_{0}\cdots g_{i-2})X_{s_{i-1}}\\
\cap(g_{0}\cdots g_{i-2}g_{i-1}g_{i})X_{t_{i+1}}\cap\cdots\cap(g_{0}\dots g_{n-1})X_{t_{n}}.

Second,

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\qquad(S^{n+1}\partial^{n}f)(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})(\tau)\\
&=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle\partial^{n}(f)(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
&=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle h_{t_{0}}f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
&\quad+\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-1}}h_{t_{i}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
&\quad+\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle(-1)^{n+1}f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle.\end{split}

By comparing (
34
) with (
32
), it suffices to show that the corresponding terms are equal, i.e. we want to establish the following identities.

(a)

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle h_{t_{0}}f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle.

(b) For each
1\leq i\leq n
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},\\
s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}\end{subarray}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-2}},h_{s_{i-1}},h_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle\\
=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-1}}h_{t_{i}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle.

(c)

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi\rangle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle(-1)^{n+1}f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle.

Proof of (a).

By Lemma
3.4
,
(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{0}^{-1}X_{t_{0}}\cap[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi=h_{t_{0}}^{-1}(\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi)
holds.

Indeed, evaluate both sides at any
\xi
in
N_{0}(H,Y)
, this boils down to check
X_{t_{0}}\cap g_{0}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]=[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]
, which is clear from the definition of
[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}].

Using this identity, we deduce

RHS of (a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),h_{t_{0}}^{-1}(\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{g_{0}^{-1}X_{t_{0}}\cap[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{\sqcup_{t_{0}}g_{0}^{-1}X_{t_{0}}\cap[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),(g_{0}^{-1}\tau)|_{[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mbox{LHS of (a)}.\qed

Proof of (b).

From
X_{s_{i-1}}=\sqcup_{(t_{i-1},t_{i})\in\Delta_{i-1}}X_{t_{i-1}}\cap g_{i-1}X_{t_{i}}
,
we deduce that

(35)

\displaystyle[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}]=\sqcup_{(t_{i-1},t_{i})\in\Delta_{i-1}}[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}].

Indeed, just compare definition (
31
) and (
33
).

Hence,

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{s_{i-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-2}},h_{s_{i-1}},h_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},s_{i-1},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq10})}{=}\sum_{s_{i-1}}\sum_{(t_{i-1},t_{i})\in\Delta_{i-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-2}},h_{t_{i-1}}h_{t_{i}},h_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{i-1},t_{i}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{i-2}},h_{t_{i-1}}h_{t_{i}},h_{t_{i+1}},\ldots,h_{t_{n}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]}\pi\rangle.

The last equality holds since
[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]\neq\emptyset
only if
(t_{i-1},t_{i})\in\Delta_{i-1}
for some
s_{i-1}
. (Recall
\Delta_{i-1}=\Delta_{s_{i-1}}
for simplicity.)

Therefore, (b) holds by taking sum over
t_{0},\ldots,t_{i-2},t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{n}
on both sides of the above equality.
∎

Proof of (c).

As
[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}]=[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]\cap(g_{0}\cdots g_{n-1})X_{t_{n}}
, we get

RHS of (c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]\cap(g_{0}\cdots g_{n-1})X_{t_{n}}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]\cap\sqcup_{t_{n}}(g_{0}\cdots g_{n-1})X_{t_{n}}}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\tau|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mbox{LHS of (c)}.\qed

Step 3:
S^{n}
is a bijection.

To check
S^{n}
is a bijection, consider the natural inverse map
T^{n}
defined by symmetry as follows.

For
f^{\prime}\in C_{b}(G^{n},N_{0}(G,X)^{**})
, define
f^{\prime\prime}:=T(f^{\prime})
as follows:

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\nu):=\sum_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}}\langle f^{\prime}(g_{i_{0}},\ldots,g_{i_{n-1}}),\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\circ L\rangle.

Here,
\nu\in N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
,
[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]:=Y_{i_{0}}\cap h_{0}Y_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap(h_{0}\cdots h_{n-2})Y_{i_{n-1}}
and
Y_{i_{j}}:=\{y:c^{\prime}(h_{j}^{-1},y)=g_{i_{j}}^{-1}\}
.

Let
f\in C_{b}(H^{n},N_{0}(H,Y)^{**})
, write
f^{\prime}=S^{n}f
and
f^{\prime\prime}=T^{n}f^{\prime}
.
We aim to show
T^{n}S^{n}=id
, i.e.
f^{\prime\prime}=f
.
Take any
h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1}
in
H
and any
\nu
in
N_{0}(H,Y)^{*}
, then

(36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\nu)\\
&=\sum_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}}\langle f^{\prime}(g_{i_{0}},\ldots,g_{i_{n-1}}),\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\circ L\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),(\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\circ L)|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi\rangle.\end{split}

Here,
[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]:=X_{g_{i_{0}},h_{t_{0}}}\cap g_{i_{0}}X_{g_{i_{1}},h_{t_{1}}}\cap\cdots\cap(g_{i_{0}}\cdots g_{i_{n-2}})X_{g_{i_{n-1}},h_{t_{n-1}}}
and
X_{g_{i_{j}},h_{t_{j}}}:=\{x:c(g_{i_{j}}^{-1},x)=h_{t_{j}}^{-1}\}
.

To continue the proof, we notice the following facts.

(Fact 5)
(\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\circ L)|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi=\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]\cap\phi([t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}])}
.

To prove this fact, take any
\xi\in N_{0}(H,Y)
,
h\in H
and
y\in Y
. Let
\xi^{\prime}=\pi(\xi)
and
\eta=\xi^{\prime}|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}
. We aim to prove
((\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\circ L)|_{[t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}]}\pi)(\xi)=(\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]\cap\phi([t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}])})(\xi)
; equivalently,
\nu((L\circ\eta)|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]})=\nu(\xi|_{{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]\cap\phi([t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}])}})
. This is clear by Lemma
3.4
.

(Fact 6)
[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]\cap\phi([t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}])=\emptyset
unless
h_{t_{j}}=h_{j}
for all
0\leq j\leq n-1
. When these conditions hold, the intersection equals
[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]
.

This fact can be directly checked using cocycle identity and Lemma
3.1
.

Now we can continue the computation in (
36
) as follows.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad f^{\prime\prime}(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1})(\nu)\\
&\overset{Fact~5}{=}\sum_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{t_{0}},\ldots,h_{t_{n-1}}),\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]\cap\phi([t_{0},\ldots,t_{n-1}])}\rangle\\
&\overset{Fact~6}{=}\sum_{i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}}\langle f(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1}),\nu|_{[i_{0},\ldots,i_{n-1}]}\rangle\\
&=\langle f(h_{0},\ldots,h_{n-1}),\nu\rangle.\end{split}

Therefore,
f^{\prime\prime}=f
and the proof is finished.

6.
Concluding remarks

We end this paper with several remarks addressing various issues related to our main theorems.

(1) It is routine to check that the isomorphism
S
constructed in the proof preserves the Johnson classes
[
4
, Definition 20]
and the fundamental classes
[
3
, Definition 3]
for the two actions, which were introduced to study amenability of group actions. Since the triviality of the Johnson class
[
4
, Theorem 1]
and the nontriviality of the fundamental class
[
3
, Theorem 9]
are used to characterize the amenability of an action, we see that for two topologically free actions, topologically amenability is an invariant property under continuous orbit equivalence.

(2) Under the same assumptions as in our theorems, it is natural to ask whether
H^{uf}_{n}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong H^{uf}_{n}(H\curvearrowright Y)
for
n\geq 1
holds. For this question, our method does not work. Indeed, we use crucially the decomposition of elements in
N_{0}(G,X)
as a finite sum of elements in
N_{0}(G,X)
with respect to a finite partition of
X
into clopen subsets. The summands do not belong to
W_{0}(G,X)
anymore if we decompose an element in
W_{0}(G,X)
. This may be interpreted as saying
W_{0}(G,X)
is not res-invariant (w.r.t.
X
), following
[
15
, Definition 4.1]
in spirit or reflecting the fact
W_{0}(G,X)
is not a
(G,X)
-module in the sense of
[
17
]
.

In fact, the following suggests the above isomorphism may fail in general. First, by
[
3
, Corollary 5]
, we know that for a topologically amenable action
G\curvearrowright X
,
H^{uf}_{n}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong H_{n}(G,\mathbb{R})\oplus H_{n}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
holds. Then, we consider two continuous actions which are both topologically amenable and topologically free, e.g. left translation actions on the Stone-C̆ech compactifications of free groups with different rank, say
F_{2}
and
F_{3}
. As
F_{2}
is quasi-isometric to
F_{3}
, we know they are also bilipschitz equivalent by
[
21
]
; equivalently (see
[
15
, Corollary 2.21]
),
F_{2}\curvearrowright\beta F_{2}\overset{coe}{\sim}F_{3}\curvearrowright\beta F_{3}
. On the one hand,
H_{1}(F_{k},\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
implies
H_{1}(F_{2},\mathbb{R})\not\cong H_{1}(F_{3},\mathbb{R})
, but on the other hand,
H_{1}(F_{2},N_{0}(F_{2},\beta F_{2})^{*})\cong H_{1}(F_{3},N_{0}(F_{3},\beta F_{3})^{*})
by our main theorems. This suggests that for the above example, one expects these two actions have non-isomorphic
H_{1}^{uf}
.

Nevertheless, under certain assumptions, we can still have some positive result.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
be topologically free actions which are COE. If
G\curvearrowright X
is topologically amenable and both
G
and
H
are finitely generated torsion free nilpotent groups with finite cohomological dimension, then
H_{i}^{uf}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong H_{i}^{uf}(H\curvearrowright Y)
for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, observe that
H\curvearrowright Y
is also topologically amenable. This is clear by previous Remark (1). One can also check this using
[
3
, Definition 7]
and the map
\pi
defined in Lemma
3.2
.

Then by
[
3
, Corollary 5]
, for topologically amenable actions, we have
H^{uf}_{i}(G\curvearrowright X)\cong H_{i}(G,\mathbb{R})\oplus H_{i}(G,N_{0}(G,X)^{*})
. The same holds for the action
H\curvearrowright Y
.

By Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that
H_{i}(G,\mathbb{R})\cong H_{i}(H,\mathbb{R})
.

Observe that COE between the above two actions implies
G
and
H
are quasi-isometric. Indeed, just fix any
x\in X
, it is easy to check
g\in G\to H\ni c(g,x)
is a quasi-isometry using Lemma
3.1
.

Now, as
G
and
H
are quasi-isometric nilpotent groups, we deduce
H^{i}(G,\mathbb{R})\cong H^{i}(H,\mathbb{R})
by
[
20
, Theorem 1.5]
. Moreover,
cd(G)=cd(H)
by
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
15
, Corollary 4.42]
. The proof is finished by noticing that for finitely generated torsion free nilpotent groups, they are
orientable Poincaré duality groups, which implies that
H^{i}(G,\mathbb{R})\cong H_{n-i}(G,\mathbb{R})
, where
n=cd(G)
. And the same holds for
H
. (For the above assertion and the definition of orientable Poincaré duality groups, see Section 10, Chapter VIII in
[
6
]
, in particular,
[
6
, Example 1, p. 222]
.)
∎

(3) (Co)homology groups associated to many coefficient modules are proved to be invariants under COE for two topologically free actions in
[
15
, Theorem 3.1, 3.5]
, but as far as we can see, the (co)homology groups considered in our paper are not covered by these theorems. It seems plausible one may also prove our theorems using the method in
[
15
]
, i.e. try to interpret the (co)homologies for
G
as (co)homologies for the transformation groupoid, but one may need to extend unitary representations of étale locally compact groupoid (
[
15
, §3.2]
,
[
19
]
) to linear isometric representations on Banach spaces. In fact, for two COE topologically free actions
G\curvearrowright X
and
H\curvearrowright Y
, it may be possible to show there is a one to one correspondence between
(G,X)
-modules of type
M
in the sense of
[
17
]
, say
E
, and
(H,Y)
-modules of type
M
, say
F
, and under this correspondence,
H_{b}^{*}(G,E^{*})\cong H_{b}^{*}(H,F^{*})
. Theorem
1.2
may be thought of as an evidence for this.

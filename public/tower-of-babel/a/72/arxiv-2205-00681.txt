Proof ideas

To prove Theorem

(a)

(b)

\ell_{\mathsf{v}}
passing through
\Pi(\mathsf{v})
and the origin intersects
\Gamma
at two points (except the origin) with
b
-values
b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}}<0<b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}
such that

b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}}<-\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{s(s-1)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon<b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}\ .

(b)

The line
\ell_{\alpha}
passing through
\Pi(\mathsf{v}(-H))
and
\Pi(\alpha)
intersects
\Gamma
at two points with
b
-values
b_{1}^{\alpha}<b_{2}^{\alpha}
such that

b_{1}^{\alpha}<\frac{k-r}{r}+\epsilon^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}\qquad-\frac{k}{s}-\frac{1}{s(s-1)}<b_{2}^{\alpha}.

Proof.

The line
\ell_{\mathsf{v}}
which is of equation
w=\frac{s-r}{k}b
connects the point
\Pi(\mathsf{v})
which is outside or on
\Gamma
to the point
(0,0)
, so it intersects
\Gamma
at two other points except the origin with
b
-values
b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}}<b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}
. The equation of
\Gamma
for
b\neq 0
is
\Gamma(b)=(1+\frac{H^{2}}{2})b^{2}-1
, thus

b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}},b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}=\frac{\frac{s-r}{k}\pm\sqrt{(\frac{s-r}{k})^{2}+2H^{2}+4}}{H^{2}+2}.

We claim if
g\gg 0
, then

b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}}<-\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{s(s-1)}

i.e.

\frac{s-r}{k}+\frac{\big(k(s-1)-1\big)(H^{2}+2)}{s(s-1)}<\sqrt{\left(\frac{s-r}{k}\right)^{2}+2H^{2}+4}\ .

Taking the power 2 of both sides shows that we only need to prove

\big(k(s-1)-1\big)\left(2\frac{s-r}{k}s(s-1)+\big(k(s-1)-1\big)(H^{2}+2)\right)<2s^{2}(s-1)^{2}

which is equivalent to

(27)

-\frac{2}{k}s(s-r)+(H^{2}+2)\left(k^{2}(s-1)+\frac{1}{s-1}-2k\right)<2rs(s-1).

When
H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, then
s\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{2r}H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
by (
4
), so the limit of the left hand side is
(H^{2})^{2}\,\frac{k^{3}}{2r}\left(k-\frac{1}{r}\right)
but the limit of the right hand side is
(H^{2})^{2}\,\frac{k^{4}}{2r}
, thus the claim follows.

The next step is to show for
g\gg 0
, we have

(28)

b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}=\frac{\frac{s-r}{k}+\sqrt{(\frac{s-r}{k})^{2}+2H^{2}+4}}{H^{2}+2}>\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\,.

This holds if

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)^{2}(H^{2}+2)<\frac{s-r}{k}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)+1\,.

The limit of the right hand side when
s\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{2r}H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
is
\frac{k}{2r}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)H^{2}
, so the above holds when
g\gg 0
. This completes the proof of part (a).

The line
\ell_{\alpha}
in part (b) is of equation
w=\theta b+\beta-1
for

(29)

\theta\coloneqq\frac{s^{2}-r^{2}-skH^{2}+\frac{rs}{2}H^{2}}{s(k-r)+kr}\ ,\qquad\beta\coloneqq\frac{r(k-r)+sk-k^{2}H^{2}+\frac{kr}{2}H^{2}}{s(k-r)+kr}.

When
s\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{2r}H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, we know

(30)

\theta\ \rightarrow\ \frac{k-r}{2r}H^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\ \rightarrow\ \frac{k-r}{k}.

Hence if
g\gg 0
, we have

(31)

\theta^{2}+2\beta(H^{2}+2)\geq 0.

Therefore
\ell_{\alpha}
intersects the parabola
w=b^{2}(\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1)-1
at two points with
b
-values

b_{1}^{\alpha},\,b_{2}^{\alpha}=\frac{\theta\pm\sqrt{\theta^{2}+2\beta(H^{2}+2)}}{H^{2}+2}\,.

We claim if
g\gg 0
, then

(32)

-\frac{k}{s}-\frac{1}{s(s-1)}<b_{2}^{\alpha}=\frac{\theta+\sqrt{\theta^{2}+2\beta(H^{2}+2)}}{H^{2}+2}

which holds if

(33)

\frac{H^{2}+2}{2}\left(\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{s(s-1)}\right)^{2}<\beta-\theta\left(\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{s(s-1)}\right).

The right hand side is equal to

\beta-\theta\left(\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{s(s-1)}\right)=\frac{\frac{H^{2}}{s-1}\left(k-\frac{r}{2}\right)+r(k-r)-\frac{s}{s-1}+\frac{kr^{2}}{s}+\frac{r^{2}}{s(s-1)}}{s(k-r)+kr}

thus its limit when
s\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{2r}H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
is equal to
\frac{1}{H^{2}}\left(2\frac{r^{2}}{k^{2}}+\frac{2r(r-k)}{k^{4}}\right)
. Since the limit of the left hand side of (
33
) is equal to
\frac{2r^{2}}{H^{2}k^{2}}
the claim (
33
) follows.

The other intersection point of
\ell_{\alpha}
with
\Gamma
satisfies

b_{1}^{\alpha}=\frac{\theta-\sqrt{\theta^{2}+2\beta(H^{2}+2)}}{H^{2}+2}<\frac{k-r}{r}+\epsilon^{\prime}

if and only if

(34)

\theta-(H^{2}+2)\left(\frac{k-r}{r}+\epsilon^{\prime}\right)<\sqrt{\theta^{2}+2\beta(H^{2}+2)}\,.

Taking power 2 from both sides shows that (
34
) holds if

\frac{H^{2}+2}{2}\left(\frac{r-k}{r}-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)^{2}<\beta-\theta\left(\frac{r-k}{r}-\epsilon^{\prime}\right)

which is satisfied by (
30
) for
g\gg 0
and
\epsilon^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2r}
.

∎

If
g\gg 0
, (
30
) implies

(35)

\beta-1<0

where
\beta
is defined as in (
29
). Thus the origin point
(0,0)
and so the line segment connecting
\Pi(\mathsf{v}(-H))
to the origin lies above
\ell_{\alpha}
. Thus it intersects the curve
\Gamma
at a point with
b
-value
b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}(-H)}
satisfying

(36)

\frac{k-r}{r}\,\leq\,b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}(-H)}\,\leq\,b_{1}^{\alpha}\ .

Consider the line
\widetilde{\ell}
passing through
\Pi(\mathsf{v})
and
\Pi(\mathsf{v}(-H))
. It is of equation

w=H^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b\,+\frac{s}{r}-H^{2}\frac{k}{r}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-1.

When
s\rightarrow\frac{k^{2}}{2r}H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, we get

(37)

\frac{s}{r}-H^{2}\frac{k}{r}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-1\ >\ 0.

This implies the origin
(0,0)
lies below
\widetilde{\ell}
. Therefore the line segment connecting
\Pi(\mathsf{v})
to
\Pi(\mathsf{v}(-H))
(which is part of
\widetilde{\ell}
) lies above
\ell_{\mathsf{v}}
and
\ell_{\mathsf{v}(-H)}
, so
\widetilde{\ell}
intersects
\Gamma
at two points with
b
-values
\widetilde{b}_{1}<\widetilde{b}_{2}
satisfying

\frac{k-r}{r}\,\leq\,\widetilde{b}_{1}\,\leq\,b_{1}^{\mathsf{v}(-H)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad b_{2}^{\mathsf{v}}\,\leq\,\widetilde{b}_{2}\,\leq\,\frac{k}{r}.

Thus (
36
) together with Proposition
4.1
implies

(38)

\frac{k-r}{r}\,\leq\,\widetilde{b}_{1}\,<\,\frac{k-r}{r}+\epsilon^{\prime}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\,<\,\widetilde{b}_{2}\,\leq\,\frac{k}{r}\,.

Thus if we assume
H^{2}
is large enough, the line
\widetilde{\ell}
intersects
\Gamma
at two points which are arbitrary close to
\Pi(\mathsf{v})
and
\Pi(\mathsf{v}(-H))
. Consider the line
\ell^{*}
as in Definition
3.1
. We set

(39)

\epsilon^{\prime}=b_{1}^{*}-\frac{k-r}{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\epsilon=\frac{k}{r}-b_{2}^{*}\,.

Then the line
\ell^{*}
lies below the parallel line
\widetilde{\ell}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The origin point
(0,0)
lies below
\ell^{*}
and

(40)

\frac{k-r}{r}<b_{1}^{*}<0<b_{2}^{*}<\frac{k}{r}.

Proof.

The equation of
\ell^{*}
is given by
w=H^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)b+\alpha-1
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. To prove the first claim we need to show
\alpha>1
. We know the intersection points of
\ell^{*}
with
\Gamma
satisfies

b_{2}^{*}-b_{1}^{*}=\frac{2\sqrt{(H^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+2\alpha(H^{2}+2)}}{H^{2}+2}\geq\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}(r+1)}-\frac{k-r}{r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}(r+1)}

This implies for
g\gg 0
, we have

(41)

2\alpha\geq(H^{2}+2)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}(r+1)}\right)^{2}-\frac{(H^{2})^{2}}{H^{2}+2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}>2.

The second claim (
40
) follows from (
38
) and the point that by the choice of
\epsilon
and
\epsilon^{\prime}
as in (
39
), we know
\widetilde{\ell}
lies above
\ell^{*}
. ∎

The above arguments completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
The final step is to prove Lemma
3.3
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

The equation of the line
\ell^{*}
is given by
w=H^{2}(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2})b+\alpha-1
where
\alpha=(b_{2}^{*})^{2}\left(\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1\right)-H^{2}(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2})b_{2}^{*}
and equation of the line
\ell_{1}
is given by
w=\frac{r}{k}w_{1}b
. We know
b_{2}^{*}=\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon
, so

\alpha=\left(\frac{H^{2}}{2}+1\right)\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)^{2}-H^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right).

Thus when
H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, we know

\alpha\ \rightarrow\ \frac{H^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon+1\right)

and so

(42)

\frac{r}{k}w_{1}\ \rightarrow\ H^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{H^{2}r}{2k}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon+1\right).

The line
\ell_{1}
intersects the vertical line
b=\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}
at a point with
w=\frac{r}{k}w_{1}(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)})
. We claim if
g\gg 0
, this point lies in
U
, i.e.

(43)

\frac{r}{k}w_{1}>\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right)\frac{1}{\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}}\,.

The limit of the right hand side when
H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
is
\frac{H^{2}}{2}(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)})
, so (
42
) shows that (
43
) holds if

\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon+1\right)>\frac{k}{r}\left(1-\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right).

This is equivalent to

\epsilon-\epsilon^{2}<\frac{k}{r^{2}(r-1)}

which is satisfied by our assumption on
\epsilon=\frac{k}{r}-b_{2}^{*}
in Definition
3.1
.

Similarly, we have

w_{2}=H^{2}\frac{k-r}{r}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\alpha-1

We require

(44)

\frac{r}{k-r}w_{2}<\Gamma\left(\frac{k-r}{r}+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\right)\frac{1}{\frac{k-r}{r}+\frac{1}{r(r-1)}}\,.

The limit of the left hand side is

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{H^{2}\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r}{k-r}w_{2}=\

\displaystyle H^{2}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{H^{2}r}{2(k-r)}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon+1\right)

and the limit of the right hand side is
\frac{H^{2}}{2}(\frac{k-r}{r}+\frac{1}{r(r-1)})
. Thus (
44
) holds for
g\gg 0
if

\displaystyle\frac{r}{k-r}\left(\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon\right)\left(-\frac{k}{r}-\epsilon+1\right)<\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{k}{r}.

This is equivalent to

\epsilon-\epsilon^{2}<\frac{r-k}{r^{2}(r-1)}

which holds again by our assumptions on
\epsilon=\frac{k}{r}-b_{2}^{*}
.
∎

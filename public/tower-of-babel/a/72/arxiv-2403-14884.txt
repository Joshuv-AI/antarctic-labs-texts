Upper bounds on the dimension of the Schur 𝖫𝗂𝖾 \mathsf{Lie} -multiplier of 𝖫𝗂𝖾 \mathsf{Lie} -nilpotent Leibniz n n -algebras
N.G. Bell Bogmis, G.R. Biyogmam, H. Safa, C. Tcheka

Abstract




Abstract.

The Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of Leibniz algebras is the Schur multiplier of Leibniz algebras defined relative to the Liezation functor.
In this paper, we study upper bounds for the dimension of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz
n
-algebras and the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of its
\mathsf{Lie}
-central factor. The upper bound obtained is associated to both the sequences of central binomial coefficients and the sum of the numbers located in the rhombus part of Pascal’s triangle.
Also, the pattern of counting the number of
\mathsf{Lie}
-brackets of a particular Leibniz
n
-algebra leads us
to a new property of Pascal’s triangle.
Moreover, we discuss some results which improve the existing upper bound published in
[
23
]
for
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras with
d
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator. In particular, it is shown that
if
\mathfrak{q}
is an
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
2
-algebra with one-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator, then

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)-1.

2010 MSC:
17A32, 17B55, 18B99.

Key words:
Leibniz
n
-algebra,
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotency, Pascal’s triangle, Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier.

1
Introduction

The notion of Schur multiplier was introduced by Issai Schur
[
24
]
in 1904 in his study of projective representations of a group. The upper bound of the Schur multiplier plays an important role in the classification of
p
-groups
[
15
]

and the characterization of nilpotent Lie algebras
[
19
]
. So, finding the best possible upper bound of the dimension of the Schur multiplier has been a focus of research in the last few years. Leibniz algebras first appeared in works published in 1965 by Blokh
[
5
]
, and were independently rediscovered by Jean-Louis Loday
[
16
,
17
]
in his study of periodicity phenomena in algebraic
K
-theory. Leibniz algebras are essentially generalization of Lie algebras. Recently, some papers
[
2
,
3
,
20
,
23
]
have considered studying notions of Leibniz algebras relative to the
Liezation functor

(-)_{\mathsf{Lie}}:{\sf Leib}\to{\sf Lie}
which assigns the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=\mathfrak{q}/\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{q})
to a given Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{q}
, where
\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{q})=\langle[x,x]|\ x\in\mathfrak{q}\rangle
.
Translating properties concerning central extensions and commutators from the absolute case to the relative one has yielded several notions such as the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier, which was first studied on Leibniz algebras in
[
6
]
and was used to characterize several notions such as
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotency and

\mathsf{Lie}
-stem covers. Leibniz
n
-algebras were introduced by Casas, Loday and Pirashvili
[
9
]
as a generalization of Leibniz algebras to
n
-ary algebras. They also generalize the concept of Filippov algebras
[
13
]
introduced by Filippov in 1987. In this paper, we investigate some results obtained in Leibniz algebras and Filippov algebras to Leibniz
n
-algebras. Our main purpose is to study the upper bound of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras which can be useful in classifying both nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras
[
17
]
and nilpotent Filippov algebras. Note that the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier can be used to provide examples in which properties on Leibniz algebras cannot be extended to Leibniz
n
-algebras. For instance, the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a Leibniz algebra is a Lie algebra.
In fact, a Leibniz algebra is
\mathsf{Lie}
-abelian if and only if it is a Lie algebra.
But, this is not true for Leibniz
n
-algebras with
n\geq 3,
as discussed in
[
23
, Remark 2.1]
.

The remaining of the paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we present some preliminaries and generalities, which include the definition of the notion of Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a Leibniz
n
-algebra, and some immediate results on its dimension. In Section
3
, we provide the main results of the paper. In particular, we prove that the dimension of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz
n
-algebra differs from the dimension of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of its
\mathsf{Lie}
-central factor by no more than the number of
\mathsf{Lie}
-brackets of
a particular Leibniz
n
-algebra. The upper bound obtained is associated to the sequences of central binomial coefficients and the sum of the numbers located in the rhombus part of Pascal’s triangle. Moreover, the way the
\mathsf{Lie}
-brackets are counted yields
the following relationship between central binomial coefficients and triangular numbers:

{2n\choose n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{2n-r-1-i\choose n-r-1}{i+r\choose r},

for every
1\leq r\leq n-1
, which is actually a new property of Pascal’s triangle (Remark
3.7
).
Next, we discuss some inequalities on the dimension of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras.
As a result, it is shown that for an
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
whose
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator is of dimension

d
, its
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier dimension is bounded as

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq{m-d+n-1\choose n}+d{m-d+n-2\choose n-1}-d,

(1.1)

which improves
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{n-1}\choose{i-1}}{m\choose i}-d
, given in
[
23
]
.
Furthermore, using another technique we prove that if
\mathfrak{q}
is an
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent
Leibniz
2
-algebra with one-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator, then

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)-1,

which improves the upper bound (
1.1
) (when
n=2
and
d=1
),
and also the upper bound
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)-d
, obtained in
[
23
]
(when
d=1
).

2
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
n\geq 2
is a fixed integer and all Leibniz
n
-algebras are
considered over a fixed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.
Recall that a Leibniz algebra
[
16
,
17
]
is a vector space
\mathfrak{q}
equipped with a bilinear map
[-,-]:\mathfrak{q}\times\mathfrak{q}\to\mathfrak{q}
, usually called the Leibniz bracket of
{\mathfrak{q}}
, satisfying the Leibniz identity

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]-[[x,z],y],

for every
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{q}
.
A Leibniz
n
-algebra
[
9
]
is a vector space
\mathfrak{q}
together with an

n
-linear map
[-,\ldots,-]:\mathfrak{q}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{q}\to\mathfrak{q}
,
satisfying the following identity:

[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},[x_{i},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}],x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}],

for all
x_{i},y_{j}\in\mathfrak{q}
,
1\leq i\leq n
and
2\leq j\leq n
.
A subspace
\mathfrak{h}
of a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
is called a subalgebra, if
[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\in\mathfrak{h}
for any
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Also, a subalgebra
\mathfrak{I}
of
\mathfrak{q}
is said to be an ideal, if the brackets
[\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]
,
[\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]
,

\ldots
,
[\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{I}]
are all contained in
\mathfrak{I}
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a Leibniz
n
-algebra. We define the bracket

[-,\ldots,-]_{\mathsf{Lie}}:\mathfrak{q}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{q}\to\mathfrak{q}
as

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}[x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}],

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group
of degree
n
. In other words

\displaystyle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}

\displaystyle=[(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\ldots,(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})]

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i_{j}\leq n}[x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{j}},\ldots,x_{i_{j}},\ldots,x_{i_{n}}].

Note that the above sigma is the sum of all
possible brackets in which
at least
two arguments coincide. More precisely, this sum
is
\sum_{\theta}[x_{\theta(1)},\ldots,x_{\theta(n)}]

where
\theta
rides over the set of all non-injective maps
\theta:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}
(see
[
23
,
22
]
for more details).
In this approach, we actually consider the relative notion of commutator when the semi-abelian category
[
10
]

is the category
{\sf{}_{n}Leib}
of Leibniz

n
-algebras and its Birkhoff subcategory
[
6
]
is
{\sf{}_{n}Lie},
the category of
n
-Lie algebras, together with the ”Liezation-like” functor

(-)_{\mathsf{Lie}}:{\sf{}_{n}Leib}\to{\sf{}_{n}Lie}
which assigns the
n
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{q}/{\sf{}_{n}Leib(\mathfrak{q})}
to a given Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q},
where

{\sf{}_{n}Leib(\mathfrak{q})}=\left\langle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n}]~|~\exists i,j:x_{i}=x_{j}~\mbox{with}~x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{q}\right\rangle.

The
\mathsf{Lie}
-center and
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator of a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
are defined as

Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\{x\in\mathfrak{q}|\ [x,\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}=0\}
and

\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}=[\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}=\langle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}|\ x_{i}\in\mathfrak{q}\rangle
, repectively. One may easily check that these are ideals of
\mathfrak{q}

(see
[
22
, Remark 2.1]
).
A Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-abelian if
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}=0
or equivalently
Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{q}
.
Also,
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent of class
c
if
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c+1}=0
and
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\not=0
, where
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{1}=\mathfrak{q}
,
and
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=[\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i-1},\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}
for
i\geq 2
.

Remark 2.1

In
[
23
]
the
\mathsf{Lie}
-commutator
[\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{n}
, since
we did not deal with the concept of
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotency there.

Let
0\rightarrow\mathfrak{r}\rightarrow\mathfrak{f}\rightarrow\mathfrak{q}\rightarrow 0
be a free presentation of a Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}

(see
[
7
]
). Following
[
23
]
the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier
of
\mathfrak{q}
is defined as

\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\dfrac{\mathfrak{r}\cap\mathfrak{f}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}{[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}.

Clearly, the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier is independent of
the chosen free presentation of
\mathfrak{q}
.
If
n=2
, then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=(\mathfrak{r}\cap[\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}})/[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}
is the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a Leibniz algebra
given in
[
6
]
.
Three other generalizations of the Schur multiplier, namely the
c
-nilpotent Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a Leibniz algebra,
the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of a pair of Leibniz algebras, and the Schur multiplier of a pair of Leibniz algebras
have already been studied in
[
3
,
20
,
4
]
, respectively.

The following results are needed in the next section.

Lemma 2.2

[
23
, Corollary 4.3]

Let
\mathfrak{q}\cong\mathfrak{f}/\mathfrak{r}
be a finite dimensional Leibniz
n
-algebra,

\mathfrak{I}
be an ideal of
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{s}
be an ideal of
\mathfrak{f}
such that
\mathfrak{I}\cong\mathfrak{s}/\mathfrak{r}
. Then

(i)

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{I}})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})+\dim\dfrac{\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}{[\mathfrak{I},\mathfrak{q},\ldots,\mathfrak{q}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}
,

(ii)

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})+\dim(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})=\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{I}})+\dim\dfrac{[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}{[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}
,

(iii)

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})+\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}=\dim\dfrac{\mathfrak{f}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}{[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}
,

(iv)

if
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
, then

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})+\dim(\mathfrak{I}\cap\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{I}})+\dim\big(\mathfrak{I}\otimes^{n-1}\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{{}_{n}{\mathsf{Leib}(\mathfrak{q})}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{I}})+\dim\big(\mathfrak{I}\otimes^{n-1}\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}\big),

where
\mathfrak{I}\otimes^{n-1}\bar{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathfrak{I}\otimes\bar{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\bar{\mathfrak{q}}
including
(n-1)
-times
\bar{\mathfrak{q}}
.

Theorem 2.3

[
23
, Corollary 4.12]

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be an
m
-dimensional Leibniz
n
-algebra. Then

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{n-1}\choose{i-1}}{m\choose i}-\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}.

Theorem 2.4

[
23
, Corollary 4.10]

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be an
m
-dimensional Leibniz
2
-algebra. Then

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)-\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}.

Also,
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)
if and only if
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-abelian.

3
Main results

This section is devoted to obtain some inequalities on the dimension of the Schur
\mathsf{Lie}
-multiplier of
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras.

An
m
-dimensional Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{q}
is said to be
filiform
if

\dim\mathfrak{q}^{i}=m-i
for every
2\leq i\leq m
,
and is called
nulfiliform
if
\dim\mathfrak{q}^{i}=m+1-i
for every
1\leq i\leq m+1
,
where
\mathfrak{q}^{i}
is the
i
th term of the lower central series of
\mathfrak{q}
(see
[
1
]
for more details).
The first definition agrees with the notion of filiform Lie algebras
[
25
]
.
Following
[
20
]
, an
m
-dimensional (non-Lie) Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{q}
is called
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform if

\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=m-i
for every
2\leq i\leq m
, and is called
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent of maximal class
if

\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=m+1-i
for every
1\leq i\leq m+1
.

Definition 3.1

An
m
-dimensional Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
(
m\geq n
) is called
filiform (resp.
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform) if
\dim\mathfrak{q}^{i}=m-n+2-i
(resp.
\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=m-n+2-i
) for every
2\leq i\leq m-n+2
.

Note that the notion of filiform Leibniz
n
-algebras agrees with the definition of filiform
n
-Lie algebras discussed in
[
14
,
21
]
.
It is easy to see that a filiform (resp.
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform) Leibniz
n
-algebra
is nilpotent (resp.
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent) of class
m-n+1
. In
n
-Lie algebras, this nilpotency class is maximal (
[
21
, Example 3.12]
),
but if
\mathfrak{q}
is a Leibniz
n
-algebra (which is not an
n
-Lie algebra), then
m-n+1
is not the maximal (
\mathsf{Lie}
-)nilpotency class of

\mathfrak{q}
(see also
[
20
, Remark 3.2]
). In fact, the concept of ”maximal class” for Leibniz
n
-algebras should be defined similar to the case where
n=2
.

Definition 3.2

An
m
-dimensional Leibniz
n
-algebra
\mathfrak{q}
is said to be nilpotent (resp.
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent) of maximal class, if

\dim\mathfrak{q}^{i}=m+1-i
(resp.
\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=m+1-i
) for every
1\leq i\leq m+1
.

Example 3.3

It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{q}
with a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
(
m\geq 2
)
and non-zero multiplications
[x_{i},x_{1},x_{1}]=x_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq m-1
, is a Leibniz 3-algebra.
Now since

[[[x_{1},\underbrace{x_{1},x_{1}],x_{1},x_{1}],\ldots,x_{1},x_{1}]}_{m-\text{times}\ x_{1},x_{1}}=[[x_{2},x_{1},x_{1}],\ldots,x_{1},x_{1}]=\cdots=[x_{m},x_{1},x_{1}]=0

and

[[[x_{1},\underbrace{x_{1},x_{1}],x_{1},x_{1}],\ldots,x_{1},x_{1}]}_{(m-1)-\text{times}\ x_{1},x_{1}}=[[x_{2},x_{1},x_{1}],\ldots,x_{1},x_{1}]=\cdots=x_{m}\not=0,

we get
\mathfrak{q}^{m+1}=0
and hence
\mathfrak{q}
is nilpotent of class
m
. Also,
\{x_{i},\ldots,x_{m}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{q}^{i}
and hence

\dim\mathfrak{q}^{i}=m+1-i
, for every
1\leq i\leq m+1
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{q}
is nilpotent of maximal class.
One may easily check that
\mathfrak{q}
is also
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent of maximal class.
Note that

[x_{i},x_{1},x_{1}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}=2[x_{i},x_{1},x_{1}]+2[x_{1},x_{i},x_{1}]+2[x_{1},x_{1},x_{i}]=2x_{i+1},

and hence

\{x_{i},\ldots,x_{m}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}
, as well.

The following result is a generalization of
[
20
, Theorem 3.4]
(when
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{q}
), and
[
3
, Corollary 4.7]
(when
c=1
). See also
[
20
, Remark 3.2]
.

Theorem 3.4

(i)
If
\mathfrak{q}
is an
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz
n
-algebra
(m>n)
,
then

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})})+k,

such that
-1\leq k\leq{2n-2\choose n-1}-1
.

(ii)
If
\mathfrak{q}
is a finite dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebra of maximal class,
then the above equality holds for
-1\leq k\leq 0
.

Proof.

(i) Since
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform, then it is
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent of class
m-n+1
,
\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n+1}=1

and
0\not=\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n+1}\subseteq Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
. Consider
0\not=x\in\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n+1}
,
x=[y,z_{2},z_{3},\ldots,z_{n}]
with

y\in\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n}
and

z_{i}\in\mathfrak{q}
,
2\leq i\leq n
. Moreover
\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n}=2
, thus one has
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n+1}\subseteq Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n}

since
y\not\in Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
. Hence
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{m-n+1}=Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
and
\dim(\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}\cap Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q}))=\dim Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=1
.
Now by considering Lemma
2.2
(i) in which
\mathfrak{I}=Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
, we have

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})})-1\leq\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q}).

On the other hand, using Lemma
2.2
(ii) we get

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}}{Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})})+\dim\dfrac{[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}{[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}-1.

It is easy to see that

\psi:Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\times\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}\times\cdots\times\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}\longrightarrow\frac{[\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}{[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}}

given by
\psi(z,\bar{q}_{1},\ldots,\bar{q}_{n-1})=[s,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}+[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}
where

\pi(s)=z
and
\pi(f_{i})=q_{i}
, is a surjective
multilinear map (
\pi:\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{q}
is the canonical epimorphism). Since

\dim Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=1
,
\dim(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})=n
, and by definition of the
\mathsf{Lie}
-bracket

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+1},x_{i},\ldots,x_{n}]_{\mathsf{Lie}},

thus
it is enough to count only the number of
\mathsf{Lie}
-brackets of the form

[s,f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\ldots,f_{i_{n-1}}]_{\mathsf{Lie}}
where

Also, the pattern of counting the number of
\mathsf{Lie}
-brackets led us to a relationship between central binomial coefficients and triangular numbers.

Triangular numbers
are created by adding up the natural numbers:

T_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}={n+1\choose 2}.

If we add up triangular numbers, we get the
tetrahedral numbers
(or triangular pyramidal numbers) which can be described by an analogous formula:

H_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{6}={n+2\choose 3}.

This pattern progresses to higher dimensions as follows:

P_{n}^{(1)}=n
,
P_{n}^{(2)}=T_{n}
,
P_{n}^{(3)}=H_{n}
and

P_{n}^{(r)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{i}^{(r-1)}=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+r-1)}{r!}={n+r-1\choose r}.

The next result is the relationship mentioned above.

Proposition 3.6

For every
1\leq r\leq n-1

{2n\choose n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}{2n-r-1-i\choose n-r-1}P_{i+1}^{(r)}.

(3.2)

Proof.
Recall that the number of non-decreasing sequences of length
s

from
\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
is
{t+s-1\choose s}
.
So the number of non-decreasing sequences of length
n

from
\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
is
{2n\choose n}
.
On the other hand, consider the following sequence

\underbrace{a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{n-r}}}_{(I)},\underbrace{a_{i_{n-r+1}},\ldots,a_{i_{n-1}},a_{i_{n}}}_{(II)},

where
i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}
and
1\leq i_{j}\leq n+1
.
Suppose that in part (I) we put only 1, so in part (II)
there are
{n+r\choose r}
sequences as
s=r
and
t=n+1
. Thus we have
{n+r\choose r}=P_{n+1}^{(r)}
sequences in this step.

Now assume that in (I) we put only 1, 2, so in (II)
there are
{n+r-1\choose r}
sequences as
s=r
and
t=n
(since here we cannot use 1, hence
t\not=n+1
).
Also in (I) we have
{n-r+1\choose n-r}-1
sequences, since

s=n-r
,
t=2
, and that
(-1)
is because of the repetitive sequence

\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{(n-r)-times}

in the previous step. By Pascal’s identity we have

{n-r+1\choose n-r}-1={n-r+1\choose n-r}-{n-r\choose n-r}={n-r\choose n-r-1}.

Therefore, there are
{n-r\choose n-r-1}P_{n}^{(r)}
sequences in this step.

In general,
suppose that in (I) we put only from
\{1,2,\ldots,n-i+1\ |\ 0\leq i\leq n\}
, so in (II)
there are
{i+r\choose r}
sequences since
s=r
and
t=n+1-(n-i)=i+1
(as we are not allowed to use
1,2\ldots,n-i
).
Also in (I) we have

{2n-r-i\choose n-r}-{2n-r-1-i\choose n-r}={2n-r-1-i\choose n-r-1}

sequences since

s=n-r
,
t=n-i+1
, and we need again to remove
{2n-r-1-i\choose n-r}
repetitive sequences in the previous step.
Thus we have

{2n-r-1-i\choose n-r-1}P_{i+1}^{(r)}
sequences in this step, which completes the proof.
\blacksquare

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+(\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}-1)\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle+

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\min\bigg\{\frac{m}{2}{2n-2\choose n-1}-1,\ {2n-1\choose n}+{2n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg\}.

Using the Pascal’s identity
{2n-1\choose n}={2n-2\choose n}+{2n-2\choose n-1}
,
one can easily check that if
m\leq 5
, the first upper bound is less than or equal to the other one
(equality occurs only when
m=5
and
n=2
), and if
m\geq 6
, the second one
is smaller.

(ii) It follows from Definition
3.2
and Corollary
3.11
.
\blacksquare

In the case
n=2
, we have:

Corollary 3.19

If
\mathfrak{q}
is an
m
-dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz algebra
(m>2)
,
then

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m-1&\ if\ m\leq 5,\\
4&\ if\ m\geq 6.\end{array}\right.

Also if
m=2
, then
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=3
.

Proof.

If
m=2
, then
\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-abelian (Remark
3.5
), and by Theorem
2.4
we get
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=3
.
\blacksquare

The following example shows that the above corollary provides a better upper bound than the previous results for
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz algebras.
Note that for a
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz algebra of dimension
3
(in this case
\dim\mathfrak{q}^{2}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=1
), the above corollary and Corollary
3.16

give the same upper bound
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq 2
. According to Definition
3.1
, for
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform Leibniz algebras if
m\geq 4
, then
\dim\mathfrak{q}^{2}_{\mathsf{Lie}}\geq 2
.
So, the bound
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)-1

in Corollary
3.16
is still the best upper bound ever presented, if
\mathfrak{q}
is a
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz algebra with
\dim\mathfrak{q}^{2}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=1
.

Example 3.20

Consider the 4-dimensional Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{q}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\rangle
with non-zero brackets

[x_{1},x_{1}]=x_{3}
,
[x_{1},x_{2}]=x_{4}
,
[x_{2},x_{1}]=x_{3}
and
[x_{3},x_{1}]=x_{4}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
).
One can easily check that
\mathfrak{q}^{2}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=\langle x_{3},x_{4}\rangle
,
\mathfrak{q}^{3}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=\langle x_{4}\rangle
and
\mathfrak{q}^{4}_{\mathsf{Lie}}=0
, i.e.

\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathsf{Lie}
-filiform. So, by the above corollary
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq 3
, while using
Corollaries
3.13
or
3.16
,
we get
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq 4
, and by Proposition
3.8
,
\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq 8
.

In Theorems
3.10
and
3.14
, induction is used on the dimension of
\mathfrak{q}
while in the next, following
[
26
]
,
it is used on the
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotency class of
\mathfrak{q}
. Moreover, the following result improves Theorem
3.10
.

Theorem 3.21

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a finite dimensional
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebra of class
c
with
\dim\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}/Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})}{(\mathfrak{q}/Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q}))_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}}=m
.
Then for every
i\geq 2
and
2\leq j\leq c

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg].

Proof.

If
c=1
or
i>c
, then the result holds.
Assume that
2\leq i\leq c
and
the result holds for
\mathsf{Lie}
-nilpotent Leibniz
n
-algebras of class less than
c
.
Clearly,
0\not=\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\subseteq Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})
,
(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c})_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}=\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}
and

Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\subseteq Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c})
. Put
A=\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}{Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c})}
,

B=\mathfrak{q}/Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})=\dfrac{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}{Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}
and
\dim(A/A_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})=m^{\prime}
. It is easy to check that
m^{\prime}\leq\dim(B/B_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})=m
.
By induction hypothesis

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}/\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}})+\dim\frac{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}\bigg[{m^{\prime}+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+\dim\frac{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg].

Now, similar to
[
12
, Theorem 3.6]
and the technique used earlier, one can show that

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}})+\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\bigg[{m^{\prime\prime}+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg],

(3.9)

where
m^{\prime\prime}=\dim(\mathfrak{q}/Z^{\mathsf{Lie}}_{c-1}(\mathfrak{q}))
and
Z^{\mathsf{Lie}}_{i}(\mathfrak{q})
is the
i
th term
of the upper
\mathsf{Lie}
-central series of
\mathfrak{q}
(see Definition 10 and Theorem 4 of
[
8
]
).
Since
\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}\subseteq Z^{\mathsf{Lie}}_{c-1}(\mathfrak{q})
, hence

\displaystyle m^{\prime\prime}=\dim(\mathfrak{q}/Z^{\mathsf{Lie}}_{c-1}(\mathfrak{q}))\leq\dim(\mathfrak{q}/(\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2}+Z_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})))=\dim(B/B_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{2})=m.

(3.10)

Thus inequalities (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) imply that

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+\dim\frac{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\bigg[{m^{\prime\prime}+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+\dim\frac{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{c}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{Lie}}(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{i}})+\dim\mathfrak{q}_{\mathsf{Lie}}^{j}\bigg[{m+n-2\choose n-1}-1\bigg],

which completes the proof.
\blacksquare

Variations of GIT quotients and dimer combinatorics for toric compound Du Val singularities
Yusuke Nakajima

Abstract
. A dimer model is a bipartite graph described on the real two-torus, and it gives the quiver as the dual graph. It is known that for any three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularity, there exists a dimer model such that a GIT quotient parametrizing stable representations of the associated quiver is a projective crepant resolution of this singularity for some stability parameter. It is also known that the space of stability parameters has the wall-and-chamber structure, and for any projective crepant resolution of a three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularity can be realized as the GIT quotient associated to a stability parameter contained in some chamber. In this paper, we consider dimer models giving rise to projective crepant resolutions of a toric compound Du Val singularity. We show that sequences of zigzag paths, which are special paths on a dimer model, determine the wall-and-chamber structure of the space of stability parameters. Moreover, we can track the variations of stable representations under wall-crossing using the sequences of zigzag paths.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Moduli descriptions of crepant resolutions of singularities

For some singularities, resolutions of singularities can be described as moduli spaces of certain objects.
For example, a minimal resolution of a two-dimensional Gorenstein quotient singularity
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G
defined by the action of a finite subgroup

G\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
can be given as the
G
-Hilbert scheme
G
-Hilb
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
which is the Hilbert scheme parametrizing certain
G
-invariant subschemes, see
[
IN99
]
.
This result was generalized to three-dimensional Gorenstein quotient singularities by
[
Nak01
]
for the abelian case and by
[
BKR01
]
for arbitrary cases.
Precisely, for a quotient singularity
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
defined by the action of a finite subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
,
a crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
can be given as
G
-Hilb
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
Also,
G
-Hilb
\mathbb{C}^{3}
is described as the moduli space of representations of the McKay quiver of
G
satisfying some stability condition.
The moduli space of representations of a quiver, introduced in
[
Kin94
]
, is defined as the GIT quotient associated to a stability parameter
(see Subsection
3.2
for the detail).
The space
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
of stability parameters associated to a quiver
Q
has the wall-and-chamber structure,
that is, it is decomposed into chambers (open cones in
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
) separated by walls (codimension one faces of the closures of chambers).
The moduli spaces associated to stability parameters contained in the same chamber are isomorphic, but if we take a stability parameter from
other chambers, then it would give a different moduli space.

For a particular choice of stability parameters, the moduli space of representations of the McKay quiver of
G
is
isomorphic to
G
-Hilb
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
On the other hand, a crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
is not unique in general, thus it is natural to expect that
any crepant resolution has a moduli description.
In fact, it was shown in
[
CI04
]
that for any finite abelian subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
,
any projective crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/G
is isomorphic to the moduli space of representations of the McKay quiver of
G
for some stability parameter.
Recently, it was shown in
[
Yam25
]
that the same statement holds for any finite subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})
.
These results can be obtained by observing the variations of moduli spaces under crossing walls.
In particular, it is important to classify walls in
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
according to an effect on moduli spaces.
Along this line, it is also important to detect the precise description of chambers and walls (i.e., detect the complete structure of
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
),
although it would not be necessary to obtain the result of
[
CI04
,
Yam25
]
.
For example, there are some results, e.g.,
[
Cra01
,
MT01
,
Wor20
]
, which observe the descriptions of walls and chambers for some McKay quivers.

Also, there are several generalizations of the result in
[
CI04
]
for other singularities, e.g.,
[
IU16
,
Jun18
,
Wem18
]
.
In particular, it was shown in
[
IU16
]
that any projective crepant resolution of a three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularity can be
described as the moduli space of representations of a quiver associated to a dimer model for some stability parameter (see Theorem
3.2
).
In this paper, we discuss the wall-and-chamber structure and the variations of moduli spaces (projective crepant resolutions) under crossing walls.
for a particular class of three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularities called toric compound Du Val (cDV) singularities.

1.3.
Summary of results

We now summarize the main results of the paper.
A
dimer model

\Gamma
is a bipartite graph described on the real two-torus, which will be introduced in Subsection
2.1
in detail.
As the dual of a dimer model
\Gamma
, we can obtain the quiver
Q_{\Gamma}
with relations, see Subsection

.
For such a quiver
Q\coloneqq Q_{\Gamma}

of stability parameters which takes the form

\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}=\Big\{\theta=(\theta_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}\mid\sum_{v\in Q_{0}}\theta_{v}=0\Big\}

For a stability parameter

, see Subsection

.
Under some conditions, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\underline{1})
is
a projective crepant resolution of a three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularity.
The space
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
has the wall-and-chamber structure, and
the moduli spaces (projective crepant resolutions) associated to stability parameters contained in the same chamber are isomorphic,
whereas a change of stability parameters crossing a wall would cause a change of the associated moduli space.
Precisely, let
C,C^{\prime}
be adjacent chambers separated by a wall

, and consider stability parameters
\theta\in C
,
\theta^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}
.
Then we sometimes have that
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}\not\cong\mathcal{M}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, in which case they are related by a flop and the wall

is called of type I.
Also, if
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}\cong\mathcal{M}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, then although the moduli spaces are isomorphic the parametrized representations of

differ from each other, in which case the wall is either of type 0 or type
{\rm I\hskip-0.7ptI\hskip-0.7ptI}
, see Subsection
3.4
.

The purposes of this paper are to detect the wall-and-chamber structure of
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}

and to observe the variations of projective crepant resolutions under crossing walls in
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
for toric cDV singularities.
As we mentioned, toric cDV singularities are classified into type
cA_{a+b-1}
and
cD_{4}
.
We here consider the case
cA_{n-1}
where
n\coloneqq a+b
.
In this situation, the walls in
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
are either of type I or type
{\rm I\hskip-0.7ptI\hskip-0.7ptI}
.
The wall-and-chamber structure of
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
and the types of walls can be determined by the combinatorics of the associated dimer models.
To state our theorem, we consider a special class of paths on a dimer model called

(see Definition

).
A zigzag path

can be considered as an element in the homology group

, in which we denote by

Also, for any lattice polygon

there exists a consistent dimer model

such that the zigzag paths on

one-to-one correspond to

by identifying zigzag paths with elements in

(see Subsection

).
Thus, we can consider a consistent dimer model giving the outer normal vectors of the polygon
\Delta(a,b)
.
In general, such a dimer model is not unique, thus we choose one of them and denote it by
\Gamma_{a,b}
.
Then we consider the set
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
of zigzag paths on
\Gamma_{a,b}
such that
[u_{k}]
is either
(0,-1)
or
(0,1)
for

,
and

,

.
Note that this set of zigzag paths is determined uniquely by the correspondence between zigzag paths on
\Gamma_{a,b}
and the outer normal vectors of

We rearrange

if necessary, and construct the sequence

Also, we define a total order

1.4.
The structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we prepare some notation concerning dimer models,
and discuss toric rings (singularities) arising from dimer models.
In Section
3
, we consider representations of the quiver obtained as the dual of a dimer model,
and review some basic facts concerning moduli spaces of stable representations obtained as GIT quotients,
which are projective crepant resolutions of a three-dimensional Gorenstein toric singularity.
Since the moduli space is a smooth toric variety, it can also be understood by using a toric fan.
Thus, we review a correspondence among stable representations, cones in a toric fan, and torus orbits in a toric variety.
In particular, perfect matchings of a dimer model explain this correspondence in terms of dimer models.
In Section
4
, we observe some properties of “boundary” perfect matchings which we will use in later sections.
In Section
5
, we focus our attention on toric cDV singularities of type
cA_{a+b-1}
,
and explain how to construct a dimer model giving rise to a projective crepant resolution of this singularity.
Section
6
is dedicated to show our main theorems.
First, for the dimer model constructed in Section
5
, we prepare some notions such as
sequences of zigzag paths, fundamental hexagons, and jigsaw pieces, which are the main ingredients of our proof.
Then we show our main results concerning the wall-and-chamber structure of the space of stability parameters and
the variations of projective crepant resolutions,
see Theorems
6.11
,
6.12
and Corollary
6.13
.
These results enable us to observe variations of stable representations and torus orbits under wall-crossings.
Thus, we study such variations in Section
7
.
In Section

, we focus on the toric cDV singularity of type

,
and show some results similar to the ones for type

2.1.
Dimer models

In this subsection, we introduce dimer models and related notions which are originally derived from theoretical physics (e.g.,

A

Definition 2.2

(see

A dimer model is said to be (

(1)

(2)

(3)

That is, if a pair of zigzag paths

a_{1}

a_{2}

Note that any edge of a dimer model is contained in at most two zigzag paths.
By the condition (2) in Definition

,

We then set another sequence

Here, a

\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\mathsf{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}

We denote the standard inner product by

For the vertices

\langle-,-\rangle

(

Precisely,

\Delta_{R}

of

.
By Theorem

\Gamma

3.2.
Stability parameters and crepant resolutions

In this subsection, we introduce moduli spaces parametrizing quiver representations satisfying a certain stability condition.

First, we consider the weight space

\Theta(Q)\coloneqq\Big\{\theta=(\theta_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\mid\sum_{v\in Q_{0}}\theta_{v}=0\Big\}

and let
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\Theta(Q)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.
We call an element
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
a
stability parameter
.

Let
M
be a representation of
(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q})
of dimension vector
\underline{1}
.
For a subrepresentation
N
of
M
, we define
\theta(N)\coloneqq\sum_{v\in Q_{0}}\theta_{v}(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}N_{v})
, and hence
\theta(M)=0
in particular.
For a stability parameter
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
, we introduce
\theta
-stable representations as follows.

Definition 3.1

(see
[
Kin94
]
)
.

Let
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
.
We say that a representation
M
is
\theta
-semistable
if
\theta(N)\geq 0
for any subrepresentation
N
of
M
, and

M
is
\theta
-stable
if
\theta(N)>0
for any non-zero proper subrepresentation
N
of
M
.
Then, we say that
\theta
is
generic
if every
\theta
-semistable representation is
\theta
-stable.

By
[
Kin94
, Proposition 5.2]
, for any
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
, one can construct the coarse moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
of
S
-equivalence classes of
\theta
-semistable representations of dimension vector
\underline{1}

(i.e.,
\theta
-semistable representations whose Jordan–Hölder filtrations have the same composition factors).
By
[
Kin94
, Proposition 5.3]
, for a generic parameter
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
,
one can construct the fine module space
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
parametrizing isomorphism classes of
\theta
-stable representations of dimension vector
\underline{1}
as the GIT (geometric invariant theory) quotient.
Moreover, this moduli space gives a crepant resolution as follows.

Theorem 3.2

(see
[
IU08
, Theorem 6.3 and 6.4]
,
[
IU16
, Corollary 1.2]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a consistent dimer model, and
Q
be the associated quiver.
Let
R
be the three-dimensional Gorenstein toric ring associated to
\Gamma
.
Then, for a generic parameter
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
is a smooth toric Calabi-Yau threefold
and a projective crepant resolution of
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Moreover, any projective crepant resolution of
\operatorname{Spec}R
can be obtained as the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
for some generic parameter
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

In the following, we let
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q},\underline{1})
for simplicity.
Let
G
be the subset of isomorphism classes of representations of
(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q})
defined as follows:

G\coloneqq\Big\{\big[((M_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}},(\varphi_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})\big]\mid\varphi_{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,\,\text{for any $a\in Q_{1}$}\Big\}.

This has the structure of an algebraic torus with a multiplication defined as

\big[((M_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}},(\varphi_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})\big]\cdot\big[((M_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}},(\varphi^{\prime}_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})\big]=\big[((M_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}},(\varphi_{a}\varphi^{\prime}_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})\big].

(3.1)

Since any representation in
G
has no proper subrepresentation, it is
\theta
-stable for any
\theta
,
and hence
G
is contained in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\theta}
for any
\theta
.
If
\theta
is generic, then
G
is the open dense torus contained in the toric variety
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}

and
G
acts on
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
by the multiplication (
3.1
), see
[
IU08
]
for more details.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
is a fine moduli space for a generic parameter
\theta\in\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
,
it carries a universal family

\mathcal{T}_{\theta}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{v},

of
\theta
-stable
A_{Q}
-modules of dimension vector
\underline{1}
, called a
tautological bundle
of
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
,
where
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is a line bundle on
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
for any
v\in Q_{0}
.
This bundle gives rise to a morphism
\phi:A_{Q}\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{M}_{\theta}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta})
of algebras.
For each point

, the fiber of the bundle map
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\theta}
at

is
the
\theta
-stable representation
M_{y}\coloneqq((M_{v})_{v\in Q_{0}},(\varphi_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})
of
(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q})
of dimension vector
\underline{1}
,
where
\varphi_{a}:M_{\mathsf{tl}(a)}\rightarrow M_{\mathsf{hd}(a)}
is obtained by restricting the map
\phi(a):\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{tl}(a)}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{hd}(a)}

to the fiber at

.
In our situation,
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
is a tilting bundle and

is an isomorphism (see
[
IU15
, Theorem 1.4]
).

In general, there is an ambiguity of a choice of
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
, that is, by tensoring a line bundle to
\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\theta}
.
Thus, we fix a vertex of

, which we denote by

\mathcal{L}_{0}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}_{\theta}}
.
On the other hand, since

is a tilting bundle, it induces an equivalence
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathsf{coh}\,\mathcal{M}_{\theta})\cong\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathsf{mod}\,\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{M}_{\theta}}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta}))

[

]

By

(

[

]

This was also proved in

]
using another method.

Since

is a smooth toric variety for a generic parameter

Namely, there is a certain smooth subdivision

•

\Sigma_{\theta}
with lattice points in the triangulation of

On the other hand, since each point

corresponds to the

As we will see in Proposition

\theta

3.3.

Note that any dimer model does not necessarily have a perfect matching, but any consistent dimer model has a perfect matching

3.4.
Wall-and-chamber structures

It is known that the space
\Theta(Q)_{\mathbb{R}}
of stability parameters has a

.
Namely, we define an equivalence relation on the set of generic parameters so that
\theta\sim\theta^{\prime}

if and only if any
\theta
-stable representation of
(Q,\mathcal{J}_{Q})

\theta^{\prime}

[

Here, a

Note that any generic parameter lies on some chamber (see
[
IU16

The moduli space

\theta

Let

separated by a wall

.
We choose generic parameters

and

We also choose a stability parameter

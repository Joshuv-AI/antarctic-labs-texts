Probabilities of large values for sums of i.i.d. non-negative random variables with regular tail of index − 1 -1
Matthias Birkner and Linglong Yuan

Abstract
Let ξ 1 , ξ 2 , … \xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots be i.i.d. non-negative random variables whose tail varies regularly with index − 1 -1 , let S n S_{n} be the sum and M n M_{n} the largest of the first n n values. We clarify for which sequences x n → ∞ x_{n}\to\infty we have ℙ ⁡ ( S n ≥ x n ) ∼ ℙ ⁡ ( M n ≥ x n ) \mathbb{P}(S_{n}\geq x_{n})\sim\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq x_{n}) as n → ∞ n\to\infty . Outside this regime, the typical size of S n S_{n} conditioned on exceeding x n x_{n} is not completely determined by the largest summand and we provide an appropriate correction term which involves the integrated tail of ξ 1 \xi_{1} . Keywords: Sum and maximum of independent heavy tailed random variables, conditional limit theorem, large deviations, heavy-tailed offspring distribution MSC 2020 subject classification: 60F10, 60E07, 92D10

Motivation and main results

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) real-valued random variables, put

S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i},\quad M_{n}:=\max\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}.

There is a considerable body of literature analysing

\overline{F}_{n}(x):=\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\geq x),\text{ as }n\to\infty,x\to\infty,

(1)

usually assuming that the right tail of
\xi_{1}
,

\overline{F}(t):=\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}\geq t),

(2)

and/or the left tail,
F(-t):=\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}\leq-t)
, are/is regularly varying in
t
as
t\to\infty
. See
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
and references therein. Additionally, many variations of (
1
) have been considered, such as letting the number
n
be random, see for instance
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
.

A natural class of models in mathematical population genetics, which can describe the evolution
of a population of fixed size
n
and incorporate substantial variation of the individuals’ reproductive
success in every generation (in an elegantly “tuneable” manner), describes reproduction as a two-step
procedure as follows: For
i=1,2,\dots,n
let individual
i
produce
\xi_{i}
(
\geq 0
) potential offspring or “juveniles”,
where the
\xi_{i}
are i.i.d. as above. Then, sample
n
times without replacement from this pool of juveniles to form the next adult generation. See, for example,
[
15
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.
When analysing such a model, it is important to understand (
1
) and the contribution to
S_{n}
from different individuals when
S_{n}
is large. In this application, the
\xi_{i}
’s take nonnegative integers.
Motivated by an application in population genetics
[
7
]
, in this paper we consider the case that
\xi_{i}
’s are i.i.d. and take nonnegative real values such that the tail (
2
) is regularly varying with index
-1
as
x\to\infty
. In this case, the population models are related to the famous Bolthausen-Sznitman coalescent, see, e.g., the case
a=1
in
[
15
, Section 3]
.

When (
2
) varies regularly with an index different from
-1
, the questions we address here have already been answered, see, e.g.
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. However, no-one we asked seemed aware of those results we needed for the case of index
-1
. The
\xi_{i}
’s we consider belong to the family of subexponential random variables. See (
[
10
]
, section 3.2) for necessary and sufficient conditions for a real-valued random variable to be subexponential.

For subexponential random variables, many researchers have investigated the following problem: Find sequences
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq x_{n}}\left|\frac{\overline{F}_{n}(x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}-1\right|=0.

(3)

Note that
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq x)=1-(1-\overline{F}(x))^{n}\sim n\overline{F}(x)
uniformly in
x\geq x_{n}
if
\overline{F}(x_{n})=o(n^{-1})
. Thus (
3
) has the interpretation that the (sufficiently far out) tail of the sum of i.i.d. random variables is determined by the tail of the largest summand. Interested readers can find results on this topic in
[
12
,
13
,
16
]
. Denisov
et al

[
8
]
provided a unifying approach to find a sufficient condition on
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
for (
3
) to hold. Moreover a local version of (
3
) was proved in
[
8
]
(see
\Delta
-subexponentiality, a notion introduced in
[
2
]
). In this paper we focus on
\overline{F}_{n}(x)
only.

For subexponential random variables, conditional on
S_{n}\geq x
for
x
sufficiently large and satisfying (
3
), Armendáriz and Loulakis
[
1
]
proved that the
n-1
smallest random variables behave like
n-1
i.i.d. random variables of the original distribution. The results of this paper also cover the cases satisfying the local version of (
3
). In the following, we will study the
n-1
smallest variables from a different perspective, namely their contributions to
S_{n}
.

The novelty of this paper is that we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for (
3
) to hold in the case that
\xi_{i}
’s are i.i.d. and nonnegative, with the right tail regularly varying of index
-1.
Furthermore, in this case, we characterise the behaviour of
S_{n}
and
M_{n}
when the threshold
x_{n}
goes to infinity but (
3
) does not hold.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
be i.i.d. nonnegative random variables with the right tail regularly varying of index
-1
:

\overline{F}(x)=x^{-1}\ell(x),\quad x\to\infty,

(4)

where
\ell(x)
is a slowly varying function. Define

G(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\overline{F}(t)dt,\quad x\geq 0.

Then

(a).

The convergence (
3
) holds if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{nG(x_{n})}=\infty.

(5)

(b).

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(x_{n}^{\prime})_{n\geq 1}
be two positive sequences such that

x_{n}\leq x_{n}^{\prime},\quad\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{nG(x_{n})}>0,\quad\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}^{\prime}}{nG(x_{n}^{\prime})}<\infty.

(6)

Then for any positive sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\in(0,\infty)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x_{n}\leq x\leq x_{n}^{\prime}}\left|\frac{\overline{F}_{n}\left(nG(x)+a_{n}x\right)}{n\overline{F}(a_{n}x)}-1\right|=0.

(7)

(c).

If
\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{nG(x_{n})}=0
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\overline{F}_{n}(x_{n})=1.

Remark 1
.

It will follow from the proof of Theorem
1
that the function
\frac{x}{G(x)}
is strictly increasing for
x
larger than a certain number. Thus, the above theorem exhibits the behaviour of
\overline{F}_{n}(x)
for different magnitudes of
x
.

Remark 2
.

1. Observe that (
4
) can be compatible with
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]<\infty
, for example when
\ell(x)=1/\log^{2}(x)
. In this case,
G(x)\to\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]
as
x\to\infty
and
(
5
) simply means that
x_{n}
grows faster than
n
.

2. When
\ell(x)\to c\in(0,\infty)
as
x\to\infty
, we have
G(x)\sim c\log(x)
and the conditions in (
6
) mean that
x_{n},x_{n}^{\prime}
are of order
n\log n
.

Next result shows the contributions of different terms to
S_{n}
.

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be any positive sequence satisfying

\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{nG(x_{n})}>0

(8)

and let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a positive sequence with
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=:a\in(0,\infty)
.

Among all the
i
’s for which
\xi_{i}
is equal to
M_{n}
, we select one uniformly. Denote the chosen index by
i_{*}
and let
\Xi
be the set of indices of the remaining
n-1
terms. Let
\Xi_{k}
be a (possibly random) subset of
\Xi
of size
k
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
, which is drawn independently of the values of
\xi_{i},i\neq i_{*}
. Let
S_{n,k}
be the sum of random variables whose indices are in
\Xi_{k}
. Then the distribution of
S_{n,k}
is independent of the particular set
\Xi_{k}
we choose and for any
\varepsilon>0
as
n\to\infty,

\max_{1\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{n,k}-kG(x_{n})|\geq\varepsilon x_{n}\,\,\,|\,\,M_{n}\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)=o(1),

(9)

and

\max_{1\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{n,k}-kG(x_{n})|\geq\varepsilon x_{n}\,\,\,|\,\,S_{n}-nG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)=o(1).

(10)

We will see in Proposition
1
below that as
n\to\infty,

\mathbb{P}\left(M_{n}\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}-nG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n},\,\,\,M_{n}\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)\sim\mathbb{P}\left(S_{n}-nG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right).

Thus, the two events
\{M_{n}\geq a_{n}x_{n}\}
and
\{S_{n}-nG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n}\}
are almost identical in the above sense. Furthermore, Theorem
2
shows that, conditional on
M_{n}\geq a_{n}x_{n}
or
S_{n}-nG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n}
, the deviation of
S_{n}-nG(x_{n})
from
0
is provided by the maximum variable. In fact, we see from Theorem
2
that
the sum of any subset of size
k
of the other
n-1
variables typically deviates from
kG(x_{n})
on a scale smaller than
x_{n}
.

The phenomenon that the maximum variable contributes substantially to
S_{n}
while the others fluctuate around a certain level, when
S_{n}
is large enough, can also be observed when
\overline{F}(x)
is regularly varying with index strictly less than
-1
and thus the
\xi_{i}
’s have a finite mean. This can be seen in the proof of the main result in
[
14
]
, although the author did not formulate results like (
9
) and (
10
). If the index lies in
(-1,0)
, this phenomenon does not occur, see (
[
15
]
, Lemma 22) for a study in the case of nonnegative random variables.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section

Lemma 1

Let

Proof.

Since

Define

By construction,

2.2

In this section, we investigate the behaviour of

Corollary 1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
,
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1},a
be as defined in Theorem
2
. Then as
n\to\infty

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}-kG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n})=O(n\overline{F}(x_{n})).

Proof.

Define
\xi_{i}^{\prime}:=\xi_{i}{\text{\Large$\mathfrak{1}$}}_{\xi_{i}\leq x_{n}}
for
i\geq 1
and
S_{k}^{\prime}:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i}^{\prime},k\geq 1
. Similarly as in (
2.1
) and (
26
), we have

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]\sim G(x_{n});\quad\mathbb{E}[S_{k}^{\prime}]\sim kG(x_{n}),\forall k=1,2,\ldots,\text{ as }n\to\infty,

and there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
n\geq 1,

\operatorname{var}(\xi_{1}^{\prime})\leq C^{\prime}x_{n}^{2}\overline{F}(x_{n});\quad\operatorname{var}(S^{\prime}_{k})=k\operatorname{var}(\xi_{1}^{\prime})\leq C^{\prime}kx_{n}^{2}\overline{F}(x_{n}),\,\,\forall k=1,2,\ldots.

Note that the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\left|S_{k}^{\prime}-kG(x_{n})\right|\geq a_{n}x_{n}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\left|S_{k}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[S_{k}^{\prime}]\right|\geq a_{n}x_{n}-\left|\mathbb{E}[S_{k}^{\prime}]-kG(x_{n})\right|\Big).

Note also that

\frac{|\mathbb{E}[S_{k}^{\prime}]-kG(x_{n})|}{x_{n}}=k\frac{|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]-G(x_{n})|}{x_{n}}=\frac{kG(x_{n})}{x_{n}}\frac{|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]-G(x_{n})|}{G(x_{n})}\leq\frac{nG(x_{n})}{x_{n}}\frac{|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]-G(x_{n})|}{G(x_{n})}.

The first factor in the last term is bounded, due to (
8
). The second factor converges to
0
, as

, since
\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}^{\prime}]\sim G(x_{n})
and

.
Then for
n
large enough,

\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{k}^{\prime}-kG(x_{n})|\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{k}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[S_{k}^{\prime}]|\geq a_{n}x_{n}/2\right)\leq 4\frac{\operatorname{var}(S_{k}^{\prime})}{a_{n}^{2}x_{n}^{2}}\leq\frac{4C^{\prime}k\overline{F}(x_{n})}{a_{n}^{2}},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq n.

Therefore

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{k}^{\prime}-kG(x_{n})|\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)=O(n\overline{F}(x_{n})),\text{ as }n\to\infty.

(38)

Now we consider the statement of the corollary. Note that

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{P}(S_{k}-kG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(S_{k}-kG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n},\,\,\,\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\xi_{i}\leq x_{n}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(S_{k}-kG(x_{n})\geq a_{n}x_{n},\,\,\,\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\xi_{i}>x_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{k}^{\prime}-kG(x_{n})|\geq a_{n}x_{n}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\xi_{i}>x_{n}\right).

(39)

Together with (
38
), the statement is proved.

1.
Introduction

Answering a question of Csikvári, Gyarmati and Sárkőzy
[
CGS12
]
, Bergelson and Hindman independently gave proofs of the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Hin09
]
[
Ber10
]

Any finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
contains monochromatic sets
\{a,b,x,y\}
with
a+b=xy.

In subsequent years many generalizations and alternate proofs of this fact have been discovered; see for example
[
Bag
,
Bag15
,
Bow22
,
DNB18
,
FM21
]
. In contrast to the simplicity of its statement, to our knowledge all previous proofs of Theorem
1.1
rely on infinatary arguments.

This has limited the accessibility of the result. For example, in their textbook
[
LR14
, Theorem 9.47]
, Landman and Robertson advertised Theorem
1.1
but did not include its proof, stating “The tools that are used to prove these results are (far) beyond the scope of this book.”

The infinatary nature of the aforementioned arguments also do not provide any quantitative bounds on the Ramsey numbers for the configuration from Theorem
1.1
. This is in contrast to the variant of the result in the modular setting, where the quantitative aspects of the problem are well understood: Sárkőzy
[
Sár05
]
gave good upper bounds for the analogue of Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},
and his argument was later extended to provide similar bounds in
\mathbb{Z}/{p^{r}}\mathbb{Z}
by Gyarmati and Sárkőzy
[
GS08
]
and extended further still to
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
by Pach
[
Pac12
]
.

In this note we give a proof of (extensions of) Theorem
1.1
that only relies on Ramsey’s theorem and gives an exponential upper bound.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
H(r)
be the smallest integer such that any
r
-coloring of
\{1,...,H(r)\}
contains a monochromatic set
\{a,b,a+b,x,y,xy\}
with
a+b=xy.
Then

2^{2^{r}}<H(r)<r^{r^{r^{3r}}}.

More generally, if
R(k;r)
is the
r
-color Ramsey number for
k-cliques,
then any
r
-coloring of
R(n+1;r^{R(m+1;r)})^{R(m+1;r)}
contains a monochromatic set
\{a_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},x_{j},\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}:i\leq m,j\leq n\}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}.

Neither the listed bounds nor the configurations considered are the best possible that follow from our argument, but we will present Theorem
1.2
as stated for ease of exposition. The lower bound for
H(r)
is just a (sub-optimal) lower bound for the multiplicative Schur number controlling the configuration
\{x,y,xy\}
and makes no reference to the additive content of the theorem. Notice that in Csikvári, Gyarmati and Sárkőzy’s original question this set was not required to be monochromatic, so it is plausible that there is an upper bound for Theorem
1.1
that is better than the lower bound for Theorem
1.2
.

Finally, we end the introduction by pointing out one of the simplest results of this type that we still do not know how to provide reasonable bounds for.

Question 1.3
.

Let
B(r)
be the smallest integer such that any
r
-coloring of
\{1,...,B(r)\}
contains sets
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2}\}
and
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{1}+y_{2}\}
with
X\cup Y\cup XY
monochromatic. Is there an exponential upper bound (i.e., a tower of height independent of
r
) for
B(r)?

The fact that
B(r)
is finite for all
r
follows from a result of Beigleböck, Bergelson, Hindman, and Strauss
[
BBHS08
, Theorem 3.7]
which was proven using ultrafilter techniques. Our proof of Theorem
1.2
can be adapted to give an elementary proof of this result, but at the expense of requiring a refinement step that leads to bounds with a tower type dependence on
r.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Before beginning, we fix a small amount of notation. We denote by
R(k;r)
the smallest integer such that for any
r
-coloring of pairs
1\leq i<j\leq R(k;r)
there are
i_{1}<i_{2}<...<i_{k}
such that all pairs
(i_{g},i_{h})
with
g<h
are monochromatic. Given a sequence
s_{1},s_{2},...\in\mathbb{N},
for
i\leq j
set
s_{i,j}=s_{i}\cdot s_{i+1}\cdot...\cdot s_{j-1},
where by convention
s_{i,i}=1.

Our argument is an extension of the standard proof of Schur’s theorem based on Ramsey’s theorem as found in
[
GRS91
]
. To motivate the main trick, observe that if
S=\{a,b,a+b\},
then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
nS=\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\}.
This ‘dilation invariance’ will allow us to reduce the
a+b=xy
Ramsey problem directly to the standard proof of the
xy=z
Ramsey problem with only a relatively small loss in the bounds we obtain. This reduction is the content of Lemma
2.1
. A similar trick could be used for any other finite dilation invariant Ramsey family (such as any of the configurations obtained from Rado’s theorem), but we will focus on Theorem
1.2
for ease of exposition.

Lemma 2.1
.

In any finite coloring of
\{1,...,R(n+1;r^{M})^{M}\},
there are finite sets
S_{1},...,S_{M}
of size
n
with distinguished elements
s_{i}\in S_{i}
such that for any
i\leq j\leq M
the color of
s_{i,j}\cdot s
for
s\in S_{j}\cup\{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{j}}s^{\prime}\}
depends only on
i
and
j
and not on the choice of
s\in S_{j}\cup\{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{j}}s^{\prime}\}.

Before proving Claim
2.1
, let us use it to prove the upper bounds from Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of the upper bounds from Theorem
1.2
.

This will follow from applying Ramsey’s Theorem to the sequence of
s_{i}
from Lemma
2.1
as in the standard proof of Schur’s Theorem. To be more precise, set
M=R(m+1,r)
and fix an
r
-coloring of
\{1,...,R(n+1;r^{M})^{M}\}
. Let
s_{i}\in S_{i}
be as in Lemma
2.1
. For
1\leq i<j\leq M,
color pair
(i,j)
based on the color of
s_{i,j}.
By our choice of
M
, there are
i_{1}<...<i_{m+1}
such that all pairs
(i_{g},i_{h})
for
g<h
are monochromatic. Let
l=i_{m+1}-1
and let
a_{1},...,a_{n}
be an arbitrary enumeration the elements of
s_{i_{1},l}\cdot S_{l}.
For
j<m
let
x_{j}=s_{i_{j},i_{j+1}}
and set
x_{m}=s_{i_{m},l}\cdot\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{l}}s^{\prime}.
Finally, observe that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}
and that these terms together with the
a_{i}
and
x_{j}
are monochromatic by our choice of
i_{1},...,i_{m+1}
and Lemma
2.1
.

The bound in the
m=n=2
case now follows from the (weak) upper bound
R(3;r)\leq r^{r}

[
CG83
]
.
∎

We now prove Lemma
2.1
, which once again follows easily from Ramsey’s theorem.

Proof.

Fix an
r
-coloring of
\{1,...,R(n+1;r^{M})^{M}\}.
For
i\in[M]
let
N_{i}=R(n+1;r^{i}).
We inductively define
s_{i}\in S_{i}\subset[N_{i}]
as follows. In the base step, apply Ramsey’s theorem to
[N_{1}]
as in the usual proof of Schur’s theorem to find a set
S_{1}\subset N_{1}
with
S_{1}\cup\{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{1}}s^{\prime}\}
monochromatic. Choose
s_{1}\in S_{1}
arbitrarily. Having defined
s_{1},...,s_{i},
to find
S_{i+1}
consider the
r^{i+1}
-coloring of
[N_{i+1}]
where
n\in N_{i+1}
is colored based on the tuple listing the color of
n
and
s_{j,i+1}\cdot n
for each
j\leq i.
By our choice of
N_{i+1}
and Ramsey’s theorem, there is an
S_{i+1}\subset N_{i+1}
such that
S_{i+1}\cup\{\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{i+1}}s^{\prime}\}
is monochromatic with respect to this coloring, and in particular satisfies the conditions from Lemma
2.1
. Choose
s_{i+1}\in S_{i+1}
arbitrarily and we are done.
∎

We end by giving the lower bound from Theorem
1.2
. Let
S_{+}(r)
be the smallest integer such that any
r
-coloring of
\{1,...,S_{+}(r)\}
contains a monochromatic set
\{a,b,a+b\}
and
S_{\times}(r)
the smallest integer such that any
r
-coloring of
\{2,...,S_{\times}(r)\}
contains a monochromatic set
\{x,y,xy\}
. Any lower bound for
S_{\times}(r)
gives a lower bound for Theorem
1.2
, so we establish one below. The argument we give here is surely known (see e.g.
[
hpg
]
), but we could not find a reference to it in the literature.

Lemma 2.2
.

S_{\times}(r)\geq 2^{S_{+}(r)-1}.
In particular,
S_{\times}(r)\geq 2^{2^{r}}.

Proof.

Consider an
r
-coloring
c_{+}
of
\{1,...,S_{+}(r)-1\}
with no monochromatic sets
\{a,b,a+b\}.
Let
P
be the set of primes. Define a coloring
c_{\times}
of
\{2,...,2^{S_{+}(r)-1}\}
where the
c_{\times}
color of
n=\prod_{p\in P}p^{m_{p,n}}\leq 2^{S_{+}(r)-1}
is the
c_{+}
color of
\sum_{p\in P}m_{p,n}.
If there is a set
\{x,y,xy\}
which is monochromatic with respect to
c_{\times},
then for
a=\sum_{p\in P}m_{p,x}
and
b=\sum_{p\in P}m_{p,y}
we have have that
\{a,b,a+b\}
is monochromatic with respect to
c_{+},
a contradiction.

The in particular part of the Lemma now follows from the well known bound
S_{+}(r)>\frac{3^{r}-1}{2}

[
CG83
]
.
∎

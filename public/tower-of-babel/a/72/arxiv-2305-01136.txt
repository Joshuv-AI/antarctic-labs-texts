Combined effect of mutually frequency-detuned strong and weak drives on a two-level system: Envelope of the Rabi oscillations
M. E. Raikh

Abstract
Near-resonant ac-drive acting on a two-level system induces the Rabi oscillations of the level occupations. It is shown that additional weak drive properly frequency-detuned from the primary drive causes a resonant response. This response manifests itself in the emergence of the envelope of the oscillations. At resonance, the inverse period of the envelope is proportional to the amplitude of the weak drive. The resonant condition reads: difference of frequencies between the two drives is equal to the ac-splitting of quasilevels in the field of the strong drive. Technically, the resonance can be inferred from the analogy between the equations for the time-evolution of the spin amplitude and the Mathieu equation, which describes e.g. the parametric resonance.

IV

III
Weak secondary drive

Within the adopted RWA approximation Eq.

II
applies for arbitrary relation between two drives
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}

and arbitrary relation between the detunings

\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
as long as they are much smaller than
\omega
. Next we take the limit of a weak secondary drive

\lambda_{2}\ll\lambda_{1}
. In the lowest order in the secondary drive we can neglect the
term
\lambda_{2}^{2}
in the right-hand side. Concerning the
left-hand side, we expand the
ratio in the brackets as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}\delta_{2}e^{i\delta_{1}t}+\lambda_{2}\delta_{1}e^{i\delta_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}e^{i\delta_{1}t}+\lambda_{2}e^{i\delta_{2}t}}

\displaystyle\approx\delta_{2}\Biggl[1+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{\delta_{1}}{\delta_{2}}-1\Bigr)e^{i(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})t}\Biggr].

(12)

With the above simplifications Eq.

takes the form

\displaystyle-\frac{d^{2}{{\tilde{A}}_{1}}}{dt^{2}}-\Biggl[\lambda_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{4}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2\lambda_{1}}\delta_{2}(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})e^{-i(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})t}\Biggr]{\tilde{A}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}e^{i(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})t}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}e^{-i(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})t}\right]{\tilde{A}}_{1}.

(13)

There are two terms
proportional to
\lambda_{2}
in
Eq.
III
. Assuming that
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are of the same order, the term in the left-hand side can be estimated as

\lambda_{2}\delta_{1}^{2}/\lambda_{1}
, while the term in the right-hand side
is of the order of
\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}
. If
\lambda_{1}
is much greater than
\delta_{1}
, which corresponds to the limit of developed Rabi oscillations, the term in the right-hand side dominates. Note now that,
if one neglects the term in the left-hand side, Eq.
III
reduces
to the classical Mathieu equation, which describes e.g. the electron motion in a weak one-dimensional potential. The time in Eq.
III
plays the role of coordinate. The sum
\lambda_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{4}

plays the role of energy. Despite the similarity, there is a dramatic difference
between Eq.
III
and the Mathieu equation, since the
Mathieu equation describes the ”forbidden gaps” or the domains
of instabilities, while Eq.
III
does not. Still, as we will see below, the position of resonance captured by Eq.
III
can be found from the same ”Bragg condition” as the
position of the center of the forbidden gap, namely

2\Biggl(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{4}\Biggr)^{1/2}=|\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}|.

(14)

To trace how the resonance Eq.
14
emerges, we search for
the solution of Eq.
III
in the Floquet form

{\tilde{A}}_{1}(t)=f\exp{\{ist\}}+g\exp{\Big\{i(s+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})t\Big\}}.

(15)

Substituting this form into Eq.
III
and equating the
coefficients in front of the two exponents, we arrive to the system

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{4}-s^{2}\right)f=-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\Bigg[\frac{\delta_{2}(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})}{2\lambda_{1}^{2}}+1\Bigg]g,

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{4}-(s+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})^{2}\right)g=-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f.

(16)

The brackets in the left-hand sides of the two equations coincide
under the condition:
s+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}=-s
. Thus we set

s=\frac{\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}}{2}+s_{1},

(17)

and assume that
s_{1}\ll|\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}|
. Upon neglecting the

s_{1}^{2}
-term, the system Eq.
III
takes the form

\displaystyle\Bigg[\lambda_{1}^{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}-2\delta_{2}\delta_{1}}{4}-s_{1}(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})\Bigg]f=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\Bigg[\frac{\delta_{2}(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})}{2\lambda_{1}^{2}}+1\Bigg]g,

\displaystyle\Bigg[\lambda_{1}^{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}-2\delta_{2}\delta_{1}}{4}+s_{1}(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})\Bigg]g=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}f.

(18)

Finally, upon multiplying the two last equations, we obtain the
expression for the Floquet exponent

\displaystyle s_{1}^{2}\left(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left[\lambda_{1}^{2}-\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}}{4}+\frac{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}{2}\right]^{2}-\lambda_{2}^{2}\left[\frac{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}-\delta_{2}^{2}}{2}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\right].

(19)

Note now that the first bracket on the right-hand side of Eq.

III
turns to zero when Eq.
14
is satisfied, i.e. at resonance. It is seen from Eq.
III

\lambda_{2}
, of the secondary drive. It is also
instructive to rewrite Eq.

14

\lambda_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\delta_{2}^{2}-2\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\right)

(20)

In the second identity we have again used the resonant
condition Eq.
14
.

Yet another way to cast Eq.
III

\delta_{2}
from
the resonant condition. This yields

s_{1}=\pm\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4\Theta_{R}}\left(\Delta_{Z}-\omega_{1}\pm 2\Theta_{R}\right).

In the last identity we have returned to the original notations, generalized Rabi frequency and the detuning of the primary drive
(see Eq.
1

\lambda_{2}\ll|\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}|

Eq.

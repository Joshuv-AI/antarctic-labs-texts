On de Sitter vacua in O(d,d) invariant cosmology
Yang Liu, Antonio Padilla, Paul M. Saffin and Robert G. C. Smith

Abstract
We perform a thorough analysis of de Sitter solutions in O ​ ( d , d ) \textrm{O}(d,d) invariant cosmologies. Starting with a homogeneous and isotropic framework we examine conditions for the existence of such solutions to the vacuum field equations, non-perturbative in α ′ \alpha^{\prime} in both the string frame and the Einstein frame. We elucidate the nature of the instability in the string frame vacuum. For the Einstein frame, we demonstrate that the de Sitter solutions cannot be eternal. We then extend our analysis to include Bianchi I universes where the O ​ ( d , d ) \textrm{O}(d,d) symmetry includes scale factor exchange as well as scale factor duality. We show how the theory can be extended to the anisotropic case so that it admits de Sitter solutions, noting the crucial role played by the O ​ ( d , d ) \textrm{O}(d,d) symmetry in satisfying any additional constraints.

Matching with (

\displaystyle f(\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}H)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}d_{k}\alpha^{\prime k}H^{2k},\qquad d_{1}={d}.

(17)

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle E_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta I}{\delta\Phi}=\kappa^{2}ne^{-\Phi}\left[2\Phi_{nn}-\Phi^{2}_{n}-f(H)\right],

\displaystyle E_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta I}{\delta q}=-\kappa^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[e^{-\Phi}f^{\prime}(H)\right].

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{n}=\Phi_{n}E_{\Phi}+HE_{q}.

3.1
dS solutions in the string frame

It was already shown in
[
40
,
41
]
that string frame de Sitter solutions are possible in this class of
\alpha^{\prime}
complete cosmologies for suitable choices of the function
f(H)
. These solutions are necessarily non-perturbative in
\alpha^{\prime}
, and could not have been found using standard techniques in perturbative string theory. However, it has also been shown that these solutions are unstable under vacuum fluctuations
[
44
,
49
,
50
]
. We will review the derivation of these solutions and add some additional insight into the nature of the instability.

We know that in a neighbourhood of the origin, the function
f(H)
is even and admits the following Taylor expansion in
H^{2}

f(H)={d}H^{2}+d_{2}H^{4}+\ldots

(25)

Although we cannot assume that the function remains analytic for all values of
H
, we shall assume that it is twice differentiable in order to have well defined field equations. We shall also assume it to be an even function in order to preserve the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry.

Here we are interested in the properties of the function in a neighbourhood of a string frame de Sitter solution with
H=H_{c}>0
, for some constant
H_{c}
. Whilst it is tempting to motivate this with the current phase of accelerated expansion, it is important to note that we are working in
d+1
dimensions. For the critical dimension (
d+1=10,26
), we must perform compactification down to four dimensions before making contact with observation. Therefore, we remain agnostic as to the value of
H_{c}
.

Setting
E_{a}=0
for
a=n,\Phi,q
, we see that
H=H_{c}>0
, for some constant
H_{c}
, if and only if
[
46
]

\Phi^{2}_{n}=-f(H_{c})=Q^{2},\quad f^{\prime}(H_{c})=0,

(26)

for some real constant
Q
. This means that
f(H)
must have a minimum at
H=H_{c}
. Thanks to the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry, it must also have a minimum at
H=-H_{c}
. This suggests the following general form

f(H)=-Q^{2}+\frac{g(H)}{H_{c}^{4}}(H^{2}-H_{c}^{2})^{2}

(27)

for some even function
g(H)
that admits a Taylor expansion in
H^{2}-H_{c}^{2}

g(H)=g_{0}+g_{1}(H^{2}-H_{c}^{2})+\ldots.

(28)

For the form of
f(H)
given in (
27
) to overlap with the form close to the origin (
25
), we obtain the following constraints

g(0)=Q^{2},\qquad g^{\prime}(0)=0,\qquad g^{\prime\prime}(0)=2{d}+\frac{4Q^{2}}{H_{c}^{2}}.

(29)

It is also important to establish the stability of these de Sitter solutions. Of course, a full analysis of cosmological perturbations requires knowledge of the underlying theory beyond the homogeneous limit under consideration. Although there has been some interesting recent work in this direction
[
52
]
, it is well known that
\textrm{O}(d,d)
invariance in
d+1
dimensions is at odds (!) with manifest diffeomorphism invariance, as evidenced through double field theory
[
32
,
33
]
(see also
[
55
]
for a related duality observed in cosmological perturbations). Faced with these limitations, we can only consider the stability of solutions under homogeneous perturbations captured by the general action (
15
). Instabilities for homogeneous and isotropic perturbations of string frame de Sitter solutions were identified in
[
44
,
46
,
49
,
50
]
with subsequent extensions to anisotropic perturbations
[
51
]
. Here we take a subtlely different approach to those works but draw the same conclusions.

We consider only homogeneous and isotropic perturbations, so that the system of equations
E_{i}=0
remains unchanged. Assuming
f^{\prime\prime}(H)\neq 0
, these can be written in the following form

f^{\prime}(H)=ce^{\Phi},\qquad\partial_{n}H=s\frac{\sqrt{L_{f}(H)}f^{\prime}(H)}{f^{\prime\prime}(H)},

(30)

where
c
is a constant of integration. Here
s=\pm 1
corresponds to the sign of
\dot{\Phi}=d\Phi/dt
, appearing when we take the square root in (
18
). This ability to take either sign was identified as a potential source of error in the stability analysis, as discussed in
[
50
]
. Next we will show how the choice of sign plays a crucial role in opening up the unstable channel.

We begin by making a choice of the time co-ordinate by setting the lapse function to unity,
n=1
, and solve for the Hubble parameter in a neighbourhood of the vacuum, giving

H=H_{c}+\delta H

(31)

where
\delta H
satisfies

\dot{\delta H}\approx\begin{cases}s\sqrt{H_{c}f^{\prime\prime}(H_{c})}\delta H^{3/2}&Q=0\\
s|Q|\delta H&Q\neq 0\end{cases}

(32)

For
Q=0
, this gives the consistent solution

\delta H\approx\frac{4}{H_{c}f^{\prime\prime}(H_{c})(t-t_{c})^{2}}

(33)

with
s=-\text{sgn}(t-t_{c})
. We immediately see the unstable channel if and only if the initial time
t_{i}<t_{c}
, or equivalently
s=+1
. For
Q\neq 0
, we also get an instability for
s=+1
, as

\delta H\approx e^{s|Q|(t-t_{c})}

(34)

In all cases, the instability is excited if and only if
\dot{\Phi}(t_{i})>0
. These results are consistent with the instability analysis presented in
[
46
]
.

The string frame and the Einstein frame are related by a conformal transformation. Labelling the Einstein frame with the letter “E”, we write
\mathbb{g}_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{A}^{2}\mathbb{g}_{\mu\nu}^{E}
and so
n=\mathcal{A}n_{E}
,
a_{S}=\mathcal{A}a_{E}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the conformal factor to be derived presently. Focussing on the leading order curvature term in the action, we note that

\int\mathrm{d}^{1+d}x\sqrt{-\mathbb{g}}e^{-2\phi}R=\int\mathrm{d}^{1+d}x\sqrt{-\mathbb{g}_{E}}e^{-2\phi}\mathcal{A}^{d-1}\left[R_{E}+\ldots\right]

(35)

where the ellipsis include derivatives of
\mathcal{A}
and
R_{E}
is the Ricci scalar for the transformed metric. For the latter to coincide with the Einstein frame metric, we must choose
\mathcal{A}=e^{\frac{2\phi}{d-1}}
.

Setting
E_{i}=0
for
i=n,\Phi,q
, we see that the vacuum field equations are equivalent to

f^{\prime}(H)=ce^{\Phi},\qquad\Phi_{n}=s\sqrt{L_{f}(H)}

(36)

where we recall that
c
is a constant of integration and
s=\pm 1
. We wish to recast these equations in terms of the Hubble parameter defined in the Einstein frame

H_{E}=\partial_{n_{E}}q_{E}

(37)

where
\partial_{n_{E}}=\frac{1}{n_{E}}\frac{d}{dt}
and
q_{E}=\ln a_{E}
. From the definition of the generalised dilaton

\Phi=2\phi-dq
and the conformal mapping between frames
q-q_{E}=\ln(n/n_{E})=\frac{2\phi}{d-1}
, we can derive the following useful relations

\Phi=-q-(d-1)q_{E}\implies\Phi_{n}=-H-(d-1)\frac{n_{E}}{n}H_{E}

(38)

and

\phi=-\frac{(d-1)}{2}(\Phi+dq_{E})\implies\frac{n}{n_{E}}=\frac{e^{-\Phi}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}

(39)

where
\mathcal{V}_{E}=a_{E}^{d}
is volume scale factor in Einstein frame.

As first noted in
[
46
]
, we can inherit an Einstein frame de Sitter vacuum from the string frame de Sitter vacua presented in the previous section, by fixing the dilaton,
\phi
, to be constant. This corresponds to the case where
\Phi_{n}=-dH_{c}
, with
H_{c}
the constant Hubble scale in string frame. These solutions preserve all the de Sitter isometries and would seem to be in contradiction of the classical no-go theorem of
[
20
]
. This suggests that the corresponding form of
f(H)
does not emerge from classical heterotic or type II strings. For this reason, we will focus instead on quasi-de Sitter solutions in Einstein frame, where the dilaton breaks the de Sitter isometries.

After some manipulations, the system of equations (
36
), (
38
) and (
39
) can be expressed as follows

e^{\Phi}=\frac{f^{\prime}(H)}{c},\qquad\frac{n}{n_{E}}=\frac{c}{f^{\prime}(H)\mathcal{V}_{E}},\qquad\partial_{n_{E}}\mathcal{V}_{E}=c\mathcal{F}(H),\qquad\partial_{n_{E}}H=\frac{c\mathcal{G}(H)}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}

(40)

where

\mathcal{F}(H)=\frac{d}{1-d}\left[\frac{H+s\sqrt{L}}{f^{\prime}(H)}\right],\qquad\mathcal{G}(H)=\frac{s\sqrt{L}}{f^{\prime\prime}(H)}

(41)

and we have assumed
f^{\prime}(H)f^{\prime\prime}(H)\neq 0
. Note that the choice
f^{\prime}(H)\neq 0
is consistent with a time dependent dilaton. To explore the possibility of a de Sitter solutions in the Einstein frame, it is convenient to construct

\partial_{n_{E}}H_{E}=dH_{E}^{2}\left[\frac{\mathcal{V}_{E}\partial_{n_{E}}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{E}}{(\partial_{n_{E}}\mathcal{V}_{E})^{2}}-1\right]=dH_{E}^{2}\left[\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(H)\mathcal{G}(H)}{\mathcal{F}(H)^{2}}-1\right],\qquad c\mathcal{F}(H)\neq 0.

(42)

If we choose the function
f(H)
such that the right hand of this equation vanishes identically, the Hubble parameter in Einstein frame is guaranteed to be constant,
H_{E}=H_{\star}
, for a solution to the vacuum field equations. Thus, the existence of the de Sitter solution in Einstein frame requires that

\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{G}}=0,

(43)

or in other words, that
f(H)
satisfies the following differential equation,

\frac{d+1}{d-1}sH\sqrt{Hf^{\prime}-f}f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{d-1}(Hf^{\prime}-f)f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d}{d-1}H^{2}f^{\prime\prime}+s\sqrt{Hf^{\prime}-f}f^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}Hf^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}=0.

(44)

Note further that we can use the third equation in (
40
) to show that the Hubble parameters in the Einstein and string frame are related according to the following formula

H_{E}=\frac{c\mathcal{F}(H)}{d\mathcal{V}_{E}}.

(45)

For a suitable choice of
f(H)
satisfying (
44
), the corresponding constant curvature solution is stable under vacuum perturbations, as it is the
only
solution to (
42
).

Finding a suitable choice of

is not easy as the differential equation (
44

,

\bar{f}(H)=(H+H_{m})^{2}-H^{2}_{m},

(46)

where
H_{m}
is a constant
[

]

With

and taking the negative (
s=-1
) root we find that
\mathcal{F}=0
and
\mathcal{G}=-H/2
. The vanishing of
\mathcal{F}
suggests this solution lies outside of the original regime of validility for which (
44
) was derived. Nevertheless, it is interesting to explore the solutions to the system of equations (
40
) in this particular case. It turns out that they imply a constant but vanishing Hubble rate in Einstein frame

H_{\star}=0,

(47)

consistent with (
45
), along with the following solutions for the string frame Hubble parameter, the dilaton and the lapse,

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{c}\exp\left(-\frac{ct}{2a_{0}^{d}}\right),

(48)

\displaystyle\Phi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left[\frac{2}{c}\left(H_{c}\exp\left(-\frac{ct}{2a_{0}^{d}}\right)+H_{m}\right)\right],

(49)

\displaystyle n(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2a_{0}^{d}}\left[H_{c}\exp\left(-\frac{ct}{2a_{0}^{d}}\right)+H_{m}\right]^{-1},

(50)

where

is a dimensionful constant of integration and we have assumed

and

. Clearly the solution runs into a singularity as

although this can be trivially avoided by taking

.

We have not been able to find an exact solution to (

We can also seek a perturbative solution to (
44
). To this end, we consider

f(H)=\bar{f}(H)\left[1+\epsilon(H)\right],

(51)

where we assume
|\epsilon|\ll 1
. Plugging this into (
44
) (with
s=-1
) and keeping terms up to linear order in
\epsilon
, we arrive at the linear differential equation

(H_{m}-H)(2H_{m}-H)H\epsilon^{\prime\prime}(H)+(2H_{m}^{2}-3HH_{m}+2H^{2})\epsilon^{\prime}(H)-H_{m}\epsilon(H)=0

(52)

with the following solution

\epsilon(H)=\frac{C_{1}H_{m}+C_{2}H+h_{m}C_{2}\ln\left|\frac{H}{H_{m}}\right|}{H+2h_{m}},

(53)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are constants of integration. Note that
C_{1}
can be removed by redefining
H_{m}\to H_{m}\left(1-C_{1}/2\right)
and working to linear order in
C_{1}
. Thus we can set
C_{1}=0
without loss of generality and express (
53
) as a simple function of the ratio
\delta=H/H_{m}
,

\epsilon(H)=C_{2}\left[\frac{\delta+\ln|\delta|}{\delta+2}\right].

(54)

Recall that this expression is valid as long as
|\epsilon(H)|\ll 1
. If we assume
|C_{2}|\ll 1
then this condition is violated if and only if
\delta
strays too close to the singularities at
\delta=0,-2
. More precisely, we have
\epsilon(H)\ll 1
whenever
|C_{2}|\ll 1
, provided

|\delta|\gg e^{-2/|C_{2}|}\ ,\quad\Big|\delta+2\Big|\gg|C_{2}|\left(2-\ln 2\right).

We now revisit the field equations (
40
) and (
43
) using the perturbed form of
f
given by (
54
). To leading order in
|C_{2}|\ll 1
(
43
) gives the following expression for Hubble rate in Einstein frame

H_{E}\approx\frac{C_{2}c}{4(d-1)\mathcal{V}_{E}}.

(55)

If we assume
H_{E}=H_{\star}
for some constant
H_{\star}
and set
n_{E}=1
, it follows that that we have
\mathcal{V}_{E}=a_{0}^{d}e^{dH_{\star}t}
. We can then solve (
55
) explicitly, giving

H_{\star}\approx\frac{1}{dt}W\left(\frac{dC_{2}ct}{4(d-1)a_{0}^{d}}\right)\approx\frac{C_{2}c}{4(d-1)a_{0}^{d}}

(56)

where we have used the fact that the Lambert W function
W(x)\approx x
for small

. We see that we have a constant and
non
-vanishing Hubble rate in the Einstein frame, with the approximation valid as long as
|t|\ll 4(d-1)a_{0}^{d}/d|c||C_{2}|
. Since
H_{\star}
is required to be positive in an expanding universe, we assume that
C_{2}c>0
. The remaining fields are dominated by their non-vanishing zeroth order (in
\epsilon
) solutions (
48
)-(
50
).
Recall that in a neighbourhood of the origin, perturbative string theory suggests that the function
f(H)
should admit a Taylor expansion of the form (
25
). We will now show that any solution to the differential equation (
44
) valid for
H>0
cannot be analytically continued towards a solution of the form (
25
) in a neighbourhood of
H=0
.

To see this, we begin by assuming that
f(H)
admits a Taylor expansions of the form (
25
) in a neighbourhood of the origin

f(H)={d}H^{2}+\mathcal{O}(H^{4}).

(57)

In this neighbourhood, it immediately follows that

\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(H)\mathcal{G}(H)}{\mathcal{F}(H)^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(H^{2}).

(58)

In order to satisfy (
44
), the right hand side of this expression should be equal to unity, which is clearly not possible in a neighbourhood of
H=0
, regardless of the sign of
s=\pm 1
. Thus, it is not possible to smoothly connect a solution to (
44
) to the desired leading order Neveu-Schwarz theory at low curvatures. However, perhaps this is too demanding. As noted earlier, for the field equations to be well defined, we only need the form of
f(H)
to be twice differentiable. For any finite
N
, it is certainly possible to match the first
N
derivatives of a solution to (
44
) to a function of the form (
57
), at some value of
H=H_{\text{match}}>0
.

It is, perhaps, optimistic to expect such a matching to emerge from a non-perturbative understanding of string theory. Nevertheless, we could imagine a discontinuity in the higher order derivatives of
f(H)
to be a remnant of the truncation to the massless sector of the theory. Indeed, if it emerges from string oscillators with string scale masses, we might expect
H_{\text{match}}\sim 1
(in units of
\alpha^{\prime}=1
).

Even if this desired form of
f(H)
can be justified, it is clear that we can only trust our de Sitter solution in the regime where
H>H_{\text{match}}
. However, recall that
H
is the Hubble parameter in the
string frame
and this will change over time. If the system evolves into a regime where
H<H_{\text{match}}
, the form of
f(H)
will no longer admit de Sitter solutions and the Einstein frame de Sitter metric is destabilised.

To avoid this instability, we need to stop
H
evolving into the unstable region with
H<H_{\text{match}}
in either time direction. In other words, we need
H
to have a turning point in its evolution at some
H_{\text{turn}}>H_{\text{match}}
. At such a turning point, the last equation in (
40
) tells us that
\mathcal{G}(H_{\text{turn}})=0
. Since
H_{\text{turn}}>H_{\text{match}}
by assumption, the differential equation (
43
) ought to be satisfied at
H=H_{\text{turn}}
, further implying that
\mathcal{F}(H_{\text{turn}})=0
must have a zero at the same point. Since we assume that
f(H)
is twice differentiable,
\mathcal{F}(H_{\text{turn}})=\mathcal{G}(H_{\text{turn}})=0
if and only if
H_{\text{turn}}=0
. This contradicts the underlying assumption that
H_{\text{turn}}>H_{\text{match}}>0
. It follows that our de Sitter solution in Einstein frame cannot be eternal.

4
Anisotropic cosmologies

We now turn our attention to anisotropic cosmologies in
d+1
spacetime and consider a string frame line element of the form

ds^{2}=-n(t)^{2}dt^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(t)^{2}dx_{i}^{2},

(59)

where
a_{i}(t)
is the scale factor along the
i
th spatial direction. A detailed derivation of the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
invariant action requires a careful computation of all the terms in (
15
), including the multitrace operators. Instead of computing that explicitly, we note that the action will take the form

I=\kappa^{2}\int\mathrm{d}t\;ne^{-\Phi}\left[-\Phi^{2}_{n}+f(H_{i})\right],

(60)

where we have assumed units with
\alpha^{\prime}=1
. The Hubble parameter along the
i
th direction is given by
H_{i}=\partial_{n}q_{i}
for
q_{i}=\ln a_{i}
, while the generalised dilaton is given in terms of the standard dilaton via the following relation

\Phi=2\phi-\sum_{i}q_{i}.

(61)

To match with the leading order Neveu-Schwarz action (
1
) at low curvatures, we expect that
f
admits a Taylor expansion near the origin such that

f(H_{i})=\sum_{i}H_{i}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(H^{4}).

(62)

The
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry now includes scale factor duality along each direction,
a_{i}\to 1/a_{i}
and scale factor exchange
a_{i}\leftrightarrow a_{j}
for each
i
and
j
. This suggests that the function
f(H_{i})
can also be expressed as

f(H_{i})=F\left(\hat{e}^{d}_{i}(H_{j}^{2})\right),

(63)

where we define the normalised elementary symmetric polynomials

\hat{e}_{i}^{d}(X_{j})=\frac{1}{\binom{d}{i}}\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\ldots j_{i}\leq d}X_{j_{1}}\ldots X_{j_{i}}

(64)

for
i=1,\ldots d
.

Varying the action (
60
) with respect to the lapse, the dilaton and each of the scale factors, we obtain
E_{a}=0
for
a=n,\Phi,q_{i}
, where

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta I}{\delta n}=\kappa^{2}e^{-\Phi}\left[\Phi^{2}_{n}-L_{f}(H_{i})\right],

(65)

\displaystyle E_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta I}{\delta\Phi}=\kappa^{2}ne^{-\Phi}\left[2\Phi_{nn}-\Phi^{2}_{n}-f(H_{i})\right],

(66)

\displaystyle E_{q_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta I}{\delta q_{i}}=-\kappa^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left[e^{-\Phi}f_{i}\right],

(67)

where we denote partial derivatives
f_{i}=\partial f/\partial H_{i}
and the Legendre transform
L_{f}(H_{i})=\sum_{i}H_{i}f_{i}-f(H_{i})
. It is also convenient to define the average Hubble parameter in string frame as
H=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}H_{i}
.

4.1
dS solutions in the string frame

We now seek solutions to the anisotropic field equations corresponding to the string frame de Sitter vacua.
Using similar arguments to the isotropic case in section
3.1
, we see that there is an isotropic solution to (
65
) to (
67
) with
H_{i}=H_{c}>0
for all
i
and some constant
H_{c}
if and only if

\Phi_{n}^{2}=-f(H_{c},\ldots,H_{c})=Q^{2},\qquad f_{i}(H_{c},\ldots,H_{c})=0.

(68)

whereb
Q
is a constant. Given the form of
f
dictated by the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry (
63
), this suggests that

f(H_{i})=-Q^{2}+\sum_{j}\frac{g_{j}(H_{l})}{H_{c}^{4j}}(\hat{e}_{j}^{d}(H_{l}^{2})-H_{c}^{2j})^{2}

(69)

where the functions
g_{j}(H_{k})
take the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetric form (
63
) and admit a Taylor expansion in each of
\hat{e}_{j}^{d}(H_{l}^{2})-H_{c}^{2j}
,

g_{j}(H_{k})=g_{j0}+\sum_{k}g_{jk}(\hat{e}_{k}^{d}(H_{l}^{2})-H_{c}^{2k})+\ldots.

(70)

Close to the origin, the form of
f
should coincide with (
62
) to recover the leading order Neveu-Schwarz theory at small curvatures.
On the isotropic line where
H_{i}=H
for all
i
, the form of
f
is compatible with the isotropic solutions (
27
), as expected

f(H_{i})|_{\text{iso}}=-Q^{2}+\sum_{j}\frac{g_{j}(H,\ldots,H)}{H_{c}^{4j}}(H^{2j}-H_{c}^{2j})^{2}.

(71)

It immediately follows that the corresponding de Sitter solution will once again be unstable to isotropic fluctuations when
\Phi_{n}(t_{i})>0
.

4.2
dS solutions in the Einstein frame

We now switch our attention to the Einstein frame to see if the family of de Sitter solutions found in the isotropic framework
[
42
]
carries over to the anisotropic framework. As usual, we perform a conformal transformation to switch to Einstein frame, with
n=\mathcal{A}n_{E}
and
a_{i}=\mathcal{A}a_{i}^{E}
where
\mathcal{A}=e^{\frac{2\phi}{d-1}}
. We therefore have
\ln(n/n_{E})=q_{i}-q_{i}^{E}=\frac{2\phi}{d-1}
for
q_{i}^{E}=\ln a_{i}^{E}
. This allows us to extract the following useful relation

\phi=-\frac{d-1}{2}\left(\Phi+\sum_{i}q_{i}^{E}\right),

(72)

implying

\frac{n}{n_{E}}=\frac{e^{-\Phi}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}},\qquad q_{i}-q_{i}^{E}=-\Phi-\ln\mathcal{V}_{E}

(73)

where
\mathcal{V}_{E}=\prod_{i}(a_{i}^{E})^{k_{i}}
is the Einstein frame volume scale factor.

In the Einstein frame, we define Hubble parameters
H_{i}^{E}=\partial_{n_{E}}q_{i}^{E}
and the corresponding average
H^{E}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}H_{i}^{E}
. These are easily related to their string frame counterparts via the relation

H_{i}=\frac{n_{E}}{n}\left(H_{i}^{E}-dH^{E}\right)-\Phi_{n}.

(74)

From the field equations (
65
) to (
67
) we can derive expressions for the generalised velocities in Einstein frame

\partial_{n_{E}}\Phi=\frac{e^{-\Phi}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}s\sqrt{L_{f}},\qquad H_{i}^{E}-H^{E}=\frac{e^{-\Phi}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}\left(H_{i}-H\right),\qquad H^{E}=\frac{e^{-\Phi}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}\frac{\Sigma}{d}\mathcal{F}

(75)

where

\mathcal{F}=\frac{d}{1-d}\left(\frac{H+s\sqrt{L_{f}}}{\Sigma}\right),\qquad\Sigma=\sqrt{\sum_{j}f_{j}^{2}}

(76)

and
s=\pm 1
, depending on the sign of
\Phi_{n}
.
From equation (
67
), we have that

e^{-\Phi}f_{i}=c_{i}\implies e^{-\Phi}\Sigma=|\underline{c}|\equiv\sqrt{\sum_{j}c_{j}^{2}}

(77)

for some constants
c_{i}
. We can now derive the following constraints on the dynamics of the system, analogous to the last two equations in (
40
)

\qquad\partial_{n_{E}}\mathcal{V}_{E}=|\underline{c}|\mathcal{F},\qquad\partial_{n_{E}}H_{i}=\frac{|\underline{c}|\mathcal{G}_{i}}{\mathcal{V}_{E}}

(78)

where

\mathcal{G}_{i}=\frac{s\sqrt{L_{f}}\sum_{j}f^{ij}f_{j}}{\Sigma}

(79)

and
f^{ij}
denotes the inverse of the Hessian of the function
f
, assumed to be well defined. We proceed in a similar way to the isotropic case, and compute the rate of change of the Einstein frame Hubble parameters. We find that

\partial_{n_{E}}H_{i}^{E}=d(H^{E})^{2}\left[(1+\xi_{i})(\mathcal{O}\ln\mathcal{F}-1)+\mathcal{O}\xi_{i}\right],

(80)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\sum_{j}\mathcal{G}_{j}\partial_{H_{j}},

(81)

\displaystyle\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{d(H_{i}-H)}{\Sigma\mathcal{F}}.

(82)

Since
\sum_{j}\xi_{j}=0
, it follows that the rate of change of the average Hubble factor is given by a somewhat simpler formula

\partial_{n_{E}}H^{E}=d(H^{E})^{2}(\mathcal{O}\ln\mathcal{F}-1),

(83)

which is strongly reminiscent of the corresponding isotropic equation (
42
), as expected.
We may alternatively express (
80
) as

\partial_{n_{E}}H_{i}^{E}-(1+\xi_{i})\partial_{n_{E}}H^{E}=d(H^{E})^{2}\mathcal{O}\xi_{i}.

(84)

Let us now evaluate our system on the isotropic line
H_{i}=H
, along which we have
\xi_{i}=0
from (
82
). For consistency, it must follow that

(\partial_{n_{E}}H^{E})|_{\text{iso}}=d(H^{E})^{2}(\mathcal{O}\ln\mathcal{F}-1)|_{\text{iso}},\qquad(\mathcal{O}\xi_{i})|_{\text{iso}}=0

(85)

where
|_{\text{iso}}
indicates evaluation on the isotropic line. Requiring the vacuum to be de Sitter in the Einstein frame on the isotropic line, i.e.
H_{i}^{E}=H^{E}=const
, now yields the following constraints

\mathcal{O}(\ln\mathcal{F})|_{\text{iso}}=1,\qquad(\mathcal{O}\xi_{i})|_{\text{iso}}=0.

(86)

Defining
\mathcal{G}=\sum_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}/d
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\ln\mathcal{F}\right)=-\sum_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}\partial_{H_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\right),

(87)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\xi_{i})=\frac{1}{\Sigma\mathcal{F}^{2}}(\mathcal{G}_{i}-\mathcal{G})-\xi_{i}\mathcal{O}\ln(\Sigma\mathcal{F}),

(88)

and impose (
86
), giving

\left[\sum_{i}\partial_{H_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\right)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\text{iso}}=0,\qquad\mathcal{G}_{i}|_{\text{iso}}=\mathcal{G}|_{\text{iso}}\neq 0.

(89)

To explore the implications of these results, consider the
N
dimensional Euclidean space spanned by the Hubble parameters,
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{d})
. It is convenient to introduce a new set of coordinates
(H,v_{\alpha})
, adapted to the isotropic line, defined in terms of the average Hubble parameter
H=\sum_{i}H_{i}/d
and
d-1
orthogonal directions

\displaystyle v_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(H_{i}-H)n_{i}^{\alpha},

(90)

so that

H_{i}=H+\sum_{\alpha}n^{\alpha}_{i}v_{\alpha}.

(91)

Here we have introduced an orthonormal set of vectors in Hubble space,
n^{\alpha}_{i}
, orthogonal to the tangent vector,
u_{i}=1/d
, on the isotropic line

\sum_{i}u_{i}n_{i}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}n_{i}^{\alpha}=0,\qquad\sum_{i}n_{i}^{\alpha}n_{i}^{\beta}=\delta^{\alpha\beta}.

(92)

We can now think of the isotropic line as being parametrized by the average Hubble parameter,
H
, with each of the remaining coordinates vanishing there,
v_{\alpha}|_{\text{iso}}=0
. At this point, we recall that the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry implies that
f(H_{i})
is given in terms of the normalised elementary symmetric polynomials, as in (
63
). In a neighbourhood of the isotropic line, we can show that

\hat{e}^{d}_{i}(H_{j}^{2})=H^{2i}+\frac{i(d+1-2i)}{d(d-1)}H^{2(i-1)}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\delta^{\alpha\beta}v_{\alpha}v_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(v^{3}).

(93)

Correspondingly we find that the function
f
takes the form

f(H_{i})=f_{0}(H)+g_{0}(H)\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\delta^{\alpha\beta}v_{\alpha}v_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(v^{3}),

(94)

where

f_{0}(H)=F(H^{2},\ldots,H^{2d}),\qquad g_{0}(H)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{i(d+1-2i)}{d(d-1)}H^{2(i-1)}\frac{\partial F}{\partial\hat{e}^{d}_{i}}\Bigg|_{\hat{e}^{d}_{j}=H^{2j}}.

(95)

For the de Sitter solution to exist, we require the conditions (
89
) to hold. The second of these is equivalent to

\left[\sum u_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right]_{\text{iso}}=\mathcal{G}_{\text{iso}}\neq 0,\qquad\left[\sum n_{i}^{\alpha}\mathcal{G}_{i}\right]_{\text{iso}}=0.

(96)

Making use of the chain rule

\frac{\partial}{\partial H}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial H_{i}},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{\alpha}}=\sum_{i}n_{i}^{(\alpha)}\frac{\partial}{\partial H_{i}},

(97)

and assuming
g_{0}(H)\neq 0
, we immediately see that the condition (
96
) holds automatically. Meanwhile, the first condition in (
89
) yields

\left[\partial_{H}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{F}}\right)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\text{iso}}=0,

(98)

which is equivalent to (
44
) for
f(H)=f_{0}(H)
. It follows that the Einstein frame de Sitter solutions found in the isotropic framework extend naturally to the anisotropic framework. This is because the underlying
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry ensures that deviations from isotropy only enter the theory at
\mathcal{O}(v^{2})\sim\mathcal{O}((H_{i}-H_{j\neq i})^{2})
.

5
Conclusions

Can we obtain de Sitter solutions in string theory? Although perturbative methods provide some rich phenomenology, they have not been able to uncover de Sitter solutions that are universally accepted as consistent and fully under control. This motivates attempts to probe the non-perturbative structure of string theory. Of course, we do not have the tools to do this in full generality but progress can be made by restricting access to the Neveu-Schwarz fields in the homogeneous limit. This is because they admit an
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry that can be extended to higher order operators, extending to all orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
.

The underlying structure of these theories is easy to write down whenever we have a homogeneous and isotropic framework, where the
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry is equivalent to scale factor dulaity
a\to 1/a
. For a suitable non-linear structure, quasi de Sitter solutions with time dependent dilatons can be found in both the string frame and the Einstein frame. As shown previously
[
44
,
46
,
49
,
50
]
, homogeneous and isotropic fluctuations about the string frame solutions can trigger an instability, although we have now shown that this is excited if and only if the generalised dilaton is growing at the initial time
\dot{\Phi}(t_{i})>0
. The situation in the Einstein frame is even more delicate. Quasi de Sitter solutions can and do exist in the Einstein frame, for particular non-perturbative extensions of the Neveu-Schwarz theory. However, these extensions do not connect smoothly to the leading order Neveu-Schwarz theory. This means the Einstein frame de Sitter solutions cannot be eternal to both past and future. In other words, a de Sitter solution can survive for a finite period of time, or even a semi-infinite time, but at some point towards either the future or the past, the solution breaks down. The solution enters a new phase of the theory in a neighbourhood of small string frame curvature, where Einstein frame de Sitter solutions are no longer possible. Perhaps this should have been expected, as the breakdown occurs in the perturbative regime where de Sitter solutions are conjectured to be absent
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

All of these results can be extended to include Bianchi I metrics. The
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry now plays a more interesting role than in the isotropic case, where scale factor duality is accompanied by scale factor exchange
a_{i}\leftrightarrow a_{j}
. This is crucial, especially for the existence of de Sitter solutions in the Einstein frame where the additional constraints required for existence in an anisotropic setting are satisfied automatically thanks to
\textrm{O}(d,d)
symmetry.

Although we have focused our analysis on the case of classical strings in a critical dimension, for which the constant
\Lambda=0
in (
1
), the results are easily extended to non-critical dimensions. This is because a non-vanishing
\Lambda\propto D-D_{c}
is easily absorbed into a redefinition of the function
f
that defines theory to all orders in
\alpha^{\prime}
,

f\to f-2\Lambda.

(99)

This changes the structure of the theory in neighbourhood of the origin, but little else. For the string frame de Sitter solutions in the isotropic framework, this will affect the boundary conditions (
29
), so that we now have
g(0)=-2\Lambda
. Similar relations hold in the anisotropic case. The stability analysis is unchanged. For the Einstein frame, we can revisit the question of whether or not the bespoke choice of
f(H)
can be smoothly connected to the desired form in a neighbourhood of
H=0
. Focusing on the isotropic case, we set
f(H)=-2\Lambda+{d}H^{2}+\mathcal{O}(H^{4})
and compute

\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(H)\mathcal{G}(H)}{\mathcal{F}(H)^{2}}=\frac{d-1}{d}+\mathcal{O}(H).

(100)

Clearly this cannot be set equal to unity, as required for Einstein frame de Sitter solutions with a time dependent dilaton. Thus, we conclude that the situation is qualitatively unchanged from the critical case,
D=D_{c}
, and one cannot have eternal de Sitter solutions in the Einstein frame.

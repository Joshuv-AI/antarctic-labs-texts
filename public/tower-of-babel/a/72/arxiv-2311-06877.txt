A study on the negative binomial distribution motivated by Chvátal’s theorem
Zheng-Yan Guo, Ze-Yu Tao, Ze-Chun Hu

Abstract
Let B ⁡ ( n , p ) B(n,p) denote a binomial random variable with parameters n n and p p . Chvátal’s theorem says that for any fixed n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , as m m ranges over { 0 , … , n } \{0,\ldots,n\} , the probability q m := P ⁡ ( B ⁡ ( n , m / n ) ≤ m ) q_{m}:=P(B(n,m/n)\leq m) is the smallest when m m is closest to 2 ​ n 3 \frac{2n}{3} . Introduction and main result

Let
B(n,p)
denote a binomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
. Chvátal’s theorem says that for any fixed
n\geq 2
, as
m
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the probability
q_{m}:=P(B(n,m/n)\leq m)
is the smallest when
m
is closest to
\frac{2n}{3}
.

Chvátal’s theorem has applications in machine learning (see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and the references therein). As to the history of Chvátal’s theorem, we refer to Barabesi et al.
[
1
]
, Janson
[
5
]
and Sun
[
8
]
.

Motivated by Chvátal’s theorem, the infimum value problem of the probability

P(X\leq E[X])
has been studied in a series paper recently, where
X
is a random variable and
E[X]
is its expectation. In the following, we list the distributions which have been considered in this aspect.

•

The Poisson distribution, the geometric distribution and the Pascal distribution (Li et al.
[
7
]
);

•

The Gamma distribution (Sun et al.
[
9
]
);

•

The Weibull distribution and the Pareto distribution (Li et al.
[
6
]
);

•

The inverse Gaussian, log-normal, Gumbel and logistic distributions (Hu et al.
[
4
]
).

As to the Pascal distribution,
[
7
]
gave only a partial result. Let
B^{*}(r,p)
denote a Pascal random variable with parameters
r
(
r
is a positive integer) and
p\,(0<p\leq 1)
such that

\displaystyle P(B^{*}(r,p)=j)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}j-1\\
r-1\end{array}\right)(1-p)^{j-r}p^{r},\ j=r,r+1,\ldots.

We know that the expectation of
B^{*}(r,p)
is
r/p
. An anonymous referee to the first version of
[
7
]
posed the following
conjecture
:

\displaystyle\inf_{0<p\leq 1}P\left(B^{*}(r,p)\leq\frac{r}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{r}.

(1.2)

[
7
]
proved that (
1.2
) holds for
r=1,2,3
.

The motivation of this note is to give an affirmative answer to (
1.2
) for any positive integer
r
. In fact, we will build the corresponding result for the negative binomial distribution, which extends the Pascal distribution in some sense.

Let
NB(r,p)
denote a negative binomial random variable with parameters
r
(
r
is a positive real number) and
p\,(0<p\leq 1)
such that

\displaystyle P(NB(r,p)=l)=\binom{-r}{l}p^{r}(-q)^{l}=\binom{r+l-1}{l}p^{r}q^{l},\ l=0,1,2,\ldots,

(1.3)

where
q=1-p
. We know that the expectation of
NB(r,p)
is
\frac{rq}{p}
.

The main result of this note is

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
r>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\inf_{0<p\leq 1}P\left(NB(r,p)\leq\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{r}.

(1.4)

Remark 1.2
.

Note that if
r
is a positive integer, then
NB(r,p)+r
is a Pascal random variable with parameters
r
and
p
, and thus

P\left(NB(r,p)\leq\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right)=P\left(NB(r,p)+r\leq\frac{r}{p}\right)=P\left(B^{*}(r,p)\leq\frac{r}{p}\right).

It follows that Theorem
1.1
gives an affirmative answer to Conjecture (
1.2
) for any positive integer
r
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. At first, we prove two lemmas. For convenience, we recall the definitions of the Beta function
\mathbf{B}(a,b)
and the Gamma function
\Gamma(s)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(a,b):=\int_{0}^{1}x^{a-1}(1-x)^{b-1}dx,\ a>0,b>0;

\displaystyle\Gamma(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}e^{-x}dx,\ s>0.

It is well known that

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)},\ \forall a>0,b>0;

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s),\ \forall s>0.

(2.2)

By (
2.2
), for any positive real number
s
, we can define

\displaystyle s!:=\Gamma(s+1).

(2.3)

If
s=n
for a nonnegative integer
n
, then the above definition is just the usual definition, i.e.

\displaystyle n!=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\mbox{if}\ n=0;\\
\Pi_{k=1}^{n}k,&\mbox{if}\ n=1,2,\ldots.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
r\in(0,\infty)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx.

(2.5)

Proof.
Let
\lambda:=\frac{r}{n+r+1}
. Then (
2.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\lambda^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}\left(1-\lambda\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}\int_{0}^{\lambda}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx.

(2.6)

By the binomial theorem, we have

(1-x)^{n}=(-x)^{n}+\binom{n}{n-1}(-x)^{n-1}+\cdots+\binom{n}{1}(-x)+1.

It follows that

\displaystyle x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}=(-1)^{n}x^{n+r-1}+\binom{n}{n-1}(-1)^{n-1}x^{n+r-2}+\cdots+\binom{n}{1}(-1)x^{r}+x^{r-1},

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+r}\lambda^{n+r}+\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n+r-1}\binom{n}{n-1}\lambda^{n+r-1}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)}{r+1}\binom{n}{1}\lambda^{r+1}+\frac{1}{r}\lambda^{r}.

(2.7)

By (
2.7
), we know that (
2.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\lambda^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}\left(1-\lambda\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}\left[\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+r}\lambda^{n+r}+\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n+r-1}\binom{n}{n-1}\lambda^{n+r-1}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)}{r+1}\binom{n}{1}\lambda^{r+1}+\frac{1}{r}\lambda^{r}\right].

(2.8)

Obviously, the left side of the above equality can also be regarded as a polynomial of
\lambda
. Hence, in order to prove (
2
), we need only to check the coefficients for each degree of
\lambda
. We decompose the check into two steps.

Step 1.
To prove that the coefficients of
\lambda^{r}
on both sides of (
2
) are equal.

By (
2
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we know that the coefficient of
\lambda^{r}
on the right side of (
2
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)r}=\frac{\Gamma(n+r+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(r)r}=\frac{(n+r)!}{n!(r-1)!r}=\binom{n+r}{r}.

The coefficient of
\lambda^{r}
on the left side of (
2
) is

\displaystyle\binom{n+r-1}{n}+\binom{n+r-2}{n-1}+\cdots+\binom{r-1}{0}.

By the combinatorial identity that
\binom{m}{k}=\binom{m-1}{k}+\binom{m-1}{k-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\binom{n+r}{n}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+r-1}{n}+\binom{n+r-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+r-1}{n}+\binom{n+r-2}{n-1}+\binom{n+r-2}{n-2}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=\binom{n+r-1}{n}+\binom{n+r-2}{n-1}+\cdots+\binom{r}{1}+\binom{r}{0}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+r-1}{n}+\binom{n+r-2}{n-1}+\cdots+\binom{r}{1}+\binom{r-1}{0}.

Hence the coefficients of
\lambda^{r}
on both sides of (
2
) are equal.

Step 2.
To prove that the coefficients of
\lambda^{r+m}
on both sides of (
2
) are equal for any
1\leq m\leq n
.

The coefficient on the left side is
(-1)^{m}\sum_{i=m}^{n}\binom{i+r-1}{i}\binom{i}{m}
, and the one on the right side is
=(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{r+m}\binom{n}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}
. So it is enough to prove

\displaystyle\sum_{i=m}^{n}\binom{i+r-1}{i}\binom{i}{m}=\frac{1}{r+m}\binom{n}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}.

(2.9)

For
i=m,\ldots,n
, define

\displaystyle a_{i}:=\binom{i+r-1}{i}\binom{i}{m},

(2.10)

\displaystyle b_{i}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{r+m}\binom{m}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(m+1,r)},&\mbox{if}\ i=m,\\
\frac{1}{r+m}\left[\binom{i}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(i+1,r)}-\binom{i-1}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(i,r)}\right],&\mbox{if}\ i=m+1,\ldots,n.\end{array}\right.

Then (
2.9
) becomes

\sum_{i=m}^{n}a_{i}=\sum_{i=m}^{n}b_{i}.

Thus it is enough to show that for any
i=m,\ldots,n
, we have
a_{i}=b_{i}
.

For
i=m
, we have

\displaystyle b_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+m}\binom{m}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(m+1,r)}=\frac{1}{r+m}\cdot\frac{(m+r)!}{m!(r-1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(m+r-1)!}{m!(r-1)!}=\binom{m+r-1}{m}=a_{m}.

For
i=m+1,\ldots,n
, by (
2
), (
2.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+m}\left[\binom{i}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(i+1,r)}-\binom{i-1}{m}\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(i,r)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+m}\left[\frac{i!}{m!(i-m)!}\cdot\frac{(i+r)!}{i!(r-1)!}-\frac{(i-1)!}{m!(i-m-1)!}\cdot\frac{(i+r-1)!}{(i-1)!(r-1)!}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+m}\cdot\frac{1}{m!(r-1)!}\left[\frac{(i+r)!}{(i-m)!}-\frac{(i+r-1)!}{(i-m-1)!}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r+m}\cdot\frac{1}{m!(r-1)!}\cdot\frac{(i+r-1)![(i+r)-(i-m)]}{(i-m)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i+r-1)!}{(r-1)!i!}\cdot\frac{i!}{m!(i-m)!}

\displaystyle=\binom{i+r-1}{i}\binom{i}{m}

\displaystyle=a_{i}.

Hence (
2
) holds and the proof is complete.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
r\in(0,\infty)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-2}\left(\frac{ru}{n+1+r}-1\right)du=\frac{r^{r}(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+r+1)^{n+r+2}}.

(2.14)

Proof.
Let
\mu:=\frac{r}{n+r+1}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{r^{r}(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+r+1)^{n+r+2}}=\frac{1}{r}\mu^{r+1}(1-\mu)^{n+1}.

(2.15)

By the change of variable
x=\frac{1}{u}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-2}\left(\frac{ru}{n+1+r}-1\right)du

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{\mu}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-2}\left(\mu u-1\right)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\mu}(1-x)^{n}x^{r}\left(\frac{\mu}{x}-1\right)dx

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{0}^{\mu}(1-x)^{n}x^{r-1}dx-\int_{0}^{\mu}(1-x)^{n}x^{r}dx

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{0}^{\mu}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}x^{k}\right]x^{r-1}dx-\int_{0}^{\mu}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}x^{k}\right]x^{r}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k+r+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k+1}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k+r+1}

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k+1}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}\right]\mu^{r+1}.

(2.16)

By the binomial theorem, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\mu^{k}x^{k-n}=\left(\frac{1}{x}-\mu\right)^{n}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\mu^{k}x^{r+k-1}

\displaystyle=x^{n+r-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\mu^{k}x^{k-n}

\displaystyle=x^{n+r-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}-\mu\right)^{n},

and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\mu^{k}x^{r+k-1}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{n+r-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}-\mu\right)^{n}dx.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k+1}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\mu^{k}x^{r+k}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{n+r}\left(\frac{1}{x}-\mu\right)^{n}dx.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{r+k+1}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}\mu^{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(x^{n+r-1}-x^{n+r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{x}-\mu\right)^{n}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{r-1}(1-x)\left(1-\mu x\right)^{n}dx

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{x^{r}}{r}(1-\mu x)^{n+1}\right|_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}(1-\mu)^{n+1},

(2.17)

where we used the fact that
\mu=\frac{r}{n+r+1}
and

\displaystyle\frac{d\ \left(\frac{x^{r}}{r}(1-\mu x)^{n+1}\right)}{dx}

\displaystyle=x^{r-1}(1-\mu x)^{n+1}-(n+1)\frac{x^{r}}{r}(1-\mu x)^{n}\cdot\mu

\displaystyle=x^{r-1}(1-\mu x)^{n}\left[1-\mu x-\frac{n+1}{r}\cdot\mu x\right]

\displaystyle=x^{r-1}(1-\mu x)^{n}\left[1-\mu x\left(1+\frac{n+1}{r}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=x^{r-1}(1-x)\left(1-\mu x\right)^{n}.

By (
2.15
)-(
2
), we obtain (
2.14
). The proof is complete.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
By (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle P\left(NB(r,p)\leq\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right]}\binom{k+r-1}{k}p^{r}(1-p)^{k}.

(2.18)

If
\left[\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right]=n
for some non-negative integer
n
, then we have

p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right]
. Now, (
2.18
) becomes

\displaystyle P\left(NB(r,p)\leq\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}p^{r}(1-p)^{k}.

(2.19)

For any
k=0,\ldots,n
, define a function
f_{k}(p):=p^{r}(1-p)^{k},p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right]
. For any
p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln f_{k}(p)}{dp}

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{p}-\frac{k}{1-p}=\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\left[r-(r+k)p\right]

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\left[r-(r+k)\frac{r}{n+r}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{p(1-p)}\left[1-\frac{k+r}{n+r}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 0,

which implies that
\ln f_{k}(p)
is strictly increasing on
\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right]
and thus
f_{k}(p)
is strictly increasing on
\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right]
. Then by (
2.19
), we get that

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1},\frac{r}{n+r}\right]}P\left(NB(r,p)\leq\frac{r(1-p)}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{k}.

(2.20)

Define a sequence

a_{r}(n):=\left(\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+r+1}\right)^{k},\ n=0,1,\ldots.

Obviously, we have

a_{r}(0)=\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{r}.

Then in order to finish the proof, it is enough to show that

a_{r}(n)\geq a_{r}(0),\ \forall n=1,2,\ldots.

In fact, we will prove the following stronger result:

\displaystyle a_{r}(n+1)-a_{r}(n)>0,\ \forall n=0,1,\ldots.

(2.21)

By Lemma 2.1, we have

\displaystyle a_{r}(n)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{B}(n+1,r)}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx.

(2.22)

By (
2.22
), (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle(\ref{2.18})\ \mbox{holds}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\Gamma(n+1+r)}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx<\frac{\Gamma(n+2+r)}{\Gamma(r)\Gamma(n+2)}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+2+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n+1}dx

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{n+1}{n+1+r}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx<\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+2+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n+1}dx

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{r}{n+1+r}\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx<\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+2+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n+1}dx.

(2.23)

By the change of variable
u=\frac{1}{x}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+1+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n}dx=\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-1}du,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{r}{n+2+r}}x^{r-1}(1-x)^{n+1}dx=\int_{\frac{n+2+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2}du.

Then, by (
2
), we have

\displaystyle(\ref{2.18})\ \mbox{holds}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-1}du-\frac{r}{n+1+r}\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-1}du<\int_{\frac{n+2+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2}du

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-1}du-\left(\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}-\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{\frac{n+2+r}{r}}\right)(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2}du

\displaystyle\quad\quad<\frac{r}{n+1+r}\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-1}du

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{\frac{n+2+r}{r}}(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2}du<\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{+\infty}(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-2}\left(\frac{ru}{n+1+r}-1\right)du.

(2.24)

Define a function

g(u):=(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2},\ u\in\left[\frac{n+1+r}{r},\frac{n+2+r}{r}\right].

For any
u\in\left[\frac{n+1+r}{r},\frac{n+2+r}{r}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-3}[n+r+2-(r+1)u]

\displaystyle<(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-3}\left[n+r+2-(r+1)\frac{n+1+r}{r}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n+1)(u-1)^{n}u^{-n-r-3}}{r}

\displaystyle<0,

which implies that

is a strictly decreasing function and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{n+1+r}{r}}^{\frac{n+2+r}{r}}(u-1)^{n+1}u^{-n-r-2}du

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{n+1+r}{r}-1\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{n+1+r}{r}\right)^{-n-r-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{r}(n+1)^{n+1}}{(n+r+1)^{n+r+2}}.

(2.25)

By (
2
) and Lemma 2.2, we obtain that the inequality in (

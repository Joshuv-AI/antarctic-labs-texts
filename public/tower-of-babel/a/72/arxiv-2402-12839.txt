Abstract
. We investigate the critical threshold phenomena in a large class of one dimensional pressureless Euler–Poisson (EP) equations, with non-vanishing background states. First, we establish local-in-time well-posedness in proper regularity spaces, which are adapted for a certain neutrality condition to hold. The neutrality condition is shown to be necessary: we construct smooth solutions that exhibit instantaneous failure of the neutrality condition, which in turn yields non-existence of solutions, even locally in time, in the classical Sobolev spaces H s ​ ( ℝ ) H^{s}(\mathbb{R}) , s ⩾ 2 s\geqslant 2 . Next, we study the critical threshold phenomena in the neutrality-condition-satisfying pressureless EP systems, where we distinguish between two cases. We prove that in the case of attractive forcing, the neutrality condition can further restrict the sub-critical region into its borderline, namely — the sub-critical region is reduced to a single line in the phase plane. We then turn to provide a rather definitive answer for the critical thresholds in the case of repulsive EP systems with variable backgrounds. As an application, we analyze the critical thresholds for the damped EP system for cold plasma ion dynamics, where the density of electrons is given by the Maxwell–Boltzmann relation .

1.
Introduction and statement of main results

1.1.
Systems and notions

In this paper, we are interested in the analysis of critical threshold phenomena in a large class of pressureless Euler–Poisson (EP) equations in one dimension. More precisely, the main purpose of our work is to propose a new method based on Lyapunov functions to investigate the super-critical region with finite-time breakdown and the sub-critical region with global regularity of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solutions to the following equations:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=-\nu u-k\partial_{x}\phi,\\
-\partial_{xx}\phi=\rho-c\end{cases}

in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,T)
subject to initial data

(1.2)

(\rho,u)(0,x)=(\rho_{0},u_{0})(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Here,
\rho=\rho(t,x)
,
u=u(t,x)
and
\phi=\phi(t,x)
are respectively, the density, velocity, and potential of the flow. The parameter
\nu\geqslant 0
indicates the strength of damping. The parameter
k
on the right of (
1.1
)
2
is a physical constant, signifying the underlying forcing of the system — either an
attractive
or
repulsive
forcing depending on whether
k<0
or, respectively,
k>0
; for simplicity, we use the re-scaled parameter
k=\pm 1
throughout the paper.

Equation (
1.1
)
3
involves a positive background state,
c=c(t,x)
, which is assumed to be uniformly bounded away from vacuum,

0<c_{-}\leqslant c(t,x)\leqslant c_{+},\quad\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

to vary smoothly in a manner specified in (
1.8
) below, and to preserve its
mass fluctuation
in the sense that

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(c(t,x)-c_{0}(x)\right)\textnormal{d}x=0,\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

Finally, we augment (
1.1
) with the following
neutrality condition

(1.4)

\rho(t,\cdot)-c(t,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho(t,x)-c(t,x))\,\textnormal{d}x=0\qquad\forall t\in[0,T).

This neutrality condition plays a central role in our discussion. Accordingly, we define a
classical solution
to be a pair,
(\rho,u)
, satisfying both — the system (
1.1
) pointwise, and the neutrality condition (
1.4
).
It is essential to observe that the neutrality condition is independent of the main system (
1.1
). Indeed, in Theorem
1.4
below we construct so-called

, in the sense of solving the main system (
1.1
) with neutrality-satisfying initial data, yet these solutions fail (
1.4
) instantaneously after the initial time.
Observe that since
c(t,x)\geqslant c_{-}>0
, then total mass
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(t,x)\textnormal{d}x
is inifinte; we impose (
1.3
) as a

with the neutrality (
1.4

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{0}(x)\right)\textnormal{d}x

1.3.
Main results

In this section, we present our main results on Euler–Poisson equation with background states. The purpose of this work is four-fold:

(i) we present a new framework for the well-posedness theory for the EP system with the background, which includes a rigorous treatment of the neutrality condition (
1.4
), while justifying this framework by presenting concrete examples;

(ii) we point out that in the attractive case, the set of relevant configurations that yield global smooth solutions — with variable and even with constant background, is, in fact, restricted to the borderline case due to the neutrality condition;

(iii) we present an in-depth threshold analysis for global solutions of the repulsive EP system with variable background; and

(iv) as an application of repulsive case, we study the case of cold plasma corresponding to exponential background
c=e^{\phi}
.

Below, we present the main results in more detail.

1.3.1.
Local well-posedness and neutrality condition

We first develop a local-in-time well-posedness theory for the EP system with a non-vanishing background state (
1.1
).
The EP system with zero-background was studied by many authors, and in particular we refer to the
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
well-posedness in
[
17
, Appendix]
. However, we cannot specify an appropriate reference for the local-in-time well-posedness theory for the EP system with
non-vanishing
background, (
1.1
). Here, the neutrality condition, (
1.4
), implies the non-integrability
\rho(t,\cdot)\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}c(t,\cdot)\textnormal{d}x=\infty

Consequently, the local well-posedness of (
1.1

H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. This is the content of our first theorem. We fix
s\geqslant 2
throughout the paper.

Theorem 1.1

(
Local well-posedness).

Suppose that the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

(1.5)

(\rho_{0}-c_{0},u_{0})\in H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}).

Then there exists a positive constant
T>0
such that the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a unique solution
(\rho,u)
satisfying

(1.6)

(\rho-c,u)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})).

Moreover, if the solutions
\rho
and
\partial_{x}u
are bounded over the time interval
[0,T]
, i.e.

|\|\{\rho,u\}|\|_{[0,T]}:=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left(\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)<\infty,

then there is propagation of higher order regularity of
(\rho,u)
expressed in terms of

|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}:=\|(\rho-c)(t)\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}
,

(1.7)

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}\leqslant C|\|\{\rho_{0}(\cdot),u_{0}(\cdot)\}|\|_{s}e^{\eta T},

where
\eta\sim|\|\{\rho,u\}|\|_{[0,T]}+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1})}+\|\partial_{x}c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})}+1
.

We emphasize that the local well-posedness stated in Theorem
1.1
does not necessarily require the neutrality condition (
1.4
) to hold. On the other hand, the following result shows that it is impossible to construct the solution map without
\dot{H}^{-1}
in (
1.6

1.1
and
1.2
tell us that it is essential to equip

with additional

regularity to secure the local well-posedness.

Theorem 1.2

(
Non-existence).

For the EP system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with
c=\bar{c}>0
, there exists
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying

(\rho_{0}-\bar{c},u_{0})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})

1.1
)–(
1.2

To provide a rigorous treatment of the extended system (

1.3.2.
The case of attractive forcing

In this part, we discuss how the neutrality condition affects the critical threshold phenomenon of the attractive EP system, which is considered to be irrelevant. The threshold conditions for the attractive EP system were derived earlier in
[
13
,
9
,
5
]
. The sub-critical condition for the existence of a global classical solution of the attractive EP system is given by

(1.11)

\left(\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{-}}}{2}\right)u_{0}^{\prime}(x)\geqslant(\rho_{0}(x)-c_{-})\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

where
c_{-}:=c>0
for the constant
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
and
c_{-}:=\min c(\cdot,\cdot)>0
for the variable background case
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
. In particular, this threshold is sharp for the constant background case; if there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}
which fails to satisfy the inequality in (
1.11
), then the solution forms singularity in a finite time. In the following theorem, however, we find that the sub-critical region should be further restricted to its borderline zone — initial configurations satisfying
equality
in (
1.11
), in order for the neutrality condition to be fulfilled.

Theorem 1.5

(
Reduction to the borderline).

Suppose that
(\rho,u)
is a global-in-time classical solution of the attractive EP system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)–(
1.4
), subject to initial data satisfying (
1.11
).
Then, the set of admissible
c(\cdot,\cdot)
’s is necessarily restricted to the constant background, namely —
c(\cdot,\cdot)=c_{-}
and (
1.11
) is reduced the borderline case

(1.12)

\left(\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{-}}}{2}\right)u_{0}^{\prime}(x)=(\rho_{0}(x)-c_{-})\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.5
shows that in order to entertain the neutrality condition, the sub-critical criteria for variable backgrounds, (
1.11
), is too restrictive in the sense that it will rule out all but the borderline cases. In particular, for the constant background case
c=c_{-}
, the solution
(\rho,u)
of neurality-satisfying attractive EP system has global-in-time regularity if and only if its initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfies (
1.12
).

This reduction to the borderline is a main theme in the attractive case. We demonstrate this in the context of variable background,
c=c(t,x)
. Now assume that
|u_{0}(x)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|x|\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.
We integrate (
1.11
) to find

(1.13)

\left(\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{-}}}{2}\right)u_{0}(x){\Big|}^{R}_{-R}\geqslant\int\limits_{-R}^{R}\big(\rho_{0}(x)-c_{0}(x)\big)\textnormal{d}x+\int\limits_{-R}^{R}\big(c_{0}(x)-c_{-}\big)\textnormal{d}x.

By assumption,
u_{0}(x)\Big|^{R}_{-R}
vanishes as
R\uparrow\infty
(in fact, equals zero on the periodic case) and the neutrality condition yields
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(c_{0}(x)-c_{-}\big)\textnormal{d}x=0
, which implies
c_{0}(x)=c_{-}
and we get equality at (
1.13
)
R→∞
. By assumption, both sides tend to zero with
R\uparrow\infty
, hence the equality (
1.11
) takes place almost everywhere. Indeed, for initial data satisfying (
1.11
), we have

\left(\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{-}}}{2}\right)\partial_{x}u(t,x)\geqslant(\rho(t,x)-c_{-})\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

so that we can repeat the previous argument for each
t>0
; in particular, we get
c(\cdot,\cdot)=c_{-}
. In Section
3
, we recover the critical threshold results for attractive EP systems with constant/variable backgrounds via the Lyapunov function method. It captures the solution trajectories in a geometric way so that it can be more intuitively understood than the previous approaches. More importantly, a notable feature of the Lyapunov-based approach is that it goes beyond the attractive forcing case; it can be employed in obtaining the critical thresholds for the repulsive EP systems, in particular, with variable backgrounds.

1.3.3.
The case of repulsive forcing

We now turn our attention to the study of
critical thresholds for the repulsive EP systems. Unlike the attractive interaction case, there is no literature on the sub-critical criteria for the repulsive EP system in the variable backgrounds. We provide the first sub-critical results in this case. In order to present our results more precisely, we must introduce the method of phase plane analysis. To this end, we consider the auxiliary variables
(s,w)
(introduced by H. Liu, S. Engelberg and the last author
[
13
]
in their study of critical thresholds of EP system with constant backgrounds),

s:=\frac{1}{\rho},\quad\mbox{and}\quad w:=\frac{\partial_{x}u}{\rho}.

Let
\square^{\prime}:=\dot{\square}(t,x(t))
denote differentiation along particle path

x(t)=x(t;\alpha)
,

(1.14)

\dot{x}(t;\alpha)=u(t,x(t;\alpha)),\ x(0;\alpha)=\alpha.

We find that (abusing notations)
w(t)=w(t,x(t))
and
s(t)=s(t,x(t))
satisfy

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{split}w^{\prime}&=-\nu w+k(1-cs)\\
s^{\prime}&=w.\end{split}\right.

We observe that the question of global-in-time regularity vs. finite-time breakdown is reduced to whether
s(t)
attains zero —
\rho(t,\cdot)
blows up — in a finite time. There are two classes of initial configurations
(w_{0},s_{0})
of our interest. We say
\Sigma^{\flat}
is a sub-critical region of (
1.15
) if it is an
invariant
set, i.e.,
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\flat}
implies
(w(t),s(t))\in\Sigma^{\flat}
for all
t>0
. On the other hand, we say
\Sigma^{\sharp}
is a super-critical region of (
1.15
) if for each
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\sharp}
, there exists
T_{*}>0
such that
s(T_{*})=0
. Hence, the set of sub/super-critical region gives rise to the criteria of initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
for which the local-in-time classical solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) becomes global or forms singularity in a finite time. Lastly, we say the critical threshold is
sharp
if

\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}=\Sigma^{\flat}\cup\Sigma^{\sharp}.

For the constant backgrounds, where the solutions to (
1.15
) can be obtained explicitly, the sharp critical threshold was obtained in
[
13
]
for the undamped case, precisely given as:

(1.16)

\Sigma^{\flat}=\left\{-\sqrt{s(2-\bar{c}s)}<w<\sqrt{s(2-\bar{c}s)}\right\},\ \Sigma^{\sharp}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\Sigma^{\flat},

where
c=\bar{c}>0
denotes the background. Recently, Bhatnagar and Liu obtained the sharp critical thresholds for the damped case
[
4
]
.

For the variable backgrounds, however, we note that
c=c(t,x(t,\alpha))
in (
1.15
)
2
varies along the characteristic and the system (
1.15
) is not closed. We overcome this non-locality by exploiting the uniform bounds of
c
. We compare the system (
1.15
) to the one associated with its extremes
c=c_{\pm}
, and we establish the critical thresholds for the variable background by the solutions originating from the constant backgrounds. This idea — a Lyapunov-based approach — is widely used for attractive and repulsive force in Sections
3
and
4
. By nature of this comparison method, all our results in the sequel are sharp in the sense that we can recover the sharp critical threshold results for the constant backgrounds once we set
c_{-}=c_{+}
.

Throughout this paper, we use

\mathcal{L}(w,s)=w+f(s)

as a general form of the Lyapunov function.
f
will be chosen so as to make the
sign
of
\mathcal{L}
determine the critical threshold regions. In particular,
\mathcal{L}(w,s)=0
designates the boundary of sub/super-critical regions — often, we will call this by threshold line.

To obtain the threshold lines of the (damped) repulsive EP system, we make use of the following Lyapunov functions,

(1.17)

\mathcal{L}_{P}(w,s):=w+\sqrt{2P(s)},\ \ \mathcal{L}_{N}(w,s):=w-\sqrt{2N(s)},

where
P
and
N
are the maximal solutions of the following ODEs

(1.18)

\frac{dP}{ds}=\nu\sqrt{2P(s)}+1-{c_{1}}s,\ \ \ P(0)=0,

(1.19)

\frac{dN}{ds}=-\nu\sqrt{2N(s)}+1-{c_{2}}s,\ \ \ N(s_{*})=0,

with constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
\nu\geqslant 0
and
s_{*}>\frac{1}{c_{2}}
. The choice of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
will depend on the context, either
\bar{c}
for the constant background or
c_{\pm}
for the variable background case.

To exploit these Lyapunov functions, we characterize the domains of
\sqrt{2P(\cdot)}
and
\sqrt{2N(\cdot)}
. By abusing notation, we denote the domain of
\sqrt{2P(\cdot)}
by
\textnormal{Dom}(P)
. We appeal the parallel statement to
N
. Indeed, the domains are characterized as

(1.20)

\textnormal{Dom}(P)=\begin{cases}[0,\infty)\quad&\nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{c_{1}}\\
[0,\tilde{s}]\quad&0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{c_{1}}\end{cases},\qquad\textnormal{Dom}(N)=\begin{cases}(-\infty,s_{*}]\quad&\nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{c_{2}}\\
[s_{**},s_{*}]\quad&0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{c_{2}}\end{cases},

where

(1.21)

\tilde{s}=\frac{1+e^{\gamma_{1}}}{c_{1}},\ \ \ s_{**}=\frac{1}{c_{2}}-\left(s_{*}-\frac{1}{c_{2}}\right)e^{\gamma_{2}},\ \ \ \gamma_{i}:=\frac{\pi\nu}{\sqrt{4c_{i}-\nu^{2}}},\ \ \ i\in\{1,2\}.

In the context of constructing the sub/super-critical regions, we always choose
s_{*}:=\tilde{s}
so as to make these regions as large as possible. Here, we mention that the formula of

plays a vital role in establishing the critical regions.

The main novelty of this approach originated with M. Bhatnagar & H. Liu in
[
4
]

expressed in terms of a maximal solution of the ODE in

–
s
plane:

\displaystyle\frac{dQ(s)}{ds}=\nu+\frac{1-\bar{c}s}{Q(s)}.

Indeed, while
\sqrt{2P}
and
-\sqrt{2N}
coincide with the maximal solution

]

, here we make a distinction between

and

In Section

1.21

) in
[

]

Comparison Principle.
We now introduce our comparison principles, deploying the Lyapunov functions (
1.17
), to establish the critical thresholds. For this, we denote
P_{\pm}
by the solution of (
1.18
) with
c_{1}=c_{\pm}
and
N_{\pm}
by that of (
1.19
) with
c_{2}=c_{\pm}
. The
weak
comparison principle is given as follows.

(1.23)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{P_{-}}(w(t),s(t))\leqslant 0\ \ \ &\mbox{if}\ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{P_{-}}(w_{0},s_{0})\leqslant 0,\\
\mathcal{L}_{N_{+}}(w(t),s(t))\geqslant 0\ \ \ &\mbox{if}\ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{N_{+}}(w_{0},s_{0})\geqslant 0\end{split}

provided that

\forall\tau\in[0,t],\ \ \ s(\tau)\in\textnormal{Dom}(P_{-})\ \ \left(\textnormal{resp.}\ \ s(\tau)\geqslant 0\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ s(\tau)\in\textnormal{Dom}(N_{+})\right).

This gives rise to the construction of super-critical regions.

\Sigma^{\sharp}:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\left\{-\sqrt{2P_{-}(s)}<w\right\}\ \ \textnormal{for}\ \ \nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{c_{-}},\ \mbox{and}

(1.24)

\Sigma^{\sharp}:=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\left\{-\sqrt{2P_{-}(s)}<w<\sqrt{2N_{+}(s)}\right\}\ \ \textnormal{for}\ \ 0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{c_{-}}.

On the other hand, we have
strong
comparison principle.

(1.25)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{P_{+}}(w(t),s(t))>0\ \ \ &\mbox{if}\ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{P_{+}}(w_{0},s_{0})>0,\\
\mathcal{L}_{N_{-}}(w(t),s(t))<0\ \ \ &\mbox{if}\ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{N_{-}}(w_{0},s_{0})<0\end{split}

as long as

\forall\tau\in[0,t],\ \ \ s(\tau)\in\textnormal{Dom}(P_{+})\ \ \left(\textnormal{resp.}\ \ s(\tau)\geqslant 0\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ s(\tau)\in\textnormal{Dom}(N_{-})\right).

It leads us to define

\Sigma^{\flat}:=\left\{-\sqrt{2P_{+}(s)}<w\right\}\ \ \textnormal{for}\ \ \nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{c_{+}},\ \mbox{and}

(1.26)

\Sigma^{\flat}:=\left\{-\sqrt{2P_{+}(s)}<w<\sqrt{2N_{-}(s)}\right\}\ \ \textnormal{for}\ \ 0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{c_{+}}.

It is not always the case that the set
\Sigma^{\flat}
(

Our final result is finite-time singularity formation and the global regularity of the damped EP system for cold plasma ion dynamics. Consider a plasma consisting of electrons and ions on

where the density of electrons

\rho_{e}=e^{\phi}
, where

Note that the system (

. We consider the system (
1.32
) around a constant state

(1.33)

(\rho,u)(t,x)\to(1,0)\quad\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty.

We notice from the above that

as

. For the system (
1.32
)–(
1.33

In order to apply the framework developed in Section

. To this end, we introduce the total energy function

, which is nonnegative for all

.

Then, as long as there exist classical solutions, one can readily check that the total energy is not increasing in time, i.e.,

for all

, and conserved in time when
\nu=0
. It was investigated in

2.1.
Local well-posedness and Non-existence

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho-c\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}}+\|u\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C\left(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}c\|_{H^{s}}+1\right)\left(\|\rho-c\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}}+\|u\|_{H^{s+1}}\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-1}},

where

In fact, the proofs of Theorems

and

The damped case can be treated in a similar way. Our analysis relies on the characteristic method and, especially, the explicit formulation of Jacobian of the characteristic flow. We briefly explain the main tools and the strategy.

For a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solution
(\rho,u)
to EP system (

defined in (

\Gamma(t,\alpha):=\partial_{\alpha}x(t,\alpha),

signifying the Jacobian of the map

\Gamma(0,\alpha)=1
and

is a diffeomorphism as long as
\Gamma(t,\cdot)>0
. In particular, we have

(2.4)

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\pm\infty}x(t,\alpha)=\pm\infty.

Taking
\partial_{\alpha}
to (

(2.5)

\partial_{x}u(t,x(t,\alpha))=\frac{\partial_{t}\Gamma(t,\alpha)}{\Gamma(t,\alpha)}.

In view of the mass conservation
\rho^{\prime}=-\rho\partial_{x}u

(2.6)

\rho(t,x(t,\alpha))=\frac{\rho_{0}(\alpha)}{\Gamma(t,\alpha)}.

Finally, we recall the explicit formulas of
\Gamma(t,\alpha)
for the undamped (
\nu=0
) case
[

]
. They will lay the foundation of the proofs throughout this section.

\bullet
Repulsive case:

(2.7)

\Gamma(t,\alpha)=1+\left(\frac{\rho_{0}(\alpha)}{\bar{c}}-1\right)(1-\cos(\sqrt{\bar{c}}t))+u_{0}^{\prime}(\alpha)\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\bar{c}}t)}{\sqrt{\bar{c}}},

\bullet
Attractive case:

(2.8)

\Gamma(t,\alpha)=1+\left(\frac{\rho_{0}(\alpha)}{\bar{c}}-1\right)(1-\cosh(\sqrt{\bar{c}}t))+u_{0}^{\prime}(\alpha)\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\bar{c}}t)}{\sqrt{\bar{c}}}.

We now prove Theorem
1.2
based on a contradiction argument. Assuming the existence of a solution satisfying (
2.9
), subject to “non-neutral” initial data, i.e.,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{0}(x)-\bar{c})\,\textnormal{d}x\neq 0,

the non-neutral effect prevents
u
from decaying at the far field that
u(t,\cdot)\notin H^{s+1}
as soon as

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We set initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
by assigning
u_{0}=0
and
\rho_{0}=\bar{c}+g(x)
where

g(x)\in L^{1}\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\neq 0.

We stress that
g(x)\notin\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
; otherwise, it would violate the
non-vanishing integral
condition above. Indeed, there are infinitely many such functions; for instance, we choose
g(x)=(1+x^{2})^{-1}
in this context.

Suppose, by way of deriving a contradiction, that there exists a solution to EP system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) satisfying

(2.9)

(\rho-\bar{c},u)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,\delta];H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})\right)

for some
\delta>0
. We note that this, in particular, requires

to decay at the far field,

(2.10)

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\pm\infty}u(t,\alpha)=0,\ \ \ \forall t\in[0,\delta].

To investigate the solution behavior at infinity, we observe that
\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}
is uniformly bounded in

. Hence, we can employ the forward characteristic
x(t,\cdot)
defined in (
1.14
). For each
t\in[0,\delta]
and

, we infer from (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(t,x(t,R))-u(t,x(t,-R))&=\int_{x(t,-R)}^{x(t,R)}(\partial_{x}u)(t,x)\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\int_{R}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)(t,x(t,\alpha))\textnormal{d}x(t,\alpha)\\
&=\int_{-R}^{R}\partial_{t}\Gamma(t,\alpha)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha.\end{aligned}

Hence, an application of the explicit formula (
2.7
), for the case of repulsive forcing, supplies that

u(t,x(t,R))-u(t,x(t,-R))=\int_{-R}^{R}g(\alpha)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha\cdot\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\bar{c}}t)}{\sqrt{\bar{c}}}.

Then, by taking

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\left(u(t,x(t,R))-u(t,x(t,-R))\right)\neq 0,

yielding a contradiction to (

2.4

∎

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\rho-c\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\partial_{x}((\rho-c)u)\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}(cu)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\rho-c\|_{H^{1}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{1}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}c\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{1}}+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}c\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}^{2}

and

\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho-c\|_{L^{1}}\leqslant 2|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho-c\|_{L^{1}}.

By adding these two inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\rho-c\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant(1+\|c\|_{L^{\infty}})\left(\|\rho-c\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{1}}\right)+4|\|\{\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\}|\|_{s}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{x}c\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Applying the Grönwall’s lemma to the above yields the desired result (
1.10
), and the theorem follows.
∎

For the case of constant background, we identify the necessary and sufficient condition of the neutrality condition. It plays a central role in constructing the
anomalous
solutions.

Lemma 2.1

(
Neutrality condition: constant background).

Consider the EP system (
1.1
) with
c=\bar{c}>0
.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
with neutrality satisfying initial data, i.e,

(2.11)

\rho_{0}-\bar{c}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\text{ with }\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{0}(x)-\bar{c})\,\textnormal{d}x=0.

Then, the propagation of neutrality condition (
1.4
) holds if and only if

u_{0}\in BV(\mathbb{R}),\text{ with }\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{\prime}_{0}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x=0.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
x(t,\alpha)
be the forward characteristic flow satisfying (
1.14
). Note that
\Gamma(t,\cdot)>0
is well-defined for each
t\in[0,T)
. We claim that

\rho(t,\cdot)-\bar{c}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\Gamma(t,\cdot)-1\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

In this context, we apply (
2.6
) to see that

\displaystyle\int_{x(t,-R)}^{x(t,R)}|\rho(t,x)-\bar{c}|\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{-R}^{R}|\rho(t,x(t,\alpha))-\bar{c}|\,\textnormal{d}x(t,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{-R}^{R}\left|\frac{\rho_{0}(\alpha)}{\Gamma(t,\alpha)}-\bar{c}\right|\Gamma(t,\alpha)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha

\displaystyle=\int_{-R}^{R}|(\rho_{0}(\alpha)-\bar{c})-\bar{c}(\Gamma(t,\alpha)-1)|\,\textnormal{d}\alpha.

On applying the triangle’s inequality with (
2.4
), the claim follows. Hence, we deduce from the explicit formulas of
\Gamma(t,\cdot)-1
recorded in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that
u_{0}^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if
\Gamma(t,\cdot)-1\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which is equivalent to
\rho(t,\cdot)-\bar{c}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

We now assume that
u^{\prime}_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly, in view of (
2.6
), one sees that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho(t,x)-\bar{c})\,\textnormal{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{0}(\alpha)-\bar{c})-\bar{c}(\Gamma(t,\alpha)-1)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha=-\bar{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Gamma(t,\alpha)-1)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha.

By inserting (
2.7
), (
2.8
) into
\Gamma(t,\cdot)-1
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, we discern that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho(t,x)-\bar{c})\,\textnormal{d}x=0\quad\mbox{ if and only if }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}^{\prime}(\alpha)\,\textnormal{d}\alpha=0.

This confirms the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth noting that if
u_{0}\notin BV(\mathbb{R})
, we infer that the local
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solution instantaneously fails the neutrality condition by taking

anomalous

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We fix
\rho_{0}=\bar{c}>0
. It satisfies (
2.11
), evidently. In view of Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
, we aim to choose
u_{0}
satisfying

u_{0}\in H^{s+1}\setminus BV(\mathbb{R}).

Consider

u_{0}(x):=\frac{\sin x}{(1+x^{2})^{\frac{3}{8}}}.

Since

∎

We, therefore, conclude from Theorems

3.
Attractive forcing and the restricted borderline threshold

In this section, we focus on the critical threshold phenomenon of the attractive EP system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). To apply the method of phase plane analysis introduced in Section
1.3.3
, we consider the following ODE system:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{split}w^{\prime}&=-\nu w-(1-c(t)s),\\
s^{\prime}&=w,\end{split}\right.

subject to
(w(0),s(0))=(w_{0},s_{0})
, where
c(t)
is smooth function satisfying
0<c_{-}\leqslant c(t)\leqslant c_{+}
.

3.1.
Constant background case

To give a motivation for the Lyapunov function, we initiate the discussion for the constant background case —
c(t)=\bar{c}
. In this case, (
3.1
) can be rewritten in the matrix form as,

(3.2)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w\\
\displaystyle s-\frac{1}{\bar{c}}\end{array}\right)^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\nu&{\bar{c}}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w\\
\displaystyle s-\frac{1}{\bar{c}}\end{array}\right).

The dynamics of solution trajectories are determined by the eigenvalues of its coefficient matrix, whose eigenvalues are given by

\lambda_{s}=\frac{-\nu-\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4\bar{c}}}{2}<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{u}=\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4\bar{c}}}{2}>0,

with its corresponding eigenvectors
X_{s}=\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda_{s}\\
1\end{array}\right)
and

X_{u}=\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\lambda_{u}\\
1\end{array}\right)
.
In particular, the equilibrium point
(0,1/\bar{c})
of (
3.2
) is unstable; the solution curves are attracted to
(0,1/\bar{c})
along
X_{s}
direction while repulsed from
(0,1/\bar{c})
along
X_{u}
direction.

This motivates us to define the following Lyapunov functions,

\mathcal{L}_{s}(w,s):=w-\lambda_{s}\left(s-\frac{1}{\bar{c}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{u}(w,s):=w-\lambda_{u}\left(s-\frac{1}{\bar{c}}\right).

In particular, the solution
(w(t),s(t))
of (
3.2
) satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{s}(w(t),s(t))=\lambda_{u}\mathcal{L}_{s}(w(t),s(t))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{u}(w(t),s(t))=\lambda_{s}\mathcal{L}_{u}(w(t),s(t)),

obtaining

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}_{s}(w(t),s(t))=\mathcal{L}_{s}(w_{0},s_{0})e^{\lambda_{u}t}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \mathcal{L}_{u}(w(t),s(t))=\mathcal{L}_{u}(w_{0},s_{0})e^{\lambda_{s}t}.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{s}(w_{0},s_{0})\geqslant 0
implies
\mathcal{L}_{s}(w(t),s(t))\geqslant 0
for all
t\geqslant 0
. This, combined with
s^{\prime}=w
and that
w
-intercept of the line
\mathcal{L}_{s}(w,s)=0
is positive, yields that
\{\mathcal{L}_{s}(w,s)\geqslant 0,s>0\}
is
invariant
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{L}_{s}(w_{0},s_{0})<0
, then (
3.3
) implies that

\mathcal{L}_{s}(w(t),s(t))\to-\infty,\ \ \mathcal{L}_{u}(w(t),s(t))\to 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

By noticing

\mathcal{L}_{s}(w,s)-\mathcal{L}_{u}(w,s)=\left(-\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{u}\right)\left(s-\frac{1}{\bar{c}}\right),

we conclude that
s(t)
attains zero in a finite time. This argument recovers the sharp critical threshold for the attractive EP with constant background
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
.

3.2.
Variable background case

To study the critical threshold for the variable background case, we consider the following Lyapunov functions:

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\pm}(w,s):=w-\lambda_{s}^{\pm}\left(s-\frac{1}{c_{\pm}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{u}^{-}(w,s):=w-\lambda_{u}^{-}\left(s-\frac{1}{c_{-}}\right),

where

\lambda_{s}^{\pm}=\frac{-\nu-\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{\pm}}}{2}<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{u}^{\pm}=\frac{-\nu+\sqrt{\nu^{2}+4c_{\pm}}}{2}>0.

We readily check that

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-}(w(t),s(t))\geqslant\lambda_{u}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-}(w(t),s(t)),\quad s(t)\geqslant 0.

Similarly, this implies that
\left\{\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-}(w,s)\geqslant 0,s>0\right\}
is a sub-critical region.
On the other hand, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{+}(w(t),s(t))\leqslant\lambda_{u}^{+}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{+}(w(t),s(t)),\ \ \ \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{u}^{-}(w(t),s(t))\geqslant\lambda_{s}^{-}\mathcal{L}_{u}^{-}(w(t),s(t)),\ \ \ s(t)\geqslant 0.

By Grönwall’s lemma, we observe that

\begin{split}\left(-\lambda_{s}^{+}+\lambda_{u}^{-}\right)s(t)&=\mathcal{L}_{s}^{+}(w(t),s(t))-\mathcal{L}_{u}^{-}(w(t),s(t))+\frac{1}{\lambda_{u}^{+}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{s}^{-}}\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{s}^{+}(w_{0},s_{0})e^{\lambda_{u}^{+}t}+\mathcal{O}(1)\end{split}

as long as
s(t)
is non-negative; in particular, if
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{+}(w_{0},s_{0})<0
, then
s(\cdot)
attains zero in a finite time. Hence, we obtain the sub/super-critical thresholds for attractive EP systems with variable backgrounds. They coincide with
[
5
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3]
.

4.1.
Finite-time breakdown

In this subsection, we prove that finite breakdown occurs for the super-critical initial data. To this end, we present the following lemma, which is crucially utilized in obtaining an upper bound of the maximal time for which a local smooth solution persists.

Lemma 4.1
.

For the solution
(w(t),s(t))
of (
4.1
), the following identity holds:

t_{2}-t_{1}\leqslant\int_{s(t_{2})}^{s(t_{1})}\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\sqrt{2P_{-}(s)}},

provided that

w_{0}+\sqrt{2P_{-}(s_{0})}\leqslant 0

and
s(\tau)\in\textnormal{Dom}(P_{-})
for all
\tau\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
. Indeed, we have equality in the integral if
c(t)=c_{-}
and
w_{0}+\sqrt{2P_{-}(s_{0})}=0
.

Proof.

By change of coordinates
s=s(t)
, we have

\int_{s(t_{2})}^{s(t_{1})}\,\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\sqrt{2P(s)}}=\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{w(t)}{\sqrt{2P(s(t))}}\,\textnormal{d}t\geqslant\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}(-1)\,\textnormal{d}t=t_{2}-t_{1},

where the inequality comes from the weak comparison principle (
1.23
). Under the latter assumption, we note that (
4.2
) turns the inequality into equality. This completes the proof.
∎

Several remarks are in order. Suppose that
P
satisfies (
1.18
) with
c_{1}=\bar{c}
.

Remark 4.2
.

For
0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{\bar{c}}
case, we obtain an explict integral formula:

\int_{\textnormal{Dom}(P)}\,\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\sqrt{2P(s)}}=\frac{\pi}{\mu_{\bar{c}}},\ \ \textnormal{with}\ \ \mu_{\bar{c}}:=\sqrt{\bar{c}-\frac{1}{4}\nu^{2}}.

The initial condition in (

for any

. In particular, for
\nu=0

\int_{\frac{2}{\bar{c}}-a}^{a}\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\sqrt{(a-s)(\bar{c}(a+s)-2)}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\bar{c}}}.

Remark 4.3
.

For
\nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{\bar{c}}
case, we have

\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\,\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\sqrt{2P(s)}}\leqslant\frac{\log{(\lambda s_{0})}\vee 2}{\lambda},\ \ \textnormal{with}\ \ \lambda:=\frac{\nu-\sqrt{\nu^{2}-4\bar{c}}}{2}>0,

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\nu\geqslant 2\sqrt{c_{-}}
. For any
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\sharp}=\{(w,s):w\leqslant-\sqrt{2P_{-}(s)},\ s>0\}
, the solution to (

Proof.

We claim that

with

Suppose not, i.e., there exists

for all

satisfying

t_{*}>T_{0}
.
Then, by weak comparison principle (

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose that
0\leqslant\nu<2\sqrt{c_{-}}
. For any

(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\sharp}=\{(w,s):s>0\}\setminus\left\{(w,s):-\sqrt{2P_{-}(s)}<w<\sqrt{2N_{+}(s)},s>0\right\},

\bullet

For

\bullet
For
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\sharp}_{2}

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\theta_{-}(w,s)}{\sqrt{c_{-}}}=&\frac{1}{1+c_{-}\left(\frac{s-\frac{1}{c_{-}}}{w}\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{w^{2}-\left(s-\frac{1}{c_{-}}\right)\left(-\nu w+1-c(t)s\right)}{w^{2}}\right)\\

Henceforth, we deduce that, for any
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\sharp}
,
s(t_{*})=0
with

4.2.
Global-in-time regularity

To establish the global regularity of the initial data in sub-critical regime, we prove the strong comparison principle (

) in the sequel. Recall from (
4.2
), (
4.3
) that if
s(\tau)
satisfies

and (resp.

) for all

(4.5)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{P_{+}}(w(t),s(t))\geqslant\mathcal{L}_{P_{+}}(w_{0},s_{0})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-c_{+}s(\tau)}{\sqrt{2P_{+}(s(\tau))}}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\right),\cr\mathcal{L}_{N_{-}}(w(t),s(t))\leqslant\mathcal{L}_{N_{-}}(w_{0},s_{0})\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{c_{-}s(\tau)-1}{\sqrt{2N_{-}(s(\tau))}}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\right).\end{split}

The proof for the strict inequalities in the strong comparison principle (

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

\Sigma^{\flat}=\left\{(w,s):-\sqrt{2P_{+}(s)}<w,s>0\right\}

Proof.

We prove that for any
(w_{0},s_{0})\in\Sigma^{\flat}
, the solution to (

) satisfies

for all

. Suppose not, i.e., there exists

such that

In particular, we have
(w(T_{*}),s(T_{*}))\in\partial\Sigma^{\flat}
. We decompose the boundary of
\Sigma^{\flat}
into two subsets:

\partial\Sigma^{\flat}=\partial\Sigma^{\flat}_{1}\cup\partial\Sigma^{\flat}_{2}:=\left\{w+\sqrt{2P_{+}(s)}=0,\ s\geqslant 0\right\}\cup\left\{w>0,\ s=0\right\}.

We shall demonstrate that

leads to a contradiction. Indeed, the nature of proof is the same for

Proposition 4.7
.

Let

\bullet

We begin by discussing the smallness assumption on

where

which is defined by

(4.7)

V(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle V_{+}(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\sqrt{2U(r)}\,\textnormal{d}r&\textrm{for $z\geqslant 0$},\\[8.53581pt]

Abstract
. We show that a Busemann space X X which is covered by parallel bi-infinite geodesics is homeomorphic to a product of another Busemann space Y Y and the real line. We also show that a semi-simple isometry on X X preserving the foliation by parallel geodesics canonically induces a semi-simple isometry on Y Y .

1.
Introduction

There are several generalization of the class of simply connected Riemannian
manifolds with nonpositive sectional curvature. One of the most studied class
is that of CAT(0) spaces.
There are many results on the structure of CAT(0) spaces. One of the most fundamental one
is the following. We say that two geodesics
\gamma,\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
on a metric space
X

is
parallel
if
d(\gamma(t),\eta(t))
is bounded.

Theorem 1.1

(A Product Decomposition Theorem
[
2
, II 2.14 ]
)
.

Let
X
be a CAT(0) space and let
c\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.

(1)

Let
X_{c}
be the union of the images of all geodesics
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X

parallel to
c
. Then
X_{c}
is a convex subspace of
X
.

(2)

Let
p
be the restriction to

X_{c}
of the nearest point projection from
X
to the image
of the geodesic
c(\mathbb{R})
. Let

X_{c}^{0}:=p^{-1}(c(0))
. Then,
X_{c}^{0}
is
convex, especially it is a CAT(0) space.

(3)

X_{c}
is isometric to the product
X_{c}^{0}\times\mathbb{R}
.

The proof of the above decomposition theorem depends on the nice
behaviour of the nearest point projection onto a bi-infinite geodesic.
It seems hard to generalize
theorem

1.1
to
more general class of metric spaces called
Busemann spaces
,
due to the luck of such nice property of the projections.
So we considered slightly weaker statement,
and we obtained the following topological decomposition theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(A Topological Product Decomposition Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Busemann space and let
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.

(1)

Let
X_{\omega}
be the union of the images of all geodesics
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X

parallel to
\omega
. Then
X_{\omega}
is a convex subspace of
X
.

(2)

Let
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of the images of all geodesics
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X

parallel to
\omega
. We equip
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
with the Hausdorff metric. Then

\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is a Busemann space.

(3)

X_{\omega}
is homeomorphic to the product
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}
.

In the above theorem, (
1
) is a direct consequence of
the
Strip Lemma
(
lemma

3.5
) by Bowditch
[
1
]
.
Our essential contribution is to show (
2
) and (
3
)
in
theorem

1.2
.

We also study the classification of isometries on
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
induced by
those of on
X_{\omega}
.
For a map
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
, we denote by
\bar{\omega}
the map defined as

\bar{\omega}(t):=\omega(-t)
. We say that an isometry

g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
preserve the foliation by
\omega
if

g\circ\omega
or
g\circ\bar{\omega}
is parallel to
\omega
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a Busemann space and let
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.
Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be an isometry preserving the foliation
by
\omega
.
Then
g
induces an isometry
g_{\omega}\colon\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
by

g_{\omega}(L)=g(L)
for
L\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, the following holds.

(1)

If
g
is elliptic, then so is
g_{\omega}
.

(2)

If
g_{\omega}
is elliptic, then
g
is either elliptic or hyperbolic.

(3)

Suppose
g
is hyperbolic.
Let
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be an axis of
g
.
If
\xi
is parallel to either
\omega
or
\bar{\omega}
,
then
g_{\omega}
is elliptic. Otherwise,
g_{\omega}
is hyperbolic.

(4)

If
g_{\omega}
is hyperbolic, then so is
g
.

Especially,
g
is semi-simple if and only if so is
g_{\omega}
.

2.
Combing and Busemann space

2.1.
Busemann space

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A map
\gamma\colon I\to X

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is a closed interval
is a
geodesic

if for all
t,s\in I
, we have

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=\left\lvert t-s\right\rvert
.
We say that
(X,d)
is a
geodesic space
if for any pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
,
there exists a geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,d(x,y)]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=x
and

\gamma(d(x,y))=y
.
We also say that
(X,d)
is a
uniquely geodesic space

if it is a geodesic space and
for any pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
, a geodesic between
x
and
y
is unique.
A
linearly reparametrised geodesic
is a map of the form
[t\mapsto\gamma(\lambda t)]

where
\gamma
is a geodesic and
\lambda
is a positive real number.

Definition 2.1
.

A geodesic space
(X,d)
is a
Busemann space
if, for any two
linearly reparametrised geodesics
\gamma,\eta\colon[0,1]\to X
,
the map
[t\mapsto d(\gamma(t),\eta(t))]
is convex in
t
.

It is easy to see that a Busemann space is a uniquely geodesic space.

2.2.
Geodesic bicombing

A geodesic bicombing
\sigma
on
X
is a map

\displaystyle\sigma\colon X\times X\times[0,1]\to X

such that for every pair
(x,y)\in X\times X
, the map

\sigma_{xy}:=\sigma(x,y,\cdot)\colon[0,1]\to X
is a linearly reparametrised geodesic
with
\sigma_{xy}(0)=x
and
\sigma_{xy}(1)=y
.

(1)

We say that
\sigma
is
conical
if for all
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in X
and
t\in[0,1]
,
we have

\displaystyle d(\sigma_{xy}(t),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(t))\leq(1-t)d(\sigma_{xy}(0),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(0))+td(\sigma_{xy}(1),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(1)).

(2)

We say that
\sigma
is
convex
if the map

[t\mapsto d(\sigma_{xy}(t),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(t))]
is convex for all
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in X
, that is,
for any
a,b\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle d(\sigma_{xy}((1-t)a+tb),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}((1-t)a+tb))

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)d(\sigma_{xy}(a),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(a))+td(\sigma_{xy}(b),\sigma_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}(b)).

(3)

We say that
\sigma
is
consistent
if for all
x,y\in X
,

0\leq a\leq b\leq 1
,
p:=\sigma_{xy}(a)
,
q:=\sigma_{xy}(b)
,
and
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{pq}(t)=\sigma_{xy}((1-t)a+tb).

Example 2.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a uniquely geodesic space.
Then
X
admits a
geodesic bicombing
\sigma
such that

\sigma_{xy}
is a unique linearly reparametrised
geodesic between
x
and
y
.
We call this the
canonical bicombing
.
Obviously, the canonical bicombing is consistent.

We remark that if a geodesic bicombing is conical and consistent, then it is convex.
Thus we have the following criterion.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a uniquely geodesic space.
If the canonical bicombing is conical, then it is convex.
Especially,
X
is a Busemann space.

3.
Parallel geodesics in Busemann space

Let
(X,d)
be a Busemann space.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic. For
x\in X
, there exists unique

y\in\gamma(\mathbb{R})
such that

d(x,y)=d(x,\gamma(\mathbb{R}))=\inf\{d(x,w):w\in\gamma(\mathbb{R})\}
.

The following argument is due to Kato
[
4
]
.

Proof..

Since
\mathbb{R}
is proper, so is the image
\gamma(\mathbb{R})
. It follows that
there exists
y\in\gamma(\mathbb{R})
such that
d(x,y)=d(x,\gamma(\mathbb{R}))
.

Now we show that such
y
is unique. Set
r:=d(x,\gamma(\mathbb{R}))
.
Suppose that there exists
y,z\in\gamma(\mathbb{R})

with
y\neq z
such that
d(x,y)=d(x,z)=r
.
Let
\sigma
be the canonical bicombing of
X
.
Set
m=\sigma_{yz}(1/2)
. By the convexity, and the minimality,
d(x,m)=r
.
By the convexity of the map

[t\mapsto d(\sigma_{yx}(t),\sigma_{yz}(t))]
, we have

d(\sigma_{yx}(1/2),m)\leq r/2
. By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle r=d(x,m)\leq d(x,\sigma_{yx}(1/2))+d(\sigma_{yx}(1/2),m)\leq r.

So
d(\sigma_{yx}(1/2),m)=r/2
.
By the same argument, we have
d(\sigma_{zx}(1/2),m)=r/2
.
Then by the uniqueness of the geodesic between
x
and
m
, we have

\sigma_{yx}(1/2)=\sigma_{zx}(1/2)
.
So set
x(1):=\sigma_{yx}(1/2)=\sigma_{zx}(1/2)
.
Replacing
x
by
x(1)
, we apply the same argument and
we obtain
x(2)\in X
such that

d(x(2),y)=d(x(2),m)=d(x(2),z)=r/2^{2}
.
Now we can apply inductively
the same arguments and obtain
x(n)\in X
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

d(x(n),y)=d(x(n),m)=d(x(n),z)=r/2^{n}
. It follows that

d(y,z)\leq r/2^{n-1}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

We say that two bi-infinite geodesics

\gamma,\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
are
parallel
if
d(\gamma(t),\eta(t))
is bounded.
This define an equivalence relation on the set of bi-infinite geodesics.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\gamma,\eta
be parallel geodesics. For any constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto d(\gamma(t),\eta(t+C))\in\mathbb{R}

is constant.

Proof..

Since the map
[t\mapsto d(\gamma(t),\eta(t+C))]
is convex, if it is bounded,
it must be constant.
∎

For closed subsets
H,K\subset X
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}(H,K)
the Hausdorff metric between

H
and
K
. For bi-infinite geodesics
\gamma,\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
, we abbreviate
the Hausdorff metric of their images
\mathcal{H}(\gamma(\mathbb{R}),\eta(\mathbb{R}))
to

\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\eta)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\gamma,\eta
be parallel geodesics.
For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\eta)=d(\gamma(t),\eta(\mathbb{R}))=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}d(\gamma(t),\eta(s)).

Bowditch showed the following Strip Lemma.

Lemma 3.5

(Strip Lemma
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a Busemann space. Let
r>0
. We suppose that

\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
are parallel geodesics such that

d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))=r
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\beta_{t}\colon[0,r]\to X
be the geodesic from

\gamma(t)
to
\gamma^{\prime}(t)
. Define a map
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\times[0,r]\to X

by
\beta(t,u):=\beta_{t}(u)
. Then:

(1)

for each
u\in[0,r]
, the map
\gamma_{u}:=[t\mapsto\beta(t,u)]

is a bi-infinite geodesic parallel to
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
;

(2)

for each
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the maps

[u\mapsto\beta(t_{0}+\lambda u,u)]
is a linearly reparametrised geodesic.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be parallel geodesics
such that
d(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
.
Set
r=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})
.
For
u\in[0,r]
, let
\gamma_{u}
be the geodesic given by
lemma

3.5

which is parallel to
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
. Then for
u,v\in[0,r]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{u},\gamma_{v})=\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert.

Proof..

Let
\beta_{0}\colon[0,r]\to X
be the geodesic from
\gamma(0)
to
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
.
For
u\in[0,r]
, by the construction of
\gamma_{u}
, we have
\gamma_{u}(0)=\beta_{0}(u)
, so

\beta_{0}(u)\in\gamma_{u}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then for
0\leq u\leq v\leq r
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{u},\gamma_{v})\leq d(\beta_{0}(u),\beta_{0}(v))=\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert.

Suppose
\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{u},\gamma_{v})<\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert
. Then

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma_{u})+\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{u},\gamma_{v})+\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{v},\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle<d(\beta_{0}(0),\beta_{0}(u))+d(\beta_{0}(u),\beta_{0}(v))+d(\beta_{0}(v),\beta_{0}(r))=r.

This is contradiction. Therefore we have
\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{u},\gamma_{v})=\left\lvert u-v\right\rvert
.
∎

The following is the key to the uniqueness of geodesics in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
be a pairwise parallel bi-infinite geodesics in
X

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})+\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}).

Let
x\in\gamma_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
y\in\gamma_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
z\in\gamma_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.
If
d(x,y)=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
d(x,z)=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})
holds,
then we have
d(y,z)=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})
.

Proof..

We choose
z^{\prime}\in\gamma_{3}(\mathbb{R})
such that
d(y,z^{\prime})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})

\displaystyle\leq d(x,z^{\prime})\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})+\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3}).

It follows that
d(x,z^{\prime})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{3})
.
Since such
z^{\prime}\in\gamma_{3}(\mathbb{R})
is unique, we have
z=z^{\prime}
, and so

d(y,z)=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})
.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic. We denote by
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
the set of
all geodesic
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
which is parallel to
\omega
.
We also denote by

\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
the set of images of geodesic in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
,
and denote by
X_{\omega}
the union of the images of geodesic in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})=\{\gamma(\mathbb{R}):\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}\},\quad X_{\omega}=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}}\gamma(\mathbb{R})=\bigcup_{L\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})}L.

It follows from
lemma

3.5
that
X_{\omega}
is a convex subspace of
X
.
We equip
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
with a Hausdorff metric
\mathcal{H}

so that
(\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{H})
is a metric space.
Now we construct a geodesic bicombing on
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\gamma,\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
. We suppose that
r:=d(\gamma(0),\eta(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\eta)
.
Let
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\times[0,r]\to X
be a map given in
lemma

3.5
.
For
u\in[0,r]
, set
\gamma_{u}=[t\mapsto\beta(t,u)]
as in
lemma

3.5
.
Then a map
[u\mapsto\gamma_{u}(\mathbb{R})]
is a geodesic from
\gamma(\mathbb{R})
to
\eta(\mathbb{R})

by
lemma

3.6
.
Therefore we define

\Sigma(\gamma,\eta,u)=\Sigma_{\gamma\eta}(u)=[u\mapsto\gamma_{ru}(\mathbb{R})]

for
u\in[0,1]
.

Remark 3.10
.

Let
\gamma,\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
. We suppose that
r:=d(\gamma(0),\eta(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\eta)
.
Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
. We define
\gamma^{a},\eta^{a}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
by
\gamma^{a}(t):=\gamma(t+a)
and

\eta^{a}(t):=\eta(t+a)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

\Sigma_{\gamma\eta}=\Sigma_{\gamma^{a}\eta^{a}}
.

It follows that
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is a geodesic space. In fact, we have the following.

Proposition 3.11
.

\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is a uniquely geodesic space.
Therefore
\Sigma
is the canonical bicombing.

Proof..

Let
\gamma,\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
. We suppose that
r:=d(\gamma(0),\eta(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\eta)
.
Let
\Gamma\colon[0,r]\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
be a geodesic
such that
\Gamma(0)=\gamma(\mathbb{R})
and
\Gamma(r)=\eta(\mathbb{R})
.
Now we define a map
\xi\colon[0,r]\to X
as follows.
Set
\xi(0):=\gamma(0)\in\Gamma(0)
. For
u\in[0,r]
, we choose
unique
\xi(u)\in\Gamma(u)
such that

d(\xi(0),\xi(u))=\mathcal{H}(\Gamma(0),\Gamma(u))
.
Then for
s,t\in[0,r]
, by

lemma

3.7
, we have

\displaystyle d(\xi(s),\xi(t))=\mathcal{H}(\Gamma(s),\Gamma(t))=\left\lvert s-t\right\rvert.

Especially,
d(\gamma(0),\xi(r))=r=d(\gamma(0),\eta(0))
and

\xi(r)\in\Gamma(r)=\eta(\mathbb{R})
. So
\xi(r)=\eta(0)
.
Therefore
\xi
is a geodesic between

\gamma(0)
and
\eta(0)
. Since
X
is a uniquely geodesic space,

\xi
is equal to the geodesic
[u\mapsto\beta(0,u)]

where
\beta
is a map given by
lemma

3.5
for
\gamma,\eta
. It follows that

\Gamma
is equal to the geodesic
\Sigma_{\gamma\eta}
.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
\sigma
be the canonical bicombing of
X
, and let
\Sigma
be
that of
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
. For
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
, and

x_{1}\in\gamma_{1}(\mathbb{R}),\,x_{2}\in\gamma_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{x_{1}x_{2}}(t)\in\Sigma_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}(t)\quad(t\in[0,1]).

Proof..

We can assume that
x_{1}=\gamma_{1}(0)
and

r:=d(\gamma_{1}(0),\gamma_{2}(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
. Then

x_{2}=\gamma_{2}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\times[0,r]\to X
be a map given by
lemma

3.5
for parallel
geodesics
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
. Then the map
[u\mapsto\beta(\lambda u,ru);u\in[0,1]]
is a unique linearly reparametrised
geodesics between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, so it coincides with
\sigma_{x_{1}x_{2}}
.
It follows that
\sigma_{x_{1}x_{2}}(t)=\beta(\lambda t,rt)\in\Sigma_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}(t)
.
∎

Theorem 3.13
.

\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is a Busemann space.

Proof..

We denote by
\Sigma
the canonical bicombing of
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
. By

proposition

3.11
,
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is a uniquely geodesic space. So by

lemma

2.3
, it is enough to show that the
canonical bicombing
\Sigma
is conical.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
.
We choose
x_{i}\in\gamma_{i}(\mathbb{R}),\,y_{i}\in\eta_{i}(\mathbb{R})
for
i=1,2
such that

\displaystyle d(x_{1},y_{1})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1}),\quad d(x_{2},y_{2})=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\eta_{2}).

Let
\sigma
be the canonical bicombing of
X
. For
t\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{x_{1}x_{2}}(t)\in\Sigma_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}(t),\quad\sigma_{y_{1}y_{2}}(t)\in\Sigma_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(t).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}(t),\Sigma_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(t))

\displaystyle\leq d(\sigma_{x_{1}x_{2}}(t),\sigma_{y_{1}y_{2}}(t))

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)d(x_{1},y_{1})+td(x_{2},y_{2})

\displaystyle=(1-t)\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1})+t\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{2},\eta_{2}).

Therefore
\Sigma
is conical and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Busemann function and topological splitting

First we introduce the Busemann function.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a metric space
and
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be an geodesic.
We define a function

b_{\eta}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle b_{\eta}(x)=\lim_{t\to\infty}(d(x,\eta(t))-t),

and call it the Busemann function associated to
\eta
.

Remark 4.2
.

Since
d(x,\eta(t))-t
is non-increasing on
t
and bounded below, the limit exists,
and
b_{\eta}
is a 1-Lipschitz function.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a Busemann space and

\eta\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.
For a geodesic
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
, the composite

b_{\eta}\circ\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex. Moreover,
if
\xi
and
\eta
are parallel,
it is strictly decreasing.

Proof..

Since the canonical bicombing is convex, the function

s\mapsto d(x,\xi(t))
is convex,
and so are its translates. Since the Busemann function
b_{\eta}

is the pointwise limit of the function

[s\mapsto d(\xi(s),\eta(t))-t]
, it is convex.

Suppose
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}d(\eta(t),\xi(t))<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}b_{\eta}(\xi(s))=-\infty,\quad\lim_{s\to-\infty}b_{\eta}(\xi(s))=\infty,

it follows that
b_{\eta}\circ\xi
is strictly
decreasing.
∎

Let
X
be a Busemann space and

\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.
We define a subset
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
of
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
and
a subset
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}(t)
of
X
where
t\in\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}:b_{\omega}(\gamma(0))=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}(t):=

\displaystyle\{\gamma(t):\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}\}.

There exists a bijection between
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
and
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore we
identify
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
with
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
, and we regard
(\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b},\mathcal{H})
as a metric
space.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define a map
\mathrm{Ev}_{t}\colon\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}(t)
by

\mathrm{Ev}_{t}(\gamma):=\gamma(t)
. Then
\mathrm{Ev}_{t}
is continuous.

Proof..

It is enough to show that
\mathrm{Ev}_{0}
is continuous. Indeed, for

\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
, by
lemma

3.3
, we have

d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))=d(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))
. Therefore, if

\mathrm{Ev}_{0}
is continuous, so is
\mathrm{Ev}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
. We fix
\epsilon>0
. Set

\delta^{\prime}:=\min\{\left\lvert b_{\omega}(\gamma(\epsilon/2))\right\rvert,\,b_{\omega}(\gamma(-\epsilon/2))\}
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\delta^{\prime}>0
. Set
\delta:=\min\{\delta^{\prime}/2,\epsilon/2\}
.
Here we use Lemma
4.3
again, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\left\lvert b_{\omega}(\gamma(t))\right\rvert>\delta^{\prime}\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\text{ with }\left\lvert t\right\rvert>\epsilon/2.

Now let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
such that

\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})<\delta
. There exists
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(0))<\delta
.
We suppose that
d(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))>\epsilon
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert t\right\rvert=d(\gamma(0),\gamma(t))\geq d(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))-d(\gamma^{\prime}(0),\gamma(t))>\epsilon-\delta\geq\epsilon/2.

Since
b_{\omega}
is 1-Lipschitz, using (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert b_{\omega}(\gamma^{\prime}(0))\right\rvert>\left\lvert b_{\omega}(\gamma(t))\right\rvert-\delta\geq\delta^{\prime}/2>0.

This contradicts that

\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
. Thus we have
d(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))\leq\epsilon
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Ev}_{0}
is continuous.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ev}\colon\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}\to X_{\omega},\quad\mathrm{Ev}(\gamma,t)=\gamma(t)

is homeomorphism.

Proof..

First we show that
\mathrm{Ev}
is continuous. By
lemma

4.4
,
for
(\gamma,t)\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\epsilon>0
,
there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}

with
\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})<\delta
, we have

d(\mathrm{Ev}_{t}(\gamma),\mathrm{Ev}_{t}(\gamma^{\prime}))=d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))<\epsilon/2
.
Then for
(\gamma^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}

with
\mathcal{H}(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})<\delta
and
\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert<\epsilon/2
, we have

\displaystyle d(\mathrm{Ev}(\gamma,t),\mathrm{Ev}(\gamma^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t^{\prime}))\leq d(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))+d(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t^{\prime}))<\epsilon.

Therefore
\mathrm{Ev}
is continuous.

Now we will see that the inverse is also continuous.
We define a map
q\colon X_{\omega}\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
such that
for
x\in X_{\omega}
, we have
x\in q(x)(\mathbb{R})
.
Since for
x\in X_{\omega}
there exists unique
\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}^{b}
with
x\in\gamma(\mathbb{R})
,
this map is well-defined.
For
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
, by
corollary

3.4
, we have
\mathcal{H}(q(x),q(x^{\prime}))\leq d(x,x^{\prime})
.
So the map
q
is continuous. We also define a map
\theta\colon X_{\omega}\to\{1,-1\}
by

\displaystyle\theta(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in q(x)([0,\infty))\\
-1&\text{else}\end{cases}

Then the map
x\mapsto\theta(x)d(x,q(x)(0))
is continuous.
Since the inverse
\mathrm{Ev}^{-1}
is given by

\mathrm{Ev}^{-1}(x)=(q(x),\theta(x)d(x,q(x)(0)))
, it is continuous.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

The map
\mathrm{Ev}_{t}
defined in
lemma

4.4

is a homeomorphism.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic which is parallel to
\omega
.
For any
u,s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle b_{\omega}(\gamma(u+s))=b_{\omega}(\gamma(u))-s.

Proof..

By
lemma

3.3
,

\displaystyle b_{\omega}(\gamma(u+s))

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\{d(\gamma(u+s),\omega(t))-t\right\}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\{d(\gamma(u),\omega(t-s))-(t-s)-s\right\}

\displaystyle=b_{\omega}(\gamma(u))-s.

∎

5.
isometries

In this section, we consider a classification of
isometries induced on the first factor of the decomposition in
theorem

1.2
.

First we review standard terminologies.
Let
X
be a metric space and

f\colon X\to X
be an isometry. The
displacement function

d_{f}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
is defined as
d_{f}(x)=d(x,f(x))
for
x\in X
.
The
minimal displacement
of
f
, denoted by
\left\lvert f\right\rvert
,
is the infimum of
d_{f}
, that is,

\displaystyle\left\lvert f\right\rvert=\inf_{x\in X}d_{f}(x).

The
minimal set
of
f
, denoted by
\mathrm{Min}(f)
, is the subset
defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Min}(f)=\{x\in X:d_{f}(x)=\left\lvert f\right\rvert\}.

We say that an isometry
f\colon X\to X
is

(i)

parabolic
if
\mathrm{Min}(f)=\emptyset
,

(ii)

elliptic
if
\mathrm{Min}(f)\neq\emptyset
and
\left\lvert f\right\rvert=0
,

(iii)

hyperbolic
if
\mathrm{Min}(f)\neq\emptyset
and
\left\lvert f\right\rvert>0
,

(iv)

semi-simple
if
f
is elliptic or hyperbolic.

It is easy to see that
f
is elliptic if and only if
f
has a fixed point.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an isometry.
We say that
f
is
axial
if
f
has no fixed point and
there exists a geodesic
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
whose image is setwise
invariant by
f
. We call such
\gamma
an
axis
of
f
.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
5
, Proposition 11.2.10]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to X
be an axial isometry and

\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be an axis of
f
.
Set
b_{\gamma}(f)=-b_{\gamma}(f(\gamma(0)))
.
Then we have
\left\lvert b_{\gamma}(f)\right\rvert=\left\lvert f\right\rvert
and
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle f\circ\gamma(t)=\gamma(t+b_{\gamma}(f)).

Axial isometries are hyperbolic (
[
5
, Corollary 11.2.9]
).
The converse holds if
X
is a Busemann space.

Proposition 5.2

(
[
5
, Corollary 11.4.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a Busemann space and
f\colon X\to X
be an isometry. Then

f
is hyperbolic if and only if
f
has an axis.

Let
X
be a Busemann space and let
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be a geodesic.
We define a geodesic
\bar{\omega}\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
by

\bar{\omega}(t)=\omega(-t)
.

Definition 5.3
.

We say that an isometry

g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}

preserve the foliation
by
\omega
if

g\circ\omega
or
g\circ\bar{\omega}
is parallel to
\omega
.

Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be an isometry preserving the foliation
by
\omega
.
For
\gamma,\eta\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
, we have

g\circ\gamma(\mathbb{R}),g\circ\eta(\mathbb{R})\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\mathcal{H}(\gamma(\mathbb{R}),\eta(\mathbb{R}))=\mathcal{H}(g\circ\gamma(\mathbb{R}),g\circ\eta(\mathbb{R}))
.
Thus we define an isometry
g_{\omega}\colon\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
by

g_{\omega}(\gamma(\mathbb{R})):=g\circ\gamma(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be an isometry preserving the foliation by
\omega
.
If
g
is elliptic, then so is
g_{\omega}
.

Proof..

If
g
is elliptic, then there exists
x\in X_{\omega}
such that
g(x)=x
. Then for

L\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
with
x\in L
, we have
g_{\omega}(L)=L
.
It follows that
g_{\omega}
is elliptic.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be an isometry
preserving the foliation by
\omega
. If
g_{\omega}
is elliptic,
then
g
is either elliptic or hyperbolic.

Proof..

If
g_{\omega}
is elliptic, then
g_{\omega}
has a fixed point, that is,
there exists
\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
such that
g\circ\gamma(\mathbb{R})=\gamma(\mathbb{R})
.
So
\gamma
is an invariant geodesic of
g
. Therefore
g
is elliptic or hyperbolic.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be a hyperbolic isometry preserving the foliation by
\omega
.
Let
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
be an axis of
g
.
If
\xi
is parallel to either
\omega
or
\bar{\omega}
,
then
g_{\omega}
is elliptic. Otherwise,
g_{\omega}
is hyperbolic.

Proof..

Suppose that
\xi
is parallel to either
\omega
or
\bar{\omega}
,
Then
g\circ\omega(\mathbb{R})=\omega(\mathbb{R})
. So
g_{\omega}
is elliptic.

Now we suppose that
\xi
is parallel to neither
\omega
nor
\bar{\omega}
.
We define a map
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\xi(t)\in\Gamma(t)
.
Since
\xi
is parallel to neither
\omega
or
\bar{\omega}
,
such
\Gamma(t)
in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
uniquely exists, so
\Gamma
is well-defined.
Then
\Gamma
is a geodesic. Indeed, let
\sigma
be the canonical bicombing of
X
, and
let
\Sigma
be that of
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
.
For
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
with
u\leq v
and for
t\in[u,v]
,
by the construction of
\Sigma
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\xi(t)=\sigma_{\xi(u)\xi(v)}((t-u)/(v-u))\in\Sigma_{\Gamma(u)\Gamma(v)}((t-u)/(v-u)).

It follows that
\Gamma(t)=\Sigma_{\Gamma(u)\Gamma(v)}((t-u)/(v-u))
. Therefore

\Gamma
is a geodesic.

Since
g(\xi(\mathbb{R}))=\xi(\mathbb{R})
, we have
g_{\omega}(\Gamma(\mathbb{R}))=\Gamma(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus
\Gamma
is an invariant bi-infinite geodesic
on which
g_{\omega}
acts as a translation.
Therefore
g_{\omega}
is hyperbolic.
∎

lemma

3.5
and the uniqueness of
geodesic in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})

proposition

).

Lemma 5.7

(Flat plane)
.

Let
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
be a bi-infinite geodesic in
\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
.
We choose
x\in\Gamma(0)
as a base point. For
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we choose
\gamma_{u}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}

such that,
\gamma_{0}(0)=x
, and for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma_{u}(\mathbb{R})=\Gamma(u)
and

\displaystyle d(x,\gamma_{u}(0))=d(\gamma_{0}(0),\gamma_{u}(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{u})=u.

We define a map
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to X_{\omega}
by
\beta(t,u):=\gamma_{u}(t)
. Then a map

[u\mapsto\beta(t_{0}+\lambda u,u)]
is a linearly reparametrised geodesic where

t_{0},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
g\colon X_{\omega}\to X_{\omega}
be an isometry
preserving the foliation by
\omega
. If
g_{\omega}
is
hyperbolic, so is
g
.

Proof..

Suppose
g_{\omega}
is hyperbolic.
For simplicity, we assume that
g\circ\omega
is parallel to
\omega
.

Let
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
be an axis of
g_{\omega}
.
We choose a base point
x\in\Gamma(0)
. For
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we choose
\gamma_{u}\in\mathbb{X}_{\omega}
as
in
lemma

5.7
and define the map
\beta
by
\beta(t,u):=\gamma_{u}(t)
.
Let
r>0
be the translation length of
g_{\omega}
.
Then we have
g(\gamma_{0}(\mathbb{R}))=\gamma_{r}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
g(x)=g(\gamma_{0}(0))\in\gamma_{r}(\mathbb{R})

there uniquely exists
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
g(x)=\gamma_{r}(s_{0})
.
By
lemma

3.3
,

\displaystyle d(g(\gamma_{0}(0)),\gamma_{0}(s_{0}))=d(\gamma_{r}(s_{0}),\gamma_{0}(s_{0}))=d(\gamma_{r}(0),\gamma_{0}(0))=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{r},\gamma_{0})=r.

We show inductively that for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle g^{n}(x)=g^{n}\circ\gamma_{0}(0)=\gamma_{rn}(s_{0}n).

We suppose that
g^{n-1}\circ\gamma_{0}(0)=\gamma_{r(n-1)}(s_{0}(n-1))

for positive
n
. Then
g^{n-1}\circ\gamma_{0}(s_{0})=\gamma_{r(n-1)}(s_{0}n)
.
So we have

\displaystyle d(g^{n}\circ\gamma_{0}(0),\gamma_{r(n-1)}(s_{0}n))

\displaystyle=d(g^{n}\circ\gamma_{0}(0),g^{n-1}\circ\gamma_{0}(s_{0}))

\displaystyle=d(g\circ\gamma_{0}(0),\gamma_{0}(s_{0}))

\displaystyle=r

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\gamma_{nr},\gamma_{(n-1)r}).

Then we have
g^{n}\circ\gamma_{0}(0)=\gamma_{rn}(s_{0}n)
.
For negative
n
, we can prove in the same way.

Now by
lemma

5.7
, a map
[u\mapsto\beta(s_{0}u,ru)=\gamma_{ru}(s_{0}u)]

is a linearly reparametrised geodesic on which the orbit of
x
by
g
lies.
It follows that this is an axis of
g
, and so
g
is hyperbolic.
∎

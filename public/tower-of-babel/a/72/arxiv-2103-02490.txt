The values of the Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle at real multiplication points
Henri Darmon, Alice Pozzi and Jan Vonk

Abstract
. The values of the so-called Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle at certain real quadratic arguments are shown to be global p p -units in the narrow Hilbert class field of the associated real quadratic field, as predicted by the conjectures of [ DD06 ] and [ DV21 ] . The strategy for proving this result combines the approach of [ DPV21 ] with one crucial extra ingredient: the study of infinitesimal deformations of irregular Hilbert Eistenstein series of weight one in the anti-parallel direction, building on the techniques of [ BDP ] .

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}_{p}
denote Drinfeld’s
p
-adic upper half plane, and let
\cM^{\times}

denote the multiplicative group of non-zero rigid meromorphic functions on

\mathcal{H}_{p}
, equipped with the translation action of the discrete group

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
by Möbius transformations. A
rigid meromorphic cocycle
on a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
is a class in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cM^{\times})
. If
\tau\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
is a
real multiplication
, or RM, point, i.e., generates a real quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, the
value
of
J
at
\tau
is defined to be

(1)

J[\tau]:=J(\gamma_{\tau})(\tau)\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\cup\{\infty\},

where
\gamma_{\tau}\in\Gamma
is the
automorph
of
\tau
,
a suitably normalised generator of the stabiliser of
\tau
in

\Gamma
.
The relevance of the RM values of rigid meromorphic cocycles to explicit class field theory for real quadratic fields
has been explored in
[
Da01
]
,

[
DD06
]

[
DV21
]
, and

[
DV
]
,
where it is
conjectured, broadly speaking, that they behave in many key respects just like the values of classical modular functions at CM points, and in particular that they belong to, and often generate, narrow ring class fields of real quadratic fields.

Theorem B below gives some theoretical evidence for this general conjecture in the simplest case where
\Gamma=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
and
J
is
analytic
, i.e., takes values in the subgroup
\cA^{\times}\subset\cM^{\times}
of rigid analytic functions. Strictly speaking, there are no interesting rigid analytic cocycles: the group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times})
is
generated, up to torsion, by the class
J_{{\rm triv}}
given by

J_{{\rm triv}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)(z)=cz+d,

whose RM values are units in the associated quadratic order – hence, algebraic, but not in an interesting way for explicit class field theory.

There is a less trivial class in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}})
arising from the classical
Dedekind–Rademacher homomorphism

\varphi_{\rm DR}:\Gamma_{0}(p)\lra\mathbb{Z}

describing the periods of the weight two Eisenstein series

(2)

E_{2}^{(p)}(q)=\dlog\left(\frac{\Delta(q^{p})}{\Delta(q)}\right)=\left(p-1+24\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{(p)}(n)q^{n}\right)\frac{dq}{q},\qquad\mbox{ where }\sigma^{(p)}(n):=\sum_{p\nmid d|n}d,

and given by

(3)

\varphi_{\rm DR}(\gamma):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{z_{0}}^{\gamma z_{0}}\!\!2E_{2}^{(p)}(z)dz.

More precisely, the description of
\Gamma
as an amalgamated product of two conjugate copies of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
intersecting in
\Gamma_{0}(p)
leads to an injection

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}).

Let
\alpha_{{}_{\rm DR}}\in Z^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
be a two-cocycle whose cohomology class is the image of
\varphi_{\rm DR}
under this map. Refining a construction
of
[
DD06
]
, Theorem A below asserts that the cocycle
p^{\alpha_{\rm DR}}
with values in
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
is trivialised in the larger group
\cA^{\times}\supset p^{\mathbb{Z}}
:

Theorem A
.
There is a one-cochain
J_{\rm DR}\in C^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times})
satisfying

\gamma_{1}J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{2})\div J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})\times J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{1})=p^{\alpha_{{}_{\rm DR}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})},\quad\mbox{ for all }\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma.

The essential triviality of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times})
shows that
J_{\rm DR}
is uniquely determined up to coboundaries and powers of the cocycle
J_{\rm triv}
above. The proof of Theorem A is given in §
1
, and constructs an explicit cochain
J_{\rm DR}
which is well defined up to coboundaries. The natural image of
J_{\rm DR}
in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}})
is the
Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle
of the title. The rigid analytic cocycles of higher level studied in
[
DD06
]
are all multiplicative combinations of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
-translates of this basic cocycle. The proof of Theorem A complements the approach of
[
DD06
]
, producing a more canonical object in level
1
which can be envisaged as an avatar of the Eisenstein series
E_{2}
in the setting of rigid meromorphic cocycles. The RM values of
J_{\rm DR}
are well defined modulo
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and it therefore makes sense to enquire about their algebraicity, and their factorisation away from
p
.

An RM point
\tau\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
is said to be of
discriminant

D
if it satisfies an equation of the form
Q(\tau,1)=0
, where
Q(x,y)=Ax^{2}+Bxy+Cy^{2}
is a primitive integral binary quadratic form of discriminant
D
. The set
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{D}
of

of a fixed discriminant

is non-empty precisely when
p
is inert or ramified in the quadratic field
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
, and is preserved by the action of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The orbit set
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathcal{H}_{p}^{D}
is in natural bijection with the class group
\Cl(D)
, by sending the orbit of
\tau
to the narrow equivalence class
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}
of the fractional ideal generated by
\tau
and
1

\tau-\tau^{\prime}
is positive. The reciprocity map

\mathrm{rec}:\Cl(D)\lra\mathrm{Gal}(H/F)

of global class field theory identifies

with the Galois group of the narrow ring class field of
H
over
F
attached to
D
. If
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{D}
is non-empty and

is inert in

and splits completely in

. The choice of an embedding

hence determines a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of

above

, which is fixed once and for all. Fix also a complex embedding
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and write
x\mapsto\bar{x}
for the action of complex conjugation on
H
(which is independent of the choice of embedding). Let

\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}_{-}

be the group of
p
-units of
H

S
-unit theorem, it is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
[H:F]/2
if

does not possess a unit of negative norm, and is finite otherwise. In particular, there is a unique element

satisfying

(4)

{\rm ord}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\sigma}}(u_{\tau})=-L(F,\mathcal{C}_{\tau}^{\sigma},0),\quad\mbox{ for all }\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(H/F),

where

(cf.

(and the prime

). The Brumer–Stark conjecture implies that

belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}
rather than to the tensor product of this group with
\mathbb{Q}
. The proof by Samit Dasgupta and Mahesh Kakde of (the prime to

[
DKa
]
in this setting shows that

belongs to
(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]
.

The principal conjecture of
[
DD06
]
, and its refinement covering the Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle itself, asserts that

is equal,
up to a small torsion ambiguity and powers of
p
,
to an integer power of the Gross–Stark unit
u_{\tau}
.
The weaker equality

(5)

{\rm Norm}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(J_{\rm DR}[\tau])={\rm Norm}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(u_{\tau}^{12})\pmod{(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})_{\rm tors},p^{\mathbb{Z}}}

involving the norms to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
of these invariants
was shown in
[

]
to follow from Gross’s
p
-adic analogue of the Stark conjecture on

-adic abelian

-series of totally real fields at

– at least, after replacing

by the closely allied quantities denoted

in

[
DD06
]
, which depend on the choice of a suitable modular unit

with auxiliary level structure.
The Gross–Stark
conjecture was then proved in
[
DDP11
]
. An important recent work of Samit Dasgupta and Mahesh Kakde

DKb

has significantly refined the approach of

to prove Gross’s

Specialising this result to the case of a real quadratic field
leads to the refinement

(6)

J_{\rm DR}[\tau]=u_{\tau}^{12}\pmod{(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}^{\times})_{\rm tors},\ \ p^{\mathbb{Z}}}

of (

The main contribution of this paper is an independent and more direct proof of (

Theorem B
.
Let
D>0
be a fundamental
discriminant that is prime to
p
.
If
\tau
is an RM point in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of discriminant
D
,
then
J_{\rm DR}[\tau]
is equal to the Gross–Stark unit

, up to torsion in
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}
and powers of

(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times})\otimes\mathbb{Z}[1/2]

To situate the approach of this paper in the context of previous works, note that Dasgupta and Kakde tackle Theorem B by studying Mazur–Tate style “tame refinements" of the techniques of
[
DDP11
]
, leading to a proof of Gross’s tame refinement of his

-adic Stark conjecture (known as the “tower of fields conjecture"
[

]
). They then show that this tame refinement implies Theorem B. Like
[
DKb
]
, the present work rests on the
careful study of deformations of Galois representations that was also exploited in
[
DDP11
]
, but otherwise differs in its approach to Theorem B by avoiding the recourse to tame deformations. Its key idea is to package the RM values of
J_{\rm DR}
as the coefficients of certain modular generating series. The resulting identities (cf. Theorem C below) are of interest in their own right and enrich the tapestry of analogies between RM values of rigid meromorphic cocycles and
CM values of modular functions.

The Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle, taken modulo
\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
rather than
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
, is a prototypical instance of a
rigid analytic theta-cocycle
: a function
J:\Gamma\lra\cA^{\times}
which satisfies the one-cocycle relation, but only up to multiplicative scalars. The proof of Theorem B rests on the study of another theta-cocycle, the so-called

(7)

J_{w}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}),

whose key properties are recalled in §
2
. The notion of RM value can be extended to theta cocycles by noting that, if the RM point
\tau
has discriminant prime to
p
, then its automorph
\gamma_{\tau}
belongs to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The groups
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
and
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
are finite of order dividing

, which implies that the restriction of
J^{12}
to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
admits an essentially unique lift
\tilde{J}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\cA^{\times})
, and the value
J[\tau]
can then be defined as in (
1

J
replaced by
\tilde{J}^{1/12}
on the right hand side. Although there is some torsion ambiguity in the resulting RM values, the
p
-adic logarithms of these RM values are well-defined.

The explicit nature of
J_{w}
can be parlayed into a proof of the following result:

Theorem C
.
Let
\tau
be as in Theorem B. There is a classical modular form
G_{\tau}
of weight two on

with
p
-adic Fourier coefficients, whose

-expansion is given by

G_{\tau}(q)=\log(u_{\tau})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log((T_{n}J_{w})[\tau])q^{n},

where

is the

-adic logarithm. The modular form
G_{\tau}
is non-trivial if and only if
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
does not admit a unit of norm

.

The modular generating series of Theorem C is constructed from the diagonal restriction of a nearly ordinary deformation of a weight one Hilbert Eisenstein series for
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
in the anti-parallel direction. The logarithm of the global
p
-unit

enters into the proof as the eigenvalue of the Frobenius at

p
-adic Galois representation, via a calculation which exploits the reciprocity law of global class field theory, thereby leveraging class field theory for

-adic deformations of irregular Hilbert Eisenstein series of weight one, which is explained in §

and forms the technical core of this article.
This approach is inspired by the study of
the local geometry of the modular eigenvariety in the neighbourhood of irregular Eisenstein points of weight one carried out in

[

]
, and its extension

[

Derivatives of

-adic families of (classical, or Hilbert) modular forms can be viewed as

of

In §

1.
The Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle

This section constructs a one-cochain satisfying Theorem A, which is well-defined up to coboundaries and whose image in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}})
is the Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle

of the introduction.

1.1.
Siegel units

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\times}
denote the multiplicative group of nowhere vanishing holomorphic functions on the Poincaré
upper half-plane, endowed with the right action of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
given by

h|\gamma(z)=h(\gamma z),

where
\gamma z
denotes the usual action of
\gamma
by Möbius transformations.

The construction of

rests on the Siegel units
{{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\times}

indexed by pairs
(\alpha,\beta)\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}-\{(0,0)\}
of order
N>1
,
depending on an auxiliary integer

which is relatively prime to
6N

They satisfy the transformation properties

(8)

{{}_{c}}g_{v\gamma}={{}_{c}}g_{v}|\gamma\qquad\mbox{ for all }v=(\alpha,\beta)\in(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2},\quad\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

(Cf.
[
Ka04
, Lemma 1.7(1)]

In particular,
{{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta}
is a unit on the open modular curve attached to the congruence subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
that fixes
(\alpha,\beta)
, and hence belongs to

\mathcal{O}^{\times}(Y_{0}(N))
.
The Siegel units also satisfy the distribution relations:

(9)

\prod_{m\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha}{{}_{c}}g_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}(z)={{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta}(z/m),\qquad\prod_{m\beta^{\prime}=\beta}{{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta^{\prime}}(z)={{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta}(mz),

which together imply that

(10)

\prod_{m(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=(\alpha,\beta)}{{}_{c}}g_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(z)={{}_{c}}g_{\alpha,\beta}(z).

(Cf.
[
Ka04
, Lemma 1.7(2)]
or
[
LLZ14
, Prop. 2.2.1 and 2.2.2]
.)

The unit
{}_{c}g_{\alpha,\beta}
is equal to
g_{\alpha,\beta}^{c^{2}}\cdot g_{c\alpha,c\beta}^{-1}
,
where the

-expansion of

is given by

(11)

g_{\alpha,\beta}(q)=-q^{w}\prod_{n\geq 0}(1-q^{n+\alpha}e^{2\pi i\beta})\prod_{n>0}(1-q^{n-\alpha}e^{-2\pi i\beta}),

where

, with
0\leq\alpha<1
.
(Cf.
[
Ka04
, §1.9]
.)

Fix a rational prime
p
, and
assume that
(\alpha,\beta)
is of
p
-power order in
(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
To lighten notations, it will be assumed below that

,
and the choice

1.2.
The Siegel distribution

Let

\bX_{0}:=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2})^{\prime}

\mu(p^{j}U)=\mu(U)\mbox{ for all $j\in\mathbb{Z}$ and all compact open }U\subset\bX.

Lemma 1.2

The distribution
\mu_{\rm Siegel}
satisfies

(17)

\begin{array}[]{llll}\mu_{\rm Siegel}(\bX_{0})&=&1&\pmod{\pm p^{\mathbb{Z}}},\\

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the fact that

The second assertion follows from the calculation

1.3.
The Dedekind–Rademacher distributions

The following general Lemmas concerning
p
-invariant distributions will be useful later.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be any element of
\bD(\bX,A)
.
If
\Lambda
is any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{2}
, and
\Lambda^{\prime}
is its set of
primitive vectors, then

\mu(\Lambda^{\prime})=\mu(\bX_{0})
.

Proof.

By compactness, there is an integer
N\geq 0
for which
p^{-N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}\subset\Lambda\subset p^{N}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
, and hence each

v\in\Lambda^{\prime}
belongs to a translate
p^{j}\bX_{0}
for a unique

. Hence one may write

\Lambda^{\prime}=p^{m_{1}}U_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup p^{m_{t}}U_{t},

for a suitable decomposition

\bX_{0}=U_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup U_{t}

of
\bX_{0}
as a disjoint union of compact open subsets.
The additivity properties of
\mu
combined with its
p
-invariance implies that

\mu(\Lambda^{\prime})=\mu(\bX_{0})
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 1.4
.

The rule which to
A
associates
\bD(\bX,A)
is
an exact (covariant) functor from the category
of
\Gamma
-modules to itself.

Proof.

The issue is right exactness. If
\varphi:A\lra B
is a surjective module homomorphism and

\mu\in\bD(\bX,B)
is a
B
-valued,
p
-invariant distribution on
\bX
, one can construct
a distribution
\tilde{\mu}\in\bD(\bX,A)
that maps to it by
choosing, for each successsive

and for each primitive vector

, the value

\tilde{\mu}(v+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2})\in A
satisfying
\varphi(\tilde{\mu}(v+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}))=\mu(v+p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2})
,
taking care at each stage that the additivity relations required of distributions be satisfied.
One obtains in this way an element of
\bD(\bX_{0},B)
, giving rise to the desired lift
in
\bD(\bX,B)
via
(
15
).
∎

Thanks to Lemma

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The one-cocycle

1.4.
The multiplicative Poisson transform

Because a distribution
\mu\in\bD(\bX,\mathbb{Z})
is
\mathbb{Z}
-valued, and hence
p
-adically bounded, it also gives rise to a

measure
: one can extend

\mu
to arbitrary continuous, compactly supported functions on
\bX
.
There is even a
multiplicative refinement
of the integral against
\mu
, defined by

\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX}f(x,y)d\mu(x,y):=\lim_{\{U_{\alpha}\}}\prod_{\alpha}f(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})^{\mu(U_{\alpha})},

where the limit is taken over finer and finer open covers
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of the support of
f
, and

(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
is a sample point in
U_{\alpha}
. Here

f:\bX\lra\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
is a continuous, compactly supported function on
\bX
(which means that it takes the value
1

outside a compact subset of
\bX
).

Let
\bD_{0}(\bX_{0},\mathbb{Z})
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-module of distributions on
\bX_{0}
satisfying

\mu(\bX_{0})=0.

The multiplicative Poisson transform of
\mu\in\bD_{0}(\bX_{0},\mathbb{Z})
is the rigid analytic function
J(\mu)
on
\mathcal{H}_{p}

defined by setting

J(\mu)(\tau)=\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX_{0}}(x\tau+y)d\mu(x,y).

This assignment gives rise to an
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivariant map

J:\bD_{0}(\bX_{0},\mathbb{Z})\lra\cA^{\times},

i.e.,

J({\mu|\gamma})(\tau)=J(\mu)|\gamma(\tau)=J(\mu)(\gamma\tau),\qquad\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}).

Identifying
\bD_{0}(\bX_{0},\mathbb{Z})
with the module
\bD_{0}(\bX,\mathbb{Z})

of distributions on
\bX
satisfying

\mu(\bX_{0})=0,\qquad\mu(pU)=\mu(U),

the same rule
J
(where one continues to integrate over the compact subset
\bX_{0}\subset\bX
)
determines a
\Gamma
-equivariant map

(20)

J:\bD_{0}(\bX,\mathbb{Z})\lra\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}}.

The reason for this somewhat weaker invariance property is that while
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
preserves the region

\bX_{0}
of integration defining
J(\mu)
, the full
p
-arithmetic group
\Gamma
does not.
Nonetheless, if
\gamma\in\Gamma
, one still can write (following the reasoning in the proof of

1.3
)

\bX_{0}\gamma=p^{m_{1}}U_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup p^{m_{t}}U_{t},\qquad\mbox{ with }\quad\bX_{0}=U_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup U_{t},

and the integrand

(x-\tau y)
arising in the definition of
J
obeys a simple transformation property under multiplication by
p
.
It follows that
J(\mu|\gamma)=J(\mu)|\gamma\pmod{p^{\mathbb{Z}}}
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Let

J_{\rm DR}:=J(\mu_{\rm DR})\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}})

be the image of
the measure-valued cocycle
\mu_{\rm DR}
under the multiplicative
Poisson transform of (
20
).
It is represented by the one-cochain
J_{\rm DR}:\Gamma\lra\cA^{\times}
(denoted by the same symbol, by an abuse of notation)
defined by

J_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(\tau)=J(\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma))(\tau),

which satisfies the cocycle relation modulo
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
,

J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{1})\times J_{\rm DR}(\gamma_{2})|\gamma_{1}^{-1}\pmod{p^{\mathbb{Z}}}.

Its restriction to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
also satisfies the
full cocycle
relation
, with no
p^{\mathbb{Z}}
-ambiguity, because of the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivariance of
J
.

In order to prove Theorem A of the introduction, it now suffices to
calculate the image of
J_{\rm DR}
under the sequence of maps

\eta:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}})\lra\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma,p^{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),p^{\mathbb{Z}}).

Theorem 1.6
.

The image of
J_{\rm DR}
under
\eta
is

\eta(J_{\rm DR})=p^{\varphi_{\rm DR}}.

Proof.

The action of
\Gamma
on the Bruhat-Tits tree of
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
leads to an expression for
\Gamma
as an amalgamated product of the groups

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\qquad\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)^{-1}\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

whose intersection is
\Gamma_{0}(p)
.
The fact that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),p^{\mathbb{Z}})=0

and that
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),p^{\mathbb{Z}})
is of order
12
ensures the existence
of unique lifts to
\cA^{\times}
of the restrictions of
J_{\rm DR}^{12}
to
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
:

\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\cA^{\times}),\qquad\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime},\cA^{\times}).

One then has, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(p)
,

(21)

\eta(J_{\rm DR}^{12})(\gamma)=\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}(\gamma)\div\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}(\gamma).

Concretely,
\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}

and
\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}
may be expressed
as multiplicative Poisson transforms of
\mu_{\rm DR}
,
by setting

\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(\tau):=\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX_{0}}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y),\qquad\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}(\gamma)(\tau):=\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX_{0}^{\prime}}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y),

where
\bX_{0}^{\prime}:=(p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\prime}
is the translate of
\bX_{0}
under the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
, a region whose stabiliser in
\Gamma
is the group
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}
.
Observe that

(22)

\bX_{0}\cap\bX_{0}^{\prime}=p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times},\qquad\bX_{0}-\bX_{0}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\qquad\bX_{0}^{\prime}-\bX_{0}=p(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Hence, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(p)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}(\gamma)\div\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX_{0}}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y)\div\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\bX_{0}^{\prime}}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y)\div\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{p(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})}(x\tau+y)^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}}p^{-12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y),

where the penultimate equality follows from (
22
) and the
last from the invariance of
\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)
under
multiplication by
p
.
Because
(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is the complement of
(p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})
in
\bX_{0}
,
and
\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(\bX_{0})=0
, this implies that

\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}(\gamma)\div\mathcal{J}_{\rm DR}^{\prime}(\gamma)=\times\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\hskip 0.97pt\int_{p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}p^{12}d\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(x,y)=p^{12\mu_{\rm DR}(\gamma)(p\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})}=p^{12\varphi_{\rm DR}(\gamma)},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
1.5
. Combining this with
(
21
)
shows that
\eta(J_{\rm DR})
and
p^{\varphi_{\rm DR}}
agree, since the group they belong to is torsion-free.
This completes the proof of Theorem A.
∎

2.
The winding cocycle

The goal of this section is to recall the definition and key properties of the winding cocycle introduced in
[
DPV21
, § 2.3]
and to reduce Theorem B of the introduction to Theorem C.

2.1.
The residue map

The group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})

of rigid analytic theta cocycles
is finitely generated and closely related to the space of modular forms of weight two on the Hecke congruence group
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. More precisely, it is a module over the Hecke algebra
{\mathbb{T}}_{0}(p)
of Hecke operators acting faithfully on the weight two modular forms on
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. To see this, let

U:=\{z\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\mathbb{C}_{p})\mbox{ with }1<|z|<p\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{p}

be the standard annulus whose stabiliser in
\Gamma
is
\Gamma_{0}(p)
. The logarithmic annular residue map

(23)

\partial_{U}:\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}\lra\mathbb{Z}_{p},\qquad\partial_{U}(f):={\rm Res}_{U}(\dlog f)

is equivariant for the action of
\Gamma_{0}(p)
, and hence composing it with the restriction to
\Gamma_{0}(p)
yields a map on cohomology

(24)

\partial_{U}:\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}\!/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})\lra\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z}_{p}),

which is denoted by the same symbol by abuse of notation. This map is compatible with the action of the Hecke operators, and with the involution
W_{\infty}
determined by the matrix

\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
, which lies in the normaliser of both
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{0}(p)
.
Let
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}\!/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{\pm}
denote the plus and minus eigenspaces for this involution in the space of rigid analytic theta cocycles, and denote by
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\pm}
the corresponding eigenspaces in the cohomology of
\Gamma_{0}(p)
.

While the map in (
23
) has an infinite rank kernel, it is notable that the induced map on rigid analytic theta cocycles is essentially an isomorphism:

Lemma 2.1
.

Up to torsion kernels and cokernels,
the map
\partial_{U}
of
(
24
)
is surjective, and its kernel is generated
by the “trivial" theta-cocycle

J_{\rm triv}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}),\qquad J_{\rm triv}\left(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\right)(z)=cz+d.

In particular, the induced map

(25)

\partial_{U}^{-}\ :\ \mathbb{Q}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{-}\ \lra\ \mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Q})^{-}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The first assertion is a reformulation of
[
DPV21
, Theorem 3.1]
. The last follows from the fact that
J_{\rm triv}
is fixed by
W_{\infty}
, as can be checked directly from the definition of
J_{\rm triv}
.
∎

2.2.
The winding cocycle

In
[
DPV21
, §2.3]
, the so-called

winding cocycle

J_{w}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{-}

is introduced. Unlike the Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle, it is not an eigenclass for the Hecke operators, although it belongs to the
-1
eigenspace for the involution
W_{\infty}
. The greater complexity of
J_{w}
on the spectral side is offset by a gain in simplicity on the geometric side, evidenced by the fact that the rigid analytic functions
J_{w}(\gamma)
admit explicit infinite product expansions.

Let
Y_{0}(p)=\Gamma_{0}(p)\backslash\mathcal{H}
be the open modular curve, and let
X_{0}(p)
be its standard compactification, obtained by adding the two cusps
0
and
\infty
. The intersection pairing on homology (Poincaré duality)
defines isomorphisms

(26)

\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{0}(p);\{0,\infty\},\mathbb{Q})^{\pm}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(Y_{0}(p),\mathbb{Q})^{\mp}=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Q})^{\mp}.

Mazur’s
winding element

\varphi_{w}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z})^{-}

is defined to be the class of the path from
0
to
\infty
in the homology of the modular curve
X_{0}(p)
relative to the cusps, viewed as an element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\mathbb{Z})
via (
26
). By
[
DPV21
, Prop. 3.3]
and its proof, the winding cocycle is characterised by the identity

(27)

\partial_{U}^{-}(J_{w})=2\varphi_{w}.

2.3.
Theorem C implies Theorem B

Theorem B of the introduction is reduced to Theorem C by writing
the modular form
G_{\tau}
of this theorem
as a linear combination of eigenforms.

To this end, observe that

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{0}(p),\bar{\mathbb{Q}})^{-}
is generated as a
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space by
the Dedekind–Rademacher morphism

\varphi_{\rm DR}
of
(
3
)
encoding the periods of the weight two Eisenstein series
E_{2}^{(p)}
defined in the introduction,
and the homomorphisms
\varphi_{f}^{-}
attached to the minus modular symbol for
f
, where
f
runs through a basis of cuspidal Hecke eigenforms in
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(p))
.
A direct calculation of integration pairings in
[
DPV21
, Lemma 3.4]
then yields the spectral decomposition of the winding element,

(28)

\varphi_{w}\ =\ \frac{1}{p-1}\cdot\varphi_{\rm DR}\ +\ \sum_{f}\lambda_{f}\cdot\varphi_{f}^{-},

where the coefficient
\lambda_{f}\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
is a suitable non-zero
multiple of
L(f,1)
whose exact nature is not germane to the proof of Theorem B. (But see
[
DPV21
, § 3]
for more details.)

Now consider the rigid analytic theta-cocycle

J_{f}^{-}\ \in\ \bar{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{-}

characterised by
\partial_{U}^{-}(J_{f}^{-})=\varphi_{f}^{-}
. By Lemma
2.1
and (
27
),

(29)

J_{w}\ =\ \frac{2}{p-1}\cdot J_{\rm DR}\ \ +\ \ \sum_{f}2\lambda_{f}\cdot J_{f}^{-}\qquad\mbox{in }\ \bar{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{-},

where additive notation has been adopted to describe the operations in this group in spite of its multiplicative nature.
For each
n\geq 1
, applying the Hecke operator
T_{n}
to this identity
then gives

(30)

\displaystyle T_{n}J_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}\cdot T_{n}J_{\rm DR}\ \ +\ \ \sum_{f}2\lambda_{f}\cdot T_{n}J_{f}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}\cdot J_{\rm DR}\cdot\sigma_{1}^{(p)}(n)\ \ +\ \ \sum_{f}2\lambda_{f}\cdot J_{f}^{-}\cdot a_{n}(f)

in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma,\cA^{\times}/\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})^{-}
. After evaluating at the RM point
\tau
and taking
p
-adic logarithms, it follows that

(31)

\log_{p}(T_{n}J_{w}[\tau])\ \ \ =\ \ \ \frac{2\log_{p}(J_{\rm DR}[\tau])}{p-1}\cdot\sigma_{1}^{(p)}(n)\ \ +\ \ \sum_{f}2\lambda_{f}\log_{p}(J_{f}^{-}[\tau])\cdot a_{n}(f).

Substituting this identity into
Theorem C of the introduction yields the spectral expansion

(32)

G_{\tau}(q)\ \ =\ \ \frac{\log J_{\rm DR}[\tau]}{12(p-1)}\cdot E_{2}^{(p)}(q)\ +\ \sum_{f}\beta_{f}\cdot f(q),

where
E_{2}^{(p)}
is the Eisenstein series of
(
2
), and

(33)

\beta_{f}=2\lambda_{f}\log_{p}(J_{f}^{-}[\tau]).

Comparing the zero-th Fourier coefficient
of
G_{\tau}
in (
32
)
with the one in Theorem C
shows that

\frac{\log J_{\rm DR}[\tau]}{12}=\log(u_{\tau}),

thereby reducing Theorem B of the introduction to Theorem C.

The remainder of the paper is devoted to the construction of the modular generating series required for the proof of Theorem C.

Remark
.
The coefficients
\beta_{f}
in (
33
) are immaterial to the proof of Theorem B but
are of independent interest, insofar as they involve the RM values of the elliptic rigid analytic theta-cocycles
J_{f}^{-}
: these values are the formal group logarithms of certain
Stark–Heegner points
in the modular Jacobian
J_{0}(p)
. Although poorly understood theoretically, these Stark–Heegner points are conjectured to be defined over the narrow ring class field
H_{\tau}
.
The approach to the algebraicity of
J_{\rm DR}[\tau]
based on deformations of Galois representation does not seem to shed any immediate light on the algebraicity of these more mysterious invariants.

3.
Deformations of Hilbert Eisenstein series

This section studies the derivatives of certain
p
-adic analytic families
of Hilbert modular forms for
F
parametrised by the weight and specialising to a certain Hilbert Eisenstein series of parallel weight one.

This Eisenstein series has several notable features. Firstly, it is cuspidal when viewed as a
p
-adic modular form, and admits cuspidal
p
-adic deformations. Secondly, it vanishes upon diagonal restriction. This implies that the derivatives of both cuspidal and Eisenstein families specialising to
f
, in spite of not displaying any simple modularity properties themselves, yield
p
-adic modular forms after taking diagonal restriction. A suitable linear combination of these derivatives is considered in §
4
, and the Fourier coefficients of its ordinary projection are related to the RM values of the winding cocycle.

While
p
-adic Eisenstein families only occur in
parallel weight, cuspidal families vary over a larger weight space. The main result of this section is Theorem
3.13
, which describes the Fourier coefficients of the derivatives of a cuspidal family in the “anti-parallel” direction of the weight space. Much like in the archimedean settings, the Fourier expansions of
p
-adic Eisenstein families are entirely explicit; however, no general expression is available for cuspidal families.
Our approach to studying cuspidal deformations of a Hilbert Eisenstein series rests on the analysis of the associated Galois deformation problems. Roughly speaking, first order deformations of the Artin representation attached to a Hilbert Eisenstein series of parallel weight one are described in terms of the Galois cohomology of the adjoint representation, which cuts out a finite abelian extension
H
of
F
. A class in the Galois cohomology of the adjoint cuts out an abelian
p
-adic Lie extension of
H
, and the Frobenius traces on the associated Galois deformation involve
p
-adic logarithms of global
p
-units in
H
, via the reciprocity law of global class field theory for
H
. This translates into the appearance of the logarithms of Gross–Stark units in the Fourier coefficients of first order deformations of Hilbert Eisenstein series, and accounts for the presence of the same quantities
in the constant term of the generating series
G_{\tau}
of Theorem C.

The Galois deformation arguments are clarified and not substantially lengthened by working in the setting where
F
is an arbitrary totally real field of degree
d
in which
p
is inert. This will be assumed until §
3.5
, when the main results will be specialised to the case where
F
is real quadratic.

3.2.
Galois cohomology and
\mathscr{L}
-invariants

This section develops some results on Galois cohomology, which will be used
later, notably
in §
3.4
,
to describe the tangent space of certain Galois deformation functors. These preliminary results are well known to experts.

Although it will not be used, it is worth noting that most of the arguments below are quite general and
also work for general number fields
F
.
Let
H
be a Galois extension of
F
with Galois group
G
.
The
E[G]
-module
\mathrm{Hom}(G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}},E)
can be described explicitly via class field theory. This will be used to show that certain global Galois cohomology classes for the totally odd character
\psi

are determined by their images in local cohomology.

A finite place
v
of
H
determines a prime ideal of
H
whose decomposition group is isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of
H_{v}
, denoted by
G_{v}
. For each
v
, there is an isomorphism between the completion of
H_{v}^{\times}
and
G_{v}^{\mathrm{ab}}
induced by the local Artin reciprocity map

\mathrm{rec}_{v}\colon H_{v}^{\times}\ \lra\ G_{v}^{\mathrm{ab}}

for which the geometric normalisation is adopted. Note that the image of
G_{v}
in
G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}}
is canonical. Thus, there is a restriction map
\mathrm{res}_{v}
defined by

(43)

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\mathrm{res}_{v}\colon&\mathrm{Hom}(G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}},E)&\lra&\mathrm{Hom}(H_{v}^{\times},E)\\
&f&\longmapsto&\mathrm{rec}_{v}\circ f|_{G_{v}^{\mathrm{ab}}}.\end{array}

The following lemma is well-known to experts, but its proof is sketched below
for completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

There is an exact sequence of
E[G]
-modules

(44)

0\to\mathrm{Hom}(G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}},E)\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{res}_{v})_{v\mid p}}\prod_{v\mid p}\mathrm{Hom}(H_{v}^{\times},E)\,\lra\,\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times},E).

In addition, the rightmost map is surjective if and only if Leopoldt’s Conjecture holds for
H
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{H}^{\times}/H^{\times}
be the idèle class group of
H
. The global Artin reciprocity map
\mathrm{rec}_{H}\colon\mathbb{A}_{H}^{\times}/H^{\times}\to G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}}
is compatible with its local versions via the restriction maps, and gives a sequence

(45)

0\to\mathrm{Hom}(G_{H}^{\mathrm{ab}},E)\to\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{A}_{H}^{\times},E)\to\mathrm{Hom}(H^{\times},E)

of continuous group homomorphisms and the topology on
H^{\times}
is discrete. This sequence must be exact for topological reasons, and the two terms on the right are understood via their restrictions to the places above
p
, by the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{A}_{H}^{\times},E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}(H^{\times},E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\prod_{v\mid p}\mathrm{Hom}(H_{v}^{\times},E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times},E)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta_{p}^{\vee}}

where
\Delta^{\vee}
,
\Delta_{p}^{\vee}
are induced by the diagonal embeddings. Note that any element in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{A}_{H}^{\times},E)
must be trivial on the units
\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
of

for any

, and standard continuity arguments then show that the resulting map
\ker\Delta^{\vee}\to\ker\Delta_{p}^{\vee}
is an isomorphism. Finally, by Dirichlet’s Unit Theorem,

\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathcal{O}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}=\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}+|\{v\mid p\}|,

so the rightmost map of (
44
) is surjective if and only if the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-rank of the closure of the image of
\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}
in
\prod_{v\mid p}\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
is equal to the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times}
, that is, if Leopoldt’s Conjecture holds for
H
.
∎

a_{\nu}=\displaystyle\sum_{I\mid(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}\psi(I)\left(1+\varepsilon\left(-\log_{p}(\nu)+\frac{\mathscr{L}_{1}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\log_{p}\Nm(I)+\frac{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\log_{p}\Nm\left(\frac{(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}{I}\right)\!\!\right)\!\!\right),

for all
\nu
that are relatively prime to
p
.
Furthermore
a_{p^{m}\nu}=a_{\nu}
for all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

The family
\mathcal{F}
is
p
-adically cuspidal, so the constant term vanishes. To compute the higher Fourier coefficients, we compute that, for any
\nu\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{-1}
such that
p\nmid\nu
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{f}(\mathbf{T}_{(\nu)\mathfrak{d}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{l}^{n}\mid\!\mid(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}\tilde{\pi}_{f}\left(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{l}^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{I\mid(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}\psi(I)\left(1+\varepsilon\left(\frac{\mathscr{L}_{1}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\log_{p}\Nm(I)+\frac{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\log_{p}\Nm\left(\frac{(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}{I}\right)\!\!\right)\!\!\right)

to first order. To determine the Fourier coefficients from this value, the
p
-adic interpolation properties of the coefficients
a_{\nu}
, and the density of classical forms, are used
to reduce to the relations between Fourier coefficients and the Hecke algebra proved for classical forms in
[
Hi91
]
.

Consider the rigid analytic fiber of the formal scheme attached to
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{n.ord}}
. For a sufficiently small affinoid neighbourhood
V=\mathrm{Spm}(A_{V})
of the point corresponding to the morphism
\pi_{f}
, there is a rigid analytic family
\mathcal{F}_{V}=\sum_{\nu}a_{V,\nu}q^{\nu}
with normalised Fourier coefficients in
a_{V,\nu}\in A_{V}
, specialising to
\mathcal{F}
in the anti-parallel direction.
By Hida’s Control Theorem, there is a Zariski-dense set of points in
V
corresponding to systems of Hecke eigenvalues
\pi_{g}\colon A_{V}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

of classical modular forms
g
of weight
({\mathbf{k}}_{g},{\mathbf{w}}_{g})
and with Fourier coefficients
a_{g,\nu}
given by the image of
a_{V,\nu}
under
\pi_{g}
.
Combining the relations for classical forms proved in
[
Hi91
, Eqn. (2.3) et seq./Eqn. (1.5)]
, one obtains

\begin{array}[]{lll}\pi_{g}(\mathbf{T}_{(\nu)\mathfrak{d}})&=&a_{g,\nu}\cdot\nu^{{\mathbf{v}}_{g}}\\
\pi_{g}(\mathbf{T}_{p^{m}})&=&a_{g,p^{m}}\end{array}

where as before
{\mathbf{v}}_{g}={\mathbf{w}}_{g}-{\mathbf{k}}_{g}+{\mathbf{t}}
. The quantity
\nu^{{\mathbf{v}}_{g}}
may be identified with the weight
({\mathbf{k}}_{g},{\mathbf{w}}_{g})
-specialisation of the universal character
\kappa^{\mathrm{univ}}
evaluated at
(\nu,1)
. The image of
\kappa^{\mathrm{univ}}(\nu,1)
under the morphism (
65
) defining the anti-parallel direction is given by

1+\varepsilon\log_{p}(\nu)

so that the density of classical points in
V
implies that

a_{\nu}=(1-\varepsilon\log_{p}(\nu))\tilde{\pi}_{f}(\mathbf{T}_{(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}).

The result follows.
∎

Retain the setup of §

4.1.
The RM values of the winding cocycle

In contrast the approach of
[
DD06
]
, the calculations below build on the viewpoint of rigid (theta) cocycles introduced in
[

, §3]
, by making essential use of the winding cocycle

of the prequel
[

]
, some of whose properties were already recalled in §

. This section describes some further results from
[

]
concerning its RM values. The first key result is an explicit formula for
T_{n}J_{w}[\tau]
, which was established in
[
DPV21
, Theorem 2.9]

In order to state it, choose, for any integer

and any RM point
\tau
in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, a finite set

of representatives for the double coset space
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{n}/\Gamma_{\tau}

M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{n}:=\{\alpha\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\mbox{ with }\det(\alpha)=n\},\qquad\quad\Gamma_{\tau}=\Stab_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\tau).

In other words

(73)

M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})_{n}=\bigsqcup_{\delta\in M_{n}(\tau)}\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot\delta\cdot\Gamma_{\tau}.

Let
\tilde{\Gamma}:=\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
be the group of invertible matrices over
\mathbb{Z}[1/p]
with positive determinant.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer coprime to
p
. Then

(74)

T_{n}J_{w}[\tau]=\prod_{\delta\in M_{n}(\tau)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\,\in\,\tilde{\Gamma}\delta\tau\\
v_{p}(w)\,=\,0\end{subarray}}w^{[0,\infty]\cdot(w^{\prime},w)}.

Proof.

See
[
DPV21
, Theorem 2.9]
.
∎

Lemma
4.2
below recalls the existence of a bijection between “level
n

[
DPV21
, § 1]
. Define the (multi)set

(75)

\RM_{n}^{+}(\tau):=\bigsqcup_{\delta\in M_{n}(\tau)}\left\{w\in\tilde{\Gamma}\delta\tau\ :\ \begin{array}[]{c}w>0>w^{\prime}\\
v_{p}(w)=0,\,v_{p}(\mathrm{disc}(w))\leq v_{p}(n)\end{array}\right\}

where
\mathrm{disc}(w)
is defined to be the discriminant of a primitive integral quadratic form that has
w
as a root. Similarly, define
\RM_{n}^{-}(\tau)
as above, with the condition
w>0>w^{\prime}
replaced by
w^{\prime}>0>w
.

Remark 6
.

Note that an RM point
w
may appear several times in the set
\RM_{n}^{+}(\tau)
, and the multiplicity with which it does is a subtle actor in the bijections discussed below. It is therefore important to use a disjoint union in this definition. The nature of the matrices
\delta
, which index the multiplicity with which an RM point

arises, was made clearer in the proof of
[
DPV21
, Lemma 1.9]
.

The sets
\RM_{n}^{\pm}(\tau)
play a crucial role in the explicit formulae for the Fourier coefficients of the diagonal restrictions of the Eisenstein family
\mathcal{E}
investigated in
[
DPV21
]
. It will be observed below that they appear again in the analysis of the diagonal restriction of the anti-parallel family
\mathcal{F}
studied in §
3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exist
two bijections

\begin{array}[]{ccll}\varphi_{1}\ :&\RM_{n}^{-}(-\tau)&\lra&\RM_{n}^{+}(\tau)\\[4.0pt]
\varphi_{2}\ :&\left\{(I,\nu):\begin{array}[]{cc}\nu\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}_{+},&p\nmid I\,|\,(\nu)\mathfrak{d}\\
\mathrm{Tr}(\nu)=n,&I\sim(1,\tau)\end{array}\right\}&\lra&\RM_{n}^{+}(\tau)\\
\end{array}

such that, after writing
\nu=p^{m}\nu_{0}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\varphi_{1}(w)&=&-w,\\
\varphi_{2}(I,\nu)&=&\nu_{0}\sqrt{\Delta}/\Nm(I).\end{array}

Proof.

A bijection
\varphi_{1}
as required may be constructed by letting
W_{\infty}
be a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues
1
and
-1
. If
w\in\tilde{\Gamma}\delta\tau
, then

-w=W_{\infty}w\ \in\ \tilde{\Gamma}\delta^{\prime}(-\tau)

where
\delta^{\prime}\in M_{n}(\tau)
is the double coset representative of
W_{\infty}\delta W_{\infty}
. To obtain a bijection
\varphi_{2}
as above, one first uses a bijection

\Phi\ :\ \ \left\{(I,\nu):\begin{array}[]{cc}\nu\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}_{+},&I\,|\,(\nu)\mathfrak{d}\\
\mathrm{Tr}(\nu)=n,&I\sim(1,\tau)\end{array}\right\}\ \ \lra\ \bigsqcup_{\delta\in M_{n}(\tau)}\left\{w\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\delta\tau\ :\ w>0>w^{\prime}\right\}

which satisfies

. Such a bijection was constructed in
[
DPV21
, Lemma 1.9]
. Note that the source of
\Phi
is almost equal to the source of the desired bijection, minus the condition

. Under the bijection
\Phi
, the condition

is equivalent to the condition that
w=w_{0}p^{m}
for some

and
p\nmid w_{0}
. The map

then defines a bijection between

\left\{w\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\delta\tau\ :\ \begin{array}[]{c}w>0>w^{\prime}\\
w=w_{0}p^{m},\,p\nmid w_{0},\,m\geq 0\end{array}\right\},

\left\{w\in\tilde{\Gamma}\delta\tau\ :\ \begin{array}[]{c}w>0>w^{\prime}\\

so that the result follows by definition of

.
∎

4.2.
Derivatives of diagonal restrictions.

The modular generating series for the RM values of the winding cocycle that is the subject of Theorem C will be constructed from three different analytic families that specialise to the Eisenstein series of parallel weight one. More specifically, the anti-parallel cuspidal family from §
3.5
, and the two Eisenstein families of Lemma
3.1
:

E^{(p)}_{1+\varepsilon}(1,\psi)\quad\mbox{and }\quad E^{(p)}_{1+\varepsilon}(\psi,1)

The
modularity
of the generating series of Theorem C will follow from two simple results:

(1)

The vanishing of the diagonal restriction of
E^{(p)}_{1}(1,\psi)
, the
p
-stabilisation of the parallel weight one Eisenstein series (for which the shorthand
f
was used in §
3
),

(2)

For any analytic family of
p
-adic modular forms whose specialisation vanishes, the specialisation of its

is also a
p
-adic modular form.

These results were also used in
[
DPV21
]
, where full proofs may be found. Since they play an important role in the argument, they will be briefly reviewed here.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
p
is inert in
F
, and
\psi
is an odd unramified character of
F
. Then

E_{1}^{(p)}(1,\psi)(z,z)=0.

Proof.

Recall that the diagonal restriction of any Hilbert modular form with Fourier coefficients
a_{\nu}
has the following
q
-expansion:

(76)

a_{0}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{-1}\\
\mathrm{Tr}(\nu)\,=\,n\end{subarray}}a_{\nu}\ q^{n}.

For the Eisenstein series
E_{1}^{(p)}(1,\psi)
, the Fourier coefficient
a_{\nu}
is equal to

4\sum_{p\nmid I\mid(\nu)\mathfrak{d}}\psi(I)

For any ideal
I
in the index set of this summation, we may write
IJ(p^{e})=(\nu)\mathfrak{d}
for some uniquely determined ideal
J
coprime to
p
, since
p
is inert in
F
. The conjugate
J^{\prime}
then defines an ideal coprime to

, dividing
(\nu^{\prime})\mathfrak{d}
. Observe that, since
\psi
is odd, we have

\psi(J^{\prime})=\psi(J)^{-1}=\psi(I)\psi(\mathfrak{d})^{-1}=-\psi(I),

and it follows that

. Therefore the diagonal restriction must vanish.
∎

The three analytic families that specialise to

therefore give families of diagonal restrictions that specialise to zero. It is easy to see that the specialisation of the

of each of these families of diagonal restrictions is a
p
-adic modular form of weight two. The following result, appearing as Lemma 2.1 in
[

]
, ascertains that it is even overconvergent, though this is not used in what follows.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}(t)
is a family of overconvergent forms of weight

, indexed by a parameter
\,t
on a closed rigid analytic disk

in weight space. Suppose that

•

the disk

is centred at an integer

,

•

the specialisation vanishes

Then the derivative
\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}(0)

.

4.3.
Proof of Theorem C

Theorem
3.13
will now be used to construct the modular generating series

of Theorem C, and calculate its constant term. The argument involves three main steps:

(1)

The definition of the power series

of Theorem

(2)

The computation of its diagonal restriction
\partial f^{+}_{\psi}

(3)

The computation of its ordinary projection

The forms constructed in these three steps lie in increasingly structured spaces:

The series

\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi}\ :=\ \mathcal{F}_{\psi}+\mathcal{E}_{\psi}\ =\ a_{0}(\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi})\ +\sum a_{\nu}(\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi})\,q^{\nu},\qquad\qquad a_{\nu}(\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi})\in E[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2}),

where the first term

\mathcal{E}_{\psi}:=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\left(E_{1+\varepsilon}^{(p)}(1,\psi)\ -\ \,E_{1+\varepsilon}^{(p)}(\psi,1)\right).

Recall the Gross–Stark unit

attached to the odd character
\psi
, defined in (

is identified with its image under the

-adic embedding

in order to lighten the notations and view it as an element of

-adic logarithm

Proposition 4.5
.

The Fourier coefficients of
\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi}

\begin{array}[]{lllll}a_{0}(\mathcal{F}^{+}_{\psi})&=&\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\log_{p}(u_{\psi}).\\[3.0pt]

Since
\psi

\psi(\mathfrak{d})=-1
. The constant term of
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}

a_{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\psi})=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}\left(a_{0}(1,\psi)-a_{0}(\psi,1)\right)=\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\cdot\frac{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\psi)}{\mathscrbf{L}(\psi)}(L_{p}^{\prime}(\psi,0)+L_{p}^{\prime}(\psi^{-1},0)).

∎

Next, we consider the

and

The power series

Proof.

4.3

The ordinary projection of the

The Compressible Euler and Acoustic Limits from quantum Boltzmann Equation with Fermi-Dirac Statistics
Ning Jiang, Kai Zhou

Abstract
. This paper justifies the compressible Euler and acoustic limits from quantum Boltzmann equation with Fermi-Dirac statistics (briefly, BFD) rigorously. This limit was formally derived in Zakrevskiy’s thesis [ 43 ] by moment method. We employ the Hilbert approach. The forms of the classical compressible Euler system and the one derived from BFD are different. Our proof is based on the analysis of the nonlinear implicit transformation of these two forms, and a few novel nonlinear estimates. Keywords. Quantum Boltzmann equation; Fermi-Dirac statistics; Hilbert expansion; Compressible Euler limit; Acoustic limit.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The BFD equation

In this paper, we investigate the motion of the quantum particles following the Fermi-Dirac statistics. Namely, the evolution of rarefied gas with quantum effect described by the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac (briefly BFD) equation:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=\mathcal{C}(F),

is studied. Here
0\leqslant F(t,x,v)\leqslant 1
is the number density of particles located at position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, at time
t\geqslant 0
. The collision integral
\mathcal{C}(F)
takes the form

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}(F)=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(v_{*}-v,\omega)\Big[\,F^{\prime}F_{*}^{\prime}(1-F)(1-F_{*})-FF_{*}(1-F^{\prime})(1-F_{*}^{\prime})\,\Big]\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}.

Here
F
,
F_{*}
,
F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}_{*}
appearing in the integrand are understood to mean
F(t,x,\cdot)
evaluated at the velocities
v
,
v_{*}
,
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}_{*}
respectively. We will always use this kind of shorthands throughout the paper. The primed velocities
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}_{*}
are velocities after collision of any pair of colliding molecules with velocities
v
and
v_{*}
before. In the process of collision, the system of equations

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} v+v_{*}=&v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*},\\
|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}=&|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

is that of the only natural conservation laws, namely, the conservation laws of momentum and kinetic energy. The solutions to (
1.3
) are represented by

(1.4)

v^{\prime}=v+[(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v_{*}^{\prime}=v_{*}-[(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

In (
1.2
), the collision kernel
b(v_{*}-v,\omega)
takes the classical factored form

b(v_{*}-v,\omega)=|v_{*}-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}\left(\left|\omega\cdot\frac{v_{*}-v}{|v_{*}-v|}\right|\right),\;-3<\gamma\leqslant 1,

when the molecular interaction is given by a long-range potential. Here we call it the hard potential kernel for
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant 1
, while soft potential kernel for
-3<\gamma<0
. However, throughout the entire paper, we consider the hard sphere collision model, i.e.,

b(v_{*}-v,\omega)=|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|.

The BFD equation is a modification of the classical Boltzmann equation, when we account for the quantum effects of molecular encounters. In some other literatures, BFD equation is also frequently called Uehling-Uhlenbeck equation or Nordheim equation. At variance with the derivation of the classical Boltzmann equation
[
13
,
32
]
, the rigorous derivation of the BFD equation is yet to be established. Since it was presented in Nordheim
[
39
]
, Uehling and Uhlenbeck
[
42
]
by heuristic arguments, works on the derivation of the BFD equation have been taking lots of time.

Mathematically, for the well-posedness of BFD equation, early results were obtained by Dolbeault
[
10
]
and Lions
[
33
]
. They studied the global weak existence of solutions in mild or distributional sense for the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
under some assumptions on the collision kernel. Lu systematically studied the global existence and stability of weak solutions for general initial data in
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
. In particular, he considered the collision kernels of very soft potential with a weak angular cutoff. On the other hand, in our paper
[
29
]
, we prove the global-in-time existence of classical solution for hard sphere potential with an assumption on the smallness of the initial data.

In the other direction, He-Lu-Pulvirenti
[
27
]
mathematically justified the semi-classical limit from homogeneous quantum Boltzmann equation to the homogeneous Fokker-Planck-Landau equation as the Planck constant
\hbar
tends to zero.

1.2.
The hydrodynamic limits for kinetic equations

In recent decades, there has a great interest on the connection between kinetic equations and their fluid limits in different scalings.
In the late 1980s, the so-called BGL program was initialized by Bardos-Golse-Levermore
[
1
,
2
]
, which aimed at obtaining rigorously the Leray’s solutions to incompressible Navier-Stokes equations from the Boltzmann equation with large initial data in the framework of DiPerna-Lions renormalized solutions
[
9
]
. This program was finally completed by Golse and Saint-Raymond
[
17
,
18
]
. For incompressible Euler limits, Saint-Raymond made the main contributions in
[
40
,
41
]
, which connected the DiPern-Lions solutions to the dissipative solutions. As for the hyperbolic scaling, say, acoustic limit, Golse-Levermore
[
16
]
established the acoustic limit with an assumption on the size of the fluctuations. Later on, the restriction has been relaxed to the borderline in
[
28
]
.

In the classical solutions framework, there are mainly two approaches: one is based on the spectral analysis of semigroup and another is the nonlinear energy method. For the former approach, Bardos and Ukai
[
4
]
took the first step to obtain the uniform in
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
(the Knusden number) global existence of classical solution to scaled Boltzmann equation for hard cutoff potential. As a result, they also established the incompressible Navier-Stokes limit from Boltzmann equation. More contributions refer to the literatures
[
5
,
6
,
14
,
38
]
. For the nonlinear energy method developed by Guo
[
21
,
22
]
, we focus on its application to the compressible Euler limits although there are many results (c.f.
[
23
,
26
,
30
]
) of incompressible fluid limits. As an improvement of Caflisch’s work
[
7
]
where the truncated Hilbert expansion with a remainder was used, Guo-Jang-Jiang
[
25
]
made the first step to prove the compressible Euler limit and acoustic limit in optimal scaling from Boltzmann equation by employing the nonlinear energy method. This method relies on our knowledge of the local well-posedness of compressible Euler equation in advance.

Although there are many results of hydrodynamic limits from the classical Boltzmann equation, the research on fluid limits from quantum Boltzmann equation is much limited. The compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes limits and incompressible Navier-Stokes limits from BFD equation for both hard potential and soft potential are formally derived by Zakrevskiy
[
43
]
. We also mention that, Filbet, Hu and Jin
[
11
]
introduced a new scheme for quantum Boltzmann equation to capture the Euler limit by numerical computations. However, due to the absence of rigorous mathematical theory on fluid limits from quantum Boltzmann equation, an attempt on rigorous proof of incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier limit from BFD equation is made by Jiang-Xiong-Zhou
[
29
]
recently. In
[
29
]
, the authors prove the global existence of classical solutions near equilibrium and in addition, they also obtain the uniform in
\varepsilon
energy estimates, by which and the structure of equations of conservation laws they establish the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier limit from BFD equation.

1.3.
Formal Analysis of Fluid Limits from BFD Equation

Our interests are the compressible Euler limit and the acoustic limit from the BFD equation. Therefore, the dimensionless number
\varepsilon>0
called the Knudsen number is introduced to rescale the BFD equation (
1.1
) as:

(1.5)

\partial_{t}F_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{C}(F_{\varepsilon}),

on which the initial data is further imposed:

(1.6)

F_{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(x,v).

Next we exhibit some basic properties of BFD equation. In the case of classical Boltzmann equation
[
8
]
, the conservation laws and Boltzmann’s
H
Theorem are statements that bear exclusively on the collision integral. As a analogy, the collision operator
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the conservation laws.

Proposition 1.1

(Proposition 2, Chapter I in
[
43
]
)
.

For any measurable function

(1.7)

F(t,x,v):\,\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad 0\leqslant F\leqslant 1\;a.e.

rapidly decaying on the infinity, there hold

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{C}(F)\mathrm{d}v=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\,\mathcal{C}(F)\mathrm{d}v=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}\mathcal{C}(F)\mathrm{d}v=0.

On the other hand,
H
Theorem is also hold for the BFD equation (c.f. Proposition 4, Chapter I in
[
43
]
).

Proposition 1.2

(
H
-Theorem)
.

For every measurable rapidly decaying
F
with at most polynomially increasing
\left|\ln\frac{1-F}{F}\right|
satisfying (
1.7
), the following properties are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{C}(F)=0
;

(2)

The entropy production rate is zero,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{C}(F)\ln\frac{1-F}{F}\mathrm{d}v=0;

(3)

F
is a (local) Fermi-Dirac distribution,

(1.8)

F_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{u},T}\equiv\mu(t,x,v)=\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2T}-\mathfrak{f}}},

Here in (3) above,
T=T(t,x)>0
is the temperature,
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the bulk velocity, and
\mathfrak{f}(t,x)/T(t,x)
is the total chemical potential. When the parameters
(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{u},T)
are constants, the corresponding distributions are called
global
Fermi-Dirac distributions.

1.3.1.
Hilbert Expansion

By utilizing the above conservation laws and
H
-Theorem for BFD equation, Zakrevskiy
[
43
]
formally shows that the solutions to the scaled BFD equation (
1.5
) converge to the local Fermi-Dirac distribution
\mu
(c.f. Theorem I.5 in
[
43
]
). As a consequence, the following Euler system for compressible fluid

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho\mathfrak{u})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho\mathfrak{u})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho\mathfrak{u}\otimes\mathfrak{u})+\nabla_{x}\mathcal{E}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{E}\right)+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left[\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+\frac{5}{2}\mathcal{E}\right)\mathfrak{u}\,\right]=0,\end{aligned}\right.

is satisfied by the fluid variables
(\rho,\mathfrak{u},\mathcal{E})
which are given by

(1.10)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu\mathrm{d}v=T^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{f}}}\mathrm{d}v,

\displaystyle\rho\mathfrak{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\mu\mathrm{d}v=T^{3/2}\mathfrak{u}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{f}}}\mathrm{d}v,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}\mu\mathrm{d}v=\frac{1}{3}T^{5/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|^{2}}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\mathfrak{f}}}\mathrm{d}v.

Although by using the so-called moment method, the literature
[
43
]
formally explained how to acquire the compressible Euler equations (
1.9
) from scaled BFD equation (
1.5
), such computations are not rigorous proofs. However, the process obtaining the Euler equations would play a role of valuable intuition.

Now we will apply the Hilbert expansion (c.f.
[
8
]
) to the scaled BFD equation (
1.5
). In the process, the arguments are largely formal but rational, hence highly instructive. To this end, the bilinear operator

(1.11)

\mathcal{Q}(F,G)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\left(F_{*}^{\prime}G^{\prime}-F_{*}G\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*},

and the trilinear operator

(1.12)

\mathcal{T}(F,G,H)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\Big[F_{*}^{\prime}G^{\prime}\left(H_{*}+H\right)-F_{*}G\left(H_{*}^{\prime}+H^{\prime}\right)\Big]\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*},

are designated respectively for the sake of simplicity. In terms of the local Fermi-Dirac distribution
\mu
, we additionally denote the local linearized collision operator
\mathcal{L}
by

(1.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}g=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\mathcal{N}\Bigg\{\left(\frac{g}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{g}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)_{*}-\left(\frac{g}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)_{*}^{\prime}-\left(\frac{g}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)^{\prime}\Bigg\}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}.

Here, the conservation laws of momentum and kinetic energy (
1.3
) allow us to denote
\mathcal{N}
appearing above by

(1.14)

\mathcal{N}=\mu_{*}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}(1-\mu_{*})(1-\mu)=\mu_{*}\mu(1-\mu_{*}^{\prime})(1-\mu^{\prime}).

By inspection, the operator
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{L}
can be equivalently formulated with respect to
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{T}
as

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(F)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(F,F)-\mathcal{T}(F,F,F),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{F}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}[\,\mathcal{Q}(\mu,F)+\mathcal{Q}(F,\mu)-\mathcal{T}(F,\mu,\mu)-\mathcal{T}(\mu,F,\mu)-\mathcal{T}(\mu,\mu,F)\,].

In general, Hilbert expansion means to seek the solutions as the power series of
\varepsilon
:

F_{\varepsilon}(t,x,v)\simeq\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\varepsilon^{n}F_{n}(t,x,v),

Plugging the form above into the scaled BFD equation (
1.5
) and collecting the coefficients of the same orders of
\varepsilon
on both sides, one can obtain

(1.16)

\begin{array}[]{rrl}O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}):&0=&\mathcal{Q}(F_{0},F_{0})-\mathcal{T}(F_{0},F_{0},F_{0}),\\
&\\

&&\quad-\mathcal{T}(F_{0},F_{1},F_{0})-\mathcal{T}(F_{1},F_{0},F_{0})-\mathcal{T}(F_{0},F_{0},F_{1}),\\

&&\qquad\quad-\mathcal{T}(F_{1},F_{0},F_{2})-\underline{\mathcal{T}(F_{0},F_{0},F_{3})};\\
&\cdots\hfill\cdots\hfill\cdots\\

We firstly expect to recover the compressible Euler equations (
1.9
) from (
1.16
). Corresponding to
O(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})

H

\mu

On the other hand,
\mu=F_{\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{u},T}
is a function of
\mathfrak{f},\,\mathfrak{u},\,T
. The compressible Euler system (
1.9
) thereby can be recasted in terms of

(1.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{u}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathfrak{f}=0,\\

\displaystyle\frac{F_{n}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}=

\mathcal{P}

(1.20)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}\}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{F_{n}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(-\frac{(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})F_{n-1}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\

\frac{F_{n}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}
is determined by the equation corresponding to
O(\varepsilon^{n})
. For each
n\geqslant 1
, applying
\mathcal{P}
to the equation corresponding to
O(\varepsilon^{n})

are imposed on (

1.3.2.
The acoustic limits from BFD equation

Since the acoustic equations are the linearization of compressible Euler equations (
1.9
) about the constant state
(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{u},T)=(1,0,1)
, we will formally acquire the acoustic limit from BFD equation (
1.5
) when
F_{\varepsilon}
is close to the global Fermi-Dirac distribution

(1.26)

F_{1,0,1}\equiv\mu_{0}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-1}}.

To this end, we apply Taylor expansion to
\mu=F_{(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{u},T)}
with

\mathfrak{f}=1+\delta\mathfrak{f}_{1},\quad\mathfrak{u}=\delta u,\quad T=1+\delta\theta,\,\delta>0

to get

(1.27)

\mu=\mu_{0}\Big[\,1+\delta(1-\mu_{0})\Big(\mathfrak{f}_{1}+v\cdot u+\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}\theta\Big)\,\Big]+O(\delta^{2}),

Same as the course of
[
3
]
, the expansion (
1.27
) indicates that we should better introduce
G_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{\varepsilon}
defined by

(1.28)

F_{\varepsilon}=\mu_{0}+\delta G_{\varepsilon}\equiv\mu_{0}+\delta\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}g_{\varepsilon}.

So that the scaled BFD equation (
1.5
) can be rewritten as

(1.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}_{0}g_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}}\left[\mathcal{Q}(\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{T}(\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{0})-\mathcal{T}(\mu_{0},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{T}(\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{0},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}}\mathcal{T}(\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon}),

where the operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is defined by (
1.13
) with
\mu
replaced by
\mu_{0}
and
G_{\varepsilon}\equiv\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}g_{\varepsilon}
. Then the Proposition
1.1
immediately implies the local conservation laws of mass, momentum and energy:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\langle G_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\nabla_{x}\cdot\langle v\,G_{\varepsilon}\rangle&=0,\\
\partial_{t}\langle v\,G_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\nabla_{x}\cdot\langle v\otimes v\,G_{\varepsilon}\rangle&=0,\\
\partial_{t}\langle|v|^{2}\,G_{\varepsilon}\rangle+\nabla_{x}\cdot\langle v|v|^{2}\,G_{\varepsilon}\rangle&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where for any integrable function
\xi=\xi(v)
, we denote the integral by
\langle\xi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi\mathrm{d}v
.

Now we are ready to give a formal theorem in the style of
[
1
]
for the acoustic equations formulated as

(1.30)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\sigma+\frac{2}{3}(\overline{K}_{A}-1)\nabla_{x}\cdot u&=0,\\
\partial_{t}u+\nabla_{x}(\sigma+\theta)&=0,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+\frac{2}{3}\nabla_{x}\cdot u&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

by assuming the relevant moments of
g_{\varepsilon}
converge in the sense of distributions as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
\overline{K}_{A}=\frac{5\overline{\mathcal{E}}}{2\overline{\rho}}
with
\overline{\mathcal{E}}
and
\overline{\rho}
corresponding to
(\mathfrak{f},T)=(1,1)
in (
1.10
).

Theorem

(Formal Acoustic Limit Theorem)
.

Let
G_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
1.28
) and the fluctuations
g_{\varepsilon}
given by (
1.28
) be a family of weak solutions to equation (
1.29
). Assume
\{g_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
converge in the sense of distributions to a function
g\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathrm{d}t;L^{2}(\mathrm{d}v)\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Furthermore, assume that the moments

\langle G_{\varepsilon}\rangle,\qquad\langle vG_{\varepsilon}\rangle,\qquad\langle v\otimes vG_{\varepsilon}\rangle,\qquad\langle v|v|^{2}G_{\varepsilon}\rangle

satisfy the local conservation laws and converge in the sense of distributions as
\varepsilon\to 0
to the corresponding moments

\langle G\rangle,\qquad\langle vG\rangle,\qquad\langle v\otimes vG\rangle,\qquad\langle v|v|^{2}G\rangle

with
G=\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}g
and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}g_{\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{L}_{0}g,\qquad\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}}\mathcal{T}(G_{\varepsilon},G_{\varepsilon},G_{\varepsilon})\to 0,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})}}\left[\mathcal{Q}(G_{\varepsilon},G_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{T}(G_{\varepsilon},G_{\varepsilon},\mu_{0})-\mathcal{T}(\mu_{0},G_{\varepsilon},G_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{T}(G_{\varepsilon},\mu_{0},G_{\varepsilon})\right]\to 0,

in the sense of distributions as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then
g
has the form

g=\left\{\sigma+u\cdot v+\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-(\overline{K}_{A}-1)\right)\theta\right\}\sqrt{\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0})},

where
(\sigma,u,\theta)
solve the acoustic equations (
1.30
).

Generally, the fluctuation amplitude
\delta
is a function of
\varepsilon
satisfying

\delta\to 0,\;\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

On can take the form

\delta=\varepsilon^{m},\quad\text{ for any }\,m>0.

Based on the Formal Acoustic Limit Theorem, by comparing (
1.28
) with the Taylor expansion of
\mu
(
1.27
), one can formally understand that
\{G_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
converge to

(1.31)

G=\left\{\sigma+v\cdot u+\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-(\overline{K}_{A}-1)\right)\theta\right\}\mu_{0}(1-\mu_{0}),

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, while
(\sigma,u,\theta)
satisfy the acoustic system (
1.30
).

\displaystyle F_{3}=

Putting the expansion (

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})

\displaystyle F_{R,\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{F_{R,\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\varepsilon^{i-1}\Big(\,\mathcal{Q}(F_{i},F_{R,\varepsilon})+\mathcal{Q}(F_{R,\varepsilon},F_{i})\,\Big)+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{Q}(F_{R,\varepsilon},F_{R,\varepsilon})

Moreover, the remainder equation (

1.6.
Sketch of Ideas

As one of a series of works, the compressible Euler limit from BFD equation is the easiest of all hydrodynamic limits in formal derivation, but also the most difficult one for which giving a rigorous proof. From the mathematical point of view, we expect to utilize the Hilbert expansion with a reminder
F_{R,\varepsilon}
to certify this limiting process in
L^{2}-L^{\infty}
framework. For this purpose, the interplay between
L^{2}
estimates and
L^{\infty}
estimates on the remainder
F_{R,\varepsilon}
play a key role.

In view of the remainder equation (
1.33
), the equation governed by
f_{R,\varepsilon}
has the form

\partial_{t}f_{R,\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R,\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}f_{R,\varepsilon}=\text{ some other terms}.

The coercivity (
2.21
) of the operator
\mathcal{L}
provides a kinetic dissipative rate
\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\varepsilon}\|\{\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}\}f_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}
for us in
L^{2}
estimates. This causes
\|f_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{2}
to be bounded by
\varepsilon^{2}\|h_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}
. On the other hand, the equation for
h_{R,\varepsilon}
is of the form (
3.20
):

\partial_{t}h_{R,\varepsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}h_{R,\varepsilon}+\frac{\nu(\mu)}{\varepsilon}h_{R,\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{K}_{w}h_{R,\varepsilon}=\text{ some other terms}.

Then, the norm
\varepsilon^{3/2}\|h_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}
can be controlled by
\varepsilon^{3/2}\|h_{R,\varepsilon}(0)\|_{\infty}+\|f_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{2}
. However, one can find there exists a disparity of order
\varepsilon^{3/2}
between
L^{2}
norm and
L^{\infty}
norm. This is mainly caused by the lack of structure of dissipative rate
\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\varepsilon}\|\{\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{P}\}f_{R,\varepsilon}\|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}
in
L^{\infty}
estimates.

Once we acquire the interplay above, we will arrive at the uniform in
\varepsilon
bound

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\{\|f_{R,\varepsilon}(t)\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{3/2}\|h_{R,\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\infty}\right\}\leqslant C,

under the assumption on uniform boundedness of
\left\{\|f_{R,\varepsilon}(0)\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{3/2}\|h_{R,\varepsilon}(0)\|_{\infty}\right\}
with respect to small
\varepsilon>0
. In the sequel, we prove that
\varepsilon^{3}F_{R,\varepsilon}
is a infinitesimal as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

As for the acoustic limit, our purpose is to estimate
G_{\varepsilon}-G
. On one hand, from the formal analysis in subsection
1.3
, we know that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
F_{\varepsilon}
is close to the local Fermi-Dirac distribution
\mu^{\delta}
constructed by the smooth solution
(\rho^{\delta},\mathfrak{u}^{\delta},\mathcal{E}^{\delta})
to the compressible Euler equation. Consequently, we first prove that

F_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\delta}=O(\varepsilon).

On the other hand, same as the expansion (
1.27
), we then are able to establish that

\mu^{\delta}-\mu_{0}=\delta G+O(\delta^{2}).

Give a glimpse of (
1.28
), by the two estimates above we conclude our Theorem
1.2
.

It should be point out that compared with
[
25
]
, the limiting process in the case of BFD equation is more complex. The parameters
(\rho_{B},\mathfrak{u}_{B},T_{B})
of local Maxwellian
M=\frac{\rho_{B}}{(2\pi T_{B})^{3/2}}\exp\{-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}_{B}|^{2}}{2T_{B}}\}
can be determined by the compressible Euler equations deduced from classical Boltzmann equation

(CEB)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho_{B}+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho_{B}\mathfrak{u}_{B})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho_{B}\mathfrak{u}_{B})+\nabla_{x}\cdot(\rho_{B}\mathfrak{u}_{B}\otimes\mathfrak{u}_{B})+\nabla_{x}p=0,\\

the standard (row) base vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the symbol
(\cdot)^{\mathrm{Tr}}
means the transpose of row vectors.

Obviously, the matrix
A_{0}^{n}
is positive definite. For the existence of local-in-time smooth solutions, we refer to the standard Friedrichs’ theory (c.f. Chapter 2 in
[
37
]
).

Now we devote ourselves to lifespan of the equations (
1.21
) and the estimate (
2.14
). It suffices to establish a priori energy estimates of (
2.15
). Taking
\partial^{\beta}
of (
2.15
) to get

\displaystyle A_{0}^{n}\partial_{t}\partial^{\beta}U_{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}^{n}\partial_{j}\partial^{\beta}U_{n}+A_{4}^{n}\partial^{\beta}U_{n}=

\displaystyle\partial^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{n}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant|\beta|-1}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{0}^{n}\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant|\beta|-1}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{j}^{n}\partial_{j}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant|\beta|-1}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{4}^{n}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}.

Then taking inner product with
\partial^{\beta}U_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)
and integrating over
[0,t]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(A_{0}^{n}\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(t),\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(t)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)}-\left(A_{0}^{n}\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(0),\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(0)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left[\partial_{t}A_{0}^{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}A_{j}^{n}-(A_{4}^{n}+(A_{4}^{n})^{Tr})\right]\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(s),\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(s)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}2\left(\partial^{\beta}\mathcal{F}_{n},\partial^{\beta}U_{n}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}2\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant|\beta|-1}\left(\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{0}^{n}\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}(s)+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{j}^{n}\partial_{j}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}(s)+\partial^{\beta-\alpha}A_{4}^{n}\partial^{\alpha}U_{n}(s),\partial^{\beta}U_{n}(s)\right)_{L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x)}\mathrm{d}s.

Since

is positive definite, summing up over

above, we arrive at

A simple Grönwall’s in equality implies that

\left\|U_{n}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}}^{2}\leqslant\left\{\left\|U_{n}(0)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{n}(s)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}+2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right\}\left\{1+C(\mathbf{E}_{0,s_{1}+2})te^{C(\mathbf{E}_{0,s_{1}+2})t}\right\},

It follows that

\sup_{t\in(0,\tau)}\left\|U_{n}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s_{1}}}^{2}\leqslant C(\tau,\mathbf{E}_{0,s_{1}+2})\mathbb{E}_{n,s_{1}},

which is exactly (
2.14

\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|\varepsilon^{3/2}h_{R,\varepsilon}(s)\|_{\infty}\leqslant C\big(\|\varepsilon^{3/2}h_{R,\varepsilon}(0)\|_{\infty}+\sup_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|f_{R,\varepsilon}(s)\|_{2}+\varepsilon^{5/2}\big).

(T^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime}
are given by

(\mathfrak{f}^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime}=2\mathfrak{f}_{d}^{\delta},\quad(\mathfrak{u}^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime}=2u_{d}^{\delta},\quad(T^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime}=2T_{d}^{\delta}.

Thus by taking
z=\delta
we obtain

\mu^{\delta,\delta}=\mu_{0}+G\delta+\frac{\mu^{\prime\prime}(\delta_{*})}{2}\delta^{2},\quad\text{ for some }0\leqslant\delta_{*}\leqslant\delta.

To certify the estimate (
4.16
), it is sufficient to show that
\|\mu^{\prime\prime}(\delta_{*})\|_{2}+\|\mu^{\prime\prime}(\delta_{*})\|_{\infty}
is bounded uniformly in
\delta_{*}
. To this end, from the uniform estimates (
4.6
), (
4.18
) and (
4.21
), we deduce the uniform pointwise estimates of

\mathfrak{f}^{\delta,z},\;\mathfrak{u}^{\delta,z},\;T^{\delta,z},\;(\mathfrak{f}^{\delta,z})^{\prime},\;(\mathfrak{u}^{\delta,z})^{\prime},\;(T^{\delta,z})^{\prime},\;(\mathfrak{f}^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime},\;(\mathfrak{u}^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime},\;(T^{\delta,z})^{\prime\prime},

for every
0\leqslant z=\delta_{*}\leqslant\delta_{0}
and any
t\leqslant\tau
.

The estimate (
4.16
) is established as announced.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall the expansion (
1.28
) and the definition (

) of
G
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|G_{\varepsilon}-G\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|G_{\varepsilon}-G\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|\frac{F_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{0}}{\delta}-G\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|\frac{F_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{0}}{\delta}-G\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|\frac{F_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{\mu^{\delta}-\mu_{0}-\delta G}{\delta}\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant\tau}\|\frac{F_{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\delta}}{\delta}+\frac{\mu^{\delta}-\mu_{0}-\delta G}{\delta}\|_{2}.

5.
Appendix

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
0<q<1
, there exists
C>0
such that

\left\langle\mu^{-q/2}\mathcal{L}f,\mu^{-q/2}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|\mu^{-q/2}f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}-C|f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}.

Proof
.

Throughout the proof, we always use the estimates (
2.16
). For some
r>0
, we define the smooth cutoff function
\chi(s)
by

\chi(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\quad 1,\qquad s\geqslant 2r,\\
&\in[0,1],\quad r\leqslant s\leqslant 2r,\\
&\quad 0,\qquad 0\leqslant s\leqslant r.\end{aligned}\right.

Then we split
\mathcal{K}
as
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{1-\chi}+\mathcal{K}^{\chi}
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{1-\chi}f=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}(1-\chi(|v_{*}-v|))\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)_{*}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}(1-\chi(|v_{*}-v|))\left\{\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)_{*}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)^{\prime}\right\}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{1}^{1-\chi}-\mathcal{K}_{2}^{1-\chi}.

Thus

\left\langle\mu^{-q/2}\mathcal{K}^{1-\chi}f,\mu^{-q/2}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}=\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{1}^{1-\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}-\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{2}^{1-\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}.

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{N}
(
1.14
), since
1-\mu
is bounded, we employ Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality to obtain for any
0<q<1

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{1}^{1-\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\iiint_{|v_{*}-v|\leqslant 2r}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega||ff_{*}|\sqrt{\mu\mu_{*}}\mu^{-q}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|f|^{2}\mu^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\mu_{*}^{\frac{1-q}{4}}\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v\right\}^{1/2}\times

\displaystyle\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega||f_{*}|^{2}\mu_{*}^{\frac{1-q}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\mu^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C|f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2},

where we have used
\mu^{-q}(v)\leqslant C\mu^{-\tfrac{q}{2}}(v)\mu^{-(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1-q}{4})}(v_{*})
, because
|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1+q}{2q}|v_{*}-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+Cr^{2}
provided
|v_{*}-v|\leqslant 2r
.
On the other hand, for the part
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{1-\chi}
,
|v_{*}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|=|v_{*}-v|\leqslant 2r
implies that

|v_{*}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{u}|\geqslant|v^{\prime}-\mathfrak{u}|-2r,\quad|v^{\prime}-\mathfrak{u}|\geqslant|v-\mathfrak{u}|-2r.

It immediately follows that for any
0<q^{\prime}<1

\sqrt{\mu_{*}\mu_{*}^{\prime}}+\sqrt{\mu_{*}\mu^{\prime}}\leqslant C\mu_{*}^{(1+q^{\prime})/4}\mu^{(1+q^{\prime})/4},

and for any
q<q^{\prime}<1

\mu^{-q}(v)\leqslant C\mu^{-q^{\prime}/2}(v_{*}^{\prime})\mu^{-q^{\prime}/2}(v^{\prime}).

As a result, since
\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}=\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}v
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{2}^{1-\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\iiint_{|v_{*}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|\leqslant 2r}|v_{*}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|\left(\,|f^{\prime}|\sqrt{\mu_{*}^{\prime}}+|f_{*}^{\prime}|\sqrt{\mu^{\prime}}\,\right)\sqrt{\mu_{*}}\mu^{-q}|f|\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|f^{\prime}|^{2}(\mu^{\prime})^{\frac{1-q^{\prime}}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{*}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|(\mu_{*}^{\prime})^{\frac{1-q^{\prime}}{4}}\mathrm{d}v_{*}^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\right\}^{1/2}\times

\displaystyle\left\{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|f|^{2}\mu_{*}^{\frac{1-q^{\prime}}{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{*}-v|\mu^{\frac{1-q^{\prime}}{4}}\mathrm{d}v\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\right\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C|f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2},

Now we treat the part

\left\langle\mu^{-q/2}\mathcal{K}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q/2}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}=\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}-\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}.

For the first term on the right hand side above, since for
|v_{*}-v|\geqslant r
,

|v_{*}-v|(\mu_{*}\mu)^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\leqslant C(\mu_{*}\mu)^{\frac{1-q}{4}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1-q}{32T}r^{2}\right\},

we obtain by using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\right|\leqslant C\iiint_{|v_{*}-v|\geqslant r}|v_{*}-v||f_{*}||f|\mu_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{q}{2}}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\iiint_{|v_{*}-v|\geqslant r}|v_{*}-v||\mu^{-q/2}f|^{2}(\mu_{*}\mu)^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}v\right)^{1/2}\left(\iiint_{|v_{*}-v|\geqslant r}|v_{*}-v||f_{*}|^{2}\mu_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\frac{1-q}{2}}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}v\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\exp\left\{-\frac{1-q}{32T}r^{2}\right\}|\mu^{-q/2}f|_{L_{v}^{2}}^{2}.

Next, we turn to the part

\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f=2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\chi(|v_{*}-v|)|(v_{*}-v)\cdot\omega|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{\mu(1-\mu)}}\right)^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{*}.

By using the variable changing
v_{*}-v=V
and the classical transformation (see page 43 in
[
15
]
)

(5.1)

\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}V=\frac{2\mathrm{d}V_{\bot}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/}}{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}},\quad V_{/\!\!/}=(V\cdot\omega)\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,V_{\bot}=V-V_{/\!\!/}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

we arrive at

|\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f|\leqslant C\iint_{|V|\geqslant r}|V_{/\!\!/}||f(v+V_{/\!\!/})|\mu^{-1/2}(v+V_{/\!\!/})\mu^{1/2}(v)\mu(v+V)\frac{\mathrm{d}V_{\bot}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/}}{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}.

Let

\eta=v+V_{/\!\!/},\quad\zeta=\frac{1}{2}(v+\eta),

then

V_{\bot}\cdot\zeta=V_{\bot}\cdot v=V_{\bot}\cdot\eta.

Thus

\displaystyle-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|\eta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-|\eta+V_{\bot}-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}=-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|\eta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-2V_{\bot}\cdot(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})-|V_{\bot}|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-2V_{\bot}\cdot(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})-2|\zeta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+2(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})\cdot(v-\mathfrak{u})-\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-|V_{\bot}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(-|V_{\bot}|^{2}-2V_{\bot}\cdot(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})-|\zeta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2})+(-|\zeta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}+2(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})\cdot(v-\mathfrak{u})-|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2})

\displaystyle=-|V_{\bot}+\zeta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}.

Note the estimates (
2.16
), we acquire

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\chi(|V|)}{|V_{/\!\!/}|}|f(v+V_{/\!\!/})|\exp\left\{-\frac{|V_{\bot}+\zeta-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{1}{8T}|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}\right\}\mathrm{d}V_{\bot}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|V_{/\!\!/}|}|f(v+V_{/\!\!/})|\exp\left\{-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}-\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/},

where we have used that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|V_{\bot}+\zeta_{\bot}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}\mathrm{d}V_{\bot}
is bounded for
\zeta_{/\!\!/}=[(\zeta-\mathfrak{u})\cdot\omega]\omega
and
\zeta_{\bot}=\zeta-\zeta_{/\!\!/}
.
As a result, we evaluate that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|V_{/\!\!/}|}\left\{\mu^{-q/2}(v+V_{/\!\!/})|f(v+V_{/\!\!/})|\right\}\left\{\mu^{-q/2}(v)|f(v)|\right\}\times

\displaystyle\exp\left\{\frac{q|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{4T}-\frac{q|v+V_{/\!\!/}-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{4T}-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}-\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/}\mathrm{d}v.

Because

\displaystyle\frac{q|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{4T}-\frac{q|v+V_{/\!\!/}-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{4T}-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}-\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-q)\left\{\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}+\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}+\frac{q}{2T}\left(\frac{|v-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|v+V_{/\!\!/}-\mathfrak{u}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{4}-|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-q)\left\{\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}+\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}+\frac{q}{2T}\left(-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2}-(\zeta-\mathfrak{u}-\tfrac{1}{2}V_{/\!\!/})\cdot V_{/\!\!/}-\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{4}-|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-q)\left\{\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}+\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}-\frac{q}{2T}\left(\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{4}+|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}+V_{/\!\!/}\cdot\zeta_{/\!\!/}\right)-\frac{q}{2T}\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-(1-q)\left\{\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}+\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\},

it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathcal{K}_{2}^{\chi}f,\mu^{-q}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|V_{/\!\!/}|}\exp\left\{-(1-q)\left\{\frac{|V_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{8T}+\frac{|\zeta_{/\!\!/}|^{2}}{2T}\right\}\right\}\mathrm{d}V_{/\!\!/}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\mu^{-q/2}(v+V_{/\!\!/})|f(v+V_{/\!\!/})|\frac{\nu^{1/2}(v+V_{/\!\!/})}{(1+|v+V_{/\!\!/}|)^{1/2}}\right\}\left\{\mu^{-q/2}(v)|f(v)|\frac{\nu^{1/2}(v)}{(1+|v|)^{1/2}}\right\}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant C|(1+|v|)^{-1/2}\mu^{-q/2}f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathbbm{l}_{|v|\leqslant r_{1}}+\mathbbm{l}_{|v|\geqslant r_{1}})\frac{1}{1+|v|}\mu^{-q}|f|^{2}\nu(\mu)\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leqslant C|f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}+\frac{C}{1+r_{1}}|\mu^{-q/2}f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}.

In summary, by taking
r
and
r_{1}
large enough, we deduce that

(5.2)

\left\langle\mu^{-q/2}\mathcal{L}f,\mu^{-q/2}f\right\rangle_{L_{v}^{2}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|\mu^{-q/2}f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}-C|f|_{\nu(\mu)}^{2}.

∎

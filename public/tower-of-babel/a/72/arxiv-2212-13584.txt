On quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with twist maps in their centroids and their relationship with quadratic Lie algebras
R. García-Delgado (1) , G. Salgado (2) and O.A. Sánchez-Valenzuela (1)

Abstract
. Hom-Lie algebras having non-invertible twist maps in their centroids are studied. Central extensions of Hom-Lie algebras having these properties are obtained and shown how the same properties are preserved. Conditions are given so that the produced central extension has an invariant metric with respect to its Hom-Lie product making its twist map self-adjoint when the original Hom-Lie algebra has such a metric. This work is focused on algebras with these properties and following Benayadi and Makhlouf we call them quadratic Hom-Lie algebras. It is shown how a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra gives rise to a quadratic Lie algebra and that the Lie algebra associated to the given Hom-Lie central extension is a Lie algebra central extension of it. It is also shown that if the Hom-Lie product is not a Lie product, there exists a non-abelian algebra, which is in general non-associative too, the commutator of whose product is precisely the Hom-Lie product of the Hom-Lie central extension. Moreover, the algebra whose commutator realizes this Hom-Lie product is shown to be simple if the associated Lie algebra is nilpotent. Non-trivial examples are provided.

Introduction

All algebras and vector spaces in this work
are assumed to be finite dimensional and defined
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic zero.

A
Hom-Lie algebra
over a field
\mathbb{F}

is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)
consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
,
a skew-symmetric bilinear product
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

and a linear map
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, called
the twist map,
satisfying:

(1)

\mu(T(x),\mu(y,z))+\mu(T(y),\mu(z,x))+\mu(T(z),\mu(x,y))=0,

for all
x,y
and
z
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

The algebraic study of Hom-Lie algebras is usually traced back to the
work of Hartwig, Larsson and Silvestrov (see
[
5
]
)
in studying deformations of the Witt and Virasoro algebras.
We shall refer our readers to the references provided
by Benayadi and Makhlouf in
[
2
]
,
and specially to the Introduction of their work,
where a rather complete and comprehensive
summary was given for the various classes of Hom-structures that had
been studied, at least up to 2014 when
[
2
]
was written.
Since then, the study of Hom-structures has grown fastly, covering
a wide range of new interesting issues
(see for example
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,

[
10
]
,
[
11
]
, or
[
12
]
).

The spirit of our work is similar to that of
[
2
]
,
in that we also construct quadratic Hom-Lie algebras
and study their connections with quadratic Lie algebras.
The main differences are that we do not start with a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
,
and our results are focused on
non-invertible twist maps

T

which are furthermore
centroids of

(\mathfrak{g},\mu)

(see for example
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
); that is,
we shall assume throughout this work that,

(2)

T\left(\mu(x,y)\right)=\mu\left(T(x),y\right),\quad\text{ for all }\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is immediate to show that if
T
is an invertible centroid,
then
\mu
is a Lie bracket. That is why we shall assume that
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)>0
,
and when be referred to a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
, the bilinear map

\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
will only be assumed to be skew-symmetric.

We are also interested in Hom-Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)

with
T
satisfying the hypotheses above
and
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a symmetric,
non-degenerate bilinear form
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{F}
,
satisfying,

\displaystyle B(\mu(x,y),z)

\displaystyle=B(x,\mu(y,z)),\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle B(T(x),y)

\displaystyle=B(x,T(y)),\quad\qquad\text{for all\ }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Such a
B
will be referred to as
a
\mu
-invariant metric
on
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)

and, following
[
2
]
, we shall say that
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
is a
quadratic Hom-Lie algebra.

If
T=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, such a
4
-tuple
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
is just a
quadratic Lie algebra.

It is shown in
[
2
]
that the notion of double extension for Lie algebras
can be generalized and carried out on quadratic Hom-Lie algebras
whose twist map is an invertible morphism.
By way of contrast,
what we do in this work is to characterize the quadratic Hom-Lie algebras
satisfying the more relaxed hypotheses just described above for the twist map

T
and the
\mu
-invariant form
B
, using central extensions of quadratic Lie algebras.

A few words are worth our while to answer the question of,
why looking
at quadratic Hom-Lie algebras with non-invertible centroids as twist maps?

It is known that the space of self-adjoint centroids for a
\mu
-invariant metric
B

on
\mathfrak{g}
plays an important role in the theory of quadratic Lie algebras, since
any pair of invariant metrics are related by an invertible self-adjoint centroid.
Also, Schur’s Lemma states that for simple Lie algebras, the
space of self-adjoint centroids is one-dimensional, which leads to the well known
fact that any invariant metric on a simple Lie algebra must be a non-zero scalar
multiple of the Cartan-Killing form.
In
[
1
]
, quadratic Lie algebras are studied under the hypothesis
that its space of self-adjoint centroids is 2-dimensional and some interesting
consequences of this fact are obtained there. For example, it is proved
that all such Lie algebras are
local;
that is, they have a unique
maximal ideal.
Thus, after our findings in
[
4
]
,
it was natural for us to try to understand some of the characteristics
shown by quadratic Hom-Lie algebras under the more general conditions
assumed for them in this work.

Here is now a more detailed

At this point we make a general observation in

that produces a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra

In fact,

With this construction we are able to exhibit an example of a

-dimensional quadratic Hom-Lie algebra

.

In
Thm.
1.2

we start from a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra

, with

in its centroid and

, to

.
Then, in

,

satisfying,

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=xy-yx,\qquad\text{and},

\displaystyle 2xy

\displaystyle=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)-[h(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[x,h(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}.

Now, one of the goals
that originally motivated this work was to
construct (and if possible, to characterize) quadratic Hom-Lie
algebras with
twist maps in their centroids

when the underlying Hom-Lie product is not a Lie algebra bracket.

Using the product

in
\mathfrak{G}
above

2.2
through the
product defined on
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}\times\mathfrak{G}

1.
Characterization of Hom-Lie algebras with twist maps in their centroids

We start by showing how to obtain a quadratic Lie algebra
from a quadratic
Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
having

T
in the centroid.

1.1 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
with
T
in its centroid.
Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be defined by
[x,y]=T\left(\mu(x,y)\right)
,
for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then,
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
is a quadratic Lie algebra
and
T([x,y])=[T(x),y]
, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Define
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
by means of
T\circ\mu
.
Since
T
is a centroid,

T([x,y])=T(\mu(T(x),y))=[T(x),y]
.
It also follows that,

\left[x,[y,z]\right]=T\left(\mu\left(T(x),\mu(y,z)\right)\right),

for any
x
,
y
and
z
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then, the Jacobi identity for
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
follows from
(
1
) and
the linearity of
T
.
Since
T
is
B
-self-adjoint,
the
\mu
-invariance of
B
leads to

B\left([x,y],z\right)=B\left(x,[y,z]\right)
,
proving that
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
is a quadratic Lie algebra.
∎

Observe
that the construction of the Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
in
Prop.
1.1

is similar to the one given in
[
2
]
(
Prop. 1.8
there).

Now, the hypothesis
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=r>0

makes it possible to associate another Hom-Lie algebra to the
quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
\left(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B\right)
;
say,
(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)
. Our first result in that direction shows
how to build up the underlying vector space
\mathfrak{G}

and how it actually has the structure of a quadratic Lie algebra.

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
\left(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B\right)
be
a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra with
T

in its centroid
and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=r>0
.
For any
r
-dimensional vector space
V
,

\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
inherits a quadratic Lie algebra structure

\left(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)
, such that,

(i)

V
is contained in its center
C(\mathfrak{G})
, and

(ii)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}
makes
V
isotropic;
ie,
for all
u
and
v
in
V
,
B_{\mathfrak{G}}(u,v)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be a complementary subspace to
\operatorname{Im}(T)
.
Then,
\operatorname{dim}\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=r
.
Let
\{\,a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\,\mid\,a_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}\,\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{a}

and let
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\,\mid\,v_{i}\in V\,\}
be a basis of
V
.

Claim A1.

There are: (1) an injective linear map
k:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
,
and, (2) a skew-symmetric bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
in
\mathfrak{G}
,
such that
k\left([x,y]\right)=\left[k(x),y\right]_{\mathfrak{G}}
for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Indeed. Define the linear map
k:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
by,

(3)

k(x)=T(x)+B(a_{1},x)v_{1}+\ldots+B(a_{r},x)v_{r},\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{g}.

This is injective because
B
is non-degenerate,
T
is
B
-self-adjoint,
and
\operatorname{Ker}(T)\cap\mathfrak{a}^{\perp}=\{0\}
.

Let
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}:\mathfrak{G}\times\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}
be the bilinear map defined by:

(4)

\displaystyle\,[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle=k\left(\mu(x,y)\right),\qquad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\,[x+u,v]_{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ for all }x\in\mathfrak{g}\ \text{ and }u,v\in V.

Since
k(x)-T(x)
belongs to
V
for any
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
,
and since
[V,\mathfrak{G}]_{\mathfrak{G}}=\{0\}
, it follows
from (
4
)
that for any
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
,

(5)

k([x,y])=k(\mu(T(x),y))=[T(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}}=[k(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}}\,.

Claim A2.

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
is a Lie bracket in
\mathfrak{G}
.

Let
x,y,z
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v,w
be in
V
. By
Claim A1
and (
4
) we have:

\displaystyle\,\left[x+u,[y+v,z+w]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right]_{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle=\!\left[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{G}}]_{\mathfrak{G}}\!=\![x,k(\mu(y,z))\right]_{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle=k\left(\mu(x,T(\mu(y,z)))\right)=k\left(\mu\left(T(x),\mu(y,z)\right)\right)

Then the Hom-Lie Jacobi identity for
\mu
implies that

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
satisfies the Jacobi identity.
Thus,
\left(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)
is a
(n+r)
-dimensional Lie algebra.

Claim A3.

There exists a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

B_{\mathfrak{G}}
on

\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\operatorname{Im}T\oplus V

under which
\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{a}
and
V
is isotropic.

Let
B_{\mathfrak{G}}:\mathfrak{G}\times\mathfrak{G}\to\mathbb{F}
be defined by,

(6)

\begin{split}&B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(T(x),T(y)\right)=B(T(x),y),\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\\
&B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\mathfrak{a}\oplus V,\operatorname{Im}(T)\right)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a})=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(V,V)=\{0\},\\
&B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(a_{i},v_{j}\right)=\delta_{ij},\quad\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i,j\leq r.\end{split}

Clearly
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
is non-degenerate.
Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\operatorname{Im}(T)
, it follows that,

(P1)

\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathfrak{G}\mid B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)=0,\,\text{ for all }y\in\mathfrak{g}\}=\mathfrak{a}
;

(P2)

\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset V^{\perp}
in
\mathfrak{G}
.

Claim A4.

\operatorname{Im}k=V^{\perp}
.

It follows from (
6
)
that
V^{\perp}=\operatorname{Im}(T)\oplus V
.
From the definition of
k
(see (
3
)),
it is clear that
B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\operatorname{Im}(k),V\right)=\{0\}
. Thus,
\operatorname{Im}(k)\subset V^{\perp}
.
Since
k

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Im}(k)=\operatorname{dim}V^{\perp}
,

\operatorname{Im}(k)=V^{\perp}
.

Claim A5.

The following relation between
B
and
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
holds true:

(7)

B(x,y)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(k(x),y+u),\,\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ and any }\,u\in V.

To prove (
7
) we recall
first from (
6
) that

B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(a_{i},v_{j}\right)=\delta_{ij}

.
Now, using the definition of
k
(see (
3
))
and the fact that
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}

\mathfrak{g}

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(a_{i},k(y))=B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(a_{i},T(y)+B(a_{1},y)v_{1}+\cdots+B(a_{r},y)v_{r}\right)=B(a_{i},y),

.
Therefore,

(8)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(a,k(y))=B(a,y),\,\,\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{a}\text{ and }y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Now, for

(see (

By

\operatorname{Im}(T)\subset V^{\perp}
.
Then, for any

and

\mathfrak{g}

(9)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(T(x),k(y))=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(T(x),T(y))=B(T(x),y).

Combining (
8
) and (
9
), we conclude that

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(a+T(x),k(y))=B(a+T(x),y)
,
for any
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
a
in
\mathfrak{a}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\operatorname{Im}(T)
we deduce that
B(x,y)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,k(y))
for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
The symmetry of both
B
and
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
leads to
B(x,y)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(k(x),y)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,k(y))
.
Finally, from
Claim A4
we have
\operatorname{Im}(k)=V^{\perp}
.
Therefore,
B(x,y)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(k(x),y+u)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
u
in
V
.

Claim A6.

B_{\mathfrak{G}}
is invariant under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
.

Let
x,y,z
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v,w
be in
V
.
We shall prove first that
B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left([x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{G}},z+w\right)=B\left(\mu(x,y),z\right)
. In fact,

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left([x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{G}},z+w\right)

\displaystyle=\!\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}},z+w\right)\!=\!\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(k(\mu(x,y)),z+w\right)

\displaystyle=B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(k(\mu(x,y)),z\right)=B(\mu(x,y),z),

where use has been made of

V\subset C(\mathfrak{G})
,
k\left(\mu(x,y)\right)=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}

(see (
4
)), and
\operatorname{Im}(k)=V^{\perp}
,
together with (
7
).
A similar argument proves that
B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(x+u,[y+v,z+w]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)=B(x,\mu(y,z))
.
Finally, the
\mu
-invariance of
B
implies that
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
is invariant under

[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
. Therefore
\left(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)

is a quadratic Lie algebra and

observe that
B
is not the restriction of
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
to
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}
,

as
V
is isotropic.
∎

Within the context of
a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)

with

a centroid,
we have the following relationship between
Prop.
1.1

and
Thm.
1.2
.

1.3 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be the quadratic Lie algebra of

Prop.
1.1

and let
(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}})

be that of
Thm.
1.2
. Then,

(i)

\mathfrak{G}
is a central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
V

(ii)

For each
1\leq i\leq r
, let
D_{i}=\mu(a_{i},\,\cdot\,):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
where
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Im}(T)\oplus\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
.
If all the
D_{i}
’s are inner derivations of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
,
then the Hom-Lie bracket
\mu
satisfies the Jacobi identity.

(iii)

If
\mu
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity,
then
\operatorname{Ker}(T)
is equal to the center
C(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
x
and
y
be in
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
u
and
v
be in
V
.
By
Prop.
1.1
,
the Lie bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
in
\mathfrak{g}

is given by
[x,y]=T(\mu(x,y))
.
Now use (
3
), the
\mu
-invariance of
B

and (
4
) to get,

(10)

\begin{split}[\,x+u\,,\,&y+v\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}=k(\mu(x,y))\\
\,&=[x,y]+B\left(\mu(a_{1},x),y\right)v_{1}+\ldots+B\left(\mu(a_{r},x),y\right)v_{r}.\end{split}

For each
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, consider the linear map
\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)=\mu(x,\,\cdot\,):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
T
is a centroid for
(\mathfrak{g},\mu)
, it follows from
(
1
) that,

\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)\left([y,z]\right)=[\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)(y),z]+[y,\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)(z)],\ \ \text{ for all }z\in\mathfrak{g};

that is,
\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)
is a derivation of the Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
. Since
B
is invariant under
\mu
, it follows that

\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)
is actually an anti-self-adjoint derivation with respect to
B
;
ie,

B\left(\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(x)(y),z\right)=-B\left(y,\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(z)\right)
.
Now, let
D_{i}=\operatorname{ad}_{\mu}(a_{i})
, with
a_{i}
running through
the basis of
\mathfrak{a}
given in
Thm.
1.2
.
From (
10
),
the map
\theta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to V

\theta(x,y)=B\left(D_{1}(x),y\right)v_{1}+\cdots+B\left(D_{r}(x),y\right)v_{r}
,
gives rise to a skew-symmetric bilinear map satisfying,

(11)

\begin{split}\,[x+u,y+v]_{\mathfrak{G}}&=[x,y]+B\left(D_{1}(x),y\right)v_{1}+\cdots+B\left(D_{r}(x),y\right)v_{r}\\
\,&=[x,y]+\theta(x,y),\ \text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\text{ and }u,v\in V.\end{split}

That is,
\theta
is actually
the 2-cocycle of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
, with values in
V

that yields
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
thus proving the statement.

(ii)

Since
T
is a centroid, (
1
) implies that,

(12)

\mu(T(x),\mu(y,z))+\mu(y,\mu(z,T(x)))+\mu(z,\mu(T(x),y))=0,

for all

,
y
and

in
\mathfrak{g}
. That is, the Jacobi identity holds true for

\mu
if at least one of the elements involved in the cyclic sum belongs to
\operatorname{Im}(T)
.
As
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Im}(T)\oplus\mathfrak{a}
, it remains to verify whether or not the Jacobi identity also
holds true for the elements in
\mathfrak{a}
.
Observe that if
each
D_{i}=\mu(a_{i},\,\cdot\,)
is an inner derivation of the Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
then, for each
1\leq i\leq r
, there exists
an element
b_{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}
such that
D_{i}(x)=[b_{i},x]
,
which is equivalent to
\mu(a_{i},x)=\mu(T(b_{i}),x)

in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
a
and

be in
\mathfrak{a}
. Since

is a centroid, we get,

(13)

\displaystyle\mu(a_{i},\mu(a,a^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mu(T(b_{i}),\mu(a,a^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\mu(a,\mu(a^{\prime},a_{i}))

\displaystyle=\mu(a,\mu(a^{\prime},T(b_{i}))),\quad\ \text{and,}

\displaystyle\mu(a^{\prime},\mu(a_{i},a))

\displaystyle=\mu(a^{\prime},\mu(T(b_{i}),a)),\quad\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq r.

We saw before if at least one of the elements involved in the cyclic sum lies
in
\operatorname{Im}(T)
, then the Jacobi identity holds true for
\mu
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}
is generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
,
(
12
) and (

) imply that,

\mu(a,\mu(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}))+\mu(a^{\prime},\mu(a^{\prime\prime},a)+\mu(a^{\prime\prime},\mu(a,a^{\prime}))=0,\,\text{ for all }a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{a}.

Therefore,
\mu
satisfies the Jacobi identity.

(iii)

It follows from
Thm.
1.2
that
V

is an isotropic ideal of the quadratic Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}})
which, by construction,
is a central extension of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot\,,\cdot\,])
by
V

with cocycle
\theta(x,y)=B(D_{1}(x),y)v_{1}+\ldots+B(D_{r}(x),y)v_{r}
.
By hypothesis, if
\mu
does not satisfy the Jacobi identity,
it follows from
(ii)
that at least one of the derivations
D_{i}
is not inner.

It was proved in
[
4
]
, (see
Lemma 2.2
and
Prop. 2.5.(ii)
there),
that under these conditions, for each
i
there exists an
x_{i}
in
\mathfrak{g}

such that,
D^{\prime}_{i}=D_{i}+[x_{i},\,\cdot\,]
, and

\displaystyle{\cap_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{Ker}(D^{\prime}_{i})}=\{0\}
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Span}\{a_{1}+T(x_{1}),\ldots,a_{r}+T(x_{r})\}
,
then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\oplus\operatorname{Im}(T)=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\operatorname{Im}(T)

and
D^{\prime}_{i}=\mu(a_{i},\,\cdot\,)+\mu(T(x_{i}),\,\cdot\,)=D_{i}+[x_{i},\,\cdot\,]
, for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Thus, the results obtained so far are valid for both

\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
.
In addition, if the 2-cocylce
\theta^{\prime}
arising from

D^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,D^{\prime}_{r}
differs from
\theta

by a coboundary, then the central extensions in
\mathfrak{G}

associated to
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
, are isomorphic.
Therefore we might as well assume that
\displaystyle{\cap_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{Ker}(D_{i})}=\{0\}
.
Then, by (
11
),
the center of
(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}})
is,

C(\mathfrak{G})=C(\mathfrak{g})\cap\left\{\left(\cap_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{Ker}(D_{i})\right)\oplus V\right\}.

Whence,
C(\mathfrak{G})=V
and
V^{\perp}=[\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]_{\mathfrak{G}}
.
By
Claim A4
,
k
satisfies
\operatorname{Im}(k)=V^{\perp}

and
k|_{\mathfrak{g}}=T
(see (
3
)).
If
\pi:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the canonical projection of the central extension,
we have,
\pi\left([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. It then follows that,

\operatorname{Im}(T)=\pi(\operatorname{Im}(k))=\pi(V^{\perp})=\pi\left([\mathfrak{G},\mathfrak{G}]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}].

Finally, since

is

-self-adjoint, it follows that

.
∎

The next result shows how to extend the
Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)
to a Hom-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)
.

1.4 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
with
T
a centroid
and let
(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}})

be the Lie algebra in

Thm.
1.2
.
Let
k:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
be the map defined in (
3
).
Then, the vector space
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V

has a Hom-Lie algebra structure

\left(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L\right)
, where
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x+u,y+v)=\mu(x,y)
, with,

(14)

L(x+u)=k(x),\,\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\,\text{ and }u,v\in V,

(15)

\,\text{satisfying,}\ \ B_{\mathfrak{G}}(L(x+u),y+v)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x+u,L(y+v))=B(x,y),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and

in
V
. Moreover,
the projection map

\pi:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
of the central extension,

(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)\twoheadrightarrow(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)

Proof.

Let
x,y,z
be in
\mathfrak{g}
and let
u,v,w
be in
V
.
From the definition of
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}
we deduce that
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\{0\}

\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(L(x+u),\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(y+v,z+w)\right)=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(k(x),\mu(y,z)\right)=\mu(T(x),\mu(y,z)),

and this implies that the triple
\left(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L\right)

Now (
14
) says that

L|_{\mathfrak{g}}=k
and
V\subset\operatorname{Ker}L
.
Then, from

Claim A5
and (
7
)
in the proof of
Thm.
1.2

B(x,y)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}(k(x),y+v)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}(L(x),y+v)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}(L(x+u),y+v)

Now (
15
) follows
using the same arguments and the symmetry of

.

Recall that

a morphism

(\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mu_{1},T_{1})\to(\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mu_{2},T_{2})

between two Hom-Lie algebras

is a linear map
\psi:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}

\psi(\mu_{1}(x,y))=\mu_{2}(\psi(x),\psi(y))

x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}

and
\psi\circ T_{1}=T_{2}\circ\psi

Now,
\pi(x+v)=x

in
\mathfrak{g}
and
v
in
V
.
Since,
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x+u,y+v)=\mu(x,y)

for all

\mathfrak{g}
and

in
V
.
Therefore,
\pi:(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)\to(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)

Since
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mathfrak{G},V)=\{0\}
and

we may say that

(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)

is a central extension of

(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)

∎

1.5 Remark
.

We now know from

Under the hypotheses of

(i)

We shall adhere ourselves to the following notation.

. In what follows we shall use the already defined

and

on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{G}
, respectively.

(i)
Now, let
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{G}

(16)

where the dual map

.
It follows from

and the non-degeneracy of

and

(Q1)

(Q2)

and

.

(Q3)

\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Ker}(h)={\mathfrak{g}}^{\perp}
.

(Q4)

\mathfrak{G}=\operatorname{Ker}(h)\oplus\operatorname{Im}(k)
.

We know from (
4
)
that
k(\mu(x,y))=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}
. Then
(Q1)
implies that,

(17)

\mu(x,y)=h([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}),\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

On the other hand, from
(Q2)
one obtains,

(18)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(h(x+u),y+v)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x+u,h(y+v)),

for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and all
u,v
in
V
.
We can now prove the first part of
(i)
; that is,
if

is

-invariant, then the 2-cocycle

in (

be in
\mathfrak{g}
and

be in
\mathfrak{G}

The

(

(ii)
We now prove that,

\mathfrak{g}

.

(

(

\displaystyle k(\mu(x,y))-\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(k(x),y)

Remark.

Our next result provides a method to generate quadratic
Hom-Lie algebras on an underlying vector space of the
form
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, when a Hom-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},\nu,S)
has been given with a centroid

S
. In fact, we shall close this section by applying
such a method to a
6
-dimensional
\mathfrak{h}
,
which is in turn interesting in itself, as its
Hom-Lie product
\nu:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}

is not a Lie bracket.

1.7 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\nu,S)
be a Hom-Lie algebra with
S

a centroid for
\nu
.
Let
\nu^{\ast}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}^{\ast})
be the linear map for which

\nu^{\ast}(x)(\alpha)\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, for any
x\in\mathfrak{h}
and any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
,
is the linear map
y\mapsto-\alpha(\nu(x,y))
, for any
y
in
\mathfrak{h}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and define
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by,

\mu(x+\alpha,y+\beta)=\nu(x,y)+\nu^{\ast}(x)(\beta)-\nu^{\ast}(y)(\alpha).

Let
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be given by,
T(x+\alpha)=S(x)+S^{\ast}(\alpha)
,
and let
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to{\mathbb{F}}
be defined by

B(x+\alpha,y+\beta)=\alpha(y)+\beta(x)
.
Then,
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra,
having
T
in its centroid.

Proof.

We shall prove first
that
T
is a centroid.
For
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, we have,
on the one hand,
T(\mu(x,\alpha))(y)=-\alpha(\nu(x,S(y)))
.
On the other hand, it is clear that,

\mu(T(x),\alpha)(y)=-\alpha(\nu(S(x),y)),\ \,\text{and}\ \,\mu(x,T(\alpha))(y)=-\alpha(S(\nu(x,y))).

Whence,
T(\mu(x,\alpha))=\mu(T(x),\alpha)=\mu(x,T(\alpha))
.

We shall now prove that the Hom-Lie Jacobi identity
(
1
) holds true for
\mu
and
T
.
Take
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
.
Using the fact that
T
is a centroid,
one obtains,

\displaystyle\mu(T(x),\mu(y,\alpha))(z)+\mu(T(y),\mu(\alpha,x))(z)+\mu(T(\alpha),\mu(x,y))(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left((\nu(S(y),\nu(x,z))+(\nu(S(x),\nu(y,z))+(\nu(S(z),\nu(y,x))\right)=0.

Thus,
\mu(T(x),\mu(y,\alpha))+\mu(T(y),\mu(\alpha,x))+\mu(T(\alpha),\mu(x,y))=0
.
The fact that
T
is self-adjoint for the given
B

follows from
B(S(x),\alpha)=\alpha(S(x))=S^{\ast}(\alpha)(x)=B(x,S^{\ast}(\alpha))
,
since
T(x)=S(x)
and
T(\alpha)=S^{\ast}(\alpha)
.
∎

An example with a
6
-dimensional Hom-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},\nu,S)
with
\nu
-equivariant
S

and
\nu
not a Lie bracket.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}

with the skew-symmetric bilinear product
\nu:\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
defined by

\nu(x_{1},x_{2})\!=\!\nu(y_{3},x_{1})\!=\!\nu(x_{3},y_{1})\!=\!y_{2}
,

\nu(x_{3},x_{1})\!=\!y_{1}
, and all other products on the basis being equal to zero.
Let
S:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h}
be the linear map defined by,

S(x_{1})=y_{1},\ S(x_{2})=y_{2},\ S(x_{3})=y_{3},\,S(y_{1})=y_{2},\ S(y_{2})=S(y_{3})=0.

Observe that both,
\nu(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})
and
\operatorname{Im}(S)
, lie in

\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}
, and

\nu(y_{j},y_{k})=0
, for all
1\leq j,k\leq 3
.
It is easy to check that
(\mathfrak{h},\nu,S)
is a 6-dimensional Hom-Lie algebra,
and that
S
is
in the centroid.
Also observe that
S
is nilpotent with
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(S)=3
. In addition,

\nu(x_{1},\nu(x_{2},x_{3}))+\nu(x_{2},\nu(x_{3},x_{1}))+\nu(x_{3},\nu(x_{1},x_{2}))=-y_{2}\neq 0,

which proves that
\nu
is not a Lie bracket.
Applying
Prop.
1.7
, we get a 12-dimensional
quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast},\mu,T,B)
,
where
T
is
in the centroid
and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=6
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\}
,
where
\alpha_{i}(x_{j})=\beta_{i}(y_{j})=\delta_{ij}
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq 3
.
Write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
.
From the prescription of
Prop.
1.7

we obtain the Hom-Lie product
\mu
on
\mathfrak{g}
given by,

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1},x_{2})=\mu(y_{3},x_{1})=\mu(x_{3},y_{1})=y_{2},\,\,\,\mu(x_{2},x_{3})=y_{3},\,\,\,\mu(x_{3},x_{1})=y_{1},

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1},\beta_{2})=-\alpha_{2}+\beta_{3},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mu(x_{2},\beta_{3})=\mu(\beta_{1},x_{1})=\mu(y_{1},\beta_{2})=-\alpha_{3},

\displaystyle\mu(x_{2},\beta_{2})=\mu(\beta_{1},x_{3})=\mu(\beta_{2},y_{3})=\alpha_{1},\,\,\,\,\mu(x_{3},\beta_{2})=\beta_{1},\,\,\,\mu(x_{3},\beta_{3})=\alpha_{2},

and the twist map
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
given by,

\displaystyle T(x_{1})=y_{1},\,\,\,T(x_{2})=y_{2},\,\,\,T(x_{3})=y_{3},\,\,\,T(y_{1})=y_{2},\,\,\,T(y_{2})=T(y_{3})=0,

\displaystyle T(\beta_{1})=\alpha_{1},\,\,\,T(\beta_{2})=\alpha_{2}+\beta_{1},\,\,\,T(\beta_{3})=\alpha_{3}.

It is easy to determine the Lie algebra bracket

[x,y]=T(\mu(x,y))
for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. In fact,
the only non-trivial brackets are:

[x_{3},x_{1}]=y_{2}
,
[x_{1},\beta_{2}]=\alpha_{3}
and
[x_{3},\beta_{2}]=\alpha_{1}
.
Thus
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
is 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra
with

C(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{Span}\{x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\beta_{1},\beta_{3}\}
,
and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\operatorname{Span}\{y_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3}\}
.

2.
Hom-Lie algebras and algebras having no non-trivial two-sided ideals

The main result of this section is that we can
recover the
Hom-Lie algebra structure
in
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V

given in
Prop.
1.4
, from an algebra
\mathcal{A}

having no non-trivial two-sided ideals.
In order to accomplish this goal, we shall prove first
the existence of a bilinear product
(x,y)\mapsto xy
in
\mathfrak{G}
that
realizes
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}
in the form
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)=xy-yx
.
This product will help us to construct the algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

2.1 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
with
T
in the centroid and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)>0
.
Choose a vector space
V
with
\operatorname{dim}V=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)

to produce the
quadratic Lie algebra
\left(\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)

of
Thm.
1.2
, and let
(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)

be the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L)
of
Prop.
1.4
.
There is a bilinear product
\mathfrak{G}\times\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}
,
denoted by
(x,y)\mapsto xy
(for all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{G}
),
such that,

(i)

\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)=xy-yx
.

(ii)

2\,xy=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)-[h(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}}+[x,h(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
with
h:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
as in (
16
),

(iii)

x^{2}=0
for all
x
in
\operatorname{Ker}(h)\cup\operatorname{Im}(L)
,
and
\mathfrak{G}=\operatorname{Ker}(h)\oplus\operatorname{Im}(L)
.

Proof.

Define the bilinear map
(x,y)\mapsto xy\in\mathfrak{G}
by means of,

(20)

2B_{\mathfrak{G}}(xy,z)\!=\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y),z\right)\!+\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(z,x),y\right)\!+\!B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(z,y),z\right),

for all
x,y
and
z
in
\mathfrak{G}
. If
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
is invariant under

\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}
, then
2\,x\,y=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)
.
By
Cor.
1.6
.(i)
, this is the case
only if the 2-cocycle
\theta
is zero.
Now observe that
(i)
follows from (
20
).
To prove

(ii)

take
x,y
and
z
in
\mathfrak{G}
.
The properties of
h
given in (
17
) and (
18
),
and the invariance of
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
under
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
lead to the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle 2\,B_{\mathfrak{G}}(xy,z)=

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y),z)+B_{\mathfrak{G}}(h([z,x]_{\mathfrak{G}}),y)+B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(h([z,y]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right),x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y),z)+B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(z,[x,h(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)+B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(z,[y,h(x)]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{\mathfrak{G}}\left(\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)+[x,h(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}-[h(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}},z\right),

Therefore,

(21)

2xy\!=\!\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)\!+\![x,h(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}\!-\![h(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}},\,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{G}.

Finally, to prove
(iii)
,
we shall first show the following:

Claim B1.

2L(x+u)L(y+v)\!\!=\!\!T([x,y])
, for all
x,\!y
in
\mathfrak{g}
and
u,\!v
in
\!V\!
.

To prove this claim we shall use the fact that
L|_{\mathfrak{g}}=k
(see (
14
)),
together with
h\circ k=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
(see
(Q1)
),
Claim A1
, and
the fact that
h([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})=\mu(x,y)
(see (
17
)).
Thus, by (
21
) we get,

(22)

\displaystyle 2\,L(x+u)L(y+v)

\displaystyle=2k(x)k(y)

\displaystyle=h\left([k(x),k(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)+[k(x),y]_{\mathfrak{G}}-[x,k(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle=h\left([k(x),T(y)]_{\mathfrak{G}}\right)=h\left(k(\mu(T(x),T(y))\right)

\displaystyle=\mu(T(x),T(y)).

Since
T
is a centroid and
\mu(T(x),y)=[x,y]
,
it follows from the right hand side of (

) that,
\mu(T(x),T(y))=T([x,y])
,
thus proving
Claim B1.

In particular,
L(x+u)^{2}=0
.

To conclude the proof, we now only have to prove the following:

Claim B2.

\,2\,aa^{\prime}=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(a,a^{\prime})
, for all
a,a^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{a}
.

Let
a
and
a^{\prime}
be in
\mathfrak{a}=\operatorname{Ker}(h)
(see
(Q3)
).
From (
21
) we get,

2aa^{\prime}=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(a,a^{\prime})
and

.
Therefore,
x^{2}=0
for all
x
in
\operatorname{Im}(L)\cup\operatorname{Ker}(h)
.
∎

2.2 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
be a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra such that
T

is a centroid and
\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{Ker}(T))=r>0
. Let
(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}})

be the quadratic Lie algebra of
Thm.
1.2

and let
(x,y)\mapsto xy
be the product in
\mathfrak{G}
of
Prop.
2.1
.

(i)
Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{F}\times\mathfrak{G}

and define the following multiplication in
\mathcal{A}

(\xi,x)(\eta,y)=(\,\xi\,\eta+B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)\,,\,\xi\,y+\eta\,x+x\,y\,),

and
\eta
in
\mathbb{F}
and all

and

\mathfrak{G}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is a non-abelian, and in general, a non-associative
algebra with unit element

(ii)

If
\mu
is not a Lie product and
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]=T\circ\mu)

\mathcal{A}
has no non-trivial two-sided ideals.
Furthermore, if
\left(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]\right)
is nilpotent then
\mathcal{A}
is simple.

(iii)
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}/\mathbb{F}\,1_{\mathcal{A}}

and
\mu^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be defined by,

\mu^{\prime}\left((\xi,x)+\mathbb{F}1_{\mathcal{A}},(\eta,y)+\mathbb{F}1_{\mathcal{A}}\right)=(\xi,x)(\eta,y)-(\eta,y)(\xi,x)+\mathbb{F}\,1_{\mathcal{A}},

for all
\xi
and
\eta
in
\mathbb{F}
and all
x
and
y
in
\mathfrak{G}
.
Let

.
Then,
\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},T^{\prime}\right)

is a Hom-Lie algebra isomorphic to

\left(\mathfrak{G},\mu_{\mathfrak{G}},L\right)
of
Prop.
1.4
.

Proof.

(i)

We define equality, addition and multiplication by scalars
in
\mathcal{A}
in the obvious manner.
Then
(1,0)
is the unit element
1_{\mathcal{A}}
of
\mathcal{A}
.
Observe that the conditions for the product in
\mathcal{A}

,
y
and

in
\mathfrak{G}
:

(i)

x\,(y\,z)=(x\,y)\,z
;

(ii)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x\,y,z)=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y\,z)
; and

(iii)

B_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)\,z=B_{\mathfrak{G}}(z,x)\,y
.
Similarly, for
\mathcal{A}
to be abelian, it would have to be true
that
\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(x,y)=\mu_{\mathfrak{G}}(y,x)
for all

and
y
in
\mathfrak{G}
;

(ii)
We shall prove that any non-trivial two-sided ideal in

contains the unit element

.
We proceed by proving the following statements:

Claim C1.

xL(y)

\operatorname{Im}(L)

\mathfrak{G}

It follows from

3.
Invariant metrics on central extensions

To round up our results, we conclude as follows.
We have started
with a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
having

T
in the centroid.
Besides, we have assumed right from the start that
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)>0
.
Then, we showed how to produce a quadratic Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
, using actually the same
B

and taking a vector space
V
with
\operatorname{dim}V=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)

we obtained a central extension
(\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}})

having an invariant metric
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
under which

V
is isotropic. Now, our final result shows how from the
quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}},B_{\mathfrak{G}})

which is a central extension of
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])

and with
V
isotropic for its metric
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
,
one may produce a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)

having
T
in its centroid
and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=\operatorname{dim}V
.
In the course of the proof we shall use some results found in

[
4
]
which actually inspired us to address the problem
of the quadratic Hom-Lie algebras in the way we have presented it
in this work.

3.1 Theorem
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,],B)
be a quadratic Lie algebra
and let
V
be an
r
-dimensional vector space.
Let
(\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}})
be a central extension of

(\mathfrak{g},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,])
by
V
. If
(\mathfrak{G},[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{G}})

admits an invariant metric
B_{\mathfrak{G}}
where
V
is isotropic,
then there exists a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)

such that
[x,y]=T(\mu(x,y))=\mu(T(x),y)
, for all
x,y
in
\mathfrak{g}

and
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=r
.

Proof.

Let
h=B^{\sharp}\circ\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}\circ B_{\mathfrak{G}}^{\flat}:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
and
k=B^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{G}}\circ\pi^{\ast}\circ B^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
,
where
\pi:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the canonical projection,
and
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{G}
the inclusion map.
For each
x
in
\mathfrak{G}
, let
\rho(x):\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
be defined by

\rho(x)(y)=h([x,y]_{\mathfrak{G}})
, for all
y
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{G}
, we may now
define
\mu:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by
\mu(x,y)=\rho(x)(y)
,
and
T:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by
T=\pi\circ k
.

Lemma 2.2
of
[
4
]
, proves that

T\circ\rho(x)=\rho(x)\circ T=\operatorname{ad}(x)
,
for each
x
in
\mathfrak{g}
, which implies that

T(\mu(x,y))=\mu(T(x),y)=[x,y]
; that is,

T
is a centroid.
Moreover,
Lemma 2.2 (vii)
of
[
4
]
proves that

\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\circ T\circ\rho(y)-\rho(y)\circ T\circ\rho(x)
,
which is equivalent to,
\mu(T(\mu(x,y)),z)=\mu(T(x),\mu(y,z))+\mu(T(y),\mu(z,x))
.
Since
T
is a centroid,
the last expression can be rewritten as,

\mu(T(x),\mu(y,z))+\mu(T(y),\mu(z,x))+\mu(T(z),\mu(x,y))=0.

Whence
(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T)
is a Hom-Lie algebra
with
T
in its centroid.
In addition,
Lemma 2.2
of
[
4
]
also
shows
T
is
B
-self-adjoint and that
\rho
actually defines
a map
\rho:\mathfrak{G}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
, making
\rho(x)
anti-self-adjoint
with respecto to
B
for each
x
in
\mathfrak{G}
. Therefore,

B(\mu(x,y),z)=B(\rho(x)(y),z)=-B(y,\rho(x)(z))=-B(y,\mu(x,z))
,
which proves that
B
is
\mu
-invariant and that

(\mathfrak{g},\mu,T,B)
is a quadratic Hom-Lie algebra.
Finally, since
V
is isotropic,
Lemma 2.7 (ii)
of
[
4
]

proves that
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ker}(T)=\operatorname{dim}V
.
∎

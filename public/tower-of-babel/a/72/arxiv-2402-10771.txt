Generalizing Geometric Nonwindowed Scattering Transforms on Compact Riemannian Manifolds
Albert Chua, Yang Yang

Abstract
Let ℳ \mathcal{M} be a compact, smooth, n n -dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary. In this paper, we generalize nonwindowed geometric scattering transforms, which we formulate as 𝐋 q ​ ( ℳ ) \mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M}) norms of a cascade of geometric wavelet transforms and modulus operators. We then provide weighted measures for these operators, prove that these operators are well-defined under specific conditions on the manifold, invariant to the action of isometries, and stable to diffeomorphisms for λ \lambda -bandlimited functions.

1.1
Invariance and Stability

In machine learning tasks, it is often necessary for a representation to have some degree of invariance with respect to the action of a Lie group. For tasks involving manifolds, one may like to have local isometry invariance. More formally, let
V_{\xi}f(x)=f(\xi^{-1}x)
for
\xi\in\text{Isom}(\mathcal{M})
and consider a representation
\Phi:\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\mathcal{B}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}
are Banach Spaces. It would be desirable to have a representation such that

\|\Phi f-\Phi V_{\xi}f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\leq 2^{-dJ}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}},

where
J
controls the degree of invariance. A simple choice is to use an averaging filter, but this potentially leads to the loss of information that can be crucial for the task.

For other tasks, such as manifold classification, a fully rigid representation may be required, and full isometry invariance is desirable. That is to say, we have

\|\Phi f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}=\|\Phi V_{\xi}f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}.

In addition to invariance, it is necessary for a representation to also have stability properties. Instead of considering an isometry, consider
\xi\in\text{Diff}(\mathcal{M})
and think of
V_{\xi}f
as a small deformation of
f
. We want

\|\Phi f-\Phi V_{\xi}f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\leq 2^{-dJ}K(\xi)\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}},

where
K(\xi)\to 0
as
\|\xi\|_{\infty}\to 0
; this is to ensure that small deformations of an input do not lead to large changes in the representation.

2
A Review of the Geometric Scattering Transform on Manifolds

2.1
Spectral Filters and the Geometric Wavelet Transform

The convolution of
f,g\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is usually defined in space as

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(y)g(x-y)\,dy.

However, for a general manifold, even under the conditions we have prescribed, a notation of translation does not necessarily exist. Instead, one can consider a spectral definition of convolution via the spectral decomposition of
-\Delta
. Denote
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Because our manifold is compact, it is well known that
-\Delta
has a discrete spectrum, and we can order the eigenvalues in increasing order and denote them as
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. We will denote the corresponding eigenfunctions as
\{e_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, which form an orthonormal basis for
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
.

Suppose
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Since the set of functions
\{e_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
forms a basis in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
, we decompose

f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\langle f,e_{n}\rangle e_{n}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(y)\overline{e_{n}(y)}\,d\mu(y)\right)e_{n}(x),

(1)

which is similar to a Fourier series. Since
e_{n}(y)
is a replacement for a Fourier mode, it is natural to let

\hat{f}(n)=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(y)\overline{e_{n}(y)}\,d\mu(y)

(2)

and define convolution on
\mathcal{M}
between functions
f,h\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
as

f\ast h(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\hat{f}(n)\hat{h}(n)e_{n}(x).

(3)

Defining the operator
T_{h}f(x):=f\ast h(x)
, it is easy to verify that the kernel for
T_{h}
is given by

\tilde{K}_{h}(x,y):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\hat{h}(n)e_{n}(x)\overline{e_{n}(y)}.

(4)

Similar to how convolution commutes with translations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is important for convolution on
\mathcal{M}
to be equivariant to a group action on
\mathcal{M}
. The authors of
[
36
]
construct an operator by convolving with functions that commute with isometries since the the geometry of
\mathcal{M}
should be preserved by a representation.

To accomplish this goal, we use a similar definition for spectral filters. A filter
h\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
is a spectral filter if
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{\ell}
implies
\hat{h}(k)=\hat{h}(\ell)
. One can prove that there exists
H:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

H(\lambda_{n})=\hat{h}(n),\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Let
G:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be nonnegative and decreasing with
G(0)>0
. A low-pass spectral filter
\phi
is given in frequency as
\hat{\phi}(n):=G(\lambda_{n})
and its dilation at scale
2^{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
is
\hat{\phi}_{j}(n):=G(2^{j}\lambda_{n})
.

Using the set of low pass filters,
\{\hat{\phi}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we define wavelets by

\hat{\psi}_{j}(n):=[|\hat{\phi}_{j-1}(n)|^{2}-|\hat{\phi}_{j}(n)|^{2}]^{1/2}=[|G(2^{j-1}n)|^{2}-|G(2^{j}n)|^{2}]^{1/2},

(5)

which are diffusion wavelets
[
40
]
.

Fix
J\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define the operators

\displaystyle A_{J}f

\displaystyle:=f\ast\phi_{J},

\displaystyle\Psi_{j}f

\displaystyle:=f\ast\psi_{j},\qquad j\leq J.

The windowed geometric wavelet transform is given by

W_{J}f:=\{A_{J}f,\Psi_{j}f:\quad j\leq J\}

(6)

and the nonwindowed geometric wavelet transform is given by

Wf:=\{\Psi_{j}f:\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(7)

We have the following theorem, which provides a condition for when our wavelet frame is a nonexpansive frame.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be nonnegative, decreasing, and continuous with
0<G(0)=C
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}G(x)=0
, and
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a set of wavelets generated by using the low pass filter
\hat{\phi}(k)=G(\lambda_{k})
in Equation
5
. Then we have

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=C^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

(8)

Proof.

For fixed
J>1
, we telescope to get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-J}^{J}|\hat{\psi}_{j}(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-J}^{J}\left[|G(2^{j-1}\lambda_{k})|^{2}-|G(2^{j}\lambda_{k})|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=|G(2^{-J-1}\lambda_{k})|^{2}-|G(2^{J}\lambda_{k})|^{2}.

Since
\lim_{J\to\infty}|G(2^{-J-1}\lambda_{k})|^{2}
and
\lim_{J\to\infty}|G(2^{J}\lambda_{k})|^{2}
both exist, it follows that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\psi}_{j}(k)|^{2}=\lim_{J\to\infty}|G(2^{-J-1}\lambda_{k})|^{2}-\lim_{J\to\infty}|G(2^{J}\lambda_{k})|^{2}=C^{2}.

We can write

\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\hat{\psi}_{j}(k)|^{2}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}|\hat{\psi}_{j}(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\psi}_{j}(k)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=C^{2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

∎

2.2
The Geometric Scattering Transform

In an analogous manner to the Euclidean definition of the scattering transform, it is valuable to find a representation that meaningfully encodes high frequency information of a signal
f
. Define the propagator as

U[j]f:=|\Psi_{j}f|\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},

(9)

which is convolution of a wavelet and applying a nonlinearity; we can also define the windowed propagator as

U_{J}[j]f:=|\Psi_{j}f|\qquad\forall j\leq J.

(10)

Similar to scattering transforms on Euclidean space, one can apply a cascade of convolutions and modulus operators repeatedly. In particular, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The
m
-layer propagator is defined as

U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f:=U[j_{m}]\cdots U[j_{1}]f=|||f\ast\psi_{j_{1}}|\ast\psi_{j_{2}}|\cdots\ast\psi_{j_{m}}|

(11)

and the
m
-layer windowed propagator is defined as

U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f:=U[j_{m}]\cdots U[j_{1}]f=|||f\ast\psi_{j_{1}}|\ast\psi_{j_{2}}|\cdots\ast\psi_{j_{m}}|,\qquad j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}\leq J

(12)

with
U[\emptyset]f=f
and
U_{J}[\emptyset]f=f.
To aggregate low frequency information and get local isometry invariance, one can apply a low pass filter in a manner similar to pooling to each windowed propagator to get windowed scattering coefficients:

S_{J}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]=A_{J}U_{J}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f=U_{J}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\ast\phi_{J},

where we defined
S_{J}[\emptyset]f=f\ast\phi_{J}
. The windowed geometric scattering transform is given by

S_{J}f=\{S_{j}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f:\,m\geq 0,\quad j_{i}\leq J\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq m\}.

(13)

The authors of
[
36
]
were able to prove that this windowed scattering operator was nonexpansive, invariant to isometries up to the scale of the low pass filter, and stable to diffeomporhisms under mild assumptions.

In addition, the authors consider a nonwindowed scattering transform, which removes the low pass filtering. For applications such as manifold classification, one desires full isometry invariance instead of isometry invariance up to scale
2^{J}
. We see that

\lim_{J\to\infty}S[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f=\text{vol}(\mathcal{M})^{-1/2}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\|_{1}.

(14)

As a proxy, one can consider

\overline{S}f[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]=\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\|_{1},

(15)

This motivates
[
36
]
defining the nonwindowed geometric scattering transform as

\overline{S}f=\{\overline{S}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f:\,m\geq 0,\quad j_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq m\}.

(16)

Instead of considering
\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})
norms of
m
-layer propagators, we can instead consider
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})
norms of
m
-layer propagators for
q\in(1,2]
, which we define as
m
-layer
q
-nonwindowed geometric scattering coefficients (which are also referred to as scattering moments in other works):

\overline{S}_{q}^{m}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f=\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}\qquad\forall(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},

(17)

which has seen application in quantum chemistry
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and for point cloud data
[
38
]
. As shorthand notation, we will use the following notation for one layer coefficients:

\overline{S}_{q}[j]f=\|U[j]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(18)

To measure stability, invariance, and equivariance, we define the following norm for
q
-nonwindowed geometric scattering coefficients:

\|\overline{S}_{q}^{m}f\|_{\mathbf{\ell}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}^{q}:=\left(\sum_{j_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\ldots\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\overline{S}_{q}^{m}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f|^{2}\right)^{q/2},

(19)

which follows the definition in
[
37
]
. Since many of the results in
[
37
]
rely on bounds of Littlewood Paley
g
-functions, we will provide extensions of these results to compact manifolds, with some extra restrictions added.

3
Generalization of the Littlewood Paley
g
-function

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space, suppose that
g:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{B}
, and
x\to\|g(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}
is measurable with respect to the measure induced by the Riemannian volume. Define
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{M})
for
1\leq p<\infty
to be

\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{M})}^{p}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\|g(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,.

Also, for
1\leq p<\infty
, define

\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{\delta>0}\delta\cdot\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|g(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\delta\})^{1/p}\,,

It is clear that

\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathcal{B}}^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{M})}\,.

In particular, let our Banach space be
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and consider the operator
\vec{T}f:\mathcal{M}\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
given by

\vec{T}(f)(x):=\{T_{j}f(x)\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}=\left\{\int_{\mathcal{M}}K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}.

with kernel given by
\vec{K}=\{K_{2^{-j/2}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
associated to the wavelets
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
generated using a low pass filter
G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
satisfying the conditions satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1
for Equation
5
. This can be seen as an extension of the Littlewood Paley
g
-function for a compact Riemannian manifold since

\|\vec{T}(f)(x)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|f\ast\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

From Theorem
1
,

\|\vec{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}.

Our goal is to extend this operator and prove that for all
q\in(1,2)
, there exists
C_{q}
such that

\|\vec{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{q}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}.

Before providing any proofs, we will state preliminary lemmas that will be vital to our approach. The first few lemmas concern the kernel of our convolution operator.

Lemma 2

(
[
41
]
)
.

Suppose that
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, the space of Schwartz functions restricted to
\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
F(0)=0
. For the kernel

K_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F(t^{2}\lambda_{n})e_{n}(x)\overline{e_{n}(y)},

the following pointwise bound holds for some

and for all

and all

|K_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C_{n}t^{-n}}{\left(1+\tfrac{r(x,y)}{t}\right)^{n+1}},

We remind the reader that
n
is defined as the dimension of
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open cover for
\mathcal{M}
. A number

if and only if for all

B(x,\gamma)\subset\mathcal{U}_{x}

B(x,r_{0}):=\{y\in X\,:\,r(x,y)<r_{0}\}.

(20)

Lemma 3

(
[
42

)

\mathcal{M}

there exists a chart

c_{1}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,I}\left(\min_{x\in\overline{P}_{i}}\underline{\Lambda}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

∎

For the rest of this paper, we fix collections
\{P_{i}\}
,
\{V_{i}\}
,
\{\varphi_{i}\}
, and constants
\delta,c_{1},c_{2}
from the previous Lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
r(y,z)<\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}r(x,y),\delta\right\}
so that
y
and
z
lie on the same ball of the covering. Assume that there exist
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
from Lemma
3
with
c_{1}c_{2}<2.
Then there exists a constant
C_{\delta}
such that

|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,z)|\leq C_{\delta}\frac{r(y,z)t^{-n-1}}{\left(1+\frac{r(x,y)}{t}\right)^{n+1}}.

Proof.

Using the proof of Theorem 5.5 in
[
42
]
, for each
x\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists a point
w_{x}
on the segment connecting
y
to
z
such that

|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,z)|\leq C_{1}\frac{r(y,z)t^{-n-1}}{\left(1+\frac{r(x,w_{x})}{t}\right)^{n+1}}.

Now notice that triangle inequality implies that

r(x,y)\leq r(x,w_{x})+r(y,w_{x}).

By Lemma
3
, since
w_{x}
lies on the line segment between
y
and
z
, we see that

r(y,w_{x})\leq c_{2}|y-w_{x}|\leq c_{2}|y-z|\leq c_{1}c_{2}r(y,z).

It follows that

r(x,y)-c_{1}c_{2}r(y,z)\leq r(x,w_{x}).

Since
c_{1}c_{2}<2
and
r(x,y)\geq 2r(y,z)
, we see that

r(x,y)-\frac{c_{1}c_{2}}{2}r(x,y)\leq r(x,w_{x})

so that

for some constant

independent of

|K_{t}(x,y)-K_{t}(x,z)|\leq C_{\delta}\frac{r(y,z)t^{-n-1}}{\left(1+\frac{r(x,y)}{t}\right)^{n+1}}.

∎

Now we provide the necessary tools from classical harmonic analysis for extension. The idea is similar to the proof in the Euclidean case; we wish to prove a weak-type
(1,1)
bound and extend by interpolation. Let
X
be a set,
\beta
a quasimetric, and
\mu
a measure on
X
such that

0<\mu(B(x,r))<\infty
for all
x\in X
and
r>0
. We say that a space
(X,\beta,\mu)
is of homogeneous type, though often with the quasi-metric and measure omitted when implied, if for all
x\in X
and
r>0
there exists a constant
C_{D}
such that

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{D}\mu(B(x,r)),

(21)

where
B(x,r)
is a ball of radius
r
centered at
x
for
(X,\beta).
The property above is also known as the doubling property. It is well known that a
C^{\infty}
compact Riemannian manifold using the standard Riemannian metric and volume is of homogeneous type.

The first result we will need is a Calderon-Zygmund decomposition:

Theorem 5

(
[
43
]
)
.

Suppose that
X
is a space of homogeneous type. Suppose that
f\in\mathbf{L}^{1}(X)
and choose
\alpha>0
such that
\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{1}<\mu(X)
. Then we can decompose
f:=g+b
such that

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\alpha\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}b_{i},

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant,
\text{supp}(b_{i})\subset B(x_{i},r_{i})
for some countable collection of balls
\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}
, and each
b_{i}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{X}b_{i}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\|b_{i}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i})),

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu(B(x_{i},r_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that we choose wavelets
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
generated using
G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
in Equation
5
that satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1
and
c_{1}c_{2}<2
in Lemma
3
. The following weak
(1,1)
bound holds for some
A>0
:

\|\vec{T}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1,\infty}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}\leq A\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

Proof.

First, for any
\alpha
such that
\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}>\mu(\mathcal{M})
, we see that

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|\vec{T}f(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha\})\leq\mu(\mathcal{M})\leq\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

Now, we consider the case where
\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\mu(\mathcal{M})
. We use our Caulderon-Zygmund decomposition and write
f=g+b
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|\vec{T}f(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha\})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|\vec{T}g(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha/2\})+\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|\vec{T}b(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha/2\})

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
I_{1}
, we apply Chebyshev inequality,
\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})
boundedness of
\vec{T}
, and our assumption on
g
to find that

\displaystyle\mu(\{x\in\mathcal{M}:\|\vec{T}g(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha/2\})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\|\vec{T}g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\|\vec{T}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\|g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4C_{1}}{\alpha}\|\vec{T}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

For
I_{2}
, let
B=\bigcup_{i}B(x_{i},2r_{i})
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B)+\mu(\{x\in B^{c}:\|\vec{T}b(x)\|_{\mathcal{B}}>\alpha/2\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{D}}{\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\|\vec{T}b\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(B^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{D}}{\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\sum_{i}\|\vec{T}b_{i}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c})},

where the constant
C_{D}
comes from the fact that our measure has the doubling property.

To estimate
\|\vec{T}b_{i}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c})}
, we notice that

\displaystyle\|\vec{T}b_{i}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c})}

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\|\vec{T}b_{i}(x)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\left\|\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\vec{K}(x,y)b_{i}(y)\,dy\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\left\|\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}(\vec{K}(x,y)-\vec{K}(x,x_{i}))b_{i}(y)\,d\mu(y)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\left(\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})\right|^{2}|b_{i}(y)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,d\mu(x)\right)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}|b_{i}(y)|\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})\right|\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}|b_{i}(y)|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\left|{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-{K}_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})\right|\,d\mu(x)\right)\,d\mu(y),

where
\vec{K}
is the kernel defined on page 7. Now we consider the term inside the parentheses. We will break this argument into cases. First, consider if
2r_{i}\geq\delta
. We see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}+\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,y)\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x).

Now, since
x_{i}
is the center of
B(x_{i},r_{i})
, if
x\in B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}
, then
r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r_{i}\geq\delta
. Similarly, since
y\in B(x_{i},r_{i})
, it follows that
r(y,x_{i})<r_{i}
and we have
2r(y,x_{i})\leq r(x,x_{i})
. Apply triangle inequality to get

r(x,x_{i})\leq r(x,y)+r(y,x_{i})\leq r(x,y)+r_{i}\leq r(x,y)+\frac{1}{2}r(x,x_{i}),

which means that
r(x,x_{i})\leq 2r(x,y).

Going back to the integral, there exists
C_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{B(x_{i},2r_{i})^{c}}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,y)\right)^{n+1}}+\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r(y,x_{i})}\frac{2^{nj}}{\left(1+2^{j}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r(y,x_{i})}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)+C_{1}\sum_{j<0}\int_{r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r(y,x_{i})}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle:=J_{1}+J_{2}.

For
J_{1}
, since
r(x,x_{i})>\delta
,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r(y,x_{i})}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/2}\delta^{n+1}<\infty.

For
J_{2}
, it is routine to see that

\sum_{j<0}\int_{r(x,x_{i})\geq 2r(y,x_{i})}\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(x)\leq\sum_{j<0}2^{nj/2}\mu(\mathcal{M})<\infty.

Now we consider the case where
2r_{i}<\delta
. In this case, we see that
r(y,x_{i})<r_{i}<\delta
, and we still have
2r(y,x_{i})<r(x,x_{i})
. Thus, the bound

|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|\leq C\frac{2^{nj/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}

still applies. We can also apply Lemma
4
to get

|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|\leq C_{\delta}\frac{r(y,x_{i})2^{(n+1)j/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}.

Taking the geometric mean, for any
s\in[0,1]
, we have

|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|\leq C_{2}\frac{2^{nj/2}(2^{j/2}r(y,x_{i}))^{s}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}

for some constant
C_{2}
. It now follows that for
C_{3}=\max\{C_{\delta},C_{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{2^{j/2}<\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|+\sum_{2^{j/2}\geq\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}|K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,y)-K_{2^{-j/2}}(x,x_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\sum_{2^{j/2}<\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}\frac{r(y,x_{i})2^{(n+1)j/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}+C_{2}\sum_{2^{j/2}\geq\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}\frac{2^{nj/2}(2^{j/2}r(y,x_{i}))^{1/2}}{\left(1+2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i})\right)^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\left(r(x_{i},y)\sum_{2^{j/2}<\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}2^{(n+1)j/2}+r(x_{i},y)^{1/2}\sum_{2^{j/2}\geq\frac{2}{r(x,x_{i})}}2^{(n+1/2)j/2}(2^{j/2}r(x,x_{i}))^{-n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(r(x_{i},y)r(x,x_{i})^{-n-1}+r(x_{i},y)^{1/2}r(x,x_{i})^{-n-1/2}).

Integrating over
2r(x_{i},y)\leq r(x,x_{i})
yields a constant independent of
r_{i}
. It now follows that

\|\vec{T}b_{i}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(B(x_{i},r_{i})^{c})}\leq C_{4}\|b_{i}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}

for some constant
C_{4}
. Using the Caulderon-Zygmund decomposition,

I_{2}\leq\left(\frac{C_{D}}{\alpha}+\frac{2C_{4}}{\alpha}\right)\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathcal{M})}.

∎

Lemma 7

(

)

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
be Banach spaces,

The following corollary is a direct result of interpolation now:

Corollary 8
.

Suppose that we choose wavelets
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

in Equation

. Then we have

\|\overline{S}_{2}^{m}f-\overline{S}_{2}^{m}g\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}\leq C^{2m}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

for all
f,g\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

In the case of
m=1
, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\overline{S}_{2}f[j]-\overline{S}_{2}g[j]|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\|f\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}-|g\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f\ast\psi_{j}-g\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(f-g)\ast\psi_{j}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}.

For the
m+1
case, we proceed recursively and see that

\displaystyle\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}}\left|\overline{S}_{2}^{m+1}f[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]-\overline{S}_{2}^{m+1}g[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}}\left|\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\ast\psi_{j+1}\|_{2}-\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\ast\psi_{j+1}\|_{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+1}}\|(U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g)\ast\psi_{j+1}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=C^{2}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\||U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]f\ast\psi_{j_{m}}|-|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]g\ast\psi_{j_{m}}|\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]f\ast\psi_{j_{m}}-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]g\ast\psi_{j_{m}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{4}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m-1}]g\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2(m+1)}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}.

Thus, the claim is proven.
∎

For proper invariance, we provide a theorem that demonstrates that the
2

Theorem 11
.

Let
\xi\in\text{Isom}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime})
, and let
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Define
f^{\prime}=V_{\xi}f
and let
\left(\overline{S}_{2}^{m}\right)^{\prime}
be the corresponding
2
-nonwindowed geometric scattering transform on
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
produced by a littlewood paley wavelet satisfying the conditions described in Theorem
1
. We have
\left(\overline{S}_{2}^{m}\right)^{\prime}f^{\prime}=\overline{S}_{2}^{m}f
.

Proof.

We see that
\overline{S}_{2}[\emptyset]f=\|f\|_{2}=\|V_{\xi}f\|_{2}
since
V_{\xi}
is an isometry. Now suppose that we consider
p=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
. Then since convolution using a spectral filter commutes with isometries and modulus operators (see Theorem 2.1 in
[
36
]
),

\displaystyle\overline{S}_{2}^{m}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f

\displaystyle=\|U[p]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\|V_{\xi}U[p]f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\|U[p]V_{\xi}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\|U[p]f^{\prime}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle=\left(\overline{S}_{2}^{m}\right)^{\prime}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f^{\prime}.

Thus, we can see that each layer is isometry invariant.

4.2
The
q
-nonwindowed Geometric Scattering Norm

Now we prove the
q
-nonwindowed Geometric Scattering Transforms, for
q\in(1,2)
, are bounded operators with respect to (
19
) under mild assumptions.

Theorem 12
.

Suppose that we choose wavelets
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
generated by using
G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
in Equation
5
,
G
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1
, and
c_{1}c_{2}<2
. Then

\|\overline{S}_{q}^{m}f-\overline{S}_{q}^{m}g\|^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}\leq C_{q}^{m}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}

for all
f,g\in\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})
, for all
m\geq 1
, and some constant
C_{q}
dependent on
q
.

Proof.

We start by providing a proof for the case of
m=1
:

\displaystyle\|\overline{S}_{q}f-\overline{S}_{q}g\|^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\overline{S}_{q}[j]f-\overline{S}_{q}[j]g|^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\|U[j]f\|_{q}-\|U[j]g\|_{q}|^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|U[j]f-U[j]g\|_{q}^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|U[j]f(x)-U[j]g(x)|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{q/2}

Via Minkowski’s Integral Intequality,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|U[j]f(x)-U[j]g(x)|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|U[j]f(x)-U[j]g(x)|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f\ast\psi_{j})(x)-(g\ast\psi_{j})(x)|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|\vec{T}(f-g)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}.

Now apply Corollary
8
to get

\|\vec{T}(f-g)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}\leq C_{q}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}.

Now assume that for some
m\geq 1
, we have

\|\overline{S}_{q}^{m}f-\overline{S}_{q}^{m}g\|^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}\leq C_{q}^{m}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}.

Similar to above, when we consider the case with
m+1
, we can mimic the steps above to get

\displaystyle\|\overline{S}_{q}^{m+1}f-\overline{S}_{q}^{m+1}g\|^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j_{m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\sum_{j_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\overline{S}_{q}^{m+1}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]f-\overline{S}_{q}^{m+1}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]g|^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\cdots\sum_{j_{m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]f(x)-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]g(x)|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\left(\sum_{j_{m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]f(x)-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]g(x)|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{q}}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j_{m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]f(x)-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m+1}]g(x)|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j_{m+1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|(U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f\ast\psi_{j_{m+1}})(x)-(U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\ast\psi_{j_{m+1}})(x)|^{2}\right)^{q/2}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{2/q}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|\vec{T}(U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=C_{q}\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\right)^{q/2}.

Now we see that we can apply the induction hypothesis to get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\|U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-U[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\right)^{q/2}

\displaystyle=\|\overline{S}_{q}^{m}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]f-\overline{S}_{q}^{m}[j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}]g\|^{q}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}^{m}\|f-g\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}^{q}.

∎

5.1
Stability Results for the
q
-nonwindowed Geometric Scattering Norm

Lemma 17
.

Suppose
\xi\in\text{Diff}(\mathcal{M})
. If
f\in\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})
is
\lambda
-bandlimited, then

\|f-V_{\xi}f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C(\mathcal{M})\lambda^{n}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}

for some constant
C_{q}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

Since
f
is
\lambda
-bandlimited,
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
as well, and the proof is nearly identical to the proof of the case when
q=2
, but we provide the steps for completeness. We define
\pi_{\lambda}
be the operator that projects a function
f\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{M})
onto the eigenspace
E_{\lambda}
and define the projection operator

P_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda}\pi_{\lambda_{n}}

with kernel

K^{(\lambda)}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda}e_{n}(x)\overline{e_{n}(y)}.

We have
P_{\lambda}f=f

\mu
-almost-everywhere. Thus, via Holder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|f(x)-V_{\xi}f(x)|

\displaystyle=|P_{\lambda}f(x)-V_{\xi}P_{\lambda}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{M}}K^{(\lambda)}(x,y)f(y)\,dy-\int_{\mathcal{M}}K^{(\lambda)}(\xi^{-1}(x),y)f(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathcal{M}}(K^{(\lambda)}(x,y)-K^{(\lambda)}(\xi^{-1}(x),y))f(y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}\left(\int_{\mathcal{M}}|K^{(\lambda)}(x,y)-K^{(\lambda)}(\xi^{-1}(x),y)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q,\text{Vol}}(\mathcal{M})\|f\|_{\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})}\|\xi\|_{\infty}\|\nabla K^{(\lambda)}\|_{\infty}

for some constant
C_{q,\text{Vol}}(\mathcal{M})
dependent on
q
and the volume of the manifold. Now, by Lemma H.1 in
[
36
]
, we have

\|\nabla K^{(\lambda)}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{q}(\mathcal{M})\lambda^{n}.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

.

Suppose
\xi\in\text{Diff}(\mathcal{M})

6
Conclusions and Future Work

We have provided a framework for understanding nonwindowed scattering coefficients. In particular, we provide a weighted measure for distortion between nonwindowed scattering coefficients, showed our weighted measure is well-defined mapping for
\mathbf{L}^{q}(\mathcal{M})
functions, and showed that nonwindowed scattering coefficients are stable to diffeomorphisms for
\lambda
-bandlimited functions.

For future work, it is of interest to see if it is possible to extend our results to manifolds that are not restricted the conditions present in Sections 4 and 5. Additionally, what are other manifolds that satisfy the conditions present in sections 4 and 5? These questions will be left to future work.

Curvature estimates for semi-convex solutions of asymptotic Plateau problem in ℍ n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n+1}
Han Hong, RUIJIA ZHANG

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the asymptotic σ k \sigma_{k} Plateau problem in hyperbolic space. We establish C 2 C^{2} estimates for semi-convex complete hypersurfaces satisfying constant σ k \sigma_{k} curvature with a prescribed asymptotic boundary at the infinity for 2 ≤ k ≤ n − 2 2\leq k\leq n-2 . The result is based on a new crucial concavity inequality derived for hessian equations.

35K55; 53E10

1.
introduction

Let

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=\{(z,z_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\ z_{n+1}>0,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

with metric

g^{\mathbb{H}^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{z_{n+1}^{2}}g^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

be the
n+1
dimensional hyperbolic space, i.e., the half space model. Let
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
denote the ideal boundary of
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
at infinity, which can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
. We assume that
\Gamma
is a closed
(n-1)
-dimensional embedded hypersurface in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, which can be viewed as the boundary of a smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The asymptotic Plateau problem for
\sigma_{k}
aims to find a complete hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1.1)

\kappa[\Sigma]\in\Gamma_{k}\ \ \text{and}\ \ P_{k}(\kappa[\Sigma])=\sigma\in(0,1)

with

(1.2)

\partial\Sigma=\Gamma.

Here,
\kappa[\Sigma]=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
denotes the hyperbolic principal curvatures of
\Sigma
. Then
P_{k}=\sigma_{k}/C_{n}^{k}
is the
k
-th normalized elementary symmetric polynomial and
\Gamma_{k}
denotes the
k
-th Garding cone, defined as

\Gamma_{k}:=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ \sigma_{k}>0,\forall\ 1\leq j\leq k\}.

When
k=1
or
n,
this problem has been solved in
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
. When
k=n-1
, this problem has been solved by S. Lu
[
10
]
. The general case when
2\leq k\leq n-2
is still open. If the hypersurface is assumed to be strictly (hyperbolic) convex, we can refer to
[
7
,
6
,
3
,
4
]
by Guan-Spruck-Xiao for positive solutions and uniqueness results.

To solve the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), one needs to find solutions to a fully nonlinear PDE with certain degeneracy. The idea is to use the method of continuity and to approximate the solution on compact subset of the domain bounded by
\Gamma
. The
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates are obtained by maximum principles with the help of geometric barriers and this has well-established for all
k
in previous works
[
7
,
6
,
3
,
4
]
. The curvature estimate can also be proven using a test function and the bound for principal curvatures depends on the lower bound of
u
. However,
u
vanishes on the boundary
\Gamma
and thus it prevents us applying diagonal argument to get the limit. The aim of this paper is to provide a curvature estimate for this problem that is uniformly independent of the lower bound of
u
under semi-convex condition by using a new concavity inequality.

We say a hypersurface
M
semi-convex if there exists a constant
A>0
such that

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(X)\geq-A,\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\quad\forall\ X\in M.

We now state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega\times\{0\}
has nonnegative (Euclidean) mean curvature, where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 4
. Suppose that
\sigma\in(0,1)
. For
2\leq k\leq n-2
, let
\Sigma
be a complete semi-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying

\displaystyle P_{k}(\kappa)=\sigma,\quad\partial\Sigma=\Gamma

with
\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\max_{x\in\Sigma;i=1,2,...,n}|\kappa_{i}|\leq C

where
C
depends only on
n
,
k
,
\sigma
,
\Omega
and
A
.

Remark 1.1
.

After completing this work, we found an interesting paper of Bin Wang
[
13
]
, where
he completely resolved this problem for the cases when
k=2
and
k=n-2
. Besides, he achieved the same curvature estimates under the assumption
\sigma_{k+1}>-A
as stated in Theorem 1.9 of
[
13
]
, and posed the question whether the results remain valid under the semi-convexity condition. We provide a positive answer to this question in Theorem
1.1
.

The main difficulty of overcoming the curvature estimates is dealing with the third-order derivatives. The usual method is to use the concavity of
q_{k}
, or, more progressively, to use the concavity of
\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}=(q_{k}\cdots q_{l+1})^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
and to throw out these terms directly. To prove this theorem, we derive a crucial concavity inequality based on Huisken-Sinestrari’s observation
[
9
]
(see Lemma
2.39
). According to
[
9
]
, when
\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}
, the concavity of
q_{k}
can be deduced from the concavity of
q_{k-1}
by induction. In particular,
-\sigma_{1}\partial^{2}_{\xi}q_{2}
can be bounded below by the length of
\xi^{\perp}
with respect to the vector
\kappa
. We observe that, by induction, the quantity of
-\partial^{2}_{\xi}q_{k}
can be controlled by
\xi
. This observation plays a crucial role in controlling the third-order derivatives.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we collect some known facts of asymptotic Plateau problem in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, collect and prove some properties of the k-th elementary symmetric functions. In section
3
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which will be divided into several cases.

Suppose that

is diagonal by choosing the appropriate orthonormal basis at one point. Then we define

\displaystyle\dot{f}^{p}:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\kappa_{p}}(\kappa)=\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{pp}(\mathcal{A}),\quad\ddot{f}^{pq}:=\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\kappa_{p}\partial\kappa_{q}}(\kappa)=\ddot{\mathcal{F}}^{pp,qq}(\mathcal{A}).

2.2.
Symmetric function

Here we state some algebraic properties of the elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)
,
m=1,\cdots,n
, where
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. We recall
\sigma_{m}(\kappa)
defined by

\sigma_{m}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{m}},\quad m=1,2,\cdots,n.

The Garding cone
\Gamma_{m}^{+}
is an open symmetric convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with vertex at the origin, given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}^{+}=\{\left(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sigma_{j}(\kappa)>0,\forall j=1,\cdots,m\}.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{+}\subset\cdots\subset{\Gamma}_{m}^{+}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma_{1}^{+}.

In particular,
\Gamma^{+}=\Gamma_{n}^{+}
is called the positive cone,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}=\{\left(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\kappa_{1}>0,\cdots,\kappa_{n}>0\}.

Always assume
\kappa_{1}\geq\kappa_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n}
. We collect some properties of
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
where
\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}
, referring to
[
1
]

[

]

Lemma 2.6
.

\sum_{p=1}^{n}\dot{\sigma}_{k}^{pp}\kappa_{p}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}
and
\sum_{p=1}^{n}\dot{\sigma}_{k}^{pp}=(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}.

Lemma 2.7
.

\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{n}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{1}}\geq 0.

Lemma 2.8
.

\dot{\sigma_{k}}^{11}\kappa_{1}\geq\frac{k}{n}\sigma_{k}

Lemma 2.9

\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k-1}}
is concave.

Lemma 2.10
.

For any

\sigma_{s}>\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\cdots\kappa_{s}
.

Lemma 2.11

\kappa_{k}>0

Proof.

From Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k-1}\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{1}\cdots\partial\kappa_{k-1}}=\kappa_{k}+\cdots+\kappa_{n}>0,

Lemma 2.12
.

If
\sigma_{k+1}>-A
for any positive constant A, then

is semi-convex, i.e.
\kappa_{\min}>-C(n,k,A)

Proof.

There exists constant

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{n}}\kappa_{n}^{2}\leq\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{i}}\kappa_{i}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}<C\sigma_{1}+(k+1)A.

Then by Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{i}}\kappa_{n}^{2}<nC\sigma_{1}+n(k+1)A.

Lemma 2.13

\kappa_{k}<C(\sigma_{k},n,A).

Proof.

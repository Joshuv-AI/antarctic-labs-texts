Spectral radius, edge-disjoint cycles and cycles of the same length Supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (11771141, 12171066 and 12011530064).
Huiqiu Lin, Mingqing Zhai and Yanhua Zhao

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we give spectral conditions to guarantee the existence of two edge disjoint cycles and two cycles of the same length. These two results can be seen as spectral analogues of Erdős and Posa’s size condition and Erdős’ classic problem on non existence of two cycles of the same length. By using double leading eigenvectors skill, we further give spectral condition to guarantee the existence of
k
edge disjoint triangles.

Keywords:
Spectral radius; two edge disjoint cycles; two cycles of the same lengths;
k
edge disjoint triangles

AMS Classification:
05C50; 05C35

Introduction

The study of cycles has a rich history in graph theory. A
folklore result states that every
graph with minimum degree at least 2 has a cycle.
On the other hand, a connected graph on
n
vertices without any cycle is a tree.
Let
{\Omega_{k}}^{\prime}
be the family of graphs with
k

edge-disjoint cycles, and
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{k}}
be the family of graphs out of
{\Omega_{k}}^{\prime}
.
Erdős and Posa proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Erdős and Posa
[
10
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges. If
G\in\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{2}}
, then
m\leq n+3
.
Furthermore, if
G\in\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{2}}
and
m=n+3
, then
G
is obtained from a subdivision

G_{0}
of
K_{3,3}
by adding a forest and exactly one edge, joining each
tree of the forest to
G_{0}
.

Bollobás in his classic book
[
2
]
posed a more general problem as follows:
What is the maximum size of a graph
G\in\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{k}}
of order
n
?
Up to now, this problem is still widely open.

Another classic problem involves cycles of the same length.
Let
f(n)
be the maximum number of edges in a graph on
n
vertices without two cycles of the same length.
Erdős posed the problem of determining
f(n)
,
which was listed as one of
50
unsolved problems in the textbook by Bondy and Murty
(see
[
1
, p.247, Problem 11]
). It has attracted the attention of many scholars.
In 1988, Shi
[
31
]
gave a lower bound of

, which was improved by Lai
[
21
]
in 2017.
Boros, Caro, Füredi and Yuster
[
3

showed that
\sqrt{n}-o(\sqrt{n})\leq f(n)-n\leq 1.98\sqrt{n}(1+o(1))
,
and they further conjectured that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(n)-n}{\sqrt{n}}=1
.
Very recently, Ma and Yang
[
24
]
confirmed their conjecture by showing that
f(n)<n+\sqrt{n}+o(n)
for any
n
-vertex 2-connected graph.
However, the exactly value of
f(n)
is still unknown.
For more related results, we refer the interested readers to
[
9
,
20
,
22
]
.

One main purpose of this paper is to investigate these two problems from a spectral perspective.

Lemma 1

(

Let

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
G^{\star}
be a graph with the maximum spectral radius among all graphs without two cycles with the same length. Then
G^{\star}
is connected, otherwise we add cut edges between components, the resulting graph will increase the spectral radius but will not produce a new cycle, which is impossible.
Now let
Z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})^{t}

and
z_{u^{\star}}=\max\{z_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}

It follows that
\rho(G^{\star})\geq\rho(K_{1,n-1}^{+})>X^{t}A(K_{1,n-1}^{+})X=\sqrt{n-1}+\frac{1}{n-1},

\rho^{2}(G^{\star})-\frac{2}{n-1}\rho(G^{\star})+\frac{1}{(n-1)^{2}}-n+1>0.

(1)

We first give the following claim which indicates that except

Claim 1
.

For each vertex

is not a cut vertex.

Proof.

By the way of contradiction, assume that

is a cut vertex.
Then there exist two components of
G^{\star}\backslash\{u\}

and

It follows that,

\rho(G^{\prime})-\rho(G^{\star})\geq Z^{t}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G^{\star}))Z=(z_{u^{\star}}-z_{u})\sum_{v\in N_{V(G_{2})}(u)}z_{v}\geq 0.

(2)

If

It follows that
\rho(G^{\prime})>\rho(G^{\star})
, which contradicts the fact that

has maximum spectral radius.
∎

For each vertex

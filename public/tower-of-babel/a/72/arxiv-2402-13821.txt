Performance Improvement Bounds for Lipschitz Configurable Markov Decision Processes
Alberto Maria Metelli

Abstract
Configurable Markov Decision Processes (Conf-MDPs) have recently been introduced as an extension of the traditional Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) to model the real-world scenarios in which there is the possibility to intervene in the environment in order to configure some of its parameters. In this paper, we focus on a particular subclass of Conf-MDP that satisfies regularity conditions, namely Lipschitz continuity. We start by providing a bound on the Wasserstein distance between γ \gamma -discounted stationary distributions induced by changing policy and configuration. This result generalizes the already existing bounds both for Conf-MDPs and traditional MDPs. Then, we derive a novel performance improvement lower bound.

Introduction

The framework of the
Configurable Markov Decision Processes

(
Metelli et al., 2018
;
Metelli et al., 2019
;
Metelli et al., 2022
, Conf-MDPs,)
has been introduced in recent years to model a wide range of real-world scenarios in which an agent has the opportunity to alter some environmental parameters in order to improve its learning experience. Conf-MDPs can be thought to as an extension of the traditional Markov Decision Processes
(
Puterman, 1994
, MDP,)
to account for scenarios that emerge quite often in the Reinforcement Learning
(
Sutton and Barto, 2018
, RL,)
problems, in which the environment rarely represents an immutable entity and can, indeed, be subject to partial control. In the Conf-MDP framework, the activity of altering the environmental parameters is named
environment configuration
and serves different purposes.

In the simplest scenario, the configuration is carried out by the agent itself that acts as a configurator. This might suggest, at a first sight, that environment configuration can be modeled within the agent actuation. While in principle this approach is possible, it tends to disregard the domain peculiarities of environment configuration that usually is performed at a slower frequency compared to policy learning and might generate notable costs. The more realistic setting is the one in which an additional entity, the
configurator
is present and in charge of the environment configuration process. The configurator acts on the environment transition model (that we will call
configuration
in this setting) and might have an objective that is different from that of the agent. Thus, we can distinguish between two scenarios
Metelli, 2021
;
Metelli, 2022
: the
cooperative
and the
non-cooperative
settings. In the former, the agent and the configurator share the same interests, i.e., the have the same reward function. In such a case, the learning problem is simple as there is no conflict between the two involved entities and several algorithms have been proposed in the literature
Metelli et al., 2018
;
Metelli et al., 2019
. In the non-cooperative setting, instead, agent and configurator have possibly diverging interests, i.e., they reward function differ. Here the definition of a solution concept is more challenging and requires considering game-theoretic equilibria
Metelli, 2021
. Recently a regret minimization approach has been proposed to address the learning problem in general-sum non-cooperative Conf-MDPs
Ramponi et al., 2021
.

In this paper, we focus on cooperative Conf-MDP

2.1
Mathematical Background

Probability

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a set and
\mathfrak{F}
be a
\sigma
-algebra over
\mathcal{X}
. We denote with
\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}
the set of probability measures over the measurable space
(\mathcal{X},\mathfrak{F})
. Let
\mathcal{Y}
be a set, we denote with
\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathcal{X}}
the set of functions with signature
\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}
and with
\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}
the set of functions with signature
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the Dirac delta measure is denoted by
\delta_{x}
.

Lipschitz Continuity

Let
(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})
and
(\mathcal{Y},d_{\mathcal{Y}})
be two metric spaces, where
d_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
and
d_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are the corresponding distance functions. A function
f\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathcal{X}}
is
Lipschitz continuous
with Lipschitz constant
L_{f}
(
L_{f}
-LC) if it holds that:

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(f(x),f(\overline{x})\right)\leq L_{f}d_{\mathcal{X}}\left(x,\overline{x}\right),\qquad\forall x,\overline{x}\in\mathcal{X}.

(1)

We define the
Lipschitz semi-norm
of function
f
as the smallest
L_{f}>0
for which Equation (
1
) holds:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L}=\sup_{x,\overline{x}\in\mathcal{X},\,x\neq\overline{x}}\frac{d_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(f(x),f(\overline{x})\right)}{d_{\mathcal{X}}\left(x,\overline{x}\right)}.

Being a semi-norm,
\|\cdot\|_{L}
is non-negative and fulfills the triangular inequality. If
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
f
is a real-valued function, we typically employ as distance function
d_{\mathcal{Y}}
the Euclidean distance, i.e.,
d_{\mathcal{Y}}(y,\overline{y})=|y-\overline{y}|
. If
\mathcal{Y}=\Delta^{\mathcal{Z}}
, i.e.,
f
has values in probability distributions, we employ as distance function the Wasserstein distance
(
Villani, 2009
)
.

Wasserstein Distance

Let
p,q\in\Delta^{\mathcal{X}}
be two probability measures over the metric space
(\mathcal{X},d_{\mathcal{X}})
. The
L_{1}
-Wasserstein (or Kantorovich) distance between
p
and
q
is defined as
Villani, 2009
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(p,q)=\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left(p(\mathrm{d}x)-q(\mathrm{d}x)\right)f(x)\right|.

If the metric
d_{\mathcal{X}}
is chosen to be the
discrete metric
, i.e.,
d_{\mathcal{X}}(x,x^{\prime})=\mathds{1}\{x\neq x^{\prime}\}
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, then the
L_{1}
-Wasserstein reduces to the Total Variation (TV) divergence.

2.2
Configurable Markov Decision Processes

A (cooperative) Configurable Markov Decision Process
(
Metelli et al., 2018
, Conf-MDP,)
is a tuple
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}
is the reward function mapping every state-action-next-state triple
(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
to the reward
r(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discount factor. The behavior of the configurator is modeled by a configuration (or transition model)
p\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
that maps every state-action pair
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
to the probability measure
p(\cdot|s,a)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}
of the next state. The behavior of the agent is modeled by a policy
\pi\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
that maps every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
to the probability measure
\pi(\cdot|s)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}
of the action to be played. We denote with
p_{\pi}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}}
the state-state transition kernel, defined as:

\displaystyle p_{\pi}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)p(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a),\qquad\forall s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}.

Value Functions

Similarly to traditional MDPs, the
value functions

Sutton and Barto, 2018
can be define for Conf-MDPs as done in
Metelli, 2021
. Let
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be a policy-configuration pair, we define the following value functions, for every
(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S\times A\times S}
:

\displaystyle v_{\pi,p}(s)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)\int_{\mathcal{S}}p(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\left(r(s,a,s^{\prime})+\gamma v_{\pi,p}(s^{\prime})\right),

\displaystyle q_{\pi,p}(s,a)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}p(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\left(r(s,a,s^{\prime})+\gamma v_{\pi,p}(s^{\prime})\right),

\displaystyle u_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})=r(s,a,s^{\prime})+\gamma v_{\pi,p}(s^{\prime}).

These value functions represent the
expected discounted cumulative reward
(i.e., the
expected return
) experienced by the agent-configurator when interacting with the environment with a policy
\pi
and a configuration
p
. While
v_{\pi,p}(s)
(i.e., state value function or V-function) and
q_{\pi,p}(s,a)
(i.e., state-action value function or Q-function) are defined analogously to the case of MDPs,
u_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})
(i.e., state-action-next-state value function or U-function) is a peculiar value function for Conf-MDPs
Metelli et al., 2018
that turns out to be relevant for learning optimal configurations. We refer the reader to
(
Metelli, 2021
, Chapter 4)
for a detailed review of the value function and the Bellman operators and equations for Conf-MDPs.

\gamma
-discounted Stationary Distributions

A policy-configuration pair
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
induces a visitation distribution of the states. The
\gamma
-discounted stationary distribution accounts the expected discounted number of visits

3

“Discounted” means that if a visit to a given state is performed at step
t
of interaction it counts
\gamma^{t}
.
induced by a policy-configuration pair
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
, starting from an initial state sampled from
\rho\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}
, and it is defined in several equivalent forms

\displaystyle\mu_{\pi,p}^{\gamma,\rho}=(1-\gamma)\rho+\gamma{p}_{\pi}\mu_{\pi,p}^{\gamma,\rho}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\gamma^{t}\rho p_{\pi}^{t}=(1-\gamma)\rho\left(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{S}}-\gamma p_{\pi}\right)^{-1}.

(2)

For the sake of brevity, whenever clear from the context, we omit the superscripts

, simply employing
\mu_{\pi,p}
.

Given an initial state distribution
\rho\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}
, we define the

of a policy-configuration pair
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
in two interchangeable ways:

\displaystyle J_{\pi,p}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\rho(\mathrm{d}s)v_{\pi,p}(s)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)p(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)r(s,a,s^{\prime}).

3
Lipschitz Configurable Markov Decision Processes

In this section, we introduce the notion of regular Conf-MDPs that we will employ in the derivation of the theoretical results. We rephrase the traditional notion of Lipschitz (LC) MDP
Rachelson and Lagoudakis, 2010
;
Pirotta et al., 2015
to the case of Conf-MDPs.

Assumption 3.1

(Lipschitz Configurable Markov Decision Processes (LC))
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r,\gamma)
be a Conf-MDP, where
(\mathcal{S},d_{\mathcal{S}})
and
(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}})
are metric spaces endowed with the corresponding distance functions
d_{\mathcal{S}}
and
d_{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively.
\mathcal{C}
is
L_{r}
-LC if it holds that:

\displaystyle\left|r(s,a,s^{\prime})-r(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right|\leq L_{r}d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}\left((s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right),\qquad\forall(s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A},

where
d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}\left((s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right)\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})+d_{\mathcal{A}}(a,\overline{a})+d_{\mathcal{S}}(s^{\prime},\overline{s}^{\prime})
.

Compared with
Rachelson and Lagoudakis, 2010
;
Pirotta et al., 2015
, we enforce a condition on the reward function only, since the transition model (i.e., configuration) does not belong to the definition of the Conf-MDP. Moreover, with negligible loss of generality, as done in
Rachelson and Lagoudakis, 2010
;
Pirotta et al., 2015
, we consider a disjoint metric for the state and action spaces. We now proceed at introducing the notion of Lipschitz configuration and policy.

Assumption 3.2

(Lipschitz Configuration and Policy (LC))
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},r,\gamma)
be a Conf-MDP, where
(\mathcal{S},d_{\mathcal{S}})
and
(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}})
are metric spaces endowed with the corresponding distance functions
d_{\mathcal{S}}
and
d_{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively.
Let
p\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be a configuration.
p
is
L_{p}
-LC if it holds that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(p(\cdot|s,a),p(\cdot|\overline{s},\overline{a})\right)\leq L_{p}d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\left((s,a),(\overline{s},\overline{a})\right),\quad\forall(s,a),(\overline{s},\overline{a})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A},

where
d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\left((s,a),(\overline{s},\overline{a})\right)\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})+d_{\mathcal{A}}(a,\overline{a})
. Let
\pi\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
be a policy.
\pi
is
L_{\pi}
-LC if it holds that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(\pi(\cdot|s),\pi(\cdot|\overline{s})\right)\leq L_{\pi}d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s}),\quad\forall s,\overline{s}\in\mathcal{S}.

The choice of employing the Wasserstein distance over other distributional divergences, such as the TV divergence
Munos and Szepesvári, 2008
, is justified by the fact that the former allows quantifying the distance between deterministic distributions. Instead, the TV divergence takes its maximum value whenever the involved distributions have disjoint support. The Wasserstein distance is a standard choice in the RL literature
Pirotta et al., 2015
;
Metelli et al., 2020
.

Lipschitz semi-norms of the Value functions

Once we enforce the LC conditions on the Conf-MDP and on the policy and configurations (Assumptions
3.1
and
3.2
), it is well-known that the state value function
v_{\pi,p}
and state-action value function
q_{\pi,p}
are LC, under the assumption that
\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi})<1

Rachelson and Lagoudakis, 2010
. In particular, their Lipschitz semi-norms (i.e., Lipschitz constants) are bounded as:

\displaystyle\left\|{q_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}\leq\frac{L_{r}}{1-\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi})},\qquad\left\|{v_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|{q_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}(1+L_{\pi})\leq\frac{L_{r}(1+L_{\pi})}{1-\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi})}.

The following result provides a bound on the Lipschitz semi-norm of the state-action-next-state value function
u_{\pi,p}
, that has been specifically introduced for the Conf-MDPs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
L_{r}
-LC Conf-MDP,
p\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be an
L_{p}
-LC configuration, and
\pi\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
be an
L_{\pi}
-LC policy. Then, the state-action-next-state value function
u_{\pi,p}
is LC, under the assumption that
\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi})<1
, with Lipschitz semi-norm:

\displaystyle\left\|{u_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}\leq L_{r}+\gamma\left\|{v_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}\leq\frac{L_{r}(2+L_{\pi}-\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi}))}{1-\gamma L_{p}(1+L_{\pi})}.

Proof.

Let
(s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
, we have:

\displaystyle\left|u_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})-u_{\pi,p}(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|r(s,a,s^{\prime})+\gamma v_{\pi,p}(s^{\prime})-r(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})-\gamma v_{\pi,p}(\overline{s}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|r(s,a,s^{\prime})-r(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right|+\gamma\left|v_{\pi,p}(s^{\prime})-v_{\pi,p}(\overline{s}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq L_{r}d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}\left((s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right)+\gamma\left\|{v_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}d_{\mathcal{S}}(s^{\prime},\overline{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\left(L_{r}+\gamma\left\|{v_{\pi,p}}\right\|_{L}\right)d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}((s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime}),

having observed that
d_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}}\left((s,a,s^{\prime}),(\overline{s},\overline{a},\overline{s}^{\prime})\right)\geq d_{\mathcal{S}}(s^{\prime},\overline{s}^{\prime})
.

\displaystyle-(1-\gamma)g_{f}(\overline{s})=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})-\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})\right)f(s^{\prime})\leq{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}},{\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}}\right)}.

Thus, we have:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}},{\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}}\right)}=\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\Bigg|(1-\gamma)\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\delta_{s}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})-\delta_{\overline{s}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})\right)f(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\gamma\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\left({p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}\right)(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})-\left({p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}\right)(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})\right)f(s^{\prime})\Bigg|

(7)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(1-\gamma)d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})+\gamma\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime\prime})-\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime\prime})\right)\underbrace{\int_{\mathcal{S}}{p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s^{\prime\prime})f(s^{\prime})}_{\eqqcolon h_{f}(s^{\prime\prime})}\right|

(8)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(1-\gamma)d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})+\gamma\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\left\|{h_{f}}\right\|_{L}\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime\prime})-\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime\prime})\right)f(s^{\prime\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=(1-\gamma)d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})+\gamma\sup_{f:\left\|f\right\|_{L{}}\leq 1}\left\|{h_{f}}\right\|_{L}{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}},{\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}}\right)},

where line (
7
) follows from the definition of
\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}
, line (
8
) follows from observing that
\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\delta_{s}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})-\delta_{\overline{s}}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime})\right)f(s^{\prime})=f(s)-f(\overline{s})\leq d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})
since
\|f\|_{L}\leq 1
and by the definition of
{p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}
. The Lipschitz semi-norm
\left\|{h_{f}}\right\|_{L}
is computed in Lemma
9.1
, resulting in
L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})
. Putting all together and exploiting the recursion, we obtain:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{s}}},{\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\delta_{\overline{s}}}}\right)}\leq\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})}d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s})\quad\implies\quad g_{f}(s)-g_{f}(\overline{s})\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})}d_{\mathcal{S}}(s,\overline{s}),

that concludes the proof.
∎

Thus, we have bounded the Wasserstein distance between the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distributions with the Wasserstein distance between the state transition kernels, averaged over the distribution
\mu_{\pi,p}
. The resulting multiplicative constant involves the Lipschitz constants of the policy
\pi^{\prime}
and of the configuration
p^{\prime}
. Therefore, remarkably, in order to obtain such a result it is not required that the policy-configuration pair
(\pi,p)
is LC.

4.2
Decoupled Bound

In some applications, it is useful to decouple the contribution of the configuration
p
and that of the policy
\pi
in the bound of the Wasserstein distance between the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distributions. Indeed, in several applicative scenarios of the Conf-MDP, as noted earlier, the configuration activity (i.e., altering the transition model of the environment) and the policy learning are carried out by two different entities, possibly at different time scales. The following corollary provides a looser bound in which the contribution of policy and configurations are separated.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Conf-MDP, let
(\pi,p),(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be two policy-configuration pairs such that
\pi^{\prime}
is
L_{\pi^{\prime}}
-LC and
p^{\prime}
is
L_{p^{\prime}}
-LC. Then, if
\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})<1
, it holds that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}},\mu_{\pi,p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)\mathcal{W}{\left(p^{\prime}(\cdot|s,a),p(\cdot|s,a)\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}}{1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}})}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\mathcal{W}{\left(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s),\pi(\cdot|s)\right)}.

4.3
Comparison with Existing Bounds

The bounds presented in the previous sections generalize several existing results present the literature. In particular, Corollary
4.2
is an extension of Lemma 3 of
Pirotta et al., 2015
for the case of Conf-MDPs in which we allow for the modification of the configuration (i.e., transition model) too. Furthermore, even in the non-configurable setting, i.e., when setting
p=p^{\prime}
a.s., our result is stronger as it involves the Wasserstein distance between policies
averaged
over the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distribution instead of its
supremum
over the state space:

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\mathcal{W}{\left(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s),\pi(\cdot|s)\right)}}_{\text{Our Corollay~\ref{coroll:BoundDisj}}}\leq\underbrace{\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{W}{\left(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s),\pi(\cdot|s)\right)}}_{\text{Lemma 3 of~\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{PirottaRB15}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}}}.

A result connected to ours, with a bound involving the modification of the transition model, appeared in
Asadi et al., 2018
for model-based RL. However, the provided equation holds under a uniform bound on the Wasserstein distance between transition models
\sup_{(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{W}}{\left({p^{\prime}(\cdot|s,a)},{p(\cdot|s,a)}\right)}
, that, instead, we relax considering the Wasserstein distance averaged over the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distribution, i.e.,
\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s){\mathcal{W}}{\left({p^{\prime}(\cdot|s,a)},{p(\cdot|s,a)}\right)}
.

Finally, in
Saleh et al., 2022
a bound on the Wasserstein distance between the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distributions, as a function of the modification of the policy only, is proposed under a different set of regularity assumptions. In particular, two conditions on the transition kernel are enforced for every
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\int_{\mathcal{S}}\nu(\mathrm{d}s)p_{\pi}(\cdot|s)},{\int_{\mathcal{S}}\nu(\mathrm{d}s)p_{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot|s)}\right)}\leq\widetilde{L}_{\pi}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\pi(\cdot|s)},{\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s)}\right)},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\int_{\mathcal{S}}\nu(\mathrm{d}s)p_{\pi}(\cdot|s)},{\int_{\mathcal{S}}\nu^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s)p_{\pi}(\cdot|s)}\right)}\leq\widetilde{L}_{\nu}{\mathcal{W}}{\left({\nu},{\nu^{\prime}}\right)},

where
\widetilde{L}_{\pi}
and
\widetilde{L}_{\nu}
are suitable Lipschitz constants. These conditions, however, are not comparable with the ones considered in the present paper (Section
3
), as they evaluate how fast the transition kernel changes when altering either the policy or the initial state distribution. Consequently, it is hard to argue whether of the resulting bound is tighter than ours. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that ours is the only one that involves the average Wasserstein distance between policies, rather than the supremum over the state (or state-action) space.

5
Relative Advantage Functions

In this section, we revise the
relative advantage functions
for Conf-MDPs that are needed for the derivation of the performance improvement bounds, as introduced in
Metelli et al., 2018
;
Metelli, 2021
. The notion of
advantage function
exists in the traditional MDP setting and evaluates the one-step improvement in executing an action
a\in\mathcal{A}
compared to executing the current policy
\pi\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}

Puterman, 1994
.

We now extend the advantage function notion to the Conf-MDP setting, properly accounting for the presence of the agent and the configuration. Specifically, we introduce three notions: the
policy
advantage function, the
configuration
advantage function, and the
coupled
advantage function, respectively defined for every
(s,a,s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{S}
as:

\displaystyle A_{\pi,p}(s,a)=q_{\pi,p}(s,a)-v_{\pi,p}(s),

\displaystyle A_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})=u_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})-q_{\pi,p}(s,a),

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})=u_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})-v_{\pi,p}(s)=A_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime})+A_{\pi,p}(s,a).

These functions evaluate the one-step performance improvement obtained in state
s\in\mathcal{S}
by either playing action
a\in\mathcal{A}
, for the policy advantage, selecting the next state
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
given that action
a\in\mathcal{A}
was played, for the model advantage, or both for the coupled advantage, compared to playing policy
\pi
and transition model
p
.

To quantify the one-step improvement in performance attained by a new policy
\pi^{\prime}
or transition model
p^{\prime}
when the current policy is
\pi
and the current model is
p
, we introduce the
(decoupled) relative advantage functions

(
Kakade and Langford, 2002
)
defined for every state-action pair
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
as:

\displaystyle A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}(s)=\int_{\mathcal{A}}\pi^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}a|s)A_{\pi,p}(s,a),

\displaystyle A_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}(s,a)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}p^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)A_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime}),

and the corresponding expected values under the
\gamma
-discounted distributions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p,\rho}^{\pi^{\prime},p}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}(s)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p,\rho}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}(s,a).

In order to account for the combined effect of choosing the action with a new policy
\pi^{\prime}
and the next state with the new configuration
p^{\prime}
, we introduce the
coupled relative advantage function
defined for every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
as:

A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\pi^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}a|s)p^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}|s,a)\widetilde{A}_{\pi,p}(s,a,s^{\prime}).

Thus,
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
evaluates the one-step improvement obtained by the new policy-configuration pair
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
over
the current one
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
, i.e., the local gain in performance achieved by selecting an action with
\pi^{\prime}
and the next state with
p^{\prime}
. The corresponding expectation under the
\gamma
-discounted distribution is given by:

\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p,\rho}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s).

To lighten the notation, we remove the subscript of the initial state distribution
\rho
whenever clear from the context. Thus, we simply write
\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}
,
\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}
, and
\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
. The following result relates the coupled relative advantage function with the corresponding (decoupled) relative advantage functions.

Lemma 5.1

(Lemma A.1 of
Metelli et al., 2018
)
.

Let
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
be the coupled relative advantage function,
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}
and
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}
be the (decoupled) policy and configuration relative advantage functions respectively. Then, for every state
s\in\mathcal{S}
it holds that:

A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)=A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}(s)+\int_{\mathcal{A}}\pi^{\prime}(\mathrm{d}a|s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}(s,a).

This result has a meaningful interpretation. In order to assess the one-step performance improvement in state
s\in\mathcal{S}
obtained moving from policy-configuration pair
(\pi,p)
to the new one
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
, i.e.,
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)
, we can add the contribution of two terms: (i) the one-step performance improvement due to the policy, i.e.,
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p}(s)
; (ii) the one-step performance improvement due to the configuration, i.e.,
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi,p^{\prime}}(s,a)
, with the action sampled from the new policy
\pi^{\prime}
.

6
Bound on the Performance Improvement

In this section, we make use of the results provided in the previous sections, to derive lower bounds on the performance improvement yielded by changing the policy and the configuration. We start the section by revising the performance difference lemma for Conf-MDPs, as derived in
Metelli et al., 2018
. Then, we proceed at proving the performance improvement lower bounds. Specifically, in Section
6.1
, we derive a tight lower bound that, unfortunately, is hardly usable in practice since it involves the presence of non-easily computable quantities and keeps together the contributions of the policy and the configuration (coupled). Then, in Section
6.2
, we derive a looser bound that separates the effect of policy and configuration (decoupled) and just requires the LC property.

Theorem 6.1

(Performance Difference Lemma - Theorem 3.1 of
Metelli et al., 2018
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Conf-MDP. The performance improvement of policy-configuration pair
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
over
(\pi,p)\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
is given by:

J_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}-J_{\pi,p}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s).

This result represents the extension of the well-known performance difference lemma of
Kakade and Langford, 2002
to the case in which we are allowed to change the transition model (i.e., configuration) too. The meaning of the result can be summarized as follows. The performance improvement
J_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}-J_{\pi,p}
attained by moving from pair
(\pi,p)
to pair
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is computed by means of the coupled relative advantage function
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
, averaged over the
\gamma
-discounted stationary distribution
\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
, induced by the new pair
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
.

6.1
Coupled Bound

From a practical perspective, the expression of Theorem
6.1
is an equality but cannot be directly used since it requires computing (or estimating) an expectation w.r.t. the
\gamma
-discounted distribution
\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
, depending on the new pair
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
that we have not access to. Instead, we would like to obtain a performance improvement lower bound that can be estimated by using the current pair
(\pi,p)
. The following result serves the purpose.

Theorem 6.2

(Coupled Bound)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
L_{r}
-LC Conf-MDP, let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
be two
L_{\pi},L_{\pi^{\prime}}
-LC policies respectively, and let
p,p^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be two
L_{p},L_{p^{\prime}}
-LC configurations. The performance improvement of policy-configuration pair
(\pi^{\prime},P^{\prime})
over
(\pi,P)
is lower bounded as:

\underbrace{J_{\pi^{\prime},{p}^{\prime}}-J_{\pi,{p}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{performance}\\
\text{improvement}\end{subarray}}\geq\underbrace{\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{A}_{\pi,{p}}^{\pi^{\prime},{p}^{\prime}}}_{\text{advantage}}-\underbrace{\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}}))}\,\left\|A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\right\|_{L}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\mathcal{W}{\left({p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot|s),{p}_{\pi}(\cdot|s)\right)}}_{\text{dissimilarity penalization}}.

Proof.

Exploiting the bounds on the
\gamma
-discounted state distributions difference (Theorem
4.1
) we can easily derive the performance improvement bound:

\displaystyle J_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}-J_{\pi,p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s)-\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\right)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)

(9)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{A}_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\bigg|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mu_{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{d}s)-\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\right)A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)\bigg|

(10)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathbb{A}_{{\pi,p}}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}}{1-\gamma}-\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)(1-\gamma L_{p^{\prime}}(1+L_{\pi^{\prime}}))}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s){\mathcal{W}}\left({p^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\cdot|s),{p}_{\pi}(\cdot|s)\right)\left\|A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\right\|_{L},

(11)

where line (
10
) follows from line (
9
) by observing that
b\geq-|b|
for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
and line (
11
) is obtained by using Theorem
4.1
and the definition of
Lipschitz semi-norm.
∎

The resulting bound is made of two terms, as the commonly known bounds in the literature
(
Kakade and Langford, 2002
;
Pirotta et al., 2013
;
Metelli et al., 2021
)
. The first term,
advantage
, accounts for the improvement in performance that can be obtained locally by replacing the pair
(\pi,p)
with the new one
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
. Instead, the second term,
dissimilarity penalization
, disincentives a choice of the next pair that is too dissimilar from the current one. As expected, the multiplicative constant in front of the Wasserstein distance between the state-state transition kernels involves the Lipschitz constants of all the constitutive elements of the Conf-MDP, policy, and configuration.

6.2
Decoupled Bound

Despite its generality, the bound of Theorem
6.2
is hardly usable in practice because of the need for computing the Lipschitz semi-norm
\|A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\|_{L}
. Intuitively, this factor strictly depends on the similarity between the policy-configuration pairs
(\pi,p)
and
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
. Indeed, when
(\pi,p)=(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
a.s., we have that
A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(s)=0
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
and, consequently,
\|A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\|_{L}=0
. Unfortunately, obtaining a bound on this Lipschitz semi-norm that depends on some form of distance between the pairs
(\pi,p)
and
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
is technically challenging and may require additional assumptions. Thus, we defer this goal to future works, in this section, we focus on a simpler (and looser) bound that makes use of the LC condition only, accepting to bound
\|A_{\pi,p}^{\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime}}\|_{L}
with a constant independent from the distance between the pairs
(\pi,p)
and
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
. The following result provides such a bound.

Theorem 6.3

(Decoupled Bound for Lipschitz Conf-MDPs)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
L_{r}
-LC Conf-MDP, let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}
be two
L_{\pi},L_{\pi^{\prime}}
-LC policies respectively, and let
p,p^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}
be two
L_{p},L_{p^{\prime}}
-LC configurations. The performance improvement of policy-configuration pair
(\pi^{\prime},P^{\prime})
over
(\pi,P)
is lower bounded as:

\displaystyle J_{\pi^{\prime},{p}^{\prime}}-J_{\pi,{p}}\geq\frac{\mathbb{A}_{\pi,{p}}^{\pi^{\prime},{p}}+\mathbb{A}_{\pi,{p}}^{\pi,{p}^{\prime}}}{1-\gamma}-

\displaystyle\Bigg(c_{1}\int_{\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\pi(\mathrm{d}a|s)\mathcal{W}{\left(p^{\prime}(\cdot|s,a),p(\cdot|s,a)\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\mu_{\pi,p}(\mathrm{d}s)\mathcal{W}{\left(\pi^{\prime}(\cdot|s),\pi(\cdot|s)\right)}\Bigg),

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are constant whose values are made explicit in the proof and depend on
\gamma
,
L_{r}
,
L_{\pi}
,
L_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
L_{p}
, and
L_{p^{\prime}}
.

Some observations are in order. First, in order to obtain this result, we need to require that the Conf-MDP and both pairs
(\pi,p)
and
(\pi^{\prime},p^{\prime})
to be LC. Indeed, the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
will depend on the Lipschitz semi-norm of the Conf-MDP elements, and on the Lipschitz constants of both policies and both configurations. Second, Theorem
6.3
represents an decoupled bound, that separates the effects of the policy and the configuration. This is made evident in the advantage term too that is now replaced with the sum of the expected decoupled relative advantages. Third, and more important, the bound displays a linear dependence on the Wasserstein distance between the transition models and the policies. Compared to well-known bounds in the literature in which the square of the divergence is present (typically TV divergence), such as
Pirotta et al., 2013
, this represents a looser dependence.

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated the performance improvement bounds for Conf-MDP under Lipschitz regularity. We have first derived bounds on the Wasserstein distance between
\gamma
-discounted stationary distributions that generalize existing bounds in the literature. Then, we have provided two performance improvement lower bounds. Future promising works include the employment of the derived bounds for devising safe and trust region learning algorithms.

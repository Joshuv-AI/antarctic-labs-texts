The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/elliptic-integrable-systems.html

Abstract
We provide an explicit expression for the first order q q -difference system for the Jackson integral of symmetric Selberg type. The q q -difference system gives a generalization of q q -analog of contiguous relations for the Gauss hypergeometric function. As a basis of the system we use a set of the symmetric polynomials introduced by Matsuo in his study of the q q -KZ equation. Our main result is an explicit expression for the coefficient matrix of the q q -difference system in terms of its Gauss matrix decomposition. We introduce a class of symmetric polynomials called interpolation polynomials , which includes Matsuo’s polynomials. By repeated use of three-term relations among the interpolation polynomials we compute the coefficient matrix.

Introduction

The Gauss hypergeometric function

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,b\\
c\end{matrix};x\right)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(c-a)}\int_{0}^{1}z^{a-1}(1-z)^{c-a-1}(1-xz)^{-b}\,{\rm d}z,

where
\operatorname{Re}c>\operatorname{Re}a>0
and
|x|<1
,
satisfies the contiguous relations

_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,b\\
c\end{matrix};x\right)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,b+1\\
c+1\end{matrix};x\right)-x\frac{a(c-b)}{c(c+1)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a+1,b+1\\
c+2\end{matrix};x\right)

(1.1)

and

_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,b\\
c\end{matrix};x\right)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a+1,b\\
c+1\end{matrix};x\right)-x\frac{b(c-a)}{c(c+1)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a+1,b+1\\
c+2\end{matrix};x\right).

(1.2)

These contiguous relations for the Gauss hypergeometric function are extended to
a difference system for a function defined by multivariable integral
with respect to the Selberg type kernel
[
34
]

\Psi(z):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{\alpha-1}(1-z_{i})^{\beta-1}(x-z_{i})^{\gamma-1}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2\tau}.

For the integral

\langle e_{i}\rangle:=\int_{C}e_{i}(z)\Psi(z)\,{\rm d}z_{1}\cdots{\rm d}z_{n},\qquad i=0,1,\ldots,n,

where
e_{i}(z)
is the function specified by

e_{i}(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{n-i}(x-z_{j})\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}(1-z_{k})

and
C
is some suitable region, the
(n+1)
-tuple
(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle e_{n}\rangle)

satisfies the following difference system.
Let
\delta_{ij}
be the symbol of Kronecker’s delta.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

Let
T_{\alpha}
be the shift operator with respect to
\alpha\to\alpha+1
,
i.e.,
T_{\alpha}f(\alpha)=f(\alpha+1)

for an arbitrary function
f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
.
Then

T_{\alpha}(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle e_{n}\rangle)=(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle e_{n}\rangle)M,

(1.3)

where the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix
M
is written in terms of its Gauss matrix decomposition as

M=LDU=U^{\prime}D^{\prime}L^{\prime}.

(1.4)

Here
L=(l_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
,
D=(d_{j}\delta_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
,
U=(u_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
are
the lower triangular, diagonal, upper triangular matrices, respectively, given by

\displaystyle l_{ij}=(-x)^{i-j}{n-j\choose n-i}\frac{(\gamma+j\tau;\tau)_{i-j}}{(\alpha+\gamma+2j\tau;\tau)_{i-j}},

\displaystyle d_{j}=\frac{x^{j}(\alpha;\tau)_{j}(\alpha+\gamma+2j\tau;\tau)_{n-j}}{(\alpha+\gamma+(j-1)\tau;\tau)_{j}(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+(n+j-1)\tau;\tau)_{n-j}},

\displaystyle u_{ij}=(-1)^{j-i}{j\choose i}\frac{(\beta+(n-j)\tau;\tau)_{j-i}}{(\alpha+\gamma+2i\tau;\tau)_{j-i}},

and
U^{\prime}=(u^{\prime}_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
,
D^{\prime}=(d^{\prime}_{j}\delta_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
,
L^{\prime}=(l^{\prime}_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}

are the upper triangular, diagonal, lower triangular matrices,
respectively, given by

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{ij}=(-x^{-1})^{j-i}{j\choose i}\frac{(\beta+(n-j)\tau;\tau)_{j-i}}{(\alpha+\beta+2(n-j)\tau;\tau)_{j-i}},

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{x^{j}(\alpha+\beta+2(n-j)\tau;\tau)_{j}(\alpha;\tau)_{n-j}}{(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+(2n-j-1)\tau;\tau)_{j}(\alpha+\beta+(n-j-1)\tau;\tau)_{n-j}},

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{ij}=(-1)^{i-j}{n-j\choose n-i}\frac{(\gamma+j\tau;\tau)_{i-j}}{(\alpha+\beta+2(n-i)\tau;\tau)_{i-j}},

where
(x;\tau)_{0}:=1
and
(x;\tau)_{i}:=x(x+\tau)(x+2\tau)\cdots(x+(i-1)\tau)
for
i=1,2,\ldots
.

In particular, when
n=1
the system (
1.3
) is given by

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle)=(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle)\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha+\gamma&0\\
-x\gamma&x\alpha\end{array}\!\right)\begin{pmatrix}\alpha+\beta+\gamma&\beta\\
0&\alpha+\gamma\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle)=(\langle e_{0}\rangle,\langle e_{1}\rangle)\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\
0&x(\alpha+\beta)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha+\beta&0\\
\gamma&\alpha+\beta+\gamma\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

(1.5c)

The system (

system of three-term equations

(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)T_{\alpha}\langle e_{1}\rangle=-\beta\langle e_{0}\rangle+x(\alpha+\beta)\langle e_{1}\rangle,

(1.6)

and

\gamma T_{\alpha}\langle e_{1}\rangle+(\alpha+\beta)T_{\alpha}\langle e_{0}\rangle=\alpha\langle e_{0}\rangle.

(1.7)

Since, for

\langle e_{0}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{\alpha-1}(1-z)^{\beta-1}(x-z)^{\gamma}\,{\rm d}z\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\langle e_{1}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}z^{\alpha-1}(1-z)^{\beta}(x-z)^{\gamma-1}\,{\rm d}z,

under the conditions
\operatorname{Re}\alpha>0

\operatorname{Re}\beta>0

1.6

) and (

can be regarded as a natural extension of the

) and (

For an arbitrary

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\big(1-c^{-1}x\big)\big(1-c^{-2}x\big)\cdots\big(1-c^{i}x\big)}&\mbox{if}\quad i=-1,-2,\ldots,\end{cases}

Throughout this paper we fix

For a point

Let

if

\lambda_{k}<\mu_{k}

z_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}z_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots z_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}

For

For

f_{r}(a_{1},a_{2};t;z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\[1.0pt]

r

, and

f_{0}(a_{1},a_{2};t;z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}-a_{1}}{a_{2}t^{i-1}-a_{1}},\qquad f_{n}(a_{1},a_{2};t;z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}-a_{2}}{a_{1}t^{i-1}-a_{2}}.

and

.
By definition the polynomial

f_{i}(a_{1},a_{2};t;z)=f_{n-i}(a_{2},a_{1};t;z).

(6.4)

When we need to specify the number of variables

Lemma 6.2

Proof.

The lemma follows from a direct computation and we omit the detail.

Lemma 6.5

(triangularity)
.

Suppose that

\xi_{j}(a_{1}):=\big(\underbrace{a_{1},a_{1}t,\ldots,a_{1}t^{j-1}\phantom{\Big|}\!\!}_{j},z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n-j}\big)\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}.

(6.7)

Moreover,
f_{i}(a_{1},a_{2};t;\xi_{i}(a_{1}))
evaluates as

f_{i}(a_{1},a_{2};t;\xi_{i}(a_{1}))=\prod_{l=1}^{n-i}\frac{z_{l}-a_{1}t^{i}}{a_{2}t^{l-1}-a_{1}t^{i}}=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{n-i}\big(1-z_{l}a_{1}^{-1}t^{-i}\big)}{\big(a_{2}a_{1}^{-1}t^{-i};t\big)_{n-i}}.

(6.8)

On the other hand, suppose that

\eta_{j}(a_{2}):=\big(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{j},\underbrace{a_{2},a_{2}t,\ldots,a_{2}t^{n-j-1}\phantom{\Big|}\!\!}_{n-j}\big)\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}.

, then

(6.9)

evaluates as

Proof.

First we show (
6.9
) by induction on
n
.
For simplicity we write
\eta_{i}(a_{2})
as

Suppose

\displaystyle f_{i}^{(n)}(a_{1},a_{2};t;\eta_{j})=\frac{a_{2}t^{n-j-1}-a_{2}t^{n-i}}{a_{1}t^{i-1}-a_{2}t^{n-i}}f_{i-1}^{(n-1)}\big(a_{1},a_{2};t;\eta_{j}^{(n-1)}\big)

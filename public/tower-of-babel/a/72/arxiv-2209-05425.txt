Frobenius Non-Stability of Nilpotent Groups
Forrest Glebe

Abstract
. A countable discrete group is said to be Frobenius stable if every function from the group to unitary matrices that is “almost multiplicative” in the Frobenius norm is “close” to a unitary representation in the Frobenius norm. The purpose of this paper is to show that finitely generated nilpotent groups that are not virtually cyclic are not Frobenius stable. Our argument proves the same result for other unnormalized Schatten p p -norms with 1 < p ≤ ∞ 1<p\leq\infty .



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
||\cdot||_{p}
denote the unnormalized Schatten
p
-norm,
||M||_{p}=(\Tr((M^{*}M)^{p/2}))^{1/p}
for
p<\infty
and operator norm for
p=\infty
. Let
U(k_{n})
be the
k_{n}\times k_{n}
complex unitary group. A
p
-asymptotic homomorphism is a sequence of functions
\rho_{n}:\Gamma\rightarrow U(k_{n})
so that for all
x,y\in\Gamma
we have
||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||_{p}\rightarrow 0
. We say that a
p
-asymptotic representation is
p
-perturbable to a genuine representation
if there is a sequence of unitary representations
\tilde{\rho}_{n}:\Gamma\rightarrow U(k_{n})
so that for all
x\in\Gamma
we have
||\tilde{\rho}_{n}(x)-\rho_{n}(x)||_{p}\rightarrow 0
. We say
\Gamma
is
p
-stable
if every
p
-asymptotic representation is
p
-perturbable to a genuine representation. Of particular interest is the
p=2
case, called
Frobenius stability
, and the
p=\infty
case, called
matricial stability
. Frobenius stability was first introduced, by Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky, and Thom in
[
7
]
. The main purpose of this paper is to prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated nilpotent group and let
1<p\leq\infty
. Then
\Gamma
is
p
-stable if and only if
\Gamma
is virtually cyclic.

The proof is at the end of Section 4 of the paper. The fact that being virtually cyclic is sufficient follows from a result in
[
7
]
, see Lemma
2.19
for an explanation.

For the torsion-free case at
p=\infty
our result follows from a more general result of Dadarlat in
[
5
]
. Combining this with an argument due to Bader, Lubotzky, Sauer, and Weinberger in
[
1
]
one can deduce our result in the
p=\infty
case with torsion as well. See the proof of Theorem
1.1
for more details.

Our result, applied to the
p=2
case, is the last of four commonly studied “stability properties” to be determined for finitely generated nilpotent groups. One such notion is
permutation stability
. Permutation stability is defined similarly to
p
-stability, but the unitary group is replaced with
S_{n}
, the symmetric group on
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and the norm metric is replaced with the normalized Hamming distance,
d_{n}(\sigma,\tau)=\frac{1}{n}|\{x\in[n]|\sigma(x)\neq\tau(x)\}|
. In
[
3
]
, Becker, Lubotzky, and Thom show that virtually polycyclic groups, and hence finitely generated nilpotent groups are permutation stable.

Another notion of stability is
Hilbert-Schmidt stability
defined similarly to
p
-stability, but the norm used is the normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm,
||M||_{\mbox{HS}}=\sqrt{\tr(M^{*}M)}=||M||_{2}/\sqrt{k}
where
\tr
denotes the normalized trace and
k
is the number of columns in
M
. It has recently been shown by Levit and Vigdorovich
[
18
]
and independently by Eckhardt and Shulman
[
8
]
that finitely generated nilpotent groups groups are Hilbert-Schmidt stable. Indeed the result of Levit and Vigdorovich shows Hilbert-Shmidt stability for finitely generated virtually nilpotent groups.

To summarize it is now known whether or not finitely generated nilpotent groups are stable in all four of the most studied ways; they are all permutation stable
[
3
]
and Hilbert-Schmidt stable
[
18
]
[
8
]
, while virtually cyclic groups are the only such groups to be matricially stable
[
5
]
or Frobenius stable (Theorem
1.1
).

In
[
21
]
Voiculescu showed that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is not matricially stable (though the terminology had not yet been invented) by constructing an explicit sequence of pairs of unitaries that commute asymptotically in the operator norm, but remain far, in the operator norm, from pairs of unitaries that commute. By the well-known bounds
||M||\leq||M||_{p}\leq k^{1/p}||M||
, where
||\cdot||
is the operator norm, this sequence also happens to show
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is not
p
-stable, for
p>1
. In
[
9
]
Eilers, Shulman, and Sørensen, give explicit the operator norm asymptotic representations that are not perturbable, in the operator norm, to genuine representations for non-cyclic torsion-free finitely generated 2-step nilpotent groups. While they do not explicitly mention the Frobenius norm or other
p
-norms, their example can easily be seen to be a
p
-asymptotic representation, for
1<p\leq\infty
, and thus proves that these groups are not
p
-stable for the same reason.

As mentioned, it is proved in
[
5
]
that torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent groups, other than
\mathbb{Z}
and the trivial group are not stable in operator norm. In
[
12
]
an explicit formula, in terms of cohomological data, of an operator norm asymptotic representation, that cannot be perturbed, in the operator norm, to a genuine representation, is given for a class of groups that contains all non-cyclic torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent groups. Unfortunately, this formula is not asymptotically multiplicative in the Frobenius norm. In this paper we modify the construction to get asymptotic multiplicativity in Frobenius norm and other Schatten
p
-norms.

To be more explicit, for our formula and the one in
[
12
]
the operator norm defect to being multiplicative,
||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||
, is bounded by
2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|/n
where
\sigma
is a 2-cocycle on the group (see Proposition
4.3
). In
[
12
]
this acts on an
n^{m}
dimensional vector space, where
m
is the Hirsch length of the group, so this does not imply asymptotic multiplicativity in the Frobenius norm. In this paper the dimension of the space that
\rho_{n}
acts on is reduced to
n
which implies that the defect to being multiplicative in the Frobenius norm is bounded above by
2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|/\sqrt{n}
. In general the defect to being multiplicative in an unnormalized Schatten
p
-norm, for
p<\infty
, is
2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|n^{\frac{1}{p}-1}
, which goes to zero for
p>1
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 has relevant background information. Section 3 introduces a condition on a cohomology class (Definition
3.1
) and proves that all non-cyclic torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent groups have a cohomology class of a this form that is non-torsion (Theorem
3.6
). In Section 4 we explain why this implies the main result by modifying arguments from
[
12
]
. First we prove the main result in the torsion-free case by making a formula for a
p
-asymptotic representation (Proposition
4.1
) in terms of a cocycle in the relevant cohomology class then showing it cannot be perturbed to a genuine representation (Theorem
4.4
). Finally, at the end of Section 4 we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
, by reducing to the torsion-free case.

2.
Background Information

2.1.
Notation

We will use
e
as the identity of a multiplicative group, and
0
for the identity of
\mathbb{Z}
. This is to avoid confusion with
1
denoting the generator of the group
\mathbb{Z}
. We will also use
e
for the trivial group in commutative diagrams.

Throughout the paper
||\cdot||_{p}
will denote the unnormalized Schatten
p
-norm, and
||\cdot||
will denote the operator norm, but in some circumstances we will write
||\cdot||_{p}
and allow for the case
p=\infty
to consider operator norm simultaneously with other norms.

We will use
U(n)
to denote the
n\times n
complex unitary group.

When we have a nested (semi)-direct product we will use coordinates to describe elements. For example if
\Gamma
is an arbitrary discrete group then a typical element of
(\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma)\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
is written as
(x,y,z)
with
x,z\in\mathbb{Z}
and
y\in\Gamma
. The identity of this group could be written as
(0,e,0)
. When writing a semi-direct product
\Gamma\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
we will simply specify an element
\gamma\in\Aut(\Gamma)
so that the action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\Gamma
is determined by
1\mapsto\gamma
.

2.2.
Group (Co)Homology

There are many ways to characterize group homology and cohomology, but to us the most useful will be to describe them as the homology and cohomology of an explicit chain complex described below. We will only use homology and cohomology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
and the trivial action in this paper. For more about this construction see
[
4
, Chapter II.3]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group. For
n>0
we define
C_{n}(\Gamma)
to be the free abelian group generated by elements of
\Gamma^{n}
, and define
C_{0}(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Z}
. We may write an element of
\Gamma^{n}
as
[a_{1}|a_{2}|\cdots|a_{n}]
with
a_{i}\in\Gamma
. We thus write a typical element of
C_{n}(\Gamma)
as

c=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}[a_{i1}|a_{i2}|\cdots|a_{in}]

with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{ij}\in\Gamma
. We define the boundary map
\partial_{n}
from
C_{n}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C_{n-1}(\Gamma)
by

\partial_{n}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n}]=

[a_{2}|\cdots|a_{n}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{i-1}|a_{i}a_{i+1}|a_{i+2}|\cdots|a_{n}]+(-1)^{n}[a_{1}|\cdots|a_{n-1}]

for
n>1
and
\partial_{1}=0
. Often we will just write
\partial
where the domain is clear from context. The group
homology
of
\Gamma
is the homology group the chain complex
(C_{\bullet}(\Gamma),\partial_{\bullet})
.

For readers who are less familiar with homological algebra we will be more explicit. It happens that
\im(\partial_{n})\subseteq\ker(\partial_{n+1})
so we define the
n
th homology group of
\Gamma
to be

H_{n}(\Gamma):=\ker(\partial_{n})/\im(\partial_{n-1}).

An element of
\ker\partial_{n}
is called an
n
-cycle
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
cohomology of
\Gamma
is the cohomology of
(C_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
.

We will again be more explicit for readers who are less familiar with homological algebra. We use the notation

C^{n}(\Gamma):=\Hom(C_{n}(\Gamma),\mathbb{Z})

and note that this is isomorphic to the group of functions from
\Gamma^{n}
to
\mathbb{Z}
for
n>0
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
for
n=0
. Then we define
\partial^{n}:C^{n}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(\Gamma)
to be the adjoint
1
1

1

Actually
[
4
]
defines the coboundary map to be
(-1)^{n+1}

of
\partial_{n+1}
. This gives us

(\partial^{n}\sigma)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=

\sigma(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}a_{i+1},a_{i+2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

for
n>0
and
\partial^{0}=0
. Then we define

H^{n}(\Gamma):=\ker(\partial^{n})/\im(\partial^{n+1}).

An element of
\ker(\partial^{n})
is called an
n
-cocycle
. If
\sigma
is an
n
-cocycle then we denote its cohomology class as
[\sigma]
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say an
n
-cocycle
\sigma
is
normalized
if for each
i
we have that
a_{i}=e
implies that
\sigma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=0
.

As with homology we will suppress the
n
in
\partial^{n}
if the domain is obvious from context.

Suppose that
f
is a group homomorphism from
\Gamma_{1}
to
\Gamma_{2}
. This induces a map
f_{*}:H_{n}(\Gamma_{1})\rightarrow H_{n}(\Gamma_{2})
defined by the formula

\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}[a_{i1}|\cdots|a_{in}]\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}[f(a_{i1})|\cdots|f(a_{in})].

Similarly there is a map from
f^{*}:H^{n}(\Gamma_{2})\rightarrow H^{n}(\Gamma_{1})
defined by

(f^{*}(\sigma))(a_{1},\ldots a_{n})=\sigma(f(a_{1}),\ldots,f(a_{n})).

Both maps are functorial.

In general cohomology is a bifunctor by allowing “coefficients” in an arbitrary abelian group with a
\Gamma
-action. For us that will mean that if
f:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is a group homomorphism this induces a map
f_{*}:H^{n}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{n}(\Gamma)
. It must be the case that
f
is multiplication by some integer
k
, and it happens that
f_{*}
is also multiplication by
k
.

Because
C^{n}(\Gamma)
is isomorphic to
\Hom(C_{n}(\Gamma),\mathbb{Z})
there is a natural bilinear map from
C^{n}(\Gamma)\times C_{n}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\left\langle\sigma,\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}[a_{i1}|\cdots|a_{in}]\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}\sigma(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{in}).

This descends to a well-defined bilinear map from
H^{n}(\Gamma)\times H_{n}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. We will use the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for both maps. The maps
f_{*}
and
f^{*}
are adjoint in the following sense

\langle f^{*}(\sigma),c\rangle=\langle\sigma,f_{*}(c)\rangle.

2.3.
Torsion-Free Finitely Generated Nilpotent Groups

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group.

Definition 2.7
.

A
Mal’cev basis
for
\Gamma
is an
m
-tuple of elements,
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\Gamma^{m}
which satisfies the following conditions

•

For all
g\in\Gamma
,
g
can be written uniquely as
g=a_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots a_{m}^{x_{m}}
for some
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. We call this presentation the
canonical form
of
g
.

•

The subgroups
\Gamma_{i}=\langle a_{i},\ldots,a_{m}\rangle
form a central series for
\Gamma
.

Every finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group has a Mal’cev basis by
[
16
, Lemma 8.23]
. When working with a torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group with a specified Mal’cev basis we will treat elements of the group as
m
-tuples of integers
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
where the
x_{i}
denote the exponents in the canonical form for
x
.

Definition 2.8
.

The cardinality of the Mal’cev basis is called the
Hirsch length
of the group.

This is independent of the choice of Mal’cev basis
[
20
, Exercise 8]
.

In order to extend this definition to finitely generated nilpotent groups with torsion we use the following well-known result.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
10
, Lemma 8.2.2]

If
\Gamma
is a nilpotent group, and
T
is the set of torsion elements, then
T
is a finite normal subgroup of
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma/T
is torsion-free.

Definition 2.10
.

If
\Gamma
is a finitely generated nilpotent group, and
T
is its torsion-subgroup then we define the
Hirsch Length
of
\Gamma
to be the Hirsch length of
\Gamma/T
, defined in Definition
2.8
.

This coincides with the definition of Hirsch length defined more generally in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated nilpotent group with Hirsch length at most 1. Then
\Gamma
is virtually cyclic.

Proof.

There must be a torsion-free subgroup
N\leq\Gamma
that is finite-index and torsion-free by
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
. Since the Hirsch length
\Gamma
is at most 1, the Hirsch length of
N
is also at most 1 by
[
14
, Theorem 1b]
so either
N\cong\mathbb{Z}
or
N=\{e\}
, which implies
\Gamma
is virtually cyclic.
∎

2.4.
The Winding Number Argument

The classical example due to Voiculescu in
[
21
]
for an asymptotic representation (in either operator or Frobenius norm) of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that is not perturbable (in either norm) to a sequence of genuine representations comes in the form:

\rho_{n}(a,b)=u_{n}^{a}v_{n}^{b}

where
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
are
n\times n
matrices such that

u_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&0&1\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&1&0\end{bmatrix}\mbox{ and }v_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\exp\left(\frac{4\pi i}{n}\right)&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\exp\left(\frac{6\pi i}{n}\right)&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}.

It can be computed that
u_{n}v_{n}u_{n}^{-1}v_{n}^{-1}=\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi i}{n}\right)\id_{n}
. Thus we see that for
1\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle||\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)-\rho_{n}(x+y)||_{p}

\displaystyle=||\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)\rho_{n}(x+y)^{-1}-\id_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}||_{p}

\displaystyle=||u_{n}^{x_{1}}v_{n}^{x_{2}}u_{n}^{y_{1}}v_{n}^{y_{2}}v_{n}^{-x_{2}-y_{2}}u_{n}^{-x_{1}-y_{1}}-\id_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}||_{p}

\displaystyle=||u_{n}^{x_{1}}(v_{n}^{x_{2}}u_{n}^{y_{1}}v_{n}^{-x_{2}}u_{n}^{-y_{1}})u_{n}^{-x_{1}}-\id_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}||_{p}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ix_{2}y_{1}}{n}\right)-1\right)\id_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\right|\right|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi|x_{2}y_{1}|n^{\frac{1}{p}-1}.

Voiculescu shows that
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
cannot be arbitrarily close to a pair of unitaries that genuinely commute with each other, in the operator norm. Because the operator norm is smaller than any other unnormalized Schatten
p
-norm they cannot be close to commuting matrices in these norms either.

The argument we will use to show non-perturbability in this paper has its roots in the “winding number argument” first discovered by Kazhdan in
[
17
]
and independently used by Exel and Loring in
[
11
]
that
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
are far from any pair of matrices that commute. A more general statement of this argument can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 3.9]
or in
[
6
]
. We will use a more homological version of the result described below.

Definition 2.16
.

If

c=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}[a_{j}|b_{j}]\in C_{2}(\Gamma)

and
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gl_{n}(\mathbb{C})
so that for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}

||\rho(a_{j}b_{j})\rho(a_{j})^{-1}\rho(b_{j})^{-1}-\id_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}||<1

we define

\langle\rho,c\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}\Tr(\log(\rho(a_{j}b_{j})\rho(b_{j})^{-1}\rho(a_{j})^{-1}))

where
\log
is defined as a power series centered at 1.

If
\partial c=0
we have that
\langle\rho,c\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}

[
12
, Proposition 3.4]
. The version of the winding number argument we are using is as follows.

Theorem 2.17
.

[
12
, Theorem 3.7]

If
\rho_{0}
is a (not necessarily unitary) representation of
\Gamma
,
\rho_{1}
is a function from
\Gamma
to
U(n)
,

c=\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}[a_{j}|b_{j}]

is a 2-cycle on
\Gamma
, and

||\rho_{1}(g)-\rho_{0}(g)||<\frac{1}{24}

for all
g\in\{a_{j},b_{j},a_{j}b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
then
\rho_{1}
is multiplicative enough for
\langle\rho_{1},c\rangle
to be defined and
\langle\rho_{1},c\rangle=0
.

2.5.
p
-Stability Results

The following result is essentially
[
1
, Lemma 2.13]
. They state the result only for Frobenius norm, but their argument holds for other unnormalized Schatten
p
-norms, including operator norm. We include a proof for the sake of completeness and to highlight the construction of our asymptotic representation.

Lemma 2.18
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a countable discrete group and
N
is a finite normal subgroup. Let
q:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma/N
be the quotient map and let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. If
\varphi_{n}
is a
p
-asymptotic representation of
\Gamma/N
then
\varphi_{n}\circ q
is a
p
-asymptotic representation of
\Gamma
; if
\varphi_{n}\circ q
is
p
-perturbable to a genuine representation so is
\varphi_{n}
. In particular, if
\Gamma
is
p
-stable then
\Gamma/N
is
p
-stable.

Proof.

Clearly
\varphi_{n}\circ q
is a
p
-asymptotic homomorphism on
\Gamma
. Suppose now that
\varphi\circ q
is
p
-perturbable to a genuine representation. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\varphi_{n}(e)=1
. By assumption, there is a sequence of group homomorphisms
\psi_{n}:\Gamma\rightarrow U(k_{n})
so that for all
x\in\Gamma
we have
||\psi_{n}(x)-\varphi_{n}(q(x))||_{p}\rightarrow 0
. Now pick
\varepsilon>0
so that for each nontrivial irreducible representation
\alpha
of
N
we have that
\max_{x\in N}||\alpha(x)-1||_{p}>\varepsilon
. Then for large enough
n
, we have that for all
x\in N

||\psi_{n}(x)-1||_{p}=||\psi_{n}(x)-\varphi_{n}(q(x))||_{p}<\varepsilon.

If
\alpha
is any irreducible subrepresentation of
\psi_{n}|_{N}
, for all
x\in N
we have

||\alpha(x)-1||_{p}\leq||\psi_{n}(x)-1||_{p}<\varepsilon.

It follows that every irreducible subrepresentation of
\psi_{n}|_{N}
is trivial so
N\leq\ker(\psi_{n})
. Thus
\psi_{n}
factors through
\Gamma/N
and
\varphi_{n}
is
p
-perturbable to a genuine representation.
∎

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated virtually free group and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
\Gamma
is
p
-stable.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Remark 5.2]
if the 2-cohomology of
\Gamma
with coefficients in a class of Banach spaces with a
\Gamma
action vanishes then
\Gamma
is
p
-stable. Since the rational cohomological dimension of virtually free groups is 1 it follows from this that they must be
p
-stable.
∎

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id_{\Gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{k\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{k\sigma}}

Proof.

The map
\bullet k
from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
induces a map from
H^{2}(\Gamma)\rightarrow H^{2}(\Gamma)
. Checking the definition of the bifunctor it is clear that this map must be multiplication by
k
so it takes
[\sigma]
to
k[\sigma]
. By the central extension view of bifunctoriality
[
4
, Chapter IV.3 Exercise 1]
it must take the class corresponding to the unique extension of
\Gamma
fitting into the bottom row of the commutative diagram in the statement of the Lemma.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free, finitely generated nilpotent group with Hirsch length at least 2 and let
\alpha\in\Hom(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
be the canonical homomorphism with respect to some Mal’cev basis of
\Gamma
. Then there is a cohomology class
[\sigma]\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
that is skinny with respect to
\alpha
and non-torsion.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on the Hirsch length. For the base case the only torsion-free nilpotent group with Hirsch length 2 (up to isomorphism) is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The two elements of the Mal’cev basis must span
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
we may assume without loss of generality that our Mal’cev basis is just the standard generators. Then the cohomology class of
\sigma(x,y)=x_{2}y_{1}
is clearly skinny with respect to the canonical homomorphism
\alpha(x)=x_{1}
. Moreover, this is not torsion because it pairs non-trivially with the homology class of

[(0,1)|(1,0)]-[(1,0)|(0,1)].

For the inductive step we will assume the theorem for
\Gamma
and show it for
\Gamma_{\omega}
for all
[\omega]\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
. This is valid since any torsion-free finitely generated nilpotent group
\Lambda
, with Mal’cev basis
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
, is the middle group in some central extension of
\Lambda/\langle a_{m}\rangle
and the canonical homomorphism for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
is the composition of the quotient map with the canonical homomorphism of a Mal’cev basis of
\Lambda/\langle a_{m}\rangle
, by Proposition
2.15
.

Let
[\sigma]\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
be a non-torsion cohomology class that is skinny with respect to the canonical homomorphism
\alpha
.

Case 1
:
\varphi_{\omega}^{*}([\sigma])
is non-torsion. In this case the result follows from Lemma
3.3
.

Case 2:

\varphi_{\omega}^{*}([\sigma])
is torsion. In this case we use the fact that
\ker(\varphi_{\omega}^{*})
is spanned by
[\omega]
, by Proposition
2.6
. Thus we conclude that
k[\omega]=j[\sigma]
where
j
and
k
are nonzero integers. Let
\psi:\Gamma_{\omega}\rightarrow\Gamma_{k\omega}
be the map from Lemma
3.5
. Note that
j[\sigma]
is also a skinny cohomology class. Since
\Gamma_{k\omega}\cong\Gamma_{j\sigma}
we may let
[\gamma]\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{k\omega})
be the cohomology class constructed in Lemma
3.4
. Let
b\in\Gamma_{\omega}
be an element so that
\alpha(\varphi_{\omega}(b))=1
. Then let
a=\psi(b)
. Note that
\varphi_{k\omega}(a)=\varphi_{\omega}(b)
by the commutative diagram in Lemma
3.5
. Using the diagram again we get that
\psi(\iota_{\omega}(1))=\iota_{k\omega}(k)
. From Lemma
3.4
we have that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\langle\gamma,[a|\iota_{k\omega}(k)]-[\iota_{k\omega}(k)|a]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\gamma,[\psi(b)|\psi(\iota_{\omega}(1))]-[\psi(\iota_{\omega}(1))|\psi(b)]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\gamma,\psi_{*}([b|\iota_{\omega}(1)]-[\iota_{\omega}(1)|b])\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi^{*}(\gamma),[b|\iota_{\omega}(1)]-[\iota_{\omega}(1)|b]\rangle.

Since the pairing on the last line is nonzero we have that
\psi^{*}([\gamma])
cannot be torsion. Moreover by Lemma
3.3
we have that
\psi^{*}([\gamma])
is skinny with respect to the homomorphism
\alpha\circ\varphi_{k\omega}\circ\psi=\alpha\circ\varphi_{\omega}
.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free finitely, generated nilpotent group with a specified Mal’cev basis, and let
[\sigma]
be a cohomology class that is skinny with respect to the canonical homomorphism. By Proposition
3.2
there is representative
\sigma
of
[\sigma]
so that
\sigma(x,y)=q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1})
for
q\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1}]
. Then for
n
co-prime to the denominators of coefficients of
q
there is a well-defined formula
\rho_{n}:\Gamma\rightarrow U(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}))
defined by

\rho_{n}(x)\delta_{\bar{j}}=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},j)\right)\delta_{\bar{j}+\bar{x}_{1}}.

Here
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\bar{j}
represents its reduction mod
n
and
\delta_{\bar{j}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
is the standard basis element corresponding to
\bar{j}
.

Proof.

This is a modification of the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 3.17]
that is pointed out in
[
12
, Remark 3.18]
.

We will show that
\rho_{n}
is well-defined. This is because if
\bar{j}=\bar{k}
then
q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},j)\equiv q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},k)\mod n
so because the exponential depends only on the reduction of
q
mod
n
the formula is well-defined. This is a unitary because it takes an orthonormal basis to another orthonormal basis.
∎

This does not define
\rho_{n}
for all
n
, but it does define
\rho_{n}
for infinitely many
n
. This is sufficient to prove that a group is not
p
-stable, because we may pick a sequence of integers
n_{j}
enumerating the
n
for which
\rho_{n}
is well-defined.

Lemma 4.2
.

Define

\chi_{n}(x,y)=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\sigma(x,y)\right)

The formula
\rho_{n}
defined in Proposition
4.1
obeys the equation

\rho_{n}(xy)\rho_{n}(y)^{-1}\rho_{n}(x)^{-1}=\chi_{n}(x,y)^{-1}\id_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})}.

Proof.

This is a modification of the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 3.19]
.

Note that since
\partial\sigma=0
we have that for all
x,y,z\in\Gamma

-\sigma(x,yz)+\sigma(xy,z)-\sigma(y,z)=-\sigma(x,y).

Exponentiating both sides we get

\chi_{n}(x,yz)^{-1}\chi_{n}(xy,z)\chi_{n}(y,z)^{-1}=\chi_{n}(x,y)^{-1}.

Next we claim that

\rho_{n}(x)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}}=\chi_{n}(x,x^{-1}y)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}}.

To check this it suffices to compute that

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x){\chi}_{n}(x,x^{-1}y)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}}

\displaystyle={\chi}_{n}(x,x^{-1}y)^{-1}\rho_{n}(x)\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}}

\displaystyle={\chi}_{n}(x,x^{-1}y)^{-1}{\chi}_{n}(x,x^{-1}y)\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\bar{y}_{1}}.

Combining these we get

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(xy)\rho_{n}(y)^{-1}\rho_{n}(x)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{z}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}(xy)\rho_{n}(y)^{-1}\chi_{n}(x,x^{-1}z)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{n}(xy)\chi_{n}(y,y^{-1}x^{-1}z)^{-1}\chi_{n}(x,x^{-1}z)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{y}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}(xy,y^{-1}x^{-1}z)\chi_{n}(y,y^{-1}x^{-1}z)^{-1}\chi_{n}(x,x^{-1}z)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{z}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n}(x,y)^{-1}\delta_{\bar{z}_{1}}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma
,
\sigma
and
\rho_{n}
be defined as in Proposition
4.1
; let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then the following bounds hold:

\displaystyle||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|}{n}

\displaystyle||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|n^{\frac{1}{p}-1}.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||

\displaystyle=||\rho_{n}(xy)\rho_{n}(y)^{-1}\rho_{n}(x)^{-1}-\id_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})}||

\displaystyle=||(\chi_{n}(x,y)^{-1}-1)\id_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})}||

\displaystyle=|\chi_{n}(x,y)^{-1}-1|

\displaystyle=\left|\exp\left(\frac{-2\pi i\sigma(x,y)}{n}\right)-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|}{n}.

For the Schatten
p
-norm note that

\displaystyle||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq||\rho_{n}(xy)-\rho_{n}(x)\rho_{n}(y)||n^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi|\sigma(x,y)|n^{\frac{1}{p}-1}.

∎

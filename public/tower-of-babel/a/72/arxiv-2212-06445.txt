Trees meet octahedron comparison
Nina Lebedeva and Anton Petrunin

Abstract
We show that trees and their products meet the octahedron comparison.



Let us recall the notion of graph comparison introduced in
[
8
]
.
Suppose
\Gamma
is a graph with vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
.

A metric space
X
meets the
\Gamma
-comparison
if for any set of points in
X
labeled by vertices of
\Gamma
there is a model configuration
\tilde{v}_{1},\dots,\tilde{v}_{n}
in the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
such that
if
v_{j}
is adjacent to
v_{j}
, then

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{i}-\tilde{v}_{j}|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leqslant|v_{i}-v_{j}|_{X},

otherwise

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{i}-\tilde{v}_{j}|_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\geqslant|v_{i}-v_{j}|_{X};

here
|p-q|_{M}
denotes the distance between points
p
and
q
in the metric space
M
.

Let us denote by
O_{3}
the graph of the octahedron.
For the labeling shown on the diagram,
O_{3}
-comparison means that any six-point configuration
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime},z,z^{\prime}\in X
can be mapped to
\mathbb{H}
so that the diagonals
xx^{\prime}
,
yy^{\prime}
, and
zz^{\prime}
are not getting shorter
and the edges are not getting longer.

Note that the 4-cycle
C_{4}
is an induced subgraph of
O_{3}
.
Therefore,
O_{3}
-comparison implies
C_{4}
-comparison
;
the latter is one of equivalent ways to describe the
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
-comparison.
In general,
C_{4}
-comparison does not imply
O_{3}
-comparison,
but the following question remains open.
It was motivated by
[
3
, 1.19
{}_{+}(e)
]
.

Question.

Is it true that
O_{3}
-comparison holds in any complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space?

An affirmative answer should lead to a classification of six-point spaces that admit an isometric embedding in a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space.
On the other hand, it follows from the next theorem,
that a counterexample would mean that
O_{3}
-comparison is something new and interesting;
so, both answers are good.

Recall that a geodesic space
T
is called a
metric tree
if any pair of points in
T
are connected by a unique geodesic,
and the union of any two geodesics
[xy]
, and
[yz]
contain the geodesic
[xz]
.
Since metric trees and their products are
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, the following theorem gives a partial answer two the question.

Theorem.

The
O_{3}
-comparison holds in products of metric trees.

Let us list the most related results.

\diamond

The so-called
(4+2)-point comparison
is another six-point comparison;
it holds in any complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space
[
1
,
2
]
.

\diamond

There are examples of six-point metric space that meets the (4+2)-point comparison, but does not admit a distance-preserving embedding into a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(0)

(see the discussion right after 7.2 in the updated arXiv version of
[
1
]
).
The
O_{3}
-comparison might be a sufficient condition for existence of such embedding.

\diamond

If a five-point space satisfies
C_{4}
-comparison, then it is isometric to a subset in a complete length
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space

[
11
,
5
]
.

\diamond

C_{4}
-comparison implies
C_{n}
-comparison for any
n
,
here
C_{n}
denotes the
n
-cycle and the intrinsic metric is not required
[
12
]
.

For other related questions, see
[
10
,
5
,
8
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
]
and the references therein.

Proof.

First, let us show that
O_{3}
-comparison holds for any metric tree
T
.

Consider a six-point configuration
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
in a tree labeled by the vertices of
O_{3}
on the diagram.
We can assume that the union of geodesics
[xx^{\prime}]
,
[yy^{\prime}]
, and
[zz^{\prime}]
is connected.
Indeed, suppose
[xx^{\prime}]
does not intersect
[yy^{\prime}]
, and
[zz^{\prime}]
.
Then we can shrink to a point a geodesic
\gamma
that minimizes the distance from
[xx^{\prime}]
to
[yy^{\prime}]\cup[zz^{\prime}]
;
in the obtained tree
T^{\prime}
the distances
|x-x^{\prime}|
,
|y-y^{\prime}|
and
|z-z^{\prime}|
remain the same and the remaining distances between
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
cannot get longer, so any model configuration for the six points in
T^{\prime}
works for the original configuration in
T
.

Let

Y=([xx^{\prime}]\cap[yy^{\prime}])\cup([yy^{\prime}]\cap[zz^{\prime}])\cup([zz^{\prime}]\cap[xx^{\prime}]).

Note that we are in one of the following two cases:

1.

The set
Y
is a
tripod
; that is,
Y
is a union of three geodesics meeting at one point, say
o
.

2.

Y
lies in one of the geodesics
[xx^{\prime}]
,
[yy^{\prime}]
, or
[zz^{\prime}]
.

Case 1.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that geodesics
[xx^{\prime}]
,
[yy^{\prime}]
, and
[zz^{\prime}]
intersect as on the diagram (possibly with some degenerate edges).

Note that we can assume that in each pair
([ox],[oy^{\prime}])
,
([oy],[oz^{\prime}])
, and
([oz],[ox^{\prime}])
one of the geodesics lies in the other.
Indeed, assume it is not true, say
[ox]\not\subset[oy^{\prime}]
and
|o-x|_{T}\leqslant|o-y^{\prime}|_{T}
.
Then moving
x
to a point on
[oy^{\prime}]
on the distance
|o-x|_{T}
from
o
will decrease the distance
|x-y^{\prime}|_{T}
and the remaining fourteen distances between
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
remain the same.
Therefore any model configuration for the new position of
x
works for the old position.

If
[ox]\subset[oy^{\prime}]
, then define
a=x
, otherwise
a=y^{\prime}
.
Similarly, chose
b
from
y
or
z^{\prime}
and
c
from
z
or
x^{\prime}
.
Consider model triangle
[\tilde{a}\tilde{b}\tilde{c}]
of
[abc]
.

Note that
[ab]\subset[yy^{\prime}]
,
[bc]\subset[zz^{\prime}]
, and
[ac]\subset[xx^{\prime}]
.
Therefore we may choose points
\tilde{x}
,
\tilde{x}^{\prime}
on the line
\tilde{a}\tilde{c}
so that the map
x\mapsto\tilde{x}
,
x^{\prime}\mapsto\tilde{x}^{\prime}
,
a\mapsto\tilde{a}
,
c\mapsto\tilde{c}
is distance-preserving.
(The quadruple
\tilde{x}
,
\tilde{x}^{\prime}
,
\tilde{a}
,
\tilde{c}
might have a identical points.)
Similarly, we may choose
\tilde{y}
,
\tilde{y}^{\prime}
on the line
\tilde{a}\tilde{b}
and
\tilde{z}
,
\tilde{z}^{\prime}
on the line
\tilde{b}\tilde{c}
.
A couple of possible configurations are shown on the diagram.

The triangle inequality implies that the obtained configuration meets the required conditions.

Case 2.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
Y\subset[xx^{\prime}]
.
Then
[yy^{\prime}]
and
[zz^{\prime}]
intersect
[xx^{\prime}]
along two subsegments, possibly degenerate.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that geodesics
[xx^{\prime}]
,
[yy^{\prime}]
, and
[zz^{\prime}]
are oriented the same way on their overlaps.

In this case, the needed configuration
\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{\prime},\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}
on a line
\mathbb{R}
can be described by the coordinates:

\displaystyle\tilde{x}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=|x^{\prime}-x|,

\displaystyle\tilde{y}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=|y^{\prime}-x|,

\displaystyle\tilde{z}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=|z^{\prime}-x|,

\displaystyle\tilde{x}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=|y^{\prime}-x|-|y^{\prime}-y|,

\displaystyle\tilde{z}

\displaystyle=|z^{\prime}-x|-|z^{\prime}-z|.

The triangle inequality implies that this configuration meets the conditions.

Final step.

It remains to show that
O_{3}
-comparison holds for a product of trees
\Pi=\penalty T_{1}\times\dots\times T_{n}
.
Any six-point configuration
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in\Pi
can be described by
n
six-point configurations
x_{i}
,
y_{i}
,
z_{i}
,
x^{\prime}_{i}
,
y^{\prime}_{i}
,
z^{\prime}_{i}\in T_{i}
for each
i
.
Choose a model configuration
\tilde{x}_{i}
,
\tilde{y}_{i}
,
\tilde{z}_{i}
,
\tilde{x}^{\prime}_{i}
,
\tilde{y}^{\prime}_{i}
,
\tilde{z}^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{H}
for each
i
.
Observe that the
n
-tuples
\tilde{x}=(\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}),\dots\penalty,\tilde{z}^{\prime}=(\tilde{z}_{1},\dots,\tilde{z}_{n})
in
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}^{\times n}
form a model configuration of
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
.
∎

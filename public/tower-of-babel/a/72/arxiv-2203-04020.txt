Mini-batch stochastic three-operator splitting for distributed optimization
Barbara Franci, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
We consider a network of agents, each with its own private cost consisting of a sum of two possibly nonsmooth convex functions, one of which is composed with a linear operator. At every iteration each agent performs local calculations and can only communicate with its neighbors. The challenging aspect of our study is that the smooth part of the private cost function is given as an expected value and agents only have access to this part of the problem formulation via a heavy-tailed stochastic oracle. To tackle such sampling-based optimization problems, we propose a stochastic extension of the triangular pre-conditioned primal-dual algorithm. We demonstrate almost sure convergence of the scheme and validate the performance of the method via numerical experiments.



I
Introduction

We consider a large class of convex optimization problems given by

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+g(x)+h(Lx),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex and continuously differentiable function,
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are closed convex and lower semi-continuous functions, and
L:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a given linear map. Such a structure is very general, and and describes many applications that range from signal processing to machine learning to control
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. In many instances of problem (
1
), the cost functions are contaminated by stochastic noise. In such settings, we are given a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
carrying a random variable
\xi:\Omega\to\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and a measurable function
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
so that

f(x)=\mathbb{E}[F(x,\xi)]\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2)

The presence of stochastic uncertainty challenges any direct solution method for problem (
1
), since the smooth function
f
is not directly accessible in practice, unless the distribution of the random variable is known. Indeed, the expectation (
2
) cannot be computed exactly, and instead we need to restore to simulation-based techniques. A reasonable assumption is that we can draw samples from the distribution of the random variable
\xi
, and stochastically approximate the necessary information about the function
f
. Specifically, we adopt an online gradient-based stochastic approximation method where a deterministic version of a numerical algorithm, known to solve problem (
1
) in its expected-value formulation, is supplied with stochastic estimators of the gradient of
f
. In this setting, the mechanism to access
f
via samples of the law of
\xi
is usually named a
stochastic oracle
(SO). The SO outputs unbiased approximations of the gradient obtained via an average over a batch of realizations. The larger the number of samples, the smaller the variance of point estimators, and thus the more precise information we obtain at the cost of generating a larger amount of i.i.d random variables. This trade-off between accuracy of estimators and the available simulation budget is what makes such
mini-batch
stochastic approximation approaches efficient methods of choice
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

I-A

Main Contributions and relation to the literature

A standard assumption in stochastic optimization is that the approximation error is uniformly bounded
[
5
]
.
Instead, the stochastic approximation approach developed in this paper allows to handle stochastic oracles with potentially unbounded moments. This is of relevance in primal-dual dynamics, which usually act on unbounded domains, for which any a-priori variance bound is rather unnatural.

Besides weaker hypothesis on the noise structure, this paper is the first stochastic primal-dual algorithm which is even provably convergent in the multi-agent formulation of problem (
1
), i.e., when a finite set of agents
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
cooperatively minimize the composite objective function

\begin{split}\min_{x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}},1\leq i\leq m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x_{i})+g_{i}(x_{i})+h_{i}(L_{i}x_{i}),\\
\text{s.t.: }A_{ij}x_{i}+A_{ji}x_{j}=b_{(i,j)}\quad\forall(i,j)\in E\end{split}

(3)

Problems of this form appear in several application fields. In distributed model predictive control,
f_{i}
can represent individual finite-horizon costs for each agent,
L_{i}
model linear dynamics of each agent, and
h_{i}
models state and input constraints
[
1
]
. In machine learning,
f_{i}
would represent a smooth data fidelity terms,
Lx
linear restrictions on the parameters and
h_{i}
and
g_{i}
take over the role of statistical penalties reflecting a-priori structure properties of the parameters to be estimated
[
2
]
.

Compared with existing work, this paper makes the following contributions:

(i)

Our algorithm is a stochastic extension of the triangular preconditioned primal-dual algorithm (TriPD), developed in
[
7
,
8
]
.

(ii)

To the best of our knowledge, our scheme is the first stochastic approximation method which is able to solve the three-operator splitting problem characterizing primal-dual pairs for (
1
).

(iii)

The analysis immediately generalizes to the multi-agent formulation (
3
), where distributed iterations, i.e., locally performed without central supervision, are obtained.

The only related contributions we are aware of are
[
9
,
10
]
. Both assume a uniformly bounded SO, which is very restrictive in primal-dual methods, essentially forcing a-priori compactness on the domain of
g
. For the special case when
g=0
, stochastic accelerated algorithms for the centralized problem (
1
) have been considered in
[
11
]
, imposing a uniformly bounded noise condition on the SO. We include the non-smooth term
g
and allow for heavy-tailed noise in the SO.

Our analysis is restricted to synchronous versions of distributed optimization algorithms. It is possible to extend our analysis to the asynchronous case and block-coordinate descent strategies, acting on general real separable Hilbert spaces. We will present these extensions in a future paper.

II
Stochastic Primal-Dual Algorithm

In this section we propose a stochastic primal-dual algorithm for solving (
1
). Let
\mathcal{H}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{G}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{m}
with inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
, and corresponding norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Assumption 1

Throughout the paper the following assumptions shall be in place:

(i)

g:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,\infty],h:\mathcal{G}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are proper, closed, convex and lower-semi continuous functions.

(ii)

L:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is a linear mapping with adjoint
L^{\ast}
.

(iii)

f:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex, continuously differentiable and there exists
\alpha>0,\mathbf{Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\beta_{f}>0
such that
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|_{\mathbf{Q}^{-1}}\leq\beta_{f}\|x-y\|_{\mathbf{Q}}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

(iv)

Let
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set and
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
a probability space. There exists a measurable function
F:\mathcal{H}\times\Xi\to\mathbb{R}
such that (
2
) holds.

(v)

The set of solutions to (
1
), denoted by
\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
, is nonempty Moreover, there exists
x\in\rint(\dom g)
such that
Lx\in\rint(\dom h)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
represent the product space with the inner product

\langle(y_{1},x_{1}),(y_{2},x_{2})\rangle=\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}+\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

and associated norm
\|(y,x)\|=\sqrt{\|y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{G}}+\|x\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}.
Whenever clear from the context, we will suppress the ambient space from the inner products and norms, respectively.

We remark that by the Baillon-Haddad theorem
[
13
, Corollary 18.17]
the
\beta_{f}
-Lipschitz smoothness of the function
f
is equivalent to the
\tfrac{1}{\beta_{f}}
-cocoercivity of
\nabla f
, i.e.,

\langle\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\prime}),x-x^{\prime}\rangle\geq\tfrac{1}{\beta_{f}}\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{\prime})\|^{2}.

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
.

Problem (
1
) can be equivalently written as a max-min problem

\max_{y\in\mathcal{G}}\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\{f(x)+g(x)+\langle Lx,y\rangle-h^{\ast}(y)\}

Then,
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
is called a primal-dual pair if

\begin{split}0&\in\partial h^{\ast}(\bar{y})-L\bar{x},\\
0&\in\partial g(\bar{x})+\nabla f(\bar{x})+L^{\ast}\bar{y}.\end{split}

Introduce the maximally monotone operators

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(y,x)\triangleq\partial h^{\ast}(y)\times\partial g(x),

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}(y,x)\triangleq[-Lx,L^{\ast}y],

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}(y,x)\triangleq[0,\nabla f(x)].

Then
\bar{z}\triangleq(\bar{y},\bar{x})
is a primal-dual pair if and only if

\bar{z}\in\Zer(\mathsf{G})\triangleq\{z\in\mathcal{Z}|0\in\mathsf{G}(z)\},
where
\mathsf{G}(y,x)\triangleq\mathsf{A}(y,x)+\mathsf{M}(y,x)+\mathsf{C}(y,x).

II-B

Stochastic TriPD

Since we cannot evaluate
\mathsf{C}(z)
directly, we let
\mathcal{C}_{k}(z)
denote its stochastic estimator. This estimator is constructed by a Monte-Carlo scheme involving mini-batches. Given an i.i.d. sample
\xi_{1:N}\triangleq\{\xi^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
drawn from the law of
\xi
, let

\bar{w}(x,\xi_{1:N})\triangleq\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^{N}}(\nabla_{x}F(x,\xi^{i})-\nabla f(x)).

The approximation take place at each iteration
k
and a sequence
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(the

) defines the number of random variables we need to sample in each iteration.

Assumption 2

We are given a sequence
\{N_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}N_{k}=\infty
.

Since complexity questions are beyond our scope, we do not specify the speed at which batch sizes grow (see, however,
[
6
]
). For a given sequence of batch-sizes, we set
w_{k}(x,\omega)\triangleq\bar{w}(x,\xi_{1:N_{k}}(\omega))
.

Assumption 3

For all
k\geq 0
,
\mathcal{C}_{k}(z,\omega)=[0,\mathcal{F}_{k}(x,\omega)]
, where

\mathcal{F}_{k}(x,\omega)=\nabla f(x)+w_{k}(x,\omega)
for all
(x,\omega)\in\mathcal{H}\times\Omega.

We let
(\mathcal{A}_{k})_{k\geq 0}
denote the filtration given by
\mathcal{A}_{0}\triangleq\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
, and for
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{A}_{k}\triangleq\sigma(\xi_{1:N_{0}},\ldots,\xi_{1:N_{k-1}})
. Define
\nu(x)\triangleq\|\nabla_{x}F(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}
, and

\sigma(x)\triangleq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\nu(x)|x]}.

Assumption 4

For all
k\geq 0
we have
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{F}_{k}(X_{k})|\mathcal{A}_{k}]=\nabla f(X_{k})
a.s.. Moreover, there exists
x^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
and
\sigma_{0}(x^{\ast})\geq 0,\sigma_{1}>0
such that

\sigma(x)\leq\sigma_{0}(x^{\ast})+\sigma_{1}\|x-x^{\ast}\|^{2}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}.

This is a heavy-tailed noise assumption which allows us to consider random perturbation even with unbounded second moment
[
4
,
14
]
.

In analogy to TriPD and in light of the approximation scheme, the equations in (
5
) correspond to those in Algorithm
1
and lead to a sequence of random maps
\{\mathbb{T}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}:\mathcal{Z}\times\Omega\to\mathcal{Z}
generating a stochastic process
\{Z_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}=\{(Y_{k},X_{k});k\geq 0\}
via recursive updates

Z_{k+1}=\mathbb{T}_{k}(Z_{k}),\quad Z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}\text{ given.}

The exact action of this mapping can be described as follows. For
z=(y,x)\in\mathcal{Z}
, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{k}(z)\triangleq\prox^{\Sigma^{-1}}_{h^{\ast}}(y+\Sigma Lx),

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{k}(z)\triangleq\prox^{\Gamma^{-1}}_{g}\left(x-\Gamma\mathcal{F}_{k}(x)-\Gamma\mathbb{Y}_{k}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{k}(z)\triangleq\left[\mathbb{Y}_{k}(z)+\Sigma L\left(\mathbb{X}_{k}(z)-x\right),\mathbb{X}_{k}(z)\right].

Let
\hat{Z}_{k}\triangleq[\mathbb{Y}_{k}(z),\mathbb{X}_{k}(z)]
, so that
Y_{k+1}=\mathbb{Y}_{k}(Z_{k})+\Sigma_{k}L\left(\mathbb{X}_{k}(z)-X_{k}\right)
and

for all

. One can verify that

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{k}=(\mathbf{R}+\mathsf{A})^{-1}(\mathbf{R}-\mathsf{M}-\mathcal{C}_{k})(Z_{k}),\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{k}(z)=z+\mathbf{S}^{-1}(\mathbf{R}+\mathsf{M}^{\ast})(\hat{Z}_{k}-z).

In this section we consider a network of agents, whose aim is to solve problem (

G=(V,E)

VI
Conclusions

We propose a stochastic triangular preconditioned primal-dual algorithm (STriPD) for solving a large family of structured convex optimization problems, and multi-agent versions thereof. Many extensions of the present work will be investigated in a more elaborate investigation. Such extensions will include block-coordinate descent implementations, allowing for asynchronous updates in the distributed case. Furthermore, we will be investigating the iteration and oracle complexity of the method in detail.

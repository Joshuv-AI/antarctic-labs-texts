Abstract
. We consider a non-linear system modelling the dynamics of a linearly elastic body immersed in an incompressible viscous fluid. We prove local existence of strong solutions and global existence and uniqueness for small data. The main result is the characterization of long-time behaviour of the elastic displacement. We show convergence either to a rest state or rigid motion, or to a time-periodic pressure wave that may occur only in specific geometric settings.

1.
Introduction

The dynamics of a linearly elastic body immersed
in an imcompressible viscous fluid can be described by the following system,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}\dot{u}+(u\cdot\nabla)u-\Div(\sigma(u,p))&=&0&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\OF,\\
\Div(u)&=&0&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\OF,\\
\sigma(u,p)n&=&\Sigma(\xi)n&\textrm{on }(0,T)\times\dOS,\\
u&=&\dot{\xi}&\textrm{on }(0,T)\times\dOS,\\
u&=&0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\dO,\\
\ddot{\xi}-\textrm{div}(\Sigma(\xi))&=&0&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\OS,\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}&\textrm{in }\OF,\\
\xi(0)&=&\xi_{0}&\textrm{in }\OS,\\
\dot{\xi}(0)&=&\xi_{1}&\textrm{in }\OS,\end{array}\end{cases}

on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that contains a viscous
fluid in
\OF\subset\Omega
and an elastic structure
\OS\subset\Omega

that is completely immersed. The fluid-solid interface corresponds
to the boundary
\dOS
and we have
\overline{\OS}\cup\OF=\Omega
.
The exterior unit normal vector field of
\OS
at
\dOS
is denoted
by
n
. The unkowns are the fluid velocity
u\colon(0,T)\times\OF\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
fluid pressure
p\colon(0,T)\times\OF\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and elastic
displacement
\xi\colon(0,T)\times\OS\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The fluid
is Newtonian with viscosity
\nu>0
and stress tensor

\sigma(u,p):=2\nu\varepsilon(u)-p\mathrm{Id}

and the structure is linear elastic with Lamé constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0

and stress tensor

\Sigma(\xi):=2\lambda_{1}\varepsilon(\xi)+\lambda_{2}\Div(\xi)\mathrm{Id},

where

\varepsilon(v):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla v+(\nabla v)^{T}\right)

is the symmetric gradient.

Our aim is to investigate long-term viscous damping in this system, i.e. to show that due to fluid viscosity and the interaction
through the boundary, either elastic displacement will disappear over time, or the way in which it can persist can be characterized.
We show that unique strong solutions exist globally in time, if the
initial data are small, and that

(1.2)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}=0,

as well as,

(1.3)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\xi(t)-\eta^{*}(t)-\varphi_{N}^{0}-r(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}=0.

Here, the constant (in time) function
\varphi_{N}^{0}
is known
a priori and satisfies

\begin{cases}\Div\Sigma(\varphi)=0,&\text{in }\OS,\\
\Sigma(\varphi)n=q_{0}n,&\text{on }\dOS,\end{cases}

with some constant
q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
\Sigma(\xi_{0})=0
,
then
\varphi_{N}^{0}=0
, so this is a correction for
\OS
being
initially deformed. The term
r(t)\in\mathrm{ker}\,\Sigma
is a rigid
velocity that remains as an artifact of linear elasticity, but it is shown
that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{r}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\ddot{r}(t)=0.

The function
\eta^{*}
in (
1.3
) is the most interesting one: it may be non-trivially time-periodic
and corresponds to internal deformation of
\OS
while
u=0
, and
thus
\dot{\xi}|_{\dOS}=0
. We call it a
pressure wave
.

For a large class of
good domains

\OS
, introduced by Avalos and Triggiani,
[
AT07
,
AT08
,
AT09a
,
AT09b
,
AT09c
,
AT13
,
Ava07
]
, it
can be shown that
\eta^{*}=0
. It may be that the only example of
a
bad domain
that allows for
\eta^{*}\neq 0
is the ball,
cf. Section
6
. This (counter-)example shows that the damping
effect in system (
1.1
) is somewhat geometric, not depending on viscosity
alone. For corresponding considerations on the influence of geometry on the controllability of the system, we refer to
[
OP99
]
. A full characterization of good and bad domains seems to be related to the solution of Serrin’s overdetermined problem
[
Ser71
]
for the Lamé system.

The “complete” standard system of fluid-structure interaction is of free-boundary
type, where the fluid domain
\OF
changes with
\xi
. The literature on this and related systems is extensive. We refer to
[
Bou07
,
CS05
,
CS06
,
KT12
,
LR14
,
MMN+22
,
GH16
,
IKLT17
,
AB15
,
MRR20
,
RV14
,
GHL19
,
MC15
,
BKS20
,
BKS21
]
and references therein for results on the
existence and uniqueness of weak and strong solutions to the complete system in three or less dimensions, and on important related models as in the case of elastic shells,
nonlinear elasticity and compressible fluids.

In
[
BGT19
]
, see also
[
RV14
]
and
[
GHL19
]
,
it was recently shown that the complete system without additional damping admits local strong solutions that
preserve initial regularity over time. This gives rise to the hope
of finding global strong solutions, in particular for small data.
Our system is much simpler in that it linearizes the fluid domain
change and only keeps the convective nonlinearity. We thus need less
regularity for solving the coupled problem and for realizing the full
interaction at the boundary. From a modelling point of view, our simplification could
be justified in that we transfer the principle of
geometric
linearization
underlying the assumption of linear elasticiy, cf.
[
Hau02
]
,
to the fluid domain. On the other hand, the non-trivial long-term asymptotic solutions in (
1.2
), (
1.3
) would also solve the complete system, as for
u=0
, the fluid domain does not change. Our results are thus a first step also towards characterizing and separating the difficulties for the complete system.

We refer
to
[
KTZ11
]
for the proof of local existence
of weak solutions to system (
1.1
). In
[
DL22
]
, we proved global existence
for small data in the two-dimensional case. Global existence of solutions
has been established for the complete problem in the case
of additional damping
[
IKLT17
]
and for the linear system with shifted
Lamé operator
[
Ava07
]
.

A key difficulty in the analysis in both linear and complete cases
is to establish a functional analytic setting that can handle the
mixed parabolic-hyperbolic character of the problem. Our setting is
weaker than the ones used in the complete case, as boundary regularity is not an issue. This enables us to find suitable
global-in-time a priori bounds. At the same time, the setting is stronger than
in the semigroup approach
[
Ava07
]
for the linear case, allowing us to treat the nonlinearity
u\cdot\nabla u

and to fully exploit boundary interaction. We also use the key techniques that have been
established for these mixed systems: optimal regularity of the mixed Dirichlet and Neumann-Stokes
[
BP07
,
GS91
]

and the Dirichlet-Lamé systems, hidden regularity for the Lamé system
[
LLT86
,
KTZ11
]
, Theorem
20
,
and additional approximation arguments that shift between regularity levels, following e.g. the appraoch in
[
BGT19
]
for the complete case.

We then show that regular and first-order
global energy estimates are sufficient for globally extending the
solution and we establish these estimates.
Some additional technical difficulties arise in both existence and convergence
proofs, e.g. due to the fact that the boundedness of
\|\xi(t)\|_{2}
may not be guaranteed
from the equations.

For the shifted linear system, geometric conditions that guarantee
strong stability of the semigroup were established in
[
Ava07
]
, see Section

6
for more details. Our general characterization of long-time asymptotic
behaviour of solutions is new particularly in the case of pressure waves, when these conditions do not hold. It can be viewed as
the main result of this paper. The proof essentially uses the mixed
hyperbolic-parabolic character of the system combined with the decomposition of candidate limit solutions into time-periodic functions, see Steps 1 and
2 in Section
7
. We hope that it can be generalized to apply to other systems
with a similar structure.

We note that in our system (
1.1
), domains do not change, so that from a mathematical point of view, the case of contact of structure and outer wall can be ignored. We refer to
[
vGL+19
,
MAA18
,
CGH21
]
for recent approaches to contact problems.

Finally, we note that for non-small data, the pressure waves
\eta^{*}\neq 0

can provide simple explicit examples of the trouble with linear elasticity, such as
extra-penetration
: even though

\dOS
remains fixed, parts of the interior
\OS
may deform to
\OF
, see Section
8.5
. This is
a problem also for the complete models including domain changes: they will allow for these
solutions, as the fluid domain
\OF
is tracked only via
\dOS
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, local existence of
strong solutions is established. In Section
3
, it is shown that solutions
extend to be global-in-time, if the initial data are sufficiently
small. In Section
4
, the main result on long-time behaviour of
\xi

is introduced. Sections
5
,
6
and
7
are concerned with the proof. The
Appendix contains preliminary results related to the Stokes and Lamé
systems, details on the estimates of the term
(u\cdot\nabla)u
and
some of the approximation arguments, and a brief discussion of examples
of pressure waves.

2.
Local existence of solutions

We first establish the existence of solutions
(u,p,\xi)

to (
1.1
) up to a (possibly small) time
T>0

in the spaces

\displaystyle X_{T}:=

\displaystyle\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))\cap\H 1^{1}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OF)),

\displaystyle Y_{T}:=

\displaystyle\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF)),

\displaystyle Z_{T}:=

\displaystyle\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OS)),

respectively, where we omit the time interval
(0,T)
for the Sobolev
spaces and
[0,T]
for the spaces of continuous functions whenever
possible. We also show that this implies the additional regularity

(2.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))\cap X_{T},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\in\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OF)).

This corresponds to the requirement that the initial data

(2.2)

u_{0}\in\H 1^{2}(\OF),\,\xi_{0}\in\H 1^{2}(\OS),\,\xi_{1}\in\H 1^{1}(\OS)

are such that there exist

(2.3)

\displaystyle u_{1},\,p_{0}\in\H 1^{1}(\OF)\text{ and }\xi_{2}\in\Lp^{2}(\OS)

which satisfy the compatibility conditions

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}u_{1}+(u_{0}\cdot\nabla)u_{0}-\Div(\sigma(u_{0},p_{0}))&=&0&\text{in }\OF,\\
\Div(u_{0})&=&0&\text{in }\OF,\\
\Div(u_{1})&=&0&\text{in }\OF,\\
\sigma(u_{0},p_{0})n&=&\Sigma(\xi_{0})n&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega_{S},\\
u_{0}&=&\xi_{1}&\text{on }\dOS,\\
u_{0}&=&0&\text{on }\dO,\\
u_{1}&=&0&\text{on }\dO,\\
\xi_{2}-\Div(\Sigma(\xi_{0}))&=&0&\text{in }\OS.\end{array}\end{cases}

Using a construction as in the proof of Lemma
25
, it can be shown that the set of compatible initial data is non-trivial.
The local existence result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let the initial data
u_{0},\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
be given such
that (
2.2
) – (
2.4
) are satisfied.

Then there exists a time

T=T\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)},\|u_{1}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)},\|\varepsilon(\xi_{0})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)},\|\xi_{1}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)},\|\xi_{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\right)>0

such that the system (
1.1
) admits a unique solution

u\in\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))\cap X_{T},p\in\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))\text{ and }\xi\in Z_{T}.

The proof can be divided into four steps.

Step 1: Linearization

A main ingredient in the proof of Theorem

is a recent result of Boulakia, Guerrero and Takahashi
[
BGT19

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}\dot{u}-\Div(\sigma(u,p))&=&f&\textrm{in }(0,T)\times\OF,\\

\displaystyle\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{5/2+1/16}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\H 1^{3/2+1/16}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(\H 1^{1/2+1/16}(\OS))\cap\C^{3}(\H 1^{-1/2+1/16}(\OS)).

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u_{0},\,u_{1},\,p_{0},\,\xi_{0},\,\xi_{1},\,\xi_{2},\,f)\in&\,\H 1^{5/2+1/16}(\OF)\times\H 1^{1}(\OF)\times\H 1^{3/2+1/16}(\OF)\\

Step 2: A-priori estimates and approximation.

The next step in the proof is to reduce the regularity needed in
Theorem
2
to norms that admit global a-priori
estimates. First, this is done in the linearized setting, using an approximation
argument. The total of kinetic and elastic energy for system (
1.1

is defined by

E(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\dot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\xi):\varepsilon(\xi)(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

and a corresponding higher-order quantity is

K(t):=\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\ddot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\dot{\xi}):\varepsilon(\dot{\xi})(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\xi_{2}^{n}-\xi_{2}^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\|f^{n}-f^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))\cap\H 1^{1}(\H 1^{-1/2+1/16}(\OF))}^{2}\Bigg).\end{split}

Hence the convergence of the data

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))}+\|p^{n}-p^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))}

\displaystyle\leq\,C\Big(T^{1/2}\|\dot{u}^{n}-\dot{u}^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}+\|f^{n}-f^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}+T^{1/2}\|\xi^{n}-\xi^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OS))}\Big).

Similarly, Theorem

\displaystyle\|\xi^{n}-\xi^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OS))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\left(\|\ddot{\xi}^{n}-\ddot{\xi}^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}+\|\xi_{0}^{n}-\xi_{0}^{m}\|_{\H 1^{2}(\OS)}+T^{1/2}\|u^{n}-u^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))}\right).

Combining the last two estimates yields

\displaystyle\|u^{n}-u^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))}+\|p^{n}-p^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))}+\|\xi^{n}-\xi^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{2}(\OS))}

\displaystyle\,C\Big(T^{1/2}\|\dot{u}^{n}-\dot{u}^{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}+\|f^{n}-f^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}+(1+T^{1/2})\|\ddot{\xi}_{n}-\ddot{\xi}_{m}\|_{\C^{0}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt+(1+T^{1/2})\|\xi_{0}^{n}-\xi_{0}^{m}\|_{\H 1^{2}(\OS)}+(T^{1/2}+T)\|u^{n}-u^{m}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))}\Big).

Consequently, for

the last term

is also a Cauchy sequence in

and therefore in

) with initial

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u^{1}-u^{2}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(\xi^{1}-\xi^{2})\|_{\C^{0}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}^{2}+\|\dot{\xi}^{1}-\dot{\xi}^{2}\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}^{2}\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u^{1}-u^{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{2}(\OF))}\\

&\hskip 8.5359pt+\|\tilde{u}^{1}\cdot\nabla(\tilde{u}^{1}-\tilde{u}^{2})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}+\|(\tilde{u}^{1}-\tilde{u}^{2})\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}^{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\Lp^{2}(\OF))}\\

3.
Existence of global solutions for small data

We show that for small initial data, the local solution
extends to a global one. Recall the energy

E(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\dot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\xi):\varepsilon(\xi)(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

associated to system (
1.1
), and the corresponding
higher-order quantity

K(t):=\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\ddot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\dot{\xi}):\varepsilon(\dot{\xi})(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Theorem 4
.

There exist constants
C_{u}>0,\,C_{E}>0,\,C_{K}>0

such that for initial data

\displaystyle d:=(u_{0},u_{1},p_{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\H 1^{2}(\OF)\times\H 1^{1}(\OF)\times\H 1^{1}(\OF)\times\H 1^{2}(\OS)\times\H 1^{1}(\OS)\times\Lp^{2}(\OS)

satisfying the compatibility conditions (
2.4
)
and the bounds

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}\leq C_{u},\hskip 14.22636ptE(0)\leq C_{E},\hskip 14.22636ptK(0)\leq C_{K},

the corresponding unique solution
(u,p,\xi)
to (
1.1
)
exists up to any time
T>0
.

Proof.

From Theorem
1
we obtain a unique solution

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u,p,\xi)\in&\left(\C^{0}(0,T;\H 1^{2}(\OF))\cap\H 1^{1}(0,T;\H 1^{1}(\OF))\cap\C^{1}(0,T;\Lp^{2}(\OF))\right)\\
&\times\C^{0}(0,T;\H 1^{1}(\OF))\\
&\times\left(\C^{0}(0,T;\H 1^{2}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(0,T;\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(0,T;\Lp^{2}(\OS))\right)\end{split}

up to some time

\displaystyle T\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)},\|u_{1}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)},\|\varepsilon(\xi_{0})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)},\|\xi_{1}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)},\|\xi_{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\right)>0.

We show that if condition (
3.1
) is satisfied for suitable

C_{E},C_{K},C_{u}>0
, then

\|u(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}\leq 1,\,E(t)\leq E(0)\text{ and }K(t)\leq K(0)\text{ for all }t\in[0,T).

We find some
0<\delta<1
such that we can choose
M:=\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}

in the proof of Theorem
1
, if the initial
data satisfy

(3.3)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2},\|u_{1}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2},\|\varepsilon(\xi_{0})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2},\|\xi_{1}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}^{2},\|\xi_{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}\right\}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Note that there exists some constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(\xi_{0})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\|\xi_{1}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}^{2}+\|\xi_{2}\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle C_{0}(E(0)+K(0)).

Consider initial data satisfying

(3.4)

\displaystyle C_{0}(E(0)+K(0))\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Moreover, assume

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2}\leq\min\left\{\frac{\delta}{2},\frac{M\delta}{2}\right\}.

In particular, this implies (
3.3
). Then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\H 1^{1}(0,T;\H 1^{1}(\OF))}^{2}\leq M=\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}.

Note that by Korn’s and Poincaré’s inequalities, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0

such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle c_{1}\|\varepsilon(v)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2}\leq c_{2}\|\varepsilon(v)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}

holds for all
v\in\H 1^{1}(\OF)
with partially vanishing trace at
the boundary
\dOF
. In particular, this is true for
u(t)
and

\dot{u}(t)
. Hence

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2}&\leq c_{2}\|\varepsilon(u_{0})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+c_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\OF}2\varepsilon(u):\varepsilon(\dot{u})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2}+c_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon(u)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon(\dot{u})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)\\
&\leq\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\|u_{0}\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{2}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}\|u\|_{\H 1^{1}(0,T;\H 1^{1}(\OF))}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{c_{2}M\delta}{2c_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}M}{c_{1}}<1\\
\\
\end{split}

for all
t\in[0,T)
. Next, we want to bound
E
. As in Lemma
3
,
we obtain the energy equality

(3.9)

\displaystyle E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}4\nu\|\varepsilon(u(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=E(0)-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\OF}(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (

),
use Hölder’s inequality, the embeddings
\H 1^{1/2}(\OF)\hookrightarrow\Lp^{3}(\OF)
,

\H 1^{1}(\OF)\hookrightarrow\Lp^{6}(\OF)
, interpolation and (

)
to estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\int_{\OF}[(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u](s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y&\leq C\|u(s)\|_{\Lp^{3}(\OF)}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}\|u(s)\|_{\Lp^{6}(\OF)}\\

&\leq C\|u(s)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{1/2}\|u(s)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{5/2}\\

for some fixed
\hat{C}>0

\OF
. This leads
to

(3.11)

\displaystyle E(t)+\int_{0}^{t}(4\nu-\hat{C}E(s)^{1/2})\|\varepsilon(u(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq E(0).

If the initial data is chosen so small that

(3.12)

\displaystyle 4\nu-\hat{C}E(0)^{1/2}>0,

then we can show that

(3.13)

\displaystyle E(t)\leq E(0)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for all }t\in[0,T):

Assume to the contrary that there is a time

such

3.12

\tilde{E}>E(0)

\displaystyle 4\nu-\hat{C}\tilde{E}^{1/2}>0.

As

\displaystyle E(t)\leq\tilde{E}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for all }t\leq t_{1}.

But then

\displaystyle E(t_{1})+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left(4\nu-\hat{C}\tilde{E})^{1/2}\right)\|\varepsilon(u(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s}_{\geq 0}\leq E(0)

, a contradiction. Moreover, by (
3.11

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon(u(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{E(0)}{4\nu-\hat{C}E(0)^{1/2}}.

By Lemma
3

(3.15)

\displaystyle K(t)+\int_{0}^{t}4\nu\|\varepsilon(\dot{u}(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=K(0)-2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\OF}\left((\dot{u}\cdot\nabla)u+(u\cdot\nabla)\dot{u}\right)\cdot\dot{u}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Combining (

3.17
)

\displaystyle K(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(4\nu-\tilde{C}\left(E(0)+K(s)\right)^{1/2}\right)\|\varepsilon(\dot{u}(s))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq K(0)

. If the initial data is chosen so small

(3.20)

\displaystyle 4\nu-\tilde{C}\left(E(0)+K(0)\right)^{1/2}>0,

\displaystyle K(t)\leq K(0)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for all }t\in[0,T).

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\varepsilon(\dot{u})(s)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{K(0)}{4\nu-\tilde{C}\left(E(0)+K(0)\right)^{1/2}}.

This proves uniform and even decreasing bounds for

,

E(t)
and

Corollary 5
.

Consider initial data
(u_{0},u_{1},p_{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})

satisfying the conditions of Theorem

Proof.

The global solution

4.

\displaystyle\to 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for }t\to\infty,

and consequently,
\hat{p}(\cdot+t)\to 0
in
\Lp^{2}((0,T/2)\times\OF)
.
Now let

E_{F}:\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1/2}(\dOS))\to\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))

denote a bounded linear extension operator which satisfies
\text{supp}(E_{F}h(t))\subset\subset\Omega

for all
h\in\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1/2}(\dOS))
and
t\in(0,T/2)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{\dOS}(\Sigma(\xi(s+t))n-q(s+t)n)\cdot h\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}S(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{\dOS}\sigma(u,\hat{p})(s+t)n\cdot h\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}S(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{\OF}\sigma(u,\hat{p})(s+t):\nabla(E_{F}h)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{\OF}\Div(\sigma(u,\hat{p}))(s+t)\cdot E_{F}h\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T/2}\|(\varepsilon(u(s+t))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)\|E_{F}h\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))}^{2}+C\|\hat{p}(\cdot+t)\|_{\Lp^{2}((0,T/2)\times\OF)}^{2}\|E_{F}h\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\left(\int_{0}^{T/2}\left(\|\dot{u}(s+t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|u(s+t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}^{4}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)\|E_{F}h\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OF))}^{2}

\displaystyle\to 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for }t\to\infty

and hence

(5.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\Sigma(\xi(\cdot+t)n-q(\cdot+t)n\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{-1/2}(\dOS))}=0.

Together with (
5.12
), this implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{t_{0}\in(0,t_{0}^{*}]}\left\|\tilde{q}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t_{n})n-\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}})n\right\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{-1/2}(\dOS))}=0.

Since
\Lp^{2}(0,T/2)
is closed in
\Lp^{2}(0,T/2;\H 1^{-1/2}(\dOS))
,
we conclude that
\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}})n\in\Lp^{2}(0,T/2)
and consequently

\eta_{t_{0}}\in A_{T/2}
. Together with (
5.11
)
and (
5.12
), this concludes the proof of
(
5.2
).
∎

6.
Characterization of
\omega

The set
\omega
contains the solutions of the hyperbolic system
(
4.3
) with both homogeneous Dirichlet and scalar
Neumann boundary conditions. We derive its structure from a separation
of variables ansatz and the solvability of the overdetermined eigenvalue
problem

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Div(\psi)&=&\mu\psi&\text{in }\OS,\\
\psi&=&0&\text{on }\dOS,\\
\Sigma(\psi)n&=&qn&\text{on }\dOS.\end{array}\end{cases}\end{split}

The solutions of (
6.1
) are a subset of the eigenvalues
of the Dirichlet-Lamé operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\eta)=-\Div(\Sigma(\eta))

with domain
D(\mathcal{L}):=\H 1_{0}^{1}(\OS)\cap\H 1^{2}(\OS)\subset\Lp^{2}(\OS)
.
This operator is self-adjoint positive definite and has compact resolvent,
admitting countably many eigenvalues
(\mu_{k})\subset(0,\infty)

that can only cluster at infinity. The corresponding eigenfunctions

(\psi_{k})\subset D(\mathcal{L})
solving

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathcal{L}(\psi_{k})&=&\mu_{k}\psi_{k}&\text{in }\OS,\\
\psi_{k}&=&0&\text{on }\dOS,\end{array}\end{cases}\end{split}

form an orthonormal base of
\Lp^{2}(\OS)
. Following the work of
Avalos and Triggiani (cf.
[
AT07
,
AT08
,
AT09a
,
AT09b
,
AT09c
,
AT13
,
Ava07
]
)
we use the following definition.

Definition 9
.

The domain
\OS
is called

•

a
good
domain, if
\psi_{k}=0
,
q_{k}=0
is the only solution
of (
6.1
).

•

a
bad
domain, if (
6.1
) admits a non-zero
solution
(\psi_{k},q_{k})
for some
\mu_{k}>0
.

Remark 10
.

It was shown in
[
AT07
,
AT13
]
for a similar wave-type system that every domain which is partially flat, partially spherical, partially elliptic, partially hyperbolic or partially parabolic is a
good
domain, and this result was transferred to the Lamé system in
[
AT09a
, Remark 1.1]
.

Remark 11
.

The only known
bad
domain is the ball. For
\OS=B_{r}(0)
,
examples of non-zero solutions to (
6.1
) are given
by

\displaystyle\psi_{i}(y)

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{r^{2}\sin\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r}|y|\right)}{r_{i}^{2}|y|^{3}}-\frac{r\cos\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r}|y|\right)}{r_{i}|y|^{2}}\right)y,

\displaystyle q_{i}

\displaystyle:=(2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\sin(r_{i}),

with eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\frac{(2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})r_{i}^{2}}{r^{2}},

where
r_{i}\in(0,\infty)
is the
i
-th positive root of the spherical
Bessel function

\displaystyle j_{1}(r)=\frac{\sin(r)}{r^{2}}-\frac{\cos(r)}{r}.

An illustrative 1d-example will be discussed below in Subsection
8.5

Remark 12
.

The characterization of all good domains seems to be related to Serrin’s
overdetermined problem
[
Ser71
]
. In light of corresponding results,
it seems reasonable to conjecture that the ball is the only bad domain.
To our knowledge, the best results are in
[
AT07
, Appendix 1]
,
[
AT09b
, Remark 2.3]
,
[
AT13
, Section 4]
.

Theorem 13
.

(a)

Let
\OS
be a
good
domain. Then
\omega_{T}=\{(0,0)\}
.

(b)

Let
\OS
be a
bad
domain. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{T}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{k\in I}(a_{k}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t)+b_{k}\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t))(\psi_{k}(y),q_{k}):I\subset\mathbb{N},\,(\psi_{k},q_{k})\text{ solve }(\ref{eq:xi_overdet_EP}),\,(a_{k}),(b_{k})\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cap(\C^{0}(\H 1_{0}^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(\H 1^{-1}(\OS))\cap\H 1^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OS)^{*})))\times\C^{0}(0,T).

Proof.

Let
(\eta,qn)\in\omega_{T}
. Since
(\psi_{k})
forms an orthonormal
base of
\Lp^{2}(\OS)
, there exist functions
(c_{k})\subset\C^{0}(0,T)

such that

\displaystyle\eta(t,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(t)\psi_{k}(y)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for all }t\in(0,T)\text{ and a.e.{} }y\in\OS.

For any
\tilde{k}\in\mathbb{N}
, integration by parts implies

\langle\ddot{\eta},\psi_{\tilde{k}}\rangle_{\H 1^{-1}(\OS),\H 1_{0}^{1}(\OS)}=-\mu_{\tilde{k}}c_{\tilde{k}}(t)

and hence

\displaystyle\ddot{\eta}(t,y)=\Div(\Sigma(\eta))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}-\mu_{k}c_{k}(t)\psi_{k}(y).

This also implies that
\ddot{c}_{k}=-\mu_{k}c_{k}
(in a weak sense)
and thus

\displaystyle c_{k}(t)=a_{k}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t)+b_{k}\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t)

for some
a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\OS
is a
good

domain, then by definition this system has the only solution
\psi_{k}=0
,

q_{k}=0
and we can conclude that
\omega_{T}=\{(0,0)\}
. For a
bad domain
\OS
, let
I
be the set of indices
k\in\mathbb{N}

for which
(\psi_{k},q_{k})
forms a non-zero solution to (
6.1
).
Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\omega_{T}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{k\in I}(a_{k}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t)+b_{k}\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}t))(\psi_{k}(y),q_{k}):(\psi_{k},q_{k})\text{ solve }(\ref{eq:xi_overdet_EP}),\,(a_{k}),(b_{k})\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

(6.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cap(\C^{0}(\H 1_{0}^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(\H 1^{-1}(\OS))\cap\H 1^{2}(\H 1^{1}(\OS)^{*})))\times\C^{0}(0,T)

\displaystyle\neq\{(0,0)\}.\qed

Corollary 14
.

Let
\OS
be a
good

domain. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\dot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\ddot{\xi}(t)\|_{\H 1^{-1}(\OS)}=0.

Moreover,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\ddot{\xi}(t+\cdot)\|_{\Lp^{2}(0,T/2;\H 1^{1}(\OS)^{*})}=0.

Proof.

We start by proving the convergence of
\dot{\xi}
. Since
\omega=\{(0,0)\}

by Theorem
13
, Theorem
8
shows
that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{t_{0}\in(0,t_{0}^{*}]}\|\xi(\cdot+t_{0}+t)-\xi(\cdot+t)\|_{U_{T/2}}=0

and

(6.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{t_{0}\in(0,t_{0}^{*}]}\|\Sigma(\xi(\cdot+t_{0}+t)-\xi(\cdot+t))n\|_{\Lp^{2}(\H 1^{1/2}(\dOS))}=0.

Here, the uniformity for small
t_{0}
is essential. By the fundamental
theorem of calculus,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\frac{\xi(t+t_{0}^{*})-\xi(t)}{t_{0}^{*}}-\dot{\xi}(t)\right\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}&=\left\|\frac{1}{t_{0}^{*}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}^{*}}\dot{\xi}(t+s)-\dot{\xi}(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\\
\\
&\leq\sup_{t_{0}\in(0,t_{0}^{*}]}\|\xi(\cdot+t_{0}+t)-\xi(\cdot+t)\|_{\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}\\
&\to 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for }t\to\infty.\end{split}

In combination with

\displaystyle\left\|\xi(t+t_{0}^{*})-\xi(t)\right\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\to 0\;\text{ for }t\to\infty

due to (
6.4
), we obtain
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\dot{\xi}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}=0
.
The convergence
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\ddot{\xi}(t)\|_{\H 1^{-1}(\OS)}=0

and (
6.3
) follow analogously from (
6.4
).
∎

7.
Proof of the Main Result

Using essentially Theorem
8
and the characterization
of
\omega
in (

) by periodic functions, we are now in a position
to give the proof of the main result Theorem
7
.
It is divided into four steps.

Step 1: Convergence of
\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}
.

In this step, we construct a fixed function
\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}\in A

such that
\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}\to\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}
in a suitable
sense. Let
(t_{n})
be the subsequence constructed in the proof
of Theorem
8
and let
\varphi\in B_{T}
be such
that (
5.5
) holds. Moreover, choose
t_{0}\in(0,T/2)\setminus\{2\pi/\sqrt{\mu_{i}}:i\in I\}

and let
(\eta_{t_{0}},q_{t_{0}})\in\omega
be the pair such that
(
5.11
) and (

) hold.
Recall that

(7.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{t_{0}}(t)=\int_{t}^{t+t_{0}}\varphi(s)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

We denote

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t_{0},n}(t)

\displaystyle:=u(t+t_{0}+t_{n})-u(t+t_{n}),

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{t_{0},n}(t)

\displaystyle:=p(t+t_{0}+t_{n})-p(t+t_{n}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0},n}(t)

\displaystyle:=\xi(t+t_{0}+t_{n})-\xi(t+t_{n}).

Due to the special structure of
\omega
, we can extend its elements
and hence
(\eta_{t_{0}},q_{t_{0}})
globally in time. Then

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}_{t_{0},n},\tilde{p}_{t_{0},n}-q_{t_{0}},\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0},n}-\eta_{t_{0}})

is a global solution to the coupled system (
1.1
) with corresponding
initial data. The energy
E_{t_{0},n}
of this system is given by

\displaystyle E_{t_{0},n}(t)

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{u}_{t_{0},n}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OF)}^{2}+\|\dot{\tilde{\xi}}_{t_{0},n}(t)-\dot{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}

Now let
\varepsilon>0
. Because of (
5.11
) and
\|u(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}\to 0

for

\displaystyle E_{t_{0},n}(0)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

we can use
\|u(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OF)}\to 0
and follow Step 2
of the proof of Theorem
4
to find some
n_{2}\geq n_{1}

such that

(7.2)

\displaystyle E_{t_{0},n}(t)\leq E_{t_{0},n}(0)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }t\geq 0

and all
n\geq n_{2}
. Now for any
n\geq n_{2}
, Korn’s inequality
implies that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\eta_{t_{0}}(t+t_{n+1}-t_{n})-\eta_{t_{0}}(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}&\leq C\|\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}(t+t_{n+1}-t_{n})-\eta_{t_{0}}(t))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\\
&\leq C\big(\|\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}(t+t_{n+1}-t_{n})-\xi_{t_{0},n+1}(t))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\\
&\hskip 22.76228pt+\|\Sigma(\xi_{t_{0},n+1}(t)-\eta_{t_{0}}(t))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\big)\\
&=C\big(\|\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}(t+t_{n+1}-t_{n})-\xi_{t_{0},n}(t+t_{n+1}-t_{n}))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\\
&\hskip 22.76228pt+\|\Sigma(\xi_{t_{0},n+1}(t)-\eta_{t_{0}}(t))\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}\big)\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\end{split}

for all
t\geq 0
. At the same time, the coefficients

(7.4)

\displaystyle\eta_{t_{0},i}(t):=\int_{\OS}\eta_{t_{0}}(t)\cdot\psi_{i}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=a_{t_{0},i}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{i}}t)+b_{t_{0},i}\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{i}}t)

of

are either constantly zero or
P_{i}
-periodic
for

and
i\in I
. We denote
by
I_{\eta_{t_{0}}}
the set of all

where
\eta_{t_{0},i}

If
I_{\eta}=\emptyset
, then
\eta_{t_{0}}=0
and
q_{t_{0}}=0

for all
t_{0}\in(0,t_{0}^{*}]
and we can skip to Step 4 in the
proof. For the moment, we assume that
I_{\eta}\neq\emptyset
. The
smallness in (

) implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{t_{0},i}(t+(t_{n+1}-t_{n})\,\text{mod}\,P_{i}))-\eta_{t_{0},i}(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{t_{0},i}(t+(t_{n+1}-t_{n}))-\eta_{t_{0},i}(t)=0

for all
t\geq 0
. Using the decomposition into sine and cosine functions of period
\leq P_{i}
in (

) and arguing by contradiction, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}(t_{n+1}-t_{n})\,\text{mod}\,P_{i}=0

for all

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}\,\text{mod}\,P_{i}=t^{i}

We define

\displaystyle\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t,y):=\sum_{i\in I_{\eta}}\eta_{t_{0},i}(t-t^{i})\psi_{i}(y).

Applying the angle sum identities and the characterization in (
6
),
we see that
\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}\in A
. It remains to show that
\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}\to\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}

in a suitable sense.
By definition, for every
t\geq 0
,

\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{k}}),\dot{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{k}})\overset{k\to\infty}{\to}\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t),\dot{\hat{\eta}}_{t_{0}}(t)\in\Lp^{2}(\OS).

Let therefore

be such that

\displaystyle\|\dot{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n})-\dot{\hat{\eta}}_{t_{0}}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n})-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t)):\varepsilon(\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n})-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }t\geq 0\text{ and all }n\geq n_{2}.

By shifting the functions appearing in (
7.2
) by

, we obtain that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\dot{\tilde{\xi}}_{t_{0}}-\dot{\hat{\eta}}_{t_{0}})(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}\\
&+\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}):\varepsilon(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}})(t)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\|\dot{\tilde{\xi}}_{t_{0}}(t)-\dot{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}})\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}+\|\dot{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}})-\dot{\hat{\eta}}_{t_{0}}(t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(\OS)}^{2}\\
&+2\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}(t)-\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}})):\varepsilon(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}(t)-\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}}))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&+2\int_{\OS}\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}})-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t)):\varepsilon(\eta_{t_{0}}(t-t_{n_{2}})-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t))\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&<2\varepsilon\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for all }t\geq t_{n_{2}}.\end{split}

The next argument is used just to improve the convergence in elastic
energy shown in (
7.2
) to convergence in norm,

\displaystyle\|(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}})(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}^{2}<C\varepsilon.

By Theorem
8
, for
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)-\eta_{t_{0}}^{t}\|_{U_{T/2}}^{2}<\varepsilon

), we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|(\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}})(\cdot+t)\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}=0.

Since

solves a weak Dirichlet

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi(t+t_{0})-\xi(t)-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(t)\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}\|f\|_{\H 1^{1}(\OS)}

\displaystyle\to 0\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ for }t\to\infty.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{\xi}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))\cap\C^{2}(\H 1^{-1}(\OS))}=0.

We want to show that also

(7.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\ddot{\tilde{\xi}}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)-\ddot{\hat{\eta}}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)\|_{\Lp^{2}(0,T/2;\H 1^{1}(\OS)^{*})}=0

Consider again
(\eta_{t_{0}}^{t},q_{t_{0}}^{t})\in\omega_{T}
which satisfies (

) and (

) for given

and

sufficiently large. The hidden regularity result in Theorem

for

implies that

\displaystyle\|\Sigma(\eta_{t_{0}}^{t}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t))n\|_{\Lp^{2}((0,T/2)\times\dOS)}\leq C\|\eta_{t_{0}}^{t}-\hat{\eta}_{t_{0}}(\cdot+t)\|_{\C^{0}(\H 1^{1}(\OS))\cap\C^{1}(\Lp^{2}(\OS))}<C\varepsilon.

Together with (

), we conclude (
7.10
). Then using the weak Neumann formulation yields (

8.5.
Discussion of pressure waves

A simple example of bad pressure waves can be constructed in 1D: Consider
the ball
\OS=(-1,1)\in\mathbb{R}
and the wave equation with two
boundary conditions corresponding to (
4.3
):

\begin{cases}\ddot{\eta}-\Delta\eta=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times\OS,\\
\eta=0&\text{on }(0,T)\times\dOS,\\
\Sigma(\eta)n=qn&\text{on }(0,T)\times\dOS.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\eta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\cos(\pi t)\sin(\pi x),

\displaystyle q(t)

\displaystyle=-\cos(\pi t),

is a solution.

In particular,
\eta(-1,-\frac{1}{2})=1
and
\eta(-1,0)
show interprenetation
at time
t=-1
, and
\eta(0,\frac{1}{2})=1
,
\eta(0,1)=0
shows
extrapenetration from inside to beyond the fixed boundary
\eta(0,1)=0

at time
t=0
. The 2- and 3d-solutions exhibit the same structure.
Fluid-structure interaction gives a simple
explicit example of linear elasticity going wrong, motivating the
use of non-linear elasticity models for non-small data. These waves

(\eta,q)
with fluid velocity
u=0
can be added to any solution
of (
1.1
), even preserving the first initial condition
\xi(0)=\xi_{0}
(as in
\eta(-\frac{1}{2},x)=0
in 1d). Since
u=0
implies that
\OF
remains
constant, this is true also for the more complex models that allow

\OF
to change with
u
.

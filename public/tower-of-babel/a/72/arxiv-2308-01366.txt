Asymptotic behavior of solutions to the extension problem for the fractional Laplacian on noncompact symmetric spaces
Effie Papageorgiou

Abstract
. This work deals with the extension problem for the fractional Laplacian on Riemannian symmetric spaces G / K G/K of noncompact type and of general rank, which gives rise to a family of convolution operators, including the Poisson operator. More precisely, motivated by Euclidean results for the Poisson semigroup, we study the long-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the extension problem for L 1 L^{1} initial data. In the case of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, we show that if the initial data is bi- K K -invariant, then the solution to the extension problem behaves asymptotically as the mass times the fundamental solution, but this convergence may break down in the non bi- K K -invariant case. In the second part, we investigate the long-time asymptotic behavior of the extension problem associated with the so-called distinguished Laplacian on G / K G/K . In this case, we observe phenomena which are similar to the Euclidean setting for the Poisson semigroup, such as L 1 L^{1} asymptotic convergence without the assumption of bi- K K -invariance.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold and
\Delta
be its Laplace-Beltrami operator. It is well understood that the long time behavior of solutions to the heat equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)&\,=\,\Delta u(t,x),\qquad\,t>0,\,\,x\in\mathcal{M},\\[5.0pt]
u(0,x)&\,=\,u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

is strongly related to the global geometry of
\mathcal{M}
. This applies also to the heat kernel
h_{t}\left(x,y\right)
, that is, the minimal
positive fundamental solution of the heat equation or, equivalently, the
integral kernel of the heat semigroup
\exp\left(t\Delta\right)
(see
for instance
[
Gri09
]
).

The connection between the long time behavior of the
solution
u(t,x)
of (
1.1
) for initial data
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mu)
(where

\mu
is the Riemannian measure on
\mathcal{M}
) and that of the heat kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
has recently been the subject of extensive studies, see for example
[
APZ23
,
GPZ22
,
V“’az19
]
or see
[
AbAl22
,
AGMP21
,
APZ23
,
P23
]
for variants and related questions. Denote by
M=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\mu(x)}\,u_{0}(x)
the mass of the initial data. In the case when
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n}

with the Euclidean metric, the heat kernel is given by

h_{t}(x,y)\,=\,(4\pi{t})^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}

and the solution to (
1.1
) satisfies as
t\rightarrow+\infty

(1.2)

\|u(t,\,.\,)\,-\,M\,h_{t}(\,.\,,x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0

and

(1.3)

t^{\frac{n}{2}}\,\|u(t,\,.\,)\,-\,M\,h_{t}(\,.\,,x_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0.

By interpolation, a similar convergence holds with respect to any
L^{p}

norm when
1<{p}<\infty
:

t^{\frac{n}{2p^{\prime}}}\,\|u(t,\,.\,)\,-\,M\,h_{t}(\,.\,,x_{0})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
.

The situation is drastically different in hyperbolic spaces: it was shown by Vázquez
[
V“’az19
]
that (
1.2
) fails for a general initial
function
f\in L^{1}
but is still true if
f
is spherically symmetric around
x_{0}.
Similar results were
obtained in
[
APZ23
]
in a more general setting of symmetric spaces of
noncompact type by using tools of harmonic analysis. In
[
GPZ22
]
, it was shown that (
1.3
) fails on connected sums
\mathbb{R}^{n}\#\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
.

The
fractional
Laplacian is the operator
(-\Delta)^{\sigma}
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
, defined as the spectral
\sigma
-th power of the Laplace-Beltrami operator, with
\text{Dom}(-\Delta)\subset\text{Dom}((-\Delta)^{\sigma})
.
It is connected to
anomalous
diffusion, which accounts for much of the interest
in modeling with fractional equations (quasi-geostrophic flows, turbulence and water waves, molecular dynamics,
and relativistic quantum mechanics of stars). It also has various applications in probability and finance. One can obtain the fractional Laplacian through a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map extension problem,
introduced by Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
CS07
]
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This extension problem was considered for fractional powers of more general self-adjoint operators in
[
ST10
]
, where also a Poisson formula was given, as well as conditions for the existence of a fundamental solution. On certain “good” noncompact Riemannian manifolds
\mathcal{M}
(e.g. Cartan-Hadamard manifolds or manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature), the problem was studied in
[
BGS15
]
.
More precisely, let
H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})
denote the usual Sobolev space on
\mathcal{M}
. Then, for any given
v_{0}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathcal{M})
there exists a unique solution of the extension problem

(1.4)

\Delta v+\frac{(1-2\sigma)}{t}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}=0,\quad 0<\sigma<1,

with
v(0,x)=v_{0}(x)
where
t>0
,
x\in\mathcal{M}
, and the fractional Laplacian, can be recovered through

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}v_{0}(x)=-2^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\Gamma(\sigma)}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{1-2\sigma}\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(x,t).

The extension problem has drawn much attention. Since the associated literature is enormous, we shall refer indicatively to
[
ARBB22
,
BGS15
,
BP22
,
BrPa23
,
FMT13
,
RT20
,
ST10
]
and the references therein. From a probabilistic point of view, the extension problem corresponds to the property that all symmetric stable processes can be obtained as traces of degenerate Bessel diffusion processes, see
[
S19
]
.

Observe that for
\sigma=1/2
we get the Poisson semigroup
e^{-t\sqrt{-\Delta}}
. In the Euclidean case
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Poisson kernel is given by

(1.5)

\mathcal{Q}_{t}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\frac{t}{(t^{2}+|x-y|^{2})^{\frac{n+1}{2}}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;t>0.

Then, for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, it holds
[
V“’azq18
]

(1.6)

\|e^{-t\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\,-\,M\,\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\,.\,,x_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0

and

(1.7)

t^{n}\,\|e^{-t\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\,-\,M\,\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\,.\,,x_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\longrightarrow\,0.

Motivated by this, we examine the long time behavior of solutions to the extension problem with
L^{1}
initial data on noncompact symmetric spaces
\mathbb{X}=G/K
, for all
0<\sigma<1
. More precisely, let
Q_{t}^{\sigma}
be the fundamental solution to the extension problem (
1.4
), which will be called
the fractional Poisson kernel
from now on. Then our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
v_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
be bi-
K
-invariant and consider the solution
v
to the extension problem (
1.4
) with initial data
v_{0}
. Set
M=\int_{\mathbb{X}}v_{0}.
Then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow+\infty.

Moreover, this convergence fails in general without the bi-
K
-invariance assumption.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first approach to examine this property on (essentially) negatively curved manifolds. The result is new even for the case of real hyperbolic space.

Remark 1.2
.

If the bi-
K
-invariant initial data is in addition compactly supported,
we obtain the better estimate

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,C\,t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\qquad\forall\,t\geq 1,

where
C>0
is a constant and
\varepsilon
is any
positive constant such that
\varepsilon<2/(\nu+2\sigma)
,
see
Section

3.2
and
Section

4.1
. Here,
\nu
denotes the so-called dimension at infinity
of
\mathbb{X}
, see
Section

2
.

Remark 1.3
.

We also provide the following sup norm (for which no bi-
K
-invariance is needed)
and
L^{p}

(1<p<\infty
) norm estimates:

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-\,M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{O}\big(t^{-(\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\sigma)}e^{-|\rho|t}\big)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-\,M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathrm{o}\big(t^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}(\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\sigma)}e^{-\tfrac{|\rho|t}{p^{\prime}}}\big)

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
Here,
p^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
p
, defined by the formula

\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
However, the sup norm estimate (
1.9
) in the present context is relatively weaker compared to
(
1.7
) in the Euclidean setting, while the
L^{p}
norm estimate is
similar.
Here,
\rho
is the
half sum of positive roots with multiplicities, see
Section

2
. This is reminiscent of the weak
L^{\infty}
convergence for the heat equation on
\mathbb{X}
, observed first on three-dimensional real hyperbolic space
[
V“’az19
]
and generalized to arbitrary rank noncompact symmetric spaces
[
APZ23
]
.

Let
S=N(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})=(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})N
be the solvable group
occurring in the Iwasawa decomposition
G=N(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})K
.
Then
S
is identifiable, as a manifold, with the symmetric space

\mathbb{X}=G/K
.
Our second main contribution is to study the asymptotic convergence
for solutions to the extension associated with the
so-called distinguished Laplacian

\widetilde{\Delta}
on
S
.
In order to state the results, let us introduce some indispensable notation,
which will be clarified in
Section

2

and
Section

5
.
Denote by

\varphi_{0}
the ground spherical function,
by
\widetilde{\delta}
the modular function on
S
,
and by
\widetilde{Q}_{t}

the fundamental solution to the extension problem

(1.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}\widetilde{v}-\frac{(1-2\sigma)}{t}\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}-\partial^{2}_{tt}\widetilde{v}=0,\quad\widetilde{v}(\,\cdot\,,0)=\widetilde{v}_{0},\quad t>0.

Let
\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}=\widetilde{\delta}^{1/2}\varphi_{0}

be the modified ground spherical function and denote by

\widetilde{M}=\tfrac{\widetilde{v}_{0}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}}{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}}
the mass function
on
S
which generalizes the mass in the Euclidean case (see
Section

5.2
).
Then, we show the following long-time asymptotic convergence results.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\widetilde{v}_{0}
belong to the class of continuous and compactly supported functions on

S
. Then, the solution to the extension problem (
1.11
) with initial data
\widetilde{v}_{0}
satisfies

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{v}(t,\,\cdot\,)-\widetilde{M}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(S)}\,\longrightarrow\,0

and

(1.13)

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\|\widetilde{v}(t,\,\cdot\,)-\widetilde{M}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Here
\ell
denotes the rank of
G/K

and
\Sigma_{r}^{+}
the set of positive reduced roots.
Analogous
L^{p}
(
1<p<\infty
) norm estimates
follow by interpolation.

Remark 1.5
.

Let us comment on (
1.12
) and (
1.13
).
Firstly, notice that the
L^{1}
convergence (
1.12
) holds
without the restriction of bi-
K
-invariance, in contrast to
Theorem

1.1
,
and the sup norm estimate
(
1.13
)
is stronger than
(
1.9
), as in the Euclidean
setting for the Poisson semigroup.
Secondly, the mass
\widetilde{M}
is a bounded function and not necessarily a constant.
Thirdly, the power
\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|

which occurs in time factor, never coincides with the dimension at infinity

\nu=\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|
and it is equal to the topological dimension

n=\ell+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}m_{\alpha}

if and only if the following equivalent conditions hold:

•

the root system
\Sigma
is reduced and all roots have multiplicity

m_{\alpha}=1
.

•

G
is a normal real form.

This paper is organized as follows. After the present introduction
in
Section

1
and preliminaries in
Section

2
, we discuss the extension problem associated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces in
Section

3
. In
Section

4
we deal with the long-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the extension problem
associated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator on symmetric spaces. We first determine the critical region where the fractional Poisson kernel concentrates. Next,
on the one hand, for continuous compactly supported initial data, we show that both the solution and the fractional Poisson kernel vanish
asymptotically outside that critical region. On the other hand, inside the critical region we discuss the role of the additional assumption on the bi-

-invariance of the initial data. The rest of this section deals with problems for more general initial data in the

L^{p}\,(p\geq 1)
setting. In
Section

5
, we investigate the asymptotic behavior
of solutions to the extension problem associated with the distinguished Laplacian.
After specifying the critical region in this context, we study the long-time
convergence in
L^{1}
and in
L^{\infty}
with compactly supported initial
data and address some questions associated with other initial data at the end
of the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review spherical Fourier analysis
on Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type. The notation is standard
and follows
[
Hel78
,
Hel00
,
GaVa88
]
.
Next we recall bounds and asymptotics of the heat kernel, for which
we refer to
[
AnJi99
,
AnOs03
]
for more details in this setting.

2.1.
Noncompact Riemannian symmetric spaces

Let
G
be a semi-simple Lie group, connected, noncompact, with finite center,
and
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The homogeneous space

\mathbb{X}=G/K
is a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
be the Cartan decomposition
of the Lie algebra of
G
. The Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
induces
a
K
-invariant inner product
\langle\,.\,,\,.\,\rangle
on
\mathfrak{p}
,
hence a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
G/K
.
We denote by
d(\,.\,,\,.\,)
the Riemannian distance on
\mathbb{X}
.

Fix a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{p}
.
The rank of
\mathbb{X}
is the dimension
\ell
of
\mathfrak{a}
.
We identify
\mathfrak{a}
with its dual
\mathfrak{a}^{*}

by means of the inner product inherited from
\mathfrak{p}
.
Let
\Sigma\subset\mathfrak{a}
be the root system of

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
and denote by
W
the Weyl group
associated with
\Sigma
.
Once a positive Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}

has been selected,
\Sigma^{+}
(resp.
\Sigma_{r}^{+}

or
\Sigma_{s}^{+}
) denotes the corresponding set of positive roots
(resp. positive reduced, i.e., indivisible roots or simple roots).
Let
n
be the dimension and
\nu
be the pseudo-dimension
(or dimension at infinity) of
\mathbb{X}
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\textstyle n\,=\,\ell+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\nu\,=\,\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|

where
m_{\alpha}
denotes the dimension of the positive root subspace

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\,=\,\{{X\in\mathfrak{g}\,|\,[H,X]=\langle{\alpha,H}\rangle{X},\quad\forall\,H\in\mathfrak{a}}\}.

Denote by
\rho\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
the half sum of all positive roots

\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
counted with their multiplicities
m_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\rho\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,m_{\alpha}\,\alpha.

Sometimes we shall use coordinates on
\mathfrak{a}
. When we do, we always refer to the
coordinates associated to the orthonormal basis
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{\ell-1},\,\rho/|\rho|
, where
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{\ell-1},
is any orthonormal basis of
\rho^{\perp}.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the nilpotent Lie subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{g}
associated with
\Sigma^{+}

and let
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
be the corresponding
Lie subgroup of
G
. We have the decompositions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\,G\,=\,N\,(\exp\mathfrak{a})\,K\qquad&\textnormal{(Iwasawa)},\\[5.0pt]
\,G\,=\,K\,(\exp\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}})\,K\qquad&\textnormal{(Cartan)}.\end{cases}

Denote by
A(x)\in\mathfrak{a}
and
x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}

the middle components of
x\in{G}
in these two decompositions, respectively, and by

|x|=|x^{+}|
the distance to the origin.
In the Cartan decomposition, the Haar measure
on
G
writes

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,f(x)=\,|K/\mathbb{M}|\,\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k_{1}}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x^{+}}\,\delta(x^{+})\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k_{2}}\,f(k_{1}(\exp x^{+})k_{2})\,,

with density

(2.2)

\displaystyle\delta(x^{+})\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\,(\sinh\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle)^{m_{\alpha}}\,\asymp\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\Big(\frac{\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}{1+\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle}\Big)^{m_{\alpha}}\,e^{2\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}\qquad\forall\,x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Here
K
is equipped with its normalized Haar measure,
\mathbb{M}
denotes the centralizer of
\exp\mathfrak{a}
in
K
and the volume
of
K/\mathbb{M}
can be computed explicitly, see
[
AnJi99
, Eq (2.2.4)]
.

Finally, let us recall that

(2.3)

\displaystyle|x^{+}-y^{+}|\leq d(xK,yK),\quad|(yx)^{+}-y^{+}|,\;|(xy)^{+}-y^{+}|\leq d(xK,eK),

see
[
AnJi99
, Lemma 2.1.2]
or
[
MMV17
, Lemma 2.1]
.

2.2.
Spherical Fourier analysis

For this subsection, our main references are
[
GaVa88
, Chap.4]
and

[
Hel00
, Chap.IV]
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
, the spherical function

\varphi_{\lambda}
is a smooth bi-
K
-invariant eigenfunction of all

G
-invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{X}
, in particular of the
Laplace-Beltrami operator:

-\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,=\,(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x).

It is symmetric in the sense that

\varphi_{\lambda}(x^{-1})=\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)
,
and is given by the integral representation

(2.4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,=\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle{i\lambda+\rho,\,A(kx)}\rangle}.

All the elementary spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}

with parameter
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
are controlled by the ground spherical
function
\varphi_{0}
, which satisfies the global estimate

(2.5)

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(\exp{x^{+}})\,\asymp\,\Big\{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}1+\langle\alpha,x^{+}\rangle\Big\}\,e^{-\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle}\qquad\forall\,x^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Let
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G}/K)
be the Schwartz space of bi-
K
-invariant
functions on
G
. The spherical Fourier transform (Harish-Chandra transform)

\mathcal{H}
is defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,\varphi_{-\lambda}(x)\,f(x)\qquad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{a},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K}),

where
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G/K})
is the
spherical function of index
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
the subspace
of
W
-invariant functions in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})
.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{S}(K\backslash{G/K})

and
\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
. The inverse spherical Fourier transform
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle f(x)\,=\,\frac{C_{0}}{|W|}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}}{|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{2}}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{H}f(\lambda)\qquad\forall\,x\in{G},\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{a})^{W},

where the constant
C_{0}=2^{n-\ell}/(2\pi)^{\ell}|K/\mathbb{M}|
depends only
on the geometry of
\mathbb{X}
, and
|\mathbf{c(\lambda)}|^{-2}
is the
so-called Plancherel density, given by an explicit formula by Gindikin-Karpelevič. Finally, if
f
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{X}
, the Helgason-Fourier transform is defined by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\lambda,k\mathbb{M})\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,f(gK)\,e^{\langle{-i\lambda+\rho,\,A(k^{-1}g)}\rangle},

which, in view of (
2.4
), boils down to the transform (
2.6
)
when
f
is bi-
K
-invariant.

2.3.
Heat kernel on symmetric spaces

The heat kernel on
\mathbb{X}
is a positive bi-
K
-invariant right
convolution kernel, i.e.,
h_{t}(xK,yK)=h_{t}(y^{-1}x)>0
,
which is thus determined by its restriction
to the positive Weyl chamber. In fact, it symmetric, i.e.
h_{t}(x)=h_{t}(x^{-1})
.
According to the inversion formula (
2.7
) of the spherical Fourier transform,
the heat kernel is given by

(2.9)

\displaystyle h_{t}(xK)\,=\,\frac{C_{0}}{|W|}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}}{|\mathbf{c}(\lambda)|^{2}}\,\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,e^{-t(|\lambda|^{2}+|\rho|^{2})}

and satisfies the global estimate

(2.10)

\displaystyle h_{t}(\exp{H})\,\asymp\,t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\,\Big\{{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}(1+t+\langle{\alpha,H}\rangle)^{\frac{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}}{2}-1}}\Big\}\,\varphi_{0}(\exp{H})e^{-|\rho|^{2}t-\frac{|H|^{2}}{4t}}

for all
t>0
and
H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
,
see
[
AnJi99
,
AnOs03
]
. Recall that
\int_{\mathbb{X}}h_{t}=1
.

Finally, in order to describe more accurately the asymptotic behavior of the ground spherical function and of the heat kernel on certain regions, let us introduce the following functions: consider

(2.11)

\bm{\pi}(i\lambda)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\langle{\alpha,\lambda}\rangle

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(\lambda)\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\,\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\Big)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\alpha}(z)\,=\,|\alpha|^{2}\,\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle})}\,\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\langle{\alpha,\rho}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\alpha}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}\,\tfrac{\Gamma(iz+1)}{\Gamma(iz+\frac{1}{2}m_{\alpha})}\,\tfrac{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}z+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha})}{\Gamma(\frac{i}{2}z+\frac{1}{4}m_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}m_{2\alpha})}.

The function
\mathbf{b}(-\lambda)^{-1}
is holomorphic for

\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}+i\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
and positive for

3.
The fractional Laplacian and the extension problem

This section deals with the notion of the fractional Laplacian and the extension problem which gives rise to a family of operators, containing the Poisson operator.

In recent years there has been intensive research on various kinds of fractional order operators. Being nonlocal objects, local PDE techniques to treat nonlinear problems for the fractional operators do not apply. To overcome this difficulty, in the Euclidean case, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
CS07
]
studied the extension problem associated with the Laplacian and realized the fractional power as the map taking Dirichlet data to Neumann data. In
[
ST10
]
Stinga and Torrea related the extension problem for the fractional Laplacian to the heat semigroup. On certain classes of noncompact manifolds, which include symmetric spaces of noncompact type, the extension problem has been studied by Banica, González and Sáez
[
BGS15
]
. Interestingly, in the noncompact setting one needs to have a precise control of the behavior of the metric at infinity and geometry plays a crucial role.

From now on, we strictly work on symmetric spaces of noncompact type
\mathbb{X}=G/K
. To begin with, using the spectral theorem, one can define fractional powers of the Laplacian via the heat semigroup,

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}{t^{1+\sigma}}\,(e^{t\Delta}f(x)-f(x))\quad\text{ in }L^{2}(\mathbb{X}),\;f\in\text{Dom}(-\Delta).

see
[
Y95
, (5), p.260]
,
[
BGS15
]
, or
[
ST10
]
.

Then, by
[
ST10
, Theorems 1.1 and 2.1]
(see also
[
BGS15
, Theorem 1.1]
) the relation between the fractional Laplacian and the extension problem (
1.4
) is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
ST10
]
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Then for
f\in\text{Dom}((-\Delta)^{\sigma})
, a solution to the extension problem

\displaystyle\Delta v+\frac{(1-2\sigma)}{t}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}=0,\quad v(0,x)\,=\,f(x),\quad t>0,\,\;x\in\mathbb{X},

is given by

v(t,x)=v(t,gK)=f\ast Q_{t}^{\sigma}(gK)=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}g)\,f(y),\quad g\in G,

where

(3.1)

Q_{t}^{\sigma}(g)=\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}},

Moreover, the fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{X}
can be recovered through

(-\Delta)^{\sigma}f(x)=-2^{2\sigma-1}\frac{\Gamma(\sigma)}{\Gamma(1-\sigma)}\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{1-2\sigma}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(x,t).

It is worth mentioning that the existence of an integral (in fact, a right convolution) kernel on symmetric spaces for the extension problem follows from
[
ST10
, pp.2099-2101]
(see also
[
BGS15
, Theorem 3.2]
), since the heat kernel
for
t\in(0,1)
and all
x\in\mathbb{X}
satisfies

(3.2)

\|h_{t}(x,\,\cdot\,)\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{4}},\quad\|\partial_{t}h_{t}(x,\,\cdot\,)\|_{2}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{4}-1}.

This follows by an explicit computation using the heat kernel estimates (
2.10
), along with (
2.5
) and (
2.2
), while for the time derivative, one may use the pointwise estimate
[
A92
, (3.1)]
.

Observe that due to the subordination (
3.1
) to the heat kernel,
Q_{t}^{\sigma}
is a positive, bi-
K
-invariant and symmetric (in the sense that
Q_{t}^{\sigma}(g)=Q_{t}^{\sigma}(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G
) function on
G
.

We next recall some large-time upper and lower bounds for the kernel
Q_{t}^{\sigma}
proved in
[
BP22
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
BP22
, Theorem 3.2]

The fractional Poisson kernel

on
\mathbb{X}

Q_{t}^{\sigma}(\exp H)\asymp\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{4^{\sigma}\,\Gamma(\sigma)}\left(\sqrt{t^{2}+|H|^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\varphi_{0}(\exp H)~e^{-|\rho|\sqrt{t^{2}+|H|^{2}}},

For large time sup norm estimates, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.3

For

,

\displaystyle\frac{\langle{\alpha,(y^{-1}x)^{+}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\langle{\alpha,\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}}\rangle}\,\frac{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}{|x^{+}|}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\langle{\alpha,\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}}\rangle+\textrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)}{\langle{\alpha,\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}}\rangle+\textrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)}\,\Big\{{1+\textrm{O}\big(t^{-2+\varepsilon}\big)}\Big\}\,=\,1+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

It remains to prove (iv). For that, we follow
[
APZ23
, Lemma 3.8]
. Let
x=k(\exp{x^{+}})k^{\prime}

in the Cartan decomposition and consider the Iwasawa decomposition

k^{-1}y=n(k^{-1}y)(\exp{A(k^{-1}y)})k^{\prime\prime}
for some
k^{\prime\prime}\in{K}
. Then

\displaystyle d(xK,yK)\,

\displaystyle=\,d\big(k(\exp{x^{+}})K,kn(k^{-1}y)(\exp{A(k^{-1}y)})K\big)

\displaystyle=\,d\big(\exp{(-x^{+})}[n(k^{-1}y)]^{-1}(\exp{x^{+}})K,\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K\big).

and we write

\displaystyle d(xK,eK)-d(xK,yK)\,

\displaystyle=\,\overbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}d(xK,eK)-d\big(\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K,eK\big)}^{I}

\displaystyle+\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}d\big(\exp{(A(k^{-1}y)}-x^{+})K,eK\big)-d(xK,yK)}_{II}.

On the one hand,
|II|
tends exponentially fast to
0
, see
[
APZ23
]
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle I\,=\,|x^{+}|-|A(k^{-1}y)-x^{+}|\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{2\langle{x^{+},A(k^{-1}y)}\rangle-|A(k^{-1}y)|^{2}}{|x^{+}|+|A(k^{-1}y)-x^{+}|}

\displaystyle=\,\big\langle{\tfrac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|},A(k^{-1}y)}\big\rangle\,+\textrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{|x^{+}|}\big)

\displaystyle=\,\big\langle{\tfrac{\rho}{|\rho|},A(k^{-1}y)}\big\rangle\,+\textrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)

by using
(ii)
, the fact that
\{{A(k^{-1}y)\,|\,k\in{K}}\}

is a compact subset of
\mathfrak{a}
and that
\textrm{O}\big(\tfrac{1}{|x^{+}|}\big)=\textrm{O}\big(t^{-2+\varepsilon}\big)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x\in K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
and let
y
be bounded. Then

\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle-\langle\rho,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle=|\rho||x^{+}|-|\rho||(y^{-1}x)^{+}|+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

Proof.

Since the rank one case is trivial, let us consider
\ell\geq 2
. Observe first that the claim follows by

(3.11)

\displaystyle\cos(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})=\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|x^{+}|^{-1}).

Indeed, by (
3.11
) and taking into account that
\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})=1+\textrm{O}(t^{-\varepsilon})
, we get

\displaystyle\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle-\langle\rho,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\rho||x^{+}|\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})-|\rho||(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\cos(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})

\displaystyle=|\rho|\,|x^{+}|\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})-|\rho|\,|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\,(\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|x^{+}|^{-1}))

\displaystyle=|\rho||x^{+}|-|\rho||(y^{-1}x)^{+}|+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

Therefore, it remains to prove (
3.11
). Observe that by (
3.2
), we have

\sin^{2}\left(\frac{(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})}{2}\right)=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\widehat{(x^{+},\rho)}}{2}\right)+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+\varepsilon}),

whence
(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
.
Next, recall the coordinates on
\mathfrak{a}
with respect to the basis
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{\ell-1},\rho/|\rho|
introduced in Section
2
, and write

x^{+}=\left(\xi,\,\xi_{\ell}\right),\qquad(y^{-1}x)^{+}=\left(\zeta,\,\zeta_{\ell}\right).

Since
\langle x^{+},\rho\rangle=\xi_{\ell}\,|\rho|
, we get

\xi_{\ell}=|x^{+}|\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho}),\qquad|\xi|=|x^{+}|\sin(\widehat{x^{+},\rho}).

Similarly,

\zeta_{\ell}=|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\cos(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho}),\qquad|\zeta|=|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\sin(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho}).

Therefore

(3.12)

\frac{|\xi|}{\xi_{\ell}}=\tan(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),\qquad|x^{+}|\asymp\xi_{\ell}

and

(3.13)

\frac{|\zeta|}{\zeta_{\ell}}=\tan(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),\qquad|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\asymp\zeta_{\ell}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left|\cos(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})-\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})\right||x^{+}|

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\zeta_{\ell}\,|x^{+}|-\xi_{\ell}\,|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\right|}{|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\left|\zeta_{\ell}\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+\xi_{\ell}^{2}}-\xi_{\ell}\sqrt{|\zeta|^{2}+\zeta_{\ell}^{2}}\right|}{\zeta_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\left|\zeta_{\ell}^{2}\,|\xi|^{2}-\xi_{\ell}^{2}\,|\zeta|^{2}\right|}{\zeta_{\ell}^{2}\,\xi_{\ell}\left(\sqrt{\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\xi_{\ell}}\right)^{2}+1}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{\zeta_{\ell}}\right)^{2}+1}\right)}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\left|\zeta_{\ell}\,|\xi|-\xi_{\ell}\,|\zeta|\right|\left|\zeta_{\ell}\,|\xi|+\xi_{\ell}\,|\zeta|\right|}{\zeta_{\ell}^{2}\,\xi_{\ell}},

due to (
3.12
) and (
3.13
). The fact that
(y^{-1}x)^{+}=x^{+}+\textrm{O}(1)
implies
\zeta_{\ell}=\xi_{\ell}+\textrm{O}(1)
and
|\zeta|=|\xi|+\textrm{O}(1)
, therefore

(3.15)

\frac{\zeta_{\ell}\,|\xi|-\xi_{\ell}\,|\zeta|}{\xi_{\ell}}=\textrm{O}(1),\qquad\frac{\zeta_{\ell}\,|\xi|+\xi_{\ell}\,|\zeta|}{\zeta_{\ell}^{2}}=\textrm{O}\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{\zeta_{\ell}}\right)=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

Altogether, we conclude by (
3.2
) and (
3.15
) that

\cos(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})-\cos(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}|x^{+}|^{-1}).

∎

The next lemma is the heart of the proof.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume that
x=k\exp(x^{+})k^{\prime}
is in the critical region
K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
and that
y
is bounded. Then,

\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(xK,yK)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(xK,eK)}=e^{\langle 2\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),

for
0<\varepsilon<2/(\nu+2\sigma)
. Here,
\nu=\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|
is the dimension at infinity.

Proof.

By (
3.6
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}x)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)}\sim\frac{\textbf{b}\left(-i|\rho|\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}^{\,-\sigma-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-|\rho|\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}\,\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}x)}{\textbf{b}\left(-i|\rho|\frac{x^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}^{\,-\sigma-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-|\rho|\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}\,\varphi_{0}(x)}.

Our aim is to show that for
x
inside the critical region and
y
bounded, the following asymptotics hold;

(i)

\frac{\textbf{b}\left(-i|\rho|\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}}{\textbf{b}\left(-i|\rho|\frac{x^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}}=1+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)
;

(ii)

\left(\frac{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}\right)^{k}=1+\textnormal{O}\left(t^{-2+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma)}\right)
, where
k=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma+\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)
;

(iii)

\exp\left\{-|\rho|\left(\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}-\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}\right)\right\}=\langle{\rho,A(k^{-1}y)}\rangle+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+2\varepsilon})
;

(iv)

\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}x)}{\varphi_{0}(x)}=\langle{\rho,A(k^{-1}y)}\rangle+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)
;

whence the claim for
Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}x)/Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)
follows, choosing
\varepsilon
small enough.

We start the proof of (i)-(iv) by some preliminary observations. Write

r=|x^{+}|=d(xK,eK),\qquad s=|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|=d(xK,yK),

and let
d(yK,eK)<\xi
, for some
\xi>0
. Then, for
x
inside the critical region, and
t
large enough we have

t^{2-\varepsilon}\leq r\leq t^{2+\varepsilon},\qquad\frac{1}{2}\,t^{2-\varepsilon}\leq s\leq 2\,t^{2+\varepsilon},\qquad|r-s|\leq\xi.

Also,
we have
t^{2}+r^{2}\asymp r^{2}
and
t^{2}+s^{2}\asymp s^{2}
. Finally, in the proof of Lemma
3.8
it was shown that the angle of
(y^{-1}x)^{+}
with the
\rho
-axis is
\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
.

Proof of (i).
Observe first that owing to (
2.12
), for all
g\in G
such that
t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\asymp|g^{+}|^{2}
, we have

(3.16)

\textbf{b}\left(-i|\rho|\frac{g^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|g|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\asymp 1.

Therefore, for
x\in K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
and
y
bounded, by the mean value theorem we get

\displaystyle\left|\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{|\rho|x^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}-\textbf{b}\left(-i\frac{|\rho|(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}\right)^{-1}\right|\lesssim\left|\frac{x^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}-\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}\right|,

where we have used the derivative bound (
2.13
) and (
3.16
). Next, owing to Lemma
3.7
(ii), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{x^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|x^{+}|^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{+}}{|x^{+}|}\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{|x^{+}|^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}=\left(\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)\right)\left(1+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+2\varepsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{|\rho|}+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}),

since
0<\varepsilon<2/(\nu+2\sigma)<2/3
. Working likewise for
\frac{(y^{-1}x)^{+}}{\sqrt{t^{2}+|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|^{2}}}
, the claim follows using (
3.16
).

Proof of (ii).
We use a similar mean value argument applied to
(t^{2}+(.)^{2})^{k}
,
k>1
, so that for some
r_{0}
between
r
and
s
we have

(3.17)

\left|\frac{(t^{2}+r^{2})^{k}}{(t^{2}+s^{2})^{k}}-1\right|\lesssim\frac{r_{0}\,(t^{2}+r_{0}^{2})^{k-1}}{(t^{2}+s^{2})^{k}}.

Given that
r_{0}\lesssim t^{2+\varepsilon}
,
s\gtrsim t^{2-\varepsilon}
and
k=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma+\frac{\nu}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)
we get the desired result.

Proof of (iii).
We first claim that

(3.18)

\frac{r+s}{\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}+\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}}}=1+O\left(t^{-2+2\varepsilon}\right).

Indeed, consider the function
f(\tau)=\sqrt{\tau^{2}+r^{2}}+\sqrt{\tau^{2}+s^{2}}
,
\tau\geq 0
, and observe that the left hand side of (
3.18
) is equal to
f(0)/f(t)
. Then, the mean value theorem for
f
in
[0,t]
together with the fact that

f^{\prime}(\tau)\lesssim\frac{\tau}{r}+\frac{\tau}{s}\lesssim t^{-1+\varepsilon},\qquad f(\tau)\gtrsim t^{2-\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\tau\in[0,t],

yield the claimed asymptotics (
3.18
).
Finally, in Lemma
3.7
(iv) it was shown that

(3.19)

r-s=d(xK,eK)-d(xK,yK)=\langle\frac{\rho}{|\rho|},A(k^{-1}y)\rangle+\textnormal{O}\big(t^{-2+\varepsilon}\big).

Therefore, by (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we get

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-|\rho|\left(\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}}-\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{|\rho|(r-s)\frac{r+s}{\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}+\sqrt{t^{2}+s^{2}}}\right\}

\displaystyle=e^{\langle\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+2\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=e^{\langle\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+2\varepsilon}),

which proves (iii).

Proof of (iv).
Since the angles of both
x^{+}
and
(y^{-1}x)^{+}
with the
\rho
-axis are
\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
we may use the ground spherical asymptotics (
2.14
).
On the one hand, by Lemma
3.7
(iii), we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{\pi}((y^{-1}x)^{+})}{\bm{\pi}(x^{+})}\,=\,\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\frac{\langle{\alpha,(y^{-1}x)^{+}}\rangle}{\langle{\alpha,x^{+}}\rangle}\,=\,1+\textrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big).

On the other hand, using Lemma
3.8
and Lemma
3.7
(iv), we have

\displaystyle e^{\langle\rho,x^{+}\rangle-\langle\rho,(y^{-1}x)^{+}\rangle}

\displaystyle=e^{\langle\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+\varepsilon})}=e^{\langle\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+\varepsilon}),

with which the proof of (iv) is complete.

Altogether, we have

\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(xK,yK)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(xK,eK)}=e^{\langle 2\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

∎

4.
Asymptotic convergence associated with
the extension problem for the Laplace-Beltrami operator

We first consider continuous compactly supported initial data
v_{0}
. We work separately outside and inside the critical region: we will show that

\|v_{0}\ast Q_{t}^{\sigma}-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(G\smallsetminus K(\exp\Omega_{t})K)}\rightarrow 0

but inside
K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
, unless
v_{0}
is bi-
K
-invariant, the convergence to the fundamental solution may break down.

4.1.
Estimates outside the critical region

In this subsection, we show that the solution
v(t,x)
to the extension problem
vanishes asymptotically
in
L^{1}(G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Then the desired convergence follows by the triangle inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x\in G\smallsetminus K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
and
y\in K(\exp B(0,\xi))K
. Denote by
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)
the solid cone around the
\rho
-axis of angle
\frac{1}{2}\,t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
. Consider in
\mathfrak{a}
the set

\Omega_{t}^{\prime\prime}=\left(B(0,2\,t^{2+\varepsilon})\smallsetminus B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\,t^{2-\varepsilon}\right)\right)\cap\Gamma^{\prime\prime}(t).

Then,

y^{-1}x\in G\smallsetminus K(\exp\Omega_{t}^{\prime\prime})K.

Proof.

Let
x\in G\smallsetminus K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
and
|y|<\xi
. Recall that by (
2.3
)

\displaystyle|(y^{-1}x)^{+}-x^{+}|\,\leq\,d(yK,eK)\,=\,|y|\,<\,\xi,

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\,\leq\,|x^{+}|+\xi\,<\,t^{2-\varepsilon}+\xi\,<\,2\,t^{2-\varepsilon},\\
|(y^{-1}x)^{+}|\,\geq\,|x^{+}|-\xi\,>\,t^{2+\varepsilon}-\xi>\,\frac{1}{2}\,t^{2+\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

for
t
large enough. In other words,

\displaystyle x\,\in\,G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\{B(0,t^{2+\varepsilon})\smallsetminus B(0,t^{2-\varepsilon})\})K}\;\Longrightarrow\;y^{-1}x\,\in\,G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\{B(0,\frac{1}{2}t^{2+\varepsilon})\smallsetminus B(0,2\,t^{2-\varepsilon})\})K}.

We finally turn to the angles. Write
\phi=(\widehat{x^{+},\rho})
and
\omega=(\widehat{(y^{-1}x)^{+},\rho})
, and observe that by (
3.2
), we have

(4.1)

\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)=\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)+\textrm{O}(t^{-2+\varepsilon}).

Using that

\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\,\phi^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\,t^{-\varepsilon},\qquad\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\omega}{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}\,\omega^{2},

we get that
\omega\geq\frac{1}{2}t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
, for
t
large enough. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The solution to the extension problem satisfies

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{1}(G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K})}\,\lesssim\,t^{-\sigma\varepsilon}

for
t>0
large enough.

Proof.

Let
\xi>0
be a constant such that the compact support of
v_{0}

belongs to
K(\exp{B(0,\xi)})K
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{G\,\smallsetminus\,K(\exp\Omega_{t})K}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,|v(t,x)|\,

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\int_{K(\exp{B(0,\xi)})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,|v_{0}(y)|\,\int_{G\,\smallsetminus\,K(\exp\Omega_{t})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\int_{K(\exp{B(0,\xi)})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,|v_{0}(y)|\,\int_{G\,\smallsetminus\,K(\exp\Omega_{t}^{\prime\prime})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{z}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(z)

where
\Omega_{t}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
is the region described in Lemma
4.1
.
Thus, working as in Proposition
3.4
, one can show that the right-hand side of
the inequality above is
\textrm{O}(t^{-\sigma\varepsilon})
. In conclusion,

\displaystyle\int_{G\,\smallsetminus\,K(\exp\Omega_{t})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,|v(t,x)|\,\lesssim\,t^{-\sigma\varepsilon}.

∎

4.2.
Long-time behavior inside the critical region

Let now
x\in K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
. By Lemma
3.9
, the right-
K
-invariance of
A(k^{-1}.)
and
v_{0}
, and the definition (
2.8
) of the Helgason-Fourier transform we have that

\displaystyle v_{0}\ast Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,(Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}x)-Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x))v_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,\left(\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}x)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)}-1\right)v_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\left\{\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,\left(e^{\langle 2\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}-1+\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})\right)v_{0}(y)\right\}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\,\big(\widehat{v_{0}}(i\rho,k\mathbb{M})\,-\,\widehat{v_{0}}(-i\rho,k\mathbb{M})\,+\,\textrm{O}\big(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\big)\big).

Notice that
\widehat{v_{0}}(\pm\,i\rho,k\mathbb{M})=\mathcal{H}v_{0}(\pm\,i\rho)=M
when
v_{0}
is bi-
K
-invariant. Then we deduce the desired convergence by integrating (
4.3
) over the critical region:

(4.4)

\int_{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,|v_{0}\ast Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\,-\,M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)|\,=\textrm{O}(t^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}).

On the other hand, using again the Cartan decomposition we have

\displaystyle\int_{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,|v_{0}\ast Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\,-\,M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)|\,

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\,\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,\Big|\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,v_{0}(y)\big(e^{\langle 2\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}\,\,-\,1\big)\Big|

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. The last integral is not constantly zero when
v_{0}
is not bi-
K
-invariant. For example, consider
v_{0}
to be a Dirac measure supported on some point
yK
other than the origin, thus for
y\notin K
. In other words, the solution now coincides with
Q_{t}(.,yK)
and the mass is equal to
1
. In this case, however, the last integral is equal to
\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,\Big|e^{\langle 2\rho,A(k^{-1}y)\rangle}\,\,-\,1\,\Big|
, thus does not vanish identically.

4.3.
Long-time convergence for general bi-
K
-invariant data

In this subsection, using the results of the previous two ones and a standard density argument, we prove Theorem
1.1
for the whole class of
L^{1}(\mathbb{X}
) functions that are bi-
K
-invariant. The argument is identical to that of
[
APZ23
, Subsection 3.3]
but we include it for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
v_{0}\in L^{1}(K\backslash{G}/K)
and
V_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(K\backslash{G}/K)

be such that
\|v_{0}-V_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}
.
Denote by
M=\int_{G}v_{0}
and
M_{V}=\int_{G}V_{0}
the
masses of
v_{0}
and
V_{0}
respectively, then

\displaystyle|M-M_{V}|\,\leq\,\|v_{0}-V_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,<\,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Let
V(t,x)=V_{0}*Q_{t}^{\sigma}(x)
be the solution to
the extension problem with initial data
V_{0}
. We deduce from (
4.4
), (
4.2
) and
Proposition
3.3
that, there exists
T>0
such that

\displaystyle\|V(t,\,\cdot\,)-M_{V}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\,\|V(t,\,\cdot\,)-M_{V}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(K(\exp\Omega_{t})K)}\,+\,\|V(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{1}(G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K})}

\displaystyle+\,|M_{V}|\,\|Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\Omega_{t})K})}

\displaystyle<\,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}

for all
t\geq{T}
. In conclusion,

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\overbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-V(t,\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}}^{\leq\,\|v_{0}-V_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\,\|Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}}+\overbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\|M_{V}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}}^{\leq\,|M-M_{V}|\,\|Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}}}

\displaystyle+\|V(t,\,\cdot\,)-M_{V}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle<\,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{3}\,=\varepsilon

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
t
large enough.
∎

Let us turn to the long-time convergence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{X})
with
p>1
. We first deal with the case
p=\infty
and conclude for all
1<p<\infty
by convexity.
Proposition
3.3
gives us the sup norm estimate:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\leq\,\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}\,\|Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,+\,|M|\,\|Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\lesssim\,t^{\sigma-\frac{\ell}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}e^{-|\rho|t}

for
t
large and for all
f\in{L^{1}(\mathbb{X})}
.
Notice that such an estimate holds without the bi-
K
-invariance assumption.
By convexity, we obtain the following estimates in the
L^{p}(\mathbb{X})

setting.

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

1.1
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|v(t,\,\cdot\,)-M\,Q_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\,=\,\mathrm{o}\big(t^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}(\sigma-\frac{\ell}{2}-\frac{1}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|)}e^{-\frac{|\rho|t}{p^{\prime}}}\big)\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow+\infty

for all
1<p<\infty
.

Remark 4.4
.

The sup norm estimate (
4.5
) is weaker
compared to the results in the Euclidean setting. More precisely, on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Poisson semigroup (
\sigma=1/2
) satisfies the strong convergence (
1.7
) (recall that
\|\mathcal{Q}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\asymp t^{-n}
). However, this is not true on noncompact symmetric spaces.
Indeed, in the lines of
[
APZ23
, Remark 3.6]
, consider the Poisson kernel
Q_{t}^{1/2}
as well as a “delayed” Poisson kernel
Q_{t+t^{\prime}}^{1/2}
for some
t^{\prime}>0
to be determined
later. Recall that
\nu=\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|
. Then

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}e^{|\rho|t}\,\|Q_{t+t^{\prime}}^{1/2}-Q_{t}^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\geq\,t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}e^{|\rho|t}\,\big(Q_{t}^{1/2}(eK)-Q_{t+t^{\prime}}^{1/2}(eK)\big)

since
Q_{t}^{1/2}(eK)
is decreasing in
t
, as seen by the subordination formula.
According to (
3.3
), there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{|\rho|t}\,\big(Q_{t}^{1/2}(eK)-Q_{t+t^{\prime}}^{1/2}(eK)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\,t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{|\rho|t}\,\big\{{\tfrac{1}{C}\,t^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{-|\rho|t}\,-\,C(t+t^{\prime})^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{-|\rho|(t+t^{\prime})}}\big\}

\displaystyle=\,C^{-1}\,-\,C\big(\tfrac{t}{t+t^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{-|\rho|t^{\prime}}\geq\,\tfrac{1}{2C}\,,

provided that
t^{\prime}>\frac{2\ln{C}+\ln 2}{|\rho|}
.
Hence

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\nu}{2}}\,e^{|\rho|t}\,\|Q_{t+t^{\prime}}^{1/2}-Q_{t}^{1/2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{X})}\,\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\displaystyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 3.75pt\kern-5.27776pt$\textstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 2.625pt\kern-4.45831pt$\scriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern 1.875pt\kern-3.95834pt$\scriptscriptstyle\not$\hss}{\longrightarrow}}}\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow+\infty.

5.
Asymptotic convergence associated with
the extension problem for the distinguished Laplacian

Let
S=N(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})=(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})N
be the solvable group
occurring in the Iwasawa decomposition
G=N(\exp{\mathfrak{a}})K
.
Then
S
is identifiable, as a manifold, with the symmetric space

\mathbb{X}=G/K
. The distinguished Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
on
S
is
given by the conjugation of the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator

\Delta+|\rho|^{2}
on
\mathbb{X}
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}\,=\,\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}\circ(\Delta+|\rho|^{2})\circ\widetilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

where the modular function
\widetilde{\delta}
of
S
is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}(g)\,=\,\widetilde{\delta}(n(\exp{A}))\,=\,e^{-2\langle{\rho,A}\rangle}\qquad\forall\,g\in{S}.

Here
n=n(g)
and
A=A(g)
denotes respectively the
N
-component and the

\mathfrak{a}
-component of
g
in the Iwasawa decomposition.
The distinguished Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
is left-
S
-invariant and
self-adjoint with respect to the right-invariant Haar measure on
S
:

\displaystyle\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,f(g)=\,\int_{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{n}\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{A}\,f(n(\exp{A}))\,=\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{A}\,e^{2\langle{\rho,A}\rangle}\int_{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{n}f((\exp{A})n).

The connection between the measures on
S

and the unimodular Haar measure on
G
is given as follows,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,f(g)\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,e^{2\langle{\rho,A(g)}\rangle}f(g)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{\ell}{g}\,f(g)\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,f(g).

Recall the heat equation associated with the distinguished Laplacian:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}(t,g)\,=\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{g}\widetilde{v}(t,g),\qquad\widetilde{v}(0,g)\,=\,\widetilde{v}_{0}(g),

where the corresponding heat kernel is given by

\widetilde{h}_{t}=\widetilde{\delta}^{1/2}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}t}h_{t}

in the sense that

\displaystyle(e^{t\widetilde{\Delta}\,}f)(g)\,=\,(f*\widetilde{h}_{t})(g)\,=\,\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{\ell}{y}\,f(y)\,\widetilde{h}_{t}(y^{-1}g)\,=\,\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{y}\,f(gy^{-1})\,\widetilde{h}_{t}(y).

Here, we still denote by
*
the convolution product on
S
or on
G
. We refer
to
[
Bou83
,
CGGM91
]
for more details about the distinguished Laplacian.

The extension problem for
\widetilde{\Delta}
on
S
writes

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widetilde{\Delta}\widetilde{v}-\frac{(1-2\sigma)}{t}\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}-\partial^{2}_{tt}\widetilde{v}=0,\qquad\;t>0,\\
\widetilde{u}(\,\cdot\,,0)=\widetilde{v}_{0}\end{cases}

which admits a fundamental solution
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
. The use of the latter is justified by the arguments in
[
ST10
]
discussed in Section
3
, and more precisely by (
3.2
), by the fact that

\partial_{t}\widetilde{h}_{t}=\widetilde{\delta}^{1/2}\,\partial_{t}(e^{(|\rho|^{2}t}h_{t})=\widetilde{\delta}^{1/2}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}t}\big(|\rho|^{2}\,h_{t}+\partial_{t}h_{t}\big),\quad t\in(0,1),

and by the observation that if
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{X})
and
\widetilde{f}=\widetilde{\delta}^{1/2}f
, then by (
5.2
) we have

\int_{S}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{r}g\,\widetilde{f}^{2}(g)=\int_{S}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{r}g\,e^{-2\langle\rho,A(g)\rangle}f^{2}(g)=\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,f^{2}(g).

Therefore, by the Stinga-Torrea subordination formula we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,\widetilde{h}_{u}(g)\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g)\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}=:\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g)Q^{\sigma,0}(g).

Remark 5.1
.

Notice that
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\textrm{d}_{r}{g}
is a probability measure
on
S
. Indeed, this follows from the subordination formula (
5.5
) and the fact that
\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,\widetilde{h}_{t}(g)=1
.

The first subsection is devoted to determine the critical region
where the kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
concentrates.
In the next two subsections, we study respectively the
L^{1}

and the
L^{\infty}
asymptotic convergences of solutions to (
5.3
)
with compactly supported initial data (no bi-
K
-invariance required).
We discuss the same questions for other initial data in the last subsection.

5.1.
Asymptotic concentration of the fractional Poisson kernel associated to the distinguished Laplacian

We first give large time asymptotics for the distinguished extension kernel. More precisely, we prove the following upper and lower bounds.

Proposition 5.2
.

The fractional Poisson kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
on
S
,
0<\sigma<1
, associated with the distinguished Laplacian, satisfies the upper and lower bounds

(5.7)

\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\asymp\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g)\,\varphi_{0}(g)\,t^{2\sigma}\,(t+|g^{+}|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma},

if
t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\geq 1
.

Proof.

Recall that by the subordination formula (
5.6
), we may write

\frac{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}{t^{2\sigma}}\widetilde{\delta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(g)\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)=\int_{0}^{t^{2}+|g|^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}+\int_{t^{2}+|g|^{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}},

and observe that due to (
2.10
) we have

u^{-1-\sigma}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}\asymp\varphi_{0}(g)\,u^{-\frac{n}{2}-1-\sigma}\,\Big\{{\prod_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}(1+u+\langle{\alpha,g^{+}}\rangle)^{\frac{m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha}}{2}-1}}\Big\}\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}}{4u}},\quad u>0.

Let
t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\geq 1
. As far as upper bounds are concerned, the claim follows in the first interval
(0,t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}]
by estimating
1+u+\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle\lesssim t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}
. For the second interval
(t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2},+\infty)
we estimate
1+u+\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle\lesssim u
, and take into account that
\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})=n-\ell.

The lower bound follows writing

1+u+\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle\geq u

and integrating over
(t^{2}+|g|^{2},+\infty)
. We omit the details.
∎

Recall that the extension kernel
Q_{t}^{\sigma}
associated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator
concentrates in
K(\exp\Omega_{t})K
, where
\Omega_{t}
is described in Proposition
3.4
.
The following proposition shows that the kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}

(associated with the extension problem for the distinguished Laplacian) concentrates in a different region.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1
. Consider in
\mathfrak{a}
the annulus

(5.8)

B(0,t^{1+\varepsilon})\smallsetminus B(0,t^{1-\varepsilon}).

Then, the fractional Poisson kernel associated with the distinguished Laplacian on
S

concentrates asymptotically in
K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K
. In other words,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{g\in{S}\,\textrm{s.t.}\,g^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,=\,0

where
g^{+}
denotes the middle component of
g
in the Cartan decomposition.

Proof.

By using (
5.2
) and (
5.6
), write

\displaystyle I(t)={\int_{g\in{S}\,\textrm{ s.t.}\,g^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)=\int_{G\smallsetminus K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(g)}\rangle}Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g).

Since
Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}
is bi-
K
-invariant on
G
, writing

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}
for the normalized Haar measure on the compact group
K
, and using Proposition
5.2
we have

\displaystyle I(t)\,

\displaystyle\asymp t^{2\sigma}\,\int_{G\smallsetminus K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,\varphi_{0}(g)\,(t+|g|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\int_{K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{k}\,e^{\langle{\rho,A(kg)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\asymp t^{2\sigma}\,\int_{G\smallsetminus K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,\varphi_{0}(g)^{2}\,(t+|g|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}.

According to the Cartan decomposition, and to the estimates
(
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle I(t)\,

\displaystyle\asymp\,t^{2\sigma}\,\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{c}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g^{+}}\,\delta(g^{+})\,(t+|g^{+}|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\,\varphi_{0}(\exp{g^{+}})^{2}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,t^{2\sigma}\,\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{c}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g^{+}}\,(t+|g^{+}|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\,(1+|g^{+}|)^{2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}.

Next, let us study the right-hand side of (
5.1
)
outside
\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}
. On the one hand, if
|g^{+}|<t^{1-\varepsilon}
then
t+|g^{+}|\asymp t
, so

\displaystyle t^{2\sigma}\int_{|g^{+}|<t^{1-\varepsilon}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g^{+}}\,(t+|g^{+}|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}(1+|g^{+}|)^{2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{t^{1-\varepsilon}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{\ell-1}t^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\,(1+r)^{2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\varepsilon(\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|)}.

On the other hand, if
|g^{+}|>t^{1+\varepsilon}
then
t+|g^{+}|\asymp|g^{+}|
, so we have

\displaystyle t^{2\sigma}\int_{|g^{+}|>t^{1+\varepsilon}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g^{+}}\,(t+|g^{+}|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}(1+|g^{+}|)^{2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2\sigma}\int_{t^{1+\varepsilon}}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\,r^{\ell-1}r^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\,(1+r)^{2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\varepsilon\sigma}.

In other words, we have proved that
I(t)=\textrm{O}(t^{-\sigma\varepsilon})
, therefore the Poisson kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
associated with the
distinguished Laplacian on
S
concentrates asymptotically in

K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The critical region for the fractional Poisson kernel associated with the distinguished Laplacian is similar to that of its Euclidean counterpart.

We now obtain precise long-time asymptotics of the kernel

Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)=\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}},

which are crucial for our proof, by a slightly more general result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
g\in S
such that
g^{+}\in\widetilde{\Omega_{t}}
. Then, as
t+|g^{+}|\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)\sim\widetilde{C}(\sigma)\,t^{2\sigma}\,\varphi_{0}(\exp g^{+})\left(t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-\sigma},

where the constant is

\widetilde{C}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}C_{0}\,2^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\pi^{\frac{\ell}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma\right)\bm{\pi}(\widetilde{\rho})\bm{b}(0)^{-2},

with
\widetilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}\alpha
and
C_{0}=2^{n-\ell}/(2\pi)^{\ell}|K/\mathbb{M}|
.

Proof.

The proof follows arguments for the asymptotics of the Poisson kernel (
\sigma=1/2
) in
[
AnJi99
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.

Let
0<\eta<1
and
t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}>1
. In view of the subordination formula (
5.6
), let us split

\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}\,h_{u}(g)\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2\sigma}}{2^{2\sigma}\Gamma(\sigma)}\{J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}\},

where the quantities
J_{1},J_{2}
and
J_{3}
are defined by the integration over the intervals
\big[0,(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1-\eta}\big),\big[(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1-\eta},(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1+\eta}\big)
and
\big[(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1+\eta},\infty\big)
respectively.

We claim that the main contribution comes from the middle integral
J_{2}
. Indeed, for the first integral
J_{1}
, working as in Proposition
5.2
, we get
J_{1}=\textrm{O}((t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{-\infty}\varphi_{0}(g))
while, similarly, for the third integral we get
J_{3}=\textrm{O}((t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{-(1+\eta)(\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma)}\varphi_{0}(g))
.

We now consider
J_{2}
. Define

(5.11)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(t,g^{+})=t^{\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,\varphi_{0}(\exp{g^{+}})^{-1}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}t+\frac{|g^{+}|^{2}}{4t}}\,h_{t}(\exp{g^{+}}),\quad t>0,\,g^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}.

Then, by (
5.11
) we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1-\eta}}^{(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1+\eta}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{u^{1+\sigma}}\,e^{|\rho|^{2}u}h_{u}(\exp g^{+})\,e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4u}}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(\exp g^{+})\int_{(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1-\eta}}^{(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{1+\eta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\,u^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-\sigma-1}e^{-\frac{t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}}{4u}}h^{\prime}(u,g^{+})

\displaystyle=2^{\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+2\sigma}(t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2})^{-\frac{\ell}{2}-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-\sigma}\int_{\frac{1}{4}(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{-\eta}}^{\frac{1}{4}(t^{2}+|g|^{2})^{\eta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\,u^{\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma-1}e^{-u}h^{\prime}\left(\frac{t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}}{4u},g^{+}\right).

Since

h^{\prime}\left(\frac{t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}}{4u},g^{+}\right)\longrightarrow C_{2}\,\bm{b}(0)^{-1},

uniformly as
t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\rightarrow+\infty
, by (
2.15
) and contradiction (see
[
AnJi99
, p.1086]
), the Laplace method we obtain that the last integral tends to

C_{2}\,\bm{b}(0)^{-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma\right).

Since
J_{1},J_{3}
are very small compared to
J_{2}
for
t
large, substituting the value of
C_{2}
(see (
2.15
)) we finally get the claimed asymptotics.
∎

5.2.
Heat asymptotics in
L^{1}
for compactly supported initial data

In this subsection, we investigate the long-time asymptotic convergence in

L^{1}(S)
of solutions to the Cauchy problem (
5.3
), where the initial data

\widetilde{v}_{0}
is assumed continuous and compactly supported in
B(eK,\xi)
.
Let

\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}=\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi_{0}

be the modified ground spherical function. The mass function is defined by

(5.12)

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}(g)\,=\,\frac{(\widetilde{v}_{0}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{0})(g)}{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0}(g)}\qquad\forall\,g\in{S}.

By using the fact that the modular function
\widetilde{\delta}
is a character
on
S
, we can also write the mass as

(5.13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}(g)\,=\,\tfrac{1}{\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\varphi_{0}(g)}\,\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{\ell}{y}\,v_{0}(gK)\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\widetilde{\delta}(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\delta}(y^{-1}g)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)=\,\frac{(v_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}

where
v_{0}(gK)=\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{v}_{0}(g)

is a right
K
-invariant function on
G
,
with compact support
(\supp\widetilde{v}_{0})K
.

The following properties of the mass function were already observed in
[
APZ23
, Remarks 4.5 and 4.6]
:

Remark 5.6
.

1. If
\widetilde{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(S)
,
then the mass function
\widetilde{M}
is bounded. This follows from the fact that

(5.14)

\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\leq C(\xi)\quad\text{if}\quad|y|<\xi.

2. The mass function
\widetilde{M}
is a constant
if
v_{0}
is bi-
K
-invariant and

\widetilde{v}_{0}=\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{0}
belongs to
L^{1}(S)
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}\,=\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,v_{0}(y)\,\varphi_{0}(y)=\,\mathcal{H}v_{0}(0).

The following lemma plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 5.7
.

For bounded
y\in{G}
and for all
g
in the critical region

K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K
, the following asymptotic behavior holds:

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\mathrm{O}\Big(t^{-1+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma-1)}\Big)\qquad\textnormal{as}\,\,\,t\rightarrow+\infty,

for
0<\varepsilon<1/(\nu+2\sigma-1)
. Here,
\nu=\ell+2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|.

Proof.

Assume that
|y|\leq\xi
for some positive constant
\xi
.
Recall that for every
H\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}
, we have

t^{1-\varepsilon}\leq|H|\leq t^{1+\varepsilon}
.
Notice also that

\displaystyle|(y^{-1}g)^{+}-g^{+}|\,\leq\,|y|\,<\,\xi

according to (
2.3
). Then, for
t
large enough, we deduce the following estimates:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|\,\leq\,|g^{+}|+\xi\,<\,t^{1+\varepsilon}+\xi\,<2\,t^{1+\varepsilon},\\[5.0pt]
|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|\,\geq\,|g^{+}|-\xi\,>\,t^{1-\varepsilon}-\xi\,>\frac{1}{2}\,t^{1-\varepsilon},\\[5.0pt]
\end{cases}

In other words, we obtain

\displaystyle y^{-1}g\,\in\,K(\exp{\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime}})K\qquad\forall\,g\in{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K},\,\,\forall\,|y|<\xi,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime}\,=\,\big\{{g\in S:\;\frac{1}{2}\,t^{1-\varepsilon}\leq|g^{+}|\leq 2\,t^{1+\varepsilon}}\big\}.

Thus the asymptotics of Theorem
5.5
yield

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(y^{-1}g)}{\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,\sim\,\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\left(\frac{\left(t^{2}+|g^{+}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma}}{\left(t^{2}+|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma}}-1\right),

On the one hand, the quotient of the ground spherical functions is bounded by the local Harnack inequality (
5.14
). On the other hand, using (
3.17
) for
r=|g^{+}|
,
s=|(y^{-1}g)^{+}|
and
k=\frac{\ell}{2}+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+\sigma=\frac{\nu}{2}+\sigma>1
(in the notation of (
3.17
), we now have
r_{0}\lesssim t^{1+\varepsilon}
) and the trivial inequality
t^{2}+s^{2}\geq t^{2}
, we get altogether

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\mathrm{O}\Big(t^{-1+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma-1)}\Big)\qquad\forall\,g\in{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K},\,\,\forall\,|y|<\xi.

∎

Now, let us prove the first part of
Theorem

1.4
. The arguments follow those of
[
APZ23
]
once Lemma
5.7
is at hand, but we include them for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of (
1.12
) in
Theorem

1.4
.

By using

\displaystyle(\widetilde{v}_{0}*\widetilde{\varphi}_{0})(g)\,=\,\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{\ell}{y}\,v_{0}(yK)\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\widetilde{\delta}(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\widetilde{\delta}(y^{-1}g)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)=\,\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{0}*\varphi_{0})(gK),

and the fact that
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}=\widetilde{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}
, let us write the solution
\widetilde{v}
to (
5.4
) as

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}(t,g)\,=\,(\widetilde{v}_{0}*\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma})(g)\,=\,\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(v_{0}*Q_{t}^{\sigma,0})(g).

We aim to study the difference

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}(t,g)-\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,

\displaystyle=\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\frac{(v_{0}*Q_{t}^{\sigma,0})(g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}\,-\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\frac{(v_{0}*\varphi_{0})(g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}

(5.15)

\displaystyle=\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,v_{0}(yK)\,\Big\{{\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}}\Big\}.

According to the previous lemma, we have

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}\,=\,\mathrm{O}\Big(t^{-1+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma-1)}\Big)\qquad\forall\,g\in{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K},\,\,\forall\,y\in\supp{v_{0},}

and therefore the integral of

\widetilde{v}(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{M}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}

over the critical region

\displaystyle\int_{S\cap{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{v}(t,g)-\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)|\,\lesssim\,t^{-1+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma-1)}\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)}_{1}\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\Big|}\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,|v_{0}(yK)|}_{\const}

tends asymptotically to
0
. Finally, we claim that the integral

\displaystyle\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{v}(t,g)-\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)|\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{v}(t,g)|

\displaystyle+\,\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{M}(g)|\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)

tends also to
0
. On the one hand, we know that
\widetilde{M}
is bounded
and that the kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
asymptotically concentrates in

K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K
, hence

\displaystyle\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{M}(g)|\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. On the other hand, notice that for all

y\in\supp{v_{0}}
and for all
g\in{G}
such that

g^{+}\notin\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}
, using the triangle inequality one can show that

(5.16)

\displaystyle(y^{-1}g)^{+}\,\notin\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime\prime}=\,\big\{{H\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}\,|\,2\,t^{1-\varepsilon}\leq|H|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\,t^{1+\varepsilon}\big\}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,|\widetilde{v}(t,g)|\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,|v_{0}(yK)|\,\int_{G\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,\widetilde{\delta}(g)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\,\underbrace{\vphantom{\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime})K}}}\int_{S}\textrm{d}_{r}{y}\,|\widetilde{v}_{0}(y)|}_{\|\widetilde{v}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(S)}}\,\underbrace{\int_{S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime\prime})K}}\textrm{d}_{r}{g}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)}_{\longrightarrow\,0}.\

This concludes the proof of the extension problem asymptotics in
L^{1}
for the
distinguished Laplacian
\widetilde{\Delta}
on
S
and for initial data

\widetilde{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(S)
.
∎

5.3.
Heat asymptotics in
L^{\infty}

for compactly supported initial data

We first recall the following lemma, which allows us to compare the middle components occurring in the Iwasawa decomposition and in the Cartan decomposition.

Lemma 5.8
.

[
APZ23
, Lemma 4.8]

For all
g\in{G}
, we have

(5.17)

\displaystyle\langle{\rho,A(g)}\rangle\,\leq\,\langle{\rho,g^{+}}\rangle

where
A(g)
denotes the
\mathfrak{a}
-component of
g
in the Iwasawa
decomposition and
g^{+}
denotes its
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
-component
in the Cartan decomposition.

In the following two propositions we collect some elementary properties of the extension problem kernel. The first one clarifies the lower
and the upper bounds of
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
, while the second one describes
its critical region for the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Proposition 5.9
.

The kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
associated with the extension problem for the distinguished Laplacian
satisfies

(5.18)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\,\asymp\,t^{-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

for
t
large enough.

Proof.

Using the global estimates (
2.5
) and
(
5.2
), we have

(5.19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\asymp\,t^{2\sigma}\,e^{-\langle{\rho,A(g)}\rangle}e^{-\langle{\rho,g^{+}}\rangle}\,(t+|g|)^{-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\Big\{{\prod\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}1+\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle}\Big\}

We obtain first the lower bound in (
5.18
)
by evaluating the right hand side of (
5.19
) at

g_{0}=\exp(-t\rho)
and by observing that

\displaystyle A(g_{0})\,=\,-t\rho\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad g_{0}^{+}\,=\,t\rho.

For the upper bound, notice that

(5.20)

\displaystyle e^{-\langle{\rho,A(g)}\rangle}e^{-\langle{\rho,g^{+}}\rangle}\,\leq\,1

according to (
5.17
), and that

\displaystyle(t+|g|)^{-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\Big\{{\prod\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Sigma_{r}^{+}}1+\langle\alpha,g^{+}\rangle}\Big\}\lesssim 1,\qquad t^{2\sigma}\,(t+|g|)^{-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|-2\sigma}\lesssim t^{-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

for
t
large enough, whence the claim follows from (
5.19
).
∎

Proposition 5.10
.

The fractional Poisson kernel
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
concentrates asymptotically
in the same critical region for the
L^{\infty}
norm as for the
L^{1}
norm.
In other words,

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,\|\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(S\smallsetminus{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K})}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\,\,\,t\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

Let us study the sup norm of
\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}
outside the critical region.
From (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) we deduce that

(5.21)

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\lesssim\,t^{2\sigma+\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}(t+|g^{+}|)^{-2\sigma-\ell-2|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}(1+|g^{+}|^{|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}).

Case 1
: Assume that
|g^{+}|<t^{1-\varepsilon}
. Then
t+|g^{+}|\asymp t
and
(1+|g^{+}|)^{|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\lesssim t^{(1-\varepsilon)|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}
.
Thus we deduce from (
5.21
) that

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\lesssim\,t^{-\varepsilon|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}

which tends to
0
.

Case 2
: Assume that
|g^{+}|>t^{1+\varepsilon}
.
Then
t+|g^{+}|\asymp|g^{+}|
, therefore

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\lesssim\,t^{2\sigma+\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}|g^{+}|^{-2\sigma-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\lesssim t^{-\varepsilon(\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|+2\sigma)}

which tends to
0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Finally, let us prove the remaining part of
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of (
1.13
) in
Theorem

1.4
.

Fix
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{\nu+2\sigma-1}
. Consider the function

t\mapsto\epsilon(t),\quad\epsilon(t)=t^{-1+\varepsilon(\nu+2\sigma-1)}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow+\infty.

In the critical region
S\cap{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}
, we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}(t,g)-\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)|\,\leq\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)\,\int_{|y|<\,\xi}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,|v_{0}(yK)|\,\Big|{\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}}\Big|

with

\displaystyle\Big|{\frac{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(y^{-1}g)}{Q_{t}^{\sigma,0}(g)}-\frac{\varphi_{0}(y^{-1}g)}{\varphi_{0}(g)}}\Big|\,\lesssim\,\epsilon(t)

according to (
5.2
)
and to
Lemma

5.7
.
Then we deduce from (
5.18
) that

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\,|\widetilde{v}(t,g)-\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)|\,\lesssim\,\epsilon(t)\qquad\forall\,g\in{S\cap{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}}

where the right-hand side tends to
0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
Outside the critical region, we estimate separately
\widetilde{v}(t,g)

and
\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)
. On the one hand, we know that

\widetilde{M}(g)
is a bounded function and that

\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)=\mathrm{o}(t^{-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|})
. Then

t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\widetilde{M}(g)\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g)

tends to
0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
On the other hand, since
g\notin{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t})K}
and

|y|<\xi
imply that
g^{-1}y\notin{K(\exp\widetilde{\Omega}_{t}^{\prime\prime})K}

(see (
5.16
)), we obtain

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}(t,g)|\,\lesssim\,\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{y}\,|\widetilde{v}_{0}(yK)|\,|\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}(g^{-1}y)|\,

which is
\textrm{o}(t^{-\ell-|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|})
outside the critical region.
In conclusion,

\displaystyle t^{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}\|\widetilde{v}(t,\,\cdot\,)-\widetilde{M}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}\,\longrightarrow\,0

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
∎

The result for the
L^{p}
norm follows by convexity.

Corollary 5.11
.

The solution
\widetilde{v}
to the Cauchy problem (
5.3
) with initial
data
\widetilde{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(S)
satisfies

(5.22)

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\ell+|\Sigma_{r}^{+}|}{p^{\prime}}}\|\widetilde{v}(t,\,\cdot\,)-\widetilde{M}\,\widetilde{Q}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(S)}\,\longrightarrow\,0\qquad\textnormal{as}\quad\,t\rightarrow+\infty,

for all
1<p<\infty
.

5.4.
Asymptotics for other initial data

We have obtained above the long-time asymptotic convergence in
L^{p}

(
1\leq{p}\leq\infty
) for the extension problem
with compactly supported initial data.
The following corollaries give some other functional spaces for which the convergence is true, but the question regarding the full
L^{1}(S)
class remains open.

Corollary 5.12
.

The asymptotic convergences (
1.12
) and (
1.13
),
hence (
5.22
), still hold with initial data

\widetilde{v}_{0}=\widetilde{\delta}^{\tfrac{1}{2}}v_{0}\in{L}^{1}(S)
when

v_{0}
is bi-
K
-invariant.

Corollary 5.13
.

The asymptotic convergences (
1.12
) and (
1.13
),
hence (
5.22
),
still hold with no bi-
K
-invariance condition but under the assumption

(5.23)

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{g}\,|v_{0}(gK)|e^{\langle{\rho,g^{+}}\rangle}\,<\,\infty.

The proofs of the above corollaries are similar to those of
[
APZ23
, Corollary 4.12]
and
[
APZ23
, Corollary 4.13]
, respectively, thus omitted.

Mass and Expansion of Asymptotically Conical Kähler Metrics
Qi Yao

Abstract
. We prove an expansion theorem on scalar-flat asymptotically conical (AC) Kähler metrics. The ADM mass is known to be defined in ALE Riemannian manifolds. The concept of ADM mass can be generalized to AC cases. Consider an AC Kähler manifold with asymptotic to a Ricci-flat Kähler metric cone with complex dimension n n . Assuming the weak decay conditions required for the mass to be well-defined, then each scalar-flat AC Kähler metric admits an expansion that the main term is given by the standard Kähler metric of the metric cone and the leading error term is of O ⁡ ( r 2 − 2 ​ n ) O(r^{2-2n}) with coefficient only depending on the ADM mass and its dimension. Besides, the mass formula by Hein-LeBrun [ 12 ] also can be proved in our setting. As an interesting application, a new version of the positive mass theorem will also be discussed in the cases of the resolutions of the Ricci-flat Kähler cones.



1.
Introduction and Statement of Main Theorems

Schwarzschild metric is known to be a solution of Einstein’s vacuum equations. In general space-time
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

(n\geq 3)
, the Schwarzschild metric has the following explicit expression.

\displaystyle g=-(1-\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}})dt^{2}+(1-\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}})dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S^{n-1}},

where the constant
m
is the mass of the the Schwarzschild metric. One can easily observe that, by restricting to each space slice
t=c
, the Schwarzschild metric differs from the Euclidean metric by quantity of order
O(r^{2-n})
.

Motivated by Schwarschild metrics, Arnowitt, Deser and Misner
[
1
]
first generalized the definition of the mass (ADM mass) to asymptotically flat metrics. Here, we apply the definition of mass to asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) manifolds. Let
(X,g)
be an complete non-compact Riemannian manifold, we say
(X,g)
is ALE if there is a compact subset
K\subseteq X
such that
X-K
has finitely many components, denoted by
X_{i,\infty}
with
X-K=\bigcup_{i}X_{i,\infty}
and each
X_{i,\infty}
is diffeomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}^{n}-B_{R})/\Gamma_{i}
, where
B_{R}
is a closed ball of radius
R
and
\Gamma
is a finite subset of
O(n)
. The metric
g
is asymptotic to the Euclidean metric with rapid decay rate. Then, the ADM mass of the end
X_{i,\infty}
is given by

\displaystyle m_{i}(g)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{4(n-1)\pi^{n/2}}\int_{S_{r}/\Gamma_{i}}(g_{ij,i}-g_{ii,j})n^{j}d\mu

(1.1)

where
S_{r}
is the Euclidean sphere of radius
r
,
n
is the outward Euclidean unit normal vector and
d\mu
is the volume form induced by standard metric on
S_{r}
. Bartnik
[
2
]
and Chruściel
[
9
]
give the appropriate decay condition to make the ADM mass coordinate-invariant, for Sobolev case and Hölder case respectively. Here, we list the decay condition of Hölder case for the metric
g
on each end
X_{i,\infty}
,

(i)

the scalar curvature
R
of
g
belongs to
L^{1}
.

(ii)

the metric
g
is asymptotic to the Euclidean metric
\delta_{ij}
at the end with decay rate
-\tau
for some
\tau>(n-2)/2
,

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(r^{-\tau}),\qquad|\nabla(\psi^{-1})^{*}g)|_{g_{0}}=O(r^{-\tau-1}).

(1.2)

In this paper, one of main results is that the scalar-flat ALE Kähler metric of complex dimension
n
is of decay rate
2-2n
, which has an expansion,
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(r^{2-2n})
and the leading error term is determined by the ADM mass of
g
(see theorem
D
). Furthermore, the expansion also works in scalar-flat AC Kähler cases if we replace the Euclidean metric by the standard metric on Kähler cone.

Rather than directly introduce the expansion theorem, we first introduce an important tool in Kähler geometry, the ddbar lemma. The ddbar lemma is a standard result in compact Kähler manifolds and it can be easily proved in ALE Kähler manifolds if we assume a fast decay condition (see
[
14
, theorem 8.4.4]
). In Colon-Hein
[
10
, theorem 3.11]
, the ddbar lemma is generalized to asymptotically conical (AC) Kähler manifolds with a lower decay condition
(\text{only need }-\tau<0)
, with additional assumption of non-negative Ricci curvature. In the author’s previous paper
[
24
, Proposition 3.6]
, the ddbar lemma can be proved in negative line bundle over Kähler C-spaces (compact simply-connected homogeneous Kähler manifolds) without any decay assumption at infinity. Here, we give a new version of ddbar lemma by revising the Colon-Hein’s result and dropping the non-negative Ricci curvature assumption.

The ddbar lemma on the complement of
K\subseteq X
will also be discussed in this paper. In Goto
[
11
, section5]
and Conlon-Hein
[
10
, appendix A]
, ddbar lemma is proved in the case of complex dimension
n\geq 3
with a trivial canonical bundle. In this paper, we derive a ddbar lemma on
X\backslash K
without making the above assumptions, but with high decay rate error terms.

Let
(L,g_{L})
be a compact Riemannian manifold, the
Riemannian cone

C_{L}
associated with
L
is defined to be
L\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with Riemannian metric
g_{0}=dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{L}
. A Riemannian cone

is said to be

g_{0}
-parallel complex structure

such that the corresponding fundamental form
\omega_{0}=g_{0}(J_{0}\cdot,\cdot)

\omega_{0}=i\partial\overline{\partial}r^{2}
. If we assume that the Kähler cone
(C_{L},g_{0},J_{0})
is Ricci-flat with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}C_{L}=n
,
then, according to the standard calculation in
[
22
, section 1.4]
and
[
3
, section 11.1]
, the link

is Sasaki-Einstein with
\textup{Ric}g_{L}=2(n-1)g_{L}
.

Let
(X,J,g)
be an AC Kähler manifold asymptotic to
C_{L}
, where
(C_{L},g_{0})
is a Ricci-flat Kähler cone with link
L
. Throughout the paper we always assume that the manifolds only have one end, then there exists a compact subset
K\subseteq X
and
B_{R}=\{x\in C_{L},r(x)<R\}
, such that
\psi:X-K\rightarrow C_{L}-B_{R}
is a diffeomorphism satisfying the following decay conditions,

(i)

the scalar curvature

of

belongs to

.

(ii)

the complex structure
J
on
X
decays to
J_{0}
, the induced almost complex structure from

.

(iii)

the metric
g
is asymptotic to the reference metric

at the end with decay rate
-\tau
for some
\tau>0
, for

,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{i}((\psi^{-1})^{*}g-g_{0})|_{g_{0}}=O(r^{-\tau-i}).

(1.3)

Theorem A
.

(ddbar lemma)
Let

. Let

,

(i)

Let

-exact real

-form on

.

(ii)

Let
\omega
be a
d
-exact real
(1,1)
-form on the end
X\backslash K
. Then there exists a real function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k+2,\alpha}_{2-\delta}(X\backslash K)

\displaystyle\omega=dd^{c}\varphi+O(r^{-2n}).

We generalize the definition of mass to AC Riemannian manifolds. Let
(C_{L},g_{0})
be a Riemannian metric cone of link
(L,g_{L})
and
(X,g)
, a complete Riemannian manifold asymptotic to
(C_{L},g)
at infinity. The definition in (
1.1
) requires a coordinate system at infinity, which does not exist in general AC Riemannian manifolds. Hence, we start from a coordinate-free expression of mass. In Lee’s book
[
17
, Section 3.1.3]
, the mass is defined to be

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=\frac{1}{2(2n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L(r)}(\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)_{i})n^{i}d\operatorname{Vol}_{L(r)},

(1.4)

where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection with respect to
g_{0}
and
n
is the outer normal vector field on
L(r)
. In Hein-LeBrun
[
12
]
, the ADM mass in ALE Kähler manifolds is a quantity determined by the total scalar curvature and its topological data. A similar mass formula also holds in AC Kähler manifolds asymptotic to Ricci-flat Kähler cones. Let
\iota:H^{2}_{c}(X)\rightarrow H^{2}_{dR}(X)
be the map induced by the natural embedding of chain complex
\Omega^{\bullet}_{c}\hookrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}_{dR}
. Generally speaking,
\iota
is not an isomorphism, but the first Chern class of
(X,J)
always has a pre-image under
\iota
(see Lemma
4.2
). Then, we have the following mass formula.

Theorem B
.

Let
(X,J,g)
be an AC Kähler manifolds asymptotic to a Ricci-flat Kähler cone
(C_{L},J_{0},g_{0})
satisfying the decay condition (
1.3
) with
\tau=n-1+\epsilon
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=-\frac{2\pi\langle\iota^{-1}c_{1},[\omega]^{n-1}\rangle}{(2n-1)(n-1)!\operatorname{Vol}(L)}+\frac{1}{2(2n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\int_{X}R_{g}d\operatorname{Vol}_{g},

(1.5)

where
[\omega]
is the Kähler class of
(g,J)
,
c_{1}
is the first Chern class of
(X,J)
and
R_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
g
on
X
.

An interesting application of the mass formula (
1.5
) is the positive mass theorem. The positive mass theorem is first proved in AE manifolds of lower dimension by Schoen, Yau
[
21
]
. Afterwards, Witten
[
23
]
develop a new method to prove the positive mass theorem on spin manifolds. And in Kähler cases, Hein-Lebrun
[
12
]
confirms the positive mass theorem on AE Kähler manifolds. However, in ALE cases, Lebrun
[
15
]
constructs the first counter-example of positive mass theorem. Here, we discuss a new version of positive mass theorem on resolution spaces of Ricci-flat Kähler cones with an isolated canonical singularity at vertex.

Theorem C
.

(positive mass theorem)
Let
(X,J)
be a resolution space of a Ricci-flat Kähler cone
C_{L}
such that the only singularity
O\in C_{L}
is canonical. If
(X,g)
has scalar curvature
R\geq 0
, then the mass
\mathfrak{m}(X,g)\geq 0
, and equals zero only if
(X,J,g)
is a crepant resolution of
C_{L}
with a scalar-flat Kähler metric
g
.

Now, we can introduce the following expansion theorem in AC Kähler case.

Theorem D
.

Let
(X,J)
be a AC Kähler manifold asymptotic to a Ricci-flat Kähler cone
(C_{L},J_{0})
and we assume that
k
be a large positive integer and
\tau=n-1+\epsilon
,

(i)

Let
\omega_{1}
,
\omega_{2}
be Kähler forms on
X
with the corresponding metrics satisfying decay condition (
1.3
) and
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
, the scalar curvature of
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
. If

\bullet

[\omega_{1}]=[\omega_{2}]

\bullet

R_{1}=R_{2}
,

then
\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}+dd^{c}\varphi
with the potential
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{k+2,\alpha}_{2-2\tilde{\tau}}
, for some
\tilde{\tau}>n-1
depending on
(n,L,\tau)
.

(ii)

Let
\omega
be a scalar flat Kähler form on X satifying decay condition (
1.3
). And the complex structure
J
is asymptotic to
J_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle J=J_{0}+O(r^{2-2n-\epsilon^{\prime}}),\qquad\epsilon^{\prime}>0,

(1.6)

Then, the scalar flat Kähler form
\omega
admits the following expansion at infinity. In particular, outside a compact set of
X
, for complex dimension
n\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}r^{2}+\frac{(2n-1)\mathfrak{m}(\omega)}{2(4-2n)(n-1)}dd^{c}r^{4-2n}+O(r^{-2\tilde{\tau}}),

where
m(\omega)
is the ADM mass of
\omega
and
\tilde{\tau}>n-1
only depends on
(n,L,\epsilon^{\prime},\tau)
. And for complex dimension
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}r^{2}+\frac{3\mathfrak{m}(\omega)}{2}dd^{c}\log r+O(r^{-2\tilde{\tau}}),

where
\tilde{\tau}=\min\{\tau,3/2\}
.

As special cases of AC Kähler metrics, all ALE Kähler metrics satisfy the expansion theorem. According to the statement (i) of Theorem
D
in ALE Kähler cases, we can define Kähler potential spaces with relatively ”good” decay rate. Precisely, by fixing a Kähler metric
\omega
, then, we can define the following potential space of ALE Kähler metrics,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{-\tau}([\omega])=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{-2\tilde{\tau}}:\omega+i\partial\overline{\partial}f>0,\ \tilde{\tau}=\min\{\tau,n-\frac{1}{2}\}\Big\}

In fact,
\mathcal{H}_{-\tau}([\omega])
does not contain all ALE Kähler metrics, but it is enough for ”prescribed scalar curvature” problem, as all ALE Kähler metrics with the same scalar curvature as
\omega
are contained in
\mathcal{H}_{-\tau}([\omega])
. For the statement (ii), in ALE Kähler case, the fall-off condition of complex structure (
1.6
) is automatically satisfied. In fact, for
n\geq 3
, in the asymptotic chart, the complex structure
J
coincides with the standard one
J_{0}
, and for
n=2
, in the asymptotic chart,

J=J_{0}+O(r^{-3})
. One can check
[
12
, Lemma 2.3, Proposition 4.5]
for details.

2.1.
The Laplacian of 1-form on Riemanian cones

This subsection is mainly dedicated to preparing for the proof of Theorem

1

Let

be a closed Riemannian manifold with dimension

(

and

\displaystyle||T||_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\rho}(U)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sup_{x\in U}\big|(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}(-\rho+i)}\nabla^{i}T\big|_{g}+\sup_{x,y\in U}(r^{2}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}(-\rho+k+\alpha)}\frac{|\nabla^{k}T(x)-\nabla^{k}T(y)|_{g}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}

(2.1)

where

\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\rho}(U)
if the Hölder norm
||T||_{\mathcal{C}^{k,\alpha}_{\rho}(U)}
is finite. Similarly, we can define a weighted Hölder norm on each AC Riemannian manifold as in (

) by fixing a smooth radial function
\tilde{r}
, which is obtained by smoothly extending the radial function

to the whole manifold.

Let
\Delta=dd^{*}+d^{*}d
be the Hodge-Laplacian operator on

and
\Delta_{L}=d_{L}d^{*}_{L}+d_{L}^{*}d_{L}

Let

(listed with multiplicity) be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of
\Delta_{L}
on functions and
\kappa_{0}
(constant),
\kappa_{1},\ldots

||\kappa_{i}||_{L^{2}(L)}=1
, for all

. Viewing
\Delta_{L}
as an operator acting on the spaces of 1-forms on

\Delta_{L}d_{L}\kappa_{i}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i}d_{L}\kappa_{i}
,

. The

-normalization of
\kappa_{i}
implies that
||d_{L}\kappa_{i}||_{L^{2}(L)}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{1/2}
. Besides, according to Hodge decomposition on

, we also have a family of coclosed eigen-1-forms,
\Delta_{L}\eta_{j}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{j}\eta_{j}

(d^{*}_{L}\eta_{j}=0)

and
||\eta_{j}||_{L^{2}(L)}=1
. In summary, we have

(i)

Exact eigenforms:

with eigenvalue
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
and

, for

.

(ii)

Coclosed eigenforms:
\eta_{j}
with eigenvalue

and

.

Consider an 1-form
\beta

\beta=\kappa(r,y)dr+\eta(r,y)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa(r,y)&=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}(r)\kappa_{i}(y)\\

(2.2)

Based on calculation in
[

&-\eta^{\prime\prime}-\frac{n-3}{r}\eta^{\prime}-\frac{2}{r}d_{L}\kappa+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{L}\eta.\end{split}

(2.3)

4.2.
The Mass Formula

To give a reasonable definition of mass on AC Kähler manifolds, we will invoke an expression of mass without applying Euclidean coordinates. As mentioned in
[
17
, Section 3.1.3]
, the ADM mass can be interpreted as the integral of linearization of scalar curvature at the Euclidean background metric. This idea can be generalized to the AC Kähler cases if we replace the background Euclidean metric with the Riemannian cone metric. If we perturb the cone metric
g_{0}
and consider
g(t)=(1-t)g_{0}+tg_{1}
where
g_{1}
is another Riemannian metric defined on
C_{L}
with the corresponding scalar curvature
R(t)\in L^{1}(C_{L})
and bounded near
0
, according to Appendix A, lemma
A.2
for any real local frame on
X_{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}R(t)=\overline{\nabla}^{i}\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-\overline{\Delta}\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g

Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{C_{L}}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}R(t)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L(r)}(\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)_{i})n^{i}d\operatorname{Vol}_{L(r)},

where
n
is a outer normal vector field of
L(r)
and the integrand of the right-hand side is independent of the choice of real frame.
Let
(X,g)
be an AC Riemannian manifold asymptotic to metric cone
(C_{L},g_{0})
, we give the abstract definition of mass on
(X,g)
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=\frac{1}{2(2n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L(r)}(\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)_{i})n^{i}d\operatorname{Vol}_{L(r)}

(4.18)

Let
(X,g,J)
be an AC Kähler manifold asymptotic to a Ricci-flat Kähler cone
(C_{L},g_{0},J_{0})
of complex dimension
n
with the metric
g
asymptotic to
g_{0}
in the end
X_{\infty}
, the remaining part of this section is aimed to derive a mass formula on
(X,g,J)
which generalize the Hein-LeBrun’s mass formula
[
12
]
. Observe that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\log\frac{\det g}{\det g_{0}}\Big)_{i}+O(r^{-2\tau-1})

(4.19)

and according to the calculation in Appendix (
A.3
)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)_{i}=\frac{1}{2}g_{0}^{jk}(\overline{\nabla}_{k}g_{ij}+\overline{\nabla}_{j}g_{ik}-\overline{\nabla}_{i}g_{jk})=g_{0}^{jk}A_{jk,i},

(4.20)

where
A_{jk,i}=g_{il}A^{l}_{jk}
. Also observe that the formulas (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) can relate with the terms of
\theta_{\omega}
in (
4.15
). Then, we have the following mass formula,

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X,g,J)
be an AC Kähler manifold asymptotic to a locally Calabi-Yau cone
(C_{L},g_{0},J_{0})
of complex dimension
n
with

for

, where
\tau=1-n-\epsilon
. Let
\omega
represent the Kähler form of

on

be the connection 1-form as in (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=\frac{1}{(2n-1)(n-1)!\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{L(r)}\theta_{\omega}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

(4.21)

Proof.

According to the observation from (
4.19
) and (
4.20
), the mass formula can be rewritten on asymptotic local coframe
\{\mu_{i},\bar{\mu}_{i}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=\frac{1}{2(2n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\bigg\{\int_{L(r)}*2\operatorname{Re}\big(A_{,\bar{i}}\bar{\mu}^{i}\big)-\int_{L(r)}*\bigg(d\log\frac{\sqrt{detg}}{\sqrt{\det{g_{0}}}}\bigg)+O(r^{-2\epsilon})\bigg\}

where

is the Hodge star operator of on
(X,g)
and
A_{,i}=2g_{0}^{j\bar{k}}A_{j\bar{k},i}
. Also notice that

\displaystyle*\bigg(d\log\frac{\sqrt{\det g}}{\sqrt{\det{g_{0}}}}\bigg)=-\Big(Jd\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{|\Omega_{0}\wedge\Omega_{0}|}\Big)\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=d^{C}\log\frac{\omega^{n}}{|\Omega_{0}\wedge\Omega_{0}|}\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\alpha\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=(J\operatorname{Re}\alpha)\wedge\frac{\omega^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=*(\operatorname{Re}\alpha)

Comparing with the explicit expression of

in (
4.15

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(g)=\frac{1}{(2n-1)\operatorname{Vol}(L)}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\bigg\{\int_{L(r)}*\operatorname{Re}\big(A_{,\bar{i}}\mu^{\bar{i}}-2\alpha\big)+\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{L(r)}\theta_{\omega}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\bigg\}

It suffices to prove the difference

\displaystyle g=g_{ij}\mu^{i}\otimes\mu^{j}+g_{i\bar{j}}\mu^{i}\otimes\mu^{\bar{j}}+\overline{g_{i\bar{j}}}\mu^{\bar{i}}\otimes\mu^{j}+\overline{g_{ij}}\mu^{\bar{i}}\otimes\mu^{\bar{j}}.

\displaystyle\omega=ig_{i\bar{j}}\mu^{i}\wedge\mu^{\bar{j}}+i(g_{i\bar{k}}\mathcal{J}^{\bar{k}}_{j}-g_{j\bar{k}}\mathcal{J}^{\bar{k}}_{i})\mu^{i}\wedge\mu^{j}-i(g_{k\bar{i}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{j}}-g_{k\bar{j}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{i}})\mu^{\bar{i}}\wedge\mu^{\bar{j}}+O(r^{-2\tau}).

The (0,2)-part of

\omega^{0,2}=-i(g_{k\bar{i}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{j}}-g_{k\bar{j}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{i}})\mu^{\bar{i}}\wedge\mu^{\bar{j}}

\begin{split}d^{*}\omega^{0,2}&=i\Lambda(J\circ d(g_{k\bar{i}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{j}}-g_{k\bar{j}}\mathcal{J}^{k}_{\bar{i}})\mu^{\bar{i}}\wedge\mu^{\bar{j}})+O(r^{-2\tau-1})\\

(4.23)

Inserting (

\displaystyle\int_{L(r)}*\operatorname{Re}\big(A_{,\bar{i}}\mu^{\bar{i}}-2\alpha\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{L(r)}d*(\omega^{0,2}+\overline{\omega^{0,2}})+O(r^{-2\epsilon})=O(r^{-2\epsilon}),

hence, the proof is completed.
∎

. Notice that

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{i}g_{jk}

\displaystyle=\xi_{i}(g_{jk})-g(\overline{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}}\xi_{j},\xi_{k})-g(\xi_{j},\overline{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}}\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=g\big((\nabla_{\xi_{i}}-\overline{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}})\xi_{j},\xi_{k}\big)+g\big(\xi_{j},(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}-\overline{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}})\xi_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=g_{lk}A^{l}_{ij}+g_{jl}A^{l}_{ik}.

By permutation of the indice
i,j,k
, we have

\displaystyle A^{k}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}(\overline{\nabla}_{i}g_{jl}+\overline{\nabla}_{j}g_{il}-\overline{\nabla}_{l}g_{ij})

(A.3)

Let
A(t)=\nabla_{t}-\overline{\nabla}
and
\nabla_{t}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
g(t)=\overline{g}+t(g-\overline{g})
, then

\displaystyle R(t)=g^{ij}(t)\big(\overline{R}_{ij}+\overline{\nabla}_{k}A^{k}_{ij}(t)-\overline{\nabla}_{i}A^{k}_{kj}(t)-A^{k}_{lj}(t)\omega^{l}([\xi_{i},\xi_{k}])+A^{l}_{ij}(t)A^{k}_{lk}(t)-A^{l}_{kj}(t)A^{k}_{li}(t)\big)

Notice that
A(0)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}R(t)=&\overline{R}_{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}g^{ij}(t)\\
&+\overline{g}^{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\big(\overline{\nabla}_{k}A^{k}_{ij}(t)-\overline{\nabla}_{i}A^{k}_{kj}(t)\big)-\omega^{l}([\xi_{i},\xi_{k}])\overline{g}^{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}A^{k}_{lj}(t).\end{split}

(A.4)

Then, we compute each term in (
A.4
) separately.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}A^{k}_{lj}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{km}(\overline{\nabla}_{l}\dot{g}_{jm}+\overline{\nabla}_{j}\dot{g}_{ml}-\overline{\nabla}_{m}\dot{g}_{jl})(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{km}(\overline{\nabla}_{l}{g}_{jm}+\overline{\nabla}_{j}{g}_{ml}-\overline{\nabla}_{m}{g}_{jl})

Inserting into the third term of (
A.4
),

\displaystyle\omega^{l}([\xi_{i},\xi_{k}])\overline{g}^{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}A^{k}_{lj}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{g}^{km}(\overline{\nabla}_{[i,k]}{g}_{jm}+\overline{\nabla}_{j}{g}_{m[i,k]}-\overline{\nabla}_{m}{g}_{j[i,k]})=0

The above identity is vanishing because of the symmetry of indices; in particular, by permuting
(ij)
and
(km)
, we can check that each term in above identity is vanishing. Notice that

\displaystyle\overline{g}^{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\overline{\nabla}_{i}A^{k}_{kj}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\big(\overline{g}^{kl}(\overline{\nabla}_{j}g_{kl}+\overline{\nabla}_{k}g_{lj}-\overline{\nabla}_{l}g_{jk})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{j}\big(\overline{g}^{kl}g_{kl}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\operatorname{tr}_{\overline{g}}g

and

\displaystyle\overline{g}^{ij}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\overline{\nabla}_{k}A^{k}_{ij}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\big(\overline{g}^{kl}(\overline{\nabla}_{j}g_{il}+\overline{\nabla}_{i}g_{lj}-\overline{\nabla}_{l}g_{ji})\big)

\displaystyle=\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\big(\overline{g}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{j}g_{il}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\big(\overline{g}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{l}g_{ij}\big)

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}^{j}\overline{\nabla}^{i}g_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{\Delta}\operatorname{tr}_{\overline{g}}g,

combining with
d/dt|_{t=0}g^{ij}(t)=\overline{g}^{ik}\overline{g}^{jl}(g-\overline{g})_{kl}
and
A.4
, then we have,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}R(t)=\langle\textup{Ric}_{\overline{g}},g-\overline{g}\rangle_{\overline{g}}+\overline{\nabla}^{j}\overline{\nabla}^{i}g_{ij}-\overline{\Delta}\operatorname{tr}_{\overline{g}}g,

which completes the proof.
∎

Based on lemma
A.2
, we can quickly derive the ADM mass on AE manifolds from the integral of linearization of scalar curvature. Let
g
be a Riemannian metric in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
DR(g)
be the linearization of scalar curvature with respect to the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the integral of
DR(g)
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}DR(g)d\operatorname{Vol}_{euc}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}(r)}\big(\overline{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-(\operatorname{tr}_{g_{0}}g)\big)n^{i}d\operatorname{Vol}_{S^{n-1}(r)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}(r)}(g_{ij,j}-g_{jj,i})n^{i}d\operatorname{Vol}_{S^{n-1}(r)}.

(A.5)

Notice that the formula (
A.5
) only depends on the information of metrics at infinity. Hence, up to a constant factor, (
A.5
) can be defined to be the mass on AE manifolds.

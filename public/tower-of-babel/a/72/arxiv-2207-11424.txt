The existence of positive ground state solutions for the Choquard type equation on groups of polynomial growth
Ruowei Li

Abstract
. In this paper, let G G be a Cayley graph of a discrete group of polynomial growth with homogeneous dimension N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . We study the Choquard type equation on G G : (0.1) Δ ​ u + ( R α ∗ ∣ u ∣ p ) ​ ∣ u ∣ p − 2 ​ u = 0 , \Delta u+(R_{\alpha}\ast\mid u\mid^{p})\mid u\mid^{p-2}u=0, where α ∈ ( 0 , N ) \alpha\in(0,N) , p > N + α N − 2 p>\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2} and R α R_{\alpha} stands for the Green’s function of the discrete fractional Laplace operator, which has same asymptotics as the Riesz potential. We prove the discrete Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on such Cayley graphs, and by the discrete Concentration-Compactness principle we prove the existence of extremal functions for the corresponding Sobolev type inequalities in supercritical cases, which yields a positive ground state solution of ( 0.1 ). Moreover, we obtain positive ground state solutions of Choquard type equations with p p -Laplace, biharmonic and p p -biharmonic operators etc.

Introduction

The nonlinear Choquard equation

(1.1)

\Delta u+u=(I_{\alpha}\ast\mid u\mid^{p})\mid u\mid^{p-2}u,\;\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

where
I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})\pi^{N/2}2^{\alpha}\mid x\mid^{N-\alpha}}

is the Riesz potential, has been extensively studied in the literature.
In the physical case
N=3,p=2
and
\alpha=2,
in 1954 the problem
appeared in a work by S. I. Pekar describing the quantum mechanics
of a polaron at rest
[
58
]
. In 1976 P. Choquard used (
1.1
)
to describe an electron trapped in its own hole, in a certain approximation
to Hartree–Fock theory of one component plasma
[
38
]
. In 1996
R. Penrose proposed (
1.1
) as a
model of self-gravitating matter, in a programme in which quantum
state reduction is understood as a gravitational phenomenon
[
51
]
.
The equations of type (
1.1
) are
usually called the nonlinear Schrödinger–Newton equation. If
u

solves (
1.1
) then the function

\psi
defined by
\psi(t,x)=e^{it}u(x)
is a solitary wave of the
focusing time-dependent Hartree equation

i\psi_{t}=-\Delta\psi-(I_{\alpha}\ast\mid\psi\mid^{p})\mid\psi\mid^{p-2}\psi,\;\text{in $\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

In this context (
1.1
) is also known
as the stationary nonlinear Hartree equation.

The existence of solutions was proved for
N=3,p=2
and
\alpha=2,

with the symmetrization method, the Strauss lemma
[
62
]
, and
the ordinary differential equation techniques by E. H. Lieb, P. L.
Lions and G. Menzala
[
38
,
42
,
41
]
. Li Ma and Lin Zhao classified
all positive solutions by the method of moving plane in an integral
form
[
46
]
. For higher dimensions, P. L. Lions
[
42
,
43
,
44
,
45
]

established the Concentration-Compactness method, which provided a
new idea for proving the existence result. The general idea is as
follows. Since the problem (
1.1
)
has a variational structure, take a maximizing sequence
\{u_{n}\}

for the variational problem

sup
\left\{\underset{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\int}(I_{\alpha}\ast\mid u\mid^{p})\mid u\mid^{p}:\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}=1\right\}

and regard
\left\{\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+u_{n}^{2}\right)\mathrm{dx}\right\}

as a sequence of probability measures. He proved in
[
42
,
44
]

that there are three cases of the limit of the sequence: compactness,
vanishing and dichotomy. Vanishing and dichotomy are ruled out by
the rescaling trick and the subadditivity inequality. Therefore, the
nontrivial limit function, which is called the extremal function,
exists by the compactness, and after scaling it satisfies (
1.1
).
By the Concentration-Compactness method of P. L. Lions
[
42
, Lemma I.1]

(see also
[
65
, Lemma 1.21]
,
[
17
]
), V. Moroz and

[

proved the optimal parameter range
of the existence of a positive ground state solution, i.e.
\frac{N+\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2}
,
and the range is sharp. They established regularity, constant sign,
radially symmetric, monotone decaying, and decay asymptotics of the
ground state solutions. They also studied the Choquard equations with
a general class of nonhomogeneous nonlinearities
[
54
]
, and
with a potential term
V

[
55
]
. Multiple solutions results
were obtained in
[
14
]
. There are intensive studies of the
Choquard equations, see survey papers
[

,

Let

\beta_{S}(n):=\mid B_{e}^{S}(n)\mid

[

A group

for different finite generating sets

. By Gromov’s

for any group

where the integer

is a sort of dimensional constant

In this paper, we consider the Cayley graph
(G,S)
of a group of
polynomial growth with the homogeneous dimension

\mathbb{Z}^{N}
is a Cayley graph of a free abelian group. We denote

\lVert u\rVert_{D^{2,p}(G)}\coloneqq\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{\ell^{p}(G)},\;\Delta u(x)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{y\sim x}\nabla_{xy}u,

see Section 2 for details.

By a standard trick and the isoperimetric estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{\ell^{q}}\leq C_{p}\lVert u\rVert_{D^{1,p}},\;\forall u\in D_{0}^{1,p}(G),

where

The discrete Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev (HLS for abbreviation) inequality
with the Riesz potential

(1.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\\

where

0<\alpha<N
,
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}=2
.
Since the Riesz potential

is exactly the Green’s function
of the fractional Laplace on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

it is natural to consider the discrete fractional Laplace

By the method of subordination and Bochner’s functional

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\coloneqq\sideset{\frac{1}{\mid\varGamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})\mid}}{{}_{0}^{\infty}}{\int}\left(e^{t\Delta}u-u\right)t^{-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\text{d}t,

[

And the Green’s function

of the fractional Laplace on

which has the asymptotic relation

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in G\\

\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}=2

and an equivalent form of the HLS inequality is

Similarly, (

\displaystyle\sum_{G}\left(R_{\alpha}\ast\mid u\mid^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\mid u\mid^{p}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\|u\|_{\ell^{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha}\tilde{C}_{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\lVert u\rVert_{D^{1,2}}^{2p},\;\forall u\in D^{1,2}(G),

The optimal constant in the inequality (

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a countable group. It is called finitely generated if
it has a finite generating set
S
. We always assume that the generating
set
S
is symmetric, i.e.
S=S^{-1}
. The Cayley graph of
(G,S)

is a graph structure
(V,E)
with the set of vertices

and
the set of edges

where for any

(also denoted by
x\sim y
) if

. The Cayley
graph of
(G,S)
is endowed with a natural metric, called the word
metric
[
11
]
: For any
x,y\in G
, the distance between them
is defined as the length of the shortest path connecting
x
and

y
by assigning each edge of length one,

d^{S}(x,y)=\text{inf}{\left\{k:x=x_{0}\sim\cdot\cdot\cdot\sim x_{k}=y\right\}.}

One sees easily that for two generating sets
S
and
S_{1}
the
metrics
d^{S}
and
d^{S_{1}}
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent, i.e.
there exist two constants
C_{1}(S,S_{1}),C_{2}(S,S_{1})
such that
for any

C_{1}(S,S_{1})d^{S_{1}}(x,y)\leq d^{S}(x,y)\leq C_{2}(S,S_{1})d^{S_{1}}(x,y).

Let

denote the closed ball of radius

centered at
p\in G
. By the
group structure, it is obvious that
\mid B_{p}^{S}(n)\mid=\mid B_{q}^{S}(n)\mid
,
for any
p,q\in G
. The growth function of
(G,S)
is defined as

\beta_{S}(n):=\mid B_{e}^{S}(n)\mid
where

is the unit element
of
G
. A group

is called of polynomial growth if there exists
a finite generating set

such that

for some

. One checks that this definition
is independent of the choice of the generating set

. Thus, the
polynomial growth is indeed a property of the group

. In this

(G,S)
of a group of polynomial

for some

and any

, where

is called

.

We denote by

the space of functions on

. The support of

is defined as
\textrm{supp}(u)\coloneqq\{x\in G:u(x)\neq 0\}
.
Let

be the set of all functions with finite support. For
any

, the
\ell^{p}
norm of

is defined as

\lVert u\rVert_{\ell^{p}(G)}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(\underset{x\in G}{\sum}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p}&\text{$0<p<\infty,$}\\
\underset{x\in G}{\sup}\lvert u(x)\rvert&p=\infty,\end{cases}

and we shall write
\lVert u\rVert_{{\ell^{p}(G)}}
as
\lVert u\rVert_{{p}}

for convenience. The
\ell^{p}(G)
space is defined as

\ell^{p}(G)\coloneqq\left\{u\in C(G):\lVert u\rVert_{{\ell^{p}(G)}}<\infty\right\}.

For any

, the difference operator is defined as for any

\nabla_{xy}u=u(y)-u(x).

Let

\lvert\nabla u(x)\rvert_{p}\coloneqq\left(\sum\limits_{y\sim x}\lvert\nabla_{xy}u\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p}

be the

at

We define the Laplace operator as

\Delta u(x)\coloneqq\sum\limits_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x)).

The

(

) norms of

\lVert u\rVert_{D^{1,p}(G)}\coloneqq\lVert\lvert\nabla u\rvert_{p}^{p}\rVert_{\ell^{1}(G)}^{1/p}=\left(\sum\limits_{x\in G}\sum\limits_{y\sim x}\lvert\nabla_{xy}u\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p},

\lVert u\rVert_{D^{2,p}(G)}\coloneqq\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{\ell^{p}(G)}=\left(\sum\limits_{x\in G}\lvert\sum\limits_{y\sim x}\nabla_{xy}u\rvert^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

We define

(

) as the completion of

in the

norm and define

D^{k,p}(G)\coloneqq\left\{u\in\ell^{\frac{Np}{N-kp}}(G):\lVert u\rVert_{D^{k,p}(G)}<\infty\right\}.

In fact

, see
[

for a proof.

Let

be a subset of

. We denoted by

the vertex boundary of

, possibly an empty set. We set

.

Let

be the completion of

in

\lVert\mu\rVert\coloneqq\sup\limits_{u\in c_{0}(G),\lVert u\rVert_{\infty}=1}\langle\mu,u\rangle,\qquad\forall\mu\in\ell^{1}(G).

By definition,

\mu_{n}\xrightharpoonup{w^{\ast}}\mu\;\text{in}\;\ell^{1}(G)\;\text{if and only if}\;\langle\mu_{n},u\rangle\longrightarrow\langle\mu,u\rangle,\forall u\in c_{0}(G).

In the proof, we will use the following facts (see
[

]
).

Fact 2.1
.

(a) Every bounded sequence of
\ell^{1}(G)
contains a

-convergent

(b) If

in

is bounded and

\lVert\mu\rVert\leq\varliminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert\mu_{n}\rVert.

(c) If

then

\lVert\mu\rVert=\langle\mu,1\rangle.

Next, we prove the discrete HLS inequalities on

.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
r,s>1,

0<\alpha<N
,
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{N-\alpha}{N}=2
,
we get the discrete HLS inequality

(2.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in G\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}R_{\alpha}(x,y)f(x)g(y)\leq C_{r,s,\alpha}\|f\|_{\ell^{r}}\|g\|_{\ell^{s}},\>\forall f\in\ell^{r}(G),g\in\ell^{s}(G),

and an equivalent form is

(2.2)

\lVert R_{\alpha}\ast f\rVert_{\ell^{t}}\leq C_{r,\alpha}\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{r}},\>\forall f\in\ell^{r}(G),

where
1<r<\frac{N}{\alpha},0<\alpha<N,t=\frac{Nr}{N-\alpha r}
.

Proof.

By the method of subordination and Bochner’s functional
calculus
[
59
,
9
]
, the fractional Laplace operator on

G
is defined as

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\coloneqq\sideset{\frac{1}{\mid\varGamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})\mid}}{{}_{0}^{\infty}}{\int}\left(e^{t\Delta}u-u\right)t^{-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\text{d}t,

where

is the semigroup of
\Delta
, see
[
37
]
.
Since
G
with the homogenous dimension

satisfies the weak Poincaré
inequality, the heat kernel

of the Laplace operator
on

has the Gaussian heat kernel bounds
[
4
, Theorem 6.19]
.
Hence the Green’s function
R_{\alpha}
of the fractional Laplace
is

R_{\alpha}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}k_{t}(x,y)t^{-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\text{d$t$},\;x,y\in G,

which has the asymptotic relation
R_{\alpha}(x,y)\simeq\left(d^{S}(x,y)\right)^{\alpha-N}
.
Then by the Young’s inequality for weak type spaces

[
22
, Theorem 1.4.25.]
,

\lVert R_{\alpha}\ast f\rVert_{\ell^{t}}\lesssim\lVert R_{\alpha}\rVert_{\ell^{\frac{N}{N-\alpha},\infty}}\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{r}}\simeq\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{r}},

where
\lVert f\rVert_{\ell^{p,\infty}}\coloneqq\underset{t>0}{\text{sup }}t\lambda_{f}^{1/p}(t),\,\lambda_{f}(t)\coloneqq\sharp\left\{x:\mid f(x)\mid>t\right\}
.
And (
2.1
) follows from the H
\ddot{o}
lder inequality.
∎

Then we introduce the classical Brézis-Lieb lemma
[
10
, Theorem 1]
.
Consider a measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
, which consists of
a set
\Omega
equipped with a
\sigma

\Sigma
and a

\mu:\Sigma\to\left[0,\infty\right]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Brézis-Lieb lemma) Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)

be a measure space,
\{u_{n}\}\subset L^{p}(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
,
and
0<p<\infty.
If

(a)
\;\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}
,

(b)
\;u_{n}\to u,n\to\infty

\mu
-almost everywhere in
\Omega
,
then

(2.3)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}-\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p})=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Remark 2.4
.

(1) The preceding lemma is a
refinement of the Fatou’s Lemma.

(2) Since
\;\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}
, passing
to a subsequence if necessary, we have

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{p}^{p}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lVert u_{n}-u\rVert_{p}^{p}+\lVert u\rVert_{p}^{p}.

(3) If
\Omega
is countable and
\mu
is a positive measure defined
on
\Omega
, then we get a discrete version of the classical Brézis-Lieb
lemma.

(4) If
p\geq 1
, for every
q\in[1,p]
,
\{u_{n}\}\subset L^{p}

still satisfies conditions (a) and (b), then there exists an easy
variant of the classical Brézis-Lieb lemma

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\intop\mid\mid u_{n}\mid^{q}-\mid u_{n}-u\mid^{q}-\mid u\mid^{q}\mid^{\frac{p}{q}}\text{d$\mu$}=0.

(5) For
p>1
, (a) and (b) yield
u_{n}\xrightharpoonup{w}u
weakly
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, that is, the pointwise convergence of a bounded
sequence yields the weak convergence, see
[
8
, Proposition 4.7.12]
.

Now we are ready to prove three discrete versions of the Brézis-Lieb

Lemma 2.5

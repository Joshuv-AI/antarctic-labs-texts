Constrained Optimization of Rank-One Functions with Indicator Variables
Soroosh Shafiee Fatma Kılınç-Karzan

Abstract
Optimization problems involving minimization of a rank-one convex function over constraints modeling restrictions on the support of the decision variables emerge in various machine learning applications. These problems are often modeled with indicator variables for identifying the support of the continuous variables. In this paper we investigate compact extended formulations for such problems through perspective reformulation techniques. In contrast to the majority of previous work that relies on support function arguments and disjunctive programming techniques to provide convex hull results, we propose a constructive approach that exploits a hidden conic structure induced by perspective functions. To this end, we first establish a convex hull result for a general conic mixed-binary set in which each conic constraint involves a linear function of independent continuous variables and a set of binary variables. We then demonstrate that extended representations of sets associated with epigraphs of rank-one convex functions over constraints modeling indicator relations naturally admit such a conic representation. This enables us to systematically give perspective formulations for the convex hull descriptions of these sets with nonlinear separable or non-separable objective functions, sign constraints on continuous variables, and combinatorial constraints on indicator variables. We illustrate the efficacy of our results on sparse nonnegative logistic regression problems.

Introduction

We consider specific classes of the general mixed-binary convex optimization problem with indicator variables

\displaystyle\min_{{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}}\left\{H({\bm{x}}):~({\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal X}\times{\cal Z},~x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d]\right\},

(1)

where
H:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex function,
{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
denotes the feasible set, and
[d]:=\left\{1,\dots,d\right\}
. Each binary variable
z_{i}
determines whether a continuous variable
x_{i}
is zero or not by requiring
x_{i}=0
when
z_{i}=0
and allowing
x_{i}
to take any value when
z_{i}=1
. The sets
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Z}
model restrictions on continuous and binary variables, respectively.

The most commonly studied constraints defining
{\cal Z}
are cardinality, hierarchy and multicolinearity constraints. For example, the cardinality set, i.e.,
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\leq\kappa\}
, is widely used in the best subset selection problem in statistics
(
Bertsimas et al. 2016
;
Bertsimas and Van Parys 2020
)
. Several other restrictions for
{\cal Z}
also arise in statistical problems; e.g., hierarchy constraints of the form of
z_{i}\leq z_{j}
are used in
(
Bien et al. 2013
;
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020b
)
,
constraints on a subset of indicator variables to prevent multicollinearity are proposed in
(
Bertsimas and King 2016
)
,
constraints linking the indicator variables associated with regression coefficients in the same group in group variable selection are explored in
(
Hazimeh et al. 2023
;
Huang et al. 2012
)
,
and constraints for cycle prevention in causal graphical variable selection problems in regression problems are examined in
(
Kucukyavuz et al. 2020
;
Manzour et al. 2021
)
.

Various other constraints on both continuous and discrete variables emerge as means to enforce priors from human experts to enhance interpretability and/or improve prediction performance, see e.g.,
(
Cozad et al. 2014
)
. Among these, nonnegativity constraints on continuous variables naturally appear in a range of applications, including signal recovery
(
Atamtürk et al. 2021
)
, portfolio selection
(
Atamtürk and Gómez 2022
;
Han and Gómez 2021
)
, healthcare and criminal justice
(

)
, chemical process simulation
(

;
Cozad et al. 2015
)

(

)

Problem (
1
) is NP-hard
(
Natarajan 1995
)
, and as a result in the literature its convex surrogates like lasso
(
Tibshirani 1996
;
Hastie et al. 2015
)

(
Bertsimas et al. 2016
)
have been studied. In this paper, we study the epigraph associated with (
1

\displaystyle\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}:~H({\bm{x}})\leq\tau,~x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d]\right\},

and provide its closed convex hull characterization.
The primary challenge in this class of problems stems from the complementary constraints between
x_{i}
and
z_{i}
. Whenever an a priori bound on the magnitude of the continuous variables
|x_{i}|
is known, these constraints can be linearized via the big-M method. However, finding a suitable bound on the continuous variables to set the big-M parameter can be very challenging, and the resulting big-M formulations are known to be weak. Dating back to
Ceria and Soares 1999
, the perspective functions have played a significant role in offering big-M free reformulations of mixed-binary convex optimization problems. In particular,
Frangioni and Gentile 2006
introduce perspective cuts based on a linearization of perspective functions.
Aktürk et al. 2009
then show that perspective relaxations can be viewed as implicitly including all (infinitely many) perspective cuts.
Günlük and Linderoth 2010
examine a
separable
structure where the objective function of (
1
) constitutes a sum of univariate functions, taking the form
H(x)=\sum_{i\in[d]}h_{i}(x_{i})
for some lower semicontinuous and convex functions
h_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, and
study the mixed-binary set

\displaystyle{\cal H}:=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}:~\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{i\in[d]}h_{i}(x_{i})\leq\tau,\\[4.30554pt]
x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d]\end{array}\right\}

when
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\cal Z}=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
. Using the perspective reformulation technique,
Günlük and Linderoth 2010
presents an
ideal
formulation of the closed convex hull of
{\cal H}
(
\cl\conv({\cal H})
) in the original space of variables.

\cl\conv({\cal H})

{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\cal Z}=\{z\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}:{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\leq\kappa\}

are convex and quadratic.

extend these findings to convex differentiable functions under certain constraint qualification conditions. More recently, under this separable function assumption, using a support function argument,

In a number of important applications, including portfolio optimization in finance
(
Bienstock 1996
;
Wei et al. 2022a
)

As a result, a growing stream of research
(
Jeon et al. 2017
;
Atamtürk and Gómez 2018
;
Frangioni et al. 2020
;
Atamtürk et al. 2021
;
Liu et al. 2022

studies (
1

is a

More recently, a number of papers
(
Atamtürk and Gómez 2019
;
Atamtürk and Gómez 2022

offer strong perspective-based relaxations for rank-one non-separable objective functions by analyzing the closed convex hull of the mixed-binary set

\displaystyle{\cal T}:=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}:~\begin{array}[]{l}h({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\leq\tau,\\[4.30554pt]
x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d]\end{array}\right\}

under various assumptions on
h(\cdot)
,
{\cal X}
, and
{\cal Z}
.
In particular,
Atamtürk and Gómez 2019
examine rank-one convex quadratic functions when
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\cal Z}=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
.
Wei et al. 2020
extend these findings by allowing constraints on the binary variables, that is,
{\cal Z}\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
. Following up on this,
Wei et al. 2022b
give a perspective formulation of the closed convex hull of
{\cal T}
(
\cl\conv({\cal T})
) in the original space of variables when
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\cal Z}\subset\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
, and the function

is lower semicontinuous and convex. Nonetheless, the formulations in
(
Wei et al. 2020
;
Wei et al. 2022b
)
require including a nonlinear convex inequality for each facet of
\conv({\cal Z}\backslash\left\{{\bm{0}}\right\})
. Note that
\conv({\cal Z}\backslash\left\{{\bm{0}}\right\})
itself may be complicated and may require an exponential number of inequalities for its description even when
{\cal Z}
is a simple boolean set itself.
In a separate thread, when
{\cal Z}=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
,
Atamtürk and Gómez 2022
examine
\cl\conv({\cal T})
when
h
is a convex quadratic function and there are additional nonnegativity requirements on some of the continuous variables, i.e.,
{\cal X}=\{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:~x_{i}\geq 0,~\forall i\in{\cal I}\}
for some
{\cal I}\subseteq[d]
, and propose classes of valid inequalities for
\cl\conv({\cal T})
. However, these inequalities given in the original problem space require a cutting-surface based implementation, which may result in numerical issues. To address such issues,
Han and Gómez 2021
present compact extended formulations for
\cl\conv({\cal T})
when
{\cal Z}=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
. These extended formulations are not only applicable to general lower semicontinuous and convex function
h
, but are also easier to implement as they can be embedded within standard branch-and-bound based integer programming solvers.

In this paper, by linking the perspective formulations to conic programming, we propose a conic-programming based approach to address constrained optimization of rank-one functions with indicator variables. Our approach generalizes the existing results by studying simultaneously both sign restrictions on continuous variables and combinatorial constraints on binary variables. Specifically, we provide perspective formulations for
\cl\conv({\cal H})
and
\cl\conv({\cal T})
when
{\cal X}\!=\!\left\{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{i}\geq 0,\,\forall i\in{\cal I}\right\}
for some
{\cal I}\subseteq[d]
,
{\cal Z}\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
and all functions are proper, lower semicontinuous, and convex.
The crux of our approach relies on understanding the recessive and rounding directions associated with these sets involving complementarity constraints. Based on this understanding, we reduce the given complementarity constraints to fewer and much simpler to handle complementarity relations in a lifted space. For example, in the case of the set
{\cal T}
when
{\cal X}
does not contain any sign restrictions, following this approach we analyze a set involving a
single
complementarity constraint with a single new binary variable. In this way, our approach provides an effective way to arrive at compact extended formulations for
\cl\conv({\cal H})
and
\cl\conv({\cal T})
in a more direct manner while simultaneously handling arbitrary
{\cal Z}\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
and arbitrary sign restrictions included in
{\cal X}
. In contrast, the proof techniques in
(
Atamtürk and Gómez 2019
;
Atamtürk and Gómez 2022
;
Wei et al. 2020
;
Han and Gómez 2021
;
Wei et al. 2022b
)
are based on support function arguments and disjunctive programming methods, which have resulted in addressing restrictions on the sets
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Z}
in separate studies through different approaches.
The key contributions of this paper and its organization are summarized below.

Technical Tools

In this section we build theoretical tools necessary for our study of separable and nonseparable sets.
In Section
2.1
, we begin by examining perspective functions, recognizing that their epigraphs are convex cones, and relating their epigraphs back to the sets
{\cal H}
and
{\cal T}
of our interest as well as the conic mixed-binary sets
{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
. We then study the convex hull characterization of
{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
in Section
2.2
and conclude with technical tools to handle a simple linking constraint in taking convex hulls and the closure operation in an extended space.

2.1
Perspective function and its closure

Perspective function plays an important role in our analysis.
For a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
with
h(\bm{0})=0
, we define its
perspective function

h^{+}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
as

\displaystyle h^{+}({\bm{x}},w):=\begin{cases}w\,h({\bm{x}}/w),&\qquad\text{if }w>0,\\
0,&\qquad\text{if }w=0\text{~and~}{\bm{x}}=\bm{0},\\
+\infty,&\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The epigraph of
h^{+}
is given by

\epi(h^{+}):=\left\{(t,{\bm{x}},w)\!\in\!{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:~t\geq h^{+}({\bm{x}},w)\right\}.

Note that our definition of perspective function
h^{+}
is almost matching with the classical definition of the perspective function given in
(
Hiriart-Urruty and Lemaréchal 2004
;
Combettes 2018
)
as

\widetilde{h}({\bm{x}},w):=\begin{cases}w\,h({\bm{x}}/w),&\qquad\text{if }w>0,\\
+\infty,&\qquad\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where the main distinction between
h^{+}
and
\widetilde{h}
is that
h^{+}(\bm{0},0)=0
whereas
\widetilde{h}(\bm{0},0)=+\infty
.
We first establish that the epigraph of the perspective function
h^{+}
is a convex cone under standard assumptions.

Lemma 1

Let
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\!\to\!\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function with
h(\bm{0})=0
. Then,
\epi(h^{+})
is a convex cone containing the origin.

Proof

Since
h
is assumed to be proper, lower semicontinuous and convex, the perspective function
h^{+}
is proper and convex. This follows from
(
Rockafellar 1970
, p. 35)
.
Therefore, the set
\epi(h^{+})
is convex. Moreover,
(\bm{0},0)\in\epi(h^{+})
as
h^{+}(\bm{0},0)=0
.
Additionally,
\epi(h^{+})
is indeed a cone, i.e., for any
\lambda>0
and
(t,{\bm{x}},w)\in\epi(h^{+})
, we have
(\lambda t,\lambda{\bm{x}},\lambda w)\!\in\!\epi(h^{+})
. This is because for any
w>0
and any
(t,{\bm{x}},w)\in\epi(h^{+})
, we have
w\,h({\bm{x}}/w)\leq t
or
\lambda w\,h(\lambda{\bm{x}}/(\lambda w))\leq\lambda t
. We thus conclude that
\epi(h^{+})
is a cone containing the origin.
∎

While
\epi(h^{+})
is a convex cone under standard assumptions, it is not closed. Therefore, we also study the closure of the perspective function.
Recall that for a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
with
h(\bm{0})=0
, the
closure of the perspective function

h^{\pi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
is defined as

\displaystyle h^{\pi}({\bm{x}},w):=\begin{cases}w\,h({\bm{x}}/w),&\qquad\text{if }w>0,\\
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}s\,h({\bm{x}}/s),&\qquad\text{if }w=0,\\
+\infty,&\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is well known that the closure of
\widetilde{h}
, and consequently
h^{+}
, is given by
h^{\pi}
; see for example
(
Hiriart-Urruty and Lemaréchal 2004
, Proposition 2.2.2)
.
The epigraph of
h^{\pi}
is given by
\epi(h^{\pi})\!=\!\{(t,{\bm{x}},w)\!\in\!{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\!\!:\!t\geq h^{\pi}({\bm{x}},w)\}
. We analyze the cone generated by the perspective function and its closure in the next lemma.

Lemma 2

Let
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function such that
h(\bm{0})=0
. Then,
\cl(\epi(h^{+}))=\epi(h^{\pi})
. If, additionally,
\epi(h)
does not contain a line,
\epi(h^{\pi})
is pointed.

Proof

Note that the perspective function
h^{+}({\bm{x}},w)
coincides with its closure
h^{\pi}({\bm{x}},w)
for
w>0
and
(\bm{0},0)\in\dom(h^{\pi})
. Then, by lower semicontinuity of
h^{\pi}
(see
(
Rockafellar 1970
, p. 37 and Theorem 13.3)
), we conclude
\cl(h^{+})=h^{\pi}
.
Therefore, we have
\cl(\epi(h^{+}))=\epi(h^{\pi})
.

To see that
\epi(h^{\pi})
is pointed, suppose both
(-\bar{t},-\bar{\bm{x}},-\bar{w}),(\bar{t},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{w})\!\in\!\epi(h^{\pi})
. Then, we must have
\bar{w}=0
. The closure of the perspective function at
({\bm{x}},0)
satisfies

\displaystyle h^{\pi}({\bm{x}},0)=\lim_{w\downarrow 0}w\,h({\bm{x}}/w)=\sup\left\{{\bm{g}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}:{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})\right\},

where
h^{*}
denotes the conjugate of

and the last equality follows from
(
Rockafellar 1970
, p. 37 and Theorem 13.3)
. Thus, as both
(-\bar{t},-\bar{\bm{x}},0),(\bar{t},\bar{\bm{x}},0)\in\epi(h^{\pi})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}h^{\pi}(\bar{\bm{x}},0)=\sup\left\{{\bm{g}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}:{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})\right\}\leq\bar{t}\\
h^{\pi}(-\bar{\bm{x}},0)=\sup\left\{-{\bm{g}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}:{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})\right\}\leq-\bar{t},\end{cases}

which enforces that
{\bm{g}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=\bar{t}
for all
{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})
. Hence, for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the function
h
satisfies

\displaystyle h(\gamma\bar{\bm{x}})=\sup_{{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})}\gamma{\bm{g}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}-h^{*}({\bm{g}})=\sup_{{\bm{g}}\in\dom(h^{*})}\gamma\bar{t}-h^{*}({\bm{g}})=\gamma\bar{t}-h(\bm{0})=\gamma\bar{t},

where the first equality holds because
h=h^{**}
(as
h
is a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function and thus
(
Rockafellar 1970
, Theorem 12.2)
applies), the second equality follows from the observations that
{\bm{g}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=\bar{t}
, the third equality follows from the definition of biconjugate function and the relation
h(\bm{0})=h^{**}(\bm{0})=0
.
If
(\bar{t},\bar{\bm{x}})\neq\bm{0}
, we have
(\gamma\bar{t},\gamma\bar{\bm{x}})\in\epi(h)
for any
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, which contradicts our assumption that
\epi(h)
does not contain a line. Hence, we have shown that
(-\bar{t},-\bar{\bm{x}},-\bar{w}),(\bar{t},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{w})\in\epi(h^{\pi})
only if
(\bar{t},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{w})=\bm{0}
, implying that
\epi(h^{\pi})
is pointed.
This then completes the proof.
∎

2.2
Conic binary sets

Our results in this subsection and also later on rely on Carathéodory’s theorem. Carathéodory’s theorem states that if a point lies in the convex hull of a finite-dimensional set, it can be written as a convex combination of a finite number of points from the original set.
We first establish a description for the convex hull of
{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
.

Proposition 1

Consider the set
{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
defined as in (
6
), where
\Delta\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
,
{\mathbb{K}}=\times_{i\in[d]}\,{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
, and each
{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
is a convex cone containing the origin for every
i\in[p]
. Then, we have
\conv({\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}}))={\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
.

Proof

We will proceed by showing that
\conv({\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}}))\subseteq{\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
and
\conv({\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}}))\supseteq{\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
. The first direction trivially holds as
{\mathbb{K}}
is convex and
\conv(A\cap B)\subseteq\conv(A)\cap\conv(B)
. Therefore, we focus on establishing
\conv({\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}}))\supseteq{\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
.
Consider
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})\in{\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
. Then, as
\bar{\bm{\delta}}\in\conv(\Delta)
, by Carathéodory’s theorem, we have
\bar{\bm{\delta}}=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\delta}}^{k}
for some finite
q
,
{\bm{\delta}}^{k}\in\Delta
, and
\lambda_{k}>0
with
\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}=1
. For each
k\in[q]
, we construct the vector
{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}
with subvectors
{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}
as follows

\displaystyle{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i},&\text{~if~}\bar{\delta}_{i}=0\\[4.30554pt]
{\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}}/{\bar{\delta}_{i}},&\text{~if~}\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0\text{~and~}\delta^{k}_{i}=1\\[4.30554pt]
\bm{0},&\text{~if~}\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0\text{~and~}\delta^{k}_{i}=0\end{array}.\right.

Next, we prove that
({\bm{\alpha}}^{k},{\bm{\delta}}^{k})\!\in\!{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
by showing that
{\bm{A}}_{i}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\delta^{k}_{i}\!\in\!{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
for all
k\in[q]
and
i\in[p]
.
Consider any index
i\in[p]
.
Note that as
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})\in{\cal S}(\conv(\Delta),{\mathbb{K}})
, we have
{\bm{A}}_{i}\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\bar{\delta}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
.
If
\bar{\delta}_{i}=0
, then
{\bm{A}}_{i}\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
. Also, we have
\delta^{k}_{i}=0
for all
k\in[q]
because
\bar{\delta}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}\delta^{k}_{i}=0
,
\lambda_{k}>0
, and
\delta^{k}_{i}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
for every
k\in[q]
. Thus, the point
({\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i},\delta^{k}_{i})
satisfies
{\bm{A}}_{i}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\delta^{k}_{i}={\bm{A}}_{i}\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
for every
k\in[q]
.
If
\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0
, then
{\bm{A}}_{i}(\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}/\bar{\delta}_{i})+{\bm{B}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
as
{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
is a cone.
Moreover, as
\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0
, there exists at least one index
\hat{k}\in[q]
with
\delta^{\hat{k}}_{i}=1
.
For any
k\in[q]
such that
\delta^{k}_{i}=1
, by definition
({\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i},\delta^{k}_{i})=(\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}/\bar{\delta}_{i},1)
and thus it satisfies
{\bm{A}}_{i}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\delta^{k}_{i}={\bm{A}}_{i}(\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}/\bar{\delta}_{i})+{\bm{B}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
.
Finally, for any
k\in[q]
such that
\delta^{k}_{i}=0
, as
\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0
, by definition
({\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i},\delta^{k}_{i})=(\bm{0},0)
and thus it satisfies
{\bm{A}}_{i}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\delta^{k}_{i}=\bm{0}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
as
{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
contains the origin. All in all, we have shown that
{\bm{A}}_{i}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+{\bm{B}}_{i}\delta^{k}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
for all
k\in[q]
and
i\in[p]
, which implies that
({\bm{\alpha}}^{k},{\bm{\delta}}^{k})\in{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
for all
k\in[q]
.

We next prove that
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}({\bm{\alpha}}^{k},{\bm{\delta}}^{k})
. Recall that
\bar{\bm{\delta}}=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\delta}}^{k}
where
{\bm{\delta}}^{k}\in\Delta
for all
k\in[q]
. Then,
\bar{\delta}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}\delta^{k}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]:\delta^{k}_{i}=1}\lambda_{k}
holds for all
i\in[p]
. Moreover, for any
i\in[p]
with
\bar{\delta}_{i}\neq 0
, from the definition of
{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]:\delta^{k}_{i}=1}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}+\sum_{k\in[q]:\delta^{k}_{i}=0}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}=\frac{\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}}{\bar{\delta}_{i}}\sum_{k\in[q]:\delta^{k}_{i}=1}\lambda_{k}=\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i},

where the last equality follows from
\bar{\delta}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]:\delta^{k}_{i}=1}\lambda_{k}
.
Furthermore, for any
i\in[p]
with
\bar{\delta}_{i}=0
, by the definition of
{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}
we have
\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}{\bm{\alpha}}^{k}_{i}=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}=\bar{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}
. Thus, we conclude that
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}({\bm{\alpha}}^{k},{\bm{\delta}}^{k})
, which shows

, as desired.
∎

{\bm{A}}_{i}(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}^{j}_{i}/\varepsilon)+{\bm{B}}_{i}\tilde{\delta}^{j}_{i}={\bm{B}}_{i}\tilde{\delta}^{j}_{i}\in{\mathbb{K}}_{i}

Using these relations, we may thus write

\displaystyle\textstyle(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}~\sum_{k\in[q]}(1-\varepsilon d)(\hat{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})+\sum_{i\in[d]}\varepsilon(\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}^{j}/\varepsilon,\tilde{\bm{\delta}}^{j}).

Then,
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})
is written as the limit of convex combinations of points from
{\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}})
. Thus,
(\bar{\bm{\alpha}},\bar{\bm{\delta}})\in\cl\conv({\cal S}(\Delta,{\mathbb{K}}))
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4

3
Applications

In this section we analyze the convex hull of the sets
{\cal H}
and
{\cal T}
defined in (
1
) and (
1
), respectively.
We assume that all univariate functions vanish at zero. This in fact holds without loss of any generality if zero is in the domain of the functions. In this case, we can always define a new function by subtracting the constant term
h(0)
from
h
.

We will employ a simple yet powerful proof strategy to characterize the closed convex hull of the sets
{\cal H}
and
{\cal T}
. In the first step, we identify the recessive directions of the given set, and study the associated sets that are augmented by adding these recessive directions. In particular, such augmented sets admit extended formulations with new binary variables and complementary restrictions that model the recessive directions. In the second step, we demonstrate that the original complementary restrictions can be eliminated from these new sets in the extended space.
Finally, using perspective functions, we transform these new sets into the form of the set
{\cal W}
and leverage Proposition
2
along with Lemma
4
.

3.1
Separable functions

As a warm up for the subsequent sections, we start by analyzing the separable function case in this subsection.
Recall the mixed-binary set

\displaystyle{\cal H}=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}:~\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{i\in[d]}h_{i}(x_{i})\leq\tau,\\[4.30554pt]

This type of set arises as substructure in a number of applications such as Markowitz portfolio selection
(

(
Günlük and Linderoth 2010
)
, sparse learning
(
Xie and Deng 2020

(
Bertsimas et al. 2021a
)
. The result of this section relies on the following assumption.

{assumption}

The set

. For any

. The function

Theorem 3.1

Under Assumption

\displaystyle\textstyle\cl\conv({\cal H})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times\conv({\cal Z}):~\sum_{i\in[d]}h^{\pi}_{i}(x_{i},z_{i})\leq\tau\right\}.

(

and the set presented in Theorem

and

In contrast, our proof is constructive and make use of the hidden conic structure introduced by the perspective function. As a by product, it allows us to easily include nonnegativity constraints on the set

.

We demonstrate the importance of the requirement imposed on the binary set

in Assumption

would suggest that the set

, which may not be correct in this simple case. The requirement on the binary set

If there exist an index

such that for any

and compute

from a lower dimensional set.

We conclude this section by a remark for the case of
totally unimodular
binary sets. The set
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:{\bm{F}}{\bm{z}}\leq{\bm{f}}\}
is totally unimodular if the matrix
{\bm{F}}
is totally unimodular and the vector
{\bm{f}}
is integer-valued. Recall that every square submatrix of a totally unimodular matrix has determinant
0
,
+1
or
-1
. Examples of totally unimodular sets include cardinality constraint set, in which
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\leq\kappa\}
for some
\kappa\in[d]
, weak hierarchy set, where
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:z_{d}\leq\sum_{i\in[d-1]}z_{i}\}
, and strong hierarchy set, where
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:z_{d}\leq z_{i},~\forall i\in[d-1]\}
.

Remark 2

Suppose that the set
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:{\bm{F}}{\bm{z}}\leq{\bm{f}}\}
is totally unimodular. Then, under Assumptions
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\textstyle\conv({\cal H})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times[0,1]^{d}:~\sum_{i\in[d]}h^{\pi}_{i}(x_{i},z_{i})\leq\tau,~{\bm{F}}{\bm{z}}\leq{\bm{f}}\right\}.

3.2
Rank-1 functions with
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Recall the mixed-binary set

\displaystyle{\cal T}=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\cal Z}:~h({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\leq\tau,~x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d]\right\},

where we assume that the vector
{\bm{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfies
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[d]
.
In this section we will consider the case that
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
This type of set appears as a substructure in sparse regression
(
Bertsimas et al. 2016
;
Xie and Deng 2020
)
and sparse classification
(
Bertsimas et al. 2021b
;
Wei et al. 2022b
)
.

For a given set
{\cal Z}\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
, we construct a graph
G_{{\cal Z}}=(V,E)
, where
V=[d]
denotes its nodes,
E
denotes its edge, and
\{i,j\}\in E
if and only if
i\neq j
and there exits a vector
{\bm{z}}\in{\cal Z}
with
z_{i}=z_{j}=1
.
The structure of
{\cal Z}
, as represented by its associated graph
G_{{\cal Z}}
, plays an important role in the description of
\conv({\cal T})
.

3.2.1
Connected graph

Our result relies on the following assumption.

{assumption}

The set
{\cal X}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The set
{\cal Z}\subseteq\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
satisfies
{\cal Z}\neq\left\{\bm{0}\right\}
. The graph
G_{{\cal Z}}
associated with
{\cal Z}
is connected. The vector
{\bm{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
satisfies
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[d]
. The function
h:{\mathbb{R}}\to\overline{{\mathbb{R}}}
is proper, lower semicontinuous, and convex with
h(0)=0
.

Theorem 3.2

Under Assumption
3.2.1
, the set
{\cal T}
defined in (
1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\cl\conv({\cal T})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:~\exists w\in{\mathbb{R}}\text{~s.t.~}~\begin{array}[]{l}h^{\pi}({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}},w)\leq\tau,\\[4.30554pt]
(w,{\bm{z}})\in\conv(\Delta_{1})\end{array}\right\},

where
\Delta_{1}=\{(w,{\bm{z}})\in\left\{0,1\right\}\times{\cal Z}:~w\leq{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\}
.

Theorem
3.2
provides a compact extended formulation for
\cl\conv({\cal T})
by introducing a
single
binary variable
w
and finitely many
linear
constraints describing
\conv(\Delta_{1})
. The variable
w
embeds the entire complexity of describing
\cl\conv({\cal T})
into the complexity of characterizing
\conv(\Delta_{1})
, a polyhedral set.
Another advantage of Theorem
3.2
is that it does not require a complete description of
\conv(\Delta_{1})
to be known in advance when solving the mixed-binary problem with a solver; one can simply provide the binary formulation for
\Delta_{1}
to the solver instead of
\conv(\Delta_{1})
. Moreover, as the set
\Delta_{1}
is defined by linear inequalities and today’s commercial optimization solvers have advanced features to dynamically generate effective cuts for binary (or mixed-integer) sets defined by linear inequalities, solvers easily and automatically generate strong cuts that approximate the convex hulls of such sets as
\Delta_{1}
. This is in particular crucial when
\conv(\Delta_{1})
is difficult to characterize completely. In such cases, our formulation enables the users to rely on the optimization software to generate effective cuts on the fly.

In contrast to our compact extended formulation,
Wei et al. 2022b
give an ideal formulation in the original space of variables for
\cl\conv({\cal T})
. This ideal formulation, however, relies on an explicit formulation for
\conv({\cal Z}\backslash\left\{\bm{0}\right\})
. Specifically,
(
Wei et al. 2022b
, Proposition 1)
shows that

\displaystyle\conv({\cal Z}\backslash\left\{0\right\})=\conv({\cal Z})\cap\left\{{\bm{z}}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}:~\bm{\gamma}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\geq 1,~\forall\bm{\gamma}\in{\cal F}\right\}

for some finite set
{\cal F}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Using this observation,
Wei et al. 2022b
proves that

\displaystyle\cl\conv({\cal T})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\begin{array}[]{l}h({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\leq\tau,~{\bm{z}}\in\conv({\cal Z}),\\[4.30554pt]
h^{\pi}({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}},\bm{\gamma}^{\top}{\bm{z}})\leq\tau,~\forall\bm{\gamma}\in{\cal F}\end{array}\right\}.

This description of
\cl\conv({\cal T})
requires access to
{\cal F}
, i.e., the explicit inequality description of
\conv({\cal Z}\backslash\left\{0\right\})
which may not be easy to attain.
Furthermore, the set
{\cal F}
can be very complex with possibly exponentially many (in the dimension
d
) inequalities and then the set in (
3.2.1
) will have exponentially many
nonlinear
convex inequalities. Nonlinear convex constraints as opposed to linear ones are often more expensive to handle by the optimization solvers, and this makes the ideal formulation given in (
3.2.1
) quite impractical from a computational standpoint whenever
|{\cal F}|
is large. Furthermore, to the best of our knowledge, the cut generation strategies for the nonlinear constraints as opposed to the linear ones are rather limited in today’s optimization solvers. Thus, the nonlinear inequalities included in formulation (
3.2.1
) would have a rather limited effect on the solvers in terms of generating further cuts on the fly beyond what the user specifies.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we first make an observation about the recessive direction of
{\cal T}
.

Lemma 5

Under Assumption
3.2.1

through the set

To prove Theorem

, we first make an observation about the recessive direction of

Lemma 11

Under Assumption
3.3
, if
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\cl\conv({\cal T})
, then for any
\bar{\bm{x}}\in{\cal X}
satisfying
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=0
we have
(\tau,{\bm{x}}+\bar{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\cl\conv({\cal T})
.

Proof

Based on
\bar{\bm{x}}
, we introduce the index sets
{\cal K}_{>}=\{k\in[d]:a_{k}\bar{x}_{k}>0\}
and
{\cal K}_{<}=\{k\in[d]:a_{k}\bar{x}_{k}<0\}
. If
{\cal K}_{>}={\cal K}_{<}=\varnothing
, then
\bar{\bm{x}}=\bm{0}
and the claim trivially holds. In the following we assume that
{\cal K}_{>}
and
{\cal K}_{<}
are both nonempty (as
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=0
either one of
{\cal K}_{>}
and
{\cal K}_{<}
being nonempty implies the other is also nonempty).

For every
k\in{\cal K}_{>}
and
l\in{\cal K}_{<}
, we construct the vector

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}^{kl}:=\left(\frac{a_{l}\bar{x}_{l}\bar{x}_{k}}{\sum_{j\in{\cal K}_{<}}a_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}\right){\bm{e}}_{k}+\left(\frac{a_{k}\bar{x}_{k}\bar{x}_{l}}{\sum_{q\in{\cal K}_{>}}a_{q}\bar{x}_{q}}\right){\bm{e}}_{l}.

By construction,
{\bm{x}}^{kl}
satisfies

\displaystyle\textstyle{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{kl}=(a_{k}a_{l}\bar{x}_{l}\bar{x}_{k})/(\sum_{j\in{\cal K}_{<}}a_{j}\bar{x}_{j})+(a_{k}a_{l}\bar{x}_{l}\bar{x}_{k})/(\sum_{q\in{\cal K}_{<}}a_{q}\bar{x}_{q})=0

since
\sum_{j\in{\cal K}_{<}}a_{j}\bar{x}_{j}=-\sum_{q\in{\cal K}_{>}}a_{q}\bar{x}_{q}
as
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=0
. We also have
{\bm{x}}^{kl}\in{\cal X}
as
\sign(x^{kl}_{k})=\sign(\bar{x}_{k})
and
\sign(x^{kl}_{l})=\sign(\bar{x}_{l})
and
\bar{\bm{x}}\in{\cal X}
. Furthermore, by Assumption
3.3
, there exits a binary vector
{\bm{z}}^{kl}\in{\cal Z}
such that
{\bm{z}}^{kl}_{k}={\bm{z}}^{kl}_{l}=1
. Thus, the support of
{\bm{x}}^{kl}
is covered by the binary vector
{\bm{z}}^{kl}
.
Finally, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\sum_{l\in{\cal K}_{<}}{\bm{x}}^{kl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\sum_{l\in{\cal K}_{<}}\left(\frac{a_{l}\bar{x}_{l}\bar{x}_{k}}{\sum_{j\in{\cal K}_{<}}a_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}\right){\bm{e}}_{k}+\!\!\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\sum_{l\in{\cal K}_{<}}\left(\frac{a_{k}\bar{x}_{k}\bar{x}_{l}}{\sum_{q\in{\cal K}_{>}}a_{q}\bar{x}_{q}}\right){\bm{e}}_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\bar{x}_{k}{\bm{e}}_{k}+\sum_{l\in{\cal K}_{<}}\bar{x}_{l}{\bm{e}}_{l}=\sum_{i\in[d]}\bar{x}_{i}{\bm{e}}_{i}=\bar{\bm{x}}.

Since
h({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{kl})=h(0)=0
and the support of
{\bm{x}}^{kl}
is covered by
{\bm{z}}^{kl}
, we conclude
(0,\lambda{\bm{x}}^{kl},{\bm{z}}^{kl})\in{\cal T}
for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
k\in{\cal K}_{>}
, and
l\in{\cal K}_{<}
.
Define
\kappa:=|{\cal K}_{>}|\cdot|{\cal K}_{<}|
. Then, as
\cl\conv({\cal T})
is both closed and convex, we have

\displaystyle(\tau,{\bm{x}}+\bar{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\!=\!\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\!\left[\frac{\lambda-\kappa}{\lambda}(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})+\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\sum_{l\in{\cal K}_{<}}\frac{1}{\lambda}(0,\lambda{\bm{x}}^{kl},{\bm{z}}^{kl})\right]\!\!\in\!\cl\conv({\cal T}).

Hence, the claim follows.

We next introduce the auxiliary mixed-binary set

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}:=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}):\exists{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}\text{~s.t.~}({\bm{x}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}})\in\widehat{\cal T},{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}},~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}=\tau\right\},

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\cal T}\!\!:=\!\!\left\{\!({\bm{x}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}})\!\in\!{\cal X}\!\times\!{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\!\times\!{\cal X}\!\times\!{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\!\times\!{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\!:\!\begin{array}[]{l}h(a_{i}s_{i})\leq t_{i},\,\forall i\in[d],\\[4.30554pt]
s_{i}(1-w_{i})=0,\,\forall i\in[d],\\[4.30554pt]
({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\!\in\!\Omega,~s_{i}\leq x_{i},\,\forall i\!\in\!{\cal I}\end{array}\!\right\},

and

3.4
. We first establish that the closed convex hull of

can be obtained from the set

Proposition 5

Under Assumption
3.3
, we have
\cl\conv({\cal T})=\cl\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
.

Proof

From Lemma
11
, we deduce that the set

\displaystyle{\cal R}:=\left\{(\bar{\tau},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\cal X}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:\bar{\tau}=0,~\bar{\bm{z}}=0,~{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=0\right\}

is contained in
\rec({\cal T})
. Thus,
\cl\conv({\cal T})=\cl\conv({\cal T}+{\cal R})
.
We prove the proposition by showing that
\cl\conv({\cal T})\subseteq\cl\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
and
{\cal T}+{\cal R}\supseteq\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
(which immediately implies
\cl\conv({\cal T}+{\cal R})\supseteq\cl\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
).

\bm{(\subseteq)}
Let
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal T}
. If
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}=0
, then by setting
{\bm{s}}={\bm{w}}={\bm{t}}=\bm{0}
we see that
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
. Thus, we next focus on the case of
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\neq 0
. In this case,
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})
may not necessarily be in
\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
. Nonetheless, we will show that
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
. To this end, we introduce the set

{\cal K}_{>}:=\{k\in[d]:(a_{k}x_{k})({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})>0\}
, and the vector
\bm{\delta}:=\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}x_{k}{\bm{e}}_{k}
. We next define the points

\displaystyle(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k}):=\left(\tau,{\bm{x}}-\bm{\delta}+\frac{{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bm{\delta}}{a_{k}}{\bm{e}}_{k},{\bm{z}}\right),\quad\forall k\in{\cal K}_{>}.

Note here that by definition of
{\cal K}_{>}
, we have
a_{k}\neq 0
and
x_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in{\cal K}_{>}
.
By construction, we have
\hat{\bm{x}}^{k}\circ(1-\hat{\bm{z}}^{k})=0
as
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal T}
, and also

\displaystyle{\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}-{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bm{\delta}+\frac{{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bm{\delta}}{a_{k}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{e}}_{k}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}.

Thus, we have
h({\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k})=h({\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\leq\tau=\hat{\tau}^{k}
and
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k})\!\in\!{\cal T}
for all

Moreover, we claim that
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k})\in\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
for all
k\in{\cal K}_{>}
.
To see this, define
\hat{\bm{s}}^{k}:=(({\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k})/a_{k}){\bm{e}}_{k}
,
{\bm{t}}^{k}:=(h({\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k})){\bm{e}}_{k}
and
\hat{\bm{w}}^{k}:={\bm{e}}_{k}
. Recall that
k\in{\cal K}_{>}
and so
x_{k}\neq 0
and by definition
\hat{\bm{z}}^{k}={\bm{z}}
. Then, as
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal T}
we have
x_{k}(1-z_{k})=0
which implies
z_{k}=1
. Thus, we deduce
\hat{\bm{w}}^{k}\leq\hat{\bm{z}}^{k}
. Then, by construction of
\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k}
, we immediately conclude that the following conditions

\displaystyle(\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})\in\widehat{\cal T},~{\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},~{\bm{1}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{t}}^{k}\leq\hat{\tau}^{k}

are satisfied.
Furthermore, we have
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})=\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\lambda_{k}(\hat{\tau}_{k},\hat{\bm{x}}_{k},\hat{z}_{k}),
where
\lambda_{k}=(a_{k}x_{k})/({\bm{a}}^{\top}\bm{\delta})
(as we assumed that
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\neq 0
, the set
{\cal K}_{>}
is nonempty and
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{\delta}}\neq 0
, recall also the definition of
{\cal K}_{>}
implies
(a_{k}x_{k})/({\bm{a}}^{\top}\bm{\delta})>0
for all
k
) with
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\sum_{k\in{\cal K}_{>}}\lambda_{k}=1
.
Thus, the point
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
, which establishes
{\cal T}\subseteq\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
, as desired.
Since
\conv({\cal T})
is the smallest convex set containing
{\cal T}
, we conclude
\conv({\cal T})\subseteq\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})
. Taking closure of both side proves that
\cl\conv({\cal T})\subseteq\cl\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})

\bm{(\supseteq)}
Let
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
. Then, there exists
{\bm{s}}\in{\cal X}
with
s_{i}\leq x_{i}
for all
i\in{\cal I}
and
{\bm{w}}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
with
{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}}
,
s_{i}(1-w_{i})=0
for all

, and
{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1
such that
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}
and
\sum_{i\in[d]}h(a_{i}s_{i})\leq\tau
. We will next show that
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal T}+{\cal R}
.
From
{\bm{w}}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
and
{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1
we deduce that at most one element of
{\bm{w}}
is equal to 1. Then, through the constraints
s_{i}(1-w_{i})=0
for all
i\in[d]
and
{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}}
, we deduce that
{\bm{s}}
has at most one nonzero element and
s_{i}(1-z_{i})=0
for all
i\in[d]
as well. Hence, the constraint
\sum_{i\in[d]}h(a_{i}s_{i})\leq\tau
implies that
(\tau,{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}})\in{\cal T}
. Moreover, the vector
\bar{\bm{x}}={\bm{x}}-{\bm{s}}
satisfies
\bar{\bm{x}}\in{\cal X}
(as

for all
i\in{\cal I}
) and
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}=0

{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}

(0,\bar{\bm{x}},\bm{0})\in{\cal R}
. Therefore,
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})=(\tau,{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}})+(0,\bar{\bm{x}},\bm{0})\in{\cal T}+{\cal R}
, as required. This implies that
{\cal T}+{\cal R}\supseteq\overline{{\cal T}}_{s}
.
This completes the proof.

We next show that
\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})

Lemma 12

Under Assumption
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}):\exists{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}\text{~s.t.~}\begin{array}[]{l}({\bm{x}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}})\in\conv(\widehat{\cal T})\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}=\tau,~{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\end{array}\right\},

where
\widehat{\cal T}
is as defined in (
3.3
).

Proof

Let
{\cal U}:={\mathbb{R}}\times\widehat{\cal T},~{\cal V}_{1}:=\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}\,)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{5d+1}:{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}\}
, and
{\cal V}_{2}:=\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}\,)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{5d+1}:{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\leq\tau\}
. By definition, we have

\displaystyle\conv(\overline{{\cal T}}_{s})=\conv(\Proj_{\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}}({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}\cap{\cal V}_{2}))=\Proj_{\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}}(\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}\cap{\cal V}_{2})).

Hence, the claim will follow if we prove that
\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}\cap{\cal V}_{2})=\conv({\cal U})\cap{\cal V}_{1}\cap{\cal V}_{2}
.
From Lemma
4
(i)
, we have
\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}\cap{\cal V}_{2})=\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1})\cap{\cal V}_{2}
. Therefore, in the sequel we will show that
\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1})=\conv({\cal U})\cap{\cal V}_{1}
.

As
{\cal V}_{1}
is convex, it is sufficient to show
\conv({\cal U})\cap{\cal V}_{1}\subseteq\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1})
.
Take a point
(\bar{\tau},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{s}},\bar{\bm{z}},\bar{\bm{w}},\bar{\bm{t}})\in\conv({\cal U})\cap{\cal V}_{1}
. Since this point is in
{\cal V}_{1}
, we have
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{s}}
. Additionally, as the point is in
\conv({\cal U})
, by Carathéodory’s theorem, we have

(\bar{\tau},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{s}},\bar{\bm{z}},\bar{\bm{w}},\bar{\bm{t}})=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})

for some finite
q
,
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})\in{\cal U}
, and
\lambda_{k}>0
with
\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}=1
. Define the sets

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{<}

\displaystyle:=\left\{k\in[q]:~{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}^{k}<{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\},~{\cal K}_{=}:=\left\{k\in[q]:{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}^{k}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{\cal K}_{>}:=\left\{k\in[q]:{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}^{k}>{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\}.

We consider two possible scenarios.

(i)
If
|{\cal K}_{=}|=q
, then
|{\cal K}_{<}|=|{\cal K}_{>}|=0
and
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})\in{\cal V}_{1}
for every
k\in[q]
. Thus,
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})\in{\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}
for every
k\in[q]
, which implies that
(\bar{\tau},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{s}},\bar{\bm{z}},\bar{\bm{w}},\bar{\bm{t}})\in\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1})
.

(ii)
If
|{\cal K}_{=}|<q
, then
|{\cal K}_{<}|>0
and
|{\cal K}_{>}|>0
because
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{s}}
. In the following we will use a rounding scheme that iteratively replace the points
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})
with new points
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})
.
Pick an index
j\in{\cal K}_{<}
and an index

, and consider the following construction

\displaystyle(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\quad}l}(\tau^{j},{\bm{s}}^{j},{\bm{s}}^{j},{\bm{z}}^{j},{\bm{w}}^{j},{\bm{t}}^{j})&k=j\\[4.30554pt]
(\tau^{l},{\bm{x}}^{l}+(\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{l})({\bm{x}}^{j}-{\bm{s}}^{j}),s^{l},{\bm{z}}^{l},w^{l})&k=l\\[4.30554pt]
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})&k\notin\left\{j,l\right\}.\end{array}\right.

By construction, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})=\sum_{k\in[q]}\lambda_{k}(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k}).

Moreover, from
(\tau^{j},{\bm{x}}^{j},{\bm{s}}^{j},{\bm{z}}^{j},{\bm{w}}^{j},{\bm{t}}^{j})\in{\cal U}
, we deduce

for every
i\in{\cal I}
. Then,

for every
i\in{\cal I}
. Thus, we conclude that
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})\in{\cal U}
for every
k\in[q]
.
Defining the index sets
\hat{\cal K}_{=}=\left\{k\in[q]:{\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{s}}^{k}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{k}\right\}
, one can show that
|\hat{\cal K}_{=}|>|{\cal K}_{=}|
because the index
j
now satisfies the condition
{\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{s}}^{j}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}^{j}
. We next replace the points
(\tau^{k},{\bm{x}}^{k},{\bm{s}}^{k},{\bm{z}}^{k},{\bm{w}}^{k},{\bm{t}}^{k})
with the new points
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})
, and repeat the same rounding scheme. In this way, after at most
q
iterations, we will obtain a set of points
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})
,
k\in[q]
, for which
|\hat{\cal K}_{=}|=q
. Hence,
(\hat{\tau}^{k},\hat{\bm{x}}^{k},\hat{\bm{s}}^{k},\hat{\bm{z}}^{k},\hat{\bm{w}}^{k},\hat{\bm{t}}^{k})\in{\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1}
for every
k\in[q]
and we conclude that
(\bar{\tau},\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{s}},\bar{\bm{z}},\bar{\bm{w}},\bar{\bm{t}})\in\conv({\cal U}\cap{\cal V}_{1})
. This completes the proof.

{\bm{s}}

\displaystyle\rec(\{\bm{\eta}\!\in\!\cl({\cal U})\!:\!{\bm{A}}\bm{\eta}\!=\!\bm{0},{\bm{B}}\bm{\eta}\!=\!\bm{b}\})

\displaystyle=\rec\left(\left\{(0,\bm{0},\bm{0},{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},\bm{t})\!:\!\begin{array}[]{l}h^{\pi}(a_{i}s_{i},w_{i})\leq t_{i},~\forall i\in[d]\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{s}},\in{\cal X},~s_{i}\leq 0,\,\forall i\in{\cal I}\\[4.30554pt]
({\bm{w}},\bm{0})\in\conv(\Omega)\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}=0,~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}=0\end{array}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\rec\left(\left\{(0,\bm{0},\bm{0},{\bm{s}},\bm{0},\bm{t})\!:\!\begin{array}[]{l}h^{\pi}(a_{i}s_{i},0)\leq t_{i},~\forall i\notin{\cal I}\\[4.30554pt]
s_{i}=0,\,0\leq t_{i},\,\forall i\in{\cal I}\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}=0,~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}=0\end{array}\right\}\right),

where the first equation holds by the definition of
\conv(\widehat{\cal T})
and the fact that
{\bm{A}}\bm{\eta}=\bm{0}
implies
(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})=(0,\bm{0},\bm{0})
. The second equation follows from
({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\in\conv(\Omega)
, which implies
\bm{0}\leq{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}}
and as
{\bm{z}}=0
we deduce
{\bm{w}}=0
. Moreover,
{\bm{x}}=0
implies
0\leq s_{i}\leq x_{i}
for all
i\in{\cal I}
and so
s_{i}=0
for all
i\in{\cal I}
. This also results in
h^{\pi}(a_{i}s_{i},0)=h^{\pi}(0,0)=0\leq t_{i}
for all
i\in{\cal I}
.
Since the function
h
is nonlinear, proper, lower semicontinuous and convex, the set
\{(u,v):h^{\pi}(u,0)\leq v\}
is a convex closed pointed cone thanks to Lemma
2
. Consequently, the origin is an extreme point of the set, meaning that
\sum_{i\in[d]}(a_{i}s_{i},t_{i})=(0,0)
only if
s_{i}=t_{i}=0
. Hence, the second requirement of Lemma
4

(ii)
also follows, and we can apply Lemma
4

(ii)
to conclude that

\displaystyle\cl\conv({\cal T})=\left\{(\tau,{\bm{x}},{\bm{z}}):\exists{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}~\text{~s.t.~}~\begin{array}[]{l}({\bm{x}},{\bm{z}},{\bm{s}},{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}})\in\cl\conv(\widehat{\cal T}),\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}=\tau,~{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}\end{array}\right\}.

The proof concludes by projecting out the variable
{\bm{t}}
using the Fourier-Motzkin elimination approach.
∎

We conclude this section by characterizing
\conv(\Omega)
where
\Omega=\{({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}\times{\cal Z}:~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1,~{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}}\}
for some simple integral sets
{\cal Z}
. The proofs of the subsequent results are provided in Appendix
A
.
We start with the case when
{\cal Z}
is defined by a cardinality constraint. In this case, the resulting set
\Omega
admits an immediate totally unimodular representation.

Lemma 13

Suppose
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\leq\kappa\}
, where
\kappa\in[d]\backslash\{1\}
. Then

\displaystyle\conv(\Omega)=\{({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\in[0,1]^{d+d}:~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1,~{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}},~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{z}}\leq\kappa\}.

We next characterize
\conv(\Omega)
for the weak and strong hierarchy sets. Unlike the previous case,
\Omega
does not immediately admit a totally unimodular representation.
Nonetheless, it turns out that the set
\Omega\backslash\{\bm{0}\}
is totally unimodular for weak hierarchy constraints. Using Lemma
A.2

(i)
in Appendix
A
, which provides a description of
\conv(\Omega)
based on
\conv(\Omega\backslash{\bm{0}})
, the following lemma analyzes the weak hierarchy constraints.

Lemma 14

Suppose
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:~z_{d}\leq\sum_{i\in[d-1]}z_{i}\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\textstyle\conv(\Omega)=\left\{({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\in[0,1]^{d+d}:~\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1,~{\bm{w}}\leq{\bm{z}},~z_{d}\leq\sum_{i\in[d-1]}z_{i}\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq\sum_{i\in[d-1]}z_{i}\end{array}\right\}.

We conclude this section by examining the strong hierarchy constraints. In this case, neither the set
\Omega
nor
\Omega\backslash\left\{\bm{0}\right\}
admits an immediate representation with totally unimodular matrices. Using Lemma
A.2

(ii)
in Appendix
A
, the following lemma analyzes the strong hierarchy constraints.

Lemma 15

Suppose
{\cal Z}=\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:~z_{d}\leq z_{i},\,\forall i\in[d-1]\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\conv(\Omega)\!=\!\left\{({\bm{w}},{\bm{z}})\!:\!\exists\tilde{\bm{z}}^{0},\dots,\tilde{\bm{z}}^{d}\text{ s.t. }\begin{array}[]{l}{\bm{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d},~\tilde{\bm{z}}^{0}_{d}\geq 0,~{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}\leq 1,\\[4.30554pt]
{\bm{z}}=\tilde{\bm{z}}^{0}+\sum_{i\in[d]}\tilde{\bm{z}}^{i}\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{\bm{z}}^{0}_{d}\leq\tilde{z}^{0}_{j}\leq 1-{\bm{1}}^{\top}{\bm{w}},~\forall j\in[d-1]\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{z}^{i}_{i}=w_{i},~\forall i\in[d-1],\\[4.30554pt]
0\leq\tilde{\bm{z}}^{i}_{d}\leq\tilde{z}^{i}_{j}\leq w_{i},~\forall i,j\in[d-1]\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{z}^{d}_{j}=w_{d},~\forall j\in[d]\end{array}\right\}.

Numerical Results

In this section we study the numerical performance of our conic formulations on a nonlinear logistic regression problem with quadratic features. The resulting exponential cone programs are modeled with JuMP
(
Lubin and Dunning 2015
)
and solved with MOSEK 10 on a MacBook Pro with a
2.80
GHz processor and
16
GB RAM. In these experiments, we set the time limit to
300
seconds and the number of threads of the solver to
4
.

In the nonlinear logistic regression problem with quadratic features, for an input data
\bm{\phi}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
, we construct the lifted feature vector

{\bm{a}}=((\phi_{k})_{k\in[p]},(\phi_{k}^{2})_{k\in[p]},(\phi_{k}\phi_{l})_{k,l\in[p]:l>k})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
d=p(p+3)/2
. We denote the coefficients of the nonlinear classifier and its support by the vectors
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
. With slight abuse of notation, we use the notation
{\bm{x}}=((x_{k})_{k\in[p]},(x_{kk})_{k\in[p]},(x_{kl})_{k,l\in[p]:l>k}))
to refer to the elements of
{\bm{x}}
. In a similar fashion, we use the notation
{\bm{z}}=((z_{k})_{k\in[p]},(z_{kk})_{k\in[p]},(z_{kl})_{k,l\in[p]:l>k}))
for
{\bm{z}}
. We examine the following sparse logistic regression problem

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}\displaystyle\min&\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in[N]}\log(1+\exp(-b_{j}{\bm{a}}_{j}^{\top}{\bm{x}}))+\lambda\sum_{i\in[d]}z_{i}+\mu\|{\bm{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\\[17.22217pt]
\text{s.t.}&{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d},~{\bm{z}}\in{\cal Z},~x_{i}(1-z_{i})=0,~\forall i\in[d],\end{array}

where
({\bm{a}}_{j},b_{j})_{j\in[N]}
represents the (nonlinear) feature-label pairs constructed from the input vector
(\bm{\phi}_{j})_{j\in[N]}
in the training data, and
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
denote the regularization coefficients. We use the set

{\cal Z}=\left\{{\bm{z}}\in\{0,1\}^{d}:~z_{kk}\leq z_{k},~z_{kl}\leq z_{k},~z_{kl}\leq z_{l},~\forall k,l\in[p]\text{~s.t.}~l>k\right\}

to capture strong hierarchy constraints.
We consider various reformulations of (
4
) using the convex hull results presented in Section
3
.
Namely, based on Theorem
3.1
, we introduce the

reformulation as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}\displaystyle\min&\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in[N]}t_{i}+\log(2)+\lambda\sum_{i\in[d]}z_{i}+\mu\sum_{i\in[d]}r_{i}\\[17.22217pt]
\text{s.t.}&{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d},~{\bm{z}}\in{\cal Z},~\bm{u},\bm{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\\[4.30554pt]
&(r_{i},z_{i},x_{i})\in{\cal K}_{\text{rsoc}},&\forall i\in[d],\\[4.30554pt]

&u_{j}+v_{j}\leq 2,&\forall j\in[N].\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad}l}\displaystyle\min&\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in[N]}t_{i}+\log(2)+\lambda\sum_{i\in[d]}z_{i}+\mu\sum_{i\in[d]}r_{i}\\[17.22217pt]

.

generates valid cuts for the binary set

Among

When we examine the gaps reported by the solver, we still observe that the same phenomena, but this time the associated gaps reported by the solver are considerably larger than the true optimality gaps. This is because for this class of problems, the heuristic methods utilized by the solver are not very advanced, and the good quality feasible solutions of a formulation are often found at integral nodes in the B&B tree, so essentially by chance.
Therefore, the B&B procedure for the formulations that admit quick to solve node relaxations often results in high quality feasible solutions. Consequently, in the case of expensive lifted formulations such as (
4
) while the actual optimality gaps are very close to zero, the solver is unable to report this gap due to the inferior quality of the feasible solution found in the associated B&B tree. To address this issue, BARON introduces the concept of “relaxation-only constraints”
(
Sahinidis 1996
;
Sahinidis accessed November 13, 2023
)
. These constraints are active during the relaxation step, but are disregarded during the local search step. To the best of our knowledge, MOSEK 10 does not support this feature, and thus in our figures we report both the true optimality gap and the solver reported gap for the formulations tested.

Mock Maass Forms Revisited
Yingkun Li, Christina Roehrig

Abstract
. In this paper, we use theta integrals to give a different construction of mock Maass forms studied by Sander Zwegers. With this method, we construct new real-analytic modular forms, whose Fourier coefficients are logarithms of algebraic numbers in a real quadratic field.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Coh88
]
, Cohen shows that the function

\varphi_{0}(\tau):=\sqrt{v}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}T(n)\,e^{2\pi inu/24}\,K_{0}(2\pi|n|v/24)\quad(\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H})

is a weight 0 Maass form, i.e. it transforms as a modular form of weight 0 on
\Gamma_{0}(2)
up to some explicitly given multiplier and it is an eigenfunction of the weight 0 Laplace operator
\Delta_{\tau}
defined in (
1.1
). Here,
K_{0}
denotes a modified Bessel function of the second kind, which makes immediately clear that the second property holds (see (
2.18
) for the exact definition).
As an arithemtic function,
T(n)
is defined for any
n\equiv 1\ (\operatorname{mod}24)
and describes the excess of the number of inequivalent solutions
(x,y)
of Pell’s equation
x^{2}-6y^{2}=n
with
x+3y\equiv\pm 1\ (\operatorname{mod}12)
over the number of inequivalent solutions with
x+3y\equiv\pm 5\ (\operatorname{mod}12)
.

One can also establish the relation

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv 1\ (\operatorname{mod}24)\end{subarray}}T(n)\,q^{|n|/24}=q^{1/24}\sigma(q)+q^{-1/24}\sigma^{\ast}(q),

where
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\ast}
are the
q
-hypergeometric functions defined by

\displaystyle\sigma(q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n(n+1)/2}}{(1+q)\dots(1+q^{n})}=1+\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{n+1}(1-q)\ldots(1-q^{n})

\displaystyle=1+q-q^{2}+2q^{3}-2q^{4}+q^{5}+\ldots,

respectively

\displaystyle\sigma^{\ast}(q)

\displaystyle:=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}}{(1-q)(1-q^{3})\dots(1-q^{2n-1})}=-2\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n+1}(1-q^{2})(1-q^{4})\ldots(1-q^{2n})

\displaystyle=-2q-2q^{2}-2q^{3}+2q^{7}+2q^{8}+2q^{10}+\ldots.

These functions can be rewritten as indefinite theta functions.
Zwegers
[
Zwe12
]
used this to put Cohen’s example in a more general framework and interpreted
\varphi_{0}
as the “harmonic” part
\Phi^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{a,b}
of a real-analytic modular form
\hat{\Phi}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{a,b}
, where harmonic means that the function vanishes under the modified Laplace operator

(1.1)

\tilde{\Delta}:=\Delta_{\tau}+\frac{1}{4},~\Delta_{\tau}:=v^{2}(\partial_{u}^{2}+\partial_{v}^{2}).

In general, Zwegers showed that
\tilde{\Delta}\hat{\Phi}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{a,b}
is the difference of two theta series
\theta^{(1,1)}
depending on negative lines
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
respectively (see Theorem
3.3
for details).

In this paper, we will use the theta integral approach as in
[
FK17
,
CL20
]
to extend these results and construct a preimage
\tilde{\vartheta}
of
one
such theta function
\theta^{(1,1)}

under the operator
\tilde{\Delta}
.
Furthermore, the harmonic part Fourier coefficients of
\tilde{\vartheta}
are logarithms of algebraic numbers in real quadratic fields, similar to the ones studied in
[
CL20
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be an anisotropic lattice of signature
(1,1)
and
\theta^{(1,1)}_{L+h}(\tau,t)
the theta function defined in (
2.12
) for
h\in L^{*}/L
.
Then there exists
\tilde{\vartheta}_{L+h}(\tau,t)
such that

(1.2)

\tilde{\Delta}\tilde{\vartheta}_{L+h}(\tau,t)=\theta^{(1,1)}_{L+h}(\tau,t)

for all
t>0
, and whose harmonic part coefficients
\tilde{c}_{t}
are given in (
3.17
).
Furthermore, we have
\Phi^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{h,0}=\hat{\vartheta}_{L+h}^{c_{1},c_{2}}(\tau)=\tilde{\vartheta}_{L+h}(\tau,t_{2})-\tilde{\vartheta}_{L+h}(\tau,t_{1})
when
c_{i}=c(t_{i})
via (
2.7
) with
\hat{\vartheta}
defined in (
3.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

Similar results should also hold in the isotropic case, as in
[
Li17
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

In Examples
3.8
and
3.9
, we show that our result goes beyond the work of Zwegers and produces a new type of modular form.

The paper is organized in the following way. We recall preliminary notions of theta functions in Section 2.
Section 3 contains our main construction. The first half rephrases the construction of Zwegers. The second half extends it and proves Theorem
3.7
, which implies Theorem
1.1
.
The last section proposes a generalization of the construction in Section 3, and some future directions.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall preliminary notions of quadratic space, unary theta functions and theta kernels that will be used in constructing mock Maass forms later. As usual, we denote
\mathbf{e}(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
.

Let
L
be an even, integral lattice of rank
n
with a quadratic form
Q
and associated bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
, and denote
L^{*}\subset V:=L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
its dual lattice.
When
Q
is positive definite, we have the theta function

(2.1)

\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau):=\sum_{x\in L+h}p(x)\mathbf{e}(Q(x)\tau)\quad(h\in V),

where
p:V(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C}
is a harmonic polynomial of degree
d
.
We omit the subscript
p
when it is the constant 1.
The theta function is a modular form of weight
d+n/2
and a congruence subgroup of
{\mathrm{Mp}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The vector-valued modular form

(2.2)

\Theta_{L,p}(\tau)=(\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau))_{h\in L^{*}/L}

transforms with respect to the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
on
{\mathrm{Mp}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
See
[
Bor98
,
Sch09
]
for more information about
\rho_{L}
.
When
Q
is negative definite, we set

(2.3)

\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau):=v^{d+n/2}\theta_{L^{-}+h,p}(-\overline{\tau}),

where
L^{-}
denotes the lattice
L
with the quadratic form
-Q
.
When
L
has rank 1, the polynomial
p
is necessarily linear and we denote

(2.4)

\theta_{L+h}^{(\epsilon)}:=\theta_{L+h,x^{\epsilon}},~\Theta_{L}^{(\epsilon)}:=\Theta_{L,x^{\epsilon}}

for
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
.

When
Q
is not definite, i.e. of signature
(n^{+},n^{-})
with
n^{\pm}\geq 1
, one can attach to
L
a two variable theta function in the following way.
Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be the Grassmannian of negative
n^{-}
-planes in
V(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{n^{+}}\oplus(\mathbb{R}^{n^{-}})^{-}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the definite
\mathbb{R}
-quadratic space with quadratic form

Q((x_{1},\dots,x_{n})):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}^{2}/2
.
For each
Z\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
\lambda\in V(\mathbb{R})
, let
Z^{\perp}\subset V(\mathbb{R})
be its orthogonal complement and
\lambda_{Z},\lambda_{Z^{\perp}}
the projections of
\lambda
such that
Q(\lambda)=Q(\lambda_{Z^{\perp}})+Q(\lambda_{Z})
. Then the Siegel theta function given by

(2.5)

\theta_{L+h}(\tau,Z):=v^{n^{-}/2}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q(\lambda_{Z^{\perp}})\tau+Q(\lambda_{Z})\overline{\tau}\right)

is a real-analytic modular form in
\tau
of weight
\frac{n^{+}-n^{-}}{2}
.
More generally, one can add a harmonic polynomial
p
of degree
d^{+}
, resp.
d^{-}
, in the coordinates of
{Z^{\perp}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n^{+}}
, resp.
Z\cong(\mathbb{R}^{n^{-}})^{-}
, to define
\theta_{L+h,p}
, which will be a real-analytic modular form of weight
\frac{n^{+}-n^{-}}{2}+d^{+}-d^{-}
.

This is a special case of the theta series considered by Borcherds. In Theorem 4.1 in
[
Bor98
]
, the explicit transformation behavior is given.
For example, when
V
is indefinite of dimension 2, it will have signature
(1,1)
and
V(\mathbb{R})
is isometric to the real hyperbolic plane
V_{0}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with quadratic form
Q_{0}
given by

(2.6)

Q_{0}((x_{1},x_{2})):=x_{1}x_{2}.

Let
B_{0}
be the associated bilinear form.
The symmetric space
{\mathcal{D}}
of
V_{0}
is parametrized by
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
via

(2.7)

\begin{split}c:\mathbb{R}^{\times}&\to{\mathcal{D}}\\
t&\mapsto\mathbb{R}\cdot Z^{-}_{t},~\end{split}

where we denote

(2.8)

Z^{\pm}_{t}:=\left({t}{},\pm{t^{-1}}{}\right)\in V_{0}(\mathbb{R}).

The space
{\mathcal{D}}
has two connected components, and we denote by
{\mathcal{D}}^{+}
the component parametrized by
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
under
c
.
For any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in V_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we can write it as

(2.9)

\lambda=\lambda_{t}^{+}Z_{t}^{+}-\lambda_{t}^{-}Z_{t}^{-},~\lambda_{t}^{\pm}:=\frac{B_{0}(\lambda,Z_{t}^{\pm})}{{2}}=\frac{\pm\lambda_{1}t^{-1}+\lambda_{2}t}{{2}}\in\mathbb{R},

which satisfy the equations

(2.10)

(\lambda^{+}_{t})^{2}+(\lambda^{-}_{t})^{2}=\frac{\lambda^{2}_{1}t^{-2}+\lambda^{2}_{2}t^{2}}{{2}},~(\lambda^{+}_{t})^{2}-(\lambda^{-}_{t})^{2}=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=Q_{0}(\lambda).

For a lattice
L\subset V=V_{0}
, the theta function
\theta_{L+h,p}
can be explicitly written down as

(2.11)

\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau,t)=v^{d^{-}+1/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L+h\end{subarray}}p(\lambda_{t}^{+},\lambda_{t}^{-})\mathbf{e}\left((\lambda_{t}^{+})^{2}\tau-(\lambda_{t}^{-})^{2}\overline{\tau}\right).

Note that the only harmonic polynomials on
V(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
have degree
(d^{+},d^{-})
with
d^{\pm}\leq 1
.
We denote

(2.12)

\theta_{L+h}^{(d^{+},d^{-})}(\tau,t):=\theta_{L+h,x^{d^{+}}y^{d^{-}}}(\tau,t)

where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
.
When
t=t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
satisfies
t_{0}^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, we have a rational splitting
V=U\oplus U^{\perp}
with
U
positive definite such that
U^{\perp}(\mathbb{R})=c(t_{0})
, and
P\oplus N\subset L
is a finite index sublattice with
P:=L\cap U
and
N:=L\cap U^{\perp}
definite lattices.
The theta function
\theta_{L+h}^{(d^{+},d^{-})}
has the following corresponding splitting

(2.13)

\theta^{(d^{+},d^{-})}_{L+h}(\tau,t_{0})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in P^{*}/P,~\nu\in N^{*}/N\\
P\oplus N+(\mu,\nu)\subset L+h\end{subarray}}\theta^{(d^{+})}_{P+\mu}(\tau)\theta^{(d^{-})}_{N+\nu}(\tau)

for
d^{\pm}\in\{0,1\}
.

Suppose
L
is anisotropic. Then

(2.14)

L=L_{\mathfrak{a},M}=M\mathfrak{a}

for some fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of a real quadratic field
F
with quadratic form
Q_{\mathfrak{a},M}={\mathrm{Nm}}/(AM)
,
A={\mathrm{Nm}}(\mathfrak{a})
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
.
Its dual lattice is
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}
with
\mathfrak{d}
the different of
F
.
The isometry between
V(\mathbb{R})
and
V_{0}(\mathbb{R})
is given by

(2.15)

\begin{split}\iota:V(\mathbb{R})&\to V_{0}(\mathbb{R})\\
\lambda&\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{AM}}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}).\end{split}

Using this isometry, we can give the following explicit expression of

\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau,t)
as in (
2.11
)

(2.16)

\theta_{L+h,p}(\tau,t)=v^{d^{-}+1/2}\sum_{\lambda\in L_{\mathfrak{a},M}+h}p\left(\frac{\lambda t^{-1}+\lambda^{\prime}t}{2\sqrt{AM}},\frac{-\lambda t^{-1}+\lambda^{\prime}t}{2\sqrt{AM}}\right)\mathbf{e}(Q_{\mathfrak{a},M}(\lambda)u)\mathbf{e}\left(-\pi\frac{(\lambda t^{-1})^{2}+(\lambda^{\prime}t)^{2}}{AM}\right).

The splitting in (
2.13
) happens when
t=t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
satisfies

(2.17)

t_{0}^{2}\in F^{1}:=\{\alpha\in F^{\times}:{\mathrm{Nm}}(\alpha)=1\}.

For later purpose, we will also require the
K
-Bessel function
K_{0}(x)
, which is defined by

(2.18)

K_{0}(x):=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-x\cosh(T)}dT,~x>0

and satisfies the second order differential equation

\left(x\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-x\right)K_{0}(x)=0,

with the asymptotics

(2.19)

K_{0}(x)\sim\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}e^{-x}

as
x\to\infty
.
For convenience, we also set

(2.20)

K_{0}(0):=0

and state a simple lemma that will be useful later.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote
K_{0}(x;a):=\mathrm{sgn}(a)\int_{|a|}^{\infty}e^{-x\cosh(T)}dT
.
Then

(2.21)

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{K_{0}(x;a)}{K_{0}(x)}=0.

Proof.

The claim is trivial for
a=0
and we only need to prove it for
a>0
.
In that case,
b:=\sinh(a/2)>0
and a change of variable
u=\sqrt{x}\sinh(T/2),du=\sqrt{x}\cosh(T/2)\,dT/2=\sqrt{u^{2}+x}\,dT/2
gives us

\displaystyle\sqrt{x}e^{x}K_{0}(x;a)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{x}\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-2x\sinh(T/2)^{2}}dT=2\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{x}b}e^{-2u^{2}}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^{2}/x+1}}<2\int^{\infty}_{\sqrt{x}b}e^{-2u^{2}}du.

In particular, we have the bound

(2.22)

|K_{0}(x;a)|<\frac{e^{-x(1+2b^{2})}}{xb}.

Taking the limit
x\to\infty
and applying (
2.19
) finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Mock Maass Forms

In this section, we will use the suitable integrals of Siegel theta kernels to construct higher depth analogues of the mock Maass forms constructed by Zwegers.
In particular, we will express Maass’ and Zwegers’ constructions as special examples (see Section
3.1
).

3.1.
Mock Maass Theta Functions I

Let
L=\iota(L_{\mathfrak{a},M})\subset V_{0}(\mathbb{R})

be the even, integral, anisotropic lattice as in (
2.14
), and
\Gamma_{L}\subset{\mathrm{SO}}(L)
the subgroup
of the discriminant kernel consisting of totally positive units in
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
congruent to 1 modulo
M\sqrt{D}
.
Then
\Gamma_{L}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let

\varepsilon_{L}>1
be its generator.
Given
t_{2}>t_{1}>0
and denote
c_{i}:=c(t_{i})\in{\mathcal{D}}
, we consider

(3.1)

\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau):=\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t)\frac{dt}{t}.

This is a real-analytic modular function in
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
.
When
t_{2}/t_{1}\in\Gamma_{L}
, we can evaluate (
3.1
) by unfolding.
For
h\not\in L
, we can unfold the integral to obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(t_{2}/t_{1})}{\log\varepsilon_{L}}\int^{\varepsilon_{L}}_{1}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{v}\frac{\log(t_{2}/t_{1})}{\log\varepsilon_{L}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{L}\backslash L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)u\right)\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\mathbf{e}\left(\frac{(\lambda e^{-\nu})^{2}+(\lambda^{\prime}e^{\nu})^{2}}{2}iv\right)d\nu

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{v}\frac{\log(t_{2}/t_{1})}{\log\varepsilon_{L}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{L}\backslash L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)u\right)\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-2\pi v|Q_{0}(\lambda)|\cosh(T)}dT.

Using (
2.18
), we can evaluate

(3.2)

\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau)={2\sqrt{v}}\frac{\log(t_{2}/t_{1})}{\log\varepsilon_{L}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma_{L}\backslash L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)u\right)K_{0}(2\pi v|Q_{0}(\lambda)|).

When
h\in L
, we have the additional constant term

{\sqrt{v}}\frac{\log(t_{2}/t_{1})}{\log\varepsilon_{L}}\int^{\varepsilon_{L}}_{1}\frac{dt}{t}={\sqrt{v}}\log(t_{2}/t_{1}).

This recovers the construction of Maass cusp forms by Maass
[
Maa49
]
.
For
c_{1}=c(1)
and
c_{2}=c(\varepsilon_{L})
, we denote

(3.3)

\vartheta_{L+h}(\tau):=\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau).

More generally, we can evaluate the Fourier expansion of
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}
without unfolding as

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\phi_{\tau}(\lambda^{+}_{t},\lambda^{-}_{t})\frac{dt}{t}={\sqrt{v}}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q(\lambda)u\right)\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\mathbf{e}\left(\left((\lambda^{+}_{t})^{2}+(\lambda^{-}_{t})^{2}\right)iv\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle={\sqrt{v}}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)\tau\right)\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}e^{-4\pi v(\lambda^{-}_{t})^{2}}\frac{dt}{t}={\sqrt{v}}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)\tau\right)\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}e^{-\pi vB_{0}(\lambda,Z^{-}_{t})^{2}}\frac{dt}{t}.

This is Zwegers’ function
\hat{\Phi}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{h,0}
in Definition 2.3 of
[
Zwe12
]
, and we have shown that it possesses an integral representation given in (
3.1
).
For general
a,b\in L^{*}
, we can express
\hat{\Phi}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{a,b}
as a linear combination of
\hat{\vartheta}_{\tilde{L}+\tilde{h}}
’s for a suitable sublattice
\tilde{L}\subset L
. See
3.5
for an example.

Now define for
c_{i}=c(t_{i})

(3.4)

\begin{split}\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2},+}_{L+h}(\tau)&:=\sqrt{v}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}a_{\lambda}(t_{1},t_{2})\mathbf{e}(Q_{0}(\lambda)u)K_{0}(2\pi|Q_{0}(\lambda)|v),\\
\varphi^{c_{0}}_{L+h}(\tau)&:=\sqrt{v}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\beta_{t_{0}}(\lambda\sqrt{v})\mathbf{e}(Q_{0}(\lambda)u)\end{split}

with the coefficients
a_{\lambda}
defined by

(3.5)

\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda_{t_{1}}^{-}\lambda_{t_{2}}^{-})\right)&\text{ if }Q_{0}(\lambda)>0,\\

and the function

a function on

Remark 3.2
.

It is easy to check that the coefficients

The function
\hat{\vartheta}_{L+h}^{c_{1},c_{2}}

4.
Action under
\tilde{\Delta}
.

In this section, we will generalize the mock Maass theta functions studied in
3.2
, and study their images under the differential operator
\tilde{\Delta}
.
In the notation of Sections
3.1
and
3.2
, consider

(4.1)

\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P):=\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t)P(\log t)\frac{dt}{t}=\int^{\nu_{2}}_{\nu_{1}}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,e^{\nu})P(\nu)d\nu

for any polynomial
P(X)\in\mathbb{C}[X]
.
This defines a real-analytic modular function in
\tau
.
When
P
is the constant function, this is just the function
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau)
defined in (
3.1
).
When
P(X)=X
and
t_{i}=\varepsilon_{L}^{i-1}t_{0}
, this is the function
\tilde{\vartheta}^{c_{0}}_{L+h}
from (
3.16
).
Just as the function
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}
in Section
3.1
, the generalization
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P)
has the following Fourier expansion

(4.2)

\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P)={\sqrt{v}}\sum_{\lambda\in L+h}\mathbf{e}\left(Q_{0}(\lambda)\tau\right)\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}e^{-\pi vB_{0}(\lambda,Z^{-}_{t})^{2}}P(\log t)\frac{dt}{t}.

When
t_{2}=\varepsilon_{L}t_{1}
, we simply write
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1}}_{L+h}
for
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}
.

To understand the action of
\tilde{\Delta}
on this function, we first record a standard differential operator relation satisfied by the Siegel theta kernel.

Lemma 4.1
.

In the notation above, we have

(4.3)

4\tilde{\Delta}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t)=(t\partial_{t})^{2}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t).

Proof.

One can show that the function
f(\tau,t)
defined by

\begin{split}f(\tau,t)&:=\phi_{\tau}(\lambda_{t}^{+},\lambda_{t}^{-}),~\phi_{\tau}(x,y):=\sqrt{v}\mathbf{e}\left({x^{2}}{}\tau-{y^{2}}{}\overline{\tau}\right),\end{split}

satisfies the identity

\displaystyle(t\partial_{t})^{2}f(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(2\pi v\right)^{2}\left((\lambda_{1}t^{-1})^{2}-(\lambda_{2}t)^{2}\right)^{2}-4\pi v\left((\lambda_{1}t^{-1})^{2}+(\lambda_{2}t)^{2}\right)\right)f(\tau,t)

\displaystyle=4\left(\left(4\pi v\lambda_{t}^{+}\lambda_{t}^{-}\right)^{2}-2\pi v\left((\lambda_{t}^{+})^{2}+(\lambda_{t}^{-})^{2}\right)\right)f(\tau,t).

On the other hand, a straightforward calculation yields

\tilde{\Delta}\phi_{\tau}(x,y)=\left((4\pi vxy)^{2}-2\pi v(x^{2}+y^{2})\right)\phi_{\tau}(x,y).

By the definition of the theta series in (
2.11
), we deduce that (
4.3
) holds.
∎

Now the image of
\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}
under the operator
\tilde{\Delta}
is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

4\tilde{\Delta}\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P)=(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{2})P(\nu_{2})-(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{1})P(\nu_{1})\\
\quad-\left(\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t_{2})P^{\prime}(\nu_{2})-\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t_{1})P^{\prime}(\nu_{1})\right)+\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P^{\prime\prime}).

Remark 4.3
.

Note that
t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t)=-8\pi\theta^{(1,1)}_{L+h}(\tau,t)
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
4.1
to obtain

\displaystyle 4\tilde{\Delta}

\displaystyle\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P)=4\int^{\nu_{2}}_{\nu_{1}}\tilde{\Delta}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,e^{\nu})P(\nu)d\nu=\int^{\nu_{2}}_{\nu_{1}}(\partial_{\nu}^{2}\theta_{L+h}(\tau,e^{\nu}))P(\nu)d\nu

\displaystyle=(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{2})P(\nu_{2})-(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{1})P(\nu_{1})-\int^{\nu_{2}}_{\nu_{1}}(\partial_{\nu}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,e^{\nu})P^{\prime}(\nu)d\nu

\displaystyle=(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{2})P(\nu_{2})-(t\partial_{t}\theta_{L+h})(\tau,t_{1})P(\nu_{1})-\left(\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t_{2})P^{\prime}(\nu_{2})-\theta_{L+h}(\tau,t_{1})P^{\prime}(\nu_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\hat{\vartheta}^{c_{1},c_{2}}_{L+h}(\tau;P^{\prime\prime}).\qed

Corollary 4.4

A closure theorem for Γ \Gamma -convergence and H-convergence with applications to non-periodic homogenization
Andrea Braides, Gianni Dal Maso, Claude Le Bris

Abstract
In this work we examine the stability of some classes of integrals, in particular with respect to homogenization. The prototypical case is the homogenization of quadratic energies with periodic coefficients perturbed by a term vanishing at infinity, which has been recently examined in the framework of elliptic PDE. We use localization techniques and higher-integrability Meyers-type results to provide a closure theorem by Γ \Gamma -convergence within a large class of integral functionals. From such result we derive stability theorems in homogenization which comprise the case of perturbations with zero average on the whole space. The results are also extended to the stochastic case, and specialized to the G G -convergence of operators corresponding to quadratic forms. A corresponding analysis is also carried on for non-symmetric operators using the localization properties of H H -convergence. Finally, we treat the case of perforated domains with Neumann boundary condition, and their stability. Introduction

In this work we examine the stability of some classes of integrals, and in particular their homogenization. The prototypical case is that of quadratic energies

\int_{D}\Bigl(a_{0}\Bigl({x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)+\widetilde{a}\Bigl({x\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Big)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,

(1.1)

with bounded coefficients
a_{0},\widetilde{a}
such that
a_{0}
and
a=a_{0}+\widetilde{a}
are strictly positive, on a bounded subset
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Typically
a_{0}=a_{\rm per}
is a periodic coefficient, and
\widetilde{a}
is considered as a perturbation,
for which we ask the question, whether we give general conditions on
\widetilde{a}
ensuring that such a perturbation is negligible in the problems of homogenization; that is, when
\varepsilon
tends to
0
.
This issue has been recently widely studied in the framework of the corresponding elliptic operators (see the discussion in Section
2
).

Here, we state the problem as a general stability question for the
\Gamma
-convergence of integral functionals, of which quadratic energies are a particular case.
In particular, for (
1.1
) we show that a possible condition for
\widetilde{a}
to be negligible is that
|\widetilde{a}|
has zero mean in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is,

\langle|\widetilde{a}|\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}:=\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|\widetilde{a}(y)|\,dy=0.

(1.2)

This condition is not necessary (this is evident from the analysis of the case
d=1
, from which we also produce counterexamples in higher dimension), but it is handy in dealing with a number of situations where the periodicity of the functional is perturbed.

The
\Gamma
-convergence framework gives us the possibility of extending some results in the existing literature for quadratic forms, even though in a weaker form since it is not coupled with a corrector result. These extensions include for example, the treatment of energies for non-linear problems, perforated domains, and random coefficients.

The results we prove are based on the classical localization techniques of
\Gamma
-convergence for integrals
of the form

\int_{D}f(x,\nabla u)\,dx\qquad\hbox{for $u\in W^{1,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{m})$,}

with
D
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f=f(x,\xi)\colon D\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
, where
\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
denotes the space of
m\times d
matrices.
On the one hand, this allows us to obtain compactness and integral-representation results for equicoercive classes of integral functionals of
p
-growth defined on Sobolev spaces (see Theorem
3.2
) and on the other hand it allows us to describe the integrands of such functionals in terms of minimum problems on small balls (see Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
). These localization techniques, together with Meyers-type higher-integrability estimates, are used to obtain our main result (Theorem
4.1
): a closure theorem giving a sufficient condition for sequences of functionals to be close in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence. In the particular case of two families of integrands
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
(Corollary
4.2
), this result asserts that the condition

\lim_{{\rho\to 0}}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f_{k}(y,\xi)-g_{k}(y,\xi)|\,dy=0

for all
t>0
and almost all
x\in D
provides a
stability property
; namely that, up to subsequences

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{D}f_{k}(x,\nabla u)\,dx=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{D}g_{k}(x,\nabla u)\,dx.

In the case of homogenization problems, this condition is further specialized, and reads

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(y,\xi)-g(y,\xi)|\,dy=0

(1.3)

for all
t>0
, in this case ensuring that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{D}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u\Big)\,dx=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{D}g\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla u\Big)\,dx

if either of the two limits exists.

Those stability results extend to the stochastic case, for which condition (
1.3
) is now expressed as
a hypothesis involving expectations, of the form

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(\omega,y,\xi)-g(\omega,y,\xi)|\,dy\bigg)\,dP(\omega)=0

for all
t>0
, where
({\Omega},{\mathcal{T}},P)
denotes the probability space in which the stochastic problem is formulated. In this case the almost sure homogenizability of
f
, which is usually obtained by statistical invariance and ergodic assumptions, implies the homogenizability of
g
(see Section
5.2
).

When we restrict to quadratic functionals of the form

\int_{D}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx;

that is,
f(x,\xi)=\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle
,
\Gamma
-convergence results can be translated into the corresponding
G
-convergence results regarding the behaviour of elliptic problems of the form

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k})=\phi\\
u_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(D).\end{cases}

(1.4)

In this case, the condition

\lim_{{\rho\to 0}}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}|A_{k}(y)-B_{k}(y)|\,dy=0

for almost every
x

ensures that the
G
-limits of the operators corresponding to
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are the same. In the case of homogenization, where
A_{k}(x)=A(x/\varepsilon_{k})

and
B_{k}(x)=B(x/\varepsilon_{k})
, a condition providing stability is

\limsup_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|A(x)-B(x)|\,dx=0,

which reduces to (
1.2
) in the case of isotropic matrices as in (
1.1
).
Similar conditions and results can be stated also in the stochastic case.

We can also address the case of
H
-convergence; that is, convergence of solutions to problems of the form (
1.4
) for non-symmetric
A_{k}
. To this end, we adapt the localization techniques introduced for minimizers (Section
4
) to the case of solutions of elliptic equations.

The final part of the article is dedicated to problems in perforated domains with Neumann boundary conditions, a question related to the behaviour as
\varepsilon\to 0
of functionals of the form

F^{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.

The analysis there is focussed on the perturbations of the perforation set
E
that ensure stability of the corresponding
\Gamma
-limits.

2
Comparison with the PDE approach

We comment here upon the relation of the present work with some existing results in the literature of the theory of partial differential equations. In the context of elliptic equations and systems (first in divergence form and next for some other cases), homogenization theory in the presence of perturbations in an otherwise periodic structure was developed in the work
[
11
]
by X. Blanc, P.-L. Lions and the third author, and in the subsequent works
[
12
,
13
,
8
,
14
]
. In this series of works, which all follow a PDE perspective, some necessary assumptions are limiting the applicability of the results. These assumptions essentially are

(1) only
localized
perturbations are considered; that is, perturbation coefficients
\tilde{a}
in (
1.1
) that in some sense vanish at infinity (this will be made precise below);

(2) the unperturbed coefficient
a_{0}
in (
1.1
) is assumed periodic (this is related to the technique of proof, which actually relies on earlier results by M. Avellaneda and F.-H. Lin in the seminal work
[
4
]
, complemented by
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, all precisely on the periodic case);

(3) for reasons again related to the very technique of proof the (uniform in space) Hölder continuity of both the unperturbed coefficient and the perturbation coefficient (thus of their sum) needs to be assumed.

The two exceptions to (3) are, on the one hand, the Hilbertian setting, when it is assumed that
\tilde{a}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and for which the Hölder continuity of the coefficients is unnecessary because everything is taken care of by ellipticity, and, on the other hand, the case of
equations
instead of
systems
, for which the Nash-Moser regularity theory allows to establish some results again without assuming the Hölder continuity.

In order to formalize (1); that is, the locality of the perturbation, the natural assumption on the perturbation coefficients
\tilde{a}
would be, besides the above assumptions (2)-(3), that
\tilde{a}
only converges to zero at infinity; that is,
\displaystyle\tilde{a}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to+\infty
. This “natural” setting is however not tractable mathematically.

A fortiori
, the (weaker) assumption (
1.2
), although very natural, in particular because it mimics what happen for averages of functions, cannot be handled as such.
Hence, the “locality” was encoded in imposing the stronger assumption

\tilde{a}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad\textrm{for some}\quad 1\leq p<+\infty.

Using Hölder’s inequality, this assumption clearly implies condition (
1.2
) on the vanishing average.

Given these restricting assumptions, the works
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
8
]

(i) establish that the homogenized coefficient is left unchanged by the perturbation,

(ii) prove the existence of a suitable corrector function, adapted to the perturbation,

(iii) identify a specific rate of convergence for the residual (that is,
u^{\varepsilon}
minus the first two terms of the two-scale expansion), which may be made precise depending upon the functional space (Sobolev spaces and Hölder spaces) considered for measuring the residual, the ambient dimension
d
, and the exponent
p
of the Lebesgue space in the condition
\tilde{a}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
on the perturbation coefficient.

So, as compared to the results using the
\Gamma
-limit approach and developed in the present article, the works
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
8
]
use much stronger assumptions, but prove more on the homogenization limit. They also extend to some equations and systems that are not in divergence form and/or are not self-adjoint, as the work
[
14
]
shows. That said, as opposed to the approach developed in the present work, the techniques of the works
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
8
]
do not seem to allow one to establish (i) under the only assumption (
1.2
).

In a related work
[
25
]
by R. Goudey, a variant of the above “local” regime was explored.
The idea therein is easily illustrated by the particular case when the perturbation
\tilde{a}
is in fact supported in the vicinity of the set of points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that have coordinates along the canonical axes that all are integer powers of, say, 2. Intuitively, such points become more and more rare at infinity but never fully disappear.
Such a perturbation “rare at infinity” also satisfies condition (
1.2
) and so, formally, the setting of
[
25
]
is a particular instance of that considered in the present work.

A motivation for the works
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
8
]
arises from a preceding work
[
9
]
on nonperiodic geometries.
The underlying formalism for the theory was then originally introduced in a slightly different (but intrinsically related) context, that of
thermodynamic limit problems
. A suitable set of points
\displaystyle\{X_{k}\}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
, distributed over the ambient space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, is first considered. The points are not necessarily arranged in a periodic array, but are sufficiently well organized geometrically.
Prototypical functions are next constructed using translations along this set of points; that is, functions of the form

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\psi(x-X_{k})
,
for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. If some adequate geometric conditions
are imposed on the locations of the points as well as on the two-point, three-point etc, correlations, then it is possible to construct some
algebras

\mathcal{A}
of functions that have interesting averaging properties and may be used for thermodynamic limit problems.
In a subsequent work, the construction was adapted to the context of homogenization theory, see Assumptions (H1)-(H2)-(H3
′
) in
[
10
, Section 5.3]
.
Omitting some technicalities, the question of developing a homogenization theory for equations with coefficients
a
in such an algebra
\mathcal{A}
then reduces to establishing the existence of a solution
w_{p}
to the corrector equation in this algebra.
It is again interesting to note that the first geometric condition imposed in
[
9
,
10
]
ensures that the number of points within an arbitrary ball grows at most linearly with respect to the volume of the ball. Put differently, this is a condition on the
average
of the coefficient and is therefore close in spirit to condition (
1.2
) used in the present work. For a recent example of a work in this line of thought, we refer to
[
26
]
.

Finally, we mention that a
quasilinear
setting was explored in the work
[
32
]
by S. Wolf using the techniques introduced in the series of works mentioned above. The equation considered in
[
32
]
involves the
p
-Laplacian. It reads

-\,\textrm{div}\left(a\Big({x\over\varepsilon}\Big)\,|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\,\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right)\,=\,f(x),

where the coefficient
a
is again as above. The present article will show in Section
4
that this case can be treated by
\Gamma
-convergence if (
1.2
) holds.

Notation and preliminaries

The fixed dimensions of a reference and target Euclidean space, respectively, will be denoted by
d\geq 1
and
m\geq 1
. The space of
m\times d
matrices is denoted by
\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
. If
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
then
\ell_{\xi}
denotes the linear function
x\mapsto\xi x
.
If
D
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the family of all bounded open subsets of
D
will be denoted by
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}(D)
. The open ball of centre
x
and radius

is denoted by
B_{r}(x)
. We omit

when

. The notation

is used for the characteristic function of the set
U

In the following
p>1

\alpha,\beta>0

\alpha\leq\beta

D

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(D)
of all Borel functions

\alpha|\xi|^{p}\leq f(x,\xi)\leq\beta(1+|\xi|^{p})

(3.1)

for almost all
x\in D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
, with

quasiconvex for almost all

. We recall that quasiconvexity implies the local Lipschitz condition

|f(x,\xi)-f(x,\xi^{\prime})|\leq C(1+|\xi|^{p-1}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{p-1})|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|

(3.2)

for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
, with

depending only on
\alpha,\beta,d

If
f\in\mathcal{F}
, given

and
U\in\mathcal{U}
we will set

F(u,U)=\int_{U}f(x,\nabla u)\,dx.

(3.3)

Since we will consider
\Gamma
-limits of functionals of the type
F
, the assumption that
f
is quasiconvex is not restrictive, up to a relaxation argument (see
[
21
,
18
]
), since we may assume that

is weakly lower semicontinuous in

, which is equivalent to

19
,
20
]
). This will be done throughout the article. Note that (
3.1
) and (

) imply in particular the continuity of

in the strong topology of
W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
F
is the related functional, then the values of

can be recovered from minimum problems regarding

.
Indeed, the following theorem holds (see
[
22
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

and let

be the related functional; then for almost all

and for all

the limit in (

) exists and we have

f(x,\xi)=\lim_{\rho\to 0}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}\min\big\{F(v,B_{\rho}(x)):v\in\ell_{\xi}+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{\rho}(x);\mathbb{R}^{m})\big\}.

The well-known compactness properties of functionals with integrands

is stated in the following theorem

,
19
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Compactness properties of the class
\mathcal{F}
)
.

Given

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

there exists a subsequence (still denoted by

) such that for all

\Gamma

Furthermore, this class enjoys the Urysohn property; that is,
F_{k}(\cdot,U)

Remark 3.3

(Extension to unbounded domains)
.

We can extend Theorem
3.2
to
U=D
, even if

More precisely, we can show that if

The

4
A closure theorem for
\Gamma
-convergence

The following result provides a generalization and localization of the closure theorem in
[
17
]
(see also
[
19
, Section 17]
), with an independent proof obtained by using the derivation formula in Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f_{k},g^{j}_{k}
be functions in
\mathcal{F}
, and let

F_{k},G^{j}_{k}
be the corresponding integral functionals.
Assume that for almost all
x\in D
we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\ \limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f_{k}(y,\xi)-g^{j}_{k}(y,\xi)|\,dy=0

(4.1)

for all
t>0
.
If for every
j
the sequence
G^{j}_{k}(\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
as
k\to+\infty
for every
U\in\mathcal{U}

and
g^{j}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
is the corresponding integrand, then there exists the pointwise limit

g_{\infty}(x,\xi)
of
g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\xi)
for almost every
x\in D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
, and

F_{k}(\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
G_{\infty}(\cdot,U)

for all such
U
, where
G_{\infty}
is the integral functional corresponding to
g_{\infty}
.

Proof.

For every
j
, we assume that
G^{j}_{k}(\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
as
k\to+\infty
, and, by the compactness Theorem
3.2
we may also assume that
F_{k}(\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
F_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
. Let
g^{j}_{\infty},f_{\infty}
be the integrands of
G^{j}_{\infty}
and
F_{\infty}
, respectively. By the Urysohn property of

-convergence
[

to conclude the proof it suffices to show that
there exists the limit of

as

for almost all

.

(4.2)

For every
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}
,
\rho>0
,

, and

, we set

m^{\Phi}(x,\rho,\xi)=\min\big\{\Phi(v,B_{\rho}(x)):v\in\ell_{\xi}+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{\rho}(x))\big\}.

We fix

) holds for

and

for all

, and for
F_{k}
and

. We wish to estimate
m^{F_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})-m^{G^{j}_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})
.

With fixed

and

so that we have the estimate

m^{F_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})\leq m^{G^{j}_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})+\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\nabla u)\,dy.

(4.4)

For all

By Theorem
3.5
there exist

and

, depending only on
\alpha

) holds, so that, by Hölder’s inequality

\int_{E_{t}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dy\leq|E_{t}|^{\eta\over p+\eta}\Big(\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}|\nabla u|^{p+\eta}\,dy\Big)^{p\over p+\eta}\leq C_{1}|B_{\rho}|t^{-{\eta p\over p+\eta}}

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\nabla u)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\nabla u)\,dy+\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\xi)\,dy

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}2\beta(1+|\nabla u|^{p})\,dy+\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\xi)\,dy

\displaystyle C_{2}|B_{\rho}|\Big({1\over t^{p}}+{1\over t^{{\eta p\over p+\eta}}}\Big)+\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\xi)\,dy\

for some

Letting first

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\nabla u)\,dy\leq C_{2}\Big({1\over t^{p}}+{1\over t^{{\eta p\over p+\eta}}}\Big)\ \hskip 91.04872pt\

\displaystyle+\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\xi)\,dy.

Using (

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\to 0\\
\rho\in E\end{subarray}}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\psi^{j}_{k}(y,\nabla u)\,dy\leq C_{2}\Big({1\over t^{p}}+{1\over t^{{\eta p\over p+\eta}}}\Big).

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}m^{F_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\rho}|}m^{G^{j}_{k}}(x_{0},\rho,\xi_{0})+C_{2}\Big({1\over t^{p}}+{1\over t^{{\eta p\over p+\eta}}}\Big).

Hence, after letting

) (and the analog with

As a particular case of Theorem

Corollary 4.2
.

Let

be two sequences of functions in
\mathcal{F}
, and let

be the corresponding integral functionals.
Assume that

for every

.
Assume also that for almost all

Then

\Gamma

for all

.

We give a result of independent interest that is a consequence of Theorem

.
It provides a new proof of a known property of
\Gamma
-convergence of sequences
of functionals with pointwise converging integrands.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f_{k}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}
and
f_{\infty}\in\mathcal{F}
, and let
F_{k}
and
F_{\infty}
be the corresponding functionals. Assume that we have

\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(x,\xi)=f_{\infty}(x,\xi)\hbox{ for almost every $x\in D$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}$.}

(4.12)

Then
F_{k}(\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
F_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

For every
j
we define
h_{j}
as the convex envelope of

\begin{cases}0&\hbox{ if $|\xi|\leq j$}\cr\beta(1+|\xi|^{p})&\hbox{ if $|\xi|>j$.}\end{cases}

Note that there exists a constant
R_{j}>j
such that
h_{j}(\xi)=\beta(1+|\xi|^{p})
if
|\xi|\geq R_{j}
.
We define
g^{j}_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
as

g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)=\max\{f_{k}(x,\xi),h_{j}(\xi)\}
,
which converges pointwise to
g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\xi):=\max\{f_{\infty}(x,\xi),h_{j}(\xi)\}
as
k\to+\infty
. Note that
g^{j}_{\infty}
converge pointwise to
f_{\infty}
; hence, the corresponding integrals satisfy

\lim_{j\to+\infty}G^{j}_{\infty}(u,U)=F_{\infty}(u,U)

(4.13)

for every
u\in W^{1,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

By the equi-Lipschitz condition (
3.2
) satisfied by functions in
\mathcal{F}
we have

g^{j}_{k}(x,\cdot)\to g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\cdot)\hbox{ uniformly in }\overline{B_{R_{j}}}

(4.14)

for almast all
x\in D
.
Let
\eta^{j}_{k}
be the non-negative functions defined by

\eta^{j}_{k}(z)=\sup_{|\xi\leq R_{j}}|g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)-g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\xi)|.

Such functions satisfy
\eta^{j}_{k}(x)\leq 2\beta(1+|R_{j}|^{p})
, converge to
0
for almost every

and we have

g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)\geq g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\xi)-\eta^{j}_{k}(x)\hbox{ for almost every }x\in D\hbox{ and }|\xi|\leq R_{j}.

(4.15)

Since
g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)=\beta(1+|\xi|^{p})=g^{j}_{\infty}(x,\xi)
for

we conclude that (
4.15
) holds for all

. Hence the corresponding integrals satisfy

G^{j}_{k}(u,U)\geq G^{j}_{\infty}(u,U)-\int_{U}\eta^{j}_{k}\,dx

(4.16)

for every
u\in W^{1,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Since
G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
is lower semicontinuous, this implies that
\Gamma
-
\liminf_{k\to+\infty}G^{j}_{k}(\cdot,U)\geq G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
. Since the pointwise limit of

is
G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
as

, we have the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality, and we obtain the
\Gamma
-convergence of

to
G^{j}_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
.

Trivially,
|f_{k}(x,\xi)-g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)|=0
if
|\xi|\leq j
, so that (
4.1
) holds. By Theorem
4.1
and (

F_{k}

\Gamma
-converge to
F_{\infty}
. ∎

5
Homogenization problems

In this section we consider homogenization problems, when the integrands
f_{k}
can be obtained by scaling a single integrand defined on the whole space.

5.1
Deterministic homogenization

We suppose that
there exists
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\varepsilon_{k}
, a positive sequence tending to
0
, such that

f_{k}(x,\xi)=f\Big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}},\xi\Big).

(5.1)

In this case
f_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
and the functionals
F_{k}
take the form

F_{k}(u,U)=\int_{U}f\Big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}},\nabla u\Big)\,dx.

(5.2)

We are interested in the case when
F_{k}(\cdot,U)
has a
\Gamma
-limit
F_{\infty}(\cdot,U)
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
and the corresponding integrand
f_{\infty}
does not depend on
x
nor on the sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
. We then say that
f
is
homogenizable
, and
F_{\infty}
and
f_{\infty}
are denoted by
F_{\rm hom}
and
f_{\rm hom}
, respectively.

Theorem
3.4
provides a characterization of homogenizability thanks to (
3.8
).
Indeed, by a change of variables
f
is homogenizable if and only if for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}{1\over|B_{\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{k}}}|}\min\bigg\{\int_{B_{\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{k}}}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}})}f(y,\nabla v)\,dy:v\in\ell_{\xi}+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{k}}}(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}});\mathbb{R}^{m})\bigg\}

(5.3)

exists for all
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
and is independent of
x
and of the sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
. This holds in the particular case when
f
is periodic in the first variable
[
21
,
19
]
.

We now consider a sequence of functions
g^{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the corresponding

g^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)=g^{j}\Big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}},\xi\Big).

(5.4)

The related functions
\psi^{j}_{k}
have the form
\psi^{j}_{k}(x,\xi)=\psi^{j}({x\over\varepsilon_{k}},\xi)
,
where

\psi^{j}(y,\xi)=|f(y,\xi)-g^{j}(y,\xi)|,

(5.5)

and by a change of variables hypothesis (
4.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{\rho\to 0}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{\varepsilon_{k}^{d}\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho/\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{0}/\varepsilon_{k})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}(y,\xi)\,dy=0

(5.6)

for almost all
x_{0}
. This hypothesis is satisfied if

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi^{j}(y,\xi)\,dy=0

(5.7)

for all
t>0
. This observation proves the following result, which can also be found in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(Homogenizability by approximation)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
g^{j}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that for all
t>0

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\limsup_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(y,\xi)-g^{j}(y,\xi)|\,dy=0,

(5.8)

and that each
g^{j}
is homogenizable. Then
f
is homogenizable and

f_{\rm hom}(\xi)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}g^{j}_{\rm hom}(\xi)

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
.

In the particular case
g^{j}=g
we have the following stability result as a corollary.

Corollary 5.2

(Stability of homogenizability)
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
for all
t>0

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(y,\xi)-g(y,\xi)|\,dy=0.

(5.9)

Then
f
is homogenizable if and only if
g
is homogenizable, and in this case
g_{\rm hom}=f_{\rm hom}
.

The dependence of
g^{j}
on
j
in Theorem
5.2
is justified by some homogenization results for weakly almost-periodic functionals, which are obtained by approximation, as in the following example.

Example 5.3

(Almost-periodic homogenization)
.

Theorem 4.1 can be used to obtain a homogenization theorem for quadratic forms
f(y,\xi)=a(y)|\xi|^{2}
with
a
almost periodic in the mean; that is, such that there exist trigonometric polynomials
P_{j}
satisfying

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|P_{j}(y)-a(y)|\,dy=0.

(5.10)

Then we can define
g^{j}(y,\xi)=((P_{j}(y)\vee\alpha)\wedge\beta)|\xi|^{2}
, whose coefficient is uniformly almost-periodic and hence homogenizable (see e.g.
[
28
,
16
]
). By construction
g^{j}
satisfy (
5.8
), so that we conclude the homogenizability of
f
.

More in general, Theorem 4.1 can be used to obtain a homogenization theorem for
functions
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the property that
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
there exists a sequence of trigonometric polynomials
P^{\xi}_{j}
such that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|P^{\xi}_{j}(y)-f(y,\xi)|\,dy=0.

(5.11)

Indeed, if such a condition holds we can construct a family
g^{j}
such that (
5.8
) holds and
g^{j}(\cdot,\xi)
is almost periodic uniformly with respect to
\xi
(that is, with almost periods that are independent of
\xi
) (see
[
17
]
), and hence homogenizable (see
[
16
]
). For details on homogenization of uniformly almost-periodic functions and the construction of
g^{j}
we refer to
[
19
, Chapters 15 and 17]
, respectively.

5.2
Stochastic homogenization

Let
({\Omega},\mathcal{T},P)
be a probability space; let
\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
be the collection of random integrands
f=f(\omega,x,\xi):\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)
f
is
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{B}(M^{m\times d})
-measurable;

(ii) for all
\omega\in\Omega
we have
f(\omega,\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We suppose that
{\Omega}
is equipped with a group of
P
-preserving transformations
\tau_{z}:{\Omega}\to{\Omega}
labelled by
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
An integrand
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
is said to be
stochastically periodic
if

f(\omega,x+z,\xi)=f(\tau_{z}(\omega),x,\xi)\hbox{ for all }(\omega,z,x,\xi)\in{\Omega}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}.

The group
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is said to be
ergodic
if every set
E\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\tau_{z}(E)=E
for every
z
satisfies
P(E)=0
or
P(E)=1
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
then we use the notation
f_{\varepsilon}=f(\omega,x/\varepsilon,\xi)
and
F_{\varepsilon}(\omega,u,U)
for the corresponding functional.
The following stochastic homogenization results has been proved in
[
23
]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

If
f
is stochastically periodic then for almost all
\omega
there exists
f_{\rm hom}\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
, with
f_{\rm hom}=f_{\rm hom}(\omega,\xi)
independent of
x
, such that
F_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converge to
F_{\rm hom}(\omega,\cdot,U)
corresponding to
f_{\rm hom}
for every
U
. Furthermore, if
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is also ergodic, then
f_{\rm hom}=f_{\rm hom}(\xi)
is
P
-almost everywhere independent of
\omega
.

A first result for stochastic integrands is the following theorem, which can be obtained from Theorem
5.4
by applying Corollary
5.2
to
P
-almost all
\omega\in{\Omega}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
. Assume that
f
is stochastically periodic, and assume that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(\omega,y,\xi)-g(\omega,y,\xi)|\,dy=0

(5.12)

for all
t>0
and for
P
-almost all
\omega\in{\Omega}
. If
G_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot,U)
is the functional with integrand
g_{\varepsilon}=g(\omega,x/\varepsilon,\xi)
, then

G_{\varepsilon}(\omega,\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converge to
F_{\rm hom}(\omega,\cdot,U)
given by Theorem
5.4
for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
P
-almost all
\omega\in{\Omega}
.

The pointwise condition (
5.12
) can be replaced by the convergence to zero of the corresponding expectations. In this case the result is much weaker, since the set of probability zero that is excluded may depend on the sequences
\varepsilon_{k}
and
\varepsilon_{k_{j}}
we are considering.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm stoc}
. Assume that
f
is stochastically periodic, and assume that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{B_{R}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f(\omega,y,\xi)-g(\omega,y,\xi)|\,dy\bigg)\,dP(\omega)=0

(5.13)

for all
t>0
. Then for every sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0^{+}
there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{k_{j}}
such that
G_{\varepsilon_{k_{j}}}(\omega,\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converges to
F_{\rm hom}(\omega,\cdot,U)
for
P
-almost all
\omega\in{\Omega}
and for every
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

We fix a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0^{+}
.
In analogy with the notation in the previous sections, we set

\psi(\omega,y,\xi):=|f(\omega,y,\xi)-g(\omega,y,\xi)|.

With fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
R_{k}=n/\varepsilon_{k}
, and observe that (
5.13
) implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}{1\over R_{k}^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{B_{R_{k}}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi(\omega,y,\xi)\,dy\bigg)\,dP(\omega)=0.

(5.14)

By the change of variable
y=\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}}
we then also have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}{1\over n^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\int_{B_{n}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi\Big(\omega,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},\xi\Big)\,dx\bigg)\,dP(\omega)=0.

(5.15)

Hence, there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{k_{j}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{B_{n}}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi\Big(\omega,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k_{j}}},\xi\Big)\,dx=0

(5.16)

for
P
-almost every
\omega\in{\Omega}
.
By a diagonal argument we can suppose that the subsequence
\varepsilon_{k_{j}}
does not depend on
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We now fix
\omega\in{\Omega}
such that (
5.16
) holds for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
\rho>0
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset B_{n}
. By
(
5.16
) this implies that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}\psi\Big(\omega,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k_{j}}},\xi\Big)\,dx=0.

By Corollary
4.2
we then deduce that
G_{\varepsilon_{k_{j}}}(\omega,\cdot,U)

\Gamma
-converge to
F_{\rm hom}(\omega,\cdot,U)
.
∎

6
G
-convergence

The results of the previous sections can be translated into results about the
G
-convergence of elliptic operators according to the following definition. A more direct approach, but based on the same ideas, will be described in the next section. For simplicity of exposition in this and the next sections we treat only problems for scalar-valued functions, but all results remain valid, with obvious modifications, for vector-valued
u
.

We assume that
D
is an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
{\mathbb{M}}_{\rm sym}^{d\times d}
denotes the space of symmetric
d\times d
matrices.
Given
\alpha,\beta>0
with
\alpha\leq\beta
, we let
\mathcal{M}^{\rm sym}=\mathcal{M}^{\rm sym}_{\alpha,\beta}(D)
denote the family of all measurable
A\colon D\to{\mathbb{M}}_{\rm sym}^{d\times d}
, such that

\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\beta|\xi|^{2}\qquad\hbox{ for almost all $x\in D$ and all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.}

(6.1)

Definition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
A_{k}
be a sequence in

Non-symmetric elliptic operators fall in the range of application of the stability results by

In this section we follow a more direct approach, which avoids the use of

Although we cannot exploit the results of Sections

Since we may also consider non-symmetric matrices, for
\alpha,\beta>0

\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\ \hbox{ and }\quad{|\xi|^{2}\leq\beta\langle A^{-1}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle}\ \hbox{ for almost all $x\in D$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.}

(7.1)

Definition 7.1
.

Let
D
be a bounded open set,

be a sequence in

. We say that

if for all

in the sense of distribution of

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k})=\phi\\

converges weakly in

to the solution

of

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A\nabla u)=\phi\\

and we also have

A_{k}\nabla u_{k}\rightharpoonup A\nabla u\hbox{ weakly in }L^{2}(D;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 7.2

(Localization of

-convergence)
.

Let

H-converge to

.
It is proven in

that if

is an open subset of

in the sense of distribution of

converges weakly in

to the solution

of

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A\nabla u)=\phi\\

and we also have

.

The corresponding result to Theorem
3.1
is the following derivation formula, where now

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{M}
, and let

be a Lebesgue point of

, and let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For every
\rho>0
consider the problem

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{\rho})=0\\
u_{\rho}-\ell_{\xi}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{\rho}(x_{0})).\end{cases}

(7.2)

Then we have

A(x_{0})\xi=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}A\nabla u_{\rho}\,dx.

(7.3)

Proof.

For notational simplicity, we can assume that
x_{0}=0
. For

we define
A_{\rho}(y)=A(\rho y)
, and

,
so that equation (

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A_{\rho}\nabla v_{\rho})=0\\

(7.4)

Since

is a Lebesque point for

, we have

in

which coincides with

Proof.

By the compactness of

Let

respectively. Moreover, we also consider the solutions

and

to

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A\nabla u^{\rho})=0\\

(7.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-B_{k}\nabla v_{k}^{\rho})\,dy=\int_{B_{\rho}}(A\nabla u^{\rho}-B\nabla v^{\rho})\,dy.

In order to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-B_{k}\nabla v_{k}^{\rho})\,dy=0.

(7.10)

To that end, we write

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}(A_{k}\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-B_{k}\nabla v_{k}^{\rho})\,dy\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}((A_{k}-B_{k})\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}+B_{k}(\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}))\,dy\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}||\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}|\,dy+\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|B_{k}||\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|B_{k}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}|^{2}\,dy=0\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|B_{k}|^{2}\,dy\leq\beta^{2}

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(A_{k}(\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}))={\rm div}((A_{k}-B_{k})\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}))\\

by (

Multiplying the equation in (

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\frac{\alpha}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{2}\,dy\leq\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}||\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}||\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}|^{p}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{q}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla u_{k}^{\rho}-\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{2}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|A_{k}-B_{k}|^{p}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{q}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In order to show that this last right-hand side tends to

z^{\rho}_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\rho}v_{k}^{\rho}(\rho x)
and matrices
B^{\rho}_{k}(x)=B_{k}(\rho x)
, and note that they satisfy

\begin{cases}-{\rm div}(B^{\rho}_{k}\nabla z^{\rho}_{k})=0\\
z^{\rho}_{k}-\ell_{\xi}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}).\end{cases}

(7.13)

By the Meyers-Elcrat higher-integrability theorem
[
30
, Theorem 2]

(see also
[
24
]
), there exists
q>2
, and
C>0
, independent of
\rho
and
k
, such that

\frac{1}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla v_{k}^{\rho}|^{q}\,dy=\frac{1}{|B_{1}|}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla z_{k}^{\rho}|^{q}\,dy\leq C,

which proves the claim.
∎

Let
A\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
\varepsilon_{k}
be a positive sequence tending to
0
, and let

A_{k}(x)=A\Big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}}\Big).

(7.14)

We say that
A
is
homogenizable
if
A_{k}
H-converges to some
A_{\rm hom}
independent of
x
and of the sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
.

Corollary 7.5
.

If

and (

) holds, then

is homogenizable if and only if

is homogenizable, and in this case

.

Proof.

It suffices to note that (
6.8
) guarantees the validity of (
7.6
) thanks to the change of variables as in (
5.6
) and (
5.7
).
∎

Finally, we note that the same remarks as for

-convergence at the end of Section

also hold for the stochastic homogenization by

-convergence.

8
Perforated domains with Neumann boundary conditions

We now show how we can derive a stability result for the homogenization of minimum problems in perforated domains. We only treat the quadratic case in order to concentrate on the role of the perforations.

8.1
General assumptions

We consider closed sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that there exists an extension operator
T:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the following properties

Tu(x)=u(x)\hbox{ for almost all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E,

(8.1)

\|\nabla Tu\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E)}

(8.2)

for some constant
C>0
. Note that (
8.2
) implies the connectedness of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E
.
In the case of bounded domains
D
, the definition of extension operator is more technically complex since it must take into account that
\partial D
may disconnect the set
E
; we refer to
[
1
]
for details.

Properties (
8.1
) and (
8.2
) are satisfied if
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E
is connected and with Lipschitz boundary
[
1
]
. A non-periodic example is given by sets
E=\sum_{i}E_{i}
such that
E_{i}\subset B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
with
B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\cap B_{r_{j}}(x_{j})=\emptyset
and such that there exists a constant
C>0
and extension operators
T_{i}:H^{1}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\setminus E_{i})\to H^{1}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))
such that
T_{i}u(x)=u(x)
for almost all
x\in B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\setminus E_{i}
and

\|\nabla T_{i}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i}))}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\setminus E_{i})}
.
The simplest situation is when
E_{i}
is a smaller ball concentric with
B_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
.
To check this it suffices to construct an extension operator when
E
is the ball of radius
2
and centre
0
. We then define

T(u)(x)=\begin{cases}\overline{u}&\hbox{ if }|x|<1\\
(|x|-1)u(L(x))+(2-|x|)\overline{u}&\hbox{ if }1\leq|x|<2\\
u(x)&\hbox{ if }|x|>2,\end{cases}

where
L(x)=(4-|x|){x\over|x|}
for
1<|x|<2
and

\overline{u}={1\over|B_{3}\setminus B_{2}|}\int_{B_{3}\setminus B_{2}}u(x)\,dx.

Then

\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla Tu|^{2}\,dx\leq C^{\prime}\int_{B_{3}\setminus B_{2}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u-\overline{u}|^{2})\,dx,

and (
8.2
) follows by the Poincaré inequality. Since the constant
C
in (
8.2
) for
E=B_{2}
is invariant by homothety, we can define an extension operator separately for each ball maintaining the same constant.

Note that if
E
satisfies (
8.1
) and (
8.2
), then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists an extension operator
T_{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon E
satisfying (
8.1
) and (
8.2
) with the same constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

In order to avoid trivial limits we suppose that

\liminf_{R\to+\infty}{|B_{R}\setminus E|\over R^{d}}>0.

(8.3)

This condition implies that the weak limits
\psi
of characteristic functions
1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}
satisfy
\psi>0
almost everywhere.

8.2
Stability for perforated domains

We consider functionals

F^{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(8.4)

defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Our goal is to compare the asymptotic behaviour of such functionals for different
E
in the spirit of the stability results. The corresponding
\Gamma
-limits will be computed in the space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with the
L^{2}_{\rm loc}
-topology. In the following section we will examine the relation between
\Gamma
-convergence and the solutions to some minimum problems on perforated domains.

Theorem 8.1

(Perturbations of perforated domains)
.

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be two closed sets satisfying the extension properties
(
8.1
) and (
8.2
), and such that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}|(E\triangle E^{\prime})\cap B_{R}|=0,

(8.5)

where
E\triangle E^{\prime}:=(E\setminus E^{\prime})\cup(E^{\prime}\setminus E)
denotes the symmetric difference between the sets.
Suppose that
E
satisfies (
8.3
) and that

\Gamma
-limit of
F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}
as
k\to+\infty
exists for some
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
.
Then there exists the
\Gamma
-limit of
F^{E^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}
as
k\to+\infty
, and the two
\Gamma
-limits are equal.

Remark 8.2
.

An example in which (
8.5
) is satisfied is when
E
is a
1
-periodic set and
E^{\prime}=\Phi(E)
, where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a diffeomorphism with

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}|\Phi(x)-x|=0.

(8.6)

This is a consequence of the fact that
|(E\triangle\Phi(E))\cap(k+[0,1]^{d})|\to 0
as
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
tends to
\infty
,
which is obtained by the periodicity of
E
and the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Another example in which (
8.5
) is satisfied trivially is by taking
E=\emptyset
and
E^{\prime}
a set “vanishing at infinity”; that is, such that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}|E^{\prime}\cap B_{R}|=0.

(8.7)

In order to refer to the setting of the previous sections, we introduce some coefficients as follows.
We first set

a^{E}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E\cr 0&\hbox{ if }x\in E\,,\end{cases}

(8.8)

so that we have

F^{E}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a^{E}\Big({x\over\varepsilon}\Big)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(8.9)

on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

a^{E,n}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\hbox{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E\cr\frac{1}{n}&\hbox{ if }x\in E\end{cases}

(8.10)

and

F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a^{E,n}\Big({x\over\varepsilon}\Big)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(8.11)

on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We introduce analogous coefficients and functionals with
E^{\prime}
in the place of
E
.

Note that the integrands of (
8.11
) belong to the class
\mathcal{F}
with
p=2
,
\alpha=\frac{1}{n}
, and
\beta=1
, while the integrands of (
8.9
) do not satisfy a growth condition from below on the perforation.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
E
be a set satisfying extension properties
(
8.1
) and (
8.2
) and density property (
8.3
). Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}

(8.12)

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,\eta}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}},

(8.13)

where
C
is the constant in (
8.2
).

Proof.

We only prove (
8.12
), the proof of (
8.13
) being analogous. The first inequality is trivial; to prove the second inequality we fix
u
and a sequence
u_{k}
converging to
u
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u)=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k}).

We set
v_{k}=T_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k}|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon_{k}E})
and note that, for each smooth connected
U\in\mathcal{U}
, up to subsequences, we can suppose that
v_{k}
converges to some
v
in
L^{2}(U)
. This follows from the bound on
\nabla v_{k}
, which implies pre-compactness up to a sequence of additive constants, and the fact that
v_{k}=u_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon_{k}E
, which implies that this sequence of constants is bounded, and hence pre-compactness without addition of a constant. Note that from
0=(u_{k}-v_{k})1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon_{k}E}
and the convergence of
u_{k}
to
u
and
v_{k}
to
v
we deduce that
0=(u-v)\psi
on each such
U
, where
\psi
is a weak
∗
limit in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon_{k}E}
.
Since
\psi>0
almost everywhere by (
8.3
) we deduce that
u=v
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k})=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(v_{k})+{1\over n}\int_{\varepsilon_{k}E}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k})+{1\over n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k})+{1\over n}C^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\,\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u),

which proves the claim.
∎

Note that the argument in the first part of the proof of the lemma shows that if (
8.3
) holds and we have two such extension operators
T_{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{T}_{\varepsilon}
, if we have the strong convergence of
T_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
to
u
and
\widetilde{T}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
to
\widetilde{u}
in
L^{1}(B_{R})
for all
R
, then we have

\displaystyle\psi u=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}1_{B_{R}\setminus\varepsilon E}T_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}1_{B_{R}\setminus\varepsilon E}u_{\varepsilon}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}1_{B_{R}\setminus\varepsilon E}\widetilde{T}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=\psi\widetilde{u}\quad\hbox{ in $B_{R}$,}

which implies
\widetilde{u}=u
almost everywhere since
\psi>0
and
R
is arbitrary.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

With fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

f^{n}_{k}(x,\xi)=a^{E,n}\big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}}\big)|\xi|^{2}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad g^{n}_{k}(y,\xi)=a^{E^{\prime},n}\big({x\over\varepsilon_{k}}\big)|\xi|^{2}.

We check that
f_{k}=f^{n}_{k}
and
g_{k}=f^{n}_{k}
satisfy (
4.11
) in Corollary
4.2
.
Since
B_{\rho/\varepsilon_{k}}(x/\varepsilon_{k})\subset B_{(|x|+\rho)/\varepsilon_{k}}
, we deduce that for each fixed

we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\sup_{|\xi|\leq t}|f_{k}(y,\xi)-g_{k}(y,\xi)|\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{1\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\Big|a^{E,n}({y\over\varepsilon_{k}})-a^{E^{\prime},n}({y\over\varepsilon_{k}})\Big|\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}{\varepsilon_{k}^{d}\over\rho^{d}}\int_{B_{\rho\over\varepsilon_{k}}({x\over\varepsilon_{k}})}|a^{E,\eta}(y)-a^{E^{\prime},\eta}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{2}{(|x|+\rho)^{d}\over\rho^{d}}\lim_{R\to+\infty}{1\over R^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|a^{E,\eta}(y)-a^{E^{\prime},\eta}(y)|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{2}{(|x|+\rho)^{d}\over\rho^{d}}\Big(1-{1\over n}\Big)\lim_{R\to+\infty}{|(E\triangle E^{\prime})\cap B_{R}|\over R^{d}}=0

by (
8.5
),
and (
4.11
) is satisfied.
Thanks to Theorem
3.2
the convergence hypothesis of Corollary
4.2
is satisfied up to passing to a subsequence. Hence, the integrands corresponding to the
\Gamma
-limits of the localized functionals
F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\cdot,U)
and
F^{E^{\prime},n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\cdot,U)
coincide.
Thanks to Remark
3.3
this ensures the existence and equality of the corresponding
\Gamma
-limits on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
; that is, in the notation (
8.11
), that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime},n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}.

By the existence of the limit for
E
and Lemma
8.3
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}

(8.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}.

The same argument applied to

gives

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime},n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}

(8.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{C^{2}}{n}\Big)\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime},n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}.

We now set

F_{0}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\big(\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E,n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\big(\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime},n}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big),

which exists since the functionals are decreasing in

. Letting

in (

) and (

) we then have the existence of the limits, and equality

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}F^{E^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=F_{0}.

Since the limit is independent of the subsequence used in the application of the compactness theorem, by the Urysohn property of
\Gamma
-convergence (see
[
21
]
) we conclude that the equality holds for the original sequence, and the claim.
∎

8.3
Convergence of solutions of minimum problems

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a closed set satisfying (
8.1
), (
8.2
) and such that there exists
c_{0}
for which

\liminf_{R\to+\infty}{|B_{R}\setminus E|\over R^{d}}\geq c_{0}>0,

(8.16)

which is a slightly stronger version of
(
8.3
). We suppose that the
\Gamma
-limit
F_{\rm hom}^{E}
of
F^{E}_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
exists.
In particular this holds if
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E
is a connected Lipschitz domain.

We fix
\lambda>0
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and we study the convergence of solutions of minimum problems for energies of the form

F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}(|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda u^{2}-2fu)\,dx

(8.17)

defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E)
.

Proposition 8.4

(Convergence of minimum problems)
.

Let
E
satisfy the conditions above, and let
T
and
T_{\varepsilon}
be the corresponding extension operators. Assume that there exists the weak limit
\psi
of the characteristic functions
1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the minimizer of
F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}
in (
8.17
). Then
T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
converge weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the minimizer
u
of

F^{\lambda}_{\rm hom}(u)=F_{\rm hom}^{E}(u)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\lambda u^{2}-2fu)\psi\,dx.

(8.18)

Theorem
8.1
will guarantee the stability of this convergence with respect to perturbations satisfying (
8.5
). Indeed, if we consider another set
E^{\prime}
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
8.1
, (
8.5
) guarantees that
\psi
is the same for
E
and
E^{\prime}
.

We precede the proof of Proposition
8.4
by the representation of
F^{\lambda}_{\rm hom}
as in the following lemma.

Lemma 8.5
.

There exists a measurable symmetric matrix
A_{\rm hom}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{M}^{d\times d}
satisfying the boundedness and ellipticity conditions

\frac{1}{C}|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq|\xi|^{2}\quad\hbox{for almost all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

(8.19)

where
C
is the constant in (
8.2
),
such that the
\Gamma
-limit of (
8.4
) takes the form

F_{\rm hom}^{E}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx

(8.20)

for every
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The representation of (
8.20
) and the boundedness inequality in (
8.19
) can be proved as in

[
21
, Theorem 22.1]
. In order to prove the ellipticity condition in (
8.19
), we first show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx\geq{1\over C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx

(8.21)

for
u
with compact support.
By the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence there exists a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
converging to
u
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx.

By a cut-off argument, multiplying by a function that is
1
on the support of
u
, we can also assume that the functions
u_{\varepsilon}
have support contained in a common compact set
K
, so that they strongly converge in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
R
be large enough so that we have
|(B_{R}\setminus K)\setminus\varepsilon E|\geq c>0
for all
\varepsilon
small enough by (
8.16
). Since
T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
on
(B_{R}\setminus K)\setminus\varepsilon E
we can use Poincaré’s inequality on
B_{R}\setminus K
and, arguing as in the first part of the proof of Lemma
8.3
, deduce that
T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
converges to
0
in
L^{2}(B_{R}\setminus K)
.
Let now
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\varphi=1
on
K
and support in
\overline{B}_{R}
and define
v_{\varepsilon}=\varphi T_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
. Note that
v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and by (

)

\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx.

Applying this last inequality to functions of the form
u(x)=w\big(\frac{x-x_{0}}{\eta}\big)
and letting
\eta\to 0
we deduce that

\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x_{0})\nabla w,\nabla w\rangle\,dx.

for almost all
x_{0}
and for all
w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. A use of
Parseval’s identity then implies that

\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{w}|^{2}d\xi\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x_{0})\xi,\xi\rangle|\widehat{w}|^{2}d\xi.

and concludes the proof by the arbitrariness of
w
.
∎

If
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus E
is a connected Lipschitz
1
-periodic set, then by
[
1
]

A_{\rm hom}
is a constant matrix characterized by

\langle A_{\rm hom}\xi,\xi\rangle=\min\Big\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}\setminus E}|\xi+\nabla u|^{2}\,dx:u\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hbox{ $1$-periodic}\Big\}.

Proof of Proposition
8.4
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of minimizers for
F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}
, whose extensions
T_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
converge, up to subsequences, to some
\overline{u}
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}\Big(\sqrt{\lambda}u_{\varepsilon}-{1\over\sqrt{\lambda}}f\Big)^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\varepsilon E}{f^{2}\over\lambda}\,dx\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla\overline{u},\nabla\overline{u}\rangle\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\Big(\sqrt{\lambda}\overline{u}-{1\over\sqrt{\lambda}}f\Big)^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi{f^{2}\over\lambda}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla\overline{u},\nabla\overline{u}\rangle\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\big(\lambda\overline{u}^{2}-2fu\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle A_{\rm hom}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\big(\lambda u^{2}-2fu\big)\,dx:u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big\}

Potentials for solenoidal fields using the three-dimensional φ − \varphi- harmonic cyclic algebra
Homero G. Díaz-Marín, Elifalet López-González, Osvaldo Osuna

Abstract




Abstract.

Given a PDE in
[
10
]
it is proposed a method for constructing solutions by considering an associative real algebra
\mathbb{A}
, and a suitable affine vector field

\varphi
with respect to which the components of all the
functions
\mathcal{L}\circ\varphi
are solutions, where
\mathcal{L}
is differentiable in the sense of Lorch with respect to

\mathbb{A}
. When we consider the 3D cyclic algebra and a suitable 3D affine map
\varphi
we get families of solutions for the Laplace equation with three independent variables.

Keyword
:
Functions of hypercomplex
variables, Laplace operator, Solenoidal vector fields, Calculus
over algebras, Differentiation theory.

Introduction

Harmonic vector fields
\mathbf{V}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfy that their components are harmonic, i.e.

\Delta\,\mathbf{V}=0.

(1)

where the components
V^{i},i=1,2,3,
satisfy Laplace’s equation. In 2D and 3D Laplace’s equations are respectively given
by

u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0,\qquad u_{xx}+u_{yy}+u_{zz}=0.

(2)

A special class of harmonic vector fields are lamellar or solenoidal vector fields, i.e. those that are incompressible and irrotational,

\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{V}=0,\qquad\mathrm{curl}\,\mathbf{V}=0,

(3)

We recall that the component of complex analytic functions are harmonic
functions in 2D, solving on a simply connected region, each harmonic function is
the real part of a complex analytic function.

1
Pre-twisted real three dimensional algebras

1.1
Commutative algebras with identityy

We recall that a
\mathbb{R}
-linear space
\mathbb{L}

is a
commutative algebra with identityy
if it is endowed with a bilinear product

\mathbb{L}\times\mathbb{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{L}
denoted by

(u,v)\mapsto u\centerdot v
, which is associative and commutative,
u\centerdot(v\centerdot w)=(u\centerdot v)\centerdot w
and
u\centerdot v=v\centerdot u
for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{L}
; furthermore,
there exists an identity
e\in\mathbb{L},
which satisfies
e\centerdot u=u
for
all
u\in\mathbb{L}
. An element
u\in\mathbb{L}
is called

regular
if there exists
u^{-1}\in\mathbb{L}
called

the inverse
of
u
such that
u^{-1}\centerdot u=e
. We also use the
notation
e/u
for
u^{-1}
, where
e
is the identity of
\mathbb{L}
.
If
u\in\mathbb{L}
is not regular, then
u
is called

singular
.
\mathbb{L}^{*}
denotes the set of all the regular
elements of
\mathbb{L}
. If
u,v\in\mathbb{L}
and
v
is regular,
the quotient
u/v
means
u\centerdot v^{-1}
.

It will be denoted by
\mathbb{A}
if
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and by
\mathbb{M}
if
\mathbb{L}
is a three dimensional
matrix algebra in the space of matrices
M(3,\mathbb{R})
where the
algebra product corresponds to the matrix product. We say that two
matrix algebras
\mathbb{M}_{1}
and
\mathbb{M}_{2}
are

conjugated
if there exists an invertible matrix
T
such
that
\mathbb{M}_{1}=T\mathbb{M}_{2}T^{-1}
.

The
\mathbb{A}
product between the elements of the
canonical basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

e_{i}\centerdot e_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}c_{ijk}e_{k}

where
c_{ijk}\in\mathbb{R}
for

i,j,k\in\{1,2,3\}
are called
structure constants
of

\mathbb{A}
. The
first fundamental representation
of

\mathbb{A}
is the injective linear homomorphism
R:\mathbb{A}\to M(3,\mathbb{R})
defined by
R:e_{i}\mapsto R_{i}
, where
R_{i}
is the
matrix with
[R_{i}]_{jk}=c_{ikj}
, for
i=1,2,3
.

Every three dimensional commutative algebra
\mathbb{A}
with identity is isomorphic to one algebra belonging to a parametrized family
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{r}({p_{1}},\cdots,{p_{6}})
defined as follows.

Definition 1.1

The six parameter family of 3D algebras
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{r}({p_{1}},\cdots,{p_{6}})
is the real linear space

\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the product

\begin{tabular}[]{c|ccc}$\centerdot$&$e_{r}$&$e_{s}$&$e_{t}$\\
\hline\cr$e_{r}$&$e_{r}$&$e_{s}$&$e_{t}$\\
$e_{s}$&$e_{s}$&$p_{7}e_{r}+p_{1}e_{s}+p_{2}e_{t}$&$p_{8}e_{r}+p_{3}e_{s}+p_{4}e_{t}$\\
$e_{t}$&$e_{t}$&$p_{8}e_{r}+p_{3}e_{s}+p_{4}e_{t}$&$p_{9}e_{r}+p_{5}e_{s}+p_{6}e_{t}$\\
\end{tabular},

(9)

where the identities

\begin{array}[]{ccc}p_{7}&=&-p_{1}p_{4}+p_{2}p_{3}-p_{2}p_{6}+p_{4}^{2},\\
p_{8}&=&p_{2}p_{5}-p_{3}p_{4},\\
p_{9}&=&-p_{1}p_{5}+p_{3}^{2}-p_{3}p_{6}+p_{4}p_{5},\\
\end{array}

(10)

stand for the associativity property. The identity is represented by
e=e_{r}
in
\{e_{r},e_{s},e_{t}\}=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
. See

[
6
]
and
[
14
]
. We recall that there are there are non-trivial isomorphisms classes as subsets of
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{r}({p_{1}},\cdots,{p_{6}})
.

Moreover, the first fundamental representation

R
of
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{1}({p_{1}},\cdots,{p_{6}})
is determined by

R(e_{1})=R_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\

\end{array}\right),\,\,\,R(e_{2})=R_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&p_{7}&p_{8}\\

This allows us to use the corresponding matrix algebra in order to

Thus, the following assertion can be proved.

Proposition 1.1

The following properties hold true:

1.

If
\upsilon^{\prime}\in\Pi
, then
\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}\in\Pi
for every
\upsilon\in\mathbb{A}
.

2.

If
\upsilon^{\prime}\in\mathsf{t}
, then
\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}\in\mathsf{t}
for every
\upsilon\in\mathbb{A}
.

3.

Whenever
\upsilon\in\Pi
and
\upsilon^{\prime}\in\mathsf{t}
, then

\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}.

4.

Whenever
\mathbf{0}\neq\upsilon^{\prime}\in\Pi
,
\upsilon\neq\mathbf{0}
and

\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}=\mathbf{0},

then
\upsilon\in\mathsf{t}
.

5.

For any
\upsilon^{\prime},\mu\in\Pi,

\upsilon^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{0}\neq\mu,
there exists a solution
\omega\in\mathbb{A}
of the equation

\omega\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}=\mu.

Whenever we look such solution conditioned to
\omega\in\Pi
, then such solution in unique.

6.

If
\mathbf{0}\neq\upsilon^{\prime}\in\Pi
, and
{\upsilon}\in\mathbb{A}\setminus\mathsf{t}
, then there exists a unique
\omega\in\Pi
such that

\omega\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}=\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}.

Proof.

Straightforward calculations for
\upsilon=\upsilon^{1}e_{1}+\upsilon^{2}e_{2}+\upsilon^{3}e_{3}
and
\upsilon^{\prime}=(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}e_{1}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}e_{2}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}e_{3}
yield
\omega=\upsilon\centerdot\upsilon^{\prime}
as follows

\displaystyle\omega^{1}

\displaystyle=\upsilon^{1}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}+\upsilon^{2}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}+\upsilon^{3}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2},

\displaystyle\omega^{2}

\displaystyle=\upsilon^{1}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}+\upsilon^{2}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}+\upsilon^{3}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3},

\displaystyle\omega^{3}

\displaystyle=\upsilon^{1}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}+\upsilon^{2}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}+\upsilon^{3}(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1},

Thus,
(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}=0
implies that
\omega^{1}+\omega^{2}+\omega^{3}=0
. This proves assertion
1
.

On the other hand, if
(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}=(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}=(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}
then a simple inspection yields
\omega^{1}=\omega^{2}=\omega^{3}
. Thus we have proved claim
2
.

Since
\Pi\cap\mathsf{t}=\mathbf{0}
, then properties

and

3
.

4

Then

\displaystyle(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}\upsilon^{1}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}\upsilon^{3}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}\upsilon^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}\upsilon^{2}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}\upsilon^{1}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}\upsilon^{3}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}\upsilon^{3}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}\upsilon^{2}+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{3}\upsilon^{1}

\displaystyle=0,

which altogether with the orthogonality condition

\displaystyle(\upsilon^{\prime})^{1}(\upsilon^{1}-\upsilon^{2})+(\upsilon^{\prime})^{2}(\upsilon^{3}-\upsilon^{2})

If

Corollary 1.1

The nodal plane
\Pi
is an ideal of
\mathbb{A}
.

Lemma 1.1

Let us consider the linear map
V:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}

V(\upsilon)=(\upsilon^{3}-\upsilon^{2})e_{1}+(\upsilon^{3}-\upsilon^{1})e_{2}+(\upsilon^{2}-\upsilon^{1})e_{3}

(18)

Let
\upsilon_{n}:=\upsilon\cdot\mathbf{n}
be normal component of
\upsilon
and let

\upsilon_{\tau}:=\upsilon-\upsilon_{n}\mathbf{n}\in\Pi,

be its tangential component. Then

V(\upsilon_{\tau})=V(\upsilon)

and

\ker V=\langle\mathbf{n}\rangle=\mathsf{t}.

Proof.

Form linearity
V(\upsilon)=V(\upsilon_{\tau})+\upsilon_{n}V(\mathbf{n})
. On the other hand,
V(\mathbf{n})=\boldsymbol{0}
.
\square

Remark 1.1

From orthogonality,
\upsilon_{\tau}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
for
\upsilon_{\tau}=\upsilon_{\tau}^{1}e+\upsilon_{\tau}^{2}e_{2}+\upsilon_{\tau}^{3}e_{3}\in\Pi
, then

\displaystyle V(\upsilon)

\displaystyle=V(\upsilon_{\tau})

\displaystyle=(\upsilon^{3}-\upsilon^{2})e_{1}+(\upsilon^{3}-\upsilon^{1})e_{2}+(\upsilon^{2}-\upsilon^{1})e_{3}

\displaystyle=(-\upsilon^{1}-\upsilon^{2}-\upsilon^{2})e_{1}+(-\upsilon^{1}-\upsilon^{2}-\upsilon^{1})e_{2}+(\upsilon^{2}-\upsilon^{1})e_{3}

\displaystyle=(-2\upsilon_{\tau}^{2}-\upsilon_{\tau}^{1})e_{1}-(2\upsilon_{\tau}^{1}+\upsilon_{\tau}^{2})e_{2}+(\upsilon_{\tau}^{2}-\upsilon_{\tau}^{1})e_{3}.

Moduli of objects in finite length abelian categories
Andres Fernandez Herrero, Emma Lennen, and Svetlana Makarova

Abstract
We construct moduli spaces of objects in an abelian categories satisfying some finiteness hypotheses. Our approach is based on the work of Artin-Zhang [ AZ01 ] and the intrinsic construction of moduli spaces for stacks developed by Alper-Halpern-Leistner-Heinloth [ AHLH19 ] .



1
Introduction

Moduli problems in algebraic geometry can roughly be divided into two classes: “linear” and “nonlinear”.
Linear moduli problems classify objects that can be broken down to linear algebraic data, like representations, sheaves and complexes. Nonlinear moduli problems often classify more geometric objects, like certain classes of varieties, commutative or noncommutative.
In
[
AZ01
]
, Artin and Zhang provide a framework to study the moduli of objects in a certain class of abelian categories, and they use it to construct Hilbert schemes of quotients of a noetherian object.
We view it as a general construction in the realm of linear moduli problems. The goal of this paper is to study the range of applicability of their theory.

We construct moduli spaces of objects in an abelian category
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following conditions,
see
Section

2
for definitions.

Standing Assumptions 1.1
.

\mathcal{C}
is an essentially small
k
-linear abelian category that

(A0)

is Hom-finite,

(A1)

is finite,

(A2)

has enough projective objects.

Our main results can be summarized as follows.

Main Theorem

(
4.5
,
4.17
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category satisfying the
1.1
and let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
be the stack of objects in
\mathcal{C}
as in
3.1
. Then,

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a disjoint union of algebraic stacks of finite type over
k
.

(ii)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
admits a good moduli space
M_{\mathcal{C}}
which is a disjoint union of local artinian
k
-schemes.

(iii)

K-theory classes naturally give rise to a notion of slope stability, which induces a
\Theta
-stratification on each connected component of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
. The corresponding semistable locus admits a projective good moduli space.

To illustrate the main theorem above, let us recall the analogous results by King
[
Kin94
]
, which apply to the special case when
\mathcal{C}
is the category of finite dimensional representations of an acyclic quiver. In that case the connected components of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
are in bijection with dimension vectors for the representations (more generally, in our setting we show that the connected components of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
are in natural bijection with effective G-theory classes in
\Geff(\mathcal{C})
as in
Section

2.2
). The K-theory classes are in natural correspondence with tuples of integers indexed by the vertices of the quiver. For each such tuple, King defined a notion of semistability for representations of quivers, and showed that the semistable locus admits a projective good moduli space over
k
.

Our proof of the main theorem uses the intrinsic criteria for the existence of moduli spaces developed in
[
Alp13
,
AHLH19
]
. Moreover, we follow the treatment of the moduli of abelian categories in
[
AHLH19
]
using the functor by Artin-Zhang. The main contribution of this paper is the observation that the mild conditions that we impose on our abelian categories guarantee that the moduli functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is represented by an algebraic stack which admits a good moduli space. A novelty in our approach is the use of ind-algebraic spaces to prove the existence of the good moduli space before knowing quasicompactness of the connected components of the moduli stack, and then concluding a posteriori that each connected component is quasicompact. We hope that this type of technique could apply to other stacks where quasicompactness is known only for the irreducible components (
Remark

4.6
).

In
Section

5
, we provide several examples of abelian categories satisfying
1.1
. We observe that for the examples in
Section

5.3
it is not a priori clear if the classical GIT methods can be applied at all, because the Hilbert scheme constructed by
[
AZ01
]
is only known to be an algebraic space rather than a projective scheme.

Hence, our results provide new moduli spaces – moduli of comodules over Hopf algebras of functions on quantum groups, which, to our knowledge, have not been constructed before, yet may be of interest to the representation theory community.

Even though our assumptions on the category
\mathcal{C}
seem quite restrictive, we believe that they are not very far from being sharp for the existence of a moduli space for the whole moduli functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
in the sense of Artin-Zhang (before imposing any stability conditions). Indeed,
(A0)
is necessary in order for the functor to be represented by an algebraic stack. The finite length condition in
(A1)
is necessary for the functor
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
to admit a good moduli space of finite type over
k
by
[
AHLH19
, Lem. 7.19]
. Finally, the assumption on the existence of enough projective objects
(A2)
is to our knowledge the only general categorical condition that guarantees that
\Ind(\mathcal{C})
is adically complete as in
[
AZ01
]
, which is a necessary condition for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
to be represented by an algebraic stack.

Related works

King
[
Kin94
]
applies the methods of GIT to construct moduli of finite-dimensional representations of finite-dimensional associative algebras. These categories are known to have a categorical description, namely, they satisfy
1.1
and have a finite number of simple objects up to isomorphism.
Further, various stability conditions have been described on a subclass of those in
[
FJM08
]
.

Artin and Zhang
[
AZ01
]
develop methods for defining families of objects in more general abelian categories, and prove that the Hilbert functor of quotients is representable by an algebraic space. This is a striking general result that they apply to constructing moduli spaces for noncommutative graded algebras. However, it relies on the assumption that the category is adically complete. This is guaranteed by the assumption that the category has enough projective objects, and it can also be checked directly for categories of coherent sheaves on projective schemes (commutative or noncommutative).

In
[
AHLH19
, Section 7]
, the authors apply the theory of good moduli spaces to stacks of objects as defined in
[
AZ01
]
. With a few technical assumptions, they prove existence of a proper good moduli space of Bridgeland semistable objects on the derived category of a proper smooth variety. Some of their more general results
[
AHLH19
, Thm. 7.27]
assume as an input that the moduli stack is algebraic and locally of finite type.
Some technical work in
Section

3
is dedicated to verifying these assumptions for an abstract abelian category.

There have been constructions of moduli of objects in triangulated and dg categories.
For example, Toën and Vaquié
[
TV07
]
construct derived moduli of perfect objects in dg categories, and Lieblich
[
Lie06
]
constructs classical moduli of perfect universally gluable complexes on a proper morphism. While their constructions work in vast generality, their moduli stacks only see perfect objects. On the other hand, in our work, we also construct moduli of objects that are not necessarily perfect.

Limitations

Our framework leaves out interesting examples such as the moduli of coherent sheaves on a proper scheme or the moduli of objects in the heart of a Bridgeland stability condition. In these cases the whole moduli functor does not necessarily admit a good moduli space, but instead there is a semistability condition such that the semistable locus admits a good moduli space.

The requirement that the category
\mathcal{C}
has enough projectives seems too restrictive to address some of these classical moduli problems (see
[
Kan19
, Thm. 1.1]
for a criterion of having enough projectives). Unfortunately, applying the theory of Artin and Zhang directly to arbitrary abelian categories without enough projectives does not usually yield functors represented by algebraic stacks. It seems natural to attempt to modify the moduli functor, or to consider a natural embedding of the abelian category into one where the Artin-Zhang formalism is better behaved. This is part of ongoing investigations by the authors.

Vistas

We view this project as the first step in our attempt to understand strange duality
[
DT94
,
Bea95
,
DW00
,
MO07
]
in the context of abelian categories. Exploring the strange duality phenomena for categories satisfying
1.1
will be part of our future research.

A possible avenue of exploration for the interested reader would be to understand concretely the wall crossing behavior of the stability conditions that we provide. It should be possible to use the
\Theta
-stratification and the linearity of the moduli problem to keep track of the variation of the moduli spaces in some of the examples in
Section

5
. Furthermore, we have not attempted to investigate further the geometry of the moduli spaces that can be constructed using our theory, so this is another natural direction to investigate.

Abelian categories and base-change

2.1
Finite categories

Definition 2.1
.

A
k
-linear abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is
Hom-finite
if for every
V,W\in\mathcal{C}
, the dimension of
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(V,W)
over the base field
k
is finite.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
k
-linear abelian category.

•

An object
V\in\mathcal{C}
is
simple
if it has exactly two subobjects:
0
and
V
.

•

The
length
of an object
V\in\mathcal{C}
is the largest natural number
n
for which there exists a filtration

0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset\dots\subset V_{n}=V

such that each factor
V_{i}/V_{i-1}
is simple for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Such a filtration is called a
Jordan-Hölder filtration
, and we set
\gr V=\bigoplus V_{i}/V_{i-1}
.

•

\mathcal{C}
is
finite
if every object has finite length
[
Pop73
, p. 370]
.

Remark 2.3
.

An object has finite length if and only if it is both noetherian and artinian
[
Sta23
, 0FCJ]
, and
\gr V
is independent of the choice of the Jordan-Hölder filtration
[
Sta23
, 0FCK]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
satisfy
1.1
.
For every simple object
S\in\mathcal{C}
, there is a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable projective object
P_{S}\in\mathcal{C}
that admits an epimorphism
P_{S}\twoheadrightarrow S
. If
S^{\prime}
is another simple object that is not isomorphic to
S
, then
\operatorname{Hom}(P_{S},S^{\prime})=0
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a simple object. By
(A2)
, there is a projective object
P
in
\mathcal{C}
and a nonzero morphism
P\to S
, which is necessarily an epimorphism since
S
is simple. By
(A1)
,
P
admits a finite direct sum decomposition
P=\bigoplus_{j}P_{j}
, where each
P_{j}
is indecomposable projective. Since
P_{j}\to S
is nonzero for some
j
, we can set
P_{S}=P_{j}
. Uniqueness follows from sequentially applying
[
Kra15
, Thm. 5.5, Lem. 3.6, Cor. 3.5]
.

Let
S^{\prime}
be any other simple object in
\mathcal{C}
, and suppose that there is a nonzero morphism
P_{S}\to S^{\prime}
, which necessarily is an epimorphism. Since both
S^{\prime}
and
S
are simple, the kernels of
P_{S}\to S
and
P_{S}\to S^{\prime}
are maximal proper subobjects of
P_{S}
, and so they must agree by
[
Kra15
, Lem. 3.6]
. This implies
S\cong S^{\prime}
.
∎

Notation 2.5
.

Let
I
denote the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a set of simple objects from each of the isomorphism classes, and choose their respective indecomposable projective covers
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.
By

, the latter set is independent of any choices, up to isomorphism.

Definition 2.6
.

We call

as above the

.

2.2
K-theory and G-theory

In this subsection, we recall the standard Grothendieck group construction. In algebraic geometry, this construction is often applied to the category of coherent sheaves on a smooth variety, which happens to coincide with the category of perfect objects. In general however, because abelian categories may not have finite global dimension, we need to distinguish Grothendieck groups of the given abelian category and of the subcategory of perfect objects. We mimic the construction of Quillen’s algebraic K-theory spectrum
[
Qui10
]
as a group completion of the groupoid of finitely generated projective modules, omitting the higher homotopy data.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an abelian category.

•

An object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
is
perfect
if it admits a finite resolution by projective objects in
\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\Perf(\mathcal{C})
the full additive subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of perfect objects.

•

The
(zeroth) K-theory
of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\Kzero(\mathcal{C})
and is an abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of objects in
\Perf(\mathcal{C})
, subject to the relations of additivity in short exact sequences.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an abelian category. The
(zeroth) G-theory
of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\Gzero(\mathcal{C})
and is an abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of objects in
\mathcal{C}
, subject to the relations of additivity in short exact sequences.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that a G-theory class
\alpha\in\Gzero(\mathcal{C})
is
effective
if there is
V\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\alpha={[V]}
.
We denote by
\Geff(\mathcal{C})
the submonoid of effective G-theory classes. We similarly define
\Keff(\mathcal{C})
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
1.1
. Let
V
be an object of
\Perf(\mathcal{C})
and
W
be an object of
\mathcal{C}
, then we have the following.

(i)

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(V,W)=0
for all
i\gg 0
.

(ii)

For all
i
, we have
\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(V,W)<\infty
.

(iii)

There is a natural pairing
\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Gzero(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{Z}
defined on effective classes as

([V],[W])\longmapsto\langle[V],[W]\rangle=\chi\left(\RHom_{\mathcal{C}}(V,W)\right).

We write
\langle V,W\rangle=\langle[V],[W]\rangle
for brevity. ∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
1.1
. Let
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the canonical set of projective generators as in
2.6
.

(i)

We have
\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus I}
with basis
\{[P_{i}]\}_{i\in I}
, and
\Gzero(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus I}
with basis
\{[S_{i}]\}_{i\in I}
. These bases are dual up to a scalar multiple, i.e.,
\langle P_{i},S_{i}\rangle=\tau_{i}\geq 1
and
\langle P_{i},S_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\tau_{i}
.

(ii)

The pairing
\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\times\Gzero(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{Z}
from
Lemma

2.10
is nondegenerate.

(iii)

If
\mathcal{C}=\Perf(\mathcal{C})
, then
\iota
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Part
(i)
follows from
Lemma

2.4
and
2.7
. Part
(ii)
follows at once from the existence of dual bases. Part
(iii)
follows from
2.7
.
∎

2.3
Finiteness conditions in Grothendieck categories

In this subsection, we fix a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{A}
. Recall that an abelian category is called
Grothendieck
if it has arbitrary direct sums, filtered colimits are exact, and it has a generator. A
generator
is an object
G\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(G,\underline{\ \ })
detects zero morphisms.

Definition 2.12

(
[
Pop73
, pp. 91, 92]
)
.

An object
V\in\mathcal{A}
is called

•

finitely generated (finite type)
if,
whenever
V\cong\colim_{i}V_{i}
for a filtered diagram
of subobjects
V_{i}\subset V
,
there is an index
i
for which
V=V_{i}
;

•

finitely presented
if it is finitely generated, and
the kernel of any epimorphism
W\twoheadrightarrow V
from a finitely generated object
W
is finitely generated itself; equivalently, if
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(V,\underline{\ \ })
commutes with filtered colimits;

•

noetherian
if every subobject of
V
is finitely generated; equivalently, if subobjects of
V
satisfy the ascending chain condition.

When
\mathcal{A}\cong R\text{-}\Mod
, the three notions are equivalent to the algebraic notions of finite generation, finite presentation and being noetherian, respectively.
In a more general category admitting filtered colimits, objects
V
for which
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(V,\underline{\ \ })
commutes with filtered colimits are called
compact
. These have a special importance for the moduli theory we consider.

Notation 2.13
.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{fp}}
the full subcategory of finitely presented objects in
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2.14
.

A Grothendieck category is called
locally noetherian
if it admits a set of noetherian projective generators.

We recall the following useful fact.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
Pop73
, Ch. 5, Thm. 8.7]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally noetherian category, then the three notions of finiteness for objects coincide. Hence,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{fp}}
is an abelian category whose kernels and cokernels coincide with those in
\mathcal{A}
.

2.4
Base-change of abelian categories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-linear Grothendieck abelian category and

k
-algebra. We recall some notions from

]
.

Definition 2.16

(
[
AZ01
, B2]
)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{R}
the category of pairs
(V,\psi)
where
V\in\mathcal{A}
and

is a homomorphism of
k

\psi\colon R\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{A}}(V)
. The set of morphisms between two pairs
(V,\psi)\to(W,\xi)
consists of those morphisms in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(V,W)
that are compatible with the corresponding
R

.

For any two commutative

.

In

V\otimes_{R}\underline{\ \ }\colon R\text{-}\Mod\to\mathcal{A}_{R}
for an object

(
[

)
.

Let

. ∎

Definition 2.18

(
[
AZ01

)

An object

For the current setup of moduli theory to work, we need the following condition.

Definition 2.19

(see
[

]
)

The category

is called

if for all noetherian

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
k
-linear abelian category which is Hom-finite
(A0)
and finite
(A1)
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}
its

\mathcal{A}=\Ind(\mathcal{C})

[

, §8.6]
.
By
[

,

and by
[

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{fp}}\simeq\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 2.20
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is Hom-finite
(A0)
and finite
(A1)
, then it is equivalent to the category
H\text{-}\mathrm{fdcomod}

of finite dimensional comodules over some
k
-coalgebra

.

Proof.

Our assumptions imply that
\mathcal{A}=\Ind(\mathcal{C})
is of finite type in the sense of
[

, Def. 4.4]
, because
\mathcal{A}
is Grothendieck as noted above, and the other conditions are satisfied by

and
(A1)
.
By
[

is equivalent to the category

of comodules over some coalgebra

This identifies

The fundamental theorem of coalgebra states that every

∎

Theorem 2.21
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an essentially small
k
-linear abelian category which is Hom-finite
(A0)
and finite
(A1)
, then
\mathcal{A}=\Ind(\mathcal{C})
is strongly locally noetherian.

Since
\mathcal{A}\simeq\Ind(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{fp}})
, this automatically implies that
\mathcal{A}
is strongly locally noetherian whenever it is a locally noetherian Grothendieck category for which
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{fp}}
is Hom-finite
(A0)
and finite
(A1)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is a finite category, it is noetherian, hence
\mathcal{A}
is locally noetherian.

Let
R
be a noetherian

By
[

, B3.17]
,
\mathcal{C}\otimes R=\{V\otimes R\mid V\in\mathcal{C}\}
generates

. If we show that for every

, its base change

is noetherian, then the claim will be proved. So pick

and consider the following commutative diagram of functors, which exists by
2.20
, where the vertical arrows are the forgetful functors.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H\text{-}\mathrm{Comod}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R\text{-}\Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Vect}_{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\otimes\underline{\ \ }}

By

is a finite free module over a noetherian ring

Corollary 2.22
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an essentially small
k
-linear abelian category which is Hom-finite
(A0)
and finite
(A1)
, then for any field extension

Example 2.23

(Necessity of Hom-finiteness)
.

2.21
does not hold if we remove the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is Hom-finite. As an example, let
\mathcal{C}
denote the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over the algebraic closure
\overline{k(t)}
of the field of rational functions
k(t)

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Grothendieck

-linear category. Let

be a
k
-algebra.
Let

be a finitely generated projective object and let

Then we have the following.

(i)

For all
R

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)\otimes_{R}M\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V\otimes_{R}M)

(ii)

If

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)\otimes_{R}T\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{T}}(P\otimes_{R}T,V\otimes_{R}T)

(iii)

If

Proof.

For part

, choose a presentation

of the module

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)^{\oplus J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)^{\oplus L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)\otimes_{R}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V^{\oplus J})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V^{\oplus L})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V\otimes_{R}M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Both rows are exact, because tensor products are right exact and

the two leftmost vertical morphisms are isomorphisms, and we conclude by the five lemma that

This now implies part

To prove part

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)\otimes_{R}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V)\otimes_{R}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V\otimes_{R}N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P,V\otimes_{R}M).}

By part

, both vertical morphisms are isomorphisms. Since

is a monomorphism. By left exactness of

, we conclude that the bottom morphism is injective. Therefore,

∎

Lemma 2.27
.

Assume
\mathcal{A}=\Ind(\mathcal{C})
, where
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
1.1
. Let
R
be a

-algebra,
and let

Proof.

By

\{P_{i}\otimes R\}_{i\in I}
is a set of noetherian projective generators in

∎

M_{\mathcal{C}}:=\colim_{\mathcal{Z}\in J_{\text{irr}}}Z
the ind-proper strict ind-algebraic space given by the colimit of the good moduli spaces for elements of
J_{\text{irr}}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The strict ind-algebraic space
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is represented by a separated algebraic space locally of finite type over
k
. Moreover,
M_{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the valuative criterion for properness over
k
.

Proof.

The strict ind-algebraic space
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is a functor from
k
-algebras into sets that satisfies descent for the fpqc topology (cf.
[
Ric19
, §1.2]
). In order to show that it is represented by an algebraic space locally of finite type over
k
, it suffices to find a Zariski cover by subfunctors that are represented by algebraic spaces of finite type over
k
. For each
k
-point
z\in M_{\mathcal{C}}(k)
, there exists some
\mathcal{Z}\in J_{\text{irr}}
such that
z
belongs to the good moduli space
Z
. Since
\mathcal{Z}\to Z
is a good moduli space morphism, there exists a closed point
x\in|\mathcal{Z}|
that maps to
z
. Note that
x
is also closed in the larger stack
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
, because
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a closed immersion. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
be a quasicompact open neighborhood of
x
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
. We equip the closed complement
\mathcal{Y}_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\setminus\mathcal{Q}_{x}
with the reduced substack structure.

Choose any
\mathcal{W}\in J_{\text{irr}}
containing
x
, with associated good moduli space
\varphi\colon\mathcal{W}\to W
(
Lemma

4.2
). The image of
\varphi(\mathcal{Y}_{x}\cap\mathcal{W})
is closed and does not contain the point
z\in W\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}

[
Alp13
, Thm. 4.16 (ii)-(iii)]
. We denote by
U_{z}^{W}
the open complement of
\varphi(Z_{x}\cap\mathcal{W})
in
W
. The open algebraic spaces
U_{z}^{W}\subset W
as
\mathcal{W}
runs through
J_{\text{irr}}
are compatible, and therefore yields an open subfunctor
U_{z}:=\colim_{\mathcal{W}\in J_{\text{irr}}}U_{z}^{W}
of
M_{\mathcal{C}}
. The open substack
\mathcal{Q}_{x}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is contained in only finitely many irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
, and so it is contained in some fixed
\mathcal{W}_{0}\in J_{\text{irr}}
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
is disjoint from any other component not in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
. For any
\mathcal{W}\supset\mathcal{W}_{0}
, we have
U_{z}^{W}=U_{z}^{W_{0}}
. Therefore, the colimit
\colim_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\in J_{\text{irr}}}U_{z}^{W}
eventually stabilizes for a cofinal set of indexes, and we have that
U_{z}=U_{z}^{W_{0}}
is an algebraic space of finite type over
k
. Since each closed stratum of
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is an algebraic space locally of finite type over
k
, the ind-algebraic space
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is Jacobson. This means that the set of open sub-algebraic spaces
\{U_{z}\}_{z\in M_{\mathcal{C}}(k)}
as
z
runs over all
k
-points is a Zariski cover of
M_{\mathcal{C}}
, as desired.

The fact that
M_{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the valuative criterion for properness comes from the fact that every quasicompact closed stratum is proper. From the colimit description, it follows that every quasicompact open sub-algebraic space is separated. Therefore,
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is a decent algebraic space
[
Sta23
,
Tag 03I8
]
, and hence quasi-separated
[
Sta23
,
Tag 0BB6
]
. Since
M_{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the valuative criterion for properness, this implies that
M_{\mathcal{C}}
is separated
[
Sta23
,
Tag 03KV
]
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

With notation as above:

(i)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}\to M_{\mathcal{C}}
is a good moduli space.

(ii)

There is a bijection between the set of effective G-theory classes in
\Geff(\mathcal{C})
and the set

.

(iii)

For any

Proof.

We use the same notation as in the proof of

. Since the statement can be checked Zariski locally on the target

, Prop. 4.7 (ii)]
, we can restrict to one of the quasicompact opens

. Note that

is contained in the good moduli space

for
\mathcal{W}_{0}
. We have a cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{z}\times_{M_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{W}_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{0}.}

Indeed, if a point

is not contained in
\mathcal{W}_{0}
, then it is not contained in
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
, which means that it must be contained in
\mathcal{Y}_{x}\cap\mathcal{W}
for some

Since

is a good moduli space

.

By

the

-points of

are in bijection with closed

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}

are in bijection with isomorphism classes of semisimple objects in

∎

4.2
Stability of objects in
\mathcal{C}

Fix an effective G-theory class
\alpha\in\Geff(\mathcal{C})
. In this subsection we define a family of stability conditions coming from numerical invariants on
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
in the sense of
[
HL21
]
. We first need to define some line bundles on
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\beta\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus I}
of the form
\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}[P_{i}]
, for some tuple of integers
n_{i}
. We define
\mathcal{L}_{\beta}
to be the line bundle in
\text{Pic}(\mathcal{M})
such that for every noetherian
k
-algebra
R
and every
f\colon\operatorname{Spec}(R)\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
determined by an
R
-flat element
A\in(\mathcal{A}_{R})^{\mathrm{fp}}
, we have

f^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\beta}):=\bigotimes_{i\in J}\text{det}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P_{i}\otimes R,A))^{\otimes n_{i}}.

The definition above makes sense, because each
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{R}}(P_{i}\otimes R,A)
is a vector bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
(Lemmas
2.26
and
2.27
), and
n_{i}=0
for all but finitely many
i\in I
.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}[P_{i}]\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be a rational K-theory class such that
m_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in I
. For any field
K
and any
V\in(\mathcal{A}_{K})^{\text{fp}}
, we define the
\gamma
-length of
V
by

\ell_{\gamma}(V):=\left\langle\gamma_{K},V\right\rangle\geq 0,

where
\gamma_{K}
denotes the base-change
\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}[P_{i}\otimes K]\in\Kzero((\mathcal{A}_{K})^{\mathrm{fp}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Similarly for a G-theory class
\alpha
, we set
\ell_{\gamma}(\alpha):=\langle\gamma,\alpha\rangle
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
J_{\alpha}\subset I
be the set of all
j\in I
such that
\langle P_{j},\alpha\rangle\neq 0
(equivalently, we have
\alpha=\sum_{i\in J_{\alpha}}a_{i}[S_{i}]
for some
a_{i}>0
). We say that a class
\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}[P_{i}]\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})
is
\alpha
-nondegenerate if
m_{i}>0
for all
i\in J_{\alpha}
.

If
\gamma
is an
\alpha
-nondegenerate class, then for any
B\in\mathcal{C}
with G-theory class
\alpha
and any
0\neq E\subset B
, we have
\ell_{\gamma}(E)>0
.

Example 4.12
.

Let
\gamma=\sum_{i\in J_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{\langle P_{i},S_{i}\rangle}[P_{i}]
. Then, for all
B\in\mathcal{C}
with G-theory class
\alpha
and all
E\subset B
, the
\gamma
-length
\ell_{\gamma}(E)
is just the length of
E
in the abelian category
\mathcal{C}
(i.e. the size of its Jordan-Hölder filtration).

Definition 4.13
.

Let
\beta\in K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
, and let
\gamma\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be an
\alpha
-nondegenerate class. Then
\mathcal{L}_{\beta,\gamma}
is defined to be the line bundle on
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
given by

\mathcal{L}_{\beta,\gamma}:=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}^{\otimes\ell_{\gamma}(\alpha)}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}^{\otimes-\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle}.

For the rest of this section, we fix
\beta
and
\gamma
as above.

Definition 4.14
.

For any object
E\in\mathcal{C}
with
\ell_{\gamma}(E)\neq 0
, we define the
\beta
-slope of
E
by
\sigma_{\beta}(E):=\frac{\langle\beta,E\rangle}{\ell_{\gamma}(E)}
. An object
A\in\mathcal{C}
with numerical class
\alpha
is called
\beta
-semistable
if for all nonzero subobjects
0\neq E\subset A
, we have the inequality of slopes
\sigma_{\beta}(E)\leq\sigma_{\beta}(A)
.

Our goal is to show that the set of
\beta
-semistable objects with G-theory class
\alpha
are the
k
-points of an open substack
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}^{\beta\text{-}ss}\subset(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
which admits a projective good moduli space over
k
. We would also like to develop Harder-Narasimhan theory for this stability condition. We will accomplish this via the use of a numerical invariant in the sense of
[
HL21
, Def. 0.0.3]
.

For any field
K\supset k
and any positive integer
h
, morphisms
B(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{h}_{K}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
amount to
\mathbb{Z}^{h}
-graded objects
\bigoplus_{\vec{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}}A_{\vec{w}}
in
(\mathcal{A}_{K})^{\text{fp}}
(cf.
[
AHLH19
, Prop. 7.12]
). Using this description, we define a rational quadratic norm on graded points as in
[
HL21
, Def. 4.1.12]
.

Definition 4.15
.

We denote by
b_{\gamma}
the rational quadratic norm on graded points of
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
defined as follows. For any
h>0
, morphism
g\colon B(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{h}_{K}\to(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
corresponding to a graded object
\bigoplus_{\vec{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}}A_{\vec{w}}
, and vector
\vec{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{h}
, we set

b_{\gamma}(g)(\vec{r})=\sum_{\vec{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{h}}\left(\vec{w}\cdot\vec{r}\right)^{2}\ell_{\gamma}\left(A_{\vec{w}}\right).

where
\vec{w}\cdot\vec{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}w_{j}r_{j}
denotes the standard inner product for two vectors
\vec{w}=(w_{j})_{j=1}^{h}
and
\vec{r}=(r_{j})_{j=1}^{h}
.

Definition 4.16
.

We define
\mu_{\beta}
to be the
\mathbb{R}
-valued numerical invariant on the stack given by
\mu_{\beta}=\text{wt}(\mathcal{L}_{\beta,\gamma})/\sqrt{b_{\gamma}}

We recall that under certain conditions, such numerical invariant can be used to define a
\Theta
-stratification on the stack (cf.
[
HL21
, §4.1]
or
[
HLHJ21
, §2.5]
).

Theorem 4.17
.

Fix
\beta\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})
and
\gamma\in\Kzero(\mathcal{C})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
that is
\alpha
-nondegenerate.

(i)

The numerical invariant
\mu_{\beta}
defines a
\Theta
-stratification on the stack
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
.

(ii)

The open
\mu_{\beta}
-semistable locus
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})^{\mu_{\beta}\text{-}ss}_{\alpha}
admits a good moduli space
(M_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}^{\beta\text{-}ss}
that is projective over
\operatorname{Spec}(k)
. A power of the dual line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\beta,\gamma}^{-1}
descends to an ample line bundle on
(M_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}^{\beta\text{-}ss}
.

(iii)

The
k
-points of the
\mu_{\beta}
-semistable open locus
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}^{\mu_{\beta}\text{-}ss}
are exactly the
\beta
-semistable objects.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}\to(M_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
be the separated good moduli space of the open and closed substack
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
, which exists by
4.5
. Recall that the good moduli space
(M_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
is finite over
\operatorname{Spec}(k)
. To conclude (i) and (ii), we use
[
HL21
, Thm. 5.6.1 (1)+(2)]
with
\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{Y}=(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\beta,\gamma}
and
\|\bullet\|=b_{\gamma}
. In this case, the weak
\Theta
-stratification will automatically be a
\Theta
-stratification because the characteristic of
k
is
0
.

We are left to prove (iii). Let
p\in(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})_{\alpha}(k)
corresponding to an object
A\in\mathcal{C}
with G-theory class
\alpha
. By
[
AHLH19
, Cor. 7.13]
,
\Theta
-filtrations of
p
correspond to
\mathbb{Z}
-weighted filtrations
(A_{w})_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where

•

A_{w}\subset A_{w-1}\subset A
for all
w\in\mathbb{Z}
.

•

A_{w}=0
for
w\gg 0
, and
A_{w}=A
for
w\ll 0
.

For any such filtration
f
corresponding to
(A_{w})_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}
, a direct computation shows that the value of the numerical invariant is

\mu_{\beta}(f)=\frac{\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}w\left(\ell_{\gamma}(\alpha)\cdot\langle\beta,A_{w}/A_{w+1}\rangle-\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\cdot\ell_{\gamma}(A_{w}/A_{w+1})\right)}{\sqrt{\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}w^{2}\ell_{\gamma}(A_{w}/A_{w+1})}}.

Therefore the point
p=A
is
\mu_{\beta}
-semistable if and only if for all weighted filtrations
(A_{w})_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}w(\ell_{\gamma}(\alpha)\cdot\langle\beta,A_{w}/A_{w+1}\rangle-\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\cdot\ell_{\gamma}(A_{w}/A_{w+1}))\leq 0.

Using summation by parts and the identities
\langle\beta,A\rangle=\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle
and
\ell_{\gamma}(A)=\ell_{\gamma}(\alpha)
, we rewrite this as

\sum_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}(\ell_{\gamma}(A)\langle\beta,A_{w}\rangle-\ell_{\gamma}(A_{w})\langle\beta,A_{w}\rangle)\leq 0.

A standard argument (cf.
[
HLHJ21
, Prop. 5.16]
) shows that the inequality above is satisfied for all filtrations
(A_{w})_{w\in\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if
A
is
\beta
-semistable in the sense of
4.14
.
∎

Example 5.6

Consider

is
\beta
-semistable if and only if
\beta_{2}\geq\beta_{2}
. Meanwhile,
P(-2)
is
\beta
-semistable only when
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
, which gives the trivial stability condition. We have recovered the stability conditions given in
[
FJM08
, Ex. 3.4]
.

5.3
Comodules over co-Frobenius Hopf algebras

Let
C
be a counital coalgebra.
Set
\mathcal{C}=H\text{-}\mathrm{fdcomod}
, so
\mathcal{A}=H\text{-}\mathrm{Comod}=\Ind(\mathcal{C})
by the fundamental theorem of coalgebra.
The category
\mathcal{C}
automatically satisfies
(A0)
and
(A1)
.
For the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is essentially small, we use that simple comodules are in bijection with simple subcoalgebras of
H

[
Lar71
, pg. 354]
.

Theorem 5.7

(
[
Lin77
, Thms 3, 10]
)
.

The following are equivalent for a Hopf algebra
H
.

(i)

H
is co-Frobenius.

(ii)

The injective hull of a simple
H
-comodule is finite-dimensional.

For the purpose of the present paper, we use this theorem as a definition of a co-Frobenius Hopf algebra.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
H
be a co-Frobenius Hopf algebra.
Then
\mathcal{C}=H\text{-}\mathrm{fdcomod}
satisfies
1.1
.

Proof.

It remains to check that
\mathcal{C}
has enough projective objects
(A2)
. For this, we show that every simple object
S\in\mathcal{C}
admits a surjection from a projective object
P\to S
. Since
H
is a Hopf algebra, using its antipode ensures that taking the
k
-linear dual
(\underline{\ \ })^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
is a self-antiequivalence of
\mathcal{C}
. The comodule
S^{\vee}
is still simple, so by
5.7
,
S^{\vee}\in\mathcal{C}
admits a finite-dimensional injective hull
S^{\vee}\to E
, so
E\in\mathcal{C}
. The comodule
P=E^{\vee}
is projective, since an antiequivalence sends injective objects to projective.
Hence, we get the desired surjection
P\to S
.
∎

We next present an example of a co-Frobenius Hopf algebra which has infinitely many simple comodules.
This relevant because, if there were only finitely many, the category of comodules would be equivalent to the category of representations of a finite-dimensional associative algebra, but moduli spaces have been known for them since 1994
[
Kin94
]
, see
Section

5.1
for more details.

Example 5.9

(Finite dimensional comodules over quantum groups)
.

An example known to representation theorists includes the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\subset U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{\vee}
of functions on Lusztig’s quantum group at a root of unity
q
attached to a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
was proved to be co-Frobenius in

[
AD03
, Example 4.1]
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the category of finite-dimensional comodules over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. We claim that the set of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
is infinite.
Indeed, every simple comodule is a subcomodule of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. By the fundamental theorem of coalgebra, every element of a comodule is contained in a finite-dimensional subcomodule; in particular, every simple comodule is finite-dimensional, and every element of
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is contained in a simple subcomodule.
But
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is infinite-dimensional, hence there will necessarily be infinitely many simple comodules.

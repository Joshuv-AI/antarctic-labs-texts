The ABJM Hagedorn Temperature from Integrability
Simon Ekhammar

Abstract
We use the quantum spectral curve to compute the Hagedorn temperature for ABJM theory in terms of the interpolating function h ⁡ ( λ ) h(\lambda) . At weak coupling we compute this temperature up to eight-loop order, showing that it matches the known tree-level and two-loop results. At strong coupling we compute the dependence numerically, showing that it is consistent with expectations from supergravity and the plane-wave limit for the four leading terms in the strong coupling expansion, up to an overall shift of the zero-point energy for type IIA string theory on AdS 4 × ℂ P 3 {}_{4}\times\mathbb{C}\textrm{P}^{3} . We conjecture an analytic form for this shift to leading order that is consistent with our numerical results.

The Hagedorn temperature at weak and strong coupling

2.1
Weak coupling

At zero coupling the partition function of ABJM can be computed by stringing beads together into a single-trace partition function and then exponentiating to multi-trace contributions. In ABJM we have two different types of “half-beads” coming from the two basic sets of fields,
(\phi_{a},\psi^{a})
and
(\overline{\phi}{}^{a},\overline{\psi}_{a})
, which sit in the
(N,\overline{N})
and
(\overline{N},N)
of the gauge groups, respectively. Here
\phi
’s are bosons,
\psi
’s fermions and the index
a
labels the (anti-)fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{su}_{4}
. Due to the structure of the gauge groups this means that the chain for a single-trace operator has to be alternating.

The tree-level Hagedorn temperature was first computed in
[
7
]
. We will here briefly recall how this computation is carried out. Let us define

Z=\textrm{tr}\,e^{-\beta H_{0}}=\textrm{tr}\,w^{2D_{0}}\,,

(2.1)

where
w\equiv e^{-\frac{1}{2T}}
and
D_{0}
is the dilatation operator for the free theory. Then the single-bead partition function is

\displaystyle Z_{bead}(w)=(Z_{\phi}(w)+Z_{\psi}(w))^{2}\,,

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}=\frac{4w(1+w^{2})}{(1-w^{2})^{2}}\,\quad{\rm and}\quad Z_{\psi}=\frac{8w^{2}}{(1-w^{2})^{2}}\,.

(2.3)

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle Z_{bead}=\left(\frac{4w}{(1-w)^{2}}\right)^{2}\,.

(2.4)

The tree level value of the Hagedorn temperature is then found by setting
Z_{bead}(w_{H})=1
, from which it follows that

w_{H}=3-2\sqrt{2}\,,

(2.5)

and so

\displaystyle T_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\log(3+2\sqrt{2})}\approx 0.283648\,.

(2.6)

2.2
Strong coupling and supergravity

In this subsection we generalize the discussion in
[
1
]
to theories whose supergravity dual is
AdS_{d+1}
. In this case the Euclidean
AdS
metric is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=(1+R^{2})d\tau^{2}+\frac{dR^{2}}{1+R^{2}}+R^{2}d\Omega_{d-1}^{2}\,,

(2.8)

where we have set the radius to
1
and
made the identification
\tau\equiv\tau+\beta
. We then assume that a string winds once around the
\tau
direction such that the world-sheet fermions have anti-periodic boundary conditions. These boundary conditions shift the zero-point energy of the string to a nonzero value,
C=C_{0}+\Delta C
where
C_{0}=-2/\alpha^{\prime}
is the shift in flat space. If
\beta
is tuned properly this winding mode is very light and we can use the supergravity approximation. The winding mode is a scalar field
\chi
and its contribution to the action is

\displaystyle\int d^{d+1}X\sqrt{g}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\chi\nabla_{\mu}\chi+m^{2}(R)\chi^{2}\right)\,,

(2.9)

where
m^{2}(R)
is the radial dependent mass-function

\displaystyle m^{2}(R)=(1+R^{2})\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+C\,.

(2.10)

Choosing
\chi
to be massless means finding a
\tau
independent normalizable solution to the equations of motion. Assuming that
\chi
only has
R
dependence then leads to the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{R^{d-1}}\frac{d}{dR}R^{d-1}\frac{d}{dR}\chi(R)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}R^{2}\,\chi(R)+\Delta H\,\chi(R)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\left(C+\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\chi(R)\,,

(2.11)

where

\displaystyle\Delta H=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{R^{d-1}}\frac{d}{dR}R^{d+1}\frac{d}{dR}\,.

(2.12)

We want a solution to (
2.2
) that is normalizable and hence falls off as
R\to\infty
. Hence, solving (
2.2
) is equivalent to solving the radial eigenvalue equation for a perturbed
d
-dimensional harmonic oscillator with frequency
\omega=\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}
and energy

\displaystyle E=-\frac{1}{2}\left(C+\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(2.13)

To lowest order we drop
\Delta H
and set
C=-2/\alpha^{\prime}
. The ground-state energy for the harmonic oscillator then satisfies

\displaystyle E=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\right)=\frac{d}{2}\,\omega=\frac{d}{2}\,\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

(2.14)

which has the solution

\displaystyle\beta=\pi\alpha^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{\alpha^{\prime}}+d^{2}}-d\right)\,.

(2.15)

Hence, to leading order we have that

\displaystyle T_{H}=\frac{1}{\beta}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\alpha^{\prime}}}+\frac{d}{8\pi}+\dots\,.

(2.16)

The first order correction matches that in
[
6
]
.

For the next two orders we use first and second order perturbation theory for the perturbed oscillator, as well as the correction
\Delta C
. As described in
[
1
]
and in the next subsection, an analysis of the plane-wave Hagedorn temperature suggests that

C=-\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta c+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{\prime}).

(2.17)

Furthermore, the correction to the energy is

\displaystyle\Delta E

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H|\psi_{0}\rangle+\frac{\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|\Delta H|\psi_{0}\rangle}{-2\omega}+\frac{\langle\psi_{0}|\Delta H|\psi_{4}\rangle\langle\psi_{4}|\Delta H|\psi_{0}\rangle}{-4\omega}

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{d(d+2)}{8}+0-\frac{d(d+2)}{32\omega}\,,

(2.19)

where the relevant normalized wave-functions are

\displaystyle\psi_{0}(R)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma(d/2)}}\omega^{d/4}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(R)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}}\omega^{d/4+1}\left(2R^{2}-\frac{d}{\omega}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{4}(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Gamma(d/2+2)}}\omega^{d/4+2}\left(R^{4}-\frac{d+2}{\omega}R^{2}+\frac{d(d+2)}{4\omega^{2}}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega R^{2}}\,.

Hence the equation in (
2.14
) becomes

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}-\left(\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta c\right)=\frac{d}{2}\frac{\beta}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{d(d+2)}{8}-\frac{d(d+2)}{32}\frac{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}{\beta}\,,

(2.20)

which we can rearrange into the form

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-\frac{d}{2}\beta-\frac{\beta^{2}\Delta c}{2\pi}-\frac{d(d+2)}{4}\pi\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{d(d+2)\pi^{2}(\alpha^{\prime})^{2}}{8\,\beta}\,.

(2.21)

From this we find the Hagedorn temperature

T_{H}(\alpha^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{2\alpha^{\prime}}}+\frac{d}{8\pi}+\frac{d(d+1)+8\Delta c}{16\sqrt{2}\pi}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{d(d+2)(4d-1)}{256\pi}\alpha^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}\left((\alpha^{\prime})^{3/2)}\right).

(2.22)

Based on the numerical results in

and in section

in (

2.3
Strong coupling and the plane-wave limit

In
[
1
]
we used the plane-wave limit of type IIB string theory to gain some insight on the structure of the zero-point energy corrections. We also argued that the first correction to the Hagedorn temperature can be extracted from the growth of states in the plane-wave limit. In this subsection we will find a similar structure when taking the plane-wave limit of type IIA string theory on AdS
{}_{4}\times\mathbb{C}\textrm{P}^{3}
.

The type IIA plane-wave metric and flux are given by
[

,
14
]

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-2dx^{+}dx^{-}A(x^{I})dx^{+}dx^{+}+\sum_{I=1}^{8}dx^{I}dx^{I}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad F_{+123}=\mu\,,\qquad F_{+4}=\frac{\mu}{3}\,,

(2.24)

where the prefactor
A(x^{I})
is given by

\displaystyle A(x^{I})=\left(\frac{\mu}{3}\right)^{2}\sum_{I=1}^{4}x^{I}x^{I}+\left(\frac{\mu}{6}\right)^{2}\sum_{I=5}^{8}x^{I}x^{I}\,.

(2.25)

For the plane-wave reduction from AdS
{}_{4}\times\mathbb{C}\textrm{P}^{3}
one has that
\mu=3/\sqrt{2}
.

The free energy for the non-interacting type IIA plane-wave string theory was computed in
[
15
]
. By looking for a divergence they found that the Hagedorn temperature is found by solving the equation

\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-\frac{\beta\mu}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{5\beta^{2}\mu^{2}}{36\pi}\log(2)\\
+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(k-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(k+1)}16\pi(2^{2k-2}-1)(1+2^{-2k})\zeta(2k-1)\left(\frac{\beta\mu}{6\sqrt{2}\pi}\right)^{2k}\,.

(2.26)

Note that the shift in the string zero-point energy has the form in (
2.17
).

If we use the flux for the AdS
{}_{4}\times\mathbb{C}\textrm{P}^{3}
reduction
[
7
]
, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}=2\pi-\frac{3}{2}\,\beta+\frac{5\beta^{2}}{8\pi}\log(2)+\dots\,.

(2.27)

The term linear in
\beta
matches the corresponding term in (
2.21
), hence the growth of states in the plane-wave gives the same first-order correction to the flat space Hagedorn temperature as computed from supergravity. The same result can be found by generalizing the discussion in
[
16
]
. If we compare the next term in (

) with (
2.21
) then we see that the plane wave gives

\displaystyle\Omega\simeq\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&-\frac{1}{8\sinh^{3}{\frac{1}{2T_{H}}}}\\

The interesting part of these matrices is the distribution of non-zero entries rather than their exact value, which is dependent on normalization.

Since

\mathbf{P}_{A}=\sum_{m=n_{A}}^{\infty}\frac{c_{A,m}}{x(u)^{m}}\,,

Using the parity symmetry and the asymptotic behavior described in (

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=xh+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{1,2n-1}\frac{h^{2n-1}}{x^{2n-1}}\,,

(3.19a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{2}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{2,2n}\frac{h^{2n}}{x^{2n}}\,,

(3.19b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\cosh^{4}\left(\frac{1}{4T_{H}}\right)}{3}(xh)^{4}\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}c_{3,2n-4}\frac{h^{2(n-4)}}{x^{2n}}\right)\,,

(3.19c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16\cosh^{4}\left(\frac{1}{4T_{H}}\right)}{3}(xh)^{3}\left(1+c_{4,-1}\frac{h^{-2}}{x^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}c_{4,2n-3}\frac{h^{2(n-3)}}{x^{2n}}\right)\,,

(3.19d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{5}=\mathbf{P}_{6}

\displaystyle=2\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4T_{H}}\right)(xh)^{2}\left(1+c_{5,0}\frac{h^{-2}}{x^{2}}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}c_{5,2n-2}\frac{h^{2(n-2)}}{x^{2n}}\right)\,,

Finally, to close the QSC we supplement it with the so-called gluing conditions. As a function with short cuts
\mathbf{Q}_{I}
is an upper half-plane analytic function with a ladder of cuts
(-2h-in,2h-in),n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
in the lower half-plane. In order to restore the long cut structure described previously, the analytic continuation of
\mathbf{Q}_{I}
around the branch-points on the real axis, denoted by
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{I}
, must be a lower half-plane analytic function with a ladder of cuts in the upper half-plane. Luckily, we can easily construct such a function; namely
\mathbf{Q}_{I}(-u)=\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{I}
. We demand that
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{I}=\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{J}
where
\mathcal{L}_{I}^{J}
is a matrix with periodicity
{\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}}^{[2]}=\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}
. To fix the form of
\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}
one should utilize the so-called
\mathbf{Q}\,\omega
-system as done in
[
20
]
for the spectral problem. We will not perform this analysis but rather argue the general form of
\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}
from symmetry.

To constrain
\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}
we use the asymptotic properties of
\mathbf{Q}_{I}
. At
u=i\infty
we have that
\mathbf{Q}_{1}>\mathbf{Q}_{2}>\mathbf{Q}_{3}>\mathbf{Q}_{4}
, and demand that the same ordering is true for
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{I}
at
-i\infty
. This means that
\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}
is upper-triangular. We also require that the ordering is preserved at

which determines the exponential terms on the diagonal. The square-root cuts further add the restriction
\mathcal{L}_{I}{}^{J}\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{J}{}^{K}=\delta_{I}^{K}
constraining the coefficients on the diagonal. Finally, we require that we do not add terms with different powers of
u
in their asymptotics. Supplementing these restrictions with the weak coupling algorithm in the next section we found the matrix relation

\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{I}={\begin{pmatrix}e^{-2\pi u}&0&l_{13}&0&0\\
0&-e^{-2\pi u}&0&l_{24}&0\\
0&0&-e^{2\pi u}&0&0\\
0&0&0&e^{2\pi u}&0\\
0&0&0&0&\bullet\end{pmatrix}_{I}}^{\ J}\,\underline{\mathbf{Q}}_{J}\,.

(3.20)

The explicit form of
\mathcal{L}
depends on
H
-rotations. For the choices described in this section we find that

.

3.2.1
Going to higher orders

Techniques to solve the AdS

To describe our perturbative algorithm we start by rewriting (

Q_{a|i}^{+}+\mathbf{P}_{ab}\kappa^{bc}Q^{-}_{c|j}\mathds{K}^{j}_{i}=0\,,

(3.22)

where
\mathbf{P}_{ab}=\mathbf{P}_{A}\,\sigma^{A}_{ab}
. Let
Q^{(m)}_{a|i}
solve (
3.22
) to order
\mathcal{O}(h^{2m})
and write

\displaystyle Q_{a|i}=Q_{a|i}^{(m)}+h^{2m+2}Q_{a|j}^{(0)}(b^{j}_{i})^{+}+\mathcal{O}(h^{2m+4})\,.

(3.23)

Using (
3.4
) we can solve for

as

b^{i}_{j}-(-1)^{\frac{i(i-1)+j(j-1)}{2}}(b^{[2]})^{i}_{j}=(dS^{(m+1)})^{i}_{j}\,,

(3.24)

where

\displaystyle(dS^{(m+1)})^{i}_{j}=-\mathds{K}_{k}^{i}\kappa^{kl}((Q^{a}{}_{|l})^{(0)})^{+}\mathbf{P}_{ab}\kappa^{bc}(Q_{c|j}^{(m)})^{-}\bigg|_{h^{2m+2}}\,.

(3.25)

We then notice that
(dS^{(m)})^{k}_{l}=y_{0}^{-iu\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor+iu\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}(ds^{(m)})^{k}_{l}
, where
ds^{(m)}
does not contain any exponential prefactors. If we now choose the parameterization
b^{k}_{l}=y_{0}^{-iu\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor+iu\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}p^{k}_{l}
, then (

\displaystyle p^{k}_{l}-z\left(p^{k}_{l}\right)^{[2]}=(ds^{(m+1)})^{k}_{l}\,,

\displaystyle z\in\{1,w_{H},w_{H}^{2},\frac{1}{w_{H}},\frac{1}{w_{H}^{2}}\}\,,

(3.26)

where

is given in (

). It is interesting to note that the additional signs appearing in (

.

The technology for solving equations of the type appearing in (
3.26
) was developed in
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. Let us briefly recall the procedure.
In the first iteration the source-term is
ds^{(1)}
, which from our parameterization is a Laurent polynomial in
u
. For positive powers of
u
it is straightforward to find
p
as a polynomial in
u
. For negative powers we need the twisted
\eta
-function
\eta^{z}_{s}

[
30
]
, which are functions without poles in the upper half-plane that solve

\eta^{z}_{s}-z\,(\eta^{z}_{s})^{[2]}=\frac{1}{u^{s}}\,.

(3.27)

A formal expression is given by

\eta^{z}_{s}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{(u+in)^{s}}\,.

(3.28)

Going to
\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
will require
\eta^{z}_{s}
appearing on the right-hand side of (
3.26
). To solve equations of this type we need generalized twisted
\eta
-functions, which are defined to satisfy

\eta_{s,S}^{z,Z}-zZ\left(\eta_{s,S}^{z,Z}\right)^{[2]}=\frac{Z}{u^{s}}(\eta^{Z}_{S})^{[2]}\,,

(3.29)

without poles in the upper half-plane and with
S
and
Z
multi-indices.
A formal expression for
\eta^{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k}}_{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}}
is

\eta_{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k}}^{z_{1}\,z_{2},\dots z_{k}}=\sum_{0\leq n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots<n_{k}<\infty}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{(u+in)^{s_{1}}}\frac{z_{2}^{n_{2}}}{(u+in_{2})^{s_{2}}}\dots\frac{z_{k}^{n_{k}}}{(u+in_{k})^{s_{k}}}\,.

(3.30)

There is no need to introduce any more functions.

We note that there are also homogeneous solutions to (
3.26
). For example, when
z=1
we can always add an
i
-periodic function to
p
and still solve
(
3.26
)
. However, since by construction
p
must have polynomial asymptotic behavior and cannot have poles in the upper-half plane, we can discard all homogeneous solutions except for constants. We thus write

p_{l}{}^{k}=\Psi_{z}\left((ds^{(m)})^{k}_{l}\right)+\delta_{\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{l}{2}\rfloor}(p_{H})^{k}_{l}\,,

(3.31)

with
(p_{H})^{k}_{l}
constants and
\Psi_{z}
a particular solution of (
3.26
) analytic in the upper half-plane,
i.e.
written in terms of
\eta
-functions.

Thus, at each order in
h
we can find
Q_{a|i}
as a function of
\eta
-functions and
u
with undetermined coefficients

and
p_{H}
. To fix these coefficients we follow the strategy in
[
2
]
. The first step is to construct
Q^{a}{}_{|i}
using (

) for

. In our weak-coupling expansion

so that

(3.33)

It follows that

compared to (

The final step in the algorithm is to impose (

4
Discussion

In this paper we computed the Hagedorn temperature at weak and strong coupling for ABJM using the QSC. At weak coupling we found the result up to eight loops, although there is theoretically no limit as to how high we could go. At strong coupling we found the Hagedorn temperature numerically in inverse powers of
\lambda^{1/4}
to four nontrivial orders in the expansion, showing that the last two terms are consistent with a conjectured form for the coefficients in the expansion.

Putting equations (
2.22
) and (
3.52
) together we observe a linear dependence on the dimension of the CFT for
\Delta c
. However one could twist the
R
-symmetries by turning on fugacities and explore how deformations related to the internal space affect the asymptotic behavior. In this case there is less symmetry in the QSC so the numerical calculations are slower. Perhaps recent work on numerical solutions of the QSC would be of use
[
37
]
. This investigation is in progress.

It would also be interesting to study the AdS
3
case. The first correction in the expansion was already given in
[
38
]
and verified on the world-sheet in
[
16
]
, with the next two terms given in (
2.23
) and (
3.53
). For AdS
3
there is a conjectured form for the QSC
[
39
,
40
]
and a TBA
[
41
]
. However, even for the spectral problem, it is not known how to take the AdS
3
QSC to strong coupling with the present technology due to the novel analytic properties of the curve.

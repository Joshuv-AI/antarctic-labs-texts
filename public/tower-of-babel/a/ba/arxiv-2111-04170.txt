Periodic Solutions in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} for Stationary Anisotropic Stokes and Navier-Stokes Systems
S. E. Mikhailov

Abstract




0.1
Introduction

Analysis of Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations is an established and active field of research in the applied mathematical analysis, see, e.g.,
CF 88
;
Ga 11
;
RRS 16
;
Se 15
;
So 01
;
Te 95
;
Te 01
and references therein.
In
KMW 20
;
KMW21a
;
KMW21b
;
KMW21c
this field has been extended to the transmission and boundary-value problems for stationary Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations of anisotropic fluids, particularly, with relaxed ellipticity condition on the viscosity tensor. In this chapter, we present some further results in this direction considering periodic solutions to the stationary Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations of anisotropic fluids, with an emphasis on solution regularity.

First, the solution uniqueness and existence of a stationary, anisotropic (linear) Stokes system with constant viscosity coefficients in a compressible framework are analysed on
n
-dimensional flat torus in a range of periodic Sobolev (Bessel-potential) spaces.
By employing the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem, the linear results are used to show existence of solution to the stationary anisotropic (non-linear) Navier-Stokes incompressible system on torus in a periodic Sobolev space.
Then the solution regularity results for stationary anisotropic Navier-Stokes system on torus are established.

0.2
Anisotropic Stokes and Navier-Stokes systems

Let

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}
denote a second order differential operator in the component-wise divergence form,

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}})_{k}:=\partial_{\alpha}\big(a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}})\big),\ \ k=1,\ldots,n,

were
{\mathbf{u}}\!=\!(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})^{\top}
,
E_{j\beta}({\mathbf{u}})\!:=\!\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}u_{\beta}+\partial_{\beta}u_{j})
are the entries of the symmetric part
{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbf{u}})
of
\nabla{\mathbf{u}}
(the gradient of
{\mathbf{u}}
),
and
a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}
are constant components of the tensor viscosity coefficient

{\mathbb{A}}:=\!\left({a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}}\right)_{1\leq i,j,\alpha,\beta\leq n}
, cf.
Duf 78
.

Here and further on, the Einstein summation convention in repeated indices from
1
to
n
is used unless stated otherwise.

The following symmetry conditions are assumed (see
(
OSY 92
, (3.1),(3.3))
),

\displaystyle a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}=a_{\alpha j}^{{k}\beta}=a_{k\beta}^{\alpha j}.

(1)

In addition, we require that tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
satisfies the (relaxed) ellipticity condition in terms of all
symmetric
matrices in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
with
zero matrix trace
, see
KMW21a
;
KMW21b
. Thus, we assume that there exists a constant
C_{\mathbb{A}}>0
such that,

\displaystyle a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{k\alpha}\zeta_{j\beta}\geq C_{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|^{2}\,,\ \

\displaystyle\forall\ \boldsymbol{\zeta}=(\zeta_{k\alpha})_{k,\alpha=1,\ldots,n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}

\displaystyle\mbox{ such that }\,\boldsymbol{\zeta}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\top}\mbox{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta_{kk}=0,

(2)

where
|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|^{2}=\zeta_{k\alpha}\zeta_{k\alpha}
, and the superscript
\top
denotes the transpose of a matrix.

The tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|{\mathbb{A}}\|:=\max\left\{|a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}|:k,j,\alpha,\beta=1\ldots,n\right\}\,.

Symmetry conditions (
1
) lead to the following equivalent form of the operator
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}

\begin{array}[]{lll}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}})_{k}=\partial_{\alpha}\big(a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}u_{j}\big),\ \ k=1,\ldots,n.\end{array}

(3)

Let us also define the Stokes operator
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}
as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p):=\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}-\nabla p.

(4)

Let
{\mathbf{u}}
be an unknown vector field,
p
be an unknown scalar field,
{\mathbf{f}}
be a given vector field and
g
be a given scalar field defined in
{\mathbb{T}}
. Then the equations

\begin{array}[]{lll}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p)={\mathbf{f}},\ {\rm{div}}\ {\mathbf{u}}=g\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{T}}\end{array}

(5)

determine the
anisotropic stationary Stokes system with viscosity tensor coefficient
{\mathbb{A}}=\left(A^{\alpha\beta}\right)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
in a compressible framework
.

In addition, the following nonlinear system

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p)+{({\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{u}}}={\mathbf{f}}\,,\ \ {\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}=g\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{T}}

(6)

is called the
anisotropic stationary Navier-Stokes system with viscosity tensor coefficient
{\mathbb{A}}\!=\!\left(A^{\alpha\beta}\right)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n}
in a compressible framework
.
If
g=0
in (
5
) and (
6
), then these equations are reduced, respectively, to the
incompressible anisotropic stationary Stokes and Navier-Stokes systems
.

In the
isotropic case
, the tensor
{\mathbb{A}}
reduces to

\displaystyle a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}=\lambda\delta_{k\alpha}\delta_{j\beta}+\mu\left(\delta_{\alpha j}\delta_{\beta k}+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{kj}\right),\ 1\leq i,j,\alpha,\beta\leq n\,,

(7)

where
\lambda
and
\mu
are real constant parameters with

\mu>0
(cf., e.g., Appendix III, Part I, Section 1 in
Te 01
),
and (
3
) becomes

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{L}}{\mathbf{u}}=(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}\,\mathbf{u}+\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}.

(8)

Then it is immediate that condition (
0.2
) is fulfilled (cf.
KMW21b
) and thus our results apply also to the Stokes and Navier-Stokes systems in the
isotropic case
. Assuming
\lambda=0
,
\mu=1
we arrive at the classical mathematical formulations of isotropic Stokes and Navier-Stokes systems.

0.3
Some function spaces on torus

Let us introduce some function spaces on torus and periodic function spaces (see, e.g.,

(
Agm 65
, p.26)
,
Agr 15
,
McL 91
,
(
RT 10
, Chapter 3)
,
(
RRS 16
, Section 1.7.1)

(
Te 95
, Chapter 2)
,
for more details).

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and
{\mathbb{T}}
be the
n
-dimensional torus that can be parametrized as the semi-open cube
{\mathbb{T}}=[0,1)^{n}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, cf.
(
Zi 02
, p. 312)
.
In what follows,
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{T}})=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
denotes the space of infinitely smooth real or complex functions on the torus.
As usual,
{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the set of natural numbers,
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
the set of natural numbers complemented by 0, and
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers.

Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional vector with integer components.
We will further need also the set

\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Extending the torus parametrisation to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, it is often useful to identify
{\mathbb{T}}
with the quotient space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Then the space of functions
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
on the torus can be identified with the space of
{\mathbb{T}}
-periodic (1-periodic) functions

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
that consists of functions
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\xi})=\phi(\mathbf{x})\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

Similarly, the Lebesgue space on the torus
L_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, can be identified with the periodic Lebesgue space
L_{p\#}=L_{p\#}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
that consists of functions
\phi\in L_{p,\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, which satisfy the periodicity condition (
9
) for a.e.
\mathbf{x}
.

The space dual to
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{T}})
, i.e., the space of linear bounded functionals on
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{T}})
, called the space of torus distributions is denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}})
and can be identified with the space of periodic distributions
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\#}
acting on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

The toroidal/periodic Fourier transform
mapping a function
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{\infty}
to a set of its Fourier coefficients
\hat{g}
is defined as (see, e.g.,
(
RT 10
, Definition 3.1.8)
)

\displaystyle\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=[{\cal F}_{{\mathbb{T}}}g](\boldsymbol{\xi}):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}e^{-2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}g(\mathbf{x})d\mathbf{x},\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

and can be generalised to the Fourier transform acting on distribution a
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\#}
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|:=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}^{2})^{-1/2}
be the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let us denote

\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi}):=(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2})^{1/2}.

Evidently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2}\leq|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\leq\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(9)

Similar to
(
RT 10
, Definition 3.2.2)
, for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define the
periodic/toroidal Sobolev (Bessel-potential) spaces

H_{\#}^{s}:=H_{\#}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=H^{s}({\mathbb{T}})
, which consist of the torus distributions
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}})
, for which the norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}:=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\ell_{2}}:=\left(\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2s}|\widehat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(10)

is finite, i.e., the series in (
10
) converges.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{2}}
is the standard norm in the space of square summable sequences.
By
(
RT 10
, Proposition 3.2.6)
,
H_{\#}^{s}
are Hilbert spaces.

For
g\in H_{\#}^{s}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let us consider the partial sums

g_{m}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\leq m}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}.

Evidently,
g_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{\infty}
,
\hat{g}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
if
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|\leq m
and
\hat{g}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=0
if
|\boldsymbol{\xi}|>m
.
This implies that
\|g-g_{m}\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
and hence we can write

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}},

(11)

where the Fourier series converges in the sense of norm (
10
).
Moreover, since
g
is an arbitrary distribution from
H_{\#}^{s}
, this also implies that the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
is dense in
H_{\#}^{s}
for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
(cf.
(
RT 10
, Exercise 3.2.9)
).

There holds the compact embedding
H_{\#}^{t}\hookrightarrow H_{\#}^{s}
if
t>s
, embeddings
H_{\#}^{s}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\#}^{m}
if
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
s>m+n/2
, and moreover,
\bigcap_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}H_{\#}^{s}={\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
(cf.
(
RT 10
, Exercises 3.2.10, 3.2.10 and Corollary 3.2.11)
). Note also that the torus norms on
H_{\#}^{s}
are equivalent to the corresponding standard (non-periodic) Bessel potential norms on
{\mathbb{T}}
as a cubic domain, see, e.g.,
(
Agr 15
, Section 13.8.1)
.

By (
10
),

\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}}=|\widehat{g}(\mathbf{0})|^{2}+|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{s}},

where

\displaystyle|g|_{H_{\#}^{s}}:=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\dot{\ell}_{2}}:=\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2s}|\widehat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

is the seminorm in
H_{\#}^{s}
.

For any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let us also introduce the space

\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}:=\{g\in H_{\#}^{s}:\langle g,1\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=0\}.

The definition implies that if
g\in\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}
, then
\widehat{g}(\mathbf{0})=0
and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}}=\|g\|_{H_{\#}^{s}}=|g|_{H_{\#}^{s}}=\|\rho^{s}\widehat{g}\|_{\dot{\ell}_{2}}\ .

(12)

Denoting

\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}:=\{g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}:\langle g,1\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}=0\}
,
then
\bigcap_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\dot{H}_{\#}^{s}=\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}
.

The corresponding spaces of
n
-component vector functions/distribution are denoted as
\mathbf{H}_{\#}^{s}:=(H_{\#}^{s})^{n}
, etc.

Note that the norm
\|\nabla(\cdot)\|_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}}

is an equivalent norm in
\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}
.
Indeed, by (
11
)

\displaystyle\nabla g(\mathbf{x})=2\pi i\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\boldsymbol{\xi}e^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\quad\widehat{\nabla g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=2\pi i\boldsymbol{\xi}\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

and
then (
9
) and (
12
) imply

{2\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{1}}=}2\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}}=2\pi^{2}|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{1}}\leq\|\nabla g\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{0}}\\
\leq 4\pi^{2}|g|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{1}}={4\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}}=}4\pi^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{H_{\#}^{1}}\quad\forall\,g\in{\dot{H}_{\#}^{1}}.

(13)

The vector counterpart of (
13
) takes form

\displaystyle 2\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}{=2\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}}\leq\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{(H_{\#}^{0})^{n\times n}}\leq{4\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}=}4\pi^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}.

(14)

We will further need also the first Korn inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\bf v}\|^{2}_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}\leq 2\|\mathbb{E}({\bf v})\|^{2}_{(L_{2\#})^{n\times n}}\quad\forall\,\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{1}

(15)

that can be easily proved by adapting, e.g., the proof in
(
McL 00
, Theorem 10.1)
to the periodic Sobolev space.

Let us define the Sobolev spaces of divergence-free functions/distributions,

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#\sigma}^{s}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bf w}\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}:{{\rm div}}\,{\bf w}=0\right\},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}},

endowed with the same norm (
10
).

0.4
Stationary anisotropic Stokes system on flat torus

In this section, we generalise to the isotropic and anisotropic (linear) Stokes systems in compressible framework and to a range of Sobolev spaces the analysis, available in
(
Te 95
, Section 2.2)

For the unknowns
({\mathbf{u}},p)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
and the given data

({\mathbf{f}},g)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-2}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let us consider the Stokes system

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f},

(16)

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=g,

(17)

that should be understood in the sense of distributions, i.e.,

\displaystyle-\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p),\boldsymbol{\phi}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{f},\boldsymbol{\phi}\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\phi}\in({\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#})^{n},

(18)

\displaystyle\langle{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}},\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}

\displaystyle=\langle g,\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{T}}}\quad\forall\,\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}.

(19)

For
\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, let us employ
\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\mathbf{x})=e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}
as
\phi
in (
19
) and
\bar{e}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\mathbf{x})
, multiplied by the coordinate vector, as
\boldsymbol{\phi}
in (
18
).
Then recalling (
3
)-(
4
), we arrive for each
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
at the following algebraic system for the Fourier coefficients,
\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
,
k=1,2,\dots,n
, and
\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
.

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\xi_{\alpha}a_{kj}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\beta}\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})+2\pi i\xi_{k}\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},\ k=1,2,\dots,n

(20)

\displaystyle 2\pi i\xi_{j}\hat{u}_{j}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

\displaystyle=\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\quad\ \forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(21)

The
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix,
\mathfrak{S}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
, of system (
20
)-(
21
) is in fact the principal symbol of the anisotropic Stokes system (
16
)-(
17
) that was analysed in
(
KMW21b
, Lemma 15)
to prove that the Stokes system is elliptic in the sense of Agmon–Douglis–Nirenberg.
It was, particularly proved that the matrix
\mathfrak{S}
is non-singular if
\boldsymbol{\xi}\neq 0
and hence the solution of system (
20
)-(
21
) can be represented in terms of the inverse matrix
\mathfrak{S}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
as

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)=\mathfrak{S}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\left(\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\atop\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

(22)

Moreover, using the estimates for the matrix, obtained in that lemma proof, and implementing to the algebraic system the variant of Babuska-Brezzi theory given in Theorem 2.34 and Remark 2.35(i) in
EG 04
, see also
(
KMW21b
, Theorem 10)
,
we obtain the following estimates for the solution of the algebraic system (
20
)-(
21
),

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq C_{uf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{|2\pi\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}}+C_{ug}\frac{|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|},

(23)

\displaystyle|\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\leq C_{pf}\frac{|\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|}{2\pi|\boldsymbol{\xi}|}+C_{pg}|\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

(24)

where
C_{uf}=2C_{\mathbb{A}},\ C_{ug}=C_{pf}=1+2C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbb{A}\|,\ C_{pg}=\|\mathbb{A}\|(1+2C_{\mathbb{A}}\|\mathbb{A}\|).

Remark 1

For the isotropic case (
7
),
due to (
8
),
system (
20
)-(
21
) reduces to

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\left[(\lambda+\mu)\boldsymbol{\xi}(\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}))+\mu|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right]+2\pi i\boldsymbol{\xi}\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

(26)

Taking scalar product of equation (

\displaystyle\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{f}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})}{2\pi i|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}}+(\lambda+2\mu){\hat{g}(\boldsymbol{\xi})},\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

and substituting this back to (

(cf.

,

,

, and

), (

\square

The anisotropic Stokes system (
16
)-(
17
) can be re-written as

\displaystyle S\left({\mathbf{u}}\atop p\right)=\left({\mathbf{f}}\atop g\right),

\displaystyle S\left({\mathbf{u}}\atop p\right):=\left(-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{u}},p)\atop{\rm{div}}\,{\mathbf{u}}\right),

Theorem 0.4.1

Let condition (
0.2
) hold.

(i)
For any

({\mathbf{f}},g)\in\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-2}\times\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}
,

,
the anisotropic Stokes system (
16
)-(

) in torus

has a unique solution

\displaystyle{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}),\quad p(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}}\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})

(30)

with
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
and
\hat{p}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
given by (
22
).
In addition, there exists a constant
C=C(C_{\mathbb{A}},n)>0

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}+\|p\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}}\leq C\left(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s-2}}+\|g\|_{\dot{H}_{\#}^{s-1}}\right)

(31)

and operator (
29

(ii) Moreover, if
({\mathbf{f}},g)\in(\dot{\mathcal{C}}_{\#}^{\infty})^{n}\times\dot{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{\#}

Proof

(i) Expressions (
22
) supplemented by the relations
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
,
\hat{p}(\mathbf{0})=0
imply the uniqueness.
From estimates (
23
)
and (
24
)
we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{u}}\|_{\dot{\mathbf{H}}_{\#}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\dot{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}}\rho(\boldsymbol{\xi})^{2s}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

Approximation and estimation of scale functions for spectrally negative Lévy processes Corresponding author: shimizu@waseda.jp
Haruka Irie

Abstract
The scale function holds significant importance within the fluctuation theory of Lévy processes, particularly in addressing exit problems. However, its definition is established through the Laplace transform, thereby lacking explicit representations in general. This paper introduces a novel series representation for this scale function, employing Laguerre polynomials to construct a uniformly convergent approximate sequence. Additionally, we derive statistical inference based on specific discrete observations, presenting estimators of scale functions that are asymptotically normal. Keywords: Spectrally negative Lévy process; scale function; Laguerre function; discrete observation; asymptotically normal estimator. MSC2020: 60G51; 62M86; 62P05.

Introduction

On a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}};\mathbb{F})
where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a (right-continuous) filtration, we consider a
spectrally negative Lévy process

X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
starting at
x\in\mathbb{R}
:

X_{t}=x+ct+\sigma W_{t}-L_{t},\quad t\geq 0,

where
x,c\in\mathbb{R}
are constants,
W=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-Wiener process and
L=(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-Lévy subordinator (possibly of infinite activity) with the Lévy measure
\nu
on
(0,\infty)
satisfying that

\int_{0}^{1}z\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty;\quad\nu([1,\infty))<\infty.

Note that
W_{0}=L_{0}=0\ a.s.
. The
Laplace exponent
of
X
is defined as

\psi_{X}(\theta):=\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta(X_{1}-x)}\right]=c\theta+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\theta^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\theta z}-1)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z),\quad\theta\geq 0.

We are interested in the
q
-scale function
of
X
,
W^{(q)}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
for a number
q\geq 0
, defined as follows:
W^{(q)}(x)=0
on
(-\infty,0)
and otherwise
W^{(q)}
is the unique continuous function and right continuous at the origin with the Laplace transform

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta z}W^{(q)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\psi_{X}(\theta)-q}\qquad\mbox{for \ \ $\theta>\Phi(q)$},

where
\Phi(q)
is called the
Lundberg exponent
:

\Phi(q):=\sup\{\theta\geq 0\,|\,\psi_{X}(\theta)=q\}.

The scale functions play essential roles in the fluctuation theory of Lévy processes and have various applications in insurance and finance.
For example, defining two stopping times
\tau_{\alpha}^{+}=\inf\{t>0\,|\,X_{t}>\alpha\}
and
\tau_{\alpha}^{-}=\inf\{t>0\,|\,X_{t}<\alpha\}
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have a fluctuation identity for
a>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-q\tau_{a}^{+}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{a}^{+}<\tau_{0}^{-}\}}\right]=\frac{W^{(q)}(x)}{W^{(q)}(a)}\qquad\mbox{for $x\in[0,a]$},

which is an essential identity in the theory of two-sided exit problems and useful in analyzing credit risks and barrier options, among others. As
q=0
we have that
{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{0}^{-}<\tau_{a}^{+})=1-W^{(0)}(x)/W^{(0)}(a)
, and so assuming that

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(0+)=c-\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)>0,

called the
net profit condition
in ruin theory, which implies that
W^{(0)}(\infty)=1/\psi^{\prime}(0+)
, we have the well-known identity for the ruin probability in the classical ruin theory:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{0}^{-}<\infty)=1-\psi^{\prime}(0+)W^{(0)}(x).

(1.1)

See Kyprianou
[
14
]
for the details around these identities.
Moreover, Biffis and Kyprianou
[

]
and Feng and Shimizu

showed that scale functions are useful tools to represent more general ruin-related risks. They showed some generalized Gerber-Shiu functions, the classical version introduced by Gerber and Shiu

, are also represented by

The scale functions also appear in the optimal dividend problems; see Loeffen
[
17
]
, and the Parisian-ruin probability; see Loeffen
et al.

and Baurdoux
et al.

[

and Roger

[

]

[

In considering such an application,
it will be understood that there is a practical need to identify the scale function and to estimate it statistically from observations of a given Lévy process. Indeed, the identification and approximation of scale functions is a topic that has attracted much attention in recent years.

We can find an explicit representation for some simple cases, like a compound Poisson processes; see. e.g., Hubalek and Kyprianou
[
11
]
. However, obtaining a general representation via the Laplace transform is generally challenging because the Laplace inversion is too difficult to implement. Therefore, attempts are made to get an approximate representation. The earliest work on an approximation of scale functions would be due to Egami and Yamazaki
[
8
]
, who construct an approximate sequence of
q
-scale functions using one of a compound Poisson-type Lévy process with phase-type jumps, which consist of a dense family in a class of spectrally negative Lévy processes. Landrault and Willmot
[
16
]
proposed an asymptotic expansion for Wiener-Poisson risk models by inverting the Laplace transform of scale functions and investigating some examples where explicit expansions are obtained. Behme
et al.

[
2
]
extends their results to more general Lévy processes with infinite jumps. Moreover, Xie
et al.

[
26
]
focused on a specific probabilistic representation of the
q
-scale function and provided an approximation formula using Laguerre series expansion. See also Martín-González
et al.

[
19
]
for alternative expansion, and Surya
[
25
]
for numerical methods, among others.

Thus, there are many discussions on the approximation of scale functions, but statistical inference has yet to be discussed, to the best of our knowledge. Our paper’s novelty is that it gives a new series approximation of the
q
-scale function and a data-based statistical estimation of the scale function.
Among these, we are particularly concerned with problems in insurance actuarial practice. In modern actuarial practice, it is standard to use the spectrally negative Lévy process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
for the surplus or asset processes of insurance companies, and certain discrete observations of
X
are available as real data; see Section
3.1
. However, it is usually unclear from the data which Lévy process these data follow. We therefore propose a method for estimating scale functions without specifying a specific model of
X
, by using a nonparametric method for estimating quantities associated with the Lévy measure.

We must take two steps to identify the
q
-scale function in practice. First, we introduce a new approximation formula. We concentrate on a compound geometric integral representation of the
q
-scale function obtained by Feng and Shimizu
[
9
]
. We derive a Laguerre series expansion of the corresponding compound geometric distribution function, the Stieltjes integral with respect to that gives the expansion of the
q
-scale function.
Although we also use Laguerre expansion as in Xie
et al.

[
26
]
, our approach differs from theirs,
and the formula is fundamentally different from theirs, which makes the primary contribution of this paper.

Second, we proceed to statistical inference. The following two studies, Zhang and Su
[
28
]
and Shimizu and Zhang
[
24
]
, are instructive.
The former proposes an estimator of Gerber-Shiu functions by deriving its Laguerre series expansion and estimating the coefficients of each term.
They show a consistency for their proposed estimator.
Shimizu and Zhang
[
24
]
applied the same approach to the ruin probability, and they further showed that the estimator is asymptotically normal.
As shown in equation (
1.1
), the ruin probability is represented by
W^{(0)}(x)
, so their estimator is also an asymptotically normal estimator for the 0-scale function. This paper will construct an asymptotically normal estimator of the
q
-scale function, a generalization of
[
24
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the series representation of the
q
-scale function obtained by Feng and Shimizu
[
9
]
. Under the net profit condition, the
q
-scale function has an integral representation with respect to the compound geometric distribution.
We then construct an approximation of the compound geometric distribution function using the Laguerre series expansion, which can yield the series expansion of the
q
-scale function. Section
3
is the main part and gives the estimator for the approximate sequence of
q
-scale functions.
Under a discrete sampling scheme for the underlying process
X
, we show their consistency and asymptotic normality with the explicit asymptotic variance.
The auxiliary statistics, their properties, and some proofs are summarised in the Appendix.

2.1
A compound geometric representation of
q
-scale functions

A closed expression for the
q
-scale function of the process
X
is found by Feng and Shimizu
[
9
]
in an expectation with respect to a compound geometric distribution under the following

:

NPC

:
\displaystyle c>\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)
.

To state the representation of
W^{(q)}
, we prepare some notation: In the sequel, we fix a number
q\geq 0
for
W^{(q)}
, and let

D:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2};\qquad\gamma:=\Phi(q);\qquad\beta=\begin{cases}cD^{-1}+\gamma,&(D>0)\\

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{q}(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &D^{-1}\int_{0}^{x}\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\beta(x-y)}e^{-\gamma(z-y)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z),&(D>0)\\
&c^{-1}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma(z-x)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z),&(D=0)\end{aligned}\right..

We define a distribution

with a probability density obtained from the function

F_{q}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}f_{q}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,\quad f_{q}(z)=p^{-1}\widetilde{f}_{q}(z),\quad p:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}_{q}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

is well-defined; see Lemma
C.1
.
Then, the following representation is the immediate consequence from Feng and Shimizu
[
9
]
, Proposition 4.1.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose NPC for the process
X
.
Then the
q
-scale function
W^{(q)}
of
X
has the following integral form:

W^{(q)}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{e^{\gamma x}-e^{-\beta x}}{D(1-p)(\beta+\gamma)}-\frac{1}{D(1-p)(\beta+\gamma)}\int_{[0,x)}\left[\gamma e^{\gamma(x-z)}+\beta e^{-\beta(x-z)}\right]\overline{G}_{q}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,&(D>0)\\
&\frac{e^{\gamma x}}{c(1-p)}-\frac{1}{c(1-p)}\int_{[0,x)}e^{\gamma(x-z)}\overline{G}_{q}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,&(D=0)\end{aligned}\right.,

where

and

\displaystyle G_{q}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-p)p^{k}\int_{0}^{x}f_{q}^{*k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,\quad x\geq 0,

2.2
Laguerre series expansion

Let
L_{k}(x)
be the
Laguerre polynomial
of order
k
:

L_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}e^{x}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}x^{n}}(e^{-x}x^{n})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}\frac{(-x)^{j}}{j!},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty),

by which we define the
Laguerre function
for each
\alpha>0
as follows:

\varphi_{\alpha,k}(x):=\sqrt{2\alpha}L_{k}(2\alpha x)e^{-\alpha x},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Note that the system
\{\varphi_{\alpha,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
consists of an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

satisfying that

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\varphi_{\alpha,k}(x)|\leq\sqrt{2\alpha},

(2.2)

and that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\varphi_{\alpha,k}(x)=O(e^{-(\alpha-\delta)x}),\quad x\to\infty,

since
L_{k}
is a polynomial of order
k
. Therefore, we can control the decay order of the function
\varphi_{k,\alpha}
by choosing
\alpha>0
; see Remark
2.6
.

Let
{\cal W}_{\alpha}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the
Sobolev-Laguerre space
(Bongioanni and Torrea
[
6
]
) for

:

{\cal W}_{\alpha}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\left\{f\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\,:\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{r}|\langle f,\varphi_{\alpha,k}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\right\}

There are many useful links between the Laguerre systems and the Sobolev-Laguerre space in Comte and Genon-Catalot
[
7
]
, where Proposition 7.1 and its remark provide an equivalent condition that a function belongs to

as follows.

Lemma 2.2

(Comte and Genon-Catalot

]
)
.

Let

and

. Then,

if and only if

admits the derivatives of order

\displaystyle x^{m/2}\partial_{x}^{m}f(x)\in L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\quad 0\leq m\leq r.

(2.3)

The following uniform convergence of the Laguerre series expansion is obtained by Shimizu and Zhang
[
24
]
, Proposition 3.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
K\in\mathbb{N}

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f_{K}(x)-f(x)|=O\left(K^{-(r-1)/2}\right),\quad K\to\infty.

\mbox{\boldmath$Q$}^{*}_{\alpha,K}:=\left(Q^{*}_{\alpha,0},Q^{*}_{\alpha,1},\dots Q^{*}_{\alpha,K}\right)^{\top}
with

\displaystyle P^{*}(z;p,\gamma,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\gamma^{-1}(1-e^{\gamma x})-\beta^{-1}(1-e^{-\beta x})}{D(1-p)(\beta+\gamma)},&D>0,\\
&\frac{1-e^{\gamma x}}{\gamma c(1-p)},&D=0,\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle Q^{*}_{\alpha,k}(x;p,\gamma,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\Psi_{\alpha,k}(x;\gamma)-\Psi_{\alpha,k}(x;-\beta)}{D(1-p)(\beta+\gamma)},&D>0,\\
&\frac{\Psi_{\alpha,k}(x;\gamma)}{c(1-p)},&D=0\end{aligned}\right.

According to Lemma
2.3
, if
\overline{G}_{q}\in{\cal W}_{\alpha}^{r}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
(e.g.,
f:=\overline{G}_{q}
satisfies (
2.3
)), then

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}|W^{(q)}_{K}(x)-W^{(q)}(x)|\to 0,\quad K\to\infty;

see (
2.8
). Therefore, it follows for any compact sets
V\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
that

\sup_{x\in V}|Z^{(q)}_{K}(x)-Z^{(q)}(x)|\to 0,\quad K\to\infty.

Remark 2.9
.

The approximation formulas obtained in equations (
2.10
) and (
2.12
) are essentially different from those in Xie et al.
[
26
]
, and being more elementary. Their approximation focuses on the relationship between the probability density of the “killed process”
X_{e_{t}}
(where
e_{t}
is an exponential random variable with mean 1) and its scale function, cleverly expanding the probability density into a Laguerre series. In our approximation, we utilize a Laguerre series expansion for the compound geometric distribution
\overline{G}_{q}
, essentially similar to the approach of Shimizu and Zhang
[
24
]
. The utility of this approach becomes apparent in the subsequent part of statistical inference, where similar types of estimators as in
[
24
]
are directly available and shown to be asymptotically normal, demonstrating an advantage of our method.

3
Statistical inference: Main theorems

We will proceed with the statistical estimation of scale functions.
When considering the practical use of
q
-scale functions, we usually have applications to actuarial science, where
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the dynamics of an insurance surplus model.
Then, the coefficient of the linear term
c
corresponds to the premium rate, so we specifically treat the value of
c
as known in this paper.

3.1
Sampling scheme

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We assume that the process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is observed at discrete time points
t_{i}^{n}:=i\Delta_{n}\ (i=0,1,\dots,n)
for some number
\Delta_{n}>0
:

\mbox{\boldmath$X$}^{n}:=\{X_{t_{i}^{n}}\,|\,i=0,1,\dots,n\};\quad T_{n}:=n\Delta_{n}.

In particular, the initial value
X_{t_{0}^{n}}=x
is assumed to be known.
Moreover, we also assume that “large” jumps of
X
are observed, that is, for some number
{\epsilon}_{n}>0
,
we can identify the jumps whose sizes are larger than
{\epsilon}_{n}
, which are finitely many on
[0,T_{n}]
:

\mbox{\boldmath$J$}^{n}:=\{\Delta X_{t}:=X_{t}-X_{t-}\,|\,t\in[0,T_{n}],\ |\Delta X_{t}|>{\epsilon}_{n}\}.

Assume that we have data
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}^{n}\cup\mbox{\boldmath$J$}^{n}
, and consider the following asymptotics:

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\to 0;\quad T_{n}\to\infty;\quad{\epsilon}_{n}\to 0,

(3.1)

as
n\to\infty
. In the sequel, we always consider the limit
n\to\infty
when considering the asymptotic symbols,
and assume (
3.1
) for the sampling scheme
(\Delta_{n},T_{n},{\epsilon}_{n})
without specially mentioned.

Remark 3.1
.

The data
\mbox{\boldmath$X$}^{n}
are assumed to be the data on the remaining amount of reserves that insurance companies record on a regular basis. On the other hand,
\mbox{\boldmath$J$}^{n}
are assumed to be “large” claims.
It may seem unnatural to consider such a model with infinitely many jumps when considering insurance surplus, but this is a standard surplus approximation in risk theory. Arguing under asymptotics such as (
3.1
) with data like
\mbox{\boldmath$J$}^{n}
is a usual way of theoretically justifying that the more detailed claims data you collect as much as possible, the better the estimation. As a practical matter, it is not necessary to be able to observe infinitely many “small” jumps in reality.

We shall make some assumptions on the scheme
(\Delta_{n},T_{n},{\epsilon}_{n})
:

S1

:
n\Delta_{n}^{2}\to 0
.

S2

:
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\epsilon}_{n}}z\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)+\int_{0}^{{\epsilon}_{n}}z^{2}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)=o(T_{n}^{-1/2})
.

In addition, to ensure some integrability with respect to
\nu
, we will prepare the following moment conditions on
\nu
:

M[
k
]

: For some given
k>0
, there exists some
{\epsilon}\in(0,1)
such that
\nu(|z|\vee|z|^{k+{\epsilon}})<\infty
.

Remark 3.5
.

Our asymptotic results for
\widehat{W}_{K}^{(q)}
and
\widehat{Z}_{K}^{(q)}
are all for a fixed
K\in\mathbb{N}
, and one may concern with the case where
K=K_{n}\to\infty
as well as
n\to\infty
. It is not a straightforward extension, even for consistency. For example, if we could show that, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\sup_{K\in\mathbb{N}}|\widehat{W}_{K}^{(q)}(x)-W_{K}^{(q)}(x)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}0,\quad n\to\infty,

then we can exchange the order of the limits
n\to\infty
and
K\to\infty
, which concludes that

\widehat{W}_{\infty}^{(q)}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}W_{\infty}^{(q)}(x)
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. On this point, we need further study.
As for the extension of the asymptotic normality, we need a more complicated discussion for extending to the case where
K
depends on
n
with
K=K_{n}\to\infty
. It will become a high-dimensional setting, and we need a high-dimensional central limit theorem for triangular arrays without even a martingale property. A sophisticated CLT would still need to be proven.

A.2
Estimator of
\nu
-functionals

First, we would like to estimate the integral-type functional
\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is a
\nu
-integrable function with an unknown parameter
\theta\in\Theta
:

\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}):=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\theta}^{(1)}(z)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z),\dots,\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{\theta}^{(d)}(z)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\right)

where
\Theta
is an open and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that the parameter
\theta
can be a variety of parameters depending on the context.
For instance, we will find that the parameter
p:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{f}_{q}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z
, the coefficients of Laguerre expansion, e.g.,
a_{\alpha,k}^{f}
and
a_{\alpha,k}^{F}
, are all written in terms of the integral functional of
\nu
, later.

In short, we need to estimate the parameters
(D_{0},\gamma_{0},\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}}))
, where
\theta_{0}
is the true parameter of
\theta
.
Hereafter, we assume that there exists an open and bounded set
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

(D_{0},\gamma_{0},\theta_{0})\in\Theta_{1}\times\Theta_{2}.

Moreover, we make the following assumptions on an integrands
\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}
, which are applied to a variety of
\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}
, locally in this section.

H1[
\delta
]

: For each
\theta\in\Theta
, there exists a
\delta\geq 0
such that
\nu(|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}|\vee|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}|^{2+\delta})<\infty

H2

:
\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in\overline{\Theta}}\nu(|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}|\vee|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}|^{2})<\infty
.

H3

: There exists a
\nu
-integrable function
h_{1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{\theta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}(z)|\leq h_{1}(z)

H4

: There exists a function
h_{2}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\nu(h_{2}\vee h_{2}^{2})<\infty
such that, for any
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,

\sup_{\theta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta+\kappa}(z)-\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}(z)|\leq h_{2}(z)|\kappa|.

H5

: For each
i=1,\dots,d
,

\int_{0}^{{\epsilon}_{n}}H_{\theta}^{(i)}(z)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)=o(T_{n}^{-1/2}).

As for functionals
\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})
, we can use the following threshold-type estimator:

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}):=\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{t\in(0,T_{n}]}\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}(\Delta L_{t})\mathbf{1}_{\{\Delta L_{t}>{\epsilon}_{n}\}}

Lemma A.3

(Shimizu
[
22
]
, Propositions 3.1 and 3.2)
.

(i)

Under the assumption H1[0], we have

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}),\quad\theta\in\Theta.

In addition, assuming further H2 and H4, we have the uniform consistency:

\sup_{\theta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})-\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})|\to 0.

(ii)

Under H1[
\delta
] for some
\delta>0
and H5, we have

\sqrt{T_{n}}(\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})-\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}N_{d}(\mbox{\boldmath$0$}_{d},\Sigma_{\theta}),\quad\theta\in\Theta,

where
\Sigma_{\theta}=\left(\nu(H_{\theta}^{(i)}H_{\theta}^{(j)})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
.

It will be easy to see the following version of the continuous mapping-type theorem holds for the estimator
\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})
:

Corollary A.4
.

Under the assumptions H1[0], H2–H4, it follows that

\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}}),

for any random sequence such that
\widehat{\theta}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\theta_{0}\in\Theta
.

Proof.

Note that there exists a sub-sub sequence
\{\widehat{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\}
for any subsequence of
\{\widehat{\theta}_{n}\}
such that
\widehat{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}\to\theta_{0}\ a.s.
.
Then, under H3, we can apply the Lebesgue convergence theorem to obtain that
\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n^{\prime}}})\to\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}})\ \ a.s.

That is, the sequence
\{\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})\}
has a sub-subsequence that converges to
\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}})
almost surely,
which implies that
\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}})
.
Moreover, since
{\mathbb{P}}(\widehat{\theta}_{n}\notin\Theta)\to 0
, it follows from Lemma
A.3
that, for any
{\epsilon}>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})-\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}})|>{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{\theta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\widehat{\nu}_{n}(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})-\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta})|>{\epsilon}/2,\ \widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\Theta\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(|\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\widehat{\theta}_{n}})-\nu(\mbox{\boldmath$H$}_{\theta_{0}})|>{\epsilon}/2,\ \widehat{\theta}_{n}\in\Theta\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{n}\notin\Theta\right)\to 0.

This completes the proof.

Lemma C.1
.

Under NPC, the probability density

is well-defined and uniformly bounded.

Proof.

We prove only the case where

, and the one for

is similarly proved.

First, we see from Lemma 3.2 in Feng and Shimizu
[
9

under the net profit condition NPC. Therefore

Moreover, since

for

it follows that
f_{q}
is uniformly bounded as follows: for any

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=D^{-1}\int_{0}^{x}\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}e^{-\beta(x-y)}e^{-\gamma(z-y)}\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle\leq D^{-1}\int_{0}^{x}\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

∎

Lemma C.2
.

For
F_{q}
and
G_{q}
, their tail functions satisfy that
\overline{F}_{q},\ \overline{G}_{q}\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for any

.

Since

x\in\mathbb{R}
,

The Fubini theorem yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{F}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{u}^{\infty}f_{q}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}u=\int_{0}^{\infty}xf_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}xe^{-\beta(x-y)}e^{-\gamma(z-y)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-\gamma z})\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)+\frac{1}{\beta\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

Moreover, taking the Laplace transform of the DRE (
2.4
) for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sx}\overline{G}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{p\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sx}f_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{1-p\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-sx}\overline{F}_{q}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}.

\displaystyle\big|H^{F}_{\alpha,k}(z;\theta+\kappa)

\displaystyle-H^{F}_{\alpha,k}(z;\theta)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\alpha}\left|\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}x\left(\frac{1}{D+\kappa_{2}}h_{\theta+\kappa}(x,y,z)-\frac{1}{D}h_{\theta}(x,y,z)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\alpha}\left|\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{D+\kappa_{2}}-\frac{1}{D}\right)xh_{\theta+\kappa}(x,y,z)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\left|\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{1}{D}(xh_{\theta+\kappa}(x,y,z)-xh_{\theta}(x,y,z))\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{D}-\frac{1}{D+\kappa_{2}}\right)xe^{-\frac{c}{D+\kappa_{2}}(x-y)}e^{-\gamma(z-y)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{c}\int_{0}^{z}\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{y}^{\infty}\frac{1}{D}\left(xe^{-\frac{c}{D+\kappa_{2}}(x-y)}-xe^{-\frac{c}{D}(x-y)}\right)e^{-\gamma(z-y)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{c\eta_{2}}\left(\frac{2}{\eta_{1}}z+\left(\frac{5}{c\eta_{2}}-2\eta_{1}\right)\frac{1}{\eta_{1}}\left(1-e^{-\eta_{1}^{-1}z}\right)\right)|\kappa|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\kappa|z,

uniformly in

.

Other proofs can be done similarly, so we omit them and end the proof.

C.5
Proof of Lemma
2.5

We will show only the case where
D>0
. The same calculation applies for
D=0
.
According to Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we should show that
f:=\overline{G}_{q}
satisfies (
2.3
).

Let
{\epsilon},M>0
be any constants with
{\epsilon}<M
.
We firstly show that the infinite sum
\overline{G}_{q}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-p)p^{k}\int_{x}^{\infty}f_{q}^{*k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z
can be differentiable under the summation sign on
({\epsilon},M)
.

We shall put the partial sum
g_{N}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(1-p)p^{k}\int_{x}^{\infty}f_{q}^{*k}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z
for an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and note that
g_{N}(x)\to\overline{G}_{q}(x)\ (N\to\infty)
for all
x>0
.
Next, note the differential formula for the convolution: for functions
f,g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(f*g)(x)=(f*\partial_{x}g)(x)+f(x)g(0),

In particular, if
f(0)=0
, then it follows for
k\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}f^{*k}=f*\partial_{x}f^{*(k-1)}=\dots=f^{*(k-1)}*\partial_{x}f,

(C.2)

by induction. Using this formula, we have for
m=1,2,\dots
that

\displaystyle\partial^{m}_{x}g_{N}(x)=p(1-p^{m-2})\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x)-p^{m-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-m+1}(1-p)p^{k}f_{q}^{*k}*\partial^{m-1}_{x}f_{q}(x),

where we regard
\partial_{x}^{0}f_{q}\equiv 0
as a convention.
Now,
f_{q}(x)
is bounded by Lemma
C.1
, so
f_{q}^{*k}\ (k=1,2,\dots)
is also bounded.
Moreover, it also follows that
\partial^{m}_{x}f_{q}
is bounded on
({\epsilon},M)
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}f_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{pD}\left[-\beta f_{q}(x)+\overline{\nu}(x)+\int_{x}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right],

and we see by induction that, for any
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x)\right|\lesssim 1+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left|\partial_{x}^{k-1}\overline{\nu}(x)\right|+\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right|.

and it follows for any
{\epsilon}>0
that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in({\epsilon},M)}|\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\sup_{x\in({\epsilon},M)}\left|\partial_{x}^{k-1}\overline{\nu}(x)\right|+\int_{M}^{\infty}\overline{\nu}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\lesssim 1+M^{C},

(C.3)

by the assumption (
2.7
).
Therefore we can confirm the sequnece
\{\partial^{m}_{x}g_{N}(x)\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniform Cauchy sequence on
({\epsilon},\infty)
:

\lim_{N,N^{\prime}\to\infty}\sup_{x\in({\epsilon},M)}|\partial^{m}_{x}g_{N}(x)-\partial^{m}_{x}g_{N^{\prime}}(x)|=0,

Hence “the term differential theorem” says that, for any
{\epsilon},M>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\partial^{m}_{x}g_{N}(x)=\partial_{x}^{m}\overline{G}_{q}(x),\quad x\in({\epsilon},M).

Therefore, it follows for any
x>0
that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{m}\overline{G}_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=p(1-p^{m-2})\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x)-p^{m-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-p)p^{k}f_{q}^{*k}\right)*\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}

\displaystyle=p(1-p^{m-2})\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x)-p^{m-1}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x-Z)\right],

(C.4)

where
Z
is a random variable with the distribution
G_{q}
whose probability density is given by
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-p)p^{k}f_{q}^{*k}(x)
.

Now, as for the second term in the last right-hand side of (
C.4
), we see that

\mathbb{E}\left[\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x-Z)\right]=O\left(1+x^{\kappa}\right),\quad x\to\infty

Indeed, it follows from (
C.3
) that

\sup_{x>{\epsilon}}\frac{\left|\partial_{x}^{m-1}f_{q}(x-Z)\right|}{1+x^{C}}\lesssim\sup_{x>{\epsilon}}\frac{1+x^{C}+Z^{C}}{1+x^{C}}\leq 1+|Z|^{C},

and the last term is integrable by the assumption. Then, the Lebesgue convergence theorem and the equality (

) yield that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{m}\overline{G}_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=O\left(1+x^{\kappa}\right),\quad x\to\infty,

which implies that

\alpha>0

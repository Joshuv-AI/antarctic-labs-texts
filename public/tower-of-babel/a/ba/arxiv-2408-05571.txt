Amenable actions of compact and discrete quantum groups on von Neumann algebras
K. De Commer and J. De Ro

Abstract
. Let G \mathbb{G} be a compact or a discrete quantum group and A ⊆ B A\subseteq B an inclusion of σ \sigma -finite G \mathbb{G} -dynamical von Neumann algebras. We prove that the G \mathbb{G} -inclusion A ⊆ B A\subseteq B is strongly equivariantly amenable if and only if it is equivariantly amenable, using techniques from the theory of non-commutative L p L^{p} -spaces. In particular, if ( A , α ) (A,\alpha) is a G \mathbb{G} -dynamical von Neumann algebra with A A σ \sigma -finite, the action α : A ↶ G \alpha:A\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} is strongly (inner) amenable if and only if the action α : A ↶ G \alpha:A\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G} is (inner) amenable. This result can be seen as a dynamical generalization of Tomatsu’s result on the amenability/co-amenability duality for discrete/compact quantum groups. We also provide the first explicit examples of amenable discrete quantum groups that act non-amenably on a von Neumann algebra.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

The notion of Zimmer amenability of an action of a locally compact group
G
on a von Neumann algebra
A
was introduced and studied in the papers
[
AD79
,
AD82
]
. It plays an important role in the study of the von Neumann algebra crossed product
A\rtimes G
. For example, if the von Neumann algebra
A
is injective and the action
A\curvearrowleft G
is amenable, then
A\rtimes G
is an injective von Neumann algebra. Further, the notion of Zimmer amenability provides a bridge to the notion of amenability for
C^{*}
-dynamical systems
[
BEW21
,
OS21
,
BC22
]
. For these reasons, it is natural to generalize the notion of Zimmer amenability to the setting of locally compact quantum groups. This was first done and studied in
[
Moa18
]
for discrete quantum groups and in
[
DCDR24
,
DR24
]
for general locally compact quantum groups. In this paper, we continue the study of the notion of amenability of actions, but we restrict ourselves to the setting of compact and discrete quantum groups. We will make extensive use of the general theory developed in
[
DCDR24
]
.

More concretely, in
[
DCDR24
]
, the notion of equivariant correspondence was introduced. Given a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
and two
\mathbb{G}
-

-dynamical systems

and
(B,\beta)
(= von Neumann algebras with an action

by
\mathbb{G}
), a
\mathbb{G}
-
A
-
B
-correspondence consists of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with an
A
-
B
-bimodule structure by unital normal
*
-preserving maps, and equipped with a unitary
\mathbb{G}
-representation which is compatible with the
A
-
B
-bimodule structure
[
DCDR24
]
*Definition 0.4. This notion was directly inspired by a very similar notion in the setting of compact quantum groups, introduced in
[
AS21
]
. We will comment in more detail on the precise connection in Section
2
. We will write
\mathrm{Corr}^{\mathbb{G}}(A,B)
for the
W
∗
-category of all such
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant
A
-
B
-correspondences.

There is a natural notion of weak containment
\preccurlyeq
for
\mathbb{G}
-correspondences

DCDR24
]
*Definition 3.1. This notion can be used to formulate several important approximation properties for dynamical von Neumann algebras. For example, if

is a

-equivariant inclusion, we call it

if we have the weak containment

of
\mathbb{G}
-
A
-

-correspondences

]
*Definition 4.1.

Given a

-

-correspondence

-

-

More precisely, let

be a compact quantum group with associated Hopf

-

-algebra

for its algebraic core (with

is the conjugate algebra of

as the

\mathcal{A}\square\overline{\mathcal{A}}:=\{z\in\mathcal{A}\odot\overline{\mathcal{A}}:(\alpha\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\alpha_{c})(z)\}.

We will prove that every

-

-
A
-correspondence

.
Let

be a compact quantum group, let

be a
\mathbb{G}
-

-algebra and

. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

as

-

-

-correspondences.

This significant result provides a more algebraic way to see if a given equivariant correspondence weakly contains the trivial correspondence. It provides the basis for the remainder of the paper.

For a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
, let us also recall that a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant normal inclusion
A\subseteq B
is called

if there exists a (not necessarily normal)
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant ucp conditional expectation

.
As the terminology suggests, strong equivariant amenability of a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant inclusion

implies its equivariant amenability, but the converse is an important open question. In the case of compact quantum groups however, we make use of Proposition

to establish the following general result:

Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group. Assume
A\subseteq B
is an equivariant unital normal inclusion of
\sigma
-finite
\mathbb{G}
-dynamical von Neumann algebras. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A\subseteq B
is equivariantly amenable.

(2)

A\subseteq B
is strongly equivariantly amenable.

In the non-equivariant setting, this was proven in
[
BMO20
]
*Corollary A.2. We use Proposition
2.11
and
the theory of non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces
[
JP10
]
to lift this result to the equivariant setting.

Using duality arguments, we then obtain the same result for discrete quantum groups:

Theorem
3.5

Let
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
be a discrete quantum group and assume
A\subseteq B
is an equivariant unital normal inclusion of
\sigma
-finite
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
-dynamical von Neumann algebras. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A\subseteq B
is equivariantly amenable.

(2)

A\subseteq B
is strongly equivariantly amenable.

Let us now recall from
[
DCDR24
]
the following definitions: for a locally compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
, a
\mathbb{G}
-
W^{*}
-algebra
(A,\alpha)
is said to be

•

(strongly) amenable
if the
\mathbb{G}
-inclusion
\alpha(A)\subseteq A\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is (strongly) equivariantly amenable, and

•

(strongly) inner amenable
if the
\mathbb{G}
-inclusion
\alpha(A)\subseteq A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
is (strongly) equivariantly amenable (where
A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}
is equipped with the adjoint
\mathbb{G}
-action).

If these conditions hold for
A=\mathbb{C}
, we say that
\mathbb{G}
is (strongly) amenable, resp. (strongly) inner amenable. Note that this is consistent with the usual definitions for these notions
[
Tom06
,
Cr19
]
.

As an application of Theorem
3.1
, we then obtain:

Theorem
3.6

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a compact quantum group and let
(A,\alpha)
be a
\sigma
-finite
\mathbb{G}
-
W^{*}
-algebra. The following statements hold:

(1)

(A,\alpha)
is strongly amenable if and only if
(A,\alpha)
is amenable.

(2)

(A,\alpha)
is strongly inner amenable if and only if
(A,\alpha)
is inner amenable.

By duality, we then also obtain:

Theorem
3.7

Let
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
be a discrete quantum group and
(A,\alpha)
a
\sigma
-finite
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
-
W^{*}
-algebra. The following statements hold:

(1)

(A,\alpha)
is strongly amenable if and only if
(A,\alpha)
is amenable.

(2)

(A,\alpha)
is strongly inner amenable if and only if
(A,\alpha)
is inner amenable.

In particular,
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
is strongly inner amenable if and only if
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
is inner amenable.

Note that applying Theorem
3.7
.(1) to the trivial action
\mathbb{C}\curvearrowleft\mathbbl{\Gamma}
recovers Tomatsu’s result
[
Tom06
]
, which asserts that
\check{\mathbbl{\Gamma}}
is co-amenable if and only if
\mathbbl{\Gamma}
is amenable. Therefore, Theorem
3.7
can be seen as a dynamical version of Tomatsu’s result.

In
[
AD79
]
*Proposition 3.6, it was proven that every action of an amenable locally compact group on a von Neumann algebra is automatically amenable. An analogue of this statement is no longer true in the (non-Kac) quantum setting:

Theorem
4.4
.
Let
\mathbb{H}
be a compact quantum group and
\mathbb{G}:=\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{H}
. Consider the action

\alpha:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{\mathbb{G}})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})^{\bar{\otimes}3}:z\mapsto\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{(2)}(z)_{213}.

The action
\alpha:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is (strongly) amenable if and only if
\mathbb{H}
is of Kac type.

The proof strategy for this theorem is summarised at the beginning of section 4. We also provide another strategy that allows us to show that the action of
SU_{q}(2)
on a non-standard Podleś sphere is not amenable, see Proposition
4.5
.

Further, note that if
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group and the action
\alpha:A\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
fails to be amenable, then the dual action

A\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\curvearrowleft\check{\mathbb{G}}

also fails to be amenable. Therefore, from the discussion above, we find explicit examples of amenable discrete quantum groups acting non-amenably on a von Neumann algebra. Note that in
[
Moa18
]
*Theorem 4.7, it was claimed that every action of an amenable discrete quantum group is amenable. There is no contradiction however, as the proof in
[
Moa18
]
only works in the context of unimodular discrete quantum groups, as was remarked in
[
AK24
]
.

-W

(2.7)

The implication (

3.1.
Norm estimates for

\mathrm{tr}(h)=\omega(1)\textrm{ if }h\in L^{1}_{+}(A),\omega\in M_{*}^{+}\textrm{ and }\omega\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\sigma}_{t}^{\phi}(x)\rd t\right)=\tau(xh),\qquad x\in c_{\phi}(A)^{+},

In particular,

will be a Hilbert space. It can be identified with the standard Hilbert space for

and

are through left and right multiplication, while the modular conjugation and standard cone

are defined through

J_{A}a=a^{*},\qquad P^{\natural}=\{x^{*}x\mid x\in L^{4}(A)\},\qquad a\in L^{2}(A).

We recall that

for all

, and that any element of the real span of

can be written in a unique way as a difference of orthogonal elements in

Let now
A_{0}\subseteq A
be a unital, normal von Neumann subalgebra together with a faithful normal conditional expectation

We may and will assume that
\phi=\phi\circ P
. We write
\phi_{0}=\phi_{\mid A_{0}}
.

We can identify

. If we want to emphasize this inclusion map, we write it as

Similarly, the map

-spaces, which we write

P_{p}:L^{p}(A)\rightarrow L^{p}(A_{0}),\qquad a\mapsto P(a).

Here we extend

naturally to a conditional expectation

(well-defined as

), which we then in turn may extend to the
*

\tau

With

while

, using that

and

P(axb)=aP(x)b,\qquad a\in L^{p}(A_{0}),b\in L^{q}(A_{0}),x\in L^{r}(A),\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1

Consider now on

4.
Examples of non-amenable actions

In this section, we provide examples of non-amenable actions of co-amenable compact and amenable discrete quantum groups. We start with some general remarks:

•

If
\mathbb{G}
is a locally compact quantum group, then the translation action of
\mathbb{G}
on itself is amenable if and only if the dual quantum group
\check{\mathbb{G}}
is inner amenable, and this holds automatically if

is co-amenable (
[

]

[

]

is a discrete quantum group that is not inner amenable for an appropriate matrix

).

•

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group of Kac type, then every action of
\mathbb{G}
is amenable, as easily follows by combining
[
DR24
]
*Lemma 6.12 and
[
DCDR24
]
*Theorem 6.12. It is also possible to prove this directly using modular theory, as communicated to us by S. Vaes.

•

If
\mathbb{G}
is a compact quantum group and

an action that is not amenable, then the induced action

is not inner amenable
[
DCDR24
]

]
*Proposition 6.9. Thus, any example of a co-amenable compact quantum group acting non-amenably on a von Neumann algebra gives an example of an amenable discrete quantum group acting non-amenably on a von Neumann algebra.

We now provide two strategies that can be useful to detect (non-)amenability of a given action of a compact quantum group on a von Neumann algebra, and apply these strategies to concrete examples. This will yield the first examples of co-amenable compact quantum groups acting non-amenably on von Neumann algebras (and by duality, also the first examples of amenable discrete quantum groups acting non-amenably on von Neumann algebras).

\pi_{S}(a)\delta^{1/2}(b_{(1)}^{*})\rho_{S}(b_{(0)}^{*})(\Lambda_{A}(c)\otimes\Lambda(1))=\Lambda_{A}(a_{(0)}c\sigma_{-i/2}^{A}(b_{(0)}^{*}))\otimes\Lambda(a_{(1)}\sigma_{-i/2}(b_{(1)}^{*}))\delta^{1/2}(b_{(2)}^{*}).

(4.4)

If

\displaystyle\sum_{j}a_{j,(0)}\otimes a_{j,(1)}\otimes R(a_{j,(2)})\otimes b_{j,(0)}^{*}\otimes b_{j,(1)}^{*}=\sum_{j}a_{j,(0)}\otimes a_{j,(1)}\otimes b_{j,(2)}^{*}\otimes b_{j,(0)}^{*}\otimes b_{j,(1)}^{*},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\theta_{\square}^{S_{A}{G}}(\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes\overline{b_{j}})(\Lambda_{A}(c)\otimes\Lambda(1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\Lambda_{A}(a_{j,(0)}c\sigma_{-i/2}^{A}(b_{j,(0)}^{*}))\otimes\delta^{-1/2}(a_{j,(2)})\Lambda(a_{j,(1)}\sigma_{-i/2}(b_{j,(1)}^{*})).

Therefore,

\theta_{S}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\otimes\overline{b_{j}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{A}(a_{j,(0)})\rho_{A}(b_{j,(0)}^{*})\Phi{G}(b_{j,(1)}^{*}\kappa_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})}(a_{j,(1)})).

∎

Fix a compact quantun group
\mathbb{H}
and consider the compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}:=\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{H}
. Concretely,

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),\quad\Delta{G}=(\id\otimes\varsigma\otimes\id)\circ(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}),

with
\varsigma
the flip. Then with
\Delta^{(2)}=(\Delta\otimes\id)\Delta
, it is easily verified that

\alpha:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\bar{\otimes}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})^{\bar{\otimes}3}:x\mapsto\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{(2)}(x)_{213}

defines an ergodic right action
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
. On the algebraic regular elements, the coaction restricts to

\alpha:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G}):x\mapsto x_{(2)}\otimes(x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(3)}).

To make the connection with the previous notations, write

A:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}),\quad\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

The associated left action
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}
is given by

\alpha_{c}:\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\odot\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}:\overline{x}\mapsto R(x_{(1)}^{*})\otimes R(x_{(3)}^{*})\otimes\overline{x_{(2)}}.

Given
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{H})
, we recall the notation
n_{\pi}=\dim(\pi)=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
. Consider then the character

\chi(\pi):=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(e_{i}^{\pi},e_{i}^{\pi})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

The elements
\{\chi(\pi)\}_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{H})}
are linearly independent. We write

\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]=\operatorname{span}\{\chi(\pi):\pi\in\Irr(\mathbb{H})\}=\{x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}):\Delta(x)=\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(x)\}

for the
fusion algebra
of
\mathbb{H}
. It is a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The map

\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}:\chi(\pi)\mapsto\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})\otimes\overline{U_{\overline{\pi}}(\overline{e_{j}^{\pi}},\overline{e_{k}^{\pi}})^{*}}

is an isomorphism of unital
*
-algebras.

Proof.

We have a
*
-isomorphism

\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}):\overline{x}\mapsto R(x^{*}),

and with respect to this isomorphism, we get an induced action
\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
given by

\beta:\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{G})\odot\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}):x\mapsto(x_{(3)}\otimes x_{(1)})\otimes x_{(2)}.

We then obtain an induced isomorphism

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

We claim that
\Delta{H}(\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}])\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
. Indeed, if
z\in\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]
, then

\displaystyle(\alpha\odot\id)\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}(z)=z_{(2)}\otimes z_{(1)}\otimes z_{(3)}\otimes z_{(4)},

\displaystyle(\id\odot\beta)\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}(z)=z_{(1)}\otimes z_{(4)}\otimes z_{(2)}\otimes z_{(3)}.

Since
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}(z)=\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{op}}(z)
, these expressions are equal. Conversely, let
z\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
, i.e.

(\alpha\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\beta)(z).

Applying
\id\odot\varepsilon\odot\id\odot\id
to this expression, we get
(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\Delta{H})(z)
. On the other hand, applying
\id\odot\id\odot\varepsilon\odot\id
to the same expression yields
(\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{op}}\odot\id)(z)=(\id\odot\Delta{H}^{\mathrm{op}})(z)
. Put
w:=(\id\odot\varepsilon)(z)\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
. We see that
z=\Delta{H}(w)=\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}^{\mathrm{op}}(w)
, and thus
w\in\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]
. It follows that the maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id\odot\varepsilon{H}}

are inverse to each other. Composing the isomorphisms

\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})},

we obtain the map

\chi(\pi)\mapsto\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})\otimes\overline{R(U_{\pi}(e_{k}^{\pi},e_{j}^{\pi})^{*})}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n_{\pi}}U_{\pi}(e_{j}^{\pi},e_{k}^{\pi})\otimes\overline{U_{\overline{\pi}}(\overline{e_{j}^{\pi}},\overline{e_{k}^{\pi}})^{*}}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

(1)

The
*
-representation
\epsilon_{A}:\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

\epsilon_{A}(\chi(\pi))=\dim_{q}(\pi),\quad\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{H}).

(2)

The
*
-representation
\theta_{S}:\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))
coincides with the restriction of the
*
-representation

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H})):x\mapsto\pi_{\mathbb{H}}(x_{(1)})\rho_{\mathbb{H}}(R(x_{(2)})).

Proof.

(1) We note that, e.g. by the formula

, we have the equality of Woronowicz characters
\delta_{\mathbb{H}^{\mathrm{op}}}=\delta_{\mathbb{H}}
, and so

\kappa_{A}(x)=\sigma_{-i/2}(x_{(2)})\delta^{-1/2}(x_{(1)})\delta^{-1/2}(x_{(3)})=\delta^{-3/4}(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\delta^{-3/4}(x_{(3)}),\quad x\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}).

A calculation then shows that

\kappa_{A}(U_{\pi}(\xi,\eta))=U_{\pi}(\delta^{-3/4}\xi,\delta^{-3/4}\eta),\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

Therefore, the
*
-character

\epsilon_{A}:\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\cong\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\overset{\mathbb{G}}{\square}\overline{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})}\to\mathbb{C}

is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{A}(\chi(\pi))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}\Phi_{\mathbb{H}}(U_{\overline{\pi}}(\overline{e_{j}^{\pi}},\overline{e_{k}^{\pi}})U_{\pi}(\delta^{-3/4}e_{j}^{\pi},\delta^{-3/4}e_{k}^{\pi}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}\Phi_{\mathbb{H}}(U_{\pi}(\delta^{-1/4}e_{j}^{\pi},\delta^{1/4}e_{k}^{\pi})^{*}U_{\pi}(\delta^{-3/4}e_{j}^{\pi},\delta^{-3/4}e_{k}^{\pi}))

Theorem 4.4
.

The action
\alpha:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is (strongly)
\mathbb{G}
-amenable if and only if
\mathbb{H}
is of Kac type.

Proof.

Suppose that the action
\alpha:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})\curvearrowleft\mathbb{G}
is amenable. Then the
*
-representation

\theta_{S}:C_{u}^{*}(\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}])\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))

weakly contains the
*
-representation

\epsilon_{A}:C_{u}^{*}(\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}])\to\mathbb{C}

so in particular

|\epsilon_{A}(x)|\leq\|\theta_{S}(x)\|,\quad x\in\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}].

(4.6)

By Proposition
4.3
, the
*
-representation

\theta_{S}:\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))

arises as the restriction of a
*
-representation
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\to B(L^{2}(\mathbb{H}))
. Therefore, (
4.6
) shows that

|\epsilon_{A}(x)|\leq\|x\|_{u},\quad x\in\operatorname{Fus}[\mathbb{H}]

with
\|\cdot\|_{u}
the universal norm on
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
. In particular, if
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbb{H})
, taking

yields

\dim_{q}(\pi)\leq\|\chi(\pi)\|_{u}\leq n_{\pi}\leq\dim_{q}(\pi),

so that
\mathbb{H}
is of Kac type (see e.g.
[
NT14
]
).
∎

4.2.
Second strategy

Let
\mathbb{G}

5.
Outlook

In this paper, we have shown that amenability and strong amenability coincide for compact/discrete quantum group actions on von Neumann algebras. We have then produced the first examples of amenable discrete quantum groups acting non-amenably on a von Neumann algebra.

We note that in
[
AV23
]
, a notion of co-amenability was introduced for coideals of compact quantum groups (=
embeddable
ergodic coactions), and of amenability for their dual discrete coideals. The connection to the amenability conditions studied in this paper remains to be explored.

We also note that in the ergodic case, direct connections can be made with the theory of tensor C
∗
-categories. Indeed, in this case ergodic actions of
\mathbb{G}
on von Neumann algebras are in one-to-one correspondence (up to appropriate isomorphism) with cyclic semisimple module W
∗
-categories
\mathcal{D}
for

]
. The
*
-algebra
\mathcal{A}\rtimes\mathbb{G}\ltimes\overline{\mathcal{A}}
should then, up to Morita equivalence, be definable directly from
\mathcal{C}\curvearrowright\mathcal{D}
as a ‘relative tube

-algebra’

. This would lead to a relative version of the theory developed in
[
PV15
,
GJ16
,
NY16
]
, with the tube
*
-algebra loc. cit. arising as

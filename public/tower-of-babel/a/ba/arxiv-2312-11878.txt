Nested homotopy models of finite metric spaces and their spectral homology
Sergei O. Ivanov

Abstract
. For a real r ≥ 0 , r\geq 0, we consider the notion of r r -homotopy equivalence in the category quasimetric spaces, which includes metric spaces and directed graphs. Introduction

1.1.
Background

For over a decade, two theories have been actively developed. The first theory is the theory of magnitude of metric spaces and their magnitude homology. This theory was developed by Leinster, Meckes, Shulman, Hepworth, Willerton, Yoshinaga, Asao and others

[

,

. It explores an invariant of a metric space called magnitude. On one hand, the magnitude is a real number, which is a continuous analogue of the cardinality of a finite set. On the other hand, it exhibits some properties similar to those of the Euler characteristic. The concept of magnitude is also related to the notion of diversity
[
22
]
,
[
19
]
. Subsequently it was discovered that it is possible to introduce a homology theory for metric spaces, so that for finite metric spaces the Betti numbers of this theory are related to the magnitude in the similar way as ordinary Betti numbers are related to the Euler characteristic. This homology theory is called magnitude homology.

The second theory is the GLMY-theory of digraphs. This theory was developed by Grigor’yan, Lin, Muranov, Yau and others
[
7
,

,
14

. It implements a homotopical approach to the theory of digraphs. Its central concept is the path homology of a digraph, also known as GLMY-homology. The path homology behaves in many ways similar to the homology of spaces. For example, there is an analogue of the Künneth formula; there is a notion of suspension of a digraph that shifts the path homology; there is a notion of the fundamental group of a digraph, whose abelianization equals the first path homology group, and so forth. The theory also has the notion of homotopy of morphisms in the category of digraphs, and path homology is homotopy invariant. Moreover, there is a theory of covering digraphs, analogous to the theory of covering spaces in algebraic topology
[
5
]
.

Asao later showed that for the case of digraphs, these two theories could be unified into a single theory
[
2
]
,

1

. He showed that each digraph

, whose first page is the magnitude homology and the second page contains the path homology as the zeroth row. This spectral sequence is called magnitude-path spectral sequence. He also defines the concept of

is an
r
-homotopy invariant. For
r=1

For a digraph

as the length of the shortest path from

g^{\prime}

Note that this distance is not necessarily symmetric and can be equal to infinity. However, it satisfies the triangle inequality and
d_{G}(g,g^{\prime})=0
if and only if
g=g^{\prime}.
Such generalizations of metric spaces are referred to as quasimetric spaces. We will work in the category of quasimetric spaces where morphisms are short maps.

The aim of our work is to study the concept of

-homotopy, introduced by Asao, for the case of quasimetric spaces and any real
r\geq 0.
We study this concept from two perspectives. First, we study finite quasimetric spaces that have the least cardinality among quasimetric spaces in a given
r

r
-homotopy equivalence for any quasimetric spaces, and study this invariant. Both parts are related to the magnitude-path spectral sequence: in the first part we obtain its decomposition into a direct sum of some smaller spectral sequences; in the second part we generalize its pages to the case of any quasimetric spaces.

Let

(1)

(2)

Two finite quasimetric spaces are

The

Note that we do not claim that

Using this we obtain that there is a finite number of real numbers

We call these points homotopy jumping points and set

Thus for any finite quasi-metric space

(1.2)

Unfortunately, the map

If

However, we do not claim that this decomposition is functorial by

We say that a finite digraph

Any finite digraph

1.3.
Spectral homology

The magnitude-path spectral sequence is defined for the discrete case of digraphs and its first page is the magnitude homology. However the magnitude homology is defined for all quasimetric spaces. Spectral homology is an attempt to define the magnitude-path spectral sequence for any quasimetric space so that its “first page” is the magnitude homology. In some sense the spectral homology is a “continuous” generalization of the magnitude-path spectral sequence. In order to define the spectral homology of a quasimetric space, we need to define the spectral homology of an
\mathbb{R}
-filtered chain complex so that the spectral homology is a generalization of pages of the spectral sequence associated with a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex. In other words, the spectral homology of an
\mathbb{R}
-filtered complex is an analogue of the spectral sequence of a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex.

For an
\mathbb{R}
-filtered chain complex
C
over a ring
\mathbb{K},
a real number
r\geq 0,
a non-empty interval

and a natural number

we define a
\mathbb{K}
-module

called spectral homology, or
r
-spectral homology, if we want to specify

The spectral homology generalizes the spectral sequence associated with a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex in the following sense. If

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex, then its
\mathbb{Z}
-filtration can be extended to an

In this case for a natural

Let us give the definition of

A left infinite interval in

is an interval of the form
(-\infty,a],(-\infty,a),\mathbb{R},\emptyset.
For a left infinite interval

If we present a non-empty interval

then

(1.5)

\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,I}(C)=\mathrm{Im}(H_{n}(F_{R}/F_{L-r})\longrightarrow H_{n}(F_{R+r}/F_{L})).

Moreover, there is another more technical equivalent definition of the spectral homology that shows that for

the module

is a subquotient of
\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,I}(C)

(see details in Subsection

The magnitude-path spectral sequence of a digraph

-filtered chain complex

which is called reachability chain complex (see

However, in this case we obtain an

-filtration on the chain complex

Then the spectral homology of

The spectral homology is natural by the interval

2.
r

2.1.
Quasimetric spaces

A
quasimetric space
is a set
X
equipped by a map
d:X\times X\to[0,+\infty]
such that the triangle inequality holds
d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
for any
x,y,z\in X
and
d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y.
Note that we allow the distance to be equal to infinity, and it is not necessary symmetric, but the distance between distinct points is positive. Two sources of examples of quasimetric spaces are metric spaces and directed graphs with the shortest path distance.

A map between two quasimetric spaces
\varphi:X\to Y
is called short (or 1-Lipschitz, or distance-decreasing), if

(2.1)

d(\varphi(x),\varphi(x^{\prime}))\leq d(x,x^{\prime})

for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X.
The set of all short maps from
X
to
Y
is denoted by
{\rm SMap}(X,Y).
The category of quasimetric spaces with short maps as morphisms is denoted by
{\sf QMet}.
Note that isomorphisms in this category are isometries (distance preserving bijections).

For two short maps
\varphi,\psi:X\to Y
we set

(2.2)

d(\varphi,\psi)=\underset{x}{\rm sup}\ d(\varphi(x),\psi(x)).

It is easy to see that this defines a structure of quasimetric space on
{\rm SMap}(X,Y).
Note that for any short maps
\beta:Y\to Z
and
\alpha:W\to X
we have

(2.3)

d(\beta\varphi\alpha,\beta\psi\alpha)\leq d(\varphi,\psi).

2.2.
r
-homotopy category

Let
r\geq 0
be a real number. We say that two points
x,x^{\prime}
of a quasimetric space
X
are
r
-homotopic, denoted by
x\sim_{r}x^{\prime},
if there is a sequence
x=x_{0},\dots,x_{n}=x^{\prime}
of points of
X
such that for each
0\leq i\leq n-1
we have either
d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq r
or
d(x_{i+1},x_{i})\leq r.
In other words,
\sim_{r}
is the minimal equivalence relation such that
d(x,x^{\prime})\leq r
implies
x\sim_{r}x^{\prime}.
We say that two short maps
\varphi,\psi:X\to Y
are
r
-homotopic if they are
r
-homotopic as points in
{\rm SMap}(X,Y).

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\alpha:W\to X,

\varphi,\psi:X\to Y
and
\beta:Y\to Z
are short maps between quasimetric spaces and
\varphi\sim_{r}\psi,
then
\beta\varphi\alpha\sim_{r}\beta\psi\alpha.
In other words,
\sim_{r}
is a congruence relation on the category
{\sf QMet}.

Proof.

Follows from (
2.3
).
∎

Since
\sim_{r}
is a congruence relation on
{\sf QMet},
we can define the
r
-homotopy category of quasimetric spaces as the quotient-category
{\sf QMet}/\sim_{r}
. We say that a short map is an
r
-homotopy equivalence, if its image in
{\sf QMet}/\sim_{r}
is an isomorphism, and two quasimetric spaces are
r
-homotopy equivalent, if they are isomorphic in
{\sf QMet}/\sim_{r}.

For a quasimetric space
X
we denote by
{\rm End}(X)
the monoid of endomorphisms of
X
in
{\sf QMet}.
We will also denote by
{\rm End}_{r}(X)
the submonoid consisting of short maps
\varphi:X\to X
such that
\varphi\sim_{r}1_{X}.

(2.4)

{\rm End}_{r}(X)=\mathrm{Ker}({\rm End}(X)\to{\rm End}_{{\sf QMet}/\sim_{r}}(X)).

2.3.
r
-deformation retracts

If
X
is a quasimetric space and
A\subseteq X
is a subset,
A
will be treated as a quasimetric space with the induced metric. We will denote by
\iota=\iota_{A,X}:A\to X
the canonical embedding. A

of a quasimetric space

is a subset
A\subseteq X
such that there exists a short map
\rho:X\to A
, called retraction, such that
\rho\iota=1_{A}
. We say that the retract is
proper
, if
A\neq X.
An

-deformation retract
is a retract

with a retraction
\rho
such that
\iota\rho\sim_{r}1_{X}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a finite quasimetric space and
\varphi\in{\rm End}_{r}(X)
. Then there exists a natural
n\geq 1
such that
\varphi^{n}
is idempotent, and for any such

the subset
\varphi^{n}(X)\subseteq X
is an

-deformation retract of

Proof.

Since
X
is finite, there exists such

that
\varphi^{n}
is an idempotent.
Lemma
2.1
implies that
1\sim_{r}\varphi\sim_{r}\varphi^{2}\sim_{r}\dots\sim_{r}\varphi^{n}.
Therefore
\varphi^{n}\sim_{r}1_{X}.
Set
A=\varphi^{n}(X).
Then we can present
\varphi^{n}
as the composition
\varphi^{n}=\iota\rho,
where
\iota:A\to X
is the canonical embedding and
\rho:X\to A
is some surjection. Since the quasimetric on

is induced from
X,
we obtain that
\rho
is a short map. Note that
\iota\rho\sim_{r}1_{X}.
Since
\varphi^{n}
is idempotent, we have
\iota\rho\iota\rho=\iota\rho.
Using that
\iota
is injective, and
\rho
is surjective, we obtain
\rho\iota=1_{A}.

∎

2.4.

-minimal spaces

We say that a finite quasimetric space

is

-minimal, if it has no proper

-deformation retracts.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

be a finite quasimetric space. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

is

-minimal;

(2)

{\rm End}_{r}(X)\subseteq{\rm Aut}(X)
;

(3)

\varphi\in{\rm End}(X)
such that either

or
d(\varphi,1_{X})\leq r
is an automorphism.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2).
Take
\varphi\in{\rm End}_{r}(X).
Proposition
2.2
implies that there exists

such that
\varphi^{n}
is an idempotent and
\varphi^{n}(X)\subseteq X
is an
r
-deformation retract. Since
X
is
r
-minimal, we obtain
\varphi^{n}(X)=X.
Using that
X
is finite, we obtain that
\varphi^{n}
is a bijection and an isometry.
Combining this with the fact that
\varphi
an idempotent, we obtain
\varphi^{n}=1_{X}.
Therefore

is an automorphism.

(2)\Rightarrow(3).
Obvious.

(3)\Rightarrow(1).
Consider an
r
-deformation retract

with a retraction
\rho:X\to A.
We need to prove that

Since
\iota\rho\sim_{r}1_{X},
there exists a sequence

such that for any

or

is an automorphism. The base of induction

\varphi_{i}
is an automorphism. If

then

and hence,
\varphi_{i+1}\varphi^{-1}_{i}
is an automorphism. It follows that
\varphi_{i+1}
is an automorphism. Since

is an automorphism,

∎

2.6.
Homotopy jumping points

For a finite quasimetric space
X
we consider a function from non-negative real numbers to the interval
c_{X}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
defined as the ratio of cardinalities

(2.11)

c_{X}(r)=\frac{|M_{r}(X)|}{|X|}.

Lemma 2.10
.

The function
c_{X}
is decreasing, lower semi-continuous and it has a finite number of values.

Proof.

If
r^{\prime}>r,
then
M_{r^{\prime}}(M_{r}(X))\cong M_{r^{\prime}}(X),
and hence
c_{X}(r^{\prime})\leq c_{X}(r).
Now assume that
r_{n}\to r
and prove that
c_{X}(r)\leq\underset{n\to+\infty}{\rm liminf}\>c_{X}(r_{n}).
Since
c_{X}
is decreasing, we can assume that
r_{n}>r
is an decreasing sequence. Since
X
is finite, for
n>\!>1
we have that for any two points
x,x^{\prime}
either
d(x,x^{\prime})\leq r
or
d(x,x^{\prime})>r_{n}.
Therefore, for
n>\!>1
the inequality
d(x,x^{\prime})\leq r_{n}
is equivalent to the inequality
d(x,x^{\prime})\leq r.
Hence for
n>\!>1
we have
M_{r}(X)=M_{r_{n}}(X)
and
c_{X}(r_{n})=c_{X}(r).

∎

Lemma
2.10
implies that
c_{X}
has a finite number of jumping points
0<r_{1}<\dots<r_{n}
such that

c_{X}(r_{i})<c_{X}(r)

for any
r<r_{i}.
We will call them homotopy jumping points of

3.
Magnitude-path spectral sequence of a digraph

3.1.
r
-minimal models of digraphs

We define a digraph as a pair
G=(G_{0},G_{1}),
where
G_{1}\subseteq G_{0}\times G_{0}
such that
(g,g)\in G_{1}
for any
g\in G_{0}.
Elements of
G_{0}
are called vertices, elements of
G_{1}
are called arrows, arrows of the form
(g,g)
are called degenerate arrows. A morphism of digraphs
\varphi:G\to H
is a map
\varphi:G_{0}\to H_{0}
such that
(g,g^{\prime})\in G_{1}
implies
(\varphi(g),\varphi(g^{\prime}))\in H_{1}.
The category of digraphs is denoted by
{\sf Digr}.
A path in a digraph
G
is a tuple
(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})
such that
(g_{i},g_{i+1})\in G_{1}.
Note that any morphism sends a path to a path.

Any digraph
G
defines a quasimetric space, that we denote by
{\rm Q}(G),
whose set of points is
G_{0}
and the distance from
g
to
g^{\prime}
is defined as the infimum of the lengths of paths from
g
to
g^{\prime}.
Therefore we get a functor

(3.1)

{\rm Q}:{\sf Digr}\longrightarrow{\sf QMet}.

It is easy to check that the functor is full and faithful. So we can identify
{\sf Digr}
with a full subcategory of
{\sf QMet}.
So we can define
r
-homotopic maps for digrphs.

A subdigraph
H
of a digraph
G
is called convex, if the shortest path distance on
H
coincides with the induced distance:
d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})=d_{G}(h,h^{\prime})
for any vertices
h,h^{\prime}
of
H.
A subdigraph
H\subseteq G
is called a retract, if there is a morphism of digraphs
\rho:G\to H
such that
\rho\iota=1_{H},
where
\iota:H\hookrightarrow G
is the canonical embedding.

Lemma 3.1
.

A retract of a digraph is a convex subdigraph.

Proof.

Let
H
be a retract of
G.

It is obvious that
d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})\geq d_{G}(h,h^{\prime})
for any
h,h^{\prime}\in H_{0}.
On the other hand, for any path
(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})
from
h
to
h^{\prime}
in
G
we have a path
(\rho(g_{0}),\dots,\rho(g_{n}))
from
h
to
h^{\prime}
in
H.
Hence
d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})\leq d_{G}(h,h^{\prime}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a digraph and
A
be a retract of
{\rm Q}(G).
Then
A={\rm Q}(H)
for a uniquely defined retract
H\subseteq G.

Proof.

Consider a subdigraph
H\subseteq G
such that
H_{0}=A
and
H_{1}=\{(h,h^{\prime})\mid d_{G}(h,h^{\prime})\leq 1\}.
Denote by
\rho:{\rm Q}(G)\to A
a retraction. If
(g,g^{\prime})\in G_{1},
then
d_{G}(g,g^{\prime})\leq 1,
and hence
(\rho(g),\rho(g^{\prime}))\in H_{1}.
So
\rho
defines a morphism of digraphs
\rho^{\prime}:G\to H.
Therefore
H
is a retract of
G.
In particular
H
is a convex subdigraph. It follows that the distance between points in
A
coincides with the distance in
{\rm Q}(H).

∎

Lemma
3.2
implies that for a finite digraph
G
and a natural
n
there is a digraph
M_{n}(G)
such that
{\rm Q}(M_{n}(G))=M_{n}({\rm Q}(G)),
that can be included into
G
as an
n
-deformation retract, but the embedding is not unique. Therefore for a finite digraph we obtain a nested sequence of convex sudigraphs

(3.2)

G\supseteq M_{1}(G)\supseteq M_{2}(G)\supseteq\dots,

where the inclusions are not uniquely defined, but the digraphs
M_{n}(G)
are defined uniquely up to isomorphism.

Reminder on the magnitude-path spectral sequence

Here we remind the construction and the main properties of the magnitude-path spectral sequence of a digraph that can be found in

[

,
[

]

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a commutative ring. For a set

we will denote by
\mathbb{K}\cdot S
the free
\mathbb{K}
-module freely generated by

For a digraph

we consider a filtered chain complex

over
\mathbb{K},
called reachability complex, whose

-th component is freely generated by tuples of vertices
(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})
without consecutive repetitions

such that

(3.3)

{\rm RC}_{n}(G)=\mathbb{K}\cdot\{(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})\mid d(g_{i},g_{i+1})<+\infty,g_{i}\neq g_{i+1}\}.

Tuples

\partial(g_{0},\dots,g_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(g_{0},\dots,\hat{g}_{i},\dots,g_{n}).

Its first page consists of the magnitude homology

the zero row of the second page is the path homology

(3.7)

is exhaustive and bounded below, the spectral sequence converges to the homology of the chain complex
[

(here by convergence we mean “unbounded” convergence in the sense of unbounded spectral sequences
[
25
, §5.2.11]
)

The reachability homology is equal to the homology of the reachability preorder

which is defined so that its elements are points of

and

if and only if

3.3.
Decomposition of the magnitude-path spectral sequence

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite digraph and let

{\rm hj}(G)=\{r_{1}<\dots<r_{n}\}

be the set of homotopy jumping points of
G
.
Then the magnitude-path spectral sequence
E(G)
can be decomposed into a direct sum of spectral sequences

(3.11)

E(G)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}E(G)_{[k]}\oplus E(G)_{[\infty]}

such that
E^{1+r_{k}}(G)_{[k]}=0
and for any
1\leq j\leq n
we have

(3.12)

E(M_{r_{j}}(G))\cong\bigoplus_{k=j+1}^{n}E(G)_{[k]}\oplus E(G)_{[\infty]}.

In particular,
E(M_{\rm stab}(G))=E(G)_{[\infty]}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that,
if
E
is a spectral sequence and
e:E\to E
is an idempotent morphism of spectral sequences, then
E
can be decomposed into a sum of spectral sequences
E=\mathrm{Ker}(e)\oplus\mathrm{Im}(e).
In other words, the category of spectral sequences is Karoubian. Therefore, if
E^{\prime}
is a retract of a spectral sequence
E,
then
E
is a direct sum of spectral sequences
E\cong E^{\prime}\oplus E^{\prime\prime}.

If
n=0,
then there is nothing to prove. Assume
n\geq 1.
Chose a sequence of nested minimal models

(3.13)

G=M_{r_{0}}(G)\supset M_{r_{1}}(G)\supset M_{r_{2}}(G)\supset\dots\supset M_{r_{n}}(G)

with retractions
\rho_{j}:M_{r_{j}}(G)\to M_{r_{j+1}}(G)
for
0\leq j\leq n-1.
Since
M_{r_{j+1}}(G)
is a retract in
M_{r_{j}}(G),
we obtain that
E(M_{r_{j+1}})
is a retract of
E(M_{r_{j}}).
Hence
E(M_{r_{j}})\cong E(G)_{[j+1]}\oplus E(M_{r_{j+1}}).
Then we obtain a decomposition (
3.11
) with the property (
3.12
). Since the embedding
M_{r_{j+1}}(G)\hookrightarrow M_{r_{j}}(G)
is an
r_{j+1}
-homotopy equivalence, we obtain that
E^{r_{j+1}+1}(M_{r_{j+1}}(G))\cong E^{r_{j+1}+1}(M_{r_{j}}(G)).
Therefore
E^{r_{j+1}+1}(G)_{[j+1]}=0.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

We do not claim that the decomposition in Theorem
3.3
is natural.

4.4.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a commutative ring and
X
be a quasimetric space. Define the
\mathbb{R}
-filtered reachability chain complex
{\rm RC}(X)
similarly to the case of digraphs. Its

-th component is freely generated by tuples

without consecutive repetitions

(4.47)

In this complex we identify sequences with consecutive repetitions with zero. Then the differential is defined by the formula

(4.48)

\partial(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

If we set

L(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i}d(x_{i},x_{i+1}),

the
\mathbb{R}
-filtration is defined so that for a real number
r
the component
F_{r}{\rm RC}_{n}(G)
is generated by tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})

I,
a real number

and natural

the spectral homology of

\mathbb{R}
-filtered chain complex

(4.49)

\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,I}(X)=\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,I}({\rm RC}(X)).

It is easy to see that these constructions are natural and they define functors

(4.50)

\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,I}:{\sf QMet}\longrightarrow{\sf Mod}(\mathbb{K}).

By Remark
4.6
the spectral homology is natural by the interval. In particular, we can consider the persistent spectral homology
{\rm PSH}^{r}_{n}(X)
which is a persistent module such that
{\rm PSH}^{r}_{n}(X)=\mathrm{SH}^{r}_{n,[0,\ell]}(X).

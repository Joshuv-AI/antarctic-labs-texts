Global Kuranishi charts and a product formula in symplectic Gromov–Witten theory
Amanda Hirschi, Mohan Swaminathan

Abstract
. We construct global Kuranishi charts for the moduli spaces of stable pseudoholomorphic maps to a closed symplectic manifold in all genera. This is used to prove a product formula for symplectic Gromov–Witten invariants. As a consequence we obtain a Künneth formula for quantum cohomology.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold. Let
\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(X,\omega)
be the space of
\omega
-tame almost complex structures and let
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(X,\omega)
. The fundamental object of study in Gromov–Witten theory, as introduced in
[
KM94
]
, is the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)
of stable
J
-holomorphic maps representing a class
A\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. To construct invariants using this moduli space, one must contend with two technical issues: lack of transversality and smoothness of gluing.

The first of these issues occurs when we have non-regular
J
-holomorphic curves
u\colon(C,j)\to X
, i.e., those for which the linearization of the Cauchy–Riemann operator
\bar{\partial}_{J,j}
at
u
is not surjective. In general, this issue persists even if we choose a generic
\omega
-tame
J
, due to the presence of multiply covered curves.

To explain the second issue, note that we need gluing theorems to show that the moduli space is a manifold (or orbifold) near nodal holomorphic curves, even when we have transversality. In general, determining whether two local charts for the moduli space constructed via gluing are smoothly compatible is very subtle.

When
(X,\omega)
is semi-positive and
J
is generic, one can control the codimension of the non-regular curves. In this situation,
[
RT95
,
RT97
]
gave a construction of Gromov–Witten invariants using pseudocycles. For general symplectic manifolds, the aforementioned issues require the use of
virtual techniques
. The last decades have seen the development of several virtual frameworks in symplectic geometry, see
[
LT98
,
FO99
,
CM07
,
HWZ17
,
MW17
,
Par16
,
IP19
]
for example. Most of these constructions begin by representing the moduli space locally using Kuranishi charts. They then employ delicate local-to-global arguments to extract from this a global invariant, called the virtual fundamental class, a generalization of the fundamental class for moduli spaces containing non-regular curves.

The recent work
[
AMS21
]
showed how to construct a global Kuranishi chart for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,A;J)
by building on ideas from
[
Sie98
]
. This eliminates the need for patching together local information and simplifies the definition of Gromov–Witten invariants considerably. A
global Kuranishi chart

\mathcal{K}
for a compact space
Z
consists of a finite-rank vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a manifold
\mathcal{T}
both equipped with an almost free action by a compact Lie group
G
and an equivariant section
\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{E}
, so that
\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(0)/G\cong Z
.
We refer to
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{E}
and
\mathfrak{s}
as the
thickening
,
obstruction bundle
and
obstruction section
respectively.
An
orientation
on
\mathcal{K}
consists of orientations on
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{E}
which are both
G
-invariant and an orientation on
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(G)
. The
virtual fundamental class

[Z]^{\text{vir}}_{\mathcal{K}}\in\check{H}^{\text{vdim}(Z)}(Z;\mathbb{Q})^{\vee}
associated to an oriented global Kuranishi chart
\mathcal{K}
for
Z
is defined to be the linear map

\check{H}^{\text{vdim}(Z)}(Z;\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{s}^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{E}/G}\cup(\cdot)}H_{c}^{\dim(\mathcal{T}/G)}(\mathcal{T}/G;\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{[\mathcal{T}/G]}\mathbb{Q}

where
\text{vdim}(Z):=\dim(\mathcal{T})-\dim(G)-\text{rank}(\mathcal{E})
is the
virtual dimension
of
Z
. Here
\tau_{\mathcal{E}/G}
is the Thom class of
\mathcal{E}/G
and
[\mathcal{T}/G]
is the fundamental class of
\mathcal{T}/G
in Borel–Moore homology; see §
2.3
for more details. The virtual fundamental class of
Z
can be thought of as the Euler class localized to the zero locus of
\mathfrak{s}
. As in
[
Par16
]
, we do not need
\mathcal{T}
to be a smooth manifold.

1.2.
Main results

In this article, we construct global Kuranishi charts for moduli spaces of stable pseudo-holomorphic maps, generalizing the work of
[
AMS21
]
to all genera.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.17
)
.

For integers
g,n\geq 0
and
A\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the moduli space of stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)
admits an oriented global Kuranishi chart
\mathcal{K}=(G,\mathcal{T},\mathcal{E},\mathfrak{s})
of the correct virtual dimension, depending on certain auxiliary data, but unique in the following sense.

(i)

Different choices of auxiliary data result in global Kuranishi charts which are related by certain equivalence moves that do not affect the virtual fundamental class.

(ii)

Given any other
J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}_{\tau}(X,\omega)
, there exist auxiliary data such that the associated global Kuranishi charts are cobordant.

In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)
admits a well-defined virtual fundamental class.

We have the natural map

\displaystyle\text{ev}\times\text{st}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{}X^{n}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

given by evaluation at the marked points and stabilization of the domain, where we follow the convention that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a point for
2g-2+n\leq 0
.
Using this map, we define the
Gromov–Witten class
of
(X,\omega)
to be

(1.2.1)

\text{GW}^{(X,\omega)}_{A,g,n}:=(\text{ev}\times\text{st})_{*}[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)]^{\text{vir}}\in H_{\text{vdim}}(X^{n}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q}).

This class contains the same information as the
Gromov–Witten homomorphism

\text{GW}^{(X,\omega)}_{A,g,n}\colon H^{*}(X^{n};\mathbb{Q})\to H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})

defined by the formula

\text{GW}^{(X,\omega)}_{A,g,n}(\alpha)=\text{PD}(\text{st}_{*}(\text{ev}^{*}\alpha\cap[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A;J)]^{\text{vir}})),

where
PD
denotes the Poincaré duality isomorphism with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients for orbifolds. The Kontsevich-Manin axioms in
[
KM94
]
are defined in terms of this homomorphism. They are satisfied by the invariants defined here, as was shown in
[
Hir23
]
by the first author. Specializing to
g=0
and
n=3
, we obtain the
small quantum cohomology ring

QH^{*}(X,\omega)
over the universal Novikov ring
\Lambda_{0}
as in
[
MS12
, Chapter 11]
.

The global Kuranishi charts of Theorem
1.1
can be shown to be equivalent in genus
0
to the global Kuranishi charts constructed by
[
AMS21
]
and thus, the associated Gromov–Witten invariants agree; see Remark
3.19
for more details.

Remark 1.2

(Smoothness)
.

The thickening
\mathcal{T}
we construct is not smooth, but it admits a topological submersion
\pi
to a smooth manifold
\mathcal{B}
and carries a
canonical
structure of a
rel–
C^{\infty}
manifold
over
\mathcal{B}
. This relies on the results of
[
Swa21
]
, recalled in §
2.2
and explained further in Appendix
B
.
In particular, we can use smoothing theory as in
[
AMS21
, §4.2]
to upgrade our construction to a smooth global Kuranishi chart for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,A,J)
. This allows for the definition of a Morava K-theory valued virtual fundamental class as in
[
AMS21
]
. Also, as explained in
[
BX22
]
, our global chart can be used as an input for the construction of
\mathbb{Z}
-valued Gromov–Witten type invariants in all genera.

As an application of the construction, we prove a product formula for the Gromov–Witten invariants of a product symplectic manifold. This result was conjectured in
[
KM94
]
and shown in
[
KM96
,
Beh99
]
in the algebraic setting. In the symplectic category,
[
RT95
]
prove the product formula for semi-positive symplectic manifolds in genus
0
. See
[
Hyv12
]
for related work.

Theorem 1.3

(Product formula, Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let
(X_{i},\omega_{i})
for
i=0,1
be closed symplectic manifolds and set
(X,\omega)=(X_{0},\omega_{0})\times(X_{1},\omega_{1})
. Let
A_{i}\in H_{2}(X_{i};\mathbb{Z})
and
g,n\geq 0
be such that
2g-2+n>0
and
(g,n)
is neither
(1,1)
nor
(2,0)
. Then, for any
\alpha_{i}\in H^{*}(X_{i}^{n};\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{({\text{pr}_{i}})_{*}A=A_{i}}{\text{GW}^{(X,\omega)}_{g,n,A}}(\alpha_{0}\times\alpha_{1})=\text{GW}^{(X_{0},\omega_{0})}_{g,n,A_{0}}(\alpha_{0})\smile\text{GW}^{(X_{1},\omega_{1})}_{g,n,A_{1}}(\alpha_{1}),

where the sum on the left side ranges over all
A\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
which map to
A_{i}
under the natural projection
\text{pr}_{i}:X_{0}\times X_{1}\to X_{i}
for
i=0,1
.

As a consequence of this product formula, we deduce a Künneth formula for quantum cohomology.

Corollary 1.4

(Corollary
7.2
)
.

The Künneth map induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle QH^{*}(X_{0})\otimes_{\Lambda_{0}}QH^{*}(X_{1})\to QH^{*}(X_{0}\times X_{1})

of
\Lambda_{0}
-algebras, where
\Lambda_{0}
is the universal Novikov ring.

1.3
is used crucially in
[
HW24
]
.

During the completion of this work, we were informed by the authors of
[
AMS21
]
about an independent construction of global Kuranishi charts for moduli spaces of stable maps of positive genus. This has now appeared in
[
AMS24
]
.

Remark 2.2

In

Lemma 2.3

(Polarization: existence)

There is a complex line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\to X

Proof.

By approximating

equipped with the metric

∎

Discussion 2.10
.

Let us now consider how to extend this construction to the case of stable maps of any genus. Let
u\colon C\to X
be a
J
-holomorphic stable map of positive genus.

First, the holomorphic line bundle
u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
may have non-vanishing
H^{1}
, i.e., the dimension of
H^{0}(C,u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
may jump. To resolve this issue, we replace
u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
by
(\omega_{C}\otimes(u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))^{\otimes 3})^{\otimes p}
for some

, where
\omega_{C}
denotes the dualizing line bundle of

. This ensures that the relevant

vanishes.

Second, even after replacing
u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
as above, the analogue of the forgetful map is not a topological submersion. To resolve this, we need to consider a version of Definition
2.4
where, in place of
u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
, we allow any holomorphic line bundle
L
on
C
which is topologically isomorphic to
(\omega_{C}\otimes(u^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))^{\otimes 3})^{\otimes p}
and then take a complex basis of
H^{0}(C,L)
.

\displaystyle f\otimes\overline{g}

The assertion on

Lemma 4.13

(Extension of sections)

Let

Lemma 4.14

(Achieving

-transversality for one stable map)
.

Let

Definition 4.15
.

For any integer

, define a

-vector bundle on

by

\displaystyle E_{l}=T_{X}\boxtimes({T^{*}}^{0,1}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(l)\otimes\overline{H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(l))}).

Using the
\mathbb{C}
-linear connections on all of the bundles involved, we obtain a splitting of the tangent bundle of

into the horizontal

\mathbb{P}^{N}
-directions and the vertical

-direction, i.e., we get an identification

(4.3.3)

\displaystyle T_{E_{l}}=\pi_{l}^{*}(T_{X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}}\oplus E_{l}),

where
\pi_{l}:E_{l}\to X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the projection. At any point
(x,y,\eta)\in E_{l}
, with

,

and
\eta\in(E_{l})_{(x,y)}
, we use this splitting of the tangent space of

to define the endomorphism

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{(x,y,\eta)}^{E_{l}}:=\begin{bmatrix}J_{x}&2J_{x}\langle\eta\rangle&0\\
0&J_{y}^{\mathbb{P}}&0\\
0&0&J^{E_{l}}_{(x,y)}\end{bmatrix}.

Here,
J^{\mathbb{P}}
is the standard complex structure on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
J^{E_{l}}
is the multiplication by
i
on the fibres of
E_{l}
and
\langle\eta\rangle:T_{\mathbb{P}^{N},y}\to T_{X,x}
is the
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear map obtained from
\eta
using the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(l)
.

We readily check that
\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}
squares to
-\text{Id}
and thus, defines an almost complex structure on

. It is also clear that the inclusion of

into

as the zero section is pseudo-holomorphic, as is the projection

.

The following statement is analogous to
[

, Lemma 6.18]
.

Lemma 4.16

(Gromov trick)
.

Let
C
be a Riemann surface and let
F:C\to E_{l}
be a smooth map. Let
u:C\to X
and
v:C\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the maps with
\pi_{l}\circ F=(u,v)
and let
\eta
be the smooth section of
(u,v)^{*}E_{l}
induced by
F
.

The map
F
is
\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}
-holomorphic if and only if

(1)

the map
v
is holomorphic,

(2)

we have
\eta\in H^{0}(C,u^{*}T_{X}\otimes v^{*}({T^{*}}^{0,1}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(l)))\otimes\overline{H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(l))}
and

(3)

the equation
\bar{\partial}_{J}u+\langle\eta\rangle\circ dv=0
is satisfied.

Proof.

Let
\nabla
be the pullback connection on
(u,v)^{*}E_{l}
and let
j_{C}
be the almost complex structure on
C
. With respect to the splitting (
4.3.3
) of
T_{E_{l}}
, we have

(4.3.4)

\displaystyle dF=\begin{bmatrix}du\\
dv\\
\nabla\eta\end{bmatrix}\,\Rightarrow\,\bar{\partial}_{\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}}F={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(dF+\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}\circ dF\circ j_{C})=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\partial}_{J}u+J\langle\eta\rangle\,dv\,j_{C}\\
\bar{\partial}_{J^{\mathbb{P}}}v\\
\nabla^{0,1}\eta\end{bmatrix},

from which the claim is immediate.
∎

Lemma 4.17

(Transversality in
E_{l}
is
l
-transversality)
.

Let
(u,\iota):C\to X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a framed stable map in
\mathcal{M}
. Regard
(u,\iota)
as a
\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}
-holomorphic map
F:C\to E_{l}
via the inclusion of
X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
into
E_{l}
as the zero section.

Then, the linearized Cauchy–Riemann operator
D(\bar{\partial}_{\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}})_{F}
is surjective if and only if the framed stable map is
(u,\iota):C\to X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
l
-transverse.

Proof.

Using (
4.3.4
), we will first compute the linearization

\displaystyle D(\bar{\partial}_{\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}})_{F}:\Omega^{0}(C,F^{*}T_{E_{l}})\to\Omega^{0,1}(\tilde{C},\tilde{F}^{*}T_{E_{l}}),

where
\tilde{F}
is the pullback of
F
to the normalization
\tilde{C}\to C
. Similarly, let
\iota_{\tilde{C}}
(resp.
\tilde{u}
) be the pullbacks of
\iota
(resp.
u
) to
\tilde{C}
. Since
F
maps into the zero section of
E_{l}
, we have a natural splitting
F^{*}T_{E_{l}}=u^{*}T_{X}\oplus\iota^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\oplus(u,\iota)^{*}E_{l}
into horizontal and vertical sub-bundles (defined without reference to the connection). This splitting coincides with the one induced by (
4.3.3
) and so, we need not distinguish between them in our computation below.

In terms of the notation of (
4.3.4
), we have
\eta=0
and
v=\iota
. Differentiating the expression in (
4.3.4
) in the
u
,
v
and
\eta
directions and writing the result as a matrix using the splitting
F^{*}T_{E_{l}}=u^{*}T_{X}\oplus\iota^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}\oplus(u,\iota)^{*}E_{l}
, we get

(4.3.5)

\displaystyle D(\bar{\partial}_{\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}})_{F}=\begin{bmatrix}D(\bar{\partial}_{J})_{u}&0&\langle\cdot\rangle\,d\iota_{\tilde{C}}\\
0&D(\bar{\partial}_{J^{\mathbb{P}}})_{\iota}&0\\
0&0&\nabla^{0,1}\end{bmatrix}.

For the purpose of verifying this computation, we may reduce to the case where
C
is smooth, i.e.,
\tilde{C}=C
. The following points now justify the computation (
4.3.5
).

(a)

The bottom row is the linearization of
(u,v,\eta)\mapsto\nabla^{0,1}\eta
. Since we are computing it at a point with
\eta=0
, the dependence of
\nabla^{0,1}
on
(u,v)
does not make an appearance in the linearization. Moreover,
\nabla^{0,1}
is linear in the
\eta
direction.

(b)

The middle row records the linearization of
(u,v,\eta)\mapsto\bar{\partial}_{J^{\mathbb{P}}}v
, which is manifestly independent of
(u,\eta)
. This explains the two entries which are zero. We also note that
D(\bar{\partial}_{J^{\mathbb{P}}})_{\iota}
is the standard Cauchy–Riemann operator on the holomorphic vector bundle
\iota^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}
.

(c)

The top row records the linearization of
(u,v,\eta)\mapsto\bar{\partial}_{J}u+J\langle\eta\rangle\,dv\,j_{C}
at a point with
\eta=0
. The
\bar{\partial}_{J}u
term is clearly independent of
(v,\eta)
and gives rise to the top left matrix entry
D(\bar{\partial}_{J})_{u}
. The
(u,v)
dependence in the term
J\langle\eta\rangle\,dv\,j_{C}
makes no appearance in the linearization, since we are computing it at a point where
\eta=0
. Moreover, by linearity of this term in the
\eta
direction, we find that its linearization in this direction is

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}\mapsto J\langle\eta^{\prime}\rangle\,d\iota\,j_{C}=J\langle\eta^{\prime}\rangle\,J^{\mathbb{P}}\,d\iota=-J^{2}\langle\eta^{\prime}\rangle\,d\iota=\langle\eta^{\prime}\rangle\,d\iota,

where we have used the holomorphicity of
\iota
and the
\mathbb{C}
-antilinearity of
\langle\eta^{\prime}\rangle
.

Now, let us investigate the surjectivity of (
4.3.5
). From Lemma
4.2
and the proof of smoothness in Lemma
3.2
, we already know that
D(\bar{\partial}_{J^{\mathbb{P}}})_{\iota}
is surjective. Thus, the surjectivity of (
4.3.5
) is equivalent to the surjectivity of the Cauchy–Riemann type operator on
u^{*}T_{X}\oplus(u,\iota)^{*}E_{l}
given by the matrix

\displaystyle L=\begin{bmatrix}D(\bar{\partial}_{J})_{u}&\langle\cdot\rangle\,d\iota_{\tilde{C}}\\
0&\nabla^{0,1}\end{bmatrix}.

As
L
is upper triangular, we deduce that it is surjective if and only if
\nabla^{0,1}
is surjective
and
the restriction of
\langle\cdot\rangle\,d\iota_{\tilde{C}}
to
\ker(\nabla^{0,1})
maps onto
\text{coker}(D(\bar{\partial}_{J})_{u})
. Observe that the surjectivity of
\nabla^{0,1}
is the same as (
4.3.1
). Similarly, the surjectivity of the map
\ker(\nabla^{0,1})\to\text{coker}(D(\bar{\partial}_{J})_{u})
induced by
\langle\cdot\rangle\,d\iota_{\tilde{C}}
is equivalent to the surjectivity of (
4.3.2
). We conclude that
D(\bar{\partial}_{\tilde{J}^{E_{l}}})_{F}
is surjective if and only if
(u,\iota):C\to X\times\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
l
-transverse.
∎

-transversality)

We have an embedding of

∎

to smallest integer satisfying the conclusion of Lemma

Appendix A
Holomorphic line bundles on families of curves

A.1.
Case of a single curve

Let
C
be a prestable curve. Consider the following natural exact sequence of sheaves of abelian groups on
C
in the usual (i.e., complex analytic) topology.

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi\text{i}}\mathcal{O}_{C}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times}\to 0.

Since
C
is connected, applying
H^{0}
to this exact sequence gives

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2\pi\text{i}}\mathbb{C}\xrightarrow{\exp}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to 0.

The remainder of the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology contains

\displaystyle 0\to H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{2\pi\text{i}}H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\xrightarrow{\exp}H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times})\to H^{2}(C,\mathbb{Z})\to 0,

where we have used the vanishing of
H^{2}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
to get the last
0
. Using the Čech description of sheaf cohomology, we see that
\text{Pic}(C):=H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times})
is the group of isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles on
C
under the tensor product operation, i.e., the Picard group of
C
. The map
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times})\to H^{2}(C,\mathbb{Z})
is given by the first Chern class
[
GH94
, Chapter 1, pages 139–143]
. From this, we get

(A.1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})}{H^{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\text{Pic}^{0}(C):=\ker(H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\times})\xrightarrow{c_{1}}H^{2}(C,\mathbb{Z})).

The group
H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
has the alternate Dolbeault description

\displaystyle H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=\text{coker}(\bar{\partial}:\Omega^{0}(C,\mathbb{C})\to\Omega^{0,1}(\tilde{C},\mathbb{C})),

where
\tilde{C}\to C
is the normalization. It will be useful to have an explicit description of the isomorphism (
A.1.1
) in Dolbeault terms.

Lemma A.1

(Line bundle from Dolbeault class)
.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{0,1}(\tilde{C},\mathbb{C})
. Then, the image of
[\alpha]\in H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
in
\text{Pic}^{0}(C)
is
[\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}]
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
is holomorphic line bundle defined by equipping
C\times\mathbb{C}
with the Cauchy–Riemann operator

\displaystyle\Omega^{0}(C,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{0,1}(\tilde{C},\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto\bar{\partial}F-F\alpha.

Proof.

Using the
\bar{\partial}
-Poincaré lemma, choose an open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
of
C
and smooth functions

such that
\bar{\partial}f_{i}=\alpha|_{U_{i}}
. Near a node, we are applying the
\bar{\partial}
-Poincaré lemma on
\tilde{C}
and then adjusting the primitives by adding constants to ensure that they agree on the inverse images of the node in
\tilde{C}
.

The
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-valued Čech

defined by

\displaystyle f_{ij}=f_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}-f_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}

represents
[\alpha]\in H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
. This means its image in
\text{Pic}^{0}(C)
is the isomorphism class of the holomorphic line bundle
\mathcal{L}
obtained by gluing the trivial holomorphic line bundles

using the transition functions

\displaystyle(U_{i}\times\mathbb{C})|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}(U_{j}\times\mathbb{C})|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}

given by

. Define the smooth isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathcal{L}\to C\times\mathbb{C}

of complex line bundles using
\exp(f_{i}):U_{i}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
over the open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i}
. This is well-defined since we have
\exp(f_{i})=g_{ij}\exp(f_{j})
over

under the isomorphism

, we obtain the Cauchy–Riemann operator on

which is given on

by the expression

\displaystyle F\mapsto\exp(f_{i})\bar{\partial}(F\exp(-f_{i}))=\bar{\partial}F-F\cdot\alpha|_{U_{i}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}
are isomorphic as holomorphic line bundles.

Corollary A.2

(Image of

Refinement

We use the above discussion to refine Theorem
2.19
.

Lemma B.2
.

The assignment from (
B.2.2
) defines a
C^{\infty}
map

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K

\displaystyle\to H^{k+m,\delta_{m}}

\displaystyle(\alpha,v,\kappa)

\displaystyle\mapsto\xi_{\alpha,v,\kappa}

of Banach manifolds for all integers
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

We already know that the map
\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K\to H^{k,\delta}
induced by the assignment
(\alpha,v,\kappa)\mapsto\xi_{\alpha,v,\kappa}
is sc-smooth. As
\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K
is finite dimensional, all its scales coincide, i.e.,
(\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K)_{m}=\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K
for all
m\geq 0
. The desired result is now a formal consequence of
[
HWZ10
, Proposition 2.3]
.
∎

In terms of the notation established above, let
C^{\text{int}}\subset C
be the complement of the closure of the cylindrical ends in
C^{\circ}
. Note that we have canonical embeddings
C^{\text{int}}\subset C_{\alpha}
for all small values of
\alpha\in\mathbb{G}
.

Lemma B.3
.

The following is a
C^{\infty}
map of manifolds.

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K\times C^{\text{int}}

\displaystyle\to X

\displaystyle(\alpha,v,\kappa,w)

\displaystyle\mapsto\exp_{u(w)}(\kappa(w)+\xi_{\alpha,v,\kappa}(w)).

Proof.

Fix an integer
m\geq 0
.
Lemma
B.2
shows that
(\alpha,v,\kappa)\mapsto\kappa+\xi_{\alpha,v,\kappa}
is
C^{\infty}
as a map
\mathbb{G}_{\exp}\times V\times K\to H^{k+m,\delta_{m}}
. The Sobolev embedding theorem gives a continuous restriction map
H^{k+m,\delta_{m}}\to C^{m}(C^{\text{int}},u^{*}T_{X})
. Using the smoothness of the exponential map on
X
and working locally on
C^{\text{int}}
to trivialize
u^{*}T_{X}
, we are left to consider evaluation map

\displaystyle C^{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R},\quad(f,w)\mapsto f(w)

where
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is a disc, which is evidently
C^{m}
. This shows that the map in the statement of the lemma is a composition of
C^{m}
maps and is therefore
C^{m}
. As
m\geq 0
was arbitrary, we get the desired result.
∎

Proposition B.4

(Refinement of Theorem
2.19
)
.

For any rel–
C^{\infty}
manifold
Z/T
and any diagram in
\mathfrak{M}^{\text{reg}}(\pi,X)_{A}(Z/T)
as above,
f
restricts to a rel–
C^{\infty}
map

\displaystyle f^{\circ}:\mathfrak{C}^{\circ}_{Z}/T\to X/\text{pt}.

Proof.

We reduce to the universal case, i.e.,
Z/T=\mathfrak{M}^{\text{reg}}(\pi,X)_{A}/\mathfrak{B}
and
f
is the universal pseudo-holomorphic map over it. This is possible because a diagram over a general
Z/T
is obtained from the universal case by a rel–

Given a point

and a point

is rel–

Jin Bong Lee, Bae Jun Park

Abstract
. In this paper, we study multilinear Fourier multiplier operators on Hardy spaces. In particular, we prove that the multilinear Fourier multiplier operator of Hörmander type is bounded from H p 1 × ⋯ × H p m H^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times H^{p_{m}} to H p H^{p} for 0 < p 1 , … , p m ≤ 1 0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 1 with 1 / p 1 + ⋯ 1 / p m = 1 / p 1/p_{1}+\cdots 1/p_{m}=1/p , under suitable cancellation conditions. As a result, we extend the trilinear estimates in [ 17 ] to general multilinear ones and improve the boundedness result in [ 18 ] in limiting situations.

1.
Introduction

Let
m
be a positive integer greater than or equal to
2
and
\sigma
be a bounded function on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
. The
m
-linear Fourier multiplier operator
T_{\sigma}
associated with
\sigma
is defined by

(1.1)

T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\sigma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{f_{m}}(\xi_{m})e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi_{1}+\dots+\xi_{m}\rangle}d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{m}

for Schwartz functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}dx
is the Fourier transform of
f
.
The
L^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\to L^{p}
boundedness of
T_{\sigma}
,
1/p=1/p_{1}+\dots+1/p_{m}
, is one of principal questions in multilinear harmonic analysis and there have been several satisfactory results even though multilinear operators can not be directly attacked by tools for linear operators.
Coifman and Meyer
[
6
]
proved that if
N
is a sufficiently large number and
\sigma
satisfies

(1.2)

\big|\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{\alpha_{m}}\cdots\partial_{\xi_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}\sigma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\big|\lesssim_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}}\big|(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\big|^{-(|\alpha_{1}|+\dots+|\alpha_{m}|)},\quad(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\not=\vec{\boldsymbol{0}}

for all multi-indices
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
satisfying
|\alpha_{1}|+\cdots+|\alpha_{m}|\leq N
, then
T_{\sigma}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<\infty
and
1\leq p<\infty
with
1/p=1/p_{1}+\dots+1/p_{m}
.
This result was refined by Tomita
[
24
]
who obtained a multilinear version of Hörmander’s multiplier theorem.
Let
\Psi
be a Schwartz function, defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, having the properties that

0\leq\widehat{\Psi}\leq 1
,
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\Psi})\subset\big\{\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}:2^{-1}\leq|\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}|\leq 2\big\}
, and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\Psi_{j}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}})=1
for
\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\not=\vec{0}

where
\Psi_{j}:=2^{jmn}\Psi(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)
. That is,
\{\Psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a family of Littlewood-Paley functions on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
.
For
s\geq 0
, we define the Sobolev space
L_{s}^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})
in terms of the finiteness of the norm

(1.3)

\|F\|_{L_{s}^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}:=\Big(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\big(1+4\pi^{2}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}|^{2}\big)^{s}\big|\widehat{F}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\big|^{2}\;d\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\Big)^{1/2}

and then we will use the notation

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}\big\|_{L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}

throughout this work.

Theorem A
.

[
24
]

Let
1<p,p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}<\infty
with
1/p={1}{p_{1}}+\dots+1/p_{m}
. Suppose that

s>\frac{mn}{2}.

Then we have

\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{L^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for Schwartz functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The above result was generalized to the full range of indices
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq\infty
, in some recent papers, by using a product-type Sobolev space condition, instead of
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]<\infty
. For this one, the space
L^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mu=1,\dots,m
, would be replaced by the Hardy space
H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

To recall the definition of Hardy spaces,
let
\phi
be a smooth function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\leq 1\}
.
Then the Hardy space

H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<p\leq\infty
contains all tempered distributions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1.4)

\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\Big\|\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\phi_{l}\ast f\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty

where
\phi_{l}:=2^{ln}\phi(2^{l}\cdot)
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The space provides an extension to
0<p\leq 1
in the scale of classical
L^{p}
spaces for
1<p\leq\infty
, which is more natural and useful in many respects than the corresponding
L^{p}
extension. Indeed,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p\leq\infty
and several essential operators, such as singular integrals of Calderón-Zygmund type, that are well-behaved on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
only for
1<p\leq\infty
are also well-behaved on
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<p\leq 1
.
Moreover, especially when
0<p\leq 1
, every
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be written as

(1.5)

f=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\qquad\text{ in the sense of tempered distributions}

where
\lambda_{k}
are coefficients satisfying
\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and

a_{k}
are
H^{p}
-atoms, which means that there exist cubes
Q_{k}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(a_{k})\subset Q_{k}
,
\|a_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|Q_{k}|^{-1/p}
, and

(1.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}a_{k}(x)dx=0\qquad\text{ for all multi-indices }~|\alpha|\leq L

for
L>n/p-n
.
Here,
L
may be actually an arbitrarily large number greater than
n/p-n
.
Now let
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the Schwartz space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for a given positive integer
L
let
\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be its subspace consisting of
f
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0\quad\text{ for all multi-indices}~\alpha~\text{with }~|\alpha|\leq L.

Then as mentioned in
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
, for all
0<p<\infty

(1.7)

\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})~\text{ is dense in }~H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{ if }~L>\frac{n}{p}-n.

We remark that
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also dense in
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
, but not for
0<p\leq 1
. See
[
22
, Chapter III, §5.2]
for more details.
Moreover, as discussed in
[
22
, Chapter III, §5.4]
, if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<p\leq 1
, then we must necessarily have

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0\quad\text{ for all multi-indices }~|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{p}-n.

We refer to
[
2
,
3
,
8
,
22
,
26
]
for more details.

To study multilinear multiplier operators on Hardy spaces, we now assume that the operator
T_{\sigma}
, defined in (
1.1
), initially acts on functions
f_{\mu}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\mu=1,\dots,m
, in view of (
1.7
). Here, we set
L
to be a sufficiently large number greater than
n/p-n
. That is,
L>n/p-n\geq n/p_{\mu}-n
for all
\mu=1,\dots,m
.
Then we have the following result.

Theorem B
.

[
11
,
12
,
15
,
19
]

Let
0<p<\infty
and
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq\infty
with
{1}/{p}=1/p_{1}+\dots+1/p_{m}
. Suppose that
L
is sufficiently large and

(1.9)

s_{1},\dots,s_{m}>\frac{n}{2},\qquad\sum_{\mu\in J}\Big(s_{\mu}-\frac{n}{p_{\mu}}\Big)>-\frac{n}{2}

for any nonempty subset
J
of
\{1,\cdots,m\}
.
Then we have

(1.10)

\displaystyle\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}\big\|_{L^{2}_{(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Here, the space
L_{(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})}^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})
indicates the product type Sobolev space on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, in which the norm is defined by replacing the term
(1+4\pi^{2}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}|)^{s}
in (
1.3
) by
\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\big(1+4\pi^{2}|\xi_{\mu}|^{2}\big)^{s_{\mu}}
for
\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}:=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
.
We remark that the condition (
1.9
) is sharp in the sense that if the condition does not hold, then there exists
\sigma
such that the estimate (
1.10
) fails for the corresponding operators
T_{\sigma}
. Refer to
[
21
]
for more details.

In the paper of Lee, Heo, Hong, Park, Yang, and the authors
[
18
]
, there has been some recent progress concerning multilinear multiplier theory associated with the original Hörmander condition, which provides a generalization of Theorem
A
.

Theorem C
.

[
18
]

Let
0<p<\infty
and
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq\infty
with
{1}/{p}=1/p_{1}+\dots+1/p_{m}
.
Suppose that
L
is sufficiently large and

(1.11)

s>\frac{mn}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}<\frac{s}{n}+\sum_{\mu\in J}\Big(\frac{1}{p_{\mu}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)

for any subset
J
of
\{1,\dots,m\}
, which may be empty, where we adopt the convention that the sum over the empty set is zero.
Then we have

(1.12)

\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\,\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The optimality of the condition (
1.11
) is already studied by Grafakos, He, and Honzík
[
9
]
who proved that if (
1.12
) holds, then we must necessarily have
s\geq mn/2
and
1/p-1/2\leq s/n+\sum_{\mu\in J}(1/p_{\mu}-1/2)
for all subsets
J
of
\{1,\dots,m\}
.

The estimate (
1.12
) has been recently improved in the trilinear case by replacing the target space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<p\leq 1
, under the additional cancellation condition

(1.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\big)(x)dx=0\qquad\text{ for multi-indices }~|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{p}-n

for all
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem D
.

[
17
]

Let
0<p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}<\infty
and
0<p\leq 1
with
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}+1/p_{3}
.
Suppose that
L
is sufficiently large and

s>\frac{3n}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}<\frac{s}{n}+\sum_{\mu\in J}\Big(\frac{1}{p_{\mu}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)

where
J
is an arbitrary subset of
\{1,2,3\}
, which may be empty, where we adopt the convention that the sum over the empty set is zero.
Let
\sigma
be a function on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{3}
satisfying (
1.13
).
Then we have

\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\|f_{1}\|_{H^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f_{2}\|_{H^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f_{3}\|_{H^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 1
.

The analogous result is still true in the bilinear setting, but the arguments used there could not be applied to general
m
-linear operators for
m\geq 4
.

Remark 2
.

The initial function space
\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Theorem
D
is actually
\mathscr{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\bigcap_{L\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the original version
[
17
, Theorem 1]
, but it is easy to see that everything still works with
\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as long as
L>n/p-n
.
In the present work, we employ
\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to avoid unnecessary situations.

Remark 3
.

The condition (
1.11
) is equivalent to

s>\frac{mn}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}<\frac{s}{n}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{m}\min\Big\{0,\Big(\frac{1}{p_{\mu}}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\Big\}.

Note that
J
would be empty when
p_{1},\dots p_{m}\leq 2
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish multilinear extensions of Theorem
D
in some limiting cases.
Precisely, we obtain
H^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times H^{p_{m}}\to H^{p}
estimates for general
m
-linear operators when
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 1
.
As mentioned in
[
17
]
, the condition (
1.13
) is very natural and necessary in the boundedness into the Hardy space
H^{p}
for
0<p\leq 1
, in view of (
1.8
).
For a multilinear extension of the above boundedness result, we now consider a bounded function
\sigma
on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
having the property

(1.14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)dx=0\qquad\text{ for multi-indices }~|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{p}-n

where
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
L>n/p-n
.
Then our main result is

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<p_{1},\cdots,p_{m}\leq 1
and
0<p\leq 1/m
with
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots 1/p_{m}
.
Suppose that
L
is sufficiently large and

(1.15)

s>\frac{n}{p}-\frac{n}{2}.

We also assume that the operator
T_{\sigma}
satisfies the vanishing moment condition (
1.14
).
Then we have

\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We note that the condition (
1.15
) clearly implies (
1.11
) when
0<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq 1
.

The study of boundedness into the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
0<p\leq 1
, for multilinear operators with vanishing integral, such as (
1.14
), was initiated by Coifman, Lions, Meyer and Semmes
[
5
]
who proved that certain bilinear operators are bounded from
L^{q}\times L^{q^{\prime}}
into
H^{1}
for
1<q<\infty
with
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Later, the result was generalized by Dobyinski
[
7
]
for Coifman-Meyer multiplier operators and by Coifman and Grafakos
[
4
]
for finite sums of products of Calderón-Zygmund operators. These works, for
H^{1}
-boundedness, are related to
compensated

compactness
which arises in theory of partial differential equations to assure convergence of certain nonlinear quantities. For more details, we refer to
[
1
,
5
,
20
,
23
]
and references therein.
In the works of Grafakos, Nakamura, Nguyen and Sawano
[
13
,
14
]
, the
H^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times H^{p_{m}}\to H^{p}
boundedness was established for multilinear multiplier operators
T_{\sigma}
with some cancellation condition, provided that

\sigma
satisfies the multilinear Mihlin’s condition (
1.2
) for multi-indices
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
satisfying
|\alpha_{1}|+\dots+|\alpha_{m}|\leq N
for sufficiently large
N
. However, the optimal regularity conditions were not pursued at all as it requires sufficiently large
N
.
From this point of view, Theorem
D
improves the results of Grafakos, Nakamura, Nguyen, and Sawano for trilinear operators, and Theorem
1
does for general multilinear operators although the input function spaces are limited.

The proof of Theorem
1
heavily relies on the atomic decomposition (
1.5
),
but it is not guaranteed that we can interchange infinite sums of atoms and the action of the operator
T_{\sigma}
as in

T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{1,k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{m,k_{m}}T_{\sigma}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)

for functions
f_{\mu}\in H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with atomic representation
f_{\mu}=\sum_{k_{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}}{\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}}
,
1\leq\mu\leq m
.
To settle this issue, we will additionally assume that the multiplier
\sigma
has compact support in
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, which allows us to write, with the aid of Theorem
C
,

\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\big|\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|\Big)\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)\big|.

See Section
3
below for the details.
Then we apply the method of Grafakos and Kalton
[
10
]
and pointwise estimates of the form

\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)\big|\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]b_{1}(x)\cdots b_{m}(x)\quad\text{ uniformly in }~l\in\mathbb{Z}

where

\|b_{\mu}\|_{L^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1
for
\mu=1,\dots,m
.
Such arguments are stated in Lemma
4.1
below, which is actually the key part of this paper.
Since the above estimate separates the left-hand side to
m
distinct functions of
x
, we may now apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
1/p=1/p_{1}+\dots+1/p_{m}
.
For general multipliers
\sigma
( without the compact support condition ), we apply a suitable approximation argument, which will be presented in (
3.6
).

Notation

Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
denote the sets of the natural numbers and integers, respectively, and we define
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We use the symbol
A\lesssim B
to denote
A\leq CB
for some independent constant
C
, and
A\sim B
if
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold simultaneously.
For a given cube
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
c_{Q}
,
\ell(Q)
denote its center and side length, and
\chi_{Q}
be the characteristic function of
Q
.
We denote by
Q^{*}
,
Q^{**}
,
Q^{***}
the concentric dilates of a cube
Q
with
\ell(Q^{*})=10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)
,
\ell(Q^{**})=\big(10\sqrt{n}\big)^{2}\ell(Q)
, and
\ell(Q^{***})=\big(10\sqrt{n}\big)^{3}\ell(Q)
.
We use the notation
\langle y\rangle:=\big(1+4\pi^{2}|y|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.
Preliminaries

For a locally integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\mathcal{M}f(x):=\sup_{Q:x\in Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy

be the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function of
f
where the supremum ranges over all cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
x
.
Then it turns out that the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p\leq\infty
.

We now review some lemmas that will be repeatedly needed in the proof of the main result.
The first lemma is very useful in estimating the
L^{p}
norm of the sum of functions having a compact support for
0<p\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
10
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
J
be a finite or countable index set.
Suppose that
\{Q_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in J}
is a sequence of cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\{F_{\mu}\}_{\mu\in J}
is a family of nonnegative integrable functions with
\mathrm{supp}(F_{\mu})\subset Q_{\mu}
.
Then we have

\Big\|\sum_{\mu\in J}\chi_{Q_{\mu}}F_{\mu}\;\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\bigg\|\sum_{\mu\in J}\Big(\frac{1}{|Q_{\mu}|}\int_{Q_{\mu}}F_{\mu}(y)dy\Big)\chi_{Q_{\mu}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where the constant in the inequality depends only on
p
.

We now introduce a variant of Bernstein’s inequality, which is contained in
[
11
,
19
]
, modulo a few minor modifications.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
11
,
19
]

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
. Let
K
be a function, defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, whose Fourier transform is supported in a ball of a constant radius.
Then for
\mu=1,\dots,m
and any multi-indices
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}_{0})^{n}
, there exists a constant
C_{p,q,{\alpha}}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\big\langle(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\big\rangle^{s}\partial_{{\mu}}^{{\alpha}}K(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\big\|_{L^{q}(x_{\mu})}\leq C_{p,q,{\alpha}}\big\|\big\langle(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\big\rangle^{s}K(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m})\big\|_{L^{p}(x_{\mu})}

for all
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},j\not=\mu
.

The last one will play a significant role in the proof of Theorem
1
, in conjunction with the vanishing moment condition of
T_{\sigma}
in (
1.14
).

Lemma 2.3
.

[
17
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Suppose that a function
F
, defined on

, satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{x^{\alpha}F(x)}dx=0\quad\text{for all multi-indices}~\alpha~\text{ with }~|\alpha|\leq N.

Then for any

, there exists a constant

such that

3.
Proof of Theorem
1
: Reduction

We first show that the multiplier
\sigma
can be assumed to be compactly supported in Theorem
1
, using an approximation argument.
Suppose that

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]<\infty\quad\text{ for }~s>n/p-n/2.

Let
\varphi
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{\varphi})\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq 2\}
and

\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
.
For
R>0
, let
\varphi_{R}:=R^{n}\varphi(R\,\cdot\,)
and

\widehat{\Phi_{R}}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}):=\widehat{\varphi_{R}}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{\varphi_{R}}(\xi_{m})\quad\text{ for }~(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}.

Now we define

\sigma_{R}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}}):=\sigma(\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}})\widehat{\Phi_{R}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\xi}})

so that

(3.2)

T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})=T_{\sigma}\big(\varphi_{R}\ast f_{1},\dots,\varphi_{R}\ast f_{m}\big).

It follows from Theorem
C
with (
3.1
) that

T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\to T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})~\text{ in the sense of tempered distribution}

as
R\to\infty
, because
\varphi_{R}
is an approximation of identity (as
R\to\infty
).
This clearly deduces

\lim_{R\to\infty}\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)=\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\quad\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and thus we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\liminf_{R\to\infty}\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}\Big\|\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big|\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\big\|T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where Fatou’s lemma is applied.
Moreover, using the compact support conditions of both
\widehat{\Psi}
and
\widehat{\Phi_{R}}
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma_{R}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j:2R<2^{j}\leq 4R\sqrt{m}}\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}(\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Phi_{R}}(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}+\sup_{j:2^{j}\leq 2R}\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}\big\|_{L_{s}^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}.

The second term is clearly less than
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]
and the Kato-Ponce inequality ( see
[
16
]
) yields that for
2^{j}\sim R

\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}(\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Phi}(2^{j}R^{-1}\vec{\cdot}\;)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}\lesssim\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}\big\|_{L^{2}_{s}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}+\big\|\sigma(2^{j}\vec{\cdot}\;)\widehat{\Psi}\big\|_{L^{2}((\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma],

which finally proves

(3.4)

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma_{R}]\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\quad\text{ uniformly in }R>0.

In view of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we reduce the proof of Theorem
1
to the estimate

(3.5)

\big\|T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma_{R}]\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{ uniformly in }~R>0

as this can be improved to any multipliers
\sigma
in general:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\big\|T_{\sigma}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty}\big\|T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\liminf_{R\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma_{R}]\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma]\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In order to establish (
3.5
), we apply the atomic decomposition for Hardy spaces.
Each
f_{\mu}\in H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be written as

f_{\mu}=\sum_{k_{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}

where
\{a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}\}_{k_{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
H^{p_{\mu}}
-atoms so that
\mathrm{supp}(a_{\mu,k_{\mu}})\subset Q_{\mu,k_{\mu}}
,
\|a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq|Q_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|^{-1/p_{\mu}}
for some cube
Q_{\mu,k_{\mu}}
,
\int_{Q_{\mu,k_{\mu}}}x^{\gamma}a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}(x)dx=0
for all multi-indices
|\gamma|\leq L
, and
\big(\sum_{k_{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|^{p_{\mu}}\big)^{1/p_{\mu}}\lesssim\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
where
L
is a sufficiently large number greater than
n/p-n
.
Then we claim that

(3.7)

\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\big|\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|\Big)\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)\big|.

For this one, we define

f_{\mu}^{N}:=\sum_{k_{\mu}=1}^{N}\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}a_{\mu,k_{\mu}},\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}

so that

(3.8)

\big\|f_{\mu}-f_{\mu}^{N}\big\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k_{\mu}=N+1}^{\infty}\big|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}\big|^{p_{\mu}}\Big)^{1/p_{\mu}}\to 0\quad\text{ as }~N\to\infty.

Then the left-hand side of (
3.7
) is less than

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle~+\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}^{N},f_{2}-f_{2}^{N},f_{3},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle\qquad+\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}^{N},\dots,f_{m-1}^{N},f_{m}-f_{m}^{N}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}^{N},\dots,f_{m}^{N}\big)(x)\big|.

The last term is clearly dominated by

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{N}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}=1}^{N}\Big(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|\Big)\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|\Big)\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)\big|

by the multi-linearity of the operator
\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}
.
Therefore, it suffices to show that the remaining terms approach
0
as
N\to\infty
. We will be concerned only with the first term as similar arguments can be applied to the others.
We note that the Fourier transform of
T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})
is supported on the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq 2\sqrt{m}R\}
due to the support of
\widehat{\varphi_{R}}
. This shows that

\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)=\phi_{l}\ast\Upsilon_{R}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)

for some Schwartz function
\Upsilon_{R}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose Fourier transform is equal to
1
on the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq 2\sqrt{m}R\}
and is supported on the larger ball

\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq 4\sqrt{m}R\}
. Now, using Nikolskii’s inequality (sometimes called Bernstein’s inequality) in
[
25
, Proposition 1.3.2]
,
we have

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{l}\ast T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{l}\ast\Upsilon_{R}(x-y)T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{n/p-n}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|\phi_{l}\ast\Upsilon_{R}(x-y)\big|^{p}\big|T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\big)(y)\big|^{p}dy\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{R,l,p}\big\|T_{\sigma_{R}}(f_{1}-f_{1}^{N},f_{2},\dots,f_{m})\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{L}_{s}^{2}[\sigma_{R}]\big\|f_{1}-f_{1}^{N}\big\|_{H^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\prod_{\mu=2}^{m}\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
, thanks to (
3.8
). Here, the last inequality follows from Theorem
C
. This completes the proof of (
3.7
).

We also remark that the vanishing moment condition (
1.14
), together with (
3.2
), guarantees

(3.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}T_{\sigma_{R}}\big(a_{1,k_{1}},\dots,a_{m,k_{m}}\big)(x)dx=0\quad\text{ for multi-indices }~|\alpha|\leq\frac{n}{p}-n

as each
\varphi_{R}\ast a_{\mu,k_{\mu}}
belongs to
\mathscr{S}^{L}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In conclusion, matters reduce to the following proposition, in view of (
1.4
), (
3.7
), and (
3.9
).

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{k_{\mu}\in\mathbb{N}}|\lambda_{\mu,k_{\mu}}|^{p_{\mu}}\Big)^{1/p_{\mu}}\lesssim\|f_{\mu}\|_{H^{p_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and this deduces

This completes the proof of Proposition

.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

.
Let

.
We may also assume that

(5.3)

\ell(Q_{1})=\min_{1\leq{\mathrm{i}}\leq\nu}\{\ell(Q_{{\mathrm{i}}})\}.

By using the Littlewood-Paley decomposition with

we can write

and

5.1.
The first assertion

(5.5)

x\in\Big(\bigcap_{\mu=1}^{\nu}(Q_{\mu}^{**})^{c}\cap B_{\mu,l}\Big)\mathbin{\scalebox{1.5}{$\cap$}}\Big(\bigcap_{\mu\in J_{m}\setminus J_{0}}Q_{\mu}^{**}\Big).

) by setting

Then it is clear that

for all

.
In order to establish (

),
let

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\prod_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\frac{1}{|x-c_{Q_{\mu}}|^{n/p_{\mu}+\epsilon}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y-c_{Q_{1}}|^{N_{\nu}+\epsilon_{\nu}}\big|T_{\sigma}\big(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\big)(y)\big|\;dy

Now it suffices to show that

To estimate

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{1}^{*}}|y-c_{Q_{1}}|^{N_{\nu}+\epsilon_{\nu}}\big|T_{\sigma}\big(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\big)(y)\big|\;dy\lesssim\ell(Q_{1})^{N_{\nu}+\epsilon_{\nu}}\big\|T_{\sigma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

For the other term

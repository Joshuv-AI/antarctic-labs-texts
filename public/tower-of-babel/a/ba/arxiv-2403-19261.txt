High-Temperature Modifications of Charged Casimir Wormholes
Phongpichit Channuie

Abstract
In this work, we extend the investigation of the consequences of thermal fluctuations on the Casimir effect within the context of a traversable wormhole, recently proposed by Garattini & Faizal, arXiv:2403.15174 [gr-qc], subject to charge contributions. Specifically, we focus on scenarios where the plates exhibit both constant and radial variations. In our analysis, we initially concentrate on the high temperature approximation, considering solely the influence of charge on the thermal Casimir wormholes. Additionally, upon incorporating Generalized Uncertainty Principle (GUP) corrections to the Casimir energy, we obtain a new class of wormhole solutions. Notably, we establish that the flare-out condition remains consistently satisfied. Intriguingly, our findings reveal that both the charge and GUP contributions serve to further enlarge the throat’s size in the radial variation.



I
Introduction

The Casimir effect is based on the assumption of perfectly conducting surfaces. However, real-world plates are never perfectly conducting; they exhibit a complex permittivity, varying with frequency depending on the material. This consideration alters the original Casimir force at zero temperature, transforming it into a finite-temperature thermal effect. This thermal effect incorporates both thermal and quantum fluctuations, as described by the Lifshitz theory, which accounts for correlated fluctuating charges and currents in the plates
Sabisky1973
. This adaptation of the conventional Casimir effect to finite temperature is termed the thermal Casimir effect
Canaguier-Durand:2010hwu
;
Marino:2014rfa
. It has been shown that at separations beyond a critical value, the force generated by thermal fluctuations surpasses that of zero-temperature quantum fluctuation
Sushkov:2010cv
;
Bostrom:2000zza
. This suggests the potential significance of thermal effects in scaling up the size of traversable wormholes (TWs). Very recently, the consequences of thermal fluctuations to the Casimir effect on a traversable wormhole have been investigated
Garattini:2024jkr
. The authors illustrated how thermal fluctuations alter the throat of the wormhole in both high and low temperature regimes. Furthermore, they elucidated the impact of finite temperature on the size of a wormhole. In this work, we investigate new exact and analytic solutions of the Einstein–Maxwell field equations describing Casimir wormholes including the high-temperature contribution to the Casimir energy source for a traversable wormhole with the plates radially varying and discuss the effect of the Generalized Uncertainty Principle (GUP). Notice that the electromagnetic field combined with the Casimir source has been used to examine the impacts of such an electrovacuum on a traversable wormhole
Samart:2021tvl
;
Garattini:2023qyo
.

The main purpose of this work is to extend the analysis present in Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
for the thermal Casimir wormholes by incorporating the electric charge underlying the GUP. This work is organized as follows: In Section (
II
), we formulate the Einstein–Maxwell field equations describing Casimir wormholes with charge. We next consider the thermal Casimir wormholes employing energy densities featuring the thermal modifications to the chared Casimir wormholes and solve to obtian the wormholes solutions in Section (
III
). The results also include the GUP effects. We conclude of findings in the last section.

II
Setup

Our aim is to model a wormhole solution and typically we consider an anisotropic fluid stress-energy tensor to describe the matter distribution, which is described by the following form

T^{\mu}_{\nu}=(\rho+p_{t})u^{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{t}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}-(p_{t}-p_{r})X^{\mu}X_{\nu},

(1)

where
u^{\mu}
is the fluid 4-velocity,
X_{\mu}
is the unit space like vector in the direction of radial vector and
g_{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor. Furthermore,
\rho=\rho(r)
is the energy density,
p_{r}=p_{r}(r)
and
p_{t}=p_{t}(r)
are the radial and tangential pressures, respectively. The energy-momentum tensor of electromagnetic field defined by

{{T^{\rm em}}^{\mu}}_{\nu}=\frac{1}{4}\big(-F^{\mu\gamma}F_{\nu\gamma}+\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}F^{\beta\zeta}F_{\beta\zeta}\big)\,.

(2)

We define an appropriate frame of the fluid velocity vectors
Cadoni:2020izk

\displaystyle u^{a}=e^{-\Phi}\delta^{a}_{0}\,,\quad X^{a}=\sqrt{1-\frac{b(r)}{r}}\delta^{a}_{1}\,,

(3)

so that
u_{a}u^{a}=-1
and
X_{a}X^{a}=1
as required. Now, in its covariant form, we construct the geometrical Einstein tensor on the left-hand side with an effective energy-momentum tensor on the right-hand side, as

R^{\mu}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}R=8\pi\Big(T^{\mu}_{\nu}+{{T^{\rm em}}^{\mu}}_{\nu}\Big),

(4)

where

\displaystyle{{T^{\rm em}}^{\mu}}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\rho^{\rm em},p^{\rm em}_{r},p^{\rm em}_{t},p^{\rm em}_{t})

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi r^{4}}{\rm diag.}(-1,-1,1,1)\,.

We start by considering a static, spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat space-time with the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\text{stat}}=-e^{2\Phi}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{b}{r}}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(6)

where
\Phi
and
b
are arbitrary functions of the variable ‘
r
,’ where
\Phi
is referred to as the ‘redshift function’, and ‘
b
’ is known as the ‘shape function. Taking into account the Eqs. (
1
) and (
5
), the nonzero components of the field equations (
4
) take the form:

\displaystyle\frac{b^{\prime}}{r^{2}}=8\pi\Big(\rho+\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi r^{4}}\Big),

(7)

\displaystyle 2\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\frac{\Phi^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{b}{r^{3}}=8\pi\Big(p_{r}-\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi r^{4}}\Big),

(8)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left[\Phi^{\prime\prime}+\Phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r(r-b)}\Phi^{\prime}-\frac{b^{\prime}r-b}{2r^{2}(r-b)}+\frac{\Phi^{\prime}}{r}\right]=8\pi\Big(p_{t}+\frac{q^{2}}{8\pi r^{4}}\Big),

(9)

where
\rho
represents the effective energy density,
p_{r}
is the effective radial pressure and
p_{t}
is the effective tangential pressure respectively.

III
Wormhole Solutions

Following Ref.
Garattini:2019ivd
, we can consider the plates positioned at a distance either parametrically fixed and radially varying. Thus, we consider a more realistic scenario, incorporating the effects of thermal fluctuations as a source of a traversable wormhole. To examine the dependence on the thermal Casimir effect, we consider plates positioned either at a fixed parametric distance or with radial variation.

III.1
Constant Plates separation

In this case, the form of the energy density has been proposed in Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
. It can be written as

\displaystyle\rho_{CP}=-\frac{k_{B}T}{8\pi d^{3}}\zeta(3)\,,

(10)

a shape function can be straightforwardly computed using Eq.(
7

\displaystyle b(r)=c_{1}-\frac{\zeta(3)k_{B}r^{3}T}{3d^{3}}-\frac{q^{2}}{r}\,,

(11)

where
c_{1}
is a integration constant. Taking
b(r_{0})=r_{0}
, it can be determined to yield

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{3}T}{3d^{3}}+\frac{q^{2}}{r_{0}}+r_{0}\,.

(12)

Therefore, the first EFE leads to the following shape function

\displaystyle b(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{0}+q^{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{0}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta(3)\left(r^{3}-r_{0}^{3}\right)}{6d^{4}}\Bigg(\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}\Bigg)\,,

with
\zeta(x)
being the Riemann zeta function and
T_{\text{eff}}=1/(2dk_{B})
in our convention. Setting
q=0
, the above result become that of Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
. From the shape function (

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(r_{0})=\frac{q^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{2}}{2d^{4}}\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}<1\,.

(14)

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}>\frac{2d^{4}(q^{2}-r^{2}_{0})}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}}\,.

(15)

To obtain a TW, we need to compute the redshift function. We have to impose the EoS
p_{r}(r)=\omega\rho(r)
. From Eq.(
8
) and Eq.(
13
), one finds

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{6T_{\text{eff}}\left(d^{4}\left(q^{2}(r-2r_{0})+rr_{0}^{2}\right)-d\zeta(3)k_{B}r^{4}r_{0}T\omega\right)+\zeta rr_{0}T\left(r_{0}^{3}-r^{3}\right)}{2r(r-r_{0})\left(\zeta(3)rr_{0}T\left(r^{2}+rr_{0}+r_{0}^{2}\right)-6d^{4}T_{\text{eff}}\left(q^{2}-rr_{0}\right)\right)}\,.

(16)

Close to the throat, the r.h.s. of Eq.(
38
) can be approximated by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(r)\simeq\frac{3\left(d^{4}\left(r_{0}^{3}-q^{2}r_{0}\right)-\frac{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{5}T\omega}{2T_{\text{eff}}}\right)}{r_{0}(r-r_{0})\left(\frac{3\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}T}{T_{\text{eff}}}-6d^{4}\left(q^{2}-r_{0}^{2}\right)\right)},.

(17)

Following Ref.
Garattini:2019ivd
we can choose
\omega
such that
\Phi^{\prime}(r)=0
to avoid the appearance of a horizon. This can be seen if

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{2d^{4}T_{\text{eff}}(r^{2}_{0}-q^{2})}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}T}\,.

(18)

Note that when
q=0
, the above result reduces to that of Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
, i.e.,
\omega=\frac{2d^{4}T_{\text{eff}}}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{2}T}
. With a flare-out condition Eq.(
24
), we find that

\displaystyle\omega=\frac{2d^{4}(r^{2}_{0}-q^{2})}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}}\Bigg(\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}\Bigg)<1\,,

(19)

and can be negative when
r_{0}<q
. We can generalize the above results by incorporating the GUP corrected energy density. A new class of Casimir wormholes can be basically constructed. Following
Samart:2021tvl
, it is straightforward to calculate the shape function of the traverasable wormholes with the electric charge underlying a so-called Generalized Uncertainty Principle (GUP). As proposed in Ref.
Jusufi:2020rpw
, we consider the GUP corrected Casimir energy density in a constant plate scenario. We finally find

\displaystyle b(r)=-\frac{\zeta(3)k_{B}r^{3}T\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}{3d^{5}}-\frac{q^{2}}{r}+c_{2}\,,

(20)

where
c_{2}
is a integration constant,
D_{i}
s are the constants, see Ref.
Jusufi:2020rpw
. Taking
b(r_{0})=r_{0}
, it can be determined to yield

\displaystyle c_{2}=\frac{3d^{5}q^{2}+3d^{5}r_{0}^{2}+d^{2}\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{4}T+\beta D_{i}\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{4}T}{3d^{5}r_{0}}\,.

(21)

Therefore, the following shape function is this case becomes

\displaystyle b(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{0}+q^{2}\left(\frac{1}{r_{0}}-\frac{1}{r}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle-\frac{\zeta(3)\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)\left(r^{3}+r_{0}^{3}\right)}{6d^{6}}\Bigg(\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}\Bigg)\,.

Notice that the third term includes the GUP effect. It is also straightforward to show that
b(r)/r\rightarrow 0
when
r\rightarrow\infty
. From the shape function (

), one finds that the flare-out condition is always satisfied since

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(r_{0})=\frac{q^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{2}}{2d^{4}}\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}<1\,.

(23)

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{T}{T_{\text{eff}}}>\frac{2d^{6}(q^{2}-r^{2}_{0})}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}\,.

(24)

To obtain a TW, we have to determine the redshift function. We have to impose the EoS
p_{r}(r)=\omega\rho(r)
. From Eq.(
8
), one finds

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{\frac{rr_{0}\left(3d^{5}r_{0}-\zeta(3)k_{B}r^{3}T(3\omega+1)\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)+\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{3}T\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)\right)}{d^{5}}+3q^{2}(r-2r_{0})}{2r(r-r_{0})\left(rr_{0}\left(-\frac{\zeta(3)k_{B}T\left(r^{2}+rr_{0}+r_{0}^{2}\right)\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}{d^{5}}-3\right)+3q^{2}\right)}\,,

(25)

Close to the throat, the r.h.s. of Eq.(

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(r)\simeq\frac{d^{5}\left(r_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\right)-\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{4}T\omega\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}{2(r-r_{0})\left(d^{5}\left(r_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\right)+\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{4}T\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)\right)}\,.

(26)

Following Ref.
Garattini:2019ivd
, we can choose
\omega
such that
\Phi^{\prime}(r)=0
to avoid the appearance of a horizon. This can be done if

\displaystyle\omega\simeq\frac{d^{5}\left(r_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\right)}{\zeta(3)k_{B}r_{0}^{4}T\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}\equiv\frac{2d^{6}\left(r_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\right)}{\zeta(3)r_{0}^{4}\left(d^{2}+\beta D_{i}\right)}\Bigg(\frac{T_{\text{eff}}}{T}\Bigg)\,.

(27)

The result is more general compared to the previous case. Note that when

Garattini:2024jkr

III.2
Variable Plates separation

IV
Concluding Remarks

In this work, following Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
, we have investigated the consequences of thermal fluctuations to the Casimir effect on a traversable wormhole subject to the charge contribution. To perform the calculations, we focused on scenarios where the plates are constant and radially varying. For the high temperature approximation, we first considered only charge contribution to the thermal Casimir wormholes. We investigated the effect of such finite temperature effects incorporating with a charge on the size of a wormhole. Having compared with Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
, the size of the throat is enlarged further by the effect of the charge contribution in the case when plates are radially varying given by

\displaystyle r_{0}=\zeta(3)Tk_{B}\Big(1+\sqrt{1+\frac{q^{2}}{\zeta(3)^{2}T^{2}k_{B}^{2}}}\Big)\,.

(52)

We have verified that the flare-out condition is always satisfied. Indeed, we can restore constants, e.g,
G,\,c,\,\hbar
, so that our results reflect physical interpretations. This is done by introducing the Casimir thermal wave length Ref.
Garattini:2024jkr
. The second scenario deals with the GUP corrections to the thermal Casimir energy. In this case, we have also found a new class of WH solutions. We have found that the flare-out condition is always satisfied in this case. Interestingly, the wormhole solutions in the present work can be further tested by the wormhole observations, see, e.g.,
Abe:2010ap
;
Toki:2011zu
;
Godani:2021aub
;
Ohgami:2015nra
;
Dai:2019mse
.

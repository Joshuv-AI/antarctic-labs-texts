Quantum Coin Flipping, Qubit Measurement and Generalized Fibonacci Numbers
Oktay K. Pashaev

Abstract
The problem of Hadamard quantum coin measurement in n n trials, with arbitrary number of repeated consecutive last states is formulated in terms of Fibonacci sequences for duplicated states, Tribonacci numbers for triplicated states and N N -Bonacci numbers for arbitrary N N -plicated states. The probability formulas for arbitrary position of repeated states are derived in terms of Lucas and Fibonacci numbers. For generic qubit coin, the formulas are expressed by Fibonacci and more general, N N -Bonacci polynomials in qubit probabilities. The generating function for probabilities, the Golden Ratio limit of these probabilities and Shannon entropy for corresponding states are determined. By generalized Born rule and universality of n n -qubit measurement gate, we formulate problem in terms of generic n n -qubit states and construct projection operators in Hilbert space, constrained on the Fibonacci tree of the states. The results are generalized to qutrit and qudit coins, described by generalized Fibonacci- N N -Bonacci sequences.



Keywords: Fibonacci numbers, quantum coin, qubit, qutrit, qudit, quantum measurement, Tribonacci numbers,
N
-Bonacci numbers.

For maximally random qubit state, as the Hadamard quantum coin state, this problem is the quantum analog of the classical coin flipping. The last one is the known from XVIII century problem, formulated by A. De Moivre in his book on probability theory ”The doctrine of chanses”

Here we are going to work with quantum coin as two-level quantum system, representing the qubit unit of quantum information. This quantum coin can be repreneted by spin one-half, so that the measurement of this spin in

The quantum coin flipping has application as a protocol to encrypt messages for secure quantum communication.
A single-qubit coin determines one-dimensional

In the present work we formulate quantum version of classical coin flipping problem, as quantum measurement problem for quantum coin. Maximally random qubit state as quantum coin

For one qubit state in computational basis

|\psi\rangle=c_{0}|0\rangle+c_{1}|1\rangle,

(1)

where
|c_{0}|^{2}+|c_{1}|^{2}=1
, probabilities of measurement the states
|0\rangle

and
|1\rangle
are
p_{0}=|\langle 0|\psi\rangle|^{2}
and
|\langle 1|\psi\rangle|^{2}
, correspondingly. From this point of view, the qubit can be considered as a random variable state with two outcome states and corresponding probabilities,
p_{0}+p_{1}=1
.
The Shannon entropy for these probabilities

S=-p_{0}\log_{2}p_{0}--p_{1}\log_{2}p_{1}=-|c_{0}|^{2}\log_{2}|c_{0}|^{2}-|c_{1}|^{2}\log_{2}|c_{1}|^{2},

(2)

rewritten in the form

S=-|\langle 0|\psi\rangle|^{2}\log_{2}|\langle 0|\psi\rangle|^{2}-|\langle 1|\psi\rangle|^{2}\log_{2}|\langle 1|\psi\rangle|^{2}

(3)

is the natural measure of the uncertainty in the result of a measurement
[
10
]
, quantifying the deficiency in the information, which
the outcome state
|\psi\rangle
gives about further measurements. The entropy depends on the basis states and is changing with transformation of the basis.
It takes maximal value
S=1
for
p_{0}=p_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
or
|c_{0}|=|c_{1}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, giving the Hadamard type qubit states

|\varphi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+e^{i\varphi}|1\rangle).

(4)

The Hadamard states

|\pm\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle),

(5)

are generated by the Hadamard gate,
|+\rangle=H|0\rangle,|-\rangle=H|1\rangle

and
correspond to
\varphi=0
and
\varphi=\pi
respectively. Since probabilities to measure states
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
are equal
p_{0}=p_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, the qubit state (
4
)
is naturally to call as the quantum coin. The states of this quantum coin, as maximally random states, belong to the unit circle on equator of the Bloch sphere, the midway between
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
states,

|\theta=\frac{\pi}{2},\varphi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle+e^{i\varphi}|1\rangle).

(6)

The whole set of states
|\varphi\rangle
in (
4
) can be generated by the Phase gate
R(\varphi)
,
applied to the Hadamard state
|+\rangle
, which gives rotation of the unit circle

|+\rangle\hskip 14.22636pt\line{1}{0}{50.0}\framebox{\rule[-8.5359pt]{0.0pt}{28.45274pt} R($\varphi$)}\line{1}{0}{50.0}\hskip 14.22636pt|\varphi\rangle

(7)

The classical coin could exist in two sates, but not in superposition of these states. The generic superposition of two quantum coins (maximally random qubits), is not maximally random state and is not fair quantum coin anymore. Indeed,
for the state

\alpha|+\rangle+\beta|-\rangle=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle,

(8)

where,
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, probabilities are not equal

p_{0}=\frac{1}{2}+\Re(\alpha\bar{\beta}),\,\,\,\,p_{1}=\frac{1}{2}-\Re(\alpha\bar{\beta})

(9)

and the state is not maximally random. These type of states we call as the generic qubit coin states.
To make the superposition maximally random, in (
9
) we have to choose
p_{0}=p_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
. This implies
that
\Re(\alpha\bar{\beta})=0
and as follows
\arg\alpha=\arg\beta+\frac{\pi}{2}
. Solving normalization condition we find

\alpha=\cos\gamma e^{i\arg\alpha}
,
\beta=i\sin\gamma e^{i\arg\alpha}
. Then, the superposition state (
8
) becomes

\alpha|+\rangle+\beta|-\rangle=e^{i\arg\alpha}\left[\frac{e^{-i\gamma}|0\rangle+e^{i\gamma}|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\right]=e^{i\arg\alpha-i\gamma}\left[\frac{|0\rangle+e^{2i\gamma}|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\right].

This state, up to the global phase is in the form of the quantum coin (
4
), corresponding to rotation of Hadamard states along the equatorial circle.

3.0.1
Universality of n-qubit measurement

The measurement gate
M
is the universal one qubit measurement gate.
It means that the measurement gate for an arbitrary
n
-qubit state can be realized by applying 1-qubit
measurement gate
M
to each of the
n
-qubits
[
11
]
.
This becomes clear from the generalized Born rule, applied to an arbitrary
n+1
-qubit state, which can be represented in the form

|\psi\rangle_{n+1}=c_{0}|0\rangle|\phi_{0}\rangle_{n}+c_{1}|1\rangle|\phi_{1}\rangle_{n},

(10)

where states
|\phi_{0}\rangle_{n}
and
|\phi_{1}\rangle_{n}
are normalized states, but not necessary orthogonal.
If, by applying the one-qubit M gate, one measures only single qubit (getting
|0\rangle
with
p_{0}
and
|1\rangle
with
p_{1}
),
then the
n+1
-qubit state becomes the product state
|0\rangle|\phi_{0}\rangle_{n}
or
|1\rangle|\phi_{1}\rangle_{n}
.
Applying this rule
n
-times, reduces the process of measurement to application of multiple copies of a single elementary hadware - the one-qubit measurement gate.

Here we count probability of measurement the quantum coin states in

N-plicated qubit measurement

The recursion formula and initial values for probabilities are

\displaystyle P_{n}=p_{0}(P_{n-1}+p_{1}P_{n-2}+...+p_{1}^{N-1}P_{n-N}),

(34)

\displaystyle P_{1}=P_{2}=...=P_{N-1}=0,P_{N}=p_{1}^{N}.

(35)

The first few polynomials
P_{N}=p_{1}^{N}
,
P_{N+1}=P_{N+2}=...P_{2N}=p_{0}p_{1}^{N}
,
and

P_{2N+1}=p_{0}^{2}p_{1}^{N}(1+p_{1}+p_{1}^{2}+...+p_{1}^{N-1})=p_{0}p_{1}^{N}(1-p^{N}_{1}),

(36)

are polynomials on the Bloch sphere

P_{N}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}(1-\cos\theta)^{N}
,
P_{N+1}=...=P_{2N}=\frac{1}{2^{N+1}}(1-\cos\theta)^{N}(1+\cos\theta)
.
The polynomials correspond to addition of different paths on
N
-Bonacci tree (Figure 3), by attaching probability
p_{0}
to every state
|0\rangle
and
p_{1}
to every state
|1\rangle
.

5.1
Arbitrary position of duplicated states

Here we treat the generic case of an arbitrary position of duplicated states in Hadamard coin states.
If duplicated
|1\rangle|1\rangle

n

\leftarrow\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{n-2}\otimes\underbrace{|1\rangle\otimes|1\rangle}_{2}\equiv\leftarrow\underbrace{|*\rangle|*\rangle...|*\rangle|1\rangle|1\rangle}_{n},\hskip 28.45274ptA_{n}=F_{n-1}

(37)

the Fibonacci tree is growing to the left and the number of allowed states is
A_{n}=F_{n-1}
.
If these states appear only in the first two positions
n=1
and
n=2

\rightarrow\underbrace{|1\rangle\otimes|1\rangle}_{2}\otimes\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{n-2}\equiv\rightarrow\underbrace{|1\rangle|1\rangle|*\rangle|*\rangle...|*\rangle}_{n},\hskip 28.45274ptA_{n}=F_{n-1}

(38)

the Fibonacci tree grows to the right and the number of allowed states is the same
A_{n}=F_{n-1}
.

Now, we consider the general case of allowed states with only position for
|1\rangle|1\rangle
states
at
k
and
k+1
, where
k=1,2,...,n-1

\leftarrow\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{k-1}\otimes\underbrace{|1\rangle\otimes|1\rangle}_{k\qquad k+1}\otimes\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{n-k-1}\rightarrow.

(39)

The number of states is determined now by two Fibonacci trees, one is
k+1

F_{k}
and another one is the tree of
n-k+1
qubits, growing to the right, with number of the states
F_{n-k}

.
From this we conclude that for
n
-qubit state, the probability to have duplicated

k

P_{n,k}=\frac{F_{k}\cdot F_{n-k}}{2^{n}}=\frac{L_{n}-(-1)^{k}L_{n-2k}}{5\cdot 2^{n}},

where

F_{m}\cdot F_{n}=\frac{L_{m+n}-(-1)^{n}L_{m-n}}{5}.

(41)

The number of

-qubit states in which

states appear just once, independently of the position is

\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}F_{k}\cdot F_{n-k}=\frac{n\,L_{n}-F_{n}}{5}.

(42)

The corresponding probability to have this states once, but anywhere is

\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}P_{n,k}=\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}\frac{F_{k}\cdot F_{n-k}}{2^{n}}=\frac{n\,L_{n}-F_{n}}{5\cdot 2^{n}}.

(43)

6

6.1
Generating function for duplicated probability

For duplicated states of Hadamard quantum coin, the generating function of probabilities (
15

g(x)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}P_{n}\,x^{n-2}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P_{n+2}\,x^{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{F_{n+1}}{2^{n+2}}x^{n}=\frac{1}{4-2x-x^{2}}.

(48)

This can be shown by using recursion formula (
13

For

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P_{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{F_{n+1}}{2^{n+2}}=1,

(49)

The identity is the completeness relation for duplicated states (
11
) and it shows that duplicated state will appear once in the set of all numbers
n
trials.
This relation allows to introduce information characteristic of the states by the Shannon entropy (
54
)
and orreponding qubit states, realized by random walk in Fock space of computational states.

6.1.1
Golden ratio in computational states

For duplicated
n
-qubit states (
11
), the number of states is
A_{n}=F_{n-1}
, and for duplicated
n+1
-qubit states it is
A_{n+1}=F_{n}
. The ratio of these numbers in the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_{n}}=\frac{F_{n}}{F_{n-1}}=\varphi.

(50)

6.1.2
Golden ratio in Hadamard coin measurements

The probability to get duplicated states (
11
) in
n
-measurements of the Hadamard coin states is

P_{n}=\frac{F_{n-1}}{2^{n}}
and for
n+1
-measuremnts it is
P_{n+1}=\frac{F_{n}}{2^{n+1}}
.
The ratio of these probabilities in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
is half of the Golden ratio:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{P_{n+1}}{P_{n}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{n}}{2F_{n-1}}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi.

(51)

6.1.3
Golden Ratio for arbitrary position

We take
n
-qubit state with allowed number of states
A_{n}
and
n+1
-qubit state with number
A_{n+1}
from Eq.(
42
). The ratio of these numbers in the limit

is the Golden ratio

\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_{n}}=\frac{(n+1)\,L_{n+1}-F_{n+1}}{n\,L_{n}-F_{n}}\rightarrow\varphi.

(52)

The ratio of corresponding probabilities (
43
)
to find
|1\rangle|1\rangle
pair at arbitrary position, but just only once, in the set of
n
and
n+1
trials, in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
is half of the Golden Ratio

\frac{P_{n+1}}{P_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(n+1)\,L_{n+1}-F_{n+1}}{n\,L_{n}-F_{n}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\varphi.

(53)

The same limit is valid for ratio of probabilies
P_{n,k}
in (
40
) at position
k
,
k+1
, where
k=1,2,...,n-1
.

6.1.4
Shannon entropy

The Hadamard quantum tree in Figure 1, at every level
n
determines probability
P_{n}=\frac{F_{n-1}}{2^{n}}
and as we have seen, the sum of probabilities (
49
) is one,
\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}P_{n}=1
. The level of randomness in this distribution can be characterized by the Shannon entropy

S=-\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}P_{n}\log_{2}P_{n}=-\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\frac{F_{n-1}}{2^{n}}\log_{2}\frac{F_{n-1}}{2^{n}}.

(54)

The ratio test for this series gives half of the Golden ratio

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{s_{n+1}}{s_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi<1
and convergency of this sum.
For the
n
-th term, assymptotically we have

s_{n}\approx n\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)^{n}\frac{\log_{2}\frac{2}{\varphi}}{\sqrt{5}}.

(55)

6.2
Generating function for triplicated probability

The generating function of Tribonacci numbers is defined as

g_{T}(x)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}T_{n}x^{n}=x^{2}+x^{3}+2x^{4}+4x^{5}+...

(56)

By substituting the recursion formula for Tribonacci numbers (
19
) and using values
T_{-1}=1
,
T_{-2}=-1
,
T_{-3}=0

we get

g_{T}(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{1-x-x^{2}-x^{3}}.

(57)

For Hadamard quantum coin (
4
) we can define generating function of Tribonacci probabilities

(
21
),

g(x)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P_{n+3}\,x^{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{T_{n+2}}{2^{n+3}}\,x^{n}.

(58)

By using recursion formula for Tribonacci numbers we find

g(x)=\frac{1}{2^{3}-2^{2}x-2x^{2}-x^{3}}.

(59)

If in this formula we take

(60)

The last relation allows to introduce the Shannon entropy for triplicated states in Fock space in a similar to (

P_{n}=\frac{D_{n-1}}{d^{n}}.

(92)

The recursion formula for these probabilities is

P_{n+1}=\frac{d-1}{d}P_{n}+\frac{d-1}{d^{2}}P_{n-1},

(93)

with initial values
P_{2}=\frac{1}{d^{2}},P_{3}=\frac{d-1}{d^{3}}
.

For generic qudit coin state

|\psi\rangle=c_{0}|0\rangle+c_{1}|1\rangle+...+c_{d-1}|d-1\rangle

(94)

probabilities to measure basis states are
p_{i}=|c_{i}|^{2}
,
i=0,1,...,d-1
and
\sum^{d-1}_{i=0}p_{i}=1
.
Probability
P_{n}
to measure duplicated state
|1\rangle|1\rangle
only at the end of
n
trials, satisfies the recursion formula

P_{n}=(1-p_{1})P_{n-1}+(1-p_{1})p_{1}P_{n-2},

(95)

with initial values
P_{2}=p^{2}_{1}
,
P_{3}=p_{1}^{2}(1-p_{1})
. In thess formulas
1-p_{1}=p_{0}+p_{2}+...+p_{d-1}
. The formula can be generalized also to the case, when duplicated state at the end of
n
trials is
|k\rangle|k\rangle
, where
k=0,1,2,..,d-1
. The probability to have thise state

\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{n-2}\otimes\underbrace{|k\rangle\otimes|k\rangle}_{2}\equiv\underbrace{|*\rangle|*\rangle...|*\rangle|k\rangle|k\rangle,}_{n}

(96)

satisfies recursion formula

P_{n}=(1-p_{k})P_{n-1}+(1-p_{k})p_{k}P_{n-2},

(97)

with initial values
P_{2}=p^{2}_{k}
,
P_{3}=p_{k}^{2}(1-p_{k})
and
1-p_{k}=p_{0}+...+p_{k-1}+p_{k+1}+...+p_{d-1}
.

9.2
Triplicated States

For maximally random coin, number of allowed states
B_{n}=E_{n-1}
, where
E_{n}
are generalized Tribonacci numbers

E_{n}=(d-1)(E_{n-1}+E_{n-2}+E_{n-3}),

(98)

and initial values
E_{0}=0,E_{1}=0,E_{2}=1
. First few numbers are
E_{3}=d-1
,
E_{4}=(d-1)d
,

E_{5}=(d-1)d^{2}
. For probabilities of these states it gives

P_{n}=\frac{E_{n-1}}{d^{n}}

(99)

and recursion formula

P_{n+1}=\frac{d-1}{d}\left(P_{n}+\frac{1}{d}P_{n-1}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}P_{n-2}\right),

(100)

with
P_{1}=P_{2}=0
,
P_{3}=\frac{1}{d^{3}}
,
P_{4}=\frac{d-1}{d^{4}}
.

For generic qudit coin (
94
), probability of triplicated state
P_{n}
is polynomial in
p_{1}
,

P_{n+1}=(1-p_{1})(P_{n}+p_{1}P_{n-1}+p^{2}_{1}P_{n-2}),

(101)

with
P_{1}=P_{2}=0
,
P_{3}=p^{3}_{1}
,
P_{4}=p^{3}_{1}(1-p_{1})
,
where
1-p_{1}=p_{0}+p_{2}+p_{3}+...+p_{d-1}
.

9.3
N-plicated States

For arbitrary qudit state (
94
), probability
P_{n}
to find N-plicated state

\underbrace{|*\rangle\otimes|*\rangle\otimes...|*\rangle}_{n-N}\otimes\underbrace{|1\rangle\otimes|1\rangle...\otimes|1\rangle}_{N}\equiv\underbrace{|*\rangle|*\rangle...|*\rangle|1\rangle|1\rangle...|1\rangle}_{n}

(102)

where
|*\rangle=|0\rangle,|1\rangle,...,|d-1\rangle
, is expressed by generalized
N
-Bonacci polynomials

P_{n}=(1-p_{1})(P_{n-1}+p_{1}P_{n-2}+p^{2}_{1}P_{n-3}+...+p_{1}^{N-1}P_{n-N}),

(103)

with
P_{1}=P_{2}=...=P_{N-1}=0
,
P_{N}=p^{N}_{1}
,
where
1-p_{1}=p_{0}+p_{2}+p_{3}+...+p_{d-1}
.

Non-locality, non-linearity, and existence of solutions to the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions in metric measure spaces
Josh Kline

Abstract
We study the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions for domains in metric spaces equipped with a doubling measure and supporting a (1,1)-Poincaré inequality when the boundary of the domain satisfies a positive mean curvature condition. In this setting, it was shown by Malý, Lahti, Shanmugalingam, and Speight that solutions exist for continuous boundary data. We extend these results, showing existence of solutions for boundary data that is approximable from above and below by continuous functions. We also show that for each f ∈ L 1 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) , f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega), there is a least gradient function in Ω \Omega whose trace agrees with f f at points of continuity of f f , and so we obtain existence of solutions for boundary data which is continuous almost everywhere. This is in contrast to a result of Spradlin and Tamasan, who constructed an L 1 L^{1} -function on the unit circle which has no least gradient solution in the unit disk in ℝ 2 . \mathbb{R}^{2}. Modifying the example of Spradlin and Tamasan, we show that the space of solvable L 1 L^{1} -functions on the unit circle is non-linear, even though the unit disk satisfies the positive mean curvature condition.

1
Introduction

Given a function
f
on the boundary of a domain
\Omega,
the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions is the problem of minimizing

\|Du\|(\Omega)
over all
u\in BV(\Omega)
with trace
Tu=f
a.e. on the boundary. This form of the problem, where the boundary condition is attained in the sense of traces, was originally introduced in the Euclidean setting by Sternberg, Williams, and Ziemer in
[
37
]
. There they showed that if the boundary of the domain has non-negative mean curvature and is not locally area minimizing, then existence and uniqueness of solutions is guaranteed for continuous boundary data. Furthermore, they showed that the imposed curvature conditions are necessary to guarantee existence of solutions, and if the boundary data is of class
C^{0,\alpha}
for
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then the solution is of class
C^{0,\alpha/2},
provided the boundary of the domain has strictly positive mean curvature. Their proof makes uses an important result of Bombieri, De Giorgi, and Giusti from
[
7
]
, which states that characteristic functions of superlevel sets of least gradient functions are themselves of least gradient. Using this, the authors constructed a least gradient solution by building its superlevel sets so that each was of least gradient and compatible with the boundary data.

Since the appearance of
[
37
]
, existence, uniqueness, and regularity of the above least gradient problem have been studied extensively in the Euclidean setting. For instance, we refer the interested reader to the following sampling
[
12
,
10
,
11
,
33
,
34
,
14
,
29
]
and the references therein. In particular, it was shown in
[
29
]
that there is an equivalence between least gradient solutions and solutions to the Dirichlet problem for the 1-Laplacian. Moreover, the authors showed that in strictly convex domains, uniqueness of solutions may fail for discontinuous boundary data. For more on the study of uniqueness of solutions, see
[
11
]
.

In recent decades, a theory of analysis on metric measure spaces has been developed under the assumptions that the measure is doubling and that the space supports a Poincaré inequality, see for example
[
6
,
18
,
20
,
3
]
. Miranda Jr. extended the definition of BV functions to this setting in
[
30
]
, leading to the development of a theory of least gradient functions and associated Dirichlet problems in metric spaces
[
1
,
4
,
17
,
22
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
. In
[
25
]
, Lahti, Malý, Shanmugalingam, and Speight studied the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions, originally introduced in
[
37
]
, in the setting of a doubling metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality. There they introduced a notion of positive mean curvature which makes sense in the metric setting (Definition
2.12
below). They showed that if a domain satisfies this condition and if compatibility conditions are assumed between the measure and the codimension 1 Hausdorff measure of the boundary of the domain, then existence of solutions is guaranteed for continuous boundary data. Their strategy adapts the argument from
[
37
]
to the metric setting, similarly building a solution by constructing its superlevel sets in an appropriate manner.

In contrast to
[
37
]
,
[
25
]
also provided examples in the weighted Euclidean setting which show that even for Lipschitz boundary data, solutions may fail to be continuous up to the boundary and may fail to be unique. However, it was recently shown in
[
38
]
that continuous solutions exist for the weighted Euclidean least gradient problem with continuous boundary data, provided the weights are positive, bounded away from zero, and of class
C^{2}.
This result, valid in dimensions
n\geq 2,
extends the earlier result from
[
21
]
, which guarantees existence of continuous solutions in low dimensions for
C^{1,1}
weights which are positive and bounded away from zero. In
[
21
]
, it was also shown that for such weights and continuous boundary data, solutions to the weighted problem in dimensions
n\geq 2
are unique. For more on the weighted Euclidean least gradient problem, anisotropic formulations, and connections of these problems to conductivity imaging, see
[
32
,
31
,
12
,
10
]
. Such applications provide additional motivation for the study of the least gradient problem in the metric setting. For a recent work on Gauss-Green formulas and connections to the least gradient problem in the metric setting, see
[
13
]
.

From
[
37
]
in the Euclidean setting, certain non-negative curvature conditions are required to guarantee existence of solutions to the Dirichlet problem for least gradients. From
[
5
,
9
]
, it is also known that the the trace class of BV functions on a Euclidean Lipschitz domain is the
L^{1}
-class of its boundary. In fact, analagous trace and extension results hold for BV functions in the metric setting as well, see
[
26
,
28
]
. Therefore if a Euclidean domain satisfies the curvature conditions from
[
37
]
, it is natural to ask whether all
L^{1}
-functions on the boundary of such a domain admit solutions to the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions. This question was answered in the negative by Spradlin and Tamasan in
[
36
]
. A certain fat Cantor set was constructed on the boundary of the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
[
36
]
, such that the characteristic function of that set is not the trace of a least gradient function in the unit disk, despite the fact that the unit disk satisfies the necessary curvature conditions. Thus the question of which
L^{1}
-functions arise as traces of functions of least gradient is open even in the Euclidean setting. The goal of this paper is to investigate the conditions sufficient to guarantee existence of solutions to the Dirichlet problem in both the Euclidean and metric settings.

After introducing the necessary definitions and background information in Section 2, we begin Section 3 by examining the example presented in
[
36
]
in the Euclidean setting of the unit disk (Example
3.1
below). We modify this example in such a way as to obtain a solution (Example
3.3
below) which demonstrates that the set of
L^{1}
-functions on the boundary of the unit disk for which solutions exist is non-linear. Namely, we show that the example function from
[
36
]
can be expressed as the sum of two functions, each of which arise as the trace of a least gradient function. Moreover, our example shows that even in the case of the unit disk, the Dirichlet problem is non-local in the following sense. There is a boundary data
f
for which a least gradient solution to the Dirichlet problem exists, but
\eta f
has no solution for a suitable compactly supported Lipschitz function
\eta
on the boundary. This example illustrates the significant difference between the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions and the Dirichlet problem for
p
-harmonic functions when
p>1
, see
[
6
]
.

In Sections 4 and 5, we obtain sufficient conditions for existence of solutions in the metric setting under the following standing assumptions:

•

(X,d,\mu)
is a complete metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, with
\mu
a doubling Borel regular measure.

•

\Omega\subset X
is a bounded domain with
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0.

•

\partial\Omega
has positive mean curvature as in
[
25
]
, see Definition
2.12
.

•

\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty
,
\mathcal{H}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is doubling, and
\mathcal{H}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is lower codimension 1 Ahlfors regular, see (
2.6
).

Here,
\mathcal{H}
is a codimension 1 Hausdorff measure on
\partial\Omega
, see (
2.5
). The examples presented in Section 3 are in the setting of the unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2},
which satisfies the above assumptions as well. The following is the first of the main results of the paper, proved in Section 4.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
g_{k},h_{k}\in C(\partial\Omega)
be such that
g_{k},h_{k}\to f
as
k\to\infty
pointwise
\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
, with

g_{k}\leq g_{k+1}\leq f\leq h_{k+1}\leq h_{k}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Then, there is a function
u\in BV(\Omega)
which is the minimal solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

The following is an equivalent reformulation of the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
:

•

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and assume that there is a lower semicontinuous function
g
on
\partial\Omega
and an upper semicontinuous function
h
on
\partial\Omega
such that
g\leq h
everywhere on
\partial\Omega
and
g=h=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

The key step is to show existence of minimal solutions for continuous boundary data, from which we obtain a comparison theorem for minimal solutions; note that uniqueness of solutions is not guaranteed (see
[
25
]
), and so a more general comparison theorem will not hold for least gradient functions. This is in contrast to
p
-harmonic functions with
p>1
, which always satisfy a comparison theorem (see
[
6
]
).

As a consequence, we obtain the following result regarding characteristic functions of subsets of the boundary of the domain.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F\subset\partial\Omega
be measurable, and let
\widetilde{\partial}F
denote the boundary of
F
relative to
\partial\Omega
. If
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}F)=0,
then there is a function
u\in BV(\Omega)
which is the minimal solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F}.

In Section 5, we continue to adopt the setting and assumptions from the previous section. By adapting an argument from
[
10
]
in the Euclidean setting, we use the metric technology of discrete convolution to obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, there exists a least gradient function
u\in BV(\Omega)
such that for all
x\in\partial\Omega
such that
f
is continuous at
x
, we have that
Tu(x)=f(x).
In particular, if
f
is continuous
\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
then there is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

This result was established for strictly convex, Euclidean domains in
[
10
]
. Our extension to the metric setting includes Euclidean domains that are not strictly convex but satisfy the positive mean curvature condition. For example, the capped cylinder described in Remark
5.8
below is not strictly convex, but satisfies the positive mean curvature condition.

Although Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
, and Theorem
1.3
provide sufficient conditions on the boundary data to guarantee existence of solutions in this setting, Example
3.3
below shows that these conditions are not sharp (see Remarks
4.14
and
5.10
). It seems that even for sufficiently regular domains, a characterization of
L^{1}
boundary data admitting solutions is still unknown.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
General metric measure spaces and BV theory

Throughout this paper, we assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete metric measure space equipped with a doubling Borel regular measure
\mu
. By
doubling
, we mean that there exists a constant
C_{D}\geq 1
such that

0<\mu(B(x,2r)\leq C_{D}\mu(B(x,r))<\infty

for all
x\in X
and
r>0.
By iterating the doubling condition, there are constants
C\geq 1
and
Q>1
such that

\frac{\mu(B(y,r))}{\mu(B(x,R))}\geq C^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{Q}

(2.1)

for every
0<r\leq R
and
y\in B(x,R).

A complete metric space equipped with a doubling measure is proper, that is, closed and bounded sets are compact. Thus for any open set
\Omega\subset X
, we define
L_{\text{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
as the space of functions that are in
L^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega,
i.e., for every open set
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
is a compact subset of
\Omega.
Also, if
A
and
B
are subsets of
X
, we use the notation
A\sqsubset B
to mean that
\mu(A\setminus B)=0.
By a
domain
, we mean a non-empty connected open set in
X
.

Given a function
u:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},
we say that a Borel function
g:X\to[0,\infty]
is an
upper gradient
of
u
if the following inequality holds for all non-constant compact rectifiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to X,

|u(y)-u(x)|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds,

whenever
u(x)
and
u(y)
are both finite, and
\int_{\gamma}g\,ds=\infty
otherwise. Here
x
and
y
denote the endpoints of the curve
\gamma
. Upper gradients were originally introduced in
[
19
]
.

Let
\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)
be the class of all functions in
L^{1}(X)
for which there exists an upper gradient in
L^{1}(X).
For
u\in\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X),
we define

\|u\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)}=\|u\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\inf_{g}\|g\|_{L^{1}(X)},

where the infimum is taken over all upper gradients
g
of
u
. Now, we define an equivalence relation in
\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)
by
u\sim v
if and only if
\|u-v\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)}=0.

The
Newtonian space

N^{1,1}(X)
is defined as the quotient
\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)/\sim,
and it is equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{N^{1,1}(X)}=\|u\|_{\widetilde{N}^{1,1}(X)}.
One can analogously define
N^{1,1}(\Omega)
for an open set
\Omega\subset X.
For more on Newtonian spaces, see
[
35
]
,
[
20
]
, or
[
6
]
.

We now define functions of bounded variation on metric spaces, following the definition introduced by Miranda Jr. in
[
30
]
. For
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X),
we define the
total variation
of
u
by

\|Du\|(X)=\inf\left\{\liminf_{i\to\infty}\int_{X}g_{u_{i}}\,d\mu:N^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}(X)\ni u_{i}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X)\right\},

where
g_{u_{i}}
are upper gradients of
u_{i}.
For an open set
\Omega\subset X,
we analogously define
\|Du\|(\Omega),
and for an arbitrary
A\subset X,
we define

\|Du\|(A)=\inf\left\{\|Du\|(\Omega):A\subset\Omega,\Omega\subset X\text{ open}\right\}.

For
u\in L^{1}(X),
we say that
u\in BV(X)
(
u
is of
bounded variation
) if
\|Du\|(X)<\infty.
We equip
BV(X)
with the norm

\|u\|_{BV(X)}=\|u\|_{L^{1}(X)}+\|Du\|(X).

We note that this definition coincides with the standard definition of the BV class in the Euclidean setting, see for example
[
8
,
2
]
. See also
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
for more on BV theory in the metric setting.

For
u\in BV(X),
it was shown in
[
30
, Theorem 3.4]
that
\|Du\|(\cdot)
is a finite Radon measure on
X.
Moreover, for an open set
\Omega\subset X,
if
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega),
then

\|Du\|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|Du_{k}\|(\Omega).

(2.2)

That is, the BV energy is lower semi-continuous with respect to convergence in
L^{1}
.
[
30
, Proposition 3.6]

We say that a measurable set
E\subset X
is of
finite perimeter
if
\|D\chi_{E}\|(X)<\infty,
and we denote the
perimeter
of
E
in
\Omega
by

P(E,\Omega):=\|D\chi_{E}\|(\Omega).

We have the following coarea formula, given by
[
30
, Proposition 4.2]
. If
\Omega\subset X
is an open set and
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega),
then

\|Du\|(\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\{u>t\},\Omega)\,dt,

(2.3)

and if
u\in BV(X),
then the above holds with
\Omega
replaced by any Borel set
A\subset\Omega.

2.2
Poincaré inequality and consequences

We will also assume throughout this paper that
X
supports a
(1,1)
-
Poincaré inequality
, meaning that there are positive constants
\lambda
and
C_{P}
such that for every ball
B=B(x,r),
every locally integrable function
u
, and every upper gradient
g
of
u
, we have that

\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|d\mu\leq C_{P}r\fint_{\lambda B}g\,d\mu,

where
\lambda B:=B(x,\lambda r),
and

u_{B}:=\fint_{B}u\,d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}u\,d\mu.

Throughout this paper, we let
C
denote a constant which depends, unless otherwise noted, on
C_{D},

C_{P},

\lambda
, or
\Omega.
Its precise value is not of interest here and may not be the same at each occurrence.

As shown in
[
16
]
, when
\mu
is doubling, the (1,1)-Poincaré inequality implies the following Sobolev-Poincaré inequality,

\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq C\rad(B)\fint_{\lambda B}g_{u}\,d\mu,

where
Q>1
is the exponent from (
2.1
). Given
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X),
one can apply this inequality to the approximating functions in
N^{1,1}(X)
in the definition of total variation to obtain the inequality

\left(\fint_{B}|u-u_{B}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq C\rad(B)\frac{\|Du\|(2\lambda B)}{\mu(2\lambda B)},

from which the following lemma is obtained in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
u\in BV(X)
, and for a ball
B\subset X
, let

A=\{x\in B:|u(x)|>0\}.

If
\mu(A)\leq\gamma\mu(B)
for some
0<\gamma<1,
then

\left(\fint_{B}|u|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}\leq\frac{C\rad(B)}{1-\gamma^{1/Q}}\frac{\|Du\|(2\lambda B)}{\mu(2\lambda B)},

where
Q>1
is the lower mass bound exponent given in (
2.1
).

We will use the above lemma in the proof of Lemma
5.4
to obtain
L^{1}
-bounds for a sequence of BV functions.

Given
E\subset X
, we define its
codimension
1
Hausdorff measure
,
\mathcal{H}(E)
, by

\mathcal{H}(E)=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\inf\left\{\sum_{i}\frac{\mu(B_{i})}{\rad(B_{i})}:B_{i}\text{ balls in }X,\,E\subset\bigcup_{i}B_{i},\,\rad(B_{i})<\delta\right\}.

(2.5)

We say that
\mathcal{H}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is
lower codimension
1
Ahlfors regular
if there exists
C>0
such that

\mathcal{H}(B(x,r)\cap\partial\Omega)\geq C\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r}

(2.6)

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<2\diam(\partial\Omega).

It was shown in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
that if
\mu
is doubling and
X
supports a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, then there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that whenever
E\subset X
is of finite perimeter and
A\subset X
is a Borel set, we have

C^{-1}\mathcal{H}(A\cap\partial_{M}E)\leq P(E,A)\leq C\mathcal{H}(A\cap\partial_{M}E),

where
\partial_{M}E
is the
measure-theoretic boundary
of
E
, which is the set of all points
x\in X
for which

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\cap E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(B(x,r)\setminus E)}{\mu(B(x,r))}>0.

Given an extended real-valued function
u
on
X
, we define the approximate upper and lower limits of
u
by

\displaystyle u^{\vee}(x)

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(\{u>t\}\cap B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\},

\displaystyle u^{\wedge}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(\{u<t\}\cap B(x,r))}{\mu(B(x,r))}=0\right\}.

From the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,
u^{\vee}=u^{\wedge}

\mu
-a.e. if
u\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(X).

2.3
Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions

Definition 2.7
.

Given a bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
and a function
u\in BV(\Omega),
we say that
u
has a
trace
at a point
x\in\partial\Omega
if there is a number
Tu(x)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}|u-Tu(x)|d\mu=0.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open set, and let
u\in BV_{\text{loc}}(\Omega).
We say that
u
is of
least gradient in
\Omega
if

\|Du\|(V)\leq\|Dv\|(V),

whenever
v\in BV(\Omega)
with
\overline{\{x\in\Omega:u(x)\neq v(x)\}}\subset V\Subset\Omega.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
X
with
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0,
and let
f\in BV_{\text{loc}}(X).
We say that
u\in BV_{\text{loc}}(X)
is a
weak solution to the Dirichlet problem for least gradients in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
, or simply,
weak solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, if
u=f
on
X\setminus\Omega
and

\|Du\|(\overline{\Omega})\leq\|Dv\|(\overline{\Omega}),

whenever
v\in BV(X)
with
v=f
on
X\setminus\Omega.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
X
and
f:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}.
We say that a function
u\in BV(\Omega)
is a
solution to the Dirichlet problem for least gradients in
\Omega
with boundary data
f
, or simply,
solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, if
Tu=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
and whenever
v\in BV(\Omega),
with
Tv=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
we must have

\|Du\|(\Omega)\leq\|Dv\|(\Omega).

Note that solutions and weak solutions to Dirichlet problems on a domain
\Omega
are necessarily of least gradient in
\Omega.

Definition 2.11
.

A (weak) solution
\chi_{E}
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F}
is called a
minimal (weak) solution
to the said problem if every (weak) solution
\chi_{\widetilde{E}}
corresponding to the data
\chi_{F}
satisfies
E\sqsubset\widetilde{E},
that is,
\mu(E\setminus\widetilde{E})=0
, or alternatively,
\chi_{E}\leq\chi_{\widetilde{E}}

\mu
-a.e. in
X.

It is shown in
[
25
]
that if
F\subset X
is such that
P(F,X)<\infty,
then there is a set
E\subset X
with
P(E,X)<\infty
such that
\chi_{E}
is a weak solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F}.
We call
E
a
weak solution set
. Moreover, for such an
F
, there is a minimal weak solution, and such a minimal weak solution is unique
\mu
-a.e. in
X,

[
25
, Proposition 3.7]
. However, without additional assumptions on
\Omega,
the trace of the weak solution may not agree with
\chi_{F}
on
\partial\Omega.
That is, a weak solution may not necessarily be a solution. For example, if
\Omega=(0,1)\times(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},
and
F
is the disk centered at
(1/2,0)
of radius
1/10,
then the trace of the minimal weak solution will have zero trace on
\partial\Omega,
and in fact there is no least gradient function with the appropriate trace on the boundary. To address this issue, the following definition was introduced in
[
25
]
, extending the formulation from
[
37
]
to the metric setting.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a domain
\Omega\subset X
, we say that the boundary
\partial\Omega
has
positive mean curvature
if for each
x\in\partial\Omega,
there exists a non-decreasing function
\phi_{x}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and a constant
r_{x}>0
such that for all
0<r<r_{x}
with
P(B(x,r),X)<\infty
, we have that
B(x,\phi_{x}(r))\sqsubset E_{B(x,r)},
where
E_{B(x,r)}\subset X
gives the minimal weak solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{B(x,r)},
as defined above.

In
[
25
]
, positive mean curvature is defined by existence of
\phi
and
r_{0}>0
so that the condition is satisfied for all
x\in\partial\Omega
and all
0<r<r_{0}.
However, the results from
[
25
]
hold if the definition is weakened to allow dependence on
x
, as above.

Remark 2.13
.

It is shown in
[
25
, Proposition 4.8, 4.9]
that if
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty,
and
F\subset X
is open with

and
\mathcal{H}(\partial F\cap\partial\Omega)=0,
then under the assumption of positive mean curvature, all weak solutions of
\chi_{F}
are solutions. Additionally, if

is a solution for
\chi_{F},
then extending

outside
\Omega
by
\chi_{F}
yields a weak solution.

2\sin(\mathcal{H}(A)/2)\geq 2\sin\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}(B_{1})+\mathcal{H}(C_{3})}{2}\right)+\mathcal{H}(B_{2}\setminus K_{1/4}).

Now, we repeat the argument for (
3.4
), this time setting
C_{1}:=C_{3}.
Once again, the claim holds, and so it follows that

2\sin\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}(B_{1})+\mathcal{H}(C_{3})}{2}\right)\geq 2\sin(\mathcal{H}(C_{3})/2)+\mathcal{H}(B_{1}\setminus K_{1/4}).

Thus, we have that

2\sin(\mathcal{H}(A)/2)\geq 2\sin(\mathcal{H}(C_{3})/2)+\mathcal{H}(B_{1}\setminus K_{1/4})+\mathcal{H}(B_{2}\setminus K_{1/4})>\|Dv_{n}\|(\overline{\Omega}_{A}\cap\Omega),

again contradicting the fact that
w
is a solution. Having exhausted all the possible cases, it follows that the connected component
E_{0}
cannot contain multiple arcs from
\mathcal{C}_{n}
in its boundary. Therefore
w=v_{n},
and so
v_{n}
solves the Dirichlet problem for boundary data
g_{n}
.

Let
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be given as follows. Let
\mathcal{C}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}_{n}.
For each
C\in\mathcal{C},
let
v=0
on the region of
\Omega
bounded by
C
and the chord joining the endpoints of
C.
Let
v=1
elsewhere in
\Omega.
Then,
v_{n}\to v
in
L^{1}(\Omega),
and so by
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
, it follows that
v
is a function of least gradient in
\Omega.
If
x\in\partial\Omega\setminus(K_{1/4}\cup F),
then there exists
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
x\in C.
Since each
C
is an open arc on
\partial\Omega
, it follows that
v=0
on
B(x,r)\cap\Omega
for small enough
r>0.
Thus,
Tv(x)=0=g(x).
Similarly, if
x\in F,
and
x
is not one of the endpoints of
F
, then
Tv(x)=1=g(x).

For
x\in K_{1/4},
there exists a fixed angle
\theta
such that
v=1
on the circular sector of angle
\theta
in
B(x,r)\cap\Omega
for sufficiently small
r>0.
Thus,
Tv(x)>0.
Since
v
is the characteristic function of a set in
\Omega,

Tv
must take values of either
1
or
0

\mathcal{H}
-a.e., see
[
12
, Example 4.8]
. Therefore
Tv(x)=1=g(x)
for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in K_{1/4},
and so
Tv=g

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Hence by Lemma
4.3
,
v
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g
.

Remark 3.5
.

Given a domain
\Omega,
let
\mathcal{S}\subset L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
denote the set of functions
f
on
\partial\Omega
for which there exists a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f.
Examples
3.1
and
3.3
show that
\mathcal{S}
does not necessarily form a vector space, even when
\Omega=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In particular,
\mathcal{S}
can be non-linear. As shown above,
g
and
\chi_{F}
both have solutions, but
g-\chi_{F}=\chi_{K_{1/4}}=f
has no solution.

Remark 3.6
.

Example
3.3
also shows that solutions to the Dirichlet problem for least gradient functions may be non-local in the following sense. In Example
3.3
, a solution exists for boundary data
g=\chi_{K_{1/4}\cup F}.
However, for any
I_{k,m}
from the construction of
K_{1/4}
, let
\eta_{k,m}
denote a Lipschitz function on
\partial\Omega
such that
\eta_{k,m}=1
on
I_{k,m}
and whose support is contained in the union of
I_{k,m}
and the two removed arcs adjacent to
I_{k,m}.
Then there is no solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\eta_{k,m}g=\chi_{K_{1/4}\cap I_{k,m}}.
This is because the set
K_{1/4}\cap I_{k,m}
can be constructed in the same way that
K_{1/4}
is constructed in Example
3.1
. By the choice of the parameter
a=1/4,
the same inequality between the side and base lengths of the trapezoids created in the construction holds at every stage, and so the same argument from Example
3.1
shows that no solution exists for boundary data
\eta_{k,m}g.
This non-locality contrasts the case involving solutions to the Dirichlet problem for
p
-harmonic functions when
p>1.
Such solutions are known to exist if the boundary data
f
is the trace of a function in
N^{1,p}(\Omega).
If
\eta
is a compactly supported Lipschitz function on
\partial\Omega
, then
\eta f
is similarly the trace of a function in
N^{1,p}(\Omega)
and hence has a solution. For more on the Dirichlet problem for
p
-harmonic functions and traces of functions of class
N^{1,p}
when
p>1,
see for example
[
6
]
and
[
27
]
.

4
Sufficient conditions for existence of solutions

For the remainder of this paper, we assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is a complete metric measure space supporting a
(1,1)
-Poincaré inequality, with
\mu
a doubling Borel regular measure. We also assume that
\Omega\subset X
is a bounded domain, with
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0
, such that
\partial\Omega
has positive mean curvature as in Definition
2.12
. Furthermore, we assume that
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty,
that
\mathcal{H}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is doubling, and that
\mathcal{H}\big|_{\partial\Omega}
is lower codimension 1 Ahlfors regular, as in (
2.6
). The unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies the above conditions, and so Example
3.1
above shows that an
L^{1}
-function on the boundary of such a domain need not have a solution. However, for such domains, it was shown in
[
25
]
that given
f\in C(\partial\Omega),
there exists a solution
u\in BV(\Omega)
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
. We show that the solution for continuous data constructed in
[
25
]
is in fact the minimal solution, and that if an arbitrary
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
can be approximated pointwise a.e. from above and below by continuous functions, then there exists a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
. We point out that approximation of the boundary data in this manner was used in
[
34
]
to show existence of solutions for convex polygonal domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}.
There, the boundary data belonged to
BV(\partial\Omega)
and satisfied restrictive admissibility conditions with respect to the geometry of the boundary.

Since we are interested in finding solutions to the Dirichlet problem given data
f
defined on
\partial\Omega,
we need methods and control over extensions of
f
into
\Omega
and to the entire space
X
. The following results from
[
28
]
and
[
25
]
give existence and bounds for such extensions.

Proposition 4.1
.

(
[
28
, Propositions 4.2, 4.3]
)
There exists a nonlinear, bounded extension
E:L^{1}(\partial\Omega)\to BV(\Omega),
satisfying

\displaystyle\|Ef\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\diam(\Omega)\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\|DEf\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega))\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

Moreover,
TEf=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(
[
25
, Lemma 5.1, Proposition 5.2]
)
There exists a nonlinear extension
\Ext:C(\partial\Omega)\to C(X)\cap BV(X)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\Ext f\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}+1\text{ and }

\displaystyle\|D\Ext f\|(X)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)\right)(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}+1).

Moreover, for
f\in C(\partial\Omega)
and
z\in\partial\Omega,
it follows that

\lim_{(X\setminus\partial\Omega)\ni x\to z}\Ext f(x)=f(z).

We will need the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega),
and let
u\in BV(\Omega)
be a function of least gradient in
\Omega
such that
Tu=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Then,
u
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

Proof.

Let
v\in BV(\Omega)
such that
Tv=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Then,
T(v-u)=0

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
and so by
[
26
, Theorem 6.9]
, there exists
w_{k}\in BV_{c}(\Omega)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w_{k}\to v-u
in
BV(\Omega).
Thus, it follows that

\|D(u+w_{k})\|(\Omega)\to\|Dv\|(\Omega),

as
k\to\infty.
Since
u
is of least gradient and
w_{k}\in BV_{c}(\Omega),
we have that

\|Du\|(\Omega)\leq\|D(u+w_{k})\|(\Omega)\to\|Dv\|(\Omega)

as
k\to\infty
, and so it follows that
\|Du\|(\Omega)\leq\|Dv\|(\Omega).

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
be such that
f\leq g

\mathcal{H}
-a.e., and let
u,v\in BV(\Omega)
be solutions to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
and
g
respectively. Then,
\min\{u,v\}
and
\max\{u,v\}
are solutions to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
and
g
respectively.

Proof.

By
[
24
, Lemma 3.1]
, it follows that

\|D\min\{u,v\}\|(\Omega)+\|D\max\{u,v\}\|(\Omega)\leq\|Du\|(\Omega)+\|Dv\|(\Omega).

(4.5)

For
x\in\partial\Omega
such that
f(x)\leq g(x)
and setting
U_{r}:=B(x,r)\cap\Omega,
we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|\min\{u,v\}-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\left(\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\geq v\}}|v-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}\cap\{v>u\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\geq v\}}|v-f(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu,

and it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\geq v\}}

\displaystyle|v-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\geq v\}\cap\{v>f(x)\}}|v-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\geq v\}\cap\{v\leq f(x)\}}|v-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}}|v-g(x)|d\mu.

Therefore, for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega,
we have that

\fint_{U_{r}}|\min\{u,v\}-f(x)|d\mu\leq 2\fint_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|v-g(x)|d\mu\to 0

as
r\to 0^{+}.
Thus,
T\min\{u,v\}=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
and a similar argument shows that
T\max\{u,v\}=g

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

Now, suppose that
\|D\min\{u,v\}\|(\Omega)>\|Du\|(\Omega).
Then by
(
4.5
)
we have that
\|D\max\{u,v\}\|(\Omega)<\|Dv\|(\Omega).
This is a contradiction since
T\max\{u,v\}=g

\mathcal{H}
-a.e., and
v
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g.
Thus, we have that

\|D\min\{u,v\}\|(\Omega)\leq\|Du\|(\Omega),

and so
\min\{u,v\}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f.
Similarly, we have that

\max\{u,v\}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega),
and suppose that
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
with
Tu=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Then, for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R},
we have that
T\chi_{\{u>t\}}=\chi_{\{f>t\}}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty
, it follows that
\mathcal{H}(\{f=t\})=0
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
For such
t\in\mathbb{R},
let
x\in\partial\Omega\cap\{f\neq t\}
be such that
Tu(x)=f(x).
This property holds for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega.

We first consider the case when
f(x)>t.
Setting
U_{r}:=B(x,r)\cap\Omega,
we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{\{u>t\}}-\chi_{\{f>t\}}(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\int_{U_{r}}|\chi_{\{u>t\}}-1|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu(\{u\leq t\}\cap U_{r})}{\mu(U_{r})}.

Suppose that this quantity does not go to zero as
r
goes to zero. That is, suppose there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta>0,
there exists
0<r_{\delta}<\delta
such that

\frac{\mu(\{u\leq t\}\cap U_{r_{\delta}})}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Let
\varepsilon:=|f(x)-t|\varepsilon_{0}.
Since
Tu(x)=f(x),
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle>\fint_{U_{r_{\delta}}}|u-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}\left(\int_{U_{r_{\delta}}\cap\{u>t\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r_{\delta}}\cap\{u\leq t\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}\int_{U_{r_{\delta}}\cap\{u\leq t\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle\geq|f(x)-t|\frac{\mu(\{u\leq t\}\cap U_{r_{\delta}})}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}\geq|f(x)-t|\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon,

a contradiction. Thus, it follows that

\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{\{u>t\}}-\chi_{\{f>t\}}(x)|d\mu\to 0

as
r\to 0^{+}.

If
f(x)<t,
then we have that

\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{\{u>t\}}-\chi_{\{f>t\}}(x)|d\mu=\frac{\mu(\{u>t\}\cap U_{r})}{\mu(U_{r})}.

If we suppose that this quantity does not go to zero as
r
goes to zero, then we arrive at a contradiction by the same method as above. However, in this case, we use the fact that

\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}\int_{U_{r_{\delta}}\cap\{u>t\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu\geq|f(x)-t|\frac{\mu(\{u>t\}\cap U_{r_{\delta}})}{\mu(U_{r_{\delta}})}.

Thus, for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega,
we have that

\lim_{r\to 0}\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{\{u>t\}}-\chi_{\{f>t\}}(x)|d\mu=0,

and so
T\chi_{\{u>t\}}=\chi_{\{f>t\}}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

If
u\in BV(\Omega)
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, then by the coarea formula, we have that
\chi_{\{u>t\}}\in BV(\Omega)
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
Moreover, as
u
is necessarily of least gradient in
\Omega
, it follows that for each
t\in\mathbb{R},

\chi_{\{u>t\}}
is of least gradient in
\Omega
as shown in
[
17
, Lemma 3.6]
. Thus, by Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.6
, it follows that
\chi_{\{u>t\}}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{\{f>t\}}
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
In particular, if
f=\chi_{F}
for some
F\subset\partial\Omega,
and there exists a solution
u
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, we may assume that
u=\chi_{E}
for some
E\subset\Omega
with
P(E,\Omega)<\infty.

We now show that the solution constructed in
[
25
]
for continuous data is minimal in the following sense.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
f\in C(\partial\Omega).
Then, there exists a solution
u\in BV(\Omega)
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
, such that for any solution
u^{\prime}
to the said Dirichlet problem, we have that
u\leq u^{\prime}

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.

Proof.

We follow the construction of a solution
u\in BV(\Omega)
given in the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 4.10]
and show that
u
has the desired property.

Let
\Ext f\in C(X)\cap BV(X)
be the extension of
f
to
X
given by Proposition
4.2
. For
t\in\mathbb{R},
let
F_{t}:=\{x\in X:\Ext f(x)>t\}.
Then,
F_{t}
is open by continuity of
\Ext f
, and since
\Ext f\in BV(X),
it follows from the coarea formula that
P(F_{t},X)<\infty
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
Moreover, if
x\in\partial F_{t},
then
\Ext f(x)=t
by continuity of
\Ext f,
and so
\partial F_{t}\cap\partial F_{s}=\varnothing
for
s\neq t.
Since
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty,
it then follows that
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega\cap\partial F_{t})=0
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
Let

J:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:P(F_{t},X)<\infty\text{ and }\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega\cap\partial F_{t})=0\}.

For each
t\in J,
there exists a unique minimal solution set
\widetilde{E}_{t}
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F_{t}},

[
25
, Proposition 3.7, 4.8]
. Setting

E_{t}:=\{x\in X:\chi_{\widetilde{E}_{t}}^{\vee}(x)>0\},

it follows that
\chi_{E_{t}}
is also a minimal solution. Since
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}\setminus J)=0,
we can find a countable set
I\subset J
such that
I
is dense in
\mathbb{R}.
Let
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

u(x)=\sup\{s\in I:x\in E_{s}\}.

In
[
25
, Theorem 4.10]
, it is shown that
u\in BV(\Omega)
and
u
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

Now, let
u^{\prime}\in BV(\Omega)
be another solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f.
Then, setting

E^{\prime}_{t}=\{u^{\prime}>t\},

it follows from the discussion in Remark
4.7
that
\chi_{E^{\prime}_{t}}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F_{t}}
for
\mathcal{L}
-a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}.
Letting

J^{\prime}:=\{t\in J:\chi_{E^{\prime}_{t}}\text{ is a solution for }\chi_{F_{t}}\}\subset J,

we have that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}\setminus J^{\prime})=0.
Hence, there exists a countable set
I^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime}
such that
I^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}.
For each
s\in I^{\prime},
we have that
\chi_{E_{s}}\leq\chi_{E^{\prime}_{s}}

\mu
-a.e., since
\chi_{E_{s}}
is the minimal solution for
\chi_{F_{s}}.
Therefore, letting

G_{s}:=E_{s}\setminus E^{\prime}_{s}=\left\{x\in\Omega:\chi_{E^{\prime}_{s}}(x)<\chi_{E_{s}}(x)\right\},

we have that
\mu(G_{s})=0.
Letting

G:=\bigcup_{s\in I^{\prime}}G_{s},

it follows that
\mu(G)=0.

Moreover, for
s^{*}\in I^{\prime}
and
s\in I
such that
s^{*}<s,
we have from
[
25
, Lemma 3.8]
that
E_{s}\sqsubset E_{s^{*}}
. Let

H_{s^{*},s}:=\{x\in\Omega:x\in E_{s}\setminus E_{s^{*}}\}.

Then,
\mu(H_{s^{*},s})=0.
Let

H:=\bigcup_{s^{*}\in I^{\prime}}\bigcup_{s\in I,s>s^{*}}H_{s^{*},s}.

Then, we have
\mu(H)=0.

Let
x\in X\setminus(G\cup H),
and suppose that
u^{\prime}(x)<u(x)=\sup\{s\in I:x\in E_{s}\}.
Then, by the definition of
u
, there exists
s\in I
with
u^{\prime}(x)<s\leq u(x)
such that
x\in E_{s},
and by the density of
I^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R},
there exists
s^{*}\in I^{\prime}
such that

u^{\prime}(x)<s^{*}<s\leq u(x).

Since
s^{*}<s
and
x\not\in H,
we have that
x\in E_{s^{*}}.
Moreover, since
u^{\prime}(x)<s^{*},
it follows that
x\not\in E^{\prime}_{s^{*}}.
Thus we have that

\chi_{E^{\prime}_{s^{*}}}(x)<\chi_{E_{s^{*}}}(x).

However, this is a contradiction since
x\not\in G_{s^{*}}.
Therefore, it follows that
u(x)\leq u^{\prime}(x),
and since
\mu(G\cup H)=0,
we have that
u\leq u^{\prime}

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.

∎

Remark 4.9
.

If
v
is another solution which is minimal in the sense of Proposition
4.8
, then
u=v

\mu
-a.e. Thus, we call
u
the
minimal solution
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

As an immediate corollary, we obtain a comparison-type result for minimal solutions.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
f,g\in C(\partial\Omega)
be such that
f\leq g

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
, and let
u
and
v
be minimal solutions to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
and
g
respectively. Then
u\leq v

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.

Proof.

Existence of the minimal solutions
u
and
v
is guaranteed by Proposition
4.8
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\min\{u,v\}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f.
Therefore, as
u
is a minimal solution, we have that

u\leq\min\{u,v\}\leq v

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.

∎

We note that in this setting, uniqueness of solutions is not guaranteed even for Lipschitz boundary data (see
[
25
]
), and so such a comparison theorem may not hold for solutions which are not minimal.

Using the previous corollary, we are now able to establish Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
be the minimal solutions for the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g_{k}
and
h_{k}
, respectively. The existence of such minimal solutions follows from Proposition
4.8
above. By Corollary
4.10
, we have

u_{k}\leq u_{k+1}\leq v_{k+1}\leq v_{k}

(4.11)

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.
Let
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

u:=\sup_{k}u_{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}u_{k}.

We have that
|u_{k}-u|\leq 2\max\{|u_{1}|,|v_{1}|\},

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega,
and since
u_{1},v_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega),
it follows that

\int_{\Omega}2\max\{|u_{1}|,|v_{1}|\}d\mu<\infty.

Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}-u|d\mu=0,

and so it follows that
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega).
By
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
, it follows that
u
is a function of least gradient in
\Omega.

Let
Eg_{k}\in BV(\Omega)
be the extension of
g_{k}
into
\Omega
, as given by Proposition
4.1
. Since
u_{k}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g_{k}
, and since
u_{k}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega),
we have by lower semi-continuity of the BV energy and the bounds from Proposition
4.1
that

\displaystyle\|Du\|(\Omega)\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|Du_{k}\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|DEg_{k}\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}C(1+\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega))\|g_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega))\|\max\{|g_{1}|,|h_{1}|\}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}<\infty.

Thus,
u\in BV(\Omega).

It remains to show that
Tu=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Let
x\in\partial\Omega
be such that
g_{k}(x),h_{k}(x)\to f(x)
as
k\to\infty,
and such that for all

,
Tu_{k}(x)=g_{k}(x)
and
Tv_{k}(x)=h_{k}(x).
By the hypothesis, these conditions hold for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega.
Then we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu=\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\left(\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u>f(x)\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\leq f(x)\}}|u-f(x)|d\mu\right).

Since
(
4.11
)
holds for all

it follows that
|u(y)-f(x)|\leq|v_{k}(y)-f(x)|
for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in U_{r}\cap\{u>f(x)\},
and similarly,
|u(y)-f(x)|\leq|u_{k}(y)-f(x)|
for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in U_{r}\cap\{u\leq f(x)\}.
Therefore we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\left(\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u>f(x)\}}|v_{k}-f(x)|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}\cap\{u\leq f(x)\}}|u_{k}-f(x)|d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{U_{r}}|v_{k}-f(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|u_{k}-f(x)|d\mu.

Let
\varepsilon>0.
Since
g_{k}(x),h_{k}(x)\to f(x)
as
k\to\infty,
we can choose

sufficiently large such that
|g_{k}(x)-f(x)|,|h_{k}(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon.
Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|u-

\displaystyle f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{U_{r}}|v_{k}-h_{k}(x)|+|h_{k}(x)-f(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|u_{k}-g_{k}(x)|+|g_{k}(x)-f(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle<\fint_{U_{r}}|v_{k}-h_{k}(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|u_{k}-g_{k}(x)|d\mu+2\varepsilon.

Since

and
Tv_{k}(x)=h_{k}(x),
we have that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{U_{r}}|u-f(x)|d\mu<2\varepsilon,

and so taking
\varepsilon\to 0,
it follows that
Tu(x)=f(x).
Thus,
Tu=f

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
and so by Lemma
4.3
,
u\in BV(\Omega)
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f
.

Let

be a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f.
For each

it follows that
\min\{u_{k},u^{\prime}\}
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
g_{k}
, by Lemma

. However, as

is a minimal solution, it follows that
u_{k}\leq\min\{u_{k},u^{\prime}\}\leq u^{\prime}

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega.
Since

pointwise

-a.e., it follows that

\mu

Thus

is a minimal solution, and uniqueness follows from minimality.
∎

Example 4.12
.

We point out that the functions

above need not converge to the minimal solution

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the (unweighted) unit disc, and let

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}1,&|y|>1/\sqrt{2}\\
0,&|y|\leq 1/\sqrt{2}.\end{cases}

Letting

\displaystyle g_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&|y|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}+2/k\\
k(y-(1/\sqrt{2}+1/k)),&1/\sqrt{2}+1/k<y<1/\sqrt{2}+2/k\\
-k(y+1/\sqrt{2}+1/k),&-(1/\sqrt{2}+2/k)<y<-(1/\sqrt{2}+1/k)\\
0,&|y|\leq 1\sqrt{2}+1/k\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle h_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&|y|\geq 1/\sqrt{2}-1/k\\
k(y-(1/\sqrt{2}-2/k)),&1/\sqrt{2}<y<1/\sqrt{2}-1/k\\

0,&|y|\leq 1\sqrt{2}-2/k,\end{cases}

for sufficiently large

, we have that

It follows that the

converge to the minimal solution

to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data

, and the

converge to the maximal solution

For more on non-uniqueness of solutions, see
[
29
, Example 2.7]
and
[
11
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we are able to prove Theorem
1.2
. Recall that given

we let
\widetilde{\partial}F
denote the boundary of
F

relative
to
\partial\Omega.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\varepsilon>0,
let
\eta^{+}_{\varepsilon}:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

\eta^{+}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\max\left\{1-\frac{\dist(x,F)}{\varepsilon},0\right\}.

Then
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{+}
is continuous, and
\chi_{F}\leq\eta^{+}_{\varepsilon_{1}}\leq\eta_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{+}
on
\partial\Omega
for
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}.
If
x\in F,
then
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)=1
for all
\varepsilon>0.
Likewise, if
x\in\partial\Omega
is in the exterior of
F
relative to
\partial\Omega,
then
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)=0
for sufficiently small

Therefore,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\eta_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)=\chi_{F}(x)
for all

hence
\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

Similarly, for
\varepsilon>0,
let
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{-}:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

\eta_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x)=\min\left\{\frac{\dist(x,\Omega\setminus F)}{\varepsilon},1\right\}.

Then
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{-}
is continuous, and
\eta_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{-}\leq\eta_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{-}\leq\chi_{F}
on
\partial\Omega
for
\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}.
Similarly since
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}F)=0,
we have that
\eta_{\varepsilon}^{-}\to\chi_{F}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
as

Having constructed the necessary approximating sequences, Theorem
1.1
gives the existence of the minimal solution
u\in BV(\Omega).

∎

We note that existence of a solution in Theorem
1.2
also follows from the results of Section 5. However, minimality of the solution is not guaranteed by those results.

As the following example shows, some sets
F\subset\partial\Omega
with poorly behaved boundaries still satisfy the
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}F)=0
condition. Theorem
1.2
gives us a way to ensure existence of minimal solutions even in these cases.

Example 4.13
.

Let
\Omega=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3},
and let
K\subset\partial\Omega=S^{2}
be a bi-Lipschitz embedding of the von Koch snowflake domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
onto
S^{2}.
Then,
\widetilde{\partial}K
is a curve of infinite length, and so
\chi_{K}\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)\setminus BV(\partial\Omega).
Since
\mathcal{H}\simeq\mathcal{H}^{2}
in this example, where
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is the standard 2-dimensional Hausdorff measure, it follows that
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}K)=0
. Hence by Theorem
1.2
, there exists a minimal solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{K}.

Remark 4.14
.

It should be noted that the conditions imposed on
f
in Theorem
1.1
are rather strict. Unbounded functions are excluded, for example. Furthermore, the conditions are not necessary in general to guarantee existence of a solution. As seen in Example
3.3
above, the function
g
has a solution but cannot be approximated
\mathcal{H}
-a.e. from below by continuous functions. Likewise, Example
3.3
also shows that the conditions imposed on a subset of
\partial\Omega
in Theorem
1.2
are not necessary in general. Since
g=\chi_{K_{1/4}\cup F},
we have that
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}(K_{1/4}\cup F))=\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}K_{1/4})=\mathcal{H}(K_{1/4})=1/2.

5
Solutions at points of continuity of the boundary data

In this section, we consider an arbitrary
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega),
and show that there exists a least gradient function
u\in BV(\Omega)
whose trace agrees with
f
at points of continuity of
f
. The argument follows that of
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, where an analogous result was shown for strictly convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
However, our results apply even for domains that are not strictly convex (see Remark
5.8
). We first need the following lemmas regarding minimal solution sets.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}\subset X
be open sets such that
P(F_{1},X),P(F_{2},X)<\infty
and
\mathcal{H}(\partial F_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)=0=\mathcal{H}(\partial F_{2}\cap\partial\Omega).
Suppose also that
F_{1}\cap F_{2}\cap\partial\Omega=\varnothing,
and let
E_{1},E_{2}\subset\penalty\ X
be minimal solution sets to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F_{1}}
and
\chi_{F_{2}}
respectively. Then,
\mu(E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap\Omega)=0.

Proof.

Let
\partial\Omega^{*}:=\{x\in\partial\Omega:T\chi_{E_{1}}(x)=\chi_{F_{1}}(x)\text{ and }T\chi_{E_{2}}(x)=\chi_{F_{2}}(x)\}.
We claim that
T\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}(x)=\chi_{F_{1}}(x)
for
x\in\partial\Omega^{*},
and hence for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega.

Indeed, for
x\in\partial\Omega^{*}\cap F_{1},
(setting
U_{r}:=B(x,r)\cap\Omega)
, we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}-

\displaystyle\chi_{F_{1}}(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\left(\int_{U_{r}\cap E_{2}}|\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}-1|d\mu+\int_{U_{r}\setminus(E_{1}\cup E_{2})}|\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}-1|d\mu\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu(U_{r}\cap E_{2})}{\mu(U_{r})}+\frac{\mu(U_{r}\setminus(E_{1}\cup E_{2}))}{\mu(U_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu(U_{r}\cap E_{2})}{\mu(U_{r})}+\frac{\mu(U_{r}\setminus E_{1})}{\mu(U_{r})}

\displaystyle=\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{E_{2}}-\chi_{F_{2}}(x)|d\mu+\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{E_{1}}-\chi_{F_{1}}(x)|d\mu\to 0

as
r\to 0^{+}.
Similarly, for
x\in\partial\Omega^{*}\setminus F_{1},
we have that

\displaystyle\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}-\chi_{F_{1}}(x)|d\mu=\fint_{U_{r}}\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}d\mu\leq\fint_{U_{r}}\chi_{E_{1}}d\mu=\fint_{U_{r}}|\chi_{E_{1}}-\chi_{F_{1}}(x)|d\mu\to 0

as
r\to 0^{+}.
Thus, we have that
T\chi_{E_{1}\setminus E_{2}}=\chi_{F_{1}}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.
Likewise, a symmetric argument shows that
T\chi_{E_{2}\setminus E_{1}}=\chi_{F_{2}}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega.

Now, by
[
30
, Proposition 4.7]
, we have that

\displaystyle P(E_{1}\setminus(E_{2}\cap\Omega),\Omega)

\displaystyle=P(E_{1}\setminus E_{2},\Omega)

\displaystyle=P(E_{1}\cap(\Omega\setminus E_{2}),\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq P(E_{1},\Omega)+P(\Omega\setminus E_{2},\Omega)-P(E_{1}\cup(\Omega\setminus E_{2}),\Omega)

\displaystyle=P(E_{1},\Omega)+P(E_{2},\Omega)-P(E_{2}\setminus E_{1},\Omega)

\displaystyle=P(E_{1},\Omega)+P(E_{2},\Omega)-P(E_{2}\setminus(E_{1}\cap\Omega),\Omega).

Thus, we have that

P(E_{1}\setminus(E_{2}\cap\Omega),\Omega)+P(E_{2}\setminus(E_{1}\cap\Omega),\Omega)\leq P(E_{1},\Omega)+P(E_{2},\Omega).

If
P(E_{1}\setminus(E_{2}\cap\Omega),\Omega)>P(E_{1},\Omega),
then we have that
P(E_{2}\setminus(E_{1}\cap\Omega),\Omega)<P(E_{2},\Omega)
. However, since
T\chi_{E_{2}\setminus E_{1}}=\chi_{F_{2}}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
and since
E_{2}
is a solution set for
\chi_{F_{2}},
this is a contradiction. Therefore, we have that
P(E_{1}\setminus(E_{2}\cap\Omega),\Omega)\leq P(E_{1},\Omega),
and so
E_{1}\setminus(E_{2}\cap\Omega)
is a solution set for
\chi_{F_{1}}.
Thus, since
E_{1}
is the minimal solution set, we have that
\mu(E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap\Omega)=0.

∎

The following lemma follows from a similar argument.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}\subset X
be open sets such that
P(F_{1},X),P(F_{2},X)<\infty
and
\mathcal{H}(\partial F_{1}\cap\partial\Omega)=0=\mathcal{H}(\partial F_{2}\cap\partial\Omega).
Suppose also that
F_{1}\cap\partial\Omega\subset F_{2},
and let
E_{1},E_{2}\subset X
be minimal solution sets to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F_{1}}
and
\chi_{F_{2}}
respectively. Then,
E_{1}\cap\Omega\sqsubset E_{2}.

Since
X
is doubling, it follows that for each
K\geq 1,
there exists
C_{K}>0
such that for every
r>0,
we can find a finite cover
\{B(x_{i},r)\}_{i\in J_{r}}\subset X
of
\partial\Omega
with
x_{i}\in\partial\Omega
such that
\sum_{i\in J_{r}}\chi_{B(x_{i},Kr)}\leq C_{K}
. Let
\varepsilon>0,
and consider such a cover
\{B(x_{i},\varepsilon/5)\}_{i\in J_{\varepsilon}}.
By the 5B-Lemma, there exists a disjoint subcollection
\{B(x_{i},\varepsilon/5)\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}\subset J_{\varepsilon}}
such that
\partial\Omega\subset\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}B(x_{i},\varepsilon).
Thus, we obtain a finite cover
\{B_{i,\varepsilon}:=B(x_{i},\varepsilon)\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
of
\partial\Omega
such that the set
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
is
2\varepsilon/5
-separated, and for all
K\geq 1,
we have that

\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{KB_{i,\varepsilon}}\leq C_{K}.

(5.3)

We can then find a Lipschitz partition of unity
\{\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
subject to this cover. That is, for each
i\in I_{\varepsilon},
there is a
C/\varepsilon
-Lipschitz function
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}:X\to[0,1],
(with
C
depending only on the doubling constant) such that
\overline{\supt(\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon})}\subset 2B_{i,\varepsilon},
and
\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=1
on
\partial\Omega.
For proof of these facts, see for example
[
15
, Appendix B]
,
[
18
]
, and
[
20
]
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega),
define

f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}:=\fint_{B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}f\,d\mathcal{H},

and let
f_{\varepsilon}:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

f_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}\big|_{\partial\Omega}.

Then, as
f_{\varepsilon}
is continuous, there is a minimal solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in BV(\Omega)
to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f_{\varepsilon},
by Proposition
4.8
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the minimal solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f_{\varepsilon}
, as defined above. Then,

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|Du_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)\right)<\infty.

That is,
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is bounded in
BV(\Omega).

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\partial\Omega}|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j\in I_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{j,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}.

For
i\in I_{\varepsilon},
let
J_{i,\varepsilon}:=\{j\in I_{\varepsilon}:B_{j,\varepsilon}\cap 2B_{i,\varepsilon}\neq\varnothing\}.
If
j\in J_{i,\varepsilon},
then
B_{j,\varepsilon}\subset 4B_{i,\varepsilon},
and so by the doubling property and the fact that
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}
is a
2\varepsilon/5
-separated set, there exists
C>0
depending only on the doubling constant such that
|J_{i,\varepsilon}|\leq C,
where
|J_{i,\varepsilon}|
denotes the number of elements in
J_{i,\varepsilon}.
Since
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}
is compactly supported in
2B_{i,\varepsilon},
it follows that
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}=0
on
B_{j,\varepsilon}
for
j\in I_{\varepsilon}\setminus J_{i,\varepsilon}.
Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j\in J_{i,\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{j,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j\in J_{i,\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{j,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}\left(\fint_{B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}\right)d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j\in J_{i,\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathcal{H}(B_{j,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega)}{\mathcal{H}(B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega)}\int_{B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\int_{B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H},

(5.5)

where the constant
C>0
depends on the doubling constant of
\mathcal{H}
and the bounded overlap constant from (
5.3
).

Let
Ef_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be the extension of
f_{\varepsilon}
to
\Omega
given by Proposition
4.1
. Since
u_{\varepsilon}\in BV(\Omega)
is a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
f_{\varepsilon},
and by (
5.5
), we have that

\|Du_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)\leq\|DEf_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f_{\varepsilon}|d\mathcal{H}\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}.

(5.6)

Now, let
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be given by
v_{\varepsilon}:=u_{\varepsilon}-Ef_{\varepsilon},
and let
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}
be the zero extension of
v_{\varepsilon}
to all of
X.
Since
Tu_{\varepsilon}=TEf_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega,
it follows that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\mu(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|d\mu=0

for
\mathcal{H}
-a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega.
Thus, by
[
26
, Theorem 6.1]
, it follows that
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\in BV(X)
and
\|D\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\|(X\setminus\Omega)=0.

Since
\mu(X\setminus\Omega)>0,
we can find a ball
B\subset X
such that
\Omega\subset B
and
\mu(B\setminus\Omega)>0.
By Hölder’s Inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|d\mu\leq\int_{B}|\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B)^{1/Q}\left(\int_{B}|\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}}

\displaystyle=\mu(B)\left(\fint_{B}|\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{Q-1}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{Q-1}{Q}},

where
Q>1
is the exponent from (
2.1
). Since
\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}=0
on
B\setminus\Omega,
it follows from Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B)\frac{C\rad(B)}{1-(\mu(\Omega)/\mu(B))^{1/Q}}\frac{\|D\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\|(2\lambda B)}{\mu(2\lambda B)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|D\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)+\|D\hat{v}_{\varepsilon}\|(X\setminus\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle=C\|Dv_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|Du_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)+\|DEf_{\varepsilon}\|(\Omega)\right).

Here the constant
C>0
depends on
\Omega
,
B
, and the doubling and Poincaré constants, but is independent of
\varepsilon,

f_{\varepsilon},
and
u_{\varepsilon}.

Then by
\eqref{eq:EnergyBound},
we have that

\int_{\Omega}|v_{\varepsilon}|d\mu\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H},

and by the Triangle Inequality, it follows that

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|d\mu\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}+\int_{\Omega}|Ef_{\varepsilon}|d\mu.

Therefore, by Proposition
4.1
and (
5.5
), we have that

\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|d\mu\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|f|d\mathcal{H}.

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
f
be continuous at
x\in\partial\Omega.
Then, for all
\eta>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\delta
and for all
y\in B(x,\delta)\cap\partial\Omega,
we have that
|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|<\eta.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0.
By the continuity of
f
at
x,
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
y\in B(x,\delta_{0})\cap\partial\Omega,
then
|f(y)-f(x)|<\eta.
Let
\delta:=\delta_{0}/10.
Then, for
y\in B(x,\delta)\cap\partial\Omega,
and for
0<\varepsilon<\delta,
we have that

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}-f(x)|.

Let
J_{y,\varepsilon}:=\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}:y\in 2B_{i,\varepsilon}\}.
Then,
\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)=0
for all

Thus, we have that

|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|\leq\sum_{i\in J_{y,\varepsilon}}\varphi_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}-f(x)|.

Since
0<\varepsilon<\delta,
it follows that for
i\in J_{y,\varepsilon},
we have that
B_{i,\varepsilon}\subset B(x,\delta).
Therefore,

|f_{B_{i,\varepsilon}}-f(x)|\leq\fint_{B_{i,\varepsilon}\cap\partial\Omega}|f-f(x)|d\mathcal{H}<\eta,

and so it follows that
|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|<\eta.

∎

We are now able to establish Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is bounded in
BV(\Omega),
it follows from the compact embedding theorem
[
30
, Theorem 3.7]
that there exists
u\in BV_{loc}(\Omega)
and a subsequence, also denoted
u_{\varepsilon}\in BV(\Omega),
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega),
and passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we have that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
pointwise a.e. in
\Omega.
Hence by Fatou’s lemma and Lemma
5.4
, we have that

\int_{\Omega}|u|d\mu\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|d\mu<\infty.

By lower semi-continuity of the BV energy and
\eqref{eq:EnergyBound},
we have that
\|Du\|(\Omega)<\infty
, and so
u\in BV(\Omega).
Furthermore by
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
, it follows that
u
is a function of least gradient.

Let
f
be continuous at
x\in\partial\Omega,
and let
\eta>0.
By Lemma
5.7
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\delta,
and for all
y\in B(x,\delta)\cap\partial\Omega,
we have that

|f_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|<\eta.

Let
r_{x}>0
be as in Definition
2.12
. Then by the coarea formula, and since
\mathcal{H}(\partial\Omega)<\infty,
there exists
\delta_{x}>0
such that

\min\{\delta,r_{x}\}/2<\delta_{x}<\min\{\delta,r_{x}\},

with
P(B(x,\delta_{x}),X)<\infty
and
\mathcal{H}(\partial B(x,\delta_{x})\cap\partial\Omega)=0.
Denote
F_{\eta}:=B(x,\delta_{x}),
and let
E_{\eta}\subset X
be a minimal solution set for
\chi_{F_{\eta}}.
Let
\widehat{\delta}:=\min\{\delta_{x},\phi_{x}(\delta_{x})\},
where
\phi_{x}
is as in Definition
2.12
. Then, for all
0<r<\widehat{\delta},
it follows that
B(x,r)\cap\Omega\sqsubset E_{\eta}.

For
0<\varepsilon<\widehat{\delta},
let
F^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=\{\Ext f_{\varepsilon}>t\},
where
\Ext f_{\varepsilon}
is the extension of
f_{\varepsilon}
to
X
given by Proposition
4.2
. Recall that
u_{\varepsilon}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

u_{\varepsilon}(y)=\sup\{t\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}:y\in E^{\varepsilon}_{t}\},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
and
E^{\varepsilon}_{t}
are as in the proof of Proposition
4.8
. By the choice of
\widehat{\delta},
we note that for
t\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
such that
t\geq f(x)+\eta,
it follows that
F_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cap F_{\eta}\cap\partial\Omega=\varnothing.
Thus, by Lemma
5.1
, we have that
\mu(E_{t}^{\varepsilon}\cap E_{\eta}\cap\Omega)=0.
Similarly, for
t\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
such that
t\leq f(x)-\eta,
we have that
F_{\eta}\cap\partial\Omega\subset F_{t}^{\varepsilon},
and so by Lemma
5.2
, it follows that
E_{\eta}\cap\Omega\sqsubset E_{t}^{\varepsilon}.
Therefore, for all
0<\varepsilon<\widehat{\delta}
and for all
0<r<\widehat{\delta},
it follows from the construction of
u_{\varepsilon}
that

|u_{\varepsilon}(y)-f(x)|\leq\eta

for
\mu
-a.e.
y\in B(x,r)\cap\Omega.

Therefore, for all
\eta>0,
we have that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}-f(x)|d\mu\leq\eta,

and as
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
pointwise a.e., it follows from the dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\fint_{B(x,r)\cap\Omega}|u-f(x)|d\mu=0.

Thus, we have that
Tu(x)=f(x).

∎

We note that in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we apply Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
to the regularized sets

E^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\{x\in X:\chi^{\vee}_{\widetilde{E}^{\varepsilon}_{t}}(x)>0\},

where
\widetilde{E}^{\varepsilon}_{t}
is the minimal solution set for
\chi_{F^{\varepsilon}_{t}}.
However since
\mu(E^{\varepsilon}_{t}\Delta\widetilde{E}^{\varepsilon}_{t})=0,
the lemmas still hold.

Remark 5.8
.

We note that Theorem
1.3
generalizes
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
to the metric setting and also extends that result to domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which are not strictly convex but satisfy the positive mean curvature condition. For example, consider the domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
constructed by attaching half of the unit ball to either end of the cylinder
\mathbb{D}\times[0,1].
The boundary of this capped cylinder has positive mean curvature, but is not strictly convex. See the discussion from
[
25
, Section 4]
and
[
37
, Section 3]
relating the notion of positive mean curvature given above to that of domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary.

Remark 5.9
.

If we consider a measurable set
F\subset\partial\Omega
such that
\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\partial}F)=0,
as in Theorem
1.2
, we see that existence of a solution to the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\chi_{F}
follows immediately from Theorem
1.3
, since
\chi_{F}
is continuous at all points
x\in\partial\Omega\setminus\widetilde{\partial}F.
Thus we obtain another proof of the existence part of Theorem
1.2
, though it is unclear if minimality of the solution also follows from these results, as it does in Section 4.

Remark 5.10
.

As with Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2

\mathcal{H}
-a.e. in Theorem
1.3

. There, a solution exists for the Dirichlet problem with boundary data

On multidimensional nonlocal conservation laws with B ​ V BV kernels
Maria Colombo, Gianluca Crippa, Laura V. Spinolo

Abstract




We establish local-in-time existence and uniqueness results for nonlocal conservation laws in several space dimensions under weak (that is, Sobolev or
BV
) differentiability assumptions on the convolution kernel. In contrast to the case of a smooth kernel, in general the solution experiences finite-time blow up. We provide an explicit example showing that solutions corresponding to different smooth approximations of the convolution kernel in general converge to different measures after the blow-up time. This rules out a fairly natural strategy for extending the notion of solution of the nonlocal conservation law after the blow-up time.

Keywords:
nonlocal conservation laws in several space dimensions, models for pedestrian traffic, well-posedness,
BV
kernels, nonuniqueness, lack of selection

MSC (2020): 35L65, 35A01, 35R06

1.
Introduction

We consider the following Cauchy problem for nonlocal conservation laws in several space dimensions,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}[uV(t,x,u\ast\eta)]=0,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the unknown,
V:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Lipschitz continuous function, and
\eta\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a convolution kernel. We denote by
\mathrm{div}
the divergence computed with respect to the space variable only, whereas the symbol
\ast
stands for the convolution with respect to the space variable only, that is

u\ast\eta(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x-y)\eta(y)dy.

In this work we establish local-in-time existence and uniqueness results for (
1.1
) under fairly weak differentiability assumption on
\eta
, in particular we focus on the case of Sobolev and
BV
(bounded total variation) regularity. We also consider an explicit example where the solution
u
experiences finite time blow up and show that, in general, solutions corresponding to different smooth approximations of
\eta
converge to different measures after the blow-up time.

In the last few years, the analysis of nonlocal conservation laws has attracted quite some attention, owing to the wide range of applications spanning from sedimentation
[
BBKT11
]
to supply chains
[
CHM11
]
, and pedestrian
[
CGLM12
,
PT11
]
and vehicular
[
Chi21
]
traffic models. In the vehicular traffic framework, corresponding to the one-dimensional case
x\in\mathbb{R}
, several recent works have focused on the case of
anisotropic
convolutions kernels, that is,
\eta
is supported on the negative real axis
\mathbb{R}_{-}
. This aims at modeling the fact that drivers tune their velocity based on the downstream traffic density only. We refer in particular to the original paper
[
BG16
]
, the recent overview
[
Chi21
]
and the references therein for a non exaustive list of recent contributions establishing global-in-time existence and uniqueness results, maximum principle and many other properties. Anisotropic convolution kernels typically have at least one jump discontinuity at the origin, and as such do not satisfy the smoothness properties required in previous works on nonlocal conservation laws, see for instance
[
CLM13
]
. In the one-dimensional case, a systematic treatment of nonlocal conservation laws with
BV
kernels
is provided in
[
GR17
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
CDNKP22
]
. At the end of
[
CDNKP22
]
the authors quote as an open question the extension of their analysis to the multidimensional case. In the present work, we first of all provide an answer to this question. Note that well-posedness results for the nonlocal Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the multidimensional case were already well known, see for instance in
[
ACG15
,
CLM13
,
KPS18
]
, but to the best of our knowledge all previous results require much stronger regularity on
\eta
than the one we consider in the following.

A primary motivation for our analysis comes from pedestrian traffic or crowd dynamics models: as an example, in
[
PT11
]
the authors model
pedestrian flows with equations similar to (
1.1
) under the choice

V(t,x,u\ast\eta)=V_{d}(t,x)+[u\ast\eta](t,x),

where
u
represents the crowd density and
V_{d}
the desired velocity, that is the velocity that a pedestrian at the point
x
at time
t
would have if there were no crowd. The term
u\ast\eta
represents a correction term modeling the fact that pedestrians want to avoid very crowded areas. In
[
PT11
, formula (22)]
the authors consider the discontinuous convolution kernel

\eta(x):=\frac{x}{|B_{r}(0)|}\mathbbm{1}_{B_{r}(0)}(x),

where
B_{r}(0)
denotes the ball of radius
r
centered at the origin, and
\mathbbm{1}
the characteristic function. See also
[
CGLM12
,
CHM11
,
CDF+11
,
CR24
,
PR18
]
for related models. We now state our main existence result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be an
L

(i)

If
\eta\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

T^{\ast}>0
, only depending on

,

,
N
,
|D\eta|(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
and a distributional solution

(ii)

Fix

The proof of Theorem

\essup_{t\in[0,T^{\ast}[}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}=+\infty,\qquad\text{respectively}\qquad\essup_{t\in[0,T^{\ast}_{q}[}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}=+\infty,

By inspecting the proof one obtains the following lower bounds on

T^{\ast}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln 2}{K_{1}+2^{j}K_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T^{\ast}_{q}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln 2}{K_{1}+2^{j}K_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

, and

(respectively, on

).
In some cases, the nonlocal equation at the first line of (

This occurs for instance in the above-mentioned case of anisotropic kernels in one space dimension, where a maximum principle holds. Note however that, contrary to the case of smooth kernels where one always has global-in-time existence

]
, in general in the rough-kernel case finite-time blow up does indeed occur, and we refer to

) below for an explicit example. We now state our uniqueness result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an
L
-Lipschitz continuous function
and
T\in]0,+\infty]
. Then the following holds.

(i)

Assume that
\eta\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that either

(1.2)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}R^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|\eta(x)|dx=0,\quad\text{where $B_{R}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x|\leq R\}$},

or

(1.3)

u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

holds true. Then, there is at most one distributional solution

u\in L^{\infty}(]0,T[;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

(ii)

Fix
p>1
and let
q=\nicefrac{{p}}{{(p-1)}}
. Assume that
\eta\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and either

(1.4)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}R^{d}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|\eta(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}=0,\quad\text{where $B_{R}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x|\leq R\}$,}

or (
1.3
) holds true. Then, there is at most one distributional solution

u\in L^{\infty}(]0,T[;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

Interestingly, the proof of Theorem
1.2
boils down to a Grönwall-type argument applied to the functional
Q_{R}
defined by equation (
4.3
) in §
4.1
below. The use of similar functionals is by now fairly classical in the analysis of equations coming from fluid dynamics or kinetic theory, see for example
[

,
MP94
]
, but to the best of our knowledge this is one of the first instances where it is used in the nonlocal conservation laws framework. The proof is quite flexible and can actually be extended up to the case

and

a finite measure, thus recovering results from
[
CLM13
]
(see also
[
FFRst
]
for a very recent contribution in the one-dimensional, anisotropic kernels case).

To state our last result we consider the blow up example in
[
KP17
, p.4065]
, which corresponds to the choices
d=1
,
V(t,x,\xi)=\xi
,
\eta=\mathbbm{1}_{]-1,0[}
, and we choose
u_{0}=2\mathbbm{1}_{]0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}[}
, so that the Cauchy problem (
1.1

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+\displaystyle{\partial_{x}\left[u\left(\int_{x}^{x+1}u(t,y)dy\right)\right]=0,}\\
\\
u(0,x)=2\mathbbm{1}_{]0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}[}(x).\\
\end{array}\right.

The locally bounded solution of (
1.5
) is

(1.6)

u(t,x)=\frac{2}{1-2t}\mathbbm{1}_{\{(t,x):\,t\leq x\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\}},

hence it is defined up to time

, and satisfies

(1.7)

u(t,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\delta_{x=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\quad\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})$ as $t\uparrow\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$},

where

denotes the space of finite Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}
and
\delta_{x=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

. Let us now consider the following question: let
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of smooth kernels converging to
\mathbbm{1}_{]-1,0[}
, and consider the corresponding sequence of Cauchy problems

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u_{n}+\partial_{x}[u_{n}(u_{n}\ast\eta_{n})]=0,\\
u_{n}(0,x)=2\mathbbm{1}_{]0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}[}(x).\\
\end{array}\right.

Global-in-time existence and uniqueness results for the above Cauchy problem are established in several papers, see for instance
[
CGLM12
,
CLM13
,
KP17
]
. Does the solutions sequence

admit a unique accumulation point? If the answer were positive, we could regard the limit point as a suitable definition of solution of (
1.5
) after the blow-up time. However, our last result shows that this is actually not the case, thus ruling out a fairly natural strategy for extending the solution after the blow up time.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
\alpha\in[0,1]
there is a sequence
\{\eta^{\alpha}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(1.9)

\eta^{\alpha}_{n}\to\mathbbm{1}_{]-1,0[}\quad\text{in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ as $n\to+\infty$}

and the corresponding sequence
\{u^{n}_{\alpha}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of solutions of (
1.8
) satisfies

(1.10)

u^{\alpha}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u&\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $L^{\infty}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R})$ for every $T<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}$,}\\
\nu_{\alpha}&\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $L^{\infty}(]\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},+\infty[;\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}))$,}\end{array}\right.

as
n\to+\infty
,
where
u
is the locally bounded solution of (
1.5
) for
0\leq t<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
, and

\nu_{\alpha}
is
a Dirac Delta concentrated on the line
x=\alpha t+(1-\alpha)/2
, that is,

(1.11)

\nu_{\alpha}\in L^{\infty}(]\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},+\infty[;\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}))\,,\qquad\nu_{\alpha}(t):=\delta_{\alpha t+(1-\alpha)/2}\,.

The construction of
\eta_{n}^{\alpha}
is completely explicit (see formulas (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) in §
5.1
below) and the very basic idea can be handwavingly described as follows. The convergence at the first line of (

) and
conclude that, at the heuristic level, to extend the limit solution after the blow-up time, one should determine the velocity field in (
1.8

\delta_{x=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\ast\mathbbm{1}_{]-1,0[}
, which basically amounts to single out the value of the
BV

\mathbbm{1}_{]-1,0[}
at
x=0
. For every
\alpha\in[0,1]

is heuristically speaking to select
\alpha

Preliminary results

First, we recall the definition of distributional solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

Fix
T>0
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and assume that
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\eta\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
are such that

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u[\partial_{t}\varphi+V(\cdot,\cdot,u\ast\eta)\cdot\nabla\varphi]dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}\varphi(0,\cdot)dx=0.

, namely

(2.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)u(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(X(t,z))u_{0}(z)\,dz,\quad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}

and

(2.11)

u(t,X(t,x))=u_{0}(x)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\rm div}\,b(s,X(s,x))ds\right)\quad\text{for a.e. $(t,x)\in]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

(see for instance
[
AC14
, Section 2]
). Finally, such solution is
renormalized
and in particular it satisfies

\partial_{t}|u|+{\rm div}\,[b|u|]=0
in the distributional sense. This implies that the unique solution of the continuity equation with initial datum
|u_{0}|
satisfies
X(t,\cdot)_{\#}|u_{0}|=\big|X(t,\cdot)_{\#}u_{0}\big|=|u(t,\cdot)|
, and so we have

(2.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)|u(t,x)|\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(X(t,z))|u_{0}(z)|\,dz,\quad\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$.}

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
, item (i)

In the case of a smooth convolution kernel

, existence and uniqueness results for the Cauchy problem (

) are established for instance in
[

]
. See also
[
CLM12
,

]

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in[1,+\infty]
and
q=\nicefrac{{p}}{{(p-1)}}
. Assume that
\eta\in C^{\infty}\cap W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and that
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then there are
K_{1}=C(L)
,
K_{2}=C(L,d,N)\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})}
such that the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(]0,T_{q}[;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

provided

(3.1)

T_{q}=\frac{\ln 2}{K_{1}+2K_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}.

Proof.

By
[
CLM13
, eq. (2.2)]
, the solution of (
1.1
) satisfies (
2.11
), provided
b
is as in (
2.5
). This implies in particular that
u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that
u(t,\cdot)
is compactly supported for every
t\geq 0
and that the map
t\mapsto\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is continuous. We now set

(3.2)

t_{q}:=\sup\{t\in[0,T_{q}]:\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\;\text{for every $s\in[0,t]$}\},

where
T_{q}
is defined as in (
3.1
) and the constants
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are to be chosen later.
We now assume by contradiction that
t_{q}<T_{q}
(so that, by continuity,
\|u(t_{q},\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
) and reach a contradiction, thereby showing that
t_{q}=T_{q}
and hence establishing the statement of the lemma.

We fix
\beta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and by multiplying the equation at the first line of (
1.1
) times
\beta^{\prime}(u)
we obtain (renormalization formula)

\partial_{t}[\beta(u)]+{\rm div}\,[b\beta(u)]+{\rm div}\,b\,[\beta^{\prime}(u)u-\beta(u)]=0,\qquad b(t,x):=V(t,x,[u\ast\eta](t,x)).

By applying the above equation with
\beta(u)=|u|^{q}
,
1\leq q<\infty
, and space-time integrating we arrive at

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t_{q},x)|^{q}dxds\leq\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+(q-1)\|{\rm div}\,b\|_{L^{\infty}(]0,t_{q}[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{0}^{t_{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(s,x)|^{q}dxds,

which owing to Grönwall’s Lemma yields

(3.3)

\begin{split}\|u(t_{q},\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\exp\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\,t_{q}\|{\rm div}\,b\|_{L^{\infty}(]0,t_{q}[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\exp\left(t_{q}\|{\rm div}\,b\|_{L^{\infty}(]0,t_{q}[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).\end{split}

Note that by directly estimating (
2.11
) we conclude that the bound (
3.3
) holds also in the case
q=\infty
.
By (
2.7
) we conclude that

(3.4)

\begin{split}\|{\rm div}\,b(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:divb}}}{{\leq}}\underbrace{C(L)}_{:=K_{1}}+\underbrace{C(L,d,N)\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})}}_{:=K_{2}}\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:tq}}}{{\leq}}K_{1}+2K_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\qquad\text{for every $s\leq t_{q}$.}\end{split}

By plugging the above formula into (
3.3
) we get

\|u(t_{q},\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\exp(t_{q}[K_{1}+2K_{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t_{q}<T_{q},\eqref{e:T}}}{{<}}2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which contradicts the equality
\|u(t_{q},\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
∎

We are now ready to provide the proof of item (i) of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
, item (i).

We apply Lemma
3.1
with
q=+\infty
and fix
T<T_{\infty}
. Next, we proceed according to the following steps.

Step 1:
we consider two sequences
\{u_{0n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{\eta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(3.5)

u_{0n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u_{0}\quad\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$},\quad\|u_{0n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad u_{0n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and

(3.6)

\eta_{n}\to\eta\quad\text{strongly in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})$},\quad\|\nabla\eta_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d})}\leq|D\eta|(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\eta_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We term
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the solution of (
1.1
) with convolution kernel
\eta_{n}
and initial datum
u_{0n}
. By applying Lemma
3.1
we get

(3.7)

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2\|u_{0n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:u0n}}}{{\leq}}2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Owing to (
3.7
), up to subsequences

(3.8)

u_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u\,\quad\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $L^{\infty}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Assume for a moment that, as we will show in
Step 2
below, we can find a representative of the function
u\in L^{\infty}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
(which a-priori is well-defined only up to negligible sets) such that

(3.9)

u_{n}(t,\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u(t,\cdot)\quad\text{weakly${}^{\ast}$ in $L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ for every $t\in]0,T[$}.

We deduce from (
3.9
) that

(3.10)

V(\cdot,\cdot,u_{n}\ast\eta_{n})\to V(\cdot,\cdot,u\ast\eta)\;\quad\text{strongly in $L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(]0,T[\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Indeed, for every
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable and bounded we have

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|V(\cdot,\cdot,u_{n}\ast\eta_{n})&-V(\cdot,\cdot,u\ast\eta)\|_{L^{1}(]0,T[\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L\|u_{n}\ast\eta_{n}-u\ast\eta\|_{L^{1}(]0,T[\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\\
&\leq L{\|u_{n}\ast\eta_{n}-u_{n}\ast\eta\|_{L^{1}(]0,T[\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}}+L{\|u_{n}\ast\eta-u\ast\eta\|_{L^{1}(]0,T[\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}}.\end{split}

To control the first term at the right-hand side of the above expression we point out that for a.e.

|u_{n}\ast\eta_{n}-u_{n}\ast\eta|(t,x)=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x-y)[\eta_{n}-\eta](y)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:linfty}}}{{\leq}}2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\eta_{n}-\eta\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:etan}}}{{\to}}0.

To control the second term at the right-hand side of (
3.11
), we point out that by (

) for every

|u_{n}\ast\eta-u\ast\eta|(t,x)=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[u_{n}-u](t,y)\eta(x-y)\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:pointwise}}}{{\to}}0.

By Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem we get that both terms in the right-hand side of (

) vanish in
n\to+\infty
limit.
With (
3.10
) in place, we can then pass to the limit in (
2.1
) and get that

is a distributional solution of (
1.1
).

Step 2:
we are left to establish (

). The argument relies on fairly standard techniques (see for instance
[
Bre11
, Chapter 3]
and
[
Daf16
, Lemma 1.3.3]
) and we provide it for the sake of completeness. We recall (
3.7
) and term

the closed ball of radius

and center at the origin in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we endow it with the weak-
∗
topology. We fix a countable family

\{\psi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

the function

is a distance metrizing the weak
∗
topology on

. We introduce the sequence of functions

(3.12)

where

is the same as in (

is equi-continuous with respect to the distance

. We fix

and choose

\begin{split}\sum_{k=k^{\ast}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[u_{n}(t_{1},x)-u_{n}(t_{2},x)]\psi_{k}(x)dx\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:linfty}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{k=k^{\ast}}^{+\infty}\frac{4}{2^{k}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\underbrace{\|\psi_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}_{\leq 1}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.\end{split}

By using the equation for

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{k^{\ast}-1}&\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[u_{n}(t_{1},x)-u_{n}(t_{2},x)]\psi_{k}(x)dx\right|=\sum_{k=1}^{k^{\ast}-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi_{k}(x)\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial t}(s,x)dsdx\right|\\

4.1.
Proof of Theorem

the solutions of the Cauchy problems

\end{array}\right.

respectively. As pointed out before, the well-posedness of the above Cauchy problems follows from Lemma

we fix

Note that if
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we are abusing notation since actually
\chi_{R}
, and henceforth

, does not depend on

. Also, owing to
Step 1
and to the assumption

, the quantity

is finite for every

.

Step 3:
assume that we have established the equality
Q_{R^{\ast}}(t)=0
for some

We now show that this implies

. Towards this end, we point out that the equality

for a.e.

.
This yields

, since for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)[u_{1}(t,x)-u_{2}(t,x)]dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:pushf2}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[\varphi(X_{1}(t,z))-\varphi(X_{2}(t,z))]u_{0}(z)dz=0.\end{split}

Step 4:
we compute

(4.5)

\begin{split}\dfrac{dQ_{R}}{dt}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:cauchy},\eqref{e:qerre}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{R}|u_{0}||b_{1}(t,X_{1})-b_{2}(t,X_{2})|\,dx\\

where we have used the notation

for

, which is consistent with (
2.5
).
To control the first integral at the right-hand side of (

) we use the Lipschitz bound on

given by (

) and conclude that

\begin{split}I\leq[L+L|D\eta|(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{R}(x)|u_{0}(x)||X_{1}(t,x)-X_{2}(t,x)|\,dx=[L+L|D\eta|(\mathbb{R}^{d})\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}]Q_{R}(t).\end{split}

) holds for every test function

. This yields a control on the integrand in

, namely

\begin{split}|b_{1}(t,X_{2})-b_{2}(t,X_{2})|&\leq L|(u_{1}-u_{2})\ast\eta|(t,X_{2})=L\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta(X_{2}(t,x)-y)[u_{1}(t,y)-u_{2}(t,y)]\,dy\right|\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:pushf2}}}{{=}}L\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\eta(X_{2}(t,x)-X_{1}(t,y))-\eta(X_{2}(t,x)-X_{2}(t,y))\right]u_{0}(y)\,dy\right|.\end{split}

we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the case

. We recall that in this case

, plug (
4.7
) into the expression for

\begin{split}II&\leq L\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}(y)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\eta(X_{2}(t,x)-X_{1}(t,y))-\eta(X_{2}(t,x)-X_{2}(t,y))||u_{0}(x)|dxdy\\

and by plugging (
4.6
) and (
4.8

) into (
5.5
) and conclude that

(5.21)

u^{\alpha}_{n}(t,x)\geq 2,\quad\quad\text{for every $t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$, $n\geq 18$ and $x\in[X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,0),X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})]$.}

Step 2:
we show that
X^{\alpha}_{\ell}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}=X_{r}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
. Owing to (
5.19
) we have

(5.22)

X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,0)\leq t,\quad X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\geq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\quad\text{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}$}

and hence
X^{\alpha}_{\ell}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,
X^{\alpha}_{r}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\geq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. Assume by contradiction that

X^{\alpha}_{\ell}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
; this implies that there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

(5.23)

X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\varepsilon<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\leq X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})

for
n
sufficiently large. We set

E_{\varepsilon}:=\{(t,x):\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{4}}<t<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{8}}\,,\;\;\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\varepsilon<x<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}{-\nicefrac{{\varepsilon}}{{2}}}\}

and we point out that, owing to (
5.19
) and (
5.23
), we have

X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,0)\leq x\leq X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\quad\text{for every $(t,x)\in E_{\varepsilon}$}

and every
n
sufficiently large. Owing to (
5.21
), this implies

(5.24)

\iint_{E_{\varepsilon}}u^{\alpha}_{n}(t,y)dtdy\geq\varepsilon^{2}/8

for every
n
sufficiently large. On the other hand, owing to (
1.6
), we have

(5.25)

\iint_{E_{\varepsilon}}u(t,y)dtdy=0.

By combining (
5.24
),(
5.25
) and the limit at the first line of (
1.10
) we get a contradiction, which allows us to conclude that
X^{\alpha}_{\ell}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. An analogous argument yields the equality

X^{\alpha}_{r}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
.

Step 3:
we conclude the proof of (
5.20
). We recall that, owing to (
5.4
),

(5.26)

\begin{split}X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,0)&=X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)+\int_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{\alpha}_{n}(X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)-y)u^{\alpha}_{n}(s,y)dyds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:implica}}}{{=}}X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)+\int_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{t}\int_{X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)}^{X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}\eta^{\alpha}_{n}(X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)-y)u^{\alpha}_{n}(s,y)dyds.\end{split}

We now use (
5.14
), which implies that for every
y\in[X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0),X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})]
,

s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
n\geq 18

(5.27)

-3/4\leq X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)-y\leq 0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:etaalpha},\eqref{e:bderre}}}{{\implies}}\eta^{\alpha}_{n}(X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)-y)\geq\alpha.

By plugging the above inequality into (
5.26
), we get

\begin{split}X^{\alpha}_{n}(t,0)&\geq X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)+\int_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{t}\int_{X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)}^{X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}\alpha\,u^{\alpha}_{n}(s,y)dyds\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:pushf2}}}{{=}}X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)+\int_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{t}\alpha ds=X^{\alpha}_{n}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},0)+\alpha[t-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}].\end{split}

By passing to the
n\to+\infty
limit in the above inequality and recalling
Step 2
we establish the first inequality in (
5.20
).
To establish the second inequality in (
5.20
) we use an analogous argument, but instead of (
5.27
) we use the observation that, for every

y\in[X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0),X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})]

X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})-y\geq 0
and hence
\eta^{\alpha}_{n}(X^{\alpha}_{n}(s,0)-y)\leq\alpha
.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
. ∎

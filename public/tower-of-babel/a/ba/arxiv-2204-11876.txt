Probing the ν R \nu_{R} -philic Z ′ Z^{\prime} at DUNE near detectors
Garv Chauhan, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Xun-Jie Xu

Abstract
We consider a hidden U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge symmetry under which only the right-handed neutrinos ( ν R \nu_{R} ) are charged. The corresponding gauge boson is referred to as the ν R \nu_{R} -philic Z ′ Z^{\prime} . Despite the absence of direct gauge couplings to ordinary matter at tree level, loop-induced couplings of the ν R \nu_{R} -philic Z ′ Z^{\prime} via left-right neutrino mixing can be responsible for its experimental accessibility. An important feature of the ν R \nu_{R} -philic Z ′ Z^{\prime} is that its couplings to neutrinos are generally much larger than its couplings to charged leptons and quarks, thus providing a particularly interesting scenario for future neutrino experiments such as DUNE to probe. We consider two approaches to probe the ν R \nu_{R} -philic Z ′ Z^{\prime} at DUNE near detectors via (i) searching for Z ′ Z^{\prime} decay signals, and (ii) precision measurement of elastic neutrino-electron scattering mediated by the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson. We show that the former will have sensitivity comparable to or better than previous beam dump experiments, while the latter will improve current limits substantially for large neutrino couplings.

Introduction

The discovery of neutrino oscillations has established non-zero neutrino masses on a firm footing
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
. It also clearly points towards the presence of Beyond the Standard Model (BSM) physics. There has been monumental progress to understand the standard
3\times 3
neutrino mixing paradigm by measuring the mixing parameters with ever-increasing accuracy but we are yet to understand the neutrino mass mechanism. There have been several proposals in the literature (for reviews, see e.g. Refs.
Mohapatra:2006gs
;
deGouvea:2016qpx
), which always come with new interactions of neutrinos. Some of these ideas might be testable with the upcoming high-intensity frontier experiments in the near future

.

In this work, we consider a BSM scenario with a hidden
U(1)
gauge symmetry under which only the right-handed neutrinos (
\nu_{R}
) are charged
Chauhan:2020mgv
. These right-handed neutrinos interact with SM particles only through their mixing with the active neutrinos. As a result, the new gauge boson

associated with the hidden

W^{\pm}/Z

1

-philic

e^{+}e^{-}
colliders, and hence, little space for DUNE to probe, the

-philic

In our case, due to stronger couplings to neutrinos than to quarks,

for the production of neutrinophilic

2.2
Neutrino flavor states at low energies

Neutrinos in most experiments are produced via charged-current interactions.
Since
\nu_{L}
are the eigenstates of such interactions, one
might expect that neutrinos at production are simply
\nu_{L}
. However,
due to a small fraction of heavy neutrino components in
\nu_{L}
,
only a quantum superposition of light mass eigenstates (
\nu_{1}
,

\nu_{2}
,
\nu_{3}
) can be produced if the heavy mass eigenstates
are heavier than the masses of the particles responsible for neutrino production
(typically
\pi^{\pm}
,
K^{\pm},

\mu^{\pm}
, etc.). This implies
that a neutrino state produced in the laboratory is slightly different
from
\nu_{L}

flavor states at low energies as follows:

\nu_{\alpha}^{{\rm lab}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(U_{L}\right)_{\alpha i}\nu_{i}\thinspace,\ \alpha\in\{e,\mu,\tau\}\thinspace.

(24)

Further, we introduce a set of orthogonal states
\nu_{\alpha}^{\perp{\rm lab}}=\sum_{i=4}^{6}\left(U_{R}\right)_{\alpha i}\nu_{i}

to complete the basis so that
(\nu^{{\rm lab}},\ \nu^{\perp{\rm lab}})

and

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{L}\\
\nu_{R}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-iC_{L}&S\\

\nu^{\perp{\rm lab}}\end{array}\right),

(25)

where

\displaystyle C_{L,R}

\displaystyle=U_{L,R}{\rm diag}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})U_{L,R}^{\dagger}\thinspace,

(26)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=U_{L}{\rm diag}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})U_{R}^{\dagger}\thinspace.

(27)

6
Conclusion

Hidden
U(1)
symmetries in the right-handed neutrino (
\nu_{R}
) sector are theoretically well motivated and would give rise to an inherently dark gauge boson which we refer to as the
\nu_{R}
-philic
Z^{\prime}
.
Due to the loop-suppressed couplings to normal matter and comparatively larger couplings to neutrinos, neutrino experiments are the most suited to probe the
\nu_{R}
-philic
Z^{\prime}
.

In this work we studied the sensitivity of the future DUNE experiment to the
\nu_{R}
-philic
Z^{\prime}
.
We considered two complementary near detectors, DUNE-LArTPC and DUNE-MPD (HPgTPC), which could be sensitive to
Z^{\prime}
signals via elastic
\nu
-
e
scattering and via
Z^{\prime}
decay respectively. We stress here that the ratio of electron and neutrino couplings,
r=g_{\nu}/g_{e}
, which is practically a free parameter in the model, plays a crucial role. Larger neutrino couplings lead to higher elastic
\nu
-
e
scattering rates in DUNE-LArTPC but make
Z^{\prime}
decay less visible in DUNE-MPD due to the enhanced invisible decay width. The combined results are shown in Fig.
6
and compared with existing bounds. For
r=1
or
5
, DUNE-MPD exhibits a significant advantage over other beam dump experiments in the mass range
0.1{\rm GeV}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 1{\rm GeV}
. On the other hand, for larger
r
such as
r=10
or
100
, DUNE-LArTPC as a scattering experiment will be able to generate the leading constraints, exceeding the collider bounds from BaBar, LHCb, etc. We conclude that there is an excellent prospect of DUNE probing new physics hidden in the sector of right-handed neutrinos.

The proton bremsstrahlung formula presented above is valid only
if the incoming and outgoing protons as well as the
Z^{\prime}
boson are
ultra-relativistic
Blumlein:2013cua
. Typically in the literature
one imposes upper and lower bounds on
z
,
z_{\min}<z<z_{\max}
.
For instance, previous studies on the SHiP experiment often take
(z_{\min},\ z_{\max})=(0.1,\ 0.9)

deNiverville:2016rqh
;
SHiP:2020vbd
.
For the DUNE setup, we set
z_{\min}=5m_{Z^{\prime}}/E_{p}
so that the
outgoing
Z^{\prime}
is relativistic. If
z
is very close to
1
, the
outgoing proton would be non-relativistic. Hence we set the upper
bound
z_{\max}=1-5m_{p}/E_{p}
. The factor
5
here is chosen
so that the relativistic approximation holds and meanwhile the width
of the integration interval (
z_{{\rm max}}-z_{\min}\approx 1
) is
not significantly reduced. We have checked that varying this factor
does not change the result significantly, provided that the two conditions
are satisfied.

Absorption, scattering and shadow by a noncommutative black hole with global monopole
M. A. Anacleto, F. A. Brito, J. A. V. Campos, E. Passos

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the process of massless scalar wave scattering due to a noncommutative black hole with a global monopole through the partial wave method. We computed the cross section of differential scattering and absorption at the low frequency limit. We also calculated, at the high frequency limit, the absorption and the shadow radius by the null geodesic method. We showed that noncommutativity causes a reduction in the differential scattering/absorption cross section and shadow radius, while the presence of the global monopole has the effect of increasing the value of such quantities. In the limit of the null mass parameter, we verify that the cross section of differential scattering, absorption and shadow ray approach to a non-zero value proportional to a minimum mass.

II
Noncommutative Black Hole with a global monopole

In this section, we introduce the noncommutative black hole with a global monopole for the purpose of determining the differential scattering cross section and absorption for this model.
Thus, we adopt the partial wave method to calculate the phase shift at the low energy limit.
The spherically symmetrical noncommutative black hole with global monopole is described by the following line element

\displaystyle ds^{2}=F(r)dt^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{F(r)}-r^{2}\left(d\vartheta^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta d\phi^{2}\right).

The function

\displaystyle F(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-8\pi\eta^{2}-\frac{2{\cal M}_{\theta}}{r},

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-8\pi\eta^{2}-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{8M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}r^{2}}.

\displaystyle r_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2M}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}-4\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}+\cdots=\frac{2M}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}\left[1-\frac{2(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}{M}\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}\right],

(4)

\displaystyle r_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}+\cdots.

(5)

The mass

\displaystyle M=\left(1-8\pi\eta^{2}\right)\left(\frac{r_{\eta}}{2}+2\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}\right).

(6)

The Hawking temperature is given by

\displaystyle T_{H}=\dfrac{\left(1-8\pi\eta^{2}\right)}{4\pi r_{\eta}^{2}}\left(r_{\eta}-4\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}\right).

II.1
Differential Scattering Cross Section and Absorption

Now, we start our analysis of the massless scalar field equation to examine the scattered wave in the background (
1
), given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\Big(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Psi\Big)=0.

(12)

Next, we use the following separation of variables in the Klein-Gordon equation (
12
)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\omega lm}({\bf r},t)=\frac{{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)}{r}Y_{lm}(\vartheta,\phi)e^{-i\omega t},

(13)

being
Y_{lm}(\vartheta,\phi)
the spherical harmonics and
\omega
the frequency.

Hence, we find the radial equation for
{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)
as follows

\displaystyle F(r)\frac{d}{dr}\left(F(r)\dfrac{d{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)}{dr}\right)+\left[\omega^{2}-V_{eff}\right]{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)=0,

(14)

with

\displaystyle V_{eff}=\frac{F(r)}{r}\frac{dF(r)}{dr}+\frac{F(r)l(l+1)}{r^{2}},

(15)

the effective potential.

Next, we introduce a new radial function,
\chi(r)=F^{1/2}(r){\cal R}(r)
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\chi(r)}{dr^{2}}+V(r)\chi(r)=0,

(16)

being
V(r)
the potential, given by

\displaystyle V(r)=\frac{[F^{\prime}(r)]^{2}}{4F^{2}(r)}-\dfrac{F^{\prime\prime}(r)}{2F(r)}+\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{F^{2}(r)}-\frac{V_{eff}}{F^{2}(r)}.

(17)

At this point, by performing a power series expansion in
1/r
in the above equation, we find

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\chi(r)}{dr^{2}}+\left[\frac{\omega^{2}}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{2}}+{\cal U}_{eff}(r)\right]\chi(r)=0,

(18)

The effective potential is now given by

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{eff}(r)=\frac{4M{\omega}^{2}}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{3}r}+\frac{12\ell^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{32M{\omega}^{2}}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{4}r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{\pi}}+\cdots,

(19)

Here, due to the modification of the term
1/r^{2}
in the effective potential
Anacleto:2017kmg
;
Anacleto:2019tdj
, we define

\displaystyle\ell^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{(l^{2}+l)}{12(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}+\frac{r_{\eta}^{2}{\omega}^{2}}{4(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{2}}.

(20)

Note that the suitable asymptotic behavior for the effective potential is satisfied, i.e.,
{\cal U}_{eff}(r)\rightarrow 0
when
r\rightarrow\infty
.
Next, we can find the phase shift analytically, at the low frequency limit, by means of the following
Ansatz

;
Anacleto:2019tdj

\displaystyle\delta_{l}\approx 2(l-\ell)=2\left(l-\sqrt{-\frac{(l^{2}+l)}{12(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}+\frac{r^{2}_{\eta}{\omega}^{2}}{4(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{2}}}\right).

Hence, the phase shift

\displaystyle\delta_{l}=\delta_{0}+\delta_{l\geq 1},\quad\delta_{0}=-\frac{{\omega}r_{\eta}}{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})},\quad\delta_{l\geq 1}=0.

Furthermore, with determined

\displaystyle a^{1}_{l}=a^{0}_{l}-\frac{l+1}{2l+3}a^{0}_{l+1}-\frac{l}{2l-1}a^{0}_{l-1},

(24)

and

\displaystyle a^{0}_{l}=(2l+1)\left(e^{2i\delta_{l}}-1\right).

(25)

Therefore, from expression (
24

l=0

\displaystyle a^{1}_{0}=a^{0}_{0}-\frac{1}{3}a^{0}_{1},

25

\displaystyle a^{0}_{0}=e^{2i\delta_{0}}-1,

(28)

because
\delta_{l\geq 1}=0

23

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\Big|\frac{1}{2i{{\omega}}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a^{0}_{l}\frac{P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)}{1-\cos\vartheta}\Big|^{2}=\Big|\frac{1}{2i{{\omega}}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)\left(e^{2i\delta_{l}}-1\right)\frac{P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)}{1-\cos\vartheta}\Big|^{2},

(29)

and that at the small angle limit equation (

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{{\omega}^{2}\vartheta^{4}}\Big|\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)e^{i\delta_{l}}\sin(\delta_{l}){P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)}\Big|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{4}{{\omega}^{2}\vartheta^{4}}\Big|e^{i\delta_{0}}\sin(\delta_{0})P_{0}(\cos\vartheta)+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(2l+1)e^{i\delta_{l\geq 1}}\sin(\delta_{l\geq 1}){P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)}\Big|^{2}.

In order to investigate the behavior of the radial equation (
14
) in the asymptotic limits we define the tortoise coordinate
\varrho
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varrho}=F(r)\frac{d}{dr},\quad\quad F(r)=1-8\pi\eta^{2}-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{8M\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{\pi}r^{2}},

\displaystyle\varrho=r+\frac{r^{2}_{\theta}}{r_{\theta}-r_{\eta}}\ln\Big|{r-r_{\theta}}\Big|-\frac{r^{2}_{\eta}}{r_{\theta}-r_{\eta}}\ln\Big|{r-r_{\eta}}\Big|.

14

\displaystyle\dfrac{d^{2}{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)}{d\varrho^{2}}+\left[\omega^{2}-V_{eff}\right]{\cal R}_{\omega l}(r)=0.

(50)

Note that, when applying the limits
r\rightarrow r_{\eta}
and

, the effective potential defined in (
15

Consequently, we can examine the scattering problem by exploring the radial solution at these asymptotic limits.
In this way, we have the following boundary conditions:

\displaystyle R_{\omega l}(r)\approx\Big\{\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{in}e^{-iw\varrho}+A_{out}e^{iw\varrho},&\quad\varrho\rightarrow+\infty\quad(r\rightarrow\infty),\\
A_{tr}e^{-iw\varrho},&\quad\varrho\rightarrow-\infty\quad(r\rightarrow r_{\eta}),\end{array}

Next, we determine the absorption by applying equation (

\displaystyle e^{2i\delta_{l}}=(-1)^{l+1}\frac{A_{out}}{A_{in}}.

Here we present the numerical results that were obtained by numerically solving the radial equation (

III
Null Geodesics and Shadow

In this section, we will apply the null geodesic method to compute the absorption at the high frequency limit and
we will also determine the shadow radius of the noncommutative black hole with global monopole.
Therefore, at this limit, we check the validity of our numerical results.

III.1
Null Geodesics

As a starting point we consider the following Lagrangian density:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu},

(55)

being “.” the derivative with respect to an affine parameter.
Hence, by applying the line element (
1
), we have

2\mathcal{L}=F(r)\dot{t}^{2}-\dfrac{\dot{r}^{2}}{F(r)}-r^{2}\left(\dot{\vartheta}^{2}+\sin^{2}\vartheta\dot{\phi}^{2}\right).

(56)

At this point, we will consider the motion on the equatorial plane by taking
\vartheta=\pi/2
.
Hence, we can find the equations of motion from the Hamilton-Jacobi equation such that

E=F(r)\dot{t},\qquad\quad L=r^{2}\dot{\phi},

(57)

where
E
(energy) and
L
(angular momentum) are the conserved quantities.
In the null geodesics analysis we have,
g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}=0
and so we find

\dot{r}^{2}+F(r)\dfrac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}=E^{2}.

(58)

Now, by introducing a new variable
u=1/r
to write the orbital equation
in terms of the radii
r_{\eta}
and
r_{\theta}
, we obtain

\left(\dfrac{du}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{1}{b^{2}}-\left(1-8\pi\eta^{2}\right)\left(1-ur_{\eta}\right)\left(1-ur_{\theta}\right)u^{2},

(59)

here
b=L/E
is the impact parameter defined as the perpendicular distance (measured at infinity) between the geodesic and a parallel line that passes through the origin.
Hence, by differentiating Eq. (
59
), we find

\dfrac{d^{2}u}{d\phi^{2}}=\dfrac{\left(1-8\pi\eta^{2}\right)u}{2}\left[ur_{\eta}\left(1-ur_{\theta}\right)+ur_{\theta}\left(1-ur_{\eta}\right)-2\left(1-ur_{\eta}\right)\left(1-ur_{\theta}\right)\right].

(60)

The behavior of the geodesic lines for different values of the impact parameter
b
can be obtained by solving the equations
(
59

In the graphics, we have a black disk that represents the limit of the event horizon, the internal dotted circle is the radius for the photon sphere (critical radius), and the external dashed circle is the critical impact parameter (shadow).

To determine the critical radius (

b_{c}

du/d\phi=0
and
d^{2}u/d\phi^{2}=0

\displaystyle r_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\left[3\left(r_{\eta}+r_{\theta}\right)+\sqrt{9\left(r_{\eta}+r_{\theta}\right)^{2}-32r_{\eta}r_{\theta}}\right],

(61)

\displaystyle b_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{r_{c}^{2}}{\sqrt{\left(1-8\pi\eta^{2}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{\eta}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{\theta}\right)}}.

(62)

(64)

(65)

III.2
Classic Analysis

Rewriting the equation of the orbit (
59
) in terms of
r
, we have

\dfrac{d\phi}{dr}=\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\dfrac{1}{b^{2}}-\dfrac{(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{2M}{r^{3}}-\dfrac{8M}{r^{4}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\theta}{\pi}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(76)

To calculate the total angle deflected by the beam we have to integrate the above equation. One way to integrate analytically is to take the limit
M/r<<1
. Before doing this expansion let us organize the equation by making changes of variables

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{r}\left(\delta-\dfrac{M}{r}+\dfrac{(\tilde{\theta}-1)}{2}\dfrac{M^{2}}{r^{2}}\right),

(77)

\displaystyle y^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\left(\delta-\dfrac{M}{r}\right)^{2}+\left(\delta-\dfrac{M}{r}\right)\dfrac{(\tilde{\theta}-1)M^{2}}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{(\tilde{\theta}-1)^{2}}{4}\dfrac{M^{4}}{r^{4}}\right],

(78)

\displaystyle\dfrac{dy}{dr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\delta}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{2M}{r^{3}}-\dfrac{3(\tilde{\theta}-1)M^{2}}{2r^{4}},

(79)

where we define
\tilde{\theta}=\dfrac{8}{M}\sqrt{\dfrac{\theta}{\pi}}
and
\delta=1-8\pi\eta^{2}
, so the equation (
76
) is rewritten in this new variable as

\displaystyle\small\dfrac{d\phi}{dy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\delta+\dfrac{2M}{r}-\dfrac{3(\tilde{\theta}-1)}{2}\dfrac{M^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{b^{2}}-\dfrac{y^{2}}{\delta^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{\delta r^{2}}\left[(\delta-1)\left(-\delta+\dfrac{2M}{r}-\dfrac{\tilde{\theta}M^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\right.\right.

(80)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\left.\dfrac{M^{2}}{\delta r^{2}}\left(\delta-1+(\tilde{\theta}-1)\dfrac{M}{\delta r}-(\tilde{\theta}-1)^{2}\dfrac{M^{2}}{4\delta r^{2}}\right)\right]\right\}^{-1/2}.

Now expanding the above equation at the limit
M/r<<1
and organizing the terms, we find

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\phi}{dy}=\dfrac{b}{\delta^{2}\sqrt{1-\dfrac{y^{2}b^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}}\left\{\delta+\dfrac{2M}{r}-\dfrac{2\left(-4+3\delta(\tilde{\theta}-1)\right)}{\delta^{2}}\dfrac{M^{3}}{r^{3}}+\dfrac{\left(8-3(\tilde{\theta}-1)\delta\right)}{2\delta}\dfrac{M^{2}}{r^{2}}+\dfrac{b^{2}\delta^{3}(1-\delta)}{2\left(b^{2}y^{2}-\delta^{2}\right)r^{2}}+\cdots\right\}.

(81)

We can identify the
y
in terms of
M/r
and a
y^{2}
in
M^{2}/r^{2}
, so we have

\dfrac{d\phi}{dy}=\dfrac{b}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{y^{2}b^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}}\left[\delta+\dfrac{2My}{\delta}+\dfrac{\left(12-3(\tilde{\theta}-1)\delta\right)M^{2}y^{2}}{2\delta^{3}}\right].

(82)

The total angle deflected by the beam is given by

\displaystyle\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{y_{0}}\dfrac{b}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{y^{2}b^{2}}{\delta^{2}}}}\left[\delta+\dfrac{2My}{\delta}+\dfrac{\left(12-3(\tilde{\theta}-1)\delta\right)M^{2}y^{2}}{2\delta^{3}}\right]dy,

(83)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{M\sqrt{1-b^{2}y_{0}^{2}/\delta^{2}}\left(8\delta^{2}-3My_{0}(4+\delta-\tilde{\theta})\right)}{2b\delta^{3}}+\dfrac{4b^{2}\delta^{2}+3M^{2}(4+\delta-\delta\tilde{\theta})\arcsin(by_{0}/\delta)}{2b^{2}\delta^{2}}+\dfrac{4M}{b\delta}.

(84)

Knowing that at the limit
M/r<<1
we have that
y_{0}\rightarrow\delta/b
and the result becomes

\Delta\phi=\dfrac{4M}{b\delta}+\pi+\dfrac{3\pi(4+\delta(1-\tilde{\theta}))M^{2}}{4b^{2}\delta^{2}}.

(85)

Finally, the deflection angle
\chi=\Delta\phi-\pi
in terms of
\sqrt{\theta}
and
\eta^{2}
is given by

\chi=\dfrac{4M}{b(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}+\dfrac{3\pi(5-8\pi\eta^{2})M^{2}}{4b^{2}(1-8\pi\eta^{2})^{2}}-\dfrac{6\pi M}{b^{2}(1-8\pi\eta^{2})}\sqrt{\dfrac{\theta}{\pi}}.

(86)

With the relationship of the deflection angle and the impact parameter, we can combine this result with the classical differential scattering section equation

\dfrac{d\sigma_{clas}}{d\Omega}=\dfrac{b}{\sin\vartheta}\Big|\dfrac{db}{d\vartheta}\Big|,

(87)

to get the following result at the limit of small

IV
Conclusions

In summary, in this paper, we investigate the process of massless scalar wave scattering due to a noncommutative black hole with a global monopole through the partial wave method.
We have obtained, at the low frequency limit, the phase shift analytically.
The effects of the noncommutativity cause a reduction in the differential scattering/absorption cross
section and shadow radius, while the presence of the global monopole has the effect of increasing the value of such quantities.
In addition, we show that these quantities are non-zero when we take the limit of the zero mass parameter.
Rather, they are proportional to a minimum mass.
Moreover, we have also checked the analytically obtained results by numerically solving the radial equation for arbitrary frequencies.

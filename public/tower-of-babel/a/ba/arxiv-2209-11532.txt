Metastability from the large deviations point of view: A Γ \Gamma -expansion of the level two large deviations rate functional of non-reversible finite-state Markov chains
C. Landim

Abstract
. Consider a sequence of continuous-time Markov chains ( X t ( n ) : t ≥ 0 ) (X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0) evolving on a fixed finite state space V V . Let ℐ n {\mathscr{I}}_{n} be the level two large deviations rate functional for X t ( n ) X^{(n)}_{t} , as t → ∞ t\to\infty . Under a hypothesis on the jump rates, we prove that ℐ n {\mathscr{I}}_{n} can be written as ℐ n = ℐ ( 0 ) + ∑ 1 ≤ p ≤ 𝔮 ( 1 / θ n ( p ) ) ​ ℐ ( p ) {\mathscr{I}}_{n}={\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\,+\,\sum_{1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}}(1/\theta^{(p)}_{n})\,{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)} for some rate functionals ℐ ( p ) {\mathscr{I}}^{(p)} . The weights θ n ( p ) \theta^{(p)}_{n} correspond to the time-scales at which the sequence of Markov chains X t ( n ) X^{(n)}_{t} exhibit a metastable behavior, and the zero level sets of the rate functionals ℐ ( p ) {\mathscr{I}}^{(p)} identify the metastable states.

Introduction

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0)

V

, so that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathscr{L}}_{n}f)(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in V}R_{n}(x,y)\,\{\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\}\;,\quad f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}\;.

(1.1)

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi_{n}
be the unique stationary state.

Denote by

the space of probability measures on

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L^{(n)}_{t}}\;:=\;\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X^{(n)}_{s}}\;ds\;,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\delta_{x}

. Thus,

is a random element of

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{P}}(V)
and

, stands

in the time interval
[0,t]
,

As the Markov chain

is irreducible, by the ergodic

, as

, the

Donsker and Varadhan
[

,

{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[\,L^{(n)}_{t}\,\sim\,\mu\,\big]\;\approx\;e^{-t\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)}\;,\quad\text{as $t\to\infty$}\;.

In this formula,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{P}}_{\!x}={\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}
,
x\in V
,
represents the distribution of the process
X^{(n)}_{t}
starting from

x
, and
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to[0,+\infty)
be the level two
large deviations rate functional given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)\;:=\;\sup_{H}J^{(n)}_{H}(\mu)}\;:=\;\sup_{H}\,-\,\int_{V}e^{-H}\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n}e^{H}\;d\mu\;,

(1.4)

where the supremum is carried over all functions

H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. A precise statement of (
1.3
) requires
some notation and is postponed to the next section. The functional

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
provides the cost for the empirical measure
L^{(n)}_{t}
to
be close to
\mu
for a very large
t
. By Lemma
A.8
, as the
process is irreducible,
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)=0
if, and only if,

\mu=\pi_{n}
,

We examine in this article the behavior of the functionals
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

as
n\to\infty
under some natural hypotheses on the jump rates

R_{n}
. Assume, initially, that the jump rates
R_{n}(x,y)
converge, as

, to a limit represented by
{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)}\;:=\;\lim_{n}R_{n}(x,y)\;\in\,{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\;,\quad y\,\neq\,x\,\in\,V\;.

(1.5)

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{L}}_{0}
the generator associated to these
rates and by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
the corresponding large
deviations rate functional. By Proposition
2.1
, as

n\to\infty
,
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)
converges to
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)
for all

\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
.

If the Markov chain
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
induced by the jump rates

analysis of the functionals

ends with Proposition
2.1
.
In contrast, if

has more than one closed irreducible class
a finer description of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
is possible.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{V}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}_{{\mathfrak{n}}}
,
{\mathfrak{n}}\geq 2
,
the closed irreducible classes of
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
be the stationary state supported in

{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
. By Lemma
A.8
,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
vanishes at any convex
combination of the measures
\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
. Since, by Proposition

2.1
,

converges to

natural to consider the sequence
\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)
, for some

\theta_{n}\to\infty
and a convex combination

\mu=\sum_{j}\omega_{j}\,\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
of the measures
\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
,
longing to obtain a non-trivial limit.

To find the correct sequence
\theta_{n}
, remark that, by (
1.4
),

\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
represents the large deviations rate functional
of the Markov chain induced by the generator
\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
,
that is, the rate functional of the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}

observed at the time scale
\theta_{n}
:

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X^{1,n}_{t}:=X^{(n)}_{\theta_{n}\,t}
.

Denote by
\beta_{n,j}
the transition time from
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
to

\cup_{k\not=j}{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
, this is the mean time for the process

X^{(n)}_{t}
to hit
\cup_{k\not=j}{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
when it starts from

{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
. For the sake of the argument, assume that

\beta_{n}=\beta_{n,1}
.

Fix a time-scale
\theta_{n}
such that
\theta_{n}\to\infty
,

\theta_{n}/\beta_{n}\to 0
. Denote this last relation by

\theta_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
or
\beta_{n}\succ\theta_{n}
. As the transition
time from
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
to
\cup_{k\not=j}{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
is of order

\beta_{n}
and
\beta_{n}\succ\theta_{n}
, in the time-scale
\theta_{n}

starting from
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
the chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
does not visit the set

\cup_{k\not=j}{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
. Therefore, the cost for keeping the
process at
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
should vanish, and one expects

\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\pi^{\sharp}_{1})\to 0
. Actually, as

\beta_{n,j}\succ\theta_{n}
for all
j
, the same conclusion should
hold for all measures
\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
, and to derive a non-trivial
limit for
\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
one has to observe the chain

X^{(n)}_{t}
in a time-scale at least of the order
\beta_{n}
.

In the time scale
\beta_{n}
, starting from
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}

the process visits
\cup_{k\not=j}{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
. There is, in
consequence, a positive cost to maintain it at
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
and

\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\pi^{\sharp}_{1})
should converge to a positive
limit. If all sequence
\beta_{n,j}
are of the same order, this
completes the description of
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
. Otherwise, one has to go to
longer time-scales.

The main result of this article, Theorem

, presents the
time-scales
1\prec\theta^{(1)}_{n}\prec\cdots\prec\theta^{({\mathfrak{q}})}_{n}
and functionals

such that

\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\;\longrightarrow{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\;,\quad 1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}\;.

This result permits to write the functional

as the expansion

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\;=\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\;+\;\sum_{p=1}^{{\mathfrak{q}}}\frac{1}{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}\,{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\;.

(1.6)

The weights

7
,

,

,
22
,

,

sequence
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,

V

\displaystyle-\,\inf_{\mu\in{\mathscr{A}}^{o}}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)

We examine in this article the asymptotic behavior of the rate

Fix a Polish space

(i)

-liminf for the sequence

: For each

and each

, we have that

(ii)

\Gamma
-limsup.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-limsup for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
there
exists a sequence
x_{n}\to x
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}(x_{n})\;\leq\;U(x)\;.

(2.2)

Recall that we denote by

the jump rates of the Markov chain

. Assume that the rates converge, as

, to a
finite limit denoted by
{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)
, see (

), and that

{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})>0

. The jump rates

, denoted
by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathbb{X}}_{t}:t\geq 0)
, which, of course, may be
reducible. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{L}}^{(0)}
its generator and by

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}:{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
the associated occupation-time
large deviations rate functional, given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)}\;=\;\sup_{H}\,-\,\sum_{x\in V}e^{-H(x)}\,\big[\,({\mathbb{L}}^{(0)}e^{H})\,(x)\;\big]\;\mu(x)\;,

where the supremum is carried over all functions

H:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Next result is proved in Section

.

Proposition 2.1

The sequence of functionals
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
.

Assume from now on that

Main assumption

To examine the convergence of
\theta_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
for some sequence

\theta_{n}\to\infty
, we introduce a natural hypothesis on the jump
rates proposed in
[
4
]
and adopted in
[
23
,
11
,
5
]
.

For two sequences of positive real numbers
(\alpha_{n}:n\geq 1)
,

(\beta_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
or

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\beta_{n}\succ\alpha_{n}
means that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}=0
. Similarly,

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\alpha_{n}\preceq\beta_{n}
or

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\beta_{n}\succeq\alpha_{n}
indicates that either

\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
or
\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}
converges to a positive
real number
a\in(0,\infty)
.

Two sequences of positive real numbers
(\alpha_{n}:n\geq 1)
,

(\beta_{n}:n\geq 1)
are said to be
comparable
if

\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
,
\beta_{n}\prec\alpha_{n}
or

\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}\to a\in(0,\infty)
. This condition excludes the
possibility that

\liminf_{n}\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}\neq\limsup_{n}\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}
.

A set of sequences

(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{u}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathfrak{R}}
, of positive real
numbers, indexed by some finite set
{\mathfrak{R}}
, is said to be comparable
if for all
{\mathfrak{u}}
,
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\mathfrak{R}}
the sequence

(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{u}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
are
comparable.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}E\subset\{(x,y)\in V\times V:y\not=x\}

a set of directed edges, and assume that for all
n\geq 1
,

\text{$R_{n}(x,y)>0$ if, and only if, $(x,y)\in E$}\;.

(2.5)

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Sigma_{m}
,

m\geq 1
, be the set of functions
k:E\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that

\sum_{(x,y)\in E}k(x,y)=m
. We assume, hereafter, that for every

m\geq 1
the set of sequences

\big(\,\prod_{(x,y)\in E}R_{n}(x,y)^{k(x,y)}:n\geq 1\,\big)\;,\quad k\in\Sigma_{m}\;,

(2.6)

is comparable.

As observed in
[
4
]
(see Remark 2.2 in
[
5
]
), assumption
(
2.6
) is fulfilled by all statistical mechanics models which
evolve on a fixed finite state space and whose metastable behaviour
has been derived.

Tree decomposition

Under the assumptions (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
),

[
4
,
23
]
constructed a rooted tree which describes the
behaviour of the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
at all different
time-scales. We refer to
[
5
]
for a clear presentation of the
construction as well as a simple example, and recall here the main
ideas.

Denote by
{\mathfrak{q}}+1\geq 2
the number of generations of the tree. The
elements of the
p
-th generation form a partition of
V
, and are
represented by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}},\Omega_{p}

for some finite increasing sequence

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}1={\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathfrak{q}}+1}
. The set

\Omega_{p}
may be empty while the sets
{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{j}
are all
non-empty. As
{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}\geq 1
, each generation has at least one
element. Here is a list of the main properties of the tree:

(1.a)

Each generation of the tree forms a partition of
V
;

(1.b)

The root, or
0
-th generation, is the set
V
. The first
generation has one or two elements depending on whether
\Omega_{1}
is
empty or not. If
\Omega_{1}=\varnothing
, it has one element, the set

{\mathscr{W}}^{(1)}_{1}=V
. If
\Omega_{1}\neq\varnothing
, it has two
elements, the sets
{\mathscr{W}}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\Omega_{1}=({\mathscr{W}}^{(1)}_{1})^{c}
.

(1.c)

Each child of a vertex is a subset of its parent: For
each
0\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
1\leq j\leq{\mathfrak{m}}_{p+1}
, either

{\mathscr{W}}^{(p+1)}_{j}\subset{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{k}
for some
1\leq k\leq{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}

or
{\mathscr{W}}^{(p+1)}_{j}\subset\Omega_{p}
. Moreover,

\Omega_{p+1}\subset\Omega_{p}
;

(1.d)

According to the notation, the number of elements of
generation
p
is equal to

{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}+{\boldsymbol{1}}_{\Omega_{p}\neq\varnothing}
, where

{\boldsymbol{1}}_{{\mathscr{A}}\neq\varnothing}
is equal to
1
if
{\mathscr{A}}
is not
empty and
0
otherwise. Starting from the first generation, the
number of descendents of a generation strictly increases: for

1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,

{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}+{\boldsymbol{1}}_{\Omega_{p}\neq\varnothing}<{\mathfrak{m}}_{p+1}+{\boldsymbol{1}}_{\Omega_{p+1}\neq\varnothing}
.

Construction of the tree

We describe in this subsection the details of the construction of the
tree. it is formed from the leaves to the root. The leaves

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}}
are the closed
irreducible classes of the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
introduced in the
previous section, and
\Delta_{1}
the set of transient states. The sets

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}
were represented there by

, a notation
frequently adopted below. In view of the definition of the sets

,

, which we assumed in

We turn to the construction of the parents of the leaves. This
procedure will be repeated recursively to define all generations from
the leaves to the root. Denote by
\Phi_{1}:V\to S_{1}
the projection

\Phi_{1}(\,\cdot\,)\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{1}}j\,\chi_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}}(\,\cdot\,)\;,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\chi_{{\mathscr{A}}}

. Hence,

projects to

and to

.

It follows from the main results of

]

-valued Markov chain

(note that this process does not take the
value

\Phi_{1}\big(\,X^{(n)}_{t\theta^{(1)}_{n}}\,\big)\;\overset{\rm f.d.d.}{-\!-\!\!\!\longrightarrow}\;{\mathbb{X}}^{(1)}_{t}\;.

(2.7)

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}}

the recurrent classes of the

-valued Markov chain

, and by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{T}}_{1}

,

. This partition of

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{V}}^{(2)}_{m}}\;:=\;\bigcup_{j\in{\mathfrak{R}}^{(1)}_{m}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}\;,\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{T}}^{(1)}}\;:=\;\bigcup_{j\in{\mathfrak{T}}_{1}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}\;,\quad m\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{2}\;:=\;\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}\}}\;,

(2.8)

so that

x^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{j}
,

y^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{k}

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{j}

x

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then, for all

,
y\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}R^{(p)}_{n}(x,y)\;=\;{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)\;.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
and
x
,
y\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
. By (
3.1
),
decomposing the probability appearing on the right-hand side of this

R^{(p)}_{n}(x,y)\;=\;R_{n}(x,y)\;+\;\sum_{z\neq y}R_{n}(x,z)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!z}\big[H_{y}=H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;.

. As

and
(by the tree construction)

{\mathscr{V}}_{k}
,

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,R^{(p)}_{n}(x,y)\;<\;\infty

As

),

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
k\not=j\in S_{1}
such that

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}\cup{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
,

x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}
, the sequence

\theta^{(1)}_{n}\,R^{(p)}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k})
is bounded.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
p
. For
p=1
, the assertion of the
lemma follows from (
3.3
), (
3.2
) and (
2.13
).

Fix
p>1
, and assume that the assertion of the lemma holds for

1\leq q<p
. Fix
k\not=j\in S_{1}
such that

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}\cup{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
,

x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}
. By (
3.1
),

R^{(p)}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k})\;=\;\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;.

Recall that
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}
. Assume that

H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}
. Later we consider the case

H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}>H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}

to estimate

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\,,\,H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\;\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}\big]\;=\;R^{(p-1)}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{k})\;.

By the induction hypothesis this later quantity multiplied by

\theta^{(1)}_{n}
is bounded.

It remains to estimate the expression

\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}<H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;.

By construction, there exists
S^{\prime}_{p-1}\subset S_{p-1}
such that

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}\setminus{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}=\cup_{\ell\in S^{\prime}_{p-1}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{\ell}
. Mind that
S^{\prime}_{p-1}
consists of the transient points
of the
S_{p-1}
-valued Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}^{(p-1)}_{t}
. Since

x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}
, let

m\in S_{p-1}\setminus S^{\prime}_{p-1}
such that
x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{m}
.
With this notation, the previous term is bounded by

\sum_{\ell\in S^{\prime}_{p-1}}\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{\ell}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}}\big]\;=\;\sum_{\ell\in S^{\prime}_{p-1}}R^{(p-1)}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{(p-1)}_{\ell})\;.

By(
3.3
), (
3.2
) and (
2.13
), the limit as
n\to\infty

of the previous expression multiplied by
\theta^{(p-1)}_{n}
is bounded by

\sum_{\ell\in S^{\prime}_{p-1}}r^{(p-1)}(m,\ell)\;.

This sum vanishes because
m
is a recurrent point of the chains

{\mathbb{X}}^{(p-1)}_{t}
and
S^{\prime}_{p-1}
is a transient subset. To complete
the proof of the lemma, it remains to recall that

\theta^{(1)}_{n}\leq\theta^{(p-1)}_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
1\leq p<q\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then,
r^{(p)}(i,k)\,=\,0
for all

k\neq i\in S_{p}
such that
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\not\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
,

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
.

Proof.

As
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
and

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\not\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
, by the tree construction
there exists
p\leq p^{\prime}<q
such that

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}
and

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\not\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime}+1)}
. Since

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}
, there exists
\ell\in S_{p^{\prime}}

such that
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{\ell}
. As

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\not\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime}+1)}
,
\ell
is a transient
state for the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{t}
.

On the other hand, as
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
and

{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}
,

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}
. Thus, there exists

m\in S_{p^{\prime}}
such that
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m}
. As

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime}+1)}
,
m
is a
recurrent state for the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{t}
. In particular,

m\not=\ell
.

As
m
is recurrent and
\ell
transient for the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{t}
,
r^{(p^{\prime})}(m,\ell)=0
. Thus, by (
3.3
) and
(
3.2
),

0\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}\,r^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}(m,\ell)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}\,\frac{1}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m}}\pi_{n}(x)\,R^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}(x,{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{\ell})\;.

By (
3.1
), for
n
fixed the expression on right-hand side is
equal to

\theta^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}\,\frac{1}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m}}\pi_{n}(x)\,\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{\ell}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}}\big]\;.

(3.4)

Since
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{\ell}
and

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\supset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}
,

{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;\leq\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{\ell}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}}\big]\;.

Hence, as
\theta^{(p^{\prime})}_{n}\geq\theta^{(p)}_{n}
and
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p^{\prime})}_{m}\supset{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}
, by (
2.13
), (
3.4
) is bounded below by

c_{0}\,\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]

for some positive constant

.
This expression is clearly bounded below by

c_{0}\,\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,\frac{1}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\pi_{n}(x)\,\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;=\;c_{0}\,r^{(p)}_{n}(i,k)\;.

Collecting the previous estimates yields that this expression vanishes
as

, as claimed.
∎

For
1\leq p<q\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
and
i\not=j\in S_{p}
. Assume that

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}
and
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}
are contained in
{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
:

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}\cup{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}\subset{\mathscr{V}}^{(q)}
. Let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{p,q}_{n}(i,j)}\;:=\;\frac{1}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\pi_{n}(x)\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}}R^{(q)}_{n}(x,y)\;.

The difference with respect to

is that we replaced

by

, that is, the trace on

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
by the one on the smaller set

.

Corollary 3.6
.

Fix
1\leq p<q\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
i\not=j\in S_{p}
. Assume that there
exists
m\in S_{q}
such that

Proof.

By (
3.3
), (
3.2
) and (
3.1
),

r^{p}(i,j)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\pi_{n}(x)\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;.

(3.5)

Let

,

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p,q)}:=\cup_{k\in S_{p,q}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{k}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\pi_{n}(x)\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{{\mathscr{U}}^{(p,q)}}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;=\;0\;.

∎

4.
\Gamma-\limsup
of the trace

The main result of this section, Proposition
4.1
, states that

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
is a
\Gamma-\limsup
for the sequence

\theta^{(p)}_{n}{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}_{n}
. Here,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}_{n}
stands for the

.
We assume below that the reader is familiar with the notation and

Fix

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}_{n}\colon{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)})\to[0,+\infty)
the
occupation time large deviations rate functional of the trace process

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}_{n}(\mu)}\;:=\;\sup_{H}\,-\,\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\mu(x)\,e^{-H(x)}\,[\,({\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}{\mathscr{L}}_{n})\,e^{H})\,](x)\;,

(4.1)

where the supremum is carried over all functions

H\colon{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}{\mathscr{L}}_{n}
,
introduced and examined in Appendix
C

the trace process
Y^{n,p}_{t}
. The main result of this section reads
as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)})
, there exists a sequence of
measures
\mu_{n}\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)})

The proof of Proposition

is divided in several lemmata. Fix

1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
and a measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)})
which can
be represented as
\mu=\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\pi^{(p)}_{j}
for
some
\omega\in{\mathscr{P}}(S_{p})

for all

\mu_{n}\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)})

.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{Q}}^{(p)}_{a}
,

, the

{\mathbb{X}}^{(p)}_{t}
, by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S^{\rm sgl}_{p}

is an equivalent class. Clearly,

S_{p}\;=\;\bigcup_{a=1}^{\ell_{p}}{\mathfrak{Q}}^{(p)}_{a}\;=\;S^{\rm sgl}_{p}\;\cup\;\bigcup_{a=1}^{m_{p}}{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}\;.

(4.2)

Recall from Appendix
A
the definition of a Markov chain
reflected at a set and the notation used to represent its generator.
Denote by
{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}_{a}

reflected at

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}_{a}:={\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}}\,{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}

As
\omega_{j}>0
for all

A.3

there exists
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\boldsymbol{h}}_{a}\colon{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}\to{\mathbb{R}}

Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}:=\cup_{j\in{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}

Lemma 4.2
.

The Markov chain associated to the generator

{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}}{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n}

Proof.

Recall from
[
2

that the
Markov chain induced by the trace generator

{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}

Denote by

the function given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}H_{a}}\;=\;\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}}{\boldsymbol{h}}_{a}(j)\,\chi_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}}\;,

(4.3)

{\mathscr{A}}

{\mathfrak{M}}_{H_{a}}\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}}{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n}

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mu^{p,a}_{n}\in{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a})

Lemma 4.3
.

The sequence of probability measures
\mu^{p,a}_{n}
converges to

\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{D}}^{(p)}_{a}}\omega^{(a)}_{j}\,\pi^{(p)}_{j}

\nu

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{i}
.

{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}

{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S^{a}_{1}

.

in the previous formula, by Lemma

\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}}\nu(y)\,{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(y,x)\,e^{H_{a}(x)-H_{a}(y)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{W}}^{(p)}_{a}}\nu(x)\,{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)\,e^{H_{a}(y)-H_{a}(x)}\;.

By definition of the sets
{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{i}
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(z,w)=0
if

Appendix A
The rate function

Fix a finite set
V
. Consider a
V
-valued continuous-time Markov
chain
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(X_{t}:t\geq 0)
, and denote by
{\mathscr{L}}
its
generator. The jump rates are represented by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
, so that

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathscr{L}}f)(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in V}R(x,y)\,\{\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\}

(A.1)

for all functions
f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda(x)
,
x\in V
, the holding rates and by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}p(x,y)
,
x
,
y\in V
, the jump probabilities, so that

R(x,y)=\lambda(x)\,p(x,y)
. Mind that we do not suppose the
process to be irreducible. We assume, however, that
\lambda(x)>0

for all
x\in V
. The case where some holding rates might vanish is
considered at the end of this section.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{P}}(V)
the space of probability measures
on
V
endowed with the weak topology. For a function

H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
, define
J_{H}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}J_{H}(\mu)}\;:=\;-\,\int_{V}e^{-H}{\mathscr{L}}e^{H}\,d\mu\;=\;-\,\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big[\,e^{H(y)-H(x)}-1\,\big]\;,

(A.2)

and let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I(\mu)}\;:=\;\sup_{H}J_{H}(\mu)\;,

(A.3)

where the supremum is carried over all functions

H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

To stress the dependence of the functionals
J_{H}
and
I
on the
generator
{\mathscr{L}}
, we sometimes denote them by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}J_{{\mathscr{L}},H}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{\mathscr{L}}}
,
respectively. Next result collects simple properties of the
functionals
J_{H}
and
I
.

Lemma A.1
.

For each
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
, the functional
H\mapsto J_{H}(\mu)
is
concave, and
J_{H+c}(\mu)=J_{H}(\mu)
for all constants
c
. The
functional
I
is convex, lower-semicontinuous, non-negative,
and bounded by
\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)\,\lambda(x)
.

Recall that a measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
is a stationary state for the
Markov chain induced by the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
if

\int_{V}({\mathscr{L}}f)\,d\mu=0
for all function
f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
As we do not assume the chain to be irreducible, the stationary state
may not be unique.

The Euler-Lagrange equation for
I
reads as

\int_{V}({\mathfrak{M}}_{H}\,{\mathscr{L}})\,G\,d\mu\;=\;0\quad\text{for all}\;\;G\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}\;.

(A.4)

In this formula, for a function
H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}\,{\mathscr{L}}
represents the tilted generator given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}[\,({\mathfrak{M}}_{H}\,{\mathscr{L}})\,f\,]\,(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in V}e^{-H(x)}\,R(x,y)\,e^{H(y)}\,\big[\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\big]

(A.5)

for
f\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R_{H}(x,y)\,:=\,e^{-H(x)}\,R(x,y)\,e^{H(y)}
.
Next result clarify the meaning of the Euler-Lagrange equation
(
A.4
).

Lemma A.2
.

A probability measure
\mu
in
V
is a stationary state for the
Markov chain induced by the generator
{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}
if, and only if,

I(\mu)\;=\;J_{H}(\mu)\;.

(A.6)

Proof.

Suppose that
\mu
is a stationary state for the Markov chain induced
by the generator
{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}
. Then, for all functions

G\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle J_{G}(\mu)\;-\;J_{H}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\;-\,\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big[\,e^{G(y)\,-\,G(x)}\,-\,e^{H(y)\,-\,H(x)}\,\big]

\displaystyle=\;-\,\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R_{H}(x,y)\,\big[\,e^{F(y)\,-\,F(x)}\,-\,1\,\big]\;,

where

. Since
\mu
is a stationary state for the Markov chain
induced by the generator
{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}
, the previous expression is equal
to

This expression is negative because

is
positive. This proves that

Conversely, suppose that (

) holds for all

yielding that

is stationary for the Markov chain induced by
the generator

∎

n_{0}\geq 1
such that

\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big[\,e^{H_{n}(y)-H_{n}(x)}-1\,\big]\;\leq\;1

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence,

\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,e^{H_{n}(y)-H_{n}(x)}\;\leq\;C_{0}\;:=\;1\,+\,\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;.

Thus,

H_{n}(y)\,-\,H_{n}(x)\;\leq\;C_{1}\;:=\;\ln\,\frac{C_{0}}{\min_{x,y}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)}

(A.9)

for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all edges
(x,y)
such that
R(x,y)>0
. In
this equation the minimum is performed over all edges
(x,y)
such
that
\mu(x)\,R(x,y)>0
.

Fix
x\in V\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. Since the process is irreducible, there
exists a self-avoiding path
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k}=x
such that

R(x_{i},x_{i+1})>0
for all
0\leq i<k
. Hence, by (
A.9
),
H_{n}(x)=H_{n}(x_{k})-H_{n}(x_{0})\leq C_{1}\,k\,\leq\,C_{2}\,:=\,C_{1}\,|V|
.

Conversely, there exists a self-avoiding path

x=y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{j}=x_{0}
such that
R(y_{i},y_{i+1})>0
for all

0\leq i<j
. Hence, by (
A.9
),

-H_{n}(x)=H_{n}(x_{0})-H_{n}(x)=H_{n}(y_{j})-H_{n}(y_{0})\leq C_{1}\,j\leq C_{1}\,|V|\,=\,C_{2}
, which proves Claim 1.

As the sequence
H_{n}
is uniformly bounded, we may extract a
subsequence, still denoted by
H_{n}
, which converges pointwisely to
a function
H
. By definition of the sequence
H_{n}
and by continuity,

I(\mu)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{H_{n}}(\mu)\;=\;J_{H}(\mu)\;,

as asserted. Moreover,

\max_{x,y\in V}|\,H(y)-H(x)\,|\,\leq\,C_{2}
, proving (
A.8
).

We turn to the proof of uniqueness. Assume that there are two
functions, denoted by
H
and
G
, which minimize. Let

F_{\theta}=\theta H+(1-\theta)G
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. By
concavity of the functional
J
, for all
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,

I(\mu)\;\geq\;J_{F_{\theta}}(\mu)\;\geq\;\theta\,J_{H}(\mu)\;+\;(1-\theta)\,J_{G}(\mu)\;=\;I(\mu)\;.

Hence,
\theta\mapsto J_{F_{\theta}}(\mu)
is constant. Taking the
second derivative yields that

\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big\{\,\big[\,G(y)-G(x)\,\big]\,-\,\big[\,H(y)-H(x)\,\big]\,\big\}^{2}e^{F_{\theta}(y)-F_{\theta}(x)}\;=\;0

for all
0<\theta<1
. Hence,
G(y)-G(x)\,=\,H(y)-H(x)
if

\mu(x)\,R(x,y)>0
. As the process is irreducible and the measure
positive,
G=H+c
for some constant
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

To show the validity of (
A.7
), note that

I(\mu)\;=\;J_{H}(\mu)\;=\;\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big\{\,1\,-\,e^{H(y)-H(x)}\,\big\}\;.

Since, by Lemma
A.2
,
\mu
is a stationary state for the
Markov chain induced by the generator
{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}
,

0\;=\;\sum_{x,y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,e^{H(y)-H(x)}\,[\,H(y)-H(x)\,]\;.

Adding the two previous identities yields (
A.7
).
∎

Remark A.4
.

It follows from the previous proof that the minimum in the denominator
in equation (
A.8
) can be restricted to a subset of edges
E_{0}

which keeps the chain irreducible.

Next result follows from the two previous lemmata.

Corollary A.5
.

Assume that the Markov chain induced by the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
is
irreducible and that
\mu(x)>0
for all
x\in V
. Then, there exists a
function
H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\mu
is stationary for

{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}
.

Corollary A.6
.

Assume that the Markov chain induced by the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
is
irreducible and that
\mu(x)>0
for all
x\in V
. Then,
I(\mu)=0
if
and only if
\mu
is the stationary state.

Proof.

Assume that
I(\mu)=0
. Then, since
a\,e^{a}\,-\,e^{a}\,+\,1\geq 0
,
each term in the sum (
A.7
) vanishes, and
H(y)=H(x)
if

\mu(x)\,R(x,y)>0
. As the Markov chain is irreducible,
H
is
constant. To complete the argument, it remains to recall that, by
Lemma
A.2
,
\mu
is a stationary state for the chain induced by
the generator
{\mathfrak{M}}_{H}{\mathscr{L}}={\mathscr{L}}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mu
is the stationary state. Then,
\mu
is
a stationary state for the chain induced by the generator

for

. By Lemma

,

,
as claimed.

Reducible Markov chains

In this subsection, we derive a formula for
I(\mu)
in the case where
the process
X_{t}
is reducible or the support of
\mu
a proper subset
of
V
.

Denote by
{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}
,
{\mathscr{A}}
a proper subset of
V
which
is not a singleton, the generator of the Markov chain
X_{t}
reflected
at
{\mathscr{A}}
. This is the
{\mathscr{A}}
-valued Markov chain which jumps from

x\in{\mathscr{A}}
to
y\in{\mathscr{A}}
at rate
R(x,y)
. Its generator reads as

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}[\,({\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}})\,f\,]\,(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{A}}}R(x,y)\,[\,f(y)-f(x)\,]\;,\quad x\in{\mathscr{A}}\;.

(A.10)

Clearly, this chain may be reducible even if the original one is
irreducible.

Fix a probability measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
, and denote by
V_{\mu}

its support,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V_{\mu}=\{x\in V:\mu(x)>0\}
, and by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}X^{\mu}_{t}
the Markov chain reflected at
V_{\mu}
. Mind that
we do not assume
V_{\mu}
to be a proper subset of
V
.

The formula for
I(\mu)
relies on the construction of a directed
graph without directed loops. Denote by by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{Q}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{Q}}_{\ell}
the equivalence classes of
the chain
X^{\mu}_{t}
. These classes form the set of vertices of the
directed graph. Draw a directed arrow from
{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
to
{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}
if
there exists
x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
and
y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}
such that

R(x,y)>0
. Denote the set of directed edges by
{\mathbb{A}}
and the graph
by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbf{Q}},{\mathbb{A}})
, where
{\mathbf{Q}}
is the set

\{{\mathscr{Q}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{Q}}_{\ell}\}
of vertices.

A path in the graph
{\mathbb{G}}
is a sequence vertices

({\mathscr{Q}}_{a_{j}}:0\leq j\leq m)
, such that there is a directed arrow
from
{\mathscr{Q}}_{a_{j}}
to
{\mathscr{Q}}_{a_{j+1}}
for
0\leq j<m
. This directed
graph has no directed loops because the existence of a directed loop
would contradict the definition of the sets
{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
as equivalent
classes. (Mind that undirected loops might exist).

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{C}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{C}}_{p}
be the closed irreducible
classes and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{T}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{T}}_{q}
be the transient
ones, so that
p+q=\ell
. Since the sets
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}
are closed
irreducible classes, these sets are not the tail of a directed edge in
the graph. On the other hand, as the elements of
{\mathscr{T}}_{i}
are
transient for the chain
X^{\mu}_{t}
, there is a path

({\mathscr{T}}_{i}={\mathscr{T}}_{a_{0}},\dots,{\mathscr{T}}_{a_{m}-1},{\mathscr{C}}_{j})
from
{\mathscr{T}}_{i}

to some irreducible class
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}
.

Fix a transient class
{\mathscr{T}}_{i}
. Denote by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{i})
the length of the longest path from

{\mathscr{T}}_{i}
to a closed irreducible class. The function
{\mathbf{D}}
is well
defined because (a) the set of vertices is finite, (b) there is at
least a path, (c) there are no directed loops in the graph.

Fix
a
,
b
such that there is a directed arrow from
{\mathscr{T}}_{a}
to

{\mathscr{T}}_{b}
. Then,

{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{a})\;\geq\;{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{b})\;+\;1\;.

(A.11)

Indeed, it is enough to consider the longest path from
{\mathscr{T}}_{b}
to
the irreducible classes.
{\mathscr{T}}_{a}
does not belong to the path because
there are no directed loops. By adding
{\mathscr{T}}_{a}
at the beginning of
the path from
{\mathscr{T}}_{b}
to the irreducible classes, we obtain a path
from
{\mathscr{T}}_{a}
to the irreducible classes of length

{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{b})+1
, proving (
A.11
).

Setting
{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{C}}_{j})=0
for all
1\leq j\leq p
, we may extend
(
A.11
) to the closed irreducible classes. Fix
a
,
b
such that
there is a directed arrow from
{\mathscr{T}}_{a}
to
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
. Then,

{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{a})\;\geq\;{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})\;+\;1

(A.12)

because
{\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{T}}_{a})\geq 1
. Finally, we may lift the function

{\mathbf{D}}
to
V_{\mu}
by setting
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{D}}(x)={\mathbf{D}}({\mathscr{Q}}_{a})

for all
x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
.

Recall from (
A.10
) that we represent by
{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}
the
generator of the process
X_{t}
reflected at
{\mathscr{A}}
. Assume that the
chain induced by the generator
{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}
is
irreducible. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}}\colon{\mathscr{P}}({\mathscr{A}})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

the functional given by

I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu)\;:=\;\sup_{H}\,-\,\int_{{\mathscr{A}}}e^{-H}\,\big[\,(\,{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}\,)\,e^{H}\,\big]\,d\mu\;,

where the supremum is carried over all functions
H\colon{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Denote by
{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
,
1\leq a\leq m
the equivalent classes of the chain

X^{\mu}_{t}
with at least two elements. Note that
\mu({\mathscr{D}}_{a})>0
for
all
a
and that
m\leq\ell
. Let
\mu_{{\mathscr{A}}}
,
{\mathscr{A}}\subset V

such that
\mu({\mathscr{A}})>0
, be the measure
\mu
conditioned to
{\mathscr{A}}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mu_{{\mathscr{A}}}(x)}\;:=\;\frac{\mu(x)}{\mu({\mathscr{A}})}\;,\quad x\,\in\,{\mathscr{A}}\;.

(A.13)

Let
K\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
the functional given by

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}K(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu({\mathscr{D}}_{a})\,I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}})\;+\;\sum_{x\in V_{\mu}}\sum_{y\not\in V_{\mu}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)

(A.14)

\displaystyle+\;\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;.

In this formula, since closed irreducible equivalent classes are not
the tail of any directed edge, we may restrict the sum over
a
to
transient equivalent classes. Moreover, if
R(x,y)>0
for some

x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
,
y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}
, then
R(z,w)=0
for all
z\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}
,

w\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
. Hence, in the last sum, each pair
(a,b)
is counted
only once.

In view of Lemma
A.3
, we may rewrite (
A.14
) as

K(\mu)\,=\,-\,\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\setminus\{x\}}\mu(x)\,R_{H_{a}}(x,y)\;+\;\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V\setminus\{x\}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;,

(A.15)

where
H_{a}:{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the function (unique up to an
additive constant) which turns the measure
\mu
conditioned to

{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
stationary for the chain induced by

{\mathfrak{M}}_{H_{a}}{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\,{\mathscr{L}}
(the generator

{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\,{\mathscr{L}}
tilted by
H_{a}
).

Lemma A.7
.

For all
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)

I(\mu)\,=\,K(\mu)\;.

Proof.

We first prove that
I(\mu)\leq K(\mu)
. Fix
H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. By definition of
V_{\mu}
,

\int_{V}e^{-H}{\mathscr{L}}e^{H}\;d\mu\;=\;\sum_{x\in V_{\mu},y\in V}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big[\,e^{H(y)-H(x)}-1\,\big]\;.

The right-hand side can be rewritten as

\sum_{x\in V_{\mu},y\not\in V_{\mu}}c_{H}(x,y)\;+\;\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a},y\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}c_{H}(x,y)\;+\;\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}}c_{H}(x,y)\;,

(A.16)

where
c_{H}(x,y)=\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\,[\,\exp\{H(y)-H(x)\}-1\,]
.

As
c_{H}(x,y)\geq-\,\mu(x)\,R(x,y)
, the sum of the first and third
terms of the previous displayed equation are bounded below by

-\,\sum_{x\in V_{\mu},y\not\in V_{\mu}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;-\;\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;.

The second term of that formula is bounded below by

\sum_{a=1}^{m}\inf_{G}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a},y\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}c_{G}(x,y)\;=\;-\,\sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu({\mathscr{D}}_{a})\,I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}})\;.

Up to this point we proved that

\int_{V}e^{-H}{\mathscr{L}}e^{H}\;d\mu\;\geq\;-\,K(\mu)

for all
H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Multiplying by
-1
and optimising over
H

yields that
I(\mu)\leq K(\mu)
.

We turn to the converse inequality. For each set
{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
the Markov
chain induced by the generator
{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}}
is irreducible and

\mu(x)>0
for all
x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
. Hence, by Lemma
A.3
, there
exists a function
G_{a}\colon{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\to{\mathbb{R}}
which solves the
variational problem

I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}})\;=\;\sup_{H}\,-\,\int_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}e^{-H}\,({\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}})\,e^{H}\;d\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\;=\;-\,\int_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}e^{-G_{a}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}})\,e^{G_{a}}\;d\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\;,

(A.17)

where the supremum is carried over all functions

H\colon{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Recall the definition of the function
{\mathbf{D}}\colon V_{\mu}\to{\mathbb{R}}

introduced above (
A.11
). Define the sequence of functions

H_{n}\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

H_{n}(x)\;=\;\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\,n&x\not\in V_{\mu}\;,\\
\displaystyle G_{a}(x)\;+\;n\,{\mathbf{D}}(x)&x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\;,\\
\displaystyle n\,{\mathbf{D}}(x)&x\in V_{\mu}\setminus\cup_{1\leq a\leq m}{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\;.\end{cases}

(A.18)

By definition of the functional
I
and since
V_{\mu}
stands for the
support of
\mu
,

I(\mu)\;\geq\;-\,\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{V_{\mu}}e^{-H_{n}}{\mathscr{L}}e^{H_{n}}\;d\mu\;.

For a fixed
n
the previous integral is equal to the sum in
(
A.16
) with
H_{n}
replacing
H
. By definition of
H_{n}
, as

n\to\infty
, the first term in (
A.16
) converges to

-\,\sum_{x\in V_{\mu},y\not\in V_{\mu}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;.

Since
{\mathbf{D}}(x)={\mathbf{D}}(y)
for elements
x
,
y
in the same
equivalent class
{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
, by (
A.17
), for every
n\geq 1
, the
second term in (
A.16
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a},y\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}c_{G_{a}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu({\mathscr{D}}_{a})\,\int_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}e^{-G_{a}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}})\,e^{G_{a}}\;d\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}

\displaystyle=\;-\,\sum_{a=1}^{m}\mu({\mathscr{D}}_{a})\,I_{{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu_{{\mathscr{D}}_{a}})\;.

Finally, to estimate the third term in (
A.16
), fix
x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}
,

y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}
such that
R(x,y)>0
. By (
A.11
), (
A.12
),

{\mathbf{D}}(x)\geq{\mathbf{D}}(y)+1
. Thus,
H_{n}(y)-H_{n}(x)\leq-n+C_{0}
for
some finite constant
C_{0}
independent of
n
, and, as
n\to\infty
,
the second term in (
A.16
) converges to

-\,\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\sum_{b\neq a}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{Q}}_{b}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)

Collecting all previous estimates yields that

I(\mu)\;\geq\;-\,\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{V}e^{-H_{n}}{\mathscr{L}}e^{H_{n}}\;d\mu\;=\;K(\mu)\;,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

A measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
is a stationary state of the Markov chain

X_{t}
if and only if
I(\mu)=0
.

Proof.

Assume that
I(\mu)=0
. Then, by Lemma
A.7
, all terms on the
right-hand side of (
A.14
) vanish. As the second and third terms
vanish the support of
\mu
consists of the union of closed
irreducible sets of the Markov chain. Since the first term vanishes,
by Corollary
A.6
,
\mu
restricted to these irreducible classes
is a stationary state. Hence,
\mu
is a convex combination of the
stationary states, and, hence, a stationary state.

Suppose that
\mu
is a stationary state. Then, its support is the
union of closed irreducible classes. Therefore, the second and third
terms on the right-hand side of (
A.14
) vanish. Fix a closed
irreducible class of the chain contained in the support of
\mu
. The
restriction of
\mu
to this set is strictly positive. Hence, by
Corollary
A.6
, the first term on the right-hand side of
(
A.14
) also vanishes. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Degenerate generators

In this subsection, we consider generators whose holding rates might
vanish. Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V_{0}=\{x:\lambda(x)>0\}
and keep in mind
that
V_{0}
may be a proper subset of
V
.

Denote by
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
restricted to
V_{0}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathscr{L}}_{0}f)(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\in V_{0}}R(x,y)\,\{\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\}\;,\quad f\colon V_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}\;.

Fix a measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
. Clearly, if
\mu(V_{0})=0
, then for
all
H\colon V\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
J_{H}(\mu)=0
and
I(\mu)=0
. The next lemma
covers the case where
\mu(V_{0})>0
. To stress the dependence of the
functional
J_{H}
introduced at (
A.2
) on the generator
{\mathscr{L}}
,
denote it below by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}J_{{\mathscr{L}},H}
. For a function

G\colon V_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, let
J_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0},G}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V_{0})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the functional given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}J_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0},G}(\nu)}\;:=\;-\,\int_{V_{0}}e^{-G}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}e^{G}\,d\nu\;.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}I_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0}}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V_{0})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the
functional defined by (
A.3
) with
J_{H}
replaced by

J_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0},G}
and where the supremum is carried over all functions

G\colon V_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma A.9
.

For all measures
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
such that
\mu(V_{0})>0
,

J_{{\mathscr{L}},H}(\mu)\;=\;\mu(V_{0})\,\Big\{\,J_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0},H_{V_{0}}}(\mu_{V_{0}})\,-\,\sum_{x\in V_{0}}\sum_{y\in V\setminus V_{0}}\mu_{V_{0}}(x)\,R(x,y)\,\big[e^{H(y)-H(x)}-1\,\big]\,\Big\}\;,

where
H_{V_{0}}\colon V_{0}\to{\mathbb{R}}
stands for the restriction of
H

to
V_{0}
:
H_{V_{0}}(x)=H(x)
,
x\in V_{0}
. In particular,

I_{{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu)\;=\;\mu(V_{0})\,I_{{\mathscr{L}}_{0}}(\mu_{V_{0}})\;+\;\sum_{x\in V_{0}}\sum_{y\in V\setminus V_{0}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;.

Proof.

The first assertion of the lemma is a simple identity. The proof of
the second one is similar to the one of Lemma
A.7
. We argue as
in this lemma to show that
I_{{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu)
is bounded by the
right-hand side of the identity. The converse inequality is obtained
by observing that to optimize
J_{{\mathscr{L}},H}(\mu)
it is convenient to
set
H(y)=-\infty
for
y\in V\setminus V_{0}
. More precisely, one
proceeds just as in the proof of Lemma
A.7
to obtain an optimal
sequence
H_{n}
defined in
V_{0}
and then extend it to
V\setminus V_{0}

in such a way that
H_{n}(y)-H_{n}(x)\to-\infty
for all

y\in V\setminus V_{0}
,
x\in V_{0}
.
∎

The previous lemma allows us to restrict our attention to non-singular
generators. This is the content of the next result.

Corollary A.10
.

For all measures
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
such that
\mu(V_{0})>0
,

I_{{\mathscr{L}}}(\mu)\;=\;-\,\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}}\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{D}}_{a}\setminus\{x\}}\mu(x)\,R_{H_{a}}(x,y)\;+\;\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V\setminus\{x\}}\mu(x)\,R(x,y)\;,

where
{\mathscr{D}}_{a}
,
1\leq a\leq m

R_{v,{\mathscr{A}}}(x,y)\;=\;R_{v}(x,y)\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{v}(x_{0})}\,R_{v}(x,x_{0})\,R_{v}(x_{0},y)\;,

where, recall,
\lambda_{v}(x_{0})
stands for the holding time at
x_{0}

associated to the rates
R_{v}(x,y)
. Since
v
is harmonic at
x_{0}
, by
(
C.9
) and the definition of
R_{v}
, the previous expression is
equal to

\frac{v(y)}{v(x)}\,\Big\{R(x,y)\;+\;\frac{1}{\lambda(x_{0})}\,R(x,x_{0})\,R(x_{0},y)\,\Big\}\;.

Since
v
and
u
coincide on
{\mathscr{A}}
, we may replace
v(x)
,
v(y)

by
u(x)
,
u(y)
, respectively. Representing by
R_{{\mathscr{A}}}(z,w)
the
jump rates associated to the generator
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}}{\mathscr{L}}
, by
(
C.2
) once more, the previous expression is equal to

\frac{u(y)}{u(x)}\,R_{{\mathscr{A}}}(x,y)\;,

which proves the lemma in the case where
V\setminus{\mathscr{A}}
is a
singleton.

We extend the result to arbitrary sets
{\mathscr{A}}
. Fix a set
{\mathscr{A}}
, and
recall the notation introduced above Corollary
C.3
. Fix

1\leq j\leq p
. By Corollary
C.3
,
v_{k-1}
is the

({\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k-1}}\,{\mathscr{L}})
-harmonic extension of
v_{k}
. Thus, by
the first part of the proof applied to the generator

{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k-1}}{\mathscr{L}}
,

{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}\,{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{k-1}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k-1}}{\mathscr{L}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{k}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k-1}}\,{\mathscr{L}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{k}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}\,{\mathscr{L}}

(C.10)

because
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k-1}}\,{\mathscr{L}}={\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{k}}\,{\mathscr{L}}
.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}=V
,
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{0}}\,{\mathscr{L}}={\mathscr{L}}
, and (
C.10
)
for
k=1
states that

{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{1}}\,{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{0}}\,{\mathscr{L}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{1}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{1}}\,{\mathscr{L}}\;.

Applying
{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}
on both sides of this identity and then
(
C.10
) yields that

{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\,{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{0}}\,{\mathscr{L}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{M}}_{v_{2}}\,{\mathfrak{T}}_{{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\,{\mathscr{L}}\;.

Iterating this procedure completes the proof of the lemma as
v_{0}=v
,

v_{p}=u
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{0}=V
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{p}={\mathscr{A}}
.
∎

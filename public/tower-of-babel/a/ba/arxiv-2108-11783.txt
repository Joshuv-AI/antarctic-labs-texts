Non-unitary neutrino mixing in the NO ν \nu A near detector data Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ushak Rahaman, Soebur Razzaque

Abstract
The ν μ → ν e \nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e} oscillation probability over short baseline ( ≲ 1 \lesssim 1 km) would be negligible in case the mixing matrix for three active neutrinos is unitary. However, in case of non-unitary mixing of three neutrinos, this probability would be non-negligible due to the so-called “zero distance” effect. Hence, the near detector of the accelerator experiments such as NO ν \nu A can provide strong constraints on the parameters of the non-unitary mixing with very large statistics. By analyzing the NO ν \nu A near detector data we find that the non-unitary mixing does not improve fits to the ν e \nu_{e} or ν μ \nu_{\mu} events over the standard unitary mixing. This leads to constraints on the non-unitary parameters: α 00 > 0.911 \alpha_{00}>0.911 , | α 10 | < 0.020 |\alpha_{10}|<0.020 and α 11 > 0.952 \alpha_{11}>0.952 at 90% C.L. A combined analysis with the near and far detector data does not change these constraints significantly.



I
Introduction

The latest results from the NO
\nu
A
Himmel 2020
;
Acero et al. 2021
and T2K
Dunne 2020
show that there exists a tension between the two experiments. Their best-fit points in the
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}-\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
plane, with unitary mixing hypothesis, are far apart from each other and without any overlap between their respective
1\,\sigma
confidence regions.
Moreover, while separate analyses of the NO
\nu
A and T2K data prefers normal hierarchy (NH), the combined analysis prefers inverted hierarchy (IH) over NH
Kelly et al. 2021
. Several papers have tried to resolve this tension with the help of beyond the standard model (BSM) physics. Ref.
Chatterjee and Palazzo 2021
;
Denton et al. 2021
considered neutral current non-standard interaction during propagation, while Ref.
Rahaman 2021

considered CP-violating Lorentz invariance violation during propagation. However, the new physics signatures are not stronger than
1\,\sigma
significance in those analyses and no other neutrino oscillation experiments have observed those signatures yet. Ref.
Miranda et al. 2021
instead considered non-unitary
3\times 3
mixing as a solution to the tension between the NO
\nu
A and T2K data. Non-unitary mixing implies presence of extra neutrino generations, which is in accordance with the LSND
Aguilar-Arevalo et al. 2001
and MiniBooNe
Aguilar-Arevalo
et al. 2018
results. However, a recent analysis
Forero et al. 2021
shows that data from the short baseline reactor neutrino experiments
strongly disfavors non-unitary mixing.
Therefore, further analyses of more available data are needed to search for signatures of non-unitary mixing
in long baseline neutrino oscillation experiments.
Here, we analyze the NO
\nu
A near detector (ND) data with non-unitary neutrino mixing hypothesis for the first time.

The accelerator neutrino energy is in the
1-10
GeV range. Since the neutrino oscillation probability depends on
{\Delta_{31}}L/E
, where
{\Delta_{31}}=m_{3}^{2}-m_{1}^{2}
is the mass-squared-difference between the neutrinos of masses
m_{3}
and
m_{1}
,
L
is the length of the baseline and
E
is the neutrino energy. The oscillation probability for the standard unitary mixing, driven by
{\Delta_{31}}
at this energy range, is negligible at
L\sim 1
km. This is not true, however, in the case of non-unitary mixing. The non-unitary
3\times 3
mixing matrix is defined as
Escrihuela et al. 2015

N=N_{\rm NP}U_{\rm PMNS}=\left[{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha_{00}&0&0\\
\alpha_{10}&\alpha_{11}&0\\
\alpha_{20}&\alpha_{21}&\alpha_{22}\end{array}}\right]U_{\rm PMNS}\,,

(1)

where
U_{\rm PMNS}
is the unitary PMNS mixing matrix. In the
N_{\rm NP}
matrix, the diagonal term(s) must deviate from unity and/or the off-diagonal term(s) deviate from zero to allow for the non-unitarity effect. The
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
oscillation probability at
L=0
in this case is given by
Escrihuela et al. 2015

P_{\mu e}^{\rm NU}(L=0)=\alpha_{00}|\alpha_{10}|^{2}

(2)

Therefore, in the case of non-unitary mixing, it is possible to observe neutrino oscillation even over a very short distance, which is called the “zero distance” effect. This effect can be tested with data from the short baseline neutrino oscillation experiments as well as the near detector of the long baseline experiments like NO
\nu
A and T2K.
Although the analysis in Ref.
Forero et al. 2021
shows that the short baseline neutrino experiments like NOMAD
Astier et al. 2003
, NuTeV
Avvakumov et al. 2002
strongly disfavors non-unitary mixing, the reactor neutrino fluxes have large uncertainties
Dentler et al. 2017
. Accelerator neutrino fluxes are better known. In this paper, we have looked for the signal of non-unitary mixing in the NO
\nu
A ND data. Besides, we do a combined analysis of the NO
\nu
A ND and far detector (FD) data to test the two mixing schemes, which is unique for the same experiment. A similar analysis with the T2K near and far detectors would be equally interesting, however, T2K ND data is not publicly available
P. Dunne 2021
.

The NO
\nu
A ND is a 290 ton totally active scintillator detector, placed at 100 m underground (to minimize background fluxes) and 1.9 mrad off-axis at approximately 1 km away from the source at the Fermilab
Acero et al. 2018
. In the case of unitary mixing, the ND will not observe any
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
oscillation at such a short distance and the observed electron events will come from the contamination in the
\nu_{\mu}
beam.
However, as explained in equation (
2
), there will be an excess of electron events due to the
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
conversion in case of non-unitary mixing.
Effects of non-unitary mixing at the ND will be sensitive to the parameters
\alpha_{00}
and
|\alpha_{10}|
, and also on
\alpha_{11}
because of the condition
|\alpha_{10}|\leq\sqrt{(1-\alpha_{00}^{2})(1-\alpha_{11}^{2})}

Antusch and Fischer 2014
;
Escrihuela et al. 2017
. There is essentially no dependence on the phase
\phi_{10}
associated with
|\alpha_{10}|
in the ND data.
For our analyses we have used
1.10\times 10^{21}
(
1.18\times 10^{21}
) protons on target (POTs) data in the neutrino (antineutrino) mode for the ND, and
1.36\times 10^{21}
(
12.5\times 10^{20}
) POTs data for the FD
Himmel 2020
;
Acero et al. 2021
.

In this paper, we have discussed the analysis in Section
II
, and presented the result in Section
III
. The conclusion has been drawn in Section
IV
. We show the oscillation probabilities and fits to the event distributions in the Appendix.

II
Analysis of NO
\nu
A data

;
Huber et al. 2007

\nu
A Collaboration

Esteban et al. 2019
. We have also varied

2.32\times 10^{-3}\,{\rm eV}^{2}
with
3\%
uncertainty
Nichol 2012
, where
\Delta_{\mu\mu}
is related to
{\Delta_{31}}
by the following relation
Nunokawa et al. 2005

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}{\Delta_{31}}+\cos^{2}{\theta_{12}}\Delta_{32}

(3)

\displaystyle+\cos\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}\sin 2{\theta_{12}}\sin{\theta_{13}}\tan{\theta_{12}}{\Delta_{21}}.

We have varied the CP-violating phase
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
in its complete range
[-180^{\circ}:180^{\circ}]
. For the non-unitary parameters, we have varied
\alpha_{00}
and
\alpha_{11}

We have implemented automatic bin-based energy smearing for generated theoretical events as described in the GLoBES manual
Huber et al. 2005
;
Huber et al. 2007
.
For this purpose, we have used a Gaussian smearing function for the true neutrino energy
E

R^{c}(E,E^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(E-E^{\prime})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}(E)}},

(4)

where

is the reconstructed energy. The energy resolution function is given by

\sigma(E)=\alpha E+\beta\sqrt{E}+\gamma.

(5)

For the NO
\nu
A FD, we have used
\alpha=0.09\,(0.08)
and
\beta=\gamma=0
for
\nu_{\mu}
(
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
) charge current (CC) events. For
\nu_{e}
(

, for both the muon and electron events as well as for both neutrinos and anti-neutrinos. For the FD we have used

. We calculated the systematic uncertainties at the ND in the following method. We assumed the normalization and energy calibration systematic uncertainties at ND to be
0.085+s_{1}
and
0.05+s_{2}
respectively for both the
e
-like and the
\mu
-like events. Then we analyzed both ND and FD data together with the standard 3 flavour oscillation hypothesis while both

, and

being varied in the range

\sin^{2}{\theta_{13}}
,

fixed at their best-fit values for NH taken from the analysis of NO

Himmel 2020

.
\sin^{2}{\theta_{12}}
, and
{\Delta_{21}}

Nufit 2019
. We calculated the
\chi^{2}
between theory and experiment and found out the minimum

occurs at

38.5\%

We have calculated
\chi^{2}

Miranda et al. 2021
. Before proceeding to analyze data with non-unitary mixing, we have analyzed data with the standard mixing hypothesis and found that the NO

;
Rahaman 2021
we presented results from analyzing the FD data with the standard unitary mixing. We matched results of the standard analyses published by the NO

III
Results and discussion

After fitting the NO
\nu
A ND data with both the standard unitary and non-unitary mixing schemes, we found that the best-fit point occurs at
\alpha_{00}=0.998
,
|\alpha_{10}|=0.002
, and
\alpha_{11}=0.997
. In Fig.
1
we show the
\Delta\chi^{2}
values for the non-unitary parameters. The standard unitary oscillation gives as good fit as the non-unitary mixing to the data with
\Delta\chi^{2}=0.09
and with
4
degrees of freedom (d.o.f.) fewer. Therefore, it can be stated that the NO
\nu
A ND data do not have any signature of non-unitary mixing. As mentioned earlier, the ND data from NO
\nu
A do not have any sensitivity to the phase
\phi_{10}
, nor do they have any sensitivity to hierarchy. The
90\%
C.L. (
5\,\sigma
) limit on the non-unitary parameters from the ND data of NO
\nu
A are

\alpha_{00}>0.911~(0.804),\,|\alpha_{10}|<0.020~(0.035),\,\alpha_{11}>0.952~(0.866)

(6)

Fitting the combined data from the ND and FD, the best-fit points were found at
\alpha_{00}=0.991
,

, and
\alpha_{11}=0.978
. In this case also, the standard oscillation gives as good fit to the data as non-unitary mixing with
\Delta\chi^{2}=0.44

\nu
A does not show any signature of non-unitary mixing (see Fig.

) limit on the non-unitary parameters from the ND and FD data of NO

Although, as mentioned earlier, the ND data is not sensitive to the standard oscillation parameters, the combined ND and FD data is sensitive to those parameters. In Fig.

IV
Conclusion

The NO
\nu
A ND data does not show any signature of non-unitary mixing. At the best-fit point, the deviation from the unitary mixing is negligible. Although after combining the ND and FD data the deviation from the unitary mixing at the best-fit point is larger, the unitary mixing continues to give as good fit to the data as the non-unitary mixing. The limits obtained on the non-unitary parameters
\alpha_{00}
,
|\alpha_{10}|
, and
\alpha_{11}
are consistent with the limits obtained in Ref.
Forero et al. 2021
from analyzing short baseline neutrino data.
Addition of the FD data gives sensitivity to the standard oscillation parameters. However, since the ND data allows only a small deviation from the unitary mixing, the results in the
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}-\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
plane is almost similar to that obtained with the unitary mixing and with only the FD data.

The results obtained from the ND data of NO
\nu
A are in tension with the analysis of FD data alone as presented in Ref.
Miranda et al. 2021
. The FD data of NO
\nu
A prefers non-unitary mixing over unitary mixing at
1\,\sigma
C.L., and also the deviation from the unitarity at the best-fit point is quite large. Thus, it is important to fit the ND data of T2K as well as the future experiments like DUNE
Abi et al. 2018
and T2HK
Ishida 2013
to the non-unitary mixing scheme to investigate if similar trend persists in them as well.

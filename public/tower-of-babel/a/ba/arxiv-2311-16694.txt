On the singularities of quotients by 1-foliations
Quentin Posva

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the singularities of varieties obtained as infinitesimal quotients by
1
-foliations in positive characteristic. (1) We show that quotients by (log) canonical
1
-foliations preserve the (log) singularities of the MMP. (2) We prove that quotients by multiplicative derivations preserve many properties, amongst which most
F
-singularities. (3) We formulate a notion of families of
1
-foliations, and investigate the corresponding families of quotients.

1.
Introduction

On a normal variety
X
over a field of positive characteristic, a
1
-foliation is a saturated sub-sheaf of
T_{X}
that is closed under Lie brackets and
p
-th powers. A fruitful construction, when having a
1
-foliation at our disposal, is the associated infinitesimal quotient of the underlying variety. It has been used multiple times to construct surprising or pathological examples in positive characteristic: see for example

[
MR83
,
Kur81
,
Hir99a
,
Hir99b
,
Hir00
,
Lie08
,
RS22
]
. The goal of this paper is to study the singularities of such quotients.

Since any finite purely inseparable morphism between normal varieties can be decomposed into a sequence of infinitesimal quotients, some restrictions on the
1
-foliations are necessary if we want qualitative results about the singularities. It is well-known that, at least on regular varieties, quotients by so-called multiplicative derivations are particularly simple
[
Hir99b
]
(see
Section 4.1
). We give a mild generalization, in case the variety supporting the derivation is not necessarily regular. This applies in particular to quotient by
1
-foliations of rank
>1
which are formally generated, up to saturation, by a finite set of commuting multiplicative derivations (
1
-foliations with
at worst multiplicative singularities
in our terminology: see
Section 2.4
).

Theorem 1

(Iterated applications of
Theorem 4.1.3
)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety over a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a
1
-foliation on
X
with at worst multiplicative singularities. Then:

(a)

If
X
is Cohen–Macaulay, so is
X/\mathcal{F}
;

(b)

If
X
is
F
-pure (resp.
F
-rational,
F
-injective or
F
-regular), so is
X/\mathcal{F}
.

The idea is that any multiplicative derivation induces, after passing to an appropriate étale neighbourhood, an action of the infinitesimal group scheme
\mu_{p}
. Quotients by
\mu_{p}
-actions have the remarkable property that the inclusion of the sub-ring of invariants is split (see eg
[
Jan03
, I.2.11]
), and many cohomological properties are stable by taking a split sub-ring of equal dimension.

It is interesting to note that not every usual cohomological property descends to quotients by multiplicative derivations: for example, the Gorenstein property usually does not (see
Section 4.1
), while the more geometric-flavoured
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein property does (
Section 4.2
). A detailed discussion of descent of properties such as Gorenstein and Buchsbaum (for the quotient by a single, non-necessarily
p
-closed derivation that is still “multiplicative” in an appropriate sense) is given in
[
AA86
]
and
[
Ara87
]
.

Another perspective on singularities is given by the Minimal Model Program. One can define birational singularities of
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
1
-foliations just as birational singularities of varieties and pairs, as first noticed by McQuillan
[
McQ08
]
. A remarkable feature is that quotients by (log) canonical
1
-foliations can only
improve
the birational singularities of the underlying variety:

Theorem 2

(The punchline of
Theorem 4.2.5
)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety over a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
, and
\mathcal{F}
be a
1
-foliation on
X
.

(a)

If
\mathcal{F}
is canonical, then
X/\mathcal{F}
is terminal (resp. canonical, klt, log canonical) as soon as
X
is.

(b)

If
\mathcal{F}
is log canonical, then
X/\mathcal{F}
is klt (resp. log canonical) as soon as
X
is.

The proof is a direct comparison of pull-back formulas, leading to the simple equalities
()
‣
Section 4.2
. These equalities also show that any other implication (eg
X
is midly singular if both
X/\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}
are) is not possible to obtain in general: see
Section 4.2
on that matter. We apply
Theorem 2
to normalized
p
-cyclic covering to obtain Reid-type covering results (ie description of the MMP singularities of cyclic covers), see
Section 4.2
.

One drawback of
Theorem 2
is that it is difficult in general to decide whether a given
1
-foliation
\mathcal{F}
is log canonical, etc., along its singular locus.
Section 3.1
provides examples of log canonical
1
-foliations of corank
1
on singular varieties. For
1
-foliations of rank
1
on regular varieties there is a useful characterization of log canonicity due to McQuillan (
Section 3
), which turns out to be equivalent to having at worst multiplicative singularities (
Section 3
). Using a local computation from
[

]
, this leads to a complete characterization of log canonical
1
-foliations on regular surfaces:

Theorem 3

(
Section 2.5.2
,
Section 4.2
and
Theorem 4.3.3
)
.

Let
S
be a regular surface over a perfect field of characteristic

, and
\mathcal{F}
be a
1
-foliation of rank
1
on
S
. Then:

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is canonical if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is regular, if and only if
S/\mathcal{F}
is regular;

(b)

\mathcal{F}
is log canonical if and only if
S/\mathcal{F}
is
F
-regular, if and only if
S/\mathcal{F}
is
F
-pure.

(The conditions on
S/\mathcal{F}
can be equivalently be formulated for the normalization of

in the field
K(S/\mathcal{F})^{1/p}
.)

This shows that the log canonical condition on
1
-foliations is quite restrictive, at least in dimension two. In higher dimensions, little more is known apart from the following:

Corollary 1

(
Section 4.2
and
Section 3
)
.

Let
X
be a regular variety over a perfect field of positive characteristic, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-trivial
1
-foliation.

(a)

\mathcal{F}
cannot be terminal, and if it is canonical then it is regular outside a closed subset of codimension
\geq 3
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{F}
has rank
1
, then it is canonical if and only if it is regular.

(c)

If
\mathcal{F}
has rank
1
, then it is log canonical if and only if it has at worst multiplicative singularities.

Finally, we explore a notion of family of
1
-foliations. For technical reasons, we mostly restrict ourselves to the case of smooth fibrations
\mathcal{X}\to S
(see
Section 5.1
and
Section 5.1
). In this setting, a family of
1
-foliations is a coherent sub-module
\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow T_{\mathcal{X}/S}
with
S
-flat cokernel, whose restriction to every fiber is a
1
-foliation in the usual sense. The theory of the Quot scheme implies that, when we consider trivial underlying fibration
\mathcal{X}\to S
, universal families of
1
-foliations exist (
Section 5.1
). Then we consider whether the operation of taking fibers commute with the operation of taking quotients by the
1
-foliation. In general they do not, but we prove the following criterion:

Theorem 4

(
Section 5.2
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X}\to S,\ \mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow T_{\mathcal{X}/S})
be a family of
1
-foliations. Assume that
\mathcal{X}\to S
is smooth and
S
is regular. Let
\mathcal{Q}=T_{\mathcal{X}/S}/\mathcal{F}
. Then if
\mathcal{Q}_{s}
is locally free for some
s\in S
, it holds that
\mathcal{X}_{s}/\mathcal{F}_{s}=(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{F})_{s}
.

If
\mathcal{Q}_{s}
is just slightly less regular (for example not Cohen–Macaulay but
S_{\dim\mathcal{X}_{s}-1}
), then commutativity may or may not occur, see
Section 5.2
and
Section 5.2
. So a more refined criterion seems difficult to formulate in this generality. Nevertheless, a consequence of our definition of families of
1
-foliations is that the natural morphism
\varphi_{s}\colon\mathcal{X}_{s}/\mathcal{F}_{s}\to(\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{F})_{s}
is always an isomorphism in codimension one (
Section 5.2
), and this method easily produces
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein degenerations of regular schemes into non-
S_{2}
-ones.

2.2.

Let

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let

k

(that is:

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\subset A
with the property that

A=\bigoplus_{0\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n}<p}B\cdot a_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{i_{n}}

If

]

\Omega^{1}_{A/B}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}A\cdot d_{A/B}(a_{i}),

where
d_{A/B}\colon A\to\Omega^{1}_{A/B}
is the universal derivation relative to
B\to A
. By Kunz theorem
[
Sta
, 0EC0]
, if

has a
p

then

is regular and

\Omega^{1}_{A/A^{p}}=\Omega^{1}_{A/k}
will be a finite free

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular complete local

is a finite extension. Let
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
be a

-basis of

(a)

As

2.3.
Derivations

Let
R
be a ring and
A
be an
R
-algebra. A
derivation
of
A
over
R
is a
R
-linear map
D\colon A\to A
satisfying the Leibniz rule

D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a),\quad a,b\in A.

The set of those, denoted
\Der_{R}(A)
, is naturally an
A
-module. This module is endowed with a Lie bracket

[\bullet,\bullet]\colon\Der_{R}(A)\to\Der_{R}(A)\quad[D,D^{\prime}]=D\circ D^{\prime}-D^{\prime}\circ D.

While the composition of two derivations might not be a derivation, in case
R
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra the
p
-fold composition affords an
R
-linear map

\Der_{R}(A)\to\Der_{R}(A),\quad D\mapsto D^{[p]}=\underbrace{D\circ\dots\circ D}_{p\text{ times}}.

Recall Hochschild’s formula
[
Mat89
, Theorem 25.5]
: for
a\in A
and
D\in\Der_{R}(A)
we have

(2.3.0.a)

(aD)^{[p]}=a^{p}D^{[p]}+(aD)^{[p-1]}(a)D.

The
p
-th power of a sum of derivations is more complicated to describe: a formula of Jacobson
[
Jac37
, p.209]
shows that

\left(\sum_{i}D_{i}\right)^{[p]}-\left(\sum_{i}D_{i}^{[p]}\right)\ \text{is a linear combination of multi-fold commutators in the }D_{i}\text{'s}.

In particular, the naive expression
(D_{1}+D_{2})^{[p]}=D_{1}^{[p]}+D_{2}^{[p]}
holds if
[D_{1},D_{2}]=0
.

An alternative description of the module of derivation is given by the
A
-linear canonical isomorphism
\Hom_{A}(\Omega_{A/R}^{1},A)\cong\Der_{R}(A)
, obtained by pre-composing any
\varphi\colon\Omega_{A/R}^{1}\to A
by the universal
R
-linear derivation
d_{A/R}\colon A\to\Omega_{A/R}^{1}
.

Given a multiplicatively closed subset
S\subset A
, there is a canonical map
\Der_{R}(A)\to\Der_{R}(S^{-1}A)
given by the usual derivation rule for fractions. This is compatible with the localisation isomorphism
\Hom_{A}(\Omega^{1}_{A/R},A)\otimes S^{-1}A\cong\Hom_{S^{-1}A}(\Omega^{1}_{S^{-1}A/R},S^{-1}A)
. In particular the module of derivations sheafifies, and for any
R
-scheme
X
we obtain a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\Der_{R}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
which is the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-dual of
\Omega_{X/R}^{1}
. It is customary to write
\Der_{R}(\mathcal{O}_{X})=T_{X/R}
(
1
1

1

In terms of the
T^{i}
functors of Lichtenbaum and Schlessinger, we have
T_{X/R}=T^{0}_{X/R}
.
). The Lie bracket and
p

While the module of derivations commutes with localization, in general it does not commute with completion. Indeed, the module of Kähler differentials

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring essentially of finite type over a Noetherian ring

\Der_{R}(A)\otimes\widehat{A}\hookrightarrow\Der_{R}(\widehat{A})

whose image is the sub-
\widehat{A}
-module
\Der^{\text{cont}}_{R}(\widehat{A})
of continuous
R

\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion

Proof.

Since
\widehat{A}
is a flat

is a finitely presented

\Der_{R}(A)\otimes_{A}\widehat{A}\longrightarrow\Hom_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega_{A/R}^{1}\otimes_{A}\widehat{A},\widehat{A})

is an isomorphism
[
Bou61
, Chapter I, §2, n.10, Proposition 11]
. In the rest of the proof, we describe the target of this isomorphism. By the universal property of the inverse limit, it can be written as

\Hom_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega_{A/R}^{1}\otimes\widehat{A},\widehat{A})=\varprojlim\Hom_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega_{A/R}^{1}\otimes\widehat{A},A/\mathfrak{m}^{n}).

Let us describe the Hom-module into

\widehat{A}
-linear morphism
\Omega_{A/R}^{1}\otimes\widehat{A}\to A/\mathfrak{m}^{n}

\Omega^{1}_{A/R}\otimes\widehat{A}\cong\varprojlim\left(\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{A}/R}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{A}/R}\right).

Combining these two facts with
[
Gro64

\Hom_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega^{1}_{A/R}\otimes\widehat{A},A/\mathfrak{m}^{n})\cong\Hom^{\text{cont}}_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{A}/R},A/\mathfrak{m}^{n}).

Let us apply the inverse limit along

\Hom_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega^{1}_{A/R}\otimes\widehat{A},\widehat{A})\cong\Hom^{\text{cont}}_{\widehat{A}}(\Omega^{1}_{\widehat{A}/R},\widehat{A}).

The right-hand side is a sub-module of

2.3.1.

-closed, additive and multiplicative derivations

Let
R
be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra and
A
be an
R
-algebra. We say that
D\in\Der_{R}(A)
is
p
-closed
if there is
a\in A
such that
D^{[p]}=aD
. Hochschild’s formula
()
‣
Section 2.3
shows that any scaling of a
p
-closed derivation is still
p
-closed. Amongst
p
-closed derivations, we distinguish two special types as follows.

Definition 2.3.4
.

We say that
D
is
additive
if
D^{[p]}=0
. We say that
D
is
multiplicative
if
D^{[p]}=uD
for some unit
u\in A^{\times}
.

Examples 2.3.5
.

(Recall that
k
stands for a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
.)

(a)

The derivation
x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
on
k[x,y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
is additive for
i\neq 1
, and multiplicative for
i=1
.

(b)

Consider the derivation
\partial_{a,b}=ax\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+by\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
on
k[x,y]
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We have

\left(\partial_{a,b}\right)^{[p]}(x^{i}y^{j})=(ai+bj)^{p}x^{i}y^{j}.

The element
ai+bj
is to be understood as an element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, on which the Frobenius is trivial. Thus we see that
(\partial_{a,b})^{[p]}=\partial_{a,b}
. So
\partial_{a,b}
is
p
-closed and multiplicative.

(c)

Consider the derivation
D=x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
on
k[x,y]
. For
p=2
we have
D^{[2]}=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
which is not a scaling of
D
, so
D
is not
p
-closed. For
p\neq 2
, the coordinate change
x=u+v,y=u-v
gives
D=u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+v\frac{\partial}{\partial v}
which is
p
-closed and multiplicative.

Warning 2.3.6
.

The additive and multiplicative properties are usually not stable by scaling. This can be seen from Hochschild’s formula
()
‣
Section 2.3
.
Actually a scaling of an additive derivation can become multiplicative, and vice-versa, for instance
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
and
x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
on
k[x,x^{-1}]
.

There is a well-known correspondence between additive and multiplicative derivations, and actions of the infinitesimal group schemes
\alpha_{p}
and
\mu_{p}
.

Proposition 2.3.7
.

Lemma 2.3.10
.

Let
\alpha_{p}
(respectively
\mu_{p}
) acts on a
k
-scheme

by means of a derivation

, and the action is free outside the fixed locus.

Proof.

We may assume that

is affine. As
\alpha_{p}
and
\mu_{p}
have no non-trivial subgroup schemes, the action is free outside the fixed locus. The action of
\alpha_{p}
can be described as follows: given morphisms of affine schemes

, corresponding to ring maps

f^{*}\colon\left(A\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\Gamma(S^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}})\right)

and given
\lambda\in\alpha_{p}(S)=\{u\in\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})\mid u^{p}=0\}
, the morphism
\lambda\cdot f\colon S^{\prime}\to X
corresponds to the ring map

A\to\Gamma(S^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}),\quad a\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{f^{*}(D^{\circ i}(a))}{i!}\psi(\lambda^{i})

(with the convention that
0^{0}=1=0!
and

). Similarly, if we have instead an
\mu_{p}
-action, then given

as above and
\nu\in\mu_{p}(S)=\{u\in\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})\mid u^{p}=1\}
, the morphism

A\to\Gamma(S^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}),\quad a\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{f^{*}(D^{\circ i}(a))}{i!}\psi(\nu^{i}-1).

The fixed locus of the
\alpha_{p}
-action (resp. of the
\mu_{p}
-action) is the subscheme

with the following property (see eg
[

, 2.2.5]
):

for every

whose ideal is generated by the set

.
∎

2.5.1.

Let

Definition 2.5.1

The

of

It is easily seen that

Lemma 2.5.2
.

Let
A
and

be as above, and assume that
A
is integral. Then:

(a)

\Frac(A)^{D}=\Frac(A^{D}).

(b)

If
A
is

A^{D}
.

(c)

If
x,y\in A^{D}
is a regular sequence in
A
, then
x,y
is also a regular sequence in
A^{D}
.

Proof.

The derivation
D
extends to
\Frac(A)
following the usual rule for differentiating quotients. Clearly
\Frac(A^{D})\subseteq\Frac(A)^{D}
. Conversely, assume that
\frac{a}{b}\in\Frac(A)^{D}
. As
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{ab^{p-1}}{b^{p}}
we have

0=D\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=\frac{D(ab^{p-1})}{b^{p}}

and so
D(ab^{p-1})=0
. Therefore
\frac{ab^{p-1}}{b^{p}}\in\Frac(A^{D})
, showing the first point.

Assume that
A
is
F
-finite. Then
A
is a finite module over
A^{p}
, a fortiori over
A^{D}
: by Artin–Tate lemma
[
Sta
, 00IS]
it follows that

is finite over
A^{p}
. Since
A^{p}
is finite over
(A^{D})^{p}
, we obtain that
A^{D}
is
F
-finite.

Assume that
A
is normal, and suppose that

satisfies a monic polynomial equation with coefficients in

. Then

by normality of

, and

by assumption. So

, showing that
A^{D}
is normal.

Finally, assume that
x,y\in A
is a regular sequence.
Clearly

is not a zero-divisor in the subring
A^{D}
. Now assume that multiplication by

is not injective on

. Then we have an equality

with

and

. Considering this equality in

, we must have

for some

. But

hence

and so in fact

: contradiction. Hence

is a regular sequence on

.
∎

Recall that

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the volume comparison theorem related to σ 2 \sigma_{2} -curvature. In particular, we show that volume comparison theorem with respect to σ 2 \sigma_{2} -curvature holds for metrics close to strictly stable positive Einstein metrics. By applying similar techniques, we derive the local rigidity theorem for strictly stable Ricci flat manifolds with respect to σ 2 \sigma_{2} -curvature, which shows it admits no metric with positive σ 2 \sigma_{2} -curvature near strictly stable Ricci-flat metrics.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. The Ricci curvature tensor and scalar curvature are denoted by
\text{Ric}_{g}
and
\text{R}_{g}
respectively. The classic volume comparison theorem states that if

\text{Ric}_{g}\geq(n-1)g,

then

\text{V}_{M^{n}}(g)\leq\text{V}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})

where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the unit round sphere and
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is the canonical metric. Motivated by this volume comparison theorem, R. Schoen
[
17
]
proposed the following conjecture on volume comparison theorems involving scalar curvature on closed hyperbolic manifolds.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Suppose
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be any closed hyperbolic manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. Then for any metric
g
on
M^{n}
with

R_{g}\geq R_{\bar{g}},

then the following volume comparison

\text{V}_{M^{n}}(g)\geq\text{V}_{M^{n}}(\bar{g})

holds.

Schoen’s conjecture was proved confirmatively in 3-dimensional case due to the works of Hamilton
[
6
]
and Perelman
[
14
]
. For higher dimensional cases, Besson, Courtois and Gallot
[
3
]
verified it for metrics
C^{2}
-close to the canonical metric
\bar{g}
. After that, they also showed that the same result holds without assuming the metrics
C^{2}
-closeness if one replaces the assumption on scalar curvature by Ricci curvatures.

H.Bray
[
2
]
posed the following volume comparison conjecture related to Ricci curvature tensor and scalar curvature, and he gave affirmative proof for 3-dimensional case. And the conjecture is still open for
n\geq 4
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,1)
such that for any closed Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
satisfying

\displaystyle\text{R}_{g}\geq n(n-1),\ \ \ \text{Ric}_{g}\geq\varepsilon_{n}(n-1)g,

then the volume comparison

\displaystyle\text{V}_{M^{n}}(g)\leq\text{V}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})

holds, where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the unit round sphere and
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is the canonical metric.

If
g
is sufficiently closed to the stable Einstein metric
\bar{g}
, i.e.
||g-\bar{g}||_{C^{2}(M^{2},\bar{g})}<\varepsilon_{0}
for some small constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, then Yuan
[
18
]
proved the volume comparison theorem with respect to scalar curvature holds. And for the volume comparison of asymptotically hyperbolic manifold, please refer to
[
7
]
.

Before we state our results, we give the definition of the stable Einstein metric.

Definition 1.3
.

For
n\geq 3
, suppose
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is a closed Einstein manifold with

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\bar{g}}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}.

The Einstein metric
\bar{g}
is said to be strictly stable, if the Einstein operator
\Delta_{E}^{\bar{g}}=\Delta_{\bar{g}}+2Rm_{\bar{g}}
is a negative operator on
S^{TT}_{2,\bar{g}}(M)\setminus\{0\}
, where

\displaystyle S^{TT}_{2,\bar{g}}(M):=\{h\in S_{2}(M)|\delta_{\bar{g}}h=0,tr_{\bar{g}}h=0\}.

is the space of transverse-traceless symmetric
2
-tensor on
(M^{n},\bar{g})
. Moreover,
\bar{g}
is called strictly stable is the Einstein operator
\Delta_{\bar{g}}
is a strictly negative operator.

There are several different ways to define the stability of Einstein metric, and we choose the one with Einstein operator for our convenience. With this definition, Yuan proved the following volume comparison theorem with respect to scalar curvature.

Theorem 1.4
.

(
[
18
]
)
Suppose
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is a strictly stable Einstein manifold with

\displaystyle\text{Ric}_{\bar{g}}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}.

Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
for any metric
g
on
M^{n}
satisfying

R_{g}\geq R_{\bar{g}},\ \ \ ||g-\bar{g}||_{C^{2}(M^{2},\bar{g})}<\varepsilon_{0},

the following volume comparison hold:

(1)

if
\lambda>0
, then
\text{Vol}_{M}(g)\leq\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})
;

(2)

if
\lambda<0
, then
\text{Vol}_{M}(g)\geq\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})
,

with equality holds in either case if and only if
g
is isometric to
\bar{g}
.

Instead of scalar curvature, we investigate the
\sigma_{2}
-curvature tensor, a fourth order curvature tensor which has been intensively studied for decades in dimension four and higher dimensions. For a closed 4-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{4},g)
,
\sigma_{2}
-curvature is defined to be:

\sigma_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}|Ric_{g}|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}R_{g}^{2}.

It satisfies the Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula:

\int_{M^{4}}\bigg(\frac{1}{4}|W_{g}|^{2}+\sigma_{2}\bigg)dv_{g}=8\pi^{2}\chi(M),

where
W_{g}
is the Weyl curvature tensor for
(M^{4},g)
. In particular, if
(M^{4},g)
is locally conformally flat, i.e.
W_{g}=0
, it reduces to

\int_{M^{4}}\sigma_{2}dv_{g}=8\pi^{2}\chi(M).

Also, by the conformal invariance of Weyl curvature tensor,
\sigma_{2}
is also conformal invariant in four dimensional case.

In this article, we proved the following volume comparison theorem with respect to
\sigma_{2}
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M^{n},\bar{g})
be a strictly stable Einstein manifold with

\displaystyle Ric_{\bar{g}}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}

where
\lambda>0
is a constant. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
for any metric
g
on
M^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(g)\geq\sigma_{2}(\bar{g}),\ \ \ ||g-\bar{g}||_{C^{2}(M^{2},\bar{g})}<\varepsilon_{0},

the following volume comparison holds:

\displaystyle\text{Vol}_{M}(g)\leq\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g}),

with equality holds in either case if and only if
g
is isometric to
\bar{g}
.

Remark 1.6
.

The strictly stable condition is necessary, otherwise we could construct counterexamples.

Remark 1.7
.

Our theorem is slightly different from Yuan’s results. In our theorem, the Einstein manifolds must have positive constant scalar curvature.

Remark 1.8
.

In dimension
n=4
, we have a global volume comparison which is a direct consequence from Gauss-Bonnet-Chern formula. Suppose that
(M^{4},\bar{g})
is a closed locally conformally flat Riemannian manifold with positive constant
\sigma_{2}
-curvature, then for any metric
g
satisfies
\sigma_{2}(g)\geq\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})
pointwisely on
M
, we have

\displaystyle Vol_{M}(g)\leq Vol_{M}(\bar{g})-\frac{1}{4\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})}||W_{g}||_{L^{2}(M,g)}^{2}\leq Vol_{M}(\bar{g})

where equality holds if and only if
g
is also locally conformally flat.

Remark 1.9
.

The volume comparison for Ricci-flat manifolds can not be expected. This is easy to see by taking
g=c^{2}\bar{g}
for some non zero constant
c
. Clearly, in this case,
\sigma_{2}(g)=\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})=0
, but the volume
\text{Vol}_{M}(g)
can be either larger or smaller than
\text{Vol}_{M}(\bar{g})
depending on
c
is either greater or smaller than 1.

However in this case we hold the rigidity as follows.

Theorem 1.10
.

Suppose
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is a strictly stable Ricci-flat manifold, then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
for any metric
g
on
M^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(g)\geq 0,\ \ \ ||g-\bar{g}||_{C^{2}(M^{2},\bar{g})}<\varepsilon_{0},

then
g
is Ricci-flat.

Remark 1.11
.

Since
\sigma_{2}
-curvature vanishes on Ricci-flat metric, Theorem
1.10
states that there is no metric with positive
\sigma_{2}
-curvature near
\bar{g}
.

Remark 1.12
.

When we finished our work, we found the paper
[
1
]
also cover the volume comparison theorem with respect to
\sigma_{2}
-curvature with a different approach.

2

3

4

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Notations

Throughout this paper, we will always assume that
(M^{n},g)
to be an n dimensional closed Riemannian manifold with
(n\geq 3)
unless otherwise stated. Also, we list the curvature indices convention we used in this article.

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, We adopt the following convention for Riemann and Ricci curvature tensors

\displaystyle Rm(X,Y,Z,W)=\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle,

where

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.

Thus we define

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}

\displaystyle=Rm(\partial_{i},\partial_{j},\partial_{k},\partial_{l})=g_{ml}R_{ijk}^{m}

\displaystyle R_{jk}

\displaystyle=g^{il}R_{ijkl},

and denote the Schouten tensor as

\displaystyle S_{ij}=R_{ij}-\frac{R}{2(n-1)}g_{ij}.

Then the
\sigma_{k}
-curvatures are defined as the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomials

\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})=\sum\limits_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

where
\lambda_{i}(i=1,\cdots,n)
are the eigenvalues of the Schouten tensor
S_{g}
. Easy to see that

\sigma_{1}(g)=tr(S_{g})=\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}R,

thus
\sigma_{k}
-curvatures are often considered as a canonical way to generalize the scalar curvature. By simple calculations, we get

\sigma_{2}(g)=-\frac{1}{2}|Ric_{g}|^{2}+\frac{n}{8(n-1)}R^{2}_{g}.

We also denote the Weyl tensor as

\displaystyle W_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}-\frac{1}{n-2}(S_{il}g_{jk}+S_{jk}g_{il}-S_{ik}g_{jl}-S_{jl}g_{ik}).

In the case that
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is an Einstein manifold that satisfies
Ric_{\bar{g}}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}
, we hold the following:

\displaystyle S_{ij}=\frac{n-2}{2}\lambda\bar{g}_{ij},\quad\quad W_{ijkl}=R_{ijkl}-\lambda(\bar{g}_{il}\bar{g}_{jk}-\bar{g}_{ik}\bar{g}_{jl}),

\displaystyle R_{\bar{g}}=n(n-1)\lambda,\quad\quad\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{8}\lambda^{2}.

For Laplacian operator, we use the following convention:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}=g^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}.

For simplicity, we introduce following operators:

\displaystyle(h\times k)_{ij}=g^{kl}h_{ik}h_{jl},\quad\quad h\cdot k=tr(h\times k)=g^{ij}g^{kl}h_{ik}k_{jl},

and

\displaystyle(Rm\cdot h)_{jk}=R_{ijkl}h^{il},\quad\quad(W\cdot h)_{jk}=W_{ijkl}h^{il}.

Then the Einstein operator acting on symmetric 2-tensor
h\in S_{2}(M)
is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{E}h=\Delta h+2Rm\cdot h.

2.2.
Basic variational formulae

We list several formulae for linearization of curvature tensor which will show later sections.

The linearization of Ricci tensor is

\displaystyle(DRic)\cdot h=-\frac{1}{2}[\Delta_{E}h-Ric\times h-h\times Ric+\nabla^{2}(trh)+\nabla_{j}(\delta h)_{k}+\nabla_{k}(\delta h)_{j}],

and the linearization of scalar curvature is

\displaystyle(DR)\cdot h=-\Delta(trh)+\delta^{2}h-Ric\cdot h.

The first and second variations of the volume functional are

\displaystyle(D\text{Vol})\cdot h=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(trh)dv_{g},

and

\displaystyle(D^{2}\text{Vol})(h,h)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\Big((trh)^{2}-2|h|^{2}\Big)dv_{g}.

3.
The Key Functional and Corresponding Variations

In this section, we will present the variation formulae for functional
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
defined below. Throughout this section,
\bar{g}
will be an Einstein metric, that is

Ric_{\bar{g}}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}.

Suppose
g
is another metric near
\bar{g}
, then by theorem there is a diffeomorphism
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(M)
such that
h:=\phi^{*}g-\bar{g}
satisfies the TT-gauge condition

\delta_{\bar{g}}(\overset{\circ}{h})=\delta_{\bar{g}}(h-\frac{trh}{n}g)=0.

Later we denote
trh
as
u
, it is clear that we have the following

(\delta h)_{k}=-\nabla^{j}h_{jk}=-\frac{1}{n}\nabla_{k}u,\quad|h|^{2}=|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}u^{2},\quad|\nabla h|^{2}=|\nabla\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Suppose
(M^{n},\bar{g})
is a closed Einstein manifold, take the functional

\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g)=\text{Vol}(g)^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(g)dv_{\bar{g}}.

Since the volume form
dv_{\bar{g}}
is independent of
g
, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g)
is a scaling invariant with respect to metric
g
, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(c^{2}g)=c^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g)

for any non-zero constant
c
. Such volume form fixed functional has been studied by Fisher-Marsden
[
5
]
and Yuan
[
18
]
[
12
]
, where the functional was used to study rigidity theorem of scalar curvature and Q curvature and corresponding volume comparison theorems. The functional
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g)
we introduce here is designed for our proof of volume comparison and we will present its first and second variation formulae in this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

The Einstein metric
\bar{g}
is the critical point of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
.

Proof.

Let
g_{t}=\bar{g}+th
, where
h\in S_{2}(M)
,
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
for some small
\varepsilon>0
. Then the linearization of the functional
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
at
\bar{g}
is

\begin{split}D\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}\cdot h=&\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g_{t})\\
=&\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}D\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})\cdot hdv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{4}{n}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}-1}D\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\cdot h\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

Due to the linearization of Ricci tensor and the scalar curvature, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle(D\sigma_{2})\cdot h

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(|Ric|^{2})^{\prime}+\frac{n}{8(n-1)}(R^{2})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-(g^{ik})^{\prime}g^{jk}R_{ij}R_{kl}-g^{ik}g^{jl}(R^{\prime}_{ij})R_{kl}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}RR^{\prime}.

Notice that in the case
Ric=(n-1)\lambda g
, we obtain
R=n(n-1)\lambda
and

\displaystyle g^{ij}R_{ij}^{\prime}=-\frac{n-1}{n}\Delta u,\quad\quad R^{\prime}=(1-n)(\frac{1}{n}\Delta u+\lambda u),

thus

\displaystyle(D\sigma_{2})\cdot h=

\displaystyle(n-1)^{2}\lambda^{2}u-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n}\lambda\Delta u+\frac{n^{2}(1-n)\lambda}{4}(\frac{1}{n}\Delta u+\lambda u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4}\lambda^{2}u+c(n)\lambda\Delta u,

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{M}(D\sigma_{2})\cdot hdv_{\bar{g}}=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4}\lambda^{2}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}},

and

\displaystyle D\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}\cdot h

\displaystyle=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4}\lambda^{2}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{n}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}-1}\cdot\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{8}\lambda^{2}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\cdot\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=0.

Thus
\bar{g}
is the critical point of
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(g)
.
∎

In the following, we will calculate the second order variation formula of the functional
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
. With a standard method, we have

\begin{split}D^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(h,h)=&\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}D^{2}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})(h,h)dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{8}{n}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}-1}(D\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\cdot h)\int_{M}D\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})\cdot hdv_{\bar{g}}\\
&+\frac{4}{n}\Big[\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}-1}D^{2}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})(h,h)+\frac{4-n}{n}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}-2}(D\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\cdot h)^{2}\Big]\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

Note that since
\bar{g}
is assumed to be Einstein, we have the following results that can help us minimize the work. The first and second variation formulae reduce to:

\displaystyle D\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\cdot h=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}},

and

\displaystyle D^{2}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})(h,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}u^{2}-2|h|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}=\frac{n-2}{4n}\int_{M}u^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.

The integrals involved with
\sigma_{2}
and its first variation will be

\displaystyle\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{8}\text{Vol}(\bar{g}),

and

\displaystyle\int_{M}D\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})\cdot hdv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Lambda_{\bar{g}}(h)dv_{\bar{g}}=\int_{M}\Lambda^{*}(1)\cdot hdv_{\bar{g}}=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}}

And one can check that

\displaystyle(\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}})^{2}=\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\int_{M}\bar{u}^{2}dv_{\bar{g}},

here

\displaystyle\bar{u}=\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{-1}\int_{M}udv_{\bar{g}},

thus we have:

\begin{split}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{-\frac{4}{n}}D^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(h,h)=&\int_{M}D^{2}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})(h,h)dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{M}|\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\\
&+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{3}}{8n}\int_{M}u^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}(n+4)}{8n}\int_{M}\bar{u}^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

Now focus on the calculation of
D^{2}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})(h,h)
, note that in our case
R_{ij}=(n-1)\lambda g_{ij}
.

\begin{split}D^{2}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})(h,h)=&-\frac{1}{2}(|Ric|^{2})^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n}{8(n-1)}(R^{2})^{\prime\prime}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}(g^{ik}g^{jl}R_{ij}R_{kl})^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}(R\cdot R^{\prime})^{\prime}\\
=&-((g^{ik})^{\prime}g^{jl}R_{ij}R_{kl})^{\prime}-(g^{ik}g^{jl}(R_{ij})^{\prime}R_{kl})^{\prime}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}(R^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{4}R^{\prime\prime}\\
=&-(n-1)^{2}\lambda^{2}\bar{g}_{ik}(g^{ik})^{\prime\prime}-(n-1)^{2}\lambda^{2}\bar{g}_{ij}\bar{g}_{kl}(g^{ik})^{\prime}(g^{jl})^{\prime}-4(n-1)\lambda(g^{ik})^{\prime}(R_{ik})^{\prime}\\
&-\bar{g}^{ik}\bar{g}^{jl}(R_{ij})^{\prime}(R_{kl})^{\prime}-(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}^{ij}R^{\prime\prime}_{ij}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}(R^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\lambda R^{\prime\prime}\\
=&-3(n-1)^{2}\lambda^{2}|h|^{2}+4(n-1)\lambda h^{ij}R^{\prime}_{ij}-|Ric^{\prime}|^{2}-(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}^{ij}R^{\prime\prime}_{ij}\\
&+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}(R^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{4}\lambda R^{\prime\prime}\end{split}

Notice that

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}=(g^{ij})^{\prime\prime}R_{ij}+2(g^{ij})^{\prime}R^{\prime}_{ij}+\bar{g}^{ij}R^{\prime\prime}_{ij}=2(n-1)\lambda|h|^{2}-2h^{ij}R^{\prime}_{ij}+\bar{g}^{ij}R^{\prime\prime}_{ij},

thus

\begin{split}D^{2}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})(h,h)=&\frac{(n-1)(n^{2}-6n+6)}{2}\lambda^{2}|h|^{2}+\frac{-n^{2}+8n-8}{2}\lambda h^{ij}R^{\prime}_{ij}-|Ric^{\prime}|^{2}\\
&+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}\lambda\bar{g}^{ij}R^{\prime\prime}_{ij}+\frac{n}{4(n-1)}(R^{\prime})^{2}\end{split}

With above calculations, we can express the integrals in a compact way. We split the result in following porpositions.

Proposition 3.2
.

\int_{M}h^{ij}R^{\prime}_{ij}dv_{\bar{g}}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{n-1}{n^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.

Proof.

First, we denote

\Delta_{L}h_{jk}:=\Delta h_{jk}+2R_{ijkl}h^{il}-R_{jp}h^{p}_{k}-R_{pk}h^{p}_{j}=\Delta_{E}h_{jk}-R_{jp}h^{p}_{k}-R_{pk}h^{p}_{j}

\Delta_{L}
is the Lichnerowicz Laplacian. Recall that in our case
R_{ij}=(n-1)\lambda\bar{g}_{ij}
, thus
R_{ijkl}=W_{ijkl}+\lambda(\bar{g}_{il}\bar{g}_{jk}-\bar{g}_{ik}\bar{g}_{jl})
, we have

\Delta_{L}h_{jk}=\Delta h_{jk}+2W_{ijkl}h^{il}+2\lambda(tr_{\bar{g}}h)\bar{g}_{jk}-2n\lambda h_{jk}.

Thus

\begin{split}\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}&=\Delta\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}+2W_{ijkl}\overset{\circ}{h}^{il}-2n\lambda\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}\\
\Delta_{L}h_{jk}&=\Delta\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\bar{g}_{jk}+2W_{ijkl}\overset{\circ}{h}^{il}-2n\lambda\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}=\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\bar{g}_{jk}.\end{split}

From linearization of Ricci tensor we have

\begin{split}R^{\prime}_{jk}&=-\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{L}h_{jk}+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}trh+\nabla_{j}(\delta h)_{k}+\nabla_{k}(\delta h)_{j})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\bar{g}_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{n}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}u)\end{split}

Thus

\begin{split}\int_{M}h^{jk}R^{\prime}_{jk}dv_{\bar{g}}=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(\overset{\circ}{h}^{jk}+\frac{u}{n}g^{jk})(\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}_{jk}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\bar{g}_{jk}+\frac{n-2}{n}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}u)dv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{1}{2n}\int_{M}u\Delta udv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{n-2}{2n^{2}}\int_{M}u\Delta udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{n-1}{n^{2}}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\end{split}

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

\int_{M}|Ric^{\prime}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\Delta_{L}^{2}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\overset{\circ}{h}dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{n-1}{4n}\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4n^{2}}\lambda\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.

Proof.

First we show that
\int_{M}\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\nabla^{2}udv_{\bar{g}}=0
.

\begin{split}\int_{M}\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\nabla^{2}udv_{\bar{g}}=&\int_{M}(\Delta\overset{\circ}{h}+2W\cdot\overset{\circ}{h}-2n\lambda\overset{\circ}{h})\cdot\nabla^{2}udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&\int_{M}\Delta\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\nabla^{2}udv_{\bar{g}}+2\int_{M}W(\overset{\circ}{h},\nabla^{2}u)dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

here we use the fact that
\delta\overset{\circ}{h}=0
, on the other hand

\begin{split}\int_{M}\Delta\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\nabla^{2}udv_{\bar{g}}=&\int_{M}\nabla_{k}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}udv_{\bar{g}}=-\int_{M}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ij}\nabla_{j}udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&-\int_{M}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ij}+R_{ikp}^{k}\nabla^{p}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ij}+R_{ikp}^{i}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{pj}+R_{ikp}^{j}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ip})\nabla_{j}udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&\int_{M}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{i}_{j}\nabla^{k}\nabla^{j}u+\int_{M}R_{ikjp}\nabla^{k}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ip}\nabla^{j}udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&\int_{M}(\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\overset{\circ}{h}^{i}_{j}+R_{ikp}^{i}\overset{\circ}{h}^{p}_{j}-R_{ikj}^{p}\overset{\circ}{h}_{p}^{i})\nabla^{k}\nabla^{j}u-\int_{M}R_{ikjp}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ip}\nabla^{k}\nabla^{j}udv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&-2\int_{M}R_{ikjp}\overset{\circ}{h}^{ip}\nabla^{k}\nabla^{j}udv_{\bar{g}}=-2\int_{M}W(\overset{\circ}{h},\nabla^{2}u).\end{split}

here we use the fact
\nabla^{k}R_{ikjp}=0
from the second Bianchi identity.Now we have

\begin{split}\int_{M}|Ric^{\prime}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}=&\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}|\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}+\frac{\Delta u}{n}\bar{g}+\frac{n-2}{n}\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}|\Delta_{L}\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}(\Delta u)^{2}+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{2(n-2)}{n^{2}}(\Delta u)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\\
=&\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\Delta_{L}^{2}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\overset{\circ}{h}+\frac{3n-4}{n^{2}}(\Delta u)^{2}+\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{n^{2}}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.\end{split}

From Bochner formula we obtain

\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}=\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}-(n-1)\lambda\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{M}|Ric^{\prime}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\Delta_{L}^{2}\overset{\circ}{h}\cdot\overset{\circ}{h}dv_{\bar{g}}+\frac{n-1}{4n}\int_{M}(\Delta u)^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)^{2}}{4n^{2}}\lambda\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}.

∎

Proof.

Lemma 4.1

g
satisfies

\displaystyle||g-\bar{g}||_{C^{2}(M,\bar{g})}<\epsilon

there exists a diffeomorphism
\varphi
such that
\varphi^{*}g\in\mathcal{U}_{\bar{g}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{\bar{g}}
is defined in Proposition
4.5
.

Notice that
D\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}\cdot h=0
and
D^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(h,h)\leq 0
, thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}}(\varphi^{*}g)=\text{Vol}(\varphi^{*}g)^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(\varphi^{*}g)dv_{\bar{g}}\geq\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{\frac{4}{n}}\int_{M}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})dv_{\bar{g}}=\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}}(\bar{g}),

we hold that
\varphi^{*}g=c^{2}g
for some constant
c>0
according to Proposition
4.5
. Now assume that we have the reversed condition, which is
\text{Vol}(g)\geq\text{Vol}(\bar{g})
, it is obvious to see that

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(g)=c^{n}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\geq\text{Vol}(\bar{g})

which implies
c\geq 1
, but in other hands we hold

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(g)=c^{-4}\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})\geq\sigma_{2}(\bar{g})

which implies
c\leq 1
, thus
c=1
which is to say
g
is diffeomorphic to
\bar{g}
and we finish the proof.
∎

When
\lambda=0
, we do not have the volume comparison, while we hold that in this case
g
is conformal to
\bar{g}
, now we show the proof of Theorem
1.10
:

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Recall Proposition 3.6 and let
\lambda=0
we hold

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(\bar{g})^{-\frac{4}{n}}D^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}(h,h)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}|\Delta_{E}\overset{\circ}{h}|^{2}dv_{\bar{g}}\leq 0

Thus
D^{2}\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
is non-positive definite, since
\bar{g}
is a critical metric for
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}
, following the same method in the previous proof, there exists a diffeomorphism
\varphi
such that
\varphi^{*}g\in U_{\bar{g}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}}(\varphi^{*}g)\geq\mathcal{H}_{\bar{g}}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\bar{g}}}(\bar{g})=0.
This implies that
g=c^{2}\bar{g}
thus
g
is Ricci-flat.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The stability condition is necessary, otherwise we have counterexamples. If the metric
\bar{g}
is unstable, according to Proposition
3.6
,
\bar{g}
is nolonger a local maximum. According to
[
8
]
, a product of positive Einstein manifolds with identical Einstein constant is still Einstein but not stable, therefore, we can construct such product manifold as our counterexample. Let
(S^{2},g_{S^{2}})
be the unit sphere with canonical metric. Consider the canonical product manifold
(S^{2}\times S^{2}\times S^{2}\times S^{2},\bar{g})
, where
\bar{g}=g_{S^{2}}+g_{S^{2}}+g_{S^{2}}+g_{S^{2}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sigma(\bar{g})=-\frac{1}{2}|Ric|^{2}_{\bar{g}}+\frac{1}{7}R^{2}_{\bar{g}}=\frac{36}{7}.

If we choose

\displaystyle g_{t}=(1+4t^{2})^{-1}g_{S^{2}}+(1+4t^{2})^{-1}g_{S^{2}}+(1+3t)^{-1}g_{S^{2}}+(1-3t)^{-1}g_{S^{2}}

then we have

\begin{split}\sigma(g_{t})=&-\frac{1}{2}[2(1+4t^{2})^{2}+2(1+4t^{2})^{2}+2(1+3t)^{2}+2(1-3t)^{2}]\\
&+\frac{1}{7}[2(1+4t^{2})+2(1+4t^{2})+2(1+3t)+2(1-3t)]^{2}\\
=&-[2(1+8t^{2}+16t^{4})+2(1+9t^{2})]+\frac{4}{7}(4+8t^{2})^{2}\\
=&-(4+34t^{2}+32t^{4})+\frac{64}{7}(1+4t^{2}+4t^{4})\\
=&\frac{36}{7}+\frac{18}{7}t^{2}+\frac{32}{7}t^{4}.\end{split}

It follows that

\displaystyle\sigma(g_{t})\geq\sigma(\bar{g}),

and the equality holds if and only if
t=0
.

On the other hand, for the volume, we have

\begin{split}\text{Vol}(g_{t})&=(1+4t^{2})^{-1}(1+4t^{2})^{-1}(1+3t)^{-1}(1-3t)^{-1}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t^{2}-56t^{4}-144t^{6}}\text{Vol}(\bar{g})\end{split}

It follows that

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(g_{t})\geq\text{Vol}(\bar{g})

if
|t|
is chosen small enough and the equality holds if and only if
t=0
.

So if we choose
0\leq|t|\leq\varepsilon
for some small number
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\begin{split}\sigma(g_{t})>\sigma(\bar{g}),\ \ \ \text{Vol}(g_{t})>\text{Vol}(\bar{g}).\end{split}

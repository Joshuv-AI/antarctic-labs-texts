Ramsey-type results for path covers and path partitions. II. Digraphs
Shuya Chiba, Michitaka Furuya

Abstract
Recently, the authors [ 4 ] gave Ramsey-type results for the path cover/partition number of graphs. In this paper, we continue the research about them focusing on digraphs, and find a relationship between the path cover/partition number and forbidden structures in digraphs. Let D D be a weakly connected digraph. A family 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of subdigraphs of D D is called a path cover (resp. a path partition ) of D D if ⋃ P ∈ 𝒫 V ⁡ ( P ) = V ⁡ ( D ) \bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}V(P)=V(D) (resp. ⋃ ˙ P ∈ 𝒫 ​ V ​ ( P ) = V ⁡ ( D ) \dot{\bigcup}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}V(P)=V(D) ) and every element of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is a directed path. The minimum cardinality of a path cover (resp. a path partition) of D D is denoted by pc ⁡ ( D ) {\rm pc}(D) (resp. pp ⁡ ( D ) {\rm pp}(D) ). In this paper, we find forbidden structure conditions assuring us that pc ⁡ ( D ) {\rm pc}(D) (or pp ⁡ ( D ) {\rm pp}(D) ) is bounded by a constant.

1.1
Definitions

All graphs and digraphs considered in this paper are finite and simple.
Furthermore, to simplify the situations, we assume that all digraphs in this paper have no
2
-cycles (and such digraphs are sometimes called

).
However, one might notice that our results are easily extended to digraphs with
2
-cycles.
For terms and symbols not defined in this paper, we refer the reader to

8
]

Let

be a graph.
Let
V(G)
and

denote the
vertex set
and the

of
G
, respectively.
For a vertex
x\in V(G)
, let
N_{G}(x)
denote the
neighborhood
of
x
in
G
; thus
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G):xy\in E(G)\}
.
For a subset

of
V(G)
, let
N_{G}(X)=(\bigcup_{x\in X}N_{G}(x))\setminus X
.
Let

denote the

of
G
, i.e., the maximum cardinality of an independent set of
G
.
Let
K_{n}
,
P_{n}
and
K_{1,n}
denote the
complete graph
of order
n
, the
path
of order
n
and the
star
of order

, respectively.
For two positive integers
n_{1}
and

, the

R(n_{1},n_{2})
is the minimum positive integer

such that any graph of order at least
R
contains a clique of cardinality
n_{1}
or an independent set of cardinality
n_{2}
.

Let
D
be a digraph.
Let
V(D)
and
E(D)
denote the
vertex set
and the
arc set
of
D
, respectively.
Let
G_{D}
be the underlying graph of
D
, i.e.,
G_{D}
is the graph on
V(D)
with
E(G_{D})=\{xy:(x,y)\in E(D)\mbox{ or }(y,x)\in E(D)\}
.
A digraph is said to be

if its underlying graph is connected.
For a vertex
x\in V(D)
, let
d^{+}_{D}(x)
and

denote the

and

of
x
in
D
, respectively; thus
d^{+}_{D}(x)=|\{y\in V(D):(x,y)\in E(D)\}|
and

.
A digraph

is a

if
G_{P}
is a path.
For a pseudo-path
P
, let
r(P)=|\{x\in V(P):d_{P}^{+}(x)=2\mbox{ or }d_{P}^{-}(x)=2\}|
.
A pseudo-path
P
is called a
directed path
if
r(P)=0
.
A (non-oriented) path
P
with
V(P)=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq l\}
and
E(P)=\{x_{i}x_{i+1}:1\leq i\leq l-1\}
is often denoted by
P=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{l}
.
Furthermore, if there is no fear of confusion, a directed path
P
with
V(P)=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq l\}
and
E(P)=\{(x_{i},x_{i+1}):1\leq i\leq l-1\}
is also denoted by
P=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{l}
.

For a (di)graph
\Gamma
and a subset
X
of
V(\Gamma)
, let
\Gamma[X]
(resp.
\Gamma-X
) denote the sub(di)graph of
\Gamma
induced by
X
(resp.
V(\Gamma)\setminus X
).
For two (di)graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
,
\Gamma
is said to be
\Gamma^{\prime}
-free
if
\Gamma
contains no induced copy of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
For a family
{\mathcal{G}}
of (di)graphs, a (di)graph
\Gamma
is said to be
{\mathcal{G}}
-free
if
\Gamma
is
\Gamma^{\prime}
-free for every
\Gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
In this context, the members of
{\mathcal{G}}
are called

.

Let
\Gamma
be a (di)graph.
When
\Gamma
is a graph, a family
{\mathcal{P}}
of subgraphs of
\Gamma
is called a
path cover
of
\Gamma
if
\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}V(P)=V(\Gamma)
and each element of
{\mathcal{P}}
is a path.
When
\Gamma
is a digraph, a family
{\mathcal{P}}
of subdigraphs of
\Gamma
is called a
path cover
of
\Gamma
if
\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}V(P)=V(\Gamma)
and each element of
{\mathcal{P}}
is a directed path.
Note that some elements of a path cover of
\Gamma
might have common vertices.
A path cover
{\mathcal{P}}
of
\Gamma
is called a
path partition
of
\Gamma
if the elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
are pairwise vertex-disjoint.
Since
\{\Gamma[\{x\}]:x\in V(\Gamma)\}
is a path partition of
\Gamma
(and so it is a path cover of
\Gamma
), the minimum cardinality of a path cover (or a path partition) of any (di)graph is well-defined.
The value
\min\{|{\mathcal{P}}|:{\mathcal{P}}\mbox{ is a path cover of }\Gamma\}
(resp.
\min\{|{\mathcal{P}}|:{\mathcal{P}}\mbox{ is a path partition of }\Gamma\}
), denoted by
{\rm pc}(\Gamma)
(resp.
{\rm pp}(\Gamma)
), is called the

(resp. the

) of
\Gamma
.
It is trivial that
{\rm pc}(\Gamma)\leq{\rm pp}(\Gamma)
.
Since a digraph
D
has a Hamiltonian directed path if and only if
{\rm pp}(D)=1
, the decision problem for the path partition number is a natural generalization of the Hamiltonian directed path problem in digraphs.

1.2
Ramsey-type problem and main results

The statement of a classical Ramsey theorem equals to the following:
For an integer
n\geq 1
, there exists a constant
c=c(n)
depending on
n
only such that every
\{K_{n},\overline{K_{n}}\}
-free graph
G
satisfies
|V(G)|\leq c
, where
\overline{K_{n}}
is the complement of
K_{n}
.
Its analogy for connected graphs is well-known as a folklore (see
[
8
, Proposition 9.4.1]
):
For an integer
n\geq 1
, there exists a constant
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(n)
depending on
n
only such that every connected
\{K_{n},K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-free graph
G
satisfies
|V(G)|\leq c^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, some researchers have tried to extend the result to a theorem for some invariants (other than order), and found Ramsey-type properties which claim the following (see, for example,
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
11
]
):
For a given graph-invariant
\mu
, if we impose forbidden structure conditions on a connected graph
G
, then
\mu(G)
is bounded by a constant.

Recently, the authors
[
4
]
gave Ramsey-type theorems for the path cover/partition number of graphs (see Theorem
A
below).
In this paper, we continue the research about them focusing on digraphs, and find a relationship between the path cover/partition number and forbidden structures in digraphs.
Although

\bullet

the problems finding a longest directed path or a path partition in digraphs are major topics in graph theory (see
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
) and

\bullet

there are many results on forbidden subgraph conditions for the existence of a Hamiltonian path in graphs (see
[
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
),

there are only a few papers which treat directed paths in digraphs together with forbidden structure conditions.
One of its reasons is the number of forbidden structures for the existence of directed paths having a good property.
Indeed, our main theorem (Theorem
1.1
) essentially uses eight or ten forbidden structures.
Note that half of them derives from the assumptions in Theorem
A
which treats a small path cover/partition problem in graphs.
In general, when we analyze a relationship between directed paths and forbidden structures, it is necessary to previously clarify forbidden subgraph conditions for a path of graphs in quite some detail.
Thus, after getting the results in
[
4
]
, we estimate that it is a suitable time to start to analyze forbidden structure conditions for the problem finding a small path cover/partition of digraphs.

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1

For an integer
n\geq 2
, the following hold:

(i)

There exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)
depending on
n
only such that
{\rm pc}(D)\leq c_{1}
for every weakly connected digraph
D
satisfying (D1), (D2) and (D3).

(ii)

There exists a constant
c^{\prime}_{1}=c^{\prime}_{1}(n)
depending on
n
only such that
{\rm pp}(D)\leq c^{\prime}_{1}
for every weakly connected digraph
D
satisfying (D’1), (D2) and (D3).

Strictly speaking, the condition (D3) is not a forbidden subdigraph condition.
However, when we consider conditions for bounding the path cover/partition number of digraphs, we must forbid a pseudo-path
P
with large
r(P)
because
{\rm pc}(P)={\rm pp}(P)=r(P)+1
for every pseudo-path
P
.
Since no finite forbidden subdigraph conditions can exclude all pseudo-paths
P
with large
r(P)
, the condition (D3) is reasonable in our main results.

The condition that “weakly connected digraph
D
satisfying (D1) (or (D’1)), (D2) and (D3)” is best possible in a sense because essentially weaker conditions than the conditions (D1) (resp. (D’1)), (D2) and (D3) cannot assure us the existence of a small path cover (resp. a small path partition) of digraphs.
(We omit its details, but the discussion in
[
4
]
will be a good reference for the checking.)

We introduce some lemmas in Section
2
, and prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
3
.
We remark that the assumptions imposed on digraphs in Theorem
1.1
are closely divided into two parts:

\bullet

Fundamental assumptions for the underlying graph
G_{D}
((D1) and (D’1)), which lead to the existence of small path cover/partitions of
G_{D}
, and

\bullet

additional assumptions which are peculiar to digraphs ((D2) and (D3)).

However in general, when we compare Ramsey-type problems on graphs and digraphs, the above situation does not always occur.
To demonstrate it, in Section
4
, we give a similar theorem on the cycle cover/partition number of digraphs and show that the assumptions for the underlying graph do not assure us the existence of small cycle cover/partitions of graphs.

4
Ramsey-type theorem for the cycle cover/partition number of digraphs

A digraph
C
is called a
directed cycle
if
G_{C}
is a cycle and
d^{+}_{C}(x)=d^{-}_{C}(x)=1
for every
x\in V(C)
.
Let
\Gamma
be a (di)graph.
When
\Gamma
is a graph, a family
{\mathcal{C}}
of subgraphs of
\Gamma
is called a
cycle cover
of
\Gamma
if
\bigcup_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}}V(C)=V(\Gamma)
and each element of
{\mathcal{C}}
is either a cycle or a connected graph of order at most two.
When
\Gamma
is a digraph, a family
{\mathcal{C}}
of subdigraphs of
\Gamma
is called a
cycle cover
of
\Gamma
if
\bigcup_{C\in{\mathcal{C}}}V(C)=V(\Gamma)
and each element of
{\mathcal{C}}
is either a directed cycle or a weakly connected digraph of order at most two.
A cycle cover
{\mathcal{C}}
of
\Gamma
is called a
cycle partition
of
\Gamma
if the elements of
{\mathcal{C}}
are pairwise vertex-disjoint.
The value
\min\{|{\mathcal{C}}|:{\mathcal{C}}\mbox{ is a cycle cover of }\Gamma\}
(resp.
\min\{|{\mathcal{C}}|:{\mathcal{C}}\mbox{ is a cycle partition of }\Gamma\}
), denoted by
{\rm cc}(\Gamma)
(resp.
{\rm cp}(\Gamma)
), is called the
cycle cover number
(resp. the
cycle partition number
) of
\Gamma
.
In
[
4
]
, the authors proved the following Ramsey-type result for the cycle partition number of graphs.
(They also showed that the
\{K^{*}_{n},K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-freeness in the theorem is necessary to bound the cycle cover number by a constant.
Thus Theorem
B
also offers tight forbidden subgraph conditions on a Ramsey-type theorem for the cycle cover number of graphs.)

Theorem B

(Chiba and Furuya
[
4
]
)

For an integer
n\geq 2
, there exists a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(n)
depending on
n
only such that
{\rm cp}(G)\leq c_{0}
for every connected
\{K^{*}_{n},K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-free graph
G
.

In this section, we give an analogy of Theorem
B
for digraphs.
Let
T_{n}
denote a
transitive tournament
of order
n
, i.e.,
T_{n}
is a digraph on
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
such that
E(T_{n})=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n\}
.
Our main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 4.1

For an integer
n\geq 2
, there exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)
depending on
n
only such that
{\rm cp}(D)\leq c_{1}
for every weakly connected
T_{n}
-free digraph
D
such that
G_{D}
is
\{K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-free.

Since
T_{n}
has no directed cycles, we have
{\rm cp}(T_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.
In particular, when we consider a forbidden subdigraph condition assuring us that the cycle cover/partition number of digraphs is bounded by a constant, some transitive tournaments appear in the condition.
Thus the forbidden structure condition in Theorem
4.1
is best possible in a sense.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Let
D
be a weakly connected
T_{n}
-free digraph such that
G_{D}
is
\{K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-free.
If
G_{D}
contains a clique
C
with
|C|=2^{n-1}
, then it is well-known that
D
contains a copy of
T_{n}
as an induced subdigraph, which is a contradiction.
Thus
G_{D}
is
K_{2^{n-1}}
-free.
For every connected graph
G
, it is known that
|V(G)|\leq\Delta(G)^{\mbox{diam}(G)}
, where
\Delta(G)
and
{\rm diam}(G)
are the maximum degree and the diameter of

, respectively.
Since
G_{D}
is
\{K_{2^{n-1}},K_{1,n}\}
-free, we can easily verify that the maximum degree of
G_{D}
is at most
R(2^{n-1}-1,n)-1
.
Since
G_{D}
is
P_{n}
-free, the diameter of
G_{D}
is at most
n-2
.
Consequently,
{\rm cp}(D)\leq|V(D)|=|V(G_{D})|\leq\Delta(G_{D})^{\mbox{diam}(G_{D})}\leq(R(2^{n-1}-1,n)-1)^{n-2}
, as desired.
∎

One might notice that the

Thus the forbidden structure in Theorem

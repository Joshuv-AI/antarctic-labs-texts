Abstract
. In this work we construct sequences of locally recoverable AG codes arising from a tower of function fields and give bound for the parameters of the obtained codes. In a particular case of a tower over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} for any odd q q , defined by Garcia and Stichtenoth in [ 3 ] , we show that the bound is sharp for the first code in the sequence, and we include a detailed analysis for the following codes in the sequence based on the distribution of rational places that split completely in the considered function field extension. Key words: Function fields, Towers, Codes, LRC, Asymptotic behavior 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification: 94B27, 14H05, 11G20, 11T71



1.
Introduction

Let
q
be a prime power. A linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
elements is a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. The dimension of the code
k
is the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
as a
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space and its minimum distance
d
is the minimum of the number of non-zero entries of a vector in
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
. A linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
and dimension
k
is a locally recoverable error-correcting code, (LRC for short) with locality
r
if it is a code that produces an
n
-coordinate codeword from
k
information symbols and, for any coordinate of the codeword, there exist at most
r
other coordinates that allow to recover the lost coordinate. LRCs were introduced in
[
4
]
motivated by the significant use of coding techniques applied to data distribution and cloud storage systems, since local recovery techniques enable us to repair lost encoded data by making use of small amount of data within the received vector instead of all information.

One of the problems of interest in the field is the construction of long non trivial codes, in which the ground field cardinality is not much larger than the code length. It is well known that one way of obtaining long codes is by the use of algebraic curves with many rational points, or equivalently, algebraic function fields with many rational places. In this work we are interested in working with this type of algebraic-geometry codes (AG codes for short), or codes coming from the evaluation of rational points on an algebraic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, (see Section
2
for precise definitions). In this direction, Barg, Tamo and Vladut
[
1
]
constructed algebraic-geometry locally recoverable codes, generalizing previous works of Barg and Tamo
[
9
,
10
]
. Haymaker, Malmskog and Matthews in
[
5
]
, used fiber products of maximal curves to construct LRC with availability, giving in this way a generalization of
[
1
]
for more than one recovery set.

Since we aim to build sequences of AG codes that are also LR codes, we will work with towers
\mathcal{F}=(F_{0},F_{1},\ldots)
of function fields over the same finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, in the way defined by Garcia and Stichtenoth (see for example
[
3
,
8
]
).
By using these type of asymptotically optimal towers, Tamo, Barg and Frolov in
[
11
]
gave a construction of asymptotically good locally recoverable codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with locality
r=q-1
whose rate
R
and relative distance
\delta
satisfy the following equation

(1)

R\geq\frac{r}{r+1}\left(1-\delta-\frac{3}{q+1}\right).

In our work, we also employ the Garcia-Stichtenoth function field given in
[
3
]
to build asymptotically good LRCs. Our method can be viewed as an extension of the construction given in
[
1
]
. Furthermore, our construction allows to compute the exact dimension of the code in severals steps on the tower, using a different evaluation set but in this case, although we can improve the dimension, we have a cost to pay in the bound for the minimum distance. Nevertheless, we are able to construct a family of LRC over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with locality
r=q-1
whose rate
R
and relative distance
\delta
satisfy

(2)

R\geq\frac{r}{r+1}\left(1-\delta-\frac{2}{q}\right),

which improves the asymptotic Gilbert-Varshamov type bound for LRCs with a single recovery given by (
1
).
This bound is a particular case of the bound given by Li, Ma and Xing in
[
6
]
, in which they use the same tower and the automorphism group of the function fields involved to build asymptotically good LRCs. Nevertheless, note that the main difference with this work is that we are able to compute the exact dimension of the constructed codes.

This work is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give some preliminary definitions and facts on the behavior of towers of function fields. Section
3
is devoted to build locally recoverable codes from a tower of function fields, see Theorem
3.4
.
Determining the exact minimum distance of linear codes can be a hard problem. In particular, few results are known for the exact minimum distance of LRCs. In
[
2
]
, this problem is addressed by using the construction given in
[
5
]
, and analyzing relative parameters. In Section
4
, and in particular in Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
, we compute the exact minimum distance of the constructed codes in one step extension of the tower, and we show that the method used to compute this distance will not be enough to compute the minimum distance in further steps. Finally, in the last section of this paper, we compute some asymptotic parameters and show the improvements mentioned in Equation (
2
).

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this work, we will use the terminology of function fields for defining AG-codes (instead of algebraic curves). An algebraic function field
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a finite algebraic extension of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, where
x
is a transcendental element over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For a complete introduction on algebraic function field and their relationship with codes see for example
[
8
]
.

Let
D
and
S=P_{1}+\cdots+P_{n}
be two disjoint divisors of an algebraic function field
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
where
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are different rational places of
F
, and consider the Riemann-Roch space associated to
D

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{f\in F\setminus\{0\}:(f)\geq-D\}\cup\{0\},

where
(f)
denotes the principal divisor of
f\in F
. The algebraic-geometry code
defined by
F
,
D
and
S
is

\mathcal{C}=C_{\mathcal{L}}(S,D)=\big\{\big(f(P_{1}),f(P_{2}),\ldots,f(P_{n})\big)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:f\in\mathcal{L}(D)\big\},

where
f(P_{i})
stands for the residue class of
f
modulo
P_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
.

Since our aim is to build infinite sequences of AG codes that are also LR codes, we will work with infinite sequences of function fields. For fixing our notation and settings we will use the following definitions.

Definition 1
.

We will consider sequences
\mathcal{F}=(F_{0},F_{1},\ldots)
, of function fields over the same finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

1.

F_{0}\subsetneq F_{1}\subsetneq F_{2}\subsetneq\cdots
,

2.

the field extension
F_{i+1}/F_{i}
is finite and separable for all
i\geq 0
, and

3.

\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the full constant field of each
F_{i}
for all
i\geq 0
, i.e., the only elements of
F_{i}
which are algebraic over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Following the works of Garcia and Stichtenoth (see for example
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
), we will say that the sequence
\mathcal{F}
is a
tower of function fields over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
, if the genus
g(F_{i})
grows to infinity as
i\rightarrow\infty
.

Definition 2
.

A sequence (or a tower)
\mathcal{F}=(F_{0},F_{1},\ldots)
is
recursively defined
if there exist a bivariate polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[S,T]
and transcendental elements
x_{i}

i=1,2,\ldots
, such that for all
i\geq 0
the following statements hold:

1.

F_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x_{0})
is the rational function field.

2.

F_{i+1}=F_{i}(x_{i+1})
with
f(x_{i},x_{i+1})=0
.

3.

[F_{i+1}:F_{i}]=\deg_{T}f
.

The following definitions are important in the study of the asymptotic behavior of sequences of function fields. As usual, we will denote by
\mathbb{P}(F)
the set of all places of the function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given a finite extension
E/F
and a place
P\in\mathbb{P}(F)
there are finitely many places
Q\in\mathbb{P}(E)
lying above
P
. We will write
Q|P
when
Q
lies over
P
. If
\mathcal{F}=(F_{0},F_{1},\ldots)
is a sequence of function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we say that a place
P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{i})

splits completely
in
\mathcal{F}
if
P
splits completely in each extension
F_{j}/F_{i}
(and in this case we have
[F_{j}:F_{i}]
different places over
P
). The
splitting locus
of
\mathcal{F}
over
F_{0}
is defined as

\mathrm{Split}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0}):=\{P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{0})\,:\,\text{$\deg(P)=1$ and $P$ splits completely in $\mathcal{F}$}\}\,,

where
\deg(P)
is the degree of the place
P
.
A place
P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{i})
is
ramified
in
\mathcal{F}
if
P
is ramified in any extension
F_{j}/F_{i}
.
The
ramification locus
of
\mathcal{F}
over
F_{0}
is the set

\mathrm{Ram}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0}):=\{P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{0})\,:\,\text{$P$ ramified in some extension }F_{n}/F_{0}\}.

A place
P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{i})

is totally ramified
in
\mathcal{F}
if
P
is totally ramified in each extension
F_{j}/F_{i}
(in this case we have only one place
Q
in
\mathbb{P}(F_{j})
over
P
and the ramification index
e(Q|P)
is equal to
[F_{j}:F_{i}]
). The
complete ramification locus
of
\mathcal{F}
over
F_{0}
is defined as

\mathrm{CRam}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0}):=\{P\in\mathbb{P}(F_{0})\,:\,\text{$\deg(P)=1$ and $P$ is totally ramified in $\mathcal{F}$}\}\,.

Since every place
Q\in\mathbb{P}(F_{i})
lying above a place in
\mathrm{Split}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})\cup\mathrm{CRam}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})
is a rational place (i.e. of degree one), we have that

(3)

N(F_{i})\geq[F_{i}:F_{0}]|\mathrm{Split}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})|+|\mathrm{CRam}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})|\,,

where
N(F_{i})
is the number of rational places of
F_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a sequence such that
\mathrm{Split}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})\neq\emptyset
then
\mathcal{F}
is a tower.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Split}(\mathcal{F}/F_{0})\neq\emptyset
there is a rational place
P
in
F_{0}
that splits completely in each extension
F_{i}/F_{0}
then, by the Hasse-Weil bound, we have that
g(F_{i})\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
so that
\mathcal{F}
is actually a tower.
∎

The
limit
of a tower of function fields
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\lambda(\mathcal{F})=\frac{N(F)}{g(F)},

it always exist and it is a non negative amount, so it is said that the tower is
asymptotically good
if
\lambda(\mathcal{F})>0
and
asymptotically bad
on the other case. Therefore, a tower is asymptotically good if and only if it has non-emply splitting locus and finite ramification locus (see
[
8
, Proposition 7.2.6]
).

In this work we will “lift” divisors from one function field
F
to an extension
E
by using the conorm map on divisors

\operatorname{Con}_{E/F}:\operatorname{Div}(F)\rightarrow\operatorname{Div}(E),

that we now recall.
If
P
is a place in
F
, the conorm divisor of
P
is the divisor

\operatorname{Con}_{E/F}(P)=\sum_{{}_{Q|P}}e(Q|P)\,Q

in
E
, where
e(Q|P)
is the ramification index of the place
Q
in
E
over
P
.
Now, the
conorm divisor
of
D=\sum_{P}n_{P}P\in\operatorname{Div}(F)
in
E
is given by

D^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Con}_{E/F}(D)=\sum_{P}n_{P}\operatorname{Con}_{E/F}(P).

3.
Sequences of LRC

First we will present the construction of a LR code on an extension of function fields. This construction can be found Theorem 3.1 in
[
1
]
. We included here for the sake of completeness and adapting it to the function field language.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F
a function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
E=F(x)
a function field extension of degree
m
. Let
S
be a set of places of
F
that split completely in
E/F
and such that

\{Q\in\mathbb{P}(E):Q\cap F\in S\}\cap\{Q\in\mathbb{P}(E):\nu_{Q}(x)<0\}=\emptyset

and let
\mathcal{B}=\{Q\in\mathbb{P}(E):Q\cap F\in S\}
. Then, if
|S|=s>0
we have that
|\mathcal{B}|=sm
. Choose a divisor
D
of
F
of degree
l
such that
\operatorname{Supp}(D)\cap S=\emptyset
, and let
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\}
a basis for
\mathcal{L}(D)
, the Riemann-Roch space associated to
D
. Let
r=m-1
and consider the space
V
generated by

\{f_{w}x^{e}:w=1,\ldots,\ell;e=0,\ldots,r-1\}.

Since
\operatorname{Supp}(D)
is disjoint from S, the evaluation map

\begin{array}[]{cccl}ev:&V&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(r+1)s}\\
&f&\rightarrow&(f(P_{11}),\ldots,f(P_{ms}))\end{array}

is well defined. The image of this mapping is a locally recoverable code
\mathcal{C}
with locality
r
, which we denote by

C(S,D)
. The code coordinates are naturally partitioned into
s
subsets
A_{j}=\{P_{ij}\}_{i=1,...,r+1}
of size
r+1
each. Denoting by
g(F)
the genus of
F
and
h=[E:\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)]
, we have that the parameters of the code satisfy

•

n=(r+1)s
,

•

k=r\ell\geq r(l+1-g(F))
,

•

d\geq n-l(r+1)-(r-1)h
,

provided that the right-hand side of the inequality for
d
is a positive integer. Local recovery of an erased symbol
c_{ij}=f(P_{ij})
can be performed by polynomial interpolation through the positions of the points in the recovery set
A_{j}\setminus\{P_{ij}\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that the previous construction can be performed for any step in a tower of function fields, either consecutive or not.

In particular, we can consider a tower of function fields, and built a LR code, but starting with the rational function field, as the base field in the tower.

A modification of the previous result allow us to compute the exact dimension of the code, using a different vector space. Note that in this case, although we can compute the exact dimension, and improve the dimension in some cases, we have to pay a cost in the bound for the minimum distance of the code.

∎

Proof.

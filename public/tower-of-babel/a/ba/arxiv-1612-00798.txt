Global Existence and Exponential Stability for a Nonlinear Thermoelastic Kirchhoff-Love Plate
Irena Lasiecka, Michael Pokojovy, Xiang Wan

Abstract
We study an initial-boundary-value problem for a quasilinear thermoelastic plate of Kirchhoff & Love-type with parabolic heat conduction due to Fourier, mechanically simply supported and held at the reference temperature on the boundary. For this problem, we show the short-time existence and uniqueness of classical solutions under appropriate regularity and compatibility assumptions on the data. Further, we use barrier techniques to prove the global existence and exponential stability of solutions under a smallness condition on the initial data. It is the first result of this kind established for a quasilinear non-parabolic thermoelastic Kirchhoff & Love plate in multiple dimensions.



Key words:

Kirchhoff-Love plates; nonlinear thermoelasticity; Hyperbolic-Parabolic PDE systems;
global well-posedness; classical solutions; exponential stability

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2\mbox{ or }3
) be a bounded domain with a smooth boundary
representing the mid-plane of a thermoelastic plate.
With
w
and
\theta

denoting the vertical deflection and an appropriately weighted thermal moment with respect to the plate thickness,
both depending on a scaled time variable
t>0
and the space variable
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega
,
the nonlinear Kirchhoff & Love thermoelastic plate system reads as

\displaystyle w_{tt}-\gamma\triangle w_{tt}+a(-\triangle w)\triangle^{2}w+\alpha\triangle\theta

\displaystyle=f(-\triangle w,-\nabla\triangle w)\phantom{0}\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\beta\theta_{t}-\eta\triangle\theta+\sigma\theta-\alpha\triangle w_{t}

\displaystyle=0\phantom{f(-\triangle w,-\nabla\triangle w)}\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega

(1.1b)

along with the boundary conditions (hinged mechanical/Dirichlet thermal)

w=\triangle w=\theta=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega

(1.1c)

and the initial conditions

w(0,\cdot)=w^{0},\quad w_{t}(0,\cdot)=w^{1},\quad\theta(0,\cdot)=\theta^{0}\text{ in }\Omega.

(1.1d)

Here,
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\eta,\sigma
are positive constants and

a\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
as well as
f\colon\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions.
For thin plates,
\gamma
behaves like
h^{2}
as
h\to 0
(cf.
[
11
, Equation (2.16), p. 13]
) and is, therefore, neglected in some literature.
In Section
2

we present a short physical deduction of Equations (
1.1a
)–(
1.1d
).

\displaystyle w_{tt}+\triangle^{2}w-\triangle\theta+a\triangle\big((\triangle w)^{3}\big)

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-\triangle\theta+\triangle w_{t}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega

(1.2b)

together with boundary conditions (
1.1c
)
and initial conditions (
1.1d
)
for an arbitrary

.
For the initial-boundary-value problem
(
1.2a
)–(
1.2b
),

The existence proof was based on a Galerkin approximation and compactness theorems,
while the uniform stability was obtained with the aid of energy techniques.

In their monograph
[
3
]
, Chueshov and Lasiecka give an extensive study on the von Kármán
plate system both in pure elastic and thermoelastic cases.
With
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
denoting the vertical displacement
and

of a plate with its mid-plane occupying in the reference configuration a domain

the pure elastic version of Kármán plate system reads as

\displaystyle w_{tt}-\alpha\triangle w_{tt}+\triangle^{2}u-[u,v+F_{0}]+Lu

\displaystyle=p\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(1.3a)

\displaystyle\triangle^{2}v+[u,u]

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(1.3b)

where
[v,w]:=v_{x_{1}x_{1}}w_{x_{2}x_{2}}+v_{x_{2}x_{2}}w_{x_{1}x_{1}}-2v_{x_{1}x_{2}}w_{x_{1}x_{2}}
,

is a first-order differential operator and

are given “force” functions.
Imposing standard initial conditions, under various sets of boundary conditions,
Chueshov and Lasiecka proved Equations (
1.3a
)–(
1.3b
)
possess a unique generalized, weak or strong solution depending on the data regularity.
The proof was based on a nonlinear Galerkin-type approximation.
Further, they studied the semiflow associated with the solution to Equations (
1.3a
)–(
1.3b
),
in particular, they analyzed its long-time behavior and the existence of attracting sets.
Various damping mechanisms, thermoelastic effects, structurally coupled systems
such as acoustic chambers or gas flow past a plate were studied.
An extremely detailed and comprehensive literature overview was also given.

[

on the space

Lasiecka and Wilke
[

presented an

For

They pointed out that similar arguments can be used to obtain a short-time existence for arbitrarily large initial data.
Finally, they studied the first- and higher-order differentiability as well as analyticity of solutions under appropriate assumptions on the data.

Recently, Denk and Schnaubelt
[
5
]
considered a structurally damped elastic plate equation

w_{tt}+\triangle^{2}w-\rho\triangle w_{t}=f\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega

(1.5)

in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, being either the whole space, a half-space or a bounded

-domain,
subject to inhomogeneous Dirichlet-Neumann boundary conditions

By showing the

-sectoriality of the operator driving the flow

-maximum regularity for the generator on any finite time horizon

.

-domains, a standard localization technique was adopted to

-regularity for any time horizon

.

In the present article, we study the quasilinear PDE system
associated with Equations (
1.1a
)–(
1.1d
).
In contrast to earlier works, dealing with a quasilinear system without maximal
L^{p}
-regularity property,
it is technically beneficial to look for classical rather than weak or strong solutions.
The necessity of studying smooth solutions results in a much higher complexity of the existence and uniqueness proof
as it has to be carried out at a higher energy level,
which, in turn, is based on a Kato-type approximation procedure rather than a Galerkin scheme.
The paper is structured as follows. After a short introduction Section
1
,
we present in Section
2
a brief physical deduction of the Kirchhoff & Love plate from Equations
(
1.1a
)–(
1.1d
).
In Section
4
,
an existence and uniquess result for Equations
(
1.1a
)–(
1.1d
)

The long-time behavior of Equations (
1.1a
)–(
1.1d
)
is studied in Section
5
.
Under a smallness assumption on the initial data, the global existence and uniquess of solution is proved using energy estimates and the barrier method.
Further, this global solution is shown to decay at an exponential rate to the zero equilibrium state.
Finally, in the appendix Section
A
,
we present a well-posedness theory along with higher energy estimates
for a linear wave equation with time- and space-dependent coefficients
as well as the homogeneus isotropic heat equation.

Model Description
(d=2)

Consider a prismatic solid plate of uniform thickness
h>0
and constant material density
\rho>0

occupying in a reference configuration the domain
\mathcal{B}_{h}:=\Omega\times(-\tfrac{h}{2},\tfrac{h}{2})
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is bounded.
The underlying material is assumed to be elastically and thermally isotropic.
Further, we restrict ourselves to the case of infinitesimal thermoelasticity
with both stresses/strains and temperature gradient/heat flux being small.
Additionally, we assume the strains linearly decompose into elastic and thermal ones.
Despite of these linearity assumptions, a nonlinear (hypo)elastic law will be postulated
allowing for materials with genuinely nonlinear response such as rubber, liquid crystal elastomers, etc.
Figure
1
below (adopted from
[
15
, Chapter 1]
)
displays a prismatic plate together with its mid-plane in the reference configuration.

Figure 1:
Prismatic plate

We start by interpreting the plate as a 3D body.
Let
\mathbf{U}=(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3})^{T}
be the displacement vector in Lagrangian coordinates,
T
stand for the absolute temperature
and
\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})^{T}
be the associated heat flux.
Denote by
T_{0}>0
a reference temperature for which the body occupies the reference configuration and is free of any stresses or strains.
Further, let
S
denote the entropy and

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{ij})_{i=1,2,3}^{j=1,2,3}\mbox{\quad and \quad}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\nabla\mathbf{U}+(\nabla\mathbf{U})^{T}\big)

stand for the first Piola & Kirchhoff stress tensor and the infinitesimal Cauchy strain tensor.
We assume the total stress tensor decomposes into elastic and thermal stresses according to

\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{elast}}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{therm}}.

(2.1)

In the absense of external body forces and heat sources,
the momentum and energy balance equations (cf.
[
1
, p. 142]
and
[
11
, Chapter 1]
) read then as

\displaystyle\rho\mathbf{U_{tt}}+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{B}_{h},

(2.2a)

\displaystyle TS_{t}+\operatorname{div}\mathbf{q}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{B}_{h}.

(2.2b)

Similar to Ilyushin
[
7
, p. 42]
,
we define the elastic strain intensity
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}

as a properly scaled second invariant of the elastic strain deviator tensor by means of

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{elast}}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\Big((\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{elast}})^{2}-\operatorname{tr}\big((\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{elast}})^{2}\big)\Big).

Similarly, we can define the elastic stress intensity via

\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{elast}}=\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\Big((\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{elast}})^{2}-\operatorname{tr}\big((\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{elast}})^{2}\big)\Big).

Within the classical hypoelasticity, we need to postulate a relation between these two quantities.
Here, we consider a general material law given by

\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}=\kappa(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{int}}),

(2.3)

which generalizes power-law-type materials considered by Ambartsumian et al. in
[
1
, Equation (6)]
.
For the thermal stresses and strains, we select a linear material law

\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{therm}}=\tfrac{E}{1-2\mu}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{therm}},

(2.4)

where
E
and
\mu
play the role of Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio and can be reconstructed from the Hooke’s law resulting
from linearizing Equation (
2.3
) around zero.

With
\tau=T-T_{0}
denoting the relative temperature, the thermal linearity and isotropy assumptions imply

\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{therm}}=\alpha\tau\mathbf{I}_{3\times 3},

(2.5)

where
\alpha>0
is the thermal expansion coefficient (cf.
[
11
, p. 29]
).
According to Nowicki
[
14
, Chapter 1]
,
a linear approximation for the entropy reads as

S=\gamma\operatorname{tr}\big(\varepsilon^{\mathrm{elast}}\big)+\tfrac{\rho c}{T_{0}}\tau,

(2.6)

where
c>0
is the heat capacity and
\gamma=\frac{E\alpha}{1-2\mu}
.
Plugging Equations (
2.1
),
(
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
)
into Equations (
2.2a
)–(
2.2b
)
and linearizing with respect to
\tau
around zero, we get

\displaystyle\rho\mathbf{U}_{tt}+\operatorname{div}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathrm{elastic}}+\gamma\nabla\tau

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{B}_{h},

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\rho c\tau_{t}-\lambda_{0}\triangle\tau+\gamma T_{0}\operatorname{tr}\big(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{elast}}_{t}\big)

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{B}_{h}.

(2.7b)

Together with Equation (
2.3
),
Equations (
2.7a
)–(
2.7b
)
constitute the PDE system of 3D thermoelasticity.
In the following, we exploit these equations to deduce our thermoelastic plate model.

As it is typical for most plate theories,
we postulate the hypothesis of undeformable normals,
i.e., the linear filaments being perpendicular to the mid-plane before deformation
should also remain linear after the deformation.
Since we are interested in obtaining a Kirchhoff & Love-type plate model,
we additionally assume these deformed filaments remain perpendicular to the deformed mid-plane.
The in-plane displacements are assumed negligible.
Mathematically, these structural assumptions can be written as

\begin{split}U_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})&=-x_{3}w_{x_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad U_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=-x_{3}w_{x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\\
U_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})&=\phantom{-x_{3}}w(x_{1},x_{2}),\end{split}

(2.8)

where
w
is referred to as the bending component or the vertical displacement.
Thus, the elastic behavior of our plate can fully be described merely by
w
.
Figure
2
is self-describing and illustrates these structural assumptions.

Figure 2:
Mid-plane of a plate as well as
plate cross-sections
x_{2}=0
before and after the deformation

As for the thermal part of the system,
a properly weighted momentum of the relative temperature
\tau
with respect to
x_{3}
given by

\theta(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{12\alpha}{h^{3}}\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}x_{3}\tau(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mathrm{d}x_{3}

will play a crucial role.
Proceeding as Lagnese and Lions
[
11

,
Equation (
2.7b
) can be reduced to

\rho c\theta_{t}-\lambda_{0}\triangle\theta+\tfrac{12\lambda_{0}}{\rho ch^{2}}\big(\tfrac{h\lambda_{1}}{2}+1\big)\theta+\tfrac{\alpha\gamma}{\rho c}\triangle w_{t}=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

where
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
is the parameter from the Newton’s cooling law applied to the lower and upper faces of the plate.

Returning to the elastic part and assuming for a moment the material response
\kappa(\cdot)

from Equation (
2.3
) is an analytic function
possessing a Taylor expansion with the vanishing constant term

\kappa(s)=\sum_{m=1}a_{m}s^{m}\text{ for some }a_{m}\in\mathbb{R},

we combine the approaches of Ambartsumian et al.
[
1
]
and Lagnese & Lions
[
11
, Chapter 1]
to deduce

\rho hw_{tt}-\tfrac{\rho h^{3}}{12}\triangle w_{tt}+\triangle K(\triangle w)+D\tfrac{1+\mu}{2}\triangle\theta=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

where
D=\frac{Eh^{3}}{12(1-\mu^{2})}
denotes the flexural rigidity
and
K(\cdot)
is obtained from
\kappa(\cdot)
by means of

K(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\big)^{n+1}\tfrac{h^{n+1}}{n+2}a_{n}s^{n}.

In contrast to
[
1
]
,
the
\triangle w_{tt}
-term is not neglected here allowing for an adequate description of thicker plates
than those accounted for by the standard theory.
Obviously,
K(\cdot)
is also analytic and its Taylor series has the same absolute convergence region as
\kappa(\cdot)
.
Taking into account

\displaystyle K(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\big)^{n+1}\tfrac{h^{n+2}}{n+2}a_{n}s^{n-1}=s^{-3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\big)^{n+1}\tfrac{h^{n+2}}{n+2}a_{n}s^{n+2}

\displaystyle=s^{-3}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\big)^{-1}\tfrac{\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)^{n+2}}{n+2}a_{n}s^{n}=h^{3}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)^{-3}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\big)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tfrac{\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)^{n+2}}{n+2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4h^{3}}{3}\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)^{-3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tfrac{\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)^{n+2}}{n+2}=\tfrac{4h^{3}}{3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\big[I(\cdot)^{n}\big]\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)

with the linear operator

\big(If\big)(s)=s^{-3}\int_{0}^{s}\xi f(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\text{ for }s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

the function
K
can equivalently be written as

K(s)=\tfrac{4}{3}h^{3}[I\kappa]\big(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}hs\big)\text{ for }s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

By density and continuity,
I
can uniquely be extended to a mapping
from the set of continuous functions differentiable and vanishing in 0 with the following norm being bounded

\|f\|=\max\big\{\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|,|f^{\prime}(0)|\big\}

into the space of continuous functions vanishing at
0
.

Summarizing, our thermoelastic plate system reads as

\displaystyle\rho hw_{tt}-\tfrac{\rho h^{3}}{12}\triangle w_{tt}+\triangle K(\triangle w)+D\tfrac{1+\mu}{2}\triangle\theta

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(2.9a)

\displaystyle\rho c\partial_{t}\theta-\lambda_{0}\triangle\theta+\tfrac{12\lambda_{0}}{\rho ch^{2}}\big(\tfrac{h\lambda_{1}}{2}+1\big)\theta+\tfrac{\alpha\gamma\lambda_{0}}{\rho c}\triangle w_{t}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega.

(2.9b)

Various boundary conditions can be adopted.
We refer to
[
2
, Chapter 2]
,
[

and
[

Here, we consider a simply supported plate held at the reference temperature at the boundary:

w=\triangle w=\theta=0\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega.

The Main Results

Theorem 5.6

(Global Existence)
.

Let Assumption
4.4
be satisfied for some
s\geq 3
.
Then, there exists a positive number
\epsilon
such that for any initial data satisfying
X(0)<\epsilon

(which roughly means the smallness of

the associated local solution of system (

)–(
5.2d
) from Theorem

exists globally, namely,

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
X(0)>0
. Indeed, if
X(0)=0
, the only solution to (
5.2a
)–(
5.2d
) is the trivial one
and, therefore, exists globally.

We argue by contradiction. Assume
T_{\max}<\infty
.

Step 0:
Let

\epsilon=\min\{1,\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}\},

(5.64)

where
\epsilon_{1}
is given in Equation (
5.8
) in Lemma
5.2

and
\epsilon_{2}
is defined in Step 1 below.
Note that due to the validness of Equation (
5.8
) to be established below (cf. proof of Step 3),
the hyperbolicity of Equation (
5.2a
) is satisfied for all times
and the solution can only cease to exist if
X(t)
blows-up.

By Theorem
4.5
, there exists a (local) solution on the maximal interval
[0,T_{\max})
.
Define

\displaystyle k(x)

\displaystyle=x-C_{4}(x^{2}+x^{4}+x^{6}+x^{8})\text{ and }

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=C_{1}x-C_{3}\left[x^{3/2}+x^{2}+x^{3}+x^{4}+x^{9/2}+x^{6}+x^{9}\right],

and

5.60
).
Since
X(0)<\epsilon\leq 1
, Equation (
5.13
) is satisfied. Therefore, the estimate in Equation (

), or (

), holds and can be rewritten as

Further, we observe that the (algebraic) equation

has a unique positive solution denoted by
\eta
.
There also holds

for
x\in[0,\eta)
.
Similarly,
h(x)=0
has a unique positive solution denoted by
\xi
. Besides,

for

Step 1: Small initial data

.

Consider the inequality

Due to the continuity of

,

.

\displaystyle X(0)<\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle<\delta_{2},

(5.66)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle<\xi,

(5.67)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle<\epsilon_{1}\text{ and }

(5.68)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle<\xi\mbox{ so that }h\big[X(0)\big]>0.

Step 2: Barrier method.
We claim

(Uniform Stability)
.

with

\displaystyle X(T)

\displaystyle\left\{1-C_{4}\left[X(T)+X^{3}(T)+X^{5}(T)+X^{7}(T)\right]\right\}

c_{1}\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\|(z,\theta)\|_{X}\leq\|(z,\theta)\|_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{3}\times{\mathcal{T}}_{3}}\leq c_{2}\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\|(z,\theta)\|_{X}\quad\text{ for some }c_{1}\mbox{ and }c_{2}>0.

(5.80)

Proof.

The former inequality is trivial. For the latter one, in contrast to Lemma
5.5
, all superlinear terms are linearly dominated because they are bounded by 1,
and we are only left to show Equation (
5.63
) with a constant independent of
T
.
However, due to the exponential decay of
X(t)
(Equation (
5.72
)), (
5.63
) becomes

\|z_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\int_{0}^{\infty}X(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\tilde{C}C\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-kt}X(0)\mathrm{d}t\leq c_{2}X(0)\leq c_{2}\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}X(t).

(5.81)

With
A
’s maximal
L^{2}
-regularity on
(0,\infty)
, the estimate for
\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\big\|A^{1/2}\theta_{tt}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
follows.
∎

Since the smallness assumption is satisfied in both Equation (
5.64
) of Theorem
5.6

and Equation (
5.75
) of Corollary
5.7
,
we resubstitute
w=A^{-1}z
and conclude with the desired results Theorem
3.5
and
3.6
.

Appendix A
Existence Theory for Linear Evolution Equations

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain with a
C^{s}
-boundary
\partial\Omega
for some
s\geq\lfloor\tfrac{d}{2}\rfloor+2

and let
T>0
be arbitrary, but fixed.
The following well-posedness results are based on Kato’s solution theory
[
9
]
for abstract time-dependent evolution equations
and its improved version presented by Jiang and Racke in
[
8
, Appendix A]

as well as maximal
L^{p}
-regularity theory (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
).

Thoughout this appendix and in the proof of Theorem
4.5
,
we employ the following notation. For
n\geq 0
, we define

\bar{D}^{n}:=\big((\partial_{t},\nabla)^{\alpha}\,|\,0\leq|\alpha|\leq n\big)\text{ and }H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)\equiv H^{0}(\Omega):=L^{2}(\Omega).

Let
\phi_{\delta}\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)

denote the one-dimensional Friedrichs’ mollifier with a ‘bandwidth’
\delta>0
.
For an
L^{1}
-function
z\colon[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we let

z_{\delta}(t,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{\delta}(t-s)z(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}s\text{ for }t\in[0,T]\text{ in }\Omega.

For details on approximation properties of mollifiers, we refer to
[
16
, Chapters 8 and 9]
.
The following result is known from
[
8
, Lemma A.12]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
a\in C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{\infty}(\Omega)\big)
,

v\in C^{0}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)
and

w\in L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega)\big)
.
Then, for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, there holds

\begin{split}\int_{\varepsilon}^{T-\varepsilon}\big\|\partial_{t}\big((av)_{\delta}(t,\cdot)-av_{\delta}(t,\cdot)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}t&\to 0\text{ and }\\
\int_{\varepsilon}^{T-\varepsilon}\|w_{\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}t&\to\int_{\varepsilon}^{T-\varepsilon}\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\text{ as }\delta\to 0.\end{split}

A.1
Linear Wave Equation

We consider a general linear wave equation with time- and space-dependent coefficients:

\displaystyle z_{tt}(t,x)-\bar{a}_{ij}(t,x)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z(t,x)

\displaystyle=\bar{f}(t,x)\phantom{0}\text{ for }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,

(A.1a)

\displaystyle z(t,x)

\displaystyle=0\phantom{\bar{f}(t,x)}\text{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,

(A.1b)

\displaystyle z(0,x)=z^{0}(x),\quad z_{t}(0,x)

\displaystyle=z^{1}(x)\phantom{00}\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

(A.1c)

Assumption A.2
.

Let
s\geq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+2
be a fixed integer
and let
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
be positive numbers.
Assume the following conditions are satisfied.

1.

Coefficient symmetry
:

\bar{a}_{ij}(t,x)=\bar{a}_{ji}(t,x)
for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}
.

2.

Coefficient regularity
:

\bar{a}_{ij}\in C^{0}\big([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\big)
and

\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{a}_{ij}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{s-1}(\Omega)\big),\quad\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{s-1-m}(\Omega)\big)

for
m=1,2,\dots,s-1
.

3.

Coercivity
: For
z\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\|z\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\gamma_{0}\Big(\langle\bar{a}_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}z,\partial_{x_{j}}z\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|z\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big).

4.

Elliptic regularity
:
For
m=0,1,\dots,s-2
,
z(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z(t,\cdot)\in H^{m}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
implies

u(t,\cdot)\in H^{m+2}(\Omega)
and

\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}\leq\gamma_{1}\Big(\|\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}+\|z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

5.

Right-hand side regularity
:
For
m=0,1,\dots,s-2
,

\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{f}\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{s-2-m}(\Omega)\big),\quad\partial_{t}^{s-1}\bar{f}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

6.

Compatibility conditions
:
For
m=0,1,\dots,s-1
,

\bar{z}^{m}\in H^{s-m}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\bar{z}^{s}\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
\bar{z}^{m}
is recursively defined by

\begin{split}\bar{z}^{0}(x)&=z^{0}(x),\quad\bar{z}^{1}(x)=z^{1}(x),\\
\bar{z}^{m}(x)&=\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{m-2}{m-2\choose n}\partial_{t}^{n}\bar{a}_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{z}^{m-2-n}+\partial_{t}^{m-2}\bar{f}_{i}\Big)(0,x)\text{ for }m\geq 2\end{split}

for
x\in\Omega
.

Note that Assumption
A.2
.2 differs from
[
8
, Assumption A.2.1.1]
.
This extra regularity for
\bar{a}_{ij}
will enable us
to prove our
a priori
estimate at an energy level which is one order lower than in
[
8
, Theorem A.13]
.

Theorem A.3
.

Under Assumption
A.2
,
the initial boundary value problem (
A.1a
)-(
A.1c
)
possesses a unique classical solution, which satisfies

z\in\bigcap_{m=0}^{s-1}C^{m}\big([0,T],H^{s-m}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{s}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

Moreover, for
d\in\{2,3\}
, letting

\begin{split}\phi_{0}&=\|\bar{a}_{ij}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{a}_{ij}(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)},\\
\phi&=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\|\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{x_{k}}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}+\sum_{m=1}^{s-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1-m}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

there exists a positive number
K_{1}
,
which is a continuous function of
\phi_{0}
,
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
,
and a positive number
K_{2}
, which continuously depends on
\phi
,
\gamma_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}
,
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq K_{1}\Lambda_{0}\exp\big(K_{2}T^{1/2}(1+T^{1/2}+T+T^{3/2})\big),

where

\Lambda_{0}:=\sum_{m=0}^{s}\|\bar{z}\|_{H^{s-m}(\Omega)}^{2}+(1+T)\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big\|\bar{D}^{s-2}\bar{f}(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+T^{1/2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}^{s-1}\bar{f}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

Our proof is based on an abstract well-posedness and regularity result
[
8
, Theorems A.3 and A.9]
.

Existence and uniqueness at basic regularity level.

Similar to the proof of
[
8
, Theorem A.11]
,
we define for
t\in[0,T]
a bounded linear operator

A(t):=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}&0\end{pmatrix}\colon Y_{1}\longrightarrow X_{0},

(A.2)

where the Hilbert space
X_{0}:=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)

is equipped with the standard inner product induced by the product topology,
whereas the inner product on the Hilbert space

Y_{1}:=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
reads as

\langle V,\bar{V}\rangle_{t}:=\big\langle\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{x_{i}}z,\partial_{x_{j}}\bar{z}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\langle y,\bar{y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad

(A.3)

for
V=(z,y)
and
\bar{V}=(\bar{z},\bar{y})\in X_{0}
.
Due to uniform coercivity of
\bar{a}_{ij}
and by virtue of Poincaré-Friedrichs’ inequality,
each of the norms induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{t}
for any

is equivalent to the standard norm on
X_{0}
.
With this notation, letting
V:=(z,\partial_{t}z)
,
Equations (
A.1a
)–(
A.1c
)
can be rewritten as an abstract Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}V(t)+A(t)V(t)=F(t)\text{ in }(0,T),\quad V(0)=V^{0}

(A.4)

with
F=(0,\bar{f})
and
V^{0}=(z^{0},z^{1})
.

We want to show that the triple
\big(A;X_{0},Y_{1}\big)
is a CD-system
in sense of
[
8
, Section A.1]
.
For
t\in[0,T]
, consider the elliptic problem

\big(A(t)+\lambda\big)V=F\text{ with }F\in X_{0}.

Recalling Assumption
A.2
.3,
Lemma of Lax & Milgram implies the resolvent estimate

\big\|\big(A(t)+\lambda\big)^{-1}\big\|_{L(X_{0})}\leq\tfrac{1}{\lambda-C}\text{ for }\lambda>\beta\text{ for some constants }\beta,C>0,

(A.5)

where we used Assumption
A.2
.2 and Sobolev’s imbedding theorem to deduce

a_{ij}(t,\cdot)\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\text{ for any }t\in[0,T].

The continuity of the bilinear form follows similarly.
By standard elliptic regularity theory applied to
A(t)
,
which is possible because of Assumption
A.2
.2 and
A.2
.4
as well as
C^{s}
-smoothness of
\partial\Omega
,
the maximal domain of
A(t)
coincides with
Y_{1}
. Hence, the operator
A(t)
is closed.
This along with Equation (
A.5
) implies

(\beta,\infty)\subset\rho\big(A(t)\big)
.
Therefore,
\big(A(t);t\in[0,T]\big)
is a stable family of infinitesimal negative generators
of
C_{0}
-semigroups on
X_{0}
with stability constants
1,\beta
.
Taking into account regularity conditions from Assumption
A.2
.5,
we can apply
[
8
, Theorem A.3]
, we get a unique classical solution

V\in C^{0}\big([0,T],Y_{1}\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T],X_{0}\big)

at the at basic regularity level, which is equivalent to

z\in C^{2}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

Higher regularity.

For the proof of higher solution regularity,
we consider the following increasing double scale
(X_{j},Y_{j})
of Hilbert spaces

\begin{split}X_{j}&=\big(H^{j+1}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\times H^{j}(\Omega)\text{ for }j\geq 1,\\
Y^{j}&=\big(H^{j+1}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\times\big(H^{j}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\text{ for }j\geq 1,\quad Y_{0}=X_{0}.\end{split}

By virtue of Equation (
A.2
), the condition

\partial_{t}A\in\mathrm{Lip}\big([0,T],L(Y_{j+r+1},X_{j})\big)\text{ for }j=0,\dots,s-r-1\text{ and }r=0,\dots,s-2

is equivalent to

\partial_{t}^{r}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\in\mathrm{Lip}\big([0,T],L\big(H^{j+r+2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),H^{j}(\Omega)\big)\big)

(A.6)

for
j=0,\dots,s-r-1
and
r=0,\dots,s-2
,
while the latter is a direct consequence of Assumption
A.2
.2
and Sobolev imbedding theorem due to the fact

H^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Similarly, exploiting Assumption
A.2
.4,
one can easily verify for
j=0,\dots,s-2
and
\phi\in Y_{1}
and a.e.

that

implies

\phi\in Y_{j+1}\text{ and }\|\phi\|_{Y_{j+1}}\leq K\big(\|A(t)\phi\|_{X_{j}}+\|\phi\|_{X_{0}}\big)\text{ for some constant }K>0,

which does not depend on
\phi
.
Further, Assumption
A.2
.5 yields

\partial_{t}F\in C^{0}\big([0,T],X_{s-1-k}\big)\text{ for }k=0,\dots,s-2\text{ and }\partial_{t}^{s-1}F\in L^{1}(0,T;X_{0}).

Finally, Assumption
A.2
.6 implies compatibility conditions
in sense of
[
8
, Equations (A.8) and (A.9)]
.
Hence, applying
[
8
, Theorem A.9]
at the energy level
s-1
,
we obtain additional regularity for the classical solution satisfying

V\in\bigcap_{m=0}^{s-1}C^{m}\big([0,T],Y_{s-1-m}\big).

Rewriting
z
in terms of
V
, this yields the desired regularity for
z
.

Energy estimates.

For
n=1,\dots,s-1
, applying the
\partial_{t}^{n-1}
-operator
to Equation (

),
we obtain a linear wave equation for

\partial_{t}^{2}\big(\partial_{t}^{n-1}z\big)-\bar{a}_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\big(\partial_{t}^{n-1}z\big)=h^{n-1}\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,

(A.7)

where we used Leibniz’ rule to compute

h^{n-1}=\partial_{t}^{n-1}\bar{f}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{m}\big(\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\big)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{t}^{n-1-m}z.

(A.8)

Multiplying Equation (
A.7
)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{t}^{n}z
, applying Green’s formula
and using Young’s inequality, we obtain the estimate

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{n}&z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bar{a}(t,\cdot)\nabla\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\big\|\big(\partial_{x_{i}}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\big)\partial_{x_{j}}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|h^{n-1}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

Integrating w.r.t. to
t
over
[0,T]
, exploiting Assumption
A.2
.3
and recalling the definition of
\phi_{0}
, we get

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+&\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(\gamma_{0},\phi_{0})\big(\|\bar{z}^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bar{z}^{n-1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\\
&+C(\gamma_{0},\phi)\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+C(\gamma_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\|h^{n-1}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

(A.9)

where we used Sobolev imbedding theorem to estimate

\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\bar{a}_{ij}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1}(\Omega)}\leq\phi.

Here and in the sequel,
C

the unknown function

To derive an estimate for
\partial_{t}^{s}z
and
\nabla\partial_{t}^{s-1}z

[
8
, Section A.2]
.
First, we select
0<\delta<\varepsilon<T

Convolving Equations (
A.7
) for
n=s-1

with
\phi_{\delta}

(\partial_{t}^{s}z)_{\delta}-\bar{a}_{ij}\big(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{t}^{s-2}z\big)_{\delta}=(h^{s-2})_{\delta}+\eta^{s}(\cdot,\cdot;\delta)

with a correction term

\eta^{s}(t,\cdot;\delta)=\big(\bar{a}_{ij}\partial_{t}^{s-2}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z\big)_{\delta}-\bar{a}_{ij}\big(\partial_{t}^{s-2}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z\big)_{\delta}\text{ for }t\in[0,T].

Differentiating Equation (
A.10
) w.r.t.

\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}^{s}z)_{\delta}-\partial_{t}\big(\bar{a}_{ij}\big(\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{t}^{s-2}z\big)_{\delta}\big)=\partial_{t}(h^{s-2})_{\delta}+\partial_{t}\eta^{s}(\cdot,\cdot;\delta),

with
\partial_{t}^{s}z_{\delta}
,
applying Green’s formula and using Young’s inequality, we estimate

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\big(\|(\partial_{t}^{n}z)_{\delta}&(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bar{a}(t,\cdot)\nabla(\partial_{t}^{n-1}z)_{\delta}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\\

(A.11)

Here, we exploited the fact
(\partial_{t}z)_{\delta}=\partial_{t}z_{\delta}

is once weakly differentiable w.r.t.
x_{i}
.
Now, integrating Equation (
A.11
)
w.r.t.
t
over
[\varepsilon,T-\varepsilon]
,
letting
\delta
and then
\varepsilon
go to zero,
exploiting the regularity of

, applying Lemma
A.1

and using Assumption

.3 , we get

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{s}&z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{s-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(\gamma_{0},\phi_{0})\big(\|\bar{z}^{s}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bar{z}^{s-1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\\
&+C(\gamma_{0},\phi)(1+T^{-1/2})\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+C(\gamma_{0})T^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t}\|h^{s-2}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

(A.12)

Combining Equations (
A.9
) and
(
A.12
)
leads to

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{s}&\big(\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{m-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\leq C(\gamma_{0},\phi_{0})\Lambda_{0}\\
&+C(\gamma_{0},\phi)(1+T^{-1/2})\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&+C(\gamma_{0})\sum_{n=1}^{s-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|h^{n-1}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau+C(\gamma_{0})T^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}h^{s-2}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

(A.13)

Using Sobolev imbedding theorem

W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)\text{ for }d\leq 3,

we can estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij})\partial_{t}^{n-1-m}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\min\{n-1,1\}}\|\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{n-1-m}z\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=\min\{n,2\}}^{n-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{n-1-m}z\|_{W^{2,4}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\phi)\|\bar{D}^{n}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\sum_{m=\min\{n,2\}}^{n-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{n-1-m}z\|_{W^{3,2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\phi)\|\bar{D}^{n}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\sum_{m=\min\{n,2\}}^{n-1}\|\partial_{t}^{m}\bar{a}_{ij}\|_{H^{s-1-m}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\bar{D}^{n-m+2}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\phi)\|\bar{D}^{s-1}z\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Recalling Equation (
A.8
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\|h^{n-1}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau&\leq t\max_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}\bar{f}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&+C(\phi)\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\end{split}

(A.14)

for
n=1,\dots,s-1
and
t\in[0,T]
.
Similarly, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}h^{s-2}(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}^{s-1}\bar{f}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau+C(\phi)\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(A.15)

Now, combining Equation (
A.13
)
as well as Equations (
A.14
) and
(
A.15
), we arrive at

\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{s}\big(&\|\partial_{t}^{n}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq C(\gamma_{0},\phi_{0})\Lambda_{0}+C(\gamma_{0},\phi)(1+T^{1/2}+T^{-1/2})\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\text{ for }t\in[0,T].\end{split}

(A.16)

To finish the proof, we need to establish estimates for the remaining derivatives.
For
n=1,\dots,s-1
, consider Equation (
A.7
).
Application of the elliptic regularity (viz. Assumption
A.2
.4)
with
m=s-n-1
yields

\begin{split}\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m+2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\gamma_{1}\big(&\|\partial_{t}^{n+1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|h^{n-1}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
+&\|\partial_{t}^{n-1}z(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\text{ for }t\in[0,T].\end{split}

(A.17)

Using Assumption
A.2
.1,
Sobolev imbedding theorem and Jensen’s inequality
and applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to the second term
on the right-hand side of Equation (
A.17
),
we obtain

\begin{split}\|h^{n-1}&(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\\
&\leq C(\phi_{0})\Lambda_{0}+CT\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\partial_{t}\big((\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{a}_{ij})\partial_{t}^{n-1-k}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}z\big)\big\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}(\tau,\cdot)\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq C(\phi_{0})\Lambda_{0}+C(\phi)T\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{split}

(A.18)

Note that this estimate is only true if
s\geq 3
,
which is trivially satisfied due to Assumption
A.2
.
Combining Equations (
A.16
),
(
A.17
) and
(
A.18
)
finally yields

\begin{split}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&\leq C(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\phi_{0})\Lambda_{0}\\
&+C(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\phi)(1+T^{1/2}+T+T^{-1/2})\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{D}^{s}z(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\end{split}

for any
t\in[0,T]
.
The claim is now a direct consequence of Gronwall’s inequality.
∎

Remark A.4
.

It should be pointed out that our proof differs from that of Jiang and Racke
[
8
]

as we can carry it out at the energy level
s\geq[\tfrac{d}{2}]+2

whereas Jiang and Racke
[
8
]
require
s\geq[\tfrac{d}{2}]+3
.
This “improvement” is possible since Theorem
A.3

is applied to a quasilinear wave equation
with the quasilinearity depending on the function itself and not its gradient.
A comment on this issue can also be found in
[
9
, Remark 14.4]
.

A.2
Linear Heat Equation

In this appendix section, we consider an initial-boundary-value problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions for
the linear homogeneous isotropic heat equation reading as

\displaystyle\theta_{t}(t,x)-a\triangle\theta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}(t,x)\phantom{0}\text{ for }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,

(A.19a)

\displaystyle\theta(t,x)

\displaystyle=0\phantom{\bar{g}(t,x)}\text{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,

(A.19b)

\displaystyle\theta(0,x)

\displaystyle=\theta^{0}(x)\phantom{0}\;\,\text{ for }x\in\Omega.

(A.19c)

We present a well-posedness result for Equations (
A.19a
)–(
A.19c
).
In contrast to
[
8
, Chapter A.3]
, our proof is based on the operator semigroup theory,
in particular, the maximal
L^{2}
-regularity theory, which is equivalent to analyticity of the semigroup generated by Dirichlet-Laplacian.
A different technique is employed here to obtain a higher solution regularity needed for the fixed-point iteration in Theorem
4.5
.
Besides, the topologies used for the data
(\theta^{0},\bar{g})
and the solution
\theta
differ from those in
[
8
, Chapter A.3]
.

Assumption A.5
.

Let
s\geq 2
and
a>0
.
Assume the following assumptions are satisfied.

1.

Right-hand side regularity:
For
k=0,1,\dots,s-1
,

\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{s-1-k}(\Omega)\big)
.
Recall
H^{0}_{0}(\Omega)\equiv H^{0}(\Omega):=L^{2}(\Omega)
.

2.

Regularity and compatibility conditions:
For
k=0,1,\dots,s-2
, let

\bar{\theta}^{k}\in H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\bar{\theta}^{s-1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\bar{\theta}^{k}
’s are given by

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}^{l}(x)=a^{l}\triangle^{l}\theta^{0}(x)+\sum_{n=0}^{l-1}a^{n}\triangle^{n}\theta^{0}(x)\,\partial_{t}^{l-1-n}\bar{g}(0,x)\text{ for }x\in\Omega\text{ and }l=0,\dots,s-1.

Theorem A.6
.

Under Assumption
A.5
,
the initial-boundary-value problem
(
A.19a
)–(
A.19c
)
possesses a unique classical solution satisfying

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{k}\theta

\displaystyle\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\text{ for }k=0,\dots,s-2,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{s-1}\theta

\displaystyle\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\text{ and }\partial_{t}^{s}\theta\in L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{t}^{s-1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|\triangle\partial_{t}^{s-1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{s}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Theta_{0},

where

\Theta_{0}=(1+T)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\|\bar{\theta}^{k}\|_{H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\bar{\theta}^{s-1}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\max_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1-k}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big).

Proof.

Let
A:=\triangle_{D}
denote the
L^{2}
-realization of the Dirichlet-Laplacian with the domain

D(A):=\big\{\theta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\,\big|\,\triangle\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)\big\}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where the latter identity follows by standard elliptic regularity theory.
(Note the difference in sign over Sections
4
and
5
.)
Using Lax & Milgram lemma to prove the resolvent identity

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(-\infty,0]}\big\|\lambda(\lambda-A)^{-1}\big\|_{L(L^{2}(\Omega))}<\infty,

we conclude that
aA
generates a bounded analytic semigroup of angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Due to the Hilbert space structure,
[
10
, 1.7 Corollary]
further implies
aA
has the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity property.

Consider the solution map
\mathscr{S}
sending
(\tilde{\theta}^{0},\tilde{g})
to the (mild) solution
\tilde{\theta}
of

\tilde{\theta}_{t}-aA\tilde{\theta}=\tilde{g}\text{ in }(0,T),\quad\tilde{\theta}(0,\cdot)=\tilde{\theta}^{0}.

(A.20)

By classic
C_{0}
-semigroup theory and the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity theory, we have:

—

The mapping

\mathscr{S}\colon H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\to H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)

(A.21)

is well-defined as an isomorphism between the two spaces.

—

The mapping

\begin{split}\mathscr{S}\colon\big(H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\times&C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\to\\
&C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\end{split}

(A.22)

is well-defined and continuous in respective topologies.

Existence and uniqueness at basic level:

On the strength of Assumption
A.5
,
we (in particular) have
\theta^{0}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}
and
\bar{g}\in C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)
.
Hence, there exists a unique classical solution

\theta\in C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)

(A.23)

to Equations (
A.19a
)–(
A.19c
).

Higher regularity in time:

We argue by induction over
k=1,\dots,s-2
starting at
k=1
.
Applying
\partial_{t}^{k}
to Equation (
A.19a
)
and using Assumption
A.5
.2, we obtain (in distributional sense)

\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}^{k}\theta)-aA(\partial_{t}^{k}\theta)=\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}\text{ in }(0,T).

(A.24)

This motivates to consider Equation (
A.20
) with

\tilde{\theta}^{0}=\bar{\theta}^{k}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\text{ and }\tilde{g}=\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}\in C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

(A.25)

By Equation (
A.22
),

\tilde{\theta}\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

We now show the function

\bar{\theta}(t,\cdot)=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\bar{\theta}^{l}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}\tilde{\theta}(\tau,\cdot)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}t_{k-1}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{1}

coincides with
\partial_{t}^{k}\theta
.
By construction,
\bar{\theta}
satisfies

\partial_{t}(\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{\theta})-aA(\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{\theta})=\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}\text{ in }(0,T).

(A.26)

Subtracting Equation (
A.26
) from Equation (
A.24
),
multiplying with
\partial_{t}^{k-1}(\bar{\theta}-\theta)(t,\cdot)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and using Green’s formula, we get

\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\big\|\partial_{t}^{k-1}(\bar{\theta}-\theta)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+a\big\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{k-1}(\bar{\theta}-\theta)(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

(A.27)

This along with the fact
\partial_{t}^{l}\bar{\theta}(0,\cdot)\equiv\bar{\theta}^{l}\equiv\partial_{t}^{l}\theta(0,\cdot)
for
l=0,\dots,k-1

enables us to use the Gronwall’s inequality together with the fundamental theorem of calculus to deduce
\bar{\theta}\equiv\theta
.
Therefore, we have shown

\partial_{t}^{k}\theta\equiv\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{\theta}\equiv\tilde{\theta}\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big).

(A.28)

For
k=s-1
, a slightly modified argument needs to be utilized.
In this case, we only have

\tilde{\theta}^{0}=\theta^{s-1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\text{ and }\tilde{g}=\partial_{t}^{s-1}\bar{g}\in C^{0}\big([0,T],L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\hookrightarrow L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)

to plug into Equation (
A.20
).
Instead of Equation (
A.22
), we use (
A.21
) to infer

\tilde{\theta}\in H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

(A.29)

Defining

\bar{\theta}(t,\cdot)=\sum_{l=0}^{s-2}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\bar{\theta}^{l}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\dots\int_{0}^{t_{s-2}}\tilde{\theta}(\tau,\cdot)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}t_{s-2}\dots\mathrm{d}t_{1},

by the same kind of argument, we have
\bar{\theta}\equiv\theta
and, therefore,

\partial_{t}^{s-1}\theta\equiv\tilde{\theta}\in H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\hookrightarrow C^{0}\big(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

(A.30)

Higher regularity in space:

For
k=0,\dots,s-2
, applying
\partial_{t}^{k}
to Equation (
A.19a
), we observe

A\partial_{t}^{k}\theta=-\tfrac{1}{a}\partial_{t}^{k+1}\theta+\tfrac{1}{a}\partial_{t}^{k}\bar{g}.

(A.31)

Hence, using the fact
A
is an isomorphism between
H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{s-1-k}(\Omega)
along with
Assumption
A.5
.1 and Equations (
A.28
), (
A.30
),
we inductively obtain (beginning at
k=s-2
and going downward to
k=0
)

\partial_{t}^{k}\theta\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{s+1-k}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\text{ for }k=0,1,\dots,s-2.

The ‘remaining’ case
k=s-1
has already been treated in the previous step so that

\partial_{t}^{s-1}\theta\in C^{0}\big([0,T],H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap H^{1}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

Energy estimate.

The energy estimate easily follows from the solution operator continuity.
∎

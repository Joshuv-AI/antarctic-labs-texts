Twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras and NS- 3 3 -Lie algebras
Shuai Hou, Yunhe Sheng

Abstract
. In this paper, first we introduce the notion of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a 3 3 -Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} with a representation on V V . We show that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator induces a 3-Lie algebra structure on V V , which represents on 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . By this fact, we define the cohomology of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator and study infinitesimal deformations of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator using the second cohomology group. Then we introduce the notion of an NS- 3 3 -Lie algebra, which produces a 3-Lie algebra with a representation on itself. We show that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator induces an NS- 3 3 -Lie algebra naturally. Thus NS- 3 3 -Lie algebras can be viewed as the underlying algebraic structures of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3 3 -Lie algebras. Finally we show that a Nijenhuis operator on a 3-Lie algebra gives rise to a representation of the deformed 3-Lie algebra and a 2-cocycle. Consequently, the identity map will be a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on the deformed 3-Lie algebra. We also introduce the notion of a Reynolds operator on a 3 3 -Lie algebra, which can serve as a special case of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Cohomology of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras and 3-Lie algebras

The notion of a Rota-Baxter operator on an associative algebra was introduced in the probability study of G. Baxter and later found important applications in the Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization of quantum field theory
[
7
]
. For further details, see
[
16
,
17
]
. A Rota-Baxter operator on a Lie algebra is naturally the operator form of a classical
r
-matrix
[
28
]
under certain conditions.
To better understand such connection in general, Kupershmidt introduced the notion of an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator (also called
a relative Rota-Baxter operator
[
25
]
or a generalized Rota-Baxter operator
[
33
]
)
on a Lie algebra in
[
20
]
. Recently the deformation theory and the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators on both Lie and associative algebras are studied in
[
8
,
31
]
.

3-Lie
algebras and more generally,
n
-Lie
algebras (also called Filippov algebras)
[
15
]
, have attracted attention
from both mathematics and physics. See the review article
[
11
,
24
]
for more details.
In
[
2
]
, The authors introduced the notion of a Rota-Baxter operator on a 3-Lie algebra and studied its relation with Rota-Baxter Lie algebras. To construct solutions of the classical 3-Lie Yang-Baxter equation, the authors introduced a more general notion,
an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator (also called a relative Rota-Baxter operator) on a 3-Lie algebra with respect to a representation in
[
1
]
. In
[
32
]
, the authors constructed a Lie 3-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are relative Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras, and studied cohomologies and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras. See
[
14
]
for more details about deformations of 3-Lie algebras.

1.2.
Nijenhuis operators, Reynolds operators and NS-algebras

Nijenhuis operators play an important role in deformation
theories due to their relationship with trivial infinitesimal
deformations. There are interesting applications of Nijenhuis operators on associative algebras and Lie algebras such as constructing biHamiltonian systems to study the integrability of nonlinear evolution equations
[
4
,
12
]
. The notion of a Nijenhuis operator on an
n
-Lie algebra was introduced in
[
23
]
to study deformations of
n
-Lie algebras. Then it was applied to study product structures and complex structures on a 3-Lie algebra in
[
29
]
. See
[
35
]
for a different notion of Nijenhuis operators on 3-Lie algebras.

Reynolds operators were introduced by Reynolds in
[
27
]
in the study of fluctuation theory in fluid dynamics.
In
[
18
]
, the author coined the concept of the Reynolds operator and regarded the operator as a mathematical subject in general. In
[
34
]
, the authors provided examples and properties of the Reynolds operators and studied the free Reynolds algebras.

NS-algebras were introduced by Leroux in
[
22
]
. In
[
21
]
, the authors studied the relationship between the category of Nijenhuis algebras and the category of NS-algebras. Uchino defined the notion of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras and observed that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator induces an NS-algebra in
[
33
]
. In
[
10
]
, Das studied the cohomology of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras and the cohomology of NS-algebras and gave various application. Das also introduced the notions of twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators on Lie algebras and NS-Lie algebras, and showed that NS-Lie algebras are the underlying algebraic structures of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras in
[
9
]
.

1.3.
Main results and outline of the paper

In this paper, we introduce the notion of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator (by a 2-cocycle) on a 3-Lie algebra, and study its cohomology theory and the underlying algebraic structure. Note that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator
T:V\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
on a 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation on
V
naturally induces a 3-Lie algebra structure on
V
with a representation on
\mathfrak{g}
. The corresponding cohomology is defined to be the cohomology of the relative Rota-Baxter operator. As applications, we use the second cohomology group to study infinitesimal deformations of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras. We introduce the notion of an NS-3-Lie algebra and show that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a 3-Lie algebra naturally induces an NS-3-Lie algebra. Thus, NS-3-Lie algebras can be viewed as the underlying algebraic structures of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras. We study two special classes of twisted Rota-Baxter operators on 3-Lie algebras:

•

We show that the identity map is a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on the deformed 3-Lie algebra by a Nijenhuis operator;

•

We introduce the notion of a Reynolds operator on a
3
-Lie algebra, which turns out to be a special twisted Rota-Baxter operator.

We also give concrete examples of Reynolds operators on some infinite dimensional 3-Lie algebras including the
\omega_{\infty}

3
-Lie algebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a 3-Lie algebra and establish the corresponding cohomology theory. Applications are given to study infinitesimal deformations. In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of an NS-3-Lie algebra and show that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a 3-Lie algebra naturally induces an NS-3-Lie algebra. In Section
4
, first we show that a Nijenhuis operator on a 3-Lie algebra induces a representation of the deformed 3-Lie algebra and a 2-cocycle. Consequently, the identity map is a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on the deformed 3-Lie algebra. Then we introduce the notion of a Reynolds operator on a
3
-Lie algebra, which turns out to be a special twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Finally we give examples of Reynolds operators on some infinite dimensional 3-Lie algebras.

2.1.
Twisted Rota-Baxter operators on
3
-Lie algebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a 3-Lie algebra and show that a linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph of
T
is a subalgebra of the
\Phi
-twisted semidirect product 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Phi}V
, where
\Phi
is a 2-cocycle of the 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
V
. Consequently, a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
T
induces a
3
-Lie algebra structure on
V.
We also use a
T
-admissible
1
-cocycle
f
to construct a new
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
T_{f}
, and the
3
-Lie algebra structures on
V
induced by
T
and
T_{f}
are isomorphic.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
15
]
)

A
3-Lie algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
together with a skew-symmetric linear map
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:\otimes^{3}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, such that for
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq 5
, the following
Fundamental Identity
holds:

(1)

~[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

A
derivation
on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

(2)

\displaystyle D[x_{1},x_{2},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[Dx_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{1},Dx_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{1},x_{2},Dx_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}.

For
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},
define
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{3}=[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then (
1
) is equivalent to that
\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}
is a derivation, i.e.

\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x_{3},x_{4},\mathrm{ad}_{x_{1},x_{2}}x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
19
]
)

A
representation
of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a pair
(V;\rho)
, where
V
is a vector space, and
\rho:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map satisfying, for all
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in\mathfrak{g},

(3)

\displaystyle[\rho(x_{1},x_{2}),\rho(x_{3},x_{4})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

(4)

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\rho(x_{3},x_{4})+\rho(x_{2},x_{3})\rho(x_{1},x_{4})+\rho(x_{3},x_{1})\rho(x_{2},x_{4}).

See
[
13
]
for representations of vector product
n
-Lie algebras.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. The linear map
\mathrm{ad}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
defines a representation
of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, which is called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(V;\rho)
be a representation of a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Denote by

C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V):=\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{(n-1)}\wedge\mathfrak{g},V),\quad(n\geq 1),

which is the space of
n
-cochains.
The coboundary operator
{\rm d}:C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)\rightarrow C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g};V)
is defined by

\displaystyle({\rm d}f)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k-1},[x_{j},y_{j},x_{k}]_{\mathfrak{g}}\wedge y_{k}+x_{k}\wedge[x_{j},y_{j},y_{k}]_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{X}_{k+1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{j},y_{j},x_{n+1}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}\rho(x_{j},y_{j})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mathfrak{X}_{j}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\Big(\rho(y_{n},x_{n+1})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},x_{n})+\rho(x_{n+1},x_{n})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n-1},y_{n})\Big),

for all
~\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},~i=1,2,\cdots,n~and~x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}.
It was proved in
[
5
,
30
]
that
{\rm d}\circ{\rm d}=0.
Thus,
(\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V),{\rm d})
is a cochain complex.

Definition 2.4
.

The
cohomology
of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
V
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
(\oplus_{n=1}^{+\infty}C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V),{\rm d})
. Denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)
and
\mathcal{B}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)

the set of
n
-cocycles and the set of
n
-coboundaries, respectively. The
n
-th cohomology group is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V)/\mathcal{B}_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g};V).

A
2
-cochain
\Phi\in C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g};V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g},V)
is a
2-cocycle
on
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in
(V;\rho)
if
\Phi
satisfies

(5)

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\Phi([x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{4},x_{5})-\Phi(x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{5})-\Phi(x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle+\rho(x_{1},x_{2})\Phi(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})-\rho(x_{3},x_{4})\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{5})-\rho(x_{4},x_{5})\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})-\rho(x_{5},x_{3})\Phi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

for all
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},1\leq i\leq 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}.
For any
2
-cocycle
\Phi\in C_{\mathsf{3Lie}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g};V),

there is a
3
-Lie algebra structure on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
of
vector spaces, defined by

(6)

\displaystyle[(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)]_{\Phi}=([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(y,z)u+\rho(z,x)v+\Phi(x,y,z)),

for
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in V.
This
3
-Lie algebra is called the
\Phi
-twisted semi-direct product
3
-Lie algebra
and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Phi}V.

In the sequel,
\Phi
will always denote a
2
-cocycle.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation of

A linear operator

.

In

, the authors introduced the notion of a Rota-Baxter operator of weight

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation. Suppose that
f:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow V
is an invertible linear map. Then
T=f^{-1}:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator with
\Phi=-{\rm d}f.

Proof.

By a direct computation, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\Phi(Tu,Tv,Tw)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-({\rm d}f)(Tu,Tv,Tw)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho(Tu,Tv)w-\rho(Tv,Tw)u-\rho(Tw,Tu)v+f([Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

By applying
T
to both sides of (
8
), we obtain the identity (
7
).
∎

The identity (
7
) can be characterized by the graph of
T
being a subalgebra.

Theorem 2.8
.

A linear map
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho)
if and only if the graph
Gr(T)=\{(Tu,u)|u\in V\}
is a subalgebra of the
\Phi
-twisted semi-direct product
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Phi}V.

Proof.

Let
T:V\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map.
For all
u,v,w\in V,
we have

\displaystyle[(Tu,u),(Tv,v),(Tw,w)]_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\Phi(Tu,Tv,Tw)),

which implies that the graph
Gr(T)=\{(Tu,u)|u\in V\}
is a subalgebra of the

\Phi
-twisted semi-direct product
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\Phi}V
if and only if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}=T\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\Phi(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big),

which means that
T
is a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator.
∎

Since

and

are isomorphic as vector spaces, we get the following result immediately.

.

Let

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a

-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation. Then
\Psi_{f}
is an isomorphism from the
\Phi
-twisted semi-direct product

-twisted semi-direct product

For all

\displaystyle\Psi_{f}[(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)]_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{f}([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(z,x)v+\rho(y,z)u+\Phi(x,y,z))

\displaystyle([x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x,y)w+\rho(z,x)v+\rho(y,z)u+\Phi(x,y,z)+f[x,y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}_{T}(V;\mathfrak{g})=\mathcal{Z}^{n}(V;\mathfrak{g})/\mathcal{B}^{n}(V;\mathfrak{g}),\quad n\geq 1.

.

-twisted Rota-Baxter operators.

Definition 2.17
.

Let

and
T^{\prime}
be
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operators on a

-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation

consists a

-Lie algebra homomorphism

and a linear map

\displaystyle\phi\circ T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\prime}\circ\psi,

(19)

\displaystyle\psi(\rho(x,y)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\phi(x),\phi(y))(\psi(u)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V,

\displaystyle\psi\circ\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi\circ(\phi\otimes\phi\otimes\phi).

\phi
and

is called an

from

to

.

Definition 2.18
.

Let

be a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator

if and only if for any

(21)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}w,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)u+\rho(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv)w+\rho(Tw,\mathfrak{T}u)v+\rho(\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)u

\displaystyle+\rho(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v)w+\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,Tw)+\Phi(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)+\Phi(Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{T}\Big(\rho(Tu,Tv)w+\rho(Tv,Tw)u+\rho(Tw,Tu)v+\Phi(Tu,Tv,Tw)\Big),

(22)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,T(w)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w,T(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\mathfrak{T}w,\mathfrak{T}u,T(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}\Big(\rho(Tw,\mathfrak{T}u)v+\rho(\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)u+\rho(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v)w+\rho(\mathfrak{T}w,Tu)v+\rho(Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)u

\displaystyle+T\Big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v)w+\rho(\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)u+\rho(\mathfrak{T}w,\mathfrak{T}u)v

\displaystyle+\Phi(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)+\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)+\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)\Big),

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)+\mathfrak{T}\Big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v)w+\rho(\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)u+\rho(\mathfrak{T}w,\mathfrak{T}u)v\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{T}\Big(\Phi(Tu,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)+\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,Tv,\mathfrak{T}w)+\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,Tw)\Big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}\Phi(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}v,\mathfrak{T}w)=0.

Note that (

) means that
\mathfrak{T}
is a

-cocycle of the

-twisted Rota-Baxter operator

defines a cohomology class in

.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
T
be a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho).
Two one-parameter infinitesimal deformations
T^{1}_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
T^{2}_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are said to be

if there exist
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
such that
({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{X}},{\rm{Id}}_{V}+t\rho(\mathfrak{X})+t\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T))
is a homomorphism from
T^{1}_{t}
to
T^{2}_{t}
, where
\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T):V\rightarrow V
is defined by

\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T)(u)=\Phi(\mathfrak{X},Tu),\quad\forall u\in V.

In particular, a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}
of a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator

is said to be
trivial
if there exist
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathfrak{g}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
such that
({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{X}},{\rm{Id}}_{V}+t\rho(\mathfrak{X})+t\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T))
is a homomorphism from
T_{t}
to
T.

Let
({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{X}},{\rm{Id}}_{V}+t\rho(\mathfrak{X})+t\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T))
be a homomorphism from
T^{1}_{t}
to
T^{2}_{t}.

By (

\quad({\rm{Id}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathrm{ad}_{\mathfrak{X}})(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1})(u)=(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2})({\rm{Id}}_{V}+t\rho(\mathfrak{X})+t\Phi(\mathfrak{X},T))(u),

which implies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{}\mathfrak{T}_{1}(u)-\mathfrak{T}_{2}(u)&=&T\rho(\mathfrak{X})u-[\mathfrak{X},Tu]_{\mathfrak{g}}+T\Phi(\mathfrak{X},Tu),\\
{}[\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{T}_{1}(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}&=&\mathfrak{T}_{2}(\rho(\mathfrak{X})u+\Phi(\mathfrak{X},Tu)).\end{array}\right.

Now we are ready to give the main result in this section.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
T
be a
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a
3
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to a representation
(V;\rho).

If two one-parameter infinitesimal deformations

and
T^{2}_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are equivalent, then
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}

are in the same cohomology class in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{T}(V;\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see from the first condition of (

) that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{2}(u)+(\partial\mathfrak{X})(u),\quad\forall u\in V,

which implies that
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are in the same cohomology class.
∎

3.

-Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of an NS-

-Lie algebra. An NS-

-Lie algebra gives rise to a 3-Lie algebra and a representation on itself. We show that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator induces an NS-

-Lie algebra. Thus NS-

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a vector space together with two linear maps

in which
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]
is skew-symmetric. The triple

is called an

3
-Lie algebra
if the following identities hold:

(28)

\displaystyle\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{x_{2},x_{1},x_{3}\},

(29)

\displaystyle\{x_{1},x_{2},\{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x_{3},x_{4},\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}\}\}+\{\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right\rrbracket,x_{4},x_{5}\}

\displaystyle+\{x_{3},\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}\right\rrbracket,x_{5}\},

(30)

\displaystyle\{\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\right\rrbracket,x_{4},x_{5}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}}\{x_{1},x_{2},\{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}\},

(31)

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2},\left\llbracket x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\right\rrbracket]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}}[x_{3},x_{4},\left\llbracket x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}\right\rrbracket]-\{x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]\}

\displaystyle+\circlearrowleft_{x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}}\{x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]\},

for all

(32)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\{x,y,z\}+[x,y,z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in A,

Remark 3.2
.

Let
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])

Thus, NS-

\displaystyle-\{x_{4},x_{5},\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}\}-\{x_{4},x_{5},\{x_{2},x_{3},x_{1}\}\}-\{x_{4},x_{5},\{x_{3},x_{1},x_{2}\}\}-\{x_{4},x_{5},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]\}

\displaystyle 0,

which implies that

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

be a

-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a

-Lie algebra

(33)

\displaystyle\qquad\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}=\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{3}\quad and\quad[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}]=\Phi(Tu_{1},Tu_{2},Tu_{3}),\quad\forall u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in V,

For any

\displaystyle\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\}=\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{3}=-\rho(Tu_{2},Tu_{1})u_{3}=-\{u_{2},u_{1},u_{3}\}.

Furthermore, for

), (

) and (

), we have

\displaystyle\{u_{1},u_{2},\{u_{3},u_{4},u_{5}\}\}-\{u_{3},u_{4},\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{5}\}\}-\{\left\llbracket u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\right\rrbracket,u_{4},u_{5}\}-\{u_{3},\left\llbracket u_{1},u_{2},u_{4}\right\rrbracket,u_{5}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u_{1},u_{2},\rho(Tu_{3},Tu_{4})u_{5}\}-\{u_{3},u_{4},\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{5}\}

\displaystyle\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})\rho(Tu_{3},Tu_{4})u_{5}-\rho(Tu_{3},Tu_{4})\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{5}

\displaystyle-\rho\Big(Tu_{3},T\rho(Tu_{1},Tu_{2})u_{4}+T\rho(Tu_{2},Tu_{4})u_{1}+T\rho(Tu_{4},Tu_{1})u_{2}+T\Phi(Tu_{1},Tu_{2},Tu_{4})\Big)u_{5}

\displaystyle 0.

\displaystyle\{\left\llbracket u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\right\rrbracket,u_{4},u_{5}\}-\circlearrowleft_{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}}\{u_{1},u_{2},\{u_{3},u_{4},u_{5}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
T
and

be
\Phi
-twisted Rota-Baxter operators on a

-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a representation

and

For all

)-(

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(\rho(Tu,Tv)w)=\rho(\phi(Tu),\phi(Tv))\psi(w)=\rho(T^{\prime}\psi(u),T^{\prime}\psi(v))\psi(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\psi(u),\psi(v),\psi(w)\}^{\prime},

4.2.

-Lie algebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of a Reynolds operator on a

-Lie algebra, which turns out to be a special twisted Rota-Baxter operator. The relation between Reynolds operators and derivations on

-Lie algebras are investigated.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a

if

(43)

[Rx,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R\Big([Rx,Ry,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Rx,y,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[Rx,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big),\quad\forall~x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, a
3
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a Reynolds operator
R
is called a
Reynolds
3
-Lie algebra
. We denote it by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},R).

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{1},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}},R_{1})
and
(\mathfrak{g}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}_{2}},R_{2})
be Reynolds
3
-Lie algebras. A linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is called a

of Reynolds

-Lie algebras if
\phi
is a
3
-Lie algebra homomorphism and
\phi\circ R_{1}=R_{2}\circ\phi
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},R)
be a Reynolds

-Lie algebra. Define a multiplication
[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{R}
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

(44)

[x,y,z]_{R}=[Rx,Ry,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Rx,y,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[Rx,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then

(a)

[Rx,Ry,Rz]_{\mathfrak{g}}=R([x,y,z]_{R})
;

(b)

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{R})
is a
3
-Lie algebra;

(c)

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{R},R)
is a Reynolds
3
-Lie algebra;

(d)

R
is a Reynolds

-Lie algebra homomorphism from

to

Proof.

It follows directly from (

).

Obviously,

is skew-symmetric.
For

by (

) and (

), we have

\displaystyle[x_{1},x_{2},[x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{R}]_{R}-[[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{R},x_{4},x_{5}]_{R}-[x_{3},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{4}]_{R},x_{5}]_{R}-[x_{3},x_{4},[x_{1},x_{2},x_{5}]_{R}]_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Rx_{1},Rx_{2},[Rx_{3},Rx_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Rx_{1},Rx_{2},[x_{3},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Rx_{1},Rx_{2},[Rx_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+[x_{1},Rx_{2},[Rx_{3},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Rx_{1},x_{2},[Rx_{3},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-[[Rx_{1},Rx_{2},x_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[[Rx_{1},x_{2},Rx_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[[x_{1},Rx_{2},Rx_{3}]_{\mathfrak{g}},Rx_{4},Rx_{5}]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle 0.

-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

By (

u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle(D+\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Id}})[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[(D+\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Id}})u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,(D+\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Id}})v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,v,(D+\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Id}})w]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[u,v,w]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

For convenience, we denote
P=D+\frac{1}{2}{\rm{Id}}.

If
P
is invertible, we put
Pu=x,Pv=y~\mbox{and}~Pw=z,

we get

\displaystyle[P^{-1}x,P^{-1}y,P^{-1}z]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{-1}\Big([x,P^{-1}y,P^{-1}z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[P^{-1}x,y,P^{-1}z]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+[P^{-1}x,P^{-1}y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[P^{-1}x,P^{-1}y,P^{-1}z]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big),

which implies that
P^{-1}
is a Reynolds operator. The proof is finished.
∎

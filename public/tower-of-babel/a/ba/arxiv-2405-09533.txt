Five-dimensional spinor helicity for all masses and spins
Andrzej Pokraka, Smita Rajan, Lecheng Ren, Anastasia Volovich, W. Wayne Zhao

Abstract
​​ We develop a spinor helicity formalism for five-dimensional scattering amplitudes of any mass and spin configuration. While five-dimensional spinor helicity variables have been previously studied in the context of 𝒩 = 2 , 4 \mathcal{N}=2,4 supersymmetric Yang-Mills scattering amplitudes with spin less than two [ 1 ] , we propose an alternative viewpoint that stems from d d -dimensional spinor helicity variables avoiding the use of the exceptional low-dimensional isomorphism SO ⁡ ( 4 , 1 ) ≅ USp ⁡ ( 2 , 2 ) \SO(4,1)\cong\USp(2,2) and the decomposition of a massive momentum into the sum of two massless momenta. By enumerating all possible independent little group tensors, we systematically build the full space of five-dimensional three-point tree-level scattering amplitudes for any configuration of spins and masses. Furthermore, we provide a prescription for computing the high energy limit of scattering amplitudes written in our spinor helicity variables. We also expect that our formalism will be applicable to effective field theories with higher spin, in particular, the scattering of highly spinning black holes in five dimensions.

4
High energy limit

In this section, we provide a prescription for taking the high energy limit of the massive spinor helicity variables (section

In particular, intricate cancellations are often required to arrive at gauge invariant amplitudes in the high energy limit (see (

Lastly, at the end of section

4.1
High energy limit of the spinor helicity variables

Since any massive momentum
p^{\mu}
(
p^{2}=-m^{2}

k^{\mu},q^{\mu}

p^{\mu}=k^{\mu}+m^{2}q^{\mu}\,,\qquad k\cdot q=-\frac{1}{2}\,,

one can decompose the massive spinor helicity variables into massless
k
and

\displaystyle|p_{\alpha}\rangle=(|k_{I}\rangle+m|q_{I}\rangle)U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}\,,\qquad|p_{\dot{\alpha}}]=(|k_{I}\rangle-m|q_{I}\rangle)U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(113)

where

\displaystyle\langle k_{I}|q_{J}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}=\varepsilon_{IJ}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}=U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(114)

Here,

and
U^{I}_{\dot{\alpha}}
are a little group conversion matrices that transform the
\SO(4)

\SO(3)

\displaystyle U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}\ U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\beta}}\ C^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}\ U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\beta}}\ \epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}}=-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{IJ}=-\frac{1}{2}C^{IJ}\,,

(115)

\displaystyle U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}\ U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\beta}\ C^{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle=U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}\ U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\beta}\ \epsilon^{\beta\alpha}=-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{IJ}=-\frac{1}{2}C^{IJ}\,.

(116)

While the

To find an explicit parameterization for the

and

p^{\mu}=(E,\sqrt{E^{2}-m^{2}},0,0,0)\,.

(117)

\displaystyle k^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(E+\sqrt{E^{2}-m^{2}},E+\sqrt{E^{2}-m^{2}},0,0,0\right)\,,

4.2
High energy limit of amplitudes

In this section, we consider several examples to illustrate how to take the high energy limit of massive amplitudes. In particular, we demonstrate that intricate cancellations are often needed to produce gauge invariant massless amplitudes.

Example (high energy limit of
\bm{\chi\chi\phi}
-scattering):

Consider
\chi\chi\phi
-scattering where
\chi
is a massive fermion (
s_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\bar{s}_{1}=0
) and
\phi
is a massless scalar.
According to Section
3.3
, there is only one independent term whose high energy limit is straightforward to take

A(\chi,\chi,\phi)=A(\mathbf{1}^{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbf{2}^{\frac{1}{2}},{3}^{0})=\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|\mathbf{2}_{\beta}\rangle\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\langle k_{1,I}|k_{2,J}\rangle U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\beta}\,.

(120)

Example (high energy limit of
\bm{W\phi\phi}
-scattering):

A less trivial example is the high-energy limit of a massive vector boson (
W
) with two massless scalars (
\phi
).
Like the previous example, there is one independent term.
However, this time, the leading term vanishes

\displaystyle A(W,\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=A(\mathbf{1}^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}},{2}^{0},3^{0})=\,\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|3|\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}]

\displaystyle\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\left(\langle k_{1,I}|3|k_{1,J}\rangle+m\ \langle q_{1,I}|3|k_{1,J}\rangle-m\ \langle k_{1,I}|3|q_{1,J}\rangle-m^{2}\ \langle q_{1,I}|3|q_{1,J}\rangle\right)U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=m\left[\langle q_{1,I}|3|k_{1,J}\rangle-\langle k_{1,I}|3|q_{1,J}\rangle\right]U^{I}_{\kern 4.09723pt\alpha}U^{J}_{\kern 4.09723pt\dot{\alpha}}+\mathcal{O}(m^{2})\,.

(121)

The above expression is unsatisfactory
since we use
|k_{1,I}\rangle
to label the massless state after taking the high energy limit.
Therefore, we need to express the spinor
|q_{1,I}\rangle
in terms of
|k_{1,I}\rangle
.

To do this, we use the following identity

{\vphantom{|p_{I}\rangle}}_{A}|q_{I}\rangle={\vphantom{|p_{I}\rangle}}_{A}|q_{J}\rangle\,\varepsilon^{JK}\,\langle q_{K}|k_{I}\rangle={\vphantom{|p_{I}\rangle}}_{A}|q|k_{I}\rangle\,,

(122)

where
q^{\mu}
should be regarded as a
reference momentum
for the massless momentum
k^{\mu}
with the associated reference spinor
|q_{I}\rangle
.
The spinor
|q_{I}\rangle
must satisfy (
114

This gauge freedom can be parameterized as

|q^{\prime}_{I}\rangle=|q_{J}\rangle+|k_{J}\rangle{L^{J}}_{I}\,,

(123)

where
L
is an arbitrary little group transformation.

Applying (
122
) to (
121
), one finds that the leading term in the high energy limit is a true massless amplitude that is gauge invariant

A(W,\phi,\phi)\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}m\left[\langle 1_{I}|q_{1}|3|1_{J}\rangle-\langle 1_{I}|3|q_{1}|1_{J}\rangle\right]+\mathcal{O}(m^{2})\,,

(124)

where we have dropped the
U
-matrices.
Gauge invariance is guaranteed because
(q_{1})_{A}^{\kern 4.09723ptB}
is always contracted into a
\langle 1_{I}|^{A}
.

As a sanity check, we provide an additional example illustrating how terms conspire to keep the leading term of the high energy limit gauge invariant.

Example (high energy limit of
\bm{WW\phi}
-scattering):

There are two independent terms in this case

\displaystyle A(W,W,\phi)=A(\mathbf{1}^{\frac{1}{2}+\bar{\frac{1}{2}}},\mathbf{2}^{\frac{1}{2}+\bar{\frac{1}{2}}},3^{0})=g_{1}\,{}_{\alpha_{1}}\langle{\bf 12}\rangle_{\beta_{1}}\,{}_{\dot{\alpha}_{2}}[\mathbf{12}]_{\dot{\beta}_{2}}+g_{2}\,{}_{\alpha_{1}}\langle\mathbf{12}]_{\dot{\beta}_{2}}\,{}_{\dot{\alpha}_{2}}[\mathbf{12}\rangle_{\beta_{1}}\,.

(125)

It is useful to make a change of basis from the couplings
(g_{1},g_{2})
to
(g_{+},g_{-})=(g_{1}+g_{2},g_{1}-g_{2})
and study the high energy limit of the

In the high energy limit, the

A(W,W,\phi)|_{g_{-}=0}\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\langle 1_{I_{1}}2_{J_{1}}\rangle\langle 1_{I_{2}}2_{J_{2}}\rangle+\langle 1_{I_{1}}2_{J_{2}}\rangle\langle 1_{I_{2}}2_{J_{1}}\rangle\sim u_{1,I_{1}}u_{1,I_{2}}u_{2,J_{1}}u_{2,J_{2}}\,,

which is exactly the three-point massless amplitude with spin (1,1,0).

On the other hand, the

\displaystyle A(W,W,\phi)|_{g_{+}=0}\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\,

\displaystyle\Big(\langle 1_{I_{1}}|2_{J_{1}}\rangle{+}m\langle 1_{I_{1}}|q_{1}|2_{J_{1}}\rangle{+}m\langle 1_{I_{1}}|q_{2}|2_{J_{1}}\rangle{+}m^{2}\langle q_{1,I_{1}}|q_{2,J_{1}}\rangle\Big)

(127)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\langle 1_{I_{2}}|2_{J_{2}}\rangle{-}m\langle 1_{I_{2}}|q_{1}|2_{J_{2}}\rangle{-}m\langle 1_{I_{2}}|q_{2}|2_{J_{2}}\rangle{+}m^{2}\langle q_{1,I_{2}}|q_{2,J_{2}}\rangle\Big)-(I_{1}\leftrightarrow I_{2})

\displaystyle A(W,W,\phi)|_{g_{+}=0}\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle-m\langle 1_{I_{1}}2_{J_{1}}\rangle\Big(\langle 1_{I_{2}}|q_{1}|2_{J_{2}}\rangle+{}_{I_{2}}\langle 1|q_{2}|2\rangle_{J_{2}}\Big)+(I_{1},J_{1})\leftrightarrow(I_{2},J_{2})

\displaystyle=\,-2m\,\langle 1_{I_{1}}|q_{1}|2|1_{I_{2}}\rangle\,\varepsilon_{J_{1},J_{2}}+2m\,\langle 2_{J_{1}}|q_{2}|3|2_{J_{2}}\rangle\,\varepsilon_{I_{1},I_{2}}\,.

Note that the first and second terms are precisely the spin-(1,0,0) and spin-(0,1,0) three-point massless amplitudes, respectively.
It is not surprising that the

Therefore, a four-dimensional vector should reduce to a vector
\,\oplus\,
scalar in three dimensions.

The mass dimension and spin drop.

As exemplified by (
124
) and (
128
), the mass dimension of a massive amplitude is not preserved by the high energy limit.
In addition, the spin of a massive amplitude can also drop after taking the high energy limit.
To see how mass dimension and spin drops arise, we consider the high energy limit of antisymmetrization and symmetrization of the little group indices of
\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|[\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}|
.

We start by computing the high energy limit of antisymmetrization

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|_{A}\,[\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}|_{B}-[\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}|_{A}\,\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|_{B}

\displaystyle\qquad\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\Big(\langle 1_{I}|_{A}+\langle 1_{I}|q|_{A}\Big)\Big(\langle 1_{J}|_{B}-\langle 1_{J}|q|_{B}\Big)-\Big(\langle 1_{J}|_{A}-\langle 1_{J}|q|_{A}\Big)\Big(\langle 1_{I}|_{B}+\langle 1_{I}|q|_{B}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\varepsilon_{IJ}(p_{1})_{AB}+m(\varepsilon_{1}^{i})_{AB}\cdot\Gamma_{IJ}^{i}+m^{2}\,\varepsilon_{IJ}(q)_{AB}\,,

(129)

where the
\Gamma^{i}_{IJ}
are
\SO(3)

\Gamma
-matrices.
The first term above the leading term that corresponds to the scalar component in the high energy limit.
The second term is subleading and corresponds to the vector component in the high energy limit.
The third subsubleading term is not gauge invariant and cannot appear as the leading high energy limit in a physical amplitude.

On the other hand, the symmetrization is

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|_{A}\,[\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}|_{B}{+}[\mathbf{1}_{\dot{\alpha}}|_{A}\,\langle\mathbf{1}_{\alpha}|_{B}

\displaystyle\overset{m\ll E}{\longrightarrow}\Big(\langle 1_{I}|_{A}\,\langle 1_{J}|_{B}+\langle 1_{J}|_{A}\,\langle 1_{I}|_{B}\Big)+m\Big({}_{A}|1|q|_{B}{-}{}_{A}|q|1|_{B}\Big)\varepsilon_{IJ}

\displaystyle\qquad+m^{2}\Big(\langle q_{I}|_{A}\,\langle q_{J}|_{B}+\langle q_{J}|_{A}\,\langle q_{I}|_{B}\Big)

(130)

where

While the mass dimension of massive amplitudes is not well defined, the mass dimension of their high energy limit is.
Therefore, one can categorize the possible independent terms in amplitudes by the number of symmetrizations/antisymmetrizations performed when taking the high energy limit.
Roughly speaking, each antisymmetrization lowers either the mass dimension or spin by one, while symmetrization preserves both mass dimension and spin (in some cases, it can do both).

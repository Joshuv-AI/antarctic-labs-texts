Real versus complex plane curves
Giulio Bresciani

Abstract
. We prove that a smooth, complex plane curve C C of odd degree can be defined by a polynomial with real coefficients if and only if C C is isomorphic to its complex conjugate. Counterexamples are known for curves of even degree. More generally, we prove that a plane curve C C over an algebraically closed field K K of characteristic 0 0 with field of moduli k C ⊂ K k_{C}\subset K is defined by a polynomial with coefficients in k ′ k^{\prime} , where k ′ / k C k^{\prime}/k_{C} is an extension with [ k ′ : k C ] ≤ 3 [k^{\prime}:k_{C}]\leq 3 and [ k ′ : k C ] ∣ deg C [k^{\prime}:k_{C}]\mid\operatorname{deg}C .

Introduction

The main purpose of this note is to prove the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
C
be a smooth complex plane curve of odd degree and

its complex conjugate. The following are equivalent.

(i)

There exist a homogeneous polynomial with real coefficients

(ii)

There exists a homogeneous polynomial

such that

(iii)

The curves

and

are isomorphic as abstract curves.

We find it surprising that such a fact has gone unnoticed until now. Still, we couldn’t find it in the literature. Counterexamples are known in even degree
[

The implications

are trivial, while

is a direct consequence of the well-known fact that a smooth plane curve of degree

The actual content of Theorem

Theorem

The index of a gerbe

In order to prove the main theorems, we need some abstract facts about the index of a finite étale gerbe. A reader not interested in such abstract facts might read the statement of Corollary
9

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite étale gerbe over a field
k
of arbitrary characteristic, write
k^{s}/k
for a separable closure. By
[

, there exists a section
b:\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\to\mathcal{G}
. Write
G=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{k^{s}},b)

1\to G\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\to 1.

Lemma 4
.

The gerbe
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to the quotient stack
[\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}/\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)]
, where
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)
acts on
\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}
with the projection
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the fact that the morphism

is a universal cover which is a Galois
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)
-cover.
∎

If we have a rational point
p:\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{G}
, by functoriality we get a section
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},p)
. An étale path on
\mathcal{G}_{k^{s}}
from
p
to
b
defines an isomorphism
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},p)\simeq\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)
which commutes with the projection to
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)
, hence we get a section
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)
well defined up to conjugation by elements of
G
(which identifies with the group of étale paths from
b
to itself in
\mathcal{G}_{k^{s}}
). Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}/k}
the set of sections
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\to\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)
modulo the action of
G
by conjugation, we have constructed a map

\mathcal{G}(k)\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}/k}.

Notice that
\mathcal{G}(k)
is a groupoid, whereas

is a set. The following lemma is well-known, see e.g.
[

, Proposition 9.3]

Lemma 5
.

The map
\mathcal{G}(k)\to\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}/k}
identifies
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}/k}
with the set of isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{G}(k)
.

Proof.

Consider two sections

with equal image in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}/k}
and write
p^{s},q^{s}
for the two compositions
\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\to\operatorname{Spec}k\to\mathcal{G}
; equivalently, there is an étale path
\gamma
from
p^{s}
to
q^{s}
in
\mathcal{G}_{k^{s}}
such that the natural map
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\xrightarrow{q_{*}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},q)
is equal to the composition
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)\xrightarrow{p_{*}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},q)\xrightarrow{\gamma^{*}}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},p)
. This means that the two natural actions of
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)
on the two fiber functors associated with
p^{s}
and
q^{s}
are identified by
\gamma
.

Consider the étale cover

, its fiber over

has a preferred object which

maps to a point in the fiber over

, which by construction is the set of isomorphisms

; we have thus constructed a preferred isomorphism

. Now consider the fibered product

, where the two maps

are
p
and

k
which represents isomorphisms between

and
q
; in particular, we have a preferred point
\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\to E
. The fact two actions of
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)
on the two fiber functors associated with
p^{s}
and

are identified by

.

Consider now a section

. Notice that

is a
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)
-torsor over
k

as the induced

-torsor

\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\times^{\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)}\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)=(\operatorname{Spec}k^{s}\times\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b))/\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)

using

. Since

is a section of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},b)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k^{s}/k)

∎

3.
Generalities about fields of moduli of plane curves

Before proving the main theorems, let us recall some concepts from previous works.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
with algebraic closure
K
and
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
a smooth plane curve over
K
. The case
\deg C\leq 2
is trivial. If
\deg C=3
, i.e.
C
is elliptic, it is well-known that
C
can be defined by a polynomial with coefficients in
k(j_{C})
, where

is the
j
-invariant. Two elliptic curves over
K
are isomorphic if and only if they have the same
j
-invariant, this implies that
k(j_{C})
is the field of moduli of
C
.

Assume
\deg C\geq 4
. The automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
of
C
coincides with the automorphism group of the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)

[
ACGH85
, Appendix A, §1, Exercise 18]
; this implies that the fields of moduli of
C
and of the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)
coincide
[
Brec
, Lemma 13]
.

Up to base change, we can assume that
k
is the field of moduli. There is a finite étale gerbe
\mathcal{G}
over
k
, called residual gerbe
[
BV
, §3.1]
, which classifies twisted forms of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)
; this gerbe also classifies twisted forms of
C
as an abstract curve
[
Brec
, Lemma 13]
. In particular,
C
is defined over the field of moduli if and only if
\mathcal{G}(k)\neq\emptyset
, and every model of
C
over
k
embeds in a unique Brauer–Severi surface over
k
.

There is a universal twisted form
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{G}
of
(\mathbb{P}^{2},C)
over
\mathcal{G}

[
BV
, §5.1]
, and the base change

identifies naturally with

. The compression

[

is the coarse moduli space of

, and the natural map

is birational since the action of

on

is faithful. In particular, we get a rational map
\mathbf{P}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
by composition the inverse

. Similarly, the coarse moduli space

of

has a rational map

The typical strategy we use is to find a rational point

Notice that if

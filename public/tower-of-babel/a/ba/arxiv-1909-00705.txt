On the cells and associated varieties of highest weight Harish-Chandra modules
Zhanqiang Bai, Yixin Bao, Zhao Liang and Xun Xie

Abstract
. Let G G be a Hermitian type Lie group with the complexified Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . We use L ⁡ ( λ ) L(\lambda) to denote a highest weight Harish-Chandra G G -module with infinitesimal character λ \lambda . Let w w be an element in the Weyl group W W . 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra. In the 1960s, Gelfand and Kirillov
[
GK66
]
introduced an quantity to measure the size of finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, which is now known as the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension. For a finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module
M
, Bernstein
[
Ber71
]
constructed a variety
V(M)
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
, which is called the associated variety of
M
. Identifying
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
with
\mathfrak{g}
via the Killing form, the associated variety could also be viewed as a variety in
\mathfrak{g}
. It is proved that the dimension of
V(M)
equals to the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of
M
. The characterization of associated varieties has risen as a significant topic of infinite-dimensional representation theory of Lie algebras and Lie groups in recent years.

Fix a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{\mathfrak{n}}
so that
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{n}
is a Borel subalgebra. Let
\Phi
be the root system of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
with the positive system
\Phi^{+}
and simple system
\Delta
. Denote by
W
the Weyl group of
\Phi
.
Let
G
denote the algebraic adjoint group of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
B
denote the Borel subgroup of
G
corresponding to
\mathfrak{b}
. For
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
, let
L(\lambda)
be the highest weight module of
\mathfrak{g}
with highest weight
\lambda-\rho
, where
\rho
is half the sum of positive roots. We use
L_{w}
to denote the simple highest weight module with highest weight
-w\rho-\rho
.

Joseph
[
Jos84
]
proved that the associated variety
V(L_{w})
is a union of orbital varieties defined as follows. Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a nilpotent
G
-orbit. The irreducible components of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}\cap\mathfrak{n}
are called
orbital varieties
of
\mathcal{O}
. They all take the form
\mathcal{V}(w)=\overline{B(\mathfrak{n}\cap w\mathfrak{n})}
for some
w\in W
.
The associated variety
V(L_{w})
is called
irreducible
if and only if it contains only one orbital variety. Tanisaki
[
Tan88
]
showed that there exist reducible associated varieties of the form
V(L_{w})
in type
B
and
C
.
For a long time, people conjectured that all associated varieties of the form
V(L_{w})
are irreducible in the case of type
A
(see
[
BB85
]
and
[
Mel93
]
). However,
Williamson
[
Wil15
]
showed that there exist counter examples. So the structure of
V(L_{w})
or
V(L(\lambda))
is still mysterious even in type
A
.

Suppose
G
is a Hermitian type Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
and complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
K
be the maximal compact Lie group of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}
and complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{p}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{+}
be the usual decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
as a

-module. When

is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module for

, the associated variety of

is contained in
(\mathfrak{g}/(\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{p}^{+}))^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{p}^{+}
.
It is well-known that the closures of the

-orbits in

form a chain

(1.1)

\{0\}=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{0}\subsetneq\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{r}=\mathfrak{p}^{+}\!,

where

, which is also equal to the rank of the symmetric space

. It follows from
[

that

When
L(\lambda)
is a unitary highest weight Harish-Chandra module, its associated variety is given By Bai-Hunziker
[

]
in a uniform formula.
Recently, a combinatorial algorithm was found to compute the Gelfand–Kirillov dimensions of all highest weight Harish-Chandra modules in
[

In this paper, we want to give some characterization for highest weight Harish-Chandra modules by their associated varieties and Kazhdan–Lusztig right cells.

We refer to

]
or §

for the definition of Kazhdan–Lusztig right cell and use

is a Harish-Chandra module. Define

2.1.
GK dimension and associated variety

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. Let
M
be a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module generated by a finite-dimensional subspace
M_{0}
. Let
\{U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\}_{n\geq 0}
be the standard filtration of

. The graded module of
M
is defined by
\text{gr}(M)=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{gr}_{n}M
(here
M_{n}=U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})M_{0}
,
\text{gr}_{n}M=M_{n}/{M_{n-1}}
), which is a finitely generated
\text{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
(
\approx S(\mathfrak{g})
)-module.

Definition 2.1
.

Then the
Gelfand–Kirillov dimension
of
M
is defined by

\operatorname{GKdim}M=\overline{\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\log\dim(U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})M_{0})}{\log n}.

It is well-known that the above definition is independent of the choice of
M_{0}
(see
[
NOT01
]
).

The
associated variety
of
M
is defined by

V(M):=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\mid p(X)=0\ \text{for~ all~}p\in\operatorname{Ann}_{S(\mathfrak{g})}(\operatorname{gr}M)\}.

From
[
NOT01
]
, we have
\dim V(M)=\operatorname{GKdim}M
.

2.2.
Kazhdan–Lusztig cell and Harish-Chandra cell

The Weyl group
W
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a Coxeter group generated by
S=\{s_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Delta\}
. Let
\ell(-)
be the
length function
on
W
. The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathcal{A}:=\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
is generated by
T_{w}
,
w\in W
with relations

T_{w}T_{w^{\prime}}=T_{ww^{\prime}}\text{ if }\ell(ww^{\prime})=\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime}),

\text{and }(T_{s}+v^{-1})(T_{s}-v)=0\text{ for any }s\in S.

The unique elements
C_{w}
such that

\overline{C_{w}}=C_{w},\text{~and~}C_{w}\equiv T_{w}~(\mathrm{mod}{~\mathcal{H}_{<0}})

is known as the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis of
\mathcal{H}
, where
\bar{\,}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is the bar involution such that

\bar{q}=q^{-1},~\overline{T_{w}}=T_{w^{-1}}^{-1},\text{~and~}\mathcal{H}_{<0}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathcal{A}_{<0}T_{w}

with
\mathcal{A}_{<0}=v^{-1}\mathbb{Z}[v^{-1}]
.

If
C_{y}
occurs in the expansion of
hC_{w}
(resp.
C_{w}h
) with respect to the KL-basis for some
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we write
y\leftarrow_{L}w
(resp.
y\leftarrow_{R}w
). Extend
\leftarrow_{L}
(resp.
\leftarrow_{R}
) to a preorder
\leq_{L}
(resp.
\leq_{R}
) on
W
. For
x,w\in W
, write
x\leq_{LR}w
if there exists
x=w_{0},w_{1},\cdots,w_{n}=w
such that for every
0\leq i<n
we have
w_{i}\leq_{L}w_{i+1}
or
w_{i}\leq_{R}w_{i+1}
. Let
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L}}{{\sim}}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle LR}}{{\sim}}
be the equivalence relations associated with
\leq_{L}
,
\leq_{R}
,
\leq_{LR}
. The equivalence classes on
W
for
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L}}{{\sim}}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle LR}}{{\sim}}
are called
left cells
,
right cells
and
two-sided cells
respectively. From
[
Lus03
, §8.1]
, we have
x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}y
if and only if
x^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L}}{{\sim}}y^{-1}
.

Now we recall the concept of
Harish-Chandra cells, which is defined by Barbasch–Vogan
[
BV83
]
. Let
X,Y
be two irreducible (Harish-Chandra) modules with the same infinitesimal character. We write
X>Y
if there exists a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module
F
such that
Y
appears a sub-quotient of
X\otimes F
. We write
X\sim Y
if both
X>Y
and
Y>X
. The equivalence classes for the relation
\sim
are called cells of (Harish-Chandra) modules or (Harish-Chandra) cells.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
BV83a
, Prop. 2.9]
)
.

Two highest weight Harish-Chandra modules
L_{w}
and
L_{y}
belong to the same Harish-Chandra cell with infinitesimal character
\rho
if and only if
w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}y
.

It follows
from the definition that if a Harish-Chandra cell contains one highest weight Harish-Chandra module, then all representations in this cell are highest weight Harish-Chandra modules. Also the associated variety is constant on any given Harish-Chandra cell.

From
[
BZ17
, §3.2]
, we know that a Harish-Chandra cell
\mathcal{C}_{HC}
will determine a representation of
W
which is spanned
by
\mathcal{C}_{HC}
and will be denoted by
V(\mathcal{C}_{HC})
. Suppose
\mathcal{O}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complex nilpotent orbit appeared in the associated variety of the annihilator of the highest weight Harish-Chandra module
L(\lambda)
. Then the irreducible
W
-module
\pi(\mathcal{O}^{\mathbb{C}},1)
for

\mathcal{O}^{\mathbb{C}}
via the Springer correspondence is a
W
-submodule of
V(\mathcal{C}_{HC})
. Thus we have
\#\mathcal{C}_{HC}=\dim V(\mathcal{C}_{HC})\geq\dim\pi(\mathcal{O}^{\mathbb{C}},1)
.

3.
Associated varieties of highest weight modules

In this section, we recall the relation between associated varieties and cells.

3.1.
Right cells and associated varieties

Recall that any subset
I\subseteq\Delta
generates a subsystem
\Phi_{I}\subseteq\Phi
with corresponding parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{I}=\mathfrak{l}_{I}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{I}\supset\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We say
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is
\Phi_{I}^{+}
-dominant
if and only if
(\lambda,\alpha^{\vee})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for all
\alpha\in I
. So
L(\lambda)
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra module if and only if
\lambda
is
\Phi^{+}(\mathfrak{k})
-dominant.

(
[

]
)

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple complex Lie algebra and

-dominant if and only if

.

]
and

. For a nilpotent orbit

the set of irreducible components in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}\cap\mathfrak{n}
. Each irreducible component of

is called an

, which can be written as
\mathcal{V}(w):=\overline{B(\mathfrak{n}\cap w\mathfrak{n})}
for some

[

]
.

.

Suppose that

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose

Proof.

If

. In view of
[

and
[

Definition 4.1

For an element

4.1.
The case of
\mathfrak{su}(p,q)

In this subsection, we assume
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{su}(p,q)
with
p+q=n
.
A weight
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{n})
is called integral if and only if
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and called
(p,q)
-dominant
if and only if
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for
1\leq i<j\leq p
and
p+1\leq i<j\leq p+q=n
. From Enright–Howe–Wallach
[
EHW83
]
, we know that the highest weight
SU(p,q)
-module
L(\lambda)
is a Harish-Chandra module if and only if
\lambda
is
(p,q)
-dominant. Therefore,
L_{w}
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra
SU(p,q)
-module if and only if
-w\rho
is
(p,q)
-dominant. The number of these modules is
|\mathcal{W}|=|S_{n}/(S_{p}\times S_{q})|=\frac{n!}{p!q!}
.

From
[
NOT01
]
, for
G=SU(p,q)
we know that

\mathcal{O}_{j}=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&c\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)|c\in M_{p\times q}(\mathbb{C}),\text{rank}(c)=j\}

is the
j
-th
K_{\mathbb{C}}
-orbit of
G
.
Also we have

\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{m}=\coprod\limits_{j\leq m}\mathcal{O}_{j}=\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&c\\
0&0\\
\end{array}\right)|c\in M_{p\times q}(\mathbb{C}),\text{rank}(c)\leq j\}.

Sometime we will write it as
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{m}(p,q)
to emphasize that it comes from
SU(p,q)
.

Suppose
s\geq t
. An element
w\in S_{n}
is called
(s,t)
-decreasing
if
w=(x_{s},...,x_{1},y_{t},...,y_{1})
with
x_{s}>...>x_{1}
,
y_{t}>...>y_{1}
and
x_{1}<y_{1}

x_{t}<y_{t}

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose
L_{w}
is a highest weight module of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Then the followings are equivalent:

(1)

L_{w}
is a Harish-Chandra
SU(p,q)
-module.

(2)

V(L_{w})=\mathcal{V}(w)=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}(p,q)
for some
0\leq k\leq\min\{p,q\}
.

(3)

w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}w_{p,k}=(n,...,p+k+1,p,...,1,p+k,...,p+1).

Proof.

When
L_{w}
is a Harish-Chandra
SU(p,q)
-module, we will have
V(L_{w})=\mathcal{V}(w)=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}(p,q)
by Proposition
3.18

and Proposition
4.4
. Thus (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).

From Proposition
3.1
,
V(L_{w})=\mathcal{V}(w)=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}(p,q)\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{+}
will imply that
L_{w}
is a highest weight Harish-Chandra
SU(p,q)
-module. Thus (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

Suppose (1) holds. Now we prove that
w

w_{p,k}=(n,n-1,...,p+k+1,p,p-1,...,1,p+k,p+k-1,...,p+1).

(p,q)
-dominant. Let
k

1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<...<i_{k}\leq p<p+1\leq j_{k}<...<j_{1}\leq n

such that

\lambda_{i_{1}}-\lambda_{j_{1}}\leq 0,\lambda_{i_{2}}-\lambda_{j_{2}}\leq 0,...,\lambda_{i_{k}}-\lambda_{j_{k}}\leq 0.

Then from
[
BX19

with two columns or one column (for
w=-Id
).
We only consider the nontrivial case. Then from
[
BX19
, Lem. 4.5]
,
P(\lambda)
and
P(w)
have the same shape. From the construction of
P(\lambda)
, we know that the number of entries in the second column of
P(\lambda)
equals to
k
. Suppose the entries in the first column of
P(w)
are
(s_{1},...,s_{n-k})
and the entries in the second column of
P(w)
are
(a_{1},...,a_{k})
from top to bottom. Then we get

w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}(s_{n-k},...,s_{1},a_{k},...,a_{1}):=\pi_{w},

while
\pi_{w}
is
(n-k,k)
-decreasing. By Proposition
3.18
and Proposition
4.4

V(L_{w})=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}(p,q)=\mathcal{V}(w)=\mathcal{V}(\pi_{w}).

4.2.
The size of highest weight Harish-Chandra cells of type
A_{n-1}

Recall the concept of Harish-Chandra cells in Section 2. In the case of type
C
, Barchini–Zierau
[
BZ17
]
had computed the size of each Harish-Chandra cell consisting of highest weight Harish-Chandra modules with a given associated variety. In the case of type
A
, we will give a similar result. To prove our result, we recall the famous hook formula. Some details can be found in James
[
Jam78
]

Proposition 4.7

(The hook formula)
.

Let
P
be a standard Young tableau and
\mathscr{C}
be the corresponding Kazhdan–Lusztig right cell in the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Then

\#\mathscr{C}=\frac{n!}{h_{1}h_{2}...h_{n}},

where
h_{i}
are the ”hooks” of the Young tableau of shape
P
.

Theorem 4.8
.

We use
\mathcal{C}_{k}
to denote a Harish-Chandra cell consisting of highest weight Harish-Chandra
SU(p,q)
-modules
L_{w}
with associated variety
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}(p,q)
, then for
2\leq k\leq\min\{p,q\}
, we have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{C}_{k}

\displaystyle=\dim\pi_{[2^{k},1^{n-k}]}=\text{the number of standard Young tableaux of shape~}P(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)...(n-k+2)(n-2k+1)}{k!}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n}{k-1}.

And
\#\mathcal{C}_{0}=1
,
\#\mathcal{C}_{1}=n-1
.

Proof.

From the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we know that the number of modules in the Harish-Chandra cell
\mathcal{C}_{k}
equals the number of elements in the right cell
\mathscr{C}_{k}
which contains
w_{p,k}
. On the other hand,
\#\mathscr{C}_{k}
equals the number of the standard Young tableau of shape
p(w_{p,k})=[2^{k},1^{n-k}]
, which is just the dimension of
\pi_{p(w_{p,k})}
. We know the numbers of entries in the two columns of
P(w_{p,k})
are
c_{1}(P(w_{p,k}))=n-k
and
c_{2}(P(w_{p,k}))=k
. From James
[
Jam78
]
, the corresponding hooks of
P(w_{p,k})
is a Young tableau
P_{k}
consisting of two columns. The entries in the first column of
P_{k}
are
(n-(k-1),n-k,...,n-2k+2,n-2k,...,1)
and the entries in the second column of
P_{k}
are
(k,k-1,...,1)
from top to bottom. From Proposition
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle\#\mathscr{C}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{(n-(k-1))\times...\times(n-2k+2)\times(n-2k)\times...\times 1\times k\times...\times 1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)...(n-k+2)(n-2k+1)}{k!}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n}{k-1}.

When
k=1
, we will have
c_{1}(P(w_{p,k}))=n-1
and
c_{2}(P(w_{p,k}))=1
, so

\#\mathcal{C}_{1}=\frac{n!}{n\times(n-2)\times...\times 1\times 1}=n-1.

When
k=0
, we will have
c_{1}(P(w_{p,k}))=n
and
c_{2}(P(w_{p,k}))=0
, so
\#\mathcal{C}_{0}=1
.

∎

If we set
\binom{n}{0}=1
and
\binom{n}{-1}=0
, our formula in Theorem
4.8
will be uniform.

5.3.
The size of highest weight Harish-Chandra cells of type

First we recall Lusztig’s

-symbols
[
Lus77
]
. Let

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\\
\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots~\mu_{m}\end{pmatrix},m\geq 0

be a tableau of nonnegative integers such that entries in each row are strictly increasing. Define an equivalence relation on the set of all such tableaux via

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots\mu_{m}\\
\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\\
\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots\mu_{m}\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}0~\lambda_{1}+1~\lambda_{2}+1~\cdots~\lambda_{m}+1\\
0~\mu_{1}+1~\mu_{2}+1~\cdots\mu_{m}+1\end{pmatrix}.

Denote by
\Sigma_{D}
the set of equivalence classes under this relation
\sim
. Use the same notation
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\\
\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots\mu_{m}\end{pmatrix}\in\Sigma_{D}
to denote its equivalence class, called a
D
-symbol
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
. Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{\bf{d}}
be a nilpotent orbit of
\mathfrak{g}
with partition
{\bf d}=[d_{1},\dots,d_{k}]
of
2n
, then
k
is even. From
[
CM93
, §10.1]
,
there is a
D
-symbol

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\\
\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots\mu_{m}\end{pmatrix}
such that, as multisets

\{2\lambda_{i}+1,2\mu_{j}\mid i\leq m,j\leq m\}=\{d_{l}+k-l\mid l\leq k\},

where
2m=k
.

Recall that the elements of
\mathrm{Irr}(W^{\prime}_{n})
are parameterized by the unordered bipartitions
\{\bf{d},\bf{f}\}
with
|{\bf d}|+|{\bf f}|=n
, except that if
n=2m
is even and
\bf{d}=\bf{f}
, then the unordered bipartition
\{\bf{d},\bf{d}\}
corresponds to two representations,
\pi^{I}_{\{\bf{d},\bf{d}\}}
and
\pi^{II}_{\{\bf{d},\bf{d}\}}
. So there is a one-to-one correspondence between D-symbols and unordered bipartitions except that if
n=2m
is even and
\bf{d}=\bf{f}
. It can be described as follows. Let
\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}~\lambda_{2}~\cdots~\lambda_{m}\\
\mu_{1}~\mu_{2}~\cdots\mu_{m}\end{pmatrix}\in\Sigma_{D}
. We subtract

from the

Then we get an unordered bipartition
\{\bf{d},\bf{f}\}
corresponding to the new top row and bottom row.

First we recall the method of finding out the minimal length element
w_{\lambda}
for an integral weight
\lambda

w_{\lambda}^{-1}\lambda=\mu

Let
\Delta=\{\alpha_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
be the simple roots of an exceptional type root system
\Phi

\{\omega_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}

The Cartan matrix is defined by

A_{ij}=(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}^{\vee})

A_{ij}\in\{0,-1,-2,-3\}
for
i\neq j

Then
\alpha_{j}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}A_{jk}\omega_{k}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

\lambda=\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}k_{i}\omega_{i}
, where
k_{i}=(\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee})
. Then an integral weight
\lambda
is antidominant if and only if
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Now we want to find
w_{\lambda}
for an integral weight
\lambda
. When
\lambda
is antidominant (resp. dominant),
w_{\lambda}=\mathrm{Id}
(resp.
-\mathrm{Id}
). Suppose
\lambda
is not antidominant, then there exist some
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We choose the largest index
i_{\lambda}={i_{1}}
such that
k_{i_{1}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Denote the simple reflection
s_{\alpha_{i}}
by
s_{i}
. Then we have

s_{i_{1}}\lambda=\lambda-k_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{1}}=\lambda-k_{i_{1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}A_{i_{1}j}\omega_{j}=\lambda-2k_{i_{1}}\omega_{i_{1}}-k_{i_{1}}\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i_{1}}^{n}A_{i_{1}j}\omega_{j}.

Then the coefficient of
\omega_{i_{1}}
in
s_{i_{1}}\lambda
becomes
-k_{i_{1}}
.

The above process is called the
positive reduction algorithm
.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
Don23
, Lem. 3.1]
)
.

For any integral weight
\lambda
(regular or singular), we can get an antidominant weight
\mu
after finite steps of the positive reduction algorithm. We multiply all the
s_{i}
appeared in these steps and get a
w_{\lambda}
. Then this
w_{\lambda}
has the minimal length in
W
such that
w_{\lambda}^{-1}\lambda
is antidominant.

Example 6.2
.

Let
\lambda=(-1,1,-1,-1,-1,1,1,-1)
be an integral weight of type
E_{6}
. We can write
\lambda=-\omega_{1}+2\omega_{3}-2\omega_{4}:=[-1,0,2,-2,0,0]
. The largest index of
\lambda
with a positive element is
i_{1}=3
. Recall that
\alpha_{3}=-\omega_{1}+2\omega_{3}-\omega_{4}=:[-1,0,2,-1,0,0]
. Thus
s_{3}\lambda=\lambda-2\alpha_{3}=[1,0,-2,0,0,0]

s_{3}\lambda
with a positive element is

\alpha_{1}=2\omega_{1}-\omega_{3}

s_{1}s_{3}\lambda=s_{3}\lambda-\alpha_{1}=[-1,0,-1,0,0,0]
, which is already antidominant. Thus we have
w_{\lambda}=s_{3}s_{1}
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{sp}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
L(\lambda)
is an integral highest weight Harish-Chandra module with highest weight
\lambda-\rho
and
V(L(\lambda))=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
with
n
being odd. Suppose
w_{\lambda}\in W
is the minimal length element such that
\mu=w_{\lambda}^{-1}\lambda
is antidominant, then we have

w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}(n,n-1,\dots,2,1).

Proof.

Set
w=(n,n-1,\dots,2,1)
. Then
-w\rho=(-1,-2,\dots,n)
and
V(L_{w})=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
by Proposition
3.19
.

For an integral highest weight Harish-Chandra module
L(\lambda)
, if
V(L(\lambda))=V(L(w_{\lambda}))=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{n}=V(L_{w})
, we will have
w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}w
by Theorem
3.8
.

∎

By using Theorem
3.8
, we have the following result.

Corollary 7.8
.

When
G=Sp(n,\mathbb{R})
, let
L(\lambda)
be an integral highest weight Harish-Chandra module.
Then we have the followings:

(1)

suppose
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq k\leq n
, then
V(L(\lambda))=V(L_{w})=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
if and only if

w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}(\frac{k}{2},-\frac{k}{2}-1,\frac{k}{2}-1,-\frac{k}{2}-2,\frac{k}{2}-2,\dots,-(k-1),1,-k,-(k+1),\dots,-n).

(2)

suppose
n
is odd, then

V(L(\lambda))=V(L_{w})=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
if and only if

w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\sim}}(n,n-1,\dots,2,1).

Corollary 7.9
.

We use
\mathcal{C}_{k}
to denote a Harish-Chandra cell consisting of highest weight Harish-Chandra
Sp(n,\mathbb{R})
-modules
L_{w}
with associated variety
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{k}
for some
k\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq k\leq n
. Then we have

\#\mathcal{C}_{k}=\begin{cases}\binom{n+1}{\frac{k}{2}},&\text{~if~}k\in 2\mathbb{Z}\text{~and~}0\leq k\leq n,\\
\binom{n}{\frac{n-1}{2}},&\text{~if~}k=n\text{~is~odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows from
[
BZ17
, Prop. 33]
.

Corollary 7.10
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathfrak{sp}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
L(\lambda)
is a half integral highest weight Harish-Chandra module with highest weight
\lambda-\rho
. Then we have
V(L(\lambda))=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{2k+1}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq 2k+1\leq n
if and only if

w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle LR}}{{\sim}}z_{k}:=\begin{cases}(k+1,-(k+2),k,-(k+3),k-1,\dots,&\\
-2k,2,-(2k+1),1,-(2k+2),\dots,-n),&\text{~if~}n-k\text{~is~ odd},\\
(k+1,-(k+2),k,-(k+3),k-1,\dots,&\\
-2k,2,-(2k+1),-1,-(2k+2),\dots,-n),&\text{~if~}n-k\text{~is~ even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

When
w_{\lambda}
belongs to the given two-sided cell containing
z_{k}
, we have
q_{2}^{\mathrm{ev}}=k
since there are
k
even boxes in the second columns of the Young tableaux appeared in the argument of Theorem
5.1
. By Proposition
3.19
, we have
V(L(\lambda))=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{2k+1}
.

Conversely, suppose that
L(\lambda)
is a half integral highest weight Harish-Chandra module and we have
V(L(\lambda))=\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{2k+1}
.
Then by Proposition
3.19
, we have
k=q_{2}^{\mathrm{ev}}

w_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle LR}}{{\sim}}z_{k}
.
∎

Generalized Schauder Theory and its Application to Degenerate/Singular Parabolic Equations
Takwon Kim, Ki-Ahm Lee, Hyungsung Yun

Abstract
. In this paper, we study generalized Schauder theory for the degenerate/singular parabolic equations of the form u t = a i ′ ​ j ′ ​ u i ′ ​ j ′ + 2 ​ x n γ / 2 ​ a i ′ ​ n ​ u i ′ ​ n + x n γ ​ a n ​ n ​ u n ​ n + b i ′ ​ u i ′ + x n γ / 2 ​ b n ​ u n + c ​ u + f ( γ ≤ 1 ) . u_{t}=a^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}u_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}a^{i^{\prime}n}u_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{nn}u_{nn}+b^{i^{\prime}}u_{i^{\prime}}+x_{n}^{\gamma/2}b^{n}u_{n}+cu+f\quad(\gamma\leq 1). When the equation above is singular, it can be derived from Monge–Ampère equations by using the partial Legendre transform. Also, we study the fractional version of Taylor expansion for the solution u u , which is called s s -polynomial. To prove C s 2 + α C_{s}^{2+\alpha} -regularity and higher regularity of the solution u u , we establish generalized Schauder theory which approximates coefficients of the operator with s s -polynomials rather than constants. The generalized Schauder theory not only recovers the proof for uniformly parabolic equations but is also applicable to other operators that are difficult to apply the bootstrap method to obtain higher regularity.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study generalized Schauder theory and fractional order expansion of solutions
u
for the following degenerate/singular parabolic equations

(1.1)

u_{t}=Lu+f\quad\textnormal{in }Q_{1}^{+},

where

L=a^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(X)D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}a^{i^{\prime}n}(X)D_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{nn}(X)D_{nn}+b^{i^{\prime}}(X)D_{i^{\prime}}+x_{n}^{\gamma/2}b^{n}(X)D_{n}+c(X)

with a constant
\gamma\leq 1
and

Q_{1}^{+}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}:|x_{i}|<1\,(1\leq i\leq n)\}\times(-1,0].

The repeated index with prime
i^{\prime}
means the summation from
1
to
(n-1)
, that is

A^{i^{\prime}}B_{i^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}A^{i}B_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}B_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}A^{ij}B_{ij}.

In classical regularity theory, to obtain
C^{k,\alpha}
-regularity of the solution
u
, it is shown that the following statement holds (cf.
[
Wan92
]
): For each
Y\in\overline{\Omega}
, there exists a polynomial
p^{Y}
of degree
k
satisfying

(1.2)

\|u-p^{Y}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(Y)\cap\Omega)}\leq Cr^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }r>0.

Furthermore, we can obtain higher order partial derivative at
Y
of
u
can be obtained from the coefficients of the polynomial
p^{Y}
. However, the function that is not smooth enough cannot be approximated by polynomials. Breaking away from the stereotype that polynomial approximation should be used, we consider generalized polynomials represented by monomials of fractional order, and we call them
s
-polynomials in this study. In simple terms,
s
-polynomial approximation can be understood as a fractional version of Taylor expansion. The coefficients of an
s
-polynomial
p
approximating the solution can be regarded as suitable constant multiples of the weighted derivative, and the degree of
p
gives the order of regularity. While conventional regularity theory so far has focused on differentiability for classical solutions, in this study, we are interested in how well
s
-polynomial approximates the solution instead of differentiability. In other words, a generalized concept of regularity theory can be developed through the
s
-polynomial.

The regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) can be expected to be
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity up to the boundary, but higher regularity up to the boundary can be obtained by considering
s
-polynomials and a new metric that preserves scaling. In classical Schauder theory, by showing that the derivative satisfies the equations of the same class,
C^{2,\alpha}
-regularity of the gradient
Du
is obtained, and iteratively, higher regularity can be obtained. However, this bootstrap method is not applicable in (
1.1
) since the equation that the partial derivative
u_{n}
satisfies is not of the same class as (
1.1
), so the generalized Schauder theory was developed to solve this issue. It is also applicable to other operators that are difficult to apply the bootstrap method to obtain higher regularity.

The study on the regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) was inspired by several previous studies.

(1)

Monge–Ampère equations: Daskalopoulos and Savin
[
DS09
]
converted the Monge–Ampère equations

\det D^{2}u=(x^{2}+y^{2})^{\gamma/2}\quad\text{in }B_{1}

into the singular equations with
\gamma<0

|x|^{\gamma}v_{xx}+v_{yy}=0\quad\text{in }B_{1}

using the partial Legendre transform. In
[
DS09
]
, they solved the problem for Monge–Ampère equation by finding the fractional order expansion

\displaystyle v(x,y)

\displaystyle=a_{1}+a_{2}x+a_{3}y+a_{4}xy

\displaystyle\qquad+a_{5}\left(\frac{1}{2}y^{2}-\frac{1}{(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)}|x|^{2-\gamma}\right)+O\big((y^{2}+|x|^{2-\gamma})^{1+\delta}\big)

for some universal constant
\delta=\delta(\gamma)>0
.

In the study of Schauder estimates up to boundary for degenerate Monge–Ampère equations, Le and Savin
[
LS17
]
show that a bounded solution
w
of the singular equations with
\gamma<0

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{x^{\prime}}w+x_{n}^{\gamma}w_{nn}&=0&&\text{in }B_{1}^{+}\\
w&=0&&\text{on }\{x_{n}=0\}\end{aligned}\right.

satisfies

|w(x)-p(x^{\prime})x_{n}|\leq C(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots x_{n-1}^{2}+x_{n}^{2-\gamma})^{\frac{3-\gamma}{2-\gamma}}

for all
x\in B_{1/2}^{+}
, where
C
is a universal constant and
p(x^{\prime})
is a standard polynomial of degree
1
.

As they used a distance function that allows solutions and equations to be scaling invariant, we also defined a distance function corresponding to (
1.1
) in our study. It is noteworthy that such a fractional order expansion and estimates are developed to higher orders by
s
-polynomial and the distance function.

(2)

Gauss Curvature Flow: Related studies can also be found in differential geometry, for example, Daskalopoulos and Hamilton
[
DH99
]
showed that the regularity of the interface for the Gauss curvature flow with flat sides can be transformed into the regularity of solutions for the following degenerate equations

(1.3)

v_{t}=xv_{xx}+v_{yy}+\nu v_{x}+f\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times(0,T].

They showed Schauder estimates for smooth solutions of (
1.3
) using a new metric that preserves scaling.

(3)

Non-local Equations: In the research field of non-local equations, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
CS07
]
showed that the solution
u
of the degenerate/singular equations

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{x}u+z^{\frac{2s-1}{s}}u_{zz}&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\\
u&=f&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\end{aligned}\right.

satisfies

(-\Delta)^{s}f(x)=-C(n,s)u_{z}(x,0)

for some constant
C(n,s)>0
. The regularity of solutions for the extension problem (
1.4
) can be applied to the regularity of solutions for the fractional Laplace equations. In fact, in
[
CS07
]
, Hölder’s regularity of solutions for fractional Laplace equations was shown by using the Harnack inequality for the extension problem (
1.4
).

(4)

Mathematical Finance: The Black–Scholes equations for the constant elasticity of variance (CEV) model which was introduced by Cox
[
Cox75
]
and Cox and Ross
[
CR76
]
. The risky asset’s price
X_{t}
of the CEV model evolves according to the following stochastic differential equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=\mu X_{t}\,dt+\sigma X_{t}^{\gamma/2}\,dW_{t}\\
X_{0}&=x,\end{aligned}\right.

where
W_{t}
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion for some positive constants
\mu
,
\sigma
, and
\gamma
. Using the Feynman-Kac formula, we can derive the following degenerate equations

(1.5)

u_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{\gamma}u_{xx}+rxu_{x}-ru=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times[0,T).

Kim, Lee, and Yun
[
KLY23
]
generalized (
1.5
) to
n
-dimensions and showed higher regularity of (
1.5
) in the case of
1<\gamma<2
. However, as with the aforementioned equation, many applications occur for
\gamma\leq 1
, so we need to study (
1.1
) for
\gamma\leq 1
.

Although there is a slight difference compared to (
1.1
), the methodology covered in our study is expected to be applicable to equations that have similar degenerate/singular structures of (
1.1
) like aforementioned equations. In addition, it is a new version of Schauder theory in the sense that can directly show higher regularity of solutions without using the bootstrap method for uniformly parabolic equations.

In order to obtain the heuristic idea of
s
-polynomial, let us start with a simplified version of (
1.1
). The following equation

(1.6)

u_{t}=x^{\gamma}u_{xx}+1\quad\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}\quad(0<\gamma<1)

admits a solution of the form

(1.7)

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}x-\frac{1}{(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)}x^{2-\gamma}+\frac{t}{(3-\gamma)(2-\gamma)}x^{3-\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{(5-2\gamma)(4-2\gamma)(3-\gamma)(2-\gamma)}x^{5-2\gamma}.

Since
u
is not twice differentiable at
x=0
in the
x
-direction, the regularity of
u
for a spatial variable is at most
C^{1,\alpha}
, so the polynomial that satisfies (
1.2
) with
k=1
is
p(x,t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}x
. However, considering the following estimate

(1.8)

\left|u(x,t)-\frac{t^{2}}{2}x-\frac{1}{(2-\gamma)(1-\gamma)}x^{2-\gamma}-\frac{t}{(3-\gamma)(2-\gamma)}x^{3-\gamma}\right|\leq Cr^{5-2\gamma}

for all
X\in Q_{r}^{+}
, it can be expected that fractional order expansion is possible. Also, (
1.8
) contains more terms than
p
and provides a more accurate approximation.

Looking at the pattern of each monomial when it is substituted into (
1.6
), it is possible to find a solution with a fractional order expansion of more terms than (
1.7
). This analysis can be extended for (
1.1
), which is the motivation to consider
s
-polynomials. Although the forcing term
f
is a general function rather than a constant function, the solution of (
1.1
) can be approximated by such a fractional order expansion. This is why we established Schauder estimates using
s
-polynomials rather than standard polynomials. In addition, the distance function for uniformly parabolic equations does not preserve the scaling at the boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
, so it is not suitable for describing the behavior of solutions for (
1.1
) in the neighborhood of
\{x_{n}=0\}
. Inspired by
[
DH99
,
DS09
,
LS17
]
, we define a scaling-preserving distance function
s:\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}\times\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}\to[0,\infty)
and use it to show Schauder estimates.

Since the bootstrap method is not applicable in (
1.1
), it is necessary to obtain
C_{s}^{k,2+\alpha}
-regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) directly from
C_{s}^{k,\alpha}
-regularity of the coefficients of
L
. However, it is not enough to consider perturbative methods, by “freezing” the coefficients around a certain point. In order to solve this difficulty, we will use
s
-polynomial approximation of coefficients that fully reflects the regularity of coefficients and study equations with
s
-polynomial coefficients first.

The solution
u
of (
1.1
) satisfies the following estimates depending on the range of
\gamma
.

(1.9)

\displaystyle|u(X)|\leq

\displaystyle Cx_{n}\approx Cs[X,O]^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}

(\gamma<1)

(1.10)

\displaystyle|u(X)|\leq

\displaystyle Cx_{n}^{2-\gamma}\approx Cs[X,O]^{2}

(1<\gamma<2)

for all
X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}}
, where
C>0
is a universal constant. Assuming
f=0
, the order of (
1.10
) can be improved to a higher order, but not in the case of (
1.9
). In the case of
1<\gamma<2
,
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) is directly obtained from the improved estimates of (
1.10
), and then the generalized coefficient freezing method can be applied immediately thanks to
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-regularity. However, for
\gamma<1
, the regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) expected from (
1.9
) is
C_{s}^{\alpha}
-regularity or
C_{s}^{1,\alpha}
-regularity depending on the range on
\gamma
. Thus, a process to show
C_{s}^{2+\alpha}
-regularity of solutions for (
1.1
) is necessary. In addition, since
s
-polynomials for
\gamma=1
include logarithmic functions, an appropriate method for dealing with them is also required.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we introduce notations used throughout this paper and state the main theorem. Also, the maximal principle, comparison principle, the existence of a unique solution for the degenerate/singular equations are covered in
Section

2
. It is similar to uniformly parabolic equations, so readers familiar with it can skip it. In the last part of

2
, we derive global regularity from interior regularity and boundary regularity of solutions for (
1.1
). In

, we prove the boundary Lipschitz estimates of solutions for (

) and use it to show global

-regularity of solutions for (

). In

, we prove
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity and
C_{s}^{k,2+\alpha}
-regularity of solutions for equations with constant coefficients. In
Section

5
, we deal with generalized Schauder theory, which is the main idea of this study. Unlike equations with constant coefficients,
C_{s}^{k,2+\alpha}
-regularity cannot be obtained immediately, so
C_{s}^{2,\alpha}
-regularity for solutions of (
1.1
) and

-regularity of solutions for equations with

-polynomial coefficients are first shown, and then
C_{s}^{k,2+\alpha}
-regularity of solutions for (

) can be obtained by using generalized coefficient freezing method.

s

\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\coloneqq\|u\|_{C^{k,0}(\overline{\Omega})}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|+2i=k\\

Next, we define the Hölder norm with respect to the distance function

\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}\coloneqq\sup_{Y\in\overline{\Omega}}\sup_{r>0}\,\inf_{p}\bigg\{\frac{|u(X)-p(X)|}{r^{k+\alpha}}+\sum_{\beta,i,j,l}|A^{\beta ijl}|:X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}(Y)}\cap\overline{\Omega}\bigg\},

s
-polynomials of degree

. In particular, we denote the

\|u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}\coloneqq\|u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}.

\|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}\coloneqq\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|(\partial_{t}-L)u\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{\Omega})}

2.2.
Main Result

In this subsection, we are going to state the main theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0<\alpha<1
with
k+2+\alpha\notin\mathcal{D}
, and assume

a^{ij},\,b^{i},\,c,\,f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})\quad(i,j=1,2,\cdots,n).

Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q^{+}_{1})\cap C(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})
is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
u=0
on
\{X\in\partial_{p}Q^{+}_{1}:x_{n}=0\}
. Then
u\in C^{k,2+\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})
and

\|u\|_{C^{k,2+\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}+\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}\right),

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
k
,
\alpha
,
\|a^{ij}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
,
\|b^{i}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
, and
\|c\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
.

2.3.
Maximum Principle

First, we will show the maximum principle for the following initial/boundary-value problem

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}&=Lu+f&&\textnormal{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
u&=g&&\textnormal{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfies
u_{t}<Lu
. If
u<0
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
, then

u(X)<0\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}.

Proof.

Suppose not. Then there exists
\tau=\inf\{t:u(x,t)\geq 0\mbox{ for some }(x,t)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}\}
.
Since
u\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
and
u<0
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
, we know that there is
Y=(y,\tau)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}\setminus\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
such that
u(Y)=0
. Meanwhile
\tau
is the first time
u
becomes nonnegative, the function
U(x)\coloneqq u(x,\tau)
attains its maximum at
y
and hence
u_{t}(Y)\geq 0
,
Du(Y)=0
, and
D^{2}u(Y)\leq 0
.
Then we have

\mathscr{D}^{2}u(Y)\coloneqq\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{n-1}&\textbf{0}\\
\hline\cr\textbf{0}&y_{n}^{\gamma/2}\end{array}\right)D^{2}u(Y)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{n-1}&\textbf{0}\\
\hline\cr\textbf{0}&y_{n}^{\gamma/2}\end{array}\right)\leq 0

and this implies that
u_{t}(Y)-Lu(Y)=u_{t}(Y)-\mbox{tr}\big(a^{ij}(Y)\mathscr{D}^{2}u(Y)\big)\geq 0
. This yields a contradiction to
u_{t}<Lu
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
satisfies
u_{t}\leq Lu
. If
u\leq 0
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
, then
u\leq 0
in
\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, consider
v(X)=u(X)-\varepsilon(t+2)
. Then we have
v_{t}<Lv
in
Q_{1}^{+}
and
v<0
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
. Thus, by
Lemma

2.3

u(X)<\varepsilon(t+2)
for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
. We finish the proof by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a solution of (
2.4
). Then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right),

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

Let
v=u-e^{t+1}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
. Then we have
v_{t}\leq Lv
in
Q_{1}^{+}
and
v\leq 0
on
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}.
By
Lemma

2.4
, we have
u(X)\leq e(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
for all
X\in Q_{1}^{+}
. Considering the function
w=-u-e^{t+1}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
, we can obtain the lower bound in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a solution of (
2.4
) with
\gamma<0
and
x_{n}^{-\gamma}f\in L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})
.
Then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right),

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

Let
w=e^{\mu(t+1)}\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}(x_{n}-x_{n}^{2})
. Then for sufficiently large
\mu>0
, we have
w_{t}-Lw\geq\lambda\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}x_{n}^{\gamma}
in
Q_{1}^{+}
. As in the proof
Lemma

2.5
, we can see the desired result, by considering the function
v=u-(w/\lambda+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
.
∎

2.4.
Existence of Solutions

In this subsection, we assume that
a^{ij}
,
b^{i}
,
c
, and
f
are
C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{\alpha}(Q_{1}^{+})
for the existence of a classical solution.

Definition 2.7
.

A function
v\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is called a subsolution (resp. supersolution) of (
2.4
) if

v\leq g\,(\textnormal{resp}.\geq g)\quad\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}

and if for any
\Omega\subset Q_{1}^{+}
, the solution
\tilde{v}\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{v}_{t}&=L\tilde{v}+f&&\mbox{in }\Omega\\
\tilde{v}&=v&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

is greater (resp. less) than or equal to
v
in
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.8

(Comparison Principle)
.

If
w\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a supersolution of (
2.4
) and
v\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
is a subsolution of (
2.4
), then

w\geq v\quad\text{in }Q_{1}^{+}.

Proof.

Suppose
h=v-w
has a positive maximum value
M
achieved at some point
Y=(y,\tau)\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}\setminus\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
. Then, we have
h(Y)=M>0
and
h(X)\leq M
for all
X\in\Omega,
where
\Omega=\{X\in Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}>y_{n}/2,\,t<\tau\}
. Take
\tilde{v}\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
and
\tilde{w}\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{v}_{t}&=L\tilde{v}+f&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\tilde{v}&=v&&\text{on }\partial_{p}\Omega\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{w}_{t}&=L\tilde{w}+f&&\text{in }\Omega\\
\tilde{w}&=w&&\text{on }\partial_{p}\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, the function
\tilde{h}=\tilde{v}-\tilde{w}-M
satisfying
\tilde{h}_{t}\leq L\tilde{h}
in
\Omega
and
\tilde{h}\leq 0
on
\partial_{p}\Omega
. Furthermore, since
v
and
w
are subsolution and supersolution of (
2.4
), respectively, we know that
\tilde{v}\geq v
in
\Omega
and
\tilde{w}\leq w
in
\Omega
. Hence, we have
\tilde{h}(Y)\geq 0
.
The operator
(\partial_{t}-L)
is a uniformly parabolic in
\Omega
since
\Omega
is away from the boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
. By the strong maximum principle for uniformly parabolic equations, we have
\tilde{h}=\tilde{v}-\tilde{w}-M=0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. It follows that
v-w=M
on
\partial_{p}\Omega
. Since
\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\cap\partial_{p}\Omega
is nonempty, this contradicts the assumption that
v\leq g\leq w\quad\text{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0<\alpha<1
, and assume

a^{ij},\,b^{i},\,c,\,f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})~(i,j=1,2,\cdots,n)\qquad\text{and}\qquad g\in C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}).

Then there exists a unique bounded solution
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{k+2,\alpha}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
of (
2.4
) such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right),

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a set of bounded subsolutions of (
2.4
) and let
u=\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{A}}}w
. Consider the function
\varphi_{\pm}=\pm e^{t+1}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})})
. Note first that
\varphi_{+}
is a supersolution of (
2.4
) and
\varphi_{-}
is a subsolution of (
2.4
), so
u
is bounded by
\varphi_{\pm}
by
Lemma

2.8
. For any cube
Q_{r}(Y)\subset\joinrel\subset Q_{1}^{+}
, the operator
(\partial_{t}-L)
is a uniformly parabolic in
Q_{r}(Y)
since
Q_{r}(Y)
is away from the boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
. Thus, we can apply Perron’s method for the problem (
2.4
) as in
[
KLY23
, Lemma 3.3]
and hence
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{k+2,\alpha}(Q_{1}^{+})
. Finally, for any
Y=(y,\tau)\in\{X\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}=0\}
, take a local barrier
w
near
Y
given by

w(x,t)=|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}+(t-\tau)^{2}+\begin{cases}\frac{2x_{n}}{r}-\left(\frac{x_{n}}{r}\right)^{2-\gamma/2}-\left(\frac{x_{n}}{r}\right)^{2-\gamma}&(\gamma<0)\\
\frac{x_{n}}{r}-\left(\frac{x_{n}}{r}\right)^{2-\gamma}&(0\leq\gamma<1)\\
-\frac{x_{n}}{r}\log x_{n}&(\gamma=1).\\
\end{cases}

for some small
r>0
. The continuity of
u
up to the boundary
\{x_{n}=0\}
is automatically guaranteed since
Y
is a regular boundary point. In the case
Y\in\{X\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}\neq 0\}
, the continuity of
u
is guaranteed by using the barrier function of the uniformly parabolic equations as in
[
Lie96
]
.
∎

2.5.
Global Regularity

The proof of
Theorem

2.2
is sufficient if we show higher regularity of solutions at the boundary. Main equation (
1.1
) is a uniformly parabolic equation in any
\Omega\subset\joinrel\subset Q_{1}^{+}
, so if the coefficients and forcing term are in
C_{s}^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
, the solution of (
1.1
) is in
C^{k+2,\alpha}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega)
and global regularity can be obtained by combining interior regularity and boundary regularity. More strictly speaking, it is described by the following lemma and theorem.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0<\alpha<1
, and assume

a^{ij},\,b^{i},\,c,\,f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})\quad(i,j=1,2,\cdots,n).

Suppose
u\in C_{\textnormal{loc}}^{k+2,\alpha}(Q^{+}_{1})
be a solution of (
1.1
). Then,
for each
Y\in\{X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}}:x_{n}>0\}
, there is an
s
-polynomial
p
of degree
(k+2)
at
Y
such that

\|p\|_{C_{s}^{k+2,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{r/2}^{+}(Y)})}\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)

and

|u(X)-p(X)|\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C_{s}^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)s[X,Y]^{k+2+\alpha}.

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r/2}^{+}(Y)}
and fixed
\rho\in(1/2,1)
, where
r=y_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}
and
C>0
is a constant which is independent of
Y
. Moreover

|p(X)|\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)s[X,Y]^{k+2+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
with
s[X,Y]\geq r/2
.

Proof.

For each
Y=(y,\tau)\in\{X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}}:x_{n}>0\}
, we consider the function
v
defined in a standard cube
Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)
by
v(x,t)=u(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t),
where
\rho\in(1/2,1)
and
r=y_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}
. Then,
v
satisfies the uniformly parabolic equation

(2.5)

v_{t}=\tilde{a}^{ij}(X)v_{ij}+\tilde{b}^{i}(X)v_{i}-\frac{\gamma}{2-\gamma}\frac{\tilde{a}^{nn}(X)}{x_{n}}v_{n}+\tilde{c}(X)v+\tilde{f}(X)\quad\mbox{in }Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{a}^{ij}(X)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a^{ij}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i,j\not=n\\
\frac{2-\gamma}{2}a^{in}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i\not=n,j=n\\
\frac{2-\gamma}{2}a^{nj}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i=n,j\neq n\\
\frac{(2-\gamma)^{2}}{4}a^{nn}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i=j=n,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{b}^{i}(X)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}rb^{i}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i\not=n\\
\frac{2-\gamma}{2}rb^{n}(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t)&\text{if }i=n,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{c}(X)

\displaystyle=r^{2}c(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(X)

\displaystyle=r^{2}f(y^{\prime}+rx^{\prime},(rx_{n})^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}},\tau+r^{2}t).

Since
Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)
is away from
\{X\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}=0\}
, we can show that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \|\tilde{a}^{ij}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}&\leq C\|a^{ij}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}\\
\|\tilde{b}^{i}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}&\leq C\|b^{i}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}\\
\|\tilde{c}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}&\leq C\|c\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}\end{aligned}\right.

and
\|\tilde{f}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}\leq r^{2}C\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}
for some constant
C
which is independent
Y
. Thus, by the interior Schauder estimate, there exists a polynomial
\tilde{p}
of degree
(k+2)
of the form

\tilde{p}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|+i+2j\leq k+2\\
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1},\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}A^{\beta ij}{x^{\prime}}^{\beta}(x_{n}-1)^{i}t^{j}

and

(2.6)

\|\tilde{p}\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}\leq C\Big(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0))}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}\Big),

where
C
depends on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
k
,
\alpha
,
\|\tilde{a}^{ij}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}
,
\|\tilde{b}^{i}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}
, and
\|\tilde{c}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}
such that

(2.7)

|v(X)-\tilde{p}(X)|\leq C\Big(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{\rho}(0^{\prime},1,0)})}\Big)d[X,(0^{\prime},1,0)]^{k+2+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1/2}(0^{\prime},1,0)}
. Putting

\displaystyle p(X)

\displaystyle=\tilde{p}\big(r^{-1}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}),r^{-1}x_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}},r^{-2}(t-\tau)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|+i+2j\leq k+2\\
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1},\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{A^{\beta ij}}{r^{|\beta|+i+2j}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})^{\beta}(x_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}-y_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}})^{i}(t-\tau)^{j},

we can rewrite (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) into

\|p\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{r/2}^{+}(Y)})}\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)

and

\displaystyle|u(X)-p(X)|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}+r^{2}\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}\Big)d[(r^{-1}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}),r^{-1}x_{n}^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}},r^{-2}(t-\tau)),(0^{\prime},1,0)]^{k+2+\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad=C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)s[X,Y]^{k+2+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r/2}^{+}(Y)}
. From (
2.6
), we know that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|+i+2j\leq k+2\\
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1},\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}|A^{\beta ij}|\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}+r^{2}\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}\Big)

and hence, we have

\displaystyle|p(X)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\beta|+i+2j\leq k+2\\
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1},\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{r^{k+2+\alpha}}{r^{|\beta|+i+2j}}s[X,Y]^{|\beta|+i+2j}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y))}}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}+\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{r\rho}^{+}(Y)})}}{r^{k+\alpha}}\right)s[X,Y]^{k+2+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
with
s[X,Y]\geq r/2
.
∎

Theorem 2.11

(Global Regularity up to

)
.

Lemma 3.1

in

on

,

,

(i=1,2,\cdots,n)
,

in

for all

.

-direction. We now consider the function

∎

Lemma 4.4

\|h\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})},

where each
\tilde{h}^{ij}
is a smooth functions for
(x^{\prime},t)

is a universal constant.

In fact,

4.8
holds true even when the coefficients are
s
-polynomial, and the general case will be proved in

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
0<\alpha<1
with
k+1+\alpha-\frac{2}{2-\gamma}\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and assume
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})
. Suppose

is a solution of (
4.1
) satisfying
u=0
on
\{X\in\partial_{p}Q^{+}_{1}:x_{n}=0\}
. Then there exists an
s
-polynomial
p
of degree
m
corresponding to
\kappa\coloneqq k+2+\alpha
at
O
such that

|u(X)-p(X)|\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}+\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}\right)s[X,O]^{k+2+\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1}},

where
C>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

By considering
u/(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})})
for
\varepsilon>0
, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\leq 1
and
\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}\leq\varepsilon
. Since
f\in C^{k,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})
, we also assume that

(4.20)

\displaystyle|f(X)-F(X)|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon s[X,O]^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}},

where
F
is an
s
-polynomials with degree
\tilde{m}
corresponding to
(k+\alpha)
at
O
. By
Lemma

4.8
, there exists a function
h\in C_{s}^{k+2,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})
of the form (
4.19
) such that

(4.21)

|(F+L_{0}h-h_{t})(X)|\leq C_{*}\varepsilon s[X,O]^{k+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}

and
\|h\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})}\leq C_{*}\varepsilon,
where
C_{*}
is a universal constant.

Decompose
(u-h)
into the sum of
v^{1}
and
w^{1}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{1}_{t}&=L_{0}v^{1}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
v^{1}&=u-h&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{t}^{1}&=L_{0}w^{1}+\tilde{f}^{1}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
w^{1}&=0&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{f}^{1}=f+L_{0}h-h_{t}
. Then, combining (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) leads us to the estimate

(4.22)

|\tilde{f}^{1}(X)|\leq|f(X)-F(X)|+|(F+L_{0}h-h_{t})(X)|\leq(1+C_{*})\varepsilon s[X,O]^{k+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
.
By
Lemma

2.5
, for fixed
r\in(0,1)
, (
4.22
) gives

(4.23)

|w^{1}(X)|\leq C\|\tilde{f}^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq(1+C_{*})\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}

and hence, we have

\displaystyle|u(X)-v^{1}(X)-h(X)|=|w^{1}(X)|\leq(1+C_{*})\varepsilon\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}.

Choose now
\varepsilon
small enough such that
(1+C_{*})\varepsilon<r^{k+2+\alpha}
. Then,

|u(X)-v^{1}(X)-h(X)|\leq r^{k+2+\alpha}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}.

Let us now define

u^{2}(X)\coloneqq\frac{(u-v^{1}-h)(rX)}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{f}^{2}(X)\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{f}^{1}(rX)}{r^{k+\alpha}}.

Then,
\|u^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
and
u_{t}^{2}=L_{0}^{2}u^{2}+\tilde{f}^{2}
in
Q_{1}^{+}
, where

\displaystyle L_{0}^{2}=A^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}A^{i^{\prime}n}D_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}A^{nn}D_{nn}+rB^{i^{\prime}}D_{i^{\prime}}+x_{n}^{\gamma/2}rB^{n}D_{n}+r^{2}C^{-}.

Furthermore, (
4.22
) leads us to the estimate

\displaystyle|\tilde{f}^{2}(X)|

\displaystyle\leq r^{-(k+\alpha)}\left(\left|f(rX)-F(rX)\right|+\left|(F+L_{0}h-h_{t})(rX)\right|\right)\leq(1+C_{*})\varepsilon s[X,O]^{k+\alpha}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
. That is, the same hypotheses as before are fulfilled. Repeating the same procedure, we decompose
u^{2}
into the sum of
v^{2}
and
w^{2}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{2}_{t}&=L_{0}^{2}v^{2}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
v^{2}&=u^{2}&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+},\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{t}^{2}&=L_{0}^{2}w^{2}+\tilde{f}^{2}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
w^{2}&=0&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+},\end{aligned}\right.

and
|u^{2}(X)-v^{2}(X)|\leq r^{k+2+\alpha}
for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}
. By substituting back, we have

|u(X)-h(X)-v^{1}(X)-r^{k+2+\alpha}v^{2}(r^{-1}X)|\leq r^{2(k+2+\alpha)}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{r^{2}}^{+}}.

Continuing iteratively, for each integer
l\geq 3
, let us define the sequence of functions
\{u^{l}\}
inductively as follows:

u^{l}(X)\coloneqq\frac{(u^{l-1}-v^{l-1})(rX)}{r^{k+2+\alpha}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{f}^{l}(X)\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{f}^{l-1}(rX)}{r^{k+\alpha}}.

Then,
\|u^{l}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
and
u_{t}^{l}=L_{0}^{l}u^{l}+\tilde{f}^{l}
in
Q_{1}^{+}
, where

\displaystyle L_{0}^{l}

\displaystyle=A^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}A^{i^{\prime}n}D_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}A^{nn}D_{nn}+r^{l-1}B^{i^{\prime}}D_{i^{\prime}}+x_{n}^{\gamma/2}r^{l-1}B^{n}D_{n}+r^{2(l-1)}C^{-}.

Furthermore,
|\tilde{f}^{l}(X)|\leq(1+C_{*})\varepsilon s[X,O]^{k+\alpha}
for all
X\in\overline{Q_{1}^{+}}
, and hence we decompose
u^{l}
into the sum of
v^{l}
and
w^{l}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} v^{l}_{t}&=L_{0}^{l}v^{l}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
v^{l}&=u^{l}&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+},\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{t}^{l}&=L_{0}^{l}w^{l}+\tilde{f}^{l}&&\mbox{in }Q_{1}^{+}\\
w^{l}&=0&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+},\end{aligned}\right.

and
|u^{l}(X)-v^{l}(X)|\leq r^{k+2+\alpha}
for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r}^{+}}
. By substituting back, we have

(4.24)

\left|u(X)-h(X)-\sum_{i=1}^{l}r^{(i-1)(k+2+\alpha)}v^{i}(r^{-i+1}X)\right|\leq r^{l(k+2+\alpha)}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q_{r^{l}}^{+}}.

Since
k+1+\alpha-\frac{2}{2-\gamma}\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so we can choose
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

k+1+\alpha-\frac{2}{2-\gamma}<N<k+2+\alpha-\frac{2}{2-\gamma}.

By
Lemma

4.7
and (
4.18
), for each
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an
s
-polynomial

p^{l}(X)=\sum_{|\beta|+i+2j+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}<k+2+\alpha}A_{l}^{\beta ij}x^{\prime\beta}x_{n}^{1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2}i}t^{j}

of degree
(N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma})
at
O

such that

(4.25)

|v^{l}(X)-p^{l}(X)|\leq Cs[X,O]^{N+1+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}\quad\text{for all }X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1}},

where
C>0
is a universal constant. Combining (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) leads us to the estimate

\displaystyle\left|u(X)-h(X)-\sum_{i=1}^{l}r^{(i-1)(k+2+\alpha)}p^{i}(r^{-i+1}X)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq r^{l(k+2+\alpha)}+C\sum_{i=1}^{l}r^{(i-1)(k+2+\alpha)}s[r^{-i+1}X,O]^{N+1+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq r^{l(k+2+\alpha)}+Cr^{l(N+1+\frac{2}{2-\gamma})}\sum_{i=1}^{l}r^{-(i-1)(N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}-k-1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\quad=r^{l(k+2+\alpha)}+\frac{C(r^{(l+1)(N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}-k-1-\alpha)}-r^{N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}-k-1-\alpha})}{r^{N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}-k-1-\alpha}-1}r^{l(k+2+\alpha)}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\quad\leq Cr^{l(k+2+\alpha)}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r^{l}}^{+}}
. Now put

P^{l}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r^{(i-1)(k+2+\alpha)}p^{i}(r^{-i+1}X).

Then, (
4.26
) gives

\displaystyle|P^{l}(X)-P^{l-1}(X)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|u(X)-h(X)-P^{l}(X)\right|+\left|u(X)-h(X)-P^{l-1}(X)\right|

(4.27)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{l(k+2+\alpha)}

for all
X\in\overline{Q_{r^{l}}^{+}}
. Consider the rescaling
s
-polynomial

\displaystyle\tilde{P}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq P^{l}(r^{l}X)-P^{l-1}(r^{l}X)

\displaystyle=r^{(l-1)(k+2+\alpha)}\sum_{|\beta|+i+2j+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}<k+2+\alpha}r^{|\beta|+i+2j+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}A_{l}^{\beta ij}x^{\prime\beta}x_{n}^{1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2}i}t^{j}.

From (
4.27
), we know that
\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq Cr^{l(k+2+\alpha)}
. Since the coefficients of
s
-polynomial
\tilde{P}
on

are controlled by the

norm, we have

|A_{l}^{\beta ij}|\leq\frac{Cr^{k+2+\alpha}}{r^{|\beta|+i+2j+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}}

for all

and

with

. It follows that

converges uniformly to an

-polynomial

P^{\infty}(X)=\sum_{|\beta|+i+2j+\frac{2}{2-\gamma}<k+2+\alpha}B^{\beta ij}x^{\prime\beta}x_{n}^{1+\frac{2-\gamma}{2}i}t^{j}

of degree
(N+\frac{2}{2-\gamma})
at
O
such that

|u(X)-h(X)-P^{\infty}(X)|\leq Cs[X,O]^{k+2+\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\overline{Q^{+}_{1}}.

Finally, as in
Lemma

4.7
, if

is approximated with an
s
-polynomial, the desired

-polynomial

can be obtained.
∎

5.

5.1.

-Regularity

In this section, we establish

-regularity of solutions for (

-polynomials rather than constants, boundary

-regularity and higher regularity for equations with

).

For this section only,

is considered the operator with the following constant coefficients

A^{ij}=a^{ij}(O),\quad B^{i}=b^{i}(O),\quad C^{-}=c(O).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
with
2+\alpha\notin\mathcal{D}
, and assume

a^{ij},\,b^{i},\,c,\,f\in C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q_{1}^{+}})\quad(i,j=1,2,\cdots,n).

Suppose

is a solution of (

) satisfying

on

. Then
u\in C^{2+\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}})
and

\|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1/2}})}\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}+\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}\right),

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
\|a^{ij}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
,
\|b^{i}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
, and
\|c\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Approximation Lemma)
.

Let
0<\alpha<\min\{\frac{2}{2-\gamma},1\}
, and assume
f\in L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})
. Suppose
u\in C^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})\cap C(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})
is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
u=0
on
\{X\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}=0\}
with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
. If for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\|a^{ij}-A^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\|b^{i}-B^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\leq\varepsilon,\quad\|c-C^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\leq\varepsilon,

then there exists a solution
h\in C^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{3/4})\cap C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{3/4}})
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} h_{t}&=L_{0}h&&\mbox{in }Q_{3/4}^{+}\\
h&=u&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{3/4}^{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

such that

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{+})}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{\theta}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\right),

where
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
are universal constants.

Proof.

By
Corollary

3.2
and
Corollary

3.4
, there exists a unique bounded solution
h\in C^{2}(Q^{+}_{3/4})\cap C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{3/4}})
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} h_{t}&=L_{0}h&&\mbox{in }Q_{3/4}^{+}\\
h&=u&&\mbox{on }\partial_{p}Q_{3/4}^{+},\end{aligned}\right.

and

\|h\|_{C_{s}^{\alpha}(\overline{Q^{+}_{3/4}})}\leq C\Big(\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{+})}+\|u\|_{C_{s}^{\alpha}(\overline{Q_{3/4}^{+}})}\Big)\leq C\left(2+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right).

For
X\in\partial_{p}Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+}
, we can choose
Y\in\partial_{p}Q_{3/4}^{+}
such that
s[X,Y]=\delta
and hence we have

|u(X)-h(X)|\leq\|u-h\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{3/4}})}s[X,Y]^{\alpha}\leq C\delta^{\alpha}\left(2+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\right).

By
Lemma

2.5
and
Theorem

4.1
, we have for any
\delta\in(0,1/4)

\|h\|_{C_{s}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+}})}\leq C\delta^{-2-\alpha}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{+})}\leq C\delta^{-2-\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{+})}\leq C\delta^{-2-\alpha}.

Let
v=u-h
, then
v_{t}=Lv+\tilde{f}
in
Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+}
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=(a^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}-A^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}})h_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}(a^{i^{\prime}n}-A^{i^{\prime}n})h_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}(a^{nn}-A^{nn})h_{nn}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+(b^{i^{\prime}}-B^{i^{\prime}})h_{i^{\prime}}+x_{n}^{\gamma/2}(b^{n}-B^{n})h_{n}+(c-C^{-})h+f.

By
Lemma

2.5
and
Lemma

2.6
with scaling argument,

\displaystyle\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}C\left(\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}\right)&(0<\gamma<1)\\
C\left(\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial_{p}Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}+\|x_{n}^{-\gamma}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}\right)&(\gamma<0)\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\delta^{\alpha}(2+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})})+\varepsilon\delta^{-2-\alpha}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\right).

Take
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(2+\alpha)}}
and
\theta=\frac{\alpha}{2(2+\alpha)}
, we conclude that

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{3/4-\delta}^{+})}\leq C(\varepsilon^{\theta}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}).

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<\alpha<\min\{\frac{2}{2-\gamma},1\}
with

, and assume

Suppose

is a solution of (
1.1
) satisfying
u=0
on
\{X\in\partial_{p}Q_{1}^{+}:x_{n}=0\}
. Then there exists an
s
-polynomial

of degree

corresponding to
\kappa\coloneqq 2+\alpha
at

such that

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
,
\|a^{ij}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
,
\|b^{i}\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}
, and
\|c\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}

Proof.

By considering
u/(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})})

\varepsilon>0
, we may assume that

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} |a^{ij}(X)-A^{ij}|&\leq[a^{ij}]_{C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{1}})}s[X,O]^{\alpha}\leq\varepsilon\\

for all

. By considering

we may assume

. By

5.2
, there exists a bounded solutions
h^{1}\in C^{\infty}(Q_{3/4}^{+})\cap C^{\alpha}_{s}(\overline{Q^{+}_{3/4}})
of

such that

\|u-h^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2}^{+})}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{\theta}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{1})}\right)\leq 2C\varepsilon^{\theta}.

By
Theorem

4.1
,
h^{1}\in C_{s}^{2+\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})
and

(5.2)

\|h^{1}\|_{C_{s}^{2+\alpha}(\overline{Q_{1/2}^{+}})}\leq C,

where
C
is a universal constant. Let
r\in(0,1/2)
be a fixed number and choose now

small enough such that

. Then,

(5.3)

\|u-h^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{+})}\leq r^{2+\alpha}.

Let us now define

u^{2}(X)\coloneqq\frac{(u-h^{1})(rX)}{r^{2+\alpha}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{f}^{2}(X)\coloneqq\frac{(f+Lh^{1}-L_{0}h^{1})(rX)}{r^{\alpha}}.

Then,
\|u^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq 1
and
u_{t}^{2}=L^{2}u^{2}+\tilde{f}^{2}
in
Q_{1}^{+}
, where

\displaystyle L^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(rX)D_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+2x_{n}^{\gamma/2}a^{i^{\prime}n}(rX)D_{i^{\prime}n}+x_{n}^{\gamma}a^{nn}(rX)D_{nn}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+rb^{i^{\prime}}(rX)D_{i^{\prime}}+rx_{n}^{\gamma/2}b^{n}(rX)D_{n}+r^{2}c(rX).

From (
5.1
), (
5.2
), and (

), we can see that the same hypotheses as before are fulfilled. Replacing the decomposition of solutions with
Lemma

, as in the proof of

, we can show that there exists an
s
-polynomial
p^{\infty}
of degree

corresponding to
(2+\alpha)
at
O
such that

∎

Satisfying the assumptions of

, we know that

for any

. Applying the approximation lemma again to

for any

with

.

5.2.
Higher regularity of solutions for equations with

-polynomial coefficients

In the previous section,

-regularity can be obtained by freezing coefficients method. However due to the degeneracy/singular order of (

), higher regularity cannot be obtained inductively like the classical bootstrap method. We solve this problem by considering freezing coefficients by
s
-polynomial. In this section, we consider equations of the form

(5.4)

where the operator

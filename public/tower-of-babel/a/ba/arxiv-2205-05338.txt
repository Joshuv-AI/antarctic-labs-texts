Carleman inequalities and unique continuation for the polyharmonic operators
Eunhee Jeong, Yehyun Kwon, and Sanghyuk Lee

Abstract
. We obtain a complete characterization of L p − L q L^{p}-L^{q} Carleman estimates with weight e v ⋅ x e^{v\cdot x} for the polyharmonic operators. Our result extends the Carleman inequalities for the Laplacian due to Kenig–Ruiz–Sogge. Consequently, we obtain new unique continuation properties of higher order Schrödinger equations relaxing the integrability assumption on the solution spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
d
and
k
be positive integers and
\Omega
a non-empty connected open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We denote by
V
a complex-valued function defined on
\Omega
of which precise description to be given below. We say that the differential inequality

(1.1)

|\Delta^{k}u|\leq|Vu|\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega

has the
unique continuation property
(UCP) in the Sobolev space
W^{2k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if every
u\in W^{2k,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.1
) and vanishing on a non-empty open subset of
\Omega
is identically zero in
\Omega
.

When
k=1
and
d\geq 3
, the inequality (
1.1
) includes the Schrödinger equation
(-\Delta+V)u=0
as a special case, of which UCP has been extensively studied over the past several decades by numerous authors. We refer the reader to the survey articles
[
15
,
27
,
17
]
and
[
22
]
, and references therein. For
V\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{d/2}(\Omega)
, Kenig, Ruiz, and Sogge
[
16
]
proved that the inequality

(1.2)

|\Delta u|\leq|Vu|\ \ \text{in}\ \ \Omega

has the UCP in
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,p}(\Omega)
whenever
p>\frac{2d}{d+3}
. The main ingredient in their argument is the Carleman inequality of the form

(1.3)

\|e^{v\cdot x}u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|e^{v\cdot x}\Delta u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

where
C
is a constant independent of
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. For
p,q
satisfying

(1.4)

\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{2}{d}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tfrac{d+1}{2d}<\tfrac{1}{p}<\tfrac{d+3}{2d},

they deduced (
1.3
) from the more general
uniform Sobolev inequality

(1.5)

\|u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|(-\Delta+a\cdot\nabla+b)u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\ \ \forall(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\times\mathbb{C},

which holds if and only if (
1.4
) is satisfied.

In
[
13
]
the authors characterized the full range of
(p,q)
on which the Carleman inequality (
1.3
) holds. More precisely, under the assumption
1<p,q<\infty
, it was proved that (
1.3
) holds if and only if

\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{2}{d}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tfrac{d^{2}-4}{2d(d-1)}\leq\tfrac{1}{p}\leq\tfrac{d+2}{2(d-1)}.

This range is larger than that of (
1.4
) whenever
d\geq 4
. Consequently, the integrability assumption on the solution space in which the differential inequality (
1.2
) has the UCP can be relaxed. That is to say, (
1.2
) has the UCP in
\bigcup_{p>1}W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
d=3
or
4
, and in
W^{2,\frac{2(d-1)}{d+2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
d\geq 5
.

The polyharmonic operators
\Delta^{k}
,
k\geq 2
, are prototypical examples of higher order elliptic operators and have abundant applications in various physical contexts (
[
24
,
8
]
). In this paper, we aim to study the UCP of the differential inequality (
1.1
), which is a natural higher order analogue of (
1.2
). The UCP of (
1.1
) is closely tied to the Carleman inequality

(1.6)

\|e^{v\cdot x}u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|e^{v\cdot x}(-\Delta)^{k}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

with a constant
C
, independent of
v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

If
p=\frac{2d}{d+2k}
and
q=p^{\prime}=\frac{2d}{d-2k}
, then (
1.6
) can be obtained from the Carleman inequality with weights
|x|^{-\tau}
proved in
[
14
,
21
]
, as was observed by Wolff
[
27
]
for the case
k=1
. See
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
.

In this paper, we completely characterize the range of
p
and
q
, on which the Carleman inequality (
1.6
) holds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
. The inequality (
1.6
) holds if and only if

(1.7)

\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{2k}{d}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tfrac{(d+2k)(d-2)}{2d(d-1)}\leq\tfrac{1}{p}\leq\tfrac{d+2k}{2(d-1)}.

If
\frac{d-2}{2}\leq k<\frac{d}{2}
, then the second condition in (
1.7
) is vacuously true provided that
1<p,q<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{2k}{d}
. Hence, in this case, the inequality (
1.6
) holds for all
1<p,q<\infty
satisfying the gap condition
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{2k}{d}
. For
k<\frac{d-2}{2}
, however, the second condition in (
1.7
) is nontrivial.

By a standard density argument the Carleman inequality (

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we basically follow the strategy of the authors’ previous work
[
13
]
. This reduces the Carleman inequality (
1.6
) to obtaining

boundedness of a Fourier multiplier operator of which singularity is on the

embedded in

, and obtain sharp bounds for the dyadic pieces. In this approach, the Fourier restriction-extension operator (see Section

) defined by

naturally arises if we write the Fourier multiplier in the cylinderical coordinates in

.

However, direct use of the bounds on the restriction-extension operator as in
[

does not yield any sharp estimate when

. In such cases, the associated multiplier

can be regarded to have singularity of degree

. So, the bounds on the Fourier restriction-extension operator, whose multiplier is of order

, is not enough to handle the operator defined by

. Our novelty in this paper is in overcoming the difficulty by making use of the Bochner–Riesz operators of indices less than

If we make use of Theorem

and adjust the argument in
[

]
, then we obtain the following unique continuation result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq k<\frac{d}{2}
,
\Omega
a non-empty connected open set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and let

{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{X}}_{k,d}(\Omega):=\begin{cases}\bigcup_{p>1}W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2k,p}(\Omega)&\text{if}\ \ k\geq\frac{d-2}{2},\\
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2k,\frac{2(d-1)}{d+2k}}(\Omega)&\text{if}\ \ k<\frac{d-2}{2}.\end{cases}

Then, for every
V\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{d/2k}(\Omega)
the differential inequality (
1.1
) has the UCP in
\mathbf{X}
.

When
k=1
, Koch and Tataru
[
18
]
proved that the potential class
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{d/2}
is critical in the scale of Lebesgue spaces for (
1.2
) to have the UCP; for every
r<d/2
they constructed a nontrivial smooth function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\Delta u/u\in L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. By following their construction, it is possible to show that for every
k\in[2,d/2)
the
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{d/2k}
-potentials are critical for (
1.1
) to have the UCP in
C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(
[
12
]
).

On the other hand, the problem of finding the largest possible solution space where the UCP holds is an interesting problem. That is, given a
V\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{d/2k}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, one can ask whether or not the integrability of the solution space
\mathbf{X}
in Theorem
1.2
is optimal for the UCP of (
1.1
). Although we obtain Theorem
1.2
by means of the Carleman inequality (
1.6
), which is completely characterized in Theorem
1.1
, the condition (
1.7
) itself does not give the sharpness of the integrability assumption of
\mathbf{X}
in Theorem
1.2
. Even when
k=1
, it is an open problem to find the smallest
p^{\ast}
such that (
1.2
) with
V\in L^{d/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
has the UCP in
W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,p}(\Omega)
if
p>p^{\ast}
. Also, it seems an interesting open problem to consider the UCP of (
1.1
) when
k\geq d/2
.

Notations

For a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we denote by
C_{0}^{\infty}(A)
the class of smooth functions each of which can be extended to a smooth function defined on some open set
U
containing
A
and supported in
A
. Denoting by
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
a multi-index we use the standard notation

W^{m,p}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega)\colon\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty,\,|\alpha|\leq m\}

for the usual Sobolev space. Moreover,
W^{m,p}_{0}(\Omega)
denotes the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
. For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we define

\|T\|_{p\to q}=\sup\{\|Tf\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\colon f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ \|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=1\}

for a linear operator
T
acting on the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For the Fourier transform we follow the convention
\widehat{f}(\xi)={\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx
, so the inverse Fourier transform is defined by
f^{\vee}(x)={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}{\mathcal{F}}f(-x)
. For a bounded function
m
we denote by
m(D)
the associated Fourier multiplier operator, that is,
m(D)f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(m{\mathcal{F}}f)
. We slightly abuse notation so that the dimension of the Fourier transform and its inversion may vary depending on context.

2.
The Bochner–Riesz operator of negative index

Let us recall from
[
11
, Chapter III]
the analytic family of distributions
\chi_{+}^{a}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
defined by

\chi_{+}^{a}=\frac{x_{+}^{a}}{\Gamma(a+1)},\ \ \mathrm{Re\,}a>-1.

\chi_{+}^{a}
can be continued analytically to all
a\in\mathbb{C}
so that
d\chi_{+}^{a}/dx=\chi_{+}^{a-1}
. Since
\chi_{+}^{0}
is the Heaviside function it follows that
\chi_{+}^{-k}=\delta_{0}^{(k-1)}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us denote by
\lambda
the smooth function from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

n\geq 2

\lambda(\theta)=1-|\theta|^{2}.
Then the Bochner–Riesz operator

on

of (negative) index

is the Fourier multiplier operator defined by

T_{\alpha}f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big((\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-\alpha}){\mathcal{F}}f\big),

where
\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is the pullback of
\chi_{+}^{-\alpha}
via the function
\lambda
(see
[
11
, Chapter VI]
). In particular, denoting by
d\sigma_{S^{n-1}}
the surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
we have

(2.1)

\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-1}=\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}=c\,d\sigma_{S^{n-1}}

for some constant
c
depending on
n
. Hence
T_{1}
is the Fourier restriction-extension operator on the sphere
S^{n-1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
be integers, and let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the duality pairing on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\times C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Then, for
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
we have

(2.6)

\langle\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-k},\phi\rangle=\langle\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-1},L^{k-1}\phi\rangle,

where
L
is a differential operator defined by

L\phi(\theta)=\frac{1}{2|\theta|^{2}}(n-2+\theta\cdot\nabla)\phi(\theta),\ \ \theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

It is enough to prove (
2.6
) for
k\geq 2
. We denote by
\{\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\colon 1\leq j\leq n\}
the standard orthonormal frame on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The chain rule (see, e.g.,
[
11
, p. 135]
) gives

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}\partial_{j}\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-2)}=\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}\partial_{j}\lambda=-2|\theta|^{2}\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-1)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-k},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-1)},\phi\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big\langle\tfrac{\theta_{j}}{|\theta|^{2}}\partial_{j}\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-2)},\phi\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\lambda^{\ast}\delta_{0}^{(k-2)},\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}\big(\tfrac{\theta_{j}\phi}{|\theta|^{2}}\big)\big\rangle=\langle\lambda^{\ast}\chi_{+}^{-(k-1)},L\phi\rangle.

Repeating this argument we get the identity (
2.6
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d
and

be positive integers such that

and let

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{P}_{k}^{d}\cup[B_{k}^{d},D_{k}^{d}]
, then

(2.8)

\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\tau)e^{it\tau}\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-k},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{iy\cdot\theta}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau\bigg\|_{L^{q,\infty}_{x}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{C^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

uniformly in
\rho\sim 1
. Here

and
(\theta,\tau)
denote the spatial and frequency variables, respectively, in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
. In fact, if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in(B_{k}^{d},D_{k}^{d}]

Proof.

We only prove the restricted weak type estimate (
2.8

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in P_{k}^{d}\cup(B_{k}^{d},D_{k}^{d}]
follow similarly by using the estimates (

We note

\int\phi(\tau)e^{it\tau}\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-k},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{iy\cdot\theta}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau=2\pi\int\phi^{\vee}(t-s)\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-k},\mathcal{F}(f(\cdot,s))(\theta)e^{iy\cdot\theta}\big\rangle_{\theta}ds,

where

denotes the

(2.9)

\int\phi(\tau)e^{it\tau}\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-k},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{iy\cdot\theta}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau=(2\pi)^{d}\int\phi^{\vee}(t-s)T_{k,\rho}(f(\cdot,s))(y)ds,

where

is the
d-1

-k
. We also use the following simple inequalities (see, e.g.,
[
13
, p. 780]

(2.10)

\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}_{x}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{q,\infty}_{y}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{p,1}_{y}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p,1}_{x}}.

Making use of the equality (

2.10
), and Minkowski’s inequality,

\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(\tau)e^{it\tau}\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-k},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{iy\cdot\theta}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau\bigg\|_{L^{q,\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\vee}(t-s)\|T_{k,\rho}(f(\cdot,s))\|_{L^{q,\infty}_{y}}ds\bigg\|_{L^{q}_{t}}.

Applying Young’s inequality, the estimate (
2.5
) with
n=d-1
, and the second inequality in (
2.10
), we see that

\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\vee}(t-s)\|T_{k,\rho}(f(\cdot,s))\|_{L^{q,\infty}_{y}}ds\bigg\|_{L^{q}_{t}}\lesssim\|\phi^{\vee}\|_{L^{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{p,1}_{y}}\leq\|\phi^{\vee}\|_{L^{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}_{x}}

for
r\in[1,\infty]
satisfying
1+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}.
Since
\mathrm{supp\,}\phi\subset[-2,2]
, we have
\|\phi^{\vee}\|_{r}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{C^{2}}
for every
r\in[1,\infty]
. Therefore, we obtain (
2.8
).
∎

3.
Carleman inequalities

Let us start with three simple observations. First, if
v=0
then (
1.6
) is the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality, which is valid if and only if
1<p,q<\infty
satisfy the gap condition

(3.1)

\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{q}=\tfrac{2k}{d}.

Hence it is enough to consider
v\neq 0
. Secondly, since the estimate (
1.6
) is invariant under rotations we may assume
v=|v|e_{d}
. Thirdly, by scaling it is easy to see that the gap condition (
3.1
) is necessary for (
1.6
) to hold.

Under the condition (
3.1
), by rescaling the Carleman inequality (
1.6
) (with nonzero
v
) is equivalent to the following estimate for a single Fourier multiplier operator:

(3.2)

\bigg\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)}{(|\xi|^{2}+2i\xi_{d}-1)^{k}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

We first make a decomposition in
\xi_{d}
. Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-2,-1/2]\cup[1/2,2])
be a nonnegative even function such that
\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi(2^{-j}t)=1
for
t\neq 0
, and let
\psi_{0}(t)=1-\sum_{j\geq 1}\psi(2^{-j}t)
. We write
\xi=(\eta,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and, fixing a small positive dyadic number
{\varepsilon}_{\circ}\leq 2^{-5}
, set
\mathbb{D}:=\{2^{\nu}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{\circ}]\colon\nu\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Then, we break

(3.3)

\frac{1}{(|\eta|^{2}-1+\tau^{2}+2i\tau)^{k}}=m_{L}(\eta,\tau)+m_{G}(\eta,\tau)

where

(3.4)

m_{L}(\eta,\tau)=\sum_{{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{D}}m_{\varepsilon}(\eta,\tau):=\sum_{{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{\psi_{0}({\varepsilon}_{\circ}^{-1}(1-|\eta|^{2}))\psi({\varepsilon}^{-1}\tau)}{(|\eta|^{2}-1+\tau^{2}+2i\tau)^{k}}.

Since
m_{G}
vanishes in a neighborhood of the set
S^{d-2}\times\{0\}
where the original multiplier is singular, it is clear that

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\big(|\xi|^{2k}m_{G}(\xi)\big)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. Thus, the Mikhlin multiplier theorem and the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality yield the inequalities

\|m_{G}(D)f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{-2k}\widehat{f}\,)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
p,q
satisfying
1<p\leq q<\infty
and (
3.1
). Thus, the remaining task is to clarify
L^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of
m_{L}(D)
.

3.1.
Estimates for localized frequencies

In the summation (
3.4
), if we rescale each term
m_{\varepsilon}
by
\tau\to{\varepsilon}\tau
, then

(3.5)

\|m_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}={\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q},

where

(3.6)

\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(\eta,\tau)=m_{\varepsilon}(\eta,{\varepsilon}\tau)=\frac{\psi_{0}({\varepsilon}_{\circ}^{-1}(1-|\eta|^{2}))\psi(\tau)}{(|\eta|^{2}-1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2}+2i{\varepsilon}\tau)^{k}}.

Hence, it is enough to study
L^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of
\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)
instead of
m_{\varepsilon}(D)
, which is the main result in this section (see Proposition
3.1
).

Let us set
C=(\frac{1}{2},0)
and

A^{d}=\big(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{d-2}{2d}\big)\ \ \text{for}\ \ d\geq 3.

For a positive integer
k
such that

, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{d}_{k}

\displaystyle=[A^{d},B^{d}_{k},C,D^{d}_{k}]\setminus[B^{d}_{k},D^{d}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbf{Q}\colon\tfrac{1}{2}\leq x<\tfrac{d-2+2k}{2(d-1)},\,0\leq y\leq\tfrac{d-2}{d}(1-x)\big\}.

See Figures

and

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{D}
. If
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}^{d}_{k},
then

(3.7)

\|\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d-2+2k}{2}}.

The estimate (
3.7
), combined with the identity (
3.5
), yields the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{D}
. If
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{k}^{d}
, then

(3.8)

\|m_{{\varepsilon}}(D)\|_{p\to q}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{d-2+2k}{2}}.

In particular, if

\|m_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d+2}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-k}.

The second estimate (

In order to prove Proposition

as follows. For

\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\eta,\tau)=\frac{\zeta(\delta^{-1}(1-|\eta|^{2}))\psi(\tau)}{(|\eta|^{2}-1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2}+2i{\varepsilon}\tau)^{k}},\ \ (\eta,\tau)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}.

When

, we simply write

. Also, notice that the multiplier

defined in (

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and
L
the differential operator defined in Lemma
2.1
with
n
replaced by
d-1
. That is,

Lh(\eta)=\frac{1}{2|\eta|^{2}}(d-3+\eta\cdot\nabla)h(\eta),\ \ \eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}.

For
h\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})
supported away from the origin, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{{\varepsilon}^{\ell}_{\circ}(k-1-\ell)!\ell!}\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell)}](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-1-\ell}h\rangle,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-1}h\rangle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-1)!}{\delta^{\ell}\ell!}\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-\ell}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell)},\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle.

Proof.

First, we prove the identity (
3.11
). It is trivial if
k=1
, so we assume
k\geq 2
. Direct differentiation gives

\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta](\eta,\tau)=-\frac{1}{k-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}_{\circ}}\widetilde{m}^{k-1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{\prime}](\eta,\tau)+\frac{\eta}{2|\eta|^{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\eta}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}^{k-1}[\zeta](\eta,\tau)\bigg).

Hence, integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}_{\circ}}\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{\prime}](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\langle\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}^{k-1}[\zeta](\cdot,\tau),\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta_{j}}(\frac{\eta_{j}}{2|\eta|^{2}}h)\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(k-1){\varepsilon}_{\circ}}\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{\prime}](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle+\frac{1}{k-1}\langle\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}^{k-1}[\zeta](\cdot,\tau),Lh\rangle.

By this identity and the induction hypothesis we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(k-1){\varepsilon}_{\circ}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{{\varepsilon}^{\ell}_{\circ}(k-2-\ell)!\ell!}\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell+1)}](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-2-\ell}h\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{{\varepsilon}^{\ell}_{\circ}(k-2-\ell)!\ell!}\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell)}](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-1-\ell}h\rangle.

Rearranging the summands gives (
3.11
).

Since
L^{t}=-\frac{\eta}{2|\eta|^{2}}\cdot\nabla
we see that

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{m}^{j}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\cdot,\tau),Lh\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle L^{t}\widetilde{m}^{j}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\langle\widetilde{m}^{j}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{\prime},\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle+j\langle\widetilde{m}^{j+1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle

for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, we prove the second identity (
3.12
). It is clear when
k=1
, so let us assume
k\geq 2
. By the induction hypothesis,

\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,\delta](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-1}h\rangle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{(k-2)!}{\delta^{\ell}\ell!}\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-1-\ell}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell)},\delta](\cdot,\tau),Lh\rangle.

By (
3.13
) this is equal to

\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-2}\frac{(k-2)!}{\delta^{\ell}\ell!}\bigg(\frac{1}{\delta}\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-1-\ell}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell+1)},\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle+(k-1-\ell)\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-\ell}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta^{(\ell)},\delta](\cdot,\tau),h\rangle\bigg),

from which (
3.12
) follows.
∎

In the following, making use of Tomas–Stein’s restriction estimate (

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

and
\frac{2d}{d-2}\leq q\leq\infty
. For
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big([-2,2]\setminus[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]\big)
,

, and

When

the estimate also holds with
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{0}([-2,2])

Proof.

For brevity’s sake, let us set

M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}=\widetilde{m}^{k}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta,2^{j}{\varepsilon}].

By interpolation it is enough to prove that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}(D)f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}(D)f\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Obviously,

\displaystyle|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}(D)f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim|\mathrm{supp\,}M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

If
\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{0}\big([-2,2]\setminus[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]\big)
, then

is supported in

(3.16)

\displaystyle\big\{(\eta,\tau)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\colon\sqrt{1-2^{j+1}{\varepsilon}}\leq|\eta|\leq\sqrt{1-2^{j-1}{\varepsilon}},\ \tfrac{1}{2}\leq|\tau|\leq 2\big\}

\displaystyle\cup\big\{(\eta,\tau)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}\colon\sqrt{1+2^{j-1}{\varepsilon}}\leq|\eta|\leq\sqrt{1+2^{j+1}{\varepsilon}},\ \tfrac{1}{2}\leq|\tau|\leq 2\big\}.

Hence it is clear that

. On the support of

which is greater than

since

. Hence

\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{-k}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})},

If

. Thus, a similar argument gives (
3.14
) with

and
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-2,2])
.

Thus, by the Minkowski inequality
\|M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}(D)f\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}
is bounded by a constant times

(3.19)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\psi}\bigg(\frac{1-\rho^{2}}{2^{j}{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{S^{d-2}}e^{i(\rho y\cdot\theta+t\tau)}\big(M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\widehat{f}\big)(\rho\theta,\tau)d\sigma(\theta)d\tau\right\|_{L_{y,t}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}}\rho^{d-2}d\rho.

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{S^{d-2}}e^{i(\rho y\cdot\theta+t\tau)}\big(M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\widehat{f}\big)(\rho\theta,\tau)d\sigma(\theta)d\tau\right\|_{L_{y,t}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\left\|\int_{S^{d-2}}e^{i\rho y\cdot\theta}\big(M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\widehat{f}\big)(\rho\theta,\tau)d\sigma(\theta)\right\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}({\mathbb{R}})}\bigg\|_{L_{y}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})}

Since

\lesssim\big\|\big\|\big(M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\widehat{f}\big)(\rho\theta,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}(S^{d-2})}\big\|_{L_{\tau}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\big\|\big(M^{k}_{{\varepsilon},j}\widehat{f}\big)(\rho\theta,\tau)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,\tau}(S^{d-2}\times{\mathbb{R}})}.

Let us denote by

the support of the function

, that is,

\mathcal{I}_{j}:=\begin{cases}\big[\sqrt{1-2^{j+1}{\varepsilon}}\,,\sqrt{1-2^{j-1}{\varepsilon}}\,\big]\cup\big[\sqrt{1+2^{j-1}{\varepsilon}}\,,\sqrt{1+2^{j+1}{\varepsilon}}\,\big]&\text{if}\ \ j\geq 1,\\[3.0pt]
\big[\sqrt{1-2{\varepsilon}}\,,\sqrt{1+2{\varepsilon}}\,\big]&\text{if}\ \ j=0.\end{cases}

From the Minkowski inequality and Lemma
2.2
it follows that

\displaystyle\|I_{\ell,j}\|_{L^{q,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\bigg(\frac{1-\rho^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\bigg)\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau}\phi_{{\varepsilon},\ell}(\rho,\tau)\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-(k-\ell)},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{i\theta\cdot y}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau\bigg\|_{L_{y,t}^{q,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}{\varepsilon}\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau}\phi_{{\varepsilon},\ell}(\rho,\tau)\big\langle\lambda_{\rho}^{*}\chi_{+}^{-(k-\ell)},\widehat{f}(\theta,\tau)e^{i\theta\cdot y}\big\rangle_{\theta}d\tau\bigg\|_{L_{y,t}^{q,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}{\varepsilon}\bigg[\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\|\phi_{{\varepsilon},\ell}(\rho,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}}\bigg]\|f\|_{L^{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

for
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in[B^{d}_{k},D^{d}_{k}]
. Elementary calculation shows that
\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal{I}_{j}}\|\phi_{{\varepsilon},\ell}(\rho,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}}\lesssim(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{-1}
(see Lemma
3.5
below). Hence we obtain (
3.22
).

Now, we turn to prove (
3.23
). We cannot follow the strategy of the proof of (
3.22
) which relies on (
3.24
) and boundedness of the Bochner–Riesz operator
T_{k-\ell}
of order
-(k-\ell)
, since
T_{k-\ell}
is unbounded from
L^{2}
to

for any

and
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
. To get over the issue, using the identity (
3.12
), we integrate by parts again (in the definition (
3.21
) of
I_{\ell,j}
) to remove
L^{k-1-\ell}
and then apply Lemma
3.4
.

Let us define
\zeta_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-2,2])
and
\zeta_{j}\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-2,2]\setminus[-1/2,1/2])
,
j\geq 1
, by setting

\zeta_{j}(t)=\begin{cases}\psi_{0}^{(\ell)}({\varepsilon}t/{\varepsilon}_{\circ})\psi_{0}(t)&\text{if}\ \ j=0,\\
\psi_{0}^{(\ell)}(2^{j}{\varepsilon}t/{\varepsilon}_{\circ})\psi(t)&\text{if}\ \ j\geq 1.\end{cases}

Then

\psi_{j}({\varepsilon}^{-1}(1-|\eta|^{2}))\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\psi_{0}^{(\ell)}](\eta,\tau)=\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{j},2^{j}{\varepsilon}](\eta,\tau),\ \ j\geq 0,

so

I_{\ell,j}(y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau}\big\langle\widetilde{m}^{1}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{j},2^{j}{\varepsilon}](\cdot,\tau),L^{k-1-\ell}h_{y,\tau}\big\rangle d\tau.

Applying the identity (
3.12
) we have

\displaystyle I_{\ell,j}(y,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1-\ell}\frac{(k-1-\ell)!}{(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{r}r!}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\tau}\big\langle\widetilde{m}^{k-\ell-r}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{j}^{(r)},2^{j}{\varepsilon}](\cdot,\tau),h_{y,\tau}\big\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k-1-\ell}\frac{(k-1-\ell)!}{(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{r}r!}\iint\widetilde{m}^{k-\ell-r}_{\varepsilon}[\zeta_{j}^{(r)},2^{j}{\varepsilon}](\eta,\tau)\widehat{f}(\eta,\tau)e^{i(y\cdot\eta+t\tau)}d\eta d\tau.

if

and

is bounded uniformly in

and

\|I_{\ell,j}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\sum_{r=0}^{k-1-\ell}(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{-r}(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}-(k-\ell-r)}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim(2^{j}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}-k+\ell}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

3.2.
Sharpness of the local estimates

The estimate (
3.8
) is sharp in the sense that the exponent of
{\varepsilon}
in (
3.8
) cannot be made larger. To show this, we begin with a general fact regarding
L^{p}-L^{q}
norm of Fourier multipliers.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. For
a\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

(3.25)

\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}=\|\overline{a}(D)\|_{p\to q}.

Consequently,

(3.26)

\|\mathrm{Re\,}a(D)\|_{p\to q}\leq\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \|\mathrm{Im\,}a(D)\|_{p\to q}\leq\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}.

Proof.

By the definition,
\overline{a}(D)f(x)=\overline{a(D)h(-x)}
where
h
is defined by
\widehat{h}(\xi)=\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi)}
so that
h(x)=\overline{f(-x)}
. Hence

\|\overline{a}(D)f\|_{L^{q}}=\|a(D)h\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}\|h\|_{L^{p}}=\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}\|f\|_{L^{p}},

which shows
\|\overline{a}(D)\|_{p\to q}\leq\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}
. Similarly,
\|a(D)\|_{p\to q}\leq\|\overline{a}(D)\|_{p\to q}
, and this gives the identity (
3.25
). The inequalities in (
3.26
) are clear.
∎

Sharpness of the estimate (
3.9
) can be proved by a Knapp type example adapted to the cylinder
S^{d-2}\times[1/2,2]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
d
and
k
be positive integers and let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Then, if
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\delta_{\circ}}
for some small
{\delta_{\circ}}>0
,

\|m_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}\gtrsim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d+2}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-k}.

Proof.

By (
3.5
) it is enough to prove

\|\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}\gtrsim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-k},

and furthermore, by the second inequality in (
3.26
), we need only show

(3.27)

\|\mathrm{Im\,}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)\|_{p\to q}\gtrsim{\varepsilon}^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-k}.

We note that
\mathrm{Im\,}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(\eta,\tau)
is equal to

(3.28)

\frac{\psi_{0}({\varepsilon}_{\circ}^{-1}(1-|\eta|^{2}))\psi(\tau)}{((|\eta|^{2}-1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2})^{2}+4{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2})^{k}}\sum_{l=1}^{\lfloor\frac{k+1}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{l}\binom{k}{2l-1}(|\eta|^{2}-1+{\varepsilon}^{2}\tau^{2})^{k-2l+1}(2{\varepsilon}\tau)^{2l-1}.

Let us choose a nonnegative smooth function
\phi
on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\mathrm{supp\,}\phi\subset[1/2,2]
and
\phi=1
on

, and define

for a small constant

and
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\delta_{\circ}}
. It is clear that

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}\sim(\delta_{\circ}{\varepsilon})^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{2p}}.

If

, we have

and

. This yields

if

whenever
\delta_{\circ}
is small enough. Clearly,
\mathrm{Im\,}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}
is either negative or positive on

. Hence, if
x
lies in the set

S_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\colon|x_{d}|\leq 10^{-3},\,|x_{d-1}|\leq{\varepsilon}^{-1},\,|x_{j}|\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ \text{for}\ 1\leq j\leq d-2\big\}

for
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\delta_{\circ}}
and
\delta_{\circ}
is small enough, then

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Im\,}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(D)f_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle\geq\bigg|\iint\cos\Big(x_{d}\tau+x_{d-1}(\eta_{d-1}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}x_{j}\eta_{j}\Big)\mathrm{Im\,}\widetilde{m}_{\varepsilon}(\eta,\tau)\widehat{f_{\varepsilon}}(\eta,\tau)d\eta d\tau\bigg|

), this implies

which gives the desired estimate (
3.27
).
∎

We can also prove that the estimate (

Therefore, the proof of (

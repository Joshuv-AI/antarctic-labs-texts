Vectorized implementation of primal hybrid FEM in MATLAB
Harish Nagula Mallesham, Kamana Porwal, Jan Valdman, Sanjib Kumar Acharya

Abstract
We present efficient MATLAB implementations of the lowest-order primal hybrid finite element method (FEM) for linear second-order elliptic and parabolic problems with mixed boundary conditions in two spatial dimensions. We employ the Crank-Nicolson finite difference scheme for the complete discrete setup of the parabolic problem. All the codes presented are fully vectorized using matrix-wise array operations. Numerical experiments are conducted to show the performance of the software.

Introduction

MATLAB is a popular computing platform in academia and industry, with many built-in functions and toolboxes developed by Mathworks Computer Software Corporation. However, codes containing for loops, particularly the assembly of finite elements of stiffness and mass matrices in MATLAB are extremely slow compared to C and FORTRAN (cf.
Alberty et al. 1999
;
Bahriawati and Carstensen 2005
). To overcome this issue, an efficient and flexible MATLAB assembly procedure of nodal element stiffness and mass matrices for 2D and 3D was introduced by Rahman and Valdman
Rahman and Valdman 2013
. They vectorized for-loops by extending the element-wise array operations into matrix-wise array operations, where the array elements are matrices rather than scalars, resulting in a faster and more time-scalable algorithm. This idea is exploited for the MATLAB assembly of 2D and 3D edge elements by Anjam and Valdman in
Anjam and Valdman 2015
. In
Funken et al. 2011
, Funken et al. adopted a similar approach while assembling the element stiffness matrices. For more detailed literature, we refer to Cuvelier et al., see
Cuvelier et al. 2016
, Moskovka and Valdman
Moskovka and Valdman 2022
and the references therein.

In this paper, we present an efficient MATLAB implementation procedure of the primal hybrid finite element method, which is based on an extended variational principle introduced by Raviart and Thomas in
Raviart and Thomas 1977
for elliptic problems. Acharya and Porwal
Acharya and Patel 2016
;
Acharya and Porwal 2020
extended this technique to parabolic second- and fourth-order problems and established the optimal order error estimates. The benefit of this method is that two unknown variables (primal and hybrid) can be computed simultaneously without losing accuracy. The constrained space using Lagrange multipliers leads to a non-conforming space; for instance, the lowest-order primal hybrid FEM (using unconstrained space) is equivalent to the Crouzeix-Raviart nonconforming (using constrained space) finite element space. It is also called the generalized nonconforming method. For a detailed review of the literature and applications, see
Acharya and Patel 2016
;
Acharya and Porwal 2020
;
Wriggers and Carstensen
;
Oliari et al. 2024
and the references therein. To the best of the author’s knowledge, no work has been reported on the implementation procedure of this method.

The main contributions are as follows.

•

Edge generation function edge.m (cf.
Bahriawati and Carstensen 2005
) and a uniform mesh refinement function based on red-refinement (cf.
Funken and Schmidt 2021
;
Bey 2000
) have been vectorized using matrix-wise array operations.

•

We present a vectorized implementation of the lowest-order primal hybrid FEM for a second-order general elliptic problem with mixed boundary conditions.

•

Also, we implement this method for the general second-order parabolic problem with mixed boundary conditions using the Crank-Nicolson finite difference scheme in the temporal direction.

•

Numerical experiments have been conducted, and run-time has been presented. The experiments were carried out using MATLAB R2020a on a computer equipped with an
\mathrm{i}5-10210\mathrm{U}
CPU operating at a frequency of 1.60GHz-2.10 GHz, with 16GB RAM and a
\mathrm{x}64
-based processor with 1TB of system memory. A linear regression analysis shows that the codes have an approximate optimal-order time scale.

•

The software can be downloaded through the following link:

https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/136359
.

3
Implementation of second order elliptic model problem

3.1
Problem formulation

Consider the general second-order elliptic problem

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u+up)+\delta u

\displaystyle=f~\text{in}~~\Omega,

(3.1)

\displaystyle~~u

\displaystyle=u_{D}~~\text{on}~~\Gamma_{D},

(3.2)

\displaystyle~~(A\nabla u+up)\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=g~~\text{on}~~\Gamma_{N},

(3.3)

where
A
is a symmetric and positive definite matrix whose entries are constants,
p
is a constant vector and
\delta
is a constant. Also,
f,u_{D}
, and
g
are assumed to be sufficiently smooth.

We consider the broken Sobolev space (cf.
Raviart and Thomas 1977
), i.e., a space of functions that belong to Sobolev space
H^{1}(T)
on each element
T
,

X=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):{v|}_{T}\in H^{1}(T),\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}=\displaystyle\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}H^{1}(T).

The broken Sobolev space has the norm

||v||_{X}=\bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}||\nabla v||^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We shall denote by
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
the space of traces
{v|}_{\Gamma}

over
\Gamma
of the functions
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
denotes the dual space of
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.
The Lagrange multiplier space is defined as

\displaystyle M=\bigg\{\chi\in\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}H^{-1/2}(\partial T/\Gamma_{N}):

\displaystyle~\text{there exists}~\textbf{q}\in H(\text{div},\Omega)~\text{such that},~\textbf{q}\cdot\nu_{T}=\chi~\text{on}~\partial T/\Gamma_{N},\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigg\}.

The Lagrange multiplier space is provided with the norm

\|\chi\|_{M}=\displaystyle\sup_{v\in X}\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\langle\chi,v\rangle_{E}}{{\|v\|}_{X}},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E}
denotes the duality paring between
H^{-1/2}(E)
and

H^{1/2}(E)
.

Primal hybrid formulation of (
3.1
)-(
3.3
) is to seek a pair of solutions

(u,\kappa)\in X\times M
such that

\displaystyle a(u,v)+b(v,\kappa)

\displaystyle=(f,v)+\langle g,v\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}}\hskip 5.69046pt\forall v\in X,

(3.4)

\displaystyle b(u,\chi)\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle=\langle u_{D},\chi\rangle,\hskip 19.91684pt\forall\chi\in M,

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\left((A\nabla u+up)\cdot\nabla v+\delta uv\right)\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle~b(v,\chi)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\langle\chi,v\rangle_{E},

\displaystyle\langle u_{D},\chi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\langle\chi,u_{D}\rangle_{E}.

Here, the Lagrange multiplier

\kappa=(A\nabla u+up)\cdot\nu_{T}\hskip 5.69046pton~\partial T/\Gamma_{N},~\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

(3.6)

is associated with the constraint
u\in\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):v=u_{D}~\text{on}~\Gamma_{D}\right\}
.

3.2
Discrete problem and algebraic formulation

Let
P_{k}
be the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
, for integer
k\geq 0
. We define the finite-dimensional spaces

\displaystyle X_{h}=\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}P_{1}(T),

\displaystyle M_{h}=\bigg\{\chi_{h}\in\prod_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}P_{0}(E):{\chi_{h}|}_{\partial T_{+}}+{\chi_{h}|}_{\partial T_{-}}=0~\text{on}~T_{+}\cap T_{-},~\text{for any adjacent pair}~T_{\pm}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigg\}.

Implementation of second order parabolic model problem

4.1
Parabolic problem and algebraic formulation

Consider a second-order parabolic model problem with mixed boundary condition

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u+up)+\delta u

\displaystyle=f(x,t)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,t_{\tilde{N}}],

(4.1)

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=u_{D}(x,t)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{on}~\Gamma_{D}\times(0,t_{\tilde{N}}],

(4.2)

\displaystyle(A\nabla u+ub)\cdot\nu

\displaystyle=g(x,t)~~\text{on}~~\Gamma_{N}\times(0,t_{\tilde{N}}]

(4.3)

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x)\hskip 8.5359pt\text{in}~\Omega,

(4.4)

where
f
,
g
and
u_{0}
are appropriate smooth functions.
Primal hybrid formulation of (
4.1
)-(
4.4
) is to seek a pair of solutions

(u,\kappa):[0,t_{\tilde{N}}]\rightarrow X\times M
such that

\displaystyle(u_{t},v)+

\displaystyle a(u,v)+b(v,\kappa)=(f,v)+\langle g,v\rangle_{\Gamma_{N}},\hskip 5.69046pt\forall v\in X,

(4.5)

\displaystyle b(u,\chi)\qquad\qquad=\langle u_{D},\chi\rangle,\hskip 19.91684pt\forall\chi\in M,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad u(0)=u_{0},

(4.7)

Let the time interval
[0,t_{\tilde{N}}]
be partitioned into equally spaced points

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{\tilde{N}}

with time step parameter
k
such that
\tilde{N}=t_{\tilde{N}}/k
and
t_{n}=nk
for
n\in\{1,2,\dots,\tilde{N}\}
.
For
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}[0,t_{\tilde{N}}]
,
we set the backward difference operator
\partial
as

\partial\varphi^{n}=(\varphi^{n}-\varphi^{n-1})/k

and for
t_{n-1/2}=(n-1/2)k
the midpoint value as

\varphi^{n-1/2}=(\varphi^{n}+\varphi^{n-1})/2.

By employing the Crank-Nicolson implicit scheme in the time direction, we seek a solution pair

(u_{h}^{n},\kappa_{h}^{n})\in X_{h}\times M_{h}

satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\partial u_{h}^{n}v_{h}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\nabla u_{h}^{n-1/2}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}

\displaystyle(u_{h}^{n-1/2}p)\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\delta u_{h}^{n-1/2}v_{h}\,{\rm d}x-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\int_{E}\kappa_{h}^{n-1/2}[\![v_{h}]\!]_{E}\,{\rm d}\gamma

\displaystyle=F^{t_{n-1/2}}(v_{h}),

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\int_{E}\chi_{h}[\![u_{h}^{n-1/2}]\!]_{E}\,{\rm d}\gamma

\displaystyle=-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}/\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\int_{E}\chi_{h}u^{n-1/2}_{Dh}\,{\rm d}\gamma,

for any testing pair
(v_{h},\chi_{h})\in X_{h}\times M_{h}
. Here,

F^{t_{n-1/2}}(v_{h})=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}f(t_{n-1/2})v_{h}\,{\rm d}x+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\int_{E}g(t_{n-1/2})v_{h}\,{\rm d}\gamma.

Remark 3
.

For this implicit scheme, we obtain a second-order convergence for the primal variable in
L^{2}
-norm (cf.
Thomée
), one can construct a complete discrete setup using any other implicit or explicit scheme.

Using the matrices
\mathbb{M},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{D}
, and
\mathbb{C}
constructed for the elliptic case, we write the above algebraic formulation in matrix-vector form,

\text{M}_{+}~\text{W}^{n}=\text{M}_{-}~\text{W}^{n-1}+\text{F}.

(4.8)

Here,

\displaystyle\text{M}_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(1\pm\frac{1}{2}k\delta)\mathbb{M}\pm\frac{1}{2}k\mathbb{B}\pm\frac{1}{2}k\mathbb{D}&\quad\mp\frac{1}{2}k\mathbb{C}^{\prime}\\
\mp\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}&0\\
\end{array}\right)_{(N+L)\times(N+L)},

\displaystyle\text{W}^{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U^{n}\\
\Lambda^{n}\\
\end{array}\right)_{(N+L)\times 1},\quad\text{W}^{n-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U^{n-1}\\
\Lambda^{n-1}\\
\end{array}\right)_{(N+L)\times 1},

\displaystyle\text{F}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}kF^{n-1/2}\\
b_{D}^{n-1/2}\\
\end{array}\right)_{(N+L)\times 1},\quad F^{n-1/2}=(F_{j}^{n-1/2})_{N\times 1}.

For global basis functions
\phi_{j}
,

\displaystyle F_{j}^{n-1/2}=b^{n-1/2}_{j}+LN^{n-1/2}_{j},

where

\displaystyle b^{n-1/2}_{j}

\displaystyle\approx\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}f^{n-1/2}\phi_{j}\,{\rm d}x,\quad LN^{n-1/2}_{j}\approx\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}\int_{E}g^{n-1/2}\phi_{j}\,{\rm d}\gamma.

Then,

b^{n-1/2}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}b^{n-1}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}b^{n}_{j}\approx\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}\left(f^{n-1}\phi_{j}+f^{n}\phi_{j}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

Here,
b^{n}_{j}
is obtained as follows

\displaystyle b^{n}_{j}

\displaystyle\approx\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{|T|}{3}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(m_{E_{i}},t_{n})\phi_{j}(m_{E_{i}}).

(4.9)

The Neumann boundary term
LN^{n-1/2}_{j}=\displaystyle(LN^{n-1}_{j}+LN^{n}_{j})/2
, and
LN^{n}_{j}
is obtained by

LN^{n}_{j}\approx\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}^{h}_{N}}|E|g(m_{E},t_{n})\phi_{j}(m_{E}),~~~~~~\text{where}~m_{E}\in\mathcal{N}_{m}(\Gamma_{N}).

(4.10)

The component related to the Dirichlet boundary
b_{D}^{n-1/2}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle b_{D}^{n-1/2}=(b_{D_{l}}^{n-1/2})_{L\times 1},\quad b_{D_{l}}^{n-1/2}\approx-\displaystyle\int_{E_{l}}\psi_{l}u^{n-1/2}_{Dh}\,{\rm d}\gamma.

Similarly, we obtain
b_{D_{l}}^{n-1/2}=(b_{D_{l}}^{n-1}+b_{D_{l}}^{n})/2
, and
b_{D_{l}}^{n}
is obtained by

\displaystyle b_{D_{l}}^{n}\approx-\displaystyle\int_{E_{l}}\psi_{l}u^{n}_{Dh}\,{\rm d}\gamma

\displaystyle\approx-|E_{l}|\sigma_{l}u^{n}_{Dh}(m_{E_{l}}),\quad\text{where}~m_{E_{l}}\in\mathcal{N}_{m}(\Gamma_{D})

\displaystyle=-|E_{l}|u^{n}_{Dh}(m_{E_{l}}),\quad\text{since}~\sigma_{l}=1~\text{for}~l=1,\dots,L.

(4.11)

The functions
\mathbf{StiffMassConv\_PH.m}
and
\mathbf{Lambda\_PH.m}
for the parabolic model problem will be similar to those for the elliptic model problem, which will handle the assembly of the matrices
\mathbb{M},~\mathbb{B},~\mathbb{C},~\mathbb{D}

F^{n-1/2}
at
t_{n-1/2}

t_{n}

t_{n-1}

\mathbf{load\_t.m}

\mathbf{ParabolicMain.m}

\mathbf{load\_t.m}
calculates the values of
b^{n}
(
4.9
) and
LN^{n}
(
4.10
) at different time points
t_{n}

5
Concluding remarks

Primal hybrid FEM has been implemented efficiently in MATLAB for general two-dimensional elliptic and parabolic problems with mixed boundary conditions. Numerical experiments show that the codes are of nearly linear time-scaling, and the method converges optimally. The presented codes are flexible and may be extended to non-linear problems with an adequate amount of modification. We will include the implementations for the three-dimensional case in our future work.

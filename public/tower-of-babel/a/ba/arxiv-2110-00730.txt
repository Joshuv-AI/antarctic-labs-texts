Gibbs measures for SOS models with external field on a Cayley tree


Abstract
We consider a nearest-neighbor solid-on-solid (SOS) model, with several spin values 0 , 1 , … , m , 0,1,\ldots,m, m ≥ 2 , m\geq 2, and nonzero external field, on a Cayley tree of degree k k (with k + 1 k+1 neighbors). We are aiming to extend the results of [ 6 ] where the SOS model is studied with (mainly) three spin values and zero external field. The SOS model can be treated as a natural generalization of the Ising model (obtained for m = 1 m=1 ). We mainly assume that m = 2 m=2 (three spin values) and study translation-invariant (TI) and splitting (S) Gibbs measures (GMs).(Splitting GMs have a particular Markov-type property specific for a tree.) For m = 2 m=2 , in the antiferromagnit (AFM) case, a TISGM is unique for all temperatures with an external field. In the ferromagnetic (FM) case, for m = 2 , m=2, the number of TISGMs varies with the temperature and the external field: this gives an interesting example of phase transition. Our second result gives a classification of all TISGMs of the Three-State SOS-Model on the Cayley tree of degree two with the presence of an external field. We show uniqueness in the case of antiferromagnetic interactions and existence of up to seven TISGMs in the case of ferromagnetic interactions, where the number of phases depends on the interaction strength and external field.

Cayley tree, Gibbs measure, SOS model, external field, configuration.

2
Set-up

Following
[
8
]
we start by summarizing the basic consepts for Gibbs measures on a Cayley tree, and also fix some notation.

Cayley tree.
Let
\mathbb{T}^{k}
be a (homogeneous) Cayley tree of degree
k\geq 2
, that is, an infinite connected cycle-free (undirected) regular graph with each vertex incident to
k+1
edges. For example,
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{Z}.
Denote by
V=\{x\}
the set of the vertices of the tree and by
E=\{\langle x,y\rangle\}
the set of its (non-oriented) edges connecting pairs of neighbouring vertices. The natural distance
d(x,y)
on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
is defined as the number of edges on the unique path connecting vertices
x,y\in V.
In particular,
\langle x,y\rangle\in E
whenever
d(x,y)=1.
A (non-empty) set
\Lambda\subset V
is called
connected
if for any
x,y\in\Lambda
the path connecting
x
and
y
lies in
\Lambda.
We denote the complement of
\Lambda
by
\Lambda^{c}:=V\backslash\Lambda
and its
boundary
by
\partial\Lambda:=\{x\in\Lambda^{c}:\exists y,d(x,y)=1\},
and we write
\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda\bigcup\partial\Lambda.
The subset of edges in
\Lambda
is denoted
E_{\Lambda}:=\{\langle x,y\rangle\in E:x,y\in\Lambda\}.

Fix a vertex
x_{0}\in V
, interpreted as the
root
of the tree. We say that
y\in V
is a
direct successor
of
x\in V
if
x
is penultimate vertex on the unique path leading from the root
x_{0}
to the vertex
y
; that is,
d(x_{0},y)=d(x_{0},x)+1
and
d(x,y)=1
. The set of all direct successors of
x\in V
is denoted by
S(x).
It is convenient to work with the family of the radial subsets centred at
x_{0}
, defined for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0\}\bigcup\mathbb{N}
by

V_{n}:=\{x\in V:d(x_{0},x)\leq n\},\quad W_{n}:=\{x\in V:d(x_{0},x)=n\},

interpreted as the ’ball’ and ’sphere’, respectively, of radius
n
centred at the root
x_{0}
. Clearly,
\partial V_{n}=W_{n+1}.
Note that if
x\in W_{n}
then
S(x)=\{y\in W_{n+1}:d(x,y)=1\}
. In the special case
x=x_{0}
we have
S(x_{0})=W_{1}.
For short, we set
E_{n}:=E_{V_{n}}.

Remark 1
Note that the sequence of balls (
V_{n}
) (
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) is
cofinal
(see
[
14
]
, section 1.2, page 17, see also
[
8
]
), that is, any finite subset
\Lambda\subset V
is contained in some
V_{n}.

The SOS model and Gibbs measures.
In the SOS model with external field, the spin at each vertex
x\in V
can take values in the set
\Phi:=\{0,1,...,m\}.
Thus, the spin configuration on
V
is a function
\sigma:V\rightarrow\Phi
and the set of all configurations is
\Phi^{V}
. For a subset
\Lambda\subset V
, we denote by
\sigma_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Phi
the restriction of configuration
\sigma
to
\Lambda,

\sigma_{\Lambda}:=\sigma(x),\quad x\in\Lambda.

The SOS model with external field is defined by the formal Hamiltonian

(2.1)

H(\sigma)=-J\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle\in E}|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)|-\sum_{x\in V}\alpha_{\sigma(x),x},

where
\sigma\in\Phi^{V},\penalty\
J\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\textbf{\textalpha}}_{x}=(\alpha_{0,x},\alpha_{1,x},...,\alpha_{m,x})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is the external (possible random) field.

Here,
J<0
gives a ferromagnetic and
J>0
an anti-ferromagnetic model.

For each finite subset
\Lambda\subset V
(
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
) and any fixed subconfiguration
\eta\in\Phi^{\Lambda^{c}}
(called the
configurational boundary condition
), the
Gibbs distribution

\gamma^{\eta}_{\Lambda}
is a probability measure in
\Phi^{\Lambda}
defined by the formula

(2.2)

\gamma^{\eta}_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)=\frac{1}{Z^{\eta}_{\Lambda}(\beta)}\exp\Big\{-\beta H_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)+\beta J\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{c}}|\varsigma(x)-\eta(y)|\Big\},\quad\varsigma\in\Phi^{\Lambda},

where
\beta\in(0,\infty)
is a parameter (having the meaning of
inverse temperature
),
H_{\Lambda}
is the restriction of the Hamiltonian (
2.1
) to subconfigurations in
\Lambda,

(2.3)

H_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)=-J\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle\in E_{\Lambda}}|\varsigma(x)-\varsigma(y)|-\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\alpha_{\varsigma(x),x},\quad\varsigma\in\Phi^{\Lambda},

and
Z^{\eta}_{\Lambda}
is the normalizing constant (often called the
canonical partition function
),

Z^{\eta}_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\varsigma\in\Phi^{\Lambda}}\exp\Big\{-\beta H_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)+\beta J\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{c}}|\varsigma(x)-\eta(y)|\Big\}.

Due to the nearest-neighbour interaction, formula (
2.2
) can be rewritten as

(2.4)

\gamma_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)=\frac{1}{Z^{\eta}_{\Lambda}}\exp\Big\{-\beta H_{\Lambda}(\varsigma)+\beta J\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\partial\Lambda}|\varsigma(x)-\eta(y)|\Big\}.

Finally, a measure
\mu=\mu_{\beta,\alpha}
on
\Phi^{V}
is called a
Gibbs measure
if, for any non-empty finite set
\Lambda\subset V
and any
\eta\in\Phi^{\Lambda^{c}},

(2.5)

\mu(\sigma_{\Lambda}=\varsigma|\sigma_{\Lambda^{c}}=\eta)\equiv\gamma^{\eta}_{\Lambda}(\varsigma),\quad\varsigma\in\Phi^{V}.

SGM construction.
It is convenient to construct Gibbs measures on the Cayley tree
\mathbb{T}^{k}
using a version of Gibbs distributions on the balls (
V_{n}
) defined via auxiliary fields encapsulating the interaction with the exterior of the balls. More precisely, for a vector field
V\ni x\mapsto h_{x}=(h_{0,x},...,h_{m,x})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define a probability measure in
V_{n}
by the formula

(2.6)

\mu^{h}_{n}(\sigma_{n})=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\Big\{-\beta H_{n}(\sigma_{n})+\beta\sum_{x\in W_{n}}h_{\sigma_{n}(x),x}\Big\},\quad\sigma_{n}\in\Phi^{V_{n}},

where
Z_{n}=Z_{n}(\beta,h)
is the normalizing factor and
H_{n}:=H_{V_{n}}
, that is (see (
2.3
))

(2.7)

H_{n}(\sigma_{n})=-J\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle\in E_{n}}|\sigma_{n}(x)-\sigma_{n}(y)|-\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\alpha_{\sigma_{n}(x),x},\quad\sigma_{n}\in\Phi^{V_{n}}.

The vector field
\{h_{x}\}_{x\in V}
in (
2.6
) is called
generalized boundary condition (GBC)
.

We say that the probability distributions (
2.6
) are compatible (and the intristic GBC
\{h(x)\}
are
permissible
) if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the following identity holds,

(2.8)

\sum_{\omega\in\Phi^{W_{n+1}}}\mu^{h}_{n+1}(\sigma\vee\omega)\equiv\mu^{h}_{n}(\sigma),\quad\sigma_{n}\in\Phi^{V_{n}},

where the symbol
\vee
stands for concatenation of subconfigurations. A criterion for permissibility of GBC is provided by Theorem 1 (see below). By Kolmogorov’s extension theorem (see
[
19
]
, chapter II, section 3, theorem 4, page 167), the compatibility condition (
2.8
) ensures that there exists a unique measure
\mu^{h}=\mu^{h}_{\beta,\alpha}
on
\Phi^{V}
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(2.9)

\mu^{h}(\sigma_{V_{n}}=\sigma_{n})\equiv\mu^{h}_{n}(\sigma_{n}),\quad\sigma_{n}\in\Phi^{V_{n}},

or more explicitly (substituting (
2.6
)),

(2.10)

\mu^{h}_{n}(\sigma_{V_{n}}=\sigma_{n})=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\exp\Big\{-\beta H_{n}(\sigma_{n})+\beta\sum_{x\in W_{n}}h_{\sigma_{n}(x),x}\Big\},\quad\sigma_{n}\in\Phi^{V_{n}}.

Definition.
Measure
\mu^{h}
satisfying (
2.9
) is called a
splitting Gibbs measure (SGM)
.

Remark 2.
Note that adding a constant
c=c_{x}
to all coordinates
h_{i,x}
of the vector
\textbf{h}_{x}
does not change the probability measure (
2.6
) due to the normalization
Z_{n}
. The same is true for the external field
\textbf{\textalpha}_{x}
in the Hamiltonian (
2.1
). Therefore, without loss of generality we can consider
reduced GBC

\tilde{\textbf{h}}_{x}
, for example defined as

\tilde{h}_{i,x}=h_{i,x}-h_{m,x},\quad i=0,...,m-1.

The same remark also applies to the external field
α
and its reduced version
\tilde{\textbf{\textalpha}}(x)
defined by

\tilde{\alpha}_{i,x}:=\alpha_{i,x}-\alpha_{m,x}\quad i=0,...,m-1.

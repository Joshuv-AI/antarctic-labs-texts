Interior pointwise C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} estimates for Stokes systems in divergence form Note: Research supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China [grant 12001419 and grant 12071365] and the Fundamental Research Funds for the Central Universities [grant xzy012022004].
Rong Dong, Dongsheng Li

Abstract
Interior pointwise C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} estimates are established for Stokes systems in divergence form where no continuity in time variable is assumed for the coefficients and the given data. The estimates are attained by iteration and are presented by Campanato’s characterization. The sharpness of the conclusions can be seen from Serrin’s counterexample.

Introduction

The primary purpose of this paper is to establish interior pointwise
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for Stokes systems with variable coefficients in divergence form. Precisely, the system concerned here is the following

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptu_{t}+\mathcal{L}u+\nabla p=\mbox{div}f~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{T};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\mbox{div}~u=g~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{T},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where

\mathcal{L}:=-\mbox{div}\Big(A(x,t)\nabla\Big)=:-D_{i}\Big(a^{ij}(x,t)D_{j}\Big),

\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(-T,0]
is a cylinder with
T>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
).
Throughout this paper, the summation convention is assumed and the coefficient matrix
A(x,t)=\big(a^{ij}(x,t)\big)_{n\times n}
always satisfies

(
\bf H
) Ellipticity:
There exists
\lambda\in(0,1]
such that

a^{ij}(x,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2}~~\mbox{and}~~|a^{ij}(x,t)|\leq\lambda^{-1}

for any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
and
\xi=(\xi_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For Stokes systems with constant coefficients, Solonnikov
[
14
]
established the global
C^{2,\alpha}

(Schauder) estimates by using potential theory. Later, he further developed the global
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates to Stokes systems in non-divergence form with variable coefficients (cf.
[
15
]
and the references therein).
In
[
1
]
, Abe and Giga showed the analyticity of the Stokes semigroup in
L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
, where the above estimates played an important part. For the Stokes systems in divergence form (1.1), the counterpart is the
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates and this area seems remaining almost untouched. Here are some related interesting results with constant coefficients
and divergence free velocities.
Chang and Kang
[
4
]
solved the system in Hölder spaces by giving a global Hölder estimate of the velocities. Naumann and Wolf
[
12
]
showed that any weak solution is Hölder continuous with respect to the spatial variables that belong to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Since Stokes systems are parabolic with degeneration, the global regularity heavily depends on the boundary data (cf.
[
1
]

[
4
]

[
14
]
and
[
15
]
)
and the interior regularity is very different from the global ones.
This paper will investigate the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for (1.1).
From Serrin’s counterexample (cf.
[
13
]
or the following Remark 1.3 (ii)), one may find that the velocity is not Hölder continuous in the time variable even though the coefficients are constant and the righthand side terms are vanishing. That is, the regularity in the time direction is weak.
Actually, we establish the interior pointwise
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of velocities in the spatial variables provided
A
and
f
are only Hölder continuous in spatial variables, and
\nabla g
is bounded. (This much improves the results in
[
12
]
.)
However an astonished result is that for curls of velocities, the Hölder continuity holds with respect to both spatial variables and time variable. (Observe no Hölder continuity in time variable is assumed for the coefficients and the righthand side terms.)
A corresponding result for
L_{p}
estimates of (non-degenerate) parabolic equations is given by Krylov
[
10
]
, where
W^{2}_{p}
and
W^{1}_{p}
estimates were presented for equations in non-divergence form and divergence form respectively
as the coefficients are only VMO in spatial variables. Such results (cf. Lemma 2.4 in the following) will be used in our proofs.
The conclusions in this paper are similar to directional regularity which one tries to reveal under conditions possessing regularity only on some directions.
Tian and Wang
[
17
]
obtained directional
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity for elliptic equations, while Dong and Kim
[
5
]
further gave directional
C^{1,\alpha}
and
C^{2,\alpha}
estimates for both elliptic and parabolic equations. Dong, Li and Wang
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
studied directional homogenization problems and found different regularity between homogenization directions and non-homogenization directions.

Pointwise interior
C^{2,\alpha}
and
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates for fully nonlinear elliptic equations were originated by L.Caffarelli in his celebrated paper
[
2
]
.
Roughly speaking, under some structure regularity for the fully nonlinear elliptic operators, the previous estimate states that the solution will be
C^{2,\alpha}
continuous at the same point if the nonlinear elliptic operator and the righthand side term are Hölder continuous at
x_{0}
; and the latter gives the
C^{1,\alpha}
continuity of solutions as the oscillation of the nonlinear elliptic operator is of small BMO and the righthand side term is of
C^{\alpha-1}
at the point concerned.
We will use the idea in
[
2
]
(cf. also
[
3
]
) to show the pointwise
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate for the velocity of (1.1) in the space direction. That is, the velocity is approximated at a
(1+\alpha)
-th order of the scales by a spatial variable first order polynomial whose coefficients may depend on the time. The approximation is indeed Campanato’s characterization of
C^{1,\alpha}
(cf.
[
8
]

[
9
]
and
[
11
]
) and the polynomial will be obtained by an iteration. In the iteration, the velocity, its gradient and its curl should be involved simultaneously and beyond expectation, for the curl of the velocity, the pointwise
C^{\alpha}
estimate is achieved. We will estimate the curl of velocity by the gradient of it via parabolic equations, while estimate the gradient by the curl and velocity itself via elliptic equations.

The following are the main results of this paper.

.

.

Let

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 14.22636pt\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(-T,0;L_{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\times u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\|f\|_{C_{x}^{\alpha}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\nabla g\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega_{T})}\Big)

where

n
,
\lambda
,
\alpha
,

Remark 1.3
.

(i) Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 are effectively
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity for weak solutions of Stokes systems in divergence form.

(ii) Let

be a harmonic function in
\Omega
and
\sigma
be a function of

. Then
u(x,t)=\nabla h(x)\int_{-T}^{t}\sigma(s)ds
and

is a pair of weak solution of (1.1) with vanishing

and

.
(This counterexample was given by Serrin
[

]

It is easy to see that

(iii) Observe that

has Hölder continuity in

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

,

and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}}\sup_{r>0}\Big(\fint_{Q_{r}(x,t)\cap\Omega_{T}}|u(y,s)-\overline{u}_{Q_{r}(x,t)\cap\Omega_{T}}|^{2}dyds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Ar^{\alpha}

for some constant

depending only on
\alpha
,
n
and
\Omega_{T}
.

(ii) If there exist constants

and functions

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T>0
,
a(t)\in L_{\infty}(-T,0;\mathbb{R})
and
b(t)\in L_{\infty}(-T,0;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
If there exist
A
and

such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(-T,0]}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|a(t)-b(t)x|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq A,

(2.1)

then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(-T,0]}|a(t)|\leq CA~~\mbox{and}~~\sup_{t\in(-T,0]}|b(t)|\leq CA/r

(2.2)

for some constant
C
depending only on
n
.

Proof.
Denote
P_{1}
the space of one order polynomials and suppose

with
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since both

and
\left(\int_{B_{1}}|a+bx|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
are norms on

,
we have they are equivalent, that is, there exists a constant

depending only on

It follows that

C^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|a+bx|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\max\{|a|,r|b|\}\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{r}}|a+bx|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

2.1
).
∎

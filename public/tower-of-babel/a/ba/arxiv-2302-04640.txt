Prefixes of the Fibonacci word
Jeffrey Shallit

Abstract
Mignosi, Restivo, and Salemi (1998) proved that for all ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 there exists an integer N N such that all prefixes of the Fibonacci word of length ≥ N \geq N contain a suffix of exponent α 2 − ϵ \alpha^{2}-\epsilon , where α = ( 1 + 5 ) / 2 \alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2 is the golden ratio. In this note we show how to prove an explicit version of this theorem with tools from automata theory and logic. Along the way we gain a better understanding of the repetitive structure of the Fibonacci word.



1
Introduction

The Fibonacci word
{\bf f}[0..\infty)=01001010\cdots
is a
well-studied
infinite binary word with many interesting properties
[
2
]
.
It can be defined in many ways; for example, as the limit of the
sequence of finite words
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
, with
X_{1}=1
,
X_{2}=0
,
and
X_{n}=X_{n-1}X_{n-2}
for
n\geq 3
.

Let
x=x[0..n-1]
be a nonempty finite word.
We say
x
has
period

p
if

x[i]=x[i+p]
for
0\leq i<n-p
. The least positive period of
a word
x
is called
the
period, and is denoted
\per(x)
. The

exponent
of a word, denoted
\exp(x)
, is defined to be
|x|/\per(x)
. Thus

entente
has exponent
7/3
. We say a word
x
is
an
\alpha
-power
, for
\alpha\geq 1
a real number, if

|x|=\lceil\alpha\per(x)\rceil
. Thus, for example,

underfund
is a
\sqrt{2}
-power.

Let
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
be the golden ratio.
In 1997 this author conjectured, and Mignosi, Restivo, and Salemi
later proved
[
5
]
, that
an infinite word
\bf x
has the property
that every sufficiently long prefix
has a suffix that is an
\alpha^{2}
-power if and only if
\bf x
is ultimately
periodic. This is an example of the “local periodicity implies
global periodicity” phenomenon.

Furthermore, they proved that the constant
\alpha^{2}
is best possible.
Define
e(n)
to be the largest exponent of a suffix of
{\bf f}[0..n-1]
and
for a real number
\gamma

define
M_{\gamma}=\{n\,:\,e(n)\geq\gamma\}
.
Mignosi et al. proved that for all
\epsilon>0
,
the set
M_{\alpha^{2}-\epsilon}
contains all but finitely many natural
numbers.

This latter result was proved as their Proposition 2, using a
somewhat complicated induction. In this note we
show how to prove an explicit version of the result using
Walnut
,
a free theorem-prover for automatic sequences
[
6
,
9
]
.
We do not claim our proof to be simpler than the previous one,
but it does give a more explicit
version of the theorem, and is more straightforward in some sense.
As a bonus, we gain a more detailed understanding of the repetitive
properties of the prefixes of
\bf f
.

2
Prefixes

Define, as usual, the Fibonacci numbers
by the recurrence
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
with initial values

F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
, and the Lucas numbers

L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}
with initial values

L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
.
By the Binet formulas we have

F_{n}=(\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})/\sqrt{5}
, where

\beta=(1-\sqrt{5})/2
, and

L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}
, which also serve to define

F_{n}
and
L_{n}
for negative indices. Note that

F_{-n}=(-1)^{n+1}F_{n}
and

L_{-n}=(-1)^{n+1}L_{n}

for all integers
n
.

By a result of Currie and Saari
[
3
, Cor. 4]
,
we know that all (least) periods of the
Fibonacci word are of length
F_{n}
for
n\geq 2
.
(This can also be proved with
Walnut
; see

[
7
, Thm. 3.15]
.)
Thus, if we are to look
for large repetitions, we can restrict our attention to
periods of length a Fibonacci number.

We start by determining
G:=M_{\alpha^{2}}
: that is,
those “good”
n
for which
{\bf f}[0..n-1]

has a suffix of exponent
>\alpha^{2}
.
With
Walnut
we can characterize
the set

G=\{13,14,22,23,24,26,27,34,35,36,37,38,39,40,43,\ldots\}.

To do so, we need
to phrase the assertions as a first-order logical formula in a
language
Walnut
can understand.
We represent numbers in the so-called
Fibonacci (or Zeckendorf) numeration systems;
see
[
4
]
or
[
10
]
for more details.
Here is the
Walnut
code:

reg isfib msd_fib "0*10*":
reg evenfib msd_fib "0*1(00)*":
reg oddfib msd_fib "0*10(00)*":
reg adjfib msd_fib msd_fib "([0,0]*[1,1])|[0,0]*[1,0][0,1][0,0]*":
def ffactoreq "?msd_fib At (t<n) => F[i+t]=F[j+t]":
def suff "?msd_fib n>=x & x>=y & y>=1 & $ffactoreq(n-x,(n+y)-x,x-y)":
reg shift {0,1} {0,1} "([0,0]|[0,1][1,1]*[1,0])*":
def phi2n "?msd_fib (s=0 & n=0) | Ex,y $shift(n-1,x) &
$shift(x,y) & s=y+2":
def good "?msd_fib Ex,y,z $suff(n,x,y) & $phi2n(y,z) & x>z":

The explanation is as follows:

•

asserts that its argument is a positive Fibonacci
number.

•

asserts that its argument is a positive

•

asserts that its argument is a positive

•

.

•

asserts that

•

is code from
[

asserts that
s=\lfloor\alpha^{2}n\rfloor
.

•

{\bf f}[0..n-1]
has a suffix
of length

with period

.

•

asserts that
{\bf f}[0..n-1]
has a suffix
with exponent

\displaystyle B_{1}

.

For (2) we let

def b1 "?msd_fib $isfib(x) & $isfib(y) & x>2*y & y>=2 & n+1+y=x":

.

(Ex,y $b1(n,x,y)|$b2(n,x,y)) )":

returns

.

3
The main result

We will need a classic identity about Fibonacci numbers
[
1
, p. 68]
, which
can be easily proved with the Binet formula, namely,

F_{a}F_{b+2}-F_{a+2}F_{b}=(-1)^{b}F_{a-b}

(1)

for all integers
a,b
.

Next, we need some estimates.

Lemma 3
.

For
i\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}+{{(-1)^{i}}\over{F_{2i}}}

\displaystyle<\hphantom{ck}{{F_{i+2}}\over{F_{i}}}

\displaystyle<\alpha^{2}+{{(-1)^{i}}\over{F_{2i}-(-1)^{i}}}

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}+{1\over{F_{2i-1}+2}}

\displaystyle<{{F_{2i+1}+1}\over{F_{2i-1}}}

\displaystyle<\alpha^{2}+{1\over{F_{2i-1}}}.

(3)

Proof.

Directly follows from the Binet formula.
∎

Finally, we need some technical estimates.

Lemma 4
.

(i)

(F_{k+1}+1)^{2}\leq 3F_{2k-1}
for
k\geq 4
;

(ii)

F_{4k-2}^{2}\geq 100F_{2k+1}
for
k\geq 3
.

(iii)

F_{2k+2}\leq 6F_{k}^{2}
for
k\geq 5
.

(iv)

F_{2k}^{2}\geq 8F_{2k+2}
for
k\geq 4
.

(v)

F_{12k-4}^{2}\geq 10000F_{6k+3}
for
k\geq 2
.

(vi)

F_{2k+1}^{2}F_{6k+3}\leq 6F_{6k-2}^{2}
for

k\geq 2
.

(vii)

F_{4k+2}^{2}\geq 4F_{6k+5}
for
k\geq 3
.

Proof.

We prove only (i) and (iii); the others are simpler and are left to the reader.
From the Binet form, we can verify that

3F_{2k-1}-(F_{k+1}+1)^{2}={1\over 5}(8F_{2k-3}+4F_{2k-2}-10F_{k+1}-5-2(-1)^{k})\geq 0

for
k\geq 4
. This proves (i).

Similarly, we can verify that

6F_{k}^{2}-F_{2k+2}={1\over 5}(3F_{2k-1}-4F_{2k-2}-12(-1)^{k})\geq 0

for
k\geq 5
. This proves (iii).
∎

Theorem 5
.

For all
n\geq 1
the prefix
{\bf f}[0..n-1]

has a suffix of exponent
\geq\alpha^{2}-3n^{-1/2}
.

Proof.

We can check the result for
1\leq n\leq 21
. In what follows, then,
we assume
n>21
.
There are three cases to consider:

Case 1:

n\in G
. In this case, we have
already seen above that there is a suffix
of
{\bf f}[0..n-1]
of exponent
>\alpha^{2}
.

Case 2:

n\in B_{1}
, that is,

n=n_{i,j}=F_{i}-F_{j}-1
for
i\geq 5
and
3\leq j\leq i-2
.
Fix
i
; using Lemma
1

we will find a lower bound for
e(n_{i,j})
applicable
to all
j
,
3\leq j\leq i-2
. Note that
n_{i,j}\leq F_{i}
.
Define

\displaystyle f(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{j}-1)/F_{j-2}

\displaystyle g(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i}-F_{j}-1)/F_{i-2}

\displaystyle\rho(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i}-F_{j}-1)F_{j-2}-(F_{j}-1)F_{i-2}.

Clearly
g(i,j)\geq f(i,j)
iff
\rho(i,j)\geq 0
.

We now claim that
f
is increasing with increasing
j
.
To see this, note that

\displaystyle f(i,j+1)-f(i,j)

\displaystyle={{F_{j+1}-1}\over{F_{j-1}}}-{{F_{j}-1}\over{F_{j-2}}}

\displaystyle={{F_{j+1}F_{j-2}-F_{j-1}F_{j}+F_{j-1}-F_{j-2}}\over{F_{j-1}F_{j-2}}}

\displaystyle={{(-1)^{j+1}+F_{j-3}\over{F_{j-1}F_{j-2}}}}\geq 0

for
j\geq 3
. Here we have used
Eq. (
1
) with
a=j-2
and
b=j-1
.

Similarly,
g
is decreasing with increasing
j
.
To see this, note that

g(i,j+1)-g(i,j)={{F_{j}-F_{j+1}}\over{F_{i-2}}}\leq 0

for
i\geq 3
. Hence, for each fixed
i\geq 3
,
there is a “crossover point” where the graphs of

f(i,j)
and
g(i,j)
cross. This crossover point determines
where
\min_{j}\max(f(i,j),g(i,j))
occurs. See
Figure
2
for an example for
i=20
.

Figure 2:
The crossover point for
f(20,j+2)
versus
g(20,j+2)
.

Observe that
\rho(i,j)
is increasing with increasing
i
:

\displaystyle\rho(i+1,j)-\rho(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i+1}-F_{j}-1)F_{j-2}-(F_{j}-1)F_{i-1}-(F_{i}-F_{j}-1)F_{j-2}+(F_{j}-1)F_{i-2}

\displaystyle=F_{i-1}F_{j-2}-(F_{j}-1)F_{i-3}

\displaystyle=F_{i-3}-(-1)^{j}F_{i-j-1}\geq 0.

Here we used Eq. (
1
) with
a=i-3
and
b=j-2
.

Using the Binet form one can verify that

\displaystyle\rho(2k+1,k+1)

\displaystyle={1\over 5}(L_{2k-2}-5F_{k-1}-5F_{-k}-3(-1)^{k})

\displaystyle\rho(2k+2,k+2)

\displaystyle={1\over 5}(-L_{2k-2}-5F_{k}+5F_{-k}+3(-1)^{k}).

It follows that
\rho(2k+2,k+1)\geq\rho(2k+1,k+1)>0
for
k\geq 4
and
and
\rho(2k+1,k+2)\leq\rho(2k+2,k+2)<0
for
k\geq 1
.
Set
j^{\prime}=\lceil i/2\rceil
.
Hence
g(i,j^{\prime})-f(i,j^{\prime})\geq 0
for
i\geq 6
and

g(i,j^{\prime}+1)-f(i,j^{\prime}+1)<0
for
i\geq 0
.
It follows that if
n\in B_{1}
, and
n=F_{i}-F_{j}-1
for some
i\geq 8
,
then
e(n)\geq\min(g(i,j^{\prime}),f(i,j^{\prime}+1))
.

There are now two subcases to consider:
i
is odd and
i
is even.

Suppose
i
is odd.
Then
i=2k+1
and
j^{\prime}=k+1
for some

.
Then using the Binet form one can verify that

F_{k}F_{2k-1}(f(2k+1,k+2)-g(2k+1,k+1))={1\over 10}(-2(-1)^{k}+2L_{2k-3}+10F_{k}+5F_{-k}+5L_{-k})>0

for

.
Hence
e(n)\geq g(2k+1,k+1)
.

From Lemma
4
(i) we know that
(F_{k+1}+1)^{2}/F_{2k-1}\leq 3
for

k\geq 4
. From Eq. (
2
) we know that

F_{2k+1}/F_{2k-1}\leq\alpha^{2}
.
Multiplying these together and dividing by
F_{2k+1}
, we get

(F_{k+1}+1)^{2}/F_{2k-1}^{2}\leq 3\alpha^{2}F_{2k+1}^{-1}\leq 3\alpha^{2}n^{-1}

for
k\geq 4
.
Hence

(F_{k+1}+1)/F_{2k-1}\leq\sqrt{3}\alpha n^{-1/2}

for
k\geq 3
.
Then

\displaystyle e(n)\geq g(2k+1,k+1)

\displaystyle={{F_{2k+1}}\over{F_{2k-1}}}-{{F_{k+1}+1}\over{F_{2k-1}}}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha^{2}-{1\over{F_{4k-2}}})-\sqrt{3}\alpha n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-{1\over 10}n^{-1/2}-\sqrt{3}\alpha n^{-1/2}\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{bnds} (ii))}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-3n^{-1/2}

for
k\geq 2
.

If
i
is even then
i=2k+2
and
j^{\prime}=k+1
.
Then using the Binet
form one can verify that

F_{k}F_{2k}(g(2k+2,k+1)-f(2k+2,k+2))={1\over 10}(2(-1)^{k}+2L_{2k-1}-10F_{k}-5F_{1-k}+5L_{1-k})\geq 0

for
k\geq 3
.
Hence
e(n)\geq f(2k+2,k+2)
.

Now from Lemma
4
(iv) we get
-1/F_{2k}\geq-8^{-1/2}F_{2k+2}^{-1/2}\geq-8^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}
.
Similarly from Lemma

-1/F_{k}\geq-\sqrt{6}F_{2k+2}^{-1/2}\geq-\sqrt{6}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle e(n)\geq f(2k+2,k+2)

\displaystyle={{F_{k+2}-1}\over{F_{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha^{2}-{1\over F_{2k}})-{1\over{F_{k}}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-8^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}-\sqrt{6}n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-3n^{-1/2}.

Case 3:

n\in B_{2}
, that is,
n=n_{i,j}=F_{i}-F_{2j+1}

for
i\geq 5
and
1\leq j\leq(i-3)/2
. (For these

it turns

Using Lemma
2
, we find lower bound for

applicable to all

j
,
1\leq j\leq(i-3)/2
.
Define

\displaystyle r(i,j)

\displaystyle=F_{2j+1}/F_{2j-1}

\displaystyle s(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i}-F_{2j+1})/F_{i-2}

\displaystyle\psi(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i}-F_{2j+1})F_{2j-1}-F_{i-2}F_{2j+1}.

Again we need to determine the “crossover point”, illustrated in

.

.

Clearly

is increasing with increasing

. To see

\displaystyle r(i,j+1)-r(i,j)

\displaystyle={{F_{2j+3}}\over{F_{2j+1}}}-{{F_{2j+1}}\over{F_{2j-1}}}

\displaystyle={{F_{2j+3}F_{2j-1}-F_{2j+1}^{2}}\over{F_{2j+1}F_{2j-1}}}

\displaystyle={1\over{{F_{2j+1}F_{2j-1}}}}>0.

Here we used Eq. (

) with

and

.

Next, we show that

\displaystyle s(i,j+1)-s(i,j)

\displaystyle={{F_{i}-F_{2j+3}}\over{F_{i-2}}}-{{F_{i}-F_{2j+1}}\over{F_{i-2}}}

\displaystyle={{F_{2j+1}-F_{2j+3}}\over{F_{i-2}}}<0,

where we used Eq. (

) with

Also

,

\displaystyle\psi(i+1,j)-\psi(i,j)

\displaystyle=(F_{i+1}-F_{2j+1})F_{2j-1}-F_{i-1}F_{2j+1}-(F_{i}-F_{2j+1})F_{2j-1}-F_{i-2}F_{2j+1}

\displaystyle=F_{i-1}F_{2j-1}-F_{i-3}F_{2j+1}

(4)

\displaystyle=F_{i-2j-2}\geq 0.

Using the Binet form we can easily check that

\displaystyle\psi(6k,k)

\displaystyle=(L_{4k-2}-3)/5

\displaystyle\psi(6k+5,k+1)

\displaystyle=(-L_{4k}-3)/5.

This, combined with (

\psi(6k+5,k)\geq\psi(6k+4,k)\geq\psi(6k+3,k)\geq\psi(6k+2,k)\geq\psi(6k+1,k)\geq\psi(6k,k)\geq 0

\psi(6k,k+1)\leq\psi(6k+1,k+1)\leq\psi(6k+2,k+1)\leq\psi(6k+3,k+1)\leq\psi(6k+4,k+1)\\
\leq\psi(6k+5,k+1)\leq 0.

Now choose
j^{\prime}=\lfloor i/6\rfloor
.
Then
r(i,j^{\prime})\leq s(i,j^{\prime})
and

r(i,j^{\prime}+1)\geq s(i,j^{\prime}+1)
for
i\geq 1
.
So for
i\geq 1
we have
e(n)\geq\min(s(i,j^{\prime}),r(i,j^{\prime}+1))
.

There are now two cases to consider:

(a)

i=6k+a
,
a\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, and
j=k
, and

(b)

i=6k+a
,
a\in\{4,5\}
, and
j=k
.

In case (a) we have, for
i=6k
, that

\displaystyle F_{2k+1}F_{6k-2}(r(6k,k+1)-s(6k,k))

\displaystyle=F_{6k-2}F_{2k+3}-F_{2k+1}F_{6k}+F_{2k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle={1\over{20}}(7L_{4k-3}+15F_{4k}+8)\geq 0.

For
i=6k+1
:

\displaystyle F_{2k+1}F_{6k-1}(r(6k+1,k+1)-s(6k+1,k))

\displaystyle=F_{6k-1}F_{2k+3}-F_{2k+1}F_{6k+1}+F_{2k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle={1\over 5}(-2L_{4k-3}+5F_{4k}+2)\geq 0.

For
i=6k+2
:

\displaystyle F_{2k+1}F_{6k}(r(6k+2,k+1)-s(6k+2,k))

\displaystyle=F_{6k}F_{2k+3}-F_{2k+1}F_{6k+2}+F_{2k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle={1\over{10}}(L_{4k-3}+5F_{4k}+4)\geq 0.

For
i=6k+3
:

\displaystyle F_{2k+1}F_{6k+1}(r(6k+3,k+1)-s(6k+3,k))

\displaystyle=F_{6k+1}F_{2k+3}-F_{2k+1}F_{6k+3}+F_{2k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle={1\over{20}}(-3L_{4k-3}+5F_{4k}+8)\geq 0.

Hence

\displaystyle e(n)\geq s(6k+a,k)

\displaystyle={{F_{6k+a}-F_{2k+1}}\over{F_{6k+a-2}}}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha^{2}-{1\over{F_{2k+2a-4}}})-{{F_{2k+1}}\over{F_{6k+a-2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-(1/100)F_{6k+3}^{-1/2}-\sqrt{6}F_{6k+a}^{-1/2}\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{bnds} (vi))}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-(1/100+\sqrt{6})n^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-3n^{-1/2}.

In case (b) we have
a\in\{4,5\}
. Then

\displaystyle e(n)\geq r(6k+a,k+1)

\displaystyle={{F_{2k+3}}\over{F_{2k+1}}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-{1\over{F_{4k+2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-{1\over 2}F_{6k+5}^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-{1\over 2}F_{6k+a}^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{2}-2n^{-1/2}\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{bnds} (vii))}

for
k\geq 3
.

Thus in all cases
e(n)\geq\alpha^{2}-3n^{-1/2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

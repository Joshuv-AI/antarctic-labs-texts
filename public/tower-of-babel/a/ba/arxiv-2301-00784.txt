On a determinant formula for some real regular representations
Léa Bittmann

Abstract
. We interpret a formula established by Lapid-Mínguez on real regular representations of GL n {\rm GL}_{n} over a local non-archimedean field as a matrix determinant. We use the Lewis Carroll determinant identity to prove new relations between real regular representations. Through quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality, these relations generalize Mukhin-Young’s Extended T T -systems , for representations of the quantum affine algebra U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ^ k ) U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}) , which are themselves generalizations of the celebrated T T -system relations.



1.
Introduction

The context of this work is the representation theory of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
(where
F
is a non-archimedean local field), or equivalently of the type
A
quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
(where
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is not a root of unity). Indeed, through Chari-Pressley’s
quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality

[
3
]
, the category of complex smooth finite-length representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
is equivalent to the category of (level
n
) finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules, when
k\geq n
. Since both contexts are equivalent, we will work with the category
\mathcal{C}
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
representations in most of this paper. Both these categories have been intensively (and independently) studied, but some important natural questions remain open.

The normalized parabolic induction, denoted by
\times
, endows this category with a ring category structure, and its Grothendieck group
\mathcal{R}
with a ring structure.
Irreducible representations in the category
\mathcal{C}
have been classified by Zelevinsky
[
25
]
using
multisegments
(formal sums of segments). For
\mathfrak{m}=\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}+\cdots+\Delta_{N}
a multisegment, the corresponding irreducible representation
Z(\mathfrak{m})
is obtained as the unique irreducible subrepresentation of the
standard representation

\zeta(\mathfrak{m}):=Z(\Delta_{1})\times Z(\Delta_{2})\times\cdots\times Z(\Delta_{N})
.
The classes of the irreducible representations and the standard representations form two bases of the Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{R}
, the change of basis matrix between them is unitriangular, with coefficients which can be expressed in terms of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials (see
[
26
]
,
[
2
]
). A similar story was established for finite-dimensional representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
(see the work of Nakajima
[
22
]
). This gives an algorithm to compute the classes of the simple representations from the classes of the standard representations. However, in practice the actual computation of the coefficients can be very difficult.

For some specific classes of irreducible representations, remarkable formulas have been established to compute their classes as linear combinations of classes of standard representations. The work of Tadić
[
24
]
, and then Chenevier-Renard
[
5
]
, established such a formula for
Speh representations
.
Cleverly, this formula can be seen as the computation of the determinant of a matrix, and it was then proved using the
Lewis Carroll identity
(also called
Dodgson’s rule of determinant
). In
[
16
]
, Lapid-Mínguez generalized Tadić’s formula to a larger class of representations called
ladder representations
.
Then, in
[
18
]
the same authors established an even more general formula (see (
4.1
) below), for
regular representations
which are
real
-
Z(\mathfrak{m})
such that
Z(\mathfrak{m})\times Z(\mathfrak{m})
is irreducible.

Furthermore, in
[
16
]
, Lapid-Mínguez used the Lewis Carroll identity to obtain a remarkable relation between the classes of some of these ladder representations. For
Z(\mathfrak{m})=Z(\Delta_{1}+\cdots+\Delta_{N}
) a ladder representation, we have the following relation in
\mathcal{R}

[
16

:

(1.1)

Z(\Delta_{1}+\cdots+\Delta_{N-1})\times Z(\Delta_{2}+\cdots+\Delta_{N})=Z(\mathfrak{m})\times Z(\Delta_{2}+\cdots+\Delta_{N-1})\\
+Z(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}),

where

are also ladders (see Theorem

Through quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality, relation (

[

for representations of the quantum affine algebra

-systems

The extended

-systems are generalizations of the famous

-system relations

). For representations of quantum affine algebras, the

between classes of Speh representations (called

or

]
and in all types by Hernandez
[

]
. Additionally, the

-systems, and their extended version, can be interpreted as

More recently, the

-systems gained a new interpretation as

in a Fomin-Zelevinsky cluster algebra

. Indeed, in
[

Hernandez-Leclerc proved this interpretation of

[

another generalization of the

In the present work, we establish formulas generalizing the extended
T
-systems of Mukhin-Young, for some real regular representations. Regular representations have a permutation associated to them and in
[
18
]
, Lapid-Mínguez gave a sufficient condition for a regular representation to be real, as a pattern avoidance condition on the permutation associated to the representation. We show, using the notion of

and Corollary

) a regular representation such that the associated permutation

avoids the patterns

and
4231

((

) and (

(1.2)

\displaystyle Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{N})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{\sigma(N)})

\displaystyle=Z(\mathfrak{m})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{N},\Delta_{\sigma(N)})+Z(\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{\prime})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}_{1}^{\prime\prime}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{1})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{\sigma(1)})

\displaystyle=Z(\mathfrak{m})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}\setminus\Delta_{1},\Delta_{\sigma(1)})+Z(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\prime})\times Z(\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\prime\prime}),

As part of Theorem

]

The paper is organized as follows. We start with some reminders about segments, multisegments,

In Section

Let

\displaystyle{\rm LI}(Z(\Delta),Z(\mathfrak{m}))

\displaystyle{\rm RI}(Z(\Delta),Z(\mathfrak{m}))

3.2.

Lemma 3.2

Proof.

We will prove the first equivalence, the second being exactly analog. From the definition of the condition

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

If

is equivalent to
\sigma^{-1}
is strictly decreasing on

•

\sigma^{-1}
is strictly decreasing on

Proof.

As before, we only prove the first statement. Fix

. If

Suppose

Y_{\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m}}\subset X_{\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m}}\cup\{i\}.

Suppose

. Let

. Then, since

is ordered,
e(\Delta_{j_{1}})<e(\Delta_{j_{2}})<\cdots<e(\Delta_{j_{m}})
.

If
\sigma^{-1}
is strictly decreasing on

X_{\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m}}
, then
b(\Delta_{j_{1}})<b(\Delta_{j_{2}})<\cdots<b(\Delta_{j_{m}})
. Since all

. Thus the function

(3.5)

\begin{array}[]{rrll}f:&X_{\Delta,\mathfrak{m}}&\to Y_{\Delta,\mathfrak{m}},\\
&j_{1}&\mapsto i,\\
&j_{\ell+1}&\mapsto j_{\ell},&1\leq\ell\leq m-1,\end{array}

is a matching function from
X_{\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m}}
to
Y_{\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m}}
. Thus
{\rm LC}(\Delta_{i},\mathfrak{m})
.

If

to

, as

Conversely, suppose

and let

to

is strictly decreasing on

.
∎

From Lemma

4.
Determinant formula

4.1.
Alternate sum formula

One of the results of
[
18
]
is an alternate sum formula for every regular real representation using standard representations. Let
\pi=Z(\mathfrak{m})
be a regular real representation, with
\mathfrak{m}=[a_{1};b_{1}]+\cdots+[a_{N};b_{N}]
such that
b_{1}>\cdots>b_{N}
.

In the Grothendieck ring,

(4.1)

\pi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{N}\\
\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma\end{subarray}}\sgn(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)Z([a_{\sigma(1)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}])\times Z([a_{\sigma(2)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)}])\times\cdots\times Z([a_{\sigma(N)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(N)}]),

where
\sigma=\sigma_{\mathfrak{m}}
and for all
i
,

\sigma_{0}^{-1}(i)=\max\left\{j\leq x_{i}\mid j\notin\sigma_{0}^{-1}(\{i+1,\ldots,N\})\right\},\quad\text{with }x_{i}=\#\{j\mid a_{j}\leq b_{i}+1\}.

Remark 4.1
.

The permutation
\sigma_{0}
satisfies

\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma^{\prime}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,N\},a_{\sigma(i)}\leq b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}+1.

We deduce that equation (
4.1
) is equivalent to

(4.2)

\pi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{N}\\
\sigma^{\prime}\leq\sigma\end{subarray}}\sgn(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)Z([a_{\sigma(1)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}])\times Z([a_{\sigma(2)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)}])\times\cdots\times Z([a_{\sigma(N)};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(N)}]).

Indeed, for
\sigma^{\prime}>\sigma_{0}
, at least one of the
Z([a_{\sigma(i)},b_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}])
is not defined, and the term does not contribute to the sum in (
4.2
).

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, set
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{\sigma(i)}
, then equation (
4.2
) can be rewritten

(4.3)

\pi=\sgn(\sigma)\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in[\id,\sigma]}\sgn(\sigma^{\prime})Z([a^{\prime}_{1};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}])\times Z([a^{\prime}_{2};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(2)}])\times\cdots\times Z([a^{\prime}_{N};b_{\sigma^{\prime}(N)}]),

where
[\id,\sigma]
denotes the Bruhat interval of permutations in
S_{N}
lower than
\sigma
.

The following result is usually called the

For

\det(M)\det(M_{a,a^{\prime}}^{b,b^{\prime}})=\det(M_{a}^{b})\det(M_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}})-\det(M_{a}^{b^{\prime}})\det(M_{a^{\prime}}^{b}).

6.
Relation to quantum affine algebras representations

6.1.
Translation of results

As mentioned above, the result of Theorem
5.1
has a quantum affine analog through
quantum affine Schur-Weyl duality
. Indeed, when
q
is not a root of unity, Chari-Pressley
[
4
]
have established an equivalence of categories between the category of finite-dimensional representations of the affine Hecke algebra
\dot{H}_{q^{2}}(n)
and the category of (level
n
) finite-dimensional representations of the quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
, when
k\geq n
. Moreover, through
type theory
(see for example
[
9
]
), it is known that finite-dimensional representations of the affine Hecke algebra
\dot{H}_{q^{2}}(n)
are equivalent to finite length representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
.

This equivalence is
monoidal
, in the sense that the parabolic induction of two representations in
\mathcal{C}
is translated into the tensor product of the corresponding
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules.

Instead of multisegments, finite-dimensional irreducible
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules have been classified
[
3
]
using
Drinfeld polynomials
, which correspond to their highest-weights. By a process similar to the reduction to cuspidal lines described in the beginning of Section
2.2
, the study of the category of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules amounts to the study of a skeleton Serre subcategory
\mathscr{C}
, introduced by Hernandez-Leclerc (see
[
11
, Section 3.7]
), in relation to cluster algebras. Let
\mathscr{R}
denote the Grothendieck ring of the monoidal category
\mathscr{C}
.

Simple objects in the category
\mathscr{C}
are then parametrized, up to isomorphism, by monomials in the formal variables
Y_{i,p}
,
(i,p)\in\hat{I}:=\left\{\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\times\mathbb{Z}\mid i+p+1\in 2\mathbb{Z}\right\}
. The correspondence between segments and formal variables is as follows:

(6.1)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{[}a;b]&\mapsto&Y_{b-a+1,-a-b},\\
{[}\frac{1-i-p}{2};\frac{i-p-1}{2}]&\mapsfrom&Y_{i,p}.\end{array}

Since we are using the Zelevinsky classification for the representations of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
, from now on irreducible
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
-modules will be denoted
L(M)
, with
M
their highest loop-weight, in the set of dominant loop-weights:

\hat{P}_{\ell}:=\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{N}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\mid~\forall~1\leq j\leq N,(i_{j},p_{j})\in\hat{I}\right\}.

Through this correspondence, ladder representations are usually called
snake modules
in the context of quantum affine algebras. For completeness, recall the definition of snakes modules by Mukhin-Young. For
M=\prod_{j=1}^{N}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\in\hat{P}_{\ell}
, the simple module
L(M)
is a snake module if and only if, for all
1\leq j\leq N
,

p_{j+1}-p_{j}\geq|i_{j+1}-i_{j}|+2.

It clearly translates to the definition of ladders, as in Definition
2.10
. Note that a definition of snake modules for type
B
quantum affine algebras was also introduced by Mukhin-Young.

Moreover, as stated above, Theorem
5.5
from
[
16
]
was previously established by Mukhin-Young in terms of snake modules.

For
M=\prod_{j=1}^{N}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\in\hat{P}_{\ell}
such that
L(M)
is a snake module, we have the following relation, in the Grothendieck ring
\mathscr{R}

[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
:

(6.2)

\left[L\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\right)\right]\cdot\left[L\left(\prod_{j=2}^{N}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\right)\right]=\left[L\left(\prod_{j=2}^{N-1}Y_{i_{j},p_{j}}\right)\right]\cdot\left[L(M)\right]\\
+\left[L(M^{\prime})\right]\cdot\left[L(M^{\prime\prime})\right],

where
M^{\prime},M^{\prime\prime}
are called the
neighboring snakes
of
M
, and correspond to
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime\prime}
in this case. Note that relation (
6.2
) was established in
[
21
]
also in type
B
. Moreover, as in our result, both terms on the left hand side of (
6.2
) correspond to irreducible modules.

For these reasons, our theorem
5.1
is a generalization of
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, and we have established some new relations between irreducible representations of
U_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k})
.

6.2.
Relation to cluster algebras

In
[
12
]
, Hernandez and Leclerc proved that the Grothendieck ring
\mathscr{R}
had a
cluster algebra
structure for which the initial cluster variables are
Kirillov-Reshetikhin
modules (or Speh representations, as in Definition
2.8
). Moreover, one of the key ingredients used for this result is the fact that the
T
-system relations (of which the Mukhin-Young extended
T
-systems are generalizations) correspond to
exchange relations
in the cluster algebra structure. The same authors also conjectured
[
12
, Conjecture 5.2]
that the cluster variables were in bijection with the prime real simple modules. Part of this conjecture was proven by Kashiwara-Kim-Oh-Park in
[
14
]
, where they proved that all cluster variables correspond to prime real simple modules.

In
[
7
]
Duan-Li-Luo proved that prime snake modules correspond to cluster variables, thus proving Hernandez-Leclerc’s conjecture for snake modules, and for that purpose introduced new relations in the Grothendieck ring
\mathscr{R}
, which they interpreted as exchange relations. However, it is unclear whether (some of) the Mukhin-Young extended
T
-systems can be interpreted as exchange relations.

One of the motivations behind this work was to obtain more generalizations of the
T
-system relations, which could be interpreted as exchange relations. We conjecture that, equipped with more explicit relations such as (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), one could prove that all prime real regular representations (for which there exists the criterion of Theorem
2.18

[
18
]
) correspond to cluster variables.

However, we already observe that not all relations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) have the form of an exchange relation. For example, in the relation in Example
6.1
(1), one of the factors in the left hand side is not prime
L(Y_{3,-2}Y_{1,-2})\cong L(Y_{3,-2})\cdot L(Y_{1,-2})
. Thus the left hand side is a product of three prime irreducible modules, and the relation cannot be an exchange relation.

The ribbon category framework for topological quantum computing
Willie Aboumrad

Abstract
. This expository article supplies the mathematical background underpinning the braid representation calculator introduced in [ Abo22 ] ; those representations describe the sets of logic gates available to a topological quantum computer for processing encoded qubits. Assuming little background in category theory, we first recall the notion of a ribbon fusion category (RFC), collecting most of the necessary definitions. Then we discuss how certain RFCs arise from the representation theory of quantum groups. We explore the braiding in these categories in detail, since it is essential for the quantum computing application.



1.
Introduction

The notion of a
ribbon fusion category
(RFC) features prominently in the story of topological quantum computers powered by anyons. This expository article recalls that notion, explains how RFCs induce representations of the Artin braid group, and describes how RFCs arise from quantum groups. We will more or less introduce all the necessary terminology. In particular, this article provides the background in category theory assumed in the author’s article
[
Abo22
]
on computing the braid representations arising from anyon systems. However, the reader need not grasp every detail in order to start doing braid computations using
SageMath
.

Ribbon categories are certain abelian monoidal, or
tensor
, categories. Tensor categories play an important role in many areas of modern mathematics, such as algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, number theory, mathematical physics, and quantum computation. There are spectacular connections between the theory of tensor categories and representation theory, quantum groups, infinite dimensional Lie algebras, conformal field theory and vertex algebras, operator algebras, and invariants of knots and 3-manifolds which arose from the works of Drinfeld, Moore and Seiberg, Kazhdan and Lusztig, Jones, Witten, Reshetikhin and Turaev, and many others
[
Lus88
,
Dri89c
,
Dri89a
,
MS89
,
RT90
,
GK92
,
KL93
,
Jon06
,
DFMS11
]
. See also
[
EGNO15
]
and references therein. The theory of anyonic computation relies on these connections.

In this context it is helpful to think of category theory as a “categorification” of ordinary algebra. In other words, there is a dictionary between the two subjects, such that the usual algebraic structures can be recovered from the categorical structures by passing to the isomorphism classes of objects
[
EGNO15
]
. For instance, the notion of a category is the categorification of the notion of a set
1
1

1

There is a foundational subtlety that is not relevant in our context: in general objects and morphisms in a category are defined to form a
class
rather than a set. This is to circumvent Russell’s paradox regarding the existence of a set of all sets. In this work we deal only with
small
categories, which means their objects and hom-sets are indeed sets.
and the notion of a monoidal category is the categorification of the notion of a monoid.

This correspondence allows for a theory of algebra representations without vectors, in the same sense that category theory allows for a theory of sets without elements. For instance, the representation theory of a quasitriangular (braided) Hopf algebra may be formulated in terms of a ribbon category.

In the setting of quantum computing, the correspondence allows us to capture the dynamics of anyons, modeled as simple objects in an RFC, without knowing much about what they “actually look like.” This is explained in
[
Abo22
]
.

This article is structured as follows.
Section

2
introduces ribbon categories and necessary terminology. In addition, it discusses the induced braid representations and briefly presents the associated diagrammatical calculus, which is ubiquitous and useful for computations.
Section

3
fixes our notation for quantum groups. Then

elucidates the connection between semisimple ribbon categories and the representation theory of quantum groups. The aim is to show that the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
of finite-dimensional modules over the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a ribbon category whose braiding is generated infinitesimally. This means the braid group representation induced by the categorical braiding of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
on any tensor product
V^{\otimes m}
is equivalent to the representation induced by the exponential of the symmetric invariant
2

defined by the enveloping algebra’s Casimir element. This representation has a geometric interpretation in terms of the monodromy of the Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations, which motivates the connection between certain conformal field theories and the RFC framework
[

\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})

U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.
The ribbon category framework

We assume the notion of a category, subcategory, functor, and their equivalences. Otherwise we start from the beginning. MacLane
[
Lan78
]
provides a great introduction to the subject of category theory.

2.1.
Abelian categories

To begin, we need the notion of
additive
categories.

Definition 2.1
.

[
EGNO15
, Definition 1.2.1]

An
additive category
is a category
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

Every hom-set
\Hom(X,Y)
is equipped with the structure of an abelian group (written additively) making composition of morphisms biadditive.

(ii)

There exists a zero object
0\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\Hom(0,0)=0
.

(iii)

(Direct sums exist.) For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
there exists an object
S\in\mathcal{C}
and morphisms
p_{X}\colon S\to X
,
p_{Y}\colon S\to Y
,
i_{X}\colon X\to S
, and
i_{Y}\colon Y\to S
such that
p_{X}i_{X}=\mathrm{id}_{X}
,
p_{Y}i_{Y}=\mathrm{id}_{Y}
, and
i_{X}p_{X}+i_{Y}p_{Y}=\mathrm{id}_{S}
.

A universal property shows the object
S
in (iii) is unique up to isomorphism. It is the
direct sum
of
X
and
Y
, and we denote it by
X\oplus Y
. This means every additive category
\mathcal{C}
is equipped with a bifunctor
\oplus\colon\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
.

The categories considered in this work are
\mathbb{C}
-linear
, or
enriched over

\mathbb{C}
, which means every hom-set
\Hom(X,Y)
is equipped with the structure of a complex vector space making composition of morphisms
\mathbb{C}
-linear.

Introducing the notion of a
decomposition
of an object requires the notion of
kernels
and
cokernels
. These are categorifications of the usual notions of kernels and cokernels in linear algebra. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
. The
kernel

\ker(f)
of
f
(if it exists) is an object
K
together with a morphism
k\colon K\to X
such that
fk=0
. If
k^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}\to X
is such that
fk^{\prime}=0
then there exists a unique morphism
\ell\colon K^{\prime}\to K
such that
k\ell=k^{\prime}
. Thus when
\ker(f)
exists it is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

Dually, the
cokernel

\mathrm{coker}(f)
of
f
(if it exists) is an object
C
together with a morphism
c\colon Y\to C
such that
cf=0
. Similarly, if
c^{\prime}\colon Y\to C^{\prime}
is such that
c^{\prime}f=0
then there exists a unique morphism
\ell\colon C\to C^{\prime}
such that
\ell c=0
. Therefore if
\mathrm{coker}(f)
exists then it is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

Definition 2.2
.

[
EGNO15
, Definition 1.3.1]

An
abelian category
is an additive category
\mathcal{C}
in which for every morphism
f\colon X\to Y
there exists a sequence

K\xrightarrow{\,\smash{{\scriptstyle k}}\,}X\xrightarrow{\,\smash{{\scriptstyle i}}\,}I\xrightarrow{\,\smash{{\scriptstyle j}}\,}Y\xrightarrow{\,\smash{{\scriptstyle c}}\,}C

with the following properties:

(i)

ji=f
,

(ii)

(K,k)=\ker(f)
and
(C,c)=\mathrm{coker}(f)
,

(iii)

(I,i)=\mathrm{coker}(k)
and
(I,j)=\ker(c)
.

The object
I
is the
image
of
f
and we denote it by
\mathrm{Im}(f)
.

The notion of a kernel allows us to identify subobjects in a given object
X
. Concretely, a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
in an abelian category is a
monomorphism
if
\ker(f)=0
. Then a
subobject
of
X
is an object
Y
together with a monomorphism
i\colon Y\to X
. In the quantum computing application, we interpret subobjects of a tensor product as the possible outcomes of anyon fusion.

The following theorem justifies thinking of direct sums, morphisms, kernels and cokernels in an abelian category in terms of ordinary linear algebra.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
Fre66
]

Every abelian
\mathbb{C}
-linear category is equivalent, as an additive category, to a full subcategory of the category of modules over a unital associative
\mathbb{C}
-linear algebra.

Moreover, a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is
finite
if it is equivalent to the category
\operatorname{A-Mod_{fr}}
of finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A

[
EGNO15
, Definition 1.8.5]
. Note that
\mathcal{C}
does not uniquely or canonically determine the algebra
A
, but rather its Morita equivalence class.

Crucially, the notion of a kernel allows us to define a distinguished set of elements in an abelian category. A non-zero object
X
in an abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is
simple
if its only subobjects are
0
and
X
. In a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category,
X
is simple if and only if
\End(X)\cong\mathbb{C}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is
semisimple
, the simple objects can be viewed as a generating set for
\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
under
\oplus
.

Definition 2.4
.

An abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is
semisimple
if each object is semisimple; an object in
\mathcal{C}
is
semisimple
if it is a finite direct sum of simple objects.

Since we only consider
finite
direct sums, there is an alternative characterization of finite
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian categories: a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category is
finite
if and only if it has finitely many simple objects and its hom-sets are finite-dimensional.

2.2.
Tensor categories

Whereas abelian categories are equipped with an additive structure,

are equipped with a multiplicative one.

Definition 2.5
.

[
Lan78

A

\mathcal{C}
with a bifunctor
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}

(2.1)

(U\otimes V)\otimes(W\otimes X)

a_{U,V,W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{X}\quad\quad
a_{U,V\otimes W,X}
\quad\quad\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes a_{V,W,X}
a_{U,V,W\otimes X}

The natural isomorphism

is known as the

. In addition, the associativity constraint is compatible with the

A monoidal category is said to be

if the associativity and unit constraints

are all identities of the category.

A
tensor category
is a monoidal abelian category
[
EGNO15
]
. This means every tensor product of objects in a tensor category is a direct sum. In other words, tensor categories are the categorifications of rings.

If
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian categories are categories of modules over a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, then
\mathbb{C}
-linear

Notice that any bialgebra corresponding to a strict tensor category must be coassociative. Relaxing strictness results in the notion of a
quasi-bialgebra
, which is coassociative up to conjugation by a distinguished element known as an
associator
.
Section

4.2
discusses quasi-bialgebras in more detail.

2.3.
Braids and ribbons

In order to construct representations of the braid group, we need
braided categories
.

Definition 2.6
.

A tensor category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I,a,l,r)
is
braided
if there exists a commutativity constraint
c
, called a
braiding
, such that for any pair of objects
V,W
the map

c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V

is a natural isomorphism. We require that
c
satisfies the Hexagon Axioms, encoded in the commutativity following diagrams.

(2.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(U\otimes V)\otimes W}

The braiding

in a braided category

is

if

(2.5)

c_{W,V}\circ c_{V,W}=\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes W}

for all objects

Note that in a braided category, the isomorphism

is

.

Moreover, observe that braided tensor categories are still categories of modules over a bialgebra, but the bialgebra is not cocommutative in general. However, it is always
quasitriangular (braided)
: cocommutativity is enforced only up to conjugation by a distinguished element called the universal
R

Next we show how to obtain representations of the

from a braided category. For starters, recall the braid group

is generated by
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m-1}
subject to the

:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},\quad\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i},\quad\text{for}\quad|i-j|>1.

The braid group

models isotopy classes of braids on

Now let
V
be any object in a braided category
\mathcal{C}
and set
\check{R}=c_{V,V}
. The map
\check{R}
is known as a

\check{R}
satisfies the celebrated
Yang-Baxter
equation in
\End(V\otimes V\otimes V)
:

(2.7)

(\check{R}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\check{R})(\check{R}\otimes\mathrm{id})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\check{R})(\check{R}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes\check{R}).

Thus if we let
\check{R}_{i}
denote the endomorphism of
V^{\otimes m}
acting as
\check{R}
on the
(i,i+1)
st pair of tensor factors,
Equation

2.7
implies
\check{R}_{i}\check{R}_{i+1}\check{R}_{i}=\check{R}_{i+1}\check{R}_{i}\check{R}_{i+1}
. Together with the obvious far commutativity
\check{R}_{i}\check{R}_{j}=\check{R}_{j}\check{R}_{i}
for
|i-j|>1
, this means there is a homomorphism
B_{m}\to\End_{\mathcal{C}}(V^{\otimes m})
satisfying

(2.8)

\sigma_{i}\to\check{R}_{i}.

So far our dictionary between categories and algebras does not include Hopf algebras. This requires a notion of dual objects at the category level because at the algebra level, the Hopf algebra antipode turns the dual of any module into a module. Concretely, recall that if
H
is a Hopf algebra with antipode
S
and
V
is an
H
-module, then
H
acts on
V^{*}
via
S
:

(h\rhd f)(v)=f\left(S(h)v\right),\qquad h\in H,\,\,f\in V^{*},\,\,v\in V.

Thus we introduce
left
and
right dualities
on a tensor category.

Definition 2.7
.

[
Kas95
, Definition XIV.2.1]

Let
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I)
be a strict tensor category with unit object
I
. It is a tensor category with
left duality
if for each object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
there exists an object
V^{*}
and morphisms

b_{V}:I\to V\otimes V^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{V}:V^{*}\otimes V\to I

satisfying the
rigidity axioms

(2.9)

(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes d_{V})(b_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V})=\mathrm{id}_{V},\quad\text{ and }\quad(d_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}})(\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}}\otimes b_{V})=\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}}.

For any morphism
f:V\to W
, we define the
left transpose

f^{*}:W^{*}\to V^{*}
of
f
by the formula

(2.10)

\displaystyle f^{*}=(d_{W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}})(\mathrm{id}_{W^{*}}\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V^{*}})(\mathrm{id}_{W^{*}}\otimes b_{V}).

Alternatively, the tensor category
\mathcal{C}
has
right duality
if for each object
V
of
\mathcal{C}
there exists an object
\prescript{*}{}{V}
and morphisms

b^{\prime}_{V}\colon I\to\prescript{*}{}{V}\otimes V\quad\text{and}\quad d^{\prime}_{V}\colon V\otimes\prescript{*}{}{V}\to I

satisfying

(2.11)

\displaystyle(d^{\prime}_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes b^{\prime}_{V})=\mathrm{id}_{V}\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{id}_{\prescript{*}{}{V}}\otimes d^{\prime}_{V})(b^{\prime}_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\prescript{*}{}{V}})=\mathrm{id}_{\prescript{*}{}{V}}

Of course we may define the
right transpose
of a morphism, but we do not need it here.

Although a right duality enjoys properties analogous to those of a left duality, they are different in general. However, when
\mathcal{C}
is a
ribbon category
the left and right duals coincide
[
Kas95
, Section XIV.5]
.

Definition 2.8
.

A strict braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
with left duality is a
ribbon category
if there exists a family
\theta_{V}:V\to V
of natural isomorphisms, called
twists
, such that

\displaystyle\theta_{V\otimes W}=(\theta_{V}\otimes\theta_{W})c_{V,W}c_{W,V}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{V^{*}}=(\theta_{V})^{*}.

Equation

2.10
defines the transpose
f^{*}
of a morphism
f
.

Moreover, a ribbon structure also controls the categorical braiding: while the symmetry constraint
c_{W,V}\circ c_{V,W}=\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes W}
is too strict in general, in a ribbon category we require this composition to be a kind of coboundary instead
[
KRT97
]
. This has important implications for the braiding in ribbon categories, especially for semisimple ones.

In particular, the twists determine induced action of braid generators, up to a choice of signs, in any semisimple ribbon category. Concretely, suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a semisimple ribbon category and let
(\theta_{W})_{W\in\mathcal{C}}
denote a family of twists in
\mathcal{C}
. For each simple object
W
, let
\check{\theta}_{W}
denote the complex scalar defined by
\theta_{W}=\check{\theta}_{W}\mathrm{id}_{W}\in\End(W)\cong\mathbb{C}
. Now choose any simple object
V
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is semisimple, there is a set
\mathcal{I}
of simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
and integers
N_{X}^{V,V}
such that

V\otimes W=\bigoplus_{X\in\mathcal{I}}N^{V,V}_{X}X.

Suppose each
N_{X}^{V,V}=1
. In this case, we say the decomposition of
V\otimes V
is
multiplicity-free
. This means there are maps
i_{X}\colon X\to V\otimes V
and
p_{X}\colon V\otimes V\to X
for every
X\in\mathcal{I}
satisfying

i_{X}p_{Y}=\delta_{X,Y}\mathrm{id}_{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{X\in\mathcal{I}}i_{X}p_{X}=\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes V}.

Let
P[X]\colon V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
, with
P[X]=i_{X}p_{X}
, denote the projection of
V\otimes V
onto its subobject
X
. The set of
P[X]
, for
X\in\mathcal{I}
is a complete set of orthogonal idempotents:

P[X]P[Y]=\delta_{X,Y}\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes V}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{X\in\mathcal{I}}P[X]=\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes V}.

Finally, let
\check{R}\coloneqq c_{V,V}
denote the braiding operator on
V\otimes V
and set
\check{R}^{V,V}_{X}=\sqrt{\check{\theta}_{X}\check{\theta}_{V}^{-2}}\in\mathbb{C}
for every
X\in\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

With the notation of the previous paragraph in mind,
there exists a sign
\epsilon\colon\mathcal{I}\to\{\pm 1\}
such that

\check{R}=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{I}}\epsilon(X)\check{R}^{V,V}_{X}P[X].

Proof.

The naturality of the twist implies
p_{X}\theta_{V\otimes V}=\theta_{X}p_{X}
for any
X\in\mathcal{I}
. By definition
\theta_{V\otimes V}=(\theta_{V}\otimes\theta_{V})\check{R}^{2}
and
\theta_{V}\otimes\theta_{V}=\check{\theta}_{V}\check{\theta}_{V}
because
V
is simple. Substituting and rearranging yields
p_{X}\check{R}^{2}=\check{\theta}_{X}\check{\theta}_{V}^{-2}p_{X}
. Pre-multiplying by
i_{X}
and adding over
X\in\mathcal{I}
implies
\check{R}^{2}=\sum_{X}\left(\check{R}^{V,V}_{X}\right)^{2}P[X]
. The theorem follows because the
P[X]
form a complete set of orthogonal idempotents.
∎

. In particular,

exclusively.

Definition 2.10
.

A
ribbon fusion category (RFC)
is a finite

-linear semisimple ribbon category.

Remark 2.11
.

In the literature, RFCs are sometimes known as
premodular categories

[

]
. An RFC becomes a

if its braiding satisfies a non-degeneracy condition making the so-called

-matrix
non-singular
[

, Section II.1.4]
.

2.4.
Diagrammatical calculus

3.
Conventions for Lie algebras and quantum groups

In this section we fix our notation for Lie algebras and recall some basic definitions. We will use

throughout to denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Following
[

]
, we use the
Chevalley presentation
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote a complex semisimple Lie algebra, let

denote its

, and let

denote the diagonal matrix of root lengths such that

is symmetric positive definite. Recall

and

whenever

.

The semisimple algebra

is generated by
H_{i},E_{i}
, and

, for

, subject to the relations

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}=0,\quad[H_{i},E_{j}]=a_{ij}E_{j},\quad[H_{i},F_{j}]=-a_{ij}F_{j},\quad[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}H_{i},\\
\end{gathered}

along with the

for

:

(3.2)

(\mathrm{ad}_{E_{i}})^{1-a_{ij}}(E_{j})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{ad}_{F_{i}})^{1-a_{ij}}(F_{j})=0

Remark
.

Authors like Jantzen
[

]
, Lusztig
[

]
, and Sawin
[

]
prefer the notation

and

We will denote by

associated to

.

Now fix the

\mathfrak{h}
generated by the

. The

of
\mathfrak{g}
are the linear functionals
\alpha_{i}\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\alpha_{i}(H_{j})=a_{ji}.

We let

(3.3)

\mathcal{P}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\lambda(H_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\lambda(H_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

denote the lattice of

and

, respectively.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, there exists a unique non-degenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form
\langle,\rangle\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle H_{i},H_{j}\rangle=d_{j}^{-1}a_{ij},\quad\langle H_{i},E_{j}\rangle=\langle H_{i},F_{j}\rangle=0,\\
\langle E_{i},E_{j}\rangle=\langle F_{i},F_{j}\rangle=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle E_{i},F_{j}\rangle=d_{i}^{-1}\delta_{ij},\end{split}

for all

[

, Theorem 2.2]
. When
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
we take

to be the non-degenerate trace bilinear form of the natural representation.

The bilinear form
\langle,\rangle
induces an isomorphism of vector spaces

under which
H_{i}
corresponds to the
coroot

\alpha_{i}^{\vee}=d_{i}^{-1}\alpha_{i}
. The induced form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
satisfies

(3.4)

\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=d_{i}a_{ij}.

Since

, we must have

. Thus we obtain a formula for the

:

(3.5)

a_{ij}=\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle={2\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\rangle\over\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\rangle}.

Notice the bilinear form induced by

is normalized so that

\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\rangle=2d_{i}.

When

is simple

roots.

If

, define the

The elements of

\langle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}\rangle\text{ if }\alpha\neq\beta\text{ and }\langle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{h}\rangle\text{ if }\alpha\neq 0

\mathfrak{g}=\big(\bigoplus_{\alpha<0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\big)\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\big(\bigoplus_{\alpha>0}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\big).

We let
\rho
denote the half sum of the positive roots in

. Alternatively, the

is uniquely characterized by
\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\rho\rangle=1
. Thus its image

\rho=\sum_{i}b_{i}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho^{*}=\sum_{i}b_{i}H_{i}.

The

Since

\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle=\sum_{ij}\lambda_{i}\mu_{j}\langle\omega_{i},\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{ij}(DA^{-1})_{ij}\lambda(H_{i})\mu(H_{j}).

denote the corresponding dual basis with respect to

. The next formula uniquely characterizes the

. In addition,

, Corollary 2.6]
.

In what follows, the term “quantum group” refers to an associative Hopf algebra

as presented in
[
CP94
, Definition 9.1.1]
. For convenience, we reproduce the definition.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote a semisimple Lie algebra and let
A
denote its generalized Cartan matrix with the diagonal matrix
D=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
such that
DA
is symmetric. Let
q
be
indeterminate
and write

.
The

is the associative unital algebra over
\mathbb{C}(q)
generated by the elements
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}
, and
K_{i}^{-1}
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
, subject to the relations:

(3.13)

\begin{gathered}K_{i}K_{j}=K_{j}K_{i},\quad K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}=K_{i}^{-1}K_{i}=1,\\
K_{i}E_{j}K_{i}^{-1}=q_{i}^{a_{ij}}E_{j},\quad K_{i}F_{j}K_{i}^{-1}=q_{i}^{-a_{ij}}F_{j},\\
E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\end{gathered}

together with the
q
-Serre relations for

,

(3.14)

\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\begin{bmatrix}1-a_{ij}\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q_{i}}X_{i}^{k}X_{j}X_{i}^{1-a_{ij}-k}=0.

In the last equality every

is either an

or an

. The
q
-integer is given by
[n]_{q_{i}}=\frac{q_{i}^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}}
, the
q
-factorial
[n]_{q_{i}}!=[n]_{q_{i}}\cdots[1]_{q_{i}}
, and the
q
-binomial coefficient is defined analogously.

Unless otherwise stated, we assume

is equipped with the co-algebra structure defined by the comultiplication map
\Delta\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})^{\otimes 2}
satisfying

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta(E_{i})&=E_{i}\otimes K_{i}+1\otimes E_{i}\\
\Delta(F_{i})&=F_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}^{-1}\otimes F_{i},\quad\text{and}\\
\Delta(K_{i})&=K_{i}\otimes K_{i},\end{split}

as in
[
LZZ10
]
.

In this work, we only consider finite-dimensional type
(1,\ldots,1)

. Refer to Chapters 9 and 10 in
[
CP94
]
for definitions. Every type
(1,\ldots,1)

and each simple

-module parametrized by a dominant weight of

[

, Theorem 5.10]
. Moreover, corresponding modules have the same weight multiplicities
[

, Lemma 5.14]
.

4.
Ribbon categories from quantum groups

In this section we show that the category
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
of finite-dimensional modules over the Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is a semisimple ribbon category. The interested reader may refer to
[
AP95
]
to construct RFCs as finite quotients of
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
.

In addition, we show that the braiding on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
is characterized by an infinitesimal braiding on the category of finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, which is itself characterized by the infinitesimal braiding on
U(\mathfrak{g})
induced its Casimir element.
Section

4.1
recalls infinitesimal braidings. This characterization of the braiding on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
has a geometric interpretation in terms of the Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (KZ) equations; the connection was developed in the works
[
Dri89b
,
KL93
,
Koh02
]
by Drinfeld, Kazhdan and Lusztig, and Kohno, amongst others.

A key step in our construction is showing that the braiding on
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by the universal
R
-matrix of the topological ribbon algebra
\widetilde{U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}
, defined in
Section

4.3
as a certain completion of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. This requires an understanding of topological quantized enveloping algebras, which is developed in
Section

4.2
. In particular,
Section

4.2
culminates in a Drinfeld-Kohno theorem: the braiding on
any
topological quantized enveloping algebra over
\mathfrak{g}
is generated by some infinitesimal braiding on
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

We omit most proofs where precise references are provided.

4.1.
Infinitesimal braids

We recall the notion of infinitesimal braids and show
U(\mathfrak{g})
carries infinitesimal braidings. This means the collection of finite-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules is an infinitesimal symmetric category in the sense of
4.4
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
infinitesimal braid algebra

\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is the unital associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by
t_{ij}
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
subject to the following relations, for all pairwise distinct
i,j,k,l
:

(4.1)

t_{ij}=t_{ji},\qquad[t_{ij},t_{kl}]=0,\qquad[t_{ij},t_{ik}+t_{jk}]=0.

Here
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the usual algebra commutator.

The
infinitesimal braidings
on
U(\mathfrak{g})
, that is, the homomorphisms
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\to U(\mathfrak{g})^{\otimes n}
, are determined by structural properties of
U(\mathfrak{g})
as a cocommutative Hopf algebra. Thus we study infinitesimal braidings in this more general context first.

Let
H
denote a cocommutative Hopf algebra. Every
symmetric invariant
2
-tensor
on
H
determines an infinitesimal braiding on
H^{\otimes n}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathrm{Prim}(H)=\{x\in H\mid\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes x\}
denote the subset of
primitive elements
in
H\otimes H
. A
symmetric invariant 2-tensor
on
H
is an element
\theta\in\mathrm{Prim}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Prim}(H)
such that
\theta_{21}=\theta
and
[\Delta(x),\theta]=0
for all
x\in H
.

Proposition 4.3
.

[
Kas95
, Lemma XIX.3.2]

Let
\theta=\sum_{a}x_{a}\otimes y_{a}
denote a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor on
H
. For all
i\neq j
, let
\theta_{ij}
denote the element of
H^{\otimes n}
defined by

\theta_{ij}=\sum_{a}x_{a}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{a}^{(n)},

where
x_{a}^{(i)}=x_{a}
,
y_{a}^{(i)}=y_{a}
, and
x_{k}^{(\ell)}=1
otherwise. There is an algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\to H^{\otimes n}
satisfying
t_{ij}\to\theta_{ij}
.

We will also need the dual notion at the level of categories.

Definition 4.4
.

[
Kas95
, Definition XX.4.1]

An
infinitesimal braiding
on a strict monoidal category
\mathcal{S}
with involutive braiding
\sigma_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
is a family of functorial endomorphisms

t_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to V\otimes W,

defined for all pairs
(V,W)
of objects in
\mathcal{S}
, which statisfies

(4.2)

\sigma_{V,W}\circ t_{V,W}=t_{W,V}\circ\sigma_{V,W},

and

(4.3)

t_{U,V\otimes W}=t_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W}+(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})^{-1}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes t_{U,W})\circ(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})

for all objects
U,V,W
in
\mathcal{S}
.

An
infinitesimal symmetric category
is a symmetric braided category equipped with an infinitesimal braiding.

Observe that
Equation

4.2
implies
Equation

4.3
is equivalent to

(4.4)

t_{U\otimes V,W}=\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes t_{V,W}+(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes\sigma_{V,W})^{-1}\circ(t_{U,W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes\sigma_{V,W}).

The following proposition partially explains the terminology: it shows
Equations

4.3
and
4.4
are infinitesimal versions of the Hexagon
Equations

2.3
and
2.4
imposed on a braiding in a braided category.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided category in which the morphisms are given as formal series in a parameter
h
. In particular, suppose the braiding
c_{V,W}
is of the form

c_{V,W}=\sigma_{V,W}(\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes W}+ht_{V,W}+O(h^{2}))

for some involutive symmetry
\sigma_{V,W}
. Here
O(h^{2})
denotes a sum of terms of degree
2
or higher in
h
. Then
t_{V,W}
satisfies
Equations

4.3
and
4.4
.

Proof.

In view of
Equation

4.2
, we need only verify
Equation

4.3
. Together with
(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\sigma_{U,W})=\sigma_{U,V\otimes W}
,
Equation

4.2
implies

\displaystyle(c_{U,V}

\displaystyle\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes c_{U,W})=(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\sigma_{U,W})(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})

\displaystyle\qquad+h(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\sigma_{U,W})\left((\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})(t_{U,W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})+(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes t_{U,W})(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{U,V\otimes W}(1+h((t_{U,W}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})+(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes t_{U,W})(\sigma_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})),

modulo
h^{2}
. The Hexagon
Equation

2.3
implies

c_{U,V\otimes W}=(c_{U,V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W})(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes c_{U,W}).

Identifying the linear terms in the last equality proves the claim.
∎

Since
H
is cocommutative, the category
\operatorname{H-Mod}
of modules over
H
is a symmetric braided category with the flip map
\tau_{V,W}
taking
v\otimes w\to w\otimes v
as its symmetry.

Moreover, there is a one-to-one correspondence between infinitesimal braidings on
\operatorname{H-Mod}
and symmetric invariant
2
-tensors on
H
. On one hand, each symmetric invariant
2
-tensor on
H
defines an infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{H-Mod}
:
4.3
implies that if
\theta
is a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor, there is an infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{H-Mod}
satisfying

(4.5)

t_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=\theta\rhd v\otimes w

for every pair of
H
-modules
V,W
. Conversely, every infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{H-Mod}
arises this way: if
t_{V,W}
denotes an infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{H-Mod}
then
\theta=t_{H,H}(1\otimes 1)
is a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor.

We can characterize the infinitesimal braidings on an infinitesimal symmetric category more precisely when there is additional structure in the category, such as a left duality in the sense of
2.7
. In such categories, every infinitesimal braiding has the form
[
Kas95
, Section XX.4]

(4.6)

t_{V,W}=\frac{1}{2}\big(C_{V\otimes W}-\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes C_{W}-C_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{W}\big),

with
C_{V}:V\to V
denoting the natural endomorphism defined by

(4.7)

C_{V}=-\bigg(\mathrm{id}_{V}\otimes\big(d_{V}\circ t_{V^{*},V}\big)\bigg)\circ(b_{V}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{V}).

Thus since the subcategory
\operatorname{H-Mod_{f}}
of finite-dimensional
H
-modules has left duality, every infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{H-Mod_{f}}
is induced by the action of a
single
element
\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}\in H
, such that
\theta=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
is a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor.

Specializing
H=U(\mathfrak{g})
, this means each infinitesimal braiding on
\operatorname{U(\mathfrak{g})-Mod_{f}}
is induced by the action of an element
\sum_{i}x_{i}y_{i}
, with
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathrm{Prim}(U(\mathfrak{g}))=\mathfrak{g}
. An important example concerns the Casimir element
C=\sum_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
introduced in
Equation

3.11
. Note that the associated
2
-tensor

(4.8)

t={1\over 2}\left(\Delta(C)-C\otimes 1-1\otimes C\right)=\sum_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}\otimes X^{\alpha}

is symmetric and it is invariant because
C
is canonical and central in
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

We shall see in
Section

that this infinitesimal braiding generates the braiding on the category of finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules. To see this, however, we will need to go through a certain topological quantized enveloping algebra first.

4.2.
(Topological) quantized enveloping algebras

We define topological quantized enveloping algebras (QUEs) in the sense of
[
Dri87
]
. As a certain deformation of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, a QUE is equipped with a braiding that is generated infinitesimally by a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor on
U(\mathfrak{g})
. The Drinfeld-Kohno
4.15
makes this precise. We single out the QUE
U_{h}(\mathfrak{g})
because it carries a braiding whose action on any tensor product is equivalent to that induced by the matrix
e^{ht}
, with
t
as in
Equation

4.8
, and, in addition, it has a ribbon element.

We begin by recalling topological algebras and deformations. Fix a parameter
h
and let
\mathbb{C}[[h]]
denote the ring of formal power series in
h
with complex coefficients.

Definition 4.6
.

[
BFGP94
, A.4.1]

A
topological vector space (t.v.s.)
is a complex vector space with a locally convex Hausdorff topology. A
topological algebra
over a ring
R
is a triple
(A,\mu,\eta)
where
A
is a complete t.v.s., and the multiplication
\mu:A\widetilde{\otimes}A\to A
and unit
\eta:R\to A
are continuous linear maps satisfying

\mu\circ(\mu\widetilde{\otimes}\mathrm{id}_{A})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\widetilde{\otimes}\mu)\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\circ(\eta\widetilde{\otimes}\mathrm{id}_{A})=\mathrm{id}_{A}=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\widetilde{\otimes}\eta)

with
\widetilde{\otimes}
denoting the completion of the projective topological tensor product.

Remark 4.7
.

When the topology of the t.v.s.
A
is generated by a family of seminorms
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}}
, the topology on
A\widetilde{\otimes}A
is generated by the family
(p_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\beta})_{\alpha,\beta}
of all possible tensor products of the
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

[
Gro55
]
. Recall each tensor product
p_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\beta}:A\widetilde{\otimes}A\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(p_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\beta})(z)=\inf_{(x_{i}),(y_{i})}\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}p_{\alpha}(x_{i})p_{\beta}(y_{i}).

for any
z=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}
.

Definition 4.8
.

[
BS03
, Definition 1]

A
deformation
of a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A
is a
\mathbb{C}[[h]]
-algebra
\tilde{A}
such that
\tilde{A}/hA\cong A
. A deformation is
trivial
if it is isomorphic to
A
considered as a
\mathbb{C}[[h]]
-algebra by base field extension. Two deformations are
equivalent
if they are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}[[h]]
-algebras.

Now set
A=U(\mathfrak{g})[[h]]
. Then
A
becomes a topological algebra when we equip it with the
h
-adic topology: since the topology is metrizable, the completion of the projective topological tensor product coincides with the completion of
A\otimes A
in the
h

Quasi-bialgebras were first introduced by Drinfeld in

]

At the level of categories, the representation theory of a braided quasi-bialgebra

We may now introduce

.

[

(2)

For

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}(a\otimes a^{\prime})+\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu_{n}(a\otimes a^{\prime})h^{n},

\displaystyle\Delta(a)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}(a\otimes a^{\prime})+\sum_{n\geq 1}\Delta_{n}(a)h^{n},\quad\text{and}

with

Definition 4.12
.

In our context, the canonical

.

Definition 4.13
.

[

, Definition XV.3.1]

A

on the topological quasi-bialgebra

Remark 4.14
.

Gauge transforms are sometimes referred to as
Drinfeld twists
and less frequently as
skrooching
in the literature, e.g. in
[
Dri89b
]
and
[
Sta92
]
. The term “skrooching” results from the transliteration of a Russian term for twisting.

Notice that twisting defines an equivalence relation:
(A_{F})_{F^{-1}}=A=(A_{F^{-1}})_{F}
and if
F
and
F^{\prime}
are gauge transformations, then
FF^{\prime}
defines a gauge transformation such that
(A_{F})_{F^{\prime}}=A_{FF^{\prime}}
. Therefore, we say that two (braided) quasi-bialgebras
(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,\Phi)
and
(A^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if there exists a gauge transform
F
on
A^{\prime}
and an isomorphism
\alpha\colon A\to A_{F}^{\prime}
of (braided) quasi-bialgebras.

We use gauge transformations to obtain new QUEs from old ones. In particular, if
A=(A,\mu,\eta,\Delta,\varepsilon,\Phi)
is a topological quasi-bialgebra and
F
is a gauge transformation on
A
, we obtain another topological quasi-bialgebra

, with

\Delta_{F}(a)=F\Delta(a)F^{-1},\,\,a\in A,\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{F}=F_{23}(\mathrm{id}\widetilde{\otimes}\Delta)(F)\Phi(\Delta\widetilde{\otimes}\mathrm{id})(F^{-1})F^{-1}_{12}

by “twisting”

[
Kas95
, Proposition XV.3.2]
.

Moreover, if

is braided with universal
R
-matrix
\mathcal{R}
, then so is

, with
R
-matrix
\mathcal{R}_{F}=F_{21}\mathcal{R}F^{-1}

[
Kas95
, Proposition XV.3.6]
. This means canonical

-tensors are invariant under gauge transformation:

(\mathcal{R}_{F})_{21}\mathcal{R}_{F}=F\mathcal{R}_{21}\mathcal{R}F^{-1}=1\otimes 1+2ht\mod h^{2}.

It also means the braiding operators

and

induced by

and

are similar:

(4.15)

\check{R}_{F}=\tau\circ\mathcal{R}_{F}=F\check{R}F^{-1}.

Equation

implies that if

and

are equivalent topological braided quasi-bialgebras and

is any

with

and

induce equivalent braid group representations on

It turns out the braid representations induced by any QUE are equivalent to those induced by a QUE of the form

where

is a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor on

, in the sense of

. Notice

is a quantization of the pair

, in the sense of

, and it is equipped with the undeformed multiplication and comultiplication of

. While

is cocommutative because the invariance of

commutes with

Theorem 4.15
.

[
Dri89b
, Theorem 1]

If
(A,\Delta,\Phi,\mathcal{R})
is a QUE, there exists an associator
\Phi\in U(\mathfrak{g})[[h]]^{\otimes 3}
and a symmetric invariant

-tensor

on

such that

is equivalent to

as a topological braided quasi-bialgebra. If

is known explicitly, then

is the limit of

as

.

At the categorical level, this result implies that for any QUE

there is a symmetric invariant

-tensor

on

such that

and

are tensor equivalent, since the categories of modules over equivalent QUEs are tensor equivalent

We obtain a geometric interpretation of the Drinfeld-Kohno

via the
KZ equations
; see e.g.
[

,
KL93
,
Fin96
]
.

Definition 4.16
.

Given a symmetric invariant
2
-tensor
\theta
on
U(\mathfrak{g})
as in
4.2
, a complex parameter
h
, a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
, and

, the
Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (KZ) differential system
is given by

(4.16)

dw=\frac{h}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}(dz_{i}-dz_{j})w,

where
w=w(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is a function on (ordered) configuration space with values in
V^{\otimes n}
.

In particular, consider a QUE
A
, let
\theta
denote its canonical
2
-tensor, and fix any topologically free finite-rank
A
-module
V[[h]]
. Recall
4.3
, which shows that the map
t_{ij}\to\theta_{ij}
defines a representation
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\to\End(V^{\otimes n})
of the infinitesimal braid algebra, and note that the fundamental group of (ordered) configuration space is the
pure braid group

P_{n}
, defined as the kernel of the projection
B_{n}\to S_{n}
of the braid group onto the symmetric group
[
Kas95
, Remark XIX.2.3]
. The KZ equations define a flat connection on
V^{\otimes n}
, so they induce a monodromy representation of
P_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
. We may therefore view the inifinitesimal braid algebra, with Lie bracket given by the commutator, as the Lie algebra analogue of a Lie group for the pure braid group
P_{n}
; monodromy is the analogue of integrating a representation of the Lie algebra to a representation of the Lie group. In fact, infinitesimal braids were first introduced by Kohno
[
Koh02
]
to explain the monodromy of the KZ equations as they arise in Wess-Zumino-Witten conformal field theories (CFTs).

The monodromy representation of
P_{n}
may be extended to a representation of the full braid group and Drinfeld shows this (analytic) braid representation is equivalent to that induced by the
R
-matrix
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}=e^{h\theta}
, once we identify analytic functions with formal series
[
Dri89b
]
. In view of
4.15
, this representation is in turn equivalent to the representation induced by
A
on
V[[h]]^{\otimes n}
.

Moreover, Drinfeld constructs the associator
\Phi
mentioned in
4.15
explicitly using solutions to KZ system
[
Dri89b
]
. Drinfeld’s construction shows that, conversely, solutions to the KZ equations give rise to braided quasi-bialgebras.

can also be constructed in the PBW-type basis as a sum of canonical elements analogous to the Casimir element of
U(\mathfrak{g})
, by defining a non-degenerate bilinear pairing between
U_{q}^{+}
and
U_{q}^{-}
. For details, see
[
KS97
, Section 6.3.3]
.

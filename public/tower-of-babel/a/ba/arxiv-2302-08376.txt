Abstract
. We show that if a finitely generated prime algebra Δ \Delta is a finitely generated maximal Cohen-Macaulay module over its centre Z Z , and has global dimension equal to dim Z \dim Z , then the pair given by its centre and ramification divisor is Kawamata log terminal.



1.
Introduction

We will work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Noncommutative crepant resolutions (NCCRs), as discussed in
[
2
]
, indicate an interplay between noncommutative algebra and algebraic geometry.
It is not clear when a commutative algebra admits an NCCR, and various necessary conditions have been found. In
[
18
]
, it is shown that any commutative algebra admitting an NCCR has rational singularities. In particular, they show that if an affine prime algebra
\Delta
that is finite over its centre is homologically homogenous, or equivalently
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay over its centre with finite global dimension, then its centre has rational singularities. We use standard results about the singularities of the minimal model program
[
11
]
, and the argument of
[
18
]
to show that
the singularities of the log pair given by the centre and the ramification divisor is Kawamata log terminal. This shows in particular that the pair is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. We now review the contents of this paper.

In Section 2, we discussion the singularities of the minimal model program and we recall the definitions and properties of log canonical covers. In Section 3, we compute the centralizers of the canonical bimodule
\omega
for a hereditary order over a DVR. We also quote relevant results from
[
11
]
. This will be used in the proof of the main result. In Section 4, we recall the properties of homologically homogeneous algebras that are finite over their centres. This material is from
[
18
]
.
In Section 5, we prove the main result. Given
\Delta
, a prime affine algebra that finite over its centre and homologically homogeneous, with centre
Z
and discriminant
D
, we show that the centre of the canonical cover
\Gamma
of the order
\Delta
is the log canonical cover of the pair
(Z,\Delta)
.
Lastly, in Section 6, we list algebras to which the main theorem applies, and present some relevant examples.

There are many cases of singularities which are known to not admit a NCCR, for example see
[
7
]
.

The results of this paper suggest the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
Z
be a commutative local ring. Under what conditions is
Z
the centre of a prime affine homologically homogeneous ring
A
?
In other words, if we allow the flexibility of adding a boundary divisor
D
, and allowing
A
to have a non-trivial generic Brauer class, can we obtain a twisted ramified NCCR of the pair
(\mathrm{Spec}\,Z,D)
?

See
4.2
for the definitions of the relevant terms.
The results and a sketch of the proofs of this paper were presented in
[
9
]
. The paper
[
8
]
works in the setting of NCCRs and Theorem 1.1(a) and Corollary 1.7 of
[
8
]
prove our main results in that special case.

,

2.1.
Kawamata log terminal, log terminal and canonical singularities

Let
X
be a normal variety. A
\mathbb{Q}
-
divisor
in
X
is a linear
combination of codimension one subvarieties of
X
with rational
coefficients. A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor is
effective
if all its coefficients
are non-negative.
A
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-
Cartier
if there is an integer
m

so that
mD
is a Cartier divisor. A
log variety
is a pair

(X,D)
consisting of a normal
variety
X
with a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
.

Let
(X,D)
be a log variety such that
K_{X}+D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. In
this case we say that
(X,D)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-
Gorenstein.

Then for a proper birational morphism
f:Y\to X
with
Y
smooth,
we write

K_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+D)+\sum_{E}a_{E}E,\,a_{E}\in\mathbb{Q}

where
E
runs over exceptional divisors and strict transforms of
prime divisors appearing in
D.
The coefficient
a_{E}
is independent
of the morphism
f
and depends only on the divisorial valuation
of the function field associated to
E
. The map
f:Y\to(X,D)
is
called
crepant
if all
a_{E}=0,
even in the case where
Y
is only
normal and not
necessarily smooth. We say that
(X,D)
has

Kawamata log terminal (klt) singularities
if
a_{E}>-1
for every
E.

When
(X,D)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and
D=0,
then
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. In this case, we say that

X
has
log terminal (resp. canonical) singularities
if
a_{E}>-1

(resp.
\geq 0
) for every
E.
Note that
K_{X}
is Cartier if and only
if
X
is Gorenstein. We recall the following well known results.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
11
, Cor. 5.24]
)
.

Suppose that
K_{X}
is Cartier.
Then
X
has rational singularities if and only if it has canonical
singularities.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
11
, Th. 5.22]
)
.

If
(X,D)
has Kawamata log terminal
singularities with
D
effective, then
X
has rational singularities.

2.2.
Log canonical covers

Next we recall the standard technique of log canonical covers (see

[
10
, §8.5]
[
14
, §1.3]
).

Suppose now that a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein log variety
(X,D)
has its boundary divisor of
the form

D=\sum_{i}\frac{e_{i}-1}{e_{i}}D_{i},\,\,e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

with
D_{i}
prime divisors. We say that
D
has
standard
coefficients
. Let
m
be the
index
of
(X,D),
that
is, the least positive integer such that
m(K_{X}+D)
is Cartier.
Given a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
we write
\mathcal{F}^{(n)}:=(\mathcal{F}^{\otimes n})^{**}
, the reflexive hull of the tensor power.
Locally on
X,
fixing an isomorphism
\omega_{X}^{(m)}(mD)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
we obtain an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra

(2.1)

\mathcal{A}:=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\omega_{X}^{(i)}\left(\left\lfloor iD\right\rfloor\right).

(In
[
10
,
14
]
, a slightly different description of the sheaf,
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\left\lfloor-i(K_{X}+D)\right\rfloor\right)
,
is used, though it is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
.) The associated finite
morphism

\phi:\widetilde{X}:=\mathcal{S}\mathrm{pec}_{X}\,\mathcal{A}\to X

is called a log canonical cover and has the following properties:

Proposition 2.3
.

[
10
, §8.5]
[
14
, §1.3]

Let
(X,D)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein log variety with log canonical cover

\widetilde{X}
. Then
\widetilde{X}
is an irreducible normal variety,
K_{\widetilde{X}}

is Cartier, and

K_{\widetilde{X}}=\phi^{*}(K_{X}+D).

Note that the map
\psi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow(X,D)

is crepant by definition.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
11
, Prop. 5.20]
With
the notation as above,
\widetilde{X}
has canonical singularities if
and only if
(X,D)
has Kawamata log terminal singularities.

3.
Centralizers of Powers of
\omega

Let
R
be a DVR with parameter
t
.
We write

(t^{i})=t^{i}R
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the fractional ideal generated by
t^{i}
and

(t^{0})=(1)=R
.
Let
\Delta
be a
standard hereditary order
over
R
with ramification index
e,
defined to be

\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}(1)&\cdots&\cdots&(1)\\
(t)&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(t)&\cdots&(t)&(1)\end{pmatrix}\subseteq R^{\,e\times e}.

The dualizing module of
\Delta
is
described as a bimodule by
\omega=\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\Delta,\omega_{R})
as described in

[
18
, Prop. 2.7]
. We compute this bimodule
by forming the dual of
\Delta
into
R
component wise and taking the
transpose. Let
J

be the Jacobson radical of
\Delta
, we have:

\omega\simeq\begin{pmatrix}(1)&(t^{-1})&\cdots&(t^{-1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&(t^{-1})\\
(1)&\cdots&\cdots&(1)\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }J=\begin{pmatrix}(t)&(1)&\cdots&(1)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&(1)\\
(t)&\cdots&\cdots&(t)\end{pmatrix}.

Let

y=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&1\\
t&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

Note that
y
is a normal element in
{\Delta}
and

J=y{\Delta}={\Delta}y\quad\quad y^{e}=t\quad\quad\omega=y^{1-e}{\Delta}=yt^{-1}{\Delta}.

An induction computation shows that

J^{i}=y^{i}{\Delta}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{-i}{e}\rfloor}\right)&\cdots&\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{e-1-i}{e}\rfloor}\right)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{-e+1-i}{e}\rfloor}\right)&\cdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

We note that

\omega^{i}=y^{-i(e-1)}{\Delta}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{i(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right)&\cdots&\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{(i+1)(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{(i-1)(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right)&\cdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

So the centralizer of the bimodule
\omega^{i}
is

Z(\omega^{i})=\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{i(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right).

Write
a^{m\times n}
for the
m\times n
matrix with entries all
a
.
We will use this notation for
a
being an ideal or an element of a ring.

Given a sequence of natural numbers
[N]=[n_{1},\ldots,n_{e}]
, we
let
\Delta
be a
block hereditary order
given by

\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}(1)^{n_{1}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&\cdots&(1)^{n_{1}\times n_{e}}\\
(t)^{n_{2}\times n_{1}}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(t)^{n_{e}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&(t)^{n_{e}\times n_{e-1}}&(1){}^{n_{e}\times n_{e}}\end{pmatrix}

which we
abbreviate as

\Delta\simeq\begin{pmatrix}(1)&\cdots&\cdots&(1)\\
(t)&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(t)&\cdots&(t)&(1)\end{pmatrix}^{[N]}

We note that
\Delta
is Morita equivalent to the standard hereditary
R
-order with
ramification index
e
.
Since we can multiply matrices by blocks, we have the following equations

J=(y\Delta)^{[N]}=(\Delta y)^{[N]}\quad\quad(y^{e})^{[N]}=t\quad\quad\omega=(y^{1-e}\Delta)^{[N]}=(yt^{-1}\Delta)^{[N]}.

As above we get the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be a DVR with parameter
t.

Let
\Delta
be a block hereditary order over with ramification index
e
.
Then the centralizer of powers of the dualizing module
\omega^{i}

are given by

Z(\omega^{i})=\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{i(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right).

4.
Noncommutative Resolutions or Algebraic set up

We firstly recall some results and notation from
[
18
]
. Recall that a
k
-algebra is
affine
if it is finitely generated as an algebra.
We let
\Delta
be an affine prime
k
-algebra which is finitely generated
as a module over its centre
Z:=Z(\Delta)
. Note that
Z
is an
integral domain
[
12
, §10, Ex. 1]
, and we will write
K
for its fraction
field. Let
A
be the simple Artinian
ring of fractions of
\Delta.
We have that
A
is a central simple

K
-algebra.

We say
\Delta
is a
tame
Z
-order
if
\Delta
is a finitely generated, reflexive
Z
module and
\Delta_{p}

is hereditary for all codimension one primes
p
of
Z
. We say
that
\Delta
is
homologically homogeneous
if the projective dimensions
of all simple
\Delta
modules are equal. We recall the following
result, mostly due to Brown-Hajarnavis
[
5
]
, which we
cite from
[
18
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
18
, Th. 2.3]
Let
\Delta
be an affine homologically homogeneous
order of dimension
d
with centre
Z
, then

(1)

\Delta
is a Cohen-Macaulay module over
Z.

(2)

\mathrm{gldim}\,\Delta=\mathrm{GKdim}\,\Delta=d
.

(3)

Z
is an affine Cohen-Macaulay normal domain.

(4)

\Delta
is a tame
Z
-order.

Note that the third conclusion depends on our base field
k
having
characteristic zero.

We will now define the discriminant of a tame order in codimension one. Let

p
be a codimension one prime of
Z
. Since
\Delta_{p}
is hereditary,
there is an étale extension
R
of the DVR
Z_{p}
so that
\Delta_{R}:=\Delta\otimes R

is isomorphic to

\Delta_{R}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}(1)^{n_{1}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&\cdots&(1)^{n_{1}\times n_{e_{p}}}\\
(t)^{n_{2}\times n_{1}}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(t)^{n_{e_{p}}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&(t)^{n_{e_{p}}\times n_{e_{p}-1}}&(1){}^{n_{e_{p}}\times n_{e_{p}}}\end{pmatrix}

where
t
is a parameter of
R

[
1
,
16
]
. We will write
[N]=[n_{1},\ldots,n_{e_{p}}]

and abbreviate as

\Delta_{R}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}(1)&\cdots&\cdots&(1)\\
(t)&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(t)&\cdots&(t)&(1)\end{pmatrix}^{[N]}

We call
e_{p}
the
ramification index
of
\Delta
at
p
.
We define
the
discriminant
of
\Delta
to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

D=\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}p\subset Z\\
\mathrm{cod}\,p=1\end{array}}\left(\frac{e_{p}-1}{e_{p}}\right)D_{p}.

Note that this sum is finite since
\left\lceil D\right\rceil
is the
classical discriminant
of the order
\Delta
which is defined via the reducued trace pairing
[
16
, §25]
.
We will
combine the centre of
\Delta
with its discriminant into the
log
centre,

\mathrm{logZ}(\Delta)=(\mathrm{Spec}\,Z(\Delta),D)
.

Suppose
\Delta
is an affine prime algebra that is a finite module over its centre. We have
[
18
, Lemma 2.4]
that
\Delta
is homologically homogeneous
if and only if
\Delta
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module and
\mathrm{GKdim}\,\Delta=\mathrm{gldim}\,\Delta
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a prime, affine
k
-algebra, module finite over its centre. If
\Delta
is homologically homogeneous, or equivalently
by
[
18
, Lemma 2.4]
, the algebra
\Delta
is a Cohen-Macaulay with
\mathrm{GKdim}\,\Delta=\mathrm{gldim}\,\Delta
, then we say that
\Delta
is a
twisted ramified noncommutative crepant resolution (NCCR) of the pair

\mathrm{logZ}(\Delta)=(X,D).
If furthermore

D=0
then we say the NCCR is
unramified.
Let
K
be the fraction field of the centre
Z
.
The twist of a twisted NCCR
\Delta
is the class of
\Delta\otimes_{Z}K
in
\mathrm{Br}\,K.
If this is trivial, then we say
\Delta
is an untwisted NCCR of the pair
\mathrm{logZ}(\Delta)
. A
classical
NCCR is untwisted and unramified, and this the usual defintion of an NCCR in the literature.

Note that in the case that
Z(\Delta)
is Gorenstein, our definition of a classical NCCR is equivalent to the definition of an NCCR in
[
2
, Defn. 2.2]
and our definition of twisted unramified NCCR is equivalent to the definition of a twisted NCCR in
[
2
, Defn. 2.2]
.

The next statement is well known.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
\Delta
is a maximal order and a classical NCCR of the pair
(Z,D)
then
D=0
and there is a reflexive
Z
-module
M
so that
\Delta\simeq\mathrm{End}_{Z}(M)
.

Proof.

Since the generic Brauer class of
\Delta
is trivial and
Z
is a normal integral domain, the above statement is true in codimension one, and so taking the reflexive hull gives us the result.
∎

5.
Noncommutative Crepant Resolutions and Canonical Covers

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\Delta\oplus\omega_{\Delta}\oplus\cdots\oplus\omega_{\Delta}^{(r-1)}

be the canonical cover of
\Delta
and put
\widetilde{Z}:=Z(\Gamma)

and
\widetilde{X}:=\mathrm{Spec}\,{}\widetilde{Z}.
Then the morphism
\widetilde{X}\to X

is a log canonical cover of
(X,D)
.

Proof.

Let
p
be a codimension one
prime of
Z
. By
[
1
]
, there is an étale extension
R

of
\Delta_{p}
so that
\Delta_{R}:=\Delta\otimes R
is isomorphic
to

\Delta_{R}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}R^{n_{1}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&\cdots&R^{n_{1}\times n_{k}}\\
(tR)^{n_{2}\times n_{1}}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(tR)^{n_{k}\times n_{1}}&\cdots&(tR)^{n_{k-1}\times n_{k}}&R{}^{n_{k}\times n_{k}}\end{pmatrix}

where
t
is a parameter of the DVR
R
. Now Lemma
3.1
shows that

Z(\omega_{\Delta_{R}}^{i})=\left(t^{-\lfloor\frac{i(e-1)}{e}\rfloor}\right)

and so

Z(\omega_{\Delta}^{(i)})=\omega_{X}^{(i)}(\left\lfloor iD\right\rfloor).

étale locally in codimension one. Both sides are naturally isomorphic at the generic point, so we obtain this isomorphism globally since both sides are reflexive. It follows immediately that

\widetilde{Z}=Z(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}Z(\omega_{\Delta}^{(i)})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\omega_{X}^{(i)}(\left\lfloor iD\right\rfloor)

where
m
is the index of
K_{X}+D
.

∎

Let
\Delta
be an affine prime
k
-algebra that is module finite over its centre. Suppose further that
\Delta
is homologically homogeneous. Let

Z:=Z(\Delta)
its centre and
X:=\mathrm{Spec}\,{}Z
its spectrum. Stafford
and Van den Bergh proved an important result about the possible singularities
of the centre:

Theorem 5.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

X
has only rational singularities.

In fact, we can prove the following along the same line, which is
a refinement of their result because of Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
D
be the discriminant divisor on
X
associated to
\Delta.

Then the log variety
(X,D)
has Kawamata log terminal singularities.
Moreover, if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, then
X
has log terminal
singularities.

Proof.

Since the proof is local on
X,
we may suppose that if
m
is the
index of
(X,D),
then
\omega_{X}^{(m)}(mD)
is isomorphic to
Z
.
Then putting
\widetilde{Z}:=\bigoplus\omega_{X}^{(i)}(\left\lfloor iD\right\rfloor),

we can construct a log canonical cover of
(X,D),

\phi:\widetilde{X}=\mathrm{Spec}\,{}\widetilde{Z}\to X=\mathrm{Spec}\,{}Z

as in (
2.1
). On the other hand, the isomorphism

\omega_{X}^{(m)}(mD)\cong Z
defines an isomorphism
\omega_{\Delta}^{(m)}\cong\Delta

as
\Delta
-bimodules and we have a canonical cover of
\Delta,

\Gamma:=\Delta\oplus\omega_{\Delta}\oplus\cdots\oplus\omega_{\Delta}^{(m-1)}.

We show in
5.1
that
Z(\omega_{\Delta}^{(i)})=\omega_{X}^{(i)}(\left\lfloor iD\right\rfloor)

and
Z(\Gamma)=\widetilde{Z}.

Since
[
18
, Prop. 3.1(1)]
shows that
\Gamma
is also homologically homogeneous, so by Theorem
5.2
,

\widetilde{Z}
has rational singularities. Now, from Propositions
2.1

and
2.4
, the pair
(X,D)
has Kawamata log
terminal singularities. The last assertion is straightforward.
∎

We note the following corollaries.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
A
be an unramified NCCR of a commutative algebra
R
. Then
R
is log terminal and
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (twisted, ramified) NCCR of the pair
(X,D)
. Then the pair is Kawamata log terminal and
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.

We present an alternate proof of the theorem above. First note that:

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Delta
be an affine, prime, finite over centre, homologically homogeneous
k
-algebra. Let
\Gamma
be the canonical cover of
\Delta
as in

.
Let
(X,D)
the the log centre of
\Delta
and let
\widetilde{X}=\mathrm{Spec}\,Z(\Gamma)
. Then
we have that
K_{\widetilde{X}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+D)
.

Proof.

Let
m
be the index of
K_{X}+D
and
let
\hat{\otimes}
denote the reflexive hull of the tensor product.
We have the following isomorphisms:

\Gamma\hat{\otimes}_{\Delta}\omega_{\Delta}\simeq\omega_{\Gamma}

\omega_{\Gamma}\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\hat{\otimes}_{\widetilde{X}}\Gamma

\omega_{\Delta}^{(m)}=\omega_{X}^{(m)}(mD)\otimes_{X}\Delta.

In other words if we consider the
following diagram:

\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}\,{}\Delta\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Spec}\,{}\Gamma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(X,D)}
\textstyle{\widetilde{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We know that all maps except the bottom one are crepant. Hence the

Using Prop.
5.6
we obtain another proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is homologically homogeneous with

,
by Thm.
5.2
, we know that
\mathrm{Spec}\,\widetilde{Z}=\widetilde{X}
is has rational singularities. Therefore

is canonical

and hence log terminal. By Prop.
5.6
we have that
K_{\widetilde{X}}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+D).

So by Prop.
2.4
, we have that
(X,D)
is
Kawamata log terminal. In particular if
X
is
\mathbb{Q}-
Gorenstein then it is
log terminal.
∎

6.
Corollaries and Examples

Some algebras are well known to be homologically homogeneous and so provide examples of twisted and ramified NCCRs. We would like to thank Kenneth Brown for the references for the following statement.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
\Delta
is finite over its centre and
\Delta
is one of

a Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field.

(2)

a connected graded affine noetherian algebra of finite global dimension.

(3)

a not necessarily connected Artin-Schelter regular algebra.

then
\Delta
is a (twisted, ramified) NCCR of the pair
(Z,D)=\mathrm{logZ}(\Delta)
which is Kawamata log terminal and
\mathbb{Q}
-Goreinstein.

Proof.

The first case is
[
4
, Prop. 1.6]
.
The second case is in
[
19
]
.
The third case is in
[
17
]
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

The log centres of the following algebras are Kawamata log terminal.

•

Quantised function algebras of semisimple groups
\mathcal{O}_{q}(G)

Example 6.3
.

Consider the quiver with vertices
Q_{0}=\{0,1\}
with two arrows
u,v
from
0
to
1
and two arrows
\bar{u},\bar{v}
from
1
to
0
.
We let
A
be the path algebra of this quiver with relations

u\bar{u}v=v\bar{u}u\quad u\bar{v}v=v\bar{v}u

\bar{u}u\bar{v}=\bar{v}u\bar{u},\quad\bar{u}v\bar{v}=\bar{v}v\bar{u}.

Consider the commutative ring and fractional ideals

R=k[a,b,c,d]/(ad-bc)

I=aR+cR\quad\quad I^{*}=R+\frac{b}{a}R.

Note also that

A\simeq\begin{pmatrix}R&I\\
I^{*}&R\end{pmatrix}

under the isomorphism

u\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\quad v\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&c\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

\bar{u}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\quad\bar{v}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
\frac{b}{a}&0\end{pmatrix}.

It is well known that
A
is Azumaya except at the origin, and is an
NCCR of
R
.
We consider an action of
\mathbb{Z}/2
on
A
that fixes the vertices and acts as

-1,1,1,1
on the arrows
u,v,\bar{u},\bar{v}
respectively.
Note that the skew group ring

A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2

has global dimension three and is homologically homogeneous. Hence it is a ramified NCCR of its log centre.

We compute

Z(A)=R=k[a,b,c,d](ad-bc)

S=Z(A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2)=Z(A)^{\mathbb{Z}/2}=k[a^{2},ab,b^{2},c,d]\subset R.

The referee noted

S\simeq k[x,y,z,c,d]/(xz-y^{2},xd-yc,yd-zc)
which is the base of the Francia flip. So there is a small resolution
f:Y\to\mathrm{Spec}\,S
given by blowing up the ideal
(c,d)
. So
K_{Y}
must be ample or anti-ample, and
K_{Y}=f^{*}K_{S}.
For more details see
[
3
, (2
\cdot
1),Lem. 2]
or
[
15
, (3.8)]
.
If
S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein then
nK_{S}=0
for some
n
, giving a
contradiction. We also give a toric computation below which shows that
S
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.

We write
N=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
N^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and let

M=N^{\vee},M^{\prime}=N^{\prime\vee}
be the dual lattices. Let
\sigma
is the cone
generated by the columns of the matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1&1\\
0&1&0&1\\
1&1&1&1\end{pmatrix}.

We will see that the map of toric varieties determined by

\sigma\subset N
to
\sigma\subset N^{\prime}
is the map
\mathrm{Spec}\,R\to\mathrm{Spec}\,S
.
We write

k[M]=k[x^{\pm 1},y^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 1}]\subset k[x^{\pm 1},y^{\pm 1},z^{\pm 2}]=k[M^{\prime}].

We have

R=k[a,b,c,d]=k[zx^{-1},zy^{-1},y,x]=k[\sigma^{\vee}]\subset k[M^{\prime}].

S=k[a^{2},ab,b^{2},c,d]=k[z^{2}x^{-2},z^{2}x^{-1}y^{-1},z^{2}y^{-2},y,x]=k[\sigma^{\vee}]\subset k[M^{\prime}].

We compute that
A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2
is ramified with index two on the toric divisor
D_{\rho}
corresponding to the ray generated by
\rho=(0,0,1/2)\in\sigma\subset N^{\prime}
.
Hence the pair
(\mathrm{Spec}\,S,\frac{1}{2}D_{\rho})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and admits a ramified NCCR.
If we write the primitive generators of the rays of
\sigma
in terms of the basis

e_{1},e_{2},\frac{1}{2}e_{3}
of
N^{\prime},
we obtain the columns of the following matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1&1\\
0&1&0&1\\
1&2&2&2\end{pmatrix}.

Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{4}
be the toric Weil divisors corresponding to the rays.
A toric Weil divisor
n_{1}D_{1}+n_{2}D_{2}+n_{3}D_{3}+n_{4}D_{4}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier if and only
if
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{4})
is in the
\mathbb{Q}
span of the rows of the matrix
B
.
It is clear that
K_{\mathrm{Spec}\,S}=-D_{1}-D_{2}-D_{3}-D_{4}
is not in the row span of
C
. However as above we have
\frac{1}{2}D_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}D_{1}
then

K+B=-\frac{1}{2}D_{1}-D_{2}-D_{3}-D_{4}

is in the row span of
C
, verifying that the pair
(\mathrm{Spec}\,S,\frac{1}{2}D_{\rho})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.
So we see that the toric variety
\mathrm{Spec}\,S
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Goreinstein and does
not admit an unramified NCCR, and there is a ramified NCCR of the pair
(\mathrm{Spec}\,S,\frac{1}{2}D_{\rho})
.

In the next example, the singularity
X
is well known to not have a classical NCCR
[
7
, Thm. 3.1,Rmk. 3.2]
, but with an appropriate divisor
D
, we obtain a twisted ramified NCCR of the pair
(X,D)
. This shows that the pair is Kawamata log terminal and
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein. We would like to thank Michael Wemyss for providing us with this example.

Example 6.4
.

Let
A
be the
k
-algebra generated by
a,b,c
with relations

ac+ca,\quad bc+cb,\quad ab-ba-2c^{3}.

The centre
Z(A)
is generated by

x:=a^{2},\quad y:=b^{2},\quad z:=ab+ba,\quad t:=c^{2}.

One may verify that

z^{2}-4xy=(ab-ba)^{2}=4c^{6}=4t^{3}.

So the centre has the compound Du Val singularity with a quadric cone in its resolution
[
13
, Fig. 8-3-5]
. This singularity does not have a noncommutative crepant resolution
[
7
, Thm. 3.1,Rmk. 3.2]
.

The resolution

0\to A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}b&a&c\end{pmatrix}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}-c&0&-a\\
0&c&b\\
-b&a&-2c^{2}\end{pmatrix}}}{{\longrightarrow}}A^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\\
c\end{pmatrix}}}{{\to}}A\to k\to 0

shows that
A
is Artin-Schelter regular and so is homologically homogeneous.
Lastly, we compute that
A
is ramified on the divisor
B
defined by
t=0.

So if we let
X=\mathrm{Spec}\,Z(A)
and
D=\frac{1}{2}B
we have that
(X,D)
has a twisted noncommutative crepant resolution and so is Kawamata log terminal and
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein.

We also point out that
A
can be viewed as a Clifford algebra. Let

Z=k[x,y,z,t]/(z^{2}-4xy-4t^{3})

and define a quadratic form
Q
on
Z
given by the Gram matrix

\begin{pmatrix}2x&z&0\\
z&2y&0\\
0&0&2t\end{pmatrix}.

Write
\mathrm{Cl}(Q)
for the Clifford algebra of this form, generated by
a,b,c
as an
Z
-algebra with relations

2a^{2}=2x,\quad ab+ba=z,\quad 2b^{2}=2y,\quad ac+ca=bc+cb=0,\quad 2c^{2}=2t.

Note that in this algebra we have
(ab-ba)^{2}=(2c^{3})^{2}
giving
a prime ideal generated by
(ab-ba-2c^{3})
so we have

A=\mathrm{Cl}(Q)/(ab-ba-2c^{3}).

This description can be used to show that
A
is non-trivially twisted.

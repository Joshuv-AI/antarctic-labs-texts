1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime. The Artin-Hasse exponential
series is the formal power series in
\mathbb{Q}[[X]]
defined as

(1)

\AH(X)=\exp\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}X^{p^{i}}/p^{i}\biggr)=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\exp\left(X^{p^{i}}/p^{i}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}X^{n}.

As an immediate application of the Dieudonné-Dwork criterion, its coefficients
are
p
-integral, hence they can be evaluated modulo
p
.
Let
\E_{p}(X)=\sum a_{n}X^{n}
denote the reduction modulo
p

of the Artin-Hasse exponential series,
hence thought of as a series in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
.
The coefficients of
\E_{p}(X)
, and how to compute them, are the main focus of this paper.

The coefficients
u_{n}
have a remarkable combinatorial interpretation as
u_{n}=h_{n}/n!
, where
h_{n}
is the number of
p
-power order elements in the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Explicitly

(2)

u_{n}=\sum_{n=k_{0}+k_{1}p+\cdots+k_{r}p^{r}}\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0}^{r}k_{i}!\;p^{ik_{i}}}.

In particular,
u_{p}=1/p!+1/p
, and hence

u_{p}=w_{p}/(p-1)!\equiv-w_{p}\pmod{p}
, where
w_{p}
denotes the Wilson quotient.

The coefficients
u_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
of the Artin-Hasse series may be computed recursively based on

(3)

u_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{n-p^{i}},

where
u_{0}=1
and we naturally read
u_{m}=0
for
m<0
.
This recursive formula (see
[
KS79
, Lemma 1]
) follows from Equation (
1
) by differentiation.
A natural question is whether and how their residues modulo
p
, that is, the coefficients of
\E_{p}(X)
,
can be computed from the preceding ones avoiding reference to the rational coefficients of
\AH(X)
.
A version modulo
p
of Equation (
3
) may be used as long as
n
is not a multiple of
p
,
thus reducing the question to the recursive calculation of the coefficients of the form
a_{kp}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Note that computing
a_{kp}
from
u_{kp}
using (
2
) and reducing modulo
p
requires
supercongruences for binomial coefficients which soon become unavailable.
We give the first few such expressions for
a_{kp}
in terms of preceding
a_{jp}

at the beginning of Section
3
.
A contribution to computing
a_{kp}
from coefficients
a_{jp}
with
j<k

(avoiding explicit reduction of
u_{kp}
modulo
p
)
is the following result from
[
AM
]
, but only for
k
even.

Proposition 1

(Proposition 2 in
[
AM
]
)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\E_{p}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}X^{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
the reduction modulo
p
of the Artin-Hasse exponential series.
Then we have

(4)

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}a_{sp}X^{sp}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}X^{rp}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X^{p^{i}}=X^{p},

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
.

In fact, Proposition
1
determines the value of

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}

for any
k
, but that sum involves
a_{kp}
only when
k
is even.
In particular, for
k<p^{2}
that sum vanishes unless
k
is a multiple of
p-1
,
and equals alternately
1
and
-1
otherwise,
as detailed in Proposition
2
.
This paper was born out of an attempt to produce analogous results to cover the case where
k
is odd.

We present a conjectural equation for the value of

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}ra_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}
in the initial range
k<p
,
which involves
divided Bernoulli numbers

B_{n}/n
.
We state that as Conjecture
4
.
It may conveniently be written as
a polynomial congruence involving the power series

G(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{np}X^{n}

and the polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
defined as

\gamma(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}(B_{n}/n)X^{p-n}.

A better understanding of this polynomial appears crucial for proving Conjecture
4
.
The related power series

\gamma_{0}(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(B_{n}/n)X^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[[X]]

was studied by Zagier in
[
Zag14
]
,
along with other series involving Bernoulli numbers.
Although
\gamma_{0}(X)
might not be expressible in closed form, Zagier found a functional equation for it,
which we quote here as Equation (
12
).
In Proposition
7
we produce an analogous functional equation
for the polynomial
\gamma(X)
, namely,

\gamma(X-1)-\gamma(X)=\pounds_{1}(X)+X^{p-1}-w_{p}-1,

where
\pounds_{1}(X)
is the truncated logarithm.

Rather than attempting to adapt Zagier’s argument to a proof of
Proposition
7
, we relate that to a congruence of
Nielsen
[
Nie15
, Equation (7) at page 519]
, which is essentially the content of
our Proposition
6
.
Because Nielsen’s original statement is slightly incorrect,
and in fact was amended in
[
Fou85
, Proposition 3.1]

with a different proof based on
p
-adic integration,
we provide a fresh proof of Nielsen’s result
which is close to his original argument.

Our functional equation for
\gamma(X)
,
resulting from this new look at Nielsen’s congruence,
allows us to deduce some numerical congruences (modulo
p
) for certain finite sums involving
divided Bernoulli numbers.
We present those in Corollaries
8
and
9
.

2.
On the coefficients of the Artin-Hasse series modulo a prime

As observed in the Introduction, Equation (
3
) does not help in calculating
coefficients
a_{rp}
from previous coefficients.
Our next result allows us to compute some initial coefficients
a_{kp}
from previous
a_{rp}

as long as
k
is even.

Proposition 2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\E_{p}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}X^{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
the Artin-Hasse exponential series.
Then for
0\leq k<p^{2}-1
we have

(5)

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{j}&\textrm{if $k=j(p-1)$,}\\
\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

According to Proposition
1
we have

G(X)G(-X)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}X^{p^{i}-1}=1

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
,
where
G(X)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}X^{r}
.
Because

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}X^{p^{i}-1}\equiv 1+X^{p-1}\pmod{X^{p^{2}}}

we deduce

G(X)G(-X)\equiv 1/(1+X^{p-1})\pmod{X^{p^{2}}}

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
,
and the conclusion follows after expanding into a geometric series.
∎

Remark 3
.

An application of Proposition
2
for
k=2
yields
a_{2p}=a_{p}^{2}/2
when
p>3
,
and
a_{6}=1-a_{3}^{2}=0
when
p=3
.
Of course one may reach the same conclusion by expanding
u_{2p}
, the coefficient of
X^{2p}
in the Artin-Hasse exponential series regarded as series in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[X]]
, and then viewing it modulo
p
.
In fact,

u_{2p}=\frac{1+\binom{2p-1}{p-1}(2(p-1)!+(p-1)!^{2})}{(2p)!},

and because
(p-1)!=-1+pw_{p}

the numerator of this expression for
u_{2p}
equals
1+\binom{2p-1}{p-1}(-1+w_{p}^{2}p^{2})
.
When
p>3
Wolstenholme’s Theorem, namely,
\binom{2p-1}{p-1}\equiv 1
modulo
p^{3}
, implies
u_{2p}\equiv w_{p}^{2}/2\equiv u_{p}^{2}/2\pmod{p}
, as desired.
This argument can be easily reversed to produce
an alternate proof of Wolstenholme’s Theorem starting from the identity

a_{2p}=a_{p}^{2}/2

obtained from Equation (
5
) as above.

In order to extend Proposition
2
beyond the stated range one may consider, more generally, the sums

s_{k}=\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}a_{(k-r)p},

for
k\geq 0
, which we conveniently interpret as zero for
k<0
.
Then Equation (
4
) implies

X^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{k}X^{kp}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X^{p^{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}s_{k+1-p^{i}}\right)X^{(k+1)p}.

Consequently, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}s_{k+1-p^{i}}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if $k=0$}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, we deduce that
s_{k}=0
unless
k
is a multiple of
p-1
.

3.
A conjecture

Proposition
2
allows one to express the early
a_{kp}

in terms of preceding coefficients of the form
a_{jp}
only when
k
is even.
We already found
a_{2p}=a_{p}^{2}/2
in Remark
3
.
For
a_{3p}
we need to resort to direct calculation starting from the explicit expression for
u_{3p}
given by Equation (
2
), which reads

u_{3p}=\frac{1+3\binom{3p-1}{p-1}(p-1)!+3\binom{3p-1}{p-1}\binom{2p-1}{p-1}(p-1)!^{2}+\binom{3p-1}{p-1}\binom{2p-1}{p-1}(p-1)!^{3}}{(3p)!}.

Using known supercongruences for the binomial coefficients involved, shows

a_{3p}=\frac{a_{p}^{3}}{3!}-\frac{B_{p-3}}{9}

for
p>3
, where
B_{p-3}
denotes a Bernoulli number.
Now, Proposition
2
yields

a_{4p}=\frac{a_{p}^{4}}{4!}-\frac{B_{p-3}}{9}a_{p}

for
p>5
.
By similarly applying known supercongruences for binomial coefficients
to the expression for
u_{5p}
one finds

a_{5p}=\frac{a_{p}^{5}}{5!}-\frac{B_{p-3}}{9}a_{2p}-\frac{B_{p-5}}{25},

and then Proposition
2
yields

a_{6p}=\frac{a_{p}^{6}}{6!}-\frac{B_{p-3}}{9}a_{3p}-\frac{B_{p-5}}{25}a_{p}-\frac{B_{p-3}^{2}}{2\cdot 9^{2}}

for
p>7
.

To proceed further one would need higher supercongruences for binomial coefficients,
which appear currently unavailable.
In general, obtaining
a_{kp}
by direct calculation from
u_{kp}
(for
k
odd) requires congruences modulo
p^{k+1}
for the binomial coefficients involved in the numerator of
u_{kp}
.

Alternately, one would need a replacement for Proposition
2
that gives a nontrivial conclusion
for
k
odd.
The following conjectural recursion formula would do.

Conjecture 4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\E_{p}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}X^{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
the Artin-Hasse exponential series.
For every integer
1<k<p
we have

(6)

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}ra_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}=\frac{B_{p-k}}{k},

where
B_{n}
denotes the
n
-th Bernoulli number.

Here we adopt the prevalent convention where
B_{1}=-1/2
.
For even
k
, Conjecture
4

is a consequence of our results.
In fact, in that case, replacing
r
with
k-r
in the left-hand of Equation (
6
) and comparing with the original
expression shows

2\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}ra_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}=k\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}a_{rp}a_{(k-r)p}.

However, the right-hand side of this equation vanishes for
0<k<p-1

according to Proposition
2
.
So does the right-hand side of Equation (
6
)
because of the vanishing of odd-indexed Bernoulli numbers,
with the exception of
B_{1}
.
The same argument for
k=p-1
together with Proposition
2

yields

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{r}ra_{rp}a_{(p-1-r)p}=-B_{1}.

Thus, Conjecture
4
remains unproved only for odd
k
.
The value
k=1
is excluded because the right-hand side is not

p
-integral.
However, when
k=1
the left-hand side reads
-a_{p}
, which is the value modulo
p
of

-u_{p}\equiv w_{p}\equiv B_{p-1}-(p-1)/p\pmod{p}
,
because of the well-known congruence

(7)

(1+pB_{p-1})/p\equiv w_{p}+1\pmod{p},

see
[
Leh38
, Equation (24)]
.
If Conjecture
4
holds, then, for example, one would deduce

a_{7p}=\frac{a_{p}^{7}}{7!}-\frac{B_{p-3}}{9}a_{4p}-\frac{B_{p-5}}{5^{2}}a_{2p}-\frac{B_{p-3}^{2}}{2\cdot 9^{2}}a_{p}-\frac{B_{p-7}}{7^{2}}

for
p>7
.

Conjecture
4
can be equivalently formulated as the polynomial congruence

(8)

XG^{\prime}(X)G(-X)\equiv w_{p}X-\gamma(X){\pmod{X^{p}}}

where
G^{\prime}(X)
denotes the derivative of the power series

G(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{np}X^{n}
,
and
\gamma(X)
is the polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
defined as

\gamma(X)=-\sum_{k=2}^{p-1}(B_{p-k}/k)X^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}(B_{n}/n)X^{p-n}.

Because
XG(X)G(-X)\equiv X\pmod{X^{p}}
,
an equivalent form of Equation (
8
) is

(9)

X\frac{G^{\prime}(X)}{G(X)}\equiv w_{p}X-\gamma(X){\pmod{X^{p}}}.

The logarithmic derivative appearing
at the left-hand side suggests looking at
the derivative of
\pounds_{1}(G(X))
.
Here
\pounds_{r}(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}X^{k}/k^{r}\in\mathbb{Q}[X]
, for any integer
r
,
and implicitly depending on a given prime
p
, is standard notation
for the
finite polylogarithms
.
The
truncated logarithm

\pounds_{1}(X)
is a partial sum of the series
-\log(1-X)
.
Note that we have

X(d/dX)\pounds_{r}(X)=\pounds_{r-1}(X)
, and

\pounds_{0}(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}X^{k}=X(X^{p-1}-1)/(X-1)
.

Lemma 5
.

For any
g(X)\in 1+\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
we have

\pounds_{0}(g(X))\equiv\begin{cases}X^{p-1}-1{\pmod{X^{p}}}&\textrm{if $g^{\prime}(0)\neq 0$},\\
-1{\pmod{X^{2p-2}}}&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Because

X^{p}-1=(X-1)(\pounds_{0}(X)+1)
we have

(g(X)-1)^{p-1}=\pounds_{0}(g(X))+1.

If
g^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, then
g(X)-1
is a multiple of
X
but not of
X^{2}
(in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
).
Since
(g^{\prime}(0))^{p-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\ast}
the power series

(g(X)-1)^{p-1}

belongs to

X^{p-1}+X^{p}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]
,
and hence

\pounds_{0}(g(X))\equiv X^{p-1}-1{\pmod{X^{p}}}

as desired.
If, however,
g^{\prime}(0)=0
, then
g(X)-1
is a multiple of
X^{2}
, and hence

\pounds_{0}(g(X))\equiv-1{\pmod{X^{2p-2}}}
.
∎

In particular, taking
g(X)=G(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{np}X^{n}

in Lemma
5

we deduce

X\pounds_{0}(G(X))\equiv-X\pmod{X^{p}}
.
Hence

X\frac{d}{dX}\pounds_{1}(G(X))=X\frac{\pounds_{0}(G(X))}{G(X)}G^{\prime}(X)\equiv-X\frac{G^{\prime}(X)}{G(X)}\pmod{X^{p}},

and so our conjectured Equation (
8
) amounts to

(10)

X\frac{d}{dX}\pounds_{1}(G(X))\equiv-w_{p}X+\gamma(X){\pmod{X^{p}}}.

This suggests integrating both sides after dividing them by
X
.
Set

\rho(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}\frac{B_{n}}{n^{2}}X^{p-n},

whence
X(d/dX)\rho(X)=-\gamma(X)
.
Noting that the constant term of
\pounds_{1}(G(X))
is
\pounds_{1}(1)=0
,
yet another equivalent form of our conjectured Equation (
8
) reads

(11)

\pounds_{1}(G(X))\equiv-w_{p}X-\rho(X){\pmod{X^{p}}}.

Unfortunately, the polynomial
\rho(X)
remains rather mysterious.
However, in the next section we will obtain some information on the polynomial
\gamma(X)
.

4.
A polynomial involving divided Bernoulli numbers

Several polynomial forms of our Conjecture
4

given in the last section involve the polynomial

\gamma(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}(B_{n}/n)X^{p-n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
.
Don Zagier noted in
[
Zag14
]
that
the related power series

\gamma_{0}(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(B_{n}/n)X^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[[X]]

satisfies the functional equation

(12)

\gamma_{0}\left(\frac{X}{1-X}\right)-\gamma_{0}(X)=\log(1-X)+X.

Because

\bigl(X/(1-X)\bigr)^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{k-1}{n-1}X^{k}
,
Equation (
12
) is equivalent to the identity

(13)

\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{n-1}\frac{B_{n}}{n}=-\frac{1}{k},\quad\text{for $k>1$.}

Note that
\bigl(B_{p-1}/(p-1)\bigr)X^{p-1}
is the earliest term of the series
\gamma_{0}(X)

whose coefficient is not in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, but this term cancels in the difference at the left-hand side of
Equation (
12
).
Thus, after viewing Equation (
12
) modulo
X^{p}
we may view the result modulo
p
,
and find

(14)

\gamma^{\ast}\left(\frac{X}{1-X}\right)-\gamma^{\ast}(X)\equiv X-\pounds_{1}(X)\pmod{X^{p}},

in terms of the polynomial

\gamma^{\ast}(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}(B_{n}/n)X^{n}=X^{p}\gamma(1/X)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
.

One can actually refine the congruence of Equation (
14
)
to an identity in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[X]]

after an appropriate modification, and even to a polynomial identity
when expressed in terms of
\gamma(X)
, namely, Equation (
17
) below.
Although it is possible to prove Equation (
17
) by adapting direct arguments
in
[
Zag14
]
that rely on Equation (
13
),
we take a different route.

A polynomial closely related to
\gamma(X)
, namely,

\sum_{k=1}^{p-2}(B_{k}/k)(X^{p-1-k}-1)
,
was considered by Nielsen in
[
Nie15
]
,
and in more recent times by Fouché starting with
[
Fou85
]
.
We quote a crucial congruence for Nielsen’s work,
in an equivalent formulation that is closer to our polynomial
\gamma(X)
.

Proposition 6
.

For every integer
x
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{p-2}(B_{k}/k)x^{p-k}\equiv x\,q_{p}(x)+w_{p}\,x+\lfloor x/p\rfloor\pmod{p}.

Here

q_{p}(x)=(x^{p-1}-1)/p
, a Fermat quotient.
The product
x\,q_{p}(x)
is sometimes easier to handle than
q_{p}(x)
,
as it is an integer for every integer
x
.
Proposition
6
is
essentially
[
Fou85
, Proposition 3.1]
, an amended version of Nielsen’s congruence.
As Fouché pointed out,
the congruence as stated by Nielsen,
namely,
[
Nie15
, Equation (7) at page 519]
,
also quoted in
[
Dic66
, page 112]
,
is only correct in the range
0<x<p
.
Fouché provided a proof based on
p
-adic integration.
For the reader’s convenience we present a short proof
of Proposition
6

that is close to Nielsen’s original argument.

Proof.

First note that the statement follows inductively from its special case
where
0\leq x<p
, because

(x+p)\,q_{p}(x+p)\equiv x\,q_{p}(x)-1\pmod{p}
.
According to Faulhaber’s formula, for any non-negative integer
x
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{x}k^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{p}{j}B_{j}x^{p-j}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{x^{p}+pB_{p-1}x}{p}-\sum_{j=1}^{p-2}(B_{j}/j)x^{p-j}\pmod{p}.

\displaystyle\equiv x\,q_{p}(x)+(w_{p}+1)x-\sum_{j=1}^{p-2}(B_{j}/j)x^{p-j}\pmod{p},

where we have used

\frac{1}{p}\binom{p}{j}=\frac{1}{j}\binom{p-1}{j-1}\equiv(-1)^{j-1}/j\pmod{p}

for
0<j<p
, and then Equation (
7
).
The desired conclusion follows because

\sum_{k=0}^{x}k^{p-1}\equiv x\pmod{p}

for
0\leq x<p
.
∎

The left-hand side of Nielsen’s congruence in
Proposition
6

resembles an evaluation of our polynomial
\gamma(X)
, but the expression

x\,q_{p}(x)
at the right-hand side
cannot be viewed as a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
as it depends on the value of
x
modulo
p^{2}
.
Although the expression

x\,q_{p}(x)+\lfloor x/p\rfloor

can, handling Fermat quotients as polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
is more elegantly done by considering the symmetrized expression

(15)

x\,q_{p}(x)+(1-x)\,q_{p}(1-x)=\frac{x^{p}-1+(1-x)^{p}}{p}\equiv-\pounds_{1}(x)\pmod{p},

see
[
MT13
, Equation 12]
, for example.
Now

\lfloor x/p\rfloor+\lfloor(1-x)/p\rfloor

equals
0
if
x\equiv 0,1\pmod{p}
, and
-1
otherwise,
and the polynomial

1-X^{p-1}-(1-X)^{p-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
takes the value
0
on
0,1
,
and
-1
on the remaining elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
It follows that
\gamma(X)
satisfies

(16)

\gamma(X)+\gamma(1-X)=-\pounds_{1}(X)-X^{p-1}-(1-X)^{p-1}+w_{p}+1,

because both sides have degree less than
p
, and agree when
evaluated on elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
according to Proposition
6
.
Using the fact that
\gamma(X)+X^{p-1}/2
is an odd polynomial turns
Equation (
16
)
into the following finite analogue of Equation (
12
)
found by Zagier.

Proposition 7
.

The polynomial

\gamma(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}(B_{n}/n)X^{p-n}

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]

satisfies the functional equation

(17)

\gamma(X-1)-\gamma(X)=\pounds_{1}(X)+X^{p-1}-w_{p}-1.

Equation (
17
) actually characterizes
\gamma(X)
uniquely among the
polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
of degree less than
p
and without constant term.
That is because one easily sees that a polynomial

f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
satisfies
f(X-1)-f(X)=0

if and only if it has the form

f(X)=g(X^{p}-X)
for some
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
.

It is also easy to deduce Proposition
6
from
Proposition
7
,
working inductively
from the case
x=1
, which follows from
Equation (
17
) because

\gamma(0)=0
and
\pounds_{1}(1)=0
.

Despite this logical equivalence, Proposition
7

(or Equation (
16
))
is more flexible than Proposition
6
,
because of its formulation in terms of polynomials
allows evaluation on
p
-integral algebraic numbers.

We present some applications where
\pounds_{1}(X)

admits nice evaluations modulo
p
on those numbers,
such as

\pounds_{1}(1)\equiv 0\pmod{p},

\pounds_{1}(1/2)\equiv q_{p}(2)\pmod{p},

and

\pounds_{1}(-\omega)\equiv 0\pmod{p},

where
\omega=(-1+i\sqrt{3})/2

(for
p>2
and
p>3
in the second and third case).
All these follow easily from

(18)

\pounds_{1}(X)\equiv\frac{1-X^{p}-(1-X)^{p}}{p}\pmod{p},

a polynomial formulation of Equation (
15
).
Note also the symmetries

\pounds_{1}(1-X)\equiv\pounds_{1}(X)\pmod{p},

which follows from Equation (
18
), and

\pounds_{1}(1/X)\equiv-\pounds_{1}(X)/X^{p}\pmod{p}
.

Thus, taking
X=1
in Equation (
16
)
or (
17
) yields

\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}B_{n}/n\equiv w_{p}\pmod{p},

and taking
X=1/2
in Equation (
16
) yields

\sum_{n=1}^{p-2}2^{n}B_{n}/n\equiv-q_{p}(2)+w_{p}-1\pmod{p}.

Before dealing with the slightly more complicated application
where
X=-\omega
,
recall that
\gamma(X)
may be more conveniently written as

\gamma(X)=-\sum_{n=2}^{p-1}(B_{p-n}/n)X^{n}
, and that the term for

n=2
vanishes if
p>3
.

Corollary 8
.

For every prime
p>3
we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor(p-5)/6\rfloor}\frac{B_{p-6m-3}}{2m+1}\equiv\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{4}\Bigl(\frac{p}{3}\Bigr)\pmod{p}.

Here
(\frac{p}{3})
denotes a Legendre symbol, hence the sum is congruent to
1

or
-1/2
modulo
p
according as
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

Let
\omega=(-1+i\sqrt{3})/2
, hence
-\omega
and
-\omega^{-1}
are the primitive
complex sixth roots of unity.
We will evaluate Equation (
17
)
on
X=-\omega^{-1}=\omega+1
and its reciprocal (which equals its complex conjugate).
Because

\sum_{j=0}^{5}(-1)^{j}(-\omega)^{jn}
equals
6
if
n\equiv 3\pmod{6}

and zero otherwise,
we have

\displaystyle\gamma(1)

\displaystyle-\gamma(-\omega)+\gamma(\omega^{-1})-\gamma(-1)+\gamma(\omega)-\gamma(-\omega^{-1})

\displaystyle=-6\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq n\leq p-1,\\
n\equiv 3\pmod{6}\end{subarray}}\frac{B_{p-n}}{n}=-2\sum_{m=0}^{\lfloor(p-5)/6\rfloor}\frac{B_{p-6m-3}}{2m+1}

Because

\pounds_{1}(-\omega)\equiv\pounds_{1}(-\omega^{-1})\equiv 0\pmod{p}

as noted earlier,
according to Equation (
17
) we have

\gamma(\omega)-\gamma(-\omega^{-1})=(-\omega^{-1})^{p-1}-w_{p}-1
,
and

\gamma(\omega^{-1})-\gamma(-\omega)=(-\omega)^{p-1}-w_{p}-1
.
Equation (
17
) also implies

\gamma(1)=w_{p}
and
\gamma(-1)=-w_{p}-1
.
In conclusion, we find

\displaystyle\gamma(1)-\gamma(-\omega)+\gamma(\omega^{-1})-\gamma(-1)+\gamma(\omega)-\gamma(-\omega^{-1})

\displaystyle=\omega^{p-1}+\omega^{1-p}-1

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\Bigl(\frac{p}{3}\Bigr),

and the desired congruence follows.
∎

For our final application of the functional equation(s) for
\gamma(X)

we need the congruence

\pounds_{1}(i)+\pounds_{1}(-i)\equiv-q_{p}(2)\pmod{p}
,
which can be proved as follows starting from Equation (
18
), with congruences taking place in the ring of algebraic integers:

\displaystyle\pounds_{1}(i)+\pounds_{1}(-i)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2-(1-i)^{p}-(1+i)^{p}}{p}\pmod{p},

\displaystyle=\frac{2-2\cdot 2^{(p-1)/2}\cdot(-1)^{(p^{2}-1)/8}}{p}

\displaystyle=-q_{p}(2)+\frac{\bigl(2^{(p-1)/2}-(-1)^{(p^{2}-1)/8}\bigr)^{2}}{p}

\displaystyle\equiv-q_{p}(2)\pmod{p},

where the last congruence follows from

2^{(p-1)/2}\equiv(-1)^{(p^{2}-1)/8}\pmod{p}
,
due to the quadratic character of
2
.

Corollary 9
.

For every prime
p>3
we have

-\sum_{n=3}^{p-2}\frac{B_{p-n}}{n}\cdot(-1)^{(n^{2}-1)/8}\cdot 2^{(n+1)/2}\equiv q_{p}(2)+2w_{p}+3\frac{1-(-1)^{(p-1)/2}}{2}\pmod{p}.

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Corollary
8
,
we will evaluate Equation (
16
)
on
X=i
and
X=-i
, and add together the resulting equations.

We first deal with the quantities at the left-hand side of Equation (
16
).
Because
\gamma(X)+\gamma(-X)=-X^{p-1}
we have

\gamma(i)+\gamma(-i)=(-1)^{(p+1)/2}
.
Next, because

(1-i)^{n}+(1+i)^{n}=2^{-n/2}\left(\biggl(\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}}\biggr)^{n}+\biggl(\frac{1-i}{\sqrt{2}}\biggr)^{n}\right)=(-1)^{(n^{2}-1)/8}\cdot 2^{(n+1)/2}

for any odd integer
n
, we find

\displaystyle\gamma(1-i)

\displaystyle+\gamma(1+i)+\frac{1}{2}(1-i)^{p-1}+\frac{1}{2}(1+i)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=3}^{p-2}\frac{B_{p-n}}{n}(1-i)^{n}-\sum_{n=3}^{p-2}\frac{B_{p-n}}{n}(1+i)^{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=3}^{p-2}\frac{B_{p-n}}{n}\cdot(-1)^{(n^{2}-1)/8}\cdot 2^{(n+1)/2}.

Turning to the right-hand side of Equation (
16
),
we have

\pounds_{1}(i)+\pounds_{1}(-i)\equiv-q_{p}(2)\pmod{p}

as noted earlier, and

(\pm i)^{p-1}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}
.
Furthermore, because

(1\pm i)^{p}\equiv 1\pm i^{p}=1\pm(-1)^{(p-1)/2}i\pmod{p}

we find

(1-i)^{p-1}+(1+i)^{p-1}\equiv 1+(-1)^{(p-1)/2}\pmod{p}.

Therefore,
evaluating the right-hand side of Equation (
16
)
on
X=i
and
X=-i
and adding the results yields

q_{p}(2)+2w_{p}+1-3(-1)^{(p-1)/2}.

Equating this to the sum of the left-hand side of Equation (
16
)
evaluated on
X=i
and
X=-i
, as discussed earlier,
yields the desired conclusion.
∎

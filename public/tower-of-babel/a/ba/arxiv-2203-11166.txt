Identification of maximal C ∗ -covers of some operator algebras
Benton L. Duncan

Abstract
. We use results on inclusions of free products and extensions of completely positive maps to determine the maximal C ∗ -envelope for upper triangular 3 × 3 3\times 3 matrices. We consider these same results in the context of larger upper triangular matrices and graph algebras associated to cycle graphs.

1.
Introduction

In 1999, Blecher
[
2
]
introduced the concept of the maximal C
∗
-cover of an operator algebra. This algebra encodes the completely contractive representation theory of an operator algebra. In particular, given an operator algebra
A
, the maximal C
∗
-cover C
{}^{*}_{\rm max}(A)
is the C
∗
-algebra generated by a completely isometric copy of
A
so that any completely contractive representation of
A
into a C
∗
-algebra extends to a
∗
-representation of C
{}^{*}_{\rm max}(A)
into the same C
∗
-algebra. In that paper, Blecher provided a concrete example for the operator algebra
T_{2}
of the upper triangular
2\times 2
matrices which is the universal C
∗
-algebra generated by a contractive nilpotent of degree 2 (see the first row of Table 1 in
[
15
]
).

Several authors have extended the use of the maximal C
∗
-cover for operator algebras. It has found use in
[
4
]
to understand a version of
Ext
for nonselfadjoint operator algebras. More recently, in
[
7
]
and
[
19
]
the maximal C
∗
-cover of an operator algebra was used to study a notion of residually finite dimensional nonselfadjoint operator algebras. At the same time, the use of the maximal C
∗
-covers is constrained somewhat by the lack of concrete examples of maximal C
∗
-covers. The author made a general attempt to understand the maximal C
∗
-cover of an operator algebra using Banach
*
-algebra methods in
[
10
]
, but even there concrete examples were absent.

In a similar spirit to that of this paper, in
[
13
]
, the authors consider the universal C
∗
-algebra generated by an operator system (arising in a similar manner from universal properties) and there they construct maximal C
∗
-algebras of the operator systems
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C},
and
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
. They also show that the universal C
∗
-algebra of the operator system
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is non-exact. An important difference to note is that viewing these as operator systems is different from viewing them as operator algebras, since in all three cases, as C
∗
-algebras, their (algebraic) maximal C
∗
-cover is themselves. In that context, maximal C
∗
-envelopes of operator systems were used by Davidson and Kennedy in a recent preprint
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we do two things. First, we build tools to more readily understand inclusions of free products of C
∗
-algebras. These extend results of
[
16
]
concerning pushout diagrams and their extensions in
[
1
]
to general inclusions of C
∗
-algebras in free products. We also extend recent results on conditional expectations of free products from
[
8
]
. Second, we use the tools we develop to consider a method to build concrete maximal C
∗
-covers in two contexts. There we will see that even simple operator algebras give rise to non-exact maximal C
∗
-covers. We will also demonstrate how quickly the complications of this technique interfere in coherent natural generalizations of the example we build.

We refer the reader to the introduction of
[
8
]
for the relevant details on the construction of amalgamated free products. For our purposes, given unital C
∗
-algebras
C_{i}
with common C
∗
-subalgebra
C
the amalgamated free product
C_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{2}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,\cdots\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{n}
contains a dense subalgebra spanned by elements of the form
c_{i_{1}}c_{i_{2}}\cdots c_{i_{m}}
where
c_{i_{j}}
is in one of the
C_{i}

c_{i_{j}}
and
c_{i_{j+1}}
are contained in the same

. Thus a continuous linear map with domain
C_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{2}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,\cdots\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{n}
is uniquely determined by its action on these generating elements. Now, when there exist conditional expectations
E_{i}:C_{i}\rightarrow C
, we can say more. In this case the algebra
C_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{2}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,\cdots\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,C_{n}
is the closure of

C\oplus\left(\oplus_{1\leq p\leq\infty,i_{k}\neq i_{k+1}}\ker E_{i_{1}}\otimes\ker E_{i_{2}}\otimes\ker E_{i,p}\right).

This allows
[
8
]

Proposition 2.5
.

Consider a finite family of unital C
∗
-algebras with inclusions
B_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

∗

C^{\prime}\subseteq B_{i}
for all
i

C\subseteq A_{i}
for all
i

C^{\prime}\subseteq C

F_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow C

Since the maps

are onto conditional expectations, they are completely positive and are the identity on their range which is
B_{i}

and notice that by hypothesis
G_{i}|_{C}=G_{j}|_{C}
for all
i,j
.

Now let

be a Hilbert space so that there exists

\pi:B_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C^{\prime}}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,B_{2}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C^{\prime}}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,\cdots\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C^{\prime}}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,B_{k}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})

which is a faithful

\Phi:A_{1}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,A_{2}\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,\cdots\underset{\scriptscriptstyle C}{\displaystyle{\ast}}\,A_{n}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})

\Phi|_{A_{i}}=\pi\circ G_{i}

\Phi(a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}})=\pi\circ G_{j_{1}}(a_{j_{1}})\pi\circ G_{j_{2}})(a_{j_{2}})\cdots\pi\circ G_{j_{k}}(a_{j_{k}})

means that

is the closed linear span of the space

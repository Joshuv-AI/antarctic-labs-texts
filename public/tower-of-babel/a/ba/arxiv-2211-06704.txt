A Remark on a Nonlocal-in-Time Heat Equation
Christoph Walker

Abstract
. Schauder’s fixed point theorem is used to derive the existence of solutions to a semilinear heat equation. The equation features a nonlinear term that depends on the time-integral of the unknown on the whole, a priori given, interval of existence.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35K58

1.
Introduction

This note is dedicated to the nonlocal problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\big(d(x)\nabla u\big)+\varphi\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s,x)u(s,x)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)u

\displaystyle=f(t,x)\,,\quad(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\Omega\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)=u^{0}\,,\quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(1.1b)

on a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
d\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
such that
d(x)\geq d_{0}>0
for
x\in\Omega
. The potential
\varphi
, the weight
a
, the initial datum
u^{0}
, and the right-hand side
f
are suitable given functions.

Equation (
1.1
) is used in the modeling of a biological nanosensor in the chaotic dynamics of a polymer chain in an aqueous solution and has been introduced and considered in
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. It can also be seen as a toy model for equilibrium states in age-structured diffusive populations (with
t
referring to the age of individuals), see
[
8
,
9
]
for instance.

Note that the unknown weighted time-integral

\bar{u}=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(s)\,u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

depends on the whole, a priori
given
interval of existence
(0,\infty)
(the case of a bounded time interval
(0,T)
is included in (
1.1
), of course, by taking
a
with bounded support). Hence, only global solutions are of interest. Moreover, (
1.1
) is no usual evolution problem satisfying a Volterra property since solutions at a time instant depend also on future time instants. For the homogeneous version of (
1.1
) on a bounded time interval
(0,T)
with vanishing right-hand side
f
and without weight
a
, existence of weak solutions was derived in
[
5
,
6
]
and strong solutions in
[
10
]
. The non-homogeneous problem (
1.1
) on a bounded interval
(0,T)
was investigated in
[
7
]
where, for unbounded potentials
\varphi
, a truncation approach and weak compactness methods were used to prove the existence of weak solutions under fairly general conditions.

The purpose of this work is to propose an alternative approach to (
1.1
) for deriving the existence of mild and strong solutions under slightly different conditions. This approach has been used in
[
10
]
and may also be a template for other nonlocal problems. More precisely,
we shall use the fact that solutions to (
1.1
) may be written as mild solutions in the form

u(t)=e^{tA(\bar{u})}u^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

(1.2)

where
(e^{tA(\bar{u})})_{t\geq 0}
is the contraction semigroup on
L_{p}(\Omega)
generated by the operator

A(\bar{u}):=\big[w\mapsto\mathrm{div}\big(d(x)\nabla w\big)-\varphi(\bar{u})w\big]

subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions (see below for details).
Integrating the representation (
1.2
) yields the equivalent fixed point equation

\bar{u}=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,e^{tA(\bar{u})}u^{0}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

(1.3)

for
\bar{u}
. We then shall focus on this fixed point equation and prove, in particular, that the right-hand side of (
1.3
) enjoys suitable compactness properties with respect to
\bar{u}
that allow us to apply Schauder’s theorem leading to the following existence result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))
,
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{+})
, and
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
for some
p\in(\max\{1,n/2\},\infty)
and let
u^{0}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then there is a mild solution
u\in C\big(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega)\big)

to (
1.1
) such that

\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{L_{1}((0,t),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\,.

If
f\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{q}(\Omega))+C(\mathbb{R}^{+},W_{q}^{\theta}(\Omega))
with
\theta>0
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, then
u
is a strong solution with

u\in C\big(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\cap C^{1}\big(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+},L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\cap C\big(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+},W_{q}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\,,

where
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{+}:=(0,\infty)
.

In Section
2
we prove Theorem
1.1
. The crucial compactness properties of the integral terms appearing on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) are postponed to Section
3
. The proofs there are inspired by the works
[
2
,
3
]
and may be extended to more general frameworks than the one considered herein for (
1.1
), e.g. to other semilinear and possibly quasilinear equations (see Remark
3.3
in this regard). Also some of the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
may be weaken, e.g. for linearly bounded
\varphi
or smoother
a
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Notation and Preliminaries

We use the notation

W_{p,D}^{\alpha}(\Omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\{u\in W_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\,;\,u=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\}&\text{ if }&{\frac{1}{p}}<\alpha\leq 2\,,\\[3.0pt]
W_{p}^{\alpha}(\Omega)&\text{ if }&0\leq\alpha<{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\end{array}\right.

and we write
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{H}(W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))
if
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))
generates an analytic semigroup
(e^{t\mathcal{A}})_{t\geq 0}
on
L_{p}(\Omega)
. Recall that

\big[w\mapsto\mathrm{div}\big(d(x)\nabla w\big)\big]\in\mathcal{H}(W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))

provided
d\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega}))
with
d(x)\geq d_{0}>0
for
x\in\Omega
.

Since
\varphi
is uniformly continuous and bounded on bounded sets, it follows that (considered as Nemytskii operator)

\varphi\in C\big(L_{\infty}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega)\big)\ \text{ is bounded on bounded sets}\,.

(2.1)

Given
R_{0}>0
denote by

X:=\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}(0,R_{0})

the closed ball in
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
of radius
R_{0}
centered at the origin. Recall that
p\in(\max\{n/2,1\},\infty)
and note that, given any
\bar{u}\in X
, the mapping
\varphi(\bar{u}):=[w\mapsto\varphi(\bar{u})w]\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{p}(\Omega)\big)
satisfies

\|\varphi(\bar{u})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\leq\|\varphi(\bar{u})\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{[-R_{0},R_{0}]}\varphi\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

Setting

A(\bar{u})w:=\mathrm{div}\big(d\nabla w\big)-\varphi(\bar{u})w\,,\quad w\in W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega)\,,

it then follows from standard perturbation results that

A(\bar{u})\in\mathcal{H}(W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

In fact, since
\varphi
is nonnegative,
(e^{tA(\bar{u})})_{t\geq 0}
is a positive contraction semigroup on each
L_{q}(\Omega)
for
q\in(1,\infty]
(which, however, is
not
strongly continuous for
q=\infty
), hence

\|e^{tA(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{q}(\Omega))}\leq 1\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\quad q\in(1,\infty]\,.

(2.2)

Moreover, we have
s(A(\bar{u}))\leq s_{0}<0
for its spectral bound with
s_{0}
denoting the spectral bound of the operator
\big[w\mapsto\mathrm{div}\big(d\nabla w\big)\big]
.
It then follows from
[
1
, II.Lemma 5.1.3]
that there is
\nu>0

and, given
2\theta\in[0,2]\setminus\{1/p\}
, there is
M(R_{0})\geq 1
such that

\|e^{tA(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}\leq M(R_{0})\,e^{-\nu t}\,t^{-\theta}\,,\quad t>0\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

(2.3)

In the following we fix
2\theta\in(n/p,2)
and note the compact embedding

W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c}}{{\hookrightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{p}(\Omega)\,.

(2.4)

Let us also observe that, given
t>0
and
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in X
, we have

\displaystyle e^{tA(\bar{u})}-e^{tA(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}e^{sA(\bar{v})}\,\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,\big(\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\big)\,e^{sA(\bar{v})}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(2.5)

We then use (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) to get

\displaystyle\|e^{tA(\bar{u})}-e^{tA(\bar{v})}

\displaystyle\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq c\,\|e^{tA(\bar{u})}-e^{tA(\bar{v})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|e^{sA(\bar{v})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\,e^{-\nu t}\,t^{1-\theta}\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}\,.

(2.6)

We are now in a position to provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We show that the mapping

\Phi(\bar{u}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,e^{tA(\bar{u})}u^{0}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X=\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}(0,R_{0})\,,

with

R_{0}:=\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\big(\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\big)

has a fixed point. Note first from (
2.2
) that
\|\Phi(\bar{u})\|_{\infty}\leq R_{0}
for
\bar{u}\in X
,
hence
\Phi:X\rightarrow X
. Next, due to (
2.6
) and (
2.4
) we obtain, for
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in X
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\bar{u})-\Phi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\|e^{tA(\bar{u})}-e^{tA(\bar{v})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|u^{0}\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}-e^{(t-s)A(\bar{v})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\int_{0}^{\infty}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,e^{-\nu t}\,t^{1-\theta}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\|u^{0}\|_{p}\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+c(R_{0})\int_{0}^{\infty}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-s)}\,(t-s)^{1-\theta}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}

and hence, recalling
\nu>0
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\bar{u})-\Phi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}\leq c(R_{0})\,\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\big(\|u^{0}\|_{p}+\|f||_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\big)\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}\,.

(2.7)

Therefore,
\Phi\in C(X,X)
according to (
2.1
). From Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
we deduce that
\Phi\in C(X,X)
has precompact image so that Schauder’s fixed point theorem yields
\bar{u}\in X
such that
\bar{u}=\Phi(\bar{u})
. We may then define
u
by (
1.2
)
in order to obtain a mild solution to (
1.1
) which belongs to
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
due to
[
1
, II.Theorem 5.3.1]
since
f\in L_{\infty,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
.

Finally, if
f\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{q}(\Omega))+C(\mathbb{R}^{+},W_{q}^{\theta}(\Omega))
for some
\theta>0
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, then
u
is a strong solution with
the regularity properties stated in Theorem
1.1
, see
[
1
, II.Theorem 1.2.1, II.Theorem 1.2.2]
. This proves Theorem
1.1
.∎

Remark 2.1
.

If
\varphi
is locally Lipschitz continuous, then one may derive the existence and uniqueness of a solution using Banach’s fixed point argument for
\Phi:X\to X
provided that

R_{0}=\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\big(\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\big)

is small enough.

3.
Compactness Properties

We provide the compactness results used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. This section relies on the papers
[
2
,
3
]
and adapts these ideas to our setting.

We first consider the non-homogeneous part.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))

and
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L_{\infty,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
for some
p\in(\max\{n/2,1\},\infty)
. Define

\mathcal{F}(\bar{u}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

Then the set

\left\{\mathcal{F}(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}

is precompact in
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Given
T>0
introduce

\mathbb{X}_{T}:=C([0,T],L_{\infty}(\Omega))

and

F(\bar{u})(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X=\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}(0,R_{0})\,.

It suffices to prove that
F\in C\left(X,\mathbb{X}_{T}\right)
is compact for every
T>0
since the assertion then follows by a diagonal sequence argument and the assumption
a\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))
.

(i)
Since
f\in L_{\infty,loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
, we infer from
[
1
, II.Theorem 5.3.1]
that
F(\bar{u})\in C([0,T],W_{p}^{2\theta}(\Omega))
and hence

F(\bar{u})\in\mathbb{X}_{T}
for
\bar{u}\in X
by (
2.4
).
Moreover, given
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in X
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have, as in (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\|F(\bar{u})(t)-F(\bar{v})(t)\|_{\infty}\leq c(R_{0})\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|\varphi(\bar{u})-\varphi(\bar{v})\|_{\infty}\,.

Therefore,
F\in C\left(X,\mathbb{X}_{T}\right)
owing to (
2.1
).

(ii)
In order to prove that
F\in C\left(X,\mathbb{X}_{T}\right)
has precompact image we use an idea inspired by
[
2
,
3
]
: for fixed
\lambda>0
we first show that

\displaystyle\left\{e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}\ \text{ is precompact in }\ \mathbb{X}_{T}\,.

(3.1)

To this end, we note from (
2.3
) and (
2.2
) that, for
\bar{u}\in X
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega)}\leq c(R_{0})\,\lambda^{-\theta}\,\|F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{p}\leq c(R_{0})\,\lambda^{-\theta}\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\,.

(3.2)

That is, invoking (
2.4
), the set

\displaystyle\left\{e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}\ \text{ is precompact in }\ L_{\infty}(\Omega)

(3.3)

for every
t\in[0,T]
. Before proceeding let us note that, given
\bar{u}\in X
,
\delta>0
and
h\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle e^{(\delta+h)A(\bar{u})}-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{h}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}e^{(\delta+s)A(\bar{u})}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{h}e^{(\frac{\delta}{2}+s)A(\bar{u})}A(\bar{u})e^{\frac{\delta}{2}A(\bar{u})}\,\mathrm{d}s

so that, using (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle e^{(\delta+h)A(\bar{u})}-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{h}\|e^{(\frac{\delta}{2}+s)A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega))}\,\|A(\bar{u})\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega),L_{p}(\Omega))}\,\|e^{\frac{\delta}{2}A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),W_{p,D}^{2}(\Omega))}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq c(R_{0})\,e^{-\nu\delta}\,\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{h}\left(\frac{\delta}{2}+s\right)^{-\theta}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

Consequently, invoking (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\|e^{(\delta+h)A(\bar{u})}-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq c(R_{0})\,e^{-\nu\delta}\,\delta^{-1-\theta}h\,,\qquad h\geq 0\,,\quad\delta>0\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

(3.4)

Next, given
\bar{u}\in X
and
0\leq t\leq t+h\leq T
we have, using (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})

\displaystyle(t+h)-e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{t}^{t+h}\|e^{(\lambda+t+h-s)A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+c\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(\lambda+t+h-s)A(\bar{u})}-e^{(\lambda+t-s)A(\bar{u})}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\Omega),L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

We use (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) once more along with (
3.4
) to get

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}

\displaystyle F(\bar{u})(t+h)-e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\int_{t}^{t+h}(\lambda+t+h-s)^{-\theta}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s+c(R_{0})\,h\int_{0}^{t}(\lambda+t-s)^{-1-\theta}\,\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\,\lambda^{-\theta}\int_{t}^{t+h}\|f(s)\|_{p}\,\mathrm{d}s+c(R_{0})\,h\,\lambda^{-1-\theta}\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\,.

Therefore, since
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))
we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\,\sup_{\bar{u}\in X}\,\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}

\displaystyle F(\bar{u})(\cdot+h)-e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{T}}=0\,,\quad\lambda>0\,.

(3.5)

Gathering (
3.5
) and (
3.3
) we conclude that
\left\{e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}
is indeed precompact in
\mathbb{X}_{T}
due to the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem.

(iii)
Next, we claim that

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\,\sup_{\bar{u}\in X}\,\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}

\displaystyle F(\bar{u})-F(\bar{u})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{T}}=0\,.

(3.6)

Let
\delta\in(0,T)
. Using (
2.2
) we have, for
0\leq t\leq\delta
,

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\int_{0}^{\delta}\|f(s)\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(3.7)

On the other hand, for
\delta\leq t\leq T
, we use (
2.2
) to get

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)-e^{(\lambda+\delta)A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|e^{(\lambda+\delta)A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\quad+\|e^{(\lambda+\delta)A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)\|_{\infty}\,.

For the first term on the right-hand side we use (
2.2
) to estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}=\left\|\int_{t-\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)A(\bar{u})}\,f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}s

while we use (
3.4
) and (
3.2
) for the second term to obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{(\lambda+\delta)A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)-e^{\delta A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\,\delta^{-1-\theta}\,\lambda\,\|F(\bar{u})(t-\delta)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq c(R_{0})\,\delta^{-1-\theta}\,\lambda\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\,.

Gathering these estimates we derive, for
\delta\leq t\leq T
,

\begin{split}\|e^{\lambda A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})(t)-F(\bar{u})(t)\|_{\infty}&\leq 2\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}s+c(R_{0})\,\delta^{-1-\theta}\,\lambda\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{p}(\Omega))}\,.\end{split}

(3.8)

Since
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))
we may first choose
\delta>0
small enough and then let
\lambda
tend to zero to conclude from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) that (
3.6
) indeed holds true.

(iv)
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then, due to (
3.6
), there is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda_{0}A(\bar{u})}

\displaystyle F(\bar{u})-F(\bar{u})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{T}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,,

(3.9)

while (
3.1
) yields finitely many
\bar{u}_{1},\ldots,\bar{u}_{N}\in X
such that for every
\bar{u}\in X
there exists
k\in{1,\ldots,N}
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{\lambda_{0}A(\bar{u})}F(\bar{u})-e^{\lambda_{0}A(\bar{u}_{k})}F(\bar{u}_{k})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{T}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\,.

(3.10)

Hence
\|F(\bar{u})-F(\bar{u}_{k})\|_{\mathbb{X}_{T}}\leq\varepsilon

so that
\left\{F(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}
is totally bounded in
\mathbb{X}_{T}
. This proves the assertion.
∎

We prove a compactness result for the part involving the initial condition:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))
.
Given
u^{0}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
define

\mathcal{G}(\bar{u}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,e^{tA(\bar{u})}\,u^{0}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X=\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}(0,R_{0})\,.

Then the set

\left\{\mathcal{G}(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}

is precompact in
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
and set

\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(\bar{u}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(t)\,e^{(\lambda+t)A(\bar{u})}\,u^{0}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad\bar{u}\in X\,.

Similarly as in Proposition
3.1
we infer that

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}\ \text{ is precompact in }\ L_{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

(3.11)

Taking
\delta>0
and using (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and (
3.4
) we then get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\mathcal{G}(\bar{u})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\delta}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}+\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\big\|e^{(\lambda+t)A(\bar{u})}u^{0}-e^{tA(\bar{u})}u^{0}\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\delta}\|a(t)\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\|u^{0}\|_{\infty}+c(R_{0})\,\delta^{-1-\theta}\,\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))}\,\|u^{0}\|_{p}\,\lambda\,.

Since
a\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L_{\infty}(\Omega))
we may choose
\delta>0
small to make the first term small and then let
\lambda
go to zero to conclude

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\,\sup_{\bar{u}\in X}\,\|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\mathcal{G}(\bar{u})\|_{\infty}=0\,.

(3.12)

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) we deduce that
\left\{\mathcal{G}(\bar{u})\,;\,\bar{u}\in X\right\}
is precompact in
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The compactness results of this section rely on properties (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
) and thus may also be derived for truly quasilinear operators
A(\bar{u})
based on the stability estimates of
[
1
, II.Section 5]
. One has, however, to replace the set
X\subset L_{\infty}(\Omega)
by a subset of
W_{p,D}^{2\theta}(\Omega)
with
\theta\in(0,2)\setminus\{1/p\}
and to impose suitable assumptions on the data (e.g. the weight
a
has to be sufficiently smooth in
x
).

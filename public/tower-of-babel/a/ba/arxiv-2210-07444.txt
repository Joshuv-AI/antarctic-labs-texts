Abstract
. On a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) \left(M,g\right) of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 with positive scalar curvature and not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere, we prove that the only conformal metrics to g g with constant Q \Q -curvature of order 4 are the metrics λ ​ g \lambda g with λ > 0 \lambda>0 constant.



1.
Introduction and main result

On a smooth, closed (i.e. compact and without boundary) Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, Branson’s
\Q
-curvature
[
Bra1
]
is defined as

\Q_{g}:=\frac{1}{2\left(n-1\right)}\Delta_{g}\Scal_{g}+\frac{n^{3}-4n^{2}+16n-16}{8\left(n-1\right)^{2}\left(n-2\right)^{2}}\Scal_{g}^{2}-\frac{2}{\left(n-2\right)^{2}}\left|\Ricci_{g}\right|_{g}^{2},

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{g}:=-\divergence_{g}\nabla
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator,
\Scal_{g}
is the scalar curvature and
\Ricci_{g}
is the Ricci curvature of the manifold.

In the case of the standard sphere, the conformal metrics with constant
\Q
-curvature have been classified by Lin
[
Lin
]
by using the moving-plane method. In this case, there exists an explicit, multi-dimensional family of conformal metrics with constant
\Q
-curvature. In this article, we examine the case of Einstein manifolds with positive scalar curvature and not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere. In this case, we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Einstein manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with positive scalar curvature and not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere. Then the only conformal metrics to
g
with constant
\Q
-curvature are the metrics
\lambda g
with
\lambda>0
constant.

This result extends to the
\Q
-curvature problem a result obtained by Obata
[
Oba
]
for the scalar curvature problem (see also the subsequent articles by Bidaut-Véron and Véron
[
BidVer
]
and Gidas and Spruck
[
GidSpr
]
for extensions of Obata’s result to more general second-order equations).

The transformation law for the
\Q
-curvature under a conformal change of metric is given by the equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Paneitz_{g}u+\Q_{g}=\Q_{e^{2u}g}e^{4u}\quad\text{in }M&&\text{ if }n=4\\
&\Paneitz_{g}u=\frac{n-4}{2}\Q_{u^{\frac{4}{n-4}}g}u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}},\ u>0\quad\text{in }M&&\text{ if }n\neq 4,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\Paneitz_{g}
is the Paneitz–Branson operator
[
Bra1
,
Pan
]
defined as

\Paneitz_{g}:=\Delta_{g}^{2}-\divergence_{g}\left(\left(\frac{n^{2}-4n+8}{2\left(n-1\right)\left(n-2\right)}\Scal_{g}g-\frac{4}{n-2}\Ricci_{g}\right)\nabla u\right)+\frac{n-4}{2}\Q_{g}.

In particular, in the case where
\left(M,g\right)
is Einstein, we obtain

Q_{g}=\frac{\left(n+2\right)\left(n-2\right)}{8n\left(n-1\right)^{2}}\Scal_{g}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\Paneitz_{g}=\Delta_{g}^{2}+\frac{n^{2}-2n-4}{2n\left(n-1\right)}\Scal_{g}\Delta_{g}+\frac{n-4}{2}\Q_{g}.

We mention in passing that the concept of
Q
-curvature and the corresponding operator

have been shown to have natural extensions to higher orders. Some references in this case are by Branson
[

]
, Fefferman and Graham
[

2.
Extended versions and proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove the following results, which are slightly more general than Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p\leq 4
and
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Einstein manifold of dimension
n=4
with positive scalar curvature. In the case where
p=4
, assume that
\left(M,g\right)
is not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere. Then there does not exist any non-constant solutions to the equation

\Paneitz_{g}u+\Q_{g}=e^{pu}\quad\text{in }M.

(2.1)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
n\neq 4
,
p\leq\frac{2n}{n-4}
and
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Einstein manifold of dimension

with positive scalar curvature. In the case where
p=\frac{2n}{n-4}
, assume that
\left(M,g\right)
is not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere. Then there does not exist any non-constant solutions to the equation

\Paneitz_{g}u=u^{p-1},\ u>0\quad\text{in }M.

(2.2)

We obtain Theorem
1.1
from Theorems

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

-curvature. Let

be such that

in
M
if

. Since

and

are constant, integrating (

) in

\Q_{g}\Vol_{g}\left(M\right)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Q_{\tilde{g}}\int_{M}e^{4u}dv_{g}&&\text{if }n=4\\
&\Q_{\tilde{g}}\int_{M}u^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}}dv_{g}&&\text{if }n\neq 4,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

where
\Vol_{g}\left(M\right)
is the volume of
\left(M,g\right)
and

is the volume element of
\left(M,g\right)
. Since

in
M
, it follows from (

) that
\Q_{\tilde{g}}>0

M
. We then define

\tilde{u}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u+\frac{1}{4}\ln\Q_{\tilde{g}}&&\text{if }n=4\\
&\left(\frac{n-4}{2}\Q_{\tilde{g}}\right)^{\frac{n-4}{8}}u&&\text{if }n\neq 4.\end{aligned}\right.

We can then rewrite (

) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Paneitz_{g}\tilde{u}+\Q_{g}=e^{4\tilde{u}}\quad\text{in }M&&\text{ if }n=4\\
&\Paneitz_{g}\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}^{\frac{n+4}{n-4}},\ \tilde{u}>0\quad\text{in }M&&\text{ if }n\neq 4,\end{aligned}\right.

Applying Theorems

and
2.2
, we then obtain that

is constant, which implies that

is constant. This ends the proof of Theorem

.

The proofs of Theorems

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

2.3

We closely follow the proof of Theorem 2.2 in

in

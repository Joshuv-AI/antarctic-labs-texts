The temperature and free energy of multi-black hole systems
C. J. Ramírez-Valdez, H. García-Compeán, and V. S. Manko

Abstract
In the present paper, we compute the Euclidean action of a generic system consisting of N N arbitrary Kerr-Newman black holes located on the symmetry axis and separated from each other by massless struts. This allows us to introduce the Hawking average temperature (HAT) T ^ \hat{T} of the multi-black hole system via the condition of vanishing the entire set of terms involving the singular horizons due to periodic time, and the resulting formula for this temperature contains solely the surface gravities κ i \kappa_{i} and horizon areas A i H A^{H}_{i} of the black hole constituents. We also show that the corresponding expression for the free energy of the system defined by T ^ \hat{T} is consistent with the first law of thermodynamics and Smarr mass relations.

I
Introduction

The Euclidean action approach to black hole physics was introduced
by Gibbons and Hawking in their seminal paper
GHa
where they
confirmed in an elegant new way the previously found analytic
relations of the Hawking temperature to the surface gravity

Haw
, and of the black hole’s entropy to the area of the event
horizon
Bek
. Though this approach was originally applied to
the study of the thermodynamic behavior of a single black hole, the
subsequent interest in the binary black-hole configurations,
stimulated by the development of modern solution generating
techniques and construction of various multi-black hole metrics

KNe
;
MMR
, later led to the appearance of a number of papers in
which the Euclidean formalism was employed for the analysis of some
specific double-black-hole systems with
equal
surface
gravities of the constituents. Thus, in the paper
CPe
, Costa
and Perry evaluated the free energy of two equal Schwarzschild black
holes and, compared to the paper
GHa
, the action integral of
the binary system had an additional term arising from the massless
strut
Isr
separating the constituents. The Euclidean action
of a pair of identical (up to a sign of charges)
Reissner-Nordström nonextreme black holes was calculated by
Emparan and Teo
ETe
, while the thermodynamical properties of
equal Kerr black holes, with a brief analysis of the action
integrals, were studied numerically by Herdeiro
et al.

HRR
. In our recent paper
GMR
we have computed the
Euclidean action for two different binary systems of equal
counterrotating Kerr-Newman (KN) black holes and obtained the
corresponding expressions for the free energy of each system. Note
that in the above papers
CPe
;
ETe
;
HRR
;
GMR
all the binary
systems were in thermal equilibrium because the black holes in them
had the same surface gravities and hence the same Hawking
temperature
T_{H}
.

In the paper
GMR
we have observed that it is quite simple to
guess the explicit expression of the free energy, which is closely
related to the Euclidean action, in the general case of the
double-Schwarzschild solution
BWe
from the respective formula
obtained in the special case of equal Schwarzschild black holes.
This naturally raises the question of whether the Euclidean action
method can be extended to the systems of arbitrary black holes with

nonequal
surface gravities, similar to thermodynamics which
goes far beyond the black hole configurations in thermal equilibrium

CGLP
. The reason of why such an extension of the method has
not yet been attempted up to now is in fact known and looks quite
meaningful at first sight – a Wick rotation of the multi-black hole
spacetime introduces conical singularities at the horizons due to
the periodic Euclidean time
\tau
, and whereas one conical
singularity, say, on the first horizon can be eliminated by choosing
appropriately the period of
\tau
, the other horizons will remain
singular unless they have the same surface gravities as the first
horizon. However, as will be shown in the present paper, the way out
of this seemingly unresolvable situation consists in calculating the
entire Euclidean action, including the terms arising from all the
singular horizons; then the condition of vanishing the combined
contribution of the latter ‘singular horizon’ terms will fix the
value of the period of
\tau
, the inverse of which will give the

Hawking average temperature
(HAT) of the system. It is
remarkable that the temperature introduced in this way turns out to
be determined by a very concise formula involving exclusively the
horizons’ areas and surface gravities; moreover, the corresponding

free energy
of the system becomes consistent with the first
law of thermodynamics and the well-known Smarr relations
Sma
.

This paper is organized as follows. In the next section we will
reexamine the derivation of the Euclidean action in the case of a
single KN black hole
NCC
, evaluating explicitly the
contribution of the conical singularity due to Euclidean time. In
Sec. III we calculate the Euclidean action for a system of
N

collinear arbitrary KN black holes described by the extended

N
-soliton electrovac solution
RMM
. Here two different types
of the conical singularity contributions will appear - the one
coming from the singular horizons and the other arising from the
struts. The Hawking average temperature
\hat{T}
is introduced in
Sec. IV where we find its form in terms of the surface gravities and
horizons’ areas of black holes; we also give the expression of the
free energy
W
defined by
\hat{T}
and show its consistency with
both the first law of thermodynamics and Smarr’s mass formula.
Sec. V contains concluding remarks.

Throughout the paper, units are used in which
c=G=\hbar=k_{B}=1
.

II
Euclidean action of a single Kerr-Newman black hole

The Euclidean action we are interested in can be represented in the
form

I_{\rm E}=I_{\rm EH}+I_{\rm GH}+I_{\rm em},

The

In the presence of a conical singularity, the action integral

As for the Gibbons-Hawking boundary term

(59)

where
h=-e^{2\gamma}\omega^{2}
, as it follows from (
53
);
then

A^{H}_{i}=\int_{\alpha_{2i}}^{\alpha_{2i-1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{-e^{2\gamma_{i}}\omega_{i}^{2}}\ dz\,d\varphi=4\pi\sigma_{i}\sqrt{-e^{2\gamma_{i}}\omega_{i}^{2}}.

(60)

Here
\gamma_{i}
and
\omega_{i}
are the constant values of the metric
functions
\gamma
and
\omega
on the
i
-th horizon, and

\sigma_{i}=(\alpha_{2i-1}-\alpha_{2i})/2
is the half length of the
i
-th
horizon. Formula (
60
) can also be rewritten in the form

A^{H}_{i}=4\pi\sigma_{i}\kappa_{i}^{-1},

(61)

where
\kappa_{i}=(-e^{2\gamma_{i}}\omega_{i}^{2})^{-1/2}
is the surface
gravity of the
i
-th black hole horizon
Car
;
Tom
.

For the corresponding deficit angle
\delta^{\tau}_{i}
related to the

i
-th horizon and associated with the periodic time
\tau
we have

\delta^{\tau}_{i}=2\pi-\int^{\beta}_{0}\kappa_{i}d\tau=2\pi\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2\pi}\beta\right),

(62)

and therefore the expression of the action term
I_{\rm EH}
takes
the form

\displaystyle I_{\rm EH}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}A^{S}_{i}\delta^{\varphi}_{i}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\delta^{\tau}_{i}

(63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}-\frac{1}{4}\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2\pi}\beta\right).

III.2
The Gibbons-Hawking term

In order to calculate the Gibbons-Hawking term (
3
) of the
Euclidean action for our multi-black hole system, it is convenient
to make use of spherical coordinates
(\zeta,\theta)
related to the
Weyl-Papapetrou cylindrical coordinates
(\rho,z)
by the formulas

\zeta=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}},\quad\cos\theta=z/\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}.

(64)

Then, by analogy with the case of a single KN black hole, we can
consider a hypersurface
\mathcal{N}
defined by
\zeta={\rm const}
. Clearly, as
\zeta\to\infty
, the asymptotic behavior of
the functions
r_{n}
of the solution (
III
) is
r_{n}\to\zeta
.

Since the spacetime under consideration is asymptotically flat, then
the component
g_{\tau\varphi}
of the metric tensor is of the order

O(\zeta^{-1})
, and the induced metric over
\mathcal{N}
can be
written in the form

d\sigma^{2}=f_{\mathcal{N}}d\tau^{2}+f^{-1}_{\mathcal{N}}\zeta^{2}\left(e^{2\gamma_{\mathcal{N}}}d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta}d\varphi^{2}\right)+O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right)d\tau d\varphi,

(65)

with the determinant

h=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{\mathcal{N}}&0&0\\
0&f^{-1}_{\mathcal{N}}\zeta^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}&O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right)\\
0&O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right)&f^{-1}_{\mathcal{N}}\zeta^{2}e^{2\gamma_{\mathcal{N}}}\end{array}\right|=f^{-1}_{\mathcal{N}}e^{2\gamma_{\mathcal{N}}}\zeta^{4}\sin^{2}{\theta}+O\left(\zeta^{-2}\right),

(66)

where the subscript
\mathcal{N}
indicates that the functions are
restricted to
\mathcal{N}(\zeta)
.

We note that
I_{\rm GH}
is defined at spatial infinity, so we are
free to use a large-
\zeta
approximation for our purposes, within
which we can neglect the terms of order
O(\zeta^{-2})
. In
particular, we will drop for that reason the last term in
(
66
) involving imaginary quantities. Computing now the volume
of
\mathcal{N}
for large
\zeta
, we obtain

{\rm Vol}\,\mathcal{N}=\int_{\mathcal{N}}\sqrt{h}\,d\tau d\varphi d\theta\simeq 4\pi\beta\zeta^{2}f_{\mathcal{N}}^{-1/2}e^{\gamma_{\mathcal{N}}},

(67)

where it has been supposed that
f_{\mathcal{N}}
as well as

\gamma_{\mathcal{N}}
are constant on
\mathcal{N}(\zeta)
.

The asymptotic behavior of the function
f_{\mathcal{N}}
of the
solution (
III
) is well known, and it is determined by the
expression

f_{\mathcal{N}}\simeq 1-\frac{2M}{\zeta},\quad M=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Re}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{N}e_{l}\right),

(68)

where
M
is the total mass of the system. In the particular case of
the black hole spacetime, the struts give zero contribution to the
total mass, the latter then representing a sum of individual Komar
masses
Kom
of the black hole constituents

M=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}M_{i}.

(69)

Of course,
M
here can also be viewed as the ADM mass of the
multi-black hole system
ADM
.

Now, taking into account (
68
), we get

\displaystyle g_{\zeta\zeta}^{-1/2}\partial_{\zeta}{\rm Vol}\,\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 4\pi\beta\left(1+\frac{2M}{\zeta}\right)^{-1/2}\partial_{\zeta}\left[\left(1+\frac{2M}{\zeta}\right)^{1/2}\zeta^{2}\right]

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\beta\left(2\zeta-M\right)+O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right),

whence it follows that

\int_{\mathcal{N}}K\sqrt{h}=g_{\zeta\zeta}^{-1/2}\partial_{\zeta}{\rm Vol}\,\mathcal{N}=4\pi\beta\left(2\zeta-M\right)+O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right).

(71)

In addition,

\int_{\mathcal{N}}K_{0}\sqrt{h}=4\pi\beta(2\zeta)+O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right),

(72)

and hence

\int_{\mathcal{N}}\left[K\right]\sqrt{h}=-4\pi\beta M+O\left(\zeta^{-1}\right).

(73)

Therefore, after taking the limit
\zeta\to\infty
in (
73
),
we finally arrive at

\displaystyle I_{\rm GH}=\frac{1}{2}\beta M.

(74)

Of course, this result is an expected one, and in the case of
asymptotically flat spacetimes it may even look trivial because all
it says is that the total mass of the system is a combined
contribution of all the constituents of the system. However, the
calculation of the total mass can become a nontrivial exercise if a
spacetime is not asymptotically flat globally
GLS
.

III.3
The electromagnetic term

Like in the case of a single KN black hole, the electromagnetic
potential
A
of the generic configuration of
N
black hole
constituents has two nonzero components and is defined by the
formula

A=A_{\tau}d\tau+A_{\varphi}d\varphi,

(75)

where the explicit form of
A_{\tau}
and
A_{\varphi}
is given in
the paper
RMM
.

To compute the electromagnetic term
I_{\rm em}
of the Euclidean
action, it is advantageous first to rewrite (
4
) as a
boundary integral

I_{\rm em}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}A\wedge\star dA,

(76)

also noting that since the field
A
vanishes at infinity we have

\partial M=\sum_{i}H_{i}\times S^{1}
,
H_{i}
denoting as usual the
horizon of
i
-th KN black hole.

To perform the calculation, we introduce a family of
N

hypersurfaces
N_{i}=C_{i}\times S^{1}
, where
C_{i}
is a cylinder with
radius
\rho={\rm const}
and height
\alpha_{2i-1}-\alpha_{2i}
. In this
way we will have the behavior
N_{i}\to H_{i}\times S^{1}
as
\rho\to 0
.
Since the integrals over the bases of the cylinders do not
contribute in the final result, the computation for each
N_{i}

yields

A\wedge\star dA|_{N_{i}}=\rho^{-1}\left[A_{\tau}(g_{\varphi\tau}\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi}-g_{\varphi\varphi}\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau})+A_{\varphi}(g_{\tau\varphi}\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau}-g_{\tau\tau}\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi})\right]d\tau\wedge dz\wedge d\varphi|_{N_{i}}.

(77)

Following Carter
Car
, we now define

\lambda_{i}:=-i\partial_{\tau}+\Omega^{H}_{i}\partial_{\varphi}
and

\Omega^{H}_{i}:=-ig_{\tau\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}|_{H_{i}}
. Then
we can rewrite the first
A_{\tau}
in (
77
) as

A_{\tau}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}A_{\varphi}+i\Omega^{H}_{i}A_{\varphi}
and rearrange
the terms, thus obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{N_{i}}A\wedge\star dA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{N_{i}-S^{1}}\rho^{-1}(A_{\tau}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}A_{\varphi})\left(g_{\varphi\tau}\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi}-g_{\varphi\varphi}\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau}\right)dzd\varphi

\displaystyle+2\pi\beta\int^{\alpha_{2i-1}}_{\alpha_{2i}}\rho^{-1}A_{\varphi}\left[(g_{\tau\varphi}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\varphi})\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau}-(g_{\tau\tau}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\tau})\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi}\right]dz.

Observing further that

g_{\tau\varphi}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\varphi}\approx 0,\quad g_{\tau\tau}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\tau}\approx\rho^{2}g_{\varphi\varphi}^{-1}

(79)

in the vicinity of the
i
-th horizon, and also that
\rho\to 0
as

N_{i}\to H_{i}\times S^{1}
, with which the second integral on the
right-hand side of (
III.3
) vanishes on the horizon, we get

\displaystyle\int_{H_{i}\times S^{1}}A\wedge\star dA

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int_{N_{i}}A\wedge\star dA

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta i\Phi_{i}\int_{H_{i}}\star F+2\pi\beta\lim_{\rho\to 0}\int^{\alpha_{2i-1}}_{\alpha_{2i}}\rho^{-1}A_{\varphi}[(g_{\tau\varphi}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\varphi})\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau}

\displaystyle-(g_{\tau\tau}-i\Omega^{H}_{i}g_{\varphi\tau})\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi}]dz,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\Phi_{i}\left(i\int_{H_{i}}\star F\right),

where we have exploited the fact that the potential

\Phi_{i}:=A(\lambda_{i})|_{H_{i}}=iA_{\tau}+\Omega^{H}_{i}A_{\varphi}|_{H_{i}}

(81)

takes constant value on the
i
-th horizon
Car
, and also made
use of the relation

\star F|_{H_{i}}=\star dA|_{H_{i}}=-\rho^{-1}\left(g_{\varphi\tau}\partial_{\rho}A_{\varphi}-g_{\varphi\varphi}\partial_{\rho}A_{\tau}\right)dz\wedge d\varphi|_{H_{i}}.

(82)

Last, using formula
Car

i\int_{H_{i}}\star F=-4\pi Q_{i},

(83)

where
Q_{i}
is the electric charge of the
i
-th KN black hole, we
arrive at the desired result for the electromagnetic action term:

I_{\rm em}=-\frac{1}{2}\beta\sum^{N}_{i=1}Q_{i}\Phi_{i}.

(84)

Putting now the expressions obtained for
I_{\rm EH}
,
I_{\rm GH}

and
I_{\rm em}
together, we can write down the final formula for
the Euclidean action
I_{\rm E}
of
N
arbitrary KN black holes:

I_{\rm E}=\frac{\beta}{2}\left(M-\sum^{N}_{i=1}Q_{i}\Phi_{i}\right)+\beta\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}-\frac{1}{4}\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2\pi}\beta\right).

(85)

It is clear that the last term in (
85
) involving
A^{H}_{i}
and

\kappa_{i}
is likely to be removed from the action for the
regularity reason.

IV
Hawking average temperature and free energy of
N
KN black holes

We have seen that in the case of a single KN black hole the conical
singularity formed on the horizon by the periodic time can be
eliminated by an appropriate choice of
\beta
, which is equivalent
to introducing the Hawking temperature of a black hole. However, in
the configurations consisting of various black holes which have
different surface gravities, the conical singularity can be removed
(by fixing
\beta
) only on one horizon, as it follows from the
structure of the last term in (
85
), whereas the horizons of
the other black holes will remain singular. This in particular
explains why the Euclidean action method has been applied so far
exclusively to the binary systems of equal black holes.

At the same time, the advantage of evaluating explicitly the
singular terms in the Euclidean action is quite clear – this opens
a good opportunity to analyze these terms in detail, and helps one
understand better the whole problem with the conical singularities
due to Euclidean time and possible approaches to tackle it. Thus, an
immediate proposal for regularizing the Euclidean action would be
demanding that the entire combined contribution of the singular
horizon terms in (
85
) must vanish. As can be easily seen,
this fixes
uniquely
the choice of the parameter
\beta
in the
action; indeed,

\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2\pi}\beta\right)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\beta\equiv\hat{\beta}=\left(\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\right)\left(\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{\kappa_{i}}{2\pi}A^{H}_{i}\right)^{-1},

(86)

whence we get the expression for the temperature that generalizes
the notion of the Hawking temperature to the case of multiple black
holes:

T\equiv\hat{T}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\sum^{N}_{i=1}\kappa_{i}A^{H}_{i}\right)\left(\sum^{N}_{i=1}A^{H}_{i}\right)^{-1}.

(87)

From now on we will call
\hat{T}
the
Hawking average
temperature
(HAT) of a multi-black hole system.

Note that in the particular cases of one black hole
(
\kappa_{1}=\kappa
,
\kappa_{i}=0
,
i=2,...,N
), or a system of black
holes possessing the same surface gravity (
\kappa_{i}=\kappa
,

i=1,...,N
), one recovers from (
87
) the well-known
conventional formula for the Hawking temperature:

T_{\rm H}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}.

(88)

Therefore, for the regularized Euclidean action in the presence of
massless struts we obtain the formula

I_{\rm E}=\frac{\hat{\beta}}{2}\left(M-\sum^{N}_{i=1}Q_{i}\Phi_{i}\right)+\hat{\beta}\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i},

(89)

which in turn supplies us, via (
5
), with the expression for
the
free energy
of the system:

W=\frac{1}{2}\left(M-\sum^{N}_{i=1}Q_{i}\Phi_{i}\right)+\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i}.

(90)

We would like to emphasize that the introduction of HAT
\hat{T}

does not actually deny anyhow the existence of individual Hawking
temperatures
T_{i}
of the black hole constituents. Precisely the
possibility of removing the conical singularity on any particular
horizon by means of the choice
\beta_{i}=1/T_{i}=2\pi/\kappa_{i}
shows,
on the one hand, that the individual black hole temperatures do
exist and are of Hawking’s type and, on the other hand, that they
may not coincide with the average temperature
\hat{T}
of the
system. In this respect it should be pointed out that we assume as
usual that each black hole satisfies the Smarr mass relation

Sma
, and the sum of such relations can be written as

M=\sum^{N}_{i=1}M_{i}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left(2T_{i}S_{i}+2\Omega^{H}_{i}J_{i}+\Phi_{i}Q_{i}\right),

(91)

where
J_{i}
is the angular momentum and
S_{i}
the entropy of the

i
-th black hole, while
\Omega^{H}_{i}
introduced in the previous
section represents the angular velocity of the
i
-th horizon and is
equal to the inverse of the metric function
\omega
evaluated on
that horizon. In fact, it is not difficult to find a representation
of
\hat{T}
in terms of the individual Hawking temperatures
T_{i}
.
Indeed, performing in (
87
) the substitutions

\kappa_{i}=T_{i}/2\pi
,
A^{H}_{i}=4S_{i}
, and introducing the total entropy

S
of the system by the formula

S:=\sum^{N}_{i=1}S_{i}=\sum^{N}_{i=1}\frac{A^{H}_{i}}{4},

(92)

we get for
\hat{T}
the following concise expression:

\hat{T}=\frac{1}{S}\sum^{N}_{i=1}T_{i}S_{i},

(93)

with which for instance the Smarr relation (
91
) rewrites as

M=2\hat{T}S+\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left(2\Omega^{H}_{i}J_{i}+\Phi_{i}Q_{i}\right).

(94)

A useful corollary of (
94
) is yet another form of the free
energy, namely,

W=M-\hat{T}S-\sum^{N}_{i=1}\left(\Omega^{H}_{i}J_{i}+\Phi_{i}Q_{i}\right)+\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}\mathcal{F}_{i},

(95)

which generalizes the respective formula for
W
known for the case
of two equal black hole constituents
CPe
;
GMR
.

As was shown in
GMR
, the variation of
W
is given by the
formula

dW=-Sd\hat{T}-\sum^{N}_{i=1}(J_{i}d\Omega^{H}_{i}+Q_{i}d\Phi_{i})+\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}\mathcal{F}_{i}dl_{i},

(96)

so by taking the differential of (
95
) and making use of
(
96
) we obtain the first law of thermodynamics for the system
of
N
KN black holes:

dM=\hat{T}dS+\sum^{N}_{i=1}(\Omega^{H}_{i}dJ_{i}+\Phi_{i}dQ_{i})-\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}l_{i}d\mathcal{F}_{i}.

(97)

Returning now to the temperature
\hat{T}
, let us consider its
representation involving the quantities
\sigma_{i}
that are defined by
the parameters
\alpha_{n}
of the
N
-soliton solution and represent the
half lengths of the respective horizons. As it follows from the
formula (
61
), we have
\kappa_{i}A^{H}_{i}=4\pi\sigma_{i}
, so that,
after changing the sum of the horizon areas to the sum of the
entropies, we can rewrite (
87
) in the form

\hat{T}=\frac{1}{2S}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\sigma_{i}.

(98)

The above representation of
\hat{T}
is of interest for two reasons.
First, it demonstrates in a simple way that in the limiting case of

N
extreme KN black holes (when all
\sigma_{i}=0
) the HAT
\hat{T}

takes zero value. Second, formula (
98
) simplifies even
further for a static configuration of
N
Schwarzschild black holes

IKh
, the thermodynamics of which has recently been studied in
the papers
GLS
;
LPo1
;
LPo2
, because in this case the quantities

\sigma_{i}
are equal to the masses of black holes,
\sigma_{i}=M_{i}
, and, as a
consequence, (
98
) takes a remarkably simple form

\hat{T}=\frac{M}{2S},

(99)

where
M
is the total mass of the Schwarzschild black holes.

In the simplest case of two nonequal Schwarzschild black holes

BWe
, the explicit form of
\hat{T}
can be worked out with the
aid of the formulas of paper
MRS
, yielding

\hat{T}=\frac{M[R^{2}-(M_{1}-M_{2})^{2}]}{8\pi(R+M)[R(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2})-M(M_{1}-M_{2})^{2}]},\quad M=M_{1}+M_{2},

(100)

where
R
is the coordinate distance between the centers of black
holes. If
M_{1}>M_{2}
, then
\hat{T}
turns out to be greater than the
Hawking temperature of the black hole with larger mass
M_{1}
and
less than the temperature of the black hole with smaller mass
M_{2}
:

\frac{R+M_{1}-M_{2}}{8\pi M_{1}(R+M)}<\hat{T}<\frac{R-M_{1}+M_{2}}{8\pi M_{2}(R+M)},

(101)

which lends support to our interpretation of
\hat{T}
as an average
temperature of the system. Note that
\hat{T}
is an increasing
function of
R
, taking its maximum value,

\hat{T}_{\rm max}=\frac{M}{8\pi(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2})},

(102)

in the limit
R\to\infty
, and its minimum value
T_{\rm min}=(8\pi M)^{-1}
at
R=M
, when the two black holes merge and form one
Schwarzschild black hole with mass
M
. The gravitational
interaction of black holes therefore decreases the temperature of
the system!

V
Concluding remarks

Therefore, we have computed the Euclidean action describing the
system of
N
arbitrary KN black holes and shown how the problem of
singular horizon terms due to periodic time can be obviated by
introducing the notion of average temperature of a multi-black hole
configuration. In this way we have extended the applicability of the
path-integral method to the systems of nonequal black holes with
different surface gravities, which in particular permitted us to
obtain the expression for the free energy of the system in the
generic case and find a natural generalization of the Hawking
temperature to the case of multiple black holes in the form of HAT

\hat{T}
determined concisely by the surface gravities and horizon
areas of black holes. It is worth noting in this respect that the
Euclidean action method seems to be ideally suited for treating the
black hole systems as it permits a far-reaching analysis just on the
basis of the well-known general properties and characteristics of
black holes, without the need to use the explicit form of the
multi-black hole solution.

While the average temperature
\hat{T}
introduced in the present
paper offers a possibility to approximate a system of black holes by
treating it as one body with mass
M
(the total mass of the system)
and temperature
\hat{T}
, one may also ask a question whether the
system could have some other averaged physical characteristics
describing it as an integral unit. Interestingly, looking at the
Smarr relation in the form (
94
), it is tempting to speculate
that the second and third terms on the right-hand side of
(
94
) could be rewritten similar to the first term involving
the total entropy. Indeed, by introducing the total angular momentum

J
and total charge
Q
of the system by the usual formulas

J=\sum^{N}_{i=1}J_{i},\quad Q=\sum^{N}_{i=1}Q_{i},

(103)

we can define, in analogy with the HAT
\hat{T}
, the average angular
velocity
\hat{\Omega}
and average electric potential
\hat{\Phi}
as

\hat{\Omega}=\frac{1}{J}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\Omega^{H}_{i}J_{i},\quad\hat{\Phi}=\frac{1}{Q}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\Phi_{i}Q_{i},

(104)

with which the Smarr relation (
94
) takes the form

M=2\hat{T}S+2\hat{\Omega}J+\hat{\Phi}Q.

(105)

The only reason why this formula has not received attention in the
literature so far is of course the necessity to justify
\hat{T}
as
the average temperature of the system, something that slightly
surpasses the limits of the classical black hole thermodynamics. We
hope that our paper providing such a justification for
\hat{T}
by
means of the Euclidean action approach makes formula (
105
)
fully plausible now.

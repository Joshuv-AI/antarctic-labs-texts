Mean field control and finite agent approximation for regime-switching jump diffusions Thanks: E. Bayraktar is supported in part by the National Science Foundation under grant DMS-2106556 and in part by the Susan M. Smith Professorship.
Erhan Bayraktar, Alekos Cecchin, Prakash Chakraborty

Abstract
. We consider a jump-diffusion mean field control problem with regime switching in the state dynamics. The corresponding value function is characterized as the unique viscosity solution of a HJB master equation on the space of probability measures. Using this characterization, we prove that the value function, which is not regular, is the limit of a finite agent centralized optimal control problem as the number of agents go to infinity, with an explicit convergence rate. Assuming in addition that the value function is smooth, we establish a quantitative propagation of chaos result for the optimal trajectory of agent states.

1.
Introduction

Mean field control problems with regime switching state dynamics is a growing activity of research starting from the works of
[
23
,
26
,
27
,
29
]
and most was recently considered by
[
18
]
. This line of research activity is motivated from the need to control large identical interacting control systems where the interaction is mean-field and there exists a common noise affecting each system. Though Brownian common noise has been studied at length in the literature, a simpler example is a Markov chain representing the regime switches experienced by each system in the network. From a modeling perspective too such hybrid models have been shown to be extremely powerful
[
13
,
14
,
28
,
30
]
. In contrast to a common Markov switching, it is indeed possible to have a network where each system has its own switching mechanism. Such a model has been analyzed in
[
26
,
29
]
. However since there is already a mean field interaction between the systems it is sensible to have just one common random switching mechanism to incorporate the hybrid nature of the network.

In this work we model each individual system identically using a controlled jump-diffusion, where the jumps are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and the drift, diffusion and jump intensity coefficients are modulated by the same Markov chain. The mean-field limit of this network is expected to be characterized by the distribution, conditioned on the history of the switching process, of the solution of a McKean Vlasov jump diffusion with regime switching coefficients. This mean field limit in an uncontrolled setup and without jumps has been analyzed in
[
22
]
. In some papers including
[
21
,
23
,
29
]
the mean field interaction has been represented by the conditional expectation of the solution of the McKean Vlasov jump diffusion. In
[
22
,
29
]
, the stochastic maximum principle has been studied and obtained. In contrast, we use Bellman’s dynamic programming principle approach to obtain a master equation as in
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
. This equation can be studied by Lions’ lifting of measure-dependent functions to appropriate functions on the space of random variables. Traditionally the Wasserstein space of measures and the
\mathcal{L}^{2}
space of random variables are used for this purpose. If now the solution is regular enough a classical interpretation of the master equation is used. Else, one relies on appropriate viscosity solution theory. Because of the jump terms present in our model, working with Lion’s derivative is not enough to state the master equation in our case, and one needs to rely on the linear functional derivative alone. This has been mentioned and explored in
[
4
]
while analyzing a controlled McKean Vlasov jump diffusion without regime switches. In addition
[
4
]
provides a theory of viscosity solutions on the space of probability measures that does not rely on lifting to a Hilbert space. It is this notion of viscosity solution that we will use, modified of course to account for the regime switches, in addition to the metric (equivalent to weak topology) on the space of probability measures defined therein.

The mean field control problem we analyze in the paper is the same as in
[
4
]
, and is slightly different from the usual one. Namely, in the finite-agent centralized optimization, the strategy of any agent is usually expected to depend on the private state and on the empirical measure of the system, while here it is independent of the private state. In other words, each agent uses as control the same stochastic process which is given by the central planner, and we believe that there are several situations which can be modeled in this way. As a consequence, the controls employed in the limiting mean field control problem are just deterministic functions of time.

In order to prove convergence of the finite state optimization to the mean field optimization, in the usual setup, there are several approaches. Let us mention
[
12
,
11
,
19
]
where the problem is tackled probabilistically through compactness arguments. In addition convergence with rates can be obtained through FBSDE techniques, see
[
8
]
and
[
20
]
, the latter with interaction also through the law of the control; but these papers assume convexity in the measure argument. Another recent paper
[
17
]
establishes a convergence rate, assuming that the limit value function is smooth (which should hold e.g. under convexity).

In this present work, we prove convergence of the value functions with a convergence rate without assuming convexity, nor that the value function is smooth. Instead, we make use of the viscosity solution characterization of the optimal value function, which is in general not differentiable.
This is most related to the recent papers
[
10
]
and
[
16
]
. In
[
16
]
, the viscosity solutions of HJB equations in finite-agent deterministic or stochastic optimal control problems are shown to converge to that of a limiting HJB equation in the space of probability measures. The latter equation is interpreted through Lions’ lifting in the
\mathcal{L}^{2}
sense. However convergence rates are absent in
[
16
]
. But since we rely on our particular viscosity solution structure, we can adopt the ideas in
[
10
]
even though the problem addressed there is in the space of probability measures with finite support. We employ a doubling of variables argument, using the distance-like function introduced in
[
4
]
.

A recent preprint
[
5
]
provides a rate for the convergence of the value function, under general assumptions. As explained above, the mean field control problem we consider here is slightly different and thus the proof of convergence is completely different. As a consequence, we obtain a better rate than in
[
5
]
(see Remark
4.2
). We mention, however, that we impose a structural assumption on the coefficients, in order to apply the theory of
[
4
]
.

We also provide, for completeness, a quantitative propagation of chaos for the convergence of the optimal trajectories, but, to obtain this result, we suppose that the limit value function is smooth, as in
[
17
]
. We mention, in this respect, a recent preprint
[
6
]
where the authors establish a quantitative propagation of chaos, without assuming regularity of the limit value function: they show that the value function is
C^{1}
in an open and dense subset of the space of probability measures and thus prove convergence if the initial distribution belongs to that set.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we mention the mean field control problem as motivation to introduce associated assumptions, the state space under consideration, along with the particular metric on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
borrowed from
[
4
]
used in our paper. These notions will be used throughout the rest of this article. In Section
3
, we study the mean field control problem in detail. In particular, we obtain an HJB equation, define a viscosity solution theory suitable for our purposes and prove that the value function is the unique viscosity solution to that HJB. Next in Section
4
, we introduce the finite agent centralized control problem and prove the main convergence results: we show using viscosity solutions that the value function in the finite agent control problem is uniformly approximated by that in the mean field control problem. Finally, in Section
5
we prove under additional assumptions, a propagation of chaos result showing that the optimal trajectories are uniformly close as well.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The control problem

Consider a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},({\mathcal{F}}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
on which is defined a Brownian motion
(W_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
, where
T>0
is an arbitrary fixed time horizon. Let
\alpha
be a continuous time Markov chain with finite state space
\mathcal{S}=\{1,\ldots,s_{0}\}
and generator
Q=(q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq s_{0}}
. Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
denote the class of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
. For
(t,\rho)\in[0,T]\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
we consider the following controlled McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation with initial condition
\mathcal{L}(X_{t}|\mathcal{F}_{t-}^{\alpha})=\rho
and
\alpha_{t}=i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
:

(2.1)

dX_{s}=b(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})ds+\sigma(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})dB_{s}+dJ_{s},\qquad t\leq s\leq T,

where
\mu_{s}=\mathcal{L}(X_{s}|\mathcal{F}_{s-}^{\alpha})
,
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}
is the filtration generated by the Markov chain
\alpha
,
J_{s}
is a purely discontinuous process with controlled intensity
\lambda(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})
and the jump sizes are i.i.d. from some distribution
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore,
v_{s}:=v(s,\mu_{s},\alpha_{s-})
is a deterministic feedback control of time
s
, conditional law
\mu_{s}
and regime state
\alpha_{s-}
taking values in a prescribed Polish space
A
. Denote
\mathcal{A}
to be this class of admissible controls. The solution to (
2.1
) depends on
t
,
\rho
,
i_{0}
and
v
. However for ease in presentation we will sometimes omit these and the solution will be denoted just by
X^{v}
or just
X
, when the dependence on the initial conditions and the control is clear from the context.

We then consider the value function

(2.2)

V(t,\rho,i_{0}):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})ds+h(T,X_{T},\mu_{T},\alpha_{T})\right],

for given functions
f
and
h
, and where
v_{s}\equiv v(s,)

Assumption 2.1
.

The space of probability measures is endowed with the weak* topology
\sigma(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}))
where
\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of continuous and bounded functions in
\mathbb{R}
. The weak* convergence
\mu_{n}\to\mu
is equivalent to:
\langle\mu_{n},f\rangle
converges to
\langle\mu,f\rangle
for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
. Whenever required we can use any of the metrics
d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
which permits it to be topologically equivalent to the weak* topology. In particular, in the sequel we will use the metric
d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})=\sum_{j}c_{j}|\langle\mu-\nu,f_{j}\rangle|
, when restricted to a suitable compact set
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\{f_{j}\}_{j}
is a class of polynomials containing all monomials (see later for more details). If needed,
\mathcal{S}
is endowed with the metric
d_{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying
d_{\mathcal{S}}(i_{0},j_{0})=\mathbf{1}_{\{i_{o}\neq j_{0}\}}
.

Assumption 2.2
.

There exist constants
C_{0}

(i)

For any
s\in[0,T]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
,
v\in A
,
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,

|b(s,\mu,v,i_{0})|+|\sigma(s,\mu,v,i_{0})|+|\lambda(s,\mu,v,i_{0})|\leq C_{0}

(ii)

There exists a finite set
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
,
t,s\in[0,T]
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}

|b(t,\mu,v,i_{0})-b(s,\mu^{\prime},v,i_{0})|+|\sigma(t,\mu,v,i_{0})-\sigma(s,\mu^{\prime},v,i_{0})|\\
+|\lambda(t,\mu,v,i_{0})-\lambda(s,\mu^{\prime},v,i_{0})|\leq\kappa_{0}\left(|t-s|+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\lvert\left\langle\mu-\mu^{\prime},x^{k}\right\rangle\rvert\right).

(iii)

\gamma
has
\delta
-exponential moment:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(\delta|x|)\gamma(dx)<\infty.

(iv)

f
and
h
are bounded. Furthermore there exists a finite set
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
,
t,s\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
:

\left|f(t,x,\mu,v,i_{0})-f(s,y,\mu^{\prime},v,i_{0})\right|+\left|h(t,x,\mu,i_{0})-h(s,y,\mu^{\prime},i_{0})\right|\\
\leq\kappa_{1}\left(|t-s|+|x-y|+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\left|\left\langle\mu-\mu^{\prime},x^{k}\right\rangle\right|\right).

(v)

All the functions
b,\sigma,\lambda,f,h
are Lipschitz-continuous in the measure argument for the 2-Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We should note that although the 2-Wasserstein distance and the distance
d
are topologically equivalent, they are not strongly equivalent. We need both of (ii) and (v) above for our main result, Theorem
4.1
. See e.g. Propositions
3.4
and
4.1
.

Definition 2.1

Remark 2.3

Consider the linear function

3.
Mean field control problem

3.1.
Dynamic Programming

Lemma 3.1
.

The following dynamic programming principle holds:

(3.1)

V(t,\rho,i_{0})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{\theta}f(s,X_{s},\mu_{s},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})ds+V(\theta,\mu_{\theta},\alpha_{\theta})\right],\quad\forall\theta\in[t,T],

Proof.

Since the McKean-Vlasov control problem considered here is deterministic, the dynamic programming principle follows from classical results. See, for instance
[
15
]
. ∎

Notation 3.1
.

For a given class of functions
{u}=(u(t,\mu,i_{0},x))_{i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}
, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\mu,i_{0},v}
acts on the
x
and
i_{0}
variables and is given by

(3.2)

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\mu,i_{0},v}[{u}](x):=b(t,x,\mu,v,i_{0})\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}(t,\mu,i_{0},x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(t,x,\mu,v,i_{0})\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}(t,\mu,i_{0},x)\\
+\lambda(t,x,\mu,i_{0},v)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u(t,\mu,i_{0},x+y)-u(t,\mu,i_{0},x)\right)\gamma(dy)

From (
3.1
) one obtains the following dynamic programming equation:

(3.3)

-\partial_{t}V(t,\mu,i_{0})+\sup_{v\in A}\mathcal{H}^{v}(t,\mu,i_{0},{D_{m}V})-\sum_{j_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{i_{0},j_{0}}\left(V(t,\mu,j_{0})-V(t,\mu,i_{0})\right)=0,

where

(3.4)

\mathcal{H}^{v}(t,\mu,i_{0},{D_{m}V})=-\left\langle\mu,f(t,\cdot,\mu,v,i_{0})+\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\mu,i_{0},v}[{D_{m}V}]\right\rangle.

3.2.
Viscosity solutions and test functions

The viscosity sub- and super-solutions are defined similar to

]

Definition 3.1
.

A cylindrical function is a map of the form
(t,\mu,i_{0})\mapsto F(t,\langle\mu,f\rangle,i_{0})
for some function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
F:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. This function is called cylindrical polynomial if
f
is a polynomial, and
F(\cdot,\cdot,i_{0})
is continuously differentiable for all
i_{0}
.

Definition 3.2
.

For
E\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, a viscosity test function on
E\times\mathcal{S}
is a function of the form

\varphi(t,\mu,i_{0})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(t,\mu,i_{0}),\quad(t,\mu,i_{0})\in E\times\mathcal{S},

where
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j}
is a sequence of cylindrical polynomials satisfying:

(i)

\{\phi_{j}^{i_{0}}:=\phi_{j}(\cdot,\cdot,i_{0})\}_{j}
are absolutely convergent at every
(t,\mu)
for every
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,

(ii)

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
,

\lim_{M\to\infty}\sum_{j=M}^{\infty}\sup_{(t,\mu)\in E}\sum_{k=0}^{\textnormal{deg}(D_{m}\varphi_{j}^{i_{0}})}\left|\left\langle\mu,(D_{m}\varphi_{j}^{i_{0}})^{(k)}\right\rangle\right|=0.

We let
\Phi_{E\times\mathcal{S}}
be the set of all viscosity test functions on
E\times\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 3.3
.

For
E\subseteq\bar{\mathcal{O}}
and
(t,\mu,i_{0})\in E\times\mathcal{S}
with

, the superjet of
u
at

\left.\bigg|\varphi\in\Phi_{E\times\mathcal{S}},(u-\varphi)(t,\mu,i_{0})=\max_{E}(u-\varphi)(\cdot,\cdot,i_{0})\right\}.

The subjet of
u
at

Definition 3.4
.

For a subspace

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{X}_{r})\sigma^{2}(r,\tilde{\mu}_{r},v_{r},\alpha_{r})-\frac{1}{2}f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(X_{r})\sigma^{2}(r,\mu_{r},v_{r},\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda(r,\tilde{\mu}_{r},v_{r},\alpha_{r})\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f(\tilde{X}_{r}+y)-f(\tilde{X}_{r}))\gamma(dy)-\lambda(r,\mu_{r},v_{r},\alpha_{r})\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f(X_{r}+y)-f(X_{r}))\gamma(dy)\Big\}dr\Big|.

Since

g_{j}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(f_{j}(x+y)-f_{j}(x))\gamma(dy)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{deg}(f_{j})}\frac{f_{j}^{(i)}(x)}{i!}\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{i}\gamma(dy)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{deg}(f_{j})}m_{i}f_{j}^{(i)}(x),

the definition of
c_{j}(b)
(with
b=Me^{K^{*}T}
), and the property
\sum_{j}c_{j}\langle\mu,f_{j}\rangle^{2}\leq 1
, as
\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{M}
, and the boundedness of
b
,
\sigma
and
\lambda
yield

\displaystyle d(\tilde{\mu}_{s},\mu_{s})

\displaystyle\leq d(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+\int_{t}^{s}\Big\{\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[f_{j}^{\prime}(\tilde{X}_{r})-f_{j}^{\prime}(X_{r})]||b|_{\infty}+C\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[f_{j}^{\prime}(X_{r})]|d(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}_{r}),\mathcal{L}(X_{r}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{X}_{r})-f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(X_{r})]||\sigma^{2}|_{\infty}+C\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[f_{j}^{\prime\prime}(X_{r})]|d(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}_{r}),\mathcal{L}(X_{r}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[g_{j}(\tilde{X}_{r})-g_{j}(X_{r})]||\lambda|_{\infty}+C\sum_{j}c_{j}|\mathbb{E}^{\alpha}[g_{j}(X_{r})]|d(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}_{r}),\mathcal{L}(X_{r}))\Big\}dr

\displaystyle\leq d(\mu,\tilde{\mu})+C\int_{t}^{s}d(\mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}_{r}),\mathcal{L}(X_{r}))dr,

Thus Gronwall’s lemma, taking expectation, gives the claim.
∎

4.
Finite agent centralized control problem

4.1.
Problem setup and viscosity solution definition

We assume the following state dynamics interpreted in the weak sense for an
N-
agent system similar to the controlled McKean-Vlasov jump-diffusion (
2.1
)

(4.1)

dX_{s}^{k}=b(s,X_{s}^{k},\mu_{s}^{N},v_{s}^{N},\alpha_{s-})ds+\sigma(s,X_{s}^{k},\mu_{s}^{N},v_{s}^{N},\alpha_{s-})dB_{s}^{k}+dJ_{s}^{k},\quad s>t,\quad k=1,\ldots,N,

with initial condition
{X}_{s}^{k}=x_{k}
and where
\{B^{k}\}_{k}
are independent Brownian motions,
\{J^{k}\}_{k}
are independent purely discontinuous processes with controlled intensity
\lambda(s,X_{s}^{k},\mu_{s}^{N},v_{s},\alpha_{s-})
and the jump sizes are i.i.d. from the distribution
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying Assumption
2.2
(iii). The Markov chain
\alpha
initialized at
\alpha_{t}=i_{0}
is the noise common to all agents, and is the same as in (
2.1
). Admissible controls are of the form
v_{s}^{N}=v(s,\mu_{s}^{N},\alpha_{s-})
taking values in the Polish space
A
. The coefficients
b
,
\sigma
,
\lambda
, and the distribution
\gamma
all satisfy the same Assumption
2.2
. The same is true for the running cost
f
and the terminal cost
h
in the value function

(4.2)

u^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})=\inf_{v^{N}\in\mathcal{A}}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s}^{k},\mu_{s}^{N},v_{s}^{N},\alpha_{s-})ds+h(T,X_{T}^{k},\mu_{T}^{N},\alpha_{T})\right],

where
\mathbf{X}_{t}={\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
The corresponding HJB turns out to be

(4.3)

-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})+\sup_{v}\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}H_{k}^{v}(t,{\bf x},i_{0},Nu^{N},NDu^{N},ND^{2}u^{N})\\
-\sum_{j_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{i_{0}j_{0}}\left[u^{N}(t,{\bf x},j_{0})-u^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\right]=0,\qquad(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\times\mathcal{S},

where

H_{k}^{v}(t,{\bf x},i_{0},u,\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})=\left[-\left[f(t,x_{k},\mu^{N}({\bf x}),v,i_{0})+b(t,x_{k},\mu^{N}({\bf x}),v,i_{0})\gamma_{i}\right.\right.\\
+\left.\left.\dfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}(t,x_{k},\mu^{N}({\bf x}),v,i_{0})\tilde{\gamma}_{ii}+\lambda(t,x_{k},\mu^{N}({\bf x}),v,i_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[u^{N}(t,{\bf x}+e_{k}y,i_{0})-u^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\right]\gamma(dy)\right]\right],

where
\mu^{N}({\bf x})=\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{k}}
.
Denote

(4.4)

H^{v}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}H_{k}^{v}

and
H:=\sup_{v}H^{v}
.

Definition 4.1
.

(i)
A function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity subsolution of (
4.3
) if whenever
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S})
and
(u^{\ast}-\phi)(\cdot,\cdot,i_{0})
has a local maxima at
(t,{\bf x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then

-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(t,{\bf x},i_{0})+H(t,{\bf x},i_{0},N\phi,ND\phi,ND^{2}\phi)+\sum_{j_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{i_{0}j_{0}}\left[u(t,{\bf x},j_{0})-u(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\right]\leq 0.

(ii)
A function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity supersolution of (
4.3
) if whenever
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S})
and
(u_{\ast}-\phi)(\cdot,\cdot,i_{0})
has a local minima at
(t,{\bf x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then

-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi(t,{\bf x},i_{0})+H(t,{\bf x},i_{0},N\phi,ND\phi,ND^{2}\phi)+\sum_{j_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{i_{0}j_{0}}\left[u(t,{\bf x},j_{0})-u(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\right]\geq 0.

(iii)
A function
u
is a viscosity solution of (
4.3
) if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution of (
4.3
).

Notation 4.1
.

We use the following notation to transform
u^{N}
to a function on
\mathcal{O}_{M}\times\mathcal{S}
:

(4.5)

\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0}):=u^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0}),\text{ for }(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}.

In addition the empirical projection of any
\varphi
is given by

\tilde{\varphi}^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})=\varphi(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0}),\text{ for }(t,{\bf x},i_{0})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}.

Let also

\mathcal{O}^{N}=\left\{(t,\mu)\in\mathcal{O}:\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}\text{ for some }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}

be the set of empirical measures with finite exponential moment, and

\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}=\left\{(t,\mu)\in\mathcal{O}_{M}:\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}\text{ for some }{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\}.

Let us remark that every empirical measure has exponential moments, since it is a finite measure. Moreover, the value function
\hat{u}^{N}
is defined on
\mathcal{O}^{N}
and not on
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}
, because the latter set is not invariant for the dynamics of the empirical measure process, while it is invariant for the limiting dynamics.

Proposition 4.1
.

The value function
u^{N}
is the unique viscosity solution to the HJB (
4.3
). Furthermore
\hat{u}^{N}
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mu\in\mathcal{O}
for
W_{2}
and is
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder-continuous in
[0,T]
:

(4.6)

|\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}(\mathbf{x}))-\hat{u}^{N}(s,\mu^{N}(\mathbf{y}))|\leq C\big(W_{2}(\mu^{N}(\mathbf{x}),\mu^{N}(\mathbf{y}))+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Proof.

In a setting without regime switches, comparison principle for the viscosity solution of (
4.3
) is true
[
24
]
. Consequently the comparison principle when the regime switches are present follows just as in the Proof of Proposition
3.3
. Then by an application of the Stochastic Perron’s method
[
2
,
3
]
we have uniqueness of
u^{N}
.

Since coefficients are
W_{2}
-Lipschitz, it is easy to prove that
\hat{u}^{N}
is
W_{2}
-Lipschitz in
\mu
.
From the dynamic programming principle, it then follows the 1/2 Holder-continuity in time.
∎

4.2.
Convergence to mean field control

Lemma 4.1
.

The following relations hold (see for example
[
9
]
)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}{\phi}^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}D_{m}\tilde{\phi}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0},x_{i}),

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}{\phi}^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}D_{m}\tilde{\phi}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0},x_{i})+\dfrac{1}{N^{2}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}D_{m^{2}}^{2}\tilde{\phi}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0},x_{i},x_{i}),

where
\phi^{N}
and
\tilde{\phi}^{N}
are related by

{\phi}^{N}(t,{\bf x},i_{0})=\tilde{\phi}^{N}(t,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),i_{0}).

Remark 4.1
.

In the following we will often use the following distance like quantity instead of
d^{2}(\mu,\nu)
:

\hat{d}(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}{\left\langle\mu-\nu,f_{j}\right\rangle}^{2}.

We stress that
d
depends on
M
, which is the bound on the exponential moments of the measure, because the
{c_{j}}
do.
It is readily checked using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the relation
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}\leq 1
, that

(4.7)

d^{2}(\mu,\nu)\leq\hat{d}(\mu,\nu).

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
M>0
we have

(4.8)

\sup_{(t,\mu,i_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}}\left|V(t,\mu,i_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu,i_{0})\right|\leq\dfrac{C_{M}}{N^{\frac{1}{4}}},

for a constant
C_{M}
depending on
M
.

Remark 4.2
.

The exponent
1/4
comes from the
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuity in time of the value functions, as it is clear from the proofs. Assuming more regularity of the coefficients, we may obtain Lipschitz regularity in time; in the case without jumps and regime switching, such regularity is proved in
[
5
]
. Thus, in this case, we can obtain
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as convergence rate.

Proof.

We use the following notations:

\displaystyle E_{N}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,\mu,i_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}}\left(V(t,\mu,i_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu,i_{0})\right),

\displaystyle E_{N}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,\mu,i_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}}\left(\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu,i_{0})-V(t,\mu,i_{0})\right).

In order to prove (
4.8
) it is enough to show that each of
E_{N}^{+}
and
E_{N}^{-}
satisfy the same bound.
In the following we will only show that

(4.9)

E_{N}^{+}\leq\frac{C}{N^{\frac{1}{4}}}

and the other case can be done similarly. Note that
E_{N}^{+}
can be taken to be positive as otherwise inequality (
4.9
) holds trivially.
The proof has been broken into parts for ease in reading.
We fix
M>0
and define the distance
d
according to the constant
b=Me^{K^{*}}T
.
Please note that in the following, the constants
C
may depend on
M
and might change from line to line, but they are not renamed.

Step 1: Doubling of variables.

For a positive sequence
(\varepsilon_{N},\eta_{N})\to(0,0)
, we define the following map on
(\mathcal{O}_{M}\times\mathcal{S})\times(\mathcal{O}^{N}\times\mathcal{S})
, where
\mathcal{P}^{N}
is the set of empirical measures
\nu^{N}(\mathbf{x})
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

(4.10)

\Phi_{N}(t,\mu,i_{0},s,\nu,j_{0})=V(t,\mu,i_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(s,\nu,j_{0})-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\hat{d}(\mu,\nu)-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}(t-s)^{2}\\
-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\tilde{d}(i_{0},j_{0})-\dfrac{2T-t-s}{4T}E_{N}^{+}-\eta_{N}\log\Big(1+\sum_{j}c_{j}\left\langle\nu,f_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\Big),

where
\hat{u}^{N}
is defined above. Note that we add the penalization because
\mathcal{O}^{N}
is not compact.

Step 2: Maximum attained.

The maximum of
\Phi_{N}
is attained and at some
(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i_{0}},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j_{0}})
. This is because
\mathcal{O}_{M}
is compact,

\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\rightarrow+\infty}\Phi_{N}(t,\mu,i_{0},s,\nu^{N}(\mathbf{x}),j_{0})=-\infty

and
\Phi_{N}
is continuous in
t,\mu,s,\nu
.

Step 3: Bound on
d(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
.

Since
\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})\geq\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\nu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})
, from the Lipschitz continuity of
V
in (
3.9
), we have

\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\hat{d}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\leq V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-V(\bar{t},\bar{\nu},\bar{i}_{0})\leq Cd(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\leq C\sqrt{\hat{d}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})}.

This implies

\sqrt{\hat{d}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})}\leq C\varepsilon_{N},

which using relation (
4.7
) implies

d(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})\leq C\varepsilon_{N}.

Step 4: Bound on
|\bar{t}-\bar{s}|
.

Since
\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})\geq\Phi_{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})
, we have that

\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}(\bar{t}-\bar{s})^{2}\leq V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-V(\bar{s},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})+\dfrac{\bar{t}-\bar{s}}{4T}E_{N}^{+}\leq C|\bar{t}-\bar{s}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+\dfrac{\bar{t}-\bar{s}}{4T}E_{N}^{+},

where we have utilized the Hölder property of
V
. Since
E_{N}^{+}
is bounded (
V
and
u^{N}
both bounded on
\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}
) we obtain

|\bar{t}-\bar{s}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon_{N}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Step 5: Bound on
\tilde{d}(i_{0},j_{0})
.

Since
\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})\geq\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{j}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})
, we have that

\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\tilde{d}(i_{0},j_{0})\leq V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{j}_{0}).

Since the right hand side is bounded we have

\tilde{d}(i_{0},j_{0})\leq C\varepsilon_{N}.

Since
\varepsilon_{N}\to 0
, for
N
large enough we have that
\bar{i}_{0}=\bar{j}_{0}
. In the sequel we will assume that
N
is large enough so that this is indeed the case.

Step 6 Case I:
\bar{t}=T
.
For
(t,\mu,i_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{M}^{N}\times\mathcal{S}
, we have

\Phi_{N}(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0},\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})\geq\Phi_{N}(t,\mu,i_{0},t,\mu,i_{0})
. This implies

\displaystyle V(t,\mu,i_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(t,\mu,i_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2T-2t}{4T}E_{N}^{+}+V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\hat{d}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}(\bar{t}-\bar{s})^{2}-\dfrac{\bar{t}-\bar{s}}{4T}E_{N}^{+}

\displaystyle\qquad-\eta_{N}\log\Big(1+\sum_{j}c_{j}\left\langle\bar{\nu},f_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)+\eta_{N}\log\Big(1+\sum_{j}c_{j}\left\langle\mu,f_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{2}E_{N}^{+}+V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})+\eta_{N}\log\Big(1+\sum_{j}c_{j}\left\langle\mu,f_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\Big).

We notice that

\displaystyle V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bar{\mu},h(T,\cdot,\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})\right\rangle-\left\langle\bar{\nu},h(T,\cdot,\bar{\nu},\bar{i}_{0})\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\hat{u}^{N}(T,\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0}).

Using the Lipschitz assumption on
h
and Holder continuity of
u^{N}
in time, we get

V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-\hat{u}^{N}(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})\leq C\left(d(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})+|T-\bar{s}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq C\varepsilon_{N}^{\frac{1}{3}},

as long as
\varepsilon_{N}\gtrsim\frac{1}{N}
. Now taking supremum over
(t,\mu,i_{0})
, and recalling that

if

, we obtain

(4.11)

E_{N}^{+}\leq C\big(\varepsilon_{N}^{\frac{1}{3}}+\eta_{N}\big)+\frac{1}{2}E_{N}^{+}\implies E_{N}^{+}\leq C\big(\varepsilon_{N}^{\frac{1}{3}}+\eta_{N}\big).

Step 7 Case II:
\bar{s}=T
.
Similar to Case I.

Step 7 Case III:
0\leq\bar{t},\bar{s}<T
.
We use the viscosity solution properties.

(i)
\hat{u}^{N}-\varphi
has a minimum at
(\bar{s},\bar{\nu},\bar{j}_{0})
where

\begin{split}\varphi(s,\nu,j_{0})&=V(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\hat{d}(\bar{\mu},\nu)-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}(\bar{t}-s)^{2}\\
&-\dfrac{1}{2\varepsilon_{N}}\tilde{d}(\bar{i}_{0},j_{0})-\dfrac{2T-\bar{t}-s}{4T}E_{N}^{+}-\eta_{N}\log\Big(1+\sum_{j}c_{j}\left\langle\nu,f_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\Big).\end{split}

Recall that
\bar{\nu}=\mu^{N}({\bf x}^{\bar{\nu}})
for some
{\bf x}^{\bar{\nu}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us now define the function

\tilde{\varphi}^{N}(s,{\bf x},j_{0}):=\varphi(s,\mu^{N}({\bf x}),j_{0}).

It is readily checked that
\tilde{\varphi}^{N}\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{S})
.
This implies

(4.12)

\dfrac{\bar{s}-\bar{t}}{\varepsilon_{N}}-\dfrac{1}{4T}E_{N}^{+}+\dfrac{1}{N}\sup_{v}\sum_{k=1}^{N}H_{k}^{v}(\bar{s},{\bf x}^{\bar{\nu}},\bar{j}_{0},N\tilde{\varphi}^{N},ND\tilde{\varphi}^{N},ND^{2}\tilde{\varphi}^{N})\\
+\sum_{j_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}q_{i_{0}j_{0}}\left[\tilde{\varphi}^{N}(\bar{s},{\bf x}^{\bar{\nu}},j_{0})-\tilde{\varphi}^{N}(\bar{s},{\bf x}^{\bar{\nu}},\bar{j}_{0})\right]\geq 0.

It is readily checked that

Here using Lemma
4.1
and the definition of linear derivative

\dfrac{1}{N}\sup_{v}\sum_{k=1}^{N}H_{k}^{v}(\bar{s},{\bf x}^{\nu},\bar{j}_{0},N\tilde{\varphi}^{N},ND\tilde{\varphi}^{N},ND^{2}\tilde{\varphi}^{N})\\

where

and

+\lambda(t,x,\nu,v,i_{0})\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\left\{D_{m}\phi^{N}(t,\nu+\dfrac{r}{N}(\delta_{x+y}-\delta_{x}),i_{0},x+y)\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.-D_{m}\phi^{N}(t,\nu+\dfrac{r}{N}(\delta_{x+y}-\delta_{x}),i_{0},x)\right\}dr\right.\\
\left.-\left(D_{m}\phi^{N}(t,\nu,i_{0},x+y)-D_{m}\phi^{N}(t,\nu,i_{0},x)\right)\right]\gamma(dy)

(ii)
V-\psi
has a maximum at
(\bar{t},\bar{\mu},\bar{i}_{0})

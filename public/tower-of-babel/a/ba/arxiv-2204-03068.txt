A Fractal Uncertainty Principle for the Short-Time Fourier Transform and Gabor Multipliers
Helge Knutsen

Abstract
. We study the fractal uncertainty principle in the joint time-frequency representation, and we prove a version for the Short-Time Fourier transform with Gaussian window on the modulation spaces. This can equivalently be formulated in terms of projection operators on the Bargmann-Fock spaces of entire functions. Specifically for signals in L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , we obtain norm estimates of Daubechies’ time-frequency localization operator localizing on porous sets. The proof is based on the maximal Nyquist density of such sets, and for multidimensional Cantor iterates we derive explicit upper bound asymptotes. Finally, we translate the fractal uncertainty principle to discrete Gaussian Gabor multipliers.

The

denotes the characteristic function of a subset

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\Omega(h)}\pazocal{F}_{h}\chi_{T(h)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq Ch^{\beta}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

Alternatively, if we disentangle

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\Omega(h)}\pazocal{F}\chi_{T(h)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq Ch^{\beta}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

On this form, the FUP more clearly reads as an uncertainty principle as, depending on our choice of

Inspired by the FUP in the separate representation and motivated by the understanding that uncertainty principles should be present regardless of time-frequency representation, we search for analogous results in the joint representation. In particular, we consider and have considered Daubechies’ localization operator
P_{\Omega}
based on the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) with the Gaussian window that projects onto some subset
\Omega

[
19
]
,
[

In the present paper, however, we abandon the radial assumption and instead consider the more general problem of optimal localization on

Preliminaries

2.1.
From the Short-Time Fourier Transform to the Fock space

Consider some fixed window function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, and introduce the basic operations
T_{x}\phi(t):=\phi(t-x)
and
M_{\omega}\phi(t):=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\phi(t)
, i.e., time-translation and frequency-modulation, respectively. The
Short-Time Fourier Transform
(STFT) of some signal
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with respect to window
\phi
, evaluated at point
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is then given by the inner product

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\omega):=\langle f,M_{\omega}T_{x}\phi\rangle.

Observe that if
\phi\equiv 1
, the STFT coincides with the regular Fourier transform. For non-constant windows, however, we obtain a joint time-frequency description of our signal. Furthermore, for
\|\phi\|_{2}=1
, the STFT becomes an
isometry
onto some subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, i.e.,
\langle V_{\phi}f,V_{\phi}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\langle f,g\rangle
. In this case, we have an inversion formula, namely

(2.1)

\displaystyle f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)M_{\omega}T_{x}\phi\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\omega,

where the integral is interpreted in the weak-sense.
Daubechies’ time-frequency localization operator
,
P_{S}^{\phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with some bounded symbol
S
, is then obtained by modifying the above integrand by the multiplicative weight function
S(x,\omega)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle P_{S}^{\phi}f:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}S(x,\omega)\cdot V_{\phi}f(x,\omega)M_{\omega}T_{x}\phi\ \mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\omega

(2.2)

\displaystyle\iff\langle

\displaystyle P_{S}^{\phi}f,g\rangle=\langle S\cdot V_{\phi}f,V_{\phi}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\ \ \forall\ \ g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This could equivalently be viewed as modifying the resulting STFT by multiplication by
S
before inversion. Oftentimes, we consider
S=\chi_{\Omega}
, i.e., the characteristic function of a subset
\Omega
of the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, so that the operator
P_{\Omega}^{\phi}:=P_{\chi_{\Omega}}^{\phi}
is interpreted as projecting signals onto said time-frequency domain. The associated operator norm
\|P_{\Omega}^{\phi}\|_{\text{op}}
then measures the optimal localization on
\Omega
.

A popular choice for window function is the
Gaussian
function, which on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
reads

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(x):=2^{d/4}e^{-\pi x^{2}},\ \ \text{where }x^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{d}^{2}\ \ \text{for }\ x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}).

With this window choice, we can, in fact, replace Daubechies’ operator by a Toeplitz operator on the Bargmann-Fock space or simply Fock space,
\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.

We reference Zhu’s book
[
29
]
for a detailed introduction to Fock spaces in
\mathbb{C}
.
For a complex vector
z=x+i\omega=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we distinguish between
z^{2}=z\cdot z=z_{1}^{2}+\dots+z_{d}^{2}
and
|z|^{2}=z\cdot\overline{z}=|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{d}|^{2}
. Now, for arbitrary
p\geq 1
, let
\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(z):=e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)
denote the Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, where
\mathrm{d}A(z)
is the volume measure
\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\omega
. The associated
L^{p}
-space,
L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d},\mathrm{d}\mu_{p})
, is simply denoted by
\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. The
Fock space

\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is then defined as the Banach space of entire functions
F\in\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, with norm
1
1

1

For consistent and simple notation, we denote the norm in the Fock space by
\|\cdot\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}
rather than
\|\cdot\|_{\pazocal{F}^{p}}
. In particular, this is to avoid switching notation for functions in
\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, e.g., when we consider
F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}
for
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
and
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
, we let
\pazocal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
denote the space of measurable functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{\infty}}=\esssup\left\{|F(z)|e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}\ \big|\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\right\}<\infty.

Again, the Fock space
\pazocal{F}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is the Banach space of entire functions in
\pazocal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
For
p=2
, we find that the Fock space forms a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, with reproducing kernel
K_{\xi}(z)=e^{\pi z\cdot\overline{\xi}}
so that
\langle F,K_{\xi}\rangle_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}=F(\xi)
. Utilizing this kernel, we obtain an orthogonal projection
P:\pazocal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\to\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, defined by

\displaystyle PF(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}K_{\xi}(z)F(\xi)e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

For a bounded measurable function
S:\mathbb{C}^{d}(\cong\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the
Toeplitz operator

\pazocal{T}_{S}:\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\to\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, with symbol
S
, by

\displaystyle\pazocal{T}_{S}F(z):=P(SF)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}S(\xi)\cdot K_{\xi}(z)F(\xi)e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

If we initially consider test functions
G\in\pazocal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\cap\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, then, by Fubini’s theorem and the reproducing property of the kernel
K_{\xi}(z)
, the inner product attains the simple form

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\pazocal{T}_{S}F,G\rangle_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}=\langle S\cdot F,G\rangle_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}

By a density argument, it follows that (
2.3
) holds for all
G\in\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, more akin to the inner product (
2.2
).

The precise connection to Daubechies’ time-frequency operator with a Gaussian window is established through the Bargmann transform. The
Bargmann transform
, first introduced in
[
4
]
, is an isometric isomorphism
\pazocal{B}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\pazocal{B}f(z):=2^{d/4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)e^{2\pi t\cdot z-\pi t^{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}z^{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

The transform is related to the STFT with Gaussian window via the following identity (see Proposition 3.4.1 in
[
14
]
)

(2.5)

\displaystyle V_{\phi_{0}}f(x,-\omega)=e^{\pi ix\cdot\omega}\pazocal{B}f(z)e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|z|^{2}}\ \ \text{for }z=x+i\omega.

Define
S^{c}(z):=S(\overline{z})
, so that
\langle P_{S}^{\phi_{0}}f,g\rangle

=\langle\pazocal{T}_{S^{c}}\pazocal{B}f,\pazocal{B}g\rangle_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}
, from which the one-to-one correspondence between Daubechies’ operator
P_{S}^{\phi_{0}}
and the Toeplitz operator
\pazocal{T}_{S^{c}}
is evident. In particular, we have that their norms coincide.

In the subsequent discussion, we shall consider Toeplitz operators projecting onto
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
, which we will simply denote by
\pazocal{T}_{\Omega}=\pazocal{T}_{\chi_{\Omega}}
. By (
2.3
), the operator norm is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\pazocal{T}_{\Omega}\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{\|F\|_{\pazocal{F}^{2}}=1}\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z).

For general
p\geq 1
, we consider upper bounds for the quantity

\displaystyle\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}=\frac{\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}\ \ \text{with }\ F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}.

By the use of complex interpolation (see Appendix
A
for details), the above quotients are actually bounded by the estimate for
p=1
, i.e.,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}\leq\sup_{F\in\pazocal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}},

which allows for estimates
without
any
p
-dependence.
Unsurprisingly, the added structure provided by the Fock space turns out to be beneficial when estimating the norm. Namely, we shall exploit subaveraging properties of
subharmonic
functions.

2.2.
Gabor frames and Gabor multipliers

In general, a family of vectors
\{\phi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in the Hilbert space
\pazocal{H}
is called a
frame
if there exist constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
, i.e., frame bounds, such that

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}|\langle f,\phi_{\lambda}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\ \ \forall\ \ f\in\pazocal{H}.

The associated frame operator
\pazocal{S}:\pazocal{H}\to\pazocal{H}
is given by
\pazocal{S}f:=\sum_{\lambda}\langle f,\phi_{\lambda}\rangle\phi_{\lambda}
with norm
\|\pazocal{S}\|_{\pazocal{H}}\in[A,B]
.
If
A=B
, the frame is called a
tight frame
. By renormalizing the vectors
\phi_{\lambda}\mapsto\phi_{\lambda}/\sqrt{A}
, any tight frame can be turned into a
Parseval frame
, i.e.,
A=B=1
, where we also have the representation
f=\sum_{\lambda}\langle f,\phi_{\lambda}\rangle\phi_{\lambda}
, i.e.,
\pazocal{S}=\text{id}
.

The
Gabor frame
for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is based on the idea of discretizing the STFT inversion formula (
2.1
).
For this purpose consider a lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
of sampled points. Oftentimes, we consider rectangular lattices of the form
\Lambda=a\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times b\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with parameters
a,b>0
. Further, fix a window function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
and define the time-frequency shifts
\pi(x,\omega)\phi:=M_{\omega}T_{x}\phi
. If the family of time-frequency shifts
\{\pi(\lambda)\phi\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame, we call this system a Gabor frame with generating function
\phi
over lattice
\Lambda
. We may also include a normalization factor based on the density of the lattice. More precisely, to each lattice we can associate a connected neighbourhood of the origin
A_{\Lambda}
called the fundamental region such that
\cup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}(\lambda+A_{\Lambda})=\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
|(\lambda+A_{\Lambda})\cap(\xi+A_{\Lambda})|=0
whenever
\lambda\neq\xi\in\Lambda
. With the normalization
\sqrt{|A_{\Lambda}|}\{\pi(\lambda)\phi\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, the associated frame operator
\pazocal{S}_{\Lambda}^{\phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
reads

(2.8)

\displaystyle\pazocal{S}_{\Lambda}^{\phi}f=|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi\rangle\pi(\lambda)\phi.

We immediately recognize
\pazocal{S}_{\Lambda}^{\phi}f
as a Riemann sum of the integral (
2.1
).
Thus, for a sequence of Gabor frames
\sqrt{|A_{\Lambda_{n}}}|\{\pi(\lambda)\phi\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}
where
A_{\Lambda_{n}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we expect the discretization (
2.8
) to converge weakly to the integral (
2.1
) and the frame bounds to tighten. Notably for the sequence of square lattices
\Lambda_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\right)
and
f,\phi
in Feichtinger’s algebra
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, Weisz shows in
[
27
]
that
S^{\phi}_{\Lambda_{n}}f
converges to
f
in the
M^{1}
-norm as
n\to\infty
. While
\phi
remains in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, this result is extended to
f
in the modulation space
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with convergence in the
M^{p,q}
-norm. In particular, we have convergence in the
L^{2}
-norm when
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The
Gabor multiplier
,
\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,b}^{\phi}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, represents a discretization of Daubechies’ operator in (
2.2
), where the sum (
2.8
) is weighted by a bounded symbol
b
defined on the lattice
\Lambda
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,b}^{\phi}f:=|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b(\lambda)\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi\rangle\pi(\lambda)\phi.

For localization on a specific subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, we shall consider Gabor symbols
b
that mimic the behaviour of the characteristic function
\chi_{\Omega}
.
One natural option is to consider the portion of a lattice point region
\lambda+A_{\Lambda}
containing the subset
\Omega
, i.e.,

\displaystyle b_{\Omega}(\lambda):=\frac{|\Omega\cap(\lambda+A_{\Lambda})|}{|A_{\Lambda}|}\in[0,1].

Alternatively, we only distinguish between whether the lattice point region
\lambda+A_{\Lambda}
contains a non-zero part of
\Omega
. That is, we apply the ceiling function
\lceil\cdot\rceil
, rounding up to the nearest integer, so that

\displaystyle\lceil b_{\Omega}(\lambda)\rceil\in\{0,1\}.

Evidently
0\leq b_{\Omega}(\lambda)\leq\lceil b_{\Omega}(\lambda)\rceil
, from which it is easily verified that the operator norms also satisfy
\|\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,b_{\Omega}}^{\phi}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\|\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\lceil b_{\Omega}\rceil}^{\phi}\|_{\text{op}}
. Thus, when estimating upper bounds for the operator norm in section
5
, we only consider the second symbol suggestion,
\lceil b_{\Omega}\rceil
. Notice that utilizing symbol
\lceil b_{\Omega}\rceil
is the same as restricting the summation (
2.8
) to a subset of the lattice
\Lambda
, namely

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\Omega}:=\big\{\lambda\in\Lambda\ \big|\ |\Omega\cap(\lambda+A_{\Lambda})|>0\big\}.

For simplicity, we denote the Gabor multiplier with symbol
\lceil b_{\Omega}\rceil
by
\mathscr{G}^{\phi}_{\Lambda,\Omega}
, which, by the above observation, is given by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi}f=|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi\rangle\pi(\lambda)\phi.

2.3.
Porous sets and Cantor sets

We shall define ”fractal sets” in terms of the general notion of
\nu
-porosity. It is based on Definition 2.7 in
[
8
]
, adjusted to higher dimensions. Informally, in order for a set to be classified as porous, we require the set to contain gaps or pores within certain scale bounds.

Def. 2.1
.

(
\nu
-porosity)
Suppose
0<\nu<1
,
0\leq\alpha_{\min}\leq\alpha_{\max}\leq\infty
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is closed. We say that
\Omega
is
\nu
-porous on scales
\alpha_{\min}
to
\alpha_{\max}
if for every ball
B_{r}(x)
of radius
r\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]
there exists a ball
B_{\nu r}(y)\subseteq B_{r}(x)
of radius
\nu r
such that
|\Omega\cap B_{\nu r}(y)|=0
.

Notice that in one dimension, the
\nu
-porous set resembles a Cantor type set, where we are able to remove a
\nu
-portion of any interval inductively down to the lower bound scale. The Cantor sets represent a popular and easy to understand family of fractal sets, which we construct as follows:

Let
M>1
be a fixed integer, and let
\mathscr{A}
be a non-empty proper subset of
\{0,1,\dots,M-1\}
. The
n
-iterate (
n
-order)
discrete Cantor set
with base
M
and alphabet
\mathscr{A}
is then defined as

\displaystyle\pazocal{C}_{n}^{(d)}(M,\mathscr{A}):=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}M^{j}\ \Big|\ a_{j}\in\mathscr{A},\ j=0,1,\dots,n-1\right\}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,M^{n}-1\}.

The ”continuous”
n
-iterate Cantor set based in the interval
[0,L]
is given by

\displaystyle\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A}):=LM^{-n}\cdot\pazocal{C}_{n}^{(d)}(M,\mathscr{A})+[0,LM^{-n}]\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots

The iterates are nested, i.e.,
\pazocal{C}_{n+1}\subseteq\pazocal{C}_{n}
, and the (limit) Cantor set is then given by the intersection of all the
n
-iterates.
While the Cantor set itself has measure zero, each
n
-iterate does not.
If we let
|\mathscr{A}|
denote the cardinality of the alphabet
\mathscr{A}
, the measure of the
n
-iterate Cantor set is given by

\displaystyle|\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})|=\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{n}L.

Note that for
M=3
and
\mathscr{A}=\{0,2\}
, we obtain the standard mid-third
n
-iterate Cantor set, with measure
(2/3)^{n}L
.

Unsurprisingly, the Cantor sets are indeed
\nu
-porous. Below we present a simple estimate for the porosity constant and the scales (see Appendix
B
for details):

Lemma 2.1
.

The
n
-iterate Cantor set
\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})
with base
M>1
and alphabet size
|\mathscr{A}|<M
, based in the interval
[0,L]
, is
\nu
-porous on scales
M^{-n+1}
to
\infty
, with any
\nu\leq M^{-2}
.

In multiple dimensions
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, we consider two possible Cantor set constructions:

(1)

For a ball of radius
R>0
centered at the origin, we consider the
radially symmetric

n
-iterate Cantor set as a subset of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{n}^{2d}(R,M,\mathscr{A}):=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{2d})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ \big|\ \left(x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{2d}^{2}\right)^{d}\in\pazocal{C}_{n}(R^{2d},M,\mathscr{A})\right\}.

In particular, localizing on
\mathscr{C}_{n}^{1}(R,M,\mathscr{A})
has been discussed extensively in
[
20
]
.
In general, these radially symmetric Cantor iterates are constructed such that all annuli that make up the set have the
same
measure. The total measure is given by

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}_{n}^{2d}(R,M,\mathscr{A})|=\left|\pazocal{C}_{n}\left((\pi R^{2})^{d}/d!,M,\mathscr{A}\right)\right|=\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{n}\frac{(\pi R^{2})^{d}}{d!},

where we recognize
\frac{(\pi R^{2})^{d}}{d!}
as the volume of the
2d
-dimensional ball of radius
R
.

(2)

Alternatively, we can consider the
Cartesian product
of 1-dimensional
n_{j}
-iterate Cantor sets,
\{\pazocal{C}_{n_{j}}(L_{j},M_{j},\mathscr{A}_{j})\}_{j}
, that is,

\displaystyle\pazocal{C}_{n_{1}}(L_{1},M_{1},\mathscr{A}_{1})\times\pazocal{C}_{n_{2}}(L_{2},M_{2},\mathscr{A}_{2})\times\dots\times\pazocal{C}_{n_{2d}}(L_{2d},M_{2d},\mathscr{A}_{2d}).

If all iterates coincide, the above Cartesian product reduces to
\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})^{2d}
based in the hypercube
[0,L]^{2d}
.

Remark
.

In
[
8
]
fractal sets are originally defined in terms of
\delta
-regularity for some
0<\delta<1
(see Definition 2.2 in
[
8
]
). While only formulated in
1
-dimension, the notion of
\delta
-regularity can also be extended to higher dimensions. Compared to
\nu
-porosity, this concept offers a different perspective on fractal sets: For instance with regard to the
n
-iterate Cantor set
\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})
, we find that the
\delta
corresponds to fractal dimension (or Hausdorff dimension) of the iterate, namely
\frac{\ln|\mathscr{A}|}{\ln M}
. However, the notion of
\delta
-regularity might appear more abstract than
\nu
-porosity as it does not immediately read as a set containing gaps, and less so the size of those gaps. Nonetheless, as shown in
[
8
]
Proposition 2.10, any
\delta
-regular set is
\nu
-porous, and the scales associated to
\delta
-regularity coincide with the
\nu
-porous scales up to multiplicative constants. Thus, formulating the FUP on
\nu
-porous sets directly translates to an FUP on
\delta
-regular sets.

3.
Fractal Uncertainty Principle in Joint Representation

In this section we present the FUP for the joint time-frequency representation. Initially, in section
3.1
we derive the FUP for the Fock spaces. In the subsequent section
3.2
we introduce the modulation spaces, and translate the FUP for the Fock spaces to an uncertainty principle for the STFT on modulation spaces.

3.1.
Fractal Uncertainty Principle for Fock spaces

Theorem 3.1
.

(FUP for Fock Spaces
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
)

Let
0<h\leq 1
, and suppose
\Omega(h)\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is an
h
-dependent family of sets which is
\nu
-porous on scales
h
to
1
. Then for all
p\geq 1
and all
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
there exist constants
C,\beta>0
only dependent on
\nu
(and
d
) such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega(h)}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}\leq Ch^{\beta}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

In particular, for
p=2
, the Toeplitz operator
\pazocal{T}_{\Omega(h)}
satisfies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\pazocal{T}_{\Omega(h)}\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq Ch^{\beta}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

The essential property of the Fock space that we utilize is the subaveraging property, where point evaluations can be bounded by an average over the ball. The statement is found in
[
29
]
for
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C})
, which generalized to
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
(see Appendix
C
for details) reads:

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and any point
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we have for all
R>0
that

(3.3)

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

Proceeding, we require the following two concepts: For a set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
R>0
, we define the
maximal Nyquist density
,
\rho(\Omega,R)
, by

(3.4)

\displaystyle\rho(\Omega,R)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}}|\Omega\cap B_{R}(z)|\leq\max\{|\Omega|,|B_{R}(0)|\}.

Further, we define the
R
-
thickened set
,
\Omega_{R}
, by

\displaystyle\Omega_{R}

\displaystyle:=\Omega+B_{R}(0)=\cup_{z\in\Omega}B_{R}(z).

With these notions and Lemma
3.2
, we present an upper bound estimate for the integral over
\Omega
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
measurable. Then for any
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and any
R>0
, we have that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}\frac{\rho(\Omega,R)}{1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)\mathrm{d}A(z).

Since
z\in\Omega
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)=\int_{\Omega_{R}}\chi_{B_{R}(z)}(\xi)\cdot|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).\ \ \ \ \ \

By Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)\mathrm{d}A(z)=\int_{\Omega_{R}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\chi_{B_{R}(\xi)}(z)\mathrm{d}A(z)\right)|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\pi\frac{p}{2}|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\Omega\cap B_{R}(\xi)|\cdot|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)\leq\rho(\Omega,R)\int_{\Omega_{R}}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

∎

In the above lemma notice that the integral over
\Omega_{R}
is
always
bounded by the integral over
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, that is,

\displaystyle\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(\Omega,R)}{1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}}\ \|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}\ \ \forall\ \ R>0.

Further, by the interpolation result (
2.7
), we can optimize the right-hand side with the estimate for
p=1
so that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}\leq 2^{-d}

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(\Omega,R)}{1-e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}}\ \|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}\ \ \forall\ \ R>0.

Regardless, this shows that estimates of the quotient
\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}/\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}
can be solely based on estimates of the maximal Nyquist density. E.g., as shown in the subsequent section
4
, under certain growth conditions, we are able to obtain good estimates for Nyquist density for the standard Cartesian product of Cantor sets and radial Cantor sets. For the general case of porous sets, however, we also take into account the integral over the thickened set.

Besides the trivial upper bounds of (
3.4
), if we suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is
\nu
-porous on scales
\alpha_{\min}
to
\alpha_{\max}
and consider a radius
R
within the scale bounds, we obtain, by Def.
2.1
, the simple estimate

\displaystyle\rho(\Omega,R)\leq\sup_{|z-\xi|\leq(1-\nu)R}\big|B_{R}(z)\setminus B_{\nu R}(\xi)\big|=\left(1-\nu^{2d}\right)|B_{R}(0)|=\left(1-\nu^{2d}\right)\frac{\left(\pi R^{2}\right)^{d}}{d!}.

We apply this upper bound to Lemma
3.3
, which yields the corollary:

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is
\nu
-porous on scales
\alpha_{\min}
to
\alpha_{\max}
. Then for any radius
R\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]
and any function
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, we have that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)\leq\kappa_{d}\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu^{2d}\right)\int_{\Omega_{R}}|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z),

where the function
\kappa_{d}
is given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\kappa_{d}(x):=\frac{x^{d}}{d!}\left(1-e^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}.

The crucial observation, moving forward, is that for some choices of
R>0
, the thickened set
\Omega_{R}
is itself a porous set provided the original set
\Omega
is porous. A special case of this observation is presented in Proposition 2.11
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(Thickening of porous set)
Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is
\nu
-porous on scales
\alpha_{\min}
to
\alpha_{\max}
. For any
0\leq r<\nu\cdot\alpha_{\max}
, consider the
r
-thickened set
\Omega_{r}:=\Omega+B_{r}(0)=\cup_{z\in\Omega}B_{r}(z)
. Then for any
R\in[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]
with
\frac{r}{\nu}<R
, the set
\Omega_{r}
is
\left(\nu-\frac{r}{R}\right)
-porous on scales
R
to
\alpha_{\max}
.

Proof.

Consider
\alpha_{\min}\leq R\leq L\leq\alpha_{\max}
. By
\nu
-porosity of
\Omega
, for any
B_{L}(z)
there exists
B_{\nu L}(\xi)\subseteq B_{L}(z)
such that
|B_{\nu L}(\xi)\cap\Omega|=0
. After
r
-thickening, we maintain a zero intersection with
\Omega_{r}
if the radius of
B_{\nu L}(\xi)
is reduced to
\nu L-r
. Since
L
is arbitrary and

\displaystyle\left(\nu-\frac{r}{R}\right)L\leq\nu L-r\implies|B_{\left(\nu-\frac{r}{R}\right)L}(\xi)\cap\Omega_{r}|\leq|B_{\nu L-r}(\xi)\cap\Omega_{r}|=0,

the set
\Omega_{r}
satisfies the claimed porosity properties. The conditions on
r
and
R
ensure that the new porosity is positive and that the scale bounds are valid.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof.

(Theorem
3.1
)
By the interpolation result (
2.7
), it suffices to prove the statement for the case
p=1
. Nonetheless, for notational convenience, we will rather consider the case
p=2
as the only difference in the proof between
p=2
and general

is that
\kappa_{d}(x)
is replaced by
\kappa_{d}(\frac{p}{2}x)
.

Recall that for some
0<h\leq 1
we presume
\Omega=\Omega(h)
is
\nu
-porous on scales
h
to
1
. For some finite sequence of radii
\{R_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, we consider an associated sequence of thickened sets
\{\Omega^{(j)}\}_{j=0}^{n-1}
, given by

\displaystyle\Omega^{(0)}:=\Omega\ \ \text{and }\ \Omega^{(j)}:=\Omega_{R_{1}+R_{2}+\dots+R_{j}}\ \ \text{for }j=1,2,\dots,n-1.

We chose the radii such that the set
\Omega^{(j)}
maintains the porous property, in the sense that

\displaystyle\Omega^{(j)}\ \text{is }\nu_{j}\text{-porous on scales }R_{j+1}\ \text{to }1.

By Lemma
3.4
, the new porosity constants
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j}
are given by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nu_{j}=\nu-\frac{R_{1}+\dots+R_{j}}{R_{j+1}},\ \ \text{where }\ R_{j+1}>\frac{R_{1}+\dots+R_{j}}{\nu}\ \ \text{for }j=0,1,\dots,n-1.

Since the radii must also satisfy
h\leq R_{j}\leq 1
, the length of the sequences

cannot be infinite and must depend on

.
From the above conditions, it is clear that radii must at least grow geometrically, i.e., (in the equality case)
R_{j}=c\cdot a^{j}
for
j=1,2,\dots,n
where
a>1
and

.
In particular, the conditions are satisfied for the sequence of radii
R_{j}=c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{j}
for

such that
c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{n}\leq 1<c\cdot\left(\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{n+1}

, it follows that
0<n+\ln h\cdot\left(\ln\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{-1}\leq 1
, which means

.

Now we make repeated use of Corollary
3.1
to the sequence of thickened sets
\{\Omega^{(j)}\}_{j}
, which reveals that the integral over
\Omega
is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\prod_{k=1}^{j}\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{k}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{k-1}^{2d}\right)\right]\cdot\int_{\Omega^{(j)}}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\prod_{k=1}^{j}\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{k}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{k-1}^{2d}\right)\right]\cdot\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}^{2}\ \ \text{for }j=1,2,\dots,n.

It is straightforward to verify that
\lim_{x\to 0}\kappa_{d}(x)=1
and the derivative
\kappa_{d}^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x>0
.
Combined with the fact that the sequence of porosity constants
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j}
is decreasing, we conclude that the sequence
\{\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{j}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{j-1}^{2d}\right)\}_{j}
is monotonically
increasing
. Thus, we are interested in the index reduction
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{j}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{j-1}^{2d}\right)<1\ \ \text{for }j=1,2,\dots,n-n_{0}.

Since
\lim_{h\to 0}\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{1}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{0}^{2d}\right)=1-\nu^{2d}<1
, for sufficiently small
h<h_{0}
, we have that there exists an
n_{0}
such that the difference
n-n_{0}>0
. Next, we claim that there exists an index reduction
n_{0}
which is
independent
of

, implying

. Note that the procedure below does

, merely shows that such an

exists.

From (

) with
R_{j}=c\left(\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{j}
, we find the following lower bound for the porosities

\displaystyle\nu_{j}=\nu-\left(\frac{\nu}{3}\right)^{j+1}\sum_{k=1}^{j}\left(\frac{3}{\nu}\right)^{k}\geq\frac{\nu}{2}\ \ \ \forall\ \ \ j\in\mathbb{N}\ \ \text{and }\ 0<\nu<1.

Now, let

denote the (unique) solution to
\kappa_{d}(\pi r^{2})\cdot\left(1-\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)^{2d}\right)=1
, from which it is evident that
\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{j}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{j-1}^{2d}\right)<1
for all

. Now, consider any fixed
r_{0}<r
, and observe that since
r_{0}
is independent of

, and the radius

is bounded, there must exist a finite number

also

such that

.

In summary, by our choice of index reduction

, we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa_{d}\left(\pi R_{j}^{2}\right)\cdot\left(1-\nu_{j-1}^{2d}\right)\leq\kappa_{d}(\pi r_{0}^{2})\cdot\left(1-\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)^{2d}\right)=:(1-\epsilon)<1\ \ \text{for }\ j=1,2,\dots,n-n_{0}.

Since

, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(z)\leq(1-\epsilon)^{n-n_{0}}\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}^{2}\sim h^{\beta}\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}^{2}\ \ \text{for some }\beta>0.

∎

From the above relation, we observe that

Furthermore, similarly to the condition imposed in

Def. 4.1
.

Let the interval length
L
be a function
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The interval length

satisfies condition
(I_{M})
with constants

\displaystyle c_{1}M^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq L(n)\leq c_{2}M^{\frac{n}{2}}\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots

For the
2d

\mathscr{C}_{n}^{d}(R,M,\mathscr{A})

[

Def. 4.2

Let the radius

\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

(D_{M})
with constants

(4.2)

\displaystyle c_{1}M^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq R^{2d}(n)\leq c_{2}M^{\frac{n}{2}},\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots

With these conditions, we present an FUP for the two multidimensional Cantor set constructions from section

Theorem 4.1
.

(FUP for Cantor sets)
We consider the two constructions separately:

(i)

Let
\mathscr{C}_{n}^{2d}(R,M,\mathscr{A})

(4.3)

Let

In particular, when all iterates are equal to say
\pazocal{C}_{n}(L(n),M,\mathscr{A})
, we find that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|V_{\phi_{0}}f\cdot\chi_{\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})^{2d}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{p}\leq\gamma\cdot\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{n\cdot d}\|V_{\phi_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{p}\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2\dots

Remark
.

For localization on the radially symmetric Cantor set with
d=1
and
p=2
, we retrieve the same upper bound asymptote as in
[
20
]
Theorem 3.3. In fact, Theorem 3.3 reveals that the general asymptote is optimal in the sense that there are alphabets where the asymptote is precise. However, the same theorem also state that there are alphabets where the asymptote is not precise.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is based on inequality (
3.12
), combined with estimates of the maximal Nyquist density of the multidimensional Cantor iterates.
To begin with, we consider the 1-dimensional
n
-iterate
\pazocal{C}_{n}(M,\mathscr{A}):=\pazocal{C}_{n}(1,M,\mathscr{A})
based in
[0,1]
, to which we associate the so-called
Cantor function

\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
, given by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(x):=|\pazocal{C}_{n}(M,\mathscr{A})|^{-1}\begin{cases}0,\ \ x\leq 0,\\
|\pazocal{C}_{n}(M,\mathscr{A})\cap[0,x]|,\ \ x>0.\end{cases}

For the iterates based in
[0,L]
, we consider the dilated Cantor function
\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(\cdot\ L^{-1})
. The Cantor function is a useful concept as the difference
\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(y)-\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(x)
measures the
portion
of the
n
-iterate
\pazocal{C}_{n}(M,\mathscr{A})
contained in the interval
[x,y]
. By definition, the Cantor function is said to be
subadditive
if the difference is bounded by
\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(y-x)
. While this is the case for the mid-third Cantor set (see
[
7
]
), we cannot guarantee subadditivity with an arbitrary alphabet. Nonetheless, for our purpose, we only require a
weaker
version utilizing the canonical alphabet
\overline{\mathscr{A}}:=\{0,1,\dots,|\mathscr{A}|\}
(see
[
20
]
Appendix A).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}
denote the Cantor-function, defined in (
4.5
). Then for any
x\leq y
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(y)-\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(x)\leq\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}}}(y-x).

Re-scaling to Cantor iterates based
[0,L]
, we find, by the above lemma, that the maximal Nyquist density is bounded by

\displaystyle\rho\big(\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A}),x\big)=\max_{a\in\mathbb{R}}|\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})\cap[a,a+x]|\leq|\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}})\cap[0,x]|.

In the next lemma, we enforce condition (
4.1
), which yields a more explicit estimate for the upper bound.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that the alphabet
\mathscr{A}
is a proper subset of
\{0,1,\dots,M-1\}
, and suppose that the length
L(n)>0
satisfies condition
(I_{M})
with constants
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
. Then for any fixed
x>0
, there exists a finite constant
\gamma>0
dependent only on
x,c_{1},c_{2}
such that

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})\cap[0,x]|=\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}\big(xL^{-1}\big)\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{n}L\leq\gamma\cdot\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\to 0\ \text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Fix some positive integer
n_{0}<n
, and observe that for coefficients
a_{j}\in\mathscr{A}
, the sum
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{j}M^{j-n}<M^{n_{0}-n}
only if
a_{j}=0
for
j\geq n_{0}
. Thus, the Cantor function is bounded by

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(x)\leq|\mathscr{A}|^{n_{0}-n}\ \ \forall\ \ x<M^{n_{0}-n}.

Evidently, we also have that
xM^{-m}<M^{n_{0}+m-(n-2m)}
, which, by the above estimate, yields

(4.8)

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(xM^{-m})\leq|\mathscr{A}|^{n_{0}+m-(n+2m)}=|\mathscr{A}|^{-m}|\mathscr{A}|^{n_{0}-n}\ \ \text{for }\ m=0,1,2,\dots

Now, suppose
x
is
any
fixed number, to which there exists a threshold iterate
N\geq 0
so that
xM^{-\frac{N}{2}}<M^{-1}
, and we are in a position to apply (
4.8
). From here, we deduce that there exists a constant only dependent on
x
, say
\kappa=\kappa(x)
such that

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(xM^{-\frac{n}{2}})\leq\kappa(x)|\mathscr{A}|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots

Enforce the lower bound condition
L(n)\geq c_{1}M^{\frac{n}{2}}
. By monotonicity of the Cantor function,

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(xL^{-1})\leq\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\mathscr{A}}(xc_{1}^{-1}M^{-\frac{n}{2}})\leq\kappa(xc_{1}^{-1})|\pazocal{A}|^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

Finally, enforce the upper bound condition
L(n)\leq c_{2}M^{\frac{n}{2}}
, and inequality (
4.7
) follows with constant
\gamma=\kappa(xc_{1}^{-1})c_{2}
. Since
0<c_{1}\leq c_{2}<\infty
, the constant
\gamma
is indeed finite.
∎

We are now ready to estimate upper bounds for the maximal Nyquist densities for the multidimensional Cantor iterates, which, combined with (
3.12
), proves Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

(Nyquist density of Cantor sets)
Let
r>0
be fixed.

(i)

Let
\mathscr{C}_{n}^{2d}(R,M,\mathscr{A})
denote the radial Cantor iterate, and suppose the radius
R(n)>0
satisfies condition
(D_{M})
with constants
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. Then there exist a constant
\gamma_{r}>0
only dependent on
M,|\mathscr{A}|
and
c_{1},c_{2}
such that the maximal Nyquist density

\displaystyle\rho\left(\mathscr{C}_{n}^{2d}(R,M,\mathscr{A}),r\right)\leq\gamma_{r}\cdot\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}}{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots

(ii)

Let
\Omega:=\pazocal{C}_{n_{1}}(L_{1},M_{1},\mathscr{A}_{1})\times\dots\times\pazocal{C}_{n_{2d}}(L_{2d},M_{2d},\mathscr{A}_{2d})
denote the Cartesian product, and suppose the interval lengths
L_{j}(n_{j})
satisfy condition
(I_{M_{j}})
with the same constants
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
. Then there exist a constant
\gamma_{r}>0
only dependent on
\{(M_{j},|\mathscr{A}_{j}|)\}_{j}
and
c_{1},c_{2}
such that the maximal Nyquist density is bounded by

\displaystyle\rho\left(\Omega,r\right)\leq\gamma_{r}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{2d}\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}_{j}|}{M_{j}}\right)^{n_{j}/2}\ \ \text{for }\ n=0,1,2,\dots\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
r\geq 1
. For part (i), fix a cutoff value
N\gg 1
and distinguish between two cases for
B_{r}(a)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, (1)
|a|\leq Nr
and (2)
|a|>Nr
:

For case (1),
B_{r}(a)\subseteq B_{(N+1)r}(0)
so that
|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(\dots)\cap B_{r}(a)|\leq|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(\dots)\cap B_{(N+1)r}(0)|
. By definition of the radial Cantor iterate, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(R,M,\mathscr{A})\cap B_{r}(a)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{d}}{d!}\left|\pazocal{C}_{n}(R^{2d},M,\mathscr{A})\cap\left[0,(N+1)^{2d}r^{2d}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\ \ \text{(by Lemma \ref{lemma_cantor_function_decay})}

for some constant
\gamma_{1}>0
.

For case (2),
B_{r}(a)\subseteq A_{r}(|a|):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ |\ 0<|a|-r\leq|z|\leq|a|+r\}
. For the annulus
A_{r}(|a|)
with
r<|a|
, we have again, by definition of the radial Cantor iterate, that

\displaystyle\big|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(R,M,\mathscr{A})\cap A_{r}(|a|)\big|=\frac{\pi^{d}}{d!}\big|\pazocal{C}_{n}(R^{2d},M,\mathscr{A})\cap\left[(|a|-r)^{2d},(|a|+r)^{2d}\right]\big|

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{d}}{d!}\big|\pazocal{C}_{n}(R^{2d},M,\overline{\mathscr{A}})\cap\left[0,(|a|+r)^{2d}-(|a|-r)^{2d}\right]\big|\ \ \text{(by Lemma \ref{lemma_Cantor_function_weak_subadditivity_property})}.

Since
|a|>1
and since the leading term
|a|^{2d}
cancels, it follows that

\displaystyle(|a|+r)^{2d}-(|a|-r)^{2d}\leq|a|^{2d-1}\left((1+r)^{2d}-(1-r)^{2d}\right).

Furthermore, by subadditivity, the Cantor function
\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}}}(m\cdot x)\leq m\cdot\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}}}(x)
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. In general, this means

\displaystyle\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}}}(m\cdot x)\leq(m+1)\cdot\pazocal{G}_{n,M,\overline{\mathscr{A}}}(x)\ \ \forall\ \ m>0.

With these observations inequality (
4.9
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\big|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(R,M,\mathscr{A})\cap A_{r}(|a|)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{d}}{d!}\left(|a|^{2d-1}+1\right)\big|\pazocal{C}_{n}(R^{2d},M,\overline{\mathscr{A}})\cap\left[0,(1+r)^{2d}-(1-r)^{2d}\right]\big|

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{2}\cdot\left(|a|^{2d-1}+1\right)\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}|}{M}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\ \ \text{(by Lemma \ref{lemma_cantor_function_decay})}

for some constant
\gamma_{2}>0

independent
of
|a|
. Evidently, the right-hand side of the above inequality is unbounded in terms of
|a|
. The ball
B_{r}(a)
, however, only represents a fraction of the annulus
A_{r}(|a|)
, which warrants a closer comparison between
|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(\dots)\cap A_{r}(|a|)|
and
|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(\dots)\cap B_{r}(a)|
.
Let
\partial B_{\eta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\ |\ |z|=\eta\}
denote the
(2d-1)
-dimensional sphere of radius
\eta>0
with associated surface area
|\partial B_{\eta}|
. We consider the optimal surface quotient

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{|\partial B_{\eta}\cap B_{r}(a)|}{|\partial B_{\eta}|}\ \Big|\ \eta\in\big[|a|-r,|a|+r\big]\right\}\leq\alpha\cdot\left(\frac{r}{|a|}\right)^{2d-1}

for some constant
\alpha>0
only dependent on
N\gg 1
and
d
. Hence,

\displaystyle|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(R,M,\mathscr{A})\cap B_{r}(a)|\leq\alpha\cdot\left(\frac{r}{|a|}\right)^{2d-1}|\mathscr{C}^{2d}_{n}(R,M,\mathscr{A})\cap A_{r}(|a|)|,

which, combined with (
4.10
), yields the desired conclusion of part (i).

Part (ii) turns out to be much simpler. Since any ball
B_{r}(a)
is contained in some shifted hypercube
a+[-r,r]^{2d}
, we consider the maximal Nyquist density
\rho\big(\pazocal{C}_{n_{j}}(L_{j},M_{j},\mathscr{A}_{j}),r)
in each direction
j=1,2,\dots,2d
. In total, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(\Omega,r)\leq\prod_{j=1}^{2d}\rho\big(\pazocal{C}_{n_{j}}(L_{j},M_{j},\mathscr{A}_{j}),r),

where the desired conclusion follows once we apply the 1-dimensional result Lemma
4.3
.
∎

5.
Fractal Uncertainty Principle for Gabor Multipliers

In this section we present one simple translation of Theorem
3.1
to Gabor multipliers. Specifically, since the previous theorems are based on the Gaussian window
\phi_{0}
, we proceed with Gabor multipliers on the form (
2.10
) with
\phi_{0}
as generating function, i.e.,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi_{0}}f=|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}\rangle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}

for some subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and lattice
\Lambda
. Furthermore, since we consider an
h
-dependent family
\Omega(h)
of sets for the FUP, we also let the lattice
\Lambda
depend on the parameter
0<h\leq 1
, meaning, we let
\Lambda(h)
become sufficiently dense so to capture the fractal details of
\Omega(h)
. In particular, we consider the following condition:

Def. 5.1
.

We say that an
h
-dependent family of lattices
\Lambda(h)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
for
0<h\leq 1
satisfies condition
(H)
with constant
L>0
if the fundamental region,
A_{\Lambda(h)}
, satisfies the inclusion

\displaystyle A_{\Lambda(h)}\subseteq B_{hL}(0)\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

Utilizing condition (
H
), we formulate the FUP for Gaussian Gabor multipliers:

Theorem 5.1
.

(FUP Gaussian Gabor multipliers)
Let
0<h\leq 1
, and suppose
\Omega(h)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is an
h
-dependent family of sets which is
\nu
-porous on scales
h
to
1
. Let
\pazocal{G}_{\Lambda(h),\Omega(h)}^{\phi_{0}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Gaussian Gabor multiplier defined in (
5.1
), whose lattice
\Lambda(h)
satisfies condition
(H)
with constant
L>0
. Then there exists constants
C,\beta>0
only dependent on
\nu,L
(and
d
) such that the operator norm is bounded by

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda(h),\Omega(h)}^{\phi_{0}}\|_{\text{op}}\leq Ch^{\beta}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

First, we reformulate the problem to an estimate in the Fock space.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega^{*}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\ |\ \overline{z}\in\Omega\}
denote the complex conjugate set of
\Omega\subseteq\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. The operator norm of the Gaussian Gabor multiplier
\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi_{0}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi_{0}}\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{F\in\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ \|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{2}}=1}|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega^{*}}}|F(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}}.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality on
\ell^{2}
-sequences, the operator norm is given by

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi_{0}}\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{\|f\|_{2}=1}\langle\mathscr{G}_{\Lambda,\Omega}^{\phi_{0}}f,f\rangle=\sup_{\|f\|_{2}=1}|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega}}|\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}\rangle|^{2}.

By identity (
2.5
), each term can be expressed in terms of the Bargmann transform, namely
|\langle f,\pi(\overline{\lambda})\phi_{0}\rangle|^{2}=|\pazocal{B}f(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}}
, and since the Bargmann transform
\pazocal{B}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is an isometry onto the Fock space, the result follows.
∎

Proof.

(Theorem
5.1
) By Lemma
5.2
, we consider the sum
|A_{\Lambda}|\sum_{\lambda}|F(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}}
for any normalized
F\in\pazocal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. By the subaveraging property in Lemma
3.2
, for every
R>0

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}|F(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-e^{-\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{(\pi R^{2})^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\lambda}\int_{B_{R}(\lambda)}|F(\xi)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-e^{-\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{(\pi R^{2})^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}\gamma(R,\Lambda)\cdot\int_{\bigcup_{\lambda}B_{R}(\lambda)}|F(\xi)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)

where

\displaystyle\gamma(R,\Lambda):=\sup_{z\in A_{\Lambda}}\mathbf{card}\big\{\lambda\in\Lambda\ \big|\ |B_{R}(z)\cap(A_{\Lambda}+\lambda)|>0\big\}

takes into account the possible
overlap
between the balls
B_{R}(\lambda)
.
In particular, for
R=h
and by the condition
A_{\Lambda(h)}\subseteq B_{Lh}(0)
, there clearly exists some finite constant
C_{d}>0
dependent on

and otherwise independent of the lattice structure so that

\displaystyle\gamma(h,\Lambda(h))\leq C_{d}\frac{|B_{h}(0)|}{|A_{\Lambda(h)}|}\ \ \forall\ \ 0<h\leq 1.

In total, we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{\Lambda(h)}|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega^{*}}(h)}|F(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}}\leq C_{d}\cdot\kappa_{d}(\pi h^{2})\int_{\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\Omega^{*}(h)}}B_{h}(\lambda)}|F(\xi)|^{2}e^{-\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi),

where
\kappa_{d}(x)

\kappa_{d}(x)

Now, by definition (

Remark
.

The question immediately arises whether the Gabor systems
\{\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda(h)}
with
\Lambda(h)
satisfying condition (

0<h\leq 1
or for sufficiently small

. In

, by the results obtained independently by Lyubarskii
[

]
and Seip and Wallsten
[
23
]
,
[

]
, we have a simple density criterion. Namely, that the system
\{\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a Gabor frame if and only if the fundamental region satisfies
|A_{\Lambda}|<1
. In higher dimensions

, characterizations of lattices
\Lambda
that yield Gaussian Gabor frames becomes much more intricate, which has been studied in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
. In particular, the density criterion does not translate, e.g., as shown in
[

]

does not generate a Gaussian Gabor frame even though

. Further, a sufficient condition is formulated in
[
21
]
Theorem 1.2 combining a density criterion with the notion of transcendental lattices, which, as remarked by the authors, represents a large family of lattices in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Nonetheless, our estimates of the Gabor multiplier are not reliant on the operator being associated to a Gabor frame.

We conclude this section with a simple example of Gabor multipliers based on Cantor sets, which also illustrates an alternative approach to choosing the lattice restriction
\Lambda_{\Omega}
.

Example 5.1
.

(Cantor set) For simplicity, we let
d=1
and consider the symmetric Cartesian product of Cantor iterates
\Omega:=\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})\times\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})
. For this case one obvious choice of lattices are square lattices
\Lambda=a\left(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\right)
with density
a\sim LM^{-n}
. Unsurprisingly, the restriction
\Lambda_{\Omega}
closely resembles the Cartesian product of scaled discrete Cantor iterates
\Omega^{(d)}:=[L\cdot\pazocal{C}_{n}^{(d)}(M,\mathscr{A})]^{2}
. Thus, for Gabor multipliers localizing on such Cartesian products, it seems more natural to consider sampled points directly from the already available discrete set, i.e., we consider the operator

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{\phi_{0}}(\Omega^{(d)})f:=(LM^{-n})^{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\left[L\cdot\pazocal{C}^{(d)}(M,\mathscr{A})\right]^{2}}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}\rangle\pi(\lambda)\phi_{0}.

In order to estimate the operator norm, we follow the same procedure as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
. After one
\frac{1}{2}LM^{-n}
-thickening and then utilizing inequality (
3.12
), we obtain for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
r>0
that

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{\phi_{0}}(\Omega^{(d)})f\|_{2}\leq\frac{\kappa_{1}\big(\frac{\pi}{4}(LM^{-n})^{2}\big)}{1-e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi r^{2}}}\rho\left(\Omega^{(d)}+B_{\frac{1}{2}LM^{-n}}(0),r\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

By definition of the discrete and ”continuous” Cantor iterate, the Nyquist density must satisfy
\rho(\Omega^{(d)}+B_{\frac{1}{2}LM^{-n}}(0),r)\leq\rho(\Omega,r)
, so the operator norm is in turn bounded by

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{G}_{\phi_{0}}(\Omega^{(d)})\|_{\text{op}}\leq\frac{\kappa_{1}\big(\frac{\pi}{4}(LM^{-n})^{2}\big)}{1-e^{\frac{1}{2}\pi r^{2}}}\rho(\Omega,r).

If we now suppose the length
L
depends on the iterates
n
according to condition (
I_{M}
), we simply apply the estimate for the Nyquist density in Lemma
4.4
to retrieve the same asymptotic estimate for the operator norm as in Theorem
4.1
.

Appendix A
Complex interpolation in Fock space

We prove the following inequality between the Fock spaces
\pazocal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
for
p\geq 1
.

Lemma A.1
.

For any measurable subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
and any
p\geq 1
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}}\leq\sup_{F\in\pazocal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}}{\|F\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}}.

The proof combines central results from
complex interpolation
.
An introduction to complex interpolation, based on Hadamard’s three line theorem, can be found in
[
28
]
Chapter 2. We follow the notation in
[
28
]
and let
X_{\theta}=[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}
for
\theta\in[0,1]
denote the complex interpolation space between two (compatible) Banach spaces
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
.
To begin with, we present a general result regarding linear operators between interpolation spaces and the associated operator norm (from
[
28
]
Theorem 2.4 (c) combined with subsequent remark).

Theorem A.2
.

Suppose
X_{0},X_{1}
and
Y_{0},Y_{1}
are compatible pairs of Banach spaces, and suppose the mapping

\displaystyle T:X_{0}+X_{1}\to Y_{0}+Y_{1}

is bounded, linear such that
T:X_{k}\to Y_{k}
is bounded with norm
\|T\|_{k}\leq M_{k}
for
k=0,1
. Then the mapping
T
satisfies
T:[X_{0},X_{1}]_{\theta}\to[Y_{0},Y_{1}]_{\theta}
with norm estimate
\|T\|_{\theta}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}
for all
\theta\in[0,1]
.

In order to relate the above theorem to our context, we need to consider interpolation between weighted
L^{p}
-spaces and between Fock spaces. The next statements are all found in
[
29
]
Chapter 2.4, formulated for spaces over
\mathbb{C}
but easily generalized to
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. First, we consider the
Stein-Weiss
interpolation theorem, first published in
[
26
]
, for weighted
L^{p}
-spaces.

Theorem A.3
.

Suppose
w,w_{0}
and
w_{1}
are positive weight functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Then for any
1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have that

\displaystyle\left[L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{C}^{d},w_{0}\mathrm{d}A),L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{d},w_{1}\mathrm{d}A)\right]_{\theta}=L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d},w\mathrm{d}A),

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\ \ \text{and }\ w^{\frac{1}{p}}=w_{0}^{\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}}w_{1}^{\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}}.

In particular, for the
L^{p}
-spaces with Gaussian measures,
\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, we obtain:

Corollary A.1
.

For any
1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have that

\displaystyle\left[\pazocal{L}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\pazocal{L}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right]_{\theta}=\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ \ \text{where}\ \ \frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

For Fock spaces we have the following interpolation:

Theorem A.4
.

For any
1\leq p_{0}\leq p_{1}\leq\infty
and
\theta\in[0,1]
, we have that

\displaystyle\left[\pazocal{F}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\pazocal{F}^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right]_{\theta}=\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ \ \text{where}\ \ \frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

With these interpolation results, we are ready to prove Lemma
A.1
.

Proof.

(Lemma
A.1
)
Note that the statement is equivalent to the linear mapping

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}:\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\to\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ \ F\mapsto F\cdot\chi_{\Omega}

satisfying the operator norm inequality
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{p}}^{p}\leq\|T_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}
for
p\geq 1
. Therefore, we consider the mapping
T_{\Omega}
between the spaces

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}:\pazocal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})+\pazocal{F}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\to\pazocal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d})+\pazocal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),

which is clearly bounded. By Theorem
A.2
,

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}:\left[\pazocal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\pazocal{F}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right]_{\theta}\to\left[\pazocal{L}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\pazocal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right]_{\theta},

and the associated operator norm is bounded by
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{1}}^{1-\theta}\|T_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{\infty}}^{\theta}
. By Corollary
A.1
and Theorem
A.4
, these interpolation spaces correspond to
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\pazocal{L}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, respectively, with
p^{-1}=1-\theta
. In addition,
\|T_{\Omega}\|_{\pazocal{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1
, from which the norm estimate readily follows.
∎

Appendix B
Omitted proof: Simple porosity estimate of Cantor sets

We shall prove the following simple porosity estimate for the
n
-iterate Cantor set in
1
-dimension.

Lemma B.1
.

The
n
-iterate Cantor set
\pazocal{C}_{n}(L,M,\mathscr{A})
with base
M>1
and alphabet size
|\mathscr{A}|<M
, based in the interval
[0,L]
, is
\nu
-porous on scales
LM^{-n+1}
to
\infty
, with any
\nu\leq M^{-2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
L=1
. Consider an interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of size
M^{-m+1}\leq|I|\leq M^{-m+2}
for some integer
1\leq m\leq n
. Suppose first that the intersection
I\cap\pazocal{C}_{m}(\dots)
does
not
form an interval. Then, by the Cantor set construction, there exists an interval
J\subseteq I
of size
|J|=M^{-m}\geq M^{-2}|I|
such that
|J\cap\pazocal{C}_{m}(\dots)|=0
. Conversely, suppose the intersection forms an interval, effectively dividing the remainder
I\setminus\pazocal{C}_{m}(\dots)
into two intervals
J_{1},J_{2}
. Again, by the Cantor set construction, we have the upper bound
|I\cap\pazocal{C}_{m}(\dots)|\leq|\mathscr{A}|M^{-m}\leq(M-1)M^{-m}
, so that
\max\{|J_{1}|,|J_{2}|\}\geq\frac{1}{2}(|I|-(M-1)M^{-m})
. Hence, we conclude that there exists an interval
J\subseteq I
with
|I\cap\pazocal{C}_{n}(\dots)|=0
of size

\displaystyle|J|=\left(\frac{|I|-(M-1)M^{-m}}{2|I|}\right)|I|\geq M^{-2}|I|.

Since
m
is arbitrary and
\pazocal{C}_{n}(\dots)\subseteq\pazocal{C}_{m}(\dots)
, the statement holds for all intervals
I
with
M^{-n+1}\leq|I|\leq M
. For
|I|\geq M
the statement becomes trivial as
\pazocal{C}_{n}(\dots)\subset[0,1]
.
∎

Appendix C
Subaveraging in Fock space

We shall generalize the subaveraging statement made in Lemma 2.32 in
[
29
]
for the Fock space over
\mathbb{C}
to the space over
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, namely:

Lemma C.1
.

For any
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and any point
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we have for all
R>0
that

(C.1)

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

In one dimension, the proof is based on the subaveraging property of subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{C}
. More precisely, for
u:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
subharmonic, we have that

\displaystyle u(z)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(re^{i\theta}+z)\mathrm{d}\theta\ \ \forall\ r>0.

In
d
-dimensions, we instead consider plurisubharmonic functions.

Def. C.1
.

(Plurisubharmonic)

Let
X
be a domain in
\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
. We say that a function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is plurisubharmonic if

(a)

u
is upper semi-continuous, and

(b)

for every
z,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
the function
\tau\mapsto u(\xi\tau+z)
is subharmonic in the open subset of
\mathbb{C}
where it is defined.

Many well-known examples of plurisubharmonic functions are based on the holomorphic functions. In particular, if
F
is entire, then
|F|^{p}
is a plurisubharmonic function in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
for every
p>0
. Hence, by point (b) in Def.
C.1
, every
F\in\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
satisfies

(C.2)

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|F|^{p}(\xi re^{i\theta}+z)\mathrm{d}\theta\ \ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\ \ \text{and}\ \ r>0.

Notice that since left-hand side of (
C.2
) is independent of
\xi
(and
r
), we may multiply both sides by a positive function of
\xi
, integrate with respect to
\xi
and keep the inequality
intact
.

Introduce the notation

\displaystyle\partial B_{1}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\ \big|\ |z|=1\big\}

for the
(2d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere, and let
|\partial B_{1}|
denote the associated surface area. Further, let
\mathrm{d}S
denote the surface measure on
\partial B_{1}
.
To begin with, we show that the point evaluation
|F(z)|^{2}
is bounded by an average over the sphere.

Lemma C.2
.

For every entire function
F
, we have that

(C.3)

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{|\partial B_{1}|}\int_{|\xi|=1}|F|^{p}(\xi r+z)\mathrm{d}S(\xi)\ \ \forall\ r>0.

Proof.

Consider the equivalence relation
\sim
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, where

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\sim\xi_{2}\iff\exists\ \ \theta\in[0,2\pi]\ \text{such that}\ \xi_{1}=e^{i\theta}\xi_{2}.

This induces the quotient space
\partial B_{1}/\sim
of ”remaining angles” over the unit sphere, with measure
\mathrm{d}\Phi
such that
\mathrm{d}S=\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\Phi
and

\displaystyle|\partial B_{1}|=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{\partial B_{1}/\sim}\mathrm{d}\Phi=2\pi\int_{\partial B_{1}/\sim}\mathrm{d}\Phi.

With the above decomposition, we may integrate both sides of (
C.2
) over the space
\partial B_{1}/\sim
, which leaves the desired result.
∎

Proceeding, we relate subaverage over the sphere to an subaverage over the ball
B_{R}(0)
.

Lemma C.3
.

For every entire function
F
, we have for all
R>0
that

(C.4)

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}\leq\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)^{d}\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|F|^{p}(z+\xi)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

Proof.

For any
\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, we have the basic decomposition
\omega=r\xi
for
|\xi|=1
and
r=|\omega|
, so that the volume measure can be expressed
\mathrm{d}A(\omega)=r^{2d-1}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}S(\xi)
. Since we consider the Gaussian measure of the Fock space
\pazocal{F}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, we integrate both sides of inequality (
C.3
) against
e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi r^{2}}r^{2d-1}\mathrm{d}r
. For the right-hand side, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\partial B_{1}|}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{|\xi|=1}|F|^{p}(\xi r+z)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi r^{2}}r^{2d-1}\mathrm{d}S(\xi)\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{1}|}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|F|^{p}(\omega+z)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\omega|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\omega).

While for the left-hand side, using the formula

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}r^{k}e^{-r}\mathrm{d}r=k!\left(1-e^{-L}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{L}{j!}\right)\ \ \text{for }k=0,1,2,\dots,

we obtain

\displaystyle|F(z)|^{p}\int_{0}^{R}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi r^{2}}r^{2d-1}\mathrm{d}r=|F(z)|^{p}\frac{(d-1)!}{2}\left(\frac{2}{p\pi}\right)^{d}\left(1-e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{\left(\frac{p}{2}\pi R^{2}\right)^{j}}{j!}\right).

Since the surface area of the
(2d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere is given by
|\partial B_{1}|=2\pi^{d}/(d-1)!
, inequality (
C.4
) follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
C.1
.

Proof.

(Lemma
C.1
)
Consider the integral over the ball centered at
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

(C.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(z)}|F(\xi)|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|F|^{p}(z+\xi)e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z+\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|F(z+\xi)e^{-\pi\xi\cdot\overline{z}}|^{p}e^{-\frac{p}{2}\pi|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}A(\xi).

Define
G_{z}(\xi):=F(z+\xi)e^{-\pi\xi\cdot\overline{z}}
, which is an entire function with respect to
\xi
. Since
G_{z}(0)=F(z)
, the inequality follows once we apply Lemma
C.3
to
|G_{z}(0)|^{p}
, combined with (
C.5
).
∎

The blow-up analysis on 𝐁 2 ( 1 ) \mathbf{B}_{2}^{(1)} affine Toda system: Local mass and affine Weyl group
Leilei Cui, Jun-cheng Wei, Wen Yang, Lei Zhang

Abstract
. It has been established that the local mass of blow-up solutions to Toda systems associated with the simple Lie algebras 𝐀 n , 𝐁 n , 𝐂 n \mathbf{A}_{n},~\mathbf{B}_{n},~\mathbf{C}_{n} and 𝐆 2 \mathbf{G}_{2} can be represented by a finite Weyl group. In particular, at each blow-up point, after a sequence of bubbling steps (via scaling) is performed, the transformation of the local mass at each step corresponds to the action of an element in the Weyl group. In this article, we present the results in the same spirit for the affine 𝐁 2 ( 1 ) \mathbf{B}_{2}^{(1)} Toda system with singularities. Compared with the Toda system with simple Lie algebras, the computation of local masses is more challenging due to the infinite number of elements of the affine Weyl group of type 𝐁 2 ( 1 ) \mathbf{B}_{2}^{(1)} . In order to give an explicit expression for the local mass formula we introduce two free integers and write down all the possibilities into 8 types. This shows a striking difference to previous results on Toda systems with simple Lie algebras. The main result of this article seems to provide the first major advance in understanding the relation between the blow-up analysis of affine Toda system and the affine Weyl group of the associated Lie algebras.



Key Words:
Affine Toda system, Pohozaev identity, Liouville equation, sinh-Gordon equation, Affine Weyl group.

Introduction and main results

The main focus of this article is to derive the relation between the quantization result from the blow up analysis and the associated algebraic structure for the following system

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}e^{u_{j}}=4\pi\gamma_{i}\delta_{0}\quad&\mbox{in}\quad B_{1}(0)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad i=1,2,3,\\
u_{1}+u_{2}+2u_{3}=0\quad&\mathrm{in}\quad B_{1}(0),\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{i}>-1
for

\delta_{0}

(1.2)

\mathbf{B}_{2}^{(1)}

\Delta\eta+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\omega+\eta}-\frac{1}{2}e^{-\omega-\eta}=2\pi(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})\delta_{0}.\end{cases}

G=\langle\mathfrak{R}_{1},~\mathfrak{R}_{2},~\mathfrak{R}_{3}\rangle

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{2}=\mathfrak{R}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{1},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{R}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{2}|=2,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{R}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{3})^{2}=(\mathfrak{R}_{3}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{1})^{2},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{R}_{1}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{3}|=4,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{R}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{3})^{2}=(\mathfrak{R}_{3}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{2})^{2},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{R}_{2}\circ\mathfrak{R}_{3}|=4,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{1}^{2}=\mathfrak{R}_{2}^{2}=\mathfrak{R}_{3}^{2}=\mathfrak{I},

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{R}_{i}|=2,~i=1,2,3,

{\mathfrak{R}_{12}=\mathfrak{R}_{1}\mathfrak{R}_{2},~\mathfrak{R}_{13}=\left(\mathfrak{R}_{1}\mathfrak{R}_{3}\right)^{2},~\mathfrak{R}_{23}=\left(\mathfrak{R}_{2}\mathfrak{R}_{3}\right)^{2}.}

To state the main result, we formulate the problem as follows.

Let
{\bf u}^{k}=(u_{1}^{k},u_{2}^{k},u_{3}^{k})
be a sequence of blow up solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying the following conditions

(1.10)

\begin{cases}(i):&0~\mbox{is the only blow up point of }{\bf u}^{k}~\mbox{in}~B_{1}(0),~\mbox{i.e.},\\
&\max\limits_{i}\sup\limits_{x\in B_{1}(0)}{u_{i}^{k}(x)+2\gamma_{i}\log|x|}\to+\infty~\mbox{and}~\max\limits_{i}\sup\limits_{K\subseteq B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}}u_{i}^{k}\leq C(K),\\
(ii):&|u_{i}^{k}(x)-u_{i}^{k}(y)|\leq C,~\forall x,y~\mbox{on}~\partial B_{1}(0),~i=1,2,3,\\
(iii):&\int_{B_{1}(0)}e^{u_{i}^{k}}\mathrm{d}x\leq C,~i=1,2,3.\end{cases}

For this sequence of blow up solutions we define the local mass by

(1.11)

\sigma_{i}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}(0)}e^{u_{i}^{k}}\mathrm{d}x,\quad i=1,2,3.

We shall see in next section that
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
always satisfies the Pohozaev identity

(1.12)

(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3})^{2}+(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})^{2}=4(\mu_{1}\sigma_{1}+\mu_{2}\sigma_{2}+2\mu_{3}\sigma_{3}),\quad\text{where}~\mu_{i}=1+\gamma_{i},~i=1,2,3.

For a given
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
we introduce the set
\Gamma(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
for the local mass quantity
\bm{\sigma}
via the following way:

(i)

{\bf 0}=(0,0,0)\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
.

(ii)

If
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
, then
\mathfrak{R}\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
for any
\mathfrak{R}\in G
satisfying (
1.8
) and (
1.9
), where each generator
\mathfrak{R}_{i},~i=1,2,3
sends
\bm{\sigma}
to
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}
with

(\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma})_{j}=\begin{cases}4\mu_{i}-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\sigma_{j}+\sigma_{i},\quad&\mbox{if}~j=i,\\
\sigma_{j},\quad&\mbox{if}~j\neq i.\end{cases}

Here,
(a_{ij})_{3\times 3}
is given in (
1.2
).

It is easy to see that for any
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
,

is a degree one polynomial of
\mu_{1},~\mu_{2}
and
\mu_{3}
. Now we are able to state the first result of this article

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2},u^{k}_{3})
be a sequence of solutions of system (
1.1
)
satisfying (

such that

{\sigma}_{i}=\hat{\sigma}_{i}+4m_{i},~m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},~i=1,2,3.

In section
3

in some concrete way. We have already pointed out

satisfies (
1.12

\Gamma(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu}):=\left\{\bm{\sigma}~\big|\big.~\bm{\sigma}~\mbox{satisfies}~\eqref{1.12}~\mbox{and}~\sigma_{i}=4\sum_{j=1}^{3}n_{ij}\mu_{j},~n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i=1,2,3\right\}.

In fact, we can give explicit formulas for the coefficients

Theorem
1.1
has several important applications for the affine Toda system defined on compact Riemann surface. For example, let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface,
\Delta_{g}
be the Beltrami operator on
M
, we consider a sequence of solutions
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2},u^{k}_{3})
to the following system

(1.14)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}u^{k}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\rho^{k}_{j}\left(\frac{h^{k}_{j}e^{u^{k}_{j}}}{\int_{M}h^{k}_{j}e^{u^{k}_{j}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}}-\frac{1}{|M|}\right)=4\pi{\sum\limits_{p\in S}\alpha_{p,i}\left(\delta_{p}-\frac{1}{|M|}\right)},\quad i=1,2,3,\\
u^{k}_{1}+u^{k}_{2}+2u^{k}_{3}=0\quad\text{on}\quad M,\end{cases}

where
h^{k}_{1},h^{k}_{2},h^{k}_{3}
are positive and smooth functions on
M
with their
C^{3}(M)
norm being uniformly bounded in
M
,
S
is a finite set of
M
,
\alpha_{p,i}>-1
is the strength of the Dirac mass
\delta_{p}
, and
\bm{\rho}^{k}=(\rho^{k}_{1},\rho^{k}_{2},\rho^{k}_{3})
is a sequence of constant vectors with nonnegative components satisfying
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\bm{\rho}^{k}=(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3})
. As the Toda system with simple Lie algebra and Liouville equation, (
1.14
) can be regarded as a natural extension of the local version (
1.1
) to the case on Riemannian manifold. Equation (
1.14
) together with (
1.1
) are closely related to the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations (
[
19
]
) and the minimal tori in
\mathbb{S}^{4}
(
[
18
]
). We notice that (
1.14
) remains the same if
u_{i}
is replaced by
u_{i}+c_{i}
for any constant
c_{i}
. Thus it is reasonable to assume that each component of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
is in

\mathring{H}^{1}(M)=\left\{f\in H^{1}(M)~\big|~\int_{M}f\mathrm{d}V_{g}=0\right\}.

Simultaneously, from the equation
u_{1}^{k}+u_{2}^{k}+2u_{3}^{k}=0
one can easily verify that

(1.15)

\alpha_{p,1}+\alpha_{p,2}+2\alpha_{p,3}=0,\quad\forall p\in S.

By introducing the following Green function on Riemann surface

\Delta G(x,p)+\left(\delta_{p}-\frac{1}{|M|}\right)=0,\quad\int_{M}G(x,p)\mathrm{d}V_{g}=0,

we decompose

u_{i}^{k}(x)=\tilde{u}_{i}^{k}(x)-4\pi\sum\limits_{p\in S}\alpha_{p,i}G(x,p),\quad i=1,2,3.

Then (

) can be rewritten as

(1.16)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{g}\tilde{u}_{i}^{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\rho^{k}_{j}\left(\frac{\tilde{h}^{k}_{j}e^{\tilde{u}^{k}_{j}}}{\int_{M}\tilde{h}^{k}_{j}e^{\tilde{u}^{k}_{j}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}}-\frac{1}{|M|}\right)=0,\quad i=1,2,3,\\
\tilde{u}^{k}_{1}+\tilde{u}^{k}_{2}+2\tilde{u}^{k}_{3}=0\quad\text{on}\quad M,\end{cases}

where

\tilde{h}_{i}^{k}=h_{i}^{k}\exp\left(-4\pi\sum\limits_{p\in S}\alpha_{p,i}G(x,p)\right),\quad i=1,2,3.

Let
\mu_{p,i}=1+\alpha_{p,i}
for
i=1,2,3
and denote

\Gamma_{i}=\left\{2\pi\sum\limits_{p\in R}\sigma_{p,i}+8\pi m_{i}~\big|~(\sigma_{p,1},\sigma_{p,2},\sigma_{p,3})\in\Gamma(\mu_{p,1},\mu_{p,2},\mu_{p,3}),~R\subseteq S,~m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

The second result in this article is the following a priori estimate for the system (
1.16
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\rho_{i}\notin\Gamma_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\rho_{i}
such that for any solution
(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{u}_{2},\tilde{u}_{3})
of system (
1.16
) in
\mathring{H}^{1}(M)
,

|\tilde{u}_{i}(x)|\leq C,~\forall x\in M,\quad i=1,2,3.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we shall determine the local mass at each blow-up point of solutions
\mathbf{\tilde{u}}^{k}
. Here the blow up point

is defined by

\exists\mbox{~a sequence }p^{k}\to p~\mbox{such that }\max_{i}\tilde{u}_{i}^{k}(p^{k})\to+\infty.

The set
B
of all blow up points is called the blow up set. For each point

\sigma_{i}(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\rho^{k}_{i}\int_{B(p,r)}\tilde{h}^{k}_{i}e^{\tilde{u}^{k}_{i}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}}{\int_{M}\tilde{h}^{k}_{i}e^{\tilde{u}^{k}_{i}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}},\quad i=1,2,3.

By Theorem

. Together with the assumption

\left(\mathfrak{R}_{2}\bm{\sigma}^{(L)}\right)_{2}=\left(-4n_{21}^{(L)}+8n_{31}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{1}+\left(4-4n_{22}^{(L)}+8n_{32}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{2}+\left(-4n_{23}^{(L)}+8n_{33}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{3},

and

\left(\mathfrak{R}_{3}\bm{\sigma}^{(L)}\right)_{3}=\left(4n_{11}^{(L)}+4n_{21}^{(L)}-4n_{31}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{1}+\left(4n_{12}^{(L)}+4n_{22}^{(L)}-4n_{32}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{2}+\left(4+4n_{13}^{(L)}+4n_{23}^{(L)}-4n_{33}^{(L)}\right)\mu_{3}.

Obviously, the coefficients of
\mu_{i}
in the above components are multiples of
4
. Thus we shall prove that these coefficients are nonnegative. We divide the proof into two steps as follows.

Step 1.
We claim

(3.1)

4-4n_{11}^{(L)}+8n_{31}^{(L)}\geq 0,\quad-4n_{12}^{(L)}+8n_{32}^{(L)}\geq 0,\quad-4n_{13}^{(L)}+8n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 0,

and

(3.2)

-4n_{21}^{(L)}+8n_{31}^{(L)}\geq 0,\quad 4-4n_{22}^{(L)}+8n_{32}^{(L)}\geq 0,\quad-4n_{23}^{(L)}+8n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 0.

Since the proof for (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are almost the same, we shall only give details for the first one. Using (
2.4
) and substituting the expression of
\bm{\sigma}^{(L)}
(or
\mathfrak{R}_{1}\bm{\sigma}^{(L)}
) into (
2.4
), we can obtain the following three equations by comparing the coefficients of
\mu_{j}^{2},~j=1,2,3
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left(n_{11}^{(L)}-n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{21}^{(L)}-n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=n_{11}^{(L)},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left(n_{12}^{(L)}-n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{22}^{(L)}-n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=n_{22}^{(L)},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left(n_{13}^{(L)}-n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{23}^{(L)}-n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2n_{33}^{(L)}.

By (
3.3
), we can regard
n_{11}^{(L)}
as a solution of the following quadratic equation

(3.6)

x^{2}-\left(2n_{31}^{(L)}+1\right)x+\left(n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{21}^{(L)}-n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}=0.

Since
\left(n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{21}^{(L)}-n_{31}^{(L)}\right)^{2}\geq 0
, then the other solution of (
3.6
) satisfies

2n_{31}^{(L)}+1-n_{11}^{(L)}\geq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~4-4n_{11}^{(L)}+8n_{31}^{(L)}\geq 0.

In a similar manner, by (
3.4
) we can view
n_{12}^{(L)}
as a solution of the following quadratic equation

(3.7)

x^{2}-2n_{32}^{(L)}x+\left(n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{22}^{(L)}-n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{22}^{(L)}=0.

It is not difficult to check that

\left(n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{22}^{(L)}-n_{32}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{22}^{(L)}\geq 0.

Hence, the other solution of (
3.7
) satisfies

2n_{32}^{(L)}-n_{12}^{(L)}\geq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~-4n_{12}^{(L)}+8n_{32}^{(L)}\geq 0.

As
n_{11}^{(L)}
and
n_{12}^{(L)}
, by (
3.5
) we can treat
n_{13}^{(L)}
as a solution of the following quadratic equation

(3.8)

x^{2}-2n_{33}^{(L)}x+\left(n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{23}^{(L)}-n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-2n_{33}^{(L)}=0.

Notice that if

(3.9)

\left(n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{23}^{(L)}-n_{33}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-2n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 0,

then the other solution of (
3.8
) satisfies

2n_{33}^{(L)}-n_{13}^{(L)}\geq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~-4n_{13}^{(L)}+8n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 0.

Therefore, (
3.1
) is proved. So it remains to verify (
3.9
). For the cases of
n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 2
and
n_{33}^{(L)}=0

n_{33}^{(L)}=1

\left(n_{23}^{(L)}-1\right)^{2}\geq 1
. Indeed, comparing the coefficients of
\mu_{2}\mu_{3}
in the Pohozaev identity, we find
n_{23}^{(L)}
is even. Hence (
3.1
) is proved.

Step 2.
We claim that

(3.10)

4n_{11}^{(L)}+4n_{21}^{(L)}-4n_{31}^{(L)}\geq 0,~4n_{12}^{(L)}+4n_{22}^{(L)}-4n_{32}^{(L)}\geq 0,~4+4n_{13}^{(L)}+4n_{23}^{(L)}-4n_{33}^{(L)}\geq 0.

As
Step 1
, we can apply (
2.4
) to get that

are the solution to the following three equations respectively

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2x^{2}-2\left(n_{11}^{(L)}+n_{21}^{(L)}\right)x+\left(n_{11}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{21}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{11}^{(L)}=0,\\
2x^{2}-2\left(n_{12}^{(L)}+n_{22}^{(L)}\right)x+\left(n_{12}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{22}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{22}^{(L)}=0,\\

\left(n_{11}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{21}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{11}^{(L)}\geq 0,~\left(n_{12}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{22}^{(L)}\right)^{2}-n_{22}^{(L)}\geq 0,~\left(n_{13}^{(L)}\right)^{2}+\left(n_{23}^{(L)}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

then by the same argument of

Hence (

) is proved and we finish the proof.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
be defined as

(3.11)

\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})=\left\{\bm{\sigma}~\big|~\bm{\sigma}~\text{satisfies the Pohozaev identity~ \eqref{CYL-Ch-2-Eq-Pohozaev-identity}},~\sigma_{i}=4\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}n_{ij}\mu_{j},~n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i=1,2,3\right\}.

Then

\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma(\bm{\mu}).

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\Gamma(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
by Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.3
. Thus it remains to show
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
. For any
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
, by Proposition
3.1
,
\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}
satisfies the Pohozaev identity (
2.4
). So we shall prove

(3.12)

\text{if}~\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu}),~\text{then}~\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu}),\quad i=1,2,3.

Let
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
,
{\sigma}_{i}=4\sum_{j=1}^{3}n_{ij}\mu_{j},~i=1,2,3
, where
n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then by the same arguments of Proposition
3.3
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} 4-4n_{11}+8n_{31}\geq 0,\\
-4n_{12}+8n_{32}\geq 0,\\
-4n_{13}+8n_{33}\geq 0,\end{aligned}\right.\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} -4n_{21}+8n_{31}\geq 0,\\
4-4n_{22}+8n_{32}\geq 0,\\
-4n_{23}+8n_{33}\geq 0,\end{aligned}\right.~\text{and}~\left\{\begin{aligned} 4n_{11}+4n_{21}-4n_{31}\geq 0,\\
4n_{12}+4n_{22}-4n_{32}\geq 0,\\
4+4n_{13}+4n_{23}-4n_{33}\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

This completes the proof of (
3.12
).

Next we define a partial order
\preceq
in
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
, we say

\bm{\sigma}_{1}\preceq\bm{\sigma}_{2}~\text{provided}~(\bm{\sigma}_{1})_{i}\leq(\bm{\sigma}_{2})_{i},\quad i=1,2,3.

Then there must hold

\text{either}~~~\bm{\sigma}\preceq\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}~~~\text{or}~~~\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}\preceq\bm{\sigma},\quad i=1,2,3.

For any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
, let

\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}:=\left\{\mathfrak{R}_{i_{1}}\mathfrak{R}_{i_{2}}\cdots\mathfrak{R}_{i_{n}}\bm{\sigma}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i_{k}\in\{1,2,3\},~1\leq k\leq n\right\}.

Thus for any
\bm{\sigma}_{1},\bm{\sigma}_{2}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
, we have either

(3.13)

\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{1}}=\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{2}}~~~\text{or}~~~\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{1}}\cap\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}_{2}}=\emptyset.

Now we can prove that
\bm{0}=(0,0,0)\in\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}
for any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
. An element
\hat{\bm{\sigma}}\in\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}
is minimal if
\widetilde{\bm{\sigma}}\preceq\hat{\bm{\sigma}}
for some
\widetilde{\bm{\sigma}}\in\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}
, then
\widetilde{\bm{\sigma}}=\hat{\bm{\sigma}}
. By well-ordering Principle, we conclude that
\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}
has a local minimal element
\bm{\sigma}_{0}=(\sigma_{1,0},\sigma_{2,0},\sigma_{3,0})
, i.e.,

\bm{\sigma}_{0}\preceq\mathfrak{R}_{i}\bm{\sigma}_{0},\quad i=1,2,3.

Therefore, we have

4\mu_{i}-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\sigma_{j,0}\geq 0,\quad i=1,2,3.

On the other hand, since
\bm{\sigma}_{0}
satisfies the Pohozaev identity (
2.4
), we get

0\leq\sigma_{1,0}\left(2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{1j}\sigma_{j,0}-4\mu_{1}\right)+\sigma_{2,0}\left(2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{2j}\sigma_{j,0}-4\mu_{2}\right)+2\sigma_{3,0}\left(2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{3j}\sigma_{j,0}-4\mu_{3}\right)\leq 0,

which implies that
\sigma_{i,0}=0,~i=1,2,3
. Otherwise, we must have

\displaystyle 2\left(\sigma_{1,0}-\sigma_{3,0}\right)=4\mu_{1},~2\left(\sigma_{2,0}-\sigma_{3,0}\right)=4\mu_{2},~2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1,0}-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2,0}+\sigma_{3,0}\right)=4\mu_{3},

then
2\mu_{1}+2\mu_{2}+4\mu_{3}=0
, a contradiction. Hence
\bm{0}\in\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}
. By (
3.13
), we obtain
\Gamma_{\bm{\sigma}}=\Gamma_{\bm{0}}
for any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
. This amounts to say that

\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma(\bm{\mu}).

The proof of Proposition
3.4
is complete.
∎

Proof.

Let

\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})=\left\{\bm{\sigma}~|~\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{where}~\mathbb{F}~\text{admits}~\mathbb{F}^{(\ell)},~\ell=1,\cdots,8\right\}.

Our aim is to show
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})=\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
.

On one hand, for any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})
with
\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(\ell)}\bm{\mu}^{t}
for some
\ell\in\{1,\cdots,8\}
, one can directly check that
\bm{\sigma}
satisfies the Pohozaev identity (
2.4
) and
f_{ij}^{(\ell)}
are multiples of

. Hence
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
by the definition of
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
in (
3.11
).

On the other hand, we can prove
\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})
by induction. For any
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
,
\sigma_{i}=4\sum_{j=1}^{3}n_{ij}\mu_{j}
,

. Let

(3.17)

\frac{1}{4}\left(N({\sigma}_{1})-N({\sigma}_{3})\right)=m_{1},\quad\frac{1}{4}\left(N({\sigma}_{2})-N({\sigma}_{3})\right)=m_{2}.

Suppose
\bm{\sigma}
is of type
(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})
, where
(m_{1},m_{2})\equiv(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})
(mod
4
). We recall the binary tree diagram given in the proof of Proposition
3.3
and directly check that

\displaystyle\bm{0}=(0,0,0)~\text{is of type}~(0,0),

\displaystyle\bm{0}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(1)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(0,0);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(4\mu_{1},0,0)~\text{is of type}~(1,0),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(3)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(1,0);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(0,4\mu_{2},0)~\text{is of type}~(0,1),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(2)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(0,1);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(0,0,4\mu_{3})~\text{is of type}~(3,3),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(8)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(-1,-1);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(4\mu_{1},4\mu_{2},0)~\text{is of type}~(1,1),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(4)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(1,1);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(4\mu_{1},0,4\mu_{1}+4\mu_{3})~\text{is of type}~(3,2),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(7)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(-1,-2);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(6)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(-2,-1);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(4\mu_{1}+8\mu_{3},0,4\mu_{3})~\text{is of type}~(2,3),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(6)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(2,-1);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(0,4\mu_{2}+8\mu_{3},4\mu_{3})~\text{is of type}~(3,2),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(7)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(-1,2);

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}=(4\mu_{1},4\mu_{2},4\mu_{1}+4\mu_{2}+4\mu_{3})~\text{is of type}~(2,2),

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}^{t}=\mathbb{F}^{(5)}\bm{\mu}^{t}~\text{for}~(m_{1},m_{2})=(-2,-2).

This implies that
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})
for those

in the
\mathcal{L}
-th level with
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{N}(\bm{\mu})
,

. In particular, all possibilities

. Suppose that
\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}(\bm{\mu})

In Lemma

, we assume that

(i)

The Harnack-type inequality holds

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu_{i}=1+\gamma_{i}
. Assume that (i) and (ii) hold.

(a)

If
u^{k}_{i}
has slow decay on
\partial B(0,s_{k})
for some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, then

2\mu_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\sigma_{j}>0.

(b)

At least one component of
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2},u^{k}_{3})
has fast decay on
\partial B(0,s_{k})
.

Proof.

(a) Performing the following scaling for the system (
4.1
)

v^{k}_{i}(y)=u^{k}_{i}(s_{k}y)+2\log{s_{k}}\quad\text{for}\quad y\in B_{2}(0),\quad i=1,2,3,

gives

\displaystyle\Delta v^{k}_{i}(y)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}e^{v^{k}_{j}(y)}

\displaystyle=4\pi\gamma_{i}\delta_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2}(0),\quad i=1,2,3.

If there is at least one component of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
has slow decay on
\partial B(0,s_{k})
, we denote
J
the set of consecutive indices such that
u^{k}_{i}

(i\in J)
has slow decay on
\partial B(0,s_{k})
, then
v^{k}_{i}

(i\in J)
converges to the solution
v_{i}

(i\in J)
of a Toda system (or Liouville equation). Precisely, one of the following alternatives holds:

Case (1).
If
J=\{i\}
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Then
v^{k}_{j}(y)\to-\infty
in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus J
, and
v^{k}_{i}(y)\to v(y)
in
C^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
, where
v(y)
satisfies

-\Delta v=e^{v}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

Case (2).
If
J=\{1,2\}
. Then
v^{k}_{3}(y)\to-\infty
in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
, and
(v^{k}_{1},v^{k}_{2})\to(v_{1},v_{2})
in
C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
, where
(v_{1},v_{2})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}\Delta v_{1}+e^{v_{1}}=0\\
\Delta v_{2}+e^{v_{2}}=0\end{array}\right.\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

Case (3).
If
J=\{i,3\}
for
i=1
or
2
. Then
v^{k}_{j}(y)\to-\infty
in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
for
j\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus J
, and
(v^{k}_{i},v^{k}_{3})\to(v_{i},v_{3})
in
C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
, where
(v_{i},v_{3})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}\Delta v_{i}+e^{v_{i}}-e^{v_{3}}=0\\
\Delta v_{3}-\frac{1}{2}e^{v_{i}}+e^{v_{3}}=0\end{array}\right.\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

Case (4).
If
J=\{1,2,3\}
. Then
(v^{k}_{1},v^{k}_{2},v^{k}_{3})\to(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
in
C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\})
, where
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
satisfies

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta v_{1}=e^{v_{1}}-e^{v_{3}}\\
-\Delta v_{2}=e^{v_{2}}-e^{v_{3}}\\
-\Delta v_{3}=-\frac{1}{2}e^{v_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}e^{v_{2}}+e^{v_{3}}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{in}\quad B_{2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

We shall prove the point (a) by concerning the
Case (4)
, the proofs for the other cases are similar and simpler. The strength of the Dirac measure at
0
for (
4.3
) can be expressed by

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\frac{\partial v_{1}(y)}{\partial\nu}\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(4\pi\gamma_{1}-\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{1}(y)}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{3}(y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\gamma_{1}-2\pi(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3})=:4\pi\beta_{1},

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\frac{\partial v_{2}(y)}{\partial\nu}\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(4\pi\gamma_{2}-\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{2}(y)}\mathrm{d}y+\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{3}(y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\gamma_{2}-2\pi(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})=:4\pi\beta_{2},

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\int_{\partial B(0,r)}\frac{\partial v_{3}(y)}{\partial\nu}\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(4\pi\gamma_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{1}(y)}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{2}(y)}\mathrm{d}y-\int_{B(0,r)}e^{v^{k}_{3}(y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)

\displaystyle=4\pi\gamma_{3}-2\pi\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}\right)=:4\pi\beta_{3}.

This implies that there is an extra term written as

on the R.H.S. of (
4.3
) for each
i=1,2,3
. It is known that if

for some

4.3
) has no solutions. Hence

\beta_{1}:=\gamma_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3})>-1,~\beta_{2}:=\gamma_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})>-1,~\beta_{3}:=\gamma_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}+\sigma_{3}\right)>-1,

which implies that

(4.4)

2\mu_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\sigma_{j}>0,\quad i=1,2,3.

(b) Since
\mathbf{u}^{k}
has fast decay on
\partial B(0,l_{k})
,
\bm{\sigma}
satisfies the Pohozaev identity (

), i.e.,

(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{3})^{2}+(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3})^{2}=4(\mu_{1}\sigma_{1}+\mu_{2}\sigma_{2}+2\mu_{3}\sigma_{3}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{1j}\sigma_{j}-2\mu_{1}\right)+\sigma_{2}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{2j}\sigma_{j}-2\mu_{2}\right)+2\sigma_{3}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{3j}\sigma_{j}-2\mu_{3}\right)=2\mu_{1}\sigma_{1}+2\mu_{2}\sigma_{2}+4\mu_{3}\sigma_{3}\geq 0.

As a consequence, there exists

such that
\sum_{j=1}^{3}a_{i_{0}j}\sigma_{j}-2\mu_{i_{0}}\geq 0
. However, if all components of
\mathbf{u}^{k}
have slow decay on
\partial B(0,s_{k})

has fast decay on

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that the Harnack-type inequality (

\text{\eqref{CYL-Ch-4-Eq-4} is impossible for}~i\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}.

Adding the above three equations we get that

and this is impossible due to that

for each
i
. Thus
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
is not empty. We set

(4.7)

\delta_{2}=\frac{1}{10000}\min\left\{\min\limits_{i\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}}\Big|2\mu_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\hat{\sigma}_{j}\Big|,\delta_{1},1\right\},

Obviously,
\delta_{2}>0
. Using Harnack-type inequality (
4.2

J\subsetneqq\{1,2,3\}
such that
u^{k}_{i}~(i\in J)
has slow decay on
\partial B(0,l^{k}_{l_{0}}(S))
. Let

v^{k}_{i}(y)=u^{k}_{i}(l^{k}_{l_{0}}(S)y)+2\log{l^{k}_{l_{0}}(S)}.

Then we go back to the proof of
Case (1)
by replacing
s_{k}
by
l^{k}_{l_{0}}(S)
. The rest proof is similar and we skip the details.
∎

As a consequence, we are able to provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{\tilde{u}}^{k}
is a sequence of solutions to system (
1.16
). Let

\hat{u}^{k}_{i}(x)=\tilde{u}^{k}_{i}(x)+\log\frac{\rho^{k}_{i}}{\int_{M}h^{k}_{i}e^{u^{k}_{i}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}},\quad i=1,2,3.

Then the system (
1.16
) can be rewritten as follows

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{u}^{k}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\tilde{h}^{k}_{j}e^{\hat{u}^{k}_{j}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\frac{a_{ij}\rho_{j}}{|M|}=0,\quad i=1,2,3.

We notice that

\hat{u}_{i}^{k}\leq\tilde{u}_{i}^{k}+\log\rho_{i}^{k}-\int_{M}u_{i}^{k}\mathrm{d}V_{g}-C\quad\mbox{for some universal constant}~C,

where we have used the Jensen’s inequality and
h_{i}^{k}
are smooth positive functions. Thus,

(6.3)

\hat{u}_{1}^{k}+\hat{u}_{2}^{k}+2\hat{u}_{3}^{k}=C_{k},\quad C_{k}~\mbox{is uniformly bounded above with respect to}~k.

Around each blow up
p
we take
r
such that
B\cap B_{r}(p)=\{p\}
, and set

\bar{u}_{i}^{k}=\begin{cases}\hat{u}_{i}^{k},\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\notin S,\\
\hat{u}_{i}^{k}+4\pi\alpha_{p,i}G(x,p),&\mbox{if}\quad p\in S.\end{cases}

Together with (
1.15
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) we see that
\bar{u}_{i}^{k}
satisfies

(6.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta\bar{u}_{i}^{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}a_{ij}\bar{h}_{j}^{k}e^{\bar{u}_{j}^{k}}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}\frac{a_{ij}\rho_{j}}{|M|}+\frac{4\pi\beta_{p,i}}{|M|}=4\pi\beta_{p,i}\delta_{p},\quad i=1,2,3,\\
\bar{u}_{1}^{k}+\bar{u}_{2}^{k}+2\bar{u}_{3}^{k}=C_{k},\quad C_{k}~\mbox{is uniformly bounded above with respect to}~k.\end{cases}

where
\bar{h}_{i}^{k}=\tilde{h}_{i}^{k}\exp\left(-4\pi\beta_{p,i}G(x,p)\right),~i=1,2,3
and

\beta_{p,i}=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\notin S\\
\alpha_{p,i},\quad&\mbox{if}\quad p\in S.\end{cases}

Then it is not difficult to see that
\bar{h}_{i}^{k},~i=1,2,3
are
smooth and positive in
B_{r}(p)
. Though there are some extra constant terms in (
6.4
) and
\Delta_{g}
contains some other terms than the Euclidean Laplace operator in the local coordinate, they can be ignored when we perform the blow up analysis for (
6.4
) since we only need the local information for computing the local mass. By Theorem
1.1
and the Remark
1
we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B(p,r)}\bar{h}^{k}_{j}e^{\bar{u}^{k}_{j}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}=\sigma_{p,j}^{*}+4m_{p,j},~j=1,2,3,

where
(\sigma_{p,1}^{*},\sigma_{p,2}^{*},\sigma_{p,3}^{*})\in\Gamma(\mu_{p,1},\mu_{p,2},\mu_{p,3})
and
m_{p,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. This amounts to say that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\rho^{k}_{j}\int_{B(p,r)}h^{k}_{j}e^{u^{k}_{j}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}}{\int_{M}h^{k}_{j}e^{u^{k}_{j}}\mathrm{d}V_{g}}=\sigma_{p,j}^{*}+4m_{p,j},~j=1,2,3.

Together with the fact there is at least one component which possesses the concentration property, without loss of generality, we may assume the first component has such property. Then we get that

\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\rho^{k}_{1}=2\pi\sum\limits_{p\in R}\sigma_{p,1}^{*}+8\pi m_{1}~\mbox{for some}~m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}.

It obeys the choice of
\rho_{1}\notin\Gamma_{1}
. Therefore, we must have
{\mathbf{\tilde{u}}}^{k}
is uniformly bounded and it finishes the proof.
∎

7.
Blow-up analysis for the sinh-Gordon equation

In this section, we consider the blow-up analysis for the sinh-Gordon equation

(7.1)

\Delta u+e^{u}-e^{-u}=0.

Let
u_{1}=u,~u_{2}=-u
, then (
7.1
) can be written as the following equation with
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
,

(7.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta u_{1}+e^{u_{1}}-e^{u_{2}}&=4\pi{\gamma_{1}}\delta_{0},\\
\Delta u_{2}-e^{u_{1}}+e^{u_{2}}&=4\pi{\gamma_{2}}\delta_{0},\\
u_{1}+u_{2}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The local mass for the blow up solutions to equation (
7.1
) with
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
has been derived by Jost-Wang-Ye-Zhou
[
30
]
and Jevnikar-Wei-Yang
[
24
]
by different methods. Now we shall consider a more general situation, where

(7.3)

\gamma_{i}>-1,~i=1,2,\quad\gamma_{1}=-\gamma_{2}\neq 0.

Suppose that
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2})
is a sequence of blow-up solutions of system (
7.2
) in
B_{1}(0)
satisfying

(7.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{B_{1}(0)}e^{u_{i}^{k}(x)}\mathrm{d}x\leq C,\quad i=1,2,\\
&|u^{k}_{i}(x)-u^{k}_{i}(y)|\leq C,~i=1,2,~\forall x,y\in\partial B_{1}(0),~\text{for some constant}~C>0,\\
&\max\limits_{i=1,2}\max\limits_{K\subset\subset\overline{B_{1}(0)}\setminus\{0\}}u^{k}_{i}(x)\leq C(K),~\max\limits_{i=1,2}\max\limits_{\overline{B_{1}(0)}}(u^{k}_{i}(x)+2\gamma_{i}\log|x|)\to+\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Denote the local mass
\bm{\sigma}
corresponding to the solutions
\mathbf{u}^{k}
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim\limits_{r\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{r}(0)}e^{u^{k}_{i}(x)}\mathrm{d}x,~i=1,2.

Then applying the same arguments from Section
2
to Section
6
in the present article, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{u}^{k}=(u^{k}_{1},u^{k}_{2})
is a sequence of solutions of system (
7.2
) satisfying (
7.3
)–(
7.4
). Let
\mu_{i}=1+\gamma_{i},~i=1,2
. Then there exists
{\bm{0}\neq\hat{\bm{\sigma}}}=(\hat{\sigma}_{1},\hat{\sigma}_{2})\in\Gamma(\bm{\mu})
such that

{\sigma}_{i}=\hat{\sigma}_{i}+4m_{i},~m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},~i=1,2,

where

(7.5)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\bm{\mu})=

\displaystyle\bigg\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})~\big|~\sigma_{i}=4\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}n_{ij}\mu_{j},~n_{ij}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i=1,2,~(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})^{2}=4(\mu_{1}\sigma_{1}+\mu_{2}\sigma_{2})\bigg\}.

Furthermore, one can easily check that (
7.5
) is equivalent to

\Gamma(\bm{\mu})=\bigg\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})~\bigg|~\begin{aligned} &\sigma_{1}=(m+1)^{2}\mu_{1}+(m^{2}-1)\mu_{2},~~\sigma_{2}=(m^{2}-1)\mu_{1}+(m-1)^{2}\mu_{2},~m\equiv 1~(\mathrm{mod}~2)\\
&\sigma_{1}=m^{2}\mu_{1}+\left((m-1)^{2}-1\right)\mu_{2},~\sigma_{2}=\left((m+1)^{2}-1\right)\mu_{1}+m^{2}\mu_{2},~m\equiv 0~(\mathrm{mod}~2)\end{aligned}\bigg\}.

Remark 8
.

For the case of
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
, we obtain

\Gamma(\bm{\mu})=\big\{(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})=\left(2m(m+1),2m(m-1)\right)~\text{or}~\left(2m(m-1),2m(m+1)\right)\big\}.

This result coincides with
[
30
, Corollary 1.2]
and
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
.

8.
Appendix: classification results

In this appendix, we provide a quantization result which has been used frequently in the grouping process. We consider the following system

(8.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u+e^{u}-\frac{1}{2}e^{v}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,1}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\quad&\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\Delta v-e^{u}+e^{v}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,2}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\quad&\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u}\mathrm{d}x,~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty,\end{cases}

where
p_{\ell}
are distinct points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\alpha_{\ell,i}>-1
for
1\leq\ell\leq N
,
i=1,2
.

Let

\sigma_{u}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\sigma_{v}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{v}\mathrm{d}x.

The main result of this section is the following

Theorem 8.1
.

Suppose that
(u,v)
is a solution to system (
8.1
).

(a).

If
N=1
, i.e., there is only one singular source on the right hand side of (
8.1
), then there holds

(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v})=(8\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2}+12,8\alpha_{1,1}+8\alpha_{1,2}+16).

(b).

If
\alpha_{\ell,i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i=1,2,~1\leq\ell\leq N
, then there holds
(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v})=(4\tilde{N}_{1},4\tilde{N}_{2})
for some positive integers
\tilde{N}_{1}
and
\tilde{N}_{2}
.

(c).

If
\alpha_{1,i}>-1,~i=1,2
and
\alpha_{\ell,i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},~i=1,2,~2\leq\ell\leq N
, then there holds

(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v})=(\sigma_{u}^{*}+4N_{1}^{*},\sigma_{v}^{*}+4N_{2}^{*}),

where
N_{i}^{*}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i=1,2
and

\displaystyle(\sigma_{u}^{*},\sigma_{v}^{*})\in\Big\{

\displaystyle(0,0),(4\alpha_{1,1},0),(0,4\alpha_{1,2}),(4\alpha_{1,1},8\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2}),(4\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2},4\alpha_{1,2}),

\displaystyle(4\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2},8\alpha_{1,1}+8\alpha_{1,2}),(8\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2},8\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2}),(8\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2},8\alpha_{1,1}+8\alpha_{1,2})\Big\}.

Proof.

Let

u=u_{1}+\log 2,\quad v=u_{2}+\log 2.

Then we have

(8.2)

(\sigma_{u_{1}},\sigma_{u_{2}})=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{u},\sigma_{v}),~\quad{where}\quad\sigma_{u_{i}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{u_{i}}\mathrm{d}x,

and
(u_{1},u_{2})
satisfies

(8.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta u_{1}+2e^{u_{1}}-e^{u_{2}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,1}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\\
\Delta u_{2}-2e^{u_{1}}+2e^{u_{2}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,2}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\end{cases}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We notice that equation (
8.3
) is exactly the standard
\mathbf{C}_{2}
Toda system, which can be obtained from
{\bf A}_{3}
Toda system with some symmetry condition. Precisely, we consider the following system

(8.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta\tilde{u}_{1}+2e^{\tilde{u}_{1}}-e^{\tilde{u}_{2}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,1}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\\
\Delta\tilde{u}_{2}-e^{\tilde{u}_{1}}+2e^{\tilde{u}_{2}}-e^{\tilde{u}_{3}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,2}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\\
\Delta\tilde{u}_{3}-e^{\tilde{u}_{2}}+2e^{\tilde{u}_{3}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\alpha_{\ell,3}\delta_{p_{\ell}},\end{cases}\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Setting

(8.5)

\tilde{u}_{1}=\tilde{u}_{3}=u_{1},\quad\tilde{u}_{2}=u_{2},\quad\alpha_{\ell,1}=\alpha_{\ell,3},~1\leq\ell\leq N.

Then (
8.4
) can be reduced to (
8.3
).

When there is only one singularity on the right hand side of (
8.4
) we get from
[
37
, Theorem 1.3]
that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\tilde{u}_{1}}\mathrm{d}x=4\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2}+6,\quad\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\tilde{u}_{2}}\mathrm{d}x=4\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2}+8,

which together with (
8.2
) implies the conclusion (a).

It is easy to see that conclusion (b) is an easy consequence of (c). So in the following we shall focus on the point (c). By
[
40
, Theorem 8.1]
, we have

\sigma_{u_{1}}=2\sum_{l=1}^{f(0)}\alpha_{1,l}+2\bar{N}_{1},\quad\sigma_{u_{2}}=2\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{f(j)}\alpha_{1,l}-\sum_{l=1}^{j}\alpha_{1,l}\right)+2\bar{N}_{2},

where
\bar{N}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i=1,2
,
\alpha_{1,l}
,
l=1,2,3
are given as in (
8.5
) and
f
is a permutation map from
\{0,1,2,3\}
to itself satisfying

f(l)+f(3-l)=3,~l=0,1,2,3.

Exhausting all the possibilities of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\left(2\sum_{l=1}^{f(0)}\alpha_{1,l},2\sum_{j=0}^{1}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{f(j)}\alpha_{1,l}-\sum_{l=1}^{j}\alpha_{1,l}\right)\right)\in\Big\{

\displaystyle(0,0),(2\alpha_{1,1},0),(0,2\alpha_{1,2}),(2\alpha_{1,1},4\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2}),

\displaystyle(2\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2},2\alpha_{1,2}),(2\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2},4\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2}),

\displaystyle(4\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2},4\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2}),(4\alpha_{1,1}+2\alpha_{1,2},4\alpha_{1,1}+4\alpha_{1,2})\Big\}.

Together with (
8.2
), we get the conclusion (c) and it finishes the proof.
∎

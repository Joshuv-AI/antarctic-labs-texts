Convergence of supercritical fractional flows to the mean curvature flow
L. De Luca, A. Kubin, M. Ponsiglione

Abstract
. We consider a core-radius approach to nonlocal perimeters governed by isotropic kernels having critical and supercritical exponents, extending the nowadays classical notion of s s -fractional perimeter, defined for 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 , to the case s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 . We show that, as the core-radius vanishes, such core-radius regularized s s -fractional perimeters, suitably scaled, Γ \Gamma -converge to the standard Euclidean perimeter. Under the same scaling, the first variation of such nonlocal perimeters gives back regularized s s -fractional curvatures which, as the core radius vanishes, converge to the standard mean curvature; as a consequence, we show that the level set solutions to the corresponding nonlocal geometric flows, suitably reparametrized in time, converge to the standard mean curvature flow. Finally, we prove analogous results in the case of anisotropic kernels with applications to dislocation dynamics. Keywords: Fractional perimeters; Γ \Gamma -convergence; Local and nonlocal geometric evolutions; Viscosity solutions; Level set formulation; Fractional mean curvature flow; Dislocation dynamics AMS subject classifications: 35D40 49J45 35K93 35R11 35Q74 35B40



Introduction

This paper deals with systems governed by strongly attractive nonlocal potentials. Our analysis is geometrical, so that the energy functionals are defined on measurable sets rather than on densities, and can be understood as nonlocal perimeters, whose first variation
are nonlocal curvatures, driving the corresponding geometric flows.

We focus on power law pair potentials acting on measurable sets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whose corresponding nonlocal energy is of the type

(0.1)

J^{s}(E):=\int_{E}\int_{E}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x.

For
-d<s<0
the interaction kernel is nothing but Riesz potential; in such a case, the functionals
J^{s}
are nonlocal perimeters in the sense of
[
18
]
. Such a geometric interpretation is supported by the fact that, as a consequence of Riesz inequality, balls are minimizers of
J^{s}
under volume constraints; moreover, the first variation of
J^{s}
, referred to as nonlocal curvature, is monotone with respect to set inclusion. The latter provides a parabolic maximum principle which
yields global existence and uniqueness of level set solutions
to the corresponding geometric evolutions
[
18
,
16
]
.

For positive
s
the kernel in (
0.1
) is not integrable, and the corresponding energy is infinite. Nevertheless, for
0<s<1
, changing sign to the interaction and letting
E
interact with its complementary set instead of itself, gives a finite quantity: the well-known fractional perimeter
[
12
]

(0.2)

\tilde{J}^{s}(E):=\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x.

In fact, fractional perimeters can been rigorously obtained as limits of renormalized Riesz energies by removing the infinite core energy and letting the core radius tend to zero. This has been done in
[
21
]
, showing that the energies in (
0.1
) and (
0.2
) belong to a one parameter family of nonlocal
s
-perimeters, with
-d<s<1
(see also
[
25
]
); in particular, for
s=0
a new perimeter emerges, referred to as
0
-fractional perimeter.

Remarkably, as
s\to 1^{-}
,
s
-fractional perimeters, suitably scaled, converge to the standard perimeter
[
10
,
11
,
19
,
31
,
7
,
17
]
, and the corresponding (reparametrized in time) geometric flows converge to the standard mean curvature flow
[
26
,
16
]
.

For
s\geq 1
fractional perimeters are always infinite. Nevertheless, as discussed above, the critical case
s=1
corresponds, at least formally, to the Euclidean perimeter. Notice that for
s=1
the fractional perimeter can be seen, again formally, as the square of the (infinite)
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
Gagliardo seminorm of the characteristic function of
E
. This fractional energy is particularly relevant in Materials Science, for instance in the theory of dislocations. This is why much effort has been done to derive the Euclidean perimeter directly as the limit of suitable regularizations of the
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
seminorm, mainly through phase field approximations
[
2
]
.
A specific phase field model for the energy induced by planar dislocations within the Nabarro-Peierls theory has been introduced in
[
24
]
and studied in
[
22
]
, where the line tension energy induced by planar dislocations is derived in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence.
In
[
20
,
4
,
13
]
the authors study dislocation dynamics within the level set formulation á la Slepčev
[
32
]
, by considering the geometric flow associated to a suitable regularization of the formal first variation of the line tension energy. Numerical schemes have been implemented in
[
3
,
15
]
.

In this paper we introduce a core-radius approach to renormalize by scaling the generalized
s
-fractional perimeters and curvatures in the critical and supercritical cases
s\geq 1
. We show that, as the core-radius tends to zero, the
\Gamma
-limit of the nonlocal perimeters is the Euclidean perimeter, the nonlocal curvatures converge to the standard mean curvature, and the corresponding geometric flows converge to the mean curvature flow. Moreover, we consider also the anisotropic variants of such perimeters, with applications to dislocation dynamics.
Now we discuss our results in more detail.

In Section
1
we introduce the core-radius regularized critical and supercritical perimeters (see (
1.4
)). In Theorem
1.5
we show that, suitably scaled, they
\Gamma
-converge to the Euclidean perimeter. This analysis is very related with, and in some respects generalizes, many results scattered in the literature, mainly for
s>1

[
29
,
9
,
30
]
.

Sections
2
and
3
are devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
The proof of the lower bounds providing compactness and
\Gamma
-liminf inequality rely on techniques developed in
[
1
]
and for
s=1
in
[
22
]
. As a byproduct of our
\Gamma
-convergence analysis, we provide a characterization of finite perimeter sets (Theorem
3.4
) in terms of uniformly bounded renormalized supercritical fractional perimeters. Analogous results for
0<s<1
have been obtained in
[
10
,
19
,
31
,
27
]
.

In Section
4
we compute the first variations of the renormalized critical and supercritical perimeters, and we show that they converge, as the core-radius vanishes, to the standard mean curvature. The estimates are robust enough to apply the theory of stability for geomertic flows developed in
[
16
]
. As a consequence, in Theorem
4.7
we prove that the level-set solutions of supercritical fractional geometric flows, suitably reparametrized in time, locally uniformly converge to the level set solution of the classical mean curvature flow. This result extends somehow the analysis done in
[
20
]
for
s=1
and in
[
14
]
for
1\leq s<2
; in the latter, the authors consider a threshold dynamics based on the
s
-parabolic flow and, in turn, on the notion of
s
-Laplacian, which is well defined only for
0<s<2
.

In Section
5
we show that our renormalization procedures are robust enough to treat also the double limit as
s\to 1^{+}
and the core-radius vanishes simultaneously (see Theorem
5.1
).

In Section
6
we generalize our results to the case of possibly anisotropic kernels (Subsection
6.1
) and we present a relevant application to dislocation dynamics (Subsection
6.2
). It is well known that planar dislocation loops formally induce an infinite elastic energy that can be seen as an anisotropic version of the (squared)
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
seminorm of the characteristic function of the slip region enclosed by the dislocation curve. As mentioned above, renormalization procedures are needed to cut off the infinite core energy. In
[
20
,
4
,
13
]
, the authors consider the geometric evolution of dislocation loops and face the corresponding renormalization issues: their approach consists in formally computing the first variation of the infinite energy induced by dislocations, deriving a nonlocal infinite curvature.
Then, they regularize such a curvature through convolution kernels, obtaining a finite curvature driving the dynamics.
As the convolution regularization kernel concentrates to a Dirac mass, they recover in the limit a local anisotropic mean curvature flow. The main issue in their analysis is that the convolution regularization produces a positive part in the nonlocal curvature (corresponding to a negative contribution in the normal velocity), concentrated on the scale of the core of the dislocation, giving back an evolution which does not satisfy the inclusion principle. Therefore, solutions exist only for short time. Moreover, adding strong enough forcing terms, or assuming that the positive part of the curvature is already concentrated on a point (instead of being diffused on the core region), they show that the curvature is in fact monotone with respect to inclusion of sets; as a consequence, they get a globally defined dynamics, converging, as the core-radius vanishes, to the correct anisotropic local mean curvature flow. Here we show that, if one first regularizes the nonlocal perimeters removing the core energy and then computes the corresponding first variation, then the positive part of the curvature is actually concentrated on a point (see Remark
6.3
), so that the mathematical assumption in
[
20
]
is physically correct and fully justified through the solid core-radius formalism.
Finally, in Subsection
6.2
we show that
the convergence analysis of the geometric flows done in
[
20
]
using the approach
[
32
]
can be directly deduced from the analysis developed in Section
4
and Subsection
6.1
, providing then a self-contained proof relying on the general theory of nonlocal evolutions and their stability developed in
[
18
,
16
]
.

This paper, together with
[
21
,
16
]
completes the variational analysis of nonlocal interactions (
0.1
) and of the corresponding geometric flows for all positive values of
d+s
. Such functionals account Riesz functionals for
-d<s<0
. After suitable renormalization procedures, the case
s=0
leads to the
0
-fractional perimeter, while for
0<s<1
we have the nowadays classical
s
-fractional perimeters. This paper focuses on the supercritical case
s\geq 1
leading to the classical perimeter and mean curvature flow, together with their anisotropic variants, in the limit as the core-radius regularization vanishes.

&-\omega_{d-1}\mathrm{Per}(E)\frac{1}{d(d+1)}r^{1-s}.\end{split}

Furthermore, by using (
1.16
), we obtain

(1.19)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{s}\int_{\partial^{*}E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{E\cap\big(B(y,1)\setminus B(y,r)\big)}\frac{(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=&-\frac{1}{s}\int_{\partial^{*}E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\big)\cap\big(B(y,1)\setminus B(y,r)\big)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\omega_{d-1}\mathrm{Per}(E)\frac{1}{s}\gamma^{s}(r)\,.\end{split}

We notice that

\frac{1}{s}\gamma^{s}(r)+\frac{d+s}{ds}r^{1-s}-\frac{1}{d(d+1)}r^{1-s}=\sigma^{s}(r)+\bm{\alpha}^{s}\,;

therefore, plugging (
1.17
), (
1.18
), (
1.19
) into (
1.12
), and using (
1.9
), we obtain the claim.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

We prove the claim under the assumption that

is smooth. For
s>1
, the same proof, with
\partial E
replaced by
\partial^{*}E
, works also for sets

having finite perimeter.
We will use the decomposition of
\tilde{J}_{r}^{s}
in Lemma

Clearly the first contribution

\omega_{d-1}\mathrm{Per}(E)

2^{\mathrm{nd}}
addend:

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{F^{s}_{1}(E)}{\sigma^{s}(r)}=0\,.

3^{\mathrm{rd}}

\sigma^{s}(r)r^{s-1}

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\frac{d+s}{dsr^{s}}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H^{-}_{\nu_{E}(y)}(y)\big)\cap B(y,r)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\

4^{\mathrm{th}}
addend
. Trivially, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\frac{1}{dr^{s}}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\big)\cap B(y,r)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{r^{d}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\frac{1}{dr^{s}}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\big)\cap B(y,r)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d}}\;\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

where the last integral vanishes as shown above.

5^{\mathrm{th}}

.
We first discuss the simpler case

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\big)\cap\big(B(y,1)\setminus B(y,r)\big)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}x\\

\leq&C(d,s)\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(\frac{E-y}{r}\triangle(H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)-\frac{y}{r})\big)\setminus B(0,1)}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+s-1}}\;\mathrm{d}z\,,\end{split}

where the last double integral vanishes as

in virtue of the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem using that
{\chi}_{\frac{E-y}{r}}\to{\chi}_{H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)-\frac{y}{r}}
in

as

and the fact that

\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,1))

Notice that the reasoning above does not apply to the case
s=1
since for

is not in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,1))
. Let now

Since

Therefore, we have that

(1.20)

E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\subset(H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\setminus B^{-})\cup(H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{+}(y)\setminus B^{+})\,,

where

are defined in (

Fix

and let

be a rotation of

(1.21)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log r|}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(E\triangle H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\big)\cap\big(B(y,1)\setminus B(y,r)\big)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log r|}\int_{\partial E}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\int_{\big(H_{\nu_{E}(y)}^{-}(y)\setminus B^{-}\big)\cap B(y,1)}\frac{|(y-x)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap\big(B(0,1)\setminus B(\delta e_{d},\delta)\big)}\frac{z_{d}}{(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+z_{d}^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}z_{d}

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{A}_{\delta}}\frac{z_{d}}{(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+z_{d}^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}z_{d}+\int_{B(0,1)\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{z_{d}}{(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+z_{d}^{2})^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}z_{d}

\displaystyle\int_{B^{\prime}(0,\delta)}\;\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\delta-\sqrt{\delta^{2}-|z^{\prime}|^{2}}}\frac{c_{\delta}|z^{\prime}|^{2}}{|z^{\prime}|^{d+1}}\;\mathrm{d}z_{d}+\int_{B(0,1)\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|z|^{d}}\;\mathrm{d}z

2.1.
Preliminary results

We first recall the following classical result (see also
[
6

).

Theorem 2.1

(Compactness in
\mathrm{BV}
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\biggl\{\int_{A}|u_{n}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}x+|Du_{n}|(A)\biggr\}<+\infty\quad\forall A\subset\subset\Omega\;\text{open}\,.

Then, there exist a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a function
u\in\mathrm{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n_{k}}\rightarrow u
in
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(\Omega)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

Now we prove a non-local Poincaré-Wirtinger type inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<r<l
be such that
\omega_{d}r^{d}\leq\frac{l^{d}}{2}
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(lQ+\xi)
. Then, for every
s\geq 1
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{lQ+\xi}\bigg|u(y)-\frac{1}{|(lQ+\xi)\setminus B(y,r)|}\int_{(lQ+\xi)\setminus B(y,r)}u(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2d^{\frac{d+s}{2}}l^{s}\int_{lQ+\xi}\int_{lQ+\xi}|u(y)-u(x)|k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\,.

Proof.

By translational invariance, it is enough to prove the claim only for
\xi=0
.
By assumption, for every
y\in lQ
we have

|lQ\setminus B(y,r)|\geq l^{d}-\omega_{d}r^{d}\geq\frac{l^{d}}{2}.

As a consequence, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{lQ}\bigg|u(y)-\frac{1}{|lQ\setminus B(y,r)|}\int_{Q\setminus B(y,r)}u(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&\int_{lQ}\frac{1}{|lQ\setminus B(y,r)|}\int_{lQ\setminus B(y,r)}|u(y)-u(x)|\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{lQ}\frac{1}{|lQ\setminus B(y,r)|}\int_{lQ\setminus B(y,r)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{|y-x|^{d+s}}|y-x|^{d+s}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&\int_{lQ}\frac{2d^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}{l^{d}}\int_{lQ\setminus B(y,r)}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{|y-x|^{d+s}}l^{d+s}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&2d^{\frac{d+s}{2}}l^{s}\int_{lQ}\int_{lQ}|u(y)-u(x)|k^{s}_{r}(|y-x|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

i.e., (
2.1
).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<r<l
be such that
\omega_{d}r^{d}<\frac{l^{d}}{4}
. For every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for every
E\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds

(2.2)

\frac{1}{l^{d}}|(lQ+\xi)\setminus E||(lQ+\xi)\cap E|\\
\leq\int_{lQ+\xi}\bigg|\chi_{E}(x)-\frac{1}{|(lQ+\xi)\setminus B(x,r)|}\int_{(lQ+\xi)\setminus B(x,r)}\chi_{E}(y)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}x\,.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\xi=0
.
It is enough to prove (
2.2
) only in the case
|lQ\cap E|\geq\frac{l^{d}}{2}
; indeed, once proven the inequality (
2.2
) in such a case,
if
|lQ\setminus E|\geq\frac{l^{d}}{2}
, then the set
\tilde{E}=lQ\setminus E
satisfies
|lQ\cap\tilde{E}|\geq\frac{l^{d}}{2}
, and hence
\tilde{E}
and, in turn,
E
satisfy (
2.2
).

Let
|lQ\cap E|\geq\frac{l^{d}}{2}
; then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(2.3)

|(lQ\cap E)\setminus B(x,r)|\geq|lQ\cap E|-\omega_{d}r^{d}\geq|lQ\cap E|-\frac{l^{d}}{4}\geq\frac{|lQ\cap E|}{2}\,,

so that

\begin{split}&\int_{lQ}\bigg|\chi_{E}(x)-\frac{1}{|lQ\setminus B(x,r)|}\int_{lQ\setminus B(x,r)}\chi_{E}(y)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=&\int_{lQ\cap E}\bigg|1-\frac{|(lQ\setminus B(x,r))\cap E|}{|lQ\setminus B(x,r)|}\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{lQ\setminus E}\frac{|(lQ\setminus B(x,r))\cap E|}{|lQ\setminus B(x,r)|}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&\frac{1}{l^{d}}\biggl(\int_{lQ\cap E}|(lQ\setminus B(x,r))\setminus E|\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{lQ\setminus E}|(lQ\setminus B(x,r))\cap E|\;\mathrm{d}x\biggr)\\
=&\frac{2}{l^{d}}\int_{lQ\setminus E}|(lQ\cap E)\setminus B(x,r)|\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&\frac{1}{l^{d}}|lQ\cap E|\,|lQ\setminus E|\,,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used formula (
2.3
).
∎

The following result is a localized isoperimetric inequality for the non-local perimeters

Lemma 2.4

The proof of Lemma

Before proving Lemma

Lemma 2.5

(
[
22
]
)

Let
\Omega\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a bounded set with Lipschitz continuous boundary and
|\Omega|=1
and

\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,1);[0,+\infty))

For every
\eta\in(0,1)

\eta\leq|A|\leq 1-\eta

\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{A}\int_{\Omega\setminus A}\phi_{\delta}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\geq C(\Omega,\phi,\eta).

The above lemma has been stated and proven in

We are now in a position to prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Fix
\eta\in(0,1)
,
r\in(0,1)
and let
I\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2^{-I-1}\leq r\leq 2^{-I}

Notice that

(2.5)

k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\geq(2^{d+s})^{\min\{i,I\}}\qquad\textrm{ if }0\leq|z|\leq 2^{-i}\,,\textrm{ with }i\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Let
\phi

\phi_{\delta}
(for every

Now we claim that there exists a constant

(2.6)

Before proving the claim we show that (
2.6
) implies (
2.4
).
Indeed, first notice that

\frac{|\log r|}{\log 2}-1\leq I\leq\frac{|\log r|}{\log 2}

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s-1})^{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I+1\geq\frac{|\log r|}{\log 2}&\textrm{ if }s=1\,,\\
\frac{(2^{I+1})^{s-1}-1}{2^{s-1}-1}\geq\frac{r^{1-s}-1}{2^{s-1}-1}&\textrm{ if }s>1\,.\end{array}\right.

so that, recalling the very definition of
\sigma^{s}(r)
in (
1.5
), for
r
small enough we have

(2.7)

\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s-1})^{i}\geq C(d,s)\sigma^{s}(r)\,.

Therefore, by applying (
2.6
) and Lemma
2.5
with
\delta
replaced by
2^{-i}
, we get

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{\Omega\setminus A}k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(\Omega,\phi,d,s)\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s-1})^{i}\,2^{i}\int_{A}\int_{\Omega\setminus A}\phi_{2^{-i}}(x-y)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(\Omega,\phi,d,s,\eta)\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s-1})^{i}\geq C(\phi,d,s,\eta)\sigma^{s}(r)\,,

where the last inequality follows from (
2.7
).

Now we prove the claim (
2.6
). Suppose first that
0\leq|z|\leq 2^{-I}
.
By applying (
2.5
) with
i=I
, we get

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)\leq

\displaystyle\sup\phi\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{d+s})^{i}=\sup\phi\sum_{i=0}^{I}\frac{1}{(2^{d+s})^{I-i}}(2^{d+s})^{I}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\phi\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{d+s})^{j}}\,(2^{d+s})^{I}=\frac{2^{{d+s}}}{2^{{d+s}}-1}\sup\phi\,(2^{d+s})^{I}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\phi,d,s)k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\,.

Analogously, if
2^{-\bar{\imath}-1}<|z|\leq 2^{-\bar{\imath}}
for some
\bar{\imath}=0,1,\ldots,I-1
, using that
\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)=0
for every
i=\bar{\imath}+1,\ldots,I
, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\bar{\imath}}(2^{s})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)\leq\sup\phi\sum_{i=0}^{\bar{\imath}}(2^{d+s})^{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\phi\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(2^{d+s})^{j}}(2^{d+s})^{\bar{\imath}}=\frac{2^{{d+s}}}{2^{{d+s}}-1}\sup\phi\,(2^{d+s})^{\bar{\imath}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\phi,d,s)k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\,,

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
2.5
).

Finally, if
|z|\geq 1
we have that
\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)=0
for every
i
so that

(2.11)

\sum_{i=0}^{I}(2^{s})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)=0\leq k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\,.

Therefore, by (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we deduce (
2.6
), thus concluding the proof of the lemma .
∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
(i)

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.5
(i).

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and set
l_{n}:=r_{n}^{\alpha}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\{Q_{h}^{n}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
be a disjoint family of cubes of sidelength
l_{n}
such that
\bigcup_{h\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{h}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
|E_{n}|\leq|U|
, there exists
H(n)\in\mathbb{N}
, such that, up to permutation of indices,

(2.12)

\displaystyle|Q_{h}^{n}\cap E_{n}|\geq\frac{l_{n}^{d}}{2}\qquad\textrm{for every }h=1,\cdots,H(n),

\displaystyle|Q_{h}^{n}\setminus E_{n}|>\frac{l_{n}^{d}}{2}\qquad\textrm{for every }h\geq H(n)+1\,.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\tilde{E}_{n}:=\bigcup_{h=1}^{H(n)}Q_{h}^{n}\,.

Let
\tilde{n}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that for all
n>\tilde{n}
the pair
(r_{n},l_{n})
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
.
We claim that there exists a constant
C(d,s)>0
such that

(2.13)

|\tilde{E}_{n}\triangle E_{n}|\leq C(d,s)l_{n}^{s}\sigma^{s}(r_{n})M\qquad\textrm{for every }n\geq\tilde{n},

where
M
is the constant in (
1.25
).
Indeed,

\begin{split}|E_{n}\triangle\tilde{E}_{n}|=&|\tilde{E}_{n}\setminus E_{n}|+|E_{n}\setminus\tilde{E}_{n}|\\
=&\sum_{h=1}^{H(n)}|Q_{h}^{n}\setminus E_{n}|+\sum_{h=H(n)+1}^{\infty}|E_{n}\cap Q_{h}^{n}|\\
=&2\sum_{h=1}^{H(n)}\frac{1}{l_{n}^{d}}|Q_{h}^{n}\setminus E_{n}|\frac{l_{n}^{d}}{2}+2\sum_{h=H(n)+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l_{n}^{d}}|E_{n}\cap Q_{h}^{n}|\frac{l_{n}^{d}}{2}\\
\leq&2\sum_{h=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{l_{n}^{d}}|Q_{h}^{n}\setminus E_{n}||Q_{h}^{n}\cap E_{n}|\\
\leq&2\sum_{h=1}^{+\infty}\int_{Q_{h}^{n}}\bigg|\chi_{E_{n}}(x)-\frac{1}{|Q_{h}^{n}\setminus B(x,r_{n})|}\int_{Q_{h}^{n}\setminus B(x,r_{n})}\chi_{E_{n}}(y)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&\sum_{h=1}^{+\infty}8d^{\frac{d+s}{2}}l_{n}^{s}\int_{Q_{h}^{n}\cap E_{n}}\int_{Q_{h}^{n}\setminus E_{n}}k^{s}_{r_{n}}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq&C(d,s)l^{s}_{n}\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s}(E_{n})\leq C(d,s)l_{n}^{s}\sigma^{s}(r_{n})M,\end{split}

where the second inequality follows by formula (
2.2
), the third inequality is a consequence of (
2.1
), whereas the last one follows directly by (
1.25
).

Step 2.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
l_{n}
and
\tilde{E}_{n}:=\bigcup_{h=1}^{H(n)}Q^{n}_{h}
be as in Step 1.
We claim that there exists a constant
C(\alpha,d,s)
such that for
n
large enough

(2.14)

\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E}_{n})\leq C(\alpha,d,s)\bar{J}_{r_{n}}^{s}(E_{n})\,.

To ease notation, we omit the dependence on
n
by setting
r:=r_{n}
,
l:=l_{n}
,
E:=E_{n}
,
Q_{h}:=Q^{n}_{h}
,
H:=H(n)
, and
\tilde{E}:=\tilde{E}_{n}
.

We define the family
\mathcal{R}
of rectangles
R=\tilde{Q}\cup\hat{Q}
such that
\tilde{Q}
and
\hat{Q}
are adjacent cubes (of the type
Q_{h}
introduced above),

\tilde{Q}\subset\tilde{E}
and
\hat{Q}\subset\tilde{E}^{c}
.

Notice that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E})\leq

\displaystyle 2dl^{d-1}\sharp\mathcal{R}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{r}^{s}(E)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2d\,\sigma^{s}(r)}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\int_{R\cap E}\int_{R\setminus E}k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\,.

We recall that, by Lemma
2.4
, for every rectangle
\bar{R}
given by the union of two adjacent unitary cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
\rho_{0}>0
such that

(2.16)

\displaystyle C(d,s):=

\displaystyle\inf\biggl\{\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(\rho)}\int_{F}\int_{\bar{R}\setminus F}k^{s}_{\rho}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x:

\displaystyle 0<\rho<\rho_{0},\;F\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\;F\subset\bar{R}\,,\;\frac{1}{2}\leq|F|\leq\frac{3}{2}\biggr\}>0\,.

Furthermore, by the very definition of
\sigma^{s}(r)
in (
1.5
), using that
l=r^{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{s}(r)}{l^{1-s}}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{|\log(r^{1-\alpha})|}{1-\alpha}&\textrm{if }s=1\\
\displaystyle\frac{d+s}{d+1}\frac{r^{(1-\alpha)(1-s)}}{s-1}&\textrm{if }s>1\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\sigma^{s}(r^{1-\alpha})&\textrm{if }s=1\\
\displaystyle\sigma^{s}(r^{1-\alpha})&\textrm{if }s>1\,,\end{array}\right.

so that

(2.17)

\frac{l^{1-s}}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\geq C(\alpha)\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r^{1-\alpha})}=C(\alpha)\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(\frac{r}{l})}\,.

For every set
O\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set
O^{l}:=\frac{O}{l}
.
By (
2.15
), (
1.2
), (
2.17
) and by applying (
2.16
) with
\bar{R}=R^{l}
for every
R\in\mathcal{R}
, for
r
small enough we obtain

\begin{split}\bar{J}_{r}^{s}(E)\geq&\frac{C(d)}{\sigma^{s}(r)}l^{2d}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\int_{R^{l}\cap E^{l}}\int_{R^{l}\setminus E^{l}}k^{s}_{r}(|l(x-y)|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=&C(d)\frac{l^{1-s}}{\sigma^{s}(r)}l^{d-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\int_{R^{l}\cap E^{l}}\int_{R^{l}\setminus E^{l}}k^{s}_{\frac{r}{l}}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&C(\alpha,d)l^{d-1}\sum_{R\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(\frac{r}{l})}\int_{R^{l}\cap E^{l}}\int_{R^{l}\setminus E^{l}}k^{s}_{\frac{r}{l}}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
\geq&C(\alpha,d)l^{d-1}\sharp\mathcal{R}\,C(d,s)\geq C(\alpha,d,s)\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E})\,,\end{split}

i.e., (
2.14
).

Step 3.
Here we conclude the proof of the compactness result.
We fix
\alpha\in(1-\frac{1}{s},1)
so that, by (
2.13
),
|E_{n}\triangle\tilde{E}_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.

By assumption and by the very definition of
\tilde{E}_{n}
in Step 1, we have that
\tilde{E}_{n}\subset U
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, by formula (
2.14
) and by (
1.25
) for
n
large enough we have

\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E}_{n})\leq C(\alpha,d,s)\bar{J}_{r_{n}}^{s}(E_{n})\leq C(\alpha,d,s)M\,.

It follows that the sequence
\{\chi_{\tilde{E}_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
2.1
, and hence there exists a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathrm{Per}(E)<+\infty
such that, up to a subsequence,
\chi_{\tilde{E}_{n}}\rightarrow\chi_{E}
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\to+\infty
.
Since
|E_{n}\triangle\tilde{E}_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
we obtain that
\chi_{E_{n}}\rightarrow\chi_{E}
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(U)
, i.e., the claim of Theorem
1.5
(i).
∎

The following result follows by the proof of Theorem
1.5
(i).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
s\geq 1
. Let
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,+\infty)
be such that
r_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Let
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\chi_{E_{n}}\rightarrow\chi_{E}
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, for some
E\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s}(E_{n})}{\sigma^{s}(r_{n})}\leq M\,,

then
E
has finite perimeter.

Proof.

The proof of this corollary is fully analogous to the proof of Theorem
1.5
(i), and we adopt the same notation introduced there.
Arguing as in the proof of Steps 1 and 2 we have that for
n
large enough

\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E}_{n})\leq C(\alpha,d,s)\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s}(E_{n})}{\sigma^{s}(r_{n})}\leq C(\alpha,d,s)M\,,

and that if
\alpha\in(1-\frac{1}{s},1)
, then
|\tilde{E}_{n}\triangle E_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.
By assumption, this implies that

\chi_{\tilde{E}_{n}}\rightarrow\chi_{E},\;\text{ in $\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}\quad n\rightarrow+\infty\,,

and by the lower semicontinuity of the perimeter,

\mathrm{Per}(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mathrm{Per}(\tilde{E}_{n})\leq C(\alpha,d,s)M\,.

∎

3.
Proof of the
\Gamma
-limit

This section is devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.5
(ii) and (iii), which are the content of Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
respectively.

3.1.
Proof of the lower bound

The proof of Theorem
1.5
(ii) closely follows the strategy used in
[
22
]
.
We recall that for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
Q^{\nu}
is a unit square centered at the origin with one face orthogonal to
\nu
. Moreover, we recall that
H^{+}_{\nu}(0)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,x\cdot\nu\geq 0\}
.

The following result is the adaptation to our setting of
[
22
, Lemma 18]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s\geq 1
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
r_{0},\delta_{0}>0
such that for every
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, for every
E\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

(3.1)

|(E\triangle H^{-}_{\nu}(0))\cap Q^{\nu}|\leq\delta_{0}\,,

and for every
r<r_{0}
it holds

(3.2)

\int_{Q^{\nu}\cap E}\int_{Q^{\nu}\cap E^{c}}k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\omega_{d-1}(1-\varepsilon)\sigma^{s}(r)\,.

Proof.

Up to a rotation, we can assume that
\nu=-e_{d}
so that
Q^{\nu}\equiv Q=[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{d}
and
H^{-}_{\nu}(0)=:\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
.
Let
0<r<1
.
We can assume without loss of generality that
E\subset Q
.
Using the change of variable
y=x+z
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{Q\cap E^{c}}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{Q\cap E}k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q\cap E^{c}}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,x+z\in E\}}k^{s}_{r}(|-z|)\;\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q\cap E^{c}}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\chi_{E}(x+z)\;\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{E^{c}\cap Q}(x)\chi_{E}(x+z)\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)|E^{c}\cap(E-z)\cap Q|\;\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)m(z)\;\mathrm{d}z\,,

where we have set
m(z):=|E^{c}\cap(E-z)\cap Q|
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\lambda<1
and let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

be such that
|z|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1-\lambda}{2}
and
z_{d}>0
. Since
|(E-z)\cap\lambda Q|=|E\cap(\lambda Q+z)|
, by triangular inequality, we get

\displaystyle|(E-z)\cap\lambda Q|-|E\cap\lambda Q|=\int_{\lambda Q+z}\chi_{E}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\lambda Q}\chi_{E}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda Q+z}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\lambda Q}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{(\lambda Q+z)\triangle\lambda Q}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda^{d-1}z_{d}-\int_{U_{\lambda,z}}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where we have set
U_{\lambda,z}:=(\lambda+|z|_{\infty})Q\setminus(\lambda-|z|_{\infty})Q
and we have used that
(\lambda Q+z)\triangle\lambda Q\subset U_{\lambda,z}
. As a consequence, we deduce that

(3.4)

\displaystyle m(z)=

\displaystyle|E^{c}\cap(E-z)\cap Q|\geq|E^{c}\cap(E-z)\cap\lambda Q|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|E^{c}\cap\lambda Q|+|(E-z)\cap\lambda Q|-|\lambda Q|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|E^{c}\cap\lambda Q|+|E\cap\lambda Q|+\lambda^{d-1}z_{d}-\int_{U_{\lambda,z}}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x-|\lambda Q|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{d-1}z_{d}-\int_{U_{\lambda,z}}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where the last equality follows by noticing that
|E\cap\lambda Q|+|E^{c}\cap\lambda Q|=|\lambda Q|
.

Let now
0<\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{64}
to be chosen later on and
set

A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}:=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|z|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}{2},\,z_{d}>0\Big\}\,.

We fix
z\in A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}
and we set
J:=\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}{|z|_{\infty}}\rfloor
.
We set
\lambda_{0}:=1-4\sqrt{\delta_{0}}
and we cover
(\lambda_{0}+2J|z|_{\infty})Q\setminus\lambda_{0}Q
with
J
squared annuli of thickness
2|z|_{\infty}
, namely we set
\lambda_{j}:=\lambda_{0}+2j|z|_{\infty}
and
U_{j}:=\lambda_{j}Q\setminus\lambda_{j-1}Q
for
j=1,\ldots,J
.
Moreover, we set
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}:=\lambda_{0}+(2j-1)|z|_{\infty}

for every
j=1,\ldots,J
and we notice that
\frac{1}{2}<\tilde{\lambda}_{j}<1
for every
j=1,\ldots,J
.
Since
z\in A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}
, we have that
|z|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1-\tilde{\lambda}_{J}}{2}\leq\frac{1-\tilde{\lambda}_{j}}{2}
for every
j=1,\ldots,J
.
Therefore, for every
j=1,\ldots,J
we can apply (
3.4
) with
\lambda=\tilde{\lambda}_{j}
in order to get

(3.5)

\displaystyle m(z)\geq

\displaystyle z_{d}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{d-1}-\int_{U_{\tilde{\lambda}_{j},z}}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle z_{d}\lambda_{j-1}^{d-1}-\int_{U_{j}}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where we have used also that
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}-|z|_{\infty}=\lambda_{j-1}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{j}+|z|_{\infty}=\lambda_{j}
so that
U_{\tilde{\lambda}_{j},z}=U_{j}
.
Summing (
3.5
) over
j=1,\ldots,J
we get

Jm(z)\geq z_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j-1}^{d-1}-\int_{Q}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

which, dividing by
J
and using discrete Jensen inequality (namely, convextiy), yields

(3.6)

m(z)\geq z_{d}\Big(\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j-1}\Big)^{d-1}-\frac{1}{J}\int_{Q}|\chi_{E}-\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\geq z_{d}\lambda_{0}^{d-1}-2|z|_{\infty}\sqrt{\delta_{0}}\,,

where in the last inequality we have used (
3.1
) and the fact that
J\geq\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}{|z|_{\infty}}-1
.
Therefore, we have proven that (
3.6
) holds true whenever
z\in A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}
, which combined with (
3.3
), yields

(3.7)

\int_{Q\cap E^{c}}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{Q\cap E}k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\geq\lambda_{0}^{d-1}\int_{A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}}z_{d}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z-2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}\int_{A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}}|z|_{\infty}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z\,.

As for the first integral on the right hand side of (
3.7
), by using polar coordinates
z=\rho\theta
with
\rho>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and using the very definition of
\sigma^{s}(r)
in (
1.5
), for
\delta_{0}
small enough and for all
r<\delta_{0}
we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}}z_{d}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\frac{z_{d}}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}z+\int_{(B(0,\delta_{0})\setminus B(0,r))\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\frac{z_{d}}{|z|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{d}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\theta_{d}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\int_{r}^{\delta_{0}}\rho^{-s}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cap\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\theta_{d}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{d-1}\frac{r^{1-s}}{d+1}+\omega_{d-1}\int_{r}^{\delta_{0}}\rho^{-s}\;\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\omega_{d-1}\sigma^{s}(r)-\omega_{d-1}C(\delta_{0},s)\,,

where

C(\delta_{0},s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\log\delta_{0}|&\textrm{if }s=1\\
\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{0}^{1-s}}{s-1}&\textrm{if }s>1\,.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, since
|z|_{\infty}\leq|z|
, it holds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{A^{+}_{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}}|z|_{\infty}k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z\leq\int_{B(0,1)}|z|k^{s}_{r}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\int_{B(0,r)}|z|\;\mathrm{d}z+\int_{B(0,1)\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{|z|^{d+s-1}}\;\mathrm{d}z\leq

\displaystyle C(d,s)\sigma^{s}(r)\,,

for some
C(d,s)>0
.

Now we define the function
\eta(t):=1-(1-4\sqrt{t})^{d-1}
, and we notice that

\eta(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
Therefore, by (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
),
using that
\lambda_{0}^{d-1}=1-\eta(\delta_{0})
,
we deduce that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{Q\cap E^{c}}\;\mathrm{d}x\int_{Q\cap E}k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\omega_{d-1}\sigma^{s}(r)\Big(1-\eta(\delta_{0})-\big(1-\eta(\delta_{0})\big)\frac{C(\delta_{0},s)}{\sigma^{s}(r)}-2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}\frac{C(d,s)}{\omega_{d-1}}\Big)\,,

so that, choosing
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\eta(\delta_{0})+2\sqrt{\delta_{0}}\frac{C(d,s)}{\omega_{d-1}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

and
r_{0}>0
such that (for every
0<r<r_{0}
)

4.

Notice that if

4.1.

Although this fact may be immediate for the experts, we show that

is the first variation of the functional

Proposition 4.1

(First variation)

Let

Let

\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

\Phi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}

(4.2)

\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\tilde{J}_{r}^{s}(E_{t}){\bigg|_{t=0}}=\int_{\partial E}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)\Psi(x)\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\,.

4.5
), and using the divergence theorem, we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}}{\;\mathrm{d}t}\tilde{J}_{r}^{s}(E_{t})\bigg|_{t=0}=&\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}(k_{r}^{s})^{\prime}(|x-y|)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\cdot(\Psi(x)-\Psi(y))\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)(\mathrm{Div}\Psi(x)+\mathrm{Div}\Psi(y))\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=&\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}(k_{r}^{s})^{\prime}(|x-y|)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\cdot(\Psi(x)-\Psi(y))\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{E^{c}}\biggl[-\int_{E}(k_{r}^{s})^{\prime}(|x-y|)\,\Psi(x)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\int_{\partial E}k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\,\Psi(x)\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\biggr]\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{E}\biggl[\int_{E^{c}}(k_{r}^{s})^{\prime}(|x-y|)\,\Psi(y)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{\partial E}k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\,\Psi(y)\cdot\nu_{E}(y)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(y)\biggr]\;\mathrm{d}x\\
=&\int_{\partial E}\Psi(x)\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x)\\
=&\int_{\partial E}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)\Psi(x)\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x),\end{split}

whence (
4.3
) follows.
∎

In Proposition
4.2
we prove some qualitative properties of the curvatures
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
defined in (
4.1
), which imply in particular that
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
are non-local curvatures in the sense of
[
18
,
16
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
s\geq 1
,
0<r<1
the functionals
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
defined in (
4.1
) satisfy the following properties:

(M)

Monotonicity: If

E,F\in\mathfrak{C}
with
E\subseteq F
, and if
x\in\partial F\cap\partial E
,
then
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,F)\leq\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)
;

(T)

Translational invariance: for any
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
,

y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)=\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x+y,E+y)

(S)

Symmetry:
For all
E\in\mathfrak{C}
and for every
x\in\partial E
it holds

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)=-\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overset{\circ}{E})\,,

where
\overset{\circ}{E}
denotes the interior of
E
.

(B)

Lower bound on the curvature of the balls:

(4.8)

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho))\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial B(0,\rho)\,,\,\rho>0\,;

(UC)

Uniform continuity: There exists a modulus of continuity
\omega_{r}
such that the following holds.
For every
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
,
and for every diffeomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of class
C^{2}
,
with
\Phi=\mathrm{Id}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,1)
, we have

|\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)-\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(\Phi(x),\Phi(E))|\leq\omega_{r}(\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}})\,.

Proof.

We prove separately the properties above.

Property
(M): Let
E,F\in\mathfrak{C}
such that
E\subseteq F
, then
-\chi_{F}\leq-\chi_{E}
and

\chi_{F^{c}}\leq\chi_{E^{c}}
. Therefore for all
x\in\partial E\cap\partial F
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,F)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{F^{c}}(y)-\chi_{F}(y))k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k^{s}_{r}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y=\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)\,.\end{split}

Property
(T): Let
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By the change of variable
\zeta=\eta-y
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x+y,E+y)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E^{c}+y}(\eta)-\chi_{E+y}(\eta))k^{s}_{r}(|x+y-\eta|)\;\mathrm{d}\eta\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E^{c}}(\zeta)-\chi_{E}(\zeta))k^{s}_{r}(|x-\zeta|)\;\mathrm{d}\zeta=\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,E)\,.\end{split}

Property
(S): Let
E\in\mathfrak{C}
and
x\in\partial E
, then we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,E)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y-x|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{E}(y)-\chi_{E^{c}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y=-\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overset{\circ}{E}).\end{split}

Property
(B): Let
\rho>0
and
\bar{x}\in\partial B(0,\rho)
.
Since
B(2\bar{x},\rho)\subset B^{c}(0,\rho)=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}(0,\rho)
, we get

(4.9)

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(\bar{x},\overline{B}(0,\rho))=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B^{c}(0,\rho)}(y)-\chi_{B(0,\rho)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\bar{x}-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\geq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B(2\bar{x},\rho)}(y)-\chi_{B(0,\rho)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\bar{x}-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y=0,\end{split}

where in the last equality we have used the change of variable
z=2\bar{x}-y
and the radial symmetry of
k^{s}_{r}
to deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(2\bar{x},\rho)}(y)k_{r}^{s}(|\bar{x}-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi_{B(0,\rho)}(z)k^{s}_{r}(|\bar{x}-z|)\;\mathrm{d}z\,.

Hence, by formula (
4.9
), (
4.8
) follows.

Property
(UC):
Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a diffeomorphism of class
C^{2}
, with
\Phi(y)=y
for all
|y-x|\geq 1
. We set
\mathcal{E}:=\Phi(E)
.
Let moreover
\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by
\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(\eta):=\int_{B(0,\eta)}k_{r}^{s}(|z|)\;\mathrm{d}z
.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
\eta_{\varepsilon}>0
be small enough such that

(4.10)

2\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(\eta_{\varepsilon}),\;\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(2\eta_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Notice that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\leq\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(\eta_{\varepsilon})\,,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B(\Phi(x),\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\leq\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(\eta_{\varepsilon})\,,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B(\Phi(x),2\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\leq\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(2\eta_{\varepsilon})\,.

By (
4.11
), (
4.12
), and (
4.10
), using triangular inequality, we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}&|\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,E)-\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(\Phi(x),\Phi(E))|\\
\leq&\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\bigg|\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{B^{c}(\Phi(x),\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\\
\leq&\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\bigg|\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\\
+&\bigg|\int_{\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{B^{c}(\Phi(x),\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|.\end{split}

By the change of variable
z=\Phi(y)
and using that
\Phi(y)=y
if
|y-x|\geq 1
, we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(z)-\chi_{E}(z))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(z)|)\mathcal{J}\Phi(z)\;\mathrm{d}z\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|)\mathcal{J}\Phi(y)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}(x,1)}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y

(4.16)

\displaystyle+\int_{B(x,1)\setminus B(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|)\mathcal{J}\Phi(y)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y\,.

Now, assuming that
\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}}
is small enough, by using Lagrange Theorem one can show that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}(x,1)}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega(\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}})\int_{B^{c}(x,1)}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d+s+1}}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}\,,

for some modulus of continuity
\omega
.
Analogously, for
\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}}
small enough, one can easily check that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,1)\setminus B(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|)\mathcal{J}\Phi(y)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,1)\setminus B(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}\Big|k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)-k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{B(x,1)\setminus B(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)|)\big|1-\mathcal{J}\Phi(y)\big|\;\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}\,.

Therefore, by (
4.15
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
), (
4.18
) we deduce that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-\int_{\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\,.

In the end, we observe that, for
\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}}
small enough, it holds

\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))\triangle B^{c}(\Phi(x),\eta_{\varepsilon})\subset B(\Phi(x),2\eta_{\varepsilon})\,,

which, in view of (
4.13
), yields

(4.20)

\begin{split}&\bigg|\int_{\Phi(B^{c}(x,\eta_{\varepsilon}))}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{B^{c}(\Phi(x),\eta_{\varepsilon})}(\chi_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{\mathcal{E}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|\Phi(x)-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\leq\theta_{k_{r}^{s}}(2\eta_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.\end{split}

Plugging (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) into (
4.14
) , we conclude the proof of property (UC) and of the whole proposition.
∎

4.2.
The classical mean curvature

For every
E\in\mathfrak{C}
, and for every
x\in\partial E
, we denote by
\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)
the (scalar) mean curvature of
\partial E
at
x
, i.e., the sum of the principal curvatures of
\partial E
at
x
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{K}^{1}
is nothing but the first variation of the perimeter.
Let
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
and assume that
\nu_{E}(x)=e_{d}
;
then in a neighborhood of
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\partial E
we have that
\partial E
is the graph of a
C^{2}
- function
f:B^{\prime}(x^{\prime},r)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, for some
r>0
with
\mathrm{D}f(x^{\prime})=0
so that
B(x,r)\cap E=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in B(x,r)\,:\,x_{d}\leq f(x^{\prime})\}
. In this case the mean curvature of
\partial E
at
x
is given by

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Div}\bigg(\frac{-\mathrm{D}f}{\sqrt{1+|\mathrm{D}f|^{2}}}\bigg)(x^{\prime})=-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{x_{i}^{2}}}f(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x^{\prime})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,,

where
\theta^{*}
is the row vector obtained by transposing the (column) vector
\theta
and
\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x^{\prime})
denotes the Hessian matrix of
f
evaluated at
x^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The standard mean curvature
\mathcal{K}^{1}
satisfies the following properties:

(M)

Monotonicity: If

E,F\in\mathfrak{C}
with
E\subseteq F
, and if
x\in\partial F\cap\partial E
,
then
\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,F)\leq\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)
;

(T)

Translational invariance: For every
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
,

y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds:
\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)=\mathcal{K}^{1}(x+y,E+y)
;

(B)

Lower bound on the curvature of the balls:

\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho))\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }x\in\partial B(0,\rho)\,,\,\rho>0\,;

(S)

Symmetry:
For every
E\in\mathfrak{C}
and for every
x\in\partial E
it holds

\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)=-\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overset{\circ}{E})\,.

(UC’)

Uniform continuity: Given
R>0
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega_{R}
such that the following holds.
For every
E\in\mathfrak{C}
,
x\in\partial E
, such that
E
has both an internal
and external ball condition of radius
R
at
x
,
and for every diffeomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of class
C^{2}
,
with
\Phi=\mathrm{Id}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,1)
, we have

(4.22)

|\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E)-\mathcal{K}^{1}(\Phi(x),\Phi(E))|\leq\omega_{R}(\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}})\,.

Proof.

We prove only the property (UC’) , since the check of the remaining properties is straightforward.
Let
R>0
and let
E\in\mathfrak{C}
be such that
E
satisfies both an internal
and external ball condition of radius
R
at a point
x\in\partial E
.
In order to get the claim, we can always assume without loss of generality that
\|\Phi-\mathrm{Id}\|_{C^{2}}\leq 1
.

By the Implicit Function Theorem we have that
E\cap B(x,r)=\{z\in B(x,r):\;g(z)<0\}
, for some
r>0
and
g\in C^{2}(B(x,r))
. Moreover, in suitable coordinates we have that
x=0
,
\mathrm{D}g(0)=e_{d}
and, for all
i\neq j
, with
i,\,j=1,\cdots,d
,
\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z_{i}\partial z_{j}}(0)=0
. Then, it is well known that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{1}(0,E)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Div}_{\tau}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{D}g}{|\mathrm{D}g|}\Big)(0)=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z_{i}^{2}}(0)\,,

where
\mathrm{Div}_{\tau}
denotes the tangential divergence operator.
Since the mean curvature is invariant by translations and rotations, up to small perturbations of
\Phi
in
C^{2}
we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\Phi(0)=0
and that the normal to
\Phi(E)
at
\Phi(0)=0
is still
e_{d}
. Since

\Phi(E)\cap B(0,\tilde{r})=\{y\in B(0,\tilde{r}):\;g(\Phi^{-1}(y))<0\}

for some
\tilde{r}>0
, setting
h:=g\circ\Phi^{-1}
,
we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{1}(0,\Phi(E))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial y_{j}^{2}}(0)-\frac{1}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\,\frac{\partial|\mathrm{D}h|}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\,.

Therefore, using that

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}h(0)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}g(0)\,\mathrm{D}\Phi^{-1}(0)=e_{d}\,\mathrm{D}\Phi^{-1}(0)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial y_{j}\partial y_{k}}(0)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z_{i}^{2}}(0)\,\frac{\partial\Phi_{i}^{-1}}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\,\frac{\partial\Phi_{i}^{-1}}{\partial y_{k}}(0)+\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{d}^{-1}}{\partial y_{j}\partial y_{k}}(0)\,,

we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial y^{2}_{j}}(0)-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z^{2}_{i}}(0)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi^{-1}_{d}}{\partial y_{j}^{2}}(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z^{2}_{i}}(0)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\Big(\frac{\partial\Phi^{-1}_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\Big)^{2}-1\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle+\frac{|\mathrm{D}h(0)-1|}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|}\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial^{2}g}{\partial z^{2}_{i}}(0)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[\|\mathrm{D}^{2}\Phi^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}g\|_{C^{0}}\|\mathrm{Id}-(\mathrm{D}\Phi^{-1})^{2}\|_{C^{0}}+\|\mathrm{D}^{2}g\|_{C^{0}}\|\mathrm{Id}-\mathrm{D}\Phi^{-1}\|_{C^{0}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(1+\frac{1}{R}\Big)\|\mathrm{Id}-\Phi\|_{C^{2}}\,.

Similar computations (that are left to the reader) show that

(4.26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathrm{D}h(0)|^{2}}\bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{\partial h}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\,\frac{\partial|\mathrm{D}h|}{\partial y_{j}}(0)\bigg|\leq C\Big(1+\frac{1}{R}\Big)\|\mathrm{Id}-\Phi\|_{C^{2}}\,.

Therefore, (
4.22
) follows from (
4.23
), (
4.24
), (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) .
∎

4.3.
Convergence of
k_{r}^{s}
-curvature flow to mean curvature flow

We now prove that the viscosity solutions to the
k_{r}^{s}
-curvature flow converge to the classical mean curvature flow as
r\to 0^{+}
. To this end, we will adopt notation and we will use results in
[
16
]
.

We preliminarily notice that since the curvatures
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
defined in (
4.1
) satisfy property (UC) in Proposition
4.2
, they also satisfy property (UC’) in Proposition
4.3
(with
\mathcal{K}^{1}
replaced by
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
). As a consequence
\mathcal{K}^{1}
and
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
(for every
0<r<1
and
s\geq 1
) satisfy the following continuity property:

(C)

Continuity: If
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathfrak{C}
,
E\in\mathfrak{C}
and
E_{n}\to E
in
\mathfrak{C}
, then the corresponding curvatures of
E_{n}
at
x
converge to the curvature of
E
at
x
for every
x\in\partial E_{n}\cap\partial E
.

Such a property, together with properties (M) and (T) (see Propositions
4.3
and
4.2
), implies that the functionals
\mathcal{K}^{1}
and
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
(for every
s\geq 1
and for every
r\in(0,1)
) are non-local curvatures in the sense of
[
16
, Definition 2.1]
(see also
[
18
]
).

Moreover, since by Propositions
4.3
and
4.2
,
\mathcal{K}^{1}
and
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
satisfy also properties (B) and (UC’) (referred to as (C’) in
[
16
]
), they both satisfy the assumptions of
[
16
, Theorem 2.9]
that guarantee existence and uniqueness of suitably defined viscosity solutions of the corresponding geometric flows. We refer to
[
16
, Definition 2.3]
for the precise definition of viscosity solution in this setting, while for the reader’s convenience we state the existence and uniqueness result specialized to the geometric evolutions considered in this paper.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
s\geq 1
and
r>0
. Let
u_{0}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a uniformly continuous function with
u_{0}=C_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(0,R_{0})
for some
C_{0},R_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
with
R_{0}>0
. Then, there exists a unique viscosity solution - in the sense of
[
16
, Definition 2.3]
-
u^{s}_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
to the Cauchy problem

(4.27)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+|\mathrm{D}u(x,t)|\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,\{y\,:\,u(y,t)\geq u(x,t)\})=0\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, the same result holds true if
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
is replaced by (any multiple of)
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}
and let
\{M_{r}\}_{r>0},\{N_{r}\}_{r>0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}
be such that
M_{r}\rightarrow M,\;N_{r}\rightarrow N
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Then, for every
\delta>0
it holds

(4.28)

\begin{split}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}&\bigg(\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\Big)\bigg)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N-M)\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}:=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\;(y^{\prime})^{*}M_{r}y^{\prime}\leq y_{d}\leq(y^{\prime})^{*}N_{r}y^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}_{r,\delta}:=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\;(y^{\prime})^{*}N_{r}y^{\prime}\leq y_{d}\leq(y^{\prime})^{*}M_{r}y^{\prime}\}\,.

Proof.

For every
\alpha>0
we set

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\alpha}:=&\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,y^{\prime}=\rho\theta,\;0\leq\rho\leq\alpha,\;\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}\,,\\
&\rho^{2}\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta\leq y_{d}\leq\rho^{2}\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta\}\end{split}

Therefore, for
r
small enough,

\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}=\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\cap B(0,\delta)\,,\qquad\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}\cap B(0,r)=\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\cap B(0,r)\,,\\
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}\setminus B(0,r)=\big((\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r})\cap(B(0,\delta)\big)\cup\big(\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)\big)\,.

It follows that

(4.29)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\cap B(0,r)}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\cap B(0,r)}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\,.\\
\end{split}

We set

A^{1}_{r}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}\,:\,\theta^{*}(M_{r}-N_{r})\theta\leq 0\}

and we notice that

(4.30)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{0}^{r}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{d-2}\int_{\rho^{2}\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\rho^{2}\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\;\mathrm{d}y_{d}\\
&+\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{d-2}\int_{\rho^{2}\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\rho^{2}\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(\rho^{2}+y_{d}^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}y_{d}\\
=&\frac{1}{d+1}\frac{r^{d+1}}{r^{d+s}}\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
&+\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\,\rho^{d-2}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(\rho^{2}+\rho^{4}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\frac{r^{1-s}}{d+1}\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
&+\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t\,,\end{split}

where in the last but one equality we have used the change of variable
y_{d}=\rho^{2}t
.

Moreover, trivially we have

(4.31)

\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\delta}\setminus B(0,\delta)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq C(\delta)\,,

for some
C(\delta)>0
.
Furthermore, it is easy to see that, for
r
small enough,

\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)\subset\big(B^{\prime}(0,r)\setminus B^{\prime}(0,r-cr^{2})\big)\times[-cr^{2},cr^{2}]

for some constant
c>0
independent of
r
;
as a consequence,

|\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)|\leq Cr^{d+2}\,,

whence we deduce that

(4.32)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq Cr^{2-s}\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{|y|^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}^{1}_{r}\setminus B(0,r)}\frac{1}{r^{d+s}}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq Cr^{2-s}\,.

Therefore, by (
4.29
), (
4.30
), (
4.31
) and (
4.32
), we obtain

(4.33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{1-s}}{(d+1)\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{A^{1}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+f^{1}(r)\,,

where
f^{1}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0^{+}
.

Now we set

A^{2}_{r}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-2}\,:\,\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\leq 0\}\,;

by arguing as in the proof of (
4.33
) we obtain

(4.34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{1-s}}{(d+1)\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{A^{2}_{r}}\theta^{*}(M_{r}-N_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{A^{2}_{r}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+f^{2}(r)\,,

where
f^{2}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 0^{+}
. Therefore by formulas (
4.33
) and (
4.34
), using that

, we get

(4.35)

\begin{split}&\bigg(\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{r,\delta}}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\Big)\bigg)\\
=&\bigg[\frac{r^{1-s}}{(d+1)\sigma^{s}(r)}\bigg]\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
&+\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t\\
&+f^{1}(r)-f^{2}(r)\,.\end{split}

Since

\frac{r^{1-s}}{(d+1)\sigma^{s}(r)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{(d+1)|\log r|}&\textrm{ if }s=1\\
\frac{s-1}{d+s}&\textrm{ if }s>1\,,\end{array}\right.

and recalling that
M_{r}
and
N_{r}
converge to
M
and

(4.36)

\begin{split}&\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{r^{1-s}}{(d+1)\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N_{r}-M_{r})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 0&\text{if $s=1$},\\
\displaystyle\frac{s-1}{d+s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N-M)\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\theta)&\text{if $s>1$\,.}\end{cases}\end{split}

Moreover, for every

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{r}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho=\frac{d+1}{d+s}\,,

which, by the Dominate Convergence Theorem, yields

(4.37)

\begin{split}&\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{\theta^{*}M_{r}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{r}\theta}\;\mathrm{d}t\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{r}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s}}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\\
=&\frac{d+1}{d+s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N-M)\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,.\end{split}

By formulas (
4.35
), (
4.36
) and (
4.37
) we obtain (
4.28
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
s\geq 1
. Let
\{E_{r}\}_{r>0}\subset\mathfrak{C}

\mathfrak{C}

, it holds

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,E_{r})}{\sigma^{s}(r)}=\omega_{d-1}\mathcal{K}^{1}(x,E).

Proof.

Let
x\in\partial E\cap\partial E_{r}
for all
r>0
. By Proposition
4.2
we have that the curvatures
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
satisfy properties (S) and (T); moreover, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}
are invariant by rotations. Therefore, we can assume without loss of generality that
E
and
\{E_{r}\}_{r>0}
are compact, and that
x=0

\nu_{E}(0)=\nu_{E_{r}}(0)=e_{d}
for all

.
Since

in
\mathfrak{C}
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
we have that there exist
\delta>0
and functions

such that
\phi_{r}\rightarrow\phi

C^{2}
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
\phi(0)=\phi_{r}(0)=0
,
\mathrm{D}\phi(0)=\mathrm{D}\phi_{r}(0)=0
and

\begin{split}\partial E\cap B(0,\delta)&=\{(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})):\;y^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,\delta)\}\cap B(0,\delta)\,,\\
\partial E_{r}\cap B(0,\delta)&=\{(y^{\prime},\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})):\;y^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,\delta)\}\cap B(0,\delta)\,,\\
E\cap B(0,\delta)&=\{(y^{\prime},y_{d}):\;y^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,\delta)\,,\;y_{d}\leq\phi(y^{\prime})\}\cap B(0,\delta)\,,\\
E_{r}\cap B(0,\delta)&=\{(y^{\prime},y_{d}):\;y^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,\delta)\,,\;y_{d}\leq\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\}\cap B(0,\delta)\,.\end{split}

Let
\eta>0
be fixed ; for
\delta

(4.38)

\Big|\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}\Big|\leq\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\quad\textrm{ for every }0<r<1\,,\;y^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,\delta).

We define the following sets

\displaystyle A(r):=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\,-\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\leq y_{d}\leq\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\}\,,

\displaystyle B(r):=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\,\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\leq y_{d}\leq-\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\}\,,

\displaystyle C(r):=

\displaystyle\big(E_{r}^{c}\setminus B(r)\big)\cap B(0,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\,y_{d}\geq\max\{\phi_{r}(y^{\prime}),-\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\}\}\,,

\displaystyle D(r):=

\displaystyle\big(E_{r}\setminus A(r)\big)\cap B(0,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\,y_{d}\leq\min\{\phi_{r}(y^{\prime}),-\phi_{r}(y^{\prime})\}\}\,,

where the equalities above are understood in the sense of measurable sets, i.e., up to negligible sets.
We notice that

E_{r}\cap B(0,\delta)=A(r)\cup D(r)\,,\qquad E^{c}_{r}\cap B(0,\delta)=B(r)\cup C(r)\,,\\
\int_{C(r)}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y=\int_{D(r)}k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\,,

whence we deduce that

(4.39)

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(0,E_{r})=&\int_{B(0,\delta)}(\chi_{E_{r}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E_{r}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{B^{c}(0,\delta)}(\chi_{E_{r}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E_{r}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B(r)}(y)-\chi_{A(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{C(r)}(y)-\chi_{D(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{B^{c}(0,\delta)}(\chi_{E_{r}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E_{r}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B(r)}(y)-\chi_{A(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\int_{B^{c}(0,\delta)}(\chi_{E_{r}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E_{r}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\,.\end{split}

Trivially,

\Big|\int_{B^{c}(0,\delta)}(\chi_{E_{r}^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E_{r}}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\Big|\leq d\omega_{d}\frac{\delta^{-s}}{s}\,.

In order to study the limit

we define the following sets

\displaystyle A^{-}(r):=\Big\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:

\displaystyle A^{+}(r):=\Big\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}-\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\leq y_{d}\leq\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}+\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle B^{-}(r):=\Big\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}+\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\leq y_{d}\leq-\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}-\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle B^{+}(r):=\Big\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}-\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\leq y_{d}\leq-\frac{1}{2}(y^{\prime})^{*}\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi_{r}(0)y^{\prime}+\eta|y^{\prime}|^{2}\Big\}\,.

By (

) we have that

and hence

(4.40)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B^{-}(r)}(y)-\chi_{A^{+}(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\

\leq&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B^{+}(r)}(y)-\chi_{A^{-}(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Then, by applying Lemma
4.5
and using (
4.40
), we obtain

(4.41)

\begin{split}&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(0)+2\eta\mathrm{Id})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
\leq&\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B(r)}(y)-\chi_{A(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\chi_{B(r)}(y)-\chi_{A(r)}(y))k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(0)-2\eta\mathrm{Id})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
4.39
) and (
4.41
), we get

\begin{split}&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(0)+2\eta\mathrm{Id})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
\leq&\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(0,E_{r})\leq\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(0,E_{r})\\
\leq&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi(0)-2\eta\mathrm{Id})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,,\end{split}

which, sending
\eta

4.21

Theorem 4.7

By Theorem
4.6

\rho>0

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho)\geq 0

4.6
we get that
\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{K}^{s}_{r}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho))<+\infty
.
This trivially implies the following property:

(UB)

There exists
K>0
such that
\inf_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho))\geq-K\rho
for all
\rho>1
,
x\in\partial B(0,\rho)
and
\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\mathcal{K}_{r}^{s}(x,\overline{B}(0,\rho))<+\infty
for all
\rho>0
,
x\in\partial B(0,\rho)
.

Properties (M), (T), (S), (UC’) (referred to as (C’) in
[
16
]
) and (UB), together with the convergence of the curvatures on regular sets, are exactly the assumptions of
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
, which, in our case, establishes the convergence of

to

locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T]
for every

.
∎

5.
Stability as
r\to 0^{+}
and

simultaneously

In this section we study
\Gamma
-convergence and compactness properties for the

-fractional perimeters

defined in (

and

(with

) simultaneously. Moreover, we study the convergence of the corresponding geometric flows in such a case.
In fact, we will consider only the critical case

Let

\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)

and
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,+\infty)
be such that
r_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
s_{n}\to 1^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.
Recalling the definitions of
\sigma^{s}(r)
in (
1.5
) and
\bm{\alpha}^{s}
in (

), we set

(5.1)

\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n}):=\sigma^{s_{n}}(r_{n})+\bm{\alpha}^{s_{n}}=\frac{d+s_{n}}{d+1}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-1}{s_{n}-1}+\frac{1}{d+1}

and we notice that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-1}{s_{n}-1}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{r_{n}}^{1}\rho^{-s_{n}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{r_{n}}^{1}\rho^{-1}\;\mathrm{d}\rho=\lim_{n\to+\infty}|\log r_{n}|=+\infty\,.

5.1.
\Gamma
-convergence and compactness

In Theorem
5.1
below we study the
\Gamma
-convergence of the functionals
\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}
as
n\to+\infty
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
and
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,+\infty)
be such that
r_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
s_{n}\to 1^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.
The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness) Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set and let
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
E_{n}\subset U
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(E_{n})\leq M\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})\quad\text{for every $n\in\mathbb{N}$,}

for some constant
M
independent of
n

\chi_{E_{n}}\rightarrow\chi_{E}
strongly in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some set

with
\mathrm{Per}(E)<+\infty
.

(ii)

(Lower bound) Let
E\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\chi_{E_{n}}\to\chi_{E}
strongly in

\omega_{d-1}\mathrm{Per}(E)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(E_{n})}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\,.

(iii)

(Upper bound) For every
E\in\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists
\{E_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{M}_{f}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\chi_{E_{n}}\to\chi_{E}

\omega_{d-1}\mathrm{Per}(E)=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\tilde{J}_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(E_{n})}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\,.

5.1.1.
Proof of compactness

Lemma 5.2

Let

Proof.

The proof is fully analogous to the one of Lemma
2.4

For every

By arguing verbatim as in the proof of (

and for every

(5.3)

\displaystyle k_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(|z|)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{d+s_{n}}-1}{2^{d+s_{n}}}\frac{1}{\sup\phi}\sum_{i=0}^{I_{n}}(2^{s_{n}})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{d+1}}{2^{d+1}-1}\frac{1}{\sup\phi}\sum_{i=0}^{I_{n}}(2^{s_{n}})^{i}\phi_{2^{-i}}(z)\,.

Moreover, since

\frac{|\log r_{n}|}{\log 2}-1\leq I_{n}\leq\frac{|\log r_{n}|}{\log 2}\,,

setting

(5.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{I_{n}}(2^{s_{n}-1})^{i}=&\frac{\big(2^{s_{n}-1}\big)^{I_{n}+1}-1}{2^{s_{n}-1}-1}\geq\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-1}{2^{s_{n}-1}-1}\\

\geq&\frac{m(S)}{2}\frac{d+1}{d+S}\Big(\frac{d+s_{n}}{d+1}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-1}{s_{n}-1}+\frac{1}{d+1}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-1}{s_{n}-1}\Big)\\
\geq&\frac{m(S)}{2}\frac{d+1}{d+S}\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})\,,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used that, in view of (

Therefore, by arguing as in (
2.8

Convergence of the

Here we study the convergence of the curvatures

in (

First we prove the following lemma which is the analogous of Lemma
4.5
in the case treated in this section.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(1,+\infty)
and
\{r_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
be such that
r_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
s_{n}\to 1^{+}
as
n\to+\infty
.
Let
M,N\in\mathbb{R}^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}
and let
\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{N_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}
be such that
M_{n}\rightarrow M,\;N_{n}\rightarrow N
as
n\to+\infty

Then, for every
\delta>0
, it holds

(5.19)

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}&\bigg(\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{n,\delta}}k_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n,\delta}}k_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\Big)\bigg)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N-M)\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{1}_{n,\delta}:=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\;(y^{\prime})^{*}M_{n}y^{\prime}\leq y_{d}\leq(y^{\prime})^{*}N_{n}y^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n,\delta}:=

\displaystyle\{y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(0,\delta)\,:\;(y^{\prime})^{*}N_{n}y^{\prime}\leq y_{d}\leq(y^{\prime})^{*}M_{n}y^{\prime}\}\,.

Proof.

By arguing verbatim as in the proof of (
4.35
) one can show that

(5.20)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{n,\delta}}k_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{n,\delta}}k_{r_{n}}^{s_{n}}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\\
=&\frac{{r_{n}}^{1-s_{n}}}{(d+1)\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N_{n}-M_{n})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\\
&+\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\int_{\theta^{*}M_{n}\theta}^{\theta^{*}N_{n}\theta}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}t\,,\\
&+\eta_{n}\,,\end{split}

where
\eta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
.
It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{{r_{n}}^{1-s_{n}}}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}=0\,,

whence we get

(5.21)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}}{(d+1)\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}\theta^{*}(N_{n}-M_{n})\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)=0\,.

Now we claim that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

(5.22)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho=1\,,

which in view of
(
5.20
) and (
5.21
) and of the Dominate Convergence Theorem, implies (
5.19
).
In order to prove (
5.22
), we first notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho-\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\frac{r_{n}^{1-s_{n}}-\delta^{1-s_{n}}}{s_{n}-1}-\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\rho\,,

so that, by the very definition of
\bm{\beta}
in (
5.1
), it is enough to show that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\rho=0\,.

As for the proof of (
5.23
) we argue as follows. First we notice that, setting
S:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}s_{n}
,

(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}\leq 1+C(d,S,t)\rho^{2}\,,

so that, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{\rho^{s_{n}}}\frac{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}-1}{(1+\rho^{2}t^{2})^{\frac{d+s_{n}}{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bm{\beta}(r_{n},s_{n})}\int_{r_{n}}^{\delta}C(d,S,t)\rho^{2-s_{n}}\;\mathrm{d}\rho\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }n\to+\infty\,,

thus concluding the proof of (
5.23
) and of the whole lemma.
∎

\begin{split}\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}&\bigg(\frac{1}{\sigma^{s}(r)}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{F}^{1}_{r,\delta}}g\Big(\frac{y}{|y|}\Big)k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathcal{F}^{2}_{r,\delta}}g\Big(\frac{y}{|y|}\Big)k_{r}^{s}(|y|)\;\mathrm{d}y\Big)\bigg)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-2}}g(\theta,0)\theta^{*}(N-M)\theta\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-2}(\theta)\,.\end{split}

If
\nu_{E}(x)=e_{d}

, clearly using (

) in place of (

∎

Theorem 6.6
.

6.2.
Applications to dislocation dynamics

Here we apply the results in Subsection
6.1
to the motion of curved dislocations in the plane.
To this purpose, we briefly recall and describe, in an informal way, some notions about the isotropic linearized elastic energy induced by planar dislocations; such notions are well known to experts and we refer to classic books such as
[
23
]
for an exhaustive monography on this subject.

Let
E
be a bounded region of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,z_{3}=0\}
, representing a plastic slip region with Burgers vector
b=e_{1}=(1,0,0)
. Formally, the elastic energy induced by such a dislocation is given by

(6.11)

J(E):=\frac{\bm{\mu}}{8\pi}\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{5}}\Big(\frac{1+\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}x_{1}^{2}+\frac{1-2\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}x_{2}^{2}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where
\bm{\mu}>0
and
\bm{\nu}\in(-1,\frac{1}{2})
are the shear modulus and the Poisson’s ratio, respectively.
Formula (
6.11
) can be deduced by
[
23
, formula (4-44)]
, by integrating by parts.
Clearly, the energy
J
in (
6.11
) is always infinite whenever
E
is non-empty.
It is well understood that such an infinite energy should be suitably truncated through ad hoc core regularizations, specific of the microscopic details of the underlying crystal. The specific choice of the core regularization, giving back the physically relevant (finite) elastic energy induced by the dislocation is, for our purposes, irrelevant. Here we adopt the energy-renormalization procedure introduced in (
6.1
). First we set

(6.12)

g(\xi):=\frac{\bm{\mu}}{8\pi}\Big(\frac{1+\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}\xi_{1}^{2}+\frac{1-2\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}\xi_{2}^{2}\Big)\,,\quad\textrm{ for every }\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\,,

and we notice that the energy in (
6.11
) can be (formally) rewritten as

\displaystyle J(E)=\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}g\Big(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{3}}\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{E}\int_{E^{c}}k^{g}(x-y)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where
k^{g}
is defined by
k^{g}(z):=g(\frac{z}{|z|})\frac{1}{|z|^{3}}
.
The core-regularization of
J
is given by the functional
\tilde{J}^{g,1}_{r}
defined by (
6.1
), where the parameter
r>0
plays the role of the core-size.
Now, consider the dynamics of a dislocation curve, enclosing a (moving) bounded set
E
, with Burgers vector equal to
e_{1}
, governed by a self-energy release mechanism. We consider a geometric evolution, that can be formally understood as the gradient flow of the self-energy
\tilde{J}^{g,1}_{r}
with respect to an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
structure on the (graphs locally describing the) evolving dislocation curve. If the energy were the standard perimeter, this evolution would be nothing but the standard mean curvature flow. Notice that the energy considered here is nonlocal; moreover, although it is derived from isotropic linearized elasticity, it has in fact an anisotropic dependence (induced by the direction of the given Burgers vector) on the normal to the curve. Another possible source of anisotropy is the so-called mobility, depending on the microscopic details of the underlying crystalline lattice; here, for simplicity, we assume that such a mobility is in fact isotropic, equal to one. The dynamics discussed above corresponds
to the geometric evolution where the normal velocity of the evolving dislocation curve at any point
x
is given by
-\mathcal{K}^{g,1}_{r}
, defined in (
6.6
).

In order to study the asymptotic behavior, as
r\to 0^{+}
, of the dynamics described above we use the results developed in Subsection
6.1
.
First, we notice that that the function
g
defined in (
6.12
) is continuous (actually, it is smooth) and even, so that it satisfies the assumptions required in Subsection
6.1
. Moreover, recalling (
6.3
) and (
6.8
), for the choice of
g
in (
6.12
), an easy computation shows that

\displaystyle\varphi^{g}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{\mu}}{12\pi}\Big(\frac{1+\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}(1+\nu_{1}^{2})+\frac{1-2\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}(1+\nu_{2}^{2})\Big)\,,\textrm{ for every }\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{g,1}(x,E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{\mu}}{8\pi}\Big(\frac{1+\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}(\nu_{E}(x))_{2}^{2}+\frac{1-2\bm{\nu}}{1-\bm{\nu}}(\nu_{E}(x))_{1}^{2}\Big)\,,\textrm{ for every }E\in\mathfrak{C}\,,x\in\partial E\,.

Therefore,
by Theorem
6.6
, the unique (in the level set sense) dislocation dynamics described above, converges, as
r\to 0^{+}
, to a degenerate evolution where the dislocation disappear instantaneously. After a logarithmic in time reparametrization, the evolution converges to the anisotropic mean curvature flow governed by the release of the line tension energy
\mathrm{Per}^{g}
(
6.2
), corresponding to the anisotropic energy density
\varphi^{g}
defined above. Such a dynamics is nothing but the evolution
t\mapsto\partial E_{t}
, where the normal velocity of the evolving dislocation curve
\partial E_{t}
at any point
x\in\partial E_{t}
is given by the (opposite of the) anisotropic curvature
\mathcal{K}^{g,1}(x,E_{t})
defined above.

Integrable structure of BCD conformal field theory and boundary Bethe ansatz for affine Yangian
Alexey Litvinov, Ilya Vilkoviskiy[] 1. Landau Institute for Theoretical Physics, 142432 Chernogolovka, Russia 2. Center for Advanced Studies, Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology, 143026 Moscow, Russia 3. National Research University Higher School of Economics, 119048 Moscow, Russia

Abstract
In these notes we study integrable structures of conformal field theory with BCD symmetry. We realise these integrable structures as affine Yangian 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 1 ) \mathfrak{gl}(1) ”spin chains” with boundaries. We provide three solutions of Sklyanin KRKR equation compatible with affine Yangian R R -matrix and derive Bethe ansatz equations for the spectrum.

\mathrm{BCD}

The integrable systems studied in this paper can be realized by the

[\mathbf{I}_{r},\mathbf{I}_{s}]=0.

(2.2)

The best way to describe our integrable systems goes through affine Toda QFT

5.2

Our

Being rewritten in terms of

Two immediate consequences of these relations are:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{K}|\mathfrak{h}(u)=\langle\mathcal{K}|\mathfrak{h}(-u),

(5.28)

with

(5.29)

This equation immediately follows from (
5.26
), (
5.27
) after substitution

\displaystyle=-\kappa(u)\langle\mathcal{K}|\mathfrak{h}(u)\mathfrak{f}(-u-\epsilon_{3})=-\frac{\epsilon_{3}\kappa(u)}{2u+\epsilon_{3}}\langle\mathcal{K}|\mathfrak{f}(u)\mathfrak{h}(u)-\frac{2u\kappa(u)}{2u+\epsilon_{3}}\langle\mathcal{K}|\mathfrak{f}(-u-\epsilon_{3})h(u)

Finally we have

\displaystyle\kappa(u)=1\,,\quad r(u-\epsilon_{3}/2)=-\frac{u+\epsilon_{3}/2}{u-\epsilon_{3}/2}\quad\text{for the {D} case},

(5.30)

\displaystyle\kappa(u)=\frac{u-\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}/2}{u+\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}/2}\,,\quad r(u-\epsilon_{3}/2)=-\frac{u+\epsilon_{i}/2}{u-\epsilon_{i}/2}\quad\text{for the {BC} case},

(5.31)

where in the last line

corresponds to the B case and
\{i,j\}=\{2,1\}
corresponds to the C case.

Relations (

(5.32)

R
-matrix satisfies two basic properties of

\displaystyle\text{Unitarity}:

\displaystyle R[\partial\varphi_{i}-\partial\varphi_{j}]R[\partial\varphi_{j}-\partial\varphi_{i}]=1,

(6.1)

\displaystyle\text{T-symmetry}:

\displaystyle R^{t}[\partial\varphi_{i}-\partial\varphi_{j}]=R[\partial\varphi_{i}-\partial\varphi_{j}],

(6.2)

which follow immediately from the defining relations (

R^{t_{i}}[\partial\varphi_{i}-\partial\varphi_{j}]R^{t_{i}}[\partial\varphi_{j}-\partial\varphi_{i}]=1.

(6.3)

is more subtle, as it mixes different levels and involves infinite sums of matrix elements. It is questionable if one can make it any sense. Even if we believe that (
6.3

\mathrm{D}

\mathcal{T}(u)=\textrm{Tr}\Big|_{0}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\bar{0},1}\dots\mathcal{R}_{\bar{0},n}\mathcal{R}_{0,n}\dots\mathcal{R}_{0,1}\right)

(6.4)

This formula requires more accurate definition as it involves divergent summation over infinite dimensional Fock space. In the
\mathrm{A}
case this divergence has been regularised by an introduction of the twist parameter which preserved the integrability. It is unclear whether such a twist can be introduced in the present case as well. This certainly remains as open interesting question.

K
-matrices.

We have mentioned in the main text that there are only three solutions of Sklyanin reflection equation (
3.12
), which commute with the level. In such a case one can always set the vacuum eigenvalue of
\mathcal{K}
operator to
1
. Then, if we denote

\mathcal{K}a_{-1}|u\rangle=f(u)a_{-1}|u\rangle,

(6.13)

the reflection relation (
3.12
) on level
1
is equivalent to the functional relation

(u+v)(f(u)-f(v))=(u-v)(f(u)f(v)-1)\implies f(u)=\frac{\xi+u}{\xi-u},

(6.14)

where
\xi
is an arbitrary parameter. The reflection relation on level

Appendix A
Restoring the symmetry between
\epsilon_{\alpha}

One may note that affine Yangian commutation relations (
3.28
) are symmetric with respect to permutations of all
\epsilon_{\alpha}
. Nevertheless Bethe Ansatz equations (
4.13
) are not symmetric in all
\epsilon_{\alpha}
, because of the source term
A(x)=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x-u_{k}+\frac{\epsilon_{3}}{2}}{x-u_{k}-\frac{\epsilon_{3}}{2}}
. We are now in a position to restore the symmetry, which will help us to build more general integrable systems. The resolution of the paradox is the following: there actually exists three types of Fock modules
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{x}
. In order to describe Integrable systems, we have to define an
\mathcal{R}
-matrix acting between different Fock spaces
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{x_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{x_{2}}
. In the following we will use the results of
[
17
,
18
]
and also
[
12
]
where various integrable systems of this type considered in details for the
q
-deformed case. To the Fock module
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{v}
we assign a free bosonic field (
3.3
):

\partial\varphi(x)=-i\frac{v}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\gamma}}}+\sum\limits_{n\neq 0}a_{n}e^{-inx},\qquad[a_{m},a_{n}]=m\delta_{m,-n},

(A.1)

here
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\textrm{perm}(1,2,3)
. To the tensor product of two Fock modules we have to assign a
W
-algebra and an
R
-matrix. If the both Fock modules are of the same type
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{x_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{x_{2}}
then we assign to them two Screening currents:

S^{\pm}_{\alpha}=\oint e^{\left(\frac{\epsilon_{\beta}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}\right)^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\varphi_{1}(x)-\varphi_{2}(x))}dx,

(A.2)

where
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\text{perm}(1,2,3)
. The
W
-algebra which commutes with these Screenings consists of two currents of spin
1
and
2

\displaystyle W^{\alpha}_{1}=\partial\varphi_{1}(x)+\partial\varphi_{2}(x)

(A.3)

\displaystyle W^{\alpha}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi_{1}(x)-\partial\varphi_{2}(x))^{2}+\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\gamma}}}(\partial^{2}\varphi_{1}(x)-\partial^{2}\varphi_{2}(x))

(A.4)

defines the
R

\mathcal{R}_{1,2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}|\epsilon_{\alpha},\epsilon_{\beta},\epsilon_{\gamma})W^{\alpha}_{1,2}=W^{\alpha}_{1,2}\Big|_{\varphi_{1}\leftrightarrow\varphi_{2}}\mathcal{R}_{1,2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}|\epsilon_{\alpha},\epsilon_{\beta},\epsilon_{\gamma}).

(A.5)

Here

-matrix (

Now to Fock modules of different types:
\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{x_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\beta}_{x_{2}}

(A.6)

This screening called ”fermionic” because it is a zero mode of a free fermion:

\displaystyle\psi(x)=e^{\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{1}(x)-\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\beta}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{2}(x)}\,,\quad\psi^{\dagger}(x)=e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{1}(x)+\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\beta}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{2}(x)}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\psi(x)\psi^{\dagger}(y)=\frac{1}{\sin(x-y)}+reg

(A.8)

\displaystyle S_{f,{\gamma}}=\psi_{0}

(A.9)

Corresponding
W-

1
,
2

\displaystyle W_{f;1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\alpha}}}\partial\varphi_{1}(x)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{\beta}}}\partial\varphi_{2}(x)

(A.10)

\displaystyle W_{f;2}=(\partial\Phi(x))^{2}+\partial^{2}\Phi(x),

(A.11)

where
\Phi(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\alpha}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{1}(x)-\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\beta}}{\epsilon_{\gamma}}}\varphi_{2}(x)

\mathcal{R}_{1,2}^{\alpha,\beta}\ W_{f;1,2}=P_{1,2}(W_{f;1,2})\,\mathcal{R}_{1,2}^{\alpha,\beta},

(A.12)

here
P_{1,2}

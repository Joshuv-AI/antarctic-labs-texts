Maximum cliques in a graph without disjoint given subgraph
Fangfang Zhang, Yaojun Chen, Ervin Győri, Xiutao Zhu

Abstract
The generalized Turán number ex ⁡ ( n , K s , F ) {\rm ex}(n,K_{s},F) denotes the maximum number of copies of K s K_{s} in an n n -vertex F F -free graph. Let k ​ F kF denote k k disjoint copies of F F . Gerbner, Methuku and Vizer [DM, 2019, 3130-3141] gave a lower bound for ex ⁡ ( n , K 3 , 2 ​ C 5 ) {\rm ex}(n,K_{3},2C_{5}) and obtained the magnitude of ex ⁡ ( n , K s , k ​ K r ) {\rm ex}(n,K_{s},kK_{r}) . In this paper, we determine the exact value of ex ⁡ ( n , K 3 , 2 ​ C 5 ) {\rm ex}(n,K_{3},2C_{5}) and described the unique extremal graph for large n n . Moreover, we also determine the exact value of ex ⁡ ( n , K r , ( k + 1 ) ​ K r ) {\rm ex}(n,K_{r},(k+1)K_{r}) which generalizes some known results. Keywords : Generalized Turán number, disjoint union, extremal graph.

Suppose

is large enough and let

be an

-vertex

-free graph with

copies of triangles. Since

K_{3}(G)\geq\lfloor(n-1)^{2}/4\rfloor
. Next we will show that

,

C_{5}

K_{3}(G\setminus C)\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(n-5)^{\frac{3}{2}}+o((n-5)^{\frac{3}{2}}).

We claim that there is at least one vertex in

whose neighborhood contains a copy of

. To prove this, we need a theorem obtained by Bushaw and Kettle
[
3
]

Theorem 7

(Bushaw and Kettle
[

]
)
For

,

{\rm ex}(n,kP_{\ell})=\binom{k\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor-1}{2}+(k\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor-1)(n-k\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor+1)+\lambda,

where
\lambda=1
if

is odd, and

if

is even.

By Theorem 7, we know
{\rm ex}(n,6P_{4})\leq\max\left\{\binom{872}{2},11(n-6)\right\}
. Now suppose no vertex in
V(C)
contains
6P_{4}
in its neighborhood. Then the number of triangles containing

is at most

e(G[N(v_{i})])\leq{\rm ex}(n,6P_{4})=11n+o(n).

Therefore, the total number of triangles satisfies

\begin{split}K_{3}(G)&\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}+o(n^{\frac{3}{2}})+55n+o(n)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}+o(n^{\frac{3}{2}})\\
&<\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}.\end{split}

The last inequality holds when
n
is large. A contradiction.

Therefore, we may assume that

is the vertex in

. If

-free. So we have

K_{3}(G)\leq e(G\setminus v_{1})+K_{3}(G\setminus v_{1}).

(2.1)

So if we have

, then the proof is completed. To prove this, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1

. If

-free graph on

. For each integer

, if

contains a triangle, then

\displaystyle\phi(n)<\phi(n-1)-1.

First suppose that

\sum_{s=2}^{r}\left(\binom{kr}{s}-\binom{a}{s}\right)K_{r-s}(N)\leq\left(\binom{kr}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right)\binom{r-1}{r-2}\left(\frac{n-kr}{r-1}\right)^{r-2}+O(n^{r-3}).

Hence,

K_{r}(\overline{U})\leq\left(kr+\left(\binom{kr}{2}-\binom{a}{2}\right)\binom{r-1}{r-2}\right)\left(\frac{n-kr}{r-1}\right)^{r-2}+O(n^{r-3}).

(3.2)

Therefore, by inequality (
3
) and (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle K_{r}(G)\leq a\left(\frac{n-kr}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}+\left(kr+\binom{kr}{2}\binom{r-1}{r-2}\right)\left(\frac{n-kr}{r-1}\right)^{r-2}+O(n^{r-3}).

(3.3)

On the other hand, since
K_{k}+T_{r-1}(n-k)
is
(k+1)K_{r}
-free, we know that

\displaystyle K_{r}(G)\geq k\left(\frac{n-k}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}+O(n^{r-2}).

(3.4)

When
n
is greater than some constant
n_{0}(k,r)
, inequalites (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) hold mean
a=k
and then
U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}
.

Let
G^{\prime}=G\setminus U
. We claim that
G^{\prime}
is also
K_{r}
-free. Suppose not,
G^{\prime}
contains a
r
-clique, denote by
X_{0}^{\prime}
. Since each
u_{i}
is joined to at least
kr+1
independent copies of
K_{r-1}
’s in
N
, at least
(k-1)r+1
of whom are disjoint with
X_{0}^{\prime}
for each
i\in[k]
. Then we can find a
r
-clique
X_{1}^{\prime}
such that
u_{1}\in X_{1}^{\prime}
and
V(X_{1}^{\prime})\cap V(X_{0}^{\prime})=\emptyset
. Next, we claim that we may find another
k
independent
r
-cliques such that each is disjoint with
X_{0}^{\prime}
. Suppose we have found pairwise disjoint
r
-cliques
X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{i-1}^{\prime}
such that
u_{j}\in X_{j}^{\prime}
for
j\in[i-1]
and
i\leq k
. Then, in
G^{\prime}[N(u_{i})]
, there are at least
(k-1)r+1-(i-1)(r-1)\geq 1
independent
(r-1)
-cliques which disjoint with
\{X_{0}^{\prime},X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{i-1}^{\prime}\}
.
That is we can choose a
(r-1)
-clique and thus a
r
-clique
X_{i}^{\prime}
such
u_{i}\in X_{i}^{\prime}
and
X_{0}^{\prime},X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{i}^{\prime}
are pairwise disjoint. The procedure can keep going until we find
k
independent
r
-cliques
X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime}
. Then
X_{0}^{\prime},X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{k}^{\prime}
forms a
(k+1)K_{r}
, a contradiction.

Since
G^{\prime}
is
K_{r}
-free, by Zykov’s Theorem,
K_{r-i}(G^{\prime})\leq K_{r-i}(T_{r-1}(n-k))
and the equality holds if and only if
G^{\prime}=T_{r-1}(n-k)
. Thus

K_{r}(K_{k}+T_{r-1}(n-k))\leq K_{r}(G)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{k}{i}K_{r-i}(G^{\prime})=K_{r}(K_{k}+T_{r-1}(n-k)).

The condition of the equality holds means
G=K_{k}+T_{r-1}(n-k)
. The proof of Theorem
6
is completed.

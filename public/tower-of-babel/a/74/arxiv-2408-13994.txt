Introduction

In this paper, we only consider finite, simple and undirected graphs. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, where
V
is the vertex set and
E
is the edge set of
G
. We use
n
and
e(G)
to denote the order and size of
G
, respectively.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a family of graphs. A graph
G
is
\mathscr{F}
-free if
G
does not contain any
F\in\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph. The Turán number, denoted by
ex(n,\mathscr{F})
, is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
\mathscr{F}
-free graph. If
\mathscr{F}=\{F\}
, we write
ex(n,F)
instead of
ex(n,\mathscr{F})
.

The well-known result by Turán
[
15
]
asserted that
ex(n,K_{k+1})=e(T(n,k))
, where
T(n,k)
is the balanced complete
k
-partite graph. The Turán Theorem is considered as the origin of extremal graph theory.

Let
K_{l,t}
(
l\leq t
) be a complete bipartite graph, the numbers of vertices in each part are
l
and
t
respectively. A matching
M_{s}
is consist of
s
independent edges. There are lots of results about
ex(n,K_{l,t})
.
G
is
K_{1,t}
-free if and only if the maximum degree of
G
is less than
t
and then
ex(n,K_{1,t})=\lfloor\frac{(t-1)n}{2}\rfloor
.
Erdős and Gallai
[
7
]
showed that

ex(n,M_{s+1})=\max\left\{ns-{s+1\choose 2},{2s+1\choose 2}\right\}.

For
K_{2,2}=C_{4}
, i.e. a cycle with length
4
, the first result about
ex(n,C_{4})
was given by Kővári, Sós and Turán
[
12
]
. That is

ex(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{n}{4}(1+\sqrt{4n-3}).

(1)

Brown
[
4
]
, and Erdős, Rényi and Sós
[
14
]
independently and simultaneously proved that

ex(q^{2}+q+1,C_{4})\geq\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)^{2}

for all prime powers
q
.
Füredi
[
8
,
9
]
proved that for all prime powers
q\geq 14,

ex(q^{2}+q+1,C_{4})=\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)^{2}.

He, Ma and Yang
[
11
]
showed that when
q
is large enough and is an even integer, all
C_{4}
-free graphs
G
with
q^{2}+q+1
vertices and
e(G)\geq\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)^{2}-0.2q+1
are subgraphs of a unique polarity graph of order
q.
But there is still no results about the value of
ex(n,C_{4})
for a general positive integer
n
.
A result of
ex(n,K_{2,t})
was given by Füredi
[
16
]
who proved when
t\geq 2
,

(n,K_{2,t})=(1+o(1))\sqrt{t-1}n^{3/2}/2.

On general case when
2\leq l\leq t
, the celebrated Kővári–Sós–Turán Theorem
[
12
]
stated that

ex(n,K_{l,t})=O(n^{2-\frac{1}{t}}),

one of its proof was given by Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
2
]
. But the upper bound has not been proved to be tight unless
l\in\{2,3\}
or
t
is sufficiently large with respect to
l

[
3
,
5
]
.

Recently, Alon and Frankl
[
1
]
proposed to consider the maximum number of edges of a
F
-free graph on
n
vertices with matching number at most
s
, that is,
ex({n,\{F,M_{s+1}\}})
. In the same paper, they proved that
ex(n,\{K_{k+1},M_{s+1}\})=e(G(n,k,s)),

where
G(n,k,s)
denotes the complete
k
-partite graph on
n
vertices consisting of
k-1
vertex classes of sizes as equal as possible whose total size is
s
, and one additional vertex class of size
n-s
. They also showed that for any
F
with chromatic number
k+1
and a color-critical edge (an edge that will decrease the chromatic number when deleted),
ex(n,\{F,M_{s+1}\})=e(G(n,k,s)).

Later, Gerbner
[
10
]
gave several results about
ex({n,\{F,M_{s+1}\}})
. For example, he proved that if the chromatic number of
F
is at least 3 and
n
is large enough, then
ex(n,\{F,M_{s+1}\})=ex(s,\mathscr{F})+s(n-2)
, where
\mathscr{F}
is the family of graphs obtained by deleting an independent set from
F
. If
F
is a bipartite graph, let
p(F)
denote the smallest possible order of a color class in a proper two-coloring of
F
, then
ex(n,\{F,M_{s+1}\})=(p-1)n+O(1)
, where
p(F)\leq s
.
Chvátal and Hanson
[
6
]
gave the result of maximum star-free graph with bounded matching number, i.e.
ex(n,\{K_{1,t},M_{s+1}\})
. See
[
6
]
for more details.

In this paper, we consider
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
for
t\geq l\geq 2
. Note that if
s\leq l
, then
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=ex(n,M_{s+1})
because the matching number of
K_{l,t}
is
l
. On the other hand, if
n\leq 2s
, then
ex(n,\{F,M_{s+1}\})=ex(n,F).
Hence we just consider the case
s\geq l+1
and
n\geq 2s+1
. Let

\begin{array}[]{rcl}F_{1}(x)&=&(l-1)n+(t-1){x\choose l}+{2(s-x)+1\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil,\\
F_{2}(x)&=&(l-1)n+(t-1){x\choose l}+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil-(2(s-x)+1)(l-1).\end{array}

Denote
r_{1}=\max_{\max\{2s-t+1,2l\}\leq 2x\leq 2s}\{F_{1}(x)\}
and
r_{2}=\max_{2l\leq 2x\leq 2s-t}\{F_{2}(x)\}
.
Let

n(x)=x+(t-1){x\choose l}+2(s-x)+1

and
n_{1}=\max_{0\leq x\leq s}n(x)
. It is easy to find that
n_{1}=\max\{n(0),n(s)\}
. The following theorems are our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
3\leq l\leq t
and
n\geq n_{1}
.
Then we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\geq\max\{r_{1},r_{2}\}.

When
s
is large enough and
n\geq 2{3s\choose 2}
, we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\leq(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

If
s
is large enough, we can show that the exact value of
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
is the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
3\leq l\leq t
. If
s
is large enough and
n\geq n(s)
, then

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

Proof.

It is easy to check that when
s
is large enough and
l\geq 3
,
n_{1}=n(s)
and
n(s)\geq 2{3s\choose 2}
.
Note that
ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})=O((s-x)^{2-1/t}).
If
l\geq 3
,
F_{1}(x)
and
F_{2}(x)
are monotonically increasing when
s
is large enough.
Hence

\max\{r_{1},r_{2}\}=F_{1}(s)=(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

By Theorem
1.1
, we are done.

For
K_{2,t}
with
t\geq 2
, we can give more specific results.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq{s\choose 2}+s+1
and
s\geq 12
. Then we have

ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})=n+{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
n\geq{s\choose 2}+s+1
,
t\geq 3
and
s
is large enough, then we have

ex(n,\{K_{2,t},M_{s+1}\})=n+(t-1){s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil.

In section

The following theorem is about the matching number of a graph.

Theorem 2.1
(Tutte-Berge Theorem
[
13
]
).

Let
G
be a graph. Then the matching number of
G
is

\frac{1}{2}\min_{X\subseteq V(G)}(|V(G)|+|X|-odd(G-X)),

where
odd(G-X)
is the number of odd components of
G-X
.

By
Theorem
2.1
, we have the following result which describes a helpful property of graphs with bounded matching number.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices. Then the matching number of
G
is at most
s
if and only if there is a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
such that

|X|+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lfloor\frac{|V(C_{i})|}{2}\right\rfloor\leq s,

(2)

where
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
are the components of
G-X
.

Proof.

Assume the matching number of
G
is at most
s
. By
Theorem
2.1
, there is
X\subseteq V(G)
such that
|V(G)|+|X|-odd(G-X)\leq 2s
. Let
odd(G-X)=t
and
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
be the components of
G-X
. Assume
C_{1},\ldots,C_{t}
are the odd components of
G-X
. Let
|X|=x
and
|V(C_{i})|=c_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Then we have

and
x+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}=n
. Then

|X|+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lfloor\frac{|V(C_{i})|}{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{n-x}{2}-\frac{t}{2}+x\leq s.

Assume there is a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
such that
|X|+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lfloor\frac{|V(C_{i})|}{2}\right\rfloor\leq s
. Then
|V(G)|+|X|-odd(G-X)\leq s
. By Theorem

.

\displaystyle\frac{(l-1)(l-2)}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})+(l-1)(n-l+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(l-1)n+\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})\leq ex(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V(C_{i})|-m+1,K_{l,t})
.
We left to prove
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V(C_{i})|\leq 2(s-l+1)+m
.
We may assume
|V(C_{i})|
are all odd, i.e.
|V(C_{i})|=2k_{i}+1
(
1\leq i\leq m
). By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}\leq s-l+1
and then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}|V(C_{i})|\leq 2(s-l+1)+m
. Hence we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})\leq ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})
and we are done.

Combining Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2{3s\choose 2}
and
s\geq l+1\geq 3
. For all
1\leq x\leq l-1
, we have that

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}.

For
l\leq x\leq s
, we have the following results for the upper bound.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 2s+1
,
t\geq l
and
2\leq l\leq x\leq s
. Then we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(t-1){x\choose l}+(l-1)n-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t}).

Proof.

Choose
G\in\mathscr{G}_{x}
such that
e(G)=ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)
. Let
X\in\mathscr{X}
such that
|X|=x
. Suppose there is
v\in V(G)
such that
d_{X}(v)\leq l-2
. Since
|X|=x\geq l
, there is
u\in X\setminus N_{X}(v)
such that
uv\notin E(G)
. Let
G^{\prime}=G+uv
. By Lemma
2.2
and
d_{X}(v)\leq l-2
, we have
G^{\prime}\in\mathscr{G}_{k}
with
e(G^{\prime})>e(G)
, a contradiction with the choice of
G
. Hence

d_{X}(v)\geq l-1
for any
v\in V(G)
.

Let
T
be a
l
-horn of
X
. Since
G
is
K_{l,t}
-free,
|C(T)|\leq t-1
. Thus by calculating the number of
l
-horns of
X
, we have

\displaystyle(t-1){x\choose l}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}{d_{X}(v)\choose l}=\sum_{v\in X}{d_{X}(v)\choose l}+\sum_{v\notin X}{d_{X}(v)\choose l}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in X}(d_{X}(v)-(l-1))+\sum_{v\notin X}(d_{X}(v)-(l-1))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}d_{X}(v)-\left\lfloor\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rfloor+\sum_{v\notin X}(d_{X}(v)-(l-1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}d_{X}(v)+\sum_{v\notin X}d_{X}(v)-(l-1)n+\left\lceil\frac{(l-1)x}{2}\right\rceil.

Since
e(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in X}d_{X}(v)+\sum_{v\notin X}d_{X}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}e(C_{i})
and
e(C_{i})\leq ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})
, we have

e(G)\leq(t-1){x\choose l}+(l-1)n-\left\lceil\frac{(l-1)x}{2}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t}),

where

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lfloor\frac{|V(C_{i})|}{2}\right\rfloor+k\leq s.

(5)

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we have that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}ex(|V(C_{i})|,K_{l,t})\leq ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})
. Then

e(G)\leq(t-1){x\choose l}+(l-1)n-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})

and we are done.

Note that
ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})=O((s-x)^{2-1/t}).
If
l\geq 3
, then the following function

(t-1){x\choose l}+(l-1)n-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})

is monotonically increasing when
s
is large enough. Thus by Lemma
3.4
, we easily have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
n\geq 2s+1
,
t\geq l
and
2\leq l\leq x\leq s
. If
s
is large enough, then we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

Now we are going to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The lower bound in Theorem
1.1
is given by the Constructions
2.3
and
2.4
. So we just show the upper bound.

Since
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=\max_{1\leq x\leq s}ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)
, by Corollaries
3.3
and
3.5
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\\
&\leq&\max\left\{(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2},(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil\right\}.\end{array}

Note that
ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})=O((s-x)^{2-1/t})
.
When
l\geq 2
and
s
is large enough,
(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil>(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}
. So

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\leq(l-1)n+(t-1){s\choose l}-\left\lceil\frac{s(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

\square

If we replace the condition that
s
is large enough with
s\geq l+1

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
3\leq l\leq t
,
s\geq l+1
and
n\geq 2{3s\choose 2}
. Then we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\leq(t-1){s\choose l}+(l-1)n-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}+ex(2(s-l)+1,K_{l,t}).

Proof.

Since
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=\max_{1\leq x\leq s}ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)
, by Corollaries
3.3
and
3.5
, we just need to prove that when
1\leq x\leq l-1
,
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(t-1){s\choose l}+(l-1)n-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}+ex(2(s-l)+1,K_{l,t}).

Equivalent to proof

(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}\leq(t-1){s\choose l}+(l-1)n-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}+ex(2(s-l)+1,K_{l,t}),

i.e. to proof

ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})\leq(t-1){s\choose l}+ex(2(s-l)+1,K_{l,t}).

Set
a=s-l
. Then
a\geq 1
. Since
t\geq l\geq 3
and
ex(2a+3,K_{l,t})-ex(2a+1,K_{l,t})\leq 4a+3
, we have

\displaystyle(t-1){s\choose l}\geq

\displaystyle 2{l+a\choose l}\geq 2{a+3\choose 3}=\frac{(a+3)(a+2)(a+1)}{3}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4a+3\geq ex(2a+3,K_{l,t})-ex(2a+1,K_{l,t})

and we are done.

When
t\geq 2(s-l)+2
, we can determine the exact value of
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
if
n\geq 2{3s\choose 2}.
Recall

F_{1}(x)=(l-1)n+(t-1){x\choose l}+{2(s-x)+1\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
2l+2\leq 2s\leq 2l+t-2
,
l\geq 3
,
t\geq 4
and
n\geq\max\{2{3s\choose 2},n(s)\}
.
Then we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=\max\{F_{1}(l),F_{1}(s)\}.

Proof.

Since
2s\leq 2l+t-2
, we have
2s-t+1\leq 2l
. For
t\geq l\geq 3
, we have
n_{1}=n(s)\leq n
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})\geq\max_{l\leq x\leq s}\{F_{1}(x))\}.

(6)

By Lemma
3.4
, for any
l\leq x\leq s
,

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(l-1)n+(t-1){x\choose l}+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{l,t})-\left\lceil\frac{x(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil=F_{1}(x).

By Corollary
3.3
, for any
1\leq x\leq l-1
,

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}.

Note that
ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})\leq ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})+8(s-l)+2.
Since
2(s-l-1)+1<t
,
ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})={2(s-l-1)+1\choose 2}
. Then for any
1\leq x\leq l-1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l+1)+1,K_{l,t})-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}\\
&\leq&(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})+8(s-l)+2-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}\\
&\leq&(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})+4(t-\frac{3}{2})+\frac{l+1}{2}-\lfloor\frac{l-1}{2}\rfloor-1-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}\\
&\leq&(l-1)n+ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})+(t-1)(l+1)-\left\lceil\frac{l-1}{2}\right\rceil-1-\frac{l(l-1)}{2}\\
&\leq&(l-1)n+(t-1)(l+1)+ex(2(s-l-1)+1,K_{l,t})-\left\lceil\frac{(l+1)(l-1)}{2}\right\rceil\\
&=&F_{1}(l+1)\leq\max_{l\leq x\leq s}\{F_{1}(x)\}.\end{array}

By
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=\max_{1\leq x\leq s}ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\},x)
and (

), we have

ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})=\max_{l\leq x\leq s}\{F_{1}(x)\}.

It is easy to check that
F_{1}(y+2)-F_{1}(y+1)\geq F_{1}(y+1)-F_{1}(y)
. So
\max_{l\leq x\leq s}\{F_{1}(x)\}=\{F_{1}(l),F_{1}(s)\}
and we are done.

Notice that when

, the gap between the lower bound given in Theorem

and the upper bound given in Lemma

is
(2(s-x)+1)(l-1)
. The reason for this gap may be the edges between
S
and
X
in the Construction
2.4
, and this might be difficult to determine which depending on the specific value of

and

.

for

.

First we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Recall
ex(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{n}{4}(1+\sqrt{4n-3})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Note that when
s\geq 12
,
n(0)=2s+1\leq{s\choose 2}+s+1=n(s)
,
then the Construction
2.3
with
l=t=2
and
x=s
gives a lower bound of
ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})
, which implies when

,

ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})\geq n+{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil.

(7)

Lemma
3.2
implies that

ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\},1)\leq n+ex(2s-1,K_{2,2})-1.
By Lemma
3.4
, for any
2\leq x\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle x\choose 2}+n-\left\lceil\frac{x}{2}\right\rceil+ex(2(s-x)+1,K_{2,2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle x\choose 2}+n-\left\lceil\frac{x}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{2(s-x)+1}{4}(1+\sqrt{8(s-x)+1}).

Setting
f(x)=\frac{x(x-1)}{2}+n-\left\lceil\frac{x}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{2(s-x)+1}{4}(1+\sqrt{8(s-x)+1})
. We can easily find its convexity by taking the derivative. Then we have
ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq\max\{f(s),f(2)\}
, i.e.

ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\},x)\leq\max\left\{n+{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{3}{4},n+\frac{2s-3}{4}(1+\sqrt{8s-15})\right\},

where

.

ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})=\max_{1\leq x\leq s}ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\},x)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})\\
&\leq&\max\left\{n+ex(2s-1,K_{2,2})-1,n+{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{3}{4},n+\frac{2s-3}{4}(1+\sqrt{8s-15})\right\}.\end{array}

When
s\geq 12
, we have

{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{3}{4}\geq\frac{2s-3}{4}(1+\sqrt{8s-15})

and

{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil+\frac{3}{4}\geq\frac{2s-1}{4}(1+\sqrt{8s-7})-1\geq ex(2s-1,K_{2,2})-1.

So we have
ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})\leq n+{s\choose 2}-\left\lceil\frac{s}{2}\right\rceil
when
s\geq 12
. Thus the result holds.

\square

Next, we consider

with

mentioned before is

(1+o(1))\sqrt{t-1}n^{3/2}/2.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

and

Proof.

Let
G

-free graph with

and
T
a

for any

.
Since
G

-free,
|C(T)|\leq t-1
. Hence we have

(t-1){n\choose 2}\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}{d(v)\choose 2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{2}(G)}{n}-2e(G)\right),

which implies
4e^{2}(G)-2ne(G)-(t-1)(n^{3}-n^{2})\leq 0
.
Thus the result holds.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

When
l=2
,

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we give the results about maximum number of edges of
K_{l,t}
-free graphs with bounded matching number. In the case of
l\geq 3
, when
s
is sufficiently large with respect to
l
or when
t
is sufficiently large with respect to
s-l
, we can give the exact value of
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
with the extremal graph. But in other case, there exists a gap between the lower bound and the upper bound of
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
. In the case of
l=2
, we give more specific result of
ex(n,\{K_{2,2},M_{s+1}\})
and
ex(n,\{K_{2,t},M_{s+1}\})
when
s
is larger than some values depending on
t
. From the proofs of our main Theorems, we guess the exact value of
ex(n,\{K_{l,t},M_{s+1}\})
will be attached at the Construction
2.4
by adding some edges between
S
and
X
when
s
is relatively small.
But it is still difficult to get the specific value when
s
is relatively small. We believe it depends on the specific value of
s,l
and
t
.

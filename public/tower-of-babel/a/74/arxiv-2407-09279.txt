Abstract
. Assuming Gödel’s axiom of constructibility 𝕍 = 𝕃 , \mathbb{V}=\mathbb{L}, we present a characterization of those groups L L for which there exist arbitrarily large groups H H such that Aut ⁡ ( H ) ≅ L {\rm Aut}(H)\cong L . In particular, we show that it suffices to have one such group H H such that the size of its center is bigger than 2 | L | + ℵ 0 2^{|L|+\aleph_{0}} .

Introduction

The representation problem from group theory asks:

Problem 0.1
.

For a given group
L
, is there a group
H
such that
{\rm Aut}(H)\cong L
?

There are a lot of interesting research papers in this area. Here, we recall only a short list of them. The study of automorphism groups of finite abelian groups started with Shoda
[
13
]
.
Hallett–Hirsch
[
8
]
and Corner
[
3
]
classified
finite groups which are the automorphism
group of some torsion-free group. For infinite groups, Beaumont–Pierce
[
2
]
studied automorphism groups of torsion-free groups of rank two.
May
[
10
]
has more results on abelian automorphism groups of torsion-free groups of countable rank.

A related problem is the classical problem of establishing whether two algebraic structures are isomorphic when their automorphism groups are isomorphic. Thanks to
[
9
]
, the answer is yes for abelian
p
-groups, when
p\neq 2
. Also, by the work of Corner–Goldsmith
[
4
]
,
the answer for reduced torsion-free modules over the ring
J_{p}
of
p
-adic integers, where
p\neq 2
, is affirmative.
The question of whether two abelian
2
-groups are isomorphic when their automorphism groups are isomorphic is still an open problem.
For an excellent survey on these and related topics, see Fuchs
[
6
]
.

Despite lots of work, the study of the automorphism group of groups is not documented very well, and there is no universal solution to Problem
0.1
.
There are several examples of groups that can never be the automorphism group of some
abelian group, so that Problem
0.1
reduces to characterizing those groups
L
that can be the automorphism
group of another group.

Throughout this paper, we work with arbitrary groups, so they are not required to be abelian. In order to study Problem

0.1
, we study the structure of the automorphism group and inner automorphisms of a given group.
It may be worth noting that Baer
[
1
]
studied the normal subgroup structure of
{\rm Aut}(H)
for infinite
p
-groups
H
.
For more details on this and further achievements, see again the book of Fuchs
[
6
]
.

We suggest a good version of the problem:

Problem 0.2
.

Characterize the groups
L
such that for arbitrarily large cardinals
\lambda
there is a group
H
of cardinal
\lambda
such that
{\rm Aut}(H)\cong L

We approach Problem
0.2
as follows. Given groups

Notation 1.2

(1)

(2)

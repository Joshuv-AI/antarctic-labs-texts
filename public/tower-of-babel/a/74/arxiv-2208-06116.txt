Foliations formed by generic coadjoint orbits of Lie groups corresponding to a class 7-dimensional solvable Lie algebras Journal: Topology and its Applications
Tuyen T. M. Nguyen, Vu A. Le, Tuan A. Nguyen

Abstract
We consider all connected and simply connected 7-dimensional Lie groups whose Lie algebras have nilradical 𝔤 5 , 2 = span { X 1 , X 2 , X 3 , X 4 , X 5 : [ X 1 , X 2 ] = X 4 , [ X 1 , X 3 ] = X 5 } \mathfrak{g}_{5,2}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4},X_{5}\colon[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{4},[X_{1},X_{3}]=X_{5}\} of Dixmier. First, we give a geometric description of the maximal-dimensional orbits in the coadjoint representation of all considered Lie groups. Next, we prove that, for each considered group, the family of the generic coadjoint orbits forms a measurable foliation in the sense of Connes. Finally, the topological classification of all these foliations is also provided.

Introduction

The study of foliations on manifolds has a long history in mathematics. Since the works of Ehresmann and Reeb
[
4
]
in 1944, Ehresmann
[
5
]
in 1951, and Reeb
[
12
]
in 1952, the foliations on manifolds have enjoyed rapid development. Nowadays, they become the focus of a great deal of research activity (see
[
9
]
).
In general, the leaf space of a foliation with quotient topology is a fairly intractable topological space. To improve upon the shortcoming, Connes
[
1
]
proposed the notion of measurable foliations in 1982 and associated each such foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
with a C*-algebra
C^{*}(V,\mathcal{F})
. It represents the leaf space
V/\mathcal{F}
in the following sense: when the foliation comes from a fibration (with connected fibers)
p:V\to M
then
C^{*}(V,\mathcal{F})
is isomorphic to
C_{0}(M)\otimes\mathcal{K}
, where
C_{0}(M)
is the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions defined on
M
vanishing at infinity and
\mathcal{K}
denotes the C*-algebra of compact operators on an (infinite-dimensional) separable Hilbert space. During the last few decades, these concepts of Connes have become important tools of non-commutative differential geometry and have attracted much attention from mathematicians around the world.

In 1962, Kirillov
[
8
, Section 15]
invented the method of orbits that quickly became the most important one in the
representation theory of Lie groups and Lie algebras. The key to Kirillov’s method of orbits is generic (in a certain sense) orbits in the coadjoint representation (K-orbits, for short) of Lie groups. Hence, the problem of describing the geometry of (generic) K-orbits of each Lie group is very important to study. A concept closely related to Kirillov’s method of orbits
is the so-called
MD-group
proposed by Diep
[
2
]
in 1980. An MD-group, in terms of Diep
[
2
, Section 4.1]
, is a finite-dimensional solvable real Lie group whose K-orbits are of dimension zero or maximal dimension. It is very remarkable that the family of maximal-dimensional K-orbits of an MD-group
G
forms a so-called measurable foliation in the sense of Connes
[
1
]
which is called MD-foliation associated with
G
.

Thus, the nature of the problem concerning MD-groups, MD-foliations, and their Connes’ C*-algebras
is an interesting combination of Kirillov’s method of orbits with Connes’ method in non-commutative geometry. Therefore, this problem is worth studying.
During the period from 1990 to now, there have been much research works on MD-groups and MD-foliations, typically working

G
forms a measurable foliation on the open manifold
V_{G}

in the sense of Connes
[
1
]
, and it is called the foliation associated with
G
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the case of MD-groups in
[
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
and Lie groups in
[
17
, Theorem 21]
.
It consists of two steps as follows

1.

Step 1: For any
G\in\mathcal{A}
, construct a suitable differential system
S_{G}
of rank six on the manifold
V_{G}

such that each K-orbit
\Omega
from
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is a maximal connected integral submanifold corresponding to
S_{G}
.

2.

Step 2: Afterwards, show that the Lebesgue measure is invariant for some smooth polyvector field
\mathfrak{X}

of degree six such that it generates
S_{G}
.

As emphasized in Remark
2.4
, sixteen families of Lie groups in
\mathcal{A}
can be divided into three subfamilies as follows

\mathcal{A}=\left\{G^{\lambda}_{1},G_{2},G_{3},G_{4}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},G_{5},G_{6}^{\lambda},G_{7},G_{8}^{\lambda},G_{9},G^{\lambda}_{10},G_{11}\right\}\cup\left\{G_{12}^{\lambda}\right\}\cup\left\{G^{\lambda}_{13},G_{14}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},G_{15},G_{16}^{\lambda}\right\},

where groups in the same subfamily have almost the same picture of generic K-orbits. To prove Theorem
2
,
we thus choose in each of these subfamilies a representative group and calculate carefully for that selected group.

Note that the assertion of Theorem
2
for cases
G_{2}
,
G_{3}
,
G_{9}
and
G^{\lambda}_{10}

had been proved in
[
17
]
. In the following, we choose three groups
G_{4}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
,
G_{12}^{\lambda}
and
G_{13}^{\lambda}

and prove Theorem
2
explicitly for this cases.

Let
G=G_{4}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
.
Proof of Step 1.
For any
F(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},,\alpha_{5},\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
,
by Theorem
1
, the generic K-orbit
\Omega_{F}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{G}
if and only if
\alpha_{4}\alpha_{5}\neq 0
.
Furthermore, if we denote by
v
the element
(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{5},x^{*},y^{*})\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
then

\displaystyle\Omega_{F}=\Bigg\{v\in\mathcal{G}^{*}\,\bigg|\left(x^{*}_{2}-\frac{x^{*}_{3}x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{5}}\right)\frac{{x^{*}_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{x^{*}_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}=\left(\alpha_{2}-\frac{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}{\alpha_{5}}\right)\frac{{\alpha_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{\alpha_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}},\alpha_{4}x^{*}_{4}>0,\,\alpha_{5}x^{*}_{5}>0\Bigg\}.

Now we consider on
V_{G}
the following differential system
S_{G}
:

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{X}_{1}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}}\\
\mathfrak{X}_{2}:=\lambda_{1}x^{*}_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}+x^{*}_{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}}+(\lambda_{1}+1)x^{*}_{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{5}}\\
\mathfrak{X}_{3}:=x^{*}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{2}}+\lambda_{2}x^{*}_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}+x^{*}_{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}}+\lambda_{2}x^{*}_{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{5}}\\
\mathfrak{X}_{4}:=x^{*}_{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{2}}+x^{*}_{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}\\
\mathfrak{X}_{5}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}}\\
\mathfrak{X}_{6}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{*}}.\end{cases}

Obviously,
\operatorname{rank}(S_{G})=6
and all
\mathfrak{X}_{i}\,(i=1,\ldots,6)
are smooth over
V_{G}
.
We claim that
S_{G}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{G}
, i.e. each K-orbit
\Omega
from
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is a maximal connected integral submanifold of
S_{G}
.

If fact, we first consider
\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{5},\mathfrak{X}_{6}
. Clearly, their flows (i.e. one-parameter subgroups) are determined as follows

\begin{array}[]{l l}\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}:&F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}(F):=(x^{*}_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5},\alpha,\beta),\\
\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}-\alpha}:&F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}-\alpha}(F):=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5},x^{*},\beta),\\
\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6}}_{y^{*}-\beta}:&F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6}}_{y^{*}-\beta}(F):=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5},\alpha,y^{*}).\end{array}

Next, we consider
\mathfrak{X}_{2}
. For some positive
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, assume that

\varphi:t\mapsto\varphi(t)=\left(x^{*}_{1}(t),x^{*}_{2}(t),x^{*}_{3}(t),x^{*}_{4}(t),x^{*}_{5}(t),x^{*}(t),y^{*}(t)\right),\quad t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)

is an integral curve of
\mathfrak{X}_{2}
passing
F=\varphi(0)
. Then, we must have
\varphi^{\prime}(t)={\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{\varphi(t)}
and it is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{l}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{5}x^{*^{\prime}}_{i}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{i}}+x^{*^{\prime}}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}}+y^{*^{\prime}}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{*}}=\lambda_{1}x^{*}_{3}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}+x^{*}_{4}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}}+(1+\lambda_{1})x^{*}_{5}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{5}}\cdot\end{array}

By identifying coefficients on two sides, we obtain

\begin{cases}x^{*^{\prime}}_{1}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}_{2}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}(t)=y^{*^{\prime}}(t)=0\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{3}(t)=\lambda_{1}x^{*}_{3}(t)\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{4}(t)=x^{*}_{4}(t)\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{5}(t)=(1+\lambda_{1})x^{*}_{5}(t).\end{cases}

(3.1)

Since
F=\varphi(0)
, system (
3
) gives us

x^{*}_{1}=\alpha_{1},\,x^{*}_{2}=\alpha_{2},\,x^{*}_{3}=\alpha_{3}e^{\lambda_{1}t},\,x^{*}_{4}=\alpha_{4}e^{t},\,x^{*}_{5}=\alpha_{5}e^{(1+\lambda_{1})t},\,x^{*}=\alpha,\,y^{*}=\beta.

Therefore, the flow of
\mathfrak{X}_{2}
is as follows

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x}(F):=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}e^{\lambda_{1}x},\alpha_{4}e^{x},\alpha_{5}e^{(1+\lambda_{1})x},\alpha,\beta).

Similarly, the flows of
\mathfrak{X}_{3}
is as follows

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{y}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{y}(F):=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}e^{y},\alpha_{3}e^{\lambda_{2}y},\alpha_{4}e^{y},\alpha_{5}e^{\lambda_{2}y},\alpha,\beta).

Now, we consider
\mathfrak{X}_{4}
. Assume that

\varphi:t\mapsto\varphi(t)=(x^{*}_{1}(t),x^{*}_{2}(t),x^{*}_{3}(t),x^{*}_{4}(t),x^{*}_{5}(t),x^{*}(t),y^{*}(t))

be an integral curve of
\mathfrak{X}_{4}
passing
F=\varphi(0)
, where
t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}
for some positive
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then we have
\varphi^{\prime}(t)={\mathfrak{X}_{4}}_{\varphi(t)}
which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{5}x^{*^{\prime}}_{i}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{i}}+x^{*^{\prime}}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}}+y^{*^{\prime}}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{*}}=x^{*}_{4}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{2}}+x^{*}_{5}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{*^{\prime}}_{1}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}_{4}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}_{5}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}(t)=y^{*^{\prime}}(t)=0\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{2}(t)=x^{*}_{4}(t)\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{3}(t)=x^{*}_{5}(t).\end{cases}

(3.2)

Since
F=\varphi(0)
, equation (
3
) gives us

x^{*}_{1}=\alpha_{1},\,x^{*}_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}t,\,x^{*}_{3}=\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{5}t,\,x^{*}_{4}=\alpha_{4},\,x^{*}_{5}=\alpha_{5},\,x^{*}=\alpha,\;y^{*}=\beta.

Therefore, the flow of
\mathfrak{X}_{4}
is as follows

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{4}}_{x_{1}}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x_{1}}(F):=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}x_{1},\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{5}x_{1},\alpha_{4},\alpha_{5},\alpha,\beta).

Finally, we set
\theta=\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6}}_{y^{*}-\beta}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}-\alpha}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{4}}_{x_{1}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{y}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}
. Then

\displaystyle\theta(F)

\displaystyle=\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6}}_{y^{*}-\beta}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}-\alpha}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{4}}_{x_{1}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{y}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}(F)

\displaystyle=(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{5},x^{*},y^{*})

where
x^{*}_{1},x^{*},y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
and

\begin{array}[]{l l}x^{*}_{2}=\alpha_{2}e^{y}+\alpha_{4}x_{1}e^{y},&x^{*}_{3}=\alpha_{3}e^{\lambda_{1}x+\lambda_{2}y}+\alpha_{5}x_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}x+\lambda_{2}y},\\
x^{*}_{4}=\alpha_{4}e^{x+y},&x^{*}_{5}=\alpha_{5}e^{\lambda_{1}x+x+\lambda_{2}y}.\end{array}

By direct calculations, we get

\begin{array}[]{l}x^{*}_{2}=\frac{x^{*}_{3}x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{5}}+\left(\alpha_{2}-\frac{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}{\alpha_{5}}\right)\frac{{\alpha_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{\alpha_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}\frac{{x^{*}_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{x^{*}_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}\Leftrightarrow\left(x^{*}_{2}-\frac{x^{*}_{3}x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{5}}\right)\frac{{x^{*}_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{x^{*}_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}=\left(\alpha_{2}-\frac{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}}{\alpha_{5}}\right)\frac{{\alpha_{4}}^{\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}{{\alpha_{5}}^{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}-1}}}\cdot\end{array}

Hence,
\left\{\theta(F)\,|\,x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{5},x^{*},y^{*}\in\mathbb{R},\alpha_{4}x^{*}_{4}>0,\alpha_{5}x^{*}_{5}>0\right\}\equiv\Omega_{F}
,
i.e.
\Omega_{F}
is a maximal connected integral submanifold corresponding to
S_{G}
.
In other words,
S_{G}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{G}
and
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is a six-dimensional foliation for
G=G_{4}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
,
where
V_{G}=V_{1}
as in (
2.1
).

Proof of Step 2.
We have to show that the foliation
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is measurable in the sense of Connes.
As mentioned in
[
17
, Subsection 2.2]
, to prove that
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is measurable,
we only need to choose some suitable pair (
\mathfrak{X},\mu
) on
V_{G}
, where
\mathfrak{X}
is some smooth 6-vector field defined on
V_{G}
,

\mu
is some measure on
V_{G}
such that
\mathfrak{X}
generates
S_{G}
and
\mu
is
\mathfrak{X}
-invariant.
Namely, we choose
\mu
to be exactly the Lebesgue measure on
V_{G}
and set

\mathfrak{X}:=\mathfrak{X}_{1}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{2}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{3}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{4}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{5}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{6}
.
Clearly,
\mathfrak{X}
is smooth, non-zero everywhere on
V_{G}
and it is exactly a polyvector field of degree six.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{X}
generates
S_{G}
. In other words, if we choose a suitable orientation on
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
then

\mathfrak{X}\in C^{\infty}\left(\Lambda^{6}(\mathcal{F})\right)^{+}
. Besides, the invariance of the Lebesgue measure
\mu
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}

is equivalent to the invariance of
\mu
for the K-representation that is restricted to the foliated submanifold
V_{G}
in
\mathcal{G}^{*}
.
For any
U(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x,y)\in\mathcal{G}
, direct computations show that the Jacobi determinant

J_{U}
of differential map
K\left(\exp_{G}(U)\right)
is a constant which depends only on
U
but does not depend on
the coordinates of any point which moves in each generic K-orbit
\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
.
This means that the Lebegue measure
\mu
is
\mathfrak{X}
-invariant.
This completes the proof for the case
G=G_{4}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}
.

Next, we consider the case
G=G_{12}^{\lambda}
.
Proof of Step 1.

F(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4},,\alpha_{5},\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{G}^{*}

, systemn (

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{*^{\prime}}_{1}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}_{4}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}_{5}(t)=x^{*^{\prime}}(t)=y^{*^{\prime}}(t)=0\\

(3.6)

Since

(3.7)

Therefore, the flow of

is

By setting

\displaystyle=\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6}}_{y^{*}-\beta}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}-\alpha}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{4}}_{x_{1}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{y}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}_{x}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-\alpha_{1}}(F)

\displaystyle=(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{5},x^{*},y^{*})

4
Conclusion

We have considered connected and simply connected Lie groups corresponding to 7-dimensional Lie algebras with nilradical
\mathfrak{g}_{5,2}
.
The main results of the paper are as follows. First, we describe the picture of maximal-dimensional K-orbits of considered Lie groups
as well as their geometric characteristics in Theorem
1
, Remark
2.2
and Remark
2.4
.
Afterwards, Theorem
2
proves that the families of all generic maximal-dimensional K-orbits of considered Lie groups
form measurable foliations (in the sense of Connes) which is called GMD-foliations.
Finally, the topological classification of all GMD-foliations is given Theorem
3
, and their Connes’ C*-algebras
are also described in Corollary
3.3
.

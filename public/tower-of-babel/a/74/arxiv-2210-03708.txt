Cohomological properties of different types of weak amenability
M. J. Mehdipour and A. Rejali

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we deal with cohomological properties of weak amenability, cyclic amenability, cyclic weak amenability and point amenability of Banach algebras. We look at some hereditary properties of them and show that continuous homomorphisms with dense range preserve cyclically weak amenability, however, weak amenability and cyclically amenability are preserved under certain conditions. We also study these cohomological properties of the
\theta-
Lau product
A\times_{\theta}B
and the projective tensor product
A\hat{\otimes}B
. Finally, we investigate the cohomological properties of
A^{**}
and establish that cyclically weak amenability of
A^{**}
implies cyclically weak amenability of
A
. This result is true for point amenability instead of cyclically weak amenability.

Introduction

For a Banach algebra
A
, a linear operator
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
is said to be a
derivation
if
D(ab)=D(a)\cdot b+a\cdot D(b)
for all
a,b\in A
. If for every
a,b\in A

\langle D(a),b\rangle+\langle D(b),a\rangle=0,

D
is called
cyclic
. One can prove that
D
is a cyclic derivation if and only if
\langle D(a),a\rangle=0
for all
a\in A
. In the case where
A
is unital, then
D
is a cyclic derivation if and only if
\langle D(a),1_{A}\rangle=0
for all
a\in A
; see
[
19
]
.
For every
F\in A^{*}
, the bounded linear operator
a\mapsto F\cdot a-a\cdot F
is called an
inner derivation
and is denoted by
\hbox{ad}_{F}
, where
\langle F\cdot a,x\rangle=\langle F,ax\rangle
and

\langle a\cdot F,x\rangle=\langle F,xa\rangle

for all
x\in A
.

A Banach algebra
A
is called
weakly amenable
if inner derivations are the only continuous derivation from
A
into
A^{*}
.
Also,
A
is called
cyclically weakly amenable
if every continuous derivation from
A
into
A^{*}
is cyclic, and
A
is said to be
cyclically amenable
if every cyclic derivation from
A
into
A^{*}
is inner.

Let us recall that a linear functional
d
on
A
is called a
continuous point derivation of

A

at

\varphi\in\Delta_{0}(A)
if for every
a,b\in A
, we have

d(ab)=d(a)\varphi(b)+\varphi(a)d(b),

where
\Delta_{0}(A):=\Delta(A)\cup\{0\}
and
\Delta(A)
is the character space of
A
.
We call
A

point amenable
(0-
point amenable
) if there is no non-zero continuous point derivation of
A
at
\Delta(A)
(respectively,
\Delta_{0}(A)
).

Cohomology properties of Banach algebras studied by many authors
[
3
,
5
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
20
]
. For example, Bade, Curtis and Dales
[
3
]
introduced the concept of weak
amenability for commutative Banach algebras. Johnson [25] gave this concept for arbitrary
Banach algebras. Gronbaek
[
14
]
studied the hereditary properties of weak amenability. He
[
15
]
introduced and investigated the concept of cyclically amenablity. Recently, the authors
[
19
]
introduced the notions of cyclically weak amenability and point amenability for Banach algebras. They proved that
A
is weakly amenable if and only if
A
is both cyclically weakly amenable and cyclically amenable. They showed that if
A
is commutative, then weakly amenability and cyclically weak amenability of
A
coincide. They also proved that every Banach algebra with non-empty character space is cyclically weakly amenable if and only if
A
is 0-point amenable; or equivalently, it is point amenable and essential. In
[
19
]
, it is shown that weak amenability, cyclically weak amenability and point amenability of commutative unital Banach algebras are equivalent.

In this paper we investigate the hereditary properties of cyclically weak amenability and point amenability. We also find some properties concerning weak amenability and cyclically amenability. In Section 2, we study some hereditary properties. We verify that continuous homomorphisms with dense range are cyclically weak amenability preserving, but they are not neither weak amenability nor cyclic amenability preserving in general. We give condition under which continuous homomorphisms with dense range are weak amenability and cyclic amenability preserving. In Section 3, we study weak amenability, cyclic amenability, cyclic weak amenability and point amenability of Banach algebras
A\times_{\theta}B
,
A\oplus B
,
A^{\sharp}
and
A\hat{\otimes}B
. In Section 4, we prove that point amenability of
A^{**}
implies point amenability of
A
, however, this result is true for cyclically weakly amenable and cyclically amenable under certain conditions.

2
Homomorphism and retraction maps on Banach algebras

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras and
\phi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
be a continuous homomorphism with dense range. Then
\phi
is said to be
(
respectively, weak, cyclic, cyclically weak, point, 0-point
)
amenability preserving
if (respectively, weak, cyclic, cyclically weak, point, 0-point) amenability of
A_{1}
implies (respectively, cyclic, cyclically weak, point, weak) amenability of
A_{2}
. Some authors investigated these properties. For instance, Paterson
[
21
]
proved that
\phi
is always amenability preserving. Groenbaek studied weak amenability preserving. He
[
14
]
showed that if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are commutative, then
\phi
is weak amenability preserving. In the case where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are noncommutative, he
[
16
]
gave some sufficient conditions. Lau and Loy
[
18
]
showed that
\phi
is point amenability preserving.

Theorem 2.1

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras and
\phi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
be a continuous homomorphism with dense range. The following statements hold.

(i)

\phi
is cyclically weak amenability preserving.

(ii)
If
A_{2}^{*}\circ\phi=A_{1}^{*}
, then
\phi
is cyclic amenability preserving.

(iii)
If
A_{2}^{*}\circ\phi=A_{1}^{*}
, then
\phi
is weak amenability preserving.

(iv)

\phi
is 0-point amenability preserving.

Proof.
Let
D:A_{2}\rightarrow A_{2}^{*}
be a continuous derivation. In view of Lemma 2.2 in
[
18
]
,
\tilde{D}:=\phi^{*}\circ D\circ\phi
is a continuous derivation from
A_{1}
into
A_{1}^{*}
.

(i) Assume that
A_{1}
is cyclically weakly amenable. Then
\tilde{D}
is cyclic. So for every
a_{1},x_{1}\in A_{1}

\langle\tilde{D}(a_{1}),x_{1}\rangle+\langle\tilde{D}(x_{1}),a_{1}\rangle=0.

Hence

\langle D(\phi(a_{1})),\phi(x_{1})\rangle+\langle D(\phi(x_{1}))\phi(a_{1})\rangle=0.

Since
\phi
is continuous homomorphism with dense range, it follows that

\langle D(a_{2}),x_{2}\rangle+\langle D(x_{2}),a_{2}\rangle=0

for all
a_{2},x_{2}\in A_{2}
. That is,
D
is cyclic. That is, (i) holds.

(ii) Let
A_{1}
be cyclic amenable and
A_{2}^{*}\circ\phi=A_{1}^{*}
. It is easy to see that if
D
is cyclic. Then
\tilde{D}
is cyclic. Thus
\tilde{D}
is inner and so there exists a functional
F\in A_{2}^{*}
such that
\tilde{D}=\hbox{ad}_{F\circ\phi}
. Hence
for every
a_{1},x_{1}\in A_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle D(\phi(a_{1})),\phi(x_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\circ\phi(a_{1}x_{1})-F\circ\phi(x_{1}a_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(\phi(a_{1})\phi(x_{1}))-F(\phi(x_{1})\phi(a_{1})).

This implies that

\langle D(a_{2}),x_{2}\rangle=F(a_{2}x_{2})-F(x_{2}a_{2})

for all
a_{2},x_{2}\in A_{2}
. Hence
A_{2}
is weakly amenable. That is (ii) holds.

(iii) The statements (i) and (ii) together with Theorem 4.1 in
[
19
]
prove (iii).

(iv) An argument like that used in the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
18
]
proves (iv).

Corollary 2.2

Let

I

Corollary 2.3

Let
A
be a Banach algebra and
B
be a dense subalgebra of
A
. If
B
is weakly amenable, then
A
is weakly amenable. This statement is true if weakly amenable is replaced by cyclically amenable, cyclically weakly amenable or point amenable.

Proof.
Let
\ell:B\rightarrow A
be the inclusion map. Then
\ell
is a continuous epimorphism with dense range. If
g\in B^{*}
, then there exists
f\in A^{*}
such that
f|_{B}=g
. So
f\circ\ell=g
. It follows that
A^{*}\circ\ell=B^{*}
. These facts together with Theorem
2.1
prove the result.

Let
(A,\|.\|_{A})
and
(B,\|.\|_{B})
be Banach algebras. Then
B
is called an
abstract Segal algebra
if
B
is a dense left ideal in
A
and there exist positive numbers
m
and
n
such that

\|b\|_{A}\leq m\|b\|_{B}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|ab\|_{B}\leq n\|a\|_{A}\|b\|_{B}.

for all
a,b\in B
.

Corollary 2.4

Let
B
be an abstract Segal algebra with respect to Banach algebra
A
. If
A
is weakly amenable, then so does
B
. This statement is true for cyclically amenable, cyclically weakly amenable or point amenable instead of wealy amenable.

For a Banach algebra
A
, a continuous linear functional
p\in(A\hat{\otimes}A)^{*}
is called a
quasi-additive functional of

A
if for every
a,b,x\in A
,

p(ax\otimes b)=p(a\otimes xb)+p(x\otimes ba).

Furthermore,
p
is called
inner
if there exists
F\in A^{*}
such that

p(a\otimes b)=F(ab-ba)

for all
a,b\in A
. A quasi-additive functional
p
of
A
is called
cyclic
if
p(a\otimes a)=0
for all
a\in A
. One can prove that
A
is weakly amenable if and only if every quasi-additve functional of
A
is inner. Also,
A
is cyclically amenable if and only if every cyclic quasi-additive functional of
A
is inner; see
[
19
]
.

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras. A continuous homomorphism
\phi:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{2}
is called a
retraction
if there exists a continuous homomorphism
\psi:A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}
such that
\phi\circ\psi=I_{A_{2}}
.

Theorem 2.5

Let
\phi
be a retraction from Banach algebra
A_{1}
into
A_{2}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)
If
A_{1}
is cyclic amenable, then
A_{2}
is cyclic amenable.

(ii)
If
A_{1}
is weakly amenable, then
A_{2}
is weakly amenable.

Proof.
Let
p
be a cyclic quasi-additive functional of
A_{2}
. Then
p\circ(\phi\otimes\phi)
is a cyclic quasi-additive functional of
A_{1}
. Thus there exists
F\in A_{1}^{*}
such that

p(\phi(a_{1})\otimes\phi(x_{1}))=F(a_{1}x_{1}-x_{1}a_{1})

for all
a_{1},x_{1}\in A_{1}
. Since
\phi
is a retraction,
\phi\circ\psi=I_{A_{2}}
for some continuous homomorphism
\psi
from
A_{2}
into
A_{1}
. For every
a_{2},x_{2}\in A_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle p(a_{2}\otimes x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\phi\psi(a_{2})\otimes\phi\psi(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\circ(\phi\otimes\phi)(\psi(a_{2})\otimes\psi(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(\psi(a_{2})\psi(x_{2})-\psi(x_{2})\psi(a_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\circ\psi(a_{2}x_{2}-x_{2}a_{2}).

This shows that
p
is inner. So (i) holds. The authors
[
19
]
proved that a Banach algebra is weakly amenable if and only if it is cyclically amenable and cyclically weakly amenable. This facts together with part (i) and Theorem
2.1
(i) proves (ii).

3
Cohomological properties of Lau Product of Banach algebras

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras and
\theta\in\Delta_{0}(A_{2})
. Let us recall that the
\theta-
Lau product
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is the Cartesian product

A_{1}\times A_{2}
with the product

(a_{1},a_{2})(x_{1},x_{2})=(a_{1}x_{1}+a_{1}\theta(x_{2})+x_{1}\theta(a_{2}),a_{2}x_{2})

and the norm
\|(a_{1},x_{1})\|=\|a_{1}\|+\|x_{1}\|
. Let us remark that if
\theta=0
, then
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is the usual direct product
A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
and if
A_{2}={C}
and
\theta
is the identity map on
{C}
, then
A_{1}\times_{\theta}{C}
is
A_{1}^{\sharp}
, the unitization of
A_{1}
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras and
\theta\in\Delta_{0}(A_{2})
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable if and only if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are 0-point amenable.

(ii)

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is 0-point amenable if and only if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are 0-point amenable.

(iii)

A_{1}
is 0-point amenable if and only if
A_{1}^{\sharp}
is 0-point amenable.

Proof.
Let
d:A_{1}\rightarrow{{C}}
be a continuous point derivation at
\varphi\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1})
. Define the bounded linear functionals
\bar{d}
and
\bar{\varphi}
on
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
by

\bar{d}(a_{1},a_{2})=d(a_{1})\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\varphi}(a_{1},a_{2})=\varphi(a_{1})+\theta(a_{2}).

It is routine to check that
\bar{d}
is a continuous point derivation at
\bar{\varphi}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2})
. So if
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable, then
\bar{d}=0
and hence
d=0
. This shows that
A_{1}
is 0-point amenable. To complete the proof, we only note that the mapping
(a_{1},a_{2})\mapsto a_{2}
is a continuous epimorphism from
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
onto
A_{2}
and it is 0-point amenable preserving. Thus
A_{2}
is 0-point amenable.

Conversely, let
d\in(A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2})^{*}
be a continuous point derivation at
\varphi\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2})
. Put
\tilde{d}_{i}:=d|_{A_{i}}
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\varphi|_{A_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
. Then
\tilde{d}_{i}
is a continuous point derivation at
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{i})
. Since
A_{i}
is a 0-point amenable,
\tilde{d}_{i}=0
for
i=1,2
. It follows that
d=0
. Consequently,
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable. Therefore, (i) holds. Since

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}=A_{1}\times_{0}A_{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptA_{1}^{\sharp}=A_{1}\times_{id}{{C}},

the statements (ii) and (iii) follow from (i).

Theorem 3.4

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras and
\theta\in\Delta(A)
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)
If
A_{1}\times_{\theta}A_{2}
is cyclic amenable, then
A_{2}
is cyclic amenable.

(ii)

A_{1}
is cyclically amenable if and only if
A_{1}^{\sharp}
is cyclically amenable.

Proof.
For
i=1,2
, let
\pi_{i}:A_{1}\times A_{2}\rightarrow A_{i}
and
\iota_{i}:A_{i}\rightarrow A_{1}\times A_{2}
be the canonical projection and the canonical injection maps, respectively. Then
\pi_{i}\circ\iota_{i}=I_{A_{i}}
. Thus
\pi_{i}
is reaction and by Theorem
2.5
the the statement (i) holds.

For (ii), let
\tilde{D}:A_{1}^{\sharp}\rightarrow(A_{1}^{\sharp})^{*}=A_{1}^{*}\oplus{C}
be a continuous cyclic derivation. Define the continuous cyclic derivation
U:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}^{*}
by

\langle U(a),b\rangle=\langle\tilde{D}(a,0),(b,0)\rangle.

If
A_{1}
is cyclically amenable, then
U=\hbox{ad}_{F}
for some
F\in A_{1}^{*}
. Define the bounded linear functional
\tilde{F}
on
A_{1}^{\sharp}
by
\tilde{F}(a,r)=F(a)
for all
a\in A_{1}
and
r\in{C}
. Thus
\tilde{D}=\hbox{ad}_{\tilde{F}}
, because, for every
a,b\in A_{1}
and
r,s\in{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\hbox{ad}_{\tilde{F}}(a,r),(b,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{F},(a,r)(b,s)-(b,s)(a,r)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{F},(ab+rb+sa)-(ba+rb+sa)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle F,ab-ba\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hbox{ad}_{F}(a),b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U(a),b\rangle.

Since
\tilde{D}
is cyclic, we have

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a,r),(b,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a,r),(b,0)\rangle+\langle\tilde{D}(a,r),(0,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a,0),(b,0)\rangle+\langle\tilde{D}(0,r),(b,0)\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a,0),(0,s)\rangle+\langle\tilde{D}(0,r),(0,s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle U(a),b\rangle.

Therefore,
A_{1}^{\sharp}
is cyclically amenable. The converse follows from (i).

(iii)

Theorem 3.8

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is cyclically weakly amenable if and only if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are cyclically weakly amenable.

(ii)

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is cyclically amenable if and only if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are cyclically amenable.

(iii)

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is weakly amenable if and only if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are weakly amenable.

Proof.
(i) Let
D:A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\rightarrow(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})^{*}
be a continuous derivation. Define the continuous derivations
D_{1}:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}^{*}
and
D_{2}:A_{2}\rightarrow A_{2}^{*}
by

\langle D_{1}(a_{1}),x_{1}\rangle=\langle D(a_{1},0),(x_{1},0)\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\langle D_{2}(a_{2}),x_{2}\rangle=\langle D(0,a_{2}),(0,x_{2})\rangle.

Then
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are cyclic. Note the mapping
F\mapsto(F_{1},F_{2})
is an isometric isomorphism from
(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})^{*}
onto
A_{1}^{*}\oplus A_{2}
, where

F_{1}(a_{1})=F(a_{1},0)\hskip 10.00002pt\hbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptF_{2}(a_{2})=F(0,a_{2})

for all
a_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2}\in A_{2}
. This implies that

\langle D(a_{1},0),(0,x_{2})\rangle=\langle D(0,a_{2}),(x_{1},0)\rangle=0

for all
a_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2}\in A_{2}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\langle D(a_{1},a_{2}),(x_{1},x_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(x_{1},x_{2}),(a_{1},a_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a_{1},a_{2}),(x_{1},0)\rangle+\langle D(a_{1},a_{2}),(0,x_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(x_{1},x_{2}),(a_{1},0)\rangle+\langle D(x_{1},x_{2}),(0,a_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a_{1},0),(x_{1},0)\rangle+\langle D(0,a_{2}),(x_{1},0)\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(a_{1},0),(0,x_{2})\rangle+\langle D(0,a_{2}),(0,x_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(x_{1},0),(a_{1},0)\rangle+\langle D(0,x_{2}),(a_{1},0)\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D(x_{1},0),(0,a_{2})\rangle+\langle D(0,x_{2}),(0,a_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D_{1}(a_{1}),x_{1}\rangle+\langle D_{1}(x_{1}),a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle D_{2}(a_{2}),x_{2}\rangle+\langle D_{2}(x_{2}),a_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

for all
a_{1},x_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2},x_{2}\in A_{2}
.
This shows that
D
is cyclic. Therefore,
A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
is cyclically weakly amenable.

Let
\tilde{D}_{1}:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}^{*}
be a continuous derivation. We define the linear operator
\tilde{D}:A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\rightarrow(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})^{*}

and
a_{2},x_{2}\in A_{2}
. Then
\tilde{D}
is a continuous derivation.
If

be cyclically weakly amenable, then
\tilde{D}
is cyclic. So for every

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D_{1}}(a_{1}),x_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D_{1}}(x_{1}),a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a_{1},0),(x_{1},0)\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(x_{1},0),(a_{1},0)\rangle=0.

This shows that
\tilde{D_{1}}
is cyclic. Hence
A_{1}
is cyclically weakly amenable. Similarly, one can prove that
A_{2}
is cyclically weakly amenable.

(ii) Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are cyclically amenable. Assume that

D:A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}^{*}\oplus A_{2}^{*}
is a cyclic derivation. Define

Then

\langle D_{1}(x),a\rangle+\langle D_{1}(a),x\rangle=\langle D(x,0),(a,0)\rangle+\langle D(a,0),(x,0)\rangle=0

for all
a,x\in A_{1}
. So
D_{1}

D_{2}
is a cyclic derivation. Hence
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are inner. Thus
D_{1}=\hbox{ad}_{F_{1}}
and

for some

. That is,

is cyclically amenable. Theorem
3.4
(i) proves the converse.

(iii) This follows from Theorem 4.1 in

]
and the statements (ii) and (iii).

4
Cohomological properties of projective tensor products for Banach algebras

For Banach algebras
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, let
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
be the projective tensor product.

Theorem 4.1

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be Banach algebras. Then
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are 0-point amenable if and only if
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable.

Proof.
Assume first that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are unital. Let
d:A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}\rightarrow{C}
be a continuous point derivation at
\varphi\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2})
of
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
. Define
d_{1}:A_{1}\rightarrow{{C}}
by

d_{1}(a)=d(a\otimes 1).

Then
d_{1}
is a continuous point derivation at
\varphi_{1}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1})
of
A_{1}
, where
\varphi_{1}
is defined by
\varphi_{1}(a_{1})=\varphi(a_{1}\otimes 1)
for all
a_{1}\in A_{1}
. Similarly,
d_{2}
defined by
d_{2}(a_{2})=d(1\otimes a_{2})
is a continuous point derivation at
\varphi_{2}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{2})
of
A_{2}
, where
\varphi_{2}(a_{2})=\varphi(1\otimes a_{2})
for all
a_{2}\in A_{2}
. If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are 0-point amenable, then
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
are zero. Hence for every
a_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2}\in A_{2}
we have

\displaystyle d(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d((a_{1}\otimes 1)(1\otimes a_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(a_{1}\otimes 1)\varphi(1\otimes a_{2})+\varphi(a_{1}\otimes 1)d(1\otimes a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{1}(a_{1})\varphi_{2}(a_{2})+\varphi_{1}(a_{1})d_{2}(a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable.

Now, let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be any Banach algebras. Assume that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are 0-point amenable. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
A_{1}^{\sharp}
and
A_{2}^{\sharp}
are 0-point amenable and so
A_{1}^{\sharp}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}^{\sharp}
is 0-point amenable. But,

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\sharp}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{1}\oplus{{C}})\hat{\otimes}(A_{2}\oplus{{C}})

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2})\oplus(A_{1}\hat{\otimes}{{C}})

\displaystyle\oplus

\displaystyle({{C}}\hat{\otimes}A_{2})\oplus({{C}}\hat{\otimes}{{C}}).

This together with Theorem
3.1
,
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable.

To prove the converse, let
d_{1}:A_{1}\rightarrow{C}
be a continuous point derivation at
\varphi_{1}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1})
. Define
d:A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}\rightarrow{C}
by
d(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=d_{1}(a_{1})\varphi_{2}(a_{2})
, where
\varphi_{2}\in\Delta_{0}(A_{2})
. Then
d
is a countinuous point derivation at
\varphi\in\Delta_{0}(A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2})
of
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
, where

\varphi(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=\varphi_{1}(a_{1})\varphi_{2}(a_{2})
for all
a_{1}\in A_{1}
and
a_{2}\in A_{2}
. Since
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is 0-point amenable,
d=0
and hence
d_{1}=0
. Thus
A_{1}
is 0-point amenable. Similarly,
A_{2}
is 0-point amenable.

Corollary 4.2

Let

Groenbaek
[

proved some hereditary properties of weak amenability for commutative Banach algebras. He also showed that if
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are commutative weakly amenable Banach algebra, then
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is weakly amenable. The converse of this result is proved by Yazdanpanah
[

. He also found some result concerning weak amenability of tensor product of noncommutative Banach algebras.

Theorem 4.3

Let
A_{i}
be a unital Banach algebra with
\Delta(A_{i})\neq\emptyset
, for
i=1,2
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)
If
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is cyclically amenable, then
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are cyclically amenable.

(ii)
If
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
is weakly amenable, then
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are weakly amenable.

Proof.
Choose
\varphi_{2}\in\Delta(A_{2})
and define the function
\Lambda:A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}\rightarrow A_{1}
by

\Lambda(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})=\varphi_{2}(a_{2})a_{1}.

Then
\Lambda
is an epimorphism. If we define the function
\Gamma:A_{1}\rightarrow A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
by

\Gamma(a_{1})=a_{1}\otimes 1,

then

, the identity map on
A_{1}
. Thus
\Lambda
is a retraction. Now, apply Theorem
2.5
to conclude that cyclic amenability of
A_{1}\hat{\otimes}A_{2}
forces

to be cyclically amenable. Similarly,

is weakly amenable. So (i) holds. The statements (i) together with Corollary
4.2
proves (ii).

5
Type of weak amenability of the second dual
Banach algebras

For a Banach algebra
A
, Gourdeau
[
13
]
proved that if
A^{**}
is amenable, then so dose
A
; see
[
11
]
for another proof of it. Ghahramani, Loy and Willis
[
11
]
investigated this result for weak amenability instead of amenability. They showed that if
A
is a left ideal in
A^{**}
, then Gourdea’s theorem remains valid for weak amenability instead of amenability. They claimed that if every derivation from
A
into
A^{*}
is weakly compact, then weak amenability of
A^{**}
implies weak amenability of
A
. Several authors doubted the correctness of it and proved that under certain circumstances the claim is true
[
1
,
2
,
7
,
8
,
10
]
. For example, Dales, Rodriguez-Palacios and Velasco
[
7
]
gave conditions under which the second transpose of a continuous derivation
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
is also a derivation. Then, they proved the claime under the additional assumption that
A
is Arens regular. Finally, Ghahramani, Loy and Willis
[
12
]
proved it for any Banach algebra; see also
[
4
]
. In the following, we prove this result for cyclic and point amenability.

Proposition 5.1

Let
A
be a Banach algebra and every continuous derivation from a Banach algebra
A
into
A^{*}
is weakly compact. Then the following statements hold.

(i)
A bounded linear operator
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
is a cyclic derivation if and only if
D^{**}
is a cyclic derivation.

(ii)
If
A^{**}
is cyclically weakly amenable, then
A
is cyclically weakly amenable.

(iii)
Assume that
A
is a unital, commutative Banach algebra. If
A^{**}
is cyclically amenable, then
A
is cyclically amenable.

Proof.
Let
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
be a continuous derivation. By assumption,
D
is weakly compact and so
D^{**}
is a continuous derivation. If
\Phi,\Psi\in A^{**}
, then there exist nets
(a_{\alpha})
and
(b_{\beta})
in
A
such that
a_{\alpha}\rightarrow\Phi
and
b_{\beta}\rightarrow\Psi
in the weak
∗
topology of
A^{**}
. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha}\lim_{\beta}\langle D(a_{\alpha}),b_{\beta}\rangle=\langle D^{**}(\Phi),\Psi\rangle.

(3)

Assume that
D
is cyclic. Then (
3
) implies that
D^{**}
is cyclic. The converse follows from the fact that
A
is a subspace of
A^{**}
. So (i) holds. The statement (ii) follows at once from (i). Finally, let
D:A\rightarrow A^{*}
be a continuous cyclic derivation. Then for every
a\in A
, we have

\langle D^{*}(1_{A}),a\rangle=\langle D(a),1_{A}\rangle=0

and so for every
\Phi\in A^{**}
, we have

\langle D^{**}(\Phi),1_{A}\rangle=\langle\Phi,D^{*}(1_{A})\rangle=0.

Hence
D^{**}
is cyclic. By assumption
D^{**}=0
. Therefore,
D=0
.

Let
A

(i)

A^{**}

Corollary 5.3

Let the second dual of Banach algebra
A
be weakly amenable. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

A
is 0-point amenable.

(ii)
If
\Delta(A)\neq\emptyset
, then
A
is cyclically weakly amenable.

(iii)
If
A
is cyclic amenable and
\Delta(A)\neq\emptyset
, then
A
is weakly amenable.

(iv)
If
A
is unital and commutative, then
A
is weakly amenable.

(v)
If
A
is semisimple, then
A
is weakly amenable.

Proof.
Let
A^{**}
be weakly amenable. Then
A^{**}
is 0-point amenable; see Theorem 4.1 in
[

]
. In view of Proposition

,

that if

is non-empty, then cyclically weak amenability and point amenability are equivalent. Also,

Let
A
be a Banach algebra with the multiplication “
\cdot
”. Then
A
with the norm
\|.\|_{A}
and the multiplication “
\bullet
” defined by

a\bullet b=b\cdot a\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt(a,b\in A)

is also a Banach algebra. This Banach algebra is called the
opposite algebra

of

A
and is denoted by
A^{op}
.

Theorem 5.4

Let
A
be a Banach algebra. Then
A
is weakly (respectively, point, cyclically) amenable if and only
A^{op}
is weakly (respectively, point, cyclically) amenable.

Proof.
Let
D:A^{op}\rightarrow(A^{op})^{*}
be a continuous derivation. We define the bounded linear operator
\tilde{D}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
by

\langle\tilde{D}(a),b\rangle=\langle D(a),b\rangle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt(a,b\in A).

Then for every
a,b,c\in A
we have

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a\cdot b),c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(b\bullet a),c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle b\bullet D(a),c\rangle+\langle D(b)\bullet a,c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a),c\bullet b\rangle+\langle D(b),a\bullet c\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D(a),b\cdot c\rangle+\langle D(b),c\cdot a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a),b\cdot c\rangle+\langle\tilde{D}(b),c\cdot a\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{D}(a)\cdot b,c\rangle+\langle a\cdot\tilde{D}(b),c\rangle.

Hence
\tilde{D}
is a continuous derivation. If
A
is weakly amenable, then

D=\hbox{ad}_{F}
for some
F\in A^{*}
. Thus

\displaystyle\langle D(a),b\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(a\bullet b)-F(b\bullet a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(b\cdot a)-F(a\cdot b)=\langle\hbox{ad}_{-F}(a),b\rangle.

It follows that
D
is inner. Therefore,
A^{op}
is weakly amenable. The other case is proved similarly. For the converse, we only note that
(A^{op})^{op}=A
.

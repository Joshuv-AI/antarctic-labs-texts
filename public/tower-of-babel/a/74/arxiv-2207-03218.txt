1.1 Background.

Consider the following two-component coupled nonlinear Schrödinger equations (also known as Gross-Pitaevskii equations):

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}\Phi_{1}+V_{1}(x)\Phi_{1}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\Phi_{1}+\mu_{1}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}\Phi_{1}+\beta|\Phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi_{1},\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0,\\
-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial_{t}}\Phi_{2}+V_{2}(x)\Phi_{2}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\Phi_{2}+\mu_{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi_{2}+\beta|\Phi_{1}|^{2}\Phi_{2},\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0,\end{cases}

where
N\leq 3
,
i
is the imaginary unit,
\hbar
is the Plank constant,
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
and
\beta\neq 0
is a coupling constant. System (
1.1
) has applications in many physical problems, such as nonlinear optics, continuum mechanics, plasma physics and Bose-Einstein condensates theory for multispecies Bose-Einstein condensates. In the context of Bose-Einstein condensates,
\Phi_{j}(x,t)(j=1,2)
are the corresponding condensate amplitudes;
V_{j}(x)
are the traping potentials;
\mu_{j}
and
\beta
are the intraspecies and interspecies scattering lengths. The physically realistic spatial dimension are
1\leq N\leq 3
. When
N=2
, problem (
1.1
) arises in the Hartree-Fock theory for a double condenstate,
i.e.
, a binary mixture of Bose-Einstein condensates in two different hyperfine states
|1\rangle
and
|2\rangle
(see
[
12
,
26
]
). The sign of
\beta
determines whether the interactions of states are repulsive or attractive. The interaction is attractive if
\beta>0
and repulsive if
\beta<0
, where the two states are in strong competition.

A standing wave solution of (
1.1
) is of the following form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(x,t)=e^{-iE_{t}/\hbar}u(x)\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \Phi_{2}(x,t)=e^{-iE_{t}/\hbar}v(x).

Substituting (
1.2
) into (
1.1
), then system (
1.1
) is reduced to the following elliptic system

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta u+(V_{1}(x)-E)u=\mu_{1}u^{3}+\beta v^{2}u,\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta v+(V_{2}(x)-E)v=\mu_{2}v^{3}+\beta u^{2}v,\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.\\
\end{cases}

Renaming the parameters by

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}},\ a(x)=V_{1}(x)-E,\ b(x)=V_{2}(x)-E,

we obtain the folowing elliptic system

(
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+a(x)u=\mu_{1}u^{3}+\beta v^{2}u\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta v+b(x)v=\mu_{2}v^{3}+\beta u^{2}v\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{cases}

Before going further, we point out that the system (

C

1.2 Motivation.
In this paper, inspired by Byeon and Wang
[
7
]
, we are concerned with the asymptotic behavior of positive ground state solutions of (

C
ε

) in the critical frequency case for
\beta>0
sufficiently large. Obviously, problem (

C
ε

) is variational. That is, our main aim is to find nontrivial solutions of problem (

C
ε

) by seeking nontrivial critical points in some weighted Sobolev space of the corresponding energy functional
\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}
given by

\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\varepsilon^{2}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2})+a(x)u^{2}+b(x)v^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\mu_{1}v^{4}+2\beta u^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{2}v^{4}).

In the sequel, the potentials
a(x)
and
b(x)
may achieve
0
with homogeneous behaviour or vanish on the closure of an open set, but remain bounded away from
0
at infinity. Throughout the paper, we assume that

(f_{1})

\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta>0
.

(f_{2})

a\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N},[0,+\infty))
and
0=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}a(x)<\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}a(x):=a_{\infty}<\infty
.

(f_{3})

b\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N},[0,+\infty))
and
0=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}b(x)<\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}b(x):=b_{\infty}<\infty
.

One of the difficulties in the study of (

C
ε

) is that it has semi-trivial solutions of type
(u,0)
and
(0,v)
, where
u(x)
and
v(x)
solve

(1.4)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+a(x)u=\mu_{1}u^{3},\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.5)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta v+b(x)v=\mu_{2}v^{3},\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

By
[

1.4

Let

Let

Definition 1.1
.

[
7
]

A function

is called positive

for all

and
W(ty)=t^{\gamma}W(y)
for any

and

For any given positive

-homogeneous function

, we consider the following coupled system

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+W(x)u=\mu_{1}u^{3}+\beta v^{2}u\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\\
-\Delta v+W(x)v=\mu_{2}v^{3}+\beta u^{2}v\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{cases}

which will play as the limit problem of (

C
ε

) with homogeneous potentials and whose corresponding functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{W}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+W(x)u^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+W(x)v^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mu_{1}v^{4}+2\beta u^{2}v^{2}+\mu_{2}v^{4}.

Here

is the completion of

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{W}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+Wu^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since the potential

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{W}:=\inf\{\mathcal{J}_{W}(u,v)|(u,v)\in H_{W}\times H_{W}\setminus\{(0,0)\},\langle\mathcal{J}_{W}^{\prime}(u,v),(u,v)\rangle=0\},

can be attained by a positive ground state solution.

It is easy to know

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2k}a(\varepsilon^{k+1}x+x_{\ast})\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2k}b(\varepsilon^{k+1}x+x_{\ast})\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mu_{1}\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{4}+2\beta\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\mu_{2}\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}^{4},

Next, we prove
(\omega,\phi)
is a critical point of
\mathcal{J}_{W_{\ast}}
. By Fatou’s Lemma and the lower semi-continuity of the norm,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\omega|^{2}+W_{\ast}\omega^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+W_{\ast}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2k}a(\varepsilon^{k+1}x+x_{\ast})\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2k}b(\varepsilon^{k+1}x+x_{\ast})\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4m.

Since
R
is arbitrary, we get that

(4.14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\omega|^{2}+W_{\ast}\omega^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+W_{\ast}\phi^{2}\leq 4m

and then

\omega,\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
For any
\xi,\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, Multiplying equation (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) by
\xi\left(\cdot-\frac{x_{*}}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}\right)
and
\eta\left(\cdot-\frac{x_{*}}{\varepsilon^{k+1}}\right)
and making
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\omega\nabla\xi+W_{*}\omega\xi+\nabla\phi\nabla\eta+W_{*}\phi\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mu_{1}\omega^{3}\xi+\beta\phi^{2}\omega\xi+\beta\omega^{2}\phi\eta+\mu_{2}\phi^{3}\eta,

which implies
\mathcal{J}_{W_{*}}^{\prime}(\omega,\phi)=0
in
(H_{W_{\ast}}\times H_{W_{\ast}})^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{J}_{W_{*}}(\omega,\phi)\geq\mathcal{E}_{W_{\ast}}
. Due to (
4.14
),
\mathcal{J}_{W_{*}}(\omega,\phi)\leq m=\mathop{\inf}\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{E}_{W_{i}}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{J}_{W_{*}}(\omega,\phi)=\mathop{\inf}\limits_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{E}_{W_{i}}.

and
(\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon},\tilde{\phi}_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(\omega,\phi)
in
\mathbb{H}_{loc}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. The proof is completed.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

This last section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.5
, which covers the case that the potential
a
and
b
vanish on the closure of some smooth bounded open set.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof.

Step 1.
Similarly to Theorem
1.2
, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta>\beta_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we prove that system (

C
ε

) has a positive ground state solution
(U_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon})
. Define

E_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{H}_{loc}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+a(x)u^{2}<\infty,\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+b(x)v^{2}<\infty\right\}

endowed with norm

\|(u,v)\|_{\varepsilon}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+a(x)u^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+b(x)v^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Define the auxiliary functional
J_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(E_{\varepsilon})
by for each
(w,z)\in E_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(w,z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{a(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}w^{2}+|\nabla z|^{2}+\frac{b(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}z^{2}-\frac{1}{4}F(w,z).

Set

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}:=\inf\{J_{\varepsilon}(w,z):\ (w,z)\in E_{\varepsilon}\setminus\{(0,0)\},\langle J^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(w,z),(w,z)\rangle=0\}.

For any
(u,v)\in E_{\ast}=H_{0}^{1}(int(\Omega))\times H_{0}^{1}(int(\Omega))
, set

\displaystyle J_{\ast}(w,z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}+|\nabla z|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\mu_{1}w^{4}+2\beta w^{2}z^{2}+\mu_{2}z^{4}.

Similarly to Lemma
3.6
, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta>\beta_{0}^{\varepsilon}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle d_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{N-4}c_{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon^{N-4}\min\{I_{\varepsilon}(U_{1\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot),0),I_{\varepsilon}(0,V_{1\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot)\}.

Moreover, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, problem (

C
ε

) admits a ground state solution
(U_{\varepsilon},V_{\varepsilon})
, which is fully nontrivial.
By virtue of the Nehari manifold method, one can show that for any
\beta>0
, the following problem

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\mu_{1}w^{3}+\beta z^{2}w,\ x\in\mbox{int}(\Omega),\\
-\Delta z=\mu_{2}z^{3}+\beta w^{2}z,\ x\in\mbox{int}(\Omega),\\
w,z\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega))\end{cases}

admits a ground state solution. Moreover, set

\displaystyle 0<c_{\ast}=\inf\{J_{\ast}(w,z):\ (w,z)\in E_{\ast}\setminus\{(0,0)\},\langle J_{\ast}^{\prime}(w,z),(w,z)\rangle=0\},

it is easy to check that
d_{\varepsilon}=J_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{-1}U_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon^{-1}V_{\varepsilon})\leq c_{\ast}\leq\tilde{c}_{\ast}
, where
\tilde{c}_{\ast}
is the least energy of the following problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\beta z^{2}w,\ x\in\mbox{int}(\Omega),\\
-\Delta z=\beta w^{2}z,\ x\in\mbox{int}(\Omega),\\
w,z\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega)).\end{cases}

Step 2.
Let
w_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}U_{\varepsilon}
and
z_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}V_{\varepsilon}
.
Noting that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{a(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{b(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}z_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq 4c_{\ast}.

It follows from
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
that there exists
C>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+w_{\varepsilon}^{2})\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+a(x)w_{\varepsilon}^{2}),

which implies that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+w_{\varepsilon}^{2})\leq 4Cc_{\ast}
if
\varepsilon\leq 1
.
Similar result holds for
z_{\varepsilon}
. So,
(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
\mathbb{H}
if
\varepsilon\leq 1
and for some
(w,z)\in\mathbb{H}
,
(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup(w,z)
in
\mathbb{H}
and
(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(w,z)
in
\mathbb{L}_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

For
R>0
large enough such that
a(x)\geq\frac{a_{\infty}}{2}
and
b(x)\geq\frac{b_{\infty}}{2}
for
|x|\geq R
. By (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\min\{a_{\infty},b_{\infty}\}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+z_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}a(x)w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+b(x)z_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leq 4c_{\ast}\varepsilon^{2}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

So Rellich’s theorem tells us that
w_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow w
and
z_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow z
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. On the other hand, due to

J_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})=0
, for
\varepsilon>0
small and some
C,C_{1}
(independent of
\varepsilon
),

\displaystyle\|(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\|_{4}^{2}\leq C\|(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq C_{1}\|(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\|_{4}^{4},

which shows that
\|(w,z)\|_{4}>0
and
(w,z)\neq(0,0)
.

We claim that
w,z\not\equiv 0
. Otherwise, without loss of generalization, if
z\equiv 0
, then
z_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Noting that
\{z_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality,
z_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
strongly in
L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{b(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}z_{\varepsilon}^{2}=0.

Then similarly to Section 4,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}d_{\varepsilon}\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon^{N-4}I_{\varepsilon}(U_{1\varepsilon}(\varepsilon\cdot),0),

which gives a contradiction by using (
5.1
) similarly as above.

Step 3.
We show that
w,z\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega))
for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
small. By (
5.3
), we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma}a(x)w^{2}+b(x)z^{2}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma}a(x)w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+b(x)z_{\varepsilon}^{2}=0,

which implies that
w=z=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Sigma
. Similarly,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma\setminus\Omega}a(x)w^{2}+b(x)z^{2}=0

which implies that
w=0
in
\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}
and
z=0
in
\mbox{int}(\mathcal{A})\setminus\mathcal{B}
. That is,
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\mathcal{A}))
and
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B}))
.

On the one hand, one can get that
w_{\varepsilon}+z_{\varepsilon}
satisfies, in the weak sense,

-\Delta(w_{\varepsilon}+z_{\varepsilon})\leq\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta\}(w_{\varepsilon}+z_{\varepsilon})^{3},\,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Recalling that
\{w_{\varepsilon}+z_{\varepsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to
\varepsilon
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
small, similarly as that in
[
6
, Proposition 2.2]
, one can get that
\{w_{\varepsilon}+z_{\varepsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Sigma)
with respect to
\varepsilon
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
small. Moreover,
\|z\|_{\infty}+\|w\|_{\infty}\leq C
for some
C
(independent of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
). On the other hand, denote by
\phi_{1}
and
\lambda_{1}
the first eigenfunction and eigenvalue of
(-\Delta,H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A})))
respectively. Since

-\Delta z_{\varepsilon}+\frac{b(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}z_{\varepsilon}=\mu_{2}z_{\varepsilon}^{3}+\beta w_{\varepsilon}^{2}z_{\varepsilon},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

we obtain that

\lambda_{1}\int_{\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}}z_{\varepsilon}\phi_{1}=\int_{\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}))}(\mu_{2}z_{\varepsilon}^{3}+\beta w_{\varepsilon}^{2}z_{\varepsilon})\phi_{1}.

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,

\int_{\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}}z\phi_{1}[\lambda_{1}-\mu_{2}z^{2}]=0.

So if
\mu_{2}>0
is small such that
\lambda_{1}-\mu_{2}z^{2}(x)>0
a. e. in
\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}
, we know
z=0
in
\mbox{int}(\mathcal{B})\setminus\mathcal{A}
. That is,
z\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega))
. Similarly,
w\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega))
.

Step 4.
For any
\eta,\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\mbox{int}(\Omega))
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{int}(\Omega)}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\nabla\eta+\nabla z_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi-\mu_{1}w_{\varepsilon}^{3}\eta-\beta z_{\varepsilon}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\eta-\beta w_{\varepsilon}^{2}z_{\varepsilon}\varphi-\mu_{2}z_{\varepsilon}^{3}\varphi=J_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})(\eta,\varphi)=0

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mbox{int}(\Omega)}\nabla w\nabla\eta+\nabla z\nabla\varphi-\mu_{1}w^{3}\eta-\beta z^{2}w\eta-\beta w^{2}z\varphi-\mu_{2}z^{3}\varphi=0.

In view of the regularity assumptions on the set
\Omega
and by classical regularity theory,
(w,z)
is a fully nontrivial solution of problem (
5.2
) and

J_{\ast}(w,z)\geq c_{\ast}
.

We also have

\displaystyle\mathop{\limsup}\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+z_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{a(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+|\nabla z_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{b(x)}{\varepsilon^{2}}z_{\varepsilon}^{2}+w_{\varepsilon}^{2}+z_{\varepsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}F(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})+\|w\|_{2}^{2}+\|z\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=F(w,z)+\|w\|_{2}^{2}+\|z\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}+|\nabla z|^{2}+w^{2}+z^{2}.

This implies
(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(w,z)
strongly in
\mathbb{H}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Due to

, the following inequality holds

J_{\ast}(w,z)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}J_{\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon},z_{\varepsilon})\leq c_{\ast},

which yields that
(w,z)
is a ground state of problem (
5.2
). The proof is completed.
∎

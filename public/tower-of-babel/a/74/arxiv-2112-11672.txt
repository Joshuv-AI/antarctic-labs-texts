1.
Introduction

Let
\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
be the moduli space of genus
g
stable curves—curves with only nodal singularities and finite automorphism group—with
n
labeled points disjoint from nodes, for any
g,n\in\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,...\}
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
. Define the forgetful map
\pi:\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
that forgets the last point, and define
\omega_{\pi}
to be the relative dualising sheaf of the map
\pi
. There are natural sections
p_{i}:\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}
of the map
\pi
for each
i=1,...,n
. Define
L_{i}=p_{i}^{*}\omega_{\pi}
to be the line bundle
L_{i}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
with fibre above
[(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})]
given by
T_{p_{i}}^{*}C
and
\psi_{i}=c_{1}(L_{i})\in H^{2}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
to be its first Chern class. Define the Hodge bundle
\mathbb{E}_{g}=\pi_{*}\omega_{\pi}
which is a rank
g
bundle over
\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
and
\lambda_{i}=c_{i}(\mathbb{E}_{g})\in H^{2i}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
to be its
i
th Chern class for
i=1,...,g
. Define the Miller-Morita-Mumford classes
\kappa_{m}=\pi_{*}\psi^{m+1}_{n+1}\in H^{2m}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
where
\pi
is the forgetful map
\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
. We will refer to
\psi_{i}
and
\kappa_{m}
as
\psi
classes, respectively
\kappa
classes.

1.1.
The construction

Consider
\{s_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\mid i>0\}
defined by

\exp\left(-\sum_{i>0}s_{i}t^{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)!!t^{k}

and
\{\sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\mid i>0\}
defined by

\exp\left(-\sum_{i>0}\sigma_{i}t^{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}k!t^{k}.

Define

\mathbb{K}=K_{0}+K_{1}+K_{2}+...=\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})

and

\mathbb{J}=J_{0}+J_{1}+J_{2}+...=\exp(\sum\sigma_{i}\kappa_{i})\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})

where
K_{m},J_{m}\in H^{2m}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
.
This defines two sequences of homogeneous polynomials in
\kappa_{m}
. The first few polynomials are given by:

K_{1}=3\kappa_{1},\quad K_{2}=\frac{3}{2}(3\kappa_{1}^{2}-7\kappa_{2}),\quad K_{3}=\frac{3}{2}(3\kappa_{1}^{3}-21\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}+46\kappa_{3})

K_{4}=\frac{9}{8}(3\kappa_{1}^{4}-42\kappa_{1}^{2}\kappa_{2}+49\kappa_{2}^{2}+184\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}-562\kappa_{4})

and

J_{1}=\kappa_{1},\quad J_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{1}^{2}-3\kappa_{2}),\quad J_{3}=\frac{1}{6}(\kappa_{1}^{3}-9\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}+26\kappa_{3})

J_{4}=\frac{1}{24}(\kappa_{1}^{4}-18\kappa_{1}^{2}\kappa_{2}+27\kappa_{2}^{2}+104\kappa_{1}\kappa_{3}-426\kappa_{4}).

Conjecture 1
.

The following polynomial relations among the
\kappa_{j}
hold:

(1)

\displaystyle K_{m}(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for}\quad m>2g-2+n\quad\text{except}\quad(m,n)=(3g-3,0);

\displaystyle J_{m}(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m>2g-2+n&\\
m=2g-2+n,&n>1.\end{array}\right.

The conjecture is trivially true when
m>3g-3+n
, and hence says nothing about
g=0
. Evidence for the conjecture is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For any polynomial
P(\psi_{1},...,\psi_{n})\in H^{2m}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
with
n>0
and
m<g-1
,

\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathbb{K}\cdot P(\psi_{1},...\psi_{n})=0.

Theorem
1
is the first step towards a proof of a weak version of Conjecture
1
involving the tautological ring
RH^{m}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n})\subset H^{2m}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
,
[
16
]
—see Corollary
2.8
in Section
2.1
. An analogous statement for
\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathbb{J}\cdot P(\psi_{1},...\psi_{n})
is reduced to a purely combinatorial statement, without reference to the moduli space of stable curves, in Section
2.2.1
.

The construction of the polynomials in
\kappa
classes

and

resembles the following construction of polynomials in
\kappa
classes used to produce polynomial relations among
\kappa
classes over the moduli space of curves of compact type
\cal M_{g,n}^{\text{ct}}
. A stable curve is of compact type if its dual graph is a tree, and we have:

\cal M_{g,n}\subset\cal M_{g,n}^{\text{ct}}\subset\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}.

In
[
32

[

. Define
\{p_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\mid i>0\}
by

\exp\left(-\sum_{i>0}p_{i}t^{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k-1)!!t^{k}

where
(-1)!!=1
. Define

\mathbb{P}=P_{0}+P_{1}+P_{2}+...=\exp(\sum p_{i}\kappa_{i})\in H^{*}(\cal M_{g,n}^{\text{ct}},\mathbb{Q})

where

.

Theorem 2

(Pixton 2013)
.

P_{m}=0\in H^{2m}(\cal M_{g,n}^{\text{ct}},\mathbb{Q}),\quad 2m>2g-2+n.

Theorem

appears in
[

[

Conjecture 2

Conjecture 3

For

The uniqueness part of Conjecture

2.
Vanishing of intersection numbers

In this section we consider intersection numbers
\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathbb{K}\cdot\omega
and
\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathbb{J}\cdot\omega
for any tautological class
\omega\in RH^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n})
. We prove Theorem
1
regarding vanishing of intersection numbers of
\mathbb{K}
with polynomials in
\psi
classes via regularity of a related potential. Using this same idea, we prove that the more general vanishing of intersection numbers with
\omega\in RH^{m}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
for
m<g-1
, i.e. a weak version of Conjecture
1
, is equivalent to a purely combinatorial problem stated in terms of regularity of correlators, without reference to the moduli space of curves.

2.1.
Topological recursion for the
\kappa
-potential

Consider two sets of independent variables
s=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots)
and
t=(t_{0},t_{1},\dots)
. Set

\mathcal{K}=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{i}\kappa_{i}\Bigr)

and introduce the function

{\cal F}(\hbar,t,s)=\sum_{g,n}\frac{\hbar^{g}}{n!}\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathcal{K}\;\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}\;t_{k_{1}}\dots t_{k_{n}}

where the sum is over all
g,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
2g-2+n>0
and
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
This is the generating function for intersection numbers of all possible monomials in
\kappa
classes and
\psi
classes. If we set
s_{i}=0
for all
i
, this specializes to the Kontsevich-Witten potential enumerating intersection numbers of
\psi
-monomials,

{\cal F}(\hbar,t,0)=F^{KW}(\hbar,t).

Define polynomials
h_{i}=h_{i}(s)
by the formal expansion

(4)

1+h_{1}t+h_{2}t^{2}+\dots=\exp(-\sum s_{i}t^{i}).

Theorem 3

(
[
26
]
)
.

The full potential
{\cal F}(\hbar,t,s)
is obtained from the Kontsevich-Witten potential
F^{KW}(\hbar,t)
by a suitable shift of variables

{\cal F}(\hbar,t,s)=F^{KW}(\hbar,t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}-h_{1}(s),t_{3}-h_{2}(s),\dots).

Remark 2.1
.

The Kontsevich-Witten potential is a power series in the variables
t_{0},t_{1},\hbar
whose coefficients are polynomials in the remaining variables. Therefore, the Taylor expansion of
F^{KW}
at the point
t=(0,0,-h_{1},-h_{2},\dots)
is well defined.

Proof.

There is a standard inductive procedure reducing computation of intersection numbers involving
\kappa
classes to those involving just
\psi
classes. This procedure is based on the following relations describing the behaviour of
\kappa
classes under the forgetful map
\pi:\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
:

\kappa_{m}=\pi_{*}(\psi_{n+1}^{m+1}),\qquad\psi_{n+1}^{a}\pi^{*}(\kappa_{m})=\psi_{n+1}^{a}(\kappa_{m}-\psi_{n+1}^{m}),~a>0.

These relations imply relations between intersection numbers which are equivalent to the following linear partial differential equations:

(5)

V_{m}{\cal F}=0,\quad m=1,2,\dots,\qquad V_{m}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}h_{i-m-1}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}.

These equations mean that the function
{\cal F}
is constant along the phase curves of the pairwise commuting vector fields
V_{m}
and thus along the whole distribution generated by these fields. The integral subvariety of this distribution passing through the point
(t,s)
intersects the subspace
s=0
at the point
(q,0)
where
q=(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2}-h_{1}(s),t_{3}-h_{2}(s),\dots)
. This implies the equality of the theorem.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Equation (
4
) provides an invertible change of
h
and
s
coordinates. From now on, it will be more convenient for us to apply this change and to use the coordinates
h_{1},h_{2},\dots
instead of
s_{1},s_{2},\dots
in all considerations. So, for example, by
\mathcal{K}(h)
we mean the class

\mathcal{K}(h)=\exp\bigl((-h_{1})\,\kappa_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}h_{1}^{2}-h_{2})\,\kappa_{2}+(-\frac{1}{3}h_{1}^{3}+h_{1}h_{2}-h_{3})\,\kappa_{3}+\dots\bigr)

which is the exponential
e^{\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i}}
expressed in terms of
h
coordinates, and similarly for the
\kappa
-potential
{\cal F}
. In
h
-coordinates, the equalities (
5
) take a simpler form

\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial h_{m}}+\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t_{m+1}}=0,\quad m=1,2,\dots.

Corollary 2.3
.

The potential
{\cal F}
obeys the following Virasoro constraints. For any
m\geq-1
we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=m-1}(2i+1)!!(2j+1)!!\left(\hbar\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal F}}{\partial t_{i}\partial t_{j}}+\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t_{i}}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t_{j}}\right)+\\
\sum_{k-i=m}(t_{i}-h_{i-1})\frac{(2k+1)!!}{(2i-1)!!}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t_{k}}+\delta_{m,-1}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}+\delta_{m,0}\frac{\hbar}{8}=0.

where we set
h_{0}=1
,
h_{-1}=0
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the Virasoro constraints for the Kontsevich-Witten potential.
∎

Here is an equivalent form of the Virasoro constraints. Define the following partial differential operators:

L_{m}=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{i+j=m-1}(2i+1)!!(2j+1)!!\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{i}\partial t_{j}}+\sum_{k-i=m}t_{i}\frac{(2k+1)!!}{(2i-1)!!}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}+\delta_{m,-1}\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}+\delta_{m,0}\frac{\hbar}{8}.

Then we have

\left(L_{m}-\sum_{k-i=m}h_{i-1}\frac{(2k+1)!!}{(2i-1)!!}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right)e^{\hbar^{-1}{\cal F}}=0,\qquad m=-1,0,1,2,\dots

There is another equivalent way to represent the Virasoro constraints, namely, in the form of
topological recursion

[
14
]
. Let us expand
{\cal F}=\sum_{g,n}\hbar^{g}F_{g,n}
where
F_{g,n}
is the contribution of intersection numbers on a single moduli space,

F_{g,n}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathcal{K}(h)\;\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}\;t_{k_{1}}\dots t_{k_{n}}.

There is a different way to collect these intersection numbers into a generating polynomial, namely, using the so called
correlator differentials

w_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}}\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\mathcal{K}(h)\;\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}~\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(2k_{i}+1)!!dz_{i}}{z^{2k_{i}+2}_{i}}.

Equivalently, introduce the
loop insertion operators

\delta_{i}=\sum_{k=0}\frac{(2k+1)!!dz_{i}}{z^{2k+2}_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}.

Then we have

w_{g,n}=\delta_{1}\dots\delta_{n}F_{g,n}.

Theorem 4
.

The differentials
w_{g,n}
satisfy the relations of topological recursion for the spectral curve

x=\frac{1}{2}z^{2},\qquad y=z+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}.

In this statement, we treat
x(z)
as a global function on the complex line
\mathbb{C}
with coordinate
z
having a ramification of order
2
at the origin, while
y(z)
is just a formal power expansion near the point
z=0
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Any potential of topological recursion with the spectral curve whose
x
-function is given by
x(z)=\frac{1}{2}z^{2}
is a specialization of the
\kappa
-potential
{\cal F}
for suitable choices of the parameters
h_{i}
(or
s_{i}
), and thus is equal to the Kontsevich-Witten potential
F^{KW}
with a suitable shift of times
t_{i}
for
i\geq 2
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Sum up all Virasoro equations of Corollary
2.3
multiplying the
m
th equation by
\frac{2dz^{2}}{z^{2m+2}}
where
z
is a new variable. Then, denoting

\mathbf{t}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\frac{z^{2k}dz}{(2k-1)!!},\quad\eta(z)=y\,dx=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{k}\frac{z^{2k+2}dz}{(2k+1)!!}

and observing
\delta{\cal F}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial{\cal F}}{\partial t_{k}}\frac{(2k+1)!!dz}{z^{2k+2}}
,
we collect all Virasoro equations into a single one that we call the
master loop equation

\hbar\Bigl(\delta^{2}{\cal F}+\frac{dz^{2}}{4z^{2}}\Bigr)+(\delta{\cal F}+\mathbf{t}(z)-\eta(z))^{2}=O(z^{2}),

in the Laurent expansion in

. The original Virasoro equations can be extracted from the above relation by selecting the coefficient of
dz^{2}z^{2i}
for non-positive

. Equivalently, this equation can be written as

(6)

\delta{\cal F}=\frac{1}{2\eta(z)}\left(\hbar\Bigl(\delta^{2}{\cal F}+\frac{dz^{2}}{4z^{2}}\Bigr)+(\delta{\cal F})^{2}+2\,\mathbf{t}\,{\cal F}+\mathbf{t}^{2}\right)+O(1)

Now, select homogeneous terms of fixed degrees
(g,n)
and apply
\delta_{2}\dots\delta_{n}
to the obtained equation. Then the loop equation takes the following form.
Redefine
in the unstable cases

w_{0,1}(z_{1})=0,\qquad w_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\delta_{2}\mathbf{t}(z_{1})

and denote

\mathcal{W}_{g,n}(z,z_{N})=w_{g-1,n+1}(z,z,z_{N})+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
I\sqcup J=N\end{smallmatrix}}w_{g_{1},|I|+1}(z,z_{I})w_{g_{2},|J|+1}(z,z_{J}).

Here, we use the notation

and
z_{I}=\{z_{i_{1}},z_{i_{2}},\ldots,z_{i_{k}}\}
for
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}
. Then we obtain

(7)

w_{g,n}(z,z_{N})=\frac{\mathcal{W}_{g,n}(z,z_{N})}{2\eta(z)}+O(1)

in the expansion in
z
at
z=0
. Notice that
w_{g,n}(z,z_{N})
contains only negative powers of the coordinate
z
, therefore, the last equation determines it uniquely by induction:
w_{g,n}(z,z_{N})
as a meromorphic
1
-form in
z
is the principal part of the pole at

of the form on the right hand side of the equation. The obtained relation is nothing but an equivalent form of the conventional topological recursion relation. In order to observe it, we remark that the projection to the principal part of the pole can be written as the residue

w_{g,n}(z,z_{N})=\mathop{\text{res}}_{\zeta=0}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{g,n}(\zeta,z_{N})}{2\eta(\zeta)}\frac{\zeta\,dz}{z^{2}-\zeta^{2}}

Remark also that
w_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})
is the odd part of the

B(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}

w_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\delta_{2}\mathbf{t}(z_{1})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{dz_{2}(2k+1)!!}{z_{2}^{2k+2}}\frac{dz_{1}z_{1}^{2k}}{(2k-1)!!}=\\
\frac{(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}^{2}-z_{2}^{2})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(B(z_{1},z_{2})-B(-z_{1},z_{2})),

and the form

is the odd part of its primitive.
∎

2.2.
Regularity of potentials

We now introduce a parameter
\epsilon
into various potentials, and prove that regularity in
\epsilon
is equivalent to vanishing of some coefficients. This is enough to prove Theorem
1
and reduces a weak version of Conjecture
1
to a regularity property which is verified numerically in small cases.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let us make an additional rescaling in the series
{\cal F}
and in the corresponding correlator differentials
w_{g,n}
by replacing the class
\mathcal{K}(h)
in their definition with
\sum_{m}\epsilon^{2g-2+n-m}\mathcal{K}_{m}(h)
where
\mathcal{K}_{m}(h)
is the degree
m
homogeneous component of
\mathcal{K}(h)
. With this rescaling, the classes
\mathcal{K}_{m}(h)
with
m>2g-2+n
are multiplied with negative powers of the parameter
\epsilon
. Therefore, the fact that intersection numbers with these classes vanish is equivalent to the fact that the resulting rescaled polynomials
F_{g,n}
and differentials
w_{g,n}
are regular at
\epsilon=0
, that is, there exists limits
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}F_{g,n}
, or equivalently, there exist limits
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}w_{g,n}
. The rescaled correlator differentials also obey topological recursion with the following rescaled spectral curve

x=\frac{1}{2}z^{2},\qquad y=\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{k}\epsilon^{-k}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}.

The rescaling
h_{k}\to h_{k}\epsilon^{-k}
corresponds to the factor
\epsilon^{-m}
of
\mathcal{K}_{m}(h)
. And introducing an additional global factor
\epsilon^{-1}
of
y
results in the factor
\epsilon^{2g-2+n}
for the whole class
\mathcal{K}(h)
.

The class
\mathbb{K}
of Theorem
1
corresponds to the specialization
h_{k}=(-1)^{k}(2k+1)!!
in
\mathcal{K}(h)
. The
y
-function of the corresponding (rescaled) spectral curve is

(8)

y=\epsilon^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)!!\epsilon^{-k}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!!}=\frac{z}{z^{2}+\epsilon}.

Then we observe that this function possesses the property that

y^{-1}=z+\epsilon z^{-1}

is regular in
\epsilon
. This observation implies immediately the statement of Theorem
1
for the class
\mathbb{K}
by induction since negative powers of
\epsilon
do not appear in the recursion (
7
).
∎

Remark 2.5

As was mentioned above, the topological recursion relation is equivalent to the loop equation which is, in turn, an equivalent form of Virasoro constraints. Therefore, the above arguments that use topological recursion for the correlator differentials can be translated to the arguments involving Virasoro constraints for the potential

3.

3.1.

A tau function

of the KdV hierarchy (equivalently the KP hierarchy in odd times

U_{t_{1}}=UU_{t_{0}}+\frac{\hbar}{12}U_{t_{0}t_{0}t_{0}},\quad U(t_{0},0,0,...)=f(t_{0}).

12

uniquely from

. See
[
27
]
for the full definition.

of the KdV hierarchy arises out of a unitary matrix model studied in

4
,

. It is defined by the initial condition

\hbar\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{0}^{2}}\log Z^{\text{BGW}}(t_{0},0,0,...)=U^{\text{BGW}}(t_{0},0,0,...)=\frac{\hbar}{8(1-t_{0})^{2}}.

]

,
[
7
]
.

are

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt+\hbar^{2}\frac{63}{1024}\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{(1-t_{0})^{6}}+O(\hbar^{3})

\psi
classes, that is, those of the form
\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}K_{2g-2+n}
. By dimensional reason, these numbers are nonzero in the case
g=1
only, and by Theorem
3
we have

F^{K}(t_{0},0,0,\dots)=\hbar\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{1,n}}K_{n}\frac{t_{0}^{n}}{n!}=\hbar F_{1}^{\text{KW}}(t_{0},0,3!!,-5!!,7!!,\dots).

Fortunately, the
g=1
component
F_{1}^{\text{KW}}
of the Kontsevich-Witten potential is known. By the Itzykson-Zuber anzatz
[
22
]
(a sequence of the string and dilaton equations), it is given by

F_{1}^{\text{KW}}=-\frac{1}{24}\log\Bigl(1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k+1}\frac{Y^{k}}{k!}\Bigr)

where
Y=Y(t_{0},t_{1},\dots)
is the solution of the implicit equation

Y=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\frac{Y^{k}}{k!}.

Denote
y(t_{0})=Y(t_{0},0,3!!,-5!!,7!!,\dots)
. Then the implicit equation specializes to

y=1+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k-1)!!\frac{y^{k}}{k!}=t_{0}+y+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2y}}-1

which gives
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2y+1}}=1-t_{0}
. Then,
1-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k+1}\frac{Y^{k}}{k!}
specializes to

1-\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k-1)!!\frac{y^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}=\frac{1}{(1+2y)^{3/2}}=(1-t_{0})^{3},

and we get finally

F^{K}(t_{0},0,0,\dots)=-\frac{\hbar}{24}\log(1-t_{0})^{3}=-\frac{\hbar}{8}\log(1-t_{0}),

\frac{\partial^{2}F^{K}(t_{0},0,0,\dots)}{\partial t_{0}^{2}}=\frac{\hbar}{8(1-t_{0})^{2}}.

This coincides with the initial condition for the BGW potential.
∎

In Section
5.2
we relate Theorem
6
to a conjectural relationship between a family of classes
\Theta_{g,n}\in H^{2(2g-2+n)}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
and the BGW potential.

4.
Cohomological field theories

A
cohomological field theory
is a pair
(V,\eta)
composed of a finite-dimensional complex vector space
V
equipped with a metric
\eta
and a sequence of
S_{n}
-equivariant maps.

\Omega_{g,n}:V^{\otimes n}\to H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})

that satisfy compatibility conditions from inclusion of strata:

\phi_{\text{irr}}:\overline{\cal M}_{g-1,n+2}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\quad\phi_{h,I}:\overline{\cal M}_{h,|I|+1}\times\overline{\cal M}_{g-h,|J|+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\ \ I\sqcup J=\{1,...,n\}

given by

(13)

\displaystyle\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\Omega_{g-1,n+2}(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n}\otimes\Delta)

(14)

\displaystyle\phi_{h,I}^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n})

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}^{*}\Omega_{h,|I|+1}\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}\Omega_{g-h,|J|+1}\big(\bigotimes_{i\in I}v_{i}\otimes\Delta\otimes\bigotimes_{j\in J}v_{j}\big)

where
\Delta\in V\otimes V
is dual to the metric
\eta\in V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
, and
\pi_{i}
,
i=1,2
is projection onto the
i
th factor of
\overline{\cal M}_{h,|I|+1}\times\overline{\cal M}_{g-h,|J|+1}
. When
n=0
,
\Omega_{g}:=\Omega_{g,0}\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g},\mathbb{Q})
. The CohFT has a
unit
if there exists a vector
1\!\!1\in V
which satisfies

(15)

\Omega_{0,3}(1\!\!1\otimes v_{1}\otimes v_{2})=\eta(v_{1},v_{2}).

We also consider the case when
\Omega_{g,n}
satisfies (
13
) and (
14
) but not (
15
) and refer to this as a CohFT without unit.

The CohFT has
flat unit
if
1\!\!1\in H
is compatible with the forgetful map
\pi:\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
by

(16)

\Omega_{g,n+1}(1\!\!1\otimes v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n})=\pi^{*}\Omega_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n})

for
2g-2+n>0
.

Given a CohFT
\Omega=\{\Omega_{g,n}\}
and a basis
\{e_{1},...,e_{N}\}
of
V
, the partition function of
\Omega
is defined by:

Z_{\Omega}(\hbar,\{t^{\alpha}_{k}\})=\exp\sum_{g,n,\vec{k}}\frac{\hbar^{g-1}}{n!}\int_{\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}}\Omega_{g,n}(e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes...\otimes e_{\alpha_{n}})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j}^{k_{j}}\prod t^{\alpha_{j}}_{k_{j}}

for
\alpha_{i}\in\{1,...,N\}
and
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the case of one-dimensional CohFTs, i.e.
\dim V=1
, with unit
1\!\!1\in V
that satisfies
\eta(1\!\!1,1\!\!1)=1
(which is related to the more general
\eta(1\!\!1,1\!\!1)\in\mathbb{C}
by a simple rescaling), we identify
\Omega_{g,n}
with its image
\Omega_{g,n}(1\!\!1^{\otimes n})
, and write
\Omega_{g,n}\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
.

Given two CohFTs
\Omega^{i}
defined on
(V_{i},\eta_{i})
,
i=1,2
, they define a CohFT
\Omega^{1}\otimes\Omega^{2}
on
(V_{1}\otimes V_{2},\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2})
via the cup products
\Omega^{1}_{g,n}(u_{1}\otimes...\otimes u_{n})\cup\Omega^{2}_{g,n}(v_{1}\otimes...\otimes v_{n})
, and known as the tensor product. The trivial CohFT
1\!\!1
defined by
1\!\!1_{g,n}=1\in H^{0}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
acts as a unit with respect to tensor product. An
invertible
CohFT is any one-dimensional CohFT
\Omega^{1}
such that there exists
\Omega^{2}
, its inverse, with
\Omega^{1}\otimes\Omega^{2}=1\!\!1
. Invertible CohFTs were classified by Manin-Zograf
[
26
]
and Teleman
[
36
]
.
From the properties
\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}\kappa_{m}=\kappa_{m}
and
\phi_{h,I}^{*}\kappa_{m}=\pi_{1}^{*}\kappa_{m}+\pi_{2}^{*}\kappa_{m}
, proven in
[
3
]
, and
\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}\mu_{m}=\mu_{m}
and
\phi_{h,I}^{*}\mu_{m}=\pi_{1}^{*}\mu_{m}+\pi_{2}^{*}\mu_{m}
, for
\mu_{k}=ch_{k}(\mathbb{E})
, which uses the decomposition of the restriction of the Hodge bundle to the boundary, Manin and Zograf
[
26
]
proved that
\exp{\sum_{i\geq 0}(s_{i}\kappa_{i}+t_{i}\mu_{2i+1})}
defines an invertible CohFT. The converse was proven by Teleman in
[
36
]
giving the following theorem.

Theorem 7

(
[
26
,
36
]
)
.

For any
\{s_{i},t_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}

\Omega_{g,n}=\exp{\sum_{i\geq 0}(s_{i}\kappa_{i}+t_{i}\mu_{2i+1})}

defines an invertible CohFT and any invertible CohFT is of this form.

In particular,
\Omega_{g,n}^{\mathbb{K}}=\mathbb{K}
and
\Omega_{g,n}^{\mathbb{J}}=\mathbb{J}
define invertible CohFTs.

4.1.
Push-forward and pull-back properties

The polynomials
K_{m}(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})
and
J_{m}(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})
satisfy push-forward and pull-back relations with respect to the forgetful map
\pi:\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
.

(17)

\pi_{*}J_{m+1}=(2g-2+n-m)J_{m},\quad\pi_{*}K_{m+1}=(6g-6+3n-2m)K_{m}.

(18)

\pi^{*}J_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}i!\psi_{n+1}^{i}J_{m-i},\qquad\pi^{*}K_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}(2i+1)!!\psi_{n+1}^{i}K_{m-i}.

The push-forward and pull-back relations are a consequence of a more general result.
For any
v=(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, define

\mathbb{L}^{v}=L^{v}_{0}+L^{v}_{1}t+L^{v}_{2}t^{2}+...=\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{j}\kappa_{j}t^{j})

for
\ell^{v}_{j}
defined by (
19
) and (
20
).

Definition/Lemma 1
.

For any
v=(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, define the sequence
\{\ell^{v}_{j}\mid j>0\}
in either of the following two equivalent ways:

(19)

\exp\left(\sum\ell_{j}^{v}t^{j}\right)=1+at-bt^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{j>0}\ell_{j}^{v}t^{j}

(20)

\exp\left(-\sum\ell_{j}^{v}t^{j}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}t^{k}\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}(a+bm).

Proof.

Define
f(t)
by the right hand side of (
20
). Then, shifting the summation index by
1
, we get

f(t)=1+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}t^{k+1}\prod_{m=0}^{k}(a+mb)=\\
1-t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a+bk)(-1)^{k}t^{k}\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}(a+mb)=1-t(a+b\,t\frac{d}{dt})f(t).

The obtained differential equation is equivalent to (
19
).
∎

The classes
L^{v}_{k}
for any
v=(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
are proven below to satisfy push-forward relations using (
19
) and pull-back relations using (
20
). These relations specialise to (
17
) and (
18
) since
\mathbb{L}^{(1,1)}=\mathbb{J}
and
\mathbb{L}^{(3,2)}=\mathbb{K}
, i.e.
if we substitute
(a,b)=(1,1)
or
(3,2)
into (
20
), the right hand side becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}t^{k}\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}(1+m)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}k!t^{k}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}t^{k}\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}(3+2m)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(2k+1)!!t^{k}

and the relations (
17
) and (
18
) are immediate corollaries of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. We have abused notation and identified
\mathbb{K}(t)=K_{0}+K_{1}t+K_{2}t^{2}+...
with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{K}(1)
since
t
simply keeps track of degree, and similarly with
\mathbb{J}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}

\pi_{*}L^{v}_{m+1}=a(2g-2+n)L^{v}_{m}-bmL^{v}_{m}.

Proof.

Each
L^{v}_{m}
is a degree
m
polynomial in
\{\kappa_{j}\}
. We can calculate its push-forward as follows:

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\mathbb{L}^{v}=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\exp\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\kappa_{i}t^{i}=\pi_{*}\exp\sum\ell^{v}_{i}(\pi^{*}\kappa_{i}+\psi^{i}_{n+1})t^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\left(\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\pi^{*}\kappa_{i}t^{i})\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\psi^{i}_{n+1}t^{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{*}\left(\pi^{*}(\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\kappa_{i}t^{i}))\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\psi^{i}_{n+1}t^{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{v}\cdot\pi_{*}\exp\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\psi^{i}_{n+1}t^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{v}\cdot\pi_{*}\left(1+a\psi_{n+1}t-b\sum_{j>0}j\ell^{v}_{j}\psi^{j+1}_{n+1}t^{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{v}\cdot\left(a(2g-2+n)t-b\sum_{j>0}j\ell^{v}_{j}\kappa_{j}t^{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}^{v}\cdot a(2g-2+n)t-b\sum kL^{v}_{k}t^{k+1}.

The third last equality uses the differential equation (
19
) with
t\mapsto\psi_{n+1}t
.
The last equality is obtained via differentiation with respect to
t
of
\mathbb{L}^{v}=\sum L^{v}_{k}t^{k}=\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{j}\kappa_{j}t^{j})
to get

\sum kL^{v}_{k}t^{k-1}=\mathbb{L}^{v}\cdot\sum_{j>0}j\ell^{v}_{j}\kappa_{j}t^{j-1}.

The coefficient of
t^{m+1}
in the expression for
\pi_{*}\mathbb{L}^{v}
yields
\pi_{*}L^{v}_{m+1}=a(2g-2+n)L^{v}_{m}-bmL^{v}_{m}
as required.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}

\pi^{*}L^{v}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i}\psi_{n+1}^{i}L^{v}_{k-i}\prod_{m=0}^{i-1}(a+mb).

Proof.

\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathbb{L}^{v}

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\kappa_{i}t^{i})=\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}\pi^{*}\kappa_{i}t^{i})=\exp(\sum\ell^{v}_{i}(\kappa_{i}-\psi_{n+1}^{i})t^{i}

where the last equality uses (

t^{k}

∎

4.2.
CohFTs without unit

A one-dimensional CohFT without unit is a collection of classes
\Omega_{g,n}\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
that satisfies (
13
) and (
14
). It may be that
\Omega_{0,3}=0
as in the following examples.

Proposition 4.4
.

The sequences
\Omega_{g,n}=K_{2g-2+n}
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{g,n}=J_{2g-2+n}
are CohFTs without unit and
\Omega^{\prime}_{g,n}=0
for
n>1
iff Conjecture

holds.

Proof.

We will treat the case of
\Omega_{g,n}=K_{2g-2+n}
first. One direction is straightforward. If

(21)

K_{m}(\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{m})=0\quad\text{for}\quad m>2g-2+n,\ n>0

then for
\phi:\overline{\cal M}_{g_{1},n_{1}+1}\times\overline{\cal M}_{g_{2},n_{2}+1}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
where
g_{1}+g_{2}=g
,
n_{1}+n_{2}=n
and
p_{i}
,
i=1,2
is projection onto the
i
th factor

(22)

\phi^{*}K_{2g-2+n}=\sum_{i+j=2g-2+n}p_{1}^{*}K_{i}\cdot p_{2}^{*}K_{j}=p_{1}^{*}K_{2g_{1}-2+n_{1}+1}\cdot p_{2}^{*}K_{2g_{2}-2+n_{2}+1}.

The first equality uses
\phi^{*}\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})=p_{1}^{*}\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})\cdot p_{2}^{*}\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})
.
For the second equality,
i+j=2g-2+n
implies that for all but one summand in (

) either

by (
21
), or
j>2g_{2}-2+n_{2}+1
in which case

again by (

). Also
\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})=\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})
implies that
\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}K_{2g-2+n}=K_{2(g-1)-2+n+2}
and together with (
22
) we see that
\Omega_{g,n}=K_{2g-2+n}
is a CohFT without unit.

For the other direction, suppose that
\Omega_{g,n}=K_{2g-2+n}
is a CohFT without unit. For
k>0
and
n>0
, consider the following stable graph
\Gamma\in G_{g,n+k}
:
\Gamma=

0
0
0
\cdots
\vdots
n-1

which defines the stratum:

\overline{\cal M}_{\Gamma}=\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}\times\overbrace{\cal M_{0,3}\times...\times\cal M_{0,3}}^{k\text{ factors}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{\Gamma}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\cal M}_{g,n+k}.

For

where the first equality uses

which vanishes for degree reasons, the second equality uses the assumption that

is a CohFT without unit, and the final equality uses the degree

\phi_{\Gamma}^{*}\mathbb{K}=\phi_{\Gamma}^{*}\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})=\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})\otimes\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})^{\otimes k}=\exp(\sum s_{i}\kappa_{i})\otimes 1^{\otimes k}.

Hence

for

and
n>0
. When

For the case

.
∎

The push-forward relations (

\Omega_{g,n}\in H^{*}(\overline{\cal M}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
define its shift depending on a parameter
\epsilon

\Omega_{g,n}(\epsilon)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\pi_{*}^{m}\Omega_{g,n+m}\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{m!}.

This defines a CohFT for values

where the sum converges.

Lemma 4.5
.

\mathbb{J}(\epsilon):=\sum_{m\geq 0}\pi_{*}^{m}\mathbb{J}\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{m!}=\sum d_{k}(\epsilon)J_{k}t^{k},\quad d_{k}(\epsilon)=e^{-(k+\chi)\epsilon}

The factor
(1-t)^{2g-2+n}
is chosen in the definition of
\mathbb{K}(\epsilon)
for convenience. Note that any factor of
\lambda^{2g-2+n}
sends a CohFT to a CohFT.

Proof.

Iterating (

), we have

\pi_{*}^{m}J_{k}=M^{m}J_{k-m},\quad M=2g-2+n-k.

Hence the coefficient of
J_{k}t^{k}
in
\sum_{m\geq 0}\pi_{*}^{m}\mathbb{J}\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{m!}
is

as required.

For

, we iterate (
17
) to get

\pi_{*}^{m}K_{k}=M(M-1)...(M-m+1)K_{k-m},\quad M=3(2g-2+n)-2k-1.

Hence the coefficient of
K_{k}t^{k}
in
\sum_{m\geq 0}\pi_{*}^{m}\mathbb{K}\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{m!}
is
\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{M+k}{k}\epsilon^{k}=(1-\epsilon)^{-M-1}
, for
|\epsilon|<1
. When we multiply by
(1-t)^{2g-2+n}
, the coefficient of

in
\mathbb{K}(\epsilon)
is
(1-\epsilon)^{2(k+\chi)}
as required.
∎

The shift is equivalent to rescaling
(a,b)
in Definition

. Then
\mathbb{J}(\epsilon)
corresponds to

and
\mathbb{K}(\epsilon)
corresponds to

and

and

5.
Geometric constructions

In this section we give three geometric constructions which may give a way to prove the vanishing results involving the classes
\mathbb{J}
and
\mathbb{K}
.

5.1.
Hodge CohFT

The Hodge bundle
\mathbb{E}_{g}\to\overline{\cal M}_{g,n}
satisfies natural restriction properties on the boundary:

(23)

\begin{array}[]{l}0\to\mathbb{E}_{g-1}\to\phi_{\text{irr}}^{*}\mathbb{E}_{g}\to\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\cal M}_{g-1,n+2}}\to 0\\
\\
0\to\pi_{1}^{*}\mathbb{E}_{h}\oplus\pi_{2}^{*}\mathbb{E}_{g-h}\to\phi_{h,I}^{*}\mathbb{E}_{g}\to 0\end{array}

where
\pi_{i}
is projection onto the
i
th factor of
\overline{\cal M}_{h,|I|+1}\times\overline{\cal M}_{g-h,|J|+1}
. A consequence of the restriction properties is that the total Chern class
c(\mathbb{E}_{g})
of the Hodge bundle defines a CohFT. The relation
\pi^{*}\mathbb{E}_{g}=\mathbb{E}_{g}
implies that the CohFT has flat unit.

Similarly, the bundle
\mathbb{E}_{g}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{g}
satisfies natural restriction properties and its total Chern class
c(\mathbb{E}_{g})^{2}=c(\mathbb{E}_{g}\oplus\mathbb{E}_{g})
defines a CohFT. Alternatively, the square of any CohFT satisfies the properties of a CohFT. Define:

\Omega^{\text{Hod}2}_{g,n}:=c(\mathbb{E})^{2}=(1+\lambda_{1}+...+\lambda_{g})^{2}.

Flatness of
\mathbb{E}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{\vee}
implies
c(\mathbb{E}\oplus\mathbb{E}^{\vee})=c(\mathbb{E})c(\mathbb{E}^{\vee})=1
hence
\lambda_{g}^{2}=0
and
\lambda_{g-1}^{2}=2\lambda_{g-2}\lambda_{g}
so

\displaystyle\Omega^{\text{Hod}2}_{g,n}

For relations of the present section it would be more convenient to consider a slightly rescaled version of the class

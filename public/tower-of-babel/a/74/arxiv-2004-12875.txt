Abstract
We present new Pieri type formulas for Jack polynomials. As an application, we give a new derivation of higher order difference equations for interpolation Jack polynomials originally found by Knop and Sahi. We also propose Pieri formulas for interpolation Jack polynomials and intertwining relations for a kernel function for Jack polynomials.

Introduction

Given a positive integer
r
and a non-zero complex parameter
d
,
we define a second-order differential operator in the variables
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})
by

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{z}):=\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{j}^{2}\partial_{z_{j}}^{2}+d\sum_{1\leq j\not=l\leq r}\frac{z_{j}^{2}}{z_{j}-z_{l}}\partial_{z_{j}},

where
\partial_{z_{j}}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
.
The
Jack polynomials
are a family of homogeneous symmetric polynomials
P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)
, indexed by partitions
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
, which are characterized by the differential equation

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{z})P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}(m_{j}-1+d(r-j)),

together with the triangularity

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\leq\mathbf{m}}c_{\mathbf{m}\mathbf{k}}m_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{z}),\qquad c_{\mathbf{m}\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{Q}(d),\qquad c_{\mathbf{m}\mathbf{m}}=1

with respect to the dominance order on partitions

\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})
of a fixed weight
|\mathbf{m}|:=m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}\leq\mathbf{m}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{l=1}^{i}k_{l}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{i}m_{l},\qquad i=1,\ldots,r,

and the monomial symmetric polynomials

m_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{z}):=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}.\mathbf{m}}z^{\mathbf{n}}
,
where
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
denotes the symmetric group of degree
r
.
Since Jack polynomials
P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)
are orthogonal polynomials associated with the root system
A_{r-1}
and eigenstates of the Calogero–Surtherland model, they play important roles in various fields of mathematics and physics.
For their fundamental properties and applications, see
[
7
,
16
,
17
]
.

The
interpolation Jack polynomials
(or
shifted Jack polynomials
) are also a family of symmetric polynomials indexed by partitions
\mathbf{m}
, which we denote by
P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)
according to the notation of Koornwinder
[
4
]
.
They are uniquely defined by the following two conditions:

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\bigg(\mathbf{m}+\frac{d}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=0,\qquad\text{unless $\mathbf{k}\subseteq\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{P}$},

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)+\text{(lower degree terms)},

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}
denotes the staircase partition
(r-1,r-2,\ldots,2,1,0)
and
\mathbf{k}\subseteq\mathbf{m}
stands for the inclusion partial order defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}\subseteq\mathbf{m}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k_{i}\leq m_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,r.

These polynomials
P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)
were introduced by Sahi
[
13
]
, Knop–Sahi
[
3
]
and Okounkov–Olshanski
[
10
]
as a continuous deformation of shifted Schur polynomials
P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{z};1\right)

[
6
,
11
]
.
The interpolation Jack polynomials appear as a multivariate analogue of the falling factorials

\displaystyle P_{m}^{\mathrm{ip}}(z):=\begin{cases}z(z-1)\cdots(z-m+1),&m\not=0,\\
1,&m=0,\end{cases}

in explicit formulas of binomial type for multivariate hypergeometric functions (see
[
4
,
8
,
9
]
).

We review some fundamental results on Jack and interpolation Jack polynomials relevant to the main results of this paper.

1) Sekiguchi operators
(Debiard
[
2
]
, Macdonald
[
7
]
, Sekiguchi
[
15
]
).
The Jack polynomials are joint eigenfunctions of a commuting family of differential operators (
Sekiguchi operators
).
We define Sekiguchi operators
H_{r,p}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})

(p=0,1,\ldots,r)
and their generating function
S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z})
by

\displaystyle H_{r,p}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z}):=\sum_{l=0}^{p}\bigg(\frac{2}{d}\bigg)^{p-l}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[r],\\
|I|=l\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{z})}\bigg(\prod_{i\in I}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}\bigg)\Delta(\mathbf{z})\bigg)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[r]\setminus I,\\
|J|=p-l\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{j\in J}z_{j}\partial_{z_{j}}\bigg),

\displaystyle S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z}):=\sum_{p=0}^{r}H_{r,p}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})u^{r-p}

respectively, where
[r]:=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
and
\Delta(\mathbf{z}):=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}(z_{i}-z_{j})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle H_{r,p}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)e_{r,p}\bigg(\mathbf{m}+\frac{d}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta}\bigg),

\displaystyle S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z})P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)=P_{\mathbf{m}}\bigg(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\bigg)I_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{m}),

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle e_{r,k}(\mathbf{z}):=\!\!\!\!\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq r}\!\!\!\!z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{k}},\qquad k=1,\ldots,r,\qquad e_{r,0}(\mathbf{z}):=1,

\displaystyle I_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{m}):=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\bigg(u+r-k+\frac{2}{d}m_{k}\bigg)=\bigg(\frac{2}{d}\bigg)^{r}\prod_{k=1}^{r}\bigg(m_{k}+\frac{d}{2}(u+r-k)\bigg).

2) Pieri type formulas for Jack polynomials
(Lassalle
[
5
]
, Stanley
[

We introduce two kinds of normalization for Jack polynomials,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbf{m}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z}):=\frac{P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)}{P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{1};\frac{d}{2}\right)},

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathbf{m}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z}):=\frac{P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{z};\frac{d}{2}\right)}{P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{m}+\frac{d}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta};\frac{d}{2}\right)}=\frac{P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{1};\frac{d}{2}\right)}{P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{m}+\frac{d}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta};\frac{d}{2}\right)}\Phi_{\mathbf{m}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z}),

where
\mathbf{1}:=(1,\ldots,1)
.
For the explicit formulas of
P_{\mathbf{m}}\left(\mathbf{1};\frac{d}{2}\right)
and
P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\mathrm{ip}}\left(\mathbf{m}+\frac{d}{2}\boldsymbol{\delta};\frac{d}{2}\right)
, we refer to
[
7
]
and
[
4
]
.
We also use the notations

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:=\{\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\mid m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{r}\geq 0\},

\displaystyle|\mathbf{z}|:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}z_{j},\qquad|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|:=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\partial_{z_{j}},\qquad A_{\pm,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}):=\!\prod_{1\leq k\not=i\leq r}\!\frac{x_{i}-x_{k}-\frac{d}{2}(i-k)\pm\frac{d}{2}}{x_{i}-x_{k}-\frac{d}{2}(i-k)},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i}:=(0,\ldots,0,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vee}}{{1}}}},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

\displaystyle\frac{(\operatorname{ad}{|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|})^{l}}{l!}S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z})

Theorem 1.1

(twisted Pieri formulas for Jack polynomials)
.

For
l=0,1,\ldots,r
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{(\operatorname{ad}{|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|})^{l}}{l!}S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z})\bigg]\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[r],\\

(1.6)

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{(\operatorname{ad}{|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|})^{l}}{l!}S_{r}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{z})\bigg]\Psi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})=\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subseteq[r],|J|=l,\\
\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{J}\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\Psi_{\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{J}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})I_{J^{c}}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{x})A_{+,J}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{J}),

(1.7)

where
J^{c}:=[r]\setminus J
,
\epsilon_{J}:=\sum_{j\in J}\epsilon_{j}
and

\displaystyle A_{\pm,J}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}):=\!\!\prod_{j\in J,\,l\in J^{c}}\!\!\frac{x_{j}-x_{l}-\frac{d}{2}(j-l)\pm\frac{d}{2}}{x_{j}-x_{l}-\frac{d}{2}(j-l)},

\displaystyle I_{J^{c}}^{(d)}(u;\mathbf{x}):=\bigg(\frac{2}{d}\bigg)^{r}\prod_{l\in J^{c}}\bigg(x_{l}+\frac{d}{2}(u+r-l)\bigg).

Since the first-order Sekiguchi operator
H_{r,1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})
coincides with the Euler operator
\sum_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}
essentially

\displaystyle H_{r,1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{2}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}+\frac{r(r-1)}{2},

we write down the operator

\displaystyle(\operatorname{ad}{|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|})H_{r,1}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})=(\operatorname{ad}{|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|})\bigg(\frac{2}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{r}z_{i}\partial_{z_{i}}\bigg)=\frac{2}{d}|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|.

Twisted Pieri formulas for Jack polynomials

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need the following summation formula.

Lemma 2.1

(mysterious summation)
.

For any
I\subseteq[r]
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\left(x_{i}+1+\frac{d}{2}(r-i)\right)A_{-,i,I\setminus{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}+\epsilon_{i})A_{+,i,I\setminus{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\sum_{i\in I}\left(x_{i}+\frac{d}{2}(r-i)\right)A_{+,i,I\setminus{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{i})A_{-,i,I\setminus{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})=|I|,

(2.1)

where
|I|
is the cardinality of
I
and

\displaystyle A_{\pm,i,I\setminus{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}):=\prod_{j\in I\setminus{i}}\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}-\frac{d}{2}(i-j)\pm\frac{d}{2}}{x_{i}-x_{j}-\frac{d}{2}(i-j)}.

If
r=1
, then (
2.1
) is equal to a trivial summation

\displaystyle(x+1)-x=1.

Proof 2.2
.

For convenience, we put

\displaystyle s_{j}:=x_{j}+\frac{d}{2}(r-j).

By Pieri type formulas for the Jack polynomials (
1.2
) and (

\displaystyle[|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|,|\mathbf{z}|]\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|\Phi_{\mathbf{x}+\epsilon_{i}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})A_{+,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{j=1}^{r}|\mathbf{z}|\Phi_{\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{j}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})s_{j}A_{-,j}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Phi_{\mathbf{x}+\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})\big((s_{j}+\delta_{j,i})A_{-,j}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}+\epsilon_{i})A_{+,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-s_{j}A_{-,j}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})A_{+,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})\sum_{i=1}^{r}\big((s_{i}+1)A_{-,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}+\epsilon_{i})A_{+,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-s_{i}A_{-,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})A_{+,i}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x}-\epsilon_{i})\big).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle[|\partial_{\mathbf{z}}|,|\mathbf{z}|]\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})=\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{z})r.

Then, we obtain (

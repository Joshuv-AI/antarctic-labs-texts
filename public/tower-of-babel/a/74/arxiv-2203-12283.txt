Higher differentiability results for solutions to a class of non-homogeneouns elliptic problems under sub-quadratic growth conditions
Albert Clop, Andrea Gentile, Antonia Passarelli di Napoli

Abstract
We prove a sharp higher differentiability result for local minimizers of functionals of the form ℱ ⁡ ( w , Ω ) = ∫ Ω [ F ⁡ ( x , D ​ w ​ ( x ) ) − f ⁡ ( x ) ⋅ w ⁡ ( x ) ] ​ 𝑑 x \mathcal{F}\left(w,\Omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left[F\left(x,Dw(x)\right)-f(x)\cdot w(x)\right]dx with non autonomous integrand F ⁡ ( x , ξ ) F(x,\xi) which is convex with respect to the gradient variable, under p p -growth conditions, with 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 . The main novelty here is that the results are obtained assuming that the partial map x ↦ D ξ ​ F ​ ( x , ξ ) x\mapsto D_{\xi}F(x,\xi) has weak derivatives in some Lebesgue space L q L^{q} and the datum f f is assumed to belong to a suitable Lebesgue space L r L^{r} . We also prove that it is possible to weaken the assumption on the datum f f and on the map x ↦ D ξ ​ F ​ ( x , ξ ) x\mapsto D_{\xi}F(x,\xi) , if the minimizers are assumed to be a priori bounded.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, with
n>2
. For
N\geq 1
and
w:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we consider the following non autonomous, non homogeneous functional

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}\left(w,\Omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left[F\left(x,Dw(x)\right)-f(x)\cdot w(x)\right]dx

In this paper we deal with the regularity properties of local minimizers of
\mathcal{F}
. To fix the ideas, we assume that
f\in L^{r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
is given, and
2<r<n
. The Carathéodory function
F:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}\to[0,+\infty)
is such that
\xi\mapsto F(x,\xi)
is
C^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}\right)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Morever, we assume that there exists real numbers
p\in(1,2)
and
\mu\in[0,1]
such that the following set of assumptions is satisfied:

•

There exist positive constants
\ell,L
such that

(1.2)

\ell\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq F(x,\xi)\leq L\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

for a. e.
x\in\Omega
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
.

•

There exists a positive constant
\nu>0,
such that

(1.3)

\langle D_{\xi}F(x,\xi)-D_{\xi}F(x,\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle\geq\nu\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|^{2}

for a. e.
x\in\Omega
and every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
.

•

There exists a positive constant
L_{1}>0,
such that

(1.4)

\left|D_{\xi}F(x,\xi)-D_{\xi}F(x,\eta)\right|\leq L_{1}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|

for a. e.
x\in\Omega
and every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
.

•

There exists a non-negative function
g\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
such that

(1.5)

\left|D_{\xi}F\left(x,\xi\right)-D_{\xi}F\left(y,\xi\right)\right|\leq\left(g(x)+g(y)\right)\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left|x-y\right|

for a.e.
x,y\in\Omega
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
.

In the last years, there has been great interest in understanding the differentiability of minimizers under some weak differentiability assumption for
F
in the
x
variable. Examples of this are the works by Passarelli di Napoli
[
20
,
21
]
for the case
p\geq 2
. In this paper, we intend to explain how the situation changes if
p
stays in the lowest range of growths, that is,
1<p<2
.

Among the big quantity of previous contributions, we wish to mention works by Di Benedetto
[
11
]
and Manfredi
[
18
]
, all in mid 80’s, in which it is proven that local minimizers have Hölder continuous derivatives under different instances, all of them involving autonomous functionals
F
(i.e. non depending on
x
) with growth exponent
p>1
, and with independent term
f=0
. Next, in
[
14
]
, the Hölder continuity of the gradient of solutions has been proved in case
p\geq 2
and Hölder continuous coefficients with
f=0
.

It was not until the work of Acerbi and Fusco
[
1
]
that an extension of this result was provided for functionals
F
that could depend on
x
in a Hölder continuous manner. In order to prove the Lipschitz continuity of local minimizers, in the same paper (
[
1
]
), for
1<p<2
but still for
f=0
, the second order regularity of the local minimizers is established in case of constant coefficients. Very recently, A. Gentile
[
12
]
extended the higher differentiability result of
[
1
]
to the case when the dependence of
F
in
x
is of Sobolev type. In presence of a bounded independent term
f
, Tolksdorf studied functionals
F
that are Lipschitz continuous in the space variable, and obtained that local minimizers are Hölder continuous.

In case of degenerate elliptic functionals, we mention the recent papers
[
4
,
9
,
8
]
, in wich Lipschitz regularity of solutions is established assuming
f\in L^{r}
, with
r>n
.

In the present paper, our first goal is to get for the minimizers not the Hölder continuous first derivatives, but up to
L^{p}
second order derivatives. We wish to do this in two ways. First, by requiring
F
the minimal possible regularity in
x
. Second, by finding optimal conditions on
f
that make the whole scheme work.

Actually, the first result we prove in this paper is the following. Below, we denote
V_{p}(\xi)=\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\xi
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and
1<p<2
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a local minimizer of the functional (
1.1
), under the assumptions (
1.2
)–(
1.5
), with

f\in L^{\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad g\in L^{n}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right).

Then
V_{p}\left(Du\right)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
, and the estimate

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Du(x)\right)\right)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{R^{\beta\left(n,p\right)}}\left[\int_{B_{R}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\int_{B_{R}}|f(x)|^{\frac{np}{n\left(p-1\right)+2-p}}dx+c\int_{B_{R}}g^{n}(x)dx+\left|B_{R}\right|\right],

holds true for any ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
, with
\beta(n,p)>0.

At this point, it is worth mentioning that
|D^{2}u|\simeq\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Du\right)\right)\right|\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{4}}
so that the above theorem establishes a weighted estimate for the second derivative. Furthermore, from Young’s inequality one has that
\left|D^{2}u\right|^{p}\simeq\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Du\right)\right)\right|^{2}+\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}
so that, in particular,
V_{p}\left(Du\right)\in W^{1,2}
implies
u\in W^{2,p}
. See Section
2.2
for details.

Also, for what concerns the assumptions on
f
, we observe that

2<\frac{np}{n\left(p-1\right)+2-p}<n,

for any
n>2
and
1<p<2
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the combination of an a priori estimate and a suitable approximation argument. In order to achieve the a priori estimate under the sharp integrability assumption on the independent term
f
, besides the use of the difference quotient method, we need to apply carefully the well known iteration Lemma on concentric balls to control the terms with critical integrability. We’d like to mention that previous higher differentiability result in the subquadratic non standard growth case has been recently obtained in
[
19
]
with independent term
f\in L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. Especially, note that

\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}<\frac{p}{p-1}\iff 1<p<2.

Also in case of degenerate elliptic equations with sub-quadratic growth, in the recent paper
[
2
]
, a fractional higher differentiability has been obtained under a Besov or Sobolev assumption on the datum
f
.

In
[
7
]
, an higher differentiability result has been established for local minimizers of (
1.1
) in the case
p\geq 2
and for
f\in L^{n}\log^{\alpha}L
, with
\alpha>0
. For other Lipschitz regularity and higher differentiability results for solutions to non-homogeeneous elliptic problems we also refer to
[
3
,
10
]
and to
[
17
]
for regularity results of solutions to problems with measure data.

We want to stress that, for what concerns the regularity of the funtion
f
, Theorem
1.1
is a sharp result: it is not possible to weaken the assumption on the integrability of the datum, as we will see with a counterexample in Section
4
below.

Independently to the previous problem, a new interest has arosen in the last years. It consists in describing the regularity properties of local minimizers which one assumes apriori bounded. The reason for this is that many times local boundedness of minimizers is available much before any sort of weak differentiability. Also, as it will be clear from our results, apriori boundedness of minimizers helps in relaxing the assumptions for
f
, at least when
n
is not too small. Results in this direction are available so far just for
p\geq 2
. An example of this can be found in Carozza, Kristensen and Passarelli di Napoli
[
6
]
, where
F
is assumed Lipschitz-continuous in
x
(see also
[
5
]
). Similar analysis have been done by Giova and Passarelli di Napoli in
[
15
]
assuming only Sobolev regularity for
F
in the
x
variable. The second goal in the present paper consists of exploring if this kind of results is available also under the assumption
1<p<2
. We obtained the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
1<p<2
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
be a local minimizer of the functional (
1.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) with

f\in L^{\frac{p+2}{p}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad g\in L^{p+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right).

Then
V_{p}\left(Du\right)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
and the estimate

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Du(x)\right)\right)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{4R})}}{R^{\frac{p+2}{p}}}\left[\int_{B_{4R}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Du(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\int_{B_{R}}g^{p+2}(x)dx+\int_{B_{R}}\left|f(x)\right|^{\frac{p+2}{p}}dx+\left|B_{R}\right|+1\right]

holds for any ball
B_{4R}\Subset\Omega
.

Notice here that for
n>2
and
1<p<2
we have
2<\frac{p+2}{p}<n
. Also, by comparing the assumptions on
f
both in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, it is worth noticing that for
1<p<2
one has

\frac{p+2}{p}<\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}\hskip 28.45274pt\Longleftrightarrow\hskip 28.45274ptn>p+2.

Therefore, Theorem
1.2
improves Theorem
1.1
whenever
n\geq 4
.

In proving Theorem
1.2
, once the a priori estimate is established, the more delicate issue is to construct some approximating problems in a convenient way. For this, these aproximating problems need to be smooth with respect to the dependence on the
x
-variable and on the datum
f
. Also, they need to have minimizers whose
L^{r}
norm is close to the
L^{\infty}
norm of the minimizer of the original problem for
r
sufficiently large. We overcome this difficulties by using the penalization method introduced in
[
6
]
. Still, we need to prove second order estimates for the approximating minimizers which, as far as we know, are available only for
p\geq 2
( see
[
15
]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we collect definitions and preliminary results. Section 3,contains the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section 4, we give the counterexample showing the optimality of the assimption on the datum. In Section 5 we give the proof of the higher differentiability of the local minimizers of a class of variational integrals with a singular penalisation term. In Section 6, we give the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.1
Difference quotients

A key instrument in studying regularity properties of solutions to problems of Calculus of Variations and PDEs is the so called
difference quotients method
.

In this section, we recall the definition and some basic results.

Definition 2.4
.

Given
h\in\mathbb{R}
, for every function

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for any
s=1,...,n
the finite difference operator in the direction
x_{s}
is
defined by

\tau_{s,h}F(x)=F\left(x+he_{s}\right)-F(x),

where
e_{s}
is the unit vector in the direction
x_{s}
.

In the following, in order to simplify the notations, we will omit the vector
e_{s}
unless it is necessary, denoting

\tau_{h}F(x)=F(x+h)-F(x),

where
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We now describe some properties of the operator
\tau_{h}
, whose proofs can be found, for example, in
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F
and
G
be two functions such that
F,G\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, with
p\geq 1
, and let us consider the set

\Omega_{|h|}:=\Set{x\in\Omega:d\left(x,\partial\Omega\right)>\left}{h}.

Then

(a)

\tau_{h}F\in W^{1,p}\left(\Omega_{|h|}\right)
and

D_{i}(\tau_{h}F)=\tau_{h}(D_{i}F).

(b)
If at least one of the functions
F
or
G
has support contained
in
\Omega_{|h|}
then

\int_{\Omega}F(x)\tau_{h}G(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}G(x)\tau_{-h}F(x)dx.

(c)
We have

\tau_{h}(FG)(x)=F(x+h)\tau_{h}G(x)+G(x)\tau_{h}F(x).

The next result about finite difference operator is a kind
of integral version of Lagrange Theorem.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
0<\rho<R
,
|h|<\frac{R-\rho}{2}
,
1<p<+\infty
,
and
F,DF\in L^{p}(B_{R})
then

\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{h}F(x)|^{p}\ dx\leq c(n,p)|h|^{p}\int_{B_{R}}|DF(x)|^{p}\ dx.

Moreover

\int_{B_{\rho}}|F(x+h)|^{p}\ dx\leq\int_{B_{R}}|F(x)|^{p}\ dx.

The following result is proved in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
F\in L^{p}\left(B_{R}\right)
with
1<p<+\infty
. Suppose that there exist
\rho\in(0,R)
and
M>0
such that

\sum_{s=1}^{n}\int_{B_{\rho}}|\tau_{s,h}F(x)|^{p}dx\leq M^{p}|h|^{p}

for
\left|h\right|<\frac{R-\rho}{2}
. Then
F\in W^{1,p}(B_{R},\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover

\left\Arrowvert DF\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}(B_{\rho})}\leq M,

\left\Arrowvert F\right\Arrowvert_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(B\rho)}\leq c\left(M+\left\Arrowvert F\right\Arrowvert_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\right),

with
c=c(n,N,p,\rho,R)
, and

\frac{\tau_{s,h}F}{\left|h\right|}\to D_{s}F\qquad\mbox{ in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right),\mbox{ as }h\to 0,

for each
s=1,...,n.

2.2
An auxiliary function

Here we define an auxiliary function of the gradient variable that will be useful in the following.

The function
V_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times N}
, defined as

V_{p}(\xi):=\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\xi,

for which the following estimates hold (see
[
1
]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
1<p<2
. There is a constant
c=c(n,p)>0
such that

(2.13)

c^{-1}\left|\xi-\eta\right|\leq\left|V_{p}(\xi)-V_{p}(\eta)\right|\cdot\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{4}}\leq c\left|\xi-\eta\right|,

for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 2.9
.

One can easily check that, for a

function

C(p)
such that

(2.14)

C^{-1}\left|D^{2}v\right|^{2}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\leq\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Dv\right)\right)\right|^{2}\leq C\left|D^{2}v\right|^{2}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

almost everywhere.

In what follows, the following result can be useful.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,
1<p<2

(2.15)

\int_{B_{r}}\left|D^{2}v(x)\right|^{p}dx\leq c\cdot\left[1+\int_{B_{R}}\left|D\left(V_{p}\left(Dv(x)\right)\right)\right|^{2}+c\int_{B_{R}}\left|Dv(x)\right|^{p}\right].

holds for any ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
and

.

Proof.

We will prove the existence of the second-order weak derivatives of

Let us consider a ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
and

.

For
|h|<\frac{R-r}{2}
, we have
0<\frac{R}{2}<r<\rho_{1}:=r+|h|<R-|h|=:\rho_{2}<R
, and by (
2.13

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\left|\tau_{s,h}Dv(x)\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

and since

, by Lemma

and Young’s Inequality, we have

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left[|h|^{2}\int_{B_{R}}\left|DV_{p}\left(Dv(x)\right)\right|^{2}dx\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{B_{r}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|Dv\left(x+he_{s}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|Dv(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right]^{\frac{2-p}{p}}

Remark 2.13
.

For further needs we record the following elementary inequality

(2.16)

\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq 2\left(\mu^{p}+\left|V_{p}(\xi)\right|^{2}\right)

for every

Note that this is obvious if
\mu=0

In case
\mu>0
, we distinguish two cases.
If

we trivially have

\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}\mu^{p}

If

\displaystyle\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)\leq 2\left(\mu^{2}+\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left|\xi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left|V_{p}(\xi)\right|^{2}

Joining two previous inequalities we get (
2.16
).

Moreover, if

which is finite by the Sobolev’s embedding Theorem, for any ball

Proof of Theorem

(see, for example
[

]
and
[

]
).

4
A Counterexample

The aim of this section is to show that we cannot weaken the assumption
f\in L^{\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\Omega\right)
in the scale of Lebesgue spaces.

Our example also shows that this phenomenon is independent of the presence of the coefficients, but it depends only on the sub-quadratic growth of the energy density.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let us set

\beta=\left(\alpha-1\right)\left(p-1\right)-1,

and consider the functional

\mathcal{F_{\alpha}}\left(u,\Omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|Du(x)\right|^{p}-\alpha\left(n+\beta\right)\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{\beta}u(x)\right]dx,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set containing the origin,
1<p<2
,
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Using the classical notation for the
p
-Laplacian

\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}\left(\left|Du\right|^{p-2}\cdot Du\right),

a local minimizer of this functional is a weak solution to the
p
-Poisson equation

(4.1)

\Delta_{p}u=f_{\alpha},

with

f_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\left(n+\beta\right)\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{\beta}.

Before going further, let us notice that (
4.1
) is an autonomous equation, whose solutions are scalar functions, so the problem we’re dealing with is much less general with respect to the assumption we considered in order to prove our main result.

It is easy to check that, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

u_{\alpha}(x)=\left|x\right|^{\alpha}

is a solution to (
4.1
).

Indeed, since, for each
i=1,...,n
, we have

D_{x_{i}}u_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\left|x\right|^{\alpha-2}x_{i},

we get

\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|=\left|\alpha\right|\left|x\right|^{\alpha-1}.

So, for every
i=1,...,n
, since
\beta=\left(\alpha-1\right)\left(p-1\right)-1,
we get

\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p-2}D_{x_{i}}u_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{(p-1)\cdot(\alpha-1)-1}x_{i}=\alpha\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{\beta}x_{i}

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p-2}D_{x_{i}}u_{\alpha}(x)\right)=\alpha\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{\beta}\left(1+\frac{\beta x_{i}^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2}}\right),

so

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm div}\left(\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p-2}\cdot Du_{\alpha}(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p-2}D_{x_{i}}u_{\alpha}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left|x\right|^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{\beta x_{i}^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2}}\right)=\alpha\left|\alpha\right|^{p-2}\left(n+\beta\right)\left|x\right|^{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(x)

Moreover, for further needs, we observe that

\left|D^{2}u_{\alpha}(x)\right|=c(\alpha)\cdot\left|x\right|^{\alpha-2},

for a constant
c(\alpha)\geq 0
.
Choosing

\alpha-1=\frac{2-n}{p}

we
have

f_{\alpha}=C_{1}(\alpha,n,p)|x|^{\frac{(2-n)(p-1)}{p}-1},

where
C_{1}(\alpha,n,p)
ia real constant, and

\left|f_{\alpha}\right|^{\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}}=C(\alpha,n,p)\left|x\right|^{-n},

with
C(\alpha,n,p)\geq 0
.

Therefore with such a choice of
\alpha
, we have
f_{\alpha}\in L^{\frac{np}{n\left(p-1\right)+2-p}-\varepsilon}\left(B_{1}(0)\right)
for every
\varepsilon>0
, but
f_{\alpha}
doesn’t belong to
L^{\frac{np}{n(p-1)+2-p}}(B_{1}(0))
.

With the same choice of
\alpha
we have

\left|Du_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{p-2}\cdot\left|D^{2}u_{\alpha}(x)\right|^{2}=c(n,p)\cdot\left|x\right|^{p\cdot(\alpha-1)-2}=c(n,p)\cdot\left|x\right|^{2-n-2}=c(n,p)\cdot\left|x\right|^{-n},

that doesn’t belong to
L^{1}\left(B_{1}(0)\right)
. Therefore we cannot weaken the assumption on datum
f
in the scale of Lebesgue spaces and obtain the same regularity for the second derivatives of the solution
u
.
